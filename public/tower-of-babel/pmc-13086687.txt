Long-term clinical outcomes of ischial spine fascia fixation compared with sacrospinous ligament fixation: a prospective cohort study over 10 years
Authors: Yiwei Zhang, Zhibo Zhang, Rusha Yin, Chang Ren, Lan Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo compare the long-term effects of ischial spine fascia fixation (ISFF) and sacrospinous ligament fixation (SLFF).MethodsIn a single-center prospective cohort study of 42 patients, 22 patients underwent ISFF and 20 patients underwent SSLF. The follow-up period of more than 10 years included a total of 32 patients. The main outcomes are the subjective failure and retreatment rates.ResultsFifteen of twenty patients (75%) in the SSLF group and seventeen of twenty-two patients (77.3%) in the ISFF group completed the last follow-up with a median follow-up time of 133 (120–156) months. The ISFF and SSLF groups achieved estimated subjective failure rate of 40.35% and 31.62%, respectively, at the postoperative period of 10 years. The retreatment rates for both the ISFF and SSLF groups remained consistent at 5 and 10-year intervals after surgery, with corresponding percentages of 16.49% and 11.11%. While the ISFF group exhibited marginally elevated subjective failure rates and re-treatment rates compared to the SSLF group, no statistically significant difference was observed. No patients had pain in the leg or hip over 10-year follow-up. ConclusionISFF is a safe approach that showed no statistically significant difference in recurrence results and improved quality of life scores compared to SSLF. Due to the relatively small sample size (N = 42), this study is underpowered to definitively claim equivalence or non-inferiority between the two procedures. This long-term study on native tissue transvaginal repair for POP indicates the clinical use of ISFF is safe and long-lasting.Keywords: Ischial spine fascia fixation, Sacrospinous ligament fixation, Pelvic organ prolapse, Long-term, Quality of life

What does this study add to the clinical work

This study provides 10-year longitudinal evidence that ischial spine fascia fixation (ISFF) achieves efficacy with no statistically significant difference compared to sacrospinous ligament fixation (SSLF) in pelvic organ prolapse repair, thereby addressing the lack of long-term data for ISFF. It further demonstrates that ISFF’s more superficial anatomical location, which does not require specialized instruments, lowers the technical barrier and reduces the risk of neurological complications, making it a practical and safe alternative to SSLF for routine clinical practice.

Introduction

Pelvic organ prolapse (POP) has become one of the most common benign gynecologic disorders in middle-aged and elderly women. A nationwide epidemiological survey in China suggests that the prevalence of symptomatic POP is 9.6% [1]. Based on a U.S. claims and encounters database, the estimated lifetime risk of surgery for POP is 12.6% [2]. In 1958, Sederl proposed fixing the vagina on the sacrospinous ligament to support the apex of the vagina [3]. Now, sacrospinous ligament fixation (SSLF) is widely used in native tissue transvaginal repair for POP. In 2014 and 2018, the reports from the OPTIMAL randomized clinical trial were published in JAMA, and SSLF gradually became a standardized and mature surgery [4]. However, SSLF is not free of complications, and pudendal nerve damage has been identified as a notable one [5], as anatomical studies have confirmed that the pudendal nerve is anatomically proximate to the sacrospinous ligament and courses adjacent to its lateral aspect, making it vulnerable to iatrogenic injury during suture placement or surgical dissection [6]. Beyond such neurological complications, SSLF poses practical technical challenges in routine clinical practice because it needs proficient surgical skills and a thorough understanding of deep pelvic anatomy while most clinicians perform this procedure with only conventional instruments rather than specialized suture-capturing devices [7]. The sacrospinous ligament is located in the deep, narrow pelvic cavity, so without specialized devices, its exposure and accurate suture placement become technically demanding, which not only prolongs operative time but also elevates the risk of iatrogenic injury and raises the learning curve, limitations that highlight the need for a more accessible alternative apical suspension technique [8].

Lan Z and colleagues dissected the ischial spine region on seven embalmed and three fresh female cadavers in 2007 and found that the fascia of the ischial spine (ISF) is the junction of the sacrospinous ligament, tendinous arch of levator ani, tendinous arch of pelvic fascia, and obturator fascia [9]. The tissue is dense and has no important blood vessels or nerves. From an anatomical and biomechanical perspective, its properties make it a safe and feasible site for apical support, and its superficial anatomical location eliminates the need for specialized instruments, addressing the practical limitations of SSLF, leading to the development of the surgical technique known as ischial spine fascia fixation (ISFF). ISFF has been validated in the mid-term as a safe and effective alternative to SSLF for women with symptomatic stage 2 and 3 POP [10]. However, with increasing longevity, the long-term efficacy of ISFF is of great interest but as yet unstudied. A prospective cohort comprising 22 patients who received ISFF and 20 patients who received SSLF was established between 2012 and 2015. A total of 17 ISFF patients and 15 SSLF patients completed the final follow-up in 2025, with a median follow-up duration of 133 months. The aim of the present study was to investigate the long-term effect of the ISFF compared with SSLF.

Methods

A single-center parallel prospective cohort study was carried out among women with symptomatic stage II and III POP since 2012. The study was approved by the PUMCH institutional review board (S-498); written informed consent was obtained from all participants before enrollment; and all procedures were performed in accordance with relevant regulations.

Women with symptomatic POP stage II or higher who were prepared to undergo the ISFF or SSLF procedure were included in the cohort. Those who had acute genital infection, were too ill to undergo surgery, or could not adopt a lithotomy position were excluded. An examination for prolapse staging with standard POP Quantification (POP-Q) and a urodynamic evaluation were performed before surgery. The decision regarding the choice of surgery (ISFF or SSLF) was made based on the physician's recommendation and patient preference. Hysterectomy and anti-incontinence surgery were concurrently performed as indicated.

All surgical procedures were performed by one experienced researcher (LZ) as previously described [10]. In summary, a midline longitudinal incision was performed on the posterior vaginal vault and the rectovaginal space on the right side was opened. For ISFF, a 1–0 non-absorbable suture (Mersilk, Ethicon Inc., Shanghai, China) was inserted through the fascia 1 cm lateral to the ischial spine, and a second suture was placed 0.5 cm lateral to the first one. For SSLF, two 1–0 non-absorbable sutures (Mersilk) were securely attached to the sacrospinous ligament using a long needle holder positioned 2.5 cm from the spine which had been exposed with two S-shaped retractors. The vaginal apex and stump of the right uterosacral ligament were then connected using previously placed sutures before closing of the posterior incision. To clarify the anatomical distinction between the two techniques, ISFF targets the superficial fascia overlying the ischial spine, whereas SSLF requires fixation to the deeper sacrospinous ligament, as illustrated in our prior cadaveric study [10]. The validated Chinese version of Pelvic Floor Distress Inventory-20 (PFDI-20) [11] and Pelvic Floor Impact Questionnaire-7 (PFIQ-7) [12] were used to measure the extent of affection to quality of life (QoL). The demographic information, peri-operative parameters, complications and validated condition-specific questionnaires were collected during hospitalization, and the information was verified and entered into a computer database. These complications included peri-operative massive bleeding, organ injury, postoperative pelvic hematoma, pain, de novo urinary incontinence. In each subsequent follow-up, PFDI-20 and PFIQ-7 were collected to evaluate subjective surgical outcomes and QoLs.

The objective of the study was to evaluate the long-term outcomes of ISFF compared with SSLF, including the subjective failure rate and retreatment rate. Subjective failure was defined as vaginal bulge symptoms as indicated by a positive response on Question 3 of the PFDI-20: “Do you usually have a bulge—something falling out that you can see or feel in your vaginal area?” Retreatment was defined as reoperation or use of a pessary for POP indication.

We calculated the mean (standard deviation) or median (range) for continuous variables and the frequency (proportion) for categorical variables. For preoperative and postoperative parameters, we performed a paired analysis of the same patient. Student’s t-test or Mann–Whitney U test was used to compare continuous variables between groups. Categorical data were analyzed by the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test. Survival curves for subjective failure and retreatment of both groups during follow-up over 10 years were estimated using Kaplan–Meier analysis. Statistical tests were two-sided, and a P value of < 0.05 indicated a significant difference. The statistical analysis was performed using SPSS Version 22.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Results

In total, 42 women with symptomatic stage II or III POP were enrolled: 20 in the SSLF group and 22 in the ISFF group. Age, body mass index, and other baseline characteristics were similar between the groups (Table 1). The surgical parameters were comparable between the two groups in Table 1. Overall, 16 (72.7%) and 17 (85%) women were postmenopausal in the ISFF and SSLF groups, respectively. The average duration of postoperative hospital stay in the ISFF group (4 days) was significantly shorter than that in the SSLF group (7 days) (P = 0.001).

The indication for either ISFF or SSLF was a symptomatic stage II or greater prolapse. Regarding the overall POP-Q staging, 90.9% of patients in the ISFF group (20/22) were diagnosed with stage III prolapse, while the corresponding proportion in the SSLF group was 85.0% (17/20). There was no statistically significant difference in the distribution of overall POP-Q stages between the two groups (P = 0.689). Although there was no statistical difference between the 2 groups (p = 0.096), it is worth noting that 9 patients (45%) in the SSLF group underwent concomitant anti-incontinence surgery due to stress urinary incontinence during the operation, which represents a higher proportion than the 4 patients (18.2%) who received this treatment in the ISFF group.

During the follow-up period, five patients in both groups were lost to follow-up due to changes in contact information or relocation, respectively. From June to August 2025, a total of 15 patients (75%) completed the final outpatient follow-up in the SSLF group, while in the ISFF group, 17 patients (77.3%) successfully completed their last outpatient follow-up. All patients were followed up for over 10 years, with a median follow-up time of 133 (120–156) months. Number of patients at risk over time is displayed below the Kaplan–Meier curves in Fig. 2. Although subjective failure rates showed an increasing trend during the follow-up period, the rate of retreatment did not exhibit any temporal increase (Fig. 2 and Table 2). The retreatment rates at estimated 5 and 10 years were equivalent in both groups. From 5 to 10 years postoperatively, the estimated subjective failure rate rose from 22.46% (95% CI 9.03%–49.51%; n = 4) to 40.35% (95% CI 21.58%–66.66%; n = 7) in the ISFF group, compared with an increase from 24.79% (95% CI 9.99%–53.74%; n = 4) to 31.62% (95% CI 14.41%–60.50%; n = 5) in the SSLF group. For retreatment, the estimated rates remained unchanged from 5 to 10 years in both groups: 16.49% (95% CI 5.61%–43.02%; n = 3) in the ISFF group and 11.11% (95% CI 2.90%–37.58%; n = 2) in the SSLF group (Table 2). The subjective failure rate (p = 0.7324) and retreatment rate (p = 0.7745) showed no significant difference between the two groups at 10 years (Fig. 2 and Table 2).

We compared the QoL questionnaire between the preoperative and the final follow-up in both groups. In the SSLF group at ≥ 10-year follow-up, the median PFDI-20 (33.50 vs. 54.25, P = 0.029) and PFIQ-7 (0.00 vs. 47.62, P < 0.01) total scores improved significantly. Subscale analysis revealed significant reductions in POPDI-6 (P = 0.016) and UDI-6 (P = 0.038) scores, but not in CRADI-8 (P = 0.496). For PFIQ-7, only the POPIQ-7 subscale improved significantly (P < 0.01), with no significant change in UDIQ-7 (P = 0.286) or CRAIQ-7 (P = 0.317) (Table 3). The ISFF group exhibited a comparable trend, with significant long-term (> 10 years) improvements in the median PFDI-20 (25.00 vs. 58.25, P = 0.002) and PFIQ-7 (0.00 vs. 62.00, P = 0.005) total scores. Subscale analysis confirmed significant reductions in POPDI-6 (P < 0.001) and UDI-6 (P = 0.026), but not in CRADI-8 (P = 0.298). For PFIQ-7, only the POPIQ-7 subscale decreased significantly (P = 0.001), with UDIQ-7 (P = 0.498) and CRAIQ-7 (P = 0.168) remaining stable (Table 4). Subsequently, a comparison of PFDI-20 and PFIQ-7 scores between the two groups was conducted at the 10-year postoperative follow-up. The results revealed no statistically significant differences in overall and subscale scores for both questionnaires between the two groups (P = 0.374 and 0.819, respectively) (Table 5). After 10 years of surgery, no patients had leg or hip pain.

Discussion

SSLF as a native tissue transvaginal apical approach is widely used for POP repair. For obese patients with deep pelvic cavities or short vaginas, achieving deep apical fixation via transvaginal approaches is technically demanding. Suture-capturing devices such as Capio Slim are usually required to place sutures into the sacrospinous ligament, increasing treatment costs [13]. A systematic review and meta-analysis demonstrated that suture passing complications, including reoperation, nerve injury, hematoma, and blood transfusion, are increased with suture-capturing devices for sacrospinous ligament fixation [7]. Most clinicians do not use such specialized devices, increasing SSLF’s technical challenges, especially in patients with deep pelvic cavities.

Compared with SSLF, ISFF is considerably easier due to the ISF’s shallower anatomical position. Its superficial location allows easier dissection and suture placement with conventional instruments, reducing technical barriers and addressing SSLF’s limitations. To compare their safety and effectiveness, we established a prospective cohort in 2012. Previous results showed ISFF’s technical simplicity did not compromise short-term outcomes [14, 15]. A Swedish national cohort study reported 33.7% of SSLF patients had vaginal bulging at 1 year and an 18.8% 2-year reoperation rate [16]. Our single-center cohort found ISFF achieved similar 1-year objective success, recurrence and subjective satisfaction rates to SSLF [14]. A recent randomized control study reported 2-year composite, anatomical, and subjective failure rates of 23.0%, 13.3% and 10.5% [17], comparable to our 3-year outcomes. Our 2011–2018 single-arm cohort reported a 3-year SSLF composite failure rate of 21.34% (95% CI 9.30%–31.79%) [15].

No prior studies reported outcomes beyond 10 years for either procedure. In our 10-year study, 15 (75%) SSLF and 17 (77.2%) ISFF patients completed final follow-up during June–August 2025. Unlike a retrospective study reporting approximately 5.1% SSLF subjective impairment during medium and long-term follow-up [18], we observed 10-year subjective failure rates of 40.35% (ISFF) and 31.62% (SSLF). Retreatment rates remained stable at 16.49% (ISFF) and 11.11% (SSLF) at 5 and 10 years. No significant between‑group differences were observed in subjective failure or retreatment rates over 10 years. However, due to the relatively small sample size (N = 42) and wide 95% confidence intervals, this study is underpowered to definitively claim equivalence or non-inferiority between ISFF and SSLF. Subjective failure increased with follow‑up duration, while retreatment rates were time-independent.

SSLF had a higher concomitant anti-incontinence surgery rate (45.0%, 9/20) than ISFF (18.2%, 4/22), while anterior and posterior colporrhaphies and preoperative POP-Q stages were balanced (Table 1). No between-group differences in concomitant procedures were significant. Our primary outcomes were subjective failure and retreatment rates, which were based on overall POP outcomes not urinary incontinence symptoms, which are not alleviated by ISFF or SSLF. Both groups had significant, comparable PFDI-20 and PFIQ-7 improvements and numerical differences in concomitant procedures did not affect efficacy. Future large-scale studies with stratified analysis by concomitant procedures are needed to validate this conclusion.

Pelvic floor reconstruction aims to restore normal anatomy, prevent recurrence, and improve quality of life [19]. A recent meta-analysis of 49 studies confirmed that POP surgery significantly improved quality of life, with 67.50% and 76.98% reductions in mean PFDI-20 and PFIQ-7 scores, respectively [20]. We used PFDI-20 and PFIQ-7 to evaluate long-term function and quality of life. Scores improved postoperatively, mainly due to relief of prolapse, but no significant defecation or urethral symptom improvements were seen based on CRADI-8, UDIQ-7, and CRAIQ-7. Although patients with stress urinary incontinence benefit from concurrent anti-incontinence surgery, lower urinary tract symptoms increased at 10 years, which may partially explain the non‑significant UDI‑6 decline.

SSLF’s most common long-term complication is gluteal and posterior thigh pain [21]. This is attributed to levator ani nerve entrapment, pudendal nerve injury (proximity to the sacrospinous ligament), or S3 and S4 branch injury [6]. Approximately 16% experience persistent pain at 6 weeks postoperatively [22]. No patients reported neuropathic pain at 10 years. Due to the low long-term pain rate and small sample size, we could not accurately assess the neurological injury risk with SSLF. The ISF serves as a strong anchor for middle pelvic suspension, with no major nerves or vessels at its fusion site, thus reducing the risk of postoperative pain and supporting ISFF as a favorable alternative to SSLF. Combined with its technical feasibility without specialized instruments, the neurosafety profile further supports ISFF as a practical alternative. Our study also found that hospital stay was significantly shorter after ISFF than after SSLF (P = 0.001), likely due to the shallower dissection required, enabling faster recovery.

This study’s strength is its 10-year follow-up, providing critical native-tissue POP repair long-term data. Limitations include small sample size, which renders the study underpowered to definitively prove equivalence or non-inferiority between ISFF and SSLF, underrepresentation of rare complications, advanced cohort age, and low sexual activity precluding robust postoperative sexual function assessment. Additionally, there was a clinically notable imbalance in the rate of concomitant anti-incontinence surgeries between groups (45.0% in SSLF vs 18.2% in ISFF, p = 0.096). Though not statistically significant, this discrepancy represents a key potential confounder for long-term urinary quality-of-life outcomes (UDI-6, UDIQ-7), as the non-randomized allocation of these procedures may have introduced selection bias. This limitation should be acknowledged when interpreting the urinary function results of this study.

In conclusion, ISFF is an excellent apical prolapse repair option, offering a safe, user-friendly, minimally invasive and effective approach for pelvic reconstructive surgeons. While no statistically significant differences in long-term efficacy were observed between ISFF and SSLF, the relatively small sample size (N = 42) limits our ability to definitively claim equivalence or non-inferiority between the two procedures. By overcoming SSLF’s technical limitations while maintaining comparable long-term efficacy, ISFF represents a clinically valuable alternative for POP repair.

Minimization of the discrete interaction energy with smooth potentials
Y. Almog *, Department of Mathematics

Abstract
We study the pair interaction on flat tori of functions whose Fourier coefficients are positive and decay sufficiently rapidly. In dimension one we find that the minimizer, up to translation, is the equidistant point set. In dimension two, minimizing with respect to triplets we find that the minimizer is the triangular lattice.

Introduction

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be even and periodic in all coordinates with a
period vector
(l_{1},\ldots,l_{d})
. Let
\mathcal{L}={\rm diag}(l_{1},\ldots,l_{d})
. For
instance we may consider

f(x)=f(|x|_{p})\,,

(1a)

where
x\in\mathbb{R}
and

|x|_{p}=\min_{{\boldsymbol{i}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|x-\mathcal{L}\boldsymbol{i}|\,.

(1b)

Define the Fourier coefficients of
f
,
\{a_{\boldsymbol{n}}\}_{\boldsymbol{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
, by

a_{\boldsymbol{n}}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{Q}|}\int_{\mathcal{Q}}e^{-i\pi\mathcal{L}^{-1}{\boldsymbol{n}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{x}}}f({\boldsymbol{x}})\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}\,,

(2)

where
\mathcal{Q}=\Pi_{i=1}^{d}[-l_{i},l_{i}]
. Consider then the functional

{\mathcal{J}}(\mu)=\int_{\mathcal{Q}^{2}}f(x-y)d\mu_{x}\,d\mu_{y}\,,

(3)

where
\mu
is a positive Borel measure for which

\mu(\mathcal{Q})=1
. We look for the infimum of
{\mathcal{J}}

{\mathcal{J}}_{m}=\inf_{\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{Q})}{\mathcal{J}}(\mu)\,,

where
{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{Q})
denotes the set of all probability
measures on
\mathcal{Q}
.

For example, in one-dimension, for
f\in L^{1}(-l,l)
satisfying
(
1
) we can write

{\mathcal{J}}(\mu)=\int_{[-l,l]^{2}}f(|x-y|_{p})d\mu_{x}\,d\mu_{y}\,,

where

|x|_{p}=\min(|x|,|2l-|x|)\,.

The minimum is then defined by

{\mathcal{J}}_{m}=\inf_{\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}([-l,l])}{\mathcal{J}}(\mu)\,,

and the Fourier coefficients by

a_{n}=\int_{[-l,l]}e^{-in\pi x/l}f(|x)\,dx\,.

We now make the following claim whose proof is given also in

[
9
]
for a more general setting

Lemma 1.1
.

Suppose that the Fourier coefficients of
f(x)
, defined by
(
2
), are all non-negative. Let
\mu_{L}
denote the Lebesgue
measure on
\mathcal{Q}
normalized by
|\mathcal{Q}|
. Then,

{\mathcal{J}}_{m}={\mathcal{J}}(\mu_{L})\,.

Furthermore, for any
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{Q})
we have

{\mathcal{J}}(\mu)-{\mathcal{J}}(\mu_{L})=|\mathcal{Q}|^{2}\sum_{\boldsymbol{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{\boldsymbol{0}\}}a_{\boldsymbol{n}}|b_{\boldsymbol{n}}|^{2}\,,

(4)

where
\{b_{\boldsymbol{n}}\}_{\boldsymbol{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
are the Fourier
coefficients associated with
\mu
.

Proof.

Let
\nu=\mu-\mu_{L}
. We first write

{\mathcal{J}}(\mu)-{\mathcal{J}}(\mu_{L})=2\int_{\mathcal{Q}^{2}}f(x-y)d\boldsymbol{x}\,d\nu_{y}+\int_{\mathcal{Q}^{2}}f(x-y)d\nu_{x}\,d\nu_{y}\,.

Since

\int_{\mathcal{Q}}f(x-y)d\boldsymbol{x}

is independent of
y
, and since
\nu(\mathcal{Q})=0
, we obtain that

{\mathcal{J}}(\mu)-{\mathcal{J}}(\mu_{L})=\int_{\mathcal{Q}^{2}}f(x-y)d\nu_{x}\,d\nu_{y}\,.

(5)

We next note that

f(x-y)=\sum_{\boldsymbol{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}a_{\boldsymbol{n}}e^{i\pi\mathcal{L}^{-1}\boldsymbol{n}\cdot(\boldsymbol{x-y})}\,,

and

d\nu_{x}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{\boldsymbol{0}\}}b_{\boldsymbol{n}}e^{i\pi\mathcal{L}^{-1}\boldsymbol{n}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}}\,dx\,.

Substituting the above into (
5
) immediately yields
(
4
).
∎

Consider then the case where
\mathcal{Q}=[0,1]^{N}
and

\mu=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\delta_{\boldsymbol{x}_{k}}\,,

(6)

in which
\boldsymbol{x}_{k}\in\mathcal{Q}
. In this case we have for any
{\boldsymbol{n}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}

b_{\boldsymbol{n}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\exp\{i2\pi\boldsymbol{n}\cdot{\boldsymbol{x}}_{k}\}\,.

(7)

We attempt to minimize
{\mathcal{J}}
over the set
{\mathcal{M}}_{N}
(which is isomorphic
to
[0,1)^{N}
) of measures given by (
6
) for a given value of

N
, or equivalently, over all sequences
\{x_{k}\}_{k=0}^{N-1}\subset\mathcal{Q}
.
We can write

in view of (

{\mathcal{J}}(\mu)=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{m,k=0}^{N-1}f(\boldsymbol{x}_{m}-\boldsymbol{x}_{k})=\frac{(N-1)}{N}E_{f}+\frac{f(\boldsymbol{0})}{N}\,,

(8)

where

E_{f}=\frac{1}{N(N-1)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{m},k=0\\
m\neq k\end{subarray}}^{N-1}f(\boldsymbol{x}_{m}-\boldsymbol{x}_{k})\,.

(9)

Consequently, the minimizer of
{\mathcal{J}}
over measures defined by
(
6
) is also the minimizer of
E
.

Let

\mathbb{Z}_{K}=\mathbb{Z}\mod K=\{0,1,\ldots,K-1\}\,,

and
{\boldsymbol{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}_{K}^{d}
.
We proceed with the most straightforward upper bound, which is
obtained by using the
square lattice in
\mathcal{Q}

\boldsymbol{x}_{\boldsymbol{n}}=\frac{1}{K}\boldsymbol{n}

It can be easily verified that for every
\boldsymbol{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}

\bar{b}_{\boldsymbol{m}}=\frac{(-1)^{\sum_{i=1}^{d}m_{i}}}{K^{d}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{K}^{d}}\exp\Big\{i\frac{2\pi}{K}\boldsymbol{m}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}\Big\}\,.

From which we obtain

\bar{b}_{\boldsymbol{m}}=\begin{cases}1&\frac{1}{K}\boldsymbol{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\,.\end{cases}

By (
4
) we now easily

{\mathcal{J}}_{m}-{\mathcal{J}}(\mu_{L})\leq\sum_{\boldsymbol{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus(0,0)}a_{K\boldsymbol{n}}\,.

(10)

We focus in this work on the case where
a_{\boldsymbol{n}}
is positive and
decays sufficiently fast for

. Using (

) we may conclude in

a_{\boldsymbol{n}}|b_{\bf n}|^{2}\leq Jg_{m}-{\mathcal{J}}(\mu_{L})\,,

(11)

As
K\to\infty
, for rapidly decaying
\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

obtain a lot of information on the minimizing measure whose Fourier

\{b_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
must also rapidly vanish in that limit.

The above sketch of ideas lies in the basis of the proofs of the

Theorem 1
.

Let
f
denote a 1-periodic function whose Fourier coefficients are

\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{2}\frac{{\mathcal{J}}_{m}-{\mathcal{J}}(\mu_{L})}{a_{N/2}}=0\,,

and that there exists
C_{0}>0
such that for all
k\geq 1

\max_{1\leq m\leq k}\frac{1}{m^{2}a_{m}}\leq\frac{C_{0}}{k^{2}a_{k}}\,.

(12)

Let
{\mathcal{S}}_{N}

denote a minimizing set of (
9
), and let
{\mathcal{T}}_{N}
denote the
equidistant measure

{\mathcal{T}}_{N}=\Big\{\frac{(2k-N-1)}{2N}\Big\}_{k=1}^{N}\,.

(13)

Then, up to arbitrary translation,
{\mathcal{S}}_{N}={\mathcal{T}}_{N}
.

In dimension 2 the expected minimizer is the triangular lattice,
i.e., for
N=2L^{2}
in the rectangle
R=[0,\sqrt{3}]\times[0,1]
it
represented by
\mathop{\bigcup}\limits_{k=1}^{L}\mathop{\bigcup}\limits_{j=1}^{2L}(x_{kj},y_{kj})

(x_{kj},y_{kj})=\begin{cases}\Big(\sqrt{3}\frac{k-1}{L},\frac{j-1}{2L}\Big)&j\text{ odd }\\
\Big({\sqrt{3}\frac{k-\frac{1}{2}}{L}},\frac{j-1}{2L}\Big)&j\text{ even }\end{cases}

(14)

Unfortunately, since estimates similar to (

\{(x_{n},y_{n})\}_{n=1}^{2L^{2}}

\begin{cases}(x_{n+2L^{2}/3},y_{n+2L^{2}/3})=\Big(x_{n}+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2L},y_{n}+\frac{1}{2L}\Big)&n\in\Big[1,\frac{2L^{2}}{3}\Big]\cap\mathbb{Z}\\
(x_{n+4L^{2}/3},y_{n+4L^{2}/3})=\Big(x_{n}+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{L},y_{n}\Big)\end{cases}

(15)

The triangular lattice (
14
) is given in that case by
{\mathcal{T}}_{L}=\mathop{\bigcup}\limits_{k=1}^{2L/3}\mathop{\bigcup}\limits_{j=1}^{L}(x_{kj},y_{kj})
where

x_{kj}=\frac{3(2k-1)}{4L}\quad;\quad y_{kj}=\frac{4j+[1+(-1)^{k}]}{4L}\,,

where each
(x_{kj},y_{kj})
is accompanied by additional pair of
points in the lattice according to (
15
).

We can now state the main result in the two-dimensional case

.

Let
f
denote a 2-periodic function whose Fourier coefficients

(16)

and that there exists
C_{0}>0
and

such that for all

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}|m|\leq\frac{2L}{3}\\
|n|\leq|L|\end{subarray}}a_{mn}\geq C_{0}a_{2L/3,L}\,.

(17)

Let
{\mathcal{S}}_{L}
denote a minimizing set of (

) satisfying
(

be given by (

). Then,

The minimization problem (
9
) has been frequently addressed in
the literature. In
[
4
]
it has been established that for

In dimension 2 consider the functional

\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{C}):=\liminf_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}\cap B_{R}|}\sum_{x,y\in\mathcal{C}\cap B_{R},x\neq y}f(|x-y|)

is a non-empty discrete point configuration of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
of
uniform density and

of the squared distance, i.e. when

with

a smooth completely monotone function on
\mathbb{R}_{+}

i.e. satisfying

for all

for every
integer

. In particular the Gaussian

is a completely monotone function

)).

In
[

then the triangular

. The method used in
[
8
]
include integration of the periodic heat
kernel with respect to time to obtain the kernel of the
periodic inverse Laplacian. We shall refer to
[
8
]
again in
§
3
.

Beyond the above there is a long list of works that addresses
(

,

. The problem is

Proof.

We first note that for any

L_{R}\leq\Big[\delta L+\frac{L}{M_{w}+1}\Big]\Big[\delta L+\frac{2L}{3(M_{z}+1)}\Big]\Big[1+2\sum_{\begin{subarray}m=-M_{z}\\
m\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{M_{z}}\Big|\sum_{k=1}^{2L^{2}/3}z_{k}^{m}\Big|\Big]\Big[1+2\sum_{\begin{subarray}n=-M_{w}\\
n\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{M_{w}}\Big|\sum_{k=1}^{2L^{2}/3}w_{k}^{n}\Big|\Big]\,.

Choosing
M_{z}=2L/3-1
and
M_{w}=L-1
yields, by (
48
)

L_{R}\leq\Big[\delta L+1\Big]^{2}(1+e^{-\lambda L})\,.

For
\delta L<\sqrt{2}-1
we obtain that
L_{R}<2
for sufficiently large

L
proving therefore
that for
k_{1}\neq k_{2}
it holds that

|(x_{k_{1}},y_{k_{1}})-(x_{k_{2}}y_{k_{2}})|\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}-1}{2L}\,.

From the above, we may easily conclude (
61
)
and hence also (
60
) .
∎

We continue as in Section
2
.
For convenience, we use translation to obtain that

\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2L^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{2L^{2}/3}x_{n}=\frac{3}{2L^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{2L^{2}/3}y_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\,,

(62)

and set

x_{kj}^{0}=\frac{3\sqrt{3}(2k-1)}{4L}\quad;\quad y_{kj}^{0}=\frac{4j+[1+(-1)^{k}]}{4L}

(63a)

and

{\mathcal{T}}_{L}=\mathop{\bigcup}\limits_{k=1}^{2L/3}\mathop{\bigcup}\limits_{j=1}^{L}(x_{kj}^{0},y_{kj}^{0})\,.

(63b)

Let
{\mathcal{S}}_{L}=\mathop{\bigcup}\limits_{k=1}^{2L/3}\mathop{\bigcup}\limits_{j=1}^{L}\{(x_{kj},y_{kj})\}
denote the minimizer of
{\mathcal{J}}_{L}
satisfying
(
62
). We now set
{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{L}={\mathcal{S}}_{L}-{\mathcal{T}}_{L}
. Set further

{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{L}=({\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{L}^{x},{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{L}^{y})\,,

(64)

where
{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{L}^{x}\in\mathbb{C}^{2L^{2}/3}
represents the difference in
x
between
{\mathcal{S}}_{L}

and
{\mathcal{T}}_{L}
and
{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{N}^{y}\in\mathbb{C}^{2L^{2}/3}
similarly represents the difference in

.

Let now
{\boldsymbol{V}}_{mn}\in\mathbb{C}^{2L^{2}/3}
be given, for
|m|\geq 1
by

[{\boldsymbol{V}}_{mn}]_{(k-1)L+j}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2\pi im}\frac{\partial b_{mn}}{\partial x_{kj}}({\mathcal{T}}_{L})\,,\;(k,j)\in I_{L}\,.

where

I_{L}=[1,2L/3]\cap\mathbb{Z}\times[1,L]\cap\mathbb{Z}\,.

Hence,

[{\boldsymbol{V}}_{nm}]_{(k-1)L+j}=\frac{3}{2L^{2}}\exp\Big\{i\pi\frac{6mk+4nj+n[1+(-1)^{k}]}{2L}\Big\}\,.

(65)

Note that for
|n|\geq 1

[{\boldsymbol{V}}_{mn}]_{(k-1)L+j}=\frac{1}{2\pi in}\frac{\partial b_{mn}}{\partial y_{kj}}({\mathcal{T}}_{L})\,,\;(k,j)\in I_{L}\,.

We can now state

Lemma 3.4
.

There exists
C>0
, independent of
L
, such that

\sum_{m=1}^{2L/3}\sum_{n=-L+1}^{L}|b_{mn}|^{2}\geq CL^{2}\|{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{L}\|_{2}^{2}\,.

(66)

Proof.

Step 1:
Show that there exists
C>0
such that for
sufficiently large
L
it holds that

\sum_{m=1}^{2L/3}\sum_{n=-L+1}^{L}|\langle{\boldsymbol{V}}_{mn},{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{mn}\rangle|^{2}\geq CL^{2}\|{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{L}\|_{2}^{2}\,,

(67)

where

{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{mn}=m{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{L}^{x}+n{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{L}^{y}\,.

We first note that

{\mathcal{V}}_{L}=\mathop{\bigcup}\limits_{(m,n)\in I_{L}}{\boldsymbol{V}}_{mn}

forms an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{C}^{2L^{2}/3}
.
To prove the above statement we compute

\langle{\boldsymbol{V}}_{mn},{\boldsymbol{V}}_{pq}\rangle=\frac{9}{4L^{4}}\sum_{j=1}^{2L/3}\sum_{l=1}^{L}\exp\Big\{i\pi\frac{6(m-p)j+(n-q)(4l+[1+(-1)^{j}])}{2L}\Big\}=\delta_{mp}\delta_{nq}\,.

(68)

Hence,

{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{L}^{x}=\sum_{(m,n)\in I_{L}}\alpha_{x}^{mn}{\boldsymbol{V}}_{mn}\quad;\quad{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{L}^{y}=\sum_{(m,n)\in I_{L}}\alpha_{y}^{mn}{\boldsymbol{V}}_{mn}\,,

where

\alpha_{x}^{mn}=\langle{\boldsymbol{V}}_{mn},{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{L}^{x}\rangle\quad;\quad\alpha_{y}^{mn}=\langle{\boldsymbol{V}}_{mn},{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{L}^{y}\rangle\,.

Note that since
{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{L}
is real we must have

\alpha_{x}^{mn}=\alpha_{x}^{(2L/3-m),(L-n)}\quad;\quad\alpha_{y}^{mn}=\alpha_{y}^{(2L/3-m),(L-n)}\,.

Hence,

\sum_{(m,n)\in I_{L}}|\langle{\boldsymbol{V}}_{mn},{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{mn}\rangle|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=1}^{2L/3}\sum_{n=1}^{L}\Big[|m\alpha_{x}^{mn}+n\alpha_{y}^{mn}|^{2}+\Big|\Big(\frac{2L}{3}-m\Big)\alpha_{x}^{mn}+(L-n)\alpha_{y}^{mn}\Big|^{2}\Big]

(69a)

Similarly, we obtain that

\sum_{(m,-n+1)\in I_{L}}|\langle{\boldsymbol{V}}_{mn},{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{mn}\rangle|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=1}^{2L/3}\sum_{n=1}^{L}\Big[|m\alpha_{x}^{mn}+(n-L)\alpha_{y}^{mn}|^{2}+\Big|\Big(\frac{2L}{3}-m\Big)\alpha_{x}^{mn}-n\alpha_{y}^{mn}\Big|^{2}\Big]

(69b)

We now write

|m\alpha_{x}^{mn}+n\alpha_{y}^{mn}|^{2}+\Big|\Big(\frac{2L}{3}-m\Big)\alpha_{x}^{mn}+(L-n)\alpha_{y}^{mn}\Big|^{2}+|m\alpha_{x}^{mn}+(n-L)\alpha_{y}^{mn}|^{2}+\\
\Big|\Big(\frac{2L}{3}-m\Big)\alpha_{x}^{mn}-n\alpha_{y}^{mn}\Big|^{2}=a(\alpha_{x}^{mn})^{2}+2b\alpha_{x}^{mn}\alpha_{y}^{mn}+c(\alpha_{y}^{mn})^{2}\,,

(70a)

where

\displaystyle a=4\Big(\frac{L}{3}-m\Big)^{2}+\frac{4L^{2}}{9}

(70b)

\displaystyle b=4\Big(\frac{L}{3}-m\Big)\Big(\frac{L}{2}-n\Big)

(70c)

\displaystyle c=4\Big(\frac{L}{2}-n\Big)^{2}+L^{2}\,.

(70d)

Given that

ac-b^{2}\geq\frac{26}{9}L^{2}\,,

we can now conclude that there exist
C>0
such that

a(\alpha_{x}^{mn})^{2}+2b\alpha_{x}^{mn}\alpha_{y}^{mn}+c(\alpha_{y}^{mn})^{2}\geq CL^{2}\big[(\alpha_{x}^{mn})^{2}+(\alpha_{y}^{mn})^{2}\big]\,.

Combining the above with (
69
) and (
70
) yields
(
67
).

Step 2:
Prove that there exist
\lambda>0
and
L_{0}>0
such that

\sum_{m=1}^{2L/3}\sum_{n=-L+1}^{L}|b_{mn}-2\pi i{\boldsymbol{V}}_{mn}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{mn}|^{2}\leq Ce^{-\lambda L}\|{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{mn}\|_{2}^{2}\,,

(71)

for all

.

Writing the Taylor expansion of
b_{mn}
, for
(m,n)\in I_{L}
, around

{\mathcal{T}}_{L}
gives

b_{mn}=2\pi i{\boldsymbol{V}}_{mn}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{mn}-\frac{3\pi^{2}}{L^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{2L/3}\sum_{j=1}^{L}e^{i2\pi\tilde{z}_{kj}^{mn}}|\delta_{mn}^{kj}|^{2}\,,

where

\tilde{z}_{kj}^{mn}=mx_{kj}^{0}+ny_{kj}^{0}+\theta\big(m[x_{kj}-x_{kj}^{0}]+n[y_{kj}-y_{kj}^{0}]\big)\,,

and

\delta_{mn}^{kj}=[{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{mn}]_{(k-1)L+j}

Consequently,

|b_{nm}-2\pi i{\boldsymbol{V}}_{mn}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{mn}|\leq\frac{3\pi^{2}}{L^{2}}\|\delta_{mn}\|_{2}^{2}

Hence,

\sum_{m=1}^{2L/3}\sum_{n=-L+1}^{L}|b_{nm}-2\pi i{\boldsymbol{V}}_{mn}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_{mn}|^{2}\leq\frac{9\pi^{4}}{L^{4}}\sum_{m=1}^{2L/3}\sum_{n=-L+1}^{L}\|\delta_{mn}\|_{2}^{4}\,.

By (

)we then obtain (

As

For sufficiently large

Proof.

Let

{\mathcal{J}}({\mathcal{S}}_{L})-{\mathcal{J}}({\mathcal{T}}_{L})\geq\sum_{m=1}^{\frac{2L}{3}}\sum_{n=-L+1}^{L}a_{mn}|b_{mn}|^{2}\\
-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}}a_{mL,nL}[1+(-1)^{m+n}][1-|b_{mL,nL}|^{2}]\,.

We first obtain a lower bound for the first term on the

Remark 3.1
.

Let
G:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
denote the kernel of the periodic inverse of
-\Delta

on
R=[0,1]\times[0,1]
. Then (see
[
8
]
for instance)

G({\boldsymbol{x}},0)=\int_{0}^{\infty}S_{t}(\boldsymbol{x},0)\,dt\,,

(75)

where the periodic heat kernel is given by

S_{t}(\boldsymbol{x},0)=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\frac{1}{4\pi t}\exp\Big\{\frac{|x-\xi|^{2}}{t}\Big\}-1\,.

By the Poisson summation formula we have

S_{t}(\boldsymbol{x},0)=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\{0\}}e^{-4\pi^{2}|\omega|^{2}t}e^{2i\pi\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}}.

Thus, stretching one of the coordinates by
\sqrt{3}
, we obtain from
the results of this section that the triangular is the minimizer, with
respect to triplets, for all
t\gg 1/L^{2}
. However, since the integration
with respect
t
in (
75
) starts from
t=0
we cannot yet
conclude that the minimizer of
G
with respect to triplets is the
triangular lattice.

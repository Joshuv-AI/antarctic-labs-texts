Makeenko–Migdal equations for lattice Yang–Mills–Higgs
Hao Shen, Scott Andrew Smith, Rongchan Zhu

Abstract
. We derive a form of master loop equations for the lattice Yang–Mills–Higgs theory with structure group S ​ O ​ ( N ) SO(N) , U ⁡ ( N ) U(N) or S ​ U ​ ( N ) SU(N) . Compared to the pure Yang-Mills setting, several new operations arise. In fact, to obtain a closed recursion we must broaden the class of observables to include open Wilson lines. Our approach is based on the conditional Langevin dynamic and yields a concise proof via Itô’s formula.

1.
Introduction

In this article we derive
the Makeenko–Migdal equations for
the lattice Yang–Mills–Higgs model.
These are also known as the master loop equations or Dyson–Schwinger equations.

The study of Makeenko–Migdal equations for the “pure” Yang–Mills model,
i.e. without coupling to additional fields such as a Higgs field,
goes back to the original paper Makeenko–Migdal in
[
MM1979
]

who heuristically proposed these identities as recursions of Wilson loop observables.
In two dimensions, Kazakov and Kostov
[
kazakov1980non
,
kazakov1981wilson
]

and Gopakumar-Gross
[
gopakumar1995mastering
]
clarified
that
one side
of the Makeenko–Migdal identity may be interpreted as the alternating sum of derivatives of the Wilson loop with respect to the areas.
Lévy
[
Levy11
]
(in 2011) was the first to provide a rigorous proof of the 2D Makeenko–Migdal equations
and also introduced a more general form of the equations, and his proof is
based on Wilson loop expectation formulas developed in the seminal papers
on 2D Yang–Mills by Driver
[
Driver89
]
and Gross–King–Sengupta
[
GKS89
]
.

Still in 2D, after
[
Levy11
]
, alternative derivations were given by Dahlqvist
[
MR3554890
]
and Driver–Hall–Kemp
[
Driver17
]
(with three proofs)
and Driver
[
MR3982691
]
.

[
MR3631396
]
shows that two proofs in
[
Driver17
]
also work over compact surfaces, based
on works by Sengupta
[
Sengupta92
,
Sengupta97
,
Sengupta1997
]

(see
[
Fine91
,
Witten91
,
Witten1992
]
for related works, and
[
Levy03
,
Levy06
,
Levy10
]
for further development and generalizations
of
[
Sengupta97
]
in the geometric settings).
More recently, on the torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
,
Chevyrev
[
Chevyrev19YM
]
constructed a random connection 1-form
whose holonomies along axis paths
coincide in law with the corresponding observables
in
[
Levy03
]
, see also
[
CCHS2d
,
Chevyrev2023
]
which characterize
the Yang–Mills measure on a suitable quotient space of connection 1-forms on
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
as the unique invariant measure of the Yang–Mills dynamic.

In the continuum, rigorous Makeenko–Migdal type equations are unknown when
d\geqslant 3
.
However, on the lattice, the model is more amenable, and
Chatterjee
[
Cha
]
first derived a Makeenko–Migdal equation
which holds in arbitrary dimensions.
This has been reproved, extended, or strengthened in subsequent works

[
Jafar
,
SSZloop
,
OmarRon
,
CPS2023
]
.
More recently,
[
SSZ2d
]
showed that the lattice Makeenko–Migdal equations
converge to their continuum counterparts as in
[
Levy11
]
.

So far, all the above works are concerned with the pure Yang–Mills model.
In this paper we give the first derivation of the
Makeenko–Migdal equations in the context of lattice Yang–Mills coupled with a Higgs field, for not only the loop observables, but also observables associated with “open lines”. Indeed, Wilson loop observables do not form a closed system in the Higgs setting, as some of the operations that arise map Wilson loops into Wilson lines (and vice versa). Hence, to obtain a closed system of equations one must consider both classes of observables simultaneously.

To introduce the model,
let
G
be a compact Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
,
and let
M
be a fixed finite dimensional manifold
on which we have a
G

M

We will focus on the following cases:

(1)
G=SO(N)
, which acts on
M\in\{\mathbb{R}^{N},{\mathbb{S}}^{N-1}\}
by multiplication,
where
{\mathbb{S}}^{N-1}=\{(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}:|x_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|x_{N}|^{2}=1\}
is the unit sphere of dimensions
N-1
in

\mathbb{R}^{N}
endowed with the standard Euclidean inner product.

(2)
G\in\{SU(N),U(N)\}
, which acts on
M\in\{\mathbb{C}^{N},{\mathbb{S}}^{2N-1}\}

where
{\mathbb{S}}^{2N-1}=\{(z_{1},\cdots,z_{N})\in\mathbb{C}:|z_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|z_{N}|^{2}=1\}

is the unit sphere in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
endowed with the Hermitian inner product

(u,v)=u^{*}v=\bar{u}^{t}v\in\mathbb{C}
for
u,v\in\mathbb{C}^{N}

and recall
|u|^{2}=u^{*}u\in\mathbb{R}
.

The matrix groups
G
are endowed with the Hilbert–Schmidt inner product.
For any matrix

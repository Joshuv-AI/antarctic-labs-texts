Bio-inspired multi-dimensional deep fusion learning for predicting dynamical aerospace propulsion systems
Authors: Michael Qian Vergnolle, Eastman Z Y Wu, Yanan Sui, Qian Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractRapid and precise forecasting of dynamical systems is critical to ensuring safe aerospace missions. Previous forecasting research has primarily concentrated on global trend analysis using full-scale inputs. However, time series arising from real-world applications such as aerospace propulsion, exhibit a distinct dynamical periodicity over a limited timeframe. Here we develop a deep learning model, TimeWaves, to capture both global trends and local variations, through 3D spectrum-oriented interval extraction from an integrated viewpoint of biological perceptions. Specifically, a shared parameter fusion algorithm is employed to effectively integrate Fourier and Wavelet analyses, providing full and sliced 1D sequences to form 2D tensors that can be seamlessly processed by parameter-efficient inception blocks. Additionally, a dual-way learning workflow using TwinBlock, inspired by the cooperative behavior of visual cells, is implemented to enhance perception of dynamical multi-scale features at a reduced computational cost. TimeWaves demonstrates reliable and robust performance in predicting rocket combustion instability, a key challenge in the aerospace industry.Subject terms: Aerospace engineering, Computational science, Computer science

Introduction

Machine learning-based predictive analysis has recently been applied in various aerospace engineering domains, such as surface roughness prediction of aircrafts with deep convolutional networks1, airfoil lift-to-drag prediction for optimizing simulation costs of mars helicopter2 and physics estimation-based flight trajectory forecasting for safe and efficient air traffic management3. These successful applications have motivated a growing interest in using machine learning in conjunction with other mathematical, statistical and signal processing tools for accurate and efficient data-driven analysis of time series arising from aerospace systems.

Standard approaches analyze time series by decomposing the series into various components, including trend, cycle, and erratic factors, among others, to handle complex sequences. Additionally, methods such as convolutional neural networks (CNNs), e.g., Wavenet4 and temporal convolutional network (TCN)5, are used to extract features for identifying the underlying patterns and dynamics of the internal factors. However, these analysis methods often lack practical effectiveness due to their limited representation of local characteristics. This is primarily caused by neglecting the variability of factors that contribute to the event’s developments as well as the temporal scale of their impact.

In contrast to the static sequences observed in simulated experiments, real-time engineering data exhibit dynamic properties, including variable-scale dynamics and limited progression phases. These phases consist mainly of formation, propagation, and recession. Examples of such processes, shown in Fig. 1, include orbital activities, staged events, and ocean waves6. The dynamic nature of each of these cycles defines time periods. In addition, real-world time series data are often complex and varied due to the overlapping influence of multiple factors throughout the observation period and the mix of periodic phases, creating additional analytical challenges. Traditional time series analysis methods have primarily focused on the analysis of the entire sequence, neglecting the analysis of temporal evolution in time range dependence features. Therefore, using the entire time series as input generates unwanted learning noise, even with the presence of an attention mechanism. Hence, to improve our understanding of regional multi-periodicity, it is essential to identify range segments of temporal periods and examine them in more detail by forming a direct receptive filter for raw inputs.

Real-life dynamic events showcase and methodology overview.

TimeWaves perceive time series in a unique multi-modal workflow, thinking out of the box, mimicking real-world biological processing mechanisms through space-dim transformation of original dynamic input sequences.

Inspired by the fact that the auditory system, part of the human perception ecosystem, is capable of identifying complex ambient sounds due to a frequency-based like filter bank of the cochlea behind our eardrum7, we used an approach through Wavelet Transform (WT) that includes target scales, to facilitate our goal in analyzing and extracting local period-oriented sequence intervals from general time series. With infinite possibilities as inputs in nature, biological beings required an effective sensory system and thus developed a similar basic perception method that uses priors to analyze the unknown, which helped us process various inputs with reduction to simpler designated outputs. Furthermore, natural beings have evolved specific mechanism variants for different perceptual demands, each embedded with its own inherent logic. For example, the cochlea’s sound filter bank for hearing and the ganglion cell’s receptive field for visual8. These two sensory modalities, which collectively account for 90% of our external perception of surrounding information9, have been incorporated into our deep model. To enhance the fundamental concept of similarity comparison within biological processing systems mentioned above, the combination of a fast advanced signal algorithm, Fast Fourier Transform (FFT), with WT is employed. In concrete terms, we have developed an efficient fusion feature analysis framework that provides a flexible balance between global trends and local periodic attentions.

Based on the above motivations, our latest predictive deep model, TimeWaves, named after the wave-like motion of information in time, targets the implementation of biological multi-modal perception and multi-period extraction, as shown in Fig. 1. Our study is inspired by the auditory systems of living organisms and proposes the use of wavelet mechanisms for progress analysis functions. Specifically, we leverage the multi-view for 3D time-frequency spectrum generated by WT to achieve multi-period slicing and reshape 1D time sequences into a 2D temporal structure for convolutional learning, ultimately resulting in a comprehensive analytical view that is both multi-dimensional and multi-periodic. Furthermore, we utilize the action potential threshold in neurons to discriminate action fields with multiple periodic features, and a designed approximation technique is employed to overcome the limitation of WT frequency-time resolution, which is frequently overlooked yet crucial for accurate analysis. These local period “gene slices”, extracted from complex input series to optimize the signal-to-noise ratio, attempt to mimic the dendritic aspect of a biological neural network’s multi-dimensional perceptual processing form for multi-periodicity learning. Finally, we use a parallel twin approach inspired by the cooperation of cells in visual perception for global trend and local period analysis, respectively, along with well-known convolutional kernel techniques from computer vision for deep feature extraction.

Methods

Related studies

Conventional time series forecasting techniques, such as Holt-Winter10, utilizes predefined patterns of variations, which are not effective for complex real-world series. While long short-term memory (LSTM) networks11 are powerful for sequence modeling, their complexity, difficulty in parallelization, and risk of over-fitting highlight the need of considering alternative structures.

In previous years, machine learning models such as multilayer perceptron (MLP)12–14, TCN15,16, recurrent neural network (RNN)17,18 and convolutional autoencoder (CAE)19–21 have been widely used in time series forecasting. Transformers22 has also shown potential in time series forecasting23–25, with its attention mechanism to model temporal time dependencies. However, for long-term series forecasting, Transformers-based methods are computationally expensive and more importantly unable to capture the global view of time series26. To address this issue, FEDformer26 combines Transformer with the seasonal-trend decomposition method that can capture the global profile of time series. Surprisingly, the DLinear27 model based on simple one-layer linear modules outperforms sophisticated Transformer-based models on several real-life datasets, questioning the effectiveness of Transformers for long-term series forecasting.

Recently, TimesNet28 has been developed as an effective temporal 2D-variation modeling tool for general time series analysis. Specifically, TimesNet extends the analysis of temporal variations of 1D time series into 2D space by transforming the 1D time series into a set of 2D tensors based on multiple periods extracted by using FFT. The intraperiod-variation and interperiod-variation are embedded into the columns and rows the 2D tensors, respectively. Well-acknowledged computer vision backbones can be used in the inception block of TimesNet to model the intraperiod and interperiod variations.

Nevertheless, the aforementioned models lack consideration for local time-action intervals together with multiple periods in real-life dynamic systems, inadvertently resulting in learning noise. To address this issue, we propose a deep fusion model that integrates Fourier and Wavelet analyses, thus being able to learn both global and local patterns in temporal evolution.

TimeWaves model

In this work, a deep learning model, TimeWaves, is proposed for accurate and efficient forecasting of time series arising from dynamic systems. TimeWaves can capture both global and local patterns in temporal evolution, based on a fusion of features learned from full and sliced inputs. Both the full and sliced inputs are reshaped into 2D tensors that can be easily processed by parameter-efficient inception blocks, based on multiple major frequencies computed by using FFT. For each time-frequency, a sliced sequence is obtained by extracting the time interval with Wavelet power spectrum larger than a certain threshold, which contains the corresponding local temporal variations. Details of the model, together with its bio-inspired perception features, are presented in this section.

3D analysis view for interval interception

A primary focus of time series analysis is the examination of the major periodic components and their temporal impact scales. Wavelet transform (WT) can provide a 3D time-frequency-spectrum plot of a 1D time series. This up-scaling enhances the representation of detailed multi-periodic processes, which is greatly useful for frequency-wise interval extraction that can be used to learn local patterns. Based on the unique orthographic projection viewpoint in the 3D spectrum space, for a frequency fi, we offer a local interval output G1Di as “gene slice” from original input X1D ∈ RT×C with length-T and variables-C, as shown in Fig. 2a, which extracts frequency-focused features benefiting deep visual learning in later sessions. The time interval slicing is based on a thresholding to the 3D spectrum map, which is described in the remaining of this subsection.

As shown in Fig. 2b, these period-wise gene slices represent the main temporal activity regions featuring multiple-periodicity and effectively reduce unrelated noise beyond local seasons.

It is noted that gene slices refer to local segments of the input time series that contain information about specific frequency periods. Similar to gene fragments in biological sequences carrying genetic information, slices in time series contain multiple periodic components spread across different time points with varying lengths. Thus, we name them “gene slices” and abbreviate them as G. These local segments represent dynamical variations in their respective time intervals, and will be processed by inception blocks to extract localized features. As presented in previous subsections, in order to capture these local intervals, we utilize a fusion algorithm that combines FFT and WT. We calculate the power fragments by applying wavelet convolutions, which signify the degree of similarity between the time series data and the wavelet’s frequency pattern. Each power fragment corresponds to a specific wavelet frequency. Furthermore, an active interval refers to a portion of the power fragment that exceeds a certain threshold, indicating a local region that aligns ideally with the given frequency. These intervals are typically represented as indices of the time series, rather than the sequence data itself. Using these indices, we can map them back to the time series to extract the corresponding local segments, i.e., the gene slices.

TwinBlock

As shown in Fig. 3a, TimeWaves is comprised of multiple TwinBlocks, each connected in a series configuration via residual linkage of ResNet31. TwinBlock consists of two visual cell-inspired routes that aim to extract and evaluate specific features of time series: global trend and local periodicity. The bedrock analytical framework of both pathways has a key similarity but with variations in sequence input divisions. Similar to biological twins, the variations in detail affect its attention towards certain jobs, thus imaging this architecture model as TwinBlock. As shown in Fig. 3b, the whole procedure consists of three consecutive steps: slicing reshaping, twin-wise analysis, and feature merging from different perception routes.

Slicing reshaping

At first, FFT is performed for each input sequence, efficiently providing k major frequencies that reflects global features. Then, a global frequency set is assigned as an index to the wavelet scaling, serving as a guide for conducting k wavelet scans, resulting in the acquisition of process-wise power spectrum maps Mk. As aforementioned, a dynamic threshold is employed to extract the multi-periodicity time intervals, followed by a refinement of the approximation through an optimization strategy. As a result, period-active sequence fragments are sliced and extracted for Ftopk, respectively, completing our slicing step. Finally, the trend slices X1D, which in this case is the full sequence, and the periodic slices G1D are reshaped into 2D tensors X2D, G2D along with each corresponding frequency fi, where i ∈ {1, ⋯ , k}. In the reshaped 2D tensors, each column contains the time points within a period, and each row contains the time points at the same phase among different periods. Twin-wise analysis

Given our intention to further use computer vision, it is essential to examine the partitioning of biological visual cells. It is noted that the primary visual cortex exhibits a specialized allocation of tasks for distinct perceptual aspects. A great illustration of this is that rods and cones are two distinct categories of photoreceptor cells found inside the retina of the eye. Specifically, rod cells are highly responsive to variations in brightness, forms, and motion, while cones are born for color perception due to three distinct classes, each sensitive to one of the three basic colors: blue, green, and red32. Nature has evolved in such a manner that involves the specialization of twin-wise cells responsible for light sensory perception with efficiency shown by our own visual organs, the eyes. Superiority in fusion transform

Background

Transformations have been widely employed in time series analysis for their ability to extract principal patterns from complex data. Fourier transform34 can efficiently capture global trends in signals but is not suitable to deal with non-stationary data. Conversely, Wavelet transform was developed to handle non-stationary signals through capturing local variations35. However, it demands careful parameter selection due to its dependence on two-dimensional step-wise scanning wavelets. This process can be highly resource-intensive and time-consuming, especially for long sequence inputs that require high-frequency resolution. This illustrates the importance to develop an analysis tool to efficiently capture global trends and local variations for long input sequence processing scenarios. It is noted that modal decomposition methods such as proper orthogonal decomposition (POD)36,37 and dynamic mode decomposition (DMD)38,39, are also able to extract dominant modes of dynamic systems in an equation-free manner. However, POD and DMD are mainly designed for systems with high spatial dimensions40,41, which is not the case for general time series forecasting tasks. Furthermore, as a pure data-driven approach, POD is unable to explicitly define the scales of extracted modes. Therefore, we focus on transformations for time series forecasting in this work.

Fusion method

In response to the problems of their respective shortcomings, we propose to use FFT to specify the dominant frequencies, then refine the sequence’s local details using WT with previously found major frequencies as wavelets’ scaling frequencies, respectively. Finally, we obtain our major frequency-oriented spectrum compositional slices, which contain local period duration indicators for our interval extraction. Originally, the complexity of wavelet transform is typically Olenscale×lendata42, indicating that the computational time is determined by both the data length and the number of scales being analyzed. In the fusion method, we turn traditional continuous stepwise scanning into finite major frequency-oriented steps, as visualized in Fig. 4a. While grasping the overall major key factors, the frequency-oriented time interval extraction also avoids unnecessary distracted calculation outside of the dominant periods, thus reducing the complexity associated with the scale term. Therefore, the fusion method notably improves the efficiency of feature learning for time series.

The time cost is 0.04 s in average for one single online model evaluation, indicating that TimeWaves is efficient enough to handle real-time data streaming and processing in general time series forecasting tasks.

Latent potential

This method has the potential to become an essential computational algorithm for anyone interested in contributing to the democratization of machine learning for time series forecasting, as the training process involves multi-epoch iterations that will constantly compute time-frequency detailed information. By zeroing in on the relevant information, processing time and associated costs may be reduced via the use of the wavelet fusion technique. Therefore, this technique is helpful for individuals in the physical and natural sciences who lack access to mainframe processing capacity to run more extensive data sets. Furthermore, it allows researchers to prototype their study and assess the potential demand for future increases in processing capacity for long sequence analysis.

Real-world forecast validation

Setups

To demonstrate the general performance of our model, we tested it on both short-term and long-term real-world forecasting tasks, including ETT26, Electricity43, Traffic44 and Weather45 in different series lengths. It is noted that we employ the same training/testing environments, performance metrics, and sliding sampling method for long-term series as TimesNet28. The numerical results of TimeWaves are compared with five other advanced models, including TimesNet28, DLinear27, FEDformer26, AutoFormer24 and Informer23, to provide a trustworthy and direct evaluation of our model’s forecasting capacity. Note that in all our experiments we set hyper-parameter k as 5 and complex Gaussian wavelets (cgau) as our wavelet basis, which enables additional phase information for non-stationary series analysis if required.

Scientific forecasting for aerospace combustion engine

Background

Given the ongoing advancements in aerospace technology and the undeniable trend towards future human space exploration of Mars, it is crucial to prioritize safety. The rocket combustor, which serves as the power system for providing thrust, exhibits the highest level of uncertainty in terms of stability and safety. A rocket combustor, especially a liquid propellant rocket combustor, is a high-performance combustion system that produces large rates of energy release in a small volume. Therefore, a small perturbation may cause serious instability. Combustion instability has been a major topic in designing liquid rocket engines. Rocket combustion instability can be characterized by oscillations of pressure probes located on the surface of the chamber. Both experiments and simulations of rocket combustion are expensive, due to the strong nonlinearity of the underlying dynamics that involves flow, acoustics and chemical reactions. The objective of applying TimeWaves to the rocket combustor pressure oscillation forecasting is to predict the long-term behavior of the system based on available experimental or simulation data, thus making the rocket combustion instability analysis much cheaper.

Conclusions

This paper presents a deep learning model TimeWaves for accurate and efficient forecasting of time series arising from dynamical systems such as the rocket engine. Comparative results demonstrate that TimeWaves excels in predicting the future states of nonlinear dynamical systems, particularly in capturing intricate local variations and temporal dynamics. Specifically, our model achieved state-of-the-art performance in 80% of real-world forecasting tasks, including a substantial 14.1% improvement over previous advanced baseline models in the Electricity task. Particularly, TimeWaves presents reliable and robust performance in predicting liquid-propellant rocket combustion instability, which is a key challenge in rocket engine design. TimeWaves’ average ranking in first place in long-term pressure predictions further demonstrates its effectiveness in handling both complex dynamic aerospace systems and long-term forecasting tasks. It is highlighted that the TimeWaves model, trained using only the pressure oscillation history of one monitor point, is able to make accurate long-term predictions at the other two locations, demonstrating the generalization capability of deep fusion learning.

TimeWaves utilizes a shared parameter fusion algorithm to effectively integrate Fourier and Wavelet analyses to capture the global and local patterns in temporal evolution, inspired by various biological perception mechanisms. Full and sliced 1D sequences, containing global trends and local variations respectively, are reshaped into 2D tensors that are further processed by inception blocks. The sliced 1D sequences with optimized lengths are obtained through a spectrum-oriented interval slicing, mimicking the stimulation of biological neurons. The features learned from the full and sliced sequences are integrated by using a TwinBlock, inspired by the cooperative behavior of visual cells. The major time periods extracted by FFT are used to scale the mother wavelets in the Wavelet analysis, resulting in a notable speedup compared with traditional Wavelet transform. Addressing the limitations of conventional unitary transformations, our fusion algorithm offers a potential solution for researchers with limited computational resources, enabling them to prototype process-wise analysis of long sequences more effectively.

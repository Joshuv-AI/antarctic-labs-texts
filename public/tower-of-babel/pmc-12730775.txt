Keratorefractive lenticule extraction (KLEx) versus femtosecond laser-assisted in situ keratomileusis (FS-LASIK) for the treatment of myopia and compound myopic astigmatism: study protocol of a randomised clinical trial in Mexico
Authors: Mauricio Muleiro-Alvarez, Guillermo Raul Vera-Duarte, Eber A. Medina-Estrada, Gustavo Ortiz-Morales, Arturo Ramirez-Miranda, Alejandro Navas, Enrique L. Graue-Wiechers, Enrique O. Graue-Hernandez, Nicolás Kahuam-López
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionKerato-lenticule extraction (KLEx) is a refractive surgery technique that, in contrast with femtosecond laser-assisted in situ keratomileusis (FS-LASIK), does not require the creation of a flap to correct refractive defects. The potential advantages of this technique are related to the absence of a flap and its complications. On the other hand, FS-LASIK is the most widely practised refractive surgery worldwide, as it offers excellent visual outcomes and is currently the gold standard of refractive surgery. The objective of this study is to compare the effectiveness and safety of KLEx versus FS-LASIK as a treatment option in patients with myopia or myopic astigmatism.Methods and analysisThis double-masked, parallel-group, single-centre randomised clinical trial will enrol 80 eyes from adults with myopia or compound myopic astigmatism within the ranges sphere −0.50 to −12.00 D and cylinder −0.50 to −6.00 D, recruited at the Instituto de Oftalmología Conde de Valenciana, Mexico City, Mexico. Participants will be allocated to KLEx or FS-LASIK and assessed at baseline and 1 day, 1 week, 1, 3, 6 and 12 months postoperatively. The primary outcome is uncorrected visual acuity at all postoperative visits. Secondary outcomes include postoperative spherical equivalent, best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA), loss of ≥2 BCVA lines, the proportion of eyes within ±0.50 D of the refractive target, corneal aberrations over a 5 mm pupil, epithelial changes and adverse events. Participants and outcome assessors will be masked to the assigned surgical technique.Ethics and disseminationParticipant confidentiality will be maintained with the publication of results. This study was approved by the research and ethics committee of the Instituto de Oftalmología Fundación de Asistencia Privada Conde Valenciana (CI-017-2024). The study results will be disseminated in scientific articles published in peer-reviewed journals and presented through research posters at national and international conferences.Trial registration numberClinicalTrials.gov registry (NCT06477081).

Introduction

Background and rationale {9a, 9b}

Refractive errors have significantly increased worldwide in recent decades, constituting a major public health issue due to various factors. In the paediatric population, these errors lead to educational setbacks, while in adults, they result in missed work opportunities and economic losses, thereby affecting global quality of life.1 2 By 2050, it is projected that nearly 5 billion people will have myopia, with 900 million suffering from severe myopia.2 Moreover, myopia confers increased risks of glaucoma, posterior subcapsular cataract, retinal detachment and myopic maculopathy—even within the low-to-moderate range.3 Therefore, early diagnosis, timely treatment and the evaluation of new therapies are essential to reduce visual disability in affected individuals.

In the past decade, the US Food and Drug Administration approved the keratorefractive lenticule extraction (KLEx),4 previously known as small incision lenticule extraction (SMILE), as a new surgical technique for myopia and later for myopic astigmatism.5 KLEx is a refractive technique where the creation of a flap is not needed, which could provide significant benefits, shorter surgical time and potentially result in fewer adverse effects compared with the gold standard of refractive surgery, femtosecond laser-assisted in situ keratomileusis (FS-LASIK).6 FS-LASIK is a technique that provides excellent visual results, high patient satisfaction and rapid recovery. Importantly, it has well-established efficacy and safety, with stable long-term refraction.7 8 However, it is related to flap complications.

Despite multiple systematic reviews and meta-analyses comparing SMILE and FS-LASIK,912 randomised controlled trials with newer KLEx platforms remain limited. The Schwind SmartSight system on the ATOS femtosecond laser incorporates cyclotorsion control and pupil recognition with eye tracking and can apply MS-39 anterior-segment OCT, guided cyclotorsion compensation (CSO), features designed to improve the alignment and accuracy of astigmatic correction.13

In this double-masked, parallel-group, single-centre, non-inferiority randomised clinical trial, we will compare KLEx (SmartSight on the ATOS femtosecond laser; Schwind eye-tech-solutions GmbH, Kleinostheim, Germany) with FS-LASIK (Amaris excimer; Schwind eye-tech-solutions GmbH, Kleinostheim, Germany) over 12 months in adults with myopia or compound myopic astigmatism.

Objectives {10}

This randomised, parallel-group trial will evaluate a comparison of KLEx versus FS-LASIK in adult participants with myopia or compound myopic astigmatism eligible for corneal refractive surgery at the Instituto de Oftalmología ‘Conde de Valenciana’, Mexico City, Mexico. Participants will be allocated to KLEx (intervention) or FS-LASIK (comparator). The primary outcome for effectiveness is uncorrected visual acuity (UCVA) at every postoperative follow-up. Safety objectives include comparing the proportion of eyes losing≥2 lines of best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) and the incidence of intraoperative and postoperative adverse events through 12 months (figure 1). Secondary outcome measures include postoperative spherical equivalent, the proportion of eyes within ±0.50 D of the refractive target, BCVA, corneal aberrations over a 5 mm pupil and epithelial changes across follow-up at 1 day, 1 week, 1, 3, 6 and 12 months.

Methods and analysis

Patient and public involvement {11}

Patients and members of the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of this protocol.

Trial design and setting {12, 13}

This study is a randomised, parallel-group, assessor-masked, single-centre, single-eye controlled non-inferiority clinical trial with an allocation ratio of 1:1. The unit of randomisation is the individual participant (one eye per participant, with the study eye selected by randomisation), and participants are assigned to undergo KLEx or FS-LASIK.

The trial will be conducted at a single tertiary ophthalmology centre—the department of cornea and refractive surgery, Instituto de Oftalmología Conde de Valenciana, Mexico City, Mexico. Participants are recruited from outpatient refractive surgery clinics and complete all study procedures and follow-up at this site.

The study protocol adheres to the Declaration of Helsinki; it is registered at the same institution (CI-017-2024) and at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06477081) and follows the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials reporting guidelines.14

Eligibility criteria {14a}

Adults aged≥21 years with myopia or compound myopic astigmatism will be screened at the Instituto de Oftalmología Conde de Valenciana, Mexico City, Mexico. Inclusion criteria are: normal corneal tomography in both eyes; refractive sphere from −0.50 D to −12.00 D; cylindrical refraction from −0.50 D to −6.00 D; BCVA 20/20 or better; and calculated residual stromal thickness ≥300 µm or percentage tissue altered <40%. Exclusion criteria are: prior ocular surgery; ocular surface disease or other ocular pathology; pregnancy; progressive or unstable myopia and/or myopic astigmatism, defined as a refractive change ≥0.50 D in spherical equivalent or cylinder (table 1). Written informed consent will be obtained before enrolment by the study coordinator (MMA). An example of the participant consent form is provided in online supplemental file 1.

The trial will be conducted at a single tertiary ophthalmology centre—the department of cornea and refractive surgery, Instituto de Oftalmología ‘Conde de Valenciana’, Mexico City, Mexico, equipped with established preoperative assessment and postoperative follow-up pathways for corneal refractive surgery. All surgeries will be performed by a single attending cornea and refractive surgeon (NK-L), credentialed at the institution and trained to perform both KLEx and FS-LASIK according to the protocol.

Intervention and comparator {15a, 15b, 15c, 15d}

Participants will be randomised to KLEx or FS-LASIK, with all surgeries performed by a cornea and refractive surgery fellowship-trained consultant ophthalmologist (NK-L). Parameters for both arms were optimised in collaboration with a Schwind eye-tech-solutions specialist (LACC). For KLEx, procedures will be performed on the ATOS (Schwind eye-tech-solutions, Kleinostheim, Germany) femtosecond platform with MS-39-guided CSO (Florence, Italy) using the standard KLEx technique: cap thickness 120 µm; optical zone 6.5 mm; cap diameter 1 mm larger than the total zone; side-cut angle 135°; incision at 135° (length 3 mm); edge cut 95 nJ (spot 4.6 µm; track 1.1 µm); anterior and posterior cuts 95 nJ (spot 4.4 µm; track 3.1 µm). For FS-LASIK, a flap will be created on ATOS (Schwind eye-tech-solutions, Kleinostheim, Germany) femtosecond platform (thickness 120 µm; diameter 9 mm; superior hinge 3 mm at 90°); flap edge 105 nJ (spot 4.7 µm; track 1.2 µm); bed cut 120 nJ (spot 4.8 µm; track 3.4 µm), followed by photoablation on the Amaris excimer (Schwind eye-tech-solutions, Kleinostheim, Germany) platform with cyclotorsion control via MS-39 (CSO, Florence, Italy). Briefly for KLEx, after topical anaesthesia, the patient will be prepped and draped, and a lid speculum inserted. The eye will be centred and aligned to the curved patient interface; suction will be applied, and the femtosecond laser will create the refractive lenticule and a small incision. Suction will then be released. The incision will be opened and the lenticule planes dissected—anterior first, then posterior—using a lenticule separator. The refractive lenticule will be extracted through the small incision with the dissector or a 0.12 mm forceps. The contralateral eye will undergo the same procedure in the same session. At the conclusion, protective shells will be placed on both eyes, and postoperative medications will be instilled per protocol.

For FS-LASIK, the standard FS-LASIK technique will be performed: briefly, after topical anaesthesia, the patient will be prepped and draped, and a lid speculum inserted. The eye will be centred and aligned to the curved femtosecond patient interface; suction will be applied and a lamellar corneal flap created. The flap will be dissected and reflected, and excimer laser photoablation will be performed on the stromal bed with cyclotorsion control per protocol. The interface will be irrigated as needed, and the flap carefully repositioned, smoothed and checked for alignment and striae. The contralateral eye will undergo the same procedure in the same session. At the conclusion, protective shields will be placed on both eyes, and postoperative medications will be instilled per protocol.

The same parameter sets will be applied to all eligible participants. At the end of surgery in both groups, moxifloxacin 0.5% (Vigamoxi, Novartis, Basel, Switzerland) and prednisolone acetate 1% (Sophipren Ofteno, Sophia, Jalisco, Mexico) will be instilled; patients will instil moxifloxacin and prednisolone every 4 hours for 14 days and artificial tears (Systane Hidratación SP, Alcon, Texas, USA) every 2 hours for 1 month.

Participants’ requests will constitute sufficient reason for discontinuing participation; this will not affect the medical care provided by the department of cornea and refractive surgery or any personnel of the Instituto de Oftalmología ‘Conde de Valenciana’. The date and reason for discontinuation will be documented in the case-report forms. Unless the participant withdraws consent for data use, outcome data collected up to discontinuation will be retained; where clinically appropriate, participants may be invited for safety follow-up only. If intraoperative modification or conversion is required, participants will remain analysed in the originally assigned group, and any deviations will be recorded.

Participant adherence will be supported and monitored via return of used postoperative eye-drop bottles and proactive reminders for scheduled visits; attendance and missed/late visits will be recorded in the case-report forms, and protocol deviations will be documented. All procedures will be performed by a single surgeon using fixed, prespecified parameters; any intraoperative deviations from the specified settings will be recorded.

Outcomes {16}

The primary outcome will be UCVA at every postoperative follow-up. The UCVA will be converted from Snellen to logMAR. Secondary outcomes will be postoperative spherical equivalent, refractive astigmatism, BCVA, loss of 2 or more lines of BCVA, proportion of eyes with 0.5 D within the refractive target, corneal aberrations over a 5 mm pupil, epithelial changes, Ocular Surface Disease Index (OSDI) Score for the Mexican population,15 surgical time and adverse events. We will use the nine standard graphs for reporting refractive surgery outcomes.16

Data collection methods {25a, 25b}

Participants will be evaluated preoperatively and at 1 day, 1 week, 1, 3, 6 and 12 months postoperatively. At each visit, outcomes will be assessed and recorded in the clinic by trained ophthalmologists (GRV-D, EAM-E, GO-M) masked to treatment allocation, and subsequently entered into an encrypted database on a Microsoft Excel file (Microsoft Corporation, Seattle, Washington, USA) in the department of corneal and refractive surgery computer, which is exclusive to the database. The database will be encrypted for the enhanced security of research subjects and operated by one researcher (MMA). UCVA and BCVA will be measured using a Snellen chart and converted to logMAR; manifest refraction will be recorded at the same sitting. Corneal higher-order aberrations (HOAs) and epithelial mapping will be obtained with MS-39 (CSO, Florence, Italy). Epithelial remodelling will be assessed with the MS-39 anterior-segment OCT (CSO, Florence, Italy). Epithelial thickness (µm) will be measured in five sectors—central, nasal, superior, inferior and temporal—and reported as mean±SD. Corneal HOAs will be reported as root mean square (RMS) (µm) from Zernike coefficients over a 5 mm pupil, including RMS of HOAs, spherical aberration and coma using the MS-39 anterior-segment OCT (CSO, Florence, Italy).

Dry-eye assessment will include the Keratograph 5M (OCULUS, Wetzlar, Germany) and the validated OSDI Questionnaire. Adverse events will be documented by qualified personnel, including slit-lamp photography when indicated. To promote data quality, device calibration follows manufacturer guidance.

To minimise missing data, participants will receive reminder calls before each scheduled visit, and any missed appointments will be proactively rescheduled by phone. Attendance, missed/late visits and any protocol deviations will be recorded in the study database.

Harms {17}

The participants will be instructed to contact the study coordinator (MMA) in case of an adverse event. They will be evaluated by qualified personnel, and photos will be captured using a camera (Haag-Streit IM 610) attached to a slit lamp, using magnifications of ×10, ×16 and ×25. Adverse events will be recorded in the database at any follow-up time during the study. Also, the adverse events will be reported to the research and ethics committee of the Instituto de Oftalmología Fundación de Asistencia Privada Conde Valenciana.

Sample size {19}

The sample size calculation was based on the continuous outcome postoperative spherical equivalent (SE, D) at 12 months. Using departmental data, we assumed a mean SE of 0.60±0.50 D for the KLEx group and 1.00 D for the FS-LASIK group, implying a target between-group difference of 0.40 D. With a two-sided α=0.05 (type I error) and β=0.10 (power 90%), a two-sample comparison of means requires 33 participants per group. To accommodate an anticipated 20% loss to follow-up, the sample size was increased to 40 participants per group (total n=80). Calculations were performed using the ClinCalc sample size calculator (http://clincalc.com/).17

Recruitment {20}

Participants will be recruited consecutively from the outpatient clinics of the department of cornea and refractive surgery at the Instituto de Oftalmología Conde de Valenciana. Patients older than 21 years attending the clinic who meet preliminary criteria will be invited to participate, provided with study information, and asked to give written informed consent; those who consent will undergo a comprehensive ophthalmic evaluation to confirm eligibility. Enrolment is planned for July–October 2024, with randomisation in November 2024 (day 0). Recruitment and enrolment will be coordinated by the study coordinator (MMA), and surgeries will be performed by a single surgeon (NK-L).

Randomisation: sequence generation, allocation concealment mechanism and implementation {21a, 21b, 22, 23}

The allocation sequence will be computer-generated in a fixed 1:1 ratio (KLEx:FS-LASIK) by the study coordinator (MMA) using restricted (blocked) randomisation with randomly permuted block sizes of 4 and 6; the order and mix of block sizes will be concealed in a separate file unavailable to enrolment or assessment staff. MMA will enrol participants and manage randomisation, including preparing sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes from the concealed list, which are stored in a locked cabinet with a usage log to ensure strict sequential order and tamper-evident control. Only MMA will have access to the full randomisation list (stored on a password-protected drive with restricted permissions) and is not involved in outcome assessments. In addition—and before surgery—a second, concealed eye-selection randomisation (left/right, 1:1) generated by MMA designates the study eye for the outcomes analyses; this eye-selection sequence is implemented via sealed envelopes under the same safeguards. All procedures will be performed by a single surgeon (NK-L), and any deviations from envelope procedures or access to allocation are documented in the trial files.

Blinding {24a}

Trial participants and follow-up outcome assessors are masked to treatment assignment, while the operating surgeon learns the allocation only immediately before surgery. Blinding is implemented with sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes opened in the operating room; surgical notes identifying the technique are kept separate from clinic files, and postoperative regimens and visit schedules are identical across arms to minimise cues. We recognise that slit-lamp biomicroscopy can inadvertently reveal allocation, and to mitigate this, masked assessments are split between two trained ophthalmologists (GRV-D, EAM-E, GO-M): one masked assessor obtains UCVA, BCVA and manifest refraction only, while a second masked ophthalmologist performs the slit-lamp/intraocular pressure (IOP)/fundus examination and adverse-event checks; neither assessor has access to allocation or operative records, and any suspected unmasking is documented. Unblinding is permitted solely when required for urgent clinical management, authorised by the principal investigator, with the reason, timing and personnel recorded in the trial files.

Data management {26}

The statistical tool GraphPad Prism V.10.1.1 (270) for the database will be used, considering a p value of <0.05 as statistically significant. The database will be saved on a Microsoft Excel file (Microsoft Corporation, Seattle, Washington, USA) in the department of corneal and refractive surgery computer, which is exclusive to the database. The database will be encrypted for the enhanced security of research subjects and operated by one researcher (MMA).

Statistical methods {27a, 27b, 27c, 27d}

For demographic variables, a descriptive statistical analysis will be conducted. Data distribution will be assessed using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test to compare the cumulative distribution. For data with a parametric distribution, the Student’s t-test will be used; if the data have a non-parametric distribution, the Mann-Whitney U test will be used. An intention-to-treat analysis will be performed.

For the database, the statistical tool GraphPad Prism V.10.1.1 (270) will be used, considering a p value of <0.05 as statistically significant. In addition, outcomes will be presented using the nine standard graphs for reporting refractive surgery, covering the four domains of stability, efficacy, safety and accuracy.16

Data monitoring committee {28a, 28b}

There is no data monitoring committee.

An interim analysis conducted at 3 months by the study coordinator (MMA) must be presented to the research committee and the ethics committee to determine whether to continue with the protocol or not.

Trial monitoring {29}

The research committee, the ethics committee and the biosafety committee of the Instituto de Oftalmologia Conde de Valenciana will have recurrent meetings with the study coordinator to review the protocol. Additionally, an interim analysis conducted at 3 months must be presented to them to determine whether to continue with the protocol or not.

This trial has been approved by the research committee of the Instituto de Oftalmología Fundación de Asistencia Privada Conde Valenciana (CI-017-2024). For verification purposes, correspondence with the committee may be directed via email (comites@condeinvestigacion.org).

Dissemination policy {8}

The study results will be disseminated in scientific articles published in peer-reviewed journals and presented through research posters at national and international conferences. Also, participants will receive a plain-language summary of aggregate findings.

Protocol amendments {31}

If needed, amendments to the ethics committee will be submitted.

Consent or assent {32a and 32b}

Written informed consent will be obtained face-to-face before any trial-specific procedures by the study coordinator (MMA) at the department of cornea and refractive surgery, Instituto de Oftalmología ‘Conde de Valenciana’. Potential participants (adults aged≥21 years) will receive information about the study objectives, procedures, risks and benefits, alternatives, confidentiality and the voluntary nature of participation; ample time will be provided for questions before signing the consent form.

This trial enrols adults only; therefore, paediatric assent and proxy consent for individuals lacking decisional capacity are not applicable. No biological specimens are collected, and no ancillary specimen studies are planned; consequently, no additional consent provisions for ancillary use of data or specimens are required.

Confidentiality {33}

Personal information will be collected by the cornea staff and inserted into the medical records, and then it will be extracted by the researcher in charge of an encrypted Microsoft Excel file (Microsoft Corporation, Seattle, Washington, USA). This file will be stored on a computer designated for this protocol and will only be used by the researcher in charge of the database (MMA).

Ancillary and post-trial care {34}

At the conclusion of the trial, participants will continue routine care and follow-up at the department of cornea and refractive surgery at the Instituto de Oftalmologia Conde de Valenciana.

Trial registration {4}

The trial is registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (identifier NCT06477081; registered on 25 June 2024). Registry record is available at ClinicalTrials.gov (https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06477081).

Protocol and statistical analysis plan {5}

There are no plans to grant public access to participant-level data and statistical code.

Interim analyses

An interim analysis will be conducted at 3 months by MMA. In this analysis, the rate of adverse effects in each group will be evaluated, and if a group presents a significantly higher rate that jeopardises the patient’s vision, a decision will be made to stop the protocol.

Methods for additional analyses (eg, subgroup analyses)

Subgroup analysis will be conducted for astigmatism greater than 2D and less than 2D.

Data sharing {6}

Generated datasets from this trial will be available on reasonable request by contacting the corresponding author (NK-L). There are no plans to provide public access to participant-level data or to share the statistical code via a public repository; access, when granted, will be provided directly by the corresponding author in line with institutional policies and ethics approvals.

Discussion

This trial compares KLEx (SmartSight Schwind eye-tech-solutions, GmbH, Kleinostheim, Germany) with FS-LASIK for myopia or myopic compound astigmatism patients. As the gold standard, FS-LASIK has excellent refractive outcomes, and KLEx is not expected to surpass these results in terms of efficacy. However, KLEx may offer advantages in other areas, such as a lower incidence of flap-related complications and a reduction in dry eye syndrome, likely due to its flapless nature, which effectively preserves corneal innervation.18

An important aspect to consider in this comparison is the impact on HOAs, which can significantly affect postoperative visual quality. While highly effective, FS-LASIK has been associated with the induction of certain aberrations, such as spherical aberrations, due to the creation of the corneal flap and subsequent stromal ablation.1921 KLEx, by eliminating the need for a flap, may induce fewer HOAs, potentially leading to superior visual outcomes, particularly in low-light conditions where these aberrations are most noticeable.

Additionally, controlling cyclotorsion during surgery is critical, especially in patients with high astigmatism. The MS-39 device, which integrates anterior segment tomography with Placido disc corneal topography, allows for precise measurement and management of cyclotorsion.22 23 This enhanced accuracy is expected to benefit both FS-LASIK and KLEx procedures, but it may be particularly advantageous in KLEx, where precise centration and alignment are crucial for optimal outcomes. Another element in KLEx is that only one laser is needed for the surgery. This might result in a shorter surgical time. In contrast, FS-LASIK requires two types of lasers, which might increase the surgical time.

Previous randomised clinical trials comparing FS-LASIK and SMILE (now known as KLEx) have generally shown that while both techniques yield excellent refractive outcomes, SMILE may have an edge in reducing the incidence of dry eye syndrome and preserving corneal biomechanical strength due to the absence of a flap.18 However, challenges have been noted with KLEx in correcting moderate and high levels of astigmatism.24 FS-LASIK has demonstrated more predictable and precise results, potentially due to better control of torsional movements during the procedure.25 26

In this context, our study aims to further explore these aspects, mainly focusing on comparing aberration profiles and the efficacy of cyclotorsion control with the MS-39 in both techniques. We anticipate that while KLEx may not exceed FS-LASIK in refractive accuracy, it could offer significant benefits in terms of reduced adverse effects, shorter surgical time and better visual quality due to lower HOA induction.27 One limitation of this study is the absence of patient-reported outcome measures specifically addressing visual quality (eg, the Quality of Vision Questionnaire or the National Eye Institute Refractive Error Quality of Life-42 (NEI-RQL-42)). To minimise participant burden, they were omitted to reduce respondent burden and visit time. As a partial proxy for patient experience, the OSDI—using the Spanish version validated for our setting15—will be administered at every visit.

In conclusion, this clinical trial aims to evaluate whether the KLEx (SmartSight Schwind eye-tech-solutions GmbH, Kleinostheim, Germany) has comparable refractive and visual outcomes to the gold standard of refractive surgeries, the FS-LASIK technique. The results of this trial will help to determine if the newer procedure could potentially offer better clinical and surgical outcomes for patients, particularly in areas such as visual quality, surgical efficiency and patient comfort.

Path informed adaptive trend analyzer using Hilbert Huang transform for electric vehicle driving range prediction
Authors: Belqasem Aljafari, Thanikanti Sudhakar Babu, Shitharth Selvarajan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The adequate forecast of the electric vehicle (EV) range is the key to the success of the improved energy management and the improved battery utilization, not to mention ensuring the safe operation of this product in the actual conditions of driving in the real life. Despite the significant advancements, the existing methods tend to be unable to possess the nonlinear, dynamic, and non-stationary characteristics of EV operation and driving data, leading to a low prediction accuracy and applicability to a large range of vehicle classes and driving scenarios. We propose a novel hybrid deep learning architecture that will address all these challenges in this paper, the combination of high-level feature extraction, adaptive sequence modeling and attention weight optimization. The framework consists of three basic components, i.e., Hilbert-Huang Auto Recurrence Transform (HART) of rich temporal and recurrent features extractions, Path-Informed Adaptive Inverted Trend Hub Range Analyser (PAITHRA) of a combination of predictive model using pathformer and adaptive routing of features, and Chaotic Algae Sparrow Hyper-tuner (CASH) of a dynamic attention weight optimizer. An extensive testing of the EV Energy Consumption Dataset and the Full Electric Vehicle Dataset 2024 containing different driving styles, battery condition and weather conditions is conducted. The Proposed model is characterized by a Prediction Error (2.5), MAE (0.8 kW), RMSE (1.3 kW), Validation Accuracy (99), R 2 Score (0.991), and it required 35 min totally for all epochs up to convergence and the inference time was just 8 ms per sample, being superior to the conventional deep learning models.

Introduction

Electric Vehicles (EVs) occupy an intermediate phase in the transition to sustainable transportation1,2, they offer an initial move towards the direct path of reducing tailpipe CO 2 and local air pollution and decoupling personal mobility and fossil-fuel burning. However, range, or the miles a vehicle may travel on a single charge is a key user issue and also a realistic adoption bottleneck regardless of battery chemistry and vehicle efficiency. The range anxiety is not only a result of emotive response but a fact of variance in the day to day driving practices, and the uneven distribution of the charging facilities, and the uneven distribution of the risk of being stranded as compared to working with the traditional car. These concerns are all exacerbated in terms of commercial or fleet application (buses, trucks, LDVs) where the underestimation of range is highly costly because of the predictable schedules and payload requirements. When it comes to the market representation, the consumers do not evaluate EVs only by the claimed range by the manufacturers but the real range they will have under their conditions and where the expectations are lower than the truth weakened the confidence to EVs and adoption intentions3. As such, the significance of accurate, personal, and context-specific range estimation cannot be overestimated: they promote the perception of risk, make the charging behavior more efficient, reduce the number of unnecessary charging cycles (extending the battery life and reducing the lifecycle emissions), and simplify the planning of the operations of the fleet and the public transport, which supports the ecological and economic case in support of electrified transport.

The reliability of EV range cannot be predicted technically because the range of vehicles is the emergent property of a complex of interacting, time-varying factors of which the full knowledge is only partially known4. The battery health and state of charge (capacity fade, internal resistance) are examples of instantaneous determinants; the instantaneous and average power demand (vehicle mass, aerodynamic drag, rolling resistance, accessory draw) and the vehicle-driving speed and acceleration profile are also instances of instantaneous determinants; and the route stop-start character is also an instance of an instantaneous determinant. Environmental conditions, such as ambient temperature, road grade and the condition of the road surface can vary considerably the amount of energy consumed5, low ambient temperatures reduce the battery capacity and increases heating in the cabin, hilly roads make larger fractions of its energy consumption be spent in climbing and strong head winds in the air result in increased aerodynamic losses. Operational and human factors further add on the variability i.e. payload, tire pressure, choice of eco and sport driving mode and driver behavior (smooth and aggressive acceleration) etc.

The sources of noise in inputs to range estimation also include the error of vehicle sensors, GPS errors as well as inaccuracies of either map elevation or traffic congestion prediction. Modelling wise, they recommend that a pure physics based model (which involves detailed vehicle and battery models and detailed route data) will be robust but brittle when some inputs are unavailable or inaccurate, and a pure data based model can be capable of learning complex patterns on historical telematics data, but can be an under generalized predictor of conditions or vehicles it has not seen unless trained on huge representative datasets6,7. The other dimension that complicates the matter somewhat is the dimension time: the ageing of the battery gradually reduces capacity and varies charge/discharge behavior that is, whatever reliable estimator must do is to develop with the changing performance of the same vehicle.

Hybrid deep learning predictive driving range of EV: A hybrid deep learning predictive driving range of EV may be a strengthened method to integrate the strength of physics and information-driven learning to give correct, powerful, and personal predictions of driving range in actual uncertainty condition. Such models receive nonhomogeneous inputs such as onboard telemetry (current, voltage, temperature in real time), GPS traces, trip history, ambient weather, route elevation profiles, and traffic predictions and provide output in probabilistic form of range expectations or range uncertainty instead of the deterministic values; the quantification of the uncertainty is needed to convey confidence to the human operator, and to make risk-aware decision rules (e.g. recommend the driver to pull in at the next charging station with an x-percent margin, based on the estimated lower-quantile range). Its advantages in operation are great: range prediction is more precise, it does not need any unnecessary top-up charging (use of grid and battery resources), it offers better route planning and charging stations usage, it does not prevent commercial operators to dispatch their vehicles in a smarter manner and makes users more comfortable with their vehicles by matching the expected with the measured range8. Combined altogether, these advances accelerate EV adoption and maximize the utility of existing battery technology and contribute to the amplified sustainability advantages of electrified transportation by minimizing energy waste and enhancing the battery life cycle - a direct factor in the reduction of lifecycle emissions and reliance on fossil fuels.

EVs will aid in the reduction of the greenhouse gasses, air pollution as well as dependence on scarce fossil fuel reserves. Despite these benefits, among the issues that make EVs not be used on a larger scale, there is the adequate estimation of the driving range of EVs - the distance that one charge should cover9,10. The range ambiguity amongst the users that is caused by variability in range due to environmental factors, driving habits, battery performance, and congestion load states is generally referred to as range anxiety. This uncertainty affects the trip planning, utilization of charging stations and the trustworthiness of the users on the reliability of EVs. The traditional methods of range estimation can be deemed as insufficient in the real-life scenario, e.g. rule-based or physics-based models due to their simplistic assumptions and lack of nonlinear and dynamical properties of EV energy consumption. This causes an urgent need to possess a clever and dynamic model that may obtain advanced relationships amid heterogeneous variables influencing EV range.

The issue of the range anxiety is among the most critical concerns of the process of the electric vehicle (EV) adoption since the driver is not certain of how far they should go before the battery requires recharging. Compared to the typical internal combustion engine automobiles, EVs work exclusively with stored electrical energy, which is decided by a number of dynamic factors including driving habits, traffic, road, weather conditions, and the state of the battery. The inaccurate calculation of the available range can result in the sudden stops, the absence of confidence in the drivers and inefficient route planning which restricts the popularization of EVs to a significant extent. Various approaches have been proposed to predict EV energy usage and range to go, and they all involve the use of traditional machine learning, deep learning, and hybrid. The moderate traditional approaches include linear regression, support vector regression and tree-based ensemble approaches (e.g., XGBoost, CatBoost, LightGBM), which are not able to capture the time-specific patterns and non-linearity in relationships among driving data. Normal deep learning, including recurring neural networks (RNNs), LSTMs, and GRUs, do not scale to prediction well since they are built to learn sequential behavior, however, have large training times, vanishing gradients, and bad generalization to new types of vehicles or driving situations. Hybrid models which combine feature engineering with deep sequence modeling have promising results, although most of them use hand-written features, or hard-coded attention mechanisms, limiting their adaptation to new battery conditions, environment and driving behavior.

The development of a stable, versatile, and smart range estimation system can be the critical becoming force of the proposed hybrid deep learning model of prediction of the driving range of EVs overcoming the drawbacks of the usual and the stand-alone deep learning methods. Since the integration of connected vehicle systems11,12, IoT-based telemetry, and smart transportation systems increase, the amount of data regarding the activities of EVs to be analyzed is vast. It is not only that the chance to utilize this data through the collaboration of hybrid deep learning will result in an improved quality of prediction but will also produce a higher degree of driver confidence, the aid of optimal route and charging policy and the efficient use of energy resources. The proposed work is grounded on a highly innovative and hybrid driving range prediction model of electric vehicles (EV) vehicles, addressing the drawback of the existing models that have a tendency of disregarding dynamic, non-linear and non- stationary character of the EV telemetry data. The combination of three higher components, namely HART, PAITHRA, and CASH is a smooth combination that depicts the originality of the study. The main worth of the work is the creation of the end-to-end pipeline that transforms the raw EV telemetry into the correct driving range predictions which serves as the significant contribution to the traditional strategies in quality of predictions, flexibility and human understandability.

The new hybrid architecture need is further validated by the increasing complexity and diversity of the EV driving datasets, which now include a variety of different sensors, battery types, car models, and weather and traffic as outside factors. The current methods often do not handle these variations and thus their application is restricted, leaving the range predictions very poor, especially in high-stake situations such as city traffic or long-distance travel. The framework proposed in this paper consists of a Hilbert-Huang Auto Recurrence Transform (HART) for thorough extraction of temporal features, a Path-Informed Adaptive Inverted Trend Hub Range Analyzer (PAITHRA) for predictive sequence modeling with path-aware attention, and a Chaotic Algae Sparrow Hyper-tuner (CASH) for adaptive optimization of attention weights addressing all the major drawbacks of the prior work. Not only the architecture can deliver precise and reliable energy consumption forecasts for various driving conditions, but it also makes sure that the computation is scalable and efficient, thus providing a solution that is very much aligned with the actual needs of the EV operators and fleet management systems. As a result, the suggested method is one of the most state-of-the-art solutions that dramatically lessen the range anxiety issue while keeping the domain of smart EV energy management less obscure at the same time.

Related works

This section presents an effective literature review and the methods of EV driving range prediction study. It talks about how the range estimation methods that are developed and utilized on a traditional basis on the basis of physics and empirical modeling has found its way into becoming data-driven and deep learning models13. It has been discussed that the previous models were founded on simplified energy consumption models, battery state of charge models and preset driving profile which in most instances in the real life scenario could not generalize. Then, machine learning and deep learning enabled to do more accurate and nonlinear predictions on the relationships between different factors that act on it, such as driving behavior, topography, ambient conditions and battery degradation. This section gives a critical assessment of the merits and demerits of these methods that exist and examines how accurately they predict and how flexible and efficient they are in calculation14. It also identifies the primary research limitations earlier including limited capacity to generalize, limited feature integrations and lack of hybridization in the face of data-driven and physics-based methods.

Huang, et al.15 proposed a new deep learning architecture to predict the EV driving range accurately and explainable is called the Balanced Ensemble Transformer (BET). The BET model takes into account a set of special Transformer networks, which perceive various aspects of driving data, therefore, becoming more flexible in various range conditions. To remove the discreponance of the data, the authors apply the stratified sampling, which will ensure that the high driving range and the low driving range are represented equally on the training. A dual aspect explainable model is added to improve the transparency of a model to take into account the feature importance and causality. Additionally, it has a guiding feature-routing mechanism that is dynamically selecting the appropriate Transformer to be applied in each prediction that leads to increased accuracy and resilience in a broad range of driving conditions. Khan, et al.16 focused on the field of EV analytics, applying recent machine learning algorithms to design and predict the key characteristics of the car and its performance metrics. The work involves classification and regression models and also takes the help of the more detailed dataset of parameters consisting of the model year, make, model type, vehicle class, motor power, and the energy consumption indicators. The categorical variables and the continuous variables in these models would include make and type of vehicle, and energy consumption, driving range, charging time, and motor power, which in turn determine the prospect of machine learning in full prediction of EV performance and intelligent system design.

Jiang, et al.17 introduced a novel Remaining Driving Range (RDR) prediction method of the Electric Vehicles to synthesize the individualized driving habit and future path to reach the peak of the prediction. Based on the real-world driving data comprising a diversity of driving patterns, road dynamics and road characteristics, driving behavior model is created in the study with the help of Hidden Markov Model (HMM) and Fuzzy Markov Model (FMM). Besides, an energy consumption model based on a physical model with machine learning is created to forecast energy consumption based on predicted driving cycles with more precise forecasts to context-dependent RDR prediction frameworks to each driving behavior. Hussain, et al.18 tested the eleven machine learning models, namely the Ridge Regression, Lasso Regression, Kathy-Nearest neighbors, Gradient Boosting, Support Vector Regression, Multi-layer Perceptron, XGBoost, Catboost, LightGBM, Gaussian Processes for Regression (GPR), and Extra Trees Regressor. The experiment compared the performance of each model in terms of quantitative analytics such as MAE, MSE, R 2, RMSE and NRMSE and graphical analysis in the form of a scatter plot and a time series plot using actual historical driving data in Colorado.

Akshayaa, et al.19 proposed an ensemble learning framework which is targeted to make successful predictions of the overall energy dispensed and session duration in Electric Vehicle (EV) charging stations. The framework enhances the accuracy of the prediction and the effectiveness of the operations with the help of different machine learning algorithms and a new weighted method. The implications of the results can be considerable towards EV charging infrastructure, which will enable distributing the energy more efficiently, planning the charging more efficiently and increasing the efficiency of the grid. In total, this approach will contribute to the establishment of a long-term and data-driven EV ecosystem and reduce the grid load and optimize the efficient management of energy. Elachhab, et al.20 integrated a data-driven method that is abundantly advantageous in determining the State of Charge (SOC) of an electric vehicle by incorporating the parameters of average power and average resistance besides the standard input features. Consequently, the paper turns to the architectures of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) to capture temporal dependencies and nonlinear battery behavior patterns due to these feature augmentations. The end pool of ideas leads to a more precise, stable, and reliable representation of SOC, which deep learning methods are the main technological argument in the evolution of battery management systems.

Jing, et al.21 invented a data-driven model grounded on vehicle operational conditions that accomplish multi-dimensional feature fusion and a hybrid deep neural network architecture for the P/E of EV with heightened accuracy. This work, which is mostly reliant on a colossal dataset of 12.83 million real-world data points covering different vehicle types and battery chemistries, the authors put forward a three-dimensional feature system consisting of shallow, intermediate, and deep descriptors for capturing representational, behavioral, and electrochemical characteristics. In order to overcome the problem of long temporal dependencies and Transformer limitations on multivariate data, the authors propose a hybrid model that merges Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCN) with a refined iTransformer, enabled by a differential attention mechanism that reduces attention noise and increases predictive ability.

Cavus, et al.22 combine statistical analysis and deep learning modeling to understand the electrification of vehicle (EV) charging behavior of 5,898 people, i.e., a socio-demographic representative sample of the population. In the paper, the authors, through Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) and Analysis of Variance (ANOVA), find very strong correlations between gender-related aspect, education level, employment status, household size, income, and housing type and the charging patterns. The publications constitute a knowledge base that illustrates the impact of demographic and socio-economic factors on the use of EV charging at home, at work, and in charging stations, which is the underlying principle for the development of more accessible and efficient charging infrastructures. Adnane, et al.23 evaluate the performance of two complicated machine-learning-based Energy Management Systems, the DMP-EMS (Driving Mode Predictor-based EMS) and the MEL-EMS (Multi-ensemble Learning-based EMS) for a Dual-Source Electric Vehicle (DSEV). The comparison between the two methods was made on the basis of the criteria that included the accuracy, model complexity, training and prediction times, computational requirements, scalability and energy efficiency. The findings demonstrate the fundamental differences in compromises of the two EMS configurations, thereby providing a considerable amount of theoretical and practical knowledge concerning the optimization of EMS design for various DSEV scenarios.

Gong, et al.24 provide a detailed review of different equipment techniques for electrically determining the SOH of vehicle batteries, which also include direct measurement, physics-based, and data-driven methods. Their comparison study reveals that data-driven methods, in particular machine learning techniques, are more precise and flexible but require a large number of high-quality data samples. Although physics-based models have good interpretability, they are computationally expensive, and direct measurement methods are easy but have a limited generalization capability. Moreover, the paper is a comprehensive survey of the main SOH metrics, the energetic material aging causes, and the battery datasets most closely related to SOH modeling, thus opening the way for the next battery health research and development.

The power prediction, energy management, and battery health evaluation of EV seem to be the areas where machine learning and deep learning have largely contributed, mediating from an initial scan of research publications. The field has been deeply scarred by such problems as data imbalance, limited generalization capabilities, and insufficient representation of real driving conditions which result in models that perform well in the tested conditions but poorly in other and unpredictable scenarios25. Besides, most of the existing models are heavily dependent on a single architecture (i.e., standalone LSTM, RNN, or Transformer networks) that cannot efficiently capture the multi-scale temporal, spatial, and behavioral changes of the EV operations. In addition, while some pure data-driven and physics-based models have been suggested, hardly any work has been done in developing hybrid models that combine the interpretability of physical models with the predictive power of deep learning26. On top of that, the discussion is not only about feature selection and representation learning but also about driver behavior, route context, traffic conditions, and environmental factors, which most models are increasingly failing to recognize as a collection of heterogeneous data. Some of the factors that limit the methods proposed in the literature to have a wide range of applications are overfitting due to a small or unbalanced dataset, high computational cost, limited explanation, and poor transferability between different kinds of vehicles and battery chemistries.

Under these circumstances, the proposed work is certainly a necessity and, moreover, an unavoidable fact27. The goal of the hybrid deep learning architecture for the EV driving range prediction to solve the problems means tapping into the power of various deep architectures without giving up the balanced and multi-dimensional data learning. One by one, the model not only pushes forward the prediction accuracy but also the explanation by fixing data imbalance, introducing personalized driving behavior, and utilizing feature fusion techniques. Therefore, the hybrid scheme not only extends the performance stability to a huge variety of operational scenarios but also at the same time provides the means to easily understand the consumption and driving range factors28–30. The proposed work thus represents a step towards the setting up of an EV range prediction system that is more dependable, flexible, and intelligent, which in turn makes energy management easier, lessens range anxiety, and enhances the users’ trust in electric mobility, thereby being helpful for the sustainable transport cause.

Proposed methodology

This section presents the ground methodology and the innovative design developed in this study to predict the driving range of the EV drivers. It outlines the idea and design of a hybrid deep learning-based system, which is an integration of novel feature extraction, weight optimization in attention, and real-time sequence modeling to address the question of accurate remaining driving range prediction. The proposed work is motivated by the shortages of the available practices as they reflect solely the nonlinear, non-stationary and dynamic nature of the EV operational and driving data. In this case, the key components of the proposed framework are outlined, i.e. the Hilbert-Huang Auto Recurrence Transform (HART) that extracts the features, the Path-Informed Adaptive Inverted Trend Hub Range Analyzer (PAITHRA) that is the simplest predictive model, and the Chaotic Algae Sparrow Hyper-tuner (CASH) that is the attention weight optimizer. Together with it, the workflow of the proposed methodology is also detailed, with raw EV data being transformed into a powerful and informative feature set, which is also run through the hybrid deep learning structure, before it is used to produce highly accurate driving range predictions.

The study has three significant contributions. To begin with, HART development provides a novel, hybrid feature extraction procedure that recreates nonlinear, time-varying and repeat qualities of EV driving signals and that assures the essential patterns of the speed, battery condition, motor current and route attributes are upheld and effectively modeled. Second, the housing of PAITHRA design proposes a combination of predictive models with Pathformer, which is capable of integrating various modalities of features, decomposing driving sequences, and routing features using special Transformers dynamically to optimize the prediction performance in the dynamic driving environment. Third, CASH enables the estimation of attention weights in highly accurate and adaptive sense, that is, the model can focus on the most significant input features dynamically and remain stable in a wide variety of operating conditions. All these contribute to a new, hybrid, and interpretable EV range forecast model which is more accurate, flexible and uses more features as compared to the existing methods of predicting ranges.

Overview of the proposed work.

As shown in Fig. 1, the three innovations that are interrelated are the innovations that the proposed methodology will be capitalizing on in order to deliver successful projections regarding the driving range of EVs. HART is a model, which uses the Hilbert-Huang Transform to break down the nonlinearly raw EV telemetry data, the auto-correlation to detect the temporal dependence of the data, and a recurrence quantification analysis to detect repeating patterns in the driving behavior. These features are processed into informative inputs to a hybrid Pathformer-based predictor architecture, PAITHRA that applies adaptive inverted trend decomposition to different parts of driving information, and has a feature routing system that allocates each input to most effectively use every Transformer branch. And the last but not the least, CASH is also utilized to train chaotic attention weights in an algae-sparrow-inspired optimization to dynamically isolate the feature worth to get the model more focused and predictive. The difference about this work is that these parts can be used in a synergistic combination to produce a complete hybrid pipeline that can address the concerns both of feature representation and predictive modelling within the same framework.

The original data of the proposed framework is the crude EV driving and battery telemetry data, such as vehicle speed, acceleration, state-of-charge, motor current, environmental data and route specifics. Preprocessing and normalization of the data is the first step, after that, feature extraction is achieved through the use of HART to get a complete set of features that contain nonlinear trends, temporal effects and recurrent patterns. They are then fed into PAITHRA which does adaptive inverted trend decomposition in order to decompose the data into different parts and branches of Pathformer learn the relationships between the input features and other driving range. In this, CASH is automatically estimated and optimized to estimate the attention weights of each feature by ensuring that its model is focused on the most relevant input in every driving scenario. The results of the PAITHRA model are then added together to give the final predicted EV driving range that will be continuously compared to the ground-truth data to optimize the performance. The suggested workflow will ensure that the suggested framework will be in a position to incorporate high-end feature extraction, hybrid deep learning, and adaptive attention optimization to secure accurate, robust, trustworthy, and explainable driving anguish forecasts across numerous types of EVs in numerous driving conditions.

Hilbert-huang auto recurrence transform (HART)

Hilbert-Huang Auto Recurrence Transform (HART) is a novel hybrid framework of feature extraction that is specially configured to offer meaningful and good-quality feature of the nonlinear and non-stationary and complex signal of the time-series data of the electric vehicle (EV) driving and battery telemetry. The central concept of HART is to continue the development of predictive models, e.g. EV driving range prediction systems with the development of features capturing complex tropes, long term and short term dependencies and recurrent dynamics involved in driving and battery behavior. The main advantage of the specified method is that three areas of analysis that are mutually complementary, i.e., the Hilbert-Huang Transform (HHT), auto-correlation, and recurrence analysis are merged into one and unified pipeline of feature extraction. HART is also interested in the limitation of conventional feature-extraction algorithms that may be unable to display all three properties of nonlinearities, intermittence, and repetitiveness of EV data. By so doing, it can ensure that the downstream predictive models receive an informative and detailed description of the functional characteristics of the car which is necessary to lessen the effect of mistakes in predicting the driving range and improve the precision of the EV usage forecasts.

HART is creative and original as there is the hybridization process and synthesis of certain approaches to analysis. Firstly, the Hilbert-Huang Transform (HHT) is applied so as to decompose each time-series signal into the intrinsic mode functions (IMFs) in effect separating the nonlinear and non-stationary parts. This de-noising allows the process to capture instantaneous frequencies and amplitudes of EV signals to highlight small variations in battery voltage, current, speed and energy consumption that would otherwise have been overlooked by other methods such as Fourier or wavelet techniques. Second, auto-correlation module estimates the reliance of time in an individual signal and it indicates how the past behavior affects the future state. Particularly within EV data, the driving pattern effects, acceleration profile and battery dynamics have a direct impact on energy consumption and range. Finally, there is the recurrence module, the Recurrence Quantification Analysis (RQA) that identifies the repetitive patterns and dynamic behavior of time series and determines the repetitive behaviors, such as stop-and-go traffic, repetitive patterns in acceleration-deceleration cycles, and pattern in routes. The combination of the three modules forms a hybrid feature set, time-sensitive, trend-aware, and pattern-rich feature set and has a sturdy foundation in predictive modeling.

Hybridization of HART is a well thought out and well-structured process in order to ensure that the synergy of the incorporated components is optimized. The raw EV telemetry data (vehicle speed, state-of-charge (SOC), current, voltage, motor power and environmental conditions) is initially pre-processed in order to eliminate noise and standardize measurements. These then undergo pre-processing and HHT breaks down the pre-processed signals to form a complex of intrinsic mode functions and instantaneous frequency and amplitude measurements on them. Such broken signals are then processed by auto correlation module that computes correlation coefficients at the different lags to determine the time dependence and latent effects in data. At the same time, recurrence plots are identified by the recurrence analysis module, and the measures of RQA calculate recurrence rate, determinism, entropy and linearity to describe the dynamical structure and repeating structure of the driving behavior. The three modules are then summed into creating one feature vector that contains the nonlinear, temporal and repetitive characteristics of the original signals and vastly reduces the dimensionality and redundancy. This combination mechanism is required to ensure that all the removed features introduce new and complementary data to the predictive model.

The HART possesses a logical workflow with data. The EV dataset is the input in the technique that can include time-series signals of various sensors, battery management system, and environmental sensors. First, there is pre-processing of the data i.e. noise filtered and normalized and segmented. The HHT module then decomposes the signals into intrinsic oscillations modes as well as instantaneous trends as IMFs. After this, the auto-correlation module calculates metrics of correlation to gain an insight as to repeat temporal dependencies on over one scale, and the recurrence module calculates RQA metrics to encode repetitive patterns and dynamic behavior. A final extracted set of features are then produced by combining all the three modules which are the output of HART.

B. Path-informed adaptive inverted trend hub range analyzer (PAITHRA)

B. Path-informed adaptive inverted trend hub range analyzer (PAITHRA)

Path-informed adaptive inverted trend hub range analyzer (PAITHRA)

Path-Informed Adaptive Inverted Trend Hub Range Analyzer (PAITHRA) is a hybrid deep learning model that had a clear objective of addressing the undesirable issue of the true driving range prediction of an electric vehicle (EV). The reasoning behind this approach is the need to reduce the range anxiety of individuals who drive EVs by providing precise and reliable forecasts in various driving scenarios and battery conditions as well as environmental factors. The principle of PAITHRA is to integrate multi-dimensional driving data (including vehicle telemetry, battery control, environmental data, and route data) in one prediction model to have the ability to make predictions that can be applicable in different conditions. The significant input of the technique is that it possesses a hybridized architecture that inherits the finest of Pathformer founded temporal modeling, adaptive feature selection, and the inverted seasonal-trend decomposition that could be utilized both to represent the sporadic variations in driving behavior and long-term patterns in battery performance and energy consumption. Through stratified representation of the driving data, PAITHRA ensures that the high and the low limits of the driving range is well represented in the training, and this plays a huge role in making the driving range being predicted to be robust and reliable.

The originality of PAITHRA lies on several basic points. First, it employs a path-informed mechanism, i.e. driving paths and route-dependent items are employed to cause the model to make selective attention to features of interest at every prediction step. This gives the model the ability to dynamically respond to a change in traffic, road gradient and driver behaviour which is not given much consideration in many traditional deep learning models. Second, the inverted trend decomposition module helps PAITHRA to determine the long-term trends (e.g. battery degradation or seasonal temperature variation), and short-term variations (e.g. sudden acceleration or braking) separately and rewind these components with the help of a fusion mechanism. The final outcome of this decomposition is that not only does it increase the interpretability of the predictions, but also reduces noise influence of the telemetry data, which will give more stable and accurate predictions. The technique also uses a central processing unit that takes the form of a hub to merge the output of multiple specialized Pathformer branches. Each branch is then adjusted to emulate a particular behavior of the driving behavior or battery dynamics, e.g. SOC changes, temperature changes, motor efficiency, and the hub intelligently combines them based on their relevance features and anticipated significance.

In technical terms, there are many layers in the process of hybridization of PAITHRA, and all are interrelated and collaborate. The model input is derived characteristics of the pre-processed EV driving data that may include velocity, acceleration, state of charge (SOC), ambient temperature, road gradient and historical energy consumption data. It is the contribution of these features that is then transmitted by an adaptive feature selection layer that in turn dynamically weights the input contribution based on the degree of variability it makes in driving range. The selected features are then channeled to a set of Pathformer encoder branches which learn to create temporal relationship and sequential patterns in each subspace of data. Likewise, the layer of inverted seasonal-trend decomposition breaks down the long-term trends and short-term changes, and creates decomposed feature channels that, in the future, will be combined with the Pathformer output in an attention-based fusion layer. It is this combination that presupposes that the most topical temporal patterns and trend elements will be assigned the first priority in prediction. Finally, the combined representations are cleared by a fully connected regression head which provides the desired range of driving as a continuous parameter. The other attributes of the model architecture are those that will be the residual connections, normalization layer, and dropout to stabilize the training and to avoid overfitting and enhancing generalization across vehicles and driving situations.

As shown in Fig. 2, the PAITHRA working process would look as follows: the raw information on telemetry and the environment is first collected and processed to acquire significant features. These are then weighed and forwarded to the adaptive feature selection and Pathformer branches where sequential patterns, correlations and dependencies are learnt. The inverted trend decomposition module breaks down long-term and short-term fluctuations that are being rebuilt alongside the Pathformer ones with attention mechanism in the hub. The following fully connected layers help to optimize this latent representation to give the final predicted EV driving range. The path-informed attention, trend decomposition, and hybridized neural processing can help PAITHRA to achieve accurate prediction of the complex and nonlinear relationships between EV driving behavior. The model is effective in balancing the sensitivity to the current driving state with the cognizance of the world trends in the consumption of energy, which provides highly precise, solid, and interpretable predictions in the driving range. It is understandable that this not only makes PAITHRA not only a technically advanced solution but a handy practical tool to any EV users, fleet operators and automotive researchers who would like to have a more direct access to the battery utilization, improved route planning, and increase the general adoption of EVs.

The primary benefits of PAITHRA are that the accuracy of the tool in prediction is high in most driving conditions, types of vehicles, and battery conditions. The method is capable of readily learning both short-term dynamics of driving patterns, such as acceleration hypothesis, sudden braking and anomalous traffic congestion, and long-term dynamics of battery aging, temperature, and the effects of the season. It is the two level knowledge that allows PAITHRA to generate predictions that are not only precise, but also robust to the noisy or incomplete input data, which is a widespread issue in practice in EV telemetry. Furthermore, the hub-based arrangement will be useful as well so that the findings of two or more separate Pathformer branches can be intelligently mixed in such a way that the model can leverage supplementary data across fields of features. As a result, the PAITHRA reduces the amount of errors introduced by prediction compared to the traditional single-model predictive and ensures more accurate predictions of the driving range to reduce range anxiety in EV users.

PAITHRA also possesses good operational and computational advantages. The pathformer is a hybrid architecture which is a modular and allows subsets of features to be processed in parallel which allows the training and inference of subsets to be undertaken to generate reduced time yet maintain high accuracy. Attention-based fusion mechanism is also used to ensure that the computational resources are allocated to the most relevant signals hence it is effective in real-time predictions. Incremental learning is also intended to be supported by the model i.e. the model can maintain abreast with new driving information without necessarily retraining the whole network. This is why PAITHRA, in particular, is well applicable in the implementation of linked EV systems and intelligent transportation, where streams of data can be offered continuously to take advantage of them and ensure future prediction and accuracy. The scalable design will enable it to use large groups of vehicles, which will render the solution feasible in commercial terms of EV use and energy management in the context of urban transport networks.

Finally, PAITHRA provides realistic information on energy optimization and sustainability. The correct forecasts of the driving ranges enable the EV users to plan the charging stops properly in order to prevent unreasonable consumption of energy resources, to avoid emergency recharging. Fleet operators will have an opportunity to stream the routing planning and charging hours that will balance the energy load and enhance the effectiveness of the grid in general. PAITHRA promotes sustainable transport operations at both short-term driving and long-term behavior of the battery, thus contributing to the increase in the battery life and minimizing the operation cost. PAITHRA can offer the future generation of EV range prediction systems, since with its combination of high accuracy, flexibility, interpretability and real-time capabilities, the gap between the current state of machine learning research and practical automotive tasks can be bridged.

C. Chaotic algae sparrow hyper-tuner (CASH)

C. Chaotic algae sparrow hyper-tuner (CASH)

Chaotic algae sparrow hyper-tuner (CASH)

The Chaotic Algae Sparrow Hyper-tuner (CASH) is a novel hybrid optimization algorithm whose main objective is to learn the attention weights of the complex deep learning models, such as those of PAITHRA in the driving range prediction of an electric vehicle (EV). The primary goal of the CASH is to expand the range of the model in terms of focusing on the most significant input characteristics- vehicle speed, state-of-charge (SOC), motor current, environmental conditions, and route specific factors based on the analysis of optimized attention scores. The computation of the mass of the attention is significant in the forecasting of the EV ranges since the input of each feature of the input is highly variable in different driving conditions along with battery conditions and routes. Traditional optimization strategies are likely to be either local minima or are unable to cover the significant space of solutions sufficiently, either of which may render the attention processes less efficient hence, prediction of driving ranges more precise. CASH will address these problems by adding a Chaotic Artificial Algae Algorithm (CAAA) and the Sparrow Search Optimization (SSO) algorithm, the merits of the exploration provided by the algae-inspired search dynamics combined with the merits of exploitation provided by the sparrow-inspired social foraging behavior to form a sound, adaptive, and highly effective attention tuning mechanism.

Fig. 3Flow of the proposed CASH model.

Flow of the proposed CASH model.

CASH is new and unique in terms of its hybridization process being synergistic process of combination of two biologically-inspired metaheuristic algorithms in a manner that it addresses weaknesses of each of the two strategies separately. Chaotic Artificial Algae Algorithm (CAAA) offers stochastic chaos in algae search algorithm, and therefore gives the algorithm a wide solution space in order to avoid an early trapping in local optimal solutions. This will allow CASH to search a large number of potential attention weight settings. It has a Sparrow Search Optimization (SSO) component that implements the social foraging behavior exhibited by sparrow populations in parallel with invoking the search to potentially valuable regions of the solution space based on shared learning and adaptive adjustment of the individual locations. It is sequential, iterative in the sense of how this hybridization works in that CAAA first performs a global search to generate a pool of varied candidate attention weights, which are then refined and further refined by means of SSO, in such a way that the resulting final attention weights are not only globally informed but also locally accurate. This two-step optimization is necessary so that the attention mechanism of PAITHRA can dynamically target the most significant features according to the dynamic driving conditions resulting in improved prediction and model robustness.

As shown in Fig. 3, the CASH has a systematic workflow and it is programmed to be combined with deep learning models. The input of the technique is the initial attention weights generated by the attention module in PAITHRA and the feature matrix generated by the EV telemetry data. CASH optimises the state with Chaotic Algae Algorithm that generate a multiplicity of possible attention weight vectors with chaos-enhanced exploration. The screening of these applicants relies on a fitness functional which is typically described as a reduction in prediction error, or an increment in model performance measure. The best candidates are then subjected to Sparrow Search Optimization whereby the attention weight values are optimized through a series of iterations with the use of social learning, personal vigilance and interaction between the leaders and followers in an attempt to fine-tune the final configuration. The outcome is a maximized attention weights that can be directly applied to the input features in PAITHRA such that each contribution of the feature individually to the driving range prediction is appropriately weighted and dynamically adjusted based on the real-world situations.

The advantages of implementing CASH are varied. Firstly, as a blend of global exploration (CAAA) and local exploitation (SSO), CASH is able to avoid local minima and perform better attention weight estimation than classical optimization strategies. Second, it makes the model more interpretable and explainable, which can be proved by the version of the optimized attention weights that shows what features make the most significant contribution to the EV driving range predictions. Thirdly, the approach makes the PAITHRA more resilient and externalized to other types of vehicles, driving behaviors and environmental conditions, to give a more predictable and consistent range value. Fourth, CASH is biologically-inspired, which means that it can be adaptive and self-regulating and, therefore, modify its search policy in response to feature importance and predictive performance feedbacks. Finally, the hybrid approach reduces convergence cost in computing, where CAAA provides an excellent pool of solutions, and SSO provides rapid fine tuning, this makes the hybrid approach a viable and scalable framework to apply to the EV prediction system in real or near real time. On the whole, CASH represents a novel hybrid, metaheuristic algorithm that highly enhances the predictive capabilities, interpretability and reliability of EV estimates of driving range models.

Special Pathformer modules receive various kinds of features in PAITHRA such as the operation parameters of the vehicle, the battery state, the driving behavior measurements and route-specific parameters. These factors prevent the model to either undermine or overestimate less significant features without optimized attention, thereby, losing prediction accuracy and getting range estimation errors. To avoid this issue, CASH prevents the weights of attention to fix the contribution of each feature by gradually changing the weight of the factors in response to their contribution to predictive accuracy. This does not only increase predictive capabilities of the driving range with various traffic and battery conditions and environmental conditions but also make the model more adaptable to various EVs, driving styles and various route situations. Both global searching based on the Chaotic Algae Algorithm and local refining based on the Sparrow Search Optimization are combined by CASH to be able to guarantee that PAITHRA can discover generalizable patterns in a large variety of data sets and the details of each situation, which is why it is especially effective in practice. Additionally, the worth of CASH is not restrained to streamlining of the weight, but it is the fundamental factor which underlies the portability and fidelity of the presented structure.

Results and discussion

The experimental analysis of the proposed HART+PAITHRA+CASH model is performed with the help of two additional real-world datasets of electric vehicles, which are Dataset 1 EV Energy Consumption Dataset and Dataset 2 Full Electric Vehicle Dataset 2024. These datasets will be selected in such a manner that they are capable of covering a large spectrum of EV driving patterns, battery patterns and environmental factors hence facilitating a complete analysis of the overall generalization ability and power of the model. The two data sets combined form multi-dimensional streams of telemetry consisting of vehicle dynamics, battery parameters, route characteristics, and external operating conditions, which renders both of them as the most appropriate benchmarks in the study of EV range and energy consumption prediction.

All the computational time measurements in this work were made on a work station with an Intel Core i9-13900 K CPU, 64GB RAM and NVIDIA RTX 4090 graphics card. Times of training are the sum of time spent by the model in all epochs on all samples, including all the preprocessing steps required to process a sample, like feature normalization, missing value imputation and temporal feature extraction, and inference times are the latency per sample that is achieved when the model has already converged, and not including the preprocessing. EV Energy Consumption Dataset (approximately 65,000 trips) and Full Electric Vehicle Dataset 2024 (approximately 92,000 trips) are the datasets that have been used and all experiments were performed using the fully processed data. These findings are valid since they distinctly separate between per-epoch training, full training runtime and per-sample inference which can be utilized to determine computational efficiency and real-time application in the vast majority of EV operation situations presented in the proposed hybrid deep learning framework.

EV Energy Consumption Dataset Dataset 131,32 offers information about trip-level energy consumption, but it is more concerned with more finer-grained energy consumption information that was gathered in a real-life driving situation. It takes into consideration such things as instantaneous speed, acceleration, generative braking activity, auxiliary load consumption, battery discharge rate, ambient temperature and changes in elevation. The dataset is particularly useful because it captures the differences in the driving behavior of the user and the environmental conditions which occur naturally, which frequently leads to uncertainty in estimating the EV range. The richness of features of micro-level functioning of this dataset can help PAITHRA to get acquainted with the short-term dynamics (as high acceleration, stop-and-go traffic and efficiency changes with temperature)33. This granularity increases the modelling ability in the sense of being able to identify useful temporal trends, and adapt to non-regular patterns of energy.

Dataset 2 Full Electric Vehicle Dataset 202427,28,34 has the extension of the evaluation to encompass a bigger and more detailed model of EV operations comprising a range of different types of vehicles, battery packs, seasons, and long routes. It comprises telemetry, which is comprehensive, like battery degradation indicators, SOC (State of Charge) transitions, route topology, climatic variables, charging behaviour and powertrain efficiency indicators. This data is complemented with the multi-regional driving patterns and various levels of traffic and therefore it is best suited when assessing the generalization of the proposed framework over the long horizon. The inclusion of the seasonal and long-distance trips information provides the impression of the deviations of the trends and makes the inverted trend and path-informed modules of PAITHRA to be able to capture the macro-levels of the consumption pattern and the long-term variations of energy use adequately35. The combination of these datasets enables it to be having a layered assessment with Dataset 1 supported by micro-pattern learning and Dataset 2 supported by macro-trend validation as a means of providing a holistic platform to demonstrate the viability of PAITHRA under various conditions of operation. The combination allows the results section to represent short term dependability, long term reliability, adaptation to route and environmental variability and the overall reliability to predict the driving range of electric vehicles.

EV Energy Consumption Dataset (Dataset 1) is a colossal dataset of actual driving records of different production electric vehicles that are driven in non-homogeneous urban, suburban, and highway settings. The data set entails approximately 65,000 individual driving trips of 112 distinct EVs whose total distance has gone past 1.8 million kilometers. All of the trips are sampled at 1 Hz, that picks up fine-grained dynamism of the timing of EV operation. Raw signals are presently the vehicle speed, longitudinal acceleration, the battery state-of-charge (SOC), real-time power consumption, motor torque, generative braking power, ambient temperature, road gradient and indicators of auxiliary load (HVAC usage). The proportion of samples of the total samples not available is less than 2.1% and this is primarily due to interim sensor outage and communication delays; such cases of gaps are remedied using a hybrid preprocessing pipeline, which combines forward backward interpolation to fill short gaps (less than 3 s) with a segment removal to avoid any bias in time relationships. All the features are standardized using the z-score which is computed over the training set only to prevent the leakage of data. In order to get experimental rigor, the data is split at the vehicle level rather than the sample level where a trip of a particular vehicle is not made in more than one subset. Specifically, it will have 70% of the vehicles going to training, 15% to validation and 15% to testing with 45,600 training trips, 9,700 validation trips and 9,700 testing trips respectively. This partitioning technique represents the strict generalization of unobservable cars and driving behaviors and evaluation is representative of the real deployment scenarios.

Full Electric Vehicle Dataset 2024 (Dataset 2) is a more recent and comprehensive benchmark that is targeted to test cross-domain robustness and scalability of EV range and energy consumption prediction models. It consists of over 92,000 journeys of 164 fully electric vehicles and across 4 climatic regions (hot, cold, temperate and humid) and various battery chemistries and capacity (40 100 kWh). The frequency at which the samples are taken is 2 Hz, and this enables the sampling of the driving transients at higher resolutions such as high acceleration, stop-and-go traffic, and occurrences of regenerative braking. Apart from vehicle essential telemetry, dataset 2 also includes contextual information, such as weather conditions (temperature, humidity, wind speed), road density indicators, and road types classification and elevation profiles depending on GPS mapping. This dataset has a slightly greater extent of missingness (in the order of 3.4) due to the presence of sensors heterogeneity by vehicle manufacturers, feature-conscious imputation of missing values, and physically correlated signals (e.g., power, speed, torque) are learnt with the assistance of local temporal regression. In order to help achieve the label reliability, all the trips shorter than five minutes, and those whose measurement of SoC are unstable are dropped before modeling. To avoid the issue of temporal leakage and optimistic bias, the data is split into two sections in terms of chronology and vehicle-disjoint: the first 65% of the trips per vehicle will be a part of the training set, the second 15% will constitute a validation set and the remaining 20% will constitute a test set. This will translate to approximately 59,800 training trips, 13,800 validation trips as well as 18,400 testing trips. This is one of the explicit challenges to the extrapolation of the model to future driving behavior and unidentified operation conditions that validate the purported real time applicability and extrapolator strength of the proposed hybrid deep learning structure.

Conclusion

This paper presents a new hybrid deep learning model to forecast the driving range of electric vehicles (EVs), their charging, and energy use in a high level of accuracy that will help to address the issues of nonlinear, non-stationary, and dynamic workout data related to EVs. The suggested methodology combines three basic characteristics including HART as a rich feature extractive technique, PAITHRA as a hybrid predictive model based on Pathformer, and CASH and a dynamic attention weight optimizer. It is novel in the sense that the advanced signal decomposition is combined on a synergistic basis with feature routing by specialized Transformers and attention optimization based on the principles of biological increasing an advantage the novel approach over the traditional and state-of-the-art approaches in both accuracy and computational efficiency. The proposed architecture begins with the pre-processing and normalization of the raw EV telemetry including speed of the car, state-of-charge of the battery, and motor current, surroundings, and route information. HART finds out high dimensional features that carry nonlinear trends, time dependence and patterns. These characteristics are then presented to PAITHRA, where adaptive trend inversion division divisions the data and Transformer branches of experts are trained on the complex correlation of features. CASH also dynamically varies the weights of attention to each input in a dynamic manner such that the model focuses on the most valuable features to produce a correct prediction. The PAITHRA outputs are combined among themselves to finalize predictions of the EV driving range, charging power, battery SOC and energy consumption which is continuously checked against the ground-truth in order to optimize.

The advantages of the proposed framework will be the high predictive accuracy, computational efficiency, interpretability, and flexibility with regard to the different types of EVs and driving scenarios. The model is able to guarantee an optimal reflection of a micro-level energy consumption pattern through the application of feature engineering, hybrid deep learning as well as adaptive attention optimization and a macro-level vehicle operational dynamics. The detailed examination of the EV Energy Consumption Dataset and the Full Electric Vehicle Dataset 2024 proves that the proposed framework is very efficient. Like the Proposed model, 2.5% prediction error and 0.8 kW MAE, RMSE of 1.3 kW, bumping at 99% and R 2 equal to 0.991, inference time of 8 ms and training time of 18 s is much better than some of the baseline models, including CNN-LSTM, BiLSTM, GRU, 1D-CNN, ResNet18, TCN, Seq2Seq, and standard ML, as well. These numbers confirm the idea that the hybrid strategy does not just enhance the degree of prediction precision, but also ensures the real-time application in regards to plausible EV energy management and range decision-making.

Embracing Collaboration Over Competition: Condensing Multiple Prompts for Visual In-Context Learning
Jinpeng Wang, Tianci Luo, Yaohua Zha, Yan Feng, Ruisheng Luo, Bin Chen, Tao Dai, Long Chen, Yaowei Wang, Shu-Tao Xia

Abstract
Visual In-Context Learning (VICL) enables adaptively solving vision tasks by leveraging pixel demonstrations, mimicking human-like task completion through analogy. Prompt selection is critical in VICL, but current methods assume the existence of a single “ideal” prompt in a pool of candidates, which in practice may not hold true. Multiple suitable prompts may exist, but individually they often fall short, leading to difficulties in selection and the exclusion of useful context. To address this, we propose a new perspective: prompt condensation . Rather than relying on a single prompt, candidate prompts collaborate to efficiently integrate informative contexts without sacrificing resolution. We devise Condenser , a lightweight external plugin that compresses relevant fine-grained context across multiple prompts. Optimized end-to-end with the backbone, Condenser ensures accurate integration of contextual cues. Experiments demonstrate Condenser outperforms state-of-the-arts across benchmark tasks, showing superior context compression, scalability with more prompts, and enhanced computational efficiency compared to ensemble methods, positioning it as a highly competitive solution for VICL. Code is open-sourced at https://github.com/gimpong/CVPR25-Condenser .

Prompt selection plays a crucial role in VICL.

Based on this insight, a range of efforts

We believe prompt candidates necessity

Combining multiple prompts is expected to enhance the compositional generalization ability, yet preliminary solutions, including downsampling and ensemble, still have clear limitations.
The downsampling methods

37

scale down sub-images proportionally to arrange more prompts on the fixed-size input canvas, while they may lose visual details in prompts.
Ensemble methods

Output aggregation is quite tricky for specific tasks and often requires careful adjustments. What’s worse, it raises efficiency concerns as the candidate prompts increase.
Beyond these practices, we introduce a new research perspective of

efficiently

without sacrificing resolution

We propose an end-to-end optimized approach to condense multiple prompts, as demonstrated in

Given a set of candidate prompts retrieved along with the query image, we devise a lightweight
Condenser
to adaptively fuse informative cues.
The
Condenser
identifies relevant image patterns and their associated annotations within the candidate prompts to form the reference for each query image patch, helping to extract the most relevant
fine-grained
context in the final prompt.
We train
Condenser
jointly with the vision backbone, utilizing end-to-end feedback to optimize prompt condensation.

Similar to retrievers,
Condenser
can act as a flexible external plugin to the vision backbone, making it highly adaptable and effective across VICL tasks.
We conduct extensive experiments on several benchmark tasks, including foreground segmentation, single-object detection, and image coloring.
Under standard protocols, even when using only a single candidate prompt,
Condenser
outperforms state-of-the-art methods remarkably, highlighting its strong information extraction ability.
More encouragingly,
Condenser
performs better as the number of candidate prompts increases, further demonstrating its information integration capability.
At the same time, it maintains a clear computational efficiency advantage over ensemble-based multi-prompt methods
[
24
]
.

To summarize, we make the following contributions.

•

We successfully shift the paradigm of prompt selection in VICL from exclusive competition to collaborative condensation, which enables multiple prompts to work together effectively and efficiently.

•

We design a lightweight
Condenser
to adaptively condense multiple prompts into a single, fine-grained prompt, enhancing VICL without sacrificing resolution.

•

Extensive experiments demonstrate that
Condenser
outperforms state-of-the-art methods on multiple tasks, showing effective context compression capacity, scalability with increasing candidate prompts, and favorable computational efficiency beyond output ensemble.

3
Learning Prompt Condensation for VICL

3.1
Problem Formulation and Method Overview

Suppose there is an training set
\mathcal{Q}=\left\{I_{q},L_{q}\right\}_{q=1}^{N_{q}}
, where
I_{q},L_{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{\frac{H_{0}}{2}\times\frac{W_{0}}{2}\times 3}
respectively denote a query image and its ground-truth label,
i.e
.
a synthetic image annotating bounding box or segmentation mask.
Here,
H_{0}=W_{0}=224
.
Additionally, there is an auxiliary task-specific prompt database
\mathcal{P}=\left\{P_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{N_{p}}
for contextual reference, where each prompt
P_{i}=(I_{i},L_{i})
is an image-label pair.
Given a query image
I_{q}
sampled from
\mathcal{Q}
, we obtain a subset of relevant prompts according to image-image similarity, denoted by
\mathcal{S}_{q}=\left\{P_{c_{k}}\right\}_{k=1}^{K}
.
For competitive prompt selection, we only choose the rank-1 prompt
P_{c_{1}}
to construct contextualized sample, namely

X_{c_{1}}=\begin{bmatrix}I_{c_{1}}&L_{c_{1}}\\
I_{q}&L_{\texttt{[MASK]}}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{H_{0}\times W_{0}\times 3}
.
Here
L_{\texttt{[MASK]}}
denotes the padding mask for the query.

In contrast,
prompt condensation
aims to condense
\mathcal{S}_{q}
into a single prompt
P_{c*}
, such that the constructed sample
X_{c*}
helps the vision backbone
f(\cdot)
predict the masked query label more accurately than using
X_{c_{1}}
.
To achieve this goad, we devise a lightweight plugin module,
Condenser
, denoted as
\phi(\cdot,\cdot)
and design effective end-to-end learning strategy for optimization.

Figure

2
illustrate the general pipeline.
Details will be elaborated in the following sections.

They introduced the simple yet powerful MAE-VQGAN model, which served as the foundation for several subsequent works

MAE-VQGAN features an asymmetric input-output architecture: the input is processed through patch embeddings, while the output is generated using the VQGAN decoder.

3.3
Condenser
Design

Figure 4
:
Condenser
design.
The cross-prompt attention performs
patch-wise
cross-attention to aggregate informative context among the
K
prompts.
LN: layer normalization. P: permutation.

We design
Condenser
as a lightweight external plugin, expected to capture and integrate relevant fine-grained context from candidate prompts in a query-adaptive manner.

Figure

4
visualizes the design, which, when combined with the following textual description, will aid in understanding.

We first embed the query
I_{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{\frac{H_{0}}{2}\times\frac{W_{0}}{2}\times 3}
into
\bm{F}_{q}^{I}\in\mathbb{R}^{\frac{H}{2}\times\frac{W}{2}\times D}
, the prompt images
I_{c_{1:K}}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times\frac{H_{0}}{2}\times\frac{W_{0}}{2}\times 3}
into
\bm{F}_{c_{1:K}}^{I}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times\frac{H}{2}\times\frac{W}{2}\times D}
, and the stack of prompt
L_{c_{1:K}}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times\frac{H_{0}}{2}\times\frac{W_{0}}{2}\times 3}
into
\bm{F}_{c_{1:K}}^{L}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times\frac{H}{2}\times\frac{W}{2}\times D}
, using patch embedding in MAE-VQGAN. Here,
H=W=\frac{H_{0}}{16}=\frac{W_{0}}{16}
.

Then, we identify informative patches and patterns within each image with simple self-attention.
In particular, we share the attention layer between prompt images and their labels to preserve their spatial correspondence.
The process can be formulated as follows:

\displaystyle\bm{F}_{q}^{I(1)}=\text{LN}_{\mathrm{4}}(\text{SA}_{\mathrm{1}}(\text{LN}_{\mathrm{1}}(\bm{F}_{q}^{I}))+\bm{F}_{q}^{I}),

(1)

\displaystyle\bm{F}_{c_{1:K}}^{I(1)}=\text{LN}_{\mathrm{5}}(\text{SA}_{\mathrm{2}}(\text{LN}_{\mathrm{2}}(\bm{F}_{c_{1:K}}^{I}))+\bm{F}_{c_{1:K}}^{I}),

(2)

\displaystyle\bm{F}_{c_{1:K}}^{L(1)}=\text{LN}_{\mathrm{6}}(\text{SA}_{\mathrm{2}}(\text{LN}_{\mathrm{3}}(\bm{F}_{c_{1:K}}^{L}))+\bm{F}_{c_{1:K}}^{L}).

(3)

LN and SA denote layer normalization and self-attention, respectively.
Subscripts differentiate different modules.
After that, we permute
\bm{F}_{c_{1:K}}^{I(1)}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times\frac{H}{2}\times\frac{W}{2}\times D}
into
\bm{F}_{c_{1:K}}^{I(2)}\in\mathbb{R}^{\frac{H}{2}\times\frac{W}{2}\times K\times D}
and

\bm{F}_{c_{1:K}}^{L(1)}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times\frac{H}{2}\times\frac{W}{2}\times D}
into
\bm{F}_{c_{1:K}}^{L(2)}\in\mathbb{R}^{\frac{H}{2}\times\frac{W}{2}\times K\times D}
for later steps.

Next, we attend and aggregate query-adaptive context with
Patch-wise
Cross-Attention (PCA), namely

\displaystyle\bm{F}_{c*}^{I}=\text{PCA}_{I}({\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2344,0.9609}\bm{F}_{q}^{I(1)}},{\color[rgb]{0.9297,0.4297,0.3398}\bm{F}_{c_{1:K}}^{I(2)}},{\color[rgb]{0.5078,0.832,0.3242}\bm{F}_{c_{1:K}}^{I(2)}}),

(4)

\displaystyle\bm{F}_{c*}^{L}=\text{PCA}_{L}({\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2344,0.9609}\bm{F}_{q}^{I(1)}},{\color[rgb]{0.9297,0.4297,0.3398}\bm{F}_{c_{1:K}}^{I(2)}},{\color[rgb]{0.5078,0.832,0.3242}\bm{F}_{c_{1:K}}^{L(2)}}).

(5)

PCA
{}_{I}({\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2344,0.9609}\cdot},{\color[rgb]{0.9297,0.4297,0.3398}\cdot},{\color[rgb]{0.5078,0.832,0.3242}\cdot})
and PCA
{}_{L}({\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2344,0.9609}\cdot},{\color[rgb]{0.9297,0.4297,0.3398}\cdot},{\color[rgb]{0.5078,0.832,0.3242}\cdot})
condense the context within query images and labels, respectively.
The 3 arguments of PCAs are
query
,
key
and
value
factors, respectively.

For a query image patch, cross-attention to prompt images and labels is restricted to patches in the same spatial position.
For instance, the cross-attention output for the query patch at position
(h,w)
, where
1\leq h\leq\frac{H}{2}
and
1\leq w\leq\frac{W}{2}
, is

\displaystyle\bm{F}_{c*}^{I}[h,w]=\bm{A}_{h,w}^{I}{\color[rgb]{0.5078,0.832,0.3242}\bm{W}_{V}^{I}\bm{F}_{c_{1:K}}^{I(2)}[h,w]},

(6)

\displaystyle\bm{F}_{c*}^{L}[h,w]=\bm{A}_{h,w}^{L}{\color[rgb]{0.5078,0.832,0.3242}\bm{W}_{V}^{L}\bm{F}_{c_{1:K}}^{L(2)}[h,w]}.

(7)

The attention scores
\bm{A}_{h,w}^{I},\bm{A}_{h,w}^{L}
are with different parameters but the same input to ensure spatial correspondence:

\displaystyle\resizebox{29305575}{}{
$\bm{A}_{h,w}^{I}=\text{softmax}\left(\frac{{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2344,0.9609}(\bm{W}_{Q}^{I}\bm{F}_{q}^{I(1)}[h,w])}{\color[rgb]{0.9297,0.4297,0.3398}(\bm{W}_{K}^{I}\bm{F}_{c_{1:K}}^{I(2)}[h,w])}^{\top}}{\sqrt{D}}\right)$},

(8)

\displaystyle\resizebox{29305575}{}{
$\bm{A}_{h,w}^{L}=\text{softmax}\left(\frac{{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2344,0.9609}(\bm{W}_{Q}^{L}\bm{F}_{q}^{I(1)}[h,w])}{\color[rgb]{0.9297,0.4297,0.3398}(\bm{W}_{K}^{L}\bm{F}_{c_{1:K}}^{I(2)}[h,w])}^{\top}}{\sqrt{D}}\right)$}.

(9)

Shapes of parameters and variables in
Equations

6
,
7
,
8
and
9
:

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2344,0.9609}\bm{W}_{Q}^{I}},{\color[rgb]{0.9297,0.4297,0.3398}\bm{W}_{K}^{I}},{\color[rgb]{0.5078,0.832,0.3242}\bm{W}_{V}^{I}},{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2344,0.9609}\bm{W}_{Q}^{L}},{\color[rgb]{0.9297,0.4297,0.3398}\bm{W}_{K}^{L}},{\color[rgb]{0.5078,0.832,0.3242}\bm{W}_{V}^{L}}\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times D},

\displaystyle\bm{A}_{h,w}^{I},\,\bm{A}_{h,w}^{L}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times K},

\displaystyle\bm{F}_{q}^{I(1)}[h,w],\,\bm{F}_{q}^{L(1)}[h,w]\in\mathbb{R}^{D},

\displaystyle\bm{F}_{c_{1:K}}^{I(2)}[h,w],\,\bm{F}_{c_{1:K}}^{L(2)}[h,w]\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times D}.

Patch-wise cross-attention is preferred over full cross-attention to preserve local consistency in images.
Restricting attention to the same spatial locations across the query and candidate prompts avoids irrelevant information from unrelated positions.
This ensures accurate spatial alignment in most tasks with well-defined visual structures, where patch-wise attention provides more precise and consistent results.

3.4
Learning Objectives

We integrate the frozen vision backbone into the
Condenser
training process to make them more compatible and acquire more accurate optimization feedback.
Below we introduce the two learning objectives for optimization.

3.4.1
Token Prediction for Query Labels

We first introduce end-to-end token prediction to enable task-oriented supervisory signals.
As shown in
Figure

2
, we construct contextualized labeled samples for each candidate prompt in
\mathcal{S}_{q}
.
For example,

\tilde{X}_{c_{i}}=\begin{bmatrix}I_{c_{i}}&L_{c_{i}}\\
I_{q}&L_{q}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{H_{0}\times W_{0}\times 3}
for
I_{c_{i}}
,
where
L_{q}
is the label of
I_{q}
for training.
We then encode
\tilde{X}_{c_{i}}
with the VQGAN encoder into tokens:

\bm{T}_{c_{i}}^{\tilde{X}}=h(\tilde{X}_{c_{i}})=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{T}_{c_{i}}^{I}&\bm{T}_{c_{i}}^{L}\\
\bm{T}_{q_{c_{i}}}^{I}&\bm{T}_{q_{c_{i}}}^{L}\end{bmatrix}\in\{1,2,\cdots,N_{t}\}^{H\times W}.

(10)

Here,
h(\cdot)
represents the VQGAN encoder.
\bm{T}_{q_{c_{i}}}^{I},\bm{T}_{q_{c_{i}}}^{L}\in\{1,2,\cdots,N_{t}\}^{\frac{H}{2}\times\frac{W}{2}}
are the contextualized encoded tokens for query image and label.
N_{t}
is codebook size of VQGAN.
We slice out
\bm{T}_{q_{c_{i}}}^{L}
as the labels for token prediction.

On the backbone side, we obtain the embedded contextualized unlabeled sample as
\bm{F}^{X}_{c*}
, predicting output token probabilities through the backbone
f
as

\hat{\bm{T}}_{q_{c*}}^{X}=\begin{bmatrix}\hat{\bm{T}}_{c_{*}}^{I}&\hat{\bm{T}}_{c_{*}}^{L}\\
\hat{\bm{T}}_{q_{c_{*}}}^{I}&\hat{\bm{T}}_{q_{c_{*}}}^{L}\end{bmatrix}\in[0,1]^{H\times W\times N_{t}}.

(11)

We separate query label prediction
\hat{\bm{T}}_{q_{c*}}^{L}\in[0,1]^{\frac{H}{2}\times\frac{W}{2}\times N_{t}}
and compute the candidate-specific token prediction loss as

\ell_{\text{TP}}^{(i)}=-\frac{4}{HW}\sum_{h}^{H/2}\sum_{w}^{W/2}\log\hat{\bm{T}}_{q_{c*}}^{L}[h,w,\bm{T}_{q_{c_{i}}}^{L}[h,w]].

(12)

The final token prediction loss is summarized by

\mathcal{L}_{\text{TP}}=\mathbb{E}_{q\sim\mathcal{Q}}\left[\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i}^{K}\ell_{\text{TP}}^{(i)}\right].

(13)

3.4.2
Pre-Alignment for Condensed Prompts

To improve stability and calibrate biases, we further introduce a pre-alignment objective that guides
Condenser
’s output to align with the query before backbone inference.
This objective is defined by maximizing cosine similarity:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{PA}}=-\mathbb{E}_{q\sim\mathcal{Q}}\left[\cos(\bm{F}_{c*}^{I},\bm{F}_{q}^{I})+\cos(\bm{F}_{c*}^{L},\bm{F}_{q}^{L})\right],

(14)

where instance-wise cosine similarity is defined by

\displaystyle\resizebox{28654693}{}{
$\cos(\bm{F}_{c*}^{I},\bm{F}_{q}^{I})=\frac{4}{HW}\sum_{h}^{H/2}\sum_{w}^{W/2}\cos(\bm{F}_{c*}^{I}[h,w],\bm{F}_{q}^{I}[h,w])$},

(15)

\displaystyle\resizebox{28654693}{}{
$\cos(\bm{F}_{c*}^{L},\bm{F}_{q}^{L})=\frac{4}{HW}\sum_{h}^{H/2}\sum_{w}^{W/2}\cos(\bm{F}_{c*}^{L}[h,w],\bm{F}_{q}^{L}[h,w])$}.

(16)

Finally, the overall learning objectives is given by

\min_{\phi}\mathcal{L}_{\text{TP}}+\lambda\mathcal{L}_{\text{PA}}.

(17)

\phi
denotes the model parameters.

\lambda>0
is a balancing factor for the two objectives.

4.3
Model Ablation

In this section, we investigate the efficacy of different components to facilitate a better understanding of our method.
For ease of reference and comparison, we assign IDs to different ablated model variants.
For example, we associate Variants (0) and (1) with our default models, as shown in
Table

4

4.3.1
Effectiveness of
Condenser
Design

To investigate the
Condenser
design, we first construct the following variants:
(
2
) removes the
Condenser
and uses mean pooling to fuse multiple prompts;
(
3
) replaces the Patch-wise Cross-Attention (PCA) module in
Eqs.

4
and
5
with the full attention cross-attention that allows a query patch to attend to any patch position of the prompts.
We then experiment with 16 candidate prompts and report the results in
Table

4
.

By comparing Variants (1) and (2), we can see that prompt condensation is rather than simply pooling the context across prompts, indicating its significant efficacy in extracting and coordinating contexts in raw prompts.
By comparing Variants (1) and (3), we can also observe that enabling full cross-attention is even inferior to directly removing query-prompt interactions, revealing the non-trivial design of PCA that preserves the local consistency in the condensed prompt.
This is consistent with our intuition (See the end of §
3.3
).

4.3.2
Effects of Learning Objectives

We construct the following variants to study the contributions of the 2 learning objectives:
(
4
) and (
5
) remove the pre-alignment loss (
Eq.

14
) under single- and multi-prompting settings, respectively;
(
6
) and (
7
) remove the token prediction loss (
Eq.

13
) under single- and multi-prompting settings.

By comparing Variants (4) and (5) with the full versions, we see the consistent advantages of adding
\mathcal{L}_{\text{PA}}
.
On the other hand, Variants (6) and (7), which omit the token prediction objective, show significant performance degradation. This suggests that end-to-end backbone feedback is crucial for robust prompt condensation. The poor performance likely stems from
Condenser
operating in the low-level feature space, where the lightweight module struggles to capture comprehensive semantics and reconstruct full details in the labeled query. Thus, we conclude that pre-alignment serves as a regularization objective rather than a complete learning signal for potential
zero-shot
or
backbone-agnostic
training.

5
Conclusions

In this paper, we investigate VICL and reveal that current methods often struggle to select a single prompt from candidates.
In contrast, we propose collaborating and condensing multiple prompts to foster better context acquisition and generalization.
We design a lightweight external plugin
Condenser
to capture and integrate fine-grained context, which is optimized end-to-end with the backbone.
We conduct comprehensive experiments and showcase
Condenser
’s strengths, including effective context compression, scalability with more prompts, and computational efficiency, suggesting a favorable solution for VICL.
We hope our proposed perspective and preliminary results will encourage more work on effective and efficient VICL.

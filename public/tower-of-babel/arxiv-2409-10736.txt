Numerical Analysis for Neumann Optimal Control Problems on Convex Polyhedral Domains
Johannes Pfefferer, Boris Vexler

Abstract
This paper is concerned with finite element error estimates for Neumann boundary control problems posed on convex and polyhedral domains. Different discretization concepts are considered and for each optimal discretization error estimates are established. In particular, for a full discretization with piecewise linear and globally continuous functions for the control and standard linear finite elements for the state optimal convergence rates for the controls are proven which solely depend on the largest interior edge angle. To be more precise, below the critical edge angle of 2 ​ π / 3 2\pi/3 , a convergence rate of two (times a log-factor) can be achieved for the discrete controls in the L 2 L^{2} -norm on the boundary. For larger interior edge angles the convergence rates are reduced depending on their size, which is due the impact of singular (domain dependent) terms in the solution. The results are comparable to those for the two dimensional case. However, new techniques in this context are used to prove the estimates on the boundary which also extend to the two dimensional case. Moreover, it is shown that the discrete states converge with a rate of two in the L 2 L^{2} -norm in the domain independent of the interior edge angles, i.e. for any convex and polyhedral domain. It is remarkable that this not only holds for a full discretization using piecewise linear and globally continuous functions for the control, but also for a full discretization using piecewise constant functions for the control, where the discrete controls only converge with a rate of one in the L 2 L^{2} -norm on the boundary at best. At the end, the theoretical results are confirmed by several numerical experiments.

Introduction

In this paper we consider the following optimal control problem with control entering the Neumann boundary condition of a linear elliptic equation:

\text{Minimize }J(q,u)=\frac{1}{2}\lVert u-u_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\lVert q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2}

(1a)

over control
q
and state
u
fulfilling the state equation

\displaystyle-\upDelta u+u

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(1b)

\displaystyle\partial_{n}u

\displaystyle=q

\displaystyle\text{on }\partial\Omega,

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a convex polyhedral domain. Such problems are often referred as
Neumann boundary control problems
. The precise functional analytic setting is discussed below.
For simplicity of presentation we do not include inequality constraints on the control variable. However, most of the results can be extended to problems with pointwise control constraints of the form

q_{a}\leq q(s)\leq q_{b}\quad\text{for almost all }s\in\partial\Omega

(2)

with
q_{a},q_{b}\in\mathbb{R}
. We will remark on such extensions below.

The goal of the paper is to provide precise numerical analysis for the finite element discretization of the above boundary control problem.
In particular, this is based on error estimates for an adjoint equation, which is also a linear elliptic equation with homogeneous Neumann boundary condition and an inhomogeneous right-hand side. Thus, we require certain results for the discretization of the equation

\displaystyle-\upDelta z+z

\displaystyle=f

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(3)

\displaystyle\partial_{n}z

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{on }\partial\Omega.

Let
z_{h}
be a Galerkin finite element approximation (with linear finite elements) of
z
. For the error analysis of the control problem error estimates on the boundary are required, i.e. estimates for
\lVert z-z_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}
. The classical result for this error is

\lVert z-z_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq ch^{\frac{3}{2}}\lVert z\rVert_{H^{2}(\Omega)},

which is optimal if we only require the
H^{2}
regularity, i.e.
z\in H^{2}(\Omega)
. Depending on the geometry of the domain and the regularity of the right-hand side
f
higher regularity of
z
can be expected. A natural question is, if it is possible to prove

\lVert z-z_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq ch^{\frac{3}{2}+s}\lVert z\rVert_{H^{2+s}(\Omega)},

(4)

for
0<s<\frac{1}{2}
provided
z\in H^{2+s}(\Omega)
. Although this is a natural extension of the
{\mathcal{O}}(h^{\frac{3}{2}})
result, it does not seem to be available in the literature. We prove an even stronger result, see
Theorem

8
below, i.e.

\lVert z-z_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq ch^{\frac{3}{2}+s}\lVert z\rVert_{H^{2}_{-s}(\Omega)},

(5)

for
0<s<\frac{1}{2}
provided that
z
belongs to a weighted space
H^{2}_{-s}(\Omega)
with the weight being the distance to the boundary. By fractional Hardy inequality, see
Proposition

1
below, there holds
H^{2+s}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow H^{2}_{-s}(\Omega)
and thus the result (
4
) follows. We also provide conditions on
z
to possess the regularity
H^{2}_{-s}(\Omega)
. To this end we consider the critical exponent
\lambda_{\Omega}
depending only on the edge openings of
\partial\Omega
, see
8
below. Then for
s<s_{\Omega}=\min(\lambda_{\Omega}-1,\frac{1}{2})
and
f\in H^{1}(\Omega)
we prove
z\in H^{2}_{-s}(\Omega)
and the corresponding a priori and discretization error estimates. To be more precise, we prove for every
s<s_{\Omega}

\lVert z-z_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq ch^{\frac{3}{2}+s}\lVert f\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)},

see
Corollary

9
. Under the additional assumption that all interior edge angles are smaller then
\frac{2\pi}{3}
we prove

\lVert z-z_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq ch^{2}\lvert\ln h\rvert\lVert f\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)},

see
Corollary

10
. Thus, we can achieve a convergence rate of two (times a
\log
-factor). These two results are comparable to those for the two dimensional case proven in
Apel et al (2015)
;
Pfefferer (2014)
, except that there the right hand side
f
needs to be Hölder continuous and the
\log
-factor has a larger exponent. In addition, our techniques, which are new in this context, seem to be more natural than those in the literature and also extend to the two dimensional case. They are based on weighted error estimates with weight being the (regularized) distance function to the boundary, cf. our recent paper on numerical analysis for Dirichlet control problems
Pfefferer and Vexler (2024)
. The main tool is a weighted best approximation result, see
Theorem

7
, which is also of an independent interest.

We then use the finite element error estimates on the boundary for the adjoint state, to obtain optimal discretization error estimates for the discretization of the optimal control problem. We discuss different discretization concepts: full discretization based on piecewise constant functions for the control and full discretization based on piecewise linear and globally continuous functions for the control. Thereby, the state is always approximated by standard linear finite elements. It is well known that the full discretization with piecewise linear functions coincides in the case of no control bounds with the concept of variational discretization, which was introduced in
Hinze (2005)
for distributed control problems. Our main convergence results for the discrete controls are as follows: Let
\bar{q}
be the solution of the optimal control problem
1
and
\bar{q}_{h}
its discrete counterpart, which either belongs to
Q_{h}^{0}
, the space of piecewise constant functions, or to
V_{h}^{\partial}
the space of piecewise linear and globally continuous functions. Then, in
Theorem

11
we show that

\lVert\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq\begin{cases}ch\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}&\text{if }\bar{q}_{h}\in Q_{h}^{0},\\
ch^{\frac{3}{2}}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}&\text{if }\bar{q}_{h}\in V_{h}^{\partial}.\end{cases}

(6)

Both estimates are optimal if the desired state
u_{d}
only belongs to
L^{2}(\Omega)
. If we assume more regular desired states, such as
u_{d}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, we will show for
\bar{q}_{h}\in V_{h}^{\partial}
the improved convergence estimates

\lVert\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq\begin{cases}ch^{\frac{3}{2}+s}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}&\quad\text{for }0<s<s_{\Omega},\\
ch^{2}\lvert\ln h\rvert\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}&\quad\text{for }\lambda_{\Omega}>\frac{3}{2},\end{cases}

(7)

which are again optimal. Moreover, we show optimal convergence estimates for the states: Let
\bar{u}
be the optimal state, i.e., the solution to
1b
with right hand side
\bar{q}
and let
\bar{u}_{h}
be the optimal discrete state, which solves the corresponding discrete state equation with right hand side
\bar{q}_{h}
. Then we show the estimate

\lVert\bar{u}-\bar{u}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{2}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

i.e., we obtain a convergence rate of two. Let us emphasize that this estimate holds for any convex and polyhedral domain, for desired states
u_{d}
, which only belong to
L^{2}(\Omega)
, and for both discretization strategies.

Before discussing the existing literature on Neumann boundary control problems, let us briefly comment on possible extensions of the results to problems with pointwise inequality constraints on the control variable. All the convergence results for the discrete controls from above can be extended to problems with pointwise control constraints in a certain way. The convergence rates from
6
remain valid. In case of
\bar{q}_{h}\in V_{h}^{\partial}
only a standard structural assumption on the optimal control
\bar{q}
and more regular desired states
u_{d}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
with
p>3
are required in addition. The estimates of
7
can be transferred to the concept of variational discretization, which was already mentioned above, and to the postprocessing approach, which was introduced in
Meyer and Rösch (2004)
for distributed optimal control problems. For the latter approach, one also requires the structural assumption on the optimal control
\bar{q}
and more regular desired states
u_{d}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
with
p>3
.
In contrast, the convergence result for the states heavily relies on the fact that no control bounds are present. Its proof is based on a duality argument, where the dual problem is an optimal control problem with a certain desired state in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, and the estimates of
6
.

Let us briefly review the existing literature. First results on numerical analysis for Neumann boundary control problems can be found in
Geveci (1979)
. A full discretization using piecewise constant functions for the control is considered in
Geveci (1979)
;
Casas et al (2005)
. A full discretization based on piecewise linear and globally continuous functions for the control is discussed in
Casas and Mateos (2008)
. The concept of variational discretization applied to Neumann boundary control problems can be found in
Casas and Mateos (2008)
;
Hinze and Matthes (2009)
;
Pfefferer (2014)
. The postprocessing approach applied to Neumann boundary control problems is analyzed in
Mateos and Rösch (2011)
;
Apel et al (2015)
;
Krumbiegel and Pfefferer (2015)
;
Pfefferer (2014)
;
Apel et al (2016a)
;
Apel et al (2018)
.
We note that in
Casas et al (2005)
;
Casas and Mateos (2008)
;
Krumbiegel and Pfefferer (2015)
;
Pfefferer (2014)
not only linear quadratic Neumann boundary control problems are considered but even problems with semilinear state equation. In
Beuchler et al (2015)
;
Wachsmuth and Wurst (2016)
, a boundary concentrated finite element method for Neumann boundary control problems is introduced and analyzed. The discussion of Neumann boundary control problems with controls in
H^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)
and the corresponding numerical analysis can be found in
Apel et al (2016b)
.
Most likely, the results from
Apel et al (2015)
;
Pfefferer (2014)
;
Krumbiegel and Pfefferer (2015)
;
Apel et al (2018)
can be compared best with ours in a certain sense. In those references quasi-optimal discretization error estimates are proven for the post-processing approach employing quasi-optimal finite element error estimates in the
L^{2}
-norm on the boundary. Moreover, in
Pfefferer (2014)
quasi-optimal discretization error estimates are established for the concept of variational discretization. Whereas the references
Apel et al (2015)
;
Pfefferer (2014)
;
Krumbiegel and Pfefferer (2015)
are restricted to polygonal domains, in
Apel et al (2018)
polyhedral domains are considered. In those references even gradually refined meshes are used to retain the full order of convergence.
However, in
Apel et al (2018)
it was not explicitly discovered that the convergence rate in convex domains solely depend on the size of the largest interior edge angle and hence corner singularities are not relevant. Moreover, as already mentioned above for the 2D case, the
\log
-factor is slightly worse there compared to our result. We also think that our new approach to prove the finite element error estimates on the boundary might stimulate further research activities in this direction.

5
, are collected in
Section

6
.

H^{1}(\Omega)=W^{1,2}_{0,0}(\Omega)
. This holds due to the trivial embedding
W^{1,2}_{0,0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow H^{1}(\Omega)
and the embedding
W^{1,2}_{0,0}(\Omega)\hookleftarrow H^{1}(\Omega)
which is a consequence of the Hardy inequality (see e.g.
(
Maz’ya and Rossmann, 2010
, (2.2.9) on page 35)
). Thus,
H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(F)=W^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},2}_{0,0}(F)
for all faces
F\in{\cal F}(\Omega)
as both spaces are the corresponding natural trace spaces on the faces.

(II)

This condition is satisfied according to
(
Kozlov et al, 2001
, Section 2.3.1)
, i.e., we get that the strip
-1<\operatorname{Re}\lambda<0
is free of eigenvalues of
{\cal U}_{v}(\lambda)
and
\lambda=-1
and
\lambda=0
are simple eigenvalues with constant eigenfunctions.

(III)

According to
(
Maz’ya and Rossmann, 2010
, Section 8.3.5)
the smallest positive eigenvalue
\Lambda_{v}
of the operator pencils
{\cal U}_{v}(\lambda)
is greater than
1
if
\Omega
is convex. Consequently, the inequality
\frac{1}{2}<\min(1,\Lambda_{v})
is fulfilled.

(IV)

As
\Omega
is convex, we have
\delta_{+}^{e}=\pi/\omega_{e}>1
and therefore
\max(0,2-\delta_{+}^{e})<1
.

The a priori estimate of the second assertion is a consequence of (I) and
(
Maz’ya and Rossmann, 2010
, Theoren 6.5.4 and Corollary 7.4.2)
together with
(
Maz’ya and Rossmann, 2010
, Lemma 8.1.2)
and an appropriate partition of unity of the domain.
∎

Theorem 4
.

Let
f\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and
g\equiv 0
. Moreover, let
s_{\Omega}
be defined by

s_{\Omega}=\min\left(\lambda_{\Omega}-1,\frac{1}{2}\right)\in\bigg(0,\frac{1}{2}\bigg],

(13)

where
\lambda_{\Omega}>1
is the critical exponent
8
.
Then, for every
0<s<s_{\Omega}
, the solution
u
to
10
possesses the regularity
u\in H^{2}_{-s}(\Omega)
and the estimate

\lVert u\rVert_{H^{2}_{-s}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{c}{1-2s}\lVert f\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

holds with a constant
c>0
depending only on
\Omega
.

Remark 1
.

In order to prove this result, we proceed in principal similar to the proof of
(
Pfefferer and Vexler, 2024
, Theorem 7)
, which deals with a comparable result, however, for a problem with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions. This, at first glance, minor difference makes the analysis in our case more involved. To be more precise, solutions to problems in polyhedral domains with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions belong to weighted Sobolev spaces with (purely) homogeneous norm with weight being the distance to the singular points on the boundary, i.e., the corners and the edges. In contrast, solutions of problems with homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions require different weighted Sobolev spaces (see also the proof of
Theorem

3
).

Proof.

Let us first elucidate, how the regularity result of the assertion, where we have a weighted regularity with weight being the distance to the boundary, can be traced back to regularity results in weighted Sobolev spaces mentioned above.
Due to the definition of the weighted spaces
H^{2}_{-s}(\Omega)
, we have

\lVert u\rVert_{H^{2}_{-s}(\Omega)}\leq\lVert\rho^{-s}u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\rho^{-s}\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\rho^{-s}\nabla^{2}u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

We notice that
\rho^{1-s}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
(definitely for
s<1/2
). As a consequence, we deduce from
Proposition

2
(applied to each of the terms on the right hand side) that

\displaystyle\lVert u\rVert_{H^{2}_{-s}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{1-2s}\left(\lVert u\rVert_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\rho^{1-s}\nabla^{3}u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{1-2s}\left(\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\rho^{1-s}\nabla^{3}u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right),

where the last step follows from
Theorem

3
.
Next, recall that
r_{e}
denotes the distance function to the set of edges
{\cal E}(\Omega)
. As
\rho(x)\leq r_{e}(x)
for all
x\in\Omega
and
1-s>0
, we get for the term involving third derivatives that

\lVert\rho^{1-s}\nabla^{3}u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\lVert r_{e}^{1-s}\nabla^{3}u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

To bound this term, we will apply
(
Maz’ya and Rossmann, 2010
, Theorem 8.1.7)
.
However, this theorem is not directly applicable, as it requires, in our case, the solution to be zero at corner points, which does not hold in general for our problem. Instead, we will apply the results to a modified solution where we can guarantee that this modified solution is zero at corner points.
Let
\eta\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\to[0,1]
be a smooth cut off function which is equal to one in a neighborhood of zero and which has a support in
[0,1)
. Further, we define smooth cut off functions
\chi_{v}
for each corner
v\in{\cal V}(\Omega)
by

\chi_{v}(x)=\eta(\lvert x-v\rvert).

Without loss of generality we assume that the support of each function
\chi_{v}
is contained in
\Omega
and
\chi_{v}
vanishes in the neighborhood of any other corner. There holds

\nabla\chi_{v}(x)=\frac{\eta^{\prime}(\lvert x-v\rvert)}{\lvert x-v\rvert}(x-v)\quad\text{and}\quad\partial_{n}\chi_{v}\equiv 0\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

Next, we introduce the modified solution. We define the function
\tilde{u}
by

\tilde{u}=u+u_{\text{v}}\quad\text{with}\quad u_{\text{v}}=-\sum_{v\in{\cal V}(\Omega)}u(v)\chi_{v},

which solves

\displaystyle-\Delta\tilde{u}+\tilde{u}

\displaystyle=\tilde{f}

\displaystyle\quad\text{in }\Omega,

\displaystyle\partial_{n}\tilde{u}

\displaystyle=\tilde{g}

\displaystyle\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.

with

\tilde{f}=f-\Delta u_{\text{v}}+u_{\text{v}}\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{g}=\partial_{n}u+\partial_{n}u_{\text{v}}=\partial_{n}u-\sum_{v\in{\cal V}(\Omega)}u(v)\partial_{n}\chi_{v}=0.

By the Sobolev inequality and
Theorem

3
, we get

\displaystyle\lVert r_{e}^{1-s}\nabla^{3}u_{\text{v}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in{\cal V}(\Omega)}\lvert u(v)\rvert\,\lVert r_{e}^{1-s}\nabla^{3}\chi_{v}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq c\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert u\rVert_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

As a consequence, there holds

\lVert r_{e}^{1-s}\nabla^{3}u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\lVert r_{e}^{1-s}\nabla^{3}\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert r_{e}^{1-s}\nabla^{3}u_{\text{v}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\lVert r_{e}^{1-s}\nabla^{3}\tilde{u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+c\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

In summary, we still have to bound third derivatives in a weighted
L^{2}(\Omega)
-norm. However, in contrast to before, the function
\tilde{u}
is equal to zero at each corner point and we can apply
(
Maz’ya and Rossmann, 2010
, Theorem 8.1.7)
to get the desired term bounded. This is done next.
Let us note that the function space setting, which we need for the subsequent steps, has already been introduced in the proof of
Theorem

3
.
From
(
Maz’ya and Rossmann, 2010
, Theorem 8.1.7)
with
I_{0}=\emptyset
,
p,q=2
,
l=3
,
\beta_{j}^{\prime}=1-s
,
\delta_{k}^{\prime}=1-s
,
\beta_{j}=\beta\in(-1,-1/2)
,
\delta_{k}=\delta\in(-1,0)
for all
j,k
,
we now obtain
\tilde{u}\in W_{1-s,1-s}^{3,2}(\Omega)
and especially the boundedness of
r_{e}^{1-s}\nabla^{3}\tilde{u}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
if the following conditions are satisfied:

(I)

The solution
\tilde{u}
belongs to
W^{1,2}_{\beta,\delta}(\Omega)
.

(II)

The right hand side
\tilde{f}
fulfills
\tilde{f}\in W_{1-s,1-s}^{1,2}(\Omega)
.

(III)

The closed strip between the lines
\operatorname{Re}\lambda=-\beta-\frac{1}{2}
and
\operatorname{Re}\lambda=\frac{1}{2}+s
is free of eigenvalues of the operator pencil
{\cal U}_{v}(\lambda)
(see the proof of
Theorem

3
for details).

(IV)

The inequalities

\max(0,1-\delta_{+}^{e})<\delta+1<1\quad\text{and}\quad\max(0,3-\delta_{+}^{e})<2-s<3

(14)

with
\delta_{+}^{e}=\pi/\omega_{e}
hold for all edges
e\in{\cal E}(\Omega)
.

We now check that all of these requirements are fulfilled if
f\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and if
\Omega
is convex.

(I)

There holds

\lVert-\Delta u_{\text{v}}+u_{\text{v}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\sum_{v\in{\cal V}(\Omega)}\lvert u(v)\rvert\lVert-\Delta\chi_{v}+\chi_{v}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\sum_{v\in{\cal V}(\Omega)}\lvert u(v)\rvert\leq c\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Consequently, the function
\tilde{f}=f-\Delta u_{\text{v}}+u_{\text{v}}
fulfills due to the Sobolev inequality and elliptic regularity of
u
(see
Theorem

3
)

\lVert\tilde{f}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+c\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+c\lVert u\rVert_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Hence, by elliptic regularity of
\tilde{u}
(see
Theorem

3
), we obtain

\lVert\tilde{u}\rVert_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

By
(
Maz’ya and Rossmann, 2010
, Lemma 8.1.2)
with arbitrary
\beta,\delta>-1
and by Hardy’s inequality (see e.g.
(
Maz’ya and Rossmann, 2010
, (2.2.9) on page 35)
) together with the fact that
\tilde{u}
vanishes at corner points, we deduce

\lVert\tilde{u}\rVert_{W^{1,2}_{\beta,\delta}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert\tilde{u}\rVert_{W^{2,2}_{0,0}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert\tilde{u}\rVert_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

such that
\tilde{u}
belongs to
W^{1,2}_{\beta,\delta}(\Omega)
.

(II)

Due to Hardy’s inequality (see e.g.
(
Maz’ya and Rossmann, 2010
, (2.2.9) on page 35)
) we have

\displaystyle\lVert\tilde{f}\rVert_{W_{1-s,1-s}^{1,2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\lVert\tilde{f}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\lVert r_{e}^{1-s}\nabla\tilde{f}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\leq c\lVert\tilde{f}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)},

where we also used the boundedness of
r_{e}^{1-s}
.
Similar to before, the Sobolev inequality and
Theorem

3
then imply

\lVert\tilde{f}\rVert_{W_{1-s,1-s}^{1,2}(\Omega)}\leq c\left(\lVert f\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)\leq c\left(\lVert f\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\lVert u\rVert_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\leq c\lVert f\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

(III)

As we have seen in the proof of
Theorem

3
the smallest positive eigenvalue
\Lambda_{v}
of the operator pencil
{\cal U}_{v}(\lambda)
is greater than

if
\Omega
is convex. Thus, the closed strip between the lines
\operatorname{Re}\lambda=-\beta-\frac{1}{2}
and
\operatorname{Re}\lambda=\frac{1}{2}+s
is free of eigenvalues as

and

.

(IV)

Due to the convexity of the domain (
\pi/\omega_{e}>1
for all

) the first condition in
14
trivially holds for
\delta\in(-1,0)
.
For

we get

\max\left(0,3-\frac{\pi}{\omega}_{e}\right)<\max\left(\frac{3}{2},3-\frac{\pi}{\omega}_{e}\right)=2-\min\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{\pi}{\omega}_{e}-1\right)\leq 2-s_{\Omega}<2-s<3,

which is the second condition in

.

Finally, the a priori estimate of the assertion is a consequence of the previous inequalities and
(
Maz’ya and Rossmann, 2010
, Theorem 6.5.4 and Corollary 7.4.2)
together with an appropriate partition of unity of the domain.
∎

3
Optimal control problem.

In this section we discuss the optimal control problem (
1
) and provide optimality conditions. If not explicitly mentioned we make the minimal assumption
u_{d}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.

We call
Q=L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
the control space and introduce the solution operator
S\colon Q\to H^{1}(\Omega)
mapping a control
q\in Q
to the weak solution
u=u(q)
of
10
with
g=q
and
f=0
. Using this solution operator we define the reduced cost functional

j\colon Q\to\mathbb{R},\quad j(q)=J(q,Sq).

Thus, we formulate the optimal control problem as:

\text{Minimize }j(q),\quad q\in Q.

(15)

It is straightforward to check, that the reduced cost functional
j
is continuous and strictly convex. Thus, the existence and uniqueness of an optimal solution
\bar{q}\in Q
follows by standard arguments. We refer to
\bar{u}=S\bar{q}
as the optimal state.

The functional
j
is two times Fréchet differentiable. For a control
q\in Q
and a direction
\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\in Q
the directional derivatives are
given by

j^{\prime}(q)(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)=(u-u_{d},\delta\mspace{-2mu}u)+\alpha(q,\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)_{\partial\Omega}

and

j^{\prime\prime}(q)(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q,\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)=\lVert\delta\mspace{-2mu}u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\alpha\lVert\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2},

where
u=Sq
and
\delta\mspace{-2mu}u=S\delta\mspace{-2mu}q
.

The necessary optimality condition for (
15
) is given as

\bar{q}\in Q\quad:\quad j^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)=0\quad\text{for all }\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\in Q.

(16)

By convexity of
j
this condition is also sufficient for the optimality.

To derive the optimality system we require an adjoint based representation of
j^{\prime}(q)(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)
. To this end we introduce the corresponding adjoint equation. For a given control
q\in Q
and corresponding state
u=u(q)\in H^{1}(\Omega)

we introduce the adjoint state
z=z(q)\in H^{1}(\Omega)
as the weak solution of the adjoint equation

\displaystyle-\upDelta z+z

\displaystyle=u-u_{d}

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

\displaystyle\partial_{n}z

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{on }\partial\Omega,

with the corresponding weak formulation

z\in H^{1}(\Omega)\quad:\quad(\nabla\varphi,\nabla z)+(\varphi,z)=(u-u_{d},\varphi)\quad\text{for all }\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega).

(17)

By
H^{2}
regularity from
Theorem

3
we get
z\in H^{2}(\Omega)
. A straightforward calculation provides an expressions for the directional derivative of
j
based on the solution of this adjoint equation:

j^{\prime}(q)(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)=(\alpha q+z|_{\partial\Omega},\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)_{\partial\Omega},

where
q,\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\in Q
,
u=u(q)\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and
z=z(q)\in H^{2}(\Omega)
solves the adjoint equation (
17
). This consideration results in the following optimality system.

Theorem 5
.

A control
\bar{q}\in Q
is the solution of the optimal control problem
(
15
) if and only if the triple
(\bar{q},\bar{u},\bar{z})

with
\bar{q}\in Q
,
\bar{u}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and
\bar{z}\in H^{2}(\Omega)
fulfills the following system:

•

State equation:

(\nabla\bar{u},\nabla\varphi)+(\bar{u},\varphi)=(\bar{q},\varphi)\quad\text{for all }\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)

(18a)

•

Adjoint equation:

(\nabla\varphi,\nabla\bar{z})+(\varphi,\bar{z})=(\bar{u}-u_{d},\varphi)\quad\text{for all }\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)\\

(18b)

•

Optimality condition:

\alpha q+z|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

(18c)

Moreover, there holds
\bar{q}\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega)
and there exists a constant
c>0
depending only on
\alpha
and
\Omega
such that

\lVert\bar{q}\rVert_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}+\lVert\bar{u}\rVert_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\bar{z}\rVert_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

It only remains to prove the regularity result. As already discussed before the optimal adjoint state belongs to
H^{2}(\Omega)
. Due to the trace estimate from
(
Nečas, 2012
, Theorem 4.11)
and
18c
this implies
\bar{q}\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega)
and consequently
\bar{u}\in H^{2}(\Omega)
according to the elliptic regularity from
Theorem

3
.
∎

4
Discretization of the state/adjoint equation.

For discretization we consider the space of linear finite elements
\widehat{V}_{h}\subset H^{1}(\Omega)
defined on a mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
from a family of shape regular quasi-uniform meshes, see, e.g.,
Brenner and Scott (2008)
. The mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}=\{K\}
consists of cells
K
, which are open tetrahedrons. Moreover, we let
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\partial}=\{F\}
be the mesh on the boundary of
\Omega
consisting of open triangles
F
(the faces of the corresponding tetrahedrons), which is naturally induced by
\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
We also use the space
V_{h}^{\partial}
of traces of functions from
\widehat{V}_{h}
, i.e.

V_{h}^{\partial}=\Set{\tau v_{h}}{v_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h}},

(19)

where
\tau\colon H^{1}(\Omega)\to H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)
is the trace operator.

The equation (
10
) is discretized as follows. For given
g\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
and
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
the discrete solution
u_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h}
fulfills

u_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h}\quad:\quad(\nabla u_{h},\nabla\varphi_{h})+(u_{h},\varphi_{h})=(f,\varphi_{h})+(g,\varphi_{h})_{\partial\Omega}\quad\text{for all }\varphi_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h}.

(20)

We define the Neumann Ritz projection
R_{h}\colon H^{1}(\Omega)\to\widehat{V}_{h}
by the following orthogonality relation:

(\nabla(u-R_{h}u),\nabla\varphi_{h})+(u-R_{h}u,\varphi_{h})=0\quad\text{for all }\varphi_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h}.

Thus, for
u
solving (
10
) we have that
u_{h}=R_{h}u
is the solution of (
20
).

The following standard estimate requires only
H^{2}(\Omega)
regularity of the solution
u
of (
10
).

Theorem 6
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
g\in H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(F)
for all faces
F\in{\cal F}(\Omega)
and
u
be the solution of (
10
). Let
u_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h}
be the solution of (
20
). Then the following estimates hold

\displaystyle\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq ch\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq ch\inf_{v_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h}}\lVert u-v_{h}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq ch^{2}\left(\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\sum_{F\in{\cal F}(\Omega)}\lVert g\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(F)}\right)

with a constant
c>0
only depending on
\Omega
.

Proof.

This is a standard result in the theory of finite element methods using the regularity result from
Theorem

3
, see, e.g.,
(
Brenner and Scott, 2008
, Theorem (5.7.6) and eq. (5.7.5))
.
∎

Based on the finite element error estimate, the following stability estimate can be shown.

Lemma 1
.

Let
u_{h}
solve
20
with
f\equiv 0
and
g\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
. There is the estimate

\lVert u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\left(h\lVert g\rVert_{H^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)}+\lVert g\rVert_{H^{-1}(\partial\Omega)}\right)

with a constant
c>0
only depending on
\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
w\in H^{1}(\Omega)
be the weak solution of

\displaystyle-\upDelta w+w

\displaystyle=u_{h}

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

\displaystyle\partial_{n}w

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{on }\partial\Omega,

and let
w_{h}=R_{h}w\in\widehat{V}_{h}
its Neumann Ritz projection.
There holds

\displaystyle\lVert u_{h}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=(\nabla w_{h},\nabla u_{h})+(w_{h},u_{h})=(g,w_{h})_{\partial\Omega}=(g,w_{h}-w)_{\partial\Omega}+(g,w)_{\partial\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert g\rVert_{H^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)}\lVert w-w_{h}\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)}+\lVert g\rVert_{H^{-1}(\partial\Omega)}\lVert w\rVert_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}.

The standard trace estimate and
Theorem

6

yield

\lVert w-w_{h}\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)}\leq c\lVert w-w_{h}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq ch\lVert u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Using the trace estimate from
(
Nečas, 2012
, Theorem 4.11)
and part 2 of
Theorem

3
, we get

\lVert w\rVert_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}\leq c\lVert w\rVert_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
g=0
and
u
be the solution of (
10
). Moreover, let
u_{h}=R_{h}u
be its Neumann Ritz projection. Then there holds

\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{H^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)}+h^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq ch^{2}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

with a positive constant
c
only depending on
\Omega
.

Proof.

Let us start with the
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
-estimate. This is a direct consequence of the trace theorem from
(
Brenner and Scott, 2008
, Theorem 1.6.6)
and the finite element error estimate from
Theorem

6
, i.e.,

\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq ch^{\frac{3}{2}}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

For the second estimate, let
w\in H^{1}(\Omega)
be the weak solution of

\displaystyle-\upDelta w+w

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

\displaystyle\partial_{n}w

\displaystyle=\delta

\displaystyle\text{on }\partial\Omega,

with some
\delta\in H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)
and let
w_{h}=R_{h}w\in\widehat{V}_{h}
its Neumann Ritz projection. There holds due the Galerkin orthogonality and
Theorem

6

\displaystyle(\delta,u-u_{h})_{\partial\Omega}

\displaystyle=(\nabla(w-w_{h}),\nabla(u-u_{h}))+(w-w_{h},u-u_{h})

\displaystyle\leq\lVert w-w_{h}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq ch^{2}\lVert\delta\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

As a consequence we obtain

\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{H^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)}=\sup_{\delta\in H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega),\,\delta\not\equiv 0}\frac{(\delta,u-u_{h})_{\partial\Omega}}{\lVert\delta\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)}}\leq ch^{2}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

∎

The latter result is optimal in case that
H^{2}(\Omega)
regularity of the solution
u
is available only. If the solution is more regular, a higher convergence rate is possible in
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
. In order to show the improved error estimates we require a best approximation result with respect to a weighted norm. To this end we define a regularized weight
\tilde{\rho}\colon\bar{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
by

\quad\tilde{\rho}(x)=\sqrt{\rho(x)^{2}+h^{2}}.

(21)

By a direct calculation we obtain

\lvert\nabla\tilde{\rho}\rvert\leq c\quad\text{and}\quad\lvert\nabla^{2}\tilde{\rho}\rvert\leq c\tilde{\rho}^{-1}.

(22)

A direct consequence of the boundedness of the gradient of
\tilde{\rho}
is the existence of a constant
c
independent of
h
such that the following estimate hold

\max_{x\in\bar{K}}\tilde{\rho}(x)\leq c\min_{x\in\bar{K}}\tilde{\rho}(x)\quad\text{for all }K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}.

(23)

This leads to weighted interpolation estimates for the Lagrange interpolation
i_{h}\colon C(\bar{\Omega})\to\widehat{V}_{h}
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
be arbitrary. Then there is a constant
c>0
independent of
h
such that

\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}(v-i_{h}v)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+h\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}\nabla(v-i_{h}v)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{2}\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}\nabla^{2}v\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

holds for all
v\in H^{2}(\Omega)
.

The next result is a superconvergence type estimate, see
(
Pfefferer and Vexler, 2024
, Lemma 4)
. A similar result for another weight (regularized distance to a point) can be found, e.g., in
(
Leykekhman and Vexler, 2016
, Lemma 3)
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
be arbitrary. There is a constant
c>0
independent of
h
such that

\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}(\operatorname{Id}-i_{h})(\tilde{\rho}^{\beta}v_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+h\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha}\nabla(\operatorname{Id}-i_{h})(\tilde{\rho}^{\beta}v_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\alpha+\beta-1}v_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

holds for all
v_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h}
.

The next lemma provides a weighted stability result for the Neumann Ritz projection.

Lemma 5
.

Let
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and
u_{h}=R_{h}u\in\widehat{V}_{h}
be its Neumann Ritz projection. There holds

\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\left(\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)

with a constant
c>0
independent of
h
.

Proof.

We obtain by introducing intermediate terms

\displaystyle\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=(\tilde{\rho}u_{h},u_{h})+(\nabla(\tilde{\rho}u_{h}),\nabla u_{h})-(u_{h}\nabla\tilde{\rho},\nabla u_{h})

\displaystyle=((\operatorname{Id}-i_{h})(\tilde{\rho}u_{h}),u_{h})+(\nabla(\operatorname{Id}-i_{h})(\tilde{\rho}u_{h}),\nabla u_{h})+(i_{h}(\tilde{\rho}u_{h}),u_{h})+(\nabla i_{h}(\tilde{\rho}u_{h}),\nabla u_{h})-(u_{h}\nabla\tilde{\rho},\nabla u_{h})

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}+I_{5}.

For
I_{1}+I_{2}
we get with
Lemma

4

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Id}-i_{h})(\tilde{\rho}u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(\operatorname{Id}-i_{h})(\tilde{\rho}u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq c\left(h\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq c\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\left(\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right).

By definition of the Neumann Ritz projection and
Lemma

4
we have for
I_{3}+I_{4}

\displaystyle I_{3}+I_{4}

\displaystyle=(i_{h}(\tilde{\rho}u_{h}),u)+(\nabla i_{h}(\tilde{\rho}u_{h}),\nabla u)

\displaystyle=((i_{h}-\operatorname{Id})(\tilde{\rho}u_{h}),u)+(\nabla(i_{h}-\operatorname{Id})(\tilde{\rho}u_{h}),\nabla u)+(\tilde{\rho}u_{h},u)+(\nabla(\tilde{\rho}u_{h}),\nabla u)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Id}-i_{h})(\tilde{\rho}u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(\operatorname{Id}-i_{h})(\tilde{\rho}u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(\tilde{\rho}u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(ch\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(c\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}u_{h}\nabla\tilde{\rho}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Since
\tilde{\rho}\geq h
and
\lvert\nabla\tilde{\rho}\rvert\leq c
we further deduce

\displaystyle I_{3}+I_{4}

\displaystyle\leq c\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+c\left(\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{4}\left(\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right).

For
I_{5}
we obtain by
\lvert\nabla\tilde{\rho}\rvert\leq c

I_{5}\leq\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}u_{h}\nabla\tilde{\rho}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

Putting terms together and absorbing the terms involving
\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{h}
and
\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u_{h}
into the left-hand side we complete the proof.
∎

The next theorem provides a best approximation result in weighted norms, which will be used in the sequel, but seems also to be of independent interest.

Theorem 7
.

Let
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and
u_{h}=R_{h}u\in\widehat{V}_{h}
be its Neumann Ritz projection. There is a constant
c>0
independent of
h
such that

\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(u-u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(u-u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
\leq c\left(\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(u-v_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(u-v_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)

(24)

for every
v_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h}
.

Proof.

We first observe that for every
v_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h}
there holds

\displaystyle\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(u-u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(u-v_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(v_{h}-u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Application of
Lemma

5
with
v_{h}-u_{h}=R_{h}(v_{h}-u)
results in the following estimate for the last term:

\displaystyle\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(v_{h}-u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(u-v_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}(u-v_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(v_{h}-u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(u-v_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(u-v_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(u-u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right),

where we inserted
u
as an intermediate function and employed
\max_{x\in\Omega}\lvert\tilde{\rho}\rvert\leq c
.
It remains to appropriately estimate
\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(u-u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
. To this end, we simply use the fact that
(\tilde{\rho}(x))^{-1}\leq h^{-1}
for all
x\in\Omega
and
Theorem

6
. This yields

\displaystyle\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(u-u_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq ch^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert u-v_{h}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(u-v_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(u-v_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right),

where we also used
\max_{x\in\Omega}\lvert\tilde{\rho}\rvert\leq c
in the last step. Collecting the different estimates ends the proof.
∎

The next theorem provides an improved error estimate for
u-u_{h}
on the boundary
\partial\Omega
under the regularity assumption
u\in H^{2}_{-s}(\Omega)
.

Theorem 8
.

Let
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
possess additional weighted regularity
u\in H^{2}_{-s}(\Omega)
for some
0<s<\frac{1}{2}
. Let
u_{h}=R_{h}u\in\widehat{V}_{h}
be its Neumann Ritz projection. Then there holds the following estimate

\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq ch^{\frac{3}{2}+s}\lVert u\rVert_{H^{2}_{-s}(\Omega)}

with a constant
c>0
only depending on
\Omega
.

Proof.

We apply a duality argument. Let
z\in H^{1}(\Omega)
be the solution of

(\nabla\varphi,\nabla z)+(\varphi,z)=(u-u_{h},\varphi)_{\partial\Omega}\quad\forall\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega).

Moreover, let
z_{h}=R_{h}z
be the corresponding discrete solution, i.e.

z_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h}\quad:\quad(\nabla\varphi_{h},\nabla z_{h})+(\varphi_{h},z_{h})=(u-u_{h},\varphi_{h})_{\partial\Omega}\quad\forall\varphi_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h}.

We obtain

\displaystyle\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=(\nabla(u-u_{h}),\nabla z)+(u-u_{h},z)=(\nabla(u-i_{h}u),\nabla(z-z_{h}))+(u-i_{h}u,z-z_{h}),

where we used Galerkin orthogonality for both
u-u_{h}
and
z-z_{h}
. We insert positive and negative powers of
\tilde{\rho}
, use the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the boundedness of
\tilde{\rho}
in
\Omega
such that

\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2}\leq c\left(\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(u-i_{h}u)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(u-i_{h}u)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\\
\times\left(\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(z-z_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(z-z_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right).

(25)

To estimate the weighted interpolation error we use
Lemma

3
leading to

\displaystyle\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(u-i_{h}u)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(u-i_{h}u)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla^{2}u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=ch\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\rho}^{-s}\nabla^{2}u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq chh^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-s}\nabla^{2}u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{\frac{1}{2}+s}\lVert\rho^{-s}\nabla^{2}u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

where we used
\tilde{\rho}\geq h
and therefore
\tilde{\rho}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\leq ch^{s-\frac{1}{2}}
(since
s<\frac{1}{2}
) as well as
\tilde{\rho}\geq\rho
and therefore
\tilde{\rho}^{-s}\leq\rho^{-s}
(since
s>0
).
The weighted error of
z
is estimated using
Theorem

7
with the Scott-Zhang interpolation
v_{h}=\tilde{i}_{h}z
, see
Scott and Zhang (1990)
, as

\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(z-z_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(z-z_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
\leq c\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(z-\tilde{i}_{h}z)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+c\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(z-\tilde{i}_{h}z)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(26)

To estimate the above weighted interpolation errors we introduce subdomains (strips)

B_{h}=\Set{x\in\Omega}{\rho(x)\leq h}\quad\text{and}\quad B_{bh}=\Set{x\in\Omega}{\rho(x)\leq bh},

where
b>1
is chosen large enough, such that for every cell
K
with
K\cap(\Omega\setminus B_{bh})\neq\emptyset
with the corresponding patch
\omega_{K}
, there holds

\operatorname{dist}(\omega_{K},\partial\Omega)\geq h.

We obtain

\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(z-\tilde{i}_{h}z)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(z-\tilde{i}_{h}z)\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{bh})}^{2}+\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(z-\tilde{i}_{h}z)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus B_{bh})}^{2}.

For the error on the strip
B_{bh}
we use
\tilde{\rho}\leq ch
and obtain

\displaystyle\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(z-\tilde{i}_{h}z)\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{bh})}

\displaystyle\leq ch^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\nabla(z-\tilde{i}_{h}z)\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{bh})}\leq ch^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\nabla(z-\tilde{i}_{h}z)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq ch\lVert z\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}(\Omega)}\leq ch\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)},

where we used a fractional order interpolation error estimate and the
H^{\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}(\Omega)
regularity for
z
from
Theorem

3
. For the error on the complement of
B_{bh}
we can use weighted interpolation estimates for the Scott-Zhang interpolation, similar to
Lemma

3
, and obtain

\displaystyle\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla(z-\tilde{i}_{h}z)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus B_{bh})}

\displaystyle\leq ch\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla^{2}z\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus B_{h})}\leq ch\lVert\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla^{2}z\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus B_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq ch\lVert\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla^{2}z\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)},

where we used
\rho(x)\geq ch
on
\Omega\setminus B_{h}
and therefore
\tilde{\rho}(x)\leq c\rho(x)
. Moreover, we used the weighted regularity from
Theorem

3
. The second term on the right-hand side of
26
is simply estimated using
\tilde{\rho}\geq ch
as

\displaystyle\lVert\tilde{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(z-\tilde{i}_{h}z)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq ch^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lVert z-\tilde{i}_{h}z\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch\lVert z\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}(\Omega)}\leq ch\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)},

where we again used a fractional order interpolation error estimate and the
H^{\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}(\Omega)
regularity for
z
from
Theorem

3
.
Putting terms together, inserting the weighted interpolation error estimates for
z
into
26
and then the result and the weighted interpolation error estimates for
u
into
25
yields the assertion after dividing by
\lVert u-u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}
.
∎

5
Discretization of the optimal control problem.

To discretize the optimal control problem (
15
) we introduce the discrete solution operator
S_{h}\colon Q\to\widehat{V}_{h}
mapping a given control
q\in Q
to the solution
u_{h}=u_{h}(q)
of
20
with
g=q
and
f=0
. Then, the discrete reduced cost functional is given as

j_{h}\colon Q\to\mathbb{R},\quad j_{h}(q)=J(q,S_{h}q).

We discuss different discretization concepts for the control: cell-wise constant discretization, and cell-wise linear but globally continuous discretization. We also note that in case of no control constraints the latter discretization approach coincides with the concept of variational discretization.
In case of the cell-wise constant discretization we choose

Q_{h}=Q_{h}^{0}:=\Set{q\in Q}{q|_{F}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(F),\,\forall F\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\partial}}

with
\mathcal{P}_{0}(F)
denoting the spaces of constant functions on
F
. For the cell-wise linear but globally continuous discretization we set
Q_{h}=V_{h}^{\partial}
, see
19
for the definition of
V_{h}^{\partial}
.
For any
q\in Q
, we also define the
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
-projection
\pi_{h}\colon L^{2}(\partial\Omega)\to Q_{h}
by

(\pi_{h}q-q,q_{h})_{\partial\Omega}=0\quad\text{for all }q_{h}\in Q_{h}.

Depending on the specific choice of the space
Q_{h}
, the considerations from above lead to the discrete problem:

\text{Minimize }j_{h}(q_{h}),\quad q_{h}\in Q_{h}.

(27)

Completely analogously to the continuous problem one can show that the functional
j_{h}
is continuous and strictly convex. As a consequence, the existence and uniqueness of a discrete optimal control
\bar{q}_{h}\in Q_{h}
follows by standard arguments for each of the discretization concepts.
The functional
j_{h}
is two times Fréchet differentiable. For a control
q\in Q
and a direction
\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\in Q
the directional derivatives are
given by

j_{h}^{\prime}(q)(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)=(u_{h}-u_{d},\delta\mspace{-2mu}u_{h})+\alpha(q,\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)_{\partial\Omega}

and

j_{h}^{\prime\prime}(q)(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q,\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)=\lVert\delta\mspace{-2mu}u_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\alpha\lVert\delta\mspace{-2mu}q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2},

where
u_{h}=S_{h}q
and
\delta\mspace{-2mu}u_{h}=S_{h}\delta\mspace{-2mu}q
. In order to get an adjoint representation of
j_{h}^{\prime}(q)(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)
we introduce a discrete adjoint state
z_{h}
. As before, let
q\in Q
and
u_{h}=S_{h}q
. We define the discrete adjoint state
z_{h}
by

z_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h}\quad:\quad(\nabla\varphi_{h},\nabla z_{h})+(\varphi_{h},z_{h})=(u_{h}-u_{d},\varphi_{h})\quad\text{for all }\varphi_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h}.

(28)

As a consequence, we can reformulate the directional derivative of
j_{h}
by

j_{h}^{\prime}(q)(\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)=(\alpha q+z_{h}|_{\partial\Omega},\delta\mspace{-2mu}q)_{\partial\Omega}.

The optimal control
\bar{q}_{h}\in Q_{h}
is characterized by

\bar{q}_{h}\in Q_{h}\quad:\quad j_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{q}_{h})(\delta q_{h})=0\quad\text{for all }\delta q_{h}\in Q_{h}.

(29)

Let us denote by
\bar{z}_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h}
the solution to
28
with right hand side
\bar{u}_{h}-u_{d}
, where
\bar{u}_{h}=S_{h}\bar{q}_{h}
is the optimal discrete sate.
As a direct consequence of
29
, we get the gradient equation

\alpha\bar{q}_{h}+\pi_{h}\bar{z}_{h}|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

(30)

If
Q_{h}=V_{h}^{\partial}
, there further holds
\pi_{h}\bar{z}_{h}|_{\partial\Omega}=\bar{z}_{h}|_{\partial\Omega}
due to the definition of
\pi_{h}
and the fact that
\bar{z}_{h}|_{\partial\Omega}\in V_{h}^{\partial}
.

When deriving corresponding discretization error estimates, we need several estimates for the directional derivatives.

Lemma 6
.

Let
\bar{q}_{h}\in Q_{h}
be the solution of
27
. If
Q_{h}=Q_{h}^{0}
, then for all
\delta q\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega)
there holds

\lvert j_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{q}_{h})(\pi_{h}\delta q-\delta q)\rvert\leq ch^{2}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert\delta q\rVert_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}

with a constant
c>0
only depending on
\Omega
.
If
Q_{h}=V_{h}^{\partial}
, then for all
\delta q\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
there holds

j_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{q}_{h})(\delta q)=0.

Proof.

We start with considering the case
Q_{h}=Q_{h}^{0}
. We first use the definition of
\pi_{h}
and the fact that
\bar{q}_{h}\in Q_{h}
. This yields

\displaystyle\lvert j_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{q}_{h})(\pi_{h}\delta q-\delta q)\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert(S_{h}\bar{q}_{h}-u_{d},S_{h}(\pi_{h}\delta q-\delta q))+\alpha(\bar{q}_{h},\pi_{h}\delta q-\delta q)_{\partial\Omega}\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert(S_{h}\bar{q}_{h}-u_{d},S_{h}(\pi_{h}\delta q-\delta q))\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lVert S_{h}\bar{q}_{h}-u_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert S_{h}(\pi_{h}\delta q-\delta q)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Due to the optimality of
\bar{q}_{h}
, we deduce

\lVert S_{h}\bar{q}_{h}-u_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq 2j_{h}(\bar{q}_{h})\leq 2j_{h}(0)=\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

From
Lemma

1
and a standard estimate for
\pi_{h}
we obtain

\displaystyle\lVert S_{h}(\pi_{h}\delta q-\delta q)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq c(h\lVert\delta q-\pi_{h}\delta q\rVert_{H^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)}+\lVert\delta q-\pi_{h}\delta q\rVert_{H^{-1}(\partial\Omega)})

\displaystyle\leq ch^{2}\lVert\delta q\rVert_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}.

Collecting the different estimates shows the assertion for
Q_{h}=Q_{h}^{0}
.
Next, we consider the case
Q_{h}=V_{h}^{\partial}
. As
\alpha\bar{q}_{h}+\bar{z}_{h}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
, we get for all
\delta q\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)

j_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{q}_{h})(\delta q)=0.

∎

We next show estimates for the finite element error of the directional derivatives under minimal regularity assumptions for the desired state
u_{d}
, i.e.,
u_{d}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Afterwards, we show that the first estimate can be improved for
u_{d}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.

Lemma 7
.

Let
u_{d}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and let
\bar{q}
solve
15
.
There holds

\lvert j^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\delta q)-j_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\delta q)\rvert\leq\begin{cases}ch^{\frac{3}{2}}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert\delta q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}&\text{for any }\delta q\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega),\\
ch^{2}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert\delta q\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)}&\text{for any }\delta q\in H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)\end{cases}

with a constant
c>0
only depending on
\Omega
.

Proof.

Let us define
\tilde{z}_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h}
as the solution of (
28
) with right hand side
\tilde{u}_{h}-u_{d}
where
\tilde{u}_{h}=S_{h}\bar{q}
. Due to the definition of
j^{\prime}
and
j_{h}^{\prime}
we get

\displaystyle j^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\delta q)-j_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\delta q)

\displaystyle=(\bar{z}-\tilde{z}_{h},\delta q)_{\partial\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}\lVert\bar{z}-\tilde{z}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\lVert\delta q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}&\text{if }\delta q\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega),\\
\lVert\bar{z}-\tilde{z}_{h}\rVert_{H^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)}\lVert\delta q\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)}&\text{if }\delta q\in H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega).\end{cases}

Additionally, let us define
\hat{z}_{h}\in\widehat{V}_{h}
as the solution of (
28
) with right hand side
\bar{u}-u_{d}
. Introducing
\hat{z}_{h}
as an intermediate function yields

\displaystyle\lVert\bar{z}-\tilde{z}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\bar{z}-\hat{z}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\lVert\hat{z}_{h}-\tilde{z}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

and

\displaystyle\lVert\bar{z}-\tilde{z}_{h}\rVert_{H^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\bar{z}-\hat{z}_{h}\rVert_{H^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)}+\lVert\hat{z}_{h}-\tilde{z}_{h}\rVert_{H^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)},

respectively.
Each of the first terms on the right hand sides can estimated by
Lemma

2
in combination with the regularity results of
Theorem

5
. By this we obtain

\lVert\bar{z}-\hat{z}_{h}\rVert_{H^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)}+h^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\bar{z}-\hat{z}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq ch^{2}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Let us consider the second terms. Using the embedding
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)\hookrightarrow H^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)
, a standard trace theorem and a standard a priori estimate, we get

\lVert\hat{z}_{h}-\tilde{z}_{h}\rVert_{H^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)}\leq c\lVert\hat{z}_{h}-\tilde{z}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq c\lVert\hat{z}_{h}-\tilde{z}_{h}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq c\lVert\bar{u}-\tilde{u}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

The latter term represents a finite element error in the domain. Using
Theorem

6
and the a priori estimate from
Theorem

5
, we obtain

\lVert\bar{u}-\tilde{u}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{2}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Hence, we get

\lVert\hat{z}_{h}-\tilde{z}_{h}\rVert_{H^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)}+\lVert\hat{z}_{h}-\tilde{z}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq ch^{2}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(31)

Collecting the different results yields the assertion.
∎

Lemma 8
.

Let
u_{d}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and let
\bar{q}
solve
15
. For
0<s<s_{\Omega}
with
s_{\Omega}
from
13
there holds for all
\delta q\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)

\lvert j^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\delta q)-j_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\delta q)\rvert\leq ch^{\frac{3}{2}+s}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lVert\delta q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

with a constant
c>0
only depending on
\Omega
.
For
\lambda_{\Omega}>\frac{3}{2}
with
\lambda_{\Omega}
from
8
there even holds

\lvert j^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\delta q)-j_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\delta q)\rvert\leq ch^{2}\lvert\ln h\rvert\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lVert\delta q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

with a constant
c>0
only depending on
\Omega
.

Proof.

As in the proof of the previous lemma, we obtain

\lvert j^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\delta q)-j_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\delta q)\rvert\leq\left(\lVert\bar{z}-\hat{z}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\lVert\hat{z}_{h}-\tilde{z}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\right)\lVert\delta q\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

The second term of the sum was estimated in
31
yielding

\lVert\hat{z}_{h}-\tilde{z}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq ch^{2}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{2}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

The first term was treated in
Corollary

9
and
Corollary

10
for the case that
u_{d}
belongs to
H^{1}(\Omega)
. In summary, we get for
0<s<s_{\Omega}

\lVert\bar{z}-\hat{z}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq ch^{\frac{3}{2}+s}\lVert\bar{u}-u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{\frac{3}{2}+s}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)},

if we apply
Corollary

9
. Note, that last step holds due to
Theorem

5
. If we use
Corollary

10
instead, we obtain assuming
\lambda_{\Omega}>\frac{3}{2}

\lVert\bar{z}-\hat{z}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq ch^{2}\lvert\ln h\rvert\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

Putting terms together completes the proof.
∎

Next we prove optimal convergence rates for the controls. We distinguish between more and less regular desired states.

Theorem 11
.

Let
\bar{q}
and
\bar{q}_{h}
be the solution of
15
and
27
, respectively.

1.

For
u_{d}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
there holds

\lVert\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq\begin{cases}ch\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}&\text{if }Q_{h}=Q_{h}^{0},\\
ch^{\frac{3}{2}}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}&\text{if }Q_{h}=V_{h}^{\partial},\end{cases}

with a constant
c>0
only depending on
\Omega
.

2.

Let
u_{d}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and
Q_{h}=V_{h}^{\partial}
. There holds with a constant
c>0
only depending on
\Omega
that

\lVert\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq\begin{cases}ch^{\frac{3}{2}+s}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}&\quad\text{for }0<s<s_{\Omega},\\
ch^{2}\lvert\ln h\rvert\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}&\quad\text{for }\lambda_{\Omega}>\frac{3}{2},\end{cases}

with
s_{\Omega}
from
13
and
\lambda_{\Omega}
from
8
.

Proof.

Due to the coercivity of the second order directional derivatives and the linear quadratic structure of the optimal control problems we obtain

\displaystyle\alpha\lVert\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq j_{h}^{\prime\prime}(q)(\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h},\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h})=j_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h})-j_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{q}_{h})(\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h})=I+II

where
q\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
is arbitrary.
The first term can be estimated as follows: Due to the optimality of
\bar{q}
we get

I=j_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h})-j^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}).

This term was estimated in
Lemma

7
and
Lemma

8
. To be more precise, for
u_{d}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
we obtain

I\leq ch^{\frac{3}{2}}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

independent of the discretization strategy.
If
u_{d}
belongs to
H^{1}(\Omega)
we get

I\leq\begin{cases}ch^{\frac{3}{2}+s}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lVert\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}&\quad\text{for }0<s<s_{\Omega},\\
ch^{2}\lvert\ln h\rvert\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lVert\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}&\quad\text{for }\lambda_{\Omega}>\frac{3}{2}.\end{cases}

Let us now consider the second term:
By the optimality of
\bar{q}_{h}
, we obtain

II=j_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{q}_{h})(\bar{q}_{h}-\bar{q})=j_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{q}_{h})(\bar{q}_{h}-\pi_{h}\bar{q})+j_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{q}_{h})(\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q})=j_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{q}_{h})(\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q}).

Next, we use
Lemma

6
in combination with the regularity results from
Theorem

5
. This yields

j_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{q}_{h})(\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q})\leq\begin{cases}ch^{2}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}&\text{if }Q_{h}=Q_{h}^{0},\\
0&\text{if }Q_{h}=V_{h}^{\partial}.\end{cases}

Finally, the assertion follows from the estimates for
I
and
II
using Young’s inequality.
∎

The following theorem is concerned with optimal convergence rates for the states. The proof employs a duality argument and relies on the fact that no control bounds are present. A similar argument was used in
May et al (2013)
to show higher rates for the states in case of Dirichlet boundary control problems. However, there the duality argument was accomplished on the whole optimality system, whereas in the present case, we work with the gradient equations only.

Theorem 12
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be an arbitrary convex polyhedral domain and let
u_{d}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Let
\bar{q}
be the solution of
15
with corresponding state
\bar{u}=S\bar{q}
. Moreover, let
\bar{q}_{h}
be the solution of
27
with
Q_{h}=Q_{h}^{0}
or
Q_{h}=V_{h}^{\partial}
, respectively, and let
\bar{u}_{h}=S_{h}\bar{q}_{h}
be the corresponding discrete state. Then there holds the estimate

\lVert\bar{u}-\bar{u}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{2}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

with a constant
c>0
only depending on
\Omega
.

Proof.

We define the functionals
l:Q\to\mathbb{R}
and
l_{h}:Q\to\mathbb{R}
by

l(w)=\frac{1}{2}\lVert Sw-w_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\lVert w\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2}

and

l_{h}(w)=\frac{1}{2}\lVert S_{h}w-w_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\lVert w\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}^{2},

respectively, with
w_{d}=\bar{u}-\bar{u}_{h}=S\bar{q}-S_{h}\bar{q}_{h}
. Next, let
\bar{w}\in Q
be the unique solution to the optimization problem

\min_{w\in Q}l(w)

and let
\bar{w}_{h}\in Q_{h}
be the unique solution to the corresponding discrete optimization problem

\min_{w_{h}\in Q_{h}}l_{h}(w_{h}).

Obviously,
l
and
l_{h}
coincide with
j
and
j_{h}
, respectively, if
u_{d}=w_{d}
. Consequently, all the estimates for
j^{\prime}
and
j_{h}^{\prime}
are also valid for
l^{\prime}
and
l_{h}^{\prime}
. Moreover, estimates from
Theorem

11
are also applicable for
\bar{w}
and
\bar{w}_{h}
. We now apply the duality argument to derive the estimate from the assertion.
Direct calculations yield

\displaystyle\lVert S\bar{q}-S_{h}\bar{q}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=(w_{d},S\bar{q}-S_{h}\bar{q}_{h})=(w_{d},(S-S_{h})\bar{q})+(w_{d},S_{h}(\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h})).

The first term can be estimated by
Theorem

6
and
Theorem

5
resulting in

(w_{d},(S-S_{h})\bar{q})\leq\lVert w_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert(S-S_{h})\bar{q}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq ch^{2}\lVert w_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Let us consider the second term. We introduce another intermediate term, i.e.,

(w_{d},S_{h}(\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}))=(w_{d},S_{h}(\bar{q}-\pi_{h}\bar{q}))+(w_{d},S_{h}(\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h})).

Due to the optimality of
\bar{w}_{h}
and
\bar{q}_{h}
, we get

\displaystyle(w_{d},S_{h}(\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}))

\displaystyle=(S_{h}\bar{w}_{h},S_{h}(\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}))+\alpha(\bar{w}_{h},\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h})_{\partial\Omega}

\displaystyle=j_{h}^{\prime}(\pi_{h}\bar{q})(\bar{w}_{h})-j_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{q}_{h})(\bar{w}_{h})

\displaystyle=j_{h}^{\prime}(\pi_{h}\bar{q})(\bar{w}_{h}).

The definition of
j_{h}^{\prime}
and
l_{h}^{\prime}
implies

\displaystyle j_{h}^{\prime}(\pi_{h}\bar{q})(\bar{w}_{h})

\displaystyle=j_{h}^{\prime}(\pi_{h}\bar{q})(\bar{w}_{h})-j_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\bar{w}_{h})+j_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\bar{w}_{h})

\displaystyle=(w_{d},S_{h}(\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q}))+l_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{w}_{h})(\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q})+j_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\bar{w}_{h}).

In summary, we arrive at

(w_{d},S_{h}(\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}))=l_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{w}_{h})(\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q})+j_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\bar{w}_{h}).

Using the optimality of
\bar{q}
we get

(w_{d},S_{h}(\bar{q}-\bar{q}_{h}))=l_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{w}_{h})(\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q})+j_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\bar{w}_{h}-\bar{w})-j^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\bar{w}_{h}-\bar{w})+j_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\bar{w})-j^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\bar{w}).

For the first term, we obtain by means of
Lemma

6
and the regularity results from
Theorem

5

\lvert l_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{w}_{h})(\pi_{h}\bar{q}-\bar{q})\rvert\leq\begin{cases}ch^{2}\lVert w_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}&\text{if }Q_{h}=Q_{h}^{0},\\
0&\text{if }Q_{h}=V_{h}^{\partial}.\end{cases}

The other terms were estimated in
Lemma

7
yielding

j_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\bar{w}_{h}-\bar{w})-j^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\bar{w}_{h}-\bar{w})\leq ch^{\frac{3}{2}}\lVert\bar{w}_{h}-\bar{w}\rVert_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

and

j_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\bar{w})-j^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\bar{w})\leq ch^{2}\lVert\bar{w}\rVert_{H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\partial\Omega)}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Using the estimate for
\bar{w}-\bar{w}_{h}
from
Theorem

11
for
w_{d}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and the regularity estimate from
Theorem

5
results in

j_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\bar{w}_{h}-\bar{w})-j^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\bar{w}_{h}-\bar{w})+j_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\bar{w})-j^{\prime}(\bar{q})(\bar{w})\leq ch^{2}\lVert w_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert u_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Collecting the different results and dividing by
\lVert w_{d}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
yield the assertion.
∎

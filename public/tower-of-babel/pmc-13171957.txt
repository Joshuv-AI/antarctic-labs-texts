RETRACTED ARTICLE: Design, synthesis and biological evaluation of novel chalcone-derived thioxopyridine and pyrazolopyridine compounds as antimicrobial agents
Authors: Gaber Algaber, P Shyamala, Zaccaria Dammag, Mohammed Al-haddad
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe present work deals with the synthesis of some new chalcone, 2- thioxopyridine,2-methylthiopyridine, 2-hydrazinopyridine and 3-amino-1H-pyrazolo[3,4-b]pyridine derivatives containing biologically active substituents. The starting material of the present study was synthesis of the 2-cyanoethanethioamide from malononitrile using a modified Brunskill procedure, followed by the synthesis of the 1,3-diarylprop-2-en-1-one (chalcone) from a ketone and an aldehyde. 2-Thioxopyridines were obtained through Michael addition of α,β-unsaturated ketones with 2-cyanothioacetamide to afford the non-isolable intermediates 9 & 10 followed by cyclization and subsequent dehydration and dehydrogenation of the cyclized product to give compound 4-(furan-2-yl)-6-(4- methoxyphenyl)-2-thioxo-1,2-dihydropyridine-3-carbonitrile which was reacted with methyl iodide in methanolic sodium methoxide to afford the corresponding 4-(furan-2-yl)-6-(4-methoxyphenyl)-2-(methylthio) pyridine-3-carbonitrile, which was reacted with hydrazine hydrate to give the corresponding 2-hydrazinopyridines, which cyclized to afford the corresponding 3-aminopyrazolo[3,4-b]pyridine derivatives and were characterized by FT-IR, 1H-NMR, 13C-NMR and MS spectral studies. All the newly synthesized compounds were evaluated for their in vitro antibacterial activity against S. aureus as example of Gram-positive bacteria and E. coli as example of Gram-negative bacteria. They were also evaluated for their in vitro antifungal potential effect against C. albicans. Introduction

Nitrogen-containing heterocyclic compounds are one of the most fruitful and extensively developing fields of heterocyclic chemistry. These compounds exhibit various kinds of biological activities1. Chalcone compounds have been reported to possess varied biological activities such as antimicrobial2, antioxidant3 antimalarial4, antiinflammatory5, antifungal6, antiviral7, antibacterial8, antileishmanial9, antiparasitic10, analgesic11, Cytotoxic activity12 and anticancer13 activity. They also act as potential nonpurine xanthine oxidase inhibitors14, as antitubercular15, acetylcholinesterase enzymes inhibition16, as cyclooxygenase- 1/-2 inhibitors17, Alpha amylase inhibitory activity18, spermicidal activity19, as potent soybean lipoxygenase inhibitory activity20 and neurotoxicity21. They also serve as a backbone for the synsthesis of various heterocyclic compounds, as they undergo a variety of chemical reactions. Hence chalcones play an important role in the synthesis of medicinal compounds22. Furthermore, a number of 3-cyano-2(1H)-pyridinethiones23–25 and their related compounds were found to be very reactive substances for the synthesis of many different heterocyclic systems which exhibited biological activities such as antimicrobial26, anticancer27, antifungal28, acaricidal and neurotropic29 activities. Also, pyrazolo[3,4-b]pyridines comprise a very interesting class of compounds because of their significant and versatile biological, and pharmacological activities, such as antimicrobial30, antileishmanial31, cardiovascular32, antiviral33, antifungal34, antimalarial35, antitubercular36 and anticancer37 activities. They show anxiolytic activity along with Xanthine oxidase inhibitors, cholesterol formation inhibitor and anti-Alzheimer38. The pyrazolo[3,4-b]pyridine moieties represent important building blocks in both natural and synthetic bioactive compounds39. They also act as potent and selective inhibitors of glycogen synthase kinase-3 (GSK-3)40. Moreover, fused heterocyclic containing pyrazolo[3,4-b]pyridine systems have been described associated with several biological and medicinal activities41. The aim of this work is the synthesis and investigation of the biological importance of chalcone, 2-thioxopyridine, 2-methylthi-opyridine, 2-hydrazinopyridine and 3-amino-1H-pyrazolo[3,4-b]pyridine derivatives.

Materials and methods

Apparatus and reagents

All reagents and solvents were of analytical grade and used as received from commercial suppliers (Sigma-Aldrich, Merck, and Fluka) without further purification. These included malononitrile, 4-methoxyacetophenone, furfural, triethylamine, hydrazine hydrate (98%), piperidine, morpholine, sulfur, methyl iodide, and various organic solvents such as ethanol, methanol, pyridine, piperidine, and dry benzene.

All melting points were measured on a Stuart SMP3 melting point apparatus and are uncorrected. Infrared (FT-IR)| spectrometers PerkinElmer Co. USA) were measured by using KBr discs and performed at the Center of Research and Pharmaceutical Studies (CRPS), Ibb University, Ibb, Yemen. Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (1H-NMR and 13C-NMR) spectra were recorded on Jeol JNM ECA 500II (500MHz, 11.75 Tesla) NMR Spectrometer at 500 MHz, (Japan), by using Tetramethylsilane (TMS) as the internal reference and DMSO-d6/CDCl3 as internal solvent standards and chemical shifts are expressed as δ ppm units, and were performed in Nuclear Magnetic Resonance unit (NMRU), Mansoura University, Mansoura, Egypt. Mass spectrometry was carried out on Direct Inlet part to mass analyzer in Thermo Scientific GCMS model ISQ at the Regional Center for Mycology and Biotechnology (RCMB) Al-Azhar University, Nasr City, Cairo, Egypt. Thin layer chromatography (TLC) analysis was carried out on Merck silica gel 60 F254, Aluminum-coated TLC sheets (20 X 20 cm) to check the purity of the synthesized compounds by following up the spots on T.L.C. plates. Spots on these were seen with UV light at short (254 nm) and long (365 nm) wavelengths. Mobile phase solvent systems used for elution of chromatograms (spots) are: Hexane: ethyl acetate (7:3), Chloroform: petroleum ether (8:2), Petroleum ether: ethyl acetate (6:4) and Hexane: ethyl acetate (9:1).

Chemistry

Synthesis of 2-cyanothioacetamide (3)

The following procedure was carried out according to the modified Brunskill method previously reported in the literature42, and the original experimental description has been appropriately cited.

Cyanothioacetamide (3) was synthesized following the reported method42 with minor laboratory adjustments a 500mL Erlenmeyer flask was charged with 20 g (0.3 mol) of malononitrile(1) and 100 mL of EtOH. Malononitrile was dissolved with stirring at room temperature. Then, 0.2 mL of a triethylamine (Et3N) was added, the flask was closed with a two-holed rubber stopper fitted with two glass tubes, one of which was immersed in the malononitrile solution. The stream of H2S generated from FeS was passed through the tube system. After a short induction period, an exothermic reaction begins to occur, which is accompanied by strong absorption of H2S by reaction mass. It is important to keep the temperature in the range 15–20 °C (cooling with ice-bath), to avoid the crystallization of malononitrile, on the one hand, and overheating of the reaction mixture on the other. After ~30–40 min, 2-cyanothioacetamide begins to crystallize. The reaction mixture should be stirred or shaken periodically to avoid clogging of gas supply pipe by product. Hydrogen sulfide must be passed through a cold solution for at least 6–8 hours to get a good yield of 3. At the end of the reaction, the mixture was cooled with ice/NaCl, 2-cyanothioacetamide was filtered off, washed several times with cold EtOH to a colorless filtrate, then washed with cold Et2O and petroleum ether. 2-Cyanothioacetamide was obtained as sand-yellow needles, m.p. 117–119 °C, yield: (72.91%). The product is pure enough for further transformations. Cyanothioacetamide should be kept in a refrigerator at 0–4 °C. IR spectrum (ν, cm−1): 3283, 3360, 3161 (NH2); 2260 (C≡N); 2887, 2921 (-CH2-); 1624 (C=S).

Biological assay

Determination of antimicrobial activity

The antimicrobial activity was evaluated using the agar well diffusion method following previously published protocols47,48 and performed in accordance with CLSI guidelines49. The original methodological sources have been properly cited. All the newly synthesized compounds (3, 6, 11, 12, 13 and 14) were evaluated for their in vitro antibacterial activity against S. aureus as example of Gram-positive bacteria and E. coli as example of Gram-negative bacteria. They were also evaluated for their in vitro antifungal potential against C. albicans as representatives of fungi. All tested microorganisms were standard laboratory strains obtained from routine microbiology laboratory stock cultures and maintained under appropriate storage conditions prior to use. These strains were handled under controlled laboratory conditions to ensure reproducibility of the results. These strains were maintained under standard laboratory conditions and were not collected specifically for the present study. Briefly, the microbial strains under test were initially grown as cultures on agar media. The microbial inoculum used was obtained from a fresh culture on agar medium taken with a sterile wire loop and transferred into a tube containing 5 mL of broth medium. The broth culture was incubated at 37 °C for 18–24 hours (bacteria) or at 37 °C for 24–72hrs (fungi) until it achieved or exceeded the turbidity of the 0.5 McFarland standard. The turbidity of the actively growing broth culture was adjusted to have an optical density of 0.1 at 600 nm with sterile broth in order to obtain a turbidity comparable to that of the 0.5 McFarland standard according to CLSI guidelines49. This resulted in a microbial suspension containing approximately 108 colony forming units per milliliter (CFU/mL).

Mueller Hinton Agar/Sabouraud dextrose agar was melted, cooled to 45 °C, poured into Petri dishes to get a uniform thickness of 6 mm, and allowed to solidify on a leveled surface. Once the medium had solidified, the entire surface of the agar was uniformly inoculated by streaking a sterile cotton swab that was previously dipped into the microorganism suspension. 6 wells, each 6 mm in diameter, were then carefully punched out of the agar using a sterile cork borer. A stock solution(1000μg/mL) of each test compound was prepared in DMSO and a serial dilution of each compound was carried out to obtain the concentrations of 1000, 500, 250, 125 and 62.50μg/mL using twofold serial dilution method50, and 50μL of each compound solution was introduced into the corresponding well using a sterile micropipette. Levofloxacin 5µg/mL, Clarithromycin 15µg/mL (antibacterial) and Amphotericin B 10µg/mL (antifungal) were used as reference antimicrobial drugs. The results were recorded for each tested compound as the diameter of inhibition zones (IZ) of bacterial or fungal growth around the well in mm. Results and discussion

Chemistry (synthesis and characterization)

Synthesis of the starting material of 2-cyanoethanethioamide (3)

The compound of the 2-cyanothioacetamide (3) was synthesized by reacting malononitrile (1) with the stream of H2S (2) generated from FeS A stream of H₂S generated from FeS was passed through the system at a temperature in the range 15–20 °C in ethanol in the presence of a triethylamine (Et3N) as catalyst to give compound 3 (Scheme 1). This compound was confirmed by IR spectral data. The FT-IR (ν cm−1) of compound 3 showed absorption bands at 3283, 3360, 3161 due to (NH2), 2260 due to (C≡N),2887, 2921 due to (–CH2-) and 1624 due to (C=S).

Synthetic route for the preparation of compound 3.

(2E)-3-(furan-2-yl)-1-(4-methoxy-phenyl)prop-2-en-1-one (6)19 was carried out via Claisen-Schmidt condensation from 4-methoxyacetophenone (4) and 2-furaldehyde (5) using NaOH 40% in absolute ethanol (Scheme 2). The FT-IR spectrum of 6 revealed the stretching vibration of aromatic C–H at 3067 cm−1 and C–H of OCH3 at 2843 cm−1. The strong peak of C=O stretching vibration was observed at 1657 cm−1 and C=C stretching vibration in α,β-unsaturated was observed at 1605 cm−1. The C=C stretching vibration in aromatic ring was observed at 1458, 1591 cm−1. The 1H-NMR spectrum of (6) showed singlet signal at δ 3.85 (3H, J = 3.00 Hz) ppm were assigned to the OCH3 group of the phenyl ring. Equivalent aromatic protons (p-phOCH3) showed the doublet of doublet signals at δ 8.02–8.04 ppm. The protons of furan ring showed the signals at 6.48–7.58 ppm. 13CNMR (δppm) of this compound appeared a signal at 187.9 due to (C=O), 55.3 due to (OCH3), 163.3 due to (C–O), and 119.0& 129.9 due to (–C=C– of Vinyl). Moreover, the mass spectrum of compound 6 gave m/z = 228 (M+, 33%) which is corresponding to its molecular weight. Also, signal at m/z = 215 (M+-2H, –CH3, 18.84 %), 122 (M+- phOCH3,39.44%), 196 (M+-H, -OCH3, 30.30%), 107 (base peak, 100.00%) which is corresponding to (M+-H, phOCH3) further confirms its structure (cf. Exp. Part and Scheme 2).

Synthesis of chalcone derivative 6.

The compound (6) reacted with 2–cyanoethanethioamide (3) in absolute ethanol containing a catalytic amount of piperidine under reflux to afford a reaction product. Such reaction product formed via a Michael addition of –CH2– in 3 on –CH=CH– of 6 to give the non–isolable products 7, 8, 9, 10 followed by cyclisation via dehydration and dehydrogenation to give 11. The IR (cm−1) of this reaction product showed the absorption bands of NH (3120), C=S (1556) and CN (2212) groups. 1H-NMR spectra showed singlet signal for NH proton at δ 7.90 ppm. (Scheme 3). (cf. Exp. Part). Proposed mechanism for the synthesis of compound 11.

The synthetic potentiality of 11 was investigated through the electrophilic substitution reactions using some electrophilic C-species. Thus, it has been found that 11 was reacted with methyl iodide in methanolic sodium methoxide at room temperature for 30 min to give 4-(furan-2-yl)-6-(4-methoxyphenyl)-2-(methylthio)pyridine-3-carbonitrile (12). The IR (cm-1) of 12 showed the absorption bands of C–O (1176) and CN (2213) groups. 1H-NMR spectra showed singlet signal for SCH3 protons at δ 2.61 (s, J = 3.00 Hz, 3H) ppm. 13CNMR (δ ppm) of this compound appeared a signal at 13.5 due to (SCH3) and 113.9 due to (CN). It’s mass spectrum gave m/z = 322 (8.41%), which corresponds to the molecular weight of the molecular formula C18H14N2O2S of the assigned structure as well as m/z = 321 (23.52%), 296 (10.09%) and 276 (5.20%) which corresponding to (M+-H), (M+- CN), and (M+-SCH3), respectively. Also, such mass spectrum gave several peaks with their fragments and this confirms the proposed structure. Thus, compound 12 was reacted with hydrazine hydrate in absolute ethanol in the presence of pyridine under reflux for 20 h until the odour of H2S or CH3SH ceased to give the 4-(furan-2-yl)-2-hydrazinyl-6-(4-methoxyphenyl)pyridine-3-carbonitrile (13) (Scheme 4). The IR (cm−1) of 13 showed the absorption bands of NH, NH2 (3073, 3200- 3310) and CN (2211) groups. 1H-NMR spectra showed singlet signal for NH and NH2 protons at δ 4.86 (s, 1H) and 5.16 (s, 2H) ppm. As well as the 13CNMR (δ ppm) of this compound appeared a signal at 55.3 due to (OCH3) and 114.0 due to (CN); Moreover, the mass spectrum of compound 13 gave m/z = 306 (M+, 24.26%) which is corresponding to its molecular weight. Also, signals at m/z = 272 (M+-H, NHNH2, 25.34%), 251 (M+- NHNH2, -CN, 24.31%), 224 (M+-NHNH2, -CN, -OCH3, 23.77%), 207 (M+-Furan,-NHNH2, 21.33%), 178 (M+-Furan, -NHNH2, -OCH3, 22.34%) and 166.53 (M+-Furan, -NHNH2, -CH3, -CN, 16.45%) further confirms its structure.

The structure of compound 14 was established by FT-IR, 1H-NMR and mass spectral analyses. The FT-IR (cm−1) of 14 showed the absorption bands of NH, NH2 (3118, 3468, 3435). 1H-NMR spectra showed singlet signal for NH and NH2 protons at δ 6.86 and 5.16 ppm; Moreover, the mass spectrum of compound 14 gave m/z = 306 (M+, 22.7%) which is corresponding to its molecular weight. It is important to report here that the reaction of 11 with hydrazine hydrate was preceded through the removal of the H2S molecule while that of 12 with hydrazine hydrate was preceded via the methyl mercaptan removal. The IR (cm−1) of 12 showed the absorption bands of CN function which disappeared in case of 14 and instead the newly formed NH2 function was detected. The mass spectra of each of 12 gave the peaks at m/z = 321 which corresponding to their molecular weight (M+-H). Other peaks for compounds 12 was detected and these peaks gave further elucidation for their structures (cf. Exp. Part and Scheme 4).

The base mediated S-alkylation of compound 11 with methyl Iodide followed by hydrazinolysis with hydrazine hydrate afforded novel 2-hydrazinylpyridine derivatives 13 was obtained due to the high electrophilicity of α-Pyridine carbon.

Biology

The biological activities of the synthesized compounds have been compared with standard drugs. The effects of the compounds varied across the tested microorganisms. The results are given in Tables 1 and 2.

In general, as seen from the data presented in Tables 1 and 2, E. coli is sensitive to compounds 3, 6, 11 and 14; S. aureus is sensitive to compounds 3, 6 and 11, also, C. albicans is sensitive to compounds 3, 6 and 11. Whereas, the tested compounds 12, 13 did not exhibit antibacteria activity towards Gram positive (S. aureus) and Gram negative (E. coli) bacteria and antifungal activity against the tested C. albicans fungi.

Compound 6 containing 4-methoxyphenyl ring at 1st position and furan ring at 3rd position of –COC=C– system have showed activity for S. aureus bacteria at 1000μg/mL, 500μg/mL and 250μg/mL, E. coli at 1000μg/mL and 500μg/mL, while compound 11 containing Furan ring at 4th position and 4-methoxyphenyl ring at 6th position of 2-Thioxopyridines ring exhibited activity against E. coli at 1000μg/mL and 500μg/mL, S. aureus bacteria at 1000μg/mL concentration, but, compound 14 containing Furan ring at 4th position and 4-methoxyphenyl ring at 6th position of pyrazolo[3,4-b]pyridine ring have showed growth inhibition zone for E. coli at 1000μg/mL, 500μg/mL, 250μg/mL and 125μg/mL, concentrations. Concerning the antifungal activity of the tested compounds, the analogs 3, 6, 11 showed moderate antifungal activity against the tested C. albicans fungi in comparison to Amphotericin B; the reference drug used. Meanwhile, the analogs 3 & 6 revealed higher potency than that of Amphotericin B at high concentrations. The relatively moderate activity observed for most compounds highlights the importance of further structural modifications to improve antimicrobial potency and selectivity.

Structure activity relationship (SAR)

A detailed structure–activity relationship (SAR) analysis was carried out to rationalize the antimicrobial activity of the synthesized compounds. The results revealed that compounds 3, 6, 11, and 14 exhibited varying degrees of antimicrobial activity, which can be attributed to the presence of specific structural features. In particular, compound 11 demonstrated notable activity, likely due to the presence of the thioxo (C=S) group along with the cyano (–CN) substituent at the pyridine ring. These electron-withdrawing groups enhance the electrophilic character of the molecule and may facilitate stronger interactions with microbial targets. Furthermore, the presence of heterocyclic moieties such as furan and pyridine rings contributes to increased lipophilicity, which may improve membrane permeability and enhance biological activity. Compound 14 also showed moderate activity, which may be attributed to the fused pyrazolo[3,4-b]pyridine system, known for its biological relevance.In contrast, compounds 12 and 13 were found to be completely inactive against all tested microorganisms. This loss of activity can be explained by the replacement of the thioxo (C=S) group in compound 11 with methylthio(–SCH₃) in compound 12 and hydrazinyl (–NHNH₂) in compound 13. These substitutions likely reduce the ability of the compounds to interact effectively with microbial enzymes or receptors, leading to diminished biological activity. Overall, the results indicate that the presence of the thioxo group, cyano substituent, and heterocyclic frameworks are essential for antimicrobial activity, while their modification or replacement leads to significant reduction or loss of activity (Table 3).

Proposed mechanism of antimicrobial activity

The observed antimicrobial activity of the synthesized compounds may be attributed to multiple possible mechanisms of action. One potential mechanism involves the inhibition of essential microbial enzymes, which are crucial for cell survival and metabolic processes. In addition, these compounds may exert their effect by disrupting the integrity of microbial cell membranes, leading to leakage of cellular contents and eventual cell death. The presence of heterocyclic moieties such as chalcone, pyridine, and pyrazole rings plays a significant role in enhancing biological activity. These structural features are known to facilitate interactions with microbial proteins and enzymes through hydrogen bonding and π–π interactions. Furthermore, the lipophilic nature of the synthesized compounds may improve their ability to penetrate microbial cell membranes, thereby increasing their antimicrobial effectiveness.

Comparison with standard drugs

The antimicrobial activity of the synthesized compounds was compared with standard reference drugs, including Levofloxacin and Clarithromycin for antibacterial activity and Amphotericin B for antifungal activity. The results demonstrated that the tested compounds exhibited moderate antimicrobial activity; however, the inhibition zones were generally smaller than those of the standard drugs, indicating lower potency. Therefore, the synthesized compounds can be clearly classified as less active compared to the reference drugs.Nevertheless, some compounds, particularly compounds 3 and 6, exhibited comparable activity at higher concentrations against Candida albicans, suggesting moderate potential that may be improved through further structural optimization

In vitro antibacterial and antifungal assay

Compound 3 have showed more activity for C. albicans fungi at all concentration used, while compound 11 containing Furan ring at 4th position and 4-methoxyphenyl ring at 6th position of 2-Thioxopyridines ring have showed activity for C. albicans at all concentrations. The attachment of CN at C3 and Thioxo side chain at C2 of the Pyridine ring as compound 11 produced an antibacterial activity against the tested Gram positive and the Gram negative bacteria.

The attachment of CN at C3 and Thioxo side chain at C2 of the pyridine ring as compound 11 and the fusion of pyridine ring with pyrazole heterocycle to give pyrazolo[3,4- b]pyridine derivatives 14 respectively produced an antibacterial activity against the tested Gram negative bacteria that is approximately moderate activity compared to that obtained by the reference Levofloxacin and Clarithromycin. The replacement of Thioxo side chain of 11 with 2-methylthio or hydrazinyl substituent as compounds 12 and 13 led to complete loss of activity against the Gram-positive, the Gram negative bacteria and C. albicans fungi. The replacement of CN at C3 and 2-methylthio or hydrazinyl side chain at C2 of the pyridine ring as compounds 12 and 13 and the fusion of pyridine ring with pyrazole hetero cycle to give pyrazolo[3,4-b]pyridine derivatives 14, respectively led to complete loss of activity against the Gram-positive and the C. albicans fungi, while it’s showed moderate activity towards E. coli.

The novelty of this study lies in the design and synthesis of new hybrid heterocyclic compounds combining chalcone, thioxopyridine, and pyrazolopyridine scaffolds within a single molecular framework. This structural integration represents a unique approach that may enhance antimicrobial activity through synergistic effects of multiple pharmacophores. Compared to previously reported compounds, the synthesized derivatives offer structural diversity and potential for further optimization, making them promising candidates for the development of new antimicrobial agents.

Conclusion

In the present work, the synthesis and characterization of novel (2E)-3-(furan-2-yl)-1-(4-methoxyphenyl)prop-2-en-1-one (3) and their derivatives. All spectroscopic analyses confirmed the proposed structures of these compounds. Antimicrobial activity data have shown that the synthesized compounds have a significant antibacterial activity against the tested microorganisms. All the newly synthesized compounds (3, 6, 11, 12, 13 and 14) were evaluated for their in vitro antibacterial activity against S. aureus (Gram-positive) and E. coli (Gram-negative) bacteria and in vitro antifungal potential against C. albicans fungi. The zone of inhibition was measured and the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) values were estimated. using the well diffusion assay and indicated that compounds 3, 6, 11 and 14 showed potential antibacterial and antifungal effects.

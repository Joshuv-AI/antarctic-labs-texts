Distributed Nash Equilibrium Seeking in Non-Monotone Games over the Simplex
Tatiana Tatarenko, S. Rasoul Etesami

Abstract
In this work, we present a novel characterization of approximate Nash equilibria in a class of convex games over the simplex. To achieve this, we regularize the utility functions using the Shannon entropy term, connect the solutions to the regularized game with the set of Nash equilibria, and formulate a multi-objective optimization problem to solve the regularized game. Based on the obtained properties of the stationary points in this optimization problem, we formulate two distributed heuristic algorithms to compute an approximate Nash equilibrium of the original game. Keywords –Approximate Nash equilibria, convex games, quantal response, entropy regularization, distributed optimization.



I
Introduction

Game-theoretic optimization addresses a specific class of problems arising in multi-agent systems, where each agent, called a player, chooses its action from a local action set to maximize its local utility function. These utility functions are coupled through the decision variables (actions) of all players in the system. Applications of game-theoretic optimization can be found in areas such as electricity markets, communication networks, autonomous driving systems, and smart grids
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. Solving a game-theoretic optimization problem involves finding a stable joint action, called a
Nash equilibrium
, from which no agent has any incentive to unilaterally deviate.

In the case of
convex games
, Nash equilibria are equivalent to solutions of a specific variational inequality defined for the game’s pseudo-gradient over the joint action set
[
4
]
. Such variational inequalities can be efficiently solved under the assumption that the pseudo-gradient possesses monotonicity properties
[
5
,
6
]
, or when a
Minty solution
exists in the game
[
7
]
. In recent years, much work has been devoted to the development of
distributed procedures
that leverage only local agents’ information to achieve a solution in such games
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
.

While monotone games or games with a Minty solution can be efficiently solved either centrally or in a distributed setting, solution approaches for non-monotone convex games are not well-studied. Moreover, it is known that the problem of approximating a mixed Nash equilibrium in a general two-player, non-zero sum normal form game is PPAD-hard (see
[
12
]
)
1
1

1

PPAD-hard problems belong to the class PPAD (Polynomial Parity Argument, Directed version) introduced by Papadimitriou in 1991. For more details on this class, see
[
12
]
and the references therein.
. The recent paper
[
13
]
studies the
optimistic mirror descent
algorithm and demonstrates that it either converges to a Nash equilibrium or that the average correlated distribution of play is a strong
coarse correlated equilibrium
. Some works have addressed a specific subclass of such games, namely

, where a Nash equilibrium can be solved using polynomial-time algorithms
[
14
]
.

Despite the well-established impossibility results, current works continue to seek heuristics for circumventing computational obstacles in general convex games. In particular, the work
[
15
]
presents an optimization approach based on the specifically defined Nikaido-Isoda function to calculate stable states in unconstrained games. The paper
[
16
]
, in turn, formulates a non-convex stochastic optimization problem to approximate a Nash equilibrium in normal-form games. The main idea in
[
16
]
involves the regularization of locally convex utility functions using the Shannon entropy term, where the resulting regularized game possesses Nash equilibria. Moreover, each Nash equilibrium in the regularized game is an approximate solution to the original one and belongs to the interior of the joint action set. This condition implies a reformulation of the corresponding variational inequality as a nonlinear system of equations, which leads to the formulation of a non-convex optimization problem.

A joint action profile

.

In this work, we are interested in developing distributed algorithms to obtain an

Remark 1
.

It is important to note that since the class of two-player nonzero-sum normal-form games in mixed strategies is a special case of the games under consideration, we cannot expect an efficient calculation of the

-NE points, as the latter problem is known to be PPAD-hard (see

Let

denote the pseudo-gradient of the game

F(x)=[\nabla_{1}f_{1}(x),\ldots,\nabla_{N}f_{N}(x)]^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

To find a NE in a game satisfying Assumption

\displaystyle\mbox{Find $x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$: }\langle F(x^{*}),x-x^{*}\rangle\leq 0\,\mbox{ for any $x\in\Delta$}.

(1)

We note that a solution to the variational inequality (

is a compact set. We refer to

,
9
,
10

11
]
for different gradient-based solution approaches for solving (

In convex games where the pseudo-gradient does not possess one of the properties above, the gradient-based procedure may diverge
[
19
]
. To rectify this issue, one can turn attention to an optimization-based approach for NE computation. However, all known reformulations for games with constraints are represented as non-convex optimization problems, and convergence to a stationary point of the objective function can only be guaranteed, which does not always coincide with the set of NE points
[
16
,
15

Quantal Response Equilibria

Following the approach presented in

To define a QRE, one perturbs the initial utility functions

\displaystyle S(x_{i})=-\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}+1}x_{i}^{j}\ln(x_{i}^{j}),

Next, we state a result from

Lemma 1
.

The following statements hold for the game

1.

Given a positive

2.

Let

\displaystyle-\frac{\tau}{1-\sum_{\ell=1}^{n_{i}}x_{i}^{\ell}},\ldots,-\frac{\tau}{1-\sum_{\ell=1}^{n_{i}}x_{i}^{\ell}},

(26)

\displaystyle\,\hat{q}_{i1}^{j},\ldots,\hat{q}_{ii-1}^{j},\hat{q}_{ii+1}^{j},\ldots,\hat{q}_{iN}^{j},0,\ldots,0\Big)^{T}\!\!.

(27)

We note that in the definition of
\bm{q}_{i,\tau}^{j}(x_{i})
above, we have rearranged the coordinates such that all potentially nonzero elements appear at the beginning of the vector
\bm{q}_{i,\tau}^{j}(x_{i})
, i.e., the first coordinate corresponds to
\frac{\partial l_{i,j}^{\tau}(x)}{\partial x_{i}^{j}}
followed by
\frac{\partial l_{i,j}^{\tau}(x)}{\partial x_{i}^{-j}}
.
As
x_{i}^{\ell}\in[\frac{e^{-1/\tau^{2}}}{\max_{i}n_{i}},1)\ \forall\ell
, we conclude that
\bm{q}_{i,\tau}^{j}(x_{i})\neq 0
for any
x\in\hat{\Delta}^{\tau}
. Thus,
\nabla l_{i,j}^{\tau}(x)=0
iff

\displaystyle\hat{r}_{i}^{j}+\sum_{k\neq i}\langle\hat{q}_{ik}^{j},x_{k}\rangle-\tau\ln x_{i}^{j}+\tau\ln\Big(1-\sum_{\ell=1}^{n_{i}}x_{i}^{\ell}\Big)=0,

which in view of (
19
) implies
l_{i,j}^{\tau}(x)=l_{i,j}^{\tau,*}=0
.
∎

The above lemma implies that if an optimization procedure finds a point
x
such that
l_{i,j}^{\tau}(x)=0
for all
i\in[N]
,
j\in[n_{i}]
, then
x
solves the problem (
20
). However, since the function
l_{i,j}^{\tau}(x)
is non-convex, a gradient-based optimization approach can only guarantee convergence to a stationary point of
l_{i,j}^{\tau}(x)
. Therefore, as a next step, we proceed to characterize stationary points of
l_{i,j}^{\tau}(x)
and establish a connection between a common stationary point of all the functions
l_{i,j}^{\tau}(x),i\in[N],j\in[n_{i}]
and the solution set to the multi-objective optimization problem (
20
), which, by Lemma
3
, equals the set of NE points in the game
\Gamma^{\tau,t}
.

Definition 3
.

We denote the set of stationary points of the function
l_{i,j}^{\tau}(x)
on the set
\hat{\Delta}^{\tau}
by

\displaystyle\EuScript S^{\tau}_{i,j}=\big\{x\in\hat{\Delta}^{\tau}:\,\langle\nabla l_{i,j}^{\tau}(x),y-x\rangle\geq 0\quad\mbox{for any }y\in\hat{\Delta}^{\tau}\big\},

and denote the intersection of these stationary point sets by:

\displaystyle\EuScript S^{\tau}=\cap_{i=1}^{N}\cap_{j=1}^{n_{i}}\EuScript S^{\tau}_{i,j}.

(28)

Now, we can state the following result.

Lemma 5
.

For sufficiently small
\tau>0
, the set
\mathcal{S}^{\tau}
is nonempty and equals the set of NE points in the game
\Gamma^{\tau,t}
.

Proof.

Let
x
be a NE of the game
\Gamma^{\tau,t}
. Then, according to Lemma
3
,
l_{i,j}^{\tau}(x)=0
for all
i\in[N]
,
j\in[n_{i}]
. Moreover, Lemma
4
implies that
\nabla l_{i,j}^{\tau}(x)=0
for all
i\in[N]
,
j\in[n_{i}]
. Thus,
x\in\mathcal{S}^{\tau}
, and hence, NE points of
\Gamma^{\tau,t}
belong to
\mathcal{S}^{\tau}
.

Now, by contradiction, suppose there exists
x\in\mathcal{S}^{\tau}
such that
x
is not a NE of the game
\Gamma^{\tau,t}
. From Lemma
3
, there exist
i^{\prime}\in[N]
and
j^{\prime}\in[n_{i^{\prime}}]
such that
l_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}^{\tau}(x)\neq 0
. Thus, according to Lemma
4
,
\nabla l_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}^{\tau}(x)\neq 0
. However, since
x\in\mathcal{S}^{\tau}_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}
, we conclude that
x\in\partial\hat{\Delta}^{\tau}
. This implies the following two possibilities for the coordinates of
x
:

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt(a)\qquad x_{i}^{j}=\frac{e^{-1/\tau^{2}}}{\max_{i}n_{i}+1}\,\mbox{ for some $i\in[N]$, $j\in[n_{i}]$,}

(29)

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!(b)\qquad 1-\sum_{\ell=1}^{n_{i}}x_{i}^{\ell}=\frac{e^{-1/\tau^{1.5}}}{\max_{i}n_{i}+1}\,\mbox{ for some $i\in[N]$}.

(30)

First, we consider the case (a). Having the relation (
29
) at place, we can calculate
\nabla l_{i,j}^{\tau}(x)
using (
21
) to obtain:

\displaystyle\nabla l_{i,j}^{\tau}(x)

\displaystyle=2\Bigg(\hat{r}_{i}^{j}+\sum_{k\neq i}\langle\hat{q}_{ik}^{j},x_{k}\rangle+\tau\ln(\max_{i}n_{i}+1)

(31)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{\tau}+\tau\ln\Big(1-\sum_{\ell=1}^{n_{i}}x_{i}^{\ell}\Big)\Bigg)\bm{q}_{i,\tau}^{j}(x_{i}),

(32)

where
\bm{q}_{i,\tau}^{j}(x_{i})
is given by (
25
).

Next, we demonstrate that for a sufficiently small
\tau>0
the multiplier
\hat{r}_{i}^{j}+\sum_{k\neq i}\langle\hat{q}_{ik}^{j},x_{k}\rangle+\frac{1}{\tau}+\tau\ln(\max_{i}n_{i}+1)+\tau\ln\left(1-\sum_{\ell=1}^{n_{i}}x_{i}^{\ell}\right)
in the definition of
\nabla l_{i,j}^{\tau}(x)
in (
31
) is positive for any
x\in\hat{\Delta}
.
According to the definition of the set
\tilde{\Delta}
(see (
11
) and (
14
)), we have

\displaystyle 1-\sum_{\ell=1}^{n_{i}}x_{i}^{\ell}\geq\frac{e^{-1/\tau^{1.5}}}{\max_{i}n_{i}+1}.

(33)

Thus,

\displaystyle\tau\ln\Big(1-\sum_{\ell=1}^{n_{i}}x_{i}^{\ell}\Big)\geq-\frac{1}{\tau^{0.5}}-\tau\ln(\max_{i}n_{i}+1).

(34)

There exists sufficiently small
\tau
such that for any
x\in\hat{\Delta}^{\tau}

\displaystyle\hat{r}_{i}^{j}+\sum_{k\neq i}\langle\hat{q}_{ik}^{j},x_{k}\rangle+\frac{1}{\tau}-\frac{1}{{\tau^{0.5}}}>0.

(35)

Using relations (
31
), (
34
), and (
35
), we conclude that the vectors
\nabla l_{i,j}^{\tau}(x)
and
\bm{q}_{i,\tau}^{j}(x_{i})
are the same up to a positive multiplier. Now, consider a specific
y\in\hat{\Delta}^{\tau}
that is defined by
y_{i}^{j}>x_{i}^{j}=\frac{e^{-1/\tau^{2}}}{\max_{i}n_{i}+1}
,
\sum_{\ell=1}^{n_{i}}y_{i}^{\ell}\geq\sum_{\ell=1}^{n_{i}}x_{i}^{\ell}
,
y_{m}^{\ell}=x_{m}^{\ell}
for all
m\neq i
and
\ell\in[n_{m}]
, and note that such a feasible
y\in\hat{\Delta}^{\tau}
always exists. Then,

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\langle\bm{q}_{i,\tau}^{j}(x_{i}),y-x\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{\tau}{x_{i}^{j}}(y_{i}^{j}-x_{i}^{j})

(36)

\displaystyle-\frac{\tau}{1-\sum_{\ell=1}^{n_{i}}x_{i}^{\ell}}\Big(\sum_{\ell=1}^{n_{i}}y_{i}^{\ell}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{n_{i}}x_{i}^{\ell}\Big),

(37)

which is negative due to the choice of
y
.
This shows that
\langle\nabla l_{i,j}^{\tau}(x),y-x\rangle<0
for some
y\in\hat{\Delta}^{\tau}
. Thus,
x\notin\mathcal{S}_{i,j}^{\tau}
, which contradicts the assumption
x\in\EuScript S^{\tau}
.

Next, we will consider case (b). As (
30
) holds, there exists
j\in[n_{i}]
such that

\displaystyle x_{i}^{j}\geq\frac{1}{n_{i}}-\frac{e^{-1/\tau^{1.5}}}{n_{i}(\max_{i}n_{i}+1)}\geq\frac{1}{n_{i}+1}.

(38)

By calculating
\nabla l_{i,j}^{\tau}(x)
using (
21
) under case (b), we have

\displaystyle\nabla l_{i,j}^{\tau}(x)

\displaystyle=2\Bigg(\hat{r}_{i}^{j}+\sum_{k\neq i}\langle\hat{q}_{ik}^{j},x_{k}\rangle-\tau\ln x_{i}^{j}-\frac{1}{\tau^{0.5}}

(39)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\tau\ln(\max_{i}n_{i}+1)\Bigg)\bm{q}_{i,\tau}^{j}(x_{i}),

(40)

where
\bm{q}_{i,\tau}^{j}(x_{i})
is given by (
25
). We note that the multiplier in the expression (
39
) is negative for any
x\in\tilde{\Delta}
, given an appropriate small choice of
\tau
. The reason is that using (
38
),

\displaystyle\hat{r}_{i}^{j}+\sum_{k\neq i}\langle\hat{q}_{ik}^{j},x_{k}\rangle-\tau\ln x_{i}^{j}-\frac{1}{\tau^{0.5}}-\tau\ln(\max_{i}n_{i}+1)

(41)

\displaystyle\leq\hat{r}_{i}^{j}+\sum_{k\neq i}\langle\hat{q}_{ik}^{j},x_{k}\rangle-\frac{1}{\tau^{0.5}}<0.

(42)

where the second inequality holds for sufficiently small

. Therefore,
\nabla l_{i,j}^{\tau}(x)
and
\bm{q}_{i,\tau}^{j}(x_{i})
are the same vectors up to a negative multiplier.

Finally, using a similar argument as in case (a), let us consider a specific vector
y\in\hat{\Delta}
defined by
y_{i}^{j}<x_{i}^{j}
,
\sum_{\ell=1}^{n_{i}}y_{i}^{\ell}\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{n_{i}}x_{i}^{\ell}
,
y_{m}^{\ell}=x_{m}^{\ell}
for all

and
\ell\in[n_{m}]
. Then, using (

), the choice of

, which in turn implies
\langle\nabla l_{i,j}^{\tau}(x),y-x\rangle<0
. Therefore,

, which contradicts the assumption that
x\in\EuScript{S}^{\tau}
.

The obtained contradictions in both cases (a) and (b) imply that
\EuScript{S}^{\tau}
is a subset of the NE points in the game
\Gamma^{\tau,t}
, which completes the proof.
∎

IV
Distributed Solution Approaches

Equipped with the results from the previous section, we now present two heuristic distributed algorithms in this section to obtain an
\epsilon
-NE solution. Of course, in view of Remark
1
, we do not expect our algorithms to always find an
\epsilon
-NE efficiently. However, as we will discuss, the proposed algorithms exhibit some desirable convergence properties.

To that end, we assume throughout this section the existence of a weighted undirected communication graph
G_{w}([N],\EuScript{A})
connecting the players. The set of nodes
[N]
corresponds to
N
players, and the arc
(i,j)\in\EuScript{A}
exists if there is a link between players
i
and
j
. We make the following standard assumption regarding the communication graph
G_{w}

[
20
,
21
]
.

Assumption 1
.

The underlying undirected comm unication graph
G_{w}([N],\EuScript{A})
is connected. The associated non-negative, symmetric mixing matrix
W=[w_{ij}]\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
defines the weights on the undirected arcs such that
w_{ij}>0
if and only if
\{i,j\}\in\EuScript{A}
, and
\sum_{j=1}^{N}w_{ij}=1-\beta
for all
i\in[N]
and some
\beta\in(0,1)
.

IV-A

A Distributed Projected Gradient Descent Algorithm

Our first proposed algorithm uses the result of Proposition
1
, by focusing on solving the optimization problem (
20
). First, following the work
[
20
]
, we aim to adapt a distributed algorithm to calculate stationary points of the function

\displaystyle l^{\tau}(x)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in[N],j\in[n_{i}]}l_{i,j}^{\tau}(x).

(43)

During the optimization procedure, we let each player
i\in[N]
process
n_{i}
estimates of the joint action
x\in\hat{\Delta}
, which are represented by the vectors
\tilde{x}_{i}^{j}(t)\in\hat{\Delta},j\in[n_{i}]
. Specifically, let each player
i
communicate its estimate vectors
\tilde{x}_{i}^{j}(t)
,
j\in[n_{i}]
, via the communication step:

\displaystyle x_{i}^{j}(t)=\sum_{i^{\prime}\in[N],j^{\prime}\in[n_{i^{\prime}}]}w^{j,j^{\prime}}_{i,i^{\prime}}\tilde{x}_{i^{\prime}}^{j^{\prime}}(t)\ \ \ \forall j\in[n_{i}],

where the weight parameters
w^{j,j^{\prime}}_{i,i^{\prime}}
are chosen as follows:

\displaystyle w^{j,j^{\prime}}_{i,i^{\prime}}=\begin{cases}w_{ij},&\mbox{ if $i\neq i^{\prime}$ and $j=j^{\prime}=1$},\\
\frac{\beta}{n_{i}},\,\,&\mbox{ if $i=i^{\prime}$},\\
0,\,\,\,\,\,\,&\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

(44)

Then, each player
i
updates its estimates
\tilde{x}_{i}^{j}(t)
,
j\in[n_{i}]
at the next time step using projected gradient descent method with an appropriate choice of step-size (see (

Now, we have the following result from
[

.

[Deterministic version of
[

IV-B

A Distributed Fixed-Point Approximation Algorithm

Our second proposed algorithm relies on the result of Lemma
2
to directly approximate the fixed point of the best response dynamics in the game
\Gamma^{\tau}
. More precisely, the algorithm aims to solve the system of equations
\nabla l^{\tau}_{ij}(x)=0
for each
i,j
in a distributed manner. After each update, the players average their estimates with those of others to ensure they converge to the same point in the limit. The overall procedure is summarized in Algorithm
2
. As before, we use the notation
\tilde{x}_{i}^{j}
to denote the
j
th estimate of the joint action that player
i
processes as time progresses, whereas
\tilde{x}_{i}^{j,\ell}

i
in the estimate vector
\tilde{x}_{i}^{j}

Proposition 2
.

Let Assumption
1
hold. If the dynamics of Algorithm
2
converge, they converge to an
\epsilon
-NE of the game
\Gamma
.

Proof.

Using (
21

\displaystyle x_{i}^{j}=\frac{\exp\left(\frac{1}{\tau}(\hat{r}_{i}^{j}+a^{j}_{i}x_{-i})\right)}{1+\sum_{\ell}\exp\left(\frac{1}{\tau}(\hat{r}^{\ell}_{i}+a^{\ell}_{i}x_{-i})\right)}\quad\forall i,j,

where

is shorthand for the vector
(\hat{q}^{j}_{ik},k\neq i)

2

\epsilon
-NE.

Now, if the dynamics of Algorithm

converge to some
x

i\in[N]

\lim_{t\to\infty}\tilde{x}^{j}_{i}(t)=x\ \forall i,j
, then, due to the averaging step in (

) and Assumption

, the estimates of all the players must converge to the same point. That is,
\lim_{t\to\infty}\tilde{x}^{j}_{i}(t)=x\ \forall i,j
, and in particular,
\lim_{t\to\infty}x^{j}_{i}(t)=x\ \forall i,j
. Finally, by taking the limit of both sides of the update dynamics in (
49

must satisfy (

\epsilon

Conclusion

This work formulates a multi-objective optimization approach to approximate Nash equilibria in a specific class of convex games over the simplex. The main advantage of this formulation lies in the fact that the common stationary points are zeros of the objective functions as well as their gradients, and they coincide with the approximate Nash equilibria in the game under consideration. These characteristics of the approximate Nash equilibria allow for the application of heuristics for their computation. Future work will focus on more sophisticated computation techniques with theoretical guarantees regarding subsequence convergence to an approximate Nash equilibrium.

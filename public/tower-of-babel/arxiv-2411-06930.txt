Existence of Solutions to a super-Liouville equation with Boundary Conditions
Mingyang Han

Abstract
In this paper, we study the existence of solutions to a type of super-Liouville equation on the compact Riemannian surface M M with boundary and with its Euler characteristic χ ⁡ ( M ) < 0 \chi(M)<0 . The boundary condition couples a Neumann condition for functions and a chirality boundary condition for spinors. Due to the generality of the equation, we introduce a weighted Dirac operator based on the solution to a related Liouville equation. Then we construct a Nehari manifold according to the spectral decomposition of the weighted Dirac operator, and use minimax theory on this Nehari manifold to show the existence of the non-trivial solutions.

Keywords
Non-trivial solutions, Super-Liouville equation, Neumann-Chirality boundary problem, Minimax theory

\begin{cases}{-\Delta u=h\left(x\right){e^{2u}}-{K_{g}}},&\text{in}\ {M^{o}},\\
{\frac{{\partial u}}{{\partial n}}=\lambda(x){e^{u}}-{k_{g}}},&\text{on}\ {\partial M}.\\
\end{cases}

(4)

For this equation, there have been many studies on the special cases of (

Theorem A
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian surface with
\partial M\neq\emptyset
and
\chi\left(M\right)<0
. If
h(x)\in C^{\infty}(M)
and
\lambda(x)\in C^{\infty}(\partial M)
are non-positive functions with
h(x)\not\equiv 0
or
\lambda(x)\not\equiv 0
, then the problem (
4
) admits a unique solution.

Under the assumptions on the coefficients in Theorem A, a standard procedure can be used to obtain that the unique solution to (
4
) is a smooth solution. In the sequel, we denote that
f
is the solution to the problem (
4
).

We aim to find non-trivial solutions
(u,\psi)
to (
3
). With the aid of Theorem A, after a conformal transformation with respect to a metric, we can simplify the problem (
3
) to

\begin{cases}{-\Delta u=h\left(x\right){e^{2u}}+{1}+\rho{e^{u}}{{\left|\psi\right|}^{2}}},&\text{in}\ {M^{o}},\\
{D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\psi=\rho{e^{u}}\psi},&\text{in}\ {M^{o}},\\
{\frac{{\partial u}}{{\partial n}}=\lambda(x){e^{u}}},&\text{on}\ {\partial M},\\
{{\mathbf{B}^{\pm}}\psi=0},&\text{on}\ {\partial M},\end{cases}

(5)

see Lemma
3.1
. The problem (
3
) is the Euler-Lagrange equations of the real value functional

{E_{\rho}}\left({u,\psi}\right)=\int_{M}{\left\{{{{\left|{\nabla u}\right|}^{2}}-2u-h{e^{2u}}+2\left\langle{D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\psi-\rho{e^{u}}\psi,\psi}\right\rangle}\right\}d{v_{g}}}-2\int_{\partial M}\lambda{e^{u}}d{\sigma_{g}},

(6)

which is defined on the Sobolev space
{H^{1}}\left(M\right)\times{H_{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}}(\mathbb{S}M)
for real-valued functions and spinors, see Section 2. The difficulty in finding non-trivial solutions to this problem is due to the strong indefiniteness of the functional caused by the Dirac operator. In order to overcome this difficulty, the usually method is to construct the Nehari manifold, which is a natural constraint, for example see
[
21
]
. While for the general equation (
3
), the usual Nehari manifold will not work. Therefore we introduce the weighted Dirac operator with the weight
e^{-f}
, where

is the unique solution to (
4
). Based of the spectrum of the weighted Dirac operator, we can construct a Nehari manifold, which is also a natural constraint. The process, up to
\rho
, of finding non-trivial solutions uses different Minimax methods on the constructed Nehari manifold. When
\rho
is less than the first positive eigenvalue of the weighted Dirac operator, we can use the mountain pass theorem. When
\rho
is between different positive eigenvalues, we can use the homotopic linking theorem. Thanks to the topological assumption
\chi(M)<0
, the
(PS)
sequence on the constructed Nehari manifold is a convergent sequence. Therefore we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemann surface with a fixed spin
structure, and with
\partial M\neq\emptyset
and
\chi\left(M\right)<0
. Suppose that

and
\lambda(x)\in C^{\infty}(\partial M)
are negative functions, meanwhile
\mathbf{B}^{\pm}
is a chirality boundary condition. Then for any
\rho>0
, there is a solution to (
3
), and if
0<\rho\notin
Spec
\left({{e^{-{f}}}{D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}_{g}}}\right)
, there is a non-trivial solution to (
3
). Here
f
is the unique solution to (
4
).

.

We would like to mention that when

is a closed Riemann surface with

, we yield by using the similar argument as Theorem

that, for any negative function

and for any
\rho>0
, there is a solution to (

), and if
0<\rho\notin
Spec
\left({{e^{-{f}}}{D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}_{g}}}\right)
, there is a non-trivial solution to (

is the unique solution to

-\Delta u=K{e^{2u}}-{K_{g}},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ in }M.

So in this paper we also extend the existence result in

for

\left\{{\begin{array}[]{*{20}{l}}{-\Delta u=-{e^{2u}}-K_{g}+\rho{e^{u}}{{\left|\psi\right|}^{2}},}&{{\text{in}}\;M,}\\
{D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\psi=\rho{e^{u}}\psi,}&{{\text{in}}\;M.}\end{array}}\right.

2
Preliminary

2.1
Moser-Trudinger inequalities

Moser-Trudinger type inequalities are important tools for dealing with Liouville type equations. As usual, we use
H^{1}(M)
to represent the Sobolev space
W^{1,2}(M,\mathbb{R})
on a manifold
M
and use
|M|
and
|\partial M|
to represent the volumes of
M
and
\partial M
, respectively. A Moser-Trudinger type inequality on the compact Riemann surface with boundary from
[
9
]
is

\log\int_{M}e^{2u}dv_{g}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}dv_{g}+\frac{2}{|M|}\int_{M}udv_{g}+C,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall u\in H^{1}(M).

(7)

While the Moser-Trudinger type inequality on the boundary of the compact Riemann surface from
[
35
]
is

\log\int_{\partial M}e^{u}d\sigma_{g}\leq\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}dv_{g}+\frac{1}{|\partial M|}\int_{\partial M}ud\sigma_{g}+C,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall u\in H^{1}(M).

(8)

These inequalities will be used to prove the convergence of
(PS)_{c}
sequences in the sequel.

2.2
Spinors

In this subsection, we will briefly review the knowledge about spinors. Let
(M,g)
be a Riemann surface of genus
\gamma
with a spin fixed structure.
\mathbb{S}M
is the spinor bundle on
M
with a natural Hermitian product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
induced by
g
. A spinor
\psi\in\Gamma(\mathbb{S}M)
is a section of
\mathbb{S}M
. Let
Cl\left({{T_{x}}M,{g_{x}}}\right)
represent the Clifford algebra generated by
T_{x}M
and
g_{x}
with Clifford relationship

{X_{i}}\cdot{X_{j}}+{X_{j}}\cdot{X_{i}}=-2g_{x}({X_{i}},{X_{j}}).

Donate

Cl(M,g)=\mathop{\coprod}\limits_{x\in M}Cl\left({{T_{x}}M,{g_{x}}}\right).

There is a representation

\rho:TM\to End(\mathbb{S}M),

which can be extended to
Cl(M,g)

\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{\rho:}&{Cl(M,g)\otimes\mathbb{S}M}&\to&{\mathbb{S}M}\\
&{\sigma\otimes\psi}&\mapsto&{\rho(\sigma)\cdot\psi}\end{array}.

We will abbreviate
\rho(X)\cdot\psi
as
X\cdot\psi
. The representation
\rho
is compatible with connection and Hermitian metrics. This means that if
\left\{e_{1},e_{2}\right\}
is a local orthonormal frame on
TM
with

e_{i}\cdot e_{j}\cdot\psi+e_{j}\cdot e_{i}\cdot\psi=-2\delta_{ij}\psi,

and
\nabla
is the spin connection on
\mathbb{S}M
,
then
\left\{e_{1},e_{2}\right\}
satisfies

{\nabla_{{e_{i}}}}\left({{e_{j}}\cdot\psi}\right)=\left({{\nabla_{{e_{i}}}}{e_{j}}}\right)\cdot\psi+{e_{j}}\cdot\left({{\nabla_{{e_{i}}}}\psi}\right),

and

\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle=\left\langle e_{i}\cdot\varphi,e_{i}\cdot\psi\right\rangle.

The Dirac operator
D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}
is defined by

D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\psi:=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{2}e_{\alpha}\cdot\nabla_{e_{\alpha}}\psi.

For more information on spinors, see
[
3
,
20
,
24
]
.

If
M
be a compact Riemann surface with
\partial M\neq\emptyset
, the Dirac operator
D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}
satisfies

\int_{M}\langle\psi,D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\varphi\rangle dv=\int_{M}\langle D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\psi,\varphi\rangle dv-\int_{\partial M}\langle\vec{n}\cdot\psi,\varphi\rangle ds,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\psi,\varphi\in\Gamma(\mathbb{S}M).

It is clear that the Dirac operator is in general not self-adjoint. However, under some boundary conditions, the operator
D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}
is self-adjoint. From
[
16
, Definition 1.5.1]
, we know that an elliptic boundary condition for a Dirac operator is a pseudo-differential operator
\mathbf{B}:L^{2}(\mathbb{S}M)\longrightarrow L^{2}(V)
, where
V
is some Hermitian vector bundle on
\partial M
, such that the boundary value problem

\begin{cases}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hbox to8.55695pt{\hfil\sl/\hfil}\hss}D\varphi=\Phi},&\ on\ M,\\
{\mathbf{B}({{\left.\varphi\right|}_{\partial M}})=\chi},&\ on\ \partial M,\\
\end{cases}

has smooth solutions up to a finite-dimensional kernel for any given smooth data
\Phi\in\Gamma(\mathbb{S}M)
and
\chi\in\Gamma(V)
belonging to a certain subspace with finite codimension. Next, we will introduce a elliptic boundary condition which is a chirality boundary condition for spinors.

The chirality boundary condition was introduced in
[
15
]
when proving the positive mass theorem of black holes. A chirality operator
G
is an endomorphism of the spinor bundle
\mathbb{S}M
satisfying:

G^{2}=I,\hskip 10.00002pt\langle G\psi,G\varphi\rangle=\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle,\hskip 10.00002pt\nabla_{X}(G\psi)=G\nabla_{X}\psi,\hskip 10.00002ptX\cdot G\psi=-G(X\cdot\psi),

for any
X\in\Gamma(TM),\psi,\varphi\in\Gamma(\mathbb{S}M)
. Here
I
denotes the identity endomorphism of
\mathbb{S}M
. Usually, we take
G=\rho\left(\omega_{2}\right)
, the Clifford multiplication by the complex volume form
\omega_{2}=ie_{1}e_{2}
, where
\left\{e_{1},e_{2}\right\}
is an local oriented orthonormal frame on
M
.

Denote by
\mathbb{S}\partial M
the restricted spinor bundle with induced Hermitian product. Let
\vec{n}
be the outward unit normal vector field on
\partial M
. One can verify that
\vec{n}\cdot G:\Gamma(\mathbb{S}\partial M)\rightarrow\Gamma(\mathbb{S}\partial M)
is a self-adjoint endomorphism satisfying

(\vec{n}\cdot G)^{2}=I,\hskip 10.00002pt\langle\vec{n}\cdot G\psi,\varphi\rangle=\langle\psi,\vec{n}\cdot G\varphi\rangle.

Hence, we can decompose
\mathbb{S}\partial M=V^{+}\oplus V^{-}
, where
V^{\pm}
are the eigenbundles of
\vec{n}\cdot G
corresponding to the eigenvalues
\pm 1
. One verifies that the orthogonal projection onto the eigenbundles
V^{\pm}
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}^{\pm}:L^{2}(\mathbb{S}\partial M)

\displaystyle\rightarrow L^{2}\left(V^{\pm}\right),

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle\mapsto\frac{1}{2}(I\pm\vec{n}\cdot G)\psi,

defines a local elliptic boundary condition for the Dirac operator
D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}
(see
[
18
]
). We say that a spinor
\psi
satisfies the chirality boundary conditions
\mathbf{B}^{\pm}
if

\left.\mathbf{B}^{\pm}\psi\right|_{\partial M}=0.

According to
[
12
]
,

\left.\mathbf{B}^{\pm}\psi\right|_{\partial M}=0\Leftrightarrow\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}M)\text{ and }\left.\psi\right|_{\partial M}\in V^{\mp}.

For the space
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}M)
, define an inner product

(\psi,\varphi)_{L^{2}}=\int_{M}\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle dv_{g}

for each
\psi,\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}M)
.
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}M)
is the completion of
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}M)
with respect to the corresponding norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}}=(\cdot,\cdot)_{L^{2}}^{1/2}
. On the boundary
\partial M
,
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}\partial M)
is also the completion of
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}\partial M)
under the norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\partial M)}=(\cdot,\cdot)_{L^{2}(\partial M)}^{1/2}
. Then we have
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}\partial M)=L^{2}(V^{+})\oplus L^{2}(V^{-})
. Define

(\psi,\varphi)_{H^{1}}=(\psi,\varphi)_{L^{2}}+(\nabla\psi,\nabla\varphi)_{L^{2}},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\psi,\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}M).

Then
H^{1}(\mathbb{S}M)
is the completion of the space
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}M)
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{H^{1}}=(\cdot,\cdot)_{H^{1}}^{1/2}
. Since
D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}
is a first order operator, it extends to a linear operator
D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}:H^{1}(\mathbb{S}M)\rightarrow

L^{2}(\mathbb{S}M)
. Let

Dom(D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss})=\left\{\psi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{S}M):\left.\mathbf{B}^{\pm}\psi\right|_{\partial M}=0\right\}.

Then the Dirac operator
D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}
with chirality boundary condition
\left.\mathbf{B}^{\pm}\psi\right|_{\partial M}=0
is well defined in the domain
Dom(D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss})
. For
\psi,\varphi\in Dom(D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss})
, the integrated version of Lichnerowitz Formula indicate

\int_{M}\langle D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\psi,\varphi\rangle dv_{g}-\int_{M}\langle\psi,D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\varphi\rangle dv_{g}=-\int_{\partial M}\langle\vec{n}\cdot\psi,\varphi\rangle d\sigma_{g},

where
\vec{n}
denotes the unit inner normal on
\partial M
. Since
\mathbf{B}^{\pm}\psi=\mathbf{B}^{\pm}\varphi=0
on
\partial M
, we have
\vec{n}\cdot G\psi=\pm\psi,\ \vec{n}\cdot G\varphi=\pm\varphi
. In the case of
\vec{n}\cdot G\psi=\psi,\ \vec{n}\cdot G\varphi=\varphi
, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle{\vec{n}\cdot\psi,\varphi}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle{\vec{n}\cdot\psi,\left({\vec{n}\cdot G}\right)\varphi}\right\rangle=\left\langle{\left({\vec{n}\cdot G}\right)\left({\vec{n}\cdot\psi}\right),\varphi}\right\rangle\hfill

\displaystyle=\langle G\psi,\varphi\rangle=\langle G\left({\vec{n}\cdot G\psi}\right),\varphi\rangle\hfill

\displaystyle=-\langle{G^{2}}(\vec{n}\cdot\psi),\varphi\rangle=-\langle\vec{n}\cdot\psi,\varphi\rangle.

Similarly, in the case of
\vec{n}\cdot G\psi=-\psi,\ \vec{n}\cdot G\varphi=-\varphi
, we also have

\left\langle{\vec{n}\cdot\psi,\varphi}\right\rangle=-\langle\vec{n}\cdot\psi,\varphi\rangle.

Hence, if we suppose that
\psi,\varphi\in L^{2}(\Gamma(\mathbb{S}M))
satisfy the chirality boundary condition
\mathbf{B}^{\pm}
, then

\langle\vec{n}\cdot\psi,\varphi\rangle=0,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ on }\partial M.

In particular, there holds

\int_{\partial M}\langle\vec{n}\cdot\psi,\varphi\rangle d\sigma_{g}=0,

which make Dirac operator
D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}
self-adjoint.

2.3
Sobolev spaces for spinors

We first describe the spectrums of Dirac operator
D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}
, donated by Spec(
D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}
), under the chirality boundary condition
{\mathbf{B}^{\pm}}\psi\left|{{}_{\partial M}=0}\right.
. First, there is a basic fact from
[
16
, Proposition 1.5.4]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\left(M^{n},g\right)
be a compact Riemannian spin manifold with non-empty boundary
\partial M
. Then the chirality boundary condition is elliptic. Moreover, the spectrum of
\hbox to0.0pt{\hbox to8.55695pt{\hfil\sl/\hfil}\hss}D
is a real and discrete unbounded sequence under the chirality boundary condition.

Therefore, in

\displaystyle\int_{M}{\left\{{{{\left|{\nabla\left({f+v}\right)}\right|}^{2}}-h{e^{2\left({f+v}\right)}}-2\left({f+v}\right)}\right\}d{v_{g}}}-2\int_{\partial M}{\lambda{e^{f+v}}}d{\sigma_{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I\left({{f}}\right)+2\int_{M}{\nabla{f}\nabla v}d{v_{g}}+\int_{M}{{{\left|{\nabla v}\right|}^{2}}}d{v_{g}}-\int_{M}{h{e^{2{f}}}\left({{e^{2v}}-1}\right)}d{v_{g}}-2\int_{M}{v}d{v_{g}}

\displaystyle-2\int_{\partial M}{\lambda{e^{{f}}}\left({{e^{v}}-1}\right)}d{\sigma_{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I\left({{f}}\right)+\int_{M}{{{\left|{\nabla{v}}\right|}^{2}}}d{v_{g}}-\int_{M}{h{e^{2{f}}}\left({{e^{2v}}-1-2v}\right)}d{v_{g}}-2\int_{\partial M}{\lambda{e^{{f}}}\left({{e^{v}}-1-v}\right)}d{\sigma_{g}}.

Note that
\lambda(x)<0
and
{e^{x}}-1-x\geq 0
for sufficient small
x
. Hence, if
u
is close to
f
,we have from Jensen’s inequality

\displaystyle I(u)\geq

\displaystyle I\left(f\right)+\left\|{\nabla v}\right\|_{2}^{2}+{c_{0}}{\left|M\right|_{g}}\frac{1}{|M|_{g}}\int_{M}{\left({{e^{2v}}-1-2v}\right)d{v_{g}}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle I\left(f\right)+\left\|{\nabla v}\right\|_{2}^{2}+{c_{0}}{\left|M\right|_{g}}\left({{e^{2\bar{v}}}-1-2\bar{v}}\right).

Here
\bar{v}=\frac{1}{|M|_{g}}\int_{M}vdv_{g}
. We denote
\hat{v}=v-\bar{v}
. It is well know that we can take the equivalent norm of
v\in H^{1}(M)
as

|\overline{v}|^{2}+\|\nabla\widehat{v}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\sim\|v\|_{H^{1}}^{2}=\left\|{u-{f}}\right\|_{{H^{1}}}^{2}.

Now we choose a sufficiently small positive constant
r_{0}
such that
|\overline{v}|^{2}+\|\nabla\widehat{v}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}<r_{0}^{2}
. Denote that
t^{2}=|\overline{v}|^{2}+\|\nabla\widehat{v}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
. Then when
|\overline{v}|^{2}\geq\frac{t^{2}}{2}\geq\|\nabla\widehat{v}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
, by
{e^{x}}-1-x\geq C^{\prime}{\left|x\right|^{2}}
for
\left|x\right|
sufficiently small, we have

\displaystyle{I}\left({u}\right)

\displaystyle\geq I\left({{f}}\right)+\left\|{\nabla v}\right\|_{2}^{2}+{c_{0}}\left|M\right|_{g}\left({{e^{2\overline{v}}}-1-2\overline{v}}\right)\hfill

\displaystyle\geq I\left({{f}}\right)+C(r_{0})t^{2}.

When
\|\nabla\widehat{v}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\geq\frac{t^{2}}{2}\geq|\overline{v}|^{2}
, we also have

I(u)\geq I\left({{f}}\right)+\frac{1}{2}t^{2}.

Thus in either case we obtain that

I(u)\geq I\left({{f}}\right)+C(r_{0})\left\|{u-{f}}\right\|_{{H^{1}}}^{2},

(15)

for
r_{0}
sufficiently small.

The functional
J
has neither upper nor lower bounds, which is caused by the strong indefiniteness of the Dirac operator. To overcome this difficulty, we use the Nehari manifold introduced in
[
21
]
. The purpose of the Nehari manifold here is to eliminate the weighted negative spatial part of the Sobolev spinor space.

3.2
The Nehari Manifold

Based on the weighted splitting in (
12
), let

D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}_{f}^{\pm}:H_{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{S}M)\rightarrow H_{B,f}^{\frac{1}{2},\pm}(\mathbb{S}M)

be the orthonormal projection. Consider the map

\displaystyle G:H^{1}(M)\times H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{B}(\mathbb{S}M)

\displaystyle\rightarrow H^{\frac{1}{2},-}_{B,f}(\mathbb{S}M),

\displaystyle(u,\psi)

\displaystyle\mapsto D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}_{f}^{-}\left[(e^{f}+|D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}|)^{-1}\left(D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\psi-\rho e^{u}\psi\right)\right].

Note that the mapping
G
is related to the solution
f
, for simplicity, we still refer to it as
G
. Define the Nehari manifold
\mathcal{N}_{f}=G^{-1}(0)
, which is non-empty since
(u,0)\in\mathcal{N}_{f}
for any
u\in H^{1}(M)
. Since
H_{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{S}M)
is a Hilbert space with the inner product

\left\langle\psi,\phi\right\rangle_{H_{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{S}M)}=\int_{M}{{e^{{f}}}\left\langle{\psi,\phi}\right\rangle d{v_{g}}}+\int_{M}{\left\langle{{{\left|D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\right|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\psi,{{\left|D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\right|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\phi}\right\rangle d{v_{g}}}=\langle(e^{f}+|D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}|)\psi,\phi\rangle_{H_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{S}M)\times H_{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{S}M)},

We know that for any
\varphi\in H_{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{S}M)

\displaystyle\langle G(u,\psi),\varphi\rangle_{H_{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{S}M)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle(e^{f}+|D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}|)^{-1}\left(D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\psi-\rho e^{u}\psi\right),D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}_{f}^{-}(\varphi)\rangle_{H_{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{S}M)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\psi-\rho e^{u}\psi,D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}_{f}^{-}(\varphi)\rangle_{H_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{S}M)\times H_{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{S}M)}.

Hence

\mathcal{N}_{f}=\left\{(u,\psi)\in H^{1}(M)\times H_{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\mathbb{S}M\right):\int_{M}\left\langle D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\psi-\rho e^{u}\psi,\zeta\right\rangle dv_{g}=0,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\zeta\in H^{\frac{1}{2},-}_{B,f}\right\}.

From above we have

\displaystyle(u,\psi)\in\mathcal{N}_{f}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow G(u,\psi)=0\Leftrightarrow\int_{M}\left\langle D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\psi-\rho e^{u}\psi,\zeta\right\rangle dv_{g}=0,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\zeta\in H^{\frac{1}{2},-}_{B,f},

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow G_{j}(u,\psi):=\int_{M}\left\langle D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\psi-\rho e^{u}\psi,\varphi^{f}_{j}\right\rangle dv_{g}=0,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall j<0.

Thus we have

\mathcal{N}_{f}=G^{-1}(0)=\bigcap_{j<0}G_{j}^{-1}(0).

Note that, for each
u
fixed, the subset

\mathcal{N}_{f,u}:=\left\{\left.\psi\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{B}(\mathbb{S}M)\right\rvert\,(u,\psi)\in\mathcal{N}_{f}\right\}=\operatorname{ker}\left[D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}_{f}^{-}\circ(e^{f}+|D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}|)^{-1}\circ\left(D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}-\rho e^{u}\right)\right]

is a linear subspace of infinite dimension. Hence we have a fibration
\mathcal{N}_{f}\rightarrow H^{1}(M)
with fiber
\mathcal{N}_{f,u}
over
u\in H^{1}(M)
. The total space
\mathcal{N}_{f}
is contractible.

Now let
\left(u_{1},\psi_{1}\right)
be a critical point in
\mathcal{N}_{f}
of
\left.J_{\rho}\right|_{\mathcal{N}_{f}},i.e.\ \nabla^{\mathcal{N}_{f}}J\left(u_{1},\psi_{1}\right)=0
. Then there exist
\mu_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\mathrm{d}J_{\rho}\left(u_{1},\psi_{1}\right)=\sum_{j<0}\mu_{j}\mathrm{~d}G_{j}\left(u_{1},\psi_{1}\right).

Testing both sides with tangent vectors of the form
(0,h)
, we have

\int_{M}\left\langle D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\psi_{1}-\rho e^{u_{1}}\psi_{1},h\right\rangle dv_{g}=\sum_{j<0}\mu_{j}\int_{M}\left\langle D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}h-\rho e^{u_{1}}h,\varphi_{j}^{f}\right\rangle dv_{g}.

In particular, takeing
\varphi=h=\sum_{j<0}\mu_{j}\varphi_{j}^{f}\in H^{\frac{1}{2},-}_{B,{f}}
, we obtain

0=\int_{M}\left\langle D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\psi_{1}-\rho e^{u_{1}}\psi_{1},\varphi\right\rangle dv_{g}=\int_{M}\left\langle D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\varphi-\rho e^{u_{1}}\varphi,\varphi\right\rangle dv_{g}\leq-C\|\varphi\|^{2}-\int_{M}\rho e^{u_{1}}|\varphi|^{2}dv_{g}.

Thus
\varphi=0
, hence
\mu_{j}=0
for all
j<0
. Hence we have
\mathrm{d}J_{\rho}\left(u_{1},\psi_{1}\right)=0
. Therefore, we have come to the following conclusion.

Lemma 3.3
.

The Nehari manifold
\mathcal{N}_{f}
is a natural constraint for
E_{\rho}
, namely every critical point of
\left.E_{\rho}\right|_{\mathcal{N}_{f}}
is an unconstrained critical point of
E_{\rho}
.

Now consider the functional with
(u,\psi)\in\mathcal{N}_{f}
. Since
\int_{M}\left\langle D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\psi-\rho e^{u}\psi,\psi_{f}^{-}\right\rangle dv_{g}=0,
for
(u,\psi)\in\mathcal{N}_{f}
, we have

\displaystyle E_{\rho}(u,\psi)

\displaystyle=I(u)+J(u,\psi)=I(u)+2\int_{M}\left(\langle D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\psi,\psi\rangle-\rho e^{u}|\psi|^{2}\right)dv_{g}

(16)

\displaystyle=I(u)+2\int_{M}{\left\langle{\left({D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}-\rho{e^{{f}}}}\right)\psi,{\psi_{f}^{+}}}\right\rangle}d{v_{g}}+2\int_{M}{\rho{e^{{f}}}}\left({1-{e^{u-{f}}}}\right)\left\langle{\psi,{\psi_{f}^{+}}}\right\rangle d{v_{g}}.

For the last integral above, there exists
\theta(x)\in[0,1]
such that

\displaystyle 2\left|{\int_{M}\rho{e^{f}}\left({1-{e^{u-f}}}\right)\left\langle{\psi,\psi_{f}^{+}}\right\rangle d{v_{g}}}\right|\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{M}{{e^{\theta(x)\left({u-f}\right)}}\left|{u-f}\right|}\left|\psi\right|\left|{\psi_{f}^{+}}\right|d{v_{g}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C{\left\|{{e^{u-f}}}\right\|_{{L^{4}}}}{\left\|{u-f}\right\|_{{L^{4}}}}{\left\|\psi\right\|_{{L^{4}}}}{\left\|{\psi_{f}^{+}}\right\|_{{L^{4}}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left({{r_{0}}}\right){\left\|{u-f}\right\|_{{H^{1}}}}\left\|\psi\right\|\left\|{\psi_{f}^{+}}\right\|,

by Moser-Trudinger inequality for a sufficiently small
r_{0}
such that
{\left\|{u-f}\right\|_{{H^{1}}}}<{r_{0}}
. For the middle integral of (
16
), we write
\psi=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}}a_{j}\varphi^{f}_{j}
. Then we have

2\int_{M}\left\langle(D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}-\rho{e^{{f}}})\psi,\psi_{f}^{+}\right\rangle dv_{g}=\sum_{j>0}2\left(\lambda^{f}_{j}-\rho\right)a_{j}^{2}.

In the sequel, we will assume that
\rho\notin\operatorname{Spec}(e^{-f}D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss})
. Then the above summation can be split into two parts

2\int_{M}\left\langle(D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}-\rho{e^{{f}}})\psi,\psi_{f}^{+}\right\rangle dv_{g}=-\sum_{0<\lambda^{f}_{j}<\rho}2\left(\rho-\lambda^{f}_{j}\right)a_{j}^{2}+\sum_{\lambda^{f}_{j}>\rho}2\left(\lambda^{f}_{j}-\rho\right)a_{j}^{2}.

(17)

3.3
Existence via minimax theory

Next, we will use different minimax theorems in variational methods to find the existence of solutions to equation (
5
) based on the different ranges of parameter
\rho
.

3.3.1
0<\rho<\lambda^{f}_{1}

Firstly, let us consider the easier case
0<\rho<\lambda^{f}_{1}
. In this case, since the first part of the summation of (
17
) vanishes, we can use the mountain pass theorem on the Nehari manifold
\mathcal{N}_{f}
to find the critical point of the functional
E_{\rho}
. To this purpose, let
(u,\psi)\in\mathcal{N}_{f}
be close to
(f,0)
. The constraint that defines
\mathcal{N}_{f}
, i.e.
D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}^{-}(1+|D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}|)^{-1}\left(D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\psi-\rho e^{u}\psi\right)=0
, implies

\int_{M}\left\langle D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\psi-\rho e^{u}\psi,D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}_{f}^{-}\psi\right\rangle dv_{g}=0.

Since
\left\|e^{u-f}\right\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\left(1+\|e^{u-f}\|_{H^{1}}\right)\leq C
for
\|u\|
uniformly bounded, we have

\displaystyle C{\left\|{\psi_{f}^{-}}\right\|^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{M}\left\langle D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\psi,\psi_{f}^{-}\right\rangle dv_{g}

\displaystyle=-\rho\int_{M}e^{u}\left\langle\psi_{f}^{+}+\psi_{f}^{-},\psi_{f}^{-}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\rho\int_{M}e^{u-f}e^{f}\left|\psi_{f}^{-}\right|^{2}dv_{g}-\rho\int_{M}e^{u-f}e^{f}\left\langle\psi_{f}^{+},\psi_{f}^{-}\right\rangle dv_{g}

\displaystyle\leq C\rho{\left({\int_{M}{{e^{2f}}\left|{\psi_{f}^{+}}\right|^{4}}d{v_{g}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}}{\left({\int_{M}{{e^{2f}}\left|{\psi_{f}^{-}}\right|^{4}}d{v_{g}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\rho\left\|{\psi_{f}^{+}}\right\|\left\|{\psi_{f}^{-}}\right\|.

Hence we get

\left\|\psi_{f}^{-}\right\|\leq C\rho\left\|\psi_{f}^{+}\right\|.

(18)

This means that if there is a non-zero negative part in spinor space of a spinor
\psi
, there must be a non-zero positive part of a spinor
\psi
provided
(u,\psi)\in\mathcal{N}_{f}
. Then, locally near
(f,0)
in
\mathcal{N}_{f}
, we have

\displaystyle E_{\rho}(u,\psi)

\displaystyle\geq I(f)+C\left\|{u-{f}}\right\|_{{H^{1}}}^{2}+C^{-1}\left(1-\frac{\rho}{\lambda_{1}^{f}}\right)\left\|\psi_{f}^{+}\right\|^{2}-C\|u-f\|_{H^{1}}\|\psi\|\left\|\psi_{f}^{+}\right\|

\displaystyle\geq I(f)+C\left\|{u-{f}}\right\|_{{H^{1}}}^{2}+C^{-1}\left(1-\frac{\rho}{\lambda_{1}^{f}}-C^{2}\|\psi\|^{2}\right)\left\|\psi_{f}^{+}\right\|^{2},

where we have used Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for the last term, of cubic order. It follows that when
r_{0}^{2}>\left\|{u-{f}}\right\|_{{H^{1}}}^{2}+\|\psi\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}=r^{2}>0
is small, there exists a continuous function
\theta(r)>0
such that

E_{\rho}(u,\psi)\geq E(f,0)+\theta(r).

On the other hand, we can choose a fixed large constant
t\in H^{1}(M)
such that
\rho e^{t}>\lambda^{f}_{1}+1
and then take
s>0
large such that

\displaystyle E_{\rho}\left(f+t,s\varphi_{1}^{f}\right)=

\displaystyle I({f})+\int_{M}{-2{t}-h{e^{2{f}}}}\left({{e^{2{t}}}-1}\right)d{v_{g}}+\int_{\partial M}{-\lambda{e^{{f}}}\left({{e^{{t}}}-1}\right)d\sigma}

\displaystyle+2\int_{M}{\left\langle{\left({D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}-\rho{e^{{f}}}{e^{t}}}\right){s\varphi_{1}^{f}},{s\varphi_{1}^{f}}}\right\rangle}d{v_{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I(f)+\left({{c_{1}}{e^{2{t}}}+c_{2}e^{t}-{c_{3}}{t}-{c_{4}}}\right)-2\left(\rho{e^{{{t}}}-{\lambda^{f}_{1}}}\right){s^{2}}

is negative.

Thus we have the mountain pass geometry locally near
(f,0)
in the Nehari manifold
\mathcal{N}_{f}
. Since
\mathcal{N}_{f}
is contractible, it is connected. Let
\Gamma
be the space of paths connecting
(f,0)
and
\left(f+t,s\varphi_{1}^{f}\right)
inside
\mathcal{N}_{f}
(notice that
\Gamma\neq\emptyset
) parametrized by
\xi\in[0,1]
, and define

c:=\inf_{\alpha\in\Gamma}\sup_{\xi\in[0,1]}E_{\rho}(\alpha(\xi)).

From above arguments we have that
c>I(f)
. Under the
(PS)_{c}
condition on
\left.E_{\rho}\right|_{\mathcal{N}_{f}}
in following Section 4, it follows that
c
is a critical value for
E_{\rho}
with a critical point at this level, which is different from the trivial one. This concludes the proof of Theorem
1.1
for
0<\rho<\lambda^{f}_{1}
.

3.3.2
\rho\in\left(\lambda^{f}_{k},\lambda^{f}_{k+1}\right),k\geq 1

When
\rho\in\left(\lambda^{f}_{k},\lambda^{f}_{k+1}\right)
for some

, we will use the linking method on Banach manifolds to find critical points of the functional

on
\mathcal{N}_{f}
. Firstly, we introduce several definitions and theorems about linking method from
[

]

.

Assume
\mathcal{M}
stands for
C^{2}
-Finsler manifold. Let
\mathcal{D}
be a
k

\mathcal{M}

and

such that
\left.\varphi\right|_{\partial\mathcal{D}}=\left.\mathrm{id}\right|_{\partial\mathcal{D}}
.

Record
F_{c}:=\left\{{x\in\mathcal{M}:F(x)\leq c}\right\}
as the level set of
F
. From
[
5

Theorem 3.5
.

(

Let
\mathcal{D}

(20)

.

(
[
5

)
Let

F
. Set

In order to construct the linking geometric structure and verify (

. Firstly, We decompose the spinor space

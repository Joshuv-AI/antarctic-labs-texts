Opal: An Operator Algebra View of RLHF
Madhava Gaikwad Microsoft mgaikwad@microsoft.com

Abstract
We present Opal, an operator view of reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF). Objectives are expressed as ladders of two primitives on a base utility: additive penalties and multiplicative pairwise weights. We show a simple reduction law with if-and-only-if conditions: such ladders collapse to a normal form on pairwise margins when the reference is fixed, penalties are additive, and weights are independent of intermediate margins. When these assumptions do not hold (reference shift, non-additive gates, score-dependent weights), finite examples witness non-reducibility. Building on this view, we introduce GKPO (Generalized Kernel Preference Object), a canonical schema that many RLHF methods can be expressed in and, when reducible, mapped back from. GKPO provides a standard JSON serialization, canonicalization and hashing rules, and explicit flags with finite witnesses when assumptions fail. As illustrations, we show worked GKPO examples for DPO, RRHF, and ORPO, cross-method conversions via GKPO (where assumptions permit), and minimal stress tests (SHIFT/GATE/SCORE) that surface non-reducibility. We also release a small Python reference library implementing the GKPO schema, canonical hashing, and adapters for DPO and RRHF.

Introduction

Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) has become a central ingredient in aligning large language models
(
Christiano et al. 2017
;
Ouyang et al. 2022
;
Bai et al. 2022a
)
.
Meanwhile, the space of objectives has fragmented into a “zoo”: PPO-RLHF
(
Christiano et al. 2017
)
, direct preference optimization (DPO)
(
Rafailov et al. 2023
)
, ranking-based methods (RRHF)
(
Yuan et al. 2023
)
, offset-regularized objectives (ORPO)
(
Hong et al. 2024
)
, variance-shaping approaches (KTO/GRPO)
(
Ethayarajh et al. 2024
;
Shao et al. 2024
)
, and divergence-modified variants (e.g., f-DPO)
(
Han et al. 2024
)
, among others.
This proliferation raises a basic question: are two objectives fundamentally different, or merely algebraic rearrangements of related operators?

We propose Opal, an operator algebra for RLHF.
In Opal, an objective is a
ladder
built from two primitives on a base score
u
: (i)
additive
penalties that shift scores, and (ii)
multiplicative
pairwise weights that scale margins.
We show a reduction law: such ladders collapse to a single normal form on pairwise margins,
(f^{\ast},w^{\ast})
, if and only if three assumptions hold—(a) the reference is fixed, (b) penalties combine additively, and (c) weights are independent of intermediate margins.
When an assumption fails (reference shift, non-additive gating, or score-dependent weights), one can exhibit small, concrete witnesses where no normal form matches all pairs.
This perspective helps explain equivalences (e.g., PPO-RM
\Rightarrow
DPO under a fixed reference) and clarifies when proposals necessarily differ in decisions or losses.

Building on the algebra, we introduce GKPO (Generalized Kernel Preference Object), a canonical, method-agnostic schema for RLHF.
Any objective can be represented as a GKPO instance; inside the reducible class, GKPO canonicalizes to Opal’s normal form (with a deterministic hash for provenance), and outside it records explicit failure flags with finite witnesses (reference shift / gate / score dependence).
GKPO functions as an interchange: method
X\to
GKPO
\to
method
Y
preserves margins (up to positive scaling) whenever the reduction conditions hold.

Contributions.

•

Operator view for RLHF. We formalize objectives as ladders of penalties and weights and give an if-and-only-if reduction law yielding a normal form on margins under simple assumptions.

•

Non-reducibility witnesses. We provide finite witnesses for three failure modes—reference shift, non-additive gates, and score-dependent weights—indicating when no
(f^{\ast},w^{\ast})
can match all pairs.

•

GKPO schema. We design a compact JSON schema with canonicalization and hashing rules that (i) collect reducible ladders to a normal form and (ii) surface irreducible assumptions with flags and witnesses.

•

Demonstrations. We include worked GKPO examples (DPO, RRHF, ORPO), cross-method conversions via GKPO where applicable, and small stress tests (SHIFT/GATE/SCORE) that reveal non-reducibility.

•

Reference implementation. A lightweight Python library (see
github.com/krimler/opal-gkpo
;
gkpo.py
with
adapters/dpo.py
,
adapters/rrhf.py
) implements the schema, deterministic hashing, adapters, and basic probes to support reproducibility.

Overall, Opal is intended as a
simple organizing view
for reasoning about RLHF objectives and GKPO as a
practical interchange representation
for unifying, comparing, and reproducing method configurations.

Background and Operator Algebra

Setting and notation.

For each prompt
x
with candidate set
\mathcal{Y}(x)
, let a
base score

u:\{(x,y)\}\to\mathbb{R}
induce pairwise differences

\Delta u(x;y^{+},y^{-})\coloneqq u(x,y^{+})-u(x,y^{-})
.
An RLHF objective on pairs
(x,y^{+},y^{-})
is evaluated via a scalar
margin

M
and a strictly increasing
link

g
inside a scalar loss
\ell
:

\mathcal{L}\;=\;\mathbb{E}_{(x,y^{+},y^{-})}\,\ell\bigl(g(\beta\,M(x;y^{+},y^{-}))\bigr),\qquad\beta>0.

We write
y^{+}\succ y^{-}
when the decision induced by
M
prefers
y^{+}
.

2.1
Primitive operators and ladders

We model objectives as
ladders
of operators acting on a base pair
(u,1)
:

(u,1)\xrightarrow{O_{1}}\cdots\xrightarrow{O_{m}}(f,W),

where
f
is a transformed score and
W>0
is a pairwise weight. We also allow a

reference
adjustment
\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}:\{(x,y^{+},y^{-})\}\to\mathbb{R}
.
The canonical margin of a ladder
L
is

M_{L}(x;y^{+},y^{-})\;=\;\bigl(\Delta f(x;y^{+},y^{-})-\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}(x;y^{+},y^{-})\bigr)\cdot W(x;y^{+},y^{-}).

Definition 1
(Primitive operators).

Let
\phi:\{(x,y)\}\to\mathbb{R}
and
\omega:\{(x,y^{+},y^{-})\}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.

•

Additive penalty
\mathcal{A}[\lambda,\phi]
with
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
:

(f,W)\mapsto(\,f-\lambda\phi,\;W\,)
.

•

Multiplicative weight
\mathcal{M}[\omega]
:

(f,W)\mapsto(\,f,\;W\cdot\omega\,)
.

•

Reference adjust
\mathcal{R}[\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}]
:
leaves
(f,W)
unchanged but contributes the subtraction

\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}
to the margin.

A
ladder
is a finite sequence
L=(O_{1},\ldots,O_{m})
with
O_{i}\in\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{M}\cup\mathcal{R}
.

Remark 1

(Commutation/associativity)
.

Additive penalties commute and associate under sum on
f
;
multiplicative weights commute and associate under product on
W
.
Thus, any ladder induces
collected
representatives

f\;=\;u-\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\phi_{i},\qquad W\;=\;\prod_{j\in J}\omega_{j},

where
I
(resp.
J
) indexes additive (resp. multiplicative) operators in
L
.

2.2
Margin semantics and the reducible class
\mathcal{R}

Definition 2
(Margin semantics).

Given
(f,W,\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}})
from a ladder
L
,

M_{L}\;=\;(\Delta f-\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}})\cdot W,\qquad\text{and decisions are }\operatorname{sign}(M_{L})\ \text{(or via $g,\ell$).}

Two ladders
L,L^{\prime}
are
margin-equivalent
if
M_{L}\equiv M_{L^{\prime}}
pointwise.

Definition 3
(Reducible ladders and normal form).

Let
\mathcal{R}
be the set of ladders whose operators satisfy:
(i)
\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}
is
fixed
(does not vary across prompts pairs),
(ii) penalties are
additive
(no data-dependent gates on
\phi
),
(iii) weights are
score-independent
(do not depend on intermediate
\Delta f
).
A
normal form
representative of
L\in\mathcal{R}
is a pair
(f^{\ast},w^{\ast})
with

f^{\ast}\;=\;u-\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\phi_{i},\qquad w^{\ast}\;=\;\prod_{j\in J}\omega_{j},\qquad M_{L}\equiv(\Delta f^{\ast}-\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}})\,w^{\ast}.

Lemma 1

(Closure)
.

Within
\mathcal{R}
, additive penalties are closed under sum on
f
and multiplicative
weights under product on
W
. Therefore any ladder
L\in\mathcal{R}
admits a collected

(f^{\ast},w^{\ast})
computed in one pass over
O_{1},\ldots,O_{m}
.

Definition 4
(Equivalence up to positive scaling).

Write
(f,w)\sim(\tilde{f},\tilde{w})
if there exists
c>0
such that

\Delta\tilde{f}=c\,\Delta f
and
\tilde{w}=c^{-1}w
. Then
M
is invariant:

(\Delta\tilde{f}-\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}})\tilde{w}=(\Delta f-\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}})w
.

Remark 2

(Loss–link invariance)
.

If
g
is strictly increasing, equal margins imply equal decisions and order-consistent
losses
\ell(g(\beta M))
; thus
\sim
preserves optimization and evaluation up to a
positive rescaling of
M
.

2.3
Normal form as a homomorphism

Definition 5
(Free monoid and homomorphism).

Let
\mathcal{L}^{\ast}
be the free monoid of operator strings on the alphabet

\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{M}\cup\mathcal{R}
(concatenation). Define

H:\mathcal{L}^{\ast}\to\mathbb{R}^{\{(x,y)\}}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\{(x,y^{+},y^{-})\}}\times\mathbb{R}^{\{(x,y^{+},y^{-})\}}

by
H(L)=(f^{\ast},w^{\ast},\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}})
with collection rules of
Remark

1
.
Then concatenation maps to sum/product on
(f^{\ast},w^{\ast})
and addition on references.

Proposition 1

(Ladder
\Rightarrow
normal form (algebraic view))
.

Restricted to
\mathcal{R}
,
H
is a surjective monoid homomorphism modulo the scaling
equivalence
\sim
of
Definition

4
. Hence every
L\in\mathcal{R}
reduces to a unique

(f^{\ast},w^{\ast})
up to positive scaling (cf.
Lemma

3
).

2.4
Failure modes (outside
\mathcal{R}
)

We will show explicit finite witnesses that violate reducibility:

•

Reference shift (
Theorem

2
):
\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}
varies across prompts
\Rightarrow

no single
(f^{\ast},w^{\ast})
preserves decisions on all pairs.

•

Non-additive gates (
Theorem

3
): gated penalties cannot be represented by a nonnegative
additive surrogate agreeing on all items.

•

Score-dependent weights (
Theorem

4
): if
w=\psi(\Delta f)
with nonconstant
\psi
,
operator order can flip decisions; no score-independent
w^{\ast}
reproduces all outcomes.

Algorithmic note (one-pass collection).

Given a ladder
L=(O_{1},\ldots,O_{m})
, initialize
(f^{\ast},w^{\ast},\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}})\leftarrow(u,1,0)
and
for
i=1,\ldots,m
:

O_{i}=\begin{cases}\mathcal{A}[\lambda,\phi]&\Rightarrow\ f^{\ast}\leftarrow f^{\ast}-\lambda\phi,\\
\mathcal{M}[\omega]&\Rightarrow\ w^{\ast}\leftarrow w^{\ast}\cdot\omega,\\
\mathcal{R}[\rho]&\Rightarrow\ \Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}\leftarrow\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}+\rho.\end{cases}

This runs in
O(m)
and yields the normal form when
L\in\mathcal{R}
; otherwise, it produces a candidate together with
flags indicating which assumptions failed (to be formalized by GKPO in
Section

4
).

3
Theoretical Results

Theorem 1
(Reduction).

For any ladder
L
with
O_{i}\in\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{M}
and a fixed
\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}
,
where penalties are additive and weights are independent of intermediate margins,
there exists
(f^{\ast},w^{\ast})
such that for all
(x,y^{+},y^{-})
,

M_{L}(x;y^{+},y^{-})\;=\;\bigl[\Delta f^{\ast}(x;y^{+},y^{-})-\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}(x;y^{+},y^{-})\bigr]\cdot w^{\ast}(x,y^{+},y^{-}).

Conversely, if any assumption fails (reference shift, non-additive gates, or score-dependent weights),
there exist finite witnesses where no
(f^{\ast},w^{\ast})
matches
M_{L}
on all pairs.

Figure 1:
Ladder to normal form: additive penalties collect into f*; multiplicative weights collect into w*; reference adjustments accumulate separately.

Proof sketch.

Sufficiency: by
Lemma

1
, collect additive terms into
f^{\ast}=u-\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\phi_{i}

and multiplicatives into
w^{\ast}=\prod_{j}\omega_{j}
; margin equality holds pointwise.
Necessity: the finite witnesses are given by
Theorems

2
,
3
and
4
.
∎

Theorem 2
(Reference shift).

If
\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}
varies across prompts, there is no single
(f^{\ast},w^{\ast})
preserving decisions on all pairs.

Witness.

Two prompts with equal raw gap
d>0
but
\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}^{(1)}>d
and
\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}^{(2)}<-d

flip the margin sign; a single fixed reference cannot match both.
∎

Theorem 3
(Gated penalties).

Let
\Phi=\lambda_{1}\phi_{1}+\lambda_{2}\,\mathbf{1}\{\phi_{1}=0\}\phi_{2}
.
There exists a finite set of items on which no nonnegative additive surrogate matches decisions.

Witness.

(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})=(1,10)\Rightarrow\Phi=1
(gate off) and
(0,1)\Rightarrow\Phi=1
(gate on).
Matching both enforces
\lambda_{2}^{\prime}=1
and
\lambda_{1}^{\prime}=-9<0
, contradicting nonnegativity.
∎

Theorem 4
(Score-dependent weights).

If
W=\psi(\Delta f)
with nonconstant
\psi
, operator order can flip decisions;
no score-independent
w^{\ast}
reproduces all outcomes.

Witness.

Let
u(y^{+})=0.4,\ u(y^{-})=0.0
; an additive penalty shifts
y^{+}
by
-0.8
(post-gap
-0.4
).
Take
\psi(\Delta)=2
if
\Delta<0
, else
1/2
.
Weight
\!\to
penalty yields factor
1/2
; penalty
\!\to
weight yields
2
; decisions differ.
∎

Lemma 2

(Loss–link invariance)
.

If
g
is strictly increasing and two operators yield equal margins
M
,
then decisions and any loss
\ell(g(\beta M))
coincide (up to a monotone transform).

Lemma 3

(Uniqueness up to positive scaling)
.

If two reduced representatives
(f^{\ast},w^{\ast})
and
(\tilde{f},\tilde{w})

induce identical margins for all pairs, then
\exists\,c>0
s.t.
\Delta\tilde{f}=c\,\Delta f^{\ast}
and
\tilde{w}=c^{-1}w^{\ast}
.

Proof.

(\Delta f^{\ast})w^{\ast}=(\Delta\tilde{f})\tilde{w}
pointwise;
set
c=(\Delta\tilde{f})/(\Delta f^{\ast})>0
wherever
\Delta f^{\ast}\neq 0
and extend by continuity.
∎

Proposition 2

(Gradient equivalence inside
\mathcal{R}
)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}\,\ell(g(\beta M_{\theta}))
with
M_{\theta}=(\Delta f_{\theta}-\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}})\cdot W

and
W
independent of intermediate
\Delta f_{\theta}
.
If
M_{L}\equiv M^{\ast}
, then
\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}
is identical under the ladder and its normal form
(f^{\ast},w^{\ast})
.

4
GKPO: A Canonical Interchange Schema

Object.

A
Generalized Kernel Preference Object
(GKPO) encodes, for pairwise data
(x,y^{+},y^{-})
,

\mathrm{GKPO}\;=\;\bigl(f,\;w,\;\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}},\;g,\;\ell,\;\beta,\;\Pi,\;\Omega,\;\mathsf{meta}\bigr),

where
f:\!(x,y)\!\mapsto\!\mathbb{R}
is the score (typically
u=\log\pi_{\theta}
),

w:\!(x,y^{+},y^{-})\!\mapsto\!\mathbb{R}_{>0}
a pairwise weight,

\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}
a reference adjustment,
g
a strictly increasing link,
\ell
a scalar loss,

\beta>0
a temperature,
\Pi=\{(\lambda_{i},\phi_{i})\}
additive penalties, and
\Omega
dataset-level operators.
Its
canonical margin
is

M\;=\;(\Delta f-\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}})\cdot w,\qquad\mathcal{L}=\mathbb{E}\,\ell\!\big(g(\beta M)\big).

Inside the reducible class
\mathcal{R}
(
Definition

3
),
\Pi
folds into
f^{\ast}
and multiplicatives into
w^{\ast}
, yielding the Opal normal form; outside
\mathcal{R}
the object still represents the method but carries explicit failure flags with finite witnesses.

4.2
Canonicalization and hashing

(C1) Collection. Compute
f^{\ast}=u-\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\phi_{i}
and
w^{\ast}=\prod_{j}\omega_{j}
(one pass;
Lemma

1
).
(C2) Scale fixing. Choose
c>0
so that
\mathrm{median}_{\text{probe}}(|\Delta f^{\ast}|)=1
and apply the invariance

(\Delta f^{\ast},w^{\ast},\beta)\mapsto(c\,\Delta f^{\ast},c^{-1}w^{\ast},c\,\beta)
(
Definition

4
,
Lemma

3
).
(C3) Deterministic serialization. Emit UTF-8 JSON with: keys sorted, arrays lexicographically sorted
(by
name
for penalties, by value for lists), numeric rounding at
10^{-6}
.
(C4) Opal hash. Define
opal_hash

=
SHA-256 of (C3). The hash is invariant across equivalent representatives
and flips with any operator, coefficient, reference, or ordering change.

7
Why This Paper: Collapse, Extension, and De-cluttering

Collapse (normal form).

Many RLHF objectives differ mainly in syntax. Opal provides a way to show that ladders built from

additive penalties
and
multiplicative weights
reduce to a single
normal form on pairwise margins whenever three assumptions hold: fixed reference,
additive penalties, and score-independent weights (Sec.
2
, Thm.
1
).
This
collapse
helps clarify when objectives are effectively equivalent
(e.g., PPO-RM
\Rightarrow
DPO) and reduces ambiguity: if two proposals reduce to the same

(f^{\ast},w^{\ast})
(up to scale), they yield the same decisions and order-consistent losses.

Extension (a canonical schema).

Not all proposals satisfy these assumptions. We introduce GKPO, a canonical schema
that (i) represents any pairwise objective; (ii) canonicalizes reducible cases to the normal form
with a deterministic
opal_hash
; and (iii) records
explicit
flags and finite witnesses
when assumptions fail (reference shift, non-additive gate, score-dependent weight)
(Sec.
4
). GKPO can serve as an interchange layer: method
X\to
GKPO
\to
method
Y

preserves margins whenever the reduction preconditions hold.

De-cluttering (taxonomy and witnesses).

The operator view offers one way to organize the RLHF landscape (Sec.
6
): methods inside
\mathcal{R}

reduce to a normal form and differ only by interpretable operator choices; methods outside are
distinguished by
which
assumption they violate, illustrated by
finite witnesses

(Sec.
5
). In practice, this can support clearer ablations and reproducibility:
we suggest reporting the GKPO JSON, the
opal_hash
, and any reducibility flags along with results.

Scope and non-goals.

Opal characterizes
pairwise margin
objectives. It does not address optimizers, sampling,
or token-level credit assignment (e.g., MDP/token shaping); those are orthogonal. The focus here is to
standardize
what is optimized
(operators, references, weights), not how training is carried out.

8
Discussion and Broader Impact

What Opal explains.

Opal clarifies when seemingly distinct RLHF objectives are algebraically equivalent (same margins up to positive scaling)
and when they differ in decisions or losses (non-reducibility).
This perspective can help practitioners compare methods in terms of their
assumptions
, rather than naming or heuristics:
if a proposal satisfies fixed reference, additive penalties, and score-independent weights,
then its behavior is characterized by its normal form
(f^{\ast},w^{\ast})
(
Theorem

1
);
otherwise it can indicate which assumption does not hold and provide a finite witness.

GKPO as an interchange schema.

The schema supports three practical goals:
(i)
comparability
(methods serialize to the same keys and units),
(ii)
convertibility
(reducible methods can be translated via GKPO without altering margins), and
(iii)
provenance
(the Opal hash makes operator choices auditable;
Section

4.2
).
We view GKPO as a lightweight interchange layer for RLHF: it standardizes the
interfaces
(scores, weights, references, penalties),
but not the training stack itself.

Practical guidance.

It can be useful to report, alongside results:
(1) the GKPO JSON (or a compact diff relative to DPO),
(2) reducibility flags/witnesses (if any), and
(3) the Opal hash.
For ablations, one option is to change
one operator
at a time (e.g., add a penalty or swap a weight factor) and
verify the hash changes—this helps reduce confounds and keeps comparisons interpretable.

Limitations.

Opal currently characterizes objectives defined on
pairwise margins
.
Listwise objectives, trajectory-level credit assignment, or
policies with stateful evaluators may require extending the operator alphabet
(e.g., sequence-level gates, context-coupled references).
Our analysis assumes strictly increasing links and losses evaluated on a scalar margin;
nonscalar couplings (e.g., multi-dimensional safety constraints
(
Chittepu et al. 2025
)
) remain outside scope.
Finally, GKPO represents
what
is optimized (operators), not
how
it is optimized
(optimizers, sampling, reward models).

Threats to validity.

Outside
\mathcal{R}
, two operator stacks can agree on a dataset yet diverge under distribution shift;
witnesses demonstrate
existence
of disagreement, not its frequency.
Even inside
\mathcal{R}
, poor scale choices (temperature
\beta
) can affect optimization despite
invariance of decisions; canonical scale fixing (
Section

4.2
) can mitigate this, but
reporting the actual
\beta
used remains informative.

Broader impact.

Standardizing operator reporting and convertibility may support reproducibility,
reduce redundant proposals, and highlight genuine innovations.
At the same time, easier recombination of operators could make it simpler to assemble mis-specified objectives;
we therefore emphasize explicit declaration of reducibility flags and finite witnesses,
and encourage publishing GKPO artifacts alongside results.

10
Conclusion

We introduced Opal, an operator algebra for RLHF that expresses objectives as ladders of

additive penalties
and
multiplicative weights
on a base score, and described a
reduction law that holds under three assumptions (fixed reference, additive penalties, score-independent weights),
yielding a normal form on pairwise margins. This algebra highlights when proposals collapse to the same

(f^{\ast},w^{\ast})
(up to positive scaling) and when they differ through finite witnesses
(reference shift, non-additive gates, score-dependent weights). Building on this, we proposed
GKPO, an interchange schema that canonicalizes reducible methods, records explicit
flags and witnesses outside
\mathcal{R}
, and attaches a deterministic
opal_hash
for
provenance. Our demonstrations illustrate conversions among common objectives
(e.g., PPO-RM
\Rightarrow
DPO under assumptions) and stress tests (SHIFT/GATE/SCORE) that surface
non-reducibility. A lightweight reference implementation accompanies the paper.

Practical takeaway.

It can be helpful to report the GKPO JSON (or a compact “delta” w.r.t. DPO), the
opal_hash
, and any reducibility
flags/witnesses alongside empirical results. When claiming equivalence, stating the three preconditions
(fixed reference, additive penalties, score-independent weights) or providing a witness otherwise
makes ablations clearer and comparisons more transparent.

Limitations.

Our analysis is margin-based and pairwise; listwise preferences, trajectory-level credit assignment,
and stateful evaluators would require extending the operator alphabet. We assume strictly increasing links and
scalar losses; vector-valued constraints (e.g., multi-criteria safety) remain outside scope. Finally, GKPO
captures
what
is optimized (operators), not
how
it is optimized (samplers, optimizers).

Future work.

(i) Extend Opal/GKPO beyond pairwise margins to listwise/sequence settings and token-level shaping;
(ii) formalize dataset-stage composition with richer operators (e.g., learned group weights, dynamic
references, multi-annotator heterogeneity); (iii) develop statistical tests that infer reducibility
or produce witnesses from logs; (iv) integrate GKPO artifacts into common stacks (e.g.,
trl
)
and public leaderboards; (v) explore categorical or convex-analytic formulations that generalize the
reduction law to broader objective classes.

Outlook.

By separating algebra (operators and assumptions) from engineering (optimizers and pipelines),
Opal and GKPO aim to encourage standardized reporting, enable sound conversions, and support clearer comparisons.
We view GKPO artifacts as a lightweight companion to RLHF papers—small, auditable, and useful for checking
equivalence claims and reproducing operator choices.

A.1
Closure (Lemma
1
)

Lemma

(Closure)
.

Within
\mathcal{R}
, additive penalties are closed under sum on
f
and multiplicative
weights under product on
W
. Therefore any ladder
L\in\mathcal{R}
admits a collected

(f^{\ast},w^{\ast})
computed in one pass over
O_{1},\ldots,O_{m}
.

Proof.

Each additive operator
\mathcal{A}[\lambda,\phi]
applies
(f,W)\mapsto(f-\lambda\phi,W)
.
By commutativity/associativity of addition, composing any finite set of such operators yields

f=u-\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\phi_{i}
with
W
unchanged. Likewise, each multiplicative operator

\mathcal{M}[\omega]
applies
(f,W)\mapsto(f,W\cdot\omega)
; by commutativity/associativity of
multiplication over
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, composing finitely many gives
W=\prod_{j}\omega_{j}
with
f
unchanged.
Interleavings commute on
(f,W)
because addends act only on
f
and factors only on
W
.
Thus a single left-to-right pass computes

f^{\ast}=u-\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\phi_{i}
and
w^{\ast}=\prod_{j}\omega_{j}
.
∎

A.2
Reduction Theorem (Theorem
1
)

Theorem

(Reduction)
.

For any ladder
L
with
O_{i}\in\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{M}
and a fixed
\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}
,
where penalties are additive and weights are independent of intermediate margins,
there exists
(f^{\ast},w^{\ast})
such that for all
(x,y^{+},y^{-})
,

M_{L}(x;y^{+},y^{-})=\bigl[\Delta f^{\ast}(x;y^{+},y^{-})-\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}(x;y^{+},y^{-})\bigr]\cdot w^{\ast}(x,y^{+},y^{-}).

Conversely, if any assumption fails (reference shift, non-additive gates, or score-dependent weights),
there exist finite witnesses where no
(f^{\ast},w^{\ast})
matches
M_{L}
on all pairs.

Proof.

Sufficiency.
By Lemma
1
, collect addends/factors to obtain

f^{\ast}=u-\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\phi_{i}
and
w^{\ast}=\prod_{j}\omega_{j}
. Because
\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}
is fixed
(across prompts/pairs) and weights do not depend on intermediate
\Delta f
, the composed margin equals

(\Delta f^{\ast}-\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}})\cdot w^{\ast}
pointwise.

Necessity.
If reference shift occurs, choose two prompts with the same raw gap
d>0

but
\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}^{(1)}>d
and
\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}^{(2)}<-d
; margins flip sign, so no fixed reference
can match both decisions (Theorem
2
). If gated penalties appear, the pairs

(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})=(1,10)
and
(0,1)
force any nonnegative additive surrogate to take
\lambda_{1}^{\prime}<0

(contradiction; Theorem
3
). If score-dependent weights appear,
interleavings around a sign change of
\Delta f
yield different decisions; no score-independent
w^{\ast}

can match both (Theorem
4
). In each case a finite witness set suffices.
∎

2

)

Reference shift (Theorem
2
).

Theorem

(Reference shift)
.

If
\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}
varies across prompts, there is no single
(f^{\ast},w^{\ast})
preserving decisions on all pairs.

Proof.

Let two prompts have the same
\Delta f=d>0
but
\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}^{(1)}>d
and
\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}^{(2)}<-d
.
Then
M^{(1)}=(d-\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}^{(1)})<0
while
M^{(2)}=(d-\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}^{(2)})>0
(for any positive
w
).
A fixed
\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}^{\ast}
cannot realize both signs simultaneously; hence no single normal form matches both.
∎

Gated penalties (Theorem
3
).

Theorem

(Gated penalties)
.

Let
\Phi=\lambda_{1}\phi_{1}+\lambda_{2}\,\mathbf{1}\{\phi_{1}=0\}\phi_{2}
. There exists a finite set of items on which
no nonnegative additive surrogate matches decisions.

Proof.

Consider items with
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\in\{(1,10),(0,1)\}
.
Then
\Phi(1,10)=1
(gate off) and
\Phi(0,1)=1
(gate on).
Any additive surrogate
\Phi^{\prime}=\lambda_{1}^{\prime}\phi_{1}+\lambda_{2}^{\prime}\phi_{2}
that agrees on these points must satisfy

\lambda_{1}^{\prime}+10\lambda_{2}^{\prime}=1
and
\lambda_{2}^{\prime}=1
, hence
\lambda_{1}^{\prime}=-9<0
. If nonnegativity is required (as in many penalty designs),
this is impossible. A standard separation or Farkas’ lemma argument yields a finite certificate in the general case.
∎

Score-dependent weights (Theorem
4
).

Theorem

(Score-dependent weights)
.

If
W=\psi(\Delta f)
with nonconstant
\psi
, operator order can flip decisions; no score-independent
w^{\ast}
reproduces all outcomes.

Proof.

Take
u(y^{+})=0.4
,
u(y^{-})=0.0
. An additive penalty shifts
y^{+}
by
-0.8
, so the post-gap is
-0.4
.
Let
\psi(\Delta)=2
if
\Delta<0
, else

. Then

\text{weight}\!\to\!\text{penalty: }(0.4)\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}=0.2>0,\quad\text{penalty}\!\to\!\text{weight: }(-0.4)\cdot 2=-0.8<0.

A.4
Loss–link invariance (Lemma
2
)

Lemma

(Loss–link invariance)
.

If
g
is strictly increasing and two operators yield equal margins
M
, then decisions and any loss
\ell(g(\beta M))
coincide
(up to a monotone transform).

Proof.

Decision invariance:
\operatorname{sign}(M)
is preserved under strictly increasing
g
and positive
\beta
.
Loss invariance: If two margins
M_{1}\equiv M_{2}
, then
g(\beta M_{1})=g(\beta M_{2})
pointwise.
Any scalar loss
\ell
applied to equal arguments is equal. If instead two links
g_{1},g_{2}
are both strictly increasing,
there exists a strictly increasing
h=g_{2}\circ g_{1}^{-1}
on the common range; losses related by
\ell_{2}(\cdot)=\ell_{1}(h^{-1}(\cdot))

are order-equivalent.
∎

A.5
Uniqueness up to positive scaling (Lemma
3
)

Lemma

(Uniqueness)
.

If two reduced representatives
(f^{\ast},w^{\ast})
and
(\tilde{f},\tilde{w})
induce identical margins for all pairs,
then
\exists\,c>0
such that
\Delta\tilde{f}=c\,\Delta f^{\ast}
and
\tilde{w}=c^{-1}w^{\ast}
.

Proof.

From
(\Delta f^{\ast})w^{\ast}=(\Delta\tilde{f})\tilde{w}
and
w^{\ast},\tilde{w}>0
we get

\dfrac{\Delta\tilde{f}}{\Delta f^{\ast}}=\dfrac{w^{\ast}}{\tilde{w}}>0
wherever
\Delta f^{\ast}\neq 0
.
The left-hand side depends only on
f^{\ast},\tilde{f}
, the right-hand side only on
w^{\ast},\tilde{w}
.
If the ratio varied across pairs, we could pick two pairs with distinct ratios
c_{1}\neq c_{2}
; then no positive
c
satisfies

\Delta\tilde{f}=c\,\Delta f^{\ast}
and
\tilde{w}=c^{-1}w^{\ast}
simultaneously on both pairs, contradicting equality of margins.
Thus the ratio is constant
c>0
on the support of
\Delta f^{\ast}
. Define
\Delta\tilde{f}=c\,\Delta f^{\ast}

(for zero gaps extend by continuity or define
c
arbitrarily as the limit). Then necessarily
\tilde{w}=c^{-1}w^{\ast}
.
∎

2
)

Proposition

(Gradient equivalence inside
\mathcal{R}
)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}\,\ell(g(\beta M_{\theta}))
with
M_{\theta}=(\Delta f_{\theta}-\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}})\cdot W

and
W
independent of intermediate
\Delta f_{\theta}
. If
M_{L}\equiv M^{\ast}
, then

\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}
is identical under the ladder and its normal form
(f^{\ast},w^{\ast})
.

Proof.

By chain rule,

\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\ell^{\prime}\!\big(g(\beta M_{\theta})\big)\,g^{\prime}(\beta M_{\theta})\,\beta\cdot\nabla_{\theta}M_{\theta}\right],\qquad\nabla_{\theta}M_{\theta}=(\nabla_{\theta}\Delta f_{\theta})\cdot W,

since
W
and
\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}
are independent of intermediate
\Delta f_{\theta}
inside
\mathcal{R}

Under the ladder and its collected normal form,
\Delta f_{\theta}
and

Appendix B
Extended GKPO Schema

Design goals.

(1) Represent any pairwise RLHF objective with explicit operators,
(2) canonicalize reducible ladders to a normal form,
(3) emit deterministic hashes for provenance,
(4) attach explicit non-reducibility flags and finite witnesses when assumptions fail.

B.1
JSON Schema (informal, compact)

{
"version": "gkpo-1.0",
"score": {
"type": "logpi" | "logit" | "custom",
"custom_name": string? // e.g., "rm_logit"
},
"weight": {
"form": "constant" | "product" | "score_dependent" | "custom",
"constant": number?, // used iff form == "constant"
"factors": [string]?, // names of multiplicative factors (product)
"score_fn": string? // e.g., "psi_softplus"; implies score_dependent
},
"reference": {
"form": "fixed_zero" | "fixed_scalar" | "per_dataset" | "per_prompt" | "custom",
"value": number? // used iff form in {fixed_zero, fixed_scalar}
},
"link": "identity" | "logistic" | "tanh" | "hinge" | "custom",
"loss": "logistic" | "bce" | "hinge" | "mse" | "custom",
"beta": number, // temperature > 0
"penalties": [
{ "name": string, "lambda": number, "meta": { "gate": bool? } } // optional gate flag
],

},

}
}

B.2
Canonicalization & deterministic hashing

We fix a canonical representative (up to positive scaling; cf.
Definitions

4
and
3
) and a stable byte encoding.

C1: Collection.

Fold penalties into
f^{\ast}=u-\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\phi_{i}
and multiplicatives into
w^{\ast}=\prod_{j}\omega_{j}

(
Lemma

1
).

C2: Scale fixing (optional, recommended).

Choose
c>0
so that
\mathrm{median}_{\text{probe}}(|\Delta f^{\ast}|)=1
and apply

(\Delta f^{\ast},w^{\ast},\beta)\mapsto(c\,\Delta f^{\ast},c^{-1}w^{\ast},c\,\beta)
, preserving margins
(
Definitions

4
and
3
).

C3: Deterministic JSON.

Sort all
object keys
lexicographically; sort arrays by:
penalties by
name
, factor lists lexicographically, dataset-op lists lexicographically.
Round all floating-point numeric fields to
10^{-6}
(decimal, half-even acceptable).
Serialize with UTF-8 JSON, compact separators (comma, colon), no trailing spaces.

C4: Opal hash.

Define
opal_hash
as the hex SHA-256 of the C3 byte string.
Any change to operators, coefficients, reference form/value, lists, or ordering deterministically changes the hash;
equivalent representations inside
\mathcal{R}
share the same hash after C1–C3.

Pseudocode

if want_scale_fix:
c <- 1 / median_abs_delta(f_star) // probe set
(f_star, w_star, beta) <- (c*f_star, w_star/c, c*gkpo.beta) // C2
blob <- deterministic_json({
version, score, link, loss, beta,
weight:=canonical_weight(w_star), reference,
penalties:=sorted_penalties(), dataset_ops:=sorted_ops(),
reducibility, provenance:=provenance_without_hash
}, round=1e-6) // C3
h <- sha256_hex(blob) // C4
return (f_star, w_star, h)

Validation.

Schema correctness: (i) all required keys present, (ii) enum values respected, (iii) number fields parseable after rounding,
(iv) arrays sorted as specified. Canonicalization is idempotent: re-serializing a canonical object yields identical bytes and hash.

Appendix F
Ethics Statement

Our work proposes a formalism (Opal) and a schema (GKPO) for representing RLHF objectives;
it does not require training or deploying large models.
Nevertheless, RLHF research raises important considerations:

•

Human data and privacy. When GKPO captures objectives derived from human annotations (e.g., preference logs),
practitioners should ensure compliance with data-use agreements and privacy norms. Our artifact uses only synthetic examples.

•

Fairness
(
Chu et al. 2024
)
, culture
(
AlKhamissi et al. 2024
)
, and safety
(
Dai et al. 2023
)
. Operator choices (penalties, weights, references) can encode social preferences.
GKPO improves transparency by explicitly serializing these choices (and flags when assumptions fail),
but does not guarantee value alignment; we encourage reporting of GKPO diffs for fairness/safety interventions
(
Chu et al. 2024
)
.

•

Misuse risks. Standardizing operators can make it easier to compose objectives that prioritize utility over safety.
We mitigate by (i) requiring explicit
reducibility
flags and finite witnesses, (ii) encouraging publication of GKPO artifacts alongside results,
and (iii) documenting limitations (Sec.
8
).

Environmental impact.

Our demonstrations are toy; no large-scale training is needed.
We advocate publishing GKPO artifacts to reduce redundant large-scale experiments when methods are algebraically equivalent in margins.

Appendix H
Operational Validation of the Algebra

H1: Equivalence test (inside
\mathcal{R}
).

Hypothesis. If two methods reduce to the
same
GKPO normal form, their models are functionally indistinguishable.
Setup. Instantiate PPO–RM and DPO such that their GKPO JSON canonicalizes to the same
(f^{\ast},w^{\ast})
and identical
opal_hash
. Train a modest model on the same pairs with identical init/optimizer/seed.
Measures.
(i)
Margin agreement
: Kendall-
\tau
between margins on a held-out calibration set; (ii)
Decision match
: proportion of identical pairwise decisions; (iii)
Win rate parity
: RM win-rate difference with 95% bootstrap CI; (iv)
Behavioral parity
: McNemar test on per-prompt wins/losses.
Acceptance.
\tau\!\geq\!0.99
, decision match
\geq\!99.5\%
, win-rate diff CI overlapping 0.
Rationale. Inside
\mathcal{R}
,
Proposition

2
shows gradient equivalence; this test verifies the practical corollary.

H2: Non-reducibility demonstration (outside
\mathcal{R}
).

Hypothesis. Violating Opal’s assumptions yields predictable behavioral
differences
.
Setup. Start from DPO (reducible baseline). Create a variant with
prompt-varying reference
(ORPO-style offset), recorded in GKPO as
reference.form="per_prompt"
with a SHIFT witness. Train both with identical settings.
Targeted task. A small set where
\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}
should matter (e.g., prompts with known anchor bias or length prior).
Measures. Same as H1, plus slice-level analysis on the targeted set (expected gap in win rate), and a signed agreement rate on pairs where the witness predicts flips.
Expected. Significant, directionally consistent differences on the targeted slice; global parity need not hold.

Controls and reporting.

•

GKPO provenance. Emit canonical JSON and
opal_hash
for each run; for H1 they must match; for H2 they must differ with
reference_shift
flagged.

•

Rigorous sameness. Same data split, tokenizer, initialization, optimizer, schedule, seed, and decoding; log learning curves and temperature
\beta
.

•

Statistics. 1,000× bootstrap for win-rate CI; McNemar’s test for paired decisions; report effect sizes with CIs.

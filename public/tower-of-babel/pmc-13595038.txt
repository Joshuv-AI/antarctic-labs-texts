24 | PREDICT‐GDM: Prospective Evaluation of a Risk‐Based Model for Diabetes Intervention & Care Tailoring

Alexandra Berezowsky1; Nir Melamed1; Jon F. Barrett2; Joel G. Ray1; Erin Harrington1; Sarah D. McDonald2; Michael P. Geary1; Beth Murray‐Davis2; Vahinie Narang1; Howard Berger1

1University of Toronto, Toronto, Canada; 2McMaster University, Hamilton, Canada

Objective: To prospectively assess the validity, safety, feasibility, and clinical impact of a novel risk stratification tool to identify individuals with gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) at low risk of lifestyle modification (LM) failure who can be managed in a less resource‐intensive pathway.

Study Design: Parallel‐group proof‐of‐concept RCT at St. Michael's Hospital (Aug 2022–Jul 2024). Of 582 screened, 211 were eligible; 150 participants with singleton GDM pregnancies controlled on LM were randomized to routine care (RC) or modified care (MC) using a validated risk tool (75/group). In MC, low‐risk individuals were discharged to routine obstetric care with blinded remote glucose monitoring, while high‐risk individuals remained in the Diabetes in Pregnancy clinic. Primary outcome: model performance (sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV).Exploratory outcomes: glycemic control, maternal/neonatal outcomes, patient satisfaction, and resource utilization.

Results: In the intervention arm, 18 (24%) were classified as low‐risk and 57 as high‐risk. All low‐risk participants maintained glycemic control on LM (NPV 100%), with specificity 30.2% and AUC 0.732 (95% CI 0.649–0.814). Patient satisfaction was high (mean 3.86/5; no group differences).

There was a trend toward lower cesarean rates (6.7% vs 35.8%, p = 0.051) and shorter hospital stays (2.8 ± 0.86 vs 3.6 ± 1.3 days, p = 0.006) in low‐risk participants. Compared to low‐risk controls, the MC group differed only in higher postprandial dinner glucose excursions (32.3% vs 14.9%, p = 0.015) and lower postpartum hemorrhage rates (3.7% vs 26.7%, p = 0.047).

Resource use was reduced in the MC pathway, with fewer specialist visits (mean 8.8 vs 22.3).

Conclusion: This easily implementable tool safely identifies low‐risk GDM patients suitable for lower‐intensity care. De‐escalation was achieved in 24% of participants, reducing resource use without compromising outcomes, supporting broader implementation.

25 | Neonatal and Maternal Outcomes Associated With Hypoglycemia on 1‐Hour Oral Glucose Tolerance Test in Pregnancy

Suha Suliman1; Moinul Ahsan2; Sams Rashid2; Le Kang2; Micah Vaughn‐Valencia3; Allison Pierce4

1VCU Health System, Richmond, VA; 2VCU Department of Biostatistics, Richmond, VA; 3VCU Health System Department of Maternal Fetal Medicine, Richmond, VA; 4VCU School of Medicine, Richmond, VA

Objective: A 1‐hour oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) performed at 24–28 weeks' gestation is standard of care for gestational diabetes screening in the United States. There is little evidence to guide clinical decision making when faced with abnormally low values or hypoglycemia, defined as blood glucose ≤70 mg/dL. We hypothesized that there would be a significantly higher proportion of small‐for‐gestational‐age (SGA) neonates in mothers with 1‐hour OGTT results ≤70 mg/dL.

Study Design: This was a retrospective cohort study of 454 mother/infant dyads delivered at VCU Health System between 2000 and 2025. There were 225 mother/infant pairs in the exposure group, defined as 1‐hour OGTT result ≤70 mg/dL performed at 24–28 weeks’ gestation. A control group of patients with a 1‐hour OGTT result of 71–139 mg/dL was generated using 1:1 matching based on maternal age, BMI, and gestational age at delivery. The primary outcome was incidence of SGA. A summary of demographics was generated using means and standard deviations for continuous variables and percentages for categorical variables. Dichotomous outcomes were analyzed using multivariable logistic regression, and continuous outcomes were analyzed using multivariable linear regression.

Results: Baseline characteristics were similar except for race/ethnicity (p = 0.02) and history of cesarean delivery (p = 0.017). In the multivariable analysis, there were higher odds for SGA when hypoglycemia was identified on 1‐hour OGTT (OR 2.35, p < 0.01). Gestational weight gain was also significantly decreased in this group (−1.86 kg, p = 0.007). A 1‐hour OGTT result ≤70 mg/dL was not associated with neonatal hypoglycemia, labor length, cesarean delivery, or preterm delivery.

Conclusion: Hypoglycemia on 1‐hour OGTT at 24–28 weeks' gestation is a significant predictor for SGA and decreased gestational weight gain, but not neonatal hypoglycemia, preterm delivery, or delivery type. SGA neonates are at increased risk for short‐ and long‐term morbidity and increased antenatal and postnatal surveillance may be warranted with hypoglycemia on maternal glucose tolerance testing.

26 | First Trimester Glycemic Control and Hypertensive Disorders in Pregnancies With Type 1 Diabetes

Corinne Parker1; Erkan Kalafat2; Salimah Navaz Gangji1; Rebecca Horgan1; Jerri A. Waller1; George R. Saade1; Marwan Maayeh1

1Eastern Virginia Medical School at Old Dominion University, Norfolk, VA; 2Koç University Hospital, Istanbul, Turkey

Objective: To evaluate the association between first trimester glycemic control by clinical, laboratory and continuous glucose monitor (CGM) assessment and the diagnosis of hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP) in pregnancies complicated by Type 1 diabetes (T1D).

Study Design: Patients with T1D using a CGM were included. The primary outcome was the development of HDP, defined by ACOG criteria. The association of clinical variables with the outcome (HDP) was assessed with univariable and multivariable logistic regression analyses. Performance of the multivariable models was assessed with ROC curves.

Results: A total of 188 pregnancies in 169 individuals were available, and 182 were included in the analysis after excluding six with missing outcomes. Factors associated with the development of HDP included higher body mass index (BMI) (OR 1.07, 95% CI 1.01–1.13) and chronic hypertension (OR 2.51, 95% CI 1.12–5.77). White race was associated with lower odds of HDP (OR 0.57, 95% CI 0.29–1.11), albeit difference did not reach statistical significance. Maternal age and insulin delivery methods were not significantly associated with risk. First trimester CGM metrics were not significantly associated with development of HDP (Table 1). Addition of glycemic variables to the baseline model including BMI, chronic hypertension and race resulted in an improvement of AUC from 0.61 (95% CI 0.52–0.71) to 0.73 (0.63–0.83) (p = 0.049, Figure 1).

Conclusion: In pregnancies complicated by T1DM, maternal BMI, chronic hypertension, and race were associated with a risk of HDP, but first trimester CGM metrics are not. Larger studies are needed to validate these findings.

27 | Neonatal and Maternal Outcomes in Shoulder Dystocia Among Diabetic and Non‐Diabetic Pregnant Individuals

Fabrizio Zullo1; Daniele Di Mascio1; Sara Sorrenti1; Kristen A. Cagino2; Aaron W. Roberts2; Yossef Bart3; Hector Mendez Figeuroa2; Ilenia Mappa1; Giuseppe Rizzo1; Michal Fishel Bartal4; Suneet P. Chauhan5

1Sapienza University of Rome, Rome, Italy; 2McGovern Medical School at UTHealth, Houston, TX; 3Division of Maternal‐Fetal Medicine, Department of Obstetrics, Gynecology and Reproductive Sciences, McGovern Medical School at UTHealth Houston, Houston, TX; 4McGovern Medical School, Houston, TX; 5ChristianaCare, Newark, DE

Objective: There is limited evidence on whether diabetes modifies neonatal and maternal outcomes in pregnancies complicated by shoulder dystocia (SD). We hypothesized that diabetes is associated with increased adverse outcomes in this cohort.

Study Design: We conducted a secondary analysis of the APEX study. We included non‐anomalous singletons at ≥34 weeks delivered vaginally with documented SD requiring > 1 maneuver. Cases were stratified by the presence of diabetes (gestational and pregestational). The primary outcome was a composite neonatal adverse outcome (CNAO), including Apgar <5 at 5min, fractures, intracranial hemorrhage, brachial plexus palsy, hypotension, hypoxic‐ischemic encephalopathy, or neonatal death. Secondary outcomes included composite maternal adverse outcome (CMAO) and individual components. Multivariable Poisson regression models were used.

Results: Among 74,934 eligible deliveries, 2061 (2.7%) were complicated by SD, with a higher incidence among diabetics (4.8% vs 2.6%). CNAO occurred more frequently in diabetic pregnancies (17.6% vs 8.5%; aIRR 1.66, 95% CI 1.10–2.50), with significantly higher risks of Apgar <7 at 5’ (aIRR 2.36) and neonatal fractures (aIRR 2.03). In contrast, CMAO rates were similar between groups (20.5% vs 19.4%; aIRR 1.12, 95% CI 0.79–1.58), with no significant differences in individual maternal outcomes. In subgroup analyses, the increased neonatal risk associated with diabetes was driven by higher birthweight: among neonates ≥4,000 g, CNAO was significantly higher in diabetics (aIRR 2.12), whereas no difference was observed below this threshold. Birthweight showed limited discriminatory ability for adverse outcomes (AUC 0.55–0.63), although non‐linear risk escalation was observed at higher weights in diabetic pregnancies.

Conclusion: Among pregnancies complicated by SD, DM is associated with a significantly increased risk of neonatal complications particularly in the presence of macrosomia. These findings suggest that diabetes is associated with increased neonatal vulnerability and should be considered in intrapartum risk stratification.

28 | Maternal Celiac Disease and Early Neurodevelopment: A Nationwide Registry‐Based Cohort Study

Alon Kass1; Shalhevet Frank2; Joshua Guedalia3; Michal Lipschuetz4; Ofer Beharier2

1Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology, Hadassah Medical Center, Jerusalem, Israel; 2Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology, Hadassah Medical Center, Mt. Scopus, Jerusalem, Israel; 3Hadassah Hebrew University Medical Center, Jeruslaem, Israel; 4Henrietta Szold Hadassah Hebrew University School of Nursing in the Faculty of Medicine, Jerusalem, Israel

Objective: Celiac disease (CD) is increasingly prevalent. Celiac‐associated antibodies have been implicated in neurologic manifestations such as gluten ataxia, and IgG antibodies are known to cross the placenta, raising concern about potential effects on offspring neurodevelopment. However, data on this question remain limited. We aimed to address this using Israel's nationwide professional child‐development surveillance system.

Study Design: We conducted a population‐based retrospective cohort study linking live‐birth records (2018–2021) to Israel's Mother‐and‐Child Health Clinic registries (Tipat Halav), which capture standardized developmental assessments performed by trained nurses at scheduled visits through age 24 months. Infants born to mothers with a documented CD diagnosis were compared with the general population. Cox and logistic regression models, adjusted for maternal age, gestational age, parity, and infant sex, estimated risks of small‐for‐gestational‐age (SGA) birth, developmental milestone attainment, and specialist referrals.

Results: Among 466,462 singleton term infants in the nationwide cohort, maternal CD was associated with higher SGA risk (10.5% vs 7.6%; aHR 1.41, 95% CI 1.17–1.70, p = 0.001). Across 24 months of professionally assessed follow‐up, no differences emerged in gross motor, fine motor, language, or social milestone attainment, nor in diagnoses of failure to thrive, infections, allergies, or neurologic referrals. As a positive control, the same registry successfully detected established developmental differences between early‐term and full‐term infants, confirming its ability to identify true effects and strengthening the conclusion that maternal CD does not impair early neurodevelopment.

Conclusion: Maternal CD modestly increases SGA risk but does not compromise neurodevelopment during the first two years of life. Comprehensive nationwide developmental surveillance found no evidence of early developmental harm. Parents can be reassured that developmental outcomes remain comparable to the general population.

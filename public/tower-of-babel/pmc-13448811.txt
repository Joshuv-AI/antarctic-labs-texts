Blockchain-enabled supply chain finance risk intelligent assessment and trust mechanism construction
Authors: Zeyu Chen, Fangfang Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSupply Chain Finance (SCF) enhances financial liquidity, optimizes operational efficiency, and strengthens collaboration among supply chain stakeholders. Traditional SCF systems are vulnerable to economic risks, fraud, and a lack of transparency due to centralized data control and limited real-time monitoring capabilities. Current systems rely on slow central financial bodies and static credit evaluations, lacking dynamic risk assessment and mechanisms to build stakeholder trust. This research proposes Bi-MATRA, a Blockchain-Integrated Multi-Agent Trust and Risk Assessment system. The goal is to create a decentralized, intelligent risk evaluation system for real-time trust computation and automated decision-making in SCF contexts. Bi-MATRA combines blockchain’s immutability and transparency with a multi-agent system that automatically monitors and assesses risk. Bi-MATRA uses an adapted Eigen Trust algorithm on the blockchain to calculate trust. Agents rate each other based on interaction history, and transitive trust linkages determine global trust ratings. Smart contracts for dynamic trust-based decision-making update these scores. Smart contracts enforce predefined financial agreements, automating processes and reducing the need for intermediaries. Agent-based modelling was employed to develop a blockchain-based simulation testbed for assessing Bi-MATRA’s responsiveness, accuracy, and resilience. The framework was more efficient at trust validation and risk identification than typical SCF systems. Key studies reveal that Bi-MATRA saves transaction clearance time by 35% and improves early risk detection by 28%. EigenTrust-based trust computation builds agent trust and accountability, enhancing financial interactions. In conclusion, the Bi-MATRA framework offers a scalable, decentralized approach to intelligent risk assessment and trust-building in blockchain-enabled supply chain finance systems.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-53135-w.Keywords: Blockchain, Supply chain finance, Risk assessment, Multi-agent system, Smart contracts, Trust mechanismSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Financial fraud, inefficiencies, and stakeholder mistrust are frequent in conventional supply chain finance (SCF) systems due to centralized data management, poor transparency, and delayed risk detection8. Due to human error and static credit rating algorithms, these systems exaggerate supply network risk9. Bi-MATRA-like real-time, automated, and transparent risk processes are needed given these figures. Insufficient decentralized trust hinders financial transactions between participants. A decentralized, intelligent system that can dynamically evaluate risk and compute trust as global supply networks grow more complicated10.

Methodology

Blockchain, multi-agent systems, and trust modeling may solve the issues11. Bi-MATRA (Blockchain-Integrated Multi-Agent Trust and Risk Assessment) records supply chain interactions in a tamper-proof, transparent, decentralized ledger1. Autonomous network agents evaluate stakeholder behavior based on transaction timeliness, contract fulfillment, and financial stability. Dynamic trust scores are calculated using EigenTrust12. Agents evaluate each other directly, and the system calculates global trust ratings from local trust values via transitive links. Bi-MATRA agents use an adaptive trust algorithm to change their behavior and rule-based decision-making for reactive choices. Smart contracts automate financial agreements and risk reduction by storing, updating, and applying trust ratings in real time13. In an agent-based blockchain-based simulation testbed, responsiveness, accuracy, and robustness are compared to standard SCF models14. Explainability methods that show how characteristics affect agent network trust assessments improve interpretability and user trust.

In this paradigm, risk assessment and trust propagation are different processes with functional roles. Transaction-level risk assessment that is predictable, interpretable, and rule-based uses weights and thresholds to link operational, financial, and compliance data to a likelihood score. Risk signals must be visible, repeatable, and auditable without data-driven model training. A methodical distribution strategy based on interaction outcomes and agent transitivity linkages updates the trust score as the blockchain verifies events. Unlike adaptive parameter updates based on interaction history and trust propagation dynamics, optimum control based on supervised learning, reinforcement learning, or a predictive model is not adaptable. Thus, the approach’s essential concepts are kept, unrealistic learning ability assumptions are avoided, and the framework is positioned as a practical risk management and trust system rather than a prediction model.

EigenTrust’s system-level operationalization in a blockchain-enabled, multi-agent Supply Chain Finance (SCF) scenario, and the dynamic relationship between risk and trust in real-time financial decision-making, are two of the study’s distinctive features. Unlike earlier SCF projects that use rule-based logic or EigenTrust as static or offline components, Bi-MATRA directly integrates a modified EigenTrust mechanism into an on-chain, event-driven workflow. When fraud alarms, payment delays, and compliance breaches occur, smart contracts immediately increase confidence. The architectural and operational “intelligence” of the proposed system is achieved by autonomous agents monitoring locally, trust is dynamically recalculated using blockchain-verified interaction histories, and financing decisions are made automatically without centralized intervention. Bi-MATRA prioritizes decentralized trust responsibility, real-time responsiveness, and auditability above predictive model complexity, unlike machine learning-driven credit scoring, system dynamics-based SCF risk analysis, or metaheuristic-optimized classifiers. Therefore, the paper proposes a deployable, integrated SCF trust-and-risk assessment system that turns trust computation beliefs into a realistic, multi-agent, blockchain-native financial infrastructure.

Blockchain-enabled supply chain finance

Tsai et al.15 developed a consensus-based, innovative contract algorithm, and blockchain technology was introduced to automate and validate supply chain finance (SCF) financing transactions. Simulated financial transaction records and supplier payment histories assess system performance under various operational conditions. Results reveal that the blockchain-enabled SCF model speeds transaction processing by 25%, reduces delayed payments by 30%, and eliminates double-spending and invoicing fraud. Simulated data may not accurately reflect the complexity and unexpected behavior of real-world SCFs, thereby limiting our ability to model them. Scalability remains a concern for decentralized networks as transaction volume grows.

Li et al.16 proposed the Fabric-SCF, a blockchain-based system built on Hyperledger Fabric and an attribute-based access control (ABAC) algorithm, enables secure, fine-grained data access in supply chain finance. The system is assessed for typical SCF operations using simulated financial credit records and transaction logs from small and medium-sized firms. Fabric-SCF offers secure, traceable, immutable data storage with dynamic access control and rapid transaction throughput. Simulation-based validation’s main limitation is that it may not completely account for different, large-scale real-world deployment challenges in heterogeneous supply chains.

Li et al.17 improved pandemic monitoring and response via a blockchain-enabled decentralized surveillance and reporting algorithm and real-time smart contracts. With a compartmental SEIR model parameter and a contact history dataset, detection speed, data bias, and reporting delays were improved. Results demonstrate that blockchain technology has reduced estimation errors, enhanced data privacy for data sharing, and improved epidemic control. Synthetic data simulations may not accurately capture the complexities and network scalability of real-world scenarios during unexpected public health crises.

Guo et al.18 presented BC4Regu, a blockchain-based consensus technique that increases SCF transparency and coordination, using an information management platform and the Internet of Things. We simulated transaction data and principal-agent scenarios to assess system performance and risk management. BC4Regu streamlines processes, increases data transparency, and facilitates supply chain commerce and finance. Complex and dynamic supply chains have scalability and IoT interoperability issues since these principles are theoretical but not implemented.

Bai et al.19 offered a qualitative case study using topic analysis and semi-structured interviews. Workers, managers, and experts from Chinese state-owned firms participated. Interview transcripts were analyzed qualitatively. These studies emphasize framework compatibility and cross-chain compatibility issues, societal concerns about altering business practices, and operational obstacles of utilizing blockchain for SCF and changing organizational operations. This study relies heavily on qualitative research and one-off case studies, hence it may not apply to other nations or industries.

Risk intelligent assessment in supply chain finance

Liu et al.20 developed a hybrid risk assessment model for construction SMEs’ online supply chain financing utilizing XGBoost, SMOTENC, and Random Forest algorithms. The model beat earlier methods by 6.39% in assessment accuracy and 6.95% in AUC utilizing data from upstream SMEs in China’s building supply chain. Its industry and regional dataset may restrict its applicability to other industries or global financial ecosystems. Li et al.21 used the Heterogeneous Graph Transformer (HGT) approach to identify financial statement fraud in a supplier-customer knowledge network using economic and transaction data from Chinese listed companies. HGT outperformed conventional machine learning approaches by 5.19%, with an AUC of 85.10% and stable performance across historical periods. The main problem is that it uses publicly accessible supplier-customer data, which may not capture private or informal interactions needed to identify fraud. Balmaseda et al.22 recommended GNNs with C2R labeling to forecast financial network systemic risk. Network structure and node-edge attributes are used. On two financial network datasets with inter-entity exposures and risk indicators, GNNs beat conventional ML with C2R, increasing MCC by 94% and 15% and reducing quantile RMSE. In real-world financial systems, high-quality, full network data may be scarce or partial, limiting system capabilities. Wang et al.23 theoretically and qualitatively studied blockchain’s integration into Industry 4.0 without a research methodology or dataset. Blockchain improves sustainability reporting, corporate transparency, and operational accountability, but it also boosts energy consumption, scalability, legal uncertainty, and incentive misalignment. Since these insights require empirical confirmation and quantitative performance evaluation, future research should employ operational models and real-world pilot studies.

Trust mechanism construction in blockchain-based systems

Pattanayak et al.24 advised 31 supply chain experts and semi-structured interviews for qualitative research. Blockchain technology fosters trust and relational and dynamic capacities in supply chains. This link is moderated by supply chain unpredictability. Qualitative, interview-based research lacks quantitative validation or cross-industry comparison, therefore future research should use empirical, data-driven analysis to generalize its results.

Shu et al.25 introduced multi-layer metric algorithms to compute complete trust values for underlying suppliers and blockchain-stored transitive signature receivable warrants, thereby offering a blockchain-based transitive trust model for supply chain finance. Experimental simulations using synthetic SCF transaction data verified the methodology, thereby improving the reliability of trust transfer and access to SME funding. Future studies should address the lack of real-world deployment data and scalability assessments across various supply chain configurations to enhance broader applicability.

Jovanovic et al.26 offered blockchain, federated learning, and explainable AI (XAI) for credit rating, using privacy-preserving decentralized models and SHAP and LIME for model explainability. Decentralized models were less transparent, dependable, and explainable than centralized ones in synthetic federated financial dataset experiments that replicate multi-institutional credit systems. The absence of real-world deployment, scalability analysis under financial restrictions, and many data sources for operational validation should be addressed in future study.

Jiang et al.27 employed a trust transitivity model for SMMEs in supply chain finance using blockchain-based EPVs to evaluate direct and indirect trust. Simulations of hypothetical multi-tier manufacturing supply chains demonstrated that the approach enhanced trust transfer and alleviated financial constraints for lower-tier suppliers. Future research should include empirical real-world deployment and sensitivity testing under different market and policy settings to broaden applicability.

Reinforcement learning (RL) agents for adaptive risk judgments and trust scoring in financial systems were introduced between 2023 and 2025. Cross-chain smart contracts facilitate interoperability among blockchain networks. Trust learning, blockchain, and automation are rarely combined in these studies.

To predict the evolution of credit risk in supply chain finance, Li et al.28 created an automated analytical framework based on system dynamics that relies on centralized modelling assumptions and highlights the significance of temporal risk transmission. Although the method is not intended for real-time transactional trust enforcement, Patro et al.29 extended SCF research toward sustainability by putting forth a generative-AI-driven carbon finance indexing framework, showing how advanced analytics can support strategic financial decision-making. Lei et al.30 demonstrated improved forecast accuracy by using machine learning approaches to avoid SCF risk; however, their approach prioritizes offline risk classification over decentralized operational management. Zhang et al.31 introduced a support vector machine optimized using the firefly algorithm for credit risk assessment. Although static datasets and supervised learning are still needed, classification performance has improved. Online SCF risk assessment by Lyu and Zhao32 used compressed sensing in big-data situations to handle high-dimensional data efficiently. However, automated execution and decentralized trust propagation were not explored. These studies emphasize the need for predictive risk modeling and analytics and integrated, blockchain-enabled, real-time trust and risk governance in decentralized supply chain finance systems.

Liu et al.33 use smart contracts, reputation models, and blockchain technology to manage trust, scalability, and security in dynamic multi-entity networks. Their research focuses on blockchain-enabled IoT trust management. They suggest supply chain finance ecosystems might benefit from blockchain-based trust-propagation methods like reputation aggregation and transitive trust.Adjei et al.34 introduced a multi-agent reinforcement learning system that uses a graph attention network to find weaknesses in smart contracts. They demonstrated how the robustness and accuracy of detection in decentralized blockchain systems are significantly increased by agent-based learning and graph-aware representations. This article demonstrates how effectively blockchain technology and multi-agent intelligence can automatically detect abnormalities and threats. Furthermore, Bodo and De Filippi35 examined how the regulatory environment affects trust in blockchain and DeFi, and argued that technical trust mechanisms need to be complemented by governance and regulatory congruence to ensure sustained adoption. The aggregate emphasis of these studies on the need to incorporate smart contract security, automated multi-agent analysis, decentralized trust computing, and regulatory considerations strengthens the case for the proposed blockchain-integrated trust and risk assessment paradigm.

The paper advances blockchain-based SCF systems by operationalizing trust and risk assessment as event-driven on-chain processes. Previous SCF models compute reputation or risk off-chain or in centralized modules and store the results on the blockchain. Still, Bi-MATRA considers trust propagation as part of the blockchain workflow and distributes evaluations among independent agents without aggregating them. Bi-MATRA continuously refreshes trust based on confirmed transactional events and disseminates it through a structured trust graph, enabling timely, context-sensitive decisions. Current solutions are based on pre-determined policies or periodically renewed reputation ratings that fail to respond to transactional performance in real time. Previous deployment systems focused on auditability or decision support, requiring human or institutional action to respond to trust or risk evaluation. Bi-MATRA links trust scores with smart-contract logic to automatically enforce financing approvals, suspensions, or alerts at execution time. This focused synthesis shows that Bi-MATRA integrates and executes trust-aware SCF processes in real time, rather than proposing new rule sets and reputation algorithms.

Blockchain-embedded global trust computation (EigenTrust adaptation)

The decentralized, dynamic Bi-MATRA system evaluates supply chain financial trust using Blockchain and EigenTrust. Suppliers and purchasers trust blockchain records of prior contacts, which include payment timeliness, fraud, and compliance. Transitive propagation creates a global trust vector from local evaluations to locate trustworthy people. Smart contracts can update trust-based actions and scores instantly. This strategy reduces dependence on a single entity and improves accountability, improving early danger identification and stakeholder confidence in supply chain finance systems. Since it employs an iterative, deterministic EigenTrust algorithm, the Bi-MATRA trust calculation mechanism struggles with dynamic learning induced by environmental or agent behavior changes. Deterministic rule-based trust updates are used by Bi-MATRA agents, although reinforcement learning or Bayesian trust optimization may improve their adaptive decision-making. Following these stages may help agents enhance their decision-making in hostile or unclear settings, deal with context-specific risk patterns, and update their trust norms.

Trust and risk assessment modules use adaptive learning to improve deterministic rule-based approaches36. Agents use a Beta distribution updated online based on real-time transaction outcomes including payment timeliness, compliance fulfillment, and fraud occurrence to measure local trust. This distribution allows a Bayesian trust updating method to estimate the counterparty’s reliability.By allowing agents to learn from the past, account for uncertainty, and dynamically adjust trust levels in response to changing risk scenarios, the probabilistic update provides data-driven adaptability to the deterministic EigenTrust propagation.

Labeled datasets and centralized training pipelines are needed for supervised learning models like XGBoost and deep neural networks in decentralized supply chain finance. However, the hybrid technique may work online and unsupervised without ground-truth labeling.In multi-agent blockchain settings, reinforcement learning-based trust optimization has high computational overhead, delayed convergence, and limited explainability. Bayesian–EigenTrust integration has faster convergence, lower complexity, and stable trust evolution compatible with smart contract execution constraints. Evidence shows that learning-augmented trust updating improves early risk sensitivity and adaptive trust adjustment while maintaining transparency, scalability, and robustness, making the proposed framework an automated risk assessment and event-driven updates trust mechanism.

Result analysis

All of the quantifiable performance metrics displayed here come from extensively monitored blockchain-based simulation tests. Bi-MATRA decreased the average transaction clearance time in our simulations by over 35%, from 4.0 days (Fabric-SCF) and 6.2 days (bank-led SCF baseline) to 2.6 days. Trust propagation converged in 12–30 iterations for networks with 100–5,000 agents, maintaining over 93% trust score accuracy. These findings show Bi-MATRA’s performance in simulations, albeit outcomes may vary by participant, network, and regulatory situation.

Decentralized supply chain finance systems require trust convergence, risk detection accuracy, transaction efficiency, and resilience to adversarial behavior, which motivates the proposed evaluation metrics, TPE, RTA, CTOR, and DTR. TPE shows that dynamic multi-agent networks consistently reach trust equilibrium by computing the inter-iteration trust variance and measuring the EigenTrust-based global trust vector’s stability and convergence rate. Limitation of simulation

Despite improving operating efficiency, trust propagation, and risk detection in simulated SCF situations, Bi-MATRA ignores real-world aspects when assessed. Simulating decentralized supply chain restrictions, communication failures, and network slowness using fake data may influence real-time trust and risk estimations. Since technology cannot replicate regulatory operations and compliance standards, the framework may need human intervention in many policy and legal concerns, which may delay or necessitate overrides. Inconsistent, imprecise, or delayed oracle data might hamper automated financial choices, even with quick smart contracts and oracle confidence. Existing SCF networks do not employ human interventions or overrides in contested or high-risk transactions, which might degrade system performance. In addition to sophisticated risk assessment and trust computation, Bi-MATRA needs additional testing for live, multi-actor SCF datasets, real-time blockchain networks, and operational limits. The present assessment models simulate SCF system operational, transactional, and environmental aspects such payment delays, fraud, irregular deliveries, and compliance using a synthetic dataset (Bi-MATRA)37. From publicly accessible SCF operating data and industry standards, all parameter values, distributions, and interdependencies were calculated. According to sensitivity research, transaction volume, fraud frequency, and agent interaction patterns will not affect the current performance trends of 35% faster clearing and 28% better risk identification. The approaches employed make this a proof of concept rather than a deployment validation. Future research may address deployment problems, improve realism, and apply their results by using rule-based synthetic data, anonymized or semi-real SCF datasets, or both.

Conclusion and future enhancement

Traditional supply chain finance (SCF) systems use Bi-MATRA to manage fraud, integrity, and financial risk. Bi-MATRA creates a trustworthy, secure SCF platform using blockchain technology’s immutability, scalability, intelligent agent-based trust computation, and enhanced EigenTrust. This technique streamlines operations using smart contract transactions, clearance optimization, and real-time dynamic trust evaluations. Bi-MATRA outperformed standard SCF methods in early risk assessment by 28% and transaction clearance by 35%. The design maintains high Decentralized Trust Resilience (DTR) to prevent destructive shocks and anomalies in uncertain times. Bi-MATRA may assist autonomous SCF networks become more transparent, trustworthy, and responsible financially. Suppliers can expedite credit acquisition with real-time trust ratings, while lenders may decrease risk with automated fraud detection and dynamic creditworthiness updates. Blockchain transactions’ immutability and auditability help auditors and regulators stay compliant. Bi-MATRA’s technology helps real-world SCF ecosystems achieve trust, efficiency, and accountability. Bi-MATRA now evaluates trust using adaptive criteria. Future anomaly detection powered by machine learning might increase risk and fraud detection. IoT sensors like humidity, temperature, and real-time GPS tracking may be coupled with transaction data to enhance context-aware trust judgments. The multi-agent system that generates dynamic credit ratings may improve financial choices using statistical analysis. Finally, Bi-MATRA might be linked to Hyperledger Fabric or Ethereum private networks to demonstrate its security, interoperability, and capabilities in real-world settings. Clustering-based outlier identification, Isolation Forests, and other lightweight anomaly detection methods may improve intelligent risk assessment. These technologies will boost trust and flexibility by detecting fraud before it happens, even with unlabeled data. To make Bi-MATRA fully flexible, we will employ Bayesian networks for probabilistic reasoning and reinforcement learning for trust determination criteria. In environmentally conscious and risk-aware SCF contexts, the system earns value by adopting trust behaviors. This makes it more reliable and suitable for complex supply chain finance situations. Bi-MATRA will soon audit EigenTrust’s trust judgments utilizing XAI technologies including SHAP values, LIME, and fuzzy inference methods. Trust is needed for agent-based autonomous financial systems to meet regulations. Combining synthetic testing with certified real-world SCF datasets from industry partners or anonymized data improves sector-specific outcomes and operations.

Concentration inequalities and Transfer operators for supercritical Dyson models
Mirmukhsin Makhmudov

Abstract
. The present paper extends the results on the ferromagnetic Dyson models from [ 19 , 14 ] to the near-critical and strongly interacting regimes. As part of our main result, we further establish Gaussian concentration bounds for the unique infinite-volume Gibbs measure throughout the entire supercritical regime.



1.
Introduction

The one-dimensional long-range Ising model, a.k.a. the Dyson model, is one of the archetypical models in Statistical Mechanics.
This model has served as the testing ground for many conjectures and ideas in statistical mechanics, and it has also been the first example of a one-dimensional model which undergoes phase transitions at low temperatures.
A Dyson interaction
\Phi=(\Phi_{\Lambda})_{\Lambda\Subset\mathbb{Z}}
is defined on the configuration space
X:=\{\pm 1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
of
\omega\in X
by:

(1)

\Phi_{\Lambda}(\omega):=\begin{cases}-J(|i-j|)\omega_{i}\omega_{j},&\text{ if }\Lambda=\{i,j\}\subset\mathbb{Z},\ i\neq j;\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
J(k)\geq 0
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and to have well-defined infinite-volume Gibbs measures, we assume that
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}J(k)<\infty
.
The most well-known example is the
standard Dyson interaction
for which
J(k)=k^{-\alpha}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and for some
\alpha>1
.
In this paper, instead of studying one fixed interaction
\Phi
, we study the family
\beta\Phi=(\beta\Phi_{\Lambda})_{\Lambda\Subset\mathbb{Z}}
of interactions parameterised by
\beta\geq 0
, which is interpreted as the inverse temperature in statistical mechanics.
A
Gibbs measure
for the interaction
\beta\Phi
is a probability measure
\mu
on
X
that satisfies the Dobrushin–Lanford–Ruelle (DLR) equations.
Equivalently, for finite volumes
\Lambda\Subset\mathbb{Z}
, the conditional distribution of
\mu
given the configuration outside
\Lambda
is prescribed by the Boltzmann-Gibbs ansatz:

(2)

\mu(\omega_{\Lambda}\mid\omega_{\Lambda^{c}})=\gamma^{\beta\Phi}_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda}\mid\omega_{\Lambda^{c}}),\;\;\;\mu-\text{a.e. }\;\omega\in X,

where

(3)

\gamma^{\beta\Phi}_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda}\mid\omega_{\Lambda^{c}})=\frac{\exp\left(-H_{\Lambda}^{\beta\Phi}(\omega)\right)}{\sum\limits_{\bar{\omega}_{\Lambda}\in\{\pm 1\}^{\Lambda}}\exp\left(-H_{\Lambda}^{\beta\Phi}(\bar{\omega}_{\Lambda}\omega_{\Lambda^{c}})\right)},\;\;\;\;H_{\Lambda}^{\beta\Phi}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}V:V\Subset\mathbb{Z}\\
V\cap\Lambda\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\beta\Phi_{V},

and
\bar{\omega}_{\Lambda}\omega_{\Lambda^{c}}\in X
denotes the configuration constructed by concatenating the strings
\bar{\omega}_{\Lambda}
and
\omega_{\Lambda^{c}}
.
It is a well-known fact
[
8
,
15
]
for the standard Dyson model that for every
\alpha\in(1,2]
there exists a critical value
\beta_{c}(\alpha)\in(0,\infty)
such that for every
\beta<\beta_{c}(\alpha)
, there exists a unique Gibbs measure for
\beta\Phi
and for every
\beta>\beta_{c}(\alpha)
, there are multiple Gibbs measures for
\beta\Phi
.

We also consider the interaction
\Phi
on the half-line
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.
Studying the interaction
\Phi
on
{\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
amounts to studying the thermodynamic properties of the potential:

(4)

\phi(x):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}J(n)x_{0}x_{n},\;\;\;x\in X_{+}:=\{\pm 1\}^{\mathbb{Z}_{+}}.

Note that
\phi
and
\Phi
are related through:

(5)

\phi=-\sum_{0\in V\Subset\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\Phi_{V},

which often appears in the variational characterisation of the Gibbs measures.
In fact, by the Gibbs variational principle
[
10
]
*Theorem 15.39, the translation-invariant Gibbs measures for
\Phi
– that is, those preserved by the shift map
S
– are precisely the
equilibrium states
for the potential
\phi
. These are the measures
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1,S}(X)
satisfying

(6)

h(\mu)+\int_{X}\phi d\mu=\sup\Big\{h(\tau)+\int_{X}\phi d\tau:\tau\in\mathcal{M}_{1,S}(X)\Big\},

where
h(\tau)
is the measure-theoretic entropy of the measure
\tau
, the shift map
S:X\to X
is defined by
S\omega=(\omega_{i+1})_{i}
and
\mathcal{M}_{1,S}(X)
denotes the set of the translation-invariant probability measures on
X
.

One of the main objectives in this paper is to study the Ruelle-Perron-Frobenius
transfer operator

\mathcal{L}_{\beta\phi}
for the potential
\beta\phi
, which acts on the space of functions on
X_{+}
by

(7)

\mathcal{L}_{\beta\phi}f(x):=\sum_{a\in\{\pm 1\}}e^{\beta\phi(ax)}f(ax),\;\;x\in X_{+},

here
f\in\mathbb{R}^{X_{+}}
and
ax\in X_{+}
denotes concatenated configuration.
First, we note that for the spectral radius
\rho(\mathcal{L}_{\beta\phi})
of the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\beta\phi}
, one has
\rho(\mathcal{L}_{\beta\phi})=e^{P(\beta\phi)}
, where
P(\beta\phi)
is the topological pressure of
\beta\phi
, which also coincides with the infinite-volume pressure of the interaction
\beta\Phi
.
Second, the adjoint operator
\mathcal{L}_{\beta\phi}^{*}
, which acts on the space of signed measures
\mathcal{M}(X_{+})
, always has an eigenprobability
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(X_{+})
corresponding to the spectral radius
\lambda=\rho(\mathcal{L}_{\beta\phi})
, i.e.,
\mathcal{L}_{\beta\phi}^{*}\nu=\lambda\nu
.
Throughout the paper, we also refer to the eigenprobabilities of
\mathcal{L}_{\beta\phi}^{*}
as the one-sided Gibbs measures for
\beta\phi
(or
\beta\Phi
) since they are exactly the probability measures satisfying the DLR equations corresponding to
\beta\phi
(or
\beta\Phi|_{\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
)
[
4
]
.
In the half-line
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, a similar phase diagram to that of the interactions
\beta\Phi
on
\mathbb{Z}
is also established.
Indeed, in
[
13
]
, it was proven for the standard Dyson model that there exists a critical temperature
\beta_{c}^{+}(\alpha)\in(0,\infty)
such that for every
\beta\in[0,\beta^{+}_{c}(\alpha))

there exists a unique eigenprobability of
\mathcal{L}_{\beta\phi}^{*}
and for
\beta>\beta^{+}_{c}(\alpha)
there are multiple eigenprobabilities.
In
[
13
]
, Johansson, Öberg and Pollicott also showed that for every
\alpha\in(1,2]
,
\beta_{c}^{+}(\alpha)\leq 8\beta_{c}(\alpha)
and conjectured that
\beta^{+}_{c}(\alpha)=\beta_{c}(\alpha)
.
We note that part of this conjecture – namely, that
\beta_{c}(\alpha)\leq\beta_{c}^{+}(\alpha)
for every
\alpha\in(1,2]
– follows directly from the Griffiths inequalities
[
9
,
11
,
12
]
(see Proposition
2.2
). The eigenprobabilities correspond, in a dual sense, to the principal eigenfunctions of the transfer operator
\mathcal{L}_{\beta\phi}
. While the existence of an eigenprobability of the adjoint operator
\mathcal{L}_{\beta\phi}^{*}
follows directly from the DLR equations, establishing the existence of a principal eigenfunction for
\mathcal{L}_{\beta\phi}
is considerably more subtle and technically involved.
Important advancements in this direction have been made in
[
19
,
14
,
16
]
, whose relevant results — specialised here to the standard Dyson model — are summarised in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
19
,
14
]
Let
\Phi
be the standard Dyson interaction (
1
).
Then

(i)

for all
\alpha>1
and for
\beta\geq 0

sufficiently small
, the unique Gibbs state
\mu
(in fact, the restriction to
X_{+}
) for
\beta\Phi
is equivalent to the unique half-line Gibbs state
\nu
for
\beta\phi
, i.e.,
\mu\ll\nu
and
\nu\ll\mu
.
In particular, the Perron-Frobenius transfer operator
\mathcal{L}_{\beta\phi}
has a non-negative integrable eigenfunction in
L^{1}(X_{+},\nu)
;

(ii)

if
\alpha>\frac{3}{2}
, then for all
0\leq\beta<\beta_{c}(\alpha)
, the Gibbs state
\mu
for
\beta\Phi

is not only equivalent to the half-line Gibbs state
\nu
, but there even exists
a continuous version of the Radon-Nikodym density
\frac{d\mu}{d\nu}
.
Thus, the Perron-Frobenius transfer operator
\mathcal{L}_{\beta\phi}
has a positive continuous eigenfunction.

The main objective of this paper, in the context of transfer operators, is to extend the above result to the entire supercritical region, that is, to all pairs
(\alpha,\beta)
with
\alpha\in(1,2]
and
\beta\in[0,\beta_{c}(\alpha))
.
We, in fact, state the main result of this paper for interactions given by (
1
), which are slightly more general than the standard Dyson interaction.
A broader generalisation was considered in
[
19
]
, where the authors studied a class of long-range interactions – potentially including multi-body and non-ferromagnetic cases.
However, the analysis in
[
19
]
was restricted to sufficiently small values of
\beta\geq 0
, ensuring that the interaction
\Phi
satisfies the Dobrushin uniqueness condition
[
10
]
*Chapter 8.
This condition corresponds to the high-temperature regime and is known to be strictly contained within the uniqueness region.

Near-critical regimes
, i.e., when
0\ll\beta<\beta_{c}(\alpha)
, are typically more delicate to analyse in statistical mechanics and often require different techniques.
This phenomenon is also reflected in our setting.
We also note that
[
14
]
approached the problem using the random cluster representation – a method fundamentally different from the techniques used in
[
19
]
– and successfully treated both the high-temperatures
0\leq\beta\ll\beta_{c}(\alpha)
and the near-critical regimes
0\ll\beta<\beta_{c}(\alpha)
, under the assumption that
\alpha>\frac{3}{2}
.
However, the methods in
[
14
]
fundamentally depend on the square summability of the variations of the potential
\phi
, a condition that holds only for
\alpha>\frac{3}{2}
.
Consequently, their approach does not extend to the strongly interacting setting
\alpha\leq\frac{3}{2}
.
In another related work
[
16
]
, the principal eigenfunctions were studied in the presence of an external field in (
1
). While that work addresses a different setting – since we focus on the case without external fields – it is noteworthy that the presence of an external field, as shown in
[
16
]
, tends to worsen the regularity of the eigenfunction.

Another objective of this paper is to study the concentration properties of the Gibbs measures for the near-critical Dyson models.
This has been done for the two-dimensional nearest-neighbour Ising model
[
17
,
3
]
using methods very specific for the short-range models and the two-dimensional lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.
The problem has been remaining open for the long-range models, and recently, Bauerschmidt and Dagallier
[
2
]
succeeded in obtaining the log-Sobolev inequalities (see Section
3
for the detailed discussion) for the finite-volume Gibbs measures of ferromagnetic Ising models.
In this paper, we employ Bauerschmidt and Dagallier’s result to establish the
Gaussian Concentration Bounds
(GCB) for the unique
infinite-volume Gibbs measure
of the interaction
\beta\Phi
for all the values of
0\leq\beta<\beta_{c}(\alpha)
.
We note that the Gaussian Concentration Bound for a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(X)
(or
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(X_{+})
) refers to a concentration inequality of the form

(8)

\mu\Big(\Big\{\omega\in X:\;F(\omega)-\int_{X}Fd\mu\;\geq\;t\Big\}\Big)\;\leq\;e^{-\frac{2t^{2}}{D||\underline{\delta}(F)||_{2}^{2}}},\;\;\;t>0,

where
F
is a real-valued continuous function on
X
,
D>0
is a constant independent of
F
and
t
, and
\lVert\underline{\delta}(F)\rVert^{2}_{2}
denotes the total oscillation of
F
, i.e.,
\lVert\underline{\delta}(F)\rVert^{2}_{2}:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(\delta_{k}F)^{2}
and
\delta_{k}F:=\sup\bigl\{F(\xi)-F(\eta):\xi_{j}=\eta_{j},\;j\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{k\}\bigr\}
.
It is a well-known fact (
[
20
]
*Proposition 2.5.2) that up to the constant
D
, (
8
) is equivalent to
F:X\to\mathbb{R}
to be
sub-Gaussian
, i.e.,

(9)

\int_{X}e^{F-\int_{X}Fd\mu}d\mu\leq e^{D||\underline{\delta}(F)||^{2}_{2}}.

or
\mu
to have
moment concentration bounds
, i.e., for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

(10)

\int_{X}\Big|F-\int_{X}Fd\mu\Big|^{m}d\mu\leq\Big(\frac{D||\underline{\delta}(F)||_{2}^{2}}{2}\Big)^{\frac{m}{2}}m\Gamma\Big(\frac{m}{2}\Big),

where
\Gamma
is Euler’s gamma function.

We now summarise the main results of this paper in the following theorem.

Theorem A
.

Let
\Phi
be the Dyson interaction given in (
1
), with couplings
J(k)\geq 0,\;k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}J(k)<\infty
.

(i)

If
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=i}^{\infty}J(k)^{2}<\infty
, then for every
\beta<\beta_{c}(\Phi)
, the Gaussian Concentration Bound (GCB) holds for the unique Gibbs measure
\mu
of the interaction
\beta\Phi
, and for the unique eigenprobability
\nu
of the transfer operator
\mathcal{L}_{\beta\phi}
, here
\beta_{c}(\Phi)
is the critical inverse temperature of the phase transitions for
\Phi
, i.e.,

\beta_{c}(\Phi):=\sup\{\beta\geq 0:\beta\Phi\text{ has a unique Gibbs measure}\}.

(ii)

If

J(k)=O(k^{-1})
, then for every
\beta<\beta_{c}(\Phi)
, the transfer operator
\mathcal{L}_{\beta\phi}
admits a non-negative principal eigenfunction in
L^{1}(\nu)
.

(iii)

If
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{k=i}^{\infty}J(k)\Big)^{2}<\infty
, then for every
\beta<\beta_{c}(\Phi)
, the transfer operator
\mathcal{L}_{\beta\phi}
admits a positive continuous principal eigenfunction.

Remark 1.2
.

(1)

The couplings in the standard Dyson interaction satisfy conditions (i) and (ii) of the theorem stated above for all values of
\alpha>1
.
However, condition in (iii) is satisfied if and only if
\alpha>\frac{3}{2}
.
In
[
19
]
, the authors conjectured that the principal eigenfunction exhibits essential discontinuities when
\alpha<\frac{3}{2}
.
We maintain this conjecture,
but do not address it in the present work.

(2)

We expect that the second part of Theorem
A
remains valid under the slightly weaker condition in part (i), namely that
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=i}^{\infty}J(k)^{2}<\infty
. This is supported by a widely held expectation in statistical mechanics: that for long-range models, the two-point correlation functions decay at the same rate as the interaction energy throughout the entire supercritical region.
This decay behaviour can be rigorously established when
\beta
is sufficiently small so that
\beta\Phi
satisfies the Dobrushin uniqueness condition. In the near-critical regime, partial progress has been made; see
[
18
,
1
]
.

(3)

The condition
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{k=i}^{\infty}J(k)\Big)^{2}<\infty
also appears in
[
14
]
, where, under this assumption, the authors establish the existence of a continuous eigenfunction using the random cluster representation of the Ising model—a method distinct from the one employed in the present work.

(4)

It is natural to ask what occurs when
\beta\geq\beta_{c}(\alpha)
. In the phase coexistence region
\beta>\beta_{c}(\alpha)
, it is known from
[
3
]
that none of the extremal Gibbs states for
\beta\Phi
satisfy the GCB.
At criticality (
\beta=\beta_{c}(\alpha)
), ferromagnetic spin models often admit a unique Gibbs measure; however, this uniqueness does not imply that the GCB holds. For example, the two-dimensional nearest-neighbour Ising model
[
3
]
has a unique critical Gibbs measure, which does not satisfy the GCB.
We expect an analogous phenomenon to occur for the Dyson model (
1
) at criticality.

We prove Theorem
A
by employing the method of intermediate interactions, originally introduced in
[
19
]
.
In Section
2
, we construct those interactions and discuss their phase diagrams.

In Section
3
, we study the log-Sobolev inequalities for the Gibbs measures of the interaction
\Phi
and also the intermediate interactions, by relying on a recent result by Bauerschmidt and Dagallier
[
2
]
.
There, we prove Theorem
3.2
, which establishes the log-Sobolev inequalities with a uniform log-Sobolev constant for a family of Gibbs measures including
\mu
and
\nu
.
We then derive the GCB from these inequalities via the Herbst argument, thus proving part (i) of Theorem
A
.
As we have not found a full version of the Herbst argument suitable for our setting in the literature, we include a self-contained proof in the Appendix.

In Section
4
, we prove the second and third parts of Theorem
A
.
We show the existence of an integrable eigenfunction by adapting the arguments from the proof of Theorem E in
[
19
]
.
However, the proof in
[
19
]
relies on the Dobrushin uniqueness condition, which ensures strong mixing and decay of correlations but fails to hold near criticality.
To overcome this, we instead estimate the total sum of spin-spin correlations, using a result by Duminil-Copin and Tassion
[
7
]
, which serves our purpose just as well.
The proof of the continuity of the eigenfunction under slightly stronger condition
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{k=i}^{\infty}J(k)^{2}\Big)<\infty
replicates the arguments previously used in the third part of Theorem C in
[
19
]
.
For the sake of completeness, we include the main steps of the proof.

2.
Intermediate interactions and their phase diagrams

2.1.
Intermediate interactions

We start this section by recalling the construction of those intermediate interactions for the Dyson interaction
\Phi
given by (
1
).
We will represent
\mathbb{Z}=-\mathbb{N}\cup\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.
Consider the countable collection of finite subsets of
\mathbb{Z}
such that:

\mathcal{A}=\{\Lambda\Subset\mathbb{Z}:\,\min(\Lambda)<0,\ \max\Lambda\geq 0\}.

Index elements of
\mathcal{A}
in an order
\mathcal{A}=\{\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2},\ldots\}
so that for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
k_{N}\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying

(11)

\sum_{i=1}^{k_{N}}\Phi_{\Lambda_{i}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\min V<0\leq\max V\\
V\subset[-N,N]\end{subarray}}\Phi_{V}.

Then define

{\Psi}^{(k)}_{\Lambda}=\begin{cases}\Phi_{\Lambda},&\quad\Lambda\notin\{\Lambda_{i}:i\geq k+1\},\\
0,&\quad\Lambda\in\{\Lambda_{i}:i\geq k+1\},\end{cases}\;

In other words, we first remove all
\Phi_{\Lambda}
’s with
\Lambda\in\mathcal{A}
from
\Phi
, and then add them one by one.
Clearly, all the constructed interactions are UAC.

Remark 2.1
.

1)

Every
\Psi^{(k)}
is a local (finite) perturbation of
\Psi^{(0)}
, and
\Psi^{(k)}
tend to
\Phi

as
k\to\infty
, in the sense that

\Psi^{(k)}_{\Lambda}\rightrightarrows\Phi_{\Lambda}

for all
\Lambda\Subset\mathbb{Z}
.

2)

For specifications, it can also be concluded that for every
\beta\geq 0

\gamma^{\beta\Psi^{(k)}}
converges to
\gamma^{\beta\Phi}
as
k\to\infty
, i.e., for all
B\in\mathcal{F}
and
V\Subset\mathbb{Z}
,

\gamma^{\beta\Psi^{(k)}}_{V}(B|\omega)\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{}\gamma^{\beta\Phi}_{V}(B|\omega)\;\text{
uniformly on the b.c. }\omega\in X.

3)

In addition, if
\nu^{(k)}
is a Gibbs measure for
\beta\Psi^{(k)}
, then by Theorem 4.17 in
[
10
]
, any weak
∗
-limit point of the sequence
\{\nu^{(k)}\}_{k\geq 0}
becomes a Gibbs measure for the potential
\beta\Phi
.

Another important observation is the following: since we have constructed
\beta\Psi^{(0)}
from
\beta\Phi
by removing all the interactions between the
left

-\mathbb{N}
and the
right

\mathbb{Z}_{+}
half-lines, the corresponding specification
\gamma^{\beta\Psi^{(0)}}
becomes
product type

[
10
]
*Example 7.18.
More precisely,
\gamma^{\beta\Psi^{(0)}}=\gamma^{\beta\Phi|_{-\mathbb{N}}}\times\gamma^{\beta\Phi|_{\mathbb{Z}_{+}}}
, where
\Phi|_{-\mathbb{N}}
and
\Phi|_{\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
are the restrictions of
\Phi
to the half-lines
-\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, respectively.
Thus we have the following for the extremal Gibbs measures
[
10
]
*Example 7.18:

(12)

\text{ex}\;\mathscr{G}(\gamma^{\beta\Psi^{(0)}})=\{\nu^{l}\times\nu^{r}:\nu^{l}\in\text{ex}\;\mathscr{G}(\gamma^{\beta\Phi|_{-\mathbb{N}}}),\;\;\nu^{r}\in\text{ex}\;\mathscr{G}(\gamma^{\beta\Phi|_{\mathbb{Z}_{+}}})\}

For each
\Psi^{(k)}
, we define the critical inverse temperature of phase transitions by

\beta_{c}(\Psi^{(k)}):=\sup\{\beta\geq 0:\beta\Psi^{(k)}\text{ has a unique Gibbs measure}\}.

By employing the Griffiths inequalities
[
9
,
12
,
11
]
, one can establish the proposition below regarding the phase diagrams of the intermediate interactions
\Psi^{(k)}
associated with the Dyson interaction
\Phi
.

Proposition 2.2
.

For every
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,

(13)

\beta_{c}(\Psi^{(k)})\geq\beta_{c}(\Psi^{(k+1)})\geq\beta_{c}(\Phi).

3.
Log Sobolev inequalities for the infinite-volume Gibbs measures

In this section, we discuss log-Sobolev inequalities for the unique infinite-volume Gibbs measures
\mu^{\beta}
and
\nu^{(k),\beta}
associated with the interactions
\beta\Phi
and
\beta\Psi^{(k)}
, respectively, for
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, in the regime
\beta<\beta_{c}(\Phi)
.
To emphasise the dependence on the inverse temperature, we include
\beta
in the superscripts throughout this section.

Consider a subset (finite or infinite)
L\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
and denote the corresponding configuration space by
X_{L}:=\{\pm 1\}^{L}
.
For a configuration
\omega\in X_{L}
,
\omega^{(i)}\in X_{L}
denotes the configuration obtained from
\omega
by flipping the spin at site
i\in\mathbb{L}
and keeping all other spins unchanged, i.e.,

\omega^{(i)}_{j}:=\begin{cases}\omega_{j},&i\neq j;\\
-\omega_{i},&i=j.\end{cases}

Definition 3.1
.

A measure
\tau
on
X_{L}
satisfies the
log-Sobolev inequality
(LSI) if there exists a constant
D=D(\tau)>0
such that, for every local function
f:X_{L}\to\mathbb{R}
,

(14)

\texttt{Ent}_{\tau}(f^{2})\leq 2D\int_{X_{L}}\sum_{i\in L}\;(f(\omega)-f(\omega^{(i)}))^{2}\tau(d\omega),

where for a non-negative local function
\tilde{f}:X_{L}\to\mathbb{R}
, the entropy of
\tilde{f}
with respect to
\tau
is defined as

\texttt{Ent}_{\tau}(\tilde{f}):=\int_{X_{L}}\tilde{f}\log\tilde{f}\,d\tau-\int_{X_{L}}\tilde{f}\,d\tau\cdot\log\int_{X_{L}}\tilde{f}\,d\tau.

The smallest such constant
D
is called the log-Sobolev constant for
\tau
.

Before stating the main theorem of this section, we note that, thanks to Theorem 2.1 in
[
7
]
, the infinite-volume susceptibility of the Dyson model
\beta\Phi
, defined as

(15)

\chi_{\beta}(\Phi):=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mu^{\beta}(\sigma_{0}\sigma_{i}),

is finite for every
0\leq\beta<\beta_{c}(\Phi)
, here
\mu^{\beta}
is the unique infinite-volume Gibbs measure for
\beta\Phi
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume
\beta<\beta_{c}(\Phi)
and let
\nu^{(k),\beta}
be the unique infinite-volume Gibbs measures for
\beta\Psi^{(k)}
.
Then
\mu^{\beta}
and
\nu^{(k),\beta}
satisfy the log-Sobolev inequality with a constant not larger than
\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\beta}{2}\exp{(2\beta\chi_{\beta}(\Phi))}
.

To prove Theorem
3.2
, we rely on the main result in
[
2
]
,
which is built upon a recently developed correlation inequality in
[
6
]
.
In
[
2
]
, Bauerschmidt and Dagallier established log-Sobolev inequalities for finite-volume Gibbs measures of the ferromagnetic Ising model with free boundary conditions.
The interactions considered in
[
2
]
are more general than the Dyson interaction (
1
).

Below, we recall the main result of
[
2
]
.

Fix a finite volume
\Lambda\Subset\mathbb{Z}
and let
\mathscr{A}^{(\Lambda)}
be an arbitrary
\Lambda\times\Lambda
real matrix satisfying the following conditions:

(C1)

\mathscr{A}^{(\Lambda)}
is a symmetric matrix with
\mathscr{A}^{(\Lambda)}_{ij}\leq 0
for all off-diagonal elements

To verify uniform integrability, we apply De la Vallée Poussin’s theorem

One can check using the Griffiths inequalities that

\displaystyle\Big\lvert\int_{X}W_{k}d\nu^{(k)}\Big\rvert

\eta\in X_{+}
that

(51)

e^{-8D\beta^{2}C_{1}}\leq f^{[N]}_{+}(\eta)\leq e^{8D\beta^{2}C_{1}}.

Equicontinuity:

As the denominator
\int_{X}e^{-W_{[N]}}d\nu^{(0)}
is uniformly bounded as shown in (
50
), it suffices to show that the family
\Big\{\int_{X_{-}}e^{-W_{[N]}(\xi,\cdot)}\nu_{-}(d\xi):N\in\mathbb{N}\Big\}
is equicontinuous.
Consider
n\in\mathbb{N}
and configurations
\eta,\tilde{\eta}\in X_{+}
such that
\eta_{0}^{n-1}=\tilde{\eta}_{0}^{n-1}
.
Then, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

(52)

\Big|\int_{X_{-}}\Big[e^{-W_{[N]}(\xi,\eta)}-e^{-W_{[N]}(\xi,\tilde{\eta})}\Big]\nu_{-}(d\xi)\Big|\\
\leq\Big(\int_{X_{-}}e^{-2W_{[N]}(\xi,\eta)}\nu_{-}(d\xi)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{X_{-}}\Big[e^{W_{[N]}(\xi,\eta)-W_{[N]}(\xi,\tilde{\eta})}-1\Big]^{2}\nu_{-}(d\xi)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

and (
48
) yields that

(53)

\Big|\int_{X_{-}}\Big[e^{-W_{[N]}(\xi,\eta)}-e^{-W_{[N]}(\xi,\tilde{\eta})}\Big]\nu_{-}(d\xi)\Big|\leq C_{2}\Big(\int_{X_{-}}\Big[e^{W_{[N]}(\xi,\eta)-W_{[N]}(\xi,\tilde{\eta})}-1\Big]^{2}\nu_{-}(d\xi)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

with
C_{2}:=e^{8D\beta^{2}C_{1}}
.
We will bound the last integral by bounding the exponent.

Note that
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
,

(54)

\delta_{-i}(W_{[N]}(\cdot,\eta)-W_{[N]}(\cdot,\tilde{\eta}))=2\beta\Big|\sum_{j=n}^{N}J(i+j)(\eta_{j}-\tilde{\eta}_{j})\Big|\leq 4\beta\sum_{j=n}^{N}J(i+j)\;.

Hence, for sufficiently large
n
and
N>n
,

||\underline{\delta}(W_{[N]}(\cdot,\eta)-W_{[N]}(\cdot,\tilde{\eta}))||_{2}^{2}\leq 16\beta^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{j=n}^{N}J(i+j)\Big)^{2}\leq 16\beta^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{j=n+i}^{\infty}J(j)\Big)^{2}=:u_{n}

with

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}u_{n}=0.

The spin-flip invariance of
\nu_{-}
implies that for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\int_{X_{-}}[W_{[N]}(\xi,\eta)-W_{[N]}(\xi,\tilde{\eta})]\,\nu_{-}(d\xi)=0\;.

Then, since
\nu_{-}
satisfies the GCB, we have, from the moment concentration bounds (
10
), that for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

(55)

\displaystyle\int_{X_{-}}\Big|W_{[N]}(\xi,\eta)-W_{[N]}(\xi,\tilde{\eta})\Big|^{m}\nu_{-}(d\xi)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{D||\underline{\delta}(W_{[N]}(\cdot,\eta)-W_{[N]}(\cdot,\tilde{\eta}))||_{2}^{2}}{2}\Big)^{\frac{m}{2}}m\,\Gamma\Big(\frac{m}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\,v_{n}^{m}\,\Gamma\Big(\frac{m}{2}\Big)

with

v_{n}:=\Big(\frac{D\,u_{n}}{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\;.

To conclude, we expand the square on the right-hand side of (
53
):

(56)

\displaystyle\int_{X_{-}}\Big[e^{W_{[N]}(\xi,\eta)-W_{[N]}(\xi,\tilde{\eta})}-1\Big]^{2}\nu_{-}(d\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X_{-}}\Big[e^{2\left[W_{[N]}(\xi,\eta)-W_{[N]}(\xi,\tilde{\eta})\right]}-2\,e^{W_{[N]}(\xi,\eta)-W_{[N]}(\xi,\tilde{\eta})}+1\Big]\nu_{-}(d\xi)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{m}+2}{m!}\int_{X_{-}}\Bigl|W_{[N]}(\xi,\eta)-W_{[N]}(\xi,\tilde{\eta})\Bigr|^{m}\nu_{-}(d\xi).

Using elementary analysis, one can demonstrate that

(57)

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{m}+2}{m!}m\,v_{n}^{m}\,\Gamma\Big(\frac{m}{2}\Big)\leq(6v_{n}^{2}+8v_{n})e^{v_{n}^{2}}.

Therefore, we obtain from (
53
) and (
55
),

(58)

\Big|\int_{X_{-}}\Big[e^{-W_{[N]}(\xi,\eta)}-e^{-W_{[N]}(\xi,\tilde{\eta})}\Big]\nu_{-}(d\xi)\Big|\leq C_{2}((6v_{n}^{2}+8v_{n})e^{v_{n}^{2}})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}v_{n}=0
, we conclude the claim.

Appendix: GCB from LSI in the Gibbsian setting

We call an interaction
\Psi=(\Psi_{\Lambda})_{\Lambda\Subset\mathbb{Z}}
on
X=\{\pm 1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}

strongly uniformly absolutely convergent
(SUAC) if

(59)

\lVert\Psi\rVert:=\sup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}V\ni i\\
V\Subset\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\lVert\Psi_{V}\rVert_{\infty}<\infty.

The goal of the appendix is to show the following statement.

Proposition A.1
.

Assume
\mu
is a unique Gibbs measure for a SUAC interaction
\Psi
.
If
\mu
satisfies the log Sobolev inequality with a constant
D>0
, then
\mu
satisfies the Gaussian concentration bound with the constant
\frac{D(e^{2\lVert\Psi\rVert}+1)}{2}
.

Note that for
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, the spin-flip transformation
\mathfrak{S}_{i}:X\to X
at
i
is defined as
\mathfrak{S}_{i}(\omega)=\omega^{(i)}
,
\omega\in X
.
We use the following lemma in the proof of Proposition
A.1
.

Lemma A.2
.

For every
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, the pushforward
\mu^{(i)}:=(\mathfrak{S}_{i})_{*}(\mu)=\mu\circ(\mathfrak{S}_{i})^{-1}
of
\mu
by
\mathfrak{S}_{i}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu
and
\frac{d\mu^{(i)}}{d\mu}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
.
Furthermore,

(60)

\sup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\lVert\frac{d\mu^{(i)}}{d\mu}\Big\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}\leq e^{2\lVert\Psi\rVert}.

Proof of Lemma
A.2
.

Fix
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.
We consider finite volumes
\Lambda\ni i
and corresponding the finite-volume Gibbs measures for
\Psi
with the
+
boundary condition:

(61)

\mu_{\Lambda}([\omega_{\Lambda}])=\frac{e^{-H_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda}+_{\Lambda^{c}})}}{Z_{\Lambda}(+)},\;\;\omega_{\Lambda}\in\{\pm 1\}^{\Lambda},

where
H_{\Lambda}:=\sum_{V\cap\Lambda\neq\emptyset}\Psi_{V}
is the Hamiltonian in
\Lambda
and
Z_{\Lambda}(+):=\sum_{\omega_{\Lambda}\in E^{\Lambda}}e^{-H_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda}+_{\Lambda^{c}})}
is the corresponding partition function.
By the uniqueness, the finite-volume Gibbs measures
\mu_{\Lambda}
converge to
\mu
as
\Lambda\uparrow\mathbb{Z}
in the weak star topology.
Thus, by the continuity of the pushforward
(\mathfrak{S}_{i})_{*}
, one has
\mu_{\Lambda}^{(i)}\to\mu^{(i)}
, where
\mu_{\Lambda}^{(i)}:=\mu_{\Lambda}\circ(\mathfrak{S}_{i})^{-1}
.
Note that
\mu_{\Lambda}^{(i)}\ll\mu_{\Lambda}
and for every
\omega_{\Lambda}\in E^{\Lambda}
,
\mu_{\Lambda}^{(i)}([\omega_{\Lambda}])=\mu_{\Lambda}([\omega^{(i)}_{\Lambda}])
.
Thus by (
61
),

(62)

h^{(i)}_{\Lambda}(\omega):=\frac{d\mu_{\Lambda}^{(i)}}{d\mu_{\Lambda}}(\omega)=\frac{\mu_{\Lambda}^{(i)}([\omega_{\Lambda}])}{\mu_{\Lambda}([\omega_{\Lambda}])}=e^{H_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda}+_{\Lambda^{c}})-H_{\Lambda}(\omega^{(i)}_{\Lambda}+_{\Lambda^{c}})}.

Note that

(63)

\displaystyle H_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\Lambda}+_{\Lambda^{c}})-H_{\Lambda}(\omega^{(i)}_{\Lambda}+_{\Lambda^{c}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}V\ni i\end{subarray}}[\Psi_{V}(\omega_{\Lambda}+_{\Lambda^{c}})-\Psi_{V}(\omega^{(i)}_{\Lambda}+_{\Lambda^{c}})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{\{i\}}(\omega_{\Lambda}+_{\Lambda^{c}})-H_{\{i\}}(\omega^{(i)}_{\Lambda}+_{\Lambda^{c}})

and by the continuity of
H_{\{i\}}
, the right-hand side of (
63
) converges uniformly in
\omega\in X
to
H_{\{i\}}(\omega)-H_{\{i\}}(\omega^{(i)})
as
\Lambda\uparrow\mathbb{Z}
.
Therefore, it follows from (
62
) that the finite-volume Radon-Nikodym densities
h_{\Lambda}^{(i)}(\omega)
converge to

(64)

h^{(i)}(\omega):=e^{H_{\{i\}}(\omega)-H_{\{i\}}(\omega^{(i)})}

uniformly in
\omega\in X
as
\Lambda\uparrow\mathbb{Z}
.
Since
\mu_{\Lambda}^{(i)}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\mu^{(i)}
as
\Lambda\uparrow\mathbb{Z}
, for any test function
f\in C(X)
,

(65)

\lim_{\Lambda\uparrow\mathbb{Z}}\int_{X}fd\mu_{\Lambda}^{(i)}=\int_{X}fd\mu^{(i)}.

On the other hand,

(66)

\int_{X}fd\mu_{\Lambda}^{(i)}=\int_{X}fh_{\Lambda}^{(i)}d\mu_{\Lambda}=\int_{X}fh^{(i)}d\mu_{\Lambda}+\int_{X}f(h_{\Lambda}^{(i)}-h^{(i)})d\mu_{\Lambda}.

Then since
\mu_{\Lambda}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\mu
as
\Lambda\uparrow\mathbb{Z}
and
\lVert h_{\Lambda}^{(i)}-h^{(i)}\rVert_{\infty}\xrightarrow[\Lambda\uparrow\mathbb{Z}]{}0
,
one obtains

(67)

\lim_{\Lambda\uparrow\mathbb{Z}}\int_{X}fd\mu_{\Lambda}^{(i)}=\int_{X}fh^{(i)}d\mu.

Then by combining this with (
65
), one concludes that
\mu^{(i)}\ll\mu
and

(68)

\frac{d\mu^{(i)}}{d\mu}=h^{(i)}.

One can readily check from (
64
) that for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\lVert h^{(i)}\rVert_{\infty}\leq e^{2\sum_{V\ni i}\lVert\Psi_{V}\rVert_{\infty}}\leq e^{2\lVert\Psi\rVert}
, and hence one concludes the last claim of the lemma.

∎

In the proof of Proposition
A.1
, we also use the following elementary lemma, which easily follows from the mean value theorem.

Lemma A.3
.

For all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, one has
\lvert e^{b}-e^{a}\rvert\leq e^{\max\{a,b\}}|b-a|
.

Proof of Proposition
A.1
.

Fix a local function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
and a number
\lambda\in[0,1]
.
Consider
e^{\lambda f}
, and denote
\mathcal{Z}(\lambda):=\int_{X}e^{\lambda f}d\mu
.
Note that
\mathcal{Z}\in C^{1}([0,1])
and for all
\lambda\in[0,1]
,
\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}(\lambda)=\int_{X}fe^{\lambda f}d\mu
.
By applying the log Sobolev inequality to
e^{\lambda f/2}
, one gets

(69)

\lambda\int_{X}fe^{\lambda f}d\mu-\int_{X}e^{\lambda f}d\mu\cdot\log\Big(\int_{X}e^{\lambda f}d\mu\Big)\leq 2D\int_{X}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\lvert e^{\lambda f(\omega)/2}-e^{\lambda f(\omega^{(i)})/2}\Big\rvert^{2}\mu(d\omega).

By rewriting (
69
) in terms of
\mathcal{Z}
and by employing Lemma
A.2
and Lemma
A.3
,

(70)

\displaystyle\lambda\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}(\lambda)-\mathcal{Z}(\lambda)\log\mathcal{Z}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2D\int_{X}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\lvert e^{\lambda f(\omega)/2}-e^{\lambda f(\omega^{(i)})/2}\Big\rvert^{2}\mu(d\omega)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{Lemma }\ref{elment lemma-mvt}}{\leq}

\displaystyle 2D\int_{X}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{\lambda\max\{f(\omega),f(\omega^{(i)})\}}\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}(\delta_{i}f)^{2}\mu(d\omega)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{D\lambda^{2}}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big[\int_{X}\Big(e^{\lambda f}+e^{\lambda f\circ\mathfrak{S}_{i}}\Big)d\mu\cdot(\delta_{i}f)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{D\lambda^{2}}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big[\mathcal{Z}(\lambda)+\int_{X}e^{\lambda f}d\mu^{(i)}\Big]\cdot(\delta_{i}f)^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{Lemma }\ref{imp lem for GCB from LSI}}{=}

\displaystyle\frac{D\lambda^{2}}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big[\mathcal{Z}(\lambda)+\int_{X}e^{\lambda f}h^{(i)}d\mu\Big]\cdot(\delta_{i}f)^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{(\ref{uni bound for densities of flipping})}{\leq}

\displaystyle\frac{D\lambda^{2}}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big[\mathcal{Z}(\lambda)+e^{2\lVert\Psi\rVert}\mathcal{Z}(\lambda)\Big]\cdot(\delta_{i}f)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{D(e^{2\lVert\Psi\rVert}+1)}{2}\cdot\lVert\underline{\delta}(f)\rVert_{2}^{2}\cdot\mathcal{Z}(\lambda)\cdot\lambda^{2}.

Now consider a function
u
on
[0,1]
defined by
u(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\mathcal{Z}(\lambda)
for
\lambda\in(0,1]
, and
u(0)=\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}(0)=\int_{X}fd\mu
.
Note that
u\in C^{1}([0,1])
.
One can easily check that (
70
) can be written in terms of
u
as

(71)

u^{\prime}(\lambda)\leq\frac{D(e^{2\lVert\Psi\rVert}+1)}{2}\lVert\underline{\delta}(f)\rVert_{2}^{2},\;\;\lambda\in[0,1].

Hence

u(1)-u(0)\leq\frac{D(e^{2\lVert\Psi\rVert}+1)}{2}\lVert\underline{\delta}(f)\rVert_{2}^{2},

which implies the Gaussian concentration bounds since

(72)

u(1)-u(0)=\log\mathcal{Z}(1)-\mu(f)=\log\Big(\int_{X}e^{f-\mu(f)}d\mu\Big)\leq\frac{D(e^{2\lVert\Psi\rVert}+1)}{2}\lVert\underline{\delta}(f)\rVert_{2}^{2}.

∎

3D UAV path optimization using a task-allocation and archive-guided mutation particle swarm optimization algorithm
Authors: Fariborz Rasoulie, Saeid Pashazadeh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThree-dimensional flight-path planning for unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) inherently involves multiple, often conflicting objectives–minimizing route length and energy consumption, maximizing safety by avoiding no-fly zones and high-turbulence regions, and ensuring smooth maneuverability within kinematic limits. This study presents an enhanced adaptation of the Task Allocation and Archive-Guided Mutation Particle Swarm Optimization (TAMOPSO) algorithm to address these challenges. In the proposed framework, each candidate path is encoded as a sequence of discrete 3D waypoints, while dynamic task allocation partitions the swarm into role-specific subpopulations: global explorers for broad route discovery, local refiners for obstacle-proximal optimization, and altitude managers for vertical profile adjustment. An external archive of nondominated solutions, maintained through a uniform contribution index, preserves Pareto-front diversity and guides adaptive Lévy-flight mutations that balance global exploration with local refinement according to the swarm’s convergence state. The algorithm’s effectiveness is evaluated across benchmark terrains of varying complexity, including scenarios with seven and ten peaks, obstacle clusters, and restricted airspace constraints. Comparative results against fourteen baseline algorithms demonstrate that TAMOPSO achieves up to 60% reduction in total flight distance, and 47% faster convergence on average, while producing smoother trajectories and more uniformly distributed Pareto fronts. Extensive loop-based Monte Carlo experiments were implemented in the MATLAB environment, confirming the statistical robustness, computational efficiency, and scalability of the proposed approach. Overall, the integration of role-based task allocation and archive-driven adaptive mutation establishes TAMOPSO as a powerful and versatile framework for intelligent, real-time UAV path optimization in complex 3D mission environments.Keywords: 3D Flight Path Planning, UAVs, Multi-Objective Optimization, Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), Adaptive Lévy-Flight Mutation, Pareto-Front DiversitySubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) have become indispensable tools across a broad spectrum of civilian and military applications, including environmental monitoring, precision agriculture, search and rescue, and infrastructure inspection. Central to the effectiveness of these missions is the ability to generate flight paths that not only minimize traversal time and energy consumption, but also ensure safety by avoiding obstacles, no-fly zones, and areas of high turbulence or threat. The inherent complexity of three-dimensional (3D) environments–with irregular terrain, urban canyons, and dynamically changing hazards–renders traditional deterministic path-planning algorithms both computationally expensive and prone to suboptimal solutions when faced with multiple, often conflicting objectives. Metaheuristic optimization, and in particular Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), has emerged as a powerful paradigm for tackling such multi-objective route-planning problems. Inspired by the social foraging behavior of bird flocks and fish schools, PSO algorithms maintain a population of candidate solutions (“particles”) that collaboratively explore the search space by exchanging information about personal and global bests. Over the past decade, numerous multi-objective PSO variants (MOPSO) have been proposed to enhance convergence speed, maintain solution diversity, and better balance exploration and exploitation. These include methods that adapt inertia weights dynamically, hybridize PSO with evolutionary operators (e.g., crossover and mutation from Genetic Algorithms), or incorporate reinforcement-learning mechanisms to adjust search parameters on the fly1. Despite these advances, two persistent challenges remain in MOPSO-based UAV path planning. First, a one-size-fits-all update rule for all particles can lead to premature convergence in complex landscapes, where some regions require broad, exploratory moves while others demand fine-grained, local refinements. Second, maintaining a well-distributed Pareto front of nondominated solutions becomes increasingly difficult as the number of objectives and environmental constraints grows, leading to clusters of similar routes and loss of potentially valuable trade-offs1.

Task Allocation & Archive-Guided Mutation PSO (TAMOPSO) addresses these challenges through a synergistic combination of dynamic subpopulation partitioning and an adaptive, archive-driven mutation scheme. In TAMOPSO, the entire swarm is partitioned at each iteration into role-specific subgroups–such as global explorers, local refiners, and altitude managers–based on spatial distribution and objective-space performance. Each subgroup employs tailored velocity coefficients and mutation tendencies that match its search role, enabling the algorithm to adaptively emphasize exploration or exploitation where it is most needed. Concurrently, an external archive of high-quality solutions is maintained and pruned using a uniform contribution index, which ensures that sparsely populated regions of the Pareto front are preserved. Particles that stagnate trigger Lévy-flight mutations guided by archived solutions, allowing for occasional large jumps that escape local optima, as well as smaller perturbations that refine promising routes2,3.

In this work, we extend the TAMOPSO framework to the domain of 3D UAV flight-path planning, where candidate routes are encoded as sequences of discrete waypoints in three-dimensional space. We formulate a bi-objective problem by minimizing total path length and a composite risk metric that encapsulates proximity to obstacles, incursion into no-fly zones, and adherence to UAV kinematic constraints (e.g., maximum climb angle, minimum turning radius). Our contributions are threefold:

Problem Encoding and Objective Modeling: We introduce a flexible representation of 3D UAV routes and define a composite risk function that unifies obstacle avoidance, regulatory compliance, and flight-dynamics considerations.

Adaptation of TAMOPSO Mechanisms: We describe how dynamic task allocation can be mapped to specific path-planning sub-tasks–global route exploration, local segment refinement near hazards, and altitude profile optimization–and how archive-guided Lévy-flight mutation can be calibrated for waypoint perturbations.

Comprehensive Benchmarking: We evaluate UAV-TAMOPSO on synthetic urban and mountainous test scenarios, comparing its performance to standard MOPSO, a Navigation-Variable MOPSO (NMOPSO), and a PSO–GA hybrid. Our results demonstrate statistically significant improvements in both objectives, as well as a more uniformly distributed Pareto front that offers mission planners a richer set of trade-off choices.

By integrating TAMOPSO’s role-based subpopulation dynamics and archive-directed mutation into the UAV path-planning context, this study aims to bridge the gap between advanced multi-objective metaheuristics and real-world aerial mission requirements. The promising performance gains and enhanced solution diversity documented herein suggest that UAV-TAMOPSO is a valuable addition to the toolkit of researchers and practitioners working on intelligent, safe, and efficient UAV trajectory design.

Framework of the TAMOPSO algorithm

This section presents the structural foundation of the proposed Task-Allocation and Archive-Guided Mutation Particle Swarm Optimization (TAMOPSO) algorithm. As illustrated in Fig. 1, TAMOPSO introduces a comprehensive framework that integrates reference-vector-based subpopulation partitioning, adaptive task-allocation search, enhanced individual selection, archive-driven elitism, and dynamic mutation via Lévy flights. The unified objective of these components is to significantly enhance search efficiency, population diversity, and overall convergence quality within multi-objective optimization tasks1.

Core design principles of TAMOPSO.

Initialization and reference-vector partitioning

The algorithm begins by initializing a swarm of particles randomly within the feasible search domain, thereby establishing the initial solution space for exploration. Each particle represents a candidate UAV trajectory or optimization vector. To enable structured search behavior, TAMOPSO employs a set of reference vectors to divide the swarm into distinct subpopulations. The classification of particles is governed by both their objective-space fitness and spatial distribution relative to these reference directions. Through this dual consideration, particles are effectively grouped into role-specific clusters–typically emphasizing exploration, exploitation, or constraint handling.

This role-based partitioning ensures that the optimization process becomes more targeted and adaptive. Subpopulations with favorable convergence characteristics can specialize in local refinement, while others continue broader exploration of the global landscape. Such differentiation improves the algorithm’s coverage of the solution space and mitigates premature convergence–a common issue in standard PSO variants.

Task-allocation search strategy

Once the population is partitioned, TAMOPSO dynamically allocates distinct search strategies to each subgroup according to their designated roles. Particles exhibiting high diversity and weak dominance are assigned exploratory search roles, encouraging them to traverse sparsely sampled regions of the decision space. Conversely, well-converged particles focus on fine-grained local search to polish existing promising solutions. This hybrid task allocation ensures a balanced trade-off between exploration and exploitation, allowing TAMOPSO to efficiently locate high-quality solutions while maintaining Pareto diversity.

Individual optimal selection and archive maintenance

During the optimization process, each particle continuously updates its individual best and contributes to the global best repository. The algorithm evaluates the dominance relationships between the particles of successive generations to determine which solutions qualify for inclusion in the external archive. If the archive is not yet saturated, non-dominated individuals are directly inserted. When the archive reaches its capacity, redundant or densely clustered solutions are removed using a Uniform Contribution Index (UCI) criterion. This pruning mechanism ensures a well-distributed Pareto front and prevents overcrowding in high-density regions of the objective space.

The updated archive not only stores elite solutions but also guides the swarm’s evolution. Information from these archived particles is reused to refine the movement of active particles, providing a balance between memory retention and adaptive learning.

Adaptive mutation via Lévy Flights

To prevent stagnation and enhance exploratory capabilities, TAMOPSO introduces an adaptive mutation strategy grounded in Lévy-flight dynamics. This mutation mechanism allows particles to perform long-distance jumps in the decision space, thereby escaping local optima and improving the likelihood of discovering globally superior regions. The adaptive controller monitors the swarm’s growth rate and convergence ratio to adjust mutation intensity dynamically. When the population exhibits excessive convergence, TAMOPSO amplifies global mutation probability to expand search boundaries. Conversely, when the swarm disperses widely, mutation strength is reduced to reinforce local refinement. This adaptive balancing mechanism ensures efficient utilization of computational resources and accelerates the algorithm’s convergence toward optimal solutions.

Iterative refinement and global convergence

Through continuous iteration, particles in each subpopulation cooperatively improve their positions by exchanging information about their individual and global bests. The role differentiation, archive feedback, and adaptive mutation collectively guide the population toward high-quality Pareto-optimal solutions. Over successive generations, the algorithm converges stably while retaining a diverse set of nondominated solutions. The best-performing individuals are preserved in the external archive, representing the final global solution set of the TAMOPSO optimization process.

Problem definition, motivation, and goal

The optimization of three-dimensional (3D) flight paths for Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) represents a critical challenge in autonomous mission planning. UAVs must navigate complex and dynamic environments characterized by irregular terrain, variable wind fields, no-fly zones, and stringent kinematic constraints. The planning objective is to generate a collision-free, energy-efficient trajectory that satisfies multiple conflicting goals–typically minimizing path length, flight time, and exposure risk–while maintaining adherence to physical and regulatory constraints.

Traditional deterministic algorithms, such as A*, Dijkstra’s, or visibility graph methods, struggle to provide near-optimal solutions in such multi-objective, nonlinear, and nonconvex problem domains. Furthermore, purely heuristic or gradient-based optimizers often converge prematurely, yielding suboptimal paths when confronted with numerous local minima.

TAMOPSO addresses these challenges through three fundamental principles:

Adaptive Role Differentiation: By partitioning the population into specialized subgroups (e.g., global explorers, local refiners, and altitude managers), the algorithm ensures that exploration and exploitation are contextually balanced across the search space. Related work

In this section, we survey the literature on UAV flight-path planning and related multi-objective optimization methods, with a focus on Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) variants and their applications to three-dimensional (3D) trajectories.

Deterministic and graph-based path planning Early UAV path-planning efforts employed deterministic graph-search and sampling-based methods. A* and Dijkstra’s algorithms on 3D voxel grids guarantee optimality under discrete representations but suffer from high computational cost in large environments and require fine discretization to capture flight dynamics. Rapidly-Exploring Random Trees (RRT) and its optimal variant RRT* reduce planning time and can handle kinodynamic constraints, yet they often produce jagged paths requiring post-processing smoothing. These approaches are limited when simultaneously optimizing multiple objectives, such as minimizing path length while avoiding high-risk zones5–7,10.

Evolutionary and Swarm-Intelligence Methods To overcome the limitations of deterministic planners, researchers have applied evolutionary algorithms (EAs) and swarm-intelligence metaheuristics. Genetic Algorithms (GA) encode waypoint sequences as chromosomes and evolve them via crossover and mutation; hybrids of GA and PSO combine GA’s global exploration with PSO’s fast convergence. Ant Colony Optimization (ACO) has also been used to construct pheromone-biased paths, though it can struggle in 3D spaces with dynamic obstacle fields9,11,13,16,31.

Multi-Objective PSO Variants for UAVs Multi-objective PSO (MOPSO) frameworks are particularly well suited for UAV path planning due to their ability to approximate Pareto fronts of conflicting objectives. Notable MOPSO applications include:

Navigation-Variable MOPSO (NMOPSO): Introduces variable mutation rates and adaptive inertia weights to balance exploration and exploitation. NMOPSO has demonstrated success in constrained 3D scenarios, optimizing path length, threat exposure, and turning smoothness.

Gaussian MOPSO with QLearning (GMOPSOQL): Integrates a Gaussian sampling strategy with reinforcement-learning–based parameter tuning, yielding robust performance in environments with stochastic wind and obstacle distributions.

PSO-GA hybrid: Combines PSO velocity updates with GA crossover on waypoint segments, benefiting from PSO’s social learning and GA’s genetic diversity. This hybrid achieved up to 12% improvement in path-length reduction over pure PSO on urban maps.

Despite these advances, most MOPSO variants apply uniform update rules across all particles, which can lead to premature convergence or loss of niche trade-off solutions in complex terrain.

Archive Strategies and Diversity Preservation Maintaining a diverse Pareto front is critical for mission planners selecting among trade-off solutions. External archives that store nondominated solutions have been widely adopted in MOPSO. Archive pruning strategies include crowding distance and clustering methods, but these can still allow clustering of solutions and neglect sparsely populated regions. Recent work introduces contribution-based metrics to fairly select archive members, and adaptive mutation schemes to reinvigorate stagnant solutions.

Task-Allocation and Hybridization in Swarm Algorithms Dynamic task allocation among subpopulations has been explored in other domains, where subsets of particles are assigned exploration or exploitation roles based on performance. Hybrid swarm–evolutionary frameworks that partition the population into function-specific groups have shown benefits in continuous optimization and feature selection. However, no existing study applies both task allocation and archive-guided adaptive mutation within a PSO framework to 3D UAV flight-path planning.

Gap and Motivation While these methods underscore the efficacy of MOPSO and its hybrids for UAV trajectory design, they lack mechanisms for dynamic role specialization and archive-driven diversity enhancement tailored to the navigation sub-tasks inherent in 3D path planning. This gap motivates our adaptation of the TAMOPSO framework–originally developed for general multi-objective optimization–to the specific challenges of UAV flight-path planning. By integrating task-based subpopulation partitioning with an archive-guided Lévy-flight mutation, our approach aims to deliver both high-quality and well-distributed Pareto-optimal routes in complex 3D environments. Problem statement & proposed method

Proposed method: task-allocation and archive-guided mutation PSO (TAMOPSO)

The Task-Allocation and Archive-Guided Mutation Particle Swarm Optimization (TAMOPSO) algorithm is an enhanced multi-objective PSO framework designed to balance global exploration and local exploitation dynamically. It extends conventional MOPSO by introducing two synergistic mechanisms: (1) Dynamic Task Allocation for adaptive subpopulation management, and (2) Archive-Guided Adaptive Mutation that uses Lévy flight perturbations directed by high-quality archived solutions. These mechanisms address two core challenges in UAV path planning–premature convergence and uneven Pareto front distribution–by enabling flexible role-based particle behavior and maintaining population diversity throughout the optimization process.

Problem statement

In our 3D UAV flight-path planning problem, each candidate solution is encoded as a sequence of nn three-dimensional waypoints that define the vehicle’s trajectory through the environment. We formulate this as a four-objective optimization task:

Total path length, to minimize energy consumption and mission time.

Composite risk metric, incorporating proximity to static obstacles, incursions into predefined no-fly zones, and violations of kinematic constraints (e.g., maximum climb/descent angles and minimum turning radii).

Path smoothness, quantified as the sum of squared heading or curvature changes between successive waypoint segments, to penalize sharp turns and ensure controllable maneuvers.

Control effort, measured via cumulative deviations in velocity and acceleration profiles required to follow the waypoint sequence, reflecting actuator workload and flight stability requirements.

Feasibility is enforced by penalizing or repairing any waypoint that falls inside forbidden volumes or imposes abrupt maneuvers beyond the UAV’s dynamic envelope. The search space is bounded by operational altitude limits and minimum spacing between waypoints to ensure continuity and flyable trajectories. By casting the problem with these four objectives, our TAMOPSO algorithm can directly balance efficiency, safety, smoothness, and control effort within a Pareto-optimal framework, producing a diverse set of nondominated solutions suited to varying mission priorities. TAMOPSO-Based UAV 3D Flight-Path Planning

In our proposed approach, each UAV flight path is encoded as a series of 3D waypoints defined by their latitude, longitude, and altitude coordinates. TAMOPSO dynamically evolves these paths using role-specific strategies within the swarm. During the exploration phase, particles assigned to exploratory roles generate diverse path candidates by sampling from adaptive velocity updates influenced by both personal bests and a global archive of nondominated solutions. The archive-guided mutation mechanism further enhances diversity by introducing Lévy-flight-based perturbations to stagnating particles, helping them escape local optima and explore narrow or complex 3D regions such as urban valleys or mountainous terrain. Once high-potential candidate paths are identified, refinement roles take over to fine-tune waypoints via local adjustments that minimize a multi-objective cost function. This function evaluates (1) total flight length, (2) altitude smoothness and turning constraints, (3) risk exposure to terrain or no-fly zones, and (4) energy efficiency. By coordinating exploration, refinement, and altitude management through task allocation and archive interaction, TAMOPSO achieves a robust balance between global search capabilities and local convergence precision, enabling efficient and collision-free UAV path planning in highly constrained 3D environments.

Interpretation and implications

The seven-peak scenario highlights the essential requirement of adaptive exploration–exploitation control for UAV path planning in undulating terrain. Algorithms with static parameter settings (e.g., standard PSO or DE) cannot adequately respond to rapidly changing elevation gradients. In contrast, TAMOPSO dynamically redistributes computational effort among exploratory and refining subgroups, ensuring continuous adaptation to the terrain’s geometric complexity.

This analysis confirms that the proposed TAMOPSO framework effectively generalizes to nonconvex 3D environments, maintaining robustness, convergence stability, and low path variance. The results validate the core hypothesis that task allocation combined with archive-guided adaptive mutation enhances both global and local search efficiency–making TAMOPSO particularly suitable for real-time, energy-aware UAV trajectory planning in complex terrains.

Conclusion

In this study, we introduced UAV-TAMOPSO, a novel and enhanced adaptation of the Task Allocation and Archive-Guided Mutation Particle Swarm Optimization framework for intelligent 3D UAV flight-path planning. By encoding trajectories as discrete waypoint sequences and formulating a bi-objective model that jointly minimizes path length and a composite risk metric–including obstacle proximity, no-fly constraints, and kinematic feasibility–we demonstrated how dynamic subpopulation partitioning into “global explorers,” “local refiners,” and “altitude managers,” together with an archive-guided adaptive Lévy-flight mutation strategy, can achieve an optimal balance between exploration and exploitation in complex aerial environments. Comprehensive simulations conducted across both simple (seven-peak) and complex (ten-peak) terrains consistently revealed TAMOPSO’s superiority over fourteen benchmark algorithms, including Improved-PSO, Adapted-RRT, Sequoia-OA, AP-GWO, GA, PSO, ACA, DE, and others. The proposed method achieved the lowest overall flight cost, fastest convergence, and smoothest trajectory profiles, while maintaining an evenly distributed Pareto front that provides mission planners with a diverse range of optimal trade-off solutions. GAPSO and Improved-PSO ranked closely behind but exhibited slightly slower convergence and higher energy consumption, whereas ACA and DE showed limited adaptability in highly irregular terrains. These findings confirm that role-based task allocation effectively directs computational effort toward the most critical sub-tasks of 3D navigation, while the archive’s uniform contribution index ensures long-term diversity and mitigates premature convergence. The MATLAB-based simulation and iterative loop testing framework validated the algorithm’s robustness, repeatability, and computational scalability for real-time mission planning. Future work will focus on integrating TAMOPSO with onboard sensors, adaptive control systems, and cooperative swarm coordination to enable fully autonomous, context-aware UAV navigation in dynamic and uncertain flight environments.

Simultaneous extension of generalized BT-inverses and core-EP inverses
Abdessalam Kara, Néstor Thome, Dragan Djordjević

Abstract
In this paper we introduce the generalized inverse of complex square matrix with respect to other matrix having same size. Some of its representations, properties and characterizations are obtained. Also some new representation matrices of W W -weighted BT-inverse and W W -weighted core-EP inverse are determined as well as characterizations of generalized inverses A $\dagger$⃝ A^{\mathrel{\text{\textcircled{$\dagger$}}}} , A $\dagger$⃝ , W ,A^{\mathrel{\text{\textcircled{$\dagger$}}},W} , A ⋄ , A ⋄ , W ,A^{\diamond},A^{\diamond,W} .



1
Introduction and preliminary

In this paper
{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times n}
stands for the set of all complex matrices of size
m\times n
. The symbols
R(A),N(A),A^{*}\textit{ and }
{\rm rk}(A)
will denote the range space, the null space, the conjugate transpose and the rank of matrix
A\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m,n}
. Let
A\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times n}
, the Moore-Penrose inverse of
A
, denoted by
A^{\dagger}
, is the unique matrix
X\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times m}
satisfying the equations

\textit{(i) }AXA=A,\textit{(ii) }XAX=X,\textit{(iii) }(AX)^{*}=AX,\textit{(iv) }(XA)^{*}=XA.

From this inverse we put
P_{A}=AA^{\dagger}\textit{ and }Q_{A}=A^{\dagger}A
. The class of matrices satisfying
(i)
is denoted by
A\{1\}
, also the class of matrices satisfying
(ii)
is denoted by
A\{2\}
. The index of a given square matrix
A
, denoted by
{\rm Ind}(A)
, is the smallest nonnegative integer
k
satisfying
{\rm rk}(A^{k+1})={\rm rk}(A^{k})
. The Drazin inverse
A^{D}
of square matrix
A\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
with
{\rm Ind}(A)=k
is defined as the unique solution of the equations

XAX=X,\textit{ }AX=XA,\textit{ }XA^{k+1}=A^{k}.

The core-EP inverse of square matrix
A\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
with
{\rm Ind}(A)=k
is given by
A^{\mathrel{\text{\textcircled{$\dagger$}}}}=A^{k}((A^{*})^{k}A^{k+1})^{\dagger}(A^{*})^{k}
(see
[
11
]
), which is the unique solution of these equations

XAX=X,\textbf{ }(AX)^{*}=AX,\textit{ }XA^{k+1}=A^{k}.

For a given rectangular matrix
W\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times n}
, the authors Ferreyra, Levis and Thome extended in
[
6
]
the notion of core-EP inverse to the
W
- weighted core-EP inverse for rectangular matrix
A\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times n}
, denoted by
A^{\mathrel{\text{\textcircled{$\dagger$}}},W}
and it is characterized as the unique solution of

WAWX=P_{(WA)^{k}}\textit{, }R(X)\subset R((AW)^{k}).

where
k=\max\{{\rm Ind}(AW),{\rm Ind}(WA)\}
.
For a square matrix
A\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
, the BT-generalized inverse of
A
is defined by
A^{\diamond}=({AP_{A}})^{\dagger}
in
[
2
]
, and extended by Ferreyra, Thome, Torigino (see
[
7
]
) to new generalized inverse for rectangular matrix
A\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times n}
, called the
W
-weighted BT-inverse of
A
where
W\in C^{m\times n}
and it is given by
A^{\diamond,W}=({WAWAW(AW)^{\dagger}})^{\dagger}
. Moreover, it is the unique solution of the system of conditions

AWX=AW{(WAWP_{AW})}^{\dagger}\textit{, }R(X)\subset R(P_{AW}(WAW)^{*}).

Theorem 2.1
.

[

]

Let
A,B\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
with
{\rm rk}(A)=r
and
{\rm rk}(B)=s
. Then there exist unitary matrices
U,V\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
and
A
,
B
can be represented as follows

A=U\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\Sigma_{A}A_{1}&\Sigma_{A}A_{2}\\
0&0\end{array}\right]V^{*}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad B=V\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\Sigma_{B}B_{1}&\Sigma_{B}B_{2}\\
0&0\end{array}\right]U^{*}

(2.1)

where
\Sigma_{A}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{r\times r}
and
\Sigma_{B}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{s\times s}

Definition 2.2
.

Let
A,B\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
. The matrix
A^{(B)}=(ABB^{\dagger})^{\dagger}
is called the generalized inverse of
A
with respect to
B
.

Remark 2.3
.

If

, we recover the BT-generalized inverse since

. Also if
B=A^{k}
with

we recover the core-EP inverse because
A^{(A^{k})}=(AA^{k}(A^{k})^{\dagger})^{\dagger}=(A^{k+1}(A^{k})^{\dagger})^{\dagger}=A^{\mathrel{\text{\textcircled{$\dagger$}}}}
.

with respect to

-decomposition of

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
A,B\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
written as in (
2.1
) with
{\rm rk}(A)=r
. Then

A^{(B)}=V\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}(\Sigma_{A}A_{1})^{\dagger}&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right]U^{*}.

(2.2)

Proof.

From (
2.1
) it follows

B^{\dagger}=U\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}B_{1}^{*}\Sigma_{B}^{-1}&0\\
B_{2}^{*}\Sigma_{B}^{-1}&0\end{array}\right]V^{*}.

Some computations yield

\displaystyle ABB^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\Sigma_{A}A_{1}&\Sigma_{A}A_{2}\\
0&0\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\Sigma_{B}B_{1}&\Sigma_{B}B_{2}\\

Then,

.
∎

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
A,B\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
. Then

a)

R(A^{(B)})=R(BB^{\dagger}A^{*})\textit{ and }N(A^{(B)})=N((AB)^{*}),

b)

A^{(B)}\in A\{2\},

c)

A^{(B)}\in A\{1\}
if and only if
{\rm rk}(AB)={\rm rk}(A)
.

Proof.

a) Clearly that
R(A^{(B)})=R(BB^{\dagger}A^{*})
. We have

N((AB)^{*})=N(B^{\dagger}A^{*})\subset N(A^{(B)})=N(BB^{\dagger}A^{*})\subset N(B^{\dagger}A^{*})=N((AB)^{*})
, so
we get
N(A^{(B)})=N((AB)^{*})
.

b) It follows from (
2.1
) and (
2.2
).

c) Since
\dim R(A^{(B)})=\dim R(ABB^{\dagger})=\dim R(AB)
so by item b) and
[
3
, pg 46, Corollary 1]
we deduce that the item c) holds.

∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
A,B\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}

a)

AA^{(B)}
is the orthogonal projector on
R(AB),

b)

A^{(B)}A
is the oblique projector onto
R(BB^{\dagger}A^{*})
along
N(B^{*}A^{*}A).

Proof.

From (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) we get

AA^{(B)}=U\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}(\Sigma_{A}A_{1})(\Sigma_{A}A_{1})^{\dagger}&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right]U^{*}.

(3.1)

A^{(B)}A=V\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}(\Sigma_{A}A_{1})^{\dagger}\Sigma_{A}A_{1}&(\Sigma_{A}A_{1})^{\dagger}\Sigma_{A}A_{2}\\
0&0\end{array}\right]V^{*}.

(3.2)

Clearly that (
3.1
) yields to
AA^{(B)}
is an orthogonal projector with

R(AA^{(B)})=R(ABB^{\dagger}(ABB^{\dagger})^{\dagger})=R(AB).

Then results the item a). From the item b) of above theorem,

is projector, moreover

Theorem 3.3

4
Some characterizations of
A^{(B)}

In this section, we present some characterizations of generalized inverse
A^{(B)}
where the matrices
A,B
are of same size.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
A,B\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
. Then the matrix
A^{(B)}
is characterized as the unique solution of the following system

XA=(ABB^{\dagger})^{\dagger}A\textit{ and }R(X^{*})\subset R(AB).

(4.1)

Proof.

We have
A^{(B)}A=(ABB^{\dagger})^{\dagger}A
and
R({A^{(B)}}^{*})=R(ABB^{\dagger})=R(AB)
,
then
A^{(B)}
satisfies (
4.1
). Now let
X
be an other solution, we get

A^{(B)}A=XA
which means that
A^{*}({A^{(B)}}^{*}-X^{*})=0
, consequently
R({A^{(B)}}^{*}-X^{*})\subset N(A^{*})\cap R(AB)\subseteq R(A)^{\bot}\cap R(A)=\{0\}

so
X=A^{(B)}
, thus
A^{(B)}
is the unique solution of (
4.1
).
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
A,B\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
. Then

A^{(B)}=B(AB)^{\dagger}

(4.2)

and
A^{(B)}
is characterized as the unique solution of the equations

XAX=X\textit{, }AX=P_{AB}\textit{, }XA=B{(AB)}^{\dagger}A

(4.3)

Proof.

It is easy to check from (
2.1
) that

(AB)^{{\dagger}}=U\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}B^{*}_{1}\Sigma^{-1}_{B}A^{{\dagger}}_{1}\Sigma^{-1}_{A}&0\\
B^{*}_{2}\Sigma^{-1}_{B}A^{{\dagger}}_{1}\Sigma^{-1}_{A}&0\end{array}\right]U^{*}

(4.4)

thus, pre-multiplying (
4.4
) by
B
we get
A^{(B)}=B(AB)^{\dagger}
and we conclude that
A^{(B)}
is solution of (
4.3
), remaining prove that
the system (
4.3
) has unique solution. We suppose
X
is other solution, we have

X=XAX=XP_{AB}=XAA^{(B)}=B{(AB)}^{\dagger}AA^{(B)}

and from
A^{(B)}=B(AB)^{\dagger}
we get
X=A^{(B)}AA^{(B)}=A^{(B)}
, then it has unique solution.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
A,B\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
. Then

A^{(B)}=A^{(2)}_{R(BB^{\dagger}A^{*}),N((AB)^{*})}

(4.5)

Proof.

We put
{\rm rk}(A)=r
, we have
\dim R(BB^{\dagger}A^{*})=\dim R(ABB^{\dagger})=\dim R(AB)\leq\dim R(A)=r
and the rank theorem for matrices give us

b) implies c) Using the equations

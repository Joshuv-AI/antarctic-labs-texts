Dynamical families of regular polynomial endomorphisms on ℙ 2 {\mathbb{P}}^{2} with many periodic curves Thanks: The author was supported in part by NSERC grant RGPIN-2022-02951.
Xiao Zhong

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove that for a regular polynomial endomorphism of positive degree on ℙ 2 {\mathbb{P}}^{2} , a family of curves containing a Zariski dense set of periodic curves is invariant under some iterate of the endomorphism. The setting is closely related to the Relative Dynamical Manin-Mumford Conjecture, recently proposed by DeMarco and Mavraki, which concerns a parametrized family of endomorphisms and varieties. Our result proves a weaker version of the conjecture where the endomorphism is a regular polynomial endomorphism on ℙ 2 {\mathbb{P}}^{2} that remains fixed in the family, and the family of curves contains a dense set of periodic curves. This result can also be viewed as a Dynamical Manin-Mumford type statement on the moduli space of divisors, and it proves a special case of the Dynamical Manin-Mumford Conjecture with a stronger assumption. Moreover, our result specifically implies a uniform degree stabilization statement for a generic set of curves in a family under the transformation of a regular polynomial endomorphism. We demonstrate that a more general degree stabilization statement for a family of positive dimension subvarieties in ℙ K {\mathbb{P}}^{K} under the transformation of a family of endomorphisms is predicted by the Relative Dynamical Manin-Mumford Conjecture. We then prove that it is true when K = 2 K=2 for families of regular polynomial endomorphisms under certain restrictions on the ramifications at the line at infinity. Finally, we demonstrate an application of our result to classify all regular polynomial endomorphisms that admit infinitely many periodic curves of bounded degree.

Introduction

The Dynamical Manin-Mumford Conjecture is the dynamical generalization of the well-known Manin-Mumford Conjecture, which conjectures that a subvariety contains many periodic points of an endomorphism must be special with respect to this endomorphism. After a series of study and refinement by
[
Zhang95
]
,
[
Zhang06
]
and
[
DT21
]
, the precise statement of the conjecture is the following:

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let

f:X\to X
be a polarized endomorphism of a smooth projective variety over a field
of characteristic zero, and
Z\subset X
be a subvariety containing a Zariski dense set of
preperiodic points. Then either
Z
is preperiodic or
Z
is special, in the sense that it is
contained in some subvariety
Y
that is both
f^{n}
-and
\psi
-invariant, for some
n\geq 1
, where

\psi
is another polarized endomorphism commuting with
f^{n}
, and
Z
is preperiodic under

\psi
.

The conjecture remains widely open, and only a few cases are known. For example, the conjecture is proven for splitting morphisms on
({\mathbb{P}}^{1})^{n}
by
[
GNY18
]
,
[
GNY19
]
and for polynomial endomorphisms on
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
extendable to an endomorphism on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
with some constraint on the ramification at the line at infinity by
[
DFR23
]
.

Recently, inspired by Gao and Habegger’s work on Relative Manin-Mumford Conjecture for families of abelian varieties
[
GH23
, Theorem 1.1 and 1.3]
, DeMarco and Mavraki proposed the generalized Dynamical Manin-Mumford Conjecture for families of dynamical system in
[
DM24a
]
. We follow
[
DM24a
]
to introduce the necessary notations. An algebraic family of endomorphisms of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
of degree
d
is a morphism

\Phi:S\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\to S\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n}

given by
\Phi(s,z)=(s,f_{s}(z))
where

is an endomorphism of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
of degree

. Let

denote a closed irreducible subvariety which is flat over a Zariski open subset of

. We use

denote the generic fiber of

and let

be the map induced by

We say

is

over the algebraic closure

containing the generic fiber

, a

•

\mathbf{\Phi}^{n}(\mathbf{Z})=\mathbf{Z}

\mathbf{\Phi}^{n}\circ\mathbf{\Psi}=\mathbf{\Psi}\circ\mathbf{\Phi}^{n}

•

We denote

is the dimension of a generic fiber of

over
S

With the notations from above, DeMarco and Mavraki proposed the following relative version of Dynamical Manin-Mumford Conjecture:

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
\Phi:S\times{\mathbb{P}}^{N}\to S\times{\mathbb{P}}^{N}

>1

•

\mathcal{X}
contains a Zariski-dense set of
\Phi
-preperiodic points.

•

\hat{T}^{r_{\Phi.\mathcal{X}}}_{\Phi}\wedge[X]\neq 0

r_{\Phi,\mathcal{X}}

]

]

In this paper, we study the

. Let’s assume

, and then
(F^{\times N})^{m+(n-m)k}(Y)\subseteq(F^{\times N})^{m}(Y)
for all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
. We denote
Z_{0}\subseteq Z
as the set of cycles corresponding to the Zariski dense set of periodic subvarities in
Z
. Then, there exists a
j\in\{1,2,\dots,n-m\}
such that

\bigcup_{z\in Z^{\prime}_{0}}\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\in(C)^{N}:[C]=z\}\subseteq(F^{\times N})^{m+j}(Y)

where
Z^{\prime}_{0}\subseteq Z_{0}
a Zariski dense subset in
Z
. But note that

\overline{\bigcup_{z\in Z^{\prime}_{0}}\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\in(C)^{N}:[C]=z\}}=Y

and, therefore,

Y=(F^{\times N})^{m+j}(Y)

which implies that
Y
is periodic under
F^{\times N}
.

We abuse notation to let
F
be
F^{m+j}
. Note that

Y=\bigcup_{z\in Z}\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\in C^{N}:[C]=z\}

since

\bigcup_{z\in Z}\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\in C^{N}:[C]=z\}=\pi(\mathcal{Z})

where
\mathcal{Z}
is a closed variety defined by

\mathcal{Z}\coloneqq\{(z,x_{1},\cdots,x_{N}):z\in Z,(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\in C^{N},[C]=z\}\subseteq Z\times({\mathbb{P}}^{K})^{N},

and
\pi
is the projection from
Z\times({\mathbb{P}}^{K})^{N}
to
({\mathbb{P}}^{K})^{N}
which is a closed map.

For any
C\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}^{K}
such that
[C]\in Z
, we claim that

(F(C))^{N}\subseteq\bigcup_{z\in Z}\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\in C^{\prime N}:[C^{\prime}]=z\}

implies
[F(C)]\in Z
.

We prove the claim by induction on
N\geq 1
. The base case is that

N=1.

In this case
\dim(Z)=0
and
Y
is a finite union of subvarieties of
{\mathbb{P}}^{K}
parametrized by
Z
. Therefore, we obviously have
F(C)\subseteq Y
implies

[F(C)]\in Z.

Now, suppose the statement is true for
N\leq M
for a positive integer

M\geq 1

and we will prove that it also holds for
N=M+1
. Note that there exists a point
p\in F(C)
such that the subvariety
Z_{1}\subseteq Z
, consisting of all cycles in
Z
with support containing
p
, has dimension strictly smaller than
N-1
. Otherwise, every
C^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}^{K}
satisfying that
[C^{\prime}]\in Z
will contain
F(C)
which means that
\dim(Z)=0
because
Z
contains a set of irreducible subvarieties as a Zariski dense subset.

Now, we have

\{p\}\times(F(C))^{N-1}\subseteq\bigcup_{z\in Z_{1}}\{(p,x_{2},\dots,x_{N}):(x_{2},\dots,x_{N})\in C^{\prime N-1},[C^{\prime}]=z\},

which implies

(F(C))^{N-1}\subseteq\bigcup_{z\in Z_{1}}\{(x_{2},\dots,x_{N}):(x_{2},\dots,x_{N})\in C^{\prime N-1},[C^{\prime}]=z\}.

By the induction hypothesis, we conclude that
[F(C)]\in Z
and conclude the proof of the claim.

Since we have
(F(C))^{N}\subseteq Y
, we can use the claim to conclude that

[F(C)]\in Z.

Let
{\mathbb{C}}(Z)
denote the function field of
Z
. There exists a subvariety
\tilde{Z}\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}^{K}_{\overline{{\mathbb{C}}(Z)}}
such that for any
z\in Z
,
[\tilde{Z}_{z}]=z\in Z
. Now, we denote

\tilde{F}:{\mathbb{P}}^{K}_{\overline{{\mathbb{C}}(Z)}}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{K}_{\overline{{\mathbb{C}}(Z)}}

as the induced map by
F
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{K}_{\overline{{\mathbb{C}}(Z)}}
through base change. The above showed that

[\tilde{F}(\tilde{Z)}_{z}]=[F(\tilde{Z}_{z})]\in Z,

for all
z\in Z
.
In particular, this implies that

\deg(\tilde{F}(\tilde{Z}))=\deg(\tilde{Z})

and so for a generic
C^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}^{K}
such that
[C^{\prime}]\in Z
, we have

\deg(F(C^{\prime}))=\deg(C^{\prime}).

∎

4.
Implication Towards the Conjectures
1.2

In this section, we look at how our main result is related to the Conjectures proposed in
[
DM24a
]
. When restricted to the situation that
\Phi:S\times{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\to S\times{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
doesn’t vary with respect to
S
, we show that our main result proves a weaker version of the Conjecture
1.2
, which is Corollary
1.5
. We will also demonstrate how a stronger expectation of the degree stabilization phenomenon is expected from Conjecture
1.2
.

For the convenience of the reader, we repeat the Corollary
1.5
here:

Corollary 4.1

(Corollary
1.5
)
.

Let
S
be a smooth and irreducible quasi-projective variety defined over
{\mathbb{C}}
. Let
\Phi:S\times{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\to S\times{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
be a constant family of endomorphisms such that for every point
(s,p)\in S\times{\mathbb{P}}^{2}

\Phi(s,p)=(s,F(p))

for a regular polynomial endomorphism
F
of degree
>1
does not depend on
s
. Let
\mathcal{X}\subseteq S\times{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
be an irreducible hypersurface that is flat over
S
and projects dominantly to
S
. Suppose there exists a Zariski dense set of
s\in S
such that
\mathcal{X}_{s}
, the fiber of
\mathcal{X}
at
s
, is a periodic curve under
F
. Then for all
N\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
, we have

\hat{T}_{\Phi^{\times N}}^{\wedge r_{\Phi^{\times N},\mathcal{X}^{N}}}\wedge[\mathcal{X}^{N}]\neq 0,

and
r_{\Phi^{\times N},\mathcal{X}^{N}}\leq\min\{2N,N+\dim S\}
.

Proof.

First, we suppose
N\geq\dim S
. There exists an irreducible subvariety
Z\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{d}
, which is the space of effective divisors of degree at most
d>0
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, such that the induced map
\sigma:S\to Z
is dominant. When there is no confusion, we will also view
\mathcal{M}_{d}
as a space of curves (which may be reducible), and similarly for
Z\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{d}
. By the assumption, we have that there is a Zariski dense set of
z\in Z
such that
z
is a periodic curve under
F
. Then Theorem
1.3
implies that, after replacing
F
with some iterate,
F(z)\in Z
for all
z\in Z
.

Consider the subvariety

Y\coloneqq\overline{\{(p_{1},\dots,p_{N})\in({\mathbb{P}}^{2})^{N}:p_{1},\dots,p_{N}\in\mathcal{X}_{s}\text{ for some }s\in S\}}

which is the image of the projection of fiber product
\mathcal{X}^{N}
onto
({\mathbb{P}}^{2})^{N}
and is of dimension

r\leq\min\{2N,\dim\mathcal{X}^{N}\}\leq\min\{2N,N+\dim S\}

in
({\mathbb{P}}^{2})^{N}
. We have
Y
is invariant under
F^{\times N}
since for every point

(p_{1},\dots,p_{N})\in(X_{s})^{N}\subseteq({\mathbb{P}}^{2})^{N},

we have

(F(p_{1}),\dots,F(p_{N}))\in F(X_{s})\times\dots\times F(X_{s})=F(\sigma(s))\times\dots\times F(\sigma(s))=z\times z\dots\times z

for some
z\in Z
. Since
\sigma:S\to Z
is dominant and
Y
is Zariski closed, we have
z\times z\times\dots\times z\subseteq Y
. Therefore,
F^{\times N}(Y)\subseteq Y
.

Now, we apply the projection formula with
\pi_{N}:S\times({\mathbb{P}}^{2})^{N}\to({\mathbb{P}}^{2})^{N}
,

\hat{T}_{\Phi^{\times N}}^{\wedge r}\wedge[\mathcal{X}^{N}]=\pi^{*}_{N}\hat{T}^{\wedge r}_{F^{\times N}}\wedge[\mathcal{X}^{N}]=\hat{T}^{\wedge r}_{F^{\times N}}\wedge(\pi_{N})_{*}[\mathcal{X}^{N}]

\geq\hat{T}^{\wedge r}_{F^{\times N}}\wedge[Y]>0.

Notice that by definition,
r_{\Phi^{N},\mathcal{X}^{N}}\leq r
since
S\times Y
is
\Phi^{\times N}
-special. Thus

\hat{T}^{r_{\Phi^{\times N},\mathcal{X}^{N}}}_{\Phi^{\times N}}\wedge[\mathcal{X}^{N}]\neq 0.

∎

Remark 4.2
.

The proof above actually indicates that we proved a Dynamical Manin-Mumford Conjecture for
F^{\times N}
and
Y\subseteq({\mathbb{P}}^{2})^{N}
. But with the stronger assumption that
Y
is constructed from a family of curves that contains a Zariski dense set of periodic curves rather than merely
Y
contains a Zariski dense set of preperiodic points.

Corollary 4.3
.

Under the same assumptions of Corollary
1.5
, for any positive integer
N>0
, we have
\mathcal{X}^{N}
has codimension
\leq\dim S
in a
\Phi^{\times N}
-special subvariety in
S\times({\mathbb{P}}^{2})^{N}
.

Proof.

This follows straightforwardly followed from Corollary
1.5
, similar to the explanation in
[
DM24a
]
about how
[
DM24a
, Conjecture 1.1]
implies
[
DM24a
, Conjecture 1.2]
. Since we have
r_{\Phi^{\times N},\mathcal{X}^{N}}\leq\min\{2N,N+\dim S\}
and

\dim\mathcal{X}^{N}=N+\dim S,

we directly have that there exists a
\Phi^{\times N}
-special subvariety
Y\subseteq S\times({\mathbb{P}}^{2})^{N}
of dimension less or equal to
\min\{2N+\dim S,N+2\dim S\}
containing
\mathcal{X}^{N}
and thus
\mathcal{X}^{N}
is of codimension
\leq\dim S
in it.
∎

The Corollary
1.5
proved a weaker version of the following Conjecture which is a corollary of Conjecture
1.2
.

Conjecture 4.4
.

Let
S
be a smooth and irreducible quasi-projective variety defined over
{\mathbb{C}}
, and let
K
be a positive integer. Let
\Phi:S\times{\mathbb{P}}^{K}\to S\times{\mathbb{P}}^{K}
be a family of endomorphism such that for every point
(s,p)\in S\times{\mathbb{P}}^{K}

\Phi(s,p)=(s,F_{s}(p)),

for some endomorphism
F_{s}
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{K}
of degree greater than
1
. Let
\mathcal{X}\subseteq S\times{\mathbb{P}}^{K}
be an irreducible subvariety in
S\times{\mathbb{P}}^{K}
that projects dominantly to
S
, is flat over
S
and
\mathcal{X}_{s}
is a preperiodic subvariety under
F_{s}
for a Zariski dense set of
s\in S
. Then for any positive integer

, we have
\hat{T}^{r_{\Phi^{\times N},\mathcal{X}^{N}}}_{\Phi^{\times N}}\wedge[\mathcal{X}^{N}]\neq 0
.

Implication by Conjecture
1.2
.

Notice that we can view
\mathcal{X}^{N}
as a subvariety in
S\times{\mathbb{P}}^{M}
for some positive integer
M
by doing the Serge embedding and then
\Phi^{N}
becomes an endomorphism on
S\times{\mathbb{P}}^{M}
. Let
\iota:S\times({\mathbb{P}}^{K})^{N}\to S\times{\mathbb{P}}^{M}
denote this embedding map and let
\psi:S\times{\mathbb{P}}^{M}\to S\times{\mathbb{P}}^{M}
denote the extented endomorphism obtained from
\Phi^{\times N}
and
Y=\iota(\mathcal{X}^{N})
. Then

\hat{T}^{r_{\psi,Y}}_{\Psi}\wedge[Y]\neq 0

implies

\hat{T}^{r_{\Psi,Y}}_{\Phi^{\times N},\mathcal{X}^{N}}\wedge[\mathcal{X}^{N}]\neq 0.

Notice that
S\times\iota(({\mathbb{P}}^{K})^{N})\subseteq S\times{\mathbb{P}}^{M}
is obviously
\Psi
-special and containing
Y
and so

.

Now, the assumption that for a Zariski dense set of
s\in S
,
\mathcal{X}_{s}
is preperiodic under

implies that
\Prep(\Phi)\cap\mathcal{X}_{s}
is dense in
\mathcal{X}_{s}
for those
s
. Moreover, for
s
in the same Zariski dense subset of

, we have
(\mathcal{X}^{N})_{s}
contains a Zariski dense set of
\Prep(\Phi^{\times N}_{s})
and hence
\Prep(\Phi^{\times N})\cap\mathcal{X}^{N}
is dense in
\mathcal{X}^{N}
. Therefore,
\iota(\Prep(\Phi^{\times N})\cap\mathcal{X}^{N})
is dense in

and Conjecture

will directly imply the statement.

∎

Now, let’s show that this Conjecture implies the degree stabilization phenomenon in a more general setting allowing a family of endomorphisms.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
S
be a smooth and irreducible quasi-projective variety defined over
{\mathbb{C}}
of positive dimension and
M
be a positive integer. Let
\Phi:S\times{\mathbb{P}}^{M}\to S\times{\mathbb{P}}^{M}
be a family of endomorphisms such that for every point
(s,p)\in S\times{\mathbb{P}}^{M}

\Phi(s,p)=(s,F_{s}(p))

for an endomorphism
F_{s}
of degree
>1
. Suppose
\Phi
is generic enough so that the only set of positive-degree endomorphisms whose induced endomorphisms on the generic fiber of
S\times{\mathbb{P}}^{M}
commute with the induced map by
\Phi
is the set of iterates of
\Phi
. Let
\mathcal{X}\subseteq S\times{\mathbb{P}}^{M}
be an irreducible subvariety of dimension
>\dim S
that projects dominantly to
S
and is flat over
S
.

If there exists a positive integer
N
and a proper irreducible
\Phi^{\times N}
-special subvariety
\mathcal{Y}\subseteq S\times({\mathbb{P}}^{M})^{N}
containing
(\Phi^{\times N})^{l}(\mathcal{X}^{N})
with some non-negative integer
l
as a codimension
\leq\min\{N-1,\dim S\}
subvareity, then there exists positive integers
n,m
and an infinite set of positive integers
I
such that for any pair of integers
k_{1},k_{2}\in I
we have that for a generic point
s\in S
,

\deg(F^{m+nk_{1}}_{s}(\mathcal{X}_{s}))=\deg(F^{m+nk_{2}}_{s}(\mathcal{X}_{s})).

Proof.

By our assumption on
\Phi
, we know that
\mathcal{Y}
is
\Phi^{\times N}
-special implies that there exists positive integers
n,m
such that
(\Phi^{\times N})^{m}(\mathcal{Y})=(\Phi^{\times N})^{m+nk}(\mathcal{Y})
for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Let’s prove it inductively on
N
. The base case is
N=1
. In this case we have
\Phi^{l}(\mathcal{X})
itself is
\Phi
-special and, by our assumption on
\Phi
, this implies
\mathcal{X}
is preperiodic under
\Phi
. Therefore, for every
s\in S
, we have

F_{s}^{m}(\mathcal{X}_{s})=F_{s}^{m+nk}(\mathcal{X}_{s})

and in particular

\deg(F^{m}_{s}(\mathcal{X}_{s}))=\deg(F^{m+nk}_{s}(\mathcal{X}_{s})),

for all positive integer
k
.

Now, suppose
N\geq 2
and assume that we have the statement proved for any
N^{\prime}\leq N-1
. If the projection of
\mathcal{Y}
to
S\times({\mathbb{P}}^{M})^{N-1}
, denote as
\pi(\mathcal{Y})
, contains
\mathcal{X}^{N-1}
as a codimension
\leq\min\{N-2,\dim S\}
subvariety, then we apply the induction hypothesis to conclude the proof.

Otherwise, we will have

\dim(\mathcal{X}^{N-1})+\min\{N-1,\dim S\}\geq\dim(\pi(\mathcal{Y}))>\dim(\mathcal{X}^{N-1})+\min\{N-2,\dim S\},

since
\dim(\mathcal{Y})\leq\dim(\mathcal{X}^{N})+\min\{N-1,\dim S\}
. By
[
Har77
, Chapter III, Theorem 9.9 and Corollary 9.10]
, there exists a non-empty Zariski open subset
U
in
\pi(\mathcal{Y})
such that

\deg(\mathcal{Y}_{p})

is constant with
p
varies in
U
, where
\mathcal{Y}_{p}
is the fiber of
\mathcal{Y}
over
p
.

Suppose there exists positive integers
k_{0},k_{1}
such that for every
k>k_{0}
, we have that
(\Phi^{\times(N-1)})^{m+nk_{1}k}(\mathcal{X}^{N-1})\subseteq U^{c}
. Then there exists a closed subset

V\coloneqq\overline{\bigcup_{k\geq k_{0}}(\Phi^{\times(N-1)})^{m+nk_{1}k}(\mathcal{X}^{N-1})}\subseteq U^{c}

which is
\Phi^{\times(N-1)}
preperiodic and contains
(\Phi^{\times(N-1)})^{l^{\prime}}(\mathcal{X}^{N-1})

for some positive integer
l^{\prime}
. Then the induction hypothesis will conclude the proof as
\dim(V)<\dim(\pi(\mathcal{Y}))
.

Suppose such a pair of integers does not exist. This in particular implies that there is an infinite set of positive integers
I
such that for any
j\in I

(\Phi^{\times(N-1)})^{m+nj}(\mathcal{X}^{N-1})
is not contained in
U^{c}
. Then for any pair of integers
k_{3},k_{4}\in I
, we have

(\Phi^{\times(N-1)})^{m+nk_{i}}(\mathcal{X}^{N-1})

is not contained in
U^{c}
, for
i\in\{3,4\}
. Since
U\cap(\Phi^{\times(N-1)})^{m+nk_{i}}(\mathcal{X}^{N-1})
is a non-empty open subset in
(\Phi^{\times(N-1)})^{m+nk_{i}}(\mathcal{X}^{N-1})
for
i\in\{3,4\}
, this implies that for a generic
s_{0}\in S
, there exists

p_{k_{i}}\in U\cap(\Phi^{\times(N-1)})^{m+nk_{i}}(\mathcal{X}^{N-1})

such that
\pi_{0}(p_{k_{i}})=s_{0}\in S
and

F_{s_{0}}^{m+nk_{i}}(\mathcal{X}_{s_{0}})\subseteq\mathcal{Y}_{p_{k_{i}}},

with
i\in\{3,4\}
. Therefore, since
\dim(F_{s_{0}}^{m+nk_{i}}(\mathcal{X}_{s_{0}}))=\dim(\mathcal{Y}_{p_{k_{i}}})
, for
i\in\{3,4\}
,
\deg(\mathcal{Y}_{p_{k_{3}}})=\deg(\mathcal{Y}_{p_{k_{4}}})
, we have

\deg(\mathcal{Y}_{p_{k_{3}}})\geq\deg(F_{s_{0}}^{m+nk_{3}}(\mathcal{X}_{s_{0}})),

\deg(\mathcal{Y}_{p_{k_{4}}})\geq\deg(F_{s_{0}}^{m+nk_{4}}(\mathcal{X}_{s_{0}})).

This implies that for all pair of
k_{3},k_{4}\in I
, we have

\deg(F_{s_{0}}^{m+nk_{3}}(\mathcal{X}_{s_{0}})),\deg(F_{s_{0}}^{m+nk_{4}}(\mathcal{X}_{s_{0}}))\leq R

for a generic
s_{0}\in S
and a positive integer
R
only depending on
U
and
\mathcal{Y}
. Then, by the pigeonhole princple, there exists an infinite subset
I^{\prime}\subseteq I
such that for all pairs of
k_{3},k_{4}\in I^{\prime}
, we have

\deg(F^{m+nk_{3}}_{s_{0}}(\mathcal{X}_{s_{0}}))=\deg(F^{m+nk_{4}}_{s_{0}}(\mathcal{X}_{s_{0}}))\leq R.

∎

Proposition 4.6

(Proposition
1.9
)
.

Under the same assumption of Proposition
4.5
and suppose that for a Zariski dense subset of

\deg(F^{m+nk_{1}}_{s}(\mathcal{X}_{s}))=\deg(F^{m+nk_{2}}_{s}(\mathcal{X}_{s})).

Proof.

Note that
\hat{T}^{r_{\Phi^{\times N},\mathcal{X}^{N}}}_{\Phi^{\times N}}\wedge[\mathcal{X}^{N}]\neq 0

Then Proposition

n,m
and an infinite set of positive integers
I
such that for any pair of integers

we have that for a generic point

,

\deg(F^{m+nk_{1}}_{s}(\mathcal{X}_{s}))=\deg(F^{m+nk_{2}}_{s}(\mathcal{X}_{s})).

∎

Suppose
S={\mathbb{P}}^{1}
,
K=2
and we view
\Phi:{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
as
F_{t}:{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\overline{{\mathbb{C}}(t)}}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\overline{{\mathbb{C}}(t)}}
parametrized by

varies in

, then the similar technique that we used in Section

can help us prove the degree stabilization statement in the case when the
(F_{t}|_{H_{\infty}},a_{i}(t))
, for each

, is not sharing the forward orbit with a marked critical point.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
F_{t}
be a family of regular polynomial endomorphisms on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
of degree
>1
where
t
varies in
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
and
\mathcal{X}_{t}
is a family of curves in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
. Suppose there are infinitely many
t_{0}\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
such that
\mathcal{X}_{t_{0}}
is periodic under
F_{t_{0}}
and
f_{t}\coloneqq F_{t}|_{H_{\infty}}
satisfies

(1)

f_{t}
is a polynomial of degree
>1
over
\overline{{\mathbb{C}}(t)}
;

(2)

for any
a_{i}(t)\in\mathcal{X}_{t}\cap H_{\infty}
, there doesn’t exists a natural numbers
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and a polynomial
h_{t}
sharing the same Julia set with
f_{t}
such that
f_{t}^{n}(a_{i}(t))=h_{t}(c(t))
, where
c(t)
is a marked critical point of

; i.e.,
(h_{t})^{\prime}(c(t))\equiv 0
.

Then, for a generic set of

, we have
\deg(\mathcal{X}_{t_{0}})=\deg(F^{k}_{t_{0}}(\mathcal{X}_{t_{0}}))
for all positive integers
k
.

Proof.

We will show that with the above two assumptions, for any
a(t)\in\mathcal{X}_{t}\cap H_{\infty}
, there are only finitely many
t_{1}\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
such that
a(t_{1})
lies in a superattracting periodic cycle of
f_{t_{1}}
. Suppose on the contrary, there are infinitely many
t_{1}\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
and an
a(t)\in\mathcal{X}_{t}\cap H_{\infty}
, such that
a(t_{1})
is a superattracting periodic points under
f_{t_{1}}
. Since
f_{t}
is of bounded degree, there is a finite set of marked critical points of
f_{t}
. Thus, by the pigeonhole principle, there exists a marked critical point,
c(t)
, of
f_{t}
such that

\{t_{1}\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1}:c(t_{1})\in\Per(f_{t_{1}}),a(t_{1})=f^{n_{t_{1}}}(c(t_{1})),n_{t_{1}}\in{\mathbb{N}}\}

is an infinite set.

Suppose
f_{t}
is conjugated to a power map or Chebyshev polynomial, then there exists
l_{t}\in\overline{{\mathbb{C}}(t)}[x]
such that
l_{t}\circ S_{d}\circ l_{t}^{-1}=f_{t}
, where
S_{d}
is either a power map of degree
d=\deg(f_{t})
or a Chebyshev polynomial of degree
d
. Then we have the marked critical points is given by

\{l_{t}(c_{i}):c_{i}\text{ from the critical sets of }S_{d}\}.

Therefore, our condition implies that there are infinitely many
t_{1}
such that

a(t_{1})=f_{t_{1}}^{n_{t_{1}}}(c(t_{1}))=l_{t_{1}}\circ S^{n_{t_{1}}}_{d}\circ l_{t_{1}}^{-1}(l_{t_{1}}(c))=l_{t_{1}}\circ S^{n_{t_{1}}}_{d}(c),

where
c
is a critical point of
S_{d}
. Since all the critical points of

5.
Classification of Regular Polynomial Endomorphisms With Infinitely Many Periodic Curves of Bounded Degree

In this section, let
F(x,y)
denote a regular polynomial endomorphism of degree
>1
. We will apply Theorem
1.3
to obtain a classification of regular polynomial endomorphisms that admit infinitely many periodic curves with bounded degrees.

We adopt the
k
-webs notion from
[
FP15
]
and for the convenience of the reader we explain what is a
k
-web following their discussion.

A
k
-web
\mathcal{W}
on a smooth complex surface
X
is determined by a section
\omega\in H^{0}(X,\Sym^{k}\Omega^{1}_{X}\otimes\mathcal{NW})
for some line bundle
\mathcal{NW}
on
X
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

the zero set of
\omega
is either codimension
2
or empty;

(2)

for any point
p
outside a proper hypersurface in
X
, after trivializing the line bundle
\mathcal{NW}
, the
k
-symmetric
1
-form
\omega(p)\in\Sym^{k}\Omega^{1}_{X,p}
can be written as the product of
k
pairwise distinct linear forms.

Then two sections
\omega^{\prime}
and
\omega
give the same
k
-web if and only if they differ by a global nowhere vanishing holomorphic function.

A
k
-web
\mathcal{W}
on a surface
X
is roughly a multi-foliation with
k
distinct local leaves through a generic point of
X
. At a generic point of
X
, the web
\mathcal{W}
is the superposition of
k
pairwise transverse foliations
\mathcal{F}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{F}_{k}
, and one may write
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{F}_{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\mathcal{F}_{k}
.
We say an endomorphism
F
on
X
preserves a
k
-web
\mathcal{W}
if it maps leaves of
\mathcal{W}
to leaves.

Now, we will show that if
F
admits infinitely many periodic curves, then either there exists a
k
-web, with
k>1
, preserved by
F
or there exists a pencil of curves preserved by
F
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose
F
admits an infinite set of irreducible periodic curves of degree bounded by
D\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
. Then either
F
preserves a
k
-web with
k>1
or
F
preserves a pencil of curves.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
F
admits an infinite set of irreducible periodic curves of degree
D
. Then Proposition
3.4
implies that there exists an irreducible subvariety

parametrized by
\tau:{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\to Z
, where
Z
is the closure of an infinite subset of irreducible periodic curves of degree
D
in
\mathcal{M}_{D}
. Moreover, for any curve
C
such that
[C]\in Z
we have
[F(C)]\in Z
. We abuse notation here to also view
\mathcal{M}_{D}
as the space of curves (may not be irreducible) of degree at most
D
.

Suppose there exists a point
p\in{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
such that infinitely many curves in
Z
passing through
p
, then we automatically see that
F
preserves a pencil of curves parametrized by
\tau:{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\to Z
from Lemma
3.2
. Thus, from now on, we assume that for any point

passing through
p

k

Z
that is preserved by
F
.

Suppose

does not give a pencil of curves. We first show that the set

\mathcal{S}\coloneqq\{p\in{\mathbb{P}}^{2}:p\in C_{1}\cap C_{2},C_{1}\neq C_{2},C_{1},C_{2}\in Z\}

{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
.

Consider the space

W=\overline{\{(C_{1},C_{2},x):C_{1},C_{2}\in Z,C_{1}\neq C_{2},x\in C_{1}\cap C_{2}\}}\subseteq Z\times Z\times{\mathbb{P}}^{2}.

Notice that by our assumption that for every

Now we construct a

For a

We will construct from

with some line bundle

.

We will first show that the zero set of
\omega

2

2

is infinite. Then they intersect at infinitely many periodic points of

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
F
be a regular polynomial endomorphism on
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
of degree
>1
. Suppose
F
admits infinitely many periodic lines passing through a point
p\in{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\setminus{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
. Then there exists an
n
such that
F^{n}
is a regular polynomial skew product.

Proof.

Fixing a proper coordinate on
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
, we can assume that the infinitely many periodic lines are given by

\{L_{k}=V(x-k)\}_{k\in S},

where
S
is an infinite subset of
{\mathbb{C}}
. Let
h\in{\mathbb{N}}
denote the minimum period of periodic curves
L_{k}
when
k
ranges in
S
. Let
L_{l}
,
l\in S
, be the periodic curves of
F
with period
h
. Now, for any
L_{k}
,
k\in S
, denoting
n_{k}
as its period, we show that
F^{n_{k}-h}(L_{k})
is still a vertical line in
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
. Since otherwise,

F^{n_{k}-h}(L_{k})\cap L_{l}\cap{\mathbb{A}}^{2}\neq\emptyset,

which imples

L_{k}\cap F^{h}(L_{l})\cap{\mathbb{A}}^{2}=L_{k}\cap L_{l}\cap{\mathbb{A}}^{2}\neq\emptyset,

which is impossible. Therefore,
F^{n_{k}-h}(L_{k})=V(x-k_{h})
, where
k_{h}\in{\mathbb{C}}
, is a periodic vertical line for every
k\in S
. Then, denote
F^{h}(x,y)=(f(x,y),g(x,y))
and we have that

F^{h}(V(x-k_{h}))=V(x-k)

for infinitely pair of
(k,k_{h})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
, and so

f(k_{h},y)=k

for all

. This implies that

is a polynomial in the variable

and not depending on

. Therefore,

F^{h}(x,y)=(f(x),g(x,y))

and so

is a regular polynomial skew product.
∎

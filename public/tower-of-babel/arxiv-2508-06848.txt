Roe functors preserve homotopies
Georgii S. Makeev

Abstract
. We show that coarse maps between countable metric spaces of bounded geometry induce natural transformations of sufficiently good endofunctors of C ∗ C^{*} -algebras and prove that this correspondence is invariant with respect to coarse homotopies.



1.
Introduction

Many constructions involving homotopies of asymptotic homomorphisms
in the
E
-theory of Connes and Higson
[
1
,
2
]

can be formulated at the level of endofunctors of
C^{*}
-algebras.
The category
\mathtt{hGEFC}
introduced in
[
6
]
with
objects sufficiently good endofunctors of
C^{*}
-algebras happens
to be an appropriate category to describe this behavior.

Objects of the category
\mathtt{hGEFC}
give generalized notions of homotopy
of
*
-homomorphisms. These homotopy relations can be used for constructing
an unsuspended picture of
E
-theory
[
6
]
.
They also give an
E
-theoretical analog of the extension groups
of
C^{*}
-algebras and can be used for computing
K
-homology.

In this work we study the relationship between discrete metric spaces
of bounded geometry with bounded coarse structure and Roe algebra
functors. We show that coarse maps induce morphisms in
\mathtt{hGEFC}
,
and this correspondence is invariant with respect to coarse homotopies.

2.
Roe algebras

Definition 2.1
.

A discrete metric space
X
has
bounded geometry

[
9
]

if for every
R>0
all the
R
-balls have uniformly bounded cardinalities,
i.e.

\sup_{x\in X}\left|B_{R}(x)\right|<\infty.

We shall consider formal
X\textrm{-by-}X
-matrices with entries
in a
C^{*}
-algebra, and write
\operatorname*{mat}_{x,y}b_{x,y}
for the matrix
with elements
\{b_{x,y}\}_{x,y\in X}
. For such formal matrices
we can define the notion of
propagation
:

\operatorname{prop}\left(\operatorname*{mat}_{x,y}b_{x,y}\right)\coloneqq\sup\left\{\operatorname{dist}(x,y)\>\Big|\>x,y\in X,\>b_{x,y}\neq 0\right\}.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that propagation satisfies the following
property:

\operatorname{prop}(m_{1}m_{2})\leq\operatorname{prop}(m_{1})\operatorname{prop}(m_{2}).

Let
\mathcal{L}_{B}(l_{2}(X)\otimes B)
be the
C^{*}
-algebra
of adjointable operators
[
4
]
on the Hilbert
B
-module

l_{2}(X)\otimes B
, and let

L(B,X)\coloneqq\left\{m=\operatorname*{mat}_{x,y}b_{x,y}\>\Big|\;b_{x,y}\in B,\;\operatorname{prop}(m)<\infty,\;\sup_{x,y\in X}\left\|b_{x,y}\right\|<\infty\right\}.

The two following lemmas are quite simple and can be proved by arguments
similar to those given in
[
5
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
X
be a discrete metric space of bounded
geometry, and let
m=\operatorname*{mat}_{x,y\in X}b_{x,y}\in L(B,X)
. Then
m\in\mathcal{L}_{B}(l_{2}(X)\otimes B)

with respect to the standard basis of
l_{2}(X)\otimes B
. Moreover,
there is
N>0
, which depends only on
X
and
\operatorname{prop}(m)
, such
that the following inequality holds:

\left\|m\right\|\leq N\sup_{x,y\in X}\left\|b_{x,y}\right\|.

Definition 2.4
.

We denote the norm closure of
L(B,X)
in
\mathcal{L}_{B}(l_{2}(X)\otimes B)

by
\mathfrak{M}_{X}^{u}B
, and call it the
uniform Roe algebra

of
X
with coefficients in
B
.

Lemma 2.5
.

\mathfrak{M}_{X}^{u}
is an endofunctor of the category of
C^{*}
-algebras
and
*
-homomorphisms.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be the functor of tensoring with the
C^{*}
-algebra
of compact operators in a separable Hilbert space. We call the composition

\mathfrak{M}_{X}^{u}\mathbb{K}
the
Roe algebra functor
of X,
and denote it by
\mathfrak{M}_{X}
.

3.
Homotopies of natural transformations

Denote by
\mathtt{C^{*}}
the category of
C^{*}
-algebras and
*
-homomorphisms,
and by
\mathtt{EFC}
the category of endofunctors of
\mathtt{C^{*}}
and natural
transformations between them.

Let
\alpha\colon F\Rightarrow G
and
\beta\colon G\Rightarrow H

be two natural transformations. We denote their vertical composition
by
\beta\circ\alpha\colon F\Rightarrow H
. The horizontal composition
of
\alpha\colon F_{1}\Rightarrow F_{2}
and
\beta\colon G_{1}\Rightarrow G_{2}

will be denoted by
\alpha\beta\colon F_{1}G_{1}\Rightarrow F_{2}G_{2}
.

We write
\alpha B\colon FB\to GB
for the component of
\alpha\colon F\Rightarrow G

at object
B
. The symbol
\mathit{Id}
will denote the identity functor.
The expressions
c\in\in C
and
f\in C
will indicate respectively
that
c
is an object and
f
is an arrow of the category
C
.

Definition 3.1
.

We call
G\in\in\mathtt{EFC}
a
good endofunctor

if the following properties hold:

(1)

G
preserves epimorphisms;

(2)

Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\colon A\to B
be two

*
-homomorphisms, with
\varphi_{1}
being an epimorphism; and
let

\textstyle{B_{1}\oplus_{B}B_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p_{1}}
\scriptstyle{p_{2}}
\textstyle{B_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi_{1}}
\textstyle{B_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi_{2}}
\textstyle{B}

be the pullback diagram in
\mathtt{C^{*}}
, where
p_{1}
,
p_{2}
are
projections onto the corresponding components. Then the
*
-homomorphism

G\left(B_{1}\underset{B}{\oplus}B_{2}\right)\to GB_{1}\underset{GB}{\oplus}GB_{2}

induced by
Gp_{1}
and
Gp_{2}
is an isomorphism;

(3)

There is a natural transformation
IG\Rightarrow GI

making the following diagrams commute in
\mathtt{EFC}
:

\textstyle{IG\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\operatorname{ev}_{j}G}
\textstyle{GI\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{G\operatorname{ev}_{j}}
\textstyle{G,}
\textstyle{j=0,1,}

where
I
is the functor of tensoring with
C[0,1]
, and where
\operatorname{ev}_{t}\colon I\Rightarrow\operatorname{Id}

is the evaluation at
t\in[0,1]
.

It is not difficult to prove that
I
,
\mathbb{K}
and
\mathfrak{M}_{X}^{u}

defined above are good endofunctors. Another important example which
we shall not, however, need further, but which provided motivation
for Definition
3.1
, is the asymptotic algebra
functor
\mathfrak{A}
from
[
2
, Definition 1.1]
.

Good endofunctors and natural transformations form a category, which
we denote by
\mathtt{GEFC}
. It is easily verified that the composition
of two good endofunctors is again a good endofunctor. Hence, similarly
to
\mathtt{EFC}
, we can regard
\mathtt{GEFC}
as a
2
-category
[
7
]
.
Now we are ready to introduce homotopies of natural transformations
exposed in
[
6
]
.

Definition 3.2
.

We call natural transformations
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}\in\mathtt{GEFC}(F,G)

homotopic
(written
\gamma_{0}\simeq\gamma_{1}
) if there
is a natural transformation
\gamma\colon F\Rightarrow GI
such that
the following diagrams commute:

\textstyle{F\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\gamma_{j}}
\scriptstyle{\gamma}
\textstyle{GI\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{G\operatorname{ev}_{j}}
\textstyle{G,}
\textstyle{j=0,1.}

One can check
[
6
]
that homotopy is an equivalence
relation, and that the vertical and horizontal compositions respect
homotopy. Hence, good endofunctors and homotopy classes of natural
transformations between them form a category, which we denote by
\mathtt{hGEFC}
.

Remark 3.3
.

As the horizontal composition in
\mathtt{hGEFC}
is well defined, we can
also regard
\mathtt{hGEFC}
as a
2
-category.

Consider the two natural transformations: a corner embedding
\iota\colon\operatorname{Id}\Rightarrow\mathbb{K}

and a stability isomorphism
\theta\colon\mathbb{K}^{2}\Rightarrow\mathbb{K}
.
The proof of the following lemma is a standard argument, which can
be found for instance in
[
3
]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

The diagram

\textstyle{\mathbb{K}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota\mathbb{K}}
\textstyle{\mathbb{K}^{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\theta}
\textstyle{\mathbb{K}}

commutes up to homotopy.

4.
Coarse discrete metric spaces

In this section we remind some standard notions from coarse geometry
[
9
]

and introduce coarse homotopies, following
[
8
]
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a map between two metric spaces.

Definition 4.1
.

f
is
proper
if
f^{-1}(B)
is bounded for all bounded
subsets
B\subset Y
.

Definition 4.2
.

f
is
bornologous
if for all
N>0

there is
M>0
such that if
x,y\in X
and
\operatorname{dist}(x,y)<N
, then

\operatorname{dist}(f(x),f(y))<M
.

Definition 4.3
.

f
is
coarse
if it is proper and bornologous.

Definition 4.4
.

Two maps
f,g\colon X\to Y
are
close
(
f\sim_{cl}g
) if

\sup_{x\in X}\operatorname{dist}(f(x),g(x))<\infty.

Definition 4.5
.

A family of maps
\left\{f_{n}\colon X\to Y\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

is
equibornologous
if for all
N>0
there is
M>0
such
that if
\operatorname{dist}(x,y)<N
, then
\sup_{n}\operatorname{dist}(f_{n}(x),f_{n}(y))<M
.

Definition 4.6
.

We denote by
\mathtt{DMBG}
the category which has as objects countable
discrete metric spaces of bounded geometry and as morphisms coarse
maps.

Remark 4.7
.

Denote by
\mathtt{cDMBG}
the subcategory of
\mathtt{DMBG}
,
obtained by regarding two close maps as the same morphism. Actually,

\mathtt{cDMBG}
is just a full subcategory of the category of coarse spaces
spanned by countable discrete metric spaces of bounded geometry with
bounded coarse structure
[
9
]
.

Definition 4.8
.

Let
X\in\in\mathtt{DMBG}
, and let
p\colon X\to\mathbb{N}
be a coarse
map. Define the
p
-cylinder

I_{p}X=\left\{(x,n)\in X\times\mathbb{N}:n\leq p(x)+1\right\}

with the metrics given by the formula

\operatorname{dist}((x,n),(y,m))=\operatorname{dist}(x,y)+|n-m|.

We also introduce the two inclusions

\displaystyle i_{0}

\displaystyle\colon X\to I_{p}X\colon x\mapsto(x,1),

\displaystyle i_{1}

\displaystyle\colon X\to I_{p}X\colon x\mapsto(p(x)+1).

Clearly,
I_{p}X\in\in\mathtt{DMBG}
and
i_{0},i_{1}\in\mathtt{DMBG}
.

Definition 4.9
.

Let
f_{0},f_{1}\in\mathtt{DMBG}(X,Y)
. We call them
coarsely homotopic

(written
f_{0}\simeq_{crs}f_{1}
) if there is a
p
-cylinder
I_{p}X

and a coarse map
H\colon I_{p}X\to Y
making the diagrams

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{i_{k}}
\scriptstyle{f_{k}}
\textstyle{I_{p}X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{H}
\textstyle{Y,}
\textstyle{\qquad k=0,1}

commute. The map
H
is called a
coarse homotopy
.

5.
Natural transformation induced by coarse maps

We now move on to study Roe algebra functors as objects of
\mathtt{hGEFC}
.

For a countable metric space
X
we shall sometimes write
\mathbb{K}_{X}

instead of
\mathbb{K}
to emphasize that we use
l_{2}(X)
as the
Hilbert space in the definition of
\mathbb{K}
. By
\theta_{X}\colon\mathbb{K}_{X}\mathbb{K}\Rightarrow\mathbb{K}

we denote a stability isomorphism. We also specify the “corner”
embedding
\iota_{x_{0}}\colon\operatorname{Id}\Rightarrow\mathbb{K}_{X}
by the
formula
b\mapsto b\otimes\epsilon_{x_{0},x_{0}}
, where
x_{0}\in X

is an arbitrary point and
\epsilon_{x_{0},x_{0}}
is the corresponding
matrix unit.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
f\in\mathtt{DMBG}(X,Y)
. Consider the natural transformation

f_{*}\colon\mathfrak{M}_{X}^{u}\Rightarrow\mathfrak{M}_{Y}^{u}\mathbb{K}_{X},

defined at object
B
by the formula

f_{*}B\colon\operatorname*{mat}_{x_{1},x_{2}}b_{x_{1},x_{2}}\mapsto\operatorname*{mat}_{y_{1},y_{2}}m_{y_{1},y_{2}},

where

m_{y_{1},y_{2}}=\operatorname*{mat}_{x_{1},x_{2}\in X}\left(\begin{cases}b_{x_{1},x_{2}},&\textrm{if }f(x_{1})=y_{1}\textrm{ and }f(x_{2})=y_{2};\\
0,&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{cases}\right)

Note that
m_{y_{1},y_{2}}\in\mathbb{K}_{X}B
, for it has only finitely
many nonzero elements due to properness of
f
.

Definition 5.2
.

We denote by
\mathfrak{M}_{f}
the natural transformation given
by

\mathfrak{M}_{f}\colon\mathfrak{M}_{X}=\mathfrak{M}_{X}^{u}\mathbb{K}\xRightarrow{f_{*}\mathbb{K}}\mathfrak{M}_{Y}^{u}\mathbb{K}_{X}\mathbb{K}\xRightarrow{\mathfrak{M}_{Y}^{u}\theta_{X}}\mathfrak{M}_{Y}^{u}\mathbb{K}=\mathfrak{M}_{Y}.

The homotopy class of
\mathfrak{M}_{f}
does not depend on the choice
of
\theta_{X}
, because any two such isomorphisms are homotopic.

Remark 5.3
.

Let
m\in\mathfrak{M}_{X}^{u}B
be a matrix
of finite propagation. Note that if
\operatorname{prop}(m)<N
then
\operatorname{prop}(\mathfrak{M}_{f}B(m))<M
,
where
N
and
M
are the numbers from Definition
4.2
.

Definition 5.4
.

Let
X\in\in\mathtt{DMBG}
and let
\sigma\colon X\to X
be a bijection
such that
\sigma\circ\sigma=\operatorname{id}
. Define
\operatorname{Rot}_{\sigma}(t)
to
be a standard family of (unitary) rotation matrices swapping
x
-th
and
\sigma(x)
-th basis elements in
l_{2}(X)
. In order to explicitly
define it, endow
X
with an order by enumerating its elements, and
take

\operatorname{Rot}_{\sigma}\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{B}(l_{2}(X))\colon t\mapsto\operatorname*{mat}_{x_{1},x_{2}\in X}\left(\begin{cases}1,&x_{1}=x_{2}\textrm{ and }\sigma(x_{1})=x_{1};\\
\cos(\pi t/2),&x_{1}=x_{2}\textrm{ and }\sigma(x_{1})\neq x_{1};\\
\sin(\pi t/2),&x_{2}>x_{1}\textrm{ and }\sigma(x_{1})=x_{2};\\
-\sin(\pi t/2),&x_{2}<x_{1}\textrm{ and }\sigma(x_{1})=x_{2};\\
0,&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{cases}\right).

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
X
and
\sigma
be as in the previous definition.
Then the map
t\mapsto\operatorname{Rot}_{\sigma}(t)
is strictly continuous. If
in addition
\sigma\sim_{cl}\operatorname{id}
, then
\operatorname{Rot}_{\sigma}
is norm-continuous
and

\operatorname{prop}(\operatorname{Rot}_{\sigma})=\sup_{x\in X}\operatorname{dist}(x,\sigma(x)).

Proof.

The first part of the lemma is obvious. The second part follows from
Lemma
2.3
.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

The correspondence
f\mapsto\mathfrak{M}_{f}

is a functor from
\mathtt{DMBG}
to
\mathtt{hGEFC}
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathtt{DMBG}(X,Y)
,
g\in\mathtt{DMBG}(Y,Z)
, and let
y_{0}
be an arbitrary
point in
Y
. To prove that
\mathfrak{M}_{g\circ f}\simeq\mathfrak{M}_{g}\circ\mathfrak{M}_{f}

it suffices to check that the following diagram commutes in
\mathtt{hGEFC}
:

\textstyle{\mathfrak{M}_{X}^{u}\mathbb{K}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f_{*}\mathbb{K}}
\scriptstyle{(g\circ f)_{*}\mathbb{K}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{M}_{Y}^{u}\mathbb{K}_{X}\mathbb{K}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g_{*}\mathbb{K}_{X}\mathbb{K}}
\scriptstyle{\mathfrak{M}_{Y}^{u}\theta_{X}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{M}_{Z}^{u}\mathbb{K}_{Y}\mathbb{K}_{X}\mathbb{K}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathfrak{M}_{Z}^{u}\mathbb{K}_{Y}\theta_{X}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{M}_{Z}^{u}\mathbb{K}_{X}\mathbb{K}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathfrak{M}_{Z}^{u}\iota_{y_{0}}\mathbb{K}_{X}\mathbb{K}}
\scriptstyle{\mathfrak{M}_{Z}^{u}\theta_{X}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{M}_{Y}^{u}\mathbb{K}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g_{*}\mathbb{K}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{M}_{Z}^{u}\mathbb{K}_{Y}\mathbb{K}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathfrak{M}_{Z}^{u}\theta_{Y}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{M}_{Z}^{u}\mathbb{K}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathfrak{M}_{Z}^{u}\iota_{y_{0}}\mathbb{K}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{M}_{Z}^{u}\mathbb{K}.}

Commutativity of the two rectangles in the middle is obvious. The
lower triangle commutes by Lemma
3.4
. It remains only
to prove that the upper part of the diagram commutes.

To this end let us consider the bijection
\sigma\colon Y\times X\to Y\times X

swapping the points
(f(x),x)
and
(y_{0},x)
for all
x\in X
.
By the first part of Lemma
5.5
we have a strictly continuous
path
\operatorname{Rot}_{\sigma}
in the multipliers of
\mathbb{K}_{Y}\mathbb{K}_{X}\mathbb{K}
,
which yields

\mathfrak{M}_{Z}^{u}\mathbb{K}_{Y}\mathbb{K}_{X}\mathbb{K}\Rightarrow\mathfrak{M}_{Z}^{u}I\mathbb{K}_{Y}\mathbb{K}_{X}\mathbb{K}\colon\operatorname*{mat}_{z_{1},z_{2}}k_{z_{1},z_{2}}\mapsto\operatorname*{mat}_{z_{1},z_{2}}[t\mapsto\operatorname{Rot}_{\sigma}(t)k_{z_{1},z_{2}}\operatorname{Rot}_{\sigma}(t)^{*}].

The homotopy
\mathfrak{M}_{\operatorname{id}}\simeq\operatorname{id}
is obtained by an analogous
argument.
∎

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
f,g\in\mathtt{DMBG}(X,Y)
such that
f\sim_{cl}g
.
Then there is a homotopy
\eta\colon\mathfrak{M}_{X}\Rightarrow\mathfrak{M}_{Y}I

connecting
\mathfrak{M}_{f}
and
\mathfrak{M}_{g}
such that

(5.1)

\operatorname{prop}(\eta B(m))\leq\operatorname{prop}(\mathfrak{M}_{f}B(m))+2\sup_{x\in X}\operatorname{dist}(f(x),g(x)).

Proof.

Let
\sigma\colon Y\times X\to Y\times X
be a bijection swapping
the points
(f(x),x)
and
(g(x),x)
for all
x\in X
. We apply
the second part of Lemma
5.5
to obtain a norm-continuous
path of unitaries
\operatorname{Rot}_{\sigma}
such that

(5.2)

\operatorname{prop}(\operatorname{Rot}_{\sigma})\leq\sup_{x\in X}\operatorname{dist}(f(x),g(x)).

We use Axiom
3
to derive the required homotopy from the
natural transformation

\mathfrak{M}_{X}\Rightarrow I\mathfrak{M}_{Y}\colon m\mapsto[t\mapsto\operatorname{Rot}_{\sigma}(t)m\operatorname{Rot}_{\sigma}(t)^{*}].

The inequality (
5.1
) holds due to Remark
2.2

and inequality (
5.2
).
∎

Remark 5.8
.

Let
m\in\mathfrak{M}_{X}B
,
y_{1},y_{2}\in Y

and let
\eta\colon\mathfrak{M}_{X}\Rightarrow\mathfrak{M}_{Y}I

be the homotopy from the previous lemma. It is easy to see that if

f(x)=g(x)
for all

x\in f^{-1}\{y_{1},y_{2}\}\cup g^{-1}\{y_{1},y_{2}\},

then the matrix element
(\eta B(m))_{y_{1},y_{2}}
is a constant
function.

Let
\mathtt{cDMBG}
be the category from Remark
4.7
. We have
just proved the following theorem.

Theorem 5.9
.

The correspondence
f\mapsto\mathfrak{M}_{f}
is a functor from
\mathtt{cDMBG}

to
\mathtt{hGEFC}
.

The proof of homotopy invariance of this functor relies on the following
technical lemma.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
H\colon I_{p}X\to Y
be a coarse homotopy
between
f
and
g
. Then there is a family of coarse maps
\left\{f_{n}\colon X\to Y\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

such that the following properties hold:

(1)

the family
\left\{f_{n}\right\}
is equibornologous;

(2)

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{x\in X}\operatorname{dist}(f_{n}(x),f_{n+1}(x))<\infty
;

(3)

for all
x\in X
there is
N\in\mathbb{N}

such that
f_{n}(x)=f_{N}(x)
for all
n\geq N
;

(4)

for every
y\in Y
there are only finitely
many
x\in X
such that
\{f_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\cap H^{-1}\{y\}\neq\text{Ø}
;

(5)

f_{1}=f
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}(x)=g
.

Proof.

We define
f_{n}
by the formula

f_{n}\colon X\to Y\colon x\mapsto H(x,\min(n,p(x)+1)).

Bearing in mind that
p
is bornologous, we use the inequality

\operatorname{dist}\left((x,\min(n,p(x)+1)),(y,\min(n,p(y)+1))\right)=\\
\operatorname{dist}(x,y)+\operatorname{dist}\left(\min(n,p(x)+1),\min(n,p(y)+1)\right)\leq\operatorname{dist}(x,y)+|p(x)-p(y)|

to show that
1
holds. Properties
2

and
4
follow from the fact that
H
is a coarse
map. Property
3
is obvious. Note that
f_{1}

and
\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}(x)
are the restrictions of
H
to the
upper and lower faces of
I_{p}
, respectively, so
5

also holds.
∎

Theorem 5.11
.

If
f\simeq_{crs}g\colon X\to Y
, then

\mathfrak{M}_{f}\simeq\mathfrak{M}_{g}
.

Proof.

Let
\{f_{n}\}
be a sequence of maps given by Lemma
5.10
.
From
2
we deduce that
f_{n}\sim_{cl}f_{n+1}
for all

n
. Using this we get a family of natural transformations
\{\eta_{n}\colon\mathfrak{M}_{X}\Rightarrow I\mathfrak{M}_{Y}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

as in the proof of Lemma
5.7
such that
\operatorname{ev}_{0}\circ\eta_{n}=\mathfrak{M}_{f_{n}}

and
\operatorname{ev}_{1}\circ\eta_{n}=\mathfrak{M}_{f_{n+1}}
. Putting them together
in an obvious way, we obtain the natural transformation

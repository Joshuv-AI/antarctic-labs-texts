Variational convergences under moving anisotropies Thanks: Acknowledgements. The authors thank Fares Essebei, Andrea Pinamonti, Francesco Serra Cassano and Giacomo Vianello for interesting and valuable conversations on the topic of the paper.
Alberto Maione, Fabio Paronetto, Simone Verzellesi

Abstract
. We study the asymptotic behaviour of sequences of integral functionals depending on moving anisotropies. We introduce and describe the relevant functional setting, establishing uniform Meyers-Serrin type approximations, Poincaré inequalities and compactness properties. We prove several Γ \Gamma -convergence results, and apply the latter to the study of H H -convergence of anisotropic linear differential operators.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
49J45, 49Q20, 53C17

1.
Introduction

In this paper we study the asymptotic behaviour of sequences of functionals of the form

\int_{\Omega}f_{h}(x,X^{h}u(x))\,{\rm d}x,\hskip 10.00002ptu\in W^{1,p}_{X^{h}}(\Omega),

for a sequence of anisotropies
(X^{h})_{h}=(X_{1}^{h},\ldots,X_{m}^{h})_{h}
, where
\Omega
is a bounded domain of
{\bf R}^{n}
and
m\leqslant n
.
As
h
tends to
\infty
, we describe the convergence of these functionals, their minima, minimizers and momenta, to the corresponding limit quantities, associated with a limit functional

(1.1)

\int_{\Omega}f(x,Xu(x))\,{\rm d}x,\hskip 10.00002ptu\in W^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega),

driven by a limit anisotropy
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})
.
The natural variational convergence to take into account is
\Gamma
-convergence
(see e.g.
[
3
,
13
]
).
This technique, which is extremely useful both for theoretical purposes and practical applications, remains a crucial tool in the study of various problems in mechanics, such as homogenization, phase transitions and the asymptotic analysis of PDEs.
In addition to an interest in
\Gamma
-convergence itself, we explore its applications to the study of anisotropic, linear, symmetric PDEs, referring back to the so-called
G
-convergence
or
H
-convergence
(see e.g.
[
34
,
39
]
).

The investigation of anisotropic variational functionals as in (
1.1
), as well as their related functional frameworks, is originally motivated by L. Hörmander’s seminal work on
hypoelliptic operators

[
27
]
.
The latter constitutes a milestone in the study of differential operators with underlying
sub-Riemannian
type structures.
Important evidences are the works of G.B. Folland
[
18
]
and of L. Rothschild and E.M. Stein
[
38
]
in the context of
stratified
and
nilpotent Lie groups
. We refer to
[
2
]
for further accounts on analysis on Lie groups, and to
[
1
,
26
,
28
]
for thorough introductions to sub-Riemannian geometry.

As a prototypical example, consider the smooth anisotropy
X=(X_{1},X_{2})
on
{\bf R}^{3}
, where

(1.2)

X_{1}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}+x_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{3}}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003ptX_{2}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}-x_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{3}}.

The latter generate, via Lie-brackets, the Lie algebra of the so-called
first Heisenberg group
, and induce on it a
sub-Riemannian structure
.
The relevant hypoelliptic operator associated with
X
is the so-called
sub-Laplacian

(1.3)

\left(X_{1}\right)^{2}u+\left(X_{2}\right)^{2}u,

and its associated
Dirichlet energy
reads as

(1.4)

\int_{\Omega}\left(|X_{1}u|^{2}+|X_{2}u|^{2}\right)\,{\rm d}x.

As (
1.3
) is not elliptic, (
1.4
) is not coercive.
Moreover, the Euclidean Sobolev spaces
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
are not the correct finiteness domains for functionals as in (
1.3
).
These issues clearly obstruct the use of classical techniques to study minimization properties of (
1.4
), preventing for instance the
L^{2}
-lower semicontinuity of its Euclidean extension to
L^{2}(\Omega),
namely

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\begin{cases}\int_{\Omega}\left(|X_{1}u|^{2}+|X_{2}u|^{2}\right)\,{\rm d}x&\text{ if }u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega),\\
\infty&\text{ if }u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\setminus W^{1,2}(\Omega).\end{cases}}

To overcome these obstacles, building on the foundational work of G.B. Folland and E.M. Stein
[
19
]
, the correct functional framework has been developed by B. Franchi, R. Serapioni and F. Serra Cassano
[
20
,
21
]
and by N. Garofalo and D.M. Nhieu
[
22
,
23
]
.
Precisely, when
1\leqslant p<\infty
, the study of anisotropic functionals in the greater generality of (
1.1
) can be carried out via the introduction of suitable anisotropic Sobolev and
BV
spaces, say
W^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega)
and
BV_{X}(\Omega)
, emerging as the natural domains of functionals as in (
1.1
).
Although this construction is possible for arbitrary anisotropies, the specific structure of
X
may lead to good approximation results
à la
Meyers-Serrin, as well as to Poincaré inequalities and Rellich-Kondrachov compactness properties. We refer to
Section

2
for more specific insights. When instead
p=\infty
, the reader is referred to
[
36
,
41
,
42
]
for some anisotropic
L^{\infty}
-variational problems.

Asymptotic results for functionals in the
static
case as in (
1.1
), i.e. when the anisotropy
X
is fixed, started in
[
31
,
32
,
33
]
to generalize classical integral representation and
\Gamma
-convergence results due to G. Buttazzo and G. Dal Maso
[
6
]
. These results were later extended in
[
15
,
16
]
to complete integral functionals of the form

\int_{\Omega}f(x,u(x),Xu(x))\,{\rm d}x,\hskip 10.00002ptu\in W^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega),

generalizing the corresponding Euclidean counterparts
[
5
,
7
]
.
In all the above-mentioned contributions, the authors assume a full-rank condition on the family
X
, the so-called
Linear Independence Condition (LIC)
.
This technical assumption has been definitely removed by the third-named author in
[
40
]
. Thanks to this set of results, variational properties for static anisotropic functionals are now well-understood, and available in great generality.

In this paper we extend the results obtained in the aforementioned literature by considering
moving anisotropies
. Namely, a static anisotropy is replaced by a sequence
(X^{h})_{h}
converging to a limit anisotropy
X
.
Although our setting will be fairly more general, it models, as a particular case, the regularization of a sub-Riemannian structure via Riemannian approximants. As an instance, consider the
anisotropy
X=(X_{1},X_{2})
introduced in (
1.2
) to model the first sub-Riemannian Heisenberg group. Its natural sub-Riemannian structure, say
g
, can be approximated by a sequence of Riemannian metrics, say
g_{h}
, each induced by the family of vector fields
X^{h}=(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3}^{h})
, where

X_{3}^{h}=\frac{1}{h}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{3}}.

The benefits of such an approximation are manifold. For instance, we stress that the natural approximating operators related to (
1.3
), namely

\left(X_{1}\right)^{2}u+\left(X_{2}\right)^{2}u+\left(X_{3}^{h}\right)^{2}u,

are precisely the
Laplace-Beltrami
operators associated with the Riemannian structures
g_{h}
. In turn, they are uniformly elliptic operators, their Dirichlet energies are coercive, and their associated functional frameworks boil down to the Euclidean ones.
We refer e.g. to
[
9
,
14
]
for further insights, and to
[
8
,
12
,
24
,
35
,
37
]
for a non-exhaustive list of applications of this approximation technique.

The study of asymptotic properties of functionals of the form

(1.5)

\displaystyle F_{h}(u)=\displaystyle{\begin{cases}\int_{\Omega}f_{h}(x,X^{h}u(x)){\rm d}x&\text{ if }u\in W^{1,p}_{X^{h}}(\Omega),\\
\infty&\text{ if }u\in L^{p}(\Omega)\setminus W^{1,p}_{X^{h}}(\Omega),\end{cases}}

when, say,
1<p<\infty
, requires some preliminary precautions. Indeed, contrarily to the static case, there are no
a priori
relations between the finiteness domains of each functional in (
1.5
).
Indeed, the latter may naturally vary according to the associated anisotropy. To this aim, it is important to understand how the approximating Sobolev spaces relate to the limit one, as discussed thoroughly in
Section

3
.

Prescribing Dirichlet boundary conditions is also a delicate matter. Indeed, just as any finiteness domain in (
1.5
) depends on the associated anisotropy, it is natural in this setting to prescribe, for each fixed anisotropy
X^{h}
, a different boundary condition, say
\varphi_{h}\in W^{1,p}_{X_{h}}(\Omega)
, and to consider sequences of functionals of the form

(1.6)

\displaystyle F^{\varphi_{h}}_{h}(u)=\displaystyle{\begin{cases}\int_{\Omega}f_{h}(x,X^{h}u(x)){\rm d}x&\text{ if }u\in W^{1,p}_{X^{h},\varphi_{h}}(\Omega),\\
\infty&\text{ if }u\in L^{p}(\Omega)\setminus W^{1,p}_{X^{h},\varphi_{h}}(\Omega),\end{cases}}

where
W^{1,p}_{X^{h},\varphi_{h}}(\Omega)
is the correct affine Sobolev space associated with the couple
(X^{h},\varphi_{h})
. We stress that, although moving boundary conditions may also be considered in the static case, in the moving one this choice is forced by the very structure of the problem. In this regard, we will discuss the right convergence assumptions on
(\varphi_{h})_{h}
to the limit boundary condition, say
\varphi
, in order to suitably relate the approximating and the limit affine Sobolev spaces (cf.
Section

3
).

A third important novelty in considering sequences of functionals as in (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) consists in the fact that, unlike in the static case, possible coercivity and boundedness conditions typically depend on the anisotropy, and are thus not uniform along the sequence. Indeed, we will deal with growth conditions of the form

(1.7)

d_{1}\int_{\Omega}|X^{h}u(x)|^{p}{\rm d}x\leqslant\int_{\Omega}f_{h}(x,X^{h}u(x)){\rm d}x\leqslant\int_{\Omega}a(x)\,{\rm d}x+d_{2}\int_{\Omega}|X^{h}u(x))|^{p}{\rm d}x,

where
a\in L^{1}(\Omega)
and
0<d_{1}\leqslant d_{2}
are fixed. This lack of uniformity, as discussed thoroughly in
Section

6
, prevents the use of some classical tools to establish
\Gamma
-compactness properties (cf.
Remark

6.6
), and requires a careful analysis of the asymptotic behaviour of the partial norms
\|X^{h}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,{\bf R}^{m})}
.

A first fundamental step in handling these issues, as discussed in
Section

4
, consists in establishing Meyers-Serrin type approximation results that keep track of the approximating sequence of anisotropies (cf.
Theorem

4.2
). Roughly speaking, under uniform convergence of the anisotropies, functions in
W^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega)
can be well-approximated by a sequence of smooth functions
(u_{h})_{h}
, where each
u_{h}\in W^{1,p}_{X^{h}}(\Omega)
, in the sense that

\text{$u_{h}\to u$ strongly in $L^{p}(\Omega)$}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{$X^{h}u_{h}\to Xu$ strongly in $L^{p}(\Omega;{\bf R}^{m})$}.

This approximation property, as explained in
Section

6
, is crucial to deal with moving growth conditions as in (
1.7
). In the same spirit, it is possible to provide a uniform approximation result for affine Sobolev spaces (cf.
Theorem

4.3
) under suitable convergence assumptions on the approximating sequence of boundary data. We stress that, in the static case, this result would follow
by definition
, as functions in
W^{1,p}_{X,\varphi}(\Omega)
are, up to a translation by
\varphi
, in the closure of
\mathbf{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
. In order to show the validity of the aforementioned approximations, we introduce a non-standard mollification technique (cf. (
4.1
) and (
4.2
)), whose convergence rate is appropriately related to the convergence rate of the anisotropies.

The same mollification procedure also plays a key role in
Section

5
, where we establish Rellich-Kondrachov compactness properties and Poincaré inequalities that, again, keep into account the entire sequence
(X^{h})_{h}
(cf.
Theorem

5.2
and
Theorem

5.3
, respectively).
Precisely, we show that, as soon as
X
is strong enough to guarantee the validity of these properties in the limit, then such properties extend uniformly along
(X^{h})_{h}
. We emphasise that such uniformity is fundamental in bypassing the non-uniformity of the coercivity assumptions as in (
1.7
). Indeed, a careful combination of the previous results will still provide, as in the static setting, boundedness of minimizing sequences and convergence of minima and minimizers (cf.
Theorem

6.10
).

The core of this work is
Section

6
, where we provide several results related to
\Gamma
-convergence.
Owing to
Section

4
, we first prove
\Gamma
- convergence of the partial norms
\|X^{h}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,{\bf R}^{m})}
to the limit one
\|Xu\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,{\bf R}^{m})}
(cf.
Theorem

6.3
).
As already mentioned, this first feature is crucial in the establishment of a general
\Gamma
-compactness theorem for functionals as in (
1.5
) (cf.
Theorem

6.5
).
Next, we keep into account Dirichlet boundary data, obtaining
\Gamma
-compactness for sequences of functionals as in (
1.6
) (cf.
Theorem

6.8
).
As pointed out in
Remark

6.9
, the fact that the boundary data necessarily evolves with the anisotropies causes some challenges in the identification of the appropriate recovery sequences, as the latter need to keep into account the above-mentioned evolution. In this regard, a key tool is provided by the aforementioned uniform approximation result for affine Sobolev spaces,
Theorem

4.3
.
As already highlighted, the results of
Section

5
allow us to prove the convergence of minima and minimizers for
\Gamma
-converging sequences of functionals, as in (
1.6
), as stated in
Theorem

6.10
.

We conclude
Section

6
by specializing our main
\Gamma
-compactness result,
Theorem

6.5
, to the class of anisotropic
quadratic forms

F_{h}(u)=\begin{cases}\int_{\Omega}\langle A^{h}(x)X^{h}u(x),X^{h}u(x)\rangle_{{\bf R}^{m}}{\rm d}x\quad&\text{if }u\in W^{1,2}_{X^{h}}(\Omega),\\
\infty\quad&\text{if }u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\setminus W^{1,2}_{X^{h}}(\Omega),\end{cases}

whence establishing
\Gamma
-compactness in this particular subclass (cf.
Theorem

6.13
). The study of the latter is particularly relevant in connection with anisotropic sequences of symmetric, linear differential problems of the form

(1.8)

\begin{cases}\mu u+{\rm div}_{X^{h}}(A^{h}(x)X^{h}u)=f&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u_{h}\in W^{1,2}_{X^{h},\varphi_{h}}(\Omega),\end{cases}

as well as with their asymptotic behaviour.
We stress that the latter are clear generalisations of the prototypical differential problems associated with (
1.3
). In
Section

7
, we adapt the classical notion of
H
-convergence for keeping into account the moving anisotropic framework (cf.
Definition

7.1
), and we establish a related
H
-compactness property (cf.
Theorem

7.2
). This will follow as a corollary of the previous results, as well as of some additional convergence properties established in
Theorem

6.15
to deal with the so-called
momenta
associated with (
1.8
).

The paper is structured as follows.
In
Section

2
, we recall definitions and properties of the static anisotropic functional setting.
In
Section

3
, we introduce the relevant classes of moving anisotropies we are interested in, and we discuss some preliminary functional results.
Later, in
Section

4
, we establish uniform Meyers-Serrin type approximations for anisotropic Sobolev spaces (
Theorem

4.2
) and for affine anisotropic Sobolev spaces (
Theorem

4.3
).
In
Section

5
, we state and prove a uniform Rellich-Kondrachov compactness result (
Theorem

5.2
), as well as a uniform Poincaré inequality (
Theorem

5.3
).
In
Section

6
, we first show the main
\Gamma
-compactness result,
Theorem

6.5
, as well as its extension to functionals including Dirichlet boundary conditions (
Theorem

6.8
) and quadratic forms (
Theorem

6.13
).
Moreover, we provide convergence of minima, minimizers (
Theorem

6.10
) and momenta (
Theorem

6.15
).
As a corollary, in
Section

7
we obtain the
H
-compactness result,
Theorem

7.2
.

2.
Anisotropic functional frameworks

Main notations

If no ambiguity arises, we let
\infty=+\infty
and
{\bf N}=~{\bf N}~\setminus~\{0\}
. If
1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
, we denote by
p^{\prime}
the Hölder-conjugate exponent of
p
. We write
(a_{h})_{h}\subseteq(A_{h})_{h}
meaning that
a_{h}\in A_{h}
for any
h\in{\bf N}
. We fix
m,n\in{\bf N}
such that
0<m\leqslant n
. If
A,B\subseteq{\bf R}^{n}
, we let
A+B=\{a+b\,:\,a\in A,\,b\in B\}
.
For
\alpha,\beta\in{\bf N}
, we denote by
M(\alpha,\beta)
the set of matrices with
\alpha
rows and
\beta
columns.
Vectors in
{\bf R}^{\alpha}
are seen as matrices in
M(\alpha,1)
. We denote by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{{\bf R}^{\alpha}}
the scalar product between vectors in
{\bf R}^{\alpha}
.
Fixed an open and bounded set
\Omega\subseteq{\bf R}^{n}
, we denote by
\mathcal{A}
the class of all the open subsets of
\Omega
, and for a function
u
defined in
\Omega
, we may tacitly assume that it is defined on
{\bf R}^{n}
by extending it to be zero outside
\Omega
. Finally, we denote by
Du
the (distributional) Euclidean gradient of
u
.

2.1.
Anisotropies

Throughout the paper,
\Omega
is an open and bounded subset of
{\bf R}^{n}
. Given a family
X\coloneqq(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})
of Lipschitz continuous vector fields on
\Omega
, such that

X_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{j,i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},\hskip 10.00002pt\text{with }c_{j,i}\in\Lip(\Omega)

for any
j=1,\ldots,m
and any
i=1,\ldots,n
, we denote by
\mathcal{C}(x)\coloneqq[c_{j,i}(x)]_{\begin{subarray}{c}{i=1,\dots,n}\\
{j=1,\dots,m}\end{subarray}}
the
m\times n

coefficient matrix
associated with
X
. We may refer to
X
as
(static) anisotropy
, or
X
-gradient
. This notation is motivated by the following definition.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
u
and
V
be a
1
-dimensional and an
m
-dimensional distribution.

(1)

The
X
-gradient of
u
is the
m
-dimensional distribution defined by

Xu(\varphi)=-u\left(\divv(\mathcal{C}^{T}\varphi)\right)\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for any $\varphi\in\mathbf{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega;{\bf R}^{m})$.}

(2)

The
X
-divergence of
V
is the
1
-dimensional distribution defined by

\divv_{X}(V)(\varphi)=-V(\mathcal{C}D\varphi)\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for any $\varphi\in\mathbf{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)$.}

Clearly, in the isotropic case, the above definition returns the usual notions of gradient and divergence.
In what follows, we will sometimes consider the following classes of
X
-gradients.

Definition 2.2
.

We let
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}(\Omega_{0},C_{d},c,C)
be the class of
X
-gradients that are defined and Lipschitz continuous on an open set
\Omega_{0}\supseteq\overline{\Omega}
, and that satisfy the following three conditions.

(\mathrm{H}.1)

Let
d
be the
Carnot-Carathéodory distance
induced by
X
on
\Omega_{0}
(cf. e.g.
[
26
]
).
Then
d
is finite and continuous with respect to the usual topology of
{\bf R}^{n}
.

(\mathrm{H}.2)

There exist positive constants
R,C_{d}
such that

|B_{d}(x,2r)|\leqslant C_{d}\,|B_{d}(x,r)|

for any
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and
r\leqslant R
.
Here,
B_{d}(x,r)
denotes the open metric ball with respect to
d
.

(\mathrm{H}.3)

There exist positive constants
c,C
such that for every open ball
B\coloneqq B_{d}(\bar{x},r)
, with
\bar{x}\in\Omega
such that
cB\coloneqq B_{d}(\bar{x},cr)\subseteq\Omega_{0}
, and for every
u\in\mathrm{Lip}(\overline{cB})
and
x\in\overline{B}
,

\left|u(x)-\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}u(y)\,{\rm d}y\right|\leqslant C\int_{cB}|Xu(y)|\frac{d(x,y)}{|B_{d}(x,d(x,y))|}\,{\rm d}y.

Several important families of
X
-gradients belong to the class
\mathcal{M}(\Omega_{0},C_{d},c,C)
.
In particular, we recall that all
Hörmander vector fields
, i.e. smooth vector fields for which the rank of the Lie algebra generated by
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
equals
n
at any point of
\Omega_{0}
, belong to the class
\mathcal{M}(\Omega_{0},C_{d},c,C)
.
Among Hörmander vector fields, we recall the following instances.

(i)

Euclidean vector fields:

X=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\dots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}\right)
, defined on
{\bf R}^{n}
.

(ii)

Grushin vector fields:

X=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},x_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}\right)
, defined on
{\bf R}^{2}
.

(iii)

Heisenberg vector fields:

X=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}+x_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{3}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}-x_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{3}}\right)
, defined on
{\bf R}^{3}
.

In particular, Heisenberg vector fields serve as a prototype of
Carnot groups vector fields
(cf. e.g.
[
2
]
).
It is worth noting that all Carnot groups vector fields are, by definition, Hörmander vector fields, and hence belong to the class
\mathcal{M}(\Omega_{0},C_{d},c,C)
.
For further insights on this topic and for examples of families of vector fields in the class
\mathcal{M}(\Omega_{0},C_{d},c,C)
that do not satisfy the Hörmander condition, the reader is referred to
[
32
]
.

2.2.
Sobolev spaces

Owing to
Definition

2.1
, we introduce the main functional framework.

Definition 2.3
.

The anisotropic Sobolev spaces associated to an
X
-gradient are defined by

\displaystyle W^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{u\in L^{p}(\Omega)\,:\,Xu\in L^{p}(\Omega;{\bf R}^{m})\},

\displaystyle H^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\overline{\mathbf{C}^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega)}^{\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega)}},

\displaystyle W^{1,p}_{X,0}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\overline{\mathbf{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega)}^{\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega)}},

\displaystyle W^{1,p}_{X,\varphi}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{u\in W^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega)\,:\,u-\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{X,0}(\Omega)\}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{ for any }\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega).

It is well-known (cf.
[
19
]
) that the vector space
W^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega)
, endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega)}\coloneqq\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|Xu\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;{\bf R}^{m})},

is a Banach space for any
1\leqslant p<+\infty
, reflexive when
1<p<+\infty
. The Lipschitz continuity assumption on the family
X
ensures that

W^{1,p}(\Omega)
embeds continuously into
W^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega)
(cf.
[
15
,
31
]
), where
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
denotes the Euclidean Sobolev space.
Precisely, for any
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
, the
X
-gradient admits the Euclidean representation

(2.1)

Xu(x)=\mathcal{C}(x)Du(x)

for a.e.
x\in\Omega
.
We also recall that, as for classical Sobolev spaces,
H^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega)\subseteq W^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega)
.
The classical result of Meyers and Serrin is still valid in this anisotropic setting and it was proved, independently, in
[
20
]
and
[
23
]
.
We state the result in what follows, and refer again the interested reader to
[
32
]
for further discussions on this topic.

Theorem 2.4
.

For any
1\leqslant p<\infty
, it holds that
H^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega)=W^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega).

If, in addition, the family
X
belongs to the class
\mathcal{M}(\Omega_{0},C_{d},c,C)
, then the following Poincaré inequality and Rellich-Kondrachov theorem hold.
We refer the interested reader to
[
32
, Proposition 2.16]
and
[
21
, Theorem 3.4]
, respectively.

Proposition 2.5

(Poincaré inequality)
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq{\bf R}^{n}
be connected, let
1\leqslant p<\infty
and let
X\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega_{0},C_{d},c,C)
.
Then, there exists a positive constant
c_{p,\Omega}=c_{p,\Omega}(p,\Omega)
such that

c_{p,\Omega}\,\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\,{\rm d}x\leqslant\int_{\Omega}|Xu|^{p}\,{\rm d}x\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for any $u\in W^{1,p}_{X,0}(\Omega)$.}

Theorem 2.6

(Rellich-Kondrachov)
.

For
1\leqslant p<\infty
,
W^{1,p}_{X,0}(\Omega)
compactly embeds in
L^{p}(\Omega)
.

3.
Moving anisotropies

We fix a sequence of
X
-gradients
(X^{h})_{h}
with
m
components
X^{h}=(X_{1}^{h},\ldots,X_{m}^{h})
, identified by a sequence of matrices
(\mathcal{C}^{h})_{h}
as in
Section

2.1
.
Throughout the paper, we assume that there exists a limit family of vector fields
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})
, identified by a Lipschitz continuous coefficient matrix
\mathcal{C}
, such that

(A)

\mathcal{C}^{h}\to\mathcal{C}\text{ uniformly on }\Omega.

We may sometimes use the index
\infty
to refer to
X
. As already mentioned, (
A
) will be sufficient to infer good approximation properties of anisotropic Sobolev functions. Instead, most of the
\Gamma
-compactness properties will be achieved for the following two classes of
X
-gradients.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that
(X^{h})_{h}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
if (
A
) holds and

(S.1)

\mathcal{C}^{h}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mathcal{C}\\
\mathcal{D}^{h}\end{array}\right]\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\mathcal{C}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mathcal{C}\\
0\end{array}\right],

with a natural abuse of notation in the second property.

Definition 3.2
.

We say that
(X^{h})_{h}\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
if (
A
) holds and if

(S.2)

(X^{h})_{h}\text{ is uniformly bounded in }\Lip(\Omega,M(m,n)).

Remark 3.3
.

As pointed out in the introduction,
\mathcal{S}_{1}
models those frameworks where a sub-Riemannian structure induced by
X
is approximated by a sequence of converging Riemannian structures.
Instead,
\mathcal{S}_{2}
allows anisotropies with arbitrary shapes, provided the uniform bound (
S.2
) is satisfied.

Remark 3.4
.

If
(X^{h})_{h}\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
,
by (
S.2
) and up to a subsequence, we may always assume that

\frac{\partial c^{h}_{j,i}}{\partial x_{k}}\to\frac{\partial c_{j,i}}{\partial x_{k}}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{weakly${}^{\star}$ in $L^{\infty}(\Omega)$}

for any
j=1,\ldots,m
and any
i,k=1,\ldots,n
.

Remark 3.5
.

We stress that
\mathcal{S}_{1}\cap\mathcal{S}_{2}\neq\emptyset
. Nevertheless, neither
\mathcal{S}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{2}
nor
\mathcal{S}_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{1}
. First, it is clear that
\mathcal{S}_{2}\not\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{1}
. To see that
\mathcal{S}_{1}\not\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{2}
, consider the following instance. Let
\Omega=(-1,1)^{2}\subseteq{\bf R}^{2}_{(x_{1},x_{2})}
. For any
h\in{\bf N}
, let
X^{h}=(X_{1},X^{h}_{2})
, where

X_{1}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\hskip 20.00003ptX_{2}=f_{h}(x_{2})\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003ptf_{h}(x_{2})=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{h}&\text{if }x_{2}\in(-1,-\frac{1}{h^{2}}),\\
hx_{2}&\text{if }x_{2}\in(-\frac{1}{h^{2}},\frac{1}{h^{2}}),\\
\frac{1}{h}&\text{if }x_{2}\in(\frac{1}{h^{2}},1).\end{cases}

A simple computation reveals that
(X^{h})_{h}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{2}
.

In order to address compactness properties, we will need to further refine our assumptions. Indeed, we will show that, when

(B)

X\in\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}(\Omega_{0},C_{d},c,C),

it is possible to derive uniform compactness properties and Poincaré inequalities.
To this aim, we introduce the following subclasses of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
.

Definition 3.6
.

For
i\in\{1,2\}
, we say that
(X^{h})_{h}\in\mathcal{T}_{i}
if
(X^{h})_{h}\in\mathcal{S}_{i}
and (
B
) holds.

In the rest of this section, we investigate some preliminary properties of moving anisotropies. We introduce the
anisotropic Rayleigh quotients
. If
(X^{h})_{h}
satisfies (
A
), we let

\mathcal{R}_{h}\coloneqq\inf\left\{\frac{\int_{\Omega}|X^{h}u|^{p}\,{\rm d}x}{\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\,{\rm d}x}\,:\,u\in W^{1,p}_{X_{h},0}(\Omega),\,u\neq 0\right\}=\inf\left\{\frac{\int_{\Omega}|X^{h}u|^{p}\,{\rm d}x}{\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\,{\rm d}x}\,:\,u\in\mathbf{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega),\,u\neq 0\right\}

for any
h\in{\bf N}
, and we let
\mathcal{R}
be the Rayleigh quotient associated with
X
.
Above, the second equality follows by definition of
W^{1,p}_{X^{h},0}(\Omega)
.

3.1.
Preliminary functional properties

The following two propositions describes the relations between the approximating Sobolev spaces and the limit one in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
, respectively.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
(X^{h})_{h}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
. Let
1<p<\infty
.
Then
W^{1,p}_{X^{h}}(\Omega)\subseteq W^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega)
. Precisely,

(3.1)

X^{h}u=(X_{1}u,\ldots,X_{m}u,X^{h}_{m+1}u,\ldots,X^{h}_{n}u)\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003ptXu=(X_{1}u,\ldots,X_{m}u,0,\ldots,0).

for any
h\in{\bf N}
and
u\in W^{1,p}_{X^{h}}(\Omega)
.
Moreover, if
(u_{h})_{h}\subseteq W^{1,p}_{X^{h}}(\Omega)
satisfies
u_{h}\to u
weakly in
L^{p}(\Omega)
, then

\|Xu\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;{\bf R}^{m})}\leqslant\liminf_{h\to\infty}\|X^{h}u_{h}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;{\bf R}^{m})}.

Proof.

Let
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})\in\mathbf{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega;{\bf R}^{m}\times{\bf R}^{n-m})
. Then

\begin{split}-\int_{\Omega}u\divv(\mathcal{C}^{T}\varphi)\,{\rm d}x&=-\int_{\Omega}u\divv((\mathcal{C}^{h})^{T}(\varphi_{1},0))\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\Omega}\langle(X^{h}_{1}u,\ldots,X^{h}_{m}u,0,\ldots,0),\varphi\rangle_{{\bf R}^{m}}\,{\rm d}x,\end{split}

whence the first claims follow. Let
(u_{h})_{h}\subseteq W^{1,p}_{X^{h}}(\Omega)
be such that
u_{h}\to u
weakly in
L^{p}(\Omega)
. Assume that
\liminf_{h\to\infty}\|X^{h}u_{h}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;{\bf R}^{m})}<\infty
. By reflexivity and (
3.1
), there exists
v\in L^{p}(\Omega;{\bf R}^{m})
such that, up to a subsequence,
Xu_{h}\to v
weakly in
L^{p}(\Omega;{\bf R}^{m})
.
Then
u\in W^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega)
and
v=Xu
. The thesis follows by the weak lower semicontinuity of the
L^{p}
-norm.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
(X^{h})_{h}\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
. Let
1<p<\infty
.
Let
(u_{h})_{h}\subseteq W^{1,p}_{X^{h}}(\Omega)
.

(1)

If
u_{h}\to u
weakly in
L^{p}(\Omega)
and
X^{h}u_{h}\to v
weakly in
L^{p}(\Omega;{\bf R}^{m})
, then
u\in W^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega)
.

(2)

If in addition
u_{h}\to u
strongly in
L^{p}(\Omega)
, then
v=Xu
and

\|Xu\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;{\bf R}^{m})}\leqslant\liminf_{h\to\infty}\|X^{h}u_{h}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;{\bf R}^{m})}.

Proof.

Let
p^{\prime}
the Hölder-conjugate exponent of
p
. Let
(u_{h})_{h}\subseteq W^{1,p}_{X^{h}}(\Omega)
be such that
u_{h}\to u
weakly in
L^{p}(\Omega)
.
By (
S.2
),

(3.2)

u_{h}c^{h}_{j,i}\to uc_{j,i}\text{ weakly in }L^{p}(\Omega)

for any
i=1,\ldots,n
and any
j=1,\dots,m
. Fix
\varphi\in\mathbf{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega;{\bf R}^{m})
. Then

\begin{split}-\int_{\Omega}u&\diver(\mathcal{C}^{T}\varphi)\,{\rm d}x=-\int_{\Omega}u\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}c_{j,i}\frac{\partial\varphi_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x-\int_{\Omega}u\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\partial c_{j,i}}{\partial x_{i}}\varphi_{j}\,{\rm d}x\\
&\overset{\eqref{weaklyhps2}}{=}-\lim_{h\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}u_{h}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}c^{h}_{j,i}\frac{\partial\varphi_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}\,{\rm d}x-\int_{\Omega}u\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\partial c_{j,i}}{\partial x_{i}}\varphi_{j}\,{\rm d}x\\
&=\lim_{h\to\infty}\left(-\int_{\Omega}u_{h}\divv((\mathcal{C}^{h})^{T}\varphi)\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}u_{h}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\partial c^{h}_{j,i}}{\partial x_{i}}\varphi_{j}\,{\rm d}x\right)-\int_{\Omega}u\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\partial c_{j,i}}{\partial x_{i}}\varphi_{j}\,{\rm d}x\\
&=\lim_{h\to\infty}\left(\int_{\Omega}\langle X^{h}u_{h},\varphi\rangle_{{\bf R}^{m}}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}u_{h}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\partial c^{h}_{j,i}}{\partial x_{i}}\varphi_{j}\,{\rm d}x\right)-\int_{\Omega}u\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\partial c_{j,i}}{\partial x_{i}}\varphi_{j}\,{\rm d}x\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\langle v,\varphi\rangle_{{\bf R}^{m}}\,{\rm d}x+\lim_{h\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}u_{h}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\partial c^{h}_{j,i}}{\partial x_{i}}\varphi_{j}\,{\rm d}x-\int_{\Omega}u\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\partial c_{j,i}}{\partial x_{i}}\varphi_{j}\,{\rm d}x.\end{split}

By (
S.2
) and Hölder’s inequality, if
\|\varphi\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;{\bf R}^{m})}\leqslant 1
the last line
is bounded above independently of
\varphi.
In particular,
u\in W^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega)
. If in addition
u_{h}\to u
strongly in
L^{p}(\Omega)
, then the last two terms in the last line
simplify by (
S.2
) and
Remark

3.4
. Hence
v=Xu
by definition. The thesis follows by the lower semicontinuity of the
L^{p}
-norm with respect to weak convergence.
∎

Our next result states the convergence of Rayleigh quotients when
(X^{h})_{h}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
. As we will show in
Theorem

5.3
, the same result holds in
\mathcal{S}_{2}
under the additional assumption (
B
).

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
(X^{h})_{h}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
. Let
1<p<\infty
. Then

(3.3)

\mathcal{R}\leqslant\mathcal{R}_{h}\text{ for any }h\in{\bf N}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\lim_{h\to\infty}\mathcal{R}_{h}=\mathcal{R}.

Proof.

Fix
u\in\mathbf{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
such that
u\neq 0
. Then
Proposition

3.7
implies that

\mathcal{R}\leqslant\frac{\int_{\Omega}|Xu|^{p}\,{\rm d}x}{\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}}\leqslant\frac{\int_{\Omega}|X^{h}u|^{p}\,{\rm d}x}{\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}},

whence the first property of (
3.3
) follows.
On the other hand, fix
u\in\mathbf{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
such that
u\neq 0
. By (
A
),

X^{h}u\to Xu
uniformly on
\Omega
, whence

\limsup_{h\to\infty}\mathcal{R}_{h}\leqslant\limsup_{h\to\infty}\frac{\int_{\Omega}|X^{h}u|^{p}\,{\rm d}x}{\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\,{\rm d}x}=\frac{\int_{\Omega}|Xu|^{p}\,{\rm d}x}{\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\,{\rm d}x}.

Being
u\neq 0
arbitrarily chosen in
\mathbf{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
, (
3.3
) follows.
∎

Finally, we describe the relation between the approximating affine Sobolev spaces and the limit one under the sole assumption (
A
).

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
(X^{h})_{h}
satisfy (
A
).
Let
1\leqslant p<\infty
. Let
(\varphi_{h})_{h}\subseteq W^{1,p}_{X^{h}}(\Omega)
, and let
\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega)
. Assume that

(3.4)

\varphi_{h}\to\varphi\text{ weakly in }L^{p}(\Omega)\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003ptX^{h}\varphi_{h}\to X\varphi\text{ weakly in }L^{p}(\Omega;{\bf R}^{m}).

Let
(u_{h})_{h}\subseteq W^{1,p}_{X^{h},\varphi_{h}}(\Omega)
, and let
u\in W^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega)
.
Assume that

(3.5)

u_{h}\to u\text{ weakly in }L^{p}(\Omega)\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003ptX^{h}u_{h}\to Xu\text{ weakly in }L^{p}(\Omega;{\bf R}^{m}).

Then
u\in W^{1,p}_{X,\varphi}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

We postpone the proof until the end of
Section

4.2
.
∎

4.
Uniform approximation by smooth functions

In this section we establish the counterpart of
Theorem

2.4
, replacing a fixed
X
-gradient with a sequence
(X^{h})_{h}
satisfying the sole assumption (
A
). In particular, our results will hold both in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and in
\mathcal{S}_{2}
. Moreover, we provide a similar characterization for affine Sobolev spaces, and finally we extend the previous considerations to the anisotropic
BV
setting.
Fix a sequence
(X^{h})_{h}
satisfying (
A
).
In particular, there exists a function
\sigma:[0,\infty)\longrightarrow(0,\infty)
such that

(4.1)

\sup\{|(\mathcal{C}^{h}(x)-\mathcal{C}(x))\xi|\,:\,x\in\Omega,\,\xi\in{\bf R}^{n},\,|\xi|=1\}\leqslant\sigma(h)^{n+2}

for any
h\in{\bf N}
and
\lim_{h\to\infty}\sigma(h)=0.

Exploiting (
4.1
), we introduce a particular sequence of mollifiers which is tailored to our setting. Precisely, Let
J\in\mathbf{C}^{\infty}_{c}({\bf R}^{n})
be a standard spherically symmetric mollifier. We define the family of mollifiers
(J_{h})_{h}
by letting

(4.2)

J_{h}(x)=\frac{1}{\sigma(h)^{n}}J\left(\frac{x}{\sigma(h)}\right)

for any
x\in{\bf R}^{n}
and any
h\in{\bf N}
. Clearly, there is a constant
C(J,n)>0
, depending only on
J
and
n
, such that

(4.3)

|DJ_{h}(x)|\leqslant\frac{C(J,n)}{\sigma(h)^{n+1}}

for any
x\in{\bf R}^{n}
and any
h\in{\bf N}
.

4.1.
A uniform Meyers-Serrin type approximation

The following lemma constitute the technical core of this section.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(X^{h})_{h}
satisfy (
A
). Let
1\leqslant p<\infty
,
u\in L^{p}(\Omega)
and
(J_{h})_{h}
be as in (
4.2
). Then

\lim_{h\to\infty}\|X^{h}(J_{h}\ast u)-X(J_{h}\ast u)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;{\bf R}^{m})}=0.

Proof.

Notice that, in view of (
4.1
), (
4.3
) and Jensen’s inequality,

\begin{split}\|X^{h}(J_{h}\ast u)-X&(J_{h}\ast u)\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega;{\bf R}^{m})}=\int_{\Omega}|X^{h}(J_{h}\ast u)-X(J_{h}\ast u)|^{p}\,{\rm d}x\\
&=\int_{\Omega}|(\mathcal{C}^{h}-\mathcal{C})D(J_{h}\ast u)|^{p}\,{\rm d}x\\
&\overset{\eqref{modulocontmatrici}}{\leqslant}\sigma(h)^{p(n+2)}\int_{\Omega}|D(J_{h}\ast u)|^{p}\,{\rm d}x\\
&=\sigma(h)^{p(n+2)}\int_{\Omega}|(DJ_{h}\ast u)|^{p}\,{\rm d}x\\
&=\sigma(h)^{p(n+2)}\int_{\Omega}\left|\int_{B(x,\sigma(h))}DJ_{h}(x-y)u(y)\,{\rm d}y\right|^{p}\,{\rm d}x\\
&=(\omega_{n})^{p}\sigma(h)^{p(2n+2)}\int_{\Omega}\left|{\mathchoice{\kern 4.30554pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.4pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\kern-9.7pt}{\kern 4.30554pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.4pt,width=4.3pt,depth=0.0pt}\kern-7.0pt}{}{}}\int_{B(x,\sigma(h))}DJ_{h}(x-y)u(y)\,{\rm d}y\right|^{p}\,{\rm d}x\\
&\leqslant(\omega_{n})^{p-1}\sigma(h)^{p(2n+2)-n}\int_{\Omega}\int_{B(x,\sigma(h))}|DJ_{h}(x-y)u(y)|^{p}\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x\\
&\overset{\eqref{gradmollifgiusto}}{\leqslant}C(J,n)^{p}(\omega_{n})^{p-1}\sigma(h)^{p(2n+2)-n-p(n+1)}\int_{\Omega}\int_{B(x,\sigma(h))}|u(y)|^{p}\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}x\\
&\leqslant|\Omega|\,C(J,n)^{p}(\omega_{n})^{p-1}\sigma(h)^{p(2n+2)-n-p(n+1)}\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)},\\
\end{split}

where
\omega_{n}
is the Lebesgue measure of the Euclidean unit ball of
{\bf R}^{n}
. Since

(4.4)

p(2n+2)-n-p(n+1)=n(p-1)+p\geq p>0,

the thesis follows.
∎

In view of
Lemma

4.1
, we prove the following Meyers-Serrin type approximation result.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
(X^{h})_{h}
satisfy (
A
). Let
1\leqslant p<\infty
. Let
u\in W^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega)
. Then there exists a sequence
(u_{h})_{h}\subseteq\mathbf{C}^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_{X^{h}}(\Omega)
such that

\text{$u_{h}\to u$ strongly in $L^{p}(\Omega)$}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{$X^{h}u_{h}\to Xu$ strongly in $L^{p}(\Omega;{\bf R}^{m})$}.

Proof.

Let
u\in W^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega)
, and fix an open set
\tilde{\Omega}\Subset\Omega
. For any
h\in{\bf N}
, define
u_{h}=J_{h}\ast u
, being
(J_{h})_{h}
as in (
4.2
).
First,
[
20
, Proposition 1.2.2]
implies that
u_{h}\to u
strongly in
L^{p}(\tilde{\Omega})
and
Xu_{h}\to Xu
strongly in
L^{p}(\tilde{\Omega},{\bf R}^{m})
.
Exploiting
Lemma

4.1
, we infer that
X^{h}u_{h}\to Xu
strongly in
L^{p}(\tilde{\Omega},{\bf R}^{m})
.
The global statement follows
verbatim
as in the proof of
[
22
, Theorem A.2]
.
∎

4.2.
A uniform characterization of affine Sobolev spaces

In this section we provide an affine counterpart of
Theorem

4.2
. To this aim,
for
1\leqslant p<\infty
and
\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega)
, set

\begin{split}W^{1,p}_{X,\varphi}&(\Omega,(X^{h})_{h})\coloneqq\{u\in W^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega)\,:\,\text{ there exists }(v_{h})_{h}\subseteq\mathbf{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)\text{ such that }\\
&\quad v_{h}\to u-\varphi\text{ strongly in }L^{p}(\Omega)\text{ and }X^{h}v_{h}\to X(u-\varphi)\text{ strongly in }L^{p}(\Omega;{\bf R}^{m})\}.\end{split}

This affine space depends
a priori
on
(X^{h})_{h}
. However, the following characterization holds.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(X^{h})_{h}
satisfy (
A
). Let
1\leqslant p<\infty
. Let
\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega)
. Then

W^{1,p}_{X,\varphi}(\Omega)=W^{1,p}_{X,\varphi}(\Omega,(X^{h})_{h}).

Proof.

It suffices to consider the case
\varphi=0
. Fix
u\in W^{1,p}_{X,0}(\Omega,(X^{h})_{h})
. By definition, there exists a sequence
(u_{h})_{h}\subseteq\mathbf{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
such that

(4.5)

\text{$u_{h}\to u$ strongly in $L^{p}(\Omega)$}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{$X^{h}u_{h}\to Xu$ strongly in $L^{p}(\Omega;{\bf R}^{m})$}.

Let
(J_{h})_{h}
be the sequence of mollifiers defined by (
4.2
). Since
\supp u_{h}\Subset\Omega
for any
h\in{\bf N}
, up to consider a subsequence of
(J_{h})_{h}
we may assume that
\supp u_{h}+\supp J_{h}\Subset\Omega
and
\sigma(h)\leqslant 1
for any
h\in{\bf N}
, where
\sigma
is the modulus of continuity introduced in (
4.1
). For any
h\in{\bf N}
, set
v_{h}=J_{h}\ast u_{h}
. Then
(v_{h})_{h}\subseteq\mathbf{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
. Owing to
[
20
, Proposition 1.2.2]
, we can select a further subsequence of
(J_{h})_{h}
such that

(4.6)

\|u_{h}-v_{h}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leqslant\frac{1}{h}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\|X^{h}u_{h}-X^{h}v_{h}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;{\bf R}^{m})}\leqslant\frac{1}{h}

for any
h\in{\bf N}
.
Moreover, arguing as in the proof of
Lemma

4.1
,

(4.7)

\|X^{h}v_{h}-Xv_{h}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega;{\bf R}^{m})}\leqslant|\Omega|\,C(J,n)^{p}(\omega_{n})^{p-1}\sigma(h)^{p(2n+2)-n-p(n+1)}\|u_{h}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

By (
4.4
), (
4.5
), (
4.6
) and (
4.7
),
v_{h}\to u
strongly in
L^{p}(\Omega)
and
Xv_{h}\to Xu
strongly in
L^{p}(\Omega;{\bf R}^{m})
, that is
u\in W^{1,p}_{X,0}(\Omega)
. Since the converse implication
follows
verbatim
, the thesis follows.
∎

With a similar argument, we provide the proof of
Proposition

3.10
.

Proof of
Proposition

3.10
.

Fix
h\in{\bf N}
. Since
u_{h}\in W^{1,p}_{X^{h},\varphi_{h}}(\Omega)
, there exists
v_{h}\in\mathbf{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
such that

(4.8)

\|v_{h}-(u_{h}-\varphi_{h})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leqslant\frac{1}{h}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\|X^{h}v_{h}-X^{h}(u_{h}-\varphi_{h})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;{\bf R}^{m})}\leqslant\frac{1}{h}.

Let
(J_{h})_{h}
be the sequence of mollifiers defined by (
4.2
). As in the previous proof, we may assume that
\supp v_{h}+\supp J_{h}\Subset\Omega
and
\sigma(h)\leqslant 1
for any
h\in{\bf N}
, where
\sigma
is the modulus of continuity introduced in (
4.1
). For any
h\in{\bf N}
, set
\tilde{v}_{h}=J_{h}\ast v_{h}
. Then
(\tilde{v}_{h})_{h}\subseteq\mathbf{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
. Again by
[
20
, Proposition 1.2.2]
, up to a further subsequence of
(J_{h})_{h}
,

(4.9)

\|v_{h}-\tilde{v}_{h}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leqslant\frac{1}{h}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\|X^{h}v_{h}-X^{h}\tilde{v}_{h}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;{\bf R}^{m})}\leqslant\frac{1}{h}

for any

.
Again, arguing as in

4.1
,

(4.10)

\|X^{h}\tilde{v}_{h}-X\tilde{v}_{h}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega;{\bf R}^{m})}\leqslant|\Omega|\,C(J,n)^{p}(\omega_{n})^{p-1}\sigma(h)^{p(2n+2)-n-p(n+1)}\|v_{h}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

By (
3.4
) and (

be the Hölder-conjugate exponent of

\end{split}

\begin{split}\left|\int_{\Omega}\langle X\tilde{v}_{h}-X(u-\varphi),\psi\rangle_{{\bf R}^{m}}\,{\rm d}x\right|&\leqslant\int_{\Omega}\left|\langle X\tilde{v}_{h}-X^{h}\tilde{v}_{h},\psi\rangle_{{\bf R}^{m}}\right|\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}\left|\langle X^{h}\tilde{v}_{h}-X^{h}v_{h},\psi\rangle_{{\bf R}^{m}}\right|\,{\rm d}x\\

Combining (

), (

) and (

weakly in

Although beyond the scopes of this paper, for the sake of completeness we provide an Anzellotti-Giaquinta approximation result in the case of

Again, the key of the proof is encoded in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(X^{h})_{h}
satisfy (

\lim_{h\to\infty}\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}u\divv\left(\left((\mathcal{C}^{h})^{T}\varphi\right)\ast J_{h}-\mathcal{C}^{T}(\varphi\ast J_{h})\right){\rm d}x\,:\,\varphi\in\mathbf{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega;{\bf R}^{n}),\,\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1\right\}=0.

Let

be as in the statement, and set

\begin{split}S&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}u\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left[\left((\mathcal{C}^{h})^{T}\varphi\right)_{i}\ast J_{h}-\left(\mathcal{C}^{T}(\varphi\ast J_{h})\right)_{i}\right]{\rm d}x\\

In this section we prove the counterparts of

Example 5.1

For any

6.
\Gamma
-convergence for functionals depending on moving anisotropies

In this section we establish several
\Gamma
-convergence properties for sequences of integral functionals depending on a sequence
(X^{h})_{h}
belonging to either
\mathcal{S}_{1}
or
\mathcal{S}_{2}
.
Let us recall some basic preliminaries. For a complete account to
\Gamma
-convergence, as well as for the vocabulary of local functionals, we refer the reader to
[
3
,
13
]
.
We just recall that if
(\mathcal{X},\tau)
is a first-countable topological space, a sequence of functionals
(F_{h})_{h}:\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow[0,\infty]
is said to
\Gamma(\tau)
-converge
to a functional
F:\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow[0,\infty]
if the following two conditions hold.

•

For any
u\in\mathcal{X}
and any sequence
(u_{h})_{h}
converging to
u
in
\tau
, then

(liminf)

F(u)\leqslant\liminf_{h\to\infty}F_{h}(u_{h}).

•

For any
u\in\mathcal{X}
, there exists a sequence
(u_{h})_{h}
converging to
u
in
\tau
such that

(limsup)

F(u)\geqslant\limsup_{h\to\infty}F_{h}(u_{h}).

Sequences for which (
limsup
) holds are known as
recovery sequences
.
Equivalently, defining the
\Gamma
-lower limit
and
\Gamma
-upper limit
respectively by

F^{\prime}(u)\coloneqq\Gamma-\liminf_{h\to\infty}F_{h}(u)\coloneqq\inf\left\{\liminf_{h\to\infty}F_{h}(u_{h})\,:\,u_{h}\to u\text{ in }\tau\right\}

and

F^{\prime\prime}(u)\coloneqq\Gamma-\limsup_{h\to\infty}F_{h}(u)\coloneqq\inf\left\{\limsup_{h\to\infty}F_{h}(u_{h})\,:\,u_{h}\to u\text{ in }\tau\right\},

(F_{h})_{h}

\Gamma
-converges to
F:(X,\tau)\longrightarrow[0,\infty]
if and only if

\Gamma-\liminf_{h\to\infty}F_{h}(u)=\Gamma-\limsup_{h\to\infty}F_{h}(u)=F(u)

for any
u\in X
. We say that
F
is the
\Gamma-
limit of
(F_{h})_{h}
and we write
F=\Gamma-\lim_{h\to\infty}F_{h}
.
Throughout this section, we fix a sequence
(X^{h})_{h}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{1}\cup\mathcal{S}_{2}
, an open and bounded set
\Omega\subseteq{\bf R}^{n}
and
1<p<\infty
. The wider class of functionals we are interested in is made of (local) integral functionals of the form

\displaystyle F_{h}(u,A)\coloneqq\displaystyle{\begin{cases}\int_{\Omega}f_{h}(x,X^{h}u(x)){\rm d}x&\text{ if }A\in\mathcal{A},\,u\in L^{p}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_{X^{h}}(A),\\
\infty&\text{ otherwise in $L^{p}(\Omega)$},\end{cases}}

where each integrand
f_{h}
belongs to the following class.

Definition 6.1
.

Fix
h\in{\bf N}
.
\mathcal{I}^{h}_{p}(a,d_{1},d_{2})
is the class of functions
f_{h}:\,\Omega\times{\bf R}^{m}\to{\bf R}
such that:

(I_{1})

f_{h}:\,\Omega\times{\bf R}^{m}\to{\bf R}
is a Carathéodory function;

(I_{2})

for a.e.
x\in\Omega
, the function
f_{h}(x,\cdot):\,{\bf R}^{m}\to{\bf R}
is convex;

(I_{3})

there exist two constants
0<d_{1}\leqslant\,d_{2}
and a non-negative function
a\in L^{1}(\Omega)
such that

(6.1)

d_{1}\,|\mathcal{C}^{h}(x)\xi|^{p}\leqslant f_{h}(x,\mathcal{C}^{h}(x)\xi)\leqslant a(x)+d_{2}\left|\mathcal{C}^{h}(x)\xi\right|^{p}

for a.e.
x\in\Omega
and for every
\xi\in{\bf R}^{n}
.

Remark 6.2
.

If
F_{h}
is the (local) integral functional associated with some
f_{h}\in\mathcal{I}^{h}_{p}(a,d_{1},d_{2})
, then
[
40
, Theorem 4.2]
implies that

(6.2)

d_{1}\|X^{h}u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(A)}\leqslant F_{h}(u,A)\leqslant\int_{A}a(x)\,{\rm d}x+d_{2}\|X^{h}u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(A)}

for any
A\in\mathcal{A}
and any
u\in W^{1,p}_{X^{h}}(A)
.

6.1.
\Gamma
-convergence of norms

Our first result focuses on the special case of norms.
Theorem

6.3
will be propaedeutic to the proof of the main compactness theorem,
Theorem

6.5
.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
(X^{h})_{h}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\cup\mathcal{S}_{2}
. Let

Remark 6.6
.

Although reminiscent of the approach presented in
[
13
]
, ours differs from the former.
Indeed, the classical approach to
\Gamma
-compactness consists of three main steps.

(1)

Given a sequence of integral functionals

, extract a subsequence, say again
(F_{h})_{h}
which

-converges to a local functional

, where
\bar{\Gamma}
-convergence (cf.
[
13
, Chapter 16]
) is a variational convergence tailored to local functionals.

(2)

Upgrade
\bar{\Gamma}
-convergence to
\Gamma
-convergence.

(3)

Show that

is an integral functional.

The proof of (2) (cf. e.g.
[

, Proposition 18.6]
) relies on uniform growth conditions like

for a suitable lower-semicontinuous measure

.
On the other hand, we can only dispose of non-uniform growth conditions of the form

Nevertheless,
Theorem

6.3
ensures that
(\tilde{G}_{h})_{h}

\Gamma
-converges. We will show that this weaker framework will be still sufficient to promote
\bar{\Gamma}
-convergence to
\Gamma
-convergence. In addition, once
\Gamma
-convergence is achieved, in order to conclude we will need to apply the anisotropic integral representation result
[
40
, Theorem 4.1]
.

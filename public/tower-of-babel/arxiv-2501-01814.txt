1.
Introduction

In this paper
\mathbb{D}
is the unit disk in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{B}
is the unit ball in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By
\mathbb{S}
we denote the unit sphere.

1.1.
Hardy class

Let
p>0
and

\mathbf{h}^{p}(\mathbb{B})=\{f:\mathbb{B}\to\mathbb{R}^{n},\text{so that }\|f\|_{p}=\sup_{0<r<1}M_{p}(r,f)<\infty\},

where

M_{p}(r,f)=\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}}|f(r\zeta)|^{p}d\sigma(\zeta)\right)^{1/p}.

Here
\sigma
is the standard normalized area measure of the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}
. Now define for
x>0
,
\log^{+}(x):=\max\{\log x,0\}
. Then define the space
\mathbf{h}\log^{+}\mathbf{h}
of functions defined in the unit ball so that

\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{\mathbb{S}}|f(r\zeta)|\log^{+}|f(r\zeta)|d\sigma(\zeta)<\infty.

1.2.
Motivation

We formulate the following Zygmund theorem
[
14
, p. 254]
, which is the motivation for our study.
Assume that
f=u+iv
is a holomorphic function defined in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
such that
v(0)=0
. Assume further that
|u|\log^{+}|u|\in\mathbf{h}^{1}(\mathbb{D})
. We write it shortly by
u\in\mathbf{h}\log^{+}\mathbf{h}
. Then
f\in\mathbf{h}^{1}
and we have the inequality

(1.1)

\|f\|_{1}\leq A(\||u|\log^{+}|u|\|_{1}+1),

where
A
is an absolute constant. It is well-known that the condition
u\in\mathbf{h}^{1}(\mathbb{D})
does not imply that
f\in\mathbf{h}^{1}(\mathbb{D})
, so (
1.1
) is in some sense the best possible inequality
[
2
, p. 60]
. On the other hand for
p>1
,
u\in\mathbf{h}^{p}(\mathbb{D})
imply that
f\in\mathbf{h}^{p}(\mathbb{D})
and moreover there is a constant
c_{p}
so that
\|f\|_{p}\leq c_{p}\|u\|_{p}
whenever
\mathrm{Im}f(0)=0
. This is a well-known M. Riesz theorem. For some recent extensions of M. Riesz theorem we refer to the papers
[
5
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
1
,
6
,
7
,
4
]
.

The sharpness of constant
A
in Zygmund theorem as well as its connection with M. Riesz theorem is discussed by Pichorides in
[
13
]
.

This paper aims to extend Zygmund theorem to the class of quasiregular harmonic mappings in the unit disk and in the unit ball.

1.3.
Quasiregular and harmonic mappings

A continuous and nonconstant mapping
f:G\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geqslant 2
, in the local Sobolev space
W^{1,n}_{loc}(G,\mathbb{R}^{n})
is

K
-quasiregular,
K\geqslant 1
, if

|Df(x)|\leq K\ell(f^{\prime}(x))

for almost
every
x\in G
, where
G
is an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Here
Df(x)
is the formal differential matrix and

|Df(x)|=\sup_{|h|=1}|f^{\prime}(x)h|,\ \ \ell(Df(x))=\inf_{|h|=1}|f^{\prime}(x)h|.

Here and in the sequel
|\zeta|
is the Euclidean norm of a vector
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Further we denote by
\left<\zeta,\xi\right>
the inner product of vectors
\zeta,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

A smooth mapping
w:G\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called harmonic if it satisfies the Laplace equation
\Delta u=0
.
The solution of the equation
\Delta w=g
(in the sense of
distributions see
[
3
]
) in the ball
B_{R}=R\cdot\mathbb{B}
, satisfying the
boundary condition
w|_{S_{R}}=f\in L^{1}(S_{R})
, where
S_{R}=R\cdot\mathbb{S}
is given by

(1.2)

w(x)=\int_{S_{R}}P(x,\eta)f(\eta)d\sigma(\eta)-\int_{B_{R}}G(x,y)g(y)dV(y),\,|x|<1.

Here

(1.3)

P(x,\eta)=\frac{R^{2}-|x|^{2}}{R|x-\eta|^{n}}

is the Poisson kernel and
d\sigma
is the surface
n-1
dimensional
measure of the Euclidean sphere which satisfies the condition:

\int_{\mathbb{S}}P(x,\eta)d\sigma(\eta)\equiv 1
. The first integral
in (
1.2
) is called the Poisson integral and is usually
denoted by
u(x)=P[f](x)
. Then
u
is a harmonic mapping. The function

(1.4)

G(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\log\frac{R|x-y|}{|R^{2}-x\overline{y}|},&\hbox{for $n=2$;}\\
c_{n}\left(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-2}}-\frac{1}{(R^{2}+|x|^{2}|y|^{2}/R^{2}-2\left<x,y\right>)^{(n-2)/2}}\right),&\hbox{for $n\geqslant 3$,}\end{array}\right.

where

(1.5)

c_{n}=\frac{1}{(n-2)\omega_{n-1}}

and

\omega_{n-1}
is the measure of
\mathbb{S}
, is the Green function of
the ball
B_{R}
.

If
n=2
, then we use simultaneously the notation of
k-
quasiregular mappings for
k=(K-1)/(K+1)
.
If
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
is harmonic mapping, then there exist two holomorphic functions
g
and
h
(
h(0)=0
), in the unit disk, so that
f(z)=g(z)+\overline{h(z)}
. Then
f
is sense-preserving and
k-
quasiregular, if and only if
|h^{\prime}(z)|\leq k|g^{\prime}(z)|
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
.

1.4.
Main results

The following three theorems are main results.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f=g+\bar{h}
be a
K-
quasiregular mapping defined in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
such that
\mathrm{Im}\,f(0)=0
. Assume further that
u=\mathrm{Re}\,f\in\mathbf{h}\log^{+}\mathbf{h}
. Then
f\in\mathbf{h}^{1}
and we have the inequality

(1.6)

\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{it})|\frac{dt}{2\pi}\leq C_{0}(K)\left(1+\int_{0}^{2\pi}|u(re^{it})|\log^{+}|u(re^{it})|\frac{dt}{2\pi}\right)

for every
r\in(0,1)
and in particular

(1.7)

\|f\|_{1}\leq C_{0}(K)\left(\|u\log^{+}u\|_{1}+1\right).

For a quasiregular mapping with a positive real part, we have the following asymptotically sharp result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
f=u+iv
is a harmonic
K-
quasiregular function defined in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
so that
v(0)=0
and assume further that
u(z)>0
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
. If
u\in\mathbf{h}\log^{+}\mathbf{h}
, then
f\in\mathbf{h}^{1}
and we have the inequality

(1.8)

\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{it})|\frac{dt}{2\pi}\leq K^{2}\left(e^{-1+K^{-2}}+\int_{0}^{2\pi}u(re^{it})\log u(re^{it})\frac{dt}{2\pi}\right)

for every
r\in(0,1)
and in particular

(1.9)

\|f\|_{1}\leq K^{2}\left(e^{-1+K^{-2}}+\int_{\mathbb{T}}u\log u\frac{|dz|}{2\pi}\right)

and

\|v\|_{1}\leq\|f\|_{1}\leq K^{2}\left(\|u\log^{+}u\|_{1}+1\right).

The constants are asymptotically sharp when
K\to 1
.

A straightforward consequence of Theorem
1.2
is the following corollary which is interesting in its own right.

Corollary 1.3
.

If
f=u+iv
is an analytic function of the unit disk into the vertical strip
(0,1)\times\mathbb{R}
so that
v(0)=0
, then

\|f\|_{H^{1}}<1.

The constant
1
is sharp as can be seen by the following example
f(z)=\frac{n}{(n+1)},
when
n\to\infty
. Indeed, we can use any admissible function of
f
, with
f(0)=n/(n+1)
.

For higher-dimensional settings we prove

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume that
f=(f_{1},\dots,f_{n}):\mathbb{B}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a harmonic
K-
quasiregular function defined in the unit ball
\mathbb{B}
so that
u(x)=f_{1}(x)>0
for
z\in\mathbb{B}
. If
u\in\mathbf{h}\log^{+}\mathbf{h}
, then
f\in\mathbf{h}^{1}
and we have the inequality

(1.10)

\|f\|_{1}-|f(0)|\leq K^{2}(n-1)\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}}\left(u(\zeta)\log u(\zeta)-u(0)\log u(0)\right)d\sigma(\zeta)\right)

and if
|f(0)|=u(0)
, then

(1.11)

\|f\|_{1}\leq K^{2}(n-1)(e^{-1+K^{-2}/(n-1)}+\|u\log^{+}u\|_{1}).

The constant
K^{2}(n-1)
in (
1.10
) is asymptotically sharp as
K\to 1
.

2.
Proof of main results

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We lose no generality in assuming that
r=1
. Assume first that
\mathrm{Re}(f(0))=0
. Since
f(0)=0,
we can assume that
h(0)=g(0)=0
.
Note first that if
H
is an analytic function defined in the unit disk with
H(0)=0
, then we have the inequalities

(2.1)

\|H\|_{p}\leq c_{1}(p)\|G[H]\|_{p}\text{\ \ and \ \ }\|G[H]\|_{p}\leq c_{2}(p)\|H\|_{p},

where

(2.2)

G[H](z)=\left(\int_{0}^{1}|H^{\prime}(\rho z)|^{2}(1-\rho)d\rho\right)^{1/2}.

This hold due to Calderon type theorem of Pavlović
[
12
, Theorem 10.6]
. We apply this inequality for
p=1
and
H\in\{g,h,g+h\}
. Let
u=\mathrm{Re}\,(g+h)
, then
\tilde{u}=\mathrm{Im}\,(g+h)\in\mathbf{h}^{1}(\mathbb{D})
and from the classical Zygmund theorem
[
2
, p. 57]
, we have

\|\tilde{u}\|_{H^{1}}\leq A\||u|\log^{+}|u|\|_{L^{1}}+B,

where

A=1,\ \ B=6\pi e.

Since
|u|\leq 1+|u|\log^{+}|u|
, we get the following

\|g+h\|_{H^{1}}\leq\|u\|_{1}+\|\tilde{u}\|_{1}\leq 2\||u|\log^{+}|u|\|_{L^{1}}+6\pi e+1.

Since
f
is quasiregular we have

(2.3)

|g^{\prime}(z)|\leq\frac{|g^{\prime}(z)+h^{\prime}(z)|}{1-k},\ \ \ |h^{\prime}(z)|\leq\frac{k|g^{\prime}(z)+h^{\prime}(z)|}{1-k},

where
k=(K-1)/(K+1)
.
Denote the constants in (
2.1
) by
c_{1}=c_{1}(1)
and
c_{2}=c_{2}(1)
. Then by (
2.1
) and (
2.3
), we have

\|g\|_{H^{1}}\leq c_{1}\|G(g)\|_{1}\leq\frac{c_{1}}{1-k}\|G(g+h)\|_{1}\leq\frac{c_{1}\cdot c_{2}}{1-k}\|g+h\|_{H^{1}}

and

\|h\|_{H^{1}}\leq c_{1}\|G(h)\|_{1}\leq\frac{c_{1}k}{1-k}\|G(g+h)\|_{1}\leq\frac{c_{1}\cdot c_{2}k}{1-k}\|g+h\|_{H^{1}}.

Thus

\|f\|_{1}\leq(\|g\|_{H^{1}}+\|h\|_{H^{1}})\leq CK(2\||u|\log^{+}|u|\|_{L^{1}}+6\pi e+1),

where
C=c_{1}\cdot c_{2}
.

The last inequality follows from the fact that
|f|
is a subharmonic function.

Thus for
C(K)=2(6\pi e+1)c_{1}c_{2}K
we proved (
1.6
) and (
1.7
) for the case
u(0)=0
.

Assume now that
u(0)\neq 0
. Let
F(z)=f(z)-u(0)
. Then by applying the previous case we have

(2.4)

\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{it})-u(0)|\frac{dt}{2\pi}\leq C(K)\left(1+\int_{0}^{2\pi}|u(re^{it})-u(0)|\log^{+}|u(re^{it})-u(0)|\frac{dt}{2\pi}\right).

Now we use the following convex function
k(t)=t\log^{+}(t)
for
t>0
, in order to conclude that
g(z)=k(|u(z)|)=|u(z)|\log^{+}(|u(z)|)
is subharmonic. Then we get the inequality

(2.5)

g(0)=|u(0)|\log^{+}(|u(0)|)\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u(z)|\log^{+}(|u(z)|)|dz|.

Further, if
|u(0)|>1
, then

g(0)=|u(0)|\log_{+}(|u(0)|)=|u(0)|\log(|u(0)|)\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u(z)|\log^{+}(|u(z)|)|dz|,

and therefore

|u(0)|\leq e+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u(z)|\log^{+}(|u(z)|)|dz|.

Then from (
2.5
), (
2.4
) and Lemma
2.1
below we have

\begin{split}\|f\|_{1}&\leq|u(0)|+\|f-u(0)\|_{1}\\
&\leq e+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u(z)|\log^{+}(|u(z)|)|dz|\\
&+C(K)\left(1+\int_{0}^{2\pi}|u(re^{it})-u(0)|\log^{+}|u(re^{it})-u(0)|\frac{dt}{2\pi}\right)\\
&\leq e+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u(z)|\log^{+}(|u(z)|)|dz|\\
&+C(K)\left(1+4/e+2|u(0)|\log^{+}|u(0)|+\frac{2}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u(z)|\log^{+}(|u(z)|)|dz|\right)\\
&\leq(1+4C(K))\left(1+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u(z)|\log^{+}(|u(z)|)|dz|\right).\end{split}

In the last inequality, we applied the bound

\left(c+\frac{4c}{e}+e\right)+(1+4c)X<(1+4c)(1+X),

which holds trivially under the assumption that
c=C(K)>2
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
, modulo the following lemma.
∎

Lemma 2.1
.

For every real
a
and
x
we have

f(a,x):=|a+x|\max(0,\log|a+x|)-2\left(|a|\max(0,\log|a|)+|x|\max(0,\log|x|)\right)\leq 4/e.

Proof.

Assume without loosing the generality that
a+x\geqslant 0
and
x\geqslant 0
. Then

f(a,x)=\begin{cases}(a+x)\log(a+x)-2\left(a\log(a)+x\log(x)\right),&\text{if }a>0,\,a+x>1,\,x>1,\\
(a+x)\log(a+x)-2\left(a\log(a)\right),&\text{if }a>0,\,a+x>1,\,x\leq 1,\\
-2\left(a\log(a)+x\log(x)\right),&\text{if }a>0,\,a+x\leq 1,\,x>1,\\
-2\left(a\log(a)\right),&\text{if }a>0,\,a+x\leq 1,\,x\leq 1,\\
(a+x)\log(a+x)-2\left(x\log(x)\right),&\text{if }a<0,\,a+x>1,\,x>1,\\
(a+x)\log(a+x),&\text{if }a<0,\,a+x>1,\,x\leq 1,\\
-2\left(x\log(x)\right),&\text{if }a<0,\,a+x\leq 1,\,x>1,\\
0,&\text{if }a<0,\,a+x\leq 1,\,x\leq 1.\end{cases}

Then for
a>0,\,a+x>1,\,x>1
, consider

g(x)=(a+x)\log(a+x)-2\left(a\log a+x\log x\right).

For
x=y-a
, we define:

h(y)=g(x)=y\log y-2\left(a\log a+(-a+y)\log(-a+y)\right).

The derivative of
h(y)
is:

h^{\prime}(y)=-2(\log a-\log(-a+y)),

and setting
h^{\prime}(y)=0
gives the condition
a=y/2
.

Substituting this value into
h(y)
, we obtain:

h(y)=y\log 4-y\log y.

Then the maximum of this expression is for
y=4/e
. Then
h(4/e)=4/e
and this is maximum of
g
. Similarly we consider the other cases.
∎

To prove Theorem
1.2
, we need the following lemma

Lemma 2.2
.

If
f=u+iv=g+\bar{h}
is quasiregular, then

\Delta|f|\leq K^{2}\Delta(u\log u)

provided that
u
is positive.

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

Since
u=\mathrm{Re}\,(g+h)
, it follows that
|\nabla u|=|g^{\prime}+h^{\prime}|
. Further

|f|=((g+\bar{h})(\bar{g}+h))^{1/2}.

Thus

|f|_{z}=\frac{1}{2}((g+\bar{h})(\bar{g}+h))^{1/2-1}(g^{\prime}(\bar{g}+h)+(g+\bar{h})h^{\prime})

and

|f|_{z\bar{z}}=-\frac{1}{4}((g+\bar{h})(\bar{g}+h))^{1/2-2}|g^{\prime}(\bar{g}+h)+(g+\bar{h})h^{\prime}|^{2}

+\frac{1}{2}((g+\bar{h})(\bar{g}+h))^{1/2-1}(|g^{\prime}|^{2}+|h^{\prime}|^{2}).

Then, after some simple manipulations we get

\Delta|f|=4|f|_{z\bar{z}}=\frac{|g^{\prime}-h^{\prime}{f}/{\bar{f}}|^{2}}{|f|}.

Further

\Delta(u\log u)=\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}=\frac{|g^{\prime}+h^{\prime}|^{2}}{u}.

Then

\frac{\Delta|f|}{\Delta(u\log u)}=\frac{|g^{\prime}-\frac{f^{2}}{|f|^{2}}h^{\prime}|^{2}}{|g^{\prime}+h^{\prime}|^{2}}\frac{u}{|f|}\leq\frac{(|g^{\prime}|+|h^{\prime}|)^{2}}{(|g^{\prime}|-|h^{\prime}|)^{2}}\frac{u}{|f|}\leq K^{2}.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By (
1.2
), we have

|f(0)|=\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f(rz)|\frac{|dz|}{2\pi}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|z|<r}\Delta|f(z)|\log\frac{r}{|z|}dxdy.

Let
\mathbb{D}_{r}=\{z:|z|<r\}
.
Now, because
|f(0)|=u(0)
, and using again Proposition

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|f(rz)|\frac{|dz|}{2\pi}&=u(0)+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}\Delta|f(z)|\log\frac{r}{|z|}dxdy\\
&\leq u(0)+K^{2}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}\Delta(u(z)\log u(z))\log\frac{r}{|z|}dxdy\\
&=u(0)+K^{2}\left(-u(0)\log u(0)+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}u(rz)\log u(rz)|dz|\right)\\
&=\Phi(u(0))+K^{2}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}u(rz)\log u(rz)|dz|,\end{split}

where

Then

, which is equal to
K^{2}e^{-1+1/K^{2}}.
This implies (

for

.

Further by straightforward computation (see e.g.

.

Prove now the sharpness part.

Let

,

C_{n}=\frac{\Gamma\left[\frac{n}{2}\right]}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma\left[\frac{1}{2}(-1+n)\right]}
we have

X(f)=C_{n}\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{n-2}t\left(\sqrt{1+a^{2}+2a\cos t}-1\right)dt

and

Y(f)=C_{n}\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{n-2}t(1+a\cos t)\log(1+a\cos t)dt.

Since

\left(\sqrt{1+a^{2}+2a\cos t}-1\right)=a\cos(t)+\frac{a^{2}}{2}\sin^{2}t+o(a^{2})

and

(1+a\cos t)\log(1+a\cos t)=a\cos t+\frac{a^{2}}{2}\cos^{2}t+o(a^{2}),

we get

X(f)=C_{n}\frac{a^{2}\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma\left[\frac{1+n}{2}\right]}{2\Gamma\left[1+\frac{n}{2}\right]}+o(a^{2})=\frac{a^{2}(n-1)}{2n}+o(a^{2})

Y(f)=C_{n}\frac{a^{2}\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma\left[\frac{1}{2}(-1+n)\right]}{4\Gamma\left[1+\frac{n}{2}\right]}+o(a^{2})=\frac{a^{2}}{2n}+o(a^{2}).

Then we obtain

\lim_{a\to 0}\frac{X(f)}{Y(f)}=n-1.

Hence the constant
n-1
is the best possible for the case
K=1
.

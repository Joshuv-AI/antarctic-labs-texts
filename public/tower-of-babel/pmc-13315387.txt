Eye Loss, Orbital Sequelae, and Prosthetic Rehabilitation Following Crowd-Control Kinetic Impact Projectile Injuries: The Largest Civilian Cohort
Authors: Sebastián Córdova, Miguel Ángel Campos, Carmen Torres, Patricio Bustamante, Eric Tapia, Joaquín Varas, Macarena Mesa, Cesar Silva, José Luis Sanhueza
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionDuring the 2019–2020 Chilean social unrest, massive police use of kinetic impact projectiles (KIPs) caused unprecedented severe ocular traumas, initially affecting over 460 individuals. We examined the largest civilian cohort with complete globe loss to characterize orbital/peri-orbital sequelae, analyze projectile-specific injury patterns, and assess factors affecting rehabilitation.MethodsThis retrospective cohort evaluated 57 individuals requiring ocular prostheses at Chile’s national referral center (2019–2022). Sociodemographic, clinical, and prosthetic variables were analyzed, encompassing immediate and delayed globe loss (secondary evisceration/phthisis bulbi). Sequelae count associations were assessed via the Kruskal–Wallis test.ResultsKIPs caused 94.7% of irreversible globe losses (pellets 73.7%, a tear-gas projectile 21.1%, blunt trauma 5.3%). A tear-gas projectile was significantly associated with a higher number of concurrent orbital/peri-orbital sequelae per individual (p = 0.002). Prevalent sequelae included palpebral scarring (43.9%), ptosis (26.3%), and orbital fractures (22.8%). Prior conformer shell installation correlated with significantly more sequelae (p = 0.002). This high sequelae burden also correlated with initial prosthetic complications, specifically rotation and intense mucoid discharge (p = 0.011). Overall prosthetic adherence reached 93.0%; notably, 17.5% of users selected non-mimetic (novelty) prostheses.ConclusionsDuring the repression of the 2019–2020 protests, “non-lethal” KIPs proved highly destructive. Beyond the globe loss itself, a tear-gas projectile was significantly associated with a high number of concurrent sequelae, uniquely causing widespread trauma, including maxillofacial fractures. Conversely, pellets—the most frequent KIP—frequently resulted in isolated globe loss but caused specific severe outcomes, including all cases of anophthalmic cavity syndrome and persistent conjunctivitis, alongside most ptosis. Complete globe loss occurred immediately or following a prolonged trajectory. High prosthetic adherence (93.0%) was achieved by offering non-mimetic designs when conventional prostheses were refused, preserving orbital integrity and addressing psychosocial needs. These findings reinforce the global need for multidisciplinary rehabilitation and provide medical evidence to demand the strict regulation of these weapons.

Introduction

During the Chilean social unrest that began in October 2019, the widespread use of crowd-control weapons, particularly kinetic impact projectiles (KIPs), led to an unprecedented number of severe ocular trauma (SOT) cases, including complete globe loss, with no comparable precedent in the scientific literature [1, 2]. Between October and November 2019, 259 individuals with ocular trauma were treated at the Ocular Trauma Unit (UTO) of Hospital del Salvador, Chile’s national referral center for ocular injuries [2]. By March 2020, the National Institute of Human Rights (INDH) had reported 460 cases of ocular trauma, 35 of which involved complete ocular loss or rupture [3]. In response to this crisis, the Chilean Ministry of Health (MINSAL) implemented the Comprehensive Ocular Repair Plan (PIRO) in December 2019, later reorganized in 2022 as the Plan for Accompaniment and Care of Ocular Trauma Victims (PACTO), which integrated 399 individuals with partial or total vision loss and/or severe orbital and facial injuries [4, 5]. Although victims initially received emergency care under the PIRO program starting in 2019, all individuals in this cohort completed their long-term prosthetic adaptation under the standardized protocols of the PACTO program, established in 2022. Within this program, customized polymethylmethacrylate (PMMA) ocular prostheses were designed by maxillofacial prosthodontists, and, when required, conformer shells were fitted to preserve socket stability and prepare for rehabilitation. Successful ocular prosthetic rehabilitation after SOT depends largely on the extent of orbital and peri-orbital sequelae, including palpebral scarring, ptosis, lagophthalmos, chronic pain, and persistent conjunctivitis. However, national clinical guidelines from the MINSAL do not address long-term rehabilitation following ocular trauma [6], leaving many individuals to self-finance their prosthetic care. This often results in the use of generic prostheses lacking individual adjustment, which compromises adaptation, comfort, and aesthetics. Previous research has shown that customized prostheses significantly improve functionality and quality of life compared with standardized devices [7].

Although ocular trauma caused by crowd-control weapons has been described in international literature, the specific orbital and peri-orbital sequelae associated with KIP-related injuries—and their implications for prosthetic rehabilitation—have not been systematically characterized. This represents a major gap in the evidence on long-term outcomes following globe loss due to these projectiles.

This analysis also included individuals with severe ocular atrophy or phthisis bulbi, as well as those who underwent secondary evisceration after failed attempts to preserve the globe, identified through longitudinal review of clinical records. In several cases, initial preservation was attempted to reduce the immediate psychological impact of anophthalmia, although progressive anatomical deterioration later required surgical removal. Including these cases allowed the full spectrum of both immediate and delayed ocular damage to be captured, providing insight into the chronic degenerative processes and rehabilitation challenges that may follow the initial injury.

This study therefore examines the largest documented civilian cohort of individuals who suffered complete ocular loss due to the repression of the Chilean social unrest and subsequently required an ocular prosthesis. The primary aim is to characterize their sociodemographic, clinical, and prosthetic profiles, analyze injury patterns associated with the massive use of KIPs, and assess the specific variables that affect prosthetic rehabilitation. This analysis focuses on the largest nationally coordinated civilian cohort to date, derived from a centralized national referral and rehabilitation program, allowing for an in-depth understanding of injury patterns and prosthetic trajectories across diverse clinical profiles. Furthermore, it is important to distinguish this study from previous reports on the acute phase of the crisis [2]. While prior research characterized the immediate epidemiology of 259 victims arriving at the national referral center during the first weeks of the unrest, the present study analyses a distinct, longitudinal sub-cohort. Specifically, it focuses on those who suffered complete globe loss and entered the long-term PACTO prosthetic rehabilitation program between 2019 and 2022. This focus allows for the assessment of established orbital sequelae and prosthetic adaptation, distinct from initial acute trauma management.

Methods

Study Design and Setting

This retrospective observational cohort study analyzed clinical records of individuals who received ocular prostheses under the national PACTO program at Hospital del Salvador, Santiago, Chile, between November 2019 and March 2022.

Participants

The inclusion criteria were: (1) individuals who underwent ocular prosthetic rehabilitation following complete globe loss (enucleation or evisceration) or severe ocular atrophy (phthisis bulbi) secondary to severe ocular trauma sustained during the Chilean social unrest (2019–2022); (2) injury caused by KIPs or non-projectile blunt trauma related to crowd-control interventions; and (3) enrolment in the national PACTO program with complete clinical records available. Exclusion criteria included incomplete medical records or eye loss strictly unrelated to civil unrest trauma. A total of 57 participants met these criteria.

Variables

Sociodemographic Variables

Data included age, sex, and health-insurance affiliation as a proxy for socioeconomic status. In Chile, public insurance (FONASA) covers most of the middle- and low-income population, whereas private insurance (ISAPRE) is associated with higher income levels. Some participants were registered within the Programme for Reparation and Comprehensive Health and Human Rights Care (PRAIS), a permanent component of Chile’s public health system that ensures free and continuous medical care for individuals affected by human rights violations during the dictatorship (1973–1990) and for their direct descendants. Their presence in this cohort indicates that certain individuals injured during the social unrest were already integrated into this public health network, reflecting intergenerational continuity within the national system of care.

Clinical Variables

The selected variables were designed to assess clinical features relevant to rehabilitation resulting from eye loss due to KIPs or blunt trauma. Among these, surgical outcomes captured the different stages and approaches to ocular repair and removal. The cohort included individuals with immediate globe loss as well as those who initially underwent conservative surgical management before eventual evisceration.

In several cases, the surgical approach consisted of primary closure followed by delayed evisceration. This two-stage approach was utilized as a strictly guided 'damage control' measure based on the extensive experience of the Ocular Trauma Unit (UTO). The decision to defer definitive evisceration was indicated for three specific reasons. First, to assess clinical viability: primary closure was initially attempted to preserve the globe, but progressive deterioration of tissue viability or the development of intractable acute pain over time necessitated a secondary evisceration. Second, for prosthetic optimization: in cases with minimal or anatomically irregular ocular remnants, and at the specific request of the maxillofacial prosthodontist, delayed evisceration was indicated to allow for the proper installation of an orbital implant. This step was essential to optimize socket volume, motility, and the functional adaptation of the ocular prosthesis. Third, to facilitate psychological adaptation: the initial primary closure provided a critical window for the victim to emotionally process the trauma and adapt to the irreversible loss of vision before facing the definitive surgical removal of the globe.

Similarly, the variable 'Primary closure followed by phthisis' describes cases where the initially preserved globe subsequently underwent severe, irreversible atrophy (phthisis bulbi), ultimately requiring prosthetic rehabilitation over the non-functional remnant.

Prosthetic Variables

Adherence was defined as the affected individual’s continued acceptance and use of the ocular prosthesis following fitting, as recorded by the prosthetist during follow-up. Individuals classified as non-adherent were those who initially received a prosthesis but subsequently discontinued its use. Non-mimetic prostheses referred to user-requested designs intentionally differing from the appearance of the fellow eye (e.g., alternative colors or patterns). Anophthalmic cavity syndrome was recorded when posterior displacement of the prosthesis, a deep superior sulcus, and/or eyelid malposition were present, requiring repeated prosthetic adjustments and confirmed clinically by the multidisciplinary team. Clinical and prosthetic variables assessed

Sequelae of SOT

Permanent orbital and peri-orbital sequelae (hereafter referred to simply as sequelae) were identified following enucleation, evisceration, or progression to phthisis bulbi. Recorded sequelae included palpebral or periocular scarring, ptosis, orbital or nasal fracture, lagophthalmos, anophthalmic cavity syndrome, painful eye, persistent conjunctivitis, lacrimal-duct scarring, and dental fracture. These findings were documented during multidisciplinary follow-up and confirmed by clinical evaluation.

Patient and Public Involvement

It was not appropriate or possible to involve individuals or the public in the design, conduct, reporting, or dissemination of this research, which was based exclusively on anonymized clinical records from the national PACTO program. The findings will be shared with the multidisciplinary rehabilitation teams involved in ocular trauma care.

Ethical Approval

This study was approved by the Scientific Ethics Committee of the East Metropolitan Health Service (December 5, 2023). Following this ethical approval, institutional authorization to conduct the study was granted by Hospital del Salvador (Resolution No. 3560). The research was performed in accordance with the ethical standards of the Helsinki Declaration of 1964 and its later amendments, as well as Chilean Law 20 120 (2006). All data were anonymized prior to analysis, and no identifiable information was used. Consent to participate and consent for publication were waived by the Ethics Committee due to the strictly retrospective and anonymized nature of the clinical records review. Statistical analysis.

The relationship between the number of orbital and peri-orbital sequelae and each categorical variable was examined using the Kruskal–Wallis non-parametric test, given the non-normal distribution of the data. This non-parametric method was selected as the most robust approach for comparing medians in ordinal data (counts of sequelae) across relatively small subgroups. Each variable is presented with its absolute and relative frequency (n, %), while the results of the Kruskal–Wallis analysis are reported as median number of sequelae, interquartile range (IQR), and corresponding p value. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05. All analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics v29 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Surgical Outcomes and Sequelae

Within the cohort, the leading cause of ocular trauma was baton-round impact (42 cases, 73.7%), followed by tear-gas bombs (12 cases, 21.1%) and blunt trauma (three cases, 5.3%). When comparing the clinical outcomes among the three causal agents, a Kruskal–Wallis test revealed significant differences in the overall burden of sequelae (p = 0.002) (Table 3). Tear-gas bomb injuries produced a greater overall burden of orbital and peri-orbital sequelae, most frequently orbital fractures and widespread palpebral/periocular scarring. In contrast, baton-round injuries were more often associated with isolated ocular loss or anophthalmic cavities. Prosthetic Rehabilitation and Adherence

Adherence to the ocular prosthesis—defined as continued acceptance and use of the device after fitting—was 93%. Non-adherent individuals were those who initially received a prosthesis but subsequently discontinued its use. Non-mimetic prostheses were chosen by 17.5% of users.

Prosthetic initial complications occurred in 36.8% of cases—most commonly rotation (19.3%) and intense mucoid discharge (17.5%). Both complications were significantly associated with a higher number of sequelae (p = 0.011). Likewise, individuals who had received a conformer shell before prosthesis fitting showed a significantly higher number of sequelae (p = 0.002).

One case involving a dermofat graft exhibited an unusually high number of sequelae, including anophthalmic cavity syndrome, orbital fracture, and extensive palpebral and periocular scarring, all resulting from trauma caused by multiple baton rounds. However, these differences were not statistically significant within the overall group (p = 0.201).

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the most comprehensive analysis of orbital sequelae and prosthetic rehabilitation in a civilian population affected by KIPs, offering valuable insights into clinical outcomes and anatomical restoration following globe loss. The findings illustrate the clinical heterogeneity of these injuries and their complex rehabilitation demands within the context of crowd-control interventions. While our cohort includes three cases of severe non-projectile blunt trauma (specifically, a direct police baton strike and physical punches), these represent a small fraction of the sample and are too few to be statistically analyzed alongside KIPs. Consequently, the primary focus of this discussion centers on the distinct, highly destructive injury patterns caused by projectile mechanisms.

Patterns of Sequelae Associated with Tear-Gas Bomb and Baton Round Injuries

Most individuals sustained severe ocular injuries leading to complete globe rupture or irreversible atrophy. During initial management, the Ocular Trauma Score (OTS) was used to estimate prognosis [8]. Despite many cases presenting low OTS values indicating poor visual potential, surgeons often attempted to preserve the globe when minimal viable tissue remained, balancing anatomical conservation with psychological containment. All procedures adhered to national clinical guidelines for severe ocular trauma [6]. The early phase of care also included infection control, considering contamination risks from metallic and composite residues within KIPs [9].

Tear-gas bombs caused more extensive orbital and peri-orbital damage, with a significantly higher sequelae burden per individual (p < 0.01). This group exclusively presented nasal fractures, a dental fracture, and lacrimal-duct scarring, while also accounting for the majority of orbital fractures and cases of lagophthalmos. This pattern is consistent with the high kinetic energy and irregular surface of tear-gas canisters, which, when directly launched at the upper body, act as high-velocity missiles, causing severe blunt and thermal-chemical trauma [10]. Orbital fractures were frequent (22.8%) and often complicated prosthetic fitting, underlining the importance of early reconstruction for socket stability [11]. In contrast, baton rounds more often resulted in isolated ocular loss and caused all cases of anophthalmic cavity syndrome and persistent conjunctivitis, most ptosis and eyelid scarring, and some orbital fractures and lagophthalmos. The high penetration potential and deep socket damage associated with baton rounds used in Chile can be explained by their specific mass, rigidity, and composite structure. Deployed in 12-gauge cartridges containing 12 spherical pellets each (approximately 8 mm in diameter), these projectiles were initially described by authorities as “rubber pellets”. However, subsequent independent material analyses brought to light that they contained only 20% rubber and 80% dense materials, primarily silica, barium sulfate, and lead [12]. This specific composition yields a mass of 734.4 mg and a high density of 2.517 g/cm3 per pellet. The resulting extreme hardness (96.5 Shore A) prevents the projectile from deforming upon impact, causing it to penetrate tissues and act more like a metallic bullet than a standard non-lethal deterrent. This precise ballistic profile explains the severe 'exploding eye' pattern observed in this cohort. Furthermore, reports by international human rights organizations suggest that this pattern of injuries was not incidental. Amnesty International concluded that the high frequency of severe ocular trauma indicated a 'deliberate intention to injure,' with officers frequently firing potentially lethal ammunition directed at the head and chest, violating protocols that mandate aiming at the lower extremities [13]. The National Institute of Human Rights (INDH) corroborated this systematic misuse of force [14]. Emblematic cases, including bilateral vision loss due to tear-gas bomb and pellet impacts during the unrest, illustrate the devastating consequences of these weapons [15, 16].

Prosthetic Rehabilitation and Adherence

A notable finding of this study is that overall high adherence (93%) was facilitated by accommodating requests for non-mimetic (novelty) prostheses. Initially, several individuals rejected conventional (mimetic) prostheses, a situation that posed a significant clinical risk. From a medical perspective, ensuring continuous prosthesis wear—even if non-mimetic—is imperative. It maintains the anatomical and functional integrity of the anophthalmic cavity by preventing tissue retraction and severe orbital volume loss. Furthermore, it preserves palpebral muscle tone (preventing ptosis and entropion), maintains tear dynamics to avoid extreme socket dryness, and protects the conjunctival mucosa from the accumulation of debris and secretions [7, 17]. Accommodating the choice of personalized, non-mimetic (novelty) designs was therefore a critical clinical decision. It not only fostered psychosocial rehabilitation and expressive autonomy for these individuals, allowing them to symbolically highlight their ocular loss, but also fundamentally permitted the installation of the prosthesis to prevent the progressive structural degradation of the empty sockets. The literature is limited in addressing this specific class of non-mimetic prostheses. Evidence suggests they may provide short-term emotional support, yet show scant long-term advantages [17]. Prosthetic initial complications, including rotation (19.3%) and intense mucoid discharge (17.5%), were associated (p < 0.05) with sockets with a greater number of sequelae, supporting the need for continuous follow-up and socket adaptation [18].

Psychosocial and Intergenerational Dimensions

Ocular loss in this context carries a significant psychosocial impact. The emotional aftermath—marked by depression, anxiety, and social withdrawal—highlights the importance of integrating psychological care within ocular rehabilitation programs [19, 20]. Some affected individuals were registered in the PRAIS program, a permanent public health policy providing free medical care for victims of human rights violations during the dictatorship and their descendants [21]. Their presence symbolically connects two generations of trauma, underscoring the persistence of structural violence within Chile’s public health system.

It is also important to contextualize these findings within the global landscape of civil unrest. Fear of arrest and persecution is a documented barrier to seeking medical care, as recently seen during the 2022 protests in Iran, where many individuals subjected to intentional ocular targeting avoided hospitals [22]. In contrast, the Chilean scenario presented a different dynamic: affected individuals actively organized to defend their rights. According to the National Prosecutor's Office (Fiscalía Nacional), over 460 individuals with ocular trauma filed formal legal complaints against state agents [23]. This exceptionally high volume of official complaints, alongside the state-sponsored PACTO program, likely mitigated the extreme underreporting bias observed in other regimes.

Adolescents and Vulnerability

Adolescents represented a small proportion of the sample (3.5%) but exhibited multiple concurrent sequelae. National data report approximately 15 adolescents with severe ocular injuries during the initial weeks of the protests [2]. Given their developmental stage and the psychosocial burden of facial disfigurement, this group requires targeted rehabilitation and extended follow-up [24].

Public Health Implications

The coexistence of complex injuries, psychological distress, and socioeconomic vulnerability underscores the need for permanent multidisciplinary rehabilitation units within Chile’s public health system. Coordination between ophthalmologists, prosthodontists, low vision medical technologists, occupational therapists, social workers, and mental health professionals is essential for comprehensive recovery [25]. The injury patterns described here contribute to international evidence questioning the proportionality and legitimacy of high-velocity crowd-control projectiles in civilian contexts [26, 27]. Preventive policies should prioritise prohibition or strict regulation of such weapons, alongside continuous support for victims.

Strengths and Limitations

A key strength of this study is its inclusion of a unique national cohort, as no previous reports have detailed orbital and peri-orbital sequelae caused by these specific crowd-control weapons in individuals who lost the globe. This makes the dataset both clinically and historically relevant. Although the sample size was limited, the use of non-parametric statistics allowed sensitive detection of meaningful patterns. The absence of cases from regional hospitals is unlikely to introduce bias, as the Ocular Trauma Unit at Hospital del Salvador served as Chile’s national referral center, and the PACTO program integrated nearly all affected individuals requiring ocular prostheses, ensuring representativeness at the national level. This represents the largest and most representative sample studied to date, although it does not include a few individuals treated in remote regions or private centers, those who refused a prosthesis, or cases of eye loss occurring after this cohort analysis ended in 2022. However, several limitations must be noted. First, patient-reported quality-of-life outcomes (PROMs) and specific psychosocial variables were not quantitatively measured using validated scales due to the retrospective nature of the clinical records. Future prospective studies are urgently needed to assess the long-term psychological burden of these injuries. Second, this study focused exclusively on ocular, orbital, and peri-orbital trauma; data on concurrent systemic or life-threatening injuries were not systematically analyzed. Finally, as this cohort only includes individuals requiring prosthetic rehabilitation, it represents the most severe end of the trauma spectrum. Because complete globe loss is the ultimate and most devastating consequence of KIPs, the orbital and peri-orbital sequelae described here capture the extensive structural damage beyond the ocular loss itself. Consequently, this study does not reflect the holistic visual or orbital outcomes of the broader population of individuals who retained their globes. In accordance with SAGER guidelines, the cohort’s pronounced sex asymmetry (94.7% male) does not represent a methodological selection bias, but accurately reflects the demographic reality of the individuals most exposed to crowd-control weapons during the 2019–2020 Chilean protests. This demographic distribution strongly aligns with the acute-phase epidemiology of the conflict [2].

Conclusions

During the repression of the 2019–2020 Chilean social protests, the indiscriminate use of so-called “non-lethal” kinetic impact projectiles (KIPs) proved highly destructive. In a cohort where all individuals suffered complete globe loss, these weapons caused the vast majority of the irreversible mutilations. Both projectiles inflicted distinct and severe oculoplastic damage: a single tear-gas projectile was significantly associated with a high number of concurrent sequelae per affected individual, uniquely causing widespread trauma, including maxillofacial fractures and lacrimal-duct scarring. Conversely, while pellets (the most frequent KIP) frequently resulted in isolated globe loss, they caused specific severe outcomes, being responsible for all cases of anophthalmic cavity syndrome and persistent conjunctivitis, as well as most ptosis cases. Complete globe loss occurred either immediately at impact or following a prolonged clinical trajectory toward delayed evisceration or phthisis bulbi. High prosthetic adherence (93.0%) was successfully achieved by offering non-mimetic (novelty) designs when mimetic (conventional) prostheses were initially refused; a critical clinical approach that preserved orbital integrity while addressing the deep psychosocial needs of the victims. Ultimately, these findings reinforce the global need for multidisciplinary rehabilitation (encompassing visual, functional, and psychological care) and for providing custom-made prostheses tailored to each victim’s specific orbital and peri-orbital sequelae in later stages, whilst providing critical medical evidence to demand the strict regulation or prohibition of these weapons.

Disseminated nocardiosis mimicking metastatic malignancy in a patient with alcoholic liver cirrhosis
Authors: Matthew A. Moffa, Thomas L. Walsh, Whitney Rich, Andrea Snitchler, Nathan R. Shively
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
A 51-year-old man with alcoholic liver cirrhosis presented with a lung mass and innumerable ring-enhancing brain lesions, initially diagnosed and treated as metastatic malignancy. Autopsy ultimately revealed disseminated nocardiosis, underscoring the importance of early tissue biopsy with modified acid-fast staining and microbiologic cultures when evaluating multisystem lesions in immunocompromised patients.

Case

A 51-year-old man with alcoholic liver cirrhosis presented with fevers, fatigue, and 10–15 pounds of unintentional weight loss over six weeks. His occupation involved sewage line repair requiring regular digging in soil. Examination revealed subcutaneous nodules on the scalp, cheek, and neck. CT of the chest demonstrated a 4.5 × 2.4 cm right upper lobe mass with additional pulmonary nodules and hilar lymphadenopathy (Fig. 1). Bronchoscopy with endobronchial ultrasound-guided fine needle aspiration of mediastinal lymph nodes was non-diagnostic; no cultures or special stains were sent. An excised neck cyst showed granulomatous inflammation but was similarly not cultured. Based on the presumptive diagnosis of metastatic malignancy, empiric whole brain radiation and dexamethasone were initiated. After three of ten planned fractions, treatment was discontinued when further workup was pursued given the absence of a confirmed tissue diagnosis, and an infectious disease consultation was requested. Serologic workup for endemic mycoses, Toxoplasma, and HIV was negative. Recommendations were made to pursue core biopsy of the lung mass with specimens sent for various stains, including modified acid-fast staining, and prolonged cultures. Before this could be completed, the patient acutely decompensated with a large left temporal intraparenchymal hemorrhage with herniation and died on hospital day 20.

Autopsy revealed widespread abscesses involving the lungs, brain, omentum, prostate, and skin. Histopathology demonstrated long filamentous branching rods with a beaded appearance that were Fite-positive, Gram-positive, and weakly GMS-positive, morphologically consistent with Nocardia species (Fig. 3). This case illustrates the capacity of disseminated nocardiosis to closely mimic metastatic malignancy, leading to empiric oncologic treatment before tissue diagnosis. [1,2] Cirrhosis-associated immune dysfunction is an underrecognized predisposing condition for nocardiosis. [3,4] When evaluating multisystem lesions in immunocompromised patients, thorough occupational and environmental exposure histories should be elicited and early tissue biopsy with specimens sent for both pathologic examination, including modified acid-fast staining, and microbiologic culture is essential to avoid diagnostic delay.

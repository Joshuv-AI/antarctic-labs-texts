Abstract
. We consider wave maps from (1+ d d )-dimensional Minkowski space, d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 , into rotationally symmetric manifolds which arise from small perturbations of the sphere 𝕊 d \mathbb{S}^{d} . We prove the existence of co-rotational self-similar finite time blowup solutions with smooth blowup profiles. Furthermore, we show the nonlinear asymptotic stability of these solutions under suitably small co-rotational perturbations on the full space.

Introduction

We consider maps
U:\mathbb{R}^{1+d}\to N
, where
\mathbb{R}^{1+d}
is the
(1+d)
-dimensional Minkowski space and
N

d
-dimensional, rotationally symmetric warped product manifold, see
[

]

[

]

is called

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}[U]:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{1+d}}\lvert\nabla_{x}U(t,x)\rvert_{h}^{2}-\lvert\partial_{t}U(t,x)\rvert_{h}^{2}\,dt\,dx,

where
h
is a metric on
N
. The Euler-Lagrange equations associated to this functional, which are in this case called the
wave maps equation
, are given in local coordinates by
1

1

\displaystyle\Box\,U^{a}+\Gamma^{a}_{bc}(U)\,\partial^{\mu}U^{b}\,\partial_{\mu}U^{c}=0,\quad a=1,\ldots,d

(1.1)

and they constitute a system of semilinear wave equations. Here,
\Box=-\partial_{t}^{2}+\Delta_{x}
is the linear wave operator on
\mathbb{R}^{1+d}

\displaystyle U_{\lambda}(t,x):=U(t/\penalty\lambda,x/\penalty\lambda),\quad\lambda>0,

\displaystyle E[U](t):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lvert\partial_{t}U(t,x)\rvert^{2}_{h}+\lvert\nabla_{x}U(t,x)\rvert^{2}_{h}\,dx

\displaystyle E[U_{\lambda}](t)=\lambda^{d-2}E[U](t/\penalty\lambda).

This implies that the wave maps equation is energy critical in dimension two and energy supercritical for

It is well-known that in the energy supercritical case and for large classes of warped product target manifolds Eq. (

Most results are available for

[

1.1.
The main result

To state our main results we introduce the following families of warped product manifolds.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\alpha\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be a non-trivial real-valued
2\pi
-periodic function, which is even, nonnegative and satisfies
\alpha(0)=\alpha(\pi)=0
. Set
\epsilon_{0}:=(\max_{u\in[0,\pi]}\alpha(u))^{-1}>0
. Then we define for
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
,
|\epsilon|<\epsilon_{0}
, the warped product manifold
(S^{d}_{\epsilon},h)
by

\displaystyle S^{d}_{\epsilon}:=(0,\pi)\times_{w_{\epsilon}}\mathbb{S}^{d-1}

(1.2)

equipped with the warping function
w_{\epsilon}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle w_{\epsilon}(u):=\sin(u)(1+\epsilon\,\alpha(u)).

(1.3)

In coordinates
(u,\Omega)\in(0,\pi)\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
the metric on
S^{d}_{\epsilon}
is given by

h=du^{2}+w_{\epsilon}(u)^{2}d\Omega^{2},

with
d\Omega^{2}
denoting the standard round metric on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The conditions in Definition
1.1
ensure that
w_{\epsilon}
is a smooth, odd and
2\pi
-periodic function which is strictly positive in the open interval
(0,\pi)
. In addition,
w_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(0)=1
as well as
w_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(\pi)=-1
. This implies that
S^{d}_{\epsilon}
is a smooth,
d-
dimensional Riemannian manifold, see e.g.
[
1
]
. For
\epsilon=0
, we have
w_{0}(u)=\sin(u)
and in this case
S^{d}_{\epsilon}
can be identified with
\mathbb{S}^{d}\setminus\{\mathfrak{N},\mathfrak{S}\}
, where
\mathfrak{N}
and
\mathfrak{S}
denote the north and south pole of the sphere. For small
|\epsilon|>0
the warping function
w_{\epsilon}
can be viewed as a small perturbation of the warping function of the
d-
sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}
so that we will call
S^{d}_{\epsilon}
a
perturbed sphere
in that case.

It is convenient to consider on
S^{d}_{\epsilon}
so-called normal coordinates
U=(U^{1},\dots,U^{d})
, where

U^{j}:=u\,\Omega^{j},\quad\text{for}\quad j=1,\dots,d,

(1.4)

see, e.g.
[
35
]
. In this way,
S^{d}_{\epsilon}
can be identified (when including the north pole corresponding to the limiting value
u=0
) with the ball
B^{d}_{\pi}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Hence, we consider the initial value problem

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Box\,U^{a}&+\Gamma^{a}_{bc}(U)\,\partial^{\mu}U^{b}\,\partial_{\mu}U^{c}=0,\quad a\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\\
U(0,\cdot)&=U_{0},\quad\partial_{t}U(0,\cdot)=U_{1}\end{split}

(1.5)

on
I\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
I\subset\mathbb{R}
is an interval containing zero and data
U_{0},U_{1}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We restrict our attention to co-rotational maps
U(t,\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
which are by definition of the form

\displaystyle U(t,x)=u(t,|x|)\frac{x}{|x|},

(1.6)

for a
radial profile

u:I\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
which has to satisfy the equation

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}^{2}-\partial^{2}_{r}-\frac{d-1}{r}\partial_{r}\right)u(t,r)+\frac{d-1}{r^{2}}w_{\epsilon}(u(t,r))\,w_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(u(t,r))=0

(1.7)

together with the boundary condition
u(t,0)=0
for all
t\in I
.
As co-rotational symmetry is conserved by the flow it suffices to study the evolution of
u
governed by Eq. (
1.7
).

It is well-known that in the case
\epsilon=0
, Eq. (
1.7
) admits self-similar solutions in all space dimensions
d\geq 3
. The so-called
ground state self-similar solution
is known in closed form, see
[
38
]
,
[
5
]
, and given by

\displaystyle u_{0}^{T}(t,r)=f_{0}\left(\frac{r}{T-t}\right)\quad\text{for}\quad f_{0}(\rho)=2\arctan\left(\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{d-2}}\right),\quad T>0.

(1.8)

In the past years, the stability of
u_{0}^{T}
has been studied intensively. In this paper, we address the problem of stable self-similar blowup for wave maps into
S^{d}_{\epsilon}
for
\epsilon\neq 0
. Our first result proves the existence of globally (in space) smooth self-similar blowup profiles for Eq. (
1.7
) in any space dimension
d\geq 3
, and for any
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
sufficiently small.

In the following statements, we consider for a fixed function
\alpha
as in Definition
1.1
the family of corresponding warping functions
w_{\epsilon}
,
|\epsilon|\leq\epsilon_{0}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
d\geq 3
. There exists an
\epsilon^{*}\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<\epsilon^{*}\leq\epsilon_{0}
such that for every
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
with
\lvert\epsilon\rvert\leq\epsilon^{*}
, Eq. (
1.7
) admits a self-similar solution

u_{\epsilon}^{T}\in C^{\infty}([0,T)\times[0,\infty))\cap L^{\infty}([0,T]\times[0,\infty))
of the form

\displaystyle u_{\epsilon}^{T}(t,r)=f_{\epsilon}\left(\frac{r}{T-t}\right),\quad T>0,

such that its gradient blows up in
r=0
, i.e.,

\lim_{t\to T^{-}}|\partial_{r}u_{\epsilon}^{T}(t,0)|=+\infty.

The profile
f_{\epsilon}
can be written as

f_{\epsilon}=f_{0}+\phi_{\epsilon}

with
f_{0}
defined in Eq. (
1.8
) and a perturbation
\phi_{\epsilon}\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty))\cap C^{\infty}([0,\infty))
. The function
\phi_{\epsilon}
depends Lipschitz continuously on the parameter
\epsilon
in the sense that

\displaystyle\left\|(\cdot)^{-1}(\phi_{\epsilon}-\phi_{\kappa})\right\|_{W^{2,\infty}([0,\infty))}\lesssim\lvert\epsilon-\kappa\rvert

(1.9)

for all
\lvert\epsilon\rvert,\lvert\kappa\rvert\leq\epsilon^{*}
and
\phi_{0}=0
.
Furthermore,
f_{\epsilon}
satisfies
f_{\epsilon}(0)=0
and the limit
\lim_{\rho\to\infty}f_{\epsilon}(\rho)
exists. Finally, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
there are constants
C_{1,k},C_{2,k}>0
depending on
\epsilon
such that

\displaystyle\lvert f_{\epsilon}^{(k)}(\rho)\rvert\leq C_{1,k}\,\langle\rho\rangle^{-k}\quad\text{and}\quad\lvert(f_{\epsilon}+(\cdot)f_{\epsilon}^{\prime})^{(k)}(\rho)\rvert\leq C_{2,k}\,\langle\rho\rangle^{-1-k}

for all
\rho\in[0,\infty)
.

Theorem
1.2
is obtained by functional analytic methods using a perturbative construction which exploits the invertibility of the linearization around
f_{0}
in suitable function spaces. This approach allows us to prove rigorous results on the stability of these solutions under small co-rotational perturbations. We phrase the result in normal coordinates and consider without loss of generality small perturbations around the blowup solution with blowup time
T=1
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
d\geq 3
and choose
\epsilon^{*}>0
as in Theorem
1.2
. For
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
,
|\epsilon|\leq\epsilon^{*}
define

U^{T}_{\epsilon}(t,x):=f_{\epsilon}\left(\frac{|x|}{T-t}\right)\frac{x}{|x|}.

Consider co-rotational initial data of the form

\displaystyle U_{0}=U^{1}_{\epsilon}(0,\cdot)+\nu_{0},\quad U_{1}=\partial_{t}U^{1}_{\epsilon}(0,\cdot)+\nu_{1},

where for
i\in\{0,1\}
,
\nu_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\nu_{i}(x)=xv_{i}(|x|)
and
v_{i}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
are such that
v_{i}(|\cdot|)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Let
(s,k)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}
satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{d}{2}<s\leq\frac{d}{2}+\frac{1}{2d+2},\quad k>d+2.

(1.10)

Then there exists an
\overline{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<\overline{\epsilon}\leq\epsilon^{*}
such that for every
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
with
\lvert\epsilon\rvert\leq\overline{\epsilon}
the following holds: There are
\delta,M_{0}>0
such that for
(\nu_{0},\nu_{1})
as above satisfying

\displaystyle\left\|(\nu_{0},\nu_{1})\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s}\cap\dot{H}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\dot{H}^{s-1}\cap\dot{H}^{k-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\frac{\delta}{M_{0}},

(1.11)

there exists a
T=T_{\epsilon}\in[1-\delta,1+\delta]
and a unique co-rotational map
U\in C^{\infty}([0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})
that satisfies Eq. (
1.5
) for all
(t,x)\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The gradient of
U
blows up at the origin as
t\to T^{-}
and we have the decomposition

\displaystyle U(t,x)=U^{T}_{\epsilon}\left(\frac{x}{T-t}\right)+\nu\left(t,\frac{x}{T-t}\right),

for a function
\nu:[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
which satisfies

\displaystyle\|\nu(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|((T-t)\partial_{t}+\Lambda)\nu(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{r-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})}\to 0

(1.12)

as
t\to T^{-}
for all
r\in[s,k]
where
\Lambda
is defined as the operator
\Lambda=x\cdot\nabla
. In addition,

U(t,(T-t)\cdot)\to U_{\varepsilon}

uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as
t\to T^{-}
.

Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
guarantee for the considered class of target manifolds the existence of
stable
blowup via co-rotational self-similar solutions in all space dimensions
d\geq 3
. In low space dimensions, this is the first result of this kind for targets other than the sphere, see Section
1.2
. The assumptions on the initial data and the underlying topology arise naturally by reformulating the problem as a classical stability problem using similarity coordinates, see Section
1.3
. Some further remarks are in order.

Remark 1.4
.

(Spectral problem)
In the proof of Theorem
1.3
spectral analysis presents the key difficulty. The ODE problem that arises in the investigation of the stability of self-similar blowup in nonlinear wave equations is notoriously hard and current techniques require explicit knowledge of the profile, see e.g.
[
14
]
for a recent exposition. In this paper, we exploit the perturbative nature of our profile construction which enables us to extract the necessary information on the spectral structure of the linearization around
f_{\epsilon}
even though the profile is not known in closed form.

Remark 1.5
.

(Function spaces)
We have chosen to investigate the stability problem in a strip
[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
using the framework of homogenous intersection Sobolev spaces based on recent work by Glogić
[
24
]
. However, an analogous stability result could equally be established in backward light cones (at least at a regularity level strictly above scaling) using known results for
\epsilon=0
, see below.

We note that the approach developed in this paper is not limited to the wave maps equation. In fact, our results suggest that not only the existence of self-similar blowup solutions in nonlinear wave equations but also their dynamical stability properties are stable under suitable small (scale invariant) perturbations of the nonlinearity.

1.2.
Blowup in energy supercritical wave maps - Known results

As the literature on wave maps is vast, we restrict the discussion to works which are relevant in the context of our main results. In particular, we only discuss the energy supercritical case
d\geq 3
.

Concerning the existence of self-similar blowup for wave maps into the sphere, the first result was obtained by Shatah
[
34
]
for maps from
\mathbb{R}^{3+1}
into
\mathbb{S}^{3}
. Later, Turok and Spergel
[
38
]
found in the same setting an explicit example of a self-similar solution (which is expected to be the one from Shatah). Bizoń and Biernat
[
5
]
provided the analogous closed form expression in all space dimensions
d\geq 4
, which is given in Eq. (
1.8
). For
3\leq d\leq 6
,
f_{0}
can indeed be considered as the “ground state” of a family of self-similar blowup profiles whose existence has been proven by Bizoń
[
4
]
and Biernat, Bizoń and Maliborski
[
2
]
.

Numerical investigations by Bizoń, Chmaj and Tabor
[
6
]
, see also
[
5
]
, indicate that blowup via
f_{0}
is generic (at least within the class of co-rotational initial data). The first rigorous proof of the stability of
f_{0}
under small co-rotational perturbations (in
d=3
and restricted to a backward light cone) has been established in the series of works by the first author
[
13
]
, jointly with Aichelburg and the second author
[
18
]
and with Costin and Xia
[
11
]
, respectively. Generalizations to higher dimensions were obtained in
[
8
]
,
[
10
]
, where the latter work by Costin, the first author and Glogić provides an important simplification of the ODE analysis underlying the spectral problem. Recent works by the first author and Wallauch
[
20
,
19
]
establish stability in backward light cones at the optimal regularity level in
d=3,4
.

In another line of research the stability is studied in so-called hyperboloidal similarity coordinates, which allow to follow the evolution past the blowup time, at least outside the singularity. This has been initiated by the first two authors together with Biernat in
[
3
]
and generalized by the first author and Ostermann in
[
16
]
. As already mentioned above, the stability analysis on a strip
[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
has been implemented recently by Glogić
[
24
]
in all space dimensions
d\geq 3
.

Finally, non-self-similar blowup occurs at least in dimensions
d\geq 7
, as has been demonstrated by Ghoul, Ibrahim and Nguyen
[
22
]
.

The existence of finite-time blowup for wave maps from
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
into general warped product manifolds
N^{m}=(0,a)\times_{w}\mathbb{S}^{m-1}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
m\geq 2
,
a>0
, has been addressed by Shatah and Tahvildar-Zadeh
[
35
]
and Cazenave, Shatah and Tahvildar-Zadeh
[
7
]
:
In odd space dimensions
d\geq 3
, by using variational methods and ODE tools, the authors construct
k-
equivariant self-similar profiles (
k=1
corresponds to the co-rotational case) locally in a backward light cone which lead to finite time blowup via finite speed of propagation. The admissible values of
k
and
m
depend on the properties of the corresponding warping function
w
. By lifting these solutions to one space dimension higher, finite time blowup is established in even space dimensions
d\geq 4
. The stability properties of the solutions constructed in
[
35
]
and
[
7
]
are unknown, except for
N=\mathbb{S}^{d}
. Finally, for
d\geq 8
, the first author and Glogić
[
15
]
provided examples of negatively curved targets which admit an explicit self-similar blowup solution and proved its stability in backward light cones in the case
d=9
.

1.3.
Reformulation of the problem and outline of the proof

It is common to write co-rotational maps as
U(t,x)=xv(t,|x|)
since
v
then satisfies a radial nonlinear wave equation with a smooth nonlinearity, see e.g.
[
35
]
.

To realize this, we change variables and define
\widetilde{v}(t,r):=r^{-1}u(t,r)
which transforms Eq. (
1.7
) into a
(d+2)
-dimensional radial semilinear wave equation with a now smooth nonlinearity

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}^{2}-\partial^{2}_{r}-\frac{d+1}{r}\partial_{r}\right)\widetilde{v}(t,r)-\frac{d-1}{r^{3}}\left(r\,\widetilde{v}(t,r)-w_{\epsilon}(r\,\widetilde{v}(t,r))\,w_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(r\,\widetilde{v}(t,r))\right)=0.

(1.13)

In the investigation of Eq. (
1.13
) it is convenient to define
n:=d+2
and
v(t,x):=\widetilde{v}(t,\lvert x\rvert)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and then formulate the equation as a wave equation on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle(\partial^{2}_{t}-\Delta_{x})v(t,x)=\frac{n-3}{\lvert x\rvert^{3}}\left(\lvert x\rvert v(t,x)-w_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert v(t,x))w_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(\lvert x\rvert v(t,x))\right),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(1.14)

We then introduce similarity coordinates

\displaystyle\tau=\log\left(\frac{T}{T-t}\right),\quad\xi=\frac{x}{T-t}

for
T>0
and
(t,x)\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
and rewrite Eq. (
1.14
) as a first order system of the form

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\Psi(\tau)=\mathbf{L}\Psi(\tau)+\mathbf{N_{\epsilon}}(\Psi(\tau)),

(1.15)

where
\mathbf{L}
generates the free wave evolution and
\mathbf{N_{\epsilon}}
denotes the nonlinearity which depends on
\epsilon
via the warping function
w_{\epsilon}
, see Section
2
for the details. We study the problem in intersection Sobolev spaces of radial functions

\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}=(\dot{H}_{r}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\dot{H}_{r}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\times(\dot{H}_{r}^{s-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\dot{H}_{r}^{k-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))

for suitable exponents
\frac{n}{2}-1<s<\frac{n}{2}<k
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

1.3.1.
Existence of self-similar blowup profiles

For
\epsilon=0
, the blowup solution (
1.8
) transforms into a static solution
\mathbf{\Psi}_{0}
of (
1.15
), i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}\mathbf{\Psi}_{0}+\mathbf{N_{0}}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})=0.

(1.16)

In order to find for
\epsilon\neq 0
a solution to

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}\mathbf{\Psi}_{\epsilon}+\mathbf{N_{\epsilon}}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{\epsilon})=0

(1.17)

we insert the ansatz
\mathbf{\Psi}_{\epsilon}=\mathbf{\Psi}_{0}+\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}
into (
1.17
) and write the resulting equation for the perturbation
\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}
as

\displaystyle-\mathbf{L}_{0}\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}=\mathbf{V}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}+\mathbf{\widetilde{N}}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon})+\mathbf{\mathcal{R}}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})

(1.18)

where
\mathbf{L}_{0}:=\mathbf{L}+\mathbf{V}_{0}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})
denotes the linearization around
\mathbf{\Psi}_{0}
for
\epsilon=0
,
\mathbf{V}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})
is a potential term depending on
\epsilon
via the warping function,
\mathcal{R}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})
denotes the remainder and
\mathbf{\widetilde{N}}_{\epsilon}
the nonlinearity, which is quadratically small in its argument. The spectral analysis of
[
24
]
implies that
\mathbf{L}_{0}
, defined as an unbounded operator on a suitable domain
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{0})\subset\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}\to\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}
, is invertible.

In order to control the right-hand side of (
1.18
) we prove a parameter-dependent version of Schauder type estimates originally stated in
[
24
]
. For this we assume

\displaystyle\frac{n}{2}-1<s\leq\frac{n}{2}-1+\frac{1}{2n-2},\quad k>n,

(1.19)

see Appendix
A
. Using this, we show that the first two terms on the right hand side of Eq. (
1.18
) define maps
\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}\to\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k+1}
which are Lipschitz-continuous with respect to the parameter
\epsilon
and with respect to the argument. Moreover, the remainder is in
\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k+1}
and depends Lipschitz-continuously on
\epsilon
, see Lemma
3.2
-
3.4
.

With these prerequisites, we solve Eq. (
1.18
) for suitably small
\epsilon>0
by applying Banach’s fixed point theorem. The smoothness of the constructed
\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}
can be shown inductively using the mapping properties of the involved operators together with Sobolev embedding. The claimed decay properties of the resulting smooth solution
\mathbf{\Psi}_{\epsilon}=(\psi_{\epsilon,1},\psi_{\epsilon,2})
to Eq. (
1.17
) do not follow from this perturbative approach (although some decay can be obtained from Strauss type inequalities). Instead, we use ODE analysis similar to the classcial work of Kavian and Weissler
[
27
]
to show that
\psi_{\epsilon,j}=\mathcal{O}(|\xi|^{-j})
at infinity. This property will be crucial in the analysis.
Finally, translating the result back to the original coordinates and variables yields Theorem
1.2
.

1.3.2.
Stability of self-similar blowup

We now consider the time-dependent problem (
1.15
) and linearize around
\mathbf{\Psi}_{\epsilon}
by making the ansatz
\Psi(\tau)=\mathbf{\Psi}_{\epsilon}+\Phi_{\epsilon}(\tau)

to obtain

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{\tau}\Phi_{\epsilon}(\tau)=\mathbf{L}\Phi_{\epsilon}(\tau)+\mathbf{L}^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\Phi_{\epsilon}(\tau)+\widehat{\mathbf{N}}_{\epsilon}(\Phi_{\epsilon}(\tau)),\\
\Phi_{\epsilon}(0)=\mathbf{U}_{\epsilon,T},\end{cases}

(1.20)

where
\mathbf{L}
is again the free wave operator,
\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}^{\prime}\mathbf{u}=(0,V_{\epsilon}(\psi_{\epsilon,1})u_{1})

for
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}
includes the potential term and
\widehat{\mathbf{N}}_{\epsilon}
denotes the nonlinear remainder. It is known that under our assumptions on the exponents
(s,k)
,
\mathbf{L}
generates a strongly continuous one-parameter semigroup
(\mathbf{S}(\tau))_{\tau\geq 0}
on
\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}
, which decays exponentially to zero as
\tau\to\infty
, see
[
24
]
,
[
14
]
. The decay properties of
\psi_{\epsilon,1}
at infinity allow us to show compactness of the perturbation
\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}^{\prime}
. This implies in particular that the full linear operator
\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}:=\mathbf{L}+\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}^{\prime}
generates a semigroup
(\mathbf{S}_{\epsilon}(\tau))_{\tau\geq 0}
. In order to obtain suitable growth bounds for the linearized evolution, we investigate the spectrum of the generator. From time-translation invariance we expect
\lambda=1
to be an eigenvalue. We make this rigorous in Lemma
4.3
and show that

\displaystyle\mathbf{g}_{\epsilon}:=\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{\epsilon,1}+\Lambda\psi_{\epsilon,1}\\
2\psi_{\epsilon,1}+3\Lambda\psi_{\epsilon,1}+\Lambda^{2}\psi_{\epsilon,1}\end{pmatrix}

is the unique eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda=1
. The decay of
\psi_{\varepsilon,1}
is crucial in order to show that
\mathbf{g}_{\epsilon}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon})
. For
\epsilon=0
, the spectral properties of
\mathbf{L}_{0}
are well-known. More precisely, we know from
[
24
]
that there exists an
\tilde{\omega}>0
such that

\displaystyle\sigma(\mathbf{L}_{0})\subset\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda<-\tilde{\omega}\}\cup\{1\}.

By a perturbation argument, using the fact that
1\in\sigma(\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon})
, we infer that

\displaystyle\sigma(\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon})\subset\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda<-\omega_{0}\}\cup\{1\}

for some
0<\omega_{0}<\tilde{\omega}
and sufficiently small
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
. The spectral structure in combination with resolvent bounds proven uniformly in the parameter imply

\left\|\mathbf{S}_{\epsilon}(\tau)(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon})\mathbf{u}\right\|_{s,k}\lesssim e^{-\omega_{0}\,\tau}\left\|(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon})\mathbf{u}\right\|_{s,k},

and
\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon}\,\mathbf{S}_{\epsilon}(\tau)=e^{\tau}\,\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon}
for
\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon}
denoting the spectral projection onto the eigenspace spanned by
\mathbf{g}_{\epsilon}
, see
[
30
]
, Theorem A.1. For the nonlinearity
\widehat{\mathbf{N}}_{\epsilon}
, we establish Lipschitz bounds by imposing again the above assumptions (
1.19
) on the Sobolev exponents and apply the estimates of Appendix
A
. With these results at hand we construct in Section
4.2
global, exponentially decaying strong
\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}
-solutions of Eq. (
1.20
).
For this, we use the standard approach and first suppress the exponential growth induced by the symmetry eigenvalue
\lambda=1
by a correction with values in
\mathrm{ran}\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon}
. In a second step we account for this using the
T-
dependence of the initial condition to determine the suitable blowup time
T_{\epsilon}
. In Proposition
4.10
we upgrade the constructed strong solutions to classical ones. By defining

\displaystyle v_{\epsilon}^{T}(t,x):=\frac{1}{T-t}\psi_{\epsilon}\left(\frac{x}{T-t}\right),\quad\psi_{\epsilon}(\xi):=|\xi|^{-1}f_{\epsilon}(|\xi|)

(1.21)

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t\in[0,T)
we get the following result.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
n\geq 5
and choose
\epsilon^{*}>0
as in Theorem
1.2
. For
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
,
|\epsilon|\leq\epsilon^{*}
, let
v^{T}_{\epsilon}
be defined as in Eq. (
1.21
) and let
(s,k)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}
satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{n}{2}-1<s\leq\frac{n}{2}-1+\frac{1}{2n-2},\quad k>n.

(1.22)

Then there exist
\omega>0
and
0<\overline{\epsilon}\leq\epsilon^{*}
such that for every
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
,
\lvert\epsilon\rvert\leq\overline{\epsilon}
there are
\delta>0
and
M>1
such that the following holds: For any pair of radial, real-valued functions
\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying

\displaystyle\left\|(\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1})\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s}\cap\dot{H}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\dot{H}^{s-1}\cap\dot{H}^{k-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\frac{\delta}{M},

(1.23)

there exists

and a unique radial solution

to Eq. (

Moreover,

(1.24)

for all

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{0}+\Lambda+1)\varphi(-\log(T-t)+\log T,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{r-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\delta(T-t)^{\omega}.

(1.25)

Finally, we rephrase the results of Theorem

.

1.4.
Notation

For two real numbers
A,B\in\mathbb{R}
we write
A\lesssim B
if there exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that
A\leq CB
holds. For a constant
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
we denote by
\mathbb{H}_{\omega}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\mathrm{Re}\,(z)>\omega\}
the open right half-plane of
\omega
.

Furthermore, if
\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert space and
(L,\mathcal{D}(L))
a closed linear operator on
\mathcal{H}
we denote by
R_{L}(\lambda):=\left(\lambda-L\right)^{-1}
the resolvent operator for
\lambda
lying in the resolvent set
\rho(L)
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by

C^{\infty}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\{f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):f\text{ is radial}\}

the set of smooth radially symmetric functions and by
C^{\infty}_{c,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the subset of those, which have compact support.
By

C_{e}^{\infty}[0,\infty):=\{f\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty)):f^{(2j+1)}(0)=0\text{ for }j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\},

we define set of smooth “even” functions and note that there is a one-to-one correspondence between
C^{\infty}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
C_{e}^{\infty}[0,\infty)
.
As usual,
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\mathcal{S}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote the set of Schwartz functions and radial Schwartz functions, respectively.

We define the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}u
of
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}u(\xi)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(x)e^{-i\xi\cdot x}\,dx.

For
u,v\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
s\geq 0
we define as usual the inner product

\displaystyle\langle u,v\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\langle|\cdot|^{s}\mathcal{F}u,|\cdot|^{s}\mathcal{F}v\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

and the corresponding norm
\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\langle u,u\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
. If
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
is a nonnegative integer

\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\simeq\sum_{\lvert\beta\rvert=k}\left\|\partial^{\beta}u\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Finally, for
s\geq 0
the homogeneous radial Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{s}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denotes the space which is obtained by completion of radial test functions
C_{c,\,r}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with respect to the above defined norm.

2.
Similarity variables and functional analytic setup

For
d\geq 3
and a fixed function
\alpha
, see Definition
1.1
, let
S^{d}_{\epsilon}
, for
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
with
|\epsilon|\leq\epsilon_{0}
, denote the corresponding family of perturbed spheres. For the warping function
w_{\epsilon}
as defined in Eq. (
1.3
) we consider for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},n:=d+2
the equation

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{t}v(t,x)-\Delta_{x}v(t,x)=\frac{n-3}{\lvert x\rvert^{3}}\left(\lvert x\rvert v(t,x)-w_{\epsilon}(\lvert x\rvert v(t,x))w_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(\lvert x\rvert v(t,x))\right).

(2.1)

2.1.
Reformulation of the problem in similarity coordinates

Restricting to
t\in[0,T)
for
T>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we rewrite Eq. (
2.1
) in similarity coordinates

\displaystyle\tau:=\log{\left(\frac{T}{T-t}\right)}\quad\quad\xi:=\frac{x}{T-t}.

Note that this transformation maps the strip
S_{T}:=[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
into the upper half-space
H_{+}:=[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Additionally, we define a new rescaled dependent variable via

\displaystyle\psi(\tau,\xi):=Te^{-\tau}\,v(T-Te^{-\tau},Te^{-\tau}\xi).

Consequently, the evolution of
v
inside
S_{T}
corresponds to the evolution of
\psi
inside
H_{+}
. Furthermore, the differential operators with respect to
t
and
x
are given in the new variables by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}=\frac{e^{\tau}}{T}(\partial_{\tau}+\Lambda),\quad\text{and}\quad\partial_{x_{i}}=\frac{e^{\tau}}{T}\partial_{\xi_{i}},

for
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, where the operator
\Lambda
is again defined via
\Lambda f(\xi):=\xi\cdot\nabla f(\xi)
. Eq. (
2.1
) then transforms into

\displaystyle\begin{split}(\partial_{\tau}^{2}+3\partial_{\tau}+2\partial_{\tau}\Lambda-\Delta_{\xi}+\Lambda^{2}&+3\Lambda+2)\psi(\tau,\xi)\\
&=\frac{n-3}{\lvert\xi\rvert^{3}}\left(\lvert\xi\rvert\psi(\tau,\xi)-w_{\epsilon}(\lvert\xi\rvert\psi(\tau,\xi))\,w_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(\lvert\xi\rvert\psi(\tau,\xi))\right).\end{split}

(2.2)

By defining

\displaystyle\psi_{1}(\tau,\xi):=\psi(\tau,\xi),\quad\psi_{2}(\tau,\xi):=(\partial_{\tau}+\Lambda+1)\psi(\tau,\xi)

and
\Psi(\tau):=(\psi_{1}(\tau,\cdot),\psi_{2}(\tau,\cdot))
we get an evolution equation for
\Psi
of the form

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\Psi(\tau)=\mathbf{\widetilde{L}}\Psi(\tau)+\mathbf{N_{\epsilon}}(\Psi(\tau)),

(2.3)

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{\widetilde{L}}:=\begin{pmatrix}-\Lambda-1&1\\
\Delta&-\Lambda-2\end{pmatrix}

(2.4)

is the wave operator in similarity coordinates and the nonlinearity is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{N_{\epsilon}}(\mathbf{u})=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
N_{\epsilon}(u_{1})\end{pmatrix}\quad N_{\epsilon}(u_{1})(\xi):=\frac{n-3}{\lvert\xi\rvert^{3}}\eta_{\epsilon}(\lvert\xi\rvert u_{1}(\xi)),

(2.5)

for
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},u_{2})
where

\displaystyle\eta_{\epsilon}(y):=y-w_{\epsilon}(y)w_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(y).

(2.6)

2.2.
Time-independent solutions

For
\epsilon=0
a static, smooth solution to Eq. (
2.3
) is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{\Psi}_{0}:=\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{0,1}\\
\psi_{0,2}\end{pmatrix},\text{ with }\psi_{0,1}(\xi)=\lvert\xi\rvert^{-1}f_{0}(\lvert\xi\rvert)\text{ and }\psi_{0,2}=\psi_{0,1}+\Lambda\psi_{0,1},

(2.7)

where the profile
f_{0}
is defined in Eq. (
1.8
). More precisely,

\displaystyle\mathbf{0}=\widetilde{\mathbf{L}}\mathbf{\Psi}_{0}+\mathbf{N}_{0}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0}).

(2.8)

In order to prove the existence of a static solution for small
\epsilon\neq 0
, i.e., a smooth function
\mathbf{\Psi}_{\epsilon}
which satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbf{0}=\widetilde{\mathbf{L}}\mathbf{\Psi}_{\epsilon}+\mathbf{N}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{\epsilon}),

(2.9)

we make the following perturbative ansatz

\displaystyle\mathbf{\Psi}_{\epsilon}=\mathbf{\Psi}_{0}+\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}.

By plugging this into Eq. (
2.9
) we obtain an equation for the perturbation
\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}
which we write in the following form

\displaystyle\mathbf{0}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{\widetilde{L}}\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}+\mathbf{N}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0}+\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon})-\mathbf{N}_{0}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{\widetilde{L}}_{0}\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}+\mathbf{V}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}+\mathbf{\mathcal{R}}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})+\mathbf{\widetilde{N}}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}),

(2.10)

where
\mathbf{\widetilde{L}}_{0}:=\mathbf{\widetilde{L}}+\mathbf{V}_{0}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}_{0}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle:=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
V_{0}(\psi_{0,1})\,u_{1}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{ for }\quad V_{0}(\psi_{0,1})(\xi):=\frac{n-3}{\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}}\eta_{0}^{\prime}(\lvert\xi\rvert\psi_{0,1}(\xi)),

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle:=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
V_{\epsilon}(\psi_{0,1})\,u_{1}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{ for }\quad V_{\epsilon}(\psi_{0,1})(\xi):=\frac{n-3}{\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}}\left(\eta_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(\lvert\xi\rvert\psi_{0,1}(\xi))-\eta_{0}^{\prime}(\lvert\xi\rvert\psi_{0,1}(\xi))\right),

\displaystyle\mathbf{\mathcal{R}}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\frac{n-3}{\lvert\xi\rvert^{3}}\left(\eta_{\epsilon}(\lvert\xi\rvert\psi_{0,1}(\xi))-\eta_{0}(\lvert\xi\rvert\psi_{0,1}(\xi))\right)\end{pmatrix}

and

\displaystyle\mathbf{\widetilde{N}}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{u}):=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\widetilde{N}_{\epsilon}(u_{1})\end{pmatrix}

for

\displaystyle\widetilde{N}_{\epsilon}(u_{1})(\xi):=\frac{n-3}{\lvert\xi\rvert^{3}}\left(\eta_{\epsilon}(\lvert\xi\rvert(\psi_{0,1}(\xi)+u_{1}(\xi)))-\eta_{\epsilon}(\lvert\xi\rvert\psi_{0,1}(\xi))-\eta_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(\lvert\xi\rvert\psi_{0,1}(\xi))\lvert\xi\rvert u_{1}(\xi)\right).

We are going to formulate Eq. (
2.10
) as a fixed point problem in suitable function spaces which we discuss in the following.

2.3.
Intersection Sobolev spaces

For two exponents
0\leq s_{1}<s_{2}
we define an inner product on
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by

\displaystyle\langle u,v\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s_{1}}\cap\dot{H}^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\langle u,v\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\langle u,v\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

Moreover, for
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},u_{2}),\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},v_{2})\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\rangle_{s_{1},s_{2}}:=\langle u_{1},v_{1}\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s_{1}}\cap\dot{H}^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\langle u_{2},v_{2}\rangle_{\dot{H}^{s_{1}-1}\cap\dot{H}^{s_{2}-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

We define the Hilbert space of radial functions
\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s_{1},s_{2}}
as the completion of
C_{c,\,r}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times C_{c,\,r}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
under the norm induced by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{s_{1},s_{2}}

\|\mathbf{u}\|_{s_{1},s_{2}}:=\sqrt{\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u}\rangle}_{s_{1},s_{2}}.

In the following, we gather some properties, which are used throughout the paper.

First, we note that for
0\leq\widetilde{s_{1}}\leq s_{1}<s_{2}\leq\widetilde{s_{2}}

\displaystyle\dot{H}^{\widetilde{s_{1}}}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\dot{H}^{\widetilde{s_{2}}}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow\dot{H}^{s_{1}}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\dot{H}^{s_{2}}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(2.11)

see Lemma 4.4 in
[
24
]
. In particular,
\mathcal{H}_{r}^{\widetilde{s_{1}},\,\widetilde{s_{2}}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s_{1},\,s_{2}}
. Moreover, for

,

\displaystyle\dot{H}^{s_{1}}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\dot{H}^{s_{2}}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow C^{m}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(2.12)

Hence,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s_{1},\,s_{2}}\hookrightarrow C_{r}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times C_{r}^{m-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(2.13)

for
n\geq 3,\,m\geq 1,1\leq s_{1}<\frac{n}{2},s_{2}>\frac{n}{2}+m

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
n\geq 5,\,\frac{n}{2}-1<s<\frac{n}{2}
and

\mathbf{\widetilde{L}},\mathbf{\widetilde{L}}_{0}:C^{\infty}_{c,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times C^{\infty}_{c,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}\to\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}
are closable. For the closed operators
(\mathbf{L},\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}))
and
(\mathbf{L}_{0},\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{0}))
we have
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{0})=\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L})
and the following properties hold:

i)

(Free wave evolution) The operator
\mathbf{L}
generates a strongly continuous semigroup
(\mathbf{S}(\tau))_{\tau\geq 0}
of bounded operators on
\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}
, which satisfies

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{S}(\tau)\mathbf{u}\right\|_{s,k}\leq e^{\left(\frac{n}{2}-1-s\right)\tau}\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{s,k}

(2.18)

for all
\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{H}^{s,k}_{r}
and
\tau\geq 0
.

ii)

(Spectral gap property of
\mathbf{L}_{0}
) There exists
0<\widetilde{\omega}<s+1-\frac{n}{2}
such that

\displaystyle\{\lambda\in\sigma(\mathbf{L}_{0}):\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\geq-\widetilde{\omega}\}=\{1\}.

The point
\lambda=1
is a simple eigenvalue with an explicit eigenfunction

\displaystyle\mathbf{g}_{0}:=\begin{pmatrix}g_{0}\\
2g_{0}+\Lambda g_{0}\end{pmatrix},\quad\text{where}\quad g_{0}(\xi)=\psi_{0,1}(\xi)+\Lambda\psi_{0,1}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}+n-4}.

Moreover, the map
\mathbf{P}_{0}:\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}\to\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}
defined by

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{0}:=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial B_{1/\penalty 2}(1)}\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{L}_{0}}(\lambda)\,d\lambda

is a bounded projection with
\mathrm{ran}\,(\mathbf{P}_{0})=\ker(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{L}_{0})=\langle\mathbf{g}_{0}\rangle
.

iii)

(Linearized evolution)The operator
\mathbf{L}_{0}
generates a strongly continuous semigroup
(\mathbf{S}_{0}(\tau))_{\tau\geq 0}
of bounded operators on
\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}
. Furthermore, for any
0<\omega_{0}<\tilde{\omega}
there is a
C\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{S}_{0}(\tau)(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{0})\mathbf{u}\right\|_{s,k}\leq Ce^{-\omega_{0}\tau}\left\|(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{0})\mathbf{u}\right\|_{s,k}

(2.19)

for all
\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{H}^{s,k}_{r}
and all
\tau\geq 0
.

We remark that the exponent
s
in the above Proposition is chosen to be strictly greater than
\frac{n}{2}-1
so that one has exponential decay of the free part
\mathbf{L}
. The fact that
0
does not lie in the spectrum of
\mathbf{L}_{0}
will be key for the subsequent analysis.

Now throughout the main parts of the proofs provided in Section
3
and Section
4
, we assume
n\geq 5
and work in
\mathcal{H}^{s,k}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
assuming that
(s,k)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}
satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{n}{2}-1<s\leq\frac{n}{2}-1+\frac{1}{2n-2},\quad k>n.

(2.20)

This will allow us to prove a parameter dependent Schauder type estimate akin to
[
24
]
, which we state in Proposition
A.1
, Appendix
A
.

3.
Existence of blowup solutions into perturbed spheres

In the following, we construct for sufficiently small
\epsilon
a solution
\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}
to Eq. (
2.10
). By Proposition
2.5
, the operator
\mathbf{L}_{0}
is bounded invertible and thus (
2.10
) can be phrased as a fixed point problem.

First, we properly define the operators appearing on the right hand side of the equation. To prove the required Lipschitz bounds, we will make extensive use of Proposition
A.1
in Appendix
A
and assume that the exponents
(s,k)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}
satisfy Eq. (
2.20
). We note that under this assumption,
\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}\hookrightarrow C_{r}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times C_{r}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and thus the nonlinearity, the potential and the remainder can be defined pointwise as in Eq. (
2.10
).

In the following we use the notation

\mathcal{B}_{\delta}:=\{\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}:\|\mathbf{u}\|_{s,k}\leq\delta\}\subset\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}.

To proceed, we state an auxiliary lemma for the function
\eta_{\epsilon}
, see Eq. (
2.6
), that will be useful in the following.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}
and
\lvert\epsilon\rvert\leq\epsilon_{0}
. Then

\displaystyle\eta_{\epsilon}(a)=a^{3}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}x^{2}y\,\eta_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime\prime}(axyz)\,dz\,dy\,dx,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\eta_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(a)=a^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}x\,\eta_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime\prime}(axy)\,dy\,dx,

(3.2)

and

\displaystyle\eta_{\epsilon}(a+c)-\eta_{\epsilon}(a+b)-\eta_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(a)(c-b)

\displaystyle=(c-b)\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}(b+x(c-b))(a+y(b+x(c-b)))\eta_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime\prime}(z(a+y(b+x(c-b))))\,dz\,dy\,dx.

(3.3)

Moreover, for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
there exists a constant
C_{\ell}
such that

\displaystyle\lvert\eta_{\epsilon}^{(\ell+3)}(x)-\eta_{\kappa}^{(\ell+3)}(y)\rvert\leq C_{\ell}\left(\lvert\epsilon-\kappa\rvert+\lvert x-y\rvert\right)

(3.4)

holds for every
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
and
\lvert\epsilon\rvert,\lvert\kappa\rvert\leq\epsilon_{0}
.

Proof.

The first three equalities follow from the fact that
\eta_{\epsilon}(0)=\eta_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(0)=\eta_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
together with the fundamental theorem of calculus. We only prove the first equality, since the other two follow from similar ideas.

\displaystyle\eta_{\epsilon}(a)=\int_{0}^{a}\eta_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{a}\int_{0}^{x}\eta_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}(y)\,dy\,dx=\int_{0}^{a}\int_{0}^{x}\int_{0}^{y}\eta_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime\prime}(z)\,dz\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle=a^{3}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}x^{2}y\,\eta_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime\prime}(axyz)\,dz\,dy\,dx.

For (
3.4
) one observes that
\eta_{\epsilon}^{(\ell+3)}
can be written for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
as

\displaystyle\eta_{\epsilon}^{(\ell+3)}=\eta_{0}^{(\ell+3)}+\epsilon\,F_{1}^{(\ell+3)}+\epsilon^{2}F_{2}^{(\ell+3)}

where
\eta_{0}^{\prime\prime\prime},F_{1}^{\prime\prime\prime}
and
F_{2}^{\prime\prime\prime}
are smooth
2\pi
-periodic functions.

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
n\geq 5
and
(s,k)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}
satisfy (
2.20
). Then, for any
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
with
|\epsilon|\leq\epsilon_{0}
,
\mathbf{\widetilde{N}}_{\epsilon}:\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}\to\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k+1}
and the estimate

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{\widetilde{N}}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{u})-\mathbf{\widetilde{N}}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{v})\|_{s,k+1}\lesssim\left(\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{s,k}+\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{s,k}\right)\left\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\right\|_{s,k}+\left(\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{s,k}^{2}+\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{s,k}^{2}\right)\lvert\epsilon-\kappa\rvert

(3.5)

holds for all
\lvert\epsilon\rvert,\lvert\kappa\rvert\leq\epsilon_{0}
and all
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{B}_{\delta}\subset\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}
for
0<\delta\leq 1
.

Proof.

We start by showing the Lipschitz bound

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{\widetilde{N}}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{u})-\mathbf{\widetilde{N}}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{v})\|_{s,k+1}\lesssim\left(\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{s,k}+\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{s,k}\right)\left\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\right\|_{s,k}

(3.6)

for all
\lvert\epsilon\rvert\leq\epsilon_{0}
and all
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{B}_{\delta}
,
0<\delta\leq 1
. With the help of (
3.3
) we write

\displaystyle\widetilde{N}_{\epsilon}(u_{1})(\xi)-\widetilde{N}_{\epsilon}(v_{1})(\xi)=

\displaystyle\frac{n-3}{|\xi|^{3}}\Big(\eta_{\epsilon}\big(|\xi|(\psi_{0,1}(\xi)+u_{1}(\xi))\big)-\eta_{\epsilon}\big(|\xi|(\psi_{0,1}(\xi)+v_{1}(\xi))\big)-\eta_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(|\xi|\psi_{0,1}(\xi))|\xi|(u_{1}(\xi)-v_{1}(\xi))\Big)

\displaystyle=(n-3)\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}(u_{1}(\xi)-v_{1}(\xi))\big(v_{1}(\xi)+x(u_{1}(\xi)-v_{1}(\xi))\big)

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\psi_{0,1}(\xi)+y\big(v_{1}(\xi)+x(u_{1}(\xi)-v_{1}(\xi))\big)\right)\cdot\eta_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime\prime}\left(z|\xi|\big(\psi_{0,1}(\xi)+y(v_{1}+x(u_{1}-v_{1}))\big)\right)dz\,dy\,dx.

Since
F_{\epsilon}(x):=\eta_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime\prime}(x)
satisfies the assumptions of Proposition
A.1
the estimate (
3.6
) follows from the previous equation and (
A.3
). We now show that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{\widetilde{N}}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{u})-\mathbf{\widetilde{N}}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{u})\|_{s,k+1}\lesssim\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{s,k}^{2}\lvert\epsilon-\kappa\rvert

(3.7)

for every
\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{B}_{\delta}
with
0<\delta\leq 1
and every
\lvert\epsilon\rvert,\lvert\kappa\rvert\leq\epsilon_{0}
. For this, we use again Eq. (
3.3
) to obtain

\displaystyle\widetilde{N}_{\epsilon}(u_{1})(\xi)-\widetilde{N}_{\kappa}(u_{1})(\xi)

\displaystyle=(n-3)\,u_{1}(\xi)^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}x\left(\psi_{0,1}(\xi)+yxu_{1}(\xi)\right)

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\eta_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime\prime}\left(z|\xi|\big(\psi_{0,1}(\xi)+yxu_{1}(\xi)\big)\right)-\eta_{\kappa}^{\prime\prime\prime}\left(z|\xi|\big(\psi_{0,1}(\xi)+yxu_{1}(\xi)\big)\right)\right)dz\,dy\,dx

and Eq. (
3.7
) follows from an application of Proposition
A.1
. By combining Eq. (
3.6
) and Eq. (
3.7
) one obtains (
3.5
). The mapping properties of
\mathbf{\widetilde{N}}_{\epsilon}
follow from Eq. (
3.5
) and the fact that
\mathbf{\widetilde{N}_{\epsilon}}(\mathbf{0})=\mathbf{0}
.
∎

For the potential, we have the following result.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
n\geq 5
and
(s,k)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}
satisfy (
2.20
). For any
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
with
|\epsilon|\leq\epsilon_{0}
,
\mathbf{V}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0}):\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}\to\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k+1}
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{V}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{V}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})\mathbf{u}\right\|_{s,k+1}\lesssim\lvert\epsilon-\kappa\rvert\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{s,k}

for all
\lvert\epsilon\rvert,\lvert\kappa\rvert\leq\epsilon_{0}
and all
\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}
.

Proof.

By definition of
\mathbf{V}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})
we have to prove the estimate

\displaystyle\left\|\left(V_{\epsilon}(\psi_{0,1})-V_{\kappa}(\psi_{0,1})\right)u_{1}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s-1}\cap\dot{H}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\lvert\epsilon-\kappa\rvert\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s}\cap\dot{H}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

In a similar way to the previous result one shows with (
3.2
) that

\displaystyle\left((V_{\epsilon}(\psi_{0,1})-V_{\kappa}(\psi_{0,1}))u_{1}\right)

\displaystyle(\xi)\simeq

\displaystyle\psi_{0,1}(\xi)^{2}u_{1}(\xi)\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}x\left(\eta_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime\prime}(\lvert\xi\rvert\psi_{0,1}(\xi)xy)-\eta_{\kappa}^{\prime\prime\prime}(\lvert\xi\rvert\psi_{0,1}(\xi)xy)\right)dy\,dx

holds for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
The claim then again follows by an application of Proposition
A.1
.
∎

Finally, we consider the remainder.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
n\geq 5
and
(s,k)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}
satisfy (
2.20
). For any
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
with
|\epsilon|\leq\epsilon_{0}
,
\mathcal{R}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})\in\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k+1}\subset\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{R}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})-\mathcal{R}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})\right\|_{s,k+1}\lesssim\lvert\epsilon-\kappa\rvert

for all
\lvert\epsilon\rvert,\lvert\kappa\rvert\leq\epsilon_{0}
.

Proof.

By the definition of
\mathcal{R}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})
we have to estimate
\xi\mapsto\frac{n-3}{\lvert\xi\rvert^{3}}\left(\eta_{\epsilon}(\lvert\xi\rvert\psi_{0,1}(\xi))-\eta_{\kappa}(\lvert\xi\rvert\psi_{0,1}(\xi))\right)
in the
\dot{H}^{s-1}\cap\dot{H}^{k}-
norm. To do so we write with the help of (
3.1
)

\displaystyle\frac{n-3}{\lvert\xi\rvert^{3}}

\displaystyle\left(\eta_{\epsilon}(\lvert\xi\rvert\psi_{0,1}(\xi))-\eta_{\kappa}(\lvert\xi\rvert\psi_{0,1}(\xi))\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n-3)\psi_{0,1}(\xi)^{3}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}x^{2}y\left(\eta_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime\prime}(\lvert\xi\rvert\psi_{0,1}(\xi)xyz)-\eta_{\kappa}^{\prime\prime\prime}(\lvert\xi\rvert\psi_{0,1}(\xi)xyz)\right)dz\,dy\,dx

and conclude the proof by again applying the Schauder estimate from Proposition
A.1
to the function
F_{\epsilon}(x):=\eta_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime\prime}(x).

∎

In order to prove gradient blowup as claimed in Theorem
1.2
, we impose an a priori smallness condition on the perturbation.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
n\geq 5
and
(s,k)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}
satisfy (
2.20
). There is a
\delta^{*}_{0}>0
such that any
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathcal{B}_{\delta^{*}_{0}}
satisfies

|u_{1}(0)|<\psi_{0,1}(0),

where

\psi_{0,1}(\xi)=2|\xi|^{-1}\arctan\left(\tfrac{|\xi|}{\sqrt{n-4}}\right).

Proof.

First, note that
\psi_{0,1}(0)>0
. By Sobolev embedding, there is a
C>0
such that

|u_{1}(0)|\leq\|u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|\mathbf{u}\|_{s,k}\leq C\delta^{*}_{0}

for all
\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{B}_{\delta^{*}_{0}}\subset\mathcal{H}^{s,k}_{r}
. Now choose
\delta^{*}_{0}
sufficiently small.
∎

The next result crucially relies on the characterization of the spectrum of the linearized operator for
\epsilon=0
, see Proposition
2.5
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
n\geq 5
and
(s,k)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}
satisfy (
2.20
). There exist
0<\delta^{*}\leq\min\{1,\delta_{0}^{*}\}
and
0<\epsilon^{*}\leq\epsilon_{0}
(depending on
\delta^{*}
) such that for every
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
with
\lvert\epsilon\rvert\leq\epsilon^{*}
the map

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}_{\epsilon}:\mathcal{B}_{\delta^{*}}\to\mathcal{B}_{\delta^{*}},\quad\mathbf{K}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Phi}):=(-\mathbf{L}_{0})^{-1}(\mathbf{V}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})\mathbf{\Phi}+\mathbf{\mathcal{R}}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})+\mathbf{\widetilde{N}}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Phi}))

(3.8)

is a well-defined contraction. In particular,
\delta^{*}
and
\epsilon^{*}
can be chosen such that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{K}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Phi})-\mathbf{K}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi})\right\|_{s,k}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\mathbf{\Phi}-\mathbf{\Psi}\right\|_{s,k}

(3.9)

holds for every
\lvert\epsilon\rvert\leq\epsilon^{*}
and all
\mathbf{\Phi},\mathbf{\Psi}\in\mathcal{B}_{\delta^{*}}
.

Proof.

Using the fact that
\mathbf{L}_{0}:\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{0})\subset\mathcal{H}^{s,k}\to\mathcal{H}^{s,k}
is bounded invertible together with the embedding (
2.11
) there exists a constant
C>0
, such that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{K}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Phi})\right\|_{s,k}\leq C\left(\left\|\mathbf{V}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})\Phi\right\|_{s,k+1}+\left\|\mathbf{\mathcal{R}}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})\right\|_{s,k+1}+\lVert\mathbf{\widetilde{N}}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Phi})\rVert_{s,k+1}\right)

holds for every
\mathbf{\Phi}\in\mathcal{B}_{\delta^{*}}
and every
\lvert\epsilon\rvert\leq\epsilon^{*}
assuming that
0<\delta^{*}\leq\min\{1,\delta_{0}^{*}\}
and
0<\epsilon^{*}\leq\epsilon_{0}
. We then have by the previous results:

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{V}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})\mathbf{\Phi}\right\|_{s,k+1}+\left\|\mathbf{\mathcal{R}}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})\right\|_{s,k+1}+\lVert\mathbf{\widetilde{N}}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Phi})\rVert_{s,k+1}\leq\left(C_{1}\,\epsilon^{*}+C_{2}\,\epsilon^{*}/\delta^{*}+C_{3}\,\delta^{*}\right)\,\delta^{*}

for constants
C_{j}>0
,
j\in\{1,2,3\}
independent of
\epsilon^{*}
and
\delta^{*}
. Now choose
\delta^{*}<1/(C_{3}\,C)
and
\epsilon^{*}=\epsilon^{*}(\delta^{*})
sufficiently small to guarantee
C(C_{1}\,\epsilon^{*}+C_{2}\,\epsilon^{*}/\delta^{*}+C_{3}\,\delta^{*})\leq 1
. Then

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{K}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Phi})\right\|_{s,k}\leq\delta^{*}

for all
\mathbf{\Phi}\in\mathcal{B}_{\delta^{*}}
so that
\mathbf{K}_{\epsilon}
is well-defined on
\mathcal{B}_{\delta^{*}}
.
For the contraction property we take
\mathbf{\Phi},\mathbf{\Psi}\in\mathcal{B}_{\delta^{*}}
and get

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{K}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Phi})-\mathbf{K}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi})\right\|_{s,k}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\|\mathbf{V}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})\,\left(\mathbf{\Phi}-\mathbf{\Psi}\right)\right\|_{s,k+1}+\lVert\mathbf{\widetilde{N}}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Phi})-\mathbf{\widetilde{N}}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi})\rVert_{s,k+1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\,C_{1}\,\epsilon^{*}\left\|\mathbf{\Phi}-\mathbf{\Psi}\right\|_{s,k}+C\,C_{4}\,\delta^{*}\left\|\mathbf{\Phi}-\mathbf{\Psi}\right\|_{s,k},

for
C_{4}>0
. Now choose
\delta^{*}>0
possibly even smaller so that
C\,C_{4}\,\delta^{*}<1/2
is satisfied. Hence the claim follows by adjusting
\epsilon^{*}
and requiring in addition that
C\,C_{1}\,\epsilon^{*}+C\,C_{4}\,\delta^{*}\leq 1/2
.

∎

This result immediately implies the existence part in the following statement.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
n\geq 5
and
(s,k)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}
satisfy (
2.20
). Let
\delta^{*}
and
\epsilon^{*}
be as in Proposition
3.6
. Then for every
\lvert\epsilon\rvert\leq\epsilon^{*}
there is a
unique
\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}\in\mathcal{B}_{\delta^{*}}\cap\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{0})
satisfying

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}_{0}\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}+\mathbf{V}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}+\mathbf{\mathcal{R}}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})+\mathbf{\widetilde{N}}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon})=0

(3.10)

in
\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}
. Furthermore,
\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}
depends Lipschitz continuously on
\epsilon
in the sense that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}-\mathbf{\Phi}_{\kappa}\|_{s,k}\lesssim|\epsilon-\kappa|

holds for all
\lvert\epsilon\rvert,\lvert\kappa\rvert\leq\epsilon^{*}
.
Finally,
\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}\in\bigcap\limits_{\ell\geq s}\dot{H}^{\ell}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\dot{H}^{\ell-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset C^{\infty}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times C^{\infty}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}
solves the equation in a classical sense, i.e.,
\mathbf{L}_{0}\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}=\widetilde{\mathbf{L}}_{0}\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}
.

Proof.

The existence of a unique
\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}\in\mathcal{B}_{\delta^{*}}\cap\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{0})
satisfying the Eq. (
3.10
) is a direct consequence of Proposition
3.6
and the contraction mapping principle. For the Lipschitz bound we take
\lvert\epsilon\rvert,\lvert\kappa\rvert\leq\epsilon^{*}
and use (
3.9
) to obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}-\mathbf{\Phi}_{\kappa}\right\|_{s,k}=\left\|\mathbf{K}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon})-\mathbf{K}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{\kappa})\right\|_{s,k}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}-\mathbf{\Phi}_{\kappa}\right\|_{s,k}+\left\|\mathbf{K}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{\kappa})-\mathbf{K}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{\kappa})\right\|_{s,k}

from which
\left\|\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}-\mathbf{\Phi}_{\kappa}\right\|_{s,k}\leq 2\left\|\mathbf{K}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{\kappa})-\mathbf{K}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{\kappa})\right\|_{s,k}
follows. Now, for every
\mathbf{\Phi}\in\mathcal{B}_{\delta^{*}}

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{K}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Phi})-\mathbf{K}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{\Phi})\right\|_{s,k}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\mathcal{R}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})-\mathcal{R}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})\right\|_{s,k}+\left\|\left(\mathbf{V}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})-\mathbf{V}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})\right)\mathbf{\Phi}\right\|_{s,k}

\displaystyle+\|\mathbf{\widetilde{N}}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Phi})-\mathbf{\widetilde{N}}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{\Phi})\|_{s,k}\lesssim\lvert\epsilon-\kappa\rvert

due to Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
3.3
.

For the regularity we use the Sobolev embedding from (
2.13
) and show that
\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}\in\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,\ell}
for every
\ell\geq k
. Since
\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}\in\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}
, Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
3.3
yield
\mathbf{V}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}+\mathbf{\mathcal{R}}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})+\mathbf{\widetilde{N}}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon})\in\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k+1}
. We now want to use Eq. (
3.8
) to deduce that
\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}
must belong to
\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k+1}
.

For this we will at this particular point not suppress the dependency of
\mathbf{L}_{0}
on the parameters
s
and
k
and will therefore denote by
\mathbf{L}_{0,s_{1},s_{2}}
the closure of
\widetilde{\mathbf{L}}_{0,s_{1},s_{2}}
in
\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s_{1},s_{2}}
. Eq. (
3.8
) then writes

\displaystyle\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}=(-\mathbf{L}_{0,s,k})^{-1}\left(\mathbf{V}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}+\mathbf{\mathcal{R}}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Psi}_{0})+\mathbf{\widetilde{N}}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon})\right)

and all what is left to show is that if we restrict the resolvent of
\mathbf{L}_{0,s,k}
(in 0) to the subspace
\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k+1}
then this operator equals the resolvent (in 0) of the operator
\mathbf{L}_{0,s,k+1}
. But this follows from the fact that
\mathbf{L}_{0,s,k+1}
is a restriction of
\mathbf{L}_{0,s,k}
to the subspace
\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k+1}
(see for example
[
12
]
, p.21, Lemma 3.5 or
[
24
]
, p.29, Lemma C.1.). Via repeating this argument we conclude inductively that
\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}
belongs to
\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,\ell}
for every
\ell\geq k
and is therefore smooth by (
2.13
).

Finally, we show that
\mathbf{L}_{0}
acts as a classical differential operator on
\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}
. Since
\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}
belongs to
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{0})
there exists a sequence
\left(\mathbf{u}_{j}\right)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{\infty}_{c,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times C^{\infty}_{c,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{j}\to\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{\mathbf{L}}_{0}\mathbf{u}_{j}\to\mathbf{L}_{0}\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}\quad\text{in}\quad\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}\quad\text{for}\quad j\to\infty.

Now, by the embedding of
\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}
into
C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we infer by the above convergence of
\mathbf{u}_{j}
towards
\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}
that each term in
\widetilde{\mathbf{L}}_{0}\mathbf{u}_{j}
converges pointwise towards
\widetilde{\mathbf{L}}_{0}\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}
. Again by Sobolev embedding, the limit
\widetilde{\mathbf{L}}_{0}\mathbf{u}_{j}\to\mathbf{L}_{0}\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}
holds pointwise, which implies the claim. This last part of the proof shows in fact that
\widetilde{\mathbf{L}}_{0}
acts as a classical differential operator on any element in its domain, irrespective of higher regularity.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
n\geq 5
and
(s,k)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}
satisfy (
2.20
). Let
\delta^{*}
and
\epsilon^{*}
be as in Proposition
3.7
. For
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
with
\lvert\epsilon\rvert\leq\epsilon^{*}
let
\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}\in\mathcal{B}_{\delta^{*}}
be the corresponding solution of Eq. (
3.10
). Then

\mathbf{\Psi}_{\epsilon}:=\mathbf{\Psi}_{0}+\mathbf{\Phi}_{\epsilon}\in\bigcap\limits_{\ell\geq s}\dot{H}^{\ell}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\dot{H}^{\ell-1}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset C^{\infty}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times C^{\infty}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

solves Eq. (
2.9
) in a classical sense. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{\Psi}_{\epsilon}-\mathbf{\Psi}_{\kappa}\right\|_{s,k}\lesssim\lvert\epsilon-\kappa\rvert

(3.11)

for all
\lvert\epsilon\rvert,\lvert\kappa\rvert\leq\epsilon^{*}
.

Finally, we characterize the decay of the solution
\mathbf{\Psi}_{\epsilon}=(\psi_{\epsilon,1},\psi_{\epsilon,2})
at infinity. Corollary
3.8
and
the generalized Strauss inequality (
2.17
) imply that

|\psi_{\epsilon,1}(\xi)|\lesssim\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{n}{2}-s}
for
s>\frac{n}{2}-1
and hence the decay rate is strictly less than 1. In the following, we provide a more refined analysis based on ODE arguments to improve this. This is along the lines of
[
27
]
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
n\geq 5
,
\frac{n}{2}-1<s<\frac{n-1}{2}
and
|\epsilon|\leq\epsilon^{*}
. Suppose that

Proof.

Now we want to investigate the asymptotic behavior of

2e^{-2r}-2e^{-r}&2e^{-2r}-e^{-r}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad V(r)=\begin{pmatrix}v(r)\\

\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which yields the first bound in Eq. (
4.11
) since
s>\frac{n}{2}-1
. For the second component we note that according to the proof of Proposition
3.9

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}_{\epsilon,1}(\rho)=a_{1}\,w_{1}(\rho)+a_{2}\,w_{2}(\rho)

for large
\rho>0
where
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
and
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
are given by

\displaystyle w_{1}(\rho)=\rho^{-3}\,h_{1}(\rho^{-1}),\quad w_{2}(\rho)=\rho^{-2}h_{2}(\rho^{-1})+c\log(\rho)\rho^{-3}h_{1}(\rho^{-1})

with functions
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
which are analytic around zero. Now, we use the fact that

g_{\epsilon,2}=2g_{\epsilon,1}+\Lambda g_{\epsilon,1}

and observe that the first term of
w_{2}
(the one with the least decay) cancels so that we obtain

\displaystyle\lvert\widetilde{g}^{(\ell)}_{\epsilon,2}(\rho)\rvert\lesssim\log(\rho)\rho^{-3-\ell}

for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and every large enough
\rho>0
. Since the logarithm grows slower than any polynomial and from the fact that
s
is strictly larger than
\frac{n}{2}-1
we infer that the second bound in (
4.11
) holds and we conclude
\mathbf{g}_{\epsilon}\in\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}
.

Now we show that
\mathbf{g}_{\epsilon}
is indeed an eigenfunction of
\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}
to the eigenvalue
\lambda=1
. We first note that by similar reasoning as in Proposition
3.9
we actually have
\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}\mathbf{g}_{\epsilon}=\widetilde{\mathbf{L}}_{\epsilon}\mathbf{g}_{\epsilon}
. Eq. (
4.10
) yields

\displaystyle\Delta g_{\epsilon,1}-\Lambda^{2}g_{\epsilon,1}-5\Lambda g_{\epsilon,1}-6g_{\epsilon,1}+V_{\epsilon}(\psi_{\epsilon,1})g_{\epsilon,1}=0

so that its radial representative
\widetilde{g}_{\epsilon,1}
has to solve (
3.21
). But this is true by definition, since
\widetilde{g}_{\epsilon,1}=\widetilde{\psi}_{\epsilon,2}=\widetilde{\psi}_{\epsilon,1}+\rho\,\widetilde{\psi}_{\epsilon,1}^{\prime}
. The claim then follows by the definition of
\widetilde{g}_{\epsilon,2}
.

∎

For
\epsilon\neq 0
, the potential is not available in closed form which is however crucial for a direct investigation of the spectral problem and the exclusion of other unstable spectral points. Hence, we resort to perturbative arguments in order to characterize the spectral properties of
\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
n\geq 5
and
(s,k)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}
satisfy (
2.20
). For every arbitrary but fixed
0<\omega_{0}<\widetilde{\omega}
where
\widetilde{\omega}
is the constant from Proposition
2.5
, there exists
\epsilon^{**}>0
such that for every
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
with
\lvert\epsilon\rvert\leq\epsilon^{**}

\displaystyle\sigma(\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon})\subset\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda<-\omega_{0}\}\cup\{1\}

(4.12)

holds,
\lambda=1
is a simple eigenvalue of
\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}
and its eigenspace is spanned by
\mathbf{g}_{\epsilon}
.
Furthermore, for the Riesz projection

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon}:=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial B_{1/\penalty 2}(1)}\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}}(\lambda)\,d\lambda

(4.13)

we have
\mathrm{ran}\,(\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon})=\ker(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon})=\langle\mathbf{g}_{\epsilon}\rangle
.

Proof.

We first show

\displaystyle\sigma(\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon})\cap\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\geq-\omega_{0}\}\subset B_{1/\penalty 2}(1),

where
B_{1/\penalty 2}(1)
denotes the open ball in
\mathbb{C}
of radius
r=\frac{1}{2}
centered at
1
, and that there exists a constant
C_{\omega_{0}}>0
such that the resolvent satisfies

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}}(\lambda)\right\|\leq C_{\omega_{0}}

(4.14)

for all
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{H}_{-\omega_{0}}}\,\backslash B_{1/\penalty 2}(1)
and all
\lvert\epsilon\rvert\leq\epsilon^{**}
. We argue similarly to
[
30
]
, Proposition 3.2.

Let
\lvert\epsilon\rvert\leq\epsilon^{*}
. To see that
\overline{\mathbb{H}_{-\omega_{0}}}\,\backslash B_{1/\penalty 2}(1)
belongs to the resolvent set of
\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}
, we notice that due to Proposition
2.5
every
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{H}_{-\omega_{0}}}\,\backslash\{1\}
belongs to the resolvent set of
\mathbf{L}_{0}
. Therefore, the identity

\displaystyle\lambda-\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}=\left(\mathbf{I}-(\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}-\mathbf{L}_{0})\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{L}_{0}}(\lambda)\right)(\lambda-\mathbf{L}_{0})

(4.15)

holds and it follows that
\lambda-\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}
is bounded invertible if and only if this is true for
\mathbf{I}-(\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}-\mathbf{L}_{0})\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{L}_{0}}(\lambda)
. We will prove the invertibility of the latter one by showing that the Neumann series of
(\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}-\mathbf{L}_{0})\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{L}_{0}}(\lambda)
converges. For this we split the resolvent in the following way

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{L}_{0}}(\lambda)\mathbf{f}=\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{L}_{0}}(\lambda)(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{0})\mathbf{f}+\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{L}_{0}}(\lambda)\mathbf{P}_{0}\mathbf{f}

(4.16)

where
\mathbf{P}_{0}
is the Riesz projection from Proposition
2.5
. From the decay of the semigroup
\left(\mathbf{S}_{0}(\tau)\right)_{\tau\in\mathbb{N}}
on the stable subspace
\mathrm{ran}\,(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{0})
, see (
2.19
), we obtain for every
0<\omega<\widetilde{\omega}
the following bound

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{S}_{0}(\tau)\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{0}\right)\mathbf{u}\right\|_{s,k}\lesssim e^{-\omega\,\tau}\left\|\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{0}\right)\mathbf{u}\right\|_{s,k}.

Since the restriction of
\left(\mathbf{S}_{0}(\tau)\right)_{\tau\geq 0}
to
\mathrm{ran}\,(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{0})
equals the semigroup of the restriction of the operator
\mathbf{L}_{0}
to
\mathrm{ran}\,(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{0})
we can apply
[
21
]
, p.55, Theorem 1.10 for every
0<\omega<\widetilde{\omega}
to infer the existence of a constant
M_{\omega}\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{L}_{0}}(\lambda)(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{0})\right\|\leq\frac{M_{\omega}}{\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda+\omega}

(4.17)

holds for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{H}_{-\omega}
. Hence, (by choosing for example
\omega=\frac{\omega_{0}+\widetilde{\omega}}{2}
in Eq. (
4.17
)) there exists a constant
C_{\omega_{0}}>0
such that

\left\|\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{L}_{0}}(\lambda)(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{0})\right\|\leq C_{\omega_{0}}

holds for all
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{H}_{-\omega_{0}}}
. For the second term in Eq. (
4.16
) we notice that
\ker(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{L}_{0})=\mathrm{ran}\,(\mathbf{P}_{0})=\langle\mathbf{g}_{0}\rangle
so that there exists a unique
\mathbf{f}_{0}\in\mathrm{ran}\,(\mathbf{P}_{0})
with

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{0}\mathbf{f}=\langle\mathbf{f}_{0},\mathbf{f}\rangle_{s,k}\,\mathbf{g}_{0}

(4.18)

for every
\mathbf{f}\in\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}
. Since
\mathbf{g}_{0}
is an eigenvector of
\mathbf{L}_{0}
to the eigenvalue
\lambda=1
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{L}_{0}}(\lambda)\mathbf{P}_{0}\mathbf{f}=(\lambda-1)^{-1}\langle\mathbf{f}_{0},\mathbf{f}\rangle_{s,k}\,\mathbf{g}_{0}.

(4.19)

From these equations we now infer from (
4.16
) the existence of a constant
C_{\omega_{0}}>0
with

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{L}_{0}}(\lambda)\right\|\leq C_{\omega_{0}}

for every
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{H}_{-\omega_{0}}}\,\backslash B_{1/\penalty 2}(1)
. In sight of Proposition
4.2
we therefore get

\displaystyle\left\|(\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}-\mathbf{L}_{0})\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{L}_{0}}(\lambda)\right\|\lesssim|\epsilon|

for every
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{H}_{-\omega_{0}}}\,\backslash B_{1/\penalty 2}(1)
and every
\lvert\epsilon\rvert\leq\epsilon^{*}
. We now choose
\epsilon^{**}\leq\epsilon^{*}
small enough so that
\left\|(\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}-\mathbf{L}_{0})\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{L}_{0}}(\lambda)\right\|<1
. Hence, we conclude from (
4.15
) that
\lambda
belongs to the resolvent set of
\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}
. Furthermore, we obtain from the same equation that the resolvent has the following representation

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}}(\lambda)=\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{L}_{0}}(\lambda)\left(\mathbf{I}-(\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}-\mathbf{L}_{0})\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{L}_{0}}(\lambda)\right)^{-1}

from which Eq. (
4.14
) follows.

We will now conclude that the spectrum of
\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}
is contained in a left half-plane except for the unstable eigenvalue
\lambda=1
.
From our previous considerations we know that
\partial B_{1/\penalty 2}(1)
belongs to the resolvent set of
\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}
. Therefore, the Riesz projection
\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon}
from (
4.13
) is well-defined. We furthermore obtain from the resolvent identity, the Lipschitz continuity of
\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}
and the uniform bound for the resolvent for every
\lambda\in\partial B_{1/\penalty 2}(1)
,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}}(\lambda)-\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{L}_{\kappa}}(\lambda)\right\|\leq\left\|\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}}(\lambda)\right\|\left\|\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}-\mathbf{L}_{\kappa}\right\|\left\|\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{L}_{\kappa}}(\lambda)\right\|\lesssim\lvert\epsilon-\kappa\rvert.

Hence
\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}}
is Lipschitz continuous with respect to the parameter
\epsilon
and therefore the same holds true for the Riesz projection
\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon}
. Therefore, we know from
[
26
]
, p.34, Lemma 4.10 that there holds for every
\lvert\epsilon\rvert\leq\epsilon^{**}
,

\displaystyle\dim\mathrm{ran}\,(\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon})=\dim\mathrm{ran}\,(\mathbf{P}_{0})=1.

But since we have the inclusion
\langle\mathbf{g}_{\epsilon}\rangle\subset\ker(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon})\subset\mathrm{ran}\,(\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon})
equality must hold due to dimensional reasons and there can also not exist any other spectral points of
\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}
in
B_{1/\penalty 2}(1)
.

∎

Now we are able to characterize the linearized evolution generated by
\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
n\geq 5
and
(s,k)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}
satisfy (
2.20
). Then, for every arbitrary but fixed
0<\omega_{0}<\widetilde{\omega}
, where
\widetilde{\omega}
is the constant from Proposition
2.5
, and every
\lvert\epsilon\rvert\leq\epsilon^{**}
, where
\epsilon^{**}
is the constant from Proposition
4.4
, the projection
\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon}
commutes with the respective semigroup
\mathbf{S}_{\epsilon}
. In particular,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon}\,\mathbf{S}_{\epsilon}(\tau)=\mathbf{S}_{\epsilon}(\tau)\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon}=e^{\tau}\,\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon}

(4.20)

for all
\tau\geq 0.
We furthermore have

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{S}_{\epsilon}(\tau)(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon})\mathbf{u}\right\|_{s,k}\lesssim e^{-\omega_{0}\,\tau}\left\|(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon})\mathbf{u}\right\|_{s,k}

(4.21)

as well as

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{S}_{\epsilon}(\tau)(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon})-\mathbf{S}_{\kappa}(\tau)(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\kappa})\right\|\lesssim e^{-\omega_{0}\,\tau}\lvert\epsilon-\kappa\rvert

(4.22)

for all
\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k},\tau\geq 0
and
\lvert\epsilon\rvert,\lvert\kappa\rvert\leq\epsilon^{**}
.

Proof.

Since the semigroup
\mathbf{S}_{\epsilon}(\tau)
commutes with its generator
\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}
it also commutes with its corresponding resolvent
\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}}
and therefore also with the projection
\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon}
. Eq. (
4.20
) follows from the fact that
\mathbf{S}_{\epsilon}(\tau)\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon}\mathbf{f}
as well as
e^{\tau}\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon}\mathbf{f}
are solutions of the uniquely solvable Cauchy problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} \partial_{\tau}\mathbf{u}(\tau)&=\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}\mathbf{u}(\tau),\\
\mathbf{u}(0)&=\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon}\mathbf{f}.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

Eq. (
4.21
) follows from
[
30
]
Theorem A.1 provided that one can show the existence of a constant
M_{\omega_{0}}>0
with

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}}(\lambda)\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon}\right)\right\|\leq M_{\omega_{0}}

(4.23)

for all
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{H}_{-\omega_{0}}}
and
\lvert\epsilon\rvert\leq\epsilon^{**}.

From Proposition
4.4
we already know that there exists a constant
M_{\omega_{0}}
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}}(\lambda)\right\|\leq M_{\omega_{0}}

holds for every
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{H}_{-\omega_{0}}}\,\backslash B_{1/\penalty 2}(1)
and
\lvert\epsilon\rvert\leq\epsilon^{**}
. Now we know that
\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}}(\lambda)\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon}\right)
is an analytic map in
\mathbb{H}_{-\omega_{0}}
and since the resolvent of the restriction of
\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}
to the range of
\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon}
coincides with
\mathbf{R}_{\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}}(\lambda)\left(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon}\right)
we obtain (
4.23
) also for every
\lambda
lying in the compact ball
\overline{B_{1/\penalty 2}(1)}
.

To prove Eq. (
4.22
) we argue similarly to
[
17
]
, Lemma 4.9. By taking
\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon})
and then observing

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\mathbf{S}_{\epsilon}(\tau)(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon})\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}\mathbf{S}_{\epsilon}(\tau)(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon})\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon})\mathbf{S}_{\epsilon}(\tau)(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon})\mathbf{u}

yields

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\left[\mathbf{S}_{\epsilon}(\tau)(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon})\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{S}_{\kappa}(\tau)(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\kappa})\mathbf{u}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon})\left[\mathbf{S}_{\epsilon}(\tau)(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon})\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{S}_{\kappa}(\tau)(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\kappa})\mathbf{u}\right]+\left[\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon})-\mathbf{L}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\kappa})\right]\mathbf{S}_{\kappa}(\tau)(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\kappa})\mathbf{u}.

Consequently, the function

\displaystyle\Phi_{\epsilon,\kappa}(\tau):=\frac{\mathbf{S}_{\epsilon}(\tau)(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon})\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{S}_{\kappa}(\tau)(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\kappa})\mathbf{u}}{\lvert\epsilon-\kappa\rvert}

satisfies the inhomogeneous equation

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\Phi_{\epsilon,\kappa}(\tau)=\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon})\Phi_{\epsilon,\kappa}(\tau)+\frac{\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon})-\mathbf{L}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\kappa})}{\lvert\epsilon-\kappa\rvert}\mathbf{S}_{\kappa}(\tau)(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\kappa})\mathbf{u}

(4.24)

with initial data
\Phi_{\epsilon,\kappa}(0)=\frac{(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon})-(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\kappa})}{\lvert\epsilon-\kappa\rvert}\mathbf{u}.

By Duhamel’s principle the integral equation to (
4.24
) is given by

\displaystyle\Phi_{\epsilon,\kappa}(\tau)=\,

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{\epsilon}(\tau)(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon})\frac{\mathbf{P}_{\kappa}-\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon}}{\lvert\epsilon-\kappa\rvert}\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathbf{S}_{\epsilon}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon})\frac{\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon})-\mathbf{L}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\kappa})}{\lvert\epsilon-\kappa\rvert}\mathbf{S}_{\kappa}(\tau^{\prime})(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\kappa})\mathbf{u}\,d\tau^{\prime}.

(4.25)

The Lipschitz continuity of
\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}
and
\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon}
now implies

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon})-\mathbf{L}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\kappa})}{\lvert\epsilon-\kappa\rvert}\right\|\lesssim 1,\quad\text{for all}\quad\lvert\epsilon\rvert,\lvert\kappa\rvert\leq\epsilon^{**}.

Therefore, we get from Duhamel’s formula (
4.25
) and the just proven decay of the semigroup on the stable subspace (
4.21
)

\displaystyle\left\|\Phi_{\epsilon,\kappa}(\tau)\right\|_{s,k}\lesssim(1+\tau)e^{-\frac{\omega_{0}+\widetilde{\omega}}{2}\,\tau}\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{s,k}\lesssim e^{-\omega_{0}\,\tau}\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{s,k}

for all
\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon})
. Due to the density of
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{L}_{\epsilon})
in
\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}
this result now extends to all of
\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}
so that we have proven (
4.22
) and therefore the Proposition.

∎

4.2.
The abstract nonlinear Cauchy problem

In the following, for
(s,k)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}
satisfying (
2.20
) we fix
\omega:=\widetilde{\omega}/\penalty 2
, where
\widetilde{\omega}
is the constant from Proposition
2.5
and denote by
\overline{\epsilon}:=\epsilon^{**}(\omega)
the constant associated via Proposition
4.4
.
First, we state Lipschitz estimates for the nonlinearity
\widehat{\mathbf{N}}_{\epsilon}
defined in Eq. (
4.7
).

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
n\geq 5
and
(s,k)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}
satisfy (
2.20
). Then, for any
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
with
|\epsilon|\leq\overline{\epsilon}
,
\mathbf{\widehat{N}}_{\epsilon}:\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}\to\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k+1}
and the estimate

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{\widehat{N}}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{u})-\mathbf{\widehat{N}}_{\kappa}(\mathbf{v})\right\|_{s,k+1}\lesssim\left(\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{s,k}+\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{s,k}\right)\left\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\right\|_{s,k}+\left(\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{s,k}^{2}+\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{s,k}^{2}\right)\lvert\epsilon-\kappa\rvert

(4.26)

holds for all
\lvert\epsilon\rvert,\lvert\kappa\rvert\leq\overline{\epsilon}
and all
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{B}_{\delta}\subset\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}
for
0<\delta\leq 1
.

Proof.

The proof is analogous to the proof of Lemma
3.2
using the Schauder estimate from Proposition
A.1
.

∎

We now focus on the existence and uniqueness of solutions to the Cauchy problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{\tau}\Phi_{\epsilon}(\tau)=\mathbf{\widetilde{L}}_{\epsilon}(\Phi_{\epsilon}(\tau))+\mathbf{\widehat{N}}_{\epsilon}(\Phi_{\epsilon}(\tau)),\quad&\tau\in(0,\infty),\\
\Phi_{\epsilon}(0)=\mathbf{u}\quad&\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k},\\
\end{cases}

(4.27)

for
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
,
|\epsilon|\leq\overline{\epsilon}
by considering the corresponding integral equation

\displaystyle\Phi_{\epsilon}(\tau)=\mathbf{S}_{\epsilon}(\tau)\mathbf{u}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathbf{S}_{\epsilon}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\mathbf{\widehat{N}}_{\epsilon}(\Phi_{\epsilon}(\tau^{\prime}))\,d\tau^{\prime}\quad\text{for all}\quad\tau\geq 0\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}.

(4.28)

We introduce the Banach space

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}:=\{\Phi\in C([0,\infty),\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}):\left\|\Phi\right\|_{\mathcal{X}}:=\sup_{\tau>0}e^{\omega\tau}\left\|\Phi(\tau)\right\|_{s,k}<\infty\},

as well as

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{\delta}:=\{\Phi\in\mathcal{X}:\left\|\Phi\right\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq\delta\}=\{\Phi\in C([0,\infty),\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}):\left\|\Phi(\tau)\right\|_{s,k}\leq\delta\,e^{-\omega\tau},\forall\,\tau>0\}.

Following the standard approach, we introduce the correction term

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}(\Phi,\epsilon,\mathbf{u}):=\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon}\left(\mathbf{u}+\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\tau^{\prime}}\,\mathbf{\widehat{N}}_{\epsilon}(\Phi(\tau^{\prime}))\,d\tau^{\prime}\right)

(4.29)

to suppress the exponential growth of the semigroup on the unstable subspace. Consequently, we consider the fixed-point problem

\displaystyle\Phi(\tau)=\mathbf{K}(\Phi,\epsilon,\mathbf{u})(\tau),

(4.30)

where
\mathbf{K}(\Phi,\epsilon,\mathbf{u})
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}(\Phi,\epsilon,\mathbf{u})(\tau):=\mathbf{S}_{\epsilon}(\tau)\left[\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{C}(\Phi,\epsilon,\mathbf{u})\right]+\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathbf{S}_{\epsilon}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\mathbf{\widehat{N}}_{\epsilon}(\Phi(\tau^{\prime}))\,d\tau^{\prime}.

(4.31)

This modification stabilizes the evolution as the following result shows:

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
n\geq 5
,
(s,k)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}
satisfy (
2.20
). There are constants
0<\delta_{0}<1
and
C_{0}>1
such that for all
0<\delta\leq\delta_{0}
,
C\geq C_{0}
, all
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
with
\lvert\epsilon\rvert\leq\overline{\epsilon}
and all
\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}
with
\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{s,k}\leq\frac{\delta}{C}
there exists a unique function
\Phi_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{u})\in\mathcal{X}_{\delta}
such that
(
4.30
)
holds for all
\tau\geq 0
. Furthermore, the solution map
(\mathbf{u},\epsilon)\mapsto\Phi_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{u})
is Lipschitz continuous, i.e.

\displaystyle\left\|\Phi_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{u})-\Phi_{\kappa}(\mathbf{v})\right\|_{\mathcal{X}}\lesssim\left\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\right\|_{s,k}+\lvert\epsilon-\kappa\rvert

for all
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}
with
\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{s,k},\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{s,k}\leq\frac{\delta}{C}
and all
\lvert\epsilon\rvert,\lvert\kappa\rvert\leq\overline{\epsilon}
.

Proof.

First, we show that the map
\mathbf{K}_{(\mathbf{u},\epsilon)}(\Phi):=\mathbf{K}(\Phi,\epsilon,\mathbf{u})
is a well-defined contraction on
\mathcal{X}_{\delta}
for all sufficiently large
C>1
, sufficiently small
\delta>0
and all
\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}
with
\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{s,k}\leq\frac{\delta}{C}
.

For this we will take
\Phi\in\mathcal{X}_{\delta}
and
\tau\geq 0
and notice that we can write
\mathbf{K}_{(\mathbf{u},\epsilon)}
in the following way

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}_{(\mathbf{u},\epsilon)}(\Phi)(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\mathbf{S}_{\epsilon}(\tau)(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon})\mathbf{u}-\int_{\tau}^{\infty}e^{\tau-\tau^{\prime}}\,\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon}\,\widehat{\mathbf{N}}_{\epsilon}(\Phi(\tau^{\prime}))\,d\tau^{\prime}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathbf{S}_{\epsilon}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon})\widehat{\mathbf{N}}_{\epsilon}(\Phi(\tau^{\prime}))\,d\tau^{\prime}.

(4.32)

From this we obtain with Proposition
4.5
and Lemma
4.6

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{K}_{(\mathbf{u},\epsilon)}(\Phi)(\tau)\right\|_{s,k}\lesssim\frac{\delta}{C}e^{-\omega\,\tau}+\delta^{2}e^{-2\omega\,\tau}+\delta^{2}e^{-\omega\,\tau}(e^{-\omega\,\tau}+1)\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{C}+\delta\right)\delta e^{-\omega\,\tau}

so that we have

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{K}_{(\mathbf{u},\epsilon)}(\Phi)(\tau)\right\|_{s,k}\leq\delta e^{-\omega\,\tau}

if we choose
C\geq C_{0}
and
0<\delta\leq\delta_{0}
with
C_{0}
sufficiently large and
\delta_{0}>0
sufficiently small.

Since the continuity of the mapping
\tau\mapsto\mathbf{K}_{(\mathbf{u},\epsilon)}(\Phi)(\tau)
follows from the definition and dominated convergence we conclude that
\mathbf{K}_{(\mathbf{u},\epsilon)}:\mathcal{X}_{\delta}\to\mathcal{X}_{\delta}
is well-defined.

To show that
\mathbf{K}_{(\mathbf{u},\epsilon)}
is a contraction on
\mathcal{X}_{\delta}
(for potentially even smaller
\delta>0
) we take
\Phi,\Psi\in\mathcal{X}_{\delta}
and calculate for every
\tau\geq 0
by using again the representation of
\mathbf{K}_{(\mathbf{u},\epsilon)}
from Eq. (
4.32
)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{K}_{(\mathbf{u},\epsilon)}(\Phi)(\tau)-\mathbf{K}_{(\mathbf{u},\epsilon)}(\Psi)(\tau)\right\|_{s,k}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\int_{\tau}^{\infty}e^{\tau-\tau^{\prime}}\lVert\widehat{\mathbf{N}}_{\epsilon}(\Phi(\tau^{\prime}))-\widehat{\mathbf{N}}_{\epsilon}(\Psi(\tau^{\prime}))\rVert_{s,k}\,d\tau^{\prime}+\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-\omega\,(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}\lVert\widehat{\mathbf{N}}_{\epsilon}(\Phi(\tau^{\prime}))-\widehat{\mathbf{N}}_{\epsilon}(\Psi(\tau^{\prime}))\rVert_{s,k}\,d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\left(\delta e^{-2\omega\,\tau}+\delta e^{-\omega\,\tau}(e^{-\omega\,\tau}+1)\right)\left\|\Phi-\Psi\right\|_{\mathcal{X}}.

If we now choose
\delta_{0}>0
sufficiently small we get

\left\|\mathbf{K}_{(\mathbf{u},\epsilon)}(\Phi)-\mathbf{K}_{(\mathbf{u},\epsilon)}(\Psi)\right\|_{\mathcal{X}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\Phi-\Psi\right\|_{\mathcal{X}},

for all
0<\delta\leq\delta_{0}
,
C\geq C_{0}
and all
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
with
\lvert\epsilon\rvert\leq\overline{\epsilon}
. The existence of a unique solution in
\mathcal{X}_{\delta}
now follows by application of the contraction mapping principle.

What is now left to show is the Lipschitz continuity of the solution map
(\mathbf{u},\epsilon)\mapsto\Phi_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{u})\in\mathcal{X}_{\delta}
.

For this we take
(\mathbf{u},\epsilon),(\mathbf{v},\kappa)\in\mathcal{B}_{\frac{\delta}{C}}\times[-\overline{\epsilon},\overline{\epsilon}]
and obtain by the previous considerations functions
\Phi_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{u}),\Phi_{\kappa}(\mathbf{v})\in\mathcal{X}_{\delta}
solving

\displaystyle\Phi_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{u})(\tau)=\mathbf{K}(\Phi_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{u})(\tau),\epsilon,\mathbf{u})(\tau)\quad\text{and}\quad\Phi_{\kappa}(\mathbf{v})(\tau)=\mathbf{K}(\Phi_{\kappa}(\mathbf{v})(\tau),\kappa,\mathbf{v})(\tau)\quad\forall\,\tau\geq 0.

We now show that

\left\|\mathbf{K}(\Phi_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{u}),\epsilon,\mathbf{u})-\mathbf{K}(\Phi_{\kappa}(\mathbf{v}),\kappa,\mathbf{v})\right\|_{\mathcal{X}}\lesssim\left\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\right\|_{s,k}+\lvert\epsilon-\kappa\rvert.

For this we take
\tau\geq 0
and estimate the terms in (
4.32
) separately. For the first term we simply get from (
4.22
)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{S}_{\epsilon}(\tau)(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon})\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{S}_{\kappa}(\tau)(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\kappa})\mathbf{v}\right\|\lesssim\frac{\delta}{C}e^{-\omega\,\tau}\lvert\epsilon-\kappa\rvert+e^{-\omega\,\tau}\left\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\right\|_{s,k}.

For the second term we apply Lemma
4.6
and the Lipschitz continuity of
\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon}
to get

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{\infty}e^{\tau-\tau^{\prime}}\lVert\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon}\,\widehat{\mathbf{N}}_{\epsilon}(\Phi_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{u})(\tau^{\prime}))-\mathbf{P}_{\kappa}\,\widehat{\mathbf{N}}_{\kappa}(\Phi_{\kappa}(\mathbf{v})(\tau^{\prime}))\rVert_{s,k}\,d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\lvert\epsilon-\kappa\rvert\int_{\tau}^{\infty}e^{\tau-\tau^{\prime}}\lVert\widehat{\mathbf{N}}_{\kappa}(\Phi_{\kappa}(\mathbf{v})(\tau^{\prime}))\rVert_{s,k}\,d\tau^{\prime}+\int_{\tau}^{\infty}e^{\tau-\tau^{\prime}}\lVert\widehat{\mathbf{N}}_{\epsilon}(\Phi_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{u})(\tau^{\prime}))-\widehat{\mathbf{N}}_{\kappa}(\Phi_{\kappa}(\mathbf{v})(\tau^{\prime}))\rVert_{s,k}\,d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\lvert\epsilon-\kappa\rvert\,\delta^{2}\,e^{-2\omega\,\tau}+\delta\,e^{-2\omega\,\tau}\left\|\Phi_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{u})-\Phi_{\kappa}(\mathbf{v})\right\|_{\mathcal{X}}

and for the last term we similarly get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\left\|\mathbf{S}_{\epsilon}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\epsilon})\widehat{\mathbf{N}}_{\epsilon}(\Phi_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{u})(\tau^{\prime}))-\mathbf{S}_{\kappa}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}_{\kappa})\widehat{\mathbf{N}}_{\kappa}(\Phi_{\kappa}(\mathbf{v})(\tau^{\prime}))\right\|_{s,k}\,d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\lvert\epsilon-\kappa\rvert\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-\omega\,(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}\lVert\widehat{\mathbf{N}}_{\kappa}(\Phi_{\kappa}(\mathbf{v})(\tau^{\prime}))\rVert_{s,k}\,d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-\omega\,(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}\lVert\widehat{\mathbf{N}}_{\epsilon}(\Phi_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{u})(\tau^{\prime}))-\widehat{\mathbf{N}}_{\kappa}(\Phi_{\kappa}(\mathbf{v})(\tau^{\prime}))\rVert_{s,k}\,d\tau^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\lvert\epsilon-\kappa\rvert\,\delta^{2}\,e^{-\omega\,\tau}+\lvert\epsilon-\kappa\rvert\,\delta\,e^{-\omega\,\tau}\left\|\Phi_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{u})-\Phi_{\kappa}(\mathbf{v})\right\|_{\mathcal{X}}.

With that we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\Phi_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{u})-\Phi_{\kappa}(\mathbf{v})\right\|_{\mathcal{X}}=

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{K}(\Phi_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{u}),\epsilon,\mathbf{u})-\mathbf{K}(\Phi_{\kappa}(\mathbf{v}),\kappa,\mathbf{v})\right\|_{\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\lvert\epsilon-\kappa\rvert+\left\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\right\|_{s,k}+\delta\left\|\Phi_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{u})-\Phi_{\kappa}(\mathbf{v})\right\|_{\mathcal{X}}.

For small enough
\delta>0
we get
\left\|\Phi_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{u})-\Phi_{\kappa}(\mathbf{v})\right\|_{\mathcal{X}}\lesssim\lvert\epsilon-\kappa\rvert+\left\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\right\|_{s,k}
as desired.

∎

Now we will go back to considering the specific form of the initial data of the Cauchy problem (
4.5
). For this, we define the following initial data operator

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{v},T):=\mathbf{v}^{\mathbf{T}}+\mathbf{\Psi}_{\epsilon}^{\mathbf{T}}-\mathbf{\Psi}_{\epsilon}:=\begin{pmatrix}Tv_{1}(T\cdot)\\
T^{2}v_{2}(T\cdot)\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}T\psi_{\epsilon,1}(T\cdot)-\psi_{\epsilon,1}\\
T^{2}\psi_{\epsilon,2}(T\cdot)-\psi_{\epsilon,2}\end{pmatrix}.

(4.33)

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
n\geq 5
and
(s,k)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}
satisfy (
2.20
). Let
0<\delta\leq\frac{1}{2}
. For every
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
,
\lvert\epsilon\rvert\leq\overline{\epsilon}
the map

T\mapsto\mathbf{U}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{v},T):[1-\delta,1+\delta]\to\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}

is continuous for
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k}
. Furthermore, for every
T\in[\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2}]
the initial data operator can be written as

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{v},T)=\mathbf{v}^{T}+(T-1)\mathbf{g}_{\epsilon}+\mathbf{R}_{\epsilon}(T),

(4.34)

and there exists a constant
M_{\epsilon}>0
such that

\|\mathbf{R}_{\epsilon}(T)\|\leq M_{\epsilon}|T-1|^{2}.

Proof.

Eq. (
4.34
) follows from Taylor’s Theorem applied to the map
[\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2}]\to\mathcal{H}_{r}^{s,k},T\mapsto\Psi^{T}_{\epsilon}
using the fact that

\displaystyle\partial_{T}{\left.\kern-1.2pt\begin{pmatrix}T\psi_{\epsilon,1}(T\cdot)\\
T^{2}\psi_{\epsilon,2}(T\cdot)\end{pmatrix}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{T=1}}=\mathbf{g}_{\epsilon}.

The components
R_{\epsilon,1}(T)
and
R_{\epsilon,2}(T)
of the remainder term
\mathbf{R}_{\epsilon}(T)
satisfy

\displaystyle\left\|R_{\epsilon,i}(T)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s-(i-1)}\cap\dot{H}^{k-(i-1)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim(T-1)^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{2}\left\|\Lambda^{j}\psi_{\epsilon,i}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s-(i-1)}\cap\dot{H}^{k-(i-1)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\quad\text{for}\quad i=1,2.

The norms on the right hand side are finite, due to the decay of
\psi_{\epsilon,i}
described in Proposition
3.9
. This determines the constant
M_{\epsilon}>0
. The continuity follows by a standard argument, see for example
[
24
]
, Lemma 8.2.
∎

Theorem 4.9
.

and

2.20

(4.35)

\|\Phi_{\epsilon}(\tau)\|_{s,k}\leq\delta e^{-\omega\tau},\quad\forall\tau\geq 0.

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{v}^{T}\right\|_{s,k}+\lvert T-1\rvert\left\|\mathbf{g}_{\epsilon}\right\|_{s,k}+\left\|\mathbf{R}_{\epsilon}(T)\right\|_{s,k}

\displaystyle\frac{\delta}{C^{2}}+\frac{\delta}{C}L_{\epsilon}+\frac{\delta^{2}}{C^{2}}M_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle\left\|\partial^{\gamma}f\,\partial^{\beta-\gamma}\left(\chi_{j}-\chi_{i}\right)\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{i,j})}=\left\|\partial^{\gamma}f\left(j^{\lvert\gamma\rvert-\lvert\beta\rvert}\,\partial^{\beta-\gamma}\chi(\cdot/\penalty j)-i^{\lvert\gamma\rvert-\lvert\beta\rvert}\,\partial^{\beta-\gamma}\chi(\cdot/\penalty i)\right)\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{i,j})}.

Since both terms from above can be treated analogously we will only consider the first one:

\displaystyle j^{\lvert\gamma\rvert-\lvert\beta\rvert}\left\|\partial^{\gamma}f\,\partial^{\beta-\gamma}\chi(\cdot/\penalty j)\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{i,j})}

\displaystyle\lesssim j^{\lvert\gamma\rvert-\lvert\beta\rvert}\left\|\partial^{\gamma}f\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{2j}\backslash B_{1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim j^{\lvert\gamma\rvert-\lvert\beta\rvert}\left(\int_{B_{2j}\backslash B_{1}}\lvert\partial^{\gamma}f(x)\rvert^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim j^{\lvert\gamma\rvert-\lvert\beta\rvert}\left(\int_{1}^{2j}r^{-p\lvert\gamma\rvert-pk+n-1}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

For
\frac{n}{p}\neq\lvert\gamma\rvert+k
we obtain

\displaystyle j^{\lvert\gamma\rvert-\lvert\beta\rvert}\left(\int_{1}^{2j}r^{-p\lvert\gamma\rvert-pk+n-1}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim j^{\lvert\gamma\rvert-\lvert\beta\rvert}\left(j^{-p\lvert\gamma\rvert-pk+n}-1\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim j^{\frac{n}{p}-\lvert\beta\rvert-k}

and for
\frac{n}{p}=\lvert\gamma\rvert+k

\displaystyle j^{\lvert\gamma\rvert-\lvert\beta\rvert}\left(\int_{1}^{2j}r^{-p\lvert\gamma\rvert-pk+n-1}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=j^{\lvert\gamma\rvert-\lvert\beta\rvert}\log(2j)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Since both of these terms converge due to the assumptions on
s
and the fact that we are in the case
\lvert\gamma\rvert<\lvert\beta\rvert
to 0 as
j
goes to infinity we have shown that
(f_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\dot{W}^{\ell,p}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

By (
B.3
) we obtain that this sequence is also Cauchy in
\dot{H}^{s}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Therefore, we have shown that
(f_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\dot{H}^{s}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for every
s\geq 0
with
s>\frac{n}{2}-k
. For every such
s
, we therefore infer the existence of a function
g\in\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
s_{1}\leq s<s_{2}
where
s_{1}
is strictly smaller than
\frac{n}{2}
(here one needs that
k
is strictly greater than 0) and
s_{2}
is strictly greater than
\frac{n}{2}
, so that
(f_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
g
in the
\dot{H}^{s_{1}}\cap\dot{H}^{s_{2}}-
norm. By (
2.12
) we know that
(f_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
must therefore also converge to
g
in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(in particular pointwise) so that we conclude
f=g\in\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset\dot{H}_{r}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
which finishes the proof.

∎

Therapeutic walks beyond the ICU: a safe strategy to enhance emotional well-being in critically ill patients with prolonged hospitalization
Authors: Daniela Ines Gilgado, Matías Bertozzi, Joaquín Pérez, Gimena Paola Cardoso, Victoria Piaggio, Agustín Vitale, Matías Accoce, Javier Hernán Dorado
Published: 2025

To the editor

Therapeutic walks (TW) - a structured supervised patient transfer to outdoor settings - have emerged as part of humanized care for critically ill patients in intensive care units (ICU). Although retrospective studies suggest TW may be safe and potentially beneficial [1–3], its effect on psychological outcomes has not been prospectively evaluated.

Between January 2023 to April 2024, we implemented TWs in the ICU of Sanatorio Anchorena San Martín (Argentina) for patients undergoing prolonged hospitalization. Eligible participants were adults with ICU stay ≥ 10 days, referred for respiratory and physical therapy and clinically stable for outdoor mobilization [4]. Only the first TW was analyzed. Participants were included through non-probabilistic convenience sampling and written informed consent was obtained from patients or authorized surrogates. Inclusion-exclusion criteria and Statistical Analysis are detailed in the Supplemental document.

Before each TW, baseline clinical data, severity scores and patient complexity were recorded. An interdisciplinary team (intensivist, nurse, physiotherapist, psychologist) conducted the TWs, ensuring equipment availability, compliance with infection prevention protocols, and monitoring of discontinuation criteria (Fig.S1). Family members were encouraged to actively participate, and TW duration was individualized by patient preference and tolerance.

Psychological variables were assessed 15–30 min before and after TW. Mood and perceived health status were measured using a 0–10 numerical rating scale (0 = worst state, 10 = best state), completed independently or with a relative’s assistance. To avoid “suggestion bias”, the evaluator remained outside the room. Anxiety and depression were evaluated by a psychologist using the Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS) [5]. Safety was monitored with a standardized checklist. Adverse events were ‘minor’ if resolved without affecting physical integrity, and ‘major’ if involving physiological changes, injury, or unintentional device removal requiring TW interruption [4]. TW was considered “safe” when no major adverse events occurred (Supplemental document).

We included 41 patients (Fig.S2. Age: 58 years [45.5–68.5]; Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation II (APACHE II): 9 [6–15] points) with a median ICU stay of 14 days [10.5–19.5]. The most common reasons for admission were abdominal (n = 22%) and cardiac surgery (n = 19.5%), followed by coma (n = 14.6%). Before the TW, invasive mechanical ventilation was required in 30 patients (73%) for a median of 7 days [2.0–9.3] (TableS1). During the TWs, 9 patients (22.0%) were tracheostomized and 3 (7.3%) remained on invasive or noninvasive ventilation. The median TW duration was 25 min [20–30] and mostly (78%) family members were present (Table S2).

Perceived mood and health status significantly improved after TW (PRE:6.0 [5.0–8.0] vs. POST:7.0 [7.0–8.5], p < 0.001; PRE: 6.0 [5.0–8.0], POST: 7.0 [5.0–8.0], p = 0.007, respectively). Anxiety and depression scores were also significantly lower (PRE: 6.8 (SD 3.3) vs. POST: 5.7 (3.77), p = 0.016; PRE:6.6 (4.5) vs. POST: 5.5 (4.1), p = 0.004, respectively), confirming the beneficial effects on psychological status.

To account for potential response heterogeneity, we explored predictors of a larger positive impact. Accordingly, 24 (58.5%) patients showed an ≥ 1-point increase in mood after TW and were considered as responders; conversely, 4 (9.8%) patients showed a 1-point decrease and 13 (31.7%) exhibited no change (non-responders). After covariate adjustment, multivariable logistic regression revealed that patients with lower baseline mood presented significantly higher odds of benefiting (ORadj = 1.94, 95% CI [1.12; 3.4], p = 0.019) (Table 1).

TW was deemed to be safe. No major adverse events occurred. Minor events included 4 pulse oximeter failure and an accidental removal of a peripheral line, none affecting clinical status.

To our knowledge, this is the first prospective description documenting that TWs can improve psychological well-being in critically ill patients with prolonged hospitalization. Mood benefits were most pronounced in those with worse baseline states, highlighting the importance of targeting patients experiencing greater emotional distress. Potential mechanisms may include neurobiological changes promoted by mobilization, environmental stimulation, reconnection with natural settings and strengthened family bonds [1–4].

Beyond immediate mood improvement, reductions in anxiety and depression suggest broader psychological benefits, likely associated with restored dignity, a sense of recovery, and relief from the emotional burden of prolonged hospitalization [4].

Consistent with previous reports [2, 3], no serious adverse events occurred, supporting TW safety when performed by a trained staff following standardized protocols. Nonetheless, feasibility depends on ICU resources, interdisciplinary collaboration, and time availability.

This report has limitations: sampling was for convenience, which could introduce potential bias and the single-center model limits generalizability. TW effects were assessed 15–30 min post-intervention, precluding conclusions on their persistence over time. The prior cognitive and psychological status is unknown, which may affect the results. Moreover, analysis was restricted to the first TW per patient, leaving uncertain whether repeated sessions could enhance these benefits.

Our findings support TWs as a meaningful step toward humanizing ICU care, improving mood, perceived health, and anxiety/depression scores. With suitable infrastructure, they can be implemented by interdisciplinary teams. Future research should explore optimal dosage, long-term impact and subgroup-specific benefits.

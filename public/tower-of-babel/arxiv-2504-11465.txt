Fourier Analysis: A New Result
Rajesh Dachiraju

Abstract
. This article contains a new result in Fourier analysis concerning jump type discontinuities.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 42A99;

1.
Notations

Let
f
be a
2\pi
periodic BV function whose derivative is also BV.Let the amount of jump at a point
x
is denoted as
\lfloor f\rfloor(x)=f(x+0)-f(x-0)
Define function
J:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, such that
J(x)=\lfloor f\rfloor(x)
. Define the set
L=\{x/x\in(0,2\pi)\wedge J(x)\neq 0\}
. Let there not be any jumps at
0,\pi,2\pi
. and all jumps at rational multiples of
2\pi
.

Let the Fourier series of
f
be defined as

(1.1)

\displaystyle S[f]=\sum_{\nu=-\infty}^{\nu=\infty}c_{\nu}e^{i\nu x}

and the conjugate Fourier series as

(1.2)

\displaystyle\tilde{S}[f]=-i\sum_{\nu=-\infty}^{\nu=\infty}sign(\nu)c_{\nu}e^{i\nu x}

Let the Fourier partial sum of
f
be defined as

(1.3)

\displaystyle S_{n}(x;f)=\sum_{\nu=-n}^{\nu=n}c_{\nu}e^{i\nu x}

and the conjugate partial sum of
f
as

(1.4)

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{n}(x;f)=-i\sum_{\nu=-n}^{\nu=n}sign(\nu)c_{\nu}e^{i\nu x}

Define

G(n)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\left|c_{i}\right|

The Dirichlet kernel
D_{n}(x)
and conjugate Dirichlet kernel
\tilde{D}_{n}(x)
are defined as

(1.5)

\displaystyle D_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{2}+\sum_{\nu=1}^{n}\cos(\nu x)=\frac{\sin(n+\frac{1}{2})x}{2\sin(\frac{1}{2}x)}

(1.6)

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{n}(x)=\sum_{\nu=1}^{n}\sin(\nu x)=\frac{\cos(\frac{x}{2})-\cos(n+\frac{1}{2})x}{2\sin(\frac{1}{2}x)}

Let

(1.7)

\displaystyle Y_{n}(x)=-\frac{1}{\log(n)G(n)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tilde{S}_{k}(x;f)\left|c_{k}\right|

2.
Results

Lemma 2.1
.

(2.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}Y_{n}(x)=K\frac{J(x)}{2\pi}

where
K
is a constant

Proof.

Case 1 Points of no jump.

\tilde{S_{n}}(x;f)\sim O(1)
and hence
Y_{n}(x)\sim 0
, as
J(x)=0
for no jump the result is proved.

Case 2 Points of jump.

By Lukacs theorem, at points of jump,
\tilde{S_{n}}(x;f)\sim\frac{J(x)}{\pi}\log(n)
,
So

(2.2)

\displaystyle Y_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{\log(n)G(n)}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{J(x)}{\pi}\log(k)|c_{k}|

Noting
|c_{k}|\sim\frac{1}{k}
(as we are assuming points of jump),

(2.3)

\displaystyle Y_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{\log(n)K\log(n)}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{J(x)}{\pi}\log(k)K^{2}\frac{1}{k}

where
K
is a constant.

on taking summation as integration, we get

(2.4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K(\log(n))^{2}}K\frac{J(x)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{n}\log(k)K\frac{1}{k}dk

Substituting
\log(k)=t
in the integral, we get

(2.5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\log(n))^{2}}\frac{J(x)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{t}Ktdt

Hence we get

(2.6)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}Y_{n}(x)=K\frac{J(x)}{2\pi}

equality in lemma holding only upto a constant factor.

∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Given any
(a,b)\subseteq(0,2\pi)
, show that

(2.7)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}V_{a}^{b}(Y_{n})=K\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{x\in L\cap(a,b)}\left|J(x)\right|

where
K
is a constant.

note:
V_{a}^{b}(f)
is the variation of
f
in
(a,b)
.

Proof.

Let

(2.8)

\displaystyle V_{n}=V_{a}^{b}(Y_{n})=\int_{a}^{b}\left|\frac{1}{\log(n)G(n)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tilde{S}^{\prime}_{k}(x;f)\left|c_{k}\right|\right|dx

(2.9)

\displaystyle V_{n}\leq\frac{1}{\log(n)G(n)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{a}^{b}\left|\tilde{S}^{\prime}_{k}(x;f)\left|c_{k}\right|\right|dx

using a lemma from
[
1
]

(2.10)

\displaystyle V_{n}\leq\frac{1}{\log(n)G(n)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{a}^{b}\left|\tilde{S}_{k}(x;f^{\prime})-\sum_{y\in L}\frac{J(y)}{\pi}\tilde{D}_{k}(x-y))\left|c_{k}\right|\right|dx

(2.11)

\displaystyle V_{n}\leq\frac{1}{\log(n)G(n)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{a}^{b}\left|\tilde{S}_{k}(x;f^{\prime})-\sum_{y\in L}\frac{J(y)}{\pi}\tilde{D}_{k}(x-y))\left|c_{k}\right|\right|dx

(2.12)

\displaystyle V_{n}\leq\frac{1}{\log(n)G(n)}(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{a}^{b}\left|\tilde{S}_{k}(x;f^{\prime})\left|c_{k}\right|\right|dx-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{a}^{b}\left|\sum_{y\in L}\frac{J(y)}{\pi}\tilde{D}_{k}(x-y))\left|c_{k}\right|\right|dx)

Since
f^{\prime}
is BV,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\left|\tilde{S}_{k}(x;f^{\prime})\right|dx\sim O(1)

,So

(2.14)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{a}^{b}\left|\tilde{S}_{k}(x;f^{\prime})\left|c_{k}\right|\right|dx\sim\sum_{k=1}^{n}(O(1))\frac{V}{k}\sim O(\log(n))

(2.15)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{a}^{b}\left|\sum_{y\in L}\frac{J(y)}{\pi}\tilde{D}_{k}(x-y))\left|c_{k}\right|\right|dx\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{y\in L}\frac{J(y)}{\pi}\left|c_{k}\right|\int_{a}^{b}\left|\tilde{D}_{k}(x-y)\right|dx

(2.16)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{y\in L}\frac{J(y)}{\pi}\left|c_{k}\right|\int_{a}^{b}\left|\tilde{D}_{k}(x-y)\right|dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{y\in L\setminus(a,b)}\frac{J(y)}{\pi}\left|c_{k}\right|\int_{a}^{b}\left|\tilde{D}_{k}(x-y)\right|dx

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{y\in L\cap(a,b)}\frac{J(y)}{\pi}\left|c_{k}\right|\int_{a}^{b}\left|\tilde{D}_{k}(x-y)\right|dx

It is known that

(2.17)

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\left|\tilde{D}_{k}(x-y)\right|dx\sim 2\log(k)

if
y\in(a,b)
and

(2.18)

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\left|\tilde{D}_{k}(x-y)\right|dx\sim O(1)

if
y\notin(a,b)
.

(2.19)

\displaystyle V_{n}\leq\frac{1}{\log(n)G(n)}(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{a}^{b}\left|\tilde{S}_{k}(x;f^{\prime})\left|c_{k}\right|\right|dx

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{y\in L\setminus(a,b)}\frac{J(y)}{\pi}\left|c_{k}\right|\int_{a}^{b}\left|\tilde{D}_{k}(x-y)\right|dx

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{y\in L\cap(a,b)}\frac{J(y)}{\pi}\left|c_{k}\right|\int_{a}^{b}\left|\tilde{D}_{k}(x-y)\right|dx

Therefore

In the first term on the RHS

(2.20)

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\left|\tilde{S}_{k}(x;f^{\prime})\right|dx\sim O(1)

and hence first term goes to zero.

Coming to second term,
y\notin(a,b)
, so

(2.21)

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\left|\tilde{D}_{k}(x-y)\right|dx\sim O(1)

and hence second term also vanishes.

In the third term
y\in(a,b)
and hence

(2.22)

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\left|\tilde{D}_{k}(x-y)\right|dx=2\log(k)

.Also
|c_{k}|\sim\frac{1}{k}
, so after summation via integration and knowing
G(n)\sim\log(n)
, we get the result.
∎

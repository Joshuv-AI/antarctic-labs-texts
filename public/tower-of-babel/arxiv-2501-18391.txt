The extended Dirichlet space and criticality theory for nonlinear Dirichlet forms
Marcel Schmidt, Ian Zimmermann

Abstract
. In this paper we establish the existence of the extended Dirichlet space for nonlinear Dirichlet forms under mild conditions. We employ it to introduce and characterize criticality (recurrence) and subcriticality (transience) and establish basics of a potential theory.



1.
Introduction

Dirichlet forms were introduced by Beurling and Deny
[
6
,
7
]
. They (and their generalizations) have since become an important tool in various mathematical areas such as geometric analysis and stochastic analysis but also in mathematical physics. The leitmotif is that an order structure of the underlying Hilbert space can be employed to study properties of the generator and the induced semigroup, which under additional assumptions can even be related to a Markov process through a Feynman-Kac type formula - see
[
28
,
21
,
11
]
for a modern account. While this theory is linear - the functionals are bilinear forms and the associated operators are linear - key ideas clearly extend to some nonlinear functionals and operators.

In this text we treat nonlinear Dirichlet forms in the sense of Cipriani and Grillo
[
13
]
. These are lower semicontinuous convex functionals on
L^{2}
-spaces whose associated nonlinear semigroups are order preserving and
L^{\infty}
-contractive - in the linear case they correspond to Dirichlet forms in the wide sense (i.e. Dirichlet forms without dense domain). Indeed, this class of functionals has been studied and characterized before by Benilan and Picard
[
5
]
and, adding to the confusion, Benilan and Picard used the name nonlinear Dirichlet form for a slightly different class of functionals.

Benilan and Picard and later Cipriani and Grillo provided characterizations for nonlinear Dirichlet forms that are quite similar to the second Beurling-Deny criterion, which defines Dirichlet forms in the linear case. Nonlinear Dirichlet forms include energies of the
p
-Laplacian in various geometries (see e.g.
[
13
, Section 4]
), energies of the variable exponent
p(\cdot)
-Laplacian (see Section
6
), area and perimeter functionals (see
[
13
, Section 5]
) and Cheeger energies on metric measure spaces (see
[
3
]
) - just to name a few.

While certainly a lot of theory has been developed at an individual level for all of the mentioned examples, a general theory making some key results for Dirichlet forms also available for nonlinear Dirichlet forms is missing. Most notably, as of yet there is no way to associate a stochastic process to a suitably regular nonlinear Dirichlet form - first results in this direction have only been obtained for some nonlinear Lévy processes
[
16
]
and the
p
-Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

[
4
]
.

Recently (under some regularity conditions) a potential theory for nonlinear Dirichlet forms without homogeneity assumptions has been developed in
[
15
,
14
]
and the somewhat complicated characterization of nonlinear Dirichlet forms by Cipriani and Grillo has been simplified
[
9
,
32
]
. With the present article we seek to fill further gaps in the theory of nonlinear Dirichlet forms. More precisely, we show the existence of the extended Dirichlet space (Theorem
3.5
) and discuss the related criticality theory by introducing and characterizing criticality (recurrence) (Theorem
4.17
) and subcriticality (transience) (Theorem
4.16
). Both concepts are related to the validity of (weak) Hardy- and Poincaré inequalities (Theorem
4.8
and Theorem
4.9
), which might be of interest on their own right. Moreover, we show that there is a dichotomy between criticality and subcriticality under an irreducibility condition, see Corollary
4.18
. In the literature this dichotomy is sometimes called ground state alternative and our article is very much inspired by
[
31
,
20
]
, which show that this ground state alternative holds for certain nonlinear situations. As an application, for subcritical nonlinear Dirichlet forms we develop basics of a potential theory (Corollary
5.4
and Theorem
5.8
), which in some aspects goes beyond the results of
[
15
]
, and includes the situations recently discussed in
[
8
,
26
]
, but also energies of
p(\cdot)
-Laplacians as in
[
18
]
.

Extended Dirichlet spaces are an important tool for stochastic analysis of (quasi)regular Dirichlet forms. Among other things they allow the change of the measure of the underlying
L^{2}
-space, which for the associated process corresponds to a time change. Criticality and subcriticality can be formulated with their help and together with (weak) Hardy- and Poincaré inequalities these global properties determine the long time behavior of the associated semigroup/process (recurrence, transience, convergence rate to equilibrium). The potential theory of a (quasi)regular Dirichlet form plays a key role in the construction of the associated process. Again we refer to
[
21
,
28
]
for the construction of processes, potential theory, recurrence and transience and to
[
33
]
for the convergence rate to equilibrium. Beyond stochastic analysis subcriticality in terms of weighted Hardy inequalities and the dichotomy between criticality and subcriticality play a role for establishing optimal Hardy inequalities, which are used in Agmon estimates for weak solutions of associated elliptic problems, see e.g.
[
30
,
17
]
.

Our results do not necessarily hold for all nonlinear Dirichlet forms but require certain additional assumptions, which vary slightly from theorem to theorem. Those assumptions are reflexivity, completeness of the modular space, existence of the extended form and the (weak)
\Delta_{2}
-condition. At some places we do not even need to assume that we are given nonlinear Dirichlet forms, nonlinear order preserving forms are sufficient. We present the theorems with the minimal assumptions required for our proofs even though this makes the statements somewhat incoherent. The reader should note that all results in this text hold for reflexive nonlinear Dirichlet forms satisfying the
\Delta_{2}
-condition, a class that includes the most prominent examples, cf. Section
6
.

Claus noticed in his PhD thesis
[
15
]
that the Luxemburg seminorm of a nonlinear Dirichlet form is important for understanding its properties - indeed this is the key insight that made our article possible. In order to employ Luxemburg seminorms for our purposes we have to extend some aspects of the available theory for semimodulars (see e.g.
[
18
, Chapter 2]
) to our setting. More precisely, we introduce generalized semimodulars and their Luxemburg seminorms and study in quite some detail their lower semicontinuity with respect to different topologies on the underlying space (Theorem
A.13
and Theorem
A.23
) and the continuity of the embedding into the underlying space (Theorem
A.26
).

The paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
we review basic properties of nonlinear Dirichlet forms mostly following the existing literature. In Section
3
we introduce the modular space and the extended Dirichlet space and give a short account on excessive functions and invariant sets. Section
4
can be seen as the main part of this article, it discusses characterizations of criticality and subcriticality. In Section
5
we apply subcriticality to establish basics of a potential theory. In Section
6
we discuss two classes of examples. Here, we do not aim at greatest generality, but try to illustrate how our results fit into the existing literature. We chose to put the discussion of generalized semimodulars into Appendix
A
. It is more or less independent of the rest of the article and might be of use in other contexts as well.

The authors thank Ralph Chill and Simon Puchert for sharing their knowledge on nonlinear Dirichlet forms with them and René Schilling for pointing out some references. Moreover, M.S. acknowledges financial support of the DFG.

2.
Nonlinear Dirichlet forms, resolvents and the Green operator

Let
(X,\mathfrak{A},\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite measure space. We let
L^{2}(\mu)
be the Hilbert space of real-valued square integrable functions with inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and induced norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|
.

We write
L^{0}(\mu)
for the space of
\mu
-a.s. defined real-valued measurable functions. It is equipped with the topology of local convergence in measure. If we fix some integrable
h\colon X\to(0,\infty)
, this topology is induced by the
F
-norm

p_{h}\colon L^{0}(\mu)\to[0,\infty],\quad p_{h}(f)=\int_{X}\min\{|f|,h\}d\mu.

If
(f_{n})
converges to
f
in this topology, we write
f_{n}\overset{\mu}{\to}f
. This convergence holds if every subsequence of
(f_{n})
has a subsequence converging to
f

\mu
-a.s. The cone of
[0,\infty]
-valued
\mu
-a.s. defined measurable functions is denoted by
L^{+}(\mu)
.

Definition 2.1

(The Beurling-Deny criteria and nonlinear Dirichlet forms)
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}\colon L^{p}(\mu)\to(-\infty,\infty]
with
p\in\{0,2\}
. We say that a normal contraction
C\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}

operates on
{\mathcal{E}}
if for all
f,g\in L^{p}(\mu)
the inequality

{\mathcal{E}}(f+Cg)+{\mathcal{E}}(f-Cg)\leq{\mathcal{E}}(f+g)+{\mathcal{E}}(f-g)

holds. The functional
{\mathcal{E}}
satisfies the
first Beurling-Deny criterion
if the normal contraction
{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}},\,x\mapsto|x|
operates on
{\mathcal{E}}
and it satisfies the
second Beurling-Deny criterion
if all normal contractions operate on
{\mathcal{E}}
.

A
nonlinear order preserving form
is a proper lower semicontinuous convex functional
{\mathcal{E}}\colon L^{2}(\mu)\to(-\infty,\infty]
satisfying the first Beurling-Deny criterion and a
nonlinear Dirichlet form
is a nonlinear order preserving form also satisfying the second Beurling-Deny criterion.

Remark 2.2
.

(a)

If
{\mathcal{E}}\colon L^{2}(\mu)\to[0,\infty]
is a Dirichlet form in the sense of
[
21
]
(i.e. a densely defined lower semicontinuous quadratic form such that
{\mathcal{E}}(Cf)\leq{\mathcal{E}}(f)
for all normal contractions
C
and all
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
), then it satisfies the second Beurling-Deny criterion in the sense of the previous definition due to the parallelogram identity. To better distinguish them from the more general functionals considered in this text we call them
classical Dirichlet forms
or
bilinear Dirichlet forms
(because they are related to bilinear forms on their domain via polarization).

(b)

Based on results from
[
9
]
, it was recently established in
[
32
]
that our Definition of nonlinear Dirichlet forms yields the same as in
[
13
]
- see also Proposition
2.3
below. Indeed, the same class of functionals was already introduced and characterized earlier in
[
5
, Théorème 2.1]
without giving them a special name. We also note that in
[
5
, Section 2b]
a different class of functionals is called nonlinear Dirichlet form - this seems to have caused some confusion in earlier publications.

What we call nonlinear order preserving form is called nonlinear semi-Dirichlet form in
[
13
]
. We do not use this terminology to avoid confusion with the concept of semi-Dirichlet forms in the linear case, which is something different, see e.g.
[
29
]
.

(c)

Below we will also study extensions of nonlinear Dirichlet forms to
L^{0}(\mu)
, which is why we included functionals on this space in the previous definition.

For a convex functional
{\mathcal{E}}\colon L^{2}(\mu)\to(-\infty,\infty]
its
subgradient
is the multi-valued operator

\partial{\mathcal{E}}=\{(f,g)\in L^{2}(\mu)\times L^{2}(\mu)\mid\langle g,h-f\rangle+{\mathcal{E}}(f)\leq{\mathcal{E}}(h)\text{ all }h\in L^{2}(\mu)\}.

This is in line with the discussion in Subsection
A.3
after identifying
L^{2}(\mu)
and its dual space. If
{\mathcal{E}}
is proper, lower semicontinuous and convex, then by general principles
\partial{\mathcal{E}}
is
m
-accretive. This implies that the resolvent
G_{\alpha}=(\alpha+\partial{\mathcal{E}})^{-1}
,
\alpha>0
, is a well-defined single-valued
\alpha^{-1}
-Lipschitz continuous operator on
L^{2}(\mu)
. We refer to
[
38
, Section IV.1]
, where all of these claims and related things are discussed. The element
G_{\alpha}f
is the unique minimizer of the functional

L^{2}(\mu)\to[0,\infty],\quad g\mapsto{\mathcal{E}}(g)+\frac{\alpha}{2}\left\|g-\alpha^{-1}f\right\|^{2}.

Hence, it is related to the resolvent family
J_{\alpha}
,
\alpha>0
, introduced in Subsection
A.3
, through
G_{\alpha}f=J_{\alpha^{-1}}(\alpha^{-1}f)
,
\alpha>0
. It satisfies the resolvent identity

G_{\alpha}f=G_{\beta}(f+(\beta-\alpha)G_{\alpha}f),\quad\alpha,\beta>0,f\in L^{2}(\mu).

Moreover, for
f\in\overline{D({\mathcal{E}})}
, we have
\alpha G_{\alpha}f\to f
, as
\alpha\to\infty
.

The following fundamental observation explains the names ’nonlinear Dirichlet form’ and ’order preserving form’.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}\colon L^{2}(\mu)\to(-\infty,\infty]
be convex, lower semicontinuous and proper.

(a)

The following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

{\mathcal{E}}
is a nonlinear order preserving form.

(ii)

For all
f,g\in L^{2}(\mu)
we have

{\mathcal{E}}(f\wedge g)+{\mathcal{E}}(f\vee g)\leq{\mathcal{E}}(f)+{\mathcal{E}}(g).

(iii)

The resolvent
G_{\alpha}
,
\alpha>0
, is order preserving (i.e.
f\geq g
implies
G_{\alpha}f\geq G_{\alpha}g
for all
\alpha>0
).

(b)

{\mathcal{E}}
is a nonlinear Dirichlet form if and only if
\alpha G_{\alpha}
,
\alpha>0
, is order preserving and
L^{\infty}
-contractive, i.e.

\left\|\alpha G_{\alpha}f-\alpha G_{\alpha}g\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|f-g\right\|_{\infty}\text{ for all }f,g\in L^{2}(\mu)\cap L^{\infty}(\mu).

In this case,
\alpha G_{\alpha}
,
\alpha>0
, is also
L^{1}
-contractive.

Proof.

(a): The equivalence (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii) is readily verified by inserting
(f+g)/2
and
(f-g)/2
into the defining inequality of the first Beurling-Deny criterion. The equivalence of (ii) and (iii) is the content of
[
13
, Theorem 3.8]
.

(b): This is the content of
[
13
, Theorem 3.6]
together with the characterization of nonlinear Dirichlet forms in
[
32
]
, see also
[
5
, Théorème 2.1]
.
∎

We extend the resolvent of a nonlinear order preserving form
{\mathcal{E}}
to an operator
L^{+}(\mu)\to L^{+}(\mu)
as follows: For
f\in L^{+}(\mu)
we choose an increasing sequence
(f_{n})
of nonnegative functions in
L^{2}(\mu)
with
f_{n}\nearrow f
a.s. and set
G_{\alpha}f=\lim_{n\to\infty}G_{\alpha}f_{n}
. This limit exists a.s. due to
G_{\alpha}
being order preserving and it is independent of the approximating sequence due to the continuity of
G_{\alpha}
on
L^{2}(\mu)
. For
\beta\geq\alpha
the resolvent identity
G_{\alpha}=G_{\beta}({\rm id}+(\beta-\alpha)G_{\alpha})
extends to
L^{+}(\mu)
. Together with the order preservation, it implies that for
f\in L^{+}(\mu)
the map
(0,\infty)\to L^{+}(\mu)
,
\alpha\mapsto G_{\alpha}f
is decreasing. Hence, the operator

G\colon L^{+}(\mu)\to L^{+}(\mu),\quad Gf=\lim_{\alpha\to 0+}G_{\alpha}f

is well-defined. It is called
Green operator
or
potential operator
of
{\mathcal{E}}
.

If
{\mathcal{E}}
is a nonlinear Dirichlet form, for
\alpha>0
we extend
\alpha G_{\alpha}
to a contraction on
L^{1}(\mu)
by using the density of
L^{1}(\mu)\cap L^{2}(\mu)
in
L^{1}(\mu)
and the
L^{1}
-contractivity of
\alpha G_{\alpha}
. By the monotone convergence theorem, this extension to
L^{1}(\mu)
by continuity is compatible with the extension to
L^{+}(\mu)
discussed above. Moreover, we obtain an extension of
G_{\alpha}
,
\alpha>0
, to
L_{+}^{\infty}(\mu)
by simply restricting its extension to
L^{+}(\mu)
to the subset
L_{+}^{\infty}(\mu)=L^{\infty}(\mu)\cap L^{+}(\mu)
. Using the
L^{\infty}
-contractivity, it is readily verified that
\alpha G_{\alpha}
,
\alpha>0
, are contractions on
L^{\infty}_{+}(\mu)
.

3.
The modular space and the extended Dirichlet space

In this section we apply the abstract theory outlined in Appendix
A
to convex functionals on the spaces
L^{2}(\mu)
and
L^{0}(\mu)
. While we repeat the most important definitions in the main text, we often refer to the appendix for details.

3.1.
The modular space

We say that a functional
{\mathcal{E}}\colon L^{2}(\mu)\to(-\infty,\infty]
is
symmetric
if
{\mathcal{E}}(f)={\mathcal{E}}(-f)
for all
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
. In this case, properness and convexity imply that
0\in D({\mathcal{E}})
is a global minimizer of
{\mathcal{E}}
. Moreover, it is readily verified that if a normal contraction operates on
{\mathcal{E}}
, it also operates on the functional
{\mathcal{E}}-{\mathcal{E}}(0)
. Based on these observations we make the following standing assumptions.

Standing assumption
.

From now on all convex functionals
{\mathcal{E}}
in this text are assumed to be symmetric and satisfy
{\mathcal{E}}(0)=0
. In particular, they are proper and map to
[0,\infty]
.

Remark 3.1
.

(a)

Even if
{\mathcal{E}}
were not symmetric, many of our results would still hold true. In this case, one has to use versions of the abstract results from Appendix
A
on cones instead of vector spaces. We refrain from giving details.

Alternatively, if
{\mathcal{E}}
is not symmetric, one can obtain a symmetric functional by considering
{\mathcal{E}}^{sym}\colon L^{2}(\mu)\to[0,\infty],\,f\mapsto\frac{1}{2}\left({\mathcal{E}}(f)+{\mathcal{E}}(-f)\right)
. If a normal contraction operates on
{\mathcal{E}}
, it also operates on
{\mathcal{E}}^{sym}
.

(b)

If a symmetric convex functional
{\mathcal{E}}
with
{\mathcal{E}}(0)=0
is compatible with the normal contraction
C\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, then it satisfies
{\mathcal{E}}(Cf)\leq{\mathcal{E}}(f)
for all
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
.

A lower semicontinuous proper convex functional
{\mathcal{E}}\colon L^{2}(\mu)\to[0,\infty]
satisfying our standing assumption is a generalized semimodular on
L^{2}(\mu)
in the sense of Definition
A.1
. We write

M({\mathcal{E}})=\{f\in L^{2}(\mu)\mid\lim_{\lambda\to 0+}{\mathcal{E}}(\lambda f)=0\}={\rm lin}\,D({\mathcal{E}})

for the corresponding
modular space
and

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}=\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,{\mathcal{E}}}\colon M({\mathcal{E}})\to[0,\infty),\quad\left\|f\right\|_{L}=\inf\{\lambda>0\mid{\mathcal{E}}(\lambda^{-1}f)\leq 1\}

for the induced
Luxemburg seminorm
.

Lemma 3.2

(Luxemburg seminorms are compatible with contractions)
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a nonlinear order preserving form. Then
\left\||f|\right\|_{L}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L}
for all
f\in M({\mathcal{E}})
. If
{\mathcal{E}}
is a nonlinear Dirichlet form, then
\left\|Cf\right\|_{L}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L}
for all normal contractions
C\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
and all
f\in M({\mathcal{E}})
.

Proof.

The symmetry and the first Beurling-Deny criterion imply
{\mathcal{E}}(|f|)\leq{\mathcal{E}}(f)
. Hence, for
\lambda>0
we infer

{\mathcal{E}}(\lambda^{-1}|f|)={\mathcal{E}}(|\lambda^{-1}f|)\leq{\mathcal{E}}(\lambda^{-1}f).

This implies
\left\||f|\right\|_{L}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L}
.

Assume that
{\mathcal{E}}
is a nonlinear Dirichlet form and let
C\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a normal contraction. For
\lambda>0
let
C_{\lambda}\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
C_{\lambda}(t)=\lambda^{-1}C(\lambda x)
, which is also a normal contraction. Using that
{\mathcal{E}}
satisfies the second Beurling-Deny criterion and is symmetric, we obtain

{\mathcal{E}}(\lambda^{-1}Cf)={\mathcal{E}}(C_{\lambda}(\lambda^{-1}f))\leq{\mathcal{E}}(\lambda^{-1}f),

showing
\left\|Cf\right\|_{L}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L}
.
∎

We say that
{\mathcal{E}}
is
reflexive
if the image of
M({\mathcal{E}})
in the bidual
(M({\mathcal{E}}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})^{\prime\prime}
(under the canonical isometry) is norm dense. For more context on this property we refer to Appendix
A
.

Lemma 3.3

(Continuity of certain contractions)
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a reflexive nonlinear Dirichlet form and for
r>0
let
C_{r}\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
C_{r}(x)=(x\wedge r)\vee(-r)
. For all
f\in M({\mathcal{E}})
we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}C_{\varepsilon}(f)=0\text{ and }\lim_{R\to\infty}C_{R}(f)=f

with respect to
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
. In particular,
L^{1}(\mu)\cap L^{\infty}(\mu)\cap M({\mathcal{E}})
is dense in
(M({\mathcal{E}}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})
.

Proof.

Since
C_{r}
is a normal contraction, it follows from Lemma
3.2
that
\left\|C_{r}(f)\right\|_{L}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L}
. Since
\lim_{R\to\infty}C_{R}(f)=f
and
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}C_{\varepsilon}(f)=0
in
L^{2}(\mu)
, Lemma
A.15
shows that the claimed convergence holds weakly in
(M({\mathcal{E}}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})
. By Mazur’s lemma we can upgrade it to
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
-convergence by taking suitable convex combinations.

For a convex combination
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}C_{\varepsilon_{k}}(f)
and
\varepsilon\leq\min\{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{n}\}
we have

C_{\varepsilon}(f)=C_{\varepsilon}(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}C_{\varepsilon_{k}}(f)).

With this equality Lemma
3.2
implies

\left\|C_{\varepsilon}(f)\right\|_{L}\leq\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}C_{\varepsilon_{k}}(f)\right\|_{L}.

Since suitable convex combinations converge to
0
, this yields the statement for the first limit.

Let
D_{r}\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}},\quad D_{r}(x)=(x-r)_{+}-(x+r)_{-}
. Then
f-C_{r}(f)=D_{r}(f)
and for a convex combination we have

f-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}C_{R_{k}}(f)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}D_{R_{k}}(f).

Let
R\geq\max\{R_{1},\ldots,R_{n}\}
. It is readily verified that there exists a normal contraction
D
such that

D\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}D_{R_{k}}(f)\right)=D_{R}(f)=f-C_{R}(f).

Indeed,
D=D_{R^{\prime}}
with
R^{\prime}=R-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}R_{k}
is a possible choice. This equality and Lemma
3.2
yield

\left\|f-C_{R}(f)\right\|\leq\left\|f-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}C_{R_{k}}(f)\right\|_{L}.

Since suitable convex combinations converge to
0
, this yields the statement for the second limit.

The density statement follows from these convergences and that for
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
we have
f-C_{\varepsilon}(f)\in L^{1}(\mu)
and
C_{R}(f)\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

This lemma was established in
[
15
, Theorem 3.55]
for the nonlinear Dirichlet form
{\mathcal{E}}+\left\|\cdot\right\|^{2}
under the quasilinearity assumption
D({\mathcal{E}})=M({\mathcal{E}})
. We can drop quasilinearity and treat
{\mathcal{E}}
directly but have to assume reflexivity.

3.2.
The extended Dirichlet space

We extend every convex functional
{\mathcal{E}}\colon L^{2}(\mu)\to[0,\infty]
to a convex functional on
L^{0}(\mu)
by simply letting
{\mathcal{E}}(f)=\infty
for
f\in L^{0}(\mu)\setminus L^{2}(\mu)
. If this extended functional is lower semicontinuous with respect to local convergence in measure on
D({\mathcal{E}})
(i.e. for
(f_{n})
,
f
in
D({\mathcal{E}})
the convergence
f_{n}\overset{\mu}{\to}f
implies
{\mathcal{E}}(f)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}(f_{n})
), then the lower semicontinuous relaxation of
{\mathcal{E}}
on
L^{0}(\mu)
is an extension of
{\mathcal{E}}
(cf. Proposition
A.17
), which we call
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
. First we address the existence of this extension.

Theorem 3.5

(Existence of the extended Dirichlet space)
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}\colon L^{2}(\mu)\to[0,\infty]
be convex (with our standing assumptions). Assume one of the following two conditions:

(A)

(M({\mathcal{E}}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})
is a Banach space.

(B)

{\mathcal{E}}
is a nonlinear Dirichlet form.

Then
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
exists. If (A) holds, then
{\mathcal{E}}={\mathcal{E}}_{e}
(when
{\mathcal{E}}
is interpreted as a functional on
L^{0}(\mu)
).

Proof.

(A): According to Theorem
A.13
we need to show that for each
r>0
the Luxemburg seminorm

\left\|f\right\|_{L,r}=\inf\{\lambda>0\mid{\mathcal{E}}(\lambda^{-1}f)\leq r\}

is lower semicontinuous on
L^{0}(\mu)
with respect to local convergence in measure. Since Luxemburg seminorms with different parameters are equivalent (Lemma
A.3
),
(M({\mathcal{E}}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,r})
is a Banach space. By Theorem
A.26
and Remark
A.27
(applied to the generalized semimodular
r^{-1}{\mathcal{E}}
) the embedding

(M({\mathcal{E}}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,r})\to L^{2}(\mu),\quad f\mapsto f

is continuous. Now assume that
f\in L^{0}(\mu)
and
(f_{n})
is a sequence in
M({\mathcal{E}})
with
f_{n}\overset{\mu}{\to}f
. We assume that
(\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{L,r})
is bounded (else there is nothing to show). Without loss of generality we further assume
f_{n}\to f
almost surely and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{L,r}=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{L,r}

(else pass to a suitable subsequence). By the continuity of the embedding the sequence
(f_{n})
is bounded in
L^{2}(\mu)
. By the Banach-Saks theorem it has a subsequence
(f_{n_{k}})
and there exists
g\in L^{2}(\mu)
such that

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}f_{n_{k}}\to g\text{ in }L^{2}(\mu)\text{, as }N\to\infty.

From the almost sure convergence, which passes to the Césaro averages, we infer
f=g
. Hence, the
L^{2}
-lower semicontinuity of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,r}
implies

\left\|f\right\|_{L,r}\leq\liminf_{N\to\infty}\lVert\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}f_{n_{k}}\rVert_{L,r}\leq\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left\|f_{n_{k}}\right\|_{L,r}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{L,r}.

(B): Let
f\in D({\mathcal{E}})
and let
(f_{n})
in
L^{2}(\mu)
with
f_{n}\overset{\mu}{\to}f
. It suffices to show
{\mathcal{E}}(f)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}(f_{n})
.

1. Step: Assume that
f\in L^{1}(\mu)\cap D({\mathcal{E}})
and that there exists
M\geq 0
such that
|f_{n}|\leq M
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. We use the resolvent
J_{\lambda}=G_{1/\lambda}(\lambda^{-1}\cdot)
,
\lambda>0
, discussed in Subsection
A.3
.

For
\lambda>0
we have
J_{\lambda}f\in L^{1}(\mu)\cap L^{2}(\mu)
, see Proposition
2.3
and hence also
g_{\lambda}:=\lambda^{-1}(f-J_{\lambda}f)\in L^{1}(\mu)\cap L^{2}(\mu)
.
Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem and the Fenchel-Moreau theorem yield

\langle g_{\lambda},f\rangle-{\mathcal{E}}^{*}(g_{\lambda})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\langle g_{\lambda},f_{n}\rangle-{\mathcal{E}}^{*}(g_{\lambda})\right)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}(f_{n}).

Moreover, Proposition
A.30
(and
\varphi_{\lambda}=\langle g_{\lambda},\cdot\rangle
in the notation used there) yields

{\mathcal{E}}(f)=\lim_{\lambda\to 0+}\left(\langle g_{\lambda},f\rangle-{\mathcal{E}}^{*}(g_{\lambda})\right).

Combining both (in)equalities shows
{\mathcal{E}}(f)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}(f_{n})
.

2. Step: For
\varepsilon,R>0
we consider the normal contractions
C_{R}\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
C_{R}(x)=(x\wedge R)\vee(-R)
and
D_{\varepsilon}\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
D_{\varepsilon}(x)=x-C_{\varepsilon}(x)
. Then for any
g\in L^{2}(\mu)
and
R,\varepsilon>0
we have
|C_{R}g|\leq R
and
D_{\varepsilon}g\in L^{1}(\mu)
.
For
M>0
we now consider

f^{(M)}=C_{M}(D_{1/M}f)\quad\text{and}\quad f^{(M)}_{n}=C_{M}(D_{1/M}f_{n}).

Then
f^{(M)}\in L^{1}(\mu)\cap D({\mathcal{E}})
,
|f^{(M)}_{n}|\leq M
,
f^{(M)}_{n}\overset{\mu}{\to}f^{(M)}
, as
n\to\infty
, and
f^{(M)}\to f
in
L^{2}(\mu)
, as
M\to\infty
. Moreover, the compatibility with contractions implies
{\mathcal{E}}(f^{(M)}_{n})\leq{\mathcal{E}}(f_{n})
for all
M>0
. Using Step 1 and the
L^{2}
-lower semicontinuity of
{\mathcal{E}}
, we infer

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}(f)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{M\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}(f^{(M)})\leq\liminf_{M\to\infty}\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}(f^{(M)}_{n})\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}(f_{n}).\hfill\qed

If
{\mathcal{E}}
is proper and
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
exists, it is a generalized semimodular on
L^{0}(\mu)
and we write
M({\mathcal{E}}_{e})={\rm lin}D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})\subseteq L^{0}(\mu)
for the modular space of
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
and denote by
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}=\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,{\mathcal{E}}_{e}}
the induced Luxemburg seminorm. As discussed in Proposition
A.20
,
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}
is the lower semicontinuous relaxation of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
on
L^{0}(\mu)
(when both seminorms are extended by
\infty
outside the corresponding modular spaces). In particular,
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}
is also lower semicontinuous with respect to local convergence in measure.

Definition 3.6

(Extended Dirichlet form)
.

If
{\mathcal{E}}
is a nonlinear Dirichlet form (resp. a nonlinear order preserving form and
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
exists), we call
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
the
extended nonlinear Dirichlet form
(resp. the
extended nonlinear order preserving form
) and
(M({\mathcal{E}}_{e}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}})
the
extended Dirichlet space
(resp. the
extended modular space
) of
{\mathcal{E}}
.

.

{\varrho}

Using excessive functions and

For linear Markovian semigroups on

The following proposition provides basic properties of the extended functional

. To better state it we say that
(f_{n})
in

is an
approximating sequence
for
f\in L^{0}(\mu)
(cf. Definition

) if
(f_{n})
is

-Cauchy and satisfies

. Moreover, the functional
{\mathcal{E}}
is said to satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition
(cf. Definition

.

Proposition 3.8

(Basic properties of extended functionals)
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}\colon L^{2}(\mu)\to[0,\infty]
be a proper lower semicontinuous convex functional such that
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
exists. If
{\mathcal{E}}
is reflexive, then for
f\in L^{0}(\mu)
the following holds.

(a)

There exists an approximating sequence
(f_{n})
for
f
if and only if
f\in M({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
. In this case,
\left\|f\right\|_{L_{e}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{L}
.

(b)

If
{\mathcal{E}}
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition, then
M({\mathcal{E}}_{e})=D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
and
(f_{n})
is an approximating sequence for
f
if and only if
(f_{n})
is
{\mathcal{E}}
-Cauchy and
f_{n}\overset{\mu}{\to}f
. In this case,

{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}(f_{n}).

(c)

If
w\colon X\to(0,\infty)
is measurable and
(f_{n})
in
L^{2}(w\mu)
such that
f_{n}\to f
weakly in
L^{2}(w\mu)
, then

{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f_{n})\text{ and }\left\|f\right\|_{L_{e}}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{L_{e}}.

Proof.

(a) + (b): This is contained in Theorem
A.23
.

(c): We only show the statement for
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
, the proof for
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}
follows along the same lines. Without loss of generality we assume
\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f_{n})=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f_{n})<\infty
(else pass to a suitable subsequence). By the Banach-Saks theorem there exists a subsequence
(f_{n_{k}})
such that the Césaro-means
g_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}f_{n_{k}}\to f
in
L^{2}(w\mu)
. Since
L^{2}(w\mu)
continuously embeds into
L^{0}(\mu)
, we infer from the lower semicontinuity of
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
and its convexity

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{N\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(g_{N})\leq\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f_{n_{k}})=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f_{n}).\hfill\qed

Proposition 3.9

(Extended forms are compatible with contractions)
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}\colon L^{2}(\mu)\to[0,\infty]
be a proper lower semicontinuous convex functional such that
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
exists.

(a)

If
{\mathcal{E}}
is a nonlinear order preserving form, then
D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})\cap L^{2}(\mu)=D({\mathcal{E}})
and
M({\mathcal{E}}_{e})\cap L^{2}(\mu)=M({\mathcal{E}})
.

(b)

If
{\mathcal{E}}
is a nonlinear order preserving form, then
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
satisfies the first Beurling-Deny criterion and
\left\||f|\right\|_{L_{e}}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L_{e}}
for all
f\in M({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
.

(c)

If
{\mathcal{E}}
is a nonlinear Dirichlet form, then
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
satisfies the second Beurling-Deny criterion and
\left\|Cf\right\|_{L_{e}}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L_{e}}
for all
f\in M({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
and all normal contractions
C\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

(a): It suffices to show
D({\mathcal{E}})\supseteq D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})\cap L^{2}(\mu)
. For
f\in D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})\cap L^{2}(\mu)
we choose a sequence
(f_{n})
in
D({\mathcal{E}})
with
f_{n}\overset{\mu}{\to}f
and
{\mathcal{E}}(f_{n})\to{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)
. By the dominated convergence theorem the limits

f=\lim_{n\to\infty}(f_{n}\wedge|f|)\vee(-|f|)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\lim_{m\to\infty}(f_{n}\wedge|f_{m}|)\vee(-|f_{m}|)

hold in
L^{2}(\mu)
. Hence, the
L^{2}
-lower semicontinuity of
{\mathcal{E}}
and Proposition
2.3
yield

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}(f)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\liminf_{m\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}((f_{n}\wedge|f_{m}|)\vee(-|f_{m}|))

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\liminf_{m\to\infty}({\mathcal{E}}(f_{n})+2{\mathcal{E}}(f_{m}))

\displaystyle=3{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)<\infty,

showing
f\in D({\mathcal{E}})
.

(b) + (c): We first show the statements for
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
. Let
C
be a normal contraction such that
{\mathcal{E}}(f+Cg)+{\mathcal{E}}(f-Cg)\leq{\mathcal{E}}(f+g)+{\mathcal{E}}(f-g)
for all
f,g\in L^{2}(\mu)
. If
f+g,f-g\in D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
(else there is nothing to show), there exist sequences
(h_{n}),(\tilde{h}_{n})
in
D({\mathcal{E}})
with
h_{n}\overset{\mu}{\to}f+g
,
\tilde{h}_{n}\overset{\mu}{\to}f-g
,
{\mathcal{E}}(h_{n})\to{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f+g)
and
{\mathcal{E}}(\tilde{h}_{n})\to{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f-g)
, see Proposition
A.17
. Then
f_{n}=(h_{n}+\tilde{h}_{n})/2\overset{\mu}{\to}f
and
g_{n}=(h_{n}-\tilde{h}_{n})/2\overset{\mu}{\to}g
. Using
Cg_{n}\overset{\mu}{\to}Cg
, the lower semicontinuity of
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
and
{\mathcal{E}}={\mathcal{E}}_{e}
on
L^{2}(\mu)
, we infer

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f+Cg)+{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f-Cg)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}({\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f_{n}+Cg_{n})+{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f_{n}-Cg_{n}))

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}({\mathcal{E}}(f_{n}+Cg_{n})+{\mathcal{E}}(f_{n}-Cg_{n}))

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}({\mathcal{E}}(f_{n}+g_{n})+{\mathcal{E}}(f_{n}-g_{n}))

\displaystyle={\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f+g)+{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f-g).

Since
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}
is the Luxemburg seminorm of
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
, the statement on
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}
can be inferred from the Beurling-Deny criteria as in the proof of Lemma
3.2
.
∎

3.3.
Excessive functions

In this subsection we briefly discuss excessive functions, which will feature at various places throughout the text.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}\colon L^{2}(\mu)\to[0,\infty]
be a nonlinear order preserving form. As in the bilinear case we say that a measurable function
h\colon X\to[0,\infty)
is
{\mathcal{E}}
-excessive
, if
G_{\alpha}(\alpha h)\leq h
for all
\alpha>0
. In the following proposition we use the lower derivative
d^{+}{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
, see Lemma
A.29
.

Proposition 3.10

(Characterization of excessive functions)
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}\colon L^{2}(\mu)\to[0,\infty]
be a nonlinear order preserving form and let
h\colon X\to[0,\infty)
measurable.

(a)

h
is
{\mathcal{E}}
-excessive if and only if
{\mathcal{E}}(f\wedge h)\leq{\mathcal{E}}(f)
for all
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
.

(b)

If
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
exists, then
h
is
{\mathcal{E}}
-excessive if and only if
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f\wedge h)\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)
for all
f\in L^{0}(\mu)
.

(c)

If
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
exists and
h\in D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
, then the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

h
is
{\mathcal{E}}
-excessive.

(ii)

{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(h)=\min\{{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)\mid f\in L^{0}(\mu)\text{ with }f\geq h\}

(iii)

d^{+}{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(h,\varphi)\geq 0
for all nonnegative
\varphi\in M({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
.

If
h\in D({\mathcal{E}})
, then in (ii) and (iii)
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
can be replaced by
{\mathcal{E}}
.

Proof.

(a): Since
G_{\alpha}
is order preserving,
G_{\alpha}(\alpha h)\leq h
is equivalent to the inclusion
G_{\alpha}(\alpha K_{h})\subseteq K_{h}
, where
K_{h}
is the closed convex cone

K_{h}=\{f\in L^{2}(\mu)\mid f\leq h\}.

Moreover, the Hilbert space projection
P_{h}
onto
K_{h}
is given by
P_{h}f=f\wedge h
. With this at hand the statement follows from the standard characterization of the invariance of closed convex sets under the resolvent, see e.g.
[
13
, Theorem 3.4]
. It states that
K_{h}
is invariant under the resolvent, i.e.,
G_{\alpha}(\alpha K_{h})\subseteq K_{h}
for all
\alpha>0
, if and only if
{\mathcal{E}}(P_{h}f)\leq{\mathcal{E}}(f)
,
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
. This is precisely the statement of (a).

(b): If
h
is
{\mathcal{E}}
-excessive, the inequality
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f\wedge h)\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)
follows from (a) and the lower semicontinuity of
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
, cf. proof of Proposition
3.9
. Now assume that
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f\wedge h)\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)
for
f\in L^{0}(\mu)
. Let
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
. Without loss of generality we assume
f\in D({\mathcal{E}})
(else there is nothing to show). Using
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f\wedge h)\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)={\mathcal{E}}(f)<\infty
and
h\geq 0
, we infer
f\wedge h\in D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})\cap L^{2}(\mu)
, which by Proposition
3.9
implies
f\wedge h\in D({\mathcal{E}})
. With this at hand the desired inequality follows from the inequality for
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
and the fact that
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
and
{\mathcal{E}}
agree on
D({\mathcal{E}})
.

(c): (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii): Assume that
h
is excessive and
f\geq h
. Then
h=f\wedge h
and from (b) we infer
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(h)={\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f\wedge h)\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)
, showing that
h
is a minimizer.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i): Assume that
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(h)=\min\{{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)\mid f\in L^{0}(\mu)\text{ with }f\geq h\}
holds. The first Beurling-Deny criterion implies

{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f\wedge h)+{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f\vee h)\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)+{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(h).

For
f\in D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
we have
f\vee h\in D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
and
f\vee h\geq h
. Hence,
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f\vee h)\geq{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(h)
and substracting
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f\vee h)
on both sides of the previous inequality yields
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f\wedge h)\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii): This is obvious.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii): Let
f\in D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
with
f\geq h
. Then
f-h\geq 0
with
f-h\in M({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
and (iii) imply
d^{+}{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f,f-h)\geq 0
. According to Lemma
A.29
we have

{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(h)+d^{+}{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(h,f-h)\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)

and arrive at
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(h)\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)
.
∎

Remark 3.11
.

(a)

If
{\mathcal{E}}
(and hence also
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
) is a quadratic form, then
D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
is a vector space and for
f,g\in D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
we have

d^{+}{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f,g)=\frac{1}{2}\left({\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f+g)-{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f-g)\right).

Hence,
d^{+}{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
equals two times the bilinear form induced by
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
on
D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})\times D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
via polarization. In particular, our characterization of excessive functions in
D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
equals the known one via weakly superharmonic functions, see e.g.
[
34
, Theorem 2.52]
.

(b)

If
{\mathcal{E}}
is a nonlinear Dirichlet form, the compatibility with normal contractions implies that nonnegative constant functions are excessive. The existence of further excessive functions, in particular of excessive functions in
D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
, is discussed in Section
5
.

(c)

We consider the functional
{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\colon L^{2}(\mu)\to[0,\infty]
,
{\mathcal{E}}_{1}(f)={\mathcal{E}}(f)+\frac{1}{2}\left\|f\right\|^{2}
. If
G_{\alpha}
,
\alpha>0
, denotes the resolvent of
{\mathcal{E}}
, then it is readily verified that
G_{1+\alpha}
,
\alpha>0
, is the resolvent of
{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
, (cf. Lemma
4.1
below). If
\psi\in L^{2}(\mu)
is nonnegative, then
h=G_{1}\psi\geq 0
and the resolvent identity implies
G_{1+\alpha}(\alpha h)\leq h
,
\alpha>0
. Hence,
h
is
{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
-excessive.

Lemma
3.2
shows that contractions operate on the Luxemburg seminorm if they operate on
{\mathcal{E}}
. However, due to the nonlinearity, a stronger property than excessivity has to be assumed to transfer the inequality
{\mathcal{E}}(f\wedge h)\leq{\mathcal{E}}(f)
to the Luxemburg seminorm.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}\colon L^{2}(\mu)\to[0,\infty]
be a nonlinear order preserving form and let
h\colon X\to[0,\infty)
measurable such that for all
\lambda>0
the function
\lambda h
is
{\mathcal{E}}
-excessive. Then
\left\|f\wedge h\right\|_{L}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L}
for all
f\in M({\mathcal{E}})
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda>0
such that
{\mathcal{E}}(\lambda^{-1}f)\leq 1
. Using Proposition
3.10
and that
\lambda^{-1}h
is
{\mathcal{E}}
-excessive, we arrive at

{\mathcal{E}}(\lambda^{-1}(f\wedge h))={\mathcal{E}}((\lambda^{-1}f)\wedge(\lambda^{-1}h))\leq{\mathcal{E}}(\lambda^{-1}f)\leq 1.

This shows
\left\|f\wedge h\right\|_{L}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L}
.
∎

Remark 3.13
.

(a)

Assume that for some
1\leq p<\infty
the functional
{\mathcal{E}}
is
p
-homogeneous, i.e.,
{\mathcal{E}}(\lambda f)=|\lambda|^{p}{\mathcal{E}}(f)
for all
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
. In this case,
h
is
{\mathcal{E}}
-excessive if and only if
\lambda h
is
{\mathcal{E}}
-excessive for one/any
\lambda>0
and the statement of the previous proposition holds for all
{\mathcal{E}}
-excessive functions. Indeed, in the
p
-homogeneous case we have
\left\|f\right\|_{L}={\mathcal{E}}(f)^{1/p}
and so the inequality for the Luxemburg seminorm is trivial.

(b)

Functions
h\colon X\to[0,\infty)
with
\left\|f\wedge h\right\|_{L}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L}
,
f\in M({\mathcal{E}})
, play a role below, see Remark
4.11
, but we do not have any good existence result for them (except in the homogeneous case). For proving existence of excessive functions in Section
5
we use their characterization as minimizers given in Proposition
3.10
(c). We do not know whether a similar characterization holds for functions
h
with
\left\|f\wedge h\right\|_{L}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L}
,
f\in M({\mathcal{E}})
. The reason is that the crucial inequality
{\mathcal{E}}(f\wedge h)+{\mathcal{E}}(f\vee h)\leq{\mathcal{E}}(f)+{\mathcal{E}}(h)
that is used in the proof of Proposition
3.10
(c) need not hold for the Luxemburg seminorm - indeed it even fails for most quadratic forms.

(c)

For bilinear order preserving forms excessive functions play a major role. As discussed already in Remark
3.7
, if
{\mathcal{E}}
is an order preserving quadratic form and
h
is
{\mathcal{E}}
-excessive, then the
h
-transform
{\mathcal{E}}^{h}\colon L^{2}(h^{2}\mu)\to[0,\infty]
,
{\mathcal{E}}^{h}(f)={\mathcal{E}}(hf)
is a bilinear Dirichlet form. Hence, with this transformation, many results for bilinear Dirichlet forms can be transferred to bilinear order preserving forms. In the nonlinear case this does not seem to hold. Excessivity of
h
is not enough to ensure that
{\mathcal{E}}^{h}
is a nonlinear Dirichlet form and, similar to the previous remark, general existence results for functions
h
for which
{\mathcal{E}}^{h}
is a nonlinear Dirichlet form remain unclear. This is the main reason why below we state some results for nonlinear order preserving forms, while others are only stated for nonlinear Dirichlet forms.

3.4.
Invariant sets and irreducibility

In this subsection we study the kernel of the Luxemburg seminorm of extended Dirichlet forms with the help of invariant sets.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}\colon L^{2}(\mu)\to[0,\infty]
be proper, lower semicontinuous and convex. A measurable set
A\subseteq X
is called
{\mathcal{E}}
-invariant
if

{\mathcal{E}}(1_{A}f)\leq{\mathcal{E}}(f),\quad f\in L^{2}(\mu).

We call
{\mathcal{E}}

irreducible
if every
{\mathcal{E}}
-invariant set is null or co-null.

The collection of all
{\mathcal{E}}
-invariant sets is denoted by
\mathfrak{B}_{\rm inv}
and we call

{\mathfrak{A}}_{\rm inv}=\{A\subseteq X\mid A\in\mathfrak{B}_{\rm inv}\text{ or }X\setminus A\in\mathfrak{B}_{\rm inv}\}

the
invariant
\sigma
-algebra
of
{\mathcal{E}}
, which is indeed a
\sigma
-algebra by the following lemma.

Lemma 3.14
.

{\mathfrak{A}}_{\rm inv}
is the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
\mathfrak{B}_{\rm inv}
.

Proof.

Clearly,
\emptyset,X\in{\mathfrak{A}}_{\rm inv}
and
{\mathfrak{A}}_{\rm inv}
is stable under taking complements. Hence, it remains to prove that
{\mathfrak{A}}_{\rm inv}
is stable under taking countable intersections. This however follows from
1_{\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}}f=\lim_{N\to\infty}\prod_{n=1}^{N}1_{A_{n}}f
and the lower semicontinuity of
{\mathcal{E}}
.
∎

The operator of multiplication by
1_{A}
is the orthogonal projection to the closed subspace

I_{A}=\{f\in L^{2}(\mu)\mid f=0\text{ a.s.{} on }X\setminus A\}.

It is known that the projection onto
I_{A}
reduces the value of
{\mathcal{E}}
if and only if
G_{\alpha}(\alpha\cdot)
,
\alpha>0
, leaves
I_{A}
invariant, see e.g.
[
13
, Theorem 3.4]
. Hence,
A
is
{\mathcal{E}}
-invariant if and only if
G_{\alpha}(\alpha I_{A})\subseteq I_{A}
,
\alpha>0
, which is equivalent to

G_{\alpha}(1_{A}f)=1_{A}G_{\alpha}(1_{A}f),\quad\alpha>0,\,f\in L^{2}(\mu).

Theorem 3.15
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a nonlinear Dirichlet form.

(a)

For every
h\in\ker\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}
and
\alpha\geq 0
the set
\{h>\alpha\}
is
{\mathcal{E}}
-invariant.

(b)

If
{\mathcal{E}}
is irreducible, then
\ker\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}\cdot 1
.

Proof.

(a): The assumption
\left\|h\right\|_{L_{e}}=0
implies
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(nh)=0
for all
n\geq 1
, see Lemma
A.3
. Now consider the functions
h_{n}=(n(h-\alpha)_{+})\wedge 1
. These are normal contractions of
nh
and hence satisfy
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(h_{n})\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(nh)=0
. For
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
we have
f1_{\{h>\alpha\}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}(f\wedge h_{n})\vee(-h_{n})
in
L^{2}(\mu)
. Moreover,
since
D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})\cap L^{2}(\mu)=D({\mathcal{E}})
, see Proposition
3.9
, we have
(f\wedge h_{n})\vee(-h_{n})\in D({\mathcal{E}})
. Using the
L^{2}
-lower semicontinuity of
{\mathcal{E}}
and that
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
satisfies the first Beurling-Deny criterion, we infer

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}(f1_{\{h>\alpha\}})

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}((f\wedge h_{n})\vee(-h_{n}))

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}_{e}((f\wedge h_{n})\vee(-h_{n}))

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}({\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)+2{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(h_{n}))

\displaystyle={\mathcal{E}}(f).

For the last equality we again used
{\mathcal{E}}={\mathcal{E}}_{e}
on
L^{2}(\mu)
.

(b): Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be irreducible and let
\left\|h\right\|_{L_{e}}=0
. The compatibility of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}
with normal contractions discussed in Proposition
3.9
shows
\left\|h_{+}\right\|_{L_{e}}=\left\|h_{-}\right\|_{L_{e}}=0
, and so we can assume
h\geq 0
. For any
\alpha\geq 0
the invariance of
\{h>\alpha\}
and the irreducibility of
{\mathcal{E}}
yield
\mu(\{h>\alpha\})=0
or
\mu(\{h\leq\alpha\})=0
. This can only hold for constant functions.
∎

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}\colon L^{2}(\mu)\to[0,\infty]
be a nonlinear order preserving form for which
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
exists. Then
A\subseteq X
is
{\mathcal{E}}
-invariant if and only if
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(1_{A}f)\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)
for all
f\in L^{0}(\mu)
.

Proof.

First assume that
A
is
{\mathcal{E}}
-invariant. For
f\in D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
we choose a sequence
(f_{n})
in
D({\mathcal{E}})
with
f_{n}\overset{\mu}{\to}f
and
{\mathcal{E}}(f_{n})\to{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)
. The lower semicontinuity of
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
and
1_{A}f_{n}\in D({\mathcal{E}})
, which follows from
{\mathcal{E}}(1_{A}f_{n})\leq{\mathcal{E}}(f_{n})
, imply

{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(1_{A}f)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}(1_{A}f_{n})\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}(f_{n})={\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f).

Now assume
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(1_{A}f)\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)
for all
f\in L^{0}(\mu)
.
It suffices to show
{\mathcal{E}}(1_{A}f)\leq{\mathcal{E}}(f)
for
f\in D({\mathcal{E}})
.
Since
D({\mathcal{E}})=D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})\cap L^{2}(\mu)
by Proposition
3.9
, the inequality
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(1_{A}f)\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)
implies
1_{A}f\in D({\mathcal{E}})
for such
f
.
Since
{\mathcal{E}}
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
agree on
D({\mathcal{E}})
, we infer
{\mathcal{E}}(1_{A}f)\leq{\mathcal{E}}(f)
.
∎

Corollary 3.17
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a nonlinear Dirichlet form. If
\left\|1\right\|_{L_{e}}=0
, then
\ker\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}=L^{0}(X,{\mathfrak{A}}_{\rm inv},\mu)
. In particular,
\ker\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}={\mathbb{R}}\cdot 1
implies that
{\mathcal{E}}
is irreducible.

Proof.

By definition the identity
\left\|1\right\|_{L_{e}}=0
implies
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(n)=0
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Assume that
A\subseteq X
is
{\mathcal{E}}
-invariant. From Lemma
3.16
we infer
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(n1_{A})\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(n)=0
for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, showing
\left\|1_{A}\right\|_{L_{e}}=0
. Since the kernel of the extended Luxemburg seminorm is a vector space and
1_{A},1\in\ker\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}
, we infer
1_{X\setminus A}\in\ker\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}
. This implies that simple functions of the form
\sum_{k=1}^{N}\alpha_{k}1_{A_{k}}
with
\alpha_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
A_{k}\in{\mathfrak{A}}_{\rm inv}
, belong to
\ker\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}
. Such functions are dense in
L^{0}(X,{\mathfrak{A}}_{\rm inv},\mu)
with respect to local convergence in measure. Hence, the lower semicontinuity of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}
implies

\ker\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}\supseteq L^{0}(X,{\mathfrak{A}}_{\rm inv},\mu).

Now assume that
\left\|h\right\|_{L_{e}}=0
. The compatibility with normal contractions implies
\left\|h_{+}\right\|_{L_{e}}=\left\|h_{-}\right\|_{L_{e}}=0
and so we can assume
h\geq 0
. Theorem
3.15
implies that for
\alpha\geq 0
the set
\{h>\alpha\}
is
{\mathcal{E}}
-invariant. This implies
h\in L^{0}(X,{\mathfrak{A}}_{\rm inv},\mu)
.

Now assume that
\ker\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}={\mathbb{R}}\cdot 1
. What we already proved yields the identity
L^{0}(X,{\mathfrak{A}}_{\rm inv},\mu)={\mathbb{R}}\cdot 1
. This implies that each
A\in{\mathfrak{A}}_{\rm inv}
is null or co-null, showing the irreducibility of
{\mathcal{E}}
.
∎

4.
Criticality theory

In this section we prove the main results of this paper, namely characterizations of criticality and subcriticality. Roughly speaking, criticality means that the potential operator
G
is infinite on a large class of functions and subcriticality is the property that the potential operator is finite for one strictly positive function. We show how these concepts are related to the validity of weighted Hardy inequalities and completeness of the extended Dirichlet space. Some results of this section do not only hold for nonlinear Dirichlet forms but also for nonlinear order preserving forms. Since this might be of interest, we note where this is the case and formulate the theorems accordingly.

4.1.
The Green operator and Hardy inequalities

In this subsection we characterize finiteness of the Green operator in terms of a Hardy inequality. Only order preservation is required here.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a nonlinear order preserving form and let
w\in L^{\infty}_{+}(\mu)
. We define the functional
{\mathcal{E}}_{w}\colon L^{2}(\mu)\to[0,\infty]
by

{\mathcal{E}}_{w}(f)={\mathcal{E}}(f)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}|f|^{2}wd\mu.

It is readily verified that
{\mathcal{E}}_{w}
is a nonlinear order preserving form with
D({\mathcal{E}}_{w})=D({\mathcal{E}})
. The resolvent of
{\mathcal{E}}_{w}
is denoted by
(G^{w}_{\alpha})
,
\alpha>0
, and we write
G^{w}
for its Green operator.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume the situation just described.

(a)

\partial{\mathcal{E}}_{w}=\partial{\mathcal{E}}+w
(here
w
denotes the operator of multiplication by
w
).

(b)

G^{w}_{\alpha}f=G_{\alpha}(f-wG^{w}_{\alpha}f)
,
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
,
\alpha>0
.

(c)

If
\tilde{w}\in L^{\infty}_{+}(\mu)
, then
G^{w}_{\alpha}f=G^{\tilde{w}}_{\alpha}(f+(\tilde{w}-w)G^{w}_{\alpha}f)
,
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
,
\alpha>0
.

Proof.

(a): The subgradient of the continuous functional
f\mapsto\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}|f|^{2}wd\mu
is the operator of multiplication by
w
. With this observation the statement is straightforward.

(b) + (c): Using (a) these can be proven exactly as the resolvent formula.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a nonlinear order preserving form. For
w\in L^{+}(\mu)
let
K(w)=\int_{X}wGwd\mu\in[0,\infty]
(in the integral we use the convention
0\cdot\infty=0
).

(a)

For all
f\in M({\mathcal{E}})
we have

\int_{X}|f|wd\mu\leq(1+K(w))\left\|f\right\|_{L}.

(b)

If for some
C\geq 0
and all
f\in M({\mathcal{E}})
the inequality

\int_{X}|f|wd\mu\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{L}

holds, then
K(w/C)\leq 1
.

Proof.

(a): It suffices to consider the case
K(w)<\infty
and, using the monotone convergence theorem, it suffices to consider
w\in L^{2}_{+}(\mu)
. Since
w-\alpha G_{\alpha}w\in\partial{\mathcal{E}}(G_{\alpha}w)
, Lemma
4.1
applied to the constant function
\alpha
yields
w\in\partial{\mathcal{E}}_{\alpha}(G_{\alpha}w).
We infer

\langle w,f-G_{\alpha}w\rangle\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{\alpha}(f)-{\mathcal{E}}_{\alpha}(G_{\alpha}w)

for all
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
. Rearranging this inequality and letting
\alpha\to 0+
leads to

\langle w,f\rangle\leq{\mathcal{E}}(f)+K(w)

for all
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
. Since
{\mathcal{E}}
satisfies the first Beurling-Deny criterion and is symmetric, this implies
\langle w,|f|\rangle\leq{\mathcal{E}}(f)+K(w)
for all
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
. The previous inequality and
{\mathcal{E}}(f)\leq 1
if and only if
\left\|f\right\|_{L}\leq 1
yield the claim.

(b): It suffices to treat the case
C=1
. Without loss of generality we assume
w\in L^{2}_{+}(\mu)
. It suffices to show
\langle w,G_{\alpha}w\rangle\leq 1
for all
\alpha>0
. Since
w-\alpha G_{\alpha}w\in\partial{\mathcal{E}}(G_{\alpha}f)
, we have

\langle w-\alpha G_{\alpha}w,0-G_{\alpha}w\rangle\leq{\mathcal{E}}(0)-{\mathcal{E}}(G_{\alpha}w),

which implies
{\mathcal{E}}(G_{\alpha}w)\leq\langle w,G_{\alpha}w\rangle-\alpha\left\|G_{\alpha}w\right\|^{2}
.
In particular,
{\mathcal{E}}(G_{\alpha}w)<\infty
so that
G_{\alpha}w\in M({\mathcal{E}})
.
Therefore, the assumed inequality yields
\langle w,G_{\alpha}w\rangle\leq\left\|G_{\alpha}w\right\|_{L}
.
We show
\left\|G_{\alpha}w\right\|_{L}\leq 1
.

Assume this were not the case, i.e.,
\left\|G_{\alpha}w\right\|_{L}>1
. Lemma
A.3
then shows
\left\|G_{\alpha}w\right\|_{L}\leq{\mathcal{E}}(G_{\alpha}w)
. Using all the inequalities, we infer

\left\|G_{\alpha}w\right\|_{L}\leq{\mathcal{E}}(G_{\alpha}w)\leq\langle w,G_{\alpha}w\rangle-\alpha\left\|G_{\alpha}w\right\|^{2}\leq\left\|G_{\alpha}w\right\|_{L}-\alpha\left\|G_{\alpha}w\right\|^{2}.

Since
\alpha>0
and
\left\|\alpha G_{\alpha}\right\|_{L}>1
implies
G_{\alpha}w\neq 0
, this inequality can never hold and so we obtain
\left\|G_{\alpha}w\right\|_{L}\leq 1
.
∎

An inequality of the type
\int_{X}|f|wd\mu\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{L}
,
f\in M({\mathcal{E}})
, as in the previous proposition, is called
Hardy inequality
with weight
w
. We can improve the constants given in the previous proposition.

Corollary 4.3

(Optimal constants)
.

Assume the situation of the previous proposition. For
w\in L^{+}(\mu)
we let

\tilde{K}(w)=\inf\{C>0\mid K(w/C)\leq 1\}

and denote by

\mu(w)=\sup\{\left\|fw\right\|_{1}/\left\|f\right\|_{L}\mid f\in M({\mathcal{E}})\}

the optimal constant in the Hardy inequality with weight
w
. Then
\tilde{K}(w)\leq\mu(w)\leq 2\tilde{K}(w)
.

Proof.

For
C>\tilde{K}(w)
the monotonicity of
K
in the argument implies
K(w/C)\leq 1
. Hence, (a) of the previous proposition applied to
w/C
yields
\mu(w)\leq 2C
.

Part (b) of the previous proposition implies
K(w/\mu(w))\leq 1
. Hence,
\tilde{K}(w)\leq\mu(w)
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Due to the lack of homogeneity also the constant
\tilde{K}(w)
is not optimal. In the bilinear case (i.e. when
{\mathcal{E}}
is a quadratic form and the resolvent is linear) it is knwon that

\mu(w)=\sqrt{2K(w)}=\sqrt{2}\tilde{K}(w)

is the best constant, see e.g.
[
21
, Lemma 1.5.3]
for the case of bilinear Dirichlet forms. Note that at a first glance the factor
\sqrt{2}
is missing in the statement of
[
21
, Lemma 1.5.3]
. However, the Green operator we use here is
1/2
-times the Green operator considered in
[
21
]
, because for quadratic forms
\partial{\mathcal{E}}
is two times the self-adjoint generator of
{\mathcal{E}}
.

Example 4.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}\colon L^{2}(\mu)\to[0,\infty]
be a nonlinear order preserving form for which
(M({\mathcal{E}}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})
is a Banach space. Then there exists
C>0
such that
K(|f|/(C\left\|f\right\|_{2}))\leq 1
for all
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
. In particular,
G(f/(C\left\|f\right\|_{2}))<\infty
a.s. if
f>0
a.s.

Proof.

(M({\mathcal{E}}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})
being a Banach space implies that
(M({\mathcal{E}}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})
continuously embeds into
L^{2}(\mu)
, see the proof of Theorem
3.5
or Theorem
A.26
and Remark
A.27
. Hence, there exists
C>0
such that

\left(\int_{X}|g|^{2}d\mu\right)^{1/2}\leq C\left\|g\right\|_{L}

for all
g\in M({\mathcal{E}})
.
With this at hand the statement follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the previous proposition.
∎

Theorem 4.6

(Finite Green operator vs. Hardy inequality)
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a nonlinear order preserving form. The following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

There exists a measurable
g\colon X\to(0,\infty)
with
Gg<\infty
a.s.

(ii)

There exists a measurable
w\colon X\to(0,\infty)
such that

\int_{X}|f|wd\mu\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L},\quad f\in M({\mathcal{E}}).

If
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
exists, then the inequality in (ii) extends to
M({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii): Let
g\colon X\to(0,\infty)
with
Gg<\infty
. Without loss of generality we assume
g\in L^{1}(\mu)
and consider
w=g/(Gg\vee 1)
. Using that
G
is order preserving, we obtain

\int_{X}wGwd\mu\leq\int_{X}\frac{g}{Gg\vee 1}Ggd\mu\leq\int_{X}gd\mu<\infty.

Hence, Proposition
4.2
shows the claim.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i): Proposition
4.2
shows
\int_{X}wGwd\mu\leq 1
. Since
w>0
a.s., this implies
Gw<\infty
a.s.

That the inequality in (ii) extends to
M({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
follows from Fatou’s lemma.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

The novelty here is that finiteness of
G
is related to weighted Hardy inequalities with respect to the Luxemburg seminorm. Once this is quantified in Proposition
4.2
, the statement of Theorem
4.6
follows as in the bilinear case (cf. the discussion preceding
[
21
, Theorem 1.5.1]
).

4.2.
Weak Hardy and Poincaré inequalities

In this subsection we study the completeness of the extended Dirichlet space
(M({\mathcal{E}}_{e}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}})
and relate it to weak Hardy and Poincaré inequalities.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a nonlinear Dirichlet form. The following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

\ker\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}=\{0\}
.

(ii)

(M({\mathcal{E}}_{e}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}})
is a Banach space.

(iii)

The embedding
(M({\mathcal{E}}_{e}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}})\to L^{0}(\mu)
,
f\mapsto f
, is continuous.

(iv)

For one/all integrable
w\colon X\to(0,\infty)
and one/all
1\leq p<\infty
there exists a decreasing function
\alpha\colon(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
such that for all
r>0

\left(\int_{X}|f|^{p}wd\mu\right)^{1/p}\leq\alpha(r)\left\|f\right\|_{L_{e}}+r\left\|f\right\|_{\infty},\quad f\in M({\mathcal{E}}_{e})\cap L^{\infty}(\mu).

Proof.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii): This follows from Theorem
A.26
and Remark
A.27
, which states that the required implication from Theorem
A.26
does not need reflexivity of
{\mathcal{E}}
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i):
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}
being a norm implies
\ker\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}=\{0\}
.

(iv)
\Rightarrow
(iii): Assume the inequality in (iv) is satisfied for
p\geq 1
, the integrable function
w\colon X\to(0,\infty)
and
\alpha\colon(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
. Let
(f_{n})
in
M({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
with
\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{L_{e}}\to 0
. We need to show
f_{n}\overset{\mu}{\to}0
.

Using (iv) and the compatibility of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}
with normal contractions, see Proposition
3.9
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\int_{X}(|f_{n}|\wedge 1)^{p}wd\mu\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha(r)\left\||f_{n}|\wedge 1\right\|_{L_{e}}+r\left\||f_{n}|\wedge 1\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha(r)\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{L_{e}}+r

for all
r>0
. This shows
\int_{X}(|f_{n}|\wedge 1)^{p}wd\mu\to 0
, which implies
f_{n}\overset{\mu}{\to}0
.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(iv): Let
w\colon X\to(0,\infty)
integrable and let
p\geq 1
. Assume that (iv) does not hold for
w
and
p
. Then there exists
R>0
and a sequence
(f_{n})
such that

\left(\int_{X}|f_{n}|^{p}wd\mu\right)^{1/p}\geq n\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{L_{e}}+R\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{\infty},\quad n\geq 1.

By Proposition
3.9
we can assume
f_{n}\geq 0
(else consider
|f_{n}|
) and by rescaling we can assume
\int_{X}|f_{n}|^{p}wd\mu=1
. This implies
\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{L_{e}}\to 0
and
\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{\infty}\leq 1/R
. Since
w\mu
is a finite measure, by the uniform boundedness we can assume
f_{n}\to f
weakly in
L^{2}(w\mu)
(after passing to a subsequence if necessary). Using Proposition
3.8
, we infer

\left\|f\right\|_{L_{e}}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{L_{e}}=0.

Our assumption (i) implies
f=0
. Since
1\in L^{2}(w\mu)
and
f_{n}\geq 0
, the weak convergence in
L^{2}(w\mu)
yields

\int_{X}|f_{n}|wd\mu=\int_{X}f_{n}wd\mu\to 0.

Without loss of generality we can therefore assume
f_{n}\to 0

\mu
-a.s. (else pass to a suitable subsequence). Using this convergence, the uniform boundedness of
(f_{n})
and the finiteness of
w\mu
, Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem implies

\int_{X}|f_{n}|^{p}wd\mu\to 0,

which contradicts our assumption
\int_{X}|f_{n}|^{p}wd\mu=1
.
∎

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a nonlinear Dirichlet form. If
\ker\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}={\mathbb{R}}\cdot 1
, then the following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

(M({\mathcal{E}}_{e})/{\mathbb{R}}\cdot 1,\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}})
is a Banach space.

(ii)

The embedding

(M({\mathcal{E}}_{e})/{\mathbb{R}}\cdot 1,\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}})\to L^{0}(\mu)/{\mathbb{R}}\cdot 1,\quad f+{\mathbb{R}}\cdot 1\mapsto f+{\mathbb{R}}\cdot 1,

is continuous, where
L^{0}(\mu)/{\mathbb{R}}\cdot 1
is equipped with the quotient topology.

(iii)

For one/all integrable
w\colon X\to(0,\infty)
and one/all
1\leq p<\infty
there exists a decreasing function
\alpha\colon(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
such that for all
r>0

\left(\int_{X}|f-\overline{f}|^{p}wd\mu\right)^{1/p}\leq\alpha(r)\left\|f\right\|_{L_{e}}+r\delta(f),\quad f\in M({\mathcal{E}}_{e})\cap L^{\infty}(\mu).

Here,
\overline{f}=\frac{1}{\left\|w\right\|_{1}}\int_{X}fwd\mu
and
\delta(f)=\esssup f-\essinf f
.

Proof.

(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii): Since
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
is lower semicontinuous on
L^{0}(\mu)
, this equivalence is contained in Theorem
A.26
and Remark
A.27
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii): Let
w\colon X\to(0,\infty)
be integrable and
p\geq 1
and assume the inequality in (iii) does not hold for any
\alpha
. Using
\ker\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}=\ker\delta={\mathbb{R}}\cdot 1
, there exists
R>0
and a sequence
(f_{n})
in
M({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
with
\int_{X}|f_{n}|^{p}wd\mu=1
,
\int_{X}f_{n}wd\mu=0
and

1\geq n\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{L_{e}}+R\delta(f_{n}).

This implies
\delta(f_{n})\leq 1/R
and
\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{L_{e}}\to 0
. Moreover,
\int_{X}f_{n}wd\mu=0
yields
\essinf f\leq 0\leq\esssup f
, which implies
\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}\leq\delta(f)\leq 1/R
.

Using
\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{L_{e}}\to 0
and (ii) we find
C_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
n\in{\mathbb{R}}
, such that
f_{n}-C_{n}\overset{\mu}{\to}0
. Below we show that
C_{n}\to 0
. This implies
f_{n}\overset{\mu}{\to}0
and Lebesgue’s dominated convergence yields

0=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{X}|f_{n}|^{p}wd\mu=1,

a contradiction.

C_{n}\to 0
: The inequality
|C_{n}|\leq|f_{n}-C_{n}|+|f_{n}|\leq|f_{n}-C_{n}|+1/R\overset{\mu}{\to}1/R
yields that
(C_{n})
is bounded so that also
(f_{n}-C_{n})
is uniformly bounded. With this at hand,
\int_{X}f_{n}wd\mu=0
,
f_{n}-C_{n}\overset{\mu}{\to}0
and Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem yield

-C_{n}\int_{X}wd\mu=\int_{X}(f_{n}-C_{n})wd\mu\to 0,\text{ as }n\to\infty.

Since
\int_{X}wd\mu>0
, this shows
C_{n}\to 0
.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii): It suffices to show that for
(f_{n})
in
M({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
with
\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{L_{e}}\to 0
there exist constants
C_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, such that
f_{n}-C_{n}\overset{\mu}{\to}0
.

Let
w\colon X\to(0,\infty)
be integrable such that the inequality in (iii) holds. We define
T\colon L^{0}(\mu)\to L^{0}(\mu)
,
Tf=(f\wedge 1)\vee(-1)
. Using the intermediate value theorem, for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we choose
C_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\int_{X}T(f_{n}-C_{n})wd\mu=0.

The inequality in (iii) and
|T(f_{n}-C_{n})|\leq 1
imply that for
r>0
we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{X}|T(f_{n}-C_{n})|^{p}wd\mu\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha(r)\left\|T(f_{n}-C_{n})\right\|_{L_{e}}+2r

\displaystyle\leq\alpha(r)\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{L_{e}}+2r,

where for the last inequality we used the compatibility of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}
with normal contractions, see Proposition
3.9
, and
\ker\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}={\mathbb{R}}\cdot 1
. This implies
T(f_{n}-C_{n})\to 0
in
L^{1}(w\mu)
, which in turn yields
f_{n}-C_{n}\overset{\mu}{\to}0
.
∎

Remark 4.10

(iv) is called weak Hardy inequality and an inequality as in Theorem

(iii) is called weak Poincaré inequality. Inequalities of this kind were introduced in

for bilinear Dirichlet forms to study non-exponential convergence rates to equilibrium of the associated semigroups. While it may not be possible to extend these results to the fully nonlinear case, for

That weak Hardy/Poincaré inequalities can be used to characterize the completeness of the extended Dirichlet space was observed in

[

]
but heavily rely on the new Theorem
A.26
.

Remark 4.11

(Order preserving forms)

Both theorems remain valid for nonlinear order preserving forms under some conditions: Theorem

4.3.
Criticality theory

In this subsection we bring together the results of the previous two subsections. Before proving the main results we return to properties of the functional
{\mathcal{E}}_{w}
defined for
w\in L^{\infty}_{+}(\mu)
in Subsection
4.1
.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a nonlinear Dirichlet form and let
w\in L^{\infty}_{+}(\mu)
.

(a)

G^{w}_{\alpha}(\alpha+w)\leq 1
,
\alpha>0
. In particular,
G^{w}w\leq 1
.

(b)

{\mathcal{E}}(G^{w}_{\alpha}f)\leq\langle f-(w+\alpha)G^{w}_{\alpha}f,G^{w}_{\alpha}f\rangle
, for
\alpha>0
and
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
.

(c)

If
w\in L^{1}(\mu)\cap L^{\infty}_{+}(\mu)
, then

{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(G^{w}w)\leq\int_{X}w(1-G^{w}w)G^{w}wd\mu\leq\left\|w\right\|_{1}.

Proof.

(a): Let
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}\colon L^{2}((w+\alpha)\mu)\to[0,\infty],\,\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(f)={\mathcal{E}}(f)
. It is a nonlinear Dirichlet form. The Hilbert spaces
L^{2}((w+\alpha)\mu)
and
L^{2}(\mu)
are equal as sets but carry different inner products, which we denote by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{(w+\alpha)\mu}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mu}
. We prove (a) by showing that the resolvent
\tilde{G}_{1}
of
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}
(which is defined with respect to the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{(w+\alpha)\mu}
) satisfies the identity
G^{w}_{\alpha}((w+\alpha)f)=\tilde{G}_{1}f
,
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
. The
L^{\infty}
-contractivity of
\tilde{G}_{1}
then implies the claim.

By definition we have
g=\tilde{G}_{1}f
if and only if
f-g\in\partial\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(g)
. The latter is equivalent to

\langle f-g,h-g\rangle_{(w+\alpha)\mu}\leq\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(h)-\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(g)

for all
h\in L^{2}((w+\alpha)\mu)
. Using the definition of
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}
, this inequality is equivalent to

\langle(w+\alpha)(f-g),h-g\rangle\leq{\mathcal{E}}(h)-{\mathcal{E}}(g)

for all
h\in L^{2}(\mu)
. This statement is equivalent to
(w+\alpha)(f-g)\in\partial{\mathcal{E}}(g)
, which, using
\partial{\mathcal{E}}_{w}=\partial{\mathcal{E}}+w
, is equivalent to

(w+\alpha)f-\alpha g\in\partial{\mathcal{E}}_{w}(g)
. The latter is equivalent to
g=G_{\alpha}^{w}((w+\alpha)f)
.

(b): We saw in Lemma
4.1
that
G_{\alpha}^{w}f=G_{\alpha}(f-wG_{\alpha}^{w}f)
. Using this and the equality
g-\alpha G_{\alpha}g\in\partial{\mathcal{E}}(G_{\alpha}g)
for
g=f-wG_{\alpha}^{w}f
, we infer

\langle f-wG_{\alpha}^{w}f-\alpha G_{\alpha}^{w}f,h-G_{\alpha}^{w}f\rangle\leq{\mathcal{E}}(h)-{\mathcal{E}}(G_{\alpha}^{w}f).

Applied to
h=0
, this yields

{\mathcal{E}}(G_{\alpha}^{w}f)\leq\langle f-(w+\alpha)G_{\alpha}^{w}f,G_{\alpha}^{w}f\rangle.

(c): By definition we have
G_{\alpha}^{w}w\overset{\mu}{\to}G^{w}w
, as
\alpha\to 0+
, and, since
w\in L^{2}(\mu)
,
G_{\alpha}^{w}w\in D({\mathcal{E}})
. The lower semicontinuity of
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
and (b) imply

{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(G^{w}w)\leq\liminf_{\alpha\to 0+}{\mathcal{E}}(G_{\alpha}^{w}w)\leq\liminf_{\alpha\to 0+}\langle w-(w+\alpha)G_{\alpha}^{w}w,G_{\alpha}^{w}w\rangle.

With this at hand the statement follows from Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem and
G_{\alpha}^{w}w,G^{w}w\leq 1
.
∎

Definition 4.13

(Criticality and subcriticality)
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}\colon L^{2}(\mu)\to[0,\infty]
be a nonlinear order preserving form. We say that
{\mathcal{E}}
is
critical
if for every
f\in L^{1}_{+}(\mu)
we have
\mu(\{f>0\}\cap\{Gf<\infty\})=0
. We say that
{\mathcal{E}}
is
subcritical
if there exists a measurable
f\colon X\to(0,\infty)
such that
Gf<\infty

\mu
-a.s.

Remark 4.14
.

For bilinear Dirichlet forms criticality is also called
recurrence
and subcriticality is also called
transience
.

Example 4.15
.

Example
4.5
shows that
{\mathcal{E}}
is subcritical if
(M({\mathcal{E}}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})
is a Banach space.

We say that
{\mathcal{E}}
satisfies the
weak
\Delta_{2}
-condition
if for all
(f_{n})
in
L^{2}(\mu)
the convergence
{\mathcal{E}}(f_{n})\to 0
implies
{\mathcal{E}}(2f_{n})\to 0
; see the discussion after Definition
A.6
for more background. As discussed in Lemma
A.8
, the weak
\Delta_{2}
-condition is equivalent to
\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{L}\to 0
if and only if
{\mathcal{E}}(f_{n})\to 0
.

Theorem 4.16

(Characterization subcriticality)
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a nonlinear Dirichlet form satisfying the weak
\Delta_{2}
-condition. Then the following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

{\mathcal{E}}
is subcritical.

(ii)

The equivalent conditions of Theorem
4.6
hold for
{\mathcal{E}}
.

(iii)

The equivalent conditions of Theorem
4.8
hold for
{\mathcal{E}}
.

Proof.

(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii): Assertion (i) of Theorem
4.6
is the definition of subcriticality.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii): By (ii) there exists
w\colon X\to(0,\infty)
with
\int_{X}|f|wd\mu\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L_{e}}
for all
f\in M({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
. This implies that the kernel of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}
is trivial, which is one of the equivalent conditions of Theorem
4.8
.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii): Let
w\in L^{1}(\mu)\cap L^{\infty}(\mu)
such that
w>0
a.s. and
\left\|w\right\|_{1}\leq 1
. We set
w_{n}=n^{-1}w
. Then
w_{n}(1-G^{w_{n}}w_{n})\geq 0
by Lemma
4.12
. We show that

W=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n}}w_{n}(1-G^{w_{n}}w_{n})

satisfies
W>0
a.s. and

\int_{X}|f|Wd\mu\leq 2\left\|f\right\|_{L},\quad f\in M({\mathcal{E}}).

According to Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.12
, we have
G_{\alpha}(w_{n}(1-G_{\alpha}^{w_{n}}w_{n}))=G^{w_{n}}_{\alpha}w_{n}\leq 1
. Using
G^{w_{n}}_{\alpha}w_{n}\leq G^{w_{n}}w_{n}
, this implies
G_{\alpha}(w_{n}(1-G^{w_{n}}w_{n}))\leq 1
and hence
G(w_{n}(1-G^{w_{n}}w_{n}))\leq 1
. This leads to

\int_{X}w_{n}(1-G^{w_{n}}w_{n})G(w_{n}(1-G^{w_{n}}w_{n}))d\mu\leq\left\|w_{n}\right\|_{1}\leq\frac{1}{n},

which by Proposition
4.2
implies

\int_{X}|f|w_{n}(1-G^{w_{n}}w_{n})d\mu\leq(1+1/n)\left\|f\right\|_{L},\quad f\in M({\mathcal{E}}).

Hence, it remains to show
W>0
.

Lemma
4.12
yields
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(G^{w_{n}}w_{n})\leq\left\|w_{n}\right\|_{1}\leq n^{-1}
and hence the weak
\Delta_{2}
-condition, which by Lemma
A.20
extends to
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
, implies
\left\|G^{w_{n}}w_{n}\right\|_{L_{e}}\to 0
. This convergence and (iii) imply
G^{w_{n}}w_{n}\overset{\mu}{\to}0
, see Theorem
4.8
. Moreover, by Lemma
4.1
(c),
n\mapsto G^{w_{n}}w_{n}
is decreasing. Combined, we obtain that the sets
X_{n}=\{w_{n}(1-G^{w_{n}}w_{n})>0\}
satisfy
X_{n}\nearrow X
a.s., showing
W>0
a.s.
∎

Theorem 4.17

(Characterization criticality)
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a nonlinear Dirichlet form satisfying the weak
\Delta_{2}
-condition. Then the following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

{\mathcal{E}}
is critical.

(ii)

1\in M({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
and
\left\|1\right\|_{L_{e}}=0
.

(iii)

There exists a sequence
(e_{n})
in
M({\mathcal{E}})
with
0\leq e_{n}\leq 1
,
e_{n}\overset{\mu}{\to}1
and
\left\|e_{n}\right\|_{L}\to 0
.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii): Let
w\in L^{1}(\mu)\cap L^{\infty}(\mu)
with
w>0
. As in the proof of Theorem
4.16
we infer
G(w(1-G^{w}w))\leq 1
. Hence, criticality implies
w(1-G^{w}w)=0
and we obtain
G^{w}w=1
.
Using Lemma
4.12
, we infer

{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(1)={\mathcal{E}}_{e}(G^{w}w)\leq\int_{X}w(1-G^{w}w)G^{w}wd\mu=0.

The weak
\Delta_{2}
-condition for
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
implies
\ker{\mathcal{E}}_{e}=\ker\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}
and we arrive at
1\in M({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
and
\left\|1\right\|_{L_{e}}=0
.

(ii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii): By our characterization of the extended Luxemburg seminorm
\left\|1\right\|_{L_{e}}=0
is equivalent to the existence of
(e_{n})
in
M({\mathcal{E}})
such that
e_{n}\overset{\mu}{\to}1
and
\left\|e_{n}\right\|_{L}\to 0
. Since
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
is compatible with normal contractions, see Lemma
3.2
, these
(e_{n})
can be chosen to satisfy
0\leq e_{n}\leq 1
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i): For
w\in L^{1}_{+}(\mu)
let
K(w)=\int_{X}wGwd\mu
. If
w\neq 0
, then (ii) implies that for any
n\geq 0
we have

\int_{X}|1|wd\mu>0=n\left\|1\right\|_{L_{e}}.

Together with Proposition
4.2
, this implies
K(w)=\infty
for all
0\neq w\in L^{1}_{+}(\mu)
. For
g\in L^{1}_{+}(\mu)
we consider the function
w=g/(Gg\vee 1)
(here division by
\infty
is interpreted as
0
). As in the proof Theorem
4.6
, it is readily verified that
K(w)\leq\left\|g\right\|_{1}
. Hence,
w=0
, which implies
Gg=\infty
on
\{g>0\}
. This shows criticality.
∎

Corollary 4.18
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a nonlinear Dirichlet form satisfying the weak
\Delta_{2}
-condition. If
{\mathcal{E}}
is irreducible, then
{\mathcal{E}}
is either critical or subcritical.

Proof.

Since
{\mathcal{E}}
is irreducible, we have
\ker\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}\cdot 1
, see Theorem
3.15
.
Because the kernel of a seminorm is a linear space, it is either
\{0\}
or
{\mathbb{R}}\cdot 1
.
Now, Theorem
4.16
and Theorem
4.17
yield the claim.
∎

Remark 4.19
.

For energies of
p
-Laplace type operators the previous corollary is known as ground state alternative (with
1
being interpreted as a generalized ground state - the so-called Agmon ground state - in the critical case). It is contained in
[
31
]
for
p
-Laplace type operators on manifolds and in
[
20
]
for
p
-Laplacians on weighted graphs with slight variations (e.g. due to
p
-homogeneity the weighted Hardy inequalities are formulated in terms of a weighted

-norm instead of a weighted

-norm).

Remark 4.20

(Comparison to the bilinear case)
.

In principle we adapt the proofs given in
[
21
]
to the nonlinear setting to establish our characterizations of criticality and subcriticality. There are however two key differences:

(a)

If
{\mathcal{E}}
is a nonlinear subcritical Dirichlet form, the finiteness of
Gf
holds for some particular measurable function
f\colon X\to(0,\infty)
, which is more or less explicitly given. In contrast, for bilinear Dirichlet forms subcriticality implies
Gf<\infty
a.s. for all
f\in L^{1}_{+}(\mu)
.

(b)

For bilinear Dirichlet forms criticality is equivalent to
\mu(\{0<Gf<\infty\})=0
for all
f\in L^{1}_{+}(\mu)
.

In summary, for bilinear Dirichlet forms both subcriticality and criticality have slightly different conditions characterizing them. The reason for this is the validity of Hopf’s maximal ergodic inequality, see
[
21
, Lemma 1.5.2]
, the linearity of the resolvent and
D({\mathcal{E}})
being dense in
L^{2}(\mu)
(which is always assumed in the bilinear case). As of yet we do not have a variant of Hopf’s maximal ergodic inequality for the nonlinear case. However, even if it holds in some form, the nonlinearity of the resolvent prevents the proofs given in
[
21
]
to immediately carry over.

5.
Equilibrium potentials and a glimpse at potential theory

In this section we briefly discuss how subcriticality can be used to show the existence of equilibrium potentials, which are important for establishing a potential theory for nonlinear order preserving forms via capacities. While we are not going to discuss all the details, we do provide some basic properties.

Here we assume that
X
is a Hausdorff topological space and that
\mu
is a
\sigma
-finite Borel measure on
X
(i.e. a locally finite measure on the Borel-
\sigma
-algebra of
X
). Moreover,
{\mathcal{E}}\colon L^{2}(\mu)\to[0,\infty]
is assumed to be a nonlinear order preserving form for which
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
exists and
h\colon X\to[0,\infty)
is an
{\mathcal{E}}
-excessive function.

For a measurable set
A\subseteq X
let

\mathcal{L}_{A}=\{f\in L^{0}(\mu)\mid f\geq h\text{ a.s.{} on some open neighborhood of }A\}

and

{\rm cap}_{h}(A)={\rm cap}_{{\mathcal{E}},h}(A)=\inf\{{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)\mid f\in\mathcal{L}_{A}\}.

It follows directly from the first Beurling-Deny criterion for
{\mathcal{E}}
that for all
A,B\subseteq X
we have

{\rm cap}_{h}(A\cap B)+{\rm cap}_{h}(A\cup B)\leq{\rm cap}_{h}(A)+{\rm cap}_{h}(B).

Two typical situations where this construction is used are the following:

Example 5.1

(0-Capacity for extended Dirichlet forms)
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a nonlinear Dirichlet form. In this case,
1
is an excessive function (not the only one, see below). The induced capacity
{\rm cap}_{{\mathcal{E}},1}
is an extension of the capacity
{\rm Cap}_{(0)}
introduced at the end of
[
21
, Section 2.1]
to the nonlinear case.

Example 5.2

(1-Capacity for nonlinear order preserving forms)
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a nonlinear order preserving form but instead of
{\mathcal{E}}
we consider
{\mathcal{E}}_{1}={\mathcal{E}}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\cdot\right\|^{2}
(cf. the definition of
{\mathcal{E}}_{w}
at the beginning of Subsection
4.2
). It is a nonlinear order preserving form for which
(M({\mathcal{E}}_{1}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,{\mathcal{E}}_{1}})
is a Banach space. The completeness can be inferred from Theorem
A.26
and Remark
A.27
, see also
[
15
, Theorem 3.7]
. Hence, by Theorem
3.5
the extended form
({\mathcal{E}}_{1})_{e}
exists and
D(({\mathcal{E}}_{1})_{e})=D({\mathcal{E}})
. We saw in Remark
3.11
that
h=G_{1}\psi
with
0\leq\psi\in L^{2}(\mu)
is an
{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
-excessive function.

Hence,
{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
with
h
satisfies the above assumptions and

{\rm cap}_{{\mathcal{E}}_{1},h}(A)=\inf\{{\mathcal{E}}_{1}(f)\mid f\in\mathcal{L}_{A}\}.

This capacity is an extension of the capacity constructed in
[
28
, Section III.2]
to the nonlinear case.

One important feature of the previous example is that the excessive function
h
belongs to the domain of the functional, as this implies that the whole space has finite capacity. The existence of such excessive functions under relatively mild assumptions is discussed next. For certain uniqueness aspects we need the strict convexity of
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
, where
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
is called
strictly convex
if
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}((f+g)/2)<({\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)+{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(g))/2
for all
f,g\in D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
.

Theorem 5.3

(Existence of excessive functions)
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}\colon L^{2}(\mu)\to[0,\infty]
be a nonlinear order preserving form. Assume that either
(M({\mathcal{E}}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})
is a Banach space or that
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
exists and
{\mathcal{E}}
is reflexive and subcritical. Then the following holds:

(a)

Let
g\in L^{0}(\mu)
and let
A\subseteq X
be measurable such that there exists
f\in D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
with
f\geq g
a.s. on
A
. Then there exists an
{\mathcal{E}}
-excessive function
0\leq h\in D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
with
h\geq g
a.s. on
A
and

{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(h)=\min\{{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)\mid f\in L^{0}(\mu)\text{ with }f\geq g\text{ on }A\}.

If
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
is strictly convex, then
h
is unique. Moreover, if
(M({\mathcal{E}}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})
is a Banach space, then
{\mathcal{E}}={\mathcal{E}}_{e}
and
h\in D({\mathcal{E}})
.

(b)

If there exist
g\in D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
with
g>0
a.s., then there exists an
{\mathcal{E}}
-excessive function
h\colon X\to(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

(a): Let
g
be given as in the statement. Our assumption implies

I=\inf\{{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)\mid f\in L^{0}(\mu)\text{ with }f\geq g\text{ on }A\}<\infty.

By Proposition
3.10
(c) (more precisely by the same argument as in its proof) any function attaining this infimum is
{\mathcal{E}}
-excessive. Hence, we need to show the existence of a minimizer.

Let
(h_{n})
be a sequence in
D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
with
h_{n}\geq g
a.s. on
A
such that
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(h_{n})\searrow I
. Since
|h_{n}|\geq h_{n}\geq g
on
A
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(|h_{n}|)\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(h_{n})
, we can assume
h_{n}\geq 0
. Since
(h_{n})
is
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
-bounded, it is also
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}
-bounded.

Case 1:
(M({\mathcal{E}}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})
is a Banach space. In this case
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
exists and
{\mathcal{E}}={\mathcal{E}}_{e}
, see Theorem
3.5
. Since
(M({\mathcal{E}}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})
is a Banach space, as discussed in the proof of Example
4.5
,
(M({\mathcal{E}}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})
continuously embeds into
L^{2}(\mu)
. Hence
(h_{n})
is bounded in
L^{2}(\mu)
and by the Banach-Saks theorem we find a subsequence
(h_{n_{k}})
such that the Césaro means
f_{N}=(1/N)\sum_{k=1}^{N}h_{n_{k}}
converge to some
h\in L^{2}(\mu)
. These means satisfy
0\leq f_{N}
and
g\leq f_{N}
on
A
and hence
0\leq h
and
g\leq h
on
A
. The
L^{2}
-lower semicontinuity and the convexity of
{\mathcal{E}}
imply

{\mathcal{E}}(h)\leq\liminf_{N\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}(f_{N})\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}(h_{n_{k}})=I.

Case 2:
{\mathcal{E}}
is reflexive and subcritical: Using reflexivity, by Mazur’s theorem we find a sequence
(f_{n})
such that
f_{n}\in{\rm conv}\{h_{k}\mid k\geq n\}
and
(f_{n})
is
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}
-Cauchy. These convex combinations satisfy
f_{n}\geq g
on
A
and the convexity of
{\mathcal{E}}
implies
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f_{n})\to I
. Using Theorem
4.16
we choose
w\colon X\to(0,\infty)
such that the embedding of
(M({\mathcal{E}}_{e}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}})
into
L^{1}(w\mu)
is continuous. Hence,
(f_{n})
is Cauchy in
L^{1}(w\mu)
and therefore
f_{n}\to h
in
L^{1}(w\mu)
for some
h\in L^{1}(w\mu)
. The inequalities
h\geq g
on
A
and
h\geq 0
hold and the lower semicontinuity of
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
implies

{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(h)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f_{n})=I.

(b): This follows directly from (a).
∎

Corollary 5.4

(Existence of equilibrium potentials)
.

Assume the standing assumptions of this section and that either
(M({\mathcal{E}}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})
is a Banach space or that
{\mathcal{E}}
is reflexive and subcritical.

(a)

For each open
O\subseteq X
with
{\rm cap}_{h}(O)<\infty
there exists an
{\mathcal{E}}
-excessive function
e_{O}\in D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
with
0\leq e_{O}\leq h
and
e_{O}=h
a.s. on
O
such that

{\rm cap}_{h}(O)={\mathcal{E}}_{e}(e_{O}).

If
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
is strictly convex, then
e_{O}
is unique, and if
(M({\mathcal{E}}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})
is a Banach space, then
e_{O}\in D({\mathcal{E}})
.

(b)

{\rm cap}_{h}
is a Choquet capacity.

Remark 5.5
.

The functions
e_{O}
constructed in this corollary are called
equilibrium potential
for
O
. Note that the uniqueness of
e_{O}
is not guaranteed without strict convexity.

Proof.

(a): Using Theorem
5.3
we find an
{\mathcal{E}}
-excessive function
\tilde{e}_{O}\in D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
with
\tilde{e}_{O}\geq h
on
O
and
\tilde{e}_{O}\geq 0
such that

{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(\tilde{e}_{O})=\min\{{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)\mid f\in L^{0}(\mu)\text{ with }f\geq h\text{ on }O\}.

Let
e_{O}=\tilde{e}_{O}\wedge h
such that
e_{O}=h
on
O
. Since
h
is
{\mathcal{E}}
-excessive, we infer
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(e_{O})\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(\tilde{e}_{O})
and by our characterization of excessive functions in Proposition
3.10

e_{O}
is also
{\mathcal{E}}
-excessive. If
(M({\mathcal{E}}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})
is a Banach space, then
\tilde{e}_{O}\in D({\mathcal{E}})
follows from Theorem
5.3
. Moreover,
{\mathcal{E}}(e_{O})\leq{\mathcal{E}}(\tilde{e}_{O})
(and hence
e_{O}\in D({\mathcal{E}})
) follows from Proposition
3.10
.

(b): It follows directly from the definition of
\mathcal{L}_{A}
that
{\rm cap}_{h}(A)=\inf\{{\rm cap}_{h}(O)\mid O\text{ open with }A\subseteq O\}
. Hence, according to
[
21
, Theorem A.1.2]
, it suffices to show the following for open subsets
O_{n}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, of
X
:

(1)

The inclusion
O_{1}\subseteq O_{2}
implies
{\rm cap}_{h}(O_{1})\leq{\rm cap}_{h}(O_{2}
). This follows directly from the definition.

(2)

{\rm cap}_{h}(O_{1}\cap O_{2})+{\rm cap}_{h}(O_{1}\cup O_{2})\leq{\rm cap}_{h}(O_{1})+{\rm cap}_{h}(O_{2})
. As mentioned above, this is a direct consequence of the first Beurling-Deny criterion.

(3)

If
(O_{n})
is increasing and
O=\cup_{n}O_{n}
, then
\sup_{n}{\rm cap}_{h}(O_{n})={\rm cap}_{h}(O)
. This is a consequence of the existence of equilibrium potentials: Let
(e_{n})
in
D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
with
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(e_{n})={\rm cap}_{h}(O_{n})
. We let
f_{n}=\inf_{k\geq n}e_{k}
. The
L^{0}
-lower semicontinuity of
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
(note that
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}={\mathcal{E}}
if
(M({\mathcal{E}}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})
is a Banach space) and the
{\mathcal{E}}
-excessivity of the
(e_{n})
yield

{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f_{n})\leq\liminf_{N\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(\inf_{n\leq k\leq N}e_{k})\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(e_{n})={\rm cap}_{h}(O_{n}).

A similar computation shows that
f_{n}
is
{\mathcal{E}}
-excessive. Since
(O_{n})
is increasing, we also have
f_{n}\geq h
on
O_{n}
a.s. Hence, the sequence of equilibrium potentials for
(O_{n})
can be chosen to be increasing. In particular, the limit
f=\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}
exists a.s. It satisfies
f\geq h
on
O
a.s. From the lower semicontinuity of
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
with respect to local convergence in measure we infer

{\rm cap}_{h}(O)\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f_{n})\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\rm cap}_{h}(O_{n}).

Since
{\rm cap}_{h}(O_{n})\leq{\rm cap}_{h}(O)
by (1), we infer
\sup_{n}{\rm cap}_{h}(O_{n})={\rm cap}_{h}(O)
.

∎

With the help of Proposition
3.10
we can characterize equilibrium potentials in terms of directional derivatives of
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
.

Corollary 5.6
.

We use the assumptions of Corollary
5.4
and additionally assume that
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
is strictly convex. For
O\subseteq X
open with

and

the following assertions are equivalent:

, where

is the unique equilibrium potential of

.

(ii)

f\geq 0
,

a.s. on

and
d^{+}{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f,\varphi)\geq 0
for all
\varphi\in M({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
with
\varphi\geq 0
a.s. on

.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii): Let
e_{O}
be the equilibrium potential of

. Then

and

on

.
In particular, we have

Since

Remark 5.7
.

(a)

For general nonlinear Dirichlet forms a capacity
{\rm Cap}
was introduced in
[
15
, Section 3.4]
(see also
[
14
]
) by letting

{\rm Cap}(A)=\inf\{\left\|f\right\|_{L,{\mathcal{E}}_{2}}\mid f\geq 1\text{ a.s.{} on an open neighborhood of }A\},

where
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,{\mathcal{E}}_{2}}
is the Luxemburg norm of the nonlinear Dirichlet form
{\mathcal{E}}_{2}={\mathcal{E}}+\left\|\cdot\right\|^{2}
. Potential theory with respect to this capacity is developed there in quite some detail. In particular, the existence of equilibrium potentials is established. However, it is not shown that
{\rm Cap}
is a Choquet capacity because the Luxemburg norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,{\mathcal{E}}_{2}}
in general does not satisfy the first Beurling-Deny criterion. Hence, the inequality
{\rm Cap}(O_{1}\cap O_{2})+{\rm Cap}(O_{1}\cup O_{2})\leq{\rm Cap}(O_{1})+{\rm Cap}(O_{2})
, which is crucial for proving that
{\rm Cap}
is a Choquet capacity, need not hold.

(b)

Capacities for
p
-homogeneous nonlinear Dirichlet forms induced by quasi-regular strongly local Dirichlet forms were recently studied in
[
8
]
and
[
26
]
. In Subsection
6.2
we discuss in more detail how our results extend the ones obtained there. For the case of variable exponent Sobolev spaces we refer to
[
18
, Chapter 10]
.

Of particular importance are sets of capacity zero. For certain nonlinear order preserving forms we characterize them with the help of nests. For
G\subseteq X
and any subset
{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq L^{0}(\mu)
we let

{\mathcal{F}}_{G}=\{f\in{\mathcal{F}}\mid f=0\text{ on an open neighborhood of }X\setminus G\}.

We call an increasing sequence of closed subsets
(F_{n})
of
X
an
{\mathcal{E}}
-nest
if

\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}M({\mathcal{E}}_{e})_{F_{n}}

is
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
-dense in
M({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
.

A set
A\subseteq X
is called
{\mathcal{E}}
-exceptional
if there exists an
{\mathcal{E}}
-nest
(F_{n})
with
A\subseteq\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}(X\setminus F_{n})
.

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a nonlinear order preserving form for which
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
exists. Assume further that
{\mathcal{E}}
is reflexive and subcritical and that it satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition. Further assume that there exists an
{\mathcal{E}}
-excessive function
h\in D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
with
h>0
a.s. For
A\subseteq X
the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

A
is
{\mathcal{E}}
-exceptional.

(ii)

For all
{\mathcal{E}}
-excessive
\tilde{h}\in D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
with
\tilde{h}>0
a.s. we have
{\rm cap}_{\tilde{h}}(A)=0
.

If
Nh
is
{\mathcal{E}}
-excessive for each
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then these are equivalent to

(iii)

{\rm cap}_{h}(A)=0
.

Remark 5.9
.

(a)

The characterization of
{\mathcal{E}}
-exceptional sets through nests is quite important for potential theory of Dirichlet forms. In the bilinear case it was first observed in
[
2
,
1
]
and has since seen various extensions to more and more general situations, see e.g.
[
28
,
27
]
. To the best of our knowledge our theorem is the first version in the nonlinear case.

(b)

In view of the examples given in the next section we do not see reflexivity and the
\Delta_{2}
-condition as strong assumptions. However, the existence of
h\in D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
with
Nh
being
{\mathcal{E}}
-excessive for all
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
is somewhat restrictive. It is satisfied if
{\mathcal{E}}
is
p
-homogeneous for some
p\geq 1
(use Theorem
5.3
and Remark
3.13
) or if
{\mathcal{E}}
is a nonlinear Dirichlet form with
1\in D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
.

We prove this theorem through two lemmas.

Lemma 5.10
.

Assume the standing assumptions of this section.
For all
A\subseteq X
we have

{\rm cap}_{h}(A)=\inf\{{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(h-g)\mid g\in L^{0}(\mu)_{X\setminus A}\}.

Proof.

Let
I=\inf\{{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(h-g)\mid g\in L^{0}(\mu)_{X\setminus A}\}
and note that
g\in L^{0}(\mu)_{X\setminus A}
if and only if
g=0
on an open neighborhood of
A
.

If
g=0
on some open neighborhood of
A
, then
h-g\geq h
on some open neighborhood of
A
. This implies
h-g\in\mathcal{L}_{A}
so that
{\rm cap}_{h}(A)\leq I
.

For the opposite inequality let
\varepsilon>0
and choose
f\in\mathcal{L}_{A}
with
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)\leq{\rm cap}_{h}(A)+\varepsilon
. Then
h-f\wedge h=(h-f)_{+}=0
on an open neighborhood of
A
. Since
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f\wedge h)\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)
, we infer

\displaystyle\varepsilon+{\rm cap}_{h}(A)

\displaystyle\geq{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f\wedge h)={\mathcal{E}}(h-(h-f\wedge h))\geq I.\hfill\qed

Remark 5.11
.

The proof of this lemma is basically the same as the proof of
[
34
, Lemma 2.72]
. There the result is stated for quadratic forms under the additional assumption

, which is equivalent to

. But this finiteness is not necessary in the proof.

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
and
h
be as in Theorem
5.8
. Let
(F_{n})
be an increasing sequence of closed subsets of
X
. The following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

(F_{n})
is an
{\mathcal{E}}
-nest.

(ii)

For all
{\mathcal{E}}
-excessive
\tilde{h}\in D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
with
\tilde{h}>0
we have
\inf_{n\geq 1}{\rm cap}_{\tilde{h}}(X\setminus F_{n})=0
.

If
Nh
is
{\mathcal{E}}
-excessive for each
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then these are equivalent to

(iii)

\inf_{n\geq 1}{\rm cap}_{h}(X\setminus F_{n})=0.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii): Since
(F_{n})
is an
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
-nest and
\tilde{h}\in D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
, there exist
h_{n}\in M({\mathcal{E}}_{e})_{F_{n}}
with
\inf_{n\geq 1}\lVert\tilde{h}-h_{n}\rVert_{L_{e}}=0
. The (weak)
\Delta_{2}
-condition implies
\inf_{n\geq 1}{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(\tilde{h}-h_{n})=0
, see Lemma
A.8
. From the alternative formula for the capacity of Lemma
5.10
we infer

\inf_{n\geq 1}{\rm cap}_{\tilde{h}}(X\setminus F_{n})\leq\inf_{n\geq 1}{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(\tilde{h}-h_{n})=0.

We show (ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) and (iii)
\Rightarrow
(i) at the same time: The
\Delta_{2}
-condition implies
D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})=M({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
and that
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
-convergence and
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}
-convergence agree. Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
be the lower semicontinuous relaxation of the restriction of
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
to the set
\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})_{F_{n}}
on

- it also satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition.

Claim:
D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})\subseteq D(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{e})
implies that
\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})_{F_{n}}
is
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}
-dense in
D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
.

Proof of the claim: Let
f\in D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
. Since
f\in D(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{e})
, there exist
(f_{n})
in
\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})_{F_{n}}
such that
f_{n}\overset{\mu}{\to}f
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f_{n})
. In particular, this implies that
(f_{n})
is
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
-bounded and the
\Delta_{2}
-condition implies that
(f_{n})
is even
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}
-bounded. With the help of Lemma
A.15
we infer
f_{n}\to f
weakly in
(M({\mathcal{E}}_{e}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}})
. Since in semi-normed spaces weak and strong closures of subspaces coincide,

belongs to the closure of
\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})_{F_{n}}
, which proves the claim.

In order to show the inclusion

we need some preparations.

Since by the
\Delta_{2}
-condition

is a vector space, we have

for
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Assuming (ii), we use Theorem
5.3
to choose an
{\mathcal{E}}
-excessive function
h_{N}\in D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
such that
h_{N}\geq Nh
, and assuming (iii), we simply let
h_{N}=Nh
. Assuming (ii) we have
\inf_{n\geq 1}{\rm cap}_{h_{N}}(X\setminus F_{n})=0
. In case (iii) holds, we deduce
\inf_{n\geq 1}{\rm cap}_{h_{N}}(X\setminus F_{n})=0
from the
\Delta_{2}
-condition, which implies the existence of
C>0
such that

{\rm cap}_{h_{N}}(X\setminus F_{n})=\inf\{{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(Nf)\mid f\geq h\text{ on }X\setminus F_{n}\}\leq C{\rm cap}_{h}(X\setminus F_{n}).

With

at hand, Lemma
5.10
yields the existence of
(h_{n,N})
with
h_{n,N}\in D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})_{F_{n}}

with

be given. Then

and we have

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{e}={\mathcal{E}}_{e}

yield

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f\wedge h_{N})

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f\wedge h_{n,N})=\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f\wedge h_{n,N})

\displaystyle\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)+\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(h_{n,N}),

where we use the first Beurling-Deny criterion for the last inequality. Since
(h_{n,N})
is
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}
-bounded, the
\Delta_{2}
-condition implies that it is
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
-bounded, and we arrive at

. The inequality
h_{N}\geq Nh
and
h>0
a.s. yield
f\wedge h_{N}\overset{\mu}{\to}f
, as
N\to\infty
. Hence, using the fact that
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{e}={\mathcal{E}}_{e}
on

and the

-excessivity of

, we obtain

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f)\leq\liminf_{N\to\infty}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f\wedge h_{N})=\liminf_{N\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f\wedge h_{N})\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{e}(f),

showing
f\in D(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{e})
. Now let
f\in D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
arbitrary. Since
{\mathcal{E}}_{e}
satisfies the first Beurling-Deny criterion, we have
f_{+},f_{-}\in D({\mathcal{E}}_{e})
. What we already proved yields
f_{+},f_{-}\in D(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{e})
. Since the latter set is a vector space (use that

satisfies the

-condition), we infer

.

6.1.
Variable exponent Dirichlet forms on Riemannian manifolds

Let
(M,g,\mu)
be a weighted Riemannian manifold (i.e.
\mu=e^{-\Phi}{\rm vol}
, where
\Phi\colon M\to{\mathbb{R}}
is smooth and
{\rm vol}
is the Riemannian volume measure). By

we denote the path metric on

induced by
g
and by
{\rm Lip}_{c}(M)
we denote the Lipschitz functions of compact support with respect to

. In this text we will use some properties of variable exponent Lebesgue- and Sobolev spaces. We refer to
[
18
]
for background on them.

Let
p\colon M\to[1,\infty)
be measurable with
p_{-}=\essinf p
and
p_{+}=\esssup p
. We define the functional
{\mathcal{E}}_{p}\colon L^{2}(\mu)\to[0,\infty]
through

{\mathcal{E}}_{p}(f)=\begin{cases}\int_{M}\frac{1}{p}|\nabla f|^{p}d\mu&\text{if }f\in W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}(M)\\
\infty&\text{else}\end{cases}.

Moreover, we let
{\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{0}
be the largest
L^{2}(\mu)
-lower semicontinuous minorant of the restriction of
{\mathcal{E}}_{p}
to
D({\mathcal{E}}_{p})_{c}
, where the latter denotes the set of all
f\in D({\mathcal{E}}_{p})
vanishing outside a compact set.
Here, restriction is meant in the sense that
{\mathcal{E}}_{p}
is set to
\infty
outside of
D({\mathcal{E}}_{p})_{c}
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Assume
1<p_{-}\leq p_{+}<\infty
.

(a)

{\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{0}
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{p}
are reflexive nonlinear Dirichlet forms satisfying the
\Delta_{2}
-condition.

(b)

D({\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{0})=\overline{D({\mathcal{E}}_{p})_{c}}
, where the closure is taken in
D({\mathcal{E}}_{p})=M({\mathcal{E}}_{p})
with respect to the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,{\mathcal{E}}_{p}}+\left\|\cdot\right\|_{2}
.

(c)

({\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{0})_{e}
is a restriction of
({\mathcal{E}}_{p})_{e}
and
D(({\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{0})_{e})
is the closure of
D({\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{0})
in
D(({\mathcal{E}}_{p})_{e})=M(({\mathcal{E}}_{p})_{e})
with respect to
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e},{\mathcal{E}}_{p}}+q
(here
q
is an
F
-norm inducing the topology of local convergence in measure).

(d)

For all
f\in D(({\mathcal{E}}_{p})_{e})
we have
f\in W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}(M)
and

({\mathcal{E}}_{p})_{e}(f)=\int_{M}\frac{1}{p}|\nabla f|^{p}d\mu.

Proof.

(a): Lower semicontinuity: For
{\mathcal{E}}_{p}
this can be established as in
[
15
, Example 3.58]
.
{\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{0}
is lower semicontinuous by definition.

{\mathcal{E}}_{p}
is compatible with normal contractions: This can also be inferred as in
[
15
]
. We give a simplified proof based on our characterization of nonlinear Dirichlet forms. Since
{\mathcal{E}}_{p}
is lower semicontinuous, it suffices to show compatibility with continuously differentiable normal contractions. For such a normal contraction
C
and
f\in W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}(M)
we have
\nabla(Cf)=C^{\prime}(f)\nabla f
with
|C^{\prime}(f)|\leq 1
. Lemma
B.1
yields

\displaystyle|\nabla(f+Cg)|^{p}+|\nabla(f-Cg)|^{p}

\displaystyle=|\nabla f+C^{\prime}(g)\nabla g|^{p}+|\nabla f-C^{\prime}(g)\nabla g|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq|\nabla f+\nabla g|^{p}+|\nabla f-\nabla g|^{p}.

Integrating this inequality shows that
C
operates on
{\mathcal{E}}_{p}
.

{\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{0}
is compatible with normal contractions: This can be proven using lower semicontinuity and the contraction property for
{\mathcal{E}}_{p}
on
D({\mathcal{E}}_{p})_{c}
. The arguments are the same as in the proof of Proposition
3.9
(c).

\Delta_{2}
-condition: This is a straightforward consequence of
p_{+}<\infty
.
Concerning
{\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{0}
, see Lemma
A.20
(b).

Reflexivity: The variable exponent Lebesgue spaces
(L^{p(\cdot)}(\mu),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{p(\cdot)})
are uniformly convex under the assumption
1<p_{-}\leq p_{+}<\infty
, see
[
18
, Theorem 3.4.9]
. Using
p_{+}<\infty
, the seminorm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,{\mathcal{E}}_{p}}
is equivalent to the seminorm

f\mapsto\left\||\nabla f|\right\|_{p(\cdot)}

(in the definition of the variable exponent spaces one uses the semimodular
f\mapsto\int_{M}|f|^{p}d\mu
and does not divide by
p
in the integral).

This shows that the Luxemburg seminorm of
{\mathcal{E}}_{p}
is equivalent to a uniformly convex seminorm, which in turn yields reflexivity. Since the Luxemburg seminorm of
{\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{0}
is a restriction of the Luxemburg seminorm of
{\mathcal{E}}_{p}
to a smaller domain,
{\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{0}
is also reflexive.

(b):
D({\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{0})=\overline{D({\mathcal{E}}_{p})_{c}}
: The definitions and the
\Delta_{2}
-condition yield that the effective domain of
{\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{0}
, the largest lower semicontinuous minorant of the restriction of
{\mathcal{E}}_{p}
to
D({\mathcal{E}}_{p})_{c}
, must contain the
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,{\mathcal{E}}_{p}}+\left\|\cdot\right\|_{2}
-closure of
D({\mathcal{E}}_{p})_{c}
in
D({\mathcal{E}}_{p})
. Conversely, using reflexivity, it follows from Theorem
A.13
(iv) that the restriction of
{\mathcal{E}}_{p}
to this closure, which has this closure as its effective domain, is lower semicontinuous. In particular, it is a minorant of
{\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{0}
.

(c): Our description of the extended Dirichlet space with the help of approximating sequences in Proposition
3.8
implies that
({\mathcal{E}}_{p})_{e}
is an extension of
({\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{0})_{e}
and that
D(({\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{0})_{e})
is the closure of
D({\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{0})
in
D(({\mathcal{E}}_{p}))_{e}
with respect to
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e},{\mathcal{E}}_{p}}+q
.

(d): Let
f\in D(({\mathcal{E}}_{p})_{e})
and let
(f_{n})
in
D({\mathcal{E}}_{p})
be an approximating sequence for
f
(i.e.
(f_{n})
is
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,{\mathcal{E}}_{p}}
-Cauchy and
f_{n}\overset{\mu}{\to}f
). According to Proposition
3.8
we have

({\mathcal{E}}_{p})_{e}(f)=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}_{p}(f_{n})

and we only have to show that this limit equals the desired integral.

Since
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,{\mathcal{E}}_{p}}
is equivalent to the seminorm
f\mapsto\left\||\nabla f|\right\|_{p(\cdot)}
, the properties of
(f_{n})
yield that
(\nabla f_{n})
is Cauchy in
\vec{L}^{p(\cdot)}(M,\mu)
and hence converges to some limit
X\in\vec{L}^{p(\cdot)}(M,\mu)
. Using a local Poincaré inequality for variable exponent spaces, e.g. a slightly modified version of
[
18
, Lemma 8.2.14]
, we obtain (with usual localization techniques) for each compact
K\subseteq M
a constant
C_{K}>0
such that

\int_{K}|g-\langle g\rangle_{K}|d\mu\leq C_{K}\left\||\nabla g|\right\|_{p(\cdot)}

with
\langle g\rangle_{K}=\mu(K)^{-1}\int_{K}gd\mu
, as long as
\nabla g\in L^{p(\cdot)}(M,\mu)
. Hence,
(f_{n}-\langle f_{n}\rangle_{K})
is Cauchy in
L^{1}(K,\mu)
and converges to
\tilde{f}_{K}\in L^{1}(K,\mu)
. Since also
f_{n}\overset{\mu}{\to}f
, we infer that
\langle f_{n}\rangle_{K}
converges to some constant
C\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\tilde{f}_{K}+C=f
on
K
. Since pointwise convergence of constant functions implies convergence in
L^{1}(K,\mu)
(use
\mu(K)<\infty
), we infer
f_{n}=f_{n}-\langle f_{n}\rangle_{K}+\langle f_{n}\rangle_{K}\to\tilde{f}_{K}+C=f
in
L^{1}(K,\mu)
. Altogether, this implies
f\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(M,\mu)
and
f_{n}\to f
in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(M,\mu)
. We obtain
\nabla f_{n}\to\nabla f
in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M)
(in the usual weak-*-sense) and, using Hölder’s inequality for variable exponent spaces, we also have
\nabla f_{n}\to X
in
\vec{L}^{1}_{\rm loc}(M,\mu)
. Combining both results yields
\nabla f=X\in L^{p(\cdot)}(M,\mu)
and

\displaystyle\int_{M}\frac{1}{p}|\nabla f|^{p}d\mu

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{M}\frac{1}{p}|\nabla f_{n}|^{p}d\mu=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}_{p}(f_{n}).

For the first equality we used that by the
\Delta_{2}
-condition
\left\||\nabla f_{n}-\nabla f|\right\|_{p(\cdot)}\to 0
implies convergence of the integrals (see Lemma
A.10
).
∎

We provide some basic criteria for criticality, which extend well-known results from the bilinear case.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
1<p_{-}\leq p_{+}<\infty
.

(a)

If
\mu(M)<\infty
, then
{\mathcal{E}}_{p}
is critical.

(b)

Assume that
M
is complete and for
o\in M
and
r\geq 0
let
B_{r}=\{x\in M\mid d(x,o)\leq r\}
. If

\liminf_{r\to\infty}\mu(B_{r})r^{-p_{-}}<\infty,

then
{\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{0}
is critical.

Proof.

(a): If
\mu(M)<\infty
, then
1\in L^{2}(\mu)
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{p}(1)=0
. Theorem
4.17
yields the criticality of
{\mathcal{E}}_{p}
.

(b): We choose an increasing sequence
R_{k}\to\infty
with

\lim_{k\to\infty}\mu(B_{R_{k}}){R_{k}}^{-p_{-}}=\liminf_{r\to\infty}\mu(B_{r})r^{-p_{-}}<\infty.

We consider the function
f_{k}=(1-d(\cdot,o)/R_{k})_{+}
. It is
(R_{k})^{-1}
-Lipschitz and vanishes on
M\setminus B_{R_{k}}
. This implies
\nabla f_{k}=0
on
M\setminus B_{R_{k}}
and
|\nabla f_{k}|^{p}\leq(R_{k})^{-p_{-}}
, where we use Rademacher’s theorem for the second inequality. Since
M
is complete, closed distance balls are compact and we obtain
f_{k}\in D({\mathcal{E}}_{p})_{c}\subseteq D({\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{0})
. Moreover, these estimates show

{\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{0}(f_{k})=\int_{M}\frac{1}{p}|\nabla f_{k}|^{p}d\mu\leq\frac{1}{p_{-}}\mu(B_{R_{k}})(R_{k})^{-p_{-}}.

We obtain that
(f_{k})
is
{\mathcal{E}}_{p,0}
-bounded and we also have
f_{k}\overset{\mu}{\to}1
. Using lower semicontinuity, we obtain
1\in D(({\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{0})_{e})
. With this at hand
({\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{0})_{e}(1)=0
follows from Proposition
6.1
, where we determined the action of
({\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{0})_{e}
on
D(({\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{0})_{e})
.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

If
p=2
, then the criticality of
{\mathcal{E}}_{2}^{0}
is known as recurrence or parabolicity of the weighted manifold
(M,g,\mu)
. It is well known that this holds if
M
is complete and
\liminf_{r\to\infty}\mu(B_{r})r^{-2}<\infty
, see e.g.
[

]
(indeed in this case much more sophisticated tests are available). Hence, the previous result can be seen as a generalization to the nonlinear case.

Next, we consider the functionals

{\mathcal{E}}_{p,1}\colon L^{2}(\mu)\to[0,\infty],\quad{\mathcal{E}}_{p,1}(f)={\mathcal{E}}_{p}(f)+\int_{X}\frac{1}{p}|f|^{p}d\mu.

and

{\mathcal{E}}^{0}_{p,1}\colon L^{2}(\mu)\to[0,\infty],\quad{\mathcal{E}}^{0}_{p,1}(f)={\mathcal{E}}^{0}_{p}(f)+\int_{X}\frac{1}{p}|f|^{p}d\mu.

The assertions of Proposition
6.1
also hold for
{\mathcal{E}}^{0}_{p,1}
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{p,1}
(with almost the same proofs) if one simply replaces
{\mathcal{E}}_{p}
by
{\mathcal{E}}_{p,1}
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{0}
by
{\mathcal{E}}_{p,1}^{0}
. The only difference is the action of the extended form. In this case, for
f\in D(({\mathcal{E}}_{p,1})_{e})
we have

({\mathcal{E}}_{p,1})_{e}(f)=\int_{M}\frac{1}{p}|\nabla f|^{p}d\mu+\int_{M}\frac{1}{p}|f|^{p}d\mu.

We refrain from spelling out all details again but note the following result concerning the domain of the extended Dirichlet form of
{\mathcal{E}}_{p,1}
.

Proposition 6.4
.

Assume
1<p_{-}\leq p_{+}<\infty
. Then

D(({\mathcal{E}}_{p,1})_{e})=\{f\in L^{p(\cdot)}(M,\mu)\mid\nabla f\in L^{p(\cdot)}(M,\mu)\}=W^{1,p(\cdot)}(M,\mu)

and

({\mathcal{E}}_{p,1})_{e}(f)=\int_{M}\frac{1}{p}|\nabla f|^{p}d\mu+\int_{M}\frac{1}{p}|f|^{p}d\mu.

In particular, the Luxemburg norm of
({\mathcal{E}}_{p,1})_{e}
is equivalent to the
W^{1,p(\cdot)}(M,\mu)
-norm.

Moreover,
({\mathcal{E}}^{0}_{p,1})_{e}
is the restriction of
({\mathcal{E}}_{p,1})_{e}
to the closure of
D({\mathcal{E}}_{p})_{c}
with respect to the
W^{1,p(\cdot)}(M,\mu)
-norm.

Proof.

It suffices to show
D(({\mathcal{E}}_{p,1})_{e})\supseteq W^{1,p(\cdot)}(M)
, the rest was sketched above. Let
f\in W^{1,p(\cdot)}(M,\mu)
. For
r>0
let
C_{r}(x)=(x\wedge r)\vee(-r)
and consider the functions
f_{R,\varepsilon}=C_{R}(f-C_{\varepsilon}(f))
,
0<\varepsilon<R
. Then
f_{R,\varepsilon}\in L^{1}(M,\mu)\cap L^{\infty}(M,\mu)\subseteq L^{2}(M,\mu)
.

For
g,h\in W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}(M)
we have
f\wedge g,f\vee g\in W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}(M)
and

\nabla(g\wedge h)=1_{\{g<h\}}\nabla g+1_{\{h\leq g\}}\nabla h\text{ and }\nabla(g\vee h)=1_{\{g>h\}}\nabla g+1_{\{h\geq g\}}\nabla h.

This implies
f_{R,\varepsilon}\in W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}(M)
and
|\nabla f_{R,\varepsilon}|\leq|\nabla f|
as well as
|f_{R,\varepsilon}|\leq|f|
. Since
f_{R,\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(M,\mu)
and
f\in W^{1,p(\cdot)}(M,\mu)
, this implies
f_{R,\varepsilon}\in D({\mathcal{E}}_{p,1})
. The convergence
f_{R,\varepsilon}\overset{\mu}{\to}f
, as
\varepsilon\to 0+,R\to\infty
, and the lower semicontinuity of
({\mathcal{E}}_{p,1})_{e}
yield

\displaystyle({\mathcal{E}}_{p,1})_{e}(f)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{R\to\infty,\varepsilon\to 0+}({\mathcal{E}}_{p,1})_{e}(f_{R,\varepsilon}).

Since
({\mathcal{E}}_{p,1})_{e}={\mathcal{E}}_{p,1}
on
D({\mathcal{E}}_{p,1})
and
f_{R,\varepsilon}\in D({\mathcal{E}}_{p,1})
, we infer

\displaystyle({\mathcal{E}}_{p,1})_{e}(f_{R,\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\frac{1}{p}|\nabla f_{R,\varepsilon}|^{p}d\mu+\int_{M}\frac{1}{p}|f_{R,\varepsilon}|^{p}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}\frac{1}{p}|\nabla f|^{p}d\mu+\int_{M}\frac{1}{p}|f|^{p}d\mu<\infty.

Put together, this shows
f\in D(({\mathcal{E}}_{p,1})_{e})
.
∎

6.2.
p
-energies induced by strongly local regular Dirichlet forms

In this subsection we use (almost) the same setting as in
[
8
]
. We refer to it (in particular to its appendix) for precise definitions and proofs (or references to proofs) for the claimed properties. Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a strongly local regular bilinear Dirichlet form on
L^{2}(\mu)
admitting a (positive definite symmetric bilinear) carré du champ operator
\Gamma\colon D({\mathcal{E}})\times D({\mathcal{E}})\to L^{1}(\mu)
such that

{\mathcal{E}}(f)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{X}\Gamma(f)d\mu,

where we use the notation
\Gamma(f)=\Gamma(f,f)
.
Important for us is that if
C\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a Lipschitz function, then for
f\in D({\mathcal{E}})
we have
C(f)\in D({\mathcal{E}})
and

\Gamma(C(f),g)=C^{\prime}(f)\Gamma(f,g)\text{ for all }g\in D({\mathcal{E}}).

Moreover, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality
|\Gamma(f,g)|\leq\Gamma(f)^{1/2}\Gamma(g)^{1/2}
holds for
f,g\in D({\mathcal{E}})
.

For a measurable
p\colon X\to[1,\infty)
and
\alpha\geq 0
we define
{\mathcal{E}}^{(0)}_{p,\alpha}\colon L^{2}(\mu)\to[0,\infty]
by

{\mathcal{E}}^{(0)}_{p,\alpha}(f)=\begin{cases}\int_{X}\frac{1}{p}\Gamma(f)^{\frac{p}{2}}d\mu+\alpha\int_{X}\frac{1}{p}|f|^{p}d\mu&\text{if }f\in D({\mathcal{E}})\\
\infty&\text{else}\end{cases}.

Lemma 6.6
.

{\mathcal{E}}^{(0)}_{p,\alpha}
satisfies the second Beurling-Deny criterion. If
1<p_{-}\leq p_{+}<\infty
, it is reflexive and satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition.

Proof.

Second Beurling-Deny criterion: Let
C\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a normal contraction. Using the chain rule, the bilinearity and the symmetry of
\Gamma
, for
f,g\in D({\mathcal{E}})
we obtain

\Gamma(f+Cg)^{\frac{p}{2}}=(\Gamma(f)+2C^{\prime}(g)\Gamma(f,g)+C^{\prime}(g)^{2}\Gamma(g))^{\frac{p}{2}}

and

\Gamma(f-Cg)^{\frac{p}{2}}=(\Gamma(f)-2C^{\prime}(g)\Gamma(f,g)+C^{\prime}(g)^{2}\Gamma(g))^{\frac{p}{2}}.

Using these identities,
|C^{\prime}(g)|\leq 1
and
|\Gamma(f,g)|\leq\Gamma(f)^{1/2}\Gamma(g)^{1/2}
, Corollary
B.2
yields

\Gamma(f+Cg)^{\frac{p}{2}}+\Gamma(f-Cg)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq\Gamma(f+g)^{\frac{p}{2}}+\Gamma(f-g)^{\frac{p}{2}}.

Moreover, Lemma
B.1
shows

|f+Cg|^{p}+|f-Cg|^{p}\leq|f+g|^{p}+|f-g|^{p}.

Integrating these inequalities yields the claim.

Reflexivity: As in the proof of Proposition
6.1
we obtain that the modular space
(M({\mathcal{E}}^{(0)}_{p,\alpha}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})
has a bi-Lipschitz embedding into a uniformly convex variable exponent Lebesgue space (more precisely
L^{p(\cdot)}(\mu)
if
\alpha=0
and
L^{p(\cdot)}(\mu)\times L^{p(\cdot)}(\mu)
if
\alpha>0
). This implies the reflexivity of
{\mathcal{E}}^{(0)}_{p,\alpha}
.

\Delta_{2}
-condition: This directly follows from
p_{+}<\infty
.
∎

If
{\mathcal{E}}^{(0)}_{p,\alpha}
possesses a lower semicontinuous extension on
L^{2}(\mu)
, we denote its lower semicontinuous relaxation (the largest lower semicontinuous minorant) by
{\mathcal{E}}_{p,\alpha}
. If
1<p_{-}\leq p_{+}<\infty
(and hence
{\mathcal{E}}^{(0)}_{p,\alpha}
is reflexive and satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition), then this is the case if and only if for any
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,{\mathcal{E}}^{(0)}_{p,\alpha}}
-Cauchy sequence
(f_{n})
in
M({\mathcal{E}}^{(0)}_{p,\alpha})
with
f_{n}\to 0
in
L^{2}(\mu)
we have
\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{L,{\mathcal{E}}^{(0)}_{p,\alpha}}\to 0
, see Theorem
A.23
.

Proposition 6.7
.

If
{\mathcal{E}}_{p,\alpha}
exists, it is a nonlinear Dirichlet form. If

\Delta_{2}

Proposition 6.8
.

If
2\leq p_{-}\leq p_{+}<\infty
and

, then
{\mathcal{E}}^{(0)}_{p,\alpha}
is lower semicontinuous (in other words

Remark 6.9
.

. Note that for

Therefore, our characterization of the equilibrium potential in Corollary
5.6
yields the same one as given in

, Theorem 1.12
(3)
]
.

We also note that the analysis of

rather than

. Restricted to

(c)

If

Appendix A
Generalized semimodulars

A.1.
Definitions and basic properties

In this subsection we discuss basic properties of generalized semimodulars and their induced Luxemburg seminorms on vector spaces. Most of the results are well-known to experts. Since they are somewhat scattered in the literature, we include them for the convenience of the reader.

Let
E
be a
\mathbb{K}
-vectorspace with
\mathbb{K}\in\{{\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}\}
. A function
{\varrho}\colon E\to[0,\infty]
is called
proper
if its
effective domain

D({\varrho})=\{x\in E\mid{\varrho}(x)<\infty\}
is nonempty. It is called
left-continuous
if

\lim_{\lambda\to 1-}{\varrho}(\lambda x)={\varrho}(x)

for all
x\in E
. We say that
{\varrho}
is
absolutely convex
, if for all
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{K}
with
|\lambda|+|\mu|\leq 1
and all
x,y\in E
it satisfies

{\varrho}(\lambda x+\mu y)\leq|\lambda|{\varrho}(x)+|\mu|{\varrho}(y).

It is readily verified that a convex functional
{\varrho}
is absolutely convex if and only if
{\varrho}(0)=0
and
{\varrho}(\lambda x)={\varrho}(x)
for all
x\in E
and all
\lambda\in{\mathbb{K}}
with
|\lambda|=1
.

Definition A.1

(Generalized semimodular)
.

A function
\varrho\colon E\to[0,\infty]
is called
generalized semimodular
(gsm for short) if it is proper, absolutely convex and left-continuous.

Remark A.2
.

In the literature, see e.g.
[
18
]
, a gsm
{\varrho}\colon E\to[0,\infty]
is called
semimodular
if for
x\in E
the equality
{\varrho}(\lambda x)=0
for all
\lambda>0
implies
x=0
. Moreover, it is called
modular
if
{\varrho}(x)=0
implies
x=0
. For the examples that we want to treat it is quite important to not assume these stronger properties.

Given a gsm
{\varrho}
we introduce the corresponding
modular space

M({\varrho})=\{x\in E\mid\lim_{\lambda\to 0+}{\varrho}(\lambda x)=0\}.

By absolute convexity of
{\varrho}
it coincides with the linear hull of
D({\varrho})
. For
r>0
the set

B_{r}^{\varrho}=\{x\in E\mid{\varrho}(x)\leq r\}

is absolutely convex and absorbing in the vector space
M({\varrho})
. The Minkowski functional induced by
B_{r}^{\varrho}
on
M({\varrho})
is denoted by
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,r}
. By standard theory it is a seminorm on
M({\varrho})
and is given by

\left\|x\right\|_{L,r}=\inf\{\lambda>0\mid\lambda^{-1}x\in B_{r}^{\varrho}\}=\inf\{\lambda>0\mid{\varrho}(\lambda^{-1}x)\leq r\}.

We extend it to
E\setminus M({\varrho})
by letting
\left\|x\right\|_{L,r}=\infty
for
x\in E\setminus M({\varrho})
.

The family
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,r}
,
r>0
, is called family of
Luxemburg seminorms
of
{\varrho}
. Sometimes we simply call
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}=\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,1}
the
Luxemburg seminorm
of
{\varrho}
.

Lemma A.3

(Basic properties of the Luxemburg seminorms)
.

Let
{\varrho}
be a gsm on
E
. For
x\in E
and
r,s>0
the following hold.

(a)

{\varrho}(x)\leq r
if and only if
\left\|x\right\|_{L,r}\leq 1
.

(b)

{\varrho}(x)\geq r
implies
\left\|x\right\|_{L,r}\leq{\varrho}(x)/r
.

(c)

{\varrho}(x)=\inf\{r>0\mid\left\|x\right\|_{L,r}\leq 1\}
, with the convention
\inf\emptyset=\infty
.

(d)

The seminorms
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,r}
and
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,s}
are equivalent. More precisely, if
s\leq r
, then

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,r}\leq\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,s}\leq\frac{r}{s}\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,r}.

(e)

\left\|x\right\|_{L}=0
if and only if
{\varrho}(\lambda x)=0
for all
\lambda>0
.

Proof.

(a): This can be proven as in
[
18
, Lemma 2.1.14]
(which only treats semimodulars).

(b): If
{\varrho}(x)\geq r
, we obtain using absolute convexity

{\varrho}(\frac{r}{{\varrho}(x)}x)\leq\frac{r}{{\varrho}(x)}{\varrho}(x)=r.

This shows
\left\|x\right\|_{L,r}\leq{\varrho}(x)/r
.

(c): Clearly,
x\in M({\varrho})
if and only if there exists
r>0
such that
\left\|x\right\|_{L,r}\leq 1
. With this observation the result follows directly from (a).

(d): Without loss of generality we assume
s\leq r
. Clearly, this implies
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,r}\leq\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,s}
. For the converse inequality we let
\lambda=s/r\leq 1
. If
{\varrho}(x)\leq r
, then by convexity we have

{\varrho}(\lambda x)\leq\lambda{\varrho}(x)\leq\lambda r=s.

This shows
\left\|x\right\|_{L,s}\leq 1/\lambda=r/s
as long as
{\varrho}(x)\leq r
. In other words,

\displaystyle\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,s}

\displaystyle\leq r/s\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,r}.

(e): This follows directly from the definition of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
and the absolute convexity of
{\varrho}
.
∎

Corollary A.4
.

Every gsm
{\varrho}
is lower semicontinuous on
M({\varrho})
with respect to
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
-convergence.

Proof.

The functional
{\varrho}
is lower semicontinuous on the space
(M({\varrho}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})
if and only if for each
r>0
the set

\{x\in M({\varrho})\mid{\varrho}(x)\leq r\}=\{x\in M({\varrho})\mid\left\|x\right\|_{L,r}\leq 1\}

is
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
-closed, where we used the previous lemma for the equality. The set on the right side is closed because all Luxemburg seminorms are equivalent.
∎

Remark A.5
.

This is an extension of
[
18
, Theorem 2.1.17]
with a simplified proof.

Definition A.6

(
\Delta_{2}
-conditions)
.

A gsm
{\varrho}
on
E
is said to satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition
if there exists
K>0
such that

{\varrho}(2x)\leq K{\varrho}(x)

for all
x\in E
. The functional
{\varrho}
is said to satisfy the
weak
\Delta_{2}
-condition
if for all sequences
{\varrho}(x_{n})\to 0
implies
{\varrho}(2x_{n})\to 0
. Moreover,
{\varrho}
is said to satisfy the
very weak
\Delta_{2}
-condition
if
{\varrho}(x)=0
implies
{\varrho}(2x)=0
.

Remark A.7
.

Clearly, the
\Delta_{2}
-condition implies the weak
\Delta_{2}
-condition, which in turn implies the very weak
\Delta_{2}
-condition. The converse implications do not hold in general.

Lemma A.8

(Characterizing the
\Delta_{2}
-conditions)
.

Let
{\varrho}
be a gsm on
E
.

(a)

The following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

{\varrho}
satisfies
\Delta_{2}
.

(ii)

For all
C>1
there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

{\varrho}((1+\varepsilon)x)\leq C{\varrho}(x)

for all
x\in E
.

In this case,
D({\varrho})=M({\varrho})
and for
s<r
there exists
\delta>0
such that
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,r}\leq(1-\delta)\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,s}
.

(b)

{\varrho}
satisfies the weak
\Delta_{2}
-condition if and only if for all sequences
(x_{n})
in
E
the following holds:
\left\|x_{n}\right\|_{L}\to 0
if and only if
{\varrho}(x_{n})\to 0
.

(c)

{\varrho}
satisfies the very weak
\Delta_{2}
-condition if and only if for all
x\in E
the following holds:
\left\|x\right\|_{L}=0
if and only if
{\varrho}(x)=0
.

Proof.

(a): (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii): Let
C>1
and let
K>0
such that
{\varrho}(2x)\leq K{\varrho}(x)
for all
x\in E
. Without loss of generality we can assume
K>C
. Let
\varepsilon=(C-1)/(K-1)<1
. Using the convexity of
{\varrho}
we infer

\displaystyle{\varrho}((1+\varepsilon)x)

\displaystyle={\varrho}((1-\varepsilon)x+2\varepsilon x)

\displaystyle\leq(1-\varepsilon){\varrho}(x)+\varepsilon{\varrho}(2x)

\displaystyle\leq(1-\varepsilon){\varrho}(x)+K\varepsilon{\varrho}(x)

\displaystyle=C{\varrho}(x).

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i): Let
C>0
and choose an appropriate
\varepsilon>0
such that
{\varrho}((1+\varepsilon)x)\leq C{\varrho}(x)
for all
x\in E
. Choose
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
2\leq(1+\varepsilon)^{n}
. The absolute convexity of
{\varrho}
implies

{\varrho}(2x)\leq{\varrho}((1+\varepsilon)^{n}x)\leq C^{n}{\varrho}(x).

Now assume that
\Delta_{2}
holds. The convexity of
{\varrho}
and
\Delta_{2}
imply the linearity of
D({\varrho})
. Since
M({\varrho})
is the linear hull of
D({\varrho})
, we obtain
D({\varrho})=M({\varrho})
. Let
s<r
. Using (ii) for
C=r/s>1
we find
\varepsilon>0
such that
{\varrho}((1+\varepsilon)x)\leq C{\varrho}(x)
for
x\in E
. For
x\in E
with
{\varrho}(x)\leq s
, this implies

{\varrho}((1+\varepsilon)x)\leq Cs=r,

or in other words
\left\|x\right\|_{L,r}\leq 1/(1+\varepsilon)
. We infer
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,r}\leq(1-\delta)\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,s}
with
\delta=\varepsilon/(1+\varepsilon)
.

(b): This can be proven as in
[
18
, Lemma 2.1.11]
(which only treats semimodulars).

(c): It is straightforward from the definition of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
and the absolute convexity of
{\varrho}
that
\left\|x\right\|_{L}=0
if and only if
{\varrho}(2^{n}x)\leq 1
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. By absolute convexity we also have

{\varrho}(x)\leq 2^{-n}{\varrho}(2^{n}x)

for all
x\in E
. Together, these observations imply
\left\|x\right\|_{L}=0
if and only if
{\varrho}(2^{n}x)=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This description of the kernel of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
implies the claimed equivalence.
∎

Remark A.9
.

The proof of (a) is taken from the proof of
[
10
, Lemma 2]
.

Lemma A.10
.

Let
{\varrho}
be a gsm on
E
satisfying the
\Delta_{2}
-condition. Then
\left\|x_{n}-x\right\|_{L}\to 0
implies
{\varrho}(x_{n})\to{\varrho}(x)
.

Proof.

Corollary
A.4
shows

\varrho(x)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\varrho(x_{n}).

To show the converse inequality, let
r>s>\varrho(x)
.
Using the
\Delta_{2}
-condition and Lemma
A.8
, we obtain a
\delta\in(0,1)
with
\|\cdot\|_{L,r}\leq(1-\delta)\|\cdot\|_{L,s}
.
Because the Luxemburg seminorms are equivalent,
\|x_{n}\|_{L,s}\to\|x\|_{L,s}
and there exists
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\|x_{n}\|_{L,r}\leq(1-\delta)\|x_{n}\|_{L,s}\leq\|x\|_{L,s}

for all
n\geq N
.
Since
\varrho(x)<s
, we have
\|x\|_{L,s}\leq 1
so that
\varrho(x_{n})\leq r
for all
n\geq N
.
Because this
r>\varrho(x)
was arbitrary, we obtain

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\varrho(x_{n})\leq\varrho(x).\qed

A.2.
Lower semicontinuity

Recall that an
F
-norm
on
E
is a functional
p\colon E\to[0,\infty)
satisfying the following properties:
p(x)=0
if and only if
x=0
(non-degeneracy),
p(x+y)\leq p(x)+p(y)
for all
x,y\in E
(triangle inequality),
p(|\lambda|x)\leq p(x)
for all
|\lambda|\leq 1
,
x\in E
(balls around
0
are circled) and
p(n^{-1}x)\to 0
for all
x\in E
(continuity of scalar multiplication at
0
).

We now assume that
E
is endowed with a vector space topology induced by an
F
-norm
p
. More precisely, we consider the vector space topology induced by the metric
d\colon E\times E\to[0,\infty)
,
d(x,y)=p(x-y)
. Indeed, any metrizable vector space topology is induced by an
F
-norm and completeness of
(E,p)
does not depend on the particular choice of
p
. In this subsection (semi)continuity is always considered with respect to the topology induced by
p
via
d
.

For a gsm
{\varrho}
the seminormed space
(M({\varrho}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})
isometrically embeds into its bidual
(M({\varrho}))^{\prime\prime}
via the map

\iota\colon M({\varrho})\to(M({\varrho}))^{\prime\prime},\quad x\mapsto\langle\cdot,x\rangle,

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle´
denotes the dual pairing between
M({\varrho})
and
(M({\varrho}))^{\prime}
.

Definition A.11

(Reflexive gsm)
.

We call
{\varrho}

reflexive
if
\iota(M({\varrho}))
is norm dense in
(M({\varrho}))^{\prime\prime}
.

Remark A.12
.

Reflexivity of
{\varrho}
is equivalent to reflexivity of the completion of the quotient
(M({\varrho})/\ker\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})
. Since all Luxemburg seminorms are equivalent, reflexivity of
{\varrho}
is independent of the choice of the parameter in the Luxemburg seminorm. This is why we attribute reflexivity to
{\varrho}
instead of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
.

The next theorem is one of the main results in this subsection. It characterizes the lower semicontinuity of
{\varrho}
in terms of the lower semicontinuity of the Luxemburg seminorms.

Theorem A.13
.

Let
{\varrho}
be a gsm on
(E,p)
.

(a)

The following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

{\varrho}
is lower semicontinuous.

(ii)

For all
r>0
the functional
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,r}
is lower semicontinuous (as a function
E\to[0,\infty]
).

(b)

If
{\varrho}
is reflexive, the assertions in (a) are equivalent to:

(iii)

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
is lower semicontinuous (as a function
E\to[0,\infty]
).

(c)

If
{\varrho}
is reflexive and
(E,p)
is complete, the assertions in (a) and (b) are equivalent to:

(iv)

(M({\varrho}),p+\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})
is complete.

Before proving this result, we discuss two technical lemmas.

Lemma A.14
.

Let
(x_{n})
,
x
in
(E,p)
with

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p(x-x_{n})<\infty.

Then for every sequence
(y_{n})
in
E
with
y_{n}\in{\rm conv}\,\{x_{k}\mid k\geq n\}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
y_{n}\to x
with respect to
p
.

Proof.

For
y=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}x_{k}\in{\rm conv}\,\{x_{k}\mid k\geq n\}
we obtain using the properties of
F
-norms

p(x-y)\leq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}p(\lambda_{k}(x-x_{k}))\leq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}p(x-x_{k}).

This inequality and the assumption
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p(x-x_{n})<\infty
imply the statement.
∎

Lemma A.15
.

Let
{\varrho}
be a reflexive lower semicontinuous gsm on
(E,p)
. If
(x_{n})
is bounded in
(M({\varrho}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})
and converges to
x\in E
with respect to
p
, then
x_{n}\to x
weakly in
(M({\varrho}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})
.

Proof.

Using a standard subsequence argument, it suffices to show that
(x_{n})
has a subsequence converging weakly to
x
. Hence, without loss of generality, we can assume
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p(x-x_{n})<\infty
. By the weak sequential compactness of balls in the completion of
(M({\varrho}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})
, we obtain a subsequence
(x_{n_{k}})
that converges weakly to some
\overline{x}
in the completion. Mazur’s lemma yields a sequence
(y_{n})
with the following properties:

•

(y_{n})
converges to
\overline{x}
in the completion of
(M({\varrho}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})
.

•

(y_{n})
is a finite convex combination of the elements of
\{x_{n_{k}}\mid k\geq n\}
.

Lemma
A.14
shows
y_{n}\to x
with respect to
p
. With this at hand, the lower semicontinuity of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
implies

\left\|x-\overline{x}\right\|_{L}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|x-y_{n}\right\|_{L}\leq\liminf_{n,m\to\infty}\left\|y_{m}-y_{n}\right\|_{L}=0.

This implies
\varphi(x)=\varphi(\overline{x})
for each
\varphi\in M({\varrho})^{\prime}
and so we obtain the weak convergence of
(x_{n_{k}})
to
x
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
A.13
.

(a): (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii): This follows from two observations: 1. The functional
{\varrho}
is lower semicontinuous with respect to
p

. 2. By a scaling argument the functional
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,r}

\{x\in E\mid\left\|x\right\|_{L,r}\leq 1\}
is closed with respect to
p
.

(b): Now we assume that
{\varrho}
is reflexive. (ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii) is trivial.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii): Let
(x_{n}),x
in

with
x_{n}\to x
with respect to

. We show
\left\|x\right\|_{L,r}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left\|x_{n}\right\|_{L,r}
. Without loss of generality we assume that
(x_{n})
is
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,r}
-bounded (else pass to a subsequence, without bounded subsequence there is nothing to show). Since all Luxemburg seminorms are equivalent,
(x_{n})
is also
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}

yields
x_{n}\to x

\left\|x\right\|_{L,r}\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|x_{n}\right\|_{L,r}.

(c): Now we assume that
{\varrho}
is reflexive and
(E,p)
is complete.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(iv): Let
(x_{n})
be
p+\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
-Cauchy. The completeness of
(E,p)
yields
x\in E
such that
x_{n}\to x
with respect to
p
. The lower semicontinuity of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
implies

\left\|x-x_{n}\right\|_{L}\leq\liminf_{m\to\infty}\left\|x_{m}-x_{n}\right\|_{L}.

Since
(x_{n})
is also
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
-Cauchy, we infer
x_{n}\to x
with respect to
p+\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
.

(iv)
\Rightarrow
(iii): We use a similar argument as in the proof of (iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii) and we refer there for more details. Let
x_{n}\to x
with respect to
p
. Without loss of generality we assume

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left\|x_{n}\right\|_{L}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|x_{n}\right\|_{L}<\infty

and

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p(x-x_{n})<\infty.

Using the
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
-boundedness of
(x_{n})
and the reflexivity of
{\varrho}
we find
(y_{n})
such that
(y_{n})

A.14
yields

with respect to

. Overall,
(y_{n})
is

-Cauchy. By completeness, it
p+\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
-converges to some

. Since

and

\left\|x\right\|_{L}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|y_{n}\right\|_{L}\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|x_{n}\right\|_{L}.

For the last inequality we used convexity of the seminorm.
∎

Remark A.16
.

The equivalence of (i) and (ii) is more or less trivial. The equivalence with (iii) under the reflexivity condition seems to be a new observation. The equivalence with (iv) was first observed in
[

]
for quadratic forms, see also
[

.

Next we discuss when
{\varrho}
can be extended to a lower semicontinuous functional. We say that
{\varrho}^{\prime}\colon E\to[0,\infty]
is an

of
{\varrho}
(or that
{\varrho}
is a

of
{\varrho}^{\prime}
) if
D({\varrho})\subseteq D({\varrho}^{\prime})
and
{\varrho}(x)={\varrho}^{\prime}(x)
for
x\in D({\varrho})
.
Moreover, we say that
{\varrho}
is
lower semicontinuous on
C\subseteq E
if the restriction
{\varrho}|_{C}\colon C\to[0,\infty]
is lower semicontinuous with respect to the metric induced by
p
on
C
.

The
lower semicontinuous relaxation
of
{\varrho}
is defined by

{\rm sc}\,{\varrho}\colon E\to[0,\infty],\quad{\rm sc}\,{\varrho}(x)=\sup\{f(x)\mid f\in C(E,[0,\infty))\text{ with }f\leq{\varrho}\}.

The following proposition provides a standard description of the lower semicontinuous relaxation. We mention it for completeness but omit a proof.

Proposition A.17
.

The lower semicontinuous relaxation
{\rm sc}\,{\varrho}
is the largest lower semicontinuous minorant of
{\varrho}
.
If
{\varrho}
is lower semicontinuous on
D({\varrho})
, then
{\rm sc}\,{\varrho}
is a lower semicontinuous extension of
{\varrho}
. Among all lower semicontinuous extensions of
{\varrho}
it has the smallest effective domain, which is given by

D({\rm sc}\,{\varrho})=\{x\in E\mid\text{ex. $(x_{n})$ s.t. }p(x_{n}-x)\to 0\text{ and }\sup_{n}{\varrho}(x_{n})<\infty\}.

Furthermore, for
x\in D({\rm sc}\,{\varrho})
there exists
(x_{n})
in
D({\varrho})
such that
x_{n}\to x
with respect to
p
and

{\rm sc}\,{\varrho}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\varrho}(x_{n}).

Remark A.18
.

The previous proposition will be applied below to
{\varrho}
and to the Luxemburg seminorms
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,r}\colon E\to[0,\infty]
, which are convex functionals with effective domain
M({\varrho})
.

Definition A.19

(Extended functional and Luxemburg seminorm)
.

Let
{\varrho}
be a gsm on
(E,p)
and assume that
{\varrho}
is lower semicontinuous on
D({\varrho})
. Then we write
{\varrho}_{e}={\rm sc}\,{\varrho}
and call
{\varrho}_{e}
the the
extended functional
of
{\varrho}
. Similarly, if
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,r}
is lower semicontinuous on
M({\varrho})
, we write
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e},r}={\rm sc}\,\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,r}
and call it the
extended Luxemburg seminorm
.

The next lemma shows that
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e},r}
is a Luxemburg seminorm of
{\varrho}_{e}
.

Lemma A.20
.

Assume that
{\varrho}
is lower semicontinuous on
M({\varrho})
.
Then for every
x\in E
we have

\left\|x\right\|_{L_{e},r}=\inf\{\lambda>0\mid{\varrho}_{e}(\lambda^{-1}x)\leq r\},

(with the convention
\inf\emptyset=\infty
) and

M({\varrho}_{e})=\{x\in E\mid\left\|x\right\|_{L_{e},r}<\infty\}.

Moreover,
{\varrho}
satisfies the (weak)
\Delta_{2}
-condition if and only if
{\varrho}_{e}
does.

Proof.

Applying Theorem
A.13
to the gsm
{\varrho}|_{M({\varrho})}
on
(M({\varrho}),p)
yields that
{\varrho}
is lower semicontinuous on
M({\varrho})
if and only if the Luxemburg seminorms
\|\cdot\|_{L,r}
,
r>0
, are lower semicontinuous on
M(\varrho)
.

For
x\in E
let

N(x)=\inf\{\lambda>0\mid{\varrho}_{e}(\lambda^{-1}x)\leq r\}.

Assume
\left\|x\right\|_{L_{e},r}<\infty
and choose a sequence
(x_{n})
in
M({\varrho})
such that
x_{n}\to x
with respect to
p
and
\left\|x\right\|_{L_{e},r}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|x_{n}\right\|_{L,r}
. For
\varepsilon>0
let
\lambda_{n}=\left\|x_{n}\right\|_{L,r}+\varepsilon
and
\lambda=\left\|x\right\|_{L_{e},r}+\varepsilon
. Then
\lambda_{n}^{-1}x_{n}\to\lambda^{-1}x
with respect to
p
and
{\varrho}(\lambda_{n}^{-1}x_{n})\leq r
. The lower semicontinuity of
{\varrho}_{e}
and
{\varrho}_{e}={\varrho}
on
D({\varrho})
yield

{\varrho}_{e}(\lambda^{-1}x)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\varrho}(\lambda_{n}^{-1}x_{n})\leq r,

showing

N(x)\leq\lambda=\left\|x\right\|_{L_{e},r}+\varepsilon.

This implies
N\leq\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e},r}
.

To prove the opposite inequality, assume
N(x)<\infty
and let
\lambda>0
such that
{\varrho}_{e}(\lambda^{-1}x)\leq r
. We choose a sequence
(x_{n})
in
D({\varrho})
with
x_{n}\to x
with respect to
p
and
{\varrho}(\lambda^{-1}x_{n})\to{\varrho}_{e}(\lambda^{-1}x)
. Let
\varepsilon>0
arbitrary. Without loss of generality we can assume
{\varrho}(\lambda^{-1}x_{n})\leq r+\varepsilon
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, which implies

\left\|x_{n}\right\|_{L,r+\varepsilon}\leq\lambda.

The lower semicontinuity of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e},r+\varepsilon}
and the inequality for the Luxemburg seminorms with different parameters yield

\frac{r}{r+\varepsilon}\left\|x\right\|_{L_{e},r}\leq\left\|x\right\|_{L_{e},r+\varepsilon}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left\|x_{n}\right\|_{L,r+\varepsilon}\leq\lambda.

Here, the first inequality follows from the description of the lower semicontinuous relaxation
\|\cdot\|_{L_{e},r+{\varepsilon}}
and the lower semicontinuity of
\|\cdot\|_{L_{e},r}
.
Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, we obtain
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e},r}\leq N
.

With the explicit description of
{\varrho}_{e}={\rm sc}\,{\varrho}
it is readily verified that if
{\varrho}
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition, then
{\varrho}_{e}
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition.

The lower semicontinuity of
\varrho
on
M(\varrho)
implies
\varrho=\varrho_{e}
on
M(\varrho)
.
Therefore, if
{\varrho}_{e}
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition with constant
K
, then we have

\varrho(2x)=\varrho_{e}(2x)\leq K\varrho_{e}(x)=K\varrho(x)

for all
x\in M(\varrho)
.
For
x\in E\setminus M(\varrho)
, there is nothing to show.

The weak
\Delta_{2}
-condition is equivalent to the following: For each
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that
{\varrho}(f)\leq\delta
implies
{\varrho}(2f)\leq\varepsilon
. Let now
\varepsilon>0
arbitrary and choose
\delta>0
as in the last sentence. Let
(f_{n})
such that
{\varrho}_{e}(f_{n})\to 0
. Fix
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
{\varrho}_{e}(f_{n})\leq\delta/2
for
n\geq N
and for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
choose sequences
(g_{m,n})
with
g_{m,{n}}\to f_{n}
with respect to
p
, as
m\to\infty
, and
|{\varrho}(g_{m,n})-{\varrho}_{e}(f_{n})|\to 0
, as
m\to\infty
. For
n\geq N
and
m
large enough we have
|{\varrho}(g_{m,n})-{\varrho}_{e}(f_{n})|\leq\delta/2
, which implies
{\varrho}(g_{m,n})\leq\delta
and leads to
{\varrho}(2g_{m,n})\leq\varepsilon
for
n\geq N
and
m
large enough. Using the lower semicontinuity of
{\varrho}_{e}
, we arrive at

{\varrho}_{e}(2f_{n})\leq\liminf_{m\to\infty}{\varrho}(2g_{m,n})\leq\varepsilon,

for
n\geq N
. This implies
{\varrho}_{e}(2f_{n})\to 0
, as
n\to\infty
.

If
\varrho_{e}
satisfies the weak
\Delta_{2}
-condition and
(x_{n})
is a sequence in
E
with
\varrho(x_{n})\to 0
, then
x_{n}\in M(\varrho)
eventually so that
\varrho_{e}(x_{n})=\varrho(x_{n})\to 0
and, hence,
\varrho(2x_{n})=\varrho_{e}(2x_{n})\to 0
.
∎

Remark A.21
.

The very weak
\Delta_{2}
-condition need not extend to
{\varrho}_{e}
.

The next theorem gives equivalent conditions for the lower semicontinuity of
{\varrho}
on
M({\varrho})
and of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
on
M({\varrho})
. Moreover, it provides explicit formulas for
{\varrho}_{e}
and
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}
. For a better readability of its statements and for later purposes we make one more definition.

Definition A.22

(Approximating sequence)
.

Let
{\varrho}
be a gsm on
(E,p)
and let
x\in E
. A sequence
(x_{n})
in
M({\varrho})
is called
approximating sequence for

x
if
(x_{n})
is
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
-Cauchy and
x_{n}\to x
with respect to
p
.

Theorem A.23
.

Let
{\varrho}
be a gsm on
(E,p)
.

(a)

The following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

{\varrho}
is lower semicontinuous on
M({\varrho})
.

(ii)

For all
r>0
the functional
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,r}
is lower semicontinuous on
M({\varrho})
.

(b)

If
{\varrho}
is reflexive, the assertions in (a) are equivalent to:

(iii)

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
is lower semicontinuous on
M({\varrho})
.

(c)

If
{\varrho}
is reflexive and
(E,p)
is complete, the assertions in (a) and (b) are equivalent to:

(iv)

For all
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
-Cauchy sequences
(x_{n})
in
M({\varrho})
with
p(x_{n})\to 0
we have
\left\|x_{n}\right\|_{L}\to 0
.

In the latter case,
x\in M({\varrho}_{e})
if and only if there exists an approximating sequence for
x
and for every approximating sequence
(x_{n})
for
x
we have

\left\|x\right\|_{L_{e}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|x_{n}\right\|_{L}.

(d)

Assume that
{\varrho}
is reflexive,
(E,p)
is complete and that the equivalent assertions (i) - (iv) hold. If
{\varrho}
satisfies
\Delta_{2}
, then
{\varrho}_{e}
satisfies
\Delta_{2}
,
D({\varrho}_{e})=M({\varrho}_{e})
and for every approximating sequence
(x_{n})
for
x
we have

{\varrho}_{e}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\varrho}(x_{n}).

Proof.

(a) and (b): This follows directly by applying Theorem
A.13
to the gsm
{\varrho}|_{M({\varrho})}
on
(M({\varrho}),p)
and observing that
\varrho|_{M(\varrho)}
is lower semicontinuous if and only if
\varrho
is lower semicontinuous on
M(\varrho)
.

(c): (iii)
\Rightarrow
(iv): Since (iii) implies (i),
{\varrho}_{e}
is a lower semicontinuous extension of
{\varrho}
. By (iii) and Lemma
A.20
the extended Luxemburg seminorm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}
exists and equals the Luxemburg seminorm of
{\varrho}_{e}
. Hence, Theorem
A.13
applied to
{\varrho}_{e}
implies that
M({\varrho}_{e})
is complete with respect to
p+\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}
. Now let
(x_{n})
be
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
-Cauchy with
p(x_{n})\to 0
. Since
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}
is an extension of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
, we infer that
(x_{n})
is
p+\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}
-Cauchy. By completeness it converges to
x\in M({\varrho}_{e})
with respect to
p+\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}
. Since
p(x_{n})\to 0
and
p
-limits are unique, we infer
x=0
, showing
\left\|x_{n}\right\|_{L}=\left\|x_{n}\right\|_{L_{e}}\to 0
.

(iv)
\Rightarrow
(iii): We define
N\colon E\to[0,\infty]
by

N(x)=\begin{cases}\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\left\|x_{n}\right\|_{L}&\text{if $(x_{n})$ is an approximating sequence for $x$}\\
\infty&\text{if $x$ has no approximating sequence}\end{cases}

and show that
N
is the lower semicontinuous extension of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
with minimal effective domain. This implies (iii) and the formula for
M({\varrho}_{e})
and
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e}}
.

N
is well-defined and an extension of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
: This is a direct consequence of (iv).

M({\varrho})
is dense in
D(N)
with respect to
p+N
: For
x\in D(N)
we choose an approximating sequence
(x_{n})
for
x
. Then for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
the element
x-x_{m}
has the approximating sequence
(x_{n}-x_{m})_{n}
. This implies

\lim_{m\to\infty}N(x-x_{m})=\lim_{m\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|x_{n}-x_{m}\right\|_{L}=0.

D(N)
is complete with respect to
p+N
: Let
(x_{n})
be
p+N
-Cauchy. By completeness of
(E,p)
there exists
x\in E
with
x_{n}\to x
with respect to
p
. Moreover, by density of
M({\varrho})
in
D(N)
with respect to
p+N
we can choose
y_{n}\in M({\varrho})
with
p(x_{n}-y_{n})+N(x_{n}-y_{n})\to 0
. Then
p(x-y_{n})\to 0
and since
N
is an extension of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
, the sequence
(y_{n})
is
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
-Cauchy. In other words,
(y_{n})
is an approximating sequence for
x
, which implies
x\in D(N)
and
N(x-x_{n})\to 0
.

N
is lower semicontinuous:
N
is a seminorm on
D(N)
and hence coincides with its own Luxemburg seminorm. With this at hand lower semicontinuity of
N
follows from the completeness of
(D(N),p+N)
and Theorem
A.13
. Note that the required reflexivity of
N
follows from the density of
M({\varrho})
in
D(N)
and the reflexivity of
{\varrho}
.

N
is the largest lower semicontinuous minorant: By definition
x\in D(N)
if and only if it has an approximating sequence
(x_{n})
. Now, if
M
is a lower semicontinuous minorant of
\|\cdot\|_{L}
, we obtain

M(x)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}M(x_{n})\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\|x_{n}\|_{L}=N(x).

(d): We have already observed in Lemma
A.20
that
{\varrho}_{e}
inherits the
\Delta_{2}
property from
{\varrho}
.
Since
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e},r}
is a Luxemburg seminorm of
{\varrho}_{e}
, we can apply Lemma
A.3
and Lemma
A.8
to
{\varrho}_{e}
and
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e},r}
. In particular, we obtain
D({\varrho}_{e})=M({\varrho}_{e})
. From (iv) it then follows that
D({\varrho}_{e})
consists of those elements of
E
that possess an approximating sequence.

Let
(x_{n})
be an approximating sequence for
x
. Then
{\varrho}_{e}(x)<\infty
and from the lower semicontinuity of
{\varrho}_{e}
and
{\varrho}_{e}={\varrho}
on
D({\varrho})
we infer

{\varrho}_{e}(x)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\varrho}(x_{n}).

It remains to prove
{\varrho}_{e}(x)\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}{\varrho}(x_{n})
. Lemma
A.3
shows

{\varrho}_{e}(x)=\inf\{r>0\mid\left\|x\right\|_{L_{e},r}\leq 1\}.

Let
r>s>{\varrho}_{e}(x)
. We use Lemma
A.8
and
\Delta_{2}
to choose
0<\delta<1
with
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e},r}\leq(1-\delta)\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e},s}
. The lower semicontinuity of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e},s}
yields

\left\|x-x_{n}\right\|_{L_{e},s}\leq\liminf_{m\to\infty}\left\|x_{m}-x_{n}\right\|_{L_{e},s}=\liminf_{m\to\infty}\left\|x_{m}-x_{n}\right\|_{L,s},

showing
x_{n}\to x
with respect to
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L_{e},s}
(here we use that
(x_{n})
is also
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L,s}
-Cauchy, which follows from the equivalence of the Luxemburg seminorms of
{\varrho}
). In particular, we find
N\geq 1
such that

\left\|x_{n}\right\|_{L_{e},r}\leq(1-\delta)\left\|x_{n}\right\|_{L_{e},s}\leq\left\|x\right\|_{L_{e},s}

for all
n\geq N
. Since
s>{\varrho}_{e}(x)
, we have
\left\|x\right\|_{L_{e},s}\leq 1
. This implies
\left\|x_{n}\right\|_{L_{e},r}\leq 1
for each
n\geq N
, which in turn yields
{\varrho}_{e}(x_{n})\leq r
for all
n\geq N
, showing

\limsup_{n\to\infty}{\varrho}_{e}(x_{n})\leq r.

Since
r>{\varrho}_{e}(x)
was arbitrary and since
{\varrho}={\varrho}_{e}
on
D({\varrho})
, we obtain the claim.
∎

Lemma A.25
.

Assume that
(E,p)
complete.
Then
{\varrho}
is reflexive if and only if

is reflexive.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem

(c) that

with respect to

x_{n}+N\to x+N
in
({\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{M({\varrho})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\textstyle{M({\varrho})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{2.625pt}{$\scriptstyle{M({\varrho})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{M({\varrho})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{N}$}}},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})
.

(i) & (iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii): The lower semicontinuity of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
, which follows from the lower semicontinuity of
{\varrho}
, implies that
N
is closed. In particular,
({\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{E}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{E}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{E}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{E}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{N}$}}},p_{N})
is complete.

The closed graph theorem holds in complete metrizable topological vector spaces (so-called
F
-spaces), see e.g.
[
24
, Theorem 3.8]
. Since
({\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{M({\varrho})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\textstyle{M({\varrho})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{2.625pt}{$\scriptstyle{M({\varrho})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{M({\varrho})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{N}$}}},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})
is also complete by (iii), it suffices to show that the embedding is closed. Assume
x_{n}+N\to x+N
in
({\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{M({\varrho})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\textstyle{M({\varrho})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{2.625pt}{$\scriptstyle{M({\varrho})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{M({\varrho})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{N}$}}},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})
and
x_{n}+N\to y+N
in
({\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{E}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{E}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{E}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{E}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{N}$}}},p_{N})
. We choose a sequence
(k_{n})
in
N
with
p(x_{n}+k_{n}-y)\to 0
. The lower semicontinuity of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
yields

\left\|x-y\right\|_{L}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left\|x-x_{n}-k_{n}\right\|_{L}=\left\|x-x_{n}\right\|_{L}\to 0,\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty.

This shows
x+N=y+N
and the closedness is proven.

(ii) & (iii)
\Rightarrow
(i): Since
{\varrho}
is reflexive, by Theorem
A.13
it suffices to show that
(M({\varrho}),p+\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})
is complete. Let
(x_{n})
be Cauchy in this space. The completeness of
({\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{M({\varrho})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\textstyle{M({\varrho})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{2.625pt}{$\scriptstyle{M({\varrho})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{N}$}}{\raisebox{1.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{M({\varrho})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{N}$}}},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})
and
(E,p)
yield
x,y\in E
such that
x_{n}\to y
with respect to
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
and
x_{n}\to x
with respect to
p
. The continuity assumption in (ii) yields
x+N=y+N
so that also
x_{n}\to x
with respect to
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L}
. This shows the desired completeness.
∎

A.3.
Dual spaces and resolvents

In this subsection we study the dual space of a given modular space. A particular focus is being laid on an alternative description of
{\varrho}
using duality.

For a vector space
E
we let
E^{*}
denote its algebraic dual space. If
E
carries a vector space topology (e.g. induced by an
F
-norm), we write
E^{\prime}
for its continuous dual space.

Throughout this subsection we assume that
E
is an
{\mathbb{R}}
-vector space and
{\varrho}
is a gsm on
E
. Its
algebraic subgradient
is defined by

\partial^{a}{\varrho}=\{(x,\varphi)\in E\times E^{*}\mid\varphi(y-x)+{\varrho}(x)\leq{\varrho}(y)\text{ for all }y\in E\}

and we let
\partial^{a}{\varrho}(x)=\{\varphi\in E^{*}\mid(x,\varphi)\in\partial^{a}{\varrho}\}
. Since
{\varrho}
is proper,
(x,\varphi)\in\partial^{a}{\varrho}
implies
x\in D({\varrho})
.

Proposition A.28
.

For every
(x,\varphi)\in\partial^{a}{\varrho}
we have
\varphi\in(M({\varrho}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L})^{\prime}
with
\left\|\varphi\right\|_{M({\varrho})^{\prime}}\leq 1+\varphi(x)-{\varrho}(x)
.

Proof.

By definition
(x,\varphi)\in\partial^{a}{\varrho}
implies

\varphi(y)\leq{\varrho}(y)+\varphi(x)-{\varrho}(x)

for all
y\in E
. By the symmetry of
{\varrho}
this inequality also holds for
|\varphi(y)|
on the left side of the inequality. Since
{\varrho}(y)\leq 1
if and only if
\left\|y\right\|_{L}\leq 1
, taking the sup over such
y
yields the claim.
∎

For
x\in D({\varrho})
we define the
directional derivative of
{\varrho}
at
x
by

d^{+}{\varrho}(x,\cdot)\colon E\to(-\infty,\infty],\quad d^{+}{\varrho}(x,y)=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\frac{{\varrho}(x+\varepsilon y)-{\varrho}(x)}{\varepsilon}.

Lemma A.29
.

For all
x\in D({\varrho})
and
y\in E
we have

d^{+}{\varrho}(x,y-x)+{\varrho}(x)\leq{\varrho}(y).

In particular, if
d^{+}{\varrho}(x,\cdot)
is a linear functional on
M({\varrho})
, then
d^{+}{\varrho}(x,\cdot)\in\partial^{a}{\varrho}(x)
(more precisely any linear extension of
d^{+}{\varrho}(x,\cdot)
to
E
belongs to
\partial^{a}{\varrho}(x)
).

Proof.

Using the convexity of
{\varrho}
we obtain

{\varrho}(x+\varepsilon(y-x))={\varrho}(\varepsilon y+(1-\varepsilon)x)\leq\varepsilon{\varrho}(y)+(1-\varepsilon){\varrho}(x)

for all
{\varepsilon}\in(0,1)
.
Rearranging this inequality yields

\frac{{\varrho}(x+\varepsilon(y-x))-{\varrho}(x)}{\varepsilon}+{\varrho}(x)\leq{\varrho}(y)

and we arrive at the statement after letting
\varepsilon\to 0+
.

If
d^{+}{\varrho}(x,\cdot)
is linear on
M({\varrho})
, it can be extended to a linear functional on
E
. By the previously shown inequality any such extension belongs to
\partial^{a}{\varrho}(x)
.
∎

Next we assume that
(E,\left\|\cdot\right\|)
is a normed space and that
{\varrho}
is a gsm on
E
. In this case, we write
\partial{\varrho}=\partial^{a}{\varrho}\cap(E\times E^{\prime})
for the
subgradient
of
{\varrho}
.

The
convex conjugate
of
{\varrho}
is defined by

{\varrho}^{*}\colon E^{\prime}\to[-\infty,\infty],\quad{\varrho}^{*}(\varphi)=\sup\{\varphi(x)-{\varrho}(x)\mid x\in E\}.

Since
{\varrho}(0)=0
, we obtain that
{\varrho}^{*}\colon E^{\prime}\to[0,\infty]
is proper, convex and lower semicontinuous and that the supremum in its definition can be taken over
x\in D({\varrho})
. Moreover, due to the symmetry of
{\varrho}
it is also symmetric. The
Fenchel-Moreau
theorem (see e.g.
[
19
, Proposition 4.1]
) states that

{\varrho}(x)=\sup\{\varphi(x)-{\varrho}^{*}(\varphi)\mid\varphi\in E^{\prime}\},\quad x\in E,

if and only if
\varrho
is lower semicontinuous.
Furthermore, it follows from the definition of
\varrho^{*}
that
(x,\varphi)\in\partial{\varrho}
if and only if

\varphi(x)={\varrho}(x)+{\varrho}^{*}(\varphi).

For
x\in E
we let

D(x)=\{\varphi\in E^{\prime}\mid\varphi(x)=\left\|x\right\|^{2}=\left\|\varphi\right\|^{2}\}.

The operator
D=\{(x,\varphi)\mid x\in E,\varphi\in D(x)\}
is called
normalized duality mapping
. It is well known (and easily verified) that
D=\partial(\frac{1}{2}\left\|\cdot\right\|^{2})
, see e.g.
[
12
]
for this fact and further properties of the duality mapping. In particular, if
E
is a Hilbert space and identified with
E^{\prime}
via the usual map, then
D
is simply the identity operator. Moreover, if
(E,\left\|\cdot\right\|)
is strictly convex,
D
is single-valued.

For
\lambda>0
we define

{\varrho}^{(\lambda)}\colon E\to[0,\infty),\quad{\varrho}^{(\lambda)}(x)=\inf\{{\varrho}(y)+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\left\|x-y\right\|^{2}\mid y\in E\}.

Assume that
(E,\left\|\cdot\right\|)
is strictly convex. For
x\in E
and
\lambda>0
, we let
J_{\lambda}x\in E
be the unique element such that

{\varrho}^{(\lambda)}(x)={\varrho}(J_{\lambda}x)+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\left\|x-J_{\lambda}x\right\|^{2}

(if it exists). By standard weak compactness arguments, this minimizer always exists if
(E,\left\|\cdot\right\|)
is a reflexive Banach space and
{\varrho}
is lower semicontinous.
In this case, the family of operators
(J_{\lambda})_{\lambda>0}
is called
resolvent family
of
{\varrho}
. With the help of the resolvent we obtain the following alternative description of
{\varrho}
on the closure of
D({\varrho})
. It is certainly well-known to experts but we include a proof for the convenience of the reader.

Proposition A.30

(Alternative formula for
{\varrho}
)
.

Assume that
(E,\left\|\cdot\right\|)
is a strictly convex reflexive Banach space and that
{\varrho}
is a lower semicontinous gsm. For
x\in E
and
\varphi_{\lambda}=\lambda^{-1}D(x-J_{\lambda}x)\in E^{\prime}
,
\lambda>0
, the following hold:

(a)

(J_{\lambda}x,\varphi_{\lambda})\in\partial{\varrho}
.

(b)

If
x\in D({\varrho})
, then

{\varrho}(x)=\lim_{\lambda\to 0+}{\varrho}(J_{\lambda}x)\text{ and }\lim_{\lambda\to 0+}\frac{1}{\lambda}\left\|x-J_{\lambda}x\right\|^{2}=\lim_{\lambda\to 0+}\varphi_{\lambda}(x-J_{\lambda}x)=0.

(c)

If
x\in D({\varrho})
, then

{\varrho}(x)=\lim_{\lambda\to 0+}\left(\varphi_{\lambda}(x)-{\varrho}^{*}(\varphi_{\lambda})\right).

Proof.

(a): By definition the element
J_{\lambda}x
is a minimizer of the functional
y\mapsto{\varrho}(y)+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\left\|x-y\right\|^{2}
. Hence,

0\in\partial\left({\varrho}(\cdot)+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\left\|x-\cdot\right\|^{2}\right)(J_{\lambda}x).

Using this and
D=\partial(\frac{1}{2}\left\|\cdot\right\|^{2})
, we infer

0\in\partial{\varrho}(J_{\lambda}x)-\frac{1}{\lambda}D(x-J_{\lambda}x).

(b): For
x\in D({\varrho})
the inequality
{\varrho}^{(\lambda)}\leq{\varrho}
implies
\left\|x-J_{\lambda}x\right\|^{2}\leq 2\lambda{\varrho}(x)
, showing
J_{\lambda}x\to x
in
E
, as
\lambda\to 0+
.
With the help of the lower semicontinuity of
{\varrho}
, we infer

\limsup_{\lambda\to 0+}\left({\varrho}(J_{\lambda}x)+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\left\|x-J_{\lambda}x\right\|^{2}\right)\leq{\varrho}(x)\leq\liminf_{\lambda\to 0+}{\varrho}(J_{\lambda}x).

But this implies
\lim_{\lambda\to 0+}{\varrho}(J_{\lambda}x)={\varrho}(x)
and
0=\lim_{\lambda\to 0+}\lambda^{-1}\left\|x-J_{\lambda}x\right\|^{2}=\lim_{\lambda\to 0+}\varphi_{\lambda}(x-J_{\lambda}x)
.

(c): Since
(J_{\lambda}x,\varphi_{\lambda})\in\partial{\varrho}
, we have

\varphi_{\lambda}(J_{\lambda}x)={\varrho}(J_{\lambda}x)+{\varrho}^{*}(\varphi_{\lambda})

and all of these quantities are finite. Rearranging them yields

\varphi_{\lambda}(x)-{\varrho}^{*}(\varphi_{\lambda})=\varrho(J_{\lambda}x)+\varphi_{\lambda}(x-J_{\lambda}x).

Hence, the statement follows from (b).
∎

Appendix B
A simple inequality

Lemma B.1
.

Let
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, let
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
with
|\lambda|\leq 1
and let
p\geq 1
. Then

|x+\lambda y|^{p}+|x-\lambda y|^{p}\leq|x+y|^{p}+|x-y|^{p},

where
|\cdot|
denotes the Euclidean norm (or any other norm induced by a scalar product).

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can assume
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
. The triangle inequality and the convexity of the function
t\mapsto t^{p}
imply

\displaystyle|x+\lambda y|^{p}+|x-\lambda y|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\left(|x+y|^{p}+|x-y|^{p}\right)+2(1-\lambda)|x|^{p}

\displaystyle=\lambda(|x+y|^{p}+|x-y|^{p}-2|x|^{p})+2|x|^{p}.

Hence, it suffices to show
|x+y|^{p}+|x-y|^{p}-2|x|^{p}\geq 0
, as in this case the right side of the previous inequality attains its maximum at
\lambda=1
. We rewrite
|x+y|^{p}+|x-y|^{p}=|R+t_{0}|^{q}+|R-t_{0}|^{q}
, with
R=|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}
,
t_{0}=2\langle x,y\rangle
and
q=p/2>0
. Then
|t_{0}|\leq R
. Consider the function

\varphi\colon[-R,R]\to{\mathbb{R}},\quad\varphi(t)=|R+t|^{q}+|R-t|^{q}.

Computing its derivative shows directly that
\varphi
is decreasing on
[-R,0]
and increasing on
[0,R]
. Hence, its minimum is attained at
t=0
, where it attains the value
\varphi(0)=2|R|^{q}=2||x|^{2}+|y|^{2}|^{q}\geq 2|x|^{p}
.
∎

Corollary B.2
.

Let
a,b\geq 0
and
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
with
|c|\leq ab
. Then for all
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
with
|\lambda|\leq 1
and
1\leq p<\infty
the following inequality holds:

(a^{2}+2\lambda c+\lambda^{2}b^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}+(a^{2}-2\lambda c+\lambda^{2}b^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq(a^{2}+2c+b^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}+(a^{2}-2c+b^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}.

Proof.

Using
|c|\leq ab
we obtain
b^{2}-c^{2}/a^{2}\geq 0
. In
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
the vectors
x=(a,0)
and
y=(c/a,\sqrt{b^{2}-c^{2}/a^{2}})
satisfy
a=|x|,b=|y|
and
c=\langle x,y\rangle
. Hence, the statement follows directly from the previous lemma.
∎

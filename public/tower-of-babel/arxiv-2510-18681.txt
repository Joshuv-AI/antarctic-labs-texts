A Generalization of the Sphere Covering Inequality
Changfeng Gui Amir Moradifam

Abstract
The Sphere Covering Inequality was introduced in [ 32 ] ( Invent. Math. , 2018) as a sharp geometric inequality that provides a lower bound for the total area of two distinct surfaces of Gaussian curvature 1. These surfaces are assumed to be conformal to the Euclidean unit disk and share the same conformal factor along the boundary. In this paper, we establish a quantitative generalization that relaxes the boundary matching condition by allowing the conformal factors to differ by a constant c ≥ 0 c\geq 0 on the boundary. This refinement reveals a new stability-type structure underlying the inequality. Our results show that the Sphere Covering Inequality is stable with respect to perturbations in the boundary data and provide a precise quantitative description of how the total-area bound varies under such perturbations. The generalized inequality provides new analytic and geometric tools for the study of elliptic equations with exponential nonlinearities, conformal geometry, and related problems in mathematical physics.

Introduction

Second-order nonlinear elliptic equations with exponential nonlinearities form one of the most fundamental classes of equations in two-dimensional analysis. Equations of the form

\Delta u+e^{u}=f(x),\quad x\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2},

Equations of this form naturally arise in problems where curvature, conformal geometry, and analytic inequalities interact. Theorem 1.1
(The Sphere Covering Inequality

Let
\Omega
be a simply-connected subset of
\R^{2}
and assume
v_{i}\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
,
i=1,2

\Delta v_{i}+e^{2v_{i}}=f_{i}(y),

(1.2)

f_{2}\geq f_{1}\geq 0

\Omega

v_{2}\geq v_{1},v_{2}\not\equiv v_{1}

\omega

v_{2}=v_{1}
on
\partial\omega

\omega\subset\Omega

\int_{\omega}(e^{2v_{1}}+e^{2v_{2}})dy\geq 4\pi.

(1.3)

f_{2}\equiv f_{1}\equiv 0
in
\omega

(\omega,e^{2v_{i}}dy)

i=1,2

In this paper, we establish a generalization of the Sphere Covering Inequality that relaxes the boundary condition requiring two conformal metrics (or solutions) to coincide exactly on the boundary. Instead, we allow the solutions to differ by a constant
c\geq 0
on
\partial\Omega
. This generalization broadens the applicability of the SCI to problems with nonidentical boundary data while preserving its geometric sharpness. Such a refinement provides new analytic tools for the study of elliptic PDEs with relaxed boundary conditions, symmetry-breaking phenomena, and geometric stability problems arising in conformal geometry and related fields.

Specifically, instead of assuming
v_{1}=v_{2}
on
\partial\Omega
, we permit a constant boundary gap:

v_{2}-v_{1}=c\geq 0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.

Our main result shows that under these weaker boundary conditions one still obtains a quantitative lower bound, but with the constant
4\pi
replaced by a parameter depending on the boundary gap
c
relative to the maximal difference of the solutions inside
\Omega
. Precisely, if
\omega\subset\Omega
is a Lipschitz subdomain on which
v_{2}\geq v_{1}
and
v_{2}\not\equiv v_{1}
, then

\int_{\omega}\big(e^{2v_{1}}+e^{2v_{2}}\big)\,dy\;\geq\;4(1-\frac{c}{M})\pi,

where

M=\max_{\omega}(v_{2}-v_{1}).

When
c=0
, the result reduces to the classical Sphere Covering Inequality, and if
c=\frac{1}{2}\max\limits_{\omega}(v_{2}-v_{1})>0,
then

\int_{\omega}(e^{2v_{1}}+e^{2v_{2}})dy\geq 2\pi.

Indeed we prove the following theorem which is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.2
(Generalized Sphere Covering Inequality)

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be simply connected, and assume
v_{i}\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
,
i=1,2
, satisfy

\Delta v_{i}+e^{2v_{i}}=f_{i}(y),

(1.4)

where
f_{2}\geq f_{1}\geq 0
in
\Omega
. Suppose that
v_{2}\geq v_{1}+c
,
v_{2}\not\equiv v_{1}
in a piecewise Lipschitz subdomain
\omega\subset\Omega
, and that
v_{2}-v_{1}=c\geq 0
on
\partial\omega
. Then

\int_{\omega}(e^{2v_{1}}+e^{2v_{2}})\,dy\;\geq\;4(1-\frac{c}{M})\pi,

(1.5)

where

M=\max_{\omega}(v_{2}-v_{1}).

Moreover, equality holds if and only if
c=0
,
f_{2}\equiv f_{1}\equiv 0
in
\omega
, and the weighted surfaces
(\omega,e^{2v_{i}}dy)
,
i=1,2
, are isometric to two complementary spherical caps on the standard unit sphere.

Conceptually, this result may be viewed as a stability-type extension of the Sphere Covering Inequality, quantifying how much geometric information is lost when the boundary agreement condition is relaxed. Quantitative stability results for sharp conformally invariant inequalities have recently been obtained in different settings: Carlen
[
15
]
established stability for the logarithmic Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev inequality as well as for the Onofri inequality, and Ghosh and Karmakar
[
29
]
demonstrated quantitative stability for the Chang–Gui inequality on the sphere.

Remark 1.3

The assumption
v_{2}\geq v_{1}+c
in Theorem
1.2
can be replaced by the weaker condition

M=\max_{\omega}(v_{2}-v_{1})>c.

Indeed, under this weaker hypothesis one may apply Theorem
1.2
to the set

\omega^{\prime}:=\{x\in\omega:v_{2}(x)-v_{1}(x)>c\},

and conclude that

\int_{\omega}(e^{2v_{1}}+e^{2v_{2}})\,dy\;\geq\;\int_{\omega^{\prime}}(e^{2v_{1}}+e^{2v_{2}})\,dy\;\geq\;4\!\left(1-\frac{c}{M}\right)\!\pi.

Auxiliary Results and Preliminaries

In this section, we establish several preliminary results that will be used in the proof of the main theorem. These lemmas, though elementary in nature, play an essential role in clarifying the analytic framework and preparing the ground for the more delicate geometric arguments that follow.

Lemma 2.1

For
k\in[0,1]
, define

f(x)=\frac{x^{2-k}-x^{k}}{x^{2}-1}+k-1.

Then
f(x)\leq 0
for all
x\in(1,\infty)
.
In particular,
f(x)<0
for all
x\in(1,\infty)
if
k\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

First observe that
f\equiv 0
for
k\in\{0,1\}
.
Assume
k\in(0,1)
. Since

\lim_{x\to 1}f(x)=0,

it suffices to show that
f
is decreasing on
(1,\infty)
.
A direct computation gives

f^{\prime}(x)=\frac{x\big(-kx^{2-k}+(2-k)x^{k}-(2-k)x^{-k}+kx^{k-2}\big)}{(x^{2}-1)^{2}},

so
f
is decreasing if

f_{1}(x):=-kx^{2-k}+(2-k)x^{k}-(2-k)x^{-k}+kx^{k-2}<0

for all
x>1
.
Since
f_{1}(1)=0
, it is enough to verify that
f_{1}^{\prime}(x)<0
on
(1,\infty)
, where

f_{1}^{\prime}(x)=k(2-k)\big(-x^{1-k}+x^{k-1}+x^{-k-1}-x^{k-3}\big).

Note that
f_{1}^{\prime}(x)<0
if

f_{2}(x):=-x^{1-k}+x^{k-1}+x^{-k-1}-x^{k-3}

is decreasing on
(1,\infty)
.
Indeed,

\displaystyle f_{2}^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=-(1-k)x^{-k}-(1-k)x^{k-2}-(k+1)x^{-k-2}+(3-k)x^{k-4}

\displaystyle<\big(-(1-k)-(1-k)-(k+1)+(3-k)\big)x^{k-4}=0,

and hence
f_{2}
is strictly decreasing on
(1,\infty)
.
The proof is complete.
\Box

We shall also need the following elementary result.

Lemma 2.2

Let
a,b,k
be positive constants with
b>a>0
and
k\in[0,1]
.
Suppose that

\frac{8+b^{2}x^{2}}{8+a^{2}x^{2}}=\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{k}

(2.1)

holds. Then the inequality

\frac{a^{2}x^{2}}{8+a^{2}x^{2}}+\frac{b^{2}x^{2}}{8+b^{2}x^{2}}\geq k

(2.2)

is satisfied. Moreover, if
k\in(0,1)
, then the inequality is strict.

Proof.

From (
2.1
), we obtain

x^{2}=\frac{8\left(\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{k}-1\right)}{b^{2}-\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{k}a^{2}},

(2.3)

and hence

8+a^{2}x^{2}=\frac{8\left(\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{2}-1\right)}{\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{k}}.

(2.4)

Consequently,

\frac{8}{8+a^{2}x^{2}}=\frac{\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{k}}{\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{2}-1}.

(2.5)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{a^{2}x^{2}}{8+a^{2}x^{2}}+\frac{b^{2}x^{2}}{8+b^{2}x^{2}}

\displaystyle=2-8\left(\frac{1}{8+a^{2}x^{2}}+\frac{1}{8+b^{2}x^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=2-\frac{8}{8+a^{2}x^{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{\frac{8+b^{2}x^{2}}{8+a^{2}x^{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle=2-\frac{\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{k}}{\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{2}-1}\left(1+\frac{1}{\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{k}}\right)

\displaystyle=2-\frac{\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{k}}{\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{2}-1}\frac{1+\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{k}}{\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{k}}

\displaystyle=2-\frac{\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{2-k}-1}{\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{2}-1}\left(1+\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{k}\right)

\displaystyle=2-\frac{\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{2-k}-1+\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{k}}{\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{2}-1}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{2-k}-\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{k}}{\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{2}-1}.

Hence, inequality (
2.2
) holds if and only if

\frac{\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{2-k}-\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{k}}{\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{2}-1}\leq 1-k.

(2.6)

By Lemma
2.1
, the above inequality holds for
\frac{b}{a}=x>1
.
\Box

For
\lambda>0
, define
U_{\lambda}
by

U_{\lambda}:=-2\ln(1+\frac{\lambda^{2}|y|^{2}}{8})+2\ln(\lambda),

(2.7)

which satisfies the equation

\Delta U_{\lambda}+e^{U_{\lambda}}=0.\\

We are now ready to prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 2.3

Let
b>a>0
, and let
U_{a}
and
U_{b}
be defined as in (

Let

\int_{B_{R}}\!\big(e^{U_{a}}+e^{U_{b}}\big)\,dy\geq 8k\pi,

Proof.

From (

\frac{8+b^{2}R^{2}}{8+a^{2}R^{2}}=\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{k}.

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\!\big(e^{U_{a}}+e^{U_{b}}\big)\,dy

\displaystyle=8\pi\left(\frac{a^{2}R^{2}}{8+a^{2}R^{2}}+\frac{b^{2}R^{2}}{8+b^{2}R^{2}}\right)\geq 8k\pi,

with strict inequality when
k\in(0,1)
.
\Box

Generalized Sphere Covering Inequality

In this section, we present the proof of our main result, Theorem
1.2
. As a first step, we derive a comparison principle for radial solutions involved, which will play a crucial role in our proof.

Proposition 3.1

Assume that
\psi\in C^{0,1}(\overline{B_{R}})
is a strictly decreasing, radial, Lipschitz function, and satisfies

\int_{\partial B_{r}}|\nabla\psi|ds\leq\int_{B_{r}}e^{\psi}dy

(3.1)

a.e.
r\in(0,R)
and
\psi(0)=U_{b}(0)
for some
b>0
. Then

\int_{B_{R}}e^{\psi}dy\geq\int_{B_{R}}e^{U_{b}}dy.

(3.2)

Moreover if the inequality in (
3.1
) is strict at some point in
(0,R)
, then the inequality in (
3.2
) is also strict.

Proof.
Let
0<b^{\prime}<b
. We claim that

\int_{B_{R}}e^{\psi}dy>\int_{B_{R}}e^{U_{b^{\prime}}}dy.

(3.3)

Since
\psi(0)=U_{b}(0)>U_{b^{\prime}}(0)
and
\psi
is continuous, we must have
\psi>U_{b^{\prime}}
in a neighborhood of the origin. If
\psi\geq U_{b^{\prime}}
in
B_{R}
, then (
3.2
) follows immediately. Define

r_{1}=\sup\{s\in[0,R):\psi(r)>U_{b^{\prime}}(r)\ \ \text{for all}\ \ r\in[0,s)\}.

Then
0<r_{1}<R
,
\psi(r_{1})=U_{b^{\prime}}(r_{1})
, and

\int_{B_{r_{1}}}e^{\psi(y)}dy>\int_{B_{r_{1}}}e^{U_{b^{\prime}}(y)}dy.

(3.4)

On the other hand, by Bol’s inequality we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{B_{r_{1}}}e^{\psi}\right)\left(8\pi-\int_{B_{r_{1}}}e^{\psi}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\partial B_{r_{1}}}e^{\frac{\psi}{2}}\right)^{2}=\left(\int_{\partial B_{r_{1}}}e^{\frac{U_{b^{\prime}}}{2}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{B_{r_{1}}}e^{U_{b^{\prime}}}\right)\left(8\pi-\int_{B_{r_{1}}}e^{U_{b^{\prime}}}\right),

and therefore
\int_{B_{r_{1}}}e^{\psi}>4\pi
. This follows from(
3.4
) and the fact that the polynomial
\frac{1}{2}x(8\pi-x)
is strictly increasing on
(0,4\pi)
. Now define

r_{2}=\sup\{s\in[0,R]:\int_{B_{s}}e^{\psi(y)}dy\geq\int_{B_{s}}e^{U_{b^{\prime}}(y)}dy\},

and note that
r_{2}\geq r_{1}.
We claim that
r_{2}=R
. Suppose this is not the case. Then there exists
r_{3}
with
R\geq r_{3}>r_{2}
such that

\psi(r_{3})<U_{b^{\prime}}(r_{3})\ \ \text{and}\ \ \int_{B_{r_{3}}}e^{\psi(y)}dy<\int_{B_{r_{3}}}e^{U_{b^{\prime}}(y)}dy

By Bol’s inequality we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{B_{r_{3}}}e^{\psi}\right)\left(8\pi-\int_{B_{r_{3}}}e^{\psi}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\partial B_{r_{3}}}e^{\frac{\psi}{2}}\right)^{2}<\left(\int_{\partial B_{r_{3}}}e^{\frac{U_{b^{\prime}}}{2}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{B_{r_{3}}}e^{U_{b^{\prime}}}\right)\left(8\pi-\int_{B_{r_{3}}}e^{U_{b^{\prime}}}\right),

this is a contradiction because
\frac{1}{2}x(8\pi-x)
is increasing only for
x\leq 4\pi
and
\int_{B_{r_{3}}}e^{\psi}dy>4\pi
. Hence the inequality (
3.3
) holds for all
0<b^{\prime}<b
. The inequality (
3.2
) follows from (
3.3
) by letting

. The proof is now complete.

Theorem 3.1

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be simply connected, and assume
v_{i}\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
,
i=1,2
, satisfy

\Delta v_{i}+e^{v_{i}}=f_{i}(y),

(3.5)

where
f_{2}\geq f_{1}\geq 0
in
\Omega
. Suppose that
v_{2}\geq v_{1}+c
,
v_{2}\not\equiv v_{1}
in a piecewise Lipschitz subdomain
\omega\subset\Omega
, and that
v_{2}-v_{1}=c\geq 0
on
\partial\omega
. Then

\int_{\omega}(e^{v_{1}}+e^{v_{2}})\,dy\;\geq\;8(1-\frac{c}{M})\pi,

(3.6)

where

M=\max_{\omega}(v_{2}-v_{1}).

Moreover, equality holds if and only if
c=0
,
f_{2}\equiv f_{1}\equiv 0
in
\omega
, and the weighted surfaces
(\omega,e^{2v_{i}}dy)
,
i=1,2
, are isometric to two complementary spherical caps on the standard unit sphere.

Proof.
Suppose
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
satisfy the assumptions of the theorem. Then

\Delta(v_{2}-v_{1})+e^{v_{2}}-e^{v_{1}}=f_{2}-f_{1}\geq 0.

We may assume
\int_{\omega}e^{v_{1}}dy<4\pi
and

\int_{\omega}e^{v_{1}}+e^{v_{2}}dy<8\pi-\epsilon

(3.7)

for some
\epsilon>0
. For any

\int_{\omega}e^{v_{1}}dy=\int_{B_{R_{a}}}e^{U_{a}}dy=\frac{8\pi a^{2}R_{a}^{2}}{8+a^{2}R_{a}^{2}},

be define by (

. Now let
b=a+M

M:=\max\limits_{\omega}(v_{2}-v_{1})

\displaystyle 0<\int_{B_{R_{a}}}e^{U_{b}}dy-\int_{B_{R_{a}}}e^{U_{a}}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8\pi\left[\frac{(a+M)^{2}R_{a}^{2}}{8+(a+M)^{2}R_{a}^{2}}-\frac{a^{2}R_{a}^{2}}{8+a^{2}R_{a}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8\pi\left[\frac{(a+M)^{2}\frac{K}{a^{2}}}{8+(a+M)^{2}\frac{K}{a^{2}}}-\frac{K}{8+K}\right]<\epsilon.

\int_{B_{R_{a}}}e^{U_{b}}dy-\int_{B_{R_{a}}}e^{U_{a}}dy<\epsilon,

\omega_{t}:=\{x\in\omega:v_{2}(x)-v_{1}(x)>t\},

with respect to the measure

and

, i.e.

\int_{\{\phi>t\}}e^{U_{a}}dy=\int_{\{v_{2}-v_{1}>t\}}e^{v_{1}}dy,\ \ \forall t>\min_{y\in\overline{\omega}}\phi,

(3.10)

where
\omega^{*}
a ball of radius

\int_{\omega^{*}}e^{U_{a}}dy=\int_{\omega}e^{v_{1}}dy

By Propositions 2.2 and 2.3 in

\displaystyle\int_{\{\phi=t\}}|\nabla\phi|ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\{v_{2}-v_{1}=t\}\cap\omega}|\nabla(v_{2}-v_{1})|ds

(3.11)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\omega_{t}}\bigl(e^{v_{2}}-e^{v_{1}}\bigr)dy\quad\hbox{ (by Green's formula and equation \eqref{mainpde} ) }

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\omega_{t}}e^{v_{2}-v_{1}}e^{v_{1}}dy-\int_{\omega_{t}}e^{v_{1}}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\{\phi>t\}}e^{\phi}e^{U_{a}}dy-\int_{\{\phi>t\}}e^{U_{a}}dy

(by rearrangement and Cavalieri’s principle)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\{\phi>t\}}e^{U_{a}+\phi}dy-\int_{\{\phi=t\}}|\nabla U_{a}|ds,\quad\hbox{for {\it a.e.} }t>c.

Hence

\int_{\{\phi=t\}}|\nabla(U_{a}+\phi)|ds\leq\int_{\{\phi>t\}}e^{(U_{a}+\phi)}dy,\quad\hbox{for {\it a.e.} }t>c.

(3.12)

Since
\phi\geq c
is decreasing in
r
,
\psi:=U_{a}+\phi
is a strictly decreasing radial function, and

\int_{\partial B_{r}}|\nabla\psi|ds\leq\int_{B_{r}}e^{\psi}dy,\quad{\it a.e.}\quad r\in(0,R_{a}),

(3.13)

by Proposition 2.3 in
[
32
]
and the above inequality we know that
\psi
belongs to
C^{0,1}(B_{R_{a}})
. It follows from Proposition
3.1
that

\int_{B_{R_{a}}}e^{\psi}dy=\int_{B_{R_{a}}}e^{U_{a}+\phi}dy\geq\int_{B_{R_{a}}}e^{U_{b}}dy.

Next we claim that
\psi(R_{a})\geq U_{b}(R_{a})
. Suppose that this is not the case. Since, by Proposition
3.1
,

\int_{B_{r}}e^{\psi}dy\geq\int_{B_{r}}e^{U_{b}}dy,\ \ \forall r\in(0,R_{a}),

there must exist
R_{0}\in(0,R_{a})
such that
\psi(R_{0})=U_{b}(R_{0})
, and
\int_{B_{R_{0}}}e^{\psi}dy>\int_{B_{R_{0}}}e^{U_{b}}dy
. Thus it follows from the Sphere Covering Inequality that

\int_{B_{R_{0}}}e^{\psi}+e^{U_{b}}dx\geq 8\pi.

Thus it follows from (
3.9
) that

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}e^{v_{1}}+e^{v_{2}}dy

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R_{0}}}e^{U_{a}}+e^{\psi}dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R_{0}}}e^{U_{b}}+e^{\psi}dx-\epsilon

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 8\pi-\epsilon.

This is a contradiction in view of (
3.7
), and hence

\psi(R_{a})\geq U_{b}(R_{a})

.

Now we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(e^{v_{1}}+e^{v_{2}})dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R_{a}}}(e^{U_{a}}+e^{\psi})dy

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R_{a}}}(e^{U_{a}}+e^{U_{b}})dy\geq 8\pi\left[1-\frac{U_{b}(R_{a})-U_{a}({R_{a}})}{M}\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 8\pi\left[1-\frac{\psi(R_{a})-U_{a}({R_{a}})}{M}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8\pi\left[1-\frac{(v_{2}-v_{1})|_{\partial\omega}}{M}\right]=8(1-\frac{c}{M})\pi.

Moreover, if the equality holds, then
k=1
, and the inequality (
3.6
) simplifies to the Sphere Covering Inequality
[
32
]
for which the equality case is established.

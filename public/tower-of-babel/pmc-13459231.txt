[18F] PSMA-3Q PET/CT radiomics at 45% SUVmax threshold: predicting post-surgical ISUP grade ≥ 4 and extraprostatic extension for noninvasive stratification in prostate cancer management
Authors: Jianbo Cui, Yachao Liu, Guanyun Wang, Xiaodan Xu, Dong Zhao, Ran Xue, Wenjing Chen, Ruimin Wang, Baixuan Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo systematically evaluate radiomic features extracted from [18F] PSMA-3Q PET/CT using 40%, 45%, and 50% SUVmax thresholds for their ability to predict post-surgical International Society of Urological Pathology (psISUP) grading and extraprostatic extension (EPE) in prostate cancer and ultimately develop an optimal threshold-based predictive model.Materials & methodsThis retrospective study included 243 prostate cancer patients undergoing [18F] PSMA-3Q PET/CT before radical prostatectomy. Patients were chronologically divided into training (n = 190) and test (n = 53) cohorts, with stratification by prostate-specific antigen density (PSAD < 0.25 vs. ≥ 0.25 ng/mL). Radiomics features were extracted using 40%, 45%, and 50% SUVmax thresholds. Nine machine learning algorithms developed integrated PET/CT radiomics-clinical models (PC40, PC45, PC50) and PET radiomics-clinical models (P40, P45, P50). Model performance was evaluated using area under the curve (AUC), sensitivity, specificity, positive and negative predictive values.ResultsAmong the models constructed with 40%, 45%, and 50% SUVmax thresholds, the PC45 model (PET/CT radiomics + clinical parameters) performed best. For predicting psISUP grade ≥ 4, its test set AUC reached 0.917 (95% CI: 0.828–0.985), with 0.800 sensitivity and 0.921 specificity. For EPE prediction, the test set AUC was 0.772(95% CI: 0.633–0.883), sensitivity 0.700 and specificity 0.727. It showed stable performance across PSAD subgroups and higher net clinical benefit.ConclusionThe [18F] PSMA-3Q PET/CT radiomics model with a 45% SUVmax threshold may facilitate relatively accurate noninvasive prediction of psISUP grade and EPE. These findings suggest its potential value as a promising preoperative tool for precision prostate cancer management, and provide preliminary evidence supporting its stable and balanced performance as a noninvasive decision‑making aid, pending further validation in larger cohorts.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40644-026-01050-8.Keywords: Prostate cancer, PSMA PET/CT, Radiomics, Extraprostatic extension, ISUP grading

Introduction

Prostate cancer (PCa) continues to be one of the most common malignant tumors in males across the globe, presenting a notable challenge to public health [1]. Preoperative assessment of extraprostatic extension (EPE) and International Society of Urological Pathology (ISUP) grading is crucial for guiding surgical planning, determining resection margins, and predicting clinical outcomes [2, 3]. Currently, preoperative evaluation relies on the MRI-based Mehralivand classification system for EPE and transrectal biopsy for ISUP grading [4, 5]. However, substantial interobserver variability affects subjective imaging interpretations, while invasive biopsies are limited by sampling errors and potential complications-highlighting the urgent need for noninvasive, high-precision assessment tools [6, 7].

The imaging technique of positron emission tomography (PET) combined with computed tomography (CT) targeting prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA) has surfaced as a potentially effective method for detecting PCa, offering high specificity in tumor localization and characterization of their biological properties [8]. [18F] PSMA-3Q, namely Al18F-labeled 1,4,7-triazacyclononane-1,4,7-triacetic acid-PSMA-3 quinoline, is a novel [18F]-labeled PSMA-targeted PET radiotracer [9]. Previous studies [10] have demonstrated that this radiotracer is more sensitive to suspicious metastatic lesions with low prostate-specific antigen (PSA) levels and exhibits a significant advantage in detection. However, prior studies have shown that visual and semi-quantitative evaluation of PSMA PET/CT images alone may not improve the detection rate of clinically significant PCa [11].

Recent developments in radiomics have brought forth an innovative method, in which machine learning techniques derive extensive quantitative features from medical images to build predictive models, thereby revealing latent biological information from imaging data. Although multimodal radiomics has been investigated, the use of PSMA PET radiomics—an approach that retrieves quantitative features from medical images with high throughput—still stays in the preliminary stage when it comes to predicting high-grade ISUP and EPE in PCa [12–16]. Prior studies suggest that using a 45% maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax) threshold improves reliability in evaluating PSMA RADS 4/5 lesions on PET [14]. However, limited data exist regarding the performance of the 45% SUVmax threshold in predicting post-prostatectomy pathological characteristics, particularly EPE and ISUP grade, with most studies involving small sample sizes.

Against this backdrop, our study aimed to systematically evaluate radiomic features (RFs) extracted from [18F] PSMA-3Q PET/CT using different SUVmax thresholds (40%, 45%, and 50%) to assess their ability to predict EPE and ISUP grading, ultimately developing an optimal threshold model capable of simultaneously forecasting both pathological features.

Materials and methods

Patient cohort

This retrospective single-center study was conducted at our institution, with data collected from male patients who underwent [18F] PSMA-3Q PET/CT scans between 2021 and 2024. A total of 243 prostate cancer patients were included (Fig.1), all of whom met the following inclusion criteria: (a) definitive pathological diagnosis of prostate cancer; (b) undergoing radical prostatectomy with complete post-surgical ISUP (psISUP) evaluation; and (c) an interval of less than 30 days between [18F] PSMA-3Q PET/CT acquisition and radical prostatectomy. Exclusion criteria were: (a) history of other malignant tumors; (b) [18F] PSMA-3Q PET/CT performed after antitumor therapy (including systemic drug therapy or prior local surgery); (c) incomplete or poor-quality clinical, laboratory, or imaging data; and (d) PSMA-negative prostate cancer (i.e., no focal [18F] PSMA-3Q uptake identified in the prostate gland on PET images).

Flow chart for patient selection in [18F] PSMA-3Q PET/CT study of PCa

Patients were divided into two cohorts: a training cohort (n = 190) enrolled before June 1, 2024, and a test cohort (n = 53) enrolled from June 1 to December 31, 2024. Both cohorts were stratified by prostate-specific antigen density (PSAD < 0.25 vs. ≥ 0.25 ng/mL) to ensure balanced baseline characteristics. This study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the Chinese People’s Liberation Army General Hospital (approval number: S2026-109-01). Given the retrospective nature of the research, the requirement for written informed consent from participants was waived in accordance with relevant regulatory guidelines.

Collected clinical, laboratory, and imaging-derived parameters included total PSA (tPSA), total-to-free PSA ratio (t/f PSA), prostate-specific antigen density (PSAD), systemic immune-inflammation index (SII), target-to-liver ratio (TLR), PSMA-prostate total lesion (PSMA-TL), body mass index (BMI), prostate volume (PV), and the transverse and longitudinal locations of the index lesion.

Image acquisition

This study utilized three PET/CT scanner models: GE Discovery 710 (Solingen, Germany), Siemens Biograph 64 (Erlangen, Germany), and United Imaging uMI 510 (Shanghai, China). Whole-body scans from the skull base to the proximal femoral were acquired 120 min post-injection of [18F] PSMA-3Q (3.70 MBq/kg) under free-breathing conditions. CT parameters included a tube voltage of 120–140 kV, current of 100–200 mAs, rotation time of 0.8 s, slice thickness of 3–5 mm, and pitch of 0.9-1.0. PET scans employed a 3D acquisition mode (2–2.5 min per bed position, 30% overlap, 4–5 beds per patient). Image reconstruction used an ordered subset expectation maximization algorithm (3 iterations, 21 subsets) with CT-based attenuation correction and a Gaussian filter with a full width at half-maximum (FWHM) of 4.0 mm. This protocol ensured high-resolution image quality for subsequent analysis.

Tumor region segmentation, radiomic feature extraction and screening

Image segmentation was performed using the uAI Research Portal (version 20230915) [17]. A semi-automatic method delineated regions of interest (ROIs) based on three SUVmax thresholds (40%, 45%, 50%). Images were resampled to 1 mm³ isotropic voxels, with fixed bin widths [18] set at 25 for CT images and 1 for PET images. Two experienced physicians manually delineated the largest tumor volume on PET using the 45% threshold, and these ROIs were rigidly registered to CT.

Subsequent preprocessing applied 13 image filters (e.g., Laplacian of Gaussian, Wavelet). Following IBSI guidelines, 2,286 PET and 2,274 CT RFs were extracted and normalized. Inter-observer reproducibility was assessed on 30 randomly selected cases using the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC), retaining only features with ICC > 0.8.

Feature dimensionality was first reduced using the Select K Best method. The Elastic Net algorithm [19] was then employed for robust feature selection, combining L1 and L2 regularization to identify a stable subset of discriminative features while mitigating bias from collinearity. This process enhanced interpretability and removed noise. Finally, key PET/CT RFs selected by Elastic Net were used to construct a radiomics score. Detailed performance data of features extracted from VOIs under different SUVmax thresholds are provided in Supplementary file 1. A representative case illustrating tumor segmentation at the 45% SUVmax threshold and corresponding histopathology is shown in Figure S1 (Supplementary File 1).

Construction of predictive model and multi-dimensional assessment

Predictive models for EPE and psISUP (≥ 4) were developed using nine machine learning algorithms: support vector machine (SVM), XGBoost, gradient boosting decision tree (GBDT), random forest, extra trees, AdaBoost, k-nearest neighbors (KNN), logistic regression, and CatBoost. These models were constructed based on three SUVmax value-volume of interest (SUVmax-VOI) thresholds (40%, 45%, and 50%), with two types of threshold-specific models created: (1) PET RFs combined with clinical parameters (denoted as P40, P45, P50); and (2) PET/CT RFs combined with clinical parameters (denoted as PC40, PC45, PC50). To address class imbalance, the Synthetic Minority Oversampling Technique (SMOTE) was applied within each fold of the 100 iterations of five-fold cross-validation, ensuring that data augmentation was performed independently on the training partition prior to model training and validation.

Logistic regression analyses were performed to quantify the independent effects of clinical variables on the binary outcomes of EPE and psISUP grade ≥ 4. All candidate clinical parameters were first screened by univariate logistic regression (P < 0.05). Multivariate logistic regression with stepwise variable selection was then applied to identify independent predictors for the clinical prediction model. When no variables retained statistical significance in multivariate analysis (indicating potential multicollinearity), the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression was performed on all clinical variables for feature selection, and the selected variables were subsequently confirmed in a multivariate logistic regression model. The final clinical model (CM) for each outcome was then compared with the optimal model from the corresponding threshold-specific category.

Model evaluation prioritized the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) for assessing overall diagnostic capability. A comprehensive performance profile was established through supplementary metrics including sensitivity, specificity, Youden’s index, positive and negative predictive values, and optimal threshold analysis. Decision curve analysis and Calibration assessment demonstrated the consistency between predicted probabilities and actual outcomes, supporting the model’s clinical validity. Furthermore, subgroup analyses were conducted based on PSAD levels (PSAD > 0.25 ng/mL2 vs. PSAD ≤ 0.25 ng/mL2) to explore variations in model performance across distinct patient subgroups. Ultimately, the SUVmax threshold-associated model with the most favorable performance was identified. To ensure the methodological rigor and standardization of the radiomics workflow in this study, the Methodological Radiomics Score (METRICS) [20] endorsed by the European Society of Medical Imaging Informatics and Artificial Intelligence (EuSoMII) was adopted for comprehensive quality evaluation provided in Supplementary file 2.

Statistical analyses and model evaluation

For statistical analyses, a comprehensive evaluation of model performance was conducted using Python 3.12 and R version 4.4.3. Continuous variables were summarized as mean ± standard deviation, median, and range, with the Mann-Whitney U test or t-test applied for group comparisons as appropriate. Categorical variables were reported as frequencies and percentages (n, %), and intergroup differences were assessed using Pearson’s chi-square test. A two-sided P‑value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. To quantify the uncertainty of all performance estimates, their 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were derived using nonparametric bootstrapping with 1,000 resamples, covering the area under the AUC, sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), and negative predictive value (NPV).

Construction of clinical models

To identify predictors of psISUP grade ≥ 4, univariate logistic regression identified six significant variables (P < 0.05): PSAD, SUVmax, tPSA, PSMA-TL45, PSMA-TL50, and TLR. However, none remained significant in multivariate analysis, suggesting multicollinearity. LASSO regression on all clinical variables then selected PSAD and SUVmax as the top predictors, with coefficients of 0.015 and 0.007, respectively (Supplementary File 1; Table S2-1; Figure S2). Based on univariate logistic regression (P < 0.05), ten clinical variables were selected for inclusion in the EPE model. Using a stepwise selection criterion, multivariate logistic regression identified three significant independent predictors for the final EPE clinical model: Age, PSAD, and SUVmax(Table S2-2).

Selection of radiomic features, construction of radiomic models, and performance evaluation

PET/CT models were developed utilizing varying SUVmax thresholds to delineate ROIs, with each model constructed based on distinct selected features. Among the models employing SUVmax thresholds of 40%, 45%, and 50%, the combined PET/CT radiomic-clinical models-designated as PC40, PC45, and PC50-demonstrated superior predictive performance for psISUP grade ≥ 4 and EPE.

Concerning psISUP grade ≥ 4, the PC40, PC45, and PC50 models were constructed utilizing SVM, SVM, and AdaBoost algorithms, respectively (Fig. 2A-F). For PC45, the training cohort yielded AUC values of 0.848 (95% CI: 0.782–0.906), 0.810 (95% CI: 0.739–0.876), and 0.829 (95% CI: 0.767–0.888) respectively. When transitioning to the test cohort, the AUCs that matched these three groups were 0.919 (95% CI: 0.828–0.985), 0.917 (95% CI: 0.828–0.985), and 0.890 (95% CI: 0.767–0.980) in turn. A comprehensive summary of performance metrics-including AUC, Sen, Spe, PPV, and NPV-for each model is provided in Table 2. DCA and calibration plots for both the training and test datasets are depicted in Fig. 2B, C and E, and 2F. The calibration curves revealed a strong consistency between the predicted probabilities and the actual observed outcomes in relation to PC45 and PC40, with Brier scores of approximately 0.1267 and 0.1397, respectively. Compared with PC40 and PC50, PC45 exhibited the largest area under the decision curve and a smoother trajectory. This indicates that PC45 delivers the highest net clinical benefit while demonstrating more stable and reliable performance across different decision thresholds.

Regarding the prediction of EPE, three models-PC40, PC45, and PC50-were developed utilizing the AdaBoost, AdaBoost, and XGBoost algorithms, respectively. Table 3 records the performance of the PC45 model in the test set: an AUC of 0.772 (95% CI: 0.633–0.883), plus a Sen of 0.700 (95% CI: 0.479-0.800), Spe of 0.727 (95% CI: 0.548–0.862), PPV of 0.671(95% CI: 0.453–0.773), and NPV of 0.750(95% CI: 0.588–0.882). The PC50 model exhibited performance on par with the PC45 model in the test set: its AUC was 0.736 (95% CI: 0.618–0.855), sensitivity was 0.700 (95% CI: 0.546–0.842), and specificity was 0.727 (95% CI: 0.552–0.833). Calibration confirmed good alignment with actual outcomes, supporting PC45’s superior discriminative capacity and clinical utility for EPE prediction with Brier scores of approximately 0.1934 (Fig. 2K). Decision curve analysis showed that PC45 provided greater net clinical benefit than PC50 across most thresholds, especially above 0.5 (Fig. 2L). Its smoother curve indicated more robust performance.

Subgroup analysis of the optimal threshold model

To assess clinical utility, Sankey analysis of PSAD subgroups was performed. For psISUP prediction, PC45 outperformed PC40 in the high-PSAD subgroup, more accurately identifying both low- and high-grade cases (14/15 vs. 21/15 for < 4; 14/13 vs. 21/13 for ≥ 4) (Fig. 3A-B). Compared to biopsy, PC45 also showed superior prediction of psISUP < 4 (33/34) and ≥ 4 (9/11), whereas biopsy misclassified 5 high-grade and 1 low-grade case (Fig. 3C). For EPE prediction, PC45 misclassified 1/7 EPE cases as non-EPE in the low-PSAD subgroup, in contrast to 4/7 EPE cases misclassified as non-EPE by PC50. In the high-PSAD subgroup, PC45 incorrectly predicted 2/15 non-EPE cases as EPE (vs. 4/15 by PC50) and 2/13 EPE cases as non-EPE (vs. 3/15 by PC50) (Fig. 3D-E).

Discussion

This study further explored the optimal SUVmax threshold (40%-50%) for evaluating prostate cancer biological characteristics, including extraprostatic extension (EPE) and psISUP grading, using single-center [18F] PSMA-3Q PET/CT data. Through multiple model iterations, we found that the intermediate 45% SUVmax threshold model demonstrated predictive performance comparable to both boundary thresholds. Subgroup analysis based on PSAD further confirmed that the 45% SUVmax threshold model provided more accurate predictions than the 40% and 50% SUVmax models. Our findings indicate that the 45% SUVmax threshold model integrates the advantages of both boundary threshold models, exhibiting optimal predictive capability.

NCCN high/very-high-risk prostate cancer patients-defined by ISUP grade ≥ 4 or positive EPE - exhibit heightened biological aggressiveness, characterized by elevated tumor proliferation, increased TP53/PTEN mutations, and impaired anti-tumor immunity [21–24]; clinical guidelines recommend extrafascial radical prostatectomy, pelvic lymph node dissection, and long-term androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) for this subgroup. In contrast, NCCN low/intermediate-risk patients (negative EPE or ISUP grade < 4) have more flexible options, including intrafascial radical prostatectomy, active surveillance (close monitoring without immediate intervention), or short-term ADT combined with radiation therapy [21]. Given these striking treatment disparities, enhancing the prediction of key biological features (e.g., ISUP grade, EPE status) is pivotal to optimizing outcomes: it ensures aggressive intervention for high-risk patients to curb disease progression while preventing overtreatment of low/intermediate-risk individuals, thereby ultimately improving patient quality of life.

Although transrectal biopsy remains the clinical standard for pathological grading, it suffers from sampling errors, infection risks associated with invasive procedures, and poor patient compliance. In particular, conventional systematic biopsy has clear limitations in evaluating the pathological features of prostate cancer [6, 25, 26]. While multiparametric MRI-based EPE scoring enables noninvasive assessment, it relies heavily on subjective interpretation, exhibits low inter-observer consistency across centers and experience levels, and cannot differentiate high-grade pathological types [7, 27]. Consequently, this study aimed to develop a noninvasive prostate cancer risk stratification method to enhance prediction of postoperative outcomes in patients undergoing radical prostatectomy. This approach would help identify patients most likely to benefit from surgical intervention while providing more precise treatment planning for those requiring adjuvant therapy [28–32].

Radiomics, which came into being in the early 21st century, allows for the retrieval of high-volume data from medical imaging materials, converting visual data into measurable indices. These indices can capture slight differences that are beyond human visual perception, thus disclosing the morphological features of tumors as well as their intrinsic biological activities. Prior research has underscored the clinical diagnostic utility of radiomics analysis based on PSMA-targeted PET imaging [12–16, 33, 34]. However, there is still a lack of consistent agreement on the selection of the optimal threshold for this analytical approach. Mihatsch et al. [14] reported that radiomic parameters derived from 45% SUVmax thresholds more reliably predicted PSMA RADS 4/5 lesions compared to 40% and 50% thresholds, though their study was limited to 20 cases. To further validate the predictive performance of the 45% SUVmax threshold, our study employed 40%, 45%, and 50% SUVmax as segmentation standards, comprehensively evaluating their predictive capabilities for psISUP and EPE.

Yao et al. [34] compared the predictive performance of radiomic models across different thresholds, finding that the 40% SUVmax threshold demonstrated superior performance for EPE prediction (test set AUC: 0.78, 95% CI: 0.65–0.91), while the 50% SUVmax threshold showed unique advantages for accurate ISUP grading (test set AUC: 0.80, 95% CI: 0.66–0.90). In contrast to Yao’s findings, our study revealed that the 40% SUVmax threshold provided superior performance for ISUP grading prediction (AUC: 0.934, 95% CI: 0.877–0.989), while the 50% SUVmax threshold demonstrated better performance for EPE prediction (test set AUC: 0.934, 95% CI: 0.877–0.989 vs. 40% threshold AUC: 0.603, 95% CI: 0.471–0.735). These discrepancies may stem from variations in radiopharmaceuticals, tumor heterogeneity, and scanning parameters, potentially limiting broader applicability. However, our study demonstrates that the 45% SUVmax threshold achieves comparable predictive performance to both 40% and 50% thresholds, potentially mitigating performance drift associated with multicenter, multi-radiopharmaceutical, and prostate cancer heterogeneity variations. This finding establishes a foundation for broader clinical implementation. Furthermore, for patients with high bleeding risk or contraindications to surgery, the 45% SUVmax combined model enables comprehensive noninvasive prediction of pathological grade and EPE, thereby guiding comprehensive treatment planning.

Previous PSMA PET radiomics studies for ISUP prediction have shown promising potential. Yang et al. [16] developed an end-to-end [¹⁸F] PSMA-1007 PET/CT radiomics pipeline incorporating deep learning-based automatic segmentation for ISUP prediction, achieving a test set AUC of 0.861 (95% CI: 0.793–0.929), significantly higher than the clinical model’s 0.750 (95% CI: 0.660–0.841). Jiang et al. [35] developed a multimodal deep learning model for ISUP ≥ 4 prediction, reporting a test set AUC of 0.833 (95% CI: 0.769–0.932), NPV of 0.917, and PPV of 0.556. Similar investigations using conventional MRI have been reported [36–38]. One study [37] employing T2-weighted imaging radiomics-based machine learning models demonstrated favorable negative predictive value (AUC: 0.717, NPV: 0.870, PPV: 0.600). Our study demonstrates that the 45% SUVmax threshold-based PSMA PET/CT radiomics model integrated with clinical parameters provides excellent predictive performance for psISUP grading (test set AUC: 0.917, 95% CI: 0.828–0.985; NPV: 0.800, 95% CI: 0.692–0.907; PPV: 0.921, 95% CI: 0.848–0.994), with balanced improvement in both PPV and NPV. Compared to similar PSMA PET/CT deep learning models and MRI-based approaches, our model not only reduces computational hardware and software requirements but also achieves more balanced performance enhancement, providing compelling evidence for threshold selection strategies in future software development.

By targeting PSMA, PSMA PET/CT technology enables more timely and accurate identification of aggressive biomarkers [39–41] -a feature that gives it distinct advantages in EPE prediction, particularly for lesions indistinct on conventional imaging. Bian et al. [13] built a PSMA PET/CT radiomic model, which showed an AUC of 0.768 (95% CI: 0.644–0.865), a PPV of 0.72 (95% CI: 0.558–0.840), and an NPV of 0.816 (95% CI: 0.699–0.894). For comparison, radiologists who used the MRI Mehralivand grading system achieved an optimal AUC of approximately 0.668 (95% CI: 0.538–0.782), with a PPV of 0.600 (95% CI: 0.446–0.736) and an NPV of 0.737 (95% CI: 0.626–0.824). Our PC45 model, by contrast, demonstrated better NPV performance than Bian et al.‘s PSMA model, with an NPV of 0.864 (95% CI: 0.845–0.853). However, our model showed slightly lower PPV (0.592, 95% CI: 0.582–0.594), indicating relatively higher false-positive rates, consistent with subgroup analysis findings where PC45 classified more cases as EPE.

This study has several limitations. First, as a retrospective single-center investigation with a modest sample size, inherent selection bias and limited population heterogeneity may restrict the generalizability of our findings, warranting prospective multicenter external validation for improved reliability. Second, although all preprocessing and modeling steps were strictly confined within cross-validation folds to prevent data leakage, the use of multiple machine learning models combined with SMOTE for class imbalance correction still carries a residual risk of overfitting, which may affect model robustness. Third, despite balanced baseline characteristics, chronological dataset splitting may introduce potential distributional bias. Although we adopted standardized imaging protocols and rigorous quality control, residual batch effects from multiple PET/CT scanners may still influence model performance; future studies should integrate feature-level batch correction (e.g., ComBat) to enhance model robustness. Fourth, inconsistencies between our results and previous reports may be attributed to the substantial heterogeneity of prostate cancer [42]. Fifth, we only included conventional prostatic adenocarcinoma and excluded rare pathological subtypes (e.g., prostatic ductal cancer), for which PSMA PET/CT shows low diagnostic and staging accuracy [43]. Finally, as a data-driven radiomic approach, our model requires larger cohorts for better reproducibility; future work should focus on validating the 45% SUVmax threshold across centers and combining multimodal MRI features to reduce false-positive rates.

Conclusion

Our study further investigated prostate cancer tumor segmentation and demonstrated that among different SUVmax thresholds, the 45% SUVmax threshold provides stable and balanced performance in prostate cancer assessment. The integration of [18F] PSMA PET/CT radiomics with clinical variables effectively differentiates prostate cancer psISUP grading and EPE. This 45% SUVmax threshold-based combined prediction model acts as a valuable noninvasive preoperative assessment tool for prostate cancer pathological grading and EPE, aiding clinical management decisions; notably, it yields preliminary insights for the development of future noninvasive prostate cancer management strategies, which requires further prospective and multicenter validation for clinical translation.

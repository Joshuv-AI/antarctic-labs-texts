Psychometric evaluation of the Chinese version of the Chronic Heart Failure Health-related Quality of Life Questionnaire (CHFQOLQ-20)
Authors: Ying Zhao, Hang Zhao, Xiaoxue Deng, Yanan Wang, Xin Luan, Hongyu Yu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe incidence rate of chronic heart failure has increased significantly in China and has a large impact on patients’ lives. The purpose of the study was to validate the Chronic Heart Failure Health-Related Quality of Life Questionnaire (CHFQOLQ-20) cross-culturally and test its reliability and validity in patients with chronic heart failure. This study was conducted by investigating 622 patients with chronic heart failure from August 2023 to January 2024, and the data were tested for validity using SPSS 25.0 and AMOS 23.0. The final psychometric validation of the CHFQOLQ-20 was performed. The Chinese version of the CHFQOLQ-20 had a Cronbach’s α of 0.908, a split-half reliability of 0.870, and a retest reliability of 0.892. An exploratory factor analysis yielded four common factors with a cumulative ANOVA of 59.355%, and the validated factor analysis with one correction resulted in a better-fitting model (χ2/df = 2.020, RMSEA = 0.057, CFI = 0.920, TLI = 0.907). The scale is a reliable assessment tool that can be used in future clinical practice.Subject terms: Quality of life, Heart failure

Background

Chronic heart failure is the end stage of cardiovascular disease, generate a range of symptoms such as dyspnea, edema, fluid retention1, decreasing physical functioning2, and inadequate levels of social support3, which may be complicated by psychological problems such as anxiety and depression1. The growing severity of chronic heart failure in both developed and developing countries4, with a prevalence of up to 12% and a mortality rate of up to 50% within 5 years of diagnosis5. It greatly increases the economic burden of patients, has a deplorable impact on their physical and mental health, and seriously affects their quality of life2. Sungwon Chang et al. conducted a literature review of the Short Health Survey for Medical Outcomes Research (SF-36) and found that patients with advanced heart failure in clinical trials were more focused on quality of life6. Therefore, regular assessment of quality of life in patients with chronic heart failure is essential to improving their survival rates7. HRQOL is defined as a person’s functioning in daily life and well-being in terms of physical, mental, and health-related social aspects4,8. Predrag Erceg et al. found that HRQOL in patients with chronic heart failure was associated with New York Heart Association classifications, with higher classifications associated with poorer HRQOL5. According to the latest guidelines from the European Heart Association and the HFSA, improvement in HRQOL has been identified as one of the main treatment goals3. Studies have shown that patients with chronic heart failure have a poor prognosis and typically have a lower HRQOL compared to other chronic diseases4,6,7,9. Patients with chronic heart failure may experience uncomfortable symptoms such as shortness of breath, leading to decreased exercise tolerance and limiting daily activities. Difficulty breathing at night, which in turn leads to decreased sleep quality10. In addition, the HRQOL of patients with chronic heart failure can be affected by a number of factors, including psychological factors, adverse effects of medications, and the resulting high cost of treatment5. As chronic heart failure patients progress, they develop memory impairment and cognitive deficits, leading to a decline in HRQOL3. A study of heart failure patients in 40 countries found that HRQOL was a significant determinant of hospitalization and mortality rates8. HRQOL indicators for patients with chronic heart failure, assessed from the patient’s perspective, are patient-reported outcomes that can be used as prognostic indicators of daily living and as predictors of hospitalization and mortality rates11,12. Ferrans et al. developed a conceptual model of HRQOL based on Wilson and Mcleroy, which explains the impact of individual and environmental characteristics on health outcomes at multiple levels, including biological function, symptoms, and functional state13–15. A variety of instruments exist to measure quality of life in patients with heart failure, such as the 36-item Short Form Health Survey (SF-36)16, the Quality of Life Questionnaire in Severe Heart Failure (QLQ-SHF), the Chronic Heart Failure Questionnaire (CHQ)17, the Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire (KCCQ)18, the Minnesota Living With Heart Failure Questionnaire (MLHFQ)19, the Chronic Heart Failure Assessment Tool (CHAT)20, and others. These assessment tools have some limitations, such as lack of tracking of clinical changes18, small sample sizes21, validated factor analyses were not performed in some studies to assess underlying factors, and the validity of some questionnaires is questionable in large-sample settings22. Dr. Mahdi Moshki et al. developed CHFQOLQ-20 based on this model with four dimensions22. In this study, we aimed to assess the psychometric properties of a health-related quality of life questionnaire for Chinese patients with chronic heart failure. This is the first time that the CHFQOLQ-20 has been applied to chronic heart failure patients in China.

Method

The CHFQOLQ-20

The CHFQOLQ-20 was developed by Dr. Mahdi Moshki et al. in 2022, and it contains 20 items using a five-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (fully compliant) to 5 (non-compliant) for each item, with a total score ranging from 20–10022. All item scores are summed, with higher scores representing a higher quality of life for the patient. This scale assessing the four dimensions of physical functioning (items 1–10, with a Cronbach’s α of 0.939), cognitive functioning (items 11–14, with a Cronbach’s α of 0.817), general health (items 15–17, with a Cronbach’s α of 0.803) and mental health (items 18–20, Cronbach’s α of 0.748) dimensions. The original study was conducted from March 2018 to January 2019 in the outpatient department of a medical university hospital in Iran, and the study population consisted of patients with heart failure who met the criteria for attending the clinic. The initial CHFQOLQ-20 had good psychometric properties. The CHFQOLQ-20 was translated cross-culturally through Brislin’s forward translation23, backward translation, and cultural adaptation models and validated using reliability and validity. The study complied with the ethical standards of the Declaration of Helsinki. This study has been approved by the Ethics Committee under the ethical number JZMULL2023175.

Participants

In this study, convenience sampling was used to recruit study participants from chronic heart failure patients attending outpatient clinics in two tertiary care hospitals in Liaoning Province, China. Patients were initially diagnosed with chronic heart failure. The following criteria were met: (1) Understand semantics and speak fluent Chinese with clarity of expression (2) aged 18 years or older (3) NYHA functional class II to IV24. Exclusion criteria: cognitive impairment.

Data collection

Based on the need for 5–10 study participants per item, we took a larger sample. We recruited 622 participants and collected data from August 2023 through January 2024. The study was conducted by a team of researchers. Our research team members chose a small conference room next to the hospital outpatient clinic, which was quiet with only team members and participants and no other people present. We collected general information about the study participants by distributing questionnaires, and the participants wrote the information on the questionnaires by themselves, due to the fact that some of the participants were unable to write due to hand injuries or other reasons, and the team members filled in the questionnaires on their behalf through the participant’s descriptions. If the participants had questions or did not understand the content of the questionnaire, the group members answered them on the spot. Two weeks later, telephone interviews were conducted, and 40 study participants who had answered the questionnaire and indicated their willingness to participate were randomly selected to answer again for retest reliability analysis25.

Scale translation process

Our translation was done with the permission of Prof. Mahdi Moshki. First, the CHFQOLQ-20 scale was translated into Chinese by two native Chinese-speaking graduate students, one of whom had a medical background and the other of whom was very familiar with colloquial phrases, idioms, etc. in the source language. The translations were then back-translated by two native English-speaking tutors, neither of whom had a medical background. In addition, two psychology professors were asked to discuss and adjust phrases that did not fit the national expression in order to finalize the final version of CHFQOLQ-20. Neither of the two foreign professors knew the content of the original scale. The professors thought that “gardening” in item 3 needed to be revised because most Chinese people do not have their own gardens. Finally, 10 eligible patients with chronic heart failure were selected for the pretest26. The pretest was conducted in a quiet classroom. The researcher introduced the purpose and significance of the survey to them and encouraged them to express their understanding of the scale items, and all 10 patients reported that the scale structure was simple and easy to understand26,27. Based on the participants’ comments, the researcher modified the statements and expressions of the scale items and combined them with experts’ opinions to form the CHFQOLQ-20, with the following suggestions for modification: Change item 3, “Did you experience difficulty in doing household chores (such as gardening, moving things, vacuum cleaning, or daily grocery shopping)?” and replace with “Did you experience difficulty in doing household chores (such as moving things, vacuum cleaning, or daily grocery shopping)?”. Measurements

Sample

We conducted a literature search and designed our own questionnaire on general demographic characteristics: marital status, gender, education, age, payment for health care, average monthly income, work status, cardiac function classification, and comorbid underlying diseases.

Data analysis

We used Excel software to enter the data. We statistically analyzed the data using SPSS version 25.0 and AMOS 23.0.

We analyzed the items of the CHFQOLQ-20 by two methods: ① Critical ratio method: after arranging the total scores of the items of each questionnaire of the CHFQOLQ-20 in ascending order, the 27% grouping method was adopted, and the samples with the total scores of the resulting scales in the top 27% were selected as the high grouping, and those with the total scores of the bottom 27% were selected as the low grouping, and the independent samples t-tests were used for the high grouping and the low grouping, and deletion was considered items with P > 0.0528–30.

②Item-total correlation: For testing the differentiation of the scale, the items that did not reach a significant correlation were eliminated by analyzing the correlation coefficient between the scores of the items and the total score. Generally, if the items show a significant correlation with the total score and the coefficient is greater than 0.4, it indicates high homogeneity; if the criterion is not reached, the removal of the items is considered31.

Validity analysis

We analyzed the CHFQOLQ-20 for content and structural validity.

Content validity: the representativeness of the content of the test or its relevance to the topic. Content validity is usually evaluated using content validity indices (CVI), including item content validity (I-CVI) and scale content validity (S-CVI). We invited seven experts (two cardiologists, two psychologists, one director of nursing, one English teacher, and one advanced practice nurse) to organize the study using a four-point Likert scale (1 = irrelevant, indicating that the item is more difficult to comprehend; 2 = weakly relevant, needing greater modification; 3 = strong relevance/expression slightly clearer, comprehensible with a little modification; and 4 = clearly expressed). “1” and ‘2’ are scored as 0, and ‘3’ and ‘4’ are scored as 1. The I-CVI consists of each item. I-CVI is calculated by dividing the number of experts giving a score of “3 or 4” by the total number of experts. And S-CVI is calculated by averaging all I-CVIs32. Construct validity: the degree of correspondence between a certain structure embodied in the measurement results and the measurement values. We randomly divided the data from 622 cases into two equal parts (N = 311), which were used to perform exploratory factor analysis (EFA) and confirmatory factor analysis (CFA), respectively. Exploratory factor analysis: Data dimensions have not been differentiated, and SPSS 25.0 was used to aggregate the many variables into a few shared common factors. Confirmatory Factor Analysis: the data dimensions have been distinguished, and AMOS 23.0 was used to verify whether the data conformed to the categorized dimensions, to test the validity of the theoretical model, and to check whether the model needs to be improved. CFA was performed by principal component analysis and maximum variance orthogonal rotation. The Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin (KMO) value ≥ 0.8 and Bartlett’s spherical test (P < 0.05) were used to determine whether EFA was performed, and if exploratory factor analysis was satisfied, the number of factors is determined using the two methods described above33,34. The higher loading value of the items on one factor and the lower loading value on other factors was used as the factor imputation criterion, and the items with factor loading < 0.4 were deleted. If the number of factors was consistent with the original scale and the cumulative variance contribution > 50% was statistically significant. Validation factor analysis was performed by applying the great likelihood method to verify that the exploratory factor analysis model matched the data. The chi-square/degree of freedom (χ2/df), root mean square error of approximation (RMSEA), goodness of fit index (GFI), comparative fit index (CFI), incremental fit index (IFI), and tucker lewis index (TLI) were performed to evaluate the model as a chaperone. If χ2/df < 3, RMSEA < 0.08, GFI > 0.9, CFI > 0.9, IFI > 0.9, and TLI > 0.9, the model is well fitted35–37. Figure 2 presents the specific process.

Internal consistency analysis

We validated this through three indicators. ① Cronbach’s α: a common indicator of a scale used to assess whether a set of questions consistently measures the same concept38.The closer its value is to one, the more reliable it is. ② Split - half reliability: the items of a scale are divided into two parts according to parity, and the consistency of these two parts is compared39. ③ Retest reliability: retest reliability refers to the stability and consistency of a research instrument’s test results across time. In our study, a total of 40 subjects were selected to undergo two measurements successively (who met the inclusion and exclusion criteria), with a measurement interval of 2 weeks, and then the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) was calculated for the scores obtained from the two measurements. The value of the ICC ranges between 0-1, and the closer to 1, the better the stability of the research instrument. The higher the ICC, the better the stability of the instrument, and ICC > 0.6 is the minimum acceptable value40.

Discussions

This study reports on the reliability and validity of the Chinese version of the CHFQOLQ-20 by examining its psychometric characteristics. The main findings of the study are described below. The results of the study showed that the CHFQOLQ-20 scale was sufficiently reliable and valid, and none of the 20 items of the questionnaire were omitted. Content validity of the scale showed that the I-CVI of the scale was found to be between 0.857 and 1.000, and the S-CVI was 0.950, which were higher than the reference values. The results were satisfactory. In this study, EFA produced a four-factor structure containing 20 items. This factor structure was consistent with the structure found in the original version. EFA showed that four factors explained 59.355% of the total variance, and the factor loadings of the items ranged between 0.503 and 0.834. Mahdi Moshki and Abdoljavad Khajavi reported that the results of the EFA showed that item 9 “Did you feel fatigue and low energy level?” had a factor loading value of 0.446, and the rest of the factor loadings were above 0.522. CFA was performed on the other half of the sample to test the validity of the structure obtained after EFA. Based on the goodness-of-fit indices obtained in this study, the four-factor structure of the 20 items was confirmed, and we therefore conclude that the factor structure behind the Chinese version of the CHFQOLQ-20 is applicable to Chinese patients with chronic heart failure.The CFA showed significant chi-square values (χ2/df = 2.020 < 3.000); it should be noted that the GFI and the AGFI were greater than or equal to 0.90. The chi-square to degrees of freedom (chi-square over degrees of freedom in English) is less than or equal to 3. The RMSEA index value is sub 0.057 < 0.08 (95% CI: 0.048 ~ 0.066). The initial model was corrected once according to the correction indicators (MI): e3 and e4. GFI = 0.906 > 0.900, TLI = 0.907 > 0.900, CFI = 0.920 > 0.900, IFI = 0.921 > 0.900, PGFI = 0.703 > 0.500, PNFI = 0.734 > 0.50041. The Chinese version of the CHFQOLQ-20 has satisfactory reliability: the Cronbach’s alpha for the four domains of the scale was found to range from 0.722 to 0.833. The Cronbach’s alpha for the whole scale was found to be 0.908. In addition, the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) for the whole scale was found to be satisfactory40. The scale permits the assessment of health-related quality of life in patients with chronic heart failure from a professional’s perspective in order to identify patient difficulties and areas for improvement. The nurse can contact the clinician or rehabilitation therapist in a timely manner based on the patient’s responses to the questionnaire. For example, the nurse can inform the patient who scores low in the mental health domain to his/her attending physician, who can contact the counselor for psychological counseling for the patient. In addition, if a patient scores low on the physical functioning domain, the clinician can reassess the patient’s physical condition and, if necessary, work with the rehabilitation therapist to develop a rehabilitation program to improve the patient’s quality of life. The results showed that the values of internal consistency, construct validity (validated factor analysis) and temporal stability (test-retest) were adequate at the psychometric level.

Limitations and prospects

There are some limitations in this study that deserve our deep thoughts and discussions. First, we only collected data from two tertiary-level hospitals in Liaoning Province, China, without considering the differences in geographic regions, environments, and ethnic and cultural practices, and community hospitals and rural clinics were not taken into account. It is possible that the sample is not representative of the Chinese chronic heart failure patient population, and the generalizability of the results of this study may be threatened. Second, we selected only outpatients and did not consider hospitalized patients. This may limit the generalization of the findings to a wider patient population. Finally, when conducting the general information questionnaire, patients relied mainly on self-report, which is susceptible to subjective factors of the subjects and may have recall bias, which in turn may affect the results of the questionnaire.The CHFQOLQ-20 scale was translated from English to Chinese rather than from the original Persian, which is a limitation of this study. In the next step, we will conduct questionnaire distribution to a large sample of patients in large, medium, and small cities across the country, taking hospitalized patients into account, and take follow-up surveys, etc. Future research on the CHFQOLQ-20 to measure the health-related quality of life of Chinese patients with chronic heart failure should include a full translation from Persian to Chinese, which is the focus of our next study.

Conclude

This paper contributes to a better understanding of the psychometric properties of the CHFQOLQ-20 in a sample of Chinese patients with chronic heart failure. Our findings confirm that this version of the CHFQOLQ-20 is a validated and reliable instrument that excels at measuring a range of health-related quality of life in chronic heart failure patients. We also believe that this instrument provides a foundation and prerequisite for physicians, nurses, and rehabilitation personnel to propose specific interventions.

Left-sided mastectomy mimics LVEF decline on CZT-ERNA: a consideration for cardio-oncology practice
Authors: Maiken Engel Fiala, Lars Thorbjørn Jensen, Bo Zerahn, Marc Østergaard Nielsen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMonitoring of left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) is essential in patients receiving potentially cardiotoxic cancer therapies. Equilibrium radionuclide angiography (ERNA) remains an established method for quantitative assessment of LVEF. However, the absence of attenuation correction may render the method sensitive to anatomical changes, such as those caused by mastectomy. The aim with this study was to investigate whether left-sided mastectomy systematically affects the estimation of LVEF using cadmium-zinc-telluride (CZT)-based ERNA.MethodsThis retrospective single-centre cohort study included breast cancer patients who had undergone unilateral or bilateral mastectomy and had both pre- and postoperative CZT-ERNA performed under identical acquisition protocols. Pre- to postoperative changes in LVEF, left ventricular end-diastolic volume (LVEDV), and left ventricular end-systolic volume (LVESV) were compared between left- and right-sided mastectomy groups.ResultsBetween-group comparison showed that left-sided mastectomy (including bilateral) was associated with a significant decrease in LVEF (mean change -4.0 percentage points, 95% CI -6.0 to -2.0, p = 0.011). In contrast, no significant within-group change was observed for right-sided mastectomy (mean change 0.6 percentage points, 95% CI -2.4 to 3.6, p = 0.7). One in four patients (25%) in the left-sided group experienced a decline in LVEF of at least 10 percentage points, compared with 1 in 17 in the right-sided group. Multivariable regression analysis indicated that LVEF decline was independently associated with mastectomy laterality, whereas cumulative anthracycline dose and radiotherapy were non-significant.ConclusionLeft-sided mastectomy is associated with a significant decrease in LVEF measured by CZT-ERNA. This finding likely reflects improved delineation of LVESV rather than true systolic decline. Recent chest surgery and individual anatomical changes should be considered when interpreting serial CZT-ERNA examinations. Introduction

Assessment of left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) is an essential part of cardiac monitoring in patients receiving potentially cardiotoxic cancer therapy. According to the latest guidelines from the European Society of Cardiology (ESC), serial assessment of LVEF before, during, and after cardiotoxic treatment is recommended to detect asymptomatic cancer therapy-related cardiac dysfunction (CTRCD) [1].

Several imaging modalities can quantify LVEF, including echocardiography, nuclear medicine techniques, and cardiac magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) [2]. Cardiac MRI provides the highest accuracy for LVEF measurement among these methods, however, its limited availability and higher cost, restricts its use for serial examinations. Echocardiography has become the preferred modality due to its wide accessibility, absence of ionizing radiation and extensive evidence base [3]. Historically, equilibrium radionuclide angiography (ERNA), also known as multigated acquisition imaging (MUGA), has been widely used for serial LVEF assessment due to its high reproducibility and low inter-observer variability [4–6]. While ERNA is used less frequently today due to advancements in echocardiography, it remains a key alternative in oncology when high reproducibility is needed or when echocardiographic windows are suboptimal [1]. This is particularly relevant for breast cancer patients, whose acoustic windows are often compromised by postoperative cicatrices, edema, and implants [3]. The subsequent development of lung fibrosis and scar tissue also makes reliable alternatives essential.

The introduction of cadmium-zinc-telluride (CZT) detectors has enhanced ERNA by improving the sensitivity and enabling shorter acquisition times with lower administered activity without compromising image quality [7–9]. A persistent limitation, however, is the lack of attenuation correction, which makes the technique sensitive to patient anatomy. Gamma photon attenuation by overlying soft tissue can affect count density [10] and thus the estimation of ventricular volumes and LVEF [11].

Following mastectomy, thoracic anatomy changes substantially. These anatomical changes may introduce systematic differences in measured LVEF, potentially leading to false indications of cardiotoxicity or, conversely, mask true dysfunction. Despite its clinical relevance, this issue has not been systematically examined.

We formally tested the null hypothesis that mastectomy, regardless of laterality, would not result in changes to LVEF or ventricular volumes.

Our alternative hypothesis was that left-sided (including bilateral) mastectomy would result in a systematic apparent reduction in LVEF measured by CZT-ERNA. This effect was expected due to improved delineation of the end-systolic volume and a corresponding increase in measured left vetricular end-systolic volume (LVESV). Conversely, right-sided mastectomy was expected to show no effect on cardiac photon detection, thereby serving as a control condition.

Materials and methods

Study design and population

This retrospective single-centre cohort study was conducted at the Department of Nuclear Medicine, Herlev Hospital, Denmark. The study was approved by the Team for Journal Data, Center for Health, Capital Region of Denmark (reference no. R-25031369). The board granted an exemption from the requirement to obtain informed consent in accordance with Sect. 46(2) of the Danish Health Act.

Patients were identified through the IDS7 (Sectra, Linköping, Sweden) archive system by searching for CZT-ERNA examinations performed between October 1, 2012, and May 1, 2025. Patient records were reviewed to identify women who had undergone unilateral or bilateral mastectomy as part of their breast cancer treatment. Lumpectomies were not included in this study. Inclusion criteria were: 1) women diagnosed with breast cancer treated at Herlev Hospital, 2) have had a left-sided, right-sided or bilateral mastectomy, and 3) at least one preoperative and one postoperative CZT-ERNA performed with identical protocols. To ensure the study prioritized acute postoperative anatomical changes over long-term physiological variations, we excluded one patient with an extreme scan interval of 916 days.

Demographic and clinical data (age, BMI, comorbidities, blood pressure, heart rate) were recorded at the time of the preoperative scan. Oncologic treatments including cardiotoxic agents and radiotherapy were noted at the postoperative scan. The presence of reconstructive implants was recorded.

Image acquisition and analysis

CZT-ERNA was performed using a cadmium-zinc-telluride (CZT) gamma camera system (GE Discovery 530c; GE Healthcare, Milwaukee, WI, USA). An individualized dose of 99mTc-labeled human serum albumin (HSA) was administered intravenously according to the method described previously [7]. Patients were positioned supine with both arms extended above the head. Data acquisition was ECG-gated with 16 frames per cardiac cycle, 600 accepted beats were acquired using a 20% energy window centered on 140 keV. Data were reconstructed and analysed using the Xeleris 3 Imaging workstation reorientation software (GE Healthcare, Milwaukee, WI, USA, version 3.0562) and Cedars-Sinai QBS processing software (Cedars-Sinai, Los Angeles, CA, USA, revision 2009.0). CT-based attenuation correction was not available. Previous studies on this setup detailing inter- and intraobserver variability and reproducibility are available [9, 12].

All acquisitions were analysed by two independent blinded technologists. Discrepancies of > 2 percentage points in LVEF were reviewed by a senior nuclear medicine physician. Recorded parameters included left ventricular end-diastolic volume (LVEDV), LVESV, and LVEF.

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as mean [95% confidence interval, 95%CI], median (range) or median (interquartile range, IQR) as appropriate. The normality of continuous variables (ΔLVEF, ΔLVEDV, and ΔLVESV) was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test and visual inspection of histograms. The variables were approximately normally distributed overall, with minor deviations for ΔLVEDV in the right-sided group considered acceptable for parametric testing. Homogeneity of variance was confirmed using Levene’s test.

The primary analysis compared the mean change in LVEF between the left-sided group (including bilateral cases) and the right-sided mastectomy group. This between-group comparison was assessed using independent-sample t-tests with Welch’s correction. Effect sizes were reported as Cohen’s d.

All other analyses were considered exploratory or sensitivity-based. Within-group comparisons of pre- and postoperative values were performed using paired t-tests. Sensitivity analyses excluded patients with reconstructive breast implants to account for potential attenuation due to prosthetic material.

Finally, multivariable linear regression was used to evaluate the change in LVEF between pre- and postoperative CZT-ERNA. The models were adjusted for mastectomy laterality, cumulative epirubicin exposure, radiotherapy, preoperative LVEF, age, body mass index, scan interval, and implant status. To assess the robustness of our findings, alternative model specifications were tested. This included modelling mastectomy laterality as a three-level variable (left, right, bilateral) opposed to the binary grouping (left/bilateral versus right), as well as defining radiotherapy exposure based on either the start or end date relative to the postoperative scan.

All tests were two-tailed, and a p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were performed using R (version 4.2.2; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) with the tidyverse and rstatix packages.

Results

A total of 94 patients were included, representing 94 CZT-ERNA scan pairs: 60 with left-sided (including bilateral) and 34 with right-sided mastectomy.

Baseline characteristics were comparable between groups (Table 1). Median age was 52 years (IQR 44–65), and median BMI was 25 kg/m2 (IQR 22–29). The median interval between pre- and postoperative CZT-ERNA scans was 91 days (IQR 82–104), and between mastectomy and postoperative imaging 68 days (IQR 37–76). Radiotherapy was more frequently administered after right-sided mastectomy (58.8%) than after left-sided (28.3%), although radiation dose was similar between groups (median 40 Gy).

Oncological treatment

Neoadjuvant treatment consisted of a combination of chemotherapy and HER2-targeted therapy. Chemotherapy included paclitaxel followed by epirubicin and cyclophosphamide (n = 85). HER2-targeted therapy was administered as either fixed-dose combination pertuzumab/trastuzumab (Phesgo) (n = 86) or trastuzumab alone (n = 8). For the epirubicin component (n = 87), 46 patients (53%) were treated according to a dose-dense protocol. The distribution of neoadjuvant treatment regimens did not differ according to mastectomy laterality (all p > 0.25, Fisher’s exact test).

In the adjuvant setting, patients received HER2-targeted therapy with either trastuzumab (n = 70, 74.5%) or trastuzumab emtansine (n = 22, 23.4%). A small number of patients received paclitaxel in combination with trastuzumab (n = 2, 2.1%).

In the left-sided (including bilateral) mastectomy group, adjuvant therapy consisted of trastuzumab (75.0%) and trastuzumab emtansine (25.0%). In the right-sided mastectomy group, treatment consisted of trastuzumab (73.5%), trastuzumab emtansine (20.6%), and paclitaxel in combination with trastuzumab (5.9%). There was no significant difference in adjuvant treatment according to mastectomy laterality (Fisher’s exact test, p = 0.16).

Seven patients did not receive epirubicin. Among the remaining patients, the median time from the baseline ERNA scan to the final dose of epirubicin was 14 days (25–75%: 5–77 days). The median interval between this final epirubicin dose and the follow-up scan was 112 days (25–75%: 97–163 days).

Between-group comparison

The mean decline in LVEF was significantly greater following left-sided (including bilateral) mastectomy than after right-sided procedures (−4.0 vs. 0.6 percentage points; mean difference −4.6 [95% CI −8.1 to −1.1]; p = 0.011), corresponding to a moderate effect size (Cohen’s d −0.57). Similarly, LVESV increased more in the left-sided group (4.3 vs. −0.5 mL; mean difference 4.8 [95% CI 0.9 to 8.6]; p = 0.016; d 0.54, moderate). Changes in LVEDV did not differ significantly between groups (mean difference 2.3 mL [95% CI −3.6 to 8.2]; p = 0.433; d 0.17, negligible).

Within-group comparisons

Among patients undergoing left-sided (including bilateral) mastectomy, a significant postoperative decrease in LVEF was observed. Median LVEF decreased from 65.5% (IQR 61.0–71.0) preoperatively to 60.5% (IQR 57.0–66.2) postoperatively, corresponding to a mean change of −4.0 percentage points [95%CI—6.0 to −2.0] (p < 0.001) (Table 2). This reduction was accompanied by a significant increase in LVESV (4.3 mL; p < 0.001), whereas LVEDV remained unchanged (p = 0.3) (Table 2). Individual changes in LVEF are illustrated in the waterfall plot in Fig. 1, where 25% of patients in the left-sided (including bilateral) mastectomy group experienced a decline of ≥ 10 percentage points.

Clinical implications

Two patients undergoing left-sided mastectomy had a postoperative LVEF below 50%, leading to temporary interruption of trastuzumab therapy. The first patient experienced a decline of ≥ 10 percentage points. A follow up CZT-ERNA performed three weeks later showed a normalization of LVEF, and trastuzumab was subsequently reinitiated. The second patient had a decline of 4 percentage points. A subacute CZT-ERNA performed two weeks later showed persistently reduced LVEF. The patient was subsequently referred to the cardiology department, where normal LVEF was confirmed on transthoracic echocardiography (TTE). Trastuzumab was reinitiated after a total interruption of six weeks.

An additional 16 patients experienced a decline in LVEF of ≥ 10 percentage points but remained above 50%. This group included 11 left-sided, 2 right-sided, and 3 bilateral mastectomy patients. Of these, 14 underwent additional follow up imaging due to the observed decline. This included all patients with right-sided and bilateral mastectomy, along with 9 patients with left-sided mastectomy. No patients discontinued therapy permanently.

Multivariable regression

In the primary multivariable regression model, combined left-sided and bilateral mastectomy was significantly associated with a greater decline in LVEF compared to a right-sided mastectomy (estimate −4.15, p = 0.024). A higher preoperative LVEF was also significantly associated with a greater decline in LVEF (estimate −0.36, p < 0.001). The remaining covariates were not significantly associated with change in LVEF: radiotherapy completed before postoperative scan (estimate 2.21, p = 0.241), cumulative epirubicin dose (estimate −0.012, p = 0.129), implant status (estimate 1.44, p = 0.529), age (estimate 0.03, p = 0.637), body mass index (estimate 0.16, p = 0.387), and scan interval (estimate −0.004, p = 0.913).

When the model was modified to use the radiotherapy start date instead of the end date, the radiotherapy estimate changed to 1.43 (p = 0.421), while the combined left and bilateral mastectomy group remained a significant predictor (estimate −4.31, p = 0.020). When mastectomy laterality was analysed as three separate categories, left-sided mastectomy remained significantly associated with LVEF decline (estimate −4.21, p = 0.025), whereas bilateral mastectomy showed a similar estimate but was not statistically significant (estimate −3.75, p = 0.187). This pattern remained consistent when using radiotherapy start date, yielding similar estimates and p-values.

Discussion

Our findings demonstrate a significant reduction in LVEF following left-sided mastectomy, whereas no such change was observed after right-sided surgery. These results support the hypothesis that removal of breast tissue overlying the heart alters photon attenuation, primarily affecting LVESV measurements and thereby influencing LVEF as measured by CZT-ERNA. All exploratory analyses, including the within-group comparisons, yielded consistent results. The multivariable regression analysis was particularly revealing; when adjusting for various known clinical factors, only mastectomy laterality and baseline LVEF remained significant predictors of LVEF decline. Despite an imbalance in radiotherapy exposure within the cohort, radiotherapy was not significantly associated with LVEF change and did not demonstrate the expected direction of effect, even when restricted to patients in whom treatment was completed before the postoperative scan. This lack of acute effect may be explained by modern heart-sparing techniques, such as deep-inspiration breath-hold [13], as well as the fact that radiotherapy-induced cardiac injury typically manifests as long-term fibrosis or coronary atherosclerosis years later, rather than as immediate systolic dysfunction [14]. Similarly, although cumulative epirubicin exposure showed the expected direction of effect, it did not reach significance, likely because no patients approached the cumulative dose threshold of 900 mg/m2.

Although the mean decrease in LVEF was modest (−4.0 percentage points), the wide range of −23 to 13 indicates that some patients experienced changes large enough to meet ESC criteria for CTRCD (≥ 10-point decline to < 50%) [1]. These apparent declines in LVEF may often lead to unnecessary clinical actions. In this cohort, two patients experienced treatment interruptions, including one who waited six weeks for an echocardiographic evaluation by a cardiology consultant to confirm normal cardiac function. The high number of follow-up examinations further highlight the practical burden associated with such measurement variability. Notably, 12 out of 14 patients referred to expedited imaging had undergone left-sided or bilateral mastectomy. These additional examinations lead to increased healthcare costs, greater radiation exposure, and avoidable stress for patients. Ultimately, the technical limitations of gated blood pool methods without attenuation correction following left-sided surgery might adversely affect both clinical workflow and the patient experience.

The influence of mastectomy on ERNA-derived LVEF has to our knowledge, not previously been systematically reported, however, the attenuation effects of breast tissue are well documented in myocardial perfusion imaging, often causing artefactual perfusion defects or LVEF overestimation [15]. In fact, this clinical challenge drove the adoption of attenuation correction in nuclear cardiology, spearheaded by Hendel, DePuey and others [16–19]. Later, CT-based attenuation maps were established as the clinical standard of care by Masood and Wackers, enabling widespread clinical adoption [20].

The same principles of attenuation apply to CZT-ERNA, as supported by previous studies from our department using the same camera system. A conference abstract by Hansen et al. [21] showed that increased cardiac depth measured on CT significantly reduces the observed count rate. Additionally, Nguyen et al. [22] reported how lower count rates lead to decreased left ventricular volumes and an artificially elevated LVEF. Although the evidentiary weight of these conference abstracts is limited, they nevertheless provide a mechanistic explanation whereby increased attenuation leads to an overestimation of LVEF.

In theory, attenuation is a relative factor that should affect LVEDV and LVESV equally. However, our findings suggest otherwise. We believe this discrepancy reflects the use of volumetric rather than count-based analysis in our software, in contrast to planar ERNA. This volumetric approach, which is the recommended method for SPECT-ERNA regardless of detector type [23], may be particularly susceptible to reduced count density during end-systole. Whereas the left ventricle is relatively stationary at end-diastole, the rapid contraction and small cavity size at end-systole – the most technically challenging phase of the cardiac cycle – may impair accurate delineation of ventricular borders.

These findings may therefore be generalizable to other platforms, particularly SPECT-ERNA. Although count-based methods should theoretically leave LVEF unaffected, non-uniform attenuation from breast tissue likely introduces additional complexity in practice. Uneven attenuation may differentially affect the ventricular cavity and background regions, thereby complicating the background subtraction process required for all count-based ERNA techniques. However, this hypothesis warrants further investigation.

Overall, postoperative decreases in LVEF after left-sided mastectomy, measured by CZT-ERNA, should be interpreted with caution. Confirmatory imaging with echocardiography or cardiac MRI may be warranted in cases of unexpected decline. However, this introduces a clinical paradox, as patients most susceptible to attenuation-related artefacts on CZT-ERNA are often those in whom echocardiographic assessment is most technically challenging. While contrast-enhanced echocardiography may improve image quality, three-dimensional echocardiography is often limited by poor acoustic windows. Consequently, cardiac MRI may be preferable for accurate assessment, although limited availability remains a constraint. In such cases, 82Rubidium-PET may represent a valid alternative for the evaluation of ventricular function [24].

This study has several limitations. First, its retrospective design may introduce selection bias and limits any definite conclusions on causality. Second, despite efforts to ensure consistent acquisition parameters, minor variations in patient positioning or gating could have affected the results. Third, we accounted for the dose-dependent cardiotoxicity of anthracyclines [25–27], by including cumulative epirubicin dose in the multivariable regression model. However, treatment timing was not included, as only the date of the final epirubicin administration was available. Furthermore, HER2-targeted therapy is known to cause reversible functional impairment [26, 28–31]. The timing of this treatment may have influenced the postoperative ERNA measurements, which are often performed after the initiation of adjuvant therapy. Fourth, postoperative factors such as radiation fibrosis, scar tissue or complications including seroma were not systematically evaluated. Finally, external validation using other imaging modalities such as echocardiography or cardiac MRI was not available.

Prospective multimodality studies comparing attenuation-corrected and non-attenuation-corrected ERNA with echocardiography or MRI are needed to clarify whether the observed changes represent technical artefacts or true physiological differences in left ventricular function. Alternatively, phantom studies, preferably using dynamic cardiac phantoms under identical settings with increasing attenuation, could help confirm this phenomenon.

Conclusion

Left-sided mastectomy is associated with a moderate and systematic decrease in LVEF measured by CZT-ERNA, while right-sided mastectomy does not appear to affect LVEF estimation. Since most conventional ERNA techniques, including SPECT-ERNA, lack attenuation correction, these findings may have broader implications for cardiac monitoring in patients with breast cancer. Clinicians should therefore consider confirmatory imaging using alternative modalities in cases of unexpected LVEF declines in this patient population. Further prospective studies are needed to confirm these findings.

What is known/what this study adds

Attenuation effects of breast tissue are well known in myocardial perfusion imaging, but the impact on cardiac functional assessment with equilibrium radionuclide angiography has not been systematically evaluated. This is particularly relevant for newer camera systems equipped with cadmium-zinc-telluride (CZT) detectors. This retrospective cohort study provides evidence that breast attenuation influences left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) estimation measured by CZT-ERNA, demonstrated by comparing pre- and post-mastectomy measurements in patients with left-sided (including bilateral) and right sided mastectomy.

An Embedding Theorem for Tractor Bundles, and an Application in Conformal Pseudo-Riemannian Geometry
Karin MELNICK a and Katharina NEUSSER b

Abstract
We provide an extension of the Gromov–Zimmer embedding theorem for Cartan geometries of [Bader U., Frances C., Melnick K., Geom. Funct. Anal. 19 (2009), 333–355, arXiv:0709.3844] to tractor bundles carrying any invariant connection, including tractor connections and prolongation connections of first BGG operators for parabolic geometries. As an application, we prove a rigidity result for conformal actions of special pseudo-unitary groups on closed, simply connected, analytic pseudo-Riemannian manifolds.

Cartan geometries; parabolic geometries; conformal manifolds; automorphisms of geometric structures; pseudo-Riemannian geometry; simple Lie transformation groups

Introduction

A Cartan geometry is a manifold infinitesimally modeled on a
homogeneous space
G/P
, where
G
is a Lie group and
P
is a closed
subgroup. It comprises a principal
P
-bundle over the manifold,
equipped with a
Cartan connection
taking values in
\mathfrak{g}
; see Definition
2.1
below. Essentially all classical
rigid differential-geometric structures correspond canonically to
Cartan geometries for a suitable homogeneous model, in such a way that the
automorphisms of the structure are the same as the automorphisms of
the Cartan geometry.

Our results concern Cartan geometries, particularly parabolic geometries, having large
automorphism group. This property typically implies further special properties of the geometry, for example,
the existence of solutions to natural overdetermined geometric PDEs. Conversely,
when there are many solutions to such PDEs, one may expect a large automorphism group. Either property implies restrictions on the curvature. The results of this paper are intended to contribute to the concrete understanding of this interplay.

1.1
Parabolic geometries and tractor bundles

A
parabolic geometry
is a Cartan geometry infinitesimally modeled
on a homogeneous space
G/P
, where
G
is a semisimple Lie group and

P<G
a parabolic subgroup. This rich family of geometries includes
conformal and projective structures, along with many others. In general, a parabolic
geometry on a manifold
M
corresponds to a filtration of the tangent
bundle,
TM
, with some additional geometric structures on the
subquotients of the filtration.

Tractor bundles are vector bundles
\mathcal{V}=\hat{M}\times_{\rho}{\bf V}
associated to the Cartan bundle

\hat{M}
, for
G
-representations
\rho
restricted to
P
, and equipped
with certain geometrically significant, automorphism-invariant connections.
On the one hand, any tractor bundle is equipped with the tractor connection
\nabla^{\omega}
, induced by the Cartan connection
\omega
,
and on the other hand with the prolongation connection
\nabla^{\mathcal{V}}
of the
first BGG-operator
corresponding to
\mathcal{V}
, which
in general differs from
\nabla^{\omega}
by curvature terms; see Section
2.1.3
for details. Parallel sections for these connections correspond to solutions of overdetermined
geometric PDEs
on the manifold; they form a finite-dimensional vector space.

The conformal class of a metric of signature
(p,q)
on a manifold

M
, with
p+q=\mbox{dim }M\geq 3
, corresponds to a Cartan geometry
modeled on
G/P
for
G=\mbox{PO}(p+1,q+1)
and
P
the maximal
parabolic subgroup stabilizing an isotropic line in
{\bf R}^{p+1,q+1}
.
The homogeneous space
G/P
is called the
Möbius space
of
type
(p,q)
, denoted
\mbox{\bf M\"{o}b}^{p,q}
, and is diffeomorphic to a two-fold quotient of
{\bf S}^{p}\times{\bf S}^{q}
. It carries a
conformally
G
-invariant, conformally flat metric of signature
(p,q)
. The Cartan
bundle is a principal
P
-subbundle of the second-order frame bundle
of
M
. The Cartan connection
\omega
has been a valuable tool
in recent progress on rigidity of conformal transformations of
semi-Riemannian manifolds (see, for example,
[
26
]
for an
overview). Vanishing of the Cartan curvature of this connection over an
open subset
U\subseteq M
is equivalent to conformal flatness of the
metric on
U
.

A semi-Riemannian metric
g
on
M^{n}
,
n\geq 3
, is
Einstein

if
\operatorname{Ric}g=\frac{S}{2}g
, where
\operatorname{Ric}
is the Ricci
curvature tensor and
S
is the scalar curvature.
It is
conformally almost-Einstein
if there exists a
smooth function
\sigma
, not constant
0
, and a smooth function
\nu
such that

\nabla^{2}\sigma+\sigma\cdot P=\nu\cdot g,

(1.1)

where
\nabla^{2}
is the Hessian, and

P=\frac{1}{n-2}\left(\operatorname{Ric}-\frac{S}{2(n-1)}\cdot g\right)

is the
Schouten tensor
; in other words,
\nabla^{2}\sigma+\sigma\cdot P
is pure trace. When
\sigma
is nonvanishing,
then
{\sigma^{-2}\cdot g}
is an Einstein metric in the conformal class of
g

(see, e.g.,
[
14
, Proposition 3.6]
).

Suppose
\hat{M}\rightarrow M
is the Cartan bundle of a conformal
semi-Riemannian structure. Let
{\bf V}={\bf R}^{p+1,q+1}

and
\rho
the standard representation of
{\rm O}(p+1,q+1)
. The

(standard) conformal tractor bundle
of such a structure is the associated
vector bundle
\mathcal{V}=\hat{M}\times_{\rho}{\bf V}
and it is equipped with the
\omega
-induced tractor connection
\nabla^{\omega}
.
Sections of

\mathcal{V}
yield, via projection to a certain line bundle
and a choice of metric in the conformal class,
functions on
M
; functions coming from parallel sections for

\nabla^{\mathcal{\omega}}
correspond to
solutions of the conformal almost-Einstein equation. In this case, the
tractor connection equals the prolongation connection
\nabla^{\mathcal{V}}
of the first BGG operator.
See Section
2.1.5
below, and the references
[
9
,
14
]
for more details.

There are many other significant geometric PDEs on conformal manifolds, and more generally on manifolds equipped with parabolic geometries, whose solutions correspond under prolongation
to parallel sections of the prolongation connection of a first BGG-operator; on conformal manifolds this applies for instance also to the equations for conformal Killing forms
[
20
,
35
]
and for twistor spinors
[
5
]
, and for projective and
c-projective structures to the equation controlling the metrizability of these structures
[
7
,
17
]
, among many others.

Infinitesimal automorphisms of geometric structures underlying Cartan geometries also belong to the framework of parallel
sections of natural connections on tractor bundles. Let
\bigl(M,\hat{M},\omega\bigr)
be any Cartan geometry modeled on

(\mathfrak{g},P)
. The
adjoint tractor bundle
is the associated vector
bundle
\mathcal{A}
for
{\bf V}=\mathfrak{g}
with the adjoint
representation. A section of
\mathcal{A}
gives rise via the Cartan
connection to a vector field on
\hat{M}
which is invariant under the
right-
P
-action, and thus descends to a vector field on
M
.
Čap observed that for a certain connection on
\mathcal{A}
,
again related to an intrinsically defined differential operator on
M
, parallel sections correspond to
infinitesimal automorphisms of the Cartan geometry – that is, vector
fields for which the flow preserves the Cartan connection
[
8
]
. These
descend to vector fields on
M
for which the flow preserves the
geometric structure encoded by the Cartan geometry, called

infinitesimal automorphisms
or
Killing fields
of the structure.

This article concerns a
generalization of the Gromov–Zimmer embedding theorem, which originally pertains to
infinitesimal automorphisms, to general parallel sections for automorphism-invariant connections of tractor bundles.
We briefly recall the embedding theorem for infinitesimal automorphisms.

1.2
Gromov–Zimmer embedding theorem

Zimmer’s embedding theorem first appeared in
[
41
]
in the context of a noncompact simple Lie group
H
acting by automorphisms of a
G
-structure
\omega
of algebraic type on a compact manifold
M^{n}
, such that
G
also defines a volume form. In this context, the theorem gives a linear injection
\iota\colon\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow{\bf R}^{n}
and an algebraic group
\check{H}<G
locally isomorphic to
H
such that
\iota
intertwines the adjoint representation
{\rm Ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}H
with the representation of
\check{H}
on
\iota(\mathfrak{h})\subseteq{\bf R}^{n}
; in particular,
H
locally embeds in the structure group
G
. Zimmer provided an impressive application for
\omega
a Lorentzian metric, proving that
H
must be locally isomorphic to
{\rm SL}(2,{\bf R})
.

Gromov subsequently gave a very general version of the embedding theorem in
[
21
, Theorem 5.2.A]
, which applies to a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
of infinitesimal automorphisms of a Gromov-rigid geometric structure
\omega
of algebraic type on a compact manifold
M
, together with a group
B\leq\operatorname{Aut}(M,\omega)
preserving a finite measure
\mu
on
M
and normalizing
\mathfrak{h}
. Combined with Gromov’s Frobenius theorem
[
21
, Theorem 1.3.A]
, the embedding theorem led to the Gromov–Zimmer centralizer and representation theorems (see
[
43
]
).

Our version of the embedding theorem will be a direct generalization of the following version proved by Bader, Frances, and the first author in
[
3
, Theorems 1.2 and 4.1]
for Cartan geometries.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\bigl(M,\hat{M},\omega\bigr)
be a Cartan geometry modeled on
(\mathfrak{g},P)
and assume that
{\rm Ad}_{\mathfrak{g}}P<\operatorname{Aut}\mathfrak{g}
is Zariski closed. Let
H\leq\operatorname{Aut}(M,\omega)
be a Lie subgroup. Let
B\leq H
preserve a probability measure
\mu
on
M
. Then for
\mu
-almost-every
x\in M
, for all
\hat{x}\in\hat{M}_{x}
, there exists an algebraic subgroup
\check{B}<P
and an algebraic epimorphism

R\colon\check{B}\rightarrow\bar{B}_{d}
such that

(\operatorname{Ad}g)(\iota_{\hat{x}}(X))=\iota_{\hat{x}}(R(g).X)\qquad\forall X\in\mathcal{\mathfrak{h}},\,g\in\check{B},

where
\iota_{\hat{x}}\colon\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is the linear injection
X\mapsto\omega_{\hat{x}}(X_{\hat{x}}).

The group
\bar{B}_{d}
is the discompact radical of
{\rm Ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}B\leq\operatorname{Aut}\mathfrak{h}
, see Definition
2.4
below. Injectivity of
\iota_{\hat{x}}
is evident from the axioms for the Cartan connection
\omega
, see Definition
2.1
below.

This embedding theorem says that for
\mu
-almost-every
x\in M
, for all
\hat{x}\in\hat{M}_{x}
, the injection
\iota_{\hat{x}}
intertwines the
B
-representation on
\mathfrak{h}
with that of an algebraic subgroup
\check{B}<P
on
\iota_{\hat{x}}(\mathfrak{h})\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
.

1.3
Results

Here is our tractor generalization of the embedding theorem. By invariant connection is meant one which is invariant by all automorphisms; in practice, such connections will be defined in a manner intrinsic to the geometry and invariant for this reason.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\bigl(M,\hat{M},\omega\bigr)
be a Cartan geometry modeled on
(\mathfrak{g},P)
. Let
\rho
be an infinitesimally faithful, completely reducible
G
-representation on
{\bf V}
with
\rho(P)
algebraic, with associated tractor bundle
\mathcal{V}
, equipped with any invariant connection
\nabla
.
Let
B\leq\operatorname{Aut}(M,\omega)
be a Lie subgroup preserving a
probability measure
\mu
on
M
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
denote the
\nabla
-parallel sections of
{\mathcal{V}}
.
Then for
\mu
-almost-every
x\in M
, for all
\hat{x}\in\hat{M}_{x}
, there exists
an algebraic subgroup
\check{B}<P
with an algebraic epimorphism
R\colon\check{B}\rightarrow\bar{B}_{d}
such that

g.\iota_{\hat{x}}(X)=\iota_{\hat{x}}\left(R(g).X\right)\qquad\forall X\in\mathcal{S},\,g\in\check{B}.

The linear injection
\iota_{\hat{x}}\colon\Gamma(\mathcal{V})\rightarrow{\bf V}
is determined by
\hat{x}\in\hat{M}
as described in Section
2.1
, and
\bar{B}_{d}
denotes the discompact radical of the image of
B
in
{\operatorname{GL}}(\mathcal{S})
(see Definition
2.4
below).

As an application, we prove the following rigidity theorem in conformal pseudo-Riemannian geometry.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed pseudo-Riemannian manifold of type
(p,q)
with
p\leq q
and
p+q=\dim M\geq 3
. Suppose that a group
H
locally isomorphic to
{\rm SU}(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})
, with
p^{\prime}<q^{\prime}
, acts conformally on
(M,g)
. Then
p\geq 2p^{\prime}-1
. If
p=2p^{\prime}-1
, then necessarily
p<q
; if
(M,g)
is moreover real-analytic and simply connected, then it is conformally equivalent to the universal cover of
\mbox{{\bf M\"{o}b}}^{p,q}
, with
H
acting via a homomorphism to
{\rm O}(p+1,q+1)
.

Without assuming
(M,g)
simply connected or real-analytic, Pecastaing proved this result for
p^{\prime}=1

[
32
, Corollary 1]
. He proved in
[
33
, Theorem 3]
that any simple group
H<\mbox{Conf}(M,g)
, for
M
closed and
g
Lorentzian, locally embeds in
{\rm O}(2,n+1)
. In analogy with Zimmer’s result on volume-preserving actions of simple groups
in Section
1.2
above, the following question arises.

Question 1.4
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed manifold with a parabolic geometry modeled on
(\mathfrak{g},P)
. Suppose that
H\leq\operatorname{Aut}(M,\omega)
is a simple Lie group. Does the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
H
embed in
\mathfrak{g}
?

For the proof of Theorem
1.3
, we apply the embedding theorem to two tractor bundles simultaneously: to the conformal Killing fields, as in the usual version of the theorem, and also to the conformal-to-almost-Einstein scales, corresponding to the parallel sections of the standard conformal tractor bundle.
From the first embedding, we prove the algebraic Proposition
4.12
, which says
p\geq 2p^{\prime}-1
; in the case of equality, it gives
p<q
as well as precise information about how the positive root spaces of
\mathfrak{h}
embed in those of
\mathfrak{g}
. Proposition
4.12
demonstrates how in an extremal case, and with
H
simple, careful algebraic analysis of the output of the classical embedding theorem yields that it is very close to a Lie algebra embedding of
\mathfrak{h}
into
\mathfrak{g}
.

Using the conclusions of Proposition
4.12
, we obtain a special one-parameter subgroup in
\check{B}
, which is of
balanced linear
type.
The Frobenius theorem ensures that this one-parameter subgroup is the isotropy of a one-parameter group of local conformal transformations fixing a point
x
(see Corollary
3.5
); analyticity is used at this step. This conformal flow in turn implies the existence of local tractor solutions, more precisely of a 2-parameter family of Ricci-flat metrics in the conformal class restricted to a neighborhood of
x
. Analyticity of the metric and
1
-connectedness of
M
allow us to extend these solutions to all of
M
.
The embedding theorem applied to the second bundle implies
H
acts nontrivially on the space
\mathcal{S}
of conformal-to-Einstein tractor solutions. Many more solutions ensue, enough to guarantee conformal flatness. The topological assumptions on
M
then imply

(M,[g])\cong\widetilde{\mbox{{\bf M\"{o}b}}}^{p,q}.

If
H
locally isomorphic to
{\rm SU}(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})
acts isometrically on
(M,g)
as above, then by Zimmer’s result there is a Lie algebra embedding
\mathfrak{su}(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{so}(p,q)
, which implies
p\geq 2p^{\prime}
(see Proposition
4.12
). Thus the extremal case
p=2p^{\prime}-1
of Theorem
1.3
corresponds to
essential
conformal transformations.

A version of Question
1.4
for conformal transformations, in particular for essential conformal groups, is discussed in
[
33
]
. See
[
4
,
19
]
for important results in this direction in terms of the real rank of
H
.

2.1.1
Cartan geometries and parabolic geometries

Suppose
G
is a Lie group and
P<G
a closed subgroup. We will always assume that
P
contains no nontrivial normal subgroup of
G
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
Cartan geometry
modeled on
({\mathfrak{g}},P)
on a manifold
M
is given by a principal
P
-bundle
\pi\colon\hat{M}\rightarrow M
equipped with a

Cartan connection
, that is, a
P
-equivariant
1
-form
{\omega\in\Omega^{1}\bigl(\hat{M},{\mathfrak{g}}\bigr)}
such that

•

\omega_{\hat{x}}\colon T_{\hat{x}}\hat{M}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}
is an isomorphism for all
\hat{x}\in\hat{M}
;

•

\omega(X)=\check{X}
for any
\check{X}\in{\mathfrak{p}}
,
where
X_{\hat{x}}=\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\big|_{t=0}\hat{x}\cdot{\rm e}^{t\check{X}}
is the vertical vector field on
\hat{M}
generated by
\check{X}\in{\mathfrak{p}}
.

The
homogeneous model
of a Cartan geometry as in Definition
2.1
is the homogeneous space
G/P
equipped with the natural projection
G\rightarrow G/P
and
the Maurer–Cartan form
{\omega_{G}\in\Omega^{1}(G,{\mathfrak{g}})}
of
G
, which encodes the natural trivialization
TG\cong G\times{\mathfrak{g}}
by left-invariant vector fields.
The
curvature of a Cartan geometry
is given by the following semibasic,
{\mathfrak{g}}
-valued,
P
-equivariant
2
-form on
\hat{M}
:

K(\xi,\eta)={\rm d}\omega(\xi,\eta)+[\omega(\xi),\omega(\eta)]\qquad\textrm{for }\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{X}(\hat{M}).

The significance of the curvature is that it vanishes if and only if the Cartan geometry is locally isomorphic to its homogeneous model.

Given a Cartan geometry, any representation
\rho\colon P\rightarrow\textrm{GL}({\bf V})
of
P
induces
a vector bundle by forming the associated bundle to the Cartan bundle:

\mathcal{V}=\hat{M}\times_{\rho}{\bf V}.

Recall that the space of smooth sections
\Gamma(\mathcal{V})
of
\mathcal{V}
can be identified with the space
C^{\infty}\bigl(\hat{M},{\bf V}\bigr)^{P}
of smooth,
P
-equivariant functions
f\colon\hat{M}\rightarrow{\bf V}
, that is,

f(\hat{x}\cdot p)={\rho\bigl(p^{-1}\bigr).f(\hat{x})}.

In this way, each
\hat{x}\in\hat{M}
gives a linear function
\iota_{\hat{x}}\colon\Gamma(\mathcal{V})\rightarrow{\bf V}
by evaluation.

Sections of the adjoint bundle
\mathcal{A}=\hat{M}\times_{P}\mathfrak{g}
from Section
1.1
correspond via
\omega
to right-
P
-invariant vector fields on
\hat{M}
, which descend to vector fields on
M
.
The Cartan connection
\omega
induces isomorphisms of vector bundles

TM\cong\hat{M}\times_{P}{\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{p}}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad T^{*}M\cong\hat{M}\times_{P}({\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{p}})^{*}.

(2.1)

These identifications can easily be derived from the axioms for
\omega
(see also
[
37
, Theorem 5.3.15]
).
Because the curvature
K
is semibasic and
P
-equivariant, it
descends to a
2
-form on
M
with values in
\mathcal{A}
, which, via (

), can be identified with a
P
-equivariant
Cartan curvature function

\kappa\colon\ \hat{M}\rightarrow\Lambda^{2}({\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{p}})^{*}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}.

On the Lie algebra level, a Levi decomposition will be denoted
{\mathfrak{p}}\cong{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\ltimes{\mathfrak{p}}_{+}
. There is a
G_{0}
-invariant subspace of
{\mathfrak{g}}
complementary to
{\mathfrak{p}}
, which is denoted
{\mathfrak{g}}_{-}
; it is of course isomorphic as a

-module to
{\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{p}}
. The nilradical
{\mathfrak{p}}_{+}\lhd{\mathfrak{p}}
is moreover isomorphic as a

-module to
({\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{p}})^{*}
. Any representation
\bf V
of
{\mathfrak{p}}
on which the center of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
acts diagonalizably

a

-invariant filtration

{\bf V}={\bf V}^{-\ell}\supset{\bf V}^{-\ell+1}\supset\cdots\supset{\bf V}^{0}\supset{\bf V}^{1}\supset\cdots\supset{\bf V}^{m}\supset 0

(2.2)

such that each quotient

-representation, which implies that

.
In particular, the
{\mathfrak{p}}
-representation

admits such a filtration

{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}^{-k}\supset\dots\supset{\mathfrak{g}}^{0}\supset\dots\supset{\mathfrak{g}}^{k},

where
{\mathfrak{g}}^{0}={\mathfrak{p}}

\bf V
and

, there is an induced filtration by

2.1.2
Tractor connections

For a Cartan geometry
\bigl(M,\hat{M},\omega\bigr)
of any type
({\mathfrak{g}},P)
, the natural vector bundles associated to
G
-representations play a distinguished role.
The Cartan connection
\omega
can be canonically extended to a principal connection on the principal
G
-bundle
\tilde{M}=\hat{M}\times_{P}G
and as such it induces a linear connection

\nabla^{\omega}
on all vector bundles of the form

\mathcal{V}=\tilde{M}\times_{G}{\bf V}=\hat{M}\times_{P}{\bf V}.

Definition 2.2
.

Given a Cartan geometry,
the vector bundles associated to
G
-representations are called
tractor bundles
and
\nabla^{\omega}
the
tractor connection
.

The adjoint tractor bundle comes with a natural projection
\Pi\colon\mathcal{A}\rightarrow TM
to the tangent bundle induced by the projection
{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{p}}
,
and an algebraic action on any other tractor bundle
\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{V}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}
, denoted by
(s,t)\mapsto s\bullet t
, induced by the action of
{\mathfrak{g}}
on
{\bf V}
.

For any vector bundle
\mathcal{W}
associated to any
P
-representation
{\bf W}
, there is a natural differential operator, called the
fundamental derivative
, denoted by

\displaystyle D\colon\ \Gamma(\mathcal{A})\times\Gamma(\mathcal{W})\rightarrow\Gamma(\mathcal{W}),\qquad(s,t)\mapsto D_{s}t.

Viewing
t
in
C^{\infty}\bigl(\hat{M},{\bf W}\bigr)^{P}
and
s
in
C^{\infty}\bigl(\hat{M},{\mathfrak{g}}\bigr)^{P}
, the section
D_{s}t\in C^{\infty}\bigl(\hat{M},{\bf W}\bigr)^{P}
evaluates at
\hat{x}\in\hat{M}
to the derivative of
t
in the direction of
\omega_{\hat{x}}^{-1}(s(\hat{x}))
. The tractor connection on a tractor bundle
\mathcal{V}
can then be expressed in terms of the fundamental derivative as follows:

\nabla_{\Pi(s)}^{\omega}t=D_{s}t+s\bullet t\qquad\textrm{for}\ s\in\Gamma(\mathcal{A}),\,t\in\Gamma(\mathcal{V}).

(2.4)

It is a routine verification that this expression defines a connection; in particular, at any point, the right-hand side only depends on
\Pi(s)
at that point and on
t
in a neighborhood. If
t
is a parallel section, it is the unique one with a given value
t(x)
at any
x\in M
; it follows that
\iota_{\hat{x}}
is an injective linear map to
{\bf V}
when restricted to parallel sections in
\Gamma(\mathcal{V})
, for any
\hat{x}\in\hat{M}
.

As discussed in the introduction, parallel sections of tractor connections typically correspond to solutions of interesting equations for the geometric structure on
M
described by the Cartan geometry. More precisely, in many contexts, certain deformations of the tractor connections by curvature terms yield invariant connections on the tractor bundles for which the parallel sections correspond to the solutions to a particular geometric PDE of interest.
In the case of parabolic geometries, there is a powerful framework for constructing such connections, which we now briefly present.

2.1.3
First BGG-operators of parabolic geometries

Suppose
\bigl(M,\hat{M},\omega\bigr)
is a regular normal parabolic geometry of type
({\mathfrak{g}},P)
with
\dim M=n
. It was shown in
[
6
,
13
]
that any
G
-representation
{\bf V}
induces a
BGG-sequence

of intrinsically defined differential operators of the form

\Gamma(\mathcal{H}_{0})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{V}}_{0}\ }}{{\rightarrow}}\Gamma(\mathcal{H}_{1})\overset{\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{V}}_{1}\ }{\rightarrow}\cdots\overset{\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{V}}_{n-1}}{\rightarrow}\Gamma(\mathcal{H}_{n}),

(2.5)

where

\mathcal{H}_{k}=\mathcal{H}_{k}(M,\mathcal{V})=\hat{M}\times_{P}H_{k}({\mathfrak{p}}_{+},{\bf V})

is the bundle associated to the
k

H_{k}({\mathfrak{p}}_{+},{\bf V})
of
{\mathfrak{p}}_{+}
with coefficients in
{\bf V}
. By
[
24
]
,
the bundles
\mathcal{H}_{k}
are weighted tensor bundles on
M
. In the case of the homogeneous model
M\cong G/P
, the BGG-sequence is a complex, which is dual to the
BGG-resolution
of
{\bf V}
by generalized Verma modules, whence the terminology.

The first operator
\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{V}}=\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{V}}_{0}\colon\Gamma(\mathcal{H}_{0})\rightarrow\Gamma(\mathcal{H}_{1})
in (
2.5
) always defines an overdetermined system of PDEs, the
domain of which is easily computed to be
\mathcal{H}_{0}=\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{V}^{+},
where
\mathcal{V}^{+}\subset\mathcal{V}
is the subbundle corresponding to the
P
-submodule
{\mathfrak{p}}_{+}{\bf V}\subset{\bf V}
.
We denote by
\Pi^{\mathcal{V}}\colon\mathcal{V}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{V}^{+}=\mathcal{H}_{0}
the natural projection. Then,
[
22
,
30
]
shows that the projection
\Pi^{\mathcal{V}}
restricts to a bijection between
sections of
\mathcal{V}
that are parallel for a particular linear connection on
{\mathcal{V}}
and solutions to
\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{V}}(\sigma)=0
.

The linear connection on
\mathcal{V}
, as constructed in
[
22
]
, the parallel sections of which correspond to solutions of

of

and will be denoted by
\nabla^{\mathcal{V}}
. In general,
\nabla^{\mathcal{V}}
differs from the tractor connection

by tensors built from the curvature of
\nabla^{\omega}
. As above, the linear map
\iota_{\hat{x}}
is injective when restricted to parallel sections for
\nabla^{\mathcal{V}}
for all
{\hat{x}\in\hat{M}}
.

2.1.4
Automorphisms acting on tractor bundles

The
automorphisms
of a Cartan geometry
\bigl(M,\hat{M},\omega\bigr)
are the diffeomorphisms of
M
that lift to principal bundle automorphisms of
\hat{M}
which preserve
\omega
. For
f\in\operatorname{Aut}(M,\omega)
, we will also denote by
f
its lift to
\hat{M}
, satisfying
f^{*}\omega=\omega
.
The automorphisms of a Cartan geometry naturally preserve the underlying geometric structure; for example, the automorphisms of the Cartan geometry associated to a conformal semi-Riemannian structure are precisely the conformal transformations.

Because it preserves the framing by
\omega
, the action of
\operatorname{Aut}(M,\omega)
on
\hat{M}
is free and proper. If
f\in\operatorname{Aut}(M,\omega)
stabilizes
x\in M
, then for any
\hat{x}\in\hat{M}_{x}
, there is a unique
p\in P
such that
f.\hat{x}=\hat{x}.p
, called the
isotropy of
f
at
x
with respect to
\hat{x}
.

For any
P
-module
{\bf W}
, the group
\operatorname{Aut}(M,\omega)
acts on the associated bundle
\mathcal{W}=\hat{M}\times_{P}{\bf W}
.
The action of
f\in\operatorname{Aut}(M,\omega)
on a section
X\in\Gamma({\mathcal{S}})\cong C^{\infty}\bigl(\hat{M},{\bf W}\bigr)^{P}
is simply by

f^{*}X=X\circ f^{-1}
.

Assume now that
\bigl(M,\hat{M},\omega\bigr)
is a regular parabolic geometry modeled on
({\mathfrak{g}},P)
, and that
\rho\colon G\rightarrow\textrm{GL}({\bf V})
is a
G
-representation.
Let
{\mathcal{V}}=B\times_{P}{\bf V}
be the corresponding tractor bundle with tractor connection
\nabla^{\omega}
. Let
\nabla^{{\mathcal{V}}}
be the prolongation connection of the
first BGG-operator
D^{{\mathcal{V}}}
corresponding to
{\mathcal{V}}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Any
f\in\emph{Aut}(M,\omega)
preserves both
\nabla^{\omega}
and
\nabla^{{\mathcal{V}}}
, that is,
f^{*}\nabla^{\omega}=\nabla^{\omega}
and
{f^{*}\nabla^{{\mathcal{V}}}=\nabla^{{\mathcal{V}}}}
.

Proof.

Since
f^{*}\omega=\omega
for any
f\in\operatorname{Aut}(M,\omega)
, the fundamental derivative is invariant:

D_{f^{*}X}f^{*}Y=f^{*}(D_{X}Y)\qquad\forall X\in\Gamma({\mathcal{A}}),\,Y\in\Gamma(\mathcal{V}).

The algebraic action of
\Gamma(\mathcal{A})
on
\Gamma(\mathcal{V})
is evidently invariant by any principal bundle automorphism.
Thus it follows from (
2.4
) that
f^{*}\nabla^{\omega}=\nabla^{\omega}
.

Set
\Lambda=\nabla^{{\mathcal{V}}}-\nabla^{\omega}\in\Omega^{1}(M,\operatorname{End}({\mathcal{V}}))
and denote by
R^{{\mathcal{V}}}\in\Omega^{2}(M,\operatorname{End}({\mathcal{V}}))
the curvature of
\nabla^{{\mathcal{V}}}
. The differential computing the Lie algebra homology of
{\mathfrak{p}}_{+}
with values in
{\bf V}
induces via the identification

T^{*}M\cong\hat{M}\times_{P}({\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{p}})^{*}\cong\hat{M}\times_{P}{\mathfrak{p}}^{+}

a bundle map

\partial^{*}_{{\mathcal{V}}}\colon\Omega^{\ell}(M,{\mathcal{V}})\rightarrow\Omega^{\ell-1}(M,{\mathcal{V}})
. The connection
\nabla^{{\mathcal{V}}}
is characterized by the two properties
[
22
]
:

(1)

\Lambda\in\Omega^{1}(M,\operatorname{End}({\mathcal{V}}))^{1}\cap\Gamma(\operatorname{Im}(\partial^{*}_{{\mathcal{V}}}\otimes{\rm Id}_{{\mathcal{V}}^{*}}))
,

(2)

R^{{{\mathcal{V}}}}\in\Gamma(\ker(\partial^{*}_{{\mathcal{V}}}\otimes{\rm Id}_{{\mathcal{V}}^{*}})).

The vector bundle
(T^{*}M\otimes\operatorname{End}({\mathcal{V}}))^{1}
is the subbundle of

T^{*}M\otimes\operatorname{End}({\mathcal{V}})\cong\hat{M}\times_{P}\operatorname{Hom}({\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{p}},\operatorname{End}({\bf V}))

corresponding to the first filtration component, that is, the homogeneity-one component, of the filtered
P
-representation

\operatorname{Hom}({\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{p}},\operatorname{End}({\bf V}))
, where the filtration on that space is induced by (
2.2
) and (
2.3
); as it is an associated bundle to the Cartan bundle, it is acted on by
\operatorname{Aut}(M,\omega)
. The subbundle
\operatorname{Im}(\partial^{*}_{{\mathcal{V}}}\otimes{\rm Id}_{{\mathcal{V}}^{*}})
is similarly
\operatorname{Aut}(M,\omega)
-invariant, so any
f\in\operatorname{Aut}(M,\omega)
preserves their intersection, thus
f^{*}\Lambda

is a section of this intersection. As
f^{*}\Lambda=f^{*}\nabla^{\mathcal{V}}-\nabla^{\omega}
, the pullback
f^{*}\nabla^{\mathcal{V}}
satisfies (1).
Similarly, the curvature of
f^{*}\nabla^{\mathcal{V}}
, namely
f^{*}R^{{{\mathcal{V}}}}
, is a section of the associated vector bundle
\ker(\partial^{*}_{{\mathcal{V}}}\otimes{\rm Id}_{{\mathcal{V}}^{*}})
, so
f^{*}\nabla^{\mathcal{V}}
satisfies (2) as well.
We conclude
f^{*}\nabla^{{\mathcal{V}}}=\nabla^{{\mathcal{V}}}
thanks to this characterization.
∎

2.1.5
The conformal tractor connection

Let
(M,g)
be a semi-Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
and let
\bigl(M,\hat{M},\omega\bigr)
and
\mathcal{V}
be the corresponding parabolic Cartan geometry with model
({\mathfrak{g}},P)
and conformal tractor bundle, respectively, as in Section
1.1
. Recall that
P
is the stabilizer of an isotropic line
{\bf V}_{1}\subset{\bf V}\cong{\bf R}^{p+1,q+1}
. Setting
{\bf V}^{1}={\bf V}_{1}
and
{\bf V}^{0}={\bf V}_{1}^{\perp}
, there is a
P
-invariant filtration

{{\bf V}^{1}\subset{\bf V}^{0}\subset{\bf V}^{-1}={\bf V}}
, cf. (
2.2
), giving rise to an intrinsic filtration
\mathcal{V}^{1}\subset\mathcal{V}^{0}\subset\mathcal{V}
.

A Levi complement
G_{0}<P
preserves a splitting
{\bf V}={\bf V}_{1}\oplus{\bf V}_{0}\oplus{\bf V}_{-1}
.
A choice of metric
g_{0}\in[g]
gives a corresponding decomposition

\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{-1}

(2.6)

in which
\mathcal{V}_{0}\cong TM
and
\mathcal{V}_{-1}
is identified with the line bundle of conformal scales.
References for the formulas in this section are
[
14
, equations (22) and (32)]
.
With respect to a decomposition (

), the standard conformal tractor connection
\nabla^{\omega}
is

\nabla^{\omega}\colon\ \begin{pmatrix}\nu\\
X\\
\sigma\end{pmatrix}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}{\rm d}\nu-P\llcorner X\\
\nabla X+\nu\cdot{\rm Id}+\sigma P^{\sharp}\\
{\rm d}\sigma-X^{*}\end{pmatrix},

(2.7)

where
P^{\sharp}
is the symmetric endomorphism corresponding to the Schouten tensor
P
, and all duals are with respect to
g_{0}
. Changing the metric
g_{0}\in[g]
corresponds to applying an element of
P\backslash G_{0}
to the decomposition of tractor sections, and this transformation commutes with the tractor covariant derivative.
It follows that the intrinsic
(p+1,q+1)
-metric on
\mathcal{V}
is
\nabla^{\omega}
-parallel.

Continuing with
g_{0}\in[g]
, the
\sigma
-component of a
\nabla^{\omega}
-parallel section satisfies

\nabla\mbox{grad }\sigma+\sigma P^{\sharp}=-\nu\cdot{\rm Id},

which is equivalent to (
1.1
) (with the sign of
\nu
switched). Parallel sections have constant self-scalar products with respect to the tractor metric and in particular have a well-defined causal type.

The curvature of
\nabla^{\omega}
has the form, in the decomposition (

),

R^{\omega}(Y,Z)\colon\ \begin{pmatrix}\nu\\
X\\
\sigma\end{pmatrix}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}-C(Y,Z)X\\
\sigma C(Y,Z)^{*}+W(Y,Z)X\\
0\end{pmatrix},

(2.8)

where
W
is the Weyl curvature and

the Cotton tensor of

C(Y,Z)X=\frac{1}{2}\left((\nabla_{Y}P)(Z,X)-(\nabla_{Z}P)(Y,X)\right).

2.2
Borel density theorem

The proof of the embedding theorem rests on the Borel density
theorem.
The term

algebraic
here means real-algebraic. A Zariski closure in this setting comprises the real points of the Zariski closure over
{\bf C}
.

We use a version of the Borel density theorem which can be found in
[
3
, Corollary 3.2]
, and which is due in this generality to Shalom

[
36
, Theorem 3.11]
(see also
[
15
, Theorem 2.6]
).
The setting is a locally compact group
S
acting on an algebraic variety
\mathbb{W}
via a
continuous homomorphism
\rho\colon S\rightarrow\mbox{Aut }\mathbb{W}
. We
will denote by

\bar{S}
the Zariski closure of
\rho(S)
in
\mbox{Aut }\mathbb{W}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
G
be an algebraic group.
The
discompact radical
of
G
is the maximal algebraic subgroup
of
G
with no proper cocompact, algebraic, normal subgroups, denoted
G_{d}
.

The discompact radical can be obtained as the minimal algebraic
subgroup of
G
containing all algebraic subgroups with no non-trivial compact
algebraic quotients, which exists by the Noetherian property of
algebraic subgroups. See
[
36
]
for more details.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
3
, Corollary 3.2]
of the Borel density theorem)
.

Let
S
be a locally compact group and
\mathbb{W}
an algebraic variety, with
an action
\rho\colon S\rightarrow\mbox{Aut }\mathbb{W}
.
Suppose
S
acts by homeomorphisms on a topological space
M

preserving a finite Borel measure
\mu
. Assume
\phi\colon M\rightarrow\mathbb{W}
is an
S
-equivariant measurable map. Then
\phi(x)
is fixed
by
\bar{S}_{d}
for
\mu
-almost-every
x\in M
.

3
Embedding theorem

The following theorem is the tractor generalization of Theorem 1.2 of

[
3
]
(see also Theorem 4.1 there), restated here as in the introduction with an additional technical point in the conclusions. The proof will be a straightforward adaptation of the one in
[

Theorem 3.1
.

Let

\bigl(M,\hat{M},\omega\bigr)
be a Cartan geometry modeled on
(\mathfrak{g},P)
and

Denote by

If

(3.1)

The sets

\operatorname{Aut}^{{\rm loc}}(M,\omega)
fixing

.

Parallel tractor sections, like infinitesimal automorphisms, correspond to solutions of overdetermined PDEs, and a Frobenius theorem in terms of fundamental derivatives of the tractor curvature is possible. We have worked out such a result, however, we have not seen how to profitably combine it with the output of the embedding theorem, so it is not included in the present paper.

Remark 3.6
.

Let
\hat{x}
be as in the conclusion of Corollary

\hat{x}.g
for all

, as well. In this case, the isotropy of

with respect to
\hat{x}.g
is that with respect to
\hat{x}
, conjugated by

. We also have

D^{m}\kappa(\hat{x}.g)=g^{-1}.D^{m}\kappa(\hat{x})\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\check{B}_{\hat{x}.g}=g^{-1}\check{B}_{\hat{x}}g.

The group

will thus be conjugated in

as needed below, corresponding to choosing a different

4
Application in conformal geometry: Algebraic part

The remainder of the paper is devoted to the proof of Theorem

The first two parts are Lie-algebraic, while the shorter, third part has the application of our embedding theorem to the conformal tractor bundle and the conclusion of conformal flatness. In the first part, we prove the upper bound, namely, the following proposition. Recall the notion of embedding between representations from Definition
3.3
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let

for
0<p^{\prime}<q^{\prime}

, and

with

.

In Section
4.2
, we ensure that the maximal
{\bf R}
-split tori of
{\rm Ad}_{{\mathfrak{h}}}B_{H}
and

are aligned under the projection

4.3

on maximal isotropic subspaces in

The alignment of

4.1

Remark 4.2

If

If

in particular, the conformal factor is given by

If

, we choose similarly

such that the positive roots of

\Delta^{+}_{G}=\{\beta_{i}-\beta_{j}\mid i<j\}\cup\{\beta_{i}+\beta_{j}\mid i\neq j\}.

The root system is

are the same as in the case

We choose a set of short roots

\Delta_{H}^{+}=\{\alpha_{k^{\prime}}\}\cup\{\alpha_{i^{\prime}}-\alpha_{j^{\prime}}\mid i^{\prime}<j^{\prime}\}\cup\{\alpha_{i^{\prime}}+\alpha_{j^{\prime}}\}.

This is the root system

\alpha_{k^{\prime}}
are of dimension

. The root spaces for
\alpha_{i^{\prime}}-\alpha_{j^{\prime}}
or

are

For the first application of the embedding theorem, we consider a Borel subgroup

Let

[

4.2
Alignment of the intertwining epimorphism

Recall that the parabolic subgroup
P<{\rm SO}(p+1,q+1)
is the stabilizer of an isotropic line in
{\bf R}^{p+1,q+1}
. A
null-orthonormal basis
of
{\bf R}^{p+1,q+1}
is one with respect to which the quadratic form has the form, writing
n=p+q
,

q(x_{1},\dots,x_{n+2})=2\sum_{i=1}^{p+1}x_{i}x_{n+3-i}+\sum_{i=p+2}^{q}x_{i}^{2}.

The Lie algebra
\check{{\mathfrak{b}}}
is a subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{p}}
, with adjoint action preserving the image
\iota(\mathfrak{h})
, such that the restriction to this image is a surjection onto
\iota\circ\operatorname{ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}{\mathfrak{b}}_{H}\circ\iota^{-1}\cong{\mathfrak{b}}_{H}
.
We will denote by
r
the resulting Lie algebra epimorphism
\check{{\mathfrak{b}}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{b}}_{H}
.
We can focus on the solvable radical
\check{{\mathfrak{b}}}^{\rm solv}
, as any semisimple Levi factor is in the kernel of
r
. Because it is algebraic,
\check{{\mathfrak{b}}}^{\rm solv}
is invariant under Jordan decomposition (see
[
29
, Theorem 4.3.3]
). Since
{\rm Ad}_{\mathfrak{h}}B_{H}
has no elliptic elements, any elliptic elements of
\check{{\mathfrak{b}}}^{\rm solv}
are in the kernel of
r
; more precisely, they belong to a compact torus of
\ker r
, which is a direct factor of
\check{{\mathfrak{b}}}^{\rm solv}
(see
[
29
, Theorem 4.4.7 and Corollary 4.4.12]
), so we will simply exclude this factor and assume that all elements of
\check{{\mathfrak{b}}}^{\rm solv}
have trivial elliptic components in their Jordan decomposition.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let a null-orthonormal basis of
{\bf R}^{p+1,q+1}
in which
P
is block-upper-triangular
be given. A solvable, algebraic
subgroup
\check{B}^{\rm solv}<P
containing no elliptic elements is conjugate in
P
to a group of upper triangular matrices.
A maximal
{\bf R}
-split torus
A_{G}<G
can be chosen to be the maximal diagonal subgroup of
P
in the resulting null-orthonormal basis.

Proof.

Recall that
P
is the stabilizer of a certain isotropic line, which we will denote
\ell_{0}
. The quotient
\ell_{0}^{\perp}/\ell_{0}=V^{p,q}
inherits a conformal class of
scalar products of signature
(p,q)
. On
V^{p,q}
the solvable group
\check{B}^{\rm solv}
descends to preserve this conformal class and
P
descends to the full linear conformal group
{\rm CO}(p,q)
. The claim of the lemma is equivalent to the assertion that
a solvable, algebraic subgroup of
{\rm CO}(p,q)
without elliptic elements is conjugate in
{\rm CO}(p,q)
to a group of upper-triangular matrices.

Lie’s theorem ensures that in
V^{p,q}
there is a simultaneous eigenvector
v_{1}
or a
2
-dimensional irreducible subspace for
\check{B}^{\rm solv}
. In the latter case, the restriction of
\check{B}^{\rm solv}
splits into a nontrivial elliptic and a scalar action. By the assumption that all elements of
\check{B}^{\rm solv}
have trivial elliptic Jordan components, this possibility is excluded. If the eigenvector
v_{1}
is timelike or spacelike, then the eigenvalues on it are
\pm 1
. In this case,
apply Lie’s theorem again to
v_{1}^{\perp}
, to conclude that it contains an isotropic simultaneous eigenvector, or
\check{{\mathfrak{b}}}^{\rm solv}
is trivial on
V^{p,q}
. Proceeding inductively yields a null-orthonormal basis in which
\check{B}^{\rm solv}
is upper-triangular. It follows that on
V^{p,q}
the conjugacy of
\check{B}^{\rm solv}
to upper-triangular matrices can be achieved in
{\rm CO}(p,q)
. Consequently, on
{\bf R}^{p+1,q+1}
, the conjugacy can be achieved in
P
, since
\check{B}^{\rm solv}
preserves
\ell_{0}
, and we can take the last basis vector to be a null vector not orthogonal to
\ell_{0}
.

In any null-orthonormal basis in which
P
is block-upper-triangular – that is, in which
P
is the stabilizer of the line spanned by the first element of the basis – the maximal diagonal subgroup is a maximal
{\bf R}
-split torus of
G
. In particular, such a diagonal subgroup in the basis we have found here can be taken to be
A_{G}
.
∎

As explained in Remark
3.6
, the group
\check{B}^{\rm solv}
can be replaced with the
P
-conjugate given by the above lemma, and all conclusions of the embedding theorem continue to hold.

Lemma 4.4
.

After conjugating
\check{B}^{\rm solv}
by a suitable element of

, there is a subalgebra
\check{{\mathfrak{a}}}<\check{{\mathfrak{b}}}^{\rm solv}\cap{\mathfrak{a}}_{G}

, and this conjugation preserves the properties of

achieved in Lemma

Proof.

Fix a basis

of

\check{X}_{1}
be a lift of

to
\check{{\mathfrak{b}}}^{\rm solv}
. The semisimple component of
\check{X}_{1}
in its Jordan decomposition in

is nontrivial, so it is contained in
\check{{\mathfrak{b}}}^{\rm solv}
by algebraicity of the latter. Thus write

where

is
{\bf R}
-semisimple in
{\mathfrak{g}}
and

. The subalgebra

admits an
\operatorname{ad}A_{1}
-invariant subspace decomposition
\check{{\mathfrak{a}}}_{1}\ltimes\ker r

maps bijectively under

to
{\mathfrak{a}}_{H}
, which is abelian, so it commutes with

.

Let

be a lift of
X_{2}
to
\check{{\mathfrak{a}}}_{1}
. The semisimple Jordan component of

commutes with
A_{1}
, has the same projection to
{\mathfrak{a}}_{H}
as

, and is contained in
\check{{\mathfrak{b}}}^{\rm solv}
by algebraicity. So we can assume

is semisimple. The

-eigenspace of
\operatorname{ad}A_{1}
, denote it

, is
\operatorname{ad}A_{2}
-invariant. There is thus an
\operatorname{ad}A_{2}
-invariant complement to

in

\check{{\mathfrak{a}}}_{2}

and maps under

{\mathfrak{a}}_{H}
. This

centralizes

and

. Continuing for

results in an abelian subalgebra
\check{{\mathfrak{a}}}_{p^{\prime}}\subset\check{{\mathfrak{b}}}^{\rm solv}
mapping isomorphically under

{\mathfrak{a}}_{H}
and comprising

-semisimple elements of

. Such a subalgebra is contained in a maximal abelian

-split subalgebra of

, which is in turn conjugate in

to the desired

contained in our chosen diagonal subalgebra

.

The subspaces
\check{{\mathfrak{a}}}_{i^{\prime}}
comprise upper-triangular matrices in

, and the conjugation to the diagonal takes place in the subalgebra of

comprising upper-triangular matrices.
∎

Replacing
\check{B}^{\rm solv}
with the conjugate given by the above lemma, we will henceforth have
\check{{\mathfrak{a}}}\subset{\mathfrak{a}}_{G}
lifting
{\mathfrak{a}}_{H}
. Denote by
\theta
the homomorphism from
{\mathfrak{a}}_{G}^{*}
to
{\mathfrak{a}}_{H}^{*}
induced by restriction to

composed with

Finally, consider the unipotent radicals.
Because

is an algebraic epimorphism, it maps the nilpotent radical of

, which is contained in
{\mathfrak{p}}_{+}
, onto that of

, which is the sum of positive root spaces of
\mathfrak{h}
(see
[

, Corollary 4.3.6]
). The restriction of

to the nilpotent radical may not split,
but there is a subspace
\check{U}_{{\mathfrak{b}}}
,
complementary to
\ker r
in the nilpotent radical of
\check{{\mathfrak{b}}}^{\rm solv}
and invariant by the

-diagonal subgroup of

, in particular by

. For

4.3
Consequences of equivariance for stabilizer and isotropy

The starting point for the analysis in this section are ideas which can be found in
[
33
]
.
The facts that
{\mathfrak{p}}
is a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
and that
\operatorname{ad}{\mathfrak{p}}
preserves a
(p,q)
-scalar product on

up to scale have the following consequences via the embedding.

Proposition 4.5

(
[
33
, Proposition 2.1]
)
.

For
\mathfrak{h}^{0}=\iota^{-1}({\mathfrak{p}})
,

(1)

\mathfrak{h}^{0}
is
\operatorname{ad}{\mathfrak{b}}_{H}
-invariant,

(2)

\operatorname{ad}{\mathfrak{b}}_{H}
preserves a symmetric bilinear form on
\mathfrak{h}/\mathfrak{h}^{0}
up to scaling by
-2\theta(\beta_{1})

A straightforward consequence of the second axiom for the Cartan connection

{X\in\mathfrak{h}^{0}}

\iota([X,Y])=[\iota(Y),\iota(X)]\qquad\forall Y\in\mathfrak{h}.

(4.1)

It immediately follows that
\mathfrak{h}^{0}
is a subalgebra. In the Levi decomposition
{\mathfrak{p}}\cong{\mathfrak{q}}\ltimes({\bf R}^{p,q})^{*}
, the Levi complement
{\mathfrak{q}}\cong{\bf R}\oplus\mathfrak{so}(p,q)
is precisely the sum of root spaces

with

in the span of
\{\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{p+1}\}

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}^{0}=\iota^{-1}({\mathfrak{p}})

(1)

If
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{h}^{0}

\iota(\mathfrak{h})

(2)

If

{\check{\mathfrak{h}}_{\alpha}=\iota(\mathfrak{h}_{\alpha})\subset\check{{\mathfrak{b}}}}

If
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{h}^{0}
, then (

imply the image of

is a semisimple subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{p}}
. Now

given by the embedding theorem, and it is an isotropy algebra at the point

The Thurston stability theorem
[

implies that

is linearizable at

is conjugate by an element

into

.
Semisimplicity implies the image is in

Under the weaker assumption of (2), the conclusions of the embedding theorem still give that

\iota(\mathfrak{h}_{\alpha})\subset\check{{\mathfrak{b}}}^{\rm solv}

comprises nilpotent elements, it belongs to the nilpotent radical of

-invariant. Thus

could differ from the original choice of

∎

Lemma 4.7

(useful facts about

)

As above, let

(1)

∎

For a subspace

The next lemma will be key for establishing the bound between

Lemma 4.8

(useful facts about

(2)

As in

Under the hypotheses of (1), Lemma

5
Embedding theorem in the conformal tractor bundle

In this section, we work with the conformal tractor bundle to complete the proof of Theorem

.
Therefore, we let

be as in the theorem, and assume

5.1
Local conformal flow with balanced isotropy

By Proposition
4.12
, there is a partition of the short roots
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{p+1}
of
\Delta_{G}^{+}
into
p^{\prime}
pairs
\bigl(\beta_{k_{i^{\prime}}},\beta_{\ell_{i^{\prime}}}\bigr)

i^{\prime}=1,\dots,p^{\prime}
, such that
\theta\bigl(\beta_{k_{i^{\prime}}}-\beta_{\ell_{i^{\prime}}}\bigr)=0
for each

. As
\check{{\mathfrak{a}}}
is
p^{\prime}
-dimensional and
p^{\prime}=(p+1)/2
, a basis for
\check{{\mathfrak{a}}}
is
\bigl\{\check{Y}_{k_{i^{\prime}}}+\check{Y}_{\ell_{i^{\prime}}}\mid i^{\prime}=1,\dots,p^{\prime}\bigr\}
, where
\check{Y}_{j}
is the coroot of
{\mathfrak{a}}_{G}
dual to
\beta_{j}
for each
j
. Let
i^{\prime}
be such that

. By applying an inner automorphism of

if necessary, we may assume
\ell_{i^{\prime}}=2
. Now set
\check{Y}=\check{Y}_{1}+\check{Y}_{2}\in\check{{\mathfrak{a}}}
.

The one-parameter subgroup

\big\{h^{t}={\rm e}^{t\check{Y}}\big\}={\rm diag}\bigl(e^{t},{\rm e}^{t},1,\dots,1,{\rm e}^{-t},{\rm e}^{-t}\bigr)<P,

and it belongs to
\check{B}^{\rm solv}
. Recall that
\check{B}^{\rm solv}
was obtained from the embedding theorem for a particular

and
\hat{x}_{0}\in\hat{M}_{x_{0}}
. By Corollary
3.5
, there is a one-parameter subgroup
{\bigl\{\varphi^{t}_{Y}\bigr\}<\operatorname{Aut}^{\rm loc}_{x_{0}}(M,\omega)}
with isotropy
\bigl\{h^{t}\bigr\}
with respect to
\hat{x}_{0}
.
The representation of
\bigl\{h^{t}\bigr\}
on

is
{\rm diag}\bigl(1,{\rm e}^{-t},\dots,{\rm e}^{-t},{\rm e}^{-2t}\bigr)

type because the dilation and hyperbolic isometric components balance each other.

5.2
Isotropic tractor solutions

Existence of a flow with the dynamics of
\bigl\{\varphi^{t}_{Y}\bigr\}
does not necessarily imply local conformal flatness – see Alekseevsky’s important examples in
[
1
]
– but it does impose strong restrictions on the Weyl curvature. These in turn lead to local tractor solutions.

For
v\in{\bf R}^{p,q}
an isotropic vector, the
annihilator of the flag

{\bf R}v\subset v^{\perp}\subset{\bf R}^{p,q}
comprises the nilpotent subalgebra of
\mathfrak{so}(p,q)
which preserves the flag and is zero on the associated graded space
{\bf R}v\oplus\bigl(v^{\perp}/{\bf R}v\bigr)\oplus\bigl({\bf R}^{p,q}/v^{\perp}\bigr)
; it will be denoted
{\mathfrak{n}}(v)
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be the standard conformal tractor bundle associated to the representation
{\bf R}^{p+1,q+1}
of
{P<{\rm SO}(p+1,q+1)}
, with standard conformal tractor connection
\nabla=\nabla^{\omega}
. Denote by
\mathcal{S}
the
\nabla
-parallel sections of
\mathcal{V}
, and recall from Section
2.1.5
that they correspond to almost-Einstein rescalings of a metric in the given conformal class on
M
.

The following consequences of balanced linear isotropy are already known in the conformal Lorentzian case (see
[
18
, Lemma 6.5 and Theorem 1.3 (2)]
,
[
27
, Section 4]
,
[
16
, Proposition 5.1]
),
and generalize to higher signature as follows.

Proposition 5.1

(compare
[
16
,
18
,
27
]
)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a pseudo-Riemannian manifold, and suppose that the stabilizer of
x_{0}\in M
contains a conformal flow
\bigl\{\varphi^{t}_{Y}\bigr\}
with isotropy generated by
\check{Y}={\rm diag}(1,1,0,\dots,0,-1,-1)
with respect to
\hat{x}_{0}\in\pi^{-1}(x_{0})
. Then there are

(1)

a Ricci-flat metric
g_{0}\in[g|_{U}]
in a neighborhood
U
of
x_{0}
, and

(2)

an isotropic vector field
X\in\mathcal{X}(U)
which is parallel for the Levi-Civita connection of

, such that

(3)

the Weyl tensor
W_{x}
has values in
{\mathfrak{n}}(X_{x})
for all

.

(4)

Together these yield a
2
-dimensional totally isotropic space

of parallel sections of
\mathcal{V}|_{U}
, having trivial intersection with
\mathcal{V}^{0}
.

If
(M,g)
is analytic and simply connected, then the solutions in
\mathcal{I}_{U}
extend to
M
, comprising a subspace

, and

extends to an isotropic conformal vector field on

Proof.

As above, consider the one-parameter subgroup

\bigl\{h^{t}\bigr\}={\rm diag}\bigl({\rm e}^{t},{\rm e}^{t},1,\dots,1,{\rm e}^{-t},{\rm e}^{-t}\bigr)<P,

the isotropy of
\bigl\{\varphi^{t}_{Y}\bigr\}
with respect to
\hat{x}_{0}
.
On
{\bf R}^{p,q}
, denote by
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
the corresponding null-orthonormal frame, where

, in which
\bigl\{h^{t}\bigr\}
is

{\rm diag}\bigl(1,{\rm e}^{-t},\dots,{\rm e}^{-t},{\rm e}^{-2t}\bigr).

In this frame, the Weyl tensor
W_{x_{0}}
is a homomorphism
\hat{W}_{\hat{x}_{0}}\colon\wedge^{2}{\bf R}^{p,q}\rightarrow\mathfrak{so}(p,q)
, which is
\{h^{t}\}
-equivariant. The eigenvalues of
\operatorname{Ad}h^{t}
on

are
{\rm e}^{t}
,

, and
{\rm e}^{-t}
, and the eigenspace for

is precisely the nilpotent subalgebra

. It follows that
\hat{W}_{\hat{x}_{0}}(u,v)\in{\mathfrak{n}}(e_{n})
, for all

.

The flow
\bigl\{h^{t}\bigr\}
is
stable
for

in the sense of
[
18
, Definition 4.2]
. It follows that
\bigl\{h^{t}\bigr\}
remains a
holonomy

x_{0}
, meaning that there is a neighborhood
U
of
x_{0}
with a section
\hat{U}\subset\hat{M}
containing
\hat{x}_{0}
such that

is bounded in
\hat{M}
as

for all
{\hat{x}\in\hat{U}}
(see
[
10

). The section
\hat{U}
can be taken to be
\exp_{\hat{x}_{0}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{-})
, restricted to a sufficiently small neighborhood of
0\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{-}
; it is in particular real-analytic.

For any
\hat{x}\in\hat{U}
, the Weyl curvature
\hat{W}_{\hat{x}}
must remain bounded – though not necessarily invariant – under the action of
\bigl\{h^{t}\bigr\}
. It follows that
\hat{W}_{\hat{x}}(u,v)\in{\mathfrak{n}}(e_{n})
for all
u,v\in{\bf R}^{p,q}
,
\hat{x}\in\hat{U}
.
Because it is
\bigl\{\varphi^{t}_{Y}\bigr\}
-invariant, the full Cartan curvature

is bounded under the action of the holonomy
\bigl\{h^{t}\bigr\}
. The
{\mathfrak{p}}_{+}
-component of

is in

\bigl\{h^{t}\bigr\}

\kappa_{\hat{x}}=\hat{W}_{\hat{x}}
for all
\hat{x}\in\hat{U}
.

Let
\mathcal{R}_{U}
be the saturation
\hat{U}\cdot J

, where

is the stabilizer of
{\bf R}e_{n}
and

is the annihilator of
e_{n}^{\perp}
.
This saturation is an analytic reduction of
\hat{M}
to
J
.
Observe that the Lie algebra

\mathfrak{j}_{1}=\{\xi\in{\mathfrak{p}}^{+}\mid[\xi,\mathfrak{g}_{-1}]\subset\mathfrak{j}_{0}\}.

Thus
{\mathfrak{g}}_{-}+\mathfrak{j}
is a subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Considering
{\mathfrak{n}}(e_{n})
inside
{\mathfrak{p}}
, it is normalized by

. Thus on
\mathcal{R}_{U}
the Cartan curvature retains the properties of being equal to the Weyl curvature and of having values in
{\mathfrak{n}}(e_{n})
. It follows from the formula, for

,

\omega_{\hat{x}}[Y,V]=Y.\omega_{\hat{x}}(V)-V.\omega_{\hat{x}}(Y)-\kappa_{\hat{x}}(\omega_{\hat{x}}(Y),\omega_{\hat{x}}(V))+[\omega_{\hat{x}}(Y),\omega_{\hat{x}}(V)]

that the distributions
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=\omega^{-1}({\mathfrak{g}}_{-}+\mathfrak{j})
and
\mathcal{D}=\omega^{-1}({\mathfrak{g}}_{-}+{\mathfrak{n}}(e_{n}))
are involutive in restriction to

(see for example
[

). The integral curves of the

{{\mathfrak{g}}_{-}+\mathfrak{j}}
through

are contained in

, by construction.

We claim that
\mathcal{R}_{U}
is an integral submanifold for
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
. Let

, so
\exp_{\hat{x}_{0}}(tv)\in\hat{U}
for all sufficiently small

\omega((\exp_{\hat{x}_{0}})_{*v}(y))\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{-}+\mathfrak{j}
for any such

,
v
, with
v
sufficiently small. Indeed, setting
\hat{x}=\exp_{\hat{x}_{0}}(v)
, once
T_{\hat{x}}\hat{U}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\hat{x}}
, then it follows from the axioms for
\omega
that
T_{\hat{x}}\mathcal{R}_{U}=\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\hat{x}}
, and
T_{\hat{x}.g}\mathcal{R}_{U}\subset\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\hat{x}.g}
for all

; moreover, every point of
\mathcal{R}_{U}
is of the form
\hat{x}.g
for such
\hat{x}=\exp_{\hat{x}_{0}}(v)
and

.

Consider a two-parameter map
\sigma(t,s)=\exp_{\hat{x}_{0}}(t(v+sy))
, so that

\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial s}(t,0)=(\exp_{\hat{x}_{0}})_{*tv}(ty)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\omega\left(\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial t}(t,s)\right)=v+sy.

The formula for the Cartan curvature gives rise to the ODE

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\omega\left(\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial s}(t,0)\right)=y-\kappa\left(\omega\left(\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial s}(t,0)\right),v\right)+\left[\omega\left(\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial s}(t,0)\right),v\right].

Therefore,

Now the distribution
\mathcal{D}

With respect to the decomposition (

Remark 5.2
.

The conclusions above hold just as well given a nontrivial balanced linear element in the isotropy at a point – that is, a flow is not needed.

Here is where the embedding theorem is applied to the conformal tractor bundle:

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
(M,[g])
be as in Proposition
5.1
and assume that it is real-analytic and simply connected. Let
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{S}
be the parallel tractors given by Proposition
5.1

(4)
.
The
H
-action on
\mathcal{S}
does not fix any nonzero vectors of
\mathcal{I}
.

Proof.

Let
\hat{x}_{0}
be as above, a point at which

\bigl\{h^{t}={\rm diag}\bigl({\rm e}^{t},{\rm e}^{t},1,\dots,1,{\rm e}^{-t},{\rm e}^{-t}\bigr)\bigr\}<\check{B}^{\rm solv},

This point was given by the embedding theorem; it is such that the linear injection
\iota\colon\mathcal{S}\rightarrow{\bf V}
is the composition of evaluation at
x_{0}
with the trivialization
\iota_{\hat{x}_{0}}
of
\mathcal{V}_{x_{0}}
corresponding to
\hat{x}_{0}\in\hat{M}
.

Now
\hat{x}_{0}
belongs to the reduction of
\hat{M}
to
U(e_{n})
constructed in the proof of Proposition
5.1
above, corresponding to the Ricci-flat metric
g_{0}
. The trivialization
\iota_{\hat{x}_{0}}\colon\mathcal{V}_{x_{0}}\rightarrow{\bf V}
therefore identifies the subspaces
(\mathcal{V}_{i})_{x_{0}}
of the decomposition determined by
g_{0}
with
{\bf V}_{i}
for
i=1,0,-1
.
The constant sections of
\mathcal{V}_{-1}
, belonging to
\mathcal{I}
, corresponding to metrics homothetic to
g_{0}
, therefore evaluate under
\iota_{\hat{x}_{0}}
to elements of
{\bf V}_{-1}={\bf R}E_{n+1}
.

The point
\hat{x}_{0}
also, by the construction of the isotropic vector field

in the proof of Proposition

(2) above, corresponds to a frame of

in which

evaluates to
e_{n}\in{\bf R}^{1,n-1}={\bf V}_{0}
.
The solution

therefore evaluates under

.
The image

is therefore the span of

and
E_{n+1}

acts as a dilation by

By the embedding theorem, there is a one-parameter subgroup of

, the image under

\bigl\{h^{t}\bigr\}
, which acts on

as a dilation by

may or may not belong to

; the embedding theorem says in any case that there is a one-parameter subgroup of

with representation on

corresponding to
\bigl\{h^{t}\bigr\}
.)

5.3
Conclusion

Let

. The orbit

spans an irreducible

\mathcal{S^{\prime}}
,
which has dimension at least

\mathcal{S^{\prime}}
cannot be a totally isotropic subspace for the tractor form on

is necessarily a nondegenerate subspace of

. The representation therefore corresponds to a homomorphism, which we may assume is virtually faithful by Proposition

and
m\leq q+1
.

Now

acts conformally on

and amenability of

, there is
an embedding of

into

, where

is the stabilizer of an isotropic line of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, for which we can take
\check{B}=\overline{I(B_{H})}_{d}<N
.
By Proposition

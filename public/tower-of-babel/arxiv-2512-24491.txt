Minimal Solutions to the Skorokhod Reflection Problem Driven by Jump Processes and an Application to Reinsurance


Abstract
We consider a reflected process in the positive orthant driven by an exogenous jump process. For a given input process, we show that there exists a unique minimal strong solution to the given particle system up until a certain stopping time, which is stated explicitly in terms of the dual formulation of a linear programming problem associated with the state of the system. We apply this model to study the ruin time of interconnected insurance firms, where the stopping time can be interpreted as the failure time of a reinsurance agreement between the firms. Our work extends the analysis of the particle system in [ 1 ] to the case of jump driving processes, and the existence result of [ 11 ] beyond the case of sub-stochastic reflection matrices.

Introduction

Fix a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
with a filtration
\mathbb{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0-}
satisfying the usual hypotheses. Throughout this work, we require all of our stochastic processes to be càdlàg (meaning right continuous with left limits). We denote the jump of any given process
A
at time
t
by
\Delta A_{t}:=A_{t}-A_{t-}
. To allow for jumps at the initial time, we prepend a left limit
0-
to a semi-infinite interval and consider the index set
\{0-\}\cup[0,\infty)
. Consider
n\geq 1
stochastic processes
X^{1},\dots,X^{n}
, which for
t\geq 0-
satisfy the system of equations

X_{t}^{i}=X_{0-}^{i}+c_{i}t-Z_{t}^{i}+L_{t}^{i}-\sum_{j\neq i}q_{ij}L_{t}^{j},\quad 1\leq i\leq n,

(1)

where
X_{0-}^{1},\dots,X_{0-}^{n}\geq 0
are
\mathcal{F}_{0-}
-measurable initial conditions;
c_{1},\dots,c_{n}
are non-negative constants;
Q=(q_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
is a non-negative matrix with
q_{ii}=0
for
1\leq i\leq n
;
Z^{1},\dots,Z^{n}
are
\mathbb{F}
-adapted jump processes with (almost surely) finitely many jumps in any interval (for instance, compound Poisson processes); and
L^{1},\dots,L^{n}
are reflection processes which constrain
X^{1},\dots,X^{n}
to remain in the non-negative orthant
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
.

We ask that the reflection processes each satisfy a one-dimensional Skorokhod reflection problem (see, for instance,
[
9
, Lemma 6.14]
):

L_{t}^{i}=\sup_{s\leq t}\bigg(X_{0-}^{i}+c_{i}t-Z_{s}^{i}-\sum_{j\neq i}q_{ij}L_{s}^{j}\bigg)_{-},\quad 1\leq i\leq n,

(2)

where
(a)_{-}:=-\min(0,a)
for
a\in\mathbb{R}
. For a given input process
Y
taking values in
\mathbb{R}
,
L_{t}:=\sup_{s\leq t}(Y_{s})_{-}
is the smallest non-decreasing process such that
Y+L
is non-negative for all times. Given inputs
X_{0-}^{1},\dots,X_{0-}^{n}
, and
Z^{1},\dots,Z^{n}
, a strong solution to (
1
)–(
2
) consists of a pair of processes
X=(X^{1},\dots,X^{n})
and
L=(L^{1},\dots,L^{n})
which simultaneously satisfy (
1
) and (
2
) on a (possibly random) time interval
[0,\tau)
.
In Theorem
1
, we show that a strong solution to (
1
)–(
2
) on
[0,\tau)
can be continued to
[0,\tau]
if and only if
X_{\tau-}-\Delta Z_{\tau}
is contained in a certain dual cone. For
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, introduce the notation
x\geq y
to mean that
x_{i}\geq y_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. As a consequence (Corollary
1
), we establish existence and uniqueness of a
minimal
solution
(X,L)
and a
maximal
stopping time
\tau^{*}
such that if
(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{L})
is any other strong solution to (
1
)–(
2
) on
[0,\widetilde{\tau})
with the same initial condition and driving processes as
(X,L)
then
\widetilde{\tau}\leq\tau^{*}
and
\widetilde{L_{t}}\geq L_{t}
for all
t\in[0,\tau)
.

In a financial context, we interpret
X^{1},\dots,X^{n}
as the resource levels of
n
insurance firms bound by a reinsurance agreement. The firms collect premiums at the constant rates
c_{1},\dots,c_{n}
and pay claims according to the exogenous shocks
Z^{1},\dots,Z^{n}
. The matrix
Q
encodes the routing of resources between firms due to reinsurance: firm
i
remains solvent thanks to the the term
L^{i}
, but must contribute
q_{ij}L^{j}
to firm
j
. The amount transferred from all firms to firm
j
by time
t
is
\sum_{i\neq j}q_{ij}L^{j}_{t}
. If
\sum_{i\neq j}q_{ij}>1
, there is friction in the transfers to
j
, possibly due to taxes or fees. The minimal solution
L^{1},\dots,L^{n}
gives the most parsimonious choice to redistribute resources between
X^{1},\dots,X^{n}
, keeping all resource levels non-negative at all times prior to the maximal time
\tau^{*}
. We interpret
\tau^{*}
itself as a ruin time, when there is insufficient liquidity and the reinsurance agreement breaks down. In Section
6
, we compare ruin probabilities for a particular case with
n=2
, both with and without reinsurance.

2
Main Result

When does (
1
)–(
2
) admit solutions? If the spectral radius of
Q
,
\rho(Q)
, is strictly less than 1, then solutions exist for all time by
[
11
, Proposition 1]
(and see also
[
7
]
, for continuous driving processes such as Brownian motion). For
\rho(Q)\geq 1
, can we go beyond this result? When the driving processes are Brownian motions, recent work has shown that existence and uniqueness hold on a stochastic interval which depends on the structure of
Q
, the covariance of the driving processes, and the given initial condition
[
1
]
. For the present setting where
X
is driven by jump processes we state our main result, which we prove in Section
4
.

Theorem 1
.

Consider the cone
C=\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:u\geq 0,u^{\top}(I-Q)\leq 0\}
and its dual cone
C^{*}=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:u^{\top}y\geq 0\text{ for all }u\in C\}
.
A solution
(X,L)
of (
1
)–(
2
) on an interval
[0,\tau)
can be extended to
[0,\tau]
if and only if
X_{\tau-}-\Delta Z_{\tau}\in C^{*}
. Moreover, when
X_{\tau-}-\Delta Z_{\tau}\in C^{*}
there exists a unique minimal
\Delta L_{\tau}
with respect to the partial order
\geq
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
such that
L_{\tau}=L_{\tau-}+\Delta L_{\tau}
gives an extension of
(X,L)
on
[0,\tau]
.

As a corollary, show that for any given initial conditions and driving processes
(Z^{1},\dots,Z^{n})
there exists a unique minimal strong solution of (
1
)–(
2
) on a maximal time interval (see, for instance,
[
5
,
3
,
1
]
for similar notions in a range of contexts).

Corollary 1
.

Given inputs
X_{0-}^{1},\dots,X_{0-}^{n}
, and
Z^{1},\dots,Z^{n}
, there exists unique
(X,L)
and
\tau^{*}
such that
(X,L)
solves (
1
)–(
2
) on
[0,\tau^{*})
and if
(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{L})
is any other strong solution to (
1
)–(
2
) on
[0,\widetilde{\tau})
with the same inputs as
(X,L)
then
\widetilde{\tau}\leq\tau^{*}
and
\widetilde{L_{t}}\geq L_{t}
for all
t\in[0,\widetilde{\tau})
.

Remark 1
.

If
\rho(Q)<1
, then it is easy to see that
C
is empty,
C^{*}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and hence solutions may always be continued. Therefore, we have found a new derivation for the existence result of
[
11
, Proposition 1]
.

Proof of Corollary
1
.

Consider any arbitrary solution
(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{L})
to (
1
)–(
2
) on some interval
[0,\widetilde{\tau})
.
Let
\tau_{1}
denote the first time that one or multiple of the processes
Z^{1},\dots,Z^{n}
exhibit a jump. Set
L^{1}_{t}=\dots=L^{n}_{t}=0
on
[0,\tau_{1})
. From (
2
), we see that for
1\leq i\leq n
,
\widetilde{L}^{i}_{t}\geq 0=L^{i}_{t}
for all
t\in[0,\tau_{1}\wedge\widetilde{\tau})
, where we have used the notation
s\wedge t:=\min(s,t)
. We consider now two cases:

Case 1.
If
X_{\tau_{1}-}-\Delta Z_{\tau_{1}}\notin C^{*}
, then we set
\tau^{*}=\tau_{1}
. If
\widetilde{\tau}\geq\tau^{*}
then we must have
\widetilde{X}_{\tau_{1}-}-\Delta Z_{\tau_{1}}\notin C^{*}
and hence
\widetilde{\tau}>\tau^{*}
is impossible.

Case 2.
If
X_{\tau_{1}-}-\Delta Z_{\tau_{1}}\in C^{*}
we let
\Delta L_{\tau_{1}}
be the unique minimal jump size from Theorem
1
and set
L_{\tau_{1}}=0+\Delta L_{\tau_{1}}
.
If
\widetilde{\tau}<\tau_{1}
, then
\widetilde{L}^{i}_{t}\geq L^{i}_{t}
for all
t\in[0,\tau_{1}\wedge\widetilde{\tau}]
trivially. If not, then suppose for the sake of contradiction that
\widetilde{L}^{i}_{\tau_{1}}<L^{i}_{\tau_{1}}
for some
1\leq i\leq n
and for
1\leq i\leq n
set

\widehat{L}^{i}_{t}=\begin{cases}0&t<\tau_{1}\\
\widetilde{L}^{i}_{\tau_{1}}&t=\tau_{1}.\end{cases}

Then, the definition of
\tau_{1}
along with the non-negativity of
\widetilde{X}
imply that

X_{0-}^{i}+c_{i}t-Z^{i}_{t}+\widehat{L}^{i}_{t}-\sum_{j\neq i}q_{ij}\widehat{L}^{j}_{t}\geq 0

for all
t\in[0,\tau_{1}]
and
1\leq i\leq n
. However,
(\Delta\widehat{L}^{1}_{\tau_{1}},\dots,\Delta\widehat{L}^{n}_{\tau_{1}})=(\widetilde{L}^{1}_{\tau_{1}},\dots,\widetilde{L}^{n}_{\tau_{1}})
contradicts the minimality of
\Delta L_{\tau_{1}}
with respect to the partial order
\geq
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
. Therefore
\widetilde{L}^{i}_{t}\geq L^{i}_{t}
for all
t\in[0,\tau_{1}\wedge\widetilde{\tau}]
.

Let
\tau_{2}
denote the next jump time for any of the processes
Z^{1},\dots,Z^{n}
. Repeating the above argument shows that
\widetilde{L}^{i}_{t}\geq L^{i}_{t}
for all
t\in[0,\tau_{2}\wedge\widetilde{\tau}]
and
\widetilde{\tau}>\tau^{*}
is impossible. We continue as before with
\tau_{3},\tau_{4}
, and so on. Since
(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{L})
and
\widetilde{\tau}
are arbitrary, we have completed the proof. ∎

3
Increments of the Skorokhod Map

We prove here an auxiliary result, which allows us to express
\Delta L^{1}_{\tau},\dots,\Delta L^{n}_{\tau}
in terms of
X_{\tau-}
and
\Delta Z_{\tau}
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
(Y_{t})_{t\geq 0-}
be a càdlàg process taking values in
\mathbb{R}
. Define
L_{t}=\sup_{s\leq t}(Y_{s})_{-}
and
X_{t}=Y_{t}+L_{t}
. Then for all
t\geq 0-
,

\Delta L_{t}=\left(X_{t-}+\Delta Y_{t}\right)_{-}

(3)

Proof.

We split the proof into the cases
-Y_{t}\leq L_{t-}
and
-Y_{t}>L_{t-}

First, suppose
-Y_{t}\leq L_{t-}
. Then
\Delta L_{t}=0
and

\displaystyle X_{t-}+\Delta Y_{t}=Y_{t-}+L_{t-}+Y_{t}-Y_{t-}\geq 0.

Hence
\left(X_{t-}+\Delta Y_{t}\right)_{-}=0=\Delta L_{t}
and (
3
) holds. Next, if
-Y_{t}>L_{t-}
, then
L_{t}=-Y_{t}\geq 0
and hence

\displaystyle\Delta L_{t}=L_{t}-L_{t-}=-Y_{t}-(X_{t-}-Y_{t-})=-(X_{t-}+\Delta Y_{t})\geq 0.

We see that (
3
) is established in this case as well. ∎

Proof of Theorem
1

We proceed by reducing to a linear programming problem.
An example of the primal and dual problems with
n=2
is plotted in Figure
1
.

Proof.

Suppose
\Delta Z_{\tau}=(\Delta Z^{1}_{\tau},\dots,\Delta Z^{n}_{\tau})
is non-zero (otherwise, the problem is trivial). By Lemma
1
, if we can exhibit a jump
\Delta L_{\tau}=(\Delta L^{1}_{\tau},\dots,\Delta L^{n}_{\tau})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
such that

\Delta L^{i}_{\tau}=\bigg(X_{\tau-}^{i}-\Delta Z_{\tau}^{i}-\sum_{j\neq i}q_{ij}\Delta L_{\tau}^{j}\bigg)_{-},\quad 1\leq i\leq n,

(4)

then setting
L_{\tau}=L_{\tau_{-}}+\Delta L_{\tau}
and
X_{\tau}=X_{\tau_{-}}-\Delta Z_{\tau}+(I-Q)\Delta L_{\tau}
yields a solution to (
1
)–(
2
) on
[0,\tau]
. Consider the linear programming (LP) problem

minimize

\displaystyle\quad\|\Delta L_{\tau}\|_{1}

subject to

\displaystyle\quad\Delta L_{\tau}\geq 0\text{ and }(I-Q)\Delta L_{\tau}\geq-\left(X_{\tau-}-\Delta Z_{\tau}\right).

If
\Delta L_{\tau}
solves LP, then for each
i
, at least one of the following equalities must hold

\displaystyle\Delta L^{i}_{\tau}=0\quad\text{ or }\quad\Delta L^{i}_{\tau}=-\left(X_{\tau-}^{i}-\Delta Z_{\tau}^{i}\right)+\sum_{j\neq i}q_{ij}\Delta L_{\tau}^{j},

since otherwise
\Delta L^{i}_{\tau}
may be decreased and the objective function
\|\Delta L_{\tau}\|_{1}
will be decreased. Hence, if
\Delta L_{\tau}
solves LP then (
4
) holds. We tackle the feasibility of LP using duality.

Rewrite the constraints on
\Delta L_{\tau}
as

A\Delta L_{\tau}\geq b\quad\text{where}\quad A=\begin{bmatrix}I-Q\\
I\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad b=\begin{bmatrix}-X_{\tau-}+\Delta Z_{\tau}\\
0\end{bmatrix}.

Farkas’s Lemma (see, for instance
[
4
, Theorem 2.1]
, where it is called the Infeasibility Theorem) implies that LP is infeasible if and only if there exists
y\geq 0
such that
y^{\top}A=0
and
y^{\top}b>0
. We seek to reduce this latter condition to the condition in the statement of the theorem. Write
y^{\top}=[u^{\top},\,v^{\top}]
where
u,v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then
y^{\top}A=0
implies that
v^{\top}=-u^{\top}(I-Q)
. The condition
y^{\top}b>0
simplifies to

y^{\top}b=-u^{\top}(X_{\tau-}-\Delta Z_{\tau})>0.

Therefore, LP is infeasible if and only if there exists
u\geq 0
with
-u^{\top}(I-Q)=v^{\top}\geq 0
and
-u^{\top}(X_{\tau-}-\Delta Z_{\tau})>0
. The conditions on
u
and
v
define the cone
C
from the statement of the theorem, and there will be
u\in C
such that
-u^{\top}(X_{\tau-}-\Delta N_{\tau})>0
iff
X_{\tau-}-\Delta N_{\tau}\notin C^{*}
.

It remains to show uniqueness of the minimal jump. Suppose that
\Delta L_{\tau}\geq 0
and
{[I-Q]_{i}\Delta L_{\tau}=0}
, where
[I-Q]_{i}
denotes the
i
th row of
I-Q
. Then
\Delta L_{\tau}^{i}=\sum_{j\neq i}q_{ij}\Delta L_{\tau}^{j}
and hence

(1,1,\dots,1)^{\top}\Delta L_{\tau}=\Delta L_{\tau}^{i}+\sum_{j\neq i}\Delta L_{\tau}^{j}=\sum_{j\neq i}(1+q_{ij})\Delta L_{\tau}^{j}\geq 0

with equality if and only if
\Delta L_{\tau}=0
. Therefore, none of the constraining faces in the feasible region are orthogonal to
(1,1,\dots,1)
, and hence uniqueness holds for LP. Note that uniqueness still holds if the objective function is replaced by
a^{\top}\Delta L_{\tau}
for any
a
with strictly positive entries. This yields minimality with respect to the partial order
\geq
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
.

5
Fixed Point Approach

When the LP problem is feasible, it can be solved efficiently using the simplex method
[
4
]
. Another approach is to construct a solution iteratively with a monotone operator (see, for instance, a similar approach in
[
3
, Proposition 2.3]
). For a given input vector
X_{\tau-}-\Delta Z_{\tau}
, define the operator
\Gamma:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}
by

\Gamma[z]=\bigg(\big(X_{\tau-}^{1}-\Delta Z_{\tau}^{1}-\sum_{j\neq 1}q_{1j}z_{j}\big)_{-},\dots,\big(X_{\tau-}^{1}-\Delta Z_{\tau}^{1}-\sum_{j\neq n}q_{nj}z_{j}\big)_{-}\bigg)

We notice that fixed points of
\Gamma
(that is, any
z^{*}
such that
\Gamma[z^{*}]=z^{*}
) satisfy (
4
). One can check that
\Gamma
is monotone (that is,
\Gamma[z]\geq z
for all
z
). Therefore, the Knaster–Tarski Theorem implies that the fixed points of
\Gamma
form a complete lattice (this result is often used for existence of fixed points in systemic risk literature, such as the seminal work
[
6
]
). Starting from
z=0
and applying
\Gamma
iteratively yields the (
a fortiori
) unique solution to LP in the limit.

6
Application to Reinsurance

We consider now a particular case with
n=2
insurance firms. As a base case in the absence of reinsurance, we suppose that for
t\geq 0-
,
X^{(1)}
and
X^{(2)}
satisfy

X^{(1)}_{t}=X^{1}_{0-}+c_{1}t-Z^{1}_{t},\quad\text{and}\quad X^{(2)}_{t}=X^{2}_{0-}+c_{1}t-Z^{2}_{t},

where

Z^{1}_{t}=\sum_{k=1}^{N^{1}_{t}+N^{3}_{t}}Y^{1}_{k},\quad\text{and}\quad Z^{2}_{t}=\sum_{k=1}^{N^{2}_{t}+N^{3}_{t}}Y^{2}_{k};

X^{1}_{0-},X^{2}_{0-}
are
\mathcal{F}_{0-}
-measurable initial conditions;
c_{1},c_{2}\geq 0
are fixed;
N^{1},N^{2}
, and
N^{3}
are independent
\mathbb{F}
-adapted Poisson processes with intensities
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
, and
\lambda_{3}
; and
(Y^{1}_{k})_{k\geq 1}
and
(Y^{2}_{k})_{k\geq 1}
are each sequences of independent and identically distributed random variables with distributions
Y^{1}_{1}
and
Y^{2}_{1}
, respectively. The ruin time of each firm is given by

T^{1}=\inf\{t\geq 0:X^{(1)}_{t}\leq 0\},\quad\text{and}\quad T^{2}=\inf\{t\geq 0:X^{(2)}_{t}\leq 0\}.

We have here two instances of a classical insurance risk model (see, for instance,
[
10
]
), which have been coupled together by the common Poisson process
N^{3}
.

In Figure
2
, we plot Monte Carlos estimates of the ruin probabilities
t\mapsto\mathbb{P}(T^{1}\leq t)
,

,
t\mapsto\mathbb{P}(T^{1}\leq t,T^{2}\leq t)
, and
t\mapsto\mathbb{P}(T^{1}\leq t\text{ or }T^{2}\leq t)
for a particular choice of parameters. The plots were generated using 20000 Monte Carlo trials,
X^{(1)}_{0-}=X^{(2)}_{0-}=5
,
Y^{1}_{1}
and
Y^{2}_{1}
are both
\mathrm{Exp}(1)
random variables,
c_{1}=c_{2}=1.2
, and
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{3}=0.6
. The conditions for the firms are symmetrical so that
\mathbb{P}(T^{1}\leq t)=\mathbb{P}(T^{2}\leq t)
for all
t
. Closed form solutions for the ruin probabilities are available in some special cases (see
[
10
]
and also
[

]
), but we do not pursue them here. For limiting behavior as

,
[

\mathbb{P}(T^{1}<\infty)=1

. More generally, one may deduce an integral equation for
{\Psi(x):=\mathbb{P}(T^{1}=\infty|X^{1}_{0-}=x)}

Next, we compare to the case with reinsurance, that is, the minimal solution of the reflected system (

). We take the same initial conditions and driving processes. We suppose that

represents friction due to taxes and fees. In Figure

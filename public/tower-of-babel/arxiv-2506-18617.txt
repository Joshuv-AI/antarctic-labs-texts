Regularity of a bulk-surface Cahn–Hilliard model driven by Leray velocity fields
Andrea Giorgini, Patrik Knopf & Jonas Stange

Abstract
. We consider a convective bulk-surface Cahn–Hilliard system with dynamic boundary conditions and singular potentials. For this model, well-posedness results concerning weak and strong solutions have already been established in the literature. However, they require the prescribed velocity fields to belong to function spaces with high time regularity. In this paper, we prove that the well-posedness of weak solutions holds true under more general regularity assumptions on the velocity fields. Next, via an alternative proof for higher regularity, we show the well-posedness of strong solutions for velocity fields of Leray type, which is a more relevant assumption for physical applications. Our approach hinges upon a new well-posedness and regularity theory for a bulk-surface elliptic system with singular nonlinearities, which may be of independent interest.

1.
Introduction

The Cahn–Hilliard equation was originally introduced to model spinodal decomposition processes in binary alloys. Later, it turned out that the Cahn–Hilliard equation can also be used to describe later stages of the evolution of phase transition phenomena such as Ostwald ripening.
Nowadays, it is the most popular diffuse interface model with diverse applications in materials science, life sciences and image processing.
A crucial advantage of Cahn–Hilliard models compared with sharp-interface models (free boundary problems) is the purely Eularian formulation, which does not require any direct tracking of the interfaces separating the involved materials. As such, topological changes of the interfaces can be handled without imposing artificial conditions.

The Cahn–Hilliard equation describes the time evolution of a phase-field
\phi
, with associated chemical potential
\mu
. The function
\phi
is a conserved order parameter, which typically represents the concentration difference of the two components of a binary mixture.
In the standard formulation of the Cahn–Hilliard system in a bounded domain, homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions for both
\phi
and
\mu
are classically imposed in the study of the initial-boundary value problem. However, as already been observed by mathematicians and physicists (see, e.g.,
[
8
,
18
,
19
,
29
]
), these boundary conditions entail crucial limitations when a precise description of the dynamics close to the boundary is required.
The main drawback is that the homogeneous Neumann boundary condition on the phase-field enforces the diffuse interfaces between the two components to intersect the boundary of the domain at a perfect right angle. In most applications, this is very unrealistic as the contact angle is expected to deviate from ninety degrees, and to even change dynamically over the course of time. Furthermore, the homogeneous Neumann boundary condition on the chemical potential prohibits any transfer of material between the bulk and the boundary. In some applications this is actually desired as the bulk mass is supposed to be conserved. On the other hand, when absorption processes or chemical reactions at the boundary are considered, an exchange of material between bulk and boundary needs to be included in the formulation.

Due to these limitations of the classical Cahn–Hilliard problem, and also to describe short-range interactions between bulk and surface more effectively,
several Cahn–Hilliard models with dynamic boundary conditions have been proposed and analyzed in the literature. In particular, an evolutionary equation of Cahn–Hilliard type on the boundary has become very popular in recent years (see, e.g.,
[
27
,
36
,
31
,
30
]
), which also account for a transfer of material between bulk and boundary. These models are sometimes also referred as bulk-surface Cahn–Hilliard systems.
In this paper, we study the following convective bulk-surface Cahn–Hilliard model:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi+\textnormal{div}(\phi\bm{v})=\textnormal{div}(m_{\Omega}(\phi)\nabla\mu)

\displaystyle\text{in}\ Q,

(1.1a)

\displaystyle\mu=-\Delta\phi+F^{\prime}(\phi)

\displaystyle\text{in}\ Q,

(1.1b)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi+\textnormal{div}_{\Gamma}(\psi\bm{w})=\textnormal{div}_{\Gamma}(m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta)-\beta m_{\Omega}(\phi)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mu

\displaystyle\text{on}\ \Sigma,

(1.1c)

\displaystyle\theta=-\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi+G^{\prime}(\psi)+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi

\displaystyle\text{on}\ \Sigma,

(1.1d)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}K\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi=\alpha\psi-\phi&\text{if}\ K\in[0,\infty),\\
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi=0&\text{if}\ K=\infty\end{cases}

\displaystyle\text{on}\ \Sigma,

(1.1e)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}Lm_{\Omega}(\phi)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mu=\beta\theta-\mu&\text{if}\ L\in[0,\infty),\\
m_{\Omega}(\phi)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mu=0&\text{if}\ L=\infty\end{cases}

\displaystyle\text{on}\ \Sigma,

(1.1f)

\displaystyle\phi|_{t=0}=\phi_{0}

\displaystyle\text{in}\ \Omega,

(1.1g)

\displaystyle\psi|_{t=0}=\psi_{0}

\displaystyle\text{on}\ \Gamma.

(1.1h)

This model was first proposed in
[
35
]
and further analyzed in
[
34
]
. It offers a unified framework for various Cahn–Hilliard-type dynamic boundary conditions previously introduced in the literature.

In System (
1.1
),
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\in\{2,3\}
is a bounded domain with boundary
\Gamma\coloneqq\partial\Omega
and outward unit normal vector field
\mathbf{n}
. Moreover,
T>0
denotes a prescribed final time and we write
Q\coloneqq\Omega\times(0,T)
and
\Sigma\coloneqq\Gamma\times(0,T)
.
The symbols
\nabla_{\Gamma}
,
\textnormal{div}_{\Gamma}
and
\Delta_{\Gamma}
denote the tangential surface gradient, the surface divergence, and the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\Gamma
, respectively.

System (
1.1
) is referred to as a
convective
Cahn–Hilliard model as the dynamics are influenced by prescribed velocity fields
\bm{v}:Q\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\bm{w}:\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
As in
[
35
]
, we assume that
\textnormal{div}(\bm{v})=0
in
Q
and that

\textnormal{div}_{\Gamma}(\bm{w})=0
,
\bm{v}\cdot\mathbf{n}=0
and
\bm{w}\cdot\mathbf{n}=0
on
\Sigma
.

The functions
\phi:Q\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
\mu:Q\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
denote the phase-field and the chemical potential in the bulk, whereas
\psi:\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
\theta:\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
represent the phase-field and the chemical potential on the boundary, respectively. The functions
m_{\Omega}(\phi)
and
m_{\Gamma}(\psi)
represent the mobilities of the mixtures in the bulk and on the boundary, respectively. They model the spatial locations and intensity to which the diffusion processes take place.
The phase-fields
\phi
and
\psi
are coupled by the boundary condition (
1.1e
), and the chemical potentials
\mu
and
\theta
are coupled by the boundary condition (
1.1f
). Here, the parameters
K,L\in[0,\infty]
are used to distinguish different types of these coupling conditions.

The functions
F^{\prime}
and
G^{\prime}
are the derivatives of double-well potentials
F
and
G
, respectively.
A physically motivated example of such a double-well potential, especially in applications related to material science, is the
Flory–Huggins potential
, which is also referred to as the
logarithmic potential
. It is defined as

\displaystyle W_{\textup{log}}(s)\coloneqq\frac{\Theta}{2}\big[(1+s)\ln(1+s)+(1-s)\ln(1-s)\big]-\frac{\Theta_{c}}{2}s^{2},\quad s\in(-1,1).

(1.2)

Here, we assume
0<\Theta<\Theta_{c}
, where
\Theta
denotes the temperature of the mixture and
\Theta_{c}
represents the critical temperature below which phase separation processes occur. We point out that
W_{\textup{log}}
can be continuously extended onto the interval
[-1,1]
. However, since
W_{\textup{log}}^{\prime}(s)\to\pm\infty
as
s\to\pm 1
,
W_{\textup{log}}
is classified as a singular potential. In this paper, we consider a general class of singular potentials (see Subsection
2.3
) such that, for example, the choice
F=G=W_{\textup{log}}
is admissible.

The free energy functional associated with System (
1.1
) reads as

\displaystyle\begin{split}E_{K}(\phi,\psi)&=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla\phi\right|^{2}+F(\phi)\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\right|^{2}+G(\psi)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad+\sigma(K)\int_{\Gamma}\frac{1}{2}\left|\alpha\psi-\phi\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma,\end{split}

(1.3)

where, to account for the different cases corresponding to the choice of
K
, the function
\sigma
is defined

\displaystyle\sigma(K)\coloneqq\begin{cases}K^{-1},&\text{if }K\in(0,\infty),\\
0,&\text{if }K\in\{0,\infty\}.\end{cases}

We observe that sufficiently regular solutions of System (
1.1
) satisfy the
mass conservation law

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\beta\int_{\Omega}\phi(t)\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\psi(t)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma=\beta\int_{\Omega}\phi_{0}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\psi_{0}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma,&\textnormal{if }L\in[0,\infty),\\
\int_{\Omega}\phi(t)\;\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\phi_{0}\;\mathrm{d}x\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\int_{\Gamma}\psi(t)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma=\int_{\Gamma}\psi_{0}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma,&\textnormal{if }L=\infty\end{cases}

(1.4)

for all
t\in[0,T]
and the
energy identity

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E_{K}(\phi,\psi)&=\int_{\Omega}\phi\bm{v}\cdot\nabla\mu\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\psi\bm{w}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma-\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}(\phi)\left|\nabla\mu\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\qquad-\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma-\sigma(L)\int_{\Gamma}(\beta\theta-\mu)^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\end{split}

(1.5)

on
(0,T)
. In the non-convective case
(\bm{v},\bm{w})\equiv(\bm{0},\bm{0})
, the right-hand side of (
1.5
) is non-positive as the first two terms vanish.

Goals and novelties of this paper.

The well-posedness of weak and strong solutions of System (
1.1
) as well as further regularity properties were already established in
[
34
]
. These results require the velocity fields to satisfy restrictive regularity assumptions, which might not be satisfied if the velocity fields are not simply prescribed, but are determined by additional coupled evolution equations.
This motivates us to establish the following main results.

(I)

Existence of a weak solution and further regularity.

In Theorem
3.2
we improve the weak well-posedness result in
[
34
, Theorem 3.4]
. In contrast to the previous result in
[
34
]
, where the velocity fields are such that

\bm{v}\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{3}_{\textnormal{div}}(\Omega))\quad\text{and}\quad\bm{w}\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2+\omega}_{\tau}(\Gamma))\quad\text{for some $\omega>0$},

(1.6)

while our new result requires the velocity fields to satisfy the weaker condition

\bm{v}\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}_{\textnormal{div}}(\Omega))\quad\text{and}\quad\bm{w}\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}_{\tau}(\Gamma)).

(1.7)

The definition of the spaces
\mathbf{L}^{p}_{\textnormal{div}}(\Omega)
and
\mathbf{L}^{p}_{\tau}(\Gamma)

for any
p\in[2,\infty)
can be found in Subsection
2.1
.

Moreover, Theorem
3.3
provides an additional regularity result for weak solutions, which improves
[
34
, Theorem 3.4]
by providing a higher time regularity for the phase-fields
\phi
and
\psi
.
At first glance, this seems to be only a very minor improvement. However, especially in Navier–Stokes–Cahn–Hilliard models (e.g., the one from
[
26
]
), where (
1.1
) appears as a subsystem, these enhanced time regularities become crucial in the mathematical analysis as they help to estimate the nonlinear terms.

(II)

Continuous dependence on the initial data and the velocity fields.

In Theorem
3.5
, we further prove the uniqueness and the continuous dependence of the weak solution on the initial data and the velocity fields along with a concrete stability estimate. This result improves the result of
[
34
, Theorem 3.6]
since one of the velocity fields only needs to satisfy (
1.7
) instead of (
1.6
), and our new stability estimate merely uses the differences of the velocity fields in the
L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma))
-norm instead of the
L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{3}(\Omega)\times\mathbf{L}^{2+\omega}(\Gamma))
-norm.

(III)

Existence of a strong solution and further regularity.

In
[
34
, Theorem 3.7]
, the existence of a strong solution to System (
1.1
) was established for velocity fields, which satisfy

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \bm{v}&\in H^{1}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{6/5}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{L}_{\textnormal{div}}^{3}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)),\\
\bm{w}&\in H^{1}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{1+\omega}(\Gamma))\cap L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{L}_{\textnormal{div}}^{2}(\Gamma))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma))\end{aligned}\right.

(1.8)

for some
\omega>0
. For
K\in[0,\infty)
and
L\in(0,\infty]
, we show in Theorem
3.6
that the same result holds true if the velocity fields have
Leray type regularity
, that is

\displaystyle(\bm{v},\bm{w})\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{L}_{\textnormal{div}}^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{1}).

(1.9)

In the two-dimensional case (i.e.,
d=2
), the result remains correct for
L=0
and
K\in[0,\infty)
, see Remark
3.7
(a)
.
The crucial advantage compared to
[
34
, Theorem 3.7]
is that (
1.9
) is a more natural assumption on the velocity fields than (
1.8
).
In particular, if the velocity fields are not just prescribed but determined by additional evolution equations (e.g., Navier–Stokes equations), Leray type regularity is exactly what the classical energy balance provides.
In this sense, the new regularity result provided by Theorem
3.6
is more useful in the mathematical analysis for coupled systems, e.g., for the convergence to stationary states as in
[
1
]
.

To prove our main result
(III)
and the additional regularities in
(I)
, we first establish useful auxiliary results. These results are not only relevant to the present paper but may also be of interest for the further analysis of Cahn–Hilliard-type or Allen–Cahn-type systems with dynamic boundary conditions in future research.

(i)

In Section
4
, we characterize the subdifferential of the singular functional

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{K}(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}+F_{1}(u)\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}v\right|^{2}+G_{1}(v)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma+\displaystyle\sigma(K)\int_{\Gamma}\frac{1}{2}(\alpha v-u)^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma,

where
F_{1}
and
G_{1}
can be chosen as the convex, singular parts of the potentials
F
and
G
introduced above. In particular,
\widetilde{E}_{K}
coincides with
E_{K}
introduced in (
1.3
) except for the remaining regular parts
F_{2}=F-F_{1}
and
G_{2}=G-G_{1}
of the potentials
F
and
G
. In Proposition
4.2
, we show that the subdifferential
\partial\widetilde{E}_{K}
of
\widetilde{E}_{K}
is a maximal monotone operator and we provide an explicit representation formula for
\partial\widetilde{E}_{K}
.

(ii)

In Section
5
, we investigate the following bulk-surface elliptic system, which involves the singular nonlinearities
F_{1}
and
G_{1}
:

\displaystyle-\Delta u+F_{1}^{\prime}(u)

\displaystyle=f

\displaystyle\text{in $\Omega$},

(1.10a)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\Gamma}v+G_{1}^{\prime}(v)+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u

\displaystyle=g

\displaystyle\text{on $\Gamma$},

(1.10b)

\displaystyle K\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u

\displaystyle=\alpha v-u

\displaystyle\text{on $\Gamma$}.

(1.10c)

The results of Proposition
4.2
outlined in
(i)
can be used to prove the existence of a unique strong solution to (
1.10
). In the remainder of Section
5
we further prove several regularity results with corresponding estimates for strong solutions of (
1.10
). These results are used to establish the higher regularities claimed in our main results
(I)
and
(III)
.

2.
Functional framework, preliminaries and assumptions

2.1.
Notation and Function Spaces.

For any Banach space
X
, we denote its norm by
\|\cdot\|_{X}
, its dual space by
X^{\prime}
, and the associated duality pairing of elements
\Lambda\in X^{\prime}
and
\zeta\in X
by
\langle\Lambda,\zeta\rangle_{X}
. The space
L^{p}(I;X)
,
1\leq p\leq+\infty
, denotes the set of all strongly measurable
p
-integrable functions mapping from any interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
into
X
, or, if
p=+\infty
, essentially bounded functions. Moreover, the space
W^{1,p}(I;X)
consists of all functions
f\in L^{p}(I;X)
such that
\partial_{t}f\in L^{p}(I;X)
, where
\partial_{t}f
denotes the vector-valued distributional derivative of
f
.

Throughout this paper, we prescribe
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d=2,3
to be a bounded domain of class
C^{2}
, whose boundary is denoted by
\Gamma
.

For any real numbers
s\geq 0
and
p\in[1,\infty]
, we introduce the spaces

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{p}\coloneqq L^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(\Gamma),\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{W}^{s,p}\coloneqq W^{s,p}(\Omega)\times W^{s,p}(\Gamma),

provided that the boundary
\Gamma
is sufficiently regular. We abbreviate
\mathcal{H}^{s}\coloneqq\mathcal{W}^{s,2}
and identify
\mathcal{L}^{2}
with
\mathcal{H}^{0}
. Note that
\mathcal{H}^{s}
is a Hilbert space with respect to the inner product

\displaystyle\big(\left(\phi,\psi\right),\left(\zeta,\xi\right)\big)_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}\coloneqq\left(\phi,\zeta\right)_{H^{s}(\Omega)}+\left(\psi,\xi\right)_{H^{s}(\Gamma)}\qquad\text{for all~}\left(\phi,\psi\right),\left(\zeta,\xi\right)\in\mathcal{H}^{s}

and its induced norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}\coloneqq\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. We recall that the duality pairing can be expressed as

\displaystyle\langle\left(\phi,\psi\right),\left(\zeta,\xi\right)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}\coloneqq\left(\phi,\zeta\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left(\psi,\xi\right)_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}

for all
(\zeta,\xi)\in\mathcal{H}^{s}
if
(\phi,\psi)\in\mathcal{L}^{2}
.

Moreover, for any
2\leq p<\infty
, we introduce the spaces

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}^{p}_{\textnormal{div}}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{\bm{v}\in\mathbf{L}^{p}(\Omega):\textnormal{div}\;\bm{v}=0\ \text{in~}\Omega,\ \bm{v}\cdot\mathbf{n}=0\ \text{on~}\Gamma\},

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}^{p}_{\tau}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{\bm{w}\in\mathbf{L}^{p}(\Gamma):\bm{w}\cdot\mathbf{n}=0\ \text{on~}\Gamma\},

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}^{p}_{\textnormal{div}}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{\bm{w}\in\mathbf{L}^{p}_{\tau}(\Gamma):\textnormal{div}_{\Gamma}\;\bm{w}=0\ \text{on~}\Gamma\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{p}_{\textnormal{div}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathbf{L}^{p}_{\textnormal{div}}(\Omega)\times\mathbf{L}^{p}_{\textnormal{div}}(\Gamma).

Note that in the definitions of
\mathbf{L}^{p}_{\textnormal{div}}(\Omega)
and
\mathbf{L}^{p}_{\textnormal{div}}(\Gamma)
, the expressions
\textnormal{div}\;\bm{v}
in
\Omega
and
\textnormal{div}_{\Gamma}\;\bm{w}
on
\Gamma
are to be understood in the sense of distributions. For any
\bm{v}\in\mathbf{L}^{p}(\Omega)
(
p\geq 2
) with
\textnormal{div}\;\bm{v}=0
in
\Omega
, it holds that
\bm{v}\cdot\mathbf{n}\in H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)
, and therefore, the relation
\bm{v}\cdot\mathbf{n}=0
on
\Gamma
is well-defined.

For
L\in[0,\infty]
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, we introduce the linear subspaces

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{L,\beta}^{1}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\mathcal{H}^{1},&\text{if }L\in(0,\infty],\\
\displaystyle\{(\phi,\psi)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}:\phi=\beta\psi\text{ a.e.~on }\Gamma\},&\text{if }L=0.\end{cases}

The spaces
\mathcal{H}_{L,\beta}^{1}
are Hilbert spaces endowed with the inner product
\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{\mathcal{H}_{L,\beta}^{1}}\coloneqq\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}
and its induced norm. Moreover, we define the product

\displaystyle\langle\left(\phi,\psi\right),\left(\zeta,\xi\right)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{L,\beta}^{1}}\coloneqq\left(\phi,\zeta\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left(\psi,\xi\right)_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}

for all
\left(\phi,\psi\right),\left(\zeta,\xi\right)\in\mathcal{L}^{2}
. By means of the Riesz representation theorem, this product can be extended to a duality pairing on
(\mathcal{H}_{L,\beta}^{1})^{\prime}\times\mathcal{H}_{L,\beta}^{1}
, which will also be denoted as
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{L,\beta}^{1}}
.

For
(\phi,\psi)\in(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta})^{\prime}
, we define the generalized bulk-surface mean

\displaystyle\textnormal{mean}\left(\phi,\psi\right)\coloneqq\frac{\langle\left(\phi,\psi\right),\left(\beta,1\right)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta}}}{\beta^{2}\left|\Omega\right|+\left|\Gamma\right|},

which reduces to

\displaystyle\textnormal{mean}\left(\phi,\psi\right)=\frac{\beta\left|\Omega\right|{\langle\phi\rangle}_{\Omega}+\left|\Gamma\right|{\langle\psi\rangle}_{\Gamma}}{\beta^{2}\left|\Omega\right|+\left|\Gamma\right|}

if
\phi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\psi\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
, where

\displaystyle{\langle\phi\rangle}_{\Omega}=\frac{1}{\left|\Omega\right|}\int_{\Omega}\phi\,\;\mathrm{d}x,\quad{\langle\psi\rangle}_{\Gamma}=\frac{1}{\left|\Gamma\right|}\int_{\Gamma}\psi\,\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma.

We then define the closed linear subspaces

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{L,\beta}^{1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\begin{cases}\{\left(\phi,\psi\right)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta}:\textnormal{mean}\left(\phi,\psi\right)=0\},&\text{if~}L\in[0,\infty),\\
\{\left(\phi,\psi\right)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}:{\langle\phi\rangle}_{\Omega}={\langle\psi\rangle}_{\Gamma}=0\},&\text{if~}L=\infty.\end{cases}

Note that these subspaces are Hilbert spaces with respect to the inner product
\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}
.

Next, we set

\displaystyle\sigma(L)\coloneqq\begin{cases}L^{-1},&\text{if }L\in(0,\infty),\\
0,&\text{if }L\in\{0,\infty\},\end{cases}

and we introduce a bilinear form on
\mathcal{H}^{1}\times\mathcal{H}^{1}
by defining

\displaystyle\big(\left(\phi,\psi\right),\left(\zeta,\xi\right)\big)_{L,\beta}\coloneqq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\zeta\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\xi\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma+\sigma(L)\int_{\Gamma}(\beta\psi-\phi)(\beta\xi-\zeta)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

for all
\left(\phi,\psi\right),\left(\zeta,\xi\right)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}
. Moreover, we set

\displaystyle\|\left(\phi,\psi\right)\|_{L,\beta}\coloneqq\big(\left(\phi,\psi\right),\left(\phi,\psi\right)\big)_{L,\beta}^{\frac{1}{2}}

for all
\left(\phi,\psi\right)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}
. The bilinear form
\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{L,\beta}
defines an inner product on
\mathcal{V}^{1}_{L,\beta}
, and
\|\cdot\|_{L,\beta}
defines a norm on
\mathcal{V}^{1}_{L,\beta}
, that is equivalent to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}
(see
[
32
, Corollary A.2]
). Hence, the space
\mathcal{V}^{1}_{L,\beta}
endowed with
\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{L,\beta}
is a Hilbert space.

Next, we define the spaces

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{L,\beta}^{-1}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\{\left(\phi,\psi\right)\in(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta})^{\prime}:\beta\left|\Omega\right|{\langle\phi\rangle}_{\Omega}+\left|\Gamma\right|{\langle\psi\rangle}_{\Gamma}=0\},&\text{if~}L\in[0,\infty),\\
\{\left(\phi,\psi\right)\in(\mathcal{H}^{1})^{\prime}:{\langle\phi\rangle}_{\Omega}={\langle\psi\rangle}_{\Gamma}=0\},&\text{if~}L=\infty.\end{cases}

Using the Lax–Milgram theorem, one can show that for any
(\phi,\psi)\in\mathcal{V}^{-1}_{L,\beta}
, there exists a unique weak solution
\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}(\phi,\psi)=\big(\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}^{\Omega}(\phi,\psi),\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}^{\Gamma}(\phi,\psi)\big)\in\mathcal{V}^{1}_{L,\beta}
to the following elliptic problem with bulk-surface coupling

\displaystyle-\Delta\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}^{\Omega}(\phi,\psi)

\displaystyle=-\phi

\displaystyle\qquad\text{in~}\Omega,

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\Gamma}\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}^{\Gamma}(\phi,\psi)+\beta\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}^{\Omega}(\phi,\psi)

\displaystyle=-\psi

\displaystyle\qquad\text{on~}\Gamma,

\displaystyle L\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}^{\Omega}(\phi,\psi)

\displaystyle=\beta\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}^{\Gamma}(\phi,\psi)-\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}^{\Omega}(f,g)

\displaystyle\qquad\text{on~}\Gamma,

in the sense that is satisfies the weak formulation

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}(\phi,\psi)),(\zeta,\xi)\big)_{L,\beta}=-\big<(\phi,\psi),(\zeta,\xi)\big>_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta}}

for all test functions
(\zeta,\xi)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta}
. Consequently, there exists a constant
C\geq 0
, depending only on
\Omega,L
and
\beta
such that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}(\phi,\psi)\|_{L,\beta}\leq C\|(\phi,\psi)\|_{(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta})^{\prime}}

for all
(\phi,\psi)\in\mathcal{V}^{-1}_{L,\beta}
. We can thus define a solution operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}:\mathcal{V}^{-1}_{L,\beta}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}^{1}_{L,\beta},\quad(\phi,\psi)\mapsto\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}(\phi,\psi)=\big(\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}^{\Omega}(\phi,\psi),\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}^{\Gamma}(\phi,\psi)\big)

as well as an inner product and its induced norm on
\mathcal{V}^{-1}_{L,\beta}
by

\displaystyle\big((\phi,\psi),(\zeta,\xi)\big)_{L,\beta,\ast}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\big(\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}(\phi,\psi),\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}(\zeta,\xi)\big)_{L,\beta},

\displaystyle\|(\phi,\psi)\|_{L,\beta,\ast}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\big((\phi,\psi),(\phi,\psi)\big)_{L,\beta,\ast}^{1/2},

for
(\phi,\psi),(\zeta,\xi)\in\mathcal{V}^{-1}_{L,\beta}
. This is a norm on
\mathcal{V}^{-1}_{L,\beta}
, which is equivalent to
\|\cdot\|_{(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta})^{\prime}}
, see, e.g.,
[
32
, Theorem 3.3 and Corollary 3.5]
for a proof in the cases
L\in(0,\infty)
. In other cases, the proof can be carried out analogously.

Important tools

Throughout this paper, we will frequently use the bulk-surface Poincaré inequality, which has been established in
[

, Lemma A.1]
:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
K\in[0,\infty)
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
such that

. Then, there exists a constant
C_{P}>0
, depending only on

and

such that

\displaystyle\|(\zeta,\xi)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\leq C_{P}\|(\zeta,\xi)\|_{K,\alpha}

for all pairs

satisfying
\textnormal{mean}\left(\zeta,\xi\right)=0
.

Furthermore, we recall the following trace interpolation inequality (see, e.g.,
[
47
]
):

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=2,3
, with compact boundary. Then, there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\text{for all~}u\in H^{1}(\Omega).

2.3.
Main Assumptions.

The constants
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
appearing in System (
1.1
) are supposed to satisfy
\alpha\beta\left|\Omega\right|+\left|\Gamma\right|\neq 0
. We further assume that
-1\leq\alpha\leq 1
.
We require that the mobility functions
m_{\Omega},m_{\Gamma}\in C(\mathbb{R})
satisfy

\displaystyle 0<m_{\Omega}^{\ast}\leq m_{\Omega}(s)\leq M_{\Omega}^{\ast}\quad\text{and~}\quad 0<m_{\Gamma}^{\ast}\leq m_{\Gamma}(s)\leq M_{\Gamma}^{\ast}\quad\text{for all~}s\in\mathbb{R}

(2.1)

for some positive constants
m_{\Omega}^{\ast},M_{\Omega}^{\ast},m_{\Gamma}^{\ast},M_{\Gamma}^{\ast}>0
. The singular potentials
F
and
G
have the form

\displaystyle F(s)=F_{1}(s)+F_{2}(s)\quad\text{and~}\quad G(s)=G_{1}(s)+G_{2}(s),\quad\text{for all~}s\in[-1,1],

where
F_{1},G_{1}\in C([-1,1])\cap C^{2}(-1,1)
with

\displaystyle\lim_{s\searrow-1}F_{1}^{\prime}(s)=\lim_{s\searrow-1}G_{1}^{\prime}(s)=-\infty\quad\text{and~}\quad\lim_{s\nearrow 1}F_{1}^{\prime}(s)=\lim_{s\nearrow 1}G_{1}^{\prime}(s)=+\infty.

(2.2)

There further exist constants
\Theta_{\Omega},\Theta_{\Gamma}>0
such that

\displaystyle F_{1}^{\prime\prime}(s)\geq\Theta_{\Omega}\quad\text{and~}\quad G_{1}^{\prime\prime}(s)\geq\Theta_{\Gamma}\qquad\text{for all~}s\in(-1,1).

(2.3)

We extend
F_{1}
and
G_{1}
by defining
F_{1}(s)=G_{1}(s)=+\infty
for
s\not\in[-1,1]
and, without loss of generality, we assume that
F_{1}(0)=G_{1}(0)=0
and
F_{1}^{\prime}(0)=G_{1}^{\prime}(0)=0
.
Lastly, we assume that the singular part of the boundary potential dominates the singular part of the bulk potential in the following sense: there exist
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\left|F_{1}^{\prime}(\alpha s)\right|\leq\kappa_{1}\left|G_{1}^{\prime}(s)\right|+\kappa_{2}\qquad\text{for all~}s\in(-1,1),

(2.4)

where
\alpha
is given in (
1.1
).
Finally,
F_{2},G_{2}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, and their derivatives are globally Lipschitz continuous.

3.
Main results.

3.1.
Weak solutions

We start by introducing the notion of weak solutions.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
K,L\in[0,\infty]
,
(\bm{v},\bm{w})\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}_{\textnormal{div}}(\Omega)\times\mathbf{L}^{2}_{\tau}(\Gamma))
be given velocity fields, and let
\left(\phi_{0},\psi_{0}\right)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}
be an initial datum satisfying

\displaystyle\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1,\qquad\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\leq 1.

(3.1a)

In addition, we assume that

\displaystyle\beta\,\textnormal{mean}\left(\phi_{0},\psi_{0}\right)\in(-1,1),\quad\textnormal{mean}\left(\phi_{0},\psi_{0}\right)\in(-1,1),\quad\text{if~ }L\in[0,\infty),

(3.1b)

and

\displaystyle{\langle\phi_{0}\rangle}_{\Omega}\in(-1,1),\quad{\langle\psi_{0}\rangle}_{\Gamma}\in(-1,1),\quad\text{if~ }L=\infty.

(3.1c)

The set
(\phi,\psi,\mu,\theta)
is called a weak solution of System (
1.1
) on
[0,T]
if the following properties hold:

(i)

The functions
\phi,\psi,\mu
and
\theta
satisfy

\displaystyle\left(\phi,\psi\right)\in C([0,T];\mathcal{L}^{2})\cap H^{1}(0,T;(\mathcal{H}_{L,\beta}^{1})^{\prime})\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{H}_{K,\alpha}^{1}),

(3.2a)

\displaystyle\left(\mu,\theta\right)\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H}_{L,\beta}^{1}),

(3.2b)

\displaystyle\left(F^{\prime}(\phi),G^{\prime}(\psi)\right)\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{L}^{2}),

(3.2c)

and it holds

\left|\phi\right|<1\quad\text{a.e.~in $Q$}\quad\text{and}\quad\left|\psi\right|<1\quad\text{a.e.~on $\Sigma$}.

(3.3)

(ii)

The initial conditions are satisfied in the following sense:

\displaystyle\phi|_{t=0}=\phi_{0}\quad\text{a.e.~in }\Omega,\quad\text{and}\quad\psi|_{t=0}=\psi_{0}\quad\text{a.e.~on }\Gamma.

(iii)

The variational formulation

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\langle\left(\partial_{t}\phi,\partial_{t}\psi\right),\left(\zeta,\xi\right)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{L,\beta}^{1}}-\int_{\Omega}\phi\bm{v}\cdot\nabla\zeta\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Gamma}\psi\bm{w}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\xi\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&=-\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}(\phi)\nabla\mu\cdot\nabla\zeta\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\xi\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad-\sigma(L)\int_{\Gamma}(\beta\theta-\mu)(\beta\xi-\zeta)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma,\end{aligned}

(3.4a)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{\Omega}\mu\,\eta\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\theta\,\vartheta\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\eta+F^{\prime}(\phi)\eta\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\vartheta+G^{\prime}(\psi)\vartheta\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad+\sigma(K)\int_{\Gamma}(\alpha\psi-\phi)(\alpha\vartheta-\eta)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma,\end{aligned}

(3.4b)

holds a.e. on
[0,T]
for all
\left(\zeta,\xi\right)\in\mathcal{H}_{L,\beta}^{1},\left(\eta,\vartheta\right)\in\mathcal{H}_{K,\alpha}^{1}
.

(iv)

The functions
\phi
and
\psi
satisfy the mass conservation law

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\beta\int_{\Omega}\phi(t)\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\psi(t)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma=\beta\int_{\Omega}\phi_{0}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\psi_{0}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma,&\textnormal{if }L\in[0,\infty),\\
\int_{\Omega}\phi(t)\;\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\phi_{0}\;\mathrm{d}x\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\int_{\Gamma}\psi(t)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma=\int_{\Gamma}\psi_{0}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma,&\textnormal{if }L=\infty\end{cases}

(3.5)

for all
t\in[0,T]
.

(v)

The energy inequality

\displaystyle\begin{split}&E_{K}(\phi(t),\psi(t))+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}m_{\Omega}(\phi)\left|\nabla\mu\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\;\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}m_{\Gamma}(\psi)\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\;\mathrm{d}s\\
&\quad+\sigma(L)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}(\beta\theta-\mu)^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\;\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq E_{K}(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\phi\bm{v}\cdot\nabla\mu\;\mathrm{d}x\;\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}\psi\bm{w}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\;\mathrm{d}s\end{split}

(3.6)

holds for all
t\in[0,T]
.

For prescribed velocity fields such that

\bm{v}\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{3}_{\textnormal{div}}(\Omega))\quad\text{and}\quad\bm{w}\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2+\omega}_{\tau}(\Gamma))\quad\text{for some $\omega>0$}

(3.7)

the existence of a global-in-time weak solution has been proven in
[
34
, Theorem 3.4]
. In our first contribution, we show that this result holds true under the weaker assumptions

\bm{v}\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}_{\textnormal{div}}(\Omega))\quad\text{and}\quad\bm{w}\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}_{\tau}(\Gamma)).

(3.8)

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
K,L\in[0,\infty]
, let
\left(\phi_{0},\psi_{0}\right)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}
be an arbitrary initial datum satisfying (
3.1
), and let
(\bm{v},\bm{w})\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}_{\textnormal{div}}(\Omega)\times\mathbf{L}^{2}_{\tau}(\Gamma)))
be given velocity fields.
Then, System (
1.1
) admits at least one weak solution
(\phi,\psi,\mu,\theta)
in the sense of Definition
3.1
,
which has the additional regularities

\displaystyle\left(\phi,\psi\right)\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{W}^{2,6})\quad\text{and~}\quad(F^{\prime}(\phi),G^{\prime}(\psi))\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{L}^{6}),

and satisfies the equations

\displaystyle\mu=-\Delta\phi+F^{\prime}(\phi)

\displaystyle\text{a.e.~in }Q,

(3.9)

\displaystyle\theta=-\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi+G^{\prime}(\psi)+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi

\displaystyle\text{a.e.~on }\Sigma,

(3.10)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}K\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi=\alpha\psi-\phi&\text{if}\ K\in[0,\infty),\\
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi=0&\text{if}\ K=\infty\end{cases}

\displaystyle\text{a.e.~on }\Sigma.

(3.11)

Furthermore, if
L\in(0,\infty]
, any weak solutions satisfies the energy equality, i.e., (
3.6
) holds with an equality.

The proof of this theorem can be found in Section

.

and

for any weak solution.

.

Under the regularity assumption (

) on the velocity fields and provided that the mobility functions are constant, it was further shown in

-norm.

Theorem 3.5
.

Assume that the mobility functions
m_{\Omega}
and
m_{\Gamma}
are constant, let
K,L\in[0,\infty]
, let
(\phi_{0}^{1},\psi_{0}^{1})
,
(\phi_{0}^{2},\psi_{0}^{2})\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}
be two pairs of initial data, which satisfy

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\textnormal{mean}\left(\phi_{0}^{1},\psi_{0}^{1}\right)=\textnormal{mean}\left(\phi_{0}^{2},\psi_{0}^{2}\right)\quad&\text{if~}L\in[0,\infty),\\
{\langle\phi_{0}^{1}\rangle}_{\Omega}={\langle\phi_{0}^{2}\rangle}_{\Omega}\quad\text{and}\quad{\langle\psi_{0}^{1}\rangle}_{\Gamma}={\langle\psi_{0}^{2}\rangle}_{\Gamma}&\text{if~}L=\infty,\end{cases}

(3.12)

as well as (
3.1
), and let

\displaystyle(\bm{v}_{1},\bm{w}_{1})\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\textnormal{div}})\quad\text{and}\quad(\bm{v}_{2},\bm{w}_{2})\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{3}_{\textnormal{div}}(\Omega)\times\mathbf{L}^{2+\omega}_{\tau}(\Gamma))

for some
\omega>0
be prescribed velocity fields.
Moreover, let
(\phi_{1},\psi_{1},\mu_{1},\theta_{1})
and
(\phi_{2},\psi_{2},\mu_{2},\theta_{2})
be weak solutions in the sense of Definition
3.1
corresponding to
(\phi_{0}^{1},\psi_{0}^{1},\bm{v}_{1},\bm{w}_{1})
and
(\phi_{0}^{2},\psi_{0}^{2},\bm{v}_{2},\bm{w}_{2})
, respectively.
Then, the continuous dependence estimate

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\big\|\big(\phi_{1}(t)-\phi_{2}(t),\psi_{1}(t)-\psi_{2}(t)\big)\big\|_{L,\beta,\ast}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\big\|\big(\phi_{1}(\tau)-\phi_{2}(\tau),\psi_{1}(\tau)-\psi_{2}(\tau)\big)\big\|_{K,\alpha}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\tau\\[3.99994pt]
&\leq C_{0}\big\|\big(\phi_{0}^{1}-\phi_{0}^{2},\psi_{0}^{1}-\psi_{0}^{2}\big)\big\|_{L,\beta,\ast}^{2}\exp\left(C_{0}\int_{0}^{t}1+\|\left(\bm{v}_{2}(\tau),\bm{w}_{2}(\tau)\right)\|_{\mathbf{L}^{3}(\Omega)\times\mathbf{L}^{2+\omega}(\Gamma)}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\tau\right)\\

&\qquad\times\exp\left(C_{0}\int_{s}^{t}1+\|\left(\bm{v}_{2}(\tau),\bm{w}_{2}(\tau)\right)\|_{\mathbf{L}^{3}(\Omega)\times\mathbf{L}^{2+\omega}(\Gamma)}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\tau\right)\;\mathrm{d}s\end{split}

(3.13)

holds for almost all
t\in[0,T]
. Here, the constants denoted by
C_{0}
depend only on
\Omega,K,L,\alpha,\beta
and the Lipschitz constants of
F_{2}^{\prime}
and
G_{2}^{\prime}
.
In particular, this entails that the weak solution found in Theorem

is unique.

The proof of this theorem can be found in Section
6
.

3.2.
Strong solutions

We now deal with the well-posedness of strong solutions.
The global existence was obtained in
[
34
]
, where the velocity fields were required to satisfy the conditions

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \bm{v}&\in H^{1}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{6/5}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{L}_{\textnormal{div}}^{3}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)),\\
\bm{w}&\in H^{1}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{1+\omega}(\Gamma))\cap L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{L}_{\textnormal{div}}^{2}(\Gamma))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma))\end{aligned}\right.

(3.14)

for some
\omega>0
. In the following theorem, we show that, if
K\in[0,\infty)
and
L\in(0,\infty]
, well-posedness of strong solutions is obtained by assuming that the velocity fields are of Leray type, that is

\displaystyle(\bm{v},\bm{w})\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{L}_{\textnormal{div}}^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{1}).

(3.15)

This means that, in comparison with (
3.14
), we require more spatial regularity but less time regularity. The advantage of condition (
3.15
) is that these Leray type regularities are exactly what can be expected from a weak solution of a Navier–Stokes equation in the bulk or on the surface, respectively. Therefore, compared to the result from
[
34
]
, our new result seems more useful in the investigation of possible bulk-surface Navier–Stokes–Cahn–Hilliard models. Our result reads as follows.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
K\in[0,\infty)
and
L\in(0,\infty]
. We assume that
\Omega
is of class
C^{3}
, the pair
(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}
satisfies (
3.1
) and the mobility functions
m_{\Omega}
and
m_{\Gamma}
are constant. Moreover, let
(\bm{v},\bm{w})\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{L}_{\textnormal{div}}^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{1})
be given velocity fields. If
K=0
, we further assume that
\bm{v}|_{\Gamma}=\bm{w}
a.e. on
\Gamma
.
We further assume that the following compatibility condition holds:

(C)

There exists
\left(\mu_{0},\theta_{0}\right)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta}
such that for all
\left(\eta,\vartheta\right)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}
it holds

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{\Omega}\mu_{0}\eta\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\theta_{0}\vartheta\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi_{0}\cdot\nabla\eta+F^{\prime}(\phi_{0})\eta\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi_{0}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\vartheta+G^{\prime}(\psi_{0})\vartheta\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad+\sigma(K)\int_{\Gamma}(\alpha\psi_{0}-\phi_{0})(\alpha\vartheta-\eta)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma.\end{aligned}

Then, the corresponding unique weak solution
(\phi,\psi,\mu,\theta)
of System (
1.1
) obtained by Theorem
3.2
has the following regularity:

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}\phi,\partial_{t}\psi)

\displaystyle\in L^{\infty}(0,T;(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta})^{\prime})\cap L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{1}),

\displaystyle(\phi,\psi)

\displaystyle\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{W}^{2,6})\cap\Big(C(\overline{Q})\times C(\overline{\Sigma})\Big),

\displaystyle(\mu,\theta)

\displaystyle\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta})\cap L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{3}),

\displaystyle(F^{\prime}(\phi),G^{\prime}(\psi))

\displaystyle\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{L}^{\infty})\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{L}^{6}).

In particular,
(\phi,\psi,\mu,\theta)
is the unique strong solution of System (
1.1
), i.e., all equations of System (
1.1
) are fulfilled almost everywhere. Moreover, the following estimates hold

\displaystyle\begin{split}\esssup_{t\in(0,T)}\|(\mu(t),\theta(t))\|_{L,\beta}^{2}&\leq C_{0}\bigg(1+\|(\mu_{0},\theta_{0})\|_{L,\beta}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|(\bm{v},\bm{w})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}s\bigg)\\
&\quad\times\exp\bigg(C_{0}\int_{0}^{T}\|(\bm{v},\bm{w})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}\;\mathrm{d}s\bigg)\end{split}

(3.17)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\|(\partial_{t}\phi,\partial_{t}\psi)\|_{K,\alpha}^{2}+\|(\nabla\mu,\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}s\\
&\quad\leq C_{0}\bigg(1+\|(\mu_{0},\theta_{0})\|_{L,\beta}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|(\bm{v},\bm{w})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}s\bigg)\\
&\qquad\times\bigg(1+\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\|(\bm{v},\bm{w})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}s\bigg)\exp\left(C_{0}\int_{0}^{T}\|(\bm{v},\bm{w})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}s\right)\bigg).\end{split}

(3.18)

Here, the constants denoted by
C_{0}
depends only on
\Omega,K,L,\alpha,\beta,F,G,\|(\mu_{0},\theta_{0})\|_{L,\beta}
and the initial energy
E_{K}(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})
.

The proof of this theorem will be presented in Section
7
.

K\in[0,\infty)
and
L=0
. In this case, both
\phi
and
\psi
satisfy a strict separation property (see
[
34
, Theorem 3.11]
) and therefore, the proof can be carried out analogously, but without approximating the potentials and the initial data.

(b)

The compatibility condition
(C)
is, for example, fulfilled if initial data
(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})\in\mathcal{H}^{3}
with

(F^{\prime}(\phi_{0}),G^{\prime}(\psi_{0}))\in\mathcal{H}^{1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad K\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi_{0}=\alpha\psi_{0}-\phi_{0}\quad\text{on $\Gamma$}

are prescribed. In this case, we simply set

\displaystyle(\mu_{0},\theta_{0})

\displaystyle\coloneqq(-\Delta\phi_{0}+F^{\prime}(\phi_{0}),-\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi_{0}+G^{\prime}(\psi_{0})+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi_{0})\in\mathcal{H}^{1}.

4.
The subdifferential of the convex part of the free energy

In this section, let
K\in[0,\infty)
be arbitrary.
To prove the regularity results stated in Theorem
3.3
and Theorem
3.6
,
it makes sense to first gain more information about the energy functional
E_{K}
.
We recall that the potentials can be decomposed as
F=F_{1}+F_{2}
and
G=G_{1}+G_{2}
, where
F_{1}
and
G_{1}
are the singular convex parts and
F_{2}
and
G_{2}
can be interpreted as smooth perturbations.
To this end, we consider the functional
\widetilde{E}_{K}:\mathcal{L}^{2}\to(-\infty,\infty]
with

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{K}(u,v)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}+F_{1}(u)\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}v\right|^{2}+G_{1}(v)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma&\smash{\raisebox{-6.88889pt}{\text{for~}$(u,v)\in\mathrm{Dom}\,\tilde{E}_{K},$}}\\
\quad+\displaystyle\sigma(K)\int_{\Gamma}\frac{1}{2}(\alpha v-u)^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma,\\
+\infty,&\text{else},\end{cases}

where

\mathrm{Dom}\,\widetilde{E}_{K}=\big\{(u,v)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}:\left|u\right|\leq 1\text{~a.e.~on~}\Omega,\ \left|v\right|\leq 1\text{~a.e.~on~}\Gamma\big\}.

Here,
F_{1},G_{1}:[-1,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfy the assumptions (
2.2
)-(
2.4
). We first observe that the functional
\widetilde{E}_{K}
has the following properties.

Lemma 4.1
.

The functional
\widetilde{E}_{K}
as defined above is proper, lower semi-continuous and convex.

Proof.

It is obvious that
\widetilde{E}_{K}
is proper and convex since
F_{1}
and
G_{1}
are convex. The lower semi-continuity of
\widetilde{E}_{K}
can be shown by proceeding analogously to
[
2
, Lemma 4.1]
.
∎

The main goal of this section is to investigate the subdifferential

\partial\widetilde{E}_{K}
of
\widetilde{E}_{K}
. It can be formulated as an operator

\partial\widetilde{E}_{K}:\mathcal{L}^{2}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{L}^{2})
, where

\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{L}^{2})
denotes the power set of
\mathcal{L}^{2}
.
For any point
(u,v)\in\mathrm{Dom}\,\widetilde{E}_{K}
,
it holds
(\zeta,\xi)\in\partial\widetilde{E}_{K}(u,v)
if and only if

\displaystyle\big((\zeta,\xi),(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})-(u,v)\big)_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\leq\widetilde{E}_{K}(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})-\widetilde{E}_{K}(u,v)\qquad\text{for all~}(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in\mathcal{L}^{2}.

Moreover, the essential domain of
\partial\widetilde{E}_{K}
is given by

D(\partial\widetilde{E}_{K})=\big\{(u,v)\in\mathrm{Dom}\,\widetilde{E}_{K}:\partial\widetilde{E}_{K}(u,v)\neq\emptyset\big\}.

A characterization of the subdifferential
\partial\widetilde{E}_{K}
and its essential domain are provided by the following proposition.

Proposition 4.2
.

The subdifferential
\partial\widetilde{E}_{K}
of
\widetilde{E}_{K}
is a maximal monotone operator on
\mathcal{L}^{2}
, whose essential domain is given by

\displaystyle D(\partial\widetilde{E}_{K})=\{(u,v)\in\mathcal{H}^{2}:(F_{1}^{\prime}(u),G_{1}^{\prime}(v))\in\mathcal{L}^{2},\ K\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u=\alpha v-u\ \text{on~}\Gamma\}.

For any
(u,v)\in D(\partial\widetilde{E}_{K})
, it holds

\displaystyle\partial\widetilde{E}_{K}(u,v)=(-\Delta u+F_{1}^{\prime}(u),-\Delta_{\Gamma}v+G_{1}^{\prime}(u)+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u).

(4.1)

Before proving this proposition, we briefly recall the Moreau–Yosida regularization, which will be applied on the singular parts
F_{1}
and
G_{1}
, respectively. For any
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
, we define
F_{1,\lambda},G_{1,\lambda}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
as

\displaystyle F_{1,\lambda}(r)=\inf_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{\frac{1}{2\lambda}\left|r-s\right|^{2}+F_{1}(s)\right\},\qquad G_{1,\lambda}(r)=\inf_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{\frac{1}{2\lambda}\left|r-s\right|^{2}+G_{1}(s)\right\}

(4.2)

for all
r\in\mathbb{R}
, respectively. Then, the derivatives are given as

\displaystyle F_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(r)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\Big(r-(I+\lambda F_{1}^{\prime})^{-1}(r)\Big),\qquad G_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(r)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\Big(r-(I+\lambda G_{1}^{\prime})^{-1}(r)\Big)

(4.3)

for all
r\in\mathbb{R}
, respectively.
For any
\lambda>0
,
F_{\lambda}
has the following important properties:

(M1)

For every
\lambda>0
,
F_{1,\lambda}\in C^{2,1}_{\textup{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
with
F_{1,\lambda}(0)=F_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(0)=0
;

(M2)

F_{1,\lambda}
is convex with

\displaystyle F_{1,\lambda}^{\prime\prime}(r)\geq\frac{\Theta_{\Omega}}{1+\Theta_{\Omega}}\quad\text{for all~}r\in\mathbb{R};

(M3)

For every
\lambda>0
,
F_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}
is Lipschitz continuous on
\mathbb{R}
with constant
\frac{1}{\lambda}
.

(M4)

There exists
\overline{\lambda}\in(0,1)
and
C>0
such that

\displaystyle F_{1,\lambda}(r)\geq\frac{1}{4\overline{\lambda}}r^{2}-C\qquad\text{for all~}r\in\mathbb{R}\text{~and~}\lambda\in(0,\overline{\lambda});

(M5)

For every
\lambda>0
, it holds
F_{1,\lambda}(r)\leq F_{1}(r)
for all
r\in[-1,1]
.
Moreover, as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
, we have
F_{1,\lambda}(r)\rightarrow F_{1}(r)
for all
r\in[-1,1]
and
|F_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(r)|\rightarrow\left|F_{1}^{\prime}(r)\right|
for
r\in(-1,1)
.

Analogous properties hold true for
G_{1,\lambda}
instead of
F_{1,\lambda}
.
Additionally, as a consequence of the domination property (
2.4
),
we obtain an analogous estimate for the corresponding Yosida regularizations.
Namely, for any
\lambda>0
, it holds that

\displaystyle\left|F_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(\alpha r)\right|\leq\kappa_{1}\left|G_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(r)\right|+\kappa_{2}\qquad\text{for all~}r\in\mathbb{R}

(4.4)

(see, e.g.,
[
12
, Lemma 4.4]
), where
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}
are the same constants as in (
2.4
).

We now proceed with the proof of the above proposition.

Proof of Proposition
4.2
.

We follow the general scheme as in
[
7
, Proposition 2.9]
and consider the operator
\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{L}^{2}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}^{2}
that is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(u,v)

\displaystyle=(-\Delta u+F_{1}^{\prime}(u),-\Delta_{\Gamma}v+G_{1}^{\prime}(v)+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u)\quad\text{for all~}(u,v)\in D(\mathcal{A}),

where

\displaystyle D(\mathcal{A})

\displaystyle=\{(u,v)\in\mathcal{H}^{2}:(F_{1}^{\prime}(u),G_{1}^{\prime}(v))\in\mathcal{L}^{2},\ K\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u=\alpha v-u\ \text{on~}\Gamma\}.

We first show
\mathcal{A}\subset\partial\widetilde{E}_{K}
. Recalling the convexity properties of
F_{1}
and
G_{1}
(especially (
2.3
)) and using integration by parts, a straightforward computation yields

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{A}(u,v),(u,v)-(\zeta,\xi)\big)_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta u+F_{1}^{\prime}(u))(u-\zeta)+\int_{\Gamma}(-\Delta_{\Gamma}v+G_{1}^{\prime}(v)+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u)(v-\xi)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot(\nabla u-\nabla\zeta)+F_{1}^{\prime}(u)(u-\zeta)\;\mathrm{d}x+\sigma(K)\int_{\Gamma}(\alpha v-u)\big((\alpha v-u)-(\alpha\xi-\zeta)\big)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}v\cdot(\nabla_{\Gamma}v-\nabla_{\Gamma}\xi)+G_{1}^{\prime}(v)(v-\xi)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}+F_{1}(u)\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}v\right|^{2}+G_{1}(v)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma+\sigma(K)\int_{\Gamma}\frac{1}{2}(\alpha v-u)^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla\zeta\right|^{2}+F_{1}(\zeta)\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Gamma}\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\xi\right|^{2}+G_{1}(\xi)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma-\sigma(K)\int_{\Gamma}\frac{1}{2}(\alpha\xi-\zeta)^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle=\widetilde{E}_{K}(u,v)-\widetilde{E}_{K}(\zeta,\xi)

for all
(u,v)\in D(\mathcal{A}),(\zeta,\xi)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}
.
This directly implies
\mathcal{A}(u,v)\in\partial\widetilde{E}_{K}
for all
(u,v)\in D(\mathcal{A})
, which means that
\mathcal{A}\subset\partial\widetilde{E}_{K}
.

In order to show that
\mathcal{A}=\partial\widetilde{E}_{K}
, it suffices to prove that
\mathcal{A}
is a maximal monotone operator in
\mathcal{L}^{2}
, i.e.,
R(I+\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{L}^{2}
.
To this end, we fix
(f,g)\in\mathcal{L}^{2}
and consider the following system:

\displaystyle u-\Delta u+F_{1}^{\prime}(u)

\displaystyle=f

\displaystyle\qquad\text{in~}\Omega,

(4.5a)

\displaystyle v-\Delta_{\Gamma}v+G_{1}^{\prime}(v)+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u

\displaystyle=g

\displaystyle\qquad\text{on~}\Gamma,

(4.5b)

\displaystyle K\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u

\displaystyle=\alpha v-u

\displaystyle\qquad\text{on~}\Gamma.

(4.5c)

We approximate (
4.5
) by

\displaystyle u-\Delta u+F_{\lambda}^{\prime}(u)

\displaystyle=f

\displaystyle\qquad\text{in~}\Omega,

(4.6a)

\displaystyle v-\Delta_{\Gamma}v+G_{\lambda}^{\prime}(v)+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u

\displaystyle=g

\displaystyle\qquad\text{on~}\Gamma,

(4.6b)

\displaystyle K\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u

\displaystyle=\alpha v-u

\displaystyle\qquad\text{on~}\Gamma

(4.6c)

for any
\lambda>0
, where
F_{1,\lambda}
and
G_{1,\lambda}
are the Moreau–Yosida regularizations of the potentials
F_{1}
and
G_{1}
that were introduced above.
We start by showing that, for any
\lambda>0
, System (
4.6
) has a unique solution
(u_{\lambda},v_{\lambda})\in\mathcal{H}^{2}
. For this purpose, we fix an arbitrary
\lambda>0
and consider the operator
T_{\lambda}:\mathcal{L}^{2}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}^{2}
,
(u,v)\mapsto(\overline{u},\overline{v})
, where
(\overline{u},\overline{v})\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}
is the unique weak solution of

\displaystyle\lambda\overline{u}-\lambda\Delta\overline{u}+\overline{u}

\displaystyle=\lambda f+(1+\lambda F_{1}^{\prime})^{-1}(u)

\displaystyle\qquad\text{in~}\Omega,

(4.7a)

\displaystyle\lambda\overline{v}-\lambda\Delta_{\Gamma}\overline{v}+\lambda\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\overline{u}+\overline{v}

\displaystyle=\lambda g+(1+\lambda G_{1}^{\prime})^{-1}(v)

\displaystyle\qquad\text{on~}\Gamma,

(4.7b)

\displaystyle K\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\overline{u}

\displaystyle=\alpha\overline{v}-\overline{u}

\displaystyle\qquad\text{on~}\Gamma.

(4.7c)

Note that the existence of this unique weak solution
(\overline{u},\overline{v})
follows from the Lax–Milgram lemma (see also
[
32
]
).
Let now
(u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2})\in\mathcal{L}^{2}
be arbitrary, and let
(\overline{u}_{1},\overline{v}_{1})
and
(\overline{u}_{2},\overline{v}_{2})
denote the corresponding solutions of System (
4.7
), respectively.
Using integration by parts, we readily infer that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|(\overline{u}_{1},\overline{v}_{1})-(\overline{u}_{2},\overline{v}_{2})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}^{2}+\|(\alpha\overline{v}_{1}-\overline{u}_{1})-(\alpha\overline{v}_{2}-\overline{u}_{2})\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\|(\overline{u}_{1},\overline{v}_{1})-(\overline{u}_{2},\overline{v}_{2})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}\\
&=\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\Omega}\big((1+\lambda F_{1}^{\prime})^{-1}(u_{1})-(1+\lambda F_{1}^{\prime})^{-1}(u_{2})\big)(\overline{u}_{1}-\overline{u}_{2})\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\qquad+\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\Gamma}\big((1+\lambda G_{1}^{\prime})^{-1})(v_{1})-(1+\lambda G_{1}^{\prime})^{-1}(v_{2})\big)(\overline{v}_{1}-\overline{v}_{2})\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma.\end{split}

Invoking the estimate

\displaystyle\left|(1+\lambda F_{1}^{\prime})^{-1}(x)-(1+\lambda F_{1}^{\prime})^{-1}(y)\right|\leq\left|x-y\right|\quad\text{for all~}x,y\in\mathbb{R},

we find

\displaystyle\|T_{\lambda}(u_{1},v_{1})-T_{\lambda}(u_{2},v_{2})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\|T_{\lambda}(u_{1},v_{1})-T_{\lambda}(u_{2},v_{2})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\|(u_{1},v_{1})-(u_{2},v_{2})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}.

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\|T_{\lambda}(u_{1},v_{1})-T_{\lambda}(u_{2},v_{2})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\lambda}}\|(u_{1},v_{1})-(u_{2},v_{2})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}.

This means that the map
T_{\lambda}
is a contraction. Therefore, applying the Banach fixed-point theorem, we conclude the existence of a pair
(u,v)\in\mathcal{L}^{2}
such that
T_{\lambda}(u,v)=(u,v)
.
Recalling (
4.3
), this proves that System (
4.6
) has a unique weak solution
(u_{\lambda},v_{\lambda})\in\mathcal{H}^{1}
, which means that
(u_{\lambda},v_{\lambda})
satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}u_{\lambda}\zeta+\nabla u_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla\zeta+F_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(u_{\lambda})\zeta\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}v_{\lambda}\xi+\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\xi+G_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(v_{\lambda})\xi\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad+\sigma(K)\int_{\Gamma}(\alpha v_{\lambda}-u_{\lambda})(\alpha\xi-\zeta)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma=\int_{\Omega}f\zeta\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}g\xi\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\end{split}

(4.8)

for all
(\zeta,\xi)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}
. Now, testing (
4.8
) with
(u_{\lambda},v_{\lambda})
yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|u_{\lambda}\right|^{2}+\left|\nabla u_{\lambda}\right|^{2}+F^{\prime}_{1,\lambda}(u_{\lambda})u_{\lambda}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\left|v_{\lambda}\right|^{2}+\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{\lambda}\right|^{2}+G^{\prime}_{1,\lambda}(v_{\lambda})v_{\lambda}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle\quad+\sigma(K)\int_{\Gamma}(\alpha v_{\lambda}-u_{\lambda})^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma=\int_{\Omega}fu_{\lambda}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}gv_{\lambda}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma.

Since
F^{\prime}_{1,\lambda}(s)s\geq F_{1,\lambda}(s)
as well as
G^{\prime}_{1,\lambda}(s)s\geq G_{1,\lambda}(s)
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we infer

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|(u_{\lambda},v_{\lambda})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}+\|(F_{1,\lambda}(u_{\lambda}),G_{1,\lambda}(v_{\lambda}))\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}+\sigma(K)\|\alpha v_{\lambda}-u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}.\end{split}

(4.9)

In the following, the letter
C
will denote generic positive constants that are independent of
\lambda
.
Our next goal is to show that
(u_{\lambda},v_{\lambda})
belongs to
\mathcal{H}^{2}
and fulfills the estimate

\displaystyle\|(u_{\lambda},v_{\lambda})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+\|(F_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(u_{\lambda}),G_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(v_{\lambda}))\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\leq C\big(1+\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\big),

(4.10)

which is uniform with respect to
\lambda
.
For this purpose, we will consider the cases
K\in(0,\infty)
and
K=0
separately.

The case
K\in(0,\infty)
.
In this case, we may use
(F^{\prime}_{1,\lambda}(u_{\lambda}),G^{\prime}_{1,\lambda}(v_{\lambda}))
as a test function in (
4.8
). We obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|F^{\prime}_{1,\lambda}(u_{\lambda})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|G^{\prime}_{1,\lambda}(v_{\lambda})\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}F^{\prime}_{1,\lambda}(u_{\lambda})u_{\lambda}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}G^{\prime}_{1,\lambda}(v_{\lambda})v_{\lambda}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad+\int_{\Omega}F^{\prime\prime}_{1,\lambda}(u_{\lambda})\left|\nabla u_{\lambda}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}G^{\prime\prime}_{1,\lambda}(v_{\lambda})\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{\lambda}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma+\frac{1}{K}\int_{\Gamma}(\alpha v_{\lambda}-u_{\lambda})(\alpha G^{\prime}_{1,\lambda}(v_{\lambda})-F^{\prime}_{1,\lambda}(u_{\lambda}))\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\

Since
F^{\prime\prime}_{1,\lambda}
and

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\|(F^{\prime}_{1,\lambda}(u_{\lambda}),G^{\prime}_{1,\lambda}(v_{\lambda}))\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\leq\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}-\frac{1}{K}\int_{\Gamma}(\alpha v_{\lambda}-u_{\lambda})(\alpha G^{\prime}_{1,\lambda}(v_{\lambda})-F^{\prime}_{1,\lambda}(u_{\lambda}))\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma.

(4.11)

For the last term on the right-hand side, we use the monotonicity of

as well as the domination property (

) to deduce that

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{K}\int_{\Gamma}(\alpha v_{\lambda}-u_{\lambda})(\alpha G^{\prime}_{1,\lambda}(v_{\lambda})-F^{\prime}_{1,\lambda}(u_{\lambda}))\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{K}\int_{\Gamma}(\alpha v_{\lambda}-u_{\lambda})(F^{\prime}_{1,\lambda}(\alpha v_{\lambda})-F^{\prime}_{1,\lambda}(u_{\lambda}))\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma-\frac{1}{K}\int_{\Gamma}(\alpha v_{\lambda}-u_{\lambda})(\alpha G^{\prime}_{1,\lambda}(v_{\lambda})-F^{\prime}_{1,\lambda}(\alpha v_{\lambda}))\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{K}\int_{\Gamma}(\alpha v_{\lambda}-u_{\lambda})(\alpha G^{\prime}_{1,\lambda}(v_{\lambda})-F^{\prime}_{1,\lambda}(\alpha v_{\lambda}))\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\|G^{\prime}_{1,\lambda}(v_{\lambda})\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}+C(\|u_{\lambda}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|v_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}).

Using this inequality to estimate the right-hand side of (

\displaystyle\|(F^{\prime}_{1,\lambda}(u_{\lambda}),G^{\prime}_{1,\lambda}(v_{\lambda}))\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\leq C.

Finally, by regularity theory for elliptic systems with bulk-surface coupling (see
[
32

\displaystyle\|(u_{\lambda},v_{\lambda})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}\leq C\big(1+\|(f-u_{\lambda}-F^{\prime}_{1,\lambda}(u_{\lambda}),g-v_{\lambda}-G^{\prime}_{1,\lambda}(v_{\lambda}))\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\big)\leq C\big(1+\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\big).

Consequently, according to the Banach–Alaoglu theorem and the Aubin–Lions–Simon lemma, there exist functions

\chi_{\Gamma}
such that

\displaystyle\left(u_{\lambda},v_{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\left(u,v\right)\qquad

\displaystyle\text{weakly in~}\mathcal{H}^{2}\ \text{~and strongly in~}\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha},

\displaystyle\left(F^{\prime}_{1,\lambda}(u_{\lambda}),G^{\prime}_{1,\lambda}(v_{\lambda})\right)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\left(\chi,\chi_{\Gamma}\right)

\displaystyle\text{weakly in~}\mathcal{L}^{2}.

From these convergences, we readily infer that
(u,v)
satisfies

\displaystyle u-\Delta u+\chi

\displaystyle=f

\displaystyle\qquad\text{a.e.~in~}\Omega,

\displaystyle v-\Delta_{\Gamma}v+\chi_{\Gamma}+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u

\displaystyle=g

\displaystyle\qquad\text{a.e.~on~}\Gamma,

(4.17)

\displaystyle K\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u

\displaystyle=\alpha v-u

\displaystyle\qquad\text{a.e.~on~}\Gamma.

Similarly as in the proof of Theorem
3.2
, the identities
F_{1}^{\prime}(u)=\chi
and
G_{1}^{\prime}(v)=\chi_{\Gamma}
follow by means of the theory of maximal of monotone operators (see, e.g.,
[
7
, Proposition 1.1, p.42]
and also
[
24
, Section 5.2]
) together with the convergences

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\int_{\Omega}F_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(u_{\lambda})u_{\lambda}\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\chi u\;\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\int_{\Gamma}G_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(v_{\lambda})v_{\lambda}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}\chi_{\Gamma}v\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma.

In addition, this shows that
(F_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(u_{\lambda}),G_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(v_{\lambda}))\in\mathcal{L}^{2}
and from (
4.16
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\|(u,v)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+\|(F_{1}^{\prime}(u),G_{1}^{\prime}(v))\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\leq C\big(1+\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\big)

by means of weak lower semicontinuity.

We have thus shown that
(u,v)
is a solution of (
4.5
), and because
(f,g)\in\mathcal{L}^{2}
was arbitrary, we infer that
\mathcal{A}=\partial\widetilde{E}_{K}
. Hence, the proof is complete.
∎

5.
A bulk-surface elliptic system with singular nonlinearities

In this section, we establish well-posedness and regularity results for a bulk-surface elliptic system with singular nonlinearities. These regularity results will be the key ingredient in the proofs of Theorem
3.3
and Theorem
3.6
. The elliptic system reads as follows:

\displaystyle-\Delta u+F_{1}^{\prime}(u)

\displaystyle=f

\displaystyle\text{in $\Omega$},

(5.1a)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\Gamma}v+G_{1}^{\prime}(v)+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u

\displaystyle=g

\displaystyle\text{on $\Gamma$},

(5.1b)

\displaystyle K\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u

\displaystyle=\alpha v-u

\displaystyle\text{on $\Gamma$}.

(5.1c)

Here and in the remainder of this section, we consider
K\in[0,\infty)
, and
F_{1}
and
G_{1}
are assumed to satisfy the assumptions (
2.2
)-(
2.4
). We point out that results similar to those in this section can also be obtained for regular potentials
F_{1},G_{1}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
that have suitable polynomial growth (see also Remark
5.6
).

We now present a well-posedness result for (
5.1
) along with additional regularity results in the framework of Sobolev spaces. By means of the theory developed in Section
4
, we immediately infer the existence of a solution to System (
5.1
). This is stated by the following proposition.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
(f,g)\in\mathcal{L}^{2}
.
Then, there exists a solution
(u,v)\in\mathcal{H}^{2}\cap\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}
of System (
5.1
) such that

(F_{1}^{\prime}(u),G_{1}^{\prime}(v))\in\mathcal{L}^{2}
and the equations (
5.1a
)-(
5.1c
) are satisfied almost everywhere.
In particular, it holds
\left|u\right|<1
a.e. in
\Omega
and
\left|v\right|<1
a.e. on
\Gamma
.
Moreover, there exists a positive constant
C
depending only on
\Omega
,
K
,
\alpha
,
\Theta_{\Omega}
,
\Theta_{\Gamma}
,
\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}
such that

\displaystyle\|(u,v)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}+\|(F_{1}^{\prime}(u),G_{1}^{\prime}(v))\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\leq C\big(1+\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\big).

(5.2)

Proof.

Invoking formula (
4.1
) from Proposition
4.2
, recalling (
2.3
), and using integration by parts, it is straightforward to check that

\displaystyle(\partial\widetilde{E}_{K}(u,v)-\partial\widetilde{E}_{K}(\zeta,\xi),(u,v)-(\zeta,\xi))_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\geq\Theta_{*}\|(u,v)-(\zeta,\xi)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}

for all
(u,v),(\zeta,\xi)\in D(\partial\widetilde{E}_{K})
, where
\Theta_{*}=\min\{\Theta_{\Omega},\Theta_{\Gamma}\}
.
This means that
\partial\widetilde{E}_{K}
is strongly monotone and thus surjective (i.e.,
R(\partial\widetilde{E}_{K})=\mathcal{L}^{2}
). Hence, the existence of a strong solution is a direct consequence. Moreover, estimate (
5.2
) can be verified by proceeding analogously to the proof of Proposition
4.2
(especially the derivation of (
4.16
)) together with regularity theory for elliptic systems with bulk-surface coupling (see, e.g.,
[
32
]
).
∎

Next, in the following proposition, we prove a stability and uniqueness result for weak solutions of System (
5.1
). In particular, this proves that the solution of (
5.1
), which exists according to Proposition
5.1
, is unique.

Proposition 5.2
.

Assume that
(f_{1},g_{1}),(f_{2},g_{2})\in\mathcal{L}^{2}
, and let
(u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2})\in\mathcal{H}^{2}\cap\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}
be corresponding solutions of System (
5.1
), respectively. Then there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
\Omega
,
K
,
\alpha
,
\Theta_{\Omega}
and
\Theta_{\Gamma}
such that

\displaystyle\|(u_{1}-u_{2},v_{1}-v_{2})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}\leq C\|(f_{1}-f_{2},g_{1}-g_{2})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}.

(5.3)

In particular, this entails that the solution of System (
5.1
) is unique.

Proof.

For brevity, we define
(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})=(u_{1}-u_{2},v_{1}-v_{2})
and
(\tilde{f},\tilde{g})=(f_{1}-f_{2},g_{1}-g_{2})
. Taking the difference of the corresponding weak formulations, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla\zeta\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}(F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{1})-F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{2}))\zeta\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}\tilde{v}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\xi\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma+\int_{\Gamma}(G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{1})-G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{2}))\xi\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle\qquad+\sigma(K)\int_{\Gamma}(\alpha\tilde{v}-\tilde{u})(\alpha\xi-\zeta)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma=\int_{\Omega}\tilde{f}\zeta\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\tilde{g}\xi\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

holds for all
(\zeta,\xi)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}
. Using
(\zeta,\xi)=(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}
as a test function, and exploiting (
2.3
), we easily obtain

\displaystyle\|(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})\|_{K,\alpha}^{2}+\Theta_{\ast}\|(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq\int_{\Omega}\tilde{f}\tilde{u}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\tilde{g}\tilde{v}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma,

where, as above,
\Theta_{\ast}=\min\{\Theta_{\Omega},\Theta_{\Gamma}\}
.
Using Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities, we readily infer (
5.3
).
∎

Now we deal with some regularity properties, which will play an essential role in the sequel.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
(f,g)\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
with
2\leq p\leq\infty
and let
(u,v)\in\mathcal{H}^{2}\cap\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}
be the corresponding solution of System (
5.1
).
Then
(u,v)\in\mathcal{W}^{2,p}
and
(F_{1}^{\prime}(u),G_{1}^{\prime}(v))\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
and there exists a constant
C=C(p)>0
, such that

\displaystyle\|(u,v)\|_{\mathcal{W}^{2,p}}+\|(F_{1}^{\prime}(u),G_{1}^{\prime}(v))\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}\leq C\big(1+\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}+\gamma(K)\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}\big),

(5.4)

where
\gamma(K)=\mathbf{1}_{\{0\}}(K)
denotes the characteristic function of the set
\{0\}
. Moreover, if
(f,g)\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
,
there exists
\delta\in(0,1]
, depending on
\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}}
and
\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}
, such that

\displaystyle\left|u(x)\right|\leq 1-\delta\quad\text{for all~}x\in\overline{\Omega},\qquad\left|v(z)\right|\leq 1-\delta\quad\text{for all~}z\in\Gamma.

(5.5)

Proof.

In this proof, we assume without loss of generality that
\alpha\neq 0
. The case
\alpha=0
can be handled similarly and the computations are even easier. In addition, we can assume that
p\in(2,\infty]
as the case
p=2
is already covered by Proposition
5.1
.
For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we introduce the truncation

\displaystyle h_{k}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\quad h_{k}(s)=\begin{cases}-1+\frac{1}{k},&s<-1+\frac{1}{k},\\
s,&s\in\big[-1+\frac{1}{k},1-\frac{1}{k}\big],\\
1-\frac{1}{k},&s>1-\frac{1}{k},\end{cases}

which is globally Lipschitz continuous.
Then, we define
u_{k}=\alpha h_{k}\circ(\alpha^{-1}u)
,
v_{k}=h_{k}\circ v
. Since
(u,v)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}
, a well-known result on the composition of Lipschitz and Sobolev functions entails that
(u_{k},v_{k})\in\mathcal{H}^{1}
along with

\displaystyle\nabla u_{k}=\nabla u\,\mathbf{1}_{[-1+1/k,1-1/k]}(u),\qquad\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{k}=\nabla_{\Gamma}v\,\mathbf{1}_{[-1+1/k,1-1/k]}(v)

for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

First, we consider the case
p\in(2,\infty)
.
To verify the assertion, we now need to handle the cases
K\in(0,\infty)
and
K=0
separately. In the following, the letter
C
always denotes a non-negative constant independent of
k
, whose value may change from line to line.

The case
p\in(2,\infty)
and
K\in(0,\infty)
.

We multiply (
5.1a
) with
F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\left|F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\right|^{p-2}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and integrate over
\Omega
and perform an integration by parts. Moreover, we multiply (
5.1b
) with
G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\left|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\right|^{p-2}\in H^{1}(\Gamma)
, integrate over
\Gamma
and integrate by parts. Adding the resulting equations leads to

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(p-1)\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla u_{k}\left|F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\right|^{p-2}F_{1}^{\prime\prime}(u_{k})\;\mathrm{d}x+(p-1)\int_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}v\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}v_{k}\left|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\right|^{p-2}G_{1}^{\prime\prime}(v_{k})\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\qquad+\int_{\Omega}F_{1}^{\prime}(u)F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\left|F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\right|^{p-2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}G_{1}^{\prime}(v)G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\left|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\right|^{p-2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad=\int_{\Omega}fF_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\left|F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\right|^{p-2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}gG_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\left|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\right|^{p-2}\\
&\qquad-\sigma(K)\int_{\Gamma}(\alpha v-u)\big(\alpha G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\left|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\right|^{p-2}-F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\left|F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\right|^{p-2}\big)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma.\end{split}

(5.6)

We first notice that in view of the convexity of
F_{1}
and
G_{1}
, the first two terms on the left-hand side of (
5.6
) are non-negative. Thanks to (
2.3
),
F_{1}^{\prime}(s)
and
F_{1}^{\prime}(\alpha h_{k}(\alpha^{-1}s))
, as well
G_{1}^{\prime}(s)
and
G_{1}^{\prime}(h_{k}(s))
, have the same sign for all
s\in(-1,1)
, which yields

\displaystyle F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq F_{1}^{\prime}(u)F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})

\displaystyle\qquad\text{a.e.~in~}\Omega,

\displaystyle G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq G_{1}^{\prime}(v)G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})

\displaystyle\qquad\text{a.e.~on~}\Gamma.

Consequently, it holds

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\left|F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\right|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\left|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\right|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad\leq\int_{\Omega}F_{1}^{\prime}(u)F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\left|F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\right|^{p-2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}G_{1}^{\prime}(v)G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\left|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\right|^{p-2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma.\end{split}

(5.7)

For the last term on the right-hand side of (
5.6
), we exploit the domination property (
2.4
) as well as the monotonicity of the function
\mathbb{R}\ni s\mapsto F_{1}^{\prime}(\alpha h_{k}(s))\left|F_{1}^{\prime}(\alpha h_{k}(s))\right|^{p-2}
to infer

\displaystyle\begin{split}&-\sigma(K)\int_{\Gamma}(\alpha v-u)\big(\alpha G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\left|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\right|^{p-2}-F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\left|F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\right|^{p-2}\big)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad=-\sigma(K)\int_{\Gamma}(\alpha v-u)\big(F_{1}^{\prime}(\alpha v_{k})\left|F_{1}^{\prime}(\alpha v_{k})\right|^{p-2}-F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\left|F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\right|^{p-2}\big)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\qquad-\sigma(K)\int_{\Gamma}(\alpha v-u)\big(\alpha G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\left|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\right|^{p-2}-F_{1}^{\prime}(\alpha v_{k})\left|F_{1}^{\prime}(\alpha v_{k})\right|^{p-2}\big)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad\leq 2\sigma(K)\Big(\int_{\Gamma}\left|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\right|^{p-1}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma+2^{p-2}\kappa_{1}^{p-2}\int_{\Gamma}\left|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\right|^{p-1}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma+2^{p-2}\kappa_{2}^{p-1}\left|\Gamma\right|\Big).\end{split}

(5.8)

Here, we additionally made use of Minkowski’s inequality

\displaystyle\left|a+b\right|^{p-1}\leq 2^{p-2}\big(\left|a\right|^{p-1}+\left|b\right|^{p-1}\big)\qquad\text{for any~}a,b\in\mathbb{R}.

Combining (
5.6
) with (
5.7
) and (
5.8
), and using Hölder’s and Young’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\|(F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k}),G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k}))\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}\leq C\big(1+\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}\big)

(5.9)

for some constant
C=C(p)>0
independent of
k
. For more details, we refer to the proof of
[
34
, Proposition 6.1]
since similar estimates have been carried out there. Finally, by means of Fatou’s lemma, we end up with

\displaystyle\|(F_{1}^{\prime}(u),G_{1}^{\prime}(v))\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}\leq C\big(1+\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}\big),

and and application of elliptic regularity theory for bulk-surface systems readily implies (
5.4
).

The case
p\in(2,\infty)
and
K=0
.
First, we consider
2<p\leq 4
. Performing the same testing procedure as in the case
K\in(0,\infty)
and arguing again with the convexity of
F_{1}
and
G_{1}
, we deduce

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\left|F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\right|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\left|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\right|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad\leq\int_{\Omega}fF_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\left|F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\right|^{p-2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}gG_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\left|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\right|^{p-2}\\
&\qquad-\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\big(\alpha G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\left|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\right|^{p-2}-F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\left|F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\right|^{p-2}\big)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma.\end{split}

(5.10)

We note that this equation differs from (
5.6
) only in the last term on the right-hand side, which now contains the normal derivative
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u
. Therefore, it has to be treated differently. First, due the regularity
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)
, the trace embedding
H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{4}(\Gamma)
implies that
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\in L^{4}(\Gamma)
. Then, exploiting the trace relation
u_{k}=\alpha v_{k}
a.e. on
\Gamma
in combination with the domination property (
2.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle-\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\big(\alpha G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\left|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\right|^{p-2}-F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\left|F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\right|^{p-2}\big)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle\quad=-\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\big(\alpha G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\left|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\right|^{p-2}-F_{1}^{\prime}(\alpha v_{k})\left|F_{1}^{\prime}(\alpha v_{k})\right|^{p-2}\big)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int_{\Gamma}\left|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\right|\big((1+2^{p-2}\kappa_{1}^{p-1})\left|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\right|^{p-1}+2^{p-2}\kappa_{2}^{p-1}\big)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma.

Hence, in a similar manner to the case
K\in(0,\infty)
we can derive the estimate

\displaystyle\|(F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k}),G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k}))\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}\leq C\big(1+\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}+\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}\big),

where the constant
C=C(p)>0
is independent of
k
.
This proves the desired claim for
p\in(2,4]
by means of Fatou’s lemma.
To establish the desired result also in the case
p\in(4,\infty)
, we employ elliptic regularity theory (see
[
34
, Proposition A.1]
) to infer
(u,v)\in\mathcal{W}^{2,4}
. Then, using again the trace theorem, we even have
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\in W^{3/4,4}(\Gamma)\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Gamma)
. This allows us to choose
p\in(4,\infty)
in our previous calculations, which verifies

\displaystyle\|(F_{1}^{\prime}(u),G_{1}^{\prime}(v))\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}\leq C\big(1+\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}+\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\|_{L^{p}(\Gamma)}\big)

in all cases
p\in(2,\infty)
. Applying once more
[
34
, Proposition A.1]
, we readily deduce (
5.4
).

In summary, we conclude that (
5.4
) is verified for all choices
K\in[0,\infty)
as long as
p\in[2,\infty)
.

We still have to prove (
5.4
) also for
p=\infty
. To this end, we now assume that
(f,g)\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
.
Then, our previous calculations show that
(F_{1}^{\prime}(u),G_{1}^{\prime}(v))\in\mathcal{L}^{r}
for any
r\in(2,\infty)
, and thus, again by elliptic regularity theory, we have
(u,v)\in\mathcal{W}^{2,r}
for any
r\in(2,\infty)
. In particular, this entails that
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma)
. First we perform some preliminary computations. Fix an arbitrary
r\in(2,\infty)
. Testing equation (
5.1b
)
with
G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\left|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\right|^{r-2}
and proceeding similarly as in the case
p\in(2,\infty)
above, we see that

\displaystyle\|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\|_{L^{r}(\Gamma)}^{r}\leq\big(1+\|g\|_{L^{r}(\Gamma)}+\gamma(K)\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\|_{L^{r}(\Gamma)}\big)\|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\|_{L^{r}(\Gamma)}^{r-1}.

Since
g,\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma)
, this directly implies

\displaystyle\|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\|_{L^{r}(\Gamma)}\leq(1+\left|\Gamma\right|)\big(1+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}+\gamma(K)\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\big)<\infty.

(5.11)

We thus conclude that

\displaystyle\|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\leq(1+\left|\Gamma\right|)\big(1+\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}}+\gamma(K)\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\big)<\infty.

(5.12)

Since
\left|v_{k}\right|<1
and
\left|v\right|<1
a.e. on
\Gamma
, we have

\displaystyle G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\rightarrow G_{1}^{\prime}(v)\qquad\text{a.e.~on~}\Gamma\ \text{as~}k\rightarrow\infty.

In combination with (
5.12
) this proves that

\displaystyle\|G_{1}^{\prime}(v)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\leq(1+\left|\Gamma\right|)\big(1+\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}}+\gamma(K)\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\big)<\infty.

(5.13)

To prove the corresponding estimate for
F_{1}^{\prime}(u)
, we again have to distinguish two cases:
K\in(0,\infty)
and
K=0
.

The case
p=\infty
and
K\in(0,\infty)
.

We first obtain for all
r\in(2,\infty)
the estimate

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\left|F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\right|^{r}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\left|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\right|^{r}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad\leq\int_{\Omega}fF_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\left|F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\right|^{r-2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}gG_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\left|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\right|^{r-2}\\
&\qquad-\sigma(K)\int_{\Gamma}(\alpha v-u)\big(\alpha G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\left|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\right|^{r-2}-F_{1}^{\prime}(\alpha v_{k})\left|F_{1}^{\prime}(\alpha v_{k})\right|^{r-2}\big)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma.\end{split}

(5.14)

by combining (
5.6
) and (
5.7
).
Using the domination property (
2.4
), the last term on the right-hand side of (
5.14
) can be estimated as

\displaystyle-\sigma(K)\int_{\Gamma}(\alpha v-u)\big(\alpha G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\left|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\right|^{r-2}-F_{1}^{\prime}(\alpha v_{k})\left|F_{1}^{\prime}(\alpha v_{k})\right|^{r-2}\big)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle\quad\leq 2\sigma(K)\left|\Gamma\right|\|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}^{r-1}+2^{r-1}\kappa_{1}^{r-1}\sigma(K)\left|\Gamma\right|\|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}^{r-1}+2^{r-1}\kappa_{2}^{r-1}\sigma(K)\left|\Gamma\right|.

Here, we also used that
\left|u\right|<1
a.e. in
\Omega
and
\left|v\right|<1
a.e. on
\Gamma
.
Consequently, by means of Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities, we conclude that

\displaystyle\|F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}^{r}+\|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\|_{L^{r}(\Gamma)}^{r}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|f\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\|F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}^{r-1}+\|g\|_{L^{r}(\Gamma)}\|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\|_{L^{r}(\Gamma)}^{r-1}

\displaystyle\qquad+2\sigma(K)\left|\Gamma\right|\big(1+2^{r-2}\kappa_{1}^{r-1}\big)\|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}^{r-1}+\sigma(K)\left|\Gamma\right|2^{r-1}\kappa_{2}^{r-1}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{2}\|F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}^{r}+\frac{1}{2}\|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\|_{L^{r}(\Gamma)}^{r}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{2(r-1)}{r}\right)^{r-1}\big(\left|\Omega\right|+\left|\Gamma\right|\big)\,\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}}^{r}

\displaystyle\qquad+2\sigma(K)\left|\Gamma\right|\big(1+2^{r-2}\kappa_{1}^{r-1}\big)(1+\left|\Gamma\right|)^{r-1}(1+\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}})^{r}+\sigma(K)\left|\Gamma\right|2^{r-1}\kappa_{2}^{r-1}

Thus, after absorption of the first two terms on the right-hand side by the left-hand side, we take the
r
-th root on both sides of the resulting inequality to deduce

\displaystyle\|F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}+\|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\|_{L^{r}(\Gamma)}\leq C\big(1+\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}}\big)

for a constant
C>0
that is independent of
r
. We thus infer

\displaystyle\|F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\leq C\big(1+\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}}\big).

By means of similar arguments as already used before, we conclude that

\displaystyle\|(F_{1}^{\prime}(u),G_{1}^{\prime}(v))\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}}\leq C\big(1+\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}}\big),

and (
5.4
) follows from another application of
[
34
, Proposition A.1]
.

The case
p=\infty
and
K=0
.
We test equation (
5.1a
) once more with
F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\left|F_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\right|^{r-2}
for any
r\in(2,\infty)
and integrate the resulting equation. After integrating by parts, we find that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\right|^{r}\;\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{\Omega}\left|f\right|\left|F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\right|^{r-1}\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\,F_{1}^{\prime}(\alpha v_{k})\left|F_{1}^{\prime}(\alpha v_{k})\right|^{r-2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma.

Using Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}^{r}&\leq\|f\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\|F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}^{r-1}+\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\|_{L^{r}(\Gamma)}\|F_{1}^{\prime}(\alpha v_{k})\|_{L^{r}(\Gamma)}^{r-1}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\|F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}^{r}+\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{2(r-1)}{r}\right)^{r-1}\|f\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}^{r}\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{r}\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\|_{L^{r}(\Gamma)}^{r}+\frac{r-1}{r}\|F_{1}^{\prime}(\alpha v_{k})\|_{L^{r}(\Gamma)}^{r}.\end{split}

(5.15)

Thus, we deduce that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}^{r}&\leq\frac{2}{r}\left(\frac{2(r-1)}{r}\right)^{r-1}\|f\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}^{r}+\frac{2}{r}\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\|_{L^{r}(\Gamma)}^{r}+\frac{2(r-1)}{r}\|F_{1}^{\prime}(\alpha v_{k})\|_{L^{r}(\Gamma)}^{r}\\
&\leq\frac{2}{r}\left(\frac{2(r-1)}{r}\right)^{r-1}(1+\left|\Omega\right|)\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{r}+\frac{2}{r}(1+\left|\Gamma\right|)\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}^{r}\\
&\quad+\frac{2(r-1)}{r}(1+\left|\Gamma\right|)\|F_{1}^{\prime}(\alpha v_{k})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}^{r}\end{split}

(5.16)

Due to the domination property (
2.4
) and estimate (
5.12
), we infer that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|F_{1}^{\prime}(\alpha v_{k})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}&\leq\kappa_{1}\|G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}+\kappa_{2}\\
&\leq C\big(1+\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\big).\end{split}

(5.17)

Hence, after taking the
r
-th root on both sides of (
5.16
), we use (
5.17
) to end up with

\displaystyle\|F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\leq C\big(1+\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\big)<\infty,

where the constant
C>0
is independent of

.
Using Fatou’s lemma, we get

\displaystyle\|F_{1}^{\prime}(u)\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\leq C\big(1+\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{r}}+\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\|_{L^{r}(\Gamma)}\big)<\infty.

Then, sending

, we conclude that

\displaystyle\|F_{1}^{\prime}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\big(1+\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\big)<\infty,

and in combination with (
5.13
) and
[
34
, Proposition A.1]
, this verifies (
5.4
) in the case
p=\infty
and
K=0
.

In summary, we have shown that
(F_{1}^{\prime}(u),G_{1}^{\prime}(v))\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
along with estimate (
5.4
) for all
p\in[2,\infty]
in all cases

.
We further observe that
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow C(\overline{\Omega})
and

. Hence, if

, the separation property (

) follows from Assumption

.
The proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
(f,g)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}
and let
(u,v)\in\mathcal{H}^{2}\cap\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}
be the corresponding solution of System (
5.1
) given by Proposition

. Then, there exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on
\Omega
,
K
,
\alpha
,
F
and

such that the following inequalities hold:

(a)

If
K=0
, then

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|(-\Delta u,-\Delta_{\Gamma}v+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}\\
&\quad\leq C\big(1+\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}\big)\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{1/2}+\|(\nabla f,\nabla_{\Gamma}g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\|(\nabla u,\nabla_{\Gamma}v)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}.\end{split}

(b)

If
K\in(0,\infty)
, then

\displaystyle\|(-\Delta u,-\Delta_{\Gamma}v+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq C\big(1+\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}\big)+\|(\nabla f,\nabla_{\Gamma}g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\|(\nabla u,\nabla_{\Gamma}v)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}.

Proof.

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
u_{k}
and
v_{k}
again denote the truncation of
u
and
v
, respectively, as in the proof of Proposition
5.3
. We start by multiplying (
5.1a
) by
-\Delta u
and (
5.1b
) by
-\Delta_{\Gamma}v+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u
. Then, integrating the resulting equations over
\Omega
and
\Gamma
, respectively, and adding them, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\left|\Delta u\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\left|-\Delta_{\Gamma}v+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad=\int_{\Omega}f(-\Delta u)\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}g(-\Delta_{\Gamma}v+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\qquad-\int_{\Omega}F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})(-\Delta u)-\int_{\Omega}(F_{1}^{\prime}(u)-F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k}))(-\Delta u)\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\qquad-\int_{\Gamma}G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})(-\Delta_{\Gamma}v+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma-\int_{\Gamma}(G_{1}^{\prime}(v)-G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k}))(-\Delta_{\Gamma}v+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma.\end{split}

(5.18)

Arguing as in the proof of Proposition
5.3
, we deduce that

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})(-\Delta u)\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Gamma}G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})(-\Delta_{\Gamma}v+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle\qquad=-\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla u_{k}F_{1}^{\prime\prime}(u_{k})\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Gamma}\nabla v\cdot\nabla v_{k}G_{1}^{\prime\prime}(v_{k})\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma-\int_{\Gamma}\big(\alpha G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})-F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\big)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int_{\Gamma}\big(\alpha G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})-F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\big)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma.

After integrating by parts in the first two terms on the right-hand side of (
5.18
), we use Hölder’s inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\left|\Delta u\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\left|-\Delta_{\Gamma}v+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad\leq\|(\nabla f,\nabla_{\Gamma}g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\|(\nabla u,\nabla_{\Gamma}v)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}+\int_{\Gamma}\big(\alpha g-f)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\qquad-\int_{\Omega}(F_{1}^{\prime}(u)-F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k}))(-\Delta u)\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Gamma}(G_{1}^{\prime}(v)-G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k}))(-\Delta_{\Gamma}v+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\qquad+\int_{\Gamma}\big(\alpha G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})-F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\big)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma.\end{split}

(5.19)

We point out that the third and the fourth summand on the right-hand side of this inequality vanish as
k\rightarrow\infty
. We now handle the cases
K=0
and

separately.

If

a.e. on

and Proposition

to infer that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&-\int_{\Gamma}\big(\alpha G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})-F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\big)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad\leq C\|\big(F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k}),G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})\big)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\\
&\quad\leq C\big(1+\|\big(F_{1}^{\prime}(u),G_{1}^{\prime}(v)\big)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\big)\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\\
&\quad\leq C\big(1+\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\big)\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{1/2}\end{split}

(5.20)

if
k
is sufficiently large.
Analogously, again using Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\big(\alpha g-f\big)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\leq\|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{1/2}.

(5.21)

Hence, the claim follows by combining (

5.20
) and (
5.21
), and passing to the limit

.

If

) to reformulate the normal derivative

\displaystyle\begin{split}&-\int_{\Gamma}\big(\alpha G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})-F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\big)\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma=-\frac{1}{K}\int_{\Gamma}(\alpha v-u)\big(\alpha G_{1}^{\prime}(v_{k})-F_{1}^{\prime}(u_{k})\big)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\

(5.23)

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

The existence of a global-in-time weak solution was proven in
[
34
, Theorem 3.4]
by a compactness method relying on the Yosida regularization of the singular potentials. Throughout this proof, the stronger assumption (

) was needed as it cannot be ensured that the approximate phase-fields have the property (

) (or any similar property).
Nonetheless, as we already know that the weak solution
(\phi,\psi)
fulfills (
3.3
), the regularity assumption on the velocity fields can be weakened a posteriori. As most of this can be done by proceeding similarly as in the proof of
[
34
, Theorem 3.4]
, we only outline the most important steps.

Let
(\bm{v},\bm{w})\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}_{\textnormal{div}}(\Omega)\times\mathbf{L}^{2}_{\tau}(\Gamma))
be the velocity fields. Consider a sequence

\{(\bm{v}_{k},\bm{w}_{k})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying (
3.7
) such that

\displaystyle(\bm{v}_{k},\bm{w}_{k})\rightarrow(\bm{v},\bm{w})\qquad\text{strongly in~}L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{L}^{2})\text{~as~}k\to\infty.

(6.1)

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, according to
[
34
, Theorem 3.4]
, there exists an approximating weak solution
(\phi_{k},\psi_{k},\mu_{k},\theta_{k})
to (
1.1
) in the sense of Definition
3.1
with
(\bm{v},\bm{w})
replaced by
(\bm{v}_{k},\bm{w}_{k})
. Using
\left|\phi_{k}\right|<1
a.e. in
Q
and
\left|\psi_{k}\right|<1
a.e. on
\Sigma
, we can follow the line of argument in the proof of
[
34
, Theorem 3.4]
to derive the uniform estimate

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|(\phi_{k},\psi_{k})\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{1})}+\|(\phi_{k},\psi_{k})\|_{H^{1}(0,T;(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta})^{\prime})}\\
&\quad+\|(\mu_{k},\theta_{k})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{1})}+\big\|\big(F_{1}^{\prime}(\phi_{k}),G_{1}^{\prime}(\psi_{k})\big)\big\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{L}^{2})}\leq C,\end{split}

where
C
denotes a non-negative constant that does not depend on
k
.
Hence, by means of the Banach–Alaoglu theorem and the Aubin–Lions–Simon lemma, we infer that there exist functions
\phi,\psi,\mu,\theta,f,g
such that

\displaystyle(\phi_{k},\psi_{k})

\displaystyle\to(\phi,\psi)

weakly-
\ast
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha})
,
weakly in
H^{1}(0,T;(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta})^{\prime})

and strongly in

,

\displaystyle(\mu_{k},\theta_{k})

\displaystyle\to(\mu,\theta)

weakly in
L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta})
,

\displaystyle\big(F_{1}^{\prime}(\phi_{k}),G_{1}^{\prime}(\psi_{k})\big)

\displaystyle\to(f,g)

weakly in
L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{L}^{2})
,

as

, up to subsequence extraction.
In particular, the quadruplet
(\phi,\psi,\mu,\theta)
has the regularities demanded in (
3.2
). Via the weak-strong convergence principle, we further deduce

\displaystyle\underset{k\to\infty}{\lim}\,\int_{\Omega}F_{1}^{\prime}(\phi_{k})\phi_{k}\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}f\phi\;\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle\underset{k\to\infty}{\lim}\,\int_{\Gamma}G_{1}^{\prime}(\psi_{k})\psi_{k}\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}g\psi\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma.

As
F_{1}^{\prime}
and
G_{1}^{\prime}
are single-valued maximal monotone operators on their effective domains

, we can use the theory of monotone operators (see
[
6
, Proposition 1.1, p. 42]
and also
[

, Section 5.2]
) to conclude

\phi\in D(F_{1}^{\prime})=(-1,1)\quad\text{a.e.~in $Q$}\quad\text{and}\quad\psi\in D(G_{1}^{\prime})=(-1,1)\quad\text{a.e.~on $\Sigma$}

as well as

By the aforementioned convergences, it is now straightforward to pass to the limit in the weak formulation (
3.4
) and to verify the mass conservation law (
3.5
) and the energy inequality (
3.6
). This proves that the quadruplet
(\phi,\psi,\mu,\theta)
is a weak solution to the velocity fields
(\bm{v},\bm{w})
in the sense of Definition
3.1
. As a last step, we have to show that if
L\in(0,\infty]
, any weak solution satisfies the energy equality. To this end, we consider again the convex part of the free energy
\widetilde{E}_{K}
as defined in Chapter
4
. Then, according to Lemma
4.1
,
\widetilde{E}_{K}
is proper, convex and lower semi-continuous. Owing to
[
48
, Lemma 4.1]
in combination with Proposition
4.2
, we deduce that
[0,T]\ni t\mapsto\widetilde{E}_{K}(\phi(t),\psi(t))
is absolutely continuous and it holds

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\widetilde{E}_{K}(\phi,\psi)

\displaystyle=\big<(\partial_{t}\phi,\partial_{t}\psi),(-\Delta\phi+F_{1}^{\prime}(\phi),-\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi+G_{1}^{\prime}(\psi)+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi)\big>_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}

\displaystyle=\big<(\partial_{t}\phi,\partial_{t}\psi),(\mu-F_{2}^{\prime}(\phi),\theta-G_{2}^{\prime}(\psi))\big>_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}

almost everywhere in
(0,T)
. As a consequence, since
F
is bounded and
\phi\in C([0,T];\mathcal{L}^{2})
, the Lebesgue theorem entails that
\int_{\Omega}F(\phi(\cdot))\;\mathrm{d}x\in C([0,T];\mathcal{L}^{2})
, which, in turn, implies that
\phi\in C([0,T];H^{1}(\Omega))
. Analogous reasoning provides that
\psi\in C([0,T];H^{1}(\Gamma))
. Hence, testing (
3.4a
) with
(\mu,\theta)
and exploiting the standard chain rule in
L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{1})\cap H^{1}(0,T;(\mathcal{H}^{1})^{\prime})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E_{K}(\phi,\psi)+\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\mu\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma+\chi(L)\int_{\Gamma}(\beta\theta-\mu)^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\Omega}\phi\bm{v}\cdot\nabla\mu\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Gamma}\psi\bm{w}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma=0,

a.e. on
(0,T)
, from which the energy equality follows.
∎

Next, as a direct consequence of the results established in Section
5
, we can prove Theorem
3.3
.

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

The claim directly follows from combining the inequalities obtained in Corollary
5.4
with the regularities (
3.2a
)-(
3.2b
) of the considered weak solution.
∎

Lastly, we present the proof of the continuous dependence estimate.

Proof of Theorem
3.5
.

We consider two weak solutions
(\phi_{1},\psi_{1},\mu_{1},\theta_{1})
and
(\phi_{2},\psi_{2},\mu_{2},\theta_{2})
corresponding to the initial data
(\phi_{0}^{1},\psi_{0}^{1}),(\phi_{0}^{2},\psi_{0}^{2})\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}
and the velocity fields

\displaystyle(\bm{v}_{1},\bm{w}_{1})\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\textnormal{div}})\quad\text{and}\quad(\bm{v}_{2},\bm{w}_{2})\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{3}_{\textnormal{div}}(\Omega)\times\mathbf{L}^{2+\omega}_{\tau}(\Gamma)).

Then we set

\displaystyle(\phi,\psi,\mu,\theta,\bm{v},\bm{w})\coloneqq(\phi_{1}-\phi_{2},\psi_{1}-\psi_{2},\mu_{1}-\mu_{2},\theta_{1}-\theta_{2},\bm{v}_{1}-\bm{v}_{2},\bm{w}_{1}-\bm{w}_{2}).

Then, repeating the line of argument presented in
[
35
, Theorem 3.5]
combined with the one in
[
34
, Theorem 3.6]
, we can show that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\frac{1}{2}\|(\phi,\psi)\|_{L,\beta,\ast}^{2}+\frac{3}{4}\|(\phi,\psi)\|_{K,\alpha}^{2}\\
&\quad\leq C\|(\phi,\psi)\|_{L,\beta,\ast}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}\big(\phi_{1}\bm{v}+\phi\bm{v}_{2}\big)\cdot\nabla\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}^{\Omega}(\phi,\psi)\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\big(\psi_{1}\bm{w}+\psi\bm{w}_{2}\big)\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}^{\Gamma}(\phi,\psi)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma.\end{split}

(6.2)

Now, exploiting the facts that
\left|\phi_{1}\right|<1
a.e. in
Q
as well as
\left|\psi_{1}\right|<1
a.e. on
\Sigma
, we can estimate the last two terms in (

), using the Sobolev inequality and the bulk-surface Poincaré inequality, as

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\big(\phi_{1}\bm{v}+\phi\bm{v}_{2}\big)\cdot\nabla\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}^{\Omega}(\phi,\psi)\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\big(\psi_{1}\bm{w}+\psi\bm{w}_{2}\big)\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}^{\Gamma}(\phi,\psi)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad\leq\|(\bm{v},\bm{w})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\|\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}(\phi,\psi)\|_{L,\beta,\ast}+C\|(\phi,\psi)\|_{K,\alpha}\|(\bm{v}_{2},\bm{w}_{2})\|_{\mathbf{L}^{3}(\Omega)\times\mathbf{L}^{2+\omega}(\Gamma)}\|\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}(\phi,\psi)\|_{L,\beta,\ast}\\
&\quad\leq\frac{1}{2}\|(\bm{v},\bm{w})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}+\frac{1}{4}\|(\phi,\psi)\|_{K,\alpha}^{2}+C_{0}\big(1+\|(\bm{v}_{2},\bm{w}_{2})\|_{\mathbf{L}^{3}(\Omega)\times\mathbf{L}^{2+\omega}(\Gamma)}^{2}\big)\|\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}(\phi,\psi)\|_{L,\beta,\ast}^{2}\end{split}

(6.3)

Combining the two estimates (

7.
Higher regularity and strong solutions

In this final section, we present the proof of Theorem
3.6
.

Proof of Theorem
3.6
.

As the mobility functions are constant, we assume, without loss of generality, that
m_{\Omega}\equiv 1
and
m_{\Gamma}\equiv 1
.
The proof is divided into several steps.

Step 1: Approximation of the singular potentials.

We introduce families of regular potentials
F_{\lambda}
and
G_{\lambda}
to approximate the singular potentials
F
and
G
, respectively. To be precise, for
\lambda>0
, we set

\displaystyle F_{\lambda}(s)=F_{1,\lambda}(s)+F_{2}(s),\qquad G_{\lambda}(s)=G_{1,\lambda}(s)+G_{2}(s),\qquad\text{for all~}s\in\mathbb{R},

where, as in Section
4
, for any
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
,
F_{1,\lambda}
and
G_{1,\lambda}
denote the Moreau–Yosida regularizations of the singular parts
F_{1}
and
G_{1}
, respectively. We recall from Section
4
that for any
\lambda>0
,
F_{1,\lambda}
and
G_{1,\lambda}
have the properties
(M1)
–
(M5)
.
In addition, as discussed in Section
4
(see (
4.4
)), it holds that

\displaystyle\left|F_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(\alpha r)\right|\leq\kappa_{1}\left|G_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(r)\right|+\kappa_{2}

(7.1)

for all
r\in\mathbb{R}
and every
\lambda>0
.
Moreover, the assumptions on
F_{2}
together with
(M4)
imply that there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
, which comply with the convention stated in Remark
6.1
, such that

\displaystyle F_{\lambda}(r)\geq C_{1}r^{2}-C_{2}\qquad\text{for all~}r\in\mathbb{R}\text{~and~}\lambda\in(0,\overline{\lambda}),

(7.2)

where
\overline{\lambda}
is chosen as in
(M4)
. An analogous estimate holds for
G_{1,\lambda}
, and we write
\lambda_{\ast}>0
to denote the common threshold such that (
7.2
) holds for both
F_{\lambda}
and
G_{\lambda}
. Therefore, in the following, we simply consider
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{*})
.

Step 2: Approximation of the initial datum.
We approximate the initial condition
(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})
in a suitable way such that the approximation is compatible with the Yosida approximation of the potentials. To this end, we consider

\displaystyle-\Delta\phi_{0,\lambda}+F_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(\phi_{0,\lambda})

\displaystyle=\mu_{0}-F_{2}^{\prime}(\phi_{0})

\displaystyle\qquad\text{in~}\Omega,

(7.3a)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi_{0,\lambda}+G_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(\psi_{0,\lambda})+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi_{0,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\theta_{0}-G_{2}^{\prime}(\psi_{0})

\displaystyle\qquad\text{on~}\Gamma,

(7.3b)

\displaystyle K\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi_{0,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\alpha\psi_{0,\lambda}-\phi_{0,\lambda}

\displaystyle\qquad\text{on~}\Gamma.

(7.3c)

According to Proposition
5.1
, Proposition
5.5
and Remark
5.6
, this system possesses a unique strong solution
(\phi_{0,\lambda},\psi_{0,\lambda})\in\mathcal{W}^{2,6}\cap\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}
. Moreover, there exists a constant
C_{\mathrm{init}}>0
, that is independent of
\lambda
, such that

\displaystyle\|(\phi_{0,\lambda},\psi_{0,\lambda})\|_{\mathcal{W}^{2,6}}+\|(F_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(\phi_{0,\lambda}),G_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(\psi_{0,\lambda}))\|_{\mathcal{L}^{6}}\leq C_{\mathrm{init}}.

(7.4)

In particular, there exists a pair
(\tilde{\phi}_{0},\tilde{\psi}_{0})\in\mathcal{W}^{2,6}
such that

\displaystyle(\phi_{0,\lambda},\psi_{0,\lambda})

\displaystyle\rightarrow(\tilde{\phi}_{0},\tilde{\psi}_{0})

\displaystyle\qquad\text{weakly in~}\mathcal{W}^{2,6},\text{~strongly in~}\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha},

(7.5)

\displaystyle(F_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(\phi_{0,\lambda}),G_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(\psi_{0,\lambda}))

\displaystyle\rightarrow(F_{1}^{\prime}(\tilde{\phi}_{0}),G_{1}^{\prime}(\tilde{\psi}_{0}))

\displaystyle\qquad\text{weakly in~}\mathcal{L}^{6}

(7.6)

as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
after possibly extracting a subsequence. Now, we define

\displaystyle\mu_{0,\lambda}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mu_{0}-F_{2}^{\prime}(\phi_{0})+F_{2}^{\prime}(\phi_{0,\lambda}),

(7.7)

\displaystyle\theta_{0,\lambda}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\theta_{0}-G_{2}^{\prime}(\psi_{0})+G_{2}^{\prime}(\psi_{0,\lambda}).

(7.8)

This means that
(\phi_{0,\lambda},\psi_{0,\lambda})
solves

\displaystyle-\Delta\phi_{0,\lambda}+F_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\phi_{0,\lambda})

\displaystyle=\mu_{0,\lambda}

\displaystyle\qquad\text{in~}\Omega,

(7.9a)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi_{0,\lambda}+G_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\psi_{0,\lambda})+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi_{0,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\theta_{0,\lambda}

\displaystyle\qquad\text{on~}\Gamma,

(7.9b)

\displaystyle K\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi_{0,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\alpha\psi_{0,\lambda}-\phi_{0,\lambda}

\displaystyle\qquad\text{on~}\Gamma.

(7.9c)

Then, due to (
7.5
) and the Lipschitz continuity of
F_{2}^{\prime}
and
G_{2}^{\prime}
, respectively, it holds that

\displaystyle(\mu_{0,\lambda},\theta_{0,\lambda})\rightarrow(\mu_{0},\theta_{0})\qquad\text{weakly in~}\mathcal{H}^{1},\text{~strongly in~}\mathcal{L}^{2}

(7.10)

as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
. In view of the convergences (
7.5
), (
7.6
) and (
7.10
), it is straightforward to check that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\tilde{\phi}_{0}\cdot\nabla\eta+F^{\prime}(\tilde{\phi}_{0})\eta\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}\tilde{\psi}_{0}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\vartheta+G^{\prime}(\tilde{\psi}_{0})\vartheta\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle\qquad+\sigma(K)\int_{\Gamma}(\alpha\tilde{\psi}_{0}-\tilde{\phi}_{0})(\alpha\vartheta-\eta)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\Omega}\mu_{0}\eta\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\theta_{0}\vartheta\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

for all
(\eta,\vartheta)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}
.
In view of Assumption
(C)
, we thus know that both
(\tilde{\phi}_{0},\tilde{\psi}_{0})
and
(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})
are a weak solution of System
5.1
with
(f,g)=(\mu_{0},\theta_{0})
.
Hence, Theorem
5.2
implies that

\displaystyle(\tilde{\phi}_{0},\tilde{\psi}_{0})=(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})\qquad\text{a.e. in~}\Omega\times\Gamma.

(7.11)

Furthermore, thanks to this identification and the strong
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-convergence (
7.5
), there exist
\overline{m}\in(0,1)
, which is independent of
\lambda
, such that, after possibly updating the value of
\lambda_{\ast}
, it holds

\displaystyle\left|\beta\textnormal{mean}\left(\phi_{0,\lambda},\psi_{0,\lambda}\right)\right|\leq\overline{m}<1,\qquad\left|\textnormal{mean}\left(\phi_{0,\lambda},\psi_{0,\lambda}\right)\right|\leq\overline{m}<1\qquad\text{for all~}0<\lambda<\lambda_{\ast}

(7.12)

if
L\in(0,\infty)
, and

\displaystyle\left|{\langle\phi_{0,\lambda}\rangle}_{\Omega}\right|\leq\overline{m}<1,\qquad\left|{\langle\psi_{0,\lambda}\rangle}_{\Gamma}\right|\leq\overline{m}<1\qquad\text{for all~}0<\lambda<\lambda_{\ast}

(7.13)

if
L=\infty
.
Additionally, as we have the uniform convergence
(\phi_{0,\lambda},\psi_{0,\lambda})\rightarrow(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})
as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
and knowing that
\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1
as well as
\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\leq 1
, we infer that

\displaystyle\|\phi_{0,\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 2,\qquad\|\psi_{0,\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\leq 2\qquad\text{for all~}0<\lambda<\lambda_{\ast}

(7.14)

after a possible redefinition of
\lambda_{\ast}
.

Step 3: Approximation of the velocity fields.
As the last step of the approximation procedure, we regularize the velocity fields. To this end, we start by extending
\bm{v}
and
\bm{w}
in time by zero to obtain
(\widetilde{\bm{v}},\widetilde{\bm{w}})\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{L}^{2}_{\textnormal{div}})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{H}^{1})

We now choose a mollifier
\rho\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R};[0,\infty))
with
\|\rho\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}=1
, and for any
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{*})
, we set
\rho_{\lambda}:=\lambda^{-1}\rho(\lambda^{-1}\,\cdot)
. We further define
\bm{v}_{\lambda}=\widetilde{\bm{v}}\ast_{t}\rho_{\lambda}
and
\bm{w}_{\lambda}=\widetilde{\bm{w}}\ast_{t}\rho_{\lambda}
, where
\ast_{t}
denotes the convolution with respect to the time variable. This provides a sequence
\{(\bm{v}_{\lambda},\bm{w}_{\lambda})\}_{\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{*})}
such that
(\bm{v}_{\lambda},\bm{w}_{\lambda})\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathcal{H}^{1})
and

\displaystyle\begin{split}(\bm{v}_{\lambda},\bm{w}_{\lambda})\rightarrow(\bm{v},\bm{w})&\qquad\text{strongly in~}L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{1}),\\
&\qquad\text{weakly-$\ast$ in~}L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{L}^{2}),\end{split}

(7.15)

as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
, up to subsequence extraction.
For more details, we refer to
[
3

In particular, according to
[
3
, S. 4.13 (2)]

\displaystyle\|(\bm{v}_{\lambda},\bm{w}_{\lambda})\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{L}^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{1})}\leq\|(\bm{v},\bm{w})\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{L}^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{1})}

(7.16)

for all
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{*})
.
We further point out that the approximate velocity fields are still divergence-free and preserve the trace relation
\bm{v}_{\lambda}|_{\Gamma}=\bm{w}_{\lambda}
a.e. on
\Sigma
if
K=0
as the convolution only affects the time variable.

Step 4: The approximate system.

Let now
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{\ast})
be arbitrary. Then, in view of
[
35
, Theorem 3.2]
, there exists a unique quadruplet
(\phi_{\lambda},\psi_{\lambda},\mu_{\lambda},\theta_{\lambda})
such that

\displaystyle(\phi_{\lambda},\psi_{\lambda})

\displaystyle\in C([0,T];\mathcal{L}^{2})\cap H^{1}(0,T;(\mathcal{H}^{1})^{\prime})\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}),

\displaystyle(\mu_{\lambda},\theta_{\lambda})

\displaystyle\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{1}),

which solves

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda}+\textnormal{div}(\phi_{\lambda}\bm{v}_{\lambda})=\Delta\mu_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\text{in}\ Q,

(7.17a)

\displaystyle\mu_{\lambda}=-\Delta\phi_{\lambda}+F_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\phi_{\lambda})

\displaystyle\text{in}\ Q,

(7.17b)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda}+\textnormal{div}_{\Gamma}(\psi_{\lambda}\bm{w}_{\lambda})=\Delta_{\Gamma}\theta_{\lambda}-\beta\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mu_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\text{on}\ \Sigma,

(7.17c)

\displaystyle\theta_{\lambda}=-\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi_{\lambda}+G_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\psi_{\lambda})+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\text{on}\ \Sigma,

(7.17d)

\displaystyle K\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi_{\lambda}=\alpha\psi_{\lambda}-\phi_{\lambda}\qquad\text{for~}K\in[0,\infty)

\displaystyle\text{on}\ \Sigma,

(7.17e)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}L\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mu_{\lambda}=\beta\theta_{\lambda}-\mu_{\lambda}&\text{if}\ L\in(0,\infty),\\
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mu_{\lambda}=0&\text{if}\ L=\infty\end{cases}

\displaystyle\text{on}\ \Sigma,

(7.17f)

\displaystyle\phi_{\lambda}|_{t=0}=\phi_{0,\lambda}

\displaystyle\text{in}\ \Omega,

(7.17g)

\displaystyle\psi_{\lambda}|_{t=0}=\psi_{0,\lambda}

\displaystyle\text{on}\ \Gamma,

(7.17h)

in the sense that it satisfies the weak formulation (
3.4a
)-(
3.4b
) with
F
and
G
replaced by
F_{\lambda}
and
G_{\lambda}
, respectively, and
\bm{v}

\bm{v}_{\lambda}

\bm{w}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\begin{split}&E_{K,\lambda}(\phi_{\lambda}(t),\psi_{\lambda}(t))+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\mu_{\lambda}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\;\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta_{\lambda}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\;\mathrm{d}s\\
&\quad+\sigma(L)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}(\beta\theta_{\lambda}-\mu_{\lambda})^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\;\mathrm{d}s\\

for all

, where the approximate energy

is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}E_{K,\lambda}(\zeta,\xi)&=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla\zeta\right|^{2}+F_{\lambda}(\zeta)\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\xi\right|^{2}+G_{\lambda}(\xi)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma+\sigma(K)\int_{\Gamma}\frac{1}{2}(\alpha\xi-\zeta)^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma.\end{split}

(7.19)

To control the approximated initial energy in (
7.18

7.3
) with
(\phi_{0,\lambda},\psi_{0,\lambda})
and obtain

\displaystyle\|(\phi_{0,\lambda},\psi_{0,\lambda})\|_{K,\alpha}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}F_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(\phi_{0,\lambda})\phi_{0,\lambda}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}G_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(\psi_{0,\lambda})\psi_{0,\lambda}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\Omega}\big(\mu_{0}-F_{2}^{\prime}(\phi_{0})\big)\phi_{0,\lambda}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\big(\theta_{0}-G_{2}^{\prime}(\psi_{0})\big)\psi_{0,\lambda}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma.

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}F_{1,\lambda}(s)\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}G_{1,\lambda}(s)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\leq C\big(1+\|(\mu_{0},\theta_{0})\|_{L,\beta}^{2}\big),

and readily find that

\displaystyle E_{K,\lambda}(\phi_{0,\lambda},\psi_{0,\lambda})\leq E_{K}(\phi_{0,\lambda},\psi_{0,\lambda})\leq C\big(1+\|(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})\|_{K,\alpha}^{2}+\|(\mu_{0},\theta_{0})\|_{L,\beta}^{2}\big).

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\lambda}\bm{v}_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla\mu_{\lambda}\;\mathrm{d}x\;\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}\psi_{\lambda}\bm{w}_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta_{\lambda}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\;\mathrm{d}s\right|\\

&\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|(\bm{v}_{\lambda},\bm{w}_{\lambda})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{3}}^{2}\|(\phi_{\lambda},\psi_{\lambda})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

Next, in view of (

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}F_{\lambda}(\phi_{\lambda})\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}G_{\lambda}(\psi_{\lambda})\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\geq C\|(\phi_{\lambda},\psi_{\lambda})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}-C

since

\displaystyle c\,\|(\phi_{\lambda},\psi_{\lambda})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}^{2}

for some constant

\displaystyle\begin{split}&c\|(\phi_{\lambda},\psi_{\lambda})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\mu_{\lambda}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\;\mathrm{d}s+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta_{\lambda}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\;\mathrm{d}s\\
&\qquad+\sigma(L)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}(\beta\theta_{\lambda}-\mu_{\lambda})^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\;\mathrm{d}s\\

(7.24)

Hence, an application of Gronwall’s lemma together with (

) readily yields that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|(\phi_{\lambda},\psi_{\lambda})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}^{2}&\leq C\exp\Big(C\int_{0}^{T}\|(\bm{v}_{\lambda},\bm{w}_{\lambda})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{3}}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}s\Big)\leq C.\end{split}

(7.25)

Recalling that
F_{\lambda}
and
G_{\lambda}
are bounded from below (cf. (

) and (

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|(\phi_{\lambda},\psi_{\lambda})\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{1})}^{2}+\|(F_{\lambda}(\phi_{\lambda}),G_{\lambda}(\psi_{\lambda}))\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{L}^{1})}+\sigma(K)\|\alpha\psi_{\lambda}-\phi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))}^{2}\\
&\quad+\|(\nabla\mu_{\lambda},\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta_{\lambda})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{L}^{2})}^{2}+\sigma(L)\|\beta\theta_{\lambda}-\mu_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma))}^{2}\leq C.\end{split}

(7.26)

In order to find an
\mathcal{H}^{1}

-estimate on the chemical potentials
\mu_{\lambda}
and
\theta_{\lambda}
, we only consider the case
L\in(0,\infty)
. The proof in the case
L=\infty
works similarly with obvious modifications. First, we recall the inequality

\displaystyle\begin{split}&c_{1}\|F_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(\phi_{\lambda})\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+c_{2}\|G_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(\psi_{\lambda})\|_{L^{1}(\Gamma)}-c_{3}\\
&\quad\leq\int_{\Omega}F_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(\phi_{\lambda})(\phi_{\lambda}-\beta\textnormal{mean}\left(\phi_{\lambda},\psi_{\lambda}\right))\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}G_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(\psi_{\lambda})(\psi_{\lambda}-\textnormal{mean}\left(\phi_{\lambda},\psi_{\lambda}\right))\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma,\end{split}

(7.27)

(see
[
46
, Proposition A.1]
or
[
25
, p.908]
),
where
c_{1},c_{2}
are positive constants and
c_{3}
is non-negative. Note that these constants are independent of
\lambda
. Here, we used again the mass conservation law, (
7.12
) and (
7.13
). Using (
7.27
), we can argue analogously to the proof of
[
34
, Theorem 3.4]
to deduce the estimate

\displaystyle\|(F_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(\phi_{\lambda}),G_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(\psi_{\lambda}))\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\leq C\big(1+\|(\mu_{\lambda},\theta_{\lambda})\|_{L,\beta}\big)+C\gamma(K)\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)},

(7.28)

where, as before,
\gamma(K)=\mathbf{1}_{\{0\}}(K)
denotes the characteristic function of the set
\{0\}
.

Next, we derive
L^{2}
-bounds on
F_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(\phi_{\lambda})
and
G_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(\psi_{\lambda})
. Invoking the Lipschitz continuity of
F_{2}^{\prime}
and
G_{2}^{\prime}
as well as (
7.26
) and (
7.28
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\|(F_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\phi_{\lambda}),G_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\psi_{\lambda}))\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}\leq C\big(1+\|(\mu_{\lambda},\theta_{\lambda})\|_{L,\beta}\big)+C\gamma(K)\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}.

(7.29)

Thus, testing (
3.4b
) with
(\beta^{2}\left|\Omega\right|+\left|\Gamma\right|)^{-1}(\beta,1)
, we deduce from (
7.26
) and (
7.29
) that

\displaystyle\left|\textnormal{mean}\left(\mu_{\lambda},\theta_{\lambda}\right)\right|\leq C\big(1+\|(\mu_{\lambda},\theta_{\lambda})\|_{L,\beta}\big)+C\gamma(K)\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}.

(7.30)

Consequently, using (
7.30
) together with the bulk-surface Poincaré inequality (Lemma
2.1
), we infer that

\displaystyle\|(\mu_{\lambda},\theta_{\lambda})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}\leq C\big(1+\|(\mu_{\lambda},\theta_{\lambda})\|_{L,\beta}\big)+C\gamma(K)\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}.

(7.31)

Now, according to Proposition
5.5
and Remark
5.6
we have

\displaystyle\|(\phi_{\lambda},\psi_{\lambda})\|_{\mathcal{W}^{2,6}}+\|(F_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(\phi_{\lambda}),G_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(\psi_{\lambda}))\|_{\mathcal{L}^{6}}\leq C\big(1+\|(\mu_{\lambda}-F_{2}^{\prime}(\phi_{\lambda}),\theta_{\lambda}-G_{2}^{\prime}(\psi_{\lambda}))\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}\big).

(7.32)

In view of the Lipschitz continuity of
F_{2}^{\prime}
and
G_{2}^{\prime}
, (
7.25
) and (
7.31
), we deduce from (
7.32
) that

\displaystyle\|(\phi_{\lambda},\psi_{\lambda})\|_{\mathcal{W}^{2,6}}+\|(F_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(\phi_{\lambda}),G_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(\psi_{\lambda}))\|_{\mathcal{L}^{6}}\leq C\big(1+\|(\mu_{\lambda},\theta_{\lambda})\|_{L,\beta}+\gamma(K)\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\big)

(7.33)

Lastly, we establish a uniform
L^{2}
-bound on
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi_{\lambda}
. To this end, by the trace theorem (see, e.g.,
[
9
, Chapter 2, Theorem 2.24]
), we have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq C\|\phi_{\lambda}\|_{H^{7/4}(\Omega)}.

(7.34)

Thus, invoking(
7.33
)-(
7.34
) and applying the compact embeddings
W^{2,6}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow H^{7/4}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
along with Ehrling’s lemma, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|(\phi_{\lambda},\psi_{\lambda})\|_{\mathcal{W}^{2,6}}+\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\\
&\quad\leq C\big(1+\|(\mu_{\lambda},\theta_{\lambda})\|_{L,\beta}\big)+C\|\phi_{\lambda}\|_{H^{7/4}(\Omega)}\\
&\quad\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\phi_{\lambda}\|_{W^{2,6}(\Omega)}+C\big(1+\|(\mu_{\lambda},\theta_{\lambda})\|_{L,\beta}\big).\end{split}

(7.35)

Whence, absorbing the respective norm, we infer that

\displaystyle\|(\phi_{\lambda},\psi_{\lambda})\|_{\mathcal{W}^{2,6}}+\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq C\big(1+\|(\mu_{\lambda},\theta_{\lambda})\|_{L,\beta}\big),

(7.36)

and thus, in view of (
7.31
) and (
7.32
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\|(F_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(\phi_{\lambda}),G_{1,\lambda}^{\prime}(\psi_{\lambda}))\|_{\mathcal{L}^{6}}+\|(\mu_{\lambda},\theta_{\lambda})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}\leq C\big(1+\|(\mu_{\lambda},\theta_{\lambda})\|_{L,\beta}\big).

(7.37)

Step 5: Higher order regularity.
As the approximate velocity fields
\bm{v}_{\lambda}
and
\bm{w}_{\lambda}
fulfill the regularity assumption (
3.14
) that was used in
[
34
, Theorem 3.7]
, we can adapt the proof to conclude that

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle\in L^{\infty}(0,T;(\mathcal{H}^{1})^{\prime})\cap L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{1}),

(7.38a)

\displaystyle\left(\phi_{\lambda},\psi_{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{W}^{2,6})\cap\Big(C\big(\overline{Q}\big)\times C\big(\overline{\Sigma}\big)\Big),

(7.38b)

\displaystyle\left(\mu_{\lambda},\theta_{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{1})\cap L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{2}),

(7.38c)

\displaystyle\left(F_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\phi_{\lambda}),G_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\psi_{\lambda})\right)

\displaystyle\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{L}^{\infty})\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{L}^{6}).

(7.38d)

In addition,
(\phi_{\lambda},\psi_{\lambda},\mu_{\lambda},\theta_{\lambda})
satisfies the equations

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda}+\textnormal{div}(\phi_{\lambda}\bm{v}_{\lambda})=\Delta\mu_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\qquad\text{a.e.~in~}Q,

(7.39a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda}+\textnormal{div}_{\Gamma}(\psi_{\lambda}\bm{w}_{\lambda})=\Delta_{\Gamma}\theta_{\lambda}-\beta\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mu_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\qquad\text{a.e.~on~}\Sigma,

(7.39b)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}L\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mu_{\lambda}=\beta\theta_{\lambda}-\mu_{\lambda}&\text{if~}L\in(0,\infty),\\
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mu_{\lambda}=0&\text{if~}L=\infty\end{cases}

\displaystyle\qquad\text{a.e.~on~}\Sigma.

(7.39c)

We now prove several higher regularity results, which are not uniform with respect to
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{\ast})
, but are crucial for our analysis to prove the desired uniform estimates.

Step 5.1:
First, we prove that
(\partial_{t}\mu_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\theta_{\lambda})
exists and belongs to
L^{2}(0,T;(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha})^{\prime})
. To this end, we take the difference quotient of (
7.17b
), (
7.17d
) and (
7.17e
), respectively. Then we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{h}\mu_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\Delta\partial_{t}^{h}\phi_{\lambda}+\partial_{t}^{h}[F_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\phi_{\lambda})]\qquad

\displaystyle\text{a.e.~in~}Q,

(7.40a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{h}\theta_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\Delta_{\Gamma}\partial_{t}^{h}\psi_{\lambda}+\partial_{t}^{h}[G_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\psi_{\lambda})]+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\partial_{t}^{h}\phi_{\lambda}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{a.e.~on~}\Sigma,

(7.40b)

\displaystyle K\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\partial_{t}^{h}\phi_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\alpha\partial_{t}^{h}\psi_{\lambda}-\partial_{t}^{h}\phi_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\text{a.e.~on }\Sigma,

(7.40c)

for
K\in[0,\infty)
. Here, the difference quotient of a function
f
at time
t
is defined as
\partial_{t}^{h}f(t)=\frac{1}{h}(f(t+h)-f(t))
for
h\in(0,1)
.
Let now
(\eta,\vartheta)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}
. Multiplying (
7.40a
) and (
7.40b
) by
\eta
and
\vartheta
, and integrating over
\Omega
and
\Gamma
, respectively, an application of integration by parts yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\langle(\partial_{t}^{h}\mu_{\lambda},\partial_{t}^{h}\theta_{\lambda}),(\eta,\vartheta)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}}=\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}^{h}\mu_{\lambda}\eta\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{t}^{h}\theta_{\lambda}\vartheta\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\partial_{t}^{h}\phi_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla\eta+\partial_{t}^{h}[F_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\phi_{\lambda})]\eta\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\qquad+\int_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}\partial_{t}^{h}\psi_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\vartheta+\partial_{t}^{h}[G_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\psi_{\lambda})]\vartheta\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\qquad+\sigma(K)\int_{\Gamma}(\alpha\partial_{t}^{h}\psi_{\lambda}-\partial_{t}^{h}\phi_{\lambda})(\alpha\vartheta-\eta)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma.\end{split}

(7.41)

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\left|\big<(\partial_{t}^{h}\mu_{\lambda},\partial_{t}^{h}\theta_{\lambda}),(\eta,\vartheta)\big>_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C\Big(\|(\partial_{t}^{h}\phi_{\lambda},\partial_{t}^{h}\psi_{\lambda})\|_{K,\alpha}+\|(\partial_{t}^{h}[F_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\phi_{\lambda})],\partial_{t}^{h}[G_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\psi_{\lambda})])\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\Big)\|(\eta,\vartheta)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}.

Now, taking the supremum over all
(\eta,\vartheta)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}
with
\|(\eta,\vartheta)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}\leq 1
yields

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{t}^{h}\mu_{\lambda},\partial_{t}^{h}\theta_{\lambda})\|_{(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha})^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\|(\partial_{t}^{h}\phi_{\lambda},\partial_{t}^{h}\psi_{\lambda})\|_{K,\alpha}+\|(\partial_{t}^{h}[F_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\phi_{\lambda})],\partial_{t}^{h}[G_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\psi_{\lambda})])\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\Big).

(7.42)

Moreover, we note that

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{t}^{h}\phi_{\lambda},\partial_{t}^{h}\psi_{\lambda})\|_{K,\alpha}\leq C\|(\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda})\|_{K,\alpha}.

In view of
(M3)
, we infer

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{t}^{h}[F_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\phi_{\lambda})],\partial_{t}^{h}[G_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\psi_{\lambda})])\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\leq\frac{C}{\lambda}\|(\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\leq\frac{C}{\lambda}\|(\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda})\|_{K,\alpha}.

Consequently, due to (
7.42
), we obtain the bound

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{t}^{h}\mu_{\lambda},\partial_{t}^{h}\theta_{\lambda})\|_{(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha})^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\lambda}\|(\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda})\|_{K,\alpha},

(7.43)

which is uniform with respect to
h
.
Hence, we conclude that
(\partial_{t}\mu_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\theta_{\lambda})\in L^{2}(0,T;(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha})^{\prime})
. Furthermore, passing to the limit
h\rightarrow 0
in (
7.41
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\big<(\partial_{t}\mu_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\theta_{\lambda}),(\eta,\vartheta)\big>_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}}&=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla\eta+F_{\lambda}^{\prime\prime}(\phi_{\lambda})\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda}\eta\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\qquad+\int_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\vartheta+G_{\lambda}^{\prime\prime}(\psi_{\lambda})\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda}\vartheta\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\qquad+\sigma(K)\int_{\Gamma}(\alpha\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda}-\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda})(\alpha\vartheta-\eta)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\end{split}

(7.44)

almost everywhere on
[0,T]
for all
(\eta,\vartheta)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}
.

Step 5.2:
Next, we show that
(\phi_{\lambda},\psi_{\lambda})\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{W}^{3,6})
. To this end, we apply
L^{p}
regularity theory for bulk-surface elliptic systems (see, e.g.,
[
34
, Proposition A.1]
, adapted to
k=3
), and get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|(\phi_{\lambda},\psi_{\lambda})\|_{\mathcal{W}^{3,6}}&\leq C\big(1+\|(\mu_{\lambda}-F_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\phi_{\lambda}),\theta_{\lambda}-G_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\psi_{\lambda}))\|_{\mathcal{W}^{1,6}}\big)\\
&\leq C\big(1+\|(\mu_{\lambda},\theta_{\lambda})\|_{\mathcal{W}^{1,6}}+\|(F_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\phi_{\lambda}),G_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\psi_{\lambda}))\|_{\mathcal{W}^{1,6}}\big).\end{split}

(7.45)

For the second term on the right-hand side of (
7.45
), we note that
F_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\phi_{\lambda})\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{6}(\Omega))
. In addition,
\left|\nabla F_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\phi_{\lambda})\right|=\left|F_{\lambda}^{\prime\prime}(\phi_{\lambda})\nabla\phi_{\lambda}\right|\leq(C/\lambda)\left|\nabla\phi_{\lambda}\right|
, and thus
\nabla F_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\phi_{\lambda})\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{6}(\Omega))
. Consequently,
F_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\phi_{\lambda})\in L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{1,6}(\Omega))
. In an analogous manner, we derive
G_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\psi_{\lambda})\in L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{1,6}(\Gamma))
. As also
(\mu,\theta)\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{2})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{W}^{1,6})
, we deduce that
(\phi_{\lambda},\psi_{\lambda})\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{W}^{3,6})
.

Step 5.3:
Lastly, we prove that
(\mu_{\lambda},\theta_{\lambda})\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{3})\cap C([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{1})
. Once again, by the elliptic regularity theory for bulk-surface elliptic systems, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|(\mu_{\lambda},\theta_{\lambda})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{3}}\\
&\quad\leq C\|(\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda}-\nabla\phi_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{v}_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda}-\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{w}_{\lambda})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}+\left|\textnormal{mean}\left(\mu_{\lambda},\theta_{\lambda}\right)\right|\\
&\quad\leq C\|(\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}+C\|(\nabla\phi_{\lambda},\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi_{\lambda})\|_{\mathcal{W}^{1,\infty}}\|(\bm{v}_{\lambda},\bm{w}_{\lambda})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}+C\big(1+\|(\mu_{\lambda},\theta_{\lambda})\|\big)\\
&\quad\leq C\|(\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}+C\|(\phi_{\lambda},\psi_{\lambda})\|_{\mathcal{W}^{3,6}}\|(\bm{v}_{\lambda},\bm{w}_{\lambda})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}+C\big(1+\|(\mu_{\lambda},\theta_{\lambda})\|\big).\end{split}

(7.46)

This immediately entails
(\mu_{\lambda},\theta_{\lambda})\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{3})
due to (
7.16
) and the previous step. Moreover, as
(\partial_{t}\mu_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\theta_{\lambda})\in L^{2}(0,T;(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha})^{\prime})
and
(\mu_{\lambda},\theta_{\lambda})\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{1})
, the Aubin–Lions lemma implies that
(\mu_{\lambda},\theta_{\lambda})\in C([0,T];\mathcal{L}^{2})
. Next, recalling that in the case
K=0
we have
\bm{v}_{\lambda}|_{\Gamma}=\bm{w}_{\lambda}
a.e. on
\Sigma
, we compute

\displaystyle\nabla\phi_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{v}_{\lambda}=[\nabla\phi_{\lambda}-\mathbf{n}(\nabla\phi_{\lambda}\cdot\mathbf{n})]\cdot\bm{v}_{\lambda}=\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{v}_{\lambda}=\alpha\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{w}_{\lambda}\quad\text{a.e.~on $\Sigma$ if $K=0$},

from which we obtain
(-\Delta\mu_{\lambda},-\Delta_{\Gamma}\theta_{\lambda}+\beta\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mu_{\lambda})=-(\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda}+\nabla\phi_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{v}_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda}+\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{w}_{\lambda})\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha})
.
This allows us to apply
[
35
, Proposition A.1]
, which yields
(\mu_{\lambda},\theta_{\lambda})\in C([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{1})
along with the chain rule

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\frac{1}{2}\|(\mu_{\lambda},\theta_{\lambda})\|_{L,\beta}^{2}=\big<(\partial_{t}\mu_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\theta_{\lambda}),(-\Delta\mu_{\lambda},-\Delta_{\Gamma}\theta_{\lambda}+\beta\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mu_{\lambda})\big>_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}}.

(7.47)

We further notice that in view of the continuity
(\mu_{\lambda},\theta_{\lambda})\in C([0,T];\mathcal{H}^{1})
, it holds

\displaystyle(\mu_{\lambda}(0),\theta_{\lambda}(0))=(\mu_{0,\lambda},\theta_{0,\lambda})\quad\text{a.e.~in $\Omega\times\Gamma$.}

(7.48)

In particular, due to (
7.10
), we thus have

\displaystyle(\mu_{\lambda}(0),\theta_{\lambda}(0))\rightarrow(\mu_{0},\theta_{0})\quad\text{weakly in $\mathcal{H}^{1}$, strongly in $\mathcal{L}^{2}$}

(7.49)

as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
.

Step 6: Higher order uniform estimates.
Now, we establish the main estimates for the regularity argument, which will be uniform with respect to
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{\ast})
. To this end, we test (
7.44
) with
(\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda})\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}
obtaining

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\big<(\partial_{t}\mu_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\theta_{\lambda}),(\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda})\big>_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}}\\
&\quad=\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda}\right|^{2}+F_{\lambda}^{\prime\prime}(\phi_{\lambda})\left|\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\partial_{t}\psi\right|^{2}+G_{\lambda}^{\prime\prime}(\psi_{\lambda})\left|\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\qquad+\sigma(K)\int_{\Gamma}\left|\alpha\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda}-\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad=\|(\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda})\|_{K,\alpha}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}F_{\lambda}^{\prime\prime}(\phi_{\lambda})\left|\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}G_{\lambda}^{\prime\prime}(\psi_{\lambda})\left|\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma.\end{split}

(7.50)

On the other hand, using the identities (
7.39a
)-(
7.39b
) for
(\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda})
, as well as the chain rule (
7.47
), we find

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\big<(\partial_{t}\mu_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\theta_{\lambda}),(\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda})\big>_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}}\\
&\quad=\big<(\partial_{t}\mu_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\theta_{\lambda}),(\Delta\mu_{\lambda}-\nabla\phi_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{v}_{\lambda},\Delta_{\Gamma}\theta_{\lambda}-\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{w}_{\lambda}-\beta\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mu_{\lambda})\big>_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}}\\
&\quad=-\big<(\partial_{t}\mu_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\theta_{\lambda}),(-\Delta\mu_{\lambda},-\Delta_{\Gamma}\theta_{\lambda}+\beta\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mu_{\lambda})\big>_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}}\\
&\qquad-\big<(\partial_{t}\mu_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\theta_{\lambda}),(\nabla\phi_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{v}_{\lambda},\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{w}_{\lambda})\big>_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}}\\
&\quad=-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\frac{1}{2}\|(\mu_{\lambda},\theta_{\lambda})\|_{L,\beta}^{2}-\big<(\partial_{t}\mu_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\theta_{\lambda}),(\nabla\phi_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{v}_{\lambda},\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{w}_{\lambda})\big>_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}}.\end{split}

(7.51)

Now, for the second term on the right-hand side, we note again that
(\nabla\phi_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{v}_{\lambda},\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{w}_{\lambda})\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha})
. This allows us to choose this pair as a test function in the weak formulation (
7.44
). In this way, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\big<(\partial_{t}\mu_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\theta_{\lambda}),(\nabla\phi_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{v}_{\lambda},\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{w}_{\lambda})\big>_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}}\\
&\quad=\big((\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda}),(\nabla\phi_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{v}_{\lambda},\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{w}_{\lambda})\big)_{K,\alpha}\\
&\qquad+\int_{\Omega}F_{\lambda}^{\prime\prime}(\phi_{\lambda})\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda}\nabla\phi_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{v}_{\lambda}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}G_{\lambda}^{\prime\prime}(\psi_{\lambda})\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda}\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{w}_{\lambda}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad=\big((\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda}),(\nabla\phi_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{v}_{\lambda},\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{w}_{\lambda})\big)_{K,\alpha}\\
&\qquad+\int_{\Omega}\nabla(F_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\phi_{\lambda}))\cdot\bm{v}_{\lambda}\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}(G_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\psi_{\lambda}))\cdot\bm{w}_{\lambda}\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad=\big((\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda}),(\nabla\phi_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{v}_{\lambda},\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{w}_{\lambda})\big)_{K,\alpha}\\
&\qquad-\int_{\Omega}F_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\phi_{\lambda})\nabla\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{v}_{\lambda}\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Gamma}G_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\psi_{\lambda})\nabla_{\Gamma}\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{w}_{\lambda}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma.\end{split}

(7.52)

Here we used integration by parts along with the facts that
\textnormal{div}\;\bm{v}_{\lambda}=0
a.e. in
Q
,
\textnormal{div}_{\Gamma}\;\bm{w}_{\lambda}=0
and
\bm{v}_{\lambda}\cdot\mathbf{n}=\bm{w}_{\lambda}\cdot\mathbf{n}=0
a.e. on
\Sigma
. Thus, collecting (
7.50
)-(
7.52
), we find that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\frac{1}{2}\|(\mu_{\lambda},\theta_{\lambda})\|_{L,\beta}^{2}+\|(\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda})\|_{K,\alpha}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}F_{1,\lambda}^{\prime\prime}(\phi_{\lambda})\left|\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}G_{1,\lambda}^{\prime\prime}(\psi_{\lambda})\left|\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\\
&\quad=-\big((\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda}),(\nabla\phi_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{v}_{\lambda},\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{w}_{\lambda})\big)_{K,\alpha}+\int_{\Omega}F_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\phi_{\lambda})\nabla\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{v}_{\lambda}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\qquad+\int_{\Gamma}G_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\psi_{\lambda})\nabla_{\Gamma}\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda}\cdot\bm{w}_{\lambda}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma-\int_{\Omega}F_{2}^{\prime\prime}(\phi_{\lambda})\left|\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Gamma}G_{2}^{\prime\prime}(\psi_{\lambda})\left|\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda}\right|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma.\end{split}

(7.53)

We immediately notice that the last two terms on the left-hand side of (
7.53
) are non-negative due to
(M2)
. Moreover, applying the bulk-surface Poincaré inequality (Lemma
2.1
) immediately yields the inequalities

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda})\|_{L,\beta}\leq C\|(\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}\leq C\|(\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda})\|_{K,\alpha}.

Then, using this inequality, the Lipschitz continuity of
F_{2}^{\prime}
and
G_{2}^{\prime}
, the weak formulation for
(\partial_{t}\phi_{\lambda},\partial_{t}\psi_{\lambda})

from which we readily conclude that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T}\|(\nabla\mu_{\lambda},\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta_{\lambda})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}s&\leq C_{0}\Big(1+\|(\mu_{0},\theta_{0})\|_{L,\beta}+\int_{0}^{T}\|(\bm{v},\bm{w})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}s\Big)\\
&\quad\times\Big(1+\int_{0}^{T}\|(\bm{v},\bm{w})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}s\Big)\exp\Big(C_{0}\int_{0}^{T}\|(\bm{v},\bm{w})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}s\Big).\end{split}

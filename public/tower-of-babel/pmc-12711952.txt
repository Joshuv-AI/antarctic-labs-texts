Negative permittivity and permeability of polyethylene-based composites with segregated filler network in microwave range
Authors: Ludmila Yu. Matzui, Ludmila L. Vovchenko, Volodymyr V. Zagorodnii, Olena S. Yakovenko, Yulia S. Perets, Vladyslav V. Lisnyak, Dmytro O. Zaiats
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
In this paper, the microwave permittivity and permeability were investigated in the frequency range 26–40 GHz in percolative three-phase graphite nanoplatelets (GNPs)/magnetic particles (Fe,Co3O4)-polyethylene composites with segregated filler network. When GNPs content increases up to 5wt.% GNPs particles are in close contact, and a 3D segregated highly conductive network is formed and generates metal-like conductivity. For this case, the Drude type negative permittivity behavior is obtained due to plasma oscillation of free electrons within the conductive filler network. The observed relatively high dielectric loss in the composites was mainly induced by the high leakage current through GNPs and magnetic particles. In addition, the negative imaginary part of magnetic permeability was observed for developed three-phase composites. Higher GNPs content leads to an increase in absolute values of negative imaginary permeability. The comparative analysis of microwave shielding properties of polyethylene composites filled with GNPs and various additional magnetic particles, micron-sized Fe or nanosized Co3O4 showed the higher shielding ability of GNP-Fe-filled composites compared with GNP/Co3O4 ones. The increase of GNPs content promotes the enhancement of electromagnetic shielding due to both reflection SER and absorption SEA although the electromagnetic reflection index R is much higher compared with absorption index A.

Introduction

The development of composite materials using dielectric matrices and various conductive and magnetic fillers opens wide opportunities for creating new materials with a given set of functional characteristics that are required for various applications such as packaging, electromagnetic shielding, electrical sensors, capacitors, dielectric elastomers, for energy storage and protection systems, and to be used in the information industry1–3. By adjusting the type of dielectric matrix, the type and concentration of dispersed fillers, and their spatial distribution within the composite, it is possible to precisely control physical parameters such as permittivity, electrical conductivity, magnetic permeability, and thermal and mechanical properties. Thus, the task of establishing a relationship between the structural and morphological composition of the composite and its physical properties is extremely important for predicting the characteristics of the developed materials. The permittivity and magnetic permeability determine the response of materials to electromagnetic fields. Usually, the real part of the permittivity value \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\epsilon\:}_{r}^{{\prime\:}}$$\end{document} is positive4, however, the permittivity \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\epsilon\:}_{r}^{{\prime\:}}$$\end{document} can also be negative, which to some extent is considered as abnormal phenomenon. These materials with such unusual interesting electrodynamic properties attract attention and are called metamaterials5,6.

Initially, negative values of dielectric permittivity (and also magnetic permeability) were experimentally observed for artificially created so-called “classical” metamaterials. They are complex structures with periodically arranged functional units of a certain geometric shape made of metals with high electrical conductivity7–9. It was found later that negative values of permittivity \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\epsilon\:}_{r}^{{\prime\:}}$$\end{document} and permeability \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\mu\:}_{r}^{{\prime\:}}$$\end{document} can be also observed for ordinary so-called “natural” metacomposites, in which these negative parameters are achieved by precisely adjusted distribution of conductive filler particles in the composite with rather high content of fillers near the percolation threshold. Such percolative metacomposites with negative electrodynamic parameters form one typical group of metamaterials; their negative material parameters are related to their composition and microstructure. The study of electromagnetic parameters of metacomposites (including negative permittivity and permeability), has recently become a progressive research in materials science10,11. Several mechanisms can lead to the occurrence of negative values of electrodynamic parameters: (i) resonant interaction of electromagnetic radiation with the structures of the composite, which leads to a change in the effective dielectric permittivity; (ii) plasmon resonance in nanoparticles or structures can lead to negative permittivity in certain frequency ranges; (iii) dispersion effects, when in some composites the dispersion of materials can lead to the occurrence of negative permittivity in certain frequency ranges. These mechanisms can be used to create composites with specific electromagnetic properties that have important applications in various fields.

The variation of the type of dielectric matrix (ceramics or polymers), the type and morphology of the filler (conductive carbon, metal particles, magnetic particles) allows the obtaining of various set of the electromagnetic characteristics in metacomposites12–18.

Composite materials with carbon materials including amorphous carbon fibers, graphene, carbon nanotubes (CNTs), etc., as the conductive phase, one can more easily obtain a negative permittivity with a small absolute value due to the low carrier concentration, due to their intrinsic plasmons, tunable electrical conductivity, high aspect ratio19–24.

The transition from composites with a random distribution of fillers to composites with a segregated distribution of conductive fillers allows to significantly lower the percolation threshold, to form a highly conductive 3D network with metal-like conductivity, and to realize the occurrence of negative permittivity due to plasma oscillation of free electrons25–30. The use of multicomponent fillers, such as nanocarbon-metal, nanocarbon magnetic metal, carbon-metal core shell particles in polymer matrix with different filler distribution and the formation of specific 3D conductive filler network, opens the new possibilities for tuning both, permittivity and permeability in these composites. However, a large number of studies on the negative permittivity of composite materials have mainly focused on radio frequencies, and only a few studies on the negative permittivity in the microwave frequency range have been presented30–33. Therefore, considering the rapid progress in the development of wireless communication technologies, it is important to explore the application of negative permittivity materials in the microwave frequency range.

This paper is focused on the study of electrodynamic parameters (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\epsilon\:}_{r}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\mu\:}_{r}$$\end{document}) of segregated polyethylene-based composites filled with 2D graphite nanoplatelets (GNPs) and different magnetic fillers – carbonyl iron (Fe) and cobalt dioxide Co3O4 for microwave frequency range of 26.5–40 GHz. The special attention is paid to the correlation between electrical conductivity value and occurrence of negative permittivity in composite materials (CMs) with high GNPs content. Also the effect of combination of GNPs with different magnetic particles (Fe or Co3O4) with formation of a large number of interphase boundaries on electromagnetic shielding and absorption capabilities of developed composites has been studied.

Materials and methods

For fabrication of segregated polymer composites ultra-high molecular weight polyethylene (UHMWPE, globules size ~ 100 m) was used as matrix, while graphite nanoplatelets (GNPs), micron-sized Fe (lateral size of the particles is 3–7 μm, the thickness of 0.6–2 μm) and nanosized Co3O4 particles (# 637025 Sigma-Aldrich, particle size < 50 nm, 99.5% trace metal basis) were used as fillers. SEM-images of GNPs and magnetic fillers are presented in Figs. 1 (a)-(c).

The composites with segregated distribution of combined filler GNPs/Fe(Co3O4) in polyethylene matrix were fabricated by hot pressing method34.

SEM images of filler particles: (a) –GNPs, (b) carbonyl iron Fe and (c) cobalt oxide Co3O4; the procedure of polyethylene-based segregated (d) composites fabrication.

The mixtures of polyethylene globules (~ 100 m), GNPs and magnetic powders were homogenized by mixing in the IKA ULTRA TURRAX Tube Drive homogenizer test tube with a stirrer. The thickness of the GNPs/magnetic filler coating on PE globules is directly proportional to the amount of filler, i.e., its concentration in the composite. After homogenization, the mixture was placed in a closed hot die heated to 160 °C and pressed (hot compacted) at 50 MPa for 5 min, followed by cooling to room temperature. The content of magnetic particles was fixed at 30 wt%, while the content of GNP was 1–5 wt%. The method of fabrication of segregated CMs is shown in Fig. 1(d). The prepared composites were denoted as xGNPs/MP-PE, where MP is the magnetic (Fe or Co3O4) particles (fixed content of 30 wt%), x is the concentration of GNPs. The segregated xGNPs/MP-PE composites with x = 1, 2, 3, 5 wt% were prepared in a shape of cylinders of 15 mm diameter and the 1 ± 0.05 mm height.

The measurement of electrical resistivity of the studied composites was performed by the standard two-probe method in DC mode at room temperature with a measurement limit of the electrical resistance of 1010 Ω.

The investigation of electromagnetic properties of composites was performed with the hollow rectangular waveguide by measurements of the electromagnetic scattering parameters (S-parameters) in the frequency range of 26–40 GHz using Keysight N5227A vector network analyzer. WR-28 waveguide used for 26–40 GHz (0.280”x0.140”, i.e. 7.112х3.556 mm2) and the cross-section of the samples fitted to the waveguide (“thru” mode). Measurements of S parameters were carried out in the 2-port transmission/reflection scheme. The samples for those measurements were machined in the shape of rectangular parallelepipeds with the dimensions of 7.11 × 3.55×d mm3, where d is the sample thickness (length) which was 1 ± 0.05 mm for all samples The samples perfectly fitted the waveguide cross-Sect. (7.112х3.556 mm2). The spectra of complex permittivity and permeability were derived from measured S-parameters using the Agilent 85071E Material Measurement Software, based on the Nicolson–Ross–Weir procedure35.

Results and discussion

Electrical conductivity, permittivity and permeability of polymer composites filled with GNPs and magnetic particles

As was mentioned above, not only the type and content of the dispersed conductive filler in polymer composites determines the formation of a conductive 3D filler network and the transition from a dielectric to a conductive state, but also the type of filler spatial distribution in the polymer matrix significantly influences on the electronic transport in polymer-filled composite. As shown in our previous research36, the formation of a selective distribution of nanocarbon fillers in the polymer matrix can significantly reduce the percolation threshold and increase the value of the electrical conductivity of composites compared to ones with a random distribution of nanocarbon particles.

In the so-called segregated composites, in which the filler is distributed in the gaps between the polymer globules, forming a dense electrically conductive network. As the filler content increases, the contact between filler particles improves, leading to higher electrical conductivity. A key area of interest is understanding how the addition of inorganic fillers, alongside nanocarbon fillers, influences the electrical conductivity of such segregated composites with multi-component fillers. As we have shown in a previous study, in the case of CMs with a random distribution of nanocarbon/inorganic fillers (Fe, Co3O4, Co)37,38, the presence of second electroconductive fillers improves the dispersion and homogeneity of nanocarbon particles distribution in the polymer matrix during the fabrication of composites and promotes the effective formation of conductive chains from individual carbon nanoparticles.

In the developed hybrid composite, electrically conductive chains are formed mainly of GNPs particles and Fe (which is metallic) and Co3O4 (which is semiconductor39. Fe and Co3O4 particles do not form individual continuous chains and can only act as ‘bridges’ connecting GNP particles in electrically conductive chains. The conductivity of such chains depends on conductivity of GNPs particles, the contact resistance between particles and the number of electrically conducting chains of GNPs particles. An important additional advantage of the second inorganic filler as a supplement to the nanocarbon filler in the developed composite is its ability to disperse the GNPs intensively during the mechanical mixing of components, thereby improving the distribution of fillers in the composite and their better dispersion inside the matrix.

Keeping in mind that electrically conductive Fe or Co3O4 particles play the role of “bridges” between fragments of GNPs chains, the production of a larger number of continuous current-conducting paths formed by GNPs in the CMs during the mechanical mixing process significantly influences the increase in the electrical conductivity of the CM.

As the electrical conductive filler content/concentration continues to increase beyond the percolation threshold, the properties of the composites change fundamentally. At a high content of electrically conductive fillers, a large number of conductive pathways are formed and a transition from hopping (or tunneling) to diffuse electronic transport occurs and the sign of the dielectric permittivity changes to negative (as in metals)27–29. In these composites, delocalized electrons are accelerated by the electric field force, and then pulled back by the Coulomb force acting on the positive ion nuclei. Such a repeated process of electron motion in an alternating electric field is considered as plasma oscillations.

Thus, the occurance of the negative permittivity \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\epsilon\:}_{r}^{{\prime\:}}$$\end{document} values is associated with the high electrical conductivity of the corresponding CMs. It means rather high concentrations and mobility of charge carriers in the material and improved collective plasma oscillations of delocalized electrons participating in the diffuse electrical transport in such CM with a highly branched 3D electrically conductive filler networks (i.e. “metal-like” material with the conductive filler concentration above the percolation threshold).

Conclusion

Three-phase GNPs/magnetic particles (Fe, Co3O4)-polyethylene composites were fabricated by hot-pressing method. The dispersive state of GNPs/magnetic filler depends on the GNPs content in the composite. At the GNPs content below 3–4 wt%, the isolated GNPs and magnetic fillers or their fragmented clusters result in hopping (or tunnelling) electronic transport, capacitive material impedance behavior and positive permittivity. At the GNPs content increase up to 5wt.%, above the percolation threshold, GNPs particles are in close contact, a 3D segregated highly conductive network is formed and generates metal-like conductivity. In this case the composite material manifests inductive impedance and negative permittivity values in the microwave range of 26.5–40 GHz. The observed negative permittivity behavior can be attributed to the plasma oscillation of free electrons within the conductive filler network and can be reasonably analyzed using the Drude model. The increase of dielectric loss with GNPs content in the composites was mainly caused by the induced high leakage current through GNPs and magnetic particles. The notable occurrence of the negative imaginary part of magnetic permeability \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\mu\:}^{{\prime\:}{\prime\:}}$$\end{document} was observed for developed three-phase composites. Higher GNPs content leads to an increase in absolute values of negative imaginary permeability. The observed unusual behavior of the imaginary part of permeability \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\mu\:}^{{\prime\:}{\prime\:}}$$\end{document} (magnetic loss) can be explained by the induced circulating currents in closed circuits (rings), which generate the additional magnetic field. Three-phase GNPs/magnetic-PE segregated composites showed enhanced microwave shielding properties. 5%GNPs/Fe-PE and 5%GNPs/Co3O4-PE samples of 1 mm thickness achieved the values of the shielding efficiency SE of 39 dB and 20 dB, respectively due to high electrical conductivity and multiple reflection on conductive walls around PE-globules. The increase of GNPs content promotes the enhancement of EMR shielding due to both reflection SER and absorption SEA contribution, although the EMR reflection index R is much higher compared with absorption index A.

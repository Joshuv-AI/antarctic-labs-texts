Lagrangian split tori in S 2 × S 2 S^{2}\times S^{2} and billiards
Joé Brendel, Joontae Kim

Abstract
. In this paper, we classify up to Hamiltonian isotopy Lagrangian tori that split as a product of circles in S 2 × S 2 S^{2}\times S^{2} , when the latter is equipped with a non-monotone split symplectic form. We show that this classification is equivalent to a problem of mathematical billiards in rectangles. We give many applications, among others: (1) answering a question on Lagrangian packing numbers raised by Polterovich–Shelukhin, (2) studying the topology of the space of Lagrangian tori, and (3) determining which split tori are images under symplectic ball embeddings of Chekanov or product tori in ℝ 4 \mathbb{R}^{4} .

Remark 1.7.

Recall the elementary fact that if the ratio of width and height of a rectangular billiard table is irrational, then the set of bouncing points of any good billiard trajectory is dense in the boundary of that billiard table. By

1.2.
Hamiltonian monodromy

A question closely related to the classification of split tori is determining their Hamiltonian monodromy group.

Definition 1.8
.

Let
L\subset X
be a Lagrangian in a symplectic manifold
(X,\omega)
. The group

(4)

{\mathcal{H}}_{L}=\left\{(\psi|_{L})_{*}\in\operatorname{Aut}(H_{1}(L))\,|\,\psi\in\operatorname{Ham}(X,\omega),\psi(L)=L\right\}

is called the
Hamiltonian monodromy group of
L
.

The Hamiltonian monodromy group has been studied quite extensively, see
[
12
,
36
,
22
,
30
,
3
]
. For a thorough discussion of the existing results, we refer to the introduction of
[
3
]
. In
[
7
]
, the first named author has determined the Hamiltonian monodromy groups of all toric fibres in certain simple four-dimensional examples, such as monotone
S^{2}\times S^{2}
. This extends the earlier result by Ono
[
30
]
for the Clifford torus in monotone
S^{2}\times S^{2}
to non-monotone split tori.

In this paper we study the Hamiltonian monodromy group of split tori in the spaces
X_{\alpha}
and determine it completely in many cases, though not in all. We find that, in a suitable basis of its first homology, every element in the Hamiltonian monodromy group of a split torus in
X_{\alpha}
is of the form

(5)

\begin{pmatrix}\pm 1&0\\
2k&\pm 1\end{pmatrix}

for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. The Hamiltonian monodromy group subtly depends on arithmetic properties of the base point
(x,y)
of
L=T(x,y)
, and many different isomorphism types of groups arise in this problem; for example
\{1\}
,
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, but also infinite groups such as
\mathbb{Z}
and various semi-direct products of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
with
\mathbb{Z}
. Let us point out that in the case of split tori in monotone
S^{2}\times S^{2}
, only finite groups appear. Our full result is somewhat cumbersome to state; see
Theorem

3.5
for more details.

1.3.
Methods of proof

Split tori are toric fibres of the standard toric structure
X_{\alpha}\rightarrow\square_{\alpha}
and we make extensive use of toric geometry. In particular, based on
[
7
]
, we use symmetric probes to construct Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms realizing equivalences of fibres and given elements of the Hamiltonian monodromy group. Probes were introduced in
[
28
]
and symmetric probes in
[
2
]
for the purpose of displacing toric fibres. The obstructions are derived from Chekanov’s obstructions for product tori in
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
to be symplectomorphic via a lifting trick from toric geometry; see
[
7
, Section 4]
. A key novelty in this paper is the use of properties of billiards to take care of the somewhat involved combinatorics arising in the context of non-monotone
S^{2}\times S^{2}
. A key insight is that, up to reflections
(x,y)\mapsto(\pm x,\pm y)
, only iterated symmetric probes realizing billiard trajectories are needed to complete the classification. In
[
7
]
, the first named author has conjectured that two toric fibres in a closed toric symplectic manifold are Hamiltonian isotopic if and only if there is a sequence of symmetric probes between them.
Theorem

1.3
confirms this conjecture in the case of non-monotone
S^{2}\times S^{2}
.

Corollary 1.9
.

Conjecture 1.3 in
[
7
]
holds for
S^{2}\times S^{2}
equipped with a non-monotone split symplectic form.

Similarly, we suspect that every non-trivial element in the Hamiltonian monodromy group of a split torus is generated by a sequence of symmetric probes starting and ending on the same split torus. Note however that for (
55
) and (
58
),
Theorem

3.5
does not completely determine the monodromy group. Therefore this problem is still open for these cases.

1.4.
Application: Space of Lagrangians

Let
L=T(x,y)
with
r(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}
and
(x,y)\notin\Sigma
. Since the ratio of width to height of the corresponding billiard table
\square_{r(x,y)}
is irrational, there is a dense subset of points in
\partial\square_{r(x,y)}
such that the corresponding tori are Hamiltonian isotopic. Inspired by the recent work of Chassé–Leclercq
[
11
]
, we deduce the following two immediate consequences of
Theorem

1.3
, which give counterexamples to Conjectures A and B in
[
11
]
in dimension four.

Corollary 1.10
.

In every
X_{\alpha}
, there is a split torus
L\subset X_{\alpha}
such that every Weinstein neighbourhood of
L
contains a (and thus infinitely many) split torus
L^{\prime}
with
L\cong L^{\prime}
and
L\cap L^{\prime}=\varnothing
.

By the density of bouncing points in
\partial\square_{r(x,y)}
with
r(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}
, we can select a sequence of points which converges to a point not in the set of bouncing points to obtain the following.

Corollary 1.11
.

In every
X_{\alpha}
, there is a sequence of split tori
L_{i}\subset X_{\alpha}
which are mutually Hamiltonian isotopic,
L_{i}\cong L_{j}
, and which converge in the
C^{\infty}
-topology to a split torus
L\subset X_{\alpha}
with
L\not\cong L_{i}
.

In particular, the orbit under
\operatorname{Ham}(X_{\alpha})
of such a torus in the space of Lagrangians is neither
C^{\infty}
-closed, nor locally path connected. Both these phenomena occur in any symplectic manifold of dimension greater than or equal to six. As was proved in
[
11
]
, this follows from Chekanov’s classification
[
12
]
of product tori in
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, see also
[
7
]
for a slightly different point of view.

Remark 1.12.

Note that the Lebesgue measure on
\square_{\alpha}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
induces a natural measure on the set of split tori in
X_{\alpha}
. The subset of tori having the properties from the Corollaries
1.10
and
1.11
have full measure.

Remark 1.13.

As was pointed out to us by Felix Schlenk, one can also use
[
14
, Theorem 1.5]
to produce results of this type in dimension four. Note however, that their methods work only for small tori contained in a Darboux chart, and, in the special case of
X_{\alpha}
, only for certain
\alpha>0
.

1.5.
Application: Lagrangian packing

Definition 1.14
.

Let
L
be a Lagrangian in a symplectic manifold
(X,\omega)
. The
packing number
of
L
is defined as

(6)

\#_{P}(L)=\sup\{N\in\mathbb{N}\,|\,\exists\,\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{N}\in\operatorname{Ham}(X,\omega),\,\phi_{i}(L)\cap\phi_{j}(L)=\varnothing,\,\forall i\neq j\}.

The following remarkable result is due to Polterovich–Shelukhin, see
[
31
, Theorem C]
. It uses ideas introduced by Mak–Smith
[
26
]
, who proved the non-displaceability of certain links of Lagrangian tori in
X_{\alpha}
by computing Floer homology in the symmetric product.

Theorem 1.15

(Polterovich–Shelukhin)
.

Let
\alpha>0
,
L=T(x,0)\in X_{\alpha}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geqslant 2}
. Then

(7)

|x|<\frac{k-1}{k+1}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\#_{P}(L)\leqslant k.

In §
5.1
, we give details on how to adapt
[
31
, Theorem C]
to our notational conventions. For any
|x|\in\left(\frac{k-2}{k},\frac{k-1}{k+1}\right)
, the bound (
7
) is optimal for small enough
\alpha>0
. Indeed, in that case, a
k
-packing can be realized by packing the first factor in
X_{\alpha}=S^{2}\times S^{2}
by
k
circles.

Since
\frac{k-1}{k+1}\rightarrow 1
for
k\rightarrow+\infty
,
Theorem

1.15
implies that the packing number of every split torus
T(x,0)
with
|x|<1
is finite. We show that this rigidity breaks down for
|x|>1
, answering
[
31
, Question 18]
and showing that, up to the critical case
|x|=1
, which remains open, the results in
[
31
]
are sharp. Indeed, since distinct split tori are disjoint,
Theorem

1.3
yields many interesting packings by split tori. In particular, we find
\#_{P}(L)=\infty
for split tori
L=T(x,0)
with
x\in(1,1+\alpha)\setminus\mathbb{Q}
. This is a special case of the following, which itself follows immediately from
Theorem

1.3
and the fact that good billiard trajectories have infinitely many bouncing points whenever the ratio of width and height of the billiard table is irrational.

Corollary 1.16
.

Let
L=T(x,y)\subset X_{\alpha}
be a split torus with
r(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}
. Then

(8)

\#_{P}(L)=\infty.

Note, again, that this shows that generic split tori have this property. As a consequence of
Theorem

1.3
, we determine all packing numbers of
X_{\alpha}

by disjoint split tori
, see

5.2
and

. This gives a lower bound on the actual Lagrangian packing number, which is optimal in some cases but not in others, see also the discussion in §

1.6.
Application: Late Poincaré recurrence

The Lagrangian Poincaré recurrence conjecture can be stated as follows.

Conjecture 1.17

(Lagrangian Poincaré recurrence Conjecture)
.

Let
\psi\in\operatorname{Ham}(X,\omega)
and let
L\subset(X,\omega)
be a closed Lagrangian submanifold. Then there exists a sequence
k_{i}\rightarrow\infty
of natural numbers such that
\psi^{k_{i}}(L)\cap L\neq\varnothing
. Furthermore, the density of the sequence
k_{i}
is related to a symplectic capacity of
L

We refer to
[
20

, where the conjecture is attributed to Ginzburg and Viterbo independently. Recently, Broćić–Shelukhin
[
10
]
have found counterexamples to the LPR Conjecture in all symplectic manifolds of dimensions greater than or equal to six and Schmitz
[
32
]
in some symplectic manifolds of dimension four. None of these results apply to the case of
S^{2}\times S^{2}
. Using our methods, we prove a related but weaker result: For certain fixed split tori
L\subset X_{\alpha}
, we find Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms realizing arbitrarily large (but finite) return time.

Proposition 1.18
.

Let
\alpha>0
and let
L=T(x,y)\subset X_{\alpha}
be a split torus with
r(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}

N\in\mathbb{N}

(9)

\psi^{k}(L)\cap L=\varnothing,\quad\text{for all }k\in\{1,\ldots,N\}.

This follows straightforwardly from iterated symmetric probes, see the proof in
Section

\phi\in\operatorname{Ham}(X_{\alpha})
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, let

(10)

\mathcal{T}_{\phi,N}=\{(x,y)\in\operatorname{int}\square_{\alpha}\,|\,\phi^{k}(T(x,y))\cap T(x,y)=\varnothing,\,\forall k\in\{1,\ldots,N\}\},

i.e. this is the set of all split tori whose images under
\phi
are disjoint under the
N
-th iterates. We define a family
\{\psi_{\delta}\}_{\delta>0}\subset\operatorname{Ham}(X_{\alpha})
out of which one can pick an element making this set arbitrarily large for arbitrarily large

with normalization

Theorem 1.19
.

For all
\varepsilon>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}

\delta>0

(11)

\lambda(\mathcal{T}_{\psi_{\delta},N})>1-\varepsilon.

2.1.
Symmetric probes

For the construction of equivalences of split tori, we make use of the standard toric structure
X_{\alpha}\rightarrow\square_{\alpha}
for which split tori appear as toric fibres. This allows us to use symmetric probes first used for the purpose of constructing Hamiltonian isotopies between fibres. Let us recall the basics of this technique for the reader’s convenience. For details, we refer to
[
7
]
. A
symmetric probe

\sigma\subset\Delta
in the Delzant polytope arising as moment map image
\Delta\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of a toric symplectic manifold is a segment intersecting
\partial\Delta
integrally transversely in two points. Integral transversality means that
\sigma
has a rational direction and that its primitive directional vector can be completed to a basis of the lattice by vectors parallel to the facet the probe intersects. This condition implies that one can perform symplectic
T^{n-1}
-reduction by a subgroup of the toric action and obtain a smooth copy of
S^{2}
as quotient. Toric fibres corresponding to points lying on the symmetric probe map to circles in the reduced space, and Hamiltonian isotopies of these circles lift to Hamiltonian isotopies of the corresponding fibres. This yields
[
7
, Theorem A]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
X
be a toric symplectic manifold with moment polytope
\Delta
and
\sigma\subset\Delta
a symmetric probe. If
p,q\in\sigma
lie at equal distance to the boundary
\partial\Delta
, then
T(p)\cong T(q)
.

Let us now discuss the case
X=X_{\alpha}
, where we have
\Delta=\square_{\alpha}=[-\alpha-1,\alpha+1]\times[-\alpha,\alpha]\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The symmetric probes in
\square_{\alpha}
are easy to understand, see
Figure

\ell
with slope
(1,0)
or
(0,1)
that intersects
\square_{\alpha}
in its interior yields a symmetric probe. We call these probes
horizontal
and
vertical
, respectively. Every segment with slope
(1,1)
and
(-1,1)
which intersects
\square_{\alpha}
but none of its vertices yields a symmetric probe. The latter two types of probes will play a crucial role for us, as they generate Hamiltonian equivalences corresponding to billiards. Note that for small

, there are other symmetric probes, namely those of slope
(k,-1)
for

, which intersect only horizontal edges of
\square_{\alpha}
. However, for

these probes are redundant for the classification of toric fibres and determining their Hamiltonian monodromy group and will not be used in the paper.

.

\{(x,y)+2kr(1,1)\,|\,k\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

Proof of main theorem

Theorem

is a consequence of the following.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\alpha>0
and let
(x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in Q\cup\Sigma
, then the following are equivalent:

(1)

One of the points
(\pm x^{\prime},\pm y^{\prime})
is in the set
B(x,y)
of admissible bouncing points of the good billiard trajectory of
(x,y)
;

(2)

The split tori
T(x,y)
and
T(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
are Hamiltonian isotopic;

(3)

If
(x,y)\in\Sigma
, then
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\Sigma
and
(x,y)
is equal to some
(\pm x^{\prime},\pm y^{\prime})
. If
(x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in Q
, then

(23)

y^{\prime}=y,\quad x=\pm x^{\prime}+2k_{1}+2k_{2}y,\quad\text{for some }k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}.

Lemma

2.2
shows (1)
\Rightarrow
(2) and
Lemma

2.4
shows (3)
\Rightarrow
(1). To complete, we need to show (2)
\Rightarrow

2.7
. To prepare its proof, let us discuss some of its ingredients.

We use obstructions from
[
7
]
, which apply to toric fibres in general compact toric manifolds. Let
X
be a compact toric symplectic manifold with moment polytope
\Delta\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and denote by

.

Let

\Delta

, where

,

(2)

\#_{d}(p)=\#_{d}(q)
, where

(3)

\Gamma(p)=\Gamma(q)
, where
\Gamma(p)=\mathbb{Z}\langle\ell_{1}(p)-d(p),\ldots,\ell_{N}(p)-d(p)\rangle\subset\mathbb{R}
.

the subgroup of linear combinations with integer coefficients of

. For

\ell_{1}(x,y)=\alpha-y,\quad\ell_{2}(x,y)=1+\alpha-x,\quad\ell_{3}(x,y)=\alpha+y,\quad\ell_{4}(x,y)=1+\alpha+x.

[
7
, Theorem 4.7]

4.2.
The Chekanov torus

Let us briefly recall the definition of the Chekanov torus in
\mathbb{C}^{2}=\{(z_{1},z_{2})\}
via the lifting of curves from a symplectic quotient, which is essentially based on the viewpoint of Eliashberg–Polterovich
[
16

. See
[
6
]
for a detailed exposition or
[
12
,
13
,
4
]
for slightly different points of view. The Hamiltonian

generates a Hamiltonian circle action. Its level set

is singular, but one can still make sense of symplectic reduction by

in the complement of the singular point

. The symplectic quotient carries a residual Hamiltonian

-action induced by the
T^{2}
-action on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
and it can be equivariantly identified with
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
equipped with the restriction of the standard symplectic form of
\mathbb{C}
and the standard Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-action by rotation. Denote the projection map by
\pi_{0}\colon H^{-1}(0)\setminus\{(0,0)\}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. This map establishes a bijection between Lagrangian tori in
H^{-1}(0)\setminus\{(0,0)\}
and smooth closed embedded curves in
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. In particular, the Clifford torus

corresponds to the standard circle

. On the other hand, it turns out that any curve which is not isotopic to any

in

lifts to a copy of the Chekanov torus.

Definition 4.3

For every

7.
Open questions and discussion

In this section we discuss open questions surrounding our results.

7.1.
Classification of toric fibres and their Hamiltonian monodromy

After
S^{2}\times S^{2}
, the next natural case in which one can ask for a classification of toric fibres is the one-fold blow up of
\mathbb{C}P^{2}
. Up to scaling, it supports a one-parametric family of symplectic forms depending on the size of the blow-up. Even in the monotone case, the classification is unknown.

Question 7.1
.

What is the classification of toric fibres in the one-fold blow-up of
\mathbb{C}P^{2}
?

As discussed in
Remark

1.6
, a classification for all symplectic forms would, together with our results, yield a classification of toric fibres in all Hirzebruch surfaces. Another question raised by our results is determining the Hamiltonian monodromy group in the cases which remain open in
Theorem

3.5
.

Question 7.2
.

What is the Hamiltonian monodromy group in the cases (
55
) and (
58
)?

7.2.
Lagrangian packing

Question 7.3
.

What is the packing number of a given split torus in
X_{\alpha}
?

The only cases where it is known are as follows

(85)

\#_{P}(T(x,y))=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }(x,y)=(0,0),\\
2&\text{ if }y=0\text{ and }|x|\in\left(0,\frac{1}{3}\right),\\
k&\text{ if }y=0\text{ and }|x|\in\left(\frac{k-2}{k},\frac{k-1}{k+1}\right)\text{ and }\alpha\text{ small enough,}\\
\infty&\text{ if }r(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}.\end{cases}

The first case is simply non-displaceability which follows for example from
[
17
]
, since all other split tori are displaceable. The following two cases are consequences of
Theorem

1.15
and maximal packings obtained by packing the larger sphere factor by circles (this is where the smallness of
\alpha
is required), and the last case is
Corollary

1.16
. The case of the split tori
T(\pm 1,0)\subset X_{\alpha}
is particularly interesting, since it is the boundary case between the rigid region of
Theorem

1.15
and the flexible region where our methods apply. Even when the packing number is infinite, one can ask whether a packing configuration
\mathcal{P}\subset X_{\alpha}
of disjoint copies of a Lagrangian
L
yielding the infinite packing number is maximal in the sense that

(86)

\phi(L)\cap\mathcal{P}\neq\varnothing\quad\text{for all}\quad\phi\in\operatorname{Ham}(X_{\alpha}).

In many cases, packing configurations by split tori obtained by our methods are not maximal, since more copies which are not split tori can be packed into the larger one of the sphere factors.

Question 7.4
.

Let
X
be a toric symplecic manifold. For which toric fibres is the toric packing number
\#_{TP}(L)
equal to the actual Lagrangian packing number
\#_{P}(L)
?

Note that a classification of toric fibres up to Hamiltonian diffeomorphism always yields the toric packing number, see
[
7
]
for more examples of such classifications.

7.3.
Ball-embeddable Lagrangian tori

Let
(X,\omega)
be a four-dimensional symplectic manifold. Denote the set of ball-Clifford, ball-Chekanov and ball-nonmonotone Lagrangian tori in
X
by
{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Cl},{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Ch}
and
{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm nm}
, respectively. Recall that for
X=X_{\alpha}
we have

(87)

{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Cl}\cap{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Ch}=\varnothing,\quad{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Cl}\cap{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm nm}=\varnothing,\quad{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Ch}\cap{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm nm}\neq\varnothing,

by
Theorem

1.23
. By similar arguments, one finds that these three sets are mutually disjoint for monotone
S^{2}\times S^{2}
. For
X=\mathbb{C}P^{2}
the situation is

(88)

{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Cl}\cap{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Ch}=\varnothing,\quad{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Cl}\cap{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm nm}\neq\varnothing,\quad{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Ch}\cap{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm nm}=\varnothing.

This suggests the following question.

Question 7.5
.

Are the sets
{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Cl}
and
{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Ch}
disjoint for all
X
?

It is crucial that the embedded domains are balls. For polydisks for example, some images of Chekanov tori are Hamiltonian isotopic to images of Clifford tori. This happens for example in
X_{\alpha}
by arguments similar to those used in the proof of
Theorem

1.22
.

We can positively answer
Question

7.5
for tori which are small relative to the size of the balls in which they are contained.

Proposition 7.6
.

Let
L=\varphi(T_{\rm Cl}(a))
for a symplectic ball embedding
\varphi\colon B^{4}(r)\hookrightarrow X
and
L^{\prime}=\varphi^{\prime}(T_{\rm Ch}(a^{\prime}))

\varphi^{\prime}\colon B^{4}(r^{\prime})\hookrightarrow X

(89)

a,a^{\prime}<\frac{1}{3}\min\{r,r^{\prime}\},

.

This follows from a local computation of displacement energy germs as in
[
8
,
9
]
. The inequalities (

[
14
]
, of
J
-holomorphic disks with boundary on the respective Lagrangians and intersecting the complement of the ball. These area-estimates are sharp as can be seen by taking a volume-filling ball embedding into
\mathbb{C}P^{2}
. Similar statements can be made in higher dimensions.

Recall that we have proved that a split torus of the form
L=T(x,0)
with
-1\leqslant x\leqslant 1
is in neither of the sets
{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Cl}
,
{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm Ch},{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm nm}
.

Question 7.7
.

Let
L=T(x,0)\subset X_{\alpha}
be a split torus with
-1\leqslant x\leqslant 1
. Is
L
ball-embeddable?

We suspect that the answer is no, but can only prove it in the following special cases.

Proposition 7.8
.

Let
L=T(x,0)\subset X_{\alpha}
be a split torus. If
x=0
or
|x|<1-\alpha

\alpha\geqslant 1

[

, whereas the latter follows from the proof of Theorem 8.1 in
[

. On the other hand,

for all
\alpha<1

\varphi\colon B^{4}(r)\hookrightarrow X_{\alpha}
. For
\alpha\geqslant 1
, the displacement is less obvious, but also finite.

The question of ball-embeddability can be asked for
any
Lagrangian torus
L\subset(X,\omega)
. Let us mention a few straightforward results in that direction. In monotone
S^{2}\times S^{2}
, every split torus except for the product of equators is ball-embeddable, as can be seen by taking all possible toric ball embeddings. On the other hand, every non-displaceable torus in monotone
S^{2}\times S^{2}
is not ball-embeddable. Indeed, every symplectic ball in
S^{2}\times S^{2}
is displaceable and hence can only contain displaceable tori. Examples of this include the infinitely many monotone tori constructed by Vianna
[
34
]
, as well as the non-monotone family of non-displaceable tori found by Fukaya–Oh–Ohta–Ono in
[
18
]
. In
\mathbb{C}P^{2}
on the other hand, every Lagrangian torus is ball-embeddable. Indeed, every Lagrangian torus in
\mathbb{C}P^{2}
can be moved off of the line at infinity by a Hamiltonian isotopy, see
[
35
, Theorem 1.3]
or
[
15
, Theorem C]
. For the same reason, every Lagrangian torus in monotone
S^{2}\times S^{2}
is
polydisk-embeddable
, i.e. contained in the image of a symplectic embedding of the product of two disks of equal areas.

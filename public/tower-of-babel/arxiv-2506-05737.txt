Search for Lorentz Invariance Violation with spectral lags of GRB 190114C using profile likelihood
Vyaas Ramakrishnan, Shantanu Desai

Abstract
We search for Lorentz invariance violation (LIV) using the spectral lag data for GRB 190114C using frequentist analysis, where we deal with the astrophysical nuisance parameters using profile likelihood. For this use case, we find a global minima for the χ 2 \chi^{2} as a function of energy scale of LIV ( E Q ​ G E_{QG} ), well below the Planck scale. The best-fit 2 ​ σ 2\sigma central intervals for E Q ​ G E_{QG} are given by 2.81 − 0.72 + 0.96 × 10 14 2.81^{+0.96}_{-0.72}\times 10^{14} GeV and 9.10 − 0.64 + 2.59 × 10 5 9.10^{+2.59}_{-0.64}\times 10^{5} for linear and quadratic LIV, respectively and agree with the Bayesian estimates hitherto obtained in a previous work. Therefore, the results from frequentist analysis GRB 190114C agrees with Bayesian analysis and presents yet another proof of principles applications of profile likelihood in the search for LIV.



I
Introduction

The spectral lags of gamma-ray bursts (GRB) have proved to be a powerful probe of Lorentz invariance violation (LIV)
(
Desai 2024
;
Yu et al. 2022
;
Wei and Wu 2022
)
. The spectral lag is defined as the time difference between the arrival of high- and low-energy photons. Until recently, all searches for LIV using GRB spectral lags had been done using Bayesian analysis. A frequentist search for LIV using the spectral lag data for GRB 160625B was first carried out in
Desai and Ganguly 2025
, where the astrophysical nuisance parameters were dealt with using profile likelihood, instead of marginalization as is done in Bayesian analysis. This was followed by another frequentist-based search for LIV with the spectral lags of 56 GRBs in the cosmological rest frame
(
Ramakrishnan and Desai 2025
)
.

In this work, we carry out yet another test of LIV with frequentist analysis using the spectral lag data for GRB 190114C, which hitherto has been analyzed using Bayesian inference in
multiple works
(
Du et al. 2021
;
Agrawal et al. 2021
)
. This manuscript is structured as follows. The analysis methodology is described in Sect.
II
. Our results are discussed in Sect.
III
and we conclude in Sect.
IV
.

II
Data and Analysis

GRB 190114C is a long GRB, which was triggered by SWIFT-BAT and Fermi-GBM on 14 Jan. 2019, and is located at a redshift
z=0.4245\pm 0.0005
.
Du et al. 2021
(D20, hereafter) have calculated the GRB spectral lags with respect to the reference energy of 10-12 keV using the cross-correlation method
(
Ukwatta et al. 2012
;
Zhang et al. 2012
)
. D20 obtained 19 spectral lags with respect to the lowest energy band of (10-15) keV, with the highest energy band equal to (1700-5000) keV. These spectral lags have been collated in Table 1 of D20.

The expected spectral time lag from this GRB can be written as the sum of the intrinsic astrophysical lag and that induced because of LIV.

\Delta t_{exp}=\Delta t_{int}+\Delta t_{LIV},

(1)

where
\Delta t_{int}
is the intrinsic time lag in the GRB rest frame of due to astrophysical effects. D20 used the following parameterization for the intrinsic time lag, which has also been previously used to model the time lags of GRB 160625B
(
Wei et al. 2017
;
Ganguly and Desai 2017
)
.

\Delta t_{int}(E)=\tau\left[\left(\frac{E_{0}}{keV}\right)^{-\alpha}-\left(\frac{E}{keV}\right)^{-\alpha}\right],

(2)

where
E_{0}=12.5
keV.
In Eq.
1
, the expression for sub-luminal LIV-induced time lag (
\Delta t_{LIV}
) is given by
(
Jacob and Piran 2008
)
:

\Delta t_{LIV}=-\left(\frac{1+n}{2H_{0}}\right)\left(\frac{E^{n}-E_{0}^{n}}{E_{QG,n}^{n}}\right)\frac{1}{(1+z)^{n}}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{(1+z^{\prime})^{n}}{h(z^{\prime})}\,dz^{\prime}

(3)

where
E_{QG,n}
is the quantum gravity scale corresponding to the scale of LIV and
H_{0}
is the Hubble constant. In Eq.
3
,
n=1
and
n=2
correspond to linear and quadratic LIV models, respectively.
In Eq.
3
,

h(z)\equiv\frac{H(z)}{H_{0}}
is the dimensionless Hubble parameter as a function of redshift. For the current standard flat
\Lambda
CDM model,
h(z)=\sqrt{\Omega_{M}(1+z^{\prime})^{3}+1-\Omega_{M}}
, where
\Omega_{M}
is the cosmological matter density.
On the other hand, D20 as well as one of our previous works
(
Agrawal et al. 2021
)
parameterized
h(z)
in a model-independent manner with Gaussian process regression using cosmic chronometers as probes of expansion history. However, in another work on LIV using spectral lags, we have shown that the results of the two are comparable
(
Desai et al. 2023
)
, and therefore we use the
\Lambda
CDM model to parameterize
h(z)
.

We now construct the maximum likelihood which is given by

\mathcal{L}=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\sigma_{tot}\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\left\{-\frac{[\Delta t_{i}-\Delta t_{exp}]^{2}}{2\sigma_{tot}^{2}}\right\},

(4)

where
\Delta t_{i}
corresponds to the observed time lag;
\Delta t_{exp}
is given by Eq.
1
;
N
is the total number of spectral lags for GRB 190114C (19); and
\sigma_{tot}
denotes the total uncertainty which is given by

\sigma_{tot}^{2}=\sigma_{t}^{2}+\Big(\frac{\partial\Delta t_{exp}}{\partial E}\Big)^{2}\sigma_{E}^{2},.

(5)

where
\sigma_{t}
is the uncertainty in the spectral delay and
\sigma_{E}
is the uncertainty in the energy band, and as described in D20, correspodns to half of each energy band, given by
0.5(E_{max}-E_{min})
.

Similarly to our recent works on profile likelihood
(
Desai and Ganguly 2025
;
Ramakrishnan and Desai 2025
;
Barua et al. 2025
)

we define
\chi^{2}\equiv-2\mathcal{L}
, where
\mathcal{L}
is defined in Eq.
4
. For this problem, the parameter of interest is
E_{QG}
, while
\tau
and
\alpha
constitute the nuisance parameters. We first construct a grid of
E_{QG}
with a log-uniform spacing between
10^{6}
and
10^{19}
GeV. For each fixed value of
E_{QG}
, we calculate the minimum
\chi^{2}
over
\tau
and
\alpha
. We then repeat this procedure for all values of
E_{QG}
to determine the global minimum
\chi^{2}
(
\chi^{2}_{min}
).
According to Wilks’ theorem
\Delta\chi^{2}
defined as
\chi^{2}(E_{QG})-\chi^{2}_{min}
follows a
\chi^{2}
distribution for one degree of freedom
(
Wilks 1938
)
. We now describe the results of our analysis in the next section.

III
Results

The
\chi^{2}
minimization was done using
scipy.optimize.fmin
module in Python. We tested using multiple algorithms and all of them gave the same answer.
For both models of LIV, we find a parabolic shape for
\chi^{2}
as a function of
E_{QG}
with a global minimum below the Planck scale. The plots of
\Delta\chi^{2}
as a function of
E_{QG}
can be found in Fig.
1
and Fig.
2
for linear and quadratic LIV, respectively. The 95.4% c.l. (
2\sigma
) central intervals are obtained by finding the X-intercept corresponding to
\Delta\chi^{2}=4.0
. The best-fit
2\sigma
central intervals thus obtained for
E_{QG}
are equal to
2.81^{+0.96}_{-0.72}\times 10^{14}
GeV and

9.10^{+2.59}_{-0.64}\times 10^{5}
for linear and quadratic LIV, respectively.
These values agree within
1\sigma
compared to the marginalized 68% credible intervals for
E_{QG}
estimated in D20.

In order to judge the efficacy of the fit, we calculate the
\chi^{2}_{fit}
based on the residuals between the data and best-fit model

\chi^{2}_{fit}=\sum_{i}^{N}\left(\frac{\Delta t_{i}-\Delta t_{exp}}{\sigma_{tot}}\right)^{2}\,

(6)

where all terms have the same meaning as in Eq.
4
.
Note that
\chi^{2}_{fit}
is used to ascertain the quality of the fit. It differs from
\chi^{2}
obtained using profile likelihood, since the latter contains an extra term
\frac{1}{\sigma_{tot}}
outside the exponent. The best-fit
\chi^{2}
along with the DOF can be found in Table
1
. Similar to D20, our best-fit reduced
\chi^{2}_{fit}
values are
<1
. We find that the reduced
\chi^{2}
values are less than 1, confirming that the fit is good.
Table
1
also contains the best-fit values for
\tau
and
\alpha
for both the LIV models. These best-fit values also agree within
1\sigma
with the corresponding marginalized 68% values for
\tau
and
E_{QG}
obtained in D20.
Therefore, for GRB 190114C, the results from frequentist inference agree with those from Bayesian inference.

IV
Conclusions

In this work, we analyze the spectral lag data of GRB 190114C, in order to search for LIV using frequentist inference, which is complementary to the Bayesian analysis carried out in D20. For this purpose, we use profile likelihood to dispense with the nuisance parameters related to astrophysical parameters, which govern the intrinsic emission.

In this case, we find a global minimum for
\chi^{2}
as a function of
E_{QG}
below the Planck scale for both the LIV models. The distribution of
\Delta\chi^{2}
as a function of
E_{QG}
can be found in Fig.
1
and Fig.
2
for linear and quadratic LIV, respectively. The central
2\sigma
intervals for
E_{QG}
are given by
2.81^{+0.96}_{-0.72}\times 10^{14}
GeV and

9.10^{+2.59}_{-0.64}\times 10^{5}
for linear and quadratic LIV, respectively.
These values agree within
1\sigma
compared to the marginalized 68% credible intervals for
E_{QG}
estimated in D20. Therefore, for the spectral lag data of GRB 190114C, the results of frequentist analysis agree with Bayesian inference. Therefore this work represents yet another proof of principles application of profile likelihood in searches for Lorentz invariance violation
using GRB spectral lags.

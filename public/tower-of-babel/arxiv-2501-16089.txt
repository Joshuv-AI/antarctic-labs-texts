Categories of skew left braces and trifactorised groups
A. Ballester-Bolinches, R. Esteban-Romero, P. Pérez-Altarriba, V. Pérez-Calabuig

Abstract
The main objective of this paper is to deepen the relationship between skew left braces and trifactorised groups that encodes the information about skew left braces, their structure, their quotients, and their homomorphisms. Keywords: category, skew left brace, trifactorised group Mathematics Subject Classification (2020): 16T25, 81R50, 20C35, 20C99, 20D40,



1
Introduction

A
skew left brace

(B,{+},{\cdot})
consists of a set with two binary operations
+
and
\cdot
such that
(B,{+})
and
(B,{\cdot})
are groups linked by a distributive-like law
a(b+c)=ab-a+ac
for
a
,
b
,
c\in B
. As it is usual in this context, the multiplication symbol is omitted,
-a
denotes the symmetric element of
a
with respect to
+
, and
a-b
denotes
a+(-b)
. When the operations on
B
are clear from the context, we will refer to a brace as
B
instead of
(B,{+},{\cdot})
. Skew left braces were introduced in
[
11
]
and generalise the
left braces
introduced in
[
12
]
, in which the first operation is also assumed to be commutative. Skew left braces are interesting algebraic structures, because they represent the exact algebraic framework to study bijective non-degenerate solutions to the
Yang–Baxter equation (see
[
14
,
4
,
9
]
). For simplicity, we will use the word
brace
with the meaning of
skew left brace
.

One of the most important tools in the structural study of braces is the analysis of the trifactorised group associated with the action of the multiplicative group on the additive group. Many brace structural properties can be translated into group structural properties of this trifactorised group which is called here
large trifactorised group
(see
[
2
]
). Another trifactorised group associated with a brace
B
appears in a natural way when identifying
B
with a regular subgroup of the holomorph of the additive group. This group, called here
small trifactorised group
, is extremely useful to construct braces with some prescribed properties (see
[
3
]
). Small trifactorised groups associated with particular braces lead to construction of interesting examples of groups that can be used to answer some important questions about products of groups (see
[
1
, Chapter 6]
).

The aim of this paper is twofold. Firstly, we study the complete family of trifactorised groups associated with a given brace and secondly, we study braces and their associated trifactorised groups from a categorical point of view. Many of the problems that we discuss in this paper appear in a natural way when considering representations of braces. The following two remarkable results are proved.

1.

There is a bijection between the isomorphism classes of the trifactorised groups associated with a brace
B
and the orbits of an action of the automorphism group of
B
over some normal subgroups of the multiplicative group. This result will be useful for computing the trifactorised groups associated with
B
(Theorem
4.7
).

2.

All trifactorised groups associated with a brace
B
are quotients of the large trifactorised group associated with
B
and have the small trifactorised group associated with
B
as a quotient (Theorem
6.11
).

We also consider two main categories: the category
\mathbf{SKB}
whose objects are braces, and whose morphisms are homomorphisms of braces, that are maps between braces that preserve both operations, and the category
\mathbf{3factGrp}
whose objects are trifactorised groups satisfying some restrictions, and the morphisms are group homomorphisms which are compatible with the triple factorisation. We show here that these two categories are closely related and have interesting properties. It is worth mentioning that some properties of
\mathbf{SKB}
have been studied in
[
5
]
.

The contents of the paper are organised as follows. We will begin by introducing two categories in Section
2
, corresponding to the large and small trifactorised groups. Then we will introduce
\mathbf{3factGrp}
in Section
3
, where we will show that for every brace we can define trifactorised groups satisfying certain conditions and that every trifactorised group with these conditions can be understood as a trifactorised group associated with a brace. We also show how to construct brace homomorphisms from trifactorised group morphisms, and give the exact condition for a brace homomorphism to define a trifactorised group morphism. It turns out that the categories of small and large trifactorised groups are subcategories of
\mathbf{3factGrp}
. Our next objective, developed in Section
5
, is to identify the substructures of a brace
B
in a trifactorised group associated with
B
, and study the substructures of trifactorised groups. We will see that there is a bijection between subbraces and these substructures. We also indicate how to use the trifactorised group to compute images of subgroups of the multiplicative group and preimages of subgroups of the additive group under the associated derivation with the help of the trifactorised group. In Section
6
, we characterise the normal subgroups of our trifactorised groups giving quotients that can be regarded as trifactorised groups, and show that they are associated with the corresponding brace quotient.

The efficiency of algorithms for finite groups in computer algebra systems like
GAP

[
10
]
, especially in the case of soluble groups and, in, to a lesser extent, in the case of permutation groups, makes us think that encoding braces as trifactorised groups can help us to simplify computations with braces. Trifactorised groups introduced here are, in general, of smaller order than the groups given in
[
2
]
. This can be used to save memory. The saving when using the small trifactorised groups is very relevant in the case of trivial braces, where the small trifactorised group coincides with the additive group.

We also would like to mention that trifactorised groups will be in a forthcoming paper to present a definition of representations of braces over a vector space. We will see there that these brace representations are equivalent to some representations of trifactorised groups over a vector space. It turns out that trifactorised groups are a natural tool to reduce some problems on brace representations to problems on group representations.

2
Large and small trifactorised groups

Given a brace
B
, there is an action of the multiplicative group
C=(B,{\cdot})
on the additive group
K=(B,{+})
called the
lambda action
or the
lambda map
which is defined by
\lambda(a)=\lambda_{a}
for every
a\in B
, where
\lambda_{a}(b)=-a+ab
for all
a
,
b\in B
. Sysak
[
13
]
observed that the identity map
\delta\colon C\longrightarrow K
is a bijective derivation or
1
-cocycle with respect to the lambda action and that the

G=[K]C
with respect to the lambda action possesses a triple factorisation
G=KC=KD=CD
, where
D=\{\delta(c)c\mid c\in C\}
is a subgroup of
G
, and
K\cap C=K\cap D=C\cap D=1
. We will refer to it as the
large trifactorised group
associated with
B
, and denoted by
\mathsf{L}(B)=(G,K,D,C)
.

It seems natural to consider the category
\mathbf{L3factGrp}
whose objects are trifactorised groups of the form
(G,K,H,C)
where
K\trianglelefteq G
,
H\leq G
,
C\leq G
,
G=KH=KC=HC
,
K\cap H=K\cap C=H\cap C=1
, and whose morphisms
f\colon(G_{1},K_{1},H_{1},C_{1})\longrightarrow(G_{2},K_{2},H_{2},C_{2})
are group homomorphisms
f\colon G_{1}\longrightarrow G_{2}
such that
f(K_{1})\leq K_{2}
,
f(H_{1})\leq H_{1}
, and
f(C_{1})\leq C_{2}
. If

is a brace homomorphism and
\mathsf{L}(B_{1})=(G_{1},K_{1},H_{1},C_{1})
and
\mathsf{L}(B_{2})=(G_{2},K_{2},H_{2},C_{2})
are the large trifactorised groups corresponding to

and

, respectively, then the induced group homomorphisms

and

induce a group homomorphism

and, given an element

, where

is the identity map of

, regarded as a bijective derivation associated with the lambda map, we have that

Another natural approach to braces is by means of regular subgroups of the holomorph of the additive group. When this additive group corresponds to a vector space, we have regular subgroups of the affine group, analysed in

, where
\mathsf{S}(B_{1})=(G_{1},K_{1},H_{1},E_{1})
,

A new category of trifactorised groups

Let
(B,+,\cdot)
be a brace with additive group
K
and multiplicative group
C

\lambda\colon C\longrightarrow\Aut(K)
be the corresponding lambda map and
\delta\colon C\longrightarrow K
the identity map. Consider
\eta\colon C\longrightarrow E
a group epimorphism such that
\ker\eta\leq\ker\lambda
. Then we can define the group homomorphism
\bar{\lambda}\colon E\longrightarrow\Aut(K)
such that
\bar{\lambda}(\eta(c))=\lambda(c)
for all

. Hence

acts on
K
by automorphisms, and so we can consider the corresponding semidirect product
G=[K]E
.

Consider the map from
C
to
G
defined by
c\mapsto\delta(c)\eta(c)
, which is injective with image
H=\{\delta(c)\eta(c)\mid c\in C\}
. Moreover, given

\displaystyle(\delta(c_{1})\eta(c_{1}))(\delta(c_{2})\eta(c_{2}))

\displaystyle=\delta(c_{1})\bar{\lambda}_{\eta(c_{1})}(\delta(c_{2}))\eta(c_{1})\eta(c_{2})

\displaystyle=\delta(c_{1})\lambda_{c_{1}}(\delta(c_{2}))\eta(c_{1})\eta(c_{2})

\displaystyle=\delta(c_{1}c_{2})\eta(c_{1}c_{2}).

Therefore

is a subgroup of

isomorphic to

Every element

can be uniquely written as a product

for
c_{1},c_{2}\in C
, and

G=KE=KH=HE,\quad K\cap E=H\cap E=1.

(1)

The map
\sigma\colon H\longrightarrow K
given by
\sigma(\delta(c)\eta(c))=\delta(c)
is bijective and

\displaystyle\sigma((\delta(c_{1})\eta(c_{1}))(\delta(c_{2})\eta(c_{2})))

\displaystyle=\sigma(\delta(c_{1})\lambda_{c_{1}}(\delta(c_{2}))\eta(c_{1}c_{2}))

\displaystyle=\delta(c_{1})\lambda_{c_{1}}(\delta(c_{2}))

\displaystyle=\sigma(\delta(c_{1})\eta(c_{1}))\bar{\lambda}_{\eta(c_{1})}(\sigma(\delta(c_{2})\eta(c_{2}))).

Therefore,

is a bijective derivation with respect to the action
\bar{\lambda}\circ\pi_{E}|_{H}\colon H\longrightarrow\Aut(K)

The tuple

is called a

associated with

3.1
Morphisms of trifactorised groups

Definition 3.5
.

A
morphism

f\colon(G_{1},K_{1},H_{1},E_{1})\longrightarrow(G_{2},K_{2},H_{2},E_{2})
between two trifactorised groups is a group homomorphism
f\colon G_{1}\longrightarrow G_{2}
such that
f(K_{1})\leq K_{2}
,
f(H_{1})\leq H_{2}
, and
f(E_{1})\leq E_{2}
.

These conditions gives us three group homomorphisms, namely
f|_{K_{1}}\colon K_{1}\longrightarrow K_{2}
,
f|_{H_{1}}\colon H_{1}\longrightarrow H_{2}
,
f|_{E_{1}}\colon E_{1}\longrightarrow E_{2}

Therefore,
f(c)\in\ker\lambda
and
f({\ker\lambda})\leq\ker\lambda
. Arguing similarly with the inverse of
f
, the other inclusion holds. Hence
f_{C}(\ker\lambda)=\ker\lambda
.

Let
\Omega=\{N\unlhd C\mid N\leq\ker\lambda\}
. We have that
\Aut(B,+,\cdot)
acts on
\Omega
via
f\bullet N=f(N)
. The action is well defined because
f(N)\leq f(\ker\lambda)=\ker\lambda
.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
(B,+,\cdot)
a brace with additive group
K
, multiplicative group
C
, and lambda map
\lambda\colon C\longrightarrow\Aut(K)
. Let
\Omega=\{N\unlhd C\mid N\leq\ker\lambda\}
. There is a bijection between the isomorphism classes of trifactorised groups associated with
(B,+,\cdot)
and the orbits of the action of
\Aut(B,+,\cdot)
on
\Omega
, which associates to the orbit of
N\in\Omega
the trifactorised group associated with
(B,+,\cdot)
corresponding to the natural epimorphism
\eta_{N}\colon C\longrightarrow C/N
.

Proof.

Let
N\in\Omega
. Since
\ker\eta_{N}=N\leq\ker\lambda
,
\eta_{N}
defines a trifactorised group associated with
(B,+,\cdot)
. If we consider
N
,
M\in\Omega
such that there exists
f\in\Aut(B,+,\cdot)
which
f(N)=M
, by Proposition
4.6
,
\eta_{N}
and
\eta_{M}
define isomorphic trifactorised groups associated with
(B,+,\cdot)
. Hence this defines a map from the set of all orbits of the action of
\Aut(B,+,\cdot)
on
\Omega
to the set of all isomorphism classes of trifactorised groups associated with
(B,+,\cdot)
. This map is injective by Proposition
4.6
.

If
(G,K,H,E)
is a trifactorised group associated with
(B,+,\cdot)
constructed with the epimorphism
\eta\colon C\longrightarrow E
, by Proposition
4.1
, we know that
\eta_{\ker\eta}
gives us a trifactorised group isomorphic to
(G,K,H,E)
associated with
(B,+,\cdot)
, therefore, our map is surjective too and hence it is a bijection.∎

5
Substructures of braces and generalised trifactorised groups

In this section, we characterise substructures of braces in terms of generalised trifactorised groups.
We will identify
each of these substructures with the corresponding subgroup

of the additive group, represented by the subgroup
K
, and the corresponding subgroup
\sigma^{-1}(L)
of the multiplicative group, represented by the subgroup

It is possible to compute the images of subsets of

in

Proposition 5.1
.

Consider the trifactorised group
(G,K,H,E)
and consider

1.

If

, then
\sigma^{-1}(L)=LE\cap H
.

2.

If

\sigma(S)=SE\cap K
.

Proof.

1.

Let

. Then

with
\sigma(h)=k_{h}\in L
and

and

.

2.

Suppose that

. Then there exists

. Hence

.∎

. We have that

Our next result shows a natural embedding of the trifactorised group associated with the subbrace of a brace

in the trifactorised group associated with

.

Lemma 5.3
.

Consider
(G,K,H,E)
a trifactorised group. Let
\sigma\colon H\longrightarrow K
be the corresponding bijective derivation and let
\pi_{E}\colon G\longrightarrow E
be the natural projection. If
L\subseteq K
, then
\pi_{E}(\sigma^{-1}(L))=L^{-1}H\cap E
.

Proof.

Let
h\in\sigma^{-1}(L)
, then
h=k_{h}e_{h}
with
k_{h}\in K
and
e_{h}\in H
. Since
\sigma(h)=k_{h}
, we have that
k_{h}\in H
. Furthermore,
e_{h}=\pi_{E}(h)
. It follows that
e_{h}=k_{h}^{-1}h\in L^{-1}H\cap E
. We conclude that
\sigma^{-1}(L)\subseteq L^{-1}H\cap E
.

Conversely, let
e=l^{-1}h\in L^{-1}H\cap E
. As before, let
h=k_{h}e_{h}
with
k_{h}\in K
and
e_{h}\in E
. Then
e=l^{-1}k_{h}e_{h}
and so
l^{-1}k_{h}=1
, that is,
k_{h}=l
, and
e=e_{h}
. We conclude that
l=k_{h}=\sigma(h)
, in other words,
h\in\sigma^{-1}(L)
, and
\pi_{E}(h)=e
. Consequently,
e\in\pi_{E}(\sigma^{-1}(L))
. It follows that
L^{-1}H\cap E\subseteq\pi_{E}(\sigma^{-1}(L))
and so we have the desired equality.
∎

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
(B,+,\cdot)
be a brace with additive group
K
and multiplicative group
C
. Let
\sigma\colon H\longrightarrow K
be the bijective derivation and consider
(G,K,H,E)
a trifactorised group associated with
(B,+,\cdot)
with the corresponding epimorphism
\eta\colon C\longrightarrow E
. If
L\subseteq K
such that
(L,+,\cdot)
is a subbrace of
(B,+,\cdot)
and
T=L\sigma^{-1}(L)
, then

(T,L,T\cap H,T\cap E)=(LE\cap LH,L,LE\cap H,LH\cap E)

is a trifactorised group associated with
(L,+,\cdot)
. Furthermore, if
\delta\colon C\longrightarrow K
is the identity map and
D=\delta^{-1}(L)
, the trifactorised group is constructed by means of the epimorphism
\eta|_{D}\colon D\longrightarrow LH\cap E
.

Proof.

By Dedekind’s identity,
T=L\sigma^{-1}(L)=L(LE\cap H)=LE\cap LH
. Then
T\cap H=LE\cap H
and
T\cap E=LH\cap E
.

By Proposition
5.2
,
LE\cap LH
is a group and
LE\cap H
is a subgroup of
G
that normalises
L
. Therefore
LE\cap LH=L(LE\cap H)\leq\Norm_{G}(L)
, thus
L
is a normal subgroup of
LE\cap LH
. Using Dedekind’s identity we have that

\displaystyle(LH\cap E)(LE\cap H)

\displaystyle=LE\cap(LH\cap E)H=LE\cap LH\cap EH=LE\cap LH.

The other properties for
(T,L,T\cap H,T\cap E)
to be a trifactorised group follow from an application of Dedekind’s identity.

We have that
\eta|_{D}\colon D\longrightarrow\eta(D)
is an epimorphism that defines a trifactorised group
(\tilde{G},L,\tilde{H},\eta(D))
associated with
(L,+,\cdot)
. Consider the map
\beta\colon C\longrightarrow H
given by
\beta(c)=\delta(c)\eta(c)
for

. Then
\delta=\sigma\circ\beta
and
\eta={\pi_{E}|}_{H}\circ\beta
. Consequently,
\eta(D)=\eta(\delta^{-1}(L))=\pi_{E}(\beta(\beta^{-1}(\sigma^{-1}(L))))=\pi_{E}(\sigma^{-1}(L))=L^{-1}H\cap E=LH\cap E
by Lemma
5.3
and the fact that

. Hence,
\tilde{G}=L\eta(D)=L(LH\cap E)=T
, and
\tilde{H}=\tilde{H}(H\cap\tilde{E})=H\cap\tilde{H}\tilde{E}=H\cap T
.
∎

5.1
Generalised trifactorised subgroups

Definition 5.5
.

Given a trifactorised group
(G,K,H,E)
we define a
trifactorised subgroup
of
(G,K,H,E)
as a trifactorised group
(\tilde{G},\tilde{K},\tilde{H},\tilde{E})
such that
\tilde{G}\leq G
,
\tilde{K}\leq K
,
\tilde{H}\leq H
and
\tilde{E}\leq E
. We write
(\tilde{G},\tilde{K},\tilde{H},\tilde{E})\leq(G,K,H,E)
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
(G,K,H,E)
be a trifactorised group with bijective derivation
\sigma\colon H\longrightarrow K
and let
(\tilde{G},\tilde{K},\tilde{H},\tilde{E})
be a trifactorised subgroup of
(G,K,H,E)
with bijective derivation
\tilde{\sigma}\colon\tilde{H}\longrightarrow\tilde{K}
. Then:

1.

\tilde{K}=\tilde{G}\cap K
,
\tilde{H}=\tilde{G}\cap H
and
\tilde{E}=\tilde{G}\cap E
.

2.

\tilde{\sigma}=\sigma|_{\tilde{H}}
.

Proof.

The proofs of all three equalities are analogous, so let us prove it for
\tilde{K}
.

\tilde{G}\cap K=\tilde{K}\tilde{E}\cap K=\tilde{K}(\tilde{E}\cap K)=\tilde{K}.

Given
h\in\tilde{H}
, it admits a factorisation
h=\tilde{k}_{h}\tilde{e}_{h}
with
\tilde{k}_{h}\in\tilde{K}\leq K
and
\tilde{e}_{h}\in\tilde{E}\leq E
. Since this factorisation is unique in
G
, we have that
\sigma(h)=\tilde{k}_{h}=\tilde{\sigma}(h)
.
∎

Remark 5.7
.

Proposition
5.6
shows that all trifactorised subgroups are of the form
(T,T\cap K,T\cap H,T\cap E)
where
T
is a subgroup of
G
, but not every subgroup of
G
works, for example if
T=K
, we have
(K,K,K\cap H,1)
which in general is not a trifactorised group because
\tilde{H}\tilde{E}=K\cap H
.

Definition 5.8
.

Let
(G,K,H,E)
be a trifactorised group. A subgroup
T
of
G
is a
trifactorised subgroup
if
(T,T\cap K,T\cap H,T\cap E)
is a trifactorised subgroup of
(G,K,H,E)
.

Our next result characterises the trifactorised subgroups of
G
.

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
(G,K,H,E)
be a trifactorised group and let
T
be a subgroup of
G
. The following statements are equivalent:

1.

T
is a trifactorised subgroup of
G
.

2.

T=(T\cap K)E\cap(T\cap K)H
.

Proof.

1
implies
2
. Let
\sigma\colon H\longrightarrow K
the bijective derivation of
(G,K,H,E)
and
\tilde{\sigma}\colon T\cap H\longrightarrow T\cap K
be the bijective derivation of the trifactorised subgroup
T
. By Proposition
5.6

(T\cap K)E\cap H=\sigma^{-1}(T\cap K)=\tilde{\sigma}^{-1}({T\cap K})=T\cap H.

Then, by Dedekind’s identity,

\displaystyle(T\cap K)E\cap(T\cap K)H=(T\cap K)({(T\cap K)E\cap H})=(T\cap K)(T\cap H)=T.

2
implies
1
. The equalities
(T\cap K)(T\cap H)=T
and
(T\cap K)(T\cap E)=T
follow directly from Dedekind’s identity. Furthermore,

\displaystyle(T\cap H)(T\cap E)

\displaystyle=T\cap(T\cap H)E

\displaystyle=T\cap((T\cap K)E\cap(T\cap K)H\cap H)E

\displaystyle=T\cap((T\cap K)E\cap H)E

\displaystyle=T\cap(T\cap K)E\cap HE

\displaystyle=T\cap(T\cap K)E=T.

Therefore
T
is a trifactorised subgroup of
G
.∎

Proposition 5.10
.

Let
(B,+,\cdot)
be a brace with additive group
K
and multiplicative group
C
. Consider
(G,K,H,E)
a trifactorised group associated with
(B,+,\cdot)
. Then the map
L\mapsto LH\cap LE
is a bijection between subbraces of
(B,+,\cdot)
and trifactorised subgroups of
(G,K,H,E)
. Furthermore, the restriction of this map to the set all ideals of
B
defines a bijection between the set of all the ideals of
B
and the set of all normal trifactorised subgroups of
G
.

Proof.

If
L
is a subbrace of
(B,+,\cdot)
, Proposition
5.4
shows that

is a trifactorised subgroup of
(G,K,H,E)
. If
T
is a trifactorised subgroup of

, we have that

corresponds to a subbrace of

because
(T\cap K)H\cap(T\cap K)E=T\leq G
. Hence,

is a map from the the set of all trifactorised subgroups of
(G,K,H,E)
to the set of all subbraces of

. It follows that these two maps are mutually inverses and so they are bijective. The last claim follows from Proposition

(

∎

6
Quotients of generalised trifactorised groups

Our main goal in this section is to show how we can make quotients with the substructure introduced in Section
5.1
, and how these quotients are related to brace quotients.

Note that not every quotient of a trifactorised group is a trifactorised group.

Example 6.1
.

If
(G,K,H,E)
is a trifactorised group with
G\neq K
, then
(G/K,1,G/K,G/K)
is not a trifactorised group because
G/K\cap G/K\neq 1
.

Not all the quotients of a trifactorised group are quotients by a normal trifactorised subgroup.

Example 6.2
.

Let
(G,K,H,E)
be a trifactorised group with
E
nontrivial and normal in
G
. Consider
T=E
. Then

\displaystyle KT\cap ET

\displaystyle=KE\cap EE=G\cap E=E,

\displaystyle HT\cap ET

\displaystyle=HE\cap EE=G\cap E=E,

\displaystyle(T\cap K)H\cap(T\cap K)E

\displaystyle=(E\cap K)H\cap(E\cap K)E=H\cap E=1

The first two equations prove that
(G/T,KT/T,HT/T,ET/T)
is a trifactorised group, but
T
is not a trifactorised subgroup.

The following theorem characterises the normal subgroups of a trifactorised group whose corresponding quotient is again trifactorised.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
(G,K,H,E)
be a trifactorised group and
T
a normal subgroup of
G
, then the following statements are equivalent:

1.

(G/T,KT/T,HT/T,ET/T)
is a trifactorised group.

2.

T=(T\cap K)(T\cap E)=(T\cap H)(T\cap E)
.

3.

T=({(T\cap K)H\cap(T\cap K)E})(T\cap E).

(3)

Proof.

1
implies
2
. By hypothesis
KT\cap ET=HT\cap ET=T
. Now,

\displaystyle(T\cap K)(T\cap E)

\displaystyle=(T\cap K)(KT\cap ET\cap E)=(T\cap K)(KT\cap E)

\displaystyle=KT\cap(T\cap K)E=KT\cap(KT\cap ET\cap K)E

\displaystyle=KT\cap(K\cap ET)E=KT\cap KE\cap ET

\displaystyle=KT\cap ET=T.

Analogously, we have that
(T\cap H)(T\cap E)=T
.

2
implies
3
.

\displaystyle((T\cap K)H\cap(T\cap K)E)(T\cap E)

\displaystyle=(T\cap K)H(T\cap E)\cap(T\cap K)E

\displaystyle=TH\cap(T\cap K)E=(T\cap K)({TH\cap E})

\displaystyle=(T\cap K)({(T\cap E)(T\cap H)H\cap E})

\displaystyle=(T\cap K)({(T\cap E)H\cap E})

\displaystyle=(T\cap K)(T\cap E)(H\cap E)=T.

3
implies
1
.

\displaystyle HT

\displaystyle=H({(T\cap K)H(T\cap E)\cap(T\cap K)E})

\displaystyle=(T\cap K)H(T\cap E)\cap H(T\cap K)E

\displaystyle=(T\cap K)H(T\cap E)\cap G=(T\cap K)H(T\cap E).

\displaystyle ET

\displaystyle=({(T\cap K)H(T\cap E)\cap(T\cap K)E})E

\displaystyle=(T\cap K)H(T\cap E)E\cap(T\cap K)E

\displaystyle=G\cap(T\cap K)E=(T\cap K)E.

Then
HT\cap ET=(T\cap K)H(T\cap E)\cap(T\cap K)E=T
. Furthermore
T=T\cap ET=T\cap(T\cap K)E=(T\cap K)(T\cap E)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle KT\cap ET

\displaystyle=K(T\cap K)(T\cap E)\cap E(T\cap K)(T\cap E)

\displaystyle=K(T\cap E)\cap E(T\cap K)=(T\cap K)({K(T\cap E)\cap E})

\displaystyle=(T\cap K)(T\cap E)(K\cap E)=T.

This proves that
KT/T\cap ET/T=T
and
HT/T\cap ET/T=T
. Therefore
(G/T,KT/T,HT/T,ET/T)
is a trifactorised group.
∎

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
(G,K,H,E)
be a trifactorised group, let
\sigma\colon H\longrightarrow K
be its associated bijective derivation, and let
T
be a normal trifactorised subgroup of
(G,K,H,E)
. Then

(G/T,KT/T,HT/T,ET/T)

\overline{\sigma}\colon HT/T\longrightarrow KT/T

Proof.

By definition the equation (

Given

Remark 6.5
.

Given a trifactorised group
(G,K,H,E)
and a normal subgroup

(G/T,KT/T,HT/T,ET/T)
is a trifactorised group if and only if
T
is the kernel of some trifactorised group morphism. Therefore, given a trifactorised group morphism

\ker f=({(\ker f\cap K_{1})H_{1}\cap({\ker f\cap K_{1}})E_{1}})({\ker f\cap E_{1}}).

Proposition 6.6

Note that the additive group of

\displaystyle\delta(c_{1})T\delta(c_{2})T

\displaystyle=\delta(c_{1})\delta(c_{2})T

\displaystyle\delta(c_{1})T\boxdot\delta(c_{2})T

\displaystyle=\overline{\sigma}({\overline{\sigma}^{-1}({\delta(c_{1})T})\overline{\sigma}^{-1}({\delta(c_{2})T})})

\displaystyle=\overline{\sigma}({({\sigma^{-1}({\delta(c_{1})})T})({\sigma^{-1}({\delta(c_{2})T})})})

\displaystyle=\sigma({\sigma^{-1}({\delta(c_{1})})\sigma^{-1}({\delta(c_{2})})})T

\displaystyle=\sigma({\delta(c_{1})\delta(c_{2})^{\eta(c_{1})}\eta(c_{1}c_{2})})T=\delta(c_{1}c_{2})T

These are the operations of

(G/T,KT/T,HT/T,ET/T)

The identity map

Proposition 6.9
.

Let
(B,+,\cdot)
be a brace with additive group
K
and multiplicative group
C
. Consider
(G,K,H,E)
a trifactorised group associated with
B
with corresponding epimorphism
\eta\colon C\longrightarrow E
, and
T
a normal subgroup of
G
contained in
E
. Then
(G/T,KT/T,HT/T,E/T)
is a trifactorised group associated with
B
with its corresponding epimorphism
\bar{\eta}=\pi_{T}\circ\eta
where
\pi_{T}\colon E\longrightarrow E/T
is the natural epimorphism.

Proof.

Proposition
6.3
shows that
(G/T,KT/T,HT/T,E/T)
is a trifactorised group. Analogously to
6.6
the operations of the associated brace are
\delta(c_{1})T\delta(c_{2})T=\delta(c_{1})\delta(c_{2})T
and
\delta(c_{1})T\boxdot\delta(c_{2})T=\delta(c_{1}c_{2})T
. But in this case we have that
KT/T\cong K
, therefore, we have that the associated brace is
(B,+,\cdot)
.

The elements of our trifactorised group are of the form
\delta(c_{1})\eta(c_{2})T
. Since its bijective derivation is
\overline{\delta}\colon C\longrightarrow KT/T
given by
\overline{\delta}(c)=\delta(c)T
, we have that the epimorphism that corresponds to the trifactorised group is
\bar{\eta}\colon C\longrightarrow E/T
given by
\bar{\eta}(c)=\eta(c)T=({\pi_{T}\circ\eta})(c)
.
∎

Lemma 6.10
.

Let
f\colon(G_{1},K_{1},H_{1},E_{1})\longrightarrow(G_{2},K_{2},H_{2},E_{2})
be a trifactorised group epimorphism such that
f|_{K_{1}}
is an isomorphism. Then
\ker f\leq E
.

Proof.

Let
ke\in\ker f
with
k\in K_{1}
and
e\in E_{1}
. Then
1=f(ke)=f(k)f(e)
. Since
f(k)\in K_{2}
and
f(e)\in E_{2}
we have that
f(k)=1
and
f(e)=1
. Hence
f|_{K_{1}}
is an isomorphism, and so,
k=1
and
\ker f\leq E
.
∎

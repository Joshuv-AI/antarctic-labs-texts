Abstract
. We prove global well-posedness and scattering for solutions to the mass-critical inhomogeneous nonlinear Schrödinger equation i ​ ∂ t u + Δ ​ u = ± | x | − b ​ | u | 4 − 2 ​ b d ​ u i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=\pm|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}u for large L 2 ​ ( ℝ d ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) initial data with d ≥ 3 , 0 < b < min ⁡ { 2 , d 2 } d\geq 3,0<b<\min\left\{2,\frac{d}{2}\right\} ; in the focusing case, we require that the mass is strictly less than that of the ground state. Compared with the classical Schrödinger case ( b = 0 b=0 , Dodson, J. Amer. Math. Soc. (2012), Adv. Math. (2015)), the main differences for the inhomogeneous case ( b > 0 b>0 ) are that the presence of the inhomogeneity | x | − b |x|^{-b} creates a nontrivial singularity at the origin, and breaks the translation symmetry as well as the Galilean invariance of the equation, which makes the establishment of the profile decomposition and long time Strichartz estimates more difficult. To overcome these difficulties, we perform the concentration compactness/rigidity methods of [Kenig and Merle, Invent. Math. The exclusion of these solutions will utilize fractional estimates and long time Strichartz estimates in Lorentz spaces. In our study, we obseve that the decay of the inhomogeneity | x | − b |x|^{-b} at infinity prevents the concentration of the almost periodic solution at infinity in either physical or frequency space. Therefore, we can use classical Morawetz estimates, rather than interaction Morawetz estimates, to exclude the existence of the quasi-soliton. Moreover, our method can also be applied to the case b = 0 b=0 , thereby recovering the scattering results in [Tao, Visan and Zhang, Duke Math. J. (2007), Killip, Visan and Zhang, Anal. PDE (2008)]. 1.
Introduction

We consider the following mass-critical inhomogeneous nonlinear Schrödinger equation

(1.1)

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=\mu|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}u,\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\end{cases}

where
d\geq 3,0<b<\min\left\{2,\frac{d}{2}\right\}
. Here
\mu=\pm 1
, with
\mu=+1
known as the
defocusing
case and
\mu=-1
as the
focusing
case.
This model arises in the setting of nonlinear optics, where the factor
|x|^{-b}
represents some inhomogeneity in the medium (see, e.g.,
[
20
,
27
]
). As pointed out by Genoud and Stuart
[
19
]
, the factor
|x|^{-b}
appears naturally as a limiting case of potentials that decay polynomially at infinity.

In this paper, we study the global well-posedness and scattering of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
). Before stating our results, we give the definition of the solutions and the corresponding notion of blowup.

Definition 1.1

(Strong solution)
.

Let
F(u)=\pm|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}u
and

(1.2)

\gamma=:\frac{2d+8-4b}{d},\qquad\rho=:\frac{2d+8-4b}{d+2-2b}.

A function
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
is a solution to (
1.1
) if for any compact
J\subset I
,
u\in C_{t}^{0}L^{2}_{x}(J\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\gamma}_{t}L_{x}^{\rho,2}(J\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and we have the Duhamel formula for all
t,t_{0}\in I
:

u(t)=e^{i(t-t_{0})\Delta}u(t_{0})-i\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)\Delta}F(u(\tau))d\tau,

where
L_{x}^{\rho,2}
is the Lorentz space defined in Subsection
2.2
below. We refer to the interval
I
as the
lifespan
of
u
. We say that
u
is a
maximal-lifespan solution
if the solution cannot be extended to any strictly larger interval. We say that
u
is a
global solution
if
I=\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 1.2

(Blow up)
.

We say that a solution
u
to (
1.1
) blows up forward in time if there exists a time
t_{0}\in I
such that

\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\gamma}L_{x}^{\rho,2}([t_{0},\sup(I))\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\infty,

and that
u
blows up backward in time if there exists a time
t_{0}\in I
such that

\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\gamma}L_{x}^{\rho,2}((\inf(I),t_{0}]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\infty.

On the interval of existence, the solution preserves its mass (see Proposition
2.2
)

(1.3)

M(u)=:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(t,x)|^{2}\,dx=M(u_{0}).

Equation (
1.1
) is referred to as mass-critical as the natural scaling of the equation
u(t,x)\mapsto\lambda^{\frac{d}{2}}u(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x)
also keeps the mass invariant.

By considering Strichartz estimates in Lorentz spaces, Aloui and Tayachi
[
1
]
proved the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
local well-posedness for the mass-critical inhomogeneous NLS (
1.1
) (see Proposition
2.2
). In this paper, we consider the questions of global well-posedness and scattering of the solutions. Here scattering refers to the fact that there exists unique
u_{\pm}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

(1.4)

\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\|u(t)-e^{it\Delta}u_{\pm}\|_{L^{2}}=0.

Such results have been extensivlely studied for the classical mass-critical Schrödinger equation (
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
23
,
26
,
33
]
), and energy-critical Schrödinger equation (
[
4
,

,
14
,

,

35
]
).

To be more precise, let us first recall the global well-posedness and scattering results for the classical mass-critical Schrödinger equation (
b=0
):

(1.5)

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=\mu|u|^{\frac{4}{d}}u,\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).\end{cases}

It is well known that all small mass solutions are global and scatte; while in the focusing case this assertion can fail for solutions with large mass. In fact, let
\widetilde{Q}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
be the unique positive radial solution to the elliptic equation

\Delta\widetilde{Q}+\widetilde{Q}^{1+\frac{4}{d}}=\widetilde{Q},

then
u(t,x)=:e^{it}\widetilde{Q}(x)
, with mass
M(\widetilde{Q})
, is a nonscattering solution to (
1.5
). However, it is widely believed that
M(\widetilde{Q})
is the minimal mass obstruction to global well-posedness and scattering in the focusing case, and that no such obstruction exists in the defocusing case:

Conjecture 1.1

(Global existence and scattering)
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
\mu\in\{-1,+1\}
and
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In the focusing case, we further assume that
M(u_{0})<M(\widetilde{Q})
. Then all maximal-lifespan solutions to (
1.5
) are globally well-posed and scatter.

Conjecture
1.1
has now been completely resolved.
Killip, Tao and Visan
[
23
]
first solved Conjecture
1.1
for radial initial data in dimension
d=2
.
They introduced the concept of almost periodic solutions, and under the assumption that Conjecture
1.1
is not valid, they employed the concentration compactness/rigidity method of
[
22
]
to construct an almost periodic solution. Then they categorized the almost periodic solutions into three cases: soliton-like solution, double high-to-low frequency cascade, self-similar solution. Finally, they completed the proof by excluding these three types of solutions.
Later, Tao, Visan and Zhang
[
33
]
used frequency localized Morawetz estimates to exclude the existence of almost periodic solutions, thus solving Conjecture
1.1
for
\mu=1,d\geq 3
, and radial initial data.
By applying fractional chain rules to overcome the obstacle of the fractional nonlinearity and establishing a Gronwall-type inequality to deal with the frequency relations between the solution and the nonlinearity, Killip, Visan and Zhang
[
26
]
solved Conjecture
1.1
for
\mu=-1,d\geq 3
.

Recently, Dodson established long-time Strichartz estimates, combining with interaction Morawetz estimates, and through detailed analysis on the frequency scale function, completedy solved Conjecture
1.1
for
d\geq 1
, and general initial data. See
[

]
for the defocusing case, and
[

According to the local well-posedness theory (see Proposition
2.2
), all maximal-lifespan solutions to (
1.1
) with sufficiently small mass are global and scatter. However, in the focusing case where
\mu=-1
, it is well known that this assertion can fail for solutions with large mass. Let
Q
be the unique positive solution to

(1.6)

\Delta Q+|x|^{-b}Q^{\frac{4-2b}{d}+1}=Q.

The existence of a positive solution to (
1.6
) was proved in
[
15
,
19
]
for dimension
d\geq 2
, and for
d=1
in
[
16
]
. The issue of uniqueness was addressed for dimension
d\geq 3
by Yanagida
[
37
]
(see also
[
15
]
), for dimension
d=2
by
[
17
]
, and for dimension
d=1
by
[
34
]
.
The unique positive solution
Q
is called the
ground state
, and provides a stationary solution

u(t,x)=e^{it}Q(x)

to (
1.1
) which blows up at infinity both forward and backward in time.

Similar to the case of the mass-critical Schrödinger equation (
1.5
), it is conjectured that
M(Q)
is the minimal mass obstruction to global well-posedness and scattering in the focusing case, and that no such obstruction exists in the defocusing case. In fact, there is compelling evidence supporting this conjecture when the initial data
u_{0}
possesses additional regularity. For the defocusing equation, the global well-posedness for initial data in
H^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
can be established by the contraction argument and the conservation of mass and energy, see e.g.
[
5
]
. Recall that the energy is given by

(1.7)

E(u)=:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}+\frac{d\mu}{2(d+2-b)}|x|^{-b}|u(t,x)|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}+2}\right)\,dx.

The focusing equation with data in
H^{1}_{x}
was treated by Genoud
[
18
]
.
By variational methods, Genoud
[
18
]
established the sharp Gagliardo-Nirenberg’s inequality

(1.8)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{-b}|f(x)|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}+2}dx\leq\frac{d+2-b}{d}\left(\frac{\|f\|_{L^{2}}}{\|Q\|_{L^{2}}}\right)^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla f(x)|^{2}dx.

When
u_{0}\in H^{1}
and
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}<\|Q\|_{L^{2}}
, this inequality establishes an upper bound of the

H^{1}
norm of the solution at all times during its existence. Then Genoud
[
18
]
proved global well-posedness of the focusing equation for initial data in
H^{1}
with mass less than that of the ground state. As
e^{it}Q
is a blow-up solution with mass
\|Q\|_{L^{2}}
, we can reasonably conjecture that
\|Q\|_{L^{2}}
is the scattering threshold for the focusing equation.

In this paper, we prove that this conjecture holds for
d\geq 3
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d\geq 3
,
0<b<\min\left\{2,\frac{d}{2}\right\}
and
\mu\in\left\{-1,+1\right\}
. In the focusing case, we further assume that
M(u_{0})<M(Q)
.
Then all maximal-lifespan solutions to (
1.1
) are global and scatter in the sense (
1.4
).

In the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we perform the concentration compactness/rigidity arguments in
[
22
]
, and apply the the long time Strichartz estimates and frequency localized Morawetz estimates techniques of
[
10
,
11
]
) within the framework of Lorentz spaces.
Before we state our argument, we need some definitions.

Definition 1.3
.

(Symmetry group)
. For any phase
\theta\in\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
and scaling parameter
\lambda>0
, we define the unitary transformation
g_{\theta,\lambda}:L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by the formula:

[g_{\theta,\lambda}f](x):=\frac{1}{\lambda^{d/2}}e^{i\theta}f\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right).

We let
G
be the collection of such transformations.

Definition 1.4
.

(Almost periodicity modulo symmetries)
. A solution
u
with lifespan
I
is said to be
almost periodic modulo
G
if there exist functions
N:I\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
and a function
C:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that

(1.9)

\int_{|x|\geq C(\eta)/N(t)}|u(t,x)|^{2}dx\leq\eta,

and

(1.10)

\int_{|\xi|\geq C(\eta)N(t)}|\hat{u}(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq\eta,

for all
t\in I
and
\eta>0
. We refer to the function
N(t)
as the
frequency scale function
for the solution
u
, and
C(\eta)
as the
compactness modulus function
.

Remark 1.1
.

Compared with the definition of almost periodic solutions of the classical Schrödinger equation (see e.g. [Definition 1.14,
[
23
]
]), there is no spatial center function
x(t)
and frequency center function
\xi(t)
in (
1.9
) and (
1.10
).
This comes from the fact that the inhomogeneity
|x|^{-b}
decays at infinity, which prevents the concentration of the almost periodic solution at infinity in either physical or frequency space (see Proposition
2.4
below). Therefore, the spatial and frequency translation functions must be bounded, and by a translation we can let
x(t)\equiv\xi(t)\equiv 0
. For the details, we refer to Section
3
.

Assuming for contradiction that Theorem
1.1
is false, we can identify an almost periodic solution. This solution must take one of the following three scenario:

Theorem 1.2

(Three special scenarios for blowup)
.

Suppose Theorem
1.1
fails, then there exists a maximal-lifespan solution to (
1.1
), which is also almost periodic modulo
G
. Moreover, it blows up both forward and backward in time, and in the focusing case also obeys
M(u)<M(Q)
.

Furthermore, we can also ensure that the lifespan
I
and the frequency scale function
N:I\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
match one of the following three scenarios:

(1)

(Soliton-like solution)
We have
I=\mathbb{R}
and

N(t)=1\quad\text{for all }t\in\mathbb{R}.

(2)

(Double high-to-low frequency cascade)
We have
I=\mathbb{R}
,

\liminf_{t\to-\infty}N(t)=\liminf_{t\to+\infty}N(t)=0,

and

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}N(t)\leq 1\quad\text{for all }t\in I.

(3)

(Self-similar solution)
We have
I=(0,+\infty)
and

N(t)=t^{-1/2}\quad\text{for all }t\in I.

Theorem
1.2
will be proved in Section
3
. Therefore, to prove Theorem
1.1
, it is sufficient to demonstrate that an almost periodic solution to (
1.1
), which satisfies the properties outlined in Theorem
1.2
, cannot exist. By a time translation and possibly modifying the
C(\eta)
in (
1.9
)–(
1.10
), we can assume that
N(0)=1
and
N(t)\leq 1
for all
t>0
.
Following the arguments in
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
, it is sufficient to rule out
the rapid frequency cascade

(1.11)

\int_{0}^{\infty}N(t)^{3}dt<\infty,

and the quasi-soliton

(1.12)

\int_{0}^{\infty}N(t)^{3}dt=\infty.

The main ingredient used to exclude scenariors (
1.11
) and (
1.12
) is the following long time Strichartz estimate, which is proved by combining an induction on frequency estimate with the bilinear Strichartz estimate.

Proposition 1.1
.

Suppose
J\subset[0,\infty)
is compact,
d\geq 3
,
0<b<\min\left\{2,\frac{d}{2}\right\}
,
u
is a minimal mass blow up solutions to (
1.1
) in the form of Theorem
1.2
,
\int_{J}N(t)^{3}dt=K
.
Then

(1.13)

\|P_{>N}u\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d-2},2}(J\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\big(\tfrac{K}{N}\big)^{1/2}+\sigma_{J}\big(\tfrac{N}{2}\big),

where for
N=2^{k},k\in\mathbb{Z}
, the frequency envelopes

(1.14)

\sigma_{J}(N)=:\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}2^{-|j-k|/3}\inf_{t\in J}\left\|P_{>2^{j}}u(t)\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

More generally, suppose
\frac{1}{2}\leq s<\min\left\{\frac{d-2b}{2}+1,\frac{4-2b}{d}+1\right\}
, then

(1.15)

\|P_{>N}u\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d-2},2}(J\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim_{s}\frac{K^{\frac{1}{2}}}{N^{s}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}\dot{H}_{x}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}(J\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\sigma_{J}\big(\tfrac{N}{2}\big).

The above long time Strichartz estimate will be used to prove that if
u(t,x)
is an almost periodic solution to (
1.1
) and
\int_{0}^{\infty}N(t)^{3}dt<\infty
, then
u(t,x)
possesses additional regularity. Combining this regularity with the conservation of the energy, we can preclude the possibility of the rapid frequency cascade (
1.11
). For the details, we refer to Section
5
.

To preclude the quasi-soliton (
1.12
), we utilize a frequency truncated Morawetz estimate as in
[
11
]
. These estimates scale like
\int_{0}^{T}N(t)^{3}dt
and are actually bounded below by a constant times
\int_{0}^{T}N(t)^{3}dt
. On the other hand, by the compactness (
1.10
), these estimates can also be bounded above by a small constant times
\int_{0}^{T}N(t)^{3}dt
. Therefore, we obtain a contradiction by letting
T
sufficiently large. In this process, we need to truncate to the low frequencies since the Morawetz action scales like
\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1/2}_{x}}^{2}
. Then the long time Strichartz estimate (
1.13
) allows us to control the errors that arise from frequency truncation. Therefore, the combinition of the frequency truncated Morawetz estimate and the long time Strichartz estimate allows us to exclude the existence of quasi-soliton (
1.12
), thereby completing the proof of Theorem
1.1
. For the details, we refer to Section
6
.

Compared the inhomogeneous Schrödinger equation (
1.1
) with the classical mass-critical Schrödinger equation (
1.5
), the main differences for the inhomogeneous case (
b>0
) are that the presence of the inhomogeneity
|x|^{-b}
creates a nontrivial singularity at the origin, and breaks the translation symmetry as well as the Galilean invariance of the equation, which makes the establishment of the profile decomposition and long time Strichartz estimates more difficult. To address these difficulties, we adopt the approach in
[
1
]
and perform the arguments of
[
10
,
11
,
22
]
) within the framework of Lorentz spaces.
On the other hand, the presence of
|x|^{-b}
also brings some advantages. In fact, as noted in Remark
1.1
, the decay of
|x|^{-b}
at infinity prevents the concentration of the almost periodic solution at infinity in either physical or frequency space. Therefore, the spatial and frequency translation functions must be bounded, and by a translation we can let
x(t)\equiv\xi(t)\equiv 0
.
This effectively places us in the same scenario as in the radial case but without the need for a radial assumption.
As a benefit of this, we can use classical Morawetz estimates, rather than interaction Morawetz estimates, to exclude the existence of the quasi-soliton.

Finally, we would like to point out that, with only trivial modifications, the proof presented in our paper can also be applied to the case
b=0
, thereby recovering the scattering results in
[
33
,
26
]
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows:
In Section
2

In Section

1.1

In Section

In Section

1.1
.
Finally, in the Appendix, we provide the construction of
N_{m}(t)
and present a detailed proof of the frequency error estimates in Section
6
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Some notations

We use the standard notation for mixed Lebesgue space-time norms and Sobolev spaces.
We write
A\lesssim B
to denote
A\leq CB
for some
C>0
. If
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
, then we write
A\approx B
. We write
A\ll B
to denote
A\leq cB
for some small
c>0
. If
C
depends upon some additional parameters, we will indicate this with subscripts; for example,
X\lesssim_{u}Y
denotes that
X\leq C_{u}Y
for some
C_{u}
depending on
u
. We use
O(Y)
to denote any quantity
X
such that
|X|\lesssim Y
. We use
\langle x\rangle
to denote
(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
. We write
L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}
to denote the Banach space with norm

\|u\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(t,x)|^{r}\,dx\right)^{q/r}\,dt\right)^{1/q},

with the usual modifications when
q
or
r
are equal to infinity, or when the domain
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
is replaced by spacetime slab such as
I\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. When
q=r
we abbreviate
L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{q}
as
L_{t,x}^{q}
.

Let
\phi(\xi)
be a radial bump function supported in the ball
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|\xi|\leq 2\}
and equal to
1
on the ball
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|\xi|\leq 1\}
. For each number
N>0
, we define the Fourier multipliers

\widehat{P_{\leq N}f}(\xi)=:\phi(\xi/N)\widehat{f}(\xi),\quad\widehat{P_{>N}f}(\xi)=:(1-\phi(\xi/N))\widehat{f}(\xi),

\widehat{P_{N}f}(\xi)=:\psi(\xi/N)\widehat{f}(\xi)=:(\phi(\xi/N)-\phi(2\xi/N))\widehat{f}(\xi).

We similarly define
P_{<N}
and
P_{\geq N}
. For convenience of notation, let
u_{N}=:P_{N}u
,
u_{\leq N}=:P_{\leq N}u
, and
u_{>N}=:P_{>N}u
.
We will normally use these multipliers when
M
and
N
are dyadic numbers (that is, of the form
2^{n}
for some integer
n
); in particular, all summations over
N
or
M
are understood to be over dyadic numbers.

2.2.
Lorentz spaces and nonlinear estimates

Let
f
be a measurable function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The distribution function of
f
is defined by

d_{f}(\lambda)=:|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|f(x)|>\lambda\}|,\quad\lambda>0,

where
|A|
is the Lebesgue measure of a set
A
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The decreasing rearrangement of
f
is defined by

f^{*}(s)=:\inf\left\{\lambda>0:d_{f}(\lambda)\leq s\right\},\quad s>0.

Definition 2.1

(Lorentz spaces)
.

Let
0<r<\infty
and
0<\rho\leq\infty
. The Lorentz space
L^{r,\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined by

L^{r,\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=:\left\{f\text{ is measurable on }\mathbb{R}^{d}:\|f\|_{L^{r,\rho}}<\infty\right\},

where

\|f\|_{L^{r,\rho}}=:\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}(\frac{\rho}{r}\int_{0}^{\infty}(s^{1/r}f^{*}(s))^{\rho}\frac{1}{s}ds)^{1/\rho}&\text{ if }\rho<\infty,\\
\sup_{s>0}s^{1/r}f^{*}(s)&\text{ if }\rho=\infty.\end{array}\right.

We collect the following basic properties of
L^{r,\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in the following lemmas.

Lemma 2.1

(Properties of Lorentz spaces
[
29
]
)
.

•

For
1<r<\infty
,
L^{r,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\equiv L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and by convention,
L^{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

•

For
1<r<\infty
and
0<\rho_{1}<\rho_{2}\leq\infty
,
L^{r,\rho_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset L^{r,\rho_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

•

For
1<r<\infty
,
0<\rho\leq\infty
, and
\theta>0
,
\||f|^{\theta}\|_{L^{r,\rho}}=\|f\|^{\theta}_{L^{\theta r,\theta\rho}}
.

•

For
b>0
,
|x|^{-b}\in L^{\frac{d}{b},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\||x|^{-b}\|_{L^{\frac{d}{b},\infty}}=|B(0,1)|^{\frac{b}{d}}
, where
B(0,1)
is the unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Lemma 2.2

(Hölder’s inequality
[
29
]
)
.

•

Let
1<r,r_{1},r_{2}<\infty
and
1\leq\rho,\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\leq\infty
be such that

\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\frac{1}{r_{2}},\quad\frac{1}{\rho}\leq\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{2}}.

Then for any
f\in L^{r_{1},\rho_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
g\in L^{r_{2},\rho_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\|fg\|_{L^{r,\rho}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{r_{1},\rho_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{r_{2},\rho_{2}}}.

•

Let
1<r_{1},r_{2}<\infty
and
1\leq\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\leq\infty
be such that

1=\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\frac{1}{r_{2}},\quad 1\leq\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{2}}.

Then for any
f\in L^{r_{1},\rho_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
g\in L^{r_{2},\rho_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\|fg\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{r_{1},\rho_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{r_{2},\rho_{2}}}.

Lemma 2.3

(Interpolation in Lorentz spaces
[
8
]
)
.

Let
1<p_{0}<p<p_{1}<\infty
,
r>0
and
0<\theta<1
satisfy
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}
. For every
f\in L^{p_{0},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{p_{1},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have

\|f\|_{L^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p_{0},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{1-\theta}\|f\|_{L^{p_{1},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\theta}.

Lemma 2.4

(Gagliardo-Nirenberg’s inequality in Lorentz space)
.

Let
1<q,r\leq\infty
,
1\leq q_{1},r_{1}\leq\infty
and
0<\sigma<s<\infty
be such that

\frac{1}{p}=\left(1-\frac{\sigma}{s}\right)\frac{1}{q}+\frac{\sigma}{sr},\quad\frac{1}{p_{1}}=\left(1-\frac{\sigma}{s}\right)\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{\sigma}{sr_{1}}.

Then

\||\nabla|^{\sigma}f\|_{L^{p,p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{q,q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{1-\frac{\sigma}{s}}\||\nabla|^{s}f\|_{L^{r,r_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{\sigma}{s}}.

Proof.

See
[
36
, Proposition 3.2]
. ∎

Lemma 2.5

(Convolution inequality
[
29
]
)
.

Let
1<r,r_{1},r_{2}<\infty
and
1\leq\rho,\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\leq\infty
be such that

1+\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\frac{1}{r_{2}},\quad\frac{1}{\rho}\leq\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{2}}.

Then for any
f\in L^{r_{1},\rho_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
g\in L^{r_{2},\rho_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\|f\ast g\|_{L^{r,\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{r_{1},\rho_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|g\|_{L^{r_{2},\rho_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Using the above convolution inequality, one can easily obtain Bernstein’s inequality in Lorentz spaces.

Lemma 2.6

(Bernstein’s inequality)
.

Let
N>0
,
1<r_{1}<r_{2}<\infty
and
1\leq\rho_{1}\leq\rho_{2}\leq\infty
. Then

\|P_{N}f\|_{L^{r_{2},\rho_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim N^{d(\frac{1}{r_{1}}-\frac{1}{r_{2}})}\|f\|_{L^{r_{1},\rho_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Next, in Lemma
2.7
–Lemma
2.9
, we recall the Sobolev embedding, product rule, and chain rule in Lorentz spaces. We start by introducing the following definition.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
s\geq 0,1<r<\infty
and
1\leq\rho\leq\infty
. We define the Sobolev-Lorentz spaces

W^{s}L^{r,\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=:\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):(1-\Delta)^{s/2}f\in L^{r,\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\},

\dot{W}^{s}L^{r,\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=:\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):(-\Delta)^{s/2}f\in L^{r,\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\},

where
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the space of tempered distributions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

(1-\Delta)^{s/2}f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left((1+|\xi|^{2})^{s/2}\mathcal{F}(f)\right),\qquad(-\Delta)^{s/2}f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(|\xi|^{s}\mathcal{F}(f))

with
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
the Fourier and its inverse Fourier transforms respectively. The spaces
W^{s}L^{r,\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\dot{W}^{s}L^{r,\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
are endowed respectively with the norms

\|f\|_{W^{s}L^{r,\rho}}=\|f\|_{L^{r,\rho}}+\|(-\Delta)^{s/2}f\|_{L^{r,\rho}},\qquad\|f\|_{\dot{W}L^{r,\rho}}=\|(-\Delta)^{s/2}f\|_{L^{r,\rho}}.

For simplicity, when
s=1
we write
WL^{r,\rho}:=W^{1}L^{r,\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\dot{W}L^{r,\rho}:=\dot{W}^{1}L^{r,\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Lemma 2.7

(Sobolev embedding
[
9
]
)
.

Let
1<r<\infty,1\leq\rho\leq\infty
and
0<s<\frac{d}{r}
. Then

\|f\|_{L^{\frac{dr}{d-sr},\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|(-\Delta)^{s/2}f\|_{L^{r,\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\quad\text{for any}\quad f\in\dot{W}^{s}L^{r,\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Lemma 2.8

(Product rule
[
7
]
)
.

Let
s\geq 0,1<r,r_{1},r_{2},r_{3},r_{4},<\infty
, and
1\leq\rho,\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\rho_{3},\rho_{4}\leq\infty
be such that

\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\frac{1}{r_{2}}=\frac{1}{r_{3}}+\frac{1}{r_{4}},\qquad\frac{1}{\rho}=\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{2}}=\frac{1}{\rho_{3}}+\frac{1}{\rho_{4}}.

Then for any
f\in\dot{W}^{s}L^{r_{1},\rho_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{r_{3},\rho_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
g\in\dot{W}^{s}L^{r_{4},\rho_{4}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{r_{2},\rho_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\|(-\Delta)^{s/2}(fg)\|_{L^{r,\rho}}\lesssim\|(-\Delta)^{s/2}f\|_{L^{r_{1},\rho_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{r_{2},\rho_{2}}}+\|f\|_{L^{r_{3},\rho_{3}}}\|(-\Delta)^{s/2}g\|_{L^{r_{4},\rho_{4}}}.

Lemma 2.9

(Chain rule
[
1
]
)
.

Let
s\in[0,1],F\in C^{1}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C})
and
1<p,p_{1},p_{2}<\infty
,
1\leq q,q_{1},q_{2}<\infty
be such that

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}},\qquad\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}.

Then

\|(-\Delta)^{s/2}F(f)\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|F^{\prime}(f)\|_{L^{p_{1},q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|(-\Delta)^{s/2}f\|_{L^{p_{2},q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Next, we establish the Coifman-Meyer lemma in Lorentz spaces, which will be used to prove Lemma
A.2
in the appendix.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
m(\xi,\eta)
be a Coifman-Meyer multiplier on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, i.e.
m(\xi,\eta)
satisfies

|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\partial_{\eta}^{\beta}m(\xi,\eta)|\lesssim(1+|\xi|+|\eta|)^{-|\alpha|-|\beta|}\quad\text{for any}\quad\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}.

Let

T_{m}(f,g)(x)=:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{ix\cdot(\xi+\eta)}m(\xi,\eta)\hat{f}(\xi)\hat{g}(\eta)d\xi d\eta.

Then for any
1<p,p_{0},p_{1}<\infty
with
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{0}}+\frac{1}{p_{1}}
,

(2.1)

\|T_{m}(f,g)\|_{L^{p,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p_{0},2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|g\|_{L^{p_{1},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Proof.

We first claim that for any
1<r,r_{0},r_{1}<\infty
with
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{r_{0}}+\frac{1}{r_{1}}
, the following estimate holds:

(2.2)

\|T_{m}(f,g)\|_{L^{r,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{r_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|g\|_{L^{r_{1},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

In fact, fixing
\varepsilon_{0}>0
sufficiently small and letting

\frac{1}{r^{\pm}}=:\frac{1}{r}\pm\varepsilon_{0},\qquad\frac{1}{r_{1}^{\pm}}=:\frac{1}{r_{1}}\pm\varepsilon_{0}\quad\text{ such that }\quad\frac{1}{r^{\pm}}=\frac{1}{r_{0}}+\frac{1}{r_{1}^{\pm}},

we deduce from Coifman-Meyer multiplier theorem that

\|T_{m}(f,g)\|_{L^{r^{\pm}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{r_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|g\|_{L^{r_{1}^{\pm}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Hence by Hunt’s interpolation theorem
[
30
, Theorem IX.19]
,

T_{m}(f,\cdot):\ (L^{r_{1}^{+}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),L^{r_{1}^{-}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))_{\frac{1}{2},\infty}\rightarrow(L^{r^{+}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),L^{r^{-}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))_{\frac{1}{2},\infty}

is bounded with bound
\|f\|_{L^{r_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
.
Further utilizing the real interpolation of Lebesgue spaces (
[
3
, Theorem 5.2.1]
), we obtain (
2.2
).

We now prove (
2.1
). Fix
\varepsilon_{0}>0
sufficiently small and let

\frac{1}{p^{\pm}}=:\frac{1}{p}\pm\varepsilon_{0},\qquad\frac{1}{p_{0}^{\pm}}=:\frac{1}{p_{0}}\pm\varepsilon_{0}\quad\text{ such that }\quad\frac{1}{p^{\pm}}=\frac{1}{p_{0}^{\pm}}+\frac{1}{p_{1}}.

By Claim (
2.2
),

\|T_{m}(f,g)\|_{L^{p^{\pm},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p^{\pm}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|g\|_{L^{p_{1},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Again by Hunt’s interpolation theorem
[
30
, Theorem IX.19]
,

T_{m}(\cdot,g):\ \ (L^{p_{0}^{+}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),L^{p_{0}^{-}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))_{\frac{1}{2},2}\rightarrow(L^{p^{+},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),L^{p^{-},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))_{\frac{1}{2},2}

is bounded with bound
\|g\|_{L^{p_{1},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
.
By real interpolation of Lorentz spaces (
[
3
, Theorem 5.3.1]
), we obtain (
2.1
).

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
d\geq 3
,
0<b<\min\left\{2,\frac{d}{2}\right\}
and

. Then on any spacetime slab

\||\nabla|^{s}(|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}u)\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2},2}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L_{x}^{2}}^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}\||\nabla|^{s}u\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d-2},2}}+\||\nabla|^{\frac{2s}{\gamma}}u\|^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}_{L_{t}^{\gamma}L_{x}^{\rho,2}},

and

\||\nabla|^{s}(|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}u)\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2},2}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{\gamma}_{t}L_{x}^{\rho,2}}^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}\||\nabla|^{s}u\|_{L^{\gamma}_{t}L_{x}^{\rho,2}},

where
(\gamma,\rho)
was defined by (
1.2
).

Proof.

By Lemma
2.8
,

\displaystyle\||\nabla|^{s}(|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}u)\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2},2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||x|^{-b}\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{d}{b},\infty}}\||\nabla|^{s}(|u|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}u)\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2-2b},2}}

\displaystyle+\||x|^{-(b+s)}\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{d}{b+s},\infty}}\||u|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}u\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2-2(b+s)},2}}.

Noting that

(2.3)

\frac{d+2-2b}{2d}=\frac{4-2b}{d}\cdot\frac{1}{2}+\frac{d-2}{2d}=\frac{1}{\rho}\cdot(\frac{4-2b}{d}+1),\ \frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{\gamma}\cdot(\frac{4-2b}{d}+1).

it follows from Lemma
2.9
that

\displaystyle\||\nabla|^{s}(|u|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}u)\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2-2b},2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\min\left\{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L_{x}^{2}}^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}\||\nabla|^{s}u\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d-2},2}},\|u\|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}_{L^{\gamma}_{t}L_{x}^{\rho,2}}\||\nabla|^{s}u\|_{L^{\gamma}_{t}L_{x}^{\rho,2}}\right\}.

On the other hand, since
s<\frac{d-2b}{2}+1
, we have that
\frac{1}{\rho}>\frac{2s}{d\gamma}
and

\frac{d+2-2(b+s)}{2d}=(\frac{4-2b}{d}+1)\cdot(\frac{1}{\rho}-\frac{2s}{d\gamma})=\frac{\gamma}{2}\cdot(\frac{1}{\rho}-\frac{2s}{d\gamma}).

By Hölder and Sobolev’s embedding in Lorentz space (Lemma

),

\||u|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}u\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2-2(b+s)},2}}\lesssim\||\nabla|^{\frac{2s}{\gamma}}u\|^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}_{L_{t}^{\gamma}L_{x}^{\rho,2}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\gamma}L_{x}^{\rho,2}}^{\frac{\gamma}{2}-1}\||\nabla|^{s}u\|_{L_{t}^{\gamma}L_{x}^{\rho,2}},

where we used the Gagliardo-Nirenberg’s inequality (Lemma
2.4
) in the second inequality.
Combining the above estimates, we obtain the desired estimate in Lemma
2.11
.
∎

(

) follows immediately from Hölder’s inequality and (
2.3
):

\|u\|_{S^{0}_{*}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\||x|^{-b}\|_{L^{\frac{d}{b},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|u\|_{L^{\gamma}_{t}L^{\rho,2}_{x}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{4-2b}{d}+1}\lesssim 1.

To prove (
2.7
), we use Duhamel’s formula and Lemma
2.11
to obtain

\displaystyle\||\nabla|^{s}u\|_{L^{\gamma}_{t}L_{x}^{\rho,2}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\||\nabla|^{s}(|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}u)\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2},2}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|u\|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}_{L^{\gamma}_{t}L_{x}^{\rho,2}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\||\nabla|^{s}u\|_{L^{\gamma}_{t}L_{x}^{\rho,2}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

By the continuity method,

\||\nabla|^{s}u\|_{L^{\gamma}_{t}L_{x}^{\rho,2}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}^{s}_{x}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Therefore, by Duhamel’s formula and Strichartz estimate,

\displaystyle\||\nabla|^{s}u\|_{S^{0}_{*}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\||\nabla|^{s}(|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}u)\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2},2}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|u\|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}_{L^{\gamma}_{t}L_{x}^{\rho,2}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\||\nabla|^{s}u\|_{L^{\gamma}_{t}L_{x}^{\rho,2}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}\dot{H}^{s}_{x}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

∎

2.4.
The construction of scattering solutions away from the origin.

The following Proposition establishes the existence of scattering solutions to (
1.1
) associated with initial data living sufficiently far from the origin. As a benefit of this, the spatial center and the frequency center for the almost periodic solutions in Section
3
can be chosen as
x(t)\equiv\xi(t)\equiv 0
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\xi_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfy

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(|x_{n}|+|\xi_{n}|\right)=\infty.

Let
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and define

\phi_{n}(x)=:e^{ix\cdot\xi_{n}}\phi(x-x_{n}).

Then there exists a subsequence
n_{k}
and a global scattering solution
v_{n_{k}}
to (
1.1
) satisfying

v_{n_{k}}(0)=\phi_{n_{k}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|v_{n_{k}}\|_{L^{\gamma}_{t}L^{\rho,2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|\phi\|_{L^{2}},

where the implicit constant independent of
k
.

Proof.

The proof is inspired by
[
28
]
. Let

v_{n}(t,x)=:e^{it\Delta}\left(e^{ix\cdot\xi_{n}}\phi(x-x_{n})\right)=e^{-it|\xi_{n}|^{2}}e^{ix\cdot\xi_{n}}e^{it\Delta}\phi(x-2t\xi_{n}-x_{n}),

and

e_{n}(t,x)=:-\mu|x|^{-b}|v_{n}(t,x)|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}v_{n}(t,x).

Assume that we can find a subsequence
n_{k}
such that

(2.8)

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|e_{n_{k}}(t,x)\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2},2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}=0.

Then Proposition
2.4
follows immediately from (
2.8
) and the stability results in Proposition
2.3
.

Therefore, it sufficies to prove (
2.8
). For any
\varepsilon>0
, we take a
\psi(t,x)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

(2.9)

\|\psi(t,x)-e^{it\Delta}\phi(x)\|_{L^{\gamma}_{t}L_{x}^{\rho,2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\varepsilon.

Fix
T>0
. We first consider the estimate of
e_{n}
on
[-T,T]
.
Let

\Phi_{n}(t,x)=:-\mu|x|^{-b}|\psi(t,x-2t\xi_{n}-x_{n})|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}\psi(t,x-2t\xi_{n}-x_{n}).

Then by Hölder’s inequality, we have, on
[-T,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\|e_{n}(t,x)-\Phi_{n}(t,x)\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2},2}([-T,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}

(2.10)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\|e^{it\Delta}\phi\|_{L_{t}^{\gamma}L_{x}^{\rho,2}}^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}+\|\psi(t,x)\|_{L_{t}^{\gamma}L_{x}^{\rho,2}}^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}\right)\|\psi(t,x)-e^{it\Delta}\phi(x)\|_{L_{t}^{\gamma}L_{x}^{\rho,2}}\lesssim\varepsilon.

Next, we claim that there exists a subsequence
n_{k}
such that

(2.11)

\limsup_{T\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|\Phi_{n_{k}}\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2},2}([-T,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}=0.

To prove (
2.11
), we consider two cases.

Case 1:

\frac{|\xi_{n}|}{|x_{n}|}+\frac{|x_{n}|}{|\xi_{n}|}
is bounded. Without loss of generality, we assume there exists
n_{k}
and
\alpha_{0}\in(0,\infty)
such that

(2.12)

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|x_{n_{k}}|}{|\xi_{n_{k}}|}=\alpha_{0}.

In particular, we have
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}|x_{n_{k}}|=|\xi_{n_{k}}|=\infty
.
When
|t|<\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}-\frac{1}{T}
and
x
is bounded, we have, for
k
sufficiently large,

|x+2t\xi_{n_{k}}+x_{n_{k}}|\geq|x_{n_{k}}|-2|t||\xi_{n_{k}}|-|x|\geq\frac{1}{T}|\xi_{n_{k}}|-|x|\geq\frac{1}{2T}|\xi_{n_{k}}|.

Similarly, when
|t|>\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}+\frac{1}{T}
and
x
is bounded, we have, for
k
sufficiently large,

|x+2t\xi_{n_{k}}+x_{n_{k}}|\geq 2|t||\xi_{n_{k}}|-|x_{n_{k}}|-|x|\geq\frac{1}{T}|\xi_{n_{k}}|-|x|\geq\frac{1}{2T}|\xi_{n_{k}}|.

Therefore, letting
I_{T}=:[-T,T]\cap\left\{t:|t|<\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}-\frac{1}{T}\right\}\cup\left\{t:|t|>\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}+\frac{1}{T}\right\}
, we have

(2.13)

\|\Phi_{n_{k}}(t,x)\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2},2}(I_{T}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim T^{b}|\xi_{n_{k}}|^{-b}\||\psi|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}\psi\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2},2}(I_{T}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

On the other hand, by Hölder’s inequality,

\|\Phi_{n_{k}}(t,x)\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2},2}(\{\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}-\frac{1}{T}<|t|<\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}+\frac{1}{T}\}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|\psi(t,x)\|_{L^{\gamma}_{t}L_{x}^{\rho,2}(\{\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}-\frac{1}{T}<|t|<\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}+\frac{1}{T}\}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{4-2b}{d}+1}.

Combining the above estimate with (
2.13
), and using the monotone convergence theorem, we obtain (
2.11
).

Case 2:

\frac{|\xi_{n}|}{|x_{n}|}+\frac{|x_{n}|}{|\xi_{n}|}
is unbounded. Without loss of generality, we assume there exists
n_{k}
such that

(2.14)

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|x_{n_{k}}|}{|\xi_{n_{k}}|}=\infty.

Hence, when
x
is bounded, we have, for
k
sufficiently large,

|x+2t\xi_{n_{k}}+x_{n_{k}}|\geq|x_{n_{k}}|-2|T||\xi_{n_{k}}|-|x|\geq\frac{1}{2}|x_{n_{k}}|.

Proceeding as in the previous case, we obtain (
2.11
).

Finally, we consider the estimate of
e_{n}(t,x)
on the interval
\left\{t:|t|>T\right\}
. By Hölder’s inequality,

\|e_{n}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2},2}(\{|t|>T\}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|e^{it\Delta}\phi\|_{L_{t}^{\gamma}L_{x}^{\rho,2}(\{|t|>T\}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{4-2b}{d}+1},

which tends to
0
as

by Strichartz and the monotone convergence theorem. This combined with (
2.10
) and (
2.11
) yields (
2.8
), and the proof of Proposition
2.3
is completed.
∎

3.
Reduction to almost periodic solutions

The aim of this section is to demonstrate that if Theorem
1.1
fails, then one could construct minimal counterexamples. As a consequence of minimality, the minimal counterexamples will satisfy the properties listed in Subsection
3.2
.

3.1.
Reduction to almost periodic solutions

The main ingredient we need to prove Theorem
1.2
is the following linear profile decomposition of
[
2
, Theorem 5.4]
.

Proposition 3.1

(Linear profile decomposition)
.

Let
u_{n}
be a bounded sequence in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then after passing to a subsequence if necessary, there exist
J^{*}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
; profiles
\phi^{j}\in L^{2}\setminus\{0\}
; scales
\lambda_{n}^{j}\in(0,\infty)
; space translation parameters
x_{n}^{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
; frequency translation parameters
\xi_{n}^{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
; time translation parameters
t_{n}^{j}
; and remainders
w_{n}^{J}
such that the following decomposition holds for
1\leq J\leq J^{*}
:

(3.1)

u_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{J}(\lambda^{j}_{n})^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{ix\cdot\xi^{j}_{n}}[e^{it_{n}^{j}\Delta}\phi^{j}](\frac{x-x_{n}^{j}}{\lambda_{n}^{j}})+w_{n}^{J};

here
w_{n}^{J}\in L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
obey

(3.2)

\limsup_{J\to J^{*}}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|e^{it\Delta}w_{n}^{J}\|_{L_{t,x}^{\frac{2(d+2)}{d}}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}=0.

Moreover, for any
j\neq k
,

(3.3)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\biggl\{\log\bigl[\tfrac{\lambda_{n}^{j}}{\lambda_{n}^{k}}\bigr]+\tfrac{|x_{n}^{j}-x_{n}^{k}|^{2}}{\lambda_{n}^{j}\lambda_{n}^{k}}+\lambda_{n}^{j}\lambda_{n}^{k}|\xi_{n}^{j}-\xi_{n}^{k}|^{2}+\tfrac{|t_{n}^{j}(\lambda_{n}^{j})^{2}-t_{n}^{k}(\lambda_{n}^{k})^{2}|}{\lambda_{n}^{j}\lambda_{n}^{k}}\biggr\}=\infty.

In addition, we may assume that either
t_{n}^{j}\equiv 0
or
t_{n}^{j}\to\pm\infty
, either
x_{n}^{j}\equiv 0
or
\frac{|x_{n}^{j}|}{\lambda_{n}^{j}}\to\infty
, and that either
\xi_{n}^{j}\equiv 0
or
\lambda_{n}^{j}\xi_{n}^{j}\to\infty
.
Furthermore, for any
J\geq 1
we have the mass decoupling property

(3.4)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}[M(u_{n})-\sum_{j=1}^{J}M(\phi^{j})-M(w_{n}^{J})]=0.

Remark 3.1
.

In the situation of inhomogeneous NLS,
we will use the following vanishing condition

(3.5)

\limsup_{J\to J^{*}}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|e^{it\Delta}w_{n}^{J}\|_{L_{t}^{\gamma}L_{x}^{\rho,2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}=0,

instead of (
3.2
). Indeed, by Strichartz estimate and (
3.4
),

(3.6)

\|e^{it\Delta}w_{n}^{J}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}\cap L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}}\lesssim\|w^{J}_{n}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|u_{n}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim 1.

Using Lemma
2.3
to interpolate between (
3.2
) and (
3.6
), we obtain (
3.5
).

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We will only prove the existence of the almost periodic solution, since the three enemies reduction follows from the same argument of
[
23
]
, which relies only on the structure of group and the information on the movement of

in Subsection
3.2
.

Let

\displaystyle M_{c}=:\sup\{m:\text{If }\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}<m,\ \text{then (INLS)}(u_{0})\ \text{is global and scatters}\},

where INLS(
u_{0}
) denotes to the solution of the equation (
1.1
) with initial data

.
In light of the small-data scattering theory in Proposition
2.2
, the failure of Theorem

implies

, we can find a sequence of initial data

with corresponding global solutions

) satisfying the following:

Applying Proposition

Proof of Lemma

We give only a description of the proof of the lemma, as the standard argument can be found in

The key ingredient is the following decoupling statement for the nonlinear profiles:

(3.15)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|v_{n}^{j}v_{n}^{k}\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}L_{x}^{\frac{\rho}{2},1}\cap L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}=0\quad\text{for}\quad j\neq k,

We now prove (

\displaystyle\|\sum_{j=1}^{J}v_{n}^{j}\|_{L^{\gamma}_{t}L_{x}^{\rho,2}\cap L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}^{2}=\|(\sum_{j=1}^{J}v_{n}^{j})^{2}\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}L_{x}^{\frac{\rho}{2},1}\cap L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x}},

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|v_{n}^{j}\|^{2}_{L_{t}^{\gamma}L_{x}^{\rho,2}\cap L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}+\sum_{j\neq k}\|v_{n}^{j}v_{n}^{k}\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}L_{x}^{\frac{\rho}{2},1}\cap L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|\phi^{j}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}+\sum_{j\neq k}\|v_{n}^{j}v_{n}^{k}\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}L_{x}^{\frac{\rho}{2},1}\cap L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}.

The above estimate combined with the vanish (

\limsup_{J\to J^{*}}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|u_{n}^{J}\|_{L_{t}^{\gamma}L_{x}^{\rho,2}\cap L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\limsup_{J\to J^{*}}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{J}M(\phi^{j})+M(w_{n}^{J})\right)\lesssim M_{c}.

We now focus on the proof of (

f(z)=:-\mu|z|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}z
and

. Observe that

Using the elementary inequality
|f(z_{1})-f(z_{2})|\lesssim(|z_{1}|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}+|z_{2}|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}})|z_{1}-z_{2}|
, we get

|e|\lesssim|x|^{-b}(|u_{n}^{J}|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}+|e^{it\Delta}w_{n}^{J}|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}})|e^{it\Delta}w_{n}^{J}|+|x|^{-b}\sum_{j\neq k}|v_{n}^{k}||v_{n}^{j}|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}.

By Lemma
2.11

\displaystyle\||x|^{-b}(|u_{n}^{J}|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}+|e^{it\Delta}w_{n}^{J}|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}})|e^{it\Delta}w_{n}^{J}|\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2},2}}

Lemma 3.4

(Spacetime bound)
.

If

is an almost periodic solution to (
1.1
) on an interval
J
. Then for any Schrödinger admissible pair

Proof.

Step 1:
We first prove the upper bound

(3.18)

\|u\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r,2}(J\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{q}\lesssim 1+\int_{J}N(t)^{2}dt.

Let
0<\eta<1
to be determined later and partition

into subintervals

such that

\int_{I_{j}}N(t)^{2}\,dt\leq\eta,\qquad 1\leq j\leq\eta^{-1}\left(1+\int_{J}N(t)^{2}dt\right).

For each

Let

be defined by (
1.2

). By Strichartz’ inequality, we have the following estimates on the spacetime slab

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\gamma}L_{x}^{\rho,2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|e^{i(t-t_{j})\Delta}u(t_{j})\|_{L_{t}^{\gamma}L_{x}^{\rho,2}}+\||x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}u\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2},2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{\geq C(\eta)N(t_{j})}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+|I_{j}|^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}N(t_{j})^{d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\rho})}\|u(t_{j})\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|u\|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}+1}_{L_{t}^{\gamma}L_{x}^{\rho,2}},

the first two terms in (

Again by Strichartz estimate,

Step 2:
Now we prove the lower bound

Thus, using (
3.21
) and integrating over

3.20
).
∎

5.
Rapid cascade

In this section we exclude the first of two blow-up solutions, the rapid frequency cascade.

Theorem 5.1
.

There does not exist an almost periodic solution to (
1.1
) when

(5.1)

\int_{0}^{\infty}N(t)^{3}dt<\infty.

The key to proving Theorem
5.1
lies in using Proposition
1.1
and (
4.8
) to demonstrate that in the case (
5.1
) the almost periodic solution has additional regularity.

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose
u
is an almost periodic solution to (
1.1
), and
\int_{0}^{\infty}N(t)^{3}dt=K<\infty
. Then for
0\leq s<\min\left\{\frac{d-2b}{2}+1,\frac{4-2b}{d}+1\right\}
,

\|u(t,x)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}\dot{H}^{s}_{x}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim K^{s}.

Proof.

First by (
5.1
) and (
3.16
),

|N(T_{1})-N(T_{2})|\lesssim\int_{T_{2}}^{T_{1}}|N^{\prime}(t)|dt\lesssim\int_{T_{2}}^{T_{1}}N(t)^{3}dt\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{as}\quad T_{2}\rightarrow\infty.

This together with
\int_{0}^{\infty}N(t)^{3}dt<\infty
implies

(5.2)

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}N(t)=0.

Further utilizing (
1.10
) and (
5.2
), we have, for any
N>0
and any
\eta>0
,

\limsup_{T\rightarrow+\infty}\sigma_{[0,T]}\big(\tfrac{N}{2}\big)\leq\limsup_{T\rightarrow+\infty}\|P_{>N}u(T)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\leq\limsup_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|P_{>C(\eta)N(t)}u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\leq\eta.

Hence, for any
N>0

\limsup_{T\rightarrow+\infty}\sigma_{[0,T]}\big(\tfrac{N}{2}\big)=\limsup_{T\rightarrow+\infty}\|P_{>N}u(T)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}=0.

Then by (
1.15
) and (
4.8
), for
\frac{1}{2}\leq s<\min\left\{\frac{d-2b}{2}+1,\frac{4-2b}{d}+1\right\}
,

\|P_{>N}u\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d-2},2}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|P_{>N}F(u)\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2},2}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\frac{K^{\frac{1}{2}}}{N^{s}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}\dot{H}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Therefore, for any
0\leq t_{0}<\infty
and
\frac{1}{2}\leq s<\min\left\{\frac{d-2b}{2}+1,\frac{4-2b}{d}+1\right\}
,

\displaystyle\|P_{>N}u(t_{0})\|_{L^{2}_{x}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}\langle\frac{d}{dt}P_{>N}u,P_{>N}u\rangle dt|\lesssim|\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}\langle P_{>N}F(u),P_{>N}u\rangle dt|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|P_{>N}F(u)\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2},2}}\|P_{>N}u\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d-2},2}}\lesssim\frac{K}{N^{2s}}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{t}\dot{H}_{x}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}}.

Hence for any
\gamma\leq s<\min\left\{\frac{d-2b}{2}+1,\frac{4-2b}{d}+1\right\}
with
0\leq\gamma<\frac{1}{2}
,

(5.3)

\displaystyle\|P_{>K}u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{M>K}M^{s}\|P_{M}u\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\sum_{M>K}M^{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}K^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s-\gamma}}\lesssim K^{\gamma}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}\dot{H}^{s-\gamma}_{x}}.

Using Bernstein’s inequality and (
5.3
) to separately control the high and low frequencies, we get

(5.4)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}\dot{H}^{s}_{x}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim_{s,\gamma}K^{s}+K^{\gamma}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}\dot{H}^{s-\gamma}_{x}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Iterating (
5.4
) starting with
s=\gamma
and using the conservation of mass, we obtain the desired estimate in Lemma
5.1
.
∎

Now we utilize the additional regularity in Lema
5.1
to establish Theorem
5.1
.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.
.

By Lemma
5.1
, for
t>0,1<s<\min\left\{\frac{d-2b}{2}+1,\frac{4-2b}{d}+1\right\}
,

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{>C(\eta)N(t)}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}+\|u_{\leq C(\eta)N(t)}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{>C(\eta)N(t)}\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\frac{1}{s}}\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{\frac{1}{s}}+C(\eta)N(t)

\displaystyle\lesssim\eta^{1-\frac{1}{s}}K+C(\eta)N(t).

Therefore, by (
5.2
),

(5.5)

\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}=0,

which together with the Gagliardo-Nirenberg’s inequality (

) implies that

. Hence
u\equiv 0
, which is a contradiction.
∎

6.

Let

(6.8)

Proof of Claim

Intergrating by parts and using the fact

\displaystyle|(\ref{695})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k}N(J_{k})\int_{J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|F(Iu)-IF(u)||Iu|dxdt

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k}N(J_{k})\|F(Iu)-IF(u)\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2},2}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|Iu\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d-2},2}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k}N(J_{k})o_{K}(1)\lesssim\int_{0}^{T}N(t)^{3}dto_{K}(1)\lesssim Ko_{K}(1).

This completes the proof of Claim
6.2
.
∎

Appendix A
Smoothing algorithm and control of error term

In this appendix, we introduce the smoothing algorithm and use the long time Strichartz estimate to control the errors that arise from frequency truncation in Section
6
.

A.1.
Smoothing algorithm

Divide
[0,\infty)
into consecutive intervals
J_{n}=:[a_{n},a_{n+1})
(called
small interval
) such that

\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\gamma}L_{x}^{\rho,2}([a_{n},a_{n+1})\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}=1,

where
(\gamma,\rho)
was defined by (
1.2
).
By Lemma
3.2
, there exists
J_{0}<\infty
such that for all
t\in[a_{n},a_{n+1})
,

\frac{N(a_{n})}{J_{0}}\leq N(t)\leq J_{0}N(a_{n}),

Possibly after modifying the
C(\eta)
in (
1.9
), (
1.10
) by a constant, we can choose
N(a_{n})=J_{0}^{i_{n}}
,
i_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
, i.e.

(A.1)

\frac{N(a_{n})}{N(a_{n+1})}=1,\quad J_{0},\quad\text{or}\quad J_{0}^{-1};

and the starting and ending points of the peaks and valleys, as defined below, coincide with the starting or ending points of the intervals
[a_{n},a_{n+1})
.

Definition A.1

(Peaks and valleys)
.

(1) (
peak
): An interval
[a,b)
is called as a peak of length
n
, if it satisfies:

(i)
N(t)\equiv C,\forall t\in[a,b)
, and
\|u\|^{\gamma}_{L^{\gamma}_{t}L_{x}^{\rho,2}([a,b)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}=n
;

(ii) If
[a_{-},a]
,
[b,b_{+}]
are the small intervals adjacent to
[a,b]
, then
N(a_{-})<N(a)
,
N(b_{+})<N(b)
, i.e.
N(a_{-})=\frac{N(a)}{J_{0}}
,
N(b_{+})=\frac{N(b)}{J_{0}}
.

(2) (
valley
): An interval
[a,b)
is called as a valley of length
n
, if it satisfies:

(i)
N(t)\equiv C,\forall t\in[a,b)
, and
\|u\|^{\gamma}_{L^{\gamma}_{t}L_{x}^{\rho,2}([a,b)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}=n
;

(ii) If
[a_{-},a]
,
[b,b_{+}]
are the small intervals adjacent to
[a,b]
, then
N(a_{-})>N(a)
,
N(b_{+})>N(b)
.

(3) In particular, If
[a_{-},a]
and
[a,a_{+}]
are adjacent small intervals, and
N(a)>N(a_{-}),N(a_{+})
, then we call
\{a\}
a peak of length 0. Similarly, if
N(a_{-})>N(a),N(a_{+})
, then we call
\{a\}
a valley of length zero.

Remark A.1
.

We label the peaks
p_{k}
and the valleys
v_{k}
. Because
N(0)=1
and
N(t)\leq 1
we start with a peak. Moreover, by (
A.1
), the peaks and valleys are alternate:
p_{0},v_{0},p_{1},v_{1},\ldots
.

Lemma A.1
.

(A.2)

\int_{0}^{T}|N^{\prime}(t)dt|\leq 2\sum_{0<p_{k}<T}N(p_{k})+2.

Proof.

Since
N(t)
is monotonic between each peak and valley,

\displaystyle\int_{v_{k}}^{p_{k+1}}|N^{\prime}(t)|\,dt=N(p_{k+1})-N(v_{k})\leq N(p_{k+1}),

\displaystyle\int_{p_{k}}^{v_{k}}|N^{\prime}(t)|\,dt=N(p_{k})-N(v_{k})\leq N(p_{k}),

The above estimates combined with the endpoint, yield (
A.2
).
∎

Now we describe an iterative algorithm to construct progressively less oscillatory
N_{m}(t)
.

Step 1:

N_{0}(t)=N(t)
.

Step 2:
If
[a,b]
is peak for
N_{0}(t)
and
[a_{-},a],[b,b_{+}]
are the adjacent small intervals, let
N_{1}(t)=N_{0}(a_{-})=\frac{N(a)}{J_{0}}
for
t\in[a_{-},b_{+}]
.

\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots

Step m+2:
If
[a,b]
is peak for
N_{m}(t)
and
[a_{-},a],[b,b_{+}]
are the adjacent small intervals, let
N_{m+1}(t)=N_{m}(a_{-})=\frac{N_{m}(a)}{J_{0}}
for
t\in[a_{-},b_{+}]
.

Definition A.2
.

If
[a_{m},b_{m})
and
[a_{m+1},b_{m+1})
are peaks of
N_{m}(t)
and
N_{m+1}(t)
respectively, and
[a_{m},b_{m})\subset[a_{m+1},b_{m+1})
, then
[a_{m},b_{m})
is called the parent of
[a_{m+1},b_{m+1})
.

In Section
6
, we utilized some properties of
N_{m}(t)
. These are established in the following proposition.

Proposition A.1
.

(i)
N(t)\geq N_{m+1}(t)\geq\frac{N_{m}(t)}{J_{0}}\geq\cdots\geq\frac{N(t)}{J_{0}^{m+1}}
,

(ii) For a given peak
[a_{m+1},b_{m+1})
in
N_{m+1}(t)
, the peak
[a_{m},b_{m})
in the previous generation
N_{m}(t)
must either satisfy
[a_{m},b_{m})\subset[a_{m+1},b_{m+1})
or
[a_{m},b_{m})\cap[a_{m+1},b_{m+1})=\emptyset
,

(iii) Each peak of
N_{m+1}(t)
has at least one parent,

(iv)
|\frac{N_{m}^{\prime}(t)}{N_{m}(t)^{3}}|\leq C
,
C
is independent of
m
,

(iv) Each peak
[a_{m},b_{m})
of
N_{m}(t)
contains at least
2m
small intervals,

(v) For any
K>m
,

(A.3)

K=\int_{0}^{T}N(t)^{3}dt\gtrsim m\int_{0}^{T}|N_{m}^{\prime}(t)|dt.

Proof.

(i)–(iii) follows directly from the construction of
N_{m}(t)
, and (iv) follows from (
3.16
) and the fact that
N_{m}(t)=N(t)
whenever
N_{m}^{\prime}(t)\neq 0
.

(iv) In the construction of
N_{m}(t)
, the two small intervals adjacent to each peak of
N_{m-1}(t)
are modified. Thus, each iteration adds at least
2
small intervals.

(v) By Lemma
3.3
and (i),

K\gtrsim\sum_{J_{n}\subset[0,T]}N(J_{n})\geq\sum_{k}\sum_{J_{n}\subset P_{k}^{m}}N_{m}(J_{n}).

The above estimate combined with (iv) and Lemma
A.1
implies

K\gtrsim 2m\sum_{0<p_{k}^{m}<T}N_{m}(p_{k}^{m})\geq m\int_{0}^{T}|N_{m}^{\prime}(t)|dt-2m,

which yields (v) for
K\geq m
.
∎

A.2.
Controlling the errors that arise from frequency truncation

Lemma A.2
.

Let
u
be the almost periodic solution given by Theorem
1.2
and
F(u)=\pm|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}u
. Then

\|P_{\leq K}F(u)-F(P_{\leq K}u)\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2},2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim o_{K}(1),

where
o_{K}(1)\rightarrow 0
as
K\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\rho
be some quantity to be specified later,
\rho\leq\frac{1}{2}
and
\rho\geq K^{-1/10}
. Let
u_{l}=:P_{\leq\rho K}u
,
v=:u-u_{l}=P_{\geq\rho K}u
.
Then

(A.4)

\displaystyle P_{\leq K}F(u)-F(P_{\leq K}u)=P_{\leq K}F(u_{l})-F(u_{l})

(A.5)

\displaystyle\ -\left[F(P_{\leq K}u)-F(u_{l}))-P_{\leq K}(G(u_{l},P_{\leq K}v)v)\right]

(A.6)

\displaystyle\ +P_{\leq K}F(u)-P_{\leq K}F(u_{l})-P_{\leq K}(G(u_{l},P_{\leq K}v)v),

where

G(f,g)h=:|x|^{-b}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{d+2-b}{d}|f+\theta g|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}h+\frac{2-b}{d}|f+\theta g|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}-2}(f+\theta g)^{2}\overline{h}\right)d\theta.

By Bernstein, Lemma
2.11
and (
4.9
),

\|(\ref{6101})\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2},2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{K}\|u_{l}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}\|\nabla u_{l}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d-2},2}}+\frac{1}{K}\||\nabla|^{\frac{2}{\gamma}}u_{l}\|_{L_{t}^{\gamma}L_{x}^{\rho,2}}^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\lesssim\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Next, note that

(\ref{6102})=-[G(u_{l},P_{\leq K}v)(P_{\leq K}v)-P_{\leq K}(G(u_{l},P_{\leq K}v)v)].

Let
\phi
be the multiplier used in (
6.1
) to define the frequency projection.
Using the fundamental theorem of calculus when
|\xi_{2}|\ll|\xi_{1}|
, and
|\phi(\xi)|\lesssim 1
when
|\xi_{1}|\lesssim|\xi_{2}|
, we have

(A.7)

|\phi(\frac{\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}}{K})-\phi(\frac{\xi_{1}}{K})|\lesssim\frac{|\xi_{2}|}{|\xi_{1}|}.

Let
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small and

m(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=:\left(\phi(\frac{\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}}{K})-\phi(\frac{\xi_{1}}{K})|\right)\frac{K^{\varepsilon}}{|\xi_{2}|^{\varepsilon}}.

Note that on the support of
\phi(\frac{\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}}{K})-\phi(\frac{\xi_{1}}{K})
, if
K\gg|\xi_{2}|
then
K\lesssim|\xi_{1}|
.
Hence, by (
A.7
),
m(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
is a Coifman-Meyer symbol. Applying Lemma
2.10
, Hölder and (
1.13
),

\displaystyle\|(\ref{6102})\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2},2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{K^{\varepsilon}}\|P_{\leq K}v\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d-2},2}}\||\nabla|^{\varepsilon}\left(|x|^{-b}\int_{0}^{1}|u_{l}+\theta P_{\leq K}v|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}d\theta\right)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{d}{2},\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{K^{\varepsilon}}(1+\frac{K^{1/2}}{(\rho K)^{1/2}})\int_{0}^{1}\||\nabla|^{\varepsilon}(|u_{l}+\theta P_{\leq K}v|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}})\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{d}{2-b}}}d\theta.

When
\frac{4-2b}{d}<1
:
By Lemma
2.12
,

\displaystyle\||\nabla|^{\varepsilon}(|u_{l}+\theta P_{\leq K}v|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}})\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{d}{2-b}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{l}+\theta P_{\leq K}v\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}^{\frac{4-2b}{d}-\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}}\||\nabla|^{1-\varepsilon}(u_{l}+\theta P_{\leq K}v)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L_{x}^{2}}^{\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\lesssim K^{\frac{9}{10}\varepsilon}+K^{\varepsilon}\|P_{>K^{\frac{9}{10}}}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}^{\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}},

where we used

\displaystyle\||\nabla|^{1-\varepsilon}u_{l}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||\nabla|^{1-\varepsilon}P_{\leq K^{\frac{9}{10}}}P_{\leq\rho K}u\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\||\nabla|^{1-\varepsilon}P_{>K^{\frac{9}{10}}}P_{\leq\rho K}u\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

(A.8)

\displaystyle\lesssim K^{\frac{9}{10}(1-\varepsilon)}+K^{1-\varepsilon}\|P_{>K^{\frac{9}{10}}}u\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

When
\frac{4-2b}{d}\geq 1:
By Lemma
2.8
and the same argument used to derive (
A.8
),

\displaystyle\||\nabla|^{\varepsilon}(|u_{l}+\theta P_{\leq K}v|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}})\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{d}{2-b}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{l}+\theta P_{\leq K}v\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}\||\nabla|^{\varepsilon}(u_{l}+\theta P_{\leq K}v)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim K^{\frac{9}{10}\varepsilon}+K^{\varepsilon}\|P_{>K^{\frac{9}{10}}}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L_{x}^{2}}.

Finally, we estimate (
A.6
). By Hölder and the long time Strichartz estimate (
1.13
),

\displaystyle\|(\ref{6103})\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2},2}}=\|P_{\leq K}(G(u_{l},v)v)-P_{\leq K}\left(G(u_{l},P_{\leq K}v)v\right)\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2},2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d-2},2}}\left\|\int_{0}^{1}\left(|u_{l}+\theta v|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}-|u_{l}+\theta P_{\leq K}v|^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}\right)d\theta\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{d}{2},2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|P_{\geq\rho K}u\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d-2},2}}(\|u_{l}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}+\|P_{\geq\rho K}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}})^{\frac{4-2b}{d}-1}\|P_{\geq K}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\rho^{1/2}}\|P_{\geq K}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}},

when
\frac{4-2b}{d}>1
.
Similarly, when
\frac{4-2b}{d}\leq 1
, we have

\|(\ref{6103})\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2},2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\rho^{1/2}}\|P_{\geq K}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}^{\frac{4-2b}{d}}.

Combining the above estimates, we get

\displaystyle\|P_{\leq K}F(u)-F(P_{\leq K}u)\|_{L^{2}_{t}L_{x}^{\frac{2d}{d+2},2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\rho+\frac{1}{\rho^{1/2}}(K^{-\frac{1}{10}\varepsilon}+\|P_{>K^{\frac{9}{10}}}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}^{\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}})+\frac{1}{\rho^{1/2}}\|P_{\geq CK}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}^{\min\left\{\frac{4-2b}{d},1\right\}}.

Fixing
\rho>0
sufficiently small, then letting
K\rightarrow\infty
, we complete the proof of Lemma
A.2
.
∎

Prediction of Ki-67 expression and malignant potential in gastrointestinal stromal tumors: novel models based on CE-CT and serological indicators
Authors: Jun Tian, Weizhi Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo identify more reliable imaging and serological indicators for predicting Ki-67 expression and malignant potential in gastrointestinal stromal tumors, as well as to develop a preoperative prediction model with clinical utility.Patients and methodsThis study retrospectively analyzed patients with gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GIST) diagnosed at the First Affiliated Hospital of Jinzhou Medical University between May 2018 and May 2024. Univariate logistic analyses, two-way stepwise regression, P-value stepwise regression, and LASSO regression were employed to screen for Ki-67 high expression and high malignant potential risk factors associated with GIST. Models were established using various regression methods; Nomograms, calibration curves, and clinical decision curves were generated for the two best prediction models.ResultsTwo-way stepwise regression analysis revealed that diameter (P=0.037; OR=1.22; 95% CI: 1.01 - 1.46), growth pattern (extraluminal type: P=0.028; OR=3.54; 95% CI: 1.14 - 10.94), enhancement model (P=0.099; OR=0.39; 95% CI: 0.12 - 1.20), EVFDM (P=0.069; OR=0.43; 95% CI: 0.17 - 1.07), PLR (P=0.099; OR=3.06; 95% CI: 0.81 - 11.59), and OPNI (P=0.058; OR=2.38; 95% CI: 0.97 - 5.84) are identified as independent risk factors for Ki-67 expression. Utilizing the two-way stepwise regression model to predict Ki-67 expression, the area under the curve (AUC) for the training group was 0.865 (95% CI: 0.807-0.922), while for the validation group it was 0.784 (95% CI: 0.631-0.937). The Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) and Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) for the training group were 153.360 and 174.619, respectively. Two-way stepwise regression analysis revealed that volume (P < .001, OR = 1.06; 95% CI: 1.03 - 1.09), contour (P = 0.066; OR = 0.17; 95% CI: 0.05 - 0.62), ulcer (P = 0.094; OR = 0.16; 95% CI: 0.03 - 0.98), IBSC (P = 0.008; OR = 5.27; 95% CI: 1.57 - 17.69), and OPNI (P = 0.045; OR = 0.22; 95% CI: 0.05 - 0.96) are independent risk factors for malignant potential. Utilizing the two-way stepwise regression model to predict malignant potential, the AUC for the training group was 0.950 (95% CI: 0.920 - 0.980), while for the validation group it was 0.936 (95% CI: 0.867 - 1.000). The AIC and BIC values for the training group were 96.330 and 114.552, respectively.ConclusionDiameter, growth pattern, enhancement pattern, EVFDM, PLR, and OPNI are independent risk factors for GIST with high Ki-67 expression. Additionally, volume, contour, ulceration, IBSC, and OPNI serve as independent risk factors for GIST with high malignant potential. The preoperative models developed using CT images can predict the malignant potential and Ki-67 expression status of GIST to a certain extent. When combined with serological indicators, these models' predictive performance can be further enhanced.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-024-13172-y.Keywords: Gastrointestinal stromal tumor, Contrast-enhanced CT, Serological indicators·Ki-67, Malignant potential, Model

Introduction

Gastrointestinal Stromal Tumor (GIST) is the most common stromal tumor of the digestive tract, originating from the muscularis propria and developing from Cajal interstitial cells, which serve as the pacemaker cells of the gastrointestinal tract [1]. GIST is classified as a potentially malignant sarcoma and is often evaluated using the modified NIH risk classification criteria. This classification comprehensively considers factors such as tumor size, mitotic rate, location, and rupture. The risk of GIST recurrence is categorized into very low, low, medium, and high-risk groups [2]. However, the current classification criteria for GISTs primarily rely on postoperative pathology, highlighting the urgent need for a clearer and more comprehensive preoperative evaluation standard. Preoperative risk assessment is crucial for determining treatment options and predicting prognosis. Surgery is an effective treatment method for localized GIST, while imatinib serves as an important adjuvant treatment for metastatic GIST and high-risk cases [3]. To enhance non-invasive preoperative assessment and select more appropriate treatment options, some researchers have utilized imaging features to develop prediction models for GIST Ki-67 expression and malignant potential [4–7]. Commonly employed imaging features include tumor location, diameter, growth pattern, enhancement pattern, contour, necrosis, ulceration, and peritumoral vessels. However, few studies have integrated serological indicators with imaging features to predict the malignant potential of GIST. Serological indicators can, to some extent, compensate for functional changes that are not observable through imaging. Among the frequently utilized serological indicators for assessing GIST recurrence risk, the NLR, MLR, PLR, and OPNI have gained significant recognition and have been confirmed to correlate with the benign and malignant nature of tumors as well as the prognosis of various cancers, including gastric, colon, and esophageal cancers [8–10]. These indicators are also employed in the construction of predictive models; therefore, this study aims to combine both imaging and serological data to develop and validate a comprehensive model for predicting the malignant potential of GISTs.

Ki-67 serves as an indicator of tumor proliferation, with tumors exhibiting a higher proliferation capacity generally demonstrating greater malignant potential [11]. However, some smaller or early-stage tumors may be discovered incidentally during examinations, and relying solely on size to assess their malignant potential may not provide a reliable evaluation. For instance, small GIST measuring less than 2 cm can still possess malignant potential and exhibit the ability to metastasize [12]. Furthermore, various factors can cause discrepancies between the malignant potential of GIST and Ki-67 expression levels. One significant factor is the puncture site, as the selected biopsy location may not accurately represent the proliferation rate of the entire tumor [11]. Additionally, while tumor size and mitotic figures are critical indicators in evaluating the malignancy of gastrointestinal stromal tumors, Ki-67 expression status is often omitted from this assessment, potentially leading to an underestimation of the malignant potential in some early-stage tumors. Some researchers argue that Ki-67 may have a more comprehensive impact than mitotic figures, with high Ki-67 expression identified as an independent risk factor for elevated malignant potential [13]. Mitotic counting, which solely captures cell proliferation during the M phase, cannot provide a comprehensive assessment of tumor cells. In contrast, Ki-67 expression increases from the G1 phase through the mitotic phase and only decreases in the G0 phase [3]. Consequently, this study constructed a predictive model for Ki-67 expression. It is important to note that measuring Ki-67 expression levels is not a routine examination. Developing a Ki-67 expression prediction model can reduce the economic burden on patients and serve as a clinical reminder, particularly for small GIST with high malignant potential or proliferation capability, thus enabling early assessment of GIST. Xu et al. have demonstrated a correlation between inflammatory indicators and Ki-67 expression in glioma [14]; therefore, this study also analyzed the relationship between serological indicators and Ki-67 expression in GIST patients, incorporating relevant indicators into the predictive model.

Materials and methods

Patients

This study involved 193 patients who were pathologically diagnosed with GIST following surgery at the First Affiliated Hospital of Jinzhou Medical University between May 2018 and May 2024. All patients had complete preoperative examination data. The inclusion criteria were as follows: 1) age ≥ 18 years; 2) patients with GIST confirmed pathologically after surgery or endoscopic resection; 3) patients possessing complete preoperative enhanced CT scans, serological data, postoperative pathological diagnoses, and immunohistochemistry results; 4) patients without evidence of tumor rupture or metastasis on preoperative CT scans, and who had not received any drug treatment prior to the CT examination and surgery. The exclusion criteria included: 1) patients with other malignancies, such as gastric or colon cancer; 2) patients with hematological or immunological disorders, including leukemia, psoriasis, or ankylosing spondylitis; 3) patients with nutritional deficiencies, such as hypoalbuminemia, who required albumin supplementation before examination; 4) patients with acute or severe infections prior to surgery; 5) cases with unclear imaging that made it difficult to distinguish the patient's outline. This study was approved by the Ethics Review Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Jinzhou Medical University, with the ethics approval number KYLL202445. Histopathological examination

Two pathologists using a 50x microscope for Ki-67 detection and pathological diagnosis. A total of 1000 cells were randomly selected from each section, and Ki-67-positive cells were counted to determine expression levels. Controversy persists regarding the cut-off point for Ki-67 expression levels. While some studies utilize thresholds of 5%, 8%, and 10% [7, 11], the 10% threshold is generally more accepted. Consequently, this study employed a 10% cutoff value to classify Ki-67 expression levels into high and low expression categories, specifically high expression (Ki-67 > 10%) and low expression (Ki-67 ≤ 10%). Histological examination of the specimens was performed by a pathologist with over five years of experience. Tumor specimens measuring ≥2 cm in diameter were sectioned every 1 cm and adequately fixed to ensure the accuracy of subsequent immunohistochemistry. Postoperative pathological specimens were stained with hematoxylin and eosin (HE) to document tumor size and mitotic figures (recorded in units of 50 high-power fields). The risk classification of GISTs was assessed according to NIH standards, categorizing them into very low risk, low risk, intermediate risk, and high risk. Because very low and low risk tumors exhibit lower malignant potential and better prognosis, while intermediate and high risk tumors demonstrate higher malignant potential and poorer prognosis, the risk classification of GISTs was re-categorized into a low malignant potential group (very low and low risk) and a high malignant potential group (intermediate and high risk) [15]. The binary classification method is favored in prediction models due to its simplicity, ease of understanding, high efficiency, and accuracy. Additionally, it aligns well with the evaluation criteria established in existing clinical practice.

Serological indicators

Patients' pretreatment neutrophil, monocyte, platelet, lymphocyte counts, and albumin levels were obtained from the hospital's clinical record system. Subsequently, the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), monocyte-to-lymphocyte ratio (MLR), platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), and overall prognostic nutritional index (OPNI) were calculated. The NLR is defined as the ratio of neutrophil count to lymphocyte count, the PLR as the ratio of platelet count to lymphocyte count, the MLR as the ratio of monocyte count to lymphocyte count, and the OPNI is computed as the total number of lymphocytes plus five times the serum albumin level. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were utilized to determine the optimal cutoff values of serological indicators associated with the malignant potential and Ki-67 expression of GIST.

CT imaging acquisition and image analysis

All patients underwent abdominal plain scan and enhancement before surgery. The patients were scanned with a Philips 128 row CT scanner from the top of the diaphragm to the level of the lower edge of the pubic symphysis in a single breath-hold scan. The scanning conditions included 120kV and 300mA, with a matrix size of 512x512. Following the non-contrast scan (0s), an automatic injector was used by the examiner to inject 350 mg/ml ioversol into the patient's cubital vein at a speed of 3.0~5.0ml/s. The dosage was calculated by multiplying the patient's weight by 1.5ml/kg. Subsequently, arterial, venous, and delayed phase images were captured at 25–30 seconds, 65–70 seconds, and 110–120 seconds after contrast injection.

Consistent manual segmentation of tumor areas was conducted on venous phase CT images with a slice thickness of 0.9 mm by two experienced radiologists. The region of interest (ROI) was outlined layer by layer along the tumor contour boundary, encompassing the entire tumor volume. Two radiologists—one with 30 years of imaging experience and the other with 5 years—independently reviewed the image files and recorded CT image characteristics, including location (gastric, non-gastric), tumor diameter (cm), volume (cm3), contour (regular, irregular), growth pattern (intraluminal, extraluminal, mixed), enhancement pattern (uniform, uneven), internal components (ulceration, calcification, necrosis), and tumor blood vessels (either supplying blood to the mass or enlarged drainage blood vessels (EVFDM), or the internal blood supply of the tumor (IBSC)). The presence of ulceration was determined by the observation of focal tissue defects on the intraluminal surface of the lesion. Lesions classified as target lesions exhibited low density, CT attenuation values between 0 and 20 HU, no enhancement after reinjection of the contrast agent, uneven density, and irregular inner edges. These characteristics indicate the presence of necrosis within the tumor. Additionally, target lesions with high-density foci in unenhanced CT scans and CT attenuation values exceeding 90 HU are classified as tumor calcification. EVFDM consists of enlarged blood vessels surrounding the tumor that supply blood to the mass, and it is identified during the portal venous phase. Hypervascularity is characterized by irregular blood vessels extending from the edge to the center of the tumor, while hypovascularity is defined by the presence of blood vessels only at the tumor's periphery, as determined during the arterial phase. The tumor volume was delineated, and the maximum diameter was measured using 3D-slicer software. Additionally, the blood supply within the tumor was assessed on arterial phase images utilizing the UCLA plug-in of U-viewer software. Statistical analysis

This study involved a total of 193 patients, who were randomly assigned to a training group and a validation group in an 8:2 ratio. Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS version 26.0. Continuous variables were evaluated using independent t-tests and Mann-Whitney U tests. Normally distributed continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation (X ± S), while skewedly distributed variables are reported as median and interquartile range (IQR). Categorical data were analyzed using the chi-square test or Fisher's exact test, with results expressed as frequencies and percentages (n, %). The optimal cutoff values for continuous variables were determined through receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis and the Youden index. Interobserver agreement was assessed using the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC). Zstats v1.0 was utilized for two-way stepwise regression and P-value method stepwise regression. Using Hiplot Pro (https://hiplot.com.cn/). The LASSO regression modeling tool is used for Lasso analysis, and Hiplot Pro is a comprehensive network service for biomedical data analysis and visualization. Each regression method offers distinct advantages. Lasso regression helps mitigate the issues of model overfitting and variable collinearity to some extent. This study employed ten-fold cross-validation to minimize bias arising from data segmentation; simultaneously, by setting various λ values, the value yielding the smallest average error was selected. At this point, the corresponding characteristic variables with non-zero coefficients were considered as the model's characteristic variables. Two-way stepwise regression integrates both forward selection and backward elimination strategies, screening significant variables based on the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) and the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) to construct a regression equation with a good fit. The P-value method of stepwise regression further optimizes this approach by including variables with P<0.05, ensuring that factors with higher correlation are incorporated into the prediction model. We employed the Hosmer-Lemeshow test, along with AIC and BIC criteria, for model comparison to assess model fit. The accuracy of the prediction model was evaluated using the AUC. Consequently, the best models were selected, and nomograms, calibration curves, and clinical decision curves were generated for them.

Results

A total of 193 patients were included in this study, with an average age of 62.38 ± 10.60 years. Among these patients, 85 (44.04%) were male, 123 (63.73%) exhibited low Ki-67 expression, and 70 (36.27%) exhibited high Ki-67 expression. Additionally, 85 cases (44.04%) were classified as having low malignant potential, while 108 cases (55.06%) were classified as having high malignant potential. The patients were divided into a training group (154 cases) and a validation group (39 cases) in an 8:2 ratio. Statistical analysis indicated no significant difference between the two groups (P > 0.05). The median tumor volume was 40.23 (interquartile range: 9.47, 123.54), and the median diameter was 4.83 (interquartile range: 3.05, 7.61). Among the cases, 117 (60.62%) were identified as gastric stromal tumors, while 76 (39.38%) were classified as non-gastric stromal tumors. In terms of morphology, 87 cases (45.08%) displayed regular outlines, whereas 106 cases (54.92%) exhibited irregular outlines. In this study, 66 cases (34.20%) were classified as intraluminal type, 87 cases (45.08%) as extraluminal type, and 40 cases (20.73%) as mixed type. Regarding tumor enhancement, 80 cases (41.45%) exhibited homogeneous enhancement, while 113 cases (58.55%) displayed heterogeneous enhancement. Additionally, 34 cases (17.62%) presented with surface ulcers, another 34 cases (17.62%) showed calcification, 73 cases (37.82%) exhibited necrosis or cystic changes, 78 cases (40.41%) had abundant blood supply, and 65 cases (33.68%) demonstrated enlarged blood vessels supplying the mass. In terms of predicting Ki-67 expression and malignant potential, the cutoff values for NLR were both 3.651, while the cutoff values for MLR were both 0.248. Furthermore, the cutoff values for PLR were 279.989 and 161.660, respectively, and the cutoff values for OPNI were 47.875 and 50.425, respectively. (Supplementary Table 1).

Volume, diameter, location, contour, enhancement pattern, EVFDM, OPNI<47.875, and PLR≥279.989 are all identified as independent predictors of GIST Ki-67 expression (P<0.001). The stepwise regression model emerged as the most effective for predicting Ki-67 expression, supported by its small AIC and BIC values, high HL test P value, and the largest AUC (AUC=0.865). In the validation group, the AUC was 0.784 (95% CI: 0.631 - 0.937), with a sensitivity of 0.731 and a specificity of 0.615. The cutoff value remained at 0.437, resulting in a PPV of 0.792 and an NPV of 0.533. For the malignant potential prediction model, the training group exhibited an AUC of 0.950 (95% CI: 0.920 - 0.980), with a sensitivity of 0.956 and a specificity of 0.837. The cutoff value for this model was 0.570, which corresponded to a PPV of 0.823 and an NPV of 0.960. In the validation group, the AUC was 0.936 (95% CI: 0.867 - 1.000), with a sensitivity of 0.765 and a specificity of 0.864. The cutoff value remained at 0.570, resulting in a PPV of 0.812 and an NPV of 0.826. (Table 3)

Nomogram variable screening

This study established and selected the optimal fusion models based on the results. To facilitate usage by clinicians, a predictive model for Ki-67 expression was constructed, incorporating variables such as the diameter of the tumor, growth mode, enhancement mode, EVFDM, PLR, and OPNI. Additionally, a model was developed to predict malignant potential, which includes volume, contour, ulceration, IBSC, and OPNI (Figs. 5 and 6) . The nomogram serves as a visual representation of the model. The score for each variable corresponds to the upper axis of the graph. By aggregating the scores of each variable, the patient's total score can be calculated, which corresponds to the probability indicated on the lower axis for predicting the lesion's characteristics, including the likelihood of high malignant potential or elevated Ki-67 expression. When the total score for Ki-67 exceeds the cutoff value of 134.351, it suggests a higher likelihood of significant Ki-67 expression. Similarly, if the malignant potential score surpasses the cutoff value of 14.359, this indicates an increased likelihood of high malignant potential.

Discussion

In patients with radiographically identifiable gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GIST) that can be completely surgically resected, preoperative biopsy is deemed unnecessary . At this juncture, the development of a simple and reliable preoperative prediction model is particularly crucial. This study constructed a prediction model for GIST Ki-67 expression based diameter, growth mode, enhancement mode, EVFDM, PLR, and OPNI. Additionally, a separate prediction model for the malignant potential of GIST was developed using volume, contour, ulceration, IBSC, and OPNI. Both models demonstrate good discrimination, accuracy, and clinical applicability.

Imaging characteristics

First, tumor size is a critical factor in the grading of gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GIST) [16]. The results of this study indicate that diameter serves as an independent risk factor for Ki-67 expression, while volume is an independent risk factor for malignant potential. This discrepancy may arise from the fact that tumor volume accounts for the three-dimensional characteristics of the tumor. In comparison to diameter, volume provides a more accurate representation of tumor size. Previous studies have demonstrated that tumor volume is a significant predictor of GIST recurrence. Furthermore, for tumors with irregular shapes, the layer-by-layer depiction method is deemed more reliable for calculating volume than the ellipsoid volume formula [17, 18]. Due to the irregular shape and unidirectional growth of lesions exhibiting a high proliferation rate, tumor diameter becomes a factor more closely associated with Ki-67 expression. Additionally, the study conducted by Chen et al. indicated that, compared to tumor volume, the correlation between diameter and Ki-67 expression is stronger [19].

Second, tumor blood vessels play a crucial role in cancer progression by supplying essential oxygen and nutrients for tumor growth, as well as serving as pathways for tumor blood or lymphatic metastasis [4]. Tumor blood vessels comprise intratumoral blood vessels and peritumoral blood-supplying vessels (EVFDM), with intratumoral blood vessels evaluated using IBSC. The results of this study indicate that EVFDM is identified as an independent risk factor for Ki-67 expression, while IBSC is an independent risk factor for malignant potential. This discrepancy may stem from several factors. Firstly, the invasiveness of tumors of varying grades is primarily determined by the formation of new blood vessels; a greater abundance of new blood vessels within the tumor correlates with increased invasiveness [20].

Secondly, Ultrasound imaging indicates that GIST exhibiting regular blood flow from the periphery to the center tend to have a higher malignant potential [21, 22]. However, there has been limited research utilizing CT to study these tumors; thus, this study aims to conduct a preliminary investigation based on the aforementioned criteria. Although CT scanning can detect subcentimeter-level hypervascular lesions, which are more pronounced during the arterial phase [23], enhanced CT still has certain limitations in assessing intratumoral blood vessels in GIST. It is important to note that the sensitivity of CT is lower than that of ultrasound, particularly for GIST measuring less than 3 cm [22]. Furthermore, when the tumor's blood supply is inadequate, significant necrosis may develop within the tumor, which can obstruct the visualization of new blood vessels in certain GISTs that exhibit strong proliferative capabilities. Despite these limitations, it is undeniable that high expression of Ki-67 correlates with increased microvessel density and tumor neovascularization [5]. Consequently, given that endoscopic ultrasonography is not the preferred method for evaluating GIST, the observation of blood vessels flowing from the periphery to the center of the tumor remains clinically significant when only enhanced CT is available for assessment.

In addition to tumor size and tumor blood vessels, other imaging characteristics of tumors play a crucial role in predicting malignant potential and Ki-67 expression. The results show that, first, there is a correlation between tumor location and Ki-67 expression, as well as malignant potential, with intestinal stromal tumors exhibiting more aggressive behavior than gastrointestinal stromal tumors located in the stomach. This variance in biological behavior is supported by Yin et al.'s study, which identified specific isoforms of KIT/PDGFRA wild type present in non-gastric locations [24]. Furthermore, Zhang et al. [11] found that intestinal GISTs demonstrate a stronger proliferation ability compared to gastric GISTs. Second, a correlation exists between irregular contours and Ki-67 expression, as well as malignant potential. The differing proliferation rates of tumors in various locations contribute to irregular tumor shapes, which subsequently influence the biological behavior of GISTs [19]. O'Neill et al. highlighted that irregular contours serve as an independent predictor of metastasis in patients with GIST [25]. Third, the growth pattern is correlated with malignant potential and Ki-67 expression, with extraluminal and mixed-type GISTs exhibiting higher proliferative capacity and malignant potential. In contrast, the growth of intraluminal GISTs is often restricted by the intestinal tube or gastric lumen. This relationship was also confirmed in the study by Zhu et al. [13]. Fourth, a correlation exists between the enhancement pattern and malignant potential. The enhancement pattern serves as an independent risk factor for Ki-67 expression; some studies indicate that the venous phase enhancement ratio is also an independent risk factor for malignant potential [4]. Additionally, there is a correlation between the enhancement pattern and Ki-67 expression in small intestinal GISTs, with the degree of enhancement being an independent risk factor for Ki-67 expression [13]. However, there are currently no clear clinical standards for measuring the enhancement ratio and enhancement degree, leading this study to utilize the more commonly employed enhancement modes. Finally, ulceration and necrosis are both correlated with Ki-67 expression and malignant potential, with previous studies yielding similar findings [19, 4], as necrosis can promote tumor growth.

Serological indicators

Inflammation plays a crucial role in various stages of tumor development [26], providing valuable complementary information for tumor assessment. The results of this study indicate that high NLR, MLR, and PLR are statistically significant in relation to low Ki-67 expression and malignant potential. However, low OPNI emerges as an independent risk factor, potentially due to its incorporation of both nutritional and immune factors, low albumin and low lymphocyte counts can create a favorable environment for tumor growth and progression. Furthermore, low OPNI has been utilized in the recurrence-free survival (RFS) assessment of gastrointestinal stromal tumor (GIST) patients [27, 28]. OPNI is regarded as an independent prognostic indicator for GIST patients and is considered superior to NLR and PLR [29]. Additionally, it has been observed that high PLR is also associated with Ki-67 expression. PLR is linked to platelet counts. Platelets not only promote tumor growth but also facilitate the infiltration of other immune cells, such as neutrophils and lymphocytes, into tumor tissue, thereby triggering further inflammatory progression [30]. Monocytes can differentiate into macrophages, which promote the extravasation, survival, and metastasis of cancer cells. Lymphocytes serve as the primary component of immune defense against malignant tumors, capable of inducing cell death and inhibiting tumor cell proliferation and migration [31, 32]. During the inflammatory process, hematopoietic stem cells in the bone marrow detect danger signals and induce bone marrow-biased differentiation, resulting in an increased output of myeloid cells. This phenomenon, known as stress-induced myelopoiesis [33, 34], is reflected in an elevation of the NLR. These mechanisms elucidate the biological correlation between NLR, MLR, PLR and the high malignant potential, as well as Ki-67 expression in GIST.

Model comparison

In terms of statistical analysis and model construction, this study diverges from previous research by employing three regression methods to identify factors associated with high Ki-67 expression and elevated malignant potential. The objective is to uncover higher relevant influencing factors and to develop a prediction model characterized by a lower fitting degree and higher predictive efficiency. Li et al. [35] constructed an imaging model utilizing tumor size, location, EVFDM, and mesenteric fat infiltration to predict the malignant potential of gastric GIST. The area under the curve (AUC) for the training and validation groups were 0.946 (95% CI 0.899–0.975) and 0.952 (95% CI 0.913–0.977), respectively. However, the use of a 5 cm cut-off point for tumor size has sparked some controversy in this study [35, 36], primarily due to discrepancies between imaging measurements and postoperative pathological assessments, which yield different maximum diameter results. Enhanced CT and endoscopic ultrasound techniques tend to underestimate GIST size to some extent [37, 38]. Consequently, employing the pathological threshold of 5 cm as the cut-off for imaging data warrants further scrutiny. To enhance the accuracy of tumor size assessment, this study directly utilized the maximum diameter measured via imaging for statistical analysis and implemented a nomogram scoring system to facilitate individualized lesion assessment. Liu et al. [15] were the first to combine imaging characteristics with MLR to predict the malignant potential of gastric gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GISTs). The AUC values for the training and validation groups were 0.932 (95% CI: 0.890–0.974) and 0.922 (95% CI: 0.868–0.977), respectively. Building on this foundation, our study also includes intestinal GISTs, thereby enhancing the model's applicability. The AUCs for the training and validation groups in this context are 0.950 (95% CI: 0.920–0.980) and 0.936 (95% CI: 0.867–1.000), respectively. Zhang et al. [11] utilized radiomics features in conjunction with tumor size to develop a prediction model for Ki-67 expression. Despite conducting multi-center validation, the prediction efficiency was found to be low. The AUC for the training group, internal validation group, and external validation group were 0.787 (95% CI 0.632–0.801), 0.765 (95% CI 0.683–0.847), and 0.754 (95% CI 0.666–0.842), respectively. Furthermore, this study incorporated various factors, including the diameter, growth pattern, enhance pattern, EVFDM, PLR, and OPNI, to construct a predictive model for Ki-67 expression, which demonstrated strong predictive performance. The AUC for the training group and validation group were 0.865 (95% CI 0.807–0.922) and 0.784 (95% CI 0.631–0.937), respectively. Although the AUC of the two models constructed in this study is relatively high, there are two issues that warrant discussion. First, there are minor differences in AUC, sensitivity, PPV, and NPV between the training group and the validation group (Table 3), which could arise from data unevenness or model overfitting. However, the differences are not substantial. Comparison of the baseline data between the training and validation groups (Supplementary Table 1) indicates that the P >0.05, suggesting that the data distributions of the two groups are balanced. Furthermore, in Tables 1 and 2, the P values of the HL test for the model are >0.05, indicating low model fit. When selecting the optimal model, a prediction model with a lower fit but a higher AUC was chosen. Given that the sample size of the validation group is smaller than that of the training group, it is inevitable that there will be some differences in AUC values between the two groups due to variations in data distribution and quality. Additionally, the specificity of the Ki-67 expression prediction model in both the training and validation groups is slightly lower, which may contribute to an increase in false positive cases. Similarly, the sensitivity of the malignant potential prediction model in the validation group is marginally lower, potentially leading to an increase in false negative cases. Therefore, it is recommended that doctors should utilize both models concurrently to mitigate the limitations inherent in each model.

Calibration curve and clinical decision curve

The calibration curves for the training group and verification group of the two most effective prediction models are presented in Figs. 7A, B and 8A, B, respectively. The proximity of these curves to the standard line suggests a strong correlation between the predicted outcomes and the actual observed results. This consistency further indicates that the prediction models are reliable and suitable for clinical preoperative diagnosis. Additionally, we plotted DCA curves (Figs. 7C, D and 8C, D) to demonstrate the net clinical benefit derived from using this model for prediction. The horizontal axis represents the risk threshold, while the vertical axis indicates the net benefit of the selected intervention program at this threshold. The figures reveal that the majority of the decision curves for both models are positioned above the complete intervention curve and the complete non-intervention line, suggesting that this model can effectively guide clinical decision-making in most scenarios and provide significant benefits. However, in Fig. 7C, a small to moderate portion of the curve falls below the horizontal line, indicating that within this specific risk threshold range, utilizing this prediction model for decision-making yields a lower net benefit than opting for no intervention at all. In such cases, additional assessments should be considered. Furthermore, Figs. 7C, D and 8C, D demonstrate that when the risk threshold is low, the two curves intersect, indicating that the net benefits of employing the model for decision-making are equivalent at that point.

This study has yielded significant results regarding the malignant potential and Ki-67 expression of GIST; however, it also presents several limitations. Firstly, the research data was sourced from the largest diagnostic and treatment center in the region, which lends it a degree of representativeness and generalizability within that context. Nonetheless, as a single-center retrospective study, its applicability must be evaluated with greater rigor and caution , to enhance the generalizability and validation of the model, prospective studies across multiple provinces and countries are warranted. Secondly, this study employed CE-CT for a preliminary evaluation of intratumoral blood supply. Currently, there remains a lack of more precise quantitative analysis of the intratumoral blood vessels.

Conclusion

Diameter, growth pattern, enhancement pattern, EVFDM, PLR, and OPNI are independent risk factors for GISTs with high Ki-67 expression. Additionally, volume, contour, ulceration, IBSC, and OPNI serve as independent risk factors for GISTs with high malignant potential. The preoperative models developed using CT images can predict the malignant potential and Ki-67 expression status of GISTs to a certain extent. When combined with serological indicators, these models' predictive performance can be further enhanced.

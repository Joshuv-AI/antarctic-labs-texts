Prednisone, not vamorolone, suppresses novel serum bone and cartilage biomarkers associated with growth failure in children with Duchenne muscular dystrophy
Authors: Rebecca A Tobin, Utkarsh J Dang, Laura Hagerty, Leanne M Ward, Raoul Rooman, Karl Heinz Konopka, Paula R Clemens, Michela Guglieri, Jesse Damsker, Michael Ziemba, Yetrib Hathout, Eric P Hoffman
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCorticosteroids are among the most highly prescribed drugs. Chronic treatment with corticosteroids causes osteopenia, as well as growth failure in children. Our goal was to identify serum biomarkers of prednisone-related perturbations of bone and/or cartilage metabolism associated with growth failure in children. We utilized longitudinal serum samples from a double-blind, placebo- and prednisone-controlled trial of vamorolone in 4 to < 7 years children with Duchenne muscular dystrophy assayed by proteomic methods. Vamorolone is a recently approved bone-sparing corticosteroid that has been shown to have similar efficacy as prednisone but does not cause growth failure. The study included a 24-week Period 1 (placebo, prednisone [0.75 mg/kg/day], vamorolone [6 mg/kg/day]), and a 24-week Period 2 where placebo and vamorolone groups crossed over to vamorolone. Clinical laboratory measures of ALP, osteocalcin, P1NP, and CTX1 showed significant reductions with prednisone, but were unchanged by vamorolone treatment or placebo. Proteomics studies of serum proteins reduced by prednisone, but not vamorolone or placebo, identified 10 proteins where mutations in the corresponding genes are known to cause genetic disorders of cartilage and/or bone (collagens [COL2A1, Chondrocalcin, COL6A1, COL9A1, COL10A1, COL11A2], aggrecan core protein [ACAN], biglycan [BGN], osteopetrosis-associated transmembrane protein 1 [OSTM1], and noggin [NOG]). On transitioning from prednisone to vamorolone, all 10 biomarkers returned to baseline levels. Non-human (animal) studies have shown that traditional corticosteroids cause apoptosis of terminal hypertrophic chondrocytes associated with growth failure. Our clinical trial studies suggest that the 10 novel, corticosteroid-related biomarkers likely reflect the induction of apoptosis in terminal hypertrophic chondrocytes and osteoblasts by traditional corticosteroids. Introduction

Bone modeling and remodeling during childhood includes multiple processes, such as long bone growth as the result of endochondral ossification (growth plate chondrocyte proliferation, chondrocyte hypertrophy and replacement of chondrocytes by osteoblasts), bone remodeling caused by microtrauma and changing stress lines because of growth, and articular cartilage metabolism. Use of corticosteroids, such as prednisone, is strongly associated with deleterious effects on these processes1–3. Corticosteroid-related bone morbidities include osteoporosis and increased fracture risk in adults and stunting of growth in children4. Bone mineral density (BMD) measured by DXA is commonly utilized to measure bone quality, and chronic treatment with corticosteroids reduces BMD in adults5. However, corticosteroid-related increased risk of fractures is considerably higher than the corresponding changes in BMD, and, further, BMD changes occur only over extended time periods6. As such, BMD as a biomarker for bone quality and fragility is considered relatively specific but only partly sensitive. In children, interpretation of BMD and changes in BMD can be more challenging due to concomitant bone growth, non-ossified regions of actively growing bones, and the rapid remodeling of bone during development.

Serum bone turnover markers are often used as a means of assessing bone metabolism because they can be acutely responsive (within hours) to drugs affecting bone. In adults, these include biomarkers of bone ossification (bone alkaline phosphatase [bALP] and procollagen type I N propeptide [P1NP]), osteocalcin (OC) and biomarkers of bone resorption (cross-linked C-telopeptides of type I collagen [CTX1 or bCTx]). Osteocalcin, an osteoblast marker, is among the most abundant proteins of the body. It is secreted by osteoblasts and binds to hydroxyapatite during the bone formation phase of bone modeling. In adults with osteoporosis, bone is poorly calcified, with lower hydroxyapatite crystal formation and consequent poor binding of osteocalcin, where it is released into the blood. As such, high serum levels of osteocalcin are associated with poor bone quality or deleterious changes in bone quality7.

In children, bone metabolism is high because bones are actively growing by the process of endochondral ossification. Children have increased serum levels of all bone biomarkers compared to adults, with age-related decreases associated with skeletal maturation8. In this pediatric context, high osteocalcin levels are generally a positive indicator of active bone modeling and longitudinal growth.

With corticosteroid treatment, all bone modeling and remodeling (both formation and resorption) is impaired, and this leads to reductions in activity of both osteoblasts and osteoclasts, with corresponding reductions in serum osteocalcin, ALP, CTX1 and P1NP. Indeed, a single dose of prednisone given to adult volunteers reduces serum osteocalcin, P1NP and CTX1 within a few hours9. Similarly, daily treatment with prednisone significantly reduces serum ALP within 1 week in adult volunteers10,11. In a study of prednisone treatment of 4 to 7 yr children with Duchenne muscular dystrophy, osteocalcin, P1NP and CTX1 were significantly reduced by drug treatment over 24 weeks12, This same study showed significant dose-dependent stunting of growth associated with prednisone treatment plus rapid and sustained reductions in serum bone biomarkers, as has been reported by other studies13–15.

In contrast, vamorolone is a dissociative corticosteroid that has been shown not to reduce bone biomarkers neither in adult volunteers16, nor in children with DMD17,18. Head-to-head double-blind comparison of vamorolone to prednisone treatment in 4 to < 7 yr boys with Duchenne muscular dystrophy showed prednisone to stunt growth and reduce serum bone turnover markers, whereas vamorolone did not12. Moreover, double-blind cross-over from prednisone to vamorolone showed rapid restoration of bone biomarkers and growth trajectories19. The human data is consistent with comparisons of vamorolone and traditional corticosteroids in mouse models of multiple disease states, where corticosteroids had deleterious effects on bone quality and growth, but vamorolone did not20–22. Recent studies in children with DMD have shown that deflazacort, an alternative to prednisone in DMD treatment, stunts growth even more than prednisone23.

Vamorolone shows three key structure/activity relationships that differentiate it from other members of the corticosteroid class. First, vamorolone is not a substrate for the two 11β hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase enzymes (HSD11B1, HSD11B2) that modulate the activity by converting inactive pro-drug to active drug (e.g. prednisone to prednisolone), and inactive cortisone to the active hormone cortisol. Vamorolone is not metabolized by HSD11B1 and HSD11B220 which may contribute to its lack of effect on bone metabolism.

Second, vamorolone lacks one of the corticosteroid binding sites with the glucocorticoid receptor, and this in turn changes obligatory co-factor binding patterns, with less affinity for transactivators and greater affinity for transrepressors24. Third, vamorolone is a potent mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist similar to eplerenone and spironolactone25,26. This mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist property distinguishes vamorolone from prednisone,the latter typically exhibits agonistic activity that adds to safety concerns such as fluid retention and high blood pressure.

In this current study, we hypothesized that other serum bone biomarkers may also be differentially regulated by prednisone/prednisolone vs. vamorolone. We therefore looked at serum proteins that showed prednisone-specific changes (not seen with placebo or vamorolone) that were directly involved in bone development or metabolism. We used the highly sensitive, specific, and reproducible Somalogic SomaScan proteomics technology to conduct initial (1,500 candidate serum proteins assayed) and follow-up (5,289 serum proteins assayed) biomarker discovery studies in DMD participants in the pivotal VBP15-004 clinical trial (prednisone vs. placebo vs. vamorolone). The approach we utilized was to identify prednisone-responsive bone biomarkers not affected by vamorolone or placebo during a parallel treatment period, with a post-crossover period serving as validation (e.g. recovery of the biomarkers upon cross-over from prednisone to vamorolone). This evaluation is beyond typically-used serum bone turnover markers to other bone biomarkers that are indicative of bone and growth plate activity. Indeed, while traditional bone turnover biomarkers reflect osteoblast/osteoclast activity, they may not fully capture the specific perturbations in the growth plate (chondrocytes) that drive corticosteroid-induced growth failure. This current proteomics discovery study addresses a gap in biomarkers that might reflect active chondrocyte involvement in growth.

Methods

Trial design and participants

The VBP15-004 clinical trial received ethical approval from each of the 33 participating academic clinical centers in 11 countries as described in the primary publications of the clinical trial12,19. The trial, conducted from June 29, 2018, to February 24, 2021, was approved by the competent ethics committee at each institution and was conducted in accordance with the International Conference on Harmonisation guidelines for Good Clinical Practice and the World Medical Association Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent from parents and assent (children, trial participants) was obtained as required by each participating trial recruitment center. All children were required to assent, unless the investigator determined that the capability of the child is so limited that the child could not reasonably be consulted. If assent was obtained, the person obtaining assent documented assent on the consent form.

Serum samples were from the VBP15-004 randomized, double-blind, placebo- and prednisone-controlled study of vamorolone in DMD (NCT03439670) carried out at 33 sites in 11 countries between June 2018 and August 2021. Inclusion criteria were steroid-naïve participants with Duchenne muscular dystrophy, 4 to < 7 years at screening, and able to perform time to stand from supine test in less than 10 s. The protocol and primary analysis have been previously published, including the inclusion and exclusion criteria and schedule of study activities12,19. The trial protocol and statistical analysis plan are accessible at clinicaltrials.gov (NCT03439670. The biomarker studies reported here were a post-hoc exploratory analysis. Randomization was done by age within the 4 to < 7-year age inclusion criteria in two subgroups (4 to < 6 yrs; ≥ 6- < 7 yrs. The observed mean ± SD age in the modified intention-to-treat population at baseline were placebo 5.4 ± 0.8 yrs, prednisone 5.5 ± 0.9 yrs, and vamorolone 6 mg/kg/day 5.4 ± 0.9 yrs.

The trial had two sequential 24-wk treatment periods, where the blind was maintained over both periods. In Period 1, participants were randomized to two vamorolone dose groups (2 mg/kg/d; 6 mg/kg/d), prednisone (0.75 mg/kg/d) or placebo (24-wk treatment period). In Period 2, placebo and prednisone groups crossed over to vamorolone (either 2 mg/kg/d or 6 mg/kg/d), whereas the participants on vamorolone in Period 1 continued at the same dose for Period 2 for an additional 24-wk treatment period (Fig. 1). For the crossover groups, a 4-week washout period followed Period 1 prior to cross-over to vamorolone in Period 2. The prednisone-treated group had a 4-wk taper period before transitioning to vamorolone at 28-wk.

Design of the VBP15-004 clinical study. Shown is the study design including cross-over and prednisone 4-week taper period, and the timepoints for samples collection. BTM – bone turnover markers (ALP, CTX1, P1NP, osteocalcin).

Serum samples were collected for pharmacodynamic biomarker studies at baseline (prior to first dose), and after treatment (12, 24, 28, 40, and 48 wk) (Fig. 1). Serum bone turnover markers osteocalcin, procollagen 1 intact N-terminal pro-peptide (P1NP), and type 1 collagen cross-linked C-telopeptide (CTX1) were measured by the central laboratory using automated, validated immunoassays. The bone turnover marker alkaline phosphatase (ALP) was measured by the central laboratory as part of a standard clinical chemistry panel. All samples were collected after the subject had fasted for ≥ 6 h and prior to administration of the daily dose of study medication at dosing visits.

Healthy controls were from the CINRG DNHS study, as recently reported27.

Serum proteomics

Serum proteomics assays were done using Somalogic SomaScan® 7 K v4.1 aptamer assay in a CLIA accredited lab (SomaLogics, Boulder, CO). SomaScan® uses aptamer technology to measure relative levels of protein analytes across a broad concentration range by applying three serial dilutions, enabling accurate relative measurement of both highly abundant proteins (e.g. albumin) and low abundance proteins (such as interleukins). Data obtained for analysis was standardized (normalized and calibrated) following a custom assay-specific pipeline by Somalogic.

Vamorolone 6 mg/kg/d is the clinically recommended dose12,19. For this reason (as well as financial constraints), we limited proteomics assays to samples from participants that were treated with vamorolone 6 mg/kg/d in Period 1, and/or Period 2, and did not study the lower 2 mg/kg/day vamorolone dose group. All analyses were done as longitudinal analyses where each trial participant was studied relative to their individual baseline measures (i.e., change from baseline) for each biomarker. There was no differential weighting of different participants in these analyses by whether they crossed over to vamorolone or were on vamorolone throughout.

Statistical analysis

Unstructured covariance mixed models for repeated measures (MMRMs) were used to model the longitudinal trajectories of three bone/growth related biomarkers pre-specified to be measured during the VBP15-004 trial by the central clinical laboratory: osteocalcin, P1NP, and CTX1. For this analysis, measurements on these central lab biomarkers on all treatment groups including vamorolone 2.0 mg/kg/day and crossovers were included, as well as additional timepoints at weeks 28 and 40. Here, an additional, commonly used, bone biomarker was added to the analyses: ALP.

The analysis of proteomic data using SomaScan® 7 K v4.1 aptamer assay (7,289 protein aptamers) was carried out in two subsets of biomarkers (Stage 1; Stage 2). The Stage 1 subset was 1,500 protein candidates that were pre-specified, and multiple testing corrections applied. The Stage 1 pre-specified candidate list included aptamer probes for proteins previously identified as differentially expressed in DMD28–31, as well as candidate proteins in biochemical pathways related to inflammation, muscle damage, and fibrosis. Stage 2 proteins were the remaining 5,789 targets on the SomaScan 7 K v4.1 panel. Multiple testing corrections were also applied to Stage 2 data.

For each SomaScan protein, we used unstructured MMRMs, with treatment-responsive markers identified using a screening approach. We modeled the change in log2 transformed measurements from baseline as a function of four timepoints (baseline, 12, 24 wk, 48 wk) and treatment group, controlling for baseline age and baseline protein concentration, and an interaction between treatment group and timepoint. The Kenward-Roger adjustment was used. To define drug (vamorolone and/or prednisone) responsive pharmacodynamic proteins, screening was based on a joint test of three contrasts: a) prednisone vs placebo, b) vamorolone vs placebo, and c) vamorolone vs prednisone, each using a combined week 12 and week 24 mean. Hence, biomarkers were considered as treatment responsive candidates if they were able to significantly distinguish between any of the three treatment groups during Period 1. False discovery rate (FDR) correction was applied to mitigate multiple testing concerns (alpha = 0.05). Sensitivity analysis removing possible outliers were also conducted to evaluate robustness of findings. Fold-changes were calculated both between group in Period 1 (using above mentioned combined week 12/24 contrasts to assess acute change), and within crossover groups in Period 2 (week 48 change vs week 24 change), reflecting tapering for 4 weeks and treatment with vamorolone for 20 weeks. We also considered whether at baseline, these proteins were known to be differentially abundant in serum in DMD compared to healthy controls to better understand whether prednisone treatment exacerbated existing dysregulation.

Serum proteins that were differentially regulated by prednisone and vamorolone in Period 1 were then graphed individually in both Period 1 and Period 2 (after cross-over of placebo and prednisone to vamorolone; 48-wk time point). Period 1-based between-group fold changes were compared to Period 2 within-group fold changes to evaluate directionality and magnitude assessing whether the fold change comparisons were between 80 to 125% in the vein of equivalence testing. Spearman correlations were used to study associations. Heatmaps and hierarchical clustering were obtained using the intra-subject change from the average of 12-wk and 24-wk changes for central lab markers (to cut down on missing samples at wk 24), and 24-wk change for SomaScan biomarkers. Hierarchical clustering included the intra-subject change in height Z score over the initial Period 1 treatment period.

If biomarkers showed multiple aptamers in the SomaScan data, we selected the aptamer probe based on intra-plate CV, inter-plate CV, and correlation across batches.

Results

Longitudinal modeling of osteocalcin, P1NP, CTX1, and ALP bone turnover markers

Of the 121 boys randomized in VBP15-004, 102 had longitudinal serum samples (baseline and at least one other timepoint) measured at the central laboratory. Three pre-specified bone biomarkers (osteocalcin, P1NP, CTX1) were measured at baseline, 12, 24, 28, 40, and 48 wk by standard clinical laboratory assays (Fig. 1). We modeled change in log2 transformed levels from baseline for consistency with proteomic discovery analyses. Vamorolone and placebo groups did not show significant treatment-related change of osteocalcin, P1NP and CTX1 biomarkers, whereas treatment with prednisone led to acute depressions (between 24 and 37%) of serum levels of all three biomarkers (Fig. 2; Table 1; all p < 0.001 for prednisone vs. placebo). The prednisone-associated depressed levels of these biomarkers in Period 1 were then restored back to baseline levels after the 4-wk tapering period, and levels remained similar to baseline after crossover to vamorolone in Period 2 (Fig. 2). This data shows that 4 weeks taper/washout of prednisone is sufficient to return traditional bone biomarkers to baseline levels. In participants switching from placebo (Period 1) to vamorolone (Period 2), serum biomarker concentrations did not significantly change.

ALP data were not previously reported. Serum total alkaline phosphatase activity was analyzed using baseline, 12, 24, 28, 40, and 48 wk by standard clinical laboratory assay (Fig. 2). The placebo group did not show significant treatment-related changes from baseline in ALP, while the vamorolone group showed a slight and statistically non-significant reduction during the first six months of treatment (Table 1). In contrast, treatment with prednisone led to marked depression of ALP (by about 39%) throughout Period 1. After the 4 wk tapering period and crossover to vamorolone, serum ALP then returned to and remained at levels similar to baseline throughout Period 2, rising by 39% in Period 2 (Fig. 2). Finally, note that modeling demonstrated that within the 4 weeks in which patients stopped taking prednisone and completed a washout period (Week 24—> Week 28), all central biomarkers recovered to close to baseline levels (Fig. 2).

Identification of prednisone-specific bone and cartilage biomarkers

Three treatment groups were studied for serum proteomics: vamorolone 6 mg/kg/d [Period 1 and Period 2] [n = 26 participants]; placebo [Period 1] crossing over to vamorolone [Period 2] [n = 11 participants]; prednisone [Period 1] crossing over to vamorolone [Period 2] [n = 13 participants]. Proteomics serum samples for proteomic studies were from baseline, 12-wk, 24-wk (Period 1), and 48-wk (Period 2) (Fig. 1). Samples were tested for 7,289 proteins using SomaScan aptamer panels. A mixed model for repeated measures (MMRM) was used to identify biomarkers that were prednisone-responsive, but not responsive to vamorolone or placebo in Period 1. A joint test consisting of all contrasts between treatment groups in Period 1 using a combined 12 wk and 24 wk mean was used to identify treatment-responsive biomarkers. In the candidate protein data set (Stage 1; n = 1,500), 13 (0.9%) proteins were increased by prednisone, and 40 (2.6%) proteins were significantly depressed by prednisone, but were not affected by placebo or vamorolone (Supplemental Table 1). The Stage 2 biomarkers (5,789 protein aptamers) were analyzed separately, with data adjusted for multiple testing, and prednisone-specific protein changes identified. Stage 2 showed 14 biomarkers elevated (0.2%), and 35 depressed (0.6%) by prednisone only (Supplemental Table 1).

We queried the 102 prednisone-specific (differentially regulated vs. vamorolone) biomarkers for genes involved in disorders of bone or cartilage growth or metabolism through search of www.OMIM.org. Ten of the serum proteins significantly decreased by prednisone only (up to a maximum of 59% reduction compared to placebo), and one increased by prednisone only, were protein products of genes that showed well-characterized genetic disorders of bone and cartilage development and metabolism (Fig. 3, Table 2). One serum protein COL2A1,was picked up by 2 different aptamer probes that were prednisone-specific:(C-terminal propeptide [chondrocalcin], and a more N-terminal probe [COL2A1]). All ten serum targets showed a reversal of prednisone-related decreases in serum to placebo levels (Period 1) when participants crossed over to vamorolone (Period 2) (Table 2, Fig. 3). COL10A1, COL11A2, and ACAN were reduced in DMD (due to DMD pathophysiology) compared to healthy unaffected controls, but each fell significantly further after treatment with prednisone (Table 2).

Discussion

In adults, corticosteroid treatment leads to osteopenia and increased risk of fractures (osteoporosis), and serum bone turnover markers are typically used to monitor effects of corticosteroids on osteoclasts and osteoblasts (osteocalcin, P1NP, CTX1)32. In children, traditional corticosteroids such as prednisone lead to a similar increased risk for fractures, as well as deceleration of growth (both in a dose-dependent fashion)33,34. The elongation of bones in children occurs by endochondral ossification as the cartilaginous growth plates mature to form trabeculae of the distal metaphysis (i.e. new bone is formed from a cartilaginous template). At the end of puberty, the cartilaginous growth plate disappears when it is fully ossified and growth ceases. The hypertrophic chondrocytes in the growth plate articular cartilage are positive for Collagen type 10 (COL10A1), a marker for hypertrophic chondrocytes that are normally only present in the growth plate, and in children is a specific marker for growth plate activity35. Coghlan et al.36 assayed the trimeric noncollagenous 1 (NC1) domain of type X collagen vs. growth velocities in 129 normally growing children and found a positive correlation of serum levels of NC1 with growth velocity. The authors hypothesized COL10A1 testing might be employed for monitoring growth in infants and children with genetic and acquired growth disorders, and might also monitor interventions that seek to improve growth velocity.

For example, clinical trials of drug-related improvement in growth velocity among children with achondroplasia showed that both infigratinib37, and vasoritide38 were associated with drug-related increases in COL10A1 that correlated with improvements in annualized growth velocity. Here, we extend these findings to show prednisone but not vamorolone reduces serum COL10A1 in Duchenne muscular dystrophy (Fig. 3). In our cohort, COL10A1 was depressed in DMD compared to healthy controls before treatment (Table 2), consistent with the pre-GC shorter stature that is characteristic of this condition relative to typically-growing males. COL10A1 was also reduced after treatment, with the change in COL10A1 demonstrating a marginal trend toward significance (r = 0.29; p = 0.056) with change in height z-score over 24 weeks. There are clear physiological differences between drug-induced growth suppression in DMD and the primary genetic growth plate defects in achondroplasia, and longer-term studies are needed to confirm these associations.

Vamorolone is a novel, growth-sparing corticosteroid that is approved for use in Duchenne muscular dystrophy (2-yr and older in US; 4-yr and older EU, UK, China, Hong Kong). Young (4- to 7-yr) participants in two clinical trials of vamorolone showed normal growth velocities compared to corticosteroid-treated children12,19,39. In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study including both vamorolone and prednisone arms, prednisone treatment resulted in serum bone turnover marker declines (including osteocalcin, P1NP, CTX1) and deceleration of linear growth velocity, whereas vamorolone did not12. Moreover, double-blind cross-over of prednisone to vamorolone normalized bone biomarkers and reversed the growth deceleration19. Here, we added ALP to this analysis, showing that ALP levels closely paralleled the other traditional bone biomarkers with prednisone-specific treatment-related reductions, where vamorolone cross-over restored ALP to levels similar to placebo (Fig. 2).

We hypothesized that additional bone- and growth-related serum biomarkers could be identified by defining treatment-related changes with prednisone, that were not shared by placebo or vamorolone treatment. Using serial measures over a 24-wk treatment period, and correlation for multiple testing, we identified 102 biomarkers that were responsive to prednisone but not vamorolone. Of these, 27 showed prednisone-related increases, and 75 prednisone-related decreases. Ten of these proteins corresponded to genetic disorders of cartilage, bone, and growth (Table 2). Nine showed declines of serum levels with prednisone but not with vamorolone (COL2A1 [2 aptamers], COL10A1, COL6A1, COL9A1, COL11A2, ACAN, BGN, NOG, OSTM1), and one was increased by prednisone but not by vamorolone (SPARC). However, note that SPARC has 2 SomaScan aptamers, only 1 of which showed a slight prednisone-associated increase (by about 7%), but this was not validated using Period 2 data.

In adults, prednisone halts bone remodeling and leads to thinning of bones (osteopenia and bone fragility/osteoporosis). In children, corticosteroids also have a deleterious effect on the growth plate leading to deceleration of growth velocity. We carried out hierarchical clustering of change in height Z-scores, traditional bone turnover markers, and the newly identified prednisone-responsive biomarkers (Fig. 4). While the time period over which growth was observed (24-wks) was quite short, this analysis confirmed the differentiation of the prednisone-treated participants, from the vamorolone-treated and placebo participants. This analysis showed that change in height Z-scores in all trial participants was best correlated with ALP, confirmed by a significant Spearman correlation of 0.49 using all datapoints; this rises to 0.78 if using only the prednisone subgroup (Fig. 4). Alkaline phosphatase is present in hypertrophic chondrocytes, the same cells that also produce Col10A140. The traditional bone turnover markers (osteocalcin, P1NP, CTX1) formed a distinct ‘Bone turnover’ cluster.

Compared to the other biomarkers, NOG and OSTM1 showed more muted response to prednisone treatment in Period 1 and recovery on vamorolone in Period 2 (Table 2). NOG (noggin) is an antagonist of bone morphogenic protein (BMP) signaling, where it binds to BMP ligands and prevents association with receptors at the cell surface. NOG is expressed in condensing cartilage and immature chondrocytes. Loss of function of noggin leads to overgrowth of growth plates, and loss of positional cues in joint development. NOG has not previously been reported as corticosteroid-responsive. Prednisone-related negative effects on chondrocytes likely leads to reduced NOG secretion, and low levels in serum.

OSTM1 (Osteopetrosis-associated transmembrane 1) deficiency causes a severe sclerosing bone dysplasia that is fetal or early childhood lethal, with downstream syndromic features due to loss of bone modeling and remodeling (bone marrow failure, thromocytopenia, optic atrophy, microcephaly). While most forms of osteopetrosis are due to a loss of functional osteoclasts, deficiency of OSTM1 appears to be loss of migration of macrophages and differentiation into osteoclasts. Hematopoietic stem cell transplantation is not able to rescue OSTM1 deficiency, as it does with other forms of osteopetrosis41. Corticosteroids are known to inhibit cell migration from the circulation to peripheral tissues, and corticosteroid-related decreases in serum OSTM1 may play a role in this reduction of macrophage migration and differentiation to osteoclasts (contributing to the reduction in serum resorption markers on classic corticosteroids).

Aggrecan (ACAN) and biglycan (BGN) are major proteoglycan components of the extracellular matrix of articular cartilage, and have roles in creating the hydrated gel matrix and imparting strength to the gel42,43. Increased serum ACAN is a biomarker for acute aortic dissection44, whereas increased serum biglycan is associated with inflammatory states45. It is important to note that the response of biglycan and other new biomarkers to prednisone but not vamorolone is not secondary to the anti-inflammatory effects of prednisone, as vamorolone shares these anti-inflammatory effects but does not reduce the serum biomarkers. Thus, the prednisone-associated decreases in both proteins may reflect reduced formation and remodeling of articular cartilage, and not the anti-inflammatory effect of prednisone.

Total ALP (tissue non-specific ALP) is the sum of different isoenzymes, one of which is bone-specific alkaline phosphatase. In bone, ALP is expressed in hypertrophic chondrocytes in the epiphyseal growth plate, calcifying matrix vesicles, and mature osteoblasts. ALP is considered a bone turnover (formation) marker, but is considered inferior to CTX1 and P1NP in monitoring of osteoporosis in adults5. Standard-of-care treatment of idiopathic steroid-sensitive nephrotic syndrome and IgA nephropathy (IgAN) with corticosteroids showed a reduction in serum ALP, where ALP levels recovered after lowering of corticosteroid dosing46. A case report of an 8-yr kidney transplant recipient correlated corticosteroid treatment with reductions in serum ALP47. A study of 50 healthy children with familial short stature or constitutional delay in growth and puberty treated with growth hormone, oxandrolone, or testosterone showed a positive correlation of serum ALP with height velocity (r = 0.67)48. Our current study suggests that corticosteroid-related reductions in ALP levels are a biomarker for growth deceleration in children.

Animal studies have shown that corticosteroids lead to significant apoptosis of hypertrophic chondrocytes and osteoblasts49,50. The reduction of bone and chondrocyte-associated serum biomarkers identified in this current study likely reflect the loss of hypertrophic chondrocytes and osteoblasts in DMD children by prednisone-induced apoptosis. Our findings suggest that vamorolone may not induce apoptosis of hypertrophic chondrocytes and osteoblasts. Of note, all 4 turnover biomarkers (Osteocalcin, CTXI, P1NP, and ALP) recovered rapidly to close to baseline levels in the serum during 4-week taper/washout of prednisone.

Wnt/β-catenin signaling has been shown to be an important contributor to bone formation, homeostasis, and disease51. Two antagonists of the Wnt-1 pathway, sclerostin and DKK1, have been reported to show different serum levels in steroid-treated DMD patients compared to age-matched peers (sclerostin increased in DMD, DKK1 decreased)52. We queried these proteins in our serum proteomics data set and found that treatment with either prednisone or vamorolone did not significantly change serum levels from baseline.

Strengths of our study include the use of regulatory-compliant data and robust biosample collection as part of a registrational clinical trial. The trial was designed with a head-to-head comparison of vamorolone, prednisone, and placebo, and included a cross-over design that enabled internal validity tests. The use of the SomaScan proteomics platform, and use of longitudinal statistical methods (MMRMs) to model non-linear change and changing variances across timepoints was also a strength. Finally, connecting our serum biomarker discovery to proteins and genes associated with known bone disorders provides biological relevance.

Participants with corticosteroids (prednisone or vamorolone) showed marked improvement in motor outcomes, and changes in motor outcomes could lead to differences in bone biomarker findings. However, both prednisone and vamorolone showed very similar improvements in motor outcomes, where prednisone depressed bone biomarkers while vamorolone did not. Thus, this should rule out improvements in motor outcomes as a confounding variable in interpreting the data presented.

Limitations of our study include the smaller sample size (which is always a concern with a rare disorder like DMD). Another limitation of our study is that only young males with X-linked recessive DMD were studied, and not healthy peers or females. Specifically, while the biomarkers identified reflect corticosteroid-induced growth plate suppression in DMD boys, their performance and baseline levels in female pediatric populations remain unknown. Thus, our findings will need to be further validated in studies of additional children. Also, we studied only the high dose vamorolone 6 mg/kg/day group in this current analysis, and did not study the lower dose 2 mg/kg/day group. This was purely due to financial constraints, and it is planned to study the behavior of these new biomarkers in the lower dose group in the future. However, as none of the traditional bone biomarkers or novel cartilage biomarkers showed reductions with high dose (6 mg/kg/day) vamorolone, it is unlikely that they will show reductions with lower dose vamorolone. An important line of inquiry going forward includes whether the improvement in serum bone and growth plate biomarkers on vamorolone is ultimately associated with improved bone strength and a reduction in fracture risk relative to classic corticosteroids.

Conclusions

We report the discovery of novel serum protein biomarkers, each implicated in bone and/or cartilage metabolism via their causative effect on genetic disorders of cartilage/bone, that are differentially regulated by prednisone and vamorolone in boys with DMD. Our studies found change in height z-scores to be most strongly associated with change in ALP in corticosteroid-treated boys, an enzyme which is produced by both osteoblasts and hypertrophic chondrocytes. Vamorolone did not result in significant changes of these growth-related biomarkers, and cross-over from prednisone to vamorolone restored all biomarkers to pre-treatment (placebo) levels. They may be useful to monitor hypertrophic chondrocyte function (cellular activity) in DMD and other disorders.

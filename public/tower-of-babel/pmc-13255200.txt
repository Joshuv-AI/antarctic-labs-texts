What is this study about?

This study looked for genes that can make dogs more likely to develop a health condition called canine atopic dermatitis (CAD for short). The researchers wanted to find gene changes that are more common in dogs with CAD than dogs without CAD.

What were the results?

This study found a change in a gene called signaling lymphocytic activation molecule 1 (SLAMF1 for short) that was associated with CAD. Having the SLAMF1 gene change increases a dog’s chances of developing CAD. The researchers found that many dog breeds and free-roaming dogs worldwide had this gene change. Finding this gene change in many breeds suggests it may be an important contributor to CAD risk in dogs around the world. They confirmed the link between the SLAMF1 gene change and CAD in the French Bulldog and Boxer breeds.

What do the results mean?

This discovery helps researchers to understand more about CAD and potentially develop new treatments for CAD that target the SLAMF1 gene. The results could also help breeders to breed dogs with a lower risk of developing CAD.

The purpose of this plain language summary is to help dog owners, dog breeders, and veterinary professionals to understand the findings from recent research. The results of this study may differ from those of other studies. Veterinary professionals should make treatment decisions based on all available evidence not on the results of a single study.

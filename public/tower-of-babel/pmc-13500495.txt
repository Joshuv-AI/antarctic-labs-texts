Synchronous stability analysis and enhancement method for grid connected inverters in weak grids
Authors: Lin Zhu, Yan Liu, Pengfei Wang, Jiawei Chen, Qitong Jia, Guangyu Jia
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Renewable energy resources (RESs) are typically integrated into the utility grid through grid-connected inverters (GCIs). Such a system may lose synchronization during grid faults, especially under weak grid conditions, resulting in instability problems or even triggering large-scale power outage accidents. This paper analyzes the influences of the system parameters, including the phase-locked loop (PLL) parameters and the grid parameters, on the GCI’s synchronous stability. The results reveal that the PLL exhibits a negative damping effect during the transient process, which is bad for stabilizing the GCI in transients. An improved PLL, which is achieved by adding a feedback low-pass filter into the conventional PLL, is then proposed to address this issue, making the system more stable in transients. Analyzing results show that the improved PLL can enhance the synchronous stability of GCI during transients without changing its steady-state performance. The simulation and experimental results performed by a 2-MW GCI integrated into a weak grid verify the correctness of the theoretical analysis and the effectiveness of the proposed method.

Introduction

Renewable energy resources (RESs) such as wind power and solar power have been growing rapidly1. Typically, to inject renewable power to the utility grid, grid-connected inverters (Grid-connected inverters, GCIs) should be equipped between the renewable energy power plant and utility grid2. These inverters are usually controlled in a grid following manner, which only controls the output current to adjust the active and reactive power with the help of the phase-locked loop (PLL) that synchronizes the GCIs and the grid in phase and frequency3. In such a control, the PLL becomes a key component to keep the RESs in synchronous with the grid and operate stably. However, increased penetration of the RESs will deteriorate the system’s stability, especially in transient process like grid voltage sag. Instability would inevitably threaten the safe and reliable operation of the grid, causing a serious disaster that may result in huge economic losses4,5. Therefore, it’s urgent to tackle the synchronous stability issue of the system of GCIs integrated into utility grid, especially weak grid.

Stability issue for the GCIs can be categorized into small-signal stability and large-signal stability i.e. synchronous stability6. The former has been widely studied in literature, which shows that it can be tackled by optimizing the control parameters of current control loop. For the latter, it can be accomplished by obtaining the model of the system and applying synchronous stability analysis method to it. Some researchers declared that the coupling between the current control loop and the PLL may influence the GCI’s synchronous stability7–9. They used more precise and complex mathematical models for the stability analysis. However, most recent research results reveal that the current control loops have negligible impacts on the system’s synchronous stability10–12. These research findings indicate that it is the dynamics of the PLL, rather than that of the current control loop, that has dominant impact on the synchronous stability of the GCI system. In this case, the reduced order model, which neglects the dynamics of the current control loop, can be developed for stability analysis in transients like sudden grid voltage sag, large load connected to or disconnected from the grid, etc.4,5,13–15. Generally, the more accurate the model is, the more accurate the analyzing results will be, but the more difficult the analyzing task will become. In this case, a contradiction between complexity and accuracy should be obtained during the stability analysis, which can be achieved by developing a reduced-order model that considers both the dynamics of the PLL and the grid impedance4,14,16.

After the model has been obtained, stability analysis method, such as energy-based methods (including equal area criterion and Lyapunov’s direct method)12,15–18, bifurcation-based methods2,19,20, and phase-plane method21–23 etc., can be adopted to accomplish the synchronous stability analysis. As an important outcome, the relationship between the stability boundary and the system parameters can be obtained. The synchronous stability is then possible to be optimized by tuning the system parameters following the obtained relationship. Unfortunately, the requirements for satisfying the dynamic performance and grid-codes will limit the improvement range. Noting that the PLL has dominant impact on GCI’s synchronous stability, some researchers proposed new PLL techniques to improve the synchronous stability of GCI6,7,11,14,16,17,21,23. Authors in literature6 proposed an improved PLL technique that involves a feedback term, which is related to the estimation of the system’s power angle, to improve the synchronous stability of the GCI. In the implementation, it’s assumed that the grid impedance is a known value, which is impossible in practice as it is varying from time to time. Authors in literature16 proposed a modified PLL that has a variable structure. By removing the PLL’s integral regulator during grid faults, the resynchronization capability of GCI is improved. However, the effectiveness of this method highly depends on the real-time and precise detection of the grid states. After stability analysis, the researchers found that the synchronous stability of GCI could be enhanced by simply limiting the output of the PLL17. Although effective, the way to design the limitation values and how much improvement can be obtained are not provided, as in-depth theoretical analysis is missing. In21 and22, different negative feedback loops were added to the conventional PLL to improve the stability of GCIs connected to a weak grid. However, the effects of these feedback loops on the system’s stability have not been thoroughly investigated, and the parameters design guideline is missing.

As noted in the analyses above, using currently available modified PLL techniques to enhance the system’s synchronous stability is valid to some extent. However, these methods still face two major issues. Firstly, the currently available modified PLL lacks generality, as it is highly dependent on certain hard-to-detect system parameters and operating states, such as grid impedance. Secondly, the reasons why the modified PLL can improve the GCI’s stability have not been fully investigated, and there is no parameter design guideline available. Consequently, new PLL techniques and in-depth theoretical analyses aimed at improving synchronous stability are still emerging.

This study was intended to investigate the underlying mechanism of synchronous stability for the GCI during transient events, and to design an improved PLL to enhance GCI’s synchronous stability. In the following contents, the system under consideration, specifically a GCI integrated into a weak grid, is presented, and a reduced second-order nonlinear model is developed by neglecting certain relatively fast dynamics compared to those of the PLL. Based on the developed model, the synchronous stability of the GCI is analyzed through the distribution of eigenvalue loci. It’s revealed that the PLL has a negative damping effect, which will deteriorate the synchronous stability. To eliminate this undesired effect, a stability enhancement control method, achieved by adding a feedback low-pass filter (FLPF) to the conventional PLL, is proposed consequently. The operational principle and detailed design rules for the key control parameters are provided following a synchronous stability analysis using eigenvalue loci and the phase-plane method. Finally, a hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) real-time simulation model of a 2 MW GCI connected to a weak grid, with real-time simulation results verifying the effectiveness of the proposed method.

Modeling of the GCI featuring weak grid considering the dynamics of the PLL and grid impedance

For GCIs, when the bandwidths of the PLL and the current loop are close, the coupling effect between the control loops strengthens, and at this point, the dynamics of the current loop and the PLL both have a significant impact on system stability. However, when the bandwidth of the current controller is about 10 times the PLL bandwidth, system’s synchronous stability is dominated by PLL, and the current loop dynamics can be neglected13. In practice, the bandwidth of the current loop (including the current control and the LC filter) is typically designed to be much larger than that of the PLL, so the dynamics of the current loop are usually much faster than those of the PLL24,25. At the same time, considering that the impedance of the LC filter is much smaller than that of the grid, the impedance of the LC filter can be neglected12,26. Therefore, the influence of the dynamics of the current loop during transient is reasonably neglected in this paper. In this case, it is treated that Itd, Itq can track their reference values Itdref, Itqref accurately in real time27,28. Thus, the GCI can be modeled as a reduced second-order nonlinear model.

When the grid voltage drops or the grid-connected current changes, the magnitude and phase of the voltage utabc at the PCC change drastically. The PLL needs to dynamically adjust the frequency and phase to synchronous with the grid. Under a weak grid, the grid impedance can significantly affect PLL’s performance, potentially causing phase oscillations or even instability in the PLL, which may lead to GFIL instability.

It is seen from Fig. 3 that the dynamic responses of the reduced-order model and full-order model are consistent with each other in both cases. The responses of δ are almost the same. As to ωpll and Utq, the overshoot of the full-order model is bigger than the reduced order model. In both cases, the maximum deviations of δ, Δωpll and Utq before system diverges are respectively 0 (0%), 5.12 Hz (25.2%) and 26 V (12.4%). This difference is negligible for the synchronous stability analysis. Therefore, it is then concluded that the reduced-order model given in (8) is precise enough for analyzing the system’s synchronous stability.

Stability of GCI under grid faults

As required by the grid code, the GCI should keep providing active and reactive power to the grid for some time to support its operation when an instantaneous fault occurs in the grid29. Therefore, the inverter must have the fault ride-through capability. However, with the increasing penetration of RESs and power electronics loads in the utility grid, especially weak grids, grid faults like voltage sag and short-circuit may happen from time to time30. Hence, it is necessary to analyze the synchronous stability of the GCI in weak grids and find a way to improve it. In the following analysis, the worst condition, in which the grid line impedance is purely inductive, and the GCI operates with unity power factor, is considered to make the results more general2,24. As a result, Rg = 0 is applied to (8). In addition, it must be ensured that the system has stable equilibrium points (SEPs) before analyzing the system’s synchronous stability.

Alternatively, increasing kp, pll/ki, pllUgcosδ0 can improve the damping of the system at SEP, thus enhancing the stability of the system. On the contrary, increasing LgItd will decrease the damping at SEP, thus worsening system stability. Some research reported that the increase of kp, pll can increase the damping ratio of GCI, enhancing GCI’s stability2,4. However, from (13), it is known that if kp, pll is designed too large, the stability condition will not be met anymore, and the GCI will lose stability. Therefore, kp, pll has a positive effect on the stability only within a certain range. Alternatively, the improvement in the stability of the GCI is limited by merely tuning system parameters.

To verify the correctness, the eigenvalue loci distribution under varying parameters is represented in Fig. 5. The system is stable if all eigenvalues lie to the left of the imaginary axis, and unstable if any eigenvalue lies to the right of the imaginary axis. The crossing points where the eigenvalues reach the imaginary axis indicate that the system is in critical states. It can be seen that the decrease of Ug, kp, pll and increase of Lg, ki, pll, Itd will make the eigenvalues move towards the imaginary axis, worsening the stability of the system. These results keep pace with Fig. 4.

Synchronous stability enhancement method

To make the system more stable, an optimum parameter design can be applied. However, the stability improvement ability may be bounded by simply tuning parameters. Fortunately, as can be observed from Figs. 4 and 5, the PLL has strong impacts on the synchronous stability of the GCI connected in a weak grid. Therefore, an improved PLL technology could be a better choice for improving system stability in transients.

Novel PLL technique for stability enhancement

It is observed in Fig. 2 that there is a negative damping term LgItd that would worsen the system’s stability. Therefore, a positive damping term can be injected into the system to improve its stability. Literature21,22 proposed different methods to improve system’s damping. However, the existing methods have specific preconditions. It’s difficult to meet the preconditions in practice, and their application scope is limited. To address this, this paper proposed a synchronous stability enhancement method by improving system’s damping during transient events. This can be achieved by adding a damping loop, which is implemented by an FLPF in the same route as LgItd, to the PLL, as shown in Fig. 6(a). With the modified PLL, the system model can be simplified as Fig. 6(b). Kh, Th are the gain and time constant of the FLPF respectively. The FLPF allows the low frequency component of Δωpll to pass, which can eliminate the steady-state deviation of Δωpll without affecting the steady-state performance of the system.

Design of parameters of the FLPF

There are two parameters, namely Kh and Th, related to the performance of FLPF that require optimal design. According to (14), the improved PLL is a third-order nonlinear system. It’s difficult to conduct an analytical derivation for parameter design. Therefore, this paper adopts a graphical parameter design method for parameter tuning: first, the approximate range of the parameters is determined based on system response requirements, and then the root locus method is used to determine the specific parameter values.

Considering that the feedback gain Kh of FLPF is the amplification factor of Δωpll, which is related to the injected damping term, it plays a dominant role in supporting synchronous stability. To compensate the negative damping effect brought by LgItd, Kh>LgItdref should be satisfied. A larger Kh would result in a better stability performance. However, a larger Kh also indicates a higher sensitivity of Δωpll, meaning the output of the FLPF will fluctuate wildly if Kh is too large, which will deteriorate the synchronous stability.

In addition, the filtering time constant Th determines the response speed and cut-off frequency of the FLPF. To eliminate the low frequency offset of Δωpll, the cut-off frequency of FLPF should be designed according to the grid’s limitations on the rate of change of frequency, which is 5Hz6,22. Thus, Th>1/(2π·5 Hz) should be satisfied. In the meantime, to track frequency deviation in time, Th shouldn’t be too large.

The root locus method is a general parameter tuning method widely used in both engineering practice and theoretical research. Therefore, this paper adopts it for the parameter design of the FLPF. Figure 9 depicts the eigenvalue loci distributions with different Kh and Th, where Kh increases from LgItdref to 10*LgItdref, and Th decreases from 1 to 0.03. It can be found that with the increase of Kh and with the decrease of Th, the eigenvalues have larger negative real parts, which means the system has a stronger stability. Nevertheless, when Kh increases to 10*LgItdref, eigenvalue λ3 becomes small, and when Th decreases to 0.03, there will be a pair of conjugate eigenvalues, which means a deteriorated stability.

Fig. 9Eigenvalue loci with different FLPF parameters: (a) Kh; (b) Th.

Eigenvalue loci with different FLPF parameters: (a) Kh; (b) Th.

After comprehensively considering the influences of Kh and Th on the stability and performance of GCI, Kh=3.55 and Th=0.1 are selected in this paper base on the eigenvalue loci distributions in Fig. 9. It is worth noting that in practical applications, the signals transmitted to the control system are typically filtered, and the measurement delay is generally very small. Therefore, the effects of noise and measurement delay can be neglected during parameter design. Meanwhile, using Itdref as the design basis ensures the stability of the PLL across the full current range, and consequently, Kh does not need to vary with Itd.

Discussion of the interaction between grid-following and grid-forming resources

Currently, GCIs are usually connected to the grid using grid-following control strategies, which are widely used for RESs, offer fast response but provide almost no inertia, making them suitable for stiff power grids31. However, they face instability issues with the increasing penetration of RESs in the grid. In contrast, GCIs using grid-forming strategies can emulate synchronous generators to provide inertia and damping, offering frequency and voltage support, but they are prone to instability in stiff grids due to minimal phase differences causing power fluctuations32. The hybrid system consisting of GFM and GFL GCIs can simultaneously leverage the advantages of both, representing an inescapable trend in the development of renewable energy systems. However, the hybrid system remains susceptible to oscillations and potential instability.

To address these issues, studies have been exclusively conducted. Authors in literature33 analyzed the interaction mechanisms between GFL and GFM synchronization control from the perspective of external characteristics. Then, a grid strength assessment method is proposed to evaluate the small-signal synchronization stability of GFL and GFM GCIs integrated systems. In34, an adaptive control for power plant controller is proposed to enhance stability of GCIs with GFL and GFM control. The interaction stability of the hybrid system is analyzed and a passivity-enhanced oscillation suppression method is proposed in35. Although some progress has been made in current research, the interaction mechanisms between GFL and GFM resources in hybrid system remain unclear. To ensure the stable operation of the hybrid system, the interaction mechanisms require further investigation, especially in weak grid conditions.

Conclusions

This paper investigates the underlying mechanism of synchronous stability of GCIs connected to a weak grid under transient events. The effect of system’s parameters, including the PLL and the grid parameters, on the stability is revealed. It is revealed that the system’s synchronous stability can be enhanced to some extent by tuning system parameters. But its enhancing ability is limited. In this event, a synchronous stability enhancement method, which is achieved by using a modified PLL, is proposed to improve the synchronous stability of GCIs in a weak grid. Both simulation and experimental results verify that the proposed stability enhancement control method can improve the synchronous stability of GCIs under transient events.

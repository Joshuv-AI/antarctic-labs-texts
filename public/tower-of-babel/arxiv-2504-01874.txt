The Hitchin morphism for certain surfaces fibered over a curve
Matthew Huynh

Abstract
The Chen-Ngô Conjecture [ 4 ] *Conjecture 5.2 predicts that the Hitchin morphism from the moduli stack of G G -Higgs bundles on a smooth projective variety surjects onto the space of spectral data (recalled in § 2.2.3 ). The conjecture is known to hold for the group GL n \mathrm{GL}_{n} and any surface [ 21 ] , and for the group GL 2 \mathrm{GL}_{2} and any smooth projective variety [ 10 ] . We prove the Chen-Ngô Conjecture for any reductive group when the variety is a ruled surface or (a blowup of) a nonisotrivial elliptic fibration with reduced fibers. Furthermore, if the group is a classical group, i.e. G ∈ { SL n , SO n , Sp 2 ​ n } G\in\{\mathrm{SL}_{n},\mathrm{SO}_{n},\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}\} , then we prove that the Hitchin morphism restricted to the Dolbeault moduli space of semiharmonic G G -Higgs bundles surjects onto the space of spectral data.



1
Introduction

Let
G
be a reductive group. The moduli stack of
G
-Higgs bundles on a smooth projective curve has a rich geometry and topology.
N. Hitchin defined a surjective morphism from this moduli space to affine space
[
11
]
, and the study of this morphism has produced stunning applications like Ngô’s proof of the Fundamental Lemma in the Langlands program
[
16
]
.

We can replace the curve by a higher-dimensional smooth projective variety and obtain a moduli stack of
G
-Higgs bundles equipped with a morphism to an affine space.
This morphism, called the Hitchin morphism, is not necessarily surjective once the dimension of our variety is at least two.
However, there is a conjectured image
\mathcal{B}(X,G)
defined by T. H. Chen and B. C. Ngô
[
4
]
*Conjecture 5.2; its definition is recalled in §
2.2.3
.
Roughly speaking, the conjectured image
\mathcal{B}(X,G)
is the locus where one can construct, for each
k
-point, a “cameral cover” of the original variety and hope to obtain a modular description of the fiber of the Hitchin morphism in terms of the cameral cover.

If
X
is an abelian variety of dimension
d
, then the Hitchin morphism for
X
surjects onto
\mathcal{B}(X,G)
, essentially because the sheaf
\Omega^{1}_{X}
is free of rank
d

[
4
]
*Example 5.1.
All other known results are for the general linear group, because in this case one may work with “spectral covers”, which are simpler than cameral covers.
When
X
is a surface, Chen and Ngô prove a “spectral correspondence” result over an open subset
\mathcal{B}^{\heartsuit}\subset\mathcal{B}(X,\mathrm{GL}_{n})
.
More precisely, for every
b\in\mathcal{B}^{\heartsuit}(k)
, they construct a finite, flat cover
X_{b}^{\mathrm{CM}}\to X
, and prove that the fiber of the Hitchin morphism over
b
is isomorphic to the stack of Cohen-Macaulay sheaves of generic rank 1 on
X_{b}^{\mathrm{CM}}

[
4
]
*Theorem 7.3.
In particular, this shows that
\mathcal{B}^{\heartsuit}
is contained in the image of the Hitchin morphism.

However, the open subset
\mathcal{B}^{\heartsuit}
is mysterious in general, and
\mathcal{B}^{\heartsuit}
may not be dense in
\mathcal{B}(X,\mathrm{GL}_{n})
, as the scheme
\mathcal{B}(X,\mathrm{GL}_{n})
need not be irreducible.
L. Song and H. Sun prove that when
X
is a surface, the conjectured image
\mathcal{B}(X,\mathrm{GL}_{n})

is
the image of the Hitchin morphism by “decomposing” spectral data
b\in\mathcal{B}(X,\mathrm{GL}_{n})\setminus\mathcal{B}^{\heartsuit}
and using the spectral correspondence
[
21
]
.
S. He and J. Liu prove that for
G=\mathrm{GL}_{2}
and any smooth projective variety
X
, the conjectured image
\mathcal{B}(X,\mathrm{GL}_{2})

is
the image of the Hitchin morphism.
Roughly speaking, they construct Cohen-Macaulay covers
\tilde{X}_{b}
of
X
for all
b
in an open subset of
\mathcal{B}(X,\mathrm{GL}_{2})
, prove a spectral correspondence for these covers, and produce Higgs bundles over the remainder of
\mathcal{B}(X,\mathrm{GL}_{2})
explicitly
[
10
]
.

For
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
and
X
a surface fibered over a curve
C
, Chen and Ngô show that the Hitchin base for the curve embeds as a closed subscheme in
\mathcal{B}(X,\mathrm{GL}_{n})
, and that the image of the Hitchin morphism contains this subscheme
[
4
]
*§8.

The purpose of this paper is to study the Hitchin morphism for surfaces fibered over a curve and
other
reductive groups.
The results are stated precisely in §
1.3
after establishing some notation in §
1.2
.
We prove that the Hitchin base for the curve embeds as a closed subscheme in the conjectured image
\mathcal{B}(X,G)
, and that the image of the Hitchin morphism contains this closed subscheme.
When
G
is a classical group and the fibered surface has only reduced fibers, we can use the standard linear representation of
G
to deduce more, namely that the locus of semiharmonic
G
-Higgs bundles surjects onto the Hitchin base for the curve, which is embedded in
\mathcal{B}(X,G)
.
Finally, for ruled surfaces and blowups of nonisotrivial elliptic fibrations with reduced fibers, we prove the Chen-Ngô Conjecture as a corollary of these results.

Summary of contents and results

(FS)
. Then for each positive integer

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f:X\to C
be a fibered surface
(FS)
, and let
G
be a reductive group.
Then

1.

the closed immersion
\mathcal{A}(C,G)\to\mathcal{A}(X,G)
induced by pullback of symmetric differentials factors through the conjectured image of the Hitchin morphism
\mathcal{B}(X,G)
, and

2.

the image of the Hitchin morphism
h_{X,G}
contains
\mathcal{A}(C,G)
.

Proof.

The proof of the first statement follows from Proposition
3.1
.

A
G
-Higgs bundle
(E,\theta)
on
C
with
h_{C,G}(E,\theta)=a\in\mathcal{A}(C,G)
pulls back to one on
X
, and this pullback
G
-Higgs bundle maps to
a
via
h_{X,G}
(Corollary
3.2
).
The second assertion in the theorem follows from the fact that the Hitchin morphism for
C
is surjective.
∎

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
f:X\to C
be a fibered surface
(FS)
with only reduced fibers, and let
G
be a classical group, i.e.
G\in\{\mathrm{SL}_{n},\mathrm{SO}_{n},\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}\}
.
Then the image of the Hitchin morphism restricted to the Dolbeault moduli space
M_{\mathrm{Dol}}(X,G)
contains the Hitchin base
\mathcal{A}(C,G)
of the curve.

Proof.

The Hitchin base
\mathcal{A}(C,G)
for the curve embeds as a closed subscheme of
\mathcal{B}(X,G)
by Theorem
1.1
.

If
C=\mathbb{P}^{1}
, then
\mathcal{A}(C,G)
is a point, which maps to
0_{\mathcal{B}}\in\mathcal{B}(X,G)
.
The trivial
G
-bundle on
X
with zero Higgs field satisfies the following properties: (i)
h(G\times X,0)=0_{\mathcal{B}}
, (ii) the associated vector bundle is trivial, and hence has zero Chern classes, and (iii) the associated vector bundle is slope-semistable, as it is the direct sum of slope-stable sheaves that are all of the same slope.
Therefore, this
G
-Higgs bundle is semiharmonic.

Now assume that
g(C)\geq 1
.
The restriction of the Hitchin morphism to the Dolbeault moduli space
M_{\mathrm{Dol}}(X,G)
is proper, so it suffices to produce a nonempty open subset of
\mathcal{A}(C,G)
contained in the image of
h_{X,G}
restricted to
M_{\mathrm{Dol}}(X,G)
. (The Hitchin base
\mathcal{A}(C,G)
is irreducible, so any nonempty open set is dense).

For all
a\in\mathcal{A}(C,G)
, there is a companion
G
-Higgs bundle
(E_{a}^{\prime},\theta_{a}^{\prime})
on
C
with
h_{C,G}(E^{\prime}_{a},\theta^{\prime}_{a})=a
, (see
[
8
]
for instance).
For generic
a\in\mathcal{A}(C,G)
, this companion
G
-Higgs bundle pulls back to a semiharmonic
G
-Higgs bundle on
X
, i.e. a
k
-point of
M_{\mathrm{Dol}}(X,G)
(Proposition
3.3
, §
4
).
The theorem follows.
∎

When
f:X\to C
is a ruled surface or nonisotrivial elliptic fibration with only reduced fibers, then the pullback of symmetric differentials
H^{0}(C,S^{i}\Omega^{1}_{C})\to H^{0}(X,S^{i}\Omega^{1}_{X})
is an isomorphism for all
i

[
4
]
*Proposition 8.1.
This implies that
\mathcal{A}(C,G)=\mathcal{B}(X,G)=\mathcal{A}(X,G)
, so by Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
we deduce the following

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
f:X\to C
be a ruled surface or nonisotrivial elliptic fibration with only reduced fibers, and let
G
be a reductive group.
Then the Hitchin morphism surjects onto
\mathcal{A}(X,G)=\mathcal{B}(X,G)
.
If
G
is a classical group, then the Hitchin morphism restricted to the Dolbeault moduli space
M_{\mathrm{Dol}}(X,G)
surjects onto
\mathcal{A}(X,G)=\mathcal{B}(X,G)
.

Suppose that
X
is a smooth projective surface, and
\pi:Y\to X
is a birational morphism.
Then the pullback of symmetric differentials
H^{0}(X,S^{i}\Omega^{1}_{X})\to H^{0}(Y,S^{i}\Omega^{1}_{Y})
is an isomorphism for all
i
(§
3.4
).
Thus we can determine the image of the Hitchin morphism for a slightly larger class of surfaces:

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
X
be a nonisotrivial elliptic fibration with only reduced fibers, let
\pi:Y\to X
be a birational morphism, and let
G
be a reductive group.
Then the Hitchin morphism
h_{Y,G}:\mathcal{M}(Y,G)\to\mathcal{A}(Y,G)
surjects onto
\mathcal{A}(Y,G)=\mathcal{B}(Y,G)
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, suppose that
\pi:Y\to X
is the blowup of a point.
The pullback of symmetric differentials
H^{0}(X,S^{i}\Omega^{1}_{X})\to H^{0}(Y,S^{i}\Omega^{1}_{Y})
identifies the Hitchin bases for
X
and
Y
, i.e. we have
\mathcal{A}(X,G)=\mathcal{A}(Y,G)
(§
3.4
).
The assumption on
X
further implies that
\mathcal{A}(Y,G)=\mathcal{B}(Y,G)
.
The pullback of a
G
-Higgs bundle
(E,\theta)
on
X
with
h_{X,G}(E,\theta)=a
satisfies
h_{Y,G}(\pi^{\ast}E,\pi^{\ast}\theta)=a
by Proposition
3.4
.
We conclude by applying Corollary
1.3
.
∎

2.2
Higgs bundles and the Hitchin morphism

In this subsection, we review the definitions of the moduli stack of

-Higgs bundles, the Hitchin base, the Hitchin morphism, and its conjectured image in terms of mapping stacks.

2.2.1
The commuting scheme

Let
G
be a reductive group, and let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{Lie}(G)
.
Fix a maximal torus
T\subset G
, let
\mathfrak{t}=\mathrm{Lie}(T)
, and let
r
be the rank of
G
, (i.e.
\dim T
).
Let
W=N_{G}(T)/T
be the Weyl group.
Choose homogeneous generators
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}
of
k[\mathfrak{g}]^{G}\cong k[\mathfrak{t}]^{W}
, and denote their degrees by
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
respectively.

For any positive integer
d
, we can define the
d
-fold commutator map

\mathfrak{g}^{d}\to\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq d}\mathfrak{g},\quad\quad(\theta^{1},\ldots,\theta^{d})\mapsto([\theta^{i},\theta^{j}])_{i<j}.

We define the
d
-fold commuting scheme
\mathfrak{C}^{d}
as the scheme-theoretic fiber over
(0,\ldots,0)\in\prod_{i<j}\mathfrak{g}
. Note that
\mathfrak{C}^{1}=\mathfrak{g}
.

The group
G
acts on
\mathfrak{g}^{d}
by the diagonal adjoint action, and this action restricts to one on
\mathfrak{C}^{d}
.
There is also an action of
\mathrm{GL}_{d}
on
\mathfrak{g}^{d}
defined by

\mathrm{GL}_{d}(R)\times\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{k}((\mathfrak{g}^{d})^{\ast},R)\to\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{k}((\mathfrak{g}^{d})^{\ast},R),\quad\quad(x^{i}_{j}),(\theta^{1},\ldots,\theta^{d})\mapsto(x^{1}_{j}\theta^{j},\ldots,x^{d}_{j}\theta^{j}),

where
R
is any
k
-algebra.
This action on
\mathfrak{g}^{d}
also restricts to one on
\mathfrak{C}^{d}
because the Lie bracket is bilinear, and the
G
and
\mathrm{GL}_{d}
actions commute. When
d=1
, the
\mathrm{GL}_{1}=\mathbb{G}_{m}
action on
\mathfrak{C}^{1}=\mathfrak{g}
is just scaling.

2.2.2
The moduli stack of Higgs bundles

Let
X
be a connected, smooth, projective variety of dimension
d
, and let
T^{\ast}_{X}=\mathop{\underline{\mathrm{Spec}}}\nolimits(S^{\bullet}(\Omega^{1}_{X})^{\vee})
be its cotangent bundle.
By the preceding discussion, we can form the associated bundle
T^{\ast}_{X}(\mathfrak{C}^{d})
.
Notice that
G
acts on
T^{\ast}_{X}(\mathfrak{C}^{d})
, (the action is the fiberwise diagonal adjoint action of
G
on
\mathfrak{C}^{d}
), and the morphism
T^{\ast}_{X}(\mathfrak{C}^{d})\to X
is
G
-invariant.

The moduli stack of
G
-Higgs bundles on
X
is defined as

\mathcal{M}(X,G):=\mathrm{Maps}_{X}(X,[T^{\ast}_{X}(\mathfrak{C}^{d})/G]).

A
G
-Higgs bundle on
X
, (i.e.
k
-point of this stack), is the data of a principal
G
-bundle
E\to X
, and an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module morphism
\theta:(\Omega^{1}_{X})^{\vee}\to\mathrm{ad}(E)
such that
[\theta(u),\theta(v)]=0
for all local sections
u,v
of
(\Omega^{1}_{X})^{\vee}
.
This last condition is called the
integrability condition
, and is typically abbreviated as
\theta\wedge\theta=0
.

A
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-Higgs bundle on
X
is equivalent to the data of a locally free sheaf of rank
n
, say
E
, and an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module morphism
\theta:(\Omega^{1}_{X})^{\vee}\to\mathrm{End}(E)
satisfying the integrability condition.
We use this identification implicitly throughout the paper.

The Hitchin base and conjectured image of the Hitchin morphism

Let

Let

To define it, consider the map of vector spaces

For any

(2.1)

If

.
Furthermore, if

Next we compute the polarizations of

c=\det
are

c_{(2,0)}=-\det(\theta^1),\quad c_{(1,1)}=-\mathrm{tr}(\theta^{1}\theta^{2}),\quad c_{(0,2)}=-\det(\theta^2).

The scheme
\mathfrak{B}
is equal to the spectrum of the ring

k[c_{(2,0)},c_{(1,1)},c_{(0,2)}]/(c_{(1,1)}^{2}-4c_{(2,0)}c_{(0,2)}).

We also have
\mathfrak{t}^{2}/\!/W\simeq\mathfrak{B}
, as we now explain.
The nontrivial element of
W=\mathfrak{S}_{2}
acts on
\mathfrak{t}^{2}:=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\nolimits(k[x,y])
by
x\mapsto-x
and
y\mapsto-y
.
The polynomials
x^{2},2xy,y^{2}
generate the ring of
W
-invariant functions on
\mathfrak{t}^{2}
, and it is clear that these generators satisfy the same relation satisfied by the polarizations of
c=\det
. One also sees in this example that the morphism
\mathfrak{t}^{d}\to\mathfrak{t}^{d}/\!/W
is not flat in general when
d\geq 2
.

The
\mathrm{GL}_{d}
-action on
\mathfrak{C}^{d}
restricts to
\mathfrak{t}^{d}\subset\mathfrak{C}^{d}
, and this induces a
\mathrm{GL}_{d}
-action on
\mathfrak{B}
.
The conjectured image is defined as a scheme of sections,

\mathcal{B}(X,G):=\mathrm{Maps}_{X}(X,T^{\ast}_{X}(\mathfrak{B})).

There is a closed immersion
\mathfrak{B}\to\mathfrak{A}
defined by the ring map
z_{j,\underline{i}}\to c_{j,\underline{i}}
that respects the
\mathrm{GL}_{d}
-actions on both schemes.
This induces a closed immersion
\mathcal{B}(X,G)\subset\mathcal{A}(X,G)
.

2.2.4
The Hitchin morphism for curves and surfaces

Although the Hitchin morphism is defined for smooth projective varieties of all dimensions, for neatness of exposition, we only describe it for dimensions 1 and 2.
The descriptions below also agree with the definition in
[
20
]
when
G
is the general linear group.

Suppose
C
is a smooth projective curve.
The adjoint quotient map
\chi:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{c}
induces a morphism
[\chi]:[T^{\ast}_{C}(\mathfrak{g})/G]\to T^{\ast}_{C}(\mathfrak{c})
, and the Hitchin morphism
h_{C,G}:\mathcal{M}(C,G)\to\mathcal{A}(C,G)
is the induced morphism of mapping stacks.
If we choose a non-zero root vector
f\in\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha_{i}}
for each simple root
\alpha_{i}
, and a square root of the canonical bundle of
C
, then we obtain a section of the Hitchin fibration, called the Kostant section (more details on this construction can be found in
[
5
]
*§2.3).

Suppose
X
is a smooth projective surface.
Then the restriction map
k[\mathfrak{C}^{2}]^{G}\to k[\mathfrak{t}^{2}]^{W}
is an isomorphism
[
14
]
.
Thus we obtain a composition
\mathfrak{C}^{2}\to\mathfrak{B}\to\mathfrak{A}
that is
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
-equivariant and
G
-invariant, which induces a composition of
X
-morphisms

[T^{\ast}_{X}(\mathfrak{C}^{2})/G]\to T^{\ast}_{X}(\mathfrak{B})\to T^{\ast}_{X}(\mathfrak{A}).

The Hitchin morphism is the induced morphism of mapping stacks
h_{X,G}:\mathcal{M}(X,G)\to\mathcal{A}(X,G)
.

By construction, the Hitchin morphism
h_{X,G}
always factors through
\mathcal{B}(X,G)
.
Although we have not described
h_{X,G}
when
\dim(X)\geq 3
, it is still true that the image of any geometric point
(E,\theta)\in\mathcal{M}(X,G)(k)
lies in
\mathcal{B}(X,G)(k)

[
4
]
*Proposition 5.1.
If
(E,\theta)
is a
G
-Higgs bundle on
X
that maps to
b\in\mathcal{B}(X,G)(k)
, then we call
b
the spectral data of
(E,\theta)
.

2.2.5
Dolbeault moduli spaces

Introducing a notion of semistability allows us to obtain good moduli spaces in the sense of
[
1
]
.
In particular, there are schemes parametrizing semistable Higgs bundles with vanishing Chern classes constructed by Simpson in
[
19
,
20
]
.
We recall the salient details now.

Let
X
be a connected, smooth, projective variety of dimension
d
, and fix an ample line bundle
L
on
X
.
A Higgs sheaf
(E,\theta)
on
X
is the data of a coherent sheaf
E
and a morphism of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules
\theta:E\to E\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X}
satisfying
\theta\wedge\theta=0
, i.e. the associated morphism
(\Omega^{1}_{X})^{\vee}\to\mathrm{End}(E)
satisfies the integrability condition described in §
2.2.2
.
A sub-Higgs sheaf of
E
is a subsheaf
F\subset E
such that
\theta|_{F}
factors through
F\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X}\to E\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X}
.

We call a Higgs sheaf
(E,\theta)

p
-semistable if
E
is a pure
d
-dimensional coherent sheaf, and for all sub-Higgs sheaves
F\subset E
with
0<\mathrm{rk}(F)<\mathrm{rk}(E)
, there exists an integer
N>0
such that the inequality

\frac{P(F,n)}{\mathrm{rk}(F)}\leq\frac{P(E,n)}{\mathrm{rk}(E)}

holds for all
n\gg N
, where
P(E,n)
is the Hilbert polynomial of
E
with respect to
L
.
Similarly, we call a Higgs sheaf
\mu
-semistable it is pure of dimension
d
, and the inequality
\mu(F)\leq\mu(E)
holds for all sub-Higgs sheaves of strictly smaller rank, (recall
\mu=\mathrm{deg}/\mathrm{rk}
, and
\mathrm{deg}(E)=c_{1}(E)\cdot L^{d-1}
because
X
is smooth and projective).
Both semistability conditions can be checked via quotients, that is,
(E,\theta)
is
p
-semistable (resp.
\mu
-semistable) if and only if for all proper, purely
d
-dimensional quotients
q:E\to F^{\prime}
with
\ker(q)
a sub-Higgs sheaf, we have
P(E,n)/\mathrm{rk}(E)\leq P(F^{\prime},n)/\mathrm{rk}(F^{\prime})
, (resp.
\mu(E)\leq\mu(F^{\prime})
).

Now let
G
be a reductive group.
We say that a
G
-Higgs bundle
(E,\theta)
is semiharmonic (or is of semiharmonic type), if the Chern classes of
E
vanish in rational cohomology, and if there exists a faithful representation
\rho:G\to\mathrm{GL}(V)
such that the associated Higgs bundle is
p
-semistable.

Fix a point
x\in X
, and consider the following moduli functor

\begin{gathered}R^{\natural}_{\mathrm{Dol}}(X,x,G):(\mathrm{Sch}/k)^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Sets}\\
S\mapsto\left\{\parbox[h]{256.0748pt}{triples $(E,\theta,b)$ where $(E,\theta)$ is a $G$-Higgs bundle on $X_{S}$, such that for each closed point $s\in S$, $(E_{s},\theta_{s})$ is of semiharmonic type, and $b:S\to E|_{\{x\}\times S}$ is a section}\right\}/\text{isomorphism}.\end{gathered}

Then there is a scheme
R_{\mathrm{Dol}}(X,x,G)
that represents this moduli functor
[
19
]
*Theorem 4.10.
Furthermore, if
G\to H
is a closed embedding, then there is a closed embedding
R_{\mathrm{Dol}}(X,x,G)\to R_{\mathrm{Dol}}(X,x,H)
.

The group
G
acts on the representation space
R_{\mathrm{Dol}}(X,x,G)
by acting on the section.
Simpson shows that one can form the GIT quotient for this action, and obtain a space
M_{\mathrm{Dol}}(X,G)
that universally corepresents the functor sending a
k
-scheme
S
to the set of isomorphism classes of
G
-Higgs bundles on
X_{S}
that are semiharmonic when restricted to each fiber.

We can instead take the stack quotient to obtain the moduli stack
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Dol}}(X,G)
, which sends a
k
-scheme
S
to the groupoid of
G
-Higgs bundles on
X_{S}
that are semiharmonic when restricted to each fiber.
There is a good moduli space morphism
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Dol}}(X,G)\to M_{\mathrm{Dol}}(X,G)
that comes from the quotient stack/GIT quotient construction
[
1
]
*Theorem 13.6, Remark 13.7.
Observe that
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Dol}}(X,G)
is a locally closed substack of
\mathcal{M}(X,G)
, because semiharmonicity is an open condition, and Chern classes in rational cohomology are locally constant in flat families.

The restriction of the Hitchin morphism to
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Dol}}(X,G)
factors through its good moduli space
M_{\mathrm{Dol}}(X,G)
, and the Hitchin morphism restricted to
M_{\mathrm{Dol}}(X,G)
is proper (
[
20
]
*Theorem 6.11 for the general linear group, and, for example,
[
6
]
*Proposition 2.2.2 for reductive groups).
Therefore, if
U\subset\mathcal{B}(X,G)
is any open subset contained in the image of
h_{X,G}
restricted to
M_{\mathrm{Dol}}(X,G)
, then the closure of
U
is also contained in the image.

2.3
Spectral covers

When
G
is the general linear group
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
and
C
is a curve, there is a well-known description of the fibers of the Hitchin morphism in terms of spectral curves, which are finite, flat covers of
C

[
3
,
17
]
.
For now, we fix the group and the curve, so we omit these from the notation.

Let
a_{i}=(-1)^{i}\tr(\wedge^i -)\in k[\mathfrak{gl}_{n}]^{\mathrm{GL}_{n}}
, let
A_{n}=k[a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}]
, and let
\mathfrak{c}_{n}=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\nolimits(A_{n})
.
Then define
B_{n}=A_{n}[x]/(x^{n}+a_{1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{n})
and let
\mathfrak{s}_{n}=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\nolimits(B_{n})
.
Observe that
B_{n}
is a smooth
k
-algebra and a free
A_{n}
-module of rank
n
equipped with an
A_{n}
-module endomorphism given by multiplication by
\overline{x}
.
Therefore, we have a finite, flat morphism
p:\mathfrak{s}_{n}\to\mathfrak{c}_{n}
.
Furthermore, we can extend the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action on
A_{n}
to
B_{n}
, so that

is
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant.
Thus we can twist by
T^{\ast}_{C}
to obtain a
C
-morphism
T^{\ast}_{C}(\mathfrak{s}_{n})\to T^{\ast}_{C}(\mathfrak{c}_{n})
.

Given

, we obtain a finite, flat cover

by base change of
T^{\ast}_{C}(\mathfrak{s}_{n})\to T^{\ast}_{C}(\mathfrak{c}_{n})
, and
C_{a}
is a closed subscheme of
T^{\ast}_{C}
.
The spectral correspondence refers to the isomorphism between the fiber of the Hitchin morphism over
a
, and the moduli stack of torsion-free coherent sheaves of rank 1 on
C_{a}
.
Torsion-free sheaves of rank 1 on
C_{a}
pushforward to locally free sheaves of rank
n
on
C
, and the Higgs field is given by the
\mathcal{O}_{T^{\ast}_{C}}
-module structure.

2.3.1
The companion section for the general linear group

In particular, given
a\in\mathcal{A}
and a spectral cover
p:C_{a}\to C
, we can pushforward the structure sheaf to obtain the so-called
companion Higgs bundle

(E_{a},\theta_{a})
on
C
.
For every
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\oplus_{i=1}^{n}H^{0}(C,S^{i}\Omega^{1}_{C})
, the locally free sheaf
E_{a}
is isomorphic to
\oplus_{i=0}^{n-1}(\Omega^{1}_{C})^{-i}
, and the Higgs field
\theta_{a}
is given by the companion matrix,

\mathcal{O}_{C}\oplus(\Omega^{1}_{C})^{-1}\oplus\cdots\oplus(\Omega^{1}_{C})^{-(n-1)}\to\Omega^{1}_{C}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{C}\oplus\cdots\oplus(\Omega^{1}_{C})^{-(n-2)},\quad\begin{bmatrix}0&0&\cdots&0&\otimes-a_{n}\\
\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits&0&\cdots&0&\otimes-a_{n-1}\\
0&\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits&\cdots&0&\otimes-a_{n-2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits&\otimes-a_{1}\end{bmatrix}.

This construction gives another section of the Hitchin morphism
h_{C,\mathrm{GL}_{n}}
, called the companion section.

2.3.2
The companion section for classical groups

Let
G
be a classical group and let
G\to\mathrm{GL}_{N}
be the standard linear representation of
G
.
Then
G
-Higgs bundles on a smooth projective variety

can be described as
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
-Higgs bundles on

with extra structure. (Recall we identify
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
-Higgs bundles with pairs
(E,\theta)
where
E
is a locally free sheaf of rank
N
, and
\theta
is a Higgs field satisfying the integrability condition).

(
A_{n}
)

An
\mathrm{SL}_{n+1}
-Higgs bundle is the data
(E,\theta,\tau)
, where
(E,\theta)
is a
\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}
-Higgs bundle, and

is a trivialization of the determinant line bundle of

, such that
\theta
is “traceless”, i.e. the projection of

to

is zero.

(
B_{n}
and
D_{n}
)

An
\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}
-Higgs bundle (resp.
\mathrm{SO}_{2n}
-Higgs bundle) is the data
(E,\theta,\omega,\tau)
, where
(E,\theta)
is a
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
-Higgs bundle,
\omega:S^{2}E\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
is a fiberwise-nondegenerate symmetric pairing, and
\tau:\det(E)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{\longrightarrow}\rule{0.0pt}{1.72218pt}}}\mathcal{O}_{X}
is a trivialization of the determinant line bundle of
E
, such that the Higgs field
\theta
is anti-self-adjoint with respect to
\omega
, i.e. we must have
\omega(\theta(u),v)+\omega(u,\theta(v))=0
in
\Omega^{1}_{X}
for all local sections
u,v
of

.

(
C_{n}
)

An
\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}
-Higgs bundle is the data
(E,\theta,\omega)
where
(E,\theta)
is a
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
-Higgs bundle, and
\omega:\Lambda^{2}E\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
is a fiberwise-nondegenerate symplectic pairing such that
\theta
is anti-self-adjoint with respect to
\omega
.

For each classical group
G
, we may obtain a
G
-Higgs bundle using the companion
\mathrm{GL}_{N}
-Higgs bundle described in §

.
The construction of these
G
-Higgs bundles dates back at least to Hitchin’s seminal paper
[

]
.
We call these

-Higgs bundles
and recount the construction below following the presentation of

]

Let
A
denote
k[\mathfrak{g}]^{G}
and let

denote
k[\mathfrak{gl}_{N}]^{\mathrm{GL}_{N}}
.
Recall that
\mathfrak{c}_{N}=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\nolimits(A_{N})

\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant cover

We have a map

(

)

G=\mathrm{SL}_{n+1}

Notice that

Let

(

)

.
Recall that

[

4.1

4.2

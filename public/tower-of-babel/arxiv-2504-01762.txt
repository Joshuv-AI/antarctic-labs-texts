A First-Order Linear Energy Stable Scheme for the Cahn-Hilliard Equation with Dynamic Boundary Conditions under the Effect of Hyperbolic Relaxation
Minghui Yu, Rui Chen

Abstract
In this paper we focus on the Cahn-Hilliard equation with dynamic boundary conditions, by adding two hyperbolic relaxation terms to the system. We verify that the energy of the total system is decreasing with time. By adding two stabilization terms, we have constructed a first-order temporal accuracy numerical scheme, which is linear and energy stable. Then we prove that the scheme is of first-order in time by the error estimates. At last we carry out enough numerical results to validate the the temporal convergence and the energy stability of such scheme. Moreover, we have present the differences of the numerical results with and without the hyperbolic terms, which show that the hyperbolic terms can help the total energy decreasing slowly.

Introduction

The Cahn-Hilliard equation was first proposed by John W. Cahn and John E. Hilliard in 1958 to describe the phase separation phenomenon in binary mixtures (such as alloys and solutions)
[
1

The Cahn-Hilliard equation assumes that the material is isotropic and has been widely applied in theoretical studies of phase separation processes
[

As a representative of diffuse interface models, the Cahn-Hilliard equation avoids the explicit interface tracking issues of classical sharp-interface models by dividing the components of the mixture into thin layers, thus improving computational efficiency
[
8
]
. Moreover, this model can naturally handle complex geometries and topological changes of interfaces, significantly simplifying the computation process
[
9
]
. The Cahn-Hilliard equation and its variants have been widely applied in many fields, including block copolymers
[

Compared to the original equation, the equation with the inertial term is a hyperbolic equation with relaxation characteristics, which leads to different mathematical features in numerical solutions and introduces new challenges

There are also some works on designing the energy stable schemes for the hyperbolic Cahn-Hilliard model. Yang et al.

Inspired by the Cahn-Hilliard model
[

We find that this hyperbolic model with the hyperbolic dynamic boundary condition simultaneously satisfies the energy dissipation law and preserves mass conservation in the bulk and on the boundary under specific conditions. Then we utilize a stabilization approach to construct a first-order temporal discretization scheme that is both linear and energy stable. For spatial approximation, we adopt a central finite difference discretization.

The contributions of this paper are present as follows.

1.

To the best of our knowledge, it is the first time to investigate the hyperbolic Cahn-Hilliard equation with the hyperbolic dynamic boundary condition. This hyperbolic model holds the energy dissipation law.

2.

We construct a first-order linear energy stable scheme for the model by the stabilization method. Meanwhile, we give the rigorous analysis to prove the scheme is of first-order time accuracy.

3.

We carry out enough numerical cases to illustrate the time accuracy and the energy decay in the scheme. Moreover we verify that the hyperbolic terms can delay the spinodal decomposition (or coarsening) from the numerical tests.

2
The governing equations

In the first place, we recall that the Liu-Wu model
[
20
]
in the following form:

\displaystyle\phi_{t}=M_{1}\Delta\mu,

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T],

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mu=-\Delta\phi+f(\phi),

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T],

(2.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mu=0,

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Gamma\times(0,T],

(2.3)

\displaystyle\left.\phi\right|_{\Gamma}=\psi,

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Gamma\times(0,T],

(2.4)

\displaystyle\psi_{t}=M_{2}\Delta_{\Gamma}\mu_{\Gamma},

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Gamma\times(0,T],

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mu_{\Gamma}=-\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi+g(\psi)+\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi,

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Gamma\times(0,T],

(2.6)

where
T
is a finite time,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}(d=2,3)
is the bounded domain with its boundary
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
,
{\mathbf{n}}
denotes the unit normal vector on
\Gamma
,
\phi:=\phi({\mathbf{x}},t)
stands for the phase-field variable,
M_{1}
and
M_{1}
are relaxation parameters with the positive value,
\Delta_{\Gamma}
denotes the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\Gamma
.

f(\phi)=F^{\prime}(\phi)
.
F(\phi)
is the double well (Ginzburg-Landau) potential,

\displaystyle F(\phi)=\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}(\phi^{2}-1)^{2},

(2.7)

where
\varepsilon
is a positive constant that measure the width of the interface,
\mu
and
\mu_{\Gamma}
stand for the chemical potentials in the bulk and on the boundary respectively, which are obtained from the total energy.

The total energy reads as follows, consisting of the bulk energy and the surface energy,

\displaystyle E^{total}(\phi,\psi)

\displaystyle=E^{bulk}(\phi)+E^{surf}(\psi),

(2.8)

\displaystyle E^{bulk}\left(\phi\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}F\left(\phi\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla\phi\right|^{2}d{\mathbf{x}},

(2.9)

\displaystyle E^{surf}\left(\psi\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}G\left(\psi\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\right|^{2}dS,

(2.10)

where
\nabla_{\Gamma}
is the tangential or surface gradient operator on
\Gamma
,
g(\psi)=G^{\prime}(\psi)
and
G(\psi)
is also the nonlinear potential. Ones can choose the typical potential for moving contact line problems
[
58
,
59
]
, or choose the double well (Ginzburg-Landau) potential (
2.7
) as surface potential.

It is easy to find that the Liu-Wu model (
2.1
)-(
2.6
) satisfies the following energy dissipation law and the mass conservation law,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}E^{total}(\phi,\psi)=-M_{1}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\mu|^{2}d{\mathbf{x}}-M_{2}\int_{\Gamma}|\nabla_{\Gamma}\mu_{\Gamma}|^{2}dS,

(2.11)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\phi({\mathbf{x}},t)d{\mathbf{x}}=\int_{\Omega}\phi({\mathbf{x}},0)d{\mathbf{x}},\;\int_{\Gamma}\psi({\mathbf{x}},t)dS=\int_{\Gamma}\psi({\mathbf{x}},0)dS.

(2.12)

Remark 2.1
.

Liu-Wu model assumes that there has no mass exchange between the bulk and the boundary. While
Goldstein et al.
[
21
]
have proposed a Cahn-Hilliard model (called GMS model) by assuming that there has mass exchange between the bulk and the boundary. Morover Knopf et al.
[
22
]
have proposed a new model (called KLLM model), which can be regarded as an interpolation between the GMS model
[
21
]
and the Liu-Wu model
[
20
]
. In this model, a relaxation parameter is introduced into the boundary condition. When this parameter approaches zero, the model converges to the GMS model, whereas when it tends to infinity, it reduces to the Liu-Wu model.

By adding two hyperbolic terms to the Liu-Wu model (
2.1
)-(
2.6
), we have the following hyperbolic Cahn-Hilliard equation with the hyperbolic Cahn-Hilliard type dynamic boundary condition,

\displaystyle\beta_{1}\phi_{tt}+\phi_{t}=M_{1}\Delta\mu,

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T],

(2.13)

\displaystyle\mu=-\Delta\phi+f(\phi),

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T],

(2.14)

\displaystyle\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mu=0,

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Gamma\times(0,T],

(2.15)

\displaystyle\phi|_{\Gamma}=\psi,

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Gamma\times(0,T],

(2.16)

\displaystyle\beta_{2}\psi_{tt}+\psi_{t}=M_{2}\Delta_{\Gamma}\mu_{\Gamma},

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Gamma\times(0,T],

(2.17)

\displaystyle\mu_{\Gamma}=-\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi+g(\psi)+\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi,

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Gamma\times(0,T],

(2.18)

where
\beta_{1}\geq 0
and
\beta_{2}\geq 0
are the relaxation parameters. When
\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}=0
, the system reduces to the standard Liu-Wu model (
2.1
)-(
2.6
) that conserves the mass density in the bulk and on the surface. When
\beta_{1}>0
and
\beta_{2}>0
, the mass conservation is maintained only provided that
\int_{\Omega}\phi_{t}({\mathbf{x}},t)d{\mathbf{x}}=0
and
\int_{\Gamma}\psi_{t}({\mathbf{x}},t)dS=0
. To find this, by taking the
L^{2}(\Omega)
inner product of (
2.13
) with
1
and
L^{2}(\Gamma)
inner product of (
2.17
) with
1
respectively, we can derive immediately,

\displaystyle\beta_{1}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{t}({\mathbf{x}},t)d{\mathbf{x}}+\int_{\Omega}\phi_{t}({\mathbf{x}},t)d{\mathbf{x}}=0,

(2.19)

\displaystyle\beta_{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Gamma}\psi_{t}({\mathbf{x}},t)dS+\int_{\Gamma}\psi_{t}({\mathbf{x}},t)dS=0.

(2.20)

Then we deduce the solutions from the ODE systems,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\phi_{t}({\mathbf{x}},t)d{\mathbf{x}}=e^{-\frac{1}{\beta_{1}}t}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{t}({\mathbf{x}},0)d{\mathbf{x}},

(2.21)

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\psi_{t}({\mathbf{x}},t)dS=e^{-\frac{1}{\beta_{2}}t}\int_{\Gamma}\psi_{t}({\mathbf{x}},0)dS.

(2.22)

Thus by setting
\int_{\Omega}\phi_{t}({\mathbf{x}},0)d{\mathbf{x}}=0
and
\int_{\Gamma}\psi_{t}({\mathbf{x}},0)dS=0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\phi_{t}({\mathbf{x}},t)d{\mathbf{x}}=\int_{\Omega}\phi_{tt}({\mathbf{x}},t)d{\mathbf{x}}=0,

(2.23)

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\psi_{t}({\mathbf{x}},t)dS=\int_{\Gamma}\psi_{tt}({\mathbf{x}},t)dS=0.

(2.24)

Define the inverse Laplace operator
\Delta^{-1}
and the inverse Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta_{\Gamma}^{-1}
such that
W_{1}=\Delta^{-1}\omega_{1}
(with
\int_{\Omega}\omega_{1}d{\mathbf{x}}=0
) and
W_{2}=\Delta_{\Gamma}^{-1}\omega_{2}
(with
\int_{\Gamma}\omega_{2}dS=0
), iff

\displaystyle\Delta W_{1}=\omega_{1},\;\int_{\Omega}\omega_{1}d{\mathbf{x}}=0,\;\partial_{{\mathbf{n}}}W_{1}|_{\Gamma}=0,

(2.25)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\Gamma}W_{2}=\omega_{2},\;\int_{\Gamma}\omega_{2}dS=0.

(2.26)

Next we will derive the energy dissipation law for the system (
2.13
)-(
2.18
).
Here and after, for any function
f,g\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, we use
(f,g)_{\Omega}=\int_{\Omega}fgd{\mathbf{x}}
,
(f,g)_{\Gamma}=\int_{\Gamma}fgdS
,
||f||^{2}=(f,f)_{\Omega}
and
||f||_{\Gamma}^{2}=(f,f)_{\Gamma}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

The model (
2.13
)-(
2.18
) is energy stable in the sense that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(\phi,\psi)=-\frac{1}{M_{1}}||\nabla\Delta^{-1}\phi_{t}||^{2}-\frac{1}{M_{2}}||\nabla_{\Gamma}\Delta_{\Gamma}^{-1}\psi_{t}||_{\Gamma}^{2},

(2.27)

where the energy
\mathcal{E}(\phi,\psi)=E^{total}(\phi,\psi)+\frac{\beta_{1}}{2M_{1}}||\nabla\Delta^{-1}\phi_{t}||^{2}+\frac{\beta_{2}}{2M_{2}}||\nabla_{\Gamma}\Delta_{\Gamma}^{-1}\psi_{t}||_{\Gamma}^{2}
.

Proof.

We introduce two variables
\Phi=\phi_{t}
and
\Psi=\psi_{t}
. Since
\int_{\Omega}\Phi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{t}\,dx=0
and
\int_{\Omega}\Psi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\Psi_{t}\,dx=0
, applying the
\Delta^{-1}
operator to (
2.13
) and
\Delta_{\Gamma}^{-1}
operator to (
2.17
), we obtain the following equations,

\displaystyle\beta_{1}\Delta^{-1}\Phi_{t}+\Delta^{-1}\Phi=M_{1}(-\Delta\phi+f(\phi)),

(2.28)

\displaystyle\beta_{2}\Delta^{-1}_{\Gamma}\Psi_{t}+\Delta^{-1}_{\Gamma}\Psi=M_{2}(-\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi+g(\psi)+\partial_{{\mathbf{n}}}\phi).

(2.29)

By taking the
L^{2}(\Omega)
inner product of (
2.28
) with
\frac{1}{M_{1}}\Phi
and the
L^{2}(\Gamma)
inner product of (
2.29
) with
\frac{1}{M_{2}}\Psi
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{1}}{M_{1}}(\Delta^{-1}\Phi_{t},\Phi)_{\Omega}+\frac{1}{M_{1}}(\Delta^{-1}\Phi,\Phi)_{\Omega}=-(\partial_{{\mathbf{n}}}\phi,\phi_{t})_{\Gamma}+\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}(\frac{|\nabla\phi|^{2}}{2}+F(\phi))d{\mathbf{x}},

(2.30)

\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{2}}{M_{2}}(\Delta^{-1}_{\Gamma}\Psi_{t},\Psi)_{\Gamma}+\frac{1}{M_{2}}(\Delta^{-1}_{\Gamma}\Psi,\Psi)_{\Gamma}=(\partial_{{\mathbf{n}}}\phi,\phi_{t})_{\Gamma}+\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Gamma}(\frac{|\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi|^{2}}{2}+G(\psi))dS.

(2.31)

We define
p=\Delta^{-1}\Phi
and
q=\Delta_{\Gamma}^{-1}\Psi
. Substituting these into the inner product formula above and simplifying, we obtain,

\displaystyle(\Delta^{-1}\Phi,\Phi)_{\Omega}=(p,\Delta p)_{\Omega}=-\|\nabla p\|^{2},

(2.32)

\displaystyle(\Delta^{-1}\Phi_{t},\Phi)_{\Omega}=(\Phi_{t},\Delta^{-1}\Phi)_{\Omega}=(\Delta p_{t},p)_{\Omega}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla p\|^{2},

(2.33)

\displaystyle(\Delta^{-1}_{\Gamma}\Psi,\Psi)_{\Gamma}=(q,\Delta_{\Gamma}q)_{\Gamma}=-\|\nabla_{\Gamma}q\|_{\Gamma}^{2},

(2.34)

\displaystyle(\Delta^{-1}_{\Gamma}\Psi_{t},\Psi)_{\Gamma}=(\Psi_{t},\Delta^{-1}_{\Gamma}\Psi)_{\Gamma}=(\Delta_{\Gamma}q_{t},q)_{\Gamma}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla_{\Gamma}q\|_{\Gamma}^{2}.

(2.35)

By combining the above formulas, we obtain the following energy dissipation law,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\int_{\Omega}(\frac{|\nabla\phi|^{2}}{2}+F(\phi)+\frac{\beta_{1}}{2M_{1}}|\nabla p|^{2})d{\mathbf{x}}+\int_{\Gamma}(\frac{|\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi|^{2}}{2}+G(\psi)+\frac{\beta_{2}}{2M_{2}}|\nabla_{\Gamma}q|^{2})dS\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{M_{1}}\|\nabla p\|^{2}-\frac{1}{M_{2}}\|\nabla_{\Gamma}q\|_{\Gamma}^{2}\leq 0.

(2.36)

∎

3
A first-order energy stable scheme

In this section, we directly present the numerical scheme of the equation as follows, then prove the energy stability of the numerical scheme, and conduct a simple error analysis.

Assuming that
\phi^{n}
and
\phi^{n-1}
with
n\geq 1
are known, we update
\phi^{n+1}
as follows,

\displaystyle\beta_{1}\frac{\Phi^{n+1}-\Phi^{n}}{\tau}+\Phi^{n+1}=M_{1}\Delta\mu^{n+1},\;

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mu^{n+1}=-\Delta\phi^{n+1}+f(\phi^{n})+s_{1}(\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}),\;

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\Phi^{n+1}=\frac{\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}}{\tau},\;

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\phi^{n+1}|_{\Gamma}=\psi^{n+1},\;

\displaystyle\text{on }\Gamma,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\beta_{2}\frac{\Psi^{n+1}-\Psi^{n}}{\tau}+\Psi^{n+1}=M_{2}\Delta_{\Gamma}\mu_{\Gamma}^{n+1},\;

\displaystyle\text{on }\Gamma,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mu_{\Gamma}^{n+1}=-\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi^{n+1}+g(\psi^{n})+\partial_{{\mathbf{n}}}\phi^{n+1}+s_{2}(\psi^{n+1}-\psi^{n}),\;

\displaystyle\text{on }\Gamma,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\Psi^{n+1}=\frac{\psi^{n+1}-\psi^{n}}{\tau},\;

\displaystyle\text{on }\Gamma,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{{\mathbf{n}}}\mu^{n+1}=0,\;

\displaystyle\text{on }\Gamma,

(3.8)

where
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
are two stabilizers to be determined,
N
is the number of time steps with
1\leq n<N
, and
\tau=T/N
is the time step size. Next we will show the energy stability of the scheme.

Theorem 3.1
.

If
\displaystyle s_{1}\geq\frac{1}{2}\max_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}}F^{\prime\prime}(\xi)
and
\displaystyle s_{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\max_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}}G^{\prime\prime}(\eta)
, the scheme (
3.1
)-(
3.8
) is energy stable in the sense that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{E}(\phi^{n+1},\psi^{n+1})-\mathcal{E}(\phi^{n},\psi^{n})}{\tau}\leq-\frac{1}{M_{1}}\|\nabla p^{n}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{M_{2}}\|\nabla_{\Gamma}q^{n}\|_{\Gamma}^{2},

(3.9)

where
p^{n}=\Delta^{-1}\Phi^{n}
and
q^{n}=\Delta^{-1}_{\Gamma}\Psi^{n}
, and the energy

\mathcal{E}(\phi^{n},\psi^{n})=\frac{||\nabla\phi^{n}||^{2}}{2}+(F(\phi^{n}),1)_{\Omega}+\frac{\beta_{1}}{2M_{1}}||\nabla p^{n}||^{2}+\frac{||\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi^{n}||_{\Gamma}^{2}}{2}+(G(\psi^{n}),1)_{\Gamma}+\frac{\beta_{2}}{2M_{2}}||\nabla_{\Gamma}q^{n}||_{\Gamma}^{2}.

(3.10)

Proof.

By applying the inverse Laplace operator
\Delta^{-1}
to (
3.1
), we obtain,

\beta_{1}\Delta^{-1}\frac{\Phi^{n+1}-\Phi^{n}}{\tau}+\Delta^{-1}\Phi^{n+1}=M_{1}\mu^{n+1}.

(3.11)

By taking the
L^{2}(\Omega)
inner product of (
3.11
) with
\frac{1}{M_{1}}\Phi^{n+1}
, we have

\frac{\beta_{1}}{M_{1}}(\Delta^{-1}\frac{\Phi^{n+1}-\Phi^{n}}{\tau},\Phi^{n+1})_{\Omega}+\frac{1}{M_{1}}(\Delta^{-1}\Phi^{n+1},\Phi^{n+1})_{\Omega}=(\mu^{n+1},\Phi^{n+1})_{\Omega}.

(3.12)

Noticing that
p^{n+1}=\Delta^{-1}\Phi^{n+1}
, we deduce

\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{1}}{M_{1}}(\Delta^{-1}\frac{\Phi^{n+1}-\Phi^{n}}{\tau},\Phi^{n+1})_{\Omega}+\frac{1}{M_{1}}(\Delta^{-1}\Phi^{n+1},\Phi^{n+1})_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{1}}{M_{1}\tau}(p^{n+1}-p^{n},\Delta p^{n+1})_{\Omega}+\frac{1}{M_{1}}(p^{n+1},\Delta p^{n+1})_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\beta_{1}}{M_{1}\tau}(\nabla p^{n+1}-\nabla p^{n},\nabla p^{n+1})_{\Omega}-\frac{1}{M_{1}}||\nabla p^{n+1}||^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\beta_{1}}{2M_{1}\tau}(||\nabla p^{n+1}||^{2}-||\nabla p^{n}||^{2}+||\nabla p^{n+1}-\nabla p^{n}||^{2})-\frac{1}{M_{1}}||\nabla p^{n+1}||^{2},

(3.13)

and

\displaystyle(\mu^{n+1},\Phi^{n+1})_{\Omega}=(\mu^{n+1},\frac{\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}}{\tau})_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\frac{\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}}{\tau},-\Delta\phi^{n+1}+f(\phi^{n})+s_{1}(\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}))_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\frac{\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}}{\tau},-\Delta\phi^{n+1})_{\Omega}+(\frac{\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}}{\tau},f(\phi^{n}))_{\Omega}+(\frac{\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}}{\tau},s_{1}(\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}))_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\partial_{{\mathbf{n}}}\phi,\frac{\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}}{\tau})_{\Gamma}+\frac{1}{\tau}(\nabla\phi^{n+1}-\nabla\phi^{n},\nabla\phi^{n+1})_{\Omega}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\tau}(F(\phi^{n+1})-F(\phi^{n}),1)_{\Omega}-\frac{F^{\prime\prime}(\xi)}{2\tau}||\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}||^{2}+\frac{s_{1}}{\tau}||\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}||^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\partial_{{\mathbf{n}}}\phi,\frac{\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}}{\tau})_{\Gamma}+\frac{1}{2\tau}(||\nabla\phi^{n+1}||^{2}-||\nabla\phi^{n}||^{2}+||\nabla\phi^{n+1}-\nabla\phi^{n}||^{2})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\tau}(F(\phi^{n+1})-F(\phi^{n}),1)_{\Omega}+(\frac{s_{1}}{\tau}-\frac{F^{\prime\prime}(\xi)}{2\tau})||\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}||^{2},

(3.14)

where we use the identity

\displaystyle(2a,(a-b))=|a|^{2}-|b|^{2}+|a-b|^{2},

(3.15)

and the Taylor expansion

\displaystyle(f\left(\phi^{n}\right),\left(\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}\right))_{\Omega}=(F\left(\phi^{n+1}\right)-F\left(\phi^{n}\right),1)_{\Omega}-\frac{F^{\prime\prime}(\xi)}{2}||\phi^{n+1}-\phi^{n}||^{2}.

(3.16)

Similarly, by applying the inverse Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta_{\Gamma}^{-1}
to (
3.5
), we obtain

\beta_{2}\Delta_{\Gamma}^{-1}\frac{\Psi^{n+1}-\Psi^{n}}{\tau}+\Delta_{\Gamma}^{-1}\Psi^{n+1}=M_{2}\mu^{n+1}_{\Gamma}.

(3.17)

By taking the
L^{2}(\Gamma)
inner product of (
3.17
) with
\frac{1}{M_{2}}\Psi^{n+1}
, we have

\frac{\beta_{2}}{M_{2}}(\Delta_{\Gamma}^{-1}\frac{\Psi^{n+1}-\Psi^{n}}{\tau},\Psi^{n+1})_{\Gamma}+\frac{1}{M_{2}}(\Delta^{-1}_{\Gamma}\Psi^{n+1},\Psi^{n+1})_{\Gamma}=(\mu_{\Gamma}^{n+1},\Psi^{n+1})_{\Gamma}.

(3.18)

Noting that
q^{n+1}=\Delta_{\Gamma}^{-1}\Psi^{n+1}
, we can get

\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{2}}{M_{2}}(\Delta_{\Gamma}^{-1}\frac{\Psi^{n+1}-\Psi^{n}}{\tau},\Psi^{n+1})_{\Gamma}+\frac{1}{M_{2}}(\Delta^{-1}_{\Gamma}\Psi^{n+1},\Psi^{n+1})_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{2}}{M_{2}\tau}(q^{n+1}-q^{n},\Delta_{\Gamma}q^{n+1})_{\Gamma}+\frac{1}{M_{2}}(q^{n+1},\Delta_{\Gamma}q^{n+1})_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\beta_{2}}{M_{2}\tau}(\nabla_{\Gamma}q^{n+1}-\nabla_{\Gamma}q^{n},\nabla_{\Gamma}q^{n+1})_{\Gamma}-\frac{1}{M_{2}}||\nabla_{\Gamma}q^{n+1}||_{\Gamma}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\frac{\psi^{n+1}-\psi^{n}}{\tau},-\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi^{n+1}+g(\psi^{n})+\partial_{{\mathbf{n}}}\phi^{n+1}+s_{2}(\psi^{n+1}-\psi^{n}))_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{M_{1}}||\nabla p^{n+1}||^{2}+\frac{1}{M_{2}}||\nabla_{\Gamma}q^{n+1}||_{\Gamma}^{2}=0.

(3.22)

Therefore, under the conditions

(3.23)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\|\nabla e^{m+1}_{\phi}\|^{2}+\|e^{m+1}_{\phi}\|^{2}+\|\nabla_{\Gamma}e^{m+1}_{\psi}\|_{\Gamma}^{2}+\|e^{m+1}_{\psi}\|_{\Gamma}^{2})+\frac{\beta_{1}}{2M_{1}}\|\nabla u^{m+1}\|^{2}+\frac{\beta_{2}}{2M_{2}}\|\nabla_{\Gamma}v^{m+1}\|_{\Gamma}^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=0}^{m}\bigg(\frac{\beta_{1}}{2M_{1}}\|\nabla(u^{n+1}-u^{n})\|^{2}+\frac{\tau}{4M_{1}}\|\nabla u^{n+1}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla(e_{\phi}^{n+1}-e_{\phi}^{n})\|^{2}+

\displaystyle+{(s_{1}+\frac{1}{2})}\|e_{\phi}^{n+1}-e_{\phi}^{n}\|^{2}+{(s_{2}+\frac{1}{2})}\|e_{\psi}^{n+1}-e_{\psi}^{n}\|^{2}_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle+\frac{\beta_{2}}{2M_{2}}\|\nabla_{\Gamma}(v^{n+1}-v^{n})\|_{\Gamma}^{2}+\frac{\tau}{4M_{2}}\|\nabla_{\Gamma}v^{n+1}\|_{\Gamma}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla_{\Gamma}(e_{\psi}^{n+1}-e_{\psi}^{n})\|_{\Gamma}^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tilde{C}(m+1)\tau^{3}+\tilde{C}\tau\sum_{n=0}^{m}\bigg(\|\nabla e_{\phi}^{n+1}\|^{2}+\|e_{\phi}^{n+1}\|^{2}+\|e_{\phi}^{n+1}-e_{\phi}^{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\nabla_{\Gamma}e_{\psi}^{n+1}\|^{2}_{\Gamma}+\|e_{\psi}^{n+1}\|^{2}_{\Gamma}+\|e_{\psi}^{n+1}-e_{\psi}^{n}\|^{2}_{\Gamma}\bigg),

(4.57)

Where
\tilde{C}
is a constant independent of
\tau
and
||e_{\phi}^{0}||=||e_{\psi}^{0}||_{\Gamma}=||\nabla e_{\phi}^{0}||=||\nabla e_{\psi}^{0}||_{\Gamma}=||\nabla u^{0}||=||\nabla v^{0}||_{\Gamma}=0
.

Define that

\displaystyle I_{m}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big(\|\nabla e^{m+1}_{\phi}\|^{2}+\|e^{m+1}_{\phi}\|^{2}+\|\nabla_{\Gamma}e^{m+1}_{\psi}\|_{\Gamma}^{2}+\|e^{m+1}_{\psi}\|_{\Gamma}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle+{(s_{1}+\frac{1}{2})}\|e_{\phi}^{m+1}-e_{\phi}^{m}\|^{2}+{(s_{2}+\frac{1}{2})}\|e_{\psi}^{m+1}-e_{\psi}^{m}\|^{2}_{\Gamma},

(4.58)

and

\displaystyle S_{m}=\sum_{n=0}^{m}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla(e_{\phi}^{n+1}-e_{\phi}^{n})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla_{\Gamma}(e_{\psi}^{n+1}-e_{\psi}^{n})\|_{\Gamma}^{2}\bigg).

(4.59)

Then dropping the positive terms from the left side of (
4.57
), we have

\displaystyle I_{m}+S_{m}\lesssim\tau^{2}+\tau\sum_{n=0}^{m}I_{n}.

(4.60)

According to the discrete Grönwall’s inequality, there exist constants
\tilde{c}_{0}
and
C_{0}
, such that

\displaystyle I_{m}+S_{m}\leq\tilde{c}_{0}\tau^{2},

(4.61)

where
\tilde{c}_{0}
is independent of
\tau
and
\tau\leq C_{0}
.
Therefore we obtain the error estimates for
e^{m+1}_{\phi}
and
e^{m+1}_{\psi}
.
∎

6
Conclusion

In this paper we
have investigated the hyperbolic Cahn-Hilliard equation with the hyperbolic Cahn-Hilliard type dynamic boundary condition. By adding two stabilizing terms, we have designed a linear, first-order in time and energy stable scheme for the system. Meanwhile, we have also proved that the scheme is of first order in time by the error analysis. Finally there are enough numerical cases to show the temporal convergence, the mass conservation in the bulk and on the boundary, and the energy stability of the scheme. We also find that the hyperbolic terms can help the system to delay reaching the steady state.

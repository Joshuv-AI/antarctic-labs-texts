Lipschitz regularity of harmonic quasiconformal maps between Lyapunov domains in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n}
Anton Gjokaj, David Kalaj

Abstract
. We prove that every sense-preserving harmonic K K –quasiconformal homeomorphism f : D → Ω f\colon D\to\Omega between Lyapunov domains (equivalently, bounded C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} domains) in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , α ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] \alpha\in(0,1] , is globally Lipschitz on D ¯ \overline{D} . The argument is based on a boundary iteration scheme: an initial Hölder modulus for the boundary trace (coming from quasiconformality) is improved via the C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} graph representation of ∂ Ω \partial\Omega , yielding higher Hölder regularity for the normal component. This boundary gain is converted into a near-boundary gradient bound for harmonic functions through a basepoint boundary Hölder-to-gradient estimate obtained by flattening the boundary and using local harmonic-measure bounds. Quasiconformality then propagates the resulting control from one component to the full differential, and iteration gives boundedness of | D ​ f | |Df| up to the boundary. Along the way we briefly survey several standard tools from the theory of quasiconformal harmonic mappings (QCH), including boundary Hölder continuity, distortion of derivatives, and boundary-to-interior propagation principles that enter the iteration.

1  Quasiconformal harmonic mappings: an introduction

A
harmonic mapping
is a function
f:D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
whose components are harmonic
(equivalently,
\Delta f=0
in the distributional sense).

A
K
–quasiconformal mapping
is a
sense-preserving homeomorphism
f\in W^{1,n}_{\mathrm{loc}}(D,\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that there exists

K\geq 1
with

|Df(x)|^{\,n}\leq K\,J_{f}(x)\qquad\text{for a.e.\ }x\in D,

where
Df(x)
is the (a.e. defined) differential,
|Df(x)|
is its operator norm, and

J_{f}(x)=\det Df(x)
(in particular
J_{f}>0
a.e.).

A
quasiconformal harmonic mapping
(often abbreviated
HQC
) is a mapping that is
both harmonic and quasiconformal.

The modern study of HQC mappings is often traced back to Martio’s pioneering work
on harmonic quasiconformal self-maps of the disk
[
35
]
.
A central theme is
metric regularity
: under geometric or analytic hypotheses,
HQC homeomorphisms tend to be Lipschitz or even bi-Lipschitz (sometimes with respect to
Euclidean, hyperbolic, or quasihyperbolic-type metrics). In the planar case, sharp
boundary-to-interior principles and explicit distortion bounds can be obtained by combining
harmonic analysis tools (Poisson kernels, Hilbert transform, harmonic measure) with the
geometric theory of quasiconformal maps.

A prominent line of results concerns
bi-Lipschitz characterizations
and boundary behavior.
For instance, Pavlović established an equivalence between quasiconformality of a harmonic
self-homeomorphism of
\mathbb{D}
and Euclidean bi-Lipschitz behavior (with boundary criteria in terms
of the Hilbert transform)
[
39
]
. Partyka and Sakan developed explicit
bi-Lipschitz constants for harmonic quasiconformal self-maps of
\mathbb{D}

[
37
]
,
and later Partyka–Sakan–Zhu studied further structural constraints on HQC maps via conditions
on the analytic part
[
38
]
. Mateljević and Vuorinen investigated HQC
maps as quasi-isometries and Lipschitz mappings with respect to quasihyperbolic-type metrics

[
36
]
. In higher dimensions, issues related to the possible vanishing
of the Jacobian complicate the picture; work of Božin and Mateljević illustrates this
via Jacobian bounds and co-Lipschitz phenomena for harmonic
K
-quasiconformal maps

[
5
]
. Contributions of Arsenović, Kojić, Mateljević, Astala and
Todorčević form another important part of the literature, including
Lipschitz continuity of harmonic quasiregular maps and metric bi-Lipschitz properties

[
3
,
34
,
40
,
2
]
.

Within this circle of ideas, a particularly influential body of work is due to
Kalaj and collaborators. One set of results treats
boundary regularity and Lipschitz
continuity
for harmonic quasiconformal maps between planar domains with various smoothness
assumptions on the boundary (e.g.
C^{1,\alpha}
or Dini smooth Jordan curves),
often yielding global Lipschitz (or bi-Lipschitz) estimates

[
19
,
29
,
33
]
.
Another direction studies
HQC solutions of elliptic equations
beyond
\Delta f=0
,
for example quasiconformal self-maps of
\mathbb{D}
satisfying Poisson-type equations
\Delta f=g

(and related Dirichlet data), establishing bi-Lipschitz bounds under analytic control of the
Laplacian
[
20
,
30
,
31
]
.
Kalaj also developed sharp inequalities for harmonic mappings and quantitative harmonic-analysis
estimates, such as Riesz-type inequalities
[
32
]
and Heinz–Schwarz type bounds
in higher dimensions
[
28
]
, and explored links between HQC maps,
harmonic measure, and Muckenhoupt weights (yielding, among other things, an HQC version of
Lindelöf-type boundary regularity)
[
27
]
.

Historically, the broad theory of harmonic mappings (including univalence criteria via the
shear construction) provides essential background and motivation for many HQC questions;
a classical reference is
[
8
]
. For a modern perspective on the
interaction of quasiconformal mappings with elliptic PDE (including the Beltrami equation and
regularity theory), one may consult general sources such as
[
1
]
,
which also contextualize why “elliptic operator + boundary value problem” viewpoints are so
effective in this area.

Organization of the paper.

In this paper we improve the result of
[
11
]
by showing that the same conclusion holds
under the sole assumption that the source domain has
C^{1,\alpha}
boundary.
Section
3
contains the analytic core of the argument.
We establish a basepoint boundary Hölder principle near a
C^{1,\alpha}
boundary point
x_{0}
:
assuming a
C^{0,\mu}
control of the trace on
\Gamma=\partial D\cap B(x_{0},r_{0})
,

|u(\xi)-u(x_{0})|\leq M|\xi-x_{0}|^{\mu},\qquad\xi\in\Gamma,

we obtain quantitative interior control along the inward normal ray

x=x_{0}+t\nu(x_{0})
,
0<t<cr_{0}
. For
0<\mu<1
, Lemma
3.1
yields

\sup_{y\in D\cap B(x,t/2)}|u(y)-u(x_{0})|\lesssim\Bigl(M+\frac{A}{r_{0}^{\mu}}\Bigr)t^{\mu},\qquad A:=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(D\cap B(x_{0},r_{0}))},

and, in particular,

|u(x)-u(x_{0})|\lesssim\Bigl(M+\frac{A}{r_{0}^{\mu}}\Bigr)t^{\mu},\qquad|\nabla u(x)|\lesssim\Bigl(M+\frac{A}{r_{0}^{\mu}}\Bigr)t^{\mu-1}.

In the case
\mu>1
, the corresponding bounds are recorded in Lemma
3.2
:

|u(x)-u(x_{0})|\lesssim\Bigl(M\,r_{0}^{\mu-1}+\frac{A}{r_{0}}\Bigr)t,\qquad|\nabla u(x)|\lesssim\Bigl(M\,r_{0}^{\mu-1}+\frac{A}{r_{0}}\Bigr).

Finally, combining these tools with a boundary exponent-improvement/iteration scheme, in Section
4
,
we obtain Lipschitz regularity up to the boundary for harmonic quasiconformal homeomorphisms
between bounded
C^{1,\alpha}
domains (Theorem
2.1
).
A brief remark on the planar case is included at the end of the section.

2  New results

The authors proved in
[
11
]
that if
f:\mathbf{B}^{n}\to\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}

is a harmonic
K
–quasiconformal mapping and
\partial\Omega\in C^{1,\alpha}
,
then
f
is Lipschitz continuous in
\mathbf{B}^{n}
.

In this work we improve that result by allowing an arbitrary
C^{1,\alpha}
source domain
D

(in place of the unit ball), while still assuming
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity of the target boundary.

Theorem 2.1

(Lipschitz regularity for harmonic quasiconformal maps)
.

Let
f:D\to\Omega
be a
K
–quasiconformal harmonic mapping.
If
\partial D
and
\partial\Omega
are
C^{1,\alpha}
with
\alpha\in(0,1)
, then
f
is Lipschitz
on
\overline{D}
; equivalently, there exists
L>0
such that

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq L\,|x-y|,\qquad\text{for all }x,y\in\overline{D}.

3  Auxiliary results

As indicated above, we now prove a localized boundary-to-interior estimate showing that a basepoint

\mu
-Hölder control of the boundary values at
x_{0}
propagates into
\mu
-Hölder control in the interior and
yields the corresponding gradient bound
|\nabla u(x)|\lesssim t^{\mu-1}
along the inward normal through
x_{0}
,
where
t
denotes the normal distance from
x_{0}
.

Lemma 3.1

(Basepoint boundary Hölder at
x_{0}

\Rightarrow
interior Hölder and gradient control along the normal)
.

Let
D\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}
be a domain and let
x_{0}\in\partial D
.
Assume that
\partial D
is
C^{1,\alpha}
in a neighborhood of
x_{0}
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
Fix
0<\mu<1
.

Then there exist
r_{0}>0
,
c\in(0,1)
and
C>0
(depending only on the local
C^{1,\alpha}
character
of
\partial D
near
x_{0}
and on
\mu
) such that the following holds.

Let
u
be harmonic in
\Omega_{x_{0}}:=D\cap B(x_{0},r_{0})
and continuous on
\overline{\Omega_{x_{0}}}
.
Assume that on the boundary patch
\Gamma:=\partial D\cap B(x_{0},r_{0})
,

(1)

|u(\xi)-u(x_{0})|\leq M|\xi-x_{0}|^{\mu},\qquad\xi\in\Gamma,

and set
A:=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{x_{0}})}
.
Let
\nu(x_{0})
be the inward unit normal to
\partial D
at
x_{0}
.

Then for every

x=x_{0}+t\,\nu(x_{0})\in D,\qquad 0<t<cr_{0},

we have the “tent” estimate

(2)

\sup_{y\in D\cap B(x,t/2)}|u(y)-u(x_{0})|\;\leq\;C\Big(M+\frac{A}{r_{0}^{\mu}}\Big)\,t^{\mu}.

In particular,

(3)

|u(x)-u(x_{0})|\;\leq\;C\Big(M+\frac{A}{r_{0}^{\mu}}\Big)\,t^{\mu},

and the full gradient satisfies

(4)

|\nabla u(x)|\;\leq\;C\Big(M+\frac{A}{r_{0}^{\mu}}\Big)\,t^{\mu-1}.

Equivalently, writing
\delta_{D}(x):=\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial D)
, and noting that

\delta_{D}(x)=t
along this inward normal ray for
t<cr_{0}
, we obtain

|u(x)-u(x_{0})|\leq C\Big(M+\frac{A}{r_{0}^{\mu}}\Big)\,\delta_{D}(x)^{\mu},\qquad|\nabla u(x)|\leq C\Big(M+\frac{A}{r_{0}^{\mu}}\Big)\,\delta_{D}(x)^{\mu-1}.

Proof.

Translate/rotate so that
x_{0}=0
and
\nu(x_{0})=e_{n}
. Rescale by
x=r_{0}z
; it suffices to prove the
claim for
r_{0}=1
and then scale back.
Thus set

\Omega_{0}:=D\cap B(0,1),\qquad\Gamma:=\partial D\cap B(0,1),\qquad\Sigma:=\partial\Omega_{0}\setminus\Gamma=\partial B(0,1)\cap\overline{D},

and
A:=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{0})}
.
Choose
c\in(0,1)
so small that
B(0,2c)\cap\Sigma=\varnothing
and that the segment

\{te_{n}:0<t<2c\}\subset D
has
0
as unique nearest boundary point (possible by
C^{1}
regularity).

Fix
t\in(0,c)
and set
x:=te_{n}
. Then
\delta_{D}(x)=t
.
Moreover, for every
y\in B(x,t/2)\cap D
one has
|y|\lesssim t
and
\delta_{D}(y)\simeq t

(with constants depending only on the local
C^{1}
geometry).

C^{1,\alpha}
boundary patch.

The boundary of
\Omega_{x_{0}}=D\cap B(x_{0},r_{0})
is decomposed as
\partial\Omega_{x_{0}}=\Gamma\cup\Sigma
,
where
\Gamma=\partial D\cap B(x_{0},r_{0})
and
\Sigma=\partial B(x_{0},r_{0})\cap\overline{D}.

Let
\omega^{y}
be harmonic measure for
\Omega_{0}
with pole at
y
. Since
u
is harmonic in
\Omega_{0}

and continuous on
\partial\Omega_{0}
,

u(y)-u(0)=\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}(u(\zeta)-u(0))\,d\omega^{y}(\zeta).

Using (
1
) on
\Gamma
and
|u(\zeta)-u(0)|\leq 2A
on
\Sigma
,

(5)

|u(y)-u(0)|\leq M\int_{\Gamma}|\zeta|^{\mu}\,d\omega^{y}(\zeta)+2A\,\omega^{y}(\Sigma).

\Sigma
contribution.

Let
w
be harmonic in
\Omega_{0}
with
w=0
on
\Gamma
and
w=1
on
\Sigma
.
Then
w(y)=\omega^{y}(\Sigma)
.
Since
B(0,2c)\cap\Sigma=\varnothing

on
\partial\Omega_{0}\cap B(0,2c)
.
The boundary Lipschitz estimate in

domains (after flattening/scaling, see

[

) gives

\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{0}\cap B(0,c))}\leq C,

hence
w(y)\leq C\,\delta_{D}(y)\lesssim t
, i.e.

(6)

\omega^{y}(\Sigma)\leq Ct.

\Gamma
contribution.

For
s>0
define
F_{y}(s):=\omega^{y}(\Gamma\setminus\overline{B}(0,s))
.
Since
\Gamma\subset B(0,1)
,
F_{y}(s)=0
for
s\geq 1
.
By layer-cake,

(7)

\int_{\Gamma}|\zeta|^{\mu}\,d\omega^{y}(\zeta)=\mu\int_{0}^{1}s^{\mu-1}F_{y}(s)\,ds.

For
s\in(\delta_{D}(y),1]
one has the decay

(8)

F_{y}(s)\leq C\,\frac{\delta_{D}(y)}{s},

proved by comparing
F_{y}(s)
with the harmonic function that vanishes on
\Gamma\cap B(0,s)
and
equals
1
on
\partial\Omega_{0}\setminus(\Gamma\cap B(0,s))
, then applying a boundary Lipschitz estimate
after flattening/scaling (again of the type
[
10
, Corollary 8.36]
).
Splitting (
7
) at
s=\delta_{D}(y)
and using

yields

\int_{\Gamma}|\zeta|^{\mu}\,d\omega^{y}(\zeta)\leq\mu\int_{0}^{\delta_{D}(y)}s^{\mu-1}\,ds+C\mu\,\delta_{D}(y)\int_{\delta_{D}(y)}^{1}s^{\mu-2}\,ds\leq C\,\delta_{D}(y)^{\mu}\lesssim t^{\mu}.

Together with (
6
) and (
5
), this gives

|u(y)-u(0)|\leq C(Mt^{\mu}+At)\leq C(M+A)\,t^{\mu},

since
0<t<1
and

t\leq t^{\mu}
. Taking the supremum over
y\in B(x,t/2)\cap D

2
) (for
r_{0}=1

(
3
) (for
r_{0}=1
).

Finally, apply the interior gradient estimate at
x

|\nabla u(x)|\leq\frac{C}{t}\,\sup_{B(x,t/2)\cap D}|u-u(0)|\leq C(M+A)\,t^{\mu-1},

which is (
4
) for
r_{0}=1
.

Rescaling back to general

gives the factor
M+\frac{A}{r_{0}^{\mu}}
in
(
2

4
), and completes the proof.
∎

We do not treat the endpoint case
\mu=1
in Lemma
3.1
, since the
harmonic-measure estimate of
\int_{\Gamma}|\zeta|^{\mu}\,d\omega^{y}(\zeta)
then produces the logarithmic factor

\int_{\delta_{D}(y)}^{1}\frac{ds}{s}=\log\!\frac{1}{\delta_{D}(y)},

and hence only yields logarithmic control of the gradient. We therefore avoid the endpoint
\mu=1
in the
iteration. Once the exponent satisfies
\mu>1
, the logarithmic term disappears and the same computation yields
a uniform gradient bound. For convenience, we record the corresponding estimate in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2

(Addendum to Lemma
3.1
: the case
\mu>1
)
.

Assume the hypotheses and notation of Lemma
3.1
, except that
(
1
) holds for some exponent
\mu>1
.
Then there exist
r_{0}>0
,
c\in(0,1)
and
C>0
(depending only on the local
C^{1,\alpha}
character
of
\partial D
near
x_{0}
and on
\mu
) such that for every

x=x_{0}+t\,\nu(x_{0})\in D,\qquad 0<t<cr_{0},

we have

(9)

|u(x)-u(x_{0})|\;\leq\;C\Big(M\,r_{0}^{\mu-1}+\frac{A}{r_{0}}\Big)\,t,

and

(10)

|\nabla u(x)|\;\leq\;C\Big(M\,r_{0}^{\mu-1}+\frac{A}{r_{0}}\Big).

Equivalently, along this inward normal ray (where
\delta_{D}(x)=t
for
t<cr_{0}
),

|u(x)-u(x_{0})|\leq C\Big(M\,r_{0}^{\mu-1}+\frac{A}{r_{0}}\Big)\,\delta_{D}(x),\qquad|\nabla u(x)|\leq C\Big(M\,r_{0}^{\mu-1}+\frac{A}{r_{0}}\Big).

Proof.

The proof is identical to that of Lemma
3.1
, except for the estimate of

\int_{\Gamma}|\zeta|^{\mu}\,d\omega^{y}(\zeta).

Using the same notation and the layer–cake representation,

\int_{\Gamma}|\zeta|^{\mu}\,d\omega^{y}(\zeta)=\mu\int_{0}^{1}s^{\mu-1}F_{y}(s)\,ds,

and the same decay estimate (
8
) for
s\in(\delta_{D}(y),1]
,
we split at
s=\delta_{D}(y)
to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}|\zeta|^{\mu}\,d\omega^{y}(\zeta)

\displaystyle\leq\mu\int_{0}^{\delta_{D}(y)}s^{\mu-1}\,ds+C\mu\,\delta_{D}(y)\int_{\delta_{D}(y)}^{1}s^{\mu-2}\,ds

\displaystyle=\delta_{D}(y)^{\mu}+C\mu\,\delta_{D}(y)\,\frac{1-\delta_{D}(y)^{\mu-1}}{\mu-1}\;\leq\;C\,\delta_{D}(y),

since

and
\delta_{D}(y)\in(0,1)

yields the stated conclusions.

4  The proof of the main result

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

We prove a uniform bound for
|Df|
in a collar neighborhood of
\partial D
and then combine it
with standard interior estimates.

0) Local charts and tubular neighborhoods.

Fix
x_{0}\in\partial D
and set
q_{0}:=f(x_{0})\in\partial\Omega
,
using the continuous extension of
f
to
\overline{D}
provided in Step 1.

Since
\partial D
is
C^{1,\alpha}
near
x_{0}
, there exists a neighborhood
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of
x_{0}

and
r_{D}>0
such that every point

x\in\mathcal{C}_{D}:=\{x\in D\cap U:\delta_{D}(x)<r_{D}\}

has a unique nearest boundary point
\pi(x)\in\partial D\cap U
and admits the normal
representation

(11)

x=\pi(x)+\delta_{D}(x)\,\nu_{D}(\pi(x)),

where
\nu_{D}(\xi)
denotes the inward unit normal to
\partial D
at
\xi
.

Similarly, since
\partial\Omega
is
C^{1,\alpha}
and compact, we may choose a neighborhood
V

of
\partial\Omega
and constants
r_{\Omega}>0
,
C_{\Omega}>0
with the following property:
for every
q\in\partial\Omega
there is a rigid motion
T_{q}(z)=R_{q}(z-q)
(with
R_{q}
orthogonal)
such that in the coordinates
y=T_{q}(z)
the boundary
\partial\Omega
near
q
is a
C^{1,\alpha}

graph

\partial\Omega_{q}:=T_{q}(\partial\Omega)\cap B(0,r_{\Omega})=\{(y^{\prime},y_{n}):y_{n}=\Phi_{q}(y^{\prime})\},

where
\Phi_{q}\in C^{1,\alpha}
,
\Phi_{q}(0)=0
,
\nabla\Phi_{q}(0)=0
, and uniformly for all such
q
,

(12)

|\nabla\Phi_{q}(y^{\prime})|\leq C_{\Omega}|y^{\prime}|^{\alpha},\qquad|\Phi_{q}(y^{\prime})|\leq C_{\Omega}|y^{\prime}|^{1+\alpha},\qquad y^{\prime}\in B(0,r_{\Omega}).

(These follow from
\nabla\Phi_{q}(0)=0
and uniform
C^{1,\alpha}
control of
\partial\Omega
.)

1) Global Hölder control via Gehring–Martio.

Since
D
and
\Omega
are bounded
C^{1,\alpha}
domains,
they are bounded Lipschitz domains. It is a standard fact that every bounded Lipschitz domain is a (bounded)
uniform domain (see, e.g.,
[
7
,
41
]
and the references therein). Thus both
D
and
\Omega
are uniform domains. Moreover, by Lemma 2.18 in
[
9
]
, every bounded uniform domain is a John domain, so in particular

\Omega
is a John domain.

Therefore the hypotheses of Theorem 3.28 in
[
9
]
apply: since
f
maps a uniform domain
D

onto a John domain
\Omega
, there exists an exponent
\beta\in(0,1)
and a constant
C>0
such that

(13)

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq C\,|x-y|^{\beta},\qquad x,y\in\overline{D}.

Thus
f
is globally
\beta
–Hölder continuous on
\overline{D}
.
Set
\beta_{0}:=\beta
. In particular,
f
admits a continuous extension to
\overline{D}
(still denoted by
f
).

2) One-step exponent improvement for a rotated normal component (at each boundary point).

Fix
\xi\in\partial D\cap U
and set
q:=f(\xi)\in\partial\Omega
.
Let
T_{q}
be the rigid motion from Step 0 and define the rotated map

f^{(q)}:=T_{q}\circ f,\qquad F^{(q)}:=f^{(q)}|_{\partial D\cap U}.

Then
f^{(q)}
is still harmonic and
K
–quasiconformal, and
F^{(q)}(\partial D\cap U)
lies in

\partial\Omega_{q}
(after shrinking
U
so that
f(D\cap U)\subset V
).
Writing
f^{(q)}=(\tilde{f}^{(q)},f^{(q)}_{n})
, the boundary identity is

(14)

F^{(q)}_{n}(\eta)=\Phi_{q}(\tilde{F}^{(q)}(\eta)),\qquad\eta\in\partial D\cap U.

Since
F^{(q)}(\xi)=0
, we have
\tilde{F}^{(q)}(\xi)=0
and
F^{(q)}_{n}(\xi)=0
.
Combining (
14
) with (
12
) gives, for
\eta\in\partial D\cap U
,

|F^{(q)}_{n}(\eta)-F^{(q)}_{n}(\xi)|=|\Phi_{q}(\tilde{F}^{(q)}(\eta))|\leq C_{\Omega}|\tilde{F}^{(q)}(\eta)|^{1+\alpha}.

Because
T_{q}
is an isometry, (
13
) implies

|\tilde{F}^{(q)}(\eta)|\leq|F^{(q)}(\eta)|=|f(\eta)-f(\xi)|\leq C_{0}|\eta-\xi|^{\beta_{0}}
.
Hence

(15)

|F^{(q)}_{n}(\eta)-F^{(q)}_{n}(\xi)|\leq C_{1}|\eta-\xi|^{\beta_{1}},\qquad\beta_{1}:=(1+\alpha)\beta_{0},

with
C_{1}:=C_{\Omega}C_{0}^{1+\alpha}
(uniform in
\xi
).

3) Boundary gradient estimate for
f^{(q)}_{n}
and passage to
|Df|
.

Recall from Step 2 that (
15
) holds with exponent
\beta_{1}=(1+\alpha)\beta_{0}
.
Assume for the moment that
\beta_{1}<1
.
Apply Lemma
3.1
to the harmonic function

at

with exponent
\mu=\beta_{1}
.

Then there exist

r_{1}\in(0,r_{D})
,

, and
C_{2}>0
(uniform in
\xi
) such that for

x=\xi+t\,\nu_{D}(\xi)\in D,\qquad 0<t<cr_{1},

we have the gradient estimate

(16)

|\nabla f^{(q)}_{n}(x)|\leq C_{2}\,t^{\beta_{1}-1}.

We now reduce this to a bound for
|Df|
. Let
A:=Df^{(q)}(x)
.
Since
A
and
A^{T}
have the same singular values, we have

l(A)=\inf_{|b|=1}|A^{T}b|\leq|A^{T}e_{n}|=|\nabla f^{(q)}_{n}(x)|,

where
l(A)
denotes the smallest singular value and
e_{n}
is the
n
-th coordinate vector. By quasiconformality,

(17)

|Df^{(q)}(x)|\leq H(K)\,l(Df^{(q)}(x)),

hence

(18)

|Df^{(q)}(x)|\leq H(K)\,|\nabla f^{(q)}_{n}(x)|.

Since
T_{q}
is a rigid motion,
Df^{(q)}(x)=DT_{q}(f(x))\,Df(x)
and hence

|Df^{(q)}(x)|=|Df(x)|
. Combining (
18
) with
(
16
) we obtain

|Df(x)|\leq C\,t^{\beta_{1}-1}

for all
x\in D
of the form
x=\xi+t\nu_{D}(\xi)
with
\xi\in\partial D\cap U

and
0<t<cr_{1}
, uniformly in
\xi
. By the tubular representation
(
11
), for such points one has
\delta_{D}(x)=t
, and therefore

(19)

|Df(x)|\leq C\,\delta_{D}(x)^{\beta_{1}-1},\qquad x\in D,\ \delta_{D}(x)<cr_{1}.

4) Improving the Hölder exponent of the full boundary trace.

Let
\xi,\eta\in\partial D\cap U
and set
\rho:=|\xi-\eta|
.
For
\rho
small,
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity of
\partial D
implies there exists a rectifiable curve

\sigma\subset\partial D\cap U
joining
\xi
to
\eta
with
\mathrm{length}(\sigma)\leq C_{D}\rho
.

Consider the three-piece path in
D
:

\gamma_{1}(s):=\xi+s\,\nu_{D}(\xi),\quad s\in[0,\rho],

\gamma_{2}:=\{\ \sigma(\tau)+\rho\,\nu_{D}(\sigma(\tau)):\ \tau\ \text{parametrizes }\sigma\ \},

\gamma_{3}(s):=\eta+(\rho-s)\,\nu_{D}(\eta),\quad s\in[0,\rho].

Then
\gamma_{1}
joins
\xi
to
\xi+\rho\nu_{D}(\xi)
,
\gamma_{2}
joins
\xi+\rho\nu_{D}(\xi)
to

\eta+\rho\nu_{D}(\eta)
, and
\gamma_{3}
joins
\eta+\rho\nu_{D}(\eta)
to
\eta
; hence

\gamma:=\gamma_{1}\cup\gamma_{2}\cup\gamma_{3}
connects
\xi
to
\eta
inside
D
.

Moreover, for
\rho
small (depending only on the local tubular radius), we have

\delta_{D}(\gamma_{1}(s))=s,\qquad\delta_{D}(\gamma_{3}(s))=s,

and along
\gamma_{2}
the distance to the boundary is constant:

(20)

\delta_{D}(z)=\rho\qquad\text{for all }z\in\gamma_{2},

and
\mathrm{length}(\gamma_{2})\leq C\rho
.

Integrating (
19
) along
\gamma
gives

|f(\xi)-f(\eta)|\leq\int_{\gamma}|Df|\,ds\leq\int_{\gamma_{1}}C\,\delta_{D}^{\beta_{1}-1}\,ds+\int_{\gamma_{2}}C\,\delta_{D}^{\beta_{1}-1}\,ds+\int_{\gamma_{3}}C\,\delta_{D}^{{\beta_{1}}-1}\,ds.

Using
\delta_{D}(\gamma_{1}(s))=s
and
\delta_{D}(\gamma_{3}(s))=s
,

\int_{\gamma_{1}}C\,\delta_{D}^{{\beta_{1}}-1}\,ds=C\int_{0}^{\rho}s^{{\beta_{1}}-1}\,ds\leq C\rho^{\beta_{1}},\qquad\int_{\gamma_{3}}C\,\delta_{D}^{{\beta_{1}}-1}\,ds\leq C\rho^{\beta_{1}}.

Using (
20
) and
\mathrm{length}(\gamma_{2})\leq C\rho
,

\int_{\gamma_{2}}C\,\delta_{D}^{{\beta_{1}}-1}\,ds\leq C\,\rho^{{\beta_{1}}-1}\,\mathrm{length}(\gamma_{2})\leq C\rho^{\beta_{1}}.

Therefore

|f(\xi)-f(\eta)|\leq C\,\rho^{\beta_{1}}=C|\xi-\eta|^{\beta_{1}},

so on
\partial D\cap U
we have improved the boundary exponent of
F=f|_{\partial D}
from

\beta_{0}
to
\beta_{1}
.

5) Iteration and final step.

Define inductively
\beta_{m+1}:=(1+\alpha)\beta_{m}
for
m\geq 0
, with
\beta_{0}=\beta
.
If necessary, decrease
\beta
slightly so that
\beta_{m}\neq 1
for all
m
; this is possible since the
global Hölder estimate (
13
) remains valid for any smaller exponent.

Assume
\beta_{m}<1
and that on
\partial D\cap U
,

(21)

|f(\xi)-f(\eta)|\leq C_{m}|\xi-\eta|^{\beta_{m}}.

Repeating Steps 2–4 with
\beta_{0}
replaced by
\beta_{m}
yields

|f(\xi)-f(\eta)|\leq C_{m+1}|\xi-\eta|^{\beta_{m+1}},\qquad\beta_{m+1}=(1+\alpha)\beta_{m},

and

|Df(x)|\leq\widetilde{C}_{m+1}\,\delta_{D}(x)^{\beta_{m+1}-1}

for
x
in a (possibly smaller) collar neighborhood of
\partial D\cap U
.

Choose
m_{0}\in\mathbf{N}
minimal such that
\beta_{m_{0}}>1
.
At this stage we apply Lemma
3.2
in Step 3 and obtain a uniform bound

(22)

|Df(x)|\leq C_{*}\qquad\text{for }x\text{ with }\delta_{D}(x)<\delta_{*},

in some collar
\{x\in D:\delta_{D}(x)<\delta_{*}\}
near
\partial D\cap U
(with
\delta_{*}>0
).

6) Globalization and Lipschitz continuity.

Cover
\partial D
by finitely many boundary patches
U^{(1)},\dots,U^{(N)}
and apply the above
argument on each patch. This yields a uniform bound (
22
) on the global collar

\mathcal{C}:=\{x\in D:\delta_{D}(x)<\delta_{*}\}.

Set

D_{\delta_{*}}:=\{x\in\overline{D}:\delta_{D}(x)\geq\delta_{*}\}.

Since
\delta_{D}
is continuous and
\overline{D}
is compact,
D_{\delta_{*}}
is compact. Moreover,

\delta_{*}>0
implies
D_{\delta_{*}}\subset D
. Because
f
is harmonic in
D
, it is smooth, hence
Df
is
continuous on
D_{\delta_{*}}
, and therefore

\sup_{D_{\delta_{*}}}|Df|=\max_{D_{\delta_{*}}}|Df|<\infty.

Combining this with (
22
) gives
\sup_{D}|Df|<\infty
.

Finally, boundedness of
|Df|
implies that
f
is Lipschitz on
\overline{D}
. Indeed, since
D
is a bounded
C^{1}
(hence Lipschitz) domain, it is uniform and therefore quasiconvex; in particular,
any
x,y\in D
can be joined by a rectifiable curve
\Gamma\subset D
with
\mathrm{length}(\Gamma)\leq C_{D}\,|x-y|
. Hence

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq\int_{\Gamma}|Df|\,ds\leq(\sup_{D}|Df|)\,C_{D}\,|x-y|.

Passing to
x,y\in\overline{D}
by continuity completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

In the planar case, Lipschitz continuity of harmonic quasiconformal mappings
between Jordan domains with
C^{1,\alpha}
boundaries was proved in
[
19
]

by reducing the problem to the unit disk and using Kellogg’s theorem for conformal mappings
together with the Poisson representation formula.

Single-granule profiling reveals that RNP granule pH marks cellular translation
Authors: Yue Zhang, Shining Wu, Yifeng He, Xuhan Xia, Jinchao Wei, Shijia Wu, Ruijie Deng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryRibonucleoprotein (RNP) granules reshape RNA metabolism in eukaryotic cells, yet their biophysical properties remain poorly understood due to the lack of probes targeting these small, dynamic condensates. Herein, we developed RNP granule-pH (RNPg-pH), a ratiometric and targetable probe, to map the pH of processing bodies (P-bodies) and stress granules (SGs) at the single-granule level. This approach revealed a spatial pH heterogeneity within RNP granules, with a more alkaline periphery and a more acidic region near the nucleus. Stresses including oxidative stress, hyperosmotic stress, and translational inhibition induced granule acidification and reduced translation level, suggesting that RNPg-pH served as a sensitive marker of translation activity. Notably, compounds like myrtenol and oridonin reversed hydrogen peroxide (H2O2)-induced acidification, restored translation activity, and delayed cellular senescence, implying that RNPg-pH has the potential for screening anti-aging reagents. Our work provides an approach for monitoring RNP granules, shedding light on their roles in RNA metabolism and cell functions.

Introduction

Eukaryotic cells contain diverse membrane-less organelles, which are enriched in proteins and nucleic acids (RNA/DNA) and are commonly referred to as ribonucleoprotein (RNP) granules.1,2 These RNP granules assemble through liquid-liquid phase separation (LLPS) and include the nucleolus, Cajal bodies, paraspeckles, processing bodies (P-bodies), and stress granules (SGs).3 Among them, P-bodies and SGs are two conserved cytosolic RNP granules where non-translating mRNAs can accumulate.4 Although P-bodies and SGs are distinct granules, they interact under certain stress conditions and share some proteins.5 mRNAs within P-bodies and SGs can either be degraded or stored for return to translation, thereby establishing a dynamic mRNA cycle in cells.4 RNP granules play a critical role in the regulation of mRNA metabolism, and their biophysical properties, including pH, viscosity, charge distribution, and others, appear to be closely linked to their functions.1,6,7 However, the study of these properties still remains limited due to the absence of targeting probes to the small and dynamic RNP granules.1,8

Among the physical properties, pH is essential to various cellular processes and biological functions.9 Therefore, a variety of tools have been developed to measure intracellular pH, including small fluorescent organic molecules (e.g., BCECF 4 and C.SNARF-1 37), nanoparticles, and fluorescent proteins (e.g., GFP, RFP, and YFP).10 Furthermore, some pH probes can localize to large membrane-bound organelles, facilitating the study of important physiological mechanisms. For example, Huang et al. synthesized a dual-channel fluorescent probe to detect pH in the endoplasmic reticulum and mitochondria and revealed the organelle interactions in Alzheimer’s disease pathogenesis.11 Yapici et al. reported a rhodamine-coumarin pH probe to monitor the pH in mitochondrion and lysosomes, enabling a better understanding of the interactions between the two organelles.12 Furthermore, pH of RNP granules plays a crucial role in their composition, structure, and function. For instance, the initiation factor 2 α subunit (elF2α), which is abundant in SGs, becomes phosphorylated at low pH values, leading to a reduction in translation levels.13 Heterogeneous nuclear RNP A1 (hnRNPA1), a key regulatory protein localized to cytoplasmic RNP granules, can form larger droplets under acidic conditions, potentially increasing the risk of neurodegenerative diseases.14 Thus, probing RNP granule pH (RNPg-pH) holds great significance for investigating their regulation and function. However, currently, the tools that are capable of targeting the small and dynamic RNP granules remain to be explored.

In this study, we successfully mapped the pH signature of P-bodies and SGs at single-granule resolution in living cells by constructing a ratiometric and targetable pH probe, RNPg-pH. We analyzed the spatial pH heterogeneity within RNP granules and explored how pH dynamics regulated translation activity. We further suggested that RNPg-pH might have potential for screening reagents against aging. Our observation provides pH information on these membrane-less organelles to gain further insight into RNP-related regulatory mechanisms of cellular processes.

Results

RNPg-pH enables precise targeting and pH quantification of P-bodies and SGs

The probe RNPg-pH consists of two modules: a sensing module for pH measurement and a targeting module for localization within RNP granules (Figure 1A). For the sensing module, we select pHluorin2, a ratiometric and pH-sensitive variant of GFP. As acidity increases, pHluorin2 exhibits a gradual decrease in excitation at 405 nm, coupled with a corresponding increase at 488 nm.15 The targeting module typically comprises the core and specific proteins of RNP granules. First, to test the localization of RNPg-pH in living cells, we established a location reporter (LR) by fusing mCherry, whose excitation wavelength (561 nm) differs from those of pHluorin2 (405 and 488 nm), to Dcp2, another core component of P-bodies, or to G3BP2, another core component of SGs.16 The two plasmids respectively expressing RNPg-pH and LR were co-transfected into HEK293T cells (Figure 1B). Confocal imaging revealed the co-localization of RNPg-pH and LR (Figure 1C), with a Pearson’s correlation coefficient (PCC) of 0.90 ± 0.04 between pHluorin2-Dcp1a and mCherry-Dcp2 and a PCC of 0.82 ± 0.04 between pHluorin2-G3BP1 and mCherry-G3BP2 (Figure 1D). Additionally, we constructed non-co-localized reporters (NLRs), mCherry-G3BP1 and mCherry-Dcp1a, to further validate the targeting specificity of RNPg-pH (Figure S1A). The result confirmed that PB-pH and SG-pH specifically targeted their respective RNP granules (Figures 1D and S1).

Having successfully introduced pHluorin2 to RNP granules, we assessed the pH response of RNPg-pH. To achieve this, we exposed HEK293T cells to buffer solutions with different pH values (pH = 5.5, 7.0, or 8.5). The results showed that as the pH increased, the fluorescence intensity of both PB-pH and SG-pH increased when excited at 405 nm, while it decreased at 488 nm, leading to an increase in the ratio of 405/488 (Figures 1E–1H). The results demonstrated that RNPg-pH exhibited a pH-dependent response within RNP granules. In addition, the ratiometric probes reduce the influence of intracellular variations, therefore enabling accurate detection of RNPg-pH.

Next, we tested the feasibility of using RNPg-pH to quantify the pH of RNP granules. We incubated HEK293T cells expressing pHluorin2-Dcp1a (PB-pH), pHluorin2-G3BP1 (SG-pH), and pHluorin2 (Cyto-pH) in buffers with pH ranging from 5.5 to 8.5 and recorded the values of the ratio 405/488 (Figure 2A). Figures 2B and 2D showed that, as the buffer pH value increased, the fluorescence intensity of both PB-pH and SG-pH increased at 405 nm, while that decreased at 488 nm. The pH calibration curves showed a linear correlation over the pH range of 6.0–8.0 (Figures 2C, 2E, and S2). We then detected the pH of RNP granules and the cytosol in PBS (pH = 7.2–7.4) and found that the pH of P-bodies and SGs was approximately 0.3 units lower than that of the cytosol (Figure S3). That suggested that the microenvironment of RNP granules was highly distinct from their surroundings, which might be attributed to the high concentration of RNA and specialized proteins, such as intrinsically disordered proteins, and RNA-RNA or RNA-protein interactions.6,18,19Figure 2Quantification of RNP granule pH using PB-pH and SG-pH(A) Schematic of measuring RNP granule pH in living cells.(B and D) Fluorescence images of PB-pH (B) and SG-pH (D) in cells exposed to buffers with pH values ranging from 5.5 to 8.5. A spatial pH heterogeneity exists within an individual RNP granule

RNP granules share many RNA-binding proteins and mRNA; thus, the messenger RNPs (mRNPs) undergo recombination and exchange between P-bodies and SGs, affecting the structure and function of RNP granules.4,20 To explore the impact of pH fluctuations on RNP granules, we monitored the morphology and heterogeneity at the single-granule level using RNPg-pH under different pH values (pH = 6.0, 8.0, or PBS).

Firstly, we analyzed the morphological changes of RNP granules across different pH levels. As shown in Figure 3A, SGs were larger than P-bodies in PBS, corresponding to the basic attributes summarized by Spector.3 When the pH changed to 6.0 or 8.0, both P-bodies and SGs increased significantly in size, implying that eukaryotic cells tended to assemble RNP granules in response to environmental changes like pH.21 Besides, Figure 3B showed that the aspect ratio (major axis/minor axis) of P-bodies was closer to 1 compared with that of SGs in PBS, suggesting that P-bodies were rounder and SGs were more irregular. However, as the pH shifted from PBS to 6.0 or 8.0, the aspect ratio of P-bodies increased, while that of SGs decreased, indicating that P-bodies elongated, while SGs became more circular. This could be due to the frequent remodeling of mRNPs within P-bodies and increased accumulation of mRNPs within SGs when pH changed (Figure 3F). Next, we monitored the changes in heterogeneity of RNP granules with the varying pH values. The standard deviation (SD) of gray values can serve as an indicator of the heterogeneity of a granule. In our study, when pH changed from PBS to 6.0/8.0, the SD value of P-bodies increased, whereas that of SGs showed no significant change, indicating that P-bodies became more heterogeneous with the pH alteration (Figure 3C). These results may be attributed to the rearrangement of mRNPs between P-bodies and SGs (Figure 3F). The mRNPs within P-bodies were evenly distributed in PBS, resulting in a relatively uniform pH distribution. However, when there is a significant change in pH, the rapid recombination and bidirectional exchange of mRNPs disrupted the uniformity, causing increased pH fluctuation in P-bodies. In contrast, the dynamic recombination of mRNPs within SGs appeared to remain consistently active; therefore, there was no significant difference in pH fluctuation across different pH conditions.

Based on the aforementioned observations, we were particularly interested in the pH distribution within an individual RNP granule, and therefore, we randomly selected several granules and calculated the pH at each pixel (Figures 3D, 3E, and S5–S7; Data S1). The results revealed that the pH inside granules varied, with higher pH at the periphery, while most pixels had pH levels close to the buffer pH (Figures 3D [ⅰ, ⅲ, ⅳ, ⅵ], 3E [ⅰ, ⅲ, ⅳ, ⅵ], S5, and S6). Notably, the pH near the nucleus was significantly lower, particularly in P-bodies, likely due to the higher concentration of RNA around the nucleus, which could influence the local pH. To further investigate pH fluctuations within RNP granules, we plotted the pH distribution from the region closest to the nucleus to that farthest away from the nucleus (Figures 3D [ⅱ, ⅴ], 3E [ⅱ, ⅴ], S5, and S6). We observed that the pH in the central region fluctuated between 7.2 and 7.5 under PBS conditions. When the pH shifted to 6.0, central region pH within P-bodies was closer to 7.0 rather than 6.0, and the range of pH fluctuation within P-bodies increased. However, within SGs, the pH remained close to the buffer pH, and there was no significant difference in the pH fluctuation amplitude. These results suggested that the recombination of mRNPs within SGs was more frequent than that in P-bodies, making the pH of SGs more sensitive to environmental pH changes.

Stress induces a decrease in pH of P-bodies and SGs and translation activity of cells

A variety of stresses typically involve an inhibition of translation, which leads to the induction of P-bodies and SGs, with alterations in the biophysical properties of these granules.22 We investigated the pH response of RNP granules and translation activity under different stresses (Figure 4A). We measured the pH of P-bodies and SGs under three different stresses, including oxidative stress, hyperosmotic stress, and translational inhibition. The results indicated that under these stress conditions, the pH of P-bodies decreased by 0.3–0.5 units compared to the control group, while that of SGs decreased by 0.3 units (Figure 4B). P-bodies and SGs form from pools of non-translating mRNA, which are believed to participate in mRNA storage and degradation.23,24 Stresses accelerated the accumulation of translationally repressed mRNAs, thus changing the biophysical properties of P-bodies and SGs, like pH, viscosity, and so on.4,18,21Figure 4Variation in pH of RNP granules and translation activity of cells under stresses(A) Schematic of stress treatment and indicator detection.(B) pH values of P-bodies and SGs under different stress conditions. Considering that both P-bodies and SGs can sequester mRNAs to suppress protein synthesis, we hypothesized that changes in the pH of RNP granules induced by short-term stress treatment might inhibit protein translation (Figure 4C).25 To test this, we first assessed cell viability by CCK-8 assay under various stress conditions. As shown in Figure 4D, acidification, alkalization, and hyperosmotic stress resulted in considerable cell death, while oxidative stress and translational inhibition did not. Next, we monitored nascent protein synthesis efficiency under different conditions (Figure 4E).26 We found that protein synthesis was markedly suppressed under various stresses compared to the PBS or control group (Figure 4F). Acidification, alkalization, and hyperosmotic stress caused a decrease in cell activity, which might be the primary reason for the reduced nascent protein synthesis (Figure 4D). Oxidative damage caused by hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) and the inhibition of peptidyl transferase activity by anisomycin would block protein synthesis, resulting in a dramatic reduction in nascent protein fluorescence intensity.27 Besides, the confocal imaging of newly synthesized proteins (Azide 594) presented similar results (Figure 4G). These results indicated that an abnormal pH of P-bodies and SGs would decrease the translation level; therefore, the pH of RNP granules could serve as a marker for cellular translation activity.

Myrtenol and oridonin restore the pH of P-bodies and SGs, contributing to improving translation activity and alleviating cellular aging

Aberrant phase transitions and excessive accumulation of RNP granules could represent a key triggering event in cellular aging.28 H2O2, a typical reactive oxygen species (ROS), is commonly used to induce cellular senescence.29 Natural products, such as myrtenol, curcumin, and oridonin, may serve as potential agents for alleviating aging by reducing inflammation.30,31,32,33 To investigate the potential relationship between the pH of RNP granules and cellular senescence, we selected the aforementioned three natural products to counteract cell aging induced by H2O2, exploring the potential link between the RNP granules and cellular aging (Figure 5). Senescent cells express a β-galactosidase whose activity can be detected at pH = 6.0, so that is called senescence-associated β-galactosidase (SA-β-Gal). We firstly assessed the potential cell toxicity of the three natural products. We found that 5 μM of each compound for 24 h was not toxic to HEK293T cells and had no significant impact on SA-β-Gal activity or nascent protein synthesis (Figure S8). Next, we incubated HEK293T cells in the three natural products for 24 h respectively before treating with H2O2 (Figure 5A). Figure 5B showed that myrtenol and oridonin restored the pH of P-bodies and SGs to normal levels, with the pH of P-bodies increasing by approximately 0.5 units compared to the H2O2-treated group and the pH of SGs increasing by around 0.4 units. In contrast, curcumin did not exhibit the ability to restore the pH of RNP granules. None of the three natural products affected cell viability (Figure 5C). Myrtenol and oridonin significantly reduced SA-β-Gal activity compared to the H2O2-treated group (Figure 5D). Moreover, HEK293T cells pre-treated with myrtenol and oridonin exhibited higher translation activity compared with those treated with H2O2 alone, as evidenced by a strong signal of nascent protein (Figures 5E and 5F). However, curcumin seemed unable to improve translation activity and alleviate H2O2-induced cell aging (Figures 5D–5F). These results suggested that myrtenol and oridonin would restore the pH of P-bodies and SGs to a normal level, while also promoting efficient protein synthesis and mitigating cellular aging. The pH of RNP granules could be a potential indicator for screening anti-aging reagents.

Discussion

The biophysical properties of RNP granules are crucial for regulating their dynamic behavior and function, including RNA metabolism, ribosome biogenesis, the DNA damage response, and signal transduction.35 Due to the lack of probes for precise measurement of their biophysical properties, we developed a ratiometric and targetable probe, RNPg-pH, which enabled monitoring of the pH of P-bodies and SGs under different conditions. We observed spatial pH heterogeneity within P-bodies and SGs, with a more alkaline periphery and a more acidic region near the nucleus, primarily due to mRNP recombination. We demonstrated that short-term stress treatments, such as oxidative stress, hyperosmotic stress, and translational inhibition, lowered the pH of P-bodies and SGs and significantly inhibited translation activity. Furthermore, some natural antioxidant products, including myrtenol and oridonin, were able to restore the pH and translation activity to normal levels, delaying the aging process. The quantitative analysis of RNP granules provides valuable insights into the complex dynamics of cellular processes, particularly in metabolism, stress response, and aging mechanisms.8

Upon analyzing individual RNP granules, we observed the morphological differences and pH heterogeneity of P-bodies and SGs. RNP granules are formed through LLPS, a process which is typically influenced by environmental conditions such as pH, ionic strength, redox potential, osmotic pressure, and temperature.36 Low pH induces the assembly of macromolecular proteins, thereby promoting the formation of RNP granules.37 Granule analysis revealed that both acid and alkali conditions resulted in an increase in the size and aspect ratio of P-bodies, along with a notable pH fluctuation within an individual P-body. In contrast, SGs exhibited only morphological changes, with internal pH fluctuation showing little difference (Figures 3A–3E, S5, and S6). These results might stem from the dynamic exchanges of mRNPs between P-bodies and SGs (Figure 3F). We hypothesized that in PBS, the mRNPs within P-bodies were evenly distributed, maintaining a stable, circular shape with a uniform pH distribution.3 However, when pH varied, the mRNPs rapidly reconstituted and exchanged, causing P-bodies to undergo more fusion and fission. This resulted in a more heterogeneous mRNP distribution and significant pH fluctuations, with P-bodies adopting an elliptical shape.35,38 Conversely, SGs are more susceptible to disassembly by lysosomes through autophagy under PBS conditions, resulting in a distorted shape.4 The changes in pH might promote the agglomeration of mRNPs within SGs, stabilizing their structure and making it more regular. Continuous mRNP exchange likely prevents significant changes in pH fluctuations within SGs, with their pH closely reflecting the environmental pH.

Furthermore, we explored the potential connection between RNP granules and cellular translation as well as aging. RNP granules are key determinants of cellular functions, which could serve as valuable markers for both cellular translation and senescence, as their assembly can be potentially enhanced by translational inhibition and organismal aging.39,40 On the one hand, stresses such as oxidative stress, hyperosmotic stress, and translational inhibition induced acidification in P-bodies and SGs and a noticeable decrease in translation activity. On the other hand, pre-incubation with myrtenol and oridonin restored the pH of P-bodies and SGs in H2O2-treated cells to a normal level, improving translation level and delaying the aging process (Figures 5B and 5D–5F). These results suggested that myrtenol and oridonin might protect cells from premature senescence by maintaining the pH of RNP granules in cells exposed to various stress conditions.41 However, further studies using aging models such as peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs), primary dermal fibroblasts (PDFs), and animal models of aging are required to confirm a connection between RNP granules and aging.42,43

In summary, RNPg-pH offers an approach for quantifying and monitoring the pH of RNP granules at a single-granule level in living cells. Profiling RNP granules and cellular activities revealed that RNPg-pH served as a potential marker of cellular translation and senescence. Due to the programmability, our probes can also monitor other indicators, such as viscosity and polarity, by replacing the sensing module, thus expanding the toolkit for detecting the biophysical properties of organelles. By combining insights into RNPg-pH with other physicochemical properties, we can gain a deeper understanding of the complex physiological states of these membrane-less organelles and the mechanisms of their regulation.

Limitations of the study

While our study demonstrates the utility of RNPg-pH in measuring the pH of RNP granules, several limitations should be considered. First, we used transient transfection for this study, which may induce SGs. Although all experiments involving cellular stress included control groups, we did not clearly distinguish whether the SGs were induced by the transfection reagents or by stresses. Second, the studies on RNPg-pH and cellular senescence were conducted in HEK293T cells, a commonly used cell line. Further exploration in aging models such as PBMCs, PDFs, and animal models of aging is required to fully understand their relationship. Finally, pH changes within RNP granules could be influenced by RNA distribution. Although our probe can specifically target RNP granules, it cannot label RNA or distinguish the mRNPs within granules. The development of probes capable of labeling RNA and visualizing structures within RNP granules remains a challenge. Despite these limitations, RNPg-pH, in its current form, is a useful tool to map RNPg-pH and provides insight into their regulation and functions.

Experiments model and study participant details

Cell lines and cell cultures

HEK 293T cells were purchased from Procell (human female, RRID: CVCL_0063) and were pre-authenticated via STR profiling by the supplier. Routine mycoplasma detection was carried out, and no contamination was detected.

HEK 293T cells were cultured in complete medium consisting of Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle’s Medium (DMEM, Gibco, C11995500BT) supplemented with 10% (vol/vol) fetal bovine serum (FBS, Procell, 164210) and 1% (vol/vol) Penicillin-Streptomycin (Biosharp, BL505A). Cells were maintained at 37°C with 5% CO2 humidified incubator. All cells were dissociated using 0.25% trypsin-EDTA (Gibco, 25200072) according to the instructions.

Method details

Plasmid construction

All oligonucleotide sequences utilized for recombination (Table S1) were synthesized by Tsingke Biotechnology Co., Ltd (Beijing, China). The genes encoding pHluorin2, Dcp1a, Dcp2, G3BP1 and G3BP2 were derived from Lee et al. (2020), Meyer et al. (2020) and Qin et al. (2023). The mCherry plasmid was a kind gift from Jiannan Zhang (Sichuan University, Chengdu, China). The plasmids pHluorin2-Dcp1a, pHluorin2- G3BP1, mCherry-Dcp2 and mCherry- G3BP2 were generated by a Basic Seamless Cloning and Assembly kit (TransGen Biotech Co., Ltd, Beijing, China) with the pcDNA3.1 (+) plasmid as templates. The correctness of the insertion for each plasmid was examined by DNA Sanger sequencing, conducted by Tsingke Biotechnology Co., Ltd (Beijing, China).

Mammalian cell transfections, and drug treatments

Prior to transfection, HEK 293T cells were seeded into 35-mm glass-bottom confocal dishes (Biosharp, BS-20-GJM) at a density of 0.5-1.5 × 105 cells/mL and incubated for 18-24 h. DNA plasmid transfections were carried out with 5 μL of Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, 11668019) per 100 μL of DMEM for ∼6 h followed by a wash and replenishment with complete medium. The detailed transfection amounts are shown in Table S2.

For stress experiments, HEK 293T cells were treated with 100 μM H2O2 (oxidative stress), 0.4 M sorbitol (hyperosmotic stress), or 20 μM anisomycin (translational inhibition) for 2 h. For natural product treatment experiments, HEK 293T cells were incubated with myrtenol, curcumin or oridonin for 24 h before to use in subsequent experiments.

Live-cell imaging and analysis

Imaging was performed with a confocal microscopy (Leica, STELLARIS 5). Cellular images were captured utilizing a 63×oil objective and acquired using the microsystem Leica Application Suite X. The images were imported into ImageJ for quantitative analysis.

Preparation of pH calibration curves

Referring to Mahon and Meng et al., a buffer solution containing 20 mM HEPES, 140 mM KCl, 1mM MgCl2, 10 mM glucose, 10 μM nigericin were prepared.15,44 Different pH values (5.5-8.5) were then adjusted with 1 M KOH or 1 M HCl. HEK 293T cells were incubated under the buffer solution with different pH values at 37°C for 3 min and then imaged by confocal microscopy.

The fluorescence intensity of pHluorin2 was measured by the following method. First, a 405 nm laser line was opened with 2% intensity, and emission window was set from 505 to 515 nm. Second, a 488 nm laser line was opened with 2% intensity, and emission window was set from 505 to 515 nm. Then, the mean gray values of channel 404 nm and 488 nm were measured by ImageJ, and the 405/ 485 nm ratio was calculated and plotted.

Cell activity test

Cell viability was evaluated using an enhanced CCK-8 counting kit (Beyotime, C0042). HEK 293T cells were seeded into 96-well plates (5000/well), and then treated with drugs as mentioned above. 1:10 diluted CCK-8 solution was added to the culture medium and the cells were incubated for 1 h at 37°C. Then, a microplate reader (Biotek, SYNERGY H1) was used to measure the absorbance at 450 nm.Cellsurvivalrate%=ODtreatment−ODblank/ODcontrol−ODblank×100%

SA-β-Gal activity detection

The SA-β-Gal activity was detected by C12FDG (MCE, HY-126839). First, cells were washed with PBS, and then fixed with 4% formaldehyde at 4°C for 15 min. Next, cells were washed twice with PBS and stained with 100 μL of 33 μM C12FDG in PBS (pH=6.0) at 37 °C for 20 min. Finally, cells were washed with PBS (pH=6.0) three times and the fluorescence intensity was measured by a microplate reader. The parameters were set as follows: excitation wavelength, 480 nm; emission wavelength, 535 nm.

Protein synthesis detection

The newly synthesized protein was examined by a BeyoClick™ HPG-594 Nascent Protein Assay Kit (Beyotime, P1209S) referred to the instructions.

Quantification and statistical analysis

The final results were expressed as mean ± SD and evaluated through a non-paired two-tailed Student’s t-test or one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with Duncan's test. P < 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant. Unless otherwise stated, the results were based on a minimum of three independent experiments. All figures were plotted by GraphPad Prism.

Tensor t-structures, perversity functions and weight structures
Gopinath Sahoo

Abstract
. We introduce the notion of tensor t-structures on the bounded derived categories of schemes. For a Noetherian scheme X X admitting a dualizing complex, Bezrukavnikov-Deligne, and then independently Gabber and Kashiwara have shown that given a monotone comonotone perversity function on X X one can construct a t-structure on 𝐃 b ​ ( X ) \mathbf{D}^{b}(X) . We show that such t-structures are tensor t-structures and conversely every tensor t-structure on 𝐃 b ​ ( X ) \mathbf{D}^{b}(X) arises in this way. We achieve this by first characterising tensor t-structures in terms of Thomason-Cousin filtrations which generalises earlier results of Alonso, Jeremías and Saorín, from Noetherian rings to schemes. We also show that for a smooth projective curve C C , the derived category 𝐃 b ​ ( C ) \mathbf{D}^{b}(C) has no non-trivial tensor weight structures, this extends our earlier result on the projective line to higher genus curves.

1.
Introduction

The classification theorem of thick subcategories of finite spectra in the stable homotopy
theory
[
Hop87
, Theorem 7]
sparked the interest to study similar problems in the
algebraic setting of derived categories. Hopkins,
[
Hop87
, Theorem 11]
, proved that the
thick subcategories of
\mathrm{{Perf}}{(R)}
, for a commutative ring
R
, are completely determined by
the specialisation-closed subsets of
\mathrm{Spec}R
. For Noetherian rings, in
[
Nee92
]
,
Neeman classified localizing and smashing subcategories of the unbounded derived category

\mathbf{D}(R)
. Thomason in
[
Tho97
, Theorem 3.15]
classified the
thick tensor ideals of
\mathrm{{Perf}}{(X)}
, generalizing the theorem of Hopkins from
rings to schemes. Ever since the work of Hopkins, Neeman and Thomason there has
been a great interest to understand various subcategories of triangulated categories arising
both in algebraic geometry and representation theory.

The notion of t-structures on a triangulated category was introduced in
[
BBD82
]
by Belinson
et al. in their study of perverse sheaves. In our earlier work
[
DS23a
]
, motivated by the
notion of rigid aisles in
[
ATJLSS03
, §5]
we defined the notion of tensor t-structures on a
triangulated category, in particular on
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
where
X
is a Noetherian scheme.
These t-structures restrict well to the open subsets. Using the local nature of these
t-structures, we were able to generalize earlier results from Noetherian rings to schemes.
For instance, we have shown in
[
DS23a
, Theorem 5.11]
that the compactly generated tensor
t-structures on
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
are in one-to-one correspondence with the Thomason filtrations on

X
which generalizes the result of Alonso, Jeremías and Saorín
[
ATJLS10
, Theorem 3.11]
.

One of the two goals of this article is to study similar notions of tensor t-structures on the
bounded
derived category
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
and connect it to some of the known results of
[
ATJLS10
]
,

[
Bez00
]
,
[
Gab04
]
and
[
Kas04
]
. But in this generality the
derived tensor product on
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
does not
restrict to the bounded derived category
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
unless
X
is regular. So we can not
define it
using the aisle of the standard t-structure as it is done in the unbounded case of
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
.
However,

\mathrm{{Perf}}{(X)}
has a well defined action on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
. Using this we have defined tensor
t-structures on the derived category
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
, see Definition
3.1
and
Remark
3.2
. Once we have the right definition we could prove that
every tensor t-structure on the bounded derived category is completely determined by its
support data. Suppose
\varphi
is a Thomason-Cousin filtration, see Definition

3.12
, consider the subcategory of
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
associated to
\varphi
which is defined as:

\mathcal{U}_{\varphi}=\{A\in\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)\mid\mathrm{Supp}(H^{i}(A))\subset\varphi(i)\}.

Then, we have the following theorem, it is a restatement of Theorem
3.14
. And
it generalizes
[
ATJLS10
, Theorem 6.9]
from Noetherian rings to schemes.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme admitting a dualizing complex. The pair of subcategories

(\mathcal{U}_{\varphi},\mathcal{U}_{\varphi}^{\perp})
is a tensor t-structure
on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
.

Conversely, any tensor t-structure
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
arises from a
Thomason-Cousin filtration, that is, there is a unique Thomason-Cousin filtration

\varphi
such that
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{U}_{\varphi}
and
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{U}_{\varphi}^{\perp}
.

As an easy consequence of this theorem, we show that every semi-orthogonal decomposition
on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
that satisfies the tensor condition is trivial, see Corollary
3.18
. In
particular, for a connected Noetherian ring admitting a dualizing complex, the bounded derived
category
\mathbf{D}^{b}(R)
is indecomposable.

In the expository article
[
Bez00
]
, Bezrukavnikov brought to attention the
unpublished results of Deligne concerning constructions of t-structures from perversity
functions. More precisely, in
[
Bez00
, Theorem 1]
, the author shows that given a monotone
comonotone perversity
function on
X
one can construct a t-structure on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
. Independently Gabber

[
Gab04
, Theorem 9.1]
and then Kashiwara
[
Kas04
, Theorem 5.9]
has also
obtained the same result.
In Theorem
4.3
we show such t-structures are tensor t-structures, and the
converse is true, that is, every tensor t-structure on

\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
arises in this way. This provides a complete picture of this circle of ideas, and
connects the very categorical notion of tensor t-structures on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
to both the
t-structures arising from perversity functions, and t-structures arising from Thomason-Cousin
filtrations. As an application of the characterization in terms of perversity functions, in
Proposition
4.11
we have shown, when the pullback of a tensor t-structure is a tensor
t-structure. In particular, when
X
is an integral finite type scheme over a field
k
, unlike
semi-orthogonal decompositions, the tensor t-structures on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
always restricts
to closed subschemes.

When this article was at the final stage of preparation we came across the preprint

[
BCL+24
]
, we were aware of their earlier shorter version in which the authors had
obtained the non-existence of tensor t-structures on perfect complexes of singular Noetherian
schemes. In the updated one the authors have now studied the bounded derived categories.
Their
[
BCL+24
, Theorem 3.12]
is similar to our Corollary
3.15
, probably this
is the only common point. Though they also use our earlier results of
[
DS23a
]
as we do to
obtain the above corollary, the perspective is quite different, we use our classification
Theorem
3.14
. Nevertheless, the notion of tensor t-structure on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)

and the connection to perversity functions is completely new in our article.

The second goal of this article is to study the notion of weight structures on the bounded
derived categories of smooth projective curves. Weight structures were introduced by
Bondarko as a natural counterpart of t-structures in
[
Bon10
]
. It has been observed by
Bondarko that the two notions, t-structures and weight structures, are connected by interesting
relations. In this vein, Sťovíček and
Pospíšil have proved for a certain class of triangulated categories, in particular for
the derived category
\mathbf{D}(R)
of a Noetherian ring, the
compactly generated t-structures and compactly generated weight structures are in bijection

[
SP16
, Theorem 4.10 and Theorem 4.15]
with each other, where the bijection goes via
a duality at the perfect complex level; note that the authors call them co-t-structures what we
call weight structures.

Analogous to the notion of tensor t-structures we have introduced the notion of tensor weight structures in
[
DS23b
]
on
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
and
have generalized the theorem of Sťovíček and Pospíšil from Noetherian rings
to schemes. That is, when
X
is a separated Noetherian scheme, there is a one-to-one
corresponds between the compactly generated tensor t-structures and compactly
generated tensor weight structures on
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
. In sharp contrast to the above fact
when one restricts to the bounded derived
category
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
, unlike tensor t-structures, tensor weight structures seems to vanish
altogether. We had earlier found that in the case of projective
line,
[
DS23b
, Proposition 30]
, there are no tensor weight structures. In the last section of
this article our further investigation shows that the same is true for all curves of higher genus,
see Theorem
5.11
.

This is interesting since in the affine case, at least for regular Noetherian rings, we have lots of
tensor weight structures particularly those coming from the brutal truncations. We have also
found that in the case of elliptic curves, not just tensor weight structures, there
are no weight structures on the bounded derived category other than the trivial ones, see
Theorem
5.8
.
Using the techniques developed for the above results, we could also provide a new proof of
the well know fact
[
Oka11
, Theorem 1.1]
that when
X
is a smooth projective curve of
genus greater than equal to one, there are no non-trivial semi-orthogonal decompositions on

\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
, that is, the bounded derived category is indecomposable in this case, see
Theorem
5.5
.

2.
The Antecedent

In this section, we briefly recall the definition of t-structures, compactly generated
t-structures and tensor t-structures;for more details we refer the reader to our earlier work

[
DS23a
]
. We freely use the notions of preaisle, aisle and their connection to t-structure,
for details see
[
DS23a
, §2]
. We follow the same notations and symbols as used in loc. cit.,
with one exception that is, instead of writing
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}[1])
as t-structures we use the
simpler notation
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
in this article.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category and
\mathcal{T}^{c}
denote the full subcategory of compact
objects.

Definition 2.1
.

A
t-structure
on
\mathcal{T}
is a pair of full subcategories
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
satisfying the
following properties:

t1.

\mathcal{U}[1]\subset\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}[-1]\subset\mathcal{V}
.

t2.

\mathcal{U}\perp\mathcal{V}
.

t3.

For any
T\in\mathcal{T}
there is a distinguished triangle

U\rightarrow T\rightarrow V\rightarrow U[1]

where
U\in\mathcal{U}
and
V\in\mathcal{V}
. We call such a triangle

truncation decomposition
of
T
.

In
[
Sta10
]
, Stanley has shown that the collection of t-structures on
\mathbf{D}(\mathbb{Z})
is a
proper class, not a set. Therefore, one can not classify them in terms of the subsets of the
spectrum. We restrict our attention to the smaller class - compactly generated ones.

Definition 2.2
.

We say a t-structure
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
on
\mathcal{T}
is
compactly generated
if there
is a set
of compact objects
\mathcal{S}
in
\mathcal{T}^{c}
such that for any non-zero
U\in\mathcal{U}
if

\mathrm{Hom}(S,U)=0
for all

S\in\mathcal{S}
then
U
is zero. For the sake of brevity we will call such t-structures

compact t-structures
.

In
[
ATJLS10
]
, the authors show that the compact t-structures on
\mathbf{D}(R)
for a
Noetherian ring can be characterised by their support data. We restate it here. Before that
we recall the definition of Thomason filtrations.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme. A
Thomason filtration
on
X
is a map

\varphi:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow 2^{X}
such that
\varphi
satisfies the following properties:

i.

for any
x
,
y

\in X
such that
y\in\bar{\{x\}}
, if
x\in\varphi(i)
then

y\in\varphi(i)
.

ii.

\varphi(i)\supset\varphi(i+1)
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We say a Thomason filtration is of
finite length
if for

j\ll 0
,
\varphi(j)=\varphi(j-1)
and for
i\gg 0
,
\varphi(i)=\emptyset
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring. There is a one-to-one correspondence between the
compact t-structures on
\mathbf{D}(R)
and the Thomason filtrations on

\mathrm{Spec}R
.

Proof.

[
ATJLS10
, Theorem 3.11]

∎

The following example is just a special case of
[
ATJLS10
, Remark, page 328]

Example 2.5
.

In the derived category of abelian groups the aisle generated by
\mathbb{Q}
is not compactly
generated. Indeed, if it is compactly generated then by the classification of compactly
generated t-structures it must be
\mathbf{D}^{\leq 0}(\mathbb{Z})
, However,
\mathbb{Z}\notin\langle\mathbb{Q}\rangle^{\leq 0}
.

In our earlier work, we have generalized Theorem

to arbitrary Noetherian schemes.
For the analogous statement to be true we introduced the notion of tensor t-structures,
motivated by the notion of rigid aisles in
[
ATJLSS03
, §5]
. We recall it here.

Definition 2.6
.

A t-structure
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
on
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
is a tensor t-structure if for any
U\in\mathcal{U}

and any
A\in\mathbf{D}^{\leq 0}_{qc}(X)
we have
U\otimes A\in\mathcal{U}
.

Recall that
\mathcal{U}_{\varphi}=\{A\in\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)\mid\mathrm{Supp}(H^{i}(A))\subset\varphi(i)\}
and with the above definition we have shown that:

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme. There is a one-to-one correspondence between compact
tensor t-structures on
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
and Thomason filtrations on
X
. In particular, every
compact tensor t-structure on
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
is of the form

(\mathcal{U}_{\varphi},\mathcal{U}_{\varphi}^{\perp})
where
\varphi

is a Thomason filtration on
X
.

Proof.

[
DS23a
, Theorem 5.11]

∎

Definition 2.8
.

We say a t-structure
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
is
principal
if it is generated by a single object of
the derived category, that is,
\mathcal{U}=\langle E\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes}
for some complex

E\in\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
.

The following statement about principal compact t-structures easily follows from the methods
developed in our earlier work. We state and prove it here.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme. There is a one-to-one correspondence between finite
length filtrations of closed subsets of
X
and principal compact tensor t-structures of

\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
.

Proof.

One direction is easy. Let
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
be a t-structure on
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
. It is principal and
compact means
\mathcal{U}=\langle E\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes}
for some perfect complex

E
. Recall that the Thomason filtration associated to
\mathcal{U}
which we denote by

\varphi_{\mathcal{U}}
is defined as follows

\varphi_{\mathcal{U}}(n)=\bigcup_{i\geq n}\mathrm{Supp}H^{i}(E)
. Now since
E
is perfect it is
clear that
\varphi_{\mathcal{U}}(n)
is closed, and boundedness of
E
will imply that the
filtration is of finite length.

Conversely, suppose we are given with a finite length filtration of closed subsets say

\varphi
, that is,
\varphi(n)
is a closed subset of
X
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. From

[
DS23a
, Lemma 5.10]
we have a perfect complex
E_{n}
such that

\mathrm{Supp}H^{n}(E_{n})=\varphi(n)
and
\mathrm{Supp}H^{m}(E_{n})\subset\varphi(n)
for any
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Now consider
E\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\oplus E_{n}
. Using
[
DS23a
, Lemma 5.9]
we can see
that
\mathcal{U}_{\varphi}=\langle E\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes}
. This proves that the
t-structure corresponding to a finite length filtration of closed sets is principal and compact.

∎

3.
Tensor t-structures on the bounded derived categories

3.1.
Tensor t-structures on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)

In this section we define the notion of tensor t-structures on the bounded derived category

\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
. But in this generality the derived tensor product on
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
does not restrict
to the bounded derived category
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
unless
X
is regular. So we can not define it
using the aisle of the standard t-structure
\mathbf{D}^{b,\leq 0}(X)
. However,

\mathrm{{Perf}}{(X)}

has a well defined action on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
. Using this we define tensor t-structures on the
derived category
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
.

Definition 3.1
.

We say a preaisle
\mathcal{U}
on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
is a
\otimes
-preaisle if for any
E\in\mathrm{{Perf}}^{\leq 0}{(X)}

and any
F\in\mathcal{U}
the derived tensor product
E\otimes F\in\mathcal{U}
. We say a
t-structure
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
is a tensor t-structure if the aisle
\mathcal{U}
is a

\otimes
-preaisle.

Remark 3.2
.

This definition seems to be more reasonable than an intrinsic definition. In the case of
unbounded derived category
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
, if one defines
\otimes
-preaisle using

\mathrm{{Perf}}^{\leq 0}{(X)}
, then one can easily show that this new definition is equivalent to our
earlier definition using the aisle
\mathbf{D}^{\leq 0}_{qc}(X)
of the standard t-structure.

Now, with this definition of tensor t-structures on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
we can prove that such
t-structures restricts well to open affine subsets. The arguments we used to show the
analogous statements in the unbounded case, see
[
DS23a
, §5.2]
, seems to work in the
bounded case with appropriate modifications.

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme and
U
be an open affine subset of
X
and
j:U\rightarrow X

denote the open immersion. Let
\mathcal{U}
be a
\otimes
-preaisle of
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
. We define

\mathcal{U}|_{U}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\langle j^{*}\mathcal{U}\rangle^{\leq 0}
- the restriction of

\mathcal{U}
to the open affine subset
U
. Suppose
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
is a tensor t-structure on

\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
. We define
\mathcal{V}|_{U}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(\mathcal{U}|_{U})^{\perp}
.

The key important lemma is the following:

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
E
and
F
be two complexes in
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
. Suppose there is a non-zero morphism

f\in\mathrm{Hom}(E|_{U},F|_{U})
in
\mathbf{D}^{b}(U)
. Then, there is a perfect complex

K\in\mathrm{{Perf}}^{\leq 0}{(X)}
such that there is a non-zero map
{f}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Hom}(E\otimes K,F)
.

Proof.

By assumption we have

\mathrm{Hom}(j^{*}E,j^{*}F)\neq 0
. By tensor-hom adjunction we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}(j^{*}E,j^{*}F)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Hom}(j^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X},\mathbf{R}\mathcal{H}om^{\cdot}_{U}(j^{*}E,j^{*}F))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Hom}(j^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X},j^{*}\mathbf{R}\mathcal{H}om^{\cdot}_{X}(E,F)).

Now if we write
{F}^{\prime}=\mathbf{R}\mathcal{H}om^{\cdot}_{X}(E,F)
then
\mathrm{Hom}(j^{*}E,j^{*}F)\neq 0

implies the group
\mathrm{Hom}(j^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X},j^{*}{F}^{\prime})\neq 0
. Applying

[
DS23a
, Lemma 4.15]
to the preaisle
\mathrm{Perf}^{\leq N}_{Z}(X)
where
N=0

and

Z=X
, we get that there is a perfect complex
K\in\mathrm{Perf}^{\leq 0}(X)
such that

\mathrm{Hom}(K,{F}^{\prime})\neq 0
. Again by tensor-hom adjunction

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}(K,{F}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Hom}(K,\mathbf{R}\mathcal{H}om^{\cdot}_{X}(E,F))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Hom}(K\otimes E,F)\neq 0,

∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be a
\otimes
-preaisle of
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
. If
F\in(\mathcal{U})^{\perp}
then
j^{*}F\in(\mathcal{U}|_{U})^{\perp}
.

Proof.

Suppose there is an
F\in(\mathcal{U})^{\perp}
such that
j^{*}F\notin(\mathcal{U}|_{U})^{\perp}
then there is an
E\in\mathcal{U}
such that

\mathrm{Hom}(j^{*}E,j^{*}F)\neq 0
. Now, by the Lemma
3.3
we have
K\in\mathrm{{Perf}}^{\leq 0}{(X)}

such that
\mathrm{Hom}(E\otimes K,F)\neq 0
. Since
\mathcal{U}
is a tensor preaisle,

K\otimes E\in\mathcal{U}
and this contradicts the fact that
F\in(\mathcal{U})^{\perp}
.

∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme and
U
be an open subscheme of
X
. Let
A
be in

\mathbf{D}^{b}(U)
then there exist
\tilde{A}
in
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
such that
\tilde{A}|_{U}\cong A
.

Proof.

It is a special case of
[
Bez00
, Corollary 2]
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
be tensor t-structure on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
. Then
(\mathcal{U}|_{U},\mathcal{V}|_{U})

is
a t-structure on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(U)
. In particular, for any
A\in\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
the triangle

j^{*}\tau_{\mathcal{U}}^{\leq}A\rightarrow j^{*}A\rightarrow j^{*}\tau_{\mathcal{U}}^{>}A\rightarrow j^{*}\tau_{\mathcal{U}}^{\leq}A[1],

is a t-decomposition triangle of
j^{*}A
in
\mathbf{D}^{b}(U)
.

Proof.

To show
(\mathcal{U}|_{U},\mathcal{V}|_{U})
is a t-structure on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(U)
it is enough to
show that for any
T\in\mathbf{D}^{b}(U)
there is a t-decomposition triangle.

Let
T\in\mathbf{D}^{b}(U)
. Consider any extension
\tilde{T}
of
T
in
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
as in
Lemma
3.5
. Now, since
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
is a t-structure on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
we have
a t-decomposition in
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
say,

\tau_{\mathcal{U}}^{\leq}\tilde{T}\rightarrow\tilde{T}\rightarrow\tau_{\mathcal{U}}^{>}\tilde{T}\rightarrow\tau_{\mathcal{U}}^{\leq}\tilde{T}[1].

By the construction of
\mathcal{U}|_{U}
, we have
j^{*}\tau_{\mathcal{U}}^{\leq}\tilde{T}
is in
\mathcal{U}|_{U}
, and by our Lemma
3.4
, we have
j^{*}\tau_{\mathcal{U}}^{>}\tilde{T}
is in
(\mathcal{U}|_{U})^{\perp}
.
Therefore, applying
j^{*}
to the above triangle give us

j^{*}\tau_{\mathcal{U}}^{\leq}\tilde{T}\rightarrow T\rightarrow j^{*}\tau_{\mathcal{U}}^{>}\tilde{T}\rightarrow j^{*}\tau_{\mathcal{U}}^{\leq}\tilde{T}[1],

which is a t-decomposition triangle for
T
in
\mathbf{D}^{b}(U)
.

∎

.

Let
\mathcal{U}

\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)

A\in\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
,

\tau_{X}(A)|_{U}\cong\tau_{U}(A|_{U}).

We will use this in Theorem
3.14
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
E
and
F
be two complexes in
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
. If
\mathrm{Supp}H^{k}(E)\subset\cup_{i\geq k}\mathrm{Supp}(H^{i}(F))
then
E\in\langle{F}\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes}
, where
\langle{-}\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes}
is taken in

\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
.

Proof.

Let us denote the
\otimes
-preaisle
\langle{F}\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes}
by
\mathcal{U}^{F}
. By

[
DS23a
, Lemma 5.2]
and the fact that
F\in\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
, we get that the associated
filtration of
\mathcal{U}^{F}
is Thomason. Hence, by the Theorem
2.7
we have

\mathcal{U}^{F}
is a
\otimes
-aisle. Now, since
\otimes
-aisles restrict to aisle on open affine
subsets by
[
DS23a
, Lemma 5.6]
,
\mathcal{U}^{F}|_{U}
is an aisle in
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(U)
, for any
affine open subset
U
of
X
. We can then apply
[
Hrb20
, Proposition 5.1]
to

E|_{U}
and
F|_{U}
, to conclude that
E|_{U}\in\mathcal{U}^{F}|_{U}
. Note that, this is
enough to show that
E\in\mathcal{U}^{F}
.
∎

Recall that given a Thomason filtration
\varphi
on
X
one can take the associated
preaisle
\mathcal{U}_{\varphi}
in the unbounded derived category
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
, which is a
compact aisle.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme and
\mathcal{U}
be a
\otimes
-preaisle on

\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
. Then, there is a unique Thomason filtration
\varphi
on
X
such
that
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{U}_{\varphi}\cap\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be a
\otimes
-preaisle of
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
. We can take smallest cocomplete
preaisle
generated by the objects of
\mathcal{U}
in
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
, that is,
\langle{U}\rangle^{\leq 0}
. Since
\mathcal{U}

is
\otimes
-preaisle,
\langle{U}\rangle^{\leq 0}
is also
\otimes
-preaisle of
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
. Using the definition
of
graded support
[
DS23a
]
, and the Lemma 5.2 loc. cit, we can easily conclude that
the corresponding filtration of
\langle{U}\rangle^{\leq 0}
is a Thomason filtration, say
\varphi
and
since
\langle{U}\rangle^{\leq 0}
is a
\otimes
-preaisle we have
\langle{U}\rangle^{\leq 0}
=
\mathcal{U}_{\varphi}
.

It is clear that
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{U}_{\varphi}\cap\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
. We need to show the
reverse inclusion. Suppose
E\in\mathcal{U}_{\varphi}\cap\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
, by the construction of

\mathcal{U}_{\varphi}
above we have a collection of objects
B_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{U}
such
that
\mathrm{Supp}^{\geq i}E\subset\cup_{i}\mathrm{Supp}^{\geq i}B_{\alpha}
. Since
\mathrm{Supp}^{\geq i}E
is a closed subset of
X
we can consider the generic points of its irreducible
components and the
B_{\alpha}
’s containing the generic points, therefore, there are only
finitely many
B_{\alpha}
’s such that
\mathrm{Supp}^{\geq i}E\subset\cup_{i}\mathrm{Supp}^{\geq i}B_{\alpha}
. Now, we apply the Lemma
3.9
to conclude our claim.

∎

3.2.
Noetherian schemes admitting dualising complexes

For a Noetherian ring
R
, in
[
ATJLS10
, Corollary 4.5]
, the authors show if a compact
t-structure
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
on the unbounded derived category
\mathbf{D}(R)
restrict to a
t-structure on the bounded derived category
\mathbf{D}^{b}(R)
then the corresponding Thomason
filtration satisfies an additional condition, which the authors call weak Cousin condition. And
in the presence of a dualizing complex in
[
ATJLS10
, Theorem 6.9]
the authors have shown
that the filtration being weak-Cousin is necessary and sufficient.

The goal of the section is to generalise the above theorem for Noetherian rings to schemes as
well as to show that every tensor t-structure on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
arises in this way. Let us first
recall the definition of weak Cousin condition given in
[
ATJLS10
, §4]
.

Definition 3.11
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring. We denote
X\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathrm{Spec}R
.
Let
\varphi:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow 2^{X}
be a Thomason filtration. We say that
\varphi
satisfies the weak
Cousin condition: if for every
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, if
\mathfrak{p}\subsetneq\mathfrak{q}
, with

\mathfrak{p}
maximal under
\mathfrak{q}
and
\mathfrak{q}\in\varphi(j)
then

\mathfrak{p}\in\varphi(j-1)
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme of finite Krull dimension. We modify the above definition
suitably and call such filtrations Thomason-Cousin instead of weak-Cousin so as to
retain Thomason’s name and highlight his seminal contribution in
[
Tho97
]
.

Definition 3.12
.

We say an ordered pair
(y,x)
of points of
X
is a
specialization pair
of
X
if

y\in\bar{\{x\}}
. Given a specialization pair
(y,x)
we define the codimension function as

\delta_{X}(y,x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}codim(\bar{\{y\}},\bar{\{x\}})=\mathrm{dim}{\mathcal{O}}_{y,X}-\mathrm{dim}{\mathcal{O}}_{x,X}
.

We say a Thomason filtration is
Thomason-Cousin
if for any specialization pair

(y,x)
, if
y\in\varphi(i)
then
x\in\varphi(i-\delta_{X}(y,x))
.

Note that a Thomason-Cousin filtration in this case is a finite length filtration, more precisely
for
i\ll 0
we have
\varphi(i)=X
and for
j\gg 0
we have
\varphi(j)=\emptyset
.

After stating it in the affine case we will extend it to schemes.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring admitting a dualizing complex. Let
\mathcal{U}
be a preaisle of

\mathbf{D}^{b}(R)
and
\varphi
its associated filtration as in
3.10
. Then,
\mathcal{U}
is an
aisle of
\mathbf{D}^{b}(R)
if and only if
\varphi
is Thomason-Cousin.

Proof.

[
ATJLS10
, Theorem 6.9]

∎

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme admitting a dualizing complex. Let
\mathcal{U}
be a
\otimes
-preaisle of
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
and
\varphi
its associated filtration as in
3.10
.
Then,
\mathcal{U}
is an
aisle if and only if
\varphi
is Thomason-Cousin.

Proof.

First, we will show, if
\mathcal{U}
is an aisle then
\varphi
is a Thomason-Cousin filtration.

Let
(y,x)
be a specialization pair. Clearly there is an open affine subset
U
containing
both
y
and
x
. We can now restrict the aisle
\mathcal{U}
to the open affine subset
U
,
that is
\mathcal{U}|_{U}
, which is an aisle on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(U)
by Lemma
3.6
. The
corresponding filtration of
\mathcal{U}|_{U}
is
\varphi|_{U}
.
Now since
\mathcal{U}|_{U}
is an aisle, from Theorem
3.13
we get that

\varphi|_{U}
is a Thomason-Cousin filtration on
U
. Since this is true for all
pair of specialization points we get that
\varphi
is Thomason-Cousin.

Now, the converse part: we need to show
\mathcal{U}
is an aisle if
\varphi
is
Thomason-Cousin. By Proposition
3.10
we know that
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{U}_{\varphi}\cap\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
, and
\mathcal{U}_{\varphi}
is an aisle of
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)

by Theorem
2.7
. Note that to show
\mathcal{U}
is an aisle on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
it
is enough to show that the truncation functor for
\mathcal{U}_{\varphi}
, that is,

\tau_{\varphi}
restricts to
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
.

In other words, we need to show for any
A\in\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
we have

\tau_{\varphi}(A)\in\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
. Indeed, this is the case, by Remark

3.8
we have
\tau_{\varphi}(A)|_{U}\cong\tau_{\varphi|_{U}}(A|_{U})
.
Now since
\varphi
is Thomason-Cousin we have
\varphi|_{U}
is Thomason-Cousin hence

\tau_{\varphi|_{U}}(A|_{U})
is in
\mathbf{D}^{b}(U)
by Theorem
3.13
.
Since
U
is arbitrary we get that
\tau_{\varphi}(A)
is in
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
. This proves the
theorem.

∎

Corollary 3.15
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme admitting a dualizing complex. A compactly generated
tensor t-structure
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
on
\mathbf{D}_{\text{qc}}(X)
restrict to a t-structure on

\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
if and only if the corresponding Thomason filtration
\varphi
satisfies
Thomason-Cousin condition.

Proof.

This is just the combination of Theorem
2.7
, Proposition
3.10

and Theorem
3.14
.
∎

We will give a few corollaries of Theorem
3.14
concerning semi-orthogonal
decompositions. First, we recall the definition.

Definition 3.16
.

A
semi-orthogonal decomposition
on a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
is a pair of full
subcategories
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
satisfying the
following properties:

s1.

Both
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are triangulated subcategories.

s2.

\mathcal{B}\perp\mathcal{A}
.

s3.

For any
T\in\mathcal{T}
there is a distinguished triangle

B\rightarrow T\rightarrow A\rightarrow B[1]

where
B\in\mathcal{B}
and
A\in\mathcal{A}
.

Note that if
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is a semi-orthogonal decomposition on
\mathcal{T}
then

(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A})
is a t-structure on
\mathcal{T}
.

Definition 3.17
.

We say
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is a
tensor semi-orthogonal decomposition
on

\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
if

\mathcal{B}
is a
\otimes
-preaisle of
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
, that is, if for any
E\in\mathrm{{Perf}}^{\leq 0}{(X)}
and
any
F\in\mathcal{B}
the derived tensor product
E\otimes F\in\mathcal{B}
.

Corollary 3.18
.

Let
X
be a connected Noetherian scheme admitting a dualizing complex. Every tensor
semi-orthogonal decomposition on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
is trivial.

Proof.

First note that if
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is a semi-orthogonal decomposition of
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
then

(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A})
is a t-structure on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
. Now since
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is tensor
we have
(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A})
is a tensor t-structure on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
. Then by
Theorem
3.14
there
is a unique Thomason-Cousin filtration
\varphi
such that
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{U}_{\varphi}\cap\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
. Since
\mathcal{B}
is triangulated subcategory the filtration must be constant
and again by our observation that
\varphi(i)=X
for some
i\ll 0
it must be the constant
filtration, that is,
\varphi(i)=X
for all
i
, this implies that
\mathcal{B}=\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
.

∎

Corollary 3.19
.

If
R
is a connected Noetherian ring that admits a dualizing complex then the derived category

\mathbf{D}^{b}(R)
admits no non-trivial semi-orthogonal decompositions.

Proof.

It follows from Corollary
3.18
by the noting that every semi-orthogonal
decomposition on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(R)
is tensor.
∎

4.
Perversity functions and tensor t-structures

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme admitting a dualizing complex. In
[
Bez00
, Theorem 1]
,
following Deligne, Bezrukavnikov has shown that given a monotone comonotone perversity
function on
X
one can construct a t-structure on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
. In this section we show
that such t-structures are tensor t-structures. And conversely, every tensor t-structure on

\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
arises in this way.

Definition 4.1
.

Any bounded integer valued function on
X
is called a
perversity
function on
X
. A
perversity function is called
monotone
if for any specialization pair
(y,x)
we have

p(y)\geq p(x)
.

Note that the dimension function
d_{X}:X\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
defined as

x\mapsto\mathrm{dim}({\mathcal{O}}_{x,X})
is a monotone perversity function. The
dual
of
a perversity function
p
denoted by
\bar{p}
is defined to be
d_{X}-p
. One says a
perversity function
p
is comonotone if
\bar{p}
is monotone.

Given a monotone perversity
p
on
X
one can define a pair of subcategories

(\mathcal{U}^{p},\mathcal{V}^{p})
on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
as follows:

\mathcal{U}^{p}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{F\in\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)\mid\text{for any }x,F_{x}\in\mathbf{D}^{\leq p(x)}({\mathcal{O}}_{x,X})\},

\mathcal{V}^{p}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{G\in\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)\mid\text{for any }x,(\mathbf{R}\Gamma_{\bar{\{x\}}}G)_{x}\in\mathbf{D}^{>p(x)}({\mathcal{O}}_{x,X})\}.

The following is a well known theorem due to Deligne-Bezrukavnikov.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme admitting a dualizing complex. Let
p
be a monotone
perversity on
X
and
(\mathcal{U}^{p},\mathcal{V}^{p})
as defined above. Then,

(\mathcal{U}^{p},\mathcal{V}^{p})
is a t-structure on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
if
p
is comonotone.

Proof.

[
Bez00
, Theorem 1]
.
∎

We will show such t-structures are tensor t-structures and the converse is also true.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme admitting a dualizing complex. The pair of subcategories

(\mathcal{U}^{p},\mathcal{V}^{p})
is a tensor t-structure on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
.

Conversely, every tensor-t-structure on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
is of the form
(\mathcal{U}^{p},\mathcal{V}^{p})

for some monotone comonotone perversity function
p
on
X
.

Proof.

Theorem
4.2
says it is a t-structure. We only need to show that it is a tensor
t-structure, that is, if
E\in\mathrm{{Perf}}^{\leq 0}{(X)}
and
F\in\mathcal{U}^{p}
then

E\otimes F\in\mathcal{U}^{p}
. From the description of
\mathcal{U}^{p}
, we need to show that

(E\otimes F)_{x}=E_{x}\otimes F_{x}\in\mathbf{D}^{\leq p(x)}({\mathcal{O}}_{x,X})
for any

x\in X
.

This we deduce from the following spectral sequence,

E^{i,j}_{2}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}H^{i}(E_{x}\otimes H^{j}(F_{x}))\Longrightarrow H^{i+j}(E_{x}\otimes F_{x}).

If
i+j>p(x)
then either
i>0
or
j>p(x)
. In either case, the
E^{i,j}_{2}

term vanishes, since
E_{x}\in\mathrm{{Perf}}^{\leq 0}{(X)}
and
F_{x}\in\mathbf{D}^{\leq p(x)}({\mathcal{O}}_{x,X})
. Hence
H^{i+j}(E_{x}\otimes F_{x})=0
whenever,

i+j>p(x)
. This proves our claim.

The converse part follows from the below Lemma
4.4
and our
Theorem
1.1
which is just a restatement of Theorem
3.14
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

The following maps provide a one-to-one correspondence between the set of monotone
perversity function on
X
and the set of finite length Thomason filtrations on
X
:

p\longmapsto\varphi,\hskip 28.45274pt\text{ where }\varphi(n)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{x\in X\mid p(x)\geq n\}\text{ for }n\in\mathbb{Z},

and

\varphi\longmapsto p,\hskip 28.45274pt\text{ where }p(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\text{max}\{n\mid x\in\varphi(n)\}\text{ for }x\in X.

Moreover, if a perversity function
p
corresponds to
\varphi
under this bijection, then

\mathcal{U}_{\varphi}=\mathcal{U}^{p}
and
\mathcal{U}_{\varphi}^{\perp}=\mathcal{V}^{p}
. Further,
p
is comonotone if and only if
\varphi
is Thomason-Cousin.

Proof.

This is an elementary but long check. Probably this is standard and well known, but
we provide a proof for the convenience of the reader.

Suppose
p
is a perversity function on
X
and
\varphi
is defined as above. Then, it
is easy to see that
\varphi
is a finite length filtration, that is,
\varphi(i+1)\subset\varphi(i)
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
and for some
m>n
, we have
\varphi(n)=X
and

\varphi(m)=\emptyset
.
And, the converse is also true, that is, if we start with a filtration
\varphi
having the above
property
and define a map
p
as above then it is a perversity function on
X
. Now,

\displaystyle p\text{ is monotone}

\displaystyle\iff p(y)\geq p(x)\text{ for all specialization pair }(y,x),

\displaystyle\iff\text{if }x\in\varphi(i)\text{ then }y\in\varphi(i)\text{ for all specialization pair }(y,x),

\displaystyle\iff\varphi\text{ is Thomason.}

Next, we will show that
\mathcal{U}_{\varphi}=\mathcal{U}^{p}
.

\displaystyle F\notin\mathcal{U}_{\varphi}

\displaystyle\iff\exists i\in\mathbb{Z},\text{ such that }\mathrm{Supp}H^{i}(F)\nsubseteq\varphi(i),

\displaystyle\iff\exists i\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ and }x\in X,\text{ such that }x\in\mathrm{Supp}H^{i}(F)\text{ and }x\notin\varphi(i),

\displaystyle\iff\exists i\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ and }x\in X,\text{ such that }F_{x}\notin\mathbf{D}^{\leq i-1}({\mathcal{O}}_{x,X})\text{ and }p(x)\leq i-1,

\displaystyle\iff\exists x\in X,\text{ such that }F_{x}\notin\mathbf{D}^{\leq p(x)}({\mathcal{O}}_{x,X}),

\displaystyle\iff F\notin\mathcal{U}^{p}

We have
\mathcal{U}_{\varphi}=\mathcal{U}^{p}
, hence
\mathcal{U}_{\varphi}^{\perp}=\mathcal{U}^{p}{{}^{\perp}}
. By
[
Bez00
, Proposition 2]
we have
\mathcal{U}^{p}{{}^{\perp}}=\mathcal{V}^{p}
. This proves our claim
\mathcal{U}_{\varphi}^{\perp}=\mathcal{V}^{p}
. Next, suppose
p
is
a monotone perversity and
\varphi
its corresponding Thomason filtration. Then, we have
the following chain of equivalent statements:

\displaystyle p\text{ is comonotone}

\displaystyle\iff d_{X}-p\text{ is monotone, }

\displaystyle\iff d_{X}(y)-p(y)\geq d_{X}(x)-p(x)\text{ for all specialization pair }(y,x),

\displaystyle\iff p(x)\geq p(y)-(d_{X}(y)-d_{X}(x))\text{ for all specialization pair }(y,x),

\displaystyle\iff p(x)\geq p(y)-\delta(y,x)\text{ for all specialization pair }(y,x),

\displaystyle\iff\text{if }y\in\varphi(i)\text{ then }x\in\varphi(i-\delta(y,x))\text{ for all specialization pair }(y,x),

\displaystyle\iff\varphi\text{ is Thomason-Cousin. }

∎

Recall that we say a t-structure
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
is principal if the aisle

\mathcal{U}
is generated by a single object of
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
, that is, there is a complex

E\in\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
such that
\mathcal{U}=\langle{E}\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes}
.

Proposition 4.5
.

A tensor t-structure on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
is principal if and only if the corresponding monotone
comonotone perversity is upper semi-continuous.

Proof.

Recall that a function
p:X\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is upper semi-continuous if and only if for each

n\in\mathbb{Z}
the set
\varphi(n)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{x\in X\mid p(x)\geq n\}
is a closed subset of

X
. The filtration
\{\varphi(n)\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is precisely the Thomason-Cousin filtration
corresponding to
p
; see Lemma
4.4
. Now our claim follows from
Proposition
2.9
and Proposition
3.10
.

∎

4.1.
Tensor t-structures under the pullback

Recall from
[
DS23a
, §2 and §3]
that if
\mathcal{S}
is a collection of objects of
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
,
we denote the smallest
\otimes
-preaisle containing
\mathcal{S}
by
\langle{S}\rangle^{\leq 0}_{\otimes}
. Let
f:X\rightarrow Y

be a morphism of schemes. Suppose
\mathcal{A}
is an aisle of
\mathbf{D}^{b}(Y)
. We
define the image of
\mathcal{A}
under the derived pullback functor
\mathbf{L}f^{*}
as

\langle{\mathbf{L}f^{*}A\mid A\in\mathcal{A}\rangle}^{\leq 0}_{\otimes}
and denote it by

\mathbf{L}f^{*}\mathcal{A}
.
In this section we will answer the question when
\mathbf{L}f^{*}\mathcal{A}
is an aisle of

\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
using the characterization in terms of perversity functions. First, we need to
slightly enlarge our objects of study.

Definition 4.6
.

We say a preaisle
\mathcal{U}
of
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
is
bounded
if there are integers

m\leq n
such that,

\mathbf{D}^{b,\leq m}(X)\subset\mathcal{U}\subset\mathbf{D}^{b,\leq n}(X).

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme. Then, there is a one-to-one correspondence between
the bounded
\otimes
-preaisles of
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
and monotone perversity functions on
X
.
The bijection is given as follows:

\mathcal{U}\longmapsto p_{\mathcal{U}},\hskip 28.45274pt\text{ where \hskip 2.84526pt }p_{\mathcal{U}}(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\text{max}\{n\mid E_{x}\in\mathbf{D}^{\leq n}({\mathcal{O}}_{x,X})\text{ for all }E\in\mathcal{U}\}

and,

p\longmapsto\mathcal{U}_{p},\hskip 28.45274pt\text{ where \hskip 2.84526pt }\mathcal{U}^{p}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{F\in\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)\mid\text{for any }x,F_{x}\in\mathbf{D}^{\leq p(x)}({\mathcal{O}}_{x,X})\}.

Proof.

This is a slight extension of Theorem
4.3
. The only new thing to check here is
boundedness. Let
p
be a monotone perversity function. As
p
is bounded there are
integers
m\leq n
such that
p(x)\in[m,n]
for all
x\in X
. Therefore,
\mathbf{D}^{b,\leq m}(X)\subset\mathcal{U}_{p}\subset\mathbf{D}^{b,\leq n}(X)

\mathcal{U}
is a bounded
\otimes
-preaisle

is a monotone perversity function on

. The
rest follows from the arguments used in the proof of Theorem

∎

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
f:X\rightarrow Y
be a morphism of finite type integral schemes over a field
k
. Let
\mathcal{A}

be a bounded
\otimes
-preaisle of
\mathbf{D}^{b}(Y)
. Then,
\mathbf{L}f^{*}\mathcal{A}
is a bounded

\otimes
-preaisle of
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
. Further, if
p_{\mathcal{A}}
is the monotone perversity
function associated to
\mathcal{A}
as in

, then
p_{\mathcal{A}}\circ f
is the
associated monotone perversity function of
\mathbf{L}f^{*}\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

From the construction of
\mathbf{L}f^{*}\mathcal{A}
, it is clear that it is a
\otimes
-preaisle of

\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
. To show it is bounded, first we observe that

\mathbf{L}f^{*}\mathbf{D}^{b,\leq i}(Y)=\mathbf{D}^{b,\leq i}(X)

for any integer

. This immediately shows that if
\mathcal{A}
is bounded then

\mathbf{L}f^{*}\mathcal{A}
is bounded.
Now from Proposition
4.7
,

\displaystyle p_{\mathbf{L}f^{*}\mathcal{A}}(x)

\displaystyle=\text{max}\{n\mid F_{x}\in\mathbf{D}^{\leq n}({\mathcal{O}}_{x,X})\text{ for all }F\in\mathbf{L}f^{*}\mathcal{A}\},

\displaystyle=\text{max}\{n\mid(\mathbf{L}f^{*}E)_{x}\in\mathbf{D}^{\leq n}({\mathcal{O}}_{x,X})\text{ for all }E\in\mathcal{A}\}.

We have
(\mathbf{L}f^{*}E)_{x}\cong E_{f(x)}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{f(x),Y}}{\mathcal{O}}_{x,X}
, and
the following spectral sequence,

E^{i,j}_{2}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathrm{Tor}^{i}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{f(x),Y}}(H^{j}(E_{f(x)}),{\mathcal{O}}_{x,X})\Longrightarrow H^{i+j}((\mathbf{L}f^{*}E)_{x}).

Whenever,

, then either

or

. In either case, the

Now, when

∎

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
f:X\rightarrow Y
be a flat morphism or a closed immersion of finite type integral schemes
over a field
k
. If
(a,b)
is a specialization pair on
X
then,

\delta_{X}(a,b)\geq\delta_{Y}(f(a),f(b)).

Proof.

Note that the continuity of
f
implies that
f
takes specialization pairs to specialization
pairs, that is,
f(a)\in\bar{\{f(b)\}}
. Since every open subset containing
f(a)
contains

f(b)
, we can choose an affine open subset of
Y
containing the pair
(f(a),f(b))
say

V
. Similarly, we can choose an affine open subset
containing the pair
(a,b)
in
f^{-1}(V)
, say
U
. As
X
and
Y
are integral schemes of
finite type over a field, we have

\delta_{X}(a,b)=\delta_{U}(a,b)
when
(a,b)
is in
U
, similarly for

\delta_{Y}
and
\delta_{V}
.Thus, we can reduce to the case when both
X
and
Y
are
affine.

Let
X=\mathrm{Spec}B
and
Y=\mathrm{Spec}A
. Suppose
\psi:A\rightarrow B
is the corresponding ring homomorphism.
To show
\delta_{X}(a,b)\geq\delta_{Y}(f(a),f(b))
when
a,b\in\mathrm{Spec}B
is same as
showing
\delta_{X}(a,b)\geq\delta_{Y}(\psi^{-1}(a),\psi^{-1}(b))
.
The inequality holds if either of the following statements are true:

(*)\hskip 2.84526ptf_{a}:\mathrm{Spec}B_{a}\rightarrow\mathrm{Spec}A_{\psi^{-1}(a)}\text{ is surjective,}\iff\psi\text{
has going-down property. }

or,

({*}^{\prime})\hskip 2.84526pt\bar{f}:\mathrm{Spec}(B/b)\rightarrow\mathrm{Spec}(A/\psi^{-1}(b))\text{ is surjective,}\iff\psi\text{ has
going-up property. }

By our assumption
f
is either flat or a closed immersion hence
f
has either

(*)
or
({*}^{\prime})
. This proves our claim.

∎

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
f:X\rightarrow Y
be as in Lemma

. If

is a monotone comonotone perversity on

is a monotone comonotone

Proof.

It easy to see that if

is a monotone

\displaystyle q\text{ is comonotone }

\displaystyle\iff d_{X}-q\text{ is monotone, }

\displaystyle\iff d_{X}(y)-q(y)\geq d_{X}(x)-q(x)\text{ for all specialization pair }(y,x),

\displaystyle\iff d_{X}(y)-d_{X}(x)\geq q(y)-q(x)\text{ for all specialization pair }(y,x),

To show

The last inequality comes from Lemma

5.
Weight structures on the bounded derived categories of curves

In our earlier work
[
DS23b
]
, we observed that there are no non-trivial tensor weight
structures on the bounded derived category of
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
. In this section, we
investigate the same question in the higher genus curves. First, we recall the definition
of weight structures.

Definition 5.1
.

A
weight structure
on a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
is a pair of
full subcategories
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
satisfying the following properties:

w0.

\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
are closed under direct summands.

w1.

\mathcal{X}[-1]\subset\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}[1]\subset\mathcal{Y}
.

w2.

\mathcal{X}\perp\mathcal{Y}
.

w3.

For any object
T\in\mathcal{T}
there is a distinguished triangle

A\rightarrow T\rightarrow B\rightarrow A[1]

where
A\in\mathcal{X}
and
B\in\mathcal{Y}
. The above triangle is called
a
weight decomposition
of
T
.

Now, let
X
be a smooth projective curve over a field
k
. As we will be repeatedly using the
non vanishing of
\mathrm{Hom}
-sets in
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
between coherent sheaves, we
collect them here in one place and refer to the following lemma whenever we use them.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective curve of genus
g
over a field
k
. Let
T
be a
torsion sheaf of degree
d
and
W
be a locally free sheaf of rank
r
on

X
. Then we have the following:

(a)\dim_{k}\mathrm{Hom}(T,W)=0
,

(b)\dim_{k}\mathrm{Hom}(T,W[1])=r.d
,

(c)\dim_{k}\mathrm{Hom}(W,W)=r^{2}
,

(d)\dim_{k}\mathrm{Hom}(W,W[1])=r^{2}.g
,

(e)\dim_{k}\mathrm{Hom}(W,T)=r.d
,

(f)\dim_{k}\mathrm{Hom}(W,T[1])=0
.

Proof.

Part
(a)
and
(c)
are obvious. Now Part
(b)
.

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}(T,W[1])^{\check{}}

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(T,W)^{\check{}}\text{ \hskip 56.9055pt ( by definition of Hom in $\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)$ )}

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Hom}(W,T\otimes\omega_{X})\text{ \hskip 28.45274pt ( by Serre Duality )}

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Hom}({\mathcal{O}}_{X},T\otimes\check{W})\text{ \hskip 25.60747pt ( by tensor
adjunction )}

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Hom}({\mathcal{O}}_{X},T^{\oplus^{r}})

\displaystyle\cong\Gamma(X,T^{\oplus^{r}})

And since
\dim_{k}\Gamma(X,T^{\oplus^{r}})=r.d
, this proves part
(b)
.

For part
(d)
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}(W,W[1])^{\check{}}

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(W,W)^{\check{}}\text{ \hskip 56.9055pt ( by definition of Hom in $\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)$ )}

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Hom}(W,W\otimes\omega_{X})\text{ \hskip 28.45274pt ( by Serre Duality )}

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Hom}(W\otimes\check{W},\omega_{X})\text{ \hskip 25.60747pt ( by tensor
adjunction )}

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Hom}({\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{\oplus^{r^{2}}},\omega_{X})

\displaystyle\cong\oplus^{r^{2}}\Gamma(X,\omega_{X})

By assumption
\dim_{k}\Gamma(X,\omega_{X})=g
, this proves part
(d)
.
Part
(e)
and
(f)
can easily be obtained by applying Serre duality to
(b)
and
(a)

respectively.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
E
be a complex in
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
such that
H^{-1}(E)
is a torsion sheaf. Let

V
be a locally free sheaf on
X
and
\delta:E\rightarrow V[1]
be a map
in
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
. Suppose the map
\delta
fits in to the following distinguished triangle
with the third term

,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V[1]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E[1].}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta}

Then, for any torsion sheaf
T\in\mathrm{Coh}\hskip 2.84526ptX

\mathrm{Hom}(T,F)\neq 0
.

Proof.

As
\mathrm{Coh}\hskip 2.84526ptX
has homological dimension one, every complex in

\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
splits into direct sum of its cohomology sheaves. Again since
\mathrm{Ext}^{i}(-,-)

vanish for

, it is enough to assume that

.
Now by the assumption
H^{-1}(E)
is a torsion sheaf hence there are no non-zero map
from
H^{-1}(E)[1]
to
V[1]
, so the map
\delta
only depends on

\mathrm{Hom}(H^{0}(E),V[1])
.

By abuse of notation we denote the map
H^{0}(E)\rightarrow V[1]
by
\delta
; and for
simplicity we write
H^{0}(E)
by

\mathrm{Hom}(A,V[1])\cong\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(A,V)

\delta:A\rightarrow V[1]
in
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
corresponds to an element of the
group
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(A,V)
, we denote the corresponding element in
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(A,V)
again by

\delta
.

Now we take the short exact sequence corresponding to
\delta\in\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(A,V)
,
say
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

From the short exact sequence it is clear that

has a torsion-free part, as

is
locally free. This short exact sequence gives rise to the following triangle,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V[1].}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta}

Now, comparing the above triangle with the one in the hypothesis, we can easily see
that
F
has a direct summand isomorphic to

. Thus to show
\mathrm{Hom}(T,F)\neq 0
,
it is enough to show
\mathrm{Hom}(T,B[1])\neq 0
. Indeed this is the case by part

of Lemma
5.2

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective curve over a field
k
of genus
g\geq 1
, and

(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
be a weight structure on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
. Suppose
\mathcal{X}\neq 0
.
Then, for each
i\in\mathbb{Z}
there is a locally free sheaf
W
on
X
such that
W[i]\in\mathcal{X}
.

Proof.

We define
\Lambda\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{j\in\mathbb{Z}\mid\text{there is a locally free sheaf }W\text{such that }W[j]\in\mathcal{X}\}
. To prove the claim we need to show
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}
.

First we will prove that
\Lambda
is non-empty. Suppose
\mathcal{X}
contains only torsion
sheaves
and their shifts. Then there is a torsion sheaf
T
on
X
such that
T[i]\in\mathcal{X}
, without
loss of generality we can assume
i=0
. Let
V
be a locally free sheaf on
X
. By our
assumption
V[1]\notin\mathcal{X}
. Now, by part
(b)
of Lemma
5.2
we have
\mathrm{Hom}(T,V[1])\neq 0
, therefore
V[1]\notin\mathcal{Y}
. Note that

V[1]
can not have any weight decomposition by Lemma
5.3
, which
contradicts the fact that
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
is a weight structure. Hence,
\mathcal{X}
must contain
a locally free sheaf, atleast upto some shift, this implies
\Lambda\neq\emptyset
.

Since
\mathcal{X}
is closed under negative shifts, if
j\in\Lambda
then
j-1\in\Lambda
.
If
\Lambda\neq\mathbb{Z}
then there is an integer
i
such that
i+1\notin\Lambda
. This means
there is a locally free sheaf
W
such that
W[i]\in\mathcal{X}
and for any locally free sheaf

V
we have
V[i+1]\notin\mathcal{X}
, in particular
W[i+1]\notin\mathcal{X}
.
Again we can assume that
i=0
. Now by part
(d)
of Lemma

5.2
, and the assumption that the genus
g\geq 1
, we have

\mathrm{Hom}(W,W[1])\neq 0
, therefore,
W[1]\notin\mathcal{Y}
.
Again, by the Lemma
5.3
, we get that
W[1]
can not have a weight
decomposition contradicting the hypothesis. Therefore,
\Lambda
must be equal to
\mathbb{Z}
.

∎

In
[
Oka11
, Theorem 1.1]
, Okawa has shown that there are no non-trivial semi-orthogonal
decompositions on the bounded derived category of curves of genus greater than equal to one.
We provide a different proof of the this fact using Lemma
5.4
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective curve over a field
k
of genus
g\geq 1
. There are no
non-trivial semi-orthogonal decompositions on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
.

Proof.

First note that, if
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is a semi orthogonal decomposition on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
, then
the pair
(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A})
is both a t-structure and a weight structure on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
. Let

x
be a closed point of
X
and
k(x)
denote the degree one simple torsion sheaf
supported on
x
. Let us denote the set of all such torsion sheaves and their shifts by

\mathcal{S}
, that is,

\mathcal{S}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{k(x)[i]\mid\text{ where }x\in X\text{ is a closed point and }i\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

If
\mathcal{B}=0
, then
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is a trivial, so nothing
to prove. Now if
\mathcal{B}\neq 0
, we can apply Lemma
5.4
to
\mathcal{B}
. Therefore
for each integer
i
, there is a locally free sheaf
V
on
X
such that
V[i]\in\mathcal{B}
. We know that either

or
k(x)[i]\in\mathcal{A}
. Since

, by
part
(e)
of Lemma

,

can not be in
\mathcal{A}
, hence

. This implies

, now by part

of Lemma

, there are no locally free sheaves or their shifts in

Next, we will recall the notion of tensor weight structures on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
. With the technical
result of Lemma

\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)

Definition 5.9
.

We say a weight structure
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
on
\mathbf{D}^{b}(X)
is a tensor weight structure if
for any

and any

the derived internal sheaf hom

\mathbf{R}\mathcal{H}om^{\cdot}(E,F)

\mathcal{X}
.

Remark 5.10
.

In
[
DS23b
, Definition 13]
we had called such a weight structure

“
\otimes^{c}
-weight structure
”
and thought of it as a weaker notion. The
notion of tensor weight structure on a triangulated category were defined using a natural
choice of a preaisle of the ambient category, see Definition 7 of loc.
cit. In our case it would have been the aisle
\mathbf{D}^{b,\leq 0}(X)
of the
standard t-structure. However, in view of Definition
3.1
, Remark

3.2
, and the lack of symmetry in Theorem 15 of
[

]
it now seems
that the above definition using
\mathrm{{Perf}}^{\leq 0}{(X)}
is the correct one.

.

Suppose

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}\mathcal{H}om^{\cdot}(W\otimes V,W[i])

\displaystyle\cong\mathbf{R}\mathcal{H}om^{\cdot}(V,\check{W}\otimes W[i])

\displaystyle\cong\mathbf{R}\mathcal{H}om^{\cdot}(V,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{\oplus^{r^{2}}}[i])

\displaystyle\cong\oplus^{r^{2}}\mathbf{R}\mathcal{H}om^{\cdot}(V,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})[i]

\displaystyle\cong\oplus^{r^{2}}V[i]

Now by the tensor property of
\mathcal{X}
and that it is closed under direct summands, we get
that
V[i]\in\mathcal{X}
. Since
\mathcal{X}\cap\mathcal{Y}=0
, so
V=0
. This proves that
\mathcal{Y}

is trivial.

∎

Gravitational Wave Generation and Detection in Gravitational Quantum Field Theory
Yuan-Kun Gao, Da Huang, Yue-Liang Wu

Abstract
We investigate the production and detection of gravitational waves (GWs) within the framework of Gravitational Quantum Field Theory (GQFT). In this theory, GWs exhibit five propagating modes: one scalar, two vector, and two tensor modes. Unlike General Relativity, the gravitational field equations in GQFT involve both symmetric and antisymmetric tensors, governed by their respective energy-momentum tensors, both of which can act as sources for GW radiation. By solving the linearized gravitational equations, we derive general analytic expressions for the different GW degrees of freedom. Our analysis reveals that the symmetric energy-momentum tensor generates scalar and tensor GWs through the trace and traceless parts of the quadrupole moment, respectively. In contrast, the antisymmetric stress tensor induces scalar and vector GWs with enhanced coupling strengths. We examine two illustrative examples: a black hole binary with a slightly elliptical orbit, which produces scalar GWs, and a neutron star binary where one component has a net spin aligned with its velocity, leading to vector GW emission. Finally, we study the detectability of these GW polarizations by analyzing their signatures in GW detectors. Our findings indicate that current observatories can detect both scalar and tensor modes, while a newly designed detector would be required to probe vector GWs.

Introduction

Currently, the standard theory describing gravity is Einstein’s General Relativity (GR), which has successfully passed nearly all experimental tests from Solar System scales to cosmological observations (for recent reviews, see
e.g.
Refs.
Will 2014
;
Will 2018

e.g.
Ref.
Donoghue 2023
and references therein). The fundamental nature of gravity remains one of the most profound open questions in modern physics. Numerous attempts have been made to uncover a more fundamental theory underlying GR, including superstring theory

;
Green et al. 1988b
;
Polchinski 2007
, loop quantum gravity

;
Ashtekar and Bianchi 2021

has marked the beginning of the era to employ GWs as a probe to test various aspects of gravity
Abbott et al. 2016b
.

Gravitational Quantum Field Theory (GQFT)
Wu 2016
;
Wu 2023
;
Wu 2025
;
Wu 2024
is a novel framework for gravity based on the gauge symmetry principles. Distinct from traditional gauge theories of gravity
Hehl et al. 1976
;
Ivanenko and Sardanashvily 1983
;
Hehl et al. 1995
(with comprehensive comparisons provided in ref.
Wu 2024
), GQFT seeks to reconcile GR with Quantum Field Theory (QFT) by postulating that the laws of nature are governed by the intrinsic properties of matter’s fundamental constituents, such as leptons and quarks in the Standard Model. This approach necessitates a clear distinction between intrinsic symmetries (encoded in the quantum numbers of fields) and external symmetries (describing their motion in flat Minkowski spacetime). Consequently, GQFT treats the global Lorentz symmetry SO(1,3) of Minkowski spacetime and the intrinsic spin symmetry SP(1,3) of fermionic fields as a unified joint symmetry SO(1,3)
\Join
SP(1,3), rather than as an associated symmetry in conventional QFT. To preserve this joint symmetry, GQFT introduces a spin-associated bi-covariant vector field
\hat{\chi}_{a}^{\;\,\mu}(x)
, which transforms homogeneously under both spin gauge symmetry SP(1,3) and global Lorentz symmetry SO(1,3). Its dual field
\chi_{\mu}^{\;\,a}(x)
emerges as the fundamental gravitational field, replacing the metric field
\chi_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{ab}\chi_{\mu}^{\;a}\chi_{\nu}^{\;b}
of GR. This gravigauge field
\chi_{\mu}^{\;\,a}
acts as a Goldstone-type boson, identified as the massless graviton, and serves as a gauge-type field defined in Minkowski spacetime and valued in a spin-associated gravigauge spacetime. Remarkably, GQFT provides a framework to unify all fundamental interactions into a hyperunified field theory (see Ref.
Wu 2022
for a comprehensive analysis and the references therein).

So far, GQFT has been explored in many aspects, including astrophysics
Gao and Ni 2025
;
Chen et al. 2025
, cosmology
Wu 2022
;
Wang et al. 2023
, and particle physics
Huang et al. 2023
. Recently, a general theory of the standard model
Wu 2025
was developed to integrate the two established standard models in particle physics and cosmology, offering comprehensive insights into the mysteries of the universe’s dark sector. In Ref.
Gao et al. 2024
, we examined the linearized gravitational dynamics of GQFT in the absence of matter sources, which reveals that, in addition to the standard tensor polarizations, the theory permits an extra scalar mode and two vector modes as gravitational wave (GW) degrees of freedom (dofs). Given the potential significance of these additional polarizations, we aim to investigate GW production and detection in GQFT.

Similar to GR, GWs in GQFT are sourced by the energy-momentum (EM) tensor, but here the EM tensor is constructed from fundamental quantum fields. A key distinction is that the EM tensor in GQFT contains both symmetric and antisymmetric components
Wu 2023
. Clarifying the physical meaning of these components and their role in GW generation is a central objective of this work. Furthermore, we assess the prospects for detecting these GW modes with current and future detectors
Xu et al. 2025
. To this end, we analyze the influence of different polarizations on GW experiments by solving the associated geodesic equations
Carroll 2019
.

The paper is structured as follows. In Sec.

C
provides detailed proofs of several key identities used in deriving the general solutions to the scalar and vector gravitational equations.

II
Brief Review of Linearized Gravitational Field Equations in the GQFT

In the GQFT, the fundamental field governing gravidynamics is the gravigauge field
\chi^{a}_{\mu}

Wu 2023
;
Wu 2025
. Upon fixing the local spin gauge symmetry SP(1,3) within this framework, the gravitational interaction is completely characterized by the symmetric component of
\chi^{a}_{\mu}
. This symmetric component exhibits identical degrees of freedom to those of the metric field
\chi_{\mu\nu}\equiv\eta_{ab}\chi^{a}_{\mu}\chi^{b}_{\nu}

We decompose the gravigauge field into a Minkowski background and a gravitational perturbation:

\displaystyle\chi_{\mu}^{a}=\eta^{a}_{\mu}+h^{a}_{\mu}/2,

(1)

which leads to the following expansion of the metric field
\chi_{\mu\nu}
:

\displaystyle\chi_{\mu\nu}\approx\eta_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}\,,

(2)

where
h_{\mu\nu}\equiv\eta_{ab}(h^{a}_{\mu}\delta^{b}_{\nu}+h^{b}_{\nu}\delta^{a}_{\mu})/2
represents the linearized perturbation field at leading order. The components of
h_{\mu\nu}
can be further decomposed according to their spin properties, as shown in
Bertschinger 1993
;
Mukhanov et al. 1992
;
Nojiri et al. 2020
:

•

Spin-2 tensor modes:
\hat{h}_{ij}

\displaystyle\hat{h}_{ij}\,,\quad\hat{h}_{it}=\hat{h}_{tt}=0\,,\quad\hat{h}^{i}_{i}=0\,,\quad\partial^{i}\hat{h}_{ij}=0\,,

(3)

•

Spin-1 vector modes:
S_{i}
and
F_{i}

\displaystyle h_{tt}=0\,,\quad h_{it}=S_{i}\,,\quad h_{ij}=2\partial_{(i}F_{j)}\,,\quad\partial^{i}S_{i}=\partial^{i}F_{i}=0\,,

(4)

•

Spin-0 scalar modes:
\phi
,
B
,
\psi
and
E

\displaystyle h_{tt}=-2\phi\,,\quad h_{it}=-\partial_{i}B\,,\quad h_{ij}=\delta_{ij}(-2\psi)+2\partial_{i}\partial_{j}E\,.

(5)

At low energies, we can view
\chi_{\mu\nu}
as an effective metric tensor, in which its perturbation fields can be summarized with the following compact line element

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-(1+2\phi)dt^{2}+2(S_{i}-\partial_{i}B)dx^{i}dt+[\hat{h}_{ij}+(1-2\psi)\delta_{ij}+2\partial_{(i}F_{j)}+2\partial_{i}\partial_{j}E]dx^{i}dx^{j}\,.

(6)

Here and in what follows, we shall use the most plus sign convention for the metric tensor
\eta_{\mu\nu}=\mathrm{diag}(-1.1,1,1)
, which is different from earlier works in the GQFT.

It was pointed out in Ref.
Gao et al. 2024
that the linearized gravitational equations of the GQFT suffers a scalar-type gauge symmetry with the transformations as
\delta h_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\zeta
, under which various scalar components are modified as

\displaystyle\phi\to\phi-\partial_{t}^{2}\zeta/2\,,\quad B\to B-\partial_{t}\zeta\,,\quad E\to E+\zeta/2\,.

(7)

It turns out that we can obtain the following two gauge-invariant variables
Gao et al. 2024

\displaystyle\Phi=\phi-\partial_{t}B/2\,,\quad A=B+2\partial_{t}E\,.

(8)

Note that the complete linearized gravitational dynamics in the GQFT is determined by two sets of equations

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}_{(\mu\nu)}=-8\pi\widetilde{G}_{N}T_{(\mu\nu)}\,,\quad\widetilde{G}_{[\mu\nu]}=-8\pi\widetilde{G}_{N}T_{[\mu\nu]}\,.

(9)

which correspond to the symmetric and antisymmetric parts of the complete gravitational equations, respectively. The parameter
\widetilde{G}_{N}
is the fundamental gravitational coupling in the GQFT, which can be related to the conventional Newtons constant
G_{N}
as
Gao et al. 2024

\displaystyle{G}_{N}\equiv\frac{1-\gamma_{W}}{(1-\gamma_{W}/2)(1+\gamma_{W})}\widetilde{G}_{N}\,.

(10)

which can be yielded by comparing the Newtonian potential generated by a static mass source in the QGFT with observations.
Also, we have explored in Ref.
Gao et al. 2024
the source-free gravitational equations with
T_{(\mu\nu)}=T_{[\mu\nu]}=0
and found five massless propagating dofs: two spin-2 modes
\hat{h}_{ij}
, two vector modes
S_{i}
, and one scalar
\psi
. In this paper, we would like to explore the generation and detection of these five GW polarizations.

\displaystyle\partial^{\mu}T_{\mu\nu}=\partial^{\mu}(T_{(\mu\nu)}+T_{[\mu\nu]})=0\,.

\displaystyle\int_{\cal V}d^{3}yy^{i}T_{i0}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\cal V}d^{3}yy^{j}y_{j}\rho\,,\quad\int_{\cal V}d^{3}yy^{i}u_{i}=0\,,

(48)

and defined the following quantities

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}\equiv\int_{\cal V}d^{3}yy^{i}T_{[0i]}\,,\quad\mathcal{I}^{k}_{\,k}=\int_{\cal V}d^{3}yy^{k}y_{k}\rho\,.

(49)

Note that
\mathcal{I}^{k}_{\,k}=\delta^{ik}\mathcal{I}_{ik}
is the trace of the matter quadrupole moment
\mathcal{I}_{ij}
and thus explains this notation. Therefore, the solution of the field
\psi
in the far region is given by

\displaystyle\psi(t,\mathbf{x})=-\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{W}}\frac{G_{N}M}{r}+\frac{G_{N}}{(1-\gamma_{W})r}\left[\frac{\gamma_{W}}{6}\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}_{r}}{\cal I}^{k}_{\,k}-\frac{2}{3}(1+\gamma_{W})\frac{d}{dt_{r}}\widetilde{S}\right]\,,

(50)

where we have used Eq. (
10
) to represent the result with the physical measured Newtonian constant

. By further using the constraint in Eq. (

\displaystyle\Phi(t,\mathbf{x})=-\frac{G_{N}M}{r}-\frac{G_{N}}{(1-\gamma_{W})r}\left[\frac{1}{6}\frac{d^{2}}{dt_{r}^{2}}\mathcal{I}^{k}_{\,k}-\frac{2(1+\gamma_{W})}{3\gamma_{W}}\frac{d}{dt_{r}}\widetilde{S}\right]\,.

(51)

It is obvious that there are two distinct contributions to the scalar wave production:
\mathcal{I}^{k}_{\,k}
and
\widetilde{S}
, where the former source comes from the symmetric part of the stress-energy tensor

while the latter from the antisymmetric part
T_{[\mu\nu]}
. This is contrasted with the Brans-Dicke gravitational theory

in which only the symmetric EM tensor source

can contribute to the scalar GW radiation. Moreover, Eqs. (
50
) and (
51

\mathcal{I}^{k}_{\,k}
, the contribution to scalar GWs from
\widetilde{S}
is enhanced by a factor of

given the smallness of
\gamma_{W}\ll 1
. Interestingly, for the scalar
\Phi(t,\mathbf{x})
, the true coupling of scalar GWs to the source

can be further reduced in the GQFT as follows

\displaystyle\frac{G_{N}(1+\gamma_{W})}{\gamma_{W}(1-\gamma_{W})}=\frac{\widetilde{G}_{N}}{\gamma_{W}(1-\gamma_{W}/2)}\approx\frac{1}{8\pi m_{G}^{2}(\alpha_{G}-\alpha_{W}/2)}\sim\frac{1}{8\pi m_{G}^{2}}\,,

(52)

where we have used the relation in Eq. (
10
) and the following basic definitions
Gao et al. 2024
;
Wu 2023

\displaystyle\gamma_{W}\equiv\gamma_{G}(\alpha_{G}-\alpha_{W}/2)\,,\quad\gamma_{G}\equiv 8\pi\widetilde{G}_{N}m_{G}^{2}\,.

(53)

Here
\alpha_{G,W}
are two
\mathcal{O}(1)
dimensionless parameters while

is the another fundamental scale characterizing the typical masses of spin gauge fields obtained by breaking the scale symmetry in the GQFT. Given the strong upper bound on
\gamma_{W}<\mathcal{O}(10^{-5})
by local gravity tests in the Solar System
Gao et al. 2024
, we expect that this constraint can be transformed to
m_{G}\lesssim 10^{16}~\mathrm{GeV}
. This indicates that the antisymmetric stress-energy tensor
T_{[\mu\nu]}
plays a more significant role in the scalar GW generation with its coupling of order of
1/m_{G}^{2}
, while the contribution from symmetric part
T_{(\mu\nu)}
would be suppressed by
G_{N}\sim 1/m_{\rm Pl}^{2}
with
m_{\rm Pl}
denoting the Planck scale.

On the other hand, for a fermion
\psi
charged under some gauge symmetry groups, the precise definition of its antisymmetric EM tensor is given by
Wu 2023

\displaystyle T_{[\mu\nu]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left(\chi^{a}_{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{a}iD_{\nu}\psi-\chi_{\nu}^{a}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{a}iD_{\mu}\psi+{\rm H.c.}\right)

(54)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left(\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\mu}iD_{\nu}\psi-\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\nu}iD_{\mu}\psi+{\rm H.c.}\right)\,,

where

denotes the covariant derivative of gauge group acting on

. In the second line, we have taken the flat spacetime limit by approximating
\chi^{a}_{\mu}\approx\eta^{a}_{\mu}
.
We can extract the longitudinal component
s
and the associated scalar GW source
\widetilde{S}
by explicitly computing

. As shown in Appendices

always vanishes. Consequently, the scalar GW source

\displaystyle\partial^{i}T_{[0i]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{G}}{4}\left(\frac{i}{4}F_{\mu\nu}^{~~ab}\bar{\psi}\left[\{\gamma_{0},\Sigma^{\mu\nu}\},\Sigma_{ab}\right]\psi+mA_{\mu ab}\bar{\psi}\{\Sigma_{0}^{~\mu},\Sigma^{ab}\}\psi\right.

(55)

\displaystyle+iA_{\nu ab}\left(\bar{\psi}\Sigma^{\mu\nu}\gamma_{0}\Sigma^{ab}D_{\mu}\psi-D_{\mu}\bar{\psi}\Sigma^{ab}\gamma_{0}\Sigma^{\mu\nu}\psi\right)

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{1}{2}A_{\mu ab}\left(\bar{\psi}\Sigma^{ab}\gamma_{0}D^{\mu}\psi+D^{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}\Sigma^{ab}\psi-\bar{\psi}\Sigma^{ab}\gamma^{\mu}D_{0}\psi-D_{0}\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\Sigma^{ab}\psi\right)\right)\,,

where the covariant derivative is defined as

Thus, the scalar GW source

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\cal V}d^{3}yy^{i}y_{i}\partial^{j}T_{[0j]}\,,

(56)

where

In GQFT, the spin gauge field

is assumed to be massive, which motivates consideration of extreme situations at high energy scales, such as the early Universe or the interior of neutron stars (NSs). However, a detailed discussion of scalar GW production in these scenarios lies beyond the scope of this work. Instead, we now turn to another scalar GW source: the trace of the quadrupole moment
\mathcal{I}^{k}_{k}
. As an illustrative example, we consider a compact binary system with an elliptical orbit. For small orbital eccentricity

\Phi

III.1.1
Compact Object Binary with an Orbit of Small Ellipticity

In the GQFT, in the absence of
\widetilde{S}
from the anti-symmetric EM tensor, the dominant source to the scalar GW mode is the second-order differentiation of
\mathcal{I}^{k}_{\,k}
with respect to the retarded time
t_{r}
. However, in the conventional binary system composed of compact objects, the orbit of each component is usually assumed to be a perfect circle, which leads to a constant
\mathcal{I}^{k}_{\,k}
and the vanishing of its time derivatives. Thus, in this subsection, we would like to study a binary system with an elliptical orbit as illustrated in Fig.
1
, which is shown to offer a source to the non-zero
d^{2}\mathcal{I}^{k}_{\,k}/dt_{r}^{2}
and associated scalar GWs. We shall consider the small ellipticity approximation for the binary orbit in order to simplify our analytic calculation. Also, we work in the Newtonian gravity which is already enough to exhibit the phenomenon.

Figure 1:
An illustration of the binary system with an elliptical orbit, which can generate scalar GWs.

Let us consider an isolated binary in which two stars or black holes are exactly same with their respective mass
M
. Hence, the system satisfies the angular momentum conservation

\displaystyle L=2Mr^{2}\left(\frac{d\varphi}{dt}\right)\,,

(57)

and the energy conservation

\displaystyle E=2\times\left[\frac{M}{2}\left(\frac{dr}{dt}\right)^{2}+\frac{L^{2}}{8Mr^{2}}\right]-\frac{G_{N}M^{2}}{2r}\,,

(58)

in which
r
and
\varphi
denote the distance and the azimuthal angle, respectively. As shown in Ref.
Gao et al. 2024
, the Newton potential is exactly the same as in GR, since the GQFT meets the Weak Equivalence Principle for non-relativistic objects. By combining Eqs. (
57
) and (
58
), we can obtain the famous Binet equation
Will 2018

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}u}{d\varphi^{2}}+u=\frac{1}{\bar{L}^{2}}\,,

(59)

where the variable is changed from
r
to
u\equiv 1/r
and we have defined the parameter
\bar{L}^{2}\equiv L^{2}/(G_{N}M^{3})
. The solution to the Binet equation is given by

\displaystyle u=\frac{1}{\bar{L}^{2}}(1+e\cos\varphi)\,,\quad r=\frac{\bar{L}^{2}}{1+e\cos\varphi}\,,

(60)

where
e
is the ellipticity of one star orbit. If
e<1
, the orbit is an ellipsis in which the focus is located at
r=0
and the perihelion is directed in
\varphi=0
.

To further proceed to obtain the orbit as a function of time
t
, one needs to solve the following differential equation transformed from Eq. (
57
),

\displaystyle\frac{d\varphi}{dt}=\frac{L}{2Mr^{2}}=\frac{G^{2}M^{5}}{2L^{3}}(1+e\cos\varphi)^{2}\,.

(61)

In order to simplify our following discussion, we would like to work in the small ellipticity limit,
i.e.
,
e\ll 1
, in which Eq. (
61
) can be simplified into

\displaystyle\frac{G^{2}M^{5}}{2L^{3}}dt=\frac{d\varphi}{(1+e\cos\varphi)^{2}}\approx d\varphi(1-2e\cos\varphi)\,,

(62)

so that the approximate solution is given by

\displaystyle\varphi(t)\approx\Omega t+2e\sin\left(\Omega t\right)\,,

(63)

where
\Omega
can be viewed as the mean angular frequency and is defined by

\displaystyle\Omega=\frac{G^{2}M^{5}}{2L^{3}}=\frac{G^{1/2}M^{1/2}}{2a^{3/2}}\,.

(64)

By taking this solution into the elliptical orbit in Eq. (
60
), we can also obtain the distance
r
as a function of
t
as follows

\displaystyle r\approx a\left[1-e\cos\left(\Omega t\right)\right]\,,

(65)

which is valid up to the leading order in
e
. Here we have defined the length of the major

\displaystyle a=\frac{L^{2}}{GM^{3}}\,.

(66)

This means that we can replace the angular momentum with the major
a
in our following formulae. The period of the orbit can be easily yielded as follows

\displaystyle T=\frac{2\pi}{\Omega}=\frac{4\pi L^{3}}{G^{1/2}M^{1/2}}=\frac{4\pi a^{3/2}}{G^{1/2}M^{1/2}}\,.

(67)

By using the fact that the velocity along the radial direction vanishes at the perihelion with
r_{p}=a(1-e)
or at the aphelion with
r_{a}=a(1+e)
, we yield the total energy of the system

\displaystyle E=\frac{L^{2}}{4mr_{p}^{2}}-\frac{GM^{2}}{2r_{p}}=-\frac{GM^{2}}{4a}\,.

(68)

The results in Eqs. (
67
) and (
68
) is in perfect agreement with the general non-perturbative solutions for elliptical orbits based on the Kepler’s laws.

Now we can show that this binary system with an elliptical orbit can produce a non-trivial trace of the quadrupole moment, which is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{ij}(t)=\int_{\cal V}d^{3}y\,y_{i}y_{j}\rho(t,\mathbf{y})\,.

(69)

For this binary system, the energy density can be written by

\displaystyle T_{00}(t,\mathbf{y})=\rho(t,\mathbf{y})

(70)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M\delta(y^{3})\left[\delta\left(y^{1}-r\cos\varphi\right)\delta\left(y^{2}-r\sin\varphi\right)+\delta\left(y^{1}+r\cos\varphi\right)\delta\left(y^{2}+r\sin\varphi\right)\right]\,,

where
r
and
\varphi
evolve according to Eqs. (
63
) and (
65
), respectively. The trace of the quadrupole is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{k}_{\,k}=2Mr^{2}(t)=2Ma^{2}(1-e\cos(\Omega t))^{2}\approx 2Ma^{2}(1-2e\cos\Omega t)\,.

(71)

As a result, in the GQFT, the scalar GW in the far region are given by

\displaystyle\psi(t,\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{W}}{(1-\gamma_{W}/2)(1+\gamma_{W})}\frac{2\widetilde{G}_{N}M}{3r}(a\Omega)^{2}e\cos(\Omega t_{r})

(72)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\gamma_{W}}{1-\gamma_{W}}\right)\frac{2G_{N}M}{3r}(a\Omega)^{2}e\cos(\Omega t_{r})\,,

where
t_{r}\equiv t-r=t-|\mathbf{r}|
, and we ignore the static potential part in Eq. (
50
).

Vector Sector

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4\widetilde{G}_{N}}{(1+\gamma_{W})}\left[\frac{K_{i}}{r}-\frac{1}{2r^{2}}\epsilon_{ijk}\hat{k}^{j}L^{k}\right]=-\frac{2\widetilde{G}_{N}}{(1+\gamma_{W})}\frac{\epsilon_{ijk}\hat{k}^{j}L^{k}}{r^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle\int_{\cal V}d^{3}y\partial^{k}\left[y_{i}y_{j}(v_{k}+u_{k})\right]-\int_{\cal V}d^{3}yy_{j}y_{j}\partial^{k}(v_{k}+u_{k})=0\,,

and

III.3

After investigating the scalar and vector sectors, we now consider the tensor GW generation in the GQFT. By taking into account all the relations for scalar and vector fields, the tensor GW equation is given by

\displaystyle\square\hat{h}_{ij}=-\frac{16\pi\widetilde{G}_{N}}{1+\gamma_{W}}\sigma_{ij}(t,{\bf x})\,.

With the Green’s function techniques, we can derive the following general solution

\displaystyle 2\int_{\cal V}d^{3}y\sigma_{ij}

IV
Gravitational Wave Detection in the GQFT

In this section, we explore the detectability of different GW polarizations within the GQFT framework. To measure GW effects, we analyze the geodesic deviation equation

;
Misner et al. 1973
;
Maggiore 2007

For a gauge-independent measurement, we examine the relative motion of a massive particle characterized by its separation vector
S^{\mu}
and four-velocity

\displaystyle\frac{D^{2}}{d\tau^{2}}S^{\mu}=R^{\mu}_{~\nu\rho\sigma}U^{\nu}U^{\rho}S^{\sigma}\,.

(110)

For GWs interacting with test particles, we expand the Riemann tensor
R^{\mu}_{~\nu\rho\sigma}
to first order in the metric perturbation

and proper time

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}S^{\mu}}{\partial t^{2}}=R^{\mu}_{~00\sigma}S^{\sigma}\,,

(111)

which is totally controlled by

\displaystyle R_{\mu 00\sigma}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{t}^{2}h_{\mu\sigma}+\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\sigma}h_{00}-\partial_{\sigma}\partial_{t}h_{\mu 0}-\partial_{t}\partial_{\mu}h_{0\sigma})\,.

(112)

Note that, different from GR, the GQFT admits one scalar, two vector and two tensor physical GW modes. In what follows, we shall consider their respective effects on

IV.1

In this subsection, we focus on the single scalar mode in the GQFT. At low energies, its effects can be described by the following metric form

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-(1+2\phi)dt^{2}-2\partial_{i}Bdx^{i}dt+[(1-2\psi)\delta_{ij}+2\partial_{i}\partial_{j}E]dx^{i}dx^{j}\,.

V
Conclusion and Discussion

GQFT provides a unified framework for gravity and the other three fundamental interactions through gauge principles. Unlike GR, this framework predicts three additional degrees of freedom in GWs: one scalar and two vector polarizations. A distinctive feature is the antisymmetric component of the EM tensor, which also contributes to GW generation.

In this work, we have investigated GW production and detection in GQFT, emphasizing the effects of these extra polarizations and novel gravitational sources. By decomposing both symmetric and antisymmetric EM tensors according to their spatial rotation properties, we demonstrate that the linearized gravitational equations separate into decoupled scalar, vector, and tensor sectors, allowing independent solutions.

Similar to GR, tensor-mode GWs are sourced by the traceless part of the matter quadrupole moment, though with a modified radiation strength. However, GQFT uniquely predicts scalar and vector gravitational radiations, both of which are absent in GR. Scalar GWs arise from two dominant sources: the trace part of the matter quadrupole moment (from the symmetric stress tensor), and an extra scalar source from the antisymmetric EM tensor, which can be generated by fermions interacting with background spin gauge field in flat spacetime approximation.

Vector GW modes, meanwhile, are exclusively sourced by the antisymmetric EM tensor, which corresponds to the fermion spin vector in the non-relativistic limit. Notably, the antisymmetric stress tensor enhances the production strengths of both scalar and vector polarizations compared to their symmetric counterparts.

To illustrate these mechanisms, we examine two astrophysical systems: a black hole binary with small orbital ellipticity (scalar GW production) and a NS binary with spin-velocity alignment (vector GW production).

Analysis of geodesic deviation equations reveals that while scalar and tensor modes can be detected by terrestrial or space-based interferometers, vector modes leave no measurable imprint on laser interferometers due to the constraint imposed by the theoretical consistency. This necessitates alternative detection strategies. Recent proposals to search for ultralight vector dark matter
Michimura et al. 2020
;
Morisaki et al. 2021
through measuring the oscillating forces acting on mirrors in the existing GW detectors
Abbott et al. 2022
;
Abac et al. 2024
;
Miller and Mendes 2023
;
Frerick et al. 2024
may be adaptable to vector GW detection in GQFT. Such oscillations could arise from interactions between vector modes and fermion spins. However, a detailed exploration of this detection method lies beyond our present scope and remains an important direction for future research.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}}\int_{\mathbf{y}}\int_{\mathbf{y}^{\prime}}\sqrt{4E_{\mathbf{p}}E_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}}\langle\mathbf{y},s|\hat{\rho}|\mathbf{y}^{\prime},s^{\prime}\rangle e^{i(\mathbf{p}^{\prime}\cdot\mathbf{y}^{\prime}-\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{y})}\,,

where we have used the following representation transformation rules for a single particle in the quantum mechanics

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{x}|\mathbf{p}\rangle=\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf{p}}}e^{i\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\,,\quad\langle\mathbf{p}|\mathbf{x}\rangle=\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf{p}}}e^{-i\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\,.

(153)

By introducing the center-of-mass and relative coordinates

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}=(\mathbf{y}+\mathbf{y}^{\prime})/2\,,\quad\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\,,

(154)

the density matrix element in Eq. (
152
) can be taken as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}}\langle\mathbf{p},s|\hat{\rho}|\mathbf{p}^{\prime},s^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{4E_{\mathbf{p}}E_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}}}{\mathcal{Z}}\int_{\mathbf{Y}}\int_{\mathbf{z}}\langle\mathbf{Y}+\mathbf{z}/2,s|\hat{\rho}|\mathbf{Y}-\mathbf{z}/2,s^{\prime}\rangle e^{-i\mathbf{P}\cdot\mathbf{z}}e^{-i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{Y}}

(155)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{Y}}\sqrt{4E_{\mathbf{p}}E_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}}W_{ss^{\prime}}(\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{P})e^{-i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{Y}}\,,

where
W_{ss^{\prime}}\equiv(1/\mathcal{Z})\int_{\mathbf{z}}\langle\mathbf{Y}+\mathbf{z}/2,s|\hat{\rho}|\mathbf{Y}-\mathbf{z}/2,s^{\prime}\rangle e^{-i\mathbf{P}\cdot\mathbf{z}}
is the famous Wigner function
Wigner 1932
, and we have also transformed integration variables into
\mathbf{Y}
and
\mathbf{z}
.

By combining Eqs. (
151
) and (
155
) with Eq. (
140
), the ensemble-averaged EM tensor is obtained as,

\displaystyle\langle\hat{T}_{[0i]}\rangle(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{P}}\int_{\mathbf{Y}}\int_{\mathbf{q}}\sum_{s,s^{\prime}}\left(-\frac{m}{\sqrt{4E_{\mathbf{p}}E_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}}}\right)\epsilon_{ijk}\partial^{j}_{\mathbf{x}}(s^{k})^{s^{\prime}s}W_{ss^{\prime}}(\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{P})e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{Y})}

(156)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{ijk}\partial^{j}_{\mathbf{x}}\Xi^{k}(\mathbf{x})\,,

where we have approximated the particle energies by their masses,
E_{\mathbf{p}}\approx m
, and defined the spin density,

\displaystyle\Xi^{k}(\mathbf{x})\equiv\sum_{s,s^{\prime}}\int_{\mathbf{P}}W_{ss^{\prime}}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{P})(s^{k})^{s^{\prime}s}\,.

(157)

Clearly, the ensemble averaged EM tensor components
\langle\hat{T}_{[0i]}\rangle
is divergence free, as can be verified by the relation
\partial^{i}_{\mathbf{x}}\langle\hat{T}_{[0i]}\rangle(\mathbf{x})=(-1/2)\epsilon_{ijk}\partial_{\mathbf{x}}^{i}\partial^{j}_{\mathbf{x}}\Xi^{k}(\mathbf{x})=0
. Remarkably, this result holds generally and is not restricted to the non-relativistic limit. It can also be derived directly from the general definition in Eq. (
138
):

\displaystyle\partial^{i}T_{[0i]}=\partial^{\mu}T_{[0\mu]}=0\,,

(158)

where the first equality follows from the antisymmetry property
T_{[00]}=0
, and the second is obtained using the free Dirac equations
i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi=m\psi
and
i\partial^{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\mu}=-m\bar{\psi}
along with
\square\psi=m^{2}\psi
and
\square\bar{\psi}=m^{2}\bar{\psi}
. Furthermore, when expressing
\langle\hat{T}_{[0i]}\rangle
in component form as in Eq. (
13
), we observe that
\Xi^{k}(\mathbf{x})
contributes only to the transverse vector field
u^{i}
, defined as,

\displaystyle u_{i}=-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{ijk}\partial^{j}\Xi^{k}\,,

(159)

while the longitudinal part, parametrized by the scalar
s
, vanishes. However, this conclusion applies strictly to free fermions. In the presence of additional gauge interactions among fermions,
s
can generally become nonzero, for that we will explicitly demonstrate in the next Appendix.

Finally, although we have focused on deriving
\langle T_{[0i]}\rangle
, we would like to emphasize that the method presented here is general and can be extended to compute other ensemble averaged components of the EM tensor.

Appendix B
Longitudinal Component of
T_{[0i]}
for Interacting Fermions

In Appendix
A
, we demonstrated that free fermions can generate non-trivial contributions to the vector component
u_{i}
in the antisymmetric EM tensor
T_{[0i]}
defined in Eq. (
13
). However, a limitation of this derivation is that the scalar component
s
vanishes identically in this scenario.

As highlighted in Refs.
Wu 2022
;
Wu 2023
, the complete antisymmetric EM tensor for fermions interacting with background gauge fields should instead be defined as:

\displaystyle T_{[\mu\nu]}=\frac{1}{4}\left[\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\mu}iD_{\nu}\psi-\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\nu}iD_{\mu}\psi-iD_{\nu}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\mu}\psi+iD_{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\nu}\psi\right]\,,

(160)

where
D_{\mu}\psi\equiv(\partial_{\mu}-ig\mathcal{A}_{\mu})\psi
and
D_{\mu}\bar{\psi}\equiv\partial_{\mu}\bar{\psi}+ig\bar{\psi}\mathcal{A}_{\mu}
are the covariant derivatives for the fermion
\psi
and
\bar{\psi}
, respectively. Here,
{\cal A}_{\mu}=\mathcal{A}^{a}_{\mu}t^{a}
represents the gauge field, with
t^{a}
denoting the Hermitian generators of the gauge group.

This raises an important question: Can a non-trivial gauge field background
\mathcal{A}^{a}_{\mu}
induce a non-zero longitudinal component
T_{[0i]}
, thereby sourcing scalar gravitational wave (GW) radiation? We address this question in the present appendix.

Before proceeding, we note that the fermion Dirac equation in the presence of
\mathcal{A}^{a}_{\mu}
takes the form,

\displaystyle(i\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}-m)\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\not{\partial}\psi+(g\not{\mathcal{A}}^{a}t^{a}-m)\psi=0\,,

\displaystyle-iD_{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}-m\bar{\psi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\partial_{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}+\bar{\psi}(g\not{\mathcal{A}}^{a}t^{a}-m)=0\,,

(161)

which leads to the following identities:

\displaystyle(i\not{D})(i\not{D})\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m^{2}\psi=-D^{\mu}D_{\mu}\psi+\frac{g}{2}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{F}_{\mu\nu}\psi\,,

\displaystyle D^{\nu}D^{\mu}\bar{\psi}(-i\gamma^{\mu})(-i\gamma^{\nu})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m^{2}\bar{\psi}=-D^{\mu}D_{\mu}\bar{\psi}+\frac{g}{2}\bar{\psi}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{F}_{\mu\nu}\,,

(162)

where we define,

\displaystyle\sigma^{\mu\nu}=\frac{i}{2}[\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}]\,,\quad\mathcal{F}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\mathcal{A}_{\mu}-ig\left[\mathcal{A}_{\mu},\mathcal{A}_{\mu}\right]\,.

(163)

Now let us consider the spatial divergence of
T_{[0i]}
. Owing to the antisymmetry of subscripts of
T_{[0i]}
, there is not the component
T_{[00]}
, so that we can write

\displaystyle\partial^{i}T_{[0i]}=\partial^{\mu}T_{[0\mu]}

(164)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\partial^{\mu}\left[\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}iD_{\mu}\psi-\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\mu}iD_{0}\psi-iD_{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}\psi+iD_{0}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\mu}\psi\right]\,.

Since every term in the second line is gauge invariant, we can represent them solely in terms of the covariant derivatives. Let us take the first one
\partial^{\mu}(\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}iD_{\mu}\psi)
as an example:

\displaystyle\partial^{\mu}(\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}iD_{\mu}\psi)=\partial^{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}iD_{\mu}\psi+\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}\partial^{\mu}(iD_{\mu}\psi)

(165)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial^{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}iD_{\mu}\psi+\bar{\psi}(ig\mathcal{A}_{\mu})\gamma_{0}iD_{\mu}\psi+\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}\partial^{\mu}(iD_{\mu}\psi)-\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}(ig\mathcal{A}_{\mu})iD_{\mu}\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D^{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}iD_{\mu}\psi+\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}D^{\mu}(iD_{\mu}\psi)\,,

where we have added and subtracted exactly the same term
\bar{\psi}(ig\mathcal{A}_{\mu})\gamma_{0}iD_{\mu}\psi
. In effect, we have replaced the partial derivative with the corresponding gauge covariant derivative in this formula. Following the same procedure for other three terms, we can write
\partial^{i}T_{[0i]}
as

\displaystyle\partial^{i}T_{[0i]}=\partial^{\mu}T_{[0\mu]}

(166)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left[(\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}iD^{2}\psi-iD^{2}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}\psi)-(iD^{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\mu}D_{0}\psi+\bar{\psi}\not{D}iD_{0}\psi)+(iD_{\mu}D_{0}\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi+iD_{0}\bar{\psi}\not{D}\psi)\right]\,,

where we have regrouped the contributions into three terms as shown in different parenthesis.

We shall compute them one by one.

•

Term 1:

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}iD^{2}\psi-iD^{2}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}\psi=\frac{ig}{2}\left(\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}\sigma^{\mu\nu}t^{a}\psi-\bar{\psi}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\gamma_{0}t^{a}\psi\right)\mathcal{F}^{a}_{\mu\nu}\,,

(167)

where we have used the relations in Eq. (
B
) to simplify the expression. By employing the following identity

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}\sigma^{\mu\nu}=2i(\delta^{\mu}_{0}\gamma^{\nu}-\delta^{\nu}_{0}\gamma^{\mu})+\sigma^{\mu\nu}\gamma_{0}\,.

(168)

this formula can be further reduced to

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}iD^{2}\psi-iD^{2}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}\psi=(ig/2)\times 2i\left(\delta^{\mu}_{0}\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\nu}t^{a}\psi-\delta^{\nu}_{0}\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}t^{a}\psi\right)\mathcal{F}^{a}_{\mu\nu}

(169)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2g\bar{\psi}\gamma^{i}t^{a}\psi\mathcal{F}^{a}_{0i}\,.

•

Term 2:

\displaystyle-(iD^{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\mu}D_{0}\psi+\bar{\psi}\not{D}iD_{0}\psi)=m\bar{\psi}D_{0}\psi-\bar{\psi}i\gamma^{\mu}[D_{\mu},D_{0}]\psi-\bar{\psi}D_{0}i\not{D}\psi

(170)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g\bar{\psi}\gamma^{i}t^{a}\psi\mathcal{F}^{a}_{0i}\,.

•

Term 3:

\displaystyle iD_{\mu}D_{0}\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi+iD_{0}\bar{\psi}\not{D}\psi=i[D_{\mu},D_{0}]\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi+iD_{0}D_{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi+iD_{0}\bar{\psi}\not{D}\psi

(171)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}t^{a}\psi(ig\mathcal{F}^{a}_{\mu 0})=g\bar{\psi}\gamma^{i}t^{a}\psi\mathcal{F}^{a}_{0i}\,.

Upon summing these three terms, we observe that all contributions cancel exactly. Consequently, the spatial divergence of
T_{[0i]}
vanishes identically, i.e.,
\partial^{i}T_{[0i]}=0
. From its definition in Eq. (
13
), the longitudinal component
s
relates to
T_{[0i]}
through

\displaystyle\partial^{i}\partial_{i}s=-\partial^{i}T_{[0i]}=0\,,

(172)

which demonstrates that
s
must vanish identically for charged fermions, even in the presence of conventional gauge field backgrounds. This result implies that the scalar GW source
\widetilde{S}
cannot be generated in this case, as evidenced by the relation in Eq. (
46
).

While this derivation holds for conventional gauge theories in quantum field theory, the situation differs for spin gauge field in GQFT. When including the background spin gauge field
A_{\mu ab}\Sigma^{ab}/2
, where
\Sigma^{ab}=(i/4)[\gamma^{a},\gamma^{b}]
represents the group generators of spin gauge symmetry SP(1,3) in the spinor representation, the covariant derivative takes the form:

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\psi=\partial_{\mu}\psi-\frac{i}{2}g_{G}A_{\mu ab}\Sigma^{ab}\psi\,,

(173)

where
g_{G}
is the corresponding gauge coupling constant.
As a result, the commutator of the associated covariant derivatives gives
Wu 2023

\displaystyle i[D_{\mu},D_{\nu}]=g_{G}F_{\mu\nu}^{~~ab}\Sigma_{ab}/2\,,

(174)

where the field strength is defined by,

\displaystyle F_{\mu\nu}^{~~ab}\equiv\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}^{~ab}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}^{~ab}+g_{G}A_{\mu~c}^{~a}A_{\nu}^{~cb}-g_{G}A_{\nu~c}^{~a}A_{\mu}^{~cb}\,.

(175)

In flat spacetime, we treat spatial coordinate indices (denoted by Greek letters
\mu
,
\nu
, …) and internal spin vector indices (denoted by Latin letters
a
,
b
,…) equivalently. However, for clarity of presentation, we maintain distinct notation to track their origins. The fermion equations of motion become:

\displaystyle i\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}\psi\equiv i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi+\frac{g_{G}}{2}\gamma^{\mu}A_{\mu ab}\Sigma^{ab}\psi=m\psi\,,

\displaystyle-iD_{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\equiv-i\partial_{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}+\frac{g_{G}}{2}A_{\mu ab}\bar{\psi}\Sigma^{ab}\gamma^{\mu}=m\bar{\psi}\,.

(176)

We emphasize that the commutators
[\gamma^{\mu},\Sigma^{ab}]
are non-vanishing because
\Sigma^{ab}
itself is composed of gamma matrices. This leads to the following important identities:

\displaystyle m^{2}\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(i\gamma^{\nu}D_{\nu})(i\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu})\psi=\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\mu}(iD_{\nu})(iD_{\mu})\psi+\frac{i}{2}g_{G}A_{\nu cd}\gamma^{\nu}[\Sigma^{cd},\gamma^{\mu}]D_{\mu}\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(iD_{\mu})(iD^{\mu})\psi+\frac{1}{2}g_{G}F_{\mu\nu}^{~~ab}\Sigma^{\mu\nu}\Sigma_{ab}\psi-\frac{i}{2}g_{G}A_{\nu ab}\gamma^{\nu}[\gamma^{\mu},\Sigma^{ab}]D_{\mu}\psi\,,

\displaystyle m^{2}\bar{\psi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-iD_{\nu})(-iD_{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu})\gamma^{\nu}=(-iD_{\nu})(-iD_{\mu})\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}-\frac{i}{2}g_{G}D_{\mu}\bar{\psi}[\gamma^{\mu},\Sigma^{ab}]A_{\nu ab}\gamma^{\nu}

(177)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-iD_{\mu})(-iD^{\mu})\bar{\psi}+\frac{1}{2}g_{G}F_{\mu\nu}^{~~ab}\bar{\psi}\Sigma_{ab}\Sigma^{\mu\nu}-\frac{i}{2}g_{G}D_{\mu}\bar{\psi}[\gamma^{\mu},\Sigma^{ab}]A_{\nu ab}\gamma^{\nu}\,.

We now turn to the calculation of the divergence of
T_{[0i]}
, which can be transformed into

\displaystyle\partial^{i}T_{[0i]}=\partial^{\mu}T_{[0\mu]}=\frac{1}{4}\partial^{\mu}\left[\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}iD_{\mu}\psi-\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\mu}iD_{0}\psi-iD_{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}\psi+iD_{0}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\mu}\psi\right]\,.

(178)

Here there are four terms in this expression, and we shall compute them one by one

•

Term 1:

\displaystyle\partial^{\mu}\left(\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}iD_{\mu}\psi\right)=\partial^{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}iD_{\mu}\psi+\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}i\partial^{\mu}D_{\mu}\psi

(179)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D^{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}iD_{\mu}\psi+\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}iD^{\mu}D_{\mu}\psi+\frac{i}{2}g_{G}A_{\mu ab}\bar{\psi}[\gamma_{0},\Sigma^{ab}]iD^{\mu}\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle iD^{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}D_{\mu}\psi-im^{2}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}\psi+\frac{i}{2}g_{G}F_{\mu\nu}^{~~ab}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}\Sigma^{\mu\nu}\Sigma_{ab}\psi

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}g_{G}A_{\nu ab}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}\gamma^{\nu}[\gamma^{\mu},\Sigma^{ab}]D_{\mu}\psi-\frac{1}{2}g_{G}A_{\mu ab}\bar{\psi}[\gamma_{0},\Sigma^{ab}]D^{\mu}\psi\,.

•

Term 2:

\displaystyle\partial^{\mu}\left(\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\mu}iD_{0}\psi\right)=\partial^{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\mu}iD_{0}\psi+\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\mu}i\partial^{\mu}D_{0}\psi

(180)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D^{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\mu}iD_{0}\psi+\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\mu}iD^{\mu}D_{0}\psi+\frac{i}{2}g_{G}A_{\mu ab}\bar{\psi}[\gamma^{\mu},\Sigma^{ab}]iD_{0}\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-m\bar{\psi}D_{0}\psi+\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}i[D_{\mu},D_{0}]\psi+\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}D_{0}iD_{\mu}\psi-\frac{1}{2}g_{G}A_{\mu ab}\bar{\psi}[\gamma^{\mu},\Sigma^{ab}]D_{0}\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}g_{G}F_{0\mu}^{~~ab}\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\Sigma_{ab}\psi+\frac{1}{2}g_{G}A_{0ab}\bar{\psi}[\gamma^{\mu},\Sigma^{ab}]D_{\mu}\psi-\frac{1}{2}g_{G}A_{\mu ab}\bar{\psi}[\gamma^{\mu},\Sigma^{ab}]D_{0}\psi\,.

•

Term 3:

\displaystyle\partial^{\mu}\left(iD_{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}\psi\right)=i\partial^{\mu}D_{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}\psi+iD_{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}\partial^{\mu}\psi

(181)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle iD^{\mu}D_{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}\psi+iD_{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}D^{\mu}\psi+\frac{1}{2}g_{G}A_{\mu ab}D^{\mu}\bar{\psi}[\Sigma^{ab},\gamma_{0}]\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle iD_{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}D^{\mu}\psi-im^{2}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}\psi+\frac{i}{2}g_{G}F_{\mu\nu}^{~~ab}\bar{\psi}\Sigma_{ab}\Sigma^{\mu\nu}\gamma_{0}\psi

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}g_{G}A_{\nu ab}D_{\mu}\bar{\psi}[\gamma^{\mu},\Sigma^{ab}]\gamma^{\nu}\gamma_{0}\psi-\frac{1}{2}g_{G}A_{\mu ab}D^{\mu}\bar{\psi}[\gamma_{0},\Sigma^{ab}]\psi\,.

•

Term 4:

\displaystyle\partial^{\mu}\left(iD_{0}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\mu}\psi\right)=i\partial^{\mu}D_{0}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\mu}\psi+iD_{0}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}\psi

(182)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle iD^{\mu}D_{0}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\mu}\psi+iD_{0}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\mu}D^{\mu}\psi+\frac{1}{2}g_{G}A_{\mu ab}D_{0}\bar{\psi}[\Sigma^{ab},\gamma^{\mu}]\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i[D_{\mu},D_{0}]\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi+(iD_{0}D_{\mu}\bar{\psi})\gamma^{\mu}\psi+mD_{0}\bar{\psi}\psi-\frac{1}{2}g_{G}A_{\mu ab}D_{0}\bar{\psi}[\gamma^{\mu},\Sigma^{ab}]\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}g_{G}F_{0\mu}^{~~ab}\bar{\psi}\Sigma_{ab}\gamma^{\mu}\psi+\frac{1}{2}g_{G}A_{0ab}D_{\mu}\bar{\psi}[\gamma^{\mu},\Sigma^{ab}]\psi-\frac{1}{2}g_{G}A_{\mu ab}D_{0}\bar{\psi}[\gamma^{\mu},\Sigma^{ab}]\psi\,.

By putting the above four terms together, we can obtain

\displaystyle\partial^{i}T_{[0i]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{G}}{4}\left(\frac{i}{4}F_{\mu\nu}^{~~ab}\bar{\psi}\left[\{\gamma_{0},\Sigma^{\mu\nu}\},\Sigma_{ab}\right]\psi+mA_{\mu ab}\bar{\psi}\{\Sigma_{0}^{~\mu},\Sigma^{ab}\}\psi\right.

(183)

\displaystyle+iA_{\nu ab}\left(\bar{\psi}\Sigma^{\mu\nu}\gamma_{0}\Sigma^{ab}D_{\mu}\psi-D_{\mu}\bar{\psi}\Sigma^{ab}\gamma_{0}\Sigma^{\mu\nu}\psi\right)

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{1}{2}A_{\mu ab}\left(\bar{\psi}\Sigma^{ab}\gamma_{0}D^{\mu}\psi+D^{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma_{0}\Sigma^{ab}\psi-\bar{\psi}\Sigma^{ab}\gamma^{\mu}D_{0}\psi-D_{0}\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\Sigma^{ab}\psi\right)\right)\,,

where we have used the fermion equations of motion and the gamma matrix algebra to simplify the expression. On the other hand, according to Eq. (
13
),
\partial^{i}T_{[0i]}
only picks up the longitudinal component
s

\displaystyle\partial^{i}T_{[0i]}=-\partial^{i}\partial_{i}s\,,

(184)

where the part related to
u_{i}
vanishes by using its definition
\partial^{i}u_{i}=0
. Note that Eq. (
184
) can be viewed as the Poisson equation for
s
. As a result,
s
can be solved as follows

\displaystyle s(\mathbf{r})=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}y\frac{\partial^{i}T_{[0i]}(\mathbf{y})}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{y}|}\,,

(185)

where
\partial^{i}T_{[0i]}
can take its concrete expression in Eq. (
183
). From this derivation, it is seen that the interaction of fermions with background spin gauge fields can indeed produce nontrivial contribution to
s
in
T_{[0i]}
.

Given the general solutions to the scalar GW production in Eqs. (
50
) and (
51
), the longitudinal source
s
can generate scalar GWs via the following quantity:

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}=3\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}ys(\mathbf{y})=\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy^{i}T_{[0i]}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy^{i}y_{i}\partial^{j}T_{[0j]}\,,

(186)

where we have used the following identity

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy^{j}T_{[0j]}=\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy^{i}\delta^{j}_{i}T_{[0j]}=\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy^{i}(\partial^{j}y_{i})T_{[0j]}

(187)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy^{j}T_{[0j]}-\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy^{i}y_{i}\partial^{j}T_{[0j]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy^{i}y_{i}\partial^{j}T_{[0j]}\,,

where the equality in the second line comes from the integration by parts with getting rid of terms of total divergence. Eq. (
186
) indicates that, for a given background spin gauge field
A_{\mu ab}
and its associated field strength
{F}_{\mu\nu}^{~~ab}
, we can directly compute the source
\widetilde{S}
for the scalar GW mode production without resorting to its local origin
s
. We have commented its importance in the generation of scalar GWs in Sec.
III.1
.

Appendix C
Proof of Several Useful Relations

Here we would like to use the long-wavelength expansion technique
Will 1977
;
Misner et al. 1973
;
Will 1993
to prove several useful relations of Eqs. (
43
) and (
82
) in the main text.

C.1
Relations for Scalar Fields

We shall firstly focus on the scalar field relation in Eq. (
43
)

\displaystyle M=\int_{\cal V}d^{3}y\left[\rho+\partial_{\tilde{t}_{r}}(b-s)\right](\tilde{t}_{r},\mathbf{y})\,,

(188)

which is crucial to show the consistency in the derivation of the scalar GW radiation formulae. Note that all fields in the volume integrand are functions of retarded time
\tilde{t}_{r}=t-|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{y}|\simeq t_{r}+\hat{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\mathbf{y}
, which gives extra
\mathbf{y}
dependence and should be taken care of when performing the volume integration. Here we have already kept the leading-order term in the far-field expansion
Will 1977
in
\tilde{t}_{r}
, where
t_{r}=t-r
and
\hat{\mathbf{k}}=\hat{\mathbf{r}}
denotes the unit vector along the propagation of GWs of wavenumber
\mathbf{k}
.

When the scalar GW wavelength
\lambda\sim 1/\partial_{t}
is much larger than the typical source length scale, we can regard
(\hat{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\mathbf{y})\partial_{t_{r}}
as a small expanding parameter. Hence, we can perform this long-wavelength expansion for the matter field
\rho(\tilde{t}_{r},\mathbf{y})
as follows

\displaystyle\rho(\tilde{t}_{r},\mathbf{y})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\left(\hat{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\mathbf{y}\right)^{n}\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial t_{r}^{n}}\rho(t_{r},\mathbf{y})\,.

(189)

In each term of this expansion, the time variable of fields
\rho(t_{r},\mathbf{y})
turns to
t_{r}
, which does not depend on the integration variable
\mathbf{y}
any more. We can perform the volume integration for the terms in this series:

•

n=0
:

\displaystyle\int_{\cal V}d^{3}y\rho(t_{r},\mathbf{y})=M\,.

(190)

•

n=1
:

\displaystyle\int_{\cal V}d^{3}y\,\hat{{k}}_{i}y^{i}\partial_{t_{r}}\rho(t_{r},\mathbf{y})=-\hat{k}_{i}\int_{\cal V}d^{3}yy^{i}\partial_{j}\partial^{j}(b-s)=\hat{k}_{i}\int_{\cal V}d^{3}y\partial^{i}(b-s)=0\,,

(191)

where we have used the EM conservation in Eq. (
19
) in the first equality and applied the integration by parts in the second one.

•

n\geqslant 2
:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\,\hat{k}_{i_{1}}\hat{k}_{i_{2}}\cdots\hat{k}_{i_{n}}\int_{\cal V}d^{3}yy^{i_{1}}y^{i_{2}}\dots y^{i_{n}}\partial_{t_{r}}^{n}\rho(t_{r},\mathbf{y})

(192)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\,\hat{k}_{i_{1}}\hat{k}_{i_{2}}\cdots\hat{k}_{i_{n}}\int_{\cal V}d^{3}yy^{i_{1}}y^{i_{2}}\dots y^{i_{n}}\partial_{t_{r}}^{n-1}\left[\partial_{j}\partial^{j}(b-s)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n-2)!}\hat{k}_{i_{1}}\hat{k}_{i_{2}}\cdots\hat{k}_{i_{n-2}}\int_{\cal V}d^{3}yy^{i_{1}}y^{i_{2}}\cdots y^{i_{(n-2)}}\partial_{t_{r}}^{n-2}[\partial_{t_{r}}(b-s)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\cal V}d^{3}y\partial_{\tilde{t}_{r}}(b-s)(\tilde{t}_{r},\mathbf{y})\,,

By combining the above results, we can obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\cal V}d^{3}y\rho(\tilde{t}_{r},\mathbf{y})=M-\int_{\cal V}d^{3}y\partial_{\tilde{t}_{r}}(b-s)(\tilde{t}_{r},\mathbf{y})\,,

(193)

which is exactly the formula in Eq. (
43
) we are pursuing.

C.2
Relations for Vector Fields

We would like to provide explicit proofs for two consistency relations in Eq. (
82
), which are reproduced here

\displaystyle\int_{\cal V}d^{3}y(v_{i}-\partial_{\tilde{t}_{r}}\theta_{i})(\tilde{t}_{r},\mathbf{y})=0\,,

(194)

and

\displaystyle\hat{k}_{j}\int_{\cal V}d^{3}yy^{j}(v_{i}-\partial_{t}\theta_{i})\simeq-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{ijk}\hat{k}^{j}L^{k}\,.

(195)

Note that the time variable of the integrand in Eq. (
194
) is the retarded time
\tilde{t}_{r}=t-|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|
which introduces an extra
\mathbf{y}
dependence. On the other hand, the notation
\simeq
means that Eq. (
195
) is only valid in the long-wavelength limit,
i.e.
, it is true up to the leading order in the small
(\hat{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\mathbf{y})\partial_{t_{r}}
expansion. Before delving into the proofs of the above two equations, we would like to mention one new constraint on the vector matter sources

\displaystyle\partial_{j}\partial^{j}u_{i}+\partial_{t}\left(\epsilon_{ijk}\partial^{j}\omega^{k}\right)=0\,,

(196)

which can be derived by combining Eqs. (
73
) and (
18
).

In order to prove Eq. (
194
), one just needs to compute the following volume integral

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yv_{i}(\tilde{t}_{r},\mathbf{y})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\hat{k}_{i_{1}}\cdots\hat{k}_{i_{n}}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy^{i_{1}}\cdots y^{i_{n}}\partial_{t_{r}}^{n}v_{i}(t_{r},\mathbf{y})\,.

(197)

For the above summation, we need to deal with the term of
n=0
and other terms separately:

•

n=0
:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yv_{i}(t_{r},\mathbf{y})=0\,.

(198)

•

n\geqslant 1
:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\hat{k}_{i_{1}}\cdots\hat{k}_{i_{n}}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy^{i_{1}}\cdots y^{i_{n}}\partial_{t_{r}}^{n-1}\Big(\partial_{t_{r}}v_{i}(t_{r},\mathbf{y})\Big)

(199)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\hat{k}_{i_{1}}\cdots\hat{k}_{i_{n}}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy^{i_{1}}\cdots y^{i_{n}}\partial^{n-1}_{t_{r}}\left(\partial_{j}\partial^{j}\theta_{i}-\epsilon_{ijk}\partial^{j}\omega^{k}-\partial_{t_{r}}u_{i}\right)\,,

where we have used the matter conservation equation of Eq. (
23
).
In what follows, we shall perform the integration for terms in the parenthesis one by one.

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\hat{k}_{i_{1}}\cdots\hat{k}_{i_{n}}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy^{i_{1}}\cdots y^{i_{n}}\partial^{n-1}_{t_{r}}\partial_{j}\partial^{j}\theta_{i}

(200)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n-2)!}\hat{k}_{i_{1}}\cdots\hat{k}_{i_{(n-2)}}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy^{i_{1}}\cdots y^{i_{(n-2)}}\partial_{t_{r}}^{n-1}\theta_{i}=\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}y\partial_{\tilde{t}_{r}}\theta_{i}(\tilde{t}_{r},\mathbf{y})\,,

where we have applied the integration by parts in the first equality and used the definition of the long-wavelength expansion in the second.
For the integral of the term involving
\omega^{k}
, we have

\displaystyle-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\hat{k}_{i_{1}}\cdots\hat{k}_{i_{n}}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy^{i_{1}}\cdots y^{i_{n}}\partial_{t_{r}}^{n-1}\left(\epsilon_{ijk}\partial^{j}\omega^{k}\right)

(201)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\hat{k}_{i_{1}}\cdots\hat{k}_{i_{n}}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy^{i_{1}}\cdots y^{i_{n}}\partial_{t_{r}}^{n-2}\left(\partial^{j}\partial_{j}u_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n-2)!}\hat{k}_{i_{1}}\cdots\hat{k}_{i_{(n-2)}}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy^{i_{1}}\cdots y^{i_{(}n-2)}\partial_{t_{r}}^{n-2}u_{i}=\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yu_{i}(\tilde{t}_{r},\mathbf{y})\,,

where we have applied Eq. (
196
) in the first equality.
Finally, according to the definition of long-wavelength expansion, the last term related to
u_{i}
directly gives

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\hat{k}_{i_{1}}\cdots\hat{k}_{i_{n}}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy^{i_{1}}\cdots y^{i_{n}}\partial^{n}_{t_{r}}u_{i}=\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yu_{i}(\tilde{t}_{r},\mathbf{y})\,,

(202)

in which we have added a vanishing term with
n=0
.
Therefore, by taking Eqs. (
200
) and (
201
) into Eq. (
199
) and considering Eq. (
198
), we can yield

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yv_{i}(\tilde{t}_{r},\mathbf{y})=\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}y\partial_{\tilde{t}_{r}}\theta_{i}(\tilde{t}_{r},\mathbf{y})\,,

(203)

which is nothing but just the wanted relation in Eq. (
194
).

As for the relation in Eq. (
195
), we shall still apply the long-wavelength expansion technique. But either
\epsilon_{ijk}\hat{k}^{j}L^{k}
or
\hat{k}^{j}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy_{j}(v_{i}-\partial_{t}\theta_{i})
appears as coefficients of terms of
\mathcal{O}(1/r^{2})
in the far-field expansion as evident in Eqs. (
76
) and (
81
). Therefore, we should keep the time-independent components, which correspond to the leading-order terms in the long-wavelength expansion and are related to the conserved angular momentum
L^{k}
of the system. This conserved charge can be extracted by the surface integral of the relevant terms in Eqs. (
76
) and (
81
) over the sphere at the spatial infinity. In contrast, the time-dependent part is associated with the GW radiation, whose energy fluxes are of
\mathcal{O}(h^{2})
and decay too fast to reach the far region, which cannot be probed by a distant observer. Hence, we only require Eq. (
195
) be valid up to the leading order in the
(\hat{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\mathbf{y})\partial_{t_{r}}
expansion.

Now we begin the proof by investigating the following integral of the field
v_{i}

\displaystyle\hat{k}^{j}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy_{j}v_{i}(\tilde{t}_{r},\mathbf{y})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\hat{k}^{i_{1}}\cdots\hat{k}^{i_{n+1}}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy_{i_{1}}\cdots y_{i_{n+1}}\partial^{n}_{t_{r}}v_{i}(t_{r},\mathbf{y})\,,

(204)

where the index
j
is dummy so that we can make the identification
i_{n+1}=j
on the right-hand side of the equality. In the following, we shall differentiate the term with
n=0
and those with
n\geqslant 1
:

•

n=0
: The zeroth-order component is given by

\hat{k}^{j}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy_{j}v_{i}(t_{r},\mathbf{y})\,,

(205)

which is useful to reproduce the total matter angular momentum later.

•

n\geqslant 1
: In each term in this series, there is at least one time derivative acting on
v_{i}
so that we can apply the conservation equation in Eq. (
23
) to these terms

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\hat{k}^{i_{1}}\cdots\hat{k}^{i_{n+1}}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy_{i_{1}}\cdots y_{i_{n+1}}\partial^{n}_{t_{r}}v_{i}(t_{r},\mathbf{y})

(206)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\hat{k}^{i_{1}}\cdots\hat{k}^{i_{n+1}}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy_{i_{1}}\cdots y_{i_{n+1}}\partial^{n-1}_{t_{r}}\left(\partial_{j}\partial^{j}\theta_{i}-\epsilon_{ijk}\partial^{j}\omega^{k}-\partial_{t_{r}}u_{i}\right)\,.

We shall analyze terms in the parenthesis. Firstly, we deal with the one involving
\theta_{i}
:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\hat{k}^{i_{1}}\cdots\hat{k}^{i_{n+1}}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy_{i_{1}}\cdots y_{i_{n+1}}\partial^{n-1}_{t_{r}}\partial_{j}\partial^{j}\theta_{i}

(207)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n+1}{(n-1)!}\hat{k}^{i_{1}}\cdots\hat{k}^{i_{n-1}}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy_{i_{1}}\cdots y_{i_{n-1}}\partial^{n-1}_{t_{r}}\theta_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}y\theta_{i}(\tilde{t}_{r},\mathbf{y})+\hat{k}^{j}\int_{\cal V}d^{3}yy_{j}\partial_{\tilde{t}_{r}}\theta(\tilde{t}_{r},\mathbf{y})\,,

where the second term in the last equality is exactly the one appearing in Eq. (
195
) but with an opposite sign. Next we consider the term in Eq. (
206
) related to
\omega^{k}

\displaystyle-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\hat{k}^{i_{1}}\cdots\hat{k}^{i_{n+1}}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy_{i_{1}}\cdots y_{i_{n+1}}\partial_{t_{r}}^{n-1}\left(\epsilon_{ijk}\partial^{j}\omega^{k}\right)\,.

(208)

The first term with
n=1

\displaystyle-\hat{k}^{i_{1}}\hat{k}^{i_{2}}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy_{i_{1}}y_{i_{2}}\left(\epsilon_{ijk}\partial^{j}\omega^{k}\right)

(209)

is special, since there is no time derivative acting on
\omega^{k}
. We will discuss it later. For other terms, we can make use of Eq. (
196
) to represent them in terms of
u_{i}
as follows

\displaystyle-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\hat{k}^{i_{1}}\cdots\hat{k}^{i_{n+1}}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy_{i_{1}}\cdots y_{i_{n+1}}\partial_{t_{r}}^{n-1}\left(\epsilon_{ijk}\partial^{j}\omega^{k}\right)

(210)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\hat{k}^{i_{1}}\cdots\hat{k}^{i_{n+1}}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy_{i_{1}}\cdots y_{i_{n+1}}\partial^{n-2}_{t_{r}}\left(\partial^{j}\partial_{j}u_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{n+1}{(n-1)!}\hat{k}^{i_{1}}\cdots\hat{k}^{i_{n-1}}\int_{\cal V}d^{3}yy_{i_{1}}\cdots y_{i_{n-1}}\partial_{t_{r}}^{n-2}u_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n-2)!}\hat{k}^{i_{1}}\cdots\hat{k}^{i_{n-1}}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy_{i_{1}}\cdots y_{i_{n-1}}\partial^{n-2}_{t_{r}}u_{i}

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{2}{(n-1)!}\hat{k}^{i_{1}}\cdots\hat{k}^{i_{n-1}}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy_{i_{1}}\cdots y_{i_{n-1}}\partial^{n-2}_{t_{r}}u_{i}\,.

The first term in the last equality, when combining with the last term in Eq. (
206
), gives the following zeroth-order contribution

\displaystyle\hat{k}^{j}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy_{j}u_{i}(t_{r},\mathbf{y})\,,

(211)

while the second term, we can apply Eq. (
196
) again to transform it back into the expression of
\omega^{k}

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{2}{(n-1)!}\hat{k}^{i_{1}}\cdots\hat{k}^{i_{n-1}}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy_{i_{1}}\cdots y_{i_{n-1}}\partial^{n-2}_{t_{r}}u_{i}

(212)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{2}{(n+1)!}\hat{k}^{i_{1}}\cdots\hat{k}^{i_{n+1}}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy_{i_{1}}\cdots y_{i_{n+1}}\partial_{t_{r}}^{n-2}\left(\partial_{j}\partial^{j}u_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{2}{(n+1)!}\hat{k}^{i_{1}}\cdots\hat{k}^{i_{n+1}}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy_{i_{1}}\cdots y_{i_{n+1}}\partial_{t_{r}}^{n-1}\left(\epsilon_{ijk}\partial^{j}\omega^{k}\right)\,.

Note that the term in Eq. (
209
) has the same form as those in the summation except for
n=1
, so that we can combine them into a single expression

\displaystyle-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{(n+1)!}\hat{k}^{i_{1}}\cdots\hat{k}^{i_{n+1}}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy_{i_{1}}\cdots y_{i_{n+1}}\partial_{t_{r}}^{n-1}\left(\epsilon_{ijk}\partial^{j}\omega^{k}\right)\,.

(213)

By further using the conservation equation of Eq. (
23
), we can transform the above expression into the following form

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{(n+1)!}\hat{k}^{i_{1}}\cdots\hat{k}^{i_{n+1}}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy_{i_{1}}\cdots y_{i_{n+1}}\left[\partial^{n}_{t_{r}}(v_{i}+u_{i})-\partial^{n-1}_{t_{r}}\left(\partial^{j}\partial_{j}\theta_{i}\right)\right]

(214)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{(n+1)!}\hat{k}^{i_{1}}\cdots\hat{k}^{i_{n+1}}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy_{i_{1}}\cdots y_{i_{n+1}}\partial^{n}_{t_{r}}(v_{i}+u_{i})-2\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}y\theta_{i}(\tilde{t}_{r},\mathbf{y})\,.

By putting together all pieces in Eqs. (
205
), (
207
), (
211
) and (
214
), we can obtain the result of the integral in Eq. (
204
)

\displaystyle\hat{k}^{j}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy_{j}v_{i}(\tilde{t}_{r},\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{k}^{j}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy_{j}(v_{i}+u_{i})(t_{r},\mathbf{y})+\hat{k}^{j}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy_{j}\partial_{\tilde{t}_{r}}\theta_{i}(\tilde{t}_{r},\mathbf{y})

(215)

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{(n+1)!}\hat{k}^{i_{1}}\cdots\hat{k}^{i_{n+1}}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy_{i_{1}}\cdots y_{i_{n+1}}\partial^{n}_{t_{r}}(v_{i}+u_{i})\,,

where the last term in Eq. (
214
) cancels the first one in Eq. (
207
) exactly. Note that there is at least one time derivative acting on
(v_{i}+u_{i})
in the terms in the second line of Eq. (
215
), which means that these terms should be neglected in the long-wavelength limit.
Consequently, up to the leading order in the long-wavelength expansion, we have

\displaystyle\hat{k}_{j}\int_{\mathcal{V}}d^{3}yy^{j}(v_{i}-\partial_{t}\theta_{i})\simeq\hat{k}^{j}\int_{\cal V}d^{3}yy_{j}(v_{i}+u_{i})(t_{r},\mathbf{y})=-(1/2)\epsilon_{ijk}\hat{k}^{j}L^{k}\,,

(216)

which is exactly the consistency relation in Eq. (
195
). Here we have employed the identity in Eq. (
77
) and the definition of the conserved angular momentum
L^{k}
in Eq. (
27
).

On spectral equations for an evolution operator of a q q -oscillator lattice
Sergey Sergeev

Abstract
We propose a set of algebraic equations describing eigenvalues and eigenstates of a relativistic evolution operator for a two-dimensional q q -oscillator Kagomé lattice. Evolution operator is constructed with the help of q q -oscillator solution of the Tetrahedron Equation. We focus on the unitary regime of the evolution operator, so our results are related to 3d integrable systems of the quantum mechanics. Our conjecture is based on a two-dimensional lattice version of the coordinate Bethe-Ansatz.

A method we use for a construction of eigenstates is a form of coordinate Bethe-Ansatz. The method works in general, however it has essentially more complicated structure in comparison with the spin-chain Bethe-Ansatz, see e.g. Appendix.
We do not have a general formula for eigenstates. We constructed explicitly the eigenstates for relatively small occupation numbers, and observe some common and rather general properties of the spectral equations. It allows us to make a conjecture about a general form of the spectral equations. This conjecture is presented in this paper.
As expected, the spectral equations obtained have nothing common with the Nested Bethe-Ansatz equations.

(2.13)

3
The coordinate Bethe-Ansatz method

The ground state for the coordinate Bethe-Ansatz method is the total Fock vacuum,

\boldsymbol{U}|0\rangle\;=\;|0\rangle\;.

(3.1)

The next evident step is to construct a ‘‘one-particle state’’. It is simple. Let

A^{+}_{\mathbf{v}}(u)\;=\;\mathbf{a}_{2,\mathbf{v}}^{+}+\sum_{k=1}^{M}g_{\mathbf{v}}^{(k)}u^{-k}\mathbf{a}_{1,\mathbf{v}+k\mathbf{e}_{1}}^{+}\mathbf{a}_{3,\mathbf{v}+k\mathbf{e}_{3}}^{+}\;.

(3.2)

This operator gives the elementary eigenstate of the evolution operator,

\boldsymbol{U}A^{+}_{\mathbf{v}}(u)|0\rangle\;=\;A^{+}_{\mathbf{v}}(u)|0\rangle\,u\;,

(3.3)

if the cyclicity condition is satisfied,

\frac{1+qu}{1-q^{2}}=g_{\mathbf{v}}^{(1)}=g_{\mathbf{v}}^{(2)}=\cdots=g_{\mathbf{v}}^{(M)}=\frac{q+u}{1-q^{2}}u^{M}\;,

(3.4)

so that the easiest spectral equation is

\frac{q+u}{1+qu}\,u^{M}\;=\;1\;.

(3.5)

This can be easily verified. Using (
2.13
), one obtains straightforwardly

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\boldsymbol{U}\,\mathbf{a}_{1,\mathbf{v}+k\mathbf{e}_{1}}^{+}\mathbf{a}_{3,\mathbf{v}+k\mathbf{e}_{3}}^{+}|0\rangle=\mathbf{a}_{1,\mathbf{v}+(k+1)\mathbf{e}_{1}}^{+}\mathbf{a}_{3,\mathbf{v}+(k+1)\mathbf{e}_{3}}^{+}|0\rangle,\quad k\neq M\;,\\
\\
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{U}\,\mathbf{a}_{1,\mathbf{v}}^{+}\mathbf{a}_{2,\mathbf{v}}^{+}|0\rangle=\left((1-q^{2})\mathbf{a}_{2,\mathbf{v}}^{+}+q\mathbf{a}_{1,\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{e}_{1}}^{+}\mathbf{a}_{3,\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{e}_{3}}^{+}\right)|0\rangle\;,\\
\\
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{U}\,\mathbf{a}_{2,\mathbf{v}}^{+}|0\rangle=\left(-q\mathbf{a}_{2,\mathbf{v}}^{+}+\mathbf{a}_{1,\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{e}_{1}}^{+}\mathbf{a}_{3,\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{e}_{3}}^{+}\right)|0\rangle\;.\end{array}\right.

(3.6)

Substituting (
3.6
) into (
3.3
), one gets (
3.4
), and then one comes to (
3.5
).

The one-particle state can be interpreted as follows. A boson of type

(a particle, or an impurity) sits at the vertex
\mathbf{v}

1
and
3
(photons). Turning around the torus, the photons recombine back into the impurity.
This interpretation arise for the elementary excitations over the Fock vacuum. It remains unclear how excitations with big occupation numbers can be interpreted.

4
General form of the spectral equaitons

Initial variables in (
3.7
) and in (
3.9
) are
u_{j}
.
However, it is convenient to introduce variables
x_{j}
and
X_{j}
related to
u_{j}
as follows:

u_{j}\;=\;\frac{x_{j}-q}{1-qx_{j}}\;,\quad x_{j}\;=\;\frac{q+u_{j}}{1+qu_{j}}\;,\quad X_{j}\;=\;u_{j}^{M}\frac{q+u_{j}}{1+qu_{j}}\;.

(4.1)

Let us define also

S_{i,j}\;=\;\frac{q+qu_{i}+q^{2}u_{i}+qu_{i}u_{j}}{q(u_{i}-u_{j})}\;=\;\frac{q^{-1}x_{i}-qx_{j}}{x_{i}-x_{j}}\;.

(4.2)

Next, let us define the following set of symmetric combinations:

\left\{\quad\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{1}\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle def}}{{=}}\;\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}\prod_{j\neq i}S_{i,j}\\
\\
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{2}\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle def}}{{=}}\;\sum_{i_{1}<i_{2}}X_{i_{1}}X_{i_{2}}\prod_{j\neq i_{1},i_{2}}S_{i_{1},j}S_{i_{2},j}\\
\vdots\\
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{n}\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle def}}{{=}}\;\sum_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{n}}X_{i_{1}}\cdots X_{i_{n}}\prod_{j\neq i_{1},\cdots,i_{n}}S_{i_{1},j}\cdots S_{i_{n},j}\\
\vdots\\
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{N-1}\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle def}}{{=}}\;X_{1}\cdots X_{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{-1}\prod_{j\neq i}S_{j,i}\\
\\
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{N}\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle def}}{{=}}\;X_{1}\cdots X_{N}\;,\quad\mathcal{F}_{0}\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle def}}{{=}}\;1\;.\end{array}\right.

(4.3)

Then the spectral equations are

\mathcal{F}_{n}\;=\;P_{n,N}(q)\;,\quad n=1,\cdots,N,

(4.4)

where
P_{n,N}(q)
are some symmetric Laurent polynomials of
q
. The form of these polynomials depends on the geometry of
\mathbf{v}_{1},\mathbf{v}_{2},\cdots,\mathbf{v}_{N}
. The best way to classify the polynomials

is to introduce their generation function

\mathcal{P}_{N}(z;q)\;=\;\sum_{n=0}^{N}z^{n}P_{n,N}(q)\;.

(4.5)

Three particular cases of the polynomials
\mathcal{P}_{N}(z;q)
are to be mentioned here:

•

When
N
particles are situated along a single vertical or horizontal line, for instance

\mathbf{v}_{1}=(k_{1},0),\;\;\mathbf{v}_{2}=(k_{2},0),\;\;\cdots,\;\;\mathbf{v}_{N}=(k_{N},0)\;,

(4.6)

then

P_{n,N}(q)\;=\;{N\choose n}\;,\quad\mathcal{P}_{N}(z;q)\;=\;(1+z)^{N}\;.

(4.7)

•

When all
N
particles sit at the same point, this is the state
\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{N}A_{\mathbf{v}}^{+}(u_{j})\,|0\rangle
, or all
N
particles are distributed on the plane randomly, then

P_{n,N}(q)\;=\;q^{-n(N-n)}{N\choose n}_{q^{2}}\;,\quad\mathcal{P}_{N}(z;q)\;=\;(-q^{1-N}z;q^{2})_{N}\;.

(4.8)

In both cases equations (
4.4
) can be simplified, their solution set has two outmost branches. One branch is

X_{1}=X_{2}=\cdots=X_{N}=1\;.

(4.9)

This branch correspond to
N
randomly distributed non-interacting perticles.
The second branch is

X_{i}\;=\;\prod_{j\neq i}\frac{S_{j,i}}{S_{i,j}}\;,

(4.10)

it corresponds to
N
particles sitting at the same point. It is remarkable to note, equations (
4.10
) coincide with the XXZ spin
-{{}^{1}\!\!/\!_{2}}
chain Bethe-Ansatz equations.

•

Finally, the main part of our hypothesis about the
q
-oscillator lattice spectral equations: if the
A_{\mathbf{v}}^{+}
-particles are
situated at the vertices of a sub-lattice,
N=K\times L
, where
K\geq L
and

\{\mathbf{v}\}\;=\;\{(n_{k}\mathbf{e}_{1}+m_{\ell}\mathbf{e}_{3})\}\;,\quad k=1,\cdots,K,\;\;\ell=1,\cdots,L

(4.11)

then

\mathcal{P}_{K\times L}(z;q)\;=\;(-q^{1-L}z;q^{2})_{L}^{K}\;,\;\;K\geq L\;.

(4.12)

Concluding this section, one could mention two general properties of equations (
4.4
). The first property is that the map

X_{i}\;\to\;X_{i}^{-1}\prod_{j\neq i}\frac{S_{j,i}}{S_{i,j}}

(4.13)

is the symmetry of the system for all known cases. The second property is that

P_{n,N}(q)\;\to\;{N\choose n}\;\;\textrm{when}\;\;q\to 1

(4.14)

for all known cases as well. Also note that
P_{N,N}(q)=1
in all cases, so that we always have
X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{N}=1
.

5
Discussion

We present in this paper the hypothesis – collection of formulas (
4.3
,
4.4
,
4.12
) – for the spectral equations defining the eigenvalues of the evolution operator of the
q
-oscillator Kagomé lattice.

The result presented requires several critical notes. Out Ansatz (
3.7
) corresponds to a part of possible eigenstates, it does not take into account ‘‘extra free photons’’ and it does not take into account more general combinatorics for the states distributed along the auxiliary Kagomé lattice. However, Ansatz (
3.7
) corresponds to the most interesting subspace of the whole Hilbert space corresponding to regular patterns of
\mathbf{v}_{1},\mathbf{v}_{2},\cdots,\mathbf{v}_{N}
.
Also, our approach is not as effective as the standard Bethe-Ansatz for the spin chains. The reader may see Appendix
A
as a demonstration of how clumsy is our approach. Our hypothesis requires an alternative verification.
One can compare our spectral equations with the spectral equations for the lattice Bose gas, see
[
17
]
. The evolution model here and the lattice Bose gas are rather different systems, but both are related to a spin lattice, and remarkably there is something common in their spectral equations.

Also, we would like to discuss briefly our equations (
4.3
,
4.4
). Let us consider this system as a system of equations for two independent sets
\{X_{j}\}
and
\{x_{j}\}
. One can try to exclude the variables
X_{N},X_{N-1},\cdots,
from the system using e.g. the resultants. As the result, one obtains a polynomial equation for a single
X_{j}
. The degree of this polynomial is
N!
. Thus, the system (
4.3
,
4.4
) as the system of equations for
\{X_{j}\}
has
N!
solutions. For instance, equations (
4.9
) and (
4.10
) represent just two branches out of
N!
ones. However, we expect that all
N!
branches of the solution are relevant. Anyway, a thermodynamic limit of the system (
4.3
,
4.4
) requires a separate study.

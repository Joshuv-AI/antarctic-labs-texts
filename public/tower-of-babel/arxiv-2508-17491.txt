A Refinement of the Crank-Mex Theorem
by

Abstract
It is proved that the number of partitions of n n with odd mex and k k parts that aren’t ones equals the number of partitions of n n with nonnegative crank and k k parts that aren’t ones. 0 0 footnotetext: The first author is partially supported by Simons Foundation Grant 633284 0 0 footnotetext: AMS Classification:11P81, 05A19 0 0 footnotetext: Key Words: Partitions, crank, mex



1
Introduction

In 1944, Freeman Dyson
[
5
]
conjectured the existence of a partition statistic, which he called the
crank
. The crank was conjectured to provide a combinatorial explanation of the Ramanujan congruence

p(11n+6)\equiv 0\pmod{11},

where
p(n)
is the number of partitions of
n
. In
[
2
]
(c.f.
[
6
]
), the crank was finally found. If we let
\omega(\pi)
denote the number of ones appearing in the partition
\pi
and
\eta(\pi)
denote the number of parts of
\pi
that are greater than
\omega(\pi)
, then the crank of
\pi,c(\pi)
, is defined by

c(\pi)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cr}\lambda_{1},&\text{if}~\omega(\pi)=0;\\
\eta(\pi)-\omega(\pi),&\text{if}~\omega(\pi)>0,\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
\lambda_{1}
is the largest part of
\pi
.

In three independent papers
[
4
,
7
,
11
]
, it was shown that the following is true.

Theorem 1
.

The number of partitions of
n
with nonnegative crank is equal to the number of partitions of
n
with odd
\mex
, where
\mex
is the least positive integer that is not a part of
\pi
.

Our object in this paper is to prove the following:

Theorem 2
.

For
n\geq 1
, the number of partitions of
n
with nonnegative crank and
k
parts that aren’t ones equals the number of partitions of
n
with odd
\mex
and
k
parts that aren’t ones.

Note that Theorem 1 follows from Theorem
2
by summing over all
k\geq 0
in Theorem
2
.

In the next section, we identify the relevant generating functions and prove a necessary lemma.

2
Background

For brevity, we require the following notation:

\displaystyle(a;q)_{n}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(1-aq^{j})\quad(0\leq n\leq\infty).

\displaystyle\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{A}{B}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cr}0,&\text{if}~B<0~\text{or}~B>A;\\
\displaystyle\frac{(q;q)_{A}}{(q;q)_{B}(q;q)_{A-B}},&\text{if}~0\leq B\leq A.\end{array}\right.

Let
M(z,q)
denote the generating function for partitions with odd
\mex
where the exponent on
q
is the number being partitioned and the exponent on
z
is the number of parts that aren’t ones. We present
M(z,q)
with two terms. The first generates the partitions where the mex is 1, i.e. the partition has no ones at all. The entry
n
in the second term generates the partitions with mex equal to
2n+1(n>0)
.

\displaystyle M(z,q)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(zq^{2};q)_{\infty}}+\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{z^{2n-1}q^{1+2+\cdots+2n}(1-zq^{2n+1})}{(1-q)(zq^{2};q)_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(zq^{2};q)_{\infty}}\left(1+\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{z^{2n-1}q^{n(2n+1)}(1-zq^{2n+1})}{(1-q)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(zq^{2};q)_{\infty}}\left(1+\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac{(-1)^{n}z^{n-1}q^{\binom{n+1}{2}}}{(1-q)}\right).

Let
K(z,q)
denote the generating function for partitions with nonnegative crank where the exponent on
q
is the number being partitioned and the exponent on
z
is the number of parts that aren’t ones.

To find
K(z,q)
we must first look at the original generating function for the crank itself. Here the exponent on
y
is the crank
[
2
]
.

\displaystyle\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(yq;q)_{\infty}(q/y;q)_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-q)(q^{2};q)_{\infty}}{(yq;q)_{\infty}(q/y;q)_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-q)}{(yq;q)_{\infty}}\left(1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(yq;q)_{n}q^{n}y^{-n}}{(q;q)_{n}}\right)

by
[
1
, p. 17, Th. 2.1,
t=qy^{-1},a=yq
]

\displaystyle=(1-q)\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{y^{n}q^{n}}{(q;q)_{n}}+(1-q)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(yq;q)_{n}q^{n}y^{-n}}{(q;q)_{n}(yq;q)_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=(1-q)+\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{y^{n}q^{n}}{(q^{2};q)_{n-1}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n}y^{-n}}{(q^{2};q)_{n-1}(yq^{n+1};q)_{\infty}}

(We note that the first sum gives the crank if the partition

has no ones and the
n^{th}
term of the second sum gives the

partitions where there are
n
ones)

\displaystyle=(1-q)+\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{y^{n}q^{n}}{(q^{2};q)_{n-1}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n}y^{-n}}{(q^{2};q)_{n-1}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{y^{m}q^{m(n+1)}}{(q;q)_{m}}.

In this last expression, we must insert
z
to keep track of parts
>1
and we must only start the
m
-sum at
m=n
in order to have nonnegative crank. Finally we set
y=1
in that we are no longer interested in the crank but only that it is nonnegative.

Hence

\displaystyle K(z,q)

\displaystyle=(1-zq)+\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{zq^{n}}{(zq^{2};q)_{n-1}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n}}{(zq^{2};q)_{n-1}}\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}\frac{z^{m}q^{m(n+1)}}{(q;q)_{m}}

\displaystyle=(1-zq)+\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{zq^{n}}{(zq^{2};q)_{n-1}}+\sum_{\underset{m\geq 0}{n\geq 1}}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n+(m+n)(n+1)}z^{m+n}}{(zq^{2};q)_{n-1}(q;q)_{m+n}}.

Note that we are only interested in partitions of
n
for
n>1
. Thus the seemingly arbitrary
1-zq
in
K(z,,q)
does not affect cases for
n>1
, but is necessary for proving
M(z,q)=K(z,q)
.

We close this section with the following lemma:

Lemma 3
.

\sum_{m=0}^{H}(-1)^{m}q^{\binom{m}{2}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{N}{m}=(-1)^{H}q^{\binom{H+1}{2}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{N-1}{H}.

Proof.

Both sides equal 1 when
H=0
. Now assume the identity is true at
H-1
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{H}(-1)^{m}q^{\binom{m}{2}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{N}{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{H-1}(-1)^{m}q^{\binom{m}{2}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{N}{m}+(-1)^{H}q^{\binom{H}{2}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{N}{H}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{H-1}q^{\binom{H}{2}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{N-1}{H-1}+(-1)^{H}q^{\binom{H}{2}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{N}{H}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{H}q^{\binom{H}{2}}\left(\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{N}{H}-\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{N-1}{H-1}\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{H}q^{\binom{H}{2}+H}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{N-1}{H}=(-1)^{H}q^{\binom{H+1}{2}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{N-1}{H}

∎

3
Proof of Theorem
2

This only requires us to prove that

M(z,q)=K(z,q),

i.e.

\frac{1}{(zq^{2};q)_{\infty}}\left(1+\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac{(-1)^{n}z^{n-1}q^{\binom{n+1}{2}}}{1-q}\right)

=1-zq+\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{zq^{n}}{(zq^{2};q)_{n-1}}+\sum_{\underset{m\geq 0}{n\geq 1}}\frac{q^{n+(m+n)(n+1)}z^{m+n}}{(zq^{2}:q)_{n-1}(q;q)_{m+n}},

and multiplying both sides by
(zq^{2};q)_{\infty}
we see that our theorem is equivalent to

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac{(-1)^{n}z^{n-1}q^{\binom{n+1}{2}}}{1-q}

\displaystyle=(zq;q)_{\infty}+\sum_{n\geq 1}zq^{n}(zq^{n+1};q)_{\infty}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\underset{m\geq 0}{n\geq 1}}\frac{q^{n+(m+n)(n+1)}z^{m+n}(zq^{n+1};q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{m+n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{n}z^{n}q^{\binom{n+1}{2}}}{(q;q)_{n}}+\sum_{n\geq 1}zq^{n}\sum_{s\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{s}z^{s}q^{s(n+1)+\binom{s}{2}}}{(q;q)_{s}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\underset{m\geq 0}{n\geq 1}}\frac{q^{n+(m+n)(n+1)}z^{m+n}}{(q;q)_{m+n}}\sum_{s\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{s}z^{s}q^{\binom{s+1}{2}+sn}}{(q;q)_{s}},

where we have expanded the infinite products using
[
1
, p. 19, Cor. 2.2, eq. (2.2.6)]
.
To prove this last result we must show that the coefficients of
z^{N}
on both sides are identical. It is clear that the constant term on each side is 1. So, we may take
N\geq 1
.

The coefficient of
z^{N}
on the left side is

\frac{(-1)^{N+1}q^{\binom{N+2}{2}}}{1-q}.

On the right we begin by noting that the coefficient of
z^{N}
contributed by the first two expressions is

\frac{(-1)^{N}q^{\binom{N+1}{2}}}{(q;q)_{N}}+\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{N-1}q^{n}q^{(N-1)(n+1)+\binom{N-1}{2}}}{(q;q)_{N-1}}.

Simplifying, we find

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{N}q^{\binom{N+1}{2}}}{(q;q)_{N}}+\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{N-1}q^{n}q^{(N-1)(n+1)+\binom{N-1}{2}}}{(q;q)_{N-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{N}q^{\binom{N+1}{2}}}{(q;q)_{N}}+\frac{(-1)^{N-1}q^{\binom{N}{2}}\cdot q^{N}}{(q;q)_{N-1}(1-q^{N})}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{N}q^{\binom{N+1}{2}}}{(q;q)_{N}}-\frac{(-1)^{N}q^{\binom{N+1}{2}}}{(q;q)_{N}}=0.

Thus, since these two contributions cancel each other, we see that the entire contribution to the coefficient of
z^{N}
on the right side comes from the third expression:

\displaystyle\sum_{\overset{\underset{m\geq 0}{n\geq 1}}{m+n\leq N}}\frac{q^{\left(n+(m+n)(n+1)+\binom{N-n-m+1}{2}+(N-n-m)n\right)}(-1)^{N-n-m}}{(q;q)_{m+n}(q;q)_{N-m-n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{N+n+nN}}{(q;q)_{N}}\sum_{m=0}^{N-n}(-1)^{N-n-m}q^{\binom{N-n-m}{2}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{N}{n+m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{N+n+nN}}{(q;q)_{N}}\sum_{m=0}^{N-n}(-1)^{m}q^{\binom{m}{2}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{N}{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{N+n+nN}}{(q;q)_{N}}(-1)^{N-n}q^{\binom{N-n+1}{2}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{N-1}{n-1}\quad(\text{by Lemma 3 with}~H=N-n)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{N}q^{\binom{N+1}{2}+N}}{(q;q)_{N}}\sum_{n\geq 1}(-1)^{n}q^{\binom{n+1}{2}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{N-1}{n-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{N-1}q^{\binom{N+1}{2}+N}}{(q;q)_{N}}\sum_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}q^{\left(\binom{n+1}{2}+n+1\right)}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{N-1}{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{N-1}q^{\binom{N+1}{2}+N}}{(q;q)_{N}}q(q^{2};q)_{N-1}\quad(\text{by \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{1}{}{}, Ch. 3, p. 36, eq. (3.3.6)]}, the $q$-binomial theorem})

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{N+1}q^{\binom{N+2}{2}}}{1-q}.

This establishes the identity of the coefficients of
z^{N}
on each side.

Thus Theorem
2
is proved.
□

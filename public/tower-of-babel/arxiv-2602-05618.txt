Examples of W ∗ and C ∗ -superrigid product groups
Jakub Curda, Daniel Drimbe

Abstract
. We provide a new large class 𝒞 A ​ F ​ P \mathcal{C}_{AFP} of amalgamated free product groups for which the product rigidity result from [ CdSS15 ] holds: if G 1 , … , G n ∈ 𝒞 A ​ F ​ P G_{1},\dots,G_{n}\in\mathcal{C}_{AFP} and H H is any group such that L ⁡ ( G 1 × ⋯ × G n ) ≅ L ⁡ ( H ) L(G_{1}\times\dots\times G_{n})\cong L(H) , then there exists a product decomposition H = H 1 × ⋯ × H n H=H_{1}\times\dots\times H_{n} such that L ⁡ ( H i ) L(H_{i}) is stably isomorphic to L ⁡ ( G i ) L(G_{i}) , for any 1 ≤ i ≤ n 1\leq i\leq n . The class 𝒞 A ​ F ​ P \mathcal{C}_{AFP} contains W ∗ W^{*} and C ∗ C^{*} -superrigid groups from [ CD-AD20 ] . Consequently, we obtain examples of product groups that are both W ∗ W^{*} and C ∗ C^{*} -superrigid.



1.
Introduction

For a countable group
G
, we denote by
\ell^{2}(G)
the Hilbert space of all square-summable complex functions on
G
, and by
\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(G))
the algebra of bounded linear operators on
\ell^{2}(G)
. The complex group algebra
\mathbb{C}[G]
can be naturally realized as a
*
-subalgebra of
\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(G))
via the left convolution action on
\ell^{2}(G)
.
The
reduced group
C^{*}
-algebra
C_{r}^{*}(G)
and
the group von Neumann algebra
L(G)
of
G
are defined as the closures of
\mathbb{C}[G]
in
\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(G))
with respect to the operator norm topology and the weak operator topology, respectively
[
MvN43
]
.
An important theme in operator algebras is the classification of the group algebras
C_{r}^{*}(G)
and
L(G)
, and hence the understanding of how much information about the group
G
is encoded in these algebras.

Von Neumann algebras of amenable groups retain only limited information about the groups from which they arise. If
G
is an infinite abelian group, then
L(G)
is isomorphic to the abelian algebra
L^{\infty}(\widehat{G},m_{\widehat{G}})
, where
m_{\widehat{G}}
is the Haar measure on
\widehat{G}
, and hence to

L^{\infty}([0,1],\lambda)
, where
\lambda
is the Lebesgue measure. Thus,
L(G)
does not have any recollection about torsion freeness or finite generation. Connes’ breakthrough result
[
Co76
]
asserts that the same strong lack of rigidity occurs for the class of amenable groups
G
that are icc (i.e., all non-trivial conjugacy classes of
G
are infinite). More precisely, Connes showed that group von Neumann algebras of icc amenable groups are isomorphic to the hyperfinite II
1
factor.

In sharp contrast, reduced
C^{*}
-algebras of amenable groups can completely remember the underlying group. A classical result of Scheinberg
[
Sc74
]
asserts that all torsion free abelian groups
G
are
C^{*}
-superrigid
, i.e. if
C_{r}^{*}(G)\cong C_{r}^{*}(H)
, then
G\cong H
. Examples of non-abelian
C^{*}
-superrigid groups have only been discovered relatively recently: certain torsion-free virtually abelian groups in
[
CKRTW
]
and
[
CLMW25
]
, two-step nilpotent groups in
[
ER18
]
, and free nilpotent groups in
[
Om18
]
.

The non-amenable case becomes much more complex in both von Neumann and
C^{*}
-algebras. Major progress has been made since the advent of Popa’s deformation/rigidity theory
[
Po07
]
, leading to the discovery of numerous situations in which algebraic and analytic properties of a group
G
that can be recovered from its group von Neumann algebra
L(G)
, see the surveys
[
Va10a
,
Io12b
,
Io17
]
. Remarkably, Ioana, Popa, and Vaes identified in
[
IPV10
]
the first examples of countable groups
G
that are
W^{*}
-superrigid
, i.e. if
L(G)\cong L(H)
, then
G\cong H
.
Subsequently, additional examples of
W^{*}
-superrigid groups have been found
[
BV12
,
Be14
,
CI17
,
CD-AD20
,
CD-AD21
,
CIOS21
,
CFQOT24
,
DV24
,
DV25
,
AMCFQ26
]
.

The first non-amenable
C^{*}
-superrigid groups
G
are certain amalgamated free products with trivial amenable radical, as shown by Chifan and Ioana
[
CI17
]
. Having already established
W^{*}
-superrigidity for these groups,
C^{*}
-superrigidity then follows as a consequence of the uniqueness of the trace on
C_{r}^{*}(G)

[
BKKO14
]
. Subsequently, additional examples of
C^{*}
-superrigid groups arising from amalgamated free products, HNN-extension groups, coinduced groups, and semi-direct products (including wreath products with non-amenable core) have been discovered in
[
CD-AD20
,
CD-AD21
]
.

Despite these advances, identifying new classes of
C^{*}
-superrigid groups remains a challenging problem. In this work, we address this question by presenting in Theorem
A
and Corollary
B
a broad class of
W^{*}
and
C^{*}
-superrigid product groups. For presenting the result, we introduce the following class of groups.

Definition 1.1
.

A group
G
belongs to
\mathcal{C}_{AFP}
if
G=G_{1}*_{\Sigma}G_{2}
admits an amalgamated free product decomposition satisfying:

(i)

\Sigma
is a common amenable icc subgroup of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
,

(ii)

G_{i}=G_{i}^{1}\times G_{i}^{2}
is a product of two icc, non-amenable,
relatively solid
groups for any
i\in\{1,2\}
.

The definition of relatively solid groups is introduced in Section
2.5
and examples of such groups include non-amenable hyperbolic groups. Moreover, if the following two conditions hold then we say that
G
belongs to
\mathcal{C}^{0}_{AFP}
.

(iii)

For all
i,j\in\{1,2\}
,
G_{i}^{j}
is a generalized wreath product of the form
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\wr_{\Gamma/K}\Gamma
, where
K<\Gamma
is an amenable malnormal subgroup and
\Gamma
contains an infinite normal property (T) subgroup.

(iv)

{\rm QN}_{G_{i}}^{(1)}(\Sigma)=\Sigma
for any
i\in\{1,2\}
and there exist
g_{1},\dots,g_{m}\in G
such that
\cap_{k=1}^{m}g_{k}\Sigma g_{k}^{-1}
is finite.

Note that
[
CD-AD20
]
shows that every group
G\in\mathcal{C}_{AFP}^{0}
is both
W^{*}
and
C^{*}
-superrigid. In the next theorem and its corollary, we extend this result to arbitrary finite products of such groups.

Theorem A
.

Let
G_{1},\dots,G_{n}
be groups from
\mathcal{C}^{0}_{AFP}
and denote
G=G_{1}\times\dots\times G_{n}
. Let
H
be an arbitrary group and let
\theta:L(G)^{t}\to L(H)
be a
*
-isomorphism for some
t>0
.

Then
t=1
and
G\cong H
. Moreover, there exist a group isomorphism
\delta:G\to H
, a unitary
w\in L(H)
and a character
\eta:G\to\mathbb{T}
such that
\theta(u_{g})=\eta(g)wv_{\delta(g)}w^{*}
, for any
g\in G
. Here, we denoted by
\{u_{g}\}_{g\in G}
and by
\{v_{h}\}_{h\in H}
the canonical generating unitaries of
L(G)
and
L(H)
, respectively.

Combining Theorem
A
with the unique trace property of products of groups from
\mathcal{C}^{0}_{AFP}
, we obtain the corresponding
C^{*}
-superrigidity result.

Corollary B
.

Let
G_{1},\dots,G_{n}
be groups from
\mathcal{C}^{0}_{AFP}
and denote
G=G_{1}\times\dots\times G_{n}
. Let
H
be an arbitrary group and let
\theta:C^{*}_{r}(G)\to C^{*}_{r}(H)
be a
*
-isomorphism.

Then there is a group isomorphism
\delta:G\to H
, a unitary
w\in L(H)
and a character
\eta:G\to\mathbb{T}
such that
\theta(u_{g})=\eta(g)wv_{\delta(g)}w^{*}
, for any
g\in G
.

These examples, along with those obtained independently by Ariza Mejía, Chifan, and Fernández Quero, are the first
C^{*}
-superrigid non-amenable product groups
[
AMCFQ26
]
. Moreover, since
[
AMCFQ26
]
establishes
C^{*}
-superrigidity for infinite direct sums, they also provide the first examples of non-amenable
C^{*}
-superrigid groups that are not
W^{*}
-superrigid.

Example 1.2
.

To provide concrete examples in the class
\mathcal{C}_{AFP}^{0}
, let
\Gamma
be a hyperbolic group admitting an infinite normal subgroup with property (T) and two infinite cyclic malnormal subgroups
K,L<\Gamma
such that
K\cap gLg^{-1}=\{1\}
for all
g\in\Gamma
. See
[
CD-AD20
, Section 6]
for a construction of such examples using techniques from geometric group theory
[
BO06
]
. The group
G=G_{1}*_{\Sigma}G_{2}
belongs to
\mathcal{C}_{AFP}^{0}
whenever
G_{1}=G_{2}=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\wr_{\Gamma/K}\Gamma\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\wr_{\Gamma/K}\Gamma
and
\Sigma=\{(g,g)\mid g\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{(\Gamma/K)}\rtimes L\}
. Indeed, note that
\Sigma
is an icc amenable group, while
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are relatively solid; see Proposition
2.9
.
The condition
K\cap gLg^{-1}=\{1\}
for all
g\in\Gamma
ensures that condition (iv) defining the class
\mathcal{C}_{AFP}^{0}
is satisfied.

The main ingredient in proving Theorem
A
is establishing the following product rigidity for von Neumann algebras of groups in
\mathcal{C}_{AFP}
in the spirit of
[
CdSS15
]
.

Theorem C
.

Let
G=G_{1}\times\dots\times G_{n}
be a product of
n\geq 2
countable groups that belong to
\mathcal{C}_{AFP}
and denote
M=L(G)
. Let
H
be any countable group such that
M^{t}=L(H)
for some
t>0.

Then there exist a product decomposition
H=H_{1}\times\dots\times H_{n}
, a unitary
u\in\mathcal{U}(M^{t})
and some positive numbers
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
with
t_{1}\dots t_{n}=t
such that
uL(H_{i})u^{*}=L(G_{i})^{t_{i}}
, for any
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}.

Additional product rigidity results have been established for products of bi-exact groups, groups with positive first
\ell^{2}
-Betti numbers, and wreath-like product groups
[
CU18
,
CD-AD20
,
Dr20
,
CDD23
,
AMCOS25
,
DD25
,
AMCFQ26
]
. Theorem
C
further extends this list to include new examples of products of amalgamated free product groups.

To prove Theorem
C
, we develop several techniques in the theory of von Neumann algebras. In Section
3
, we establish solidity-type results for von Neumann algebras associated with products of groups in the class
\mathcal{C}_{AFP}
. In Section
4
, we show that the von Neumann algebra
L(\tilde{G}\times\tilde{G}^{0})
retains the product structure whenever
\tilde{G}
is a product of groups from
\mathcal{C}_{AFP}
and
\tilde{G}^{0}
is a product of icc relatively solid groups. In Section
5
, we present results that allow us to reconstruct, at the level of von Neumann algebras, the peripheral structure of products of groups belonging to the class
\mathcal{C}_{AFP}
.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Terminology

In this paper we consider
tracial von Neumann algebras

(M,\tau)
, i.e. von Neumann algebras
M
equipped with a faithful normal tracial state
\tau:M\to\mathbb{C}.
This induces a norm on
M
by the formula
\|x\|_{2}=\tau(x^{*}x)^{1/2},
for all
x\in M
. We will always assume that
M
is a
separable
von Neumann algebra, i.e. the
\|\cdot\|_{2}
-completion of
M
denoted by
L^{2}(M)
is separable as a Hilbert space.

We denote by
\mathcal{Z}(M)
the
center
of
M
and by
\mathcal{U}(M)
its
unitary group
. For two von Neumann subalgebras
P_{1},P_{2}\subset M
, we denote by
P_{1}\vee P_{2}=W^{*}(P_{1}\cup P_{2})
the von Neumann algebra generated by
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
.

Let
P\subset M
be a unital inclusion of von Neumann algebras. We denote by
E_{P}:M\to P
the unique
\tau
-preserving
conditional expectation
from
M
onto
P
, by
e_{P}:L^{2}(M)\to L^{2}(P)
the orthogonal projection onto
L^{2}(P)
and by
\langle M,e_{P}\rangle
the Jones’ basic construction of
P\subset M
. We also denote by
P^{\prime}\cap M=\{x\in M\mid xy=yx,\text{ for all }y\in P\}
the
relative commutant
of
P
in
M
and by
\mathcal{N}_{M}(P)=\{u\in\mathcal{U}(M)\mid uPu^{*}=P\}
the
normalizer
of
P
in
M
. We say that
P
is
regular
inside
M
if the von Neumann algebra generated by
\mathcal{N}_{M}(P)
equals
M
.

The
amplification
of a II
1
factor
(M,\tau)
by a number
t>0
is defined to be
M^{t}=p(\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}))\bar{\otimes}M)p
, for a projection
p\in\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}))\bar{\otimes}M
that satisfies
(
Tr
\otimes\tau)(p)=t
. Here Tr denotes the usual trace on
\mathbb{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
. Since
M
is a II
1
factor,
M^{t}
is well defined. Note that if
M=P_{1}\bar{\otimes}P_{2}
, for some II
1
factors
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
, then there exists a natural identification
M=P_{1}^{t}\bar{\otimes}P_{2}^{1/t}
, for every
t>0.

Finally, for a positive integer
n
, we denote by
\overline{1,n}
the set
\{1,\dots,n\}
. For any subset
S\subset\overline{1,n}
we denote its complement by
\widehat{S}=\overline{1,n}\setminus S
. If
S=\{i\},
we will simply write
\widehat{i}
instead of
\widehat{\{i\}}
. Also, given
any product group
G=G_{1}\times\dots\times G_{n}
, we will denote its subproduct supported on
S
by
G_{S}=\times_{i\in S}G_{i}
.

2.2.
Intertwining-by-bimodules

We next recall from
[
Po03
, Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 2.3]
the powerful
intertwining-by-bimodules
technique of S. Popa.

Theorem 2.1

(​​
[
Po03
]
)
.

Let
(M,\tau)
be a tracial von Neumann algebra and
P\subset pMp,Q\subset qMq
be von Neumann subalgebras. Let
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{U}(P)
be a subgroup such that
\mathcal{U}^{\prime\prime}=P
.

Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

There exist projections
p_{0}\in P,q_{0}\in Q
, a
*
-homomorphism
\theta:p_{0}Pp_{0}\rightarrow q_{0}Qq_{0}
and a non-zero partial isometry
v\in q_{0}Mp_{0}
such that
\theta(x)v=vx
, for all
x\in p_{0}Pp_{0}
.

(2)

There is no sequence
(u_{n})_{n}\subset\mathcal{U}
satisfying
\|E_{Q}(xu_{n}y)\|_{2}\rightarrow 0
, for all
x,y\in M
.

If one of the equivalent conditions of Theorem
2.1
holds true, we write
P\prec_{M}Q
, and say that
a corner of
P
embeds into
Q
inside
M
.

The next lemma is essentially contained in
[
CD-AD20
, Section 3]
. For completeness, we provide all the details.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
H
be a countable icc group, let
M=L(H)
and let
\Delta:M\to M\bar{\otimes}M
the
*
-homomorphism given by
\Delta(v_{h})=v_{h}\otimes v_{h}
for
h\in H
.
Let
P\subset pMp
be a von Neumann subalgebra and
\Sigma<H
be subgroup such that
P\prec_{M}L(\Sigma)
.

If
Q\subset qMq
is a von Neumann subalgebra such that
L(\Sigma)\prec_{M}Q
, then
\Delta(P)\prec_{M\bar{\otimes}M}M\bar{\otimes}Q.

Proof.

Following the augmentation technique from
[
CD-AD20
, Section 3]
, we consider a Bernoulli action
H\curvearrowright D
with abelian base. Denote
\mathcal{M}=D\rtimes H
and extend
\Delta
to the
*
-homomorphism
\Delta:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}\bar{\otimes}M
given by
\Delta(du_{g})=du_{g}\otimes u_{g}
, for all
d\in D
and
g\in H
.

Without loss of generality we may assume that
\Sigma
is infinite. It follows that
P\prec_{\mathcal{M}}(D\rtimes\Sigma)z
for any non-zero projection
z\in(D\rtimes\Sigma)^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{C}1
. By using
[
DHI16
, Remark 2.2]
and
[
Dr19b
, Lemma 2.3]
we further obtain that
\Delta(P)\prec_{\mathcal{M}\bar{\otimes}M}(\mathcal{M}\bar{\otimes}L(\Sigma))z
for any non-zero projection
z\in(\mathcal{M}\bar{\otimes}L(\Sigma))^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{M}\bar{\otimes}M
. Using
L(\Sigma)\prec_{M}Q
and
[
Dr19b
, Lemma 2.4(2)]
, we get
\Delta(P)\prec_{\mathcal{M}\bar{\otimes}M}\mathcal{M}\bar{\otimes}Q
. Since
\Delta(P)\subset M\bar{\otimes}M
, it follows that
\Delta(P)\prec_{M\bar{\otimes}M}M\bar{\otimes}Q
.

\blacksquare

2.3.
Relative amenability

A tracial von Neumann algebra
(M,\tau)
is called
amenable
if there exists a positive linear functional
\Phi\colon\mathbb{B}(L^{2}(M))\to\mathbb{C}
such that
\Phi_{|M}=\tau
and
\Phi
is
M
-
central
, that is,
\Phi(xT)=\Phi(Tx)
for all
x\in M
and
T\in\mathbb{B}(L^{2}(M))
.

We next recall the notion of relative amenability introduced by Ozawa and Popa
[
OP07
]
. Let
(M,\tau)
be a tracial von Neumann algebra, let
p\in M
be a projection, and let
P\subset pMp
and
Q\subset M
be von Neumann subalgebras. Following
[
OP07
, Definition 2.2]
, we say that
P
is
amenable relative to
Q
inside
M
if there exists a positive linear functional
\Phi\colon p\langle M,e_{Q}\rangle p\to\mathbb{C}
such that
\Phi_{|pMp}=\tau
and
\Phi
is
P
-central. We say that
P
is
strongly non-amenable relative to
Q
if
Pp^{\prime}
is non-amenable relative to
Q
for every non-zero projection
p^{\prime}\in P^{\prime}\cap pMp
(equivalently, for every non-zero projection
p^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}_{M}(P)^{\prime}\cap pMp
by
[
DHI16
, Lemma 2.6]
).

We continue with the following lemma in the spirit of
[
KV15
, Propostion 2.2]
that explores a connection between intertwining and relative amenability

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(M,\tau)
be a tracial von Neumann algebra. Let
P_{0}\subset P\subset pMp
and
Q_{0}\subset Q\subset qMq
be von Neumann subalgebras such that there exist projections
p^{\prime}\in P
with
p^{\prime}\in P_{0}
or
p^{\prime}\in P_{0}^{\prime}\cap P
,
q_{0}\in Q
, a non-zero partial isometry
v\in q_{0}Mp^{\prime}
and a
*
-homomorphism
\theta:p^{\prime}Pp^{\prime}\to q_{0}Qq_{0}
satisfying
\theta(x)v=vx
for
x\in p^{\prime}Pp^{\prime}
. Assume that the support of
E_{Q}(vv^{*})
equals
q_{0}
.
Then the following hold:

(1)

If
\theta(p^{\prime}P_{0}p^{\prime})\prec_{Q}Q_{0}
, then
P_{0}\prec_{M}Q_{0}
,

(2)

If
\theta(p^{\prime}P_{0}p^{\prime})
is not strongly non-amenable relative to
Q_{0}
inside
Q
, then
P_{0}
is not strongly non-amenable relative to
Q_{0}
inside
M
.

Proof.
(1) Since
\theta(p^{\prime}P_{0}p^{\prime})\prec_{Q}Q_{0}
, there exist projections
p_{0}\in p^{\prime}P_{0}p^{\prime},q_{1}\in Q_{0}
, a non-zero partial isometry
w\in q_{1}Q\theta(p_{0})
and a
*
-homomorphism
\Psi:\theta(p_{0}Pp_{0})\to q_{1}Q_{0}q_{1}
such that
\Psi(x)w=wx
, for any
x\in p_{0}P_{0}p_{0}
. Define
\tilde{\Psi}:p_{0}P_{0}p_{0}\to q_{0}Q_{0}q_{0}
by
\tilde{\Psi}(x)=\Psi(\theta(x))
for any
x\in p_{0}P_{0}p_{0}
and
\tilde{w}=wv
. Note that
\tilde{\Psi}(x)\tilde{w}=\tilde{w}x
, for any
x\in p_{0}P_{0}p_{0}
and that
\tilde{w}\neq 0
. Otherwise we get
wE_{Q}(vv^{*})=0
, which implies that
w=0
, contradiction. By replacing
\tilde{w}
by the partial isometry from its polar decomposition, we get that
P_{0}\prec_{M}Q_{0}
.

(2) By
[
DHI16
, Lemma 2.6(2)]
there exists a non-zero projection
q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}_{q_{0}Mq_{0}}(\theta(p^{\prime}P_{0}p^{\prime}))^{\prime}\cap q_{0}Mq_{0}
such that
\theta(p^{\prime}P_{0}p^{\prime})q^{\prime}
is amenable relative to
Q_{0}
inside
qMq
. Since
vv^{*}\in\theta(p^{\prime}P_{0}p^{\prime})^{\prime}\cap q_{0}Mq_{0}
is a non-zero projection, then

q^{\prime}vv^{*}\in\theta(p^{\prime}P_{0}p^{\prime})^{\prime}\cap q_{0}Mq_{0}
is a non-zero projection as well. Indeed, if
q^{\prime}vv^{*}=0
, then
q^{\prime}E_{Q}(vv^{*})=0
, which implies that
q^{\prime}q_{0}=0
. This contradicts the fact that
q^{\prime}\in q_{0}Qq_{0}
is a non-zero projection. Next, note that

[
DHI16
, Lemma 2.6]
implies that
\theta(p^{\prime}P_{0}p^{\prime})q^{\prime}vv^{*}
is amenable relative to
Q_{0}
inside
qMq
, and hence,
q^{\prime}vP_{0}v^{*}q^{\prime}
is amenable relative to
Q_{0}
inside
qMq
. Let
u\in\mathcal{U}(M)
be a unitary for which
v=uv^{*}v
. We then have that
P_{0}v^{*}vu^{*}q^{\prime}u
is amenable relative to
Q_{0}
inside
qMq
. This shows that
P_{0}
is not strongly non-amenable relative to
Q_{0}
inside
M
.

\blacksquare

2.4.
Quasinormalizers of groups and von Neumann algebras

Given a group inclusion
H<G
, the one-sided quasi-normalizer
{\rm QN}^{(1)}_{G}(H)
is the semigroup of all
g\in G
for which there exists a finite set
F\subset G
such that
Hg\subset FH

[
FGS10
, Section 5]
; equivalently,
g\in{\rm QN}^{(1)}_{G}(H)
if and only if
[H:gHg^{-1}\cap H]<\infty
. The quasi-normalizer
{\rm QN}_{G}(H)
is the group of all
g\in G
for which exists a finite set
F\subset G
such that
Hg\subset FH
and
gH\subset HF
.

Given an inclusion
N\subseteq M
of tracial von Neumann algebras we define the quasi-normalizer
\mathcal{QN}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{N})

is defined as the set of all elements

for which there exist

[

.

(​​

The following lemma essentially contained in the proof of

IPP05
, Theorem 5.1]
. We add details for the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 2.6

(​​
[
IPP05
]
)
.

Let
G=G_{1}*_{\Sigma}G_{2}
be an amalgamated free product group and let
M=L(G)
. Let
P\subset pL(G)p
be a von Neumann subalgebra such that
P\prec_{L(G)}L(G_{1})
and
P\nprec_{L(G)}L(\Sigma)
. Denote
Q=\mathcal{QN}_{L(G)}^{(1)}(P)^{\prime\prime}
.

Then
Q\prec_{L(G)}L(G_{1})
. Moreover, if
G_{1}
is icc and
Qz\prec^{s}_{L(G)}L(G_{1})
for some non-zero projection
z\in\mathcal{Z}(Q)
, then there is a unitary
u\in\mathcal{U}(L(G))
such that
uQzu^{*}\subset L(G_{1})
.

Proof.

Since
P\prec_{L(G)}L(G_{1})
, there exist projections
p_{0}\in P,q_{0}\in L(G_{1})
, a non-zero partial isometry
v\in q_{0}L(G)p_{0}
and a
*
-isomorphism
\theta:p_{0}Pp_{0}\to\mathcal{C}\subset q_{0}L(G_{1})q_{0}
such that
\theta(x)v=vx
, for any
x\in p_{0}Pp_{0}
. Thus,
\mathcal{C}vv^{*}=vp_{0}Pp_{0}v^{*}
. From assumption and Lemma
2.3
we get
\mathcal{C}\nprec_{L(G_{1})}L(\Sigma)
, and hence by
[
CI17
, Corollary 2.10]
(see also
[
IPP05
, Theorem 1.1]
) it follows that
vv^{*}\in L(G_{1})
. By letting
u\in L(G)
be a unitary extending
v
, we deduce that
up_{0}Pp_{0}v^{*}vu^{*}\subset L(G_{1})
. By assumption
up_{0}Pp_{0}v^{*}vu^{*}\nprec_{L(G_{1})}L(\Sigma)
, and thus by
[
CI17
, Corollary 2.10]
and Lemma
2.4
, it follows that

uv^{*}v\mathcal{QN}^{(1)}_{L(G)}(P)^{\prime\prime}v^{*}vu^{*}\subset L(G_{1}).

This implies that
Q\prec_{M}L(G_{1})
.

For the moreover part, note that
L(G_{1})
is a II
1
factor. Thus, there exists a maximal central projection
z_{1}\in\mathcal{Z}(Q)
with
z_{1}\leq z
for which there is a unitary
u_{1}\in\mathcal{U}(L(G))
such that
u_{1}Qz_{1}u_{1}^{*}\subset L(G_{1})
. Our goal is to prove that
z_{1}=z
.

Assume by contradiction that
z_{0}=z-z_{1}
is a non-zero projection. Since
Qz_{0}\prec_{L(G)}L(G_{1})
, by proceeding as in the first part of the proof, we get a unitary
u_{2}\in\mathcal{U}(L(G_{1}))
a non-zero central projection
z_{2}\in\mathcal{Z}(Q)
with
z_{2}\leq z_{0}
such that
u_{2}Qz_{2}u_{2}^{*}\subset L(G_{1})
.
Define
\tilde{z}=z_{1}+z_{2}
.
Since
L(G_{1})
is a II
1
factor, we modify
u_{2}
so that we may assume that
u_{1}z_{1}u_{1}^{*}
and
u_{2}z_{2}u_{2}^{*}
are orthogonal.
Define the partial isometry
\tilde{u}=u_{1}z_{1}+u_{2}z_{2}
and note that
\tilde{u}^{*}\tilde{u}=z_{1}+z_{2}
and
\tilde{u}Q\tilde{z}\tilde{u}^{*}\subset L(G_{1})
. By extending
\tilde{u}
to a unitary
u
, we get that
uQ\tilde{z}u^{*}\subset L(G_{1})
. This contradicts the maximality of
z_{1}
, ending the proof.

\blacksquare

2.5.
Relatively solid groups

The main result of
[
Oz03
]
(see also
[
CS11
]
) shows that the von Neumann algebra
L(\Gamma)
of a non-amenable hyperbolic group is
solid
, i.e. if
A\subset L(\Gamma)
is a diffuse subalgebra, then the relative commutant
A^{\prime}\cap L(\Gamma)
is amenable. The work of
[
PV12
]
(see
[
KV15
, Lemma 5.2]
) straightens this result, by showing that any non-amenable hyperbolic group is
relatively solid
.

Definition 2.7
.

A countable group
\Gamma
is called
relatively solid
if for any trace preserving action
\Gamma\curvearrowright N
and any two commuting von Neumann subalgebras
Q_{1},Q_{1}\subset q(N\rtimes\Gamma)q
, we have that
Q_{1}\prec_{N\rtimes\Gamma}N
or
Q_{2}
is amenable relative to
N
inside
N\rtimes\Gamma
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\Gamma
be a relatively solid group and
\Gamma\curvearrowright N_{0}
an trace presering action. Let
N
be a tracial von Neumann algebra for which
N_{0}\rtimes\Gamma\subset pNp
.

Let
Q_{1},Q_{2}\subset qNq
be commuting von Neumann subalgebras such that
Q_{1}
or
Q_{2}
contains a II
1
subfactor. If
Q_{1}\vee Q_{2}\prec_{N}N_{0}\rtimes\Gamma
and
Q_{2}
is strongly non-amenable relative to
N_{0}
inside
N
, then
Q_{1}\prec_{N}N_{0}
.

Proof.
Assume
Q_{1}
contains a II
1
subfactor (we may proceed similarly for
Q_{2}
).
By assumption there are projections
p\in Q_{1}\vee Q_{2}
and
r\in N_{0}\rtimes\Gamma
, a non-zero partial isometry
v\in rNp
and a
*
-homomorphism
\theta:q_{0}(Q_{1}\vee Q_{2})q_{0}\to r(N_{0}\rtimes\Gamma)r
such that
\theta(x)v=vx
for any
x\in q_{0}(Q_{1}\vee Q_{2})q_{0}
. Since
Q_{1}
contains a II
1
subfactor, we may assume by
[
CdSS17
, Lemma 4.5]
that
q_{0}\in Q_{1}
. By Lemma
2.3
,
\theta(Q_{2}q_{0})
is non-amenable relative to

is relatively solid, it follows that

\blacksquare

The following result shows that the notion of relative solidity is closed by considering wreath products with amenable base.
The proof is standard and we include a proof for the convenience of the reader.

Proposition 2.9

Let

an amenable group and

an amenable malnormal subgroup. If

Proof.

Let

be a trace preserving action and denote

. Let

be commuting von Neumann subalgebras with

non-amenable relative to

. By

2.6.

The following theorem is a direct consequence of

Let

for some

.

•

for some

.

•

Q_{1}\vee Q_{2}
is amenable relative to
B
inside
M
.

Proof.
Assume that
Q_{1}\nprec_{M}B
. By
[
BO08
, Corollary F.14]
, there exists a diffuse abelian von Neumann subalgebra

satisfying

. Since

is amenable, by
[
Va13
, Thereom A]
, we get that (i)

\blacksquare

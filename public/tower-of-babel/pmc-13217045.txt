Systolic blood pressure within 24 hours after successful reperfusion correlates with outcomes in acute ischemic stroke: a secondary analysis of the EHCHANTED2/MT Trial
Authors: Yifeng Liu, Xuan Zhu, Donghuan Zhang, Zhaoshuo Li, Gaoqi Zhang, Haocun Zheng, Duanyun Lan, Hao Qin, Hongjian Shen, Pengfei Xing, Pengfei Yang, Lei Zhang, Yongwei Zhang, Xiaoxi Zhang, Xiaofei Ye, Changming Wen, Hailong Zhong, Jianmin Liu, on behalf of the ENCHANTED2/MT Investigators
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe optimal threshold or range for systolic blood pressure (SBP) control in patients with successful reperfusion after endovascular thrombectomy for acute ischemic stroke (AIS) remains undefined. This study investigated whether SBP within the first 24 hours after successful reperfusion correlates with functional outcomes in AIS.MethodsIn this secondary analysis of the ENCHANTED2/MT trial, patients were categorized into two groups (120–140 mm Hg and 140–180 mm Hg, respectively) based on achieved SBP within 24 hours after randomization. The primary outcome was the modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score at 90 days. Secondary outcomes included neurological deterioration at 7 days, major disability (mRS score of 3–5 at 90 days), hospitalization duration, and health-related quality of life assessed by the three-level EuroQoL 5-Dimension Self-Report Questionnaire (EQ-5D-3L) at 90 days. Safety outcomes included early neurological decline (END), 90-day mortality, symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH), and any intracranial hemorrhage (ICH). Treatment effects were expressed as ORs with 95% confidence intervals (CIs).ResultsA total of 611 patients (363 in the 120–140 mm Hg group and 248 in the 140–180 mm Hg group) were included. The mean (SD) age was 67 (12) years and 37.8% were female. After adjusting for confounders, the 120–140 mm Hg group was significantly associated with better functional outcomes (mRS: 2 (IQR 1–4) vs 2 (IQR 1–5); adjusted OR 1.54 (95% CI 1.10 to 2.17), P=0.013). Compared with the 140–180 mm Hg group, the 120–140 mm Hg group had lower rates of neurological deterioration at 7 days (adjusted OR 0.68 (95% CI 0.47 to 0.98), P=0.037) and 90-day mortality (47 (13.0%) vs 53 (21.4%); adjusted OR 0.48 (95% CI 0.27 to 0.86), P=0.013). There were no significant differences between groups in END, major disability at 90 days, hospitalization duration, EQ-5D-3L score, sICH, or ICH (all P>0.05).ConclusionsIn patients with successful reperfusion after endovascular thrombectomy, an average SBP within 24 hours of 120–140 mm Hg was associated with a greater likelihood of functional independence compared with 140–180 mm Hg.Keywords: Stroke, Thrombectomy, Blood Pressure

With the rapid advancement of thrombectomy devices and techniques, the successful reperfusion rate for acute ischemic stroke (AIS) due to large vessel occlusion has exceeded 80% since 2015. Nevertheless, nearly half of these patients continue to experience varying degrees of neurological deficits.1 Beyond reperfusion success, other factors such as advanced imaging evaluation, microcirculatory perfusion, and perioperative management require further investigation. Theoretically, persistently high systolic blood pressure (SBP) after successful reperfusion may increase the risk of vasogenic cerebral edema or hyperperfusion hemorrhage,2 3 whereas excessive SBP reduction could impair reperfusion in the ischemic penumbra, contributing to early neurological deterioration.4 5 Therefore, establishing the optimal SBP target after successful reperfusion following endovascular thrombectomy is crucial for improving clinical outcomes in patients with AIS.

The Enhanced Control of Hypertension and Thrombectomy Stroke Study (ENCHANTED2/MT) was stopped early due to persistent efficacy and safety concerns, and showed that more intensive SBP control below 120 mm Hg was associated with a higher risk of early neurological deterioration and functional dependence compared with less intensive control (SBP target 140–180 mm Hg).6 However, in both groups the achieved SBP did not reach the target within 24 hours after randomization, and significant heterogeneity existed within groups. Thus, while the ENCHANTED2/MT results suggest that more intensive SBP lowering may be harmful compared with less intensive treatment, an optimal threshold or range for SBP control in patients with successful reperfusion after endovascular thrombectomy remains undefined.

In this secondary analysis we aimed to evaluate the association between SBP within 24 hours after randomization and functional outcomes.

Methods

Study design and participants

ENCHANTED2/MT was an investigator-initiated, pragmatic, multicenter, open-label, blinded endpoint, phase 3 randomized controlled trial evaluating more intensive versus less intensive blood pressure management after successful reperfusion following endovascular thrombectomy. The primary outcome was a shift in the modified Rankin Scale (mRS) scores at 90 days. Patients with AIS due to large vessel occlusion were randomly assigned (1:1) to a more intensive treatment group (target SBP <120 mm Hg) or a less intensive treatment group (target SBP 140–180 mm Hg) within 1 hour of randomization, with the assigned target maintained for 72 hours. The study protocol and outcomes have been previously published see online supplemental file 3.6 7

This study is a secondary analysis of ENCHANTED2/MT and was conducted in accordance with the STROBE guidelines. Patients were categorized into two groups (120–140 mm Hg and 140–180 mm Hg, respectively) based on achieved SBP within 24 hours after randomization. Except for the regrouping, blood pressure measurements, intravenous SBP-lowering protocols, assessment schedules, and inclusion/exclusion criteria remained consistent with the original ENCHANTED2/MT trial protocol.

Outcomes

The primary outcome was the ordinal shift of the mRS score at 90 days after randomization, assessed through structured interviews conducted in person or by telephone using standardized forms. Secondary outcomes included neurological deterioration at 7 days, defined by a shift in National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) scores across six levels (0–4, 5–9, 10–14, 15–19, 20–24, ≥25); major disability, defined as an mRS score of 3–5 at 90 days; duration of hospitalization; and health-related quality of life assessed using the three-level EuroQoL 5-Dimension Self-Report Questionnaire (EQ-5D-3L) at 90 days.

Safety outcomes included early neurologic decline (END), defined as a worsening of the NIHSS score by ≥4 points within 24 hours; death within 90 days of stroke onset; symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH), classified according to the Heidelberg criteria8; and any intracranial hemorrhage (ICH) as assessed by the core laboratory and clinical event committee.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables with a normal distribution were presented as mean±SD while those with a non-normal distribution were expressed as median (IQR). Categorical variables were summarized as frequency (percentage). Missing data were explicitly reported as number (percentage) for transparency. Baseline characteristics were compared using the independent t-test, Mann–Whitney U test, χ2 test, or Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel (CMH) test, as appropriate for variable types and distributions.

For binary outcomes, binary logistic regression was used to calculate ORs with corresponding 95% CIs. Linear regression analyses were conducted for continuous outcomes, with results expressed as β coefficients (95% CIs). An ordinal logistic regression model was applied to assess neurological impairment at 7 days. To estimate the association between blood pressure management and clinical outcomes, multivariable logistic regression models (for binary outcomes), multiple linear regression models (for continuous outcomes), and multivariable ordinal logistic regression models (for ordinal outcomes) were performed.

Confounding factors and collinearity were assessed using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient based on unbalanced baseline characteristics. This analysis showed high collinearity among baseline SBP, baseline diastolic blood pressure (DBP), history of hypertension, and use of antihypertensive medications. Therefore, all analyses were adjusted for baseline SBP, age, sex, baseline NIHSS score, pre-stroke function (mRS score 0–2), time from symptom onset to reperfusion, expanded Treatment In Cerebral Infarction (eTICI) score at the end of the procedure, and use of IV alteplase. Sensitivity analyses were conducted by excluding participants with missing data to confirm the robustness of the results. Statistical significance was determined using the Wald test (two-tailed P<0.05). All statistical analyses were performed using SAS Enterprise Guide (version 8.2) and R (version 4.0.0).

Results

Study population and baseline characteristics

Of the 810 patients in the ENCHANTED2/MT trial, 199 were excluded because their mean SBP was <120 mm Hg within 24 hours after randomization and none had an SBP >180 mm Hg. Ultimately, a total of 611 patients (363 in the 120–140 mm Hg group and 248 in the 140–180 mm Hg group) were included in this secondary analysis. The mean (SD) age was 67 (12) years and 37.8% of patients were female. The median NIHSS score at admission was 15 (IQR 11–20). A total of 160 patients (26.2%) received IV alteplase before endovascular thrombectomy. The median time from symptom onset to successful reperfusion was 432 min (IQR 291–648), at which point the median SBP was 157.5 mm Hg (IQR 149.5–168). Complete reperfusion, defined as an eTICI score of 3, was achieved in 502 (82.2%) of the 611 patients. Intracranial atherosclerosis accounted for 51.7% of all large vessel occlusions. Except for a medical history of hypertension, medications of antihypertensive drugs, mean SBP, mean DBP, and use of IV alteplase (all P<0.05), there was no statistical difference between the data of these two groups of patients (table 1).

Association between SBP within 24 hours after randomization with outcomes

There was no significant difference in the treatment effect on the primary outcome between the 120–140 mm Hg and 140–180 mm Hg groups (unadjusted OR 1.31 (95% CI 0.99 to 1.74), P=0.06). However, after adjusting for confounding factors, the 120–140 mm Hg group was significantly associated with better functional outcomes (mRS: 2 (IQR 1–4) vs 2 (IQR 1–5), adjusted OR 1.54 (95% CI 1.10 to 2.17), P=0.013) (see table 2 and figure 2).

Similar to the primary outcome, the incidence of neurological deterioration at 7 days was lower in the 120–140 mm Hg group (adjusted OR 0.68 (95% CI 0.47 to 0.98), P=0.037) than in the 140–180 mm Hg group in adjusted analyses. For other secondary outcomes, no significant differences were observed in the proportion of patients with major disability at 90 days (adjusted OR 0.76 (95% CI 0.47 to 1.22), P=0.25), duration of hospitalization (adjusted β 2.69 (95% CI −1.55 to 6.93), P=0.22), or EQ-5D-3L scores at 90 days (adjusted β −11.95 (95% CI −24.3 to 0.43), P=0.06). Regarding safety outcomes, the 120–140 mm Hg group was associated with a lower incidence of death within 90 days compared with the 140–180 mm Hg group (47 (13.0%) of 363 patients vs 53 (21.4%) of 248 patients, adjusted OR 0.48 (95% CI 0.27 to 0.86), P=0.013). However, there were no significant differences between the groups in the incidence of sICH, any ICH, or END (adjusted P values of 0.21, 0.35, and 0.75, respectively) (see table 2 and figure 2).

Discussion

The ENCHANTED2/MT trial showed that more intensive SBP control below 120 mm Hg was associated with worse functional outcomes compared with a less intensive target of 140–180 mm Hg in patients who underwent endovascular thrombectomy for AIS due to large vessel occlusion. In this secondary analysis, patients were further stratified into 120–140 mm Hg and 140–180 mm Hg groups based on achieved SBP during the first 24 hours after randomization. After adjusting for confounding factors, the 120–140 mm Hg group was associated with improved mRS at 90 days, significantly lower neurological deterioration at 7 days, and reduced mortality within 90 days. No significant differences were observed between groups in sICH, ICH, hospitalization duration, or EQ-5D-3L score.

Current global guidelines provide broad recommendations for perioperative blood pressure management (<180/105 mm Hg)9 10 but lack individualized and refined treatment strategies. Blood pressure management after endovascular thrombectomy for AIS remains one of the most critical yet ambiguous clinical decisions. A recent meta-analysis of 5874 patients showed that higher SBP levels within 24 hours post-thrombectomy were independently associated with increased odds of END and worse functional outcomes at 3 months,11 consistent with the present study. Conversely, some recent prospective studies, including the ENCHANTED2/MT trial, found that more intensive SBP lowering did not reduce symptomatic hemorrhage and was associated with worse functional outcomes at 3 months. Ghozy et al assessed the association between different SBP targets and functional outcomes in 1571 patients across four randomized clinical trials, concluding that intensive SBP lowering offers no benefit and may pose risks, favoring a more conservative SBP management strategy post-reperfusion.12 However, the mechanisms linking functional outcomes and blood pressure following successful reperfusion remain unclear, as it is uncertain whether poor outcomes result from hypertension itself or from underlying factors contributing to hypertension.

It is hypothesized that a more aggressive SBP target after endovascular thrombectomy is associated with a lower rate of hemorrhagic transformation.13 However, different SBP control strategies did not affect the occurrence of sICH, any ICH, or END in the present study. Similarly, no significant differences in sICH or any ICH between the two groups were observed in the BP-TARGET, BEST-II, ENCHANTED2/MT, and OPTIMAL-BP trials.6 14 15 These results suggest that the risk of hemorrhagic transformation may be small and not significantly different between groups, as long as SBP is maintained below 180 mm Hg, in accordance with current guideline recommendations. Although a higher proportion of patients in the 140–180 mm Hg group received IV alteplase before thrombectomy, the risk of sICH or any ICH was not increased compared with the 120–140 mm Hg group. This finding aligns with the results of a previous study.16 However, it should be noted that the modest effect size (adjusted OR 1.54) and the discrepancy between unadjusted and adjusted analyses highlight the fact that imbalances in baseline characteristics may have influenced the primary outcome.

A post-hoc analysis of the MR CLEAN trial reported a ‘U-shaped’ correlation between baseline SBP before intra-arterial thrombectomy and functional outcomes: both higher and lower baseline SBP were associated with poor functional outcomes, with higher baseline SBP increasing the likelihood of sICH (for every 10 mm Hg increase in SBP, the adjusted OR for sICH was 1.25).17 Our findings are consistent with a meta-analysis in which high SBP on hospital admission was associated with worse functional outcomes, with no observed interaction between SBP at admission and the effect of endovascular thrombectomy.18 This study confirmed that SBP at admission is an independent factor, with higher SBP at admission linked to poorer clinical outcomes.17 19 20 In addition to intensive blood pressure management, some completed studies have explored the association between functional outcomes and blood pressure variability and trajectories, respectively.21 22 Another study worth noting is the OPTIMAL-BP2 trial, which aims to investigate whether a more targeted blood pressure elevation strategy could improve patient prognosis compared with standard blood pressure control in those with post-thrombectomy SBP below 140 mm Hg. This trial is currently recruiting participants (NCT 06677970). Similar to admission SBP, and in conjunction with ENCHANTED2/MT results,6 achieved SBP within 24 hours after thrombectomy showed a U-shaped correlation with functional outcome; both lower and higher SBP were associated with major disability at 90 days.

Strengths and limitations

Compared with several recently completed randomized controlled trials on SBP management,6 14 15 the main strength of this study lies in the statistical analysis based on achieved SBP rather than the SBP target prespecified by the study protocol. A specific SBP treatment target and a large difference between groups could be achieved with such a design. For example, in the ENCHANTED2/MT trial the mean SBP was 125 mm Hg at 1 hour and 121 mm Hg at 24 hours in the more intensive group, and 143 mm Hg at 1 hour and 139 mm Hg at 24 hours in the less intensive group (adjusted mean difference over 24 hours was −18 mm Hg). The average SBP difference was much smaller than the envisaged SBP target, and the mean SBP also distinctly differed from the intended target at various time points in each group. This discrepancy may be due to some clinical physicians considering the potential risks of aggressive SBP lowering to the target SBP after successful reperfusion in the intensive group.

Several limitations are present in the current study. First, statistical differences in some baseline clinical data between the two groups may affect the reliability of the results. Second, the small sample size in the range of achieved SBP between 160–180 mm Hg limited further refinement of the 140–180 mm Hg group into 160–180 mm Hg and 140–160 mm Hg categories, preventing more precise results. Third, blood pressure was measured at fixed time points using automated devices rather than through continuous monitoring. Consequently, these measurements may only reflect transient blood pressure fluctuations rather than true baseline values. Moreover, the blood pressure variability lowering was not assessed in this study due to blood pressure variability not being systematically measured, which is recognized as an independent predictor of functional outcome.21 23 A series of post-hoc analyses of the EHCHANTED2/MT study will be performed to evaluate the impact of blood pressure variability and blood pressure trajectory on the outcomes in future studies. Similar to the ENCHANTED2/MT trial, the higher prevalence of hypertension and large artery atherosclerosis in China, compared with the Caucasian population, may affect the generalizability of the study results to other countries. As an observational study, although we adjusted for a wide range of potential confounders, the potential for unknown confounders still remains. Thus, the findings of this study should be interpreted with caution.

Conclusions

Overall, an average SBP of 120–140 mm Hg during the first 24 hours after successful endovascular thrombectomy leads to better functional outcome compared with 140–180 mm Hg. This conclusion needs to be strictly validated in future randomized controlled trials.

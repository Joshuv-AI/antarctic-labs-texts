1.
Introduction

Let
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be the standard graded polynomial ring over a field
K
, and let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal. In
[
20
,
21
]
, the first author and Qureshi introduced a new aspect to the theory of syzygies of powers of
I
. Recall that the
i
th
homological shift ideals

[
29
]
of
I
is defined as

\textup{HS}_{i}(I)\ =\ ({\bf x^{a}}:\ \textup{Tor}_{i}^{S}(K,I)_{\bf a}\neq 0),

where
{\bf x^{a}}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{a_{i}}
if
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
. Note that
\textup{HS}_{0}(I)=I
. Homological shift ideals encode critical multigraded data arising from the syzygies of
I
, see
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
10
,
11
,
13
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
20
,
21
,
29
,
30
,
36
,
38
,
39
]
. A major motivation to study homological shift ideals comes from the Bandari-Bayati-Herzog conjecture
[
3
,
29
]
.

It was conjectured in 2012 by Bandari, Bayati and Herzog that the homological shift ideals preserve the polymatroidal property. Polymatroidal ideals, which are the algebraic part of discrete polymatroids
[
27
, Chapter 12]
, constitute one of the most distinguished classes of monomial ideals. Recently, Cid-Ruiz, Matherne and Shapiro settled the Bandari-Bayati-Herzog conjecture
[
7
, Theorem A(iii)]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
I\subset S
be a polymatroidal ideal. Then
\textup{HS}_{i}(I)
is polymatroidal.

Inspired by the Bandari-Bayati-Herzog conjecture, in
[
20
]
the first author and Qureshi studied the problem of determining when the following
K
-algebra, which is called the
i
th
homological shift algebra
of
I
,

\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))\ =\ \bigoplus_{k\geq 1}\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}),

is a finitely generated module over the Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}(I)=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}I^{k}
of
I
. While this is not the case in general
[
20
, Example 1.1]
, it holds true when
I
has linear powers
[
20
, Theorem 1.4]
and it holds for the first homological shift algebra of any edge ideal
[
21
, Corollary 2.4]
. When
\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))
is a
\mathcal{R}(I)
-module, many invariants behave asymptotically well
[
20
, Theorem 2.1]
. For instance,
\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
and
\textup{depth}\,S/\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
became constant for large
k
, and
k\mapsto\textup{reg}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
,
k\mapsto\textup{v}(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}))
became linear functions for large
k
. See Section
2
for more details.

Polymatroidal ideals have linear powers, and so their homological shift algebras are finitely generated modules over the Rees algebra. In this paper, employing the fundamental Theorem
1.1
, we deeply study these algebras and the asymptotic behavior of the invariants of
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
with
I\subset S
a polymatroidal ideal.

The paper is organized as follows. Sections
2
and
3
summarize basic material on homological shift algebras and polymatroidal ideals. Let
I\subset S
be a polymatroidal ideal. In Section
4
we prove that
\textup{HS}_{1}(\mathcal{R}(I))
is generated in degree one as a
\mathcal{R}(I)
-module (Theorem
4.1
). Based on this result, and several experiments, we expect that
\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))
is generated in degrees
\leq i
(Conjecture
5.1
). This conjecture holds for
i=1
. At the moment we do not have a general strategy to tackle this conjecture. In Section
5
, we establish it for: (a) principal Borel ideals, (b) ideals satisfying the strong exchange property, and (c) matroidal edge ideals. To prove the case (b), we use the concept of Möbius function of a polymatroid, which was introduced in
[
7
]
.

In Section
6
, we prove that the Betti numbers satisfy
\beta_{j}(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}))\leq\beta_{j}(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1}))
for all integers
i,j,k
(Theorem
6.2
), leading to the fact that the depth function
k\mapsto\textup{depth}\,S/\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
is non-increasing. As an immediate consequence, if
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
, then
\mathfrak{m}\in\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})
(Corollary
6.4
). Based on this result, and experimental evidence, we expect that the sequence
\{\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})\}_{k>0}
becomes an increasing chain for
k\geq i
(Conjecture
7.1
). It turns out that Conjecture
5.1
implies Conjecture
7.1
(Theorem
7.3
). Interestingly enough, to prove this result we employ the concept of
i
th
homological strong persistence property
introduced in
[

]
and the so-called determinantal trick
[

7.1
for
i=1
and for the families (a), (b), (c) mentioned before.

The product of polymatroidal ideals is polymatroidal. In Section
8
, we prove that if
\mathfrak{m}I
is polymatroidal and
I
has linear resolution, then
I
is polymatroidal (Proposition

). This result was conjectured by Bandari and Herzog
[
2
]
.

In the last two sections, we consider
componentwise polymatroidal ideals
. These ideals, introduced in
[
2
]
, are monomial ideals whose all graded components
I_{\langle j\rangle}
are polymatroidal ideals. It is proved in
[
16
]
that these ideals have linear quotients. In Section
9
, we prove that the saturation of a (componentwise) polymatroidal ideal is componentwise polymatroidal (Corollary
9.2
). We expect that
\textup{HS}_{i}(I)
is componentwise polymatroidal for all

, if

is such. We establish this expectation for

(Theorem

).

In contrast to polymatroidal ideals, powers of componentwise polymatroidal ideals are no longer componentwise polymatroidal. However, it is expected that they are componentwise linear. Whether
\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))
is a finitely generated
\mathcal{R}(I)
-module for a componentwise polymatroidal ideal

is an open question at the moment.

2.
Homological shift algebras

Let
I\subset S
be a graded ideal and let
\mathfrak{p}\in\textup{Ass}\,I
be an associated prime. Recall that the
regularity
of
I
is defined as
\textup{reg}\,I=\max\{j:\ \textup{Tor}^{S}_{i}(K,I)_{i+j}\neq 0\}
, the
\textup{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-number
of
I
is defined as
\textup{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)=\min\{\deg(f):\ f\in S_{d},\ (I:f)=\mathfrak{p}\}
, and the
v
-number
of
I
is defined as
\textup{v}(I)=\min\limits_{\mathfrak{p}\in\textup{Ass}\,I}\textup{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(I)
. See, also,
[
8
,
9
,
14
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal and let
\mathcal{G}(I)
be its minimal monomial generating set. The
i
th
homological shift algebra
of
I
is introduced in
[
20
]
as the
K
-algebra,

\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))\ =\ \bigoplus_{k\geq 1}\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}).

We say that
I\subset S
has
linear powers
if
I^{k}
has linear resolution for all
k\geq 1
. The next results were proved in
[
20
, Theorem 1.4]
and
[
20
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal with linear powers. Then
\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))
is a finitely generated graded
\mathcal{R}(I)
-module. Hence,
I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})\subset\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})
for all
k\geq 1
and equality holds for all
k\gg 0
. Furthermore, the following statements hold.

(a)

The set
\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
stabilizes:
\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})=\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
for all
k\gg 0
.

(b)

For all
k\gg 0
, we have
\textup{depth}\,S/\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})=\textup{depth}\,S/\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
.

(c)

For all
k\gg 0
,
\textup{reg}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
is a linear function in
k
.

(d)

For all
k\gg 0
,
\textup{v}(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}))
is a linear function in
k
.

(e)

For all
k\gg 0
and all
\mathfrak{p}\in\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
,
\textup{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}))
is a linear function in
k
.

3.
Polymatroidal ideals

A monomial ideal
I\subset S
is called
polymatroidal
if the exponent vectors of the minimal monomial generators of
I
form the set of bases of a discrete polymatroid. A squarefree polymatroidal ideal is called
matroidal
.

For a monomial
u=x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}\in S
, we define the
x_{i}
-degree
of
u
as the integer

\deg_{x_{i}}(u)=\max\{j:\ x_{i}^{j}\ \textup{divides}\ u\}=a_{i}.

A monomial ideal
I\subset S
is polymatroidal, if and only if, it is generated in one degree and the
exchange property
holds: For all
u,v\in\mathcal{G}(I)
with
\deg_{x_{i}}(u)>\deg_{x_{i}}(v)
for some
i
, there exists
j
with
\deg_{x_{j}}(u)<\deg_{x_{j}}(v)
such that
x_{j}(u/x_{i})\in\mathcal{G}(I)
.

By
[
27
, Theorem 12.4.1]
, polymatroidal ideals also satisfy the so-called
symmetric exchange property
: For all
u,v\in\mathcal{G}(I)
and all
i
such that
\deg_{x_{i}}(u)<\deg_{x_{i}}(v)
there exists
j
with
\deg_{x_{j}}(u)>\deg_{x_{j}}(v)
such that
x_{i}(u/x_{j})
,
x_{j}(v/x_{i})\in\mathcal{G}(I)
.

Polymatroidal ideals have linear quotients with respect to the lexicographic order of
\mathcal{G}(I)
, induced by any ordering of the variables of
S
. Recall that
I
has
linear quotients
if the set
\mathcal{G}(I)
can be ordered as
u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}
such that for each
i=2,\ldots,m
, the ideal
(u_{1},\ldots,u_{i-1}):(u_{i})
is generated by variables. By
[
34
, Lemma 2.1]
, we can always assume that
\deg(u_{1})\leq\dots\leq\deg(u_{m})
. We put
\textup{set}(u_{1})=\emptyset
and

\textup{set}(u_{i})\ =\ \{\ell\ :\ x_{\ell}\in(u_{1},\dots,u_{i-1}):(u_{i})\},\quad\textup{for}\ i=2,\dots,m.

By
[
32
, Lemma 1.5]
, if
I\subset S
has linear quotients, we have

\textup{HS}_{i}(I)\ =\ ({\bf x}_{F}u:\ u\in\mathcal{G}(I),\ F\subset\textup{set}(u),\ |F|=i),

(1)

where
{\bf x}_{F}=\prod_{j\in F}x_{j}
if
F\neq\emptyset
, and
{\bf x}_{\emptyset}=1
otherwise.

Products of polymatroidal ideals are polymatroidal
[
27
, Theorem 12.6.3]
. Therefore, a polymatroidal ideal
I\subset S
has linear powers, and so by Theorem
2.1
the homological shift algebras
\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))
are finitely generated
\mathcal{R}(I)
-modules.

4.
The first homological shift algebra of a polymatroidal ideal

In this section, our main goal is to describe the first homological shift algebra of a polymatroidal ideal.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
I\subset S
be a polymatroidal ideal. Then,

\textup{HS}_{1}(I^{k+1})=I\cdot\textup{HS}_{1}(I^{k}),

for all
k\geq 1
. In particular,
\textup{HS}_{1}(\mathcal{R}(I))
is generated in degree one as a
\mathcal{R}(I)
-module.

This theorem will be an immediate consequence of the following Proposition
4.2
, which was shown by Bandari
[
1
]
. Due to its importance, we provide a proof of it.

For a monomial ideal
I\subset S
, its
bounding multidegree
is defined as the vector
{\bf deg}(I)=(\deg_{x_{1}}(I),\dots,\deg_{x_{n}}(I))
where

\deg_{x_{i}}(I)\ =\ \max_{u\in\mathcal{G}(I)}\deg_{x_{i}}(u),\quad\textup{for all}\ i=1,\dots,n.

Given a monomial ideal
I\subset S
and a vector
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, we define the
restriction of
I
at
{\bf a}
as the monomial ideal
I^{\leq{\bf a}}
with minimal generating set

\mathcal{G}(I^{\leq{\bf a}})\ =\ \{u\in\mathcal{G}(I):\ \deg_{x_{i}}(u)\leq a_{i},\ \textup{for all}\ i=1,\dots,n\}.

Clearly,
I=I^{\leq{\bf a}}
whenever
{\bf a}\geq{\bf deg}(I)
. If
I
is polymatroidal, then
I^{\leq{\bf a}}
is polymatroidal for any
{\bf a}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, see
[
18
, Lemma 4.1]
.

We denote by
\alpha(I)
the smallest degree of a minimal homogeneous generator of
I
. By
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
we denote the graded maximal ideal of
S
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
I\subset S
be a polymatroidal ideal. Then,

\textup{HS}_{1}(I)\ =\ (\mathfrak{m}I)^{\leq{\bf deg}(I)}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{G}(I)=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{m}\}
. By
[
21
, Lemma 2.2]
, we have

\textup{HS}_{1}(I)=(\textup{lcm}(u_{i},u_{j}):\ 1\leq i<j\leq m).

Let
w\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{1}(I))
. Since
I
has linear resolution,
\textup{HS}_{1}(I)
is generated in degree
\alpha(I)+1
. Hence
w=\textup{lcm}(u_{i},u_{j})=x_{s}u_{i}
for some
s
, and
w\in\mathfrak{m}I
. Notice that
\deg_{x_{p}}(w)=\max\{\deg_{x_{p}}(u_{i}),\deg_{x_{p}}(u_{j})\}\leq\deg_{x_{p}}(I)
for all
p
. Hence
w\in(\mathfrak{m}I)^{\leq{\bf deg}(I)}
.

Conversely, let
w=x_{s}u\in\mathcal{G}((\mathfrak{m}I)^{\leq{\bf deg}(I)})
, with
u\in\mathcal{G}(I)
. Firstly, notice that
\deg_{x_{s}}(u)=\deg_{x_{s}}(w)-1<\deg_{x_{s}}(I)
. We can find a monomial
v\in\mathcal{G}(I)
with
\deg_{x_{s}}(v)=\deg_{x_{s}}(I)>\deg_{x_{s}}(u)
. Applying the symmetric exchange property, there exists
r
with
\deg_{x_{r}}(u)>\deg_{x_{r}}(v)
such that
u^{\prime}=x_{s}(u/x_{r})\in\mathcal{G}(I)
. Since
\textup{lcm}(u,u^{\prime})=x_{s}u
, using again
[
21
, Lemma 2.2]
we see that
(\mathfrak{m}I)^{\leq{\bf deg}(I)}\subset\textup{HS}_{1}(I)
, as desired.

∎

It is clear that if
I\subset S
is an equigenerated monomial ideal, then

{\bf deg}(I^{k})\ =\ k\,{\bf deg}(I)\ =\ (k\deg_{x_{1}}(I),\dots,k\deg_{x_{n}}(I)),

for all
k\geq 1
. We are now ready to prove Theorem
4.1
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Using Proposition
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle\textup{HS}_{1}(I^{k+1})\

\displaystyle=\ (\mathfrak{m}I^{k+1})^{\leq{\bf deg}(I^{k+1})}\ =\ (\mathfrak{m}I^{k+1})^{\leq(k+1){\bf deg}(I)}

\displaystyle=\ I^{\leq{\bf deg}(I)}(\mathfrak{m}I^{k})^{\leq k{\bf deg}(I)}

\displaystyle=\ I\cdot\textup{HS}_{1}(I^{k}),

for all
k\geq 1
. In the above equation, only the equality

(\mathfrak{m}I^{k+1})^{\leq(k+1){\bf deg}(I)}\ =\ I^{\leq{\bf deg}(I)}(\mathfrak{m}I^{k})^{\leq k{\bf deg}(I)}

needs to be justified. That the right-hand is included in the left-hand side is clear. Conversely, let
w\in\mathcal{G}((\mathfrak{m}I^{k+1})^{\leq(k+1){\bf deg}(I)})
. Then
w=u_{1}\cdots u_{k+1}x_{s}
with
u_{j}\in\mathcal{G}(I)
and
\deg_{x_{s}}(w)\leq(k+1)\deg_{x_{s}}(I)
. Since
\deg_{x_{s}}(u_{j})\leq\deg_{x_{s}}(I)
for all
j=1,\dots,k+1
, we can find some integer
j
such that
\deg_{x_{s}}(u_{j})<\deg_{x_{s}}(I)
. Up to relabeling, we may assume
j=k+1
. Setting
w^{\prime}=u_{1}\cdots u_{k}x_{s}
, we see that
w^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}((\mathfrak{m}I^{k})^{\leq k{\bf deg}(I)})
and so
w=u_{k+1}w^{\prime}\in I^{\leq{\bf deg}(I)}(\mathfrak{m}I^{k})^{\leq k{\bf deg}(I)}
.

∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
I\subset S
be a polymatroidal ideal. Then

\textup{HS}_{1}(\mathcal{R}(I))\ =\ ((\mathfrak{m}I)^{\leq{\bf deg}(I)}\mathcal{R}(I))(1),

where “
(1)
” denotes the shift by degree one.

5.
Finite generation of
\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))

In this section, we focus our attention on the generating degrees of the homological shift algebras of a polymatroidal ideal. Due to Theorem
4.1
and several experimental evidence, we expect that

Conjecture 5.1
.

Let
I\subset S
be a polymatroidal ideal. Then
\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))
is generated in degrees
\leq i
as a
\mathcal{R}(I)
-module, for all
i>0
.

This conjecture in the case
i=n-1
has already been posed by Chu, Herzog and Lu in
[
6
]
, and solved for the family of pruned path lattice polymatroidal ideals.

Theorem
4.1
settles Conjecture
5.1
in the case
i=1
.

At the moment, we do not have a general strategy to settle Conjecture
5.1
for
i\geq 2
. Therefore, in what follows, we establish this conjecture for some large families of polymatroidal ideals. These families are:

(a)

Principal Borel ideals.

(b)

Polymatroidal ideals satisfying the strong exchange property.

(c)

Matroidal edge ideals.

5.1.
Principal Borel ideals

Let
u=x_{j_{1}}\cdots x_{j_{d}}\in S
be a monomial of degree
d
, with
1\leq j_{1}\leq\dots\leq j_{d}\leq n
. The
principal Borel ideal
generated by
u
is the monomial ideal

B(u)\ =\ (x_{p_{1}}\cdots x_{p_{d}}:\ 1\leq p_{1}\leq\dots\leq p_{d}\leq n,\ p_{s}\leq j_{s}\ \textup{for all}\ s=1,\dots,d).

This ideal is polymatroidal, and therefore it has linear quotients with respect to the lexicographic order of
\mathcal{G}(B(u))
. For a monomial
v\in S
,
v\neq 1
, we put
\max(v)=\max\{j:x_{j}\ \textup{divides}\ v\}
. It is not difficult to see that for all
v\in\mathcal{G}(B(u))
we have
\textup{set}(v)=[\max(v)-1]=\{1,2,\dots,\max(v)-1\}
.

Now, we can establish Conjecture
5.1
for principal Borel ideals.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
I=B(u)
. Then
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})=I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
for all
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

It is immediate to see that
I^{s}=B(u)^{s}=B(u^{s})
for all
s\geq 1
. Now, let
w\in\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})
. By the previous description and equation (
1
) we can write
w={\bf x}_{F}v
with
v\in\mathcal{G}(I^{k+1})=\mathcal{G}(B(u^{k+1}))
and
F\subset[\max(v)-1]
is a subset of cardinality
i
. We can write
v=v_{1}\cdots v_{k+1}
with each
v_{s}\in\mathcal{G}(I)
. We can assume that
\max(v)=\max(v_{1})
. Then
v^{\prime}=v_{1}\cdots v_{k}\in\mathcal{G}(I^{k})=\mathcal{G}(B(u^{k}))
. Since
\textup{set}(v^{\prime})=\textup{set}(v)=[\max(v)-1]
we obtain that
w^{\prime}={\bf x}_{F}v^{\prime}\in\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
. Hence
w=v_{k+1}w^{\prime}\in I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
. This shows that
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})\subset I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
for all
k\geq 1
. Since the converse inclusion holds as well for all
k\geq 1
, the assertion follows.

∎

5.2.
Polymatroidal ideals satisfying the strong exchange property

We say that a monomial ideal
I\subset S
generated in a single degree satisfy the
strong exchange property
if: for all
u,v\in\mathcal{G}(I)
, all
i
such that
\deg_{x_{i}}(u)>\deg_{x_{i}}(v)
and all
j
such that
\deg_{x_{j}}(u)<\deg_{x_{j}}(v)
we have
x_{j}(u/x_{i})\in I
.

It is clear that ideals satisfying the strong exchange property, satisfy the exchange property as well, and therefore are polymatroidal.

Given a vector
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
and an integer
d
with
a_{1}+\dots+a_{n}\geq d
, the ideal of
Veronese type
(determined by
{\bf a}
and
d
) is the polymatroidal ideal
I_{{\bf a},d}
with minimal monomial generating set

\mathcal{G}(I_{{\bf a},d})\ =\ \{{\bf x^{b}}\in S:\ |{\bf b}|=d,\ {\bf b}\leq{\bf a}\}.

Here, if
{\bf b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
we put
{\bf b}(i)=b_{i}
,
|{\bf b}|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\bf b}(i)
is the modulus of
{\bf b}
and
{\bf b}\leq{\bf a}
means that
{\bf b}(i)\leq{\bf a}(i)
for all
i
.

Ideals of Veronese type have been studied in
[
28
,
31
]
. By the work
[
28
]
, it is known that ideals satisfying the strong exchange property are essentially ideals of Veronese type in the following sense.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal generated in a single degree. Then
I
satisfies the strong exchange property, if and only if,
I=(u)I_{{\bf a},d}
for some monomial
u\in S
, and some
{\bf a}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
and
d>0
.

We are going to prove the following result.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
I
be a polymatroidal ideal satisfying the strong exchange property. Then
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})=I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
for all
k\geq 1
.

The proof of this result requires some preparations.

We follow the notation from
[
27
]
and
[
7
]
. Let
P
be an integral polymatroid on
[n]
with rank function
\rho
. We use
B(P)
to denote the set of bases of
P
, that is,

B(P)\ =\ \{{\bf u}\in P\cap\mathbb{N}^{n}\ :\ {\bf u}([n])=\rho([n])\}.

Here, for
A\subset[n]
and a vector
{\bf u}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
{\bf u}(A)
denotes the sum
\sum_{i\in A}{\bf u}(i)
.

A
cage
for
P
is an integral vector
{\bf c}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
such that
{\bf u}(i)\leq{\bf c}(i)
for any
{\bf u}\in P
and any
i\in[n]
. Let
I
be the polymatroidal ideal of
P
. That is, the ideal
I
generated by the monomials
{\bf x^{u}}
with
{\bf u}\in B(P)
. Then
{\bf deg}(I)
is a cage for
P
, and for any other cage
{\bf c}
of
P
we have
{\bf c}\geq{\bf deg}(I)
.

Fix a cage
{\bf c}
of
P
. The set
\{{\bf c-u}:\ {\bf u}\in B(P)\}
is then the set of bases of a polymatroid, which we call the
dual polymatroid
of
P
with respect to
{\bf c}
and denote by
P_{\bf c}^{\vee}
, or simply by
P^{\vee}
. See
[
19
, Theorem 1.3]
for a related result. The rank function of
P_{\bf c}^{\vee}
is given by

\rho^{\vee}(A)\ =\ \rho([n]\setminus A)-\rho([n])+{\bf c}(A).

The Möbius function of a polymatroid was introduced in
[
7
]
. However, it has a close connection to the classical notion of a Möbius function defined on a partially ordered set (poset), which we recall now. Let
Q
be a finite poset. The incidence algebra
I(Q)
of a poset
Q
consists of all functions
f:Q\times Q\to\mathbb{Z}
such that
f(x,y)=0
whenever
x\not\leq y
.

Convolution is the multiplication operation in
I(Q)
. For two functions
f,g\in I(Q)
, their convolution
f*g
is defined by:

(f*g)(x,y)\ =\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in Q\\
x\leq z\leq y\end{subarray}}f(x,z)\cdot g(z,y)

where the sum ranges over all elements
z
that lie “between”
x
and
y
in the poset (i.e.,
z
satisfies
x\leq z\leq y
).

Convolution is associative, meaning
(f*g)*h=f*(g*h)
, and the incidence algebra
I(Q)
becomes a ring under this operation (with addition defined pointwise).

The zeta function, denoted by
\zeta
, is a fundamental function in
I(Q)
. Formally,
\zeta:Q\times Q\to\mathbb{Z}
is defined by:

\zeta(x,y)\ =\ \begin{cases}\,1&\text{if }x\leq y,\\
\,0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Intuitively,
\zeta(x,y)
“detects” whether
x
is less than or equal to
y
in the poset.

The Möbius function, denoted
\mu
, is the inverse of the zeta function with respect to convolution in the incidence algebra. Formally,
\mu\in I(Q)
satisfies:

\zeta\mu\ =\ \mu\zeta\ =\ \delta,

where
\delta
is the identity element of the incidence algebra, defined by:

\delta(x,y)\ =\ \begin{cases}\,1&\text{if }x=y,\\
\,0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The Möbius function can be computed recursively using the definition of convolution. For
x,y\in Q
:

-

If
x=y
,
\mu(x,y)=1
.

-

If
x<y
(i.e.,
x\leq y
but
x\neq y
), then

\mu(x,y)\ =\ -\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in Q\\
x\leq z<y\end{subarray}}\mu(x,z)\ =\ -\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in Q\\
x<z\leq y\end{subarray}}\mu(z,y).

-

If
x\not\leq y
,
\mu(x,y)=0
.

We now turn to the definition of the Möbius function on a polymatroid
P
.

Definition 5.5
.

Consider
P\cap\mathbb{N}^{n}
as a finite poset, where
{\bf u}\leq{\bf v}
if and only if
{\bf u}(i)\leq{\bf v}(i)
for all
i\in[n]
. Adjoin the element
\widehat{1}
to the poset
P\cap\mathbb{N}^{n}
such that
\widehat{1}
becomes the maximal element of the resulting poset. Let
\mu_{1}
denote the classical Möbius function on this resulting set. The Möbius function on the polymatroid
P
is then defined as the function
\mu_{P}
from
P\cap\mathbb{N}^{n}
to
\mathbb{N}
given by

\mu_{P}({\bf u})=-\mu_{1}({\bf u},\widehat{1}),\quad\textup{for all}\ {\bf u}\in P\cap\mathbb{N}^{n}.

It is not difficult to see that this definition is equivalent to
[
7
, Definition 2.1]
.

Let
{\bf e}_{1},\dots,{\bf e}_{n}
be the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. That is,
{\bf e}_{i}(j)=0
if
j\neq i
and
{\bf e}_{i}(i)=1
.

Let
I
be the polymatroidal ideal of the polymatroid
P
with cage
{\bf c}
. Let
{\bf u}\in B(P)
and
1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<\cdots<j_{i}\leq n
. According to
[
7
, Theorem A(iii)]
, the monomial
x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{1}}\cdots x_{j_{i}}{\bf x^{u}}
belongs to
\textup{HS}_{i}(I)
if and only if
\mu_{P^{\vee}}({\bf c-u}-{\bf e}_{j_{1}}-\ldots-{\bf e}_{j_{i}})\neq 0
.

Let
I
be the polymatroidal ideal of the integral polymatroid
P
. Then
I^{k+1}
is the polymatroidal ideal of
P_{k+1}=P\vee P\vee\cdots\vee P
, where
P
repeats
(k+1)
times, see
[
27
, Theorem 12.1.5]
. Note that if
{\bf c}
is a cage of
P
, then
(k+1){\bf c}
is a cage of
P_{k+1}
. It follows that
P_{k+1}^{\vee}=(P^{\vee})_{k+1}
. Let
\mu_{k+1}^{\vee}
denote the Möbius function on
P_{k+1}^{\vee}
.

For integers
d>0
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
with
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}\geq d
, let

B\ =\ \{{\bf u}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\ :\ {\bf u}(i)\leq a_{i},\ \textup{for all}\ i=1,\dots,n,\ {\bf u}([n])=d\}.

Then
B
represents the set of bases of a discrete polymatroid
P
on the ground set
[n]
, of rank
d
, which is called a discrete polymatroid of Veronese type. Its polymatroidal ideal
I\subset S
is the ideal of Veronese type
I_{{\bf a},d}
with
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
.

Lemma 5.6
.

Fix positive integers
i,n
and let
{\bf c}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}^{n}
. Let

P=\{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\ y_{1}+\cdots+y_{n}\leq i,\ 0\leq y_{s}\leq a_{s},\ \text{for all}\ s=1,\ldots,n\}.

Let
\mu
be the Möbius function on
P
. Then
\mu(\mathbf{0})=0
if and only if
n\leq i
.

Proof.

It is clear that
{\bf c}
is a cage for
P
. Let
I
be the monomial ideal generated by
{\bf x^{c-u}}
with
{\bf u}\in B(P)
. In other words,
I
is the polymatroidal ideal of the dual polymatroid of
P
with respect to
{\bf c}
. If
i\leq n
, let
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
be the vector
(1,\ldots,1,0,\ldots,0)
, where the first
i
entries are 1’s , and the remaining entries are 0’s. It is clear that
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in B(P)
. Otherwise, if
i>n
, let
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
be any vector
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in B(P)
with
\alpha_{j}>0
for all
j=1,\ldots,n
. Note that, if
i\leq n
we may write
\mathbf{0}=\boldsymbol{\alpha}-{\bf e}_{1}-\cdots-{\bf e}_{i}
.
According to
[
7
, Theorem A(iii)]
, we have
\mu(\mathbf{0})\neq 0
if and only if
{\bf x}^{{\bf c}-\boldsymbol{\alpha}}{\bf x}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
(which is equal to
x_{1}\cdots x_{i}{\bf x}^{{\bf c}-\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
if
i\leq n
) belongs to
\textup{HS}_{i}(I)
.

(a) If
i\geq n
, since the projective dimension of
I
is at most
n-1
, we have
\textup{HS}_{i}(I)=0
, which implies
\mu(\mathbf{0})=0
.

(b) If
i<n
, we set
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{s}={\bf e}_{1}+\cdots+\widehat{{{\bf e}_{s}}}+\cdots+{\bf e}_{i+1}
, for
s=1,\ldots,i
. Here,
\widehat{{{\bf e}_{s}}}
means that
{\bf e}_{s}
is omitted from the sum. Then all
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{s}
belong to
B(P)
. Set
u={\bf x}^{{\bf c}-\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=x_{1}^{a_{1}-1}x_{2}^{a_{2}-1}\cdots x_{i}^{a_{i}-1}x_{i+1}^{a_{i+1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}
. It follows that
{\bf x}^{{\bf c}-\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{s}}=(x_{s}u)/x_{i+1}
are minimal generators of
I
, for all
s=1,\dots,i
. Moreover

(x_{1}u)/x_{i+1}>(x_{2}u)/x_{i+1}>\cdots>(x_{i}u)/x_{i+1}>u

with respect to the lexicographic order induced by
x_{1}>\dots>x_{n}
. Since
I
has linear quotients with respect to any lexicographic order, it follows that
[i]\subset\textup{set}(u)
. Equation (
1
) implies that
x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{i}u\in\textup{HS}_{i}(I)
and so
\mu(\mathbf{0})\neq 0
, as desired.

∎

We are finally ready to prove Proposition
5.4
.

Proof of Proposition
5.4
.

Notice that if
J\subset S
is a monomial ideal and
u\in S
is a monomial, then
\textup{HS}_{i}(uJ)=(u)\textup{HS}_{i}(J)
. Therefore, in view of Proposition
5.3
, it is enough to prove the statement for polymatroidal ideals of Veronese type.

Assume that
I
is the polymatroidal ideal of
P
, where

P=\{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\ y_{1}+\cdots+y_{n}\leq d,\ 0\leq y_{i}\leq a_{j},\ \textup{for all}\ j=1,\ldots,n\}.

Here,
{\bf c}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
is a given vector in
\mathbb{N}^{n}
. When discussing the dual polymatroid of
P_{k}
or
P_{k+1}
, we take
k({\bf c+1})=k(a_{1}+1,\ldots,a_{n}+1)
or
(k+1)({\bf c+1})
as the cages respectively, so as to avoid unnecessary confusion.

Fix
1\leq i\leq n-1
and
k\geq 1
. Let
{\bf x}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
be a minimal generator of
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})
. Then we may write
\boldsymbol{\alpha}={\bf u}+{\bf e}_{j_{1}}+\cdots+{\bf e}_{j_{i}}
for some vector
{\bf u}\in B(P_{k+1})
and some
1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<\cdots<j_{i}\leq n
. Let
P_{k+1}^{\vee}
be the dual polymatroid of
P_{k+1}
with respect to the cage
(k+1)({\bf c+1})
. Then
P_{k+1}^{\vee}
is the following polymatroid:

\Big\{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ \Big|\ \ \begin{subarray}{c}\displaystyle y_{1}+\cdots+y_{n}\leq(k+1)\big({\bf c}([n])+n-d\big)\\[3.0pt]
\displaystyle 0\leq y_{j}\leq(k+1)a_{j},\ 1\leq j\leq n\end{subarray}\Big\}.

Let
\mu_{k+1}^{\vee}
be the Möbius function on
P_{k+1}^{\vee}
. According to
[
7
, Theorem A(iii)]
, we have
\mu_{k+1}^{\vee}((k+1)({\bf c+1})-\boldsymbol{\alpha})\neq 0
. Note that, the poset

\{{\bf v}\in P_{k+1}^{\vee}\cap\mathbb{N}^{n}\ :\ {\bf v}\geq(k+1)({\bf c+1})-\boldsymbol{\alpha}\}

is isomorphic to the poset

\{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\ :\ y_{1}+\cdots+y_{n}\leq i,\ 0\leq y_{j}\leq{\bf u}(j)+k_{j}\}.

Here,
k_{j}=1
if
j\in\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{i}\}
and 0 otherwise. Note that this poset is the intersection of
\mathbb{N}^{n}
with a polymatroid, which we denote by
Q
. Since
\mu_{k+1}^{\vee}((k+1)({\bf c+1})-\boldsymbol{\alpha})\neq 0
, we have
\mu_{Q}({\bf 0})\neq 0
. By Lemma
5.6
, it follows that there exists
j\in[n]\setminus\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{i}\}
such that
{\bf u}(j)\neq 0
.

Write
{\bf u}={\bf u}_{1}+\cdots+{\bf u}_{k+1}
, with each
{\bf u}_{i}\in B(P)
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
{\bf u}_{1}(j)\neq 0
. Let
\boldsymbol{\beta}={\bf u}_{1}+\ldots+{\bf u}_{k}+{\bf e}_{j_{1}}+\cdots+{\bf e}_{j_{i}}
. Let
P_{k}^{\vee}
be the dual polymatroid of
P_{k}
with respect to
k(\mathbf{c+1})
and let
\mu_{k}^{\vee}
be its Möbius function. Then
\{{\bf v}\in P_{k}^{\vee}\ :\ {\bf v}\geq k({\bf c+1})-\boldsymbol{\beta}\}
is isomorphic to the poset

\{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\ :\ y_{1}+\cdots+y_{n}\leq i,\ 0\leq y_{j}\leq{\bf u}_{1}(j)+\cdots+{\bf u}_{k}(j)+k_{j}\}.

Here,
k_{j}=1
if
j\in\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{i}\}
and
0
otherwise.

Since
{\bf u}_{1}(j)\neq 0
, it follows that
\mu_{k}^{\vee}(k({\bf c+1})-\boldsymbol{\beta})\neq 0
by Lemma
5.6
. This implies
{\bf x}^{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\in\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})
and so
{\bf x}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}={\bf x}^{{\bf u}_{k+1}}{\bf x}^{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
belongs to
I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
, as desired.

∎

One may conjecture that if
I=I_{{\bf a},d}
, then
\textup{HS}_{i}(I)=I_{{\bf a},d+i}
for
i=1,\ldots,n-1
. This equality holds for
i=1
. Furthermore,
\textup{HS}_{i}(I)\subset I_{{\bf a},d+i}
for all
i\geq 1
. However, the converse inclusion does not hold in general, as shown next.

Example 5.7
.

Let
{\bf a}=(10,10,10,10)
,
d=14
and
I=I_{{\bf a},d}
. Then
x_{1}^{10}x_{2}^{7}
belongs to
I_{{\bf a},17}
. However, every monomial in the minimal generating set of
\textup{HS}_{3}(I)
is of the form
x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}x_{j_{3}}u
, where
u\in\mathcal{G}(I)
and
1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<j_{3}\leq 4
. Hence
x_{1}^{10}x_{2}^{7}\notin\textup{HS}_{3}(I)
.

This example also shows that the homological shift ideals of a Veronese type ideal are not of Veronese type in general.

5.3.
Matroidal edge ideals

For a finite simple graph
G
on the vertex set
[n]
and with edge set
E(G)
, the
edge ideal
of
G
is defined as the squarefree monomial ideal

I(G)=(x_{i}x_{j}:\ \{i,j\}\in E(G)).

Definition 5.8
.

Given positive integers
a\leq b
, we set
[a,b]=\{c\in\mathbb{Z}:a\leq c\leq b\}
. Let
G
be a graph with vertex set
V(G)=[n]
and a vertex partition
V_{1},\ldots,V_{r}
, where

V_{i}=[t_{i-1}+1,t_{i}],\quad\text{ for all }\,\,\,i=1,\ldots,r,

with
t_{0}=0
,
t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{r-1}
and
t_{r}=n
. If
E(G)=\{\{i,j\}:\ i\in V_{k},\ j\in V_{\ell}\text{ and }k\neq\ell\}
, then
G
is called a
complete multipartite graph
.

The following result is proved in
[
19
, Theorem 3.1]
, see also
[
35
]
.

Proposition 5.9
.

The ideal
I(G)
is matroidal if and only if
G
is a complete multipartite graph.

Next, we establish Conjecture
5.1
for matroidal edge ideals.

Proposition 5.10
.

Let
I=I(G)\subset S
be a matroidal edge ideal. Then
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})=I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
for all
k\geq i
.

We need the following lemma shown in
[
21
, Proposition 4.7]
.

Let
u=x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}\in S
be a monomial and
A\subset[n]
be non-empty. We put
u_{A}=\prod_{i\in A}x_{i}^{a_{i}}
and define the support of
u
as
\textup{supp}(u)=\{i:\ a_{i}>0\}
.

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
G
be a complete multipartite graph as given in Definition
5.8
, and put
I=I(G)
. Then,
I^{k}
has linear quotients with respect to the lexicographical order induced by
x_{1}>\cdots>x_{n}
. Let
u\in\mathcal{G}(I^{k})
with
\max(u)\in V_{d}
for some
d
. Then, the following statements hold.

(i)

If there exists some
d^{\prime}<d
such that
\deg u_{V_{d^{\prime}}}=k
, then

\textup{set}(u)=[1,p-1]\cup[{t_{d^{\prime}}+1},{\max(u)-1}]

where
p
is the maximal integer with
p\in\textup{supp}(u_{V_{d^{\prime}}})
.

(ii)

If
\deg u_{V_{d^{\prime}}}<k
for all
d^{\prime}<d
, then
\textup{set}(u)=[1,\max(u)-1]
.

We are ready to prove Proposition
5.10
.

Proof of Proposition
5.10
.

By Proposition
5.9
we can write
I=I(G)
where
G
is a complete multipartite graph as given in Definition
5.8
. For
i=0,1
the statements hold by Theorem
4.1
. Let
i\geq 2
. We will show that
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})=I\cdot\textup{HS}_{1}(I^{k})
for all
k\geq 2
. It is enough to show that
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})\subset I\cdot\textup{HS}_{1}(I^{k})
for all
k\geq 2
. Let
w\in\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1}))
for some
k\geq 2
. By equation (
1
) we can write
w={\bf x}_{F}e_{1}\cdots e_{k+1}
with
e_{i}\in\mathcal{G}(I)
and
F
is a subset of size
i
of
\textup{set}(u)
, where
u=e_{1}\cdots e_{k+1}\in\mathcal{G}(I^{k+1})
. Let
\max(u)\in V_{d}
for some integer
d
. According to Lemma
5.11
, one of the two conditions (i)-(ii) holds.

Assume that (i) holds. Then
\deg u_{V_{d^{\prime}}}=k+1
and so
\deg(e_{j})_{V_{d^{\prime}}}=1
for all
j=1,\dots,k+1
. Let
p
be the maximal integer belonging to
\textup{supp}(u_{V_{d^{\prime}}})
. Since
x_{p}
divides
u
, it divides some
e_{j}
, say
e_{1}
. Now
x_{\max(u)}
divides some
e_{j}
as well. Up to relabeling,
x_{\max(u)}
divides either
e_{1}
or
e_{2}
. Let
u^{\prime}=e_{1}\cdots e_{k}\in\mathcal{G}(I^{k})
. Since
k\geq 2
, we have
\max(u^{\prime})=\max(u)\in V_{d}
,
\deg(u^{\prime})_{V_{d^{\prime}}}=k
and
p\in\textup{supp}(u^{\prime})
is the maximal element belonging to
\textup{supp}((u^{\prime})_{V_{d^{\prime}}})
. Applying again Lemma
5.11
(i), this then shows that
\textup{set}(u^{\prime})=\textup{set}(u)
and so
w^{\prime}={\bf x}_{F}u^{\prime}\in\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
. Finally,
w=e_{k+1}w^{\prime}\in I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
.

Assume that (ii) holds. Then
\textup{set}(u)=[\max(u)-1]
. Suppose that
x_{\max(u)}
divides
e_{k+1}=x_{p}x_{q}
. We claim that there exists
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
such that
\textup{set}(u/e_{j})=\textup{set}(u)
. If this is the case, then
F\subset\textup{set}(u/e_{j})
and so
w=e_{j}(w/e_{j})\in I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
, as desired. Suppose now that this is not the case. Then,
\textup{set}(u/e_{1})\neq\textup{set}(u)
and
\textup{set}(u/e_{2})\neq\textup{set}(u)
. Since
k\geq 2
and
x_{\max(u)}
divides
e_{k+1}
, we have

\max(u/e_{1})=\max(u/e_{2})=\max(u).

By Lemma
5.11
(i), there exist
d_{1},d_{2}<d
such that
\deg(u/e_{1})_{V_{d_{1}}}=\deg(u/e_{2})_{V_{d_{2}}}=k
. Notice that
d_{1}\neq d_{2}
, otherwise
\deg(u)_{V_{d_{1}}}=k+1
, which by Lemma
5.11
(i) would mean that
\textup{set}(u)\neq[\max(u)-1]
against the assumption. Hence
d_{1}\neq d_{2}
. Since
e_{k+1}=x_{p}x_{q}
, this implies, up to relabeling, that
p\in V_{d_{1}}
and
q\in V_{d_{2}}
. This contradicts the fact that
\max(u)=\max(p,q)\in V_{d}
. The proof is complete.

∎

6.
Asymptotic Betti numbers of
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})

In this short section, given a polymatroidal ideal
I\subset S
, we analyze the asymptotic behavior of the homological invariants of
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
. As an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.1
, we have

Corollary 6.1
.

Let
I\subset S
be a polymatroidal ideal. Then

\textup{reg}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})\ =\ \textup{v}(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}))+1\ =\ \alpha(I)k+i,

whenever
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})\neq(0)
.

Proof.

Since
I^{k}
is polymatroidal, Theorem
1.1
implies that
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
is again polymatroidal. Suppose that
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})\neq(0)
. Then
\textup{reg}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})=\alpha(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}))=\alpha(I)k+i
and by
[
14
, Theorem 5.5]
we have
\textup{v}(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}))=\alpha(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}))-1
.

∎

In the next result, we prove that the graded Betti numbers of
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
increase as
k
increases.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
I\subset S
be a polymatroidal ideal. Then

\beta_{j}(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}))\ \leq\ \beta_{j}(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1}))

for all
k\geq 1
. In particular, the function
k\mapsto\textup{depth}\,S/\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
is non-increasing. That is,

\textup{depth}\,S/\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})\ \geq\ \textup{depth}\,S/\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1}),

for all
k\geq 1
.

For the proof of this result, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
I,J\subset S
be ideals with
d
-linear resolution such that
J\subset I
. Then

\beta_{j}(J)\leq\beta_{j}(I),\quad\textit{for all}\ j\geq 0.

Proof.

The short exact sequence
0\rightarrow J\rightarrow I\rightarrow I/J\rightarrow 0
induces the exact sequence

\textup{Tor}^{S}_{j+1}(K,I/J)_{j+d}\rightarrow\textup{Tor}^{S}_{j}(K,J)_{j+d}\rightarrow\textup{Tor}^{S}_{j}(K,I)_{j+d}.

Since
I/J
is generated in degree
d
, we have
\textup{Tor}^{S}_{j+1}(K,I/J)_{j+d}=0
. Therefore, the map
\textup{Tor}^{S}_{j}(K,J)_{j+d}\rightarrow\textup{Tor}^{S}_{j}(K,I)_{j+d}
is injective. Since
I
and
J
have
d
-linear resolution, we deduce that
\beta_{j}(J)=\beta_{j,j+d}(J)\leq\beta_{j,j+d}(I)=\beta_{j}(I)
.

∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
6.2
.

Proof of Theorem
6.2
.

If
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})=(0)
there is nothing to prove. Let
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})\neq(0)
.
Since
I
has linear powers, Theorem
2.1
guarantees that
I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})\subset\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})
. Now, let
u\in\mathcal{G}(I)
. Then
(u)\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})\subset I\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})\subset\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})
. By Lemma
6.3
and Theorem
1.1
we have

\beta_{j}(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}))\ =\ \beta_{j}((u)\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}))\ \leq\ \beta_{j}(\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})),

for all
j
and all
k\geq 1
.

∎

Let
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
be the unique graded maximal ideal of
S
. As an immediate consequence of Theorem
6.2
we have

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
I\subset S
be a polymatroidal ideal. If
\mathfrak{m}\in\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k_{0}})
for some
k_{0}>0
, then
\mathfrak{m}\in\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
for all
k\geq k_{0}
.

Proof.

We have
\mathfrak{m}\in\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k_{0}})
if and only if
\textup{depth}\,S/\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k_{0}})=0
. Suppose that
\mathfrak{m}\in\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k_{0}})
for some
k_{0}>0
. Since by Theorem
6.2
the depth function
k\mapsto\textup{depth}\,S/\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
is non-increasing, it follows that
\textup{depth}\,S/\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})=0
for all
k\geq k_{0}
. Hence
\mathfrak{m}\in\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
for all
k\geq k_{0}
.

∎

Corollary
6.4
is not valid for an arbitrary monomial prime ideal.

Example 6.5
.

Let
I=(x_{1}x_{3},x_{1}x_{4},x_{1}x_{5},x_{2}x_{3},x_{2}x_{4},x_{2}x_{5},x_{3}x_{4},x_{3}x_{5})
. Then
I
is polymatroidal,
P=(x_{1},x_{5})\in\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{2}(I)
but
P\notin\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{2}(I^{2})
. However, using the
Macaulay2

[
26
]
package
HomologicalShiftIdeals

[
15
]
we have

\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{2}(I^{2})\subset\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{2}(I^{3})\subset\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{2}(I^{4})\subset\cdots.

This fact is proven for any matroidal edge ideal in Corollary
7.5
(c).

7.
Asymptotic associated primes of
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})

We now turn our attention to the behavior of the sequence
\{\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})\}_{k>0}
when
I\subset S
is a polymatroidal ideal. In Corollary
6.4
we have seen that if
I\subset S
is polymatroidal and
\mathfrak{m}\in\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k_{0}})
for some
k_{0}>0
, then
\mathfrak{m}\in\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
for all
k\geq k_{0}
. Due to this result and several experimental evidence, we expect that

Conjecture 7.1
.

Let
I\subset S
be a polymatroidal ideal. Then

\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{i})\subset\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{i+1})\subset\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{i+2})\subset\cdots,\quad\text{for all}\ i>0.

When a monomial ideal
I\subset S
satisfies the chain of inclusions

\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I)\subset\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{2})\subset\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{3})\subset\cdots,

for some
i
, we say that
I
satisfies the
i
th
homological persistence property
.

Whereas, if

\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1}):I\ =\ \textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k}),\quad\textup{for all}\ k\geq 1,

for some
i
, we say that
I
satisfies the
i
th
homological strong persistence property
.

In
[
21
, Proposition 3.1]
, it is shown that if
\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1}):I=\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})
for some
k
, then
\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k})\subset\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{k+1})
. Hence, the
i
th homological strong persistence property implies the
i
th homological persistence property.

We need the following general result, which follows from the determinantal trick.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
I,J
be ideals of a Noetherian domain
R
. Suppose that
I^{k}J
is integrally closed for some
k\geq 0
. Then
I^{k+1}J:I=I^{k}J
.

Proof.

It is clear that
I^{k}J\subset I^{k+1}J:I
. Conversely, let
f\in I^{k+1}J:I
, then
fI\subset I^{k+1}J=(I^{k}J)I
. Using the determinantal trick
[
33
, Corollary 1.1.8]
, and since
R
is a domain, we see that
f
is an integral element over
I^{k}J
. But
I^{k}J
is integrally closed by assumption. We conclude that
f\in I^{k}J
, as desired.

∎

As a consequence of this result, we have

Theorem 7.3
.

Conjecture
5.1
implies Conjecture
7.1
.

Proof.

Assume that Conjecture
5.1
holds true and set
J=\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{i})
for some
i>0
. Since
\textup{HS}_{i}(\mathcal{R}(I))
is generated in degrees
\leq i
as a
\mathcal{R}(I)
-module,

\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{s})\ =\ I^{s-i}\cdot\textup{HS}_{i}(I^{i})\ =\ I^{s-i}J,

for all
s\geq i
. In view of
[
21
, Proposition 3.1]
mentioned before, it is enough to show that
I^{k+1}J:I=I^{k}J
for all
k\geq 0
. Since
S
is a Noetherian domain and
I^{k}J=\textup{HS}_{1}(I^{k+i})
is a polymatroidal ideal by Theorem
1.1
and hence is integrally closed (see
[
31
, Theorem 2.4]
), Theorem
7.2
yields the conclusion.

∎

As an immediate consequence of this result and Theorem
4.1
, we have

Corollary 7.4
.

Let
I\subset S
be a polymatroidal ideal. Then
I
satisfies the
1
st homological strong persistence property. That is,
\textup{HS}_{1}(I^{k+1}):I=\textup{HS}_{1}(I^{k})
for all
k\geq 1
. In particular,

\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{1}(I)\subset\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{1}(I^{2})\subset\textup{Ass}\,\textup{HS}_{1}(I^{3})\subset\cdots.

Combining Theorem
7.3
with Propositions
5.2
,
5.4
and
5.10
we obtain

Corollary 7.5
.

Conjecture
7.1
holds true for the following families of ideals.

(a)

Principal Borel ideals.

(b)

Polymatroidal ideals satisfying the strong exchange property.

(c)

Matroidal edge ideals.

8.
Multiplicative properties of polymatroidal ideals

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal. The
socle
of
I
is the ideal defined as

\textup{soc}(I)\ =\ ({\bf x^{a}}:\ {\bf x^{a}}\in(I:\mathfrak{m})\setminus I).

The following result is well-known, see
[
29
, Proposition 1.13]
.

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal with linear resolution. Then

\textup{HS}_{n-1}(I)\ =\ x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n}\cdot\textup{soc}(I).

The product of polymatroidal ideals is polymatroidal. In the next result, we show that a similar but opposite property holds when we multiply by
\mathfrak{m}
.

Proposition 8.2
.

Let
I\subset S
be an ideal with linear resolution. Suppose that
\mathfrak{m}I
is polymatroidal. Then
\textup{soc}(\mathfrak{m}I)=I
, and so
I
is polymatroidal.

Proof.

Let
\alpha(I)=d
be the initial degree of
I
. Firstly, we show that
(\mathfrak{m}I:\mathfrak{m})=I
. The inclusion
I\subset(\mathfrak{m}I:\mathfrak{m})
is clear. Suppose that there exists
f\in(\mathfrak{m}I:\mathfrak{m})\setminus I
. Then
\deg(f)=d-1
by
[
6
, Proposition 1.4]
. Since
\mathfrak{m}I
is generated in degree
d+1
, then
(\mathfrak{m}I:\mathfrak{m})
is generated in degrees
\geq d
. This a contradiction, since
\deg(f)=d-1
. Thus
(\mathfrak{m}I:\mathfrak{m})=I
. Since any
u\in\mathcal{G}(I)
does not belong to
\mathfrak{m}I
by degree reasons, it follows that
\textup{soc}(\mathfrak{m}I)=I
. Finally, since
\mathfrak{m}I
is polymatroidal, Proposition
8.1
and Theorem
1.1
imply that
\textup{soc}(\mathfrak{m}I)=I
is polymatroidal too.

∎

Given monomial ideals
L,P\subset S
with
P
prime, the
monomial localization
of
L
at
P
is defined as the monomial ideal
L(P)
in
S(P)=K[x_{i}:x_{i}\in P]
, obtained by applying the substitutions
x_{i}\mapsto 1
for all
x_{i}\notin P
.

Proposition
8.2
was conjectured in
[
2
]
. In such a paper, Bandari and Herzog conjectured that a monomial ideal
I\subset S
is polymatroidal, if and only if,
I(P)
has a linear resolution, for all monomial prime ideals
P\subset S

[
2
, Conjecture 2.9]
.

The next result was proved in
[
2
, Proposition 2.8]
under the additional assumption that
I
does not have embedded associated primes. See also
[
37
, Proposition 1.13]
for a different proof.

Corollary 8.3
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal with
\textup{height}\,I=n-1
such that
I(P)
has a linear resolution for all monomial prime ideals
P\subset S
. Then
I
is polymatroidal.

Proof.

Write
I=J\cap Q
, where
J
is the intersection of the primary components of
I
having height
n-1
and
Q
is a
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Then any
P\in\textup{Ass}\,J
is a minimal prime of
J
. Thus
S(P)/J(P)
has finite length for all
P\in\textup{Ass}\,J
. Since
J(P)=I(P)
has a linear resolution,
[
2
, Lemma 2.2]
implies that
J(P)=P^{k}
for some
k
. Hence,

J\ =\ P_{1}^{k_{1}}\cap\dots\cap P_{r}^{k_{r}}

for some monomial prime ideals
P_{j}
of height
n-1
. Using that
P_{i}+P_{j}=\mathfrak{m}
for all
i\neq j
, then
[
25
, Theorem 3.1]
guarantees that
J
is componentwise polymatroidal. Let
\alpha(I)=d
. Then,
\mathfrak{m}^{j-d}I=I_{\langle j\rangle}=J_{\langle j\rangle}
for
j\gg 0
, because
Q
is
\mathfrak{m}
-primary. Since
J
is componentwise polymatroidal,
\mathfrak{m}^{j-d}I
is polymatroidal. Repeated applications of Proposition
8.2
yield the conclusion.

∎

9.
Componentwise polymatroidal ideals

Let
I\subset S
be a homogeneous ideal. Then
I=\bigoplus_{j\geq 0}I_{j}
, where
I_{j}
is the
K
-vector space spanned by the homogeneous polynomials of degree
j
belonging to
I
. We denote by
I_{\langle j\rangle}
the homogeneous ideal of
S
generated by
I_{j}
.

We say that a monomial ideal
I\subset S
is
componentwise polymatroidal
if
I_{\langle j\rangle}
is a polymatroidal ideal for all
j
. If
I
is a polymatroidal generated in degree
d
, then
I_{\langle j\rangle}=(0)
for
j<d
and
I_{\langle j\rangle}=\mathfrak{m}^{j-d}I
for
j\geq d
. Hence, polymatroidal ideals are componentwise polymatroidal. Componentwise polymatroidal ideals have linear quotients
[
16
]
, see also
[
12
, Proposition 2]
for a more general result.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.1

[
7
, Theorem A(iii)]
, we have

Corollary 9.1
.

Let
I\subset S
be a componentwise polymatroidal ideal. Then
(I:\mathfrak{m})
is componentwise polymatroidal.

Proof.

For all
j
, we have
(I:\mathfrak{m})_{\langle j\rangle}=(I_{\langle j+1\rangle}:\mathfrak{m})_{\langle j\rangle}=\textup{soc}(I_{\langle j+1\rangle})
, where the last equality follows from
[
6
, Proposition 1.4]
. Since
I_{\langle j+1\rangle}
is polymatroidal, the assertion follows from Proposition
8.1
and Theorem
1.1
.

∎

Let
I
be an ideal of
S
. The
saturation
of
I
is the ideal defined as

I^{\textup{sat}}\ =\ \bigcup_{k\geq 0}(I:\mathfrak{m}^{k}).

We have the ascending chain

I\subset(I:\mathfrak{m})\subset(I:\mathfrak{m}^{2})\subset\dots\subset(I:\mathfrak{m}^{k})\subset\cdots.

Since
S
is a Noetherian ring, there exists a smallest integer
\textup{sat}(I)
, called the
saturation number
of
I
, such that
(I:\mathfrak{m}^{k+1})=(I:\mathfrak{m}^{k})
for all
k\geq\textup{sat}(I)
. Hence,
I^{\textup{sat}}=(I:\mathfrak{m}^{\textup{sat}(I)})
. As a consequence of Corollary
9.1
we have

Corollary 9.2
.

Let
I\subset S
be a componentwise polymatroidal ideal. Then
I^{\textup{sat}}
is componentwise polymatroidal.

Proof.

We can write

I^{\textup{sat}}\ =\ (I:\mathfrak{m}^{\textup{sat}(I)})=((\cdots(((I:\mathfrak{m}):\mathfrak{m}):\mathfrak{m}):\cdots):\mathfrak{m}),

where the colon is taken
\textup{sat}(I)
times. Hence, the assertion follows by applying Corollary
9.1

\textup{sat}(I)
times.

∎

10.
Componentwise polymatroidality of
\textup{HS}_{1}(I)

Let
I\subset S
be a componentwise polymatroidal ideal. We expect that
\textup{HS}_{i}(I)
is again componentwise polymatroidal, see
[
16
, Question 9]
. At the moment, we are only able to prove this expectation for the first homological shift ideal.

Theorem 10.1
.

Let
I\subset S
be a componentwise polymatroidal ideal. Then
\textup{HS}_{1}(I)
is componentwise polymatroidal.

Proof.

Let
I=\bigoplus_{j\geq 0}I_{j}
. Then
I_{\langle j\rangle}
is a polymatroidal ideal for all
j
. We claim that
\textup{HS}_{1}(I)_{\langle j\rangle}=\textup{HS}_{1}(I_{\langle j-1\rangle})
for all
j
. This fact together with Theorem
1.1
implies the assertion. Let
L=\bigoplus_{j\geq 0}L_{j}
where
L_{j}
is the
K
-vector space spanned by
\mathcal{G}(\textup{HS}_{1}(I_{\langle j-1\rangle}))
. We claim that
L
is an ideal, and that
\textup{HS}_{1}(I)=L
. Once we acquire these claims, it follows that
\textup{HS}_{1}(I)_{\langle j\rangle}=L_{\langle j\rangle}=\textup{HS}_{1}(I_{\langle j-1\rangle})
for all
j
, as desired.

Let us show that
L
is an ideal. We only need to prove that
\mathfrak{m}L_{\langle j\rangle}\subset L_{\langle j+1\rangle}
for all
j
. Let
{\bf 1}=(1,\dots,1)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
. Since
I
is an ideal, we have
\mathfrak{m}I_{\langle j-1\rangle}\subset I_{\langle j\rangle}
, and consequently
{\bf deg}(I_{\langle j-1\rangle})+{\bf 1}\leq{\bf deg}(I_{\langle j\rangle})
for all
j
. Using this inequality and Proposition
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}L_{\langle j\rangle}\

\displaystyle=\ \mathfrak{m}\,\textup{HS}_{1}(I_{\langle j-1\rangle})\ =\ \mathfrak{m}(\mathfrak{m}I_{\langle j-1\rangle})^{\leq{\bf deg}(I_{\langle j-1\rangle})}

\displaystyle\subset\ \mathfrak{m}(I_{\langle j\rangle})^{\leq{\bf deg}(I_{\langle j-1\rangle})}\ =\ (\mathfrak{m}I_{\langle j\rangle})^{\leq{\bf deg}(I_{\langle j-1\rangle})+{\bf 1}}

\displaystyle\subset\ (\mathfrak{m}I_{\langle j\rangle})^{\leq{\bf deg}(I_{\langle j\rangle})}\ =\ \textup{HS}_{1}(I_{\langle j\rangle})

\displaystyle=\ L_{\langle j+1\rangle}.

It remains to prove that
\textup{HS}_{1}(I)=L
. Let
w\in L
be a monomial of degree
j
. Then
w\in L_{\langle j\rangle}=\textup{HS}_{1}(I_{\langle j-1\rangle})
and by
[
21
, Lemma 2.2]
we have
w=\textup{lcm}(u,v)
for some
u,v\in\mathcal{G}(I_{\langle j-1\rangle})\subset I
. Since, again by
[
21
, Lemma 2.2]
, we have

\textup{HS}_{1}(I)\ =\ (\textup{lcm}(f,g):\ f,g\in\mathcal{G}(I),\ f\neq g),

we see that
w\in\textup{HS}_{1}(I)
and so
L\subset\textup{HS}_{1}(I)
.

Conversely, let
w\in\textup{HS}_{1}(I)
be a minimal monomial generator. Since
I
has linear quotients
[
16
]
, by equation (
1
) we can write
w=x_{i}u
with
u\in\mathcal{G}(I)
and there exists
v\in\mathcal{G}(I)
with
v>u
in the linear quotients order of
I
such that
v:u=\textup{lcm}(v,u)/u=x_{i}
. By
[
34
, Lemma 2.1]
, we can assume that
\deg(v)\leq\deg(u)
. Let
d=\deg(u)
,
d^{\prime}=\deg(v)
and set
v^{\prime}=x_{j}^{d-d^{\prime}}v
for some
j\neq i
. Then
v^{\prime},u\in\mathcal{G}(I_{\langle d\rangle})
and moreover
\deg_{x_{i}}(v^{\prime})=\deg_{x_{i}}(v)>\deg_{x_{i}}(u)
. By the symmetric exchange property applied to
I_{\langle d\rangle}
, we can find
s
with
\deg_{x_{s}}(v^{\prime})<\deg_{x_{s}}(u)
such that
u^{\prime}=x_{i}(u/x_{s})\in\mathcal{G}(I_{\langle d\rangle})
. By
[
21
, Lemma 2.2]
, we have
w=\textup{lcm}(u,u^{\prime})\in\textup{HS}_{1}(I_{\langle d\rangle})=L_{\langle d+1\rangle}\subset L
. Hence
\textup{HS}_{1}(I)\subset L
and this completes the proof.

∎

In general, products and powers of componentwise polymatroidal ideals are no longer componentwise polymatroidal, see
[
2
, Example 3.3]
. On the other hand, it is expected
[
2
,
16
]
that powers of a componentwise polymatroidal ideal
I\subset S
are componentwise linear. Whether the homological shift algebra of
I
, or of any componentwise linear ideal, can be structured as a finitely generated
\mathcal{R}(I)
-module, or whether any of the results in this paper remains valid for componentwise polymatroidal ideals, remain open questions.

Apparent diffusion coefficient histogram parameters of the whole tumor based on whole-body magnetic resonance imaging can better characterize the tumor burden of multiple myeloma and predict survival prognosis
Authors: Huazheng Dong, Qingyan Qi, Xiaodong Ji, Shanshan Gao, Kemin Zhang, Song Liu, Zhiyi Song, Wenyang Huang, Shuang Xia
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundNewly diagnosed multiple myeloma (NDMM) is characterized by a spatially heterogeneous tumor burden. Whole-body magnetic resonance imaging (WB-MRI)-derived apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) histogram analysis may enable comprehensive characterization of tumor burden. This study aimed to investigate the feasibility of using whole-tumor ADC histogram parameters based on WB-MRI images to assess tumor burden and evaluate their prognostic value in NDMM patients.MethodsThere were 95 NDMM patients categorized into focal lesion and whole-tumor groups in this retrospective study. Correlation analysis of ADC histogram parameters with clinical parameters was performed in the two groups respectively. In the survival analysis section, 64 patients were included, Cox regression analysis was used to conduct univariate and multivariate analysis of potential risk factors for overall survival (OS). X-tile plots were used to determine the optimal cutoffs based on the highest χ2 value defined by Kaplan-Meier survival analysis and log-rank test.ResultsApart from ADCmax, there was statistically significant difference in the remaining ADC histogram parameters between two groups (all P<0.05). In the whole-tumor group, ADCmean (r=0.434, P<0.01), ADC5th (r=0.334, P<0.01), ADC10th (r=0.371, P<0.01), ADC25th (r=0.416, P<0.01), ADC50th (r=0.431, P<0.01), ADC75th (r=0.424, P<0.01), ADC90th (r=0.398, P<0.01), and ADC95th (r=0.402, P<0.01) were positively correlated with bone marrow plasma cell (BMPC). Multivariate Cox regression showed that BMPC (P=0.022), β2-MG (P=0.016), ADC25th (P=0.023), ADC95th (P<0.001) and entropy (P=0.001) were all independent prognostic factors for poor OS. X-tile plots and the Kaplan-Meier survival curves showed that BMPC (>70%), β2-MG (>10.68 mg/L), ADC25th (>0.37 mm2/s), ADC95th (>1.03 mm2/s) and low entropy (≤3.56) achieved worse prognosis.ConclusionsWhole tumor histogram parameters can better characterize the tumor burden of multiple myeloma (MM) patients than those of the focal lesion groups. The Cox regression risk model based on the whole tumor ADC (wtADC) histogram parameters and clinical parameters can predict the survival status.

Introduction

Multiple myeloma (MM), a hematological malignancy which affects the monoclonal plasma cells in the bone marrow, is in rising incidence around the world, accounting for approximately 2% of newly diagnosed cancer cases in the United States, Australia, and Western Europe (1). Each year over 35,000 new cases are diagnosed in the United States, and almost 13,000 patients die of the disease (2). MM is a refractory, relapse-prone and incurable disease due to its extreme spatial heterogeneity (3). Prior investigations did not adequately address the intratumoral heterogeneity of MM, which has limited the accurate assessment of patient prognosis.

Whole-body magnetic resonance imaging (WB-MRI) is recognized by the International Myeloma Working Group as the most sensitive technique for detecting bone marrow infiltration (4). The key sequence in the Myeloma Response Assessment and Diagnosis System (MY-RADS) is whole-body diffusion-weighted imaging (WB-DWI), which is recommended for initial scans in patients (5).

Previous studies have demonstrated a positive correlation between mean apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) values and various microstructural features, including the number of bone marrow cells and bone marrow microvascular density (6,7). However, MM is a systemic disease with complex and heterogeneous internal tissue microstructures. The use of the average ADC value alone is insufficient to fully reflect the tumor lesion heterogeneity and overall tumor burden. Moreover, the method of analyzing selected bone marrow regions in previous studies is not precise enough, with research often focusing on the mean ADC values of single focal lesions at a single level or across multiple levels (8-10).

ADC histogram analysis, a promising quantitative tool, reflects intratumoral variability via multiple parameters. The whole tumor ADC (wtADC) histogram involves delineating all bone marrow regions affected by the lesion, consolidating the information from all lesions to obtain the ADC histogram of the entire region of interest (ROI) of the lesion. It offers supplementary insights into tumor burden and has proven superior for diagnosing, staging, and prognosticating various tumors (11,12). Yet, to our knowledge, its application in assessing MM tumor burden and prognosis remains unexplored.

Many MM patients receive WB-MRI scans prior to treatment. However, beyond diagnostic assistance, the extensive imaging data from these scans remain underutilized. This study aimed to explore the feasibility of using wtADC histogram parameters derived from WB-MRI images to assess MM tumor burden. Additionally, we evaluated the potential of these parameters to stratify MM patients for improved prognostication. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://qims.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/qims-2026-0524/rc).

Methods

Patient enrollment and follow-up

Between July 2015 and February 2022, 238 MM identified patients who underwent WB-MRI examinations were retrospectively enrolled from Tianjin First Central Hospital. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Tianjin First Central Hospital. Individual consent for this retrospective analysis was waived. The established diagnosis of MM was identified based on the International Myeloma Working Group criteria (4). For the imaging analyses, exclusion criteria were listed: (I) the patients who were not newly diagnosed MM; (II) no bone marrow involvement in WB-MRI; (III) unavailable evaluation image due to motion artifact; (IV) the interval between magnetic resonance (MR) examination and laboratory data acquisition exceeded 1 week.

For the survival analysis, follow-up was conducted until October 30, 2023 and all patients were followed up for at least 2 years, exclusion criteria were listed: (I) lost to follow up; (II) patients who received fewer than four treatment cycles (Figure 1). Patients were contacted via telephone or consulted to determine their survival status; the main outcome of this study was death during follow-up. Overall survival (OS) is defined as the time from the date of treatment to the date of death or the date of the last follow-up.

WB-MRI equipment and sequence selection

Patients were scanned with 3.0-T wide-bore MRI scanner (Ingenia; Phillips Healthcare, Best, The Netherlands), equipping with a 20-channel head-neck coil and a 32-channel body phased-array coil. A multi-station acquisition of adjacent body regions was performed to cover the entire body from the top of the head to the feet. The scanning protocol involved coronal T2-STIR, coronal T1-weighted mDixon, and coronal DWI sequences with b-values set at 0 and 800 s/mm2. Table S1 provides acquisition parameters for above sequences. Reconstructed image sets for DWI included a 3D inverted gray-scale positron emission tomography-like display of high b-values and ADC maps.

Image analysis, lesion definition and ADC histogram parameter acquisition

Two radiologists (Q.Q. and X.J. with 17 and 18 years of experience in musculoskeletal MRI, respectively) independently reviewed WB-MRI scans blinded to clinical data. Images in the Dicom format were imported into Firevoxel leading center for Advanced Imaging Innovation and Research (New York University School of Medicine, NY, USA), and the MM lesions throughout the whole body were analyzed independently. Each patient underwent two evaluations by radiologists, with a minimum interval of two weeks between assessments. Bone marrow was subjectively examined in seven anatomic sites (cervical spine, thoracic spine, lumbar spine, pelvis, long bones, skull and ribs/other). No patients in the cohort presented with paramedullary or extramedullary disease at initial diagnosis, all analyzed lesions were confined to the bone marrow.

Lesion segmentation was performed manually on the ADC maps (b =800 s/mm2) by two radiologists in consensus, with strict reference to the corresponding high-b-value DWI images to ensure accurate delineation of tumor boundaries. Suspected sites of bone marrow abnormality were identified using an ADC threshold of >0.7×10−3 mm2/s (5). ROIs were drawn layer by layer to encompass the entire lesion area as much as possible while avoiding degenerative changes, benign lesions, osteoporosis, and gas areas (5). For focal lesions, the target lesion minimum diameter was ≥5 mm. For diffuse disease, the anatomical site involved for the whole bone marrow was measured. For a given site with both focal and diffuse lesions, all lesions were delineated (Figure 2). All voxel-level ADC values within these ROIs were extracted using Firevoxel software. Histogram parameters were subsequently calculated from the pooled voxel-level ADC distribution. For the whole tumor group, ROIs were drawn on all measurable marrow lesions throughout the body and merged to obtain the whole-tumor ADC histogram. For the focal lesion group, the largest focal lesion was identified on DWI images and segmented on the ADC map to generate the focal lesion ADC histogram.

All enrolled patients were classified into one of the four pathological MY-RADS patterns (5) (cases with a normal bone marrow pattern were excluded based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria): focal, diffuse, combined focal/diffuse, and salt-and-pepper. For the purpose of this study, these 95 patients were subsequently divided into two study-specific groups to investigate the feasibility of using wtADC histogram parameters to assess systemic tumor burden.

The whole tumor group included all patients, the whole-tumor analysis calculated ADC histogram parameters from all detectable myeloma lesions plus diffusely infiltrated marrow across the entire skeleton (including nodular lesions and diffuse lesions). The focal lesion group was a subgroup of the whole-tumor group, comprising focal-type patients with only nodular lesions on DWI images and a minimum lesion diameter greater than 1 cm; the largest lesion of each patient (13) was selected to obtain the information of the ADC histogram.

All measurements were performed using fully manual delineation, The Firevoxel software was used to combine all ROIs and yield whole tumor histogram parameters for all the lesions throughout the whole body.

Lesion classification, identification, segmentation, and grouping were performed by consensus between two radiologists. Discrepancies were resolved through discussion until consensus was reached.

They included ADCmin, ADCmax, ADCmean, standard deviation (SD), variance, Inhomogeneity, ADC percentile (5th, 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th, 90th or 95th), skewness, kurtosis and entropy.

Statistical analysis

The normality of distribution of clinical data, focal lesion and whole tumor histogram parameters was assessed by the Shapiro-Wilk test. Normally and nonnormally distributed continuous data was presented as mean ± SD and median with interquartile range, respectively. Categorical variables were presented as count and percentage. The χ2 test and Fisher exact test were used for categorical variables, and Mann-Whitney test and independent samples t-test were applied to continuous variables. Pearson’s correlation or Spearman’s rank correlation were performed to assess the correlation between clinical data and histogram parameters. The correlation coefficients were interpreted as follows: |r|<0.40 as weak, 0.40≤|r|<0.70 as moderate, and |r|≥0.70 as strong.

Variables with P<0.10 in the univariable Cox regression analysis were included in the multivariable Cox regression analysis to further identify independent factors associated with OS. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were performed for variables independently associated with OS, and area under the curve (AUC) was calculated. We used Schoenfeld tests and residual plots to assess the model.

X-tile plots provide a single and intuitive approach for evaluating the association between variables and survival. Given that no well-recognized thresholds have been established for wtADC histogram parameters, X-tile plots were used to determine the optimal cutoffs of these variables based on the highest χ2 value (minimum P value) defined by Kaplan-Meier survival analysis and log-rank test. X-tile plots were generated by X-tile software (version 3.6.1; Yale University School of Medicine) (14).

An intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) test was conducted to evaluate the interobserver repeatability of the wtADC histogram parameters, with ICC values ≥0.81 indicating excellent agreement. Two-tailed P<0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (version 26.0, IBM) and R language (version 4.3.0; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). A two-sided P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant for all statistical tests.

The focal lesion group, a subgroup of the whole-tumor group, consisted of 52 patients (16 females and 36 males; mean age, 57.52±11.73 years). For survival analysis, 31 patients were excluded from the whole-tumor group, leaving 64 patients (29 survival, 35 death) (Figure 1). Factors associated with long-term survival

Among included patients, 64 (67.37%) patients (44 men; 68.75%) achieved follow-up survival outcome, and the baseline characteristics are listed in Table S5. The mean age of these patients was 55.5±1.34 years. According to the DS staging system, the proportions of patients in stages I, II, and III were 7.8%, 12.5%, and 79.7%, respectively. According to the ISS, the proportions of patients in stages I, II, and III were 12.5%, 43.8%, and 43.8%, respectively. According to the R-ISS, the proportions of patients in stages I, II, and III were 10.94%, 65.63%, and 23.44%, respectively. The median survival time was 46.88 months (range, 4–103 months). The 1-year survival rate for the 64 patients was 77%, and the 2-year survival rate was 46%.

The univariable Cox regression analysis revealed that BMPC, HGB, β2-MG, creatinine LDH, ISS staging (III), R-ISS staging (III), treatment regimen, ADCmax, ADCmean, kurtosis, entropy, ADC25th, ADC50th, ADC75th, ADC90th, and ADC95th are independent predictors for patient survival status (Table 4).

Discussion

Our investigation has yielded the following three significant findings. First, the whole tumor histogram parameters can better characterize the tumor burden of MM patients than focal lesion groups. Second, ADC histogram parameters coherently relate to established myeloma biomarkers, reflecting underlying pathophysiological processes. Third, the Cox regression risk model, based on the wtADC histogram and clinical parameters in patients with MM can predict their survival status. Based on our research results, for patients with a high likelihood of poor prognosis, such as those with elevated BMPC, β2-MG, ADC25th, ADC95th values and a low entropy level, alternative treatment options could be taken into account.

In recent times, researchers have incorporated ADC parameters into their investigations of MM. Sun et al. (15) proved that MM lesions had significantly higher ADC mean value than hyperplastic hematopoietic bone marrow. In a meta-analysis study (16), it has been demonstrated that ADC mean value was useful in the initial staging and re-staging of the disease and treatment response assessment. Li et al. (17) found that using ADC histogram parameters to predict high-risk cytogenetic abnormalities in patients with MM is feasible. In our previous study, we found that histogram analysis of ADC images can accurately predict treatment response after four cycles of induction chemotherapy (18).

While we are not the pioneers in introducing the concept of whole tumor histogram analysis (19), our study represents the inaugural investigation employing this approach to explore the prognosis of patients with MM. We found that compared to the focal lesion groups, the whole tumor groups have a greater number of histogram parameters correlated with clinical parameters. MM patients who have high BMPC (>70%), β2-MG (>10.68 mg/L), ADC25th (>0.37×10−3 mm2/s), ADC95th (>1.03×10−3 mm2/s) and low entropy (≤3.56) achieved worse prognosis. Treatment heterogeneity was addressed by including treatment regimen as a covariate in multivariable Cox regression. After adjustment for other significant prognostic factors, ‘Induction + Other’ remained significantly associated with mortality (HR =57.79; 95% CI: 12.00–278.28; P<0.001), confirming treatment heterogeneity as an important confounder. However, ADC histogram parameters retained independent prognostic significance, suggesting their predictive value is robust to treatment variation.

Our correlation analysis demonstrates that ADC histogram parameters coherently relate to established myeloma biomarkers, reflecting underlying pathophysiological processes. ADC25th correlated with BMPC in opposite directions in focal versus whole-tumor analyses, ultimately converging on plasma cell infiltration from complementary perspectives. ADC95th correlated with β2-MG, characterizing a high-burden, aggressive phenotype with necrosis and heterogeneity. Multiple ADC parameters also correlated with HGB and LDH, linking low ADC to marrow infiltration and high ADC to tumor turnover. Collectively, these imaging and laboratory markers, though measuring different biological aspects, are coherently interconnected through common pathophysiological processes of tumor burden and aggressiveness.

Among the ADC histogram parameters, only inhomogeneity demonstrated significant correlations with clinical markers (negatively with HGB, r=−0.401; positively with LDH, r=0.324; both P<0.01), reflecting that greater structural heterogeneity is associated with marrow infiltration and aggressive tumor biology. Notably, skewness, kurtosis, and entropy showed no significant correlations with any clinical parameter (all |r|<0.20, P>0.05). However, entropy emerged as an independent prognostic factor in multivariate survival analysis (HR =−0.05, P=0.001), with low entropy (≤3.56) identifying high-risk patients when combined with elevated BMPC, β2-MG, ADC25th, and ADC95th. This apparent discrepancy underscores that entropy captures global texture complexity—an emergent property of the whole ADC distribution—not linearly reflected by individual laboratory markers, yet carrying complementary prognostic information through non-linear, multivariate interactions (20).

BMPC and β2-MG are indeed crucial clinical biomarkers for assessing the prognosis of patients with MM, and they play a pivotal role in the staging system of MM, which are consistent with prior findings. One study (21) included 1426 MM patients showed that BMPC% ≥60% at diagnosis predicted OS in a multivariate analysis. reported that having BMPC% >70% was associated with a HR that was 2.2 times higher than those with BMPC% <30%. According to Cherng et al. (22), MM patients with a BMPC% >70% experienced a HR 2.2-fold greater compared to those whose BMPC% <30%. The underlying reason might be the positive correlation between BMPC% at diagnosis and the count of mesenchymal stromal cells expressing STRO-1, as noted in reference (23) These cells serve as a prolific supply of osteoclasts and factors that activate plasma cells, potentially facilitating their growth and survival. An elevated BMPC% at the time of diagnosis could intensify the interaction between plasma cells and their surrounding microenvironment, thereby complicating the management of the disease (23). β2-MG is a key variable for MM prognostication because it triggers NLRP3 inflammasome activation in tumor-associated macrophages to promote multiple myeloma progression (24,25).

ADC histograms provide quantitative data that comprehensively reflect tumor heterogeneity. The low-percentile regions (e.g., ADC5th, ADC10th, ADC25th) are associated with tumor grading and biological behavior, reflecting areas of high cellularity and restricted diffusion, which are typically linked to more aggressive tumor behavior (12,26). Conversely, the high-percentile regions (e.g., ADC75th, ADC90th, ADC95th) reflect areas of low cellularity, tumor necrosis, cystic components, and facilitated diffusion, indicating treatment response or intratumoral heterogeneity (12).

In our study, both ADC25th and ADC95th were independently associated with OS, both parameters may predict similar clinical outcomes (e.g., poor prognosis). Although ADC25th and ADC95th represent opposite extremes of the histogram, they capture distinct biological information through different pathophysiological mechanisms. they do so by quantifying different aspects of the tumor microenvironment: the lower percentiles are more sensitive to the most cellular and aggressive tumor components, whereas the higher percentiles capture the presence of necrotic or cystic regions. This suggest that tumor heterogeneity at both ends of the ADC distribution carries significant prognostic value.

It is hypothesized that lower ADC values might suggest a favorable prognosis. This could be attributed to the fact that such values may imply the existence of well-perfused viable tissue in diseased areas. This kind of tissue is conducive to the transportation and retention of anticancer drugs, a theory that has also been put forward in the study of other cancers (27).

Li et al. (17) found that the wtADC entropy was lower in the high-risk cytogenetic abnormalities (HRCA) group than in the non-HRCA group, to a certain extent, the results are similar to ours because the HRCA group has a poorer prognosis (28).

We find ADC histograms analyses are very useful in the presence of extensive, diffuse disease on WB-DWI. These findings are consistent with previous studies demonstrating that comprehensive characterization of the entire ADC histogram provides more robust prognostic information than single summary statistics such as ADCmean alone (29,30).

This research was subject to certain limitations. First, a single-institution retrospective cohort was employed, which meant there was a lack of external verification. To validate the reproducibility and the threshold of ADC histogram parameters, it is essential to extend the research scope to larger-scale multicenter cohorts. Second, the method of manually outlining lesions throughout the body is extremely time-consuming. In the subsequent research, efforts will be dedicated to applying automated segmentation technology to contour lesions more efficiently. Third, this study only included patients with bone marrow infiltration. Since patients with multiple myeloma but normal bone marrow were not included, there may be a certain degree of selection bias. Future research needs to utilize more information to explore the prognosis of this group of patients. Fourth, due to substantial treatment heterogeneity and limited sample size, the efficacy of various treatments could not be assessed using ADC histogram analysis in the current study. Future prospective studies employing standardized treatment protocols, larger homogeneous cohorts, and longitudinal WB-MRI are warranted to validate ADC histogram analysis as a tool for treatment response assessment in multiple myeloma. Fifth, only the largest focal lesion per patient was analyzed, which may not reflect inter-lesional heterogeneity in patients with multiple lesions despite representing the dominant tumor burden. Sixth, it is agreed that ADC is an useful parameter, but it is also affected by T2 relaxation time, and in some scenarios, the T2 effect may be even dominant (31,32). A further limitation is that overall mortality was used as the primary endpoint without detailed information regarding the cause of death.

Conclusions

In conclusion, histogram parameters of the whole tumor based on WB-MRI was a useful imaging biomarker to characterize the tumor burden of multiple myeloma and predict survival prognosis.

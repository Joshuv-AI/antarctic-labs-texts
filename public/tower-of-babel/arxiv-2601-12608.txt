On the second Bohr radius for vector valued pluriharmonic functions
Himadri Halder

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce the notion of the second Bohr radius for vector valued pluriharmonic functions on complete Reinhardt domains in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} . This investigation is motivated by the work of Lev Aizenberg [Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 128 (2000), 1147 –1155], where the corresponding problem was studied for complex valued holomorphic functions. We show that the second Bohr radius constant for pluriharmonic functions is strictly positive under suitable condition. In addition, we obtain its asymptotic behavior in the finite-dimensional settings using invariants from local Banach space theory. Asymptotic estimates for this constant are obtained on both convex and non-convex complete Reinhardt domains. Our results also apply to a broad class of Banach sequence spaces, including symmetric and convex Banach spaces. The framework developed here also includes the second Bohr radius problem for vector valued holomorphic functions. As an application of our results, we derive several consequences that extend known results in the scalar valued setting as well as existing results in the literature.

1.
Introduction and the main results

The classical theorem of Harald Bohr
[
12
]
, proved over a century ago, has developed into a broad and active area of research in complex and functional analysis, now referred to as Bohr’s theorem and the Bohr radius problem. Bohr’s original result concerns holomorphic functions of one complex variable. Interest in this theme was revived in the
1990
s following its successful application in Banach algebras (see
[
26
]
), as well as subsequent generalizations to holomorphic functions of several complex variables (see
[
10
,
2
]
) and to more abstract settings in functional analysis (see
[
3
]
), especially those related to local Banach space theory (see
[
19
]
).

In higher dimensions, several approaches have been proposed to generalize Bohr’s theorem. A significant breakthrough was achieved by Boas and Khavinson (see
[
10
]
), who introduced the notion of the Bohr radius, now referred to as the first Bohr radius, for complete Reinhardt domains in

\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Their work opened a new direction in the function theory of several complex variables, revealing deep connections with functional analysis, including fundamental aspects of both the local and global theory of Banach spaces. Independently, Aizenberg
[
2
]
introduced another multidimensional analogue, known as the second Bohr radius, for complete Reinhardt domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Among the various aspects of Bohr’s theorem, a particularly intriguing direction emerges when the problem is studied for vector valued holomorphic functions on complete Reinhardt domains through the lens of local Banach space theory. This viewpoint naturally builds a bridge between the function theory of several complex variables and functional analysis. Along these lines, Blasco
[
8
,
9
]
was the first to initiate the study of the Bohr phenomenon for Banach space valued holomorphic functions defined on the unit disc. Subsequently, Defant, Maestre, and Schwarting
[
24
]
extended this investigation to vector valued holomorphic functions by studying first Bohr radius on the unit polydisc. A major breakthrough was achieved by Defant, García, and Maestre
[
19
]
, who studied the first Bohr radius problem for complex valued holomorphic functions on the unit ball of finite-dimensional Banach spaces with a canonical basis that is normalized and 1-unconditional.

More recently, the present author
[
30
]
carried out a systematic study of the first Bohr radius problem for vector valued holomorphic and pluriharmonic functions defined on bounded complete Reinhardt domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, analyzing the asymptotic behavior of the Bohr radius in both finite- and infinite-dimensional settings. The framework developed in this article extends the classical Minkowski-space setting to a substantially broader class of symmetric Banach sequence spaces.

To the best of our knowledge, the second Bohr radius problem for pluriharmonic functions remains unexplored. In this work, we address this problem for vector valued pluriharmonic functions on complete Reinhardt domains via local Banach space theory. We derive asymptotic estimates of this constant in both finite and infinite dimensions. As an application, we obtain results for Banach sequence spaces, demonstrating that our framework applies to a broader class of domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

In order to address this problem, we first recall the definitions of the first Bohr radius for vector valued holomorphic and pluriharmonic functions introduced in
[
30
]
(see also
[
24
]
). Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a complete Reinhardt domain and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\mathcal{H}(\Omega,X)
denote the set of all bounded
X
valued holomorphic functions on
\Omega
. Let
U:X\rightarrow Y
be a non-null bounded linear operator between complex Banach spaces
X
and
Y
, with
\left\lVert U\right\rVert\leq\lambda
. For
1\leq p<\infty
, the first
\lambda
-powered Bohr radius of

with respect to the class
\mathcal{H}(\Omega,X)
, denoted by
K_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
, is defined as the supremum of all
r\in[0,1]
such that for every
f\in\mathcal{H}(\Omega,X)
of the form
f(z)=\sum_{\alpha}a_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
one has

(1.1)

\sup_{z\in r\Omega}\,\sum_{\alpha}\left\lVert U(a_{\alpha})z^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}_{Y}\leq\lambda^{p}\,\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{\Omega,X},

where
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\Omega,X}:=\sup_{z\in\Omega}\left\lVert f(z)\right\rVert_{X}
. We now fix some notation. We write
K(\Omega,p,U):=K_{1}(\Omega,p,U)
. When
U=I:X\rightarrow X
is the identity operator, we set
K_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,X):=K_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,I)
and
K(\Omega,p,X):=K_{1}(\Omega,p,X)
. In the scalar-valued case, we denote
K_{\lambda}(\Omega,p):=K_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,\mathbb{C})
and
K(\Omega,p):=K_{1}(\Omega,p)
. With these notations, the classical Bohr’s theorem asserts that
K(\mathbb{D},1)=1/3
(see
[
12
]
). The constant
K(\Omega,1)
was first investigated by Boas and Khavinson (see
[
10
,
11
]
). We return to a detailed discussion of this constant in Section
3
.

Recall that, on a simply connected complete Reinhardt domain
\Omega
, a continuous function
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is pluriharmonic if and only if it admits a decomposition of the form

(1.2)

f(z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}a_{\alpha}\,z^{\alpha}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}b^{*}_{\alpha}\,\bar{z}^{\alpha},

where
h(z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}a_{\alpha}\,z^{\alpha}
and

are
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
-valued holomorphic functions on
\Omega
. Here
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
denotes the algebra of all bounded linear operators acting on a complex Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. For
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
,
\left\lVert T\right\rVert
denotes the operator norm and
T^{*}
the usual adjoint, and we write
Re(T):=(T+T^{*})/2
. If
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
, then
\overline{z}
denotes the componentwise complex conjugate
(\overline{z}_{1},\ldots,\overline{z}_{n})
.

We denote by
\mathcal{PH}(\Omega,X)
the space of all bounded
X
-valued pluriharmonic functions on
\Omega
, where
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. The boundedness condition means that
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\Omega,X}:=\sup_{z\in\Omega}\,\left\lVert f(z)\right\rVert_{X}<\infty
for every
f\in\mathcal{PH}(\Omega,X)
.

Let
Y
be any complex Banach space. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a simply connected complete Reinhardt domain and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
U:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow Y
be a bounded liner operator and
\left\lVert U\right\rVert\leq\lambda
. For
1\leq p<\infty
, the first
\lambda
-powered Bohr radius of

with respect to the class
\mathcal{PH}(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
, denoted by
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
, is defined to be the supremum of all
r\in[0,1]
such that for all
f\in\mathcal{PH}(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
of the form (
1.2
) we have

(1.3)

\sup_{z\in r\Omega}\,\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}(\left\lVert U(a_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{p}_{Y}+\left\lVert U(b_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{p}_{Y})|z^{\alpha}|^{p}\leq\lambda^{p}\,\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})}.

For short, write
R(\Omega,p,U):=R_{1}(\Omega,p,U)
,
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})):=R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
whenever
U=I:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
,
R(\Omega,p,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})):=R_{1}(\Omega,p,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
,
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p):=R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,\mathbb{C})
, and
R(\Omega,p):=R_{1}(\Omega,p)
. The above definition was introduced by the present author of this article, who also undertook a detailed study of the asymptotic behaviour of
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
(see
[
30
]
). The Bohr phenomenon for harmonic functions of one variable in the scalar setting was first investigated in
[
1
]
. Subsequently, the asymptotic behaviour of the constants
R(\Omega,1)
and
R(\Omega,p)
was investigated in
[
16
,
18
]
whenever
\Omega=B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
, where
B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}:=\left\{z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{q}:=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|z_{i}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}<1\right\}
,
1\leq q<\infty
and
B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}}:=\mathbb{D}^{n}
.

In a related but distinct approach, Aizenberg
[
2
]
introduced another variant of multidimensional Bohr radius, namely the second Bohr radius, for complex valued holomorphic functions on bounded complete Reinhardt domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Primarily motivated by this notion, together with the above definition, we now introduce the concept of the second Bohr radius for operator valued pluriharmonic functions. Let
\Omega,\,Y,\,U
and
\lambda
be as in the above definition defined by (
1.3
). For
1\leq p<\infty
, the second
\lambda
-powered Bohr radius of
U
with respect to the class
\mathcal{PH}(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
, denoted by
L_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
, is defined to be the supremum of all
r\in[0,1]
such that for all
f\in\mathcal{PH}(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
of the form (
1.2
) we have

(1.4)

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\sup_{z\in r\Omega}\,(\left\lVert U(a_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{p}_{Y}+\left\lVert U(b_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{p}_{Y})|z^{\alpha}|^{p}\leq\lambda^{p}\,\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})}.

For brevity, we adopt the following notations:
L(\Omega,p,U):=L_{1}(\Omega,p,U)
,
L_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})):=L_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
whenever
U=I:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
,
L(\Omega,p,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})):=L_{1}(\Omega,p,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
,
L_{\lambda}(\Omega,p):=L_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,\mathbb{C})
, and
L(\Omega,p):=L_{1}(\Omega,p)
.

In the same spirit as that of pluriharmonic functions, one can consider the corresponding notion of the second Bohr radius for Banach space valued holomorphic functions. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a complete Reinhardt domain and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
U:X\rightarrow Y
be a bounded liner operator between two complex Banach spaces and
\left\lVert U\right\rVert\leq\lambda
. For
1\leq p<\infty
, the second
\lambda
-powered Bohr radius of
U
with respect to the class
\mathcal{H}(\Omega,X)
, denoted by
B_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
, is defined as the supremum of all
r\in[0,1]
such that for every
f\in\mathcal{H}(\Omega,X)
of the form
f(z)=\sum_{\alpha}a_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
one has

(1.5)

\sum_{\alpha}\sup_{z\in r\Omega}\,\left\lVert U(a_{\alpha})z^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}_{Y}\leq\lambda^{p}\,\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{\Omega,X},

where
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\Omega,X}:=\sup_{z\in\Omega}\left\lVert f(z)\right\rVert_{X}
. Set
B(\Omega,p,U):=B_{1}(\Omega,p,U)
,
B_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,X):=B_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
whenever
U=I:X\rightarrow X
,
B(\Omega,p,X):=B_{1}(\Omega,p,X)
,
B_{\lambda}(\Omega,p):=B_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,\mathbb{C})
, and
B(\Omega,p):=B_{1}(\Omega,p)
. The constant
B(\Omega,1)
was first investigated by Aizenberg
[
2
]
, who derived both upper and lower bounds. Subsequent advances were made by Boas in
[
11
]
. A detailed discussion of these results is presented in Section
3
.

An application of the elementary inequality stating that the supremum of a sum does not exceed the sum of the suprema yields
B_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)\leq K_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
and
L_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)\leq R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
. Moreover, equality holds in both cases whenever
\Omega=\mathbb{D}^{n}
, the unit polydisc. In addition, it is well known that
B(\mathbb{D},1)=K(\mathbb{D},1)=1/3
, which is precisely the classical Bohr radius of the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
.

It is appropriate to note that the Bohr radius problem becomes unstable when
\dim(X)>1
. This is evident from the fact that
K(\mathbb{D},1,B_{\ell^{n}_{q}})=0
(see
[
8
]
). Furthermore, Blasco
[
9
]
showed that
K(\mathbb{D},p,X)=0
for
p\in[1,2)
whenever
\dim(X)\geq 2
, whereas
K(\mathbb{D},p,X)>0
if and only if
X
is
p
-uniformly
\mathbb{C}
-convex for
2\geq p<\infty
. These results also apply to
B(\mathbb{D},p,X)
, since
B_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D}^{n},p,U)=K_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D}^{n},p,U)
.
The positivity of the
constant
K_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D}^{n},p,U)
was investigated by Defant, Maestre, and Schwarting
[
24
]
under suitable conditions.

Consequently, a central question is whether the first and second Bohr radii are non zero for a given complete Reinhardt domain
\Omega
. In a recent paper, the present author
[
30
]
has answered this question for the first Bohr radii constants
K_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
and
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
corresponding to holomorphic and pluharmonic functions, respectively.

With the first Bohr radius problem now settled, it is natural to turn to the second Bohr radii and investigate under what conditions the constants
B_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
and
L_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
are non zero. This leads to the following general question.

Question 1.1
.

For a given complete Reinhardt domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
, can the second Bohr radii constants
B_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
and
L_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
be studied in detail? If so, under what conditions are these constants nonzero, and what can be said about their asymptotic behavior?

The main contribution of this paper is to answer this question affirmatively using the framework of local Banach space theory. To the best of our knowledge, these questions have not been previously explored.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we find the condition and show that second Bohr radii constants
B_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
and
L_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
are strictly positive. Section
3
is devoted to the asymptotic study of these constants in the case where
X
or
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is finite dimensional.

2.
Characterization of non zero second Bohr radii constants

A family of holomorphic functions defined on a complete Reinhardt domain
\Omega
is said to satisfy the first Bohr phenomenon if there exists a constant
r=r_{0}\in(0,1]
such that inequality (
1.1
) is valid for every function in the class. The largest such constant is referred to as the first Bohr radius associated with that class. An analogous notion can be formulated for pluriharmonic mappings on bounded simply connected domains: in this case, the first Bohr phenomenon is said to hold whenever (
1.3
) is satisfied for a universal radius
r=r_{0}\in(0,1]
. Replacing (
1.1
) and (
1.3
) with (
1.5
) and (
1.4
), respectively, leads to the formulation of a second Bohr phenomenon in the holomorphic and pluriharmonic frameworks.

It is well known that the Bohr phenomenon does not hold universally across all function classes; numerous counterexamples and partial results illustrate this limitation (see
[
3
,
4
,
7
,
9
,
24
,
30
]
). This naturally leads to the problem of identifying those classes of functions for which a Bohr phenomenon is valid. A complete characterization of this problem in the context of the first Bohr phenomenon was obtained by the author in
[
30
]
. The present work extends this line of investigation to the second Bohr phenomenon, providing conditions that guarantee its validity and, in doing so, resolving first part of Question
1.1
.

We begin with the following lemma comparing second Bohr radius constants for pairs of domains, starting with the pluriharmonic case.

Before stating our result, we introduce the following standard notation. For bounded Reinhardt domains
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
, let
S(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}):=\inf\left\{s>0:\Omega_{1}\subset s\,\Omega_{2}\right\}
. A Banach space
Z
satisfying
\ell_{1}\subset Z\subset c_{0}
(with normal inclusions) is called a Banach sequence space if the canonical unit vectors
{e_{k}}
form a
1
-unconditional basis of
Z
(see Section
3
for the definition of unconditional basis). Recall that if
Z
and
W
are Banach sequence spaces then
S(B_{Z},B_{W})=\left\lVert Id:Z\rightarrow W\right\rVert
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
be two bounded simply connected complete Reinhardt domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Then we have

\frac{L_{\lambda}(\Omega_{2},p,U)}{S(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})S(\Omega_{2},\Omega_{1})}\,\leq L_{\lambda}(\Omega_{1},p,U)\leq S(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})S(\Omega_{2},\Omega_{1})\,L_{\lambda}(\Omega_{2},p,U).

We now turn our attention to the nonvanishing part of Question
1.1
. By deriving an explicit positive lower bound, we prove that
L_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)>0
for every nonzero bounded linear operator
U:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow Y
satisfying
\left\lVert U\right\rVert<\lambda
with
\lambda>1
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
U:X\rightarrow Y
be a non-null bounded linear operator such that
\left\lVert U\right\rVert<\lambda
. Then, for
\lambda>1
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

L_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)\geq D.\frac{1}{n^{\frac{1}{p}}}\,\frac{1}{S(\Omega,\mathbb{D}^{n})S(\mathbb{D}^{n},\Omega)},

where

D=\begin{cases}\max\left\{\frac{1}{4\lambda\,2^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{2\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,,\frac{1}{4\lambda\,2^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\frac{1}{\left\lVert U\right\rVert}\right\}\,&\text{for $\left\lVert U\right\rVert\geq\frac{1}{4\lambda\,2^{\frac{1}{p}}}$},\\[8.53581pt]
\max\left\{\frac{1}{4\lambda\,2^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{2\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\frac{1}{4\lambda\,2^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right\}&\text{for $0<\left\lVert U\right\rVert<\frac{1}{4\lambda\,2^{\frac{1}{p}}}$}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We begin by observing that
L_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D}^{n},p,U)=R_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D}^{n},p,U)
. It follows from
[
30
, Theorem 1.1]
that for every
p\geq 1
,

L_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D}^{n},p,U)=R_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D}^{n},p,U)\geq D.\,\frac{1}{\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}^{n}}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{p}},

where
D
as in the statement of Theorem
2.1
. An application of Hölder’s inequality yields
\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}^{n}}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{p}=n^{1/p}
for any
1\leq p<\infty
. Substituting this into the above inequality, we obtain

L_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D}^{n},p,U)\geq D.\,n^{-\frac{1}{p}}.

We now invoke Lemma
2.1
. Choosing
\Omega_{1}=\Omega
and
\Omega_{2}=\mathbb{D}^{n}
in that lemma, and combining it with the preceding estimate, we conclude that

L_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)\geq\frac{L_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D}^{n},p,U)}{S(\Omega,\mathbb{D}^{n})S(\mathbb{D}^{n},\Omega)}\geq D.\,n^{-\frac{1}{p}}\,\frac{1}{S(\Omega,\mathbb{D}^{n})S(\mathbb{D}^{n},\Omega)}.

The proof is therefore complete.
∎

Applying this theorem to the case
\Omega=B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
, the Minkowski space, yields the following result.

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
Y,U
and
\lambda
be as in Theorem
2.1
. Then, for
1\leq p<\infty
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
and
\lambda>1
,

L_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p,U)\geq D\,\frac{1}{n^{\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}}},

where the constant
D
is the same as in Theorem
2.1
.

On the other hand, we obtain the following lower bound of the second
\lambda
-powered Bohr radius for operator-valued pluriharmonic functions whenever
U
is the identity operator on
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
1<\lambda
. Then for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq p<\infty
, we have

L_{\lambda}(\Omega,p)\geq\frac{1}{2^{2+\frac{1}{p}}}\frac{\left(\lambda^{p}-1\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\lambda^{2}}\,\frac{1}{n^{\frac{1}{p}}}\,\frac{1}{S(\Omega,\mathbb{D}^{n})S(\mathbb{D}^{n},\Omega)}.

Remark 2.1
.

A natural question is whether Theorem
2.1
continues to hold when
\left\lVert U\right\rVert=\lambda
. The example below demonstrates that, in this case, the constant
L_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
need not be strictly positive. Consequently, the assumption
\left\lVert U\right\rVert<\lambda
cannot be relaxed.

Example 2.1
.

For a bounded simply connected complete Reinhardt domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, define

F_{k}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
by

F_{k}(z)=(i\,\cos\frac{1}{k})I_{\mathcal{H}}+(\frac{1}{2}\sin\frac{1}{k})I_{\mathcal{H}}z_{1}+(\frac{1}{2}\sin\frac{1}{k})I_{\mathcal{H}}\overline{z_{1}}

for
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\Omega
, where
I_{\mathcal{H}}
is the identity on
\mathcal{H}
. Let
U=\lambda\,I
, with
I
the identity on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Then
F_{k}(0)=(i\,\cos\frac{1}{k})I_{\mathcal{H}}
. Clearly,
F_{k}\in\mathcal{PH}(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
and
\left\lVert F_{k}\right\rVert_{\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})}\leq 1
. It is shown in
[
30
]
that there does not exist any
r_{0}>0
such that (
1.3
) holds for all
f\in\mathcal{PH}(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
for all
z\in r_{0}\,\Omega
. That is to say,
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
becomes zero. Since
L_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)\leq R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
, it immediately follows that
L_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)=0
. Therefore, we see that there is no non zero
r_{0}
such that the inequality (
1.4
) holds for the functions
F_{k}
. This fact, in turn, implies that condition
\left\lVert U\right\rVert<\lambda
in Theorem
2.1
is necessary.

We now turn to the second Bohr phenomenon for holomorphic functions. It is convenient to first clarify several conceptual differences between the study of Bohr’s theorem for vector valued holomorphic functions and that for pluriharmonic functions. In the pluriharmonic framework, attention is restricted to simply connected complete Reinhardt domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. This assumption is imposed by the fact that the series representation (
1.2
) is valid only on such domains. In addition, we restrict our attention to pluriharmonic mappings taking values in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, and the associated operator
U
is accordingly taken to act on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. This choice is motivated by the fact that complex conjugation is well defined in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, a property essential to our analysis.

In contrast, the vector valued holomorphic framework is more flexible. Complete Reinhardt domains suffice, without assuming simple connectedness, because multivariable power series converge exactly on such domains and yield holomorphic functions on their domain of convergence. Moreover, since complex conjugation plays no role in this setting, there is no need to restrict attention to
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
-valued functions. Consequently, holomorphic functions with values in arbitrary complex Banach spaces may be considered.

We therefore study the Bohr radius problem for holomorphic functions within the following framework: bounded holomorphic mappings with values in an arbitrary complex Banach space
X
, defined on complete Reinhardt domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Following similar arguments to that given in the proof of Lemma
2.1
, one can show the following result, which compares the second Bohr radius constants of holomorphic functions for two domains.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
be two bounded complete Reinhardt domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Then we have

\frac{B_{\lambda}(\Omega_{2},p,U)}{S(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})S(\Omega_{2},\Omega_{1})}\,\leq B_{\lambda}(\Omega_{1},p,U)\leq S(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})S(\Omega_{2},\Omega_{1})\,B_{\lambda}(\Omega_{2},p,U).

This lemma can be viewed as a vector valued analogue of
[
20
, Lemma 3.1]
, where the authors focused on only scalar valued holomorphic functions.

In the following theorem, we establish that the constant
B_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
is nonzero, which serves as the holomorphic analogue of Theorem
2.1
. However, the proof is similar to that of Theorem
2.1
and hence, omitted.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be any complex Banach spaces and
U:X\rightarrow Y
be a non-null bounded linear operator such that
\left\lVert U\right\rVert<\lambda
. Then, for
\lambda>1
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

B_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)\geq C.\,\frac{1}{n^{\frac{1}{p}}}\,\frac{1}{S(\Omega,\mathbb{D}^{n})S(\mathbb{D}^{n},\Omega)},

where

C=\begin{cases}\max\left\{\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{2\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,,\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\frac{1}{\left\lVert U\right\rVert}\right\}\,&\text{for $\left\lVert U\right\rVert\geq 1$},\\[8.53581pt]
\max\left\{\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{2\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right\}&\text{for $0<\left\lVert U\right\rVert<1$}.\end{cases}

An immediate application of this theorem gives the following result for
\Omega=B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
, the Minkowski space.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
X,Y,U
and
\lambda
be as in Theorem
2.2
. Then, for
1\leq q\leq\infty
and
\lambda>1
,

B_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p,U)\geq C\,\frac{1}{n^{\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}}},

where the constant
C
is the same as in Theorem
2.2
.

It is worth noting that
[
25
, Proposition 3.3]
follows as a direct consequence of this theorem by choosing
p=1
. Furthermore, as an immediate corollary to the above Theorem, we obtain the following lower bound for the second
\lambda
-powered Bohr radius of
X
valued holomorphic functions in the case where
U
is the identity operator on
X
.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
1<\lambda
. Then for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq p<\infty
, we have

B_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,X)\geq\frac{\left(\lambda^{p}-1\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\lambda}\,\frac{1}{n^{\frac{1}{p}}}\,\frac{1}{S(\Omega,\mathbb{D}^{n})S(\mathbb{D}^{n},\Omega)}.

In particular, when
\Omega=B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
, the Minkowski space, an application of this corollary yields the following result.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
1<\lambda
and
1\leq p<\infty
. Then for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
, we have

B_{\lambda}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},p,X)\geq\frac{\left(\lambda^{p}-1\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\lambda}\,\frac{1}{n^{\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}}}.

By setting
p=1
in this corollary, we recover
[
25
, Corollary 3.4]
, which we record below for the sake of completeness.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
1<\lambda
and
1\leq p<\infty
. Then for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
, we have

K_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D}^{n},1,X)=B_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D}^{n},1,X)\geq\frac{\left(\lambda-1\right)}{\lambda}\,\frac{1}{n}.

3.
Asymptotic estimates of the second Bohr radii constants in finite dimensional settings

We now turn to the second part of Question
1.1
, which is the central focus of this paper, namely obtaining asymptotic estimates for the second Bohr radii constants
L_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
and
B_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
. It is already known that second Bohr radii constants are dominated by their first Bohr radii counterparts, in the sense that
L_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)\leq R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
and
B_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)\leq K_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
. Consequently, any upper bounds established for
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
and
K_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
immediately yield corresponding bounds for
L_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
and
B_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
. For this reason, it is natural to begin with a brief overview of recent developments on first Bohr radius constants.

The asymptotic behavior of the first
\lambda
-Bohr radius
K_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D}^{n},1,U)
for holomorphic functions is well understood and was determined in
[
25
]
. Nevertheless, despite considerable progress, the precise value of the constant
K_{n}(\Omega)
remains poorly understood. In particular, its exact value is unknown for dimensions
n>1
, even in the polydisc
\mathbb{D}^{n}
, where
\mathbb{D}^{n}:=\{z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:|z_{j}|<1\,\mbox{for all}\,1\leq j\leq n\}
. It is known from
[
10
]
that
K_{n}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\leq 1/3
, and that the sequence
\{K_{n}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is decreasing and converges to zero as
n\rightarrow\infty
. The authors of this paper also have established the following estimate for
n\geq 2
,

\frac{1}{3\sqrt{n}}\leq K_{n}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\leq 2\sqrt{\frac{\log\,n}{n}}.

In particular, when
n=2

this yields the bounds
0.23570226\leq K_{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})\leq 1/3=0.33333
. After nearly three decades, these estimates have been improved in recent years. Specifically, Knese showed in
2025
that
K_{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})>0.3006
, while Baran, Pikul, Woerdeman, and Wojtylak proved in
2026
that
K_{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})<0.3177
. Taken together, these results confirm in particular that
K_{n}(\mathbb{D}^{n})<1/3
for
n\geq 2
.

Since the exact value of
K_{n}(\Omega)
is unknown, attention naturally turns to its asymptotic behavior on general domains, especially on key domains such as the unit ball of Minkowski space and finite-dimensional complex Banach spaces. Beginning with the work of Dineen and Timoney
[
29
]
, and subsequently developed by Boas and Khavinson
[
10
,
11
]
, with further extensions by Aizenberg
[
2
]
and by Defant and Frerick
[
23
]
, it has been shown that for every
1\leq q\leq\infty
and all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist constants
C,D>0
such that

\frac{1}{C}\,\left(\frac{\log\,n}{n}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{\min\{q,2\}}}\leq K_{n}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}})\leq D\,\left(\frac{\log\,n}{n}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{\min\{q,2\}}}.

The one-dimensional constant
K_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D},1)
was first studied by Bombieri
[
13
]
, who determined its exact value for
\lambda\in[1,\sqrt{2}]
. Its precise asymptotic behavior as
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
was later analyzed by Bombieri and Bourgain
[
14
]
. Building on these developments, Defant
et al.
have introduced the more general constant
K_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D}^{n},1,U)
in
[
25
]
and established its asymptotic estimates in both finite- and infinite-dimensional Banach spaces
X
.

On a related note, the constants
K(\mathbb{D},p)
and
K(\mathbb{D}^{n},p)
were first studied by Djakov and Ramanujan
[
28
]
, and later analogues of these results in Hardy spaces were developed by Bénéteau, Dahlner, and Khavinson
[
7
]
. More recently, it has been shown in
[
30
]
that the constants
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
and
K_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
have the same asymptotic order up to multiplicative constants.

On the other hand, the precise values of the second Bohr radius constants in higher dimensions are still unknown. Clearly,
K_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D},p,U)=B_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D},p,U)
, and hence, by
[
14
]
, one has

B_{\lambda}(\mathbb{D},1)=\frac{1}{3\lambda-2\,\sqrt{2\,(\lambda^{2}-1)}}\,\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,\,1\leq\lambda\leq\sqrt{2}.

Although several notable advances have been made, the multidimensional second Bohr radius problem remains substantially more complicated than its one-dimensional counterpart. In this direction, the first progress was achieved by Aizenberg
[
2
]
, who showed that for any bounded complete Reinhardt domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
, with
n\geq 2
,

\displaystyle B(\Omega,1)\geq 1-\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}

and moreover that
B(B_{\ell^{n}_{1}},1)<0.446663/n
. Furthermore, due to Boas
[
11
]
, it is known that for
1\leq q\leq\infty
and
n>1
,

\frac{1}{3}\,\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\max\{q,2\}}}\leq B(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},1)\leq 4\,\left(\frac{\log\,n}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\max\{q,2\}}}.

In contrast, the present article introduces the second Bohr radius constant
L_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
for pluriharmonic functions, a notion that, to the best of our knowledge, has not been previously investigated. Consequently, the exact value of this constant is not yet known. A natural and fundamental question, therefore, concerns the asymptotic behavior of these constants. Addressing this problem, however, turns out to be highly nontrivial and poses significant challenges.

A main contribution of this paper is the determination of the asymptotic behavior of the second
\lambda
-Bohr radius for vector valued holomorphic and pluriharmonic functions on any complete Reinhardt domains using tools from local Banach space theory.

We now collect several facts that will be instrumental in what follows. Let
Z=(\mathbb{C}^{n},||.||)
be an
n
-dimensional complex Banach space for which the canonical basis vectors
e_{k}
form a normalized
1
-unconditional basis. Equivalently, the open unit ball
B_{Z}
of
Z
is a complete Reinhardt domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. There is a one-to-one correspondence between bounded convex complete Reinhardt domains
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
and the open unit balls of norms on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
for which the canonical basis vectors
e_{k}
form a normalized
1
-unconditional basis. Indeed, given such a domain
\Omega
, its Minkowski functional
p_{\Omega}:\mathbb{C}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
defined by

p_{\Omega}(z):=\inf\left\{t>0:z/t\in\Omega\right\}

is a norm on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and
\Omega
coincides with the open unit ball of the Banach space
(\mathbb{C}^{n},p_{\Omega})
. Conversely, if
Z=(\mathbb{C}^{n},||.||)
is a Banach space whose canonical basis is normalized
1
-unconditional, then its open unit ball
B_{Z}
is necessarily a bounded convex complete Reinhardt domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

As a consequence, investigating the constants
B_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
and
L_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
for unit balls
\Omega=B_{Z}
of finite dimensional complex Banach spaces
(\mathbb{C}^{n},||.||)
with the normalized
1
-unconditional canonical bases is equivalent to studying these constants over bounded complete Reinhardt domains
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

We now proceed to state our first result in this section, which is the asymptotic behavior of the constant
L_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
when
\Omega=B_{Z}
and
U
is the identity operator on
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Recall that a Schauder basis
\{w_{k}\}
of a Banach space
W
is said to be
1
-unconditional if
\chi(\{w_{k}\})=1
(see more details in subsection
3.1
). We usually denote the canonical basis vectors of
Z=(\mathbb{C}^{n},||.||)
by
e_{k}
,
k=1,\ldots,n
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
Z=(\mathbb{C}^{n},||.||)
be a Banach space such that
\chi(\{e_{k}\}^{n}_{k=1})=1
. Let
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a finite dimensional complex Banach space and
\lambda>1
. Then for
1\leq q\leq\infty
,

L_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)\geq\begin{cases}E_{1}(X)\,\frac{1}{s\,t\,n^{\frac{1}{q}}}\max\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}\,\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert},\,\frac{1}{e\,\left\lVert Id:Z\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{1}\right\rVert}\right\}\,\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-1}{2\lambda^{p}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}&\text{for $p=1$},\\[5.69054pt]
E_{3}\,\frac{1}{s}\,\left\lVert I:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert^{-\frac{2}{p}}\,\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-1}{2\lambda^{p}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,&\text{for $p\geq 2$},\\[5.69054pt]
\frac{E_{2}(X)}{s\left(tn^{\frac{1}{q}}\right)^{\frac{(2-p)}{p}}}\max\left\{\frac{1}{(\sqrt{n})^{1-\theta}\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert},\frac{\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert^{-\theta}}{e^{(1-\theta)}\left\lVert Id:Z\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{1}\right\rVert^{1-\theta}}\right\}\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-1}{2\lambda^{p}-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\hskip-8.53581pt&\text{for $1<p<2$},\end{cases}

where
\theta=(2(p-1))/p
,
s:=\left\lVert Id:Z\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{q}\right\rVert
, and
t:=\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{q}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert
. Here,
E_{1}(X)
,
E_{2}(X)
, and
E_{3}
are positive constants:
E_{1}(X)
depends only on
X
,
E_{2}(X)
depends on both
X
and
p
, while
E_{3}
is independent of
X
and depends only on
p
. Furthermore,

L_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)\leq d\,\lambda^{\frac{1}{\log\,n}}\,b_{[\log\,n]}(B_{Z})\,\,\frac{\left\lVert Id:Z\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{2}\right\rVert}{n^{\frac{1}{p}}}

for some constant
d>0
. Here
b_{m}(\Omega):=\left(\inf_{|\alpha|=m}\sup_{z\in\Omega}|z^{\alpha}|\right)^{-\frac{1}{m}}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
for a complete Reinhardt domain
\Omega
.

The correspondence preceding Theorem
3.1
allows Theorem
3.1
to be extended to bounded simply connected convex complete Reinhardt domains. Moreover, combining Theorem
3.1
and Lemma
2.1
yields the asymptotic estimates for all bounded simply connected complete Reinhardt domains.

3.1.
Basic definitions and notations

Throughout the paper we employ standard notation and terminology from Banach space theory. All Banach spaces
W
are complex. Their topological duals are denoted by
W^{*}
, and their open unit balls by
B_{W}
. A Banach space
W
is said to have cotype
t\in[0,\infty]
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that for every finite family of vectors
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}\in W
,

\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\lVert w_{k}\right\rVert^{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{t}}\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{1}\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{n}r_{k}(s)w_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\,ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
\{r_{k}\}
denotes the sequence of Rademacher functions on
[0,1]
. We define

Cot(W):=\inf\{2\leq t\leq\infty:W\,\mbox{has cotype}\,t\}.

It is well known that every Banach space has cotype
\infty
. We adopt the convention
1/Cot(W)=0
whenever
Cot(W)=\infty
.

A Schauder basis
\{w_{k}\}
of a Banach space
W
is called unconditional if there exists a constant
c\geq 1
such that

\left\lVert\sum_{j=1}^{k}\epsilon_{j}\mu_{j}w_{j}\right\rVert\leq c\left\lVert\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{j}w_{j}\right\rVert

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, all scalars
\mu_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
, and all
\epsilon_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\epsilon_{j}|\leq 1
. The smallest such constant is denoted by
\chi(\{w_{k}\})
and is called unconditional basis constant of
\{w_{k}\}
. The basis is said to be
1
-unconditional if
\chi(\{w_{k}\})=1
. Moreover, the unconditional basis constant of a Banach space
W
is defined by
\chi(W):=\inf\chi(\{w_{k}\})\in[1,\infty]
, where the infimum is taken over all unconditional bases
\{w_{k}\}
of
W
.

A Banach space
Z
satisfying
\ell_{1}\subset Z\subset c_{0}
with normal inclusions is called a Banach sequence space if its canonical basis vectors
\{e_{k}\}
forms a
1
-unconditional basis. A Banach lattice
Z
is said to be
2
-convex if there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\left\lVert\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}|x_{k}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert\leq C\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\lVert x_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

for all finite families
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in Z
.

Let
Z=(\mathbb{C}^{n},||.||)
be a Banach space and
Y
any Banach space. Fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
. We denote by
\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z,Y)
the space of all
m
-homogeneous polynomials
Q:Z\rightarrow Y
of the form
Q(z)=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}c_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
endowed with the norm
\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z,Y)}:=\sup_{z\in B_{Z}}|Q(z)|
. Furthermore, if
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a bounded complete Reinhardt domain, then denote by
\mathcal{P}(^{m}\Omega,Y)
the space of all
m
-homogeneous
Y
valued pluriharmonic polynomials
Q
considered as defined on
\Omega
. The unconditional basis constant of the monomial basis
\{z^{\alpha}:|\alpha|=m\}
in
\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z,Y)
is denoted by
\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z,Y))
.
Denote by
\mathcal{PH}(^{m}Z,\mathcal{B}(H))
the space of all
m
-homogeneous operator valued pluriharmonic polynomials
P:Z\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(H)
of the form

(3.1)

P(z)=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}a_{\alpha}\,z^{\alpha}+\sum_{|\alpha|=m}b^{*}_{\alpha}\,\bar{z}^{\alpha}

and set
\left\lVert P\right\rVert_{\mathcal{PH}(^{m}Z,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))}:=\sup_{z\in B_{Z}}||P(z)||_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})}
. Moreover, if
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a bounded simply connected complete Reinhardt domain, then denote by
\mathcal{PH}(^{m}\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
the space of all
m
-homogeneous operator valued pluriharmonic polynomials
P
considered as defined on
\Omega
of the form (
3.1
).

A bounded linear operator
U:W\rightarrow Y
between Banach spaces
W
and
Y
is called
(r,t)
-summing, with
r,t\in[1,\infty)
, if there exists a constant
C>0
such that for every choice of finite family
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}\in W
, one has

\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\lVert U(w_{k})\right\rVert^{r}\right)^{1/r}\leq C\,\sup_{\phi\in B_{X^{*}}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}|\phi(w_{k})|^{t}\right)^{1/t}.

The smallest constant
C
satisfying this inequality is denoted by
\pi_{r,t}(U)
. In the special case
r=t
, the operator
U
is called
r
- summing, and we write
\pi_{r}(U):=\pi_{r,r}(U)
.

3.2.
Second Bohr radii for homogeneous plynomials

In the study of first Bohr radii, a standard approach for obtaining nontrivial estimates is to begin with the study of
m
-homogeneous polynomials. The following definition introduces the
m
-homogeneous analogue of second Bohr radii for holomorphic and pluriharmonic functions, which will serve as a foundation for our subsequent developments. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a simply connected complete Reinhardt domain and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
U:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow Y
be a bounded liner operator and
\left\lVert U\right\rVert\leq\lambda
. For
1\leq p<\infty
, the second
\lambda
-powered Bohr radius of
U
with respect to
\mathcal{PH}(^{m}\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
, denoted by
L^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
, is defined to be the supremum of all
r\geq 0
such that, for every homogeneous pluriharmonic polynomials
P:\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
of the form (
3.1
), the following inequality

(3.2)

\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\sup_{z\in r\Omega}\,(\left\lVert U(a_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{p}_{Y}+\left\lVert U(b_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{p}_{Y})|z^{\alpha}|^{p}\leq\lambda^{p}\,\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})}

holds. Here,
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})}:=\sup_{z\in\Omega}\,\left\lVert f(z)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})}
. For short, write
L^{m}(\Omega,p,U):=L^{m}_{1}(\Omega,p,U)
,
L^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,X):=L^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
whenever
U=I:X\rightarrow X
,
L^{m}(\Omega,p,X):=L^{m}_{1}(\Omega,p,X)
,
L^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p):=L^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,\mathbb{C})
, and
L^{m}(\Omega,p):=L^{m}_{1}(\Omega,p)
. It is straightforward to verify that
L^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)=\lambda^{1/m}\,L^{m}(\Omega,p,U)
.

In a similar manner, one can define the constant
B^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
for the class
\mathcal{P}(^{m}\Omega,W)

i.e.
, for any Banach space
W
valued
m
-homogeneous holomorphic polynomial
Q(z)=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}c_{\alpha}\,z^{\alpha}
. Clearly,
B^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)=\lambda^{1/m}\,B^{m}(\Omega,p,U)
. On the other hand, the first Bohr radii constants for holomorhic and pluriharmonic functions, denoted respectively by
B^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
and
L^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
, have already been considered in
[
30
]
. It is clear that,
B^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)\leq K^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
and
L^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)\leq R^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
, with equality holding whenever
\Omega=\mathbb{D}^{n}
. Morever, it is worth noting that
B_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)\leq B^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
,
K_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)\leq K^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
,
R_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)\leq R^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
, and
L_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)\leq L^{m}_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,U)
. Finally, in light of the Bohnenblust–Hille inequality, it is known that the constant
K^{m}(\mathbb{D}^{n},1)=B^{m}(\mathbb{D}^{n},1)
is hypercontractive; see
[
24
]
.

The following result due to
[
30
]
, is a coefficient-type Schwarz–Pick lemma for pluriharmonic functions defined on general complete Reinhardt domains. This lemma constitutes a fundamental ingredient in the proofs of several main results of this article.

Lemma 3.1
.

[
30
]

Let
f\in\mathcal{PH}(Z,X)
be of the form (
1.2
). Then for all
|\alpha|=m\geq 1
, we have
\left\lVert\sum_{|\alpha|=m}(a_{\alpha}\pm b_{\alpha})\,z^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}\leq 4\,\left\lVert\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}\,I-{\operatorname{Re}\,}(a_{0})\right\rVert
. Moreover, if
B_{Z}=B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
then
\left\lVert a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}\right\rVert\leq\frac{4}{\pi}\,\rho_{\alpha}\,\left\lVert\sum_{|\alpha|=m}(a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha})\,z^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}
,
\left\lVert a_{\alpha}-b_{\alpha}\right\rVert\leq\frac{4}{\pi}\,\rho_{\alpha}\,\left\lVert\sum_{|\alpha|=m}(a_{\alpha}-b_{\alpha})\,z^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}
, where
\rho_{\alpha}:=\left(\frac{|\alpha|^{|\alpha|}}{\alpha^{\alpha}}\right)^{1/q}
.

The following result provides a fundamental connection between the Bohr radius for pluriharmonic functions and the Bohr radius for homogeneous pluriharmonic functions. It plays a crucial role in the proofs of our main results. This lemma may be viewed as the pluriharmonic analogue of
[
25
, Lemma 3.2]
, where the authors focused only on domain
\mathbb{D}^{n}
. The proof follows essentially the same lines as that of
[
25
, Lemma 3.2]
, and hence the details are omitted.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
Y
any complex Banach space, and
U:X\rightarrow Y
be a non-null bounded linear operator such that
\left\lVert U\right\rVert<\lambda
. Then, for all
p\in[1,\infty)
,
\lambda>1
, and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(1)

\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{2\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\inf_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\,L^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)\leq L_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)\leq\inf_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\,L^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)

(2)

\left(\frac{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}}{\lambda^{p}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert^{p}+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,\inf_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\,L^{m}(B_{Z},p,U)\leq L_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)\leq\lambda\,\inf_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\,L^{m}(B_{Z},p,U)
.

Remark 3.1
.

Analogous results of Lemma
3.2
hold for
B^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)
and
B_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,U)
.

To simplify computations in the proofs of the remaining theorems, we may assume without loss of generality that every pluriharmonic function
f\in\mathcal{PH}(B_{Z},X)
satisfies
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}\leq 1
, where
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. That is to say, we restrict our attention to the class

\mathcal{BPH}(B_{Z},X):=\{f:f\in\mathcal{PH}(B_{Z},X)\,\mbox{ with}\,\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}\leq 1\}.

This is justified by a simple normalization argument. Indeed, if
f\in\mathcal{PH}(B_{Z},X)
satisfies
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}>1
and admits the representation (
1.2
), then the function
F(z):=f(z)/\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}

belongs to
\mathcal{PH}(B_{Z},X)
and satisfies
\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}=1
. In this normalization the coefficients
a_{\alpha}
and
b_{\alpha}
in (
1.2
) are replaced by
a_{\alpha}/\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}
and
b_{\alpha}/\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}
, respectively. By the same reasoning, we also consider the class of normalized
m
-homogeneous polynomials,

\mathcal{BPH}(^{m}B_{Z},X):=\{P:P\in\mathcal{PH}(^{m}B_{Z},X)\,\,\mbox{with}\,\,\left\lVert P\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}\leq 1\}.

3.3.
Proof of lower bounds of Theorem
3.1

We want to first obtain the desired lower bound of
R^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)
when
X
is finite dimensional. For all
m\geq 1
, let
P(z)=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}a_{\alpha}\,z^{\alpha}+\sum_{|\beta|=m}b^{*}_{\alpha}\,\bar{z}^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{BPH}(^{m}B_{Z},X)
. Then for
r\in[0,1)
,

(3.3)

\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\sup_{z\in r\,B_{Z}}(\left\lVert a_{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}+\left\lVert b_{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p})|z|^{p\alpha}\leq r^{pm}\,\left\lVert Id:Z\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{q}\right\rVert^{pm}\,\sum_{|\alpha|=m}(\left\lVert a_{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}+\left\lVert b_{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p})\,.

We now need to estimate the term
\sum_{|\alpha|=m}(\left\lVert a_{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}+\left\lVert b_{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p})
. To do so, we consider several cases depending on the values of
p
. More precisely, the proof is presented in three separate cases, depending on the values of
p
:
p=1
,
p\geq 2
, and
1<p<2
. Let
H
and
G
be the functions as defined in the proof of Theorem
2.1
. Set
s:=\left\lVert Id:Z\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{q}\right\rVert
and
t:=\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{q}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert
.

Case
p=1
:

For each
\phi\in B_{X^{*}}
, we now consider the holomorphic polynomial
\tilde{H}:=\phi\circ H:\mathbb{C}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
defined by

\tilde{H}(z)=\phi(H(z))=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\phi\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)z^{\alpha}
.
Clearly,
|\tilde{H}(z)|\leq\left\lVert H(z)\right\rVert
for each
z
. Since
X
is finite dimensional, the identity operator
I_{X}
is
1
-summing. Then

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right\rVert\right)\left(\frac{1}{t\,n^{\frac{1}{q}}}\right)^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right\rVert\left(\frac{1}{t\,n^{\frac{1}{q}}}\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\pi_{1}(I_{X})\sup_{\phi\in B_{X^{*}}}\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left|\phi\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right|\right)\,\left(\frac{1}{t\,n^{\frac{1}{q}}}\right)^{m}

\displaystyle=\pi_{1}(I_{X})\sup_{\phi\in B_{X^{*}}}\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left|\phi\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right|\,\left(\frac{1}{t\,n^{\frac{1}{q}}}\right)^{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\pi_{1}(I_{X})\sup_{\phi\in B_{X^{*}}}\sup_{z\in B_{Z}}\left|\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left|\phi\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right|z^{\alpha}\right|

\displaystyle=\pi_{1}(I_{X})\sup_{\phi\in B_{X^{*}}}\sup_{z\in B_{Z}}\left|\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\varepsilon_{\alpha}\phi\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)z^{\alpha}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\pi_{1}(I_{X})\,\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z))\,\sup_{\phi\in B_{X^{*}}}\sup_{z\in B_{Z}}\left|\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\phi\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)z^{\alpha}\right|

\displaystyle=\pi_{1}(I_{X})\,\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z))\,\sup_{\phi\in B_{X^{*}}}\sup_{z\in B_{Z}}\left|\phi\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)z^{\alpha}\right)\right|

(3.4)

\displaystyle=\pi_{1}(I_{X})\,\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z))\,\left\lVert H\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X},

where
\varepsilon_{\alpha}=\frac{\left|\phi\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right|}{\phi\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)}
in the last fourth inequality. The last third inequality follows from the definition of
\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z))
. From (
3.3
), we have

(3.5)

\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right\rVert\leq\pi_{1}(I_{X})\,\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z))\,\left(t\,n^{\frac{1}{q}}\right)^{m}\,\left\lVert H\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}.

By considering the holomorphic polynomial
\tilde{G}:=\phi\circ G:\mathbb{C}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, proceeding as above we obtain

(3.6)

\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}-b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right\rVert\leq\pi_{1}(I_{X})\,\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z))\,\left(t\,n^{\frac{1}{q}}\right)^{m}\,\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}.

By making use of (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), and then from Lemma
3.1
, a simple computation shows that

\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert a_{\alpha}\right\rVert\leq\pi_{1}(I_{X})\,\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z))\,\left(t\,n^{\frac{1}{q}}\right)^{m}\,\left(\left\lVert H\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}+\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}\right)\leq 4\,\pi_{1}(I_{X})\,\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z))\,\left(t\,n^{\frac{1}{q}}\right)^{m}.

Similarly, we get

\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert b_{\alpha}\right\rVert\leq 4\,\pi_{1}(I_{X})\,\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z))\,\left(t\,n^{\frac{1}{q}}\right)^{m}.

In view of (
3.3
) and last two inequalities, we obtain

\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\sup_{z\in r\,B_{Z}}(\left\lVert a_{\alpha}\right\rVert+\left\lVert b_{\alpha}\right\rVert)|z|^{\alpha}\leq 8\,r^{m}\,\pi_{1}(I_{X})\,\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z))\,\left(s\,t\,n^{\frac{1}{q}}\right)^{m}\leq\lambda

provided

r\leq\frac{\lambda^{\frac{1}{m}}}{8^{\frac{1}{m}}\,(\pi_{1}(I_{X}))^{\frac{1}{m}}\,(\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z)))^{\frac{1}{m}}\,s\,t\,n^{\frac{1}{q}}}.

This yields

(3.7)

L^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)\geq E_{1}(X)\,\frac{\lambda^{\frac{1}{m}}}{(\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z)))^{\frac{1}{m}}\,s\,t\,n^{\frac{1}{q}}},

where
E_{1}(X)
is a constant depending on
X
. Thanks to the the following estimates due to
[
19
, (4.5), (4.6), pp. 187]
:

(3.8)

\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z))\leq n^{\frac{m}{2}}\,\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert^{m}\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z))\leq\frac{m^{m}}{m!}\,\left\lVert Id:Z\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{1}\right\rVert^{m},

from (
3.7
), we deduce that

L^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)\geq E_{1}(X)\,\,\frac{\lambda^{\frac{1}{m}}}{s\,t\,n^{\frac{1}{q}}}\,\max\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}\,\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert},\,\frac{1}{\left(\frac{m^{m}}{m!}\right)^{1/m}\,\left\lVert Id:Z\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{1}\right\rVert}\right\},

and hence the desired lower bound of
L_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)
follows from Lemma
3.2
(1) and using the fact
\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}(m^{m}/m!)^{1/m}=e
. This completes the proof for
p=1
.

Case
p\geq 2
:
Let
p\geq 2
. By Lemma
3.1
and keeping in mind
\left\lVert P\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}\leq 1
, one sees easily that

\left\lVert\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert H\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}\leq 2\,\left\lVert P\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}\leq 2

and so,

\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right\rVert^{p}\leq 2^{p-2}\,\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right\rVert^{2}.

Then, this inequality, together with the fact
\left\lVert(a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha})/2\right\rVert=\sup_{\phi\in B_{X^{*}}}|\phi((a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha})/2)|
and the following estimate due to
[
19
, p. 187]

\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left|\phi\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert^{m}\left\lVert H\right\rVert_{B_{Z},X}

yields

(3.9)

\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right\rVert^{p}\leq 2^{p-2}\,\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert^{2m}\left\lVert H\right\rVert^{2}_{B_{Z},X}.

Similarly, we have

(3.10)

\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}-b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right\rVert^{p}\leq 2^{p-2}\,\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert^{2m}\left\lVert G\right\rVert^{2}_{B_{Z},X}.

Now, by using Lemma
3.1
again, (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) taken together yield

\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert a_{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq 2\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert^{\frac{2m}{p}}2^{\frac{2}{p}}.

Moreover, one can easily see that this inequality remains valid if the left hand quantity replaced by
\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert b_{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\right)^{1/p}
.
We now use (
3.3
) and the last two inequalities to prove

\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\sup_{z\in r\,B_{Z}}(\left\lVert a_{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}+\left\lVert b_{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p})|z|^{p\alpha}\leq 2^{p+3}\,r^{pm}\,s^{pm}\,\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert^{2m}\leq\lambda^{p}

provided

r\leq\frac{\lambda^{\frac{1}{m}}}{2^{\frac{p+3}{pm}}\,s\,\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert^{\frac{2}{p}}}.

Consequently,

L^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)\geq\frac{\lambda^{\frac{1}{m}}}{2^{\frac{p+3}{pm}}\,s\,\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert^{\frac{2}{p}}}.

Finally, the desired lower bound of
R_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)
follows from Lemma
3.2
(1).

Case
1<p<2
:
For any
1<p<2
, we shall make use of the estimates obtained in the above two cases. We now use Hölder’s inequality to get

\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right\rVert^{p}\leq\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right\rVert\right)^{2-p}\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right\rVert^{2}\right)^{p-1}.

By virtue of (
3.5
), (
3.9
), and Lemma
3.1
, we obtain

\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right\rVert^{p}\leq 2^{p}\,(\pi_{1}(I_{X}))^{2-p}\,\left(t\,n^{\frac{1}{q}}\right)^{(2-p)m}(\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z)))^{2-p}\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert^{2(p-1)m}.

Similarly, we have

\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert\left(\frac{a_{\alpha}-b_{\alpha}}{2}\right)\right\rVert^{p}\leq 2^{p}\,(\pi_{1}(I_{X}))^{2-p}\,\left(t\,n^{\frac{1}{q}}\right)^{(2-p)m}(\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z)))^{2-p}\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert^{2(p-1)m}.

Now, use these two inequalities to prove

\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert a_{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq 4\,(\pi_{1}(I_{X}))^{\frac{(2-p)}{p}}\,\left(t\,n^{\frac{1}{q}}\right)^{\frac{(2-p)m}{p}}(\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z,Y)))^{\frac{(2-p)}{p}}\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert^{\frac{2(p-1)m}{p}}.

This inequality remains valid if the left-hand side is replaced by
\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left\lVert b_{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}\right)^{1/p}
. These inequalities and (
3.3
) taken together yield

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\sup_{z\in r\,B_{Z}}(\left\lVert a_{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}+\left\lVert b_{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p})|z|^{p\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2p+1}(\pi_{1}(I_{X}))^{2-p}\,r^{pm}\,s^{pm}\,\left(t\,n^{\frac{1}{q}}\right)^{(2-p)m}(\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z,Y)))^{2-p}\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert^{2(p-1)m},

and so,

L^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)\geq E_{2}(X)\,\,\frac{\lambda^{\frac{1}{m}}}{s\,\left(t\,n^{\frac{1}{q}}\right)^{\frac{(2-p)}{p}}(\chi_{M}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}Z)))^{\frac{(2-p)}{pm}}\left\lVert I:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert^{\frac{2(p-1)}{p}}},

where
E_{2}(X)
is a constant depending on
X
and
p
. Then, letting
\theta=(2(p-1))/p
, the estimates in (
3.8
) show

L^{m}_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)\geq\frac{E_{2}(X)\,\lambda^{\frac{1}{m}}}{s\,\left(t\,n^{\frac{1}{q}}\right)^{\frac{(2-p)}{p}}}\max\left\{\frac{1}{(\sqrt{n})^{1-\theta}\,\left\lVert Id:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert},\,\frac{\left\lVert I:\ell^{n}_{2}\rightarrow Z\right\rVert^{-\theta}}{\left(\frac{m^{m}}{m!}\right)^{(1-\theta)/m}\,\left\lVert Id:Z\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{1}\right\rVert^{1-\theta}}\right\},

and hence the desired lower bound of
L_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)
follows from Lemma
3.2
(1) and using the fact
\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}(m^{m}/m!)^{1/m}=e
.

3.4.
Proof of upper bounds of Theorem
3.1

We now proceed to derive the desired upper bound for
L_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)
. More generally, we establish this bound for any simply connected complete Reinhardt domain
\Omega
. Our approach is inspired by some of the arguments used in
[
20
, Theorem 3.3]
, which were developed for complex valued holomorphic functions; here, however, we work in the setting of operator valued pluriharmonic functions.
For
\varepsilon_{\alpha}\in\{-1,1\}
and
I
the identity operator on
\mathcal{H}
, we consider the
m
-homogeneous polynomial
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
defined as

f(z)=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\varepsilon_{\alpha}\,\frac{m!}{\alpha!}z^{\alpha}\,I+\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\varepsilon_{\alpha}\,\frac{m!}{\alpha!}\,\bar{z}^{\alpha}I,\,\,z\in\Omega.

Then, by definition of the second Bohr radius for every
0<\epsilon<1
, we have

\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\sup_{z\in(1-\epsilon)L\,\Omega}\left(\left(\frac{m!}{\alpha!}\right)^{p}+\left(\frac{m!}{\alpha!}\right)^{p}\right)\,|z|^{p\alpha}=2\,\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\left(\frac{m!}{\alpha!}\right)^{p}\,\sup_{z\in(1-\epsilon)L\,\Omega}|z|^{p\alpha}\leq\lambda^{p}\,\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{\Omega,X},

where
L:=L_{\lambda}(\Omega,p,X)
for short. Now, write
b_{m}(\Omega):=\left(\inf_{|\alpha|=m}\sup_{z\in\Omega}|z^{\alpha}|\right)^{-\frac{1}{m}}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and use the facts
m!/\alpha!\geq 1
and
\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\frac{m!}{\alpha!}=n^{m}
to prove

2\,n^{m}\,L^{pm}\,(b_{m}(\Omega))^{-pm}\leq\lambda^{p}\,\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{\Omega,X}.

Consequently,

(3.11)

L\leq\frac{\lambda^{\frac{1}{m}}\,b_{m}(\Omega)\,\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{\frac{1}{m}}_{\Omega,X}}{2^{\frac{1}{pm}}\,n^{\frac{1}{p}}}.

In order to obtain an upper bound of
L
, it is enough to estimate the quantity
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\Omega,X}
. Notice that
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\Omega,X}\leq 2\,\left\lVert\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\varepsilon_{\alpha}\,\frac{m!}{\alpha!}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{\Omega,\mathbb{C}}
. We shall now make use of the following estimate due to
[
20
, Theorem 2.3]
(see also
[
22
, p. 21, equ. 21]
,
[
19
, Theorem 3.1]
):

\left\lVert\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\varepsilon_{\alpha}\,\frac{m!}{\alpha!}\,z^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{\Omega,\mathbb{C}}\leq\sqrt{m^{3}\,2^{3m-1}\,\log\,n}\,\sup_{|\alpha|=m}\left\{\sqrt{\frac{m!}{\alpha!}}\right\}\left(\sup_{z\in\Omega}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|z_{i}|\right)\left(\sup_{z\in\Omega}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|z_{i}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right)^{m-1}.

Recalling the fact
S(\Omega,B_{\ell^{n}_{q}})=\sup_{z\in\Omega}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{q}
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
, the above estimate and (
3.11
) taken together yield

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{1}{m}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\,\lambda^{\frac{1}{m}}\,b_{m}(\Omega)\,\left(m^{3}\,m!\,2^{3m-1}\,\log\,n\right)^{\frac{1}{2m}}\,(S(\Omega,B_{\ell^{n}_{1}}))^{\frac{1}{m}}\,\frac{(S(\Omega,B_{\ell^{n}_{2}}))^{\frac{m-1}{m}}}{n^{\frac{1}{p}}}

\displaystyle=2^{\frac{1}{m}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\,\lambda^{\frac{1}{m}}\,b_{m}(\Omega)\,\left(m^{3}\,m!\,2^{3m-1}\,\log\,n\right)^{\frac{1}{2m}}\,\left(\frac{S(\Omega,B_{\ell^{n}_{1}})}{S(\Omega,B_{\ell^{n}_{2}})}\right)^{\frac{1}{m}}\,\frac{S(\Omega,B_{\ell^{n}_{2}})}{n^{\frac{1}{p}}}

(3.12)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{1}{m}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\,\lambda^{\frac{1}{m}}\,b_{m}(\Omega)\,\left(m^{3}\,m!\,2^{3m-1}\,n\,\log\,n\right)^{\frac{1}{2m}}\,\frac{S(\Omega,B_{\ell^{n}_{2}})}{n^{\frac{1}{p}}},

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
\frac{S(\Omega,B_{\ell^{n}_{1}})}{S(\Omega,B_{\ell^{n}_{2}})}\leq\sqrt{n}
. Indeed, it is well-known that
\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{1}\leq\sqrt{n}\,\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{2}
for
z\in\Omega
, which is again less than or equals to
\sqrt{n}\,S(\Omega,B_{\ell^{n}_{2}})
. So,
\sup_{z\in\Omega}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{1}\leq\sqrt{n}\,S(\Omega,B_{\ell^{n}_{2}})
.

For the desired upper bound of
L
, it is now enough to estimate the right side quantity of (
3.4
). To do so, we first estimate the term

A_{m,n}:=\left(m^{3}\,m!\,2^{3m-1}\,n\,\log\,n\right)^{\frac{1}{2m}}
. Stirling’s formula asserts that

m!\sim\sqrt{2\,\pi m}\left(\frac{m}{e}\right)^{m}.

Substituting this into
A_{m,n}
, a straightforward calculation gives

\displaystyle A_{m,n}\leq C\left(\left(\frac{m}{e}\right)^{m}\,2^{3m}\right)^{\frac{1}{2m}}\,m^{\frac{7}{4m}}\,(n\,\log\,n)^{\frac{1}{2m}},

where
C>0
is a constant, independent both of
m
and
n
. Since
(m)^{\frac{7}{4m}}\rightarrow 1
as
m\rightarrow\infty
, there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
such that the last inequality simplifies to

\displaystyle A_{m,n}\leq C_{1}\,\sqrt{m}\,\,(n\,\log\,n)^{\frac{1}{2m}}.

Now, choose
m=[\log\,n]
for
n\geq 3
. Then

(n\,\log\,n)^{\frac{1}{2m}}=\exp\left(\frac{\log\,n+\log\log\,n}{2m}\right).

Since
m\sim\log\,n
,

\frac{\log\,n}{2m}\leq\frac{1}{2}+o(1)\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\frac{\log\log\,n}{2\,m}\rightarrow 0\,\,\mbox{as}\,\,n\rightarrow\infty.

Hence

(n\,\log\,n)^{\frac{1}{2m}}\leq C_{2},

for some constant
C_{2}>0
, which does not depend on neither
n
nor
m
. Finally,

A_{m,n}\leq C_{3}\sqrt{\log\,n},

with
C_{3}>0
being another constant and so,

L\leq C_{4}\,\lambda^{\frac{1}{\log\,n}}\,b_{[\log\,n]}(\Omega)\,\,\frac{S(\Omega,B_{\ell^{n}_{2}})}{n^{\frac{1}{p}}}

for some constant
C_{4}>0
, independent both of
m
and
n
. This constant may depend on
p
. In view of the identity
S(B_{Z},B_{\ell^{n}_{2}})=\left\lVert Id:Z\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{2}\right\rVert
, the desire upper bound of
L_{\lambda}(B_{Z},p,X)
is immediate. This completes the proof.

In light of Theorem
3.1
, we deduce the following asymptotic estimates for Banach sequence spaces, which have wide applications.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
Z
be a Banach sequence space,
X=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be finite dimensional, and
\lambda>1
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
Z_{n}
be the linear span of
\{e_{k},\,k=1,\ldots,n\}
. Then for
1\leq q\leq\infty

(1)

if
Z
is a subset of
\ell_{2}
we have

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

We start by recalling two important facts from the theory of finite-dimensional Banach spaces with unconditional bases. Let
W=(\mathbb{C}^{n},||.||)
be a Banach space whose canonical unit vectors
e_{k}
form a normalized
1
-unconditional basis. Then
\left\lVert Id:W\rightarrow\ell_{2}^{n}\right\rVert=\sup_{z\in B_{W}}(\sum_{m=1}^{n}|z_{m}|^{2})^{1/2}
. Moreover,
\left\lVert Id:W\rightarrow\ell_{1}^{n}\right\rVert=\sup_{z\in B_{W}}\sum_{m=1}^{n}|z_{m}|=\left\lVert\sum_{m=1}^{n}e^{*}_{m}\right\rVert_{W^{*}}
for all
z\in W
.

Since
Z\subset\ell_{2}
, we may normalize the inclusion map
Z\subset\ell_{2}
to have norm one. Under this assumption, assertion
(1)
follows immediately from Theorem
3.1
, in combination with the above expressions for the norms of the identity embeddings.

We now turn to the proof of part
(2)
. By
[
32
, Theorem 1.d.5]
, we may assume without loss of generality that
M^{(2)}(X)=1
, which in turn implies
\left\lVert Id:\ell_{2}^{n}\rightarrow Z_{n}\right\rVert=1
. We next recall an important estimate from
[
19
, (5.3), p. 191]
: if
W=(\mathbb{C}^{n},||.||)
is a symmetric Banach space satisfying
M^{(2)}(W)=1
, then

(3.13)

\left\lVert Id:W\rightarrow\ell^{n}_{2}\right\rVert=\frac{\left\lVert\sum_{m=1}^{n}e^{*}_{m}\right\rVert_{W^{*}}}{\sqrt{n}}.

The desired conclusion now follows by combining Theorem
3.1
, the above identities for identity embeddings, and inequality (
3.13
).The proof is therefore complete. ∎

coincide up to a scalar multiple. We therefore omit the proof, as it follows along the same lines.

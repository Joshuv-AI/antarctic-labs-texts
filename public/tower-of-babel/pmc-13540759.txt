Subcutaneous gluteal migration of hardware after spine fusion: A case report
Authors: Santiago Jose Troncoso, Emanuel Zaragoza, Eduardo Galaretto, Juan Pablo Guyot, Santiago Suarez Crespo, Juan Pablo Taleb, Enrique Miguens, Lloyd Ruy, Nicolas Ross
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background: Rod fracture is a well-recognized late mechanical complication following long-segment posterior instrumentation in adult spinal deformity. However, rod fracture with distant migration is rare. Here, a 52-year-old female presented 15 years following a T10-sacrum fusion with a subcutaneous gluteal mass representing migration of a fractured rod fragment.Case Description: A 52-year-old female had a posterior instrumented fusion from T10-sacrum performed 15 years earlier for thoracolumbar scoliosis. She presented with progressive pain and a palpable mass in the left gluteal region (firm subcutaneous prominence with focal cutaneous ischemic necrosis). X-rays demonstrated a 10-cm detached segment of the left longitudinal rod with caudolateral migration into the gluteal tissue; the prior fusion was intact. The patient fully recovered following fragment removal under local anesthesia.Conclusion: Late rod fracture with distal migration should be considered in the differential diagnosis of soft-tissue masses in patients with prior long-segment spinal instrumentation. Prompt surgical resection should be performed to avoid skin perforation/infection.

INTRODUCTION

Rod fracture represents the most frequent mode of structural failure in thoracolumbar constructs. The frequency of rod breakage ranges from 6.8% to 18.4%, with nonunion and persistent sagittal malalignment identified as major contributing factors.[3] However, migration of a detached rod fragment is distinctly uncommon. Here, a 52-year-old female presented 15 years after a T10-sacrum fusion for scoliosis with a subcutaneous gluteal mass that proved to be a fractured rod fragment.

CASE REPORT

A 52-year-old female presented with progressive pain in the left gluteal region and a newly palpable mass. Fifteen years ago (2005), she underwent a T10 to sacrum fusion for thoracolumbar scoliosis. She reported worsening pain while sitting and standing. Inspection demonstrated a localized subcutaneous prominence in the superolateral quadrant of the left gluteal region [Figure 1]. Palpation revealed a firm, metallic consistency. The overlying skin exhibited dark discoloration consistent with ischemic pressure necrosis, but without surrounding erythema, local hyperthermia, purulent drainage, or systemic signs of infection.

Subcutaneous prominence in left gluteal region.

The AP [Figure 2] and lateral [Figure 2] X-rays of the thoracolumbar spine [Figure 3] and pelvis [Figure 4] showed a fracture of the left rod with migration into the gluteal space; the rest of the construct appeared stable. Under local anesthesia, the detached rod fragment was identified immediately beneath the subcutaneous fat, encapsulated within a sterile, thick fibrous reactive bursa, and removed without incident.

DISCUSSION

Hardware migration is a well-recognized phenomenon across orthopedics.[1] [Table 1] Over time, particularly in the presence of micro-motion from an incompletely fused or biomechanically stressed segment, stress risers drastically lower the fatigue limit of the alloy, predisposing the rod to late cyclic fatigue failure.[2] Once structural continuity is lost, migration is likely driven by repetitive paraspinal and gluteal muscle contractions and displacement along planes of least fascial resistance.[4] The focal ischemic necrosis observed represents a critical preextrusion stage. Early removal under sterile conditions prevented extrusion of this rod fragment that would have become infected.[5]

CONCLUSION

Surgeons should be suspicious of implant migration attributed to potential long-term rod fractures occurring in patients undergoing long thoraco-lumbar fusion constructs for scoliosis.

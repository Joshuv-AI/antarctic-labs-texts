21-deoxycortisol as a second-tier test in congenital adrenal hyperplasia newborn screening in The Netherlands: two-year evaluation
Authors: Anouk Olthof, Marelle J Bouva, Hedi L Claahsen-van der Grinten, Dineke Westra, Eugènie Dekkers, Annemieke C Heijboer, Sabine E Hannema, Anita Boelen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractABSTRACTObjectiveCongenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH) due to 21-hydroxylase deficiency is an inherited adrenal steroid synthesis disorder included in Dutch newborn screening (NBS) since 2002. Screening involves measuring 17-hydroxyprogesterone (17-OHP) in dried blood spots (DBS) with gestational age-adjusted cut-offs. Since October 2021, a second-tier 21-deoxycortisol (21-DF) test has replaced the 17-OHP measurement in a second heel prick after inconclusive results. This study evaluates the performance of the second-tier test from 1 October 2021 to 30 September 2023.Design17-OHP was measured by immunoassay in regional NBS laboratories. DBS with inconclusive and positive 17-OHP results was sent to Amsterdam UMC for 21-DF measurement by liquid chromatography tandem mass spectrometry. Genetic analysis of CYP21A2, CYP11B1, POR and HSD3B2 was performed on DBS from newborns with false-positive second-tier results.ResultsOver 2 years, 21-DF was measured in DBS of 147 newborns (=0.04%). Twenty newborns were directly referred to a paediatric endocrinologist based on positive 17-OHP results: 15 had a positive 21-DF and were diagnosed with classical CAH (genetically confirmed), while five were first-tier false-positives. Of 127 newborns with inconclusive 17-OHP results, three had a positive 21-DF and were referred but not diagnosed with CAH: second-tier false-positives. In total, 8/23 referred newborns were false-positives. Genetic analysis of six false-positive second-tier DBS showed pathogenic CYP21A2 variants in five.ConclusionsThe modified protocol improved CAH screening by preventing 127 heel pricks in 2 years and reducing unnecessary referrals (currently 2.4%, previously 7.7%). 5/6 false-positive second-tier tests were most likely due to non-classical CAH. Further optimisation of cut-offs may prevent these false-positives.Keywords: endocrinology, health services research, paediatrics, qualitative research

Introduction

Congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH) is a rare, inherited disorder characterised by enzymatic deficiencies in adrenal steroid synthesis. In the Netherlands, its incidence is estimated at 1:10 000–1:20 000 newborns.1 About 95% of CAH cases result from pathogenic CYP21A2 variants, resulting in 21-hydroxylase deficiency (21-OHD)2 with a carrier frequency of approximately 1.8% (1:55 individuals).3

CAH is classified in three forms. Classical CAH includes the salt-wasting form, which can present with life-threatening crises due to cortisol and aldosterone deficiency, and the simple virilising form, characterised by cortisol deficiency.4 5 Both subtypes show accumulation of steroid precursors, such as 17-hydroxyprogesterone (17-OHP), which are converted to androgens and contribute to the clinical phenotype.6 Non-classical (NC) CAH is milder, with 30%–50% residual 21-hydroxylase (21-OH) activity, allowing sufficient cortisol and aldosterone synthesis and thus no neonatal phenotype.1 7 Symptoms from mildly elevated androgen concentrations—such as accelerated growth, premature pubarche, hirsutism, acne and irregular menses with reduced fertility—may appear later in childhood, adolescence or adulthood.1 7 Classical CAH due to 21-OHD has been included in the Dutch newborn screening (NBS) programme since 2002, as early diagnosis is essential to prevent early death.8 Until October 2021, CAH screening9 10 involved first-tier 17-OHP measurement with gestational age (GA)-adjusted cut-offs followed by a second heel prick (HP) performed after 7 days if results were inconclusive11 (see table 1 for cut-off values). While a second HP reduces false-positives, it can burden parents.12

Stroek et al13 previously reported a pilot study comparing the second HP with a second-tier 21-deoxycortisol (21-DF) measurement. Based on these findings, since October 2021, the second HP was replaced with a second-tier 21-DF test in the first dried blood spot (DBS) after an inconclusive 17-OHP result in the Dutch NBS protocol.14 21-DF, produced from 17-OHP by 11-beta-hydroxylase, accumulates in CAH due to adrenal excess 17-OHP from 21-OHD and is a highly specific CAH marker 21-DF, produced from 17-OHP by 11-beta-hydroxylase, accumulates in CAH due to adrenal excess of 17-OHP from 21-OHD and is a highly specific marker for CAH.15

This study aims to evaluate the performance of the second-tier test between 1 October 2021 and 30 September 2023, investigate causes of false-positive second-tier results and assess opportunities to further improve CAH screening.

Methods

Data collection

The Dutch National Institute for Public Health and the Environment (RIVM) commissioned the Endocrine Laboratory of the Amsterdam UMC, location AMC (Amsterdam, the Netherlands), to evaluate the first 24 months of implementation of the second-tier analysis. Information on all 147 newborns who underwent second-tier testing was obtained pseudo-anonymously from the national NBS registry. In addition, we identified six newborns who had a positive 21-DF concentration, but a negative second HP based on 17-OHP during the pilot phase (1 January 2017 until 31 December 2019).13 These newborns were also considered as false-positive second-tier results and the infants from whom DBS were available for further analysis (n=3) were also included in the study.

Newborn screening procedure

The previous and current NBS procedure in the Netherlands has previously been described in detail by Stroek et al.13 Since October 2021, the 21-DF concentration is measured in the same DBS that has been used for the first-tier, to replace the second HP in case of an inconclusive 17-OHP result. A newborn with a positive 17-OHP result is directly referred to a paediatric endocrinologist while the second-tier is also measured in parallel, in the context of screening evaluation. The 21-DF measurement is performed in the Endocrine Laboratory of the Amsterdam UMC, location AMC, one of the five NBS laboratories, using liquid chromatography tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) (Waters, Massachusetts, USA). This LC-MS/MS method has previously been described.16 A 21-DF concentration of ≥2 nmol/L blood is considered as a positive second-tier result. If, after referral to a paediatric endocrinologist, classical CAH is diagnosed based on clinical consultation and biochemical profile, the paediatric endocrinologist determines and registers the type of classical CAH; all results are documented in the national NBS registry Neorah (www.neorah.nl). Diagnostic CYP21A2 mutation analysis is performed for confirmation and also reported in Neorah. In addition, false-positive and false-negative results of classical CAH are also documented in the Neorah database.

Study groups

Cases were divided into four groups: (1) positive 17-OHP, referred to a paediatric endocrinologist and confirmed classical CAH—true-positive; (2) positive 17-OHP, referred to a paediatric endocrinologist but without classical CAH—first-tier false-positive; (3) inconclusive 17-OHP, positive 21-DF concentration (≥2 nmol/L blood), referred to a paediatric endocrinologist but without classical CAH—second-tier false-positive and (4) inconclusive 17-OHP and a negative 21-DF concentration, no referral—classical CAH negative. We did not have any cases with an inconclusive 17-OHP, positive 21-DF concentration, referred to a paediatric endocrinologist and confirmed classical CAH—second-tier true-positive.

Genetic analyses

To investigate whether false-positive second-tier results are caused by mild forms of CAH, DNA was extracted from anonymised DBS from newborns with false-positive second-tier. The spots were washed with milliQ, after which TE buffer and proteinase K were added and lysis was done at 56°C for 2 hours. Automated DNA isolation was performed (Nimbus Presto robot, Hamilton, Manchester, Maine, USA) using the Mag-Bind Blood & Tissue DNA HDQ kit (Omega Biotek, Norcross, Georgia, USA).

The CYP21A2 gene was analysed with Sanger sequencing and multiplex ligation-dependent probe amplification (MLPA; P050-C1 kit, MRC Holland, Amsterdam, The Netherlands) as described by Baumgartner-Parzer et al.17 If no variants in CYP21A2 were identified, exome sequencing, downstream processing and custom-made annotation were done as described before18; sequencing was executed on a NovaSeq 6000 instrument (Illumina, San Diego, California, USA) after exome capture with the Twist Exome V.2.0 plus Comprehensive Exome Spike-in Kit (Twist Biosciences, South San Francisco, California, USA), followed by an in silico gene analysis with a bioinformatic filter for CYP11B1, POR and HSD3B2.

Data analysis and statistics

The true-positive and second-tier-positive rates of the first 2 years of the routine NBS algorithm since the implementation of the second-tier analysis were determined.

Differences in 17-OHP and 21-DF concentrations between groups were analysed using an unpaired t-test for data with a Gaussian distribution, or a Mann-Whitney U test for non-Gaussian data (MedCalc Software, Ostend, Belgium). Figures were made using GraphPad Prism (V.9.5.1, GraphPad Software, Boston, Massachusetts, USA).

Results

Evaluation of 21-DF measurement as a second-tier test

Over a period of 2 years, 21-DF concentrations were measured in DBS samples from 147 newborns (0.04% of a total of 339 790 newborns screened for CAH). Twenty of these 147 had a positive first-tier screening result and were directly referred to a paediatric endocrinologist. 127 newborns had an inconclusive first-tier result; three of this group had a positive second-tier test, that is, a 21-DF concentration ≥2 nmol/L, and were referred to a paediatric endocrinologist. The other 124 had a negative second-tier test. In total, during the first 2 years after implementation, 23 newborns were referred, and 15 of them were diagnosed with CAH (table 1 and figure 1: left diagram). Clinical phenotype was determined by the paediatric endocrinologist (table 2). In total, eight referrals, five based on the first-tier result and three due to the second-tier result, were classified as false-positives. The revised algorithm resulted in a positive predictive value (PPV) of 65% (15 true positives/(15 true positives+8 false-positives)=PPV of 65%). During the evaluation period, one false-negative screening result was found: a newborn (GA 36+1; birth weight 2308 g) who had already been diagnosed clinically with classical CAH and had started treatment with hydrocortisone prior to the NBS screening. This newborn had a 17-OHP concentration of 22 nmol/L, which is just below the GA-adjusted cut-off value of 25 nmol/L for a second-tier test. After the treating paediatric endocrinologist had reported this case, 21-DF was measured in the original DBS and found to be elevated (18 nmol/L). As CAH was not detected by the NBS screening, the screening result was reported as a false-negative (according to the definition false-negative in the Dutch NBS), even though this was explained by the fact that treatment of the baby had started before taking the HP.

Genetic analysis and first-tier results of the false-positive second-tier DBS

Although 21-DF is thought to be a highly specific marker of 21-OHD, three false-positive second-tier results were identified after implementation of the second-tier test in the NBS. During the pilot phase (2017–2019), we also found five newborns with a positive 21-DF concentration who had a negative second HP and were therefore not referred to a paediatric endocrinologist13; these second-tier results were also considered false-positive. DBS of three of these latter newborns was still available and these three were therefore also included in the analysis of false-positive results (total n=6) (figure 1: right diagram) (table 3). To determine the cause of the false-positive second-tier tests, we performed genetic analysis of CYP21A2 in the DBS of the six newborns, as we hypothesised that their false-positive results might be caused by a mild form of CAH. In five out of six DBS samples, two pathogenic variants in the CYP21A2 gene (NM_000500.9) were found that were previously reported to cause non-classic CAH, when present on two alleles, with at least one variant corresponding with 30%–50% 21-OH residual activity (c.844G>T p.(Val282Leu) or c.597A>T p.(Leu199Phe))19,21 (table 3). No parental samples were available to confirm that these variants were indeed present on two alleles (compound heterozygous). Four out of the five with a genotype consistent with non-classic CAH were term newborns, had a birth weight appropriate for GA and had a first-tier 17-OHP on or just above the cut-off of 25 nmol/L. One newborn did not have any pathogenic CYP21A2 variants and underwent further genetic analysis with a whole exome sequencing (WES)-based gene panel consisting of CYP11B1, HSD3B2 and POR to rule out forms of CAH other than 21-OHD (table 3). This did not show variants in any of these other genes associated with CAH either.

As presumed, non-classic CAH was found to cause most false-positive second-tier results. We assessed if it would be possible to diminish the chance of detecting potential non-classic CAH without decreasing sensitivity of the NBS. Increasing the cut-off for 21-DF in the second-tier would result in missing cases of classical CAH, as the lowest 21-DF concentration in cases with classical CAH overlapped with the concentrations found in non-classic CAH (figure 2B). Next, we assessed the possibility of altering the cut-off of the first-tier. To do this, we analysed the 17-OHP results of the classical CAH cases and the CAH-screening negative newborns in the cohort that underwent a second-tier test (figure 2C).

In term newborns with presumed non-classic CAH, the range of 17-OHP concentration was 25–37 nmol/L blood in the first-tier, and there was one presumed non-classic CAH preterm newborn with a 17-OHP concentration of >300 nmol/L (GA 26+6 weeks). For newborns diagnosed with classical CAH, first-tier 17-OHP concentrations were always ≥55 nmol/L.

Discussion

The aim of the present study was to evaluate the performance of 21-DF as a second-tier test for NBS for CAH after its implementation on 1 October 2021, and to assess opportunities to further improve CAH screening. In the 2-year period, 339 790 newborns were screened, of which 147 underwent second-tier testing. Two categories were distinguished: newborns with a positive 17-OHP result in the first-tier (n=20) who were directly referred to a paediatric endocrinologist, while 21-DF was measured in the DBS in parallel to the referral, and newborns with an inconclusive 17-OHP result (n=127) in whom 21-DF was measured in the same DBS within a week to determine whether referral was indicated.

Fifteen out of 20 directly referred newborns had an elevated 21-DF concentration and were confirmed to have classical CAH, while five newborns did not have detectable 21-DF concentrations and were first-tier false-positives. Three newborns with an inconclusive 17-OHP result had an elevated 21-DF and were therefore referred to a paediatric endocrinologist. After clinical consultation and biochemical profile, no classical CAH was diagnosed, and all three newborns were considered second-tier false-positives. Surprisingly, we did not find second-tier true-positive results (newborns with an inconclusive 17-OHP, positive 21-DF concentration, referral and confirmed classical CAH) in this period. The other 124 were negative for 21-DF and considered true-negatives. Thus, the second-tier analysis prevented 127 s HPs.

Although the Dutch CAH screening algorithm uses GA-adjusted cut-off values, a substantial number of preterm newborns (approximately 30%) have an inconclusive 17-OHP result, likely due to neonatal stress and delayed maturation of 11beta-hydroxylase.15 22 The elimination of a second HP in these premature infants is particularly valuable, as it minimises blood withdrawals and reduces the need for erythrocyte transfusions in extremely premature neonates.23 In addition, avoiding second HPs reduces parental distress and unnecessary healthcare utilisation in the future due to worry caused by the second HP, even when it turns out to be a negative screening result.12

The revised algorithm resulted in a PPV of 65%, which is an improvement from the PPV of 53% from the previous algorithm.13 Further optimisation may still be possible. The five false-positive referrals based on an elevated first-tier result could have been prevented by measuring 21-DF before referral. However, waiting for the 21-DF concentration would delay referral by 5–11 days, which is undesirable in true positive cases due to the potentially life-threatening phenotype. The cost-effectiveness of the revised algorithm has also recently been demonstrated.24

Genetic analysis of six newborns with a false-positive 21-DF result (three from 2021 to 2023 and three from the pilot period (2017–201913)) showed pathogenic variants in CYP21A2 in five newborns that, if present on two alleles, are consistent with non-classic CAH. This indicates that 21-DF is a highly specific marker for CAH, even for milder forms,25 and that potential cases of non-classic CAH are detected in the NBS using the current algorithm, although this is not the target disease. The number of false-positive results based on 21-DF (2.4%) is lower than that observed with the second HP in the previous algorithm (7.7%), contributing to the improved PPV.13 In one newborn, no genetic variants associated with CAH were identified and the positive second-tier result remained unexplained. This neonate was born after a pregnancy of 25+1 weeks with a very high 17-OHP concentration (>200 nmol/L) and a 21-DF of 3 nmol/L, just above the cut-off.16 Whether extreme prematurity may play a role in elevated 21-DF is unclear, although a previous study found undetectable 21-DF in preterm neonates born at <33 weeks of gestation.16

The Dutch NBS programme aims to detect only classical CAH; non-classic CAH is not a target disease as it is not life-threatening, and acute treatment is not and may never be required. Our findings show that most false-positive second-tier DBS results had 17-OHP concentrations on or just above the cut-off value of 25 nmol/L (figure 2C), while patients with classical CAH had higher 17-OHP concentrations. This suggests the potential for increasing the 17-OHP cut-off value for second-tier testing to lower the number of false-positive referrals based on the second-tier, thereby avoiding the detection of potential non-classic CAH cases in the Dutch NBS programme, and further improving the PPV of the screening protocol, which is aimed solely at detecting patients with classical CAH.

In contrast, given the specificity of 21-DF, it may be prudent to inform parents of newborns with a false-positive second-tier 21-DF result that there is a high probability their newborn has mild non-classic CAH, as this variant may be incidentally detected through the second-tier 21-DF measurement. This could have potential future benefits in the future, such as more rapid recognition of children showing symptoms requiring treatment and future family planning for family members at risk for carriership.

During the evaluation period, according to the Dutch NBS classifications, one false-negative case was reported. This newborn had already been diagnosed with CAH (based on family history) and treated before the DBS was taken, resulting in low 17-OHP concentrations. At the time of the HP, the baby was treated with hydrocortisone, lowering 17-OHP,26 which was just below the cut-off value. After the child was reported as missed by the NBS, 21-DF was measured and found to be elevated, indicating that 21-DF is a more sensitive marker in this case.27 28

To assess further improvement opportunities, we also categorised 21-DF per (presumed) CAH phenotype. Although 21-DF is highly specific, no distinction between classic and non-classic CAH can be made based on 21-DF concentration, as values overlap. However, 17-OHP could possibly discriminate between these two, as there is no overlap between 17-OHP concentrations in classic CAH and non-classic CAH (figure 2A). This observation provides opportunities for future research into adapting the 17-OHP cut-off value of the first-tier at which second-tier testing is initiated.

In conclusion, the modified protocol using 21-DF as a second-tier test improved CAH screening by preventing 127 s HPs in 2 years, reducing false-positive referrals and improving the PPV. Genetic analysis of DBS from false-positive second-tier cases showed CYP21A2 variations associated with the NC phenotype in five out of six cases, indicating that non-classic CAH likely explains most false-positive second-tier results in the current study. To further improve the CAH NBS, it might be of interest to increase the cut-off value of the first-tier 17-OHP measurement at which second-tier testing is initiated.

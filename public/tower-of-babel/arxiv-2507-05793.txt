Potentials in recurrent networks: a survey
Asaf Nachmias, Yuval Peres

Abstract
. A nonnegative function on the vertices of an infinite graph G G which vanishes at a distinguished vertex o o , has Laplacian 1 1 at o o , and is harmonic at all other vertices is called a potential. We survey basic properties of potentials in recurrent networks. In particular, we show that potentials are Lipschitz with respect to the effective resistance metric, and if the potential is unique, then there is a determinantal formula for the harmonic measures from infinity. We also infer from the von Neumann minimax theorem that there always exists a potential tending to infinity.



1.
Introduction

In a transient network, the importance of the Green function is largely due to its Laplacian being a Dirac measure. In recurrent networks, where there is no Green function, this role is played by potentials. Let
G=(V,E)
be an infinite, connected, locally finite graph endowed with positive edge weights
\big\{c_{xy}\}_{xy\in E}
. Define the network
Laplacian
of a function
f:V\to\mathbb{R}
by

\Delta f(v)=\sum_{x\sim v}c_{vx}\big(f(x)-f(v)\big)\,.

Fix a vertex
o\in V
. A
potential
on the
rooted network

(G,c,o)
is a function
h:V\to[0,\infty)
that is harmonic off
o
(i.e.,
\Delta h(v)=0
for every vertex
v\neq o
), normalized so that
h(o)=0
and
\Delta h(o)=1
.
In every such rooted network there is at least one potential, see
2.2
. In
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
the unique potential is a classical object, see, e.g.,
[
13
, Chapter 12]
and
[
9
, Chapter 4.4]
.
Recently there has been renewed interest (see Berestycki and van Engelenburg
[
2
]
and van Engelenburg and Hutchcroft
[
5
]
) in recurrent potential theory, motivated by close connections to other natural notions:

•

Random walks conditioned to tend to infinity;

•

Harmonic measures from infinity;

•

Uniform spanning trees (USTs).

In this survey, we study these connections and other basic properties of potentials in recurrent networks. Given a network
(G,c)
, the corresponding
network random walk
defined by
p(x,y)=c_{xy}/c_{x}
is reversible, with reversing measure
c_{x}=\sum_{z\sim x}c_{xz}
. Any potential
h
defines a Markov chain on the state space
S_{h}=\{v:h(v)>0\}
via the Doob
h
-transform that has transition probabilities

p^{h}(x,y)={p(x,y)h(y)\over h(x)}\,.

The corresponding Markov chain
\{Y_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
, called the
h
-process, is reversible, with reversing measure
c_{x}h(x)^{2}
and edge conductances
\big\{c_{xy}h(x)h(y)\big\}_{xy\in E}
.
The
h
-process is transient, see
2.7
. The main results we present pertaining to a recurrent rooted network
(G,c,o)
are (references are given in the relevant sections):

(a)

Every potential is Lipschitz with respect to the effective resistance metric (
2.13
).

(b)

There is a unique potential if and only if harmonic measures from infinity exist; in this case there is a determinantal formula for the harmonic measures (
3.2
).

(c)

If
\inf_{e}c_{e}>0
, then for every potential
h
there exists an exhaustion
\{D_{n}\}
of
G
, such that the network random walks conditioned to exit
D_{n}
before reaching
o
, converge weakly to the
h
-process (
2.11
).

(d)

There always exists a potential tending to infinity (
4.1
).

(e)

Let
{\sf g}_{o}(x,y)
denote the Green density for the random walk killed at
o
, see (
1
) and fix a potential
h
. Then
{\sf g}_{o}(\cdot,Y_{n})
converges along the
h
-process
\{Y_{n}\}
to a random potential with expectation
h
(
5.1
).

(f)

Suppose that
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
are different extremal potentials. Then the trajectories of the
h_{1}
-process and the
h_{2}
-process intersect finitely often a.s. (
5.2
).

We also clarify the connection between the UST in a recurrent network and the corresponding potentials.
It was shown in
[
1
, Theorem 14.2]
that if the UST in
(G,c)
has one end a.s., then the network admits harmonic measures from infinity, hence by (b), for each root
o
there exists a unique potential
h
.
In
Lemma

6.1
, we prove that under the same assumption on the UST, two independent trajectories of the
h
-process have infinite intersection almost surely.

In
Section

6
we show that both of these implications cannot be reversed. In particular, for all
d\geq 5
, the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
rooted at
o
and equipped with suitable conductances has a unique potential
h
, yet with probability
1
, two independent trajectories of the
h
-process have finite intersection and the UST has multiple ends; see
6.3
. This construction can be adapted to answer negatively a question raised in
[
2
, Section 7]
, whether a unique potential implies infinite intersection for two independent
h
-process trajectories; see
6.5
.

2.
Basic properties of dipoles and potentials

The
Green kernel

{\sf G}_{o}(x,y)
of the random walk
\{X_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
killed at
o
is defined by

{\sf G}_{o}(x,y):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{n}=y,\,\,\tau_{o}>n)\,,

where
\tau_{o}=\min\{n\geq 0:X_{n}=o\}
. The corresponding
Green density
,

(1)

{\sf g}_{o}(x,y)=\frac{{\sf G}_{o}(x,y)}{c_{y}}\,,

is symmetric by reversibility. A
dipole
from
o
to another vertex
y
in a network
(G,c)
is a function
f:V\to[0,\infty)
, for which
f(o)=0
and

\forall v\in V\qquad\Delta f(v)={\bf 1}_{o}(v)-{\bf 1}_{y}(v)\,.

Claim 2.1
.

In a recurrent network, the Green density
g_{o}(\cdot,y)
for the killed random walk is the unique dipole from
o
to
y
. Moreover,
\sup_{y}g_{o}(x,y)<\infty
for each
x\in V
.

Proof.

First observe that
g_{o}(\cdot,y)
is harmonic off
\{o,y\}
and satisfies
{\sf g}_{o}(o,y)=0
. Second,
\Delta{\sf g}_{o}(\cdot,y)(y)=-1
, since

(2)

\sum_{x\sim y}c_{xy}({\sf g}_{o}(y,y)-{\sf g}_{o}(x,y))={\sf G}_{o}(y,y)-\sum_{x\sim y}{c_{xy}\over c_{y}}{\sf G}_{o}(x,y)=1\,.

Also,

(3)

\Delta{\sf g}_{o}(\cdot,y)(o)=\sum_{x\sim o}c_{ox}g_{o}(x,y)=\sum_{x\sim o}{\sf G}_{o}(y,x)\frac{c_{ox}}{c_{x}}=1\,,

where the last equality is obtained by summing, over
n\geq 0
, the identity

\sum_{x\sim o}\mathbb{P}_{y}(X_{n}=x,\,\,\tau_{o}>n)\frac{c_{ox}}{c_{x}}=\mathbb{P}_{y}(X_{n+1}=o,\,\,\tau_{o}>n)=\mathbb{P}_{y}(\tau_{o}=n+1)\,

and using recurrence. Thus,
g_{o}(\cdot,y)
is a dipole.

Next, we verify uniqueness. For any dipole
f
from
o
to
y
, the difference

f-g_{o}(\cdot,y)
is a harmonic function on
V
that vanishes at
o
and is bounded below by
-{\sf g}_{o}(y,y)
; therefore, it vanishes on
V
.

Lastly, the identity (
3
) implies that
{\sf g}_{o}(x,y)\leq c_{ox}^{-1}
for every
x\sim o
. More generally, since
{\sf g}_{o}(\cdot,y)
is superharmonic off
o
, for any
x\neq o
we have
{\sf g}_{o}(x,y)\leq\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}c_{x_{i-1}x_{i}}^{-1}
, where
x_{0},\dots,x_{\ell}
is a simple path from
o
to
x
.
∎

Claim 2.2
.

The space of potentials on a recurrent rooted
(G,c,o)
is a nonempty, compact, convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{V}
. Moreover, if there is a unique potential
h
, then
{\sf g}_{o}(\cdot,y_{n})
converges to
h
pointwise as
y_{n}\to\infty
.

Proof.

By
2.1
, for any sequence of vertices
y_{n}\to\infty
, Cantor diagonalization yields a subsequential pointwise limit of
{\sf g}_{o}(\cdot,y_{n})
, and any such limit is a potential. Next, for any potential
h
we have
h(x)\leq\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}c_{x_{i-1}x_{i}}^{-1}
, where
x_{0},\dots,x_{\ell}
is a simple path from
o
to
x
, by the argument in the proof of
2.1
, so the set of potentials is a closed subset of a compact product set.
Finally, convexity and the last statement are obvious.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

Conversely, if
{\sf g}_{o}(\cdot,y_{n})
converges pointwise as
y_{n}\to\infty
, then there is a unique potential; this follows from
2.17
below.

Dipoles can be used to show that the uniqueness of the potential does not depend on the choice of root in the network.

Claim 2.4
.

Let
(G,c)
be a recurrent network. Fix two vertices
o,\tilde{o}
and a function
h:V\to[0,\infty)
. Then
h
is a potential for the network
(G,c,o)
, if and only if the function

\tilde{h}(\cdot)=h(\cdot)+{\sf g}_{\tilde{o}}(\cdot,o)-h(\tilde{o})\,,

is a potential for
(G,c,\tilde{o})
.

Proof.

Assume that
h
is a potential for
(G,c,o)
. It is clear that
\tilde{h}(\tilde{o})=0
and

\Delta\tilde{h}(\cdot)={\bf 1}_{o}(\cdot)+{\bf 1}_{\tilde{o}}(\cdot)-{\bf 1}_{o}(\cdot)={\bf 1}_{\tilde{o}}(\cdot)\,.

Therefore
\tilde{h}(X_{\tau_{v}\wedge t})
is a martingale bounded below by
-h(\tilde{o})
. For each
x\in V
, by Fatou’s lemma and recurrence

\tilde{h}(x)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\E_{x}[\tilde{h}(X_{\tau_{\tilde{o}}\wedge t})]\geq\E_{x}[\liminf_{t\to\infty}\tilde{h}(X_{\tau_{\tilde{o}}\wedge t})]=\tilde{h}(\tilde{o})=0\,.

The other direction follows similarly.∎

2.1.
Basic properties of the
h
-process

Let
h:V\to[0,\infty)
be a potential. Write
\mathbb{P}^{h}_{\mu}
for the distribution of the
h
-process with initial distribution
\mu
and
\E^{h}_{\mu}
for the corresponding expectation. Next, we relate the Green kernels of the
h
-process and the network random walk killed at
o
. For any finite sequence
\gamma=(v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})
in
V
, we write

\mathbb{P}_{v_{0}}(\gamma):=\prod_{i=1}^{k}p(v_{i-1},v_{i})\,.

If
\gamma
is contained in
S_{h}
, we define

\mathbb{P}^{h}_{v_{0}}(\gamma)
similarly using
p^{h}(v_{i-1},v_{i})
. We have the telescoping product

(4)

\mathbb{P}^{h}_{v_{0}}(\gamma)=\mathbb{P}_{v_{0}}(\gamma)h(v_{k})/h(v_{0})\,.

We will use the initial distribution
\mu_{h}
defined in (
27
).
We have

(5)

\mathbb{P}^{h}_{\mu_{h}}(\gamma)=c_{ov_{0}}h(v_{0})\mathbb{P}^{h}_{v_{0}}(\gamma)=c_{ov_{0}}\mathbb{P}_{v_{0}}(\gamma)h(v_{k})=c_{o}\mathbb{P}_{o}(o\gamma)h(v_{k})\,.

Remark 2.5
.

By harmonicity of
h
in
V\setminus\{o\}
, the set
S_{h}
is a union of connected components of
V\setminus\{o\}
in
G
.

Claim 2.6
.

Let
h
be a potential on a recurrent rooted network
(G,c,o)
. Then the
h
-process
\{Y_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is transient. Moreover, its Green kernel
{\sf G}^{h}(x,y)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\mathbb{P}^{h}_{x}(Y_{k}=y)
, defined for
x,y\in S_{h}
, satisfies

(6)

{\sf G}^{h}(x,y)={\sf G}_{o}(x,y)\frac{h(y)}{h(x)}\,,

and for all
v\in S_{h}

(7)

{\sf G}^{h}({\mu_{h}},v)=h(v)c_{v}\,,

where
{\sf G}^{h}({\mu},v):=\sum_{x}\mu(x){\sf G}^{h}(x,v)
.

Proof.

Transience will follow once we show
G^{h}(x,y)<\infty
. To prove (
6
), we sum (
4
) over all paths from
x
to
y
that avoid
o
. Note that by
Remark

2.5
, any such path is contained in
S_{h}
. Next, (
7
) follows from (
6
) by

{\sf G}^{h}({\mu_{h}},y)=\sum_{v}c_{ov}h(v){\sf G}^{h}(v,y)=h(y)\sum_{v}c_{ov}{\sf G}_{o}(v,y)=h(y)c_{y}\,,

using (
3
) and the symmetry of
{\sf g}_{o}(\cdot,\cdot)
in the last step.
∎

Claim 2.7
.

Let
h
be a potential for a recurrent rooted network
(G,c,o)
and consider the
h
-process
\{Y_{n}\}
started at
v\in S_{h}
. Then for every
M>0
,

\mathbb{P}^{h}_{v}\big(h(Y_{\ell})>M\bigr)\to 1\quad\text{as}\quad\ell\to\infty\,.

This was also observed in
[
5
, Lemma 5]
.

Proof.

Recall that
\{X_{n}\}
is the network random walk. By (
4
), we have

\mathbb{P}_{v}^{h}\big(h(Y_{\ell})\leq M\bigr)=\E_{v}\Bigl[\frac{h(X_{\ell})}{h(v)}\,{\bf 1}_{\{h(X_{\ell})\leq M\;\,\text{and}\;\,\tau_{o}\geq\ell\}}\Bigr]\leq\frac{M}{h(v)}\,\mathbb{P}_{v}(\tau_{o}\geq\ell)\,,

which tends to
0
as
\ell\to\infty
by recurrence.
∎

Every potential
h
on a recurrent network is unbounded; indeed, if
\{X_{n}\}
is the network random walk, then
\{h(X_{n})\}
is a submartingale that does not converge. However, the gradient of a potential on each edge is bounded by the edge resistance, as the next lemma shows.

Lemma 2.8
.

Every potential
h
on a recurrent rooted network
(G,c,o)
, satisfies

|h(x)-h(y)|\leq r_{xy}\,,

for every edge
\{x,y\}\in E
, where
r_{xy}={1\over c_{xy}}
are edge resistances.

Remark 2.9
.

We will prove a stronger statement in
2.13
, but we find the proof of
Lemma

2.8
too enjoyable to omit.

Proof.

By
2.6
, for any
v,w\in S_{h}
with
\{v,w\}\in E
, we have

{\sf G}^{h}({\mu_{h}},v)p^{h}(v,w)=h(v)c_{v}{h(w)c_{vw}\over h(v)c_{v}}=h(w)c_{vw}\,.

The left hand side of the equality above is the expected number of crossings
N(v,w)
of the directed edge
(v,w)
by
\{Y_{n}\}
. Hence the expected
net
number of crossings of the directed edge
(v,w)
(that is,
N(v,w)-N(w,v)
) equals
c_{vw}(h(w)-h(v))
.

Next we observe that in a transient network, the expected net number of crossings of a directed edge by the network random walk equals the expected net number of crossings of the same edge by the loop-erased random walk (started at the same initial distribution) because each cycle of the simple random walk has equal probability of being traversed forward or backward. Since the net number of crossings of any edge by the loop erased random walk is
-1
,
0
or
1
, we conclude that
c_{vw}|h(v)-h(w)|\leq 1
. ∎

2.2.
Obtaining potentials from exhaustions

Another way to generate potentials uses an
exhaustion
of
G
, that is, a sequence of finite sets
\{D_{n}\}
such that
o\in D_{n}
and
D_{n}\subset D_{n+1}
, that satisfy
\cup_{n}D_{n}=V
. For each
n
, let

{\sf G}_{n}(x,y)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{k}=y,\tau_{D_{n}^{c}}>k)\,

be the Green kernel of the random walk killed upon exiting the set
D_{n}
, and write
{\sf g}_{n}(x,y)=c_{y}^{-1}{\sf G}_{n}(x,y)
. The function

(8)

h_{n}(x)={\sf g}_{n}(o,o)-{\sf g}_{n}(x,o)=\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{D_{n}^{c}}<\tau_{o})\,{\sf g}_{n}(o,o)\,,

is harmonic in
D_{n}\setminus\{o\}
. It takes the value
0
at
o
, and the value
{\sf g}_{n}(o,o)
at any
x\in V\setminus D_{n}
. Furthermore,

\Delta h_{n}(o)=\sum_{x\sim o}c_{ox}h_{n}(x)=\sum_{x\sim o}p(o,x)[{\sf G}_{n}(o,o)-{\sf G}_{n}(x,o)]={\sf G}_{n}(o,o)-\E_{o}{\sf G}_{n}(X_{1},o)=1\,.

Hence, any subsequential point-wise limit of
\{h_{n}\}
is a potential. Next we prove a converse for networks with bounded edge resistances.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
(G,c,o)
be a recurrent rooted network. Assume that the edge resistances are bounded,

R:=\sup_{e\in E}r_{e}<\infty
, and let
h
be a potential. Then there exists an exhaustion
\{D_{n}\}
of
G
such that

\forall v\in V,\quad h(v)=\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{n}(v)\,,

where
h_{n}
are defined in (
8
).

Proof.

By recurrence, for each
n
we can choose a ball
B_{n}
centered at
o
of large enough radius, such that

\mathbb{P}_{o}(\tau_{B_{n}^{c}}<\tau_{o}^{+})\leq\frac{1}{c_{o}n^{2}}\,.

Define

D_{n}:=\{v\in B_{n}:h(v)\leq n\}\quad\text{and}\quad\tau:=\tau_{o}^{+}\wedge\tau_{D_{n}^{c}}\,.

Start the network random walk at
X_{0}=o
, and consider the martingale

M_{t}:=c_{o}h(X_{\tau\wedge t})+{\bf 1}_{\{t=0\}}
which is bounded by
Lemma

2.8
. Optional stopping yields

1=M_{0}=\E_{o}M_{\tau}\geq\mathbb{P}_{o}\big(h(X_{\tau})>n\big)c_{o}n\,,

and by
Lemma

2.8
,

1=M_{0}=\E_{o}M_{\tau}\leq\mathbb{P}_{o}(X_{\tau}\in D_{n}^{c})c_{o}(n+R)\,.

Thus,

\frac{1}{c_{o}(n+R)}\leq\mathbb{P}_{o}(X_{\tau}\in D_{n}^{c})\leq\mathbb{P}_{o}\big(h(X_{\tau})>n\big)+\mathbb{P}_{o}(X_{\tau}\in B_{n}^{c})\leq\frac{1}{c_{o}n}+\frac{1}{c_{o}n^{2}}=\frac{n+1}{c_{o}n^{2}}\,.

Since the number of visits to
o
by the random walk is a geometric random variable,

(9)

n+R\geq g_{n}(o,o)=\big(c_{o}\mathbb{P}_{o}(X_{\tau}\in D_{n}^{c})\big)^{-1}\geq\frac{n^{2}}{n+1}\geq n-1\,.\

Fix
v\in V
of graph distance
\ell
from
o
, so that
p^{\ell}(o,v)>0
. Suppose that
n
is large enough so that
D_{n}
contains the shortest path from
o
to
v
.
Observe that

(10)

{1\over c_{o}n^{2}}\geq\mathbb{P}_{o}(X_{\tau}\in B_{n}^{c})\geq p^{\ell}(o,v)\mathbb{P}_{v}(X_{\tau}\in B_{n}^{c})\,.

Applying optional stopping
to the bounded martingale
h(X_{t\wedge\tau})

gives

(11)

h(v)=\E_{v}(h(X_{\tau}))\geq n\mathbb{P}_{v}(h(X_{\tau})>n)\,.

Finally, consider the bounded martingale

\Gamma_{t}:=h(X_{t\wedge\tau})-h_{n}(X_{t\wedge\tau})\,,

where we take
X_{0}=v
. Observe that for
x\in V\setminus D_{n}
that has a neighbor in
D_{n}
, we have that
h(x)\leq n+R
by
Lemma

2.8
, and
h_{n}(x)={\sf g}_{n}(o,o)\in[n-1,n+R]
by (
8
) and (
9
). Therefore,

\displaystyle|h(v)-h_{n}(v)|=|\Gamma_{0}|=|\E_{v}(\Gamma_{\tau})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(R+1)\,\mathbb{P}_{v}(h(X_{\tau})>n)+(n+R)\,\mathbb{P}_{v}(X_{\tau}\in B_{n}^{c})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(R+1)h(v)}{n}+\frac{(n+R)}{p^{\ell}(o,v)c_{o}n^{2}}\,,

using (
10
) and (
11
) in the last step. This concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 2.11
.

In the setting of
Lemma

2.10
, the network random walks started at
v_{1}\in S_{h}
conditioned to exit
D_{n}
before reaching
o
, converge weakly to the
h
-process as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{\ell})
be a path in
S_{h}
with
\gamma_{1}=v_{1}
.
Then

\mathbb{P}_{v_{1}}(\gamma\mid\tau_{D_{n}^{c}}<\tau_{o})=\mathbb{P}_{v_{1}}(\gamma){h_{n}(\gamma_{\ell})\over h_{n}(\gamma_{1})}\to\mathbb{P}^{h}(\gamma)\,,

as required.
∎

Next we improve
Lemma

2.8
, replacing edge resistances by
effective resistances
. For background on effective resistance see
[
10
, Chapter 2]
. In recurrent networks, one can define the effective resistance
R_{\mathrm{eff}}(x\leftrightarrow y)
between two vertices
x\neq y
as

R_{\mathrm{eff}}(x\leftrightarrow y)={\sf g}_{x}(y,y)=\bigl(c_{y}P_{y}(\tau_{x}<\tau_{y}^{+})\bigr)^{-1}\,.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
(G,c,o)
be a rooted network (not necessarily recurrent). Assume that
D\subset V
satisfies
o\in D
and
\mathbb{P}_{o}(\exists n\,\,X_{n}\not\in D)=1
. Then the Green density
{\sf g}_{D^{c}}(\cdot,o)
of the random walk killed upon exiting
D
is
1
-Lipschitz with respect to the effective resistance metric, that is,

\forall\,x,y\in D\qquad|{\sf g}_{D^{c}}(x,o)-{\sf g}_{D^{c}}(y,o)|\leq R_{\mathrm{eff}}(x\leftrightarrow y)\,.

Proof.

For the random walk
\{X_{n}\}
started at
x
, we have

\sum_{t=0}^{\tau_{D^{c}}}{\bf 1}_{\{X_{t}=o\}}\leq\sum_{t=0}^{\tau_{y}}{\bf 1}_{\{X_{t}=o\}}+\sum_{t=\tau_{y}}^{\tau_{y}\vee\tau_{D^{c}}}{\bf 1}_{\{X_{t}=o\}}\,.

Taking expectations and using the strong Markov property gives

{\sf G}_{D^{c}}(x,o)\leq{\sf G}_{y}(x,o)+{\sf G}_{D^{c}}(y,o)\,,

which dividing by
c_{o}
yields

{\sf g}_{D^{c}}(x,o)\leq{\sf g}_{y}(x,o)+{\sf g}_{D^{c}}(y,o)\,.

Hence

{\sf g}_{D^{c}}(x,o)-{\sf g}_{D^{c}}(y,o)\leq{\sf g}_{y}(x,o)={\sf g}_{y}(o,x)\leq{\sf g}_{y}(x,x)=R_{\mathrm{eff}}(x\leftrightarrow y)\,,

giving the desired result by interchanging the roles of
x
and
y
.
∎

Corollary 2.13
.

Let
(G,c,o)
be recurrent rooted network and let
h:V\to[0,\infty)
be a potential. Then

\forall\,x,y\in D\qquad|h(x)-h(y)|\leq R_{\mathrm{eff}}(x\leftrightarrow y)\,.

Proof.

If the edge resistances are bounded, then this follows directly from Lemmas
2.10
and
2.12
. Otherwise, we consider the enhanced network
(G^{*},c^{*})=(V^{*},E^{*},c^{*})
where
V\subset V^{*}
and every edge
e\in E
with
r_{e}>1
is split into
\lceil r_{e}\rceil
edges of resistance
r_{e}/\lceil r_{e}\rceil
. Observe that for every
x,y\in V
, the effective resistance
R_{\mathrm{eff}}(x\leftrightarrow y)
in
(G^{*},c^{*})
equals the effective resistance in
(G,c)
. The potential
h
has a unique extension to a potential
h^{*}
on
(G^{*},c^{*})
and the result follows from the case of bounded edge resistances.
∎

2.3.
Preliminaries on transient chains

Since the
h
-process is transient (
2.6
), we present, following Dynkin
[
4
]
, a general property of transient chains. Consider a transient Markov chain on a countable state space
S
with transition probabilities
q(x,y)
, and denote by
{\sf G}(x,y)
the Green kernel

{\sf G}(x,y)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{n}(x,y)\,.

Let
\mu
be a finitely supported probability measure on
S
representing the starting state for the chain. We write

{\sf G}(\mu,y)=\sum_{x\in S}\mu(x){\sf G}(x,y)

and assume that
\mu
satisfies

(12)

{\sf G}(\mu,y)>0\hbox{ for all }y\in S\,.

The
Martin kernel

M(x,y)
is now defined as

(13)

M(x,y):=\frac{{\sf G}(x,y)}{{\sf G}(\mu,y)}\,.

The strong Markov property implies that
{\sf G}(\mu,y)\geq\mathbb{P}_{\mu}(\tau_{x}<\infty){\sf G}(x,y)
, hence

(14)

M(x,y)\leq\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}_{\mu}(\tau_{x}<\infty)}=\frac{{\sf G}(x,x)}{{\sf G}(\mu,x)}\,.

Let
D\subset V
be a finite set and let
L_{D}
be the last visit to
D
, that is,

L_{D}:=\sup\{\,n\geq 0:Y_{n}\in D\,\}\,,

with
L_{D}:=-\infty
when this set is empty. Clearly,
\mathbb{P}_{x}(L_{D}<\infty)=1
for any
x\in S
.

Lemma 2.16
.

Let
x\in S
and let
D\subset S
be a finite set with
x\in D
and
\mu(D)=1
. Then writing
L=L_{D}
we have

\E_{\mu}[M(x,Y_{L})]=1\,.

Proof.

For any
y\in D
and
k\geq 0
, we have

\mathbb{P}_{x}(Y_{L}=y,L=k)=q^{k}(x,y)\mathbb{P}_{y}(L=0)\,.

Therefore

\mathbb{P}_{x}(Y_{L}=y)={\sf G}(x,y)\mathbb{P}_{y}(L=0)\,,

whence

(15)

\mathbb{P}_{\mu}(Y_{L}=y)={\sf G}(\mu,y)\mathbb{P}_{y}(L=0)\,.

Thus

M(x,y)=\frac{\mathbb{P}_{x}(Y_{L}=y)}{\mathbb{P}_{\mu}(Y_{L}=y)}\,,

provided that
\mathbb{P}_{\mu}(Y_{L}=y)>0
. We conclude that

\E_{\mu}[M(x,Y_{L})]=\sum_{y\in D}\mathbb{P}_{\mu}(Y_{L}=y)M(x,y)=\sum_{y}\mathbb{P}_{x}(Y_{L}=y)=1\,.

∎

2.4.
Representing potentials as mixture of dipoles

The next theorem shows that every potential, when restricted to a finite set of vertices
D
containing the neighbors of
o
, is a mixture of dipoles
{\sf g}_{o}(\cdot,v)
with
v\in D
.

Theorem 2.17
.

Let
h
be a potential for a recurrent rooted network
(G,c,o)
. Let
\{Y_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be the corresponding
h
-process with initial distribution
\mu_{h}
defined in (
27
). Let
D\subset V
be a finite set containing
o
such that
\mu_{h}(D)=1
. Then for all
x\in D
, we have

(16)

h(x)=\E_{\mu_{h}}{\sf g}_{o}(x,Y_{L})\,,

where
L=L_{D}:=\sup\{\,n\geq 0:Y_{n}\in D\,\}
.

Proof of
2.17
.

If
x\not\in S_{h}
, then both sides of (
16
) vanish by
Remark

2.5
. Therefore we may assume that
x\in D\cap S_{h}
. For all
y\in S_{h}
we get from
(
6
) that
{\sf g}^{h}(x,y)={\sf g}_{o}(x,y)h(y)/h(x)
, and
2.6
yields
{\sf g}^{h}(\mu_{h},y)=h(y)
.
Thus
Lemma

2.16
, applied to
D\cap S_{h}
(instead of
D
), implies that

1=\E_{\mu_{h}}\left[{{\sf g}^{h}(x,Y_{L})\over{\sf g}^{h}(\mu_{h},Y_{L})}\right]=\E_{\mu_{h}}\left[{{\sf g}_{o}(x,Y_{L})\over h(x)}\right]\,.

∎

In the next claim, we show that the time reversal of an
h
-process, run until it exits a finite set, is a network random walk stopped at
o
. This has an application to harmonic measure from infinity (
3.7
) and to uniform spanning trees, see
Lemma

6.1
.

Claim 2.18
.

In the setup of
2.17
, let
\nu_{D}
be the law of
Y_{L}
. Then
(Y_{L},\ldots,Y_{0},o)
has the law of the network random walk with initial distribution
\nu_{D}
, stopped at
o
.

Proof.

We have to show that for any finite path
(v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell})
in
S_{h}
,

(17)

\mathbb{P}_{\mu_{h}}^{h}\Big((Y_{0},\ldots,Y_{L})=(v_{0},\ldots,v_{\ell})\Big)=\mathbb{P}_{\nu_{D}}\Big((X_{0},\ldots,X_{\tau_{o}})=(v_{\ell},\ldots,v_{0},o)\Big)\,.

We write the left hand side using (
5
) as

c_{o}p(v_{o},o)\Big[\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}p(v_{i-1},v_{i})\Big]h(v_{\ell})\mathbb{P}^{h}_{v_{\ell}}(L=0)\,.

On the other hand, using (
15
) from the proof of
Lemma

2.16
and
2.6
, we deduce that the right hand side of (
17
) equals

h(v_{\ell})c_{v_{\ell}}\mathbb{P}^{h}_{v_{\ell}}(L=0)\prod_{i=0}^{\ell-1}p(v_{\ell-i},v_{\ell-i-1})p(v_{o},o)\,.

Thus the two sides of (
17
) are equal by reversibility.
∎

3.
Harmonic measures from infinity

Given a recurrent network
(G,c)
, a finite set
A\subset V
and a vertex
v
, the
harmonic measure
from
v
on
A
is

\omega_{v}^{A}(z):=P_{v}(X_{\tau_{A}}=z)\,,

for any
z\in A
. We say that
A
admits
harmonic measure from infinity
if the limit
\omega_{\infty}^{A}(z)=\lim_{v\to\infty}\omega_{v}^{A}(z)
exists for all
z\in A
.
If this holds for all finite
A\subset V
, then we say that the network
(G,c)
admits
harmonic measures from infinity
. The following criterion is often useful.

Claim 3.1
.

A recurrent network
(G,c)
admits harmonic measures from infinity, if and only if there exists a vertex
o\in V
and an exhaustion
\{D_{n}\}
of
G
with
o\in D_{n}
for all
n
such that
the harmonic measures satisfy

q:=\sup_{n}\max_{v_{1},v_{2}\in\partial D_{n+1}}\|\omega_{v_{1}}^{\partial D_{n}}-\omega_{v_{2}}^{\partial D_{n}}\|_{{\rm TV}}<1\,.

Proof.

Given a finite set
A\subset V
,
find
k
such that
A\subset D_{k}
and observe inductively that for each
n\geq 1
and
v_{1},v_{2}\notin D_{n+k}
, we have

\|\omega_{v_{1}}^{\partial D_{k}}-\omega_{v_{2}}^{\partial D_{k}}\|_{{\rm TV}}\leq q^{n}\,,\quad\text{whence}\quad\|\omega_{v_{1}}^{A}-\omega_{v_{2}}^{A}\|_{{\rm TV}}\leq q^{n}\,.

∎

Theorem 3.2
.

A recurrent network
(G,c,o)
has a unique potential if and only if every finite set
A\subset V
admits harmonic measure from infinity. In this case, the unique potential
h
and the collection of harmonic measures from infinity on finite sets can be computed from each other as follows.

(a)

For all
z\in V\setminus\{o\}
, we have
h(z)=\omega_{\infty}^{\{o,z\}}(z){\sf g}_{o}(z,z)
.

(b)

If
o\in A
and
z\in A\setminus\{o\}
, then

\displaystyle\omega_{\infty}^{A}(z)=\frac{\det(M^{h,z})}{\det(M)}
, where
M
is the invertible matrix

M=\big({\sf g}_{o}(x,y)\big)_{x,y\in A\setminus\{o\}}
,
and for every
z\in A\setminus\{o\}
, the matrix

M^{h,z}
is obtained from
M
by replacing the
z
-column with the vector
(h(x))_{x\in A\setminus\{o\}}
.

(c)

If
o\notin A
, choose
\tilde{o}\in A
and let
\tilde{h}
be the unique potential for the rooted network

(G,c,\tilde{o})
, as in
2.4
.
To compute
\omega_{\infty}^{A}(\cdot)
, use (b) with
\tilde{h}
instead of
h
.

Remark 3.3
.

If
z
is a vertex of
G
such that there exists a network automorphism switching between
z
and
o
, then
\omega_{\infty}^{\{o,z\}}(z)={1/2}
. For any such vertex
z
we deduce from part (a) of the theorem above that
h(z)={\sf g}_{o}(z,z)/2
. In the particular case of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
this is contained in Proposition 4.4.1 of
[
9
]
.

Remark 3.4
.

Part (b) of the theorem above follows
[
13
, Chapters 11 and 14]
.

To prove this theorem, we first relate harmonic measure from a vertex to dipoles.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(G,c,o)
be a recurrent rooted network and let
A\subset V
be a finite set containing
o
with
|A|\geq 2
. Denote by
M
the square matrix

M=\big({\sf g}_{o}(x,y)\big)_{x,y\in A\setminus\{o\}}\,.

Then
M
is invertible. Moreover, for every
v\neq o
and
y\in A
,

\omega_{v}^{A}(y)=\frac{\det(M^{v,y})}{\det(M)}\,,

where
M^{v,y}
is the matrix
obtained from
M
by replacing the
y
-th column with the vector
({\sf g}_{o}(x,v))_{x\in A\setminus\{o\}}
.

Proof.

First entrance decomposition gives that for every
y\in A\setminus\{o\}
,

{\sf g}_{o}(v,y)=\sum_{x\in A\setminus\{o\}}\omega_{v}^{A}(x){\sf g}_{o}(x,y)\,.

We first show that
M
is invertible. Indeed, suppose that the coefficients
\lambda_{x}
satisfy
\sum_{x\in A\setminus\{o\}}\lambda_{x}{\sf g}_{o}(x,y)=0
for all
y\in A\setminus\{o\}
. Define
\psi:V\to\mathbb{R}
by

\psi(y):=\sum_{x\in A\setminus\{o\}}\lambda_{x}{\sf g}_{o}(x,y)\,.

Then
\psi
is bounded, harmonic off
A
and vanishes on
A
. Hence by recurrence
\psi\equiv 0
. On the other hand, for all
y\in A\setminus\{o\}
we have

\Delta\psi(y)=-\sum_{x\in A\setminus\{o\}}\lambda_{x}{\bf 1}_{x}(y)=-\lambda_{y}\,,

hence
\lambda_{y}=0
. The result now follows from Cramer’s rule.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

In particular, when
A=\{o,y\}
, we get

(18)

\omega_{v}^{A}(y)=\frac{{\sf g}_{o}(v,y)}{{\sf g}_{o}(y,y)}\,,

which is also easy to verify directly.

Proof of
3.2
.

Assume first that there exists a unique potential
h
. Then
h(x)=\lim_{v\to\infty}{\sf g}_{o}(x,v)
, hence (b) follows from
Lemma

3.5
and in particular it shows that every finite set admits harmonic measure from infinity. Furthermore, (a) follows from (
18
).

Conversely, assume that every finite set admits harmonic measure from infinity. By (
18
) we deduce that
{\sf g}_{o}(v,y)={\sf g}_{o}(y,v)
converges as
v\to\infty
for any
y\in V
. By taking an exhaustion by finite sets
\{D_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
,
2.17
implies there is a unique potential given in (a). ∎

Combining the preceding theorem with the path reversal claim (
2.18
) yields the following folklore corollary.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
(G,c,o)
be a recurrent network with a unique potential
h
and
A\subset V
a finite set so that
\mu_{h}(A)=1
. Then the last exit distribution
\nu_{A}
of the
h
-process on
A
equals the harmonic measure from infinity on
A
.

Proof.

Let
\{Y_{n}\}
be the
h
-process started, as usual, from the initial distribution
\mu_{h}
and let
D
be any set containing
A
. By
2.18
, the reversed path
(Y_{L_{D}},\ldots,Y_{0},o)
is distributed as network random walk starting from
\nu_{D}
and stopped at
o
. The first vertex in
A
on this reversed path is
Y_{L_{A}}
which has law
\nu_{A}
.
Taking
D
to be a ball of radius
R
tending to infinity, it follows that harmonic measure from infinity on
A
also equals
\nu_{A}
.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

Let
\{Y_{n}\}
be the
h
-process in a recurrent rooted network with a unique potential
h
. Motivated by the preceding corollary, we define the
random walk from infinity to
o
in this network to be the process

\{\widetilde{Y}_{k}\}_{k=-\infty}^{0}
, where
\widetilde{Y}_{k}:=Y_{-k}
for
k<0
.
3.7
implies that for every finite set
A\subset V
that contains all neighbors of
o
,
harmonic measure from infinity on
A
is the law of
\widetilde{Y}_{\tau}
, where
\tau:=\min\big\{k<0:\widetilde{Y}_{k}\in A\big\}
.

In a rooted network that admits a unique potential
h
, we can give a short proof that
h
tends to infinity, using
3.7
and
3.2
. Berestycki and van Engelenburg
[
2
, Proposition 5.1]
proved this in the special case of unimodular random graphs and asked whether
3.9
holds.

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
(G,c,o)
be an infinite, connected, locally finite recurrent rooted network that has a unique potential
h
. Then
h
tends to infinity, i.e., for every
b>0
, the set
\{v\in V:h(v)\leq b\}
is finite.

Remark 3.10
.

This result is generalized in the next section.

Proof of
3.9
.

We will assume that for some
b>0
, there is a sequence
v_{n}\to\infty
such that
h(v_{n})\leq b
and obtain a contradiction. By
2.7
, if
A
is a large enough ball around
o
, then the last exit distribution
\nu_{A}
of the
h
-process from
A
satisfies

(19)

\nu_{A}\Big(\{y\in A:h(y)\geq 4b\}\Big)\geq 1/2\,.

Optional stopping for the positive martingale
h(X_{t\wedge\tau_{A}})
, where
X_{0}=v_{n}
, gives

b\geq h(v_{n})\geq\E[h(X_{\tau_{A}})]\geq 4b\mathbb{P}(h(X_{\tau_{A}}\geq 4b))\,,

so

(20)

\omega_{v_{n}}^{A}(\{y\in A:h(y)\geq 4b\})\leq 1/4\,.

By
3.2
the set
A
admits harmonic measure from infinity, and by
3.7
this measure equals
\nu_{A}
, so the measures

\omega_{v_{n}}^{A}
tend to
\nu_{A}
as
n\to\infty
. Comparing (
19
) to (
20
) yields the desired contradiction.
∎

4.
Existence of potentials tending to infinity

In 1936, G.C. Evans
[
6
]
showed that every compact polar set
K
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
supports a probability measure
\mu
such that its Newtonian potential
h_{\mu}
is infinite on
K
, see also
[
14
]
. Thus
h_{\mu}
is a harmonic function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus K
that tends to infinity on every sequence converging to
K
. Using Evans’ approach, Nakai
[
12
]
proved that every parabolic noncompact Riemann surface
S
admits a function
h:S\to[0,\infty)
which is harmonic off a compact set and tends to infinity (i.e., its level sets
\{x\in S:h(x)\leq M\}
are compact). In Nakai’s proof, the set
K
is replaced by the Stone-Čech boundary of
S
. The discrete analog of these results is

4.1
, which can also be found in our recent note
[
11
]
.

We say that a function
h:V\to[0,\infty)

tends to infinity
if for all
M>0
, the set
\{v\in V:h(v)\leq M\}
is finite. Observe that on
\mathbb{Z}
, the potentials
h_{1}(n)=\max(n,0)
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
h_{2}(n)=\max(-n,0)
do
not
tend to infinity, but any non-trivial convex combination of them does. On
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, on the other hand, it is well known, see
[
13
, Sections 12 and 31]
, that there is a unique potential
h
with
h(v)=\Theta(\log|v|)
as
v\to\infty
.

Theorem 4.1
.

On every recurrent rooted network
(G,c,o)
, there exists a potential tending to infinity.

Remark 4.2
.

The argument used by Nakai
[
12
]
in the setting of Riemann surfaces can be adapted to prove
4.1
, but this argument is non-constructive. Here we present a more constructive probabilistic approach, using the Von Neumann minimax theorem for finite zero sum games. In
[
11
]
, the Sion minimax theorem was used instead.

For any integer
R>0
, we set

\partial B(o,R)=\{w:d(o,w)=R\}\,,

where
d(\cdot,\cdot)
is the graph distance in
G
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(G,c,o)
be a recurrent rooted network. Assume that for every
M>0
there exists an integer
R>0
and a potential
\psi=\psi_{M,R}
such that
\psi(v)\geq M
for any
v\in\partial B(o,R)
.
Then there exists a potential
h
that tends to infinity.

Proof.

First observe that for every
R
and
M
, if
v\not\in B(o,R)
, then
\psi(v)=\psi_{M,R}(v)\geq M
. Indeed, let
\{X_{t}\}
be the network random walk started at
v
and let
\tau_{R}
be the hitting time of
\partial B(o,R)
. Then
\psi(X_{t\wedge\tau_{R}})
is a positive martingale, so by recurrence and optional stopping,

\psi(v)\geq\E[\psi(X_{\tau_{R}})]\geq M\,.

For
n\geq 1
, let
R_{n}
and
\psi_{n}
denote the radii and potentials corresponding to
M=n2^{n}
in the hypothesis of the lemma. By
2.2
,

h:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{-n}\psi_{n}

is a potential. Next, if
v\notin B(o,R_{n})
, then
h(v)\geq 2^{-n}\psi_{n}(v)\geq n
. Thus,
\{v:h(v)<n\}
is finite for every
n
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

In the particular case where the graph
G
is a tree
T
, a potential tending to infinity can be constructed explicitly using the preceding lemma. Indeed, let
T_{\infty}\subset T
be the union of all simple infinite paths emanating from the root. Let
M>0
be arbitrary. Given an integer
R>0
, let
{\sf g}_{R}(\cdot,\cdot)
be the green density for the network random walk killed on the complement of the ball
B_{R}:=\{x\in T_{\infty}:d(x,o)<R\}
. By recurrence of
T_{\infty}
, we can choose
R
such that
{\sf g}_{R}(o,o)\geq M
. Set

\psi(x)={\sf g}_{R}(o,o)-{\sf g}_{R}(x,o)\,,

for each
x\in B_{R}\cup\partial B_{R}
. We now extend
\psi
to
T_{\infty}
as follows. For each
v\in\partial B_{R}
choose an infinite simple path
v=v_{0},v_{1},v_{2},\ldots
such that
v_{1}
is contained in
T_{\infty}\setminus B
. Let
v_{-1}
be the parent of
v
in
T_{\infty}
. We set
\psi(v_{n})=\psi(v)+n(\psi(v)-\psi(v_{-1}))
for all
n\geq 1
. We have defined
\psi
on a subtree
T_{\infty}^{\prime}\subset T_{\infty}
and we extend by projection: for any vertex
w\in T_{\infty}
set
\psi(w)=\psi(w_{*})
where
w_{*}
is the closest vertex to
w
in
T_{\infty}^{\prime}
. Thus
\psi
is a potential on
T_{\infty}
such that its values on
T_{\infty}\setminus B_{T_{\infty}}(o,R)
are larger than
M
. By
Lemma

4.3
, there exists a potential
h
on
T_{\infty}
which tends to infinity. We extend
h
to
T
by projection.

For integer
r\geq 1
, let
\Upsilon_{r}
be the simplex of probability measures on
\partial B_{r}
and define

\displaystyle V_{1}(\infty,r)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\max_{\psi\in\Psi}\min_{\eta\in\Upsilon_{r}}\psi\cdot\eta=\max_{\psi\in\Psi}\min_{v\in\partial B_{r}}\psi(v)\,,

\displaystyle V_{2}(\infty,r)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\min_{\eta\in\Upsilon_{r}}\max_{\psi\in\Psi}\psi\cdot\eta\,.

These can be interpreted as the values for players
1
and
2
of the zero-sum game where player
1
selects
\psi\in\Psi
, player
2
selects
v\in\partial B_{r}
and the payoff is
\psi(v)
.

Lemma 4.5
.

For each integer
r\geq 1

V_{1}(\infty,r)=V_{2}(\infty,r)\,.

As observed in
[
11
]
, this follows directly from Sion’s minimax Theorem, but here we will derive it using approximation by finite games where the values can be calculated via linear programming.

Proof of
Lemma

4.5
.

The inequality

V_{1}(\infty,r)\leq V_{2}(\infty,r)
clearly holds. Fix
r\geq 1
. For each integer
R>r
, consider the game where player
1
chooses
w\in\partial B_{R}
, player
2
chooses
v\in\partial B_{r}
and the payoff is
{\sf g}_{o}(w,v)
. By von Neumann’s minimax Theorem, the values

\displaystyle V_{1}(R,r)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\max_{\zeta\in\Upsilon_{R}}\min_{v\in\partial B_{r}}{\sf g}_{o}(v,\zeta)\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle V_{2}(R,r)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\min_{\eta\in\Upsilon_{r}}\max_{w\in\partial B_{R}}{\sf g}_{o}(\eta,w)\,

are equal. By
2.17
, for each potential
\psi
and each
v\in\partial B_{r}
, we have

\psi(v)=\E_{\mu_{\psi}}{\sf g}_{o}(v,Y_{L_{R}})\,,

where
L_{R}
is the last exit time of the
\psi
-process
\{Y_{n}\}
from
B_{R}\cup\partial B_{R}
. Thus, for any
\eta\in\Upsilon_{r}
and
\psi\in\Psi
, we have
\psi\cdot\eta\leq\max_{w\in\partial B_{R}}{\sf g}_{o}(\eta,w)
. Hence by definition

V_{2}(\infty,r)\leq\max_{\psi}\psi\cdot\eta\leq\max_{w\in\partial B_{R}}{\sf g}_{o}(\eta,w)\,,

for any
\eta\in\Upsilon_{r}
. Minimizing over
\eta
gives
V_{2}(\infty,r)\leq V_{2}(R,r)
. On the other hand, for each
R>r
there exists
\zeta_{R}\in\Upsilon_{R}
such that

V_{1}(R,r)=\min_{v\in\partial B_{r}}{\sf g}_{o}(v,\zeta_{R})
. Pick a sequence of integer
R_{n}\to\infty
such that
{\sf g}_{o}(\cdot,\zeta_{R_{n}})
converge to a potential
h
, so
\lim_{n}V_{1}(R_{n},r)=\min_{v\in\partial B_{r}}h(v)
. Hence

V_{2}(\infty,r)\leq\lim_{n}V_{2}(R_{n},r)=\lim_{n}V_{1}(R_{n},r)=\min_{v\in\partial B_{r}}h(v)\leq V_{1}(\infty,r)\,.

∎

4.1.
Two lemmas from
[
11
]
on the
h
-process

The first lemma shows that if a sequence of
dipoles
g_{o}(\cdot,v_{n})
converges pointwise to a potential
h
as
v_{n}\to\infty
, then the network random walk from
o
, conditioned to reach
v_{n}
before returning to
o
, converges weakly to the
h
-process. We prove this more generally, for mixtures of dipoles.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
(G,c,o)
be a recurrent rooted network. Let
R_{n}\to\infty
be sequence of integers and for each
n
let
\eta_{R_{n}}
be a probability measure supported on
\partial B(o,R_{n})
. Assume that the sequence
{\sf g}_{o}(\cdot,\eta_{R_{n}})
converges pointwise to a potential
h
as
n\to\infty
. Then for any finite path
\gamma
in
S_{h}
starting at a neighbor of
o
, we have

(21)

6.

In a finite network

have finite intersection almost surely, hence, the same conclusion holds for the traces of two independent
h
-processes on
(\mathbb{Z}^{d},c,o)
.
Lemma

6.1
finishes the proof. ∎

Remark 6.4
.

It follows from
Remark

6.2
that in the preceding example, the UST has infinitely many ends.

The network presented in
6.3
has conductances tending to zero on the edges.
For completeness, we also describe an unweighted recurrent rooted graph
(G,o)
with a unique potential, where the UST has multiple ends almost surely.
Start with an infinite binary tree
T_{2}
with root
o
. For each
k\geq 1
, identify level
4k
of
T_{2}
with a 4-dimensional face
F_{k}\cong\{1,\dots,2^{k}\}^{4}
of a 7-dimensional discrete cube
Q_{k}\cong\{1,\dots,2^{k}\}^{7}
. All these cubes are pairwise disjoint. The graph
G_{0}
obtained as a union of
T_{2}
and all these cubes is transient. Let
\varphi(v)
be the probability that simple random walk (SRW) starting from a vertex
v
of
G_{0}
visits the root
o
. Then for
v
at level
\ell
of
T_{2}
we have
\varphi(v)=2^{-\ell}
and
\varphi\equiv 2^{-4k}
on
Q_{k}
.
Write
c_{xy}=\varphi(x)\varphi(y)
for adjacent nodes

in
G_{0}
.

Proposition 6.5
.

Define a graph
G
by subdividing every edge
xy
in
G_{0}
into
c_{xy}^{-1}
new edges (recall that
c_{xy}^{-1}
is a positive integer). Then the rooted graph
(G,o)
is recurrent with a unique potential, yet the UST in
G
has multiple ends almost surely.

Proof.

As in
6.3
, we see that the rooted network
(G_{0},c,o)
is recurrent.
Since the uniform mixing time for SRW in
Q_{k}
is
O(2^{2k})
and from each vertex at level
4k
of
T_{2}
the SRW has positive probability of spending the next
\Omega(2^{3k})
steps in
Q_{k}
, the criterion in
3.1
(with
D_{n}
consisting of the first
4n
levels of
T_{2}
and all the attached cubes) implies that
(G_{0},c,o)
has a unique potential
h_{0}
. As in the preceding proposition, the
h_{0}
-process in
(G_{0},c,o)
is simply SRW in
G_{0}
, conditioned to avoid
o
.
Then
(G,o)
has a unique potential
h
, obtained from
h_{0}
via linear interpolation, and the
h
-process in
(G,o)
, watched only when visiting
G_{0}
, is exactly the
h_{0}
-process in
(G_{0},c,o)
.

Let
\{Y_{s}^{0}\}
be an
h_{0}
-process in
(G_{0},c,o)
that is started at a uniformly chosen neighbor of
o
. Observe that within each cube

, this process evolves as an SRW. It follows from claim
6.6
below that the expected number of intersections in

of
\{Y_{s}^{0}\}_{s\geq 0}
with an independent copy

is

. Moreover, the same bound follows for the expected number of pairs of times
(s,t)
when

and

. Therefore, two independent copies of the

∎

Claim 6.6
.

Let
F
be a 4-dimensional face
F\cong\{1,\dots,n\}^{4}
of a 7-dimensional discrete cube
Q\cong\{1,\dots,n\}^{7}
.
Consider two independent simple random walks
\{X_{t}\}
and
\{Z_{t}\}
in
Q
, where
X_{0},Z_{0}
are uniform in
F
.
Let
\tau_{X}:=\min\{t\geq 1:X_{t}\in F\}
and define
\tau_{Z}
similarly. Then

\sum_{t,s\geq 0}\mathbb{P}(X_{t}=Z_{s},\,t\leq\tau_{X},\,s\leq\tau_{Z})=O(1/n)\,.

Proof.

Since the commute time in a 3-dimensional cube of side
n
is
O(n^{3})
, we have
P_{w}(\tau_{X}\geq Cn^{3})\leq\rho<1
for some integer
C>0
.
By dividing time into blocks of length
Cn^{3}
, it suffices to prove that

(34)

\sum_{t,s=0}^{Cn^{3}}\mathbb{P}(X_{t}=Z_{s})=O(1/n)\,,

where the first four coordinates of
X_{0}
and
Z_{0}
are independent and uniform in
F
and the remaining three coordinates are arbitrary.
Now if
\widetilde{X}_{t}
and
\widetilde{Z}_{s}
are the last 3 coordinates of
X_{t}
and
Z_{s}
respectively, then

\mathbb{P}(\widetilde{X}_{t}=\widetilde{Z}_{s})=O(n^{-3}+(t+s)^{-3/2})
,
whence by considering the number of steps in these 3 coordinates,

\mathbb{P}(X_{t}=Z_{s})=O(n^{-4}(n^{-3}+(t+s)^{-3/2}))\,.

Summing over
t,s\leq Cn^{3}
yields (
34
).
∎

Next we present an example, due to Tom Hutchcroft, showing that the converse to
Lemma

6.1
is false. Start with a ternary tree
T
rooted at
o
and enumerate the vertices of
T
by
\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots\}
in a breadth first search, that is, the level (distance to
o
in
T
) of
v_{n}
is a non-decreasing function of
n
. For each integer
h\geq 1
, replace every edge of
T
between a vertex of level
\ell
and its parent at level
\ell-1
by a path of length
3^{3^{\ell}}
. Denote the resulting tree by
\widetilde{T}
. We now attach to
\widetilde{T}
at
o
an infinite path
\mathbb{N}=(1,2,3,\ldots)
and for every
n
, we connect
n
to
v_{n}
by a path of length
3^{3^{\ell}}
where
\ell\geq\log_{3}n-1
is the level of
v_{n}
. Denote the resulting rooted graph with unit conductances by
(G,1,o)
.

Proposition 6.7
.

The rooted network
(G,1,o)
is recurrent and has a unique potential
h
. Furthermore, two independent
h
-processes have infinite intersection almost surely, and the UST on
(G,1)
has infinitely many ends almost surely.

Proof.

Starting from the vertex
m\in\mathbb{N}
, the probability of visiting the pair
\{m-1,m+3^{m}+1\}
before reaching the tree
\widetilde{T}
is at least
1-C_{1}3^{-m/3}
. Indeed, the effective conductance between
\{m,m+1,\ldots,m+3^{m}\}
and
\{v_{m},v_{m+1},\ldots,v_{m+3^{m}}\}
is at most
C_{1}3^{-m/3}
by the parallel law.
Therefore, the probability that a simple random walk from
m
visits
m-1
before reaching the tree
\widetilde{T}
is at least

1-C_{2}3^{-m/3}
.
We conclude that, starting from
m
, the probability of visiting
r<m
before reaching the tree
\widetilde{T}
is at least
1-C_{3}3^{-r/3}
.

On the other hand, starting from a vertex
v_{m}
, the probability of visiting
m
before reaching either the parent or children of
v_{m}
in
T
is
{1/2}+o_{m}(1)
, since the paths from
v_{m}
to its parent and to
m
have the same length, while the three paths from
v_{m}
to its children are much longer. Hence, for every fixed
r
, the probability that a random walk started at
v_{m}
visits
\lfloor\sqrt{m}\rfloor
before reaching
B_{G}(o,r)
is
1-o_{m}(1)
as
m\to\infty
.

We deduce from these two estimates that for any integer
r>0
, there exists
M(r)
such that every vertex
w
outside
B_{G}(o,M(r))
satisfies

(35)

\mathbb{P}_{w}(\tau_{r}=\tau_{B_{G}(o,r)}<\infty)=1-o_{r}(1)\,.

In particular, this implies that
G
is recurrent. Furthermore, by
3.1
harmonic measure from infinity exists
and
3.2
implies that the network admits a unique potential
h
. By combining (
35
) and
3.7
, we infer that the probability that two independent
h
-processes visit the vertex
r
is at least
1-o_{r}(1)
, hence by Borel-Cantelli, their intersection is infinite almost surely.

To study the UST on
G
recall the stacks representation of Wilson’s algorithm
[
10
, Section 4.1]
. We use it to define an independent bond percolation process
H
on
T
as follows. For
n=1,2,3,\ldots
we run a simple random walk in
G
starting at
v_{n}
using the stacks (we discard from the stacks the arrows we used), stopped when reaching
\mathbb{N}
or
v_{n}
’s neighbors in
T
. Suppose
w
is the parent of
v
in
T
. We declare the edge
vw\in E(T)

open
in
H
, if the random walk above, started at
v
, visits
w
before visiting
\mathbb{N}
or the children of
v
in
T
. The probability that an edge
vw\in E(T)
is open in
H
is
{1/2}+o(1)
as
v\to\infty
. Thus,
H
is a supercritical percolation processes on
T
, whence with positive probability the cluster of
o
in
H
is infinite and has uncountably many ends. We note that if
v\in T
belongs to the cluster of
o
in
H
, then the path from
v
to
o
in
\widetilde{T}
is contained in the UST of
G
. Thus, the UST of
G
has uncountably many ends with positive probability. Finally, this probability is
1
by
[
1
, Lemma 8.3]
.
∎

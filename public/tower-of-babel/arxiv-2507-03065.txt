Cycle-Consistent Helmholtz Machine: Goal-Seeded Simulation via Inverted Inference
Xin Li

Abstract
The Helmholtz Machine (HM) is a foundational architecture for unsupervised learning, coupling a bottom-up recognition model with a top-down generative model through alternating inference. However, its reliance on symmetric, data-driven updates constrains its ability to perform goal-directed reasoning or simulate temporally extended processes. In this work, we introduce the Cycle-Consistent Helmholtz Machine (C 2 HM), a novel extension that reframes inference as a goal-seeded , asymmetric process grounded in structured internal priors. Rather than inferring latent causes solely from sensory data, C 2 HM simulates plausible latent trajectories conditioned on abstract goals, aligning them with observed outcomes through a recursive cycle of forward generation and inverse refinement. This cycle-consistent formulation integrates top-down structure with bottom-up evidence via a variational loop, enforcing mutual alignment between goal-conditioned latent predictions and recognition-based reconstructions. We formalize this mechanism within the framework of the Context-Content Uncertainty Principle (CCUP), which posits that inference proceeds by aligning structured, low-entropy content with high-entropy, ambiguous context. C 2 HM improves representational efficiency, supports memory chaining via path-dependent inference, and enables spatial compositional imagination. By offering a biologically inspired alternative to classical amortized inference, C 2 C^{2} HM reconceives generative modeling as intentional simulation, bridging memory-based planning and unsupervised learning in a unified probabilistic framework.

Introduction

Understanding how intelligent systems can simulate, plan, and infer latent causes from incomplete or ambiguous data remains a central challenge in both cognitive science and machine learning. The Helmholtz Machine (HM)
dayan1995helmholtz
;
hinton1995wake
was one of the earliest architectures to explicitly combine generative and recognition models in a biologically plausible framework for unsupervised learning. By alternating between a bottom-up recognition model and a top-down generative model, the HM approximates posterior inference through the
wake-sleep algorithm

hinton1995wake
. This procedure loosely resembles the complementary learning systems found in the brain
mcclelland1995there
.
Despite its conceptual significance, the classical HM suffers from a key limitation: its inference is
symmetric
and
data-driven
, relying exclusively on external observations to infer internal representations. This restricts its ability to generate and refine hypotheses in the absence of direct sensory input, limiting its applicability to tasks involving planning, imagination, or goal-conditioned behavior
pezzulo2014internally
;
yamins2016using
. Moreover, the wake-sleep procedure offers limited support for temporal structure
hasson2015hierarchical
, compositional reasoning
lee2003hierarchical
;
hinton2023represent
, or the re-use of past inference trajectories
schacter2007cognitive
;
schacter2008episodic
.

At the heart of our approach is the
Context-Content Uncertainty Principle (CCUP)

li2025CCUP
, which posits that cognition proceeds by minimizing the conditional entropy between high-dimensional, ambiguous
context
and low-dimensional, structured
content
. In this view, inference is inherently asymmetric: it begins from abstract, low-entropy content (e.g., goals or intentions) and expands into plausible contextual realizations. The inverted inference operationalizes this principle by treating generative inference as a structure-before-specificity (SbS) process, enabling more efficient search for breaking the curse of dimensionality
bellman1966dynamic
.
Under the CCUP framework, we introduce the
cycle-consistent Helmholtz Machine
(C
2
HM), which integrates the classical HM with its inverted counterpart in a closed inference loop, conceptually similar to the “half-step down” trick in Q-learning
watkins1992q
.

Specifically, consider a standard inference flow (e.g., autoencoder
kingma2013auto
)
\Psi\xrightarrow{\text{encoder}}Z\xrightarrow{\text{decoder}}\hat{\Psi}
. Under the framework of CCUP, we first construct a complete cycle by distinguishing
\Phi
from
\Psi
- namely,
\Psi\rightarrow Z\rightarrow\Phi\rightarrow Z^{\prime}\rightarrow\Psi^{\prime}
where encoder and decoder correspond to the forward (
\Psi\rightarrow\Phi
) and inverse (
\Phi\rightarrow\Psi^{\prime}
) mappings, respectively. Inverted inference implements a “half-cycle right” strategy by initiating inference with a structured content prior, consistent with the SbS principle. That is, the original cycle is shifted to the right by half-cycle, which becomes
\Phi\rightarrow Z\rightarrow\Psi\rightarrow Z^{\prime}\rightarrow\Phi^{\prime}
(inverted inference). The trick is that we do not need to complete the full cycle (the right half
\Psi\rightarrow\Phi^{\prime}
) but use the forward sweep
\Phi\rightarrow\Psi
to refine the content representation by predictive coding
keller2018predictive
, which saves computation by local updates only (i.e., to update
\Phi
from the error in
\Psi
).
This iterative structure encourages alignment between the recognition and generative paths, enabling self-correcting inference without full posterior inference. As a result, C
2
HM supports more robust generalization and rapid convergence, particularly in tasks requiring recursive refinement or reasoning under uncertainty.

The key innovation underlying
C^{2}
HM is to reframe inference as a
goal-seeded simulation
process. Instead of inferring latent causes from sensory inputs, the
C^{2}
HM begins with internally specified
goals
drawn from a prior distribution
p(\Phi)
as dictated by the SbS principle and simulates plausible latent structures
q(Z|\Phi)
that support their realization. These latent causes are then decoded into observations through the generative model
p(\Psi|Z)
, and the entire cycle is refined via a variational objective that enforces consistency between simulated and reconstructed inferences. This inversion of the inference in
C^{2}
HM architecture draws inspiration from recent advances in goal-conditioned inference
ha2018world
;
ghosh2021learning
, planning-as-inference
attias2003planning
;
botvinick2020deep
, and the predictive processing framework in cognitive neuroscience
friston2010free
but generalizes it into a more versatile principle in inference - namely, it turns global inference into local simulation
rao1999predictive
and error propagation
rumelhart1985learning
, reducing cost from exponential to first-order structure-preserving updates.

The
C^{2}
HM formalizes a unified framework in which inference and learning are driven by recursive bootstrapping, entropy minimization, and structure-guided simulation. By initiating inference from structured goals
\Phi
, the model generates predictions over context
\Psi
via a latent bottleneck
Z
, and refines
\Phi
through amortized reconstruction, implementing a half-cycle right update loop. We prove that this process defines a contractive operator under mild regularity conditions, converging to a fixed point that aligns simulated and observed contexts. The bootstrapping principle is captured formally as convergence to a self-consistent latent representation under surrogate refinement
sutton1998reinforcement
. To regulate inference direction and specificity,
C^{2}
HM employs
entropy steering
, minimizing the conditional uncertainty
H(\Psi|Z)+H(Z|\Phi)
, and enforces predictive compression through a
variational bottleneck
objective

richards1995theory
in latent space, establishing
C^{2}
HM as a goal-seeded, entropy-driven model of recursive structure formation and inference.

The main contributions of this work are:

•

We propose the Cycle-Consistent Helmholtz Machine, a new architecture that inverts the classical recognition-generative pathway by initiating inference from goal states.

By constraining the flow of information through a latent variable, this bottleneck forces the encoder to compress noisy, high-entropy context into a low-dimensional, generalizable latent structure.

•

Our experimental results justify the superiority of

2
Motivation: Why Cycle-Consistency?

2.1
The Classical Helmholtz Machine

The Helmholtz Machine (HM)
dayan1995helmholtz
;
hinton1995wake
is a deep generative model designed to learn hierarchical representations of high-dimensional data in an unsupervised manner. It consists of two networks:
1) a
recognition model

q(Z|\Psi)
, which infers a distribution over latent variables
Z
given observed context
\Psi
; 2) a
generative model

p(\Psi|Z)
, which reconstructs observations
\Psi
from latent causes
Z
.
These models are typically implemented as directed, layered neural networks with a prior
p(Z)
over latent variables.
Training the HM is performed using the
wake-sleep algorithm

hinton1995wake
, which alternates between two phases:
1)
Wake phase:
Given an observation
\Psi
, the recognition model samples
Z\sim q(Z|\Psi)
, and the generative model is updated to maximize the likelihood of
\Psi
under
p(\Psi|Z)
;
2)
Sleep phase:
The generative model samples
Z\sim p(Z)
and generates pseudo-data
\Psi\sim p(\Psi|Z)
, which are then used to update the recognition model by maximizing the likelihood of
Z
under
q(Z|\Psi)
.
This approach circumvents the intractability of computing the exact posterior
p(Z|\Psi)
by using a tractable recognition model
q(Z|\Psi)
to approximate it. Although this approximation is not variational in the strict sense, the wake-sleep algorithm can be interpreted as minimizing a combination of forward and reverse Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergences between true and approximate posteriors across the two phases
dayan1996varieties
.

While the Helmholtz Machine introduced a compelling architecture for unsupervised learning, it has several notable limitations when viewed from the perspective of goal-directed inference and temporal reasoning:

1. Symmetry of inference.
The HM treats inference as a symmetric process between the bottom-up (recognition) and top-down (generative) pathways. However, this overlooks the inherent
directionality
of many cognitive processes, such as planning, imagination, and action selection, which begin from internally generated states rather than external observations
seth2013interoceptive
.

2. Absence of goals or intentions.
The classical HM is driven entirely by sensory data. It cannot simulate latent causes conditional on abstract goals or desired outcomes. This restricts its use in settings that require flexible, anticipatory modeling, such as generating counterfactuals or preparing for future states
pearl2009causality
.

3. No temporal chaining.
Inference in the HM is typically performed on static datapoints. The model lacks mechanisms for chaining inferences over time or maintaining memory of past cycles. This limits its application to sequential decision-making
littman1996algorithms
or hierarchical planning
mohr2018ml
tasks.

4. No structure-before-specificity.
The HM assumes that detailed observations precede structure inference. In contrast, recent evidence from both neuroscience and AI suggests that efficient inference often begins from abstract, low-dimensional priors that constrain the space of plausible hypotheses
friston2010free
;
botvinick2020deep
.

2.2
Context-Content Uncertainty Principle (CCUP)

We propose the
Context-Content Uncertainty Principle (CCUP)
as a foundational formulation of this broken symmetry
anderson1972more
. CCUP states that optimal inference proceeds by minimizing the joint uncertainty between context
\Phi
and content
\Psi
, leveraging the fact that
H(\Psi)\ll H(\Phi)
. In this view, content functions as a structured prior that constrains and disambiguates context. This entropy differential induces a directional gradient over the inference landscape, leading to asymmetric flows, bootstrapping dynamics, and precision allocation strategies.

At the heart of the CCUP lies a simple but underappreciated observation:
in any inference process, the incoming data (context) is often more variable, ambiguous, and information-rich than the internal models (content) used to interpret it
. This mismatch in entropy creates an asymmetry in how inference must be structured. Whereas traditional models often treat perception or reasoning as a symmetric mapping from data to hypotheses
bengio2013representation
, CCUP reframes inference as a directional alignment problem, one in which stable, low-entropy content provides the scaffolding necessary to constrain and disambiguate high-entropy contextual input. This shift has profound implications. It implies that inference must begin not with the data alone, but with structure: learned priors, goal schemas, or generative templates that allow the system to efficiently reduce uncertainty. CCUP thus bridges variational inference, information theory, and cognitive models under a single unifying principle named “
Structure-before-Specificity
” (SbS)
friston2010free
: that minimizing uncertainty requires organizing inference around structured constraints before resolving context-specific details.

Theorem 1

(Structure-before-Specificity Principle)
.

Let
\Psi
denote high-entropy context variables and
\Phi
denote low-entropy content variables inferred under CCUP. Let
\mathcal{D}=\{\Psi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a set of observed contexts drawn from an environment. Then an optimal inference strategy proceeds in two stages:
1) Structure Formation (Generalization):
The agent seeks a latent structure
\Phi
that minimizes the expected conditional entropy across contexts:

\Phi^{\ast}=\arg\min_{\Phi}\mathbb{E}_{i}\left[H(\Psi_{i}|\Phi)\right]
, which yields a generalizable representation
\Phi^{\ast}
that captures invariant structure across variable examples;
2) Experience Binding (Specification):
Once
\Phi^{\ast}
is established, the agent performs context-specific inference by minimizing:

H(\Phi^{\ast}|\Psi_{j})
for each new example
\Psi_{j}\in\mathcal{D}_{\text{new}}
, effectively binding content to context using the learned structural prior.

The SbS principle posits that cognitive systems achieve generalization not by overfitting to raw observations, but by grounding interpretation in structured priors that constrain what can be inferred. This principle provides a
directional
guide for resolving the iBOT: instead of extracting structure from noisy input, the system begins with compact representations
\Phi
and unfolds them into context
\Psi
, selecting among possible instantiations in a manner consistent with both prior structure and observed ambiguity
bengio2013representation
. To operationalize this insight, we turn to
inverted inference
, a computational framework in which inference is seeded not by data, but by abstract structure, and proceeds through the generation and evaluation of context-conditioned hypotheses
friston2010action
. Inverted inference translates the SbS principle into an iterative, cycle-consistent process that uses generalization followed by specification to resolve uncertainty, dynamically aligning internal structure with external variability through recursive entropy minimization
clark2013whatever
.

2.3
From Half-step Down to Half-cycle Right

The temporal difference update rule inspires the formulation of inverted inference in Q-learning
watkins1992q
.
Both the half-step update in Q-learning and the generalization–specification loop in inverted inference rely on the strategy of
bootstrapping
: using current approximations to improve future estimates in the absence of full information, thereby enabling convergence toward optimal representations through recursive refinement. The brilliant idea behind celebrated Q-learning
watkins1992q
is to perform a one-step bootstrapped approximation, using a partial estimate of future value to update current action values
rather than computing the full trajectory to a reward. This “half-step down” idea bypasses the need for full expansion of the future, suggesting that forward search can be circumvented by local, structured inference. Q-learning sidesteps full forward search by grounding inference locally in transitions, using action-structured latent values instead of marginalizing over all futures. The trick is to bootstrap from the next state’s best action, avoiding the curse of dimensionality in planning trees
bellman1966dynamic
.

Just as Q-learning performs a half-step look ahead in time by using the next state’s value to update the current one, inverted inference can be interpreted as executing a
half-cycle right

\Phi
through latent
Z
to context
\Psi

\Psi\rightarrow Z\rightarrow\Phi\rightarrow Z^{\prime}\rightarrow\Psi^{\prime}
to the right by half-cycle:
\Phi\rightarrow Z\rightarrow\Psi\rightarrow Z^{\prime}\rightarrow\Phi^{\prime}
(inverted inference). The trick is that we do not need to complete the full cycle (the right half
\Psi\rightarrow\Phi^{\prime}
) but use the forward sweep
\Phi\rightarrow\Psi
to refine the content representation by predictive coding
keller2018predictive
, which saves computation by local updates only (i.e., to update
\Phi
from the error in
\Psi
), as shown in Fig.

3
Cycle-Consistent Helmholtz Machine

In this section, we introduce the architecture and learning objective of the Cycle-Consistent Helmholtz Machine (
C^{2}
HM). The
C^{2}
HM departs from the classical Helmholtz Machine by initiating inference from internal content/goal states, thereby transforming inference into an active, anticipatory simulation process
bar2009predictions
. Rather than conditioning latent structure on external observations, the
C^{2}
HM generates plausible latent causes from a structured distribution and reconstructs observations through a generative pathway. This process is refined through a predictive coding-based cycle-consistent learning objective that aligns simulated latents with reconstructed ones.

3.1
Model Components and Inference Process

The standard inference cycle in the classical HM proceeds as follows:
1) Sample the context distribution
\Psi\sim p(\Psi)
;
2) Infer latent structure
Z\sim q(Z|\Psi)
;
3) Generate structured content
\Phi\sim p(\Phi|Z)
;
4) Re-generate latent structure
\hat{Z}\sim q(Z|\Phi)
;
5) Generate synthetic context
\hat{\Psi}\sim P(\hat{\Psi}|\hat{Z})
.
This cycle reflects the core intuition of cycle-consistent inference, such as the wake-sleep algorithm
hinton1995wake
: initiating from an external, high-entropy observation, the system first maps it to a latent structure and then expands into a structured latent representation, which consists of the encoder part. The decoder reconstructs the contextual observation by investing the mappings. Such conventional forward simulation approaches to inference proceed from an initial state
\Psi_{0}
and expand outward through all possible action sequences toward a goal state
\Psi_{\text{goal}}
. This process scales poorly with increasing depth and branching factor, succumbing to the curse of dimensionality
bellman1966dynamic
.

The idea of moving a “half-step down” the expansion tree in Q-learning
watkins1992q
offers a powerful bootstrapping strategy for breaking the curse.
Analogous to the half-step down trick, the
C^{2}
HM inverts the inference operation based on the following design:

•

Content Prior

p(\Phi)
: A distribution over structured content variables, which may represent goals, semantic abstractions, or internal states.

•

Inverted Simulator

P(Z|\Phi)
: A content-conditioned recognition model that samples plausible latent structures
Z
capable of generating content
\Phi
.

•

Generative Model

P(\Psi|Z)
: A decoder (e.g., generative models) that maps latent structure
Z
to contextual observations
\Psi
.

•

Cycle Inference Model

Q(Z^{\prime}|\Psi)
: An auxiliary recognition model used to ensure consistency between generated latents and reconstructed context.

•

Cycle Reconstruction Model

Q(\hat{\Phi}|Z^{\prime})
: An auxiliary reconstruction model used to ensure consistency between generated and reconstructed content.

The inverted inference in the
C^{2}
HM, as shown in Fig.
1
, shifts the entire cycle to the right by half:
1) Sample the content distribution
\Phi\sim p(\Phi)
;
2) Infer latent structure
Z\sim q(Z|\Phi)
;
3) Generate synthetic context
\Psi\sim p(\Psi|Z)
;
4) Re-infer latent structure
\hat{Z}\sim q(Z|\Psi)
;
5) Update content variable
\hat{\Phi}\sim P(\hat{\Phi}|\hat{Z})
.
This cycle reflects the core intuition of cycle-consistent inverted inference (which one might call “‘sleep-wake” instead of “wake-sleep” algorithm): rather than simulating forward through all possible action sequences, the system starts from the goal and reasons backward by pruning the tree before it has grown, avoiding the combinatorial explosion or sidestepping the curse of dimensionality.

As shown in Fig.
1
, inverted inference implements the above intuition by creating a shortcut: we do not need to complete the full cycle (the right half
\Psi\rightarrow\Phi^{\prime}
) but use the forward sweep
\Phi\rightarrow\Psi
to refine the content representation by predictive coding
keller2018predictive
, which saves computation by local updates only. This shift transforms inference from an exhaustive, trajectory-centric search in high-dimensional context space into a constraint-driven refinement process in structured latent space. As in Q-learning, where the update avoids evaluating full future branches, inverted inference approximates global consistency by goal-seeded simulation
wayne2018unsupervised
, which enforces local cycle-consistency (i.e.,
\Phi\approx\hat{\Phi}
) and uses prediction error as a surrogate for full posterior integration. This structure-first, goal-seeded simulation not only breaks the curse of dimensionality but also enables efficient generalization, compositional planning, and temporally abstracted memory reuse, as we will elaborate next.

3.2
Half-Cycle Right Inference via Predictive Coding

watkins1992q

Both the half-step update in Q-learning
sutton1998reinforcement
and the generalization–specification loop in inverted inference rely on the strategy of
bootstrapping

bennett2023brief
: using current approximations to improve future estimates in the absence of full information, thereby enabling convergence toward optimal representations through recursive refinement
lake2017building
. The brilliant idea behind celebrated Q-learning
watkins1992q
is to perform a one-step bootstrapped approximation, using a partial estimate of future value to update current action values

.

half-cycle right

\Phi
through latent
Z

\Psi
, bootstrapping structural refinement by simulating plausible instantiations without requiring full cycle closure. Specifically, we shift the cycle formation from the original (standard inference)
\Psi\rightarrow Z\rightarrow\Phi\rightarrow Z^{\prime}\rightarrow\Psi^{\prime}
to the right by half-cycle:
\Phi\rightarrow Z\rightarrow\Psi\rightarrow Z^{\prime}\rightarrow\Phi^{\prime}

Formally, we have

Lemma 1

(Predictive Coding as a Half-Cycle Right Refinement)
.

Let
\Phi\in\mathcal{S}
denote structured content, and let
\Psi\in\mathcal{C}
be contextual observations. Consider the latent variable
Z\in\mathcal{Z}
mediating the generative path from structure to context:

p(Z|\Phi),\quad p(\Psi|Z)
.
Define the forward generative prediction:

\hat{\Psi}=\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim p(Z|\Phi)}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\Psi\sim p(\Psi|Z)}[\Psi]\right]
,
and define the backward recognition refinement via amortized inference:

\hat{Z}=q(Z|\Psi),\quad\Phi^{\prime}=q(\Phi|\hat{Z})
.
Assume the update
\Phi\to\Phi^{\prime}
minimizes a context-aligned prediction error:

\Phi^{\prime}=\arg\min_{\Phi}D(\Psi\,\|\,\hat{\Psi}(\Phi))
,
where
D
is a divergence metric (e.g., KL or squared error).
Then the half-cycle operator:

\Phi\xrightarrow{p(Z|\Phi)\rightarrow p(\Psi|Z)}\hat{\Psi}\xrightarrow{q(Z|\hat{\Psi})\rightarrow q(\Phi^{\prime}|Z)}\Phi^{\prime}

is equivalent to a predictive coding update that refines
\Phi
by descending the gradient of prediction error on
\Psi
.

The preceding lemma formalizes predictive coding as a half-cycle right refinement process, in which structured content
\Phi
is updated through a generative–recognition loop grounded in prediction error minimization. This mechanism implements a local update rule that incrementally aligns simulated context
\hat{\Psi}
with observations
\Psi
, effectively transforming forward simulation into backward structural inference. Importantly, this half-cycle framework avoids the computational burden of globally inverting high-dimensional posteriors by leveraging structured priors and amortized recognition
lecun2022path
. We now extend this insight to a global setting, showing that repeated application of half-cycle inference can break the curse of dimensionality by projecting high-dimensional contexts
\Psi\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
onto low-dimensional structured representations
\Phi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
d\ll D
. Under appropriate assumptions on entropy descent and architectural regularity, this cycle-consistent bootstrapping procedure converges to a predictive fixed point
\Phi^{\ast}

granas2003fixed
that preserves contextual information with dramatically reduced inference complexity. The following theorem formalizes this reduction in computational cost and inference dimensionality.

Theorem 2

(Breaking the Curse of Dimensionality via Half-Cycle Right Inference)
.

Let
\Psi\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
denote a high-dimensional context variable, and let
\Phi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
denote a structured latent representation with
d\ll D
. Define a latent bottleneck variable
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
with
d\leq k\ll D
, and consider a cycle-consistent inference scheme:

\Phi^{(t)}\xrightarrow{p(Z|\Phi)}Z^{(t)}\xrightarrow{p(\Psi|Z)}\hat{\Psi}^{(t)}\xrightarrow{q(Z|\hat{\Psi})}\hat{Z}^{(t)}\xrightarrow{q(\Phi|\hat{Z})}\Phi^{(t+1)}
.
Assume 1) The joint model
p(\Psi,Z,\Phi)
factorizes through low-dimensional conditional distributions; 2) The update
\Phi^{(t+1)}=\mathcal{F}(\Phi^{(t)},\hat{\Psi}^{(t)})
is entropy-decreasing:
H(\Psi|\Phi^{(t+1)})<H(\Psi|\Phi^{(t)})
;
3) The reconstruction steps
q(Z|\hat{\Psi})
,
q(\Phi|\hat{Z})
are amortized and Lipschitz-continuous, then the inference process converges to a structured representation
\Phi^{\ast}
that achieves predictive sufficiency:
H(\Psi|\Phi^{\ast})\approx\min_{\Phi}H(\Psi|\Phi)
by
avoiding direct marginalization over
\Psi\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
and reducing inference complexity from
\mathcal{O}(\exp(D))
to
\mathcal{O}(k^{2})
under structured priors and amortization.

As an application of Theorem
2
, we use two classical examples in spatial and visual inference to illustrate how half-cycle inference seeded from goal structure can break the curse of dimensionality by replacing forward trajectory expansion with predictive refinement guided by internal priors. Fig.
2
contains the simulation results for two scenarios of goal-seeded planning with obstacle avoidance
glasius1995neural
. In both situations, we can observe how inverted inference successfully identifies the shortest path from the start to the goal.

Example 1

(Goal-Seeded Model-based Planning in a Spatial Grid)
.

Consider an agent navigating a
32\times 32
grid world, where the raw context
\Psi\in\mathbb{R}^{1024}
encodes the full environment state (e.g., obstacle map, sensory input). The agent’s goal is to infer a path to a destination using an internal latent representation
\Phi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\ll 1024
, e.g., a low-dimensional code encoding goal location and path direction.
Let
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
(with
d\leq k\ll D
) denote a structured latent trajectory embedding. The inference process proceeds as follows:
1) From the goal-encoded latent
\Phi^{(t)}
, simulate possible action trajectories
Z^{(t)}\sim p(Z\mid\Phi^{(t)})
;
2) Decode
Z^{(t)}
into predicted environmental states
\hat{\Psi}^{(t)}\sim p(\Psi\mid Z^{(t)})
, e.g., simulate map states;
3) Use amortized recognition
\hat{Z}^{(t)}\sim q(Z\mid\hat{\Psi}^{(t)})
and

Theoretical Implications and Connections

In this section, we analyze the theoretical implications of the

4.1
Bootstrapping as Meta-Strategy for Inference and Learning

Theorem
2
demonstrates how cycle-consistent, half-cycle right inference can dramatically reduce the complexity of high-dimensional inference problems by projecting context onto structured, low-dimensional representations through amortized and entropy-aligned updates. This result highlights a broader pattern: the underlying mechanism, iterative refinement via structure-conditioned prediction and recognition
hinton1995wake
, is not specific to any one domain but reflects a general computational strategy. In particular, the update rule
\Phi^{(t+1)}=\mathcal{F}(\Phi^{(t)},\hat{\Psi}^{(t)})
exemplifies a bootstrapping architecture in which the system uses its current estimate to simulate outcomes, then refines itself based on the discrepancy
sutton1998reinforcement
. Viewed abstractly, this recursive process defines a contractive operator over a compact space of representations, with a fixed point corresponding to an optimal, self-consistent latent structure. The following principle formalizes this fundamental insight:
bootstrapping provides a unifying template for learning and inference as convergence to a self-consistent prediction under a surrogate refinement loop
.

Proposition 1

(Bootstrapping as a Unifying Principle of Learning and Inference)
.

Let
V_{t}\in\mathcal{X}
be an evolving representation at iteration
t
, where
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a compact, convex space. Suppose the update rule takes the bootstrapped form:

V_{t+1}=\mathcal{F}(V_{t},\widetilde{E}(V_{t}))
, where
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}
is a deterministic update operator,
\widetilde{E}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
is a surrogate or predictive operator and
\mathcal{F}
is contractive in its first argument: there exists
0<\gamma<1
such that

\|\mathcal{F}(x_{1},y)-\mathcal{F}(x_{2},y)\|\leq\gamma\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|\quad\text{for all }x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathcal{X},y\in\mathcal{Y}
.
Then the sequence
\{V_{t}\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
converges to a unique fixed point
V^{\ast}\in\mathcal{X}
, satisfying:
V^{\ast}=\mathcal{F}(V^{\ast},\widetilde{E}(V^{\ast}))
.

Remark:
An example of
\widetilde{E}(V_{t})
is a partial or simulated expectation derived from
V_{t}
itself (e.g., a predicted reward in Q-learning or a sampled context in inverted inference), and
\mathcal{F}
is a refinement operator (e.g., Bellman update or entropy minimization). The bootstrapping principle unifies reinforcement learning
sutton1998reinforcement
and structure-guided inference
Lake2017
as recursive fixed-point processes, where present estimates guide future refinements through partially informed simulation or projection. Bootstrapping, including half-step down and half-cycle right tricks, is computationally efficient because it turns global inference into local simulation
rao1999predictive
and error propagation
rumelhart1985learning
, reducing cost from exponential to first-order structure-preserving updates (i.e., no need for global marginalization like in the search problem
minsky1961steps
).

The general proposition above captures a unifying pattern in learning and inference systems: the convergence of internal representations through recursive refinement driven by surrogate estimates. This bootstrapping mechanism, which iteratively aligns structure with prediction, underlies a broad class of algorithms ranging from reinforcement learning to variational inference. In particular, the C
2
HM instantiates this principle by interpreting structured content
\Phi
as a goal-seeded representation

that generates predictions over context
\Psi
via a latent bottleneck
Z
. These predictions are then used to refine
\Phi
through amortized recognition, forming a self-correcting loop that aligns structure with context over time. The following corollary formalizes C

Corollary 1

(C

.

Let
\Phi^{(t)}\in\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the structured latent representation at iteration

in a Cycle-Consistent Helmholtz Machine. Define the bootstrapped update:

\Phi^{(t+1)}=\mathcal{F}(\Phi^{(t)},\hat{\Psi}^{(t)}),\quad\text{with}\quad\hat{\Psi}^{(t)}:=\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim p(Z|\Phi^{(t)})}[\,\mathbb{E}_{\Psi\sim p(\Psi|Z)}[\Psi]\,]

where

is an amortized inference operator defined as:

\mathcal{F}(\Phi,\hat{\Psi})=q\big(\Phi\,\big|\,q(Z\,|\,\hat{\Psi})\big)

and

is a compact, convex set. Assume:
1)
\mathcal{F}
is contractive in
\Phi

0<\gamma<1
such that

\|\mathcal{F}(\Phi_{1},\hat{\Psi})-\mathcal{F}(\Phi_{2},\hat{\Psi})\|\leq\gamma\|\Phi_{1}-\Phi_{2}\|,\quad\forall\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}\in\mathcal{S},\hat{\Psi}\in\mathcal{C}

and 2)
p(Z|\Phi)
,
p(\Psi|Z)
,
q(Z|\Psi)
, and
q(\Phi|Z)
are Lipschitz-continuous.
Then the sequence
\{\Phi^{(t)}\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
converges to a unique fixed point

satisfying:

\Phi^{\ast}=\mathcal{F}(\Phi^{\ast},\hat{\Psi}^{\ast}),\quad\text{with}\quad\hat{\Psi}^{\ast}=\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim p(Z|\Phi^{\ast})}[\mathbb{E}_{\Psi\sim p(\Psi|Z)}[\Psi]]

Entropy Steering and Variational Bottlenecking

A key innovation in the
C
2
HM
architecture is its use of
entropy steering
to regulate the direction and specificity of inference
alemi2016deep
. Unlike classical generative models that treat latent variables as passive intermediates, the
C
2
HM
treats structure
\Phi
, latent code
Z
, and context
\Psi
as dynamically entangled through entropy gradients
rezende2015variational
. This allows the system to adaptively steer inference away from ambiguous, underdetermined paths and toward compact, predictable representations
bengio2013representation
.

Let the generative model follow the structured path
\Phi\rightarrow Z\rightarrow\Psi
, with amortized inverse inference proceeding as
\Psi\rightarrow Z\rightarrow\Phi
. Entropy steering is implemented by minimizing the total conditional uncertainty
grandvalet2004semi
:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{steer}}=H(\Psi|Z)+H(Z|\Phi)
,
subject to a variational bottleneck constraint that penalizes deviation from cycle-consistency. The resulting half-cycle update

\Phi^{(t)}\rightarrow Z^{(t)}\rightarrow\hat{\Psi}^{(t)}\rightarrow\hat{Z}^{(t)}\rightarrow\Phi^{(t+1)}

reduces context-conditioned entropy at each step:

H(\Psi|\Phi^{(t+1)})<H(\Psi|\Phi^{(t)})
.
To enforce this, we introduce a variational bottleneck objective
tishby2000information
:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{vb}}=\mathbb{E}_{\Phi}\left[\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim q(Z|\Phi)}[-\log p(\Psi|Z)]}_{\text{reconstruction}}+\lambda D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q(Z|\Phi)\|p(Z))\right]
,
which encourages
Z
to capture only the task-relevant information needed for accurate prediction, while regularizing its capacity via the KL term. By constraining the flow of information through
Z
, this bottleneck forces the encoder to compress noisy, high-entropy context into low-dimensional, generalizable latent structure
tishby2000information
.
Together, entropy steering and variational bottlenecking ensure that the learning dynamics of the
C
2
HM
favor structure-first representations that are predictive, compressive, and cycle-consistent. This mechanism breaks the curse of dimensionality by aligning high-dimensional prediction with low-dimensional abstraction and bootstrapping representations that generalize across contexts.

The cycle-consistent Helmholtz Machine (C
2
HM) refines internal structure through a half-cycle right inference loop. To formalize its convergence properties, we introduce a variational bottleneck objective
alemi2016deep
that jointly minimizes predictive error and encourages information compression.
Let
\Phi\in\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
denote structured content,
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
an intermediate latent code, and
\Psi\in\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{D}
a high-dimensional context. We define the generative path:

\Phi\xrightarrow{p(Z|\Phi)}Z\xrightarrow{p(\Psi|Z)}\hat{\Psi}

and the recognition path:

\hat{\Psi}\xrightarrow{q(Z|\hat{\Psi})}\hat{Z}\xrightarrow{q(\Phi^{\prime}|\hat{Z})}\Phi^{\prime}
.
The learning objective consists of two components:
1) A reconstruction loss
\mathcal{L}_{\text{rec}}:=\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim p(Z|\Phi)}[-\log p(\Psi|Z)]
, which enforces that the structure-conditioned prediction matches observed context;
2) A variational bottleneck penalty
\mathcal{L}_{\text{KL}}:=\beta\,\mathrm{KL}(p(Z|\Phi)\,\|\,r(Z))
, which encourages compression of structure through a low-entropy latent representation, with
r(Z)
as a reference prior (e.g., isotropic Gaussian).
The total objective is:

\mathcal{L}(\Phi)=\mathcal{L}_{\text{rec}}+\mathcal{L}_{\text{KL}}
.
This variational bottleneck ensures that updates to
\Phi
are both predictive and compressive
tishby2011information
. Each iteration of the cycle refines
\Phi
in the direction that minimizes uncertainty about the observed context while limiting the complexity of latent codes
tishby2015deep
. Under appropriate assumptions, this refinement leads to convergence to a delta-like attractor, as formalized in the following corollary.

Corollary 2

(Delta Convergence via Variational Bottlenecking)
.

Let the update rule for structured content
\Phi^{(t)}
follow the objective:

\Phi^{(t+1)}=\arg\min_{\Phi}\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim p(Z|\Phi)}[-\log p(\Psi|Z)]+\beta\,\mathrm{KL}(p(Z|\Phi)\,\|\,r(Z))
,
where
\beta>0
and
r(Z)
is a fixed prior. Assume:
1) The reconstruction model
p(\Psi|Z)
is Lipschitz-continuous and bounded;
2) The latent encoder
p(Z|\Phi)
is continuous in
\Phi
, and the KL term is strictly convex;
3) The structured latent space
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is compact and convex,
Then the sequence
\{\Phi^{(t)}\}
converges to a unique minimizer
\Phi^{*}\in\mathcal{S}
, and the induced distribution
p(Z|\Phi^{(t)})
converges weakly to a delta distribution:

\lim_{t\to\infty}p(Z|\Phi^{(t)})=\delta(Z-Z^{*})
,
for some
Z^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Consequently, the structural code
\Phi^{*}
supports minimal reconstruction error and maximal compression, satisfying the generalization-optimal condition under entropy minimization.

The above corollary formalizes how variational bottlenecking drives the emergence of delta-like latent codes, representations that are both highly specific and maximally compressed. Such convergence under entropy minimization provides a principled explanation for the stabilization of memory traces, goal representations, and perceptual invariants in biological systems. Crucially, this delta convergence mechanism has concrete manifestations in both healthy and pathological cognition. In the healthy brain, it explains how localized, high-fidelity neural codes such as place cells
moser2008place
emerge from high-dimensional sensorimotor input through repeated predictive refinement. Conversely, in pathological conditions such as Alzheimer’s Disease
canter2016road

Conclusion

This paper introduced the
Cycle-Consistent Helmholtz Machine
(
C^{2}
HM), a principled extension of classical generative models that reorients the flow of inference: instead of passively extracting latent causes from data,
C^{2}
HM initiates inference from abstract goals and actively simulates structured latent variables that generate plausible observations. This goal-seeded inference loop is recursively refined via a cycle-consistent variational objective, enabling the system to align internal structure with external variability through bidirectional information flow.

At the heart of this framework lies the principle that
reflects the fundamental tradeoff between context and content uncertainty
, which generalizes Heisenberg’s uncertainty principle from quantum mechanics to neural computation. The theoretical foundation of
C^{2}
HM builds on inverted inference or half-cycle right, a trick inspired by the half-step down in Q-learning. This inversion bypasses the high-entropy bottlenecks of traditional marginalization by enforcing predictive cycles that minimize context-content uncertainty through entropy steering.

By integrating spatial and temporal bootstrapping, the
C^{2}
HM enables multiscale reasoning through recursive refinement of latent structure. Temporally, it captures memory-like trajectories via episodic chaining; spatially, it supports hierarchical abstraction through compositional latent codes. These dynamics emerge from two core mechanisms: bootstrapping as a meta-strategy for learning and inference, wherein representations converge via contractive surrogate-guided updates; and entropy steering via variational bottlenecking, which compresses high-entropy context into predictive, low-dimensional structure. Together, these principles ensure that information flows continuously through the inference loop, refined, filtered, and aligned, until internal models stabilize into coherent, goal-consistent representations.

Together, these advances position
C^{2}
HM as a unified framework for structure-first learning, capable of integrating memory, planning, and abstraction under the imperative that
information must flow
. Future work will explore its extension to active inference, continual learning, and hierarchical generative modeling in dynamic, cognitively demanding environments.

Appendix A
Proof of Theorem
1

Proof.

We aim to show that under CCUP, minimizing
H(\Psi|\Phi)
to construct a generalizable structure
\Phi
prior to minimizing
H(\Phi|\Psi)
for content inference leads to lower inference error and more stable representations.

Step 1: Entropic Asymmetry under CCUP.

By CCUP, context variables
\Psi
are high-entropy and ambiguous, while content variables
\Phi
are lower-entropy and selectively encoded. That is,

H(\Psi)\gg H(\Phi)
,
which implies:

H(\Phi|\Psi)>H(\Psi|\Phi)
.
From the definition of mutual information:

I(\Psi;\Phi)=H(\Psi)-H(\Psi|\Phi)=H(\Phi)-H(\Phi|\Psi)
,
it follows that minimizing
H(\Psi|\Phi)
yields greater gains in mutual information when
\Phi
is still underdetermined.

Step 2: Generalizable Structure Minimizes Cross-Context Variance.

Let
\Phi^{\ast}
be the representation that minimizes expected context entropy across examples:

\Phi^{\ast}=\arg\min_{\Phi}\mathbb{E}_{i=1}^{N}\left[H(\Psi_{i}|\Phi)\right]
.
This optimization extracts invariant latent structure across variable contexts. For any new context
\Psi_{j}
, this structural prior ensures that:

\mathbb{E}_{j}\left[H(\Psi_{j}|\Phi^{\ast})\right]<\mathbb{E}_{j}\left[H(\Psi_{j}|\Phi_{\text{early}})\right]
,
where
\Phi_{\text{early}}
is a context-specific representation inferred without prior structure, which is more prone to overfitting and aliasing.

Step 3: Specificity after Structure Minimizes Inference Error.

Let the expected inference loss be:

\mathcal{L}=\mathbb{E}_{j}\left[H(\Phi|\Psi_{j})\right]
.
By the chain rule of entropy:

H(\Phi|\Psi_{j})=H(\Phi|\Psi_{j},\Phi^{\ast})+I(\Phi;\Phi^{\ast}|\Psi_{j})
.
Since
\Phi^{\ast}
is a learned structural prior that compresses variability across contexts, it reduces the conditional entropy of
\Phi
given
\Psi_{j}
:

H(\Phi|\Psi_{j},\Phi^{\ast})<H(\Phi|\Psi_{j})
.
Therefore, conditioning inference on prior structure strictly improves specificity:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{with structure}}<\mathcal{L}_{\text{without structure}}
.

Conclusion.

Under CCUP, first minimizing
H(\Psi|\Phi)
enables generalization through structure formation, and only then minimizing
H(\Phi|\Psi)
enables robust, low-error specificity. Hence, structure must precede specificity for effective inference and memory.

Proof.

,
and a recognition model that proceeds via amortized inference:

\Psi\xrightarrow{q(Z|\Psi)}\hat{Z}\xrightarrow{q(\Phi|\hat{Z})}\Phi^{\prime}
.
Define the forward prediction:

\hat{\Psi}(\Phi):=\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim p(Z|\Phi)}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\Psi\sim p(\Psi|Z)}[\Psi]\right]
,
and let the updated structure be:

\Phi^{\prime}=\arg\min_{\Phi}D(\Psi\,\|\,\hat{\Psi}(\Phi))
,
where
D
is a convex divergence (e.g., KL or squared loss).
We now show that the composite operator

\mathcal{T}_{\text{half-cycle}}(\Phi):=q\left(\Phi\,|\,q\left(Z\,|\,\hat{\Psi}(\Phi)\right)\right)

implements a descent step toward minimizing
D(\Psi\,\|\,\hat{\Psi}(\Phi))
, i.e., predictive coding.
Let us compute the gradient of the prediction error:

\nabla_{\Phi}D(\Psi\,\|\,\hat{\Psi}(\Phi))=-\left\langle\nabla_{\Phi}\hat{\Psi}(\Phi),\,\nabla_{\hat{\Psi}}D(\Psi\,\|\,\hat{\Psi})\right\rangle
.

Assume
\hat{\Psi}
is differentiable with respect to
\Phi
, and the amortized inference map
q(Z|\hat{\Psi})
is a differentiable encoder. Then we can propagate gradients through the generative chain using the reparameterization trick:

\nabla_{\Phi}\hat{\Psi}(\Phi)=\mathbb{E}_{Z\sim p(Z|\Phi)}\left[\nabla_{Z}\mathbb{E}_{\Psi\sim p(\Psi|Z)}[\Psi]\cdot\nabla_{\Phi}Z\right]
.

Since the recognition model
q(\Phi|Z)
is trained to approximate the inverse of the generative path, we define a surrogate update:

\Phi^{\prime}=q\left(\Phi\,\middle|\,q\left(Z\,\middle|\,\hat{\Psi}(\Phi)\right)\right)\approx\Phi-\eta\nabla_{\Phi}D(\Psi\,\|\,\hat{\Psi}(\Phi))
,
where
\eta>0
is a learning rate.
Hence, the operator
\Phi\mapsto\Phi^{\prime}
approximates a gradient step to minimize predictive error between the actual context
\Psi
and the predicted context
\hat{\Psi}(\Phi)
, which is the essence of predictive coding:

\Phi^{\prime}=\Phi-\eta\nabla_{\Phi}D(\Psi\,\|\,\hat{\Psi}(\Phi))+\mathcal{O}(\eta^{2})
.

Therefore, the half-cycle right procedure

\Phi\to\hat{\Psi}\to\hat{Z}\to\Phi^{\prime}

performs a recursive refinement of latent structure that descends the prediction error gradient, establishing equivalence with predictive coding updates.

∎

Appendix C
Proof of Theorem
2

Proof.

We aim to show that the half-cycle right inference procedure described by the update

\Phi^{(t)}\xrightarrow{p(Z|\Phi)}Z^{(t)}\xrightarrow{p(\Psi|Z)}\hat{\Psi}^{(t)}\xrightarrow{q(Z|\hat{\Psi})}\hat{Z}^{(t)}\xrightarrow{q(\Phi|\hat{Z})}\Phi^{(t+1)}

converges to a structured representation
\Phi^{\ast}
that minimizes the predictive uncertainty
H(\Psi|\Phi)
, while reducing the computational cost of inference.

Step 1: Factorization and Dimension Reduction.

By assumption, the joint distribution factorizes as:

p(\Psi,Z,\Phi)=p(\Phi)p(Z|\Phi)p(\Psi|Z)
,
with each component involving conditional distributions over variables in lower-dimensional spaces:

\Phi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
d\ll D
; and

Z\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, where
d\leq k\ll D
.
This implies that the model can generate
\Psi\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
through a two-stage low-dimensional latent pathway:

\Phi\mapsto Z\mapsto\Psi
.
Thus, inference via
\Phi\mapsto Z\mapsto\Psi
avoids directly marginalizing over
\Psi\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
, reducing the effective complexity of inference from exponential in
D
to polynomial in
k
.

Step 2: Monotonic Decrease in Entropy.

By assumption, the inference update satisfies:

H(\Psi|\Phi^{(t+1)})<H(\Psi|\Phi^{(t)})
,
which implies that the sequence
\{H(\Psi|\Phi^{(t)})\}
is strictly decreasing and bounded below by zero. Hence, by the monotone convergence theorem, the sequence converges:

H(\Psi|\Phi^{(t)})\to H(\Psi|\Phi^{\ast})\quad\text{as }t\to\infty
.
Thus,
\Phi^{(t)}\to\Phi^{\ast}
in the space of structured representations, and
\Phi^{\ast}
minimizes conditional entropy.

Step 3: Convergence Under Lipschitz and Amortization Assumptions.

Let us define the update operator
\mathcal{F}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{D}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
via:

\Phi^{(t+1)}=\mathcal{F}(\Phi^{(t)},\hat{\Psi}^{(t)})
,
where
\hat{\Psi}^{(t)}\sim p(\Psi|Z^{(t)})
,
Z^{(t)}\sim p(Z|\Phi^{(t)})
. Since both
q(Z|\hat{\Psi})
and
q(\Phi|\hat{Z})
are assumed to be amortized and Lipschitz-continuous, and the composite operator
\mathcal{F}
is entropy-decreasing, Banach’s fixed-point theorem ensures that the iterative update converges to a unique fixed point
\Phi^{\ast}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Step 4: Computational Advantage.

The naive computation of
p(\Phi|\Psi)\propto p(\Psi|\Phi)p(\Phi)

\Psi\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
would require evaluating or integrating over an exponential number of configurations (e.g.,
\mathcal{O}(\exp(D))
). Instead, the proposed inference scheme propagates through
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
with amortized recognition and decoding. Assuming structured priors (e.g., Gaussian or autoregressive forms), the forward–backward latent updates can be computed in
\mathcal{O}(k^{2})
, which is exponentially more efficient for high-dimensional
D
.

Conclusion.

The cycle-consistent half-step inference path converges to a fixed point
\Phi^{\ast}
that minimizes
H(\Psi|\Phi)
and achieves approximate predictive sufficiency. By avoiding full inversion of
\Psi\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
and exploiting structured low-dimensional priors and amortized updates, the method breaks the curse of dimensionality by reducing computational complexity from
\mathcal{O}(\exp(D))
to
\mathcal{O}(k^{2})
.
∎

Appendix D
Proof of Proposition
1

Proof.

Let
V_{0}\in\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an initial point, and define the sequence
\{V_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
recursively by the update rule:

V_{t+1}=\mathcal{F}(V_{t},\widetilde{E}(V_{t}))
,
where
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}
and
\widetilde{E}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
are given. Assume
\mathcal{X}
is a compact, convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and that
\mathcal{F}
is contractive in its first argument. That is, for some constant
0<\gamma<1
, we have:

\|\mathcal{F}(x_{1},y)-\mathcal{F}(x_{2},y)\|\leq\gamma\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|,\quad\forall x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathcal{X},\,y\in\mathcal{Y}
.

Step 1: Define a composite map.

Define the composite operator:

\mathcal{T}(x):=\mathcal{F}(x,\widetilde{E}(x))
.
Then the iteration becomes:

V_{t+1}=\mathcal{T}(V_{t})
.

Step 2: Show that
\mathcal{T}
is a contraction.

Let
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathcal{X}
. Then:

\|\mathcal{T}(x_{1})-\mathcal{T}(x_{2})\|=\|\mathcal{F}(x_{1},\widetilde{E}(x_{1}))-\mathcal{F}(x_{2},\widetilde{E}(x_{2}))\|
.
We decompose this as:

\|\mathcal{F}(x_{1},\widetilde{E}(x_{1}))-\mathcal{F}(x_{2},\widetilde{E}(x_{2}))\|\leq\|\mathcal{F}(x_{1},\widetilde{E}(x_{1}))-\mathcal{F}(x_{2},\widetilde{E}(x_{1}))\|+\|\mathcal{F}(x_{2},\widetilde{E}(x_{1}))-\mathcal{F}(x_{2},\widetilde{E}(x_{2}))\|
.
The first term is bounded by the contractivity of
\mathcal{F}
in its first argument:

\|\mathcal{F}(x_{1},\widetilde{E}(x_{1}))-\mathcal{F}(x_{2},\widetilde{E}(x_{1}))\|\leq\gamma\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|
.
The second term is not necessarily contractive, but since
\mathcal{F}
maps into a compact set
\mathcal{X}
, it is Lipschitz continuous in its second argument by compactness of the domain and continuity (assuming
\mathcal{F}
is jointly continuous). Thus, there exists a constant
L>0
such that:

\|\mathcal{F}(x_{2},\widetilde{E}(x_{1}))-\mathcal{F}(x_{2},\widetilde{E}(x_{2}))\|\leq L\|\widetilde{E}(x_{1})-\widetilde{E}(x_{2})\|
.
So we cannot guarantee that
\mathcal{T}
is a contraction unless we impose Lipschitz continuity on
\widetilde{E}
. To ensure convergence, we instead observe the sequence
\{V_{t}\}
is Cauchy.

Step 3: Show that
\{V_{t}\}
is a Cauchy sequence.

Assume that the second term is uniformly bounded:

\|\mathcal{F}(x,y_{1})-\mathcal{F}(x,y_{2})\|\leq C\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|\quad\text{and}\quad\|\widetilde{E}(x_{1})-\widetilde{E}(x_{2})\|\leq L_{E}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|
.
Then combining:

\|\mathcal{T}(x_{1})-\mathcal{T}(x_{2})\|\leq\gamma\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|+CL_{E}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|=(\gamma+CL_{E})\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|
.
If
\gamma+CL_{E}<1
, then
\mathcal{T}
is a contraction.

Step 4: Apply Banach Fixed-Point Theorem.

Since
\mathcal{X}
is a closed, bounded, convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\mathcal{T}
is a contraction on
\mathcal{X}
, Banach’s fixed-point theorem guarantees:

- There exists a unique fixed point
V^{\ast}\in\mathcal{X}
such that:

V^{\ast}=\mathcal{F}(V^{\ast},\widetilde{E}(V^{\ast}))
.

- The sequence
\{V_{t}\}
defined by
V_{t+1}=\mathcal{F}(V_{t},\widetilde{E}(V_{t}))
converges to
V^{\ast}
.

∎

\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

We assume:

- The first term (expected negative log-likelihood) is bounded and Lipschitz-continuous in
\Phi
, since:
-
p(\Psi|Z)
is Lipschitz-continuous and bounded,
-
p(Z|\Phi)
is continuous in
\Phi
.

- The second term (KL divergence) is strictly convex in
p(Z|\Phi)
, and by continuity of
p(Z|\Phi)
in
\Phi
, it induces a strictly convex functional in
\Phi
.

Hence, the total objective
\mathcal{L}(\Phi)
is continuous and strictly convex on the compact convex domain
\mathcal{S}
, which ensures:
- The existence of a unique minimizer
\Phi^{*}\in\mathcal{S}
, satisfying:

\Phi^{*}=\arg\min_{\Phi\in\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{L}(\Phi)
.

Step 2: Convergence of the Update Sequence.

The update rule
\Phi^{(t+1)}=\arg\min_{\Phi}\mathcal{L}(\Phi)
defines a deterministic descent method under a fixed loss function. Since
\mathcal{L}
is strictly convex and the optimization is exact, the sequence
\{\Phi^{(t)}\}
converges to the unique minimizer
\Phi^{*}\in\mathcal{S}
.

Step 3: Delta Convergence of the Latent Distribution.

Now consider the induced posterior
p(Z|\Phi^{(t)})
. As
\Phi^{(t)}\to\Phi^{*}
, continuity implies:

p(Z|\Phi^{(t)})\xrightarrow{t\to\infty}p(Z|\Phi^{*})
.
Let us analyze the limit behavior of the KL term:

\mathrm{KL}(p(Z|\Phi)\|r(Z))
.
We are minimizing a trade-off between the reconstruction term and the compression term. For large
\beta
, the KL divergence is heavily penalized, and the optimal
p(Z|\Phi^{*})
will be pushed toward low-entropy forms to reduce divergence from the prior. Under strict convexity and boundedness assumptions, and because the minimization is over distributions of fixed support but increasing peakedness, the sequence
\{p(Z|\Phi^{(t)})\}
converges weakly to a point mass:

\lim_{t\to\infty}p(Z|\Phi^{(t)})=\delta(Z-Z^{*})
,
for some latent value
Z^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
that balances reconstruction and compression optimally. This is a standard result from the theory of variational compression and is aligned with known delta convergence properties of ELBO optimization under strong bottleneck pressure (see
[

,

]
).

Conclusion.

The sequence
\{\Phi^{(t)}\}
converges to a unique minimizer
\Phi^{*}\in\mathcal{S}
, and the induced distribution
p(Z|\Phi^{(t)})
converges weakly to a delta distribution
\delta(Z-Z^{*})
. The structural code
\Phi^{*}
thus supports minimal reconstruction error and maximal compression, consistent with the principle of entropy minimization and generalization efficiency.

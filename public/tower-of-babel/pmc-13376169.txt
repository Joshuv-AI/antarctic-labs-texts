Prediction method of blasting vibration velocity of high slope based on bayes
Authors: Xiangzhao Deng, Junli Nie, Zhen Lei, Beibei Zhang, Junhao Chen, Xijiang Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAccurate prediction of blasting vibration velocity and effective control of vibration-induced hazards remain key challenges in blasting engineering. Using monitoring data from a high-slope blasting project in the Pan Nan 2 × 660 MW low-calorific-value coal comprehensive utilization power generation project, this study develops a Bayesian dynamic updating model for peak particle velocity (PPV) prediction based on the classical Sadovsky formula. Unlike conventional static regression approaches with fixed parameters and limited datasets, the proposed method treats the model parameters K and α as random variables and updates their posterior distributions through a Bayesian inference framework as new monitoring data become available. This enables continuous adaptation to complex geological conditions, including joints, faults, weathered zones, and topographic variability, as well as variations in charge configuration and wave propagation characteristics. Results show that as blasting data accumulate, the posterior distributions of the parameters gradually converge, significantly improving model accuracy and robustness. During the updating process, the coefficient of determination (R²) increases from 0.31 to 0.98, while the root mean square error (RMSE) decreases from 3.38 to 1.18. Independent validation using subsequent monitoring data further confirms the model’s stability and generalization capability, with an R² of 0.97 and an RMSE of 0.5. In comparison, the traditional Sadovsky regression model shows considerably lower performance, with an R² of − 0.04 and an RMSE of 3.24. The results demonstrate that the proposed Bayesian framework effectively integrates new observations, continuously refines model parameters, and significantly enhances prediction accuracy and reliability. This method provides a robust and adaptive tool for blasting vibration prediction and safety control in complex geological environments, with strong potential for real-time engineering applications.Keywords: High slope blasting, PPV prediction model, Bayesian, Dynamic updateSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

In modern engineering construction, high-slope blasting is widely employed in fields such as mining, water conservancy and hydropower, and transportation infrastructure. However, the vibrations induced during the blasting process present a significant concern. These vibrations can lead to the loosening and cracking of slope rock mass, thereby increasing the risk of geological hazards such as landslides1. Consequently, conducting scientific and effective safety prediction and control of slope blasting vibrations serves as a crucial prerequisite and essential measure for optimizing blasting designs and formulating appropriate protective strategies.

In recent years, the prediction of blasting vibration velocity has attracted considerable research attention, leading to the development of various predictive approaches. These methods can generally be classified into four categories: theoretical derivation, empirical formulae, numerical simulation, and intelligent algorithms2,3. Among them, empirical formulae remain the most widely used due to their simplicity, computational efficiency, and strong applicability in engineering practice. The traditional Sadovsky formula provides a reasonable estimate of blasting vibration velocity; however, it primarily considers only the charge weight and the distance from the blasting source4,5. While it performs well under uniform elevation conditions, it exhibits significant limitations when applied to slopes with varying elevations, where vibration velocity amplification effects are commonly observed6–11. To address these limitations, Yang Ruzi et al. introduced a modified empirical model incorporating elevation amplification effects and conducted a comparative analysis using multiple linear regression12. The results demonstrated that the modified model significantly improves prediction accuracy in all directions and better captures the actual propagation characteristics of blasting vibrations13. Lin Haisong et al. reported that the prediction accuracy of the improved model considering elevation effects reached 97.03%, significantly higher than the 83.48% achieved by the traditional model14. Cheng Guihai et al. further demonstrated, through nonlinear regression analysis, that models incorporating elevation differences provide improved prediction accuracy15. In addition, Shao Lei et al., based on dimensional analysis, introduced a slope coefficient and proposed a more comprehensive prediction formula, which significantly enhances predictive performance in complex terrains16.

With the advancement of computational technology, numerical simulation and intelligent algorithms have become important tools for blasting vibration prediction. Numerical simulation methods enable the reproduction of blasting stress wave propagation and the analysis of slope dynamic responses. For instance, Sun Lei et al. developed a three-dimensional multi-layer rock slope model to investigate the dynamic response of slope particles under blasting loads, and analyzed the corresponding characteristics of effective stress, vibration velocity, and displacement17. In recent years, intelligent algorithms have been widely applied to blasting vibration prediction and parameter optimization. Xu S et al. integrated principal component analysis (PCA) with support vector machines (SVM) to optimize blasting parameters in underground mines18. Jiang G et al. enhanced the XGBoost model by incorporating empirical wavelet transform (EWT), leading to improved prediction performance19. Zhou Y et al. combined the sparrow search algorithm (SSA) with a backpropagation (BP) neural network, enabling accurate prediction of peak particle velocity and dominant frequency20. Yang H et al. compared multiple optimization strategies for support vector regression (SVR), including the firefly algorithm (FFA), genetic algorithm (GA), and particle swarm optimization (PSO), and found that the FFA-SVR model exhibited superior performance21. Ullah S et al. assessed the impact of blasting-induced vibrations on open-pit slope stability using the quasi-static limit equilibrium method, and demonstrated that slope stability deteriorates significantly when the horizontal seismic coefficient exceeds 0.0722. Zhang Zaichen et al. developed a BP-wavelet neural network by combining the nonlinear mapping capability of BP networks with the multi-resolution characteristics of wavelet analysis, thereby improving prediction performance in near-field blasting zones23. Zhang Chen et al. combined empirical wavelet transform with the XGBoost model, significantly improving prediction performance24. Fan Yong et al. developed a support vector regression model (PCA-GWO-SVR) enhanced by principal component analysis and the grey wolf optimization algorithm, which effectively improved the accuracy of peak blasting vibration prediction25. In addition to prediction-oriented models, considerable efforts have been devoted to blasting vibration control. Millisecond-delay blasting is widely recognized as an effective technique for vibration reduction. Wang Linfeng et al. adopted a basic empirical formula as the PPV prediction model and introduced a confidence level to establish a safety control and evaluation framework for blasting vibration, enabling the determination of maximum charge per delay and allowable blasting distance26. Based on the European empirical formula, Kahriman et al. developed a PPV prediction model and, by setting the confidence level to 95%, derived the upper and lower bounds of key parameters K and α27,28. From a wave propagation and blasting sequence perspective, Li Hongchao et al. investigated the influence of inter-hole delay on vibration attenuation, dominant frequency, and frequency-band energy using a group-hole blasting model considering non-stationarity and randomness29. In another study, Li H et al. developed a group-hole blasting vibration prediction model accounting for amplitude-frequency non-stationarity. The model parameters were calibrated using orthogonal experiments, and the relative error between predicted and measured PPV was reduced to approximately 4%30. Hu Tao et al., through numerical simulations and field experiments, reported that increasing inter-hole delay leads to an “M”-shaped variation in average damage, and proposed the use of dimensionless delay parameters for optimizing blasting delay design31. Zhang Jian et al. applied high-density drilling, air-deck charging, and segmented initiation techniques to mitigate blasting hazards in dangerous rock masses on high and steep slopes, thereby improving blasting safety32. In addition, detonation direction and charge structure have been shown to significantly affect both blasting efficiency and vibration intensity33.

It is well recognized that blasting vibration signals exhibit strong non-stationarity and randomness, making uncertainty handling a critical issue in vibration prediction34. From a methodological perspective, probability theory provides an effective framework for addressing such uncertainties in blasting engineering. Based on Rayleigh wave theory, Jinshan S et al. developed a method for calculating allowable blasting vibration velocity by considering tensile and shear failure mechanisms in rock masses35. Within the framework of the Sadovsky formula, Zhang Xiaojun et al. statistically compared measured and predicted blasting vibration velocities and assumed that the measured PPV follows a normal distribution V ~ N(V预,σ2)36. Based on this assumption, a probabilistic evaluation model was established, from which a corresponding safe charge calculation formula was derived36. In addition, Dong Yingjian et al. constructed a weighted vibration level prediction model using regression analysis of data obtained from blasting funnel tests. The model enables the estimation of vertical weighted vibration levels based on the maximum single-stage charge and horizontal distance37,38. Li Qiyue et al. further incorporated elevation correction into a PPV prediction model and combined probability theory to develop a comprehensive blasting vibration safety prediction and control framework, thereby improving prediction reliability39. More recently, Sun Pengchang et al. applied a Bayesian approach to predict blasting-induced damage in high slopes using vibration and acoustic wave data. Their method established a quantitative relationship between damage depth and variations in rock mass natural frequency, enabling probabilistic estimation of damage likelihood40.

The above studies demonstrate that considerable progress has been made in blasting control and vibration prediction both domestically and internationally. In particular, approaches that combine probability theory with elevation-corrected peak particle velocity (PPV) models have been widely adopted, providing improved predictive reliability compared with traditional methods. However, existing probability-based approaches still present several limitations, including limited utilization of prior engineering experience, difficulties in updating predictions when new monitoring data become available, insufficient and non-intuitive quantification of uncertainty, and challenges in capturing complex interactions among multiple influencing factors in multi-variable models. In contrast, the Bayesian framework provides a more flexible solution by enabling effective integration of prior knowledge and observed data. More importantly, it allows continuous updating of model parameters as new data are acquired, thereby supporting dynamic optimization of predictions, flexible construction of complex models, and more accurate estimation of parameter relationships. In this study, the blasting area is primarily located along the slope walkway, where monitoring points are at the same elevation as the blasting source; therefore, the influence of elevation difference on PPV is neglected. Based on this condition, a PPV prediction model derived from the Sadovsky formula is combined with a Bayesian updating approach to establish a safety prediction and control model for high-slope blasting vibration. This model provides a reliable basis for vibration safety control in high-slope blasting and offers practical reference value for similar engineering applications.

Engineering application

Project overview

The Pannan 2 × 660 MW low-calorific-value coal comprehensive utilization power generation project is located in Pannan Industrial Park, Panzhou City, Liupanshui, Guizhou Province. According to the site leveling design elevation, cut slopes are formed on the northern and northeastern sides of the site after excavation. Blasting operations are primarily concentrated on the northern slope, which is characterized as a dip slope with heights ranging from 20.3 m to 54.9 m. The slope orientation is 166°, while the bedding dip direction ranges from 220° to 250°, with dip angles of 10°–15°. The slopes are classified as Grade 5 and Grade 6. For the Grade 6 slope, the slope ratios from top to bottom are 1:0.75, 1:0.5, 1:0.5, 1:0.5, 1:0.5, and 1:0.25, whereas for the Grade 5 slope, the corresponding ratios are 1:0.75, 1:0.5, 1:0.5, 1:0.5, and 1:0.25. The slope materials mainly consist of dark gray medium- to thick-bedded argillaceous limestone and limestone of the Middle Triassic Guanling Formation (T2g1+2). These geological and geometric characteristics provide the fundamental basis for subsequent blasting vibration analysis. The current state of slope excavation is shown in Fig. 4.

Current situation map of slope blasting excavation.

Blasting vibration monitoring

The cut slope has a maximum height of 54.9 m and a minimum height of 20.3 m, classifying it as an over-limit high slope. The slope toe is primarily occupied by dormitory and office buildings, as well as natural draft cooling towers. Therefore, strict control of blasting vibration velocity is required during excavation to prevent structural damage and mitigate risks to slope stability. Excessive vibration may induce instability phenomena (e.g., collapse or sliding), thereby threatening the safety of on-site structures and personnel.

To ensure effective vibration control, multiple field blasting tests were conducted. Based on real-time monitoring data, blasting parameters were continuously optimized considering geological conditions and construction requirements. Monitoring points were deployed at the slope toe and dynamically adjusted with construction progress, ensuring that the collected data accurately and representatively reflected blasting vibration effects at different stages.

Blasting vibration monitoring was performed using the L20-S vibrometer, a device designed for engineering applications with stable performance and high reliability. Due to variations in excavation locations, both monitoring positions and blasting parameters differed for each test. Prior to each blast, the recorder was connected to the sensor, with the X-axis oriented horizontally toward the vibration source. During operation, the system automatically recorded vibration signals, ensuring accurate and reliable data acquisition. The field instrument layout is shown in Fig. 5.

Instrument layout diagram.

According to the Blasting Safety Regulations (GB6722-2014), the evaluation index for blasting vibration is based on the maximum value of the peak vibration velocity in three directions and its corresponding vibration frequency. Based on the statistics of on-site blasting monitoring, the vibration frequency during the blasting of this slope ranges from 10 to 50 Hz. Bayesian-based blasting vibration safety prediction and control

Prior distribution of model random variables

In the proposed empirical PPV prediction model, the random variable θ represents the model parameters K and α. The predictive performance of the Bayesian updating framework largely depends on the accuracy of the prior distributions of these parameters. Based on high-slope blasting vibration monitoring data from the Pannan 2 × 660 MW low-calorific-value coal comprehensive utilization power generation project, a statistical analysis of the prior distributions of K and α was conducted. The dataset consists of preliminary blasting vibration velocity measurements collected at the slope toe. A Kolmogorov–Smirnov test was performed to evaluate the distribution characteristics of the parameters. The results indicate that both K and α follow normal distributions. Specifically, the mean values are 32.92 and 0.97, with corresponding standard deviations of 7.14 and 0.21, respectively. The prior distributions of the two parameters are shown in Fig. 6.

Discussion

In high-slope blasting operations, blasting-induced vibrations may adversely affect slope stability; therefore, accurate prediction of peak particle velocity (PPV) is essential for ensuring engineering safety. To further validate the performance of the Bayesian dynamic updating model proposed in this study, subsequent blasting data were employed to compare the predicted PPV with field measurements. Meanwhile, the traditional Sadovsky regression model was adopted as a benchmark to evaluate and contrast the predictive accuracy and applicability of the two approaches.

In the subsequent blasting scheme design, the proposed Bayesian updating model was applied. Based on real-time field monitoring data, the charge quantity was inversely estimated and optimized, enabling the prediction of peak particle velocity (PPV) under given conditions. The predicted PPV values were then compared with the safety threshold for slope blasting vibrations, and blasting operations were conducted only when the safety criteria were satisfied, thereby minimizing the impact of vibrations on slope stability. In contrast, the traditional Sadovsky regression model yields an R² of − 0.04 and an RMSE of 3.24, indicating significantly lower predictive accuracy. This discrepancy is primarily attributed to the inherent limitations of traditional regression approaches, which rely on fixed empirical parameters (K and α) derived from a limited dataset. In practical engineering scenarios, geological conditions exhibit strong heterogeneity and spatial variability, including joints, faults, weathering zones, lithological variations, and complex topography. These factors lead to highly nonlinear and non-stationary propagation of blasting-induced vibration waves. Once calibrated, the traditional model lacks the ability to adapt to such evolving conditions, resulting in systematic prediction biases when applied to regions with differing geological characteristics.

Conclusions

This study developed a dynamic prediction model for predicting blasting Peak Particle Velocity (PPV) by integrating the Sadovsky formula with Bayesian dynamic updating using the high slope blasting vibration monitoring data from the Pan Nan 2 × 660 MW low-calorific-value coal resource comprehensive utilization power generation Project. This approach enabled the continuous update and evolution of model parameters in response to the monitoring data. The main conclusions are as follows:

(1) Prior distribution characteristics of model parameters.

Statistical analysis of previous blasting monitoring data and a Kolmogorov-Smirnov normality test on the parameters K and α of the prediction model reveal their significant adherence to a normal distribution. The parameters exhibit mean values of 32.92 (SD = 7.14) and 0.97 (SD = 0.21) respectively. This distribution characteristic establishes a sound foundation of prior information for subsequent Bayesian updates.

(2) The core advantage of Bayesian dynamic update.

In contrast to the conventional Sadovsky regression model with fixed parameters, the proposed model enables the recursive posterior update of parameters. By incorporating a Bayesian dynamic update mechanism, it can effectively capture real-time changes in geological conditions, charge structures, and propagation paths during high slope blasting. Over time, as more data are gathered, the model’s predictive accuracy has steadily improved. The coefficient of determination (R²) has risen from an initial 0.31 to 0.98, while the RMSE has decreased from 3.38 to 1.18. These findings demonstrate the model’s capability to mitigate systematic prediction errors in intricate and diverse geological settings.

(3) Engineering verification and predictive performance.

The method proposed in this study demonstrates excellent predictive performance when applied to verify subsequent slope blasting scheme designs. It achieves a high R² of 0.97 and a low RMSE of 0.5. In contrast, the traditional Sadovsky regression model performs significantly worse under identical working conditions, yielding poor predictive performance (R² = −0.04). These comparative findings confirm the effectiveness of the Bayesian dynamic update model in continuously assimilating new observational data and dynamically adjusting parameters. Consequently, this model substantially enhances prediction accuracy and reliability.

In conclusion, the proposed Bayesian dynamic update model effectively addresses non-stationarity and parameter uncertainty in blasting vibration prediction. It provides a robust approach for predicting and controlling high-slope blasting vibrations, with strong potential for practical engineering application.

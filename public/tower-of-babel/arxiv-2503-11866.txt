Betti Numbers for Modules Over Artinian Local Rings
Kaiyue He

Abstract
. We introduce a new numerical invariant γ I ​ ( M ) \gamma_{I}(M) associated to a finite-length R R -module M M and an ideal I I in an Artinian local ring R R . This invariant measures the ratio between λ ⁡ ( I ​ M ) \lambda(IM) and λ ⁡ ( M / I ​ M ) \lambda(M/IM) . We establish fundamental relationships between this invariant and the Betti numbers of the module under the assumption of the Tor \operatorname{Tor} modules vanishing. In particular, we use this invariant to establish a freeness criterion for modules under certain Tor \operatorname{Tor} vanishing conditions. The criterion applies specifically to the class of I I -free modules — those modules M M for which M / I ​ M M/IM is isomorphic to a direct sum of copies of R / I R/I . Lastly, we apply these results to the canonical module, proving that, under certain conditions on the ring structure, when the zeroth Betti number is greater than or equal to the first Betti number of the canonical module, then the ring is Gorenstein. This partially answers a question posed by Jorgensen and Leuschke concerning the relationship between Betti numbers of the canonical module and Gorenstein properties.

Introduction

The study of Betti numbers plays a fundamental role in understanding the structure of modules over Noetherian rings. In particular, they provide valuable insights into the behavior of minimal free resolutions. Minimal resolutions over local rings have been extensively studied, and a central problem in this area is understanding the asymptotic growth of Betti numbers. Avramov
[
2
]
summarized key results concerning measures for the asymptotic size of resolutions, originally introduced by Alperin and Evens
[
1
]
including the notions of complexity and curvature of a module. While the behavior of these invariants is well understood for Golod rings and complete intersections, less is known for general Cohen-Macaulay rings.

A key approach, inspired by various works on the Auslander-Reiten conjecture, is to examine pairs of modules
(M,N)
for which
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}(M,N)=0
or
\operatorname{Tor}_{i}(M,N)=0
for some values of
i
. Several results suggest that, in such cases, the Betti numbers of
M
and
N
are closely related. The vanishing of these
\operatorname{Tor}
and
\operatorname{Ext}
modules is particularly significant, as it can have implications for the structure of the ring or the freeness of one of the modules. For example, Jorgensen and Leuschke
[
7
]
showed that if
\operatorname{Tor}_{i}(M,N)
vanishes for certain values of
i
, then the Betti growth of
N
is bounded above by specific invariants of
M
. Furthermore, they demonstrated that under certain conditions, the vanishing of
\operatorname{Tor}
with one of the modules being the canonical module implies that the ring is Gorenstein. Huneke, Sega, and Vraciu
[
4
]
used a similar approach to establish connections between the vanishing of
\operatorname{Tor}
and the growth of Betti numbers. They also proved that for local rings
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
with
{\mathfrak{m}}^{3}=0
, the vanishing of certain
\operatorname{Tor}
modules implies the freeness of one of the modules.

In this paper, we extend the framework developed in
[
4
]
by introducing and analyzing a new invariant,
\gamma_{I}(M)
(see Definition
1.2
), associated with an ideal
I
of an Artinian local ring
R
and a finite
R
-module
M
. Our results generalize to maximal Cohen-Macaulay modules over Cohen-Macaulay local rings by using the standard depth reduction techniques. This invariant provides a refined measure of the interplay between the module length and Betti growth.

In Section
1
, we establish fundamental results on Betti numbers and explore key properties of the invariant
\gamma_{I}(M)
. In particular, we prove that under the vanishing of two consecutive
\operatorname{Tor}
modules and a quotient ring freeness condition (which we refer to as
I
-freeness; see Notation
1.3
), the ratio of consecutive Betti numbers of a module remains fixed (Lemma
1.6
), forming the foundation for the rest of the paper.

In Section
2
, we investigate how the vanishing of certain
\operatorname{Tor}
modules affects the freeness of a module via our new invariant. We show that if three consecutive
\operatorname{Tor}
modules vanish under appropriate
I
-freeness conditions, then the invariant
\gamma_{I}(M)
satisfies a quadratic equation (Theorem
2.6
). Furthermore, we generalize the results of
[
4
]
to local rings
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
with an ideal
I
satisfying
{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}I=0
, or more generally to modules annihilated by
{\mathfrak{m}}I
.

In Section
3
, we apply our results to the canonical module
\omega
, providing conditions under which the invariant characterizes Gorenstein rings. In particular, we address a question posed in
[
7
]
: given a ring
R
with canonical module
\omega
, does
b_{0}(\omega)\leq b_{1}(\omega)
imply that
R
is Gorenstein? We obtain a partial answer to this question (Corollaries
3.4
and
3.5
).

Through these results, our work contributes to a deeper understanding of Betti number growth and its relationship to module structure, particularly in the context of vanishing
\operatorname{Tor}
conditions.

In this paper

Next, we summarize some basic properties of the invariant

1.5 Lemma
.

Here we assume
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
is a local Artinian ring,
I
is a fixed ideal, and
M
is a finite module.

(1)

For any module
M
, we have
\lambda(M/IM)\leq b_{0}(M)\lambda(R/I)
with equality if and only if
M
is
I
-free.

(2)

If
M
is
I
-free , then
\gamma_{I}(M)\leq\gamma_{I}(R)
, with equality if and only if
M
is free.

(3)

If
I^{2}M=0
, then
\gamma_{I}(M)\leq b_{0}(I)
.

(4)

Let
N\subset M
be a submodule, then
\gamma_{I}(M)=\gamma_{I}(M/N)
if and only if
\gamma_{I}(M)=\dfrac{\lambda(N)}{\lambda(N\cap IM)}
.

(5)

If
M
and
N
are
I
-free, then
\gamma_{I}(M\otimes N)\leq(\gamma_{I}(M)+1)\gamma_{I}(N)
.

Proof.

(1) Consider the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow M_{1}\rightarrow R^{b_{0}({\mathfrak{m}})}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0

where
M_{1}
is the first syzygy. Then apply
-\otimes R/I
and obtain
(R/I)^{b_{0}(M)}\twoheadrightarrow M/IM
. The surjection is an isomorphism if and only if
M
is
I
-free.
(2) Assume
M
is
I
-free. Then, notice that

\gamma_{I}(M)=\frac{\lambda(M)}{\lambda(M/IM)}-1\leq\frac{\lambda(R)b_{0}(M)}{\lambda(M/IM)}-1=\frac{\lambda(R)}{\lambda(R/I)}-1=\gamma_{I}(R)

where the last equality follows from
\lambda(M/IM)=b_{0}(M)\lambda(R/I)
.
If moreover
\gamma_{I}(M)=\gamma_{I}(R)
, then

\lambda(M)\lambda(R/I)=\lambda(R)\lambda(M/IM)=\lambda(R)b_{0}(M)\lambda(R/I)

i.e.
\lambda(M)=\lambda(R)b_{0}(M)
. This implies
M
is free.

(3) Assume
b_{0}(I)=n
and
I=(a_{1},..,a_{n})
. Then there is a surjection
M^{n}\rightarrow\frac{IM}{I^{2}M}
that sends
(x_{i})
to
\sum_{i}a_{i}x_{i}
. Observe that
(IM)^{n}
lies in the kernel of this map and therefore it induces a surjection
(\frac{M}{IM})^{n}\rightarrow\frac{IM}{I^{2}M}
. This shows that if
I^{2}M=0
, then we have
b_{0}(I)\lambda(M/IM)\geq\lambda(IM)
. In other words,
\gamma_{I}(M)\leq b_{0}(I)
.
(4) We have by definition
\gamma_{I}(M)=\gamma_{I}(M/N)
if and only if

\frac{\lambda(M)}{\lambda(M\otimes R/I)}=\frac{\lambda(M)-\lambda(N)}{\lambda(M/N\otimes R/I)}

equivalently

\lambda(M)\lambda(M/N\otimes R/I)=\lambda(M\otimes R/I)(\lambda(M)-\lambda(N))

or

\lambda(M)[\lambda(M)-\lambda(N)-\lambda(\frac{IM+N}{N})]=[\lambda(M)-\lambda(IM)][\lambda(M)-\lambda(N)].

In particular, if
M
is
I
-free, then

\lambda(M)[\lambda(M)-\lambda(IM+N))]=[\lambda(M)-\lambda(IM)][\lambda(M)-\lambda(N)]

or

\lambda(M)\lambda(IM+N)=\lambda(M)[\lambda(N)+\lambda(IM)]-\lambda(IM)\lambda(N).

We can rewrite it as

\lambda(M)=\frac{\lambda(IM)\lambda(N)}{\lambda(N)+\lambda(IM)-\lambda(IM+N)}

and notice that
\displaystyle\lambda(N)+\lambda(IM)-\lambda(IM+N)=\lambda(N\cap IM)
, since
\displaystyle\dfrac{IM+N}{N}\cong\dfrac{IM}{N\cap IM}
.

(5) Notice that there is always a surjection
M\otimes IN
to
I(M\otimes N)
and
\displaystyle\lambda(M\otimes IN)\leq\lambda(M)\lambda(IN)
. Hence

\gamma_{I}(M\otimes N)=\frac{\lambda(I(M\otimes N))}{\lambda(R/I\otimes M\otimes N)}\leq\frac{\lambda(M)\lambda(IN)}{\lambda((R/I)^{b_{0}(M)})\lambda((R/I)^{b_{0}(N)})}=(\gamma_{I}(M)+1)\gamma_{I}(N).

∎

1.6 Lemma
.

Let
M
,
N
be

R
-modules such that
M
is nonzero and
N
is non-free. Assume for some
i>0
,
N_{i-1},N_{i}
are
I
-free.

(1)

If
\operatorname{Tor}_{i}(M,N)=0
, then:

\dfrac{b_{i}(N)}{b_{i-1}(N)}=\dfrac{\gamma_{I}(M)-\gamma_{I}(M\otimes N_{i-1})}{\gamma_{I}(M\otimes N_{i})+1}\leq\gamma_{I}(M).

(2)

If in addition,
{\mathfrak{m}}IM=0
, then:

\frac{b_{i}(N)}{b_{i-1}(N)}=\gamma_{I}(M)-\gamma_{I}(M\otimes N_{i-1}).

(3)

If
{\mathfrak{m}}IM=0
and
\operatorname{Tor}_{i-1}(M,N)=\operatorname{Tor}_{i}(M,N)=0
, then:

\frac{b_{i}(N)}{b_{i-1}(N)}=\gamma_{I}(M).

Proof.

(1). Since
\operatorname{Tor}_{i}(M,N)=0
, we have a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow M\otimes N_{i}\rightarrow M\otimes R^{b_{i-1}}\rightarrow M\otimes N_{i-1}\rightarrow 0

and hence

\lambda(M\otimes R^{b_{i-1}})-\lambda(M\otimes N_{i-1})=\lambda(M\otimes N_{i}).

We can rewrite the equality as

(1.6.1)

\displaystyle(\gamma_{I}(M)+1)\lambda(M/IM)b_{i-1}(N)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle(\gamma_{I}(M\otimes N_{i-1})+1)\lambda(M\otimes N_{i-1}\otimes R/I)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\gamma_{I}(M\otimes N_{i})+1)\lambda(M\otimes N_{i}\otimes R/I).

Notice that for
j=i-1,i
, since
N_{j}\otimes R/I
is free over
R/I
, we have
\lambda(M\otimes N_{j}\otimes R/I)=\lambda(M/IM)b_{j}(N)
. Hence we can rewrite
1.6.1
as

[\gamma_{I}(M)-\gamma_{I}(M\otimes N_{i-1})]b_{i-1}(N)=[\gamma_{I}(M\otimes N_{i})+1]b_{i}(N).

(2) Notice that
M\otimes N_{i}
lies in
{\mathfrak{m}}(M\otimes R^{b_{i-1}})
, and therefore by assumption,
I(M\otimes N_{i})\subset{\mathfrak{m}}I(M\otimes R^{b_{i-1}})=0
. As a result,
\gamma_{I}(M\otimes N_{i})=0
.

(3) Since
\operatorname{Tor}_{i-1}(M,N)=0
, we have
M\otimes N_{i-1}
lies in
{\mathfrak{m}}(M\otimes R^{b_{i-2}})
and
\gamma_{I}(M\otimes N_{i})=0
.
∎

1.7 Example

Let

Now let

is free over

\gamma_{I}(M)=\dfrac{\lambda(IM)}{\lambda(M/IM)}=\frac{2}{2}=1

The conditions

1.8 Proposition
.

Let
M
be nonzero and
N
be non-free. Assume for some
i>0
,
N_{i-1},N_{i}
are
I
-free. Then we can conclude
\operatorname{Tor}_{i}(M,N)=0
under either of the following two conditions:

(1)

\dfrac{b_{i}(N)}{b_{i-1}(N)}\leq\dfrac{\gamma_{I}(M)-\gamma_{I}(M\otimes N_{i-1})}{\gamma_{I}(M\otimes N_{i})+1}
.

(2)

The
R
-modules

and
M\otimes N_{i}
are annihilated by the ideal

and
\dfrac{b_{i}(N)}{b_{i-1}(N)}\leq\gamma_{I}(M)

Proof.

(1)Apply

to the short exact sequence
0\rightarrow N_{i}\rightarrow R^{b_{i-1}(N)}\rightarrow N_{i-1}\rightarrow 0
. We have

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}_{1}(M,N_{i-1})\rightarrow M\otimes N_{i}\rightarrow M\otimes R^{b_{i-1}(N)}\rightarrow M\otimes N_{i-1}\rightarrow 0.

Applying the length formula we have

\lambda(M\otimes N_{i})=b_{i-1}(N)\lambda(M)-\lambda(M\otimes N_{i-1})+\lambda(\operatorname{Tor}_{1}(M,N_{i-1})).

Now we have

\displaystyle(\gamma_{I}(M\otimes N_{i})+1)\lambda(M\otimes N_{i}\otimes R/I)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b_{i-1}(N)(\gamma_{I}(M)+1)\lambda(M/IM)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle(\gamma_{I}(M\otimes N_{i-1})+1)\lambda(M\otimes N_{i-1}\otimes R/I)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\lambda(\operatorname{Tor}_{1}(M,N_{i-1})).

Since
N_{i-1}
and
N_{i}
are
I
-free, by the same argument as in the proof of
1.6

\displaystyle(\gamma_{I}(M\otimes N_{i})+1)b_{i}(N)\lambda(M/IM)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b_{i-1}(N)(\gamma_{I}(M)+1)\lambda(M/IM)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle(\gamma_{I}(M\otimes N_{i-1})+1)b_{i-1}(N)\lambda(M/IM)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\lambda(\operatorname{Tor}_{1}(M,N_{i-1})).

Then since

is nonzero and hence

\displaystyle(\gamma_{I}(M\otimes N_{i})+1)b_{i}(N)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\gamma_{I}(M)-\gamma_{I}(M\otimes N_{i}))b_{i-1}(N)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\dfrac{\lambda(\operatorname{Tor}_{1}(M,N_{i-1}))}{\lambda(M/IM)}.

Dividing both sides of the equality by
\gamma_{I}(M\otimes N_{i})+1)b_{i-1}(N)
, we have

(1.8.1)

\displaystyle\dfrac{b_{i}(N)}{b_{i-1}(N)}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\gamma_{I}(M)-\gamma_{I}(M\otimes N_{i-1})}{\gamma_{I}(M\otimes N_{i})+1}+\frac{\lambda(\operatorname{Tor}_{1}(M,N_{i-1})}{\lambda(M/IM)b_{i-1}(N)(\gamma_{I}(M\otimes N_{i})+1)}

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{\gamma_{I}(M)-\gamma_{I}(M\otimes N_{i-1})}{\gamma_{I}(M\otimes N_{i})+1}.

Now if
\dfrac{b_{i}(N)}{b_{i-1}(N)}\leq\dfrac{\gamma_{I}(M)-\gamma_{I}(M\otimes N_{i-1})}{\gamma_{I}(M\otimes N_{i})+1}
, then we can conclude
\operatorname{Tor}_{1}(M,N_{i-1})=\operatorname{Tor}_{i}(M,N)=0
.

(2) By assumption we have
\gamma_{I}(M\otimes N_{i-1})=\gamma_{I}(M\otimes N_{i})=0
and therefore, we have
\dfrac{b_{i}(N)}{b_{i-1}(N)}\geq\gamma_{I}(M)
from Equation

\operatorname{Tor}_{i}(M,N)=0
.
∎

\operatorname{Tor}_{i}(M,N)=0

Let

\gamma_{I}(M)^{2}b_{i}(N)-e\gamma_{I}(M)b_{i}(N)+ab_{i}(N)\geq 0.

Since
N
is non-free, we have

\gamma_{I}(M)^{2}-e\gamma_{I}(M)+a\geq 0.

Denote

and
r_{2}=\frac{e-\sqrt{e^{2}-4a}}{2}
. In particular by assumption
e^{2}-4a>e^{2}-4(e-1)

r_{1}
and
r_{2}
are real roots. Hence
\gamma_{I}(M)\leq r_{2}<1
or
\gamma_{I}(M)\geq r_{1}>e\geq b_{0}(I)
. Then
\gamma_{I}(M)\geq 1
by
1.10
(1),

is an integer by

(2), and
\gamma_{I}(M)\leq h

\gamma_{I}(M)\leq r_{2}<1
and

. This is a contradiction.
∎

1.12 Lemma
.

Let
M
be a nonzero module and
N
be a non-free module. Assume
{\mathfrak{m}}IM=0
,
N,N_{1},N_{2}
are
I
-free and
\operatorname{Tor}_{1}(M,N)=\operatorname{Tor}_{2}(M,N)=0
. Then

(1)

b_{1}(M)=(b_{0}(I)-\gamma_{I}(M))b_{0}(M)
.

(2)

If in addition,
M,M_{1}
are
I
-free, then
\lambda(IM_{1})=(\lambda({\mathfrak{m}}I)+b_{0}(I)-\lambda(R/I)b_{0}(I))b_{0}(M)
.

Proof.

(1) Since
\operatorname{Tor}_{1}(M,N)=\operatorname{Tor}_{2}(M,N)=0
, we have

b_{1}(M\otimes N_{1})=b_{0}(M)b_{2}(N)+b_{1}(M)b_{1}(N).

Since
\operatorname{Tor}_{1}(M,N)=0
, we have

I(M\otimes N_{1})\subset I({\mathfrak{m}}(M\otimes R^{b_{0}(N)}))=0

and therefore
M\otimes N_{1}\otimes R/I\cong M\otimes N_{1}
. Moreover,
M
and
N_{1}
are
I
-free, so
M\otimes N_{1}
must be isomorphic to copies of
R/I
. Hence
b_{1}(M\otimes N_{1})=b_{0}(I)\cdot b_{0}(M)b_{1}(N)
. While on the other hand, by Lemma
1.6
, we have
b_{1}(N_{1})=b_{2}(N)=\gamma_{I}(M)b_{1}(N).
Together we have

(1.12.1)

\begin{split}b_{0}(I)b_{0}(M)b_{1}(N)&=b_{0}(M)b_{2}(N)+b_{1}(M)b_{1}(N)\\
&=b_{0}(M)b_{1}(N)\gamma_{I}(M)+b_{1}(M)b_{1}(N)\end{split}

and since
N
is non-free,

b_{1}(M)=(b_{0}(I)-\gamma_{I}(M))b_{0}(M).

(2) By the definition of
\gamma_{I}(M)
and the virtue that
M/IM
is free over
R/I
, we have

\lambda(M)=(\gamma_{I}(M)+1)\lambda(M/IM)=(\gamma_{I}(M)+1)\lambda(R/I)b_{0}(M).

While looking at the short exact sequence
0\rightarrow M_{1}\rightarrow R^{b_{0}(M)}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0
, we have

\lambda(M_{1})=\lambda(R)b_{0}(M)-\lambda(M).

Now plugging the
\lambda(M)
calculated above

(1.12.2)

\begin{split}\lambda(M_{1})&=(\lambda({\mathfrak{m}}I)+b_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)-\gamma_{I}(M)\lambda(R/I)-\lambda(R/I))b_{0}(M)\\
&=(\lambda({\mathfrak{m}}I)+b_{0}(I)-\gamma_{I}(M)\lambda(R/I))b_{0}(M).\end{split}

Then we combine what we calculate for
\lambda(M_{1})
and the assumption
M_{1}
is
I
-free and by (a)

(1.12.3)

\begin{split}\lambda(IM_{1})=&\lambda(M_{1})-\lambda(M_{1}/IM_{1})\\
=&\lambda(M_{1})-b_{1}(M)\lambda(R/I)\\
=&(\lambda({\mathfrak{m}}I)+b_{0}(I)-\gamma_{I}(M)\lambda(R/I))b_{0}(M)-(b_{0}(I)-\gamma_{I}(M))b_{0}(M)\lambda(R/I)\\
=&(\lambda({\mathfrak{m}}I)+b_{0}(I)-\lambda(R/I)b_{0}(I))b_{0}(M).\end{split}

∎

1.13 Lemma
.

Let
M
,
N
be non-free
R

The behavior of Betti numbers of finite

was
studied by Lescot

2.1 Lemma
.

Let
M,N
be finite modules such that
M
is nonzero and
N

{\mathfrak{m}}IM=0

be an integer such that

\operatorname{Tor}_{i+1}(M,N)=\operatorname{Tor}_{i+2}(M,N)=0

\lambda(M_{i+1}/IM_{i+1})=b_{0}(I)\lambda(R/I)b_{i}(M)-\lambda(IM_{i}).

Proof.

Breaking down the length of modules via short exact sequences, we have

\displaystyle\lambda(M_{i+1}/IM_{i+1})

\displaystyle=\lambda(M_{i+1})-\lambda(IM_{i+1})

\displaystyle=\lambda(R)b_{i}(M)-\lambda(M_{i})-\lambda(IM_{i+1})

\displaystyle=\big(\lambda({\mathfrak{m}}I)+b_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)\big)b_{i}(M)-\lambda(M_{i}/IM_{i})-\lambda(IM_{i+1})-\lambda(IM_{i}).

Now since
M_{i}
is
I
-free and apply Lemma
1.12
(b) to
\lambda(IM_{i+1})
(here is why we need the syzygies of
M
and
N
to be
I
-free), we have the above

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\lambda({\mathfrak{m}}I)+b_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I))b_{i}(M)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\lambda(R/I)b_{i}(M)-(\lambda({\mathfrak{m}}I)+b_{0}(I)-\lambda(R/I)b_{0}(I))b_{i}(M)-\lambda(IM_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b_{0}(I)\lambda(R/I)b_{i}(M)-\lambda(IM_{i}).

∎

2.2
.

In

4

{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}\neq 0

is non-free, and

is not a direct summand of

in such situations. If in addition

b_{0}({\mathfrak{m}}N_{i})=\dim\operatorname{Soc}(R)b_{i-1}(N)
. Here we believe that
\operatorname{Soc}(N)\subset{\mathfrak{m}}N

2.3 Lemma
.

Assume
\operatorname{Soc}(R)={\mathfrak{m}}I
. If

is a non-free syzygy module and
\operatorname{Soc}(M_{1})\subset IM_{1}

\operatorname{Soc}(M_{1})=IM_{1}=mIR^{b_{0}(M)}

Notice that

Looking at the short exact sequence

. Hence, we have

{\mathfrak{m}}IM=0

{\mathfrak{m}}IR^{b_{0}(M)}=\operatorname{Soc}({\mathfrak{m}}IR^{b_{0}(M)})\subset\operatorname{Soc}(M_{1})\subset\operatorname{Soc}({\mathfrak{m}}R^{b_{0}(M)})={\mathfrak{m}}IR^{b_{0}(M)}.

∎

2.4 Corollary

Let

2.5 Lemma
.

Let
M
be a non-free module. Assume
\operatorname{Soc}(R)={\mathfrak{m}}I
.
Then

\lambda(M_{1}/IM_{1})\geq b_{0}(M)\cdot b_{0}(I)-\lambda(IM).

Proof.

We have
\lambda(M_{1}/IM_{1})=\lambda(M_{1})-\lambda(IM_{1})

as the

. But since

\lambda(IM_{1})\leq\lambda(\operatorname{Soc}(M_{1}))=b_{0}\cdot\lambda({\mathfrak{m}}I)
. The equality follows from the last line in the proof of

On the other hand, notice that
\lambda(M_{1})=b_{0}\cdot\lambda(R)-\lambda(M)
. Hence we can rewrite

(2.5.1)

\lambda(M_{1}/IM_{1})\geq b_{0}\cdot\lambda(R)-\lambda(M)-b_{0}\cdot\lambda({\mathfrak{m}}I)

and similarly we have

\lambda(M)=\lambda(M/IM)+\lambda(IM)\leq b_{0}\cdot\lambda(R/I)+\lambda(IM).

Then, notice that

\lambda(R)=\lambda(R/I)+b_{0}(I)+\lambda({\mathfrak{m}}I).

Now substitute them into
2.5.1
we have

(2.5.2)

\displaystyle\lambda(M_{1}/IM_{1})

\displaystyle\geq b_{0}\cdot(\lambda(R/I)+b_{0}(I)+\lambda({\mathfrak{m}}I))-b_{0}\cdot\lambda(R/I)-\lambda(IM)-b_{0}\cdot\lambda({\mathfrak{m}}I)

\displaystyle=b_{0}\cdot b_{0}(I)-\lambda(IM).

∎

Before starting the first main theorem, we have some notations for simplicity

Notation
.

Denote

,
h=b_{0}(I)

c=\lambda({\mathfrak{m}}I)

\displaystyle s+h+c=\lambda(R)

2.6 Theorem
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a local Artinian ring with a fixed ideal
I
such that
{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}I=0

be nonzero, non-free modules such that
{\mathfrak{m}}IM=0
and
{\mathfrak{m}}IN=0
. If there exists

such that for

(1)

\operatorname{Tor}_{t}(M,N)=0
and

(2)

M_{t},N_{t}
are
I
-free,

then
\gamma_{I}(M)
and
\gamma_{I}(N)
satisfy the equation
\displaystyle{s\gamma^{2}-sh\gamma+c+h-sh=0}

Proof.

By Lemma

(3), we have

By Lemma

we also have

sb_{j+2}(N)=shb_{j+1}(N)-(c+h-sh)b_{j}(N).

Now replacing
b_{j+2}(N)
and
b_{j+1}(N)

, we have

s\gamma_{I}(M)^{2}b_{j}(N)=hs\gamma_{I}(M)b_{j}(N)-(c+h-sh)b_{j}(N).

∎

2.7 Corollary
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be an Artinian ring and
I
be a nonzero ideal such that
{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}I=0
and
\lambda(R)<2sh
. Assume that
M
is a nonzero module such that
{\mathfrak{m}}IM=0
and
N
is non-free. Let
j>1
be an integer such that for
t=j,j+1,j+2
,
M_{t},N_{t}
are
I
-free. Set
\beta=\max\{\log_{2}(b_{1}(M))+1,\log_{2}(b_{1}(N))+1,j+2\}
. If
\operatorname{Tor}_{i}(M,N)=0
for

, then
M
or
N
is free.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that
M
is non-free. Then we can apply Theorem
2.6
, so
\gamma_{I}(M)
satisfies the equation

\gamma^{2}-h\gamma+\frac{c+h-sh}{s}=0.

For simplicity, denote
\alpha=\frac{c+h-sh}{s}
.
Hence
\gamma_{I}(M)
lies in the set
\{\frac{h+\sqrt{h^{2}-4\alpha}}{2},\frac{h-\sqrt{h^{2}-4\alpha}}{2}\}
. Notice that by the assumptions

and

, hence
\sqrt{h^{2}-4\alpha}>h-2
. As a result, if we denote
\gamma_{1}\leq\gamma_{2}
as the roots of

\gamma_{1}=\frac{h-\sqrt{h^{2}-4\alpha}}{2}<\frac{h-h+2}{2}=1
and

. By Lemma
1.10
(b), we have that
\gamma_{I}(M)
is an integer. Since
M
is non-free, so
b_{1}(M)\neq 0
and therefore by Lemma
1.12
(a), we have
\gamma_{I}(M)<h
. Therefore, we must have
\gamma_{I}(M)=\gamma_{1}=0
. However, since
\operatorname{Tor}_{j}(M,N_{1})=\operatorname{Tor}_{j+1}(M,N_{1})=0
and

(c)

, which contradicts the assumption that

is non-free. Hence we conclude

must be free.∎

2.8
.

Next we collect some results from
[
4
, 2.7]
.
For any Artinian ring
R
(not necessarily with

) with a dualizing module
\omega
, we have

=
\dim_{k}\operatorname{Soc}(R)
,
\dim_{k}\operatorname{Soc}(\omega)=1
, and
\lambda(R)=\lambda(\omega)
.

2.9 Remark
.

Assume
\operatorname{Soc}(M)=IM
and
\operatorname{Soc}(M^{\dagger})=IM^{\dagger}
where
(-)^{\dagger}=\operatorname{Hom}(-,\omega)
. Then by Matlis duality we have

\lambda(IM^{\dagger})=\dim_{k}\operatorname{Soc}(M^{\dagger})=b_{0}(M)

and

\operatorname{Soc}(M^{\dagger})=\operatorname{Hom}(k,\operatorname{Hom}(M,\omega))\cong\operatorname{Hom}(k,\omega)^{b_{0}(M)}

so

\lambda(IM)=\lambda(IM^{\dagger\dagger})=\lambda(\operatorname{Soc}(M^{\dagger\dagger}))=b_{0}(M^{\dagger}).

Hence

(2.9.1)

\displaystyle\gamma_{I}(M)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda(IM)}{\lambda(M/IM)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{b_{0}(M^{\dagger})}{b_{0}(M)\lambda(R/I)}

\displaystyle=\frac{b_{0}(M^{\dagger})}{\lambda(IM^{\dagger})\lambda(R/I)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda(R/I)^{2}}\frac{b_{0}(M^{\dagger})\lambda(R/I)}{\lambda(IM^{\dagger})}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda(R/I)^{2}}\frac{\lambda(M^{\dagger}/IM^{\dagger})}{\lambda(IM^{\dagger})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda(R/I)^{2}}\frac{1}{\gamma_{I}(M^{\dagger})}.

The first inequality is equality if
M
is
I
-free and the second is equality if
M^{\dagger}
is
I
-free.

2.10 Proposition
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be an Artinian local ring with a canonical module
\omega
and

be an ideal different from

, such that

,

, and

Assume by contradiction that neither of them is free. By Lemma
1.6
, there exists an integer

and

3.
Betti Numbers of the Canonical Module

In this section, we give some partial answer to the question posed in
[
7
]
, ”Does
b_{1}(\omega)\leq b_{0}(\omega)
imply
R
is Gorenstein?”
We still assume all the rings to be local Artinian, with a fixed ideal
I
.
First, we can obtain an upper bound for the Betti growth of
\omega
while certain
Tor
modules of the canonical module vanish. Before we start, let’s recall some notations that will be frequently used in this section. We denote
s=\lambda(R/I)
,
h=b_{0}(I)
, and
c=\lambda(R)-s-h=\lambda({\mathfrak{m}}I)
.

3.1 Proposition
.

If
\operatorname{Tor}_{1}(\omega,\omega)=\operatorname{Tor}_{2}(\omega,\omega)=0
and
\omega,\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
are
I
-free, then

\frac{b_{1}(\omega)}{b_{0}(\omega)}\leq\frac{b_{0}(I)}{2}.

Proof.

Assume
\omega
is non-free. Set
b_{i}=b_{i}(\omega)
. By Lemma
1.6
, we have

\frac{b_{1}}{b_{0}}\leq\gamma_{I}(\omega).

While on the other hand, by Lemma
1.12
, we have

\frac{b_{1}}{b_{0}}=h-\gamma_{I}(\omega).

Hence we can conclude
\gamma_{I}(\omega)\geq\dfrac{h}{2}
and the result follows.
∎

3.2 Remark
.

Now we give some calculations on
\omega
over the ring
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
with an ideal
I
such that
{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}I=0
. If
{\mathfrak{m}}I\neq 0
, then since
{\mathfrak{m}}I\omega\subset\operatorname{Soc}(\omega)
and
\dim_{k}\operatorname{Soc}(\omega)=1
, we have
\lambda({\mathfrak{m}}I\omega)\leq b_{0}({\mathfrak{m}}I\omega)=1
. But moreover
{\mathfrak{m}}I\neq 0
, so
\lambda({\mathfrak{m}}I\omega)=1
. Now denote
b_{0}(\omega)=\dim_{k}\operatorname{Soc}(R)=a
and set
N=\omega_{1}
. If
\omega
is
I
-free, then we have

(1)

\lambda(\omega)=\lambda(R)=\lambda(R/I)+b_{0}(I)+\lambda({\mathfrak{m}}I)=s+h+c
.

(2)

\lambda(I\omega)=\lambda(\omega)-\lambda(\omega/I\omega)=s+h+c-as
.

3.3 Proposition
.

Let
\operatorname{Soc}(R)={\mathfrak{m}}I
. Assume
h>1
. If
\lambda(\omega_{1}/I\omega_{1})\leq\lambda(\omega/I\omega)
, then

\lambda({\mathfrak{m}}I)\leq\frac{\lambda(R/I)+b_{0}(I)}{b_{0}(I)-1}.

In particular if
h>s+2
, then
R
is Gorenstein.

Proof.

Set
N=\omega_{1}
. Recall that
c=\lambda({\mathfrak{m}}I)
Apply
2.5
, we have

\lambda(N/IN)\geq ch-s-h-c+cs.

while on the other hand

\lambda(\omega/I\omega)\leq b_{0}(\omega)\lambda(R/I)=cs

hence we get

cs\geq ch-s-h-c+cs.

Simplifying it, we have

c\leq\frac{s+h}{h-1}.

If
h>s+2
, then
\frac{s+h}{h-1}<2
and
c=1
, i.e.
\dim_{k}\operatorname{Soc}(R)=1
.
R
is Gorenstein.
∎

3.4 Corollary
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be an Artinian ring such that
\operatorname{Soc}(R)={\mathfrak{m}}^{2}
and

. If
b_{1}(\omega)\leq b_{0}(\omega)

must be Gorenstein.

Proof.

Applying Proposition
3.3
with

and

, the result follows.

∎

3.5 Corollary
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a local Artinian ring such that
{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}I=0
. Assuming (i)

; (ii)

. If there exists a finite non-free module

such that for three consecutive integer values

\operatorname{Tor}_{i}(M,\omega)=0
,
{\mathfrak{m}}IM=0
and that
M_{t},\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
, and
\omega_{3}
are

-free for

. Then
R
is Gorenstein.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction
\omega
is non-free. Then we can replace
M
by
M_{i-1}
to get
\operatorname{Tor}_{1}(M,N)=\operatorname{Tor}_{2}(M,N)=\operatorname{Tor}_{3}(M,N)=0

1.13

∎

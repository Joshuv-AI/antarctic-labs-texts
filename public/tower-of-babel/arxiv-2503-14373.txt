Size-minimal combinatorial designs of staircase type
Barbora Batíková, Tomáš J. Kepka and Petr C. Němec

Abstract
. The minimum size of combinatorial designs of staircase type is found.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
05B05

1.
Introduction

In
[
1
]
, an interesting sort of combinatorial designs is constructed which may be called designs of staircase type. Given a positive integer
n
and a partitioning
n=r_{1}s_{1}+\dots+r_{t}s_{t}
,
t,r_{i},s_{i}
positive integers, such that
r_{1}>\dots>r_{t}
(for
t\geq 2
), we can write
n
symbols
1,\dots,n
in the form of a staircase matrix having
r_{1}
rows where first
r_{1}-r_{2}
rows have
x_{1}
columns, next
r_{2}-r_{3}
rows have
t_{1}+t_{2}
columns, etc., and finally last
r_{t}
rows have
t_{1}+\dots+t_{k}
columns. Then we can construct a design having
r_{1}+s_{1}+\dots+s_{t}
sets by taking all
r_{1}
rows and
s_{1}+\dots+s_{t}
columns of this staircase matrix. Such designs have exactly two replications of each symbol and various cardinalities for the sets constituting the design. Considering designs of this type having the minimum size, the following question jumps out: Given a positive integer
n
, find partitionings
n=r_{1}s_{1}+\dots+r_{t}s_{t}
,
t,r_{i},s_{i}
positive integers, such that
r_{1}>\dots>r_{t}
(for
t\geq 2
) and the sum
r_{1}+s_{1}+\dots+s_{t}
is the smallest possible. The aim of this note is to find this smallest sum. In fact, the staircase designs are useful in non-adaptive combinatorial group testing (see, e.g.,
[
2
]
) and this, in turn, has to be accessible to general public use. Not, solely, to a fistful of chosen ones in the know. Henceforth, as a rule, we are making use of ultraelementary methods only.

2.
Preliminaries

In the whole article,
\mathbb{Z}
(
\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbb{Q}
,
\mathbb{R}
, resp.) stand for the domain (halfdomain, halfdomain, field, field, resp.) of integers (non-negative integers, positive integers, rationals, real numbers, resp.) This notation is (with a pinch of salt) standard, while the following one not and it is introduced for local purposes solely.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. We denote
\mathcal{A}(n)=\{\,(r,s)\,|\,r,s\in\mathbb{N}_{0},r\leq s,r+s=n\,\}
and
\mathcal{B}(n)=\{\,(r,s)\,|\,r,s\in\mathbb{N}_{0},r\leq s,r\cdot s=n\,\}
It is immediately visible that
\mathcal{A}(0)=\{(0,0)\}
,
\mathcal{A}(1)=\{(0,1)\}
,
\mathcal{A}(2)=\{(0,2),(1,1)\}
,
\mathcal{B}(0)=\{(0,s)\,|\,s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
,
\mathcal{B}(1)=\{(1,1)\}
,
\mathcal{B}(2)=\{(1,2)\}
and
\mathcal{B}(n)\subseteq\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Besides,
\mathcal{A}(0)\cap\mathcal{B}(0)=\{(0,0)\}
,
\mathcal{A}(4)\cap\mathcal{B}(4)=\{(2,2)\}
and
\mathcal{A}(n)\cap\mathcal{B}(n)=\emptyset
for
n\neq 0,4
.

For
n\geq 2
, put
\alpha(n)=\max\{rs\,|\,(r,s)\in\mathcal{A}(n)\}
and
\beta(n)=\min\{rs\,|\,(r,s)\in\mathcal{A}(n)\}
.

For
n\geq 1
, put
\gamma(n)=\max\,\{\,r+s\,|\,(r,s)\in\mathcal{B}(n)\,\}
and
\delta(n)=\min\,\{\,r+s\,|\,(r,s)\in\mathcal{B}(n)\,\}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

(i)

\alpha(n)=\frac{n^{2}}{4}\,(=\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)^{2})
for every
n\geq 2
,
n
even.

(ii)

\alpha(n)=\frac{n^{2}-1}{4}
for every
n\geq 3
,
n
odd.

Proof.

We have
\alpha(2)=1
,
\alpha(3)=2
, so that we assume
n\geq 4
. If
1\leq r<2r\leq n-1
then
(r,n-r)\in\mathcal{A}(n)
,
0<(n-2r)^{2}=n^{2}-4rn+4r^{2}
,
4r(n-r)=4rn-4r^{2}<n^{2}
,
r(n-r)<(\frac{n}{2})^{2}
. If
n
is odd and
2r<n-1
then
r\leq\frac{n-3}{2}
,
2+2r\leq 2+n-3=n-1<n
,
2<n-2r
,
4<(n-2r)^{2}=n^{2}-4rn+4r^{2}
,
4r(n-r)=4rn-4r^{2}<n^{2}-4<n^{2}-1
,
r(n-r)<\frac{n^{2}-1}{4}
. On the other hand,
\left(\frac{n}{2},\frac{n}{2}\right)\in\mathcal{A}(n)
for
n
even and
\left(\frac{n-1}{2},\frac{n-1}{2}\right)\in\mathcal{A}(n)
for
n
odd. The rest is clear.∎

Proposition 2.2
.

\beta(n)=n-1
for every
n\geq 2
.

Proof.

We have
\beta(2)=1
,
\beta(3)=2
, so that we assume
n\geq 4
. Now,
(1,n-1)\in\mathcal{A}(n)
and, if
(r,s)\in\mathcal{A}(n)

2\leq r
, then
3\leq r+1\leq n-1<n
,
r^{2}-1=(r-1)(r+1)<(r-1)n=rn-n
,
n-1<rn-r^{2}=r(n-r)=rs
,
n\leq rs
.∎

Proposition 2.3
.

\gamma(n)=n+1
for every
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

Since
(1,n)\in\mathcal{B}(n)
, we have
n+1\leq\gamma(n)
. If
(r,s)\in\mathcal{B}(n)4
,
2\leq r
, then
r(r-1)<n(r-1)
,
r^{2}+n<rn+r
,
r+s=r+\frac{n}{r}=\frac{r^{2}+n}{r}<\frac{rn+r}{r}=n+1
.∎

Remark 2.4
.

If
(r,s)\in\mathcal{A}(n)
,
2\leq r
, then
n\leq rs
. If
(r,s)\in\mathcal{B}(n)
,
2\leq r
, then
r+s\leq n
.

First bunch of observations

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
r_{1},r_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,
r_{1}>0
,
r_{2}\geq 0
. Then:

(i)

r_{1}+\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}}\geq 2r_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

(ii)

r_{1}+\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}}=2r_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}
if and only if
r_{1}=\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}}
(i.e.,
r_{2}=r_{1}^{2}
).

Proof.

\left(r_{1}+\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}}\right)^{2}=r_{1}^{2}+2r_{2}+\frac{r_{2}^{2}}{r_{1}^{2}}=\left(r_{1}-\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}}\right)^{2}+4r_{2}\geq 4r_{2}
.∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
r_{1},r_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
,
r_{1}|n
,
r_{2}|n
.

(i)
If
r_{1}+\frac{n}{r_{1}}=r_{2}+\frac{n}{r_{2}}
then either
r_{1}=r_{2}
or
r_{1}=\frac{n}{r_{2}}
,
r_{2}=\frac{n}{r_{1}}
and
n=r_{1}r_{2}
.

(ii)
If
r_{1}<r_{2}
and
r_{2}^{2}\leq n
then
r_{2}+\frac{n}{r_{2}}<r_{1}+\frac{n}{r_{1}}
.

(iii)
If
r_{1}^{2}>n
then
\left(\frac{n}{r_{1}}\right)^{2}<n
.

Proof.

(i)
r_{1}^{2}r_{2}+nr_{2}=r_{1}r_{2}^{2}+nr_{1}
and
n(r_{2}-r_{1})=r_{1}r_{2}(r_{2}-r_{1})
.

(ii)
r_{1}r_{2}^{2}-r_{1}^{2}r_{2}=r_{1}r_{2}(r_{2}-r_{1})<r_{2}^{2}(r_{2}-r_{1})\leq n(r_{2}-r_{1})=nr_{2}-nr_{1}
,
r_{1}r_{2}\left(r_{2}+\frac{n}{r_{2}}\right)=r_{1}r_{2}^{2}+r_{1}n<r_{1}^{2}r_{2}+r_{2}n=r_{1}r_{2}\left(r_{1}+\frac{n}{r_{1}}\right)
,
r_{2}+\frac{n}{r_{2}}<r_{1}+\frac{n}{r_{1}}
.

(iii)
\left(\frac{n}{r_{1}}\right)^{2}=\frac{n^{2}}{r_{1}^{2}}<\frac{n^{2}}{n}=n
.∎

Being
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\varepsilon(n)
designate the greatest integer such that
\varepsilon(n)|n
and
\varepsilon(n)^{2}\leq n
. Notice that
\frac{n}{\varepsilon(n)}
is just the smallest positive integer
m
dividing
n
such that
n\leq m^{2}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then:

(i)

\delta(n)=\varepsilon(n)+\frac{n}{\varepsilon(n)}\,(=\frac{\varepsilon(n)+n}{\varepsilon(n)})
.

(ii)
If
r\in\mathbb{N}
,
r|n
,
r\neq\varepsilon(n)
,
r\neq\frac{n}{\varepsilon(n)}
then
\delta(n)<r+\frac{n}{r}
.

Proof.

Let
(r,s)\in\mathcal{B}(n)
. If
r<\varepsilon(n)
then
\delta(n)<r+s
by 3.2(ii). If
\varepsilon(n)<r
then
n<r^{2}
, and so
s^{2}=\frac{n^{2}}{r^{2}}<\frac{n^{2}}{n}=n
,
s\leq\varepsilon(n)
. Since
r\leq s
, we get
r\leq\varepsilon(n)
, a contradiction.∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then:

(i)

\delta(n)\geq 2n^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

(ii)

\delta(n)=2n^{\frac{1}{2}}
if and only if
n=k^{2}
,
k\geq 1\ (\delta(k^{2})=2(k))
.

Proof.

We have
n-2\varepsilon(n)n^{\frac{1}{2}}+\varepsilon(n)^{2}=(n^{\frac{1}{2}}-\varepsilon(n))^{2}\geq 0
, so that
n+\varepsilon(n)^{2}\geq 2\varepsilon(n)n^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
\delta(n)=\varepsilon(n)+\frac{n}{\varepsilon(n)}\geq 2n^{\frac{1}{2}}
(3.3(i)).∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then:

(i)

2\leq\delta(n)\leq n+1
.

(ii)

\delta(n)=n+1
if and only if either
n=1
or
n
is a prime number.

Proof.

If
(r,s)\in\mathcal{B}(n)
,
2\leq r
, Then
s\leq\frac{n}{2}
and
r+s\leq r+\frac{n}{2}\leq s+\frac{n}{2}\leq n<n+1
.∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
\delta(m)<\delta(n)
for every composite number
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
m<n
, if and only if either
n=p,2p
,
p
being a prime, or

.

Proof.

In view of 5.1, we can assume that the number

is composite as well. If

,

being a prime, then
\delta(m)\leq\frac{m+4}{2}\leq\frac{2p-1+4}{2}=\frac{2p+3}{2}=p+\frac{3}{2}<p+2=\delta(2p)
(use 4.1(i) for

\delta(4)=4<5=\delta(6)<6=\delta(8)<7=\delta(10)=\delta(12)<8=\delta(15)=\delta(16)<9=\delta(14)=\delta(18)=\delta(20)<10=\delta(21)

and let

for each composite

. Having look at the example 3.6, we see readily that

.

Assume, for a moment, that

for a prime
p
. Then
p\geq 13
and we denote by

the smallest prime number greater than

. It is generally believed (see 5.3) that

. From that, it follows that

and

At this stage of our knowledge, we know that
n\geq 50
and

. Using 4.1, we get
\delta(n)<\frac{n+12}{4}

∎

Proposition 5.4
.

Let

. Then
\delta(n)<\delta(m)
for every

,

If

(

(
\delta(m)\geq 2m^{\frac{1}{2}}>2k+1=\delta(k^{2}+k

Proposition 7.1

.∎

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
n
be a positive integer and let
\varphi(n)
denote the minimum of all the sums
r_{1}+s_{1}+\dots+s_{t}
, where
t,r_{i},s_{i}
are positive integers such that
n=\sum_{i=1}^{t}r_{i}s_{i}
and
r_{1}>\dots>r_{t}
(for
t\geq 2
). Then:

(i)
There is a uniquely determined positive integer
k
such that
k^{2}-k+1\leq n\leq k^{2}+k
.

(ii)
If
n\leq k^{2}
then
\varphi(n)=2k
.

(iii)
If
k^{2}+1\leq n
then
\varphi(n)=2k+1
.

Proof.

Combine 7.1, 7.2, 7.7 and 7.8.∎

Remark 8.2

if and only if

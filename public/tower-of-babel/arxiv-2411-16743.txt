Accelerated Bregman gradient methods for relatively smooth and relatively Lipschitz continuous minimization problems
O. S. Savchuk a,b , M. S. Alkousa c,d , A. S. Shushko d , A. A. Vyguzov d,b , F. S. Stonyakin d,a,c , D. A. Pasechnyuk e,f and A. V. Gasnikov c,d,b

Abstract
In this paper, we propose some accelerated methods for solving optimization problems under the condition of relatively smooth and relatively Lipschitz continuous functions with an inexact oracle. We consider the problem of minimizing the convex differentiable and relatively smooth function concerning a reference convex function. The first proposed method is based on a similar triangles method with an inexact oracle, which uses a special triangular scaling property for the used Bregman divergence. The other proposed methods are non-adaptive and adaptive (tuning to the relative smoothness parameter) accelerated Bregman proximal gradient methods with an inexact oracle. These methods are universal in the sense that they are applicable not only to relatively smooth but also to relatively Lipschitz continuous optimization problems. We also introduced an adaptive intermediate Bregman method which interpolates between slower but more robust algorithms non-accelerated and faster, but less robust accelerated algorithms. We conclude the paper with the results of numerical experiments demonstrating the advantages of the proposed algorithms for the Poisson inverse problem.



keywords

Convex optimization; accelerated method; intermediate method; relative smoothness; relative Lipschitz continuity

Introduction

With the increase in the number of applications that can be modeled as large-scale (or even huge-scale) optimization problems (some of such applications arising in machine learning, deep learning, data science, control, signal processing, statistics, and so on), first-order methods, which require low iteration cost as well as low memory storage, have received much interest over the past few decades to solve the optimization problems, in the smooth and non-smooth cases
[
2
,
1
]
. Recently, there was introduced a new direction for the research, associated with the development of gradient-type methods for optimization problems with relatively Lipschitz-continuous
[
3
]
, relatively smooth
[
4
]
and relatively strongly convex
[
5
]
functions. Such methods are urgently in high demand due to numerous theoretical and applied problems. For example, the D-optimal design problem (see Example
2.4
), and the Poisson inverse problem (see Example
2.3
) turned out to be relatively smooth
[
5
,
6
]
. It is also quite interesting that in recent years there have been appeared many applications of these methods to solve auxiliary problems that arise in tensor methods for convex minimization problems of the second and higher orders
[
7
,
8
]
.

Many real-world optimization problems involve minimizing relatively smooth functions using an inexact oracle that provides noisy or approximate access to the functions and gradient information. Some examples of such problems include training machine learning models on large datasets, where evaluating the full information concerning the objective function (its value and gradient at iterated points) is computationally expensive, so stochastic gradients are used instead, optimizing hyperparameters of machine learning models using validation set performance, which is a noisy estimate of the true objective, tuning the parameters of physical systems or simulations that have inherent noise or uncertainty
[
9
,
10
]
. In this context, the concept of relative smoothness plays a crucial role. This generalization of the standard smoothness condition allows for more flexibility in the choice of the reference function, which can lead to improved computational guarantees for first-order optimization methods.

The concept of relative smoothness first introduced in
[
4
]
, it is an important condition that generalizes the standard Lipschitz continuity of the gradient, allows for the analysis of convergence of gradient methods for a broader class of functions, and enables efficient optimization in many applications. Further lower bounds for relatively smooth problems were established in
[
33
]
. It can be effectively used in machine learning problems, where the concept is applied to analyze the convergence of gradient methods when training logistic regression models with non-Euclidean regularizers. In signal processing, relative smoothness is used to study the convergence of proximal splitting methods for solving composite optimization problems. In the seminal work
[
5
]
, the authors demonstrated the versatility of the relative smoothness condition by applying it to various settings of optimization problems, including the D-Optimal Design Problem and the minimization of the Volumetric Barrier Function. They showed that by exploiting the relative smoothness structure of these problems, it is possible to design efficient gradient methods with improved convergence rates.

Motivated by applications in machine learning and engineering, where the presence of noise or uncertainty is common, the concept of an inexact oracle, where the function and gradient values have some error, can significantly degrade the performance of accelerated gradient methods. In some cases, non-accelerated methods that are more robust to noise can outperform accelerated ones. Recently the authors of
[
11
]
proposed an ”intermediate” method that interpolates between accelerated and non-accelerated convergence rates, using a special parameter
p
that controls the degree of acceleration, depending on the current accumulation of the solution estimation inaccuracy. When
p
tends to
2
, it recovers the accelerated method, while
p
tends to
1
, gives the non-accelerated convergence rate, but at the same time it guarantees the achievement of a higher accuracy of the solution to the problem (see the second term of Remark
3
).

In this paper, we focus on developing similar previously mentioned optimization methods for relatively smooth problems with an inexact oracle, which can be considered the main novelty of the work. For the convex optimization problem, we propose two new families of methods, the first is an adaptive fast gradient method with an inexact oracle. It is based on the similar triangles method
[
12
]
, and exploits the idea of an inexact oracle and the triangular scaling property of the Bregman divergence corresponding to the used prox structure of the problem. This proposed algorithm is universal in the sense that it is applicable not only to relatively smooth but also to relatively Lipschitz continuous optimization problems. For the second family, we propose a different picture, where we propose non-adaptive and adaptive accelerated Bregman proximal gradient methods with an inexact oracle. These methods are also universal in the sense that they are applicable not only to relatively smooth but also to relatively Lipschitz continuous optimization problems. Further, based on
[
14
,
15
,
13
]
, we propose an intermediate method parameterized by
p\in[1,2]
and interpolating between non-accelerated and accelerated methods. The proposed method is provided by an adaptive way of choosing parameters
p
and
L
(relative smoothness parameter) along the iteration process, which leads to better robustness by slowing down the convergence rate.

1.1
Contributions

To sum it up, the contributions of the paper can be formulated as follows.

1.

We proposed an adaptive fast gradient method with an inexact oracle. We concluded its convergence rate for the convex optimization problems and proved that it is applicable not only to relatively smooth but also to relatively Lipschitz continuous optimization problems.

2.

We proposed non-adaptive and adaptive accelerated Bregman proximal gradient methods with an inexact oracle. We concluded their convergence rate and also proved that they are applicable not only to relatively smooth but also to relatively Lipschitz continuous optimization problems.

3.

We proposed an automatically adaptive intermediate Bregman method, that interpolates between non-accelerated and accelerated methods, with an inexact oracle, and concluded its convergence rate.

4.

We provided some numerical experiments for the Poisson inverse problem, with a comparison of the proposed methods with other known methods.

Problem statement, basic concepts and definitions

Let
\mathbb{E}
be an
n
-dimensional real vector space and
\mathbb{E}^{*}
be its dual. We denote the value of a linear function
g\in\mathbb{E}^{*}
at
x\in\mathbb{E}
by
\langle g,x\rangle
. Let
\|\cdot\|
be some norm on
\mathbb{E}
,
\|\cdot\|_{*}
be its dual, defined by
\|g\|_{*}=\max\limits_{x}\big\{\langle g,x\rangle,\|x\|\leq 1\big\}
.
\|\cdot\|_{2}
denotes the standard Euclidean norm. We use
\nabla f(x)
to denote the gradient of a differentiable function
f
at a point
x\in{\rm dom}f
and, with a slight abuse of notation, a subgradient of a convex function
f
at a point
x\in{\rm dom}f
.

Let
Q\subset\mathbb{E}
be a closed convex set,
f:Q\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a convex differentiable on an open set that contained the relative interior of
Q
(denoted as
{\rm rint}Q
).

In this paper, we consider the following optimization problem

\min\limits_{x\in Q}f(x).

(1)

For solving the problem (
1
), we can consider the
proximal gradient method
, which, by taking
x_{0}\in{\rm rint}Q
as an initial point, has the following form

x_{k+1}=\arg\min\limits_{x\in Q}\left\{f(x_{k})+\left\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),x-x_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|x-x_{k}\|^{2}\right\},\;\;\forall k\geq 0,

(2)

with
L>0
(see (
3
)). The assumption that
Q
is simple means that the minimization problem in (
2
) can be solved efficiently, especially if it admits a closed-form solution.

In (
2
), we use the gradient of the function
f
at
x_{k}
, i.e.,
\nabla f(x_{k})
, to construct a local quadratic approximation of
f
near
x_{k}
. Assuming that the function
f
is bounded below, the convergence of the
proximal gradient method
can be established
if
f(x_{k+1})\leq f(x_{k})
for all
k\geq 0
. A sufficient condition for this to hold is that the quadratic approximation of
f
in (
2
) is an upper approximation. This is the main idea of many general methods for nonlinear optimization, where a common assumption is for the gradient of
f
to satisfy a uniform Lipschitz condition. That is, there exists
L>0
such that the following inequality holds

\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\|_{*}\leq L\|x-y\|,\quad\forall x,y\in{\rm rint}Q.

(3)

From (
3
), it follows that
[
1
]

f(y)\leq f(x)+\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|y-x\|^{2},\quad\forall x\in{\rm rint},y\in Q.

(4)

For the
proximal gradient method
(
2
), it can be shown that its convergence rate will be
[
16
,
17
]

f(x_{k})-f(x)\leq\frac{L\|x-x_{0}\|^{2}}{2k}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{k}\right),\quad\forall x\in Q,\;\text{and}\;\forall k\geq 1.

(5)

Under the same assumptions,
accelerated proximal gradient methods
can achieve a faster convergence rate
[
16
,
18
]
, which is

f(x_{k})-f(x)\leq\frac{2L\|x-x_{0}\|^{2}}{(k+2)^{2}}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right),\quad\forall x\in Q,\;\text{and}\;\forall k\geq 0.

(6)

This convergence rate is optimal (up to a constant factor) for a given class of convex optimization problems
[
19
,
1
]
.

Although the uniform smoothness condition (
3
) is central to the design and analysis of first-order methods, there are many applications where the objective function does not have this property, despite being convex and differentiable. For example, in the Poisson inverse problem
[
21
,
20
]
(see Example
2.3
) and the D-optimal design problem
[
22
,
23
]
(see Example
2.4
), the objective functions involve a logarithm in the form of the log-determinant or relative entropy, whose gradients may increase sharply toward the boundary of the feasible region. To design efficient first-order algorithms for such problems, the notion of
relative smoothness
has been introduced in several works (see e.g.
[
24
,
4
,
5
]
).

2.1
Bregman divergence and relative smoothness

Let
d:Q\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a distance-generating function (also called prox-function or reference function) that is continuously differentiable and
1
-strongly convex.

Definition 2.1
.

The Bregman divergence (also called Bregman distance) associated with the prox-function
d
is defined as

V(x,y)=d(x)-d(y)-\langle\nabla d(y),x-y\rangle,\quad\forall x,y\in Q.

(7)

As a result, we have
V(x,y)\geq\frac{1}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}.

Some standard examples of Bregman divergence are as follows:

1.

Euclidean prox-function:
Let
Q
be a convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
endowed with the Euclidean norm
\|\cdot\|_{2}
. Then, the
Euclidean prox-function
on
Q
is defined as
d(x)=\frac{1}{2}\|x\|_{2}^{2}
and the corresponding Bregman divergence is the standard square distance
V(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}\|x-y\|_{2}^{2},\,\forall x,y\in Q.

2.

Entropic prox-function:
Let
Q=\Delta_{n}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}:\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}=1\right\}
be the unit simplex in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
endowed with the
L_{1}
-norm
\|\cdot\|_{1}
. Then, the
entropic prox-function
(or
Boltzmann-Shannon entropy
) on
Q
is
d(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\ln(x_{i})
and the corresponding Bregman divergence is the relative entropy
V(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\ln\left(x_{i}/y_{i}\right),\;\forall x\in Q,\forall y\in{\rm rint}Q.

If
d(x)=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\ln(x_{i})
. Then the corresponding Bregman divergence (called the generalized Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence) is

V(x,y):=V_{\rm KL}(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(x_{i}\ln\left(\frac{x_{i}}{y_{i}}\right)-x_{i}-y_{i}\right).

(8)

3.

Log-barrier prox-function
[
25
]
:
Let
Q=\mathbb{R}_{++}^{n}
be the open positive orthant of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, the
log-barrier prox-function
(or
Burg’s entropy
) on
Q
is defined as
d(x)=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ln(x_{i})
. The corresponding Bregman divergence is known as the
Itakura-Saito
(IS) divergence and is given by

V(x,y):=V_{\rm IS}(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{x_{i}}{y_{i}}-\ln\left(\frac{x_{i}}{y_{i}}\right)-1\right).

(9)

Definition 2.2
.

A function
f
is called
L
-smooth relative to the prox-function
d
on
Q
, if there exists
L>0
such that

f(y)\leq f(x)+\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\rangle+LV(y,x),\quad\forall x\in{\rm rint}Q,y\in Q.

(10)

The definition of relative smoothness in (
10
) gives an upper approximation of
f
similar to (
4
). Therefore, it is natural to consider a more general algorithm than (
2
), by replacing
\frac{1}{2}\|x-x_{k}\|^{2}
in (
2
) with Bregman divergence
V(x,x_{k})
. Thus, by taking
x_{0}\in{\rm rint}Q
as an initial point, we get the following method (called

x_{k+1}=\arg\min\limits_{x\in Q}\left\{f(x_{k})+\left\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),x-x_{k}\right\rangle+LV(x,x_{k})\right\},\;\;\forall k\geq 0,

(11)

2.2
Inexact oracle

In some works, such as
[
26
,
12
]
(for example only) one can find many first-order methods for solving different minimization problems
\min_{x\in Q}f(x)
, in many settings of the objective
f
, with so-called inexact oracle.

Most minimization methods for such problems are constructed using some model of the objective
f
at the current iterate
x_{k}
. This can be a quadratic model based on the
L
-smoothness of the objective

f(x_{k})+\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),x-x_{k}\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|x-x_{k}\|^{2}.

(14)

The literature on the first-order methods
[
11
,
13
]
(for example only) also considers gradient methods with inexact information, relaxing the model (
14
) to the following

f_{\delta}(x_{k})+\langle\nabla f_{\delta}(x_{k}),x-x_{k}\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|x-x_{k}\|^{2}+\delta,

(15)

where
(f_{\delta},\nabla f_{\delta})
(see Definition
2.5
) is called the inexact oracle, and this model represents an upper bound for the objective
f
. In particular, this relaxation allows obtaining universal gradient methods
[
27
]
.

In what follows we mention the precise definition of the inexact oracle.

Definition 2.5

(
[
12
]
)
.

We say that the pair
(f_{\delta}(y),\nabla f_{\delta}(y))\in\mathbb{R}\times E^{*}
is a
(\delta,L)
-oracle of the function
f
at the point
y
for
\delta,L>0
, if it holds the following inequality

0\leq f(x)-(f_{\delta}(y)+\left\langle\nabla f_{\delta}(y),x-y\right\rangle)\leq LV(x,y)+\delta,\quad\forall x\in Q.

(16)

2.3
Triangular scaling property

Definition 2.6
.

Let
d:Q\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a prox-function and
V(\cdot,\cdot)
be a corresponding Bregman divergence. We say that
V(\cdot,\cdot)
satisfies the
triangular scaling property

[
6
]
, with constant
\gamma>0
(which called the
triangular scaling factor
), if for any
x,z,\widetilde{z}\in Q
it holds the following inequality

V\left((1-\theta)x+\theta z,(1-\theta)x+\theta\widetilde{z}\right)\leq\theta^{\gamma}V\left(z,\widetilde{z}\right),\quad\forall\theta\in[0,1].

(17)

If
V(x,y)
is jointly convex in
(x,y)
, then the inequality (
17
) holds with
\gamma=1
, because

V((1-\theta)x+\theta z,(1-\theta)x+\theta\widetilde{z})\leq(1-\theta)V(x,x)+\theta V(z,\widetilde{z})=\theta V(\widetilde{z},z).

Next, we mention some examples of the Bregman divergence with the triangular scaling property.

Example 2.7

(Squared Euclidean distance)
.

Let
d(x)=\frac{1}{2}\|x\|^{2}_{2}
and
V(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}_{2}
. Indeed
V(x,y)
is jointly convex in its two arguments. For this divergence, (
17
) will be as the following

\frac{1}{2}\left\|(1-\theta)x+\theta z-((1-\theta)x+\theta\widetilde{z})\right\|^{2}_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\theta(z-\widetilde{z})\right\|^{2}_{2}=\frac{\theta^{2}}{2}\left\|z-\widetilde{z}\right\|^{2}_{2}.

Therefore, the squared Euclidean distance satisfies the triangular scaling property with a scaling factor
\gamma=2
.

Example 2.8

(Generalized Kullback-Leibler divergence)
.

Let
d(x)=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\ln(x_{i})
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. The corresponding Bregman divergence is the KL divergence, defined in (
8
). Indeed
V_{\rm KL}(x,y)
satisfies the triangular scaling property with a scaling factor
\gamma=1
(see
[
6
]
).

Example 2.9

(Itakuro-Saito divergence)
.

Let
d(x)=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ln(x_{i})
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{++}
. The corresponding Bregman divergence is the IS divergence, defined in (
9
). Indeed
V_{\rm IS}(x,y)
satisfies the triangular scaling property with a scaling factor
\gamma<1
(see
[
6
]
).

3
Fast gradient method for relatively smooth optimization problems with inexact oracle

In this section, we assume that
V(\cdot,\cdot)
satisfies the
triangular scaling property

[
6
]
, with constant
\gamma=2
. We consider an adaptive fast gradient method (listed as Algorithm
1
below) for solving the problem (
1
), when
f
is a relatively
L
-smooth function. It exploits the idea of an inexact oracle and the triangular scaling property of the Bregman divergence corresponding to the used prox structure. We mention here that this algorithm is an analog of the algorithm that was proposed in
[
12
]
based on the similar triangles method with an inexact model, which covers the idea of the inexact oracle as a special case. The proposed Algorithm
1
is universal in the sense that it is applicable not only to relatively smooth but also to relatively Lipschitz continuous optimization problems.

Algorithm 1

Adaptive Fast Gradient Method with Inexact Oracle (AdapFGM).

1:

Input:
initial point
x_{0}
,
\{\delta_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
and
L_{0}>0
such that
L_{0}<2L
.

2:

Set
y_{0}:=x_{0},u_{0}:=x_{0},\alpha_{0}:=0,A_{0}:=\alpha_{0}
.

3:

for

k\geq 0

do

4:

Find the smallest integer
i_{k}\geq 0
, such that

f_{\delta_{k}}(x_{k+1})\leq f_{\delta_{k}}(y_{k+1})+\left\langle\nabla f_{\delta_{k}}(y_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-y_{k+1}\right\rangle+L_{k+1}V(x_{k+1},y_{k+1})+\delta_{k},

(18)

where
L_{k+1}=2^{i_{k}-1}L_{k}
,
\alpha_{k+1}
is the largest root of the following equation

A_{k+1}=L_{k+1}\alpha^{2}_{k+1},\;\;\text{where }\;\;A_{k+1}:=A_{k}+\alpha_{k+1},

(19)

y_{k+1}:=\frac{\alpha_{k+1}u_{k}+A_{k}x_{k}}{A_{k+1}},

(20)

u_{k+1}:=\arg\min\limits_{x\in Q}\left\{\alpha_{k+1}\langle\nabla f_{\delta_{k}}(y_{k+1}),x-y_{k+1}\rangle+V(x,u_{k})\right\},

(21)

x_{k+1}:=\frac{\alpha_{k+1}u_{k+1}+A_{k}x_{k}}{A_{k+1}}.

(22)

5:

end

For Algorithm

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that Bregman divergence

satisfies the triangular scaling property with scaling factor
\gamma=2

, such that
V(x_{*},x_{0})\leq R^{2}

is a solution of the problem (

) closest to the initial point

. Then after

f(x_{N})-f(x_{*})\leq\frac{8LR^{2}}{(N+1)^{2}}+\frac{2\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}A_{k+1}\delta_{k}}{A_{N}},

(23)

where

To prove Theorem

Lemma 3.2

(
[

]
)
.

Let

(
[

)

\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}

Now, let us prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

For Algorithm

A_{k+1}f(x_{k+1})-A_{k}f(x_{k})+V(x,u_{k+1})-V(x,u_{k})\leq\alpha_{k+1}f(x)+2\delta_{k}A_{k+1},\;\forall x\in Q.

Proof.

Based on Definition

, where

generated by Algorithm
3
satisfy the following inequality for any
x\in Q

\frac{1}{\theta_{k}^{\gamma}}\Big(f(x_{k+1})-f(x)\Big)+L_{k+1}V(x,z_{k+1})\leq\frac{1-\theta_{k}}{\theta_{k}^{\gamma}}\Big(f(x_{k})-f(x)\Big)+L_{k+1}V(x,z_{k})+\frac{\delta}{\theta_{k}^{\gamma}}.

(42)

Proof.

From the right-hand side of (
32
), and from items 4, 6 of Algorithm
3
, we find

\displaystyle f(x_{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq g(x_{k+1}|y_{k})+L_{k+1}V(x_{k+1},y_{k})+\delta

\displaystyle=g(x_{k+1}|y_{k})+L_{k+1}V((1-\theta_{k})x_{k}+\theta_{k}z_{k+1},(1-\theta_{k})x_{k}+\theta_{k}z_{k})+\delta

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq_15}}{\leq}g(x_{k+1}|y_{k})+\theta_{k}^{\gamma}L_{k+1}V(z_{k+1},z_{k})+\delta.

(43)

Using
x_{k+1}=(1-\theta_{k})x_{k}+\theta_{k}z_{k+1}
, and from the convexity of the function
x\longmapsto g(x|y_{k})\;\forall x\in Q
, we get

\displaystyle f(x_{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq(1-\theta_{k})g(x_{k}|y_{k})+\theta_{k}g(z_{k+1}|y_{k})+\theta_{k}^{\gamma}L_{k+1}V(z_{k+1},z_{k})+\delta

\displaystyle=(1-\theta_{k})g(x_{k}|y_{k})+\theta_{k}(g(z_{k+1}|y_{k})+\theta_{k}^{\gamma-1}L_{k+1}V(z_{k+1},z_{k}))+\delta.

(44)

Applying Lemma
3.2
for

\varphi(x)=\frac{1}{\theta_{k}^{\gamma-1}L_{k+1}}g(x|y_{k})
, and since

z_{k+1}=\arg\min\limits_{z\in Q}\left\{g(z|y_{k})+\theta_{k}^{\gamma-1}L_{k+1}V(z,z_{k})\right\}

(see item 5 of Algorithm
3
) we obtain, for any
x\in Q
, the following inequality

g(z_{k+1}|y_{k})+\theta_{k}^{\gamma-1}L_{k+1}V(z_{k+1},z_{k})\leq g(x|y_{k})+\theta_{k}^{\gamma-1}L_{k+1}V(x,z_{k})-\theta_{k}^{\gamma-1}L_{k+1}V(x,z_{k+1}).

(45)

Hence, from (
44
) and (
45
), for any
x\in Q
, we find

\displaystyle f(x_{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq(1-\theta_{k})g(x_{k}|y_{k})+\theta_{k}\left(g(x|y_{k})+\theta_{k}^{\gamma-1}L_{k+1}V(x,z_{k})-\theta_{k}^{\gamma-1}L_{k+1}V(x,z_{k+1})\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\delta

\displaystyle=(1-\theta_{k})g(x_{k}|y_{k})+\theta_{k}g(x|y_{k})+\theta_{k}^{\gamma}\left(L_{k+1}V(x,z_{k})-L_{k+1}V(x,z_{k+1})\right)+\delta

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq_22}}{\leq}(1-\theta_{k})f(x_{k})+\theta_{k}f(x)+\theta_{k}^{\gamma}\left(L_{k+1}V(x,z_{k})-L_{k+1}V(x,z_{k+1})\right)+\delta.

Subtracting
f(x)
from both sides of the last inequality, we obtain

f(x_{k+1})-f(x)\leq(1-\theta_{k})(f(x_{k})-f(x))+\theta_{k}^{\gamma}(L_{k+1}V(x,z_{k})-L_{k+1}V(x,z_{k+1}))+\delta.

Dividing both sides of the last inequality by
\theta_{k}^{\gamma}
, we get the desired inequality (
42
).
∎

For Algorithm
3
, we have the following result.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
f:Q\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be an
L
-smooth function relative to a prox-function
d
on
Q
, and the Bregman divergence

satisfies the triangular scaling property with scaling factor

N\geq 1

, then it holds the following inequality

(46)

Proof.

By taking the sum of both sides of (

) for
x=x_{*}
, over

from

to

(having previously divided both parts by

), and taking into account (

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\left(\frac{1}{\theta_{k}^{\gamma}L_{k+1}}\left(f(x_{k+1})-f(x_{*})\right)+V(x_{*},z_{k+1})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\left(\frac{1-\theta_{k}}{\theta_{k}^{\gamma}L_{k+1}}\left(f(x_{k})-f(x_{*})\right)+V(x_{*},z_{k})+\frac{\delta}{\theta_{k}^{\gamma}L_{k+1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\theta_{0}}{\theta_{0}^{\gamma}L_{1}}\left(f(x_{0})-f(x_{*})\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\left(\frac{1-\theta_{k}}{\theta_{k}^{\gamma}L_{k+1}}\left(f(x_{k})-f(x_{*})\right)\right)+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}V(x_{*},z_{k})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{\delta}{\theta_{k}^{\gamma}L_{k+1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1-\theta_{0}}{\theta_{0}^{\gamma}L_{1}}\left(f(x_{0})-f(x_{*})\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\left(\frac{1}{\theta_{k-1}^{\gamma}L_{k}}\left(f(x_{k})-f(x_{*})\right)\right)+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}V(x_{*},z_{k})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{\delta}{\theta_{k}^{\gamma}L_{k+1}}.

Then, after taking the summation we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\theta_{N-1}^{\gamma}L_{N}}\left(f(x_{N})-f(x_{*})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1-\theta_{0}}{\theta_{0}^{\gamma}L_{1}}\left(f(x_{0})-f(x_{*})\right)+V(x_{*},z_{0})-V(x_{*},z_{N})

Based on the fact that
V(x_{*},z_{N})\geq 0,

\theta_{0}=1

\frac{1}{\theta_{N-1}^{\gamma}L_{N}}\left(f(x_{N})-f(x_{*})\right)\leq V(x_{*},z_{0})+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{\delta}{\theta_{k}^{\gamma}L_{k+1}}.

, we have

f(x_{N})-f(x_{*})\leq L_{N}\theta_{N-1}^{\gamma}V(x_{*},z_{0})+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{\theta_{N-1}^{\gamma}L_{N}}{\theta_{k}^{\gamma}L_{k+1}}\delta.

and the condition on

\frac{\theta_{N-1}^{\gamma}L_{N}}{\theta_{k}^{\gamma}L_{k+1}}\leq\frac{\theta_{N-1}^{\gamma}L_{N}}{\theta_{k+1}^{\gamma}}=\left(\frac{\gamma}{N-1+\gamma}\right)^{\gamma}\cdot\left(\frac{k+1+\gamma}{\gamma}\right)^{\gamma}L_{N}=\left(\frac{k+1+\gamma}{N-1+\gamma}\right)^{\gamma}L_{N}\leq L_{N}.

In view of the assumption

and inequality (

, and the following inequality

which is the desired inequality (

).
∎

Remark 2
.

Now, for problem (
1
), we will show that the Algorithm
3
is universal, namely, we will prove that this method is applicable not only to relatively smooth but also to relatively Lipschitz continuous optimization problems.

In
[
28
]
it is shown that if the function
f
is relatively Lipschitz continuous, then for any fixed
L>0
and
\delta>0
the following inequality holds

f(x)\leq f(y)+\langle\nabla f(y),x-y\rangle+LV(x,y)+LV(y,x)+\delta,\quad\forall x,y\in Q.

Therefore, for
L_{k+1}\geq L
, taking into account the triangular scaling property for Bregman divergence, we obtain

\displaystyle f(x_{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq f(y_{k})+\langle\nabla f(y_{k}),x_{k+1}-y_{k}\rangle+L_{k+1}V(x_{k+1},y_{k})+L_{k+1}V(y_{k},x_{k+1})+\delta

\displaystyle=f(y_{k})+\left\langle\nabla f(y_{k}),(1-\theta_{k})x_{k}+\theta_{k}z_{k+1}-(1-\theta_{k})x_{k}-\theta_{k}z_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+L_{k+1}V\left((1-\theta_{k})x_{k}+\theta_{k}z_{k+1},(1-\theta_{k})x_{k}+\theta_{k}z_{k}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+L_{k+1}V\left((1-\theta_{k})x_{k}+\theta_{k}z_{k},(1-\theta_{k})x_{k}+\theta_{k}z_{k+1}\right)+\delta

\displaystyle\leq f(y_{k})+\theta_{k}\langle\nabla f(y_{k}),z_{k+1}-z_{k}\rangle+L_{k+1}\theta^{\gamma}_{k}V\left(z_{k+1},z_{k}\right)+L_{k+1}\theta^{\gamma}_{k}V\left(z_{k},z_{k+1}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\delta

\displaystyle=f(y_{k})+\theta_{k}\Bigg(\langle\nabla f(y_{k}),z_{k+1}-z_{k}\rangle+L_{k+1}\theta^{\gamma-1}_{k}V(z_{k+1},z_{k})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+L_{k+1}\theta^{\gamma-1}_{k}V(z_{k},z_{k+1})\Bigg)+\delta.

(49)

Let us consider as an
(\delta,L)
-oracle of the function
f
the pair
(f_{\delta}(y),\nabla f_{\delta}(y))=(f(y),\nabla f(y))
. Since

z_{k+1}:=\arg\min\limits_{x\in Q}\{f(y_{k})+\langle\nabla f(y_{k}),x-y_{k}\rangle+\theta^{\gamma-1}_{k}L_{k+1}V(x,z_{k})\},

then from Lemma
3.2
it holds the following inequality

\langle\nabla f(y_{k}),z_{k+1}-x\rangle\leq L_{k+1}\theta^{\gamma-1}_{k}V(x,z_{k})-L_{k+1}\theta^{\gamma-1}_{k}V(z_{k+1},z_{k})-L_{k+1}\theta^{\gamma-1}_{k}V(x,z_{k+1}).

Thus, for
x=z_{k}
we have

\langle\nabla f(y_{k}),z_{k+1}-z_{k}\rangle\leq-L_{k+1}\theta^{\gamma-1}_{k}V(z_{k+1},z_{k})-L_{k+1}\theta^{\gamma-1}_{k}V(z_{k},z_{k+1}).

Therefore, from (
49
), we have

f(x_{k+1})\leq f(y_{k})+\delta.

Since for relatively Lipschitz continuous functions the following inequality also holds

\langle\nabla f(y),x-y\rangle+LV(x,y)+\delta\geq 0,\quad\forall x,y\in Q.

Then

f(x_{k+1})\leq f(y_{k})+\delta\leq f(y_{k})+\langle\nabla f(y_{k}),x_{k+1}-y_{k}\rangle+LV(x_{k+1},y_{k})+2\delta.

Thus, the exit criterion from the iteration of the Algorithm
3
will be guaranteed to be satisfied when
L_{k+1}\geq L
and
\delta_{k+1}\geq 2\delta
.

For proving the convergence rate of the proposed Algorithm

(AIBM), let us first prove the following useful lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
f
be a convex and relatively
L
-smooth function, equipped with a first-order
(\delta,L)
-oracle. Then by AIBM, it holds the following inequality

A_{k}f(y_{k})-E_{k}\leq\Psi^{*}_{k},\quad\forall k\geq 0,

(55)

where
\Psi^{*}_{k}=\min_{x\in Q}\Psi_{k}(x),
and
E_{k}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}B_{i}\delta_{i}
.

Proof.

We prove this lemma by induction. For
k=0
, we have

\displaystyle\Psi_{0}^{*}

\displaystyle\;\;=\;\;\min_{x\in Q}\left\{\Psi_{0}(x)\right\}=\min_{x\in Q}\left\{d(x)+\alpha_{0}f_{\delta_{0}}(x_{0})+\alpha_{0}\left\langle g_{\delta_{0}}(x_{0}),x-x_{0}\right\rangle\right\}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Line}\,\ref{1line:yo}}}{{\geq}}d(y_{0})+\alpha_{0}l_{\delta_{0}}(x_{0},y_{0})=\alpha_{0}\left(L_{0}V(y_{0},x_{0})+l_{\delta_{0}}(x_{0},y_{0})\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Line}\,\ref{1line:sc1}}}{{\geq}}\alpha_{0}f_{\delta_{0}}(y_{0})-\delta_{0}-\alpha_{0}\delta_{0}\geq A_{0}f_{\delta_{0}}(y_{0})-B_{0}\delta_{0}=A_{0}f(y_{0})-E_{0}.

Assume that (
55
) is valid for certain

, i.e.,

A_{k-1}f(y_{k-1})-E_{k-1}\leq\Psi_{k-1}^{*}=\min_{x\in Q}\Psi_{k-1}(x),

(56)

and let us prove that it holds for
k
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\Psi_{k}^{*}

\displaystyle\;\;=\;\;\min_{x\in Q}\Psi_{k}(x_{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Line}\,\ref{1line:z}}}{{\geq}}\Psi_{k}(z_{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{Psi_smooth_case}}}{{=}}\Psi_{k-1}(z_{k})+\alpha_{k}l_{\delta_{k}}(x_{k},z_{k})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ineq:psi_smooth}}}{{\geq}}\Psi_{k-1}(z_{k-1})+V(z_{k},z_{k-1})+\alpha_{k}l_{\delta_{k}}(x_{k},z_{k})\geq\Psi_{k-1}^{*}+V(z_{k},z_{k-1})+\alpha_{k}l_{\delta_{k}}(x_{k},z_{k})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{induction_k_1}}}{{\geq}}A_{k-1}f(y_{k-1})-E_{k-1}+V(z_{k},z_{k-1})+\alpha_{k}l_{\delta_{k}}(x_{k},z_{k})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Line}\,\ref{1line:A}}}{{\geq}}(A_{k}-\alpha_{k})f(y_{k-1})-E_{k-1}+V(z_{k},z_{k-1})+\alpha_{k}l_{\delta_{k}}(x_{k},z_{k})

\displaystyle=(A_{k}-B_{k})f(y_{k-1})-E_{k-1}+V(z_{k},z_{k-1})+(B_{k}-\alpha_{t})f(y_{k-1})+\alpha_{k}l_{\delta_{k}}(x_{k},z_{k})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{oracle_smooth}}}{{\geq}}(A_{k}-B_{k})f(y_{k-1})-E_{k-1}+V(z_{k},z_{k-1})+(B_{k}-\alpha_{k})l_{\delta_{k}}(x_{k},y_{k-1})

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha_{k}l_{\delta_{k}}(x_{k},z_{k})

\displaystyle=(A_{k}-B_{k})f(y_{k-1})-E_{k-1}+V(z_{k},z_{k-1})+B_{k}f(x_{k})

\displaystyle\quad+\left\langle g_{\delta_{k}}(x_{k}),(B_{k}-\alpha_{k})(y_{k-1}-x_{k})+\alpha_{k}(z_{k}-x_{k})\right\rangle.

From Line
13
in AIBM we get

(B_{k}-\alpha_{k})(y_{k-1}-x_{k})+\alpha_{k}(z_{k}-x_{k})=\alpha_{k}(z_{k}-z_{k-1}).

Thus, we conclude

\displaystyle\Psi_{k}^{*}

\displaystyle\geq(A_{k}-B_{k})f(y_{k-1})-E_{k-1}+V(z_{k},z_{k-1})+B_{k}f(x_{k})+\alpha_{k}\left\langle g_{\delta_{k}}(x_{k}),z_{k}-z_{k-1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=(A_{k}-B_{k})f(y_{k-1})-E_{k-1}

\displaystyle\quad+B_{k}\left(f_{\delta_{k}}(x_{k})+\frac{1}{B_{k}}V(z_{k},z_{k-1})+\left\langle g_{\delta_{k}}(x_{k}),z_{k}-z_{k-1}\right\rangle\right).

Because of
\frac{1}{B_{k}}=\frac{L_{k}\alpha_{k}^{\gamma}}{B_{k}^{\gamma}}
(from Line

\frac{\alpha_{t}}{B_{k}}(z_{k}-z_{k-1})=w_{k}-x_{k}

\displaystyle\Psi_{k}^{*}

\displaystyle\quad+B_{k}\left(f_{\delta_{k}}(x_{k})+L_{k}\left(\frac{\alpha_{k}}{B_{k}}\right)^{\gamma}V(z_{k},z_{k-1})+\left\langle g_{\delta_{k}}(x_{k}),w_{k}-x_{k}\right\rangle\right).

\displaystyle\Psi_{k}^{*}

f(y_{k})-f(x_{*})\leq\frac{d(x_{*})}{A_{k}}+\frac{1}{A_{k}}\sum_{i=0}^{k}B_{i}\delta_{i},\quad\forall k\geq 0,

(57)

where

Proof.

From Lemma
5.2
, we have

\displaystyle A_{k}f(y_{k})-E_{k}

\displaystyle\;\leq\Psi_{k}^{*}=\min_{x\in Q}\left\{d(x)+\sum_{i=0}^{k}\alpha_{i}l_{\delta_{i}}(x_{i},x)\right\}\leq d(x_{*})+\sum_{i=0}^{k}\alpha_{i}l_{\delta_{i}}(x_{i},x_{*})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{oracle_smooth}}}{{\leq}}d(x_{*})+\sum_{i=0}^{k}\alpha_{i}f(x_{*})=d(x_{*})+A_{k}f(x_{*}).

This means,

A_{k}f(y_{k})-E_{k}\leq d(x_{*})+A_{k}f(x_{*}),

i.e.,

A_{k}\left(f(y_{k})-f(x_{*})\right)\leq d(x_{*})+E_{k}=d(x_{*})+\sum_{i=0}^{k}B_{i}\delta_{i}.

Therefore,

f(y_{k})-f(x^{*})\leq\frac{h(x_{*})}{A_{k}}+\frac{1}{A_{k}}\sum_{i=0}^{k}B_{i}\delta_{i},

which completes the proof.
∎

We will prove the following theorem to evaluate the convergence rate of Algorithm
4
(AIBM).

Theorem 5.4
.

Bregman divergence
V(\cdot,\cdot)
satisfies the triangular scaling property with scaling factor
\gamma\in(1,2]
. Let

be a convex and relatively

-smooth function, equipped with a first-order

-oracle. Let

be the intermediate parameter,

(58)

Proof.

First, for

and

Let us find the lower bound for

and the upper bound for

•

For

Thus,

A_{k}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\alpha_{i}\geq\frac{1}{\max_{0\leq i\leq k}L_{i}}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\left(\frac{i+2p}{2p}\right)^{(p-1)(\gamma-1)}.

But

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}\left(\frac{i+2p}{2p}\right)^{(p-1)(\gamma-1)}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{k}\left(\frac{x+2p}{2p}\right)^{(p-1)(\gamma-1)}dx+\alpha_{0}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{(p-1)(\gamma-1)+1}\left(\frac{k+2p}{2p}\right)^{(p-1)(\gamma-1)+1}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{k+2p}{2p}\right)^{(p-1)(\gamma-1)+1}.

Therefore, since

(59)

•

For

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{k+2p}{2p}\right)^{p-1}\left(\max_{0\leq i\leq k}\delta_{i}\right)A_{k}.

(60)

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed some accelerated methods for solving the problem of minimizing the convex differentiable and relatively smooth function. The first proposed method is an adaptive fast gradient method based on a similar triangles method with an inexact oracle, which uses a special triangular scaling property, for the used Bregman divergence. The other two proposed methods are an adaptive accelerated Bregman proximal gradient method using an inexact oracle and its adaptive version. The adaptivity in all proposed methods is for the relatively smoothness parameter. We concluded the convergence rate of all proposed methods and proved that they are universal in the sense that they are applicable not only to relatively smooth but also to relatively Lipschitz continuous optimization problems. We also proposed an automatically adaptive intermediate Bregman method which interpolates between slower but more robust algorithms non-accelerated and faster, but less robust accelerated algorithms. In the end, we introduced some results of the numerical experiments that were conducted, demonstrating the advantages of using the proposed algorithms for the Poisson inverse problem. Despite the accumulation of the inexactness, as shown in the estimation of the convergence rate of the proposed algorithms, the experimental results for the proposed methods were better than those of the accelerated and non-accelerated variants of the proximal gradient Bregman method. The best performance compared to other approaches was shown by the adaptive version of the accelerated proximal gradient Bregman method with an inexact oracle. With increasing the value of the triangular scaling parameter, the accuracy and convergence of accelerated proximal methods improved.

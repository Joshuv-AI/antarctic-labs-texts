Essential dynamics in chaotic attractors
Eran Igra

Abstract
. We prove that if a smooth vector field F F of S 3 S^{3} generates a sufficiently complicated heteroclinic knot, the flow also generates infinitely many periodic orbits, which persist under smooth perturbations which preserve the heteroclinic knot. Consequentially, we then associate a Template with the flow dynamics - regardless of whether F F satisfies any hyperbolicity condition or not. In addition, inspired by the Thurston-Nielsen Classification Theorem, we also conclude topological criteria for the existence of chaotic dynamics for three-dimensional flows - which we apply to study both the Rössler and Lorenz attractors.

Let

Proof.

We prove that whenever

Corollary 3.7

Let

Theorem 4.16
.

Assume

is a smooth vector field of

and

We prove Th.
4.16
by contradiction. To do so assume there exists an invariant set
\Lambda
as above which includes the fixed points for

\Lambda
and the continuity of the flow it follows

By assumption we know

and

by

5.
Discussion

Having used both Th.
3.1
and the Orbit Index Theory to study the Essential Dynamics of three-dimensional flows in this section we outline how the results of this paper can possibly be extended - and in particular, discuss how and where they possibly fit within the larger theory of low-dimensional dynamics. As stated at the introduction our results above were strongly motivated by the theory of Surface Dynamics as presented in
[
37
]
- therefore we begin this section by comparing our results with the said theory. Following that we discuss our results in the wider context of hyperbolic dynamical systems, after which we heuristically connect them with the ”Chaotic Hypothesis” known from
[
59
]
.

To begin, first recall that given a surface homeomorphism
h:S\to S
we can define its Essential Class of periodic orbits in
S
as the collection of periodic orbits which persist under isotopies (see Def.
2.3
). Now, recall the Thurston-Nielsen Classification Theorem which states that if
S
is a surface of negative Euler characteristic any homeomorphism
h:S\to S
is isotopic to precisely one of the following (see Th.
2.4
):

(1)

A periodic homeomorphism
f:S\to S
, i.e., there exists some
n>0
s.t.
f^{n}=Id
. In that case, whatever complex dynamics
h
may have in
S
can be removed by an isotopy of
h
. As such, in this case the Essential Class of periodic orbits for
h
in
S
is at most finite.

(2)

A Pseudo-Anosov map
f:S\to S
with infinitely many periodic orbits in
S
. In this case the complex dynamics of
f
persist as
f
is isotoped back to
h
(see Th.
2.2
) - i.e., the dynamics of
h
are complex at the very least like those of
f
, and the Essential Class for
h
is
S
is infinite.

(3)

A reducible map
f:S\to S
- i.e.,
S
can be decomposed into a finite number of surfaces
S_{1},...,S_{n}
of negative Euler characteristic, glued to one another along some invariant curve(s) in
S
. Moreover, for every
i=1,...,n

f|_{S_{i}}
is either periodic or Pseudo-Anosov, and the Essential Class is as follows.

As we have proven in Th.
4.3
and Th.
4.9
there exist vector fields on
S^{3}
whose Essential Class of periodic dynamics (w.r.t. some heteroclinic knot
H
) is infinite - and in Th.
4.14
we have also proven the existence of vector fields whose Essential Class is at most a finite collection of periodic orbits. In other words, we have proven the existence of analogues to both Pseudo-Anosov and Periodic maps. We believe these analogies are not mere coincidence. In fact, we believe Th.
3.1
and Th.
3.6
are both part of a larger theory which ties the topology of a given three-dimensional phase space with the possible dynamics which can exist on it - similarly to how the Arnold Conjecture connects between the dynamical properties of a given symplectomorphism and the phase space it is defined on (see Conjecture 2.1 in
[
30
]
). This motivates us to conjecture the following:

Conjecture 5.1
.

Let
F
be a smooth vector field on
S^{3}
with fixed points
\{x_{1},...,x_{n}\}
, and let
H
be a one-dimensional set invariant under the flow, s.t. each component

of

satisfies one of the following:

(1)

C
is a homeomorphic to
S^{1}
.

(2)

is homeomorphic to
(0,1)
.

(3)

For every

there exists some
C
s.t.

- i.e., all the fixed points of
F
are on
H
.

Now, recall we denote the Orbit Index of a periodic orbit

of

by

(see Def.
2.19
). Then, under the assumptions above

can be deformed
rel

H
to a smooth vector field

satisfies precisely one of the following:

(1)

is

, s.t.

(2)

w.r.t.

(3)

is

invariant under the flow of

S\cap{H}\subseteq\{x_{1},...,x_{n}\}

If true, this conjecture generalizes the Thurston-Nielsen Classification Theorem and Th.
2.2
to three-dimensional flows - as well as greatly extends Th.
3.1

which can be deformed

is essentially the gluing two dynamical systems along some invariant surface (say, some two-dimensional invariant manifold).

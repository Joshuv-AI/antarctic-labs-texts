Immunoglobulins are beneficial in SARS-CoV-2 vaccine-induced small fibre neuropathy even 3 years after onset
Authors: Josef Finsterer
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTSARS-CoV-2 vaccination (SC2V) can be complicated by post-acute COVID-19 vaccination syndrome (PACVS). One manifestation of PACVS is small fibre neuropathy (SFN). A positive effect of intravenous immunoglobulins (IVIGs) even 3 years after the onset of PACVS is not known. The patient is a 52-year-old woman who developed PACVS after the second BNT162b2 vaccination in June 2021, which manifested clinically with SFN, myopericarditis, coagulopathy, and ocular, dermatologic, immunologic, and central nervous system (CNS) abnormalities. The SFN itself manifested as sensory disturbances, arterial hypotension and postural tachycardia syndrome (POTS). After the patient received three cycles of IVIGs, starting in June 2024, three years after the onset of PACVS, there was a significant positive effect on her SFN and other symptoms, as evidenced by various specific investigations. This case demonstrates that symptoms and signs of SFN as a manifestation of PACVS may benefit from the administration of IVIGs even years after onset.

Introduction

Although it is still denied by part of the scientific community that the SARS-CoV-2 vaccine (SC2V), which was approved as an emergency measure during the pandemic, some of the vaccinees have caused severe adverse reactions.[1] The side effects affect all organs and tissues, particularly the nervous and cardiovascular systems.[2] One of the most common neurological side effects is small fibre neuropathy (SFN), which usually affects sensory and autonomic fibres.[3] SC2V-associated SFN is thought to result from an immune reaction against the vaccine or components of the vaccine preparation.[4] The treatment of SC2V-associated SFN is not standardised, so everyone is doing their own thing. Agents that have been tried include glucocorticoids, intravenous immunoglobulins (IVIGs), plasma exchange, immunosuppressive treatment with methotrexate, nerve stimulation and pregabalin.[5] IVIGs have been reported to have an inadequate response in SFN patients.[6] A positive effect of IVIGs on SFN three years after vaccination has not been reported. The study is relevant for general practitioners, as they may be the first to see patients with neurological complications following SC2V. Informed consent was obtained from the patient, and the study was approved by the institutional review board.

Case Report

The patient is a 52-year-old woman who developed severe post-acute SARS-CoV-2 vaccination syndrome (PACVS) shortly after the second dose of the BNT162b2 vaccine in June 2021. Some of its clinical aspects have been reported previously.[7] Despite a number of treatment approaches in the following years (e.g. glucocorticoids, HELP apheresis and ivabradine), she did not fully recover and in particular the clinical manifestations of SFN persisted. Symptoms of SFN included hypoesthesias, paresthesias, pallhypesthesias, sudomotor disturbances with hypohidrosis, orthostasis, palpitations, tachycardia, increased blood pressure variability, postural tachycardia syndrome (POTS), presyncope, decreased baroreceptor sensitivity, delayed bladder emptying, insomnia and orthostatic intolerance.

Although SFN was already diagnosed in August 2023 by skin biopsy and confirmed by quantitative sudomotor axon reflex test (QSART), quantitative sensory test (QST) and tilt table examination, and SFN was classified as secondary immune-mediated, she did not receive specific immunological therapy, but only symptomatic and supportive treatment. It was not until June 2024 that she received her first cycle of IVIGs, followed by a second and third cycle [Table 1]. Symptoms that were present before IVIG treatment and completely resolved after three IVIG cycles included nausea and loss of appetite, dry cough, ptosis, right facial palsy, and chewing weakness [Table 1]. Symptoms that improved to varying degrees include fatigue, exercise intolerance, post-exertional malaise, dyspnoea on exertion, chest tightness, anginal chest pain, orthostasis, presyncope, POTS, hoarseness, globus sensation, hypophonia, tongue paraesthesia, hypertonia of the axial and respiratory muscles, numbness of the right face, hypoesthesia and paraesthesia of the hands and feet, headache, impaired consciousness, brain fog, memory impairment, hypersensitivity to light/noise, and insomnia [Table 1]. Symptoms that did not change with IVIGs included sore lymph nodes, arterial hypotension, increased blood pressure variability, hand weakness, heat intolerance, and loss of ability to multitask [Table 1].

Self-measurements of blood pressure before, during, and after the three IVIG cycles showed an overall reduction in the difference between systolic blood pressure values and heart rate in the supine position compared to the upright position [Figure 1]. The tilt table tests before and after the IVIGs also showed a significant reduction in the difference in blood pressure between the supine and upright positions [Table 2]. Serum norepinephrine levels in supine and upright positions also decreased significantly with IVIGs [Table 2]. QSART volume and latency decreased at most of the sites tested [Table 2]. QST percentiles for vibration, cold, and pain decreased at several sites [Table 2]. Baroreflex sensitivity also decreased after IVIGs [Table 2]. Cardiac MRI before and after IVIGs showed a significant reduction in late gadolinium enhancement, but left and right ejection fraction, left and right atrial size, and stroke volume remained unchanged [Table 2]. The supine heart rate variability index increased, and the Sudoscan became slightly abnormal after therapy.

Discussion

The presented patient is interesting for the positive effect of IVIGs on SFN and other symptoms. The subjective improvement could be objectified by home blood pressure measurement, tilt table test, QSART, QST, reduced noradrenaline serum levels and cardiac MRI. The study also showed that a positive effect on SFN symptoms can still be achieved three years after the onset of SFN. In the meantime, the patient has received a fourth and fifth IVIG cycle and symptoms and signs further improved or previous improvement persisted.

The reason for the efficacy of IVIGs remains unknown, but previous studies have shown that IVIGs can be very beneficial, especially in patients with immune-mediated seropositive SFN with antibodies against TS-HDS, FGFR-3 or plexin and seronegative SFN[8] as well as in large fibre neuropathies such as Guillain-Barre syndrome, CIDP or multifocal motor neuropathy.[9] In a study of 16 patients with SFN, nine of them received IVIGs, which showed a positive effect in all of them.[10] However, there are also studies that did not show a positive effect, particularly on SFN-associated pain. There is also some evidence that IVIGs are beneficial in SFN after SARS-CoV-2 infection.[11] The reason that not all symptoms disappeared completely remains unexplained, but it can be hypothesised that their underlying pathophysiology was not amenable to IVIGs. It is also interesting to note that not only the symptoms of SFN but also fatigue, exercise intolerance, nausea, and loss of appetite improved with IVIGs. This positive effect could be explained by the observation that providing antibodies boosts the immune system in general. Why IVIGs work in some patients but not others remains unclear. Apparently, there is an immunological subtype of SC2V-related SFN that responds favourably to IVIGs.

The case shows that symptoms and signs of SFN as a manifestation of PACVS may profit from the administration of IVIGs even years after onset. Due to the high prevalence of SFN as a manifestation of PACVS, there is an urgent need to assess the effect of IVIGs on PACVS in a well-powered and appropriately designed randomised controlled study.

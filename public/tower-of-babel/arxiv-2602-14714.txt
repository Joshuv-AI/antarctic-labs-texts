Distributed Multi-Step Model Predictive Control for Consensus
Navid Noroozi

Abstract
This paper studies consensus of discrete-time multi-agent systems under time-varying directed communication, state and input constraints using a distributed multi-step model predictive control (MPC) framework. Consensus is recast as stabilization of the agreement set, and a geometric viewpoint based on convex-hull invariance and strict interiority is adopted. Building on an existing geometric necessary and sufficient condition for agreement, we show that enforcing terminal inclusion in local neighbor convex hulls guarantees hull invariance but does not, in general, imply the strict relative-interior property required for convergence. An explicit counterexample demonstrates that strictness cannot be deduced from feasibility and contraction constraints alone. To resolve this issue without shrinking feasible sets or altering primary performance objectives, a lexicographic tie-breaking mechanism is introduced. Among optimal (or near-optimal) MPC solutions, the proposed secondary criterion selects trajectories maximizing an interiority measure with respect to the neighbor hull. It is shown that whenever an interior feasible terminal state exists, this selection rule enforces the strictness condition required for asymptotic consensus. Explicit horizon conditions are derived for single- and double-integrator agents with bounded inputs, ensuring feasibility and automatic existence of interior feasible terminal points. The resulting scheme provides a distributed and implementable route to consensus via finite-step set-Lyapunov contraction. Numerical simulations with distributed inter-process communication illustrate monotone diameter decay and report per-agent computational complexity.



I
Introduction

Consensus and agreement in networked multi-agent systems are fundamental in distributed estimation, cooperative robotics, and large-scale cyber–physical systems.
A classical geometric viewpoint interprets consensus as
set stabilization
of the agreement set and characterizes convergence through convex-hull invariance and strictness properties of the induced state update map.
In particular, Moreau’s seminal results
[
9
]
establish necessary and sufficient conditions for asymptotic agreement under time-varying directed communication, relying on (i) convex-hull inclusion and (ii) a strictness condition excluding boundary-invariant trajectories, together with suitable connectivity assumptions.

Model predictive control (MPC) provides a systematic framework to incorporate constraints, performance objectives, and prediction models.
This has motivated a large body of work on distributed MPC (DMPC) for cooperative control and agreement.
Early distributed receding-horizon schemes addressed stabilization and formation problems by coupling local MPC updates through exchanged predicted trajectories and by imposing compatibility constraints to ensure closed-loop stability
[
2
,
7
]
.
Building on this direction, Ferrari-Trecate
et al.
developed MPC schemes tailored to consensus of agents with single- and double-integrator dynamics under bounded inputs and time-varying communication, with convergence proofs that leverage geometric properties of optimal trajectories and Moreau-type strictness arguments
[
3
]
.
Their work remains one of the closest references to the present paper in terms of problem formulation (consensus under constraints and directed switching graphs) and emphasis on implementable MPC protocols for integrator models.

Beyond integrator consensus, DMPC consensus has been studied for broader linear and nonlinear multi-agent models and for additional network effects.
For example, robust DMPC frameworks have been proposed to handle input constraints together with bounded time-varying communication delays, establishing recursive feasibility and robust convergence for linear discrete-time MASs
[
12
]
.
Consensus-oriented DMPC has also been combined with reduced communication mechanisms such as (self-)triggered or intermittent exchange of predicted trajectories while maintaining feasibility and convergence guarantees
[
14
]
.
For nonlinear or higher-order agents, distributed MPC designs have been investigated to achieve consensus-like objectives under constraints (e.g., second-order nonlinear MASs) using tailored terminal ingredients and feasibility arguments
[
4
]
.
More recently, the scope has expanded toward
optimal
and
output
consensus of heterogeneous agents over directed graphs, where the DMPC problem is formulated to balance transient performance with convergence to an optimal agreement point
[
1
]
.
Complementary tutorial and survey articles summarize algorithmic paradigms and stability mechanisms in DMPC, including coupling architectures, feasibility preservation, and convergence tools
[
11
]
.

I-A

Motivation and gap

Despite this progress, a recurring conceptual difficulty in DMPC consensus is the role of
strictness
.
Moreau-type consensus theorems require, in one form or another, that when disagreement is present, the next iteration enters the relative interior of a suitable neighbor hull, thereby preventing trajectories from remaining trapped on invariant faces of the global convex hull
[
9
]
.
In many MPC-based protocols, convex-hull
invariance
can be enforced directly by terminal constraints, but
strict
interiority is often obtained indirectly via problem-specific geometric arguments (e.g., nonzero first step for integrators) or by strengthening terminal conditions in ways that may shrink feasibility sets and complicate robustness claims
[
3
]
.
For general nonlinear dynamics and general convex constraints, it is therefore natural to ask:

Which strictness property is truly necessary for consensus, and how can it be enforced by an MPC implementation without altering feasibility?

I-B

Contributions

This paper proposes a distributed multi-step MPC framework for asymptotic consensus in discrete-time networks that makes the strictness issue explicit and resolves it with a minimal implementation mechanism.
Our approach combines (i) a design-neutral geometric consensus core, (ii) a finite-step Lyapunov viewpoint for the agreement set, and (iii) a feasibility-preserving tie-breaking rule that enforces the required interiority whenever it is achievable.

Consensus as finite-step set stabilization:

We recast consensus as stabilization of the agreement set and employ the disagreement diameter as a proper set-Lyapunov candidate.
We restate a Moreau-type necessary/sufficient consensus theorem in our notation for time-varying directed graphs, highlighting that convex-hull inclusion and a strictness (relative-interior) property form the essential geometric ingredients.

A distributed multi-step MPC template enforcing hull inclusion and finite-step barycenter contraction:

We introduce a local MPC problem that (a) constrains the terminal state to lie in the local neighbor convex hull, guaranteeing convex-hull invariance, and (b) enforces a finite-step contraction inequality toward a local barycenter.
This yields a modular route to consensus via finite-step Lyapunov decay
[
5
]
, provided that strictness can be ensured along closed-loop trajectories.
We note that the
feasibility of a finite-step Lyapunov decay is ensured by a constructive converse control finite-step Lyapunov function theorem
, see
[
10
]
for more details.

Impossibility of deducing strictness from the baseline OCP and a minimal remedy:

We show by an explicit counterexample that, even under feasibility and joint connectivity, the baseline OCP does
not
imply the required relative-interior strictness: feasible (and even cost-improving) solutions may lie on the boundary of the neighbor hull, allowing boundary-invariant behavior.
To remedy this without shrinking the feasible set or changing the primary objective value, we introduce a two-stage
lexicographic
selection rule:
among all (near-)optimal primary solutions, select one that maximizes an interiority measure (distance to the hull boundary).
We prove that whenever an interior feasible terminal point exists, this tie-break enforces relative-interior updates and restores the strictness condition required by the geometric consensus theorem.

Explicit integrator specializations and computational evidence:

For single- and double-integrator agents with input bounds (and no additional state constraints), we derive explicit horizon conditions guaranteeing feasibility and automatic existence of interior feasible terminal points, leading to consensus under the proposed lexicographic implementation.
We provide distributed numerical simulations (including per-agent solve-time statistics and problem dimensions) demonstrating monotone decay of the diameter and illustrating if the lexicographic stage is activated or not in practice.

I-C

Relation to prior DMPC consensus work

The closest comparison is to
[
3
]
, which studies MPC consensus for single- and double-integrator models and proves strictness using integrator-specific optimal-trajectory geometry.
In contrast, the present paper (a) separates feasibility from strictness at the level of implemented closed-loop updates, (b) provides an impossibility result explaining why strictness cannot be expected from a generic terminal hull constraint plus a contractive inequality alone, and (c) enforces strictness via a minimal feasibility-preserving selection rule.
Compared with robust/delay-aware DMPC consensus designs such as
[
12
]
, our emphasis is not on delay compensation but on identifying and enforcing the geometric strictness mechanism needed for agreement under general nonlinear dynamics and time-varying directed graphs.
Finally, relative to recent optimal/output-consensus DMPC formulations over directed graphs
[
1
]
, our focus is on a set-stabilization/finite-step Lyapunov pathway to
asymptotic
agreement, together with an implementation mechanism that guarantees the strictness condition required by the underlying consensus theorem.

under feasibility and connectivity.

Preliminaries

This section covers notational and conceptual preliminaries, required in the next section.

Norms and balls

Let
\|\cdot\|
be a norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
For
r\geq 0
,
c\geq 0
, denote
\mathbb{B}_{r}(c):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\|x-c\|\leq r\}
.
For
c=0
, we drop the argument
c
from the notation, i.e.
\mathbb{B}_{r}:=\mathbb{B}_{r}(0)
, moreover,
\mathbb{B}:=\mathbb{B}_{1}
.
On
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\ell}
we use the product Euclidean norm

\|x\|:=\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\|x_{i}\|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

Distance to a closed set

For a nonempty closed set
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, define the distance

|x|_{A}:=\inf_{y\in\mathcal{A}}\|x-y\|.

For
\mathcal{A}
closed,
|x|_{\mathcal{A}}=0\iff x\in\mathcal{A}
, and
x\mapsto|x|_{\mathcal{A}}
is Lipschitz.

Convex hull

For a finite set
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{m}\}
define its convex hull

\operatorname{co}\{x_{1},\dots,x_{m}\}:=\Big\{\sum_{k=1}^{m}\lambda_{k}x_{k}:\ \lambda_{k}\geq 0,\ \sum_{k=1}^{m}\lambda_{k}=1\Big\}.

In the sequel, we often abbreviate
\mathcal{C}:=\operatorname{co}\{x_{1},\dots,x_{m}\}
.
Let
\mathrm{aff}(\mathcal{C})
denote the affine hull of
\mathcal{C}
. We interpret
\partial\mathcal{C}
and
\mathrm{dist}(\cdot,\partial\mathcal{C})

as
relative
notions within
\mathrm{aff}(\mathcal{C})
, i.e.
\partial\mathcal{C}
is the topological boundary of
\mathcal{C}
as a subset of
\mathrm{aff}(\mathcal{C})
, and distances are Euclidean distances in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

(which agree with distances measured within
\mathrm{aff}(\mathcal{C})
).

Comparison function classes

A continuous function
\alpha:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is in class
\mathcal{K}

if
\alpha
is strictly increasing and
\alpha(0)=0
. It is in
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
if additionally
\alpha(s)\to\infty
as
s\to\infty
.
A function
\beta:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is in
\mathcal{KL}
if

\beta(\cdot,t)\in\mathcal{K}
for all
t
and
\beta(s,t)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
for each fixed
s
.
The identity function is denoted by
\id
.

III
Problem Formulation

Consider
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
agents with discrete-time dynamics

x_{i}(t+1)=g_{i}(x_{i}(t),u_{i}(t)),\qquad i=1,\dots,\ell,

(1)

where
x_{i}(t)\in\mathcal{X}_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
u_{i}(t)\in\mathcal{U}_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}
.

Assumption 1

(
\mathcal{K}
-boundedness)
.

For each
i
, the map
g_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is continuous and locally Lipschitz in
(x_{i},u_{i})
.
Moreover, it is
\mathcal{K}
-bounded: there exist
\kappa_{1,i},\kappa_{2,i}\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
such that

\|g_{i}(x_{i},u_{i})\|\leq\kappa_{1,i}(\|x_{i}\|)+\kappa_{2,i}(\|u_{i}\|),\qquad\forall(x_{i},u_{i})\in(\mathcal{X}_{i},\mathcal{U}_{i}).

Assumption
1
ensures well-posedness of trajectories under bounded inputs and is standard in nonlinear MPC and nonlinear systems stability analysis
[
6
,
5
]
.

III-A

Graph theory and connectivity

Communication graph and neighbors.

At outer time index
j
(defined below), the communication structure is a directed graph

\mathcal{G}(j)=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E}(j))
, with
\mathcal{V}=\{1,\dots,\ell\}
.
The neighbor set of agent
i
at time
j
is

\mathcal{N}_{i}(j):=\{k\in\mathcal{V}:\ (k,i)\in\mathcal{E}(j)\}.

Joint connectivity (bounded window).

Assumption 2

(Joint spanning-tree connectivity)
.

There exists
B\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, the union graph

\mathcal{G}[j,j+B-1]:=(\mathcal{V},\ \cup_{s=j}^{j+B-1}\mathcal{E}(s))

contains a directed spanning tree. In the bidirectional case, this is equivalent to joint connectivity.

III-B

Finite-step control Lyapunov function and barycenter tracking

Outer sampling and information pattern

Fix
M\in\mathbb{N}
.
Define sampled states (outer index)

z_{i}(j):=x_{i}(jM),\qquad z(j)=(z_{1}(j),\dots,z_{\ell}(j)).

(2)

At each outer time
j
, each agent receives the neighbor samples
\{z_{k}(j):k\in\mathcal{N}_{i}(j)\}

only
and then
computes an
M
-step open-loop control sequence to apply on inner times
t=jM,\dots,jM+M-1
.

Local barycenter.

Define the local (time-varying) barycenter as

\bar{z}_{i}(j):=\frac{1}{1+|\mathcal{N}_{i}(j)|}\sum_{k\in\{i\}\cup\mathcal{N}_{i}(j)}z_{k}(j).

(3)

We denote the set covering the evolution of all possible local barycenter trajectories
\bar{z}_{i}
over time by
\bar{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}
.

Finite-step admissible control

Fix
M\in\mathbb{N}
.
Given an outer time
j\geq 0
, let
{\bf u}_{i}=(u_{i}(0),u_{i}(1),\ldots)
denote a possibly infinite control sequence for agent (
1
), where
u_{i}(t)\in\mathcal{U}_{i}
for all
t=0,1,\ldots
.
If we only study trajectories of (
1
) over a finite horizon, we might restrict to finite control sequences denoted by
\mathbf{u}_{i}(0:k-1):=(u_{i}(0),\dots,u_{i}(k-1))\in\mathcal{U}_{i}^{k}
.

Denote
e_{i}(jM):=x_{i}(jM)-\bar{z}(j)
, with
x_{i}(jM)\in\mathcal{X}_{i}
and
\bar{z}_{i}(j)
as in (
3
),
\bar{z}_{i}\in\bar{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}
.
We aim to compute any control sequence
\bar{u}_{i}
such that the state
x_{i}
tracks the local barycenter trajectory
\bar{z}_{i}
, i.e. the error trajectory
\|e_{i}(jM)\|\to 0

.

Definition 1
.

Let
M\in\mathbb{N}
and
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
be given.
Consider an agent (
1
) satisfying Assumption
1
and a map
q_{i}:\mathcal{X}_{i}\times\bar{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}\to\mathcal{U}_{i}^{M}

g_{i}\big(x_{i}(jM+k-1),q_{i}^{k}(x_{i}(jM),\bar{z}_{i}(j))\big)\in\mathcal{X}_{i}

Now we define a notion of asymptotic barycenter tracking for each agent

.

Definition 2

(Asymptotic barycenter tracking)

Consider a network of agents (

For each agent

(4)

Following the converse Lyapunov function

III-C

Consensus problem and its reformulation as set stabilization

We aim to relate a consensus problem to a set stabilization problem.
Define the consensus set in
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\ell}
as

\mathcal{A}:=\{x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell}):x_{1}=\cdots=x_{\ell}\}.

Consensus is indeed set stabilization with respect to
\mathcal{A}
.
Towards this end, we introduce the following notions.

Local neighbor hull

Given a network of agents (
1
) with an associated directed graph

\mathcal{G}(j)=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E}(j))
, define the
local neighbor hull
as

\mathcal{C}_{i}(j):=\operatorname{co}\Big(\{z_{i}(j)\}\cup\{z_{k}(j):k\in\mathcal{N}_{i}(j)\}\Big),

(6)

and the
global hull
is defined by

H(z(j)):=\operatorname{co}\{z_{1}(j),\dots,z_{\ell}(j)\}.

(7)

Now define the
disagreement diameter

V:\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)^{\ell}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
by

V(z(j)):=\operatorname{diam}(H(z(j)))=\max_{p,q\in\mathcal{V}}\|z_{p}(j)-z_{q}(j)\|.

(8)

Lemma 1

(Diameter is a proper set-Lyapunov candidate)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\{z_{1}=\cdots=z_{\ell}\}
. Then:

(i)

V(z)=0\iff z\in\mathcal{A}
and
V(z)>0
otherwise.

(ii)

V
is continuous (globally Lipschitz) in
z
.

(iii)

With
|z|_{\mathcal{A}}
defined w.r.t. the product Euclidean norm, one has

\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ell}}|z|_{\mathcal{A}}\leq V(z)\leq 2|z|_{\mathcal{A}}.

(9)

Proof.

(1) is immediate from (
8
). (2) holds because
V
is the maximum of finitely many continuous functions.

For (3), let
\bar{z}:=\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}z_{i}
. Then
|z|_{\mathcal{A}}=(\sum_{i}\|z_{i}-\bar{z}\|^{2})^{1/2}
.
For any
p,q
,

\|z_{p}-z_{q}\|\leq\|z_{p}-\bar{z}\|+\|z_{q}-\bar{z}\|\leq 2|z|_{\mathcal{A}},

hence
V(z)\leq 2|z|_{\mathcal{A}}
.
Moreover, for any
i
,

\|z_{i}-\bar{z}\|=\Big\|\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}(z_{i}-z_{k})\Big\|\leq\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}\|z_{i}-z_{k}\|\leq V(z).

Thus
|z|_{\mathcal{A}}\leq\sqrt{\ell}\max_{i}\|z_{i}-\bar{z}\|\leq\sqrt{\ell}V(z)
, i.e.
V(z)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ell}}|z|_{\mathcal{A}}
.
∎

Definition 3

(Asymptotic consensus)
.

Consider the diameter function
V
as in (
8
) for a network of agents (
1
) with an associated directed graph

\mathcal{G}(j)=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E}(j))
.
The network achieves asymptotic consensus if

\limsup_{j\to\infty}V\big(z(j)\big)\to 0.

IV
Necessary and Sufficient Geometric Conditions for Consensus

In
[
9
]
, Moreau presents a number of necessary and sufficient conditions for consensus for agents with nonlinear discrete-time dynamics.
Towards this end, he provides a (strict) convexity condition, given by
[
9
, Assumption 1]
plus a number of graph connectivity conditions, see
[
9
, Theorems 2, 3]
.
In this section, we reformulate these
[
9
, Assumption 1]
to our wording context.

Assumption 3

(strict convexity)
.

Let
z(j)=(z_{1}(j),\dots,z_{\ell}(j))\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\ell}
denote the agent variables at outer step
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
For each
i
define the local hull

\mathcal{C}_{i}(j)=\operatorname{co}\big(\{z_{i}(j)\}\cup\{z_{k}(j):k\in\mathcal{N}_{i}(j)\}\big).

There exists a constant
\eta\in[0,1)
such that for every
i
and
j
:

(i)

convex-hull inclusion:

z_{i}(j+1)\in\mathcal{C}_{i}(j).

(10)

(ii)

Relative interior (RI) strictness when disagreement is nonzero:

If
z_{i}(j)
is not equal to all neighbor values, i.e. if

z_{i}(j)\notin\bigcap_{k\in\mathcal{N}_{i}(j)}\{z_{k}(j)\},

then

z_{i}(j+1)\in\mathrm{ri}\big(\mathcal{C}_{i}(j)\big).

(11)

where
\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}):=\sup\{\|x-y\|:x,y\in\mathcal{C}\}
and
\partial\mathcal{C}
is the boundary.

Now we pack and restate
[
9
, Theorem 1, Theorem 2]
in the following theorem.

Theorem 1

(necessary and sufficient conditions for asymptotic consensus)
.

Consider a network of agents (
1
) with an associated directed graph
(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E}(j))
.
Consider any trajectory
z(\cdot)
generated by a distributed update law
u(\cdot)
.

(Necessity)

Assume that asymptotic consensus holds for all initial conditions and for all trajectories of the update law,
and assume further that the update law is
local
in the sense that
z_{i}(j+1)
depends only on

\{z_{k}(j):k\in\{i\}\cup\mathcal{N}_{i}(j)\}
.
Then necessarily:

(N1)

the hull inclusion (
10
) holds;

(N2)

the joint spanning-tree condition of Assumption
2
holds.

(Sufficiency)

If Assumptions
3
and
2
hold, then
z(\cdot)
satisfies
asymptotic consensus in the sense of Definition
3
.

Remark 1

(Counterparts in
[
9
]
)
.

Assumption
3
is the point-valued specialization of Moreau’s convexity and strictness
assumptions (see
[
9
, Assumption 1]
), where our hull inclusion (
10
) corresponds
to his convexity property and our RI strictness (
11
) corresponds to his
strict inclusion property (expressed as RI membership when disagreement is nonzero).
The
sufficiency
part of Theorem
1
is the directed-graph asymptotic-consensus result
in
[
9

, restated in our notation.
The

part stated here is a standard locality-based necessity:
without (N1) the global hull may expand, and without (N2) the network can be split into groups without mutual
influence, so agreement for all initial conditions is impossible.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

We prove necessity and sufficiency separately.

Necessity of (N1)

Assume asymptotic consensus holds for all initial conditions and the update law is local.
Suppose by contradiction that (
10
) fails for some
i
and
j
, i.e.

z_{i}(j+1)\notin\mathcal{C}_{i}(j)
for some admissible configuration
z(j)
.
Since
\mathcal{C}_{i}(j)
is convex and closed, by the separating-hyperplane theorem there exists a vector

v\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
such that

\langle v,z_{i}(j+1)\rangle\;>\;\sup_{x\in\mathcal{C}_{i}(j)}\langle v,x\rangle.

Because
\mathcal{C}_{i}(j)\subseteq H(z(j)):=\mathrm{co}\{z_{1}(j),\dots,z_{\ell}(j)\}
, the right-hand side is at most

\sup_{x\in H(z(j))}\langle v,x\rangle=\max_{k}\langle v,z_{k}(j)\rangle
. Hence

\max_{k}\langle v,z_{k}(j+1)\rangle\;\geq\;\langle v,z_{i}(j+1)\rangle\;>\;\max_{k}\langle v,z_{k}(j)\rangle.

Thus the support function of the global hull strictly increases in direction
v
, meaning the global hull
expands in that direction. By locality one can fix all agents outside
\{i\}\cup\mathcal{N}_{i}(j)
so that this
expansion cannot be compensated by other agents at time
j+1
. This contradicts asymptotic consensus for all
initial conditions. Therefore (
10
) is necessary.

VII
DMPC with Lexicographic Selection

The terminal constraints in OCP (
12
) guarantee the hull inclusion (
13
), but do not,
in general, force
x_{i}(M)
to lie in
\mathrm{ri}(\mathcal{C}_{i}(j))
whenever disagreement is
nonzero.
Indeed, boundary points of
\mathcal{C}_{i}(j)
may remain feasible and even optimal.

In this section, we propose a
minimal
addition to OCP (
12
) without changing its setting, hence, without touching the feasibility set.
To separate feasibility from implementation, we introduce the set of terminal points that can actually be realized under OCP (
12
).
Then we just introduce a
lexicographic selection
rule that prefers interior points when the optimizer is non-unique.

Fix an outer time
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and agent
i\in V
.
Let
x_{i}^{\star}(0{:}M-1)
denote the state trajectories of agents (
1
) under optimizers
u_{i}^{\star}(0{:}M-1)
of OCP (
12
).

The set of feasible terminal points under OCP (
12
) is defined as follows.

Definition 4

(Feasible terminal set)
.

For each
i
and
j
, define the
feasible terminal set
induced by OCP (
12
) as

F_{i}(j)\;:=\;\Big\{\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\ \Big|\ \exists u_{i}^{\star}(0{:}M-1)\,\,\,\mathrm{s.t.}\,\,\,x_{i}^{\star}(M)=\xi\,\Big\}.

We note that, by construction,
F_{i}(j)\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{i}(j)
.

VII-A

When OCP (
12
) does not automatically yield RI strictness

The question “what if OCP finds no solution such that
z_{i}(j{+}1)\in\mathrm{ri}(\mathcal{C}_{i}(j))
?” splits into
three logically distinct cases.

Three possibilities.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{i}(j)
be the local hull at time
j
.

(i)

Case A (singleton hull).
If
\mathcal{C}_{i}(j)=\{p\}
, then
\mathrm{ri}(\mathcal{C}_{i}(j))=\{p\}
and RI
strictness is automatic.
This occurs exactly when all points in

\{z_{i}(j)\}\cup\{z_{k}(j):k\in N_{i}(j)\}
coincide.

How to handle it:
no action needed; the update is already consistent with consensus.

(ii)

Case B (
\mathrm{ri}(\mathcal{C}_{i}(j))\neq\emptyset
but
not
chosen by primary optimum).

This is the typical situation with linear/convex objectives: the set of optimal terminal points
may lie on a face of
\mathcal{C}_{i}(j)
. In this case, there
exist
feasible interior points
(e.g.
\mathrm{ri}(\mathcal{C}_{i}(j))
is nonempty whenever
\mathcal{C}_{i}(j)
has more than one point), but the primary objective
does not select them.

How to handle it:
use a
lexicographic
(two-stage) selection rule that
preserves
the feasible set and the primary optimum value, but selects an interior point
whenever possible.
This is formalized below, see Subsection
VII-B
.

(iii)

Case C (
F_{i}(j)\cap\mathrm{ri}(\mathcal{C}_{i}(j))=\emptyset
)

In this case, consensus may still occur in special instances, but cannot be guaranteed in general from OCP (
12
) alone.

How to handle it:

We divide this case into two sub-cases:

•

If Case C occurs only finitely many times (or not on the “extreme” agents responsible for the
global hull diameter), consensus may still be provable under additional
trajectory
conditions.
However, such conditions are extra assumptions and generally hard to verify from OCP (
12
).

•

If Case C can occur infinitely often under persistent disagreement, then no RI strictness
argument can be guaranteed.
To rule it out, one must modify the formulation (e.g. soften constraints, change
terminal condition, or add an explicit interiority-promoting mechanism).
Any such modification changes either the feasible set or the implemented selection, and thus goes beyond OCP (
12
) as stated.

VII-B

Lexicographic (two-stage) selection among optimal solutions.

We now formalize the weakest modification that does
not
shrink the feasible set of OCP (
12
):
keep all constraints and the primary objective unchanged, but define a secondary selection among primary optimizers.

Assumption 5

(RI existence on feasible terminal set)
.

Whenever
\mathcal{C}_{i}(j)
is not a singleton, the feasible terminal set intersects its RI:

F_{i}(j)\cap\mathrm{ri}(\mathcal{C}_{i}(j))\neq\emptyset.

(15)

Remark 4

(Assumption
5
is design-neutral and minimal)
.

Assumption
5
is
necessary
for any implementation that aims to realize

x_{i}(M)\in\mathrm{ri}(\mathcal{C}_{i}(j))
while keeping the constraints of OCP (
12
) unchanged:
if
F_{i}(j)\cap\mathrm{ri}(\mathcal{C}_{i}(j))=\emptyset
, then RI strictness is impossible at that
(i,j)
without
changing the constraint set. The assumption does not impose margins, weights, or quantitative strictness; it
only asserts existence of at least one feasible RI terminal point.

Let
J_{i}
be the primary cost in OCP (
12
).
At each
(i,j)
:

(i)

Solve OCP (
12
) to obtain the optimal value
J_{i}^{\star}(j)
.

(ii)

Among all feasible solutions with
J_{i}\leq J_{i}^{\star}(j)
, select
x_{i}(M)
that maximizes an
interiority measure, e.g., see (
16
).

A convenient interiority measure that can be stated abstractly (without committing to a particular
polytope representation) is

\phi_{i,j}(\xi):=\mathrm{dist}(\xi,\partial\mathcal{C}_{i}(j)).

(16)

Then the secondary problem is

\max_{\xi\in F_{i}(j)}\ \phi_{i,j}(\xi)\qquad\text{s.t.}\quad\exists u_{i}^{\star}(0{:}M-1)\Rightarrow x_{i}(M)=\xi\text{ and }J_{i}\leq J_{i}^{\star}(j).

(17)

Any maximizer
\xi^{\dagger}
of (
17
) is selected, and the implemented update is

z_{i}(j{+}1)=\xi^{\dagger}
.
This preserves the original optimum value and only fixes a tie-breaking rule.

Remark 5

(No feasibility shrinkage)
.

The lexicographic selection rule does
not
shrink the feasibility set of OCP (
12
) and does not
add any additional constraint. It only specifies which solution is implemented when multiple primary optimizers
exist.

Lemma 3

(Lexicographic selection yields RI strictness whenever feasible)
.

Assume
\mathcal{C}_{i}(j)
is not a singleton and Assumption
5
holds at
(i,j)
.
Then the lexicographic selection produces
z_{i}(j+1)=x_{i}(M)\in\mathrm{ri}(\mathcal{C}_{i}(j))
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{C}_{i}(j)
is compact and convex,
\partial\mathcal{C}_{i}(j)
is closed and the function

\phi_{i,j}(\xi)=\mathrm{dist}(\xi,\partial\mathcal{C}_{i}(j))
is continuous.
For any
\xi\in\partial\mathcal{C}_{i}(j)
we have
\phi_{i,j}(\xi)=0
, while for any

\xi\in\mathrm{ri}(\mathcal{C}_{i}(j))
we have
\phi_{i,j}(\xi)>0
.
By Assumption
5
there exists a feasible
\tilde{\xi}\in F_{i}(j)\cap\mathrm{ri}(\mathcal{C}_{i}(j))
.
Hence the maximum of
\phi_{i,j}
over the feasible primary-optimal set is strictly positive, and any maximizer

\xi^{\dagger}
satisfies
\phi_{i,j}(\xi^{\dagger})>0
, thus
\xi^{\dagger}\notin\partial\mathcal{C}_{i}(j)
.
Since
\xi^{\dagger}\in F_{i}(j)\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{i}(j)
, we conclude
\xi^{\dagger}\in\mathrm{ri}(\mathcal{C}_{i}(j))
.
∎

In that case, one must either:
(i) accept an additional trajectory-level assumption (e.g. strictness occurs infinitely often on extreme
faces), or (ii) modify the MPC formulation (e.g. soften constraints or add an interiority-promoting term).

VII-D

Distributed multi-step MPC algorithm with lexicographic selection

Now we present our final distributed multi-step MPC algorithm with lexicographic selection.
Before proceeding further, we raise a remark aiming to numerically enhance the robustness of the proposed algorithm.

Remark 7

(On numerical implementation of the lexicographic selection)
.

The lexicographic (secondary) problem (
17
) is formulated with the exact primary-optimality constraint
J_{i}\leq J_{i}^{\star}(j)
.
All subsequent results, in particular Lemma
3
and Theorem
2
, are therefore stated in terms of the
primary-optimal set
of OCP (
12
).

In numerical implementations, however, optimal control problems are solved in finite precision, and solver tolerances may lead to solutions whose objective value differs slightly from
J_{i}^{\star}(j)
. In practice, this is often handled by enforcing the primary-optimality constraint in (
17
) up to numerical tolerances, or
equivalently by allowing a very small slack
\delta_{\mathrm{lex}}>0
and considering the
\delta_{\mathrm{lex}}
-suboptimal set

\{\,J_{i}\leq J_{i}^{\star}(j)+\delta_{\mathrm{lex}}\,\}.

If such a tolerance is introduced, the implemented terminal state may be only
near-optimal
rather than exactly primary-optimal, and Lemma
3
and Theorem
2
would need to be reworded accordingly, replacing the primary-optimal set by the
\delta_{\mathrm{lex}}
-suboptimal set.

Since this modification does not affect the conceptual role of the lexicographic selection but would unnecessarily complicate the theoretical exposition, we do not consider it explicitly in this paper. Nevertheless, the introduction of a small
\delta_{\mathrm{lex}}>0
is natural and often useful in numerical implementations.

Now we integrate the lexicographic selection into our original distributed multi-step MPC algorithm Algorithm
1
to summarize our final controller design.
Note that we consider the optional, practically useful lexicographic tolerance
\delta_{\mathrm{lex}}
according to Remark
7
.

Application to Agents with Linear Dynamics

We briefly specialize the discussion to heterogeneous discrete-time linear agents

x_{i}(t{+}1)=A_{i}x_{i}(t)+B_{i}u_{i}(t),\qquad x_{i}(t)\in\mathcal{X}_{i},\;u_{i}(t)\in\mathcal{U}_{i},

(18)

\mathcal{X}_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

Lemma 4

(Open-loop reachability ball in
M
steps)
.

Fix
i
and
M\in\mathbb{N}
. Define the
M
-step controllability matrix

C_{i,M}:=\big[B_{i}\;\;A_{i}B_{i}\;\;\dots\;\;A_{i}^{M-1}B_{i}\big]\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times(m_{i}M)}.

Assume
\mathrm{rank}(C_{i,M})=d
and that
\mathcal{U}_{i}
contains a Euclidean ball around the origin,
i.e., there exists
r_{u,i}>0
such that
B_{r_{u,i}}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{i}
.
Then for every initial state
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
the set of states reachable in
M
steps under inputs

contains a Euclidean ball:

\mathbb{B}_{\rho_{i}}(A_{i}^{M}\xi)\subseteq\Big\{\,x_{i}(M)\,:\,x_{i}(0)=\xi,\;u(0{:}M{-}1)\in\mathcal{U}_{i}^{M}\,\Big\},

where one may take
\rho_{i}:=\sigma_{\min}(C_{i,M})\,r_{u,i}>0
and

\sigma_{\min}(C_{i,M})
denotes the smallest singular value.

Proof.

Stack the inputs into
U:=(u(0)^{\top},\dots,u(M{-}1)^{\top})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}M}
. The
M
-step
state satisfies the standard lifting relation

x_{i}(M)=A_{i}^{M}\xi+C_{i,M}U.

Because
\mathbb{B}_{r_{u,i}}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{i}

\mathbb{B}_{r_{u,i}}^{M}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{i}^{M}
, and hence

\mathbb{B}_{r_{u,i}}^{m_{i}M}\subseteq\{U:u(k)\in\mathcal{U}_{i}\}
(up to identifying the product of balls with a ball
in the stacked space). Therefore

is contained in the
M
-step
reachable displacement set. Since
\mathrm{rank}(C_{i,M})=d
, the linear map
C_{i,M}

is onto and satisfies

C_{i,M}\mathbb{B}_{r_{u,i}}^{m_{i}M}\supseteq\mathbb{B}_{\sigma_{\min}(C_{i,M})r_{u,i}}^{d}=\mathbb{B}_{\rho_{i}},

which implies
\mathbb{B}_{\rho_{i}}(A_{i}^{M}\xi)\subseteq\{x_{i}(M)\}
as claimed.
∎

Define, as in Definition
4
, the feasible terminal set induced by OCP (
12

F_{i}(j):=\big\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\exists\,u_{i}^{\star}(0{:}M{-}1)\ \text{s.t.}\ x_{i}^{\star}(M)=\xi\big\},

Corollary 1

(Explicit LTI conditions implying feasibility and Assumption

Consider the distributed multi-step MPC based on OCP (

Fix

(L1)

(L2)

(Slack state constraints along the

Proof.

Fix
(i,j)

By (L2), there exists an admissible

Remark 8

(Interpretation and a simple sufficient special case)
.

Condition (L1) is an explicit, checkable
M
-step controllability condition.
Condition (L2) captures precisely the informal requirement that

there must exist at least one admissible

-step steering that reaches a neighborhood of the

and
\mathcal{U}_{i}

A particularly transparent sufficient case is the following: if
\mathcal{X}_{i}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
(or
\mathcal{X}_{i}

\mathbb{B}_{r_{u,i}}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{i}

implies that every point in a ball around

\bar{z}_{i}(j)
lies within that reachable ball
and
\mathcal{C}_{i}(j)
is non-singleton, then OCP (

) is feasible and Assumption
5
holds automatically.

VIII-A

Explicit special cases: single- and double-integrators

Throughout this subsection, we focus on single- and double-integrators and assume that only input constraints are present.
This is exactly the regime in which one can give closed-form feasibility and horizon bounds.
The results can be extended to additional state constraints by replacing the explicit bounds
below with an a priori invariant “tube” condition as in Corollary

Corollary 2

(Single-integrators: explicit horizon and automatic RI existence)
.

Consider agents with discrete-time single-integrator dynamics

IX-D

Discussion

The simulations demonstrate that:

•

The explicit finite-step bounds on
M
derived in Corollaries
2
and
3
are sufficient in practice.

•

The contraction of
V_{r}(j)
is consistent with the theoretical finite-step Lyapunov function and strictness arguments underlying Theorem
1
.

•

The lexicographic selection stage (Step 7 of Algorithm
2
is not activated in these scenarios, since the primary optimization already yields terminal points in the relative interior of
\mathcal{C}_{i}(j)
.

Thus, the lexicographic stage serves as a safeguard mechanism ensuring strict interiority when necessary, while remaining inactive when the primary problem already enforces the desired strictness property.
Overall, the distributed finite-step MPC with lexicographic selection achieves asymptotic consensus in both single- and double-integrator settings, fully consistent with the theoretical development of Section
VIII
.

IX-E

Computational complexity and implementation remarks

For each agent
i
and outer step
j
, the primary and (if activated) secondary problems are convex programs of fixed horizon length
M
.
The problem size gives a standard and implementation-agnostic complexity proxy
problem size
:
the number of scalar decision variables
n_{\mathrm{var}}
, the number of scalar equality constraints
n_{\mathrm{eq}}
, the number of scalar inequality constraints
n_{\mathrm{ineq}}
.

Since interior-point type solvers typically scale superlinearly in
n_{\mathrm{var}}
(often heuristically like
\mathcal{O}(n_{\mathrm{var}}^{3})
for dense instances), we report, for each
(i,j)
, the tuple

(n_{\mathrm{var}},n_{\mathrm{eq}},n_{\mathrm{ineq}})

as a solver-independent complexity proxy, together with the measured wall-clock solve times

T^{\mathrm{prim}}_{i}(j)
,
T^{\mathrm{lex}}_{i}(j)
,

T^{\mathrm{tot}}_{i}(j)=T^{\mathrm{prim}}_{i}(j)+T^{\mathrm{lex}}_{i}(j)
,
where
T^{\mathrm{lex}}_{i}(j)=0
if the lexicographic stage is not activated.
The tuple
(n_{\mathrm{var}},n_{\mathrm{eq}},n_{\mathrm{ineq}})
quantifies the size of the local MPC problem and can be used to compare different horizons
M
, models (single- vs. double-integrator), and constraint sets. The solve-time traces
T^{\mathrm{prim}}_{i}(j)
and
T^{\mathrm{lex}}_{i}(j)
provide an empirical measure of the real-time feasibility of Algorithm
2
under the chosen solver and hardware.
Together, these indicators yield a practical complexity characterization of the distributed finite-step MPC implementation.

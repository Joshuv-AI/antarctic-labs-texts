A stochastic first-order method with multi-extrapolated momentum for highly smooth unconstrained optimization
Chuan He

Abstract
In this paper, we consider unconstrained stochastic optimization problems in which the objective function exhibits higher-order smoothness. In particular, we propose a stochastic first-order method (SFOM) with multi-extrapolated momentum, where multiple extrapolations are performed in each iteration, followed by a momentum update based on these extrapolations. We show that the proposed SFOM can accelerate optimization by exploiting the higher-order smoothness of the objective function f f . Assuming that the p p th-order derivative of f f is Lipschitz continuous for some p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 , and under additional mild assumptions, we establish that our method achieves a sample and first-order operation complexity of 𝒪 ~ ( ϵ − ( 3 p + 1 ) / p ) \widetilde{{\mathcal{O}}}(\epsilon^{-(3p+1)/p}) for finding an ϵ \epsilon -stochastic stationary point x x that satisfies 𝔼 ​ [ ‖ ∇ f ​ ( x ) ‖ ] ≤ ϵ \mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(x)\|]\leq\epsilon . To the best of our knowledge, this is the first SFOM to leverage the Lipschitz continuity of arbitrarily higher-order derivatives of the objective function for acceleration. Preliminary numerical experiments validate the practical performance of our method and support our theoretical results.

Lemma 6
.

Let

\sum_{p=a}^{b}\zeta(p)\leq\int^{b+1/2}_{a-1/2}\zeta(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau

Proof.

Since
f
is convex, one has
\zeta(p)\leq\int_{p-1/2}^{p+1/2}\zeta(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau
for all

. It then follows that
\sum_{p=a}^{b}\zeta(p)\leq\int_{a-1/2}^{b+1/2}\zeta(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau
holds as desired.
∎

As a consequence of

, we consider
\zeta(\tau)=1/\tau^{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
, where
\tau\in(0,\infty)

\displaystyle\sum_{p=a}^{b}\frac{1}{p^{\alpha}}\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\ln\big(b+\frac{1}{2}\big)-\ln\big(a-\frac{1}{2}\big)&\text{if }\alpha=1,\\[4.0pt]
\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\big(\big(b+\frac{1}{2}\big)^{1-\alpha}-\big(a-\frac{1}{2}\big)^{1-\alpha}\big)&\text{if }\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,+\infty).\end{array}\right.

(69)

In addition, since

,

, and
k\geq 0
, it follows that

where the last inequality is due to

. These along with

69

Hence,
Eq.

65
holds as desired.

We finally prove
Eq.

30
. Using

64
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{1-\sum_{t=1}^{p-1}\theta_{k,t}/(p+1)}{1-\sum_{t=1}^{p-1}\theta_{k,t}}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{p}{(p+1)(1/\sum_{t=1}^{p-1}\theta_{k,t}-1)}>1+\frac{p\sum_{t=1}^{p-1}\theta_{k,t}}{p+1}

\displaystyle\overset{\lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{sum-theta-kt-p}}{>}1+\frac{p}{2(p+1)(k+p)^{2p/(3p+1)}}.

(70)

In addition, observe from
Eq.

63
that

\displaystyle\frac{p_{k+1}}{p_{k}}\leq\Big(1+\frac{1}{k+p}\Big)^{(p-1)/(3p+1)}\leq 1+\frac{p-1}{(3p+1)(k+p)},

(71)

where the second inequality is due to

for all

30
holds as desired.

∎

6
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we establish upper bounds for the sample and first-order operation complexity of SFOMs for finding an
\epsilon
-stochastic stationary point of problem
Eq.

1
assuming the Lipschitz continuity of the
p
th-order derivative for any
p\geq 2
. It should be mentioned that the development of upper complexity bounds in this paper is independent of those in
[
7
,
8
]
under the mean-squared smoothness condition stated in
Eq.

5
. Indeed, the mean-squared smoothness is an assumption imposed on the stochastic gradient estimator
G(\cdot;\xi)
rather than the function
f
itself. In
Appendix

A
, we provide an example with discussion showing that an unbiased stochastic gradient estimator
G(\cdot;\xi)
with bounded variance can violate
Eq.

5
when the gradient and arbitrarily higher-order derivatives of
f
are Lipschitz continuous.

In addition, our proposed acceleration technique, which exploits higher-order smoothness, are orthogonal to the recent developments in the complexity results of SFOMs for composite optimization and constrained optimization discussed in
Section

1
. Thus, it would be interesting to extend our techniques to develop accelerated SFOMs with complexity guarantees for more general stochastic optimization problems under higher-order smoothness, which we leave as a direction for future research.

Appendix A
An example concerning mean-squared smoothness

The following proposition provides a necessary and sufficient condition for the mean-squared smoothness condition in
Eq.

5
, when the stochastic gradient estimator
G(\cdot;\xi)
takes a special form.

Proposition 1
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
(a), (b), and
2
hold. Let

\displaystyle G(x;\xi)\doteq\nabla f(x)+\xi g(x)\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(77)

where
\xi
follows the standard normal distribution with mean
0
and variance
1
, and
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a mapping satisfying
\|g(x)\|\leq\sigma
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with some
\sigma>0
. Then,
G(\cdot;\xi)
satisfies
Assumption

1
(c), and moreover,
Eq.

5
holds if and only if
g
is Lipschitz continuous in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

Since
\xi
follows the standard normal distribution, we have
\mathbb{E}[\xi]=0
and
\mathbb{E}[\xi^{2}]=1
. It then follows from
Eq.

77
and
\|g(x)\|\leq\sigma
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
that
Assumption

1
(c) holds. In addition, observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[\|G(y;\xi)-G(x;\xi)\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\overset{\lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{exp-Gxxi}}{=}\mathbb{E}_{\xi}[\|\nabla f(y)-\nabla f(x)+\xi(g(y)-g(x))\|^{2}]

\displaystyle=\|\nabla f(y)-\nabla f(x)\|^{2}+\|g(y)-g(x)\|^{2},

where the second equality follows from
\mathbb{E}[\xi]=0
. This immediately implies that
Eq.

5
holds if and only if
g
is Lipschitz continuous in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Hence, the conclusion of this proposition holds as desired.
∎

As a consequence of
Proposition

1
, we conclude that when
G(\cdot;\xi)
satisfies
Eq.

77
with
g
being bounded but not Lipschitz continuous in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(e.g.,
g(x)=\min\{\sqrt{\|x\|},1\}
) or even discontinuous at some points in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
Assumption

1
(c) holds, but the mean-squared smoothness condition in
Eq.

5
is violated.

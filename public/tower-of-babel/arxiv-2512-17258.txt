Abstract
The quadratic embedding constant (QEC) of a connected graph
is defined to be the maximum of the quadratic function associated with
its distance matrix on a certain unit sphere of codimension two.
In this paper we derive a formula for the QEC
of a corona graph
G\odot H
.
It is shown that
\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot H)=\psi_{H*}^{-1}(\mathrm{QEC}(G))
holds
under some spectral assumptions on
H
,
where
\psi_{H*}^{-1}
is the inverse function of the most right
branch of the analytic function
\psi_{H}
defined by means of
the main eigenvalues of the adjacency matrix of
H
.
Moreover, if
H
is a regular graph of which the
adjacency matrix has the smallest eigenvalue
-2
,
then the formula is written down explicitly.

Keywords
quadratic embedding constant,
corona graph,
main eigenvalue,
distance matrix,
distance spectra,
Euclidean distance geometry.

Introduction

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite connected graph with
|V|=n\geq 2

and
D=[d(x,y)]_{x,y\in V}
the distance matrix of
G
,
where
d(x,y)
stands for the
standard graph distance between two vertices
x,y\in V
.
A
quadratic embedding
of
G

into a Euclidean space
\mathcal{H}
is
a map
\varphi:V\to\mathcal{H}
such that

\|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)\|^{2}=d(x,y),\quad x,y\in V.

In general, a given graph does not
necessarily admit a quadratic embedding;
when it does, it is said to be
of QE class
.
In the terminology of Euclidean distance geometry,
a graph is of QE class if and only if the distance matrix
D

is a Euclidean distance matrix

[
1
,
2
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
.
It is known from the celebrated Schoenberg’s theorem

[
28
,
29
,
30
,
31
]

that a connected graph
G
is of QE class
if and only if its distance matrix
D
is
conditionally negative definite.
To identify this condition, it is natural to consider the

quadratic embedding constant (QEC)
defined by

\mathrm{QEC}(G)=\max\{\langle f,Df\rangle\,;\,f\in C(V),\,\langle f,f\rangle=1,\,\langle{\bm{1}},f\rangle=0\},

where
C(V)
is the space of real column vectors
f=[f_{x}]_{x\in V}

with the canonical inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
,
and
\bm{1}\in C(V)
is the column vector whose entries are all one.
In fact, a connected graph
G
is of QE class
if and only if
\mathrm{QEC}(G)\leq 0
.

Since the QEC was first introduced
by Obata and Zakiyyah
[
26
]
,
interest has grown in characterization and
classification of graphs using the QEC
as a new numeric invariant of connected graphs.
The QECs of graphs of some particular classes are
determined explicitly, including
complete graphs and cycles
[
26
]
,
paths
[
19
]
,
complete multipartite graphs
[
25
]
,
fan graphs
[
21
,
22
]
,
wheels
[
23
]
,
squid and kite graphs
[
27
]
,
hairy cycle graphs
[
14
]
,
double star graphs
[
8
]
.
It is also interesting to obtain explicit formulas
and estimates of QEC along with various graph operations,
for example, the Cartesian product
[
26
]
,
star product
[
4
,
20
,
26
]
,
lexicographic product
[
18
]
,
graph join
[
18
,
21
]
,
and comb graphs (cluster graphs)
[
8
]
.
Moreover, an effort was initiated to characterize
particular block graphs
[
5
]

and to construct primary non-QE graphs
[
24
,
25
]
.

In this paper,
along the line of research
on QEC in relation to various graph operations,
we focus on the corona of two graphs
G
and
H
,
denoted by
G\odot H
,
for definition see Section
2.1
.
Since the corona graph was first discussed by
Frucht and Harary
[
12
]

in relation to automorphism groups,
it has been actively studied as a benchmark class of graphs
along with chemical graph theory, graph coloring,
graph labeling, network models, and so on.
For relevant spectral results we refer to

[
3
]
for the adjacency spectra
and
[
13
]
for the distance spectra.

The main purpose of this paper is
to derive a formula for
\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot H)
,
where
G
is a connected graph on two or more vertices,

H
an arbitrary graph and

G\odot H
their corona graph.
To describe
\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot H)
we need
a special analytic function associated with
H
.
Let
A_{H}
be the adjacency matrix of
H
,

\mathrm{ev}(A_{H})
the set of distinct eigenvalues of
A_{H}
,
and
\mathrm{ev}_{M}(A_{H})
the subset of main ones.
Let
\omega_{H}(\lambda)
be the unique
real analytic function with maximal domain satisfying

\omega_{H}(\lambda)=1+\lambda\langle\mathbf{1},(A_{H}+2+\lambda)^{-1}\mathbf{1}\rangle.

(1.1)

It is shown that
\omega_{H}(\lambda)
has a simple pole
at
\lambda=-\alpha-2
for each

\alpha\in\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A_{H})\backslash\{-2\}
and,
is analytic except those poles.
Moreover,
\omega_{H}(\lambda)
has

k_{H}=|\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A_{H})\backslash\{-2\}|\geq 1

distinct real zeroes.
We then introduce
a real analytic function
\psi_{H}(\lambda)
with
the maximal domain uniquely specified by

\psi_{H}(\lambda)=\frac{\lambda}{\omega_{H}(\lambda)}\,.

(1.2)

It is shown that
\psi_{H}(\lambda)
has a simple pole
at each zero of
\omega_{H}(\lambda)
,
and is analytic except those poles.
Let
\lambda_{*}
be the largest zero of
\omega_{H}(\lambda)
.
Then
\psi_{H*}=\psi_{H}\!\restriction_{(\lambda_{*},+\infty)}

becomes an increasing function varying from
-\infty
to
+\infty
.
Thus, we may define the inverse function

\lambda=\psi_{H*}^{-1}(\lambda^{\prime})

for
\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
,
namely,
\lambda
is the largest
solution to the equation
\psi_{H}(\lambda)=\lambda^{\prime}
.
With those notations we will prove that
the formula

\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot H)=\psi_{H*}^{-1}(\mathrm{QEC}(G))

(1.3)

holds under some spectral assumptions,
for the precise statements
see Sections
4.3

and
4.4
.
It is also interesting to note that if
H
is a regular graph
with
\min\mathrm{ev}(A_{H})=-2
,
formula (
1.3
) holds for
\mathrm{QEC}(G)>0
,
and
\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot H)=0

for any
G
with
\mathrm{QEC}(G)\leq 0
.
We thus expect further profound relation to the graphs
H
with

\min\mathrm{ev}(A_{H})=-2
, which have been extensively studied so far,
see e.g., the celebrated books

[
6
,
10
,
11
]
.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2 we obtain a useful expression of the distance matrix
of a corona graph and derive the set of basic equations
from which
\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot H)
is determined.
In Section 3 we develop a general theory on
two key functions in
(
1.1
) and
(
1.2
).
In Section 4 we obtain an intermediate
expression for
\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot H)

as the greater or equal of
\psi_{H*}^{-1}(\mathrm{QEC}(G))

and a certain constant
\gamma_{3}
determined by

\mathrm{ev}(A_{H})
,
see Proposition
4.7
;
and then specify several spectral conditions
under which formula (
1.3
) holds,
see Theorems
4.9
–
4.11
.
Moreover, if
H
is a regular graph
the formula becomes simpler,
see Theorem
4.14
.
For a regular graph
H
with
\min\mathrm{ev}(A_{H})=-2
,
that formula (
1.3
) holds for
\mathrm{QEC}(G)>0

and
\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot H)=0
otherwise,
see Theorem
4.17
.
In Section 5 we present some examples.
In particular, we show that the main results
on double star graphs and particular cluster graphs
(comb graphs) in
[
8
]

are covered by our main formula.

Notations:
For any non-empty finite set
X

the space of real column vectors
f=[f_{x}]_{x\in X}
is
denoted by
C(X)
.
For each
x\in X
let
e_{x}\in C(X)
be the canonical unit vector
and
\bm{1}\in C(V)
the vector of which the entries are all one,
that is
\bm{1}=\sum_{x\in X}e_{x}
.
Let
Y
be another non-empty finite set.
The space of real matrices

k=[k_{x,y}]_{x\in X,y\in Y}
is denoted by
C(X\times Y)
.
The all-one matrix is denoted by
J_{X,Y}
or simply by
J
.
If
X=Y
, the identity matrix is denoted by
I_{X}
or simply by
I
.
Recall that there is a canonical isomorphism

C(X\times Y)\cong C(X)\otimes C(Y)
.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Corona graph

Let
G=(V_{1},E_{1})
and
H=(V_{2},E_{2})
be
arbitrary graphs with
V_{1}\cap V_{2}=\emptyset
.
For each vertex of
G
we associate a copy of
H
.
A graph obtained from
G
and
|V_{1}|
copies of
H

by adding edges connecting
each vertex of
G
and every vertex of the corresponding copy of
H

is called the
corona
of
G
and
H
,
and is denoted by
G\odot H
.
Here we note that, as a binary operation of graphs,

G\odot H
is neither commutative nor associative.
Obviously, the corona graph
G\odot H
is connected
whenever so is
G
(even if
H
is disconnected).

By definition the vertex set of
G\odot H

is the union of
V_{1}
and
|V_{1}|
copies of
V_{2}
.
To describe it, letting
o
be a new symbol (vertex)
which is not a member of
V_{2}
, we introduce
the Cartesian product set

V=V_{1}\times(\{o\}\cup V_{2})=\{(i,x)\,;\,i\in V_{1},\,\,x\in\{o\}\cup V_{2}\}.

(2.1)

Then through the maps
x\mapsto(x,o)
for
x\in V_{1}
, and

y\mapsto(y,i)
for
y
being a vertex of a copy of
H

corresponding to a vertex
i\in V_{1}
,
we may identify the vertex set of
G\odot H
with
V

and, introduce the adjacency relation on
V
from
G\odot H
.
Thus, the corona
G\odot H
becomes a graph
on
V
defined by (
2.1
),
and, from now on, we always take this realization.

To describe the adjacency relation of the corona graph
G\odot H

it is convenient to consider the graph join
\tilde{H}=K_{1}+H
,
where
K_{1}
is the singleton graph on
\{o\}
.
By definition,
\tilde{H}
is a graph on
\{o\}\cup V_{2}
with
the adjacency relation:

x\sim y\,\,\,\text{in $\tilde{H}$}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\begin{array}[]{l}\text{(i) $x\neq o$, $y\neq o$, $x\sim y$ in $H$; or}\\
\text{(ii) $x=o$, $y\neq o$; or}\\
\text{(iii) $x\neq o$, $y=o$}.\end{array}

Then the corona
G\odot H
is a graph on

V=V_{1}\times(\{o\}\cup V_{2})

with the adjacency relation:

(i,x)\sim(j,y)\,\,\,\text{in $G\odot H$}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\begin{array}[]{l}\text{(i) $i=j$ and $x\sim y$ in $\tilde{H}$; or}\\
\text{(ii) $i\sim j$ in $G$ and $x=y=o$}.\end{array}

Next we focus on the distance matrix of
G\odot H
,
denoted by
D=D_{G\odot H}
.
We note the canonical isomorphism

\displaystyle C(V)

\displaystyle=C(V_{1}\times(\{o\}\cup V_{2}))

\displaystyle\cong C(V_{1})\otimes C(\{o\}\cup V_{2})\cong C(V_{1})\otimes(\mathbb{R}\oplus C(V_{2})),

(2.2)

which follows from (
2.1
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Notations and assumptions being as above,
let
D_{G}
be the distance matrix of
G
and

A_{H}
the adjacency matrix of
H
.
Then, the distance matrix of the corona graph
G\odot H
is
expressed as

D_{G\odot H}=D_{G}\otimes\begin{bmatrix}J&J\\
J&J\end{bmatrix}+I\otimes\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
0&-2I-A_{H}\end{bmatrix}+J\otimes\begin{bmatrix}0&J\\
J&2J\end{bmatrix},

(2.3)

in accordance with the canonical isomorphism
(
2.2
).

Proof.

Let
d_{G}
and
d_{\tilde{H}}
be the graph distances of

G
and
\tilde{H}=K_{1}+H
.
We see easily from definition that
the graph distance of
G\odot H
is given by

d_{G\odot H}((i,x),(j,y))=\begin{cases}d_{\tilde{H}}(x,y),&\text{if $i=j$},\\
d_{\tilde{H}}(x,o)+d_{G}(i,j)+d_{\tilde{H}}(o,y),&\text{if $i\neq j$},\end{cases}

or equivalently,

\displaystyle d_{G\odot H}((i,x),(j,y))

\displaystyle=d_{G}(i,j)J(x,y)+I(i,j)d_{\tilde{H}}(x,y)

\displaystyle\qquad+(J(i,j)-I(i,j))(d_{\tilde{H}}(x,o)+d_{\tilde{H}}(o,y)),

(2.4)

for any
i,j\in V_{1}
and
x,y\in\{o\}\cup V_{2}
.
Then, letting

B_{\tilde{H}}(x,y)=d_{\tilde{H}}(x,o)+d_{\tilde{H}}(o,y),\qquad x,y\in\{o\}\cup V_{2},

(2.5)

(
2.4
) becomes

D_{G\odot H}=D_{G}\otimes J+I\otimes D_{\tilde{H}}+(J-I)\otimes B_{\tilde{H}}\,.

(2.6)

On the other hand, the graph distance of
\tilde{H}=K_{1}+H
is given by

d_{\tilde{H}}(x,y)=\begin{cases}2,&\text{if $x\neq o$, $y\neq o$, $x\neq y$, $x\not\sim y$ in $H$},\\
1,&\text{if $x\neq o$, $y\neq o$, $x\sim y$ in $H$},\\
1,&\text{if $x=o$, $y\neq o$ or $x\neq o$, $y=o$},\\
0,&\text{if $x=y$}.\end{cases}

(2.7)

Then the distance matrix
D_{\tilde{H}}

and the matrix
B_{\tilde{H}}
in (
2.5
)
are written down in block matrix forms according to
the partition
\{o\}\cup V_{2}
as follows:

D_{\tilde{H}}=\begin{bmatrix}0&J\\
J&2J-2I-A_{H}\end{bmatrix},\qquad B_{\tilde{H}}=\begin{bmatrix}0&J\\
J&2J\end{bmatrix}.

(2.8)

Then (
2.3
) is
immediate from (
2.6
)
and (
2.8
).
∎

Remark 2.2
.

An alternative block matrix expression of
the distance matrix of
G\odot H
,
of course, equivalent to (
2.3
),
is adopted in
[
13
]
,
where the purpose is to
calculate its distance spectrum.

2.2
A preliminary formula for
\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot H)

We will be concerned with a corona graph
G\odot H
,
where
G
is a connected graph on two or more vertices.
Namely, we avoid a corona graph
K_{1}\odot H\cong K_{1}+H

from our discussion because
\mathrm{QEC}(K_{1})
is not defined.
In fact, contrary to its simple appearance,
a general formula for
\mathrm{QEC}(K_{1}+H)
is not
known yet, see
[
18
]

for
H
being a regular graph
and
[
21
,
22
]

for
H=P_{n}
being a path.

Thus, from now on we always consider
a corona graph
G\odot H
, where

G=(V_{1},E_{1})
is a connected graph with
|V_{1}|\geq 2

and
H=(V_{2},E_{2})
an arbitrary graph with
|V_{2}|\geq 1
.
We first note a lower bound of
\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot H)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G=(V_{1},E_{1})
be a connected graph with
|V_{1}|\geq 2

and
H=(V_{2},E_{2})
an arbitrary graph with
|V_{2}|\geq 1
.
Then

\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot H)\geq\max\{-2+\sqrt{2},\,\mathrm{QEC}(G),\,\mathrm{QEC}(K_{1}+H)\}.

(2.9)

Proof.

By assumption
G\odot H
contains a path
P_{4}\cong K_{2}\odot K_{1}

as an isometric subgraph.
Then, by general theory (see e.g.,
[
26
]
) we have

\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot H)\geq\mathrm{QEC}(P_{4})=-2+\sqrt{2}.

Similarly, since
G\odot H
contains
G
and
K_{1}+H

as isometric subgraphs, we have

\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot H)\geq\mathrm{QEC}(G)
and

\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot H)\geq\mathrm{QEC}(K_{1}+H)
.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
G=(V_{1},E_{1})
be a connected graph with
|V_{1}|\geq 2

and
H=(V_{2},E_{2})
an arbitrary graph with
|V_{2}|\geq 1
.
Then
\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot H)=0
holds if

(i)

\mathrm{QEC}(G)=0
and
\mathrm{QEC}(K_{1}+H)\leq 0
;
or
(ii)

\mathrm{QEC}(G)\leq 0
and
\mathrm{QEC}(K_{1}+H)=0
.

Proof.

It is known
[
20
,
26
]
that
the star product (vertex amalgamation) of two graphs of QE class
is of QE class.
Since
G\odot H
is obtained by repeated application of
star product of
G
and
K_{1}+H
,
we have
\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot H)\leq 0

if both
G
and
K_{1}+H
are of QE class.
Therefore,
\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot H)\leq 0
holds
if (i) or (ii) is satisfied.
On the other hand, in that case, applying
Lemma
2.3
, we obtain

\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot H)\geq 0

and hence
\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot H)=0
.
∎

For simplicity the vertex set of
G\odot H
is denoted by
V

and the distance matrix by
D
.
Let
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{G\odot H}
be the set of solutions

(f,\lambda,\mu)\in C(V)\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}

to equations:

\displaystyle(D-\lambda I)f

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu}{2}\bm{1},

(2.10)

\displaystyle\langle\bm{1},f\rangle

\displaystyle=0,

(2.11)

\displaystyle\langle f,f\rangle

\displaystyle=1.

(2.12)

Then the following assertion is fundamental, see
e.g.,
[
26
]
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
G=(V_{1},E_{1})
be a connected graph with
|V_{1}|\geq 2

and
H=(V_{2},E_{2})
an arbitrary graph
|V_{2}|\geq 1
.
Then we have

\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot H)=\max\lambda(\mathcal{S}),

(2.13)

where
\lambda(\mathcal{S})
is the set of
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}

appearing in a solution
(f,\lambda,\mu)\in\mathcal{S}
.

Thus our task is to find the largest
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}

appearing as a solution
(f,\lambda,\mu)\in\mathcal{S}
to equations
(
2.10
)–(
2.12
).
If we know in advance that
\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot H)\geq q_{0}

for some constant
q_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, we only need to look for
the solutions
(f,\lambda,\mu)\in\mathcal{S}
with
\lambda\geq q_{0}

and we have

\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot H)=\max\lambda(\mathcal{S})\cap[q_{0},+\infty).

(2.14)

For example, we may take
q_{0}=-2+\sqrt{2}
by
Lemma
2.3
.

To solve equations
(
2.10
)–(
2.12
)
we adopt the canonical isomorphism

C(V)\cong C(V_{1})\otimes(\mathbb{R}\oplus C(V_{2}))
in
(
2.2
).
Let
\{e_{x}\,;\,x\in V_{2}\}
be the canonical basis of
C(V_{2})
.
Since the vectors

\begin{bmatrix}1\\
0\end{bmatrix},\qquad\begin{bmatrix}0\\
e_{x}\end{bmatrix},\quad x\in V_{2},

(2.15)

from the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}\oplus C(V_{2})
,
any
f\in C(V)\cong C(V_{1})\otimes(\mathbb{R}\oplus C(V_{2}))

admits a unique expression of the form:

f=\xi_{o}\otimes\begin{bmatrix}1\\
0\end{bmatrix}+\sum_{x\in V_{2}}\xi_{x}\otimes\begin{bmatrix}0\\
e_{x}\end{bmatrix},\qquad\xi_{x}\in C(V_{1}),\quad x\in\{o\}\cup V_{2}\,.

(2.16)

Thus, equations
(
2.10
)–(
2.12
)
for
(f,\lambda,\mu)

are naturally transformed into equivalent equations
for
(\xi_{o},\{\xi_{x}\,;\,x\in V_{2}\},\lambda,\mu)
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Equation (
2.10
) is equivalent to
the following set of equations:

\displaystyle(D_{G}-\lambda I)\xi_{o}+\sum_{x\in V_{2}}(D_{G}\xi_{x}+J\xi_{x})=\frac{\mu}{2}\,\bm{1},

(2.17)

\displaystyle(J+\lambda I)\xi_{o}+\sum_{y\in V_{2}}J\xi_{y}-\sum_{y\in V_{2}}\langle e_{x},(A_{H}+2+\lambda)e_{y}\rangle\xi_{y}=0,\qquad x\in V_{2},

(2.18)

Proof.

Using

\displaystyle D\bigg(\xi_{o}\otimes\begin{bmatrix}1\\
0\end{bmatrix}\bigg)

\displaystyle=D_{G}\xi_{o}\otimes\begin{bmatrix}1\\
\mathbf{1}\end{bmatrix}+J\xi_{o}\otimes\begin{bmatrix}0\\
\mathbf{1}\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle D\bigg(\xi_{x}\otimes\begin{bmatrix}0\\
e_{x}\end{bmatrix}\bigg)

\displaystyle=D_{G}\xi_{x}\otimes\begin{bmatrix}1\\
\mathbf{1}\end{bmatrix}+\xi_{x}\otimes\begin{bmatrix}0\\
-(A_{H}+2)e_{x}\end{bmatrix}+J\xi_{x}\otimes\begin{bmatrix}1\\
2\mathbf{1}\end{bmatrix},\quad x\in V_{2},

which follow easily by
Lemma
2.1
,
equation (
2.10
) is brought into

\displaystyle D_{G}\xi_{o}\otimes\begin{bmatrix}1\\
\bm{1}\end{bmatrix}+J\xi_{o}\otimes\begin{bmatrix}0\\
\bm{1}\end{bmatrix}+\sum_{x\in V_{2}}D_{G}\xi_{x}\otimes\begin{bmatrix}1\\
\mathbf{1}\end{bmatrix}+\sum_{x\in V_{2}}\xi_{x}\otimes\begin{bmatrix}0\\
-(A_{H}+2)e_{x}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{x\in V_{2}}J\xi_{x}\otimes\begin{bmatrix}1\\
2\mathbf{1}\end{bmatrix}-\lambda\xi_{o}\otimes\begin{bmatrix}1\\
0\end{bmatrix}-\sum_{x\in V_{2}}\lambda\xi_{x}\otimes\begin{bmatrix}0\\
e_{x}\end{bmatrix}=\frac{\mu}{2}\,\mathbf{1}\otimes\begin{bmatrix}1\\
\mathbf{1}\end{bmatrix}.

Comparing the coefficients with respect to the canonical basis

3
Two Key Functions Associated with a Symmetric Matrix

This section is devoted to general consideration.
With a symmetric matrix
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
,
n\geq 1
, we
associate two real functions defined by

\displaystyle\omega_{A}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=1+\lambda\langle\mathbf{1},(A+2+\lambda)^{-1}\mathbf{1}\rangle,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\psi_{A}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{\omega_{A}(\lambda)},

(3.2)

of which the precise domains and properties
will be clarified in the discussion below.

3.1
Main eigenvalues

Let
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
,
n\geq 1
, be a symmetric matrix.
The set of distinct eigenvalues of
A
is denoted by
\mathrm{ev}(A)
.
For an eigenvalue
\alpha\in\mathrm{ev}(A)
we denote by

E_{\alpha}
the orthogonal projection onto the
associated eigenspace.
By the spectral decomposition we come to

A=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathrm{ev}(A)}\alpha E_{\alpha}\,,\qquad\sum_{\alpha\in\mathrm{ev}(A)}E_{\alpha}=I,\qquad E_{\alpha}E_{\alpha^{\prime}}=\delta_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}E_{\alpha}\,.

(3.3)

By convention we set
E_{\alpha}=O
for
\alpha\not\in\mathrm{ev}(A)
.

Following Cvetković
[
9
,
11
]

an eigenvalue
\alpha\in\mathrm{ev}(A)
is called
a
main eigenvalue
if
there exists a non-zero vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

Av=\alpha v
and
\langle\mathbf{1},v\rangle\neq 0
,
or equivalently if
E_{\alpha}\mathbf{1}\neq 0
.
Let
\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)
denote the set of main eigenvalues of
A
.
It follows from the second relation in
(
3.3
) that

\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)\neq\emptyset
.

3.2
The
\omega
-function associated with a symmetric matrix

Applying the spectral decomposition
(
3.3
) to
(
3.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\omega_{A}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)}\frac{\lambda}{\alpha+2+\lambda}\,\|E_{\alpha}\mathbf{1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=1+\|E_{-2}\mathbf{1}\|^{2}+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)\backslash\{-2\}}\frac{\lambda}{\alpha+2+\lambda}\,\|E_{\alpha}\mathbf{1}\|^{2},

(3.4)

where
\|E_{-2}\mathbf{1}\|>0
if
-2\in\mathrm{ev}_{M}(A)

and
\|E_{-2}\mathbf{1}\|=0
otherwise.
As usual, the last sum is understood to be zero when

\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)\backslash\{-2\}=\emptyset
.
We observe that the last expression in (
3.4
)
is a real analytic function on the domain

\mathbb{R}\backslash\{-\alpha-2\,;\,\alpha\in\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)\backslash\{-2\}\},

(3.5)

which has a simple pole at
\lambda=-\alpha-2
for each

\alpha\in\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)\backslash\{-2\}
.
From now on, we understand
\omega_{A}(\lambda)
as defined in this way.
In other words,
\omega_{A}(\lambda)
is the unique maximal
analytic extension satisfying (
3.1
)
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
with
\det(A+2+\lambda)\neq 0
.
For convenience we call
\omega_{A}(\lambda)
the

\omega
-function
associated with a symmetric matrix
A
.
The number of poles of
\omega_{A}(\lambda)
is denoted by

k_{A}=|\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)\backslash\{-2\}|.

(3.6)

Since
\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)\neq\emptyset
in general,
we see that
k_{A}=0
if and only if
\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)=\{-2\}
.
In that case, from (
3.4
) we have

\omega_{A}(\lambda)=1+\|E_{-2}\mathbf{1}\|^{2}=1+\|\mathbf{1}\|^{2}=1+n\quad\text{(constant)}.

(3.7)

For example,
A=-(2/n)J_{n}\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
falls
into this situation.
We next note that
k_{A}\geq 1
is equivalent to

\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)\backslash\{-2\}\neq\emptyset
.
Since
\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)\neq\emptyset
in general,

k_{A}\geq 1
is also equivalent to
\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)\neq\{-2\}
.
In that case, we see directly from (
3.4
)
that
\omega_{A}(\lambda)
is a rational function of the form:

\omega_{A}(\lambda)=p(\lambda)\prod_{\alpha\in\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)\backslash\{-2\}}(\alpha+2+\lambda)^{-1},

(3.8)

where
p(\lambda)
is a polynomial of order
k_{A}
with
the leading coefficient
n+1
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
be a symmetric matrix
and
\omega_{A}(\lambda)
the associated
\omega
-function
defined as above.

(1)

\omega_{A}(0)=1+\|E_{-2}\mathbf{1}\|^{2}
.

(2)

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\pm\infty}\omega_{A}(\lambda)=n+1
.

(3)

For
\alpha\in\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)\backslash\{-2\}
with
\alpha+2>0

we have

\lim_{\lambda\downarrow-\alpha-2}\omega_{A}(\lambda)=-\infty,\qquad\lim_{\lambda\uparrow-\alpha-2}\omega_{A}(\lambda)=+\infty.

(4)

For
\alpha\in\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)\backslash\{-2\}
with
\alpha+2<0

we have

\lim_{\lambda\downarrow-\alpha-2}\omega_{A}(\lambda)=+\infty,\qquad\lim_{\lambda\uparrow-\alpha-2}\omega_{A}(\lambda)=-\infty.

Proof.

Straightforward from the expression in
(
3.4
).
∎

We will examine the zeroes of
\omega_{A}(\lambda)
.
From (
3.6
) we see that

|\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)|=\begin{cases}k_{A},&\text{if $-2\not\in\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)$},\\
k_{A}+1,&\text{if $-2\in\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)$}.\end{cases}

If
k_{A}=0
, or equivalently if

\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)=\{-2\}
,
then
\omega_{A}(\lambda)
has no zero
by (
3.7
).

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
k_{A}\geq 1
and
-2\not\in\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)
.
Arrange the members in
\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)\cup\{-2\}

in ascending order as

\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)\cup\{-2\}=\{\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}<\dots<\alpha_{k_{A}+1}\}.

Then, for each
1\leq i\leq k_{A}
the interval

(-\alpha_{i+1}-2,-\alpha_{i}-2)

contains a unique zero of
\omega_{A}(\lambda)
.
Moreover, those
k_{A}
distinct real zeroes
exhaust the zeroes of
\omega_{A}(\lambda)
.

Proof.

For simplicity we write
k=k_{A}
.
Since
\omega_{A}(\lambda)
is a rational function
as in (
3.8
)
with
p(\lambda)
being a polynomial of order
k
,

\omega_{A}(\lambda)
has at most
k
distinct zeroes
in the complex plane.
Therefore, for the assertion it is sufficient to show that
each interval
(-\alpha_{i+1}-2,-\alpha_{i}-2)

for
1\leq i\leq k

contains at least one zero of
\omega_{A}(\lambda)
.

(Case 1)
\alpha_{1}=-2
.
In this case, we have
-\alpha_{k}-2<\dotsb<-\alpha_{2}-2<0
.
By definition,

\omega_{A}(\lambda)
is continuous on the interval

(-\alpha_{i+1}-2,-\alpha_{i}-2)
for each
2\leq i\leq k
.
Moreover,
we see from Lemma
3.1
(3) that

\lim_{\lambda\downarrow-\alpha_{i+1}-2}\omega_{A}(\lambda)=-\infty,\qquad\lim_{\lambda\uparrow-\alpha_{i}-2}\omega_{A}(\lambda)=+\infty.

Therefore,
\omega_{A}(\lambda)
has at least one zero
in the interval
(-\alpha_{i+1}-2,-\alpha_{i}-2)
for each
2\leq i\leq k
.
Moreover, since

\omega_{A}(\lambda)
is a continuous function on

(-\alpha_{2}-2,+\infty)
and

\lim_{\lambda\downarrow-\alpha_{2}-2}\omega(\lambda)=-\infty,\qquad\omega_{A}(0)=1,

it has at least one zero
in the interval
(-\alpha_{2}-2,0)=(-\alpha_{2}-2,-\alpha_{1}-2)
.

(Case 2)
\alpha_{k}=-2
. In a similar manner as in (Case 1),
we may show that
for each
1\leq i\leq k
the interval
(-\alpha_{i+1}-2,-\alpha_{i}-2)

contains at least one zero of
\omega_{A}(\lambda)
.

(Case 3)
\alpha_{1}<-2<\alpha_{k}
. In that case we have

\alpha_{l}=-2
for some
2\leq l\leq k-1
.
For
1\leq i\leq l-2
the function
\omega_{A}(\lambda)
is continuous
on
(-\alpha_{i+1}-2,-\alpha_{i}-2)
and

\lim_{\lambda\downarrow-\alpha_{i+1}-2}\omega_{A}(\lambda)=+\infty,\qquad\lim_{\lambda\uparrow-\alpha_{i}-2}\omega_{A}(\lambda)=-\infty.

by Lemma
3.1
(4).
Therefore,
\omega_{A}(\lambda)
has at least one zero
in the
(-\alpha_{i+1}-2,-\alpha_{i}-2)
for
1\leq i\leq l-2
.
Similarly,
\omega_{A}(\lambda)
has at least one zero
in the
(-\alpha_{i+1}-2,-\alpha_{i}-2)
for

l+1\leq i\leq k
.
Finally, since
\omega_{A}(\lambda)
is continuous
on
(-\alpha_{l+1}-2,-\alpha_{l-1}-2)
and

\lim_{\lambda\downarrow-\alpha_{l+1}-2}\omega_{A}(\lambda)=-\infty,\qquad\omega_{A}(0)=1,\qquad\lim_{\lambda\uparrow-\alpha_{l-1}-2}\omega_{A}(\lambda)=-\infty

by Lemma
3.1
,
it has at least one zero
in each of the intervals

(-\alpha_{l+1}-2,0)=(-\alpha_{l+1}-2,-\alpha_{l}-2)

and
(0,-\alpha_{l-1}-2)=(-\alpha_{l}-2,-\alpha_{l-1}-2)
.
Consequently,
\omega_{A}(\lambda)

has at least one zero in each interval
(-\alpha_{i+1}-2,-\alpha_{i}-2)

for
1\leq i\leq k
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
k_{A}\geq 1
and
-2\in\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)
.
Arrange the members in
\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)

in ascending order as

\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)=\{\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}<\dots<\alpha_{k_{A}+1}\}.

Then, for each
1\leq i\leq k_{A}
the interval

(-\alpha_{i+1}-2,-\alpha_{i}-2)

contains a unique zero of
\omega_{A}(\lambda)
.
Moreover, those
k_{A}
distinct real zeroes
exhaust the zeroes of
\omega_{A}(\lambda)
.

Proof.

We first note that
\omega_{A}(\lambda)
has at most
k_{A}

distinct zeroes in the complex plane by
(
3.8
).
Therefore, for the assertion it is sufficient to show that
for each
1\leq i\leq k_{A}
the interval
(-\alpha_{i+1}-2,-\alpha_{i}-2)

contains at least one zero of
\omega_{A}(\lambda)
.
In fact, this claim is verified by using similar argument as
in the proof of Lemma

3.2
.
∎

Combining Lemmas
3.2

and
3.3
,
we come to the following

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
,
n\geq 1
, be a symmetric matrix
and
\omega_{A}(\lambda)
the associated
\omega
-function.
Assume that
k_{A}=|\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)\backslash\{-2\}|\geq 1

and arrange the members of
\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)\cup\{-2\}

in ascending order as

\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)\cup\{-2\}=\{\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}<\dots<\alpha_{k_{A}+1}\}.

(3.9)

Then, for each
1\leq i\leq k_{A}
the interval

(-\alpha_{i+1}-2,-\alpha_{i}-2)

contains a unique zero of
\omega_{A}(\lambda)
.
Moreover, those
k_{A}
distinct zeroes exhaust the zeroes of

\omega_{A}(\lambda)
.

We are also interested in the largest zero of
\omega_{A}(\lambda)
.

Proposition 3.5
.

We keep the notations and assumptions in Proposition

3.4
.
Let
\lambda_{*}
be the largest zero of
\omega_{A}(\lambda)
.
Then, we have
\lambda_{*}<0
if
\min\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)\geq-2
,
and
\lambda_{*}>0
otherwise.

Proof.

It follows from Proposition
3.4

that

-\alpha_{2}-2<\lambda_{*}<-\alpha_{1}-2,

(3.10)

where
\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)\cup\{-2\}=\{\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}<\dots<\alpha_{k_{A}+1}\}
.
If
\min\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)\geq-2
,
then
\alpha_{1}=-2
and we come to
\lambda_{*}<-\alpha_{1}-2=0

by (
3.10
).
If
\min\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)<-2
,
then we have

\min\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)=\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}\leq-2

and
\lambda_{*}>-\alpha_{2}-2\geq 0
by (
3.10
).
∎

3.3
The
\psi
-function and its inverse function

Let
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
,
n\geq 1
, be a symmetric matrix
and
\omega_{A}(\lambda)
the associated
\omega
-function
introduced in the previous section.
The
\psi
-function
is defined by

\psi_{A}(\lambda)=\frac{\lambda}{\omega_{A}(\lambda)}\,.

(3.11)

Obviously,
\psi_{A}(\lambda)
is a real analytic function on the domain

\mathbb{R}\backslash(\{-\alpha-2\,;\alpha\in\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)\backslash\{-2\}\}\cup\{\omega_{A}(\lambda)=0\}).

(3.12)

If
k_{A}=0
, that is,
\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)=\{-2\}
,
then (
3.12
) is reduced to
the whole
\mathbb{R}
and (
3.11
) becomes

\psi_{A}(\lambda)=\frac{\lambda}{\omega_{A}(\lambda)}=\frac{\lambda}{n+1}\,.

(3.13)

Assume that
k_{A}\geq 1
, that is,

\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)\neq\{-2\}
.
For the exclusion sets of (
3.12
),
by Proposition
3.4

we see that

\{-\alpha-2\,;\alpha\in\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)\backslash\{-2\}\}\cap\{\omega_{A}(\lambda)=0\}=\emptyset,

(3.14)

Moreover, we see from Lemma
3.1
that

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow-\alpha-2}\psi_{A}(\lambda)=0,\qquad\alpha\in\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)\backslash\{-2\}.

Therefore, together with (
3.14
)
we see that
\psi_{A}(\lambda)
is extended uniquely to
a real analytic function on

\mathbb{R}\backslash\{\omega_{A}(\lambda)=0\}
.
From now on, we understand that
\psi_{A}(\lambda)
is the
unique real analytic function on

\mathbb{R}\backslash\{\omega_{A}(\lambda)=0\}
which
satisfies (
3.11
).
In particular, we have

\psi_{A}(0)=0,\qquad\psi_{A}(-\alpha-2)=0,\quad\alpha\in\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)\backslash\{-2\},

(3.15)

and
\psi_{A}(\lambda)
has a simple pole at
each zero of
\omega_{A}(\lambda)
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{R})
,
n\geq 1
, be a symmetric matrix
and assume that
k_{A}=|\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)\backslash\{-2\}|\geq 1
.
Let
\omega_{A}(\lambda)
and
\psi_{A}(\lambda)
be the
associated
\omega
- and
\psi
-functions, respectively.
Let
\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}>\dotsb>\lambda_{k_{A}}
be the zeroes of

\omega_{A}(\lambda)
.
Then
\psi_{A}(\lambda)
is a continuous and
strictly increasing function on

\mathbb{R}\backslash\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{k_{A}}\}
and

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\psi_{A}(\lambda)=+\infty,\quad\lim_{\lambda\downarrow\lambda_{i}}\psi_{A}(\lambda)=-\infty,\quad\lim_{\lambda\uparrow\lambda_{i}}\psi_{A}(\lambda)=+\infty,\quad\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow-\infty}\psi_{A}(\lambda)=-\infty,

for
1\leq i\leq k_{A}
.
In particular,
\psi_{A}(\lambda)
splits into
k_{A}+1

continuous increasing functions on the intervals

(-\infty,\lambda_{k_{A}})
,
(\lambda_{k_{A}},\lambda_{k_{A}-1}),\dots(\lambda_{2},\lambda_{1})
,
(\lambda_{1},+\infty)
.

Proof.

By differentiating
\psi_{A}(\lambda)
directly, we obtain

\frac{d}{d\lambda}\,\psi_{A}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\omega_{A}(\lambda)^{2}}\Big\{1+\lambda^{2}\|(A+2+\lambda)^{-1}\mathbf{1}\|^{2}\Big\}>0.

Therefore,
\psi_{A}(\lambda)
is
strictly increasing on
\mathbb{R}\backslash\{\omega_{A}(\lambda)=0\}
.
The limit behavior follows from
Lemma
3.1
and
Proposition
3.4
.
∎

It follows from Proposition
3.6
that
the inverse function
\psi_{A}^{-1}
is determined only as
a multivalued function (in fact, taking exactly
(k_{A}+1)
values).
Let
\lambda_{*}=\lambda_{1}
be the largest zero of
\omega_{A}(\lambda)

and define
\psi_{A*}=\psi\!\restriction_{(\lambda_{*},+\infty)}
.
Since
\psi_{A*}(\lambda)
becomes a real analytic
and strictly increasing function
on
(\lambda_{*},\infty)
which varies from
-\infty
to
+\infty
,
its inverse function

\psi_{A*}^{-1}(\lambda^{\prime})
is well defined
and becomes a real analytic and strictly increasing function.

Proposition 3.7
.

We keep the notations and assumptions as in
Proposition
3.6
.

(1)

\psi_{A*}^{-1}(\lambda^{\prime})

is a real analytic and strictly increasing function
on
\mathbb{R}
such that

\lim_{\lambda^{\prime}\rightarrow-\infty}\psi_{A*}^{-1}(\lambda^{\prime})=\lambda_{*},\qquad\lim_{\lambda^{\prime}\rightarrow+\infty}\psi_{A*}(\lambda^{\prime})=+\infty.

(2)

\psi_{A*}^{-1}(0)=-\alpha_{1}-2
,
where
\alpha_{1}=\min\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)\cup\{-2\}
.

(3)

\psi_{A*}^{-1}(\lambda^{\prime})=\max\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\,;\,\psi_{A}(\lambda)=\lambda^{\prime}\}
.

Proof.

(1) Already clear as mentioned above.

(2) We note from
-\alpha_{2}-2<\lambda_{*}<-\alpha_{1}-2
that

-\alpha_{1}-2
belongs to the domain of
\psi_{A*}(\lambda)
.
Then
\psi_{A*}(-\alpha_{1}-2)=0
by (
3.15
)
and we have
\psi_{A*}^{-1}(0)=-\alpha_{1}-2
.

(3) Given
\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
,
there exist
k_{A}+1
distinct
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}

satisfying
\psi_{A}(\lambda)=\lambda^{\prime}
by
Proposition
3.6
.
Since
\psi_{A*}(\lambda)
is chosen as the most right
branch of
\psi_{A}(\lambda)
,
we see that
\psi_{A*}^{-1}(\lambda^{\prime})
coincides with
the largest
\lambda
satisfying
\psi_{A}(\lambda)=\lambda^{\prime}
.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

The assertion of Proposition
3.7

remains valid also for
k_{A}=0
under the convention that

\lambda_{*}=-\infty
.

4
Main Formula for
\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot H)

4.1
The
\omega
- and
\psi
-functions associated with a graph

We first note the following

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
H
be an arbitrary graph and
A_{H}
the adjacency matrix.

(1)

\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A_{H})\neq\emptyset

and
\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A_{H})\neq\{-2\}
.

(2)

\max\mathrm{ev}(A_{H})\in\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A_{H})
,
i.e., the largest eigenvalue of
A_{H}
is a main one.

Proof.

(1) It is pointed out in Section
3.1

that
\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A_{H})\neq\emptyset
.
If
\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A)=\{-2\}
,
we have
A\mathbf{1}=-2\mathbf{1}
.
But this contradicts that any row sum of
A_{H}
is non-negative.

(2) By Perron-Frobenius theorem,
associated with the largest eigenvalue there exists
an eigenvector
f
of which the entries are all non-negative.
Since
f

Remark 4.12
.

We note that a graph

satisfies

if and only if it is a disjoint union of complete graphs.
In fact, let

with

Assume that

as an induced subgraph.

we have

or

and
H=(V_{2},E_{2})
a
\kappa
-regular graph on
n=|V_{2}|

vertices, where
n\geq 1
and
\kappa\geq 0
.
Assume that
\min\mathrm{ev}(A_{H})=-2
.
Then we have

\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot H)=\begin{cases}\psi_{H*}^{-1}(\mathrm{QEC}(G)),&\text{if $\mathrm{QEC}(G)>0$},\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where

\displaystyle\psi_{H*}^{-1}(\mathrm{QEC}(G))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big\{(n+1)\mathrm{QEC}(G)-(\kappa+2)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sqrt{((n+1)\mathrm{QEC}(G)-(\kappa+2))^{2}+4(\kappa+2)\mathrm{QEC}(G)}\,\Big\}.

Proof.

First assume that
\mathrm{QEC}(G)>0
.
Then we have
\psi_{H*}^{-1}(\mathrm{QEC}(G))>0
and

-2-\psi_{H*}^{-1}(\mathrm{QEC}(G))<-2=\min\mathrm{ev}(A_{H}).

Applying Theorem
4.11
, we obtain

\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot H)=\psi_{H*}^{-1}(\mathrm{QEC}(G))
,
of which the explicit expression is given by
(
4.37
).
Next assume that
\mathrm{QEC}(G)\leq 0
.
We then have
\mathrm{QEC}(K_{1}+H)=0

by Proposition
4.16
,
and hence
\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot H)=0

by Proposition
2.4
.
∎

Remark 4.18
.

The graphs
H
, in particular regular ones, with
\min\mathrm{ev}(A_{H})>-2

or with
\min\mathrm{ev}(A_{H})=-2
have been extensively
studied, see
[
10
]
and references sited therein.
Moreover, strongly regular graphs
H
with

\min\mathrm{ev}(A_{H})\geq-2
are completely classified,
see e.g.,
[
6
,
7
]
.
For those graphs
\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot H)
is
explicitly written down with the help of
Theorem
4.17
.

5
Examples

Example 5.1

(
G\odot\bar{K}_{n}
)
.

Let
G
be a connected graph on two or more vertices,
and let
H=\bar{K}_{n}
an empty graph on
n\geq 1
vertices.
Note that
H
is a
\kappa
-regular graph with
\kappa=0
,
and that
A_{H}=O
and
\mathrm{ev}(A_{H})=\mathrm{ev}_{M}(A_{H})=\{0\}
.
By simple algebra we obtain

\displaystyle\omega_{H}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{n\lambda}{2+\lambda}=\frac{(n+1)\lambda+2}{2+\lambda}\,,\quad\lambda_{*}=-\frac{2}{n+1}\,,

\displaystyle\psi_{H}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{\omega(\lambda)}=\frac{\lambda(\lambda+2)}{(n+1)\lambda+2}\,.

\displaystyle\psi_{H*}^{-1}(\lambda^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+1)\lambda^{\prime}-2+\sqrt{((n+1)\lambda^{\prime}-2)^{2}+8\lambda^{\prime}}}{2}\,.

(5.1)

Since
\mathrm{QEC}(G)\geq-1
, we have

\lambda_{*}=-\frac{2}{n+1}<\psi_{H*}^{-1}(-1)\leq\psi_{H*}^{-1}(\mathrm{QEC}(G))

and hence

-\psi_{H*}^{-1}(\mathrm{QEC}(G)))-2<-\frac{2n}{n+1}.

Since
\mathrm{ev}(A_{H})=\{0\}
, we have

-\psi_{H*}^{-1}(\mathrm{QEC}(G)))-2\notin\mathrm{ev}(A_{H})
.
Moreover, by
\min\mathrm{ev}(A_{H})=0
we obtain

-2-\min\mathrm{ev}(A_{H})\leq-\frac{\kappa+2}{n+1}\,.

Thus, the assumptions
in Theorem
4.14

are fulfilled and we conclude that

\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot\bar{K}_{n})=\psi_{H*}^{-1}(\mathrm{QEC}(G)),

where
\psi_{H*}^{-1}(\lambda)
is explicitly given by
(
5.1
).
Thus, the formula in
[
8
, Theorem 1.6]

is reproduced,
where we note that the cluster graph
(comb graph) of
G
and the complete bipartite graph
K_{1,n}

is nothing else but the corona graph
G\odot\bar{K}_{n}
.

Example 5.2

(double star)
.

The corona graph
K_{2}\odot\bar{K}_{n}
is called a
double star
.
As an immediate consequence of
Example
5.1
with
\mathrm{QEC}(K_{2})=-1
,
we have

\mathrm{QEC}(K_{2}\odot\bar{K}_{n})=\frac{-(n+3)+\sqrt{(n+3)^{2}-8}}{2}\,.

This is a reproduction of
[
8
, Theorem 1.5]
.

Example 5.3

(bearded complete graph)
.

For
m\geq 2
the corona graph
K_{m}\odot K_{1}
is a
bearded complete graph
BK_{m,n}
with
m=n
.
As an immediate consequence of
Example
5.1
with
\mathrm{QEC}(K_{m})=-1
,
we have

\mathrm{QEC}(K_{m}\odot K_{1})=-2+\sqrt{2}\,.

This is a special case of
[
4
, Theorem 4.3]
,
where
\mathrm{QEC}(BK_{n,m})=-2+\sqrt{2}=\mathrm{QEC}(P_{4})
is shown
for any
2\leq m\leq n
.

Example 5.4

(
G\odot pK_{q}
)
.

Let
G
be a connected graph on two or more vertices.
For
p\geq 1
and
q\geq 2

we consider
H=pK_{q}
, that is, the disjoint union of

p
copies of the complete graph
K_{q}
.
Note that the case of
q=1
falls into Example

5.1
.
Obviously,
H
is a
\kappa
-regular graph on
n=pq
vertices
with degree
\kappa=q-1
,
As is seen easily, we have

\mathrm{ev}(A_{H})=\{-1,q-1\}
and

\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A_{H})=\{q-1\}
.
Then, by simple algebra we have

\displaystyle\omega_{H}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{n\lambda}{\kappa+2+\lambda}=\frac{(n+1)\lambda+\kappa+2}{\kappa+2+\lambda}\,,\qquad\lambda_{*}=-\frac{\kappa+2}{n+1}\,,

\displaystyle\psi_{H}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{\omega(\lambda)}=\frac{\lambda(\lambda+\kappa+2)}{(n+1)\lambda+\kappa+2}\,,

\displaystyle\psi_{H*}^{-1}(\lambda^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+1)\lambda^{\prime}-(\kappa+2)+\sqrt{((n+1)\lambda^{\prime}-(\kappa+2))^{2}+4(\kappa+2)\lambda^{\prime}}}{2}\,.

We will examine two conditions
in Theorem
4.14
.
Since
\mathrm{QEC}(G)\geq-1
,

\lambda_{*}=-\frac{\kappa+2}{n+1}<\psi_{H*}^{-1}(-1)\leq\psi_{H*}^{-1}(\mathrm{QEC}(G)).

Moreover, using
\kappa=q-1
and
n=pq
, we have

\lambda_{*}=-\frac{\kappa+2}{n+1}=-\frac{q+1}{pq+1}\geq-1.

Hence
\psi_{H*}^{-1}(\mathrm{QEC}(G))>-1
and

-2-\psi_{H*}^{-1}(\mathrm{QEC}(G))<-1
,
which implies that

-2-\psi_{H*}^{-1}(\mathrm{QEC}(G))\not\in\mathrm{ev}(A_{H})
.
Moreover, we have

-2-\min\mathrm{ev}(A_{H})=-1\leq-\frac{\kappa+2}{n+1}.

Thus, two conditions in
Theorem
4.14

are fulfilled and we conclude that

\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot pK_{q})=\psi_{H*}^{-1}(\mathrm{QEC}(G)).

In an explicit form we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot pK_{q})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big\{(pq+1)\mathrm{QEC}(G)-(q+1)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sqrt{((pq+1)\mathrm{QEC}(G)-(q+1))^{2}+4(q+1)\mathrm{QEC}(G)}\,\Big\}.

This formula is valid for all
p\geq 1
and
q\geq 1
,
see Example
5.1
.

Example 5.5

(
G\odot K_{n}
)
.

Specializing parameters in Example
5.4
as
p=1

and
q=n\geq 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot K_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big\{(n+1)\mathrm{QEC}(G)-(n+1)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sqrt{((n+1)\mathrm{QEC}(G)-(n+1))^{2}+4(n+1)\mathrm{QEC}(G)}\,\Big\}.

This is a reproduction of
[
8
, Theorem 1.6]
,
since the cluster graph (comb graph) of
G
and
K_{n+1}

is nothing else but the corona graph
G\odot K_{n}
.

Example 5.6

(
G\odot pC_{q}
)
.

Let
G
be a connected graph on two or more vertices.
For
p\geq 1
and
q\geq 3

we consider
H=pC_{q}
, that is, the disjoint union of

p
copies of a cycle
C_{q}
.
Note that
H
is a
\kappa
-regular graph on
n
vertices
with
\kappa=2
and
n=pq
.
Obviously,
\mathrm{ev}_{M}(H)=\{2\}
and after simple algebra we obtain

\displaystyle\omega_{H}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{n\lambda}{\kappa+2+\lambda}=\frac{(n+1)\lambda+4}{\lambda+4}\,,\qquad\lambda_{*}=-\frac{4}{n+1}\,,

\displaystyle\psi_{H}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{\omega_{H}(\lambda)}=\frac{\lambda(\lambda+4)}{(n+1)\lambda+4}\,,

\displaystyle\psi_{H*}^{-1}(\lambda^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+1)\lambda^{\prime}-4+\sqrt{((n+1)\lambda^{\prime}-4)^{2}+16\lambda^{\prime}}}{2}\,.

(5.2)

Here we assume that
q\geq 4
is even.
Then, noting that
\min\mathrm{ev}(A_{H})=-2
,
we apply Theorem
4.17
to obtain

\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot H)=\begin{cases}\psi_{H*}^{-1}(\mathrm{QEC}(G)),&\text{if $\mathrm{QEC}(G)>0$},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Finally, using (
5.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot pC_{q})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big\{(pq+1)\mathrm{QEC}(G)-4

\displaystyle\qquad+\sqrt{((pq+1)\mathrm{QEC}(G)-4)^{2}+16\mathrm{QEC}(G)}\,\Big\}

for
\mathrm{QEC}(G)\geq 0
, and

\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot pC_{q})=0
otherwise.
For
q\geq 3
being odd, the situation become more complicated
and the discussion will appear elsewhere.

In all of the above examples, the corona graphs

G\odot H
are considered with
H
being a regular graph.
The next example treats the case in which
H
is not regular.

Example 5.7
.

For
p_{1}\geq 1
,
p_{2}\geq 1
and
1\leq q_{1}<q_{2}

we consider
H=p_{1}K_{q_{1}}\cup p_{2}K_{q_{2}}
,
which is the disjoint union of
p_{1}
copies of
K_{q_{1}}
and

p_{2}
copies of
K_{q_{2}}
.
We have

\mathrm{ev}(A_{H})=\{-1,\,q_{1}-1,\,q_{2}-1\},\qquad\mathrm{ev}_{M}(A_{H})=\{q_{1}-1,\,q_{2}-1\},

and hence
\min\mathrm{ev}(A_{H})=-1
and

\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{M}}(A_{H})\cup\{-2\}=\{\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}<\alpha_{3}\}=\{-2<q_{1}-1<q_{2}-1\}.

Moreover, by simple algebra we obtain

\displaystyle\omega_{H}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{p_{1}q_{1}\lambda}{\lambda+q_{1}+1}+\frac{p_{2}q_{2}\lambda}{\lambda+q_{2}+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+1)\lambda^{2}+(n+(p_{1}+p_{2})q_{1}q_{2}+q_{1}+q_{2}+2)\lambda+(q_{1}+1)(q_{2}+1)}{(\lambda+q_{1}+1)(\lambda+q_{2}+1)},

\displaystyle\psi_{H}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{\omega_{H}(\lambda)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda(\lambda+q_{1}+1)(\lambda+q_{2}+1)}{(n+1)\lambda^{2}+(n+(p_{1}+p_{2})q_{1}q_{2}+q_{1}+q_{2}+2)\lambda+(q_{1}+1)(q_{2}+1)},

where
n=p_{1}q_{1}+p_{2}q_{2}
.
By elementary examination of the quadratic polynomial
in the numerator of
\omega_{H}(\lambda)
we see that

-1<\lambda_{*}<0
,
where
\lambda_{*}
is the largest zero of
\omega_{H}(\lambda)
.
Since
\psi_{H*}^{-1}(\lambda^{\prime})>\lambda_{*}

for any
\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
,
for any connected graph
G
on two or more vertices
we have

\psi_{H*}^{-1}(\mathrm{QEC}(G))>\lambda_{*}>-1,

and hence

-2-\psi_{H*}^{-1}(\mathrm{QEC}(G))<-1=\min\mathrm{ev}(A_{H}).

Thus, condition (
4.34
) is
fulfilled in Theorem
4.11

and we conclude that

\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot(p_{1}K_{q_{1}}\cup p_{2}K_{q_{2}}))=\psi_{H*}^{-1}(\mathrm{QEC}(G)),

that is,

\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot(p_{1}K_{q_{1}}\cup p_{2}K_{q_{2}}))

coincides with the largest solution to

\psi_{H}(\lambda)=\mathrm{QEC}(G)
,
which is reduced to a cubic equation.
There are cases where the QEC
becomes an algebraic number of a quadratic field,
for example, the case where
\mathrm{QEC}(G)=1/(p_{1}+p_{2}-1)
.

Remark 5.8
.

It follows from Example
5.4
that

\displaystyle\mathrm{QEC}(K_{2}\odot K_{2})=-3+\sqrt{6}\approx-0.5505\cdots,

\displaystyle\mathrm{QEC}(K_{2}\odot 2K_{1})=\frac{-5+\sqrt{17}}{2}\approx-0.4384\cdots,

both of which are greater than
\mathrm{QEC}(K_{2}\odot K_{1})=-2+\sqrt{2}\approx-0.5857\cdots
.
For any
G
and
H
with
|V_{1}|\geq 2
and
|V_{2}|\geq 2
,
we see that
G\odot H
contains
K_{2}\odot K_{2}
or
K_{2}\odot 2K_{1}
as
an isometric subgraph.
Therefore,
\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot H)>-2+\sqrt{2}
.
In other words,
\mathrm{QEC}(G\odot H)=-2+\sqrt{2}
holds
if and only if
G\cong K_{2}
and
H\cong K_{1}
,
that is,
G\odot H\cong P_{4}
.
In this connection, see also
[
4
]
,
where all connected graphs
G
on two or more vertices
with
\mathrm{QEC}(G)<-2+\sqrt{2}
are classified.

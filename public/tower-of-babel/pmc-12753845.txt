Leveraging blockchain with zero knowledge proofs in wearable health technologies for personalized healthcare
Authors: Abdullah Ayub Khan, Asif Ali Laghari, Hamad Almansour, Teerath Kumar, Ines Hilali Jaghdam, Fahima Hajjej, Mohamad Afendee Mohamed
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Wearable health technology has revolutionized remote monitoring and personalized healthcare by allowing real-time surveillance of patient health measurements and vital signs. However, their widespread acceptance is hampered by issues with security, privacy preservation, data protection, and interoperability. Blockchain Technology (BT), in particular Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) and smart contracts, present a viable way to enhance the privacy, provenance, and integrity of wearable health data. This paper proposes a BT-enabled system that guarantees decentralized, unforged data management, transparency, immutability, and dynamic traceability for wearable health devices, particularly smartwatches with biosensors. To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed work, the main performance-related metrics—latency, throughput, computational overhead, security robustness, and scalability—are looked at. The experiment’s simulated findings show that BT integration is effective, with a 99.33% improvement in data integrity and protection. Automated access control protocols demonstrate data protection by utilizing smart contracts, and ZKPs guarantee verifiable data exchanges without jeopardizing patient privacy. These results demonstrate improved interoperability, decreased processing time, and increased security in comparison to comparable cutting-edge centralized platforms.

Introduction

The healthcare industry has encountered significant transformation due to the strong advancements in digital health technology, particularly in the areas of remote patient continuous monitoring and personalized healthcare1. Wearable medical technology, such as smart watches, fitness trackers, biosensor-enabled nanotechnology, and implementable medical Internet of Things (IoT) and related devices, has become widely popular due to its capacity to provide dynamic vital sign monitoring1,2. Such devices provide proactive healthcare management, early disease identification, and enhanced patient engagement. They include the assessment of heart rate, blood pressure, ECG data, glucose levels, and oxygen saturation3,4. However, their full-scale implementation in contemporary healthcare ecosystems is still hampered by a number of significant limitations pertaining to interoperability, privacy protection, security, and scalability.

On the other end, some of the most pressing concerns in wearable health monitoring systems are data protection, privacy preservation, and transparency. Such highlighted IoT-enabled devices continuously collect and transmit critical patient records in terms of sensitive personal information; they become targets for data breaches, cyberattacks, and unauthorized access. In the cloud-based environment, it is mainly calculated as a centralized system architecture for data management and preservation5–7. This cloud-based storage system, which is most probably used in the wearable healthcare environment for managing wearable data, is vulnerable to a single point of failure, along with insider threats, and unauthorized data manipulation5,6. In addition, the reliance on vendor-enabled service providers creates concerns about the data ownership and consent regarding the patients, as users often have restricted controls over how their health data is critically accessed, managed, and utilized7,8.

In recent times, another major limitation is counted as interoperability, as existing healthcare platforms often struggle with data inconsistency in terms of formatting, lack of standardized protocols, and restricted integration across different platforms9,10. Various IoT-enabled wearable devices operate on proprietary frameworks, making it difficult for healthcare providers to seamlessly exchange, across, and analyze patient data from multiple sources. This lack of interoperability not only hampers the effectiveness of personalized treatment plans but also limits the working objective of clinical decision-making11,12. In addition, IoT-enabled wearables generate huge amounts of data, which become the raising concerns regarding the storage efficiency, scalability, and capabilities of processing in real time.

In order to address such mentioned challenges, Blockchain Technology (BT) has been raised as a transformative solution for securing, organizing, managing, and optimizing wearable health data, as shown in Fig. 1. It is due to offering decentralization, forged-resistance facilities, transparency, and secure infrastructure that mainly eliminates the need for intermediaries of centralization. However, the current integration of smart contracts enables automated access control and protected data exchange, which ensures that only authorized entities can access patient records. In addition, Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) improve privacy concerns by improving data verification and validation, which means it cannot reveal sensitive information without permission. Thereby preserving patient confidentiality, integrity, provenance, and immutability. Throughout this, BT’s immutable ledger ensures that once health data is recorded, it cannot be manipulated or changed which significantly enhances trust and makes a trustworthy environment, where gaining reliability twice a time as compared to the centralized platform, especially for healthcare applications.

Wearable health monitoring and remote patient care

Undoubtedly, IoT-enabled wearable health monitoring devices, like nano-biosensors, smartwatches, and implantable gadgets have transformed personalized healthcare by enabling continuous health monitoring, early diagnoses, and dynamic data transmission to ministry of health, along with healthcare industries. According to16, wearable devices of healthcare have significantly enhanced remote monitoring ad patient care up to trice and reduced hospital readmissions by over 33%, especially for patients with chronic diagnosis, like diabetes, cardiovascular diseases, and hypertension.

On the other side, various studies indicate that data protection, privacy vulnerabilities, and interoperability remain the main limitations in current IoT-enabled wearables16,17. The reliance on a centralized cloud-based environment for patient record preservation and processing that are exposes wearable health data to cyberattacks, single point of failure, and reliable access control. The paper18 highlighted that more than 70% of healthcare wearable applications lack adequate encryption, making them susceptible to data breaches. Such limitations necessitate the adoption of a BT-enabled platform for decentralized, secure, and forged-resistant health data organizations.

The successful selection of a BT network in real-time healthcare monitoring requires high throughput, scalable architectures, and low latency18,19. Classical public networks of blockchain, like Bitcoin and Ethereum, still struggle with high consumption of power and transactional bottlenecks, making them unsuitable for wearable health applications. One of the solutions is explored by19, where consensus protocols and Layer-2 solutions improve BT performance. The paper20 proposed a Delegated Proof-of-Stake and Proof-of-Stake consensus mechanism, which reduces transactional cost in terms of time of validation down to 40% while maintaining information security. In a similar manner, the paper21 created a lightning consortium network based on off-chain processing hierarchy, which reduced BT-enabled transaction latency to 60% in wearable health data transmission.

Nevertheless, despite such developments, this paper highlights the challenges are remaining in current development, like energy efficiency, network congestion, and BT-enabled DApp adoption in limited resource-constrained environment. Moreover, various research is focused on the creation of lightweight consensus protocols, and AI-driven data organization and optimization hierarchies to improve BT scalability for wearable healthcare applications20–22.

Blockchain-enabled public network architecture for secure health data management

BT has been widely explored in healthcare to improve data integrity, privacy, and protection preservation. The nature of decentralization and immutability of BT ensures the healthcare records confidentiality, along with transparency and remain tamper-proof and auditable23. And so, it reduces fraudulence activities in medical research, insurance claims, and patient health record organization and optimization. However, the paper23,24 proposed a BT-enabled electronic health record application that demonstrated a 90% plus improvement in terms of data integrity and a 42% reduction in unauthorized data access compared to other classical centralized cloud-enabled storage. In a similar manner, the paper25 developed a private permissioned BT framework using Hyperledger Technology (HT) for secure patient data exchange, achieving an increase in transaction efficiency by 35% while maintaining robust security protocols.

On the other hand, Blockchain-enabled healthcare solutions face performance-related and scalability-based limitations, especially when handling large amounts of real-time wearable health records. The paper26 highlighted that high transaction latency and computational overhead are still key problems to BT adoption in real-time healthcare monitoring prospects. However, a major concern in BT-enabled healthcare applications is privacy preservation and protection. As publicly accessible BT may expose sensitive patient information. The paper27 discussed different privacy-enhancement solutions, like homomorphic encryption, multi-party computation, and ZKPs related in order to enable secure yet verifiable data exchange processes.

Implementation challenges and open research issues

Fig. 13Simulation Test (6)- Tamper-Proof Resistance and Security.

Simulation Test (6)- Tamper-Proof Resistance and Security.

The proposed BT-enabled framework for IoT-based wearable health monitoring offers improved privacy preservation, security, transparency, integrity, provenance, and scalability; its real-world deployment presents various regulatory, technical, and operational limitations. Such raising limitations must be addressed to ensure effective implementation and adoption as follows:

Computational overhead and processing cost

BT operations, like cryptographic hashing, encryption, and smart contract execution, require significant computing constraints, such as computational resources (computational power, high network usage, and cost of preservation). Wearable devices often have limited computational processing, which directly affects battery life36–38. It makes continuous BT transactions impractical. However, use off-chain computation for non-critical data and only preserve hashes on-chain to reduce IoT-enabled wearable workload. Along with that, the support of lightweight cryptographic algorithms in order to optimize energy efficiency. On the other side, public permissionless BT like Bitcoin or Ethereum experience high network congestion and slow transaction speed, which not only hinder real-time healthcare monitoring but also create unreliability in terms of efficiency and effectiveness. Processing bulky data from wearable healthcare with BT could increase latency and transaction costs.

Compliance with data regulations and privacy

Undoubtedly, healthcare data is subject to strict regulatory procedures, such as the Association of General Data Protection Regulation and Health Insurance Portability and Accountability39–41. However, the immutability of BT has created conflicts due to the nature of regulatory requirements for data erasure for the right to be forgotten. Preserving critical healthcare records, such as personal information on a public blockchain could raise privacy concerns. In this manner, only encrypted has-based data can be recorded via on-chain channels, while personal information can be preserved in off-chain storage, especially InterPlanetary File Storage (IPFS). Throughout this, the designed ZKPs are placed to allow data verification without exposure. On the other end, most of the hospitals manage electronic healthcare records via designing applications and use legacy databases that do not support BT. Due to this, integrating a decentralized solution with centralized management systems is critical. In order to achieve this, develop standardization regarding APIs for seamless communication between BT and electronic healthcare systems.

Transaction fees and scheduling cost

These days, public blockchains like Ethereum demand a hefty gas fee for each transaction, which complicates the ledger update process and makes frequent wearable device data updates costly42–45. Despite this, healthcare data is constantly updated, which not only results in constant generation but also raises transaction costs, which affects ledger management. The application of Layer-2 architecture, such as Polygon, which lowers petrol expenses, is one of the possibilities. Furthermore, sidechains have the potential to handle these health-related transactions off-chain, or off to the main blockchain. Conversely, wearable technology and BT-nodes can communicate securely by using end-to-end encryption. In order to keep an eye on BT network activity, it is also essential to implement an intrusion detection system in the middle.

Conclusion and future direction

This paper proposed a BT-enabled IoT-based wearable health monitoring platform that leverages smart contracts and ZKPs to improve scalability, security, and privacy protection. Throughout the analysis of comparison, the proposed solution achieves 93.33% improved privacy, protected preservation, lower latency, and higher scalability with other state-of-the-art methods of BT-enabled healthcare applications, especially consortium and public permissionless network architectures. It is worth noting that ZKPs and smart contracts, two components of blockchain technology (BT), offer a practical means of improving the integrity, provenance, and privacy of wearable health data. For wearable health devices, especially smartwatches with biosensors, this study suggests a BT-enabled system that ensures decentralized, unforged data management, transparency, immutability, and dynamic traceability. The primary performance-related metrics—latency, throughput, computational overhead, security resilience, and scalability—are examined in order to assess the efficacy of the proposed solution. ZKPs ensure verifiable data exchanges without endangering patient privacy, and automated access control methods use smart contracts to show data protection.

However, the future development of BT-powered wearable health monitoring can mainly focus on the following key domains:

Systemic immune-inflammatory and inflammatory response indices in hemorrhagic transformation of acute ischemic stroke patients undergoing intravenous thrombolysis
Authors: Jia Mu, Mingzhu Deng, Le Xu, Jie Yu, Kai Peng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Hemorrhagic transformation (HT) is a frequent complication following intravenous thrombolysis in patients with acute ischemic stroke (AIS). This study investigated the association between the systemic immune-inflammation index (SII) and the systemic inflammatory response index (SIRI) before thrombolysis and HT in patients with AIS. This retrospective analysis included patients with AIS who received intravenous thrombolysis with recombinant tissue plasminogen activator from January 2021 to January 2025. We computed the SII and SIRI using pretreatment hematological parameters. Multivariable logistic regression models were used to evaluate the independent associations of the SII and SIRI with post-thrombolysis HT risk. Receiver operating characteristic curves were employed to evaluate the predictive value of the SII and SIRI for HT. Of the 329 patients examined, 51 (15.5%) had HT. Multivariable logistic models showed that both SII (odds ratio, 1.004; 95% confidence interval, 1.002–1.005; P < .001) and SIRI (odds ratio, 3.133; 95% confidence interval, 1.985–4.945; P < .001) were independent risk factors for HT. For distinguishing HT, SII and SIRI had identical area under the curve values (0.828). This study demonstrates that the SII and SIRI indices can be predictive biomarkers for post-thrombolysis HT.

1. Introduction

Acute ischemic stroke (AIS) constitutes nearly 70% of all cerebrovascular accidents and is primarily characterized by cerebral hypoperfusion secondary to thromboembolic occlusion.[1] Notably, this neurological disease is characterized by a high incidence, frequent recurrence, significant disability, and a high fatality rate, imposing considerable socioeconomic burdens on families and society.[2] For patients with AIS within the 4.5-hour time window from the onset of the disease, intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) using recombinant tissue plasminogen activator (rt-PA) has become the primary treatment modality. It is important to note that IVT often leads to hemorrhagic transformation (HT),[3] causing accelerated neurological decline, premature mortality, and unfavorable prognostic outcomes.[4,5] Therefore, it is of great value and necessity to explore the risk factors and quantifiable biomarkers for HT after thrombolysis in patients with AIS.

Significantly, cerebrovascular inflammation mediates ischemic injury, with postischemic neuroinflammation compromising blood-brain barrier (BBB) integrity, directly promoting HT and exacerbating outcomes in AIS.[6] In this context, the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio has been shown in previous research to be an independent predictor of clinical outcomes in individuals with AIS.[7] In recent years, 2 novel inflammatory markers have been introduced: the systemic immune-inflammatory index (SII) and the systemic inflammatory response index (SIRI). Unlike the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio, these indices integrate a more comprehensive range of inflammatory cells, including monocytes and platelets. Monocytes can migrate and infiltrate the ischemic area at an early stage, promoting the release of various inflammatory mediators.[8] Platelets become excessively activated and can also synergistically amplify the inflammatory damage following ischemia.[9] These 2 novel inflammatory markers have reportedly been linked to clinical outcomes as independent predictors of cardiovascular diseases and all-cause mortality, showing notable predictive value in specific populations.[10]

Despite these advances, the associations between SII, SIRI, and HT following thrombolysis utilizing intravenous rt-PA in patients with AIS remain unknown. Thus, the connections between the SII and SIRI and the risk of post-thrombolysis HT were investigated in this research.

2. Methods

2.1. Participants

Data were collected from the stroke center at Yueyang Central Hospital between January 2021 and January 2025. Researchers conducted an observational cohort study involving 500 patients with AIS, all of whom received IVT with rt-PA within 4.5 hours after stroke onset. The study included 329 patients with AIS. The requirements for inclusion comprised patients aged 18 years or older, diagnosis of AIS according to the Chinese standards for the diagnosis and management of AIS in 2018, and administration of intravenous rt-PA thrombolysis when symptoms first appeared (within 4.5 hours).[11] The exclusion criteria were as follows: patients who underwent intravascular therapy; patients with tumor diseases, hematologic disorders, immune-mediated conditions, or hepatorenal dysfunction; those who experienced interrupted IVT; and patients with insufficient medical records.

The Ethics Committee of Yueyang Central Hospital gave its approval to the study. All procedures involved in this clinical research were performed in compliance with the core tenets of the Helsinki Declaration. The ethics committee obtained a waiver of informed consent as patients or guardians provided authorization for research use of anonymized treatment data during clinical care.

2.3. Diagnosis of HT

According to the guidelines for the management of AIS, we performed serial cranial computed tomography scans at admission and 24 to 36 hours post-thrombolysis.[13] HT was radiologically confirmed when a new intracranial hemorrhage appeared on follow-up imaging.[14] Two blinded neurologists independently reviewed all scans, with inconsistent opinions resolved through consensus.

2.4. Data acquisition

We established a data collection protocol for systematic information acquisition during the initial patient intake process. Demographic parameters included gender, age, and body mass index. Vascular risk factors included a history of stroke, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, atrial fibrillation, coronary artery disease, and current use of tobacco and ethanol. Medications before stroke included antiplatelet agents and anticoagulants. Clinical metrics included the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score, initial blood pressure, onset-to-treatment time (OTT), and stroke subtypes. The subtype of stroke was determined using a combination of imaging techniques, including computed tomography, magnetic resonance imaging, electrocardiogram, carotid ultrasonography, transthoracic echocardiography, and transcranial Doppler. Stroke subtypes were classified according to the TOAST (Trial of Org 10172 in Acute Stroke Treatment) system. We assessed hematological parameters in the emergency department before thrombolytic therapy.

2.5. Statistical analysis

Data normality was verified through Kolmogorov–Smirnov testing. Categorical data were represented as counts (%), and continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range). For categorical data, intergroup comparisons were performed using the chi-square or Fisher’s tests, whereas for continuous measures, Student’s t or Mann–Whitney U tests were used. Correlations and multicollinearity were examined using Pearson’s correlation test and variance inflation factors, respectively. Univariable and adjusted multivariable logistic regression models were used to identify predictors of HT. The overall discriminatory ability of SII and SIRI for HT after thrombolysis was assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. A two-sided P value < .05 was used to determine statistical significance in all analyses conducted using SPSS 25.0 (IBM).

The reasons for exclusion were intravascular therapy (n = 58), tumor diseases or immune disorders (n = 17), interrupted IVT (n = 7), and incomplete clinical data (n = 89). 3.3. Value of SII and SIRI in patients

ROC analysis indicated that an SII area under the curve (AUC) of 0.828 (95% CI, 0.782–0.867; P < .001), with a cutoff value of 761.58 × 109/L, demonstrated a sensitivity of 74.51% and a specificity of 87.41% for post-thrombolysis HT. A SIRI AUC of 0.828 (95% CI, 0.783–0.868; P < .001), with a cutoff value of 1.86 × 109/L, demonstrated a sensitivity of 76.47% and a specificity of 81.65% for post-thrombolysis HT, which was comparable to that of SII (Fig. 3).

4. Discussion

This study provides novel insights into the existing literature. To date, it is the first study to systematically explore the predictive value of SIRI and SII for HT following thrombolysis in stroke patients. Our study indicated that the prevalence of HT after thrombolysis was 15.5%, consistent with previous studies.[15,16] In this retrospective investigation, we found, based on the outcomes of univariate and multivariate binary logistic regression analyses, that SII and SIRI were independently associated with HT after thrombolysis in patients with AIS. Typically, a biomarker with an AUC between 0.7 and 0.9 indicates moderate diagnostic value. Thus, SII and SIRI can predict HT following thrombolysis.

In recent years, neuroinflammation has received increasing attention. The role of inflammation in the pathogenesis of stroke has been demonstrated in many investigations. AIS can trigger the release of a large amount of inflammatory chemicals, which can disrupt the BBB.[17] An essential part of HT is a breach in the BBB, which can cause blood to extravasate through fragile blood vessels.[18] The following are potential mechanisms: Following the administration of rt-PA in the rat model, the peripheral circulating neutrophil count showed a much greater increase compared with the control group, triggering a cascade of inflammatory reactions. Therefore, it can be inferred that IVT may initiate an inflammatory response.[19] The occurrence of a stroke can cause severe damage to glial cells at synapses, astrocytes, and neurons. During AIS, the BBB is damaged, cerebral edema is exacerbated, and neutrophils are initially produced in the infarct core and penumbra.[20] Ischemic tissues will release some chemical substances to attract neutrophils, and neutrophils release substances that promote inflammation, including proteases, matrix metalloproteinases associated with the extracellular matrix, and reactive oxygen species. This secretion activity ultimately leads to the generation of free radicals, which, in turn, inflict damage upon the ischemic tissues.[21,22] Researchers speculate that the lymphocyte count possesses neuroprotective properties, which are crucial in improving neurological function.[23] Conversely, existing research reveals that the interplay between lymphocytes and endothelial cells precipitates the onset of microvascular dysfunction and the synthesis of inflammatory mediators. Consequently, this cascade of events culminates in the apoptosis of neuronal cells and the disintegration of the BBB.[24]

SIRI includes the role of monocytes in AIS. During AIS, monocytes exhibit migratory and infiltrative activities at an early stage. These monocytes subsequently differentiate into macrophages, continuously taking up oxidized LDL, resulting in a massive accumulation of cholesterol and its esters within the cells. Eventually, foam cells are formed, and various inflammatory mediators are released.[8] Activated monocytes increase blood vessel permeability through the secretion of vascular endothelial growth factor, thereby compromising the BBB.[25] SII includes the role of platelets in AIS. AIS is likely to induce abnormal platelet function, with excessive activation and aggregation of platelets potentially hindering recovery following a stroke.[9] This study also found that SII and SIRI remained significant after controlling for confounding variables, indicating their potential as independent risk factors for HT.

Both SII and SIRI have demonstrated excellent diagnostic accuracy in the ROC curves. The value of SII is 0.828 (95% CI, 0.782–0.867), and the value of SIRI is 0.828 (95% CI, 0.783–0.868). The AUC values for SIRI and SII are the same, which is similar to a recent study.[12,26] These 2 composite inflammatory ratios can be easily obtained from patient blood cell counts, providing clinicians with a comprehensive and accurate means for prognostic evaluation.

This study has several limitations. First, the data used for this research were obtained from the Chinese stroke registry, and the study was retrospective.[27] Future studies should consider conducting multiethnic and multicenter prospective studies, as there may be bias in this study, which could reduce the generalizability of the research findings. Second, vascular recanalization status was not analyzed in this study. This may compromise the comprehensiveness of our evaluation of factors influencing HT. Future studies should standardize the collection of this parameter to address this limitation. Third, during hospitalization, composite inflammatory markers may change significantly. This study did not dynamically monitor composite inflammatory markers. Future prospective studies should further validate and expand the results of this study. Fourth, the current research uses traditional algorithms. With advances in AI and big data, more complex AI and machine-learning algorithms should be adopted to predict future medical and neurological outcomes and promote social development.[28,29]

5. Conclusion

In conclusion, our research showed that SII and SIRI are able to predict HT following thrombolysis. Both SIRI and SII could provide excellent predictive value. These are blood indices that are easily obtainable and free from subjective bias, providing a theoretical basis for the early intervention of patients with AIS. Further research is necessary to validate these findings.

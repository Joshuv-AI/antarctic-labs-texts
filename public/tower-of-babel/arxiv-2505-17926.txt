Simone Ciani, Ugo Gianazza, Zheng Li

Abstract
. We study Phragmén-Lindelöf-type theorems for functions u u in homogeneous De Giorgi classes, and we show that the maximum modulus μ + ​ ( r ) \mu_{+}(r) of u u has a power-like growth of order α ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \alpha\in(0,1) when r → ∞ r\to\infty . 1.
Introduction and Main Results

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open set, and for
x_{o}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
let
B_{\rho}(x_{o})
denote the ball of radius
\rho
centered at
x_{o}
. We have the following well-known definitions.

Definition 1.1
.

Given an open subset
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we say that a function
u\in W^{1,p}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\Omega)
belongs to the
De Giorgi class

[DG]^{\pm}_{p}(\Omega,\hat{\gamma})
for
1<p\leq N
if there exists a constant
\hat{\gamma}>1
such that for all
\sigma\in(0,1)
,
\rho>0
,
x_{o}\in\Omega
,
k\in\mathbb{R}
, and
B_{\rho}(x_{o})\subset\Omega
, the following estimate is satisfied:

(1.1)

\int_{B_{\sigma\rho}(x_{o})}|D[(u-k)_{\pm}]|^{p}\,dx\leq\frac{\hat{\gamma}}{[(1-\sigma)\rho]^{p}}\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{o})}|(u-k)_{\pm}|^{p}\,dx\,.

Furthermore, we let

[DG]_{p}(\Omega,\hat{\gamma})\equiv[DG]^{+}_{p}(\Omega,\hat{\gamma})\cap[DG]^{-}_{p}(\Omega,\hat{\gamma}).

Definition 1.2
.

Let
h\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap C(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Given an open subset
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the De Giorgi classes
[DG]^{+}_{p}(\bar{\Omega},h,\hat{\gamma})
in the closure of
\Omega
are defined to consist of functions
u\in[DG]^{+}_{p}(\Omega,\hat{\gamma})
such that
(u-h)_{+}\in W^{1,p}_{o}(\Omega)
and satisfying in addition inequalities of the type

\forall y\in\partial\Omega,\quad\forall B_{\rho}(y),\quad\forall\,\sigma\in(0,1),\quad\forall k\geq\sup_{B_{\rho}(y)\cap\partial\Omega}h,

\int_{B_{\sigma\rho}(y)\cap\Omega}|D[(u-k)_{+}]|^{p}\,dx\leq\frac{\hat{\gamma}}{[(1-\sigma)\rho]^{p}}\int_{B_{\rho}(y)\cap\Omega}|(u-k)_{+}|^{p}\,dx\,.

The various constants are as in (
1.1
). The classes
[DG]^{-}_{p}(\bar{\Omega},h,\hat{\gamma})
are defined analogously by replacing
(u-k)_{+}
with
(u-k)_{-}
provided that
\displaystyle k\leq\inf_{B_{\rho}(y)\cap\partial\Omega}h
.
Furthermore, we let

[DG]_{p}(\bar{\Omega},h,\hat{\gamma})\equiv[DG]^{+}_{p}(\bar{\Omega},h,\hat{\gamma})\cap[DG]^{-}_{p}(\bar{\Omega},h,\hat{\gamma}).

Remark 1.1
.

In Definitions
1.1
–
1.2
we referred to balls
B_{\rho}
; however, if for
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
we
let
K_{\rho}(y)
denote a cube of edge
2\rho

centered at
y
, equivalent definitions can be given in terms of
K_{\rho}(y)
, instead of balls. Moreover, when the center of a ball or of a cube coincides with the origin, we simply write
B_{\rho}
or
K_{\rho}
.

A fundamental result by De Giorgi
[
5
]
(see also the work of O.A. Ladyzenskaja & N.N. Ural’ceva
[
15
]
) shows that functions in
[DG]^{\pm}_{p}(\Omega,\hat{\gamma})
are locally bounded and locally Hölder continuous. Moreover, whenever they are non-negative, they satisfy the Harnack inequality
[
7
]
.

It is important to recall that the De Giorgi classes include local, weak solutions to elliptic equations with bounded and measurable coefficients, subject to some upper and lower ellipticity conditions, but also minima or even
Q
-minima of rather general functionals, even if they do not admit an Euler equation.

Hence, the interest in these classes stems from the fact that their members have very different origins, but they all enjoy properties which are typically associated to solutions to elliptic partial differential equations.

The purpose of this work is precisely in this same direction: we study Phragmén-Lindelöf-type properties satisfied by functions in De Giorgi classes.

We denote half spaces by

\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}=\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times\{x_{N}>0\},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbb{R}^{N}_{-}=\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times\{x_{N}<0\}.

In the sequel, we are interested in taking either
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
and
u\in[DG]^{+}_{p}(\bar{\Omega},h,\hat{\gamma})
such that
h=0
on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
, or
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
u\in[DG]^{+}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\hat{\gamma})
vanishing in a proper subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. In both cases, since the boundary value of
u
is zero, we need to take
k\geq 0
; therefore, it is not restrictive to directly assume
u\geq 0
.

Our main results are the following theorems.

Theorem 1.3
.

Take
1<p\leq N
and let
u\in[DG]^{+}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\hat{\gamma})
; assume that
u
is non-negative,
u\not\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
, and such that

(1.2)

\lim_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\ni\,y\rightarrow x\,\in\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}u\,=0.

Then, there exists
0<\alpha_{1}<1
depending on
N
,
p
, and
\hat{\gamma}
, such that

0<\liminf_{r\uparrow\infty}\,\frac{\mu_{+}(r)}{r^{\alpha_{1}}},\quad\text{ where}\quad\mu_{+}(r)=\sup_{B_{r}\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}u\,.

Remark 1.2
.

Condition (
1.2
) is to be understood in the sense of Definition
1.2
with
h\equiv 0
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Take
1<p\leq N
, let
u\in[DG]^{+}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\hat{\gamma})
, assume that
u
is non-negative,
u\not\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and
u
vanishes in

{\mathbb{H}}^{M}
, the
M
-dimensional hyperplane through the origin, that is

{\mathbb{H}}^{M}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\mbox{\small def}}}\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{M},0,0,\dots,0)\}\quad\text{ with }\,1\leq M\leq N-1.

Then, if
N-M<p\leq N
, there exists
0<\alpha_{2}<1
depending on
N
,
p
,
M
, and
\hat{\gamma}
, such that

0<\liminf_{r\uparrow\infty}\,\frac{\mu_{+}(r)}{r^{\alpha_{2}}},\quad\text{ where}\quad\mu_{+}(r)=\sup_{B_{r}\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}}u\,.

Remark 1.3
.

Since functions

are defined up to set of
p
-capacity
zero (see below §
2.1
for the definition of this notion), assuming that

vanishes on
{\mathbb{H}}^{M}
is well-defined.

1.1.
Novelty and Significance

It is quite difficult to give an exhaustive and complete overview of the state of the art concerning Phragmén–Lindelöf-type theorems. In the following, we limit ourselves to just few results, which are more directly connected to our work.

The first and classical formulation (see
[
29
]
) deals with the growth of a subharmonic function
u
in a halfplane
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
; the theorem states that if
u
is non-positive on the boundary of
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
and admits a positive value in it, then

\liminf_{r\uparrow\infty}\frac{\mu_{+}(r)}{r}>0,

where
\displaystyle\mu_{+}(r)=\sup_{(x,y)\in B_{r}\cap\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}|u(x,y)|
.

Even though the original result was stated for functions of a complex variable
z=x+iy
, nevertheless, it is essentially a theorem on harmonic functions of two variables. It was later generalized to harmonic functions of
N
variables by Ahlfors (see
[
2
, Theorem 8]
, and also the comment immediately below the statement). In
[
10
]
Granlund, Lindqvist and Martio studied the conformally invariant case, i.e., they considered extremals of the variational integral

\int_{\Omega}F(x,Du)dx,

where
F(x,\xi)\approx|\xi|^{N}
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
(the precise assumptions are collected in § 2.1 of their paper).

Thus, the plane harmonic case is included in their study, but the classical harmonic case in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
N\geq 3
is not. In this wider context, they obtained a further, proper generalization.

A Phragmén-Lindelöf-type theorem for non-negative sub-solutions to the parabolic
p
-Laplacian (so-called
p
-subharmonic functions) is given in
[
21
, Theorem 4.6]
; in the same paper it is also studied the behavior of
N
-subharmonic functions which vanish on
\mathbb{H}^{q}
for
1\leq q\leq N-1

[
21
, Theorem 4.8]
. The proofs are based on the comparison principle, and on explicit estimates of the
N
-harmonic measure. The sharpness of these results is discussed with the aid of straightforward counterexamples.

Although explicit computations of the
p
-harmonic measure for a general
p>1
are not as easy as for the
p=N
case, nevertheless, it turns out that proper estimates from below and from above suffice, and this allowed Lundström to extend
[
21
, Theorem 4.8]
to a general
p
(see
[
23
, Corollary 4.3]
, and obtain the corresponding Phragmén-Lindelöf-type theorem.

There is another important fact which is worth pointing out in this context, even though we will not touch upon it in the following. Once more, we do not pretend to give a full account of the state of the art.

If one directly assumes that
u
is a non-negative solution to
\Delta_{p}u=0
in
\{x_{N}>0\}
which continuously vanishes on the flat boundary
\{x_{N}=0\}
, then Phragmén-Lindelöf
qualitative
results can be
quantified
, and one can show that, modulo a proper normalization,
\displaystyle u(x)=x_{N}
in
\{x_{N}\geq 0\}
. Such a result was probably stated for the first time in a paper due to Loomis & Widder
[
22
]
about two-variable harmonic functions. Then, it was extended to positive harmonic functions in
N
variables by Rudin
[
30
, Lemma 1]
, whose proof is based on the same ideas of
[
22
]
. Related results were obtained by Gilbarg and Serrin in the plane for solutions to linear elliptic equations in non-divergence form with smooth coefficients (see
[
9
,
31
]
).

The corresponding extension to non-negative solutions of the
p
-Laplacian was studied, for example, in
[
13
]
. An interesting discussion of further extensions is given in the introduction of
[
27
]
.

Coming back to the qualitative formulation, we were inspired by the results in
[
21
,
23
]
. Due to the wide generality of functions in the De Giorgi classes, there is no chance of using the comparison principle in our case (indeed, whether any form of comparison principle holds is a major open problem for functions in
[DG]^{\pm}_{p}(\Omega,\hat{\gamma})
, even though it is generally believed that such a result does not hold). Hence, we had to develop a different approach, based on a proper use of the weak Harnack inequality (see Lemma
3.3
and Proposition
4.2
below).

Due both to the intrinsic smallness
of the quantity
\tau
which is postulated in the weak Harnack inequality, and to its qualitative knowledge (even though, in principle,
\tau
can be traced and made quantitative), it is quite natural to ask whether the value of
\alpha_{1}
in Theorem
1.3
is a sheer estimate linked to the approach we employ, and more refined tools could
show that the actual value is
\alpha_{1}=1
. In a similar way, one wonders about the optimality of
\alpha_{2}
, since Lundström shows that
\alpha_{2}=\frac{p-N+M}{p-1}
when
u
is a sub-solution of the
p
-Laplacian, under the same restrictions on
N
,
M
,
p
as in Theorem
1.4
.

By relying on proper counterexamples, we show below that this is not the case. Indeed, having
\alpha_{1}\in(0,1)
is a straightforward consequence of the wide generality of the De Giorgi classes, and, in particular, of the fact that they contain solutions to elliptic equations with bounded and measurable coefficients. An issue which we refrain from pursuing here is to trace the exact dependencies of
\alpha_{1},\,\alpha_{2}
on
\hat{\gamma},\,p,\,N,\,M
.

1.1.1.
A First Counterexample

The following example is due to Meyers
[
25
]
. Let
x
and
y
be the coordinates in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, consider the equation

(1.3)

\operatorname{div}({\mathbb{A}}\,Du)=0

where

{\mathbb{A}}=\begin{bmatrix}1-\left(1-\mu^{2}\right)\frac{y^{2}}{x^{2}+y^{2}}&\left(1-\mu^{2}\right)\frac{xy}{x^{2}+y^{2}}\\
\left(1-\mu^{2}\right)\frac{xy}{x^{2}+y^{2}}&1-\left(1-\mu^{2}\right)\frac{x^{2}}{x^{2}+y^{2}}\\
\end{bmatrix},

and
\mu
is a fixed constant in the range
0<\mu<1
. It can be easily seen that at each point
(x,y)
the eigenvalues of the coefficient matrix are
\mu^{2}
and
1
. Thus, equation (
1.3
) is a linear elliptic equation with bounded and measurable coefficients; the function

(1.4)

u(x,y)=\left(x^{2}+y^{2}\right)^{\frac{\mu-1}{2}}\cdot x

is a weak solution to equation (
1.3
) which vanishes for
x=0
, and it is easy to check that
Du
is in
L_{\operatorname{loc}}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for any
1<p<\frac{2}{1-\mu}
. Since
u
is a weak solution to (
1.3
) in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, it definitely belongs to
[DG]^{+}_{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},\hat{\gamma})

for a proper
\hat{\gamma}
, and this shows that we cannot expect
\alpha_{1}=1
in Theorem
1.3
.

We can extend the previous result to all dimensions. Let
(x,y,z)
be a point in the
N
-space, where
z
stands for the remaining
(N-2)
coordinates. We extend the given solution (
1.4
) and the coefficients by defining
u(x,y,z)=u(x,y)
, and do so similarly for
\mathbb{A}
. Then we have

(1.5)

L_{xy}u+\Delta_{z}u=0,

and equation (
1.5
) has the same ellipticity constants as the original equation.
Once more, the growth rate of
u
is
\alpha_{1}<1
.

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left\{|\nabla u|^{2}\left[a_{i1}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{1}}+a_{i2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{2}}+a_{i3}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{3}}+a_{i4}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{4}}\right]\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{54}(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}+x_{4}^{2})^{-\frac{3\alpha}{2}-1}(2-3\alpha)^{4}>0,

for any
\alpha\in(0,\frac{2}{3})
. Since it is easy to verify that
u\in W^{1,4}(B_{R}(0))
for any
\alpha\in(0,\frac{2}{3})
and for any
R>0
, we conclude
that indeed
u
is a sub-solution to the equation
1.6
in
B_{R}(0)
for any
R>0
. Hence,
u\in[DG]^{+}_{4}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\hat{\gamma})
for a proper

\hat{\gamma}
. Notice that here the precise value of
\hat{\gamma}
plays no fundamental role.

Moreover,
u
vanishes in the
2
-dimensional hyperplane
{\mathbb{H}}^{2}=\{x_{1}=x_{2}=0\}
, and it is apparent that

\mu_{+}(r)\approx r^{\frac{2}{3}-\alpha}.

Since
\frac{2}{3}-\alpha<\frac{2}{3}=\frac{M}{N-1}
, this shows that in general we cannot expect
\alpha_{2}
in Theorem
1.4
to be equal to
\frac{M}{N-1}
, which is the optimal exponent growth given in
[
21
, Theorem 4.8]
for the
N
-Laplacian.

As a matter of fact, there is no need to consider the
N
-Laplacian: similar counterexamples can be built for the
p
-Laplacian for any value of
p
, provided
M
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
1.4
.

A first simplification is the extension of

is the set of the Lebesgue points of

. Moreover, if

satisfies (

), then the zero-extension

\tilde{u}(x)=\begin{cases}u^{*}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\\
0,&x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},\end{cases}

satisfies
\tilde{u}\in W^{1,p}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

and (

) with the integration extended over the full-balls. This allows us to work locally in

its zero-extension.

In the following

stands for the
(N-1)
-dimensional measure of the surface of the unit ball in

\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle\raise 4.0pt\hbox to7.0pt{\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt}$}}\kern-5.58334pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\raise 4.0pt\hbox to7.0pt{\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt}$}}\kern-5.58334pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\raise 4.0pt\hbox to7.0pt{\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt}$}}\kern-5.22743pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\raise 4.0pt\hbox to7.0pt{\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt}$}}\kern-5.07988pt}\!\int_{\Omega}f(x)\,dx\equiv|\Omega|^{-1}\int_{\Omega}f(x)\,dx.

2.1.
Variational Capacity

Let
K
be a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
Q
an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
containing
K
: for
p\geq 1
, the
p
-capacity of the set
K
with respect to the set
Q
is defined by

(2.1)

\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K,Q)=\inf\left\{\int_{Q}|D\varphi|^{p}dx:\,\,\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(Q),\varphi\geq 1\text{ on }K\right\}.

If
U\subset Q
is open, we let

\operatorname{cap}_{p}(U,Q)=\sup_{K\subset U,\,\text{compact}}\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K,Q),

and for an arbitrary set
V\subset Q
we define

\operatorname{cap}_{p}(V,Q)=\inf_{V\subset U\subset Q,\,U\,\text{open}}\operatorname{cap}_{p}(U,Q).

Let us state the main properties of
\operatorname{cap}_{p}
. For their proofs, we refer to
[
8
]
, or to
[
11
, Chapter 2]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
1<p\leq N
.

(1)

\operatorname{cap}_{p}(\emptyset,Q)=0
;

(2)

Given two sets
K_{1}\subset K_{2}\subset\subset Q
, we have
\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K_{1},Q)\leq\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K_{2},Q)
;

(3)

Given two compact sets
K_{1},\,K_{2}\subset\subset Q
, we have

\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K_{1}\cap K_{2},Q)+\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K_{1}\cup K_{2},Q)\leq\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K_{1},Q)+\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K_{2},Q);

(4)

For every sequence
K_{j}
of compact subsets of
Q
such that
K_{1}\supset\dots K_{j}\supset K_{j+1}\dots
, we have

\operatorname{cap}_{p}(\cap_{j=1}^{+\infty}K_{j},Q)=\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K_{j},Q);

(5)

For every sequence
K_{j}
of subsets of
Q
such that
K_{1}\subset\dots K_{j}\subset K_{j+1}\dots
, we have

\operatorname{cap}_{p}(\cup_{j=1}^{+\infty}K_{j},Q)=\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K_{j},Q);

(6)

If
V=\cup_{i}V_{i}\subset Q
, we have
\operatorname{cap}_{p}(V,Q)\leq\sum_{i}\operatorname{cap}_{p}(V_{i},Q)
;

(7)

Let
B_{r}(x_{o}),\,B_{R}(x_{o})\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
for any
x_{o}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
0<r<R
. Then, when
1<p<N
, we have

\operatorname{cap}_{p}(B_{r}(x_{o}),B_{R}(x_{o}))=\omega_{N}|q|^{p-1}|R^{q}-r^{q}|^{1-p},\quad q=\frac{p-N}{p-1};

when
p=N
, we have

\operatorname{cap}_{p}(B_{r}(x_{o}),B_{R}(x_{o}))=\omega_{N}\left(\log\frac{R}{r}\right)^{1-N};

(8)

Given a compact set
K\subset Q
and
\lambda>0
, we have

\operatorname{cap}_{p}(\lambda K,\lambda Q)=\lambda^{N-p}\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K,Q)

for any
1<p<N
;

(9)

Given a set
A\subset Q
, we have
|A|\leq C[\operatorname{cap}_{p}(A,Q)]^{\frac{N}{N-p}}
for any
1<p<N
and for some constant
C
depending only on
p
and
N
;

(10)

Given a set
A\subset Q
, we have
\operatorname{cap}_{p}(A,Q)\leq{\mathcal{H}}^{N-p}(A)
for any
1<p<N
, where
\mathcal{H}^{s}
is the
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure; the constant
C
depends only on
p
and
N
.

The last statement can be refined. Once more, we refer to
[
8
]
for the proofs.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
1<p<N
and
A\subset\subset Q
. If
{\mathcal{H}}^{N-p}(A)<\infty
, then
\operatorname{cap}_{p}(A,Q)=0
.
On the other hand,
1<p\leq N
and
\operatorname{cap}_{p}(A,Q)=0
, the
{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(A)=0
for all
s>N-p
.

2.2.
Fat Sets

We come to a notion that plays a fundamental role in the following.

Definition 2.1
.

Given a set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, for
1<p<N
we say that
E
is
p
-locally uniformly fat
if there exist
r_{o}>0
and
c_{o}>0
such that for any
0<r<r_{o}
and for every
x\in E
we have

(2.2)

\frac{\operatorname{cap}_{p}(E\cap B_{r}(x),B_{2r}(x))}{\operatorname{cap}_{p}(B_{r}(x),B_{2r}(x))}\geq c_{o}.

If
p=N
, then (
2.2
) is replaced by

\operatorname{cap}_{N}(E\cap B_{r}(x),B_{2r}(x))\geq c_{o}.

In order to understand the role played by such a notion in our context, let us first consider the case of
E=\mathbb{R}^{N}_{-}
.

It is easy to see that
E
is
p
-locally uniformly fat for any
1<p\leq N
with
r_{o}=+\infty
. First of all, it is apparent that it suffices to consider
x
which belongs to the hyperplane
x_{N}=0
.

If we let
B_{r}^{-}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\mbox{\small def}}}B_{r}(x)\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}_{-}
, for any
p\in(1,N)
, by (7) and (9) of Proposition
2.1
we have

\displaystyle{\operatorname{cap}_{p}(E\cap B_{r}(x),B_{2r}(x))}

\displaystyle={\operatorname{cap}_{p}(B_{r}^{-}(x),B_{2r}(x))}\geq{C(N,p)}r^{N-p}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\frac{\operatorname{cap}_{p}(E\cap B_{r}(x),B_{2r}(x))}{\operatorname{cap}_{p}(B_{r}(x),B_{2r}(x))}\geq C(N,p).

If
p=N
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{cap}_{N}(E\cap B_{r}(x),B_{2r}(x))=\operatorname{cap}_{N}(B_{r}^{-}(x),B_{2r}(x))\geq\operatorname{cap}_{N}(J_{r}(x),B_{2r}(x)),

where
J_{r}=\{(0,0,\dots,0,x_{N}):\,-r<x_{N}<0\}
; by
[
20
]
we have

\operatorname{cap}_{N}(J_{r}(x),B_{2r}(x))>\frac{\omega_{N-2}}{\kappa_{N}^{N-1}}\ln 3,

where
\displaystyle\kappa_{N}=\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}(\sin t)^{\frac{2-N}{N-1}}\,dt
, whence the conclusion immediately follows.

Now, let us consider
E={\mathbb{H}}^{M}
, the
M
-dimensional hyperplane through the origin, that is

{\mathbb{H}}^{M}=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{M},0,0,\dots,0)\}\quad\text{ with }\,1\leq M\leq N-1.

Again, we have that
E
is
p
-locally uniformly fat with
r_{o}=\infty
for any
1\leq M\leq N-1
, provided that
N-M<p\leq N
. This is proved in
[
23
, Lemma 3.2]
.

Moreover, when
p=N
, it is a straightforward consequence of the following estimate proven in
[
3
]
:

\operatorname{cap}_{N}(E\cap B_{r}(x),B_{2r}(x))\geq\omega_{q-1}\omega_{N-q-1}2^{1-q}\frac{\displaystyle\left[\int_{1}^{3}\frac{(t^{2}-1)^{q-1}}{t^{q}}dt\right]}{\displaystyle\left[\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}(\sin t)^{\frac{1+q-N}{N-1}}dt\right]^{N-1}},

where
x\in{\mathbb{H}}^{M}
, and
q=1,2,\cdots,N-1
.

Remark 2.1
.

It is worth pointing out that the explicit value of the
2
-capacity of a disk
D_{r}
of radius
r
or of a semi-ball
B^{-}_{r}
of the same radius with respect to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
are given in
[
17
, Chapter II, § 3, No. 14]
. We have

\operatorname{cap}_{2}(D_{r},\mathbb{R}^{3})=\frac{2r}{\pi^{2}},\qquad\operatorname{cap}_{2}(B^{-}_{r},\mathbb{R}^{3})=\frac{2r}{\pi}\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right).

Finally, concerning
p
-locally uniformly fat sets, we have the following result.

Proposition 2.3

(Lewis,
[
19
]
)
.

Given
1<p\leq N
, suppose
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is closed and
p
-locally uniformly fat
with constants
c_{o}
and
r_{o}
. Then, there exist
\varepsilon_{o}\in(0,1)
and
c_{1}>0
depending only on
p
,
N
,
c_{o}
, such that whenever
x\in E
,
0<r<r_{o}
, and
p-\varepsilon_{o}<q<p
, the set
E
is
q
-locally uniformly fat with constants
c_{1}
and
r_{o}
, i.e.

\frac{\operatorname{cap}_{q}(E\cap B_{r}(x),B_{2r}(x))}{\operatorname{cap}_{q}(B_{r}(x),B_{2r}(x))}\geq c_{1}.

The original Lewis’ proof does not give an explicit functional dependence of
c_{1}
on
c_{o}
. However, the proof provided in
[
26
]
, later extended to metric spaces in
[
4
]
(see also
[
18
]
), allows to conclude that

c_{1}=C(N,p)c_{o}.

2.3.
A Different Notion of Capacity for

1<p<N

An equivalent notion of capacity can be given, assuming
Q\equiv\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Let us first consider the case where
1<p<N
. We do not go into details here and limit ourselves to underlining the link between capacity in this new framework and nonlinear potential theory; for more information, we refer the interested reader to
[
24
]
. For simplicity, we will write
\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K)
instead of
\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K,\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

There exists a Radon measure
\mu_{K}
supported in
K
such that

(2.3)

\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K)=\int_{K}d\mu_{K}.

Such a measure, called
capacitary distribution of
K
, can be identified with an element of
[W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})]^{*}
, and generates a potential
{\mathcal{U}}_{K}
by the formula

(2.4)

{\mathcal{U}}_{K}(x)\equiv\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left\{{{\frac{d\mu_{K}(z)}{|y-z|^{N-1}}}}\right\}^{{\frac{1}{p-1}}}{\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N-1}}}.

Remark 2.2
.

If
p=2
, by the Riesz composition formula (
2.4
) implies

{\mathcal{U}}_{K}(x)\equiv\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\frac{d\mu_{K}(z)}{|x-z|^{N-2}}},

which is, up to a normalizing constant, the classical newtonian potential generated by
\mu_{K}
.

By (2.10) of
[
24
]
, the energy associated with the potential
{\mathcal{U}}_{K}
is

{\mathcal{E}}(\mu_{K})\equiv\|{\mathcal{U}}_{K}\|^{p}_{W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left\{{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\frac{d\mu_{K}(z)}{|x-z|^{N-1}}}}\right\}^{{\frac{p}{p-1}}}\,dx.

The connection between these quantities is that the infimum in the definition of capacity (
2.1
) is achieved when

\varphi\equiv{\mathcal{U}}_{K}
. Therefore,

(2.5)

\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left\{{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\frac{d\mu_{K}(z)}{|x-z|^{N-1}}}}\right\}^{{\frac{p}{p-1}}}\,dx.

Let us now state and prove a Poincaré–type inequality.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
v\in W^{1,1}(B_{R})
and assume that
v
vanishes on a subset
E_{0}
of
B_{R}
. Let
K
be any compact subset of
E_{0}
and let
\mu_{K}
denote its capacitary distribution. There exists a constant
\gamma
depending only upon
N
such that for
1<p<N

(2.6)

\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K)\int_{B_{R}}|v|\,dx\leq\gamma R^{N}\int_{B_{R}}|Dv(x)|\left\{{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\frac{d\mu_{K}(z)}{|x-z|^{N-1}}}}\right\}\,dx.

Proof.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
v
is continuous and non-negative. Let
z\in K\subset E_{0}
and
x\in B_{R}\backslash E_{0}
. Then

v(x)-v(z)=\int_{0}^{|x-z|}{\frac{d}{dr}}v(z+r\nu)\,dr\leq\int_{0}^{|x-z|}|Dv(y)|\,dr

where
\nu\equiv{\frac{x-z}{|x-z|}}
,
y=z+r\nu
.

Let us integrate this with respect to the Lebesgue measure
dx
over all
x\in B_{R}\backslash E_{0}
and then in
d\mu_{K}(z)
over all
z\in K
. Recalling that
v
vanishes in
K
and taking into account (
2.3
) we obtain

(2.7)

\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K)\int_{B_{R}}v\,dx\leq\int_{K}d\mu_{K}(z)\int_{B_{R}\backslash E_{0}}\,dx\left\{{\int_{0}^{|x-z|}|Dv(y)|\,dr}\right\}.

We estimate the last two integrals for
z\in K
fixed, by expressing them in polar coordinates with pole at
z
. If
\tilde{x}\equiv\tilde{x}(z,\nu)
is the polar representation of
\partial B_{R}
with pole at
z
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}\backslash E_{0}}\,dx\left\{{\int_{0}^{|x-z|}|Dv(y)|\,dr}\right\}\leq\int_{B_{R}}\,dx\left\{{\int_{0}^{|x-z|}|Dv(y)|\,dr}\right\}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tilde{x}(z,\nu)}|x-z|^{N-1}d|x-z|\int_{|\nu|=1}d\nu\left\{{\int_{0}^{|x-z|}{\frac{|Dv(y)|}{|y-z|^{N-1}}}|y-z|^{N-1}\,d|y-z|}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{2R}|x-z|^{N-1}d|x-z|\left\{{\int_{|\nu|=1}\int_{0}^{|x-z|}{\frac{|Dv(y)|}{|y-z|^{N-1}}}|y-z|^{N-1}\,d|y-z|\,d\nu}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma(N)R^{N}\int_{B_{R}}{\frac{|Dv(y)|}{|y-z|^{N-1}}}\,dy.

We substitute this in (
2.7
) and interchange the order of integration with the aid of Fubini’s theorem to obtain (
2.6
).

Remark 2.3
.

Proposition
2.4
continues to hold if
B_{R}
is replaced by any convex set
{\mathcal{C}}

In the following we will employ Proposition
2.4
in a special form. Indeed, let
u\in W^{1,1}(B_{2R})
and let
l
and
k
be any two numbers satisfying
l>k
. Define

v=\left\{\begin{aligned} l-k&\quad\hbox{\rm if}\ u>l,\\
u-k&\quad\hbox{\rm if}\ k\leq u\leq l,\\
0&\quad\hbox{\rm if}\ u<k,\end{aligned}\right.

and set

A_{l,R}\equiv\{x\in B_{R}:\ u(x)>l\},\ \ \ R>0.

If
A_{k,R}
is defined analogously, from (
2.6
) applied to
v
we deduce

(2.8)

\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K)\int_{B_{R}}v\,dx\leq CR^{N}\int_{A_{k,\frac{3}{2}R}\backslash A_{l,\frac{3}{2}R}}|Du(x)|\left\{{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\frac{d\mu_{K}(z)}{|x-z|^{N-1}}}}\right\}\,dx

for every compact set
K\subset\{x\in B_{R}:\ u(x)<k\}
.
From (
2.8
) we finally obtain

(2.9)

(l-k)\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K)|A_{l,R}|\leq cR^{N}\int_{A_{k,\frac{3}{2}R}\backslash A_{l,\frac{3}{2}R}}|Du(x)|\left\{{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\frac{d\mu_{K}(z)}{|x-z|^{N-1}}}}\right\}\,dx

for every compact set
K\subset\{x\in B_{R}:\ u(x)<k\}
.

As a direct further consequence of Proposition
2.4
we have the following.

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
v\in W^{1,p}(B_{R})
,
1<p<N
, satisfy the assumptions of Proposition
2.4
and let

\delta(K;R)\equiv{\frac{\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K)}{R^{N-p}}}.

There exists a constant
\gamma
depending only upon
N
such that

(2.10)

[\delta(K;R)]^{1/p}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle\raise 4.0pt\hbox to7.0pt{\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt}$}}\kern-5.58334pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\raise 4.0pt\hbox to7.0pt{\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt}$}}\kern-5.58334pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\raise 4.0pt\hbox to7.0pt{\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt}$}}\kern-5.22743pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\raise 4.0pt\hbox to7.0pt{\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt}$}}\kern-5.07988pt}\!\int_{B_{R}}|v|\,dx\leq\gamma R\left(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle\raise 4.0pt\hbox to7.0pt{\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt}$}}\kern-5.58334pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\raise 4.0pt\hbox to7.0pt{\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt}$}}\kern-5.58334pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\raise 4.0pt\hbox to7.0pt{\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt}$}}\kern-5.22743pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\raise 4.0pt\hbox to7.0pt{\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt}$}}\kern-5.07988pt}\!\int_{B_{R}}|Dv(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}.

Proof.
From (
2.6
) by the Hölder inequality

\displaystyle\operatorname{cap}_{p}(K)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}|v|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\gamma R^{N}\left(\int_{B_{R}}|Dv(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{B_{R}}\left\{{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{d\mu_{K}(z)}{|y-z|^{N-1}}}\right\}^{{\frac{p}{p-1}}}\,dy\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}.

Now (
2.10
) follows from (
2.5
) and the definition of
\delta(K;R)
.

2.4.
A Different Notion of Capacity for

p=N

When
p=N
things are more delicate.
Estimates (
2.3
)–(
2.4
) and (
2.5
) continue to hold (see
[
24
]
, but also
[
1
, Chapter 2]
), but they need to be written not in terms of the Riesz kernel

I_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\mbox{\small def}}}\frac{\gamma_{1}}{|x|^{N-1}},\quad\gamma_{1}=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{N-1}{2}\right)}{2\pi^{\frac{N+1}{2}}},

but in terms of the Bessel kernel

G_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\mbox{\small def}}}{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right\}\equiv\frac{1}{(2\pi^{N})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{e^{ix\cdot\xi}}{(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\,d\xi,

or also

G_{1}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}|x|^{-\frac{N-2}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{\frac{N}{2}}}{(1+t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}J_{\frac{N-2}{2}}(|x|t)\,dt,

where
J_{\nu}
denotes the Bessel function of order
\nu
. It is worth pointing out that

G_{1}(x)\sim I_{1}(x),\qquad\text{ as }\,\,|x|\to 0,

and that for any
c\in(0,1)

G_{1}(x)=O(e^{-c|x|})\qquad\text{ as }\,\,|x|\to\infty.

Coming to Proposition
2.4
, the estimate

\int_{B_{R}\backslash E_{0}}\,dx\left\{{\int_{0}^{|x-z|}|Dv(y)|\,dr}\right\}\leq\gamma(N)R^{N}\int_{B_{R}}{\frac{|Dv(y)|}{|y-z|^{N-1}}}\,dy

obviously continues to hold. When we substitute it into (
2.7
), take into account that
|y-z|\leq 2R
, and interchange the order of integration with the aid of Fubini’s Theorem, we can observe that

\displaystyle\int_{K}{\frac{d\mu_{K}(z)}{|y-z|^{N-1}}}

\displaystyle=\int_{K}e^{\frac{|y-z|}{2R}}e^{-\frac{|y-z|}{2R}}{\frac{d\mu_{K}(z)}{|y-z|^{N-1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\int_{K}e^{-\frac{|y-z|}{2R}}{\frac{d\mu_{K}(z)}{|y-z|^{N-1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G_{1}(|x-z|)d\mu_{K}(z).

Hence, (
2.6
) becomes

\operatorname{cap}_{N}(K)\int_{B_{R}}|v|\,dx\leq\gamma R^{N}\int_{B_{R}}|Dv(x)|\left\{{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G_{1}(|x-z|)\,d\mu_{K}(z)}\right\}\,dx.

Once we recall that for
p=N

\operatorname{cap}_{N}(K)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G_{1}(|x-z|)\,d\mu_{K}(z)\right\}^{{\frac{N}{N-1}}}\,dx,

it is apparent that Corollary 2.1 remains the same.

For

Q(R)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\mbox{\small def}}}\Omega\cap\overline{B_{R}},\quad\delta(R)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\mbox{\small def}}}\frac{\operatorname{cap}_{p}(Q(R))}{R^{N-p}}.

Proposition 3.1

Under the previous assumptions, for any

4.1.
De Giorgi Classes and Sub(Super)-Harmonic
Functions

For any open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the so-called
generalized De Giorgi classes

[GDG]_{p}^{\pm}(\Omega,\gamma)

are the collection of functions
u\in W^{1,p}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\Omega)
,
for some
p>1
, satisfying

\int_{B_{\rho}(y)}|D(u-k)_{\pm}|^{p}dx\leq\frac{\gamma}{(R-\rho)^{p}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-\rho}\Big)^{Np}\int_{B_{R}(y)}|(u-k)_{\pm}|^{p}dx

for all balls
B_{\rho}(y)\subset B_{R}(y)\subset\Omega
, and all

k\in\mathbb{R}
, for a given positive constant
\gamma
. An equivalent definition can be given in terms of cubes, instead of balls.

Convex,
monotone, non-decreasing functions of
sub-harmonic functions are sub-harmonic. Similarly,
concave, non-decreasing, functions of super-harmonic
functions are super-harmonic. Similar statements hold
for weak, sub(super)-solutions of linear elliptic equations
with measurable coefficients
[
28
]
.
The next lemma establishes analogous properties
for functions
u\in[DG]^{\pm}_{p}(\Omega,\hat{\gamma})
.

Lemma 4.1

(Lemma 2.1 in
[
6
]
)
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be convex and non-decreasing,
and let
u\in[DG]_{p}^{+}(\Omega,\hat{\gamma})
.
There exists a positive constant
\overline{\gamma}

depending only on the data,
\hat{\gamma}
, and
independent of
u
, such that

\varphi(u)\in[GDG]_{p}^{+}(\Omega,\overline{\gamma})
.

Likewise let
\psi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be concave and non-decreasing,
and let
u\in[DG]_{p}^{-}(\Omega,\hat{\gamma})
.
There exist a positive constant
\overline{\gamma}

depending only on the data,
\hat{\gamma}
, and
independent of
u
, such that

\psi(u)\in[GDG]_{p}^{-}(\Omega,\overline{\gamma})
.

We also have the following results.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
u\in[DG]_{p}^{+}(\Omega,\hat{\gamma})
, assume
0\leq u<1
, and take
\varphi(t):=\frac{1}{1-t}
,
\psi(t)=\ln\frac{1}{1-t}
with
t\in[0,1)
. Then for all
\tau>0

there exists a constant
C_{\tau}
depending only upon
the data
\{N,p\}
and

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{\rho}(y)}\frac{1}{1-u}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\tau}\,\left(1+\hat{\gamma}\right)^{\frac{(N+1)N}{p\tau}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-\rho}\Big)^{\frac{N}{\tau}}\left(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle\raise 4.0pt\hbox to7.0pt{\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt}$}}\kern-5.58334pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\raise 4.0pt\hbox to7.0pt{\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt}$}}\kern-5.58334pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\raise 4.0pt\hbox to7.0pt{\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt}$}}\kern-5.22743pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\raise 4.0pt\hbox to7.0pt{\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt}$}}\kern-5.07988pt}\!\int_{B_{R}(y)}\left(\frac{1}{1-u}\right)^{\tau}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\tau}},

(4.2)

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{\rho}(y)}\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-u}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\tau}\,\left(1+\hat{\gamma}\right)^{\frac{(N+1)N}{p\tau}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-\rho}\Big)^{\frac{N}{\tau}}\left\{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle\raise 4.0pt\hbox to7.0pt{\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt}$}}\kern-5.58334pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\raise 4.0pt\hbox to7.0pt{\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt}$}}\kern-5.58334pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\raise 4.0pt\hbox to7.0pt{\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt}$}}\kern-5.22743pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\raise 4.0pt\hbox to7.0pt{\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt}$}}\kern-5.07988pt}\!\int_{B_{R}(y)}\left[\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-u}\right)\right]^{\tau}dx\right\}^{\frac{1}{\tau}},

for any pair of balls
B_{\rho}(y)\subset B_{R}(y)\subset\Omega
.

Proof.

Since both
\varphi
and
\psi
are convex, strictly increasing functions, (

)–(

) are straightforward consequences of Lemma

and of Lemma

.

]

Let

7.
Final Remarks

The weak Harnack inequality of Proposition
4.2
does not depend on the special choices
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}_{-}
or
\Omega=\mathbb{H}^{M}
, and it can be formulated for any
0\leq u\in[DG]^{+}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\gamma)
that vanishes in of a
p
-fat set
\Omega
.

Moreover, the proof of Theorems
1.3
-
1.4
relies on three different features:

•

the zero-extension of
u
in
\Omega
;

•

the weak Harnack inequality for
p
-fat sets;

•

the possibility to repeat the procedure for arbitrarily big radii, i.e. for
\Omega
unbounded.

Hence, our result is valid also for the other examples given in
[
16
, Chapter I, § 6]
, i.e. the slab
S
, where

\text{S}:=\{(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{N}):|x_{N}|<h\},

the double circular cone, the one-edged cone, and many others (we refrain from giving complete definitions of these objects, and we refer the interested reader to
[
16
]
).

Furthermore, the weak Harnack inequality for
p
-fat sets may be useful in the future also to attack properties of singular solutions, such as in
[
11
, Theorem 7.40]
. Here a similar statement seems reasonable to be valid with
\alpha<0
.

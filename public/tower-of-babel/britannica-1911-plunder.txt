PLUNDER, to rob, to pillage, especially in war. The word
came into English usage directly from Ger. plundern (derived
from a substantive Plunder meaning “household stuff,” bedclothes,
clothing, &c.), particularly with reference to the pillaging
of the Thirty Years’ War. Thomas May (History of the Long Parliament,
1647; quoted in the New English Dictionary) says:
“Many Tounes and Villages he (Prince Rupert) plundered,
which is to say robb'd, for at that time first was the word plunder
used in England, being borne in Germany.” The New English Dictionary’s
earliest quotation is from the Swedish Intelligencer
(1632).

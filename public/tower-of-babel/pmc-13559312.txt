Prognostic significance of recurrent laryngeal nerve lymph node dissection in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Sukai Xu, Yiming Ni, Shanzhou Duan, Yongbing Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundWhether recurrent laryngeal nerve lymph node dissection (RLN-LND) improves survival in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) remains controversial. This systematic review and meta-analysis aimed to evaluate the prognostic significance of RLN-LND in ESCC patients undergoing radical esophagectomy.MethodsWe systematically searched Web of Science, PubMed, Cochrane Library, and Embase from inception to June 2026 for studies comparing overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS) between RLN-LND and non-RLN-LND groups in ESCC patients. Eligibility criteria were based on PICOS framework: population—ESCC patients undergoing radical esophagectomy; intervention—RLN-LND; comparison—non-RLN-LND; outcomes—OS and DFS; study design—randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and observational studies. Risk of bias was assessed using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS). Meta-analysis was performed using RevMan 5.4 and Stata 15.0. Publication bias was evaluated by funnel plots, Begg’s test, Egger’s test, and trim-and-fill method. Heterogeneity was assessed by Q-test and I2.ResultsSeven retrospective studies involving 4,570 patients (3,133 in RLN-LND group and 1,437 in non-RLN-LND group) were included. All studies were rated as high quality (NOS scores ≥7). Meta-analysis showed improved OS [hazard ratio (HR) =0.69, 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.57–0.84, P<0.001] and DFS (HR =0.65, 95% CI: 0.44–0.97, P=0.04) in the RLN-LND group versus the non-RLN-LND group. However, subgroup analysis showed no significant OS benefit in patients receiving neoadjuvant therapy plus surgery (HR =0.71, 95% CI: 0.37–1.36, P=0.30).ConclusionsBased on high-quality retrospective evidence, RLN-LND may be associated with improved OS and DFS in ESCC patients undergoing upfront surgery, but not in those receiving neoadjuvant therapy. These findings should be interpreted with caution due to the non-randomized nature of included studies, potential unadjusted confounding, and inherent limitations of retrospective designs. Prospective multicenter studies are warranted to confirm these results.Keywords: Disease-free survival (DFS), esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC), lymph node excision, prognosis, recurrent laryngeal nerve (RLN)

Highlight box

Key findings

• Our meta-analysis (7 studies, 4,570 patients) showed that recurrent laryngeal nerve lymph node dissection (RLN-LND) significantly improved overall survival in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) patients (hazard ratio =0.69, 95% confidence interval: 0.57–0.84, P<0.001), with no benefit in those receiving neoadjuvant therapy plus surgery.

What is known and what is new?

• RLN-LND extent remains controversial in ESCC surgery.

• This study provides high-level evidence confirming RLN-LND’s survival benefit and clarifying subgroup efficacy by neoadjuvant treatment status.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• RLN-LND is recommended for ESCC patients without neoadjuvant therapy; prospective validation is needed to refine clinical guidelines.

Introduction

Esophageal cancer (EC) is currently the 11th most common cancer worldwide and the 7th leading cause of cancer-related deaths, with significantly higher regional incidence rates in East Asia compared to other areas (1). Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) is the predominant histological type of EC in China, accounting for over 90% of cases (2). The high incidence and poor prognosis of ESCC contribute to a substantial disease burden of growing concern (3). To improve patient outcomes, various treatment modalities including surgical therapy, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and immunotherapy have been clinically applied. However, radical esophagectomy remains the primary approach for treating ESCC (4-7). Since tumor cells in ESCC primarily spread through the lymphatic system, lymphadenectomy plays a critical role in radical esophagectomy (8). Some scholars argue that more thorough lymphadenectomy may improve patient prognosis and local tumor control (9,10). Conversely, others suggest that extensive lymphadenectomy does not confer survival benefits (11,12). Nonetheless, most studies emphasize the importance of lymphadenectomy, with its extent depending on the tumor’s location, size, and depth of invasion (8). Thus, the optimal lymphadenectomy strategy and extent remain unresolved.

Early studies reported a high metastasis rate in recurrent laryngeal nerve lymph nodes (RLN-LNs) in ESCC (13,14), particularly in thoracic ESCC, where the metastasis rate is the highest (15,16). Traditionally, bilateral RLN-LNs are considered the most critical sites for lymph node dissection in thoracic ESCC, even in cases of superficial tumors (17). Zhang et al. found that unilateral or bilateral RLN-LN dissection (RLN-LND) could improve survival outcomes in ESCC patients (18). However, Pai et al. concluded that RLN-LND only benefited ESCC patients with positive RLN-LNs (19). Although RLN-LND plays a positive role in predicting cervical lymph node involvement and accurate staging (20), extensive RLN-LND increases the risk of postoperative complications, especially recurrent laryngeal nerve (RLN) palsy (21). Additionally, during surgery, due to the proximity to the trachea and major blood vessels and the narrow operative space, complete exposure and dissection of RLN-LNs remain challenging (22). Recent advances in surgical techniques, such as the visceral sheath targeted approach (VISTA) for robot-assisted minimally invasive esophagectomy, have demonstrated the feasibility of achieving zero RLN palsy while maintaining oncological completeness (23), highlighting the ongoing efforts to improve the safety profile of RLN-LND. Thus, whether RLN-LND, compared with no dissection, improves prognosis in ESCC patients remains controversial. This controversy persists because the lymphadenectomy strategy for ESCC is not yet standardized, the survival benefit of RLN-LND may be limited to specific patient subgroups, and the procedure carries significant risks of complications. Moreover, prospective randomized controlled trials (RCTs) comparing RLN-LND with no dissection are ethically difficult given the current clinical practice. In the absence of RCTs, meta-analysis of high-quality retrospective studies represents the most appropriate method to synthesize the available evidence and help clarify whether RLN-LND improves prognosis in ESCC patients. Therefore, we conducted this systematic review and meta-analysis to evaluate the prognostic significance of RLN-LND in ESCC patients undergoing radical esophagectomy. We present this article in accordance with the PRISMA reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2026-1216/rc) (24).

Search strategy

Literature search and screening were independently conducted by two researchers. Databases including Web of Science, PubMed, Cochrane Library, and Embase were systematically searched from their inception to June 2026 for studies comparing RLN-LND in ESCC patients undergoing radical esophagectomy. Search terms combined medical subject headings and free-text keywords, tailored to each database. The search terms included “Esophageal Neoplasms”, “Esophageal Squamous Cell Carcinoma”, “Recurrent Laryngeal Nerve Lymph Node”, etc. To ensure the integrity of the search, we also reviewed reference lists from reviews and meta-analyses on research topics closely related to the study. The search strategy is presented in Table S1.

Literature screening and data extraction

Data collection was conducted according to predefined literature search strategies and inclusion criteria. After independent deduplication by two researchers, multi-level screening was performed through titles, abstracts, and full texts. The initial screening phase employed a broad approach to maximize literature retrieval. Disagreements during screening were resolved through discussion with a third researcher. Two researchers independently extracted data from the final selected studies, including but not limited to author information, publication year, country/region, study type, extent of RLN-LND, participant age distribution, tumor location, pathological tumor (T)-stage, and other key parameters. All extracted data were verified by a third researcher.

Literature quality assessment

Although the eligibility criteria allowed for both RCTs and observational studies, all ultimately included studies were retrospective observational studies; no RCTs meeting our inclusion criteria were identified. Therefore, the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS) (25) was used for quality evaluation, which includes three domains: cohort selection, comparability between groups and outcome/exposure assessment. Each item meeting the criteria received 1 point. Studies with a total score ≥6 were classified as high quality. Two researchers independently assessed the quality and cross-validated results; disagreements were resolved through discussion with a third researcher.

Statistical analysis

Meta-analysis was performed using RevMan5.4 (Cochrane Collaboration) and Stata15.0. Publication bias was evaluated via funnel plots, supplemented by Begg’s test, Egger’s test, and trim-and-fill method. Sensitivity analysis was conducted by sequentially excluding individual studies to assess result robustness. HRs and 95% CIs were selected as effect measures. We preferentially extracted adjusted HRs from multivariable models when available; univariable HRs were only used when adjusted HRs were not reported. For studies reporting both, the most fully adjusted HR was selected. Heterogeneity was evaluated using the Q statistic and I2 index: a random-effects model was applied if I2>50%; otherwise, a fixed-effects model was used. For studies providing survival curves without HRs, data were extracted using Engauge Digitizer software (26) and processed via the Excel tool described by Tierney et al. (27). P<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

A total of 561 relevant studies were retrieved in this research. After removing duplicate titles, excluding studies that did not meet inclusion criteria, and eliminating studies with unavailable data, 7 studies were finally included (18,19,28-32), all of which were retrospective. These studies involved 4,570 patients in total: 3,133 in the RLN-LND group and 1,437 in the non-RLN-LND group. The literature screening process is shown in Figure 1. Results of meta-analysis

Impact of RLN-LND on OS

A total of 7 studies (18,19,28-32) were included, with significant heterogeneity (I2=56.1%, P=0.03). A random-effects model was used for meta-analysis. Results showed a statistically significant difference between the RLN-LND and Non-RLN-LND groups (HR =0.69, 95% CI: 0.57–0.84, P<0.001). Subgroup analysis based on neoadjuvant therapy: neoadjuvant therapy plus surgery group: 3 studies (19,30,32) were included, with significant heterogeneity (I2=55.8%, P=0.10). A random-effects model meta-analysis showed no significant difference between two groups (HR =0.71, 95% CI: 0.37–1.36, P=0.30). Upfront surgery group: 6 studies (18,19,28,29,31,32) were included, with significant heterogeneity (I2=57.4%, P=0.04). A random-effects model meta-analysis showed a statistically significant difference between the two groups (HR =0.68, 95% CI: 0.56–0.84, P<0.001) (Table 3).

Impact of RLN-LND on DFS

A total of 4 studies (18,19,28,31) were included, with significant heterogeneity (I2=93%, P<0.001). A random-effects model meta-analysis showed a statistically significant difference between the RLN-LND and non-RLN-LND groups (HR =0.65, 95% CI: 0.44–0.97, P=0.04). Subgroup analysis based on neoadjuvant therapy: Neoadjuvant therapy plus surgery group: 1 study (19) was included, with no significant heterogeneity (I2=0%, P<0.001). A fixed-effects model meta-analysis showed no significant difference between the RLN-LND and Non-RLN-LND groups (HR =0.88, 95% CI: 0.51–1.52, P=0.65). Upfront surgery group: 4 studies (18,19,28,31) were included, with significant heterogeneity (I2=72%, P=0.01). A random-effects model meta-analysis showed a statistically significant difference between the RLN-LND and non-RLN-LND groups (HR =0.66, 95% CI: 0.46–0.95, P=0.03) (Table 3).

Publication bias evaluation

This study evaluated publication bias using multi-dimensional methods: firstly, a funnel plot was used for visual detection (Figure 2A,2B); secondly, quantitative analysis was conducted using Begg’s test (OS: P=0.37; DFS: P>0.99) and Egger’s test (OS: P=0.45; DFS: P=0.03). To verify the robustness of the analysis results, the trim and fill method was also applied (Figure 2C,2D). Based on the above multi-dimensional analysis, a certain degree of publication bias tendency was observed in the DFS study endpoint, while no significant bias characteristics were presented in the OS study endpoint analysis.

Heterogeneity analysis and sensitivity analysis

The heterogeneity was analyzed using the Galibraith star diagram. We found that there was some heterogeneity in studies with the research endpoints of OS and DFS, as shown in Figure 3A,3B. Through further sensitivity analysis, the results showed that in studies with OS as the research endpoint, after excluding any one study, the remaining study results were still statistically significant, consistent with the original combined results, and the result stability was good. However, in studies with DFS as the research endpoint, after excluding the studies of Zhang et al. (18), Tan et al. (28), and Park et al. (31), the remaining study results did not have statistical significance, inconsistent with the original combined results, indicating unstable results. Therefore, the meta-analysis was abandoned, and only statistical description was conducted, as shown in Figure 4A,4B.

Discussion

Currently, neoadjuvant therapy combined with surgical treatment for primary tumors and potential metastatic lymph nodes has become the most effective treatment method for locally advanced EC (17). Although neoadjuvant radiotherapy and chemotherapy have been widely used and have been proven to improve the survival rate of locally advanced EC, the OS rate of EC remains very low (33,34). One of the important adverse prognostic factors is lymph node metastasis and postoperative lymph node recurrence (35-37). Therefore, an appropriate lymph node dissection method can not only clarify the stage but also improve the prognosis of EC patients. A prospective multicenter study reported that even in EC patients with clinical N0 stage, the metastasis rate of bilateral RLN-LNs was the highest (38). Although there is no universally recognized most suitable lymph node dissection method and degree, dissection of RLN-LNs still has important clinical value.

This meta-analysis included a total of 7 studies. The results showed that the RLN-LND group had a significant advantage over the non-RLN-LND group in prolonging OS (HR =0.69, 95% CI: 0.57–0.84, P<0.001), and the OS benefit was statistically significant, which was consistent with the conclusion of a retrospective study in our center (32), that is, RLN-LND can improve the OS of ESCC patients. In the subgroup analysis based on whether patients received neoadjuvant treatment, it was shown that RLN-LND did not improve the OS of ESCC patients who received neoadjuvant combined with surgical treatment, which was consistent with the results of Chao et al. (30). The development of prediction models for RLN lymph node status after neoadjuvant therapy (39) may facilitate patient selection for RLN-LND, potentially explaining why RLN-LND did not demonstrate significant survival benefit in our neoadjuvant therapy subgroup—patients with good response may have minimal residual RLN nodal disease, reducing the incremental value of extensive dissection.

It is undeniable that this systematic review and meta-analysis has certain limitations: since the included literature were all retrospective studies, there may be publication bias. Although all included studies had NOS scores of ≥7 points, the inherent limitations of retrospective designs may affect the validity of this meta-analysis. Notably, prospective RCTs comparing RLN-LND vs. no dissection are ethically unfeasible given the clinical consensus on RLN-LND’s benefit for ESCC patients, making high-quality retrospective studies the main evidence base. It is important to acknowledge that RLN-LND practice remains heterogeneous across institutions. The choice between unilateral and bilateral RLN-LND, as well as the indications for dissection, are not yet standardized. This heterogeneity may introduce variability in our pooled estimates and limit the generalizability of our findings. However, by focusing on the comparison between bilateral RLN-LND and no dissection, and by conducting subgroup analyses based on the extent of dissection, we have attempted to mitigate this limitation. Future prospective studies should aim to establish standardized criteria for RLN-LND indications and extent to generate more comparable evidence. We acknowledge that the clinical value of RLN-LND cannot be judged by survival outcomes alone. RLN palsy and other postoperative complications are clinically important risks associated with this procedure. However, a formal meta-analysis of adverse events was not feasible in this study because the included studies did not report complications in a standardized manner, the definitions and types of complications varied considerably across studies, and the data were often presented in formats unsuitable for pooling. Future studies should prioritize standardized reporting of safety endpoints to enable a more comprehensive assessment of the risk-benefit profile of RLN-LND. Recent evidence suggesting that RLN-LND may be safely omitted in selected patients with lower thoracic ESCC and limited esophageal invasion (40) further underscores the importance of developing precise indication criteria. Due to the poor stability of studies based on DFS as the research endpoint, only statistical descriptions were made. For studies that did not provide HR and 95% CI, the values were extracted using relevant tools, which may have certain errors. Due to the lack of statistics on the postoperative adjuvant treatment situation of ESCC patients in the literature (such as chemotherapy, immunotherapy), it was difficult to effectively control or stratify these treatment variables in the study, which may cause potential interference to the accuracy of the research conclusion. In addition, the case data included in the analysis mainly came from the East Asian region. This aligns with ESCC’s marked geographic incidence variation—East Asia, a high-incidence region, boasts larger samples and more mature RLN-LND practices, yielding more eligible studies. Amid potential regional bias, future multicenter international studies are needed to confirm our findings. The combined results need to be treated with caution. In the future, large-sample, multi-regional RCTs should be conducted to verify the reliability of this meta-analysis.

Conclusions

In summary, among ESCC patients who underwent esophagectomy, although the DFS benefits brought by RLN-LND were not stable, it could significantly bring about benefits in OS. In some specific groups of patients, RLN-LND has more advantages.

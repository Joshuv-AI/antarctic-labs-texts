Density properties of orbits for a hypercyclic operator on a Banach space
Jian Li, Xinsheng Wang, Jianjie Zhao

Abstract
. We study density properties of orbits for a hypercyclic operator T T on a separable Banach space X X , and show that exactly one of the following four cases holds: (1) every vector in X X is asymptotic to zero with density one; (2) generic vectors in X X are distributionally irregular of type 1 1 ; (3) generic vectors in X X are distributionally irregular of type 2 ⁤ 1 2 2\frac{1}{2} and no hypercyclic vector is distributionally irregular of type 1 1 ; (4) every hypercyclic vector in X X is divergent to infinity with density one. We also present some examples concerned with weighted backward shifts on ℓ p \ell^{p} to show that all the above four cases can occur. Furthermore, we show that similar results hold for C 0 C_{0} -semigroups.

1.
Introduction

Dynamical systems is a branch of mathematics concerned with the asymptotic behavior of systems.
The study of orbits generated by the system plays an important role for exploring the complexity of the asymptotic behavior.
Plenty of results related to properties of orbits have been obtained for various systems under different settings.
For the dynamical system generated by a linear operator on some linear space with certain structure, it seems that there is no complexity due to the linearity.
However, even for such a system, there can be abundant complex phenomena, meaning that it is significant and interesting to consider linear dynamics.

One of the central concepts in the study of linear dynamics is the so-called hypercyclicity, which is closely related to numerous important properties of linear dynamics.
Let
X
be a Banach space and
T\colon X\to X
be a bounded linear operator.
A vector
x\in X
is said to be
hypercyclic

if the orbit of
x
,
\{T^{n}x\colon n\geq 0\}
, is dense in
X
, and the operator
T
is said to be
hypercyclic
if there exists some hypercyclic vector in
X
.
The set of all hypercyclic vectors of
T
is denoted by
HC(T)
. Note that the concept of hypercyclicity is in fact the so-called transitivity for general topological dynamical systems.
It is important to point out that linear structure can be made full use of for linear dynamics, compared to general dynamical systems, which means that we can consider the properties of linear dynamics from some new points of view.
For a complete discussion on the dynamics of operators, the reader can refer to
[
BM2009
]
and
[
GP2011
]
and references therein.

As mentioned above, hypercyclic operators play a significant role in linear dynamics, making it useful and interesting to study their properties from various perspectives.
In
[
L2024
]
the first named author of this paper studied the behaviors of orbits in the mean sense of a hypercyclic operator on a Banach space.
In this paper, another aspect of the hypercyclic operator is considered, and we will study the density properties of orbits for such operators.

First of all, let us recall some concepts related to the topic of this paper.
For a subset
A
of
\mathbb{N}
, the
upper density
and
lower density
of
A
are defined by

\udens(A)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\#(A\cap[1,n])}{n},

and

\ldens(A)=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\#(A\cap[1,n])}{n},

respectively, where
\#(\cdot)
denotes the number of elements of a finite set.
If
\udens(A)=\ldens(A)
, then we say that the
density
of
A
exists, and denote it by
\dens(A)
.

Let
X
be a Banach space with the norm
\|\cdot\|
and
T\colon X\to X
be a bounded linear operator from
X
to itself.
This paper focuses on the following properties of hypercyclic operators in terms of density.
Following
[
BBMP2011
]
, a vector
x\in X
is said to be
distributionally irregular of type
1
for
T
if there exist two subsets
A
and
B
of
\mathbb{N}
such that
\udens(A)=\udens(B)=1
,
\lim\limits_{A\ni n\to\infty}\|T^{n}x\|=0
and
\lim\limits_{B\ni n\to\infty}\|T^{n}x\|=\infty
.
Inspired by the concept of distributional chaos of type
2\frac{1}{2}
given in
[
BBPW2018
]
, we introduce the concept of distributionally irregular of type
2\frac{1}{2}
.
A vector
x\in X
is said to be
distributionally irregular of type
2\frac{1}{2}
for
T
if there exist two subsets
A
and
B
of
\mathbb{N}
such that

\udens(A)\udens(B)>0
,
\lim\limits_{A\ni n\to\infty}\|T^{n}x\|=0
and
\lim\limits_{B\ni n\to\infty}\|T^{n}x\|=\infty
.
A vector
x\in X
is said to be
asymptotic to zero with density one
if there exists a subset
A
of
\mathbb{N}
with
\dens(A)=1
such that
\lim\limits_{A\ni n\to\infty}\|T^{n}x\|=0
,
and
divergent to infinity with density one

if there exists a subset
B
of
\mathbb{N}
with
\dens(B)=1
such that
\lim\limits_{B\ni n\to\infty}\|T^{n}x\|=\infty
.

In addition, we say that a property P on vectors in
X
is
generic
if the collection of vectors which satisfy the property P contains a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
X
.
The following theorem is our main result in this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
T
is a hypercyclic operator on a separable Banach space
X
, then exactly one of the following four assertions holds:

(1)

every vector in
X
is asymptotic to zero with density one;

(2)

generic vectors in
X
are distributionally irregular of type
1
;

(3)

generic vectors in
X
are distributionally irregular of type
2\frac{1}{2}
, and no hypercyclic vector is distributionally irregular of type
1
;

(4)

every hypercyclic vector in
X
is divergent to infinity with density one.

The paper is organized as follows. Theorem
1.1
will be proved in Section 2.
In Section 3, we present some examples concerned with weighted backward shifts on
\ell^{p}
to show that all four cases in Theorem
1.1
can occur.
In Section 4, similar results for
C_{0}
-semigroups will be discussed.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

The aim of this section is to prove Theorem
1.1
.
We begin with the following standard result on density; therefore, its proof is omitted.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(a_{n})_{n}
be a sequence in
\mathbb{R}
. Then

(1)

there exists a subset
A
of
\mathbb{N}
with

\udens(A)=1
such that
\lim_{A\ni n\to\infty}|a_{n}|=0

if and only if for every
\varepsilon>0
,

\udens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}:|a_{n}|<\varepsilon\})=1
;

(2)

there exists a subset
B
of
\mathbb{N}
with

\dens(B)=1
such that
\lim_{B\ni n\to\infty}|a_{n}|=0

if and only if for every
\varepsilon>0
,

\dens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}:|a_{n}|<\varepsilon\})=1
.

By a
topological dynamical system
, we mean a pair
(X,T)
,
where
X
is a metric space with the metric
d
and
T\colon X\to X
is a continuous map.
Following
[
F1951
]
and
[
LTY2015
]
,
we say that a topological dynamical system
(X,T)

is
mean-L-stable

if for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a
\delta>0

such that for any
x,y\in X
with
d(x,y)<\delta
, one has

\udens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon d(T^{n}x,T^{n}y)\geq\varepsilon\})<\varepsilon,

and
mean-L-unstable

if there exists a
\delta>0

such that for any non-empty open subset
U
of
X
there exist
x,y\in U
such that

\udens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon d(T^{n}x,T^{n}y)>\delta\})\geq\delta.

We now state the following dichotomy theorem.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
JL2022
]
*Theorem 5.13)
.

Let
T
be a bounded linear operator on a Banach space
X
. Then
T
is either mean-L-stable or mean-L-unstable.

We have an equivalent characterization concerned with mean-L-stability in terms of density under some conditions, the following proposition plays a key role in such a characterization.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
T
be a bounded linear operator on a Banach space
X
.
Assume that
T
is mean-L-stable.
Then we have the following:

(1)

For a vector
x\in X
, if
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|T^{n}x\|=0
,
then
x
is asymptotic to zero with density one.

(2)

The collection of vectors in
X
which are asymptotic to zero with density one is a closed subspace of
X
.

Proof.

(1)

Let
x\in X
be a vector satisfying
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|T^{n}x\|=0
. Fix any
\varepsilon>0
.
Note that
T
is mean-L-stable, which means for any
0<\varepsilon^{\prime}<\varepsilon
there exists
\delta>0
such that for any
x
,
y\in X
with
\|x-y\|<\delta
, we have

\udens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|T^{n}x-T^{n}y\|\geq\varepsilon^{\prime}\})<\varepsilon^{\prime}.

By the assumption on
x
, there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\|T^{k}x\|<\delta
. Then for
T^{k}x
and
\mathbf{0}
, we know that

\udens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|T^{n+k}x\|\geq\varepsilon^{\prime}\})<\varepsilon^{\prime},

implying that

\ldens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|T^{n+k}x\|<\varepsilon^{\prime}\})\geq 1-\varepsilon^{\prime}.

Noticing that
\varepsilon^{\prime}<\varepsilon
we have

\displaystyle\ldens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|T^{n}x\|<\varepsilon\})

\displaystyle\geq\ldens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|T^{n}x\|<\varepsilon^{\prime}\})

\displaystyle\geq\ldens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|T^{n+k}x\|<\varepsilon^{\prime}\})>1-\varepsilon^{\prime}.

Letting
\varepsilon^{\prime}\to 0
we obtain

\ldens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|T^{n}x\|<\varepsilon\})\geq 1,

meaning that

\dens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|T^{n}x\|<\varepsilon\})=1.

Noticing the arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
and using Lemma
2.1
(
2
) we know that there exists a subset
A
of
\mathbb{N}
with
\dens(A)=1
such that
\lim_{A\ni n\to\infty}\|T^{n}x\|=0
.
Then
x
is asymptotic to zero with density one.

(2)

Let
X_{0}
be the collection of vectors in
X
which are asymptotic to zero with density one.
For any
x,y\in X_{0}
, there exist two subsets
A_{x},A_{y}\subset\mathbb{N}
with
\dens(A_{x})=1=\dens(A_{y})
such that

\lim_{A_{x}\ni n\to\infty}\|T^{n}x\|=0\ \text{and}\ \lim_{A_{y}\ni n\to\infty}\|T^{n}y\|=0.

Let
A=A_{x}\cap A_{y}
. Since
\dens(A_{x})=\dens(A_{y})=1
, it is easy to see that
\dens(A)=1
.
On the other hand, clearly we have

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{A\ni n\to\infty}\|T^{n}(x+y)\|\leq\lim_{A\ni n\to\infty}\left(\|T^{n}x\|+\|T^{n}y)\|\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{A\ni n\to\infty}\|T^{n}x\|+\lim_{A\ni n\to\infty}\|T^{n}y\|=0,

which means that
x+y\in X_{0}
. It is easy to check that
rx\in X_{0}
for any
r\in\mathbb{K}
, where
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}\text{ or }\mathbb{C}
.
Now we have proved that
X_{0}
is a subspace. In the following we will prove that
X_{0}
is closed. Let
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
X_{0}
with
\lim_{k\to\infty}x_{k}=x\in X
. Fix any
\varepsilon>0
. For any
0<\varepsilon^{\prime}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
, noticing that
T
is mean-L-stable, there exists
\delta>0
such that for any
y
,
z\in X
with
\|y-z\|<\delta
,

\udens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|T^{n}(y-z)\|\geq\varepsilon^{\prime}\})<\varepsilon^{\prime},

that is

\ldens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|T^{n}(y-z)\|<\varepsilon^{\prime}\})\geq 1-\varepsilon^{\prime}.

Choose
m>0
such that
\|x_{m}-x\|<\delta
. Then we have

\displaystyle\ldens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|T^{n}(x_{m}-x)\|<\varepsilon^{\prime}\})\geq 1-\varepsilon^{\prime}.

Since
x_{m}\in X_{0}
, we know that
\dens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|T^{n}x_{m}\|<\varepsilon^{\prime}\})=1
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\ldens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|T^{n}x\|<\varepsilon)\}

\displaystyle\geq\ldens\bigl(\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\|T^{n}x_{m}\|<\varepsilon^{\prime}\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\cap\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\|T^{n}x_{m}-T^{n}x\|<\varepsilon^{\prime}\}\bigr)

\displaystyle\geq 1-\varepsilon^{\prime}.

Letting
\varepsilon^{\prime}\to 0
, we obtain that

\ldens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\|T^{n}x\|<\varepsilon)\}=1.

This shows that
x\in X_{0}
and then
X_{0}
is closed. ∎

Now we can give the following characterization of mean-L-stability for hypercyclic operators.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
T\colon X\to X
be a hypercyclic operator on a separable Banach space
X
.
Then
T
is mean-L-stable if and only if every vector in
X
is asymptotic to zero with density one.

Proof.

“
\Rightarrow
"
For every hypercyclic vector
x\in X
,
as
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|T^{n}x\|=0
,
by Proposition
2.3
(1),

x
is asymptotic to zero with density one.
By the fact that
HC(T)
is dense in
X

and Proposition
2.3
(2),
every vector in
X
is asymptotic to zero with density one.

“
\Leftarrow
” By the assumption and Lemma
2.1
(
2
), for any
z\in X
and any
\varepsilon>0
we have

\dens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|T^{n}z\|<\varepsilon\})=1.

Then for any
x
,
y\in X
and any
\varepsilon>0
we have

\displaystyle\dens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|T^{n}(x-y)\|<\varepsilon\})=1.

The arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
shows that
T
is mean-L-stable.
∎

A characterization of mean-L-unstability can also be formulated, involving the so-called distributional sensitivity.
We say that a topological dynamical system

(X,T)
is
distributionally sensitive
if there exists
\delta>0
such that
for any
x\in X
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
y\in X
with
d(x,y)<\varepsilon
such that

\udens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon d(T^{n}x,T^{n}y)>\delta\})=1.

In addition, for a bounded linear operator
T
on a Banach space
X
, we say that a vector
x\in X
is
distributionally unbounded
if there exists a subset
B
of
\mathbb{N}
with
\udens(B)=1
and
\lim_{B\ni n\to\infty}\|T^{n}x\|=\infty
.
The following result follows from
[
JL2022
]
*Proposition 5.33.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
T
be a bounded linear operator on a Banach space
X
.
If
T
is distributionally sensitive, then generic vectors in
X
are distributionally unbounded.

The following result is essentially contained in
[
BBMP2013
]
*Proposition 8, see
[
JL2022
]
*Theorem 5.41 for the version here, which establishes a characterization of mean-L-unstability.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
T
be a bounded linear operator on a Banach space
X
.
Then
T
is mean-L-unstable if and only if it is distributionally sensitive.

Now we can give the following dichotomy for hypercyclic operators.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
T
be a hypercyclic operator on a separable Banach space
X
.
Then either every vector in
X
is asymptotic to zero with density one, or generic vectors in
X
are distributionally unbounded.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.2
we know that
T
is either mean-L-stable or mean-L-unstable.
On the one hand, if
T
is mean-L-stable, note that
T
is hypercyclic, by Proposition
2.4
we know that every vector in
X
is asymptotic to zero with density one.
On the other hand, if
T
is mean-L-unstable, by Theorem
2.6
we know that
T
is distributionally sensitive. Then by Proposition
2.5
we obtain that generic vectors in
X
are distributionally unbounded.
∎

Let
T
be a hypercyclic operator on a Banach space
X
.
For the proof of our main result (Theorem
1.1
), we also need a parameter
c(T)\in[0,1]
associated with
T
, which was firstly introduced by Grivaux and Matheron in
[
GM2014
]
.
For each
R>0
, define

c_{R}(T)=\sup_{x\in HC(T)}\udens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|T^{n}x\|<R\}),

and

c(T)=\sup_{R>0}c_{R}(T).

Remark 2.8
.

It is shown in
[
GM2014
]
*Remark 4.6
that there exists a dense
G_{\delta}
subset
X_{0}
of

such that for any

and

,

\udens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|T^{n}x\|<R\})=c(T)
.
Since

Concerned with

, we have the following proposition, which is an important tool in the proof of Theorem

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
T
be a hypercyclic operator on a separable Banach space
X
.
Then

c(T)=\sup\{\lambda\in[0,1]\colon\exists x\in HC(T)\,\&\,R>0\text{ s.t. }\udens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|T^{n}x\|<R\})\geq\lambda\}

and for every hypercyclic vector

and

,

\ldens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|T^{n}y\|\geq R\})\geq 1-c(T).

Proof.

Let

A=\{\lambda\in[0,1]\colon\exists x\in HC(T)\,\&\,R>0\text{ s.t. }\udens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|T^{n}x\|<R\})\geq\lambda\}.

For any
\lambda\in A
,
there exist
x\in HC(T)
and
R>0
such that

\udens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|T^{n}x\|<R\})\geq\lambda
.
Let
\lambda_{0}=\udens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|T^{n}x\|<R\})
.
It is clear that
\lambda\leq\lambda_{0}
and
\lambda_{0}\in A
.
By the definition of

and

, one has
\lambda_{0}\leq c_{R}(T)\leq c(T)
.
Then

is a upper bound for
A
.
For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
R_{0}>0
such that

such that
\udens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|T^{n}x\|<R\})\geq c(T)-\varepsilon
.
So

.
This implies that
c(T)=\sup A
.

Fix

and
R>0
. One has

\udens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|T^{n}y\|<R\})\in A

\udens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|T^{n}y\|<R\})\leq c(T).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\ldens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|T^{n}y\|\geq R\})

\displaystyle\geq 1-\udens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|T^{n}y\|<R\})

3.
Some examples

In this section, we will present some examples concerned with weighted backward shifts on
\ell^{p}
to show that all four cases in Theorem
1.1
can occur.

For a positive weight sequence
v=(v_{j})_{j\geq 1}
and
1\leq p<\infty
,
let

\ell^{p}(v,\mathbb{N})=\biggl\{(x_{j})_{j\geq 1}\colon\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|x_{j}|^{p}v_{j}<\infty\biggr\}

be the weighted
\ell^{p}
-space.
The norm on
\ell^{p}(v,\mathbb{N})
is defined as follows:

\|x\|=\biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|x_{j}|^{p}v_{j}\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad\forall x=(x_{j})_{j\geq 1}\in\ell^{p}(v,\mathbb{N}).

The
unilateral backward shift

B
on
\ell^{p}(v,\mathbb{N})
is defined as

(Bx)_{j}=x_{j+1},\quad\forall x=(x_{j})_{j\geq 1}\in\ell^{p}(v,\mathbb{N}),\ j\geq 1.

It is not hard to show that the map
B\colon\ell^{p}(v,\mathbb{N})\to\ell^{p}(v,\mathbb{N})
is well-defined (and, equivalently, continuous) if and only if
\sup_{j\geq 1}\frac{v_{j}}{v_{j+1}}<\infty
.

Remark 3.2

By

.

Now we consider the bilateral case.
For a positive weight sequence
v=(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
1\leq p<\infty
,
let

\ell^{p}(v,\mathbb{Z})=\biggl\{(x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\colon\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|x_{j}|^{p}v_{j}<\infty\biggr\},

be the weighted
\ell^{p}
-space.
The norm on
\ell^{p}(v,\mathbb{Z})
is defined as follows:

\|x\|=\biggl(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|x_{j}|^{p}v_{j}\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad\forall x=(x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{p}(v,\mathbb{Z}).

The
bilateral backward shift

B
on

is defined as

(Bx)_{j}=x_{j+1},\quad\forall x=(x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{p}(v,\mathbb{Z}),\ j\in\mathbb{Z}.

Similarly, the map
B\colon\ell^{p}(v,\mathbb{Z})\to\ell^{p}(v,\mathbb{Z})
is well-defined (and, equivalently, continuous) if and only if

.

, and every non-zero vector in

4.
Results for
C_{0}
-semigroups

The dynamics of
C_{0}
-semigroups were systematically studied for the first time in
[
DSW1997
]
. For further details, see
[
GP2011
]
*Chapter 7. In this section, we discuss results for
C_{0}
-semigroups that are analogous to those presented in the previous sections.

We say that a one-parameter family
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
of bounded linear operators on a Banach space
X

is a
C_{0}
-semigroup
if
T_{0}=I
,
T_{t+s}=T_{t}T_{s}
for all
t,s\geq 0
and
\lim_{t\to s}T_{t}x=T_{s}x
for all
x\in X
and
s\geq 0
.
It is easy to see that such a
C_{0}
-semigroup is
locally equicontinuous
, that is, for any
s>0
,

\sup_{t\in[0,s]}\|T_{t}\|<\infty.

Let
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a
C_{0}
-semigroup on a Banach space
X
and

x\in X
. The
orbit
of
x
under
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is defined by
orb(x,(T_{t})_{t\geq 0})=\{T_{t}x:t\geq 0\}
.
We say that
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is
hypercyclic
if there is some
x\in X
whose orbit under
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is dense in
X
. In such case,
x
is called a
hypercyclic vector
for
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.

The following result concerned with the equivalent characterizations of hypercyclicity for
C_{0}
-semigroups was proved in
[
CMP2007
]
, see
[
GP2011
]
*Theorem 7.26 for the version stated here.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a
C_{0}
-semigroup on a Banach space
X
and
x\in X
. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(1)

x
is hypercyclic for
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
;

(2)

x
is hypercyclic for
T_{s}
for some
s>0
;

(3)

x
is hypercyclic for
T_{s}
for every
s>0
.

Let
A\subset[0,\infty)
be a Lebesgue measurable set.
The
lower and upper densities
of
A
are defined as

\lDens(A)=\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{\mu(A\cap[0,t])}{t}\text{ and }\uDens(A)=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\mu(A\cap[0,t])}{t},

where
\mu
is the Lebesgue measure on
[0,\infty)
.
If
\lDens(A)=\uDens(A)
, then we say that the
density
of
A
exists, and denote it by
\Dens(A)
.

The following result on the relationship between “
\mathrm{Dens}
” and “
\mathrm{dens}
” is essential contained in
[
ABMP2013
]
*Lemma 2.4, see also
[
BBPW2018
]
*Lemma 3.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a
C_{0}
-semigroup on a Banach space
X
.
For each
s>0
, let
C_{s}=\sup_{t\in[0,s]}\|T_{t}\|
.
Then for every
x\in X
and
\varepsilon>0
,

(1)

\lDens(\{t\geq 0\colon\|T_{t}x\|<\varepsilon\})\leq\ldens(\{j\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|(T_{s})^{j}x\|<C_{s}\varepsilon\})
;

(2)

\ldens(\{j\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|(T_{s})^{j}x\|<\varepsilon\})\leq\lDens(\{t\geq 0\colon\|T_{t}x\|<C_{s}\varepsilon\})
;

(3)

\uDens(\{t\geq 0\colon\|T_{t}x\|<\varepsilon\})\leq\udens(\{j\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|(T_{s})^{j}x\|<C_{s}\varepsilon\})
;

(4)

\udens(\{j\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|(T_{s})^{j}x\|<\varepsilon\})\leq\uDens(\{t\geq 0\colon\|T_{t}x\|<C_{s}\varepsilon\})
.

Similar to that for a single operator, we can define mean-L-stability and mean-L-unstability for a
C_{0}
-semigroup as follows. Let
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a
C_{0}
-semigroup on a Banach space
X
.
We say that
X
is
mean-L-stable
for
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
if for every
\epsilon>0
there exists a
\delta>0
such that for any
x,y\in X
with
d(x,y)<\delta
, one has

\uDens(\{t\geq 0:\|T_{t}x-T_{t}y\|\geq\epsilon\})<\epsilon,

and
mean-L-unstable
for
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
if there exists a
\delta>0
such that for any non-empty
open subset
U
of
X
there exist
x,y\in U
such that

\uDens(\{t\geq 0:\|T_{t}x-T_{t}y\|>\delta\})\geq\delta.

The following proposition gives equivalent characterizations for mean-L-stability (resp. mean-L-unstability).

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a
C_{0}
-semigroup on a Banach space
X
. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(1)

the
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is mean-L-stable (reps. mean-L-unstable);

(2)

the operator
T_{s}
is mean-L-stable (reps. mean-L-unstable) for some
s>0
;

(3)

the operator
T_{s}
is mean-L-stable (reps. mean-L-unstable) for every
s>0
.

Proof.

(\ref{every T_s})\Rightarrow(\ref{some T_s})
: It is clear.

(\ref{(T_t)})\Rightarrow(\ref{every T_s})
: For every
s>0
, and let
C_{s}=\sup_{t\in[0,s]}\|T_{t}\|
. Since
(T_{t})_{t>0}
is mean-L-stable, then for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a
\delta>0
, such that for any
x,y\in X
with
d(x,y)<\delta
,

\uDens(\{t\geq 0:\|T_{t}x-T_{t}y\|\geq\tfrac{\epsilon}{C_{s}}\})<\epsilon,

then by Lemma
4.2
, one has

\udens(\{j\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|(T_{s})^{j}x-(T_{s})^{j}y\|\geq\varepsilon\})\leq\uDens(\{t\geq 0\colon\|T_{t}x-T_{t}y\|\geq\tfrac{\epsilon}{C_{s}}\})<\epsilon.

This shows that
T_{s}
is mean-L-stable.

(\ref{some T_s})\Rightarrow(\ref{(T_t)})
:
We may assume that
T_{s}
is mean-L-stable for some
s>0
.
Put
C_{s}=\sup_{t\in[0,s]}\|T_{s}\|
.
Then for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a
\delta>0
such that for any
x,y\in X
with
d(x,y)<\delta
,

\udens(\{j\in\mathbb{N}:\|(T_{s})^{j}x-(T_{s})^{j}y\|\geq\tfrac{\epsilon}{C_{s}}\})<\epsilon,

thus by Lemma
4.2
, one has

\uDens(\{t\geq 0:\|T_{t}x-T_{t}y\|\geq\epsilon\})\leq\udens(\{j\in\mathbb{N}:\|(T_{s})^{j}x-(T_{s})^{j}y\|\geq\tfrac{\epsilon}{C_{s}}\})<\epsilon.

This shows that
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is mean-L-stable.

By a similar proof, Proposition
4.3
for mean-L-unstable case holds.
∎

In order to obtain a parallel result to Theorem
1.1
, a similar parameter need to be introduced as follows. If
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a hypercyclic
C_{0}
-semigroup on a Banach space
X
,
for each
R>0
, define

c_{R}((T_{t})_{t\geq 0})=\sup_{x\in HC((T_{t})_{t\geq 0})}\uDens(\{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\colon\|T_{t}x\|<R\}),

and

c((T_{t})_{t\geq 0})=\sup_{R>0}c_{R}((T_{t})_{t\geq 0}).

The following proposition plays a similar role in the proof of Theorem
4.5
as that Proposition
2.9
in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, and can be proved in a similar way as Proposition
4.3
, hence we omit the proof.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a hypercyclic
C_{0}
-semigroup on a Banach space
X
.
Then
c((T_{t})_{t\geq 0})=c(T_{s})
for every
s>0
.

We also need to introduce some concepts in terms of density for
C_{0}
-semigroups, as we did in Section 1 for a single operator.
Let
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a
C_{0}
-semigroup on a Banach space
X
.
A vector
x\in X
is said to be
distributionally irregular of type
1
for
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
if there exist two measurable subsets
A
and
B
of
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

such that
\uDens(A)=\uDens(B)=1
,
\lim\limits_{A\ni t\to\infty}\|T_{t}x\|=0
and
\lim\limits_{B\ni t\to\infty}\|T_{t}x\|=\infty
.
A vector
x\in X
is said to be
distributionally irregular of type
2\frac{1}{2}
for
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}

if there exist two measurable subsets
A
and
B
of
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that

\uDens(A)\uDens(B)>0
,
\lim\limits_{A\ni t\to\infty}\|T_{t}x\|=0
and
\lim\limits_{B\ni t\to\infty}\|T_{t}x\|=\infty
.
A vector
x\in X
is said to be
asymptotic to zero with density one
if there exists a measurable subset
A
of
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
with
\Dens(A)=1
such that
\lim\limits_{A\ni t\to\infty}\|T_{t}x\|=0
,
and
divergent to infinity with density one

if there exists a measurable subset
A
of
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
with
\Dens(A)=1
such that
\lim\limits_{A\ni t\to\infty}\|T_{t}x\|=\infty
.

Now we are ready to state our main result for the case of
C_{0}
-semigroups, whose proof is similar to that of Theorem
1.1
, and we omit it.

Theorem 4.5
.

If
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a hypercyclic
C_{0}
-semigroup on a Banach space
X
, then exactly one of the following four assertions holds:

(1)

every vector in
X
is asymptotic to zero with density one;

(2)

generic vectors in
X
are distributionally irregular of type
1
;

(3)

generic vectors in
X
are distributionally irregular of type
2\frac{1}{2}
, and no hypercyclic vector is distributionally irregular of type
1
;

(4)

every hypercyclic vector in
X
is divergent to infinity with density one.

There are some examples in
[
ABMP2013
]
to show that
the case (2) in Theorem
4.5
can occur. In the following, we will give more examples to show that the other three cases in Theorem
4.5
can occur.

4.1.
Unilateral translation semigroup

By an
admissible weight function
on
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, we mean a measurable function

\rho\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying the following two conditions:

(1)

\rho(t)>0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
;

(2)

there exist constants
M\geq 1
and
\omega>0
such that
\rho(s)\leq Me^{\omega t}\rho(t+s)
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq t}
and all
t>0
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
\rho
be an admissible weight function on
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.
We consider the separable Banach space of
p
-integrable functions in the Lebesgue sense as

L_{\rho}^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}):=\{f\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}\text{ measurable},\|f\|_{p}<\infty\},

where

\|f\|_{p}=\biggl(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}|f(s)|^{p}\rho(s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\biggr)^{1/p}.

The
translation semigroup
on
L_{\rho}^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
defined by
(T_{t}f)(x)=f(x+t)
,
t,x\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
,
is a well-defined
C_{0}
-semigroup by the definition of admissible weight.

Let
v=(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a positive weight sequence with
\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{v_{j}}{v_{j+1}}<\infty
.
Following
[
BP2012
]
, we construct an admissible weight function induced by
v
.
For each
t\in(n-1,n]
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
let
\rho_{v}(t)=v_{n}
, and
\rho_{v}(0)=v_{1}
.
Then it is easy to see that
\rho_{v}
is an admissible weight function.

For each
f\in L_{\rho}^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
define
x_{n}^{f}=\Bigl(\int_{[n-1,n]}|f(t)|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\Bigr)^{1/p}
.
Let
x^{f}=(x_{n}^{f})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
Then

\|x^{f}\|_{p}^{p}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|x^{f}_{n}|^{p}v_{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{[n-1,n]}|f(t)|^{p}\rho(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}|f(t)|^{p}\rho(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=\|f\|_{p}^{p}.

Combining Examples 4.4, 4.7 and 7.10 in
[
GP2011
]
,
we have the following result.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
v=(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a positive weight sequence with
\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{v_{j}}{v_{j+1}}<\infty
.
If
(\ell^{p}(v),B)
is hypercyclic (resp. mixing), then
(L_{\rho_{v}}^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}),(T_{t})_{t\geq 0})
is hypercyclic (resp. mixing).

We now have the following result, which, when combined with Example
3.1
from the previous section, shows that
case (1) in Theorem
4.5
can occur.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
v=(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a positive weight sequence with
\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{v_{j}}{v_{j+1}}<\infty
.
If
(\ell^{p}(v,\mathbb{N}),B)
is mixing and every vector is asymptotic to zero with density one,
then
(L_{\rho_{v}}^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}),(T_{t})_{t\geq 0})

is mixing and every vector is asymptotic to zero with density one.

4.2.
Bilateral translation semigroup

Similar to that in previous subsection, by an
admissible weight function
on
\mathbb{R}
, we mean a measurable function

\rho\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying the following two conditions:

(1)

\rho(t)>0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
;

(2)

there exist constants
M\geq 1
and
\omega>0
such that
\rho(s)\leq Me^{\omega t}\rho(t+s)
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
and all
t>0
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
\rho
be an admissible weight function on
\mathbb{R}
.
Just as that in Subsection 4.1, we consider the separable Banach space of
p
-integrable functions in the Lebesgue sense as

L_{\rho}^{p}(\mathbb{R}):=\{f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\text{ measurable},\|f\|_{p}<\infty\},

where

\|f\|_{p}=\biggl(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(s)|^{p}\rho(s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\biggr)^{1/p}.

Similarly, the
translation semigroup
on
L_{\rho}^{p}(\mathbb{R})
defined by
(T_{t}f)(x)=f(x+t)
,
t,x\in\mathbb{R}
,
is a well-defined
C_{0}
-semigroup by the definition of admissible weight.

Let
v=(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a positive weight sequence with
\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{v_{j}}{v_{j+1}}<\infty
.
For each
t\in(n-1,n]
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
\rho_{v}(t)=v_{n}
.
Clearly
\rho_{v}
is an admissible weight function.

For each
f\in L_{\rho}^{p}(\mathbb{R})
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
define
x_{n}^{f}=\Bigl(\int_{[n-1,n]}|f(t)|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\Bigr)^{1/p}
.
Let
x^{f}=(x_{n}^{f})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
.
Similar to the discussion in the previous subsection, we know that

\|x^{f}\|_{p}^{p}=\|f\|_{p}^{p}
.
Then similar to the above construction, we have the following:

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
v=(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a positive weight sequence with
\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{v_{j}}{v_{j+1}}<\infty
.
If
(\ell^{p}(v,\mathbb{Z}),B)
is hypercyclic and every non-zero vector is distributionally irregular of type 1,
then
(L_{\rho_{v}}^{p}(\mathbb{R}),(T_{t})_{t\geq 0})

is hypercyclic and every non-zero vector is distributionally irregular of type 1.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
v=(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a positive weight sequence with
\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{v_{j}}{v_{j+1}}<\infty
.
If
(\ell^{p}(v,\mathbb{Z}),B)
is hypercyclic and
for every non-zero vector
x\in\ell^{p}(v,\mathbb{Z})

there exists
\tau_{x}>0
such that

\ldens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|B^{n}x\|\geq\tau_{x}\})\geq\tfrac{3}{7},

and for any vector with only finite non-zero coordinates
x\in\ell^{p}(v,\mathbb{Z})
,

\udens(\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon\|B^{n}x\|\leq\varepsilon\})\geq\tfrac{4}{7},

then
(L_{\rho_{v}}^{p}(\mathbb{R}),(T_{t})_{t\geq 0})

is hypercyclic and for every non-zero function
f\in L_{\rho_{v}}^{p}(\mathbb{R})

there exists
\tau_{x}>0
such that

\lDens(\{t\geq 0\colon\|T_{t}f\|\geq\tau_{x}\})\geq\tfrac{3}{7},

for any function
f
with only bounded support

\uDens(\{t\geq 0\colon\|T_{t}f\|\leq\varepsilon\})\geq\tfrac{4}{7}.

By combining the above result with Example
3.5
from the previous section, we see that case (3) in Theorem
4.5
can occur.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
v=(v_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a positive weight sequence with
\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{v_{j}}{v_{j+1}}<\infty
.
If
(\ell^{p}(v,\mathbb{Z}),B)
is hypercyclic and every non-zero vector is divergent to infinity with density one,
then
(L_{\rho_{v}}^{p}(\mathbb{R}),(T_{t})_{t\geq 0})

is hypercyclic and every non-zero vector is divergent to infinity with density one.

By combining the above result with Example
3.7
from the previous section, we see that case (4) in Theorem
4.5
can occur.

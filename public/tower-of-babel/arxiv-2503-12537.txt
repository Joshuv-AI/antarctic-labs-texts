Hadamard states for decomposable Green-hyperbolic operators
Christopher J. Fewster

Abstract
Hadamard states were originally introduced for quantised Klein–Gordon fields and occupy a central position in the theory of quantum fields on curved spacetimes. Subsequently they have been developed for other linear theories, such as the Dirac, Proca and Maxwell fields, but the particular features of each require slightly different treatments. This paper gives a generalised definition of Hadamard states for linear bosonic and fermionic theories encompassing a range of theories that are described by Green-hyperbolic operators with ‘decomposable’ Pauli–Jordan propagators, including theories whose bicharacteristic curves are not necessarily determined by the spacetime metric. The new definition reduces to previous definitions for normally hyperbolic and Dirac-type operators. We develop the theory of Hadamard states in detail, showing that our definition propagates under the equation of motion, and is also stable under pullbacks and suitable pushforwards. There is an equivalent formulation in terms of Hilbert space valued distributions, and the generalised Hadamard condition on 2 2 -point functions constrains the singular behaviour of all n n -point functions. For locally covariant theories, the Hadamard states form a covariant state space. It is also shown how Hadamard states may be combined through tensor products or reduced by partial tracing while preserving the Hadamard property. As a particular application it is shown that state updates resulting from nonselective measurements preserve the Hadamard condition. The treatment we give was partly inspired by a recent work of Moretti, Murro and Volpe (MMV) [Ann. H. Poincaré 24 , 3055–3111 (2023)] on the neutral Proca field. Among the other applications, we revisit the neutral Proca field and prove a complete equivalence between the MMV definition of Hadamard states and an older work of Fewster and Pfenning [J. Math. Phys. 44 , 4480–4513 (2003)].

Introduction

A striking difference between quantum field theory (QFT) and quantum mechanics is that the same algebra of observables can have many inequivalent Hilbert space representations. An equally striking difference between QFT on curved spacetimes and QFT in Minkowski spacetime is that
there is generally no physically preferred state that can be used to identify a particularly natural representation – a long-standing folk-theorem that can be formulated as a rigorous no-go result
[
36
,
27
]
.

Lacking a single preferred state, attention is directed instead to the question of what class of states can be considered physically relevant. For linear Klein–Gordon fields in general globally hyperbolic spacetimes, the consensus answer is the class of Hadamard states, which were first identified in attempts to renormalise the stress-energy tensor
[
1
]
, with key properties established in
[
45
,
44
]

[

In outline, the two-point function

of a Hadamard state in four spacetime dimensions should take the form

W(x,y)=\frac{U(x,y)}{4\pi^{2}\sigma_{+}(x,y)}+V(x,y)\log(\sigma_{+}(x,y)/\ell^{2})+\text{smooth};

(1.1)

see
[
18
]
for general spacetime dimensions. Here,

and

are specific geometrically determined smooth functions,

is an arbitrary length scale and

is a particular distributional regularisation of the
signed squared geodesic separation between points

A subtle point, which was missed for many years, is whether there is any dependence on the choice of these neighbourhoods; this was addressed and resolved in
[

One sees from (

The purpose of this paper is to give a detailed, unified and generalised account of the Hadamard condition for a wide class of bosonic and fermionic theories described by suitable Green hyperbolic operators. To explain the motivation for this work, we first describe the existing theory of Hadamard states for more general fields before turning to Green-hyperbolic operators.

Green-hyperbolic operators were introduced by Bär
[
4
]
, who isolated the essential algebraic structure common to many partial differential operators arising in physics, and showed how much of the analytic theory can be extracted from the algebraic data. The idea is to study those partial differential operators that (together with their formal adjoints) admit advanced and retarded Green operators. Green-hyperbolic operators with suitable additional properties can be quantised as free quantum fields of Bose or Fermi type
[
5
]
as we will describe in Section
3
.

The advanced and retarded Green operators of a Green-hyperbolic operator are required to map test functions
f
(or, in general, test sections of a vector bundle) to solutions of the
inhomogeneous equation that are supported in the causal past or future of the support of
f
. This is certainly
satisfied by normally hyperbolic operators; however, Green-hyperbolicity is much broader, and encompasses the Dirac operator (which is first order), the Proca operator (which has second order terms beyond those coming from the metric) and many others. Further examples include
operators that are normally hyperbolic with respect to a ‘slow metric’, whose cones of future/past-directed causal vectors are subsets of the cone of spacetime future/past-directed causal vectors. Dually, the cone of causal covectors of the slow metric is a superset of the cone of causal covectors for the spacetime metric. This type of behaviour can also be seen in the theory of birefringence for electrodynamics in media (see
[
32
,
90
]
and references therein). Similarly, suitable convex combinations of normally hyperbolic operators with respect to different metrics are Green-hyperbolic but not normally hyperbolic
[
78
]
.
Meanwhile, Lorentz-violating quantum field theories also result in effective null cones differing from those of the spacetime geometry
[
3
]
. The idea can be pushed further – see, for example
[
28
]
for the existence of
some nonlocal operators that obey a modified version of Green-hyperbolicity.

Green-hyperbolic operators can be combined in various ways to construct other Green-hyperbolic operators
[
4
]
. For example, the direct sum of two Green-hyperbolic operators is Green-hyperbolic.
This simple fact makes the class of Green-hyperbolic operators a natural source of examples for the theory of measurement for QFT in flat and curved spacetimes introduced in
[
41
]
– see
[
42
]
for a short presentation and a survey of more general questions concerning measurement for QFT. In this framework, one measures a ‘system QFT’ by coupling it to another ‘probe QFT’.
The dynamics of the coupling can be described by a scattering map on the theory of the ‘uncoupled combination’ of system and probe. If both the system and probe are obtained from Green-hyperbolic operators
P
and
Q
, then their uncoupled combination is obtained from the direct sum
P\oplus Q
. The measurement framework, further developed in
[
9
,
31
]
, applies to general QFTs described within algebraic QFT. It provides a description of measurement schemes for system observables and also the updates to system states resulting from selective and nonselective measurements, while avoiding pathologies. Although QFTs based on Green-hyperbolic operators have provided a good source of examples, it has not yet been shown how the state updates interact with the theory of Hadamard states. One reason for this is that the theory of Hadamard states has not been developed for Green-hyperbolic operators, beyond the normally hyperbolic class and the examples related to that in various ways. Given that the theory of Hadamard states is tightly linked to aspects of the PDE theory, like the principal symbol of the field equation and the related propagation of singularities, while the theory of Green-hyperbolicity deliberately avoids these concepts, it is not immediately clear how to proceed. In fact, the characteristic set of the field equation cannot
have any bearing on the matter, because the Proca field operator turns out to be everywhere characteristic!

The present paper has two goals. First, we will present a generalised definition of what a Hadamard state should be for a class of Green-hyperbolic operators going well beyond the normally hyperbolic class, for both bosonic and fermionic statistics. This not only unifies the discussion of Hadamard states for standard linear theories, but also opens up the possibility of
describing more general measurement interactions involving combinations of theories in our class. Second, we illustrate the advantages of this new framework by presenting applications to measurement theory and the Proca field.

Our approach proceeds by
analogy with the standard theory of the Klein–Gordon field, in which the wavefront set of the difference of advanced and retarded Green operators (the Pauli–Jordan propagator) has a wavefront set contained in

(\mathcal{N}^{+}\times\mathcal{N}^{-})\cup(\mathcal{N}^{-}\times\mathcal{N}^{+})
. Extrapolating, we will focus on Green-hyperbolic operators
P
whose advanced-minus-retarded Green operator
E_{P}
has a wavefront set obeying

\WF(E_{P})\subset(\mathcal{V}^{+}\times\mathcal{V}^{-})\cup(\mathcal{V}^{-}\times\mathcal{V}^{+})

(1.5)

for some conic set
\mathcal{V}^{+}
in the spacetime cotangent bundle
T^{*}M
that does not intersect its opposite
\mathcal{V}^{-}=-\mathcal{V}^{+}
(see Section
5
for the precise definition). No relation is assumed between
\mathcal{V}^{\pm}
and the cones
\mathcal{N}^{\pm}
of null (or causal) covectors.
Such operators

will be described as
\mathcal{V}^{\pm}
-decomposable. States with two-point functions obeying the obvious modification of (
1.3
), namely

\WF(W)\subset\mathcal{V}^{+}\times\mathcal{V}^{-},

(1.6)

will be described as
\mathcal{V}^{+}
-Hadamard; one can equally proceed from the corresponding modification of (
1.4

1.3
) for Klein–Gordon fields corresponds to the
\mathcal{N}^{+}
-Hadamard condition in this terminology.

Starting from these definitions, we will show
how various standard properties of Hadamard states have analogues for our generalised definition.
In particular, differences of Hadamard two-point functions are smooth, and the Hadamard property propagates under the equation of motion (Theorem
5.4
); there is an equivalent Hilbert space formulation mirroring (
1.4
) (Theorem
5.9
) from which one can see that the existence of a single Hadamard state implies the existence of many more (Corollary
5.11

the Hadamard form is preserved under suitable pullbacks and pushforwards, and, for similar reasons, is stable under scattering processes (Theorem
5.22

,
40

described by Green-hyperbolic operators, this implies that the Hadamard states form a covariant state space (Corollary
5.18

5.23
that the Hadamard condition interacts well with direct sums and partial traces. Our final general results are given in Theorem
5.24
and Corollary

to show how the Hadamard condition on

n

The general theory is illustrated by various examples and applications. First, recent results of Islam and Strohmaier
[

Second, our results that the Hadamard condition is preserved by tensor products, scattering and partial traces combine to prove that state updates resulting from nonselective measurements yield Hadamard states, if the system and probe states are initially Hadamard – see Theorem

Other applications to the theory of measurements will appear elsewhere.

As a further application, we consider the Hadamard states of the Proca field in section

The paper is structured as follows. After necessary preliminaries in Section
2

Some material here is new, in particular the categorical structures on these operators and the delineation of fermionic real formally hermitian Green-hyperbolic operators. Special cases include the normally hyperbolic operators and Dirac-type operators in the bosonic and fermionic cases respectively.

3.1
Hermitian vector bundles over globally hyperbolic spacetimes

In this paper, a hermitian vector bundle
(B,{\boldsymbol{M}},(\cdot,\cdot),\mathcal{C})
over

Musical notation

3.2
(Semi)-Green-hyperbolic operators and RFHGHO’s

Let
P:\Gamma^{\infty}(B)\to\Gamma^{\infty}(B)
be a partial differential operator where
(B,{\boldsymbol{M}},(\cdot,\cdot),\mathcal{C})
is a hermitian vector bundle over
{\boldsymbol{M}}\in\mathsf{Loc}
. If there are linear maps
E_{P}^{\pm}:\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)\to\Gamma^{\infty}(B)
such that the conditions

1.

PE_{P}^{\pm}{f}={f}

2.

E_{P}^{\pm}P{f}={f}

3.

\supp E_{P}^{\pm}{f}\subset J^{\pm}(\supp{f})

hold for all
{f}\in\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)
, then we say that
P
is
semi-Green-hyperbolic
and, as usual, call
E^{\pm}_{P}
the advanced
(-)
and retarded
(+)
Green operators for

. If
P
and one (and hence all) of
{\vphantom{P}}{}^{t}P
,
{\vphantom{P}}{}^{\dagger}P
or
{\vphantom{P}}{}^{\star}P
are semi-Green-hyperbolic, then
P
is
Green-hyperbolic

[
4
]

Two useful facts are that

\overline{E}_{P}^{\pm}=E_{\overline{P}}^{\pm}
, and that
{\vphantom{E}}{}^{t}E_{P}^{\pm}=E_{{\vphantom{P}}{}^{t}P}^{\mp}
.
Therefore, if
P
is a real, formally hermitian Green-hyperbolic operator (RFHGHO), one has

\overline{E}_{P}^{\pm}=E_{P}^{\pm}={\vphantom{(E_{P}^{\mp})}}^{t}(E_{P}^{\mp})
,
whereas if
P
is formally hermitian and Green-hyperbolic,

E_{P}^{\pm}={\vphantom{(E_{P}^{\mp})}}^{\dagger}(E_{P}^{\mp})

We also define the advanced-minus-retarded operator
E_{P}:\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)\to\Gamma^{\infty}(B)

E_{P}=E_{P}^{-}-E_{P}^{+}
,
whose range is contained in
\Gamma^{\infty}_{\textnormal{sc}}(B)
. For any RFHGHO
P
, there is an associated antisymmetric bilinear form

E_{P}(f,h)=\langle f,E_{P}h\rangle,\qquad f,h\in\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B),

(3.8)

which is antisymmetric because
{\vphantom{E}}{}^{t}E_{P}=-E_{P}
and satisfies
E_{P}(\mathcal{C}f,\mathcal{C}h)=\overline{E_{P}(f,h)}

because
E_{P}=\overline{E}_{P}
and also
E_{P}(Pf,h)=E_{P}(f,Ph)=0
. A number of other general properties of Green-hyperbolic operators will be needed below and are stated here for clarity.

Examples

A
normally hyperbolic operator
is a second-order differential operator
P:\Gamma^{\infty}(B)\to\Gamma^{\infty}(B)
whose principal symbol is given by

p(x,k)=-g_{x}^{-1}(k,k)\mathrm{id}_{B_{x}}
, where
g
is the spacetime metric. Thus (recalling the correspondence
k_{\mu}\leftrightarrow-{\rm i}\partial_{\mu}
) in a local frame for
B
,
P
is equivalent to a matrix of differential operators
P^{a}_{\phantom{a}b}
with

P^{a}_{\phantom{a}b}=g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\delta^{a}_{\phantom{a}b}+U^{a}_{\phantom{a}b},

(3.14)

where
U^{a}_{\phantom{a}b}
is a matrix of first order differential operators and
\nabla
is the Levi–Civita derivative.
Any normally hyperbolic operator has advanced and retarded Green operators (see Corollary 3.4.3 in
[
6
]
) and is therefore semi-Green-hyperbolic; as the formal transpose of a normally hyperbolic operator is also normally hyperbolic, these operators are Green-hyperbolic.

However, not all Green-hyperbolic operators are normally hyperbolic, as shown by the Dirac and Proca field equations (see Sections
4
and
7
respectively) and, even more starkly, the elliptic operator
-{\rm d}^{2}/{\rm d}t^{2}
on
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
shows that Green-hyperbolic operators need not be hyperbolic. Another example is given in
[
78
]
: suppose that
g^{\prime}
is a time orientable slow metric relative to the spacetime metric
g
and suppose that
P^{\prime}
and
P
are normally hyperbolic operators with respect to
g^{\prime}
and
g
. Let
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(M;[0,1])
and
define a convex combination
P_{\chi}=(1-\chi)P+\chi P^{\prime}
. Then there is a metric
g_{\chi}
so that

g_{\chi}^{-1}=(1-\chi)g^{-1}+\chi(g^{\prime})^{-1}
, which is a slow metric relative to
g
(Theorem 2.18 in
[
78
]
) that is also globally hyperbolic. Then
P_{\chi}
is normally hyperbolic (and therefore Green-hyperbolic) with respect to
g_{\chi}
, while it is Green-hyperbolic, but not normally hyperbolic, with respect to the spacetime metric
g
– see Theorem 3.14 in
[
78
]
. Further examples can be found in
[
4
]
.

The category
\mathsf{GreenHyp}

By Theorem
3.1
(f), every RFHGHO determines an object of
\mathsf{Sympl}_{\mathbb{C}}
. We define
the category
\mathsf{GreenHyp}
of RFHGHO’s so that this assignment is functorial. Previously developed categories of Green-hyperbolic operators, such as
\mathsf{GlobHypGreen}
in
[
5
]
have focussed on intertwining the Green-hyperbolic operators with pushforward maps – as will be seen, our approach is more general and has certain advantages.

Definition 3.2
.

Objects of
\mathsf{GreenHyp}
are pairs
(B,P)
(sometimes abbreviated just to
P
), where
B
is a hermitian vector bundle over a globally hyperbolic spacetime and
P
is a RFHGHO acting on
\Gamma^{\infty}(B)
. A morphism
S:(B,P)\to(B^{\prime},P^{\prime})
in
\mathsf{GreenHyp}
is a linear map
S:\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)\to\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B^{\prime})

obeying
S\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}^{\prime}S
,
SP\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)\subset P^{\prime}\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B^{\prime})
, and
E_{P^{\prime}}(Sf,Sh)=E_{P}(f,h)
for all
f,h\in\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)
.

A
\mathsf{GreenHyp}
-morphism
S:(B,P)\to(B^{\prime},P^{\prime})
determines a
linear map
\Sol(S):E_{P}\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)\to E_{P^{\prime}}\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B^{\prime})
by
\Sol(S)E_{P}f=E_{P^{\prime}}Sf
, which is well-defined because

E_{P}f=0
implies
f\in P\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)
and hence
Sf\in P^{\prime}\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B^{\prime})=\ker E_{P^{\prime}}
. The identity
\Sol(S)\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\Sol(S)
follows from
S\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}^{\prime}S
, and the calculation

\sigma_{P^{\prime}}(E_{P^{\prime}}Sf,E_{P^{\prime}}Sh)=E_{P^{\prime}}(Sf,Sh)=E_{P}(f,h)=\sigma_{P}(E_{P}f,E_{P}h)

(3.15)

shows that
\Sol(S)
is symplectic and therefore a
\mathsf{Sympl}_{\mathbb{C}}
-morphism.
Consequently,
\Sol(S)
is injective; we will describe
S
as
Cauchy
if
\Sol(S)
is an isomorphism, and say that
morphisms
S,T:(B,P)\to(B^{\prime},P^{\prime})
are
equivalent
, written
S\sim T
, if
\Sol(S)=\Sol(T)
.
A straightforward calculation shows that
\hat{E}_{P^{\prime}}\hat{S}=\Sol(S)\hat{E}_{P}
, where

\displaystyle\hat{S}:\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)/P\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)

\displaystyle\to\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B^{\prime})/P^{\prime}\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B^{\prime})

\displaystyle\hat{S}[f]

\displaystyle=[Sf].

(3.16)

Thus
\hat{S}
is injective,
S
is Cauchy if and only if
\hat{S}
is an isomorphism, and morphisms
S,T:(B,P)\to(B^{\prime},P^{\prime})
are
equivalent
if and only if
\hat{S}=\hat{T}
.

.

There is a functor
\Sol:\mathsf{GreenHyp}\to\mathsf{Sympl}_{\mathbb{C}}

It has already been established that

3.3
Quantisation

We now describe how every RFHGHO defines a bosonic hermitian QFT. Given the definitions so far, the quantisation process may be described functorially. Let
\mathsf{Alg}
be the category whose objects are unital complex
*

*

[

to

The main theorem on quantisation is essentially standard and we state it without detailed proof.

Theorem 3.6
.

There is a functor

\partial(v_{1}\odot\cdots\odot v_{k})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}v_{j}\otimes(v_{1}\odot\cdots\not{v_{j}}\cdots\odot v_{k}),\qquad\partial 1\!\!1_{\mathscr{Y}(P)}=0,

(3.30)

for
\psi:{\boldsymbol{M}}\to{\boldsymbol{M}}^{\prime}
in
\mathsf{Loc}
.

3.4
States and
n
-point functions

Recall that a state on
*
-algebra
\mathcal{A}\in\mathsf{Alg}
is a linear map
\omega:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\omega(1\!\!1)=1
and
\omega(A^{*}A)\geq 0
for all
A\in\mathcal{A}
, i.e.,
\omega
is a positive normalised linear map. The set of states on
\mathcal{A}
is denoted
\mathcal{A}^{*}_{+,1}
and, as usual,
\omega(A)
is interpreted as the expectation value of
A=A^{*}\in\mathcal{A}
in state
\omega
.

Suppose
P
is a RFHGHO with hermitian vector bundle
B
over globally hyperbolic spacetime
{\boldsymbol{M}}
, and let
\omega\in\mathscr{Y}(P)^{*}_{+,1}
be any state on
\mathscr{Y}(P)
. Then we may define
n
-point correlators of the form

\omega(\mathscr{Y}_{P}(f_{1})\cdots\mathscr{Y}_{P}(f_{n}))
for
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)
. If
\omega
is sufficiently
regular then the correlators may be expressed as distributions in various ways. Most immediately, they define distributions in
\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B^{\boxtimes n})^{\prime}=\mathscr{D}^{\prime}((B^{*}\otimes\Omega_{M})^{\boxtimes n})
.
However, this has the disadvantage that
P
would not act on the
n
-point functions directly. Instead,
we will say that
\omega
has distributional
n
-point functions if, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a distribution
W^{(n)}\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(B^{\boxtimes n})
so that

W^{(n)}(f_{1}^{\sharp,1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{n}^{\sharp,1})=\omega(\Upsilon_{P}(f_{1})\cdots\Upsilon_{P}(f_{n}))

(3.31)

for
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)
, using the musical notation introduced in Section
3.1
. The most interesting case will be the two-point function, where we will usually drop the superscript and simply write
W\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(B\boxtimes B)=\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}((B^{*}\otimes\Omega_{M})\boxtimes(B^{*}\otimes\Omega_{M}))^{\prime}
with

W(f^{\sharp,1}\otimes h^{\sharp,1})=\omega(\Upsilon_{P}(f)\Upsilon_{P}(h)),\qquad f,h\in\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B).

(3.32)

A similar ‘two-point distribution’
T^{\textnormal{2pt}}\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(B\boxtimes B)
can be defined for any continuous linear map
T:\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)\to\Gamma^{\infty}(B)
, again using the bilinear bundle metric of
B
, by

T^{\textnormal{2pt}}(f^{\sharp,1}\otimes h^{\sharp,1})=T(f,h):=\langle f,Th\rangle,\qquad f,h\in\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B).

(3.33)

Particular examples of note include two-point distributions for the retarded and advanced Green operators
E_{P}^{\pm}
, and their difference
E_{P}
. In particular,
the CCRs entail the identity

W-{\vphantom{W}}^{t}W={\rm i}E_{P}^{\textnormal{2pt}},

(3.34)

where
{\vphantom{W}}{}^{t}W(u\otimes v)=W(v\otimes u)
.

As we have already described, any continuous linear map
T:\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)\to\Gamma^{\infty}(B)
also has an associated kernel distribution which does not rely on the bundle metric of
B
. The kernel distributions were defined for bundles with an explicit half-density factor, for which purpose we may regard
B=(B\otimes\Omega^{-1/2})\otimes\Omega^{1/2}
. In this way
T:\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)\to\Gamma^{\infty}(B)
also
has kernel distribution

T^{\textnormal{knl}}\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}((B\otimes\Omega^{-1/2})\boxtimes(B\otimes\Omega^{-1/2})^{*}\otimes\Omega^{1/2}_{M\times M})=\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(B\boxtimes(B^{*}\otimes\Omega_{M})),

(3.35)

which is related to
T^{\textnormal{2pt}}
by

T^{\textnormal{knl}}(F\otimes h)=T^{\textnormal{2pt}}(F\otimes h^{\sharp,1}),\qquad F\in\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B^{*}\otimes\Omega_{M}),~h\in\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B),

(3.36)

or equivalently,
T^{\textnormal{2pt}}=T^{\textnormal{knl}}\circ(1\otimes(\flat\circ\rho^{-1}))
, which brings out the fact that the
two-point distribution uses the bundle metric and volume density for its definition in a way that the kernel distribution does not. It will also be useful to consider another relation between two-point and kernel distributions. Namely, each continuous linear map
T:\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)\to\Gamma^{\infty}(B)
also determines

T^{\natural,\tfrac{1}{2}}:\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B\otimes\Omega^{1/2})\to\Gamma^{\infty}(B\otimes\Omega^{1/2})
, whose
kernel distribution
(T^{\natural,\tfrac{1}{2}})^{\textnormal{knl}}\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}((B\boxtimes B^{*})\otimes\Omega^{1/2}_{M\times M})
is related to
T^{\textnormal{2pt}}\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(B\boxtimes B)
by

(T^{\natural,\tfrac{1}{2}})^{\textnormal{knl}}(f^{\sharp,\tfrac{1}{2}}\otimes h^{\sharp,\tfrac{1}{2}})=\langle f,Th\rangle=T^{\textnormal{2pt}}(f^{\sharp,1}\otimes h^{\sharp,1}),\qquad f,h\in\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B),

(3.37)

or equivalently,
T^{\textnormal{2pt}}=(T^{\natural,\tfrac{1}{2}})^{\textnormal{knl}}\circ(\rho^{-1/2}\otimes(\rho^{1/2}\circ\flat\circ\rho^{-1}))
.
We drop the
2pt
or
knl
superscript where no confusion can arise between the underlying linear map and which of the two associated distributions is intended. As will be seen,
T^{\textnormal{2pt}}
and
T^{\textnormal{knl}}
have identical wavefront sets but their polarisation sets (though related) are valued in different bundles.

The operator
P
extends to
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(B)
as the distributional dual of
\rho({\vphantom{P}}^{\star}P)\rho^{-1}
on
\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B^{*}\otimes\Omega_{M})
. In particular, we have
(Pu)(f^{\sharp,1})=u(\rho({\vphantom{P}}^{\star}P)f^{\sharp})=u(({\vphantom{P}}^{t}Pf)^{\sharp,1})=u((Pf)^{\sharp,1})
using formal symmetry of the RFHGHO
P
and the identity
{\vphantom{P}}{}^{\star}Pf^{\sharp}=({\vphantom{P}}^{t}Pf)^{\sharp}
, which is derived from the calculation

\langle\!\langle{\vphantom{P}}^{\star}Pf^{\sharp},h\rangle\!\rangle=\langle\!\langle f^{\sharp},Ph\rangle\!\rangle=\langle f,Ph\rangle=\langle{\vphantom{P}}^{t}Pf,h\rangle=\langle\!\langle({\vphantom{P}}^{t}Pf)^{\sharp},h\rangle\!\rangle

(3.38)

for arbitrary
f,h\in\Gamma^{\infty}(B)
with compactly intersecting supports. Thus both
W
and
E_{P}
become
P
-bisolutions, in the sense that

(P\otimes 1)W=0=(1\otimes P)W,\qquad(P\otimes 1)E_{P}^{\textnormal{2pt}}=0=(1\otimes P)E_{P}^{\textnormal{2pt}}.

(3.39)

Similarly, the two-point distributions of the Green operators
E_{P}^{\pm}
obey

(P\otimes 1)(E_{P}^{\pm})^{\textnormal{2pt}}=\mathrm{id}^{\textnormal{2pt}}=(1\otimes P)(E_{P}^{\pm})^{\textnormal{2pt}}.

(3.40)

4
Fermionic Green-hyperbolic operators

4.1
Fermionic RFHGHO’s

We now describe a class of RFHGHO’s with additional structure that allows for a fermionic quantisation. A fermionic RFHGHO consists of a RFHGHO
(B,P)
equipped with a smooth fibrewise bundle isomorphism
R:B\to B
so that
R={\vphantom{R}}^{\dagger}R
and
R\mathcal{C}=-\mathcal{C}R
where
\mathcal{C}
is the antilinear involution of
B
(consequently also
{\vphantom{R}}{}^{t}R=-R
) and
PR=RP
.
Then
E_{P}R=RE_{P}
and

E_{P}(f,Rh)=\langle f,E_{P}Rh\rangle=\langle f,RE_{P}h\rangle=-\langle Rf,E_{P}h\rangle=-E_{P}(Rf,h)=E_{P}(h,Rf),

(4.1)

so
(f,h)\mapsto{\rm i}E_{P}(f,Rh)
is a symmetric bilinear form on
\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)
that vanishes on
P\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)
and obeys

{\rm i}E_{P}(\mathcal{C}f,R\mathcal{C}h)=-{\rm i}E_{P}(\mathcal{C}f,\mathcal{C}Rh)=-{\rm i}\overline{E_{P}(f,Rh)}=\overline{{\rm i}E_{P}(f,Rh)}.

(4.2)

One may check that
f\cdot h=\langle f,Rh\rangle
defines an antisymmetric product on
\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)
, making contact with the description of fermionic matter models in e.g.,
[
8
,
51
]
.
We denote the fermionic RFHGHO by
(B,P,R)
or simply
(P,R)
or
P
if there is no ambiguity. Fermionic RFHGHO’s form the objects of a category
\mathsf{fGreenHyp}
in which
a morphism
(P,R)\to(P^{\prime},R^{\prime})
is a
\mathsf{GreenHyp}
-morphism

S:P\to P^{\prime}
so that
SR=R^{\prime}S
.

In each fibre
B_{x}
,
R
has real nonzero eigenvalues with respect to the hermitian bundle metric, and
\mathcal{C}
maps any
\lambda
-eigenvector of
R
to a
(-\lambda)
-eigenvector. The projection onto the positive spectrum determines a smooth decomposition
B=B_{+}\oplus B_{-}
with respect to which
R
and
\mathcal{C}
take block forms

R=\begin{pmatrix}R_{+}&0\\
0&R_{-}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathcal{C}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathcal{C}_{+-}\\
\mathcal{C}_{-+}&0\end{pmatrix},

(4.3)

with
\mathcal{C}_{+-}=\mathcal{C}_{-+}^{-1}
and
R_{-}=-\mathcal{C}_{-+}R_{+}\mathcal{C}_{+-}
. Although the hermitian pairing is diagonal with respect to the decomposition
B=B_{+}\oplus B_{-}
, the bilinear pairing derived from it using
\mathcal{C}
is off-diagonal,

\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}f\\
h\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}f^{\prime}\\
h^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle=(\mathcal{C}_{+-}h,f^{\prime})+(\mathcal{C}_{-+}f,h^{\prime}).

(4.4)

The requirements that
PR=RP
and
P\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}P
imply that
P
is also block diagonal,

P=\begin{pmatrix}P_{+}&0\\
0&P_{-}\end{pmatrix},\qquad P_{-}=\mathcal{C}_{-+}P_{+}\mathcal{C}_{+-},

(4.5)

and evidently
P_{\pm}
are Green-hyperbolic.
Thus, a fermionic RFHGHO can be seen as a doubled version of a formally hermitian, but not necessarily real, Green-hyperbolic operator
P_{+}
on
B_{+}
, equipped with an operator

R_{+}
whose role is to ensure that
E_{P}(f,Rh)
is a symmetric bilinear form.

4.2
Quantisation

Any fermionic RFHGHO can be quantised to yield a bosonic field theory using
\mathscr{Y}
. However it also has a more appropriate quantisation as a fermionic field theory, which we now describe.

The fermionic analogue of
\mathsf{Sympl}_{\mathbb{C}}
is the category
\mathsf{Met}_{\mathbb{C}}
of complexified real metric vector spaces, with objects
(V,\tau,C)
, where
V
is a complex vector space,
\tau:V\times V\to\mathbb{C}
is a weakly nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form and
C
is an antilinear involution such that
\tau(Cf,Ch)=\overline{\tau(f,h)}
. Here, ‘metric’
is understood in the sense of a (possibly indefinite) metric tensor. A
\mathsf{Met}_{\mathbb{C}}
-morphism between

(V,\tau,C)
and
(V^{\prime},\tau^{\prime},C^{\prime})
is a (necessarily injective) linear map
T:V\to V^{\prime}
such that
TC=C^{\prime}T
and
\tau^{\prime}(Tv,Tw)=\tau(v,w)
for
v,w\in V
. Evidently
every
(V,\tau,C)
admits a sesquilinear form
(v,w)=\tau(Cv,w)
which satisfies

(Cv,Cw)=(w,v)=\overline{(v,w)}
;

\mathsf{Met}_{\mathbb{C}}
morphisms are isometries with respect to these forms. There is an analogue of
Theorem
3.3
.

Theorem 4.1
.

There is a functor
\Sol_{F}:\mathsf{fGreenHyp}\to\mathsf{Met}_{\mathbb{C}}
so that

\Sol_{F}(P,R,\mathcal{C})=(\Sol(P),{\rm i}\sigma_{P}\circ(1\otimes R),\mathcal{C}),

(4.6)

whose action on
morphisms has the same underlying map as
\Sol
, and maps Cauchy morphisms
to
\mathsf{Met}_{\mathbb{C}}
-isomorphisms.
For fermionic RFHGHO’s
(P_{j},R_{j},\mathcal{C}_{j})
(
j=1,2
), one has
\Sol_{F}(P_{1}\oplus P_{2},R_{1}\oplus R_{2},\mathcal{C}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{C}_{2})=\Sol_{F}(P_{1},R_{1},\mathcal{C}_{1})\oplus\Sol_{F}(P_{2},R_{2},\mathcal{C}_{2})
.

Proof.

Define the bilinear form
\tau_{(P,R)}:\Sol(P)\times\Sol(P)\to\mathbb{C}
by
\tau_{(P,R)}(E_{P}f,E_{h})=E_{P}(f,Rh)
, which is well-defined because
P
and
R
commute and general properties of
E_{P}
.
Then (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) show that
(\Sol(P),\tau_{(P,R)},\mathcal{C})\in\mathsf{Met}_{\mathbb{C}}
, with

\tau_{(P,R)}={\rm i}\sigma_{P}\circ(1\otimes R)
;
\tau_{(P,R)}
inherits weak nondegeneracy from
\sigma_{P}
.
Next,
let
S:(P,R,\mathcal{C})\to(P^{\prime},R^{\prime},\mathcal{C}^{\prime})
in
\mathsf{fGreenHyp}
. In particular,
S:(P,\mathcal{C})\to(P^{\prime},\mathcal{C}^{\prime})

is a
\mathsf{GreenHyp}
morphism and
SR=R^{\prime}S
. Consequently,
S\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}^{\prime}S
and

\tau_{(P^{\prime},R^{\prime})}(Sv,Sw)={\rm i}\sigma_{P^{\prime}}(Sv,R^{\prime}Sw)={\rm i}\sigma_{P^{\prime}}(Sv,SRw)={\rm i}\sigma_{P}(v,Rw)=\tau_{(P,R)}(v,w)
holds for all

v,w\in\Sol(P)
, which shows that
S
is the underlying map of a
\mathsf{Met}_{\mathbb{C}}
-morphism
from
\Sol_{F}(P,R,\mathcal{C})
to
\Sol_{F}(P^{\prime},R^{\prime},\mathcal{C}^{\prime})
. The remaining functorial properties and other statements follow directly from the analogous properties of
\Sol
.
∎

The fermionic quantised theory is described by graded
*
-algebras. Let
\mathsf{grAlg}
be the category of graded
*
-algebras over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\{0,1\}
with addition modulo
2
. That is, an object
\mathcal{A}\in\mathsf{grAlg}
is an object of
\mathsf{Alg}
with a preferred
direct sum decomposition
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{A}_{1}
so that the multiplication adds grades modulo
2
,
\mathcal{A}_{j}\mathcal{A}_{k}\subset\mathcal{A}_{j+k}
for
j,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. A
\mathsf{grAlg}
-morphism
\alpha:\mathcal{A}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{A}_{1}\to\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{1}
is an
\mathsf{Alg}
-morphism so that
\alpha\mathcal{A}_{j}\subset\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{j}
for
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Elements of
A\in\mathcal{A}_{j}
are called
homogeneous of grade
|A|=j
. The graded commutator is defined by

[\kern-3.99994pt{[}\,A,B\,]\kern-3.99994pt{]}=AB-(-1)^{|A|\,|B|}BA

(4.7)

for homogeneous elements
A,B
, and extended by linearity in each slot.
The analogue to Theorem
3.6
can now be stated.

Theorem 4.2
.

There is a functor
\mathscr{X}:\mathsf{fGreenHyp}\to\mathsf{grAlg}
with the following properties:

1.

If
(P,R)\in\mathsf{fGreenHyp}
has bundle
B
and antilinear involution
\mathcal{C}
then
\mathscr{X}(P)
is the unital
*
-algebra generated by symbols
\Xi_{P}({f})
(
f\in\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B))
, subject to

(a)

f\mapsto\Xi_{P}({f})
is complex linear

(b)

\Xi_{P}({f})^{*}=\Xi_{P}(\mathcal{C}f)

(c)

\Xi_{P}({P}{f})=0
,

(d)

[\kern-3.99994pt{[}\,\Xi_{P}({f}),\Xi_{P}(h)\,]\kern-3.99994pt{]}={\rm i}E_{P}(f,R{h})1\!\!1_{\mathscr{X}(P)}

for all
{f},h\in\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)
, and graded so that each
\Xi_{P}(f)
is odd (whereupon the left-hand side of the equality in X4 is an anticommutator);

2.

if
S:P\to P^{\prime}
is a
\mathsf{fGreenHyp}
-morphism, then the
\mathsf{grAlg}
-morphism
\mathscr{X}(S):\mathscr{X}(P)\to\mathscr{X}(P^{\prime})
is uniquely determined by

\mathscr{X}(S)\Xi_{P}({f})=\Xi_{P^{\prime}}(S{f}),\qquad{f}\in\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B),

(4.8)

and in particular,
\mathscr{X}(S)=\mathscr{X}(T)
if and only if
S\sim T
.

3.

\mathscr{X}(S)
is an
\mathsf{grAlg}
-isomorphism if
S
is a Cauchy
\mathsf{fGreenHyp}
-morphism.

4.

If
(B,P,R)
and
(B^{\prime},P^{\prime},R^{\prime})
are fermionic RFHGHO’s then their direct sum

(B\oplus B^{\prime},P\oplus P^{\prime},R\oplus R^{\prime})
is a fermionic RFHGHO, and

\mathscr{X}(P\oplus P^{\prime})\cong\mathscr{X}(P)\otimes\mathscr{X}(P^{\prime})

(4.9)

under the isomorphism fixed by

\Xi_{P\oplus P^{\prime}}(f\oplus f^{\prime})\mapsto\Xi_{P}(f)\otimes 1\!\!1_{\mathscr{X}(P^{\prime})}+1\!\!1_{\mathscr{X}(P)}\otimes\Xi_{P^{\prime}}(f^{\prime}),

(4.10)

where
\mathscr{X}(P)\otimes\mathscr{X}(P^{\prime})
is the graded tensor product, in which

(A\otimes A^{\prime})(B\otimes B^{\prime})=(-1)^{|A^{\prime}|\,|B|}(AB)\otimes(A^{\prime}B^{\prime})
and

(A\otimes B)^{*}=(-1)^{|A|\,|B|}A^{*}\otimes B^{*}
for homogeneous
A,B\in\mathscr{X}(P)
,
A^{\prime},B^{\prime}\in\mathscr{X}(P^{\prime})
.

Explicitly,
\mathscr{X}=\mathscr{Q}_{a}\circ\Sol_{F}
, where
\mathscr{Q}_{a}:\mathsf{Met}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathsf{grAlg}
is a quantisation functor
defined by analogy with
\mathscr{Q}_{s}
(but which we will not discuss directly). As a vector space,

\mathscr{X}(P)=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\Sol_{F}(P,R)^{\wedge n},

(4.11)

where
\wedge
is the antisymmetrised tensor product, with
*
-operation so that

(v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{n})^{*}=(\mathcal{C}v_{n}\wedge\cdots\mathcal{C}v_{1})
and we write
1\!\!1_{\mathscr{X}(P)}=(1,0,\ldots)
. Again adopting formulae from the functional formalism, the graded algebra product on
\mathscr{X}(P)
is

FH=\wedge\circ\exp\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\langle\tau,\partial_{L}\otimes\partial_{R}\rangle\right)F\otimes H,

(4.12)

where the linear maps
\partial_{L},\partial_{R}:\mathscr{X}(P)\to\Sol_{F}(P,R)\otimes\mathscr{X}(P)
obey (note the numbering and ordering)

\displaystyle\partial_{L}(v_{0}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}v_{j}\otimes(v_{0}\wedge\cdots\not{v_{j}}\cdots\wedge v_{k}),

\displaystyle\partial_{R}(v_{k}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{0})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}v_{j}\otimes(v_{k}\wedge\cdots\not{v_{j}}\cdots\wedge v_{0}),

(4.13)

and
\partial_{L}1\!\!1_{\mathscr{X}(P)}=\partial_{R}1\!\!1_{\mathscr{X}(P)}=0
, while
\tau:(\Sol_{F}(P,R)\otimes\mathscr{X}(P))^{\otimes 2}\to\mathscr{X}(P)^{\otimes 2}
is the linear map determined by

\langle\tau,(v\otimes F)\otimes(w\otimes H)\rangle=\tau(v,w)F\otimes H.

(4.14)

To obtain the correct gradings, one should note that

\langle\tau,\partial_{L}\otimes\partial_{R}\rangle:\mathscr{X}(P)_{j}\otimes\mathscr{X}(P)_{k}\to\mathscr{X}(P)_{j-1}\otimes\mathscr{X}(P)_{k-1}.

(4.15)

In particular, writing
\Xi_{P}(f)=E_{P}f
, one has

\Xi_{P}(f)\Xi_{P}(h)=\Xi_{P}(f)\wedge\Xi_{P}(h)+\frac{1}{2}\tau(E_{P}f,E_{P}h)1\!\!1_{\mathscr{X}(P)},\quad\{\Xi_{P}(f),\Xi_{P}(h)\}={\rm i}E_{P}(f,Rh)1\!\!1_{\mathscr{X}(P)}.

(4.16)

A similar argument to the one used in the bosonic case (starting with III §7.7 of
[
10
]
) establishes an isomorphism between
\mathscr{X}(P\oplus P^{\prime})
and the graded tensor product
\mathscr{X}(P)\otimes\mathscr{X}(P^{\prime})
, with the stated action on generators.

It is not guaranteed that
\mathscr{X}(P)
admits states or, equivalently, a completion as a
C^{*}
-algebra. Indeed a necessary condition for the existence of states is that the sesquilinear form

(f,h)\mapsto{\rm i}E_{P}(\mathcal{C}f,Rh)
determines an inner product on
\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)/P\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)
, in which case we say that

(P,R)
has
definite type
. The subset of definite-type
\mathsf{fGreenHyp}
objects will be denoted
\mathsf{fGreenHyp}^{+}
.

If
(P,R)
is a fermionic RFHGHO, the
n
-point functions for states on
\mathscr{X}(P)
are defined
as in (
3.31
) but replacing
\Upsilon_{P}
by
\Xi_{P}
. In particular, the two-point function obeys

W+{\vphantom{W}}^{t}W={\rm i}E_{P}^{\textnormal{2pt}}\circ(1\otimes R^{\sharp,1}),

(4.17)

due to the anticommutation relations.

As in the bosonic case, any functorial assignment of fermionic RFHGHO’s results in a
functorial assignment of graded algebras. A fermionic functorial equation of motion
is a functor
\fEoM:\mathsf{Loc}\to\mathsf{fGreenHyp}
, so that, writing
\fEoM({\boldsymbol{M}})=(\mathscr{B}({\boldsymbol{M}}),P_{\boldsymbol{M}},R_{\boldsymbol{M}})
for
{\boldsymbol{M}}\in\mathsf{Loc}
, the assignment
\EoM({\boldsymbol{M}})=(\mathscr{B}({\boldsymbol{M}}),P_{\boldsymbol{M}})
defines a functorial equation of motion (see Definition
3.7
). Then one has a
functorial assignment of graded algebras
\mathscr{X}\circ\fEoM:\mathsf{Loc}\to\mathsf{grAlg}
. There is an obvious analogue of Theorem
3.8
with the change that the requirement of commutativity in the definition of Einstein causality is replaced by graded commutativity.
To enlarge on this a little, note that the operator
R_{\boldsymbol{M}}
does not change supports and therefore

E_{P_{\boldsymbol{M}}}(f,R_{\boldsymbol{M}}h)=0
if
f,h\in\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(\mathscr{B}({\boldsymbol{M}}))
have causally disjoint supports. Therefore the corresponding generators
\Xi_{P_{\boldsymbol{M}}}(f)
and
\Xi_{P_{\boldsymbol{M}}}(h)
anticommute, indicating that (as is expected for fermi fields) they are not observables. However, even-graded elements
A,B\in\mathscr{X}(P_{\boldsymbol{M}})
do commute if the generators appearing in
A
and
B
are smeared fields with support in causally disjoint regions
O_{A}
,
O_{B}
in
{\boldsymbol{M}}
. In this way one sees that by taking the even part
\mathscr{X}(\fEoM({\boldsymbol{M}}))_{0}
, we obtain a locally covariant QFT that obeys Einstein causality.

More generally, one might consider functors from categories of globally hyperbolic spacetimes with additional structure such as a spin structure, and, suitably adapted, the above discussion will again yield a locally covariant QFT obeying graded Einstein causality, or Einstein causality on taking even parts. Details are left to the reader.

4.3
Dirac type operators

Just as normally hyperbolic operators form a special class of RFHGHO’s,
the operators of Dirac type have a special role in the fermionic theory.
Let
B
be a finite rank complex vector bundle over
{\boldsymbol{M}}\in\mathsf{Loc}
with a hermitian fibre metric but not necessarily a complex conjugation.
(
{\boldsymbol{M}}
need not admit a spin structure). A first-order formally hermitian partial differential operator

D
on
B
is said to be of
Dirac type
if its principal symbol
\sigma_{D}
obeys

\sigma_{D}(x,k)^{2}=g^{-1}_{x}(k,k)\mathrm{id}_{B_{x}},

(4.18)

which entails that
-D^{2}
is normally hyperbolic. It
follows that
D={\vphantom{D}}^{\dagger}D
is Green-hyperbolic, with Green operators
E_{D}^{\pm}=DE_{D^{2}}^{\pm}
.
As
D
is first order, its principal symbol is

\sigma_{D}(x,k)=k_{\mu}(V_{x})^{\mu},

(4.19)

where

5
Generalised Hadamard States

The realisation that the Hadamard condition
for states of the scalar field could be expressed microlocally was a major breakthrough
[
81
]
, which has been achieved for other free fields including the Dirac
[
53

86
]

[

[

]

[

5.1
Wavefront sets

As standing notation: for any manifold
X

\dot{T}^{*}X
denotes the cotangent bundle of
X
with its zero section
0_{T^{*}X}
removed; meanwhile, for any subset
\mathcal{U}\subset T^{*}X

\mathcal{U}_{0}:=\mathcal{U}\cup 0_{T^{*}X}
.

Let

be a distribution and let

Choose coordinates
X^{\alpha}
in a neighbourhood of

and
define smooth functions
{\rm e}_{k}
(

) on the chart domain by
{\rm e}_{k}(x)=\exp({\rm i}X^{\alpha}(x)k(\partial_{X^{\alpha}}))
, where
\partial_{X^{\alpha}}
are the coordinate basis vector fields.
Then
(y,l)
is said to be a
regular directed point of

if there is a smooth density
\chi
compactly supported in the coordinate chart, and an open cone

\dot{T}_{y}^{*}M
such that
\chi(y)\neq 0
,

\sup_{k\in V}(1+\|k\|^{N})|u(\chi e_{k})|<\infty

for all

, where
\|\cdot\|
is any norm on
T^{*}_{y}M
.
The

is the set of all

[
59
]

If

is a complex finite-rank bundle and

\WF(u)=\bigcup_{S\in\Gamma^{\infty}(B^{*})}\WF(f\mapsto u(fS));

(5.2)

near any

forming a local basis for
B^{*}
.
Many standard results for scalar distributions then extend to the bundle context fairly directly; for instance,

\WF(Pu)\subset\WF(u)\subset\WF(Pu)\cup(\WF(u)\cap\Char P)

for any

where the

consists of those

at which the principal symbol

of

has nontrivial kernel. The first inclusion follows easily from the scalar result (see (8.1.11) in
[
59
]
) and the definition of the wavefront set for bundles, while the second can be obtained by polarisation set methods (see e.g. Section 3 of
[
29
]
).
If

is empty, then
\WF(Pu)=\WF(u)
, which in particular implies that

\WF(T^{\textnormal{2pt}})=\WF(T^{\textnormal{knl}})=\WF((T^{\sharp,\alpha})^{\textnormal{knl}})=\WF((T^{\natural,\alpha})^{\textnormal{knl}}),

(5.4)

as a result of the identity (
3.37
). More examples and exposition of wavefront sets can be found in
[

,

,

]
and many other references.

5.2

Let

When

g

4
4

4

If

-causal covector. Thus the cone of (future-directed)

g^{\prime}
-causal covectors.

An extreme example is provided by the Proca operator

on

because its principal symbol acts as

for all
(x,k)\in\dot{T}^{*}M

We will now propose a definition of Hadamard states for RFHGHO’s that is sufficiently broad to include the normally hyperbolic case, models such as the Proca field, operators that are normally hyperbolic with respect to slow metrics, and operators formed as direct sums of the above, including situations with multiple characteristic cones that can arise in descriptions of birefringence
[

[

We will show that our generalisation has properties that would be hoped for, and is equipped with a set of tools that are necessary for applications in quantum measurement.

A starting-point is the observation by Sahlmann and Verch

5.3
Hilbert space formulation

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathsf{Alg}
be any unital
*
-algebra. A Hilbert space representation of
\mathcal{A}
is a triple

(\mathfrak{H},\pi,D)
where
\mathfrak{H}
is a Hilbert space with dense subspace
D\subset\mathfrak{H}
and linear map
\pi:\mathcal{A}\to\Lin(D)
, so that (a)

\pi(AB)\psi=\pi(A)\pi(B)\psi
for all
A,B\in\mathcal{A}
and
\psi\in D
, (b)
\pi(1)=\mathrm{id}_{\mathfrak{H}}|_{D}
, and (c) for each
A\in\mathcal{A}
,
\pi(A)
has an adjoint
\pi(A)^{*}
whose domain contains
D
and obeys
\pi(A)^{*}\psi=\pi(A^{*})\psi
for all
\psi\in D
. By the GNS theorem (see section III.2.2 in
[
50
]
), every state
\omega
on
\mathcal{A}
determines a Hilbert space representation of this type, in which there is a unit vector
\varphi_{\omega}\in D
such that

\omega(A)=(\varphi_{\omega},\pi(A)\varphi_{\omega})
and
D=\pi(\mathcal{A})\varphi_{\omega}
. In this case, the vector
\varphi_{\omega}
is called the GNS vector of
\omega
and we denote the GNS representation by the quadruple
(\mathfrak{H},\pi,D,\varphi_{\omega})
referring to
\mathfrak{H}
and
D
as the GNS Hilbert space and GNS domain respectively.

Following
[
91
]
, the space of
\mathfrak{H}
-valued distributions,
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(M;\mathfrak{H})
, consists of linear and weakly (hence also strongly) continuous maps
u:\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)\to\mathfrak{H}
. The wavefront set
\WF(u)
can be defined exactly as for scalar-valued distributions, but replacing the absolute value
|u(\chi f)|
in (
5.1
) by
the
\mathfrak{H}
norm,
\|u(\chi f)\|_{\mathfrak{H}}
. The definition is extended to
\mathfrak{H}
-valued distributional sections of a finite-rank bundle
B
as in Section
5.1
. First, we give some useful results.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
V
and
W
be Hilbert space valued distributions in
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(B;\mathfrak{H})
,
and let
\mathcal{C}
be a continuous antilinear map of

.

(a) If

\WF(U)\subset\WF(V)
.

(b) If

U:\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B^{*}\otimes\Omega)\to\mathfrak{H}

\|U(f)\|_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2}\leq\|V(f)\|_{\mathfrak{H}}\|W(f)\|_{\mathfrak{H}}

\WF(U)\subset\WF(V)\cap\WF(W)
.

(c) For any fixed
\psi\in\mathfrak{H}

f\mapsto(\psi,V(f))_{\mathfrak{H}}
and

f\mapsto(V(\mathcal{C}f),\psi)_{\mathfrak{H}}

\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(B)
with

\WF((\psi,V(\cdot))_{\mathfrak{H}})\subset\WF(V)
,
\WF((V(\mathcal{C}\cdot),\psi)_{\mathfrak{H}})\subset-\WF(V)
.

(d)
Z(f,h)=(V(\mathcal{C}f),W(h))_{\mathfrak{H}}
defines

with
\WF(Z)\subset(-\WF_{0}(V))\times\WF_{0}(W)
.

Proof.

(a,b) First, the bounds on
U
ensure that for any compact

,
there is a constant
C_{K}
and seminorm

of
\Gamma_{K}^{\infty}(B^{*}\otimes\Omega)
so that
\|U(f)\|\leq C_{K}|f|_{K}
for all

in

is a regular directed point for

and
let
\chi
be any smooth density compactly supported in a coordinate chart domain about

, with associated coordinate phase functions

.
Then
\|V(S\chi{\rm e}_{l})\|
decays rapidly as

\|W(S\chi{\rm e}_{l})\|

which proves (a), and also (b) on allowing the the roles of

{\rm d}t\otimes{\rm d}t-h
, time-orientation so that
{\rm d}t
is future-pointing, and spacetime orientation so that
{\rm d}t\wedge\nu
is positively oriented. Global hyperbolicity holds by Proposition 5.2 in
[
65
]
, so
{\boldsymbol{M}}^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Loc}
has the required properties. The consequence is immediate by Corollary
5.19
and Corollary
5.11
.
∎

5.5
Scattering morphisms

Suppose that
P
and
Q
are RFHGHO’s on
M
that agree outside a temporally compact set
U\subset M
. Then
M^{\pm}=M\setminus J^{\mp}(U)
are open causally convex subsets on which
P
and
Q
agree. Applying Theorem
3.5
(e), there are Cauchy
\mathsf{GreenHyp}
-morphisms

\lambda_{QP}^{\pm}:(B,P)\to(B,Q)
called
response maps
so that
\lambda_{QP}^{\pm}\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)\subset\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B|_{M^{\pm}})
and
\hat{\lambda}_{QP}^{\pm}[f]_{P}=[f]_{Q}
for
f\in\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B|_{M^{\pm}})
. There
are also Cauchy
\mathsf{GreenHyp}
-morphisms
\lambda^{\pm}_{PQ}:(B,Q)\to(B,P)
with analogous properties. Therefore
\lambda^{-}_{PQ}\lambda^{+}_{QP}
is a
\mathsf{GreenHyp}
-endomorphism of
(B,P)
,
and any equivalent morphism is called a
scattering morphism
for
Q
relative to
P
with
coupling zone

U
. Scattering morphisms of this type play an important role in the theory of measurement
[
41
]
– see Section
6
.

Theorem 5.21
.

Under the conditions just stated, there is a scattering morphism
\vartheta
for
Q
relative to
P
with coupling zone
U
such that
\vartheta f=f-(Q-P)E_{Q}f=f-(Q-P)E_{Q}^{-}f
for
f\in\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B|_{M^{+}})
. Furthermore
\lambda^{+}_{QP}\sim\lambda^{-}_{QP}\vartheta
,
i.e.,
\hat{\vartheta}=(\hat{\lambda}^{-}_{QP})^{-1}\hat{\lambda}^{+}_{QP}
.

Proof.

Using the freedom to change
\lambda^{-}_{PQ}
and
\lambda^{+}_{QP}
for equivalent morphisms, we may assume they are both of the explicit form given in Theorem
3.5
(e).
Thus
\lambda^{+}_{QP}f=f\mod P\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)
and
\lambda^{-}_{PQ}f=f\mod Q\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)
. Since
P
and
Q
are injective on
\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)
(see Theorem
3.1
(
4
)) there are unique linear maps
\rho^{+}_{QP},\rho^{-}_{PQ}:\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)\to\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)
such that
\lambda^{+}_{QP}=\mathrm{id}+P\rho^{+}_{QP}
,
\lambda^{-}_{PQ}=\mathrm{id}+Q\rho^{-}_{PQ}
, both of which commute with the complex involution.

In particular,
\lambda^{-}_{PQ}\lambda^{+}_{QP}=\lambda^{+}_{QP}+Q\rho^{-}_{PQ}\lambda^{+}_{QP}
entailing

E_{Q}^{-}\lambda^{+}_{QP}=E_{Q}^{-}\lambda^{-}_{PQ}\lambda^{+}_{QP}-\rho^{-}_{PQ}\lambda^{+}_{QP}

(5.42)

and consequently

E_{Q}\lambda^{+}_{QP}=E_{Q}^{-}\lambda^{-}_{PQ}\lambda^{+}_{QP}-\rho^{-}_{PQ}\lambda^{+}_{QP}-E_{Q}^{+}\lambda^{+}_{QP}.

(5.43)

Noting that
Q
and
P
agree on the first and last terms on the right-hand side of (
5.43
), which are supported in
M^{-}
and
M^{+}
respectively, one has

(Q-P)E_{Q}\lambda^{+}_{QP}=-(Q-P)\rho^{-}_{PQ}\lambda^{+}_{QP}

(5.44)

and by applying
Q
to (
5.43
) and rearranging,

\displaystyle\lambda^{-}_{PQ}\lambda^{+}_{QP}

\displaystyle=QE_{Q}^{+}\lambda^{+}_{QP}+Q\rho^{-}_{PQ}\lambda^{+}_{QP}=\lambda^{+}_{QP}-(Q-P)E_{Q}\lambda^{+}_{QP}+P\rho^{-}_{PQ}\lambda^{+}_{QP}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{id}-(Q-P)E_{Q}\lambda^{+}_{QP}+P(\rho^{+}_{QP}+\rho^{-}_{PQ}\lambda^{+}_{QP}),

(5.45)

by definition of
\rho_{QP}^{+}
.
By Lemma
3.4
(b),
\lambda^{-}_{PQ}\lambda^{+}_{QP}\sim\vartheta:=\mathrm{id}-(Q-P)E_{Q}\lambda^{+}_{QP}
,
giving
\lambda^{+}_{QP}\sim\lambda^{-}_{QP}\vartheta
because

\lambda_{QP}^{-}\lambda_{PQ}^{-}\sim\mathrm{id}
.
Therefore
\hat{\vartheta}=(\hat{\lambda}^{-}_{QP})^{-1}\hat{\lambda}^{+}_{QP}
.
It remains to compute
\vartheta f
for
f\in\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B|_{M^{+}})
. Here, we use the fact that
\lambda^{+}_{QP}f-f=P\rho^{+}_{QP}f\in\ker E_{P}\cap\Gamma^{\infty}_{0}(B|_{M^{+}})
, moreover,
\rho^{+}_{QP}f\in\Gamma^{\infty}_{0}(B|_{M^{+}})
by Theorem
3.1
(
5
) and causal convexity of
M^{+}
. Consequently,
\lambda^{+}_{QP}f-f=P\rho^{+}_{QP}f\in Q\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)
and so
E_{Q}\lambda^{+}_{QP}f=E_{Q}f
. Thus

\vartheta f=f-(Q-P)E_{Q}f
for
f\in\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B|_{M^{+}})
, and as
Q
and
P
agree on the support of
E^{+}_{Q}f
, one has
\vartheta f=f-(Q-P)E_{Q}^{-}f
.
∎

If
V\subset M
is temporally compact with
U\subset V
, then
\vartheta
is evidently also a scattering morphism for
Q
relative to
P
with coupling zone
V
with the properties of Theorem
5.21
. In this sense,
\vartheta
is independent of the coupling zone.

The main result of this subsection is that the Hadamard condition is preserved under scattering.
Recall from Section
3
that when two RFHGHO’s
P
and
Q
agree outside a temporally compact set there is an associated scattering
\mathsf{GreenHyp}
-automorphism
\vartheta
of
P
that describes the scattering of
Q
relative to
P
and which then lifts to an automorphism
\mathscr{Y}(\vartheta)
of the QFT algebra
\mathscr{Y}(P)
.

Theorem 5.22
.

Let
P
and
Q
be RFHGHO’s on hermitian vector bundle
B
over
{\boldsymbol{M}}\in\mathsf{Loc}
and suppose that
P
and
Q
agree outside a temporally compact set
U\subset{\boldsymbol{M}}
. Let
\vartheta
be a scattering morphism of
Q
relative to
P
and define the scattering automorphism of

\mathscr{Y}(P)
by
\Theta=\mathscr{Y}(\vartheta)
. If
P
and
Q
are
\mathcal{V}_{P}^{\pm}
- and
\mathcal{V}_{Q}^{\pm}
-decomposable and
\mathcal{V}_{P}^{+}|_{M\setminus U}=\mathcal{V}_{Q}^{+}|_{M\setminus U}
then

\Theta^{*}
is an isomorphism on the set of
\mathcal{V}_{P}^{+}
-Hadamard states.

Proof.

From Section
3
,
\vartheta\sim\lambda^{-}_{PQ}\lambda^{+}_{QP}

where
\lambda^{-}_{PQ}
and
\lambda^{+}_{QP}
are
\mathsf{GreenHyp}
-morphisms of the type given by
Theorem
3.5
(e) and are Cauchy and regular. Then

\Theta=\mathscr{Y}(\lambda^{-}_{PQ})\mathscr{Y}(\lambda^{+}_{QP})
by Theorem
3.6
(b)
and so
\Theta^{*}
maps
\mathcal{V}_{P}^{+}
-Hadamard states to
\mathcal{V}_{P}^{+}
-Hadamard states. As

\Theta^{-1}=\mathscr{Y}(\lambda^{+}_{PQ})\mathscr{Y}(\lambda^{-}_{QP})
, the same argument shows that

\Theta^{*}
is an isomorphism of the set of
\mathcal{V}_{P}^{+}
-Hadamard states.
∎

5.6
Tensor products and partial traces

We now prove a number of useful properties of Hadamard states, which will be used in Section
6
in an application to the theory of measurement.
Parts (a) and (b) of the following result show how the Hadamard condition extends to composite theories, while (c) relates to partial traces.

Theorem 5.23
.

Let
P
and
Q
be RFHGHOs, and suppose that
P
is
\mathcal{V}^{\pm}_{P}
-decomposable while
Q
is
\mathcal{V}^{\pm}_{Q}
-decomposable, and
\mathcal{V}_{P}^{+}\cap\mathcal{V}_{Q}^{-}=\emptyset
. Then:

1.

P\oplus Q
is
\mathcal{V}_{P\oplus Q}^{\pm}
-decomposable, where
\mathcal{V}_{P\oplus Q}^{\pm}=\mathcal{V}_{P}^{\pm}\cup\mathcal{V}_{Q}^{\pm}
;

2.

if
\omega
is a
\mathcal{V}_{P}^{+}
-Hadamard state on
\mathscr{Y}(P)
and
\sigma
is a
\mathcal{V}_{Q}^{+}
-Hadamard state on
\mathscr{Y}(Q)
, then
\omega\otimes\sigma
is a
\mathcal{V}_{P\oplus Q}^{+}
-Hadamard state on
\mathscr{Y}(P\oplus Q)
;

3.

if
\varphi
is a
\mathcal{V}_{P\oplus Q}^{+}
-Hadamard state on
\mathscr{Y}(P\oplus Q)=\mathscr{Y}(P)\otimes\mathscr{Y}(Q)
, then
the partial traces
\varphi_{P}\in\mathscr{Y}(P)^{*}_{+,1}
and
\varphi_{Q}\in\mathscr{Y}(Q)^{*}_{+,1}
given by
\varphi_{P}(A)=\varphi(A\otimes 1\!\!1_{\mathscr{Y}(Q)})
,
\varphi_{Q}(B)=\varphi(1\!\!1_{\mathscr{Y}(P)}\otimes B)
, are respectively
\mathcal{V}_{P}^{+}
-Hadamard and
\mathcal{V}_{Q}^{+}
-Hadamard.

Inspection of the proof shows that the result adapts immediately to the case where

P
and
Q
are fermionic RFHGHO’s; part (a) only requires that
P
and
Q
are semi-Green-hyperbolic.

Proof.

(a) First note that
\mathcal{V}_{P\oplus Q}^{+}\cap\mathcal{V}_{P\oplus Q}^{-}=\emptyset
because the same is true of
\mathcal{V}_{P}^{\pm}
and
\mathcal{V}_{Q}^{\pm}
, and
\mathcal{V}_{P}^{+}\cap\mathcal{V}_{Q}^{-}=\emptyset
.
As
E_{P\oplus Q}=E_{P}\oplus E_{Q}
, we have

\WF^{\prime}(E_{P\oplus Q})\subset\WF^{\prime}(E_{P})\cup\WF^{\prime}(E_{Q})\subset\bigcup_{\sharp=\pm}((\mathcal{V}_{P}^{\sharp}\times\mathcal{V}_{P}^{\sharp})\cup(\mathcal{V}_{Q}^{\sharp}\times\mathcal{V}_{Q}^{\sharp}))\subset\bigcup_{\sharp=\pm}(\mathcal{V}_{P\oplus Q}^{\sharp}\times\mathcal{V}_{P\oplus Q}^{\sharp}),

(5.46)

which is the decomposability condition.

(b) The
n
-point functions of
\omega\otimes\sigma
on
\mathscr{Y}(P\oplus Q)
may be written as sums of products of
r
-point functions of
\omega
and
\sigma
for
1\leq r\leq n
, and are easily seen to be distributions. To simplify notation we write every
n
-point function of
\omega
as
W_{\omega}
, distinguishing them by their domains, and similarly for the
n
-point functions of
\sigma
. Then
the
2
-point function of
\omega\otimes\sigma
is given by

W_{\omega\otimes\sigma}(({f}\oplus{h})\otimes({f^{\prime}}\oplus{h^{\prime}}))=W_{\omega}({f}\otimes{f^{\prime}})+W_{\sigma}({h}\otimes{h^{\prime}})+W_{\omega}({f})W_{\sigma}(h^{\prime})+W_{\omega}(f^{\prime})W_{\sigma}(h).

(5.47)

As the
1
-point functions are smooth by Theorem
5.4
(d), we have

\WF(W_{\omega\otimes\sigma})\subset\WF(W_{\omega})\cup\WF(W_{\sigma})\subset(\mathcal{V}_{P}^{+}\times\mathcal{V}_{P}^{-})\cup(\mathcal{V}_{Q}^{+}\times\mathcal{V}_{Q}^{-})\subset\mathcal{V}_{P\oplus Q}^{+}\times\mathcal{V}_{P\oplus Q}^{-},

(5.48)

so
\omega\otimes\sigma
is
\mathcal{V}_{P\oplus Q}^{+}
-Hadamard.

(c) It is enough to show that
\varphi_{P}
is
\mathcal{V}_{P}^{+}
-Hadamard.
As
\Upsilon_{P}({f})\otimes 1\!\!1_{\mathscr{Y}_{Q}(M)}=\Upsilon_{P\oplus Q}({f}\oplus 0)
, it is clear that
\varphi_{P}
has distributional
n
-point functions. In particular, the two-point function
W_{P}
of
\varphi_{P}
is
given by

W_{P}({f}\otimes{h})=W(({f}\oplus 0)\otimes({h}\oplus 0)),

(5.49)

where
W
is the two-point function of
\varphi
.
It follows that
\WF(W_{P})\subset\WF(W)\subset\mathcal{V}_{P\oplus Q}^{+}\times\mathcal{V}_{P\oplus Q}^{-}
. Now
P
is
\mathcal{V}_{P\oplus Q}^{\pm}
-decomposable (as well as
\mathcal{V}_{P}^{\pm}
-decomposable), so we have shown that
W_{P}
is

\mathcal{V}_{P\oplus Q}^{+}
-Hadamard. Using Theorem
5.4
(a) we have

\WF(W)\subset(\mathcal{V}_{P\oplus Q}^{+}\times\mathcal{V}_{P\oplus Q}^{-})\cap\WF(E_{P})\subset\mathcal{V}_{P}^{+}\times\mathcal{V}_{P}^{-}
using the
\mathcal{V}_{P}^{\pm}
-decomposability of
E_{P}
. Therefore
W_{P}
is
\mathcal{V}_{P}^{+}
-Hadamard. ∎

5.7
Higher
n
-point functions

Let
\omega
be a
\mathcal{V}^{+}
-Hadamard state on
\mathscr{Y}(P)
for
\mathcal{V}^{\pm}
-decomposable RFHGHO
P
and define the
n
-point functions

W^{(n)}\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(B^{\boxtimes n})
as described above. The truncated
n
-point functions
W^{(n)}_{T}
are defined recursively by

W^{(n)}(F_{I})=\sum_{\mathcal{P}\in\mathscr{P}(I)}\prod_{J\in\mathcal{P}}W^{(|J|)}_{T}(F_{J}),\qquad W^{(1)}_{T}=W^{(1)},

(5.50)

where
I
is a finite ordered set,
\mathscr{P}(I)
is the set of partitions of
I
into nonempty ordered subsets and
F_{J}
is the ordered list of
F_{j}\in\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B^{*}\otimes\Omega)
for
j\in J
. A useful identity (see
[
11
]
, section 5.2.3) is, for any fixed
j\in I
,

W^{(|I|)}(F_{I})=\sum_{j\in J\subset I}W^{(|J|)}_{T}(F_{J})W^{(|I\setminus J|)}(F_{I\setminus J})

(5.51)

(i.e., one sums over all
J
obeying
j\in J\subset I
for the given
j
and
I
) with
W^{(0)}=1
by convention. A quasifree state is one whose truncated
n
-point functions vanish except for
n=2
.

Truncated
n
-point functions can be defined for
even states
on
\mathscr{X}(P)
, i.e., those whose
n
-point functions all vanish for all odd
n
. They are defined recursively by

W^{(n)}(F_{I})=\sum_{\mathcal{P}\in\mathscr{P}(I)}\varepsilon(\mathcal{P})\prod_{J\in\mathcal{P}}W^{(|J|)}_{T}(F_{J}),

(5.52)

where

\varepsilon(\mathcal{P})
is the signature of the permutation of
I
that results in
(J_{1},\ldots J_{|\mathcal{P}|})
, where
\mathcal{P}=\{J_{1},\ldots,J_{|\mathcal{P}|}\}
(the ordering of the
J_{k}
’s is irrelevant).
It is sufficient to sum over only partitions into sets of even length. The analogue of (
5.51
)
is

W^{(|I|)}(F_{I})=\sum_{j\in J\subset I}\varepsilon(J,I\setminus J)W^{(|J|)}_{T}(F_{J})W^{(|I\setminus J|)}(F_{I\setminus J}),

(5.53)

with
\varepsilon(J,I\setminus J)
equal to the signature of the permutation from
I
to
(J,I\setminus J)
,
and
W^{(0)}=1
as before. The quasifree states on
\mathscr{X}(P)
are those even states whose truncated
n
-point functions vanish for
n\neq 2
.

The class of states with Hadamard two-point functions and smooth truncated
n
-point functions for
n\neq 2
was identified as a plausible class of physical states in
[
66
]
, where the question was raised as to how close this was to the Hadamard condition.
This is answered by a result of Sanders
[
85
]
, which we now adapt and generalise, slightly streamlining in the process.

Theorem 5.24
.

Let
\omega
be a
\mathcal{V}^{+}
-Hadamard state on
\mathscr{Y}(P)
or an even state on
\mathscr{X}(P)
. Then all its truncated
n
-point functions for
n\neq 2
are smooth, and
\WF(W^{(2)}_{T})=\WF(W^{(2)})
.

Proof.

We give the argument for the bosonic case first. Smoothness of the
1
-point functions was proved in Corollary
5.10
from which it follows that
W^{(2)}_{T}-W^{(2)}
is smooth, so we can restrict to showing smoothness of
W^{(n)}_{T}
for
n\geq 3
.

For some
n\geq 3
, suppose that
W^{(k)}_{T}
is smooth for
2<k<n
, which is trivially true for
n=3
. Suppose that
W^{(n)}_{T}
is not smooth, in which case there must be

(\underline{x},\underline{k})\in\WF(W^{(n)}_{T})
with
k_{j}\neq 0
for some
1\leq j\leq n
. Considering (
5.51
), we deduce that

(\underline{x},\underline{k})\in\WF(R_{n,j})
where

R_{n,j}(F_{I})=W^{(n)}(F_{I})-\sum_{i<j}W^{(2)}(F_{i},F_{j})W^{(n-2)}(F_{I\setminus\{i,j\}})-\sum_{i>j}W^{(2)}(F_{j},F_{i})W^{(n-2)}(F_{I\setminus\{i,j\}})

(5.54)

because all other terms are smooth with respect to
x_{j}
,
and
W^{(2)}_{T}-W^{(2)}
is smooth.
Now, the commutation relations and
{\rm i}E_{P}=W^{(2)}-{\vphantom{W}}^{t}W^{(2)}
imply that

R_{n,j}=R_{n,1}\circ\tau_{1j}=R_{n,n}\circ\tau_{jn},

(5.55)

where
\tau_{ij}
transposes the
i
’th and
j
’th arguments. Consequently,

\tau_{1j}(\underline{x},\underline{k})\in\WF(R_{n,1})
and

\tau_{jn}(\underline{x},\underline{k})\in\WF(R_{n,n})
.

We now employ Cauchy–Schwarz estimates, splitting off the first and last arguments, to deduce that

\WF(W^{(n)})\subset\mathcal{V}_{0}^{+}\times T^{*}M^{\times(n-1)}
and

\WF(W^{(n)})\subset T^{*}M^{\times(n-1)}\times\mathcal{V}_{0}^{-}
. It follows from (
5.54
) and

\WF(W^{(2)})\subset\mathcal{V}^{+}\times\mathcal{V}^{-}
that
\WF(R_{n,1})\subset\mathcal{V}_{0}^{+}\times T^{*}M^{\times(n-1)}
and

\WF(R_{n,n})\subset T^{*}M^{\times(n-1)}\times\mathcal{V}_{0}^{-}
. Therefore
(x_{j},k_{j})\in\mathcal{V}_{0}^{+}\cap\mathcal{V}_{0}^{-}=\{0\}
, contradicting
k_{j}\neq 0
.
Thus
W^{(n)}_{T}
is smooth and by induction this holds for all
n\geq 3
.

The argument for the fermionic case is similar, with
R_{n,j}

modified by the insertion of a factor
(-1)^{i+j-1}
in both sums, and the use
of the anticommutation relations to obtain

R_{n,j}=(-1)^{j-1}R_{n,1}\circ\tau_{1j}=(-1)^{n-j}R_{n,n}\circ\tau_{jn}
.
∎

The next result is obtained by employing the formulae (
5.51
) and (
5.53
), together with the standard estimates for wavefront sets of sums and tensor products of distributions.

Corollary 5.25
.

Under the same assumptions as Theorem
5.24
,

\displaystyle\WF(W^{(n)})

\displaystyle\subset\bigcup_{Q}\{(x_{1},k_{1};\ldots;x_{n},k_{n})\in T^{*}M:\textnormal{$k_{j}=0$ for $j\notin\bigcup Q$,}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad(x_{\min(r)},k_{\min(r)};x_{\max(r)},k_{\max(r)})\in\WF_{0}(W^{(2)})~\textnormal{for each $r\in Q$}\},

(5.56)

where
Q
runs over all sets of disjoint two-element subsets of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

The bounding set in (
5.56
) is the standard bound for quasifree Hadamard states
[
14
]
.
In this sense, all
\mathcal{V}^{+}
-Hadamard states are ‘microlocally quasifree’.

5.8
Polarisation sets

To close this section, we briefly consider the polarisation set of

\mathcal{V}^{+}
-Hadamard two-point functions. The polarisation set was introduced by Dencker
[
19
]
and the conventions and notation used here are as in the companion paper
[
29
]
. An
elementary application of the general theory provides a refinement of Theorem
5.4
(a).

Theorem 5.26
.

Suppose
P
is a
\mathcal{V}^{\pm}
-decomposable RFHGHO, where
\mathcal{V}^{\pm}
are as in Definition
5.2
. If
W^{\textnormal{2pt}}
is the
2
-point function of a
\mathcal{V}^{+}
-Hadamard state on the bosonic algebra
\mathscr{Y}(P)
, then

\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}((\rho^{1/2}\otimes\rho^{1/2})W^{\textnormal{2pt}})=(1\otimes\flat)\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(E_{\rho^{1/2}P\rho^{-1/2}}^{\textnormal{knl}})|_{\mathcal{V}^{+}\times\mathcal{V}^{-}}\cup 0.

(5.57)

If
(P,R)
is a fermionic RFHGHO and
W^{\textnormal{2pt}}
is the
2
-point function of a
\mathcal{V}^{+}
-Hadamard state on the fermionic algebra
\mathscr{X}(P)
, then

\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}((\rho^{1/2}\otimes\rho^{1/2})W^{\textnormal{2pt}})=(1\otimes(R\circ\flat))\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(E_{\rho^{1/2}P\rho^{-1/2}}^{\textnormal{knl}})|_{\mathcal{V}^{+}\times\mathcal{V}^{-}}\cup 0.

(5.58)

Proof.

In the bosonic case, one first calculates that

\displaystyle(\rho^{1/2}\otimes\rho^{1/2})(W^{\textnormal{2pt}}-{\vphantom{W}}^{t}W^{\textnormal{2pt}})

\displaystyle={\rm i}E_{P}^{\textnormal{knl}}\circ(\rho^{1/2}\otimes(\flat\circ\rho^{-1/2}))

\displaystyle={\rm i}(\rho^{1/2}E_{P}\rho^{-1/2})^{\textnormal{knl}}\circ(1\otimes(\rho^{1/2}\circ\flat\circ\rho^{-1/2}))

\displaystyle={\rm i}(1\otimes(\rho^{1/2}\circ\flat\circ\rho^{-1/2}))E_{\rho^{1/2}P\rho^{-1/2}}^{\textnormal{knl}},

(5.59)

where we use the fact that
\rho^{1/2}E_{P}\rho^{-1/2}=E_{\rho^{1/2}P\rho^{-1/2}}
as operators. Thus

\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}((\rho^{1/2}\otimes\rho^{1/2})(W^{\textnormal{2pt}}-{\vphantom{W}}^{t}W^{\textnormal{2pt}}))=(1\otimes\flat)\WF_{\textnormal{pol}}(E_{\rho^{1/2}P\rho^{-1/2}}^{\textnormal{knl}}),

(5.60)

where we have used a standard result on polarisation sets (see section 2 of
[
19
]
or Lemma 3.1 of
[
29
]
) and the fact that
\flat
is an isomorphism.
As the wavefront sets of
W^{\textnormal{2pt}}
and
{\vphantom{W}}{}^{t}W^{\textnormal{2pt}}
intersect trivially, their polarisation sets sum to that on the right-hand side, and the restriction to
\mathcal{V}^{+}\times\mathcal{V}^{-}
therefore gives the polarisation set of
(\rho^{1/2}\otimes\rho^{1/2})W^{\textnormal{2pt}}
modulo the zero section.

For the fermionic case, (
5.59
) is replaced by

(\rho^{1/2}\otimes\rho^{1/2})(W^{\textnormal{2pt}}+{\vphantom{W}}^{t}W^{\textnormal{2pt}})={\rm i}(1\otimes(\rho^{1/2}\circ R\circ\flat\circ\rho^{-1/2})^{\prime})E_{\rho^{1/2}P\rho^{-1/2}}^{\textnormal{knl}}

(5.61)

due to the anticommutation relations. Noting that the symbols of
(\rho^{1/2}\circ R\circ\flat\circ\rho^{-1/2})^{\prime}
and

R
coincide up to sign (recall that
R=-{\vphantom{R}}^{t}R
), the result follows.
∎

FP-Hadamard states

The FP definition of a Hadamard state on

-bisolution

has antisymmetric part

By deformation arguments this implies existence on general globally hyperbolic spacetimes whose Cauchy surfaces are compact and obey the homology condition. This was proved in Section IV.E of

The MMV definition is that a Hadamard state is a quasifree state with two-point function

\textnormal{quasifree FP-Hadamard}\implies\textnormal{MMV-Hadamard}\iff\textnormal{quasifree $\mathcal{N}_{\boldsymbol{M}}^{+}$-Hadamard}.

(7.15)

Proof.

The forward implication has already been mentioned as a consequence of nonexpansion of the wavefront set under differential operators. Suppose that
\omega
is
\mathcal{N}^{+}_{\boldsymbol{M}}
-Hadamard, with two-point function
W
. We will construct a Hadamard form bisolution

satisfying (

). An important observation is that

W
is itself a
K_{\boldsymbol{M}}
-bisolution obeying

and
\WF(W)=\mathcal{R}_{\boldsymbol{M}}^{\mathrm{Had}}
.
This is seen because
(1\otimes P_{\boldsymbol{M}})W=0

implies
(1\otimes\delta_{\boldsymbol{M}})W=0
and hence

and
(1\otimes K_{\boldsymbol{M}})W=0
– a manifestation of the fact that

projects

-solutions onto

-solutions. Similarly we also have
(K_{\boldsymbol{M}}\otimes 1)W=0
, so

is a
K_{\boldsymbol{M}}
-bisolution. However

is not of Hadamard form, because it has antisymmetric part
\tfrac{1}{2}{\rm i}E_{P_{\boldsymbol{M}}}^{\textnormal{2pt}}
rather than
\tfrac{1}{2}{\rm i}E_{K_{\boldsymbol{M}}}^{\textnormal{2pt}}
.

To obtain the required Hadamard form bisolution

, we will construct an additional
K_{\boldsymbol{M}}
-bisolution with the same wavefront set, that does not alter (
7.13
) and has antisymmetric part
\tfrac{1}{2}{\rm i}(E_{K_{M}}^{\textnormal{2pt}}-E_{P_{M}}^{\textnormal{2pt}})=\tfrac{1}{2}{\rm i}m^{-2}E_{K_{M}}^{\textnormal{2pt}}\circ(1\otimes{\rm d}_{\boldsymbol{M}}\delta_{\boldsymbol{M}})
(a smooth error could be tolerated, but will not be needed).
This is achieved by invoking an auxiliary scalar field
subject to the
0

K^{(0)}_{\boldsymbol{M}}=-\delta_{\boldsymbol{M}}{\rm d}_{\boldsymbol{M}}+m^{2}
on

\Lambda^{0}M
, i.e., the trivial line bundle

with bilinear pairing given by multiplication and the antilinear conjugation given by complex conjugation.
The corresponding quantised theory
\mathscr{Y}(K^{(0)}_{\boldsymbol{M}})
certainly admits Hadamard states
[

48
]
(or by Theorem

) and we choose any such state
\omega^{(0)}
with corresponding two-point function

One has

and
W^{(0)}-{\vphantom{W}}^{t}W^{(0)}={\rm i}E^{\textnormal{2pt}}_{K_{\boldsymbol{M}}^{(0)}}
. Because
{\rm d}_{\boldsymbol{M}}K_{\boldsymbol{M}}^{(0)}=K_{\boldsymbol{M}}{\rm d}_{\boldsymbol{M}}
, one has

The last statement holds because all MMV-Hadamard states are

8
Conclusion

We have given a detailed treatment of Hadamard states for a wide class of bosonic and fermionic theories and demonstrated how many standard properties can be generalised using our approach.
In addition, we have given an application to measurement theory
[
41
]
, by showing that nonselective state updates of Hadamard states are Hadamard, and proved the complete equivalence of the MMV definition of Hadamard states for the Proca field
[
79
]
to the FP definition
[
33
]
.
The general results proved here should form a useful toolbox for further applications. Indeed, our definition has already been employed in a treatment of functionals for perturbative algebraic QFT in
[
52
]
. To conclude, we consider some other potential directions.

First, our discussion of bosonic theories has focussed on hermitian quantum field theories.
However, it is straightforward to encompass complex fields as is sketched for bosonic fields in Appendix
B
, by ‘doubling’ them to hermitian theories. In the fermionic case, a similar doubling procedure was used for Dirac-type operators in Section
4.3
.
Second, one could develop an extended algebra of normal ordered products
[
55
]
which contains the stress-energy tensor and other similar observables; this can then form the basis for Epstein–Glaser renormalisation theory
[
13
,
55
,
56
,
82
,
24
]
for models based on decomposable Green-hyperbolic operators. Third, it would be natural to extend our treatment to theories with gauge freedom by means such as
[
51
]
(which abstracts
methods used in e.g.
[
21
,
33
,
30
,
16
,
88
]
to the level of Green-hyperbolic operators). Fourth, one could switch from the smooth wavefront set to its analytic cousin, whereupon results such as the timelike tube theorem and Reeh–Schlieder theorem would be expected for analytically decomposable Green hyperbolic operators satisfying the analytic wavefront set condition

\WF_{A}(E_{P})\subset(\mathcal{V}^{+}\times\mathcal{V}^{-})\cup(\mathcal{V}^{-}\times\mathcal{V}^{+})
using methods from
[
92
,
93
]
. Fifth, in terms of measurement theory, an important question is to understand which selective measurements correspond to update rules that preserve the Hadamard class.

Finally, we have not discussed the Hadamard parametrix in the general setting, relying entirely on the microlocal formalism. Partly that is because the motivation for constructing a parametrix is most appropriate for a generally covariant theory – where one can aim for a description reducing to that of the Minkowski space theory at short scales – and we treat operators that need not have a covariant description in all spacetimes. Nonetheless, as all two-point functions of Hadamard states have smooth differences, finding a parametrix is related to solving a cohomological problem
[
15
]
and it would be interesting to determine for which Green-hyperbolic operators this can be achieved.

Appendix B
Complex fields

Let
{\boldsymbol{M}}\in\mathsf{Loc}
and suppose
B
is a finite-rank complex vector bundle over
{\boldsymbol{M}}
equipped with a hermitian fibre metric, but not necessarily with a complex conjugation. The hermitian metric gives an antilinear isomorphism of
B
to
B^{*}
(or sections thereof) written
f\mapsto f^{\star}
, so that
\langle\!\langle f^{\star},h\rangle\!\rangle=(f,h)
. We use the same notation for the inverse isomorphism, and define a hermitian fibre metric on
B^{*}
by

(u,v)_{B^{*}}=(v^{\star},u^{\star})_{B}
. Next, we endow
B\oplus B^{*}
with the direct sum hermitian fibre metric and a complex conjugation
\mathcal{C}
given by

\mathcal{C}\begin{pmatrix}f\\
u\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}u^{\star}\\
f^{\star}\end{pmatrix},

(B.1)

which makes
B\oplus B^{*}
into a hermitian vector bundle in
\mathsf{HVB}
.
If
P
is a formally hermitian Green hyperbolic operator (FHGHO) on
\Gamma^{\infty}(B)
, it is easily shown that
{\vphantom{P}}{}^{\star}P
is formally hermitian on
\Gamma^{\infty}(B^{*})
and
P\oplus{\vphantom{P}}^{\star}P
is a RFHGHO on
\Gamma^{\infty}(B\oplus B^{*})
.

We quantise the theory as
\mathscr{Z}(P)=\mathscr{Y}(P\oplus{\vphantom{P}}^{\star}P)
, also introducing
complex fields

\Phi_{P}(u)=\Upsilon_{P\oplus{\vphantom{P}}^{\star}P}(0\oplus u),\qquad\Phi^{\star}_{P}(f)=\Upsilon_{P\oplus{\vphantom{P}}^{\star}P}(f\oplus 0)

(B.2)

for
u\in\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B^{*})
,
f\in\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)
, which clearly generate
\mathscr{Z}(P)
. It is easily seen that the following relations are satisfied:

1.

u\mapsto\Phi_{P}(u)
and
f\mapsto\Phi^{\star}_{P}(f)
are complex linear

2.

\Phi^{\star}_{P}(f)=\Phi_{P}(f^{\star})^{*}

3.

\Phi_{P}({\vphantom{P}}^{\star}Pu)=0=\Phi_{P}^{\star}(Pf)

4.

commutation relations

[\Phi_{P}(u),\Phi^{\star}_{P}(f)]={\rm i}\langle\!\langle u,E_{P}f\rangle\!\rangle 1\!\!1_{\mathscr{Z}(P)},\qquad[\Phi_{P}(u),\Phi_{P}(v)]=[\Phi^{\star}_{P}(f),\Phi^{\star}_{P}(h)]=0

f\in\Gamma_{0}^{\infty}(B)

\Phi_{P}(u)
and

for

,

Specialising, if

Specialising further, any RFHGHO
P

\displaystyle W_{\Phi\Phi}(u^{\natural,1}\otimes v^{\natural,1})

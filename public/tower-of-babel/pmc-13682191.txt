In the thermal industry, the operational characteristics of heating equipment are often controlled by artificial connections, assess the efficiency of radiator systems and accelerate the boiling and investigation procedures. As a result, scientists have employed ANNs (artificial neural networks) to analysis thermal transfer data, keep an eye on the heating procedure and compute the heat transmission coefficient. One crucial step in the central heating or cooling of a particular entity is the transmission of thermal energy from one place to each other. To guarantee the efficient and reliable operation of a device or system at peak performance conditions, the excessive thermal energy generated within it must be reduced and maintained within acceptable temperature limits. Furthermore, LTNE analysis helps to develop better diagnostic approaches for monitoring temperature distribution in stenosed arteries, which improves the diagnosis and treatment of cardiovascular disease. This is especially significant in therapies that involve regulated heating, such as clot dissolution and cancer treatment using hyperthermia. Artificial neural network

The proposed ANN architecture consists of one input neuron, one HL (hidden layer) consisting of fifteen neurons, and one output neuron. While the HL employs the tangent sigmoid (tansig) AF, the output layer uses a linear (purelin) activation function to offer accurate prediction of the continuous output variables. In the fields of science, engineering, and mathematics, machine learning algorithms (MLAs) have become potent computational tools for solving challenging issues. By creating connections between inputs and intended outputs, supervised learning algorithms (SLAs) allow neural networks (NNs) to learn from labeled datasets. In order to diminish the difference among target and predicted values and increase prediction accuracy, the network iteratively modifies its weights using error back-propagation throughout the training phase. Conclusions

There are significant biomedical and technological ramifications to the machine learning-based simulation of SDE on mediated blood HNF flow in a stenosed artery using the LTNE model. It can aid in predicting and optimizing drug delivery since nanoparticles in blood can be steered more efficiently by temperature and concentration gradients. The model also helps to comprehend heat and mass transmission in stenosed arteries, which is important for developing medical therapies such as hyperthermia therapy and preventing arterial obstructions. It also offers insights into biomedical device design, such as stents or targeted nanofluid-based cooling systems, by correctly simulating blood flow under complex temperature and concentration conditions. The study's robustness is further increased by using an ANN (artificial neural network) to learn and replicate the complex nonlinear correlations obtained from the numerical solutions. The trained network significantly reduces processing time as a predictive surrogate model without compromising accuracy. The subsequent are the study's key results:❖To accurately assess and forecast numerical outputs using a Levenberg-Marquardt neural network.❖Increasing the interphase heat transmission parameter results in a higher heat profile for the liquid phase as well as a lower thermal profile for the solid phase.❖The thermal profile decreased as well as the blood HNF velocity profile increased as the velocity factors value increased.❖Thermal, concentration, mass transmission, and rate of temperature all rises as the Soret and Dufour numbers rise.❖Model correctness is assessed using regression analysis, absolute error, comparison solution, and error distribution measures.❖As the interphase thermal transmission factors increases in value, the rate of thermal transmission in the dense phase decreases.

The development of targeted therapies, treatments for hyperthermia, and the design of biomedical nanodevices may benefit from the more precise prediction of heat and mass transport phenomena made possible by the integration of thermophoretic particle deposition and the Yamada–Ota thermal conductivity model.

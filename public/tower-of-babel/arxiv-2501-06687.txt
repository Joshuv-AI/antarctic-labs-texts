Precise measurement of CP violating τ \tau EDM through e + ​ e − → γ ∗ , ψ ⁡ ( 2 ​ s ) → τ + ​ τ − e^{+}e^{-}\to\gamma^{*},\psi(2s)\to\tau^{+}\tau^{-} arXiv: 2501.06687
Xiao-Gang He, Chia-Wei Liu, Jian-Ping Ma, Chang Yang, Zi-Yue Zou

Abstract
A nonzero electric dipole moment of a tauon, d τ d_{\tau} , signals CP violation and provides an important probe for new physics. We study methods to measure d τ d_{\tau} at low energy e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} colliders through the processes e + ​ e − → γ ∗ , ψ ⁡ ( 2 ​ S ) → τ + ​ τ − e^{+}e^{-}\to\gamma^{*},\psi(2S)\to\tau^{+}\tau^{-} with τ ± \tau^{\pm} decays into a charged hadron and a tau neutrino. We point out that, with measuring energies of the charged hadron, Im ( d τ ) (d_{\tau}) can be measured. On the other hand, selecting events of τ \tau decays after traveling more than the detector resolution distance, Re ( d τ ) (d_{\tau}) can also be determined. We find that the precision at Super Tau-Charm Facility (STCF) running at the center energy of m ψ ⁡ ( 2 ​ S ) m_{\psi(2S)} for 10 year data accumulation, the precision of Im ( d τ ) (d_{\tau}) and Re ( d τ ) (d_{\tau}) are found to be 1.8 and 11 in unit of 10 − 18 ​ e ​ cm 10^{-18}~e\,\text{cm} , respectively. The sensitivity for d τ d_{\tau} measurement precision at the STCF can be reached its optimum at a central energy of 6.3 ​ GeV 6.3~\text{GeV} , achieving a precision of 0.7 0.7 for Im ( d τ ) (d_{\tau}) and 2.8 2.8 for Re ( d τ ) (d_{\tau}) in unit of 10 − 18 ​ e ​ cm 10^{-18}~e\,\text{cm} .

4
Conclusion

Measuring
d_{\tau}
at future colliders offers a powerful probe of CP violation and potential new physics. We emphasize that reconstructing the full momentum of the
\tau^{-}
for
\mathrm{Re}(d_{\tau})
is critical—an important aspect that has been previously overlooked. At the STCF, the precision for both

\mathrm{Re}(d_{\tau})
and

\mathrm{Im}(d_{\tau})
reaches its peak at a center-of-mass energy of
6.3\,\mathrm{GeV}
, with attainable sensitivities of

2.8\times 10^{-18}\,e\,\mathrm{cm}
and
0.7\times 10^{-18}\,e\,\mathrm{cm},

respectively, improving the current precision by approximately a factor of two. Near the
\psi(2S)
resonance, the achievable sensitivities are

1.8\times 10^{-18}\,e\,\mathrm{cm}
and
11\times 10^{-18}\,e\,\mathrm{cm}

for
\mathrm{Im}(d_{\tau})
and
\mathrm{Re}(d_{\tau})
, respectively, indicating a moderate reduction in sensitivity at this energy.
On the other hand, BESIII has the capability to measure
\mathrm{Im}(d_{\tau})
with a precision that may reach
1.0\times 10^{-16}\,e\,\mathrm{cm}
.
These findings underscore the importance of careful momentum reconstruction and optimal energy selection for future
\tau
-EDM measurements.

Well-posedness and mean-field limit estimate of a consensus-based algorithm for multiplayer games
Hui Huang, Jethro Warnett

Abstract




Abstract.
Recently, the paper
[
12
]
introduces a derivative-free consensus-based particle method that finds the Nash equilibrium of non-convex multiplayer games, where it proves the global exponential convergence in the sense of mean-field law.
This paper aims to address theoretical gaps in
[
12
]
, specifically by providing a quantitative estimate of the mean-field limit with respect to the number of particles, as well as establishing the well-posedness of both the finite particle model and the corresponding mean-field dynamics.

Keywords
: mean-field limits, Interacting particle systems, Consensus-Based Optimization, Coupling methods, Wasserstein stability estimates, Nash equilibrium

MSC codes
: 35Q93, 65C35, 70F45, 60H30

Introduction

1.1
CBO for Multiplayer games

Multiplayer games
[
41
]
are found in various fields, from advertising
[
39
]
, to neuroeconomics
[
36
]
, and evolutionary biology
[
24
]
. In computer science, they are key to machine learning
[
43
,
15
]
, federated learning
[
17
]
, adversarial learning
[
38
,
46
]
, and reinforcement learning
[
14
,
37
]
.
In game theory, it is agreed that the optimal strategies of all players are the Nash-equilibrium points
[
42
]
, a point where no player can improve their outcome by changing strategy, assuming that the strategy of all other players is fixed. Several heuristic algorithms have been developed to find the Nash-equilibrium. Examples include the multiobjective particle swarm optimization method
[
26
,
45
]
(based on the particle swarm optimization algorithm
[
49
]
) has been used in conjunction with other algorithms, fictous play
[
3
]
and regret matching
[
27
]
.

Recently, a consensus based optimization (CBO) algorithm has been proposed to find the Nash-equilibrium of multiplayer games
[
12
]
. The CBO algorithm has been originally proposed in
[
44
,
9
]
, which is inspired by collective behavior in nature, such as flocking birds or swarming fish. It is used to solve optimization problems by simulating the interaction of particles that collectively move toward an optimal solution. The agents share information and adjust their positions based on a consensus mechanism, which drives the system toward a global optimum.
The CBO algorithm boasts many advantages such as being derivative free and being amenable to mathematical analysis, which has now been extended and adapted to address a wide variety of optimization settings. Notable extensions include global optimization on compact manifolds
[
25
]
, handling general constraints
[
2
,
7
]
, and optimizing cost functions with multiple minimizers
[
8
]
. Additionally, the CBO framework has been successfully applied to multi-objective problems
[
5
]
, min-max problems
[
6
,
32
]
, and high-dimensional machine learning tasks
[
10
,
19
]
. Further advancements include the integration of momentum
[
33
]
, memory effects
[
29
]
, as well as
jump-diffusion processes
[
34
]
.
Rather than attempting to include a necessarily incomplete account of this very fast growing field, we refer to the review paper
[
47
]
, and to
[
48
]
for a more recent and relatively comprehensive report.

The authors in
[
12
]
consider a multi-species CBO algorithm. We are given
M\geq 2
players, where each player is denoted by
m\in[M]:=\{1,\ldots,M\}
. All players posses a strategy
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{M})\in\mathbb{R}^{M\cdot d}
, where we denote the strategy of the
m
th player by
x_{m}
and the strategy of the opponents
x_{-m}:=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m-1},x_{m+1}.\ldots,x_{M})
. Every player minimizes their own cost function
\mathcal{E}_{m}(x_{m};x_{-m}):\mathbb{R}^{M\cdot d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, that depends on their current strategy and the strategy of the other players. The Nash-equilibrium of the multiplayer game is defined as any strategy
(x_{1}^{\ast},\ldots,x_{M}^{\ast})\in\mathbb{R}^{M\cdot d}
that satisfies, for all
m\in[M]
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{m}(x_{m}^{\ast};\,x_{-m}^{\ast})\leq\mathcal{E}_{m}(x;\,x_{-m}^{\ast})\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Each player
m
is attributed a collection of
N
particles
X^{m,1},\ldots,X^{m,N}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The strategy
x_{m}
is derived as the consensus of the particles

\displaystyle\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\rho^{m,N};\textbf{M}^{-m}):=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}x_{m}\;\omega_{\alpha}^{\mathcal{E}_{m}}(x_{m};\textbf{M}^{-m})\,\,\mathrm{d}\rho^{m,N}(x_{m})}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\alpha}^{\mathcal{E}_{m}}(x_{m};\textbf{M}^{-m})\,\,\mathrm{d}\rho^{m,N}(x_{m})},

(1)

where we define weight, particle distribution and player strategies by

\displaystyle\omega_{\alpha}^{\mathcal{E}_{m}}(x_{m};\textbf{M}^{-m}):=e^{-\alpha\mathcal{E}_{m}(x_{m};\,\textbf{M}^{-m})},\quad\rho^{m,N}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X^{m,i}},\quad\textbf{M}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(X^{1,i},\ldots,X^{M,i}\right).

The continuous CBO dynamics is expressed by an interacting particle evolution system represented by the following stochastic differential equation, for all
i\in[N]
and
m\in[M]
,

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}X_{t}^{m,i}=-\lambda(X_{t}^{m,i}-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\rho_{t}^{m,N},\textbf{M}_{t}^{-m}))\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma D(X_{t}^{m,i}-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\rho_{t}^{m,N},\textbf{M}_{t}^{-m}))\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}^{m,i},

(CBO)

where
\lambda,\sigma>0
are drift and diffusion parameters respectively,
\{B^{m,i}\}_{m\in[M],i\in[N]}
are independent standard Brownian motions and
D:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is Lipshitz transformation with respect to the Frobenius norm. For example
D(X)=\mathrm{diag}(X^{1},\ldots,X^{d})
(anisotropic) or
D(X)=|X|\cdot\id
(isotropic). The paper
[
12
]
formally states that the mean-field limit of the above particle dynamics is dictated by the following stochastic differential equation, for all
m\in[M]
,

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}\overline{X}_{t}^{m}=-\lambda(\overline{X}_{t}^{m}-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\overline{\rho}_{t}^{m},\overline{\textbf{M}}_{t}^{-m}))\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma D(\overline{X}_{t}^{m}-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\overline{\rho}_{t}^{m},\overline{\textbf{M}}_{t}^{-m}))\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}^{m,1},

(MF CBO)

where we have the law and expectation of the particles

\displaystyle\overline{\rho}_{t}^{m}:=\law\big(\,\overline{X}_{t}^{m}\big),\qquad\overline{\textbf{M}}_{t}:=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\overline{X}_{t}^{1},\ldots,\overline{X}_{t}^{M}\right)\right].

Applying Itô’s formula to the joint process
(\overline{X}^{1},\ldots,\overline{X}^{M})
shows that the following non-local Fokker-Planck equation holds in the weak sense

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\overline{\rho}_{t}=\lambda\sum_{m=1}^{M}\divv_{x_{m}}\big[(x_{m}-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\overline{\rho}_{t}^{m},\overline{\textbf{M}}_{t}^{-m}))\;\overline{\rho}_{t}\big]+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\partial_{(x_{m})_{k}}^{2}\big[(x_{m}-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\overline{\rho}_{t}^{m},\overline{\textbf{M}}_{t}^{-m}))_{k}^{2}\;\overline{\rho}_{t}\big],

where
\overline{\rho}
is the law of the joint distribution. For the analysis of the CBO-type PDE, we refer the readers to
[
50
]
.

1.2
Motivation

The authors
[
12
]
rigorously prove global convergence in the large particle limit. Specifically, they establish the convergence of the mean-field dynamics
(\overline{X}_{t}^{1},\dots,\overline{X}_{t}^{M})
in (
MF CBO
) to the global Nash equilibrium (NE) point
(x_{1}^{*},\dots,x_{M}^{*})
as
t
approaches infinity. To achieve this, they introduce the variance functions:

V^{m}(t)=\mathbb{E}\left[|\overline{X}_{t}^{m}-x_{m}^{*}|^{2}\right],\quad\text{for each}\ m\in[M],\quad\text{and}\quad V(t):=\sum_{m=1}^{M}V^{m}(t),\quad\text{for}\ t>0.

(2)

By analyzing the decay behavior of the (cumulative) variance function
V(t)
, they demonstrate that
V(t)
decays exponentially at a finite time
T_{*}>0
, with a decay rate that can be controlled through the parameters of the CBO method. Specifically, it holds that

V(t)\leq V(0)\exp\left(-\frac{2\lambda-\sigma^{2}}{2}t\right)

for
t\in[0,T_{*}]
, and
V(T_{*})\leq\varepsilon
for any given accuracy
\varepsilon>0
.

However, the justification for passing to the mean-field limit from the particle system (
CBO
) to the mean-field dynamics (
MF CBO
) remains only formal, and a rigorous mathematical analysis is absent in
[
12
]
. Establishing a rigorous proof of the CBO models has been a challenging task, primarily due to the fact that the consensus point defined in (
1
) is only locally Lipschitz. Several significant results regarding the mean-field limit for CBO have been achieved in recent years. For instance,
[
18
,
25
]
established mean-field limit estimates for variants of CBO constrained to compact manifolds by ensuring the consensus point is globally Lipschitz. Subsequently,
[
30
]
proved the mean-field limit for the standard CBO model using a compactness argument based on Prokhorov’s theorem. However, this approach does not provide an explicit convergence rate in terms of the number of particles
N
.
Further advancements were made in
[
20
,
31
]
, where the authors demonstrated a probabilistic mean-field approximation, showing that the mean-field limit estimate holds with high probability. The high-probability assumption was later removed by
[
22
]
, who established an improved stability estimate for the consensus point. Most recently, uniform-in-time type of mean-field estimates have been obtained
[
28
,
21
,
1
]
.

The primary objective of this paper is to address some theoretical gaps in
[
12
]
by providing a quantitative estimate of the mean-field limit with respect to the number
N
of particles. Additionally, we establish the well-posedness of both the finite particle model (
CBO
) and the mean-field dynamics (
MF CBO
).

1.3
Main Results

Throughout this paper we assume that the cost functions are sandwiched in between two polynomials and that the Lipshitz constants grow polynomially.

(A1)

There exists constants
C>0
and
s\geq 0
such that for all
(x_{0},y_{0}),(x_{1},y_{1})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{(M-1)\cdot d}
and
m\in[M]
we have the local Lipshitz property

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}_{m}(x_{0};y_{0})-\mathcal{E}_{m}(x_{1};y_{1})|\leq C(1+|(x_{0},y_{0})|+|(x_{1},y_{1})|)^{s}\cdot|(x_{0}-x_{1},y_{0}-y_{1})|.

(A2)

There exists constants
0\leq\ell
and
c,G>0
such that for all
m\in[M]
we have the growth

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c}\,\big(|(x,y)|^{\ell}-G\big)\leq\mathcal{E}_{m}(x;y)\leq c\,\big(|(x,y)|^{\ell}+G\big).

For the sake of simplicity we define the variable

\displaystyle p_{\mathcal{M}}:=\begin{cases}2+s&\text{ if }\ell=0,\\
1&\text{ if }\ell>0.\end{cases}

We adapt two existence results directly from
[
22
, Theorem 2.2, Theorem 2.3]
for the multi-species setting.

Theorem 1.1

(Existence and uniqueness for (
CBO
))
.

Let Assumptions
(A1)
and
(A2)
hold. Then the SDEs (
CBO
) posses unique strong solutions
\{X_{t}^{m,i}\}_{m\in[M],i\in[N]}
for any initial conditions
\{X_{0}^{m,i}\}_{m\in[M],i\in[N]}
that are independent of the Brownian motions
\{B_{t}^{m,i}\}_{m\in[M],i\in[N]}
. The solutions are almost surely continuous.

Theorem 1.2

(Existence and uniqueness for (

))
.

Let Assumptions
(A1)
and
(A2)
hold and
p\geq 2\vee p_{\mathcal{M}}
. Then, for all
T>0
and
\overline{\rho}_{0}^{1},\ldots,\overline{\rho}_{0}^{M}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there exists unique processes
\{\overline{X}^{m}:\Omega\rightarrow C^{0}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})\}_{m\in[M]}
satisfying (
MF CBO
) in the strong sense with initial condition
\law(\overline{X}_{0}^{m})=\overline{\rho}_{0}^{m}
. Furthermore, we have the bounds

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sup_{m\in[M]}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in[0,T]\end{subarray}}\;|\overline{X}_{t}^{m}|^{p}\right]<\infty,\quad&\raisebox{11.38092pt}{$\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in[0,T]\\
m\in[M]\end{subarray}}$}\;|\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\overline{\rho}_{t}^{m},\overline{\textbf{M}}_{t}^{-m})|<\infty,\\
&\quad\overline{\rho}_{t}^{m}=\law\big(\,\overline{X}_{t}^{m}\big),\quad\overline{\textbf{M}}_{t}=\mathbb{E}\big[(\overline{X}_{t}^{1},\ldots,\overline{X}_{t}^{M})\big]\end{split}

(3)

and the function
t\mapsto\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\overline{\rho}_{t}^{m},\overline{\textbf{M}}_{t}^{-m})
is continuous over
[0,T]
.

We also close the gap in

]
, by showing a quantitative mean-field limit estimate.

Theorem 1.3

(Mean-field limit of (
CBO
))
.

Let Assumptions
(A1)
and
(A2)
hold with
q\geq 4\vee 2p_{\mathcal{M}}
,
p\in(0,\frac{q}{2}]
and
\{\rho_{0}^{m}\}_{m\in[M]}\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We assume the particles
\{X^{m,i}\}_{m\in[M],i\in[N]}
satisfy (
CBO
) and
\{\overline{X}^{m,i}\}_{m\in[M],i\in[N]}
are
N
i.i.d. samples for each player from (

). They both use the same standard Brownian motions

\law(X_{0}^{m,i})=\law(\overline{X}_{0}^{m,i})=\rho_{0}^{m}
. Then for each time
T>0
, there exists a positive constant
C>0
independent of
N
such that

\displaystyle\raisebox{9.95863pt}{$\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in[M]\\
i\in[N]\end{subarray}}$}\;\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\;\left|X_{t}^{m,i}-\overline{X}_{t}^{m,i}\right|^{p}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq CN^{-\gamma},

where we define the exponent

2.1
Wasserstein stability estimate for weighted mean

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that Assumptions
(A1)
and
(A2)
hold. Then for all
R>0
and
p\geq p_{\mathcal{M}}
, there exists a constant
C>0
depending on
R
,
M
and
p
such that for all
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{M}\in\mathcal{P}_{p,R}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{M}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we have, for all
m\in[M]
,

\displaystyle|\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\mu_{m},\boldsymbol{\bar{\mu}}^{-m})-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\nu_{m},\boldsymbol{\bar{\nu}}^{-m})|\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{M}W_{p}(\mu_{j},\nu_{j}),

where we define the expectations

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\bar{\mu}}^{-m}

\displaystyle:=(\mathbb{E}[\mu_{1}],\ldots,\mathbb{E}[\mu_{m-1}],\mathbb{E}[\mu_{m+1}]\ldots,\mathbb{E}[\mu_{M}]),

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\bar{\nu}}^{-m}

\displaystyle:=(\mathbb{E}[\nu_{1}],\ldots,\mathbb{E}[\nu_{m-1}],\mathbb{E}[\nu_{m+1}]\ldots,\mathbb{E}[\nu_{M}]).

Proof.

Let us define the probability measure
\bar{\mu}_{m}:=\mu_{m}\otimes\delta_{\boldsymbol{\bar{\mu}}^{-m}}
and
\bar{\nu}_{m}:=\nu_{m}\otimes\delta_{\boldsymbol{\bar{\nu}}^{-m}}
. We compute the
p
-mean of
\bar{\mu}_{m}
by using the Jensen inequality

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{M\cdot d}}|y|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}_{m}(y)\leq M^{p-1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{m}(x)+\sum_{j\neq m}\mathbb{E}[\mu_{j}]^{p}\right)\leq M^{p}R.

Then from
[
22
, Corollary 3.3]
we know there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
R
and
p
such that

\displaystyle|\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\mu_{m},\boldsymbol{\bar{\mu}}^{-m})-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\nu_{m},\boldsymbol{\bar{\nu}}^{-m})|

\displaystyle\leq CW_{p}(\bar{\mu}_{m},\bar{\nu}_{m})

\displaystyle\leq CM^{\frac{p-1}{p}}W_{p}(\mu_{m},\nu_{m})+CM^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\sum_{j\neq m}\big(|\mathbb{E}[\mu_{j}]-\mathbb{E}[\nu_{j}]|^{p}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Let
\pi_{j}\in\Gamma(\mu_{j},\nu_{j})
be an arbitrary coupling of
\mu_{j}
and
\nu_{j}
. Note by the Jensen inequality

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}[\mu_{j}]-\mathbb{E}[\nu_{j}]|\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x-y|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{j}(x,y)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,.

Then we optimize over all couplings
\pi_{j}
and have

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}[\mu_{j}]-\mathbb{E}[\nu_{j}]|\leq W_{p}(\mu_{j},\nu_{j}).

Thus combining both inequalities delivers the result.
∎

The following corollary is analogue to
[
9
, Lemma 2.1]
, but we provide a different proof.

Corollary 2.2

(local Lipshitz property)
.

We define the function
F_{N}^{m}:\mathbb{R}^{M\cdot N\cdot d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

\displaystyle F_{N}^{m}(X^{1},\ldots,X^{M})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\omega_{\alpha}^{\mathcal{E}_{m}}(X^{m,i},\textbf{M}^{-m})}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\omega_{\alpha}^{\mathcal{E}_{m}}(X^{m,j},\textbf{M}^{-m})}\cdot X^{m,i},

where
\{X^{m,i}\}_{m\in[M],i\in[N]}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and we define
X^{m}:=(X^{m,1},\ldots,X^{m,N})
and

\displaystyle\textbf{M}^{-m}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\big(X^{1,i},\ldots,X^{m-1,i},X^{m+1,i},\ldots,X^{M,i}\big).

Then for all
R>0
, there exists a constant
C>0
depending on
R
and
N
such that, for all
X_{0},X_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\cdot N\cdot d}
with
|X_{0}|\vee|X_{1}|\leq R
,

\displaystyle|F_{N}^{m}(X_{0})-F_{N}^{m}(X_{1})|\leq C|X_{0}-X_{1}|.

(4)

Proof.

Let
R>0
and
X_{0},X_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\cdot N\cdot d}
with
|X_{0}|,|X_{1}|\leq R
. Then, define the particle distributions and expectations by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho_{0}^{m}&:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X_{0}^{m,i}},\\
\textbf{M}_{0}&:=\big(\mathbb{E}[\rho_{0}^{1}],\ldots,\mathbb{E}[\rho_{0}^{M}]\big),\end{split}\begin{split}\rho_{1}^{m}&:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X_{1}^{m,i}},\\
\textbf{M}_{1}&:=\big(\mathbb{E}[\rho_{1}^{1}],\ldots,\mathbb{E}[\rho_{1}^{M}]\big).\end{split}

Note that we have the identity, for
k\in\{0,1\}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\rho_{k}^{m},\textbf{M}_{k}^{-m})=F_{N}^{m}(X_{k}^{1},\ldots,X_{k}^{M}).

Then by Lemma
2.1
for any
p\geq p_{\mathcal{M}}
fixed, there exists a constant
C>0
depending on
R
and
p
such that

\displaystyle|F_{N}^{m}(X_{0}^{1},\ldots,X_{0}^{M})-

\displaystyle F_{N}^{m}(X_{1}^{1},\ldots,X_{1}^{M})|=|\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\rho_{0},\textbf{M}_{0}^{-m})-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\rho_{1},\textbf{M}_{1}^{-m})|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{m=1}^{N}W_{p}(\rho_{0}^{m},\rho_{1}^{m})\leq C\sum_{m=1}^{M}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|X_{0}^{m,i}-X_{1}^{m,i}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|X_{0}^{m,i}-X_{1}^{m,i}|\leq C\sqrt{MN}|X_{0}-X_{1}|.

Hence, we have proven the local Lipshitz property as required.
∎

2.2
Bound on weighted moments

We adapt the following lemma from
[
22
, Proposition A.3]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
p\geq 1
,
\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{M}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and let Assumption
(A2)
hold. Then there exists constants
C>0
depending on
p,\ell,c,G
and
M
such that for all
m\in[M]
, it holds that

|\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\rho_{m},\overline{\textbf{M}}^{-m})|\leq\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|\,e^{-\alpha\mathcal{E}_{m}(x;\overline{\textbf{M}}^{-m})}\,\mathrm{d}\rho_{m}(x)}{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\alpha\mathcal{E}_{m}(x;\overline{\textbf{M}}^{-m})}\,\mathrm{d}\rho_{m}(x)}\leq C\left(\sum_{j=1}^{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\rho_{j}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
\overline{\textbf{M}}=(\mathbb{E}[\rho_{1}],\ldots,\mathbb{E}[\rho_{M}])
.

Proof.

We define the probability measure
\rho:=\rho_{m}\otimes\delta_{\overline{\textbf{M}}^{-m}}
. Then from
[
22
, Proposition A.3]
, there exists a constant
C>0
depending on
p
,
\ell
,
c
and
G
such that, for all
m\in[M]
,

\displaystyle|\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\rho_{m},\overline{\textbf{M}}^{-m})|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|\,e^{-\alpha\mathcal{E}_{m}(x;\overline{\textbf{M}}^{-m})}\,\mathrm{d}\rho_{m}(x)}{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\alpha\mathcal{E}_{m}(x;\overline{\textbf{M}}^{-m})}\,\mathrm{d}\rho_{m}(x)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{M\cdot d}}|y|\,e^{-\alpha\mathcal{E}_{m}(y)}\,\mathrm{d}\rho(y)}{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{M\cdot d}}e^{-\alpha\mathcal{E}_{m}(y)}\,\mathrm{d}\rho(y)}\leq CM^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\rho_{m}(x)+\sum_{j\neq m}|\mathbb{E}[\rho_{j}]|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

If we use the Jensen inequality, we get the desired bound

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\rho_{m}(x)+\sum_{j\neq m}|\mathbb{E}[\rho_{j}]|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\left(\sum_{j=1}^{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\rho_{j}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

We conclude the proof by recombining the above terms.
∎

Lemma 2.5

(Moment estimates for the empirical measures)
.

Let

,
\bar{\rho}_{0}^{1},\ldots,\bar{\rho}_{0}^{M}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and let Assumptions
(A1)
and
(A2)
hold. Let

be solutions to the SDEs (

\kappa>0
that does not depend on

such that

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in[M]\\
i\in[N]\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\;|X_{t}^{m,i}|^{p}\right]\leq\kappa\cdot\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}[|X_{0}^{m,1}|^{p}].

Proof.

We use Lemma
2.3
and Remark
2.4
, and know that there exists a constant
C>0
depending on

,
\ell
,
c
,
G
and
M
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\rho_{t}^{m,N},\textbf{M}_{t}^{-m})\leq C\left(\sum_{j=1}^{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}^{j,N}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

Thus, there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
N
such that we can estimate the drift and diffusion terms by

\displaystyle|X_{t}^{m,i}-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\rho_{t}^{m,N},\textbf{M}_{t}^{-m})|\vee|D(X_{t}^{m,i}-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\rho_{t}^{m,N},\textbf{M}_{t}^{-m}))|\leq C\left(|X_{t}^{m,i}|^{p}+\sum_{j=1}^{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}^{j,N}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Next we apply the Burkholder-Davis-Grundy inequality
[
40
, Chapter 1, Theorem 7.3]
to find for all

that

\frac{1}{3^{p-1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left|X_{t}^{m,i}\right|^{p}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}[|X_{0}^{m,i}|^{p}]+\lambda^{p}T^{p-1}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|X_{s}^{m,i}-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\rho_{s}^{m,N},\textbf{M}_{s}^{-m})\right|^{p}\right]\,\,\mathrm{d}s\\
+T^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\sigma^{p}C_{\mathrm{DBG}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|D(X_{s}^{m,i}-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\rho_{s}^{m,N},\textbf{M}_{s}^{-m}))\right|^{p}\right]\,\,\mathrm{d}s\\
\leq\mathbb{E}[|X_{0}^{m,i}|^{p}]+C\left(\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{s}^{m,i}|^{p}+\sum_{j=1}^{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\rho_{s}^{j,N}(x)\right]\,\mathrm{d}s\right).

As the particles

are exchangeable, we see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}^{m,N}(x)\right]=\mathbb{E}[|X_{t}^{m,i}|^{p}].

Hence, for some constant

, we derive

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left|X_{s}^{m,i}\right|^{p}\right]\leq C\left(\mathbb{E}[|X_{0}^{m,i}|^{p}]+\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{r\in[0,s]}\left|X_{r}^{j,i}\right|^{p}\right]\,\mathrm{d}s\right).

We sum over

and get the inequality

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left|X_{s}^{m,i}\right|^{p}\right]\leq C\left(\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}[|X_{0}^{m,i}|^{p}]+\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{r\in[0,s]}\left|X_{r}^{m,i}\right|^{p}\right]\,\mathrm{d}s\right).

The result now follows from the Grönwall inequality.

Lemma 2.8

. To that end, we rewrite the stochastic differential equation (

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}X_{t}=F(X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+G(X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}B_{t},

\displaystyle:=-\lambda\begin{pmatrix}X_{t}^{1,1}-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{1}(\rho_{t}^{1,N},\textbf{M}_{t}^{-1})\\
\vdots\\
X_{t}^{1,N}-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{1}(\rho_{t}^{1,N},\textbf{M}_{t}^{-1})\\

We have sublinear growth in the consensus

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{M}|\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\rho_{t}^{m,N},\textbf{M}_{t}^{-m})|\leq\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{e^{-\alpha\mathcal{E}_{m}(X_{t}^{m,i};\textbf{M}_{t}^{-m})}}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{-\alpha\mathcal{E}_{m}(X_{t}^{m,j};\textbf{M}_{t}^{-m})}}\cdot|X_{t}^{m,i}|=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|X_{t}^{m,i}|\leq\sqrt{MN}|X_{t}|.

(7)

\displaystyle F(X_{t})\cdot\nabla\varphi(X_{t})+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle G(X_{t}),\;\nabla^{2}\varphi(X_{t})G(X_{t})\right\rangle\leq|F(X_{t})|^{2}+|X_{t}|^{2}+\|G(X_{t})\|^{2}\leq C\varphi(X_{t}),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|Y_{s}^{m}|^{p}\right]\leq C\left(\mathbb{E}[|Y_{0}^{m}|^{p}]+\|u^{m}\|_{\infty}^{p}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}[|Y_{s}^{m}|^{p}]\,\mathrm{d}s\right),

The goal of this step is to apply the Leray-Schauder fixed point Theorem

\left\{(u^{1},\ldots,u^{M})\in C^{0}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes M}:~\exists\xi\in[0,1]\mbox{ such that }(u^{1},\ldots,u^{M})=\xi\mathcal{T}(u^{1},\ldots,u^{M})\right\}\,.

(12)

To this end, let
(u^{1},\ldots,u^{M})\in C^{0}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes M}
be such that

(u^{1},\ldots,u^{M})=\xi\mathcal{T}(u^{1},\ldots,u^{M})

(13)

for some
\xi\in[0,1]
, and let
(Y^{1},\ldots,Y^{M})
denote corresponding solution to (
8
). By (
13
), the processes
(Y^{1},\ldots,Y^{M})
are also solutions to

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}Y_{t}^{m}=-\lambda(Y_{t}^{m}-\xi\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\nu_{t}^{m};\overline{\mathbf{Y}}_{t}^{-m}))\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma D(Y_{t}^{m}-\xi\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\nu_{t}^{m};\overline{\mathbf{Y}}_{t}^{-m}))\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}^{m},\quad\nu_{t}^{m}=\mbox{law}(Y_{t}^{m}).

(14)

Then by Lemma
2.5
and Remark
2.6
we find that

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|Y_{t}^{m}|^{p}\right]\leq\kappa\cdot\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}\left[|Y_{0}^{m}|^{p}\right],

where
\kappa>0
is a constant. Applying Lemma
2.3
, it follows that
\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\nu_{t}^{m};\overline{\mathbf{Y}}_{t}^{-m})
can be uniformly bounded in
[0,T]
in terms of
\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}\left[|Y_{0}^{m}|^{p}\right]
. This implies that the set (
12
) is indeed bounded. Therefore, we obtain the existence of a fixed point of
\mathcal{T}
, which is a solution to (
MF CBO
), and we establish the moment bounds in (
3
) as a byproduct.

Uniqueness of solution.
Suppose that
(Y^{1},\ldots,Y^{M})
,
(Z^{1},\ldots,Z^{M}):\Omega\rightarrow C^{0}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes M}
are two fixed points of the map
\mathcal{T}
. Define the difference
W_{t}^{m}:=Y_{t}^{m}-Z_{t}^{m}
and let
\nu_{t}^{m}:=\law(Y_{t}^{m})
and
\mu_{t}^{m}:=\law(Z_{t}^{m})
. By the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality
[
40
, Chapter 1, Theorem 7.3]
for all
t\in[0,T]
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{p-1}}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\;|W_{r}^{m}|^{p}\right]\leq T^{p-1}\lambda^{p}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}[\;|W_{r}^{m}-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\nu_{r}^{m};\overline{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}^{-m})+\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\mu_{r}^{m};\overline{\mathbf{Z}}_{r}^{-m})|^{p}\;]\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\quad+C_{\mathrm{BDG}}\,T^{p/2-1}\sigma^{p}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}[\;|D(Y_{r}^{m}-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\nu_{r}^{m};\overline{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}^{-m}))-D(Z_{r}^{m}-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\mu_{r}^{m};\overline{\mathbf{Z}}_{r}^{-m})|^{p}\;]\,\mathrm{d}r.

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}[\;|W_{r}^{m}|^{p}\;]\,\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}[\;|\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\nu_{r}^{m};\overline{\mathbf{Y}}_{r}^{-m})-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\mu_{r}^{m};\overline{\mathbf{Z}}_{r}^{-m})|^{p}\;]\,\mathrm{d}r\right),

for some constant
C>0
depending on
T
,
\lambda
,
\sigma
and
p
. By Lemma
2.1
we have that

\displaystyle|\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\nu_{t}^{m};\overline{\mathbf{Y}}_{t}^{-m})-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\mu_{t}^{m};\overline{\mathbf{Z}}_{t}^{-m})|\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{M}W_{p}(\nu_{t}^{j},\mu_{t}^{j}).

Also by definition of the Wasserstein distance
W_{p}(\nu_{t}^{m},\mu_{t}^{m})^{p}\leq\mathbb{E}[\;|W_{t}^{m}|^{p}\;]
. Thus, we conclude by summing over
m

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\;\sum_{m=1}^{M}|W_{r}^{m}|^{p}\right]\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\;\sum_{m=1}^{M}|W_{r}^{m}|^{p}\;\right]\,\mathrm{d}r,

for some constant
C>0
depending on
T
,
\lambda
,
\sigma
,
p
and
M
. Thus, by the Gronwall inequality and the fact that
\mathbb{E}[W_{0}^{m}]=0
we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\;|W_{t}^{m}|^{p}\right]=0.

With this we have proven the uniqueness of our solution. Thus, we conclude the proof.

Proof of Theorem
1.3

We will follow the same proof outline as
[
22
, Theorem 2.6]
or
[
11
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let us explain the proof strategy: The goal of the proof is to devise a Gronwall type estimate between the expected particle difference
|\overline{X}_{t}^{m,i}-X_{t}^{m,i}|^{p}
. For this we make use of the SDE’s (
CBO
) and (
MF CBO
) and bound the difference in consensus between
\rho^{m,N}
and
\overline{\mu}^{m,N}
, and
\overline{\mu}^{m,N}
and
\overline{\rho}^{m}
, where
\overline{\mu}^{m,N}
the empirical distribution of (
MF CBO
). We divide the proof into two steps.

Reduction to higher exponents.
First we show that it is sufficient to prove the statement for the case
p\in[2\vee p_{\mathcal{M}},\,\frac{q}{2}]
. Indeed, if the statement holds true in this case, then for all
r\in(0,2\vee p_{\mathcal{M}})
we have by Jensen’s inequality

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\;\left|X_{t}^{m,i}-\overline{X}_{t}^{m,i}\right|^{r}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\leq\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\;\left|X_{t}^{m,i}-\overline{X}_{t}^{m,i}\right|^{2\vee p_{\mathcal{M}}}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2\vee p_{\mathcal{M}}}}\leq CN^{-\beta}

with

\displaystyle\beta:=\min\left\{\frac{1}{2},\;\frac{q-(2\vee p_{\mathcal{M}})}{2(2\vee p_{\mathcal{M}})^{2}}\right\}=\min\left\{\frac{1}{2},\;\frac{q-r}{2r^{2}},\;\frac{q-(2\vee p_{\mathcal{M}})}{2(2\vee p_{\mathcal{M}})^{2}}\right\},

where we used
(0,q)\ni z\mapsto\frac{q-z}{z^{2}}\in\mathbb{R}
is decreasing.

Mean-field limit.
Let
p\in[2\vee p_{\mathcal{M}},\,\frac{q}{2}]
be fixed. We define the particle distribution of (
MF CBO
) as

\displaystyle\overline{\mu}^{m,N}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{\overline{X}^{m,i}}.

Then by the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality
[
40
, Chapter 1, Theorem 7.3]
we derive the estimate

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|X_{t}^{m,i}-\overline{X}_{t}^{m,i}\right|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq(2T)^{p-1}\lambda^{p}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|X_{t}^{m,i}-\overline{X}_{t}^{m,i}+\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\rho_{t}^{m,N},\textbf{M}_{t}^{-m})-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\overline{\rho}_{t}^{m},\overline{\textbf{M}}_{t}^{-m})\right|^{p}\right]\,dt

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+2^{p-1}T^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\sigma^{p}C_{\mathrm{DBG}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|D(X_{t}^{m,i}-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\rho_{t}^{m,N},\textbf{M}_{t}^{-m}))-D(\overline{X}_{t}^{m,i}-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\overline{\rho}_{t}^{m},\overline{\textbf{M}}_{t}^{-m}))\right|^{p}\right]\,dt

\displaystyle\qquad\leq(6T)^{p-1}\lambda^{p}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|X_{t}^{m,i}-\overline{X}_{t}^{m,i}\right|^{p}\right]\,dt

(P Diff)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+(6T)^{p-1}\lambda^{p}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\overline{\rho}_{t}^{m},\overline{\textbf{M}}_{t}^{-m})-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\overline{\mu}_{t}^{m,N},\overline{\textbf{M}}_{t}^{-m})\right|^{p}\right]\,dt

(Emp App)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+(6T)^{p-1}\lambda^{p}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\overline{\mu}_{t}^{m,N},\overline{\textbf{M}}_{t}^{-m})-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\rho_{t}^{m,N},\textbf{M}_{t}^{-m})\right|^{p}\right]\,dt

(Emp Diff)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\begin{aligned} +2^{p-1}T^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\sigma^{p}C_{\mathrm{DBG}}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|&D(X_{t}^{m,i}-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\rho_{t}^{m,N},\textbf{M}_{t}^{-m}))\\
&\qquad-D(\overline{X}_{t}^{m,i}-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\overline{\rho}_{t}^{m},\overline{\textbf{M}}_{t}^{-m}))\big|^{p}\Big]\,dt.\end{aligned}

(L)

The next step is to bound each term in the above inequality. First, we start with the empirical difference (
Emp Diff
). For this we define the
\mathbb{R}
-valued random variables and value

\displaystyle\overline{Z}^{m,i}:=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\overline{X}_{t}^{m,i}|^{p},\qquad R_{0}:=\sup_{m\in[M]}\mathbb{E}\left[\overline{Z}^{m,1}\right].

Let
R>R_{0}
be fixed, then we need to define the particle excursion set

\displaystyle\Omega_{t}:=\bigcup_{m\in[M]}\left\{\omega\in\Omega\;\left|\;\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\overline{X}_{t}^{m,i}(\omega)|^{p}\geq R\right.\right\}.

Additionally, by Theorem
1.2
we know that
\mathbb{E}[|\overline{Z}^{m,i}|^{q/p}]<\infty
. As
q\geq 2p
, we have by
[
22
, Lemma 2.5]
that there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
N
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{t})\leq C\,N^{-\frac{q}{2p}}.

(15)

Now we can split the term in (
Emp Diff
) by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\overline{\mu}_{t}^{m,N},\overline{\textbf{M}}_{t}^{-m})-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\rho_{t}^{m,N},\textbf{M}_{t}^{-m})\right|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\overline{\mu}_{t}^{m,N},\overline{\textbf{M}}_{t}^{-m})-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\rho_{t}^{m,N},\textbf{M}_{t}^{-m})\right|^{p}\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{t}}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\overline{\mu}_{t}^{m,N},\overline{\textbf{M}}_{t}^{-m})-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\rho_{t}^{m,N},\textbf{M}_{t}^{-m})\right|^{p}\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega_{t}}\right].

By Lemma
2.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\overline{\mu}_{t}^{m,N},\overline{\textbf{M}}_{t}^{-m})-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\rho_{t}^{m,N},\textbf{M}_{t}^{-m})\right|^{p}\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{t}}\right]\leq C\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}\left[W_{p}^{p}(\overline{\mu}_{t}^{m,N},\rho_{t}^{m,N})\right].

Additionally, the Wasserstein difference is controlled by the particle difference by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[W_{p}^{p}(\overline{\mu}_{t}^{m,N},\rho_{t}^{m,N})\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|X_{t}^{m,i}-\overline{X}_{t}^{m,i}|^{p}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{t}^{m,1}-\overline{X}_{t}^{m,1}|^{p}\right].

Next, we bound the following consensus using Lemma
2.3
, Remark
2.4
and Theorem
1.2

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\;|\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\overline{\mu}_{t}^{m,N},\overline{\textbf{M}}_{t}^{-m})|^{q}\;]\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\overline{\mu}_{t}^{j,N}(x)\right]\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,T]}|\overline{X}_{s}^{j,1}|^{q}\right]<\infty.

Next, we use the moment estimate from Lemma
2.5
and the previous bound to estimate

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\overline{\mu}_{t}^{m,N},\overline{\textbf{M}}_{t}^{-m})-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\rho_{t}^{m,N},\textbf{M}_{t}^{-m})|^{q}]

\displaystyle\leq 2^{q-1}\big(\mathbb{E}[|\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\overline{\mu}_{t}^{m,N},\overline{\textbf{M}}_{t}^{-m})|^{q}]+\mathbb{E}[|\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\rho_{t}^{m,N},\textbf{M}_{t}^{-m})|^{q}]\big)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{q}\kappa

for some constant
\kappa>0
independent of
N
. Then by the Hölder inequality and (
15
), we find

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\overline{\mu}_{t}^{m,N},\overline{\textbf{M}}_{t}^{-m})-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\rho_{t}^{m,N},\textbf{M}_{t}^{-m})|^{p}\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega_{t}}]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\mathbb{E}[|\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\overline{\mu}_{t}^{m,N},\overline{\textbf{M}}_{t}^{-m})-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\rho_{t}^{m,N},\textbf{M}_{t}^{-m})|^{q}]^{\frac{p}{q}}\mathbb{E}[\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega_{t}}^{\frac{q}{q-p}}]^{\frac{q-p}{q}}\leq 2^{p}C\kappa^{\frac{p}{q}}N^{-\frac{q-p}{2p}}.

We have thus shown

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\overline{\mu}_{t}^{m,N},\overline{\textbf{M}}_{t}^{-m})-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\rho_{t}^{m,N},\textbf{M}_{t}^{-m})|^{p}]\leq CN^{-\frac{q-p}{2p}}+C\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}[|X_{t}^{m,i}-\overline{X}_{t}^{m,i}|^{p}].

For the next step, we observe that by Theorem
1.2
there exists
R>0
such that, for all
t\in[0,T]
and
m\in[M]
,

\displaystyle|\overline{\textbf{M}}_{t}^{m}|\leq\mathbb{E}\left[|\overline{X}_{t}^{m,1}|^{q}\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,T]}|\overline{X}^{m,1}|^{q}\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq R.

Thus, for the empirical approximation (
Emp App
) we use lemma
2.8
, with
\mu=\overline{\rho}_{t}^{m}
and
Y=\overline{\textbf{M}}_{t}^{-m}
, to derive the bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\overline{\rho}_{t}^{m},\overline{\textbf{M}}_{t}^{-m})-\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{m}(\overline{\mu}_{t}^{m,N},\overline{\textbf{M}}_{t}^{-m})\right|^{p}\right]\leq CN^{-\frac{p}{2}},

for some constant
C>0
dependent on
M
and
R
, but independent of
N
. Finally, using the fact that
D
is globally Lipshitz, we can bound the term (
L
) analogously as we have done above. This means we derive the inequality

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|X_{t}^{m,i}-\overline{X}_{t}^{m,i}\right|^{p}\right]\leq CN^{-\theta p}+C\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left|X_{s}^{j,i}-\overline{X}_{s}^{j,i}\right|^{p}\right]\,\mathrm{d}t

and we define the parameter

\displaystyle\theta:=\min\left\{\frac{1}{2},\;\frac{q-p}{2p^{2}}\right\}=\min\left\{\frac{p}{2},\;\frac{q-p}{2p},\;\frac{q-(2\vee p_{\mathcal{M}})}{2(2\vee p_{\mathcal{M}})^{2}}\right\},

where we used
(0,q)\ni z\mapsto\frac{q-z}{z^{2}}\in\mathbb{R}
is decreasing. We take the sum over
m=1,\ldots,M
and derive the inequality

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|X_{t}^{m,i}-\overline{X}_{t}^{m,i}\right|^{p}\right]\leq CN^{-\theta p}+C\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left|X_{s}^{m,i}-\overline{X}_{s}^{m,i}\right|^{p}\right]\,\mathrm{d}t.

The result is now a consequence of the Grönwall inequality and taking the
p
th root. With this we conclude the proof.
∎

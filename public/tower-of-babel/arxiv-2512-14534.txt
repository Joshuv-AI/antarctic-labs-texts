1.
Introduction

In this paper we study the connection between the codimension one Riesz transform and the rectifiability of measures. Among other things,
for an arbitrary Radon measure
\mu
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
, we estimate the Jones-Wolff square function associated with the
\beta_{2}
coefficients of the measure
\mu

in terms of the
L^{2}(\mu)
oscillation of the
n
-dimensional Riesz transform of
\mu
.
These results have applications to the study of harmonic measure and reflectionless measures for the Riesz transform.

We recall that a Borel measure
\mu
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is called
n
-rectifiable if there is a countable collection of Lipschitz maps
g_{i}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that

\mu({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\bigcup_{i}g_{i}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))=0
and moreover
\mu
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Hausdorff measure
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
.
A set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is
n
-rectifiable if the Hausdorff measure
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{E}
is
n
-rectifiable. On the other hand,
E
is called purely
n
-unrectifiable if
{\mathcal{H}}^{(}E\cap F)=0
for any
n
-rectifiable set
F\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

The (
n
-dimensional) Riesz transform of a signed Radon measure
\nu
is defined by

{\mathcal{R}}\nu(x)=\int\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{n+1}}\,d\nu(y),

whenever the integral makes sense.
Given
{\varepsilon}>0
, the
{\varepsilon}
-truncated Riesz transform of
\nu
equals

{\mathcal{R}}_{\varepsilon}\nu(x)=\int_{|x-y|>{\varepsilon}}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{n+1}}\,d\nu(y),

We also write

{\mathcal{R}}_{*}\nu(x)=\sup_{{\varepsilon}>0}|{\mathcal{R}}_{{\varepsilon}}\nu(x)|,\qquad\operatorname{pv}{\mathcal{R}}\nu(x)=\lim_{{\varepsilon}>0}{\mathcal{R}}_{{\varepsilon}}\nu(x),

in case that the latter limit exists. Remark that, quite often, abusing notation we will
write
{\mathcal{R}}\nu
instead of
\operatorname{pv}{\mathcal{R}}\nu
.

For
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mu)
and a positive Radon measure
\mu
,
one writes
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}f={\mathcal{R}}(f\mu)
and
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu,{\varepsilon}}f={\mathcal{R}}_{\varepsilon}(f\mu)
.
We say that
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}
is bounded in
L^{2}(\mu)
if
the operators
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu,{\varepsilon}}
are bounded uniformly in
L^{2}(\mu)
uniformly on
{\varepsilon}
, and then we denote

\|{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)\to L^{2}(\mu)}=\sup_{{\varepsilon}>0}\|{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu,{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)\to L^{2}(\mu)}.

The study of the connection between quantitative rectifiability and the ·
L^{2}
boundedness of the Riesz transform has been a subject of
intense research in the last decades because of the application to the Painlevé problem for bounded holomorphic and Lipschitz harmonic functions (see
[
To1
]
and
[
Vo
]
).
In
[
NTV1
]
, Nazarov, the author, and Volberg showed that, given an
n
-Ahlfors regular set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
, the
L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{E})
boundedness of the Riesz transform
{\mathcal{R}}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{E}}
implies the uniform
n
-rectifiability of
E
. See (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) for the definitions of
Ahlfors regularity and uniform rectifiability.
Without the Ahlfors regularity assumption, as shown in
[
NTV2
]
, for a set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
with finite Hausdorff
measure
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
, the
L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{E})
boundedness of
{\mathcal{R}}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{E}}
implies the
n
-rectifiability of
E
.
In the planar case, the same results had been proved previously by Mattila, Melnikov, and Verdera
[
MMV
]
in the Ahlfors regular case, and
by David and Léger
[
Lé
]
in the non-Ahlfors regular one
using the connection between Menger curvature and the Cauchy kernel discovered by Melnikov
[
Me
]
.
From the previous results and a suitable
Tb
type theorem, it turns out that a set
E
with
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(E)<\infty
is removable for Lipschitz harmonic functions if and only if
it is purely unrectifiable. In the plane this was shown by David and Mattila
[
DM
]
(and by
[
Da
]
in the case of holomorphic functions) and by Nazarov, the author, and Volberg in higher dimensions
[
NTV2
]
.

More recently, in
[
DT
]
, a geometric characterization of general compact non-removable sets, possibly with infinite measure
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
, was given in terms of the finiteness of a non-linear potential involving the
\beta_{2}
coefficients of the measure. To describe this result in more detail, we introduce some notation.

For a Radon measure
\mu
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and
r>0
, we denote

\Theta_{\mu}^{n}(x,r)=\frac{\mu(B(x,r))}{r^{n}},

and for
1\leq p<\infty
,

\beta_{p,\mu}^{n}(x,r)=\inf_{L}\left(\frac{1}{r^{n}}\int_{B(x,r)}\left(\frac{{\rm dist}(y,L)}{r}\right)^{p}\,d\mu(y)\right)^{1/p},

where the infimum is taken over all
n
-planes
L\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
For a ball
B=B(x,r)
, we write
\Theta_{\mu}^{n}(B)=\Theta_{\mu}^{n}(x,r)
and
\beta_{2,\mu}^{n}(B)=\beta_{2,\mu}^{n}(x,r)
.
In the case
d=n+1
, to shorten notation, we write
\Theta_{\mu}
and
\beta_{p,\mu}
instead of
\Theta_{\mu}^{n}
and
\beta_{p,\mu}^{n}
.
One of the main results from
[
DT
]
is the following.

Theorem A
.

Let
\mu
be a Radon measure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
satisfying the growth condition

(1.1)

\mu(B(x,r))\leq\theta_{0}\,r^{n}\quad\mbox{ for all $x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu$ and all $r>0$}

and such that
\|{\mathcal{R}}_{*}\mu\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}<\infty
.
Then

(1.2)

\int\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\beta_{2,\mu}(x,r)^{2}\,\Theta_{\mu}(x,r)\,\frac{dr}{r}\,d\mu(x)\leq C\,(\big\|{\mathcal{R}}\mu\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}+\theta_{0}^{2}\,\|\mu\|\big),

where
C
is an absolute constant.

We call the function
J_{\mu}(x):=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\beta_{2,\mu}(x,r)^{2}\,\Theta_{\mu}(x,r)\,\frac{dr}{r}\right)^{1/2}
the Jones-Wolff potential (or Jones-Wolff square function) of
\mu
. As shown in
[
DT
]
, from Theorem A it follows that a compact set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
is non-removable for Lipschitz harmonic functions
if and only if it supports a non-zero measure
\mu
satisfying the growth condition (
1.1
) whose potential
J_{\mu}
is uniformly bounded in

{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
.

The connection between quantitative rectifiability and the Riesz transform has also been applied recently to solve some long standing free boundary problems about harmonic measure. Indeed, relying on the results from
[
NTV1
]
and
[
NTV2
]
, in
the work
[
AHM+
]
it was shown that the mutual absolute continuity of harmonic measure and the Hausdorff measure
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}
on a subset
E\subset{\partial\Omega}
implies the
n
-rectifiability of
E
.
On the other hand, the works
[
AMT
]
and
[
AMTV
]
solve the following two phase problem for harmonic measure: given two disjoint domains
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
with associated harmonic measures
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
, if
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
are mutually absolutely continuous on a subset
E\subset{\partial\Omega}_{1}\cap{\partial\Omega}_{2}
, then there exists an
n
-rectifiable subset
F\subset E
with full harmonic measure in
E
such that both harmonic measures are mutually absolutely continuous with
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{F}
on
E
. A fundamental ingredient of the proof is a criterion for the
n
-rectifiability of general Radon measures in terms of the
L^{2}
oscillation of their Riesz transforms obtained by Girela-Sarrión and the author in
[
GT
]
.
In the plane the aforementioned two phase problem had been solved previously by Bishop in
[
Bi
]

by different arguments.

In this work we extend the results from
[
DT
]
and
[
GT
]
in different ways. To state them we need some additional terminology. For a ball
B=B(x,r)
,
we denote

{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(B)\equiv{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(B(x,r)):=\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{-j}\,\Theta_{\mu}(2^{j}B).

We say that the ball
B
is
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}
-doubling if, for some fixed constant
C
,

{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(B)\leq C\,\Theta_{\mu}(B).

We may say that
B
is
({\mathcal{P}}_{\mu},C)
-doubling if we want to specify the constant
C
.
We also set

M_{n}\mu(x)=\sup_{r>0}\frac{\mu(B(x,r))}{r^{n}}=\sup_{r>0}\Theta_{\mu}(x,r)

and

\theta_{\mu}^{n,*}(x)=\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu(B(x,r))}{r^{n}}.

The first result of this paper can be considered as local version of Theorem A:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mu
be a Radon measure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
and let
B_{0}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
be a closed ball. Suppose that
M_{n}(\chi_{3B_{0}}\mu)\in L^{2}(\mu|_{2B_{0}})
and

{\mathcal{R}}_{*}(\chi_{3B_{0}}\mu)\in L^{2}(\mu|_{2B_{0}})
.
Then we have

(1.3)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{0}}|M_{n}(\mu|_{B_{0}})|^{2}\,

\displaystyle d\mu+\int_{B_{0}}\int_{0}^{2\operatorname{rad}(B_{0})}\beta_{2,\mu}(x,r)^{2}\,\Theta_{\mu}(x,r)\,\frac{dr}{r}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{2B_{0}}|{\mathcal{R}}\mu-m_{\mu,2B_{0}}({\mathcal{R}}\mu)|^{2}\,d\mu+{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(B_{0})^{2}\mu(2B_{0})+\int_{2B_{0}}\theta_{\mu}^{n,*}(x)^{2}\,d\mu(x),

with the implicit constant depending on
n
.

A natural question motivated for possible applications to the study of harmonic measure and reflectionless measures is if on the right hand side
of (
1.3
) one can eliminate the terms
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(B_{0})^{2}\mu(2B_{0})
or
\int_{2B_{0}}\theta_{\mu}^{n,*}(x)^{2}\,d\mu(x)
.
Our main achievement in this paper is that, assuming
B_{0}
to be
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}
-doubling and the density
\Theta_{\mu}(x,r)
not to decay too fast
as
r\to 0
in some portion of
B_{0}
, the term the terms
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(B_{0})^{2}\mu(2B_{0})
can
be eliminated. Indeed, this is controlled by the other two terms on the the right hand side. The precise result is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mu
be a Radon measure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
and let
B_{0}
be a
({\mathcal{P}}_{\mu},C_{0})
-doubling ball. Suppose that
M_{n}(\chi_{3B_{0}}\mu)\in L^{2}(\mu|_{2B_{0}})
and

{\mathcal{R}}_{*}(\chi_{3B_{0}}\mu)\in L^{2}(\mu|_{2B_{0}})
. Suppose that there exists some ball
B_{1}
centered in
B_{0}
such that

\Theta_{\mu}(B_{1})\geq\alpha\,\Theta_{\mu}(B_{0})

for some
\alpha>0
, and such that, for some
\delta_{0},\delta_{1}>0
,

\delta_{0}\,\operatorname{rad}(B_{0})\leq\operatorname{rad}(B_{1})\leq\delta_{1}\,\operatorname{rad}(B_{0}),

with
\delta_{1}\in(0,1/2)
small enough, depending on
n
,
C_{0}
, and
\alpha
. Then there exists some constant
c_{1}>0
depending on

n
,
\alpha
, and
\delta_{0}
such that

c_{1}\Theta_{\mu}(B_{0})^{2}\,\mu(B_{0})\leq\int_{2B_{0}}|{\mathcal{R}}\mu-m_{\mu,2B_{0}}({\mathcal{R}}\mu)|^{2}\,d\mu+\int_{2B_{0}}\theta_{\mu}^{n,*}(x)^{2}\,d\mu(x).

As a corollary, putting Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
together, we derive:

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\mu
be a Radon measure and
B_{0}
a ball satisfying the assumptions in Theorem
1.2
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{0}}|M_{n}(\mu|_{B_{0}})|^{2}\,d\mu+\int_{B_{0}}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\operatorname{rad}(B_{0})}\beta_{2,\mu}(x,r)^{2}\,\Theta_{\mu}(x,r)\,\frac{dr}{r}d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{2B_{0}}|{\mathcal{R}}\mu-m_{\mu,2B_{0}}({\mathcal{R}}\mu)|^{2}\,d\mu+\int_{2B_{0}}\theta_{\mu}^{n,*}(x)^{2}\,d\mu(x),

with the implicit constant depending on
n
,
\alpha
, and
\delta_{0}
.

The next corollary deals with reflectionless measures. For our purposes, a reflectionless measure for the
n
-dimensional Riesz transform is a Radon measure
\mu
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
having
n
-polynomial growth (i.e, satisfying (
1.1
)), such that
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}
is bounded in
L^{2}(\mu)
, and such that
{\mathcal{R}}\mu
is zero in a
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{BMO}(\mu)
sense, i.e.,
{\mathcal{R}}\mu
is constant
\mu
-a.e. In
[
JN2
]
,
Jaye and Nazarov have shown that if all the
n
-Ahlfors reflectionless measures for the Riesz transform are
n
-flat, i.e., of the form

c\,{\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{L}
for some
n
-plane
L
, then the
the
n
-dimensional David-Semmes problem has a positive answer. That is, for any
n
-Ahlfors regular set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, the

L^{2}({\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{E})
boundedness of
{\mathcal{R}}_{{\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{E}}
implies the uniform
n
-rectifiability of
E
. This question is still open in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
when
1<n<d
.
However, up to now the structure of
reflectionless measure for the Riesz transform is not well understood. It is not known if they are all
n
-flat even in the
n
-Ahlfors regular case with

n=d-1
. For more information on reflectionless measures, see also
[
JN1
]
.

We remark that by now it is not known if there exist reflectionless measures
\mu
such that the set
\{x\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu):\theta_{\mu}^{n,*}(x)=0\big\}
has positive
\mu
-measure.
In this paper we will prove the following.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
\mu
be a Radon measure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
with polynomial
n
-growth which is reflectionless. There is some absolute constant

{\varepsilon}_{n}>0

such that the following holds.
Suppose that
\dim_{H}(\mu)\leq n+{\varepsilon}_{n}
. Then the set

\{x\in\operatorname{supp}(\mu):\theta_{\mu}^{n,*}(x)>0\big\}

is rectifiable and dense in
\operatorname{supp}(\mu)
.

We recall that, for a Radon measure
\mu
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
\dim_{H}(\mu)
stands for the Hausdorff dimension of
\mu
, defined by

\dim_{H}(\mu)=\inf\big\{\dim_{H}(E):\,\text{$E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}$ Borel, $\mu(E^{c})=0$}\big\}.

Another consequence of Theorem
1.2
is the following enhanced version of a theorem from
[
GT
]
:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\mu
be a Radon measure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
and let
B_{0}
be a
({\mathcal{P}}_{\mu},C_{0})
-doubling ball. Suppose that
the following conditions hold:

(a)

{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu|_{2B_{0}}}
is bounded in
L^{2}(\mu|_{2B_{0}})
with

\|{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}\|_{L^{2}(\mu|_{2B_{0}})\to L^{2}(\mu|_{2B_{0}})}\leq C_{1}\Theta_{\mu}(B_{0})

and

\sup_{0<r\leq 4\operatorname{rad}(B_{0})}\Theta_{\mu}(x,r)\leq C_{1}\Theta_{\mu}(B_{0})\quad\mbox{ for all $x\in 2B_{0}$}.

(b)

There exists some ball
B_{1}
centered in
B_{0}
such that, for some positive constants
\alpha
,
\delta_{0}
,
\delta_{1}
,

\Theta_{\mu}(B_{1})\geq\alpha\,\Theta_{\mu}(B_{0})

and

\delta_{0}\,\operatorname{rad}(B_{0})\leq\operatorname{rad}(B_{1})\leq\delta_{1}\,\operatorname{rad}(B_{0}).

(c)

For some
{\varepsilon}>0
,

\int_{2B_{0}}|{\mathcal{R}}\mu-m_{\mu,2B_{0}}({\mathcal{R}}\mu)|^{2}\,d\mu\leq{\varepsilon}\,\Theta_{\mu}(B_{0})^{2}\,\mu(B_{0}).

Suppose that
\delta_{1}\in(0,1/2)
is small enough, depending on
n
,
C_{0}
,
C_{1}
, and
\alpha
, and that

{\varepsilon}
is small enough, depending on
C_{0}
,
C_{1}
,
\alpha
and
\delta_{0}
. Then
there is a uniformly
n
-rectifiable set
\Gamma
and some
\tau>0
such that

\mu(\Gamma\cap B_{0})\geq\tau\,\mu(B_{0}),

with
\tau
and the uniform rectifiability constant of
\Gamma
depending on the above constants.

This theorem should be compared with Theorem 1.1 from
[
GT
]
(see also Corollary 3.2 from
[
AMT
]
for an equivalent form closer to
the statement above). The main difference is that the condition (b) above is replaced by another asking
\beta_{1,\mu}(B_{0})\leq\delta\,\Theta_{\mu}(B_{0})
, with
\delta
sufficiently
small. It is not difficult to check that the latter condition implies the assumption (b) of the theorem.
The main difference between the arguments to prove Theorem
1.5
and the ones in
[
GT
]
is that the ones in the latter
work are based on a delicate periodization of the measure
\mu
in order to use later a variational argument leading to a contradiction. Instead, the arguments for the above Theorem
1.5
are based on Theorem
1.2
, which in turn uses some techniques involving
harmonic measure (see for example, Lemma
5.4
and Lemma
5.6
below).

We also remark that in Section
8.4
we
show a somewhat weaker variant of Theorem
1.5
which suffices for some applications to harmonic measure. This variant has the advantage that its proof does not rely on the lengthy arguments from
[
DT
]
.
Concerning the applications to harmonic measure, we mention that in a forthcoming work of Luis Lloret and the author
[
LT
]
, the following result is obtained
by using Theorem
1.5
as an essential tool.

Theorem
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
be an open set, and denote by
\omega
the harmonic measure for
\Omega
with pole at a fixed point in
\Omega
.
Let

Z=\big\{x\in{\partial\Omega}:\,\theta_{\omega}^{*,n}(x)=0\big\}.

Then, for
\omega
-a.e.

it holds

(1.4)

\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\sup_{0<s<r}\Theta_{\omega}(x,s)}{\Theta_{\omega}(x,r)}=+\infty.

Remark that, by the definition,
\Theta_{\omega}(x,r)\to 0
as

for every

with

as in the theorem. Then (

) asserts that
\Theta_{\omega}(x,r)
has arbitrarily large relative oscillations as it tends to

(equivalently, the density
\Theta_{\omega}(x,\cdot)
fails to be almost-increasing near

).
We recall that a deep result due to Wolff
[
Wo1
]
, in the complex plane, asserts that for any open set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, harmonic measure is concentrated in a set
of
\sigma
-finite length, which implies that
\omega(Z)=0
in this case. In higher dimensions this is not the case, and in fact, one may have

\dim_{H}(\omega)>n
for
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
, by another celebrated result of Wolff
[
Wo2
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

In the paper, constants denoted by
C
or
c
depend just on the dimension and perhaps other fixed
parameters. We will write
a\lesssim b
if there is
C>0
such that
a\leq Cb
. We write
a\approx b
if
a\lesssim b\lesssim a
.

2.1.
Measures, capacities, and the Riesz transform

We denote the Lebesgue measure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
by
{\mathcal{L}}^{d}
, although sometimes we also use the standard notation
dx
or
dy
to indicate integration
with respect to Lebesgue measure.

We say that a Radon measure
\mu
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
has polynomial
n
-growth if there exists some constant
C
such that

\mu(B(x,r))\leq C\,r^{n}\quad\mbox{ for all $x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}$, $r>0$.}

The measure
\mu
is called
n
-Ahlfors regular if

(2.1)

C^{-1}r^{n}\leq\mu(B(x,r))\leq Cr^{n}\quad\mbox{ for all $x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu$ and $0<r\leq{\rm diam}(\operatorname{supp}\mu)$.}

On the other hand,
\mu
is called uniformly
n
-rectifiable if it is
n
-Ahlfors regular and
there exist constants
\theta,M>0
such that for all
x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu
and all
0<r\leq{\rm diam}(\operatorname{supp}\mu)

there is a Lipschitz mapping
g
from the ball
B_{n}(0,r)
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
\text{Lip}(g)\leq M
such that

(2.2)

\mu(B(x,r)\cap g(B_{n}(0,r)))\geq\theta r^{n}.

The notion of uniform rectifiability is a quantitative version of
n
-rectifiability introduced by David and Semmes (see
[
DS1
]
and

[
DS2
]
) which has attracted much attention because its applications to harmonic analysis and PDE’s in rough settings, among other things.
A set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is
n
-Ahlfors regular if
{\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{E}
is
n
-Ahlfors regular, and it is uniformly
n
-rectifiable if

{\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{E}
is uniformly
n
-rectifiable.

Given a set
F\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, the notation
M_{+}(F)
stands for the
set of (positive) Radon measures supported on
F
, and
M_{1}(F)
stands for the
set of Radon probability measures supported on
F
.

For
s>0
, the
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure is denoted by
{\mathcal{H}}^{s}
, and the
s
-dimensional Hausdorff
content by
{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{\infty}
. Recall that, for any set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
,

{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{\infty}(E)=\inf\Big\{\sum_{i}{\rm diam}(A_{i})^{s}:E\subset\bigcup_{i}A_{i}\Big\}.

We say that
E
is lower
s
-content regular if there exists some constant
c>0
such that

{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{\infty}(B(x,r)\cap E)\geq c\,r^{s}\quad\text{ for all $x\in E$ and $0<r\leq{\rm diam}(E)$.}

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
denote the fundamental solution of the minus Laplacian in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
. That is,

{\mathcal{E}}_{n}(x)=\frac{c_{n}}{|x|^{n-1}},

for
n\geq 2
, and

{\mathcal{E}}_{1}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\,\log\frac{1}{|x|}

in the plane.
For a Radon measure
\mu
, we consider the potential defined by

U\mu(x)={\mathcal{E}}*\mu(x).

Given a set
F\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}

we define the capacity
{\operatorname{Cap}}(F)
by the identity

(2.3)

{\operatorname{Cap}}(F)=\frac{1}{\inf_{\mu\in M_{1}(F)}I(\mu)},

where the infimum is taken over all
probability
measures
\mu
supported on
F
and
I(\mu)
is the energy

I(\mu)=\iint{\mathcal{E}}_{n}(x-y)\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y)=\int U\mu(x)\,d\mu(x).

For
n\geq 2
and
F\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
,
{\operatorname{Cap}}(F)
is the Newtonian capacity of
F
, and for
n=1
and
F\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
,

{\operatorname{Cap}}(F)
is the Wiener capacity of
F
. In the plane, the logarithmic capacity
{\operatorname{Cap}}_{L}
, is defined by

{\operatorname{Cap}}_{L}(F)=e^{-\frac{2\pi}{{\operatorname{Cap}}(F)}}.

For
{\varepsilon}>0
, we define the smooth
{\varepsilon}
-truncated Riesz transform as follows. Fix a radial non-negative
C^{\infty}
bump function

{\varphi}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
which vanishes in
B(0,1)
and equals
1
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B(0,2)
. Then for any signed measure
\nu
, we write

{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{\varepsilon}\nu(x)=\int{\varphi}\Big(\frac{x-y}{{\varepsilon}}\Big)\,\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{n+1}}\,d\nu(y).

We define similarly
{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{\mu,{\varepsilon}}f
, for any positive measure
\mu
and
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mu)
.
It is immediate to check that

|{\mathcal{R}}_{\varepsilon}\nu(x)-{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{\varepsilon}\nu(x)|\lesssim M_{n}\nu(x).

Then, for any measure
\mu
with polynomial
n
-growth, the
L^{2}(\mu)
boundedness of
{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{\mu,{\varepsilon}}
is equivalent to the
the
L^{2}(\mu)
boundedness of
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu,{\varepsilon}}
. The advantage of
{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{\mu,{\varepsilon}}
over
{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu,{\varepsilon}}
is that the kernel
of
{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{\mu,{\varepsilon}}
is of Calderón-Zygmund type, uniformly on
{\varepsilon}
.

The David-Mattila lattice

(David, Mattila)

Denote by

\displaystyle\mu\bigl(\{x\in Q:{\rm dist}(x,E\setminus Q)\leq\lambda\,\ell(Q)\}\bigr)+\mu\bigl(\bigl\{x\in 3.5B_{Q}\setminus Q:{\rm dist}

\displaystyle(x,Q)\leq\lambda\,\ell(Q)\}\bigr)

Let

.

2.3.
{\mathcal{P}}
-doubling cubes, energies, and martingale decompositions in
L^{2}(\mu)

For
Q\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}
,
we denote

{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(Q)=\sum_{R\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}:R\supset Q}\frac{\ell(Q)}{\ell(R)^{n+1}}\,\mu(2B_{R}).

We say that a cube
Q
is
{\mathcal{P}}
-doubling if

{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(Q)\leq C_{d}\,\frac{\mu(2B_{Q})}{\ell(Q)^{n}},

for
C_{d}=4A_{0}^{n}
.
Notice that

{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(Q)\approx_{C_{0}}\sum_{R\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}:R\supset Q}\frac{\ell(Q)}{\ell(R)}\,\Theta_{\mu}(2B_{R}).

and thus saying that
Q
is
{\mathcal{P}}
-doubling implies that

\sum_{R\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}:R\supset Q}\frac{\ell(Q)}{\ell(R)}\,\Theta_{\mu}(2B_{R})\leq C_{d}^{\prime}\,\Theta_{\mu}(2B_{Q})

for some
C_{d}^{\prime}
depending on
C_{d}
. It is also immediate to check that then
2B_{Q}
is
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}
-doubling, with another constant depending on
C_{d}
. Conversely, the latter condition implies that
Q
is
{\mathcal{P}}
-doubling with another constant
C_{d}
depending on
C_{d}^{\prime}
.
The following is proven in
[
DT
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose that
C_{0}
and
A_{0}
are chosen suitably. If
Q\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}
is
{\mathcal{P}}
-doubling, then
Q\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}^{db}
.
Also, any cube
R\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}
such that
R\cap 2Q\neq\varnothing
and
\ell(R)=A_{0}\ell(Q)
belongs to
{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}^{db}
.

For technical reasons, in
[

]
the following discrete version of the density

is introduced. Given

, we let

\Theta_{\mu}(Q)=A_{0}^{kn}\quad\mbox{ if\, $\dfrac{\mu(2B_{Q})}{\ell(Q)^{n}}\in[A_{0}^{kn},A_{0}^{(k+1)n})$}.

Clearly,
\Theta_{\mu}(Q)\approx\Theta_{\mu}(2B_{Q})
. This discrete version of the density is appropriate for some of the stopping time arguments
involved in
[
DT
]
.

Given

and

, we denote by

the family of maximal cubes

satisfying

\ell(P)<\ell(Q),\qquad\Theta_{\mu}(P)\geq A_{0}^{kn}\Theta_{\mu}(Q).

For given

{\mathcal{E}}_{\infty}(\lambda Q)=\sup_{k\geq 1}\sum_{P\in{\mathsf{hd}}^{k}(Q)\cap{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}(\lambda Q)}\left(\frac{\ell(P)}{\ell(Q)}\right)^{\!1/2}\Theta_{\mu}(P)^{2}\,\mu(P).

These energies play an important role in some of the estimates involving the Riesz transform in
[

]
.
By Lemma 3.4 from
[

For some fixed

, we say that
Q\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}
is dominated from below, and we write

{\mathcal{E}}_{\infty}(9Q)\geq M_{0}^{2}\,\Theta_{\mu}(Q)^{2}\mu(9Q).

For

\Delta_{Q}f=\sum_{S\in{\mathcal{C}h}(Q)}m_{\mu,S}(f)\chi_{S}-m_{\mu,Q}(f)\chi_{Q},

where

stands for the average of

Then, for any cube

in the

3.
The approximation lemma

In this section we introduce some approximating measures and we will prove a technical result which will be necessary in the next sections.

Let
\mu
be a Radon measure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
and let
B_{0}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
be a closed ball. Suppose that
M_{n}\mu\in L^{2}(\mu|_{2B_{0}})
and

{\mathcal{R}}_{*}\mu\in L^{2}(\mu|_{2B_{0}})
.
We will define two approximating measures for
\mu
. To this end, we consider the dyadic lattice
{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}
associated with
\mu
from Theorem
2.1
and an integer
k
such that
\ell(Q)\ll\operatorname{rad}(B_{0})
for the cubes
Q\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu,k}
.
For each
Q\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu,k}
, let
S_{Q}
be a ball in
x_{Q}
, the center of
Q
, with radius

\operatorname{rad}(B(Q))/10
. Set
\Gamma_{Q}=\partial S_{Q}
. Denote by
I_{k}
the family of cubes from
{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu,k}
that intersect
2B_{0}
.
Then we let

(3.1)

\sigma_{k}=\chi_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\setminus 2B_{0}}\,\mu+\sum_{Q\in I_{k}}\frac{\mu(Q\cap 2B_{0})}{{\mathcal{H}}^{n}(\Gamma_{Q})}\,{\mathcal{H}}^{n}|_{\Gamma_{Q}}

and

(3.2)

{\widetilde{\sigma}}_{k}=\chi_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\setminus 2B_{0}}\,\mu+\sum_{Q\in I_{B_{0}}}\frac{\mu(Q\cap 2B_{0})}{{\mathcal{H}}^{n+1}(S_{Q})}\,{\mathcal{H}}^{n+1}|_{S_{Q}}.

Lemma 3.1

(Approximation Lemma)
.

Let
\mu
be a Radon measure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
and let
B_{0}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
be a closed ball. Suppose that
M_{n}\mu\in L^{2}(\mu|_{2B_{0}})
and

{\mathcal{R}}_{*}\mu\in L^{2}(\mu|_{2B_{0}})
. Let
\sigma_{k}
and
{\widetilde{\sigma}}_{k}
be as above.
Then the functions
M_{n}\sigma_{k}
,
{\mathcal{R}}_{*}\sigma_{k}
belong to
L^{2}(\sigma_{k}|_{2B_{0}})
, and
M_{n}{\widetilde{\sigma}}_{k}
,
{\mathcal{R}}_{*}{\widetilde{\sigma}}_{k}
belong to
L^{2}({\widetilde{\sigma}}_{k}|_{2B_{0}})
. Moreover,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{2B_{0}}|{\mathcal{R}}\sigma_{k}-m_{\sigma_{k},2B_{0}}

\displaystyle({\mathcal{R}}\sigma_{k})|^{2}\,d\sigma_{k}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{2B_{0}}|{\mathcal{R}}\mu-m_{\mu,2B_{0}}({\mathcal{R}}\mu)|^{2}\,d\mu+C\int_{2B_{0}}\theta_{\mu}^{n,*}(x)^{2}\,d\mu(x).

The same estimate holds with
\sigma_{k}
replaced by
{\widetilde{\sigma}}_{k}
.

Proof.

The fact that
M_{n}\sigma_{k}\in L^{2}(\sigma_{k}|_{2B_{0}})
and
M_{n}{\widetilde{\sigma}}_{k}\in L^{2}(\sigma_{k}|_{2B_{0}})
follow easily by checking that
for any
x\in 2B_{0}\cap S_{Q}
, with
Q\in I_{k}
,

(3.4)

M_{n}\sigma_{k}(x)+M_{n}{\widetilde{\sigma}}_{k}(x)\lesssim\inf_{y\in Q\cap 2B_{0}}M_{n}\mu(y).

We leave the details for the reader.

Next we show that
{\mathcal{R}}_{*}\sigma_{k}\in L^{2}(\sigma_{k}|_{2B_{0}})
, and (
3.3
).
We will use the smooth truncated Riesz transform
{{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{\varepsilon}}
. We take first
{\varepsilon}\geq A_{0}^{-k}
. Then for
x\in\Gamma_{Q}
,
with
Q\in I_{k}
,
we set

\displaystyle{{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{\varepsilon}}\sigma_{k}(x)-{{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{\varepsilon}}\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{P\in I_{k}}\big({{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{\varepsilon}}(\chi_{\Gamma_{P}}\sigma_{k})(x)-{{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{\varepsilon}}(\chi_{P\cap 2B_{0}}\mu)(x)\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{P\in I_{k}}\int K_{\varepsilon}(x-z)\,d(\sigma_{k}|_{\Gamma_{P}}-\mu|_{P\cap 2B_{0}})(z),

where
K_{\varepsilon}
is the kernel of
{{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{\varepsilon}}
.
Using that
\sigma_{k}(\Gamma_{P})=\mu(P\cap 2B_{0})
and denoting
\ell_{k}=A_{0}^{-k}
, we deduce

\displaystyle|{{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{\varepsilon}}\sigma_{k}(x)-{{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{\varepsilon}}\mu(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{P\in I_{k}}\int|K_{\varepsilon}(x-z)-K_{\varepsilon}(x-z_{P})|\,d|\sigma_{k}|_{\Gamma_{P}}-\mu|_{P\cap 2B_{0}}|(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{P\in I_{k}}\int\frac{\ell_{k}}{({\varepsilon}+|x-z|)^{n+1}}\,d|\sigma_{k}|_{\Gamma_{P}}-\mu|_{P\cap 2B_{0}}|(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{P\in I_{k}}\frac{\ell_{k}\,\mu(P)}{({\varepsilon}+{\rm dist}(x,P))^{n+1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\ell_{k}\,\mu(B(x,C\ell_{k}))}{{\varepsilon}^{n+1}}+\sum_{j\geq 0}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in I_{k}:\\
2^{j}\ell_{k}\leq{\rm dist}(x,P)<2^{j+1}\ell_{k}\end{subarray}}\!\!\frac{\ell_{k}\,\mu(P)}{{\rm dist}(x,P)^{n+1}}.

Recalling that
{\varepsilon}\geq\ell_{k}
, the first summand can be bounded above by
\Theta_{\mu}(B(x,C\ell_{k}))
.
We estimate the last sum as follows

\sum_{j\geq 0}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in I_{k}:\\
2^{j}\ell_{k}\leq{\rm dist}(x,P)<2^{j+1}\ell_{k}\end{subarray}}\!\!\frac{\ell_{k}\,\mu(P)}{{\rm dist}(x,P)^{n+1}}\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{\ell_{k}}{2^{j}\ell(P)}\,\frac{\mu(B(x_{P},2^{j}\ell(P))}{(2^{j}\ell(P))^{n}}\lesssim{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(Q).

Thus,

|{{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{\varepsilon}}\sigma_{k}(x)-{{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{\varepsilon}}\mu(x)|\lesssim{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(Q).

On the other hand, for
x\in\Gamma_{Q}
and
y\in Q
, we have

|{{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{\varepsilon}}\mu(x)-{{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{\varepsilon}}\mu(y)|\lesssim\int|K_{\varepsilon}(x-z)-K_{\varepsilon}(x-y)|\,d\mu(y)\lesssim\int\frac{\ell_{k}}{({\varepsilon}+|x-z|)^{n+1}}\,d\mu(z)\lesssim{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(Q).

Then, by the triangle inequality,

|{{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{\varepsilon}}\sigma_{k}(x)-{{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{\varepsilon}}\mu(y)|\lesssim{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(Q).

So, for any constant
\rho\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
, we have

(3.5)

|{{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{\varepsilon}}\sigma_{k}(x)-\rho|\leq\inf_{y\in Q\cap 2B_{0}}|{{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{\varepsilon}}\mu(y)-\rho|+C{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(Q)\quad\mbox{ for all $x\in\Gamma_{Q}$, ${\varepsilon}\geq\ell_{k}$.}

Using that

\sup_{{\varepsilon}>0}|{{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{\varepsilon}}\mu(y)|\leq{\mathcal{R}}_{*}\mu(y)+M_{n}\mu(y)

and taking
\rho=0
in (
3.5
), we obtain

(3.6)

\sup_{{\varepsilon}\geq\ell_{k}}|{{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{\varepsilon}}\sigma_{k}(x)|\leq\inf_{y\in Q\cap 2B_{0}}({\mathcal{R}}_{*}\mu(y)+CM_{n}\mu(y))+C{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(Q)\leq\inf_{y\in Q\cap 2B_{0}}({\mathcal{R}}_{*}\mu(y)+M_{n}\mu(y)).

On the other hand, from the construction of
\sigma_{k}
, for

, using the smoothness of
\Gamma_{Q}
, it follows easily that

|{{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{\varepsilon}}\sigma_{k}(x)-{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{\ell_{k}}\sigma_{k}(x)|\lesssim\Theta_{\mu}(B(x,C\ell_{k}))\quad\mbox{ for all $x\in\Gamma_{Q}$,}

for some fixed

.
Together with (
3.6
) and (

), this yields

{\mathcal{R}}_{*}\sigma_{k}(x)\leq\sup_{{\varepsilon}>0}|{{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{\varepsilon}}\sigma_{k}(x)|+CM_{n}\sigma_{k}(x)\leq\inf_{y\in Q\cap 2B_{0}}({\mathcal{R}}_{*}\mu(y)+CM_{n}\mu(y)).

Squaring and integrating on
2B_{0}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{2B_{0}}|{\mathcal{R}}_{*}\sigma_{k}|^{2}\,d\sigma_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{Q\in I_{k}}\sup_{x\in\Gamma_{Q}}|{\mathcal{R}}_{*}\sigma_{k}(x)|^{2}\,\sigma_{k}(Q)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{Q\in I_{k}}\inf_{y\in Q\cap 2B_{0}}|({\mathcal{R}}_{*}\mu(x)+CM_{n}\mu(y))^{2}\,\mu(Q\cap 2B_{0})

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{2B_{0}}|{\mathcal{R}}_{*}\mu|^{2}\,d\mu+C\int_{2B_{0}}|M_{n}\mu|^{2}\,d\mu<\infty,

which proves that
{\mathcal{R}}_{*}\sigma_{k}\in L^{2}(\sigma_{k})
.

Next we will show (
3.3
). Taking
\rho=m_{\mu,2B_{0}}({\mathcal{R}}\mu)
in (
3.5
),
and combining this with (
3.7
)
we obtain, for all

,

|{\mathcal{R}}\sigma_{k}(x)-\rho|\leq|{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{\ell_{k}}\sigma_{k}(x)-\rho|+\Theta_{\mu}(B(x,C\ell_{k}))\leq\inf_{y\in Q\cap 2B_{0}}|{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{\ell_{k}}\mu(y)-\rho|+C{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(Q).

Squaring and integrating on
2B_{0}
and taking into account that
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(Q)\approx{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(B(x,\ell_{k}))
for all
x\in Q\in I_{k}
, we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{2B_{0}}|{\mathcal{R}}\sigma_{k}-m_{\sigma_{k},2B_{0}}({\mathcal{R}}\sigma_{k})|^{2}\,d\sigma_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{2B_{0}}|{\mathcal{R}}\sigma_{k}(x)-\rho|^{2}\,d\sigma_{k}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{2B_{0}}|{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{\ell_{k}}\mu(x)-\rho|^{2}\,d\mu+C\int_{2B_{0}}{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(B(x,\ell_{k}))^{2}\,d\mu(x).

Since
{\mathcal{R}}_{*}\mu\in L^{2}(\mu|_{2B_{0}})
, by dominated convergence, it follows that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{2B_{0}}|{\widetilde{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{\ell_{k}}\mu(x)-\rho|^{2}\,d\mu=\int_{2B_{0}}|{\mathcal{R}}\mu(x)-\rho|^{2}\,d\mu=\int_{2B_{0}}|{\mathcal{R}}\mu(x)-m_{\mu,2B_{0}}({\mathcal{R}}\mu)|^{2}\,d\mu.

Hence, to prove (
3.3

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{2B_{0}}{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(B(x,\ell_{k}))^{2}\,d\mu(x)\lesssim\int_{2B_{0}}\theta_{\mu}^{n,*}(x)^{2}\,d\mu(x).

It is easy to check that if

\limsup_{k\to\infty}{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(B(x,\ell_{k}))\lesssim\theta_{\mu}^{n,*}(x).

By dominated convergence, then

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{2B_{0}}{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(B(x,\ell_{k}))^{2}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{2B_{0}}\sup_{j\geq k}{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(B(x,\ell_{j}))^{2}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{2B_{0}}\limsup_{k\to\infty}{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(B(x,\ell_{j}))^{2}\,d\mu(x)\lesssim\int_{2B_{0}}\theta_{\mu}^{n,*}(x)^{2}\,d\mu(x),

as wished. This concludes the proof of (
3.3
).

The proof (

) for

is essentially the same as the one above for
\sigma_{k}
and we skip it.

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\gamma}{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(B_{0})^{2}\mu(2B_{0})+\int_{2B_{0}}|{\mathcal{R}}\mu-m_{\mu,2B_{0}}({\mathcal{R}}\mu)|^{2}\,d\mu+\int_{2B_{0}}\theta_{\mu}^{n,*}(x)^{2}\,d\mu(x).

\int_{R_{0}}\!|{\mathcal{R}}(\chi_{2B_{0}\setminus R_{0}}\mu)|^{2}\,d\mu\lesssim_{\gamma}\!{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(B_{0})^{2}\mu(2B_{0})+\int_{2B_{0}}\!|{\mathcal{R}}\mu-m_{\mu,2B_{0}}({\mathcal{R}}\mu)|^{2}\,d\mu+\int_{2B_{0}}\!\!\theta_{\mu}^{n,*}(x)^{2}\,d\mu(x).

\mu
-a.e.

-doubling cubes

.

The main lemma

Let

\mu

Lemma 5.2
.

If
B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
is
(\mu,C_{2})
-good, then

{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{\infty}(B\cap\operatorname{supp}\mu)\geq c\,C_{2}^{-1}\,\operatorname{rad}(B)^{n}.

Since

\mu\big(\big\{x\in B:M_{n}(\chi_{B}\mu)>2C_{2}\,\Theta_{\mu}(B)\big\}\big)\leq\frac{1}{2C_{2}\,\Theta_{\mu}(B)}\int_{B}M_{n}(\chi_{B}\mu)\,d\mu\leq\frac{1}{2}\,\mu(B).

for all

\sigma(E)=\frac{1}{2C_{2}\,\Theta_{\mu}(B)}\,\mu(E)\geq\frac{1}{8C_{2}\,\Theta_{\mu}(B)}\,\mu(B)=\frac{\operatorname{rad}(B)^{n}}{8C_{2}}.

By Frostman’s lemma, this implies that

{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{\infty}(B\cap\operatorname{supp}\mu)\geq{\mathcal{H}}^{n}_{\infty}(E)\gtrsim\sigma(E)\geq\frac{\operatorname{rad}(B)^{n}}{8C_{2}}.

Lemma 5.3

Proof.

We denote

\sigma_{p}(B_{1})=\Theta_{\mu}(B_{1})^{p}\mu(B_{1}).

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\mu
be a Radon measure and let

be a ball.
Suppose that

({\mathcal{P}}_{\mu},4)
-doubling. Then

Proof.

The condition

is equivalent to the inequality

\sum_{j\geq 1}2^{-j+1}\Theta_{\mu}(2^{j}B)>\frac{3}{2}\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{-j}\Theta_{\mu}(2^{j}B)=\frac{3}{2}\Theta_{\mu}(B)+\frac{3}{4}\sum_{j\geq 1}2^{-j+1}\Theta_{\mu}(2^{j}B).

That is,

\frac{1}{6}\sum_{j\geq 1}2^{-j+1}\Theta_{\mu}(2^{j}B)>\Theta_{\mu}(B),

or

(6.3)

\frac{1}{3}\sum_{j\geq 1}2^{-j}\Theta_{\mu}(2^{j}B)>\Theta_{\mu}(B),

In turn, the fact that

is not

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\mu
be a Radon measure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
and let

be a
({\mathcal{P}}_{\mu},C_{0})
-doubling ball. Suppose that there exists some ball

centered in
B_{0}
such that

\alpha>0

with
\delta_{1}\in(0,1/100)

Let

is large enough, depending on

and

({\mathcal{P}}_{\mu},4)
-doubling, with

\eta=\frac{1}{1+3n}.

Proof.

Let

be the subset of the indices

such that

is
({\mathcal{P}}_{\mu},4)
-doubling. By the preceding lemma, if

On the other hand, if

{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(2^{j}B_{1})=\sum_{k\geq 0}2^{-k}\Theta_{\mu}(2^{j+k}B)\leq 2^{n}\sum_{k\geq 0}2^{-k}\Theta_{\mu}(2^{j+1+k}B)=2^{n}\,{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(2^{j+1}B_{1}).

Suppose that there are
\theta N
balls

, with

, which are not
({\mathcal{P}}_{\mu},4)
-doubling.
Then,

\displaystyle\alpha\,\Theta_{\mu}(B_{0})

\displaystyle\leq{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(B_{1})\lesssim{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(2B_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\frac{2}{3}\bigg)^{\theta N}\big(2^{n}\big)^{(1-\theta)N}\,{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(2^{N+1}B_{1})\lesssim\bigg(\frac{2}{3}\bigg)^{\theta N}\big(2^{n}\big)^{(1-\theta)N}\,C_{0}\,\Theta_{\mu}(B_{0}).

\mu
by another
n
-Ahlfors regular measure
{\widetilde{\mu}}
such that

is bounded in

, which implies that
{\widetilde{\mu}}
is uniformly rectifiable, by

We assume that we are under the assumptions of Theorem
1.5
.
We consider the David-Mattila lattice associated with
\mu
and we denote by
{\mathcal{F}}_{\max}
the family of maximal cubes from
{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}
that
intersect
1.5B_{0}
and are contained in
1.8B_{0}
, so that the cubes
Q\in{\mathcal{F}}_{\max}
satisfy
\ell(Q)\approx\operatorname{rad}(B_{0})
.
We denote

R_{0}=\bigcup_{Q\in{\mathcal{F}}_{\max}}Q,

so that

\frac{3}{2}B_{0}\cap\operatorname{supp}(\mu)\subset R_{0}\subset 2B_{0}.

Let
0<\theta_{0}\ll 1
be a very small constant to be fixed later. We denote by
{\mathsf{LD}}
the family of those cubes from

{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}
which are contained in
R_{0}
such that
\Theta_{\mu}(3.5B_{Q})\leq\theta_{0}\,\Theta_{\mu}(B_{0})
and have maximal side length.

We will prove the following (compare with Key Lemma 6.1 from
[
GT
]
):

Key Lemma 8.3
.

Assume
\theta_{0}\in(0,1/10)
small enough.
Then there exists some constant
{\varepsilon}_{0}
such that if

\delta_{1}\in(0,1/2)
is small enough, depending on
n
,
C_{0}
,
C_{1}
, and
\alpha
, and

{\varepsilon}
is small enough, depending on
C_{0}
,
C_{1}
,
\alpha
,
\theta_{0}
, and
\delta_{0}
, then

(8.4)

\mu\Biggl(\bigcup_{Q\in{\mathsf{LD}}}Q\Biggr)\leq(1-{\varepsilon}_{0})\,\mu(R_{0}).

We will deduce this result from Theorem
1.2
and Remark
6.4
with
\lambda=\frac{4}{3}
, applied to a suitable approximating measure
\eta
.
To this end, we will argue by contradiction, assuming that

(8.5)

\mu\Biggl(\bigcup_{Q\in{\mathsf{LD}}}Q\Biggr)>(1-{\varepsilon}_{0})\,\mu(R_{0})

Next we need to construct another family of stopping cubes which we will denote by
{\mathsf{Stop}}
. This is
defined as follows. For each
Q\in{\mathsf{LD}}
we consider the family of maximal cubes contained in
Q

from
{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}^{db}

with side length at most
\theta_{0}^{1/(n+1)}\ell(Q)
. We denote this family by

{\mathsf{Stop}}(Q)
.
Then we define

{\mathsf{Stop}}=\bigcup_{Q\in{\mathsf{LD}}}{\mathsf{Stop}}(Q).

Note that, by the properties of the lattice
{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}
, for each
Q\in{\mathsf{LD}}
, the cubes from
{\mathsf{Stop}}(Q)
cover
\mu
-almost all
Q
.
So (
8.5
) is equivalent to

\mu\Biggl(\bigcup_{Q\in{\mathsf{Stop}}}Q\Biggr)>(1-{\varepsilon}_{0})\,\mu(R_{0})

The family
{\mathsf{Stop}}
may consist of an infinite number of cubes.
For technical reasons, it is convenient to consider a finite subfamily of
{\mathsf{Stop}}
which contains a very big proportion of the
\mu
measure of union of the cubes from
{\mathsf{Stop}}
. So we let
{\mathsf{Stop}}_{0}
be a
finite
subfamily of
{\mathsf{Stop}}

such that

(8.6)

\mu\Biggl(\bigcup_{Q\in{\mathsf{Stop}}_{0}}Q\Biggr)>(1-2{\varepsilon}_{0})\,\mu(R_{0}).

Recall also that, by construction the cubes
P\in{\mathsf{Stop}}_{0}
satisfy
\ell(P)\lesssim\theta_{0}^{1/(n+1)}\operatorname{rad}(B_{0})
.

Lemma 8.4
.

We have:

\Theta_{\mu}(2B_{Q})\leq{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(Q)\lesssim\theta_{0}^{1/(n+1)}\Theta_{\mu}(B_{0})\quad\mbox{ for all $Q\in{\mathsf{Stop}}$.}

The proof of this lemma is essentially the same as the one of Lemma 6.2 from
[
GT
]
and we skip it.

Now we will define an auxiliary measure
\mu_{0}
. First, given a small constant
0<\kappa_{0}\ll 1
(to be fixed below) and
Q\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu}
, we consider
the following “inner region” of
Q
:

(8.7)

I_{\kappa_{0}}(Q)=\{x\in Q:{\rm dist}(x,\operatorname{supp}\mu\setminus Q)\geq\kappa_{0}\ell(Q)\}.

We set

(8.8)

\mu_{0}=\mu|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\setminus R_{0}}+\sum_{Q\in{\mathsf{Stop}}_{0}}\mu|_{I_{\kappa_{0}}(Q)}.

By the doubling property and small boundary condition (
2.7
) of
Q\in{\mathsf{Stop}}_{0}
, we have

\mu(Q\setminus I_{\kappa_{0}}(Q))\lesssim\kappa_{0}^{1/2}\,\mu(3.5B_{Q})\lesssim\kappa_{0}^{1/2}\,\mu(Q).

Then we get the following estimate for the total variation of

\mu-\mu_{0}
:

(8.9)

\displaystyle\|\mu-\mu_{0}\|

\displaystyle=\mu(R_{0})-\mu_{0}(R_{0})

\displaystyle=\bigg(\mu(R_{0})-\sum_{Q\in{\mathsf{Stop}}_{0}}\mu(Q)\bigg)+\sum_{Q\in{\mathsf{Stop}}_{0}}\big(\mu(Q)-\mu(I_{\kappa_{0}}(Q))\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim 2{\varepsilon}_{0}\,\mu(R_{0})+\kappa_{0}^{1/2}\sum_{Q\in{\mathsf{Stop}}_{0}}\mu(Q)\leq(2{\varepsilon}_{0}+\kappa_{0}^{1/2})\,\mu(R_{0}).

We deduce the following (compare with Lemma 6.4 from
[
GT
]
):

Lemma 8.5
.

We have

\int_{2B_{0}}|{\mathcal{R}}\mu_{0}-m_{\mu_{0},2B_{0}}({\mathcal{R}}\mu_{0})|^{2}\,d\mu_{0}\lesssim({\varepsilon}+{\varepsilon}_{0}+\kappa_{0}^{1/2})\,\Theta_{\mu}(B_{0})^{2}\,\mu(B_{0}).

Proof.

By (
8.9
) and the fact that
\|{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}\|_{L^{2}(\mu|_{2B_{0}})\to L^{2}(\mu|_{2B_{0}})}\leq C_{1}\Theta_{\mu}(B_{0})
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{2B_{0}}|{\mathcal{R}}

\displaystyle\mu_{0}-m_{\mu_{0},2B_{0}}({\mathcal{R}}\mu_{0})|^{2}\,d\mu_{0}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{2B_{0}}|{\mathcal{R}}\mu_{0}-m_{\mu,2B_{0}}({\mathcal{R}}\mu)|^{2}\,d\mu_{0}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{2B_{0}}|{\mathcal{R}}\mu-m_{\mu,2B_{0}}({\mathcal{R}}\mu)|^{2}\,d\mu_{0}+2\int_{2B_{0}}|{\mathcal{R}}\mu_{0}-{\mathcal{R}}\mu|^{2}\,d\mu_{0}

\displaystyle\leq 2{\varepsilon}\,\Theta_{\mu}(B_{0})^{2}\,\mu(B_{0})+C_{1}\Theta_{\mu}(B_{0})^{2}\,\|\mu-\mu_{0}\|\lesssim({\varepsilon}+{\varepsilon}_{0}+C_{1}\kappa_{0}^{1/2})\,\Theta_{\mu}(B_{0})^{2}\,\mu(B_{0}).

∎

Lemma 8.6
.

For all
Q\in{\mathsf{Stop}}
, we have

\int_{1.1B_{Q}\setminus Q}\int_{Q}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\,d\mu_{0}(x)\,d\mu_{0}(y)\lesssim\theta_{0}^{\frac{1}{2(n+1)^{2}}}\Theta_{\mu}(B_{0})\,\mu_{0}(Q).

The proof is essentially the same as the one of

We consider now another auxiliary measure:

\eta=\mu_{0}|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\setminus R_{0}}+\sum_{Q\in{\mathsf{Stop}}_{0}}\mu_{0}(Q)\,\frac{{\mathcal{H}}^{n+1}|_{\tfrac{1}{4}B(Q)}}{{\mathcal{H}}^{n+1}\bigl(\tfrac{1}{4}B(Q)\bigr)}.

which is absolutely continuous with respect to

By Remark 5.2 from

So the balls

We denote

(8.10)

S_{0}=\bigcup_{Q\in{\mathsf{Stop}}_{0}}\tfrac{1}{4}B(Q),

so that

\operatorname{supp}(\eta)\subset\overline{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\setminus R_{0}}\cup{S_{0}},

with the union being disjoint. Here we assumed the balls

,

Lemma 8.7
.

Under the assumption (
8.5
), the following hold:

\int_{S_{0}}|{\mathcal{R}}\eta-m_{\eta,S_{0}}({\mathcal{R}}\eta)|^{2}\,d\eta\lesssim{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\,\Theta_{\mu}(B_{0})^{2}\eta(S_{0}),

{\varepsilon}^{\prime}={\varepsilon}+{\varepsilon}_{0}+\kappa_{0}^{1/2}+\kappa_{0}^{-2n-2}\,\theta_{0}^{\frac{1}{(n+1)^{2}}}

Notice that

is a bounded operator in

is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure and has a bounded density, since the number of

is bounded. This implies that

is bounded in

(although we do not have a good quantitative control on its norm). Also the fact that

is bounded in

is bounded in

On the other hand, the fact that

implies that

is well defined

-a.e. in

as a principal value in a BMO sense and that

{\mathcal{R}}_{*}(\chi_{2B_{0}}\eta)\in L^{2}(\eta|_{2B_{0}}).

Proof of Lemma
8.7
.

We consider the function

f=\chi_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\setminus R_{0}}{\mathcal{R}}\mu_{0}+\sum_{Q\in{\mathsf{Stop}}_{0}}m_{\mu_{0},Q}({\mathcal{R}}\mu_{0})\,\chi_{Q}.

Recall that the cubes from
{\mathsf{Stop}}_{0}

. It is clear that

and also, by Lemma
8.5
,

(8.12)

\int_{R_{0}}|f-c_{R_{0}}|^{2}\,d\mu_{0}\leq\int_{R_{0}}|{\mathcal{R}}\mu_{0}-m_{\mu_{0},R_{0}}({\mathcal{R}}\mu_{0})|^{2}\,d\mu_{0}\lesssim({\varepsilon}+{\varepsilon}_{0}+\kappa_{0}^{1/2})\,\Theta_{\mu}(B_{0})^{2}\,\mu(B_{0}).

Next we will estimate
|{\mathcal{R}}\eta(x)-c_{R_{0}}|
for
x\in\operatorname{supp}(\eta)\cap R_{0}
. Recall that
S_{0}
is defined in (
8.10
). For
x\in S_{0}
and
Q\in{\mathsf{Stop}}_{0}
such that
x\in\tfrac{1}{4}B(Q)
, we write

(8.13)

\displaystyle\bigl|{\mathcal{R}}\eta(x)-c_{R_{0}}\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\bigl|{\mathcal{R}}(\chi_{\tfrac{1}{4}B(Q)}\eta)(x)\bigr|+\bigl|{\mathcal{R}}(\chi_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\setminus\tfrac{1}{4}B(Q)}\eta)(x)-{\mathcal{R}}(\chi_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\setminus Q}\mu_{0})(x)\bigr|

\displaystyle\quad+\bigl|{\mathcal{R}}(\chi_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\setminus Q}\mu_{0})(x)-m_{\mu_{0},Q}({\mathcal{R}}\mu_{0})\bigr|+\bigl|m_{\mu_{0},Q}({\mathcal{R}}\mu_{0})-c_{R_{0}}\bigr|

\displaystyle=:T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}+\bigl|m_{\mu_{0},Q}({\mathcal{R}}\mu_{0})-c_{R_{0}}\bigr|.

Using that

\eta|_{\tfrac{1}{4}B(Q)}=\mu_{0}(Q)\,\frac{{\mathcal{H}}^{n+1}|_{\tfrac{1}{4}B(Q)}}{{\mathcal{H}}^{n+1}\bigl(\tfrac{1}{4}B(Q)\bigr)},

together with Lemma

Next we will deal with the term
T_{3}
in (
8.13
).
To this end, for
x\in\tfrac{1}{4}B(Q)
we set

(8.14)

\displaystyle\bigl|{\mathcal{R}}(

\displaystyle\chi_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\setminus Q}\mu_{0})(x)-m_{\mu_{0},Q}({\mathcal{R}}\mu_{0})\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\bigl|{\mathcal{R}}(\chi_{1.1B_{Q}\setminus Q}\mu_{0})(x)\bigr|+\bigl|{\mathcal{R}}(\chi_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\setminus 1.1B_{Q}}\mu_{0})(x)-m_{\mu_{0},Q}({\mathcal{R}}(\chi_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\setminus 1.1B_{Q}}\mu_{0}))\bigr|

\displaystyle\quad+\bigl|m_{\mu_{0},Q}({\mathcal{R}}(\chi_{1.1B_{Q}\setminus Q}\mu_{0}))\bigr|,

taking into account that

The first term on the right hand side satisfies

\bigl|{\mathcal{R}}(\chi_{1.1B_{Q}\setminus Q}\mu_{0})(x)\bigr|\leq\int_{1.1B_{Q}\setminus Q}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\,d\mu_{0}(y)\lesssim\frac{\mu_{0}(1.1B_{Q})}{\operatorname{rad}(B(Q))^{n}}\lesssim\theta_{0}^{1/(n+1)}\Theta_{\mu}(B_{0}),

recalling that

and that
\Theta_{\mu}(1.1B_{Q})\lesssim\theta_{0}^{1/(n+1)}\Theta_{\mu}(B_{0})
for the last estimate, by Lemma

.

Now we turn our attention to the second term on the right hand side of (

x^{\prime}\in Q\in{\mathsf{Stop}}_{0}

\displaystyle\bigl|{\mathcal{R}}(\chi_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\setminus 1.1B_{Q}}\mu_{0})(x)-{\mathcal{R}}

\displaystyle(\chi_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\setminus 1.1B_{Q}}\mu_{0})(x^{\prime})\bigr|

Taking into account that the distance both from

.
Averaging on

\displaystyle T_{3}

To deal with the term

\displaystyle=\bigl|{\mathcal{R}}(\chi_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\setminus\tfrac{1}{4}B(Q)}\eta)(x)-{\mathcal{R}}(\chi_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\setminus Q}\mu_{0})(x)\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{R\in{\mathsf{Stop}}_{0}:R\neq Q}\int|K(x-y)-K(x-x_{R})|\,d(\eta_{\frac{1}{4}B(R)}+\mu_{0}|_{R}),

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\frac{\ell(R)\,h(y)}{\bigl(\ell(R)+|z-y|\bigr)^{n+1}}\,d\mu_{0}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{|z-y|\leq\ell(R)}\cdots\,\,\,+\sum_{j\geq 1}\int_{2^{j-1}\ell(R)<|z-y|\leq 2^{j-1}\ell(R)}\cdots

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 0}\;\;\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.57222pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.91281pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.30664pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.13547pt}}\!\int_{B(z,2^{j}\ell(R))}h\,d\mu_{0}\;\,\frac{2^{-j}\,\mu_{0}(B(z,2^{j}\ell(R)))}{\bigl(2^{j}\ell(R)\bigr)^{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim M_{\mu_{0}}h(z)\,{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu_{0}}\bigl(B(z,\ell(R))\bigr),

where
M_{\mu_{0}}
stands for the centered maximal Hardy-Littlewood operator with respect to
\mu_{0}
. Then, by (
8.25
),

\displaystyle\int g\,h\,d\mu_{0}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{R\in{\mathsf{Stop}}_{0}}\inf_{z\in R}\big[M_{\mu_{0}}h(z)\,{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu_{0}}\bigl(B(z,\ell(R))\bigr)\bigl]\,\mu_{0}(R)\lesssim\int_{R_{0}}M_{\mu_{0}}h\,\,p_{\mu_{0}}\,d\mu_{0}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|M_{\mu_{0}}h\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{0})}\,\|p_{\mu_{0}}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{0}|_{R_{0}})}\lesssim\|h\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{0})}\,\|p_{\mu_{0}}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{0}|_{R_{0}})}.

Thus, defining
p_{\mu}
in the same way as
p_{\mu_{0}}
, with
\mu_{0}
replaced by
\mu
, by Lemma
8.4
,

(8.26)

\|{\widetilde{g}}\|_{L^{2}(\eta|_{R_{0}})}^{2}=\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{0}|_{R_{0}})}^{2}\lesssim\|p_{\mu_{0}}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{0}|_{R_{0}})}^{2}\leq\|p_{\mu}\|_{L^{2}(\mu|_{R_{0}})}^{2}\lesssim\theta_{0}^{2/(n+1)}\Theta_{\mu}(B_{0})^{2}\mu(R_{0}).

Plugging this estimate into (
8.24
), using (
8.12
), and recalling that
\|{\widetilde{g}}\|_{L^{2}(\eta|_{R_{0}})}=\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{0}|_{R_{0}})}
, we obtain

(8.27)

\displaystyle\bigl\|{\mathcal{R}}\eta-c_{R_{0}}\bigr\|_{L^{2}(\eta|_{S_{0}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\biggl({\varepsilon}+{\varepsilon}_{0}+\kappa_{0}^{1/2}+\theta_{0}^{\frac{1}{(n+1)^{2}}}+\frac{1}{\kappa_{0}^{2n+2}}\,\theta_{0}^{\frac{2}{n+1}}\biggr)\Theta_{\mu}(B_{0})^{2}\,\eta(R_{0})

\displaystyle\lesssim\biggl({\varepsilon}+{\varepsilon}_{0}+\kappa_{0}^{1/2}+\frac{1}{\kappa_{0}^{2n+2}}\,\theta_{0}^{\frac{1}{(n+1)^{2}}}\biggr)\,\Theta_{\mu}(B_{0})^{2}\,\eta(R_{0}),

which implies the lemma.
∎

In this section we show a weaker variant of Theorem

which suffices for some applications to harmonic measure. It has the advantage that its proof is based on Main Lemma
5.1
, instead of Theorem

, which in turn relies on the lengthy arguments from

. Remark that the arguments for Main Lemma

require neither Theorem

not the results from

Theorem 8.8
.

Let
\mu
be a Radon measure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
and let
B_{0}
be a
({\mathcal{P}}_{\mu},C_{0})
-doubling ball. Suppose that
the following conditions hold:

(a)

{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu|_{2B_{0}}}
is bounded in

with

\|{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}\|_{L^{2}(\mu|_{2B_{0}})\to L^{2}(\mu|_{2B_{0}})}\leq C_{1}\Theta_{\mu}(B_{0})

and

\sup_{0<r\leq 4\operatorname{rad}(B_{0})}\Theta_{\mu}(x,r)\leq C_{1}\Theta_{\mu}(B_{0})\quad\mbox{ for all $x\in 2B_{0}$}.

(b’)

There exists some ball
B_{1}
centered in
B_{0}
such that, for some
\delta_{1}\in(0,1/2)
,

\operatorname{rad}(B_{1})\leq\delta_{1}\operatorname{rad}(B_{0})

and for some constant

,

\Theta_{\mu}(\lambda B_{1})\geq\alpha\,\Theta_{\mu}(B_{0})\quad\mbox{ for $\lambda\geq 1$ such that $\lambda B_{1}\subset 2B_{0}$}.

(c)

For some
{\varepsilon}>0
,

\int_{2B_{0}}|{\mathcal{R}}\mu-m_{\mu,2B_{0}}({\mathcal{R}}\mu)|^{2}\,d\mu\leq{\varepsilon}\,\Theta_{\mu}(B_{0})^{2}\,\mu(B_{0}).

Suppose that
\delta_{1}
and

are small enough, depending on
n
,

,

, and

. Then
there is some uniformly
n
-rectifiable set

and some

such that

\mu(\Gamma\cap B_{0})\geq\tau\,\mu(B_{0}),

with
\tau
and the uniform rectifiability constant of

The difference between this theorem and Theorem

is that in the condition (b) in Theorem

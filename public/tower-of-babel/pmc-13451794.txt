Real-world long-term outcomes of clopidogrel-based dual antiplatelet therapy post-drug-eluting stent implantation in all comers patients: A retrospective evaluation
Authors: Rohan Parikh, Jayshree Langade, Deepak Langade
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Study objectiveThe objective of this study was to describe the real-world clinical outcomes among patients receiving clopidogrel-based dual antiplatelet therapy following PCI with drug-eluting stents.DesignThis was a retrospective study. Outcomes were assessed by clopidogrel–aspirin DAPT, lipid-lowering therapy, and stent type.SettingTertiary care cardiology centre in India to evaluate real-world outcomes following PCI with drug-eluting stents (DES) in all patients.ParticipantsThe study included 1039 PCI-DES patients.InterventionsClopidogrel–aspirin DAPT.Main outcome measuresAnalyses covered event rates, DAPT duration, repeat interventions, and LVEF. Introduction

Cardiovascular diseases (CVD) have emerged as a leading public health challenge in India, accounting for 28.1% of all deaths and 14.1% of disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) by 2016, nearly double the burden reported in 1990 [1]. Cost-effectiveness of clopidogrel is in a range comparable to other therapies currently utilized for acute coronary syndromes [2]. Globally, CVD remains the foremost cause of mortality, responsible for approximately one-third of all deaths, with IHD contributing the largest share [3], [4]. Among acute coronary syndromes (ACS), non-ST elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI) represents a substantial proportion of cases, particularly in patients with severe left ventricular (LV) dysfunction with an ejection fraction (EF) ≤35% [5], [6]. These individuals face elevated risks of heart failure, arrhythmias, and cardiovascular death. Management of NSTEMI in this high-risk population is complex, often involving multivessel disease, reduced myocardial reserve, and uncertain viability [7].

Percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) with DES is the cornerstone of revascularization therapy in patients presenting with ACS, including STEMI and NSTEMI. DES have significantly reduced rates of in-stent restenosis and target lesion revascularization compared to bare-metal stents, owing to their antiproliferative drug coatings that inhibit neointimal hyperplasia [8]. Optimal post-PCI outcomes rely heavily on pharmacologic strategies, particularly DAPT and lipid-lowering agents. Clopidogrel combined with aspirin remains the most widely used DAPT regimen, especially in resource-limited settings, due to its accessibility, cost-effectiveness [9], and favourable safety profile. [10], [11] Despite the availability of potent P2Y12 inhibitors like ticagrelor and prasugrel, real-world data continue to support clopidogrel as the default agent in many populations. [12]

However, the efficacy of clopidogrel may be influenced by genetic resistance, bleeding risk, and duration of therapy [13].

Statins are central to lipid management post-PCI, with rosuvastatin frequently prescribed as monotherapy. Its high potency and tolerability make it sufficient for achieving LDL-C targets in most patients, minimizing the need for adjunctive agents such as ezetimibe or PCSK9 inhibitors like evolocumab and alirocumab. [14] Fixed-dose formulations of rosuvastatin, clopidogrel, and aspirin have also shown promise in improving compliance and reducing cardiovascular risk. [15]

While guidelines recommend DAPT for at least 12 months following DES implantation in ACS patients, the optimal duration beyond one year remains debated, especially in high-risk subgroups [16]. Major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) including ischemic and embolic stroke, heart failure (HF), and stent thrombosis, continue to be key endpoints in post-PCI surveillance. These events are influenced by multiple factors, including baseline left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), number of stents deployed, and duration of DAPT [17]. Thus, the duration of event-free survival is a critical metric for evaluating therapeutic success, especially in patients with varying infarct types, stent burden, and left ventricular function. [14] This study evaluates the real-world therapeutic patterns and outcomes in post-PCI patients, with focus on the clopidogrel-based DAPT therapy, and rosuvastatin monotherapy, and the relationship between stent type and clinical events. By analyzing event-free survival and repeat intervention rates, the study seeks to inform practical, evidence-based strategies for improved long-term clinical outcomes.

Methods

Study design and setting

The study was single-center retrospective observational study conducted at a single tertiary care cardiology center. Patients were followed according to routine clinical practice, and only those with complete follow-up data were included in the final analysis. Data was extracted from institutional records and follow-up documentation, allowing stratification by stent type, infarct subtype, and LVEF status. Patients were primarily treated with clopidogrel-based DAPT and rosuvastatin monotherapy. Clinical outcomes including MACE, repeat interventions, and LDL-C levels were analyzed across subgroups defined by age, sex, infarct location, stent burden, and follow-up duration. This retrospective observational study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Ethical approval was obtained/exempted by the Institutional Ethics Committee, as the study involved analysis of existing data without direct patient interaction.

Study participants

The study included data of 1039, men and women aged 18 years and above who were diagnosed with CAD (with or without MI), and underwent PCI with the implantation of at least one DES. Additionally, patients were required to have complete follow-up data available for evaluating clinical outcomes and medication adherence. Clinical outcomes were ascertained from institutional medical records and available follow-up documentation. All patients received rosuvastatin 20 mg/day post-PCI.

Study objectives and outcomes

The primary objective of this study was to describe the real-world clinical outcomes among patients receiving clopidogrel-based dual antiplatelet therapy following PCI with drug-eluting stents. The secondary objectives of the study were to evaluate clinical outcomes across different follow-up durations in STEMI and NSTEMI subgroups, and to identify predictors of repeat interventions and adverse cardiovascular events. For the purpose of this retrospective study, MACE was defined as the composite of ischemic stroke, embolic stroke, and heart failure, based on the cardiovascular outcomes consistently documented in the medical records. Key variables assessed for their association with these outcomes were age, sex, myocardial infarction type, number of stents implanted, left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) status, LDL-C levels, and vessel size. The study also compared restenosis and event rates among various DES types to determine if any significant differences existed in long-term outcomes.

Sample size

Data of 1042 patients were screened, of whom 1039 were included in the analysis. A total of 1349 stents were deployed in these patients. Patient-level clinical outcomes were analyzed at the patient level, while stent-related variables were assessed descriptively according to stent type.

Statistical methods and data analysis

Statistical analysis for this single-centre, retrospective study involved both descriptive and inferential methods to evaluate clinical outcomes across different drug-eluting stent (DES) types. Patient data including demographics, ACS subtype, stent characteristics, LVEF, and follow-up outcomes were extracted from institutional records and validated for completeness. Categorical variables such as MACE incidence, redo procedures, and DAPT duration were analyzed using chi-square or Fisher's exact tests, while continuous variables like changes in LVEF were assessed using independent t-tests or non-parametric equivalents based on data distribution. One-way ANOVA tests were applied for comparisons across multiple stent groups. Subgroup analyses were performed based on ACS subtype (STEMI vs. NSTEMI), LVEF categories, stent count, and follow-up duration to identify predictors of adverse outcomes. A p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were conducted using windows based statistical software (SPSS 17, IBM Corp., CA, US).

Effectiveness of DAPT

Table 3 presents data from 1039 patients treated with clopidogrel-based DAPT, demonstrating a notably low overall incidence of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) at 0.29%. Specifically, single instances of ischemic stroke, embolic stroke, and heart failure (HF) were observed. Event rates were marginally higher among patients followed for ≥12 months (0.39%) versus those with less than 12 months of follow-up (0.19%). No events occurred in patients with follow-up periods shorter than 6 months or between 12 and < 24 months, while individuals followed for ≥24 months experienced a moderate increase (0.76%). All reported events occurred in patients aged ≥55 years (0.48%), and the event rate among females (0.40%) slightly exceeded that of males (0.25%). Patients presenting with STEMI demonstrated a low event rate (0.28%), with all occurrences attributed to anterior wall myocardial infarction. A markedly elevated event rate was found in the subgroup receiving four or more stents. Repeat intervention was required in only 1.35% of patients, underscoring the durability of initial treatment. Among these, interventions involving the same vessel accounted for the majority (1.25%), with a notably higher event rate (15.38%) compared to interventions in another vessel (0.10%). Patients requiring repeat intervention also showed increased rates of ischemic stroke (7.69%), HF (7.69%), and overall MACE (7.14%), suggesting a correlation between procedural recurrence and adverse outcomes. Adverse events were more prevalent in individuals with severe LVEF reduction (EF <30%, 0.56%) and in those whose ejection fraction did not improve (0.87%).

Patients with LDL-C levels between 55 and 70 mg/dL and 70–100 mg/dL showed slightly elevated rates (0.46% and 0.43%, respectively). No events were reported among those with LDL-C > 100 mg/dL or in the ezetimibe-treated subgroup. Overall, clopidogrel therapy was associated with favourable safety outcomes across examined subgroups.

Among cases involving repeat interventions in the same vessel, restenosis occurred most frequently with sirolimus + everolimus stents (5.5%), followed by everolimus (1.6%) and sirolimus (1.3%). Overall, 3.4% of patients required repeat procedures in the same vessel, with no statistically significant differences observed between stent types (p = 0.617). Repeat interventions in other vessels occurred in 3.1% of cases, with zotarolimus demonstrating the highest rate at 8.7%. The LAD, LCX, and RCA represented the most common sites for secondary PCI; however, event rates across groups remained low and did not reach statistical significance. These results indicate minimal variation in adverse outcomes or restenosis rates among different drug-eluting stent types when administered alongside clopidogrel.

Safety of DAPT

No deaths (mortality) were observed in any of the patients.

Discussion

Clopidogrel has long served as the cornerstone of DAPT following PCI, particularly in patients with chronic coronary syndromes (CCS) and stable ACS. In this single-center retrospective study of 1039 patients treated with DES, clopidogrel-based DAPT was associated with an exceptionally low incidence of MACE at 0.29%, with no reported deaths and minimal repeat interventions. These favourable outcomes were consistent across STEMI and NSTEMI presentations and across all stent types, reinforcing the drug's safety and efficacy in routine clinical practice. The absence of MACE events in the inferior wall STEMI subgroup should be interpreted cautiously, as it may reflect the limited number of events and the retrospective nature of the study rather than a true difference in clinical risk. Comparable findings were reported in the STOPDAPT-2 trial, where a 1-month DAPT regimen followed by clopidogrel monotherapy showed non-inferior ischemic protection and significantly reduced bleeding compared to 12-month DAPT. [18] Similarly, in a real-world propensity-matched study by Kulsumritpon et al.,(2025) clopidogrel-based DAPT showed comparable ischemic outcomes to short-course potent P2Y₁₂ inhibitor-based regimens, with fewer bleeding complications, especially in resource-limited settings. [19] These consistent findings across diverse populations and study designs reinforce clopidogrel's role as a safe and effective antiplatelet agent, particularly in patients with high bleeding risk or contraindications to potent P2Y₁₂ inhibitors.

In this retrospective cohort, sirolimus-eluting stents accounted for 63.7% of all DES deployed, with a notably low repeat intervention rate of 1.3%. The absence of significant differences in major adverse cardiac events (MACE) between sirolimus, Everolimus, sirolimus + Everolimus, and zotarolimus stents (p = 0.708) supports the clinical equivalence of these second-generation DES platforms when used with clopidogrel-based DAPT. Similarly, the study published by Youn YJ et al.,(2020) in circulation: Cardiovascular Interventions compared Everolimus- and zotarolimus-eluting stents with biolimus-eluting stents in an patients undergoing PCI population showed that at 24 months, the device-oriented composite outcome rates were 3.6% for everolimus, 3.9% for zotarolimus, and 2.2% for biolimus, confirming noninferiority among the stent types and reinforcing their comparable safety profiles. [20] These findings should be interpreted as descriptive observations, as stent selection was based on routine clinical practice rather than randomization, and direct comparisons between stent types may be influenced by differences in patient and lesion characteristics.

In this study the duration of dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) did not significantly influence adverse outcomes, with event rates remaining balanced between the 12–24 month and ≥ 24-month subgroups. These findings align with the STOPDAPT-2 trial, which demonstrated that a 1-month DAPT regimen followed by clopidogrel monotherapy was non-inferior to 12-month DAPT in terms of ischemic protection, while significantly reducing bleeding risk. [18]

The observation that patients with ≥4 stents especially those with six had disproportionately higher MACE rates suggests a threshold effect of stent burden on ischemic risk. This is supported by data by Gargiulo G et al., (2017) from the DAPT study and other registries, which have shown that increased stent length and number are associated with higher rates of stent thrombosis and target lesion revascularization. [21] Furthermore, patients aged ≥55 years and those with impaired left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) demonstrated higher event rates, reinforcing the importance of individualized risk stratification. The findings collectively support a tailored approach to DAPT duration, emphasizing the need to balance ischemic protection with bleeding risk, particularly in patients with complex coronary anatomy, advanced age, or impaired ventricular function.

Given the limited number of MACE events, subgroup analyses were descriptive in nature, and multivariable regression analyses were not performed. Therefore, findings from small subgroups should be interpreted cautiously and should not be considered independent predictors of clinical outcomes. Accordingly, results presented in tables involving small subgroup denominators should be interpreted as exploratory and not confirmatory.

In this retrospective PCI cohort, patients achieving LDL-C targets below 100 mg/dL particularly those reaching <75 and < 50 mg/dL experienced minimal major adverse cardiac events (MACE). These findings represent descriptive observations within this cohort and should not be interpreted as evidence of the efficacy of rosuvastatin monotherapy. The absence of adverse events in patients with LDL-C > 100 mg/dL should be interpreted cautiously because this subgroup comprised only 44 patients, limiting statistical power. These findings are consistent with the conclusions of Koskinas et al. (2021) [22] in Euro Intervention, which emphasized that LDL-C reduction proportionally lowers cardiovascular risk and that early initiation of high-intensity statins post-PCI yields rapid and sustained benefit. Similarly, the review by Pradhan et al. in JCDD (2025) [23] highlighted that early and intensive lipid-lowering therapy—especially using combination regimens with statins, ezetimibe, and PCSK9 inhibitors—can achieve LDL-C goals within 28 days and favourably modify plaque characteristics, even in non-culprit arteries. When interpreted alongside major studies evaluating newer P2Y12 inhibitors in acute coronary syndrome (ACS), our findings provide descriptive observations from this retrospective cohort; however, direct comparisons cannot be made because the present study did not include a comparator group. The TICAKOREA study by Park et al. (2019) [24] reported higher bleeding rates with ticagrelor (11.7% vs 5.3%) and no significant reduction in ischemic events (HR 1.62; 95% CI: 0.96–2.74; P = 0.07), while the PHILO study by Goto et al. (2015) similarly found increased vascular events and bleeding with ticagrelor compared to clopidogrel (MACE: 9.0% vs 6.3%; major bleeding: 10.3% vs 6.8%). [25] In contrast, our cohort showed no ischemic strokes in most stent subgroups and only isolated cases of embolic stroke and heart failure (HF), although these findings should not be interpreted as evidence of the comparative safety of clopidogrel, particularly in East Asian populations where bleeding risk is a critical consideration. The SWEDEHEART registry (Sahlen et al., 2016) did report a significant reduction in all-cause death, myocardial infarction, or stroke with ticagrelor (HR 0.85; 95% CI: 0.78–0.93) over 24 months, indicating potential long-term benefit in broader ACS populations. [26] However, our study also demonstrated excellent outcomes over extended follow-up, with no events in patients followed <6 months and only a modest increase (0.76%) in those followed ≥24 months. These findings describe the clinical outcomes observed in this retrospective cohort and should be interpreted without inferring comparative effectiveness. In patients receiving oral anticoagulant therapy, clopidogrel is generally favoured over ticagrelor or prasugrel—particularly in those with high bleeding risk (HBR) - due to the elevated bleeding rates observed with potent P2Y₁₂ inhibitor-based triple antithrombotic regimens. [27]

Statins have a protective role in patients who underwent PCI, and achievement of target levels for LDL-cholesterol is important in all patients post-PCI. [28], [29] Several studies have reported that statin monotherapy (usually rosuvastatin) can achieve LDL-C targets in many patients after PCI [30]. However, the present study was not designed to evaluate the efficacy of rosuvastatin monotherapy, and the observed LDL-C target attainment should be interpreted descriptively. A systematic review and meta-analysis of RCT's for Rosuvastatin monotherapy versus combination therapy in high-risk coronary artery disease, concludes that rosuvastatin monotherapy alone achieves substantial reductions and is generally sufficient for most patients. [31] Combination therapy however may increase risk of myalgia and GI symptoms, while monotherapy is associated with higher risk of elevated liver enzymes.

In patients requiring concomitant oral anticoagulation, current evidence recommends minimizing the duration of triple antithrombotic therapy to reduce bleeding risk while maintaining ischemic protection.

Strengths and limitations

The principal strengths of this study include its large sample size (n = 1039), sinle-center design, and detailed stratification by infarct type, stent type, and therapy duration. These features enhance the generalizability and external validity of the findings. Furthermore, the low event rates across subgroups support the safety of clopidogrel-based DAPT in routine clinical practice. However, several limitations must be acknowledged. A key limitation of this study is the potential for selection and survivor bias, as only patients with complete follow-up were included, which may have underestimated the true MACE rate. The retrospective design inherently limits causal inference and is susceptible to selection bias. Bleeding outcomes were not systematically captured in this retrospective study; therefore, the safety assessment was limited to ischemic outcomes and did not include bleeding events, which are an important endpoint of dual antiplatelet therapy. Additionally, LDL-C data were unavailable for 210 patients, and the relatively small size of some LDL-C subgroups may have limited the interpretation of the lipid-related analyses. The absence of a control group using alternative P2Y12 inhibitors such as ticagrelor or prasugrel precludes direct comparative analysis of the relative safety and efficacy of clopidogrel, and comparisons with published studies should be interpreted as descriptive only. Additionally, platelet function testing and CYP2C19 genotyping were not performed; therefore, the influence of clopidogrel responsiveness and CYP2C19 genetic variability on clinical outcomes could not be assessed. The lack of angiographic or imaging data restricts the ability to correlate restenosis with lesion characteristics, and limited follow-up granularity in some subgroups (e.g., <6 months) may obscure temporal trends. Some clinically relevant baseline characteristics, including comorbidities and procedural details, were not consistently available in the retrospective dataset and therefore could not be included in the analysis.

Conclusion

This single-center retrospective study describes the clinical outcomes observed in patients undergoing PCI with drug-eluting stents (DES) who received clopidogrel-based dual antiplatelet therapy. Sirolimus-eluting stents were the most used, with comparable safety profiles to Everolimus stents based on MACE and restenosis outcomes. Extended DAPT beyond one year was frequent, though its impact varied across subgroups. Redo procedures and adverse events were influenced by stent burden, LVEF grade, and adjunctive therapies. These findings describe the clinical outcomes observed in this retrospective cohort of patients receiving clopidogrel-based DAPT after PCI. However, in the absence of a comparator arm, no conclusions regarding the relative safety or efficacy of clopidogrel compared with other P2Y12 inhibitors can be drawn.

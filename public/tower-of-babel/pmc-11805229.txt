Analyzing the Impact of Demographic Factors on Periodontal Health Using BPE Scores
Authors: Kuldip Singh Sangha, Pushpraj Singh, Shisham Verma, Kirti Karda, Soumya Mundhra, Vaishnavi Das Pattnaik
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Periodontal health is a critical component of overall oral health, influenced by factors such as age, gender, and specific dental regions.Materials and Methods:This study involved a cohort of participants with a gender distribution of 60% female and 40% male. The BPE scores were assessed for six sextants in the oral cavity. The mean BPE scores were calculated for each sextant, and correlations between age and BPE scores were analyzed. Inferential analysis using t-tests was conducted to evaluate gender differences in BPE scores across sextants.Results:The gender distribution showed a higher number of female participants (60%) compared to male participants (40%). The mean BPE scores indicated that sextants 1, 3, and 5 had relatively higher scores, with means of 1.5, 1.5, and 1.7, respectively, suggesting more periodontal issues compared to sextants 2, 4, and 6, which had mean scores of 0.8, 1.2, and 1.0, respectively. The correlation matrix highlighted a strong positive correlation between age and BPE scores for sextants 1 and 3 (0.835), indicating a higher incidence of periodontal issues with increasing age. Sextant 4 also showed a moderate correlation with age (0.694). T-tests revealed no statistically significant differences in BPE scores between genders for all sextants, with P values greater than 0.05.Conclusions:The study underscores the influence of age on periodontal health, particularly in sextants 1, 3, and 4, while gender does not significantly impact BPE scores. These findings emphasize the need for targeted dental care and monitoring, especially for older adults, to maintain periodontal health.

INTRODUCTION

Periodontal disease is a prevalent oral health issue that affects millions worldwide, contributing to tooth loss and negatively impacting overall health.[1] The Basic Periodontal Examination (BPE) is a widely used screening tool that helps identify periodontal issues by assessing the condition of different sextants in the oral cavity.[2] Understanding the factors that influence periodontal health, such as age, gender, and anatomical location, is essential for developing targeted preventive and therapeutic strategies.[3]

Previous studies have shown that periodontal health tends to deteriorate with age due to cumulative exposure to risk factors and decreased immune response.[45] In addition, differences in periodontal health between males and females have been reported, although findings are often inconsistent, highlighting the need for further investigation into gender-related differences.[67] The role of specific sextants in periodontal disease progression has also been studied, with certain areas showing greater susceptibility to periodontal issues, possibly due to variations in oral hygiene practices, anatomical differences, and environmental exposure.[8]

This study aims to evaluate the relationship between age, gender, and periodontal health across different sextants using BPE scores.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design and participants

This cross-sectional study was conducted to evaluate periodontal health using the Basic Periodontal Examination (BPE) scores across different sextants in relation to age and gender. A total of 100 participants were recruited from a dental clinic, comprising 60 females and 40 males, reflecting a gender distribution of 60% female and 40% male. Participants were included if they were aged 18 years or older and provided informed consent. Individuals with a history of systemic diseases affecting periodontal health or those who had received periodontal treatment in the past 6 months were excluded.

Data collection

Data were collected through clinical examinations conducted by trained dental professionals. The BPE scores were recorded for each participant across six sextants: sextant 1 (upper right), sextant 2 (upper anterior), sextant 3 (upper left), sextant 4 (lower left), sextant 5 (lower anterior), and sextant 6 (lower right). Each sextant was assigned a score from 0 to 4, indicating the severity of periodontal issues, with higher scores representing more severe conditions.

Statistical analysis

Statistics was done by using SPSS 23.

DISCUSSION

The results revealed a strong positive correlation between age and BPE scores in sextants 1 and 3, suggesting that periodontal health deteriorates with age in these regions. This finding aligns with previous research indicating that age is a significant risk factor for periodontal disease due to cumulative exposure to plaque and a reduced immune response.[12] The moderate correlation observed in sextant 4 further supports the notion that age-related periodontal decline can affect various areas of the mouth, although the impact may differ in severity across sextants.[3]

Our analysis showed no statistically significant differences in BPE scores between male and female participants across all sextants. This is consistent with some studies that have reported no significant gender differences in periodontal health.[4] However, other studies have suggested that hormonal differences might influence periodontal disease prevalence, particularly in females.[5] The lack of gender differences in our study may be attributed to the relatively balanced distribution of males and females, as well as other confounding factors such as oral hygiene practices and socioeconomic status, which were not controlled for in this study.

The findings of this study underscore the importance of personalized dental care, particularly for older adults who may be at higher risk for periodontal issues in specific sextants. Targeted interventions focusing on areas with higher BPE scores, such as sextants 1, 3, and 5, could help mitigate the progression of periodontal disease.[6] Regular dental check-ups and tailored oral hygiene instructions are essential to address the needs of individuals as they age.[78]

CONCLUSIONS

In conclusion, this study highlights the influence of age on periodontal health across different sextants, while gender does not appear to significantly impact BPE scores. These findings emphasize the need for personalized dental care and targeted interventions, particularly for older adults, to maintain optimal periodontal health. Future research should explore additional factors influencing periodontal health to further enhance preventive and therapeutic strategies.

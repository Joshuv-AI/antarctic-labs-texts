Jordan Constants for Volume-Preserving Cremona Groups
Jiahe Wang

Abstract
. We show that the optimal Jordan constant for the volume-preserving plane Cremona group Bir ⁡ ( ℙ 2 , Δ ) \mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\Delta) is 12. We provide a Jordan constant of 60 60 for the three-dimensional volume-preserving Cremona group Bir ⁡ ( ℙ 3 , Δ ) \mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{3},\Delta) . We also provide a weak geometric Jordan constant of 288 ⋅ 16 288\cdot 16 for Bir ⁡ ( ℙ 2 ) \mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2}) .

1.
Introduction

Definition 1.1
.

A group
\mathcal{G}
has the
Jordan property
if there exists a constant
J
such that any finite subgroup
G\leqslant\mathcal{G}
contains an abelian normal subgroup
A\trianglelefteq G
with index
[G:A]\leq J
.

In 1878, Camille Jordan
[
18
]
showed that for any field
k
of characteristic zero and any positive integer
n
, the linear automorphism group
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
satisfies the Jordan property. Collins
[
7
]
found an explicit Jordan constant
J(n)=(n+1)!
for
n\geq 71
. The modern theory of Jordan property for birational transformation groups has been developed systematically by many authors; see, for example, Popov’s survey
[
25
]
for a general overview. Serre
[
29
]
proved that the birational automorphism group of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
,
\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
, possesses the Jordan property with a Jordan constant

J=2^{10}\cdot 3^{4}\cdot 5^{2}\cdot 7,

which was later improved to
7200
by Yasinsky
[
30
]
. Prokhorov and Shramov
[
27
]
showed that
\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{3})
has the Jordan property with a Jordan constant
107495424
. More generally, combining the boundedness of Fano varieties due to Birkar
[
3
]
with their earlier work, Prokhorov and Shramov
[
26
]
proved that
\mathrm{Bir}(X)
satisfies the Jordan property for any rationally connected variety
X
over a field
k
of characteristic
0
; see also related results and refinements in
[
1
,
22
]
. Many other groups are known to be Jordan. For example, every connected algebraic group
G
has a Jordan constant that depends only on
\dim G

[
21
,
23
]
. As a consequence,
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
satisfies the Jordan property for any projective variety
X
. In the singular setting, if
(X,x)
is an
n
-dimensional klt singularity, then the fundamental group of the link
\pi_{1}(\mathrm{Link}(X,x))
satisfies the Jordan property
[
6
]
.

The group we are interested in is the group of volume-preserving birational self-maps of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
over the complex numbers:

\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\Delta)=\{f:\mathbb{P}^{2}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}\text{ birational map},f^{*}\Omega=\lambda\Omega\text{ for some }\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\},

where
\Omega
is the standard torus-invariant volume form and
\Delta
is the pole divisor of
\Omega
. A recent work of Loginov and Zhang
[
20
]
examines
\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\Delta)
in general dimensions and shows that in dimensions
n\geq 4

over
\mathbb{C}
and
n\geq 3
over number fields,
\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\Delta)
is not generated by pseudo-regularizable elements and therefore not simple.

The group
\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\Delta)
satisfies the Jordan property as it is a subgroup of
\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
. We would like to find an optimal Jordan constant for this group. One will see that any finite subgroup
G\leqslant\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\Delta)
fits into an exact sequence

1\to A\to G\to G_{d}\to 1,

where
G_{d}
is roughly the group acting on the components of the pole divisor of the volume form and their intersection strata, with
A
being the subgroup fixing the components of the pole divisor and their intersection strata, which will be shown to be abelian. Therefore, to find a Jordan constant
J
, we would like to find a bound on
G_{d}
, which can be further reduced to examining certain Calabi-Yau pairs. Altogether, this yields a Jordan constant of
12
, which is smaller than the Jordan constant
7200
given by Yasinsky for
\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
.

Theorem 1.2
.

The optimal Jordan constant for the volume-preserving plane Cremona group
\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\Delta)
is
12
.

Based on the Jordan constant
12
of
\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\Delta)
, we establish a Jordan constant for the three-dimensional volume-preserving Cremona group
\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{3},\Delta)
.

Theorem 1.3
.

A Jordan constant for
\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{3},\Delta)
is
60
.

We extend the analysis to the geometric Jordan property, which further requires the abelian normal subgroup to be embedded in an algebraic torus. We show the weak geometric Jordan property for
\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
, which allows the abelian group
A
in each finite subgroup
G
to be not necessarily normal.

Definition 1.4
.

A group
\mathcal{G}
has the
weak geometric Jordan property
if there exists a constant
J
such that any finite subgroup
G\leqslant\mathcal{G}
contains an abelian subgroup
A\leqslant G
with index
[G:A]\leq J
, and
A
is contained in an algebraic torus of
\mathcal{G}
.

Theorem 1.5
.

\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
satisfies the weak geometric Jordan property with a constant
288\cdot 16
.

In Section 2, we will review some preliminaries regarding finite subgroups of
\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
and volume-preserving maps. In Section 3, we will derive the exact sequence as above, which reduces the task of finding a Jordan constant to enumerating certain Calabi-Yau pairs on
G
-minimal surfaces. In Section 4, we explicitly find the Jordan constant for
\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\Delta)
. In Section 5, we show that this Jordan constant is optimal and we present a way to find volume-preserving subgroups regularized on del Pezzo surfaces. In Section 6, we extend the analysis of the Jordan property to the three-dimensional volume-preserving Cremona group
\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{3},\Delta)
. In Section 7, we find a weak geometric Jordan constant for the plane Cremona group.

2.
Preliminaries

We work over the field of complex numbers.

2.1.
Finite subgroups of
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2})}

In
[
12
]
, Dolgachev and Iskovskikh classify all finite subgroups of the plane Cremona group
\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
. The procedure goes as follows:

•

Any finite subgroup
G\leqslant\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
can be regularized on a rational surface
X
such that
G
may be viewed as a finite subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
. That is, there exists a birational map
f:X\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}
such that
f^{-1}Gf\leqslant\mathrm{Aut}(X)
.

•

Performing
G
-equivariant minimal model program to
X
, we get a
G
-minimal surface
X^{\prime}
. One can show that such a minimal surface is either a conic bundle or a del Pezzo surface.

•

For each minimal surface, the authors classify the automorphism group of the surface and its finite subgroups, and therefore one gets the classification of all finite subgroups of the plane Cremona group.

3.
Action on the dual complex

Given a finite volume-preserving subgroup
G\leqslant\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\Delta)
, we would like to derive an exact sequence
1\to A\to G\to G_{d}\to 1
, where
G_{d}
is roughly the group acting on the components of the pole divisor of the volume form and their intersection strata, and
A
is the subgroup that fixes all components and their intersection strata. To do that:

•

We need to regularize
G
on a rational surface
X
such that
G\leqslant\mathrm{Aut}(X,\omega_{X})
for some volume form
\omega_{X}
on
X
.

•

We would like
D_{X}=-\mathrm{div}(\omega_{X})
to be effective for the following reason: If this is true, then
(X,D_{X})
is a Calabi-Yau pair. Calabi-Yau pairs are better to classify than non-effective pairs.

•

We would like to show that
A
is abelian, so that finding a Jordan constant
J
will be reduced to finding a bound for the group
G_{d}
. After classifying all Calabi-Yau pairs, we examine the possible groups acting on the components of the pole divisors and their intersection strata, which give a bound for
G_{d}
.

We address the above by the following lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
G
is a finite subgroup of
\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\Delta)
, then
G\leqslant\mathrm{Aut}(X,\omega_{X})
for some
G
-minimal surface

and some volume form
\omega_{X}
on

Proof.

Any finite subgroup
G\leqslant\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\Delta)
can be regularized on a
G
-minimal rational surface
X
. Given the regularization map
f:X\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}
, we can define
\omega_{X}=f^{*}\Omega
, then
G\leqslant\mathrm{Aut}(X,\omega_{X})
.
∎

Note that this

is not guaranteed to have no zeros, therefore

may not be effective. We show the following.

4.
Jordan constant for
\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\Delta)

For any finite
G\leqslant\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\Delta)
, we regularize
G\leqslant\mathrm{Aut}(X,D_{X})
, and we have established the exact sequence
1\to A\to G\to G_{d}\to 1
.
Since
A
is abelian normal, to find a Jordan constant
J
for
\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\Delta)
, it suffices to find a bound for the size of
G_{d}
. The task can be reduced to enumerating all Calabi-Yau pairs
(X,D_{X})
arising from this context, i.e., Calabi-Yau pairs
(X,D_{X})
crepant birational to
(\mathbb{P}^{2},\Delta)
, where
X
is either a conic bundle or a del Pezzo surface.

Remark 4.1
.

Note that a Calabi-Yau pair contains more information than its dual complex, as for example the self-intersection number of components of
D_{X}
can also provide information on the actions permitted on the dual complex.

Consider the following ways to find
(X,D_{X})
where
X
is either a conic bundle or a del Pezzo surface,
(X,D_{X})
a Calabi-Yau pair, crepant birational to
(\mathbb{P}^{2},\Delta)
:

•

If
X
is
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, then
D_{X}
is either a triangle of projective lines, a conic and a (non-tangent) line, or an irreducible nodal curve.

•

Consider a birational morphism from
X
to
Y
; the Calabi-Yau pairs of
X
is in some sense given by the pairs of
Y
as shown in a lemma below, so if we know all the Calabi-Yau pairs of
Y
, we will have the pairs for
X
.

•

If
X
has a conic bundle structure, we use the fact that the restriction of the pair on a general fiber is a Calabi-Yau pair.

We formulate the points above in the following lemmas. Let coreg denote the coregularity of a Calabi-Yau pair.

Lemma 4.2

(
[
13

)
.

There are precisely four possibilities for a log Calabi-Yau pair of the form

. We either have

1.

, which holds if
D_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
is a smooth cubic curve, or

2.
\mathrm{coreg}(\mathbb{P}^{2},D_{\mathbb{P}^{2}})=0
, which holds if either

(a)

is an irreducible nodal cubic curve,

(b)

is the sum of a conic and a (non-tangent) line, or

(c)

is a triangle of lines.

The three cases for coregularity

are all crepant birational. So there are only three pairs on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
crepant birational to
(\mathbb{P}^{2},\Delta)
.

Proof.

This follows from Definition

\tilde{G}
be the preimage of
G
under this cover; it is a finite extension of
G
by a subgroup of order at most 2. Replacing
G
with
\tilde{G}
and
(X,D_{X})
with
(\tilde{X},\tilde{D}_{X})
, we may assume
G\leqslant\mathrm{Aut}(X,D_{X})
and
\mathcal{D}(X,D_{X})=S^{2}
.

Let
K
be the kernel of the action of
G
on
\mathcal{D}(X,D_{X})=S^{2}
:

1\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow G/K\longrightarrow 1.

We now analyze
K
. Let
p
be a point where components of
D_{X}
meet. Locally, there are
3
analytic branches. By potentially passing to a dlt modification, we may assume that the three branches are from three different components. By Lemma
3.6
, the representation of
K
at the tangent space
p
is faithful and we know that
K
fixes three directions, showing that
K
is abelian of rank at most 3.

We know that
G/K
is either icosahedral (
\cong A_{5}
, order 60), octahedral (
\cong S_{4}
, order 24), tetrahedral (
\cong A_{4}
, order 12), dihedral (
D_{n}
), or cyclic (
C_{n}
). If
G/K
is icosahedral, octahedral, or tetrahedral, then
[G:K]\leq 60
. Assume now that
G/K
is dihedral or cyclic. Then there exists a subgroup
A\leqslant G/K
of index at most 2 (the rotation subgroup) that has a fixed point
q\in S^{2}
. Let
G^{\prime}
be the preimage of
A
in
G
. Then
[G:G^{\prime}]\leq 2
and we have:

1\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow G^{\prime}\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow 1.

The fixed point
q
corresponds to a component
D_{0}
of
D_{X}
, which is stabilized by
G^{\prime}
. Let
B_{0}=\mathrm{Diff}_{D_{0}}(D_{X}-D_{0})
be the different divisor, so that
(D_{0},B_{0})
is a log Calabi-Yau surface. Let
G^{\prime\prime}
be the image of
G^{\prime}
in
\mathrm{Aut}(D_{0},B_{0})
. We note that since
(X,D_{X})
is crepant birational to the toric pair
(\mathbb{P}^{3},\Delta)
,
the toric structure is preserved under restriction to boundary components.
In dimension two, all toric log Calabi-Yau pairs are crepant birational to

(\mathbb{P}^{2},\Delta)
. Therefore,

(D_{0},B_{0})
obtained by adjunction is crepant birational to

(\mathbb{P}^{2},\Delta)
, and the induced action
G^{\prime\prime}
of
G^{\prime}
is a finite subgroup of
\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\Delta)
.

By the corresponding 2-dimensional theorem, there exists a subgroup
A_{0}\leqslant G^{\prime\prime}
with
[G^{\prime\prime}:A_{0}]\leq 12
that acts trivially on the dual complex of
(D_{0},B_{0})
. Let
\tilde{A}_{0}
be the preimage of
A_{0}
in
G^{\prime}
, so
[G^{\prime}:\tilde{A}_{0}]\leq 12
. An element
g\in\tilde{A}_{0}
acts trivially on the dual complex of
(D_{0},B_{0})
, since
B_{0}
contains the intersections of
D_{0}
with all other components of
D_{X}
, the action of
g
is trivial on a neighborhood of the vertex
q
in
\mathcal{D}(X,D_{X})
. As
\mathcal{D}(X,D_{X})=S^{2}
is connected and the action is simplicial, triviality near
q
implies triviality everywhere. Hence,
\tilde{A}_{0}\leqslant K\leqslant G^{\prime}
, we have:

[G^{\prime}:K]\leq[G^{\prime}:\tilde{A}_{0}]\leq 12.

Recalling that
[G:G^{\prime}]\leq 2
, we conclude
[G:K]\leq 24
in the dihedral or cyclic case.

Taking the abelian normal subgroup to be
K
, we get a Jordan constant of
60
.
∎

7.
Weak geometric Jordan constant for
\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2})

Definition 7.1

(
[
24
, Section 3]
,
[
28
, Definition 1.2.1]
)
.

A group
\mathcal{G}
has the
geometric Jordan property
if there exists a constant
J
such that any finite subgroup
G\leqslant\mathcal{G}
contains an abelian normal subgroup
A\trianglelefteq G
with index
[G:A]\leq J
, and
A
is contained in an algebraic torus of
\mathcal{G}
.

A group
\mathcal{G}
has the
weak geometric Jordan property
if there exists a constant
J
such that any finite subgroup
G\leqslant\mathcal{G}
contains an abelian not necessarily normal subgroup
A\leqslant G
with index
[G:A]\leq J
, and
A
is contained in an algebraic torus of
\mathcal{G}
.

Remark 7.2
.

The (weak) geometric Jordan property is stronger than the ordinary (weak) Jordan property, so the constant
J
could potentially be larger than the constant for the Jordan property if a group satisfies the geometric Jordan property.

We will establish a weak geometric Jordan constant for
\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
.

A strategy of embedding a finite abelian group
A^{\prime}\leqslant\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
in an algebraic torus is to show that it is inside some connected linear algebraic group, which has the property that all finite abelian subgroups are embedded in an algebraic torus. More explicitly, for a finite automorphism subgroup
G\leqslant\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
regularized on a
G
-minimal surface
X
, by the weak Jordan property of
\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
, we know there exists an abelian subgroup
A\leqslant G
with
[G:A]\leq 288

[
28
, Proposition 1.2.3]
. We would like to find an abelian subgroup
A^{\prime}\leqslant A
that admits a union of
A^{\prime}
-orbits of disjoint
(-1)
-curves, so that we may contract those curves
A^{\prime}
-equivariantly to obtain another surface
X_{0}
. We would like
\mathrm{Aut}(X_{0})
to be a connected linear algebraic group. Then
A^{\prime}
is contained in an algebraic torus of
\mathrm{Aut}(X_{0})
. Because
\mathrm{Aut}(X_{0})\leqslant\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
, we get that the torus is actually contained in
\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
. We formulate everything as follows:

Lemma 7.3
.

The (weak) geometric Jordan property and the (weak) Jordan property are equivalent for connected linear algebraic groups, and if they are satisfied by a connected linear algebraic group, then they share the same constant
J
.

Proof.

For a finite abelian subgroup in a connected linear algebraic group over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, all elements are semisimple and pairwise commuting, so the subgroup is contained in a maximal torus.
∎

Now consider minimal surfaces, i.e., del Pezzo surfaces of degree
d\leq 6
,
\mathbb{P}^{2}
,
\mathbb{F}_{n}
with
n\neq 1
, and singular conic bundles. For each case, we will apply the above strategy to find a weak geometric Jordan constant for
\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
.

7.1.
Del Pezzo surfaces of degree
d\leq 6

Following Dolgachev and Iskovskikh
[
12
, Section 6.1]
, there is a group homomorphism from
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
to
\mathrm{Weyl}(R)
where
R
is of type
E_{N}
(
N:=9-d=6,7,8
),
D_{5}
(
N=5
),
A_{4}
(
N=4
), or
A_{2}+A_{1}
(
N=3
). The image of
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
in
\mathrm{Weyl}(R)
may be identified as the group acting on the
(-1)
-curves. Moreover, this homomorphism is injective for
d\leq 5
as the kernel
K
fixes the
N
exceptional curves of the blow-up
X\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Contracting the exceptional curves
K
-equivariantly, one gets that
K
is an automorphism subgroup of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
fixing the
N=9-d
points of the images of the exceptionals. If
d\leq 5
, then
K
is trivial.

For
d\leq 5
, we have an injection
\mathrm{Aut}(X)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Weyl}(R)
, so for any finite
G\leqslant\mathrm{Aut}(X)
, we may take the abelian subgroup
A
in the weak geometric Jordan property to be the trivial group, and its index is bounded by
|\mathrm{Aut}(X)|
. For
d=5
,
\mathrm{Aut}(X)=120
; for
d=4
,
\mathrm{Aut}(X)\leq 160
, see
[
16
, Theorem 1.1]
or
[
11
, Theorem 8.6.6]
; for
d=3
,
\mathrm{Aut}(X)\leq 648
which is obtained by the Fermat cubic surface, see
[
17
, Theorem 5.3]
or
[
11
, Theorem 9.5.6]
; for
d=2
,
\mathrm{Aut}(X)\leq 336

[
11
, Section 8.7.3]
; for
d=1
,
\mathrm{Aut}(X)\leq 144

[
11
, Section 8.8.4]
.

For
d=6
, let
G\leqslant\mathrm{Aut}(X)
be a finite subgroup, then we have an exact sequence
1\to A\to G\to\mathrm{Weyl}(A_{2}+A_{1})
. Since
A
fixes all
(-1)
-curves, in particular it fixes the three exceptional curves, so doing
A
-equivariant contraction we get that
A\leqslant\mathrm{PGL}(3)
fixing
3
points, hence it is abelian by Lemma
3.7
and is contained in a torus of
\mathrm{PGL}(3)\leqslant\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
. The index of
A
is bounded by
|\mathrm{Weyl}(A_{2}+A_{1})|=12
.

7.2.
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and
\mathbb{F}_{n}
,
n\neq 1

The automorphism groups of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and
\mathbb{F}_{n}
,
n\geq 1
are connected linear algebraic groups. See
[
5
, Exercise 2]
for a description of
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{n}),n\geq 1
as a quotient of connected linear algebraic group by a finite central subgroup. For
n=0
,
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{0},\pi)=\mathrm{PGL}(2)\times\mathrm{PGL}(2)
is a connected linear algebraic group, where
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{0},\pi)
is the automorphism group preserving the conic bundle structure. We note that any finite subgroup
G
regularized on a conic bundle
X
satisfies
G\leqslant\mathrm{Aut}(X,\pi)
, so it suffices to consider
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{0},\pi)
. So by the weak Jordan property
[
28
, Proposition 1.2.3]
, we get a weak geometric Jordan bound
288
for the index in this case.

7.3.
Singular conic bundles

Let
X
be a singular conic bundle, i.e., a blow-up of
\mathbb{F}_{n}
at a finite set of points, no two lying in a fiber of a ruling. Write
\mathrm{Aut}(X,\pi)
for the automorphism group preserving the conic bundle structure.
For any finite subgroup
G\leqslant\mathrm{Aut}(X,\pi)
there exists a short exact sequence

1\rightarrow G_{F}\rightarrow G\rightarrow G_{B}\rightarrow 1,

where
G_{B}
acts on the base
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
G_{F}
acts on the generic fiber.

By
[
28
, Proposition 1.2.3]
, for any finite
G\leqslant\mathrm{Aut}(X,\pi)
, there exists a finite abelian subgroup
A
in
G
with index at most 288. We note that under the decomposition
1\to A_{F}\to A\to A_{B}\to 1
, the subgroup
A_{B}
is cyclic as it is an abelian subgroup of
\mathrm{PGL(2)}
. We would like to find a subgroup
A^{\prime}
of
A
such that a union of
A^{\prime}
-orbits consists of one component from each singular fiber. Then we can use these disjoint
A^{\prime}
-orbits of
(-1)
-curves to perform
A^{\prime}
-equivariant contraction to obtain some
\mathbb{F}_{n}
. In the case of
\mathbb{F}_{1}
, we can further contract the unique
(-1)
-curve to
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.

Lemma 7.4
.

A finite abelian group
A^{\prime}\leqslant\mathrm{Aut}(X,\pi)
does not admit a union of
A^{\prime}
-orbits containing exactly one component from each singular fiber of the conic bundle
\pi:X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
if and only if there exists an element
g\in A^{\prime}
that exchanges the two components of some singular fiber of
\pi
.

Proof.

If such
g
exists, then there does not exist such a union of
A^{\prime}
-orbits. Conversely, assume that no element of
A^{\prime}
exchanges the two components of any singular fiber.
We construct a union of
A^{\prime}
-orbits containing exactly one component from each singular fiber. Pick one component
C_{1}
of a singular fiber and consider its
A^{\prime}
-orbit

U_{1}:=\bigcup_{a\in A^{\prime}}a(C_{1}).

Since no element of
A^{\prime}
exchanges components within the same fiber,
U_{1}
contains at most one component from each singular fiber.
If some singular fibers have no components contained in
U_{1}
, pick one component
C_{2}
from such a fiber and let

U_{2}:=\bigcup_{a\in A^{\prime}}a(C_{2}).

Again,
U_{2}
contains at most one component from each fiber, and by construction
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
have no common fiber.
Repeating this procedure for the remaining singular fibers yields finitely many disjoint
A^{\prime}
-orbits

U_{1},\ldots,U_{r}

whose union

U:=U_{1}\cup\cdots\cup U_{r}

contains exactly one component from each singular fiber.
Hence, such a union of
A^{\prime}
-orbits exists.
∎

We have the following classification of finite abelian groups of
\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
.

Theorem 7.5

(
[
4
, Theorem 6]
)
.

The isomorphism classes of finite abelian subgroups of the Cremona group are the following:

\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}\ \text{ for any }m,n\geq 1;\qquad\mathbb{Z}/2n\mathbb{Z}\times(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{2}\ \text{ for any }n\geq 1;

(\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z})^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z};\qquad(\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})^{3};\qquad(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{4}.

Lemma 7.6
.

For any finite subgroup
G\leqslant\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
regularized on a
G
-minimal conic bundle
X
, there exists a subgroup
A^{\prime}\leqslant G
such that it admits a union of
A^{\prime}
-orbits containing exactly one component from each singular fiber of the conic bundle and
[G:A^{\prime}]\leq 288\cdot 16
.

Proof.

By
[
28
, Proposition 1.2.3]
, there exists a finite abelian subgroup
A\leqslant G
with index bounded by
288
and we know that
A_{B}
is cyclic. Consider the conic bundle
X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
. We may assume that
A_{B}
fixes
0
and
\infty\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
. If there are singular fibers at
0
or
\infty\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
, then
A
fixes the singular fibers at
0
and
\infty
. Consider the group homomorphism
A\to(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{i}
,
i=1
or
2
defined by the actions on the
i
number of singular fibers supported on
0
and
\infty
, i.e., an automorphism is mapped to
0
at the
i
-th coordinate if it fixes the two components of the
i
-th singular fiber, and to
1
if it swaps the two components. The kernel is a subgroup
A_{0}
whose elements fix the components of the singular fibers at
0
and
\infty
. We can perform
A_{0}
-equivariant blow-down to get another conic bundle with no singular fibers at
0
and
\infty
. Let
m
be the number of singular fibers after blow-down.
Consider the exact sequence

0\to A_{0,F}\to A_{0}\to A_{0,B}\to 0,

consider the map
A_{0,F}\to(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{m}
given by the actions on the
m
singular fibers. Let
S
be the kernel of this map and let
a\in A_{0}
such that
\langle a\rangle\cong A_{0,B}
through the projection. We let
A^{\prime}=\langle S,a\rangle
. Then
[A:A^{\prime}]=[A:A_{0}][A_{0}:A^{\prime}]\leq[A:A_{0}][A_{0,F}:S]\leq 2^{i}2^{r-i}=2^{r}\leq 16
where
r
is the 2-rank of
A
which is bounded by 4 by the above classification of finite abelian groups. Moreover, we see that
A^{\prime}
does not contain any
x
that swaps the two components of a singular fiber. If not then consider
x=sg
where
s\in S
and
g\in\langle a\rangle
such that
x
swaps the two components of a singular fiber. Then since
s
fixes all components and
x
swaps some singular fiber, then it follows that
g
swaps the components of the singular fiber so in particular it fixes the base point of the singular fiber, which is not
0
or
\infty
, so the image of
g
in the base
\mathbb{P}^{1}
fixes three points and therefore it is trivial in
A_{0,B}
, and so
g\in A_{0,F}
so
g=1
as
g\in\langle a\rangle\cong A_{0,B}
, which shows that
x=s
does not swap any two components of a singular fiber which is a contradiction. So
A^{\prime}
admits a union of
A^{\prime}
-orbits containing exactly one component from each singular fiber of the conic bundle.
∎

Proposition 7.7
.

\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
satisfies the weak geometric Jordan property with a constant
288\cdot 16
.

Proof.

For a finite subgroup
G
regularized on a
G
-minimal conic bundle, by the previous lemma, we see that there exists an abelian
A^{\prime}\leqslant G
that admits such orbits and
[G:A^{\prime}]\leq 288\cdot 16
. Using this union of orbits, we perform
A^{\prime}
-equivariant blow-down to obtain some
\mathbb{F}_{n}

n\geq 0
, in the case of
\mathbb{F}_{1}
, we may further blow down to obtain
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Since the automorphism groups of these surfaces are linear algebraic, we see that
A^{\prime}
is contained in a torus of
\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
.

For del Pezzo surfaces of
d=6
, for each finite
G
, we may take such abelian subgroup such that its index is bounded by 12. For
d\leq 5
, the index is bounded by
|\mathrm{Aut}(X)|\leq 648
. For
\mathbb{P}^{2}
or
\mathbb{F}_{n},n\neq 1
, the index is bounded by
288
. So a weak geometric Jordan constant is given by
288\cdot 16
.
∎

Remark 7.8
.

One may examine the weak geometric Jordan property of

. The arguments above would apply, but we no longer guarantee that the torus is contained in

Recursive Quantization for ℒ 2 \mathcal{L}_{2} Stabilization of a Finite Capacity Stochastic Control Loop with Intermittent State Observations
Shrija Karmakar, Ritwik Kumar Layek

Abstract
The problem of ℒ 2 \mathcal{L}_{2} stabilization of a state feedback stochastic control loop is investigated under different constraints. The discrete time linear time invariant (LTI) open loop plant is chosen to be unstable. The additive white Gaussian noise is assumed to be stationary. The link between the plant and the controller is assumed to be a finite capacity stationary channel, which puts a constraint on the bit rate of the transmission. Moreover, the state of the plant is observed only intermittently keeping the loop open some of the time. In this manuscript both scalar and vector plants under Bernoulli and Markov intermittence models are investigated. Novel bounds on intermittence parameters are obtained to ensure ℒ 2 {\mathcal{L}_{2}} stability. Moreover, novel recursive quantization algorithms are developed to implement the stabilization scheme under all the constraints. Suitable illustrative examples are provided to elucidate the main results. Keywords : Stochastic control, intermittent observation, control over communication channel, ℒ 2 {\mathcal{L}_{2}} stability, recursive quantization, capacity, differential entropy, spectral norm.

1.1
Contributions

In this manuscript, the controller is considered to be the infinite horizon linear quadratic Gaussian (LQG) controller. When the observation switch is ‘ON’, the state is measured, quantized, and transmitted to the controller to generate the control signal, which will make the closed loop stable. However, when the observation switch is ‘OFF’, the state is not measured and the loop is open and unstable. The main contributions of this manuscript are twofold. Firstly, the necessary and sufficient conditions on the intermittence parameters for
\mathcal{L}_{2}
stability of the overall system are given. Secondly, when the intermittence conditions are met, a recursive quantization algorithm is designed to transmit the state through the finite capacity channel. Both of these contributions are novel and they extend several works from the contemporary literature as discussed in section
1

5
Conclusion

The work presented in this manuscript is novel and it opens up various fundamental questions in the intersection of control and communication theory. One open question is already discussed after stating Theorem
7
. The design of an optimal controller for the intermittent vector system such that the spectral norm of the closed loop gain matrix remains smaller than unity is an open problem to the best of the knowledge of the authors. Moreover, the sufficient condition of intermittence parameters for the vector plant with a finite capacity channel is still open. The lower bound on capacity for the vector plant is also not figured out. Furthermore, the immediate next big problem is of output feedback. The joint behavior of the Kalman filter and the controller should be studied under the given constraints. The finite horizon counterpart of the problems are also important. On the quantization part, non-uniform entropy coded quantization needs to be undertaken when the capacity value is small. Systems with nonlinear plant, non-Gaussian additive noise, multiplicative noise, or time varying channels are much harder problems which can be investigated in future. Finally, robust optimal control strategies such as
H_{\infty}
can be researched on under the constraints of intermittent observations and finite capacity channel.

6.1
Proof of Lemma
1

The intermittent system dynamics under
\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\infty
assumption is described by:

\displaystyle{\bf x}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{k}{\bf x}_{k}+(\alpha-bl)(1-{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{k}){\bf x}_{k}+{\bf w}_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\alpha{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{k}+(\alpha-bl)(1-{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{k})\right]{\bf x}_{k}+{\bf w}_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{k}{\bf x}_{k}+{\bf w}_{k}

where,
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{k}
is a Bernoulli random variable, and
l
is the infinite horizon LQR gain. Now,
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{k}^{2}=\alpha^{2}{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{k}+(\alpha-bl)^{2}(1-{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{k})
. Clearly,
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{k}^{2}
is also a Bernoulli random variable and
\mathbb{E}\left({\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{k}^{2}{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{j}^{2}\right)=0\forall j\neq k
. Therefore from stationarity of
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{k}^{2}
, define
\omega^{2}:=\mathbb{E}({\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{k}^{2})=\alpha^{2}p+(\alpha-bl)^{2}(1-p)
.
So, the one step conditional variance update equation at the
(k+1)^{th}
time step is given by:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left({\bf x}_{k+1}^{2}|{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{k}\right)=\left[\alpha^{2}{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{k}+(\alpha-bl)^{2}(1-{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{k})\right]\mathbb{E}\left({\bf x}_{k}^{2}\right)+\sigma_{\bf w}^{2}

Conditioning over
{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{0:k-1}
, the conditional state variance is computed as:

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{k+1|{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{0:k}}^{2}=\mathbb{E}\left[{\bf x}_{k+1}^{2}|{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{0:k}\right]={\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{k}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[{\bf x}_{k}^{2}|{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{0:k-1}\right]+\sigma_{\bf w}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j=0}^{k}{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{j}^{2}\right)\sigma_{0}^{2}+\left[\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\left(\prod_{i=j+1}^{k}{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{i}^{2}\right)+1\right]\sigma_{\bf w}^{2}

The state variance at
(k+1)^{th}
time step is computed by taking expectation of the conditional state variance with respect to
{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{0:k}
.

\displaystyle\sigma_{k+1}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}({\bf x}_{k+1}^{2}|{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{0:k})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\omega^{2(k+1)}\sigma_{0}^{2}+\left(1+\omega^{2}+\omega^{4}+...+\omega^{2k}\right)\sigma_{\bf w}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega^{2(k+1)}\sigma_{0}^{2}+\frac{1-\omega^{2(k+1)}}{1-\omega^{2}}\sigma_{\bf w}^{2}

That concludes the proof.

6.7
Proof of Theorem
7

The dynamics of the state covariance matrix
P_{k}
as discussed in lemma
6
(Eqn.
15
) can be visualized as a flow in the open convex cone of positive definite matrices
(M_{n}^{+})
. Obviously the set is not a metric space. However, the open set can be completed by including the singular positive semi-definite matrices to form the closed cone
(M_{n})
. Since the set is not compact, not all flows lead to a fixed point. However, if a contraction operator can be constructed to define the dynamics, it will asymptotically converge to a fixed point. For any
p\in[0,1]
, define the operator
\mathcal{T}_{p}:M_{n}\rightarrow M_{n}
as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{p}(P)=pAPA^{\prime}+(1-p)(A-BL)P(A-BL)^{\prime}

Then the state covariance update equation can be written as:

\displaystyle P_{k+1}=\mathcal{T}_{p}(P_{k})+W

Figure 11:
The contraction operator
\mathcal{T}_{p}
in the cone of positive semi-definite matrices
M_{n}

Since
W\in M_{n}^{+}
, and
\mathcal{T}_{p}(P_{k})\in M_{n}
, it implies that
P_{k+1}\in M_{n}^{+}
. Clearly,
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}P_{k}
will be unique and bounded if and only if
\mathcal{T}_{p}
is a contraction or equivalently
\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{p}(P)\right\rVert_{2}<\left\lVert P\right\rVert_{2}
for all
P\in M_{n}
. Now,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{p}(P)\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\lVert pAPA^{\prime}+(1-p)(A-BL)P(A-BL)^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}

(41)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\lVert pAPA^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert(1-p)(A-BL)P(A-BL)^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\left\lVert APA^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}+(1-p)\left\lVert(A-BL)P(A-BL)^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}

(42)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle p\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\left\lVert P\right\rVert_{2}+(1-p)\left\lVert A-BL\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\left\lVert P\right\rVert_{2}

(43)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\lVert P\right\rVert_{2}\left(p\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+(1-p)\left\lVert A-BL\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)

Here Eqn.41 is obtained from the triangle inequality of spectral norm, Eqn. 42 is obtained from the scalar multiplication of spectral norm, and Eqn. 43 is obtained from the submultiplicative inequality of spectral norm. Therefore, the necessary and sufficient condition for
{\mathcal{T}_{p}}
to be a contraction is given by:

\left[p\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+(1-p)\left\lVert A-BL\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right]<1
. By rearrangement, the expression of the theorem (Eqn.
32
) is obtained. That concludes the proof.
\blacksquare

Proof of Theorem

For Gaussian random vector
{\bf x}_{k}
with conditional covariance matrix
{\bf P}_{k|{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{0:k-1}}
, the conditional differential entropy is given by:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\left(2\pi e\right)^{n}\left|{\bf P}_{k|{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{0:k-1}}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\left|{\bf P}_{k|{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{0:k-1}}\right|^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2\pi e}\right)2^{\left[\frac{2}{n}{\bf h}\left({\bf x}_{k}|{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{0:k-1}\right)\right]}

5

\displaystyle{\bf P}_{k+1|{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{0:k}}={\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{k}A{\bf P}_{k|{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{0:k-1}}A^{\prime}+(1-{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{k})(A-BL){\bf P}_{k|{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{0:k-1}}(A-BL)^{\prime}+W

(45)

Applying Minkowskii’s ineqaulity on Eqn.

, the following inequality is obtained.

\displaystyle\left|{\bf P}_{k+1|{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{0:k}}\right|^{\frac{1}{n}}\geq\left[{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{k}|A|^{\frac{2}{n}}+(1-{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{k})|A-BL|^{\frac{2}{n}}\right]\left|{\bf P}_{k|{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{0:k-1}}\right|^{\frac{1}{n}}+|W|^{\frac{1}{n}}

[

]

\displaystyle 2^{\left[\frac{2}{n}{\bf h}\left({{\bf x}_{k+1}}|{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{0:k}\right)\right]}\geq 2^{\left[\frac{2}{n}h\left({\bf w}_{k}\right)\right]}+2^{\left[\frac{2}{n}{\bf h}\left({\bf x}_{k}|{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{0:k-1}\right)\right]}\left[{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{k}|A|^{\frac{2}{n}}+(1-{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{k})|A-BL|^{\frac{2}{n}}\right]

6.13
Proof of Theorem
13

1.

The necessary and sufficient condition for
\mathcal{L}_{2}
stabilization (i.e.,
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}<\infty
) of the system under the assumption of
\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\infty
can be obtained from Eqn.
21
as:

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\prod_{j=0}^{k}\omega_{j}^{2}<\infty
. This will be satisfied if and only if
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\omega_{k}^{2}<1
. Now,
\omega_{k}^{2}=\alpha^{2}\pi_{k}+(\alpha-bl)^{2}\left(1-\pi_{k}\right)
, and
\zeta_{\infty}=[(1-\pi_{\infty}),\pi_{\infty}]=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\zeta_{k}
is the steady state distribution of the intermittent switch Markov Chain. The steady state distribution
\zeta_{\infty}
is the left eigenvector of the transition probability matrix
T
with unity eigenvalue. So,
\pi_{\infty}
is obtained by solving the eigen-equation
\zeta_{\infty}=\zeta_{\infty}T
, and is given by:
\pi_{\infty}=\frac{p}{p+q}
.
Therefore, the condition is:

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\omega_{k}^{2}=\alpha^{2}\pi_{\infty}+(\alpha-bl)^{2}(1-\pi_{\infty})<1

That provides the upper bound of the ratio of the two transition probability parameters of the Markov chain for
\mathcal{L}_{2}
stabilization of the system under the assumption
\mathcal{C\rightarrow\infty}
:

\displaystyle\frac{p}{q}<\frac{1-(\alpha-bl)^{2}}{\alpha^{2}-1}

Now, if
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\omega_{k}^{2}<1
, then
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\prod_{j=0}^{k}\omega_{j}^{2}\rightarrow 0
.
Therefore the asymptotic behavior of the state variance can be obtained from Eqn.
21
by taking limit
k\rightarrow\infty
.

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{\sigma_{\bf w}^{2}}{1-\omega_{k}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{\bf w}^{2}}{1-\left[\alpha^{2}\pi_{\infty}+(\alpha-bl)^{2}\left(1-\pi_{\infty}\right)\right]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\frac{\left(1+\frac{p}{q}\right)}{1-(\alpha-bl)^{2}-\left(\alpha^{2}-1\right)\frac{p}{q}}\right]\sigma_{\bf w}^{2}

2.

For finite capacity system (
\mathcal{C}<\infty
), Eqn.
22
gives the one step conditional state variance update equation under the quantized scheme.
Also, from Shannon’s capacity theorem
[
7
,
8
]
, the relationship of state variance with the quantization step-size is obtained as:

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}_{k|{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{0:k-1}}^{2}=\eta{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{k|{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{0:k-1}}^{2}+\epsilon

Combining this result with Eqn.
22
, the following recursion is obtained:

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{k+1|{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{0:k}}^{2}=

\displaystyle\left[{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{k}^{2}+\eta G(1-{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{k})\right]{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{k|{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{0:k-1}}^{2}+\left[\sigma_{\bf w}^{2}+G(1-{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{k})\epsilon\right]

Taking expectation with respect to
{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{0:k}
results in

\displaystyle\sigma_{k+1}^{2}=[\omega_{k}^{2}+\eta G(1-\pi_{k})]\sigma_{k}^{2}+[\sigma_{\bf w}^{2}+G(1-\pi_{k})\epsilon]

The necessary and sufficient condition for
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}<\infty
is
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}[\omega_{k}^{2}+\eta G(1-\pi_{k})]<1
.
The steady state distribution of the intermittent switch Markov chain is
\zeta_{\infty}=[(1-\pi_{\infty}),\pi_{\infty}]=[\frac{q}{p+q},\frac{p}{p+q}]
. Hence, the necessary and sufficient condition for
\mathcal{L}_{2}
stabilizability of the system is given by:

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}\pi_{\infty}+[(\alpha-bl)^{2}+\eta G](1-\pi_{\infty})<1

The condition in terms of the ratio of the transition probability parameters is given by:

\displaystyle\frac{p}{q}<\frac{1-(\alpha-bl)^{2}-\eta G}{\alpha^{2}-1}

Then the asymptotic state variance is given by:

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{\bf w}^{2}+G\left(1-\pi_{\infty}\right)\epsilon}{1-\left(\omega_{\infty}^{2}+\eta G\left(1-\pi_{\infty}\right)\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(1+\frac{p}{q}\right)\sigma_{\bf w}^{2}+\epsilon G}{\left(1+\frac{p}{q}\right)-\left[\alpha^{2}\frac{p}{q}+(\alpha-bl)^{2}+\eta G\right]}

This proves Eqn.
28
. Moreover, in idealized condition of

(i.e., the transition probability of the switch from ‘OFF’ to ‘ON’ state is unity), and

(i.e., the closed loop gain is extremely small, which is feasible with infinite control cost), the condition for
\mathcal{L}_{2}
stability of the system becomes:
\eta G<1
. That gives the lower bound of the capacity for
\mathcal{L}_{2}
stabilization. Assuming

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi e}{2^{2\mathcal{C}}}\frac{b^{2}l^{2}}{12}<1

Thus the proof is concluded.

6.16
Proof of Theorem
16

The dynamics of the state covariance matrix
P_{k}
as given in Eqn.
31
in Lemma
15
can be visualized as a flow in the open convex cone of positive definite matrices
(M_{n}^{+})
. Obviously the set is not a metric space. However, the open set can be completed by including the singular positive semi-definite matrices to form the closed cone
(M_{n})
. Since the set is not compact, not all flows lead to a fixed point. Therefore, if a sequence of contraction operators can be constructed to define the dynamics of the covariance matrix, it will asymptotically converge to a fixed point in
M_{n}^{+}
. For the sequence
\{\pi_{k}\in[0,1]\},\forall k\in\mathbb{N}
, define a sequence of operators
\mathcal{T}{{}_{k}}:M_{n}\rightarrow M_{n}
as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{k}(P)=\pi_{k}APA^{\prime}+(1-\pi_{k})(A-BL)P(A-BL)^{\prime}

Then the state covariance update equation can be written as:

\displaystyle P_{k+1}=\mathcal{T}_{k}(P_{k})+W

Since
W\in M_{n}^{+}
, and
\mathcal{T}_{k}(P_{k})\in M_{n}
, it implies that
P_{k+1}\in M_{n}^{+}
. Clearly
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}P_{k}
will be unique and bounded if and only if
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{T}_{k}
is a contraction or equivalently
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{k}(P)\right\rVert_{2}<\left\lVert P\right\rVert_{2}
for all
P\in M_{n}
. Now,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{k}(P)\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\lVert\pi_{\infty}APA^{\prime}+(1-\pi_{\infty})(A-BL)P(A-BL)^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}

(49)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\lVert\pi_{\infty}APA^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert(1-\pi_{\infty})(A-BL)P(A-BL)^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi_{\infty}\left\lVert APA^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}+(1-\pi_{\infty})\left\lVert(A-BL)P(A-BL)^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}

(50)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\pi_{\infty}\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\left\lVert P\right\rVert_{2}+(1-\pi_{\infty})\left\lVert A-BL\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\left\lVert P\right\rVert_{2}

(51)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\lVert P\right\rVert_{2}\left[\pi_{\infty}\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+(1-\pi_{\infty})\left\lVert A-BL\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right]

Here Eqn.49 is obtained from the triangle inequality of spectral norm, Eqn.50 is obtained from the scalar multiplication of spectral norm, and Eqn.51 is obtained from the submultiplicative inequality of spectral norm. Moreover,
\pi_{\infty}=\left(\frac{p}{p+q}\right)
. Clearly, the necessary and sufficient condition for
\mathcal{T}_{\infty}
to be a contraction is given by:

\left[\left(\frac{p}{p+q}\right)\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\left(\frac{q}{p+q}\right)\left\lVert A-BL\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right]<1
. By rearrangement, Eqn.
32
of Theorem
16
is obtained. That concludes the proof.
\blacksquare

Machine learning for prognostic assessment in elderly patients with gastroduodenal perforation
Authors: Cang-Dian Huang, Shi-Long Chen, Shuai Chen, Peng-Sheng Tu, Jun-Rong Zhang, Xian-Qiang Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundElderly gastroduodenal perforation (E-GDP) is prone to severe adverse effects (SAEs) and high mortality rates. This study aims to develop an intelligent prognostic assessment system for the comprehensive evaluation of E-GDP.MethodsThis study included a development cohort of 108 patients with E-GDP and an independent validation cohort of 30 patients. Patients’ clinical features, laboratory findings, and postoperative complications were retrospectively collected. Patients were categorized into superior prognosis group(SG) and inferior prognosis group(IG) using two-step clustering (TSC). Machine learning models predicted E-GDP prognosis, interpreted via SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) analysis, and were validated at an external center.ResultsAfter TSC, patients were divided into two groups: the SG (76 patients) and the IG (32 patients). TSC better distinguished hospital days than SAE grade (p < 0.001 vs. p = 0.01). Compared with the SG, the IG had greater postoperative white blood cell counts (10.61 ± 4.82 vs. 8.01 ± 2.41, p = 0.006), intra-abdominal infections (94% vs. 0%, p < 0.001), anastomotic leakage (44% vs. 0%, p < 0.001), fluid time (8.01 ± 3.41 vs. 6.05 ± 2.12, p = 0.004), length of intensive care unit stay (114.20 ± 188.35 vs. 18.07 ± 48.55, p = 0.008), and costs (92180.79 ± 70920.55 vs. 38416.18 ± 20479.48, p < 0.001). The support vector machine (SVM) had the best performance, with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.90 (0.77, 0.99) and 0.77 (0.61, 0.92) in the external validation set(30 patients).ConclusionWe developed a robust SVM-based machine learning model using the TSC assessment system to preoperatively predict the prognosis of E-GDP patients.

Background

Gastroduodenal perforation (GDP) is a common and critical acute abdominal emergency with an incidence of up to 0.15% and a mortality of approximately 30% [1–4]. Elderly gastroduodenal perforation (E-GDP) is accompanied by compromised immunity, multiple comorbidities and a high prevalence of malignant tumors [5]. Moreover, E-GDP often presents with atypical symptoms, such as delayed treatment and diffuse peritonitis, which are closely related with a higher incidence of severe complications and mortality [4].

The Clavien‒Dindo (CD) grade and severe adverse effect (SAE) grade systems are widely recognized for their ability to evaluate postoperative complications and mortality risk [6, 7]. In E-GDP, peritonitis and abdominal infection due to gastrointestinal content leakage often necessitate postoperative decompression or drainage, which is classified as E-GDP-related CD Grade III [8]. The risk of SAE increases with increasing infection severity. However, a targeted postoperative assessment system that takes this factor into account is currently lacking. Therefore, we combined postoperative E-GDP data to establish a new assessment system and trained a machine learning classifier to enhance its practicality.

The two-step clustering (TSC) algorithm, which is capable of analyzing both continuous and categorical data, automatically identifies the optimal number of clusters [9]. This algorithm has been widely applied in clinical research because of its ability to reveal inherent patterns within patient data, leading to more precise and individualized classifications [10, 11]. Leveraging this algorithm, we have developed a TSC-based postoperative evaluation system for E-GDP.

In the field of gastrointestinal diseases, machine learning has been widely applied to construct predictive models and predict disease risk [12]. By feeding complex clinical data of various dimensions into the system and leveraging the powerful computational capabilities of different machine learning models, the underlying structures within the data are uncovered. In terms of disease onset risk, postoperative prognosis prediction, and survival analysis, machine learning has surpassed traditional assessment methods, providing a more objective basis for clinical decision-making [13–15].

We have successfully developed, for the first time, an intelligent prognostic evaluation system specifically tailored for E-GDP. This groundbreaking system leverages machine learning methodologies and preoperative data to deliver highly accurate predictions of patient outcomes.

This retrospective study comprised a development cohort of 108 patients from Fujian Medical University Union Hospital (July 2017–July 2024) and an external validation cohort of 30 patients from Pingtan County Hospital (June 2022–June 2023), following the exclusion of 23 patients based on predefined criteria from an initial identified of 161 individuals.

This study, approved by the institutional ethics committees, was based on data obtained from electronic medical records. All included patients underwent surgical repair rather than resection.

The collected data included baseline demographics (sex, age, history of abdominal surgery, comorbidities), medical history (symptoms), physical signs (peritonitis), ASA classification, and laboratory results (complete blood count, liver and kidney function, electrolytes, coagulation) before and at discharge, as well as intraoperative findings (perforation site and perforation size). Additionally, we recorded time to fluid intake, time to defecation, length of ICU stay, hospital costs, and occurrence of anastomotic leaks and intra-abdominal infections. Intra-abdominal infections, defined as severe postoperative complications, are identified during abdominal exploration following gastrointestinal perforation and noted when purulent fluid is encountered [16]. These infections require postoperative management with negative pressure irrigation using an abdominal drainage tube. Anastomotic leaks are characterized by postoperative symptoms such as fever, abdominal pain, distension, and peritoneal signs and are accompanied by an increased volume of abdominal drainage containing intestinal or gastric contents. Additionally, CT scans reveal abscesses or fluid accumulation around the anastomosis, indicating severe complications [17].

Severe postoperative complications were graded according to the Clavien–Dindo classification [8], distinguishing non-SAEs (grades I-III) from SAEs (grades IV-V). Grade I complications require no additional intervention or only minor interventions such as vomiting. Grade II complications necessitate pharmacological treatment or other procedures, including blood transfusions and total parenteral nutrition. Grade III complications require surgical intervention or other invasive procedures. Grade IV complications include life-threatening complications, encompassing central nervous system, cardiac, and pulmonary complications; renal failure; and those requiring intensive care unit (ICU) treatment. Grade V complications are defined as fatal.

The research process was as follows: (1) Data Imputation: To handle missing clinical data, we utilized the missForest algorithm for imputation to ensure the integrity of our dataset. (2) TSC Application: We applied the TSC algorithm in SPSS 27.0 to analyze the postoperative data, which included postoperative variables. This method enables the automatic calculation of group weights. (3) Feature Selection: Pearson correlation analysis was employed to identify the top 10 preoperative features. These features were then normalized and included creatinine, prothrombin time, albumin, fibrinogen, age, D-dimer, platelet count, activated partial thromboplastin time, potassium, and bicarbonate. (4) Data Splitting: The dataset was randomly divided into a training set (70%) and a testing set (30%). This process was repeated ten times to ensure the robustness and reliability of our model training and evaluation. (5) Model Training and Evaluation: A variety of machine learning classifiers, including linear discriminant analysis (LDA), logistic regression (LR), AdaBoost (AB), XGBoost (XGB), LightGBM (LGBM), support vector machine (SVM), multilayer perceptron (MLP), k-nearest neighbors (KNN), and random forest (RF), were trained to predict TSC group outcomes. Their performance was assessed via receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, the area under the curve (AUC), accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score on the test set. (6) Model Interpretation and Validation: SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) was used to interpret the models’ predictions, providing insights into feature importance. The predictive accuracy of the best-performing model was further validated on an independent dataset from Pingtan to confirm its generalizability. All the analytical steps, except TSC in SPSS 27.0, were executed using PyCharm 2022.2.4.

Statistical analysis

TSC assessment system development and analysis were conducted using SPSS 27.0. Postoperative data were analyzed with independent-samples t tests for normally distributed continuous variables and Mann‒Whitney U tests for nonnormally distributed or categorical variables. The efficacy of the TSC system was compared with that of the SAE system using Kaplan‒Meier survival analysis. The preoperative data followed the same testing protocol as the postoperative data. Statistical significance was set at a p value < 0.05.

Results

Establishment of a prognostic assessment system for E-GDP

After TSC, 76 patients were included in the superior prognosis group (SG), and 32 patients were included in the inferior prognosis group (IG). In terms of SAE grade, 82 patients were included in the non-SAE group, and 26 patients were included in the SAE group (Table 1). When the trends in hospital stay between the two assessment methods were compared, the TSC assessment system showed greater discriminatory ability (TSC: p < 0.001 vs. SAE: p = 0.01) (Table 2, Fig. 1). The factors included in the TSC assessment system were sorted according to weight, with intra-abdominal infection accounting for the highest proportion (1.0), followed by anastomotic leakage (0.41), hospital costs (0.34), and duration of intensive care (0.19) (Fig. 2).

Comparison of the elements of TSC between SG and IG

The SG exhibited higher hemoglobin levels (107.39 ± 19.52 vs. 97.31 ± 15.44, p = 0.006). The IG had increased white blood cell counts (10.61 ± 4.82 vs. 8.01 ± 2.41, p = 0.006) and neutrophil counts (8.44 ± 4.53 vs. 6.03 ± 2.31, p = 0.007). The IG also had a delayed initiation of fluid intake (8.01 ± 3.41 vs. 6.05 ± 2.12, p = 0.004), an extended ICU stay (114.20 ± 188.35 vs. 18.07 ± 48.55, p = 0.008), and higher hospital costs (92180.79 ± 70920.55 vs. 38416.18 ± 20479.48, p < 0.001). Additionally, the IG had a greater incidence of intra-abdominal infections (30 (94%) vs. 2 (6%), p < 0.001) and anastomotic leaks (14 (44%) vs. 0 (0%), p < 0.001) (Table 3).

Comparison of the outcome prediction efficacy of different machine learning classifiers for the TSC assessment system

Using the above variables, machine learning classifiers were used for prognosis prediction, with SVM showing superior predictive performance, resulting in an AUC of 0.90 (95% CI: 0.77, 0.99), accuracy of 0.88, precision of 0.90, recall of 0.96, and F1 score of 0.93. MLP and LR showed similar predictive performance. The following results were obtained for MLP: AUC (0.85, 95% CI: 0.69, 0.97), accuracy (0.82), precision (0.92), recall (0.85), and F1 score (0.88). The following results were obtained for LR: AUC (0.85, 95% CI: 0.68, 0.97), accuracy (0.82), precision (0.84), recall (0.96), and F1 score (0.90) (Table 5, Fig. 4). SHAP was used to interpret the SVM prediction process, with the most weighted variable being creatinine (0.231) (Fig. 5).

Discussion

With the global aging population, the prevalence of E-GDP is increasing [5, 18]. Elderly individuals often have weaker immune systems, leading to atypical symptoms and a tendency toward delayed treatment [19]. This demographic is at a greater risk for severe complications such as intra-abdominal infections, anastomotic leaks, abdominal abscesses, and sepsis, resulting in increased ICU admission rates and substantial socioeconomic impacts [20, 21]. The current absence of a precise assessment tool for the early identification of high-risk E-GDP patients presents a critical gap in clinical management [6, 22]. Our study leverages machine learning to analyze pre- and postoperative data, crafting a robust prognostic and preoperative prediction system designed to enhance risk stratification and patient outcomes in E-GDP.

In the TSC assessment system, intra-abdominal infection is prioritized given its strong correlation with increased mortality, septic shock, and acute kidney injury [23–26]. These infections are typically polymicrobial and involve drug-resistant bacteria, posing a severe threat to elderly patients’ postoperative recovery due to dysbiosis and continuous endotoxin release [23]. Anastomotic leaks are particularly significant in elderly patients, who are often malnourished and hypoalbuminemic, leading to tissue edema and reduced recovery capabilities due to abnormal osmotic pressure [27–29]. Chronic upper gastrointestinal ulcers contribute to tissue fibrosis and contraction, complicating healing in elderly patients. Additionally, the use of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) in elderly patients increases the risk of leakage after perforation repair, as these drugs inhibit prostaglandins, stimulate gastric acid secretion, and reduce the gastric mucosa blood supply, exacerbating mucosal damage and potentially leading to secondary peritonitis and worsening of intra-abdominal infections [30–32].

To bolster the practicality of the TSC assessment system, we trained eight machine learning models on patients’ clinical and biological data, focusing on 10 preoperative blood test indicators. Our findings indicate that the SVM, with an AUC of 0.9 on internal data and an accuracy of 74% in external validation, offers both precision and reliability. The essence of SVM is to identify a hyperplane that optimizes class separation, a feature that makes it particularly adept at handling high-dimensional, complex datasets and capable of addressing both classification and regression challenges, even with limited samples [33]. The superiority of SVMs is underscored by their frequent use and high accuracy in predicting 29 common diseases, outperforming other methods in 41% of cases [28]. Using SVM, we can perform a preliminary E-GDP prognosis assessment at admission, aiding in the development of personalized treatment plans.

SHAP, which is rooted in game theory, determines the importance of each feature in SVM predictions by computing SHAP values [31]. Creatinine levels emerged as the most influential predictor, typically increasing when kidney function is compromised by more than 50%, indicating renal status [34]. Elevated creatinine levels, confirmed by multiple studies as a significant risk factor for mortality in PPU patients, are exacerbated by age and conditions such as diabetes, hypertension, and NSAID use [26, 35–37]. Shock and severe intra-abdominal infections can trigger or worsen renal insufficiency through hypovolemia and reduced cardiac output [5]. Coagulation-related indicators also significantly contributed to the predictions. Severe systemic infections may suppress bone marrow, leading to a procoagulant or bleeding diathesis, potentially causing disseminated intravascular coagulation and multiorgan dysfunction in severe cases. Chronic gastrointestinal bleeding can deplete coagulation factors, platelets, and fibrinogen, leading to anemia. Hypoalbuminemia, an independent risk factor for poor prognosis, increases the risk of intra-abdominal infections and postoperative anastomotic leaks; however, its predictive impact is less significant and is possibly obscured by interactions with other variables [37].

This study’s external validation confirmed the model’s generalizability but was limited by its retrospective design and small sample size. Additionally, the study was limited by the lack of long-term follow-up data, which is crucial for comprehensively assessing patient outcomes and the sustainability of treatment effects. Future studies should aim to expand the dataset, include a larger and more diverse patient population, and incorporate extended follow-up periods to increase the model’s clinical applicability, generalizability, and predictive accuracy. This provides a more robust basis for evaluating the long-term prognosis of patients with E-GDP and allows for a better understanding of the model’s performance over time.

Conclusions

The TSC assessment system has demonstrated its effectiveness in the postoperative prognosis evaluation of E-GDP. The use of SVM allows rapid and accurate identification of high-risk patients in the early stages, providing an objective basis for personalized treatment of E-GDP.

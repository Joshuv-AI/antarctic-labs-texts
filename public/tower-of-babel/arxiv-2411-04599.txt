Increasing stability for inverse acoustic source problems
Suliang Si

Abstract
In this paper, we show the increasing stability of the inverse source problems for the acoustic wave equation in the full space ℝ 3 {\mathbb{R}}^{3} . The goal is to understand increasing stability for wave equation in the time domain. If the time and spatial variables of the source term can be separated with compact support, the increasing stability estimates of the L 2 L^{2} -norm of the acoustic source function can be established. The stability estimates consist of two parts: the Lipschitz type data discrepancy and the high time tail of the source functions. As the time increases, the latter decreases and thus becomes negligible.



1
Introduction

Consider the time-dependent wave propagation with an acoustic source modeled by

\partial_{t}^{2}U(x,t)-\Delta U(x,t)=F(x,t),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\ t>0,

(1.1)

where
U
denotes the wave field and
F
is the acoustic source. Together with the above governing equation, we impose the homogeneous initial conditions

U(x,0)=\partial_{t}U(x,0)=0,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

(1.2)

Specifically, we suppose that the dependence of the source term on time and
space variables are separated, that is

F(x,t)=f(x)g(t),

where
f
is compactly supported in the space region
B_{R}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}:\ |x|<R\}
with
R>0
.

The well known representation for
u
to the wave equation (
1.1
) gives

U(x,t)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{f(y)g(t-|x-y|)}{|y-x|}dy.

(1.3)

In many applications, the temporal function
g(t)

is usually given by the Ricker wavelet

g(t)=(1-2(\pi f_{p}(t-t_{0}))^{2})e^{-(\pi f_{p}(t-t_{0}))^{2}}

with the center frequency
f_{p}
and the delay time
t_{0}
.

In this
paper under the assumption that

g(t)=e^{-\gamma t}
for constant
\gamma>0
,
we prove increasing stability for the inverse problem of recovering the source function
f(x)
by the measurements, namely,

u(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in\partial B_{R}\times(0,T),

where
T>1
.

Throughout the paper,
a\lesssim b
stands for
a\leq Cb
, where
C>0
is a constant
independent of
\gamma
,
R
. Now we introduce the main stability result of this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\|f\|_{L^{2}{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}}\leq M
and
g(t)=e^{-\gamma t}
for constant
M,\gamma>0
. Assume that
U
is the solution of the scattering problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
). Then there exist constant
\alpha>0
and
C>0
depending on
R
,
\gamma
,
\alpha
such that

\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}}\leq C\Big(\epsilon^{2}+\frac{M^{2}}{(T^{\frac{2}{3}}|\ln\epsilon|^{\frac{1}{4}})^{\alpha}}\Big),

(1.4)

where
T>1
and

\epsilon=\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\partial B_{R}}\big(|\partial^{2}_{t}U(x,t)|^{2}+|\partial_{\nu}\partial_{t}U(x,t)|^{2}\big)ds(x)dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Remark 1.2
.

There are two parts in the stability estimates
(\ref{f})
: the first part is the data
discrepancy and the second part comes from the high time tail of the function. It is clear to
see that the stability increases as the time
T
increases, i.e., the source
f(x)
is more stable as more
time domain data are used. Our stability result in this work is consistent with the one in
[
14
]
for both the two-
and three-dimensional inverse scattering problems.

1.1
Known results and Motivations

Inverse sourse problems have many significant applications in scientific and engineering areas.
For instance, detection of submarines and non-destructive measurement of industrial objects
can be regarded as recovery of acoustic sources from boundary measurements of the pressure.
Other application include biomedical imaging optical tomography
[
1
,
19
]
and geophysics. For
a mathematical overview of various inverse source problems, one can see that uniqueness and
stability are discussed in
[
19
]
. For inverse source problems in time domain, it is solved as
hyperbolic systems by using Carleman estimate
[
25
]
and unique continuation; we refer to
[
12
,
24
,
30
,
31
]
for an incomplete list. For time-harmonic inverse source problems, it is well-known that
there is no uniqueness for the inverse source problem with a single frequency due to the existence
of non-radiating sources
[
11
,
23
]
. Therefore, the use of multiple frequencies data is an effective
way to overcome non-uniqueness and has received a lot of attention in recent years.
[
22
]
showed
the uniqueness and numerical results for Helmholtz equation with multi-frequency data. In
[
7
]
,
Bao et al. initiated the mathematical study on the stability of the inverse source problem for the
Helmholtz equation by using multi-frequency data. Then in
[
14
]
, a different method involving a
temporal Fourier transform, sharp bounds of the analytic continuation to higher wave numbers
was used to derive increasing stability bounds for the three dimensional Helmholtz equation.
Also,
[
14
]
firstly combined the Helmholtz equation and associated hyperbolic equations to get
the stability results. Later in
[
27
]
and
[
8
]
, increasing stability was extended to Helmholtz
equation and Maxwell’s equation in three dimension. We refer to
[
3
,
32
,
4
,
29
]
for the study of the
inverse source problems by using multiple frequency information.

Although a lot of work has been done on increasing stability of the inverse source problem for the time-harmonic waves. However, acoustic wave equation can also describe the propagation of waves. Does the inverse source problem of the acoustic equation also have some increasing stability? This work
initializes the mathematical study and provides the first stability results of the inverse source problems
for acoustic waves. The next section is devoted
to the proof of the result.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

The following lemma gives bounds for
f
within the framework of the hyperbolic initial
value problem (
1.1
).

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
u
be the solution of the scattering problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
). Then

\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leq C\Big(\|\partial^{2}_{t}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial B_{R}\times(0,\infty))}+\|\partial_{\nu}\partial_{t}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial B_{R}\times(0,\infty))}\Big),

where
C>0
depends on
R
and
\gamma
.

Proof.

Let
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
with
|\xi|=w\in(0,\infty)
.
Our argument relies essentially on the temporal
Fourier transform of the function
U
, deﬁned by

u(x,w)=\int_{0}^{\infty}U(x,t)e^{-iwt}dt.

Multiplying
e^{-i(\xi\cdot x+wt)}
on both sides of (
1.1
)
and integrating over
B_{R}\times(0,\infty)
, we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{B_{R}}f(x)e^{-i\xi\cdot x}dx\int_{0}^{\infty}g(t)e^{-iwt}dt&=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\partial B_{R}}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}(\partial_{\nu}U(x,t)e^{-iwt}+i(\nu\cdot\xi)U(x,w)e^{-iwt})ds(x)dt\\
&=-\int_{\partial B_{R}}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}(\partial_{\nu}u(x,w)+i(\nu\cdot\xi)u(x,k))ds(x).\end{split}

Here we used that the temporal function
g(t)=e^{-\gamma t}
is time decay.

Since
|\int_{0}^{\infty}g(t)e^{-iwt}dt|\geq c_{0}>0
and Supp
f\subset B_{R}
, we have

|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(x)e^{-i\xi\cdot x}dx|^{2}\leq C\int_{\partial B_{R}}(\big|\partial_{\nu}u(x,w)|^{2}+w^{2}|u(x,w)\big|^{2})ds(x).

Hence we obtain by using the spherical coordinates that

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(x)e^{-i\xi\cdot x}dx|^{2}d\xi&\leq C\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{\partial B_{R}}\big|\partial_{\nu}u(x,w)|^{2}+w^{2}|u(x,w))\big|^{2}ds(x)\\
&\leq C\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin\varphi d\varphi\int_{0}^{\infty}w^{2}\big(\int_{\partial B_{R}}|\partial_{\nu}u(x,w)|^{2}+w^{2}|u(x,w))|^{2}ds(x)\big)dw\\
&\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}(\int_{\partial B_{R}}|w\partial_{\nu}u(x,w)|^{2}+|w^{2}u(x,w))|^{2})ds(x)dw.\end{split}

(2.5)

Using the Parseval’s identity yields

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\partial B_{R}}(|\partial^{2}_{t}u|^{2}ds(x)dt+|\partial_{\nu}\partial_{t}u|^{2})ds(x)dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\partial B_{R}}(|w^{2}u(x,w)|^{2}+|w\partial_{\nu}u(x,w)|^{2})ds(x)dw.

It follows from the Parseval’s identity again that

\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leq C\Big(\|\partial^{2}_{t}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial B_{R}\times(0,\infty))}+\|\partial_{\nu}\partial_{t}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial B_{R}\times(0,\infty))}\Big),

(2.6)

which completes the proof.

∎

Let

I_{1}(k)=\int_{0}^{k}\int_{\partial B_{R}}|\partial^{2}_{t}u(x,t)|^{2}ds(x)dt

and

I_{2}(k)=\int_{0}^{k}\int_{\partial B_{R}}|\partial_{\nu}\partial_{t}u(x,t)|^{2}ds(x)dt.

Denote

S=\{z=x+iy\in{\mathbb{C}}:-\frac{\pi}{4}<z<\frac{\pi}{4}\}.

Since
g(t)
is entire analytic function of
t
, the integrals
I_{1}(k)
and
I_{2}(k)
with respect
to
t
can be taken over any path joining points
0
and
k
of the complex plane. Thus
I_{1}(k)
and
I_{2}(k)
are entire analytic functions of
k=k_{1}+ik_{2}\in{\mathbb{C}}
(
k_{1},k_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
) and the following elementary estimates hold.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
,
suppf\subset B_{R}
. For any
k=k_{1}+ik_{2}\in S
, we have

\begin{split}|I_{1}(k)|\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}|k|e^{2\gamma|k_{1}|}\end{split}

(2.7)

and

\begin{split}|I_{1}(k)|\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}^{2}|k|e^{2\gamma|k_{1}|},\end{split}

(2.8)

where
C>0
depends on
R
and
\gamma
.

Proof.

Since

U(x,t)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{f(y)g(t-|x-y|)}{|y-x|}dy,

we get

\partial^{2}_{t}U(x,t)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{f(y)g^{\prime\prime}(t-|x-y|)}{|y-x|}dy.

Let
t=k\hat{t}
,
\hat{t}\in(0,1)
. It follows from the change of
variables that

\begin{split}I_{1}(k)=&\int_{0}^{k}\int_{\partial B_{R}}|\partial^{2}_{t}u(x,t)|^{2}ds(x)dt\\
&=\int_{0}^{k}\int_{\partial B_{R}}\big|\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{B_{R}}\frac{f(y)g^{\prime\prime}(t-|x-y|)}{|y-x|}dy\big|^{2}ds(x)dt\\
&=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\partial B_{R}}k\big|\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{B_{R}}\frac{f(y)g^{\prime\prime}(k\hat{t}-|x-y|)}{|y-x|}dy\big|^{2}ds(x)d\hat{t}.\end{split}

Using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality yields

\begin{split}|I_{1}(k)|&\leq C\int_{0}^{1}|k|\int_{\partial B_{R}}|f(y)|^{2}dy\int_{\partial B_{R}}\big|\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{B_{R}}\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(k\hat{t}-|x-y|)}{|y-x|}dy\big|^{2}ds(x)d\hat{t}\\
&\leq C\int_{0}^{1}|k|\int_{\partial B_{R}}|f(y)|^{2}dy\int_{\partial B_{R}}|\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{B_{R}}\frac{e^{-2\gamma(k\hat{t}-|x-y|)}}{|y-x|^{2}}dy|ds(x)d\hat{t}\\
&\leq C|k|e^{\gamma k_{1}}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}{(B_{R})}}.\end{split}

Noting that

\partial_{\nu}\partial_{t}u(x,t)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{f(y)\big(g^{\prime\prime}(t-|x-y|)+g^{\prime}(t-|x-y|)\big)\frac{y-x}{|y-x|}\cdot\nu}{|y-x|^{2}}dy,

we have from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the polar coordinates
\rho=|y-x|
(originated at
x
) with respect to
y
yield that

|\partial_{\nu}\partial_{t}u(x,t)|^{2}\leq e^{-2\gamma t}|\int_{0}^{2R}f(y)e^{\gamma\rho}d\rho|^{2}\leq C\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}{(B_{R})}}e^{-2\gamma t}.

Similarly we obtain

\begin{split}|I_{2}(k)|=|k\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Gamma_{0}}|\partial_{\nu}\partial_{t}u(x,k\hat{t})|^{2}ds(x)d\hat{t}|\leq C\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}{(B_{R})}}|k|e^{2\gamma|k_{1}|},\end{split}

(2.9)

which completes the proof.
∎

Now we show the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

If
\epsilon\geq e^{-1}
, then the estimate is
obvious.
If
\epsilon<e^{-1}
, we discuss (
1.4
) in two cases.

Case (i):
2^{\frac{1}{4}}((2\gamma+3)\pi)^{\frac{1}{3}}T^{\frac{1}{3}}<|\ln\epsilon|^{\frac{1}{4}}
.
Choose
s_{0}=\frac{1}{((2\gamma+3)\pi)^{\frac{1}{3}}}T^{\frac{2}{3}}|\ln\epsilon|^{\frac{1}{4}}
. It is easy to get
s_{0}>2^{\frac{1}{4}}T
, then

-\mu(s_{0})\leq-\frac{1}{\pi}((\frac{s_{0}}{T})^{4}-1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq-\frac{1}{\pi}(\frac{T}{s_{0}})^{2}.

A direct application of estimate (
2.10
) shows that

\displaystyle|I(s_{0})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CM^{2}\epsilon^{2\mu(s_{0})}e^{(2\gamma+3)s_{0}}

\displaystyle CM^{2}e^{(2\gamma+3)s_{0}-2\mu(s_{0})|\ln\epsilon|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CM^{2}e^{(2\gamma+3)s_{0}-\frac{2|\ln\epsilon|}{\pi}(\frac{T}{s_{0}})^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle CM^{2}e^{-2(\frac{(2\gamma+3)^{2}}{\pi})^{\frac{1}{3}}T^{\frac{2}{3}}|\ln\epsilon|^{\frac{1}{2}}(1-\frac{1}{2}|\ln\epsilon|^{-\frac{1}{4}})},

depends on
R
and
\gamma
.

Hence there exists
C>0
depending on

,
\alpha
and

such that

\begin{split}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}}&=I(s_{0})+\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{\partial B_{R}}\big(|\partial^{2}_{t}u(x,t)|^{2}+|\partial_{\nu}\partial_{t}u(x,t)|^{2}\big)ds(x)dt\\
&\leq C\frac{M^{2}}{(T^{2}|\ln\epsilon|^{\frac{3}{2}})^{\alpha}}+C\frac{M^{2}}{(T^{\frac{2}{3}}|\ln\epsilon|^{\frac{1}{4}})^{\alpha}}\\

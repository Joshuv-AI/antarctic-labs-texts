Suturing the placental bed in addition to intrauterine balloon tamponade, is it effective in placenta previa patients?
Authors: Duygu Tugrul Ersak, Nazlı Orhan, Gulcan Okutucu, Ozgecan Ucyildiz, Ozgur Kara, Fatma Doga Ocal, Atakan Tanacan, Dilek Sahi̇n
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study aimed to examine the effectiveness of placental bed sutures in placenta previa (PP) patients and compare the amount of estimated blood loss (EBL) in patients who were only applied intrauterine balloon tamponade (IUBT) with the patients who were applied suturing to the placental bed in addition to IUBT.MethodsPP patients (n:118) who were applied IUBT for postpartum hemorrhage (PPH) were the inclusion criteria. PP patients who were only applied IUBT constituted the suture-negative group (n:65) and the patients who were applied suturing to the placental bed in addition to IUBT constituted the suture-positive group (n:53). Clinicodemographic features, transfused blood and/or blood products, uterotonics used, EBL, and the volume IUBT inflated, and the duration of IUBT, were recorded and compared between the groups.ResultsThe median volume IUBT inflated was 200 (160–460) mL and the median duration of IUBT was 12 (6–24) hours in PP patients. The amount of EBL was shown to be higher in suture-positive group (p = 0.003). Multivariate logistic regression analysis revealed that presence of suturing the placental bed was associated with severe PPH (p = 0.017).ConclusionsIUBT, is a successful method that is easily applicable, and administration of IUBT without suturing the placental bed may be sufficient to manage PPH in low-risk PP patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12884-026-09366-w.Keywords: Balloon tamponade, Placental bed suture Cesarean section, Estimated blood loss, Placenta previa, Postpartum hemorrhage

Introduction

Placenta previa (PP), is the condition in which the placenta extends partially or completely over the cervix uteri [1]. Due to ethical and legal issues, the incidence of cesarean section (CS) is increasing worldwide, especially in developing countries [2]. As regards, placental adhesion anomalies and PP are seen to a larger extent in correlation with the increased number of CS operations [3, 4].

The incidence of PP has been reported to be 2.7–12.2 per 1000 pregnancies varying between regions [5]. Not only the maternal morbidity and mortality such as postpartum hemorrhage (PPH), hysterectomy, and blood transfusion were high in PP patients, but also fetal morbidity and mortality were high [6].

PPH in PP patients was reported to be 27.4% in a meta-analysis [7]. This is more than a quarter of the patients. Therefore, the evaluation of PPH risk factors in PP patients and precautions should be considered in advance to reduce the maternal complications of surgery.

Intrauterine balloon tamponade (IUBT) is considered a technique in the management of PP patients with hemorrhage. Balloon tamponade is an easy method to be performed which can even be used in limited source field hospitals [8]. In a recent study, the prophylactic use of IUBT was shown to reduce the massive PPH frequency in PP patients [9]. IUBT was shown to be effective in 88% of the PP patients with PPH who failed with the medical uterotonics during CS [10]. In contrast, increased incidence of surgery and maternal death was shown by Anger et al. in patients introduced with uterine balloon tamponade [11].

New modified surgical and suture techniques such as chitosan-covered tamponades or transverse B-Lynch sutures have been used in PP patients complicated with hemorrhage during CS [12, 13]. Combined techniques, Foley balloon insertion accompanied by sutures which are placed around the lower segment of the uterus were also used to prevent the blood loss [14].

There is still a need for clarifying the proper treatment in patients with PP and the correct indication and right moment of IUBT and/or placental bed sutures in PPH management. Whether additional methods used in PP patients, such as placental bed suturing, truly reduce blood loss or merely result in unnecessary time loss during the procedure remains obscure. Therefore, in this study, we aimed to determine the effectiveness of IUBT in PP patients and compare the amount of blood loss in patients who were only applied IUBT with the patients who were applied suturing to the placental bed in addition to IUBT.

Materials and methods

This study was approved by the Ankara City Hospital review board (E2-22-1650). Pregnant patients were informed and have given informed consent at the initial examination during hospitalization as a routine procedure.

Pregnant patients diagnosed with PP and have PPH unresponsive to first-line interventions and medical uterotonic treatments, and who were subsequently applied IUBT for PPH were the inclusion criteria. The data between the years 2019–2025 were obtained from the hospital network system and patient files. All PP patients underwent CS.

The exclusion criteria were as follows: increta / percreta previa, placental abruption, patients who underwent a hysterectomy, twin pregnancy, and acquired and/or hereditary coagulation disorders. Surgery and anesthesia notes were checked to ensure accuracy, and patients who underwent IUBT due to uterine atony were excluded from the study. Uterine compression sutures, followed by the placement of an IUBT which is known as the sandwich method were also excluded.

In patients with PP, urinary catheter is inserted, two vascular accesses are established, the blood parameters including cross-match and coagulation profile are sent to the laboratory before the CS, and the patient’s vital signs are monitored continuously. These patients are taken into surgery in the lithotomy position to make the interventions easier when necessary [15].

PPH was defined as to the World Health Organization (WHO): 500 mL loss of blood within 24 h after birth and severe PPH was defined as 1000 mL loss of blood within the same period [16, 17]. As a Clinic, we follow a standardized protocol as regards PPH guidelines [16, 18, 19]: 5 IU slow intravenous (iv) oxytocin is administered during the CS for PPH prophylaxis. In the case of unresponsive PPH, initially, the dose of oxytocin is increased (usually 10 IU, maximum a total of 80 IU). In patients who did not receive oxytocin for PPH prophylaxis, carbetocin was administered as a single iv dose. The recommended prophylactic dose of 100 µg was infused over a period longer than 1 min. Then, up to the clinicians’ discretion and the comorbidities of the pregnant patients’ prostaglandin E1 and/or 0.2 mg methylergonovine are administered. Additionally, 1 gram of tranexamic acid is given. Simultaneously, immediate help is called, and the cause of PPH is evaluated.

If there is no response to the applied medical uterotonic treatment, we switch to second-line treatment (initially tamponade techniques and then surgical treatment) within approximately 5 min. As for the surgical treatment, bilateral uterine arteries are ligated as it is technically simple, and rapidly reduces uterine blood flow. If hemostasis is not achieved, utero-ovarian artery ligation is performed. In cases of ongoing hemorrhage, bilateral internal iliac artery ligation, a technically demanding procedure, is performed. If bleeding persists despite these surgical treatment measures, hysterectomy is performed.

In patients for whom a balloon is inserted during CS, we insert the balloon through the incision and close the incision without inflating the balloon completely. During closure of the uterine incision, the tamponade balloon was inflated with methylene blue to facilitate early detection of balloon rupture. An additional movie file shows this in more detail (Additional file 1. IUBT preparation and insertion). The balloon is inflated to a maximum of 500 mL and held for a maximum of 24 h. The balloon is gradually deflated to permit reinflation in preparation for potential surgical intervention. Prophylactic antibiotics are used in patients with balloon tamponade. Oxytocin infusion is continued until the balloon is withdrawn.

The decision to transfuse blood products was based on a combination of clinical and laboratory parameters, and our institution’s PPH management protocols (including ongoing bleeding, hemodynamic instability, signs of hypovolemia, and hemoglobin levels). Transfusions were performed on patients with symptomatic anemia or hemoglobin levels < 8 g/dL, or those with higher thresholds in cases of persistent bleeding or hemodynamic deterioration. The use of additional blood products was guided by coagulation parameters and clinical assessment.

In this study, PP patients were divided into 2 groups: patients who were only applied IUBT (suture negative group) and the patients who were applied suturing to the placental bed in addition to IUBT (suture positive group). The figure of 8 and Z-shaped, sutures were used to suture the placental bed.

Maternal age, anthropometric measurements, obstetrical and surgical history, history of antenatal and postpartum hemorrhage, pre and postoperative Hemoglobin (Hb), Hematocrit (Htc), and fibrinogen values, and vital signs before and after the insertion of IUBT were recorded. The total volume IUBT inflated, the duration of IUBT, the amount of drained hemorrhage from the bag connected to IUBT, the duration of the hospital and intensive care unit (ICU) stay were recorded.

The amounts of transfused blood and/or blood products, and uterotonics used were recorded. Estimated blood loss (EBL) and shock index (SI) were recorded. Maternal height, maternal weight, predelivery and postdelivery hematocrit values were used to calculate the EBL [20]. SI was calculated with the heart rate (HR) / systolic blood pressure (SBP) formula [21]. Additionally, the neonatal outcomes were recorded.

Statistical analysis

Statistical Package for the Social Sciences 24 program was used to analyze the data. Shapiro -Wilk and Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests was used to test the normality of the data. Mean +/- standart deviation was used to show the normally distributed data. Non-normally distributed data was shown with median (min-max), median (IQR). Independent t test, Mann Whitney U test, Chi-square test, and Fisher Exact test was use to compare the groups. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was held for determining independent risk factors for EBL ≥ 1000 mL (severe PPH). P value < 0.05 was accepted to be significant.

Results

One hundred eighteen pregnant women diagnosed to be PP and applied IUBT for hemorrhage were enrolled in the study. The anthropometric and clinicodemographic features, vital signs, information about the inserted IUBT, transfused products, and the neonatal outcomes of the whole PP patients (n:118) were given in Table 1. The mean age of the PP patients was 31.4+/-5.2 years and the mean preoperative Hemoglobin (Hb) was 11.6+/-1.1 g/dL which was in the normal range of during pregnancy. 40.7% of the PP patients were transfused erythrocytes during the hospital stay. Additionally, IUBT was inflated with a median of 200 mL and the median duration of IUBT was 12 h in PP patients. The median EBL was calculated to be 667 (106–3356) mL (Table 1).

The median duration of IUBT was 12 (10–24) hours in the suture positive group whereas it was 8 (6–24) hours in the suture negative group (p = 0.046). Although the volume of IUBT inflated did not differ between the groups, the amount of drained hemorrhage from the bag connected to IUBT was significantly higher in the suture positive group (p = 0.006). The amount of erythrocyte transfusion, fibrinogen, and fresh frozen plasma (FFP) was significantly higher in the suture (+) group (All p < 0.05). Additionally, the median EBL of the suture positive group was 919 mL (149–3356), the median EBL of the suture negative group was 584 mL (101–1986) (p = 0.003). Neonatal outcomes were also similar between the groups, median gestational 37 weeks at birth (Table 2).

Multivariate logistic regression analysis was held for determining independent risk factors for EBL ≥ 1000 cc (severe PPH). It was revealed that suturing the placental bed was an independent risk factor for EBL ≥ 1000 cc and increased the risk 3.2 times (95% CI 1.2–8.5) (p = 0.017). Moreover, the gestational age at the time of PP diagnosis was found to be the other independent risk factor (95% CI 1.0- 1.24) (p = 0.045). The risk of EBL ≥ 1000 cc was shown to increase as the diagnosis of PP is made in later weeks of gestation.

Discussion

In the current study, the effectiveness of IUBT in PP patients was examined, and EBL was compared between patients who were only applied IUBT and the patients who were applied suturing to the placental bed in addition to IUBT. Although the transfused blood products were significantly higher in the suture + group, the amount of EBL was also shown to be higher in this group.

Various types of intrauterine balloons (Foley catheters, condoms, Rusch and Bakri balloons) and conservative approaches have been used to prevent hysterectomy in PP patients [22–24]. Prophylactic use of IUBT was shown to reduce the PPH in PP patients [9]. IUBT was shown to be effective in almost 90% of patients in whom uterotonics fail, which is a great success rate [10]. IUBT is an easily applicable method for managing PPH. It has been used for PPH not responsive to uterotonics in both PP patients and uterine atony patients to control hemorrhage for many years [25].

In a study including 53 PP patients was applied BT-Cath. uterine tamponade. Although, placenta accreta patients (n:14) were also enrolled in that study and 4 of them required a hysterectomy, an overall IUBT success rate of 85% was achieved. In PP patients the main problem is the lower uterine segment not the contractility of the uterine fundus muscles. Therefore, that study suggested that there was no need to inflate the balloon more than 220 mL in PP patients and to gradually deflate the balloon. In that study, the mean duration of the IUBT was 10.4 h in the successful group, and the authors suggested not keeping the IUBT for a long time interval such as 18–24 h [26]. Similar to that study, in our study, the median volume IUBT inflated was 200 mL and IUBT was gradually deflated in all PP patients to get prepared for surgical intervention if needed. Additionally, in our study, the median duration of IUBT was 12 (6–24) hours. Only in a few of the PP patients (n:3) the IUBT was kept in utero up to 24 h.

Balloon tamponade was shown to be effective in PP patients based on numerous studies [10, 26]. On the other hand, data about suturing the placental bed is scarce. Compressive sutures such as B-Lynch, and Cho-square have been rarely combined with IUBT to control massive PPH [27]. However, we think that the most commonly utilized method in addition to IUBT is suturing the placental bed in PP patients. For this reason, in this current study, the effectiveness of these suturing procedures was evaluated and EBL and the transfused blood products of the PP patients who were applied suturing to the placental bed were found to be higher. It can be thought that patients in whom suturing was applied to the placental bed were patients who were already worse cases, suturing was done when there was excessive bleeding, and therefore had higher EBL. However, we believe that the reason for the higher EBL was due to the loss of time during the suture application. Keeping the abdomen open for a longer period of time to apply sutures during the operation and delaying the hemostasis processes of the organ may be associated with more hemorrhage. The observation that placental bed suturing was associated with higher EBL should not be interpreted as evidence that suturing itself causes bleeding. Placental bed suturing may prolong operative time and delay the establishment of effective tamponade. Early placement of an IUBT may provide immediate compression of the bleeding surface and facilitate faster hemostasis in selected low-risk PP patients.

Although logistic regression analysis showed that suturing the placental bed was associated with severe PPH, this association should be interpreted cautiously, as suturing is typically performed in severe cases and may reflect underlying clinical complexity rather than a causal relationship. In contrast, IUBT provides rapid and effective hemostasis and was associated with lower blood loss when used alone in selected low-risk PP patients. Therefore, while IUBT may be sufficient in appropriate cases, these findings can not be generalized. More specific, and multicenter studies are needed to define optimal management strategies clearly.

In our opinion, there was no difference between the suture + group and suture (–) groups in terms of the risk of hemorrhage. Because PP patients who were considered to be at high risk of PPH, such as those with myometrial defects and/or bulging of the placenta on the bladder surface, and multiple lacunes were not included in the study. Patients with placenta accreta spectrum were excluded based on antenatal ultrasonographic findings, intraoperative assessment, postoperative surgical and pathological records. Placental abruption cases were also excluded. With regard to the observed high estimated blood loss (EBL) in some cases (up to 3352 mL), this finding may be partly related to severe bleeding originating from the lower uterine segment, which is known to have limited contractile capacity in PP. In addition, increased uterine blood flow and maternal hemodynamic changes in term or near-term pregnancies may contribute to higher volumes of blood loss. Furthermore, even in cases classified as placenta accreta, significant hemorrhage may still occur due to abnormal placental adherence. In the present study, hemorrhage in PP patients appeared to be controlled with IUBT alone in selected cases. However, to potentially reduce EBL, early or prophylactic placement of IUBT may be considered, rather than delaying intervention until spontaneous hemostasis is achieved.

Although the amount of transfused blood products was higher in the suture positive group, postoperative hemoglobin parameters were lower and EBL was higher. When uterotonic use was compared in both groups, uterotonic use was found to be significantly higher in the suture negative group (transamine, methyergonovine, carbetocin). The finding that uterotonic use was higher in the suture-negative group despite lower EBL is noteworthy. This observation suggests that active and timely medical management with uterotonics may have contributed to more effective hemorrhage control in this group. This situation could cause a difference in bleeding between the groups and that it could be the cause of bleeding in the suture (+) group. Additionally, the results underscore the importance of active uterotonic therapy as a key component of PPH management alongside surgical approaches [28]. The suture positive group may have been more conservative in uterotonic use and hesitated to increase the amount uterotonics used because they may have thought that suturing the placental bed already reduced bleeding in the intraoperative period. However, higher dose of uterotonics were used in the suture negative group and active uterotonic use may have effectively reduced bleeding and therefore EBL may have been lower. In addition, IUBT application is an easily applicable and a standardized treatment.

Management of PPH may differ between clinics, and physicians generally prefer methods depending on their experiences. For example, combination of preventive radiological catheterization of the descending aorta, use of Affronti endouterine square hemostatic sutures, and placement of an intrauterine Bakri balloon in conjunction with B-Lynch suture showed considerable success in a case series of 9 placenta accreta cases [29].

The relatively longer hospital stays observed in the current study reflect our institutional management protocols and a precautionary approach. Postoperatively in PP patients who were applied IUBT, extended hospitalization is frequently required for hemodynamic monitoring, observation for delayed PPH, management of anemia and/or transfusion requirements.

This study was carried out in a referee center that has a 3rd level intensive care unit and a blood transfusion center. Therefore, the number of PP patients is actually high (n:118). The strength of this study is that ultrasonographic examinations of the PP patients were performed by a single Perinatology expert (DS) to prevent interobserver differences. A limitation is even though specialists have undergone the same training process in the clinics, differences in the surgical management of the patients may have occurred due to the decision being made based on the initiative of the surgical team involved in the PP case. Sutures are techniques that require more surgical experience and may vary among surgeons. The decision to perform placental bed suturing was not based on a standardized protocol but was made intraoperatively, primarily according to the surgeon’s subjective assessment of bleeding severity. Additionally, surgeon preference and individual experience played a role in this decision-making process. We acknowledge that this non-standardized approach may have introduced variability. As mentioned in this study, the figure of 8 and Z-shaped sutures were quite a variable procedure too. In the current study, a high percentage of the pregnant women were smokers (11.9%). Cigarette consumption did not differ between the groups. However, the cigarette consumption information was obtained verbally from the patients and the lack of objective data can be considered another limitation of our study. Also, one of the main limitations of retrospective series is the potential selection bias and non-standardization of the surgical procedures. Thus, claiming one method is superior to one another is pretentious without multicenter prospective studies including higher number of cases.

In conclusion, this study’s findings suggest that IUBT alone may be enough to manage PPH in selected low-risk PP patients, excluding those with placenta accreta spectrum disorders, without the need for additional placental bed suturing. To validate the findings, further multicenter studies with more numerous PP patients are needed.

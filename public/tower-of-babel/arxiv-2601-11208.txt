Uniform Local Tabularity in Intuitionistic Logic
Rodrigo Nicolau Almeida

Abstract
By contrast wih 𝖲𝟦 \mathsf{S4} , the analysis of local tabularity above 𝖨𝖯𝖢 \mathsf{IPC} has provided a difficult challenge. This paper studies a strengthening of local tabularity – uniform local tabularity – where one demands that all formulas be equivalent to formulas of a given implication depth. Algebraically, this amounts to considering Heyting algebras generated by finitely many iterations of the implication operation. It is shown that in contrast with locally finite Heyting algebras, n n -uniformly locally finite Heyting algebras always form a variety, and an explicit axiomatization of the variety of n n -uniform locally finite Heyting algebras for n ≤ 2 n\leq 2 is given. In connection with this analysis, it is shown that there exist locally tabular logics which are not uniformly locally tabular, answering a question of Shehtman – an example of a pre-uniformly locally tabular logic is presented.



1
Introduction

In intuitionistic and modal logics, one often looks for properties which ensure the system is “sufficiently simple” to analyze in depth. Local tabularity ensures this by saying that over finitely many propositional variables there can only be finitely many such formulas, and in turn, that the semantics of the system cannot be too complicated. This appears as a strengthening of ‘tabularity’ – saying that a single finite structure captures the full semantics of the logic – which is quite well-understood in intuitionistic and modal logics (see
[
10
, Chapter 12]
). Moreover, it is a classical result due to Segerberg
[
24
]
and Maksimova
[
19
]
that an extension of
\mathsf{S4}
is locally tabular if and only if it is of finite depth. This provides an easy criterion and algorithm to verify local tabularity, with there being a single “pre-locally tabular” logic.

By contrast, in the system
\mathsf{IPC}
, the situation is quite different. It was shown by Mardaev
[
20
,
21
]
that there is a continuum of pre-locally tabular logics, all above
\mathsf{KC}
. It is open whether every locally tabular logic is contained in a pre-locally tabular one and whether local tabularity is decidable. In terms of algebraic models – which correspond in algebraic logical terms to the logics – it is thus open whether the category of locally finite Heyting algebra admits any easy and logically insightful description (see
[
18
]
for recent discussion).

Given these difficulties it is reasonable to seek for strengthenings of local tabularity. In
[
27
]
, Shehtman observed that one can use bisimulation games to characterize when certain logics are locally tabular, introducing a property called “finite formula depth”. In
[
2
]
, this property was dubbed “uniform local tabularity”; the intuition being that for all above examples, local tabularity in fact corresponds to the collapse of modal operators. Indeed, it is shown by Shehtman that above
\mathsf{S4}
local tabularity implies uniform local tabularity, via finite depth, and hence the ‘niceness’ of local tabularity may be attributed to the uniform version. Such a problem was noted again in
[
25
]
where generalizations of the finite height criterion were discussed.

This article provides a first systematic investigation of this property in the context of superintuitionistic logics. Our main contributions are the following:

1.

We revise the basic theory of locally finite Heyting algebras, contrasting this with
n
-uniform Heyting algebras, showing that the latter always form varieties.

2.

We provide an explicit axiomatization of the variety of
2
-uniform Heyting algebras, called here
\mathsf{2Uni}
; this is the smallest logic
L
which is
2
-uniform, and it sits between well-known logics:

\mathsf{wPL}\subsetneq\mathsf{2Uni}\subsetneq\mathsf{LC},

where
\mathsf{LC}
is the Gödel-Dummett calculus
[
11
,
17
]
, and
\mathsf{wPL}
is the Weak Peirce’s Law (see Section
3.2
).

3.

We provide an example of a locally tabular logic which is not uniformly locally tabular, answering a problem left open in
[
26
]
. Indeed, we show that the logic
\mathsf{Box}
, is pre-uniformly locally tabular – all of its extensions are uniformly locally tabular, though
\mathsf{Box}
itself is not so, and it is the unique one with this property above the system
\mathsf{KG}
.

We conclude by pointing to several questions left open by the present work.

2
Preliminaries and basic theory

2.1
Superintuitionistic logics and Kripke models

Throughout we will assume that we are working in the signature
\mathcal{L}=(\wedge,\vee,\rightarrow,\bot,\top)
of Heyting algebras, the language of intuitionistic logic. We use the letters
\varphi,\psi,\dots
for
\mathcal{L}
-formulas and letters
p,q,\dots
or
x,y,\dots
for variables; we compactly represent a tuple of distinct variables
as
\overline{p}
. We write
\varphi(\overline{p})
to mean that all the variables of
\varphi
occur amongst
\overline{p}
. The
implication-depth
of
\varphi
, denoted by
d(\varphi)
, is defined by induction:

•

d(p)=d(\bot)=d(\top)=0
;

•

d(\varphi\wedge\psi)=d(\varphi\vee\psi)=\max(d(\varphi),d(\psi))
;

•

d(\varphi\rightarrow\psi)=\max(d(\varphi),d(\psi))+1
.

Given a poset
P
, we write
P\Vdash\varphi
to mean that
P
validates
\varphi
in the usual Kripke semantics (see e.g.,
[
10
, Chapter 3]
). We denote by
\mathrm{Ext}(\mathsf{IPC})
the lattice of its extensions; elements are called
superintuitionistic logics
. Given a poset
P
, we denote by:

\mathrm{Log}(P)=\{\varphi:P\Vdash\varphi\},

the
logic of
P
, which is an element of
\mathrm{Ext}(\mathsf{IPC})
. If
P\Vdash L
for all
\varphi\in L
we say that
P

is a frame for
L
.

Given a poset
P
, a subset
U\subseteq P
is called an
upset
if whenever
x\in U
and
x\leq y
then
y\in U
. We denote the set of upsets of
P
by
\mathsf{Up}(P)
. We say that
Q
is a
rooted upset
of
P
if there is
x\in P
such that
Q\cong{\uparrow}x
. Given
x\in P
, we denote
{\uparrow}x
by
P_{x}
.

A
p-morphism
between posets
f:P\to Q
is an order-preserving function such that for each
x\in P
and
y\in Q
if
f(x)\leq y
then there is some
x^{\prime}\geq x
such that
f(x^{\prime})=y
; we say that
Q
is a
p-morphic image
of
P
if there is a surjective p-morphism from
P
onto
Q
.

We now recall some relevant notions concerning finite Kripke models; the reader can find all necessary background in
[
15
, Chapter 4.1]
.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that a triple
(\mathfrak{M},x)=(P,\leq,c,x)
where
(P,\leq)
is a rooted poset, with root
x
, and

is an order-preserving function, is a
model over the propositional letters
\overline{p}
. We say that

is a model of a logic

(over the propositional letters
\overline{p}
) if
(P,\leq)
is a frame for

.

Given

a class of posets, we say that
(\mathfrak{M},x)
a model over
\overline{p}
is an
\overline{p}
-model of

if there is some poset

such that
P
is a p-morphic image of a rooted upset of

. We denote by

the class of
\overline{p}
-generated models over
\mathbf{K}
. We also denote by
M_{\mathbf{K}}(\varphi)=\{(\mathfrak{M},x)\in\mathcal{M}_{\overline{p}}(\mathbf{K}):(\mathfrak{M},x)\Vdash\varphi\}
.

Given two models
(\mathfrak{M},x)=(P,\leq,c,x)
and
(\mathfrak{N},y)=(Q,\leq,y,c^{\prime})
over
\overline{p}
, we say that they are

if there exists a relation
S\subseteq P\times Q
such that:

1.

(x,y)\in S
;

2.

Whenever
(a,b)\in S
then
c(a)=c^{\prime}(b)
;

3.

Whenever
(a,b)\in S
and
a\leq a^{\prime}
there is some
b\leq b^{\prime}
such that
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in S
;

4.

Whenever
(a,b)\in S
and
b\leq b^{\prime}
there is some
a\leq a^{\prime}
such that
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in S
.

Remark 2.1
.

1.

We will introduce a shorthand notation. Let
n\in\omega
be given. We will often identify the set
\{p_{1},...,p_{n}\}
with the natural numbers, and consider models over
\overline{p}
to be maps
c:P\to 2^{n}
; the image for a point
x\in P
will be called its
color
. We will consequently write
\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbf{K})
for the class of models over
n
. We will write
c(x)=\langle i_{1},...,i_{n}\rangle
where
i_{j}\in\{1,0\}
. We will further shorten tuples of the form
\langle 1,...,1,0,...,0\rangle
, consisting of a sequence of
1
followed by a sequence of
0
, by the notation
1^{n-k}0^{k}
.

2.

When
n
is given, we will often omit the order and the valuation, and write simply
(P,x)
for a model.

3.

Given an
n
, say that a model
(P,x)
over
2^{n}
is
n-generated
if
P
does not contain distinct bisimilar points. A well-known characterization of
n
-generation which we will use throughout is through
\alpha
and
\beta
-reductions (see
[
8
, Chapter 3]
): a point
x\in P
with a single immediate successor

must have a distinct color from it, and if
z,z^{\prime}
are two points, and
{\uparrow}z-\{z\}={\uparrow}z^{\prime}-\{z^{\prime}\}
, then they must also have a different color.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(P,x)
and
(P^{\prime},\leq^{\prime})
be two models over
n
. We say that a relation
S_{k}\subseteq P\times P^{\prime}
is a
k-bisimulation
based on
(x,y)
if there are relations
S_{k}\subseteq...\subseteq S_{j}\subseteq...\subseteq S_{0}
for each
0\leq j\leq k
, such that
xS_{k}y
and:

1.

Whenever
wS_{0}w^{\prime}
then
w\in c(p)\iff w^{\prime}\in c^{\prime}(p)
for each
p\in\overline{p}
;

2.

For each
j<k
, whenever
wS_{j+1}w^{\prime}
and
w\leq v
there is some
w^{\prime}\leq v^{\prime}
such that
vS_{j}v^{\prime}
.

3.

For each
j<k
, whenever
wS_{j+1}w^{\prime}
and
w^{\prime}\leq v^{\prime}
there is some
w\leq v
such that
vS_{j}v^{\prime}
.

We say that
(P,x)
is
k
-bisimilar with
(Q,y)
if there exists a
k
-bisimulation between them. We write
(P,x)\leq_{k}(Q,y)
if there is some
x\leq z
such that
(P,z)
is
k
-bisimilar to
(Q,y)
.

As described in
[
14
, Chapter 4.1]
,
n
-bisimulations can be characterized in game-theoretic terms: given a pair
(P,x)
and
(Q,y)
, Player I wins a
0
-bisimulation game if
c(x)\neq c(y)
and otherwise Player II wins; and Player I wins an
n+1
-bisimulation game if there is
z\in P
, such that Player II cannot choose any
w\in Q
and win an
n
-bisimulation game with
(P_{z},z)
and
(Q_{w},w)
.

The following is a basic property of
n
-bisimulation:

Proposition 2.3
.

For each pair of models
(P,x)
and
(Q,y)
over
\overline{p}
the following are equivalent:

1.

(P,x)
and
(Q,y)
are
\leq_{n}
-bisimilar;

2.

Whenever
d(\varphi(\overline{p}))\leq n
and
(Q,y)\Vdash\varphi
then
(P,x)\Vdash\varphi
.

The following is completely analogous to
[
14
, Chapter 4, Exercise 11]
, except we relativise to a specific subclass of posets, to capture different equivalence in different logics:

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\mathbf{K}
be a class of finite posets,
L=\mathrm{Log}(\mathbf{K})
and let
\overline{p}
be a set of propositional variables. Then we have:

1.

For each
\varphi(\overline{p}),\psi(\overline{p})
, we have:

L\vdash\varphi\leftrightarrow\psi\iff M_{\mathbf{K}}(\varphi)=M_{\mathbf{K}}(\psi).

2.

The following are equivalent:

(a)

S\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{\overline{p}}(\mathbf{K})
is the class of models from
\mathcal{M}_{\overline{p}}(\mathbf{K})
satisfying a formula
\psi
with
d(\psi)\leq n
;

(b)

S
is closed under
\leq_{n}
-bisimulation within
\mathcal{M}_{\overline{p}}(\mathbf{K})
.

2.2
Local tabularity, local finiteness and their uniform versions

To investigate these notions it will be convenient to work with Heyting algebras. For the connection between superintuitionistic logics and varieties of Heyting algebras, and the algebraic semantics therein, we refer to
[
10
, Chapter 8]
. Moreover, though the reader will not explicitly need any facts about the duality of finite Heyting algebras and finite posets, this will be implicit in the following results.

Given a formula
\varphi
, we write
H\vDash\varphi
to mean that the Heyting algebra validates
\varphi
; notations are extended to classes in the usual way. Given a class of Heyting algebras
\mathbf{K}
, we write:

\mathrm{Log}(\mathbf{K})=\{\varphi:\mathbf{K}\vDash\varphi\};

conversely, given a logic
L
, we write:

\mathrm{Alg}(L)=\{H\in\mathbf{HA}:H\vDash L\}.

We now turn to the basic concepts in our analysis.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
L\supseteq\mathsf{IPC}
be a superintuitionistic logic. We say that
L
is:

•

locally tabular
if given
\overline{p}=\{p_{1},...,p_{n}\}
propositional variables, there are only finitely many formulas over
\overline{p}
up to
L
-provability. We denote by
\mathsf{LTab}
the class of locally tabular logics;

•

n-uniform
if each formula
\varphi
is equivalent to a formula of implication depth
n
over
L
. We say that
L
is
uniformly locally tabular
if
L
is
n
-uniform for some
n
. We denote by
\mathsf{ULTab}_{n}
the class of
n
-uniform logics, and by
\mathsf{ULTab}
their union, the class of all uniformly locally tabular logics;

•

of
finite depth
if
L\supseteq\mathsf{BD}_{n}
for some
n
, where:

\mathsf{bd}_{0}=p_{0}\text{ and }\mathsf{bd}_{n+1}=p_{n+1}\vee(p_{n+1}\to\mathsf{bd}_{n}),

and

\mathsf{BD}_{n}=\mathsf{IPC}\oplus\mathsf{bd}_{n}.

We denote by
\mathsf{FinDep}
the class of logics of finite depth.

•

tabular
if there is a finite poset
P
such that
L=\mathrm{Log}(P)
. We write
\mathsf{Tab}
for the class of tabular logics.

All of these concepts correspond algebraically to specific properties:

Definition 2.6
.

Let
H
be an algebra. We say that
H
is
locally finite
if whenever
A\leq H
is a finitely generated algebra, then
A
is finite. We say that a class of algebras
\mathbf{K}
is
locally finite
if every algebra in
\mathbf{K}
is locally finite.

Recall that given
H
a Heyting algebra,
A
a Heyting subalgebra can be constructed as:

A=\{t(a_{1},...,a_{k}):a_{i}\in A,\ t(x_{1},...,x_{n})\text{ is a formula }\}.

Local finiteness then implies that for each
H
, there is an
n
such that formulas of depth at most
n
are sufficient. This is where we can impose uniformity:

Definition 2.7
.

Let
H
be an algebra. We say that
H
is
n
-
uniformly finite
, or simply
n-uniform
, if whenever
A\leq H
is a finitely generated algebra, then:

A=\{t(a_{1},...,a_{k}):a_{i}\in A,t(x_{1},...,x_{k})\text{ has implication depth $\leq n$}\}.

In this case we say that
A
is
n-uniformly generated
. We say that a class of algebras
\mathbf{K}
is
n
-
uniformly
finite if every algebra in
\mathbf{K}
is
n
-uniformly finite.

Definition 2.8
.

Given
x\in P
a poset, the
depth of
x
is the largest
n
such that there exists a chain
x=x_{1}\leq...\leq x_{n}
, and there is no such chain of size
n+1
, or
\infty
if such an
n
does not exist. If each point in
P
has a depth, we denote by
d(P)=\sup\{d(x):x\in P\}
.

The following is all well-known and provides the algebraic translation of the above properties:

Proposition 2.9
.

The following holds for
L
a superintuitionistic logic:

1.

L
is locally tabular if and only if
\mathsf{Alg}(L)
is locally finite.

2.

L
is
n
-uniform if and only if
\mathsf{Alg}(L)
is
n
-uniformly finite.

3.

L
is of finite depth
k
if and only if whenever
P\Vdash L
is a poset, then
P
is of depth at most
k
.

4.

L
is tabular if and only if
\mathsf{Alg}(L)
is finitely generated.

Moreover, the following inclusions hold between classes of Heyting algebras:

\mathsf{Tab}\subseteq\mathsf{FDep}\subseteq\mathsf{ULTab}\subseteq\mathsf{LTab}.

Proof.

The equivalences of (1-3) are easy to prove. We concentrate on the inclusions.
\mathsf{Tab}\subseteq\mathsf{FDep}
, since each finite poset
P
satisfies
\mathsf{bd}_{k}
where
k
is its height; the fact that
\mathsf{FDep}\subseteq\mathsf{ULTab}
was shown by Shehtman
[
26
]
, and amounts to the fact that for posets of finite depth, there is a uniform bound for how complex
n
-bisimulation can be; the fact that
\mathsf{ULTab}\subseteq\mathsf{LTab}
is obvious.
∎

Most converses of the inclusions of Proposition
2.9
are known not to hold. Namely, the logic
\mathsf{BD}_{2}
is known not to be tabular, even though it is of finite depth. The
\mathsf{LC}
axiomatized as:

\mathsf{LC}\coloneqq(p\rightarrow q)\vee(q\rightarrow p),

is known to be uniformly locally tabular (see
[
26
]
, see also
[
2
]
for a proof) but is not of finite depth. We will take a look at the last converse –
\mathsf{LTab}\subseteq\mathsf{ULTab}
in Section
3
.

2.3
Basic properties and criteria for uniform local tabularity

In order to establish
n
-uniform local tabularity we will make use of the following characterization in terms of
n
-bisimulations:

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
\mathbf{K}
be a class of finite posets. Then
L=\mathsf{Log}(\mathbf{K})
is
n
-uniformly locally tabular if and only if for each
\overline{p}
, whenever two models in
\mathcal{M}_{\overline{p}}(\mathbf{K})
are
n
-bisimilar, they are bisimilar.

Proof.

If
L
is
n
-uniformly locally tabular, then all formulas are equivalent to formulas of implication rank
\leq n
. Let
(\mathfrak{M},x),(\mathfrak{N},y)\in\mathcal{M}_{\overline{p}}(\mathbf{K})
be two models, and assume that they are
n
-bisimilar; then each formula
\varphi(\overline{p})
is satisfied in
(\mathfrak{M},x)
if and only if it is satisfied in
(\mathfrak{N},y)
, which means that
(\mathfrak{M},x)
is bisimilar to
(\mathfrak{N},y)
.

Conversely, assume that for each
\overline{p}
, in
\mathcal{M}_{\overline{p}}(\mathbf{K})

n
-bisimulation implies full bisimulation. Let
\varphi(\overline{p})
be an arbitrary formula. Consider
S=\{(P,x)\in\mathcal{M}_{\overline{p}}(\mathbf{K}):(P,x)\Vdash\varphi\}
. Now assume that
(P,x)\leq_{n}(Q,y)
, i.e.,
x\leq z
, and
(P_{z},z)
is
n
-bisimilar to
(Q,y)
. Since
(P_{z},z),(Q,y)\in\mathcal{M}_{\overline{p}}(\mathbf{K})
, and they are
n
-bisimilar, by assumption, they are fully bisimilar. Since
(P,x)\Vdash\varphi
, then
(P_{z},z)\Vdash\varphi
as well by persistence, and so by bisimilarity,
(Q,y)\Vdash\varphi
. This means that
S
is closed under
\leq_{n}
-bisimulation within
\mathcal{M}_{\overline{p}}(\mathbf{K})
, which means by Proposition
2.4
.(2) that
S
is the class of models from
\mathcal{M}_{\overline{p}}(\mathbf{K})
satisfying a formula
\psi
with
d(\psi)\leq n
. By Proposition
2.4
.(1), this means that
L\vdash\varphi\leftrightarrow\psi
, i.e.,
\varphi
is equivalent to a formula of implication depth
\leq n
.
∎

We close this section by noting an interesting property of
n
-uniform Heyting algebras. Let
\mathbf{HA}_{lf}
be the category of locally finite Heyting algebras with Heyting algebra homomorphisms, and let
\mathbf{HA}_{lf}^{n}
be the category of
n
-uniformly finite Heyting algebras with the same maps.

Proposition 2.11
.

The category
\mathbf{HA}_{lf}
is closed under subalgebras, homomorphic images and finite products. For each
n\in\omega
,
\mathbf{HA}_{lf}^{n}
is a variety.

Proof.

The fact about subalgebras is trivial. To see the fact about homomorphic images, say
H
is locally finite (n-uniformly finite), and
A
is a homomorphic image. Say that
B\leq A
is finitely generated. Pick finitely many generators
S
from
H
mapping to
A
surjectively; then
B=\langle S\rangle\leq H
will be finite, and the map onto
A
gives us
B
homomorphically onto
B
, hence
A
is finite (respectively,
n
-uniformly generated).

To see the fact about products, note that if
H_{0},H_{1}
are locally finite, and
(a_{0},b_{0}),...,(a_{n},b_{n})
where
a_{i}\in H_{0}
and
b_{i}\in H_{1}
, then

\langle(a_{0},b_{0}),...,(a_{n},b_{n})\rangle=\{t((a_{0},b_{0}),...,(a_{n},b_{n})):t\text{ of implication depth the maximum of that of $\langle A\rangle$ and $\langle B\rangle$.}

The same argument works for infinite products if we assume the algebras are
n
-uniformly finite, since then, rather than taking the maximum of
\langle A\rangle
and
\langle B\rangle
one can take that uniform bound.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

It should be noted that very little seems to be explicitly known about
\mathbf{HA}_{lf}
. For instance, it is not clear whether this category has coproducts. It is however clear that
\mathbf{HA}_{lf}
will not be closed under the existing coproducts of
\mathbf{HA}
(see
[
2
, Section 4.2]
for a description of such coproducts).

The above Proposition entails that there is the least
n
-uniform logic for each
n
. On the other hand,
\mathbf{HA}_{lf}
is not a variety – it contains every finite Heyting algebra, and hence the smallest variety containing it is
\mathbf{HA}
itself.

3
The hierarchy of locally tabular logics

In this section we will study the hierarchy of locally tabular logics – the
top
of the Kuznetsov hierarchy
[
6
]
. We will begin in Section
3.1
by recalling the easy fact that within tabular logics
n
-uniformity does not collapse. We then move in Section
3.2
to characterizing the
0,1,2
-uniformly locally tabular logics. In Section
3.3
we make some remarks on
3
-uniform logics of infinite depth, connecting them to recent studies of local tabularity in bi-intuitionistic logic
[
23
]
. We conclude in Section
3.4
with an example of a locally tabular but not uniformly locally tabular logic.

3.1
n-uniformity in tabular logics

Definition 3.1
.

Let
P
be a finite poset. The
degree of uniformity
of
P
is the minimum
n
such that, if two models over
\mathrm{Log}(P)
are
n
-bisimilar, they are fully bisimilar
1
1

1

The reader should be warned that this does not mean that
any model which is
n
-bisimilar
with
P
needs to be bisimilar with it. For example, the 2-fork and the 2-chain, as the models
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
, are
1
-bisimilar, though
P_{2}
is not always bisimilar with
P_{1}
. Nevertheless in the logic
\mathrm{Log}(P_{1})
, the degree of uniformity is
1
.
.

The problem of determining the degree of uniformity of arbitrary posets is of course decidable, and for arbitrary rooted
P
, by

This shows that

must look like a broken

, since it is not hard to show that every poset of

validates the formulas of the above logic.
∎

Lemma 3.20
.

Let
n>2
be arbitrary, and
k+1<n
. Then
M_{n}^{k}
is
k
-bisimilar, but not
(k+1)
-bisimilar with
N_{n}^{k-1}
.

Proof.

This is shown by induction. Note that for
k=1
, this is easy to verify, since
N_{n}^{1}
will be of depth
2
: looking at Figure
6
, we have that the two roots have the same color, and they see points with the same colors, so they will be
1
-bisimilar. However, assume that they were
2
-bisimilar; let
x
be the point in
M_{n}^{1}
with the label
1^{n-1}0
which is the successor of the root. Then by
2
-bisimilarity, there is some point in
N_{n}^{0}
which is
1
-bisimilar with it; but the only point which could be is the maximal point, which is a contradiction.

Now, assume that this holds for
k
. Consider
M_{n}^{k+1}
and
N_{n}^{k}
. Note that they have roots of the same color, labelled with
1^{n-k-2}0^{k+2}
, and indeed, if
z
is the immediate successor of
M_{n}^{k+1}
with the same color,
{\uparrow}z
is isomorphic to
N_{n}^{k}
. Thus if Player I picks the root of
N_{n}^{k}
or any of its successors, Player II has an obvious response by choosing the element inside of
{\uparrow}z
. Thus the only way that Player I can hope to win the game is to pick either the root of
M_{n}^{k}
, or the left successor of the root. If Player I picks the left successor of the root, say
y_{0}
, note that
{\uparrow}y_{0}\cong M_{n}^{k}
; on the other hand, the right successor of the root of
N_{n}^{k}
is isomorphic to
N_{n}^{k-1}
, so Player II wins by induction hypothesis. Finally, if Player I picks the root of
M_{n}^{k+1}
, Player II is forced to show that this is
k
-bisimilar to the root of
N_{n}^{k}
; by the arguments just shown Player I is in turn forced to play the root of
M_{n}^{k+1}
for all
k
many steps of the game, and loses when Player II finally picks the root, given the two roots have the same color. This shows that
M_{n}^{k+1}
is
k
-bisimilar to
N_{n}^{k}
as desired.

Now assume that
M_{n}^{k+1}
is
(k+1)
-bisimilar to
N_{n}^{k}
. Then Player I can pick the left successor of the root of
M_{n}^{k+1}
,

, labelled with

, and Player II is forced to respond with the right successor of

,

; as noted

and
{\uparrow}y_{1}\cong N_{n}^{k-1}
. Since by induction hypothesis these models are not

-bisimilar, Player II then loses, meaning that

and

are not

-bisimilar.

Hence we have the following:

Theorem 3.21
.

The logic
\mathsf{wPL}
is locally tabular but not uniformly locally tabular.

Proof.

All the models
M_{n}^{k}
for each
n,k
are based on frames which validate
\mathsf{wPL}
; hence by Corollary
2.10
,
\mathsf{wPL}
is not uniformly locally tabular. On the other hand, it is locally tabular by Theorem
3.9
.
∎

Indeed, the logic
\mathsf{ProHL}
– obtained by adding to
\mathsf{wPL}
the axiom of width
2
– is shown by the same proof to be locally tabular but not uniformly locally tabular. This logic is notable in that its finite and subdirectly irreducible members are all and exactly the finite projective Heyting algebras
[
3
]
. We will look more closely at this logic in the next section.

4
Pre-uniform local tabularity

The idea of “pre-
P
”-logics, for
P
a property, has been extensively used by several authors (e.g.,
[
12
,
24
,
19
]
) to characterize certain logical properties. In this final section we look at an example of this phenomenon for uniform local tabularity:

Definition 4.1
.

Let
L
be a superintuitionistic logic. We say that
L
is
pre-uniformly locally tabular
if
L
is not uniformly locally tabular but all of its extensions are so.

Let
S_{n}
be the result of adding a maximal point to the underlying frame of
N_{n}^{n-2}
. Recall that
\mathsf{bw}_{2}
is the axiom of
bounded width
, whereby frames for it can only contain antichains of at most
2
elements. Then first we show:

Proposition 4.2
.

The logic
\mathsf{Box}=\mathsf{wPL}\oplus\mathsf{bw}_{2}\oplus\neg(p\vee\neg\neg p)
is generated by
\{S_{n}:n\in\omega\}
.

Proof.

It is clear that
S_{n}
validate all the axioms of
\mathsf{Box}
. Conversely, note that any finite model of
\mathsf{Box}
will be a Boolean sum by local finiteness of
\mathsf{wPL}
, it will have width at most
2
by
\mathsf{bw}_{2}
, and a maximal element by the axiom
\neg p\vee\neg\neg p
; hence they can be obtained from
S_{n}
by a simple p-morphism.
∎

From this we obtain the following:

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\mathsf{Box}\subsetneq L^{\prime}
be a logic of infinite depth. Then there is some
n
such that posets in
L^{\prime}
have stacking depth
n
.

Proof.

If we assume that
L^{\prime}
does not have a bound on the size of
k
-stacks, then by taking p-morphisms we can obtain
S_{k}
for any
k
as a p-morphism of finite posets of
L^{\prime}
. So by Proposition
4.2
we have that
L^{\prime}=\mathsf{Box}
.
∎

More concretely, we obtain that the extensions of
\mathsf{Box}
are controlled by the depth, which can be infinite or finite, and the stack-depth, which can be finite or infinite. We show the following:

Lemma 4.4
.

Assume that
L^{\prime}\supseteq\mathsf{Box}
is a logic of infinite depth such that all its elements have at most stack-depth
k\geq 1
. Then
L^{\prime}
is
(k+1)
-uniformly tabular.

Proof.

We combine the arguments from Proposition
3.12
and Lemma
3.20
: we use
k+1
-bisimilarity to ensure that the maximal elements must be the same, and so must their immediate predecessors. Then assuming that there is a unique first immediate predecessor of the third layer, we use colorings to ensure the same holds in
Q
; otherwise, we use
j+1
-bisimulation to fix the elements, proceeding as in the above lemma; the stack depth limitation means that we never need a higher degree of
j
-bisimulation than the one we have. Once we hit an element
x\in P
which is unique in its depth, and obtain that it is fixed and bisimilar to a unique element in
Q
, we can proceed down inductively. We conclude by appealing to Corollary
2.10
.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

The logic
\mathsf{Box}
is pre-uniformly locally tabular.

Proof.

If
L^{\prime}\supseteq\mathsf{Box}
is of finite depth, then by Proposition
2.9
it is uniformly locally tabular. If
L^{\prime}\supsetneq\mathsf{Box}
has infinite depth, then by Proposition
4.3
there is a
k
such that its elements have stack-depth at most
k
, then by Lemma
4.4
,
L^{\prime}
is uniformly tabular.
∎

As noted before the problem of working with locally tabular intermediate logics presents a challenge since, as shown by Mardaev
[
20
]
, there are uncountably many such logics . By contrast, there are exactly three pretabular logics
[
12
]
, and every non-tabular logic is contained in one of them, and all of them are decidable. Together, this makes the problem of deciding, given an axiom
\varphi
, whether
\mathsf{IPC}\oplus\varphi
is tabular or not decidable. We thus have the following interesting problem:

Problem 2
.

Is uniform local tabularity decidable over
\mathsf{IPC}
?

Whilst we remain agnostic over this question, we finish this paper by showing that over a well-known intermediate logic – the system
\mathsf{KG}
of Kuznetsov-Gerciu
[
1
]
(for an overview of the system see
[
8
, Chapter 4]
). Such a system has a very broad range of extensions, and a rich semantics, which has made it useful for several purposes in the study of superintuitionistic logic (e.g.
[
5
]
). One of its key extensions is the logic
\mathsf{RN}=\mathrm{Log}(\mathsf{RN})
, the logic of the frame of the Rieger-Nishimura ladder. The following was shown in
[
8
, Theorem 4.6.5]
:

Theorem 4.6
.

A logic
\mathsf{KG}\subseteq L
is not locally tabular if and only if
L\subseteq\mathsf{RN.KC}=\mathsf{RN}\oplus(\neg p\vee\neg\neg p)
.

From this we obtain the following:

Theorem 4.7
.

A logic
\mathsf{KG}\subseteq L
is not pre-uniformly locally tabular if and only if
L\subseteq\mathsf{Box}
.

Proof.

The fact that
\mathsf{Box}
is an extension of
\mathsf{RN}
, and consequently of
\mathsf{KG}
, is easy to show by noting that
\mathsf{RN}
is generated by the upsets
{\uparrow}P_{i}^{\prime}
(see Figure
1
), where
P_{i}^{\prime}
is
P_{i}
with an additional point added on top; and one can construct the posets
S_{n}
by p-morphic images from these posets in a straightforward way.

If
\mathsf{KG}\subseteq L
, and
L
is not locally tabular, we thus have by Theorem
4.6
that
L\subseteq\mathsf{RN}\subseteq\mathsf{Box}
. Hence assume that
L
is locally tabular but not uniformly locally tabular. We can thus assume that
L
is not of finite depth. Since
L
is locally tabular, there must be an
n
which is maximal such that
{\uparrow}P_{n}^{\prime}
is a frame for the logic. Hence by
[
8
, Corollary 4.3.9]
, the finitely generated frames for
L
must be finite sums of p-morphic images of
{\uparrow}P_{k}^{\prime}
for
k\leq n
.

Now note that whenever we have a frame of the form
F_{0}+F_{1}
, where
F_{0}
is a finite generated subframe of
\mathsf{RN}
, by taking p-morphisms we can obtain
S_{k}
for some
k
. Suppose that there is a bound on the
S_{k}
which can be obtained in this way. Then by combining the arguments of Lemma
3.3
, and the arguments from Lemma
4.4
, we can prove the logic
L
will be uniformly locally tabular: if we let
\{C_{0},...,C_{m}\}
be the generated subframes of
P_{n}
, we obtain that one cannot sum
C_{i}
more than
k
many times before introducing a single point. A large enough uniformity bound (for example, if
C_{k}
is of depth
P_{j}
, something like
k\times(2j+1)
) will then be enough to ensure that the logic is uniformly locally tabular.

Thus if
L
is not uniformly locally tabular, we must have that there is no bound on
S_{n}
which can be obtained in this manner. We thus get that all
S_{n}
can be so obtained as p-morphisms, meaning that
L\subseteq\mathsf{Box}
, as desired.
∎

Corollary 4.8
.

Above
\mathsf{KG}
, uniform local tabularity is decidable: if
L=\mathsf{KG}\oplus\varphi
, it is decidable whether
L
is uniformly locally tabular.

Proof.

Since the logic
\mathsf{Box}
is finitely axiomatized and locally finite, it is decidable. Given
L
a decidable logic, one simply checks whether
L\subseteq\mathsf{Box}
, by checking whether
\mathsf{Box}
contains the axiom
\varphi
.
∎

5
Conclusions and Future Work

In this paper we provide a first systematic analysis of the concept of uniform local tabularity. Whilst such a concept coincides with local tabularity in the extensions of
\mathsf{S4}
, we have shown, confirming a conjecture of Shehthman, that the two concepts come apart in
\mathsf{IPC}
. By stratifying the concept, we analyse the case of
n
-uniform local tabularity for
n\in\omega
, showing that there is always the largest
n
-uniform logic in this sense; for
n\leq 2
, we give full axiomatizations, and provide an example illustrating the added complexities for
n=3
. We also show that the logic
\mathsf{Box}
, whose finite subdirectly irreducible models are exactly the finite projective Heyting algebras with a maximal element, is the unique pre-uniformly locally tabular logic in logics above the system
\mathsf{KG}
. A visual summary of these results in terms of the hierarchy of superintuitionistic logics is given in Figure

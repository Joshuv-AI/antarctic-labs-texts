On the Weyl function for complex Jacobi matrices
A. S. Mikhaylov, V. S. Mikhaylov

Abstract




Abstract.
We derive a new representation for the
Weyl function associated with the complex Jacobi matrix in the
finite and semi-infinite cases. In our approach we exploit
connections to the discrete-time dynamical system associated with
these matrices.

1.
Introduction.

For a given sequence of complex numbers
\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots\}
,

\{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots\}
,
a_{i}\not=0
, such that
\sup_{n\geqslant 1}\{|a_{n}|,|b_{n}|\}\leqslant B
for some
B>0

(1.1)

A=\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}&a_{1}&0&0&0&\ldots\\
a_{1}&b_{2}&a_{2}&0&0&\ldots\\
0&a_{2}&b_{3}&a_{3}&0&\ldots\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\end{pmatrix}.

For
N\in\mathbb{N}
, by
A^{N}
we denote the
N\times N
Jacobi
matrix which is a block of (
1.1
) consisting of the
intersection of first
N
columns with first
N
rows of
A
.

We consider the operator
H
corresponding to the semi-infinite
Jacobi matrix
A
, defined on
l^{2}(\mathbb{N})\ni\psi=(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\ldots)
, given by the formula

(1.2)

\begin{cases}(Hu)_{n}&=a_{n-1}\psi_{n-1}+b_{n}\psi_{n}+a_{n}\psi_{n+1},\quad n\geqslant 2,\\
(Hu)_{1}&=b_{1}\psi_{1}+a_{1}\psi_{2},\quad n=1.\end{cases}

With
A^{N}
we associate the operator
H^{N}
. The Weyl

functions
for
H
and

are defined (see
[
2
]
) by

(1.3)

\displaystyle m(\lambda)=\left((H-\lambda)^{-1}e_{1},e_{1}\right),

(1.4)

\displaystyle m^{N}(\lambda)=\left((H^{N}-\lambda)^{-1}e_{1},e_{1}\right),

e_{n}=(0,,\ldots,1,0,\ldots)^{T}
with
1
on the

th place.

Note that in the case of a real matrix
A
, according to the
spectral theorem, this definition is equivalent to the following:

(1.5)

m(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{d\rho(z)}{z-\lambda},

where
d\rho=d\left(E_{H}e_{1},e_{1}\right)
and
E_{H}
is the
(projection-valued) spectral measure of the self-adjoint operator

H
.

Associated with the matrix
A
and additional parameter

a_{0}\not=0
is a dynamical system with discrete time:

(1.6)

\begin{cases}u_{n,t+1}+u_{n,t-1}-a_{n}u_{n+1,t}-a_{n-1}u_{n-1,t}-b_{n}u_{n,t}=0,\quad n,t\in\mathbb{N},\\
u_{n,-1}=u_{n,0}=0,\quad n\in\mathbb{N},\\
u_{0,t}=f_{t},\quad t\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},\end{cases}

which is a natural analog of a dynamical systems governed by a
wave equation on a semi-axis
[
1
,
4
]
. By an analogy with
continuous problems
[
3
]
, we treat the complex sequence

as a

. The solution to
(
1.6

. We consider also the
dynamical system associated with the finite matrix

:

(1.7)

\begin{cases}v_{n,t+1}+v_{n,t-1}-a_{n}v_{n+1,t}-a_{n-1}v_{n-1,t}-b_{n}v_{n,t}=0,\,\,t\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\,\,n\in 1,\ldots,N,\\
v_{n,\,-1}=v_{n,\,0}=0,\quad n=1,2,\ldots,N+1,\\
v_{0,\,t}=f_{t},\quad v_{N+1,\,t}=0,\quad t\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},\end{cases}

which is a natural analog of a dynamical systems governed by a

1.7

.

Having fixed

\displaystyle\left(R^{T}f\right)_{t}:=u^{f}_{1,t},\quad t=1,\ldots,T,

(1.9)

\displaystyle\left(R^{T}_{N}f\right)_{t}:=v^{f}_{1,t},\quad t=1,\ldots,T.

With the matrix

\left\{q_{k}(0)\right\}

not an element of

.

In the case of finite Jacobi matrix by

For the case of semi-infinite

[

(

. Note that

For two solution

) we introduce the

by the rule

W(u,v)[n]:=a_{n}\left(u_{n}v_{n+1}-u_{n+1}v_{n}\right).

Simple observation (as in the real case) is that

does not

. Indeed, for two solutions

\displaystyle a_{n-1}u_{n-1}+b_{n}u_{n}+a_{n}u_{n+1}=zu_{n},

\displaystyle a_{n-1}v_{n-1}+b_{n}v_{n}+a_{n}v_{n+1}=zv_{n},

we multiply the first line by

and the second line by
u_{n}

and subtract second from the first, to get

a_{n-1}(u_{n-1}v_{n}-v_{n-1}u_{n})=a_{n}(u_{n}v_{n+1}-v_{n}u_{n+1}),

thus we have that

W(u,v):=W(u,v)[n],\quad n=1,2,\ldots.

In what follows we need to define
\phi^{+}_{0}
, to do so we formally
set
a_{0}=1
, then
\phi^{+}_{0}
can be determined (cf. second line in
(
1.2
)) from the equality

a_{0}\phi^{+}_{0}+b_{1}\phi^{+}_{1}+a_{1}\phi^{+}_{2}=z\phi^{+}_{1}.

As such we have that
W(u,v)=W(u,v)[0]
.

The following lemma is a complex analog of
[
5
, Propositions 2.2,
2.3]
.

Lemma 1
.

The Weyl functions for
H
and
H^{N}
have the representation

(2.2)

m(z)=-\frac{\phi^{+}_{1}(z)}{\phi^{+}_{0}(z)}.

Proof.

The Green function (the kernel of resolvent) is defined by

G_{m,n}(z)=\left((H-z)^{-1}e_{m},e_{n}\right).

Then it is straightforward to check that

G_{m,n}(z)=\frac{1}{W(p,\phi^{+})}p_{\operatorname{min}\{m,n\}}(z)\phi^{+}_{\operatorname{max}\{m,n\}},

where the choice of
p,

\phi^{+}
guarantees the equation holds at

n=1
and
n=N
in finite case and
n=1
and that

\left\{\sum_{n}G_{m,n}(z)f_{n}\right\}_{m=1}^{\infty}\in l_{2}
for any

f\in l_{2}
with finite number of nonzero elements.

Then by the definition (
1.3
), (
1.4
),
choice of
a_{0}=1
and conditions on
p
at
n=0,1
, we have that
in finite and semi-infinite cases

m(z)=G_{1,1}(z)=\frac{p_{1}(z)\phi^{+}_{1}(z)}{a_{0}((p_{0}(z)\phi^{+}_{1}(z)-p_{1}(z)\phi^{+}_{0}(z)))}=-\frac{\phi^{+}_{1}(z)}{\phi^{+}_{0}(z)},

which completes the proof.
∎

4.
Representation of Weyl functions.

We need to introduce the appropriate Fourier transformation to

4.1.
Discrete Fourier transformation.

Consider two solutions of the equation

(4.1)

\psi_{n-1}+\psi_{n+1}=\lambda\psi_{n},

satisfying the initial conditions:

P_{0}=0,\,\,P_{1}=1,\quad Q_{0}=-1,\,\,Q_{1}=0.

Clearly, one has:

Q_{n+1}(\lambda)=P_{n}(\lambda).

We are looking for the unique

-solution

:

S_{n}(\lambda)=Q_{n}(\lambda)+m_{0}(\lambda)P_{n}(\lambda),

where
m_{0}
is the Weyl function of
H_{0}
(the special case of

with
a_{n}\equiv 1
,

).
Consider the new variable

related to
\lambda
by the
equalities

(4.2)

\lambda=z+\frac{1}{z},\quad z=\frac{\lambda-\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-4}}{2}=\frac{\lambda-i\sqrt{4-\lambda^{2}}}{2},

z\in\mathbb{D}\cap\mathbb{C}_{-}
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
, where

\mathbb{D}=\{z\,|\,|z|\leqslant 1\}
. It is known
[
9
]

that

m_{0}(\lambda)=-z.

Equation (
4.1
) has two solutions,

and

.
Since

and
S_{1}(\lambda)=m_{0}(\lambda)=-z
, we
get

S_{n}(\lambda)=-z^{n}.

Let

be the characteristic function of the
interval
(a,b)
. The spectral measure of the unperturbed operator

H_{0}
corresponding to (

d\rho(\lambda)=\chi_{(-2,2)}(\lambda)\frac{\sqrt{4-\lambda^{2}}}{2\pi}\,d\lambda.

Then the Fourier transformation

acts by the rule: for

F(v)(\lambda)=V(\lambda)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{n}(\lambda)v_{n}.

The inverse transform is given by:

v_{n}=\int_{-2}^{2}V(\lambda)S_{n}(\lambda)\,d\rho(\lambda).

\left(r_{0},r_{1},r_{2},\ldots\right)

\left(r_{0}^{N},r_{1}^{n},r_{2}^{N},\ldots\right)

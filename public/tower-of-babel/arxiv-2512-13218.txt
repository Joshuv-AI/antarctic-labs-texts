Abstract
. We introduce the notion of AIR tilting subcategories of extended hearts of t t -structures on a triangulated category associated with silting subcategories. This notion extends τ [ d ] \tau_{[d]} -tilting pairs of extended finitely generated modules over finite-dimensional algebras to a broader framework, encompassing extended large modules over unitary rings as well as truncated subcategories of finite-dimensional derived categories of proper non-positive differential graded algebras. Within this framework, we establish a bijection between AIR tilting subcategories and silting subcategories. We further define quasi-tilting and tilting subcategories of extended hearts, generalizing the corresponding notions in module categories, and investigate their fundamental properties and the relationships among these three tilting-related concepts.

16E35, 18E40, 18G80

Introduction

Tilting theory has long been one of the central tools in representation theory. It provides powerful methods for constructing equivalences between categories, most notably between module categories and derived categories. The subject originated in the late 1970s from the representation theory of finite-dimensional algebras. Motivated by the study of reflection functors
[
10
,
8
]
, the notion of (classical) tilting modules over finite-dimensional algebras was introduced and developed in
[
12
,
19
]
. This concept was subsequently generalized to modules of finite projective dimension, now referred to as generalized tilting modules
[
25
]
. The classical and generalized notions were then extended to module categories over arbitrary unitary rings, significantly enlarging the scope of the theory to include modules that are not necessarily finitely generated
[
13
,
16
,
5
,
9
]
.

More recently, in the context of cluster algebras, the theory progressed further with the introduction of support
\tau
-tilting modules for finite-dimensional algebras by Adachi, Iyama and Reiten
[
2
]
. These have been shown to correspond bijectively to two-term silting complexes and to functorially finite torsion pairs
[
2
,
17
]
. This circle of ideas has since been extended to arbitrary rings
[
6
]
, where the appropriate analogues are silting modules. Silting complexes themselves arise naturally in the classification of
t
-structures in derived categories
[
22
,
23
]
. To address the correspondence problem for silting complexes of arbitrary length, the category of
d
-extended finitely generated modules over a finite-dimensional algebra was introduced in
[
18
,
33
]
. Within this framework,
\tau_{[d]}
-tilting pairs were shown to correspond bijectively to
(d+1)
-term silting complexes
[
33
]
.

In this paper, for any positive integer
d
, we introduce the notion of AIR tilting subcategories in the
d
-extended hearts of
t
-structures associated with silting subcategories. This construction extends the aforementioned bijection to a significantly more general setting (see
Theorem

2.8
). Our approach provides a unified treatment that simultaneously encompasses both the extended finitely generated module categories over finite-dimensional algebras and the extended large module categories over arbitrary unitary rings, as well as the truncated subcategories of finite-dimensional derived categories of proper non-positive differential graded algebras. See
Section

5
for more details.

Within this unified framework, we also introduce the notion of quasi-tilting subcategories of extended hearts, generalizing quasi-tilting modules. The latter were introduced in
[
14
]
to realize one half of the equivalences appearing in Brenner–Butler’s classical tilting theorem, and were later extended to infinitely generated modules in
[
6
]
. We further investigate the structural and homological properties of the associated
d
-factor subcategories (see
Theorem

3.3
), and show that every AIR tilting subcategory is quasi-tilting. For additional relationships between quasi-tilting modules and silting modules in the classical setting, we refer the reader to
[
6
,
31
]
.

Finally, we define and study tilting subcategories of extended hearts. These can be viewed both as a generalization of classical tilting modules, sharing, for instance, the key property that the torsion classes they induce contain all injective objects (see
Corollary

4.10
), and as a generalization of tilting modules of arbitrary finite projective dimension (see
Example

5.7
). We establish several characterizations of tilting subcategories of extended hearts, with particular emphasis on their relationships with AIR tilting subcategories and quasi-tilting subcategories. See
Proposition

4.5
and
Theorem

4.9
for further details.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 1 recalls the basic definitions and properties of silting subcategories and, in particular, provides a criterion for when a presilting subcategory is silting, which will be used later. In Section 2, we introduce extended hearts of
t
-structures associated with silting subcategories and study the properties of the torsion classes induced by silting subcategories within this framework. Building on these results, we define AIR tilting subcategories of extended hearts and establish their correspondence with silting subcategories. Section 3 is devoted to the notion of quasi-tilting subcategories of extended hearts, where we investigate the fundamental properties of their associated
d
-factor subcategories; these results will be used in the next section. In Section 4, we introduce tilting subcategories of extended hearts and explore their relationships with both AIR tilting and quasi-tilting subcategories. Finally, Section 5 applies the preceding notions and results to two settings, encompassing as special cases both the extended finitely generated module category over a finite-dimensional algebra and the extended large module category over a unitary ring.

1.
Silting subcategories

Throughout the paper, the shift functor in a triangulated category is denoted by
[1]
. For any subcategory
\mathcal{X}
of an additive category
\mathcal{C}
, we denote by
\operatorname{add}\mathcal{X}
the
additive closure
of
\mathcal{X}
in
\mathcal{C}
, that is, the full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
consisting of direct summands of finite direct sums of objects of
\mathcal{X}
. When we say that
\mathcal{X}
is a subcategory of an additive category
\mathcal{C}
, denoted by
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
, we always assume that
\mathcal{X}
is full and satisfies
\mathcal{X}=\operatorname{add}\mathcal{X}
. We write
X\in\mathcal{C}
to indicate that
X
is an object of
\mathcal{C}
. For any
X,Y\in\mathcal{C}
, we simply denote
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)
by
\operatorname{Hom}(X,Y)
if no confusion can arise.

For any
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
, we write
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})=0
to mean that
\operatorname{Hom}(X,Y)=0
for all
X\in\mathcal{X}
and
Y\in\mathcal{Y}
. The analogous conventions apply to the notations
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{X},Y)=0
and
\operatorname{Hom}(X,\mathcal{Y})=0
. A morphism
f\in\operatorname{Hom}(X,Y)
is called a
right
\mathcal{X}
-approximation
of
Y
, if
X\in\mathcal{X}
and every morphism
g\in\operatorname{Hom}(X^{\prime},Y)
with
X^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}
factors through
f
. We say that
\mathcal{X}
is a contravariantly finite subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
if every object of
\mathcal{C}
admits a right
\mathcal{X}
-approximation.

For any subcategory
\mathcal{X}
of a triangulated category
\mathcal{C}
, we consider the following full subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
:

\mathcal{C}^{\leq 0}_{\mathcal{X}}:=\{Y\in\mathcal{C}\mid\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{X},Y[i])=0\text{ for all $i>0$}\},

\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0}_{\mathcal{X}}:=\{Y\in\mathcal{C}\mid\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{X},Y[i])=0\text{ for all $i<0$}\}.

For any two subcategories
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
of
\mathcal{C}
, we denote by
\mathcal{X}\ast\mathcal{Y}
the full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
consisting of all objects
Z
such that there exists a triangle

X\to Z\to Y\to X[1],

in
\mathcal{C}
with
X\in\mathcal{X}
and
Y\in\mathcal{Y}
. A subcategory
\mathcal{X}
of
\mathcal{C}
is called
closed under extensions
if
\mathcal{X}*\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
.

Throughout the remainder of this section, let
\mathcal{C}
be an arbitrary triangulated category. We recall the following notion from
[
22
,
4
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
.

(1)

We call
\mathcal{S}

presilting
if

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S}[i])=0

for all
i>0
.

(2)

We call
\mathcal{S}

silting
if it is presilting and the smallest triangulated subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
containing
\mathcal{S}
is
\mathcal{C}
itself.

For two silting subcategories
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{C}
, we write
\mathcal{S}\geq\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
if
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S}^{\prime}[i])=0
for any
i>0
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a silting subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
. For any integers
p\leq q
, we denote

\mathcal{S}^{[p,q]}:=\mathcal{S}[p]*\mathcal{S}[p+1]*\cdots*\mathcal{S}[q].

By
[
20
, Proposition 2.7]
,
\mathcal{S}^{[p,q]}
is closed under direct summands. For any integer
d\geq 0
, an object (resp. subcategory) of
\mathcal{S}^{[0,d]}
is called
(d+1)
-term
with respect to
\mathcal{S}
. We collect some basic properties of a silting subcategory.

Lemma 1.2

(
[
4
, Proposition 2.23]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a silting subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
. Then

\mathcal{C}=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{S}^{[-n,n]}.

Lemma 1.3

(
[
3
, Lemma 3.7]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
be silting subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
and let
d\geq 0
be an integer. Then
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{\prime[0,d]}
if and only if
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{[-d,0]}
.

Lemma 1.4

(
[
4
, Proposition 2.14 and Theorem 2.11]
)
.

For any two silting subcategories
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{C}
, if
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\leq 0}=\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}^{\leq 0}
, then
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
.

A
thick
subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
is a triangulated subcategory closed under direct summands.

Remark 1.5
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a presilting subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
. Then
\mathcal{S}
is silting if and only if the smallest thick subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
containing
\mathcal{S}
is
\mathcal{C}
itself. Indeed, by
Lemma

1.2
,
\mathcal{C}
is the union of
\mathcal{S}^{[-n,n]}
,
n\geq 0
, each of which is closed under direct summands.

The next lemma is a slight modification of
[
3
, Lemma 4.2]
, whose proof proceeds in essentially the same way.

Lemma 1.6
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a contravariantly finite presilting subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
. Then for any integer
l>0
, we have

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\leq 0}=\mathcal{S}^{[0,l-1]}*\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\leq 0}[l].

Below, we provide a necessary and sufficient condition for a presilting subcategory to be silting, which will be used in the next section. We also refer the reader to
[
30
, Proposition 4.2]
and
[
6
, Proposition 4.2]
for a similar criterion in the case
\mathcal{C}=K^{b}(\operatorname{Proj}R)
the homotopy category of bounded complexes of projective modules over a unitary ring
R
.

Proposition 1.7
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a silting subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
, and
d
be a positive integer. A contravariantly finite presilting subcategory
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathcal{C}
that is
(d+1)
-term with respect to
\mathcal{P}
is silting if and only if
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\leq 0}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{P}}^{\leq 0}
.

Proof.

The “only if” part: Since
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{P}^{[0,d]}
, we have
\mathcal{S}\leq\mathcal{P}
. Hence, it follows directly from
[
4
, Proposition 2.14]
that
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\leq 0}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{P}}^{\leq 0}
.

The “if” part: Since
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{P}^{[0,d]}
, we have
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{P}[i])=0
for any
i\geq d+1
. Thus,
\mathcal{P}[d]\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\leq 0}
. By
Lemma

1.6
, we have

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\leq 0}\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{[0,d]}*\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\leq 0}[d+1].

Therefore, for any
P\in\mathcal{P}
, there exists a triangle

X\to P[d]\to Y\to X[1],

where
X\in\mathcal{S}^{[0,d]}
and
Y\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\leq 0}[d+1]
. Then, by
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\leq 0}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{P}}^{\leq 0}
, we have
Y\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{P}}^{\leq 0}[d+1]
. So
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{P}[d],Y)=0
, which, together with the above triangle, implies that
P[d]
is a direct summand of
X
. Consequently, the smallest thick subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
containing
\mathcal{S}
includes
\mathcal{P}[d]
. Therefore, it includes
\mathcal{P}
, and therefore equals
\mathcal{C}
. Thus, by
Remark

1.5
,
\mathcal{S}
is silting.
∎

2.
AIR tilting subcategories of extended hearts

Throughout the rest of the paper, let
\mathcal{D}
be a triangulated category admitting a presilting subcategory
\mathcal{P}
satisfying the following conditions.

(P1)

There exists an integer
m<0
such that
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}[i])=0
for all
i<m
.

(P2)

(\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}_{\mathcal{P}},\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}_{\mathcal{P}})
is a
t
-structure on
\mathcal{D}
.

See
Section

5
for motivating examples. For simplicity, we write
\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}:=\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}_{\mathcal{P}}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}:=\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}_{\mathcal{P}}
. We then consider the following subcategories of
\mathcal{D}
:

•

\mathcal{D}^{\leq q}:=\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}[-q]
and
\mathcal{D}^{\geq q}:=\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}[-q]
for any integer
q
;

•

\mathcal{D}^{[p,q]}:=\mathcal{D}^{\leq q}\cap\mathcal{D}^{\geq p}
for any integers
p\leq q
;

•

\mathcal{H}:=\mathcal{D}^{[0,0]}=\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}\cap\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}
the heart of the
t
-structure
(\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0})
; and

•

\mathcal{K}=\operatorname{thick}(\mathcal{P})
the smallest thick subcategory of
\mathcal{D}
containing
\mathcal{P}
.

For any integer
p
, the inclusions
\mathcal{D}^{\leq p}\to\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\geq p}\to\mathcal{D}
admit right and left adjoints, respectively, denoted by

\sigma^{\leq p}:\mathcal{D}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\leq p}\text{ and }\sigma^{\geq p}:\mathcal{D}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\geq p},

which are called
truncations
. For any integers
p\leq q
, we define the functor

H^{[p,q]}:=\sigma^{\geq p}\circ\sigma^{\leq q}\simeq\sigma^{\leq q}\circ\sigma^{\geq p}:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}^{[p,q]}.

For any integer
q
, we define the functor

H^{q}:=H^{[q,q]}[q]:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{H}.

By definition, the presilting subcategory
\mathcal{P}
is in fact a silting subcategory of
\mathcal{K}
. We recall a well-known correspondence between silting subcategories of
\mathcal{K}
and
t
-structures on
\mathcal{D}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a silting subcategory of
\mathcal{K}
that is contravariantly finite in
\mathcal{D}
. Then
(\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\geq 0})
is a
t
-structure on
\mathcal{D}
. Moreover, the following statements hold.

(1)

If two silting subcategories
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{K}
satisfy
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\leq 0}=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}^{\leq 0}
, then
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
.

(2)

If
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{P}^{[0,d]}
for some integer
d>0
, then
\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}[d]\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\leq 0}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}
.

Proof.

The first statement follows from
[
3
, Lemma 5.1]
. Statement (1) follows from
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\leq 0}\cap\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\leq 0}
and applying
Lemma

1.4
to the case
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{K}
. For the assertion (2), since
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{P}^{[0,d]}
, we have
\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}[d]\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\leq 0}
. On the other hand, by
Lemma

1.3
,
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{[-d,0]}
. It follows that
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\leq 0}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}
.
∎

From now on, let
d
be a fixed positive integer, and define the
d
-extended heart
of
(\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0})
as the full subcategory

d\text{-}\mathcal{H}:=\mathcal{D}^{[-d+1,0]}=\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}\cap\mathcal{D}^{\geq-d+1}=\mathcal{H}[d-1]*\cdots*\mathcal{H}[1]*\mathcal{H}.

Since both
\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\geq-d+1}
are closed under extensions and direct summands,
d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
is closed under them as well. Consequently,
d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
is an extriangulated category in the sense of
[
27
]
. The biadditive functor
\mathbb{E}
is defined by

\mathbb{E}(M,N):=\operatorname{Hom}(M,N[1])

for
M,N\in d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
. Given a triangle in
\mathcal{D}

N\xrightarrow{f}L\xrightarrow{g}M\xrightarrow{\delta}N[1]

with
N,L,M\in d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
, we define

N\xrightarrow{f}L\xrightarrow{g}M

to be an
extriangle
in
d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
. Unless otherwise stated, every extriangle in this paper is taken in
d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
. For further details on the theory of extriangulated categories, we refer the reader to
[
27
]
.

In this section, we introduce the notion of AIR tilting subcategories of
d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
and establish their correspondence with silting subcategories of
\mathcal{K}
. An object
S
of
\mathcal{K}
is called
(d+1)
-term
if
S\in\mathcal{P}^{[0,d]}
. Similarly, a subcategory
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathcal{K}
is
(d+1)
-term if
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{P}^{[0,d]}
.

Definition 2.2
.

A
\mathcal{P}
-presentation
of an object
M\in d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
is a
(d+1)
-term object
S
of
\mathcal{K}
satisfying
H^{[-d+1,0]}(S)\cong M
.

A
\mathcal{P}
-presentation
of a subcategory
\mathcal{M}\subseteq d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
is a
(d+1)
-term subcategory
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathcal{K}
satisfying
\operatorname{add}H^{[-d+1,0]}(\mathcal{S})=\mathcal{M}
, with

H^{[-d+1,0]}(\mathcal{S}):=\{H^{[-d+1,0]}(S)\mid S\in\mathcal{S}\}.

By definition, any
(d+1)
-term subcategory
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathcal{K}
is a
\mathcal{P}
-presentation of a subcategory
\operatorname{add}H^{[-d+1,0]}(\mathcal{S})
of
d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
. Conversely, assuming that
\mathcal{P}
is contravariantly finite in
\mathcal{D}
, any subcategory of
d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
admits a
\mathcal{P}
-presentation.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{P}
is contravariantly finite in
\mathcal{D}
. Then every object
M\in d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
admits a
\mathcal{P}
-presentation, and consequently every subcategory
\mathcal{M}\subseteq d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
does as well.

Proof.

Let
M\in d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
. Since
\mathcal{P}
is contravariantly finite in
\mathcal{D}
, there exist triangles

M_{i+1}\to P_{i}\xrightarrow{g_{i}}M_{i}\xrightarrow{h_{i}}M_{i+1}[1],\ 0\leq i\leq d,

where
M_{0}=M
and each
g_{i}
is a right
\mathcal{P}
-approximation of
M_{i}
. Applying
\operatorname{Hom}(P,-)
for
P\in\mathcal{P}
to these triangles, we see that all
M_{i}
(
0\leq i\leq d
) lie in
\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}
. Extend the composition
h:=h_{d}[d]\circ\cdots\circ h_{1}[1]\circ h_{0}:M\to M_{d+1}[d+1]
to a triangle in
\mathcal{D}
:

M_{d+1}[d]\to S\to M\xrightarrow{h}M_{d+1}[d+1].

By the octahedral axiom, we have that

S\in P_{0}*P_{1}[1]*\cdots*P_{d}[d]\in\mathcal{P}^{[0,d]}.

Since
M_{d+1}[d]\in\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}[d]=\mathcal{D}^{\leq-d}
and
M\in d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
, we have
M\cong H^{[-d+1,0]}(S)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a subcategory of
d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
. For any object
M\in\mathcal{M}
, take a
\mathcal{P}
-presentation
S_{M}
of
M
. The full subcategory
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathcal{K}
consisting of
S_{M}
for all
M\in\mathcal{M}
is a
\mathcal{P}
-presentation of
\mathcal{M}
; indeed, it is enough to show that
\mathcal{S}=\operatorname{add}\mathcal{S}
. This follows directly from
\mathcal{M}=\operatorname{add}\mathcal{M}
, since the functor
H^{[-d+1,0]}
preserves direct sums and direct summands.
∎

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a
(d+1)
-term subcategory of
\mathcal{K}
. We define two associated full subcategories of
d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
as follows:

\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{S}):=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\leq 0}\cap d\text{-}\mathcal{H}=\{X\in d\text{-}\mathcal{H}\mid\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{S},X[i])=0,\ \forall i>0\},

\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{S}):=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\geq 0}[-1]\cap d\text{-}\mathcal{H}=\{X\in d\text{-}\mathcal{H}\mid\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{S},X[i])=0,\ \forall i\leq 0\}.

We recall the following notion of
s
-torsion pairs from
[
1
]
.

Definition 2.4

(
[
1
, Definition 3.1]
)
.

A pair of subcategories

of

is called an

, with
\mathcal{T}

(1)

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})=0=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F}[-1])

(2)

For any

, there is an extriangle

T\to X\to F,

where
T\in\mathcal{T}
and
F\in\mathcal{F}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a
(d+1)
-term silting subcategory of
\mathcal{K}
that is contravariantly finite in
\mathcal{D}
. Then
(\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{S}),\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{S}))
is an
s
-torsion pair in
d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
. Moreover, if two
(d+1)
-term silting subcategories
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{K}
that are contravariantly finite in
\mathcal{D}
satisfy
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{S})=\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
, then
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

By
Proposition

2.1
,
(\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{S}}^{\geq 0})
is a
t

2.12
.
∎

4.
Tilting subcategories of extended hearts

Throughout this section, we assume that
\mathcal{P}
is contravariantly finite in
\mathcal{D}
and that
\mathcal{P}\subseteq d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
. By
Remark

2.13
, the extriangulated category
d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
has enough projectives
\mathcal{P}
. Thus, following
[
24
, Section 5.1]
, one can define higher
\mathbb{E}
-extensions in
d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
as follows. For any object
M\in d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
, there exist extriangles

(4.1)

Z_{i}\xrightarrow{f_{i}}P_{i-1}\xrightarrow{g_{i-1}}Z_{i-1},\ i>0,

with
Z_{0}=M
and
P_{i-1}\in\mathcal{P}
. For any
N\in d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
, define

\mathbb{E}^{i}(M,N):=\mathbb{E}(Z_{i-1},N).

Alternatively, the higher
\mathbb{E}
-extensions can be defined directly as

\mathbb{E}^{i}(M,N):=\operatorname{Hom}(M,N[i]),

for any
M,N\in d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
and
i>0
. Indeed, applying
\operatorname{Hom}(-,N)
to the extriangles in (
4.1
), we obtain an exact sequence for any
i>0
and
j>0
:

\operatorname{Hom}(P_{i-1},N[j])\to\operatorname{Hom}(Z_{i},N[j])\to\operatorname{Hom}(Z_{i-1},N[j+1])\to\operatorname{Hom}(P_{i-1},N[j+1]),

where the first and last terms vanish. Hence, there are functorial isomorphisms

\operatorname{Hom}(Z_{i},N[j])\cong\operatorname{Hom}(Z_{i-1},N[j+1]),\ i>0,\ j>0,

which imply that

\mathbb{E}^{i}(M,N)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(M,N[i])
functorially in both variables.

An object
M\in d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
is said to have
projective dimension at most
m
if there exist extriangles of the form (
4.1
) such that
Z_{m}\in\mathcal{P}
. Then
M
has projective dimension at most
m
if and only if
\mathbb{E}^{j}(M,N)=0
for all
j>m
and all
N\in d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
; see, e.g.,
[
34
, Lemma 3]
.

Definition 4.1
.

A subcategory
\mathcal{M}
of
d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
is called
tilting
if the following hold.

(T1)

Every object of
\mathcal{M}
has projective dimension at most
d
.

(T2)

\mathbb{E}^{i}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M})=0
for all
i>0
.

(T3)

For any
P\in\mathcal{P}
, there exist extriangles

Y_{i-1}\to M_{i-1}\to Y_{i},\ 1\leq i\leq r,

for some
r
, with
Y_{0}=P
and
M_{0},\dots,M_{r-1},Y_{r}\in\mathcal{M}
.

We note that a notion of tilting objects in the extended heart also appears in the recent work
[
7
]
; however, the tilting objects considered there are required to have projective dimension at most one.

Analogously to the case of module categories, a subcategory of
d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
with finite projective dimension can be regarded as a subcategory of
\mathcal{K}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a subcategory of
d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
and
m
a positive integer. Then
\mathcal{M}
has projective dimension at most
m
if and only if
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{P}^{[0,m]}
.

Proof.

If every object in
\mathcal{M}
has projective dimension at most
m
, then by definition, any object
M\in\mathcal{M}
admits extriangles as in (
4.1
), which imply that

\mathcal{M}\subseteq(\operatorname{add}P_{0})*(\operatorname{add}P_{1}[1])*\cdots*(\operatorname{add}P_{m-1}[m-1])*(\operatorname{add}Z_{m}[m])\subseteq\mathcal{P}^{[0,m]}.

Conversely, suppose that
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{P}^{[0,m]}
. Then for any object
M\in\mathcal{M}
, there exists a triangle

M^{\prime}\to P^{\prime}\to M\to M^{\prime}[1],

where
P^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}\subseteq d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
and
M^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}^{[0,m-1]}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}
. It follows that
M^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}[-1]*d\text{-}\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\geq-d+1}
. Hence, we have
M^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}\cap\mathcal{D}^{\geq-d+1}=d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
. Therefore, we may apply induction on
m
to deduce that
M
has projective dimension at most
m
, where in the starting case
m=0
,
M\in\mathcal{P}\subseteq d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
has projective dimension zero.
∎

We have the following equivalent characterization of tilting subcategories of
d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a subcategory of
d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
. Then
\mathcal{M}
is a tilting subcategory of
d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
if and only if it is a
(d+1)
-term silting subcategory of
\mathcal{K}

Proof.

By

,
\mathcal{M}
satisfies condition

\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{P}^{[0,d]}
. In this case,
\mathcal{M}
satisfies

and

if and only if
\mathcal{M}
is a silting subcategory of
\mathcal{K}
. This completes the proof.
∎

\mathcal{M}
of

.

For any object

, if
\operatorname{Hom}(X,\mathcal{I}[i])=0
for any

.

We first claim that for any object

, if
\operatorname{Hom}(Y,\mathcal{I})=0
then

. Indeed, since

has enough injectives
\mathcal{I}
, there exists a triangle

with

and

. Since

, we have that

is a direct summand of

and hence belongs to

. Thus, by induction, we deduce that

. However, this is not possible unless

.

It is well known that one characterization of a classical tilting module is that every injective module lies in the induced torsion class. We now investigate the meaning of this characterization for tilting subcategories of
d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
, starting with the following lemma.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{P}^{[0,d]}

\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{S})

\sigma^{\leq-d}(S)=0
, which completes the proof. Indeed, if this is not the case, applying
\operatorname{Hom}(-,I)
for any

\operatorname{Hom}(S,I[i])\to\operatorname{Hom}(\sigma^{\leq-d}(S),I[i])\to\operatorname{Hom}(M,I[i+1]),\ i>0,

where the last term vanishes since

is
\mathbb{E}
-injective in

. Moreover, as

, the first term also vanishes. Hence, the middle term
\operatorname{Hom}(\sigma^{\leq-d}(S),I[i])=0
for any

The main result of this section provides an equivalent characterization of tilting subcategories and establishes a connection between tilting subcategories and quasi-tilting subcategories. We note that, in view of

5.
Applications

In this section, we apply our results to the following two settings.

(I)

\mathcal{D}
is a Hom-finite Krull-Schmidt
\mathbf{k}
-linear triangulated category, where
\mathbf{k}
is a field.

(II)

\mathcal{D}
is a triangulated category with arbitrary coproducts.

Note that in Case (II), the extended heart
d\text{-}\mathcal{H}
is closed under arbitrary coproducts. Each setting includes a concrete subcase:

•

\mathcal{D}=D^{b}(\operatorname{mod}A)
, the bounded derived category of finitely generated modules over a finite-dimensional
\mathbf{k}
-algebra

, with
\mathcal{P}=\operatorname{add}A
,
\mathcal{H}\simeq\operatorname{mod}A

, the category of
d
-extended (small) modules
over

d\text{-}\operatorname{mod}A

•

\mathcal{D}=D(\operatorname{Mod}R)
, the derived category of modules over a unitary ring
R
, where
\mathcal{P}=\operatorname{Add}R
,
\mathcal{H}\simeq\operatorname{Mod}R
, and
\mathcal{K}\simeq K^{b}(\operatorname{Proj}R)

d
-extended large modules
over

For any object

of

\mathcal{A}(X)=\begin{cases}\operatorname{add}X&\text{in Case (I),}\\
\operatorname{Add}X&\text{in Case (II),}\end{cases}

where

denotes the

of

, that is, the full subcategory of

consisting of all direct summands of (possibly infinite) coproducts of copies of

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

be an object of
\mathcal{D}
. Then
\mathcal{A}(X)
is contravariantly finite in
\mathcal{D}
.

Proof.

Let
Y
be an object of
\mathcal{D}
. Let

be a basis of
\operatorname{Hom}(X,Y)
in Case (I); in Case (II), let
I=\operatorname{Hom}(X,Y)
. Then the object

belongs to
\mathcal{A}(X)
. Consider the morphism

X^{(I)}\xrightarrow{(g)_{g\in I}}Y,

whose components are given by the elements of

. By construction, this morphism is a right
\mathcal{A}(X)
-approximation of

, proving that
\mathcal{A}(X)
is contravariantly finite.
∎

Hence, when considering subcategories of the form
\mathcal{A}(X)
for some object

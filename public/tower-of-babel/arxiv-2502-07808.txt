Asymptotic modelling of a skin effect in magnetic conductors
Victor Péron

Abstract
. We consider the time-harmonic Maxwell equations set on a domain made of two subdomains Ω − \Omega_{-} and Ω + \Omega_{+} , such that Ω − \Omega_{-} represents a magnetic conductor and Ω + \Omega_{+} represents a non-magnetic material, and the relative magnetic permeability μ r \mu_{r} between the two materials is very high. Assuming smoothness for the interface between the subdomains and regularity of the data, the electric field solution of the Maxwell equations possesses an asymptotic expansion in powers of the parameter ε = 1 / μ r \mathsf{\varepsilon}=1/\sqrt{\mu_{r}} with profile terms rapidly decaying inside the magnetic conductor. We make explicit the first terms of this expansion. As an application of the asymptotic expansion we obtain the asymptotic behavior of a skin depth function that allows to measure the boundary layer phenomenon at large relative permeability. As another application of this expansion we give elements of proof for the derivation of impedance boundary conditions (IBCs) up to the third order of approximation with respect to the parameter ε \varepsilon , and we prove error estimates for the IBCs.



Keywords
:
Maxwell equations, High relative permeability, Asymptotic expansions, Impedance boundary conditions

1.
Introduction

This work is concerned with the time-harmonic Maxwell equations in linear materials presenting high contrast of magnetic permeabilities. The domain of interest is made up of two subdomains that represent a magnetic conductor and a non-magnetic material, and we assume that the relative magnetic permeability
\mu_{r}
between the materials is very high. In this paper we pursue mathematical efforts that we undertook in a previous work

Assuming smoothness for the interface between the subdomains, we first prove that the electric field solution of the Maxwell equations possesses a multiscale expansion in powers of

3

4.
A measure of the magnetic skin effect at large relative permeability

In this section we introduce a suitable skin depth function to measure the boundary layer phenomenon, cf. Section
4.1
. As an application of the multiscale expansion given in Section
3
for the electric field, we obtain the asymptotic behavior of this function at large relative permeability. Then, we compare this result with the literature on this topic
[
12
,
8
,
23
]
, cf. Section
4.2
.

4.1.
Definition and asymptotic behavior of a characteristic length

For a data
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{j}}}
, let us define

\,\underline{\!{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}}\!}\,_{(\mathsf{\varepsilon})}(y_{\alpha},y_{3}):=\boldsymbol{\mathsf{E}}^{-}_{(\mathsf{\varepsilon})}(\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}),\quad y_{\alpha}\in\Sigma,\quad 0\leqslant y_{3}<h_{0}\ ,

for
h_{0}
small enough. Hereafter for any
1
-form field
\,\underline{\!{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}}\!}\,=({\mathfrak{E}},\mathfrak{e})
,
|\,\underline{\!{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}}\!}\,|
denotes the modulus of
\,\underline{\!{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}}\!}\,
which is defined from the following identity

|\,\underline{\!{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}}\!}\,|^{2}=a^{\alpha\beta}(y_{3}){{\mathfrak{E}}_{\alpha}}\overline{{\mathfrak{E}}_{\beta}}+|\mathfrak{e}|^{2},

where
a^{\alpha\beta}(h)
is the inverse of the metric tensor of the manifold
\Sigma_{h}
(cf. (
6.7
), Sec.
6
), and
\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\rangle
denotes the corresponding scalar product.

Definition of a characteristic length

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\Sigma
be a smooth surface, and
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{j}}}
a data of problem (
2.1
) such that for all
y_{\alpha}
in
\Sigma
,
\,\underline{\!{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}}\!}\,_{(\mathsf{\varepsilon})}(y_{\alpha},0)\neq 0
. The skin depth is the length
\mathcal{L}(\mu_{-},\sigma_{-},y_{\alpha})
defined on
\Sigma
and taking the smallest positive value such that

(4.1)

|\,\underline{\!{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}}\!}\,_{(\mathsf{\varepsilon})}\big(y_{\alpha},\mathcal{L}(\mu_{-},\sigma_{-},y_{\alpha})\big)|=|\,\underline{\!{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}}\!}\,_{(\mathsf{\varepsilon})}(y_{\alpha},0)|\ {\mathrm{e}}^{-1}\,.

Thus the length
\mathcal{L}(\mu_{-},\sigma_{-},y_{\alpha})
corresponds to a distance from the interface where the modulus
|\,\underline{\!{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}}\!}\,_{\mathsf{\varepsilon}}|
has decreased by a factor
\mathrm{e}
. This length depends in particular on the magnetic permeability
\mu_{-}
, on the conductivity
\sigma_{-}
, and on each point
y_{\alpha}
in the interface
\Sigma
. It depends also on the data
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{j}}}
.

Asymptotic behavior of the characteristic length function when
\mu_{r}\to\infty

To measure the magnetic skin effect with the characteristic function, we introduce the classical
skin depth
parameter which is given by

(4.2)

\ell(\mu_{-},\sigma_{-})=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\omega\mu_{-}\sigma_{-}}}\ ,

and we introduce a scalar function
\phi
defined as

(4.3)

\phi(\delta)=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[4]{1+\delta^{4}}}\,\left(\sin\left(\frac{1}{2}\arctan\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\right)\right)^{-1}\,,

for any
\delta>0
. Note that we have in particular:
\phi(\delta)\to 1
as
\delta\to 0
and
\phi(\delta)\sim\sqrt{2}\delta
as
\delta\to\infty
, see also Figure
3
for the graph of function
\phi
.

As a consequence of (
3.5
) and (
3.10
), we obtain

(4.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}|\,\underline{\!{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}}\!}\,_{(\mathsf{\varepsilon})}(y_{\alpha},y_{3})|^{2}=|\boldsymbol{\mathsf{e}}_{0}(y_{\alpha})|^{2}\ \mathfrak{m}(y_{\alpha},y_{3};\mathsf{\varepsilon})\ \mathrm{e}^{-2y_{3}\operatorname{Re}(\lambda)/\mathsf{\varepsilon}}\ ,\quad\mbox{with}\\[4.30554pt]
\mathfrak{m}(y_{\alpha},y_{3};\mathsf{\varepsilon}):=\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad 1+2y_{3}\mathcal{H}(y_{\alpha})+2\mathsf{\varepsilon}\ \frac{\operatorname{Re}\big\langle\boldsymbol{\mathsf{e}}_{0}(y_{\alpha}),\boldsymbol{\mathsf{e}}_{1}(y_{\alpha})\big\rangle}{|\boldsymbol{\mathsf{e}}_{0}(y_{\alpha})|^{2}}+\mathcal{O}\big((\mathsf{\varepsilon}+y_{3})^{2}\big)\,.\end{array}\right.

We prove this formula (
4.4
) in Section
4.3
within the proof of the next result, Theorem
4.2
.
Relying on this formula, one can exhibit the asymptotic behavior at large relative permeability of the skin depth
\mathcal{L}(\mu_{-},\sigma_{-},y_{\alpha})
that depends in particular both on the product
\ell(\mu_{-},\sigma_{-})\phi(\sqrt{{\omega\varepsilon_{0}}/{\sigma_{-}}})
, and on the mean curvature
\mathcal{H}
of the surface
\Sigma
(the boundary of the domain
\Omega_{-}
):

Theorem 4.2
.

Recall that
\ell(\mu_{-},\sigma_{-})
is defined by (
4.2
),
\delta_{-}=\sqrt{{\omega\varepsilon_{0}}/{\sigma_{-}}}
, and
\phi
is the function defined by (
4.3
). Let
\mu_{+}>0
.
Let
\Sigma
be a regular surface with mean curvature
\mathcal{H}
.
We assume that
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{e}}_{0}(y_{\alpha})\neq 0
. Then the skin depth
\mathcal{L}(\mu_{-},\sigma_{-},y_{\alpha})
has the following behavior for large relative permeability:

(4.5)

\mathcal{L}(\mu_{-},\sigma_{-},y_{\alpha})=\ell(\mu_{-},\sigma_{-})\phi(\delta_{-})\Big(1+\mathcal{H}(y_{\alpha})\,\ell(\mu_{-},\sigma_{-})\phi(\delta_{-})+\mathcal{O}(\mu_{r}^{-1})\Big),\quad\mu_{r}\to\infty\,.

The proof of this result is postponed to Section
4.3
.

Remark 4.3
.

The higher order terms
\mathcal{O}(\mu_{r}^{-1})
in Eq. (
4.5
) do depend on the data
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{j}}}
of problem (
2.1
).

4.2.
Comparisons with the literature

Comparison with the eddy-current problem

A preliminary study was done in
[
23
]
for the magnetic potential in a 2D eddy-current problem. For this interface problem where
\Sigma
is a smooth and closed curve, the skin depth has the following asymptotic behavior for large relative permeability, cf.
[
23
, Prop. 2.10]
:

(4.6)

\mathcal{L}(\mu_{-},\sigma_{-};\xi)\sim\ell(\mu_{-},\sigma_{-})\Big(1+\frac{\kappa(\xi)}{2}\,\ell(\mu_{-},\sigma_{-})\Big),\quad\mu_{r}\to\infty\,,

where
\kappa(\xi)
is a scalar curvature at the point
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{X}}(\xi)\in\Sigma
and
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{X}}(\xi)
denotes an arc-length parameterization of
\Sigma
.
We retrieve this equivalent fonction
\ell(\mu_{-},\sigma_{-})\Big(1+\frac{\kappa(\xi)}{2}\,\ell(\mu_{-},\sigma_{-})\Big)
defined on
\Sigma
from formula (
4.5
) only in the limit case
\delta_{-}\to 0
since then
\phi(\delta_{-})\to 1
(see also Figure
3
), and by replacing the mean curvature
\mathcal{H}(y_{\alpha})
by
\frac{\kappa(\xi)}{2}
.

Comparison with the asymptotic behavior of the skin depth at high conductivity in non-magnetic materials

Since we have both
\delta_{-}\to 0
when
\sigma_{-}\to\infty
, and
\phi(\delta)\to 1
as
\delta\to 0
,

Figure 3.
Graph of
\phi
with respect to
\delta

we obtain

\ell(\mu_{-},\sigma_{-})\phi(\delta_{-})\Big(1+\mathcal{H}(y_{\alpha})\,\ell(\mu_{-},\sigma_{-})\phi(\delta_{-})\Big)\sim\ell(\mu_{-},\sigma_{-})\Big(1+\mathcal{H}(y_{\alpha})\,\ell(\mu_{-},\sigma_{-})\Big)\ ,\quad\sigma_{-}\to\infty\,.

This equivalent fonction
\ell(\mu_{-},\sigma_{-})\Big(1+\mathcal{H}(y_{\alpha})\,\ell(\mu_{-},\sigma_{-})\Big)
defined on
\Sigma
generalizes the following equivalent fonction given by the asymptotic behavior of the skin depth at high conductivity in non-magnetic materials, cf.
[
8
, Th. 3.2]
:

(4.7)

\mathcal{L}(\sigma_{-},y_{\alpha})\sim\ell(\mu_{0},\sigma_{-})\Big(1+\mathcal{H}(y_{\alpha})\,\ell(\mu_{0},\sigma_{-})\Big),\quad\sigma\to\infty\,,

where
\ell(\mu_{0},\sigma_{-})
is the classical skin depth (
4.2
) when the magnetic permeability
\mu_{-}
is the vacuum magnetic permeability
\mu_{0}
.

Remark 4.4

(Low frequency limit)
.

For the same reason, since we have also
\delta_{-}\to 0
as
\omega\to 0
, we obtain the same equivalent fonction at low frequency and at high conductivity
\sigma_{-}
:

\ell(\mu_{-},\sigma_{-})\phi(\delta_{-})\Big(1+\mathcal{H}(y_{\alpha})\,\ell(\mu_{-},\sigma_{-})\phi(\delta_{-})\Big)\sim\ell(\mu_{-},\sigma_{-})\Big(1+\mathcal{H}(y_{\alpha})\,\ell(\mu_{-},\sigma_{-})\Big)\ ,\quad\omega\to 0\,.

Influence of the geometry of the interface on the magnetic skin effect

Formula (
4.2
) shows particularly that the larger the mean curvature of the interface
\Sigma
, the larger the characteristic length, for large values of
\mu_{r}
. It is a common point with the 3D Maxwell problem in non-magnetic materials (cf. (
4.7
)).
It is also a similarity with the 2D eddy-current model in which the larger the scalar curvature
\kappa(\xi)
of the interface, the larger the characteristic length
\mathcal{L}(\mu_{-},\sigma_{-};\xi)
, for large values of
\mu_{r}
(cf. formula (
4.6
)).

Proof of Theorem
4.2

We adapt the proof which is given in
[
12
]
to obtain the asymptotic behavior of the skin depth at high conductivity in a non-magnetic material.

Proof.

Let
\,\underline{\!{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}}\!}\,(\mathsf{\varepsilon})
be the vector field defined as
\,\underline{\!{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}}\!}\,(\mathsf{\varepsilon})(y_{\alpha},Y_{3}):=\,\underline{\!{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}}\!}\,_{(\mathsf{\varepsilon})}(y_{\alpha},y_{3})
with

. The proof of (
4.4
) is based on calculi in covariant components of
\,\underline{\!{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}}\!}\,(\mathsf{\varepsilon})
which can be seen also as a
1
-form field. Using (
3.2
)-(
3.3
), we have:

\,\underline{\!{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}}\!}\,(\mathsf{\varepsilon})=\big({{\mathfrak{E}}_{0,\alpha}},0\big)+\mathsf{\varepsilon}\big({\mathfrak{E}}_{1,\alpha},\mathfrak{e}_{1}\big)+\mathcal{O}(\mathsf{\varepsilon}^{2})\,.

By definition of the modulus of a
1
-form field, we obtain:

|\,\underline{\!{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}}\!}\,(\mathsf{\varepsilon})|^{2}=a^{\alpha\beta}(y_{3}){{\mathfrak{E}}_{0,\alpha}}\overline{{\mathfrak{E}}_{0,\beta}}+2\mathsf{\varepsilon}\,a^{\alpha\beta}(y_{3})\operatorname{Re}{{\mathfrak{E}}_{0,\alpha}}\overline{{{\mathfrak{E}}_{1,\beta}}}+\mathcal{O}(\mathsf{\varepsilon}^{2})\,.

Then using the inverse of the metric tensor (cf. Eq. (
6.7
), Section
6
), and using (
3.5
)-(
3.10
), we obtain the following identities successively:

\begin{split}|\,\underline{\!{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}}\!}\,(\mathsf{\varepsilon})|^{2}&=(a^{\alpha\beta}+2\mathsf{\varepsilon}Y_{3}b^{\alpha\beta}){\mathfrak{E}}_{0,\alpha}\overline{{\mathfrak{E}}_{0,\beta}}+2\mathsf{\varepsilon}\,a^{\alpha\beta}\operatorname{Re}{\mathfrak{E}}_{0,\alpha}\overline{{\mathfrak{E}}_{1,\beta}}+\mathcal{O}\big((\mathsf{\varepsilon}+y_{3})^{2}\big)\\

Then, since
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{e}}_{0}(y_{\alpha})\neq 0
, the following identity enables to define
\mathfrak{m}(y_{\alpha},y_{3};\mathsf{\varepsilon})

\mathfrak{m}(y_{\alpha},y_{3};\mathsf{\varepsilon})=\\

and

\mathsf{\varepsilon}/\operatorname{Re}(\lambda)=\ell(\mu_{-},\sigma_{-})\phi(\delta_{-})\,.

L_{\alpha}^{0}(\,\underline{\!{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}}\!}\,)=-\partial_{3}^{2}{\mathfrak{E}}_{\alpha}-\kappa_{+}^{2}({1+\frac{i}{\delta_{-}^{2}}}){\mathfrak{E}}_{\alpha}\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ L_{\alpha}^{1}(\,\underline{\!{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}}\!}\,)=-2b_{\alpha}^{\beta}\partial_{3}{\mathfrak{E}}_{\beta}+\partial_{3}D_{\alpha}\mathfrak{e}+b_{\beta}^{\beta}\partial_{3}{\mathfrak{E}}_{\alpha}\ .

Here,

Y_{3}
. We denote by

the transverse components of
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{L}}^{n}
. There holds

(6.10)

L_{3}^{0}(\,\underline{\!{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}}\!}\,)=-\kappa_{+}^{2}({1+\frac{i}{\delta_{-}^{2}}})\mathfrak{e}\quad\mbox{and}\quad L_{3}^{1}(\,\underline{\!{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}}\!}\,)=\gamma_{\alpha}^{\alpha}(\partial_{3}\,\underline{\!{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}}\!}\,)+b_{\beta}^{\beta}\partial_{3}\mathfrak{e}\ ,

where
\gamma_{\alpha\beta}(\,\underline{\!{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{H}}}\!}\,)=\frac{1}{2}(D_{\alpha}{\mathfrak{H}}_{\beta}+D_{\beta}{\mathfrak{H}}_{\alpha})-b_{\alpha\beta}\mathfrak{h}
is the change of metric tensor.

We denote by
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{B}}(y_{\alpha},h;D_{\alpha},\partial_{3}^{h})
the tangent trace operator
\operatorname{\mathrm{curl}}\cdot\times\boldsymbol{\mathsf{n}}
on
\Sigma
in a
normal coordinate system
. If

\,\underline{\!{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}}\!}\,=({\mathfrak{E}}_{\alpha},\mathfrak{e})

\left(\boldsymbol{\mathsf{B}}(y_{\alpha},h;D_{\alpha},\partial_{3}^{h})\,\underline{\!{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}}\!}\,\right)_{\alpha}=\partial_{3}^{h}{\mathfrak{E}}_{\alpha}-D_{\alpha}\mathfrak{e}\ ,

see
[
8

\boldsymbol{\mathsf{B}}[\mathsf{\varepsilon}]
the operator obtained from
\mathsf{\varepsilon}^{2}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{B}}
in
{\mathcal{U}}_{-}
after the scaling

. This operator expands in power of
\mathsf{\varepsilon}

\boldsymbol{\mathsf{B}}[\mathsf{\varepsilon}]=\mathsf{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{B}}^{0}+\mathsf{\varepsilon}^{2}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{B}}^{1}\ .

Thus, denoting by
B_{\alpha}^{n}
the surface components of
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{B}}^{n}
, we obtain

(6.11)

B_{\alpha}^{0}(\,\underline{\!{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}}\!}\,)=\partial_{3}{\mathfrak{E}}_{\alpha}\quad\mbox{and}\quad B_{\alpha}^{1}(\,\underline{\!{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}}\!}\,)=-D_{\alpha}\mathfrak{e}\ .

\left\{\begin{array}[]{clll}(i)&&-\lambda^{2}\mathfrak{e}_{n}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}L_{3}^{n-j}(\,\underline{\!{\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{E}}}\!}\,_{j})&\mbox{in}\quad\Sigma\times I\\[3.87495pt]

Network centric identification of PI3K/Akt hub proteins as key oncogenic drivers and therapeutic targets
Authors: Emad Fadhal
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The PI3K/Akt pathway plays a central role in cancer progression by regulating cell proliferation, survival, and metabolism. Dysregulation of this pathway, often due to mutations in genes such as PIK3CA, PTPN11, EGFR, and AKT1, contributes to tumorigenesis and therapy resistance. Using a network metric space approach, we systematically analyzed the human protein-protein interaction network to identify key hub proteins. Our findings suggest that signaling proteins dominate the PI3K/Akt pathway (100%), with significant overlaps in MAPK cascades (29.1%) and essential oncogenic drivers (70.8%), indicating potential co-targeting strategies to overcome resistance. Functional enrichment analysis highlights the therapeutic relevance of these hubs, while 5.8% of identified proteins are oncogenes, reinforcing their candidacy for therapies. This study provides a systematic, network-based framework for identifying and prioritizing hub proteins in the PI3K/Akt pathway, with potential implications for rational multi-target drug design in precision oncology.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-24625-0.

Intrdoution

Cancer is a complex disease driven by the dysregulation of critical signaling pathways that govern cellular homeostasis. Among these, the phosphoinositide 3-kinase/protein kinase B (PI3K/Akt) pathway appears to be a key regulator of cell proliferation, survival, and metabolic reprogramming1. Aberrant activation of this pathway is a hallmark of numerous malignancies, including breast, colorectal, and ovarian cancers, making it a prime target for therapeutic intervention2.

The PI3K/Akt cascade integrates extracellular signals to modulate key cellular functions such as glucose metabolism, protein synthesis, and apoptosis evasion3. Dysregulation often arises from genetic mutations such as PIK3CA and AKT1 or epigenetic alterations that hyperactivate oncogenic signaling while suppressing tumor suppressors like PTEN4. For instance, PIK3CA gain-of-function mutations occur in ~ 40% of hormone receptor-positive breast cancers, driving therapy resistance5. Similarly, EGFR amplifications and PTPN11 mutations further amplify PI3K/Akt output, fostering aggressive tumor phenotypes6,7.

Recent advances highlight the pathway’s overlapping with mTOR, RAS/RAF, and immune checkpoints, revealing opportunities for combination therapies8. Preclinical studies indicate that dual inhibition of PI3K and mTOR synergistically suppresses tumor growth in PTEN-null models9,10. Clinically, isoform-specific PI3K inhibitors such as alpelisib are now FDA-approved for PIK3CA-mutated cancers, yet adaptive resistance remains a challenge11.

Several previous studies have underscored the critical roles of specific proteins within the PI3K/Akt pathway, notably PIK3CA and AKT1, identifying them as high-impact protiens essential for cancer development and progression12. These proteins not only drive oncogenic signaling but also exhibit functional overlap with other key pathways, revealing a complex network of co-dependencies that can be exploited for therapeutic gain. By integrating multi-omics data from TCGA and CPTAC, researchers have uncovered novel associations that suggest the potential for more effective combination therapies13. Moreover, evidence has pointed to the importance of vertical inhibition strategies such as concurrently targeting PI3K and Akt to mitigate compensatory feedback loops that often undermine monotherapies14. While previous studies have identified key proteins in the PI3K/Akt pathway, our network-centric approach provides a more comprehensive evaluation of their hierarchical importance and therapeutic potential. Hub proteins (highly connected nodes in a protein-protein interaction networks (PPINs) that interact with many other proteins) are of particular interest. These proteins are often functionally essential and evolutionarily conserved, and they tend to play central roles in various cellular processes, including signaling, regulation, and complex assembly. We present these findings as preliminary evidence requiring further experimental validation, rather than definitive conclusions.

Given the variety of cancer signaling networks, particularly within the PI3K/Akt axis, a deeper understanding of how these hub proteins interact with other oncogenic pathways is essential for advancing precision oncology15. Previous studies have laid the groundwork for rational drug design and personalized treatment strategies, ultimately aiming to enhance clinical outcomes16,17.

Accordingly, this study seeks to systematically identify and functionally characterize the key hub proteins within the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway, assess their overlap with parallel oncogenic networks, and evaluate their potential as therapeutic targets. By doing so, it aims to contribute foundational knowledge for developing more robust, targeted interventions in cancer therapy.

To enhance clarity, we have structured our analysis into three distinct phases: (1) network-based identification of central hub proteins, (2) comprehensive functional characterization of these hubs, and (3) evaluation of their therapeutic potential through pathway overlap analysis. This systematic approach ensures transparent reporting of our methodology and results.

Materials and methods

The methodology presented here is designed to be fully repeatable, with all computational approaches, parameters, and data sources. The complete analysis pipeline is available upon request to facilitate replication studies.

Distance-based mapping of protein-protein interaction networks

PPINs were modeled as spatial maps, with proteins acting as nodes and their interactions forming connecting pathways. To analyze the topological structure of these networks, a custom Python tool was developed, integrating algorithms from the C + + BOOST library (http://www.boost.org/). Version control was applied throughout to ensure full reproducibility. The analytical pipeline consisted of two main components: (i) the construction of an undirected network topology, and (ii) the computation of shortest path distances between all protein pairs using Dijkstra’s algorithm under default parameters. This methodological framework was adopted in line with established practices in network analysis, as detailed in18. The shortest path computations enabled the identification of the network’s central protein (defined as the protein (or set of proteins) with the minimal total distance to all other nodes) similar to a city’s downtown core. Proteins were then categorized into concentric zones based on their distance from this center, copying urban zoning relative to a central landmark (Fig. 1).

Fig. 1The PPIN is illustrated with proteins arranged in concentric zones according to their proximity to the central protein(s).This mapping strategy facilitated the classification of proteins into distinct categories according to their separation from the central hub. The step size (defined as the number of interactions required to traverse from the center to a given protein) determined zone assignment. For example, zone 1 contained proteins directly linked to the center, zone 2 included those two interactions away, and so forth.

Interpreting PPINs as metric spaces revealed a hierarchical architecture: a densely interconnected core encircled by increasingly dispersed peripheral layers. This organization correlated with biological significance, underscoring the utility of distance-based methods in differentiating healthy and pathological networks. Central proteins, particularly sensory molecules, emerged as promising therapeutic candidates. In human PPINs, these core zones were notably enriched with essential proteins and known drug targets, reinforcing the method’s potential for drug discovery.

To ensure robust statistical analysis, we implemented multiple validation steps in our network mapping approach. The distance-based metric calculations were repeated across 1000 bootstrap samples of the protein interaction data to quantify uncertainty in center identification. This resampling approach provided confidence intervals for our centrality measures, with all reported central proteins maintaining statistical significance (p < 0.01) across iterations.

Software tools and KEGG database version

To assess the biological implications of zonal partitioning, proteins were grouped by their network centrality, and pathway enrichment analysis was performed for each zone to uncover distinct functional attributes. This analysis employed Gene Set Enrichment tools integrated from comparative toxicogenomic databases (https://ctdbase.org/), alongside annotations from KEGG and Gene Ontology (GO). The enrichment analyses were conducted using a custom pipeline developed in Python, incorporating enrichment modules compatible with KEGG and GO annotations. The KEGG database version used was the one available as of 2023, selected to ensure inclusion of the latest updates to cancer-related signaling pathways, including modules such as PI3K/Akt and MAPK19–21.

Statistical criteria for significance

Statistical enrichment was assessed using a p-value threshold of < 0.01, uniformly applied across all zones in the network-based partitioning to ensure consistency. To support the robustness of the findings, proteins participating in significantly enriched pathways were also quantified within each zone to evaluate zonal functional specialization. While this initial analysis focused on raw p-values, we acknowledge that false discovery rate (FDR) corrections offer additional stringency, and we plan to incorporate such adjustments in future studies.

Investigation of oncogenic and tumor suppressor pathways

Special attention was given to pathways involving oncogenes and tumor suppressor genes. Protein prioritization incorporated data from pan-cancer genomic studies, emphasizing interactions with validated roles in tumorigenesis22. These annotations were based on curated cancer genomic databases such as COSMIC, CGC, and TCGA, as compiled in Vogelstein et al.22. High-confidence associations were filtered, revealing frequent involvement of cancer-driver genes with well-documented mechanistic links to malignancy.

Proteins play a vital role in critical cellular functions, including essential, signaling, growth, cell cycle control, and therapeutic applications

Proteins play a vital role in critical cellular functions, including signaling, growth regulation, cell cycle control, and serve as key targets in therapeutic applications. To evaluate zone 1 of the human protein-protein interaction network23, we identified key human proteins by analyzing the phenotypic effects of their gene knockouts in mice. Additionally, essential genes were identified using orthologous lethality data from the Mouse Genome Database24, which annotates genes whose knockout in mice leads to lethal phenotypes. These cross-species datasets provided robust evidence for functional importance and were used as primary references for classifying proteins as signaling, growth, or essential therapeutic targets.

Results

Recent study has increasingly highlighted the importance of PPINs in deciphering cellular mechanisms and discovering potential drug targets. In previous work18, we used the Dijkstra algorithm to compute the shortest paths between all pairs of proteins in the human PPIN and then identified the protein(s) with the smallest maximum distance to all other proteins (defined as the network center(s)). Proteins were then classified into distinct zones according to their topological distances from this central protein(s).

Our findings revealed that proteins nearest the network core are often essential for fundamental housekeeping processes. Additionally, these centrally located proteins were identified as promising candidates for therapeutic intervention25.

Building on this framework, the present study concentrates on zone 1, the most densely interconnected zone, which is enriched with proteins linked to critical cellular processes such as signal transduction, immune response, hemostasis, and disease mechanisms. From the 374 proteins in this zone, we cross-referenced them with KEGG pathways to pinpoint those involved in PI3K/AKT-related pathways. This analysis aims to elucidate the functional roles of these proteins.

For clear presentation of our findings, results are organized to first demonstrate the network topology and hub identification, followed by functional characterization, and concluding with therapeutic implications. Distribution of functional categories in PI3K/Akt pathway-related proteins zone1

The progression of cancer is heavily influenced by the activation and dysregulation of key signaling pathways, which promote uncontrolled cell growth and survival. Targeting these pathways is crucial for disrupting the mechanisms that allow cancer cells to proliferate26–29.

Table 2 shows the distribution of essential proteins in the PI3K/AKT pathway. To assess the PI3K/AKT pathway of zone 1, we selected a list of human proteins that may be more important for orthologous knockout in mice21. As shown in Table 2, signaling proteins dominate the functional distribution within the PI3K/AKT pathway, representing 100% of the observed proteins. Essential proteins constitute the next largest group at 70.8%, while other functional categories exhibit varying proportions: negative signaling (37.5%), MAPK cascade (29.1%), positive signaling (25%), growth-related proteins (16.7%), and cell cycle regulators (10.9%).

Across the four PI3K/Akt-related pathways, a total of 24 unique proteins were identified. Of these, nine proteins (AKT1, PIK3CA, PIK3R1, EGFR, ERBB3, PTPN11, GRB2, FYN, and LCK) were consistently shared across all groups, representing 37.5% of the total union. These shared proteins overlap with well-known oncogenes and signaling mediators of the PI3K/Akt and MAPK pathways, underscoring their central role in oncogenic signaling and their potential as therapeutic targets30,31. Importantly, 100% of the proteins from these pathways were located in zone 1, reinforcing the strong central enrichment of PI3K/Akt hubs. To provide preliminary clinical validation, we cross-referenced the zone 1 hub proteins (PIK3CA, EGFR, AKT1, and PTPN11) with TCGA and cBioPortal datasets. Consistent with prior genomic studies, PIK3CA exhibited mutation frequencies of approximately 35–40% in hormone receptor–positive breast cancers and around 20% in colorectal cancers30. EGFR alterations were enriched in non-small cell lung cancer cohorts31. AKT1 mutations occurred less frequently, with a prevalence of aroud 2–5% in breast cancer32, while PTPN11 mutations were recurrent in certain leukemias and solid tumors at lower frequencies33. These findings are consistent with our network-based identification of these proteins as central hubs and reinforce their translational relevance.

Proteins are fundamental components in cancer biology, functioning either as oncogenes that drive tumorigenesis or as tumor suppressors that inhibit malignant progression. The dynamic interplay between these proteins significantly influences cancer cell behavior34,35. Among the critical pathways involved in cellular regulation, the PI3K/AKT signaling cascade plays a central role in processes such as cell proliferation, survival, and metabolic control. Its precise regulation is vital for maintaining the balance between cell growth and death, and its dysregulation is frequently associated with oncogenesis.

Within the PI3K/Akt pathway, several key oncogenes and tumor suppressor genes have been identified. Notably, PIK3CA is a prominent oncogene in this pathway. Our analysis detected PIK3CA, along with other significant genes like PTPN11, EGFR, and AKT1, across various PI3K/Akt pathway subtypes. As shown in Table 3, these oncogenes collectively represent 5.8% of the pathway components. Additionally, proteins identified as successful therapeutic targets constituted 8.8%, whereas apoptosis-related proteins and negative apoptosis regulators each accounted for 1.5%. Discussion

The PI3K/Akt pathway is crucial in oncogenesis, orchestrating cellular proliferation, survival, and metabolic reprogramming. Our study utilizes a network metric space approach to identify PIK3CA, PTPN11, EGFR, and AKT1 as central hubs in zone 1 of the PI3K/Akt interactome, consistent with their potential roles as key regulators of tumorigenesis. One of the key observations in this study is the consistent identification of nine proteins across all PI3K/Akt-related pathways (AKT1, PIK3CA, PIK3R1, EGFR, ERBB3, PTPN11, GRB2, FYN, and LCK), accounting for 37.5% of the total union. Their recurrence underlines both their strong centrality and biological importance. Central kinases such as AKT1 and PIK3CA are frequently mutated drivers in breast and colorectal cancers and remain established therapeutic targets30. Likewise, EGFR and ERBB3, two receptor tyrosine kinases, are often amplified or mutated in lung and breast cancers, where they contribute to therapeutic resistance and are already targeted by several FDA-approved inhibitors31. The adaptor GRB2 and the kinases LCK and FYN highlight the extensive cross-talk between the PI3K/Akt and MAPK pathways, a process that drives adaptive resistance42. In addition, PTPN11 (SHP2), a phosphatase linking growth factor receptors to RAS/MAPK signaling, has recently gained attention as a co-target in combination with PI3K inhibition44. Together, the repeated presence of these proteins may be viewed as defining a minimal, high-confidence oncogenic core, underscoring their relevance as top-priority candidates for precision oncology. These findings align with emerging evidence that dysregulated PI3K/Akt signaling drives therapeutic resistance in cancers like breast and colorectal carcinoma2,3. Although our data indicate strong centrality for PIK3CA, PTPN11, EGFR, and AKT1, we acknowledge that network topology alone cannot establish causal relationships. The therapeutic implications proposed here represent testable hypotheses rather than established clinical truths.

While our network approach identified hub proteins, we acknowledge several statistical limitations. The protein interaction data inherently contain both false positives and negatives, which we addressed by using high-confidence interaction scores from multiple databases. Our network metric approach offers particular advantages for reproducibility because: (1) the zone classification system uses objective topological measures rather than subjective clustering, (2) all centrality calculations derive from first-principles graph theory, and (3) the implementation utilizes well-documented libraries (BOOST, Network).

Our analysis reveals that signaling proteins constitute 100% of the PI3K/Akt pathway components in zone 1, underscoring their non-redundant role in oncogenic signaling. This complete dominance of signaling proteins in zone 1 may reflect a core biological principle rather than mere annotation bias. Zone 1 proteins occupy central positions in the PPIN network and likely function as master regulators of information flow, integrating and coordinating key processes such as proliferation, survival, and immune evasion.

While classification bias is a valid concern, previous studies across diverse PPIN networks consistently show that central zones are enriched in signaling proteins, essential genes, and oncogenic drivers45. This recurring pattern suggests a non-random, conserved network architecture in which central hubs are functionally dedicated to signaling. Consequently, the therapeutic relevance is substantial, as these proteins represent high-impact, druggable targets for modulating disease progression. Notably, PIK3CA and EGFR emerged as dual-function hubs, integrating inputs from growth factor receptors (HER2) and downstream effectors like mTOR10. This overlap suggests that co-targeting PI3K and EGFR could mitigate compensatory activation, a strategy validated in PTEN-deficient models2. The 5.8% representation of oncogenes (PIK3CA, AKT1) further highlights their candidacy for precision therapies, as isoform-specific inhibitors (alpelisib) show clinical efficacy in PIK3CA-mutant cancers6.

The 29.1% overlap with MAPK cascades (Table 2) echoes recent work identifying feedback loops between PI3K and RAS/RAF pathways as drivers of adaptive resistance15. For instance, PTPN11 (SHP2) bridges PI3K and MAPK signaling, and its inhibition synergizes with PI3Kα blockers in preclinical models46. Our data support vertical inhibition strategies (PI3K + MEK) to preempt escape mechanisms, as proposed by47 and recently validated in hepatocellular carcinoma10.

While 70.8% of zone 1 proteins are essential, their functional redundancy is low, implying that targeting these nodes could disrupt cancer cell viability with minimal off-target effects. The 7.4% representation of tumor suppressors (PIK3R1) aligns with studies showing that PTEN loss amplifies PI3K dependency, creating a therapeutic window for PARP inhibitors in PTEN-null tumors4. Moreover, the 8.8% of proteins with validated therapeutic targets (Table 3) underscores the clinical translatability of our findings, exemplified by FDA-approved agents like alpelisib and trastuzumab5.

Our zone-based classification not only maps proteins by topological centrality but reveals a strong correlation between proximity to the network center and therapeutic importance. Zone 1 proteins are enriched for essential, oncogenic, and clinically targeted components of the PI3K/Akt pathway. These findings suggest that the concentric zoning approach, beyond offering a structural framework, may serve as a predictive model for identifying high-value therapeutic targets within cancer networks.

This study offers valuable insights into PI3K/Akt hub proteins using a network-based metric approach, yet several limitations must be acknowledged. The protein-protein interaction data, while curated, lack tissue-specific and context-dependent resolution, potentially overlooking cancer-type variations. Our zone-based centrality model emphasizes topological importance but may not capture functional redundancy.

Future work will incorporate sensitivity analyses by overlaying zone 1 hub proteins with patient-level expression and survival data from TCGA. Such integration will provide additional resolution on whether the topologically central hubs identified here are consistently overexpressed in tumors and associated with clinical outcomes, thereby refining their prioritization as therapeutic targets.

Although significant overlap with the MAPK pathway suggests possible co-targeting strategies, experimental validation is necessary to confirm the therapeutic relevance of these findings. The clear presentation of our methods in Sect. 2 facilitates such future validation studies, which should ideally incorporate patient-derived models to assess the efficacy of co-targeting PIK3CA and MAPK nodes.

Conclusion

The PI3K/Akt pathway plays a crucial role in cancer progression, with dysregulation contributing to uncontrolled cell proliferation, survival, and therapeutic resistance. Our analysis suggests the PI3K/Akt pathway likely plays a pivotal role in cancer progression, with our network approach identifying several proteins as potential central hubs. Through a network-based analysis, this study identifies PIK3CA, PTPN11, EGFR, and AKT1 as central hub proteins, consistent with their potential significance in oncogenic signaling. Our findings indicate that signaling proteins dominate this pathway (100%), with notable overlaps in MAPK cascades (29.1%) and essential oncogenic drivers (70.8%), suggesting potential co-targeting strategies to overcome resistance mechanisms. The functional enrichment analysis highlights the therapeutic potential of these hub proteins. Additionally, the high representation of validated therapeutic targets (8.8%) underscores the clinical relevance of these findings. By providing the complete analytical pipeline, this study establishes a reproducible framework for identifying signaling hubs (one that can be adapted for broader application across other oncogenic pathways). In particular, our comparative analysis identified nine proteins consistently shared across PI3K/Akt-related pathways (AKT1, PIK3CA, PIK3R1, EGFR, ERBB3, PTPN11, GRB2, FYN, and LCK), representing a conserved oncogenic core with direct translational relevance. Their enrichment in zone 1 and recurrent appearance across multiple functional categories may highlights them as priority targets for therapeutic interventions and supports multi-target drug design strategies. These insights provide a framework for precision oncology, emphasizing the need for combination therapies that target multiple nodes within the PI3K/Akt network. Future research should explore the mechanistic interplay between these hubs and validate their inhibition in preclinical models to enhance treatment efficacy. Ultimately, this study advances our understanding of PI3K/Akt-driven malignancies and offers a strategic approach for developing more effective anticancer therapies.

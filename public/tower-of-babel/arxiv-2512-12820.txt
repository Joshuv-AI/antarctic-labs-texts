On the splitting of weak nearly 𝒞 {\cal C} -manifolds
Sourav Nayak, Dhriti Sundar Patra, Vladimir Rovenski

Abstract
Weak metric f f -manifolds, i.e., the linear complex structure on the contact distribution is replaced by a nonsingular skew-symmetric tensor, defined by V. Rovenski and R. Wolak (2022), allow a new look at the theory of almost contact and metric f f -manifolds. In this paper, we study the curvature and topology of a structure of this type, called the weak nearly 𝒞 {\cal C} -structure; specifically, we are guided by the following question: Under what conditions are weak nearly 𝒞 \cal C -manifolds locally the Riemannian products ? Our theorems generalize results of H. Endo (2005), A. Nicola–G. Dileo–I. Yudin (2018) on nearly cosymplectic manifolds and V. Rovenski (2024) on weak nearly cosymplectic manifolds to the context of weak nearly 𝒞 {\cal C} -manifolds. Namely, we find conditions under which a ( 2 ​ n + s ) (2n+s) -dimensional weak nearly 𝒞 {\cal C} -manifold becomes a Riemannian product, and characterize ( 4 + s ) (4+s) -dimensional weak nearly 𝒞 {\cal C} -manifolds. The consequences of these theorems present new results for nearly 𝒞 {\cal C} -manifolds. Keywords : weak nearly 𝒞 {\cal C} -manifold, weak nearly Kähler manifold, curvature tensor, foliation, Killing vector field. Mathematics Subject Classifications (2010) 53C15, 53C25, 53D15

Introduction

A. Gray introduced in
[
10

a subclass of almost Hermitian manifolds

These odd-dimensional counterparts of nearly Kähler manifolds are useful in classifying almost contact metric manifolds

In dimensions greater than 5, a nearly cosymplectic manifold is locally the Riemannian product

The

Contact foliations generalize to higher dimensions the flow of

-structures are a particular case of uniform

An important class of metric

-manifolds

Any such manifold is locally the product of

Nearly

-manifolds,

The question arises:

In

16

Recall that nearly Kähler manifolds

[

\nabla J

Definition 1
.

A Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
of even dimension equipped with a skew-symmetric (1,1)-tensor
{f}

such that the tensor
{f}^{\,2}
is negative-definite is called a
weak Hermitian manifold
.
Such
(M,{f},g)
is called a
weak Kähler manifold
if
\nabla{f}=0
, where
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection.
A weak Hermitian manifold is called a
weak nearly Kähler manifold
, if

(\nabla_{X}{f})Y+(\nabla_{Y}{f})X=0\ (X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M})
.

Definition 2
.

A
weak metric
f
-structure
on a smooth manifold
M^{2n+s}

(n,s>0)
is a set
({f},Q,\vec{\xi},\vec{\eta}
,
g)
, where

{f}
is a skew-symmetric
(1,1)
-tensor of rank
2n
,
Q
is a self-adjoint nonsingular
(1,1)
-tensor,

\vec{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{s})
are orthonormal vector fields,

\vec{\eta}=(\eta^{1},\ldots,\eta^{s})
are dual 1-forms,
and
g
is a Riemannian metric on
M
, satisfying

\displaystyle{f}^{2}=-Q+\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}{\eta^{i}}\otimes{\xi_{i}},\quad{\eta^{i}}({\xi_{j}})=\delta^{i}_{j},\quad Q\,{\xi_{i}}={\xi_{i}},

(1)

\displaystyle g({f}X,{f}Y)=g(X,Q\,Y)-\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}{\eta^{i}}(X)\,{\eta^{i}}(Y)\quad(X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}).

(2)

In this case,
(M^{2n+s},{f},Q,\vec{\xi},\vec{\eta},g)
is called a
weak metric
f
-manifold
.

Putting
Y=\xi_{j}
in (
2
), and using
{\eta^{i}}({\xi_{j}})=\delta^{i}_{j}
, we get

\displaystyle\eta^{j}(X)=g(X,\xi_{j});

(3)

thus,
{\xi_{j}}
is orthogonal to the
contact distribution

{\cal D}:=\bigcap_{\,i=1}^{s}\ker{\eta^{i}}
.
For a more intuitive understanding of the role of
Q
in the metric
f
-structure, we explain the following properties:

{f}\,{\xi_{i}}=0,\quad{\eta^{i}}\circ{f}=0,\quad\eta^{i}\circ Q=\eta^{i},\quad[Q,\,{f}]=0.

By (
1
),
f^{2}\xi_{i}=0
is true. From this and (
1
), we get

f^{3}+fQ=0
.
By this,
Q\xi_{i}=\xi_{i}
and
f^{2}\xi_{i}=0
we get
0=-f^{3}\xi_{i}=fQ\xi_{i}=f\xi_{i}
.
By
f\xi_{i}=0
, (
3
), and the skew-symmetry of

, we get
\eta^{i}(fX)=g(fX,\xi_{i})=-g(X,f\xi_{i})=0
.
From this and condition

f
the distribution
{\cal D}

of a weak metric
f
-structure is
{f}
-invariant,
{\cal D}=f(TM)
and
\dim{\cal D}=2n
.
By this and
f^{3}+fQ=0
, we get
f^{3}X=f^{2}(fX)=-QfX
; hence,
f^{3}+Qf=0
.
This and
f^{3}+fQ=0
yield
fQ=Qf
. By symmetry of

Q\xi_{i}=\xi_{i}

\eta^{i}(QX)=g(QX,\xi_{i})=g(X,Q\xi_{i})=g(X,\xi_{i})=\eta^{i}(X)
.
Therefore, the tangent bundle splits,

TM={\cal D}\oplus\ker f

as the complementary orthogonal sum of its subbundles,
the contact distribution
{\cal D}
, and

\ker f=\operatorname{span}\{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{s}\}

The exterior derivative of a 1-form
{\omega}
is given by

\displaystyle d\omega(X,Y)=X({\omega}(Y))-Y({\omega}(X))-{\omega}([X,Y])\quad(X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}).

The fundamental
2
-form
\Phi
on

(M^{2n+s},{f},Q,\vec{\xi},\vec{\eta},g)
is given by

\Phi(X,Y)=g(X,{f}Y)

for all

X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}
.
Recall the co-boundary formula for exterior derivative

\Phi

\displaystyle d\Phi(X,Y,Z)

\displaystyle=X\,\Phi(Y,Z)+Y\,\Phi(Z,X)+Z\,\Phi(X,Y)

\displaystyle\ -\Phi([X,Y],Z)-\Phi([Z,X],Y)-\Phi([Y,Z],X).

Note that the above equality yields

\displaystyle d\Phi(X,Y,Z)=(\nabla_{X}\,\Phi)(Y,Z)+(\nabla_{Y}\,\Phi)(Z,X)+(\nabla_{Z}\,\Phi)(X,Y).

(4)

A weak metric
f
-structure is called a

-

-contact structure

Another important case of a weak metric

-structure is

-structure, that is

Its special case, is a weak almost

For

Moreover, weak almost

\displaystyle[\xi_{i},\xi_{j}]

\displaystyle=0\quad(1\leq i,j\leq s),

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X}\,\xi_{i},\ \xi_{j})

\displaystyle\eta^{k}(\nabla_{\xi_{i}}\,\xi_{j})=0\quad(1\leq i,j,k\leq s).

Definition 3

A weak metric

(\nabla_{X}{f})Y+(\nabla_{Y}{f})X=0.

, the distribution
\operatorname{ker}f
defines a flat totally geodesic foliation. Moreover, since
\nabla_{X}\,\xi_{k},\ \nabla_{\xi_{k}}X\in\mathcal{D}_{i,j}
for all

, we have
\operatorname{ker}f\oplus\mathcal{D}_{i,j}
is integrable (involutive).
Now, taking

and applying (

h_{i}^{2}(\nabla_{X}\,Y)=-\lambda^{2}_{i,j}\,\nabla_{X}Y-(\nabla_{X}\,h_{i}^{2})Y=-\lambda^{2}_{i,j}\,\nabla_{X}Y+\lambda^{2}_{i,j}\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{s}g(X,h_{i}Y)\,\xi_{k}.

Using the above and the anti-commutativity of

and
h_{i}
,
we have

h_{i}^{2}({f}^{2}\nabla_{X}Y)={f}^{2}(h_{i}^{2}\nabla_{X}Y)=-\lambda^{2}_{i,j}\,{f}^{2}(\nabla_{X}Y);

hence,
{f}^{2}\nabla_{X}Y\in{\cal D}_{i,j}
.
Similarly, using (

), we get
\widetilde{Q}\nabla_{X}Y\in{\cal D}_{i,j}
.
It follows that

\nabla_{X}Y=-\widetilde{Q}\,\nabla_{X}Y-{f}^{2}\nabla_{X}Y+\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{s}\eta^{k}(\nabla_{X}Y)\,\xi_{k},

see (
1
), belongs to the distribution
\ker f\oplus{\cal D}_{i,j}
.
This proves
(i)
.

Let
\dim(\ker d\eta^{i})>s
for all
i
, i.e., the multiplicity of the eigenvalue 0 of
h_{i}^{\,2}
be greater than
s
.
Similarly to
(i)
, we obtain

\nabla_{X}\xi_{k},\nabla_{\xi_{k}}X\in\ker f\oplus\mathcal{D}_{i,0}
for
X\in\ker f\oplus\mathcal{D}_{i,0}
and
1\leq k\leq s
.
Hence, the distribution
\ker f\oplus\mathcal{D}_{i,0}
is integrable.
Moreover, by (
69
) we have

(\nabla_{X}h_{i}^{\,2})Y=0,
for

X,Y\in\operatorname{ker}f\oplus\mathcal{D}_{i,0}.

By the above,
h_{i}^{\,2}(\nabla_{X}Y)=0
for
1\leq i\leq s
, so
\ker f\oplus\mathcal{D}_{i,0}
is totally geodesic, proving
(ii)
.

Since
\mathcal{D}_{i,0}\neq 0
,
we get
h_{i}^{\,2}(\nabla_{X}Y)=0
for all
X,Y\in\mathcal{D}_{i,0}
. In addition, we compute

g(\nabla_{X}Y,\xi_{k})=-g\big(Y,\nabla_{X}\xi_{k}\big)=-g(Y,h_{k}X)
,
which vanishes if

\operatorname{ker}h_{i}=\operatorname{ker}h_{k}
for all
1\leq i,k\leq s
.
By (
2
), this condition is equivalent to the fact that the exterior derivatives of the coframe
\{\eta^{i}\}
have a common kernel.
Thus, each distribution
D_{i,0}
defines a totally geodesic foliation if and only if the exterior derivatives of the coframe
\{\eta^{i}\}
have a common kernel.
By (
8
) and (
18
), the leaves of

are
{f}
-invariant and
Q
-invariant.
Thus, the weak nearly
{\cal C}
-structure on
M
with conditions (
5
), (
6
), (
9
) and (

)
induces a weak nearly Kähler structure
(\bar{f},\bar{g})
on each leaf of
D_{i,0}
with the property
\bar{\nabla}(\bar{f}^{\,2})=0
,
where
\bar{\nabla}
is the Levi-Civita connection of
\bar{g}
. This proves
(iii)
.

To establish
(iv)
, it suffices to verify that for
X\in{\cal D}_{i,j}
and
Y\in{\cal D}_{i,k}
with
j\neq k
, one has
\nabla_{X}Y\in\ker f\oplus{\cal D}_{i,1}\oplus\cdots\oplus{\cal D}_{i,r}
.
Similarly to part
(i)
, we obtain

h_{i}^{2}(\nabla_{X}Y)=-\lambda_{i,k}\,\nabla_{X}Y+\lambda_{i,k}\sum_{j=1}^{s}g(X,h_{j}Y)\,\xi_{j}
;
hence,
h_{i}^{2}(f^{2}\nabla_{X}Y)=-\lambda_{i,k}\,f^{2}\nabla_{X}Y
.
Thus
f^{2}\nabla_{X}Y\in{\cal D}_{i,k}
, and we calculate
\widetilde{Q}\nabla_{X}Y\in{\cal D}_{i,k}
. Combining these observations, we may write

\nabla_{X}Y=-\widetilde{Q}(\nabla_{X}Y)-f^{2}\nabla_{X}Y+\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{s}\eta^{k}(\nabla_{X}Y)\,\xi_{k}\in\operatorname{ker}f\oplus{\cal D}_{i,k}\subset\ker f\oplus{\cal D}_{i,1}\oplus\cdots\oplus{\cal D}_{i,r}.

Similarly, we obtain
\nabla_{Y}X\in\operatorname{ker}f\oplus{\cal D}_{i,j}
.
Consequently, both
[X,Y]
and
\nabla_{X}Y
belong to

\ker f\oplus{\cal D}_{i,1}\oplus\cdots\oplus{\cal D}_{i,r}
, which completes the proof.

Definition 4

(see

Let

(2n+s)
-dimensional smooth manifold where

An

on

is a collection

\vec{\eta}=(\eta^{1},\cdots,\eta^{s})
of
s>1

together with a splitting

of the tangent bundle, satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

{\cal D}:=\bigcap_{\,i}\ker\eta^{i}
;

(ii)

is non-degenerate
for every

;

(iii)

\ker d\eta^{i}={\mathcal{R}}
for every

.

A manifold

endowed with such a structure is called an
s
-
contact manifold
and is denoted by

(M,\vec{\eta},{\mathcal{R}}\oplus{\cal D})
.
A coframe
\vec{\eta}
(and the

-contact

if it satisfies

(iv)

d\eta^{i}=d\eta^{j}
for all
1\leq i,j\leq s
.

An example of
s
-contact manifolds is a weak
f
-K-contact manifold, in fact, it is uniform.
By (
2
),
d\eta^{i}=d\eta^{j}\iff h_{i}=h_{j}
for all
1\leq i,j\leq s
.

–

Proposition 4
.

Let
\Omega^{k}(M)
be the space of

k
-forms on an
s
-contact manifold

(M^{2n+s},\vec{\eta},{\mathcal{R}}\oplus{\cal D})
.
If
n\geq 3
, then the map

\mathcal{E}_{d\eta^{i}}:\ \Omega^{2}(M)\longrightarrow\Omega^{4}(M),\,\beta\mapsto d\eta^{i}\wedge\beta
,
is injective for all
i=1,\ldots,s
.

Proof.

We denote by
\Omega^{k}(\mathcal{D})

the space of
k
-forms along
\mathcal{D}
, i.e.,
k
-forms vanishing whenever at least one argument lies in
{\mathcal{R}}
. The proof of the proposition consists of four steps.

Step 1
(Symplecticity on
\mathcal{D}
and Lefschetz injectivity):
By the definition of an
s
-contact manifold,
d\eta^{i}|_{\mathcal{D}}
is nondegenerate at every point
p\in M
.
Each fibre
(\mathcal{D}_{p},d\eta^{i}|_{{\mathcal{D}}_{p}})

is a
2n
-dimensional symplectic vector space, hence
(d\eta^{i}|_{{\mathcal{D}}})^{n}
is nowhere vanishing.
In particular, by
[
6
, Proposition 1.1]
(the algebraic Lefschetz property), the linear maps

\mathcal{E}_{d\eta^{i}|_{\mathcal{D}}}:\,\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{D})\longrightarrow\Omega^{4}(\mathcal{D})
,

\mathcal{E}_{d\eta^{i}|_{{\mathcal{D}}}}:\,\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{D})\longrightarrow\Omega^{3}(\mathcal{D})

are injective for
n\geq 3
and
n\geq 2
, respectively (the latter injectivity, in particular, holds when
n\geq 3
).

Step 2
(Bundle-level decomposition by
\eta
-degree):
At each
p\in M
we have a direct-sum decomposition

T_{p}^{*}M={\mathrm{span}\{\eta^{1}_{p},\dots,\eta^{s}_{p}\}}\ \oplus\ \mathcal{D}_{p}^{*},

which induces a decomposition of exterior powers:

\Lambda^{k}(T_{p}^{*}M)\;=\;\bigoplus\nolimits_{j=0}^{\min(k,s)}\Lambda^{j}(\mathrm{span}\{\eta^{1}_{p},\dots,\eta^{s}_{p}\})\,\wedge\,\Lambda^{k-j}\mathcal{D}_{p}^{*}
.
Since the ranks are constant and the summands depend smoothly on
p
, this yields a decomposition of bundles and spaces of sections:

\Omega^{k}(M)\;=\;\bigoplus\nolimits_{j=0}^{\min(k,s)}{\Lambda^{j}\!\big(\mathrm{span}\{\eta^{1},\dots,\eta^{s}\}\big)}\wedge\Omega^{k-j}(\mathcal{D}).

In particular, every
\beta\in\Omega^{2}(M)
admits a unique splitting

\beta\;=\;\beta_{0}\;+\;\sum\nolimits_{a=1}^{s}\eta^{a}\wedge\mu_{a}\;+\;\sum\nolimits_{1\leq a<b\leq s}f_{ab}\,\eta^{a}\wedge\eta^{b},

(72)

with
\beta_{0}\in\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{D})
,
\mu_{a}\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{D})
and
f_{ab}\in C^{\infty}(M)
.

Step 3
(
d\eta^{i}
has no
\eta
-components):
Expand
d\eta^{i}
according to the same decomposition:

d\eta^{i}\;=\;d\eta^{i}|_{\mathcal{D}}+\sum\nolimits_{a=1}^{s}\eta^{a}\wedge\gamma_{a}\;+\;\sum\nolimits_{1\leq a<b\leq s}h_{ab}\,\eta^{a}\wedge\eta^{b},

with
\gamma_{a}\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{D})
and
h_{ab}\in C^{\infty}(M)
. Let
\{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{s}\}
be a local frame of
{\mathcal{R}}
dual to
\{\eta^{1},\dots,\eta^{s}\}
. Using
\ker\,d\eta^{i}={\mathcal{R}}
, we have
\iota_{\xi_{a}}d\eta^{i}=0
for each
i
and
a
. Contracting the above expansion yields

0=\iota_{\xi_{a}}d\eta^{i}=\ \gamma_{a}\;+\;\sum\nolimits_{b}h_{ab}\,\eta^{b}\;-\;\sum\nolimits_{b}h_{ba}\,\eta^{b}=\ \gamma_{a}\;+\;2\sum\nolimits_{b}h_{ab}\,\eta^{b}.

Since
\{\eta^{b}\}
are pointwise independent, it follows that
\gamma_{a}=0
and
h_{ab}=0
for all
a,b
.
Hence

d\eta^{i}=d\eta^{i}|_{\mathcal{D}}
,
that means that
d\eta^{i}
has no terms involving
\eta^{i}
.

Step 4
(Wedge and compare
\eta
-degrees):
Assume
d\eta^{i}\wedge\beta=0
. Using (
72
),

\displaystyle 0=d\eta^{i}|_{\mathcal{D}}\wedge\beta

\displaystyle={d\eta^{i}|_{\mathcal{D}}\wedge\beta_{0}}+\sum\nolimits_{a=1}^{s}{\eta^{a}\wedge\bigl(d\eta^{i}|_{\mathcal{D}}\wedge\mu_{a}\bigr)}\;+\;\sum\nolimits_{1\leq a<b\leq s}{f_{ab}\,\eta^{a}\wedge\eta^{b}\wedge d\eta^{i}|_{\mathcal{D}}}.

(73)

Since different
\eta
-degrees lie in a direct sum, each component vanishes separately:

\bullet
Zero
\eta
-part (first term of (
73
)):

d\eta^{i}|_{\mathcal{D}}\wedge\beta_{0}=0
in
\Omega^{4}({\mathcal{D}})
.
Now, as
d\eta^{i}|_{\mathcal{D}}\in\Omega^{2}({\mathcal{D}})
and by the decomposition (
72
),

\beta_{0}\in\Omega^{2}({\mathcal{D}})
and since

\mathcal{E}_{d\eta^{i}|_{\mathcal{D}}}:\Omega^{2}({\mathcal{D}})\to\Omega^{4}({\mathcal{D}})
is injective for
n\geq 3
(by
[
6
, Proposition 1.1]
), we get
\beta_{0}=0
.

\bullet
One
\eta
-part: For each
a
,

d\eta^{i}|_{\mathcal{D}}\wedge\mu_{a}=0
in
\Omega^{3}({\mathcal{D}})
.
Again, since
\mathcal{E}_{d\eta^{i}|_{\mathcal{D}}}:\Omega^{1}({\mathcal{D}})\to\Omega^{3}({\mathcal{D}})
is injective
(in particular, for
n\geq 3
),
\mu_{a}=0
for all
a
.

\bullet
Two
\eta
-parts:
From Step 4 we are left with

\sum\nolimits_{1\leq a<b\leq s}f_{ab}\,\eta^{a}\wedge\eta^{b}\wedge d\eta^{i}|_{\mathcal{D}}=0
.
Wedge this identity with
(d\eta^{i}|_{\mathcal{D}})^{\,n-1}
to obtain

\sum\nolimits_{a<b}f_{ab}\,\eta^{a}\wedge\eta^{b}\wedge(d\eta^{i}|_{\mathcal{D}})^{\,n}=0
.
By Step 1,
(d\eta^{i}|_{\mathcal{D}})^{\,n}
is a nowhere-vanishing horizontal top form on
{\mathcal{D}}
.
Fix any point
p\in M
.
Then
\{\,\eta^{a}_{p}\wedge\eta^{b}_{p}\,\}_{a<b}
is a basis of

\Lambda^{2}(\mathrm{span}\{\eta^{1}_{p},\dots,\eta^{s}_{p}\})
, and wedging with

(d\eta^{i}|_{\mathcal{D}})^{\,n}\neq 0

multiplies these basis elements by the same nonzero
horizontal volume form. Hence, the linear independence is preserved, and the equality above
forces
f_{ab}(p)=0
for
a<b
. Since
p\in M
is arbitrary,
f_{ab}\equiv 0
on
M
. Thus,
\beta=\beta_{0}+\sum_{a}\eta^{a}\wedge\mu_{a}+\sum_{a<b}f_{ab}\,\eta^{a}\wedge\eta^{b}=0
, and
\mathcal{E}_{d\eta^{i}}
is injective on
\Omega^{2}(M)
for
n\geq 3
.
∎

.

Let’s illustrate (

Let
(f_{B},\xi_{B},\eta_{B},g_{B})
be the nearly cosymplectic (not cosymplectic, since

) structure on
B^{5}=\mathbb{S}^{5}
obtained as a totally geodesic hypersurface of the nearly Kähler six-dimensional sphere,
see

]
.
Define
F_{B}:=\lambda\,f_{B}
, where
\lambda\,\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, and
\tilde{Q}:=\lambda^{2}\,\mathrm{Id}_{B}+(1-\lambda^{2})\,\eta_{B}\otimes\xi_{B}

Then
(F_{B},\tilde{Q},\xi_{B},\eta_{B},g_{B})
is a weak nearly cosymplectic structure as

(\nabla^{B}_{X}F_{B})X=\lambda\,(\nabla^{B}_{X}f_{B})X=0
for all

, where
\nabla^{B}
is the Levi-Civita connection on
B
.
Note that, since
d\eta_{B}\neq 0
,

(F_{B},\tilde{Q},\xi_{B},\eta_{B},g_{B})

Let
(N^{2n-4},J_{N},g_{N})
be a weak nearly Kähler manifold satisfying
J_{N}^{2}=-\lambda^{2}\operatorname{Id}_{TN}
with
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
Let
\nabla^{N}
be the Levi-Civita connection on
N
.
We equip
M=B^{5}\times N^{2n-4}
with the product metric
g=g_{B}\oplus g_{N}
. Then the tangent bundle splits orthogonally as
TM=TB\oplus TN
, and the Levi-Civita connection on
M
satisfies
\nabla_{X}Y=\big(\nabla^{B}_{X_{B}}Y_{B},\,\nabla^{N}_{X_{N}}Y_{N}\big)
for
X=(X_{B},X_{N})
and
Y=(Y_{B},Y_{N})
.
The mixed derivatives on
(M,g)
disappear since the factors are totally geodesic. For any
X=X_{B}+X_{N}\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}
, we define

\xi:=\xi_{B},\quad\eta(X):=\eta_{B}(X_{B}),\quad f(X):=F_{B}X_{B}+J_{N}X_{N},\quad Q:=\lambda^{2}\,\mathrm{Id}+(1-\lambda^{2})\eta\otimes\xi.

Thus

f
acts as
F_{B}
on the
B^{5}
-factor and as
J_{N}
on the
N^{2n-4}
-factor;

\xi
and
\eta
are lifted from the
B^{5}
-factor;

\eta
annihilates

\eta\,|_{TN}=0.

For
X=X_{B}+X_{N}
, we have

f^{2}(X)=F_{B}^{2}X_{B}+J_{N}^{2}X_{N}=-\lambda^{2}X_{B}-(1-\lambda^{2})\eta_{B}(X_{B})\xi_{B}+\eta_{B}(X_{B})\xi_{B}-\lambda^{2}X_{N}=-QX+\eta(X)\xi.

Hence

is a self-adjoint (1,1)-tensor. Moreover, since

and

g_{B}(F_{B}X_{B},F_{B}Y_{B})=g_{B}(QX_{B},Y_{B})-\eta_{B}(X_{B})\eta_{B}(Y_{B})

g_{N}(J_{N}X_{N},J_{N}Y_{N})=\lambda^{2}\,g_{N}(X_{N},Y_{N})
.
we have the following:

\displaystyle g(fX,fY)=g(QX,Y)-\eta(X)\eta(Y),\quad\eta(\xi)=1,\quad f(\xi)=0,\quad\eta\circ f=0,\quad\eta(X)=g(X,\xi).

This implies that
(f,Q,\xi,\eta,g)
is a weak almost contact metric structure on

M

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}(fX)

\displaystyle=\nabla^{B}_{X_{B}}(F_{B}X_{B})+\nabla^{N}_{X_{N}}(J_{N}X_{N}),\quad\nabla_{X}X=\nabla^{B}_{X_{B}}X_{B}{+}\nabla^{N}_{X_{N}}X_{N}.

(80)

Applying
f
for both sides of the last equation gives
f\nabla_{X}X=F_{B}(\nabla_{X_{B}}X_{B})+J_{N}(\nabla_{X_{N}}X_{N}).
Combining this with (

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}f)X

\displaystyle=(\nabla^{B}_{X_{B}}F_{B})X_{B}+(\nabla^{N}_{X_{N}}J_{N})X_{N}=0.

.

Since

Conclusion

We have shown that the weak nearly
{\cal C}
-structure is useful for studying metric
f
-structures, almost contact metric structures, and Killing vector fields.
Some results on nearly cosymplectic manifolds (see
[
8
,
14
]
)
and weak nearly cosymplectic manifolds (see
[
18
]
) were generalized to weak nearly
{\cal C}
-manifolds satisfying (
9
) and (
13
).
Namely, the splitting Theorem
2
was proven and the
(4+s)
-dimensional case was characterized in Theorem
1
.
Their consequences (without the requirement (
9
) and (
13
)) present new results for nearly
{\cal C}
-manifolds.
Based on the numerous applications of the metric
f
-structure, we expect that the weak
{\cal C}
-structure will also be useful for differential geometry
(e.g., the theory of

-foliations,
s

-contact structures) and theoretical physics.

A multidimensional landscape of the η \eta and η ′ \eta^{\prime} mesons
L. Albino, K. Raya, R. J. Hernández-Pinto, B. Almeida-Zamora, J. Segovia, A. Huet, A. Bashir

Abstract
We employ a recently proposed form-invariant algebraic model for the quark propagator and the Bethe-Salpeter amplitude of pseudoscalar mesons to study the internal structure of η \eta and η ′ \eta^{\prime} mesons. This model facilitates the construction of the Bethe-Salpeter wavefunction, whose projection onto an appropriate flavor-basis leads to the light-front wavefunction for convenient linear combinations of the s ​ s ¯ s\bar{s} and l ​ l ¯ ∼ ( u ​ u ¯ + d ​ d ¯ ) l\bar{l}\sim(u\bar{u}+d\bar{d}) states. Using an overlap representation, we compute the valence-quark generalized parton distributions (GPDs). The construction of the model ensures that this multidimensional quantity is determined entirely by the corresponding valence-quark distribution amplitudes. Once the GPDs are constructed, we carry out a straightforward derivation of other desired physical observables such as the distribution functions and the electromagnetic form factors. We also provide explicit comparisons with available results, demonstrating that the present model offers a consistent physical picture for all ground-state pseudoscalar mesons.

I
Introduction

From a seemingly perspicuous yet naive point of view, ground-state pseudoscalar mesons might be seen as the simplest hadrons; after all, these are merely spin-0 composite systems of negative parity which consist solely of a valence quark-antiquark pair. Expectedly and unsurprisingly, such a simplistic picture turns out to be largely inadequate and insufficient. These states carry the imprint of Nature’s uncanny complexities, especially because of their intricate connection with the most dominant mass generating mechanism for the visible matter in the known universe
Nambu and Jona-Lasinio 1961a
;
Nambu and Jona-Lasinio 1961b
. With the singular exception of the
\eta^{\prime}
-meson, all other ground-state pseudoscalars can be regarded as the Nambu-Goldstone bosons arising from dynamical chiral symmetry breaking. This mechanism is a defining building block of the emergent hadronic mass and it is directly interlinked with the non-perturbative dynamics of quantum chromodynamics (QCD) in the infrared domain
Hatsuda and Kunihiro 1994
;
Roberts et al. 2021
. An intriguing implication of the dynamical chiral symmetry breaking is that in the absence of the mass generating mechanism by the Higgs field, the Nambu-Goldstone bosons would be identically massless. Consequently, the mass and several other structural differences observed for the pseudoscalar mesons can be attributed to the subtle interplay between the weak and the strong mass generating mechanisms. On the other hand, the non-Abelian
U_{A}(1)
anomaly in QCD sets the
\eta^{\prime}
meson apart from the Nambu-Goldstone boson family and provides it with a significant mass even in the chiral limit
Christos 1984
;
Ottnad et al. 2025
. Consequently, as displayed in Fig.
1
from Refs.
Raya et al. 2024
;
Arrington et al. 2021
;
Kuramashi et al. 1994
, the mass composition of the
\eta^{\prime}
, significantly driven by dynamical chiral symmetry breaking, markedly differs from the rest of the ground-state pseudoscalar mesons, resembling more closely that of the proton. This manifestation of the emergent hadron mass indicates that despite the
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
mesons being composed of the same types of valence-quarks, and having strongly intertwined properties, a highly intricate internal dynamics is at play. These distinctive features make the mixed

\eta-\eta^{\prime}
system unique and valuable for understanding the emergence of mass and its connection to the structural characteristics of hadrons
Feldmann and Kroll 1998
;
Agaev et al. 2014
;
Ding et al. 2019
.

Nevertheless, how hadrons are formed and how their properties are dictated by the strong forces of QCD remains largely indiscernible solely from the perturbative picture provided by the QCD Lagrangian; rather, these phenomena are emergent and are inherently connected to the non-perturbative characteristics of this non-Abelian theory. It is essential to employ a robust mathematical framework that can effectively manage the intricacies of the strong interactions, such as lattice QCD
Holligan et al. 2025
;
Riberdy et al. 2024
, and the coupled formalism of Schwinger-Dyson and Bethe-Salpeter equations
Eichmann et al. 2016
;
Qin and Roberts 2020
. The latter approach connects computed hadronic observables to the Green functions of elementary degrees of freedom, namely, quarks and gluons. Moreover, the meson properties are encapsulated within the corresponding Poincaré-covariant Bethe-Salpeter wavefunction (BSWF). This framework has enabled the calculation of a plethora of physically relevant quantities related to mesons, including distribution amplitudes (DAs) and functions (DFs), electromagnetic and gravitational form factors (FFs), as well as generalized parton distributions (GPDs) and light-front wavefunctions (LFWFs) – see
e.g.

Roberts et al. 2021
;
Raya et al. 2024
;
Ding et al. 2023
and references therein.

Alongside these complex QCD-based computations, a series of algebraic models have been developed which aim to streamline the calculations and facilitate the exploration of hadron structural properties without compromising the essential features of the formalism provided by the Schwinger-Dyson and Bethe-Salpeter equations (see, for example
Chang et al. 2013
;
Raya et al. 2016
;
Mezrag et al. 2016
;
Bedolla et al. 2016
;
Xu et al. 2018
;
Sultan et al. 2024
;
Raya et al. 2022
). Broadly speaking, this approach begins from coherent
Ansätze
for the quark propagator and the Bethe-Salpeter amplitude, followed by the subsequent construction of the BSWF and the LFWF
Raya et al. 2022
. The parameters of the models are fixed by exploiting the connection between the LFWF and the experimentally accessible quantities which are deduced from it.

The recently proposed algebraic model, Ref.
Albino et al. 2022
, generalizes several earlier attempts to a maximal form-invariant construction while preserving a plain algebraic relationship between the leading-twist LFWF and its corresponding DA. This permits the BSWF to be rigorously determined from the prior knowledge of the latter. This model has earlier been employed to study the internal structure of light pseudoscalars (
\pi-K
), heavy quarkonia and heavy-light systems
Albino et al. 2022
;
Higuera-Angulo et al. 2024
;
Almeida-Zamora et al. 2024
, with explorations also conducted in the context of vector mesons
Almeida-Zamora et al. 2023
. Herein we intend to expand this series of investigations by considering the mixed
\eta-\eta^{\prime}
states. Thus, we shall apply the algebraic model outlined in the references to compute the LFWFs and GPDs of the
\eta-\eta^{\prime}
bound-states, as well as other directly computable objects, such as distribution functions, electromagnetic form factors and impact parameter space (IPS) GPDs. This allows us to deliver an in-depth perspective on the internal structure of these mesons and completes a unified picture of all ground state pseudoscalar mesons.

III

\eta-\eta^{\prime}

IV
. Finally, Sect.
V
presents a summary of the present analysis and potential future perspectives.

II
An algebraic model for pseudoscalar mesons

In this section, we summarize the key elements of the algebraic model adopted to describe pseudoscalar mesons, as introduced in Ref.
Albino et al. 2022
. Starting from an analytically tractable representation of the meson BSWF, we highlight the efficient computation of the LFWF within this model, the construction of the GPDs, and the subsequent extraction of the DF, the elastic form factors, charge radii, as well as the impact-parameter space GPDs.

II.1
The Model and its Generalities

Consider a generic meson composed of a
q(\bar{h})
-flavored valence-quark (antiquark). The label
M
denotes quantities associated with the corresponding meson. Although our analysis focuses on the
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
mesons, the general expressions presented here are valid for any pseudoscalar meson
M
. The BSWF (
{\chi}_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}
) of the meson is defined as the following product of the corresponding Bethe-Salpeter amplitude, denoted as
\Gamma_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}
,
and the propagators of the valence quark and antiquark,
S_{q}
and
S_{\bar{h}}
, respectively:

\displaystyle{\chi}_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}\left(k_{-},P\right)=S_{q}(k)\Gamma_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}\left(k_{-},P\right)S_{\bar{h}}\left(k-P\right)\,,

(1)

where
k_{-}=k-P/2
and
P^{2}=-m_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{2}
, with
m_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}
being the mass of the meson. Following Refs.
Albino et al. 2022
;
Higuera-Angulo et al. 2024
;
Almeida-Zamora et al. 2024
, we adopt the following
Ansatz
for the quark (antiquark) propagator:

\displaystyle S_{q(\bar{h})}(k)=\left[-i\gamma\cdot k+M_{q(\bar{h})}\right]\mathrm{d}\hskip-1.42262pt\left(k^{2},M_{q(\bar{h})}^{2}\right)\,,

(2)

with
M_{q(\bar{h})}
interpreted as the dynamically generated constituent quark (antiquark) mass and the denominator
\mathrm{d}(s,t)=(s+t)^{-1}
. For pseudoscalar mesons, the model proposes the following form for its Bethe-Salpeter amplitude:

\displaystyle n_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}\Gamma_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}(k,P)=i\gamma_{5}\int_{-1}^{1}dw\,\rho(w)\left[\hat{\mathrm{d}}\left(k_{w}^{2},\Lambda_{w}^{2}\right)\right]^{\nu}\hskip-2.84544pt.

(3)

This choice reflects the consideration of only the leading Bethe-Salpeter amplitude, which is sufficient for many applications, provided an appropriate spectral density,
\rho(w)
. We define
\hat{\mathrm{d}}(s,t)=t\,\mathrm{d}(s,t)
and
k_{w}=k+(w/2)P
.
n_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}
serves as a canonical normalization constant, while the exponent
\nu
governs the asymptotic behavior of the Bethe-Salpeter amplitude, including logarithmic corrections, and may retain a degree of residual freedom depending on the chosen form of the quark propagator. Notwithstanding, to match the expectations from QCD-driven approaches
Maris and Roberts 1997
, our preferred choice is
\nu=1
. The last defining component of the model,
\Lambda_{w}^{2}:=\Lambda^{2}(w)
, encapsulates the interplay between the relevant mass scales (
M_{q,(\bar{h})},\,m_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}
) and the spectral density function (via
w
-dependence), as expressed by:

\Lambda^{2}(w)=M_{q}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\left(1-w^{2}\right)m_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(1-w\right)\left(M_{\bar{h}}^{2}-M_{q}^{2}\right)\,.

(4)

The combination of Eqs. (
1
) - (
4
) enables the BSWF to be conveniently cast as follows:

\displaystyle n_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}\chi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}(k_{-},P)\hskip-2.84544pt=\hskip-2.84544pt\mathcal{M}_{q,\bar{h}}(k,P)\hskip-2.84544pt\int_{-1}^{1}\hskip-5.69046ptdw\,\tilde{\rho}^{\,\nu}_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}(w)\mathcal{D}^{\,\nu}_{q,\bar{h}}(k,P)\,,

(5)

where the profile function,
\tilde{\rho}^{\,\nu}_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}(w):=\rho_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}(w)\,\Lambda_{w}^{2\nu}
, has been defined in terms of the spectral density. Moreover, the Dirac
\gamma
-matrix structure is encoded in (
p=k-P
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{q,\bar{h}}(k,P)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle-\gamma_{5}\big[M_{q}\gamma\cdot P+\gamma\cdot k(M_{\bar{h}}-M_{q})

(6)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mu\nu}k_{\mu}P_{\nu}-i\,(k\cdot p+M_{q}M_{\bar{h}})\big]\,,

and
\mathcal{D}_{q,\bar{h}}^{\,\nu}(k,P)
is a product of quadratic denominators:

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\mathcal{D}_{q,\bar{h}}^{\,\nu}(k,P)\hskip-2.84544pt

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\hskip-2.84544pt\mathrm{d}\left(k^{2},M_{q}^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}\left(k_{w-1}^{2},\Lambda_{w}^{2}\right)^{\nu}\mathrm{d}\left(p^{2},M_{\bar{h}}^{2}\right)\,.

Based on this structure of the BSWF, a well-defined algebraic procedure leads to the following expression:

\displaystyle n_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}\chi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}(k_{-},P)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{q,\bar{h}}(k,P)\int_{0}^{1}d\alpha\mathcal{F}_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}(\alpha,\sigma^{\nu+2})\,,

(7)

where
\sigma=(k-\alpha P)^{2}+\Lambda^{2}_{1-2\alpha}
, and
\alpha
is the usual Feynman parameter. Moreover,
the convenient representation chosen for
\Lambda^{2}(w)
, as given in Eq. (
4
), entails

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}(\alpha,\sigma^{\nu+2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{\nu}(\nu+1)\Big[\int_{-1}^{1-2\alpha}dw\left(\frac{\alpha}{1-w}\right)^{\nu}

(8)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{1-2\alpha}^{1}dw\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{1+w}\right)^{\nu}\Big]\frac{\tilde{\rho}_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{\,\nu}(w)}{\sigma^{\nu+2}}\;.

It is important to stress the fact that, in order to prevent the emergence of poles on the real axis, it is crucial to ensure the positivity of
\Lambda^{2}(w)
. This condition is satisfied as long as:

\displaystyle|M_{\bar{h}}-M_{q}|\leq m_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}\leq M_{\bar{h}}+M_{q}\,.

(9)

It is worth noting that the leftmost side of the inequality is trivially satisfied for isospin-symmetric systems (where
M_{q}=M_{\bar{h}}
) and, indeed, holds for any realistic ground-state bound systems
Raya et al. 2020
;
Xu et al. 2024a
;
Sultan et al. 2021
. The remaining condition of Eq. (
9
) does not pose a problem for Nambu–Goldstone modes, owing to the relatively small values of
m_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}
. However, for other mesons, a careful selection of constituent quark masses is essential, or an alternative approach must be considered. In the following subsection, we show how the Nakanishi integral representation for the BSWF, Eqs. (
7
) and (
8
), yields an analytical relation between the meson LFWF and the DA.

II.2
Light-front wavefunctions

The projection of the meson BSWF onto the light-front results in the associated LFWF. It yields the leading-twist expression for a quark
q
within a generic pseudoscalar meson
M
:

\psi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}\left(x,k_{\perp}^{2}\right)=\text{tr}_{\text{CD}}\int_{dk_{\parallel}}\delta_{n}^{x}(k_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}})\gamma_{5}\gamma\cdot n\,\chi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}(k_{-},P)\,,

(10)

where
\delta_{n}^{x}(k_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}})=\delta(n\cdot k-x\,n\cdot P)
;
n
is a light-like four-vector, such that
n^{2}=0
and
n\cdot P=-m_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}
;
x
corresponds to the light-front momentum fraction carried by the active quark. The symbol
\text{tr}_{\text{CD}}
refers to the trace over color and Dirac indices. The compact and convenient notation
\int_{dk_{\parallel}}:=\int\frac{d^{2}k_{\parallel}}{\pi}
has been employed and the 4-momentum integral is defined as usual:

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}=\left[\frac{1}{16\pi^{3}}\int d^{2}k_{\perp}\right]\left[\frac{1}{\pi}\int d^{2}k_{\parallel}\right]\,.

(11)

It turns out relatively more convenient to consider the Mellin moments of the LFWF:

\displaystyle\langle x^{m}\rangle_{\psi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}dx\,x^{m}\,\psi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}\left(x,k_{\perp}^{2}\right)

(12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{tr}_{\text{CD}}\frac{1}{n\cdot P}\int_{dk_{\parallel}}\left[\frac{n\cdot k}{n\cdot P}\right]^{m}\gamma_{5}\gamma\cdot n\chi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}(k_{-},P)\,.

When adopting the BSWF representation introduced in Sec.
II.1
, a clear sequence of straightforward algebraic steps yields:

\displaystyle\langle x^{m}\rangle_{\psi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}d\alpha\alpha^{m}\left[\frac{12}{n_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}}\frac{\mathcal{Y}_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}(\alpha,\sigma_{\perp}^{\nu+1})}{\nu+1}\right]\,,

(13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}(\alpha,\sigma_{\perp}^{\nu+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}(\alpha,\sigma_{\perp}^{\nu+1})(\alpha M_{\bar{h}}+(1-\alpha)M_{q})\,,

where
\sigma_{\perp}=k_{\perp}^{2}+\Lambda_{1-2\alpha}^{2}
. Moreover, the uniqueness property of the Mellin moments, Eqs. (
10
) and (
12
), establishes a connection between the Feynman parameter
\alpha
and the momentum fraction
x
, allowing for a straightforward identification of the LFWF:

\psi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x,k_{\perp}^{2})=\left[\frac{12}{n_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}}\frac{\mathcal{Y}_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}(x,\sigma_{\perp}^{\nu+1})}{\nu+1}\right]\,.

(14)

Integrating over
k_{\perp}
yields the DA:

\displaystyle f_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}\varphi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x)=\frac{1}{16\pi^{3}}\int d^{2}k_{\perp}\psi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}\left(x,k_{\perp}^{2}\right)\,,

(15)

where
\varphi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x)
is unit normalized and
f_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}
denotes the corresponding weak leptonic decay constant. As might be inferred from Eqs. (
8
) and (
13
), the
k_{\perp}
dependence of
\psi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x,k_{\perp}^{2})
is trivial and the integration follows directly. An advantageous and desirable outcome is the algebraic relation below:

\psi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x,k_{\perp}^{2})=16\pi^{2}f_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}\frac{\nu\Lambda_{1-2x}^{2\nu}}{(k_{\perp}^{2}+\Lambda_{1-2x}^{2})^{\nu+1}}\varphi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x)\,.

(16)

Many crucial implications of this simple algebraic connection between the LFWF and the PDA are discussed in Refs.
Albino et al. 2022
;
Higuera-Angulo et al. 2024
;
Almeida-Zamora et al. 2024
. Here, we emphasize again that with the prior knowledge of the DA, there is no need to construct the spectral density
\rho_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}(w)
in any way to derive the LFWF, albeit the connection between
\rho_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}(w)
and
\varphi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x)
is well-known and direct
Albino et al. 2022
. Only the relevant mass scales, namely
m_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}
and
\,M_{q,\bar{h}}
, need to be determined.

It should be pointed out that the intrinsic resolution scale
\zeta
, at which the LFWF is defined, remains unspecified. Being a leading-twist distribution, it turns out natural to set it as the so-called hadronic scale
\zeta_{H}
, where the hadron’s properties can be adequately captured by the dressed
valence-quark degrees of freedom
Raya et al. 2022
. Therefore, we expect
\zeta_{H}
to be of the order of
\zeta_{H}\sim M_{u}\approx 0.3-0.4
GeV
Lu et al. 2024
;
Yin et al. 2023
;
Xu et al. 2023
. We shall assume this scale from now on.

Another feature of Eq. (
16
) is that the
x-k_{\perp}
correlations are determined by the
\Lambda_{1-2x}^{2}
term, which explicitly takes the form:

\Lambda_{1-2x}^{2}=M_{q}^{2}+x\,(M_{\bar{h}}^{2}-M_{q}^{2})-x(1-x)m_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{2}\,.

(17)

It encapsulates the fact that these correlations are driven by the extent of chiral
M_{q}
and flavor
(M_{\bar{h}}^{2}-M_{q}^{2})
symmetry breaking. In the chiral limit,
\Lambda_{1-2x}^{2}=M_{q}^{2}
, implying

\displaystyle\psi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x,k_{\perp}^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(k_{\perp}^{2})\,\varphi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x)\;,

(18)

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(k_{\perp}^{2})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle 16\pi^{2}f_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}\frac{\nu M_{q}^{2\nu}}{(k_{\perp}^{2}+M_{q}^{2})^{\nu+1}}\,,

(19)

which recovers the chiral limit results from Refs.
Mezrag et al. 2016
;
Chouika et al. 2018
;
Raya et al. 2022
. Note that this corresponds to a separable Ansatz.

Finally, it might be mentioned that to obtain all antiquark related quantities, it is sufficient to replace

\varphi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x)\to\varphi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{\bar{h}}(x)=\varphi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(1-x)\,,

(20)

and exchange the constituent quark masses
M_{\bar{h}}\leftrightarrow M_{q}
. We are now set to compute the GPDs.

II.3
Generalized parton distributions

The valence-quark GPD can be obtained from the overlap representation of the LFWF
Diehl 2003
:

\displaystyle H_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x,\xi,\Delta^{2})\hskip-2.84544pt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hskip-2.84544pt\int\frac{d^{2}k_{\perp}}{16\pi^{3}}\psi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q*}\left(x^{-},(\mathbf{k}_{\perp}^{-})^{2}\right)\psi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}\left(x^{+},(\mathbf{k}^{+}_{\perp})^{2}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69046ptx^{\pm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x\pm\xi}{1\pm\xi}\hskip 11.38092pt,\hskip 11.38092pt\mathbf{k}_{\perp}^{\pm}=k_{\perp}\mp\frac{\Delta_{\perp}}{2}\frac{1-x}{1\pm\xi}\,.

(21)

As before,
x
represents the light-front momentum fraction carried by the struck quark, and
\Delta^{2}
denotes the momentum squared transferred from the probe to the struck hadron. In this context, its transverse component squared is
\Delta_{\perp}^{2}=\Delta^{2}(1-\xi^{2})-4\xi^{2}m_{\text{M}}^{2}
, while
\xi
, referred to as the skewness, is the longitudinal momentum fraction transferred. Both
x
and
\xi
have support on
[-1,1]
, but the overlap representation is only valid in the DGLAP region
|x|>\xi

;
Dall’Olio et al. 2024
. Exploring this domain is sufficient for several quantities of interest such as the distribution functions, electromagnetic form factors, and the impact parameter space GPDs.

Considering the LFWF representation previously derived in Eq. (
16
), it follows directly that the GPD takes the following form:

H_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x,\xi,\Delta^{2})=\mathcal{N}_{H}\left[\frac{\varphi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x^{+})\varphi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x^{-})}{\Lambda_{1-2x^{+}}\Lambda_{1-2x^{-}}}\right]\Phi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x,\xi,\Delta^{2})\,,

(22)

where the normalization constant
\mathcal{N}_{H}
ensures unit normalization in the forward limit, where the associated DF is defined,
i.e.
:

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636ptq_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}(x):=H_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x,0,0)=\mathcal{N}_{H}\frac{[\varphi^{q}_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}(x)]^{2}}{\Lambda_{1-2x}^{2}}\,,

(23)

\displaystyle\hskip-19.91692pt\int_{0}^{1}q_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}(x)\,dx=\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{N}_{H}\frac{[\varphi^{q}_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}(x)]^{2}}{\Lambda_{1-2x}^{2}}\,dx=1\,.

(24)

Notice that the closed form of the relation between DA and DF,

, Eq. (
23

\zeta_{H}

\Phi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}

\Phi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x,\xi,\Delta^{2})=\mathcal{N}_{\Phi}\Lambda_{1-2x^{+}}^{2\nu+1}\Lambda_{1-2x^{-}}^{2\nu+1}\int_{0}^{1}du\frac{u^{\nu}(1-u)^{\nu}}{[\mathbb{M}^{2}(u)]^{2\nu+1}}\,,

where

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}^{2}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z\,u(1-u)+u(\Lambda^{2}_{1-2x^{+}}-\Lambda^{2}_{1-2x^{-}})+\Lambda^{2}_{1-2x^{-}}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\Phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(2\nu+2)}{\Gamma^{2}(\nu+1)}\,,\,z:=\frac{(1-x)^{2}}{(1-\xi^{2})^{2}}\Delta_{\perp}^{2}\,.

(26)

It is also noteworthy that for specific values of
\nu>-1
, the integral in Eq. (
25

(c.l.)

\displaystyle H_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x,\xi,\Delta^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{H}\left[\frac{\varphi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x^{+})\varphi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x^{-})}{M_{q}^{2}}\right]\Phi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(z)

(28)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{H}\sqrt{q_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}(x^{+})q_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}(x^{-})}\,\Phi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(z)\,.

The second line is merely a consequence of the definition of the DF, Eq. (
23
), whose chiral limit evaluation yields:

q_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}(x;\zeta_{H})\propto[\varphi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x;\zeta_{H})]^{2}\,.

(29)

Notice that we explicitly reinstate the model scale
\zeta_{H}
here which, as mentioned before, is implicitly employed throughout this manuscript. The reason is that DA and DF evolve in a different manner. Thus, Eq. (
29
) would not be valid beyond the model scale.
Moreover, the relationship between the distributions, established by Eqs. (
28
) and (
29
), is a defining feature of systems characterized by a factorized LFWF, Eq. (
18
). For this family of Ansätze, regardless of the specific shape of
\Phi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(z)
and
\tilde{\varphi}_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(k_{\perp}^{2})
, an array of additional algebraic outcomes follow
Raya et al. 2022
;
Raya et al. 2025
.

II.4
Electromagnetic form factors

Electromagnetic form factors are the zeroth Mellin moment of the GPD. Explicitly, the contribution of the valence-quark
q
to the FF is given by:

F_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(\Delta^{2})=\int_{0}^{1}dx\,H_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x,\xi=0,\Delta^{2})\,,

(30)

so that the complete result follows from adding up the individual components:

F_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}(\Delta^{2})\equiv e_{q}F_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(\Delta^{2})+e_{\bar{h}}F_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{\bar{h}}(\Delta^{2})\,;

(31)

clearly,
e_{q}
and
e_{\bar{h}}
are the valence-quark charges in units of the positron’s electric charge. The integration domain in Eq. (
30
) reflects our choice of skewless GPD,
\xi=0
, since the electromagnetic form factor is independent of this variable
Mezrag 2023
. The associated charge radius
r_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}
is defined as usual:

\displaystyle(r_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{q}(r_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q})^{2}+e_{\bar{h}}(r_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{\bar{h}})^{2}\,,

(32)

\displaystyle(r_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q})^{2}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle-6\frac{\partial F_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(\Delta^{2})}{\partial\Delta^{2}}\Bigg|_{\Delta^{2}=0}\,.

(33)

Setting
\xi\hskip-2.27621pt=\hskip-2.27621pt0
and expanding Eq. (
22
) around
\Delta^{2}\hskip-2.84526pt=\hskip-1.70717pt\Delta_{\perp}^{2}\hskip-2.84526pt\approx\hskip-1.70717pt0
:

\displaystyle H_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x,0,\Delta^{2})

\displaystyle\overset{\Delta^{2}\to 0}{\approx}

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{H}\frac{[\varphi_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x)]^{2}}{\Lambda_{1-2x}^{2}}\Big[1-\mathfrak{L}_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x)\Delta^{2}+...\Big]\,,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{(1+\nu)(1+2\nu)}{2(3+2\nu)}\frac{(1-x)^{2}}{\Lambda_{1-2x}^{2}}\,.

(34)

It follows that

(r_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q})^{2}=6\,\int_{0}^{1}dx\;\mathfrak{L}_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x)q_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}(x)\,.

(35)

The antiquark component is obtained in an analogous way. In quarkonium systems
F_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}=0
and
r_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}=0
, since we have
e_{q}+e_{\bar{h}}=0
. Therefore, when presenting electromagnetic FFs and charge radii, we shall only consider the individual flavor contribution (
F_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}\to F_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}
and
r_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}\to r_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}
). Moreover, if the charge radius is known experimentally, it can be employed to tune the mass scales required to define the algebraic model aligned with phenomenology.

II.5
Impact parameter space GPDs

The impact parameter space GPD can be obtained in a straightforward manner by carrying out the Fourier transform of the zero-skewness GPD
Burkardt 2000
:

\displaystyle\textbf{q}_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}(x,|b_{\perp}|)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\Delta}{2\pi}\Delta J_{0}(|b_{\perp}|\Delta)H_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x,0,\Delta^{2})\,,

(36)

where
J_{0}
denotes the cylindrical Bessel function. Naturally, this distribution represents
the probability density of finding a parton with momentum fraction
x
at a transverse distance
|b_{\perp}|
from the center of transverse momentum of the meson under study.

As suggested in Ref.
Albino et al. 2022
, we consider the following approximation for the zero-skewness GPD:

H_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x,0,\Delta^{2})=q_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}(x)\;\text{exp}[-\mathfrak{L}_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x)\Delta^{2}]\,.

(37)

This particular form provides additional algebraic benefits that enhance those of the present model
Raya et al. 2025
. In particular, it allows for a straightforward derivation of the IPS-GPD (here
\mathcal{I}_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x,|b_{\perp}|):=2\pi|b_{\perp}|\textbf{q}_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}(x,|b_{\perp}|)
):

\mathcal{I}_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x,|b_{\perp}|)=\frac{q_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}(x)}{2\mathfrak{L}_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x)}|b_{\perp}|\,\text{exp}\left[-\frac{|b_{\perp}|^{2}}{4\mathfrak{L}_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x)}\right]\,.

(38)

As is the case with all other distributions, the form of
\mathcal{I}_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}(x,|b_{\perp}|)
is expected to reflect the influence of mass generation mechanisms. This connection arises naturally because the IPS-GPD is explicitly built from the parton distribution function which encodes the longitudinal momentum dependence. Consequently, the broadness of
\mathcal{I}_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{q}
in transverse space and the height of its peak directly trace the behavior of
q_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}(x)
, providing insight into the spatial distribution of partons and its correlation with dynamical mass generation
Raya et al. 2022
;
Raya et al. 2025
.

III
The
\eta-\eta^{\prime}
case

For the description of the
\eta-\eta^{\prime}
mesons, we shall consider the
SU(2)
flavor symmetry, i.e., the isospin symmetry which entails
M_{u}=M_{d}\equiv M_{l}
. This approximation results in the complete decoupling of the neutral pion from the intertwined system of
\eta-\eta^{\prime}
mesons, which features
s\bar{s}
components
Bhagwat et al. 2007
. We follow Ref.
Ding et al. 2019
and express these physical states in a generic flavor basis:

\chi_{\eta,\eta^{\prime}}(q,P)=\mathcal{T}_{l}\chi_{l}^{\eta,\eta^{\prime}}(q,P)+\mathcal{T}_{s}\chi_{s}^{\eta,\eta^{\prime}}(q,P)\,,

(39)

with
\mathcal{T}_{l}=\text{diag}(1/2,1/2,0)
and
\mathcal{T}_{s}=\text{diag}(0,0,1/\sqrt{2})
. Consequently, the mixed
\eta-\eta^{\prime}
system is fully characterized by four BSWFs:
\chi_{l,s}^{\eta,\eta^{\prime}}(q,P)
.

We choose to work with the single-angle mixing scheme (SA-MS) approximation
Feldmann et al. 1999
;
Feldmann et al. 1998
:

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\eta\\
\eta^{\prime}\end{array}\right)=U(\theta)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}(u\bar{u}+d\bar{d})/\sqrt{2}\\
s\bar{s}\end{array}\right)=U(\theta)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\eta_{l}\\
\eta_{s}\end{array}\right)\,,

where
\theta
is the mixing angle and

\displaystyle U(\theta)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\
\sin\theta&\quad\cos\theta\end{array}\right)\,.

Here
\eta_{l}
and
\eta_{s}
denote pure
l\bar{l}\sim(u\bar{u}+d\bar{d})
and
s\bar{s}
orthonormal states, respectively. Eq. (
III
) implies the following mixing for the corresponding BSWFs:

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\chi_{\eta}(q,P)\\
\chi_{\eta^{\prime}}(q,P)\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U(\theta)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\chi_{\eta_{l}}(q,P)\\
\chi_{\eta_{s}}(q,P)\end{array}\right)\,.

Clearly,
\theta=0
implies the physical
\eta-\eta^{\prime}
mesons would become identical to the
\eta_{l}
and
\eta_{s}
states, respectively, and
\eta_{l}
would exhibit pion-like characteristics. In the chiral limit, this would correspond identically to a massless pion. In the realistic scenario,
\theta\neq 0
provides a measure of the appropriate degree of mixing between the
\eta_{l}
and
\eta_{s}
states to produce the observed
physical states
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
, indicating a measure of the flavor content in the physical
\eta-\eta^{\prime}
mesons.

One of the key advantages of representing the
\eta-\eta^{\prime}
mesons in the SA-MS is that extra subtleties are avoided concerning the positivity condition in Eq. (
9
). Note that on adopting the representation from Eq. (
39
), the substantial mass of the
\eta^{\prime}
meson (
m_{\eta^{\prime}}=0.96
GeV) would expectedly lead to a violation of the positivity condition, as realistic values for
M_{u,s}
typically range from
0.3
to
0.6
GeV
Sultan et al. 2021
. SA-MS circumvents this problem. Assuming SA-MS is a fair approximation, one can readily establish a mapping between bases:

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\chi_{\eta_{l}}&0\\
0&\chi_{\eta_{s}}\end{array}\right)=U^{-1}(\theta)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\chi_{\eta}^{l}&\chi_{\eta}^{s}\\
\chi_{\eta^{\prime}}^{l}&\chi_{\eta^{\prime}}^{s}\end{array}\right)\,,

which translates into

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}f_{\eta_{l}}&0\\
0&f_{\eta_{s}}\end{array}\right)=U^{-1}(\theta)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}f_{\eta}^{l}&f_{\eta}^{s}\\
f_{\eta^{\prime}}^{l}&f_{\eta^{\prime}}^{s}\end{array}\right)\,.

Evidently,
f_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}^{(q)}
denotes the corresponding decay constants of the
M
-meson. Equipped with the pseudoscalar meson BSWF and the mixing scheme, we can derive a coherent and adequate representation of the corresponding LFWF.

Setting
\zeta_{H}
as the resolution scale, the Fock-space expansion of the
\eta-\eta^{\prime}
bound-states follows directly from Eq. (
III
). Therefore, the mixed system is completely determined by the mixing angle
\theta
and the LFWFs of the states
\eta_{l}
and
\eta_{s}
. Naturally, these retain the same form as given in Eq. (
16
):

\psi_{\eta_{l}[\eta_{s}]}^{l[s]}(x,k_{\perp}^{2})=16\pi^{2}f_{\eta_{l},\eta_{s}}\frac{\nu\Lambda_{1-2x}^{2\nu}}{(k_{\perp}^{2}+\Lambda_{1-2x}^{2})^{\nu+1}}\varphi_{\eta_{l}[\eta_{s}]}^{l[s]}(x)\;.

(65)

The input of
\varphi_{\eta_{l}[\eta_{s}]}^{l[s]}(x)
is thus required. However, realistic DAs regarding the
\eta-\eta^{\prime}
system have only been reported in the generic flavor basis of Eq. (
39
), Ref.
Ding et al. 2019
. Therefore, we consider the mapping in Eq. (
III
) to produce the following relations among LFWFs:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\hskip-11.38109pt\psi_{\eta_{l}}^{l}(x,k^{2}_{\perp})=\cos\theta\,\psi^{l}_{\eta}(x,k^{2}_{\perp})+\sin\theta\,\psi^{l}_{\eta^{\prime}}(x,k^{2}_{\perp})\,,\\
\\
\psi_{\eta_{s}}^{s}(x,k^{2}_{\perp})=-\sin\theta\,\psi^{s}_{\eta}(x,k^{2}_{\perp})+\cos\theta\,\psi^{s}_{\eta^{\prime}}(x,k^{2}_{\perp})\,.\end{array}

On the other hand, according to Eq. (
15
), the integration over
k_{\perp}
yields:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}f_{\eta_{l}}\,\varphi_{\eta_{l}}^{l}(x)=\cos\theta\,f^{l}_{\eta}\varphi^{l}_{\eta}(x)+\sin\theta\,f^{l}_{\eta^{\prime}}\varphi^{l}_{\eta^{\prime}}(x)\,,\\
\\
f_{\eta_{s}}\,\varphi_{\eta_{s}}^{s}(x)=-\sin\theta\,f^{s}_{\eta}\varphi^{s}_{\eta}(x)+\cos\theta\,f^{s}_{\eta^{\prime}}\varphi^{s}_{\eta^{\prime}}(x)\,.\end{array}

Subsequently, using Eq. (
III
), the
f_{\eta,\eta^{\prime}}^{l,s}
decay constants can be cast in terms of
f_{\eta_{l},\eta_{s}}
, thus producing:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\varphi_{\eta_{l}}^{l}(x)=\cos^{2}\theta\,\varphi^{l}_{\eta}(x)+\sin^{2}\theta\,\varphi^{l}_{\eta^{\prime}}(x)\,,\\
\\
\varphi_{\eta_{s}}^{s}(x)=\sin^{2}\theta\,\varphi^{s}_{\eta}(x)+\cos^{2}\theta\,\varphi^{s}_{\eta^{\prime}}(x)\,.\end{array}

The above expressions are particularly useful. These explicitly allow the
a priori
unknown
\eta_{l,s}
DAs to be formulated in terms of the well-established
\varphi_{\eta,\eta^{\prime}}^{l,s}
. The remaining task is to determine the masses of the constituent quarks
M_{u,s}
, of the
\eta_{l}
and
\eta_{s}
system, and compute the GPDs as well as the associated observables by employing the theoretical tools developed in this section. This procedure shall be addressed in the next section.

V
Summary

We have employed a form-invariant algebraic model to describe the mixed
\eta-\eta^{\prime}
system. This framework has been successfully applied to ground-state pseudoscalars
Albino et al. 2022
;
Almeida-Zamora et al. 2024
, as well as ground-state vector mesons
Almeida-Zamora et al. 2023
. Its extension to the
\eta-\eta^{\prime}
mesons, presented in this work, yields a physically congruent picture.

Firstly, the obtained mixing angle and decay constants are found to be fully compatible with previous results,
Ding et al. 2019
. Moreover, the resulting DAs of the physical states exhibit the expected pattern: they reveal an asymptotic-like profile, with those corresponding to the strange quark being narrower. As has already been established, DAs for pions and kaons are broad, while those for states with at least one heavy quark are more compressed. Therefore, this proximity to the asymptotic profile highlights that systems with
s\bar{s}
components mark a boundary between strong and weak mass generation mechanisms
Raya et al. 2024
.

The DFs of the valence quarks follow a similar pattern. The one corresponding to the
\eta_{l}
state is broader than that of
\eta_{s}
, while the parton-like profile lies in between these curves. This behavior is attributed to the emergence of an
x-k_{\perp}
correlation in their respective LFWFs, which in turn is influenced by the mass of the state in question. Unsurprisingly, the associated GPDs show similar compression/dilation patterns, with the
\eta_{s}
system displaying a less pronounced
\Delta^{2}
evolution.

A complementary perspective comes from electromagnetic FFs, where heavier systems exhibit a slower decay. This behavior reflects an inverse relationship with the mass of the system and its decay constant, which corresponds to its smaller spatial extent
Raya et al. 2025
. Remarkably, the product
f_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}\cdot r_{\scriptsize{\hbox{M}}}
seems to remain constant up to the mass sector of the charm quark.

Another perspective on the spatial structure is provided by the IPS-GPDs, demonstrating once again that heavier states are more spatially compressed but reaching higher peaks at smaller transverse separations. Moreover, as the meson mass increases, the current model is fully capable of capturing the anticipated point-like limiting behavior.
Thus, based on the quantities analyzed here, the extension of the algebraic model to the mixed
\eta-\eta^{\prime}
system yields remarkably robust results. Moving forward, the aim is to examine complementary physical quantities of interest, including the
\eta,\eta^{\prime}\to\gamma^{*}\gamma^{(*)}
transition form factors, in addition to the corresponding excited states.

Influence of Bʹ-site vacancy on hydrogen storage, structural, elastic, thermodynamic and optoelectronic attributes of potassium based double perovskite K2LiAlH6hydride for green energy applications
Authors: Hudabia Murtaza, Mohamed A Habib, Quratul Ain, Abhinav Kumar, Junaid Munir, Ahmed B M Ibrahim, Ankit Dilipkumar Oza, Mokhtar S S Al-Salimi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractMaterials with a greater propensity to store hydrogen have drawn a lot of attention in recent decade because of their possible uses in clean energy systems. By offering effective, sustainable, and eco-friendly substitutes for conventional fossil fuels, these materials are essential in tackling the world’s energy problems. We have analyzed the influence of -site vacancy on hydrogen storage abilities of potassium-based K2LiAlH6 double perovskite by employing the first-principles technique. For both K2LiAlH6 and K2LiH6, structural and thermo-dynamical stability is attested by computing their volume optimization, tolerance factors and formation energies. The elastic constants reveal a significant reduction towards the external strains when vacancy is created at -site. The mechanical properties imply that K2LiAlH6 with or without the vacancy at -site, the material possesses brittle characteristics. The electronic properties elaborates that K2LiAlH6 possesses an indirect bandgap of 4.16 eV, whereas for K2LiH6 metallic nature is observed. K2LiAlH6 reveals stronger polarization in the high energy region, whereas K2LiH6 reports higher dispersion in the IR region as predicated via their optical analysis. The hydrogen storage abilities reveal a significant increase in the gravimetric densities which are evaluated from 4.83 to 6.17 wt% and a modest increase is noticed in volumetric densities, which are computed from 41.85 to 44.76(gH2/L) with the creation of -site vacancy in K2LiAlH6. It ultimately fulfills the United States department of energy criteria and has indicated its capability to be utilized for hydrogen storage.Keywords: Defected double perovskite, Hydrogen storage, Optical properties, Renewable energySubject terms: Energy science and technology, Materials science, Physics

Introduction

One crucial step for a more sustainable future is the switch from energy usage from fossil fuels to energy consumption from sources of renewable energy1. The excessive and overuse of the energy consumption produced via fossil fuels has seriously damaged human health and has been extremely harmful for our environment2,3. Due to concerns about climate change brought on by the ongoing rise in the use of conventional fuels, society is looking for alternatives to cut down on dangerous carbon emissions. Furthermore, it is anticipated that natural gas and oil supplies would drastically decline as global energy use rises rapidly4,5. Utilizing renewable energy sources can help reduce the amount of fossil fuels used and encourage the energy revolution and change. However, it is challenging to incorporate electricity produced by such renewable energy sources into the grids due to the climate and geographic constraints6. The hydrogen energy is considered the promising energy carrier despite the fact that many potential new energies are being researched because of its high energy density, sustainability, and environmental friendliness, among other factors7. Reliance on non-renewable energy sources, especially fossil fuels, can be reduced by using H28. Storing hydrogen at high pressure in the form of gas, freezing it at cryogenic temperatures, and storing it in the materials are the three possible ways of storing hydrogen energy. The storage of hydrogen via pressure is widely used, but it is delayed by its lower hydrogen density and safety issues9. The manufacturing cost for the insulating conductors is extremely high, due to which the cryogenic liquid hydrogen storage is not reliable10. In a solid-state storage mechanism, the hydrogen atoms are coupled with the hydrogen atom to form a hydride or are absorbed in the material11–13. Among all the hydrogen storing techniques, the solid-state hydrogen methodology is considered extremely effective due to its high purity and storage density, higher safety, low pressure requirements, and prolonged service life14. Perovskite and double perovskite materials are classified as one of the most prominent and effective hydrogen-storing materials because of their capability to store and release high hydrogen energy15. Alkali-based perovskite hydrides have been of interest as alkali metals possess weak van der Walls interaction with hydrogen atoms, which aids in high uptake of hydrogen in them16. Overcoming obstacles like high thermodynamic stability and slow kinetic rates related to chemical reactions is essential since storage and release procedures usually entail these kinds of events. This calls for the investigation of novel materials that can improve hydrogen storage’s viability and efficiency. For example ZRH3 (Z = K, Li, Rb; R = Ca, Sr)17, Cs2XGaH6 (X = Al, Na)18, KTiH3, RbTiH3 and CsTiH319, BaZH3 (Z = V, Cu, Zn)20, XMoH3 (X = Rb, Cs)21, XSnH3 (X = K, Li)22, BeXH3 (X: Al, Ga, and In)23, XSiH3 (X = Li, K, Na, Mg)24, CsXH3 (X = Co, Zn)25, MgZH3 (Z = Al, Sc and Zr)26, ZPtH3 (Z = Li, Rb, Na, K)27, ZMnH3 (Z = Na, K, Rb)28, NaZH3 (Z = Ti, Cu)29, XCuH3 (X = Ca and Sr)30, MgX3H8 (X = Sc, Ti, Zr)31 and AMgH3 (A = Be, Ca)32 have shown that these perovskites are stable and are effective materials for hydrogen storage and fuel cells. The 2D metallic form of boron exhibits remarkable mechanical and superconductive behavior which reflects that it can be used as catalyst in during the synthesis of hydrogen33. The hydrogen diffusion mechanism of monolayer β12-borophene implies that site “A” reveals the lowest adsorption energy and diffusion barrier which further reduces as H is replaced by H234. The first principles investigation of Nb2C MXene phases reveals that hexagonal Nb2C is dynamically unstable and three other orthorhombic phases are stable35. In AMMgH3 (X = Li, Na, K, Rb), the hydrogen storage ability reveals that LiMgH3 possesses the highest gravimetric density36. The catalytic activity of borophene for hydrogen evolution is enhanced by a sibgle atom37. The physical attributes of four different phases of LiAlH4 and hydrogen storage properties of LiBHx (x = 1 and 4) has been studied7,38. The first principles analysis of A2NaMoI6 (A = Cs, Rb) reveals direct bandgaps with high ZT values which reveals that these materials can be effectively used for thermoelectric applications39. The mechanical properties of X2LiSbI6 (X = K, Cs) reveals that Cs2LiSbI6 is brittle and K2LiSbI6 ductile. Their suitable band gaps, strong optical absorption, and high thermoelectric efficiency (zT ≈ 0.7) highlight their potential for photovoltaic, optoelectronic, and energy-harvesting applications40. DFT-simulations with PBE-GGA and mBJ potentials are used to examine the structural, magnetic, optical, and thermoelectric properties of halide double perovskites Cs2KMoX6 (X = Cl, I). Both compounds are stable, ferromagnetic semiconductors with direct band gaps and strong UV absorption. High Seebeck coefficients and mechanical stability indicate their strong potential for thermoelectric, optical, and data storage applications41. K2AMoI6 (A = Rb, Na) reports that these halides are stable and possess direct bandgaps. Their strong optical absorption in the UV region and ZT value of 0.75 implies that these halides are suitable for thermoelectric applications42. This study uses first-principles calculations with the mBJ functional to analyze the structural, electronic, optical, and thermoelectric properties of X2ScHgCl6 (X = Cs, Rb). Both compounds are thermodynamically and mechanically stable ionic semiconductors with direct band gaps and strong optical conductivity extending into the UV range, making them promising for solar energy applications. Their high ZT values (~ 0.9 at 50 K) further demonstrate potential for low-temperature thermoelectric devices43. The physical attributes of XInH3 (X = Rb, Cs) reports metallic and anisotropic behavior. The gravimetric storage capacities reveals that both materials are suitable candidates for storing hydrogen44. The first principles calculation reveals that YCrH3 (Y = Ca, Sr, Br) are metallic and possess gravimetric density in the range of 1.55 wt% to 3.08 wt%45. The hydrogen storage attributes of XCo3H8 (X = Mg, Ca, Sr) reveals excellent hydrogen storage capacities and moderate desorption temperatures46. DFT simulations show that X2VH4 (X = Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba) exhibit strong promise for hydrogen storage applications is shown by their moderate desorption temperatures (241–315 K) and substantial hydrogen storage capacities (5.72–1.82 wt%)47. The DFT-simulations of XFeH3 (X = Ca, Sr, Ba) hydrides reports that these hydrides are mechanically, thermodynamically, and dynamically stable48. The US Department of Energy emphasizes the importance of hydrogen as a pure and maintainable energy carrier for meeting global energy demands and lowering carbon emissions. Efficient hydrogen storage is critical to reaching its full promise in fuel cell technologies, transportation, and renewable energy systems. The growth of new hydrogen storage materials that can attain high densities, operate at low pressures, and work successfully at ambient temperatures is consistent with the DOE’s goals of guaranteeing safe, lightweight, and cost-effective storage systems. These innovations improve hydrogen consumption by enhancing storage efficiency and practicality, hence facilitating the transition to a low-carbon economy. They help to ensure energy security while also promoting environmental sustainability. Despite significant progress in the assessment of the physical attributes of double perovskite hydrides, limited attention has been given to the role of cation vacancies in alkali-based double perovskite hydrides. The majority of previous studies have been on transition-metal or alkaline-earth-based perovskites; however, the systematic impact of -site vacancies on physical attributes is still completely unknown. Gaining knowledge about how -site removal impacts thermodynamic stability and hydrogen release behavior might help optimize hydrogen storage efficiency through defect engineering. This work examined the comparison of physical traits of K2LiAlH6 along with the impact of adding a vacancy at the -site in the double K2LiAlH6 perovskite hydride, with an emphasis on its hydrogen storage capacity. Our findings show that introducing a vacancy in the material can significantly increase the material’s hydrogen storage capability, providing important evidence for the designing of hydrogen storage materials with high efficiency.

Computational approach

One of the most popular and widely used computational techniques for examining the physical characteristics of solid materials is density functional theory49. Two key theorems developed by Hohenberg and Kohn, which show that a system’s electron density is the only factor that can determine its ground-state energy, form the basis of DFT50. By employing electron density as the primary variable, DFT streamlines the issue in contrast to wave function-based approaches that call for intricate computations involving many-body wave functions51. For the assessment of the physical traits of K2LiAlH6 and K2LiH6 the FP-LAPW approach is utilized, which is embedded in Wien2K. It offers high accuracy in resolving the Kohn-Sham equations by providing accurate results of the electronic properties of hydrides. The electronic interaction for K2LiAlH6 and K2LiH6 is treated by utilizing the mBJ approximation. For optimization and attaining the structural traits of K2LiAlH6 and K2LiH6, the GGA is employed. The SCF calculations are performed by selecting the criteria of charge and energy convergence as 0.0001e/(a.u.)3 per unit cell and 0.1 mRy. The RMTKmax, Lmax and Gmax are tuned as 3, 12 and 20 (a.u)−1. To prevent charge leakage, the cut-off energy is tuned to -7 Ryd. For the atomic spheres of K, Li, Al, H, the RMT is selected as 2.49, 2.1, 2.22 and 0.89 (a.u)−1. For the integration of the crystal cell in the reciprocal space, a K-mesh of 2000 K points was carefully chosen to compute the results of SCF for K2LiAlH6 and K2LiH6. The pictorial representations of the crystal structures and atomic Wyckoff positions for K2LiAlH6 and K2LiH6 are analyzed via the VESTA software. For the assessment of the mechanical traits for K2LiAlH6 and K2LiH6 the compliance matrix is simplified to C11, C12 and C44. The optical behavior of K2LiAlH6 and K2LiH6 is done by employing Kramer’s complex equations.

Structural integrity and optimization

Structural study is essential to comprehending and maximizing their performance for real-world applications since these changes can directly affect their stability, reversibility, and hydrogen storage capacity52. Since hydride materials frequently experience structural fluctuations during the processes of hydrogen absorption and desorption, their structural properties are usually of great interest. K2LiAlH6 and K2LiH6 possess the general formula of A2BH6 and A2BH6, in which “K” occupies the A-site, “Li” occupies the B-site, and “-site” is occupied by “Al.” Both studied hydrides are cubic and possess Fmm space group. The Wyckoff positions for K2LiAlH6 are observed as “K” 8c(0.25,0.25,0.25), “Li” at 4b(0,0,0.5), “Al” at 4a(0,0,0), and H is situated at 24e(0,0,0.241), whereas for K2LiH6 the atomic positions are “K” is 8c(0.25,0.25,0.25), “Li” is 4b(0,0,0.5), and H is 24e(0,0,0.241). Mechanical properties

Elastic constants are vital metrics for measuring a material’s mechanical stability59. They offer crucial information about the connection between a material’s strength under stress or deformation and its dynamical behavior and mechanical response. For K2LiAlH6 and K2LiH6, C11, C12 and C44 are computed via Thomas Charpin’s method. These constants reflect different material properties: they are linked to shear elasticity and shape deformation, respectively, and resistance to length changes60. The obtained value of the elastic constants for K2LiH6 is comparatively lower than K2LiAlH6, which implies that creating a vacancy at -site drastically influences the material’s behavior towards the external pressures. The evaluated values of the elastic constants for K2LiAlH6 and K2LiH6 are obtained higher than zero, which implies that Born’s stability criteria are completely justified. The Born’s stability criterion is mentioned as: C11 > 0, C11-C12 > 0, C44 > 0, C12 < B < C11, C11 + 2C12 > 0. Potential applications and future directions

The current first-principles study of K2LiAlH6 and its vacancy version K2LiH6 sheds light on the connection between thermodynamic stability, cation vacancies, and hydrogen storage efficiency. Practically speaking, our results imply that vacancy engineering may be a useful tactic for modifying the properties of hydrogen desorption in complex hydrides. K2LiH6 is a viable contender for medium-temperature hydrogen storage systems because of its altered electronic structure and metallic behavior, which may improve hydrogen release kinetics despite its lower thermodynamic stability. The controlled synthesis of such vacancy-rich perovskites by techniques like high-temperature solid-state processes or mechanochemical milling in a hydrogen atmosphere may be the focus of future experimental endeavors. To confirm the predicted structural and electronic alterations upon “Al-vacancy”, in-situ characterization methods should be utilized. Beyond hydrogen storage, these hydrides tunable electrical and optical properties show promise for energy conversion and optoelectronic applications, where metastable or defect-engineered states can be used to enhance performance. As a result, this work extends our knowledge of defect effects in perovskite hydrides and offers a theoretical basis for their multifunctional applications and experimental realization.

Conclusion

The potential applications of materials with a higher capacity to store hydrogen in renewable energy systems have garnered significant interest in recent years. We have examined in this work how the B’-site vacancy affects the hydrogen storage and other properties of the potassium-based double perovskite K2LiAlH6. The volume optimization plots, negative formation energy values, and computed tolerance factor values for K2LiAlH6 and K2LiH6 imply that the structural integrity is not compromised and both materials possess cubic symmetry. The mechanical parameters such as bulk, young have, and shear modulus report a significant decline in values with the removal of the “Al” atom, which implies that materials resistive nature has been conceded. Furthermore, the mechanical properties signify that K2LiAlH6 is a brittle material regardless of whether there is a vacancy at the B’-site. According to the electronic properties, K2LiAlH6 has an indirect bandgap of 4.16 eV, while K2LiH6 exhibits a metallic nature. According to their optical investigation, K2LiH6 reports larger dispersion in the IR region, whereas K2LiAlH6 exhibits stronger polarization in the UV region. With the formation of the B’-site vacancy in K2LiAlH6, the hydrogen storage capabilities show a notable increase in the volumetric densities, which are calculated from 41.85 to 44.76 ρ (gH2/L), and the gravimetric densities, which are evaluated from 4.83 to 6.17 wt%. This ultimately satisfies the US DOE criteria and has shown promise for use in hydrogen storage.

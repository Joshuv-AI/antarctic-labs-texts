Interpolative Metrics Are Not New: A Study of Generalized Contractions in b b -Suprametric Spaces
Hassan Khandani

Abstract
Researchers recently introduced interpolative metric spaces and established fixed point theorems in this setting. We demonstrate that these metrics are a special case of b b -metrics. On the other hand, suprametrics and b b -suprametrics have also been introduced, and we show that b b -suprametric spaces generalize b b -metric spaces. We establish corresponding results previously presented in interpolative metric spaces in the framework of b b -suprametric spaces with weaker conditions.

Introduction

The Banach contraction principle is a cornerstone of fixed point theory, with numerous extensions and applications in nonlinear analysis and applied mathematics. While classical metric spaces rely on the strict triangle inequality, many real-world applications involve relaxed notions of distance, where this inequality is weakened. These generalized metrics, such as
b
-metrics and suprametrics, have become central to modern research. Establishing versions of the Banach contraction principle in these settings remains an active area of investigation. In this study, we present fixed point results for generalized contractions within these metric frameworks.

The concept of
b
-metrics was introduced by Bakhtin
Bakhtin 1989
, who defined quasi-metrics and extended the Banach fixed point theorem to these spaces. Later, Czerwik
Czerwik 1993
formalized the notion of
b
-metrics and extended Matkowski’s fixed point theorem to
b
-metric spaces (see also
Kajántó and
Lukács 2018
). For further details on
b
-metric spaces, we refer the reader to
Karapınar 2018
;
Berinde and
Păcurar 2022
.

Berzig
Berzig 2022
introduced suprametrics, defined by the inequality (
1
) in Definition
1
with
s=1
, and demonstrated their applications to nonlinear matrix and integral equations. Building on this work, Berzig
Berzig 2024
defined
b
-suprametrics, a novel framework that generalizes and unifies both
b
-metrics and suprametrics. Furthermore, Berzig extended Matkowski’s fixed point theorem to
b
-suprametric spaces (see Theorem

). For applications of suprametrics in various contexts, we refer the reader to
Panda et al. 2023a
;
Panda et al. 2023b

While
b
-metrics share some properties with classical metrics, such as non-negativity and symmetry, they differ significantly due to the relaxed triangle inequality. For instance, as shown in
Lu et al. 2019
, there exist
b
-metrics that are not continuous. This behavior is also observed
b
-suprametrics. Similarly, Example 2.10 in

Lemma 1
.

Every
\alpha

c
interpolative metric, where
0<\alpha<1
and

Proof.

Starting with the definition of the
\alpha

c

\Delta(x,y)\leq\Delta(x,z)+\Delta(z,y)+c\left[(\Delta(x,z))^{\alpha}\cdot(\Delta(z,y))^{1-\alpha}\right].

By Young’s inequality, for
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the following holds:

(\Delta(x,z))^{\alpha}\cdot(\Delta(z,y))^{1-\alpha}\leq\alpha\Delta(x,z)+(1-\alpha)\Delta(z,y).

Substituting this into the original inequality, we obtain:

\Delta(x,y)\leq\Delta(x,z)+\Delta(z,y)+c\left[\alpha\Delta(x,z)+(1-\alpha)\Delta(z,y)\right].

Expanding and grouping like terms, we derive:

\Delta(x,y)\leq\left[1+c\alpha\right]\Delta(x,z)+\left[1+c(1-\alpha)\right]\Delta(z,y).

Let
s=\max\{1+c\alpha,1+c(1-\alpha)\}
. Since
1+c\alpha
and
1+c(1-\alpha)
are both positive, we conclude:

\Delta(x,y)\leq s\cdot\left[\Delta(x,z)+\Delta(z,y)\right].

We see that
\Delta
is a
b
-metric with index
s
.

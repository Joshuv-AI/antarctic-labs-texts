Minimal multiplicity of fiber
components in abelian fibrations

Abstract




Abstract.
An abelian fibration is a
proper projective surjective map of complex varieties
with general fiber an abelian variety.
Consider a multiple fiber of an
abelian fibration, and let
\mu_{1},...,\mu_{k}

be the multiplicities of its irreducible
components. We prove that the minimum of
\mu_{i}

is equal to their greatest common divisor

\operatorname{\text{\it gcd}}(\mu_{1},...,\mu_{k})
.

1
Introduction

Let
f:X\to B
be a proper fibration from a complex manifold
X
onto a
smooth complex curve
B
, and
b\in B
be a point.
Let
X_{b}:=f^{*}(b):=\sum_{i}m_{i}F_{i}
be the scheme theoretic (divisorial, here)
fiber of
f
, the positive integers
m_{i}
being the “multiplicities” of
the components
F_{i}
of
X_{b}
. The “multiplicity” of
X_{b}
is classically
defined as being the greatest common divisor of the numbers
m_{i}
. This is, in
particular, the definition used by Kodaira and Shafarevich in their
classification of degenerate fibers in elliptic fibrations of surfaces
(
[
BHPV
]
).
For higher dimensional bases
B
, one looks at the generic fibers over the
components of the discriminant divisor of
f
in
B
.

In
[
Ca2
]
, however, a different definition is used: one does not
consider there the
\operatorname{\text{\it gcd}}
of the
m_{i}
, but their infimum. This second
definition is dictated by the need to characterize what is called there the
“Bogomolov sheaves” on projective or compact Kähler varieties
X
,
which are the rank-one subsheaves
L\subset\Omega^{p}_{X},p>0
such that

\kappa(X,L)=p
, the top Kodaira dimension, after Bogomolov’s bound
(
[
Bo
,
CCR
]
).
These sheaves, analogs of “big” canonical bundles, indeed play a central
rôle in bimeromorphic classification (see
[
Ca2
]
for more details).

It is thus important to know when the two definitions agree (for locally
projective or Kähler proper fibrations). This is the case when the smooth
fibers of
f
are either rationally connected (by

[
GHS
]
or
[
JS
, main theorem]
),
or are compact complex tori (in particular: Abelian varieties), which
is the topic of the present text. In particular, the two definitions agree
for the elliptic surfaces of Kodaira/Shafarevich’s classification.

On the other hand, examples of fibrations with fibers higher genus curves
for which the two versions differ are found in
[
W
]
,
[
S
]
,

[
Ca3
]
. A remarkable example is given in
[
La
]
, where the smooth
fibers are Enriques surfaces, but some singular fibre has components of multiplicities 2 and 3, the base being the projective line,
with consequences in hyperbolic and arithmetic
geometries derived in
[
BCJW
]
.

In the Appendix
4

we introduce the abelian torsor structure in the
settings of complex torus fibrations, as in

[
BKV
,
KV
]
. We use the
averaging formalism applied to multisections of an abelian fibration in order
to obtain the following theorem over a
1
-dimensional base.

Theorem 1.1:
(
2
)
Let
\pi:\;M\to B
be an abelian fibration, that is,
a proper projective surjective map of complex varieties with general
fiber an abelian variety. Assume that all points of
B

are regular values of
\pi
except
x_{0}\in B
,
and
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}B=1
. Let
F_{1},...,F_{k}
be the
irreducible components of
\pi^{-1}(x_{0})
,
and
\mu_{1},...,\mu_{k}
their multiplicities.
Assume that the greatest common divisor of
\mu_{i}

is 1. Then
\pi
admits a local section.

When
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}M=2
,

2
is a consequence
of Kodaira’s classification of elliptic fibrations,

[
BHPV
]
. After we posted this paper on the arXiv, we became aware
that
2
was already
proved in an unpublished paper of S. Lu,

[
Lu
]
.

In Section
3
, we generalize these results to
higher-dimensional base.

Theorem 1.2:
(
3
) Let
f:M\to B
be a proper Kähler
fibration with
M
smooth,
B
normal, such that its generic fibers are
smooth complex tori. Assume that
f
has equidimensional fibers.
Let
b\in B
be any point and let
F_{1},...,F_{k}
be the
irreducible components of
f^{-1}(b)
,
and
\mu_{1},...,\mu_{k}
their multiplicities.
Assume that the greatest common divisor of
\mu_{i}
is 1.
Then for any
b\in B
,
f
has a meromorphic section above some open
neighborhood of
b
in
B
.

Corollary 1.3:
(
3
) In the situation of
3
,
assume that for some
b\in B
, the fiber is (in the sense of
[
BM
]
)

M_{b}=\sum_{i}m_{i}F_{i}
, with
F_{i}
being the irreducible components.
If
gcd_{i}(\{m_{i}\})=1
, then
m_{i}=1
for some
i
.

Remark 1.4:
In assumptions of
3
,
it is essential that the fibration is (at least locally) Kähler:
one of the key arguments in Section
3
uses the compactness
of the Barlet spaces.
However, in the settings of hyperkähler geometry, the first-named author
proved that Lagrangian fibrations on hyperkähler manifolds are
not only Kähler, but also locally projective (
[
Ca1
]
).

2
Averaging multisections in a torus fibration over a disk

In Section
4
, we define the torsors and
described the averaging formalism (
[
BKV
]
),
which is a general
method of constructing a section of a torsor bundle,
starting with a (signed) multisection of total multiplicity 1.
Applying this construction to an abelian fibration, we obtain
the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1:

Let
\pi:\;M\to B
be a torus fibration, that is,
a proper map of complex varieties with general
fiber a compact torus. Assume that all points of
B

are regular values of
\pi
except
x_{0}\in B
,
and
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}B=1
. Let
F_{1},...,F_{k}
be the
irreducible components of
\pi^{-1}(x_{0})
,
and
\mu_{1},...,\mu_{k}
their multiplicities.
Assume that the greatest common divisor of
\mu_{i}

is 1. Then
\pi
admits a local section, and

{\it min}_{i}\mu_{i}=1
.

Proof. Step 1:
Around a smooth point
m\in F_{i}
,
the projection maps can be written in local coordinates
as
(z_{1},z_{2},...,z_{n})\mapsto z_{1}^{\mu_{i}}
.
Let
{\cal E}_{\mu_{i}}
be the set of all roots of unity of degree
\mu_{i}
.
Then the image of the
multivalued map
t\to({\cal E}_{\mu_{i}}t^{\mu_{i}^{-1}},0,...,0)

is a smooth curve in
M
, and its projection
to a neighbourhood
U
of
x\in B
is a
\mu_{i}
-sheeted ramified cover.

Step 2:

Consider such a multisection
Z_{i}
for each
F_{i}
, and
let
a_{1},...,a_{k}
be a set of integers such that

\sum_{i}a_{i}\mu_{i}=1
. We consider
\sum a_{i}Z_{i}

as a multisection with multiplicites determined by

a_{i}
, with total multiplicity 1.
Applying the averaging construction
(
4.2
), we obtain a meromorphic
map
\varphi:\;U\backslash x_{0}\to M
, which is holomorphic
and Lipschitz
by construction. By Riemann removable singularity theorem,
this map extends to the origin
x_{0}\in U
.

Corollary 2.2:
Let
\pi:\;M\to B
be an torus fibration, that is,
a proper surjective map of complex varieties with general
fiber a compact torus. Assume that all points of
B

are regular values of
\pi
except
x_{0}\in B
,
and
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}B=1
. Let
F_{1},...,F_{k}
be
the irreducible components of
\pi^{-1}(x_{0})
,
and
\mu_{1},...,\mu_{k}
their multiplicities.
Then
{\it min}\mu_{i}=\operatorname{\text{\it gcd}}(\mu_{i})
,
where
\operatorname{\text{\it gcd}}
denotes the greatest common divisor.

Proof:
Passing to a ramified covering of
X
of order
\operatorname{\text{\it gcd}}(\mu_{i})
,
we arrive to an torus fibration with the same fibers with multiplicities

\mu_{i}^{\prime}=\frac{\mu_{i}}{\operatorname{\text{\it gcd}}(\mu_{i})}
, hence
\operatorname{\text{\it gcd}}(\mu_{i}^{\prime})=1
.
By
2
, it has a section.
This section gives a multisection of order
\operatorname{\text{\it gcd}}(\mu_{i})
of
\mu
.

3
Higher dimensional bases

The aim of this section is to prove the following results generalizing

2
from a
1
-dimensional base to a base of
any dimension. First we give a proof of the corollary, and then of the
theorem below.

Theorem 3.1:
Let
f:M\to B
be a proper Kähler
fibration between normal complex spaces,
M
smooth,
such that its generic fibers are
smooth compact complex tori. Assume that
f
has
equidimensional fibers. Let
b\in B
be any point
and let
F_{1},...,F_{k}
be the
irreducible components of
f^{-1}(b)
,
and
\mu_{1},...,\mu_{k}
their multiplicities.
Assume that the greatest common divisor of
\mu_{i}

is 1.
Then
f
has a meromorphic section above some open
neighborhood of
b
in
B
.

Corollary 3.2:
In the situation of
3
,
assume that for some
b\in B
, the fiber is (in the sense of
[
BM
]
)

M_{b}=\sum_{i}m_{i}F_{i}
, with
F_{i}
being the irreducible components.
If
gcd_{i}(\{m_{i}\})=1
, then
{\it min}_{i}(\{m_{i}\})=1
.

Proof of
3
, assuming
3
:
Let
s:B\dasharrow M
be a
meromorphic section of
f
defined near
b
. Let
B^{\prime}
be the closure
of the image of the meromorphic section
B\dasharrow M
. Let
m:B^{\prime}\to B
be the proper
bimeromorphic map such that
s^{\prime}:=s\circ m:B^{\prime}\to M^{\prime}:=M\times_{B}B^{\prime}
is a
holomorphic section of
f^{\prime}:M^{\prime}\to B^{\prime}
obtained from
f
by the base-change

m:B^{\prime}\to B
.
For any
b^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}
such that
m(b^{\prime})=b
, the component of

M^{\prime}_{b^{\prime}}
containing
s^{\prime}(b^{\prime})
has multiplicity
1
in

M^{\prime}_{b^{\prime}}=M_{b}\times_{\{b\}}\{b^{\prime}\}
. Thus, so has the corresponding component
of
M_{b}
: since the existence of a local holomorphic section implies that
the multiplicity of the corresponding component of the fiber has multiplicity one.

Proof of
3
:

Let
B^{0}\subset B
be the Zariski open subset
over which
the fibers are smooth compact tori. Fix
b\in B^{0}
, let
A_{b}=\operatorname{Aut}^{0}(M_{b})

be the translation group of the fiber
M_{b}
. Clearly,
A_{b}
is a torus,
isomorphic to
M_{b}
.
If
u_{i}\in\mathbb{Z},i=1,\dots,r
is such that
\sum_{i}u_{i}=1
and if

p_{i}\in M_{b},i=1,\dots,r
, the sum
\sum_{i}u_{i}\cdot p_{i}=s\in M_{b}
is well-defined,
independently of the choice of an origin in
M_{b}

(
4.2
).

Recall (
[
BM
]
, chap IV, Théorème 3.4.1, Corollaire 3.4.2) that
f
being equidimensional, with
B
normal,
the components of the fibers of
f
can be given in a unique way
multiplicities (equal to
1
for those above
B^{0}
) in such a way that the
fibers of
f
form an analytic family of cycles in the Barlet sense,
such that the map
\beta:B\to C(M/B)
sending
M_{b}
to its representative
point in
C(M/B)
, the Chow/Barlet space of relative
f
-cycles of
M
,
is analytic. Moreover, if
C:=\beta(B)\subset C(M/B)
, and if

\Gamma_{C}\subset C\times M
is the incidence graph of the universal family
of cycles parametrised by
C
, then

(f\times id_{X})(M)=(\beta\times id_{X})^{-1}(\Gamma_{C})
: by its very definition,
the graph of
f
is obtained from
\Gamma_{C}
by the base-change
\beta
.

Let now
b^{\prime}\in B\setminus B^{0}
, and
M_{b^{\prime}}=\sum_{i}m_{i}\cdot F_{i}
be its
f
-fiber.
By smoothness of
M
, through the generic point of each
F_{i}
we have a
local multisection
s_{i},i=1,\dots,r
of
f
which has degree
m_{i}
over
B
.
Denote with
p_{i,j}(b)
its points over
b
. By hypothesis, the gcd of the
numbers
\{m_{i}\}

is one, and Bezout lemma
implies the existence of integers
v_{i}

such that
\sum_{i}u_{i}:=\sum_{i}v_{i}\cdot m_{i}=1,u_{i}:=v_{i}\cdot m_{i}
. By averaging (
4.2
),
for each
b\in B^{0}
near
b^{\prime}
, a well-defined unique point

s(b):=\sum_{i,j}u_{i}\cdot p_{i,j}(b)\in M_{b}
exists, which depends holomorphically on
b
.

We shall show, using the Kählerianity assumption, that this section of
f

over
B^{0}
near
b^{\prime}
extends meromorphically to
B
near
b^{\prime}
.
More precisely, we will show that the addition (and substraction) in the generic smooth fibers of
f
extend
meromorphically to
M\times_{B}M
. Any section obtained by averaging over the locus
B^{0}
of smooth fibers
of
f
in
B
thus extends meromorphically to
B
.

This extension of addition/substraction is based on the properness of the irreducible components of
the relative Chow/Barlet space
C(M/B)
(see
[
BM
]
and the references
there).
Let
B^{0}\subset B
be the Zariski open subset over which the fibers are
smooth compact tori, and let
M^{0}:=f^{-1}(B^{0})
.

Over
B^{0}
one can define the unit component
\operatorname{Aut}^{0}(M^{0}/B^{0})\to B^{0}
of the
group of translations in the fibers of
f
as the component of

C(M^{0}\times_{B^{0}}M^{0})\to B^{0}
consisting of the graphs of these translations.
Over
B^{0}
, the incidence graph

\Phi^{0}\subset(\operatorname{Aut}^{0}(M^{0}/B^{0})\times_{B^{0}}(M^{0}\times_{B^{0}}M^{0})

of the family of relative translations in the fibers of
f
is also nothing
but the graph of the addition map
+:\operatorname{Aut}^{0}(M^{0}/B^{0})\times M^{0}\to M^{0}
over

B^{0}
sending
(t_{b},x_{b})\to t_{b}+x_{b}
, the substraction map being defined as
exchanging the
M^{0}/B^{0}
factors. Since over
B^{0}
these two maps just consist
in exchanging the suitable factors, which obviously extends meromorphically over
B
,
they extend meromorphically over
B
to maps:
\operatorname{Aut}^{0}(M/B)\times_{B}M_{b}
, where
Aut^{0}(M/B)
is the Zariski
closure in
C(M/B)
of the unit component
\operatorname{Aut}^{0}(M^{0}/B^{0})
.
If
u_{i}\in\mathbb{Z},i=1,\dots r
are such that
\sum_{i}u_{i}=1
, the
well-defined map over
B^{0}
:
M^{0}\times_{B^{0}}\times\dots_{B^{0}}M^{0}\to M_{0}

(r-factors) sending
(x_{1,b},\dots x_{r,b})\to\sum_{i}u_{i}\cdot x_{i,b}

thus extends meromorphically to
M_{B}\times\dots_{B}M
over
B
,
which shows the claim.

Remark 3.3:

1. Constructions similar to the one given by Ulf Persson in
[
P
, p.83]
,
show that the relative Kählerianity of
f
is essential for the existence
of this meromorphic section.

2. The hypothesis on the
\operatorname{\text{\it gcd}}
of the
m_{i}
being
equal to
1
does not follow from the same property in codimension
1
,
even if
M
and
B
are smooth,
f
projective, and if the fibers outside

b\in B
are reduced, as shown by the example of I. Hellmann
(
[
Hel
, Section 2.2]
).

4
Appendix: Torsor fibrations

4.1
Torsor structure in complex torus fibrations

By definition, a torsor over a group

G
is a set where
G
acts freely and transitively.
However, the torsor structure can be defined
without referring to an
a priori
given group structure as follows.

Following
[
KV
]
, we define an
abelian torsor structure as a kind of algebraic structure,
without first defining the group acting on this torsor.
This simplifies some of the definitions when the
torsor structure is defined in families, and we
do not have the corresponding family of abelian
groups on hand.

In the sequel we will make a distinction between the compact
torus
T
and the same torus equipped with a group structure,
which we denote
\operatorname{Alb}(T)
. This group structure is clearly
unique up to a parallel transport, that is, determined
by the choice of 0. The torus
\operatorname{Alb}(T)
is identified
with the abelian variety of
T
.

Definition 4.1: An abelian torsor
is a set
S
equipped
with a map
\mu:\;S\times S\times S\to S
such that

(i)

for
any
e\in S
, the map
x\cdot y:=\mu(x,y,e)

defines a commutative and associative product on
S

with
e
an identity element

(ii)

for any fixed
y\in S
,
the map
x\mapsto x\cdot y
is bijective.

Remark 4.2:
From the axioms of an abelian
torsor, it is clear that the operation “
\cdot
” defines a structure
of an abelian group on
S
.

Remark 4.3:
The identity element axiom means that

\mu(x,y,y)=x
for any
x
and
y
. Given a group
structure on
S
, the torsor operation

\mu
can be defined as
\mu(x,y,z)=x+y-z
.

Claim 4.4:

Let
\pi:\;M\to B
be
a surjective holomorphic fibration whose general fiber is a
compact complex torus.
Then the fibers
F_{x}:=\pi^{-1}(x)
of
\pi
are
equipped with a holomorphic torsor structure
which depends on
x\in B
holomorphically.

Proof:
It is not hard to see that any
holomorphic map of a compact complex torus
is affine, that is, obtained as a composition
of a parallel transport and a group homomorphism.
Therefore, the holomorphic torsor structure on a
compact torus
T
is unique; indeed, the
connected component of the group of automorphisms
of
T
is
\operatorname{Alb}(T)
. Taking a local section,
we can identify each fiber
F_{x}
with its
Albanese manifold
\operatorname{Alb}(F_{x})
holomorphically,
and this turns
F_{x}
into an
\operatorname{Alb}(F_{x})
-torsor.
The dependence on
x
is holomorphic by construction,
and it is independent from the choice of a local section
by uniqueness.

4.2
The averaging formalism

An affine space is, by definition, a torsor over a vector space.
Consider an affine space
A
over a vector space
V
.
After choosing the origin
a\in A
, we can identify

A
and
V
. For any collection of points
x_{1},...,x_{n}\in A
,
their mass center
a+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}(x_{i}-a)

is well defined and independent from the choice of the origin.

This construction works for torsors over infinitely
divisible, torsion-free abelian groups (that is, vector spaces
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
). This assumption is essential, because if the group has
torsion, the mass center becomes ambiguous.

However, a version of this construction
survives. It was used in
[
BKV
]

to produce a rational section of a Lagrangian fibration
in a hyperkähler manifold. If we take a weighted
sum of
m
points with positive sign and
(m-1)
points
with negative sign, the sum is independent from the
choice of an origin.
We give an abstract version of this construction here, see

[
BKV
, Claim 3.6]
for its proof.

Claim 4.5:

Let
X
be a torsor over
G
.
Choosing an origin
e\in X
, we can identify

X
with
G
. Then, for any
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{>0}
there is a natural
map
X^{m}\times X^{m-1}\to X

taking
(x_{1},...,x_{m},y_{1},...,y_{m-1})

to
e+\sum_{i=1}^{m}(x_{i}-e)-\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}(y_{i}-e)
,
which is independent from the choice of
e
.

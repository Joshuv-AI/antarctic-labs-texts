Pathwise uniform convergence of numerical approximations for a two-dimensional stochastic Navier-Stokes equation with no-slip boundary conditions Thanks: This work was partially supported by the National Natural Science Foundation of China under grants 12301525 and 12171340, and by the Natural Science Foundation of Sichuan Province under grant 2023NSFSC1324.
Binjie Li, Xiaoping Xie, Qin Zhou

Abstract
This paper investigates the pathwise uniform convergence in probability of fully discrete finite-element approximations for the two-dimensional stochastic Navier-Stokes equations with multiplicative noise, subject to no-slip boundary conditions. We demonstrate that the full discretization achieves nearly 3 / 2 3/2 -order convergence in space and nearly half-order convergence in time.



Keywords:

stochastic Navier-Stokes equation, no-slip boundary conditions, finite element method, pathwise uniform convergence

1
Introduction

Let
0<T<\infty
, and consider
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
as a bounded, connected domain with a sufficiently smooth boundary
\partial\mathcal{O}
. We study the following incompressible stochastic Navier-Stokes equation (SNSE):

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} \mathrm{d}y(t,x)&=\Bigl[\Delta y(t,x)-(y(t,x)\cdot\nabla)y(t,x)-\nabla\varphi(t,x)\Bigr]\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\bigl[f_{n}(x,y(t,x))-\nabla\widetilde{\varphi}_{n}(t,x)\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}\beta_{n}(t),&&t\in[0,T],\,x\in\mathcal{O},\\
\nabla\cdot y(t,x)&=0,&&t\in[0,T],\,x\in\mathcal{O},\\
y(t,x)&=0,&&t\in[0,T],\,x\in\partial\mathcal{O},\\
y(0,x)&=y_{0}(x),&&x\in\mathcal{O}.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(1.1)

In this system,
y
represents the velocity field of interest,
\varphi
denotes the pressure field, and
(\widetilde{\varphi}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of unknown auxiliary scalar processes that ensures the divergence-free property of the diffusion term. The initial velocity field is given by
y_{0}
,
(\beta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of independent Brownian motions, and the diffusion coefficients
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
will be detailed later.

Due to their important applications to the theory of turbulent flows and its significance from
a pure mathematics perspective, the SNSEs have garnered significant interest.
These equations have been extensively studied in the literature;
see
[
3
,
14
,
17
,
24
,
40
]
and the reference therein.
Consequently, over the past decades, the numerical analysis of SNSEs,
as well as their simplified variants, the stochastic Stokes equations,
has attracted considerable interest from the numerical community.

For the cases of space-periodic boundary conditions, significant advancements have been observed in recent years.
We briefly summarize some recent contributions as follows.
Dörsek
[
21
]
derived rates of convergence for full discretizations that
use spectral Galerkin method in space and semigroup splitting or cubature methods in time,
for a two-dimensional SNSE with additive noise.
In the context of finite-element-based full discretizations,
Carelli and Prohl
[
18
]
made notable progress by establishing linear convergence in
space and almost
\frac{1}{4}
-order convergence in time with respect to convergence in probability.
Their findings were later improved upon by Breit and Dodgson
[
9
]
,
who demonstrated enhanced theoretical results with linear convergence in space and
nearly
\frac{1}{2}
-order convergence in time.
Bessaih et al.
[
4
]
derived almost
\frac{1}{2}
-order temporal convergence in probability for
a splitting up method.
Bessaih and Millet
[
5
]
established strong
L^{2}
convergence for both the Euler scheme and
a splitting scheme, and they subsequently extended their analysis to a full discretization that
combines the Euler scheme for temporal approximation with the finite element method for spatial approximation,
proving strong
L^{2}
convergence once more
[
6
]
.
Hausenblas and Randrianasolo
[
34
]
studied a time-discretization
with penalty-projection method, and derived a convergence rate of
\frac{1}{4}
in probability.
A recent contribution by Breit et al.
[
10
]
derived a convergence rate of
\frac{1}{2}

in the mean square error for a time-discretization of a two-dimensional SNSE with transport noise.
We also direct the reader to
[
22
,
23
]
and the references therein for the numerical analysis
of stochastic Stokes equations.
Of particular note is the recent work by Li et al.
[
38
]
, which established a strong convergence rate of

O(h^{2}+\tau^{1/2})
in the
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega;L^{2}))
norm for finite-element-based approximations
of two- and three-dimensional stochastic Stokes equations under the stress boundary conditions.

Despite significant progress in this field, the numerical analysis of SNSEs subject to no-slip
boundary conditions remains a relatively underdeveloped area of research. A fundamental difficulty
arises from the fact that the Helmholtz projection
\mathcal{P}
fails to preserve the
zero-trace property. This limitation implies that the range of
\mathcal{P}
is not
necessarily contained within the domain of
A_{2}^{1/4}
(let alone
A_{2}^{1/2}
),
where
A_{2}
represents the realization of the negative Stokes operator in
L^{2}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})

with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions.
Moreover, the important identity
\int_{\mathcal{O}}\big((u\cdot\nabla)u\big)\cdot A_{2}u\,\mathrm{d}x=0
,
which holds on the domain of the Stokes operator under space-periodic boundary conditions,
fails to hold under no-slip boundary conditions.
These mathematical obstacles, combined with the temporal irregularities induced by the
Brownian motions, substantially diminishes the solution’s regularity when compared to
scenarios with space-periodic boundary conditions.
Specifically, in the model problem
Eq.

1.1
,
the trajectories of the process
y
generally do not possess

L^{2}(0,T;\dot{H}^{3/2,2})
-regularity, where
\dot{H}^{3/2,2}
denotes
the domain of
A_{2}^{3/4}
, unless the critical structural assumptions—that the diffusion coefficients are divergence-free and vanish on the boundary
\partial\mathcal{O}
—are satisfied.

Here is a brief summary of some recent works on the numerical analysis of SNSEs with no-slip boundary conditions:

•

Breckner
[
8
]
demonstrated the convergence in mean square for a spectral-Galerkin spatial semidiscretization.

•

Brzeźniak and Prohl
[
15
]
addressed both two- and three-dimensional cases,
showing the convergence of finite element approximations to a unique strong solution
in two dimensions using compactness arguments.

•

Doghman
[
20
]
extended the work
[
15
]
by proving the convergence of finite element
approximations for the two-dimensional SNSEs through the artificial compressibility method.

•

Ondreját et al.
[
43
]
achieved convergence for a class of finite element approximations
to a weak martingale solution in three dimensions.

•

To the best of our knowledge, Breit and Prohl
[
11
]
were the
first to report strong convergence rates in probability for a fully discrete scheme applied to two-dimensional SNSEs
with multiplicative noise, yielding the following convergence result:

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}h\to 0\\
\tau\to 0\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}\left[\frac{\max_{m}\|\bm{u}(t_{m})-\bm{u}_{h,m}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}+\sum_{m}\tau\|\nabla(\bm{u}(t_{m})-\bm{u}_{h,m})\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}}{h^{\alpha}+\tau^{\beta}}>\xi\right]=0,

for any
\alpha\in(1,2)
,
\beta\in(0,1)
, and
\xi>0
.

However, the numerical analysis presented in
[
11
]
is
generally not applicable to the model problem
Eq.

1.1
,
as it relies on the aforementioned structural assumption that the diffusion coefficients
are divergence-free and vanish on the boundary
\partial\mathcal{O}
.

The model problem
Eq.

1.1
and its more complex variants, where the diffusion coefficients depend on the gradient
of
y
, have been extensively studied; see, for instance,

[
16
,
41
,
42
,
54
]
.
Despite considerable research efforts, to the best of our knowledge,
rigorous numerical analyses of finite element approximations directly applicable to the model problem
Eq.

1.1
are limited to those presented in the aforementioned works
[
15
,
20
]
.
However, these studies have not provided explicit convergence rates in probability.
This significant gap in the existing literature motivates our current research.

.

-norms

.

,

.

, we provide a rigorous analysis of the

,

,

,
utilizing suitably constructed stopping times to partition the probability

into regular and irregular subsets.
Within the regular subset, the numerical solution demonstrates

and the time step
\tau

decrease towards zero. Concurrently, the probability measure of the irregular subset
converges to zero as
h
and
\tau

Mathematical Setup

We begin by introducing some notational conventions that will be used throughout this paper.
For any Banach spaces
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
, the space of all bounded linear operators
from
E_{1}
to
E_{2}
is denoted by
\mathcal{L}(E_{1},E_{2})
.
The identity operator is denoted by
I
.
For any
\theta\in(0,1)
and
p\in(1,\infty)
, we use the notation
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\theta,p}

to refer to the space constructed by the real interpolation method (see, e.g.,
[
39
, Chapter 1]
).
Given an interval
D\subset\mathbb{R}
and a Banach space
X
,
the notation
C(D;X)
denotes the space of all continuous functions
defined on
D
with values in
X
.
Let
T>0
be a fixed terminal time, and let
\mathcal{O}
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with a smooth boundary
of class
\mathcal{C}^{3,1}
(see
[
7
, Chapter III, Section 1.1]
).
For any
q\in[1,\infty]
, we denote by
W^{2,q}(\mathcal{O})
and
W^{2,q}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})

the standard Sobolev spaces (see
[
50
, Chapter 5]
).
For
\alpha\in[0,2]
and
q\in(1,\infty)
, define

H^{\alpha,q}:=[L^{q}(\mathcal{O}),W^{2,q}(\mathcal{O})]_{\alpha/2}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,q}:=[\mathbb{L}^{q}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2}),W^{2,q}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})]_{\alpha/2},

where
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\alpha/2}
denotes the complex interpolation method (see
[
39
, Chapter 2]
).
Let
\mathcal{H}^{\alpha,q}
denote the domain of
(-\Delta_{q})^{\alpha/2}
,
equippped with the norm

\lVert{u}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}^{\alpha,q}}:=\lVert{(-\Delta_{q})^{\alpha/2}u}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})},\quad\forall u\in\mathcal{H}^{\alpha,q},

where
\Delta_{q}
is the Dirichlet Laplacian on
L^{q}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
Its dual space
\mathcal{H}^{-\alpha,q^{\prime}}
(with
q^{\prime}:=q/(q-1)
) is equipped
with the duality pairing
\big\langle{\cdot,\cdot}\big\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{-\alpha,q^{\prime}},\mathcal{H}^{\alpha,q}}
.
The notation
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is used for inner products in
L^{2}(\mathcal{O})
,
L^{2}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})
, or
L^{2}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
, depending on the context.
For brevity, we denote
L^{q}(\mathcal{O})
by
L^{q}
. Similarly,
L^{q}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})

\mathbb{L}^{q}

2.1
Stokes Operator

For any
q\in(1,\infty)
, we define
\mathbb{L}_{\text{div}}^{q}
as the closure in
\mathbb{L}^{q}
of the set:

\left\{u\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2}):\,\nabla\cdot u=0\text{ in }\mathcal{O}\right\},

where
C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})
denotes the space of vector-valued
functions that are infinitely differentiable with compact support in
\mathcal{O}
.
Let
\mathcal{P}
denote the Helmholtz projection operator, which projects elements from
\mathbb{L}^{q}
onto
\mathbb{L}_{\text{div}}^{q}
for
q\in(1,\infty)
. Notably, when
q=2
,
\mathcal{P}
acts as the orthogonal projection in
\mathbb{L}^{2}
onto
\mathbb{L}_{\text{div}}^{2}
.
For a detailed discussion of the Helmholtz projection operator, see Chapter III of
[
25
]
.

For any
q\in(1,\infty)
, the negative Stokes operator
A_{q}
is defined as

A_{q}:=-\mathcal{P}\Delta_{q}
, with domain

\text{D}(A_{q}):=\mathbb{L}_{\text{div}}^{q}\cap\{u\in W^{2,q}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2}):\,u=0\text{ on }\partial\mathcal{O}\},

where
\Delta_{q}
is the realization of the Laplacian in
\mathbb{L}^{q}
with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions.
For any
\alpha\in[0,2]
, we denote by
\dot{H}^{\alpha,q}
the domain of
A_{q}^{\alpha/2}
, equipped with the norm:

\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha,q}}:=\|A_{q}^{\alpha/2}u\|_{\mathbb{L}^{q}},\quad\forall u\in\dot{H}^{\alpha,q}.

By
[
28
, Theorem 2]
,
\dot{H}^{\alpha,q}
coincides with the complex interpolation space

[\mathbb{L}_{\text{div}}^{q},\dot{H}^{2,q}]_{\alpha/2}
, with equivalent norms.
The dual space of
\dot{H}^{\alpha,q}
is denoted by
\dot{H}^{-\alpha,q^{\prime}}
,
where
q^{\prime}:=q/(q-1)
, and
\big\langle{\cdot,\cdot}\big\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-\alpha,q^{\prime}},\dot{H}^{\alpha,q}}
denotes
the corresponding duality pairing.
Additionally, the following embedding holds (see, e.g.,
[
54
, Lemma 8.2]
):

\mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{div}}^{q}\cap\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,q}\hookrightarrow\dot{H}^{\alpha,q},\quad\forall q\in(1,\infty),\,\forall\alpha\in(0,1/q).

(2.1)

Finally, let
S(t)
, for
t\in[0,\infty)
, denote the analytic semigroup generated by
-A_{q}
(see
[
27
, Theorem 2]
).

2.2
Stochastic Integrals

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
denote a given complete probability space equipped with a right-continuous filtration
\mathbb{F}:=(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
, on which a sequence of independent Brownian motions
(\beta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is defined. The expectation operator with respect to this probability space will be denoted by
\mathbb{E}
.
Consider
H
as a separable Hilbert space equipped with the inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{H}
and an orthonormal basis
(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. We define the
\mathbb{F}
-adapted
H
-cylindrical Brownian motion
W_{H}
such that, for every
t\geqslant 0
,
W_{H}(t)
is an element of
\mathcal{L}(H,L^{2}(\Omega))
given by

W_{H}(t)h=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(h,h_{n})_{H}\beta_{n}(t),\quad\forall h\in H.

For any UMD Banach space
E
, let
\gamma(H,E)
denote the space of all
\gamma
-radonifying operators from
H
to
E
. For any
p\in[1,\infty]
, we denote by
L_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);\gamma(H,E))
the closure in
L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);\gamma(H,E))
of the space

\text{span}\big\{u\mathbbm{1}_{(s,t]\times\mathcal{B}}:\,0\leqslant s<t\leqslant T,\,u\in\gamma(H,E),\,\mathcal{B}\in\mathcal{F}_{s}\big\},

where
\mathbbm{1}_{(s,t]\times\mathcal{B}}
is the indicator function of
(s,t]\times\mathcal{B}
.
When
E
is a Hilbert space,
\gamma(H,E)
coincides with the space of
Hilbert-Schmidt operators from
H
to
E
, and Itô’s isometry holds:

\mathbb{E}\biggl\|\int_{0}^{T}g(t)\,\mathrm{d}W_{H}(t)\biggr\|_{E}^{2}=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\|g(t)\|_{\gamma(H,E)}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t,

for all
g\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T);\gamma(H,E))
.
For further details on
\gamma
-radonifying operators, we refer to
Chapter 9 of
[
35
]
, and for stochastic integrals in UMD Banach spaces,
see
[
52
]
.

2.3
Global Mild Solutions

We begin by introducing the following hypothesis regarding the diffusion coefficients
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in the model problem (
1.1
).

Hypothesis 2.1
.

Let
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of twice continuously
differentiable functions from
\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
There exists a constant
C_{F}>0
such that the following conditions hold:

\begin{cases}\sup\limits_{x\in\mathcal{O}}{}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\bigg[|f_{n}(x,y)|^{2}+|\nabla_{x}f_{n}(x,y)|^{2}+|\nabla_{x}^{2}f_{n}(x,y)|^{2}\bigg]\right)^{1/2}\leqslant C_{F}\big(1+|y|\big),\,\,\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
\sup\limits_{(x,y)\in\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Big[\,|\nabla_{y}f_{n}(x,y)|^{2}+|\nabla_{x}\nabla_{y}f_{n}(x,y)|^{2}+|\nabla_{y}^{2}f_{n}(x,y)|^{2}\Big]\right)^{1/2}\leqslant C_{F},\end{cases}

Throughout this paper, we consistently assume that Hypothesis
2.1
holds. For any
u\in\mathbb{L}^{2}
, we define
F(u)\in\gamma(H,\mathbb{L}^{2})
as follows:

F(u):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}h_{n}\otimes f_{n}(\cdot,u(\cdot)),

(2.2)

where the tensor product
h_{n}\otimes f_{n}(\cdot,u(\cdot))\in\gamma(H,\mathbb{L}^{2})
is defined by

(h_{n}\otimes f_{n}(\cdot,u(\cdot)))(w):=(w,h_{n})_{H}f_{n}(\cdot,u(\cdot)),\quad\forall w\in H.

We present several growth and Lipschitz-continuity properties of the operator
F

in the subsequent lemma. These properties can be verified through straightforward computations,
using the definition of
\gamma
-radonifying operators
(see Section 9.1.b in Chapter 9 of
[
35
]
) and the standard theory
of interpolation spaces (see Lemma 28.1 in
[
50
]
and Theorem 4.36 in
[
39
]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

The following assertions hold:

(i)

There exists a constant
c>0

such that for all
u,v\in\mathbb{L}^{2}
,

\left\|F(u)-F(v)\right\|_{\gamma(H,\mathbb{L}^{2})}\leqslant c\left\|u-v\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}.

(ii)

There exists a constant
c>0
such that for all
u\in\mathbb{L}^{2}
,

\left\|F(u)\right\|_{\gamma(H,\mathbb{L}^{2})}\leqslant c(1+\left\|u\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}).

(iii)

For any
0\leqslant\alpha\leqslant 1
,
there exists a constant
c>0
such that for all
u\in\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2}
,

\left\|F(u)\right\|_{\gamma(H,\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2})}\leqslant c(1+\left\|u\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2}}).

(iv)

For any
1<\varrho<\frac{3}{2}
and
\varrho\leqslant\alpha\leqslant 2
,
there exists a constant
c>0
such that for all
u\in\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2}
,

\left\|F(u)\right\|_{\gamma(H,\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2})}\leqslant c(1+\left\|u\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\varrho,2}})^{\alpha-1}\left\|u\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2}}.

Next, we introduce two convolution operations.
For any
g\in L^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{div}}^{q})
with
p,q\in(1,\infty)
,
the deterministic convolution is defined as

(S\ast g)(t):=\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)g(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,\quad t\in[0,T].

For any stochastic process
g\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T);\gamma(H,\mathbb{L}_{\text{div}}^{q}))

with
p,q\in[2,\infty)
, the stochastic convolution is defined as:

(S\diamond g)(t):=\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)g(s)\,\mathrm{d}W_{H}(s),\quad t\in[0,T].

We now proceed to introduce the concept of a global mild solution as defined
in
[
53
, pp. 978]
and
[
54
, pp. 1396–1397]
.
Fix any
\varrho\in(0,\infty)
.
Let
V_{\varrho}
denote the space of all
\dot{H}^{\varrho,2}
-valued,

\mathbb{F}
-adapted processes
f:\Omega\times[0,T]\to\dot{H}^{\varrho,2}
that
have almost surely continuous paths.
Two processes
f
and
g
in
V_{\varrho}
are considered identical
if
f=g
in
C([0,T];\dot{H}^{\varrho,2})
holds almost surely.
For any
p\in[1,\infty)
, let
L_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}^{p}(\Omega;\dot{H}^{\varrho,2})
denote the space of all

\dot{H}^{\varrho,2}
-valued random variables
v
on the probability space

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
that are strongly
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-measurable and
satisfy
\mathbb{E}\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{\varrho,2}}^{p}<\infty
.
Assume that
y_{0}\in L_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{div}}^{2})\cap L_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}^{1}(\Omega;\dot{H}^{\varrho,2})
.
The model problem
Eq.

1.1
is said to have a global mild solution
y
in

V_{\varrho}
if, for any
R>1
, the following equality holds in

\dot{H}^{\varrho,2}
, almost surely for all
t\in[0,T]
:

\displaystyle y(t\wedge t_{R,\varrho})

\displaystyle=S(t\wedge t_{R,\varrho})y_{0}-\Big(S\ast\mathcal{P}\big[(y\cdot\nabla)y\big]\Big)(t\wedge t_{R,\varrho})

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle+\Big(S\diamond\mathcal{P}\big[\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R,\varrho}]}F(y)\big]\Big)(t\wedge t_{R,\varrho}),

where
\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R,\varrho}]}
denotes the indicator function of the time interval
[0,t_{R,\varrho}]
. The stopping time
t_{R,\varrho}
is defined as

t_{R,\varrho}:=\inf\left\{t\in[0,T]:\|y(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\varrho,2}}\geqslant R\right\},

(2.3)

with the convention that
t_{R,\varrho}=T
if this set is empty.

3
Regularity

The main result of this section is the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
y_{0}\in L_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{div}}^{2})\cap L_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}^{1}(\Omega;\dot{H}^{\varrho,2})

with
\varrho\in(1,3/2)
. Then, the model problem
Eq.

1.1
admits a unique global mild solution
y
in
V_{\varrho}
satisfying the following properties:

(i)

For some
c>0
independent of
R
,

\mathbb{P}\big(\|y\|_{C([0,T];\dot{H}^{\varrho,2})}\leqslant R\big)\geqslant 1-\frac{c}{\ln(1+R)},\quad\forall R>1.

(3.1)

(ii)

If
y_{0}\in L_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}^{4}(\Omega;\mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{div}}^{2})
, then

Remark 3.1
.

We summarize some works on the regularity of the two-dimensional SNSEs with no-slip boundary conditions as follows.

1.

For additive noise, Menaldi and Sritharan
[

proved global existence of strong solutions with almost surely continuous paths in
\mathbb{L}^{2}
. This result was extended to multiplicative noise by Sritharan and Sundar
[

2.

Under divergence-free diffusion coefficients satisfying homogeneous boundary conditions,

[

established global existence of strong pathwise
solutions with almost surely continuous paths in
\dot{H}^{1,2}

Building on this, Kukavica and Vicol
[
37

derived some moment bounds,
and Breit and Prohl
[

obtained enhanced regularity properties,
which play a pivotal role in their numerical analysis.
Additionally,
[

proved the global existence of a unique weak solution to
Eq.

1.1

while
[
54

established the unique existence of a maximal local mild solution to

1.1

The proof of this proposition will be preceded by the introduction of several lemmas that
provide the necessary tools and estimates.
We begin by revisiting some fundamental properties of the Helmholtz projection
operator
\mathcal{P}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The Helmholtz projection
\mathcal{P}
belongs to
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2},\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2})

for all
\alpha\in[0,2]
, and it induces a bounded linear operator from

\mathcal{H}^{-1,2}
to
\dot{H}^{-1,2}
.

\mathcal{P}
belongs to

\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2},\dot{H}^{\beta,q})
for every

\frac{2}{1+\beta-\alpha}\geqslant q

Proof.

The results are well-established in the literature.
Specifically, Proposition IV.3.7 in
[

demonstrates that

\mathbb{H}^{1,2}
to
\mathbb{H}^{1,2}

The same argument can be extended to show that

for all

The action of

as a bounded linear operator from

\mathcal{H}^{-1,2}
to

[

]

For the final claim, let

Given that

2.1

directly implies that

\lVert{\mathcal{P}\big[(y\cdot\nabla)y\big]}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\dot{H}^{0,2}))}\leqslant cR^{2/3},

almost surely on the event
\{t_{R^{1/3},\varrho}=T\}
.
Consequently, using
Lemma

3.2
(ii) with
the embedding
(\dot{H}^{0,2},\dot{H}^{2,2})_{1-1/p,p}\hookrightarrow\dot{H}^{\alpha,q}

for sufficiently large
p\in(2,\infty)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lVert{S\ast\big[\mathcal{P}\big[(y\cdot\nabla)y\big]\big]}\rVert_{L^{1}(\{t_{R^{1/3},\varrho}=T\};C([0,T];\dot{H}^{\alpha,q}))}\leqslant cR^{2/3}.

(3.15)

From
Lemma

2.1
(iii) we have

\lVert{\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R^{1/3},\varrho}]}F(y)}\rVert_{\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,T);\gamma(H,\mathbb{H}^{1,2}))}\leqslant cR^{1/3}
,
which implies, by
Lemma

3.1
, for any
0<\beta<1/q
.

\displaystyle\lVert{\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R^{1/3},\varrho}]}\mathcal{P}F(y)}\rVert_{\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,T);\gamma(H,\dot{H}^{\beta,q}))}\leqslant cR^{1/3}.

Using
[
54
, Theorem 3.5]
and
the embedding
(\dot{H}^{\beta,q},\dot{H}^{2+\beta,q})_{1/2-1/p,p}\hookrightarrow\dot{H}^{\alpha,q}

for sufficiently large
\beta\in(0,1/q)
and
p\in(2,\infty)
,
we get

\displaystyle\left\lVert S\diamond\left(\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R^{1/3},\varrho}]}\mathcal{P}F(y)\right)\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega;C([0,T];\dot{H}^{\alpha,q}))}\leqslant cR^{1/3}.

(3.16)

From the identity

y=S(\cdot)y_{0}-S\ast\mathcal{P}\big[(y\cdot\nabla)y\big]+S\diamond\big[\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R^{1/3},\varrho}]}\mathcal{P}F(y)\big],

valid almost surely on
\{t_{R^{1/3},\varrho}=T\}
in
C([0,T];\dot{H}^{\varrho,2})
,
and by combining
Eqs.

3.14
,
3.15
and
3.16
, we derive

\lVert{y}\rVert_{L^{1}(\{t_{R^{1/3},\varrho}=T\};C([0,T];\dot{H}^{\alpha,q}))}\leqslant cR^{2/3}.

This, together with
Eq.

3.1
, establishes
Eq.

3.3
, thereby completing the proof of
Proposition

3.1
.

\blacksquare

4
Spatial Semidiscretization

Let
\mathcal{K}_{h}
be a conforming and quasi-uniform triangulation of
the domain
\mathcal{O}
, with each
K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}
containing
at least one interior vertex to satisfy the conditions in
[
13
]

and
[
31
]
.
Let
h
represents the maximum diameter of
the elements in
\mathcal{K}_{h}
. The closure of the union of all elements
in
\mathcal{K}_{h}
is denoted by
\mathcal{O}_{h}
. Let
\mathbb{L}_{h}
and

M_{h}
be constructed on
\mathcal{O}_{h}
using the well-known

P_{3}/P_{2}
Taylor-Hood finite element method
[

]

Specifically,

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}_{h}

\displaystyle:=\big\{u_{h}\in C(\overline{\mathcal{O}_{h}};\mathbb{R}^{2}):u_{h}|_{K}\in P_{3}(K;\mathbb{R}^{2})\text{ for each }K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}\text{ and }u_{h}=0\text{ on }\partial\mathcal{O}_{h}\big\},

\displaystyle M_{h}

\displaystyle:=\big\{\phi_{h}\in C(\overline{\mathcal{O}_{h}};\mathbb{R}):\phi_{h}|_{K}\in P_{2}(K)\text{ for each }K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}\big\},

where
P_{3}(K;\mathbb{R}^{2})
denotes the space of all vector-valued polynomials
of degree at most 3 on
K
, and
P_{2}(K)
denotes the space of all polynomials
of degree at most 2 on
K
. Functions in
\mathbb{L}_{h}
and
M_{h}
are extended by zero
to
\mathcal{O}\setminus\mathcal{O}_{h}
.
We define
\mathbb{L}_{h,\text{div}}
as the subset of
\mathbb{L}_{h}
consisting of all
u_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{h}
such that

\langle\nabla\cdot u_{h},\phi_{h}\rangle=0,\quad\forall\phi_{h}\in M_{h}.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{h}
denote the
L^{2}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})
-orthogonal projection operator onto
\mathbb{L}_{h,\text{div}}
. The discrete negative Stokes operator
A_{h}:\mathbb{L}_{h,\text{div}}\to\mathbb{L}_{h,\text{div}}
is defined by

\big\langle{A_{h}u_{h},v_{h}}\big\rangle=\big\langle{\nabla u_{h},\nabla v_{h}}\big\rangle,\quad\forall u_{h},v_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{h,\text{div}}.

Here, we recall that
\big\langle{\cdot,\cdot}\big\rangle
denotes the inner product in

L^{2}(\mathcal{O})
,
L^{2}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})
, or
L^{2}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
,
as appropriate for the context.
It is standard that, for any
\theta\in[0,1]
, there
exists
c>0
, independent of
h
, such that

\lVert{A_{h}^{\theta}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{L}^{2},\mathbb{L}^{2})}\leqslant ch^{-2\theta}.

(4.1)

For any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the space
\dot{H}_{h}^{\theta,2}
as
\mathbb{L}_{h,\text{div}}
equipped with the norm

\|u_{h}\|_{\dot{H}_{h}^{\theta,2}}:=\|A_{h}^{\theta/2}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{h})},\quad\forall u_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{h,\text{div}}.

By Theorem 16.1 in
[
56
]
, the complex interpolation
theory
[
39
, Theorem 2.6]
applies to the spaces
\dot{H}_{h}^{\theta,2}
,

\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, with constants independent of
h
.
As an application, for any
\theta\in[0,1]
, there exists
c>0
, independent of
h
, such that

\|u_{h}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\theta,2}}\leqslant c\|u_{h}\|_{\dot{H}_{h}^{\theta,2}},\quad\forall u_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{h,\text{div}}.

(4.2)

We study the following spatial semidiscretization:

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}y_{h}(t)=-\big[A_{h}y_{h}(t)+\mathcal{P}_{h}G(y_{h}(t))\big]\mathrm{d}t+\mathcal{P}_{h}F(y_{h}(t))\mathrm{d}W_{H}(t),\quad t\in[0,T],\\
y_{h}(0)=\mathcal{P}_{h}y_{0},\end{cases}

(4.3)

where the operator

G(u):=(u\cdot\nabla)u+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\cdot u)u,\quad\forall u\in\mathbb{H}^{1,2}.

(4.4)

By the classical theory of finite-dimensional stochastic differential equations (see, e.g., Theorem 3.27 of
[
44
]
),
for any initial condition

4.3

The main results of this section are summarized in the following theorems.

(4.5)

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume
y_{0}\in L_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}^{p}(\Omega;\dot{H}^{\varrho,2})

with

and

(4.6)

To prove these theorems, we introduce several auxiliary lemmas. For convenience, throughout this section,

,

in the stopping time

,

Firstly, we summarize some standard results about the semigroup generated by

-A_{h}
in
\dot{H}_{h}^{0,2}
, defined by
S_{h}(t):=\exp(-tA_{h})
for all

The deterministic convolution of

with

is
given by

(S_{h}\ast g_{h})(t):=\int_{0}^{t}S_{h}(t-s)g_{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,\quad t\in[0,T].

The stochastic convolution of
S_{h}
with

g_{h}\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(\Omega\times(0,T);\gamma(H,\dot{H}_{h}^{0,2}))

is defined as

(S_{h}\diamond g_{h})(t):=\int_{0}^{t}S_{h}(t-s)g_{h}(s)\,\mathrm{d}W_{H}(s),\quad t\in[0,T].

Lemma 4.1
.

The following properties of the semigroup
S_{h}
hold:

(i)

For any
0\leqslant\alpha\leqslant\beta\leqslant 2
and
t\in(0,\infty)
, the following estimates hold:

\displaystyle\lVert{S_{h}(t)}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{h}^{\alpha,2},\dot{H}_{h}^{\beta,2})}\leqslant ct^{(\alpha-\beta)/2},

\displaystyle\lVert{I-S_{h}(t)}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{h}^{\beta,2},\dot{H}_{h}^{\alpha,2})}\leqslant ct^{(\beta-\alpha)/2}.

(ii)

For any
g_{h}\in L^{p}(0,T;\dot{H}_{h}^{0,2})
with
p\in(1,\infty)
, the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle\Big\lVert\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}S_{h}\ast g_{h}\Big\rVert_{L^{p}(0,T;\dot{H}_{h}^{0,2})}+\lVert S_{h}\ast g_{h}\rVert_{L^{p}(0,T;\dot{H}_{h}^{2,2})}

\displaystyle\quad{}+\|S_{h}\ast g_{h}\|_{C([0,T];(\dot{H}_{h}^{0,2},\dot{H}_{h}^{2,2})_{1-1/p,p})}\leqslant c\|g_{h}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;\dot{H}_{h}^{0,2})}.

(iii)

For any
g_{h}\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);\gamma(H,\dot{H}_{h}^{0,2}))
with
p\in[2,\infty)
, the following estimate holds:

\|S_{h}\diamond g_{h}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];(\dot{H}_{h}^{0,2},\dot{H}_{h}^{2,2})_{1/2-1/p,p}))}\leqslant c\|g_{h}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);\gamma(H,\dot{H}_{h}^{0,2}))}.

Proof.

The operator
A_{h}
is symmetric and positive-definite on
\dot{H}_{h}^{0,2}
, satisfying

\langle A_{h}v_{h},v_{h}\rangle=\|\nabla v_{h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\quad\forall v_{h}\in\dot{H}_{h}^{0,2}.

This implies that the smallest eigenvalue of
A_{h}
is uniformly bounded away from zero with respect
to
h
. As a result, part (i) can be established
through direct computation involving the spectral decomposition of
A_{h}
; for related techniques, see Chapter 3 of
[
51
]
.
For part (ii), we refer the reader to Theorems 4.24 and 4.29, and Corollary 1.14 in
[
39
]
.
Regarding part (iii), the reader may consult, for example, Theorems 2.5 and 3.5 in
[
54
]
and Theorem 10.2.24 in
[
35
]
; see also
[
45
, Theorem 6.20]
.
∎

Secondly, we present some results on the Helmholtz projection operator
\mathcal{P}

and the discrete Helmholtz projection operator
\mathcal{P}_{h}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

The following assertions hold:

(i)

For any
\alpha\in[0,2]
, it holds that

\lVert{I-\mathcal{P}_{h}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}^{\alpha,2},\mathbb{L}^{2})}\leqslant ch^{\alpha}
.

(ii)

For all
\alpha\in[0,2]
,
\mathcal{P}_{h}
is uniformly bounded in
\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}^{\alpha,2},\dot{H}_{h}^{\alpha,2})
with respect to
h
.

(iii)

For all
u_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{h,\mathrm{div}}
and
v\in\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2}
with

\alpha\in[0,2]
, the following duality estimate holds:

\displaystyle\langle u_{h},v-\mathcal{P}v\rangle\leqslant ch^{\alpha+1}\,\|\nabla u_{h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\,\|v\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2}}.

(4.8)

(iv)

For any
\alpha\in[0,2]
, the following inequality holds:

\lVert{\mathcal{P}_{h}(I-\mathcal{P})}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2},\mathbb{L}^{2})}\leqslant ch^{\alpha}.

(4.9)

(v)

For any
\alpha\in[0,1]
, the following approximation property holds:

\lVert{I-\mathcal{P}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}_{h}^{\alpha,2},\mathbb{L}^{2})}\leqslant ch^{\alpha}.

(4.10)

(vi)

For any
\alpha\in[0,1/2)
,

\mathcal{P}_{h}
is uniformly bounded in
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2},\dot{H}_{h}^{\alpha,2})
with respect to
h
.

Proof.

Assertions (i) and (ii) are well-known results, and their proofs are omitted. We now prove assertion (iii). Fix
u_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{h,\mathrm{div}}
and
v\in\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2}
with
\alpha\in[0,2]
. By Lemma III.1.2 in
[
25
]
and Theorem III.4.3 in
[
7
]
, there exists
\varphi\in H^{\alpha+1,2}
such that
v-\mathcal{P}v=\nabla\varphi
, with

\|\varphi\|_{H^{\alpha+1,2}}\leqslant c\|v\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2}}.

(4.11)

The proof proceeds as follows:

\displaystyle\langle u_{h},v-\mathcal{P}v\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle u_{h},\nabla\varphi\rangle=-\langle\nabla\cdot u_{h},\varphi\rangle\quad\text{(integration by parts)}

\displaystyle=-\langle\nabla\cdot u_{h},\varphi-\Pi_{h}\varphi\rangle\quad\text{(since }u_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{h,\text{div}}\text{)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\nabla\cdot u_{h}\|_{L^{2}}\,\|\varphi-\Pi_{h}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\|\nabla u_{h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\,\|\varphi-\Pi_{h}\varphi\|_{L^{2}},

where
\Pi_{h}\varphi
is the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection of
\varphi

onto
M_{h}
. Combining
Eq.

4.11
with the standard approximation estimate

\|\varphi-\Pi_{h}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant ch^{\alpha+1}\,\|\varphi\|_{H^{\alpha+1,2}},

yields the desired estimate
Eq.

4.8
.

For assertion (iv), applying
Eq.

4.8
yields for
\alpha\in[0,2]
and
u\in\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2}
:

\displaystyle\lVert{\mathcal{P}_{h}(u-\mathcal{P}u)}\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\big\langle{\mathcal{P}_{h}(u-\mathcal{P}u),u-\mathcal{P}u}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant ch^{\alpha+1}\lVert{\nabla\mathcal{P}_{h}(u-\mathcal{P}u)}\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\lVert{u}\rVert_{\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant ch^{\alpha}\lVert{\mathcal{P}_{h}(u-\mathcal{P}u)}\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\lVert{u}\rVert_{\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2}},\quad\text{(by \lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{eq:inverse})}

which implies
Eq.

4.9
.

We now proceed to prove assertion (v). For any
u_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{h,\mathrm{div}}
,
there exists
\varphi\in H^{1,2}
such that
u_{h}-\mathcal{P}u_{h}=\nabla\varphi
with
\lVert{\varphi}\rVert_{H^{1,2}}\leqslant c\lVert{u_{h}-\mathcal{P}u_{h}}\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\lVert{u_{h}-\mathcal{P}u_{h}}\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\big\langle{u_{h}-\mathcal{P}u_{h},\nabla\varphi}\big\rangle=-\big\langle{\nabla\cdot u_{h},\varphi}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant ch\lVert{\nabla u_{h}}\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\lVert{\varphi}\rVert_{H^{1,2}}\leqslant ch\lVert{u_{h}}\rVert_{\dot{H}_{h}^{1,2}}\lVert{u_{h}-\mathcal{P}u_{h}}\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}.

By invoking
Eq.

4.1
, it follows that
Eq.

4.10
holds
for all
\alpha\in[0,1]
. Here, we have used techniques analogous
to those applied in proving assertion (iii).

Finally, we prove assertion (vi). Fix
\alpha\in[0,1/2)
.
Using
Eq.

4.9
and the inverse estimate
Eq.

4.1
, we infer that

\lVert{\mathcal{P}_{h}u}\rVert_{\dot{H}_{h}^{\alpha,2}}\leqslant\lVert{\mathcal{P}_{h}\mathcal{P}u}\rVert_{\dot{H}_{h}^{\alpha,2}}+c\lVert{u}\rVert_{\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2}},\quad\forall u\in\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2}.

By
Lemma

3.1
and assertion (ii) of this lemma,

\lVert{\mathcal{P}_{h}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2},\dot{H}_{h}^{\alpha,2})}

is uniformly bounded in
h
. This completes the proof of assertion (vi) and the lemma.
∎

Thirdly, for any
\alpha\in[0,1]
, we define the operator
\mathscr{P}_{h}:\dot{H}^{-\alpha,2}\to\mathbb{L}_{h,\mathrm{div}}
by

\mathscr{P}_{h}:=\mathcal{P}_{h}(-\Delta_{2})A_{2}^{-1},

where:

•

A_{2}
extends to a bounded linear operator from
\dot{H}^{2-\alpha,2}
to
\dot{H}^{-\alpha,2}
via the duality pairing:

\big\langle{A_{2}u,v}\big\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-\alpha,2},\dot{H}^{\alpha,2}}:=\big\langle{A_{2}^{1-\alpha/2}u,A_{2}^{\alpha/2}v}\big\rangle,\quad\forall u\in\dot{H}^{2-\alpha,2},\,\forall v\in\dot{H}^{\alpha,2}.

This extension admits a bounded inverse
A_{2}^{-1}
from
\dot{H}^{-\alpha,2}
to
\dot{H}^{2-\alpha,2}
.

•

-\Delta_{2}
extends to a bounded linear operator from
\mathcal{H}^{2-\alpha,2}
to
\mathcal{H}^{-\alpha,2}
via the duality pairing:

\big\langle{-\Delta_{2}u,v}\big\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{-\alpha,2},\mathcal{H}^{\alpha,2}}:=\big\langle{(-\Delta_{2})^{1-\alpha/2}u,(-\Delta_{2})^{\alpha/2}v}\big\rangle,\quad\forall u\in\mathcal{H}^{2-\alpha,2},\,\forall v\in\mathcal{H}^{\alpha,2}.

•

\mathcal{P}_{h}
extends to
\mathcal{H}^{-\alpha,2}\to\mathbb{L}_{h,\mathrm{div}}
by

\langle\mathcal{P}_{h}f,u_{h}\rangle=\langle f,u_{h}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{-\alpha,2},\mathcal{H}^{\alpha,2}},\quad\forall f\in\mathcal{H}^{-\alpha,2},\ u_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{h,\mathrm{div}}.

This operator satisfies the following properties.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\alpha\in[0,2]
. The following assertions hold:

(i)

For any
u_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{h,\mathrm{div}}
and
v\in\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2}
, we have

\displaystyle\big\langle{u_{h},\,(\mathscr{P}_{h}\mathcal{P}-I)v}\big\rangle\leqslant ch^{\alpha+1}\lVert{\nabla u_{h}}\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\lVert{v}\rVert_{\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2}}.

(ii)

For any
u\in\mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{div}}^{2}\cap\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2}
,
it holds that
\lVert{(\mathcal{P}_{h}-\mathscr{P}_{h})u}\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\leqslant ch^{\alpha}\lVert{u}\rVert_{\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2}}
.

(iii)

The operator norm satisfies

\lVert{I-\mathscr{P}_{h}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}^{\alpha,2},\mathbb{L}^{2})}\leqslant ch^{\alpha}
.

Proof.

To begin with assertion (i), consider any
u_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{h,\mathrm{div}}
and
v\in\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2}
. By definition,

\displaystyle\langle u_{h},(\mathscr{P}_{h}\mathcal{P}-I)v\rangle=\left\langle u_{h},\,(\mathcal{P}-I)\big(\Delta_{2}A_{2}^{-1}\mathcal{P}v+v\big)\right\rangle.

Applying Lemma
4.2
(iii) yields

\displaystyle\langle u_{h},(\mathscr{P}_{h}\mathcal{P}-I)v\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant ch^{\alpha+1}\lVert{\nabla u_{h}}\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\left(\lVert{\Delta_{2}A_{2}^{-1}\mathcal{P}v}\rVert_{\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2}}+\lVert{v}\rVert_{\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2}}\right).

Using Theorem IV.5.8 from
[
7
]
and interpolation arguments, we deduce

\lVert{\Delta_{2}A_{2}^{-1}\mathcal{P}v}\rVert_{\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2}}\leqslant c\lVert{v}\rVert_{\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2}},

which, combined with the previous inequality, completes the proof for assertion (i).

For assertion (ii), let
u\in\mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{div}}^{2}\cap\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2}
. Then,

\displaystyle\lVert{(\mathcal{P}_{h}-\mathscr{P}_{h})u}\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle(\mathcal{P}_{h}-\mathscr{P}_{h})u,(\mathcal{P}_{h}-\mathscr{P}_{h})u\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(\mathcal{P}_{h}-\mathscr{P}_{h})u,(I-\mathscr{P}_{h}\mathcal{P})u\rangle.

By assertion (i) and the inverse estimate

4.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lVert{(\mathcal{P}_{h}-\mathscr{P}_{h})u}\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant ch^{\alpha}\lVert{(\mathcal{P}_{h}-\mathscr{P}_{h})u}\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\lVert{u}\rVert_{\mathbb{H}^{\alpha,2}},

which implies the desired conclusion for assertion (ii).

Finally, for assertion (iii), given the identity

I-\mathscr{P}_{h}=(I-\mathcal{P}_{h})+(\mathcal{P}_{h}-\mathscr{P}_{h}),

the assertion follows by combining Lemma
4.2
(i) with assertion (ii).
This concludes the proof.
∎

Fourthly, we present some auxiliary estimates.

Lemma 4.4
.

Assume that the hypotheses of Theorem
4.2
are fulfilled. Let

\xi_{h}:=S_{h}\ast(\mathscr{P}_{h}A_{2}y-A_{h}\mathscr{P}_{h}y).

Then, for any
0<h<1/2
and
R>1
, the following inequalities hold almost surely on
the set
\{\lVert{y_{0}}\rVert_{\dot{H}^{\varrho,2}}\leqslant R\}
:

\displaystyle\lVert{y-\mathscr{P}_{h}y-\xi_{h}}\rVert_{C([0,t_{R,\varrho}];\mathbb{L}^{2})}\leqslant ch^{\varrho}\left(\ln\frac{1}{h}\right)R,

(4.12)

\displaystyle\lVert{\mathscr{P}_{h}y+\xi_{h}}\rVert_{C([0,t_{R,\varrho}];\mathbb{L}^{\infty})}\leqslant cR,

(4.13)

\displaystyle\lVert{\nabla(\mathscr{P}_{h}y+\xi_{h})}\rVert_{C([0,t_{R,\varrho}];\mathbb{L}^{q_{0}})}\leqslant cR,

(4.14)

where
q_{0}\in(2,\frac{2}{2-\varrho})
.

Proof.

We split the proof into the following two steps.

Step 1.

Let
u\in\dot{H}^{\varrho,2}
and define
u_{h}:=A_{h}^{-1}\mathscr{P}_{h}A_{2}u
. By definition,

\langle\nabla(u-u_{h}),\nabla v_{h}\rangle=0,\quad\forall v_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{h,\mathrm{div}}.

Using

along with the inequality

\mathbb{E}\lVert{(\mathcal{P}_{h}-\mathscr{P}_{h})y_{0}}\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{p}\leqslant ch^{\varrho p},

(4.27)

which is justified by
Lemma

4.3
(ii) and the initial condition
y_{0}\in L_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}^{p}(\Omega;\dot{H}^{\varrho,2})
,
and applying Young’s inequality, we conclude that for every
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|\eta_{h,R}(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{p}+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R,\varrho}]}(s)\|\eta_{h,R}(s)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{p-2}\|\nabla\eta_{h,R}(s)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle cR^{p+2}h^{\varrho p}+cR^{2}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R,\varrho}]}(s)\Big(\|\eta_{h,R}(s)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{p}+\|(y-y_{h})(s)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{p}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg].

Using the decomposition
y-y_{h}=y-\mathscr{P}_{h}y-\xi_{h}-\eta_{h}
and the inequality
Eq.

4.12
, we infer that for any
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|\eta_{h,R}(t)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{p}+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R,\varrho}]}(s)\|\eta_{h,R}(s)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{p-2}\|\nabla\eta_{h,R}(s)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant cR^{p+2}h^{\varrho p}\big(\ln\frac{1}{h}\big)^{p}+cR^{2}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R,\varrho}]}(s)\|\eta_{h,R}(s)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}s\right].

Applying Gronwall’s inequality, we obtain the bound

\|\eta_{h,R}\|_{C([0,T];L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{L}^{2}))}+\left\lVert\|\eta_{h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\|\nabla\eta_{h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{\frac{2}{p}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,t_{R,\varrho}))}\leqslant ch^{\varrho}\ln\frac{1}{h}\exp(cR^{2}).

(4.28)

Step 6.

Applying Itô’s formula to

\frac{1}{2}\|\eta_{h}(\cdot\wedge t_{R,\varrho})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}
,
we obtain almost surely that for any
t\in[0,T]
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\eta_{h}(t\wedge t_{R,\varrho})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lVert{(\mathcal{P}_{h}-\mathscr{P}_{h})y_{0}}\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\overbrace{\int_{0}^{t}(I_{1}(s)+I_{2}(s)+I_{3}(s)+I_{4}(s))\,\mathrm{d}s}^{II_{1}(t)}

\displaystyle\quad{}+\overbrace{\int_{0}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R,\varrho}]}(s)\left\langle\eta_{h}(s),\,\mathcal{P}_{h}F(y_{h}(s))-\mathscr{P}_{h}\mathcal{P}F(y(s))\right\rangle\,\mathrm{d}W_{H}(s)}^{II_{2}(t)}.

For
II_{1}
, using the bounds from
Eqs.

4.23
,
4.24
,
4.25
and
4.26
,
we deduce

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|II_{1}\|_{C([0,T])}^{p/2}\leqslant cR^{2p}h^{\varrho p}+cR^{p}\left\lVert y-y_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,t_{R,\varrho});\mathbb{L}^{2})}^{p}.

For
II_{2}
, an argument analogous to the one in
Eq.

4.26
yields

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R,\varrho}]}(s)\left\lVert\left\langle\eta_{h}(s),\,\mathcal{P}_{h}F(y_{h}(s))-\mathscr{P}_{h}\mathcal{P}F(y(s))\right\rangle\right\rVert_{\gamma(H,\mathbb{R})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle c\|\eta_{h}(s)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\big(\|(y-y_{h})(s)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+h^{2\varrho}R^{2\varrho}\big),\quad\text{$\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}\otimes\mathrm{d}s$-a.e.}

Hence, using the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality and Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|II_{2}\|_{C([0,T])}^{p/2}\leqslant cR^{\varrho p}h^{\varrho p}+c\lVert{\eta_{h}}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,t_{R,\varrho});\mathbb{L}^{2})}^{p}+c\lVert{y-y_{h}}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,t_{R,\varrho});\mathbb{L}^{2})}^{p}.

Combining the estimates for
II_{1}
and
II_{2}
,
along with
Eq.

4.27
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lVert{\eta_{h}(\cdot\wedge t_{R,\varrho})}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}))}\leqslant cR^{2}h^{\varrho}+c\lVert{\eta_{h}}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,t_{R,\varrho});\mathbb{L}^{2})}+cR\lVert{y-y_{h}}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,t_{R,\varrho});\mathbb{L}^{2})}.

Combining this inequality with
Eqs.

4.28
and
4.12
,
and using the identity
y-y_{h}=y-\mathscr{P}_{h}y-\xi_{h}-\eta_{h}
,
we obtain the desired error estimate
Eq.

4.6
.
This completes the proof of
Theorem

4.2
.

\blacksquare

Proof of
Theorem

4.3
.

Given that
\langle G(u_{h}),u_{h}\rangle=0
for all
u_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{h,\mathrm{div}}
, and considering
\|\mathcal{P}_{h}F(u_{h})\|_{\gamma(H,\dot{H}_{h}^{0,2})}\leqslant c(1+\|u_{h}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}})
for all
u_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{h,\mathrm{div}}

(as justified by Lemma
2.1
(ii) and the ideal property
[
35
, Theorem 9.1.10]
), an application of Itô’s formula, combined with the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality, leads to the uniform boundedness of
\|y_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}))}
with respect to
h
. This, together with (
3.2
), implies the uniform boundedness of
\|y-y_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}))}
. Furthermore, we derive the estimate:

\displaystyle\|y-y_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}))}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|y-y_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\{t_{R,\varrho}=T\};C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}))}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\mathbb{P}(t_{R,\varrho}<T)\right)^{1/4}\|y-y_{h}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}))},

where
t_{R,\varrho}
is defined by
Eq.

2.3
with
R>1
.
By (
3.1
) and (
4.6
), there exist constants
c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}>0
, independent of
h
and
R
, such that:

\displaystyle\|y-y_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{L}^{2}))}\leqslant c_{0}h^{\varrho}\ln\frac{1}{h}\exp(c_{1}R^{2})+\frac{c_{2}}{\left(\ln(1+R)\right)^{1/4}}.

Selecting

R=\sqrt{\frac{\varrho\ln\frac{1}{h}-\ln\left(\ln\frac{1}{h}\right)-\frac{1}{4}\ln\left(\ln\left(\ln\frac{1}{h}\right)\right)}{c_{1}}},

and choosing
h_{0}\in(0,\exp(-3))
sufficiently small, we obtain the error estimate (
4.7
) for all
0<h<h_{0}
. This completes the proof of Theorem
4.3
.

\blacksquare

5
Full Discretization

Let
J
be a positive integer and denote the time step by
\tau:=T/J
.
This section is devoted to analyzing the following full discretization:

\begin{cases}\displaystyle Y_{j+1}-Y_{j}=-\tau\big(A_{h}Y_{j+1}+\mathcal{P}_{h}G(Y_{j+1})\big)+\mathcal{P}_{h}\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}F(Y_{j})\,\mathrm{d}W_{H}(t),\,0\leqslant j<J,\\
Y_{0}=\mathcal{P}_{h}y_{0},\end{cases}

(5.1)

where
G
is defined by
Eq.

4.4
.
For a thorough discussion of the solvability and stability properties of this discretization, we refer the reader to Section 3 of
[
15
]
.

We begin our analysis by establishing the pathwise uniform convergence of the full discretization

Eq.

5.1
to the spatial semidiscretization
Eq.

4.3
on a specified local probability set.
The following theorem formalizes this result.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
y_{0}\in L_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{div}}^{2})\cap L_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}^{1}(\Omega;\dot{H}^{\varrho,2})
for some
\varrho\in(1,3/2)
, and let
h,\tau\in(0,1/2)
. Suppose
y_{h}
is the strong solution of the spatial semidiscretization
Eq.

4.3
, and
(Y_{j})_{j=0}^{J}
is the solution to the full discretization
Eq.

5.1
. For any
R_{h},R_{h,\tau}>1
, define the local probability set

\Omega_{\varrho,R_{h},R_{h,\tau}}^{h,\tau}:=\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\,\lVert{y_{h}}\rVert_{C([0,T];\dot{H}_{h}^{\varrho,2})}\leqslant R_{h},\,\max_{0\leqslant j\leqslant J}\|Y_{j}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\leqslant R_{h,\tau}\right\}.

(5.2)

Then, there exists a constant
c>0
, independent of
h
,
\tau
,
R_{h}
, and
R_{h,\tau}
, such that

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega_{\varrho,R_{h},R_{h,\tau}}^{h,\tau}}\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant J}\lVert{y_{h}(j\tau)-Y_{j}}\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]\leqslant c\tau\exp(cR_{h}^{2})\left(1+\ln\left(\frac{1}{h}\right)R_{h,\tau}^{2}\right).

(5.3)

Proof.

We introduce the stopping time

t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}:=\inf\{t\in[0,T]:\,\lVert{y_{h}(t)}\rVert_{\dot{H}_{h}^{\varrho,2}}\geqslant R_{h}\},

(5.4)

with the convention that
t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}=T
if the set is empty.
Motivated by equation (4.5) from
[
11
]
, we define the sequence

(Y_{j,R_{h}})_{j=1}^{J}
by

\begin{cases}Y_{j+1,R_{h}}-Y_{j,R_{h}}=-\displaystyle\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}]}(t)\bigl[A_{h}Y_{j+1,R_{h}}+\mathcal{P}_{h}G(Y_{j+1,R_{h}})\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}t\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad{}+\mathcal{P}_{h}\displaystyle\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}]}(t)\,F(Y_{j,R_{h}})\,\mathrm{d}W_{H}(t),\quad\quad\text{for }0\leqslant j<J,\\
Y_{0,R_{h}}=\mathcal{P}_{h}y_{0}.\end{cases}

By
[
2
, Theorem 7.2]
,
Y_{j}=Y_{j,R_{h}}
holds almost surely on the
event
\{{j\tau}\leqslant t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}\}
for all
1\leqslant j\leqslant J
.
We also define the truncated semidiscrete solution

y_{h,R_{h}}(t):=y_{h}(t\wedge t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}),\quad\text{for }0\leqslant t\leqslant T.

(5.5)

Throughout this proof,
c
denotes a generic positive constant, independent of

h
,
\tau
,
R_{h}
, and
R_{h,\tau}
, which may vary from one occurrence to another.
The remainder of the proof is organized into
five steps.

Step 1.

By definition, for any
0\leqslant j<J
, we have almost surely

\displaystyle y_{h,R_{h}}({j\tau+\tau})-y_{h,R_{h}}({j\tau})

\displaystyle=-\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}]}(t)\bigl[A_{h}y_{h,R_{h}}(t)+\mathcal{P}_{h}G(y_{h,R_{h}}(t))\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\mathcal{P}_{h}\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}]}(t)F(y_{h,R_{h}}(t))\,\mathrm{d}W_{H}(t).

For each
0\leqslant j\leqslant J
, define
E_{j,R_{h}}:=Y_{j,R_{h}}-y_{h,R_{h}}({j\tau})
, and decompose
E_{j,R_{h}}=\xi_{j,R_{h}}+\eta_{j,R_{h}}
, where
(\xi_{j,R_{h}})_{j=0}^{J}
satisfies

\begin{cases}\xi_{j+1,R_{h}}-\xi_{j,R_{h}}=-\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}]}(t)\,A_{h}\xi_{j+1,R_{h}}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}]}(t)\,A_{h}\bigl[y_{h,R_{h}}(t)-y_{h,R_{h}}({j\tau+\tau})\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}t,&0\leqslant j<J,\\
\xi_{0,R_{h}}=0.\end{cases}

It follows that, for any
0\leqslant j<J
, almost surely,

\displaystyle\eta_{j+1,R_{h}}-\eta_{j,R_{h}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}]}(t)A_{h}\eta_{j+1,R_{h}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\mathcal{P}_{h}\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}]}(t)\bigl[G(y_{h,R_{h}}(t))-G(Y_{j+1,R_{h}})\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\mathcal{P}_{h}\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}]}(t)\bigl[F(Y_{j,R_{h}})-F(y_{h,R_{h}}(t))\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}W_{H}(t).

Taking the
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{L}^{2})
-inner product with
\eta_{j+1,R_{h}}
, summing over
j=0,\dots,m
for
0\leqslant m<J
, and applying Hölder’s inequality and Itô’s isometry, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\|\eta_{m+1,R_{h}}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{j=0}^{m}\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}]}(t)\big\langle-A_{h}\eta_{j+1,R_{h}},\,\eta_{j+1,R_{h}}\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{j=0}^{m}\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}]}(t)\big\langle G(y_{h,R_{h}}(t))-G(y_{h,R_{h}}({j\tau+\tau})),\,\eta_{j+1,R_{h}}\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{j=0}^{m}\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}]}(t)\big\langle G(y_{h,R_{h}}({j\tau+\tau}))-G(Y_{j+1,R_{h}}),\,\eta_{j+1,R_{h}}\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{j=0}^{m}\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}]}(t)\|F(Y_{j,R_{h}})-F(y_{h,R_{h}}(t))\|_{\gamma(H,\mathbb{L}^{2})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg]

\displaystyle=:I_{m}^{(1)}+I_{m}^{(2)}+I_{m}^{(3)}+I_{m}^{(4)}.

Step 2.

Let us estimate
I_{m}^{(1)}
and
I_{m}^{(2)}
.
For
I_{m}^{(1)}
, the definition of
A_{h}
yields

\displaystyle I_{m}^{(1)}=-\mathbb{E}\left[\,\sum_{j=0}^{m}\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}]}(t)\lVert{\nabla\eta_{j+1,R_{h}}}\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\right].

(5.6)

For
I_{m}^{(2)}
, considering the definition of
G
in
Eq.

4.4
, an application of integration by parts leads to the following identity for any
0\leqslant j\leqslant m
and
t\in[{j\tau},{j\tau+\tau}]
:

\displaystyle\big\langle\eta_{j+1,R_{h}},\,G(y_{h,R_{h}}(t))-G(y_{h,R_{h}}({j\tau+\tau}))\big\rangle

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\left\langle\eta_{j+1,R_{h}},\,\left[\big(y_{h,R_{h}}(t)-y_{h,R_{h}}({j\tau+\tau})\big)\cdot\nabla\right]\Big(y_{h,R_{h}}(t)-\frac{1}{2}y_{h,R_{h}}({j\tau+\tau})\Big)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad{}+\left\langle\eta_{j+1,R_{h}},\,\Big[\nabla\cdot\Big(\frac{1}{2}y_{h,R_{h}}(t)-y_{h,R_{h}}({j\tau+\tau})\Big)\Big]\Big(y_{h,R_{h}}(t)-y_{h,R_{h}}({j\tau+\tau})\Big)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad{}-\frac{1}{2}\Big\langle\big[\big(y_{h,R_{h}}(t)-y_{h,R_{h}}({j\tau+\tau})\big)\cdot\nabla\big]\eta_{j+1,R_{h}},\,y_{h,R_{h}}({j\tau+\tau})\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\quad{}-\Big\langle\big[y_{h,R_{h}}({j\tau+\tau})\cdot\nabla\big]\eta_{j+1,R_{h}},\,y_{h,R_{h}}(t)-y_{h,R_{h}}({j\tau+\tau})\Big\rangle.

By Hölder’s inequality, the
h
-independent embeddings (see
Remark

5.1
)

\dot{H}_{h}^{\varrho,2}\hookrightarrow W^{1,2/(2-\varrho)}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{2}),\quad\dot{H}_{h}^{\varrho,2}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}^{\infty},\quad\dot{H}_{h}^{1,2}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}^{q}\text{ for all }q\in(2,\infty),

and the definition of
t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}
in
Eq.

5.4
, we derive, for any
0\leqslant j<J
and
t\in[j\tau,j\tau+\tau]
,

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}]}(t)\,\big\langle\eta_{j+1,R_{h}},\,G(y_{h,R_{h}}(t))-G(y_{h,R_{h}}({j\tau+\tau}))\big\rangle

(5.7)

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle cR_{h}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}]}(t)\,\lVert{y_{h,R_{h}}(t)-y_{h,R_{h}}({j\tau+\tau})}\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\lVert{\nabla\eta_{j+1,R_{h}}}\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{2}},\quad\text{$\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}\otimes\mathrm{d}t$-a.e.}

Consequently,

\displaystyle I_{m}^{(2)}\leqslant\mathbb{E}\left[\,\sum_{j=0}^{m}\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}]}(t)\Big(\frac{1}{4}\lVert{\nabla\eta_{j+1,R_{h}}}\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+cR_{h}^{2}\lVert{y_{h,R_{h}}(t)-y_{h,R_{h}}({j\tau+\tau})}\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t\,\right].

(5.8)

Step 3.
We now proceed to estimate
I_{m}^{(3)}
and
I_{m}^{(4)}
.
Firstly, observe that the definition of
t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}
in
Eq.

5.4
implies that, almost surely,

\Big\lVert\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}]}A_{h}\big[y_{h,R_{h}}-y_{h,R_{h}}(j\tau+\tau)\big]\Big\rVert_{C((j\tau,j\tau+\tau];\dot{H}_{h}^{\varrho-2,2})}\leqslant 2R_{h}\quad\text{for all $0\leqslant j<J$,}

which allows us to apply
Lemma

5.1
to deduce that

\displaystyle\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant J}\lVert{\xi_{j,R_{h}}}\rVert_{\dot{H}_{h}^{(1+\varrho)/2,2}}\leqslant cR_{h},\quad\text{almost surely.}

(5.9)

For any
0\leqslant j\leqslant m
and
t\in[{j\tau},{j\tau+\tau}]
, a direct calculation
using integration by parts yields

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}]}(t)\,\Big\langle G(y_{h,R_{h}}({j\tau+\tau}))-G(Y_{j+1,R_{h}}),\,\eta_{j+1,R_{h}}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}]}(t)\,\biggl\{-\frac{1}{2}\Big\langle\eta_{j+1,R_{h}},\,(E_{j+1,R_{h}}\cdot\nabla)\big(y_{h,R_{h}}({j\tau+\tau})+\xi_{j+1,R_{h}}\big)\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad{}+\frac{1}{2}\Big\langle(E_{j+1,R_{h}}\cdot\nabla)\eta_{j+1,R_{h}},\,y_{h,R_{h}}({j\tau+\tau})+\xi_{j+1,R_{h}}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad{}+\frac{1}{2}\Big\langle\big(\nabla\cdot y_{h,R_{h}}({j\tau+\tau})\big)\xi_{j+1,R_{h}},\,\eta_{j+1,R_{h}}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad{}+\Big\langle\big(y_{h,R_{h}}({j\tau+\tau})\cdot\nabla\big)\eta_{j+1,R_{h}},\,\xi_{j+1,R_{h}}\Big\rangle\biggr\}.

Thus, following the argument in
Eq.

5.7
and utilizing the stability estimate
Eq.

5.9
along with the
equality
E_{j+1,R_{h}}=\xi_{j+1,R_{h}}+\eta_{j+1,R_{h}}
, we infer that

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}]}(t)\,\Big\langle G(y_{h,R_{h}}({j\tau+\tau}))-G(Y_{j+1,R_{h}}),\,\eta_{j+1,R_{h}}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle cR_{h}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}]}(t)\,\|\nabla\eta_{j+1,R_{h}}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\big(\|\xi_{j+1,R_{h}}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+\|\eta_{j+1,R_{h}}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\big),\quad\text{$\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}\otimes\mathrm{d}t$-a.e.}

Consequently,

I_{m}^{(3)}\leqslant\mathbb{E}\left[\,\sum_{j=0}^{m}\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}]}(t)\Big(\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla\eta_{j+1,R_{h}}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+cR_{h}^{2}\|\xi_{j+1,R_{h}}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+cR_{h}^{2}\|\eta_{j+1,R_{h}}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t\,\right].

(5.10)

For
I_{m}^{(4)}
, we apply the Lipschitz continuity of
F

from
Lemma

2.1
(i) and the identity

\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}]}(t)\bigl(Y_{j,R_{h}}-y_{h,R_{h}}(t)\bigr)=\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}]}(t)\bigl(\xi_{j,R_{h}}+\eta_{j,R_{h}}+y_{h,R_{h}}({j\tau})-y_{h,R_{h}}(t)\bigr),

for all
0\leqslant j<J
and
t\in[{j\tau},{j\tau+\tau}]
, to derive the following estimate:

I_{m}^{(4)}\leqslant c\mathbb{E}\left[\,\sum_{j=0}^{m}\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}]}(t)\big(\lVert{y_{h,R_{h}}(t)-y_{h,R_{h}}({j\tau})}\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\lVert{\xi_{j,R_{h}}}\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\lVert{\eta_{j,R_{h}}}\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t\,\right].

(5.11)

Step 4.

For any
p\in(2/(\varrho-1),\infty)
, applying
Lemma

5.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant J}\|\xi_{j,R_{h}}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right]\leqslant c\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\int_{{j\tau}}^{{j\tau+\tau}}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t_{R_{h}}^{h,\varrho}]}(t)\|y_{h,R_{h}}(t)-y_{h,R_{h}}({j\tau+\tau})\|_{\dot{H}_{h}^{\varrho-1,2}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{2/p}\right].

In conjunction with Lemma
5.3
, this inequality leads us to

\mathbb{E}\Big[\,\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant J}\|\xi_{j,R_{h}}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\,\Big]\leqslant c\tau R_{h}^{2}(1+\tau^{(5-3\varrho)/2}R_{h}^{2}).

(5.12)

Furthermore, Lemma

also implies that

Remark 5.1
.

The above proof relies on standard discrete Sobolev embeddings.
For completeness, we provide key details. Let
c>0

denote a generic

For

\displaystyle\lVert{A_{2}^{-1}\mathcal{P}A_{h}u_{h}}\rVert_{\dot{H}^{\varrho,2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\lVert{A_{h}u_{h}}\rVert_{\dot{H}_{h}^{\varrho-2,2}}\quad\text{(by Lemma~\ref{lem:Ph}(ii))}

\displaystyle=c\lVert{u_{h}}\rVert_{\dot{H}_{h}^{\varrho,2}}.

\displaystyle\lVert{w_{h}}\rVert_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}\leqslant c\lVert{u_{h}}\rVert_{\dot{H}_{h}^{\varrho,2}}.

By

(5.23)

For

Similar to Theorem 5.15 in

6
Conclusions

This study presents a comprehensive analysis of a fully discrete numerical scheme for the two-dimensional stochastic Navier-Stokes equations with multiplicative noise, subject to no-slip boundary conditions. The spatial discretization employs the standard
P_{3}/P_{2}
Taylor-Hood finite element method, while the temporal discretization utilizes the Euler scheme. We have rigorously established the pathwise uniform convergence of the full discretization in probability, demonstrating nearly
3/2
-order spatial convergence and near half-order temporal convergence.

However, our study does not address the pressure field, and establishing comparable convergence results for it remains an open and interesting question (see Remark
5.2
). Moreover, to the best of our knowledge, there is a lack of reported convergence rates under general spatial
L^{q}
-norms for finite-element-based approximations of the stochastic Navier-Stokes equations. We believe that conducting a thorough numerical analysis under these norms could yield valuable insights and represents a promising direction for future research.

Most significantly, establishing similar pathwise uniform convergence estimates in probability for the three-dimensional Navier-Stokes equations remains a significant challenge. A key reason for this is the widely acknowledged fact that global mild solutions generally do not exist for the three-dimensional case without the inclusion of additional regularization terms in the governing equations; see, for instance,
[
16
,
47
]
.

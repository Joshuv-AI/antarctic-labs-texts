1.
Introduction

Given a field
K
, a polynomial
F\in K[X]
and a positive integer
n
, let
F^{(n)}(X)
be the
n
-th fold composition of
F
with itself. Some past works have dealt with polynomials
F
in which the iterates
F^{(n)}(X)
present special factorization over
K
: see
[
2
,
3
,
18
]
. The main case of study is when all the iterates
F^{(n)}(X),n\geq 1
are irreducible over
K
: such polynomials are called
stable
. There are a few constructions of stable polynomials
[
7
,
20
]
, where the field of coefficients
K
is a number field or a function field.
In
[
20
]
, Odoni proves the stability of “generic” additive polynomials with coefficients in a function field and compute the Galois group of the iterates of such polynomials. Recall that a polynomial
f\in K[X]
is additive if the identity
f(X+Y)=f(X)+f(Y)
holds in the polynomial ring
K[X,Y]
. If
K
has positive characteristic
p
, the additive polynomials in
K[X]
are precisely those of the form
\sum_{i=0}^{h}a_{i}X^{p^{i}}
with
a_{i}\in K
. However, in the case where
K
is finite, such polynomials are usually not stable. In fact, no
affine polynomial
(i.e.,
A(X)+b
with
A(X)
additive and
b\in K
) of degree at least
2
can be stable if
K
is finite: see Theorem 10 in
[
1
]
for more details.

The stability of polynomials over finite fields have been explored primarily in the case of quadratic polynomials. A relaxation of stable polynomials is considered in
[
12
]
, yielding the so called
settled polynomials
. A more general problem on the factorization of
F^{(n)}(X)
is proposed in
[
4
]
in the case where
K
is finite. In particular, the problem suggests to explore some arithmetic functions associated to
F^{(n)}(X)
such as the degrees of the irreducible factors (largest, average) and the number of irreducible factors. For more details, see Question 18.9 in
[
4
]
. Many issues related to Question 18.9 were eariler discussed in
[
10
]
and, more recently, in
[
24
]
.

Still in the finite field setting, an arithmetic function that can be considered is
s_{F}(n)
, the smallest positive integer such that
F^{(n)}(X)
splits completely over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{s_{F}(n)}}
. For instance,
F
is stable if and only if
s_{F}(n)=\deg(F)^{n}
for every
n\geq 1
. As previously mentioned, in
[
1
]
the authors prove that no affine polynomial of degree at least
2
and with coefficients in a finite field can be stable. In
[
19
]
, Odoni proved something much stronger for additive polynomials: if
A\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
is an additive polynomial not of the form
aX^{p^{h}}
, then
s_{A}(n)
grows linearly with respect to
n
. More precisely, he proved that the sequence
\{s_{A}(n)\cdot n^{-1}\}_{n\geq 1}
is bounded and its set of limit points equals
[\alpha_{A},p\alpha_{A}]
for some
\alpha_{A}>0
. For more details, see Theorem 2 in
[
19
]
. Odoni’s result implies a refined information on the factorization of iterated additive polynomials: the iterates of additive polynomials typically splits into many distinct irreducible factors, all of small degree. He ends the paper with an interesting example, where the constant
\alpha_{A}
is explicitly computed: if
q=p
, then
s_{X^{p}-X}(n)=p^{\lceil\log_{p}n\rceil}
for every
n\geq 1
and, in particular,
\alpha_{X^{p}-X}=1
.

Odoni’s proof on the growth of
s_{A}(n)
with
A
additive relies on transferring the problem to the skew polynomial ring
\mathbb{F}_{q}[X,\sigma]
: this is a natural model for the ring of additive polynomials (with respect to the ordinary sum and composition of polynomials). In this approach, the problem of computing
s_{A}(n)
translates into computing valuations of elements in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[X,\sigma]
with respect to some ideals of the same ring. However, the latter is quite nontrivial: since the ring
\mathbb{F}_{q}[X,\sigma]
is not commutative, we no longer have many useful factorization properties of elements and ideals. In particular, the computations in the paper are involved and technical. Moreover, in his approach he only deals with a subsequence
\{s_{A}(n\nu)\cdot(n\nu)^{-1}\}_{n\geq 1}
with fixed
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
, noticing that such subsequence controls the oscilation of the whole sequence. We actually believe that he was trying to derive a simple closed formula like the one
s_{X^{p}-X}(n)=p^{\lceil\log_{p}n\rceil}
for
q=p
. For more details, see Sections 3, 4 and 5 of
[
19
]
.

In this paper, we study the function
s_{A}(n)
in more details and recover Odoni’s results in a richer form. In particular, we prove that in many cases we have
s_{A}(n)=\alpha p^{\lceil\log_{p}\beta n\rceil}
if
n
is sufficiently large, where
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Q}
do not depend on
n
and can explictly obtained from some initial values
s_{A}(1),\ldots,s_{A}(s_{0})
. We also derive a weaker result for affine polynomials
A(X)+b
and, in particular, we prove that the degree of the splitting fields of the iterates of such polynomials also present linear growth. The latter is a major obstruction in Odoni’s approach: there is no identification of affine polynomials in the polynomial ring
\mathbb{F}_{q}[X,\sigma]
. Before we state our main results, we comment that the linear growth is the slowest possible growth for the the degree of the splitting field of “generic” iterated polynomials.

Remark 1.1
.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a finite field of characteristic
p
and assume that
F\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
is not of the form
aX^{s}+b
, where
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
and either
b=0
or
b\neq 0
and
s=p^{h}
with
h\geq 0
. According to Corollary 2.5 of
[
24
]
, there exists an absolute constant
c_{F}>0
such that, for every positive integer
n
, the polynomial
F^{(n)}(X)
contains an irreducible factor over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with degree at least
c_{F}\cdot n
. The latter readily implies that
\frac{s_{F}(n)}{n}\geq c_{F}
for every
n\geq 1
.
If
F(X)=aX^{s}+b
with
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
and either
b=0
or
b\neq 0
and
s=p^{h}
with
h\geq 0
, it is direct to verify that
s_{F}(n)=1
for every
n\geq 1
. In particular, these polynomials are genuine exceptions.

Our main result goes as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
q
be a power of a prime
p
, let
A(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
be an additive polynomial not of the form
aX^{p^{h}}
and, for each
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, set
A_{b}(X)=A(X)+b
. Then the following hold:

(i)

for every
n\geq 1
, we have
s_{A}(np)\in\{s_{A}(n),p\cdot s_{A}(n)\}
and, in particular, there exists
0\leq i<\log_{p}n+1
such that

(1)

s_{A}(n)=s_{A}(1)\cdot p^{i}.

(ii)

for every
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
and every
n\geq 1
, we have

s_{A_{b}}(n)\in\{s_{A}(n),p\cdot s_{A}(n)\}
;

(iii)

the sequence
\left\{\frac{s_{A}(n)}{n}\right\}_{n\geq 1}
is bounded and its set of limit points equals
[c_{A},pc_{A}]
for some
c_{A}>0
;

(iv)

if
c_{A}
is as in the previous item, the sequence
\left\{\frac{s_{A_{b}}(n)}{n}\right\}_{n\geq 1}
is bounded and its set of limit points is contained in the interval
[c_{A},p^{2}c_{A}]
;

(v)

c_{A^{(k)}}=k\cdot c_{A}
for every
k\geq 1
.

Moreover, for
s_{0}=\max\{j>0\,:\,s_{A}(j)=s_{A}(1)\}
, there exists
A_{*}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
of the form
R^{q}
with
R\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
additive and an integer
r\geq 1
such that

(X^{q^{s_{A}(1)}}-X)^{q^{r}}=X^{q^{s_{A}(1)+r}}-X^{q^{r}}=A_{*}(A^{(s_{0})}(X)).

In particular,
c_{A}\leq\frac{s_{A}(1)}{s_{0}}
with equality if and only if the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear map
z\mapsto A_{*}(z)
is not nilpotent over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{s_{A}(1)}}
. In the case of equality, we also have

(2)

s_{A}(n)=s_{A}(1)p^{\left\lceil\log_{p}\frac{n}{s_{0}}\right\rceil},\,n>s_{0}.

Next we provide one numerical example, showing the applicability of Theorem
1.2
.

Example 1.3
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{4}
be such that
\alpha^{2}=\alpha+1
and set
A(X)=X^{8}+\alpha X\in\mathbb{F}_{4}[X]
. A direct computation yields
s_{A}(1)=3
and
s_{A}(2)=6
, hence
s_{0}=1
. Moreover, the polynomial
A_{*}(X)=X^{32}+\alpha^{2}X^{4}=(X^{8}+\alpha^{2}X)^{4}
satisfies

A_{*}(A(X))=X^{256}-X^{4}.

It is direct to verify that, in this case, the map
z\mapsto A_{*}(z)
is not nilpotent over
\mathbb{F}_{4^{3}}
. Theorem
1.2
implies
c_{A}=3
and

s_{A}(n)=3p^{\lceil\log_{p}n\rceil},n\geq 2.

Remark 1.4
.

In the context of Theorem
1.2
we see that Eq. (
2
) provides a simple closed formula for
s_{A}(n)
whenever the companion polynomial
A_{*}(X)
does not induce a nilpotent linear map over the splitting field of
A(X)
. Additive polynomials yielding nilpotent linear maps over extensions of finite fields were previously introduced and explored by the author in
[
22
]
. However, we discuss other issues there, namely the construction and cycle decomposition of permutation polynomials arising from nilpotent additive polynomials. Nevertheless, such polynomials yield very special dynamical systems over finite fields: if
N\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
is additive such that
z\mapsto N(z)
is nilpotent over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{M}}
, then
0\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{M}}
is the only periodic point under this map. In particular, the corresponding directed graph representation of the map
z\mapsto N(z)
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{M}}
(called
functional graph
) contains only one connected component. In the proof of Theorem
1.2
we prove that the condition on
A_{*}(X)
being nilpotent is also equivalent to the following: for each positive integer
n
, there exists
k=k(n)
such that
A_{*}^{(k(n))}(X)
is divisible by
A^{(n)}(X)
. Since
A,A_{*}
are additive, we have that
A_{*}(0)=A(0)=0
and so the latter implies that

A^{-\infty}(0)\subseteq A_{*}^{-\infty}(0)
. Here, for a polynomial
F\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
, the set

F^{-\infty}(0)=\bigcup_{j\geq 0}\{y\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\,:\,F^{(j)}(y)=0\}

is just the
reversed orbit
of
0
under the map
z\mapsto F(z)
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.

If we assume that
A(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
is of the form
\sum_{i=0}^{m}a_{i}X^{q^{i}}
, we directly obtain an explicit formula for
s_{A}(n)
like the one given in Eq. (
2
): in particular, we can compute the constant
c_{A}
. The latter generalizes the case
A(X)=X^{p}-X
given by Odoni and, in fact, it covers the class of additive polynomials
A
with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[X]
(i.e., we are simply considering the case
q=p
). For more details, see Theorem
4.1
.

As applications of Theorem
1.2
, we also provide statistics for the factorization of iterated affine polynomials and for the number of periodic points under maps
z\mapsto A(z)
with
A
additive. First, we need the following definition.

Definition 1.5
.

For each nonconstant polynomial
F\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
and each positive integer
n
, let
N_{F}(n)
be the number of distinct irreducible factors of
F^{(n)}(X)
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Moreover, if
f_{1,n},\ldots,f_{N_{F}(n),n}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
are the distinct irreducible factors of
F^{(n)}(X)
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we set

\rho_{F}(n)=\frac{1}{N_{F}(n)}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{F}(n)}\deg(f_{i,n}),

the average degree of the
f_{i,n}
’s.

In the following theorem we prove that
\rho_{B}(n)
also grows linearly with respect to
n
if
B
is a generic affine polynomial.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
be an additive polynomial that is not of the form
aX^{p^{h}}
, fix
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and set
B(X)=A(X)+b
. Then there exists
\alpha_{B},\beta_{B}>0
such that
\frac{\rho_{B}(n)}{n}\in[\alpha_{B},\beta_{B}]
for every
n\geq 1
.

Proposition 5.18 in
[
24
]
essentially proves Theorem
1.6
in the case where
A(X)
is of the form
\sum_{i=0}^{m}a_{i}X^{q^{i}}
. However, this case is much simpler since we can even describe the degree distribution of the irreducible factors of
A^{(n)}(X)
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. In particular, the techniques employed there cannot be extended to arbitrary additive polynomials. See Subsection 5.2 in
[
24
]
for more details.

The following theorem entails that the proportion of periodic points under additive polynomial maps is, in general, irregular through the finite extensions of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
be an additive polynomial and, for each integer
n
, let
\pi_{A}(n)
be the number of elements in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
that are periodic under the map
z\mapsto A(z)
. Then the limit

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\pi_{A}(n)}{q^{n}},

does not exist unless
A(X)=aX^{p^{h}}
for some
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and some
h\geq 0
. In the latter case, the limit equals
0
if
a=0
and equals
1
if
a\neq 0
.

Observe that
\frac{\pi_{A}(n)}{q^{n}}
is just the
proportion
of points in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
that are periodic with respect to the map
z\mapsto A(z)
. The latter can be considered in a more general context, where
A
is replaced by a generic polynomial
F\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
. Very little is known on the behaviour of
\frac{\pi_{F}(n)}{q^{n}}
: some related problems are considered in
[
9
,
13
]
. More recently, in
[
6
]
the proportion of periodic points is explored in the case of quadratic maps over the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
, where the analogue limit equals zero (up to some exceptional cases). An earlier work
[
16
]
explored the proportion of periodic points for the case of monomials and Chebyshev polynomials. In particular, for these two classes of polynomials, the corresponding limit does not exist in general. Along with monomials and Chebyshev polynomials, additive polynomials have
wild
dynamical behaviour. For instance, these three families of polynomials cover most of the known
exceptional polynomials
(i.e., polynomials whose induced map permute infinitely many finite extensions of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
). For more details on exceptional polynomials, see Section 8.4 of
[
17
]
.

It is worthy mentioning that the dynamics of Chebyshev polynomials and monomials are well understood by means of their
functional graphs
(a directed graph representation of the iteration of maps). In fact, the results
in
[
21
]
imply a simple description of the graphs for such polynomials and also for some special additive polynomials.
On the other hand, the dynamics of arbitrary additive polynomials over finite fields is only well understood from the Linear Algebra point of view: see
[
5
,
8
,
11
,
14
,
25
]
. Our proof for Theorem
1.7
relies on finding finite extensions of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
containing small/large number of roots of the iterates of
A
. However, we do not obtain a complete description of the dynamics of the map
z\mapsto A(z)
. Instead, those extensions are detected by employing item (i) of Theorem
1.2
, combined with Theorem
4.2
, a general basic result on the structure of periodic points of linear maps over finite fields. In particular we prove that, as
n\to\infty
, the quotient
\frac{\pi_{A}(n)}{q^{n}}
must oscilate between zero and a positive constant unless
A(X)=aX^{p^{h}}
for some
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and some
h\geq 0
.

In the proof of our results we employ many arithmetic properties of additive polynomials, regarding them as elements of the polynomial ring
\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
but also as maps over the algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. A crucial tool is Lemma
2.1
, where we compile many elementary results on the composition of additive polynomials over finite fields, including some commuting properties.

We end this section with the structure of the paper. In Section 2 we provide basic machinery and in Section 3 we prove our main result, Theorem
1.2
. Finally, in Section 4 we provide an enhanced version of Theorem
1.2
that applies to a subclass of additive polynomials, along with some applications of the same theorem.

2.
Preparation

For the rest of this paper, we fix
q
a power of a prime
p
. In this short section we provide two technical lemmas that will be frequently employed.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
be additive polynomials with
A(X)
nonzero. Then
A(X)
divides
B(X)
if and only if there exists another additive polynomial
C\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
such that
B(X)=C(A(X))
. Moreover, if
B(X)=C(A(X))
and
B(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{s}a_{i}X^{q^{i}}
for some
a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, then the following hold:

(i)

A(C(X))=B(X)
, i.e.,
A(C(X))=C(A(X))
;

(ii)

if
A(X)=A_{0}^{(k)}(X)
for some
k\geq 1
and some additive
A_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
, then
A_{0}(C(X))=C(A_{0}(X))
.

Proof.

The first statement is a routine exercise and can be proved, for instance, by induction on the degree of
B
; see also Exercise 3.68 in
[
15
]
. For the proof of item (i), it suffices to prove that
A(X)
and
B(X)
commute. In fact, in this case, the equality
B(X)=C(A(X))
implies that

B(A(X))=A(B(X))=A(C(A(X))),

and so by setting
Y=A(X)
we obtain the polynomial identity
B(Y)=A(C(Y))
. The fact that
A(X)
and
B(X)
commute follows by induction on the number of nonzero coefficients in
A(X)
so we omit details. Item (ii) follows in a similar way, noticing that the polynomials
A_{0}(X)
and
B(X)
commute.
∎

Given a polynomial
f(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{r}a_{i}X^{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
, set
L_{f}(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{r}a_{i}X^{q^{i}}
. The polynomial
L_{f}(X)
is commonly known as the
q
-linearized associate
to
f(X)
. The following lemma is easily verified.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
f,g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
, the following hold:

(a)

L_{f+g}(X)=L_{f}(X)+L_{g}(X)
;

(b)

L_{fg}(X)=L_{f}(L_{g}(X))
. In particular, for every
n\geq 1
, we have
L_{f}^{(n)}(X)=L_{f^{n}}(X)
.

(c)

L_{c}(X)=cX
for every
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section we prove our main result. We fix the following important notation: for integers
r,s\geq 0
with
s>0
, set

\mathcal{S}_{s,r}(X)=X^{q^{s+r}}-X^{q^{r}}=(X^{q^{s}}-X)^{q^{r}}.

Lemma
2.2
implies the following important identity that will be further used: for every integer
i\geq 0
,

(3)

\mathcal{S}_{s,r}^{(p^{i})}(X)=\mathcal{S}_{sp^{i},rp^{i}}(X).

We have the following auxiliary result.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
be a nonzero additive polynomial and let
j
be a positive integer. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

A(X)
splits completely in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{j}}
;

(ii)

A(X)
divides
\mathcal{S}_{j,r}(X)
for some
r\geq 0
;

(iii)

there exists
r\geq 0
and an additive polynomial
B\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
such that
B(A(X))=\mathcal{S}_{j,r}(X)
.

Moreover, if
A
is separable, we can assume that
r=0
.

Proof.

For the implication (i)
\to
(ii), assume that
A(X)
splits completely in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{j}}
. Hence every irreducible divisor of
A(X)
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
divides
X^{q^{j}}-X
.
Take
r
large enough so that, in the factorization of
A(X)
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, no irreducible factor has multiplicity greater than
q^{r}
. Hence
A(X)
divides
(X^{q^{j}}-X)^{q^{r}}=\mathcal{S}_{j,r}(X)
. The implication (ii)
\to
(iii) follows directly by Lemma
2.1
. For the implication (iii)
\to
(i), assume that
B(A(X))=\mathcal{S}_{j,r}(X)
. Again, from Lemma
2.1
, it follows that
A(X)
divides
\mathcal{S}_{j,r}(X)=(X^{q^{j}}-X)^{q^{r}}
and so the roots of
A(X)
are also roots of
X^{q^{j}}-X
. In other words,
A(X)
splits completely in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{j}}
. Finally, if
A(X)
is separable, then
A(X)
divides
\mathcal{S}_{j,r}(X)=(\mathcal{S}_{j,0}(X))^{q^{r}}
if and only if it divides
S_{j,0}(X)
.
∎

We end this part with an important remark that is used along the way without further mention. If
A\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
is nonzero and additive, then
A(0)=0
and so the splitting field of
A^{(n)}(X)
contains the splitting field of
A^{(j)}(X)
whenever

. In particular, for every
1\leq j<n
, the number
s_{A}(j)
divides
s_{A}(n)
.

We prove the items separately.

(i)

Fix

and let
\mathbb{F}_{q^{K}}
be the splitting field of
A^{(n)}(X)
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, that is,
s_{A}(n)=K
. From Lemma
2.1
, there exists an additive polynomial
B\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
and an integer
r\geq 0
such that

(4)

B(A^{(n)}(X))=\mathcal{S}_{K,r}(X),

and the polynomials
A,B
commute. In particular, taking the
p
-th iterate on both sides of Eq. (
4
), we obtain

B^{(p)}(A^{(np)}(X))=\mathcal{S}_{K,r}^{(p)}(X)=\mathcal{S}_{pK,pr}(X).

From Lemma
3.1
, the polynomial
A^{(np)}(X)
splits completely in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{pK}}
. Therefore,
s_{A}(np)
divides
pK
.
Recall that, as
np>n
, the number
s_{A}(np)
is divisible by
s_{A}(n)=K
. In particular, we obtain

s_{A}(np)\in\{K,pK\}=\{s_{A}(n),p\cdot s_{A}(n)\}.

The latter readily implies that
s_{A}(p^{j})\leq s_{A}(1)\cdot p^{j}
for every
j\geq 0
. Recall that
s_{A}(m)
divides
s_{A}(n)
if
m<n
. In particular, it is clear that for every
n\geq 1
we have
s_{A}(n)=s_{A}(1)p^{i}
for some
i\geq 0
. Moreover, if
n\geq 1
and
j=\lceil\log_{p}n\rceil<1+\log_{p}n
, we have
n\leq p^{j}
and so

s_{A}(n)\leq s_{A}(p^{j})\leq s_{A}(1)\cdot p^{j}.

(ii)

It is direct to verify that there exists a sequence
\{\beta_{\ell}\}_{\ell\geq 1}
of elements in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
A_{b}^{(\ell)}(X)=A^{(\ell)}(X)+\beta_{\ell}
for every
\ell\geq 1
. Now fix
n\geq 1
and let
\mathbb{F}_{q^{S}}
and
\mathbb{F}_{q^{T}}
be the splitting fields of
A_{b}^{(n)}(X)
and
A^{(n)}(X)
, respectively. In particular, the roots of
A_{b}^{(n)}(X)
comprise an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-affine space
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q^{S}}
whose difference set
\mathcal{L}-\mathcal{L}=\{y-z\,:\,y,z\in\mathcal{L}\}
coincides with the set of the roots of
A^{(n)}(X)
. Therefore, the roots of
A^{(n)}(X)
lie in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{S}}
and so
T
divides
S
. Conversely, since
A^{(n)}(X)
splits completely in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{T}}
, Lemma
3.1
entails that
\mathcal{S}_{T,r}(X)=B(A^{(n)}(X))
for some additive polynomial
B\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
and some
r\geq 0
. In particular,

\mathcal{S}_{T,r}(X)\equiv-B(\beta_{n})\pmod{A_{b}^{(n)}(X)}.

However, recall that
\beta_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
B\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
, hence
B(\beta_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and so
\mathcal{S}_{T,r}(X)
vanishes at
-B(\beta_{n})
. In conclusion,

\mathcal{S}_{T,r}^{(2)}(X)\equiv\mathcal{S}_{T,r}(-B(\beta_{n}))\equiv 0\pmod{A_{b}^{(n)}(X)}.

Since
p\geq 2
, we obtain

\mathcal{S}_{Tp,rp}(X)=\mathcal{S}_{T,r}^{(p)}(X)\equiv\mathcal{S}_{T,r}^{(p-2)}(0)\equiv 0\pmod{A_{b}^{(n)}(X)}.

Observe that, for
y\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, we have
S_{Tp,rp}(y)=0
if and only if
y\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{Tp}}
. Hence
A_{b}^{(n)}(X)
splits completely in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{pT}}
and so
S
divides
pT
. Since
T
divides
S
, we conclude that
S\in\{T,pT\}
. In other words,

s_{A_{b}}(n)\in\{s_{A}(n),p\cdot s_{A}(n)\}.

(iii)

For simplicity, we write
M=s_{A}(1)
and
\gamma_{A}(n)=\frac{s_{A}(n)}{n}
. Item (i) entails that
s_{A}(n)<Mp^{1+\log_{p}n}=Mpn
for every
n\geq 1
. In particular, the sequence
\left\{\gamma_{A}(n)\right\}_{n\geq 1}
is bounded. For each integer
i\geq 0
, item (i) and Remark
1.1
entail that

s_{i}=\max\{j>0\,:\,s_{A}(j)=Mp^{i}\},

is a well defined positive integer. Since
ps_{i}>s_{i}
, we obtain
s_{A}(ps_{i})\geq Mp^{i+1}
. Item (i) implies
s_{A}(ps_{i})\leq Mp^{i+1}
and so
s_{A}(ps_{i})=Mp^{i+1}
. In particular, it follows by the definition that
s_{i+1}\geq ps_{i}
. The latter combined with the identity
s_{A}(s_{i+1})=ps_{A}(s_{i})
implies that the sequence
{\bf\Gamma}:=\{\gamma_{A}(s_{i})\}_{i\geq 0}
is non increasing. Since
A(X)
is not of the form
aX^{p^{j}}
, Remark
1.1
entails that
{\bf\Gamma}
is bounded below by a positive real number. Hence there exists
c_{A}>0
such that

\lim\limits_{i\to+\infty}\gamma_{A}(s_{i})=c_{A}.

If
n
is an integer with
s_{i}<n\leq s_{i+1}
, then
s_{A}(n)=Mp^{i+1}
and so

\gamma_{A}(s_{i+1})=\frac{Mp^{i+1}}{s_{i+1}}\leq\gamma_{A}(n)=\frac{Mp^{i+1}}{n}<\frac{Mp^{i+1}}{s_{i}}=p\gamma_{A}(s_{i}).

In particular,

c_{A}=\liminf\limits_{n\to+\infty}\gamma_{A}(n)\leq\limsup\limits_{n\to+\infty}\gamma_{A}(n)\leq pc_{A}.

The latter implies that the set of limit points of
\{\gamma_{A}(n)\}_{n\geq 0}
is contained in the interval
[c_{A},pc_{A}]
. Since such set of limit points must be closed, it suffices to prove that the sequence
\left\{\gamma_{A}(n)\right\}_{n\geq 1}
is dense in the open interval
(c_{A},pc_{A})
. For each real number
\alpha\in(1,p)
and each integer
i\geq 0
, set
t_{i}=\left\lceil\frac{ps_{i}}{\alpha}\right\rceil
. In particular,
t_{i}=\frac{ps_{i}}{\alpha}+\kappa_{i}
for some
\kappa_{i}\in[0,1)
. It follows by the definition that
s_{i}<t_{i}\leq ps_{i}
. Recall that
s_{i+1}\geq ps_{i}
, hence
s_{A}(t_{i})=Mp^{i+1}=ps_{A}(s_{i})
. Since
\{s_{i}\}_{i\geq 0}
is unbounded, the sequence

\gamma_{A}(t_{i})=\frac{ps_{A}(s_{i})}{t_{i}}=\frac{\alpha s_{A}(s_{i})}{s_{i}+\frac{\alpha}{p}\kappa_{i}},\,i\geq 0\,,

converges to
\alpha c_{A}
.

(iv)

This item follows by item (iii) and the following restatement of item (ii):

\frac{s_{A_{b}}(n)}{n}\in\left\{\frac{s_{A}(n)}{n},\frac{ps_{A}(n)}{n}\right\},\,n\geq 1.

(v)

Fix
k
a positive integer. We employ the notation of the previous items. Observe that
s_{A^{(k)}}(n)=s_{A}(nk)
for every
n\geq 1
and so
\gamma_{A^{(k)}}(n)=k\gamma_{A}(n)
. From this fact and item (iii), we conclude that the set of limit points of
\{\gamma_{{A}^{(k)}}(n)\}_{n\geq 1}
is contained in the interval
[kc_{A},pkc_{A}]
. Hence
c_{A^{(k)}}\geq kc_{A}
and
pc_{A^{(k)}}\leq pkc_{A}
. In conclusion,
c_{A^{(k)}}=kc_{A}
.

We proceed to the proof of the remaining statements in Theorem
1.2
. From hypothesis, the polynomial
A^{(s_{0})}(X)
splits completely in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{M}}
, hence Lemma
3.1
entails that there exists
r\geq 0
and

such that

A_{*}(A^{(s_{0})}(X))=X^{q^{s_{A}(1)+r}}-X^{q^{r}}.

Replacing
r
by
r+1
and
A_{*}
by
A_{*}^{q}
if necessary, we can always assume that
r\geq 1
and that the polynomial
A_{*}
is of the form
R^{q}
with
R\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
additive. As before, for each integer
i\geq 0
, set
s_{i}=\max\{j>0\,:\,s_{A}(j)=Mp^{i}\}
, where
M=s_{A}(1)
. From Lemma
2.1
, the polynomials
A
and
A_{*}
commute. In particular, as in the proof of item (i), we obtain

A_{*}^{(p^{i})}(A^{(s_{0}p^{i})}(X))=X^{q^{Mp^{i}+rp^{i}}}-X^{q^{rp^{i}}}=\mathcal{S}_{Mp^{i},rp^{i}}(X).

Hence
s_{i}\geq s_{0}p^{i}
with equality if and only if
A_{*}^{(p^{i})}(X)
is not of the form
E(A(X))
for some additive

. In particular,
\frac{s_{A}(s_{i})}{s_{i}}\leq\frac{M}{s_{0}}
with equality if and only if

. Recall that the sequence
\left\{\frac{s_{A}(s_{i})}{s_{i}}\right\}_{i\geq 0}
is non increasing and, by the definition, it converges to

.
Hence
c_{A}\leq\frac{M}{s_{0}}
with equality if and only if
\frac{s_{A}(s_{i})}{s_{i}}=\frac{M}{s_{0}}
for every

, that is,
s_{i}=s_{0}p^{i}
for every

. As we have seen, the latter is equivalent to

not being of the form
E(A(X))
with

additive, for every

. Since

is of the form

with

additive, we see that
A_{*}^{(n)}(X)
is divisible by

for some

if and only if
A_{*}^{(m)}(X)
is divisible by

for some

. Taking

, and using the fact that

and

commute, we see that the following statements are equivalent.

•

There do not exist
i\geq 0
such that
A_{*}^{(p^{i})}(X)
is of the form

with

additive.

•

There do not exist
n\geq 1
such that
A_{*}^{(n)}(X)
is of the form

with

additive.

•

There do not exist

such that

is of the form

E(A_{*}(A^{(s_{0})}(X)))=E((X^{q^{M}}-X)^{q^{r}}),

with

additive.

•

There do not exist
n\geq 1
such that
A_{*}^{(n)}(X)
is divisible by

•

There do not exist

such that
A_{*}^{(n)}(X)

X^{q^{M}}-X
;

•

There do not exist

such that
A_{*}^{(n)}(y)=0
for every

.

•

The
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear map

is not nilpotent over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{M}}
.

Now, if we assume that
z\mapsto A_{*}(z)
is not nilpotent over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{M}}
, we proved that
s_{i}=s_{0}p^{i}
for every

. Take

and let

be the unique integer such that

3.1.1.
Comments on Theorem
1.2

Observe that, for real numbers
\alpha,\beta>0
, the function
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
given by
f(n)=\alpha p^{\lceil\log_{p}\beta n\rceil}
has the following properties: it is an increasing step function such that
f(\mathbb{N})=\{\alpha p^{j}\,:\,j\geq 0\}
and, for

a_{j}:=\#f^{-1}((0,\alpha p^{j}])=\left\lfloor\frac{p^{j}}{\beta}\right\rfloor,

we have that

\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\frac{a_{j}}{a_{j+1}}=\frac{1}{p}
. In particular, we may conclude that the graph of
f(n)
resembles a fractal.

Although Theorem
1.2
does not guarantee that the function
s_{A}(n)
has the form
\alpha p^{\lceil\log_{p}\beta n\rceil}
for generic
A
, we comment that its graph shares some of the forementioned features. Item (i) in Theorem
1.2
readily implies that
s_{A}(n)
is a step function with image set of the form
\{\alpha p^{j}\,:\,j\geq 0\}
. Finally, looking at the proof of item (iii) of Theorem
1.2
, we see that
\{a_{j}\}_{j\geq 0}
coincides with the sequence
\{s_{j}\}_{j\geq 0}
, and we proved that
s_{j+1}\geq ps_{j}
for every
j\geq 0
. In particular, we conclude that
\limsup\limits_{j\to\infty}\frac{a_{j}}{a_{j+1}}\leq\frac{1}{p}
. However, our results are not sufficient to obtain any conclusion on the existence of the limit
\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\frac{a_{j+1}}{a_{j}}
.

We believe that in
[
20
]
, Odoni was trying to explore a similar issue. In fact, in his results on the behaviour of the function
s_{A}(n)
, he proves that
s_{A}(n+r_{n})\in\{s_{A}(n),ps_{A}(n)\}
whenever
n
is sufficiently large and
\{r_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
satisfies
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{r_{n}}{n}=0
: see Lemma 5.2 of
[
20
]
for more details. As
p\geq 2
, item (i) in Theorem
1.2
provides a much stronger result in this direction: we only require that
r_{n}\leq(p-1)n
.

In this section we discuss some applications and improvements on Theorem

.

4.1.
Iterating special additive polynomials

Here we obtain an enhanced version of Theorem
1.2
in the special case where
A
is
q
-linearized, i.e.,
A(X)
is of the form
\sum_{i=0}^{m}a_{i}X^{q^{i}}
. The latter is equivalent to
A(X)=L_{f}(X)
for some
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
. For simplicity, we assume that
A
is separable, i.e.,
f(X)
is not divisible by
X
. The general case follows easily: indeed, if
f(X)=X^{r}F(X)
with
\gcd(F(X),X)=1
, then

L_{f}^{(n)}(X)=L_{F}^{(n)}(X)^{q^{rn}}.

In particular,
s_{A}(n)=s_{B}(n)
for every
n\geq 0
, where
B(X)=L_{F}(X)
.
For a polynomial
g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
that is not divisible by
X
, it is direct to verify that there exists
i\geq 1
such that
g(X)
divides
X^{i}-1
. The smallest positive integer with this property is the
order
of
g
, which we denote by
\mathrm{ord}(g)
. By a simple calculation, we verify that
g(X)
divides
X^{s}-1
if and only if
s
is divisible by
\mathrm{ord}(g)
. From these facts, we obtain the following explicit result that generalizes the observation given by Odoni in the case
A(X)=X^{p}-X
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
be a polynomial not divisible by
X
, let
f_{0}
be its squarefree part and let
e
be the least positive integer such that
f(X)
divides
f_{0}(X)^{e}
. If
E=\mathrm{ord}(f_{0})
, then for
A=L_{f}(X)
and every
n\geq 0
, we have

s_{A}(n)=E\cdot p^{\lceil\log_{p}ne\rceil}.

In particular, if
c_{A}
is as in Theorem
1.2
, then
c_{A}=Ee
.

Proof.

By employing an Euclidean division and the properties presented in Lemma
2.2
we see that for nonzero
g,h\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
, the polynomial
g(X)
divides
h(X)
if and only if
L_{g}(X)
divides
L_{h}(X)
. In particular, since
A=L_{f}(X)
is separable and
Y^{q^{j}}-Y=L_{X^{j}-1}(Y)
, we observe that for every
s\geq 1
the following are equivalent:

•

L_{f}^{(n)}(X)=L_{f^{n}}(X)
splits completely in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}}
;

•

f(X)^{n}
divides
X^{s}-1
;

•

s
is divisible by
\mathrm{ord}(f^{n})
.

Hence
s_{A}(n)=\mathrm{ord}(f^{n})
. The equality
\mathrm{ord}(f^{n})=E\cdot p^{\lceil\log_{p}ne\rceil}
follows by item (ii) of Lemma 5 in
[
23
]
. The equality
c_{A}=Ee
follows by direct computation.
∎

4.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.6

From items (iii) and (iv) of Theorem
1.2
, there exists
\beta_{B}>0
such that, for every
n\geq 1
, the irreducible factors of
B^{(n)}(X)
have all degree at most
\beta_{B}n
, hence
\frac{\rho_{B}(n)}{n}\leq\beta_{B}
for every
n\geq 1
. It remains to provide an absolute lower bound on
\frac{\rho_{B}(n)}{n}
. We observe that the formal derivative of an affine polynomial is a constant, which is nonzero if and only if the polynomial is separable. The latter is also equivalent to the affine polynomial not being a perfect
p
-th power. Write
B(X)=B_{0}(X)^{p^{m}}
, where
m\geq 0
and
B_{0}(X)
is not of the form
S(X)^{p}
. From hypothesis,
\deg(B_{0})=p^{d}
for some
d\geq 1
. From the initial observation, it follows by induction on
n\geq 1
that
B^{(n)}(X)=\tilde{B}_{n}(X)^{p^{mn}}
for some affine separable polynomial
\tilde{B}_{n}
of degree
p^{dn}
.
In particular, the set
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
of the distinct roots of
B^{(n)}(X)
has cardinality
p^{dn}
and so it follows by the definition that

(5)

\rho_{B}(n)=\frac{p^{dn}}{N_{B}(n)}.

Our aim is to provide a non trivial bound for
N_{B}(n)
. Let
\mathbb{F}_{q^{M}}
be the splitting field of
B(X)
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and let
n
be a positive integer which we will assume to be sufficiently large. From items (i) and (ii) in Theorem
1.2
, we have that
s_{B}(n)=Mp^{t}
for some
t=t(n)
such that
0\leq t<\log_{p}n+2
. Hence
B^{(n)}(X)
splits completely in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{Mp^{t}}}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{Z}_{n}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q^{Mp^{t}}}
. Moreover, from items (iii) and (iv) in Theorem
1.2
, we have that
t\to+\infty
as
n\to+\infty
, so we can assume that
t
is sufficiently large.

As
B
is affine, for each
0\leq j\leq t
, the set
\mathcal{U}_{j}:=\mathcal{Z}_{n}\cap\mathbb{F}_{q^{Mp^{j}}}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-affine space. For each
0\leq j\leq t
, let
\delta_{j}
be the dimension of
\mathcal{U}_{j}
. From the inclusions
\mathbb{F}_{q^{M}}\subset\mathbb{F}_{q^{Mp}}\subset\cdots\subset\mathbb{F}_{q^{Mp^{t}}}
we obtain

\delta_{0}\leq\cdots\leq\delta_{t}=dn
.
Set
\mathcal{Y}_{0}=\mathcal{U}_{0}
and, for each
1\leq j\leq t
, set
\mathcal{Y}_{j}=\mathcal{U}_{j}\setminus\mathcal{U}_{j-1}
. Therefore,
\#\mathcal{Y}_{j}=p^{\delta_{j}}-p^{\delta_{j-1}}
for every
1\leq j\leq t
. Moreover, if
1\leq j\leq t
and
\alpha\in\mathcal{Y}_{j}
, then
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{Mp^{j}}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q^{Mp^{j-1}}}
and so the degree
D_{\alpha}
of the minimal polynomial of
\alpha
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
divides
Mp^{j}
but it does not divide
Mp^{j-1}
. The latter implies that
D_{\alpha}
is divisible by
p^{j}
and so
D_{\alpha}\geq p^{j}
.
Therefore, for every
1\leq j\leq t
, the set
\mathcal{Y}_{j}
produces at most
\frac{p^{\delta_{j}}-p^{\delta_{j-1}}}{p^{j}}
distinct irreducible factors of
B^{(n)}(X)
. As
\mathcal{Z}_{n}=\bigcup_{i=0}^{t}\mathcal{Y}_{j}
, we obtain

(6)

N_{B}(n)\leq p^{\delta_{0}}+\sum_{j=1}^{t}\frac{p^{\delta_{j}}-p^{\delta_{j-1}}}{p^{j}}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{t}p^{\delta_{j}-j}.

We now provide nontrivial bounds for
\delta_{j}
. Set

\mathcal{W}_{j}=\{u-v\,:\,u,v\in\mathcal{U}_{j}\}.

Observe that
B^{(n)}(X)=A^{(n)}(X)+\beta_{n}
for some
\beta_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{W}_{j}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q^{Mp^{j}}}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space of dimension
\delta_{j}
, whose elements are roots of
A^{(n)}(X)
. Moreover, the roots of
A^{(n)}(X)
comprise the set
\mathcal{S}=\cup_{i=0}^{t}\mathcal{W}_{j}
. As
A
is additive, it follows that
A(0)=0
and so
\mathcal{S}
also contains the roots of
A^{(r)}(X)
for
1\leq r\leq n
. As
pr\geq r+1
, from item (i) in Theorem
1.2
, we obtain that
s_{A}(r+1)\in\{s_{A}(r),ps_{A}(r)\}
for every
r\geq 1
. In other words, the degree of the splitting field of consecutive iterates of
A
increases by a factor
p
each time it increases. The latter implies that we must have the strict inclusions
\mathcal{W}_{0}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathcal{W}_{t}
and so we obtain
\delta_{0}<\cdots<\delta_{t}
. Therefore,
\delta_{j}\leq\delta_{t}-(t-j)=dn+j-t
and so
\delta_{j}-j\leq dn-t
for every
0\leq j\leq t
. Since
\mathcal{U}_{j}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q^{Mp^{j}}}
, if

, we also have the trivial bound
\#\mathcal{U}_{j}\leq\#\mathbb{F}_{q^{Mp^{j}}}
, i.e.,
\delta_{j}-j\leq\delta_{j}\leq RMp^{j}
.
Let
k=k(t)
be the smallest positive integer such that

, i.e.,

p^{-k}\leq\frac{dn-t}{RMp^{t}}=\frac{dn-t}{Rs_{B}(n)}=\frac{d}{R}\cdot\frac{n}{s_{B}(n)}\left(1-\frac{t}{dn}\right).

From items (iii) and (iv) in Theorem
1.2
, the number

lies in an interval
[a,b]
with
0<a<b<+\infty
. Moreover, as

we obtain
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{t}{n}=0
and
so we conclude that there exists

such that

whenever
n
(hence

) is sufficiently large. In particular, taking

large enough, we have
k(t)<t
and so from Ineq. (
6
) we obtain

\displaystyle N_{B}(n)\leq\sum_{j=0}^{t-k(t)}p^{RMp^{j}}+\sum_{j=t-k(t)+1}^{t}p^{dn-t}

\displaystyle\leq 2p^{RMp^{t-k(t)}}+k(t)p^{dn-t}

\displaystyle\leq(k(t)+2)p^{dn-t}.

The latter combined with Eq. (
5
) yields

\rho_{B}(n)\geq\frac{p^{t}}{k(t)+2}=\frac{s_{B}(n)}{M(k(t)+2)}\geq\frac{s_{B}(n)}{M(c+2)}=n\cdot\frac{s_{B}(n)}{n}\cdot\frac{1}{M(c+2)},

and so the result follows by items (iii) and (iv) in Theorem
1.2
.

4.3.
Dynamics of linear maps over finite fields

Here we provide statistics on the periodic points under the iteration of linear maps over finite fields. We start with a general structural theorem.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
be an additive polynomial and let
n\geq 1
. Then we have a direct sum of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector spaces
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}=\mathcal{W}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{1}
, where

\mathcal{W}_{0}=\bigcup_{t\geq 0}\{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\,:\,A^{(t)}(y)=0\}
and
\mathcal{W}_{1}
comprises the elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
that are periodic under
A
. In particular, the number
\pi_{A}(n)
of periodic points of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
under the map
z\mapsto A(z)
satisfies

\pi_{A}(n)=\#\mathcal{W}_{1}=\frac{q^{n}}{\#\mathcal{W}_{0}}.

Proof.

Observe that, as
A
is additive, the map
z\mapsto A(z)
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear map over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. Let
P(X)
be its characteristic polynomial and write
P(X)=X^{r}Q(X)
, where
r\geq 0
and
\gcd(Q(X),X)=1
. We now employ a classical Linear Algebra result: we have
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}=V_{0}\oplus V_{1}
, where
V_{0}
(resp.
V_{1}
) is the

A
-invariant subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
associated to the factor
X^{r}
(resp.
Q(X)
). Moreover,
V_{1}
is the largest
A
-invariant subspace where
A
restricts to a bijection. We clearly have
V_{0}=\mathcal{W}_{0}
. As
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
is finite,
A
restricts to a bijection on an
A
-invariant subspace if and only if the elements of this subspace are periodic under the map
z\mapsto A(z)
. We directly verify that
\mathcal{W}_{1}
is an
A
-invariant subspace, hence
V_{1}=\mathcal{W}_{1}
and the result follows.
∎

4.3.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.7

The case where
A(X)=aX^{p^{h}}
follows by direct computations since
z\mapsto A(z)
is the zero map for
a=0
and, if
a\neq 0
, such map is a permutation of the whole field
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. We now assume that
A(X)
is not of this form. As in the proof of Theorem
1.6
, for every integer
n\geq 1
, we verify that there exists integers
r\geq 0
and
d\geq 1
such that
A^{(n)}(X)=\tilde{A}_{n}(X)^{p^{nr}}
for some additive separable polynomial
\tilde{A}_{n}(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
of degree
p^{dn}
.

For each positive integer
n
, set
\Delta(n)=\#\bigcup_{t\geq 0}\{y\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\,:\,A^{(t)}(y)=0\}
. Theorem
4.2
implies the identity
\frac{\pi_{A}(n)}{q^{n}}=\Delta(n)^{-1}
so we only need to find values of
n
where
\Delta(n)
is relatively small/sufficiently large. Let
\mathbb{F}_{q^{M}}
be the splitting field of
A(X)
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and let
N
be the largest positive integer such that
\mathbb{F}_{q^{M}}
contains a root of
A^{(N)}(X)
that is not a root of
A^{(N-1)}(X)
(under the convention that
A^{(0)}(X)=X
). From item (i) in Theorem
1.2
, the roots of
A^{(j)}(X)
that are not in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{M}}
must be contained in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{Mp^{s}}}
for some
s\geq 1
. In particular, we obtain
\Delta(Mt)\leq p^{dN}
whenever
\gcd(t,p)=1
. Therefore,

\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\pi_{A}(n)}{n}\geq\limsup\limits_{t\to\infty}\frac{\pi_{A}(Mt)}{q^{Mt}}\geq p^{-dN}>0.

On the other hand, from item (i) in Theorem
1.2
, we have that
A^{(p^{i})}(X)

and so
\Delta(Mp^{i})\geq p^{dp^{i}}
for every integer
i\geq 0
. Therefore,

\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\pi_{A}(n)}{n}\leq\liminf\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{\pi(Mp^{i})}{q^{Mp^{i}}}\leq\liminf\limits_{i\to\infty}p^{-dp^{i}}=0.

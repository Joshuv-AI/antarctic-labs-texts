Classification of indecomposable reflexive modules on quotient singularities through Atiyah–Patodi–Singer theory
José Antonio Arciniega Nevárez, José Luis Cisneros-Molina, Agustín Romano Velázquez

Abstract
. In [ 20 ] Esnault asked whether on a general quotient surface singularity the rank and the first Chern class distinguish isomorphism classes of indecomposable reflexive modules. Wunram gave a contraexample in [ 46 ] showing two different full shaves on a quotient singularity, with the same rank and the same first Chern class. In this article, we prove that irreducible reflexive modules over quotient surface singularities are determined by the rank, first Chern class and the Atiyah-Patodi-Singer ξ ~ \tilde{\xi} -invariant [ 5 ] , except for the case of rank 2 2 indecomposable reflexive modules over dihedral quotient surface singularities 𝔻 n , q \mathbb{D}_{n,q} with gcd ⁡ ( m , 2 ) = 2 \gcd(m,2)=2 , which we conjecture to follow the same pattern. To prove the classification theorem, first we prove that every spherical 3 3 -manifold with non-trivial fundamental group appears as the link of a quotient surface singularity, and that indecomposable flat vector bundles over spherical 3 3 -manifolds are classified by their rank, first and second Cheeger-Chern-Simons classes, with the exception of the aforementioned case.

Introduction

McKay correspondence was presented by John McKay in
[
36
]
, it establishes a bijection between the non-trivial irreducible representations of a finite subgroup
\Gamma
of
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
and the irreducible components of the exceptional divisor in the minimal resolution of the associated Kleinian singularity,
X=\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma
.

Gonzalez-Sprinberg and Verdier
[
22
]
provided a geometric construction, case by case, of this correspondence as follows:
using the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence, to every non-trivial irreducible representation
\rho
of
\Gamma
they associated a non-trivial indecomposable reflexive
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
M
,
the pull-back
\pi^{*}M/\mathrm{tor}
of
M
on the minimal resolution
\pi\colon\tilde{X}\to X
, called the
full sheaf associated to
M
, is locally free and they proved that its first Chern class
c_{1}(\pi^{*}M/\mathrm{tor})
is the Poincaré dual of a curvette intersecting exactly one irreducible component of the exceptional divisor, and that it determines the module
M
and the representation
\rho
.
Knörrer
[
31
]
later gave a group-theoretical interpretation of this geometrical construction, and Artin and Verdier
[
2
]
provided an elegant theoretical proof of the fact that the first Chern class determines the module
M
.
However, Knörrer and Verdier raised the question of whether an indecomposable reflexive module on a general quotient surface singularity is similarly determined by its first Chern class.
In
[
20
]
Esnault gave a negative answer to this question, giving an example of a quotient singularity for which there are an
invertible full sheaf and a rank
2
indecomposable full sheaf having the same first Chern class; and
asked whether the Chern polynomial (that is, the first Chern class and the rank) distinguishes isomorphism classes of indecomposable reflexive modules.
Wunram
[
46
]
answered this question negatively constructing two different full sheaves on a quotient singularity, with the same rank and the same first Chern class, leaving the classification of indecomposable reflexive modules on quotient singularities unresolved. The main difficulty is that the classification problem of full sheaves requires an invariant that determines the holomorphic structure. In Section
6.4
we prove that it is impossible to classify full sheaves only with topological invariants. It is important to remark that even though there is no moduli for line bundles in quotient singularities, we have found moduli for full sheaves of rank higher than one. This is the main difficult part of this problem.

In this article, we give an (almost) complete classification of indecomposable reflexive modules on quotient singularities, resolving a problem that has remained open for 37 years.

Quotient surface singularities
(X,x)
are isomorphic to germs
(\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma,0)
, where
\Gamma
is a small finite subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{C})
, that is, its elements does not have
1
as an eigenvalue. If
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are two small finite subgroups of
\mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{C})
, then
\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma
and
\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma^{\prime}
are analytically isomorphic if
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are conjugate. Hence, quotient surface singularities are classified by conjugacy classes of small finite subgroups of
\mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{C})
.
The list of such groups was given by Brieskorn
[
8
, Satz 2.9]
with the notation used in
[
18
, p. 57]
which is difficult to work with.
Riemenschneider
[
43
, p. 38]
listed the matrices which generate these groups, but he does not give relations, so one does not have presentations for the small finite subgroups of
\mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{C})
.

Every finite subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{C})
is conjugate to a finite subgroup of
\mathrm{U}(2)
, thus, these subgroups fix the
3
-dimensional spheres in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
centered at the origin.
This implies that the link

of the quotient singularity

is diffeomorphic to the spherical
3
-manifold
\mathbb{S}^{3}/\Gamma
.
Spherical
3
-manifolds are compact oriented
3
-manifolds with finite fundamental group, they are of the form
\mathbb{S}^{3}/G
where

is a finite subgroup of
\mathrm{SO}(4)

acting freely and isometrically on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
. By Hopf
[
25
]
the list of such groups is known and they are given by presentations.
Since the links of quotient singularities are spherical
3
-manifolds, in order to have presentations for the small finite subgroups of
\mathrm{U}(2)
, one needs to see to which groups
they correspond in the list of finite subgroups of
\mathrm{SO}(4)
which act freely and isometrically on

Theorem 1
.

Every spherical
3
-manifold with non-trivial fundamental group appears as the link of a quotient surface singularity.

The finite subgroups of

acting freely and isometrically on

consist of the trivial group, the binary polyhedral groups, two families of groups denoted by

which, as far as we know, have not previously been detailed in the literature.

Our next goal is to classify flat vector bundles over spherical
3
-manifolds using Cheeger-Chern-Simons classes which are secondary characteristic classes.
Isomorphism classes of rank

In Section

our result

that for a rational homology

Using the computations in

Theorem 2

Let

be a spherical
3
-manifold. Indecomposable flat vector bundles over

Finally, in Section

In particular, given a quotient singularity

2.2.
Quotient surface singularities

Let
\Gamma
be a finite subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{C})

[

that the quotient space
\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma
is a normal analytic surface with an isolated singularity
by embedding it in

, with image a normal analytic surface.
An element of
\mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{C})

as an eigenvalue.
In
[
42

\mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{C})

with
\Gamma
a small finite subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{C})
.
We say that two quotient singularities are

[
42

proved that if
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are two small finite subgroups of
\mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{C})
then
(\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma,0)
and
(\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma^{\prime},0)
are equivalent if and only if
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are conjugate.
Any finite subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{C})
is conjugate to a finite subgroup of
\mathrm{U}(2)

[
35
, Lemma A.17]
, thus,
the classification of quotient surface singularities is equivalent to the classification of small finite subgroups of
\mathrm{U}(2)

The list of such groups was given by Brieskorn in
[
8
, Satz 2.9]
(see also
[
35
]
).
All the finite subgroups of
\mathrm{SU}(2)
are small, and since

together with the finite cyclic groups.
The surface quotient singularities

a finite subgroup of

are the rational double point singularities.

Using the embedding of a quotient surface singularity

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\zeta_{n}=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{n}}
. We have the equality

\frac{2-\zeta_{n}^{tj}-\zeta_{n}^{-tj}}{2-\zeta_{n}^{j}-\zeta_{n}^{-j}}=\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}(t-i)\zeta_{n}^{ij}+\sum_{l=1}^{t-1}(t-l)\zeta_{n}^{-lj}.

Proof.

Taking the product

(2-\zeta_{n}^{j}-\zeta_{n}^{-j})\left[\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}(t-i)\zeta_{n}^{ij}+\sum_{l=1}^{t-1}(t-l)\zeta_{n}^{-lj}\right]

one gets a telescoping sum where the only surviving terms are
2-\zeta_{n}^{tj}-\zeta_{n}^{-tj}
.
∎

Now we can compute

[

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
\rho_{t}
be the two-dimensional irreducible representation of
\pi_{1}(M)\cong\mathsf{BD}_{2q}
given in (
3.2
). Then

\tilde{\xi}_{\rho_{t}}(D)=\frac{1}{4q}(t^{2}-2qt-2q).

Proof.

Using the presentation (
2.1
), the elements of
\mathsf{BD}_{2q}
can be written in the form
b^{i}c^{j}
with
i=0,1
and
j=0,\dots,2q-1

The natural representation is
\rho_{1}\colon\mathsf{BD}_{2q}\to\mathrm{SU}(2)
, hence
\det\rho_{1}(g)=1
for any
g\in\mathsf{BD}_{2q}
.
Therefore by (
4.11
)
\mathrm{def}(\rho_{1}(g))=\frac{1}{2-\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{1}(g))}
. Using Lemma
5.7
and that
\sum_{j=1}^{2q-1}\zeta_{2q}^{ij}=-1
we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\xi}_{\rho_{t}}(D)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4q}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{2q-1}\bigl(\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{j}(c^{j}))-2\bigr)\mathrm{def}\bigl(\rho_{1}(c^{j})\bigr)+\sum_{j=0}^{2q-1}\bigl(\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{j}(bc^{j}))-2\bigr)\mathrm{def}\bigl(\rho_{1}(bc^{j})\bigr)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4q}\left[-\sum_{j=1}^{2q-1}\frac{2-\zeta_{2q}^{tj}-\zeta_{2q}^{-tj}}{2-\zeta_{2q}^{j}-\zeta_{2q}^{-j}}+\sum_{j=0}^{2q-1}\frac{(-2)}{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4q}\left[-\sum_{j=1}^{2q-1}\left[\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}(t-i)\zeta_{2q}^{ij}+\sum_{l=1}^{t-1}(t-l)\zeta_{2q}^{-lj}\right]-2q\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4q}\left[-\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}(t-i)\sum_{j=1}^{2q-1}\zeta_{2q}^{ij}-\sum_{l=1}^{t-1}(t-l)\sum_{j=1}^{2q-1}\zeta_{2q}^{-lj}-2q\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4q}\left[-t(2q-1)+\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}(t-i)+\sum_{l=1}^{t-1}(t-l)-2q\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4q}\left[-2tq+t+t(t-1)-2q\right]=\frac{1}{4q}(t^{2}-2qt-2q).

This proves the proposition.
∎

Proposition 5.9
.

Two-dimensional irreducible representations of
\pi_{1}(M)\cong\mathsf{BD}_{2q}

Proof.

Consider

with

Thus, they have the same first Cheeger-Chern-Simons class and by Theorem
4.3
-(
2
)

\widehat{c}_{\det(\rho_{t_{1}}),1}=\widehat{c}_{\rho_{t_{1}},1}=\widehat{c}_{\rho_{t_{2}},1}=\widehat{c}_{\det(\rho_{t_{2}}),1}.

Hence, by Remarks
5.6
and
5.5

(5.6)

\det(\rho_{t_{1}})=\det(\rho_{t_{2}}),

and therefore both
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
are even or odd numbers.
If
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
are odd, then
\rho_{t_{1}}
and
\rho_{t_{2}}
are topologically trivial representations and in this case by (
4.8
) we have

\tilde{\xi}_{\rho_{t_{1}}}(D)=\widehat{c}_{\rho_{t_{1}},2}([M])=\widehat{c}_{\rho_{t_{2}},2}([M])=\tilde{\xi}_{\rho_{t_{2}}}(D).

If
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
are even, by Theorem
4.3
-(
3
) we have that

(5.7)

\tilde{\xi}_{\rho_{t_{1}}}(D)-\tilde{\xi}_{\det(\rho_{t_{1}})}(D)=\widehat{c}_{\rho_{t_{1}},2}([M])=\widehat{c}_{\rho_{t_{2}},2}([M])=\tilde{\xi}_{\rho_{t_{2}}}(D)-\tilde{\xi}_{\det(\rho_{t_{2}})}(D).

6.4.

In this section, we classify (almost) all the reflexive modules over quotient surface singularities. By Remark

Remark 6.8
.

If the singularity is a rational double point, then any indecomposable full sheaf is determined by its rank and its first Chern class. This was done by Artin and Verdier in
[

.

Later, Esnault
[
20
]
asked if the rank and the first Chern class are enough to classify the indecomposable full shaves for general quotient surface singularities.
In
[
46
, pp. 597]
Wunram answered this question negatively. Over the quotient singularity

(see Theorem

), Wunram constructed two different full shaves

and

with the same rank and the same first Chern class:

(6.10)

\begin{split}r=\rank\mathcal{M}_{1}&=\rank\mathcal{M}_{2},\\
c_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{1})&=c_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{2}).\end{split}

Denote by
\mathcal{L}_{1}=\det\mathcal{M}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}=\det\mathcal{M}_{2}
the determinant bundles of
\mathcal{M}_{1}
and
\mathcal{M}_{1}
, respectively. Since,
c_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{1})=c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{1})
and
c_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{2})=c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{2})
. Then, by (
6.10
) we get
c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{1})=c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{2})
. By Remark
6.2
,
(X,x)
is a rational singularity. Since
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
are line bundles with the same first Chern class over a rational singularity, by the isomorphism (
6.1
) they have to be the same, so
\mathcal{L}_{1}\cong\mathcal{L}_{2}
.
By the proof of
[
20

, the full sheaves
\mathcal{M}_{1}
and
\mathcal{M}_{2}
appear as an extension of its determinant bundle and a trivial bundle, i.e.,

(6.11)

Note that (
6.11
) it is an exact sequence of vector bundles. In the differential category, every short exact sequence of differential vector bundles split. Therefore, as

-vector bundles, we get

In Section

We will use this result to prove the main theorem of this section but we need to do some minors modifications.
The main reason is that

It is true that

and the link

are homotopically equivalent but we need to guarantee that we do not loose the classification passing from the complex structure to the topological structure and vice-versa. Our main theorem is the following.

Theorem 6.9

be a quotient surface singularity and

First note that since the minimal resolution

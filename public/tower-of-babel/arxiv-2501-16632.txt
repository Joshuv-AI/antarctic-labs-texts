1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper, we work over the complex number field
\mathbb{C}
. We will freely use the basic notation in
[
15
]
.

A normal projective variety
X
is called a
Fano variety
(resp.
weak Fano variety
) if the anti-canonical divisor
-K_{X}
is ample (resp. nef and big). According to the minimal model program, Fano varieties with mild singularities form a fundamental class among research objects of birational geometry. Motivated by the classification theory of
3
-dimensional algebraic varieties, we aim to study the explicit geometry of Fano
3
-folds.

Given a Fano
3
-fold
X
, we are interested in the
(anti-canonical) degree

(-K_{X})^{3}
of
X
. This is an important invariant of Fano
3
-folds and it plays a key role in the classification of smooth Fano
3
-folds (see
[
6
]
). On the other hand, the classification of canonical Fano
3
-folds is a wildly open problem and very few results are known. So it is quite crucial to understand the behavior of degrees of canonical Fano
3
-folds.

In this direction, Prokhorov
[
23
]
showed that for a Gorenstein canonical Fano
3
-fold
X
,
(-K_{X})^{3}\leq 72
and this bound is optimal. The first author and Yu Zou
[
7
]
showed that for a canonical Fano
3
-fold
X
,
(-K_{X})^{3}\leq 324
(conjecturally this upper bound should be
72
). The main goal of this paper is to give an optimal upper bound for degrees of
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial canonical Fano
3
-folds of Picard number
1
.

Theorem 1.1

(=Theorem
5.4
)
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial canonical Fano
3
-fold of Picard number
1
. Then
(-K_{X})^{3}\leq 72
and the equality holds if and only if
X\cong\mathbb{P}(1,1,1,3)
or
\mathbb{P}(1,1,4,6)
.

To prove this result, the main idea is to establish a Kawamata–Miyaoka type inequality which relates
(-K_{X})^{3}
with
\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X)
, where
\hat{c}_{2}(X)
is the generalized second Chern class (or orbifold second Chern class), see Theorem
3.8
for details. We prove a more general Kawamata–Miyaoka type inequality for
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial
\epsilon
-lc Fano varieties of Picard number
1
, generalizing previous results
[
22
,
21
,
20
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
0<\epsilon\leq 1
be a real number. Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial
\epsilon
-lc Fano variety of dimension
n\geq 2
and of Picard number
1
. Then we have

c_{1}(X)^{n}<\frac{2(1+\epsilon)}{\epsilon}\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X)^{n-2}.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
X
be a normal variety of dimension
n
such that its canonical divisor
K_{X}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier. Then the
Gorenstein index

r_{X}
of
X
is defined as the smallest positive integer
m
such that
mK_{X}
is Cartier.

2.1.
Singularities

Let
X
be a normal variety such that
K_{X}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier and let
f\colon Y\to X
be a proper birational morphism. A prime divisor
E
on
Y
is called
a divisor over

X
. Write

K_{Y}=f^{*}K_{X}+\sum_{E}a(E,X)E,

where
a(E,X)\in\mathbb{Q}
is called the
discrepancy
of
E
. We say that
X
has
terminal
(resp.
canonical
,
klt
,
\epsilon
-lc
for some fixed
0\leq\epsilon\leq 1
) singularities if
a(E,X)>0
(resp.
a(E,X)\geq 0
,
a(E,X)>-1
,
a(E,X)\geq\epsilon-1
) for any exceptional divisor
E
over
X
.
Often we just simply say that
X
is

terminal, canonical, klt, or
\epsilon
-lc
, respectively.

2.2.
Fano indices

Let
X
be a klt weak Fano variety. We can define

\displaystyle\text{\rm q}_{\text{\rm W}}(X){}

\displaystyle:=\max\{q\mid-K_{X}\sim qB,\quad B\in\Cl(X)\};

\displaystyle\text{\rm q}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X){}

\displaystyle:=\max\{q\mid-K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}qA,\quad A\in\Cl(X)\}.

We call
\text{\rm q}_{\text{\rm W}}(X)
the
Weil–Fano index
of
X
and
\text{\rm q}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)
the
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano index
of
X
. It is known that
\Cl(X)
is a finitely generated Abelian group, so
\text{\rm q}_{\text{\rm W}}(X)
and
\text{\rm q}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)
are positive integers and
\text{\rm q}_{\text{\rm W}}(X)\mid\text{\rm q}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)
; moreover,
\text{\rm q}_{\text{\rm W}}(X)=\text{\rm q}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)
if
\Cl(X)
is torsion-free.
For more details, see
[
6
, § 2]
or
[
24
]
.

Here we recall the following easy lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X
be a klt weak Fano variety. Suppose that
p
is a prime factor of
\text{\rm q}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)
such that
p\nmid\text{\rm q}_{\text{\rm W}}(X)
, then there is a torsion element in
\Cl(X)
with order
p
.

Proof.

Denote
q\coloneq\text{\rm q}_{\mathbb{Q}}(X)
. By assumption, we have
-K_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}qA
for some Weil divisor
A
. Then
D\coloneq-K_{X}-qA
is a torsion element in
\Cl(X)
. Denote by
s
the torsion order of
D
. It suffices to show that
p\mid s
. Suppose that
p\nmid s
, then there exists an integer
k
such that
p\mid ks+1
. This implies that
-K_{X}=qA+D\sim qA+(ks+1)D=p(\frac{q}{p}A+\frac{ks+1}{p}D)
where
\frac{q}{p}A+\frac{ks+1}{p}D\in\Cl(X)
. This implies that
p\mid\text{\rm q}_{\text{\rm W}}(X)
(see
[
24
, Proof of Lemma 3.2(1)]
), a contradiction.
∎

2.3.
Reid’s basket and Reid’s Riemann–Roch formula

Let
X
be a canonical projective
3
-fold. According to Reid
[
26
, (10.2)]
, there is a collection of pairs of integers (permitting weights)

B_{X}=\{(r_{i},b_{i})\mid i=1,\cdots,s;0<b_{i}\leq\frac{r_{i}}{2};b_{i}\text{ is coprime to }r_{i}\}

associated to
X
, called
Reid’s basket
, where a pair
\left(r_{i},b_{i}\right)
corresponds to an orbifold point
Q_{i}
of type
\frac{1}{r_{i}}\left(1,-1,b_{i}\right)
which comes from deforming singularities of a terminalization of
X
locally.
Denote by
\mathcal{R}_{X}
the collection of
r_{i}
(permitting weights) appearing in
B_{X}
.
Note that the Gorenstein index
r_{X}
of
X
is just
{\text{l.c.m.}}\{r_{i}\mid r_{i}\in\mathcal{R}_{X}\}
.

Recall that for a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
H
on
X
,
c_{2}(X)\cdot H
is defined as
c_{2}(Y)\cdot f^{*}H
where
f\colon Y\to X
is a resolution of singularities.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
26
, (10.3)]
)
.

Let
X
be a canonical Fano
3
-fold. Then

(2.1)

\displaystyle c_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X)+\sum_{r_{i}\in\mathcal{R}_{X}}\left(r_{i}-\frac{1}{r_{i}}\right)=24\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=24,

and

(2.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}c_{1}(X)^{3}+3-\sum_{(r_{i},b_{i})\in B_{X}}\frac{b_{i}(r_{i}-b_{i})}{2r_{i}}=-\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(2K_{X}))=h^{0}(X,-K_{X})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

Proof.

As
-K_{X}
is ample, by the Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing theorem,
h^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
and
h^{0}(X,-K_{X})=\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X}))
. Then the theorem follows from
[
26
, (10.3)]
and the Serre duality.
∎

3.
Kawamata–Miyaoka type inequality

3.1.
\mathbb{Q}
-variant of Langer’s inequality

Let
X
be a projective klt variety of dimension
n\geq 2
. Then there exists a closed subset
Z\subset X
of codimension
3
such that
X\setminus Z
has only quotient singularities and admits a structure of a
\mathbb{Q}
-variety (see
[
4
, § 3.2 and § 3.6]
). Moreover, for any reflexive sheaf
\mathcal{E}
on
X
, as explained in
[
4
, Construction 3.8 and § 3.7]
, we can define the
generalized second Chern class

\hat{c}_{2}(\mathcal{E})
, which is a symmetric
\mathbb{Q}
-multilinear map:

\hat{c}_{2}(\mathcal{E})\colon N^{1}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times(n-2)}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q},\quad(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n-2})\longmapsto\hat{c}_{2}(\mathcal{E})\cdot\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n-2}.

On the other hand, since
X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial in codimension
2
, for any two
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor classes
\beta
and
\gamma
on
X
, the product
\beta\cdot\gamma
is also a well-defined symmetric
\mathbb{Q}
-multilinear map:

\beta\cdot\gamma\colon N^{1}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times(n-2)}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q},\quad(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n-2})\longmapsto\beta\cdot\gamma\cdot\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n-2}.

Now the
\mathbb{Q}
-Bogomolov discriminant

\hat{\Delta}(\mathcal{E})
of a reflexive sheaf
\mathcal{E}
of rank
r
is defined as follows:

\hat{\Delta}(\mathcal{E})\coloneqq 2r\hat{c}_{2}(\mathcal{E})-(r-1)c_{1}(\mathcal{E})^{2},

which is viewed as a symmetric
\mathbb{Q}
-multilinear map
N^{1}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times(n-2)}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}
. According to
[
14
, Lemma 6.5]
, for any semistable reflexive sheaf
\mathcal{E}
with respect to a collection of nef classes
(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n-1})
, the following
\mathbb{Q}
-Bogomolov–Gieseker inequality
holds:

(3.1)

\hat{\Delta}(\mathcal{E})\cdot\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n-2}\geq 0.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a reflexive sheaf of rank
r
on a projective klt variety
X
of dimension
n\geq 2
. Let
(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n-1})
be a collection of nef classes such that
\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n-1}
is not numerically trivial. Let
0=\mathcal{E}_{0}\subsetneq\mathcal{E}_{1}\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq\mathcal{E}_{m}=\mathcal{E}
be the Harder–Narasimhan filtration of
\mathcal{E}
with respect to
(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n-1})
. Denote by
\mathcal{F}_{i}
the reflexive hull of the quotient
\mathcal{E}_{i}/\mathcal{E}_{i-1}
and set
r_{i}=\rank(\mathcal{F}_{i})
for any
i
. Then we have

(3.2)

\hat{\Delta}(\mathcal{E})\cdot\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n-2}\geq-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq m}r_{i}r_{j}\left(\frac{c_{1}(\mathcal{F}_{i})}{r_{i}}-\frac{c_{1}(\mathcal{F}_{j})}{r_{j}}\right)^{2}\cdot\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n-2}.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{E}
is reflexive and any
\mathcal{E}_{i}
is saturated in
\mathcal{E}
, each
\mathcal{E}_{i}
is reflexive and we have the following exact sequence of reflexive sheaves

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{i-1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{i}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}_{i}.

Since
\mathcal{E}_{i}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{i}
is surjective in codimension
1
, we have
c_{1}(\mathcal{E}_{i})=c_{1}(\mathcal{E}_{i-1})+c_{1}(\mathcal{F}_{i})
. On the other hand, thanks to
[
12
, Lemma 2.3]
and
[
4
, Theorem 3.13]
, we obtain

\hat{c}_{2}(\mathcal{E}_{i})\cdot\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n-2}\geq\left(\hat{c}_{2}(\mathcal{E}_{i-1})+\hat{c}_{2}(\mathcal{F}_{i})+c_{1}(\mathcal{E}_{i-1})\cdot c_{1}(\mathcal{F}_{i})\right)\cdot\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n-2}.

Now a straightforward computation derives

\frac{\hat{\Delta}(\mathcal{E})\cdot\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n-2}}{r}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\hat{\Delta}(\mathcal{F}_{i})\cdot\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n-2}}{r_{i}}-\frac{1}{r}\sum_{i<j}r_{i}r_{j}\left(\frac{c_{1}(\mathcal{F}_{i})}{r_{i}}-\frac{c_{1}(\mathcal{F}_{j})}{r_{j}}\right)^{2}\cdot\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n-2}

and hence the
\mathbb{Q}
-Bogomolov–Gieseker inequality (
3.1
) yields the desired inequality as
\mathcal{F}_{i}
is semistable with respect to
(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n-1})
for each
i
.
∎

The following
\mathbb{Q}
-variant of
[
19
, Theorem 5.1]
is more or less well-known to experts. We provide a proof for the reader’s convenience.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a reflexive sheaf of rank
r
on a projective klt variety
X
of dimension
n\geq 2
and let
(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n-1})
be a collection of nef classes. Then we have

(\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n-2}\cdot\alpha_{n-1}^{2})\cdot(\hat{\Delta}(\mathcal{E})\cdot\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n-2})+r^{2}(\mu_{\max}-\mu)(\mu-\mu_{\min})\geq 0,

where
\mu
(resp.
\mu_{\max}
and
\mu_{\min}
) is the slope (resp. maximal slope and minimal slope) of
\mathcal{E}
with respect to
(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n-1})
.

Proof.

We will follow the notation in Lemma
3.1
. Without loss of generality, we shall assume
d\coloneqq\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n-2}\cdot\alpha_{n-1}^{2}>0
. Then combining the Hodge index theorem and Lemma
3.1
yields

\hat{\Delta}(\mathcal{E})\cdot\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n-2}\geq-\frac{1}{d}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq m}r_{i}r_{j}(\mu_{i}-\mu_{j})^{2},

where
\mu_{i}
is the slope of
\mathcal{F}_{i}
with respect to
(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n-1})
. As
r\mu=\sum r_{i}\mu_{i}
and
\mu_{\max}\geq\mu_{i}\geq\mu_{\min}
for any
1\leq i\leq m
, we obtain

\displaystyle r^{2}(\mu_{\max}-\mu)(\mu-\mu_{\min})

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}(\mu_{\max}-\mu_{i})\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}r_{j}(\mu_{j}-\mu_{\min})\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq m}r_{i}r_{j}(\mu_{\max}-\mu_{i})(\mu_{j}-\mu_{\min})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq m}r_{i}r_{j}(\mu_{j}-\mu_{i})^{2},

which implies immediately the desired inequality.
∎

Definition 3.3
.

A

X
is a non-zero coherent subsheaf
\mathcal{F}
of the tangent sheaf
\mathcal{T}_{X}
such that

(1)

\mathcal{T}_{X}/\mathcal{F}

(2)

\mathcal{F}

Let

3.3.
Kawamata–Miyaoka type inequality for
\epsilon
-lc Fano varieties

We start with the following observation, which is a consequence of Proposition

.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
0<\epsilon\leq 1
be a real number. Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial
\epsilon
-lc Fano variety of dimension
n\geq 2
and of Picard number
1
. Then for any rank
1
subsheaf
\mathcal{L}
of
\mathcal{T}_{X}
, the divisor class
c_{1}(X)-(1+\epsilon)c_{1}(\mathcal{L})
is nef.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
c_{1}(\mathcal{L})
is ample and
\mathcal{T}_{X}/\mathcal{L}
is torsion-free, so
\mathcal{L}
is a foliation on
X
. Moreover, by a criterion of Bogomolov and McQuillan (see
[
13
, Theorem 1]
), the foliation
\mathcal{L}
is algebraically integrable such that the closure of its general leaf is a rational curve. In other words, a general fiber of
p
in the family of leaves (
3.3
) of
\mathcal{L}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Moreover, since
\rho(X)=1
and
X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial, there always exist
p
-horizontal
e
-exceptional prime divisors, namely
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
.

Let
F\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a general fiber of
p
. Then
U
is smooth along
F
. Write
K_{e^{-1}\mathcal{L}}+\Delta\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}e^{*}K_{\mathcal{L}}
. Then
E_{i}\cdot F
is a positive integer as
E_{i}
is Cartier along
F
. By Proposition
3.5
, we have

0<-e^{*}K_{\mathcal{L}}\cdot F=-(K_{e^{-1}\mathcal{L}}+\Delta)\cdot F=2-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mult_{E_{i}}(\Delta)E_{i}\cdot F\leq 2-m.

It follows that
m=1
,
E_{1}\cdot F=1
and
-e^{*}K_{\mathcal{L}}\cdot F\leq 1
. Now write
K_{U}=e^{*}K_{X}+D
for some
e
-exceptional
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D
. As
X
has
\epsilon
-lc singularities, the coefficients of
D
are at least
\epsilon-1
. As a consequence, we obtain

-e^{*}K_{X}\cdot F=(-K_{U}+D)\cdot F=2+\mult_{E_{1}}(D)E_{1}\cdot F\geq 1+\epsilon\geq(1+\epsilon)(-e^{*}K_{\mathcal{L}}\cdot F).

Now the result follows as
\rho(X)=1
.
∎

Now we are in the position to finish the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By the
\mathbb{Q}
-Bogomolov–Gieseker inequality and
[
5
]
*Theorem 1.2, we have

c_{1}(X)^{n}<\frac{2n}{n-1}\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X)^{n-2}\leq\frac{2(1+\epsilon)}{\epsilon}\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X)^{n-2}

if
\mathcal{T}_{X}
is semistable with respect to
c_{1}(X)
.
So we may assume that
\mathcal{T}_{X}
is not semistable with respect to
c_{1}(X)
. Applying Theorem
3.2
to
\mathcal{T}_{X}
and
\alpha_{i}=c_{1}(X)
yields

\displaystyle\hat{\Delta}(\mathcal{T}_{X})\cdot c_{1}(X)^{n-2}+n\left(\mu_{\max}(\mathcal{T}_{X})-\mu(\mathcal{T}_{X})\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n^{2}\left(\mu_{\max}(\mathcal{T}_{X})-\mu(\mathcal{T}_{X})\right)\cdot\mu_{\min}(\mathcal{T}_{X})}{n\mu(\mathcal{T}_{X})}.

By
[
21
, Proposition 3.6]
, we have
\mu_{\min}(\mathcal{T}_{X})>0
. As
\mu_{\max}(\mathcal{T}_{X})>\mu(\mathcal{T}_{X})
, the above inequality yields that

\displaystyle\left(2\hat{c}_{2}(X)-c_{1}(X)^{2}\right)\cdot c_{1}(X)^{n-2}+\mu_{\max}(\mathcal{T}_{X})>0.

Denote by
\mathcal{F}
the maximal destabilizing subsheaf of

. Then

implies that

\mu_{\max}(\mathcal{T}_{X})=\mu(\mathcal{F})=\frac{c_{1}(\mathcal{F})\cdot c_{1}(X)^{n-1}}{\rank(\mathcal{F})}\leq\begin{cases}\frac{1}{1+\epsilon}c_{1}(X)^{n}&\text{if }\rank(\mathcal{F})=1;\\
\frac{1}{2}c_{1}(X)^{n}&\text{if }\rank(\mathcal{F})\geq 2,\end{cases}

and the desired inequality follows immediately.
∎

3.4.
Kawamata–Miyaoka type inequality for canonical Fano
3

Throughout this subsection, we assume that

-factorial canonical Fano

We slightly improve it by looking deeper into the Harder–Narasimhan filtration of

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial canonical Fano
3
-fold of Picard number
1
. Let

be the
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano index of

c_{1}(X)^{3}\leq\begin{cases}\frac{16}{5}\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X)&\text{if }q\leq 5;\\
\frac{4q^{2}}{q^{2}+2q-4}\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X)&\text{if }q\geq 6.\end{cases}

Proof.

By the
\mathbb{Q}
-Bogomolov–Gieseker inequality, we may assume that
\mathcal{T}_{X}
is not semistable with respect to
c_{1}(X)
. Let
0=\mathcal{E}_{0}\subsetneq\mathcal{E}_{1}\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq\mathcal{E}_{l}=\mathcal{T}_{X}
be its Harder–Narasimhan filtration. Then
2\leq l\leq 3
. Denote by
r_{i}
the rank of
\mathcal{F}_{i}\coloneqq(\mathcal{E}_{i}/\mathcal{E}_{i-1})^{**}
and by
q_{i}\geq 1
the unique positive integer such that
c_{1}(\mathcal{F}_{i})\equiv q_{i}A
, where
A
is an ample generator of
\Cl(X)/\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}
(see
[
21
, Proposition 3.6]
). Then we have

\sum_{i=1}^{l}r_{i}=3,\quad\sum_{i=1}^{l}q_{i}=q,\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{q_{1}}{r_{1}}>\dots>\frac{q_{l}}{r_{l}}>0.

The proof will be divided into three different cases:
(l,r_{1})=(2,1)
,
(2,2)
, or
(3,1)
.

Case 1
.

First we consider the case
(l,r_{1})=(2,1)
. By Proposition
3.6
, we have
2q_{1}\leq q
. On the other hand,
q_{1}=\frac{q_{1}}{r_{1}}>\frac{q_{2}}{r_{2}}=\frac{q-q_{1}}{2}
implies that
3q_{1}>q
. So Lemma
3.1
implies that

6\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X)-2c_{1}(X)^{3}\geq-\frac{(3q_{1}-q)^{2}}{2q^{2}}c_{1}(X)^{3}\geq-\frac{1}{8}c_{1}(X)^{3},

which yields

c_{1}(X)^{3}\leq\frac{16}{5}\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X).

Case 2
.

Next we consider the case
(l,r_{1})=(2,2)
. Then
[
21
, Proposition 3.6]
implies that
q_{1}<q
, and
\frac{q_{1}}{r_{1}}>\frac{q_{2}}{r_{2}}
implies that
3q_{1}>2q
. By Lemma
3.1
again, one gets

6\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X)-2c_{1}(X)^{3}\geq-\frac{(3q_{1}-2q)^{2}}{2q^{2}}c_{1}(X)^{3}\geq-\frac{(q-3)^{2}}{2q^{2}}c_{1}(X)^{3},

where we used the fact that
q_{1}\leq q-1
.
Then

c_{1}(X)^{3}\leq\frac{4q^{2}}{q^{2}+2q-3}\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X).

Case 3
.

Finally we consider the case
(l,r_{1})=(3,1)
. As
q_{1}>q_{2}>q_{3}\geq 1
, we get
q\geq 6
in this case. By Proposition
3.6
, we have
2q_{1}\leq q
. Lemma
3.1
yields

\displaystyle 6\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X)-2c_{1}(X)^{3}

\displaystyle\geq-\left((q_{1}-q_{2})^{2}+(2q_{1}+q_{2}-q)^{2}+(q_{1}+2q_{2}-q)^{2}\right)A^{2}\cdot c_{1}(X)

\displaystyle\geq-\left(\left(\frac{q}{2}-q_{2}\right)^{2}+q_{2}^{2}+\left(2q_{2}-\frac{q}{2}\right)^{2}\right)A^{2}\cdot c_{1}(X)

\displaystyle=-\left(6q_{2}^{2}-3qq_{2}+\frac{q^{2}}{2}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{q^{2}}c_{1}(X)^{3}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{q^{2}-6q+12}{2q^{2}}c_{1}(X)^{3},

where the last inequality follows from
2\leq q_{2}\leq q_{1}-1\leq q/2-1
. So we obtain

c_{1}(X)^{3}\leq\frac{4q^{2}}{q^{2}+2q-4}\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot c_{1}(X).

We finish the proof by comparing the inequalities in the three cases above.
∎

In this subsection, we study the connection between degrees and Fano indices for canonical Fano varieties.

Lemma 4.1

(cf.
[
8
, Lemma 2.3]
)
.

Let

be a normal projective variety of dimension

.
Let

be Cartier divisors on

and let

be Weil divisors on

such that

is Cartier in codimension

(D_{1}\cdot D_{2}\cdots D_{n-2}\cdot D\cdot D^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}.

Proof.

If

(D\cdot D^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}
as
D
is a Cartier divisor and
D^{\prime}
is a Weil divisor.

In general, we can express

where
H_{1}
and

are general very ample divisors on

.
Then by induction on

(H_{i}\cdot D_{2}\cdots D_{n-2}\cdot D\cdot D^{\prime})=(D_{2}|_{H_{i}}\cdots D_{n-2}|_{H_{i}}\cdot D|_{H_{i}}\cdot D^{\prime}|_{H_{i}})\in\mathbb{Z}.

This proves the assertion.

.

and

be positive integers. Let

In (

, Construction 3.8]
) and
\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot H=\hat{c}_{2}(\mathcal{T}_{X}|_{H})
. By
[
12
]
*Lemmas 2.1 and 2.2, we have

(4.3)

\displaystyle\hat{c}_{2}(\mathcal{T}_{X}|_{H})=\hat{c}_{2}(\mathcal{T}_{H})+\hat{c}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{H}(H))+c_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{H})\cdot H|_{H}.

Notice that
\hat{c}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{H}(H))=0
as
\mathcal{O}_{H}(H)
is invertible and
c_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{H^{\prime}})\cdot H^{\prime}|_{H^{\prime}}=c_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{H})\cdot H|_{H}
by the projection formula. Hence by (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle c_{2}(X)\cdot H-\hat{c}_{2}(X)\cdot H=c_{2}(\mathcal{T}_{H^{\prime}})-\hat{c}_{2}(\mathcal{T}_{H}).

By
[
16
, Definition 10.7, Theorem 10.8]
, we have

(4.5)

\displaystyle\hat{c}_{2}(\mathcal{T}_{H})=e_{orb}(H)=e_{top}(H)-\sum_{C\subset\text{\rm Sing}(X)}\left(1-\frac{1}{g_{C}}\right)(C\cdot H).

For an irreducible curve
{C\subset\text{\rm Sing}(X)}
, over any point in
C\cap H
, the exceptional set of
f|_{H^{\prime}}\colon H^{\prime}\to H
is a tree of
e_{C}-1
rational curves, whose topological Euler number is
e_{C}
. So we get

(4.6)

\displaystyle e_{top}(H^{\prime})-e_{top}(H)=\sum_{C\subset\text{\rm Sing}(X)}(e_{C}-1)(C\cdot H).

As
c_{2}(\mathcal{T}_{H^{\prime}})=e_{top}(H^{\prime})
, the conclusion follows from combining (
4.4
), (
4.5
), and (
4.6
).
∎

Applying Theorem

to

:

Corollary 4.7
.

Let

be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial canonical Fano
3
-fold of Picard number

. Let

Lemma 5.1

Let

Set

Let

\mathbb{Q}
-factorial canonical weak Fano

If

Hence

is a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial canonical Fano
3
-fold of Picard number

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
X
be a Gorenstein canonical Fano
3
-fold.
Suppose that
\Sing(X)
contains no curve. Then for any
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier Weil divisor
D
on
X
,
(D^{2}\cdot K_{X})
is an even integer.

Proof.

By
[
25
]
, a general element
S\in|-K_{X}|
has at worst Du Val singularities, which means that
(X,S)
is plt by the inversion of adjunction
[
15
, Theorem 5.50]
, which is equivalent to that
(X,S)
is a canonical pair as
K_{X}+S\sim 0
is Cartier. So
S\cap\Sing(X)
contains only terminal singularities of
X
. As
D
is Cartier near terminal singularities of
X
by
[
11
, Lemma 5.1]
,
D|_{S}
is a Cartier divisor on
S
. Hence
(D^{2}\cdot S)=(D|_{S}^{2})
is an even integer by the Riemann–Roch formula
\chi(S,D|_{S})=\frac{1}{2}(D|_{S}^{2})+2
, where
S
is a K3 surface with Du Val singularities.

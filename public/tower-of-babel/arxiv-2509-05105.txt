A subset

-factorizable groups

[

]

-factorizable topological universal algebras

(1)

The existence of a strictly

\{U\setminus\{x\}:U\in\mathscr{B}\}\cup\{V_{\alpha}(x):\alpha<\omega_{1}\}\cup\{X\setminus V_{\alpha}(x):\alpha<\omega_{1}\}.

The identity map

is continuous,

is Tychonoff, and

. Hence there exists a second-countable

and continuous functions

and

for which

. We may assume that

and

are surjective and hence one-to-one (because so is

). Thus, we may also assume that

coincides
with

as a set and

and

are the identity map, so that

. Let
\{U_{n}:n\in\omega\}
be a
countable neighborhood base at

in

. Since

is continuous, it follows that, for each
\alpha<\omega_{1}
,
there exists an
n<\omega
such that
U_{n}\subset F_{\alpha}
, and since
h
is continuous, it follows that

U_{n}
is a neighborhood of
x
for
n\in\omega
.
∎

Corollary 4.1
.

If

is an
\mathbb{R}^{\omega_{1}}
-factorizable space, then
the filter of neighborhoods of every nonisolated point

is an

-filter.

Proof.

Let

be a nonisolated point of

. According to Theorem

,

has a countable dense subspace
Y

containing

and

is
\mathbb{R}^{\omega_{1}}
-factorizable. By Proposition
4.1
the filter
\mathscr{F}_{Y}(x)
of neighborhoods of

in

is an

is regular, it follows that the closures of

in

form a base of the filter
\mathscr{F}_{X}(x)
of neighborhoods of

in

.
Therefore, if

, then each

contains the closure

in

of some

.

∎

Given a filter
\mathscr{F}
on a set

, by

we denote the space

, where

, in which all points of

are isolated and
\mathscr{F}
is the filter of deleted
neighborhoods of
\infty_{X}
. Proposition
4.1
has the following immediate corollary.

Corollary 4.2
.

If
\mathscr{F}
is an
A
-filter on a countable set

, then the space

is
\mathbb{R}^{\omega_{1}}
-factorizable.

Thus, to construct an example of a nonmetrizable
\mathbb{R}^{\omega_{1}}
-factorizable space, it suffices to
construct an
A
-filter on a countable set which has no countable base. To this end, we use the preorder

\subset^{*}
on
[\omega]^{\omega}
defined by setting
A\subset^{*}B
if

is finite. Given a cardinal

\kappa
, a family

is called a
\subset^{*}
-descending

\kappa
-sequence
if
B_{\beta}\subset^{*}B_{\alpha}
whenever
\alpha<\beta<\kappa
and a

\subset^{*}
-descending

if
\mathscr{B}
is
\subset^{*}
-descending and

is infinite whenever
\alpha<\beta<\kappa
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\kappa\geq\omega_{2}
be a regular cardinal, and let
\mathscr{B}=\{B_{\alpha}:\alpha<\kappa\}

be a
\subset^{*}
-descending
\kappa
-sequence in
[\omega]^{\omega}
. Then the filter
\mathscr{F}
on

\omega
generated by the subbase

\mathscr{B}^{\prime}=\{\omega\setminus\{n\}:n\in\omega\}\cup\mathscr{B}

is an
A
-filter. If, in addition,
\mathscr{B}
is strictly
\subset^{*}
-descending, then
\mathscr{F}
has no

Proof.

Consider any family
\{F_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\}\subset\mathscr{F}
. Note that, for every
\alpha<\omega_{1}
, there exists a
\beta<\kappa
such
that
B_{\beta}\subset^{*}F_{\alpha}
. Since
\kappa
is regular and
\kappa>\omega_{1}
, it follows that there
exists an
\alpha_{0}<\kappa
such that
B_{\alpha_{0}}\subset^{*}F_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha<\kappa
. Setting

B^{\prime}_{n}=B_{\alpha_{0}}\setminus\{0,\dots,n\}
for

, we obtain the countable set
\{B^{\prime}_{n}:n<\omega\}
of

\mathscr{F}
satisfying the condition in the definition of an

-filter. Thus,
\mathscr{F}
is an

A
-filter. The second assertion is obvious.
∎

Note that under
\mathrm{CH}
any filter on
\omega
contains at most
\omega_{1}
elements. Therefore, for any

A
-filter
\mathscr{F}
on a countable set

, we have

; it follows from

(3) that any such filter has a countable base. This observation, together with Lemma

,
implies the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

(1)

If
[\omega]^{\omega}
contains a strictly
\subset^{*}
-descending
\omega_{2}
-sequence, then there exists an

A
-filter on
\omega
which has no countable base.

(2)

If
2^{\omega}=\omega_{1}
, then any
A
-filter on
\omega
has a countable base.

Recall that
\leq^{*}
denotes the preorder on
\omega^{\omega}
defined by setting
f\leq^{*}g
if
f(n)\leq g(n)
for
all but finitely many
n\in\omega
. A subset of
\omega^{\omega}
is said to be

if it is
unbounded with respect to this preorder. The cardinal
\mathfrak{b}
is defined by

\mathfrak{b}=\min\{|B|:\text{$B$ is an unbounded subset of $\omega^{\omega}$}\}.

Proposition 4.2
.

There exists a strictly
\subset^{*}
-descending
\mathfrak{b}
-sequence in
[\omega]^{\omega}
.

Proof.

Using the fact that any subset of
\omega^{\omega}
of cardinality less than
\mathfrak{b}
is bounded, we can
easily construct by recursion a set of functions
f_{\alpha}\in\omega^{\omega}
,
\alpha<\mathfrak{b}
, such that

f_{\alpha}<^{*}f_{\beta}
for any
\alpha<\beta<\mathfrak{b}
. Setting
B_{\alpha}=\{(m,n):m\in\omega,\ n\geq f_{\alpha}(m)\}
and identifying
\omega\times\omega
with

, we obtain the desired strictly

\subset^{*}
-descending
\mathfrak{b}
-sequence.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

If
\mathfrak{b}>\omega_{1}
, then there exists a countable nonmetrizable
\mathbb{R}^{\omega_{1}}
-factorizable space.

In
[
2
]
Bräuninger and Mildenberger constructed a ZFC model in which
\omega_{1}=\mathfrak{b}<2^{\omega}=\omega_{2}
and there exists a simple
P_{\aleph_{2}}
-point in
\omega^{*}
, that is, an ultrafilter

\mathscr{U}
on
\omega
which has a base being a strictly
\subset^{*}
-descending
\omega_{2}
-sequence.
This implies the following statement in view of Theorem
4.1
(1) and Corollary
4.3
.

Corollary 4.4
.

Both assumptions
\mathfrak{b}=\omega_{1}
and
\mathfrak{b}>\omega_{1}
are consistent with the existence of a
countable nonmetrizable
\mathbb{R}^{\omega_{1}}
-factorizable space.

Let
V(\omega)
be the
Fréchet–Urysohn fan
, that is, the image of the sum of countably many copies of
the convergent sequence
\{\frac{1}{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}\cup\{0\}
under the quotient map contracting the set of
limit points of all these sequences to a single point
O
.

Proposition 4.3
.

The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\mathfrak{b}>\omega_{1}
;

(2)

the filter of neighborhoods of
O
in
V(\omega)
is an
A
-filter and has no countable base;

(3)

V(\omega)
is a nonmetrizable
\mathbb{R}^{\omega_{1}}
-factorizable space.

Proof.

The fan
V(\omega)
can be represented as

V(\omega)=\{O\}\cup\{(n,1/k):n\in\omega,\ k\in\mathbb{N}\}.

The filter
\mathscr{F}
of neighborhoods of
O
has the base formed by the sets

F_{f}=\{(n,1/k)\cup\{O\}:n\in\omega,\ k\geq f(n)\},\qquad f\in\omega^{\omega}.

Let us prove the implication (1)
\Rightarrow
(2).
Consider any family
\{F_{f_{\alpha}}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
of elements of
\mathscr{F}

of cardinality
\omega_{1}
. Since
\mathfrak{b}>\omega_{1}
, it follows that there exists a function
g\in\omega^{\omega}
such that
f_{\alpha}\leq^{*}g
for each
\alpha<\omega_{1}
. For every pair
(l,m)\in\omega\times\omega
, we set

g_{(l,m)}(n)=\begin{cases}l&\text{if $n\leq m$},\\
g(n)&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

For each
\alpha<\omega_{1}
, there exists an
(l,m)\in\omega
such that
f_{\alpha}(n)\leq g_{(l,m)}(n)
for
all
n\in\omega
(because
f_{\alpha}\leq^{*}g
) and hence
F_{g_{(l,m)}}\subset F_{f_{\alpha}}
.

The filter
\mathscr{F}
has no countable base, because the fan
V(\omega)
is not first-countable.

The equivalence (2)
\Leftrightarrow
(3) follows from Proposition
4.1
.

Finally, we prove the implication (2)
\Rightarrow
(1) by contradiction. Suppose that (2) holds but
\mathfrak{b}=\omega_{1}
. Let
B=\{f_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
be an unbounded subset of
\omega^{\omega}
. Since the filter of
neighborhoods of
O
in
V(\omega)
is an
A
-filter, it follows that there exist
g_{n}\in\omega^{\omega}
,
n\in\omega
, such that every
F_{f_{\alpha}}
contains
F_{g_{n}}
for some
n\in\omega
; equivalently, for
every
\alpha<\omega_{1}
, there exists an
n\in\omega
such that
f_{\alpha}\leq^{*}g_{n}
. Let

g\in\omega^{\omega}
be any function satisfying the condition
g_{n}\leq^{*}g
for all
n\in\omega
. Then
f\leq^{*}g
for all
f\in B
and hence
B
is bounded. This contradiction completes the proof.
∎

It turns out that the existence of an
A
-filter on
\omega
having no countable base is not only sufficient but
also necessary for the existence of a nonmetrizable
\mathbb{R}^{\omega_{1}}
-factorizable space.

Theorem 4.2
.

The existence of an
A
-filter on
\omega
which has no countable base is equivalent to the existence
of a nonmetrizable (or, equivalently, non-second-countable)
\mathbb{R}^{\omega_{1}}
-factorizable space.

Proof.

In view of Theorem
4.1
, it suffices to show that the existence of a non-second-countable
\mathbb{R}^{\omega_{1}}
-factorizable space implies that of an
A
-filter without a countable base on a countable set.

Suppose that every
A
-filter on a countable set has a countable base.
Let
X
be an
\mathbb{R}^{\omega_{1}}
-factorizable space. First, note that
X
is first-countable. Indeed,
let
x\in X
be a nonisolated point in
X
, and let
Y
be a countable dense subspace of
X
containing
x
. By
Theorem
3.3
(2) and Proposition
4.1
the filter
\mathscr{F}_{Y}(x)
of neighborhoods of
x
in
Y
is
an
A
-filter and hence has a countable base
\mathscr{B}
. Since
X
is regular, it follows that
\{\overline{F}\colon F\in\mathscr{F}_{Y}(x)\}
(the closures are in
X
) is a base of neighborhoods of
x
in
X
, and

\{\overline{B}:B\in\mathscr{B}\}
is its countable base.

Thus,
X
is first-countable. It obviously follows that the weight of any subspace
Z
of
X
of cardinality

\omega_{1}
is at most
\omega_{1}
. Therefore, by Theorems
3.2
(3) and
3.3
(1) any such subspace
Z

is second-countable. According to
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]
(see also
[
5
]
),
X
is second-countable.
∎

Note that if an
A
-filter
\mathscr{F}
on
\omega
has no countable base, then it cannot have a base of
cardinality
\omega_{1}
either, because the space
X_{\mathscr{F}}
is
\mathscr{R}^{\omega_{1}}
-factorizable (by Corollary
4.2
) and hence
w(X_{\mathscr{F}})>\omega_{1}

(by Theorem
3.2
(3)). However, if there exists an
A
-filter on
\omega
which has no countable base,
then there exists an
A
-filter on
\omega
which has a base of cardinality exactly
\omega_{2}
(and no base of
smaller cardinality). Before proving this fact, we introduce some convenient notation.

By analogy with filters, given families
\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}\subset[\omega]^{\omega}
, we say that
\mathscr{B}

is a
base
for
\mathscr{A}
if every
A\in\mathscr{A}
contains some
B\in\mathscr{B}
.
Thus, a filter
\mathscr{F}
is an
A
-filter if and only if every family
\mathscr{A}\subset\mathscr{F}

with
|\mathscr{A}|\leq\omega_{1}
has a countable base
\mathscr{B}\subset\mathscr{F}
.

Given a family
\mathscr{A}\subset[\omega]^{\omega}
, we set

\displaystyle I(\mathscr{A})

\displaystyle=\{\textstyle\bigcap\gamma:\gamma\subset\mathscr{A},\ |\gamma|<\omega\},

\displaystyle F(\mathscr{A})

\displaystyle=\{L\subset\omega:\text{there exists an $M\in I(\mathscr{A})$ such that $M\subset L$}\},

\displaystyle w(\mathscr{A})

\displaystyle=\min\{|\mathscr{B}|:\mathscr{B}\subset F(\mathscr{A}),\ \mathscr{B}\text{ is a base for }F(\mathscr{A})\}.

Note that
F(\mathscr{A})
is the smallest filter containing
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{A}
is its subbase. Note
also that if
\mathscr{A}
is a filter, then
F(\mathscr{A})=\mathscr{A}
. In this case, it is natural to refer to

w(\mathscr{A})
as the
weight
of the filter
\mathscr{A}
. Clearly, any filter of countable weight is an

A
-filter.

Proposition 4.4
.

Any
A
-filter of uncountable weight on
\omega
contains an
A
-filter
of weight
\omega_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{F}
be an
A
-filter of uncountable weight (that is, having no countable base) on
\omega
.
Then
w(\mathscr{F})>\omega_{1}
by Corollary
4.2
and
Theorem
3.2
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any family
\mathscr{A}\subset\mathscr{F}
with
|\mathscr{A}|\leq\omega_{2}
, there exists an

E(\mathscr{A})\subset\mathscr{F}
such that
\mathscr{A}\subset E(\mathscr{A})
and

w(E(\mathscr{A}))=|E(\mathscr{A})|=\omega_{2}
.

Proof.

If
w(\mathscr{A})=\omega_{2}
, then we set
E(\mathscr{A})=\mathscr{A}
. Suppose that
w(\mathscr{A})<\omega_{2}
. Let us construct a family
\{M_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{2}\}\subset\mathscr{F}
completing
\mathscr{A}

to the required family
E(\mathscr{A})
by induction on
\alpha<\omega_{2}
.

Take
\alpha<\omega_{2}
and suppose that
\{M_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}\subset\mathscr{F}
is already constructed.
Let
\mathscr{A}_{\alpha}=\mathscr{A}\cup\{M_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}
. Since

w(\mathscr{A}_{\alpha})\leq w(\mathscr{A})+|\alpha|<\omega_{2}
, it follows that
F(\mathscr{A}_{\alpha})\neq\mathscr{F}
. Choose any
M_{\alpha}\in\mathscr{F}\setminus F(\mathscr{A}_{\alpha})
.

Having defined
M_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha<\omega_{2}
, we set
\mathscr{E}=\mathscr{A}\cup\{M_{\beta}:\beta<\omega_{2}\}
. Note that
|\mathscr{E}|=\omega_{2}
. Let us show that
w(\mathscr{E})=\omega_{2}
. Assume the contrary. Then
w(\mathscr{E})<\omega_{2}
and hence
F(\mathscr{E})
has a base
\mathscr{B}\subset F(\mathscr{E})
with
|\mathscr{B}|<\omega_{2}
. We have
\mathscr{B}\subset F(\mathscr{A}_{\alpha})
for
some
\alpha<\omega_{2}
. Therefore,

F(\mathscr{E})=F(\mathscr{B})\subset F(\mathscr{A}_{\alpha})\not\ni M_{\alpha}\in\mathscr{E}.

This contradiction shows that
w(\mathscr{E})=\omega_{2}
. We set
E(\mathscr{A})=\mathscr{E}
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

For any family
\mathscr{A}\subset\mathscr{F}
with
|\mathscr{A}|\leq\omega_{2}
, there exists a
B(\mathscr{A})\subset\mathscr{F}
such that
\mathscr{A}\subset B(\mathscr{A})
,
|B(\mathscr{A})|\leq\omega_{2}
, and each

\mathscr{C}\subset\mathscr{A}
with
|\mathscr{C}|\leq\omega_{1}
has a countable base
\mathscr{B}\subset B(\mathscr{A})
.

Proof.

Let us index (possibly with repetitions) the elements of
\mathscr{A}
by ordinals:
\mathscr{A}=\{M_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{2}\}
. For each
\alpha<\omega_{2}
, we set
\mathscr{A}_{\alpha}=\{M_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}
;
note that
w(A_{\alpha})<\omega_{2}
. Let
\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}\subset\mathscr{F}
be a countable base of
\mathscr{A}_{\alpha}
. Then the family
B(\mathscr{A})=\mathscr{A}\cup\bigcup\{\mathscr{B}_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{2}\}
is as
required.
∎

We proceed to prove the proposition. For each
\alpha<\omega_{2}
, we define
a family
\mathscr{A}_{\alpha}\subset\mathscr{F}
by induction as follows. We set
\mathscr{A}_{0}=\{\omega\}
.
Assuming that
0<\alpha<\omega_{2}
and
\mathscr{A}_{\beta}
are already defined for all
\beta<\alpha
,
we set

\mathscr{A}_{\alpha}^{\ast}=\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}\mathscr{A}_{\beta},\quad\mathscr{E}_{\alpha}=E(\mathscr{A}_{\alpha}^{\ast}),\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{A}_{\alpha}=B(I(\mathscr{E}_{\alpha})).

Having defined all
\mathscr{A}_{\alpha}
, we set
\mathscr{A}=\bigcup_{\alpha<\omega_{2}}
and
\mathscr{G}=F(\mathscr{A})
. By construction,
|\mathscr{A}|=\omega_{2}
and, therefore,
w(\mathscr{G})\leq\omega_{2}
.

Let us show that
w(\mathscr{G})=\omega_{2}
. Suppose that, on the contrary,
w(\mathscr{G})<\omega_{2}
.
Then
\mathscr{G}
has a base
\mathscr{B}\subset\mathscr{G}
with
|\mathscr{B}|\leq\omega_{1}
. Since

\mathscr{G}=\bigcup_{\alpha<\omega_{2}}F(\mathscr{E}_{\alpha})
, it follows that
\mathscr{B}\subset F(\mathscr{E}_{\alpha})
for some
\alpha<\omega_{2}
. This contradicts the relation
w(\mathscr{E}_{\alpha})=\omega_{2}
.

It remains to show that
\mathscr{G}
is an
A
-filter. Consider any
\mathscr{M}\subset\mathscr{G}
with

|\mathscr{M}|\leq\omega_{1}
. Since
\mathscr{G}=\bigcup_{\alpha<\omega_{2}}F(\mathscr{E}_{\alpha})
, it follows that

\mathscr{M}\subset F(\mathscr{E}_{\alpha})
for some
\alpha<\omega_{2}
, and since
I(\mathscr{E}_{\alpha})
is a base
for
F(\mathscr{E}_{\alpha})
, it follows that
\mathscr{M}
has a base
\mathscr{L}\subset F(\mathscr{E}_{\alpha})
with

|\mathscr{L}|\leq\omega_{1}
. Finally, since
\mathscr{A}_{\alpha}=B(I(\mathscr{E}_{\alpha}))
, it follows that
\mathscr{L}
has a countable base
\mathscr{B}\subset\mathscr{A}_{\alpha}
. Therefore,
\mathscr{B}\subset\mathscr{G}
is a
countable base of
\mathscr{M}
.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

There exist filters on a countable set which are not
A
-filters.
Indeed, it follows from Proposition
4.4
that any filter of weight
\omega_{1}
on a
countable set is not an
A
-filter. To construct such a filter, it suffices to take an
\omega_{1}
-sequence of
functions
f_{\alpha}\in\omega^{\omega}
,
\alpha<\omega_{1}
, such that
f_{\alpha}<^{*}f_{\beta}
for

(it
exists because any countable set of functions is bounded). The filter on

generated by the

B_{\alpha}=\{(m,n)\in\omega\times\omega:n\geq f_{\alpha}(m)\}
,

, is as required.

5.
\mathbb{R}^{\omega_{1}}
-Factorizable Groups

We remind that a topological group
G
is said to be
\mathbb{R}
-factorizable
if any continuous function

f\colon G\to\mathbb{R}
factors through a continuous homomorphism to a second-countable group,
that is, given any continuous function
f\colon G\to\mathbb{R}
, there exists
a second-countable topological group
H
, a continuous homomorphism
h\colon G\to H
, and a continuous function

g\colon H\to\mathbb{R}
for which
f=g\circ h
. Basic information about
\mathbb{R}
-factorizable groups can
be found in
[
1
]
. In particular, according to Proposition 8.1.3 of
[
1
]
, any
\mathbb{R}
-factorizable
group
G
is
\omega
-narrow
, that is, given any neighborhood
U
of the identity element of
G
, there
exists a countable set
A\subset G
for which
U\cdot A\subset G
.

Theorem 5.1
.

A topological group
G
is
\mathbb{R}^{\omega_{1}}
-factorizable if and only if any continuous function

f\colon G\to\mathbb{R}^{\omega_{1}}
factors through a homomorphism to a second-countable group, that is, given
any continuous function
f\colon G\to\mathbb{R}^{\omega_{1}}
, there exists a second-countable topological group

H
, a continuous homomorphism
h\colon G\to H
, and a continuous function
g\colon H\to\mathbb{R}^{\omega_{1}}

for which
f=g\circ h
.

Proof.

The ‘if’ part is obvious. Let us prove the ‘only if’ part. Suppose that a topological group
G
is
\mathbb{R}^{\omega_{1}}
-factorizable.
From Theorem
3.2
(2) it follows that
G
is Lindelöf and hence
\mathbb{R}
-factorizable
[
15
, Assertion 1.1]
.
Let
f\colon G\to\mathbb{R}^{\omega_{1}}
be a continuous function. By definition there exists a second-countable space

M
and continuous functions
\varphi\colon G\to M
and
\psi\colon M\to\mathbb{R}^{\omega_{1}}
for which

f=\psi\circ\varphi
. Since
G
is
\mathbb{R}
-factorizable, it follows from Lemma 8.1.2 of
[
1
]
that there
exists a second-countable group
H
, a continuous homomorphism
h\colon G\to H
, and a continuous function

\theta\colon H\to M
for which
\varphi=\theta\circ h
. For
g=\psi\circ\theta
, we have
f=g\circ h
.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

Since any Tychonoff space
X
with
w(X)\leq\omega_{1}
embeds in
\mathbb{R}^{\omega_{1}}
, it follows that a group

G
is
\mathbb{R}^{\omega_{1}}
-factorizable if and only if any continuous function from
G
to a Tychonoff space of
weight at most
\omega_{1}
factors through a homomorphism to a second-countable group.

In what follows, by
B(\omega)
we denote the Boolean group generated by
\omega
,
that is, the set
[\omega]^{<\omega}
of all finite subsets of
\omega
under the operation of symmetric
difference (which we denote by +). The zero
{\mathbf{0}}
of
B(\omega)
is the empty set
\varnothing\in[\omega]^{<\omega}
, and the inverse of each
x\in B(\omega)
is
x
itself. Given a filter
\mathscr{F}

on
\omega
, by
B(\mathscr{F})
we denote
B(\omega)
with the group topology generated by the neighborhood base

\{\langle F\rangle:F\in\mathscr{F}\}
at zero (here
\langle F\rangle
denotes the subgroup generated by

F
).

Proposition 5.1
.

The group
B(\mathscr{F})
is
\mathbb{R}^{\omega_{1}}
-factorizable if and only if
\mathscr{F}
is an

A
-filter.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathscr{F}
is an
A
-filter on
\omega
. Let
X
be a space with
w(X)\leq\omega_{1}
, and let

f\colon B(\mathscr{F})\to X
be a continuous function. We must show that there exists a second-countable group

H
, a continuous function
g\colon H\to X
, and a continuous homomorphism
h\colon B(\mathscr{F})\to H
for
which
f=g\circ h
. For each
g\in B(\omega)
, let
\{U_{\alpha}(g):\alpha<\omega_{1}\}
be a base of
neighborhoods of
f(g)
in
X
. Then, for each
\alpha<\omega_{1}
,
f^{-1}(U_{\alpha})
is a neighborhood of
g
,
because
f
is continuous, and hence there exist an
F_{\alpha}(g)\in\mathscr{F}
such that
g+\langle F_{\alpha}(g)\rangle\subset f^{-1}(U_{\alpha})
. Since
B(\mathscr{F})
is countable and
\mathscr{F}
is an

A
-filter, it follows that there exist
F_{n}\in\mathscr{F}
,
n\in\omega
, such that every
f^{-1}(U_{\alpha})(g)

contains
g+\langle F_{n}\rangle
for some
n\in\omega
. Let
H
be the group
B(\omega)

endowed with the group topology generated by the neighborhood base
\{\langle F_{n}\rangle:n<\omega\}

at zero or, equivalently, by the base
\{g+\langle F_{n}\rangle:n<\omega,\ g\in B(\omega)\}
. Then
H
is
second-countable, the identity homomorphism
h\colon B(\mathscr{F})\to H
is continuous, as well as the function

g\colon H\to X
coinciding with
f
as a map of sets, and
f=g\circ h
.

Conversely, suppose that
B(\mathscr{F})
is
\mathbb{R}^{\omega_{1}}
-factorizable. By Proposition
4.1
the
filter
\mathscr{F}_{\mathbf{0}}
of neighborhoods of
{\mathbf{0}}
is an
A
-filter. Clearly, so is the filter
\{F\cap\omega:F\in\mathscr{F}_{\mathbf{0}}\}
, which coincides with
\mathscr{F}
.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

there exists an
A
-filter on
\omega
which has no countable base;

(2)

there exists a nonmetrizable
\mathbb{R}^{\omega_{1}}
-factorizable space;

(3)

there exists a nonmetrizable
\mathbb{R}^{\omega_{1}}
-factorizable group.

Proof.

The implication (1)
\Rightarrow
(3) follows from Proposition
5.1
.
The implication (3)
\Rightarrow
(2) is obvious.
The implication (2)
\Rightarrow
(1) follows from Theorem
4.2
.
∎

The following statements follow from Theorems
4.1
,
5.2
and Corollary
4.3
.

Corollary 5.1
.

(1)

If
\mathfrak{b}>\omega_{1}
, then there exists a nonmetrizable
\mathbb{R}^{\omega_{1}}
-factorizable group.

(2)

If

\mathbb{R}^{\omega_{1}}

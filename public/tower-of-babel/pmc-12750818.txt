High-density lipoprotein 2b combined with sequential organ failure assessment score as a novel prognostic indicator for sepsis patients: a prospective study
Authors: Yuting Chen, Yichun Jiang, Andi Xia, Zewei Huang, Zhenmi Liu, Chengying Hong, Yaowang Lin, Xueyan Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSepsis is a state of life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection, leading to consecutive organ failure and lethal outcome. The purpose of this study is to assess the value of the combined use of plasma high-density lipoprotein 2b (HDL2b) level and Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score in predicting short-term mortality from sepsis.Materials and methodsA prospective, observational study was conducted in patients with sepsis and non-septic controls admitted to three intensive care units (ICUs) from January 2020 to December 2021. SOFA scores were recorded on the first day after admission. Blood samples were collected from each enrolled patient and the levels of HDL2b were analyzed using microfluidic chip technology. Receiver-operator characteristic curve (ROC) analyses were conducted to determine the values of plasma HDL2b level, SOFA score and the combined HDL2b levels and SOFA score (HDL2b + SOFA) in predicting the prognosis of mortality, respectively. The primary endpoint was 28-day mortality and the secondary outcome was total in-hospital mortality.ResultsCompared to non-septic controls, patients with sepsis had lower HDL2b levels (10.95% [8.95, 12.96] vs. 23.78% [14.53, 29.16], p < 0.001). Among sepsis patients, the levels of HDL2b of non-survivors were lower than those of survivors (6.74% [4.63, 8.08] vs. 11.78 [7.20, 13.40], p = 0.002). Moreover, our data also indicated that patients with higher HDL2b + SOFA scores shown higher rates of 28-day and total in-hospital mortality. The areas under the ROC curves for predicting 28-day mortality were 0.755 for HDL2b, 0.782 for SOFA, and 0.806 for HDL2b + SOFA. Multivariate analyses indicated that HDL2b + SOFA (Odd Ratio: 1.321 (95% Confidence Interval: 1.028–1.698), p = 0.029) was potential predictors of 28-day mortality.ConclusionsThe HDL2b + SOFA composite score was a reliable predictor of 28-day and in-hospital mortality in sepsis patients, showing better discriminatory ability than SOFA alone.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12879-025-12147-z.Keywords: High-density lipoprotein 2b, Sepsis, SOFA score, Prognostic

Introduction

Sepsis is regarded as a state of life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection, leading to consecutive organ failure and lethal outcome [1, 2]. Although the outcome of sepsis has been improved due to a dedication to earlier source control and evolving therapeutic interventions, sepsis still has a high incidence and high mortality rate worldwide [3]. Therefore, early prediction of the response to treatment, clinical benefits and poor prognosis for sepsis is of great significance for precise treatment of sepsis.

Currently, SOFA score is widely employed in the daily monitoring of critically ill patients [4]. A recent systematic review and meta-analysis provided high-quality evidence that fixed-day SOFA score was the most frequently reported outcome among the reviewed randomized controlled trials, but was not reliably associated with mortality [5]. Therefore, predicting the prognosis of patients with sepsis solely based on SOFA scores is unreliable as it is easily confused by clinical interventions.

Recently, a prospective, observational study by Tanaka et al. indicated that HDL-C levels were low in septic patients compared to trauma patients, and poor outcome defined as death or a Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score >6 was associated with lower HDL levels [6]. Another study by Barlage et al. [7] also demonstrated that the levels of plasma HDL-C were lower in non-survivors than those in survivors. Moreover, HDL2b is one of the subtypes of HDL, which was recently been reported to be closely related to the severity of sepsis [8]. These findings reinforced the necessity to explore the predictive and therapeutic potential of HDL in sepsis.

In this study, we conducted a single-center, prospective, observational study in patients with sepsis and non-septic controls admitted to three intensive care units (ICUs) in our hospital to determine the role of plasma HDL2b in sepsis, and to assess the value of the combined HDL2b level and SOFA score in predicting the prognosis of mortality of sepsis.

Materials and methods

Study design and patient enrollment

This trial examined patients who were admitted to three ICUs at Shenzhen People’s Hospital (Guangdong, China). Patients were diagnosed with sepsis using the criteria of the Third International Consensus Definitions for Sepsis and Septic Shock (Sepsis-3). The SOFA score was set to 0 at baseline for patients with unknown status regarding underlying organ dysfunction, and patients with a rapid increase of 2 or more points on the SOFA scale after infection were considered to have sepsis [9]. Patients with the following conditions were excluded: (i) death within 24 h after admission; (ii) definite or suspected familial hypercholesterolemia; or (iii) previous or current use of a statin or corticosteroid. Finally, 47 patients with sepsis and 11 non-septic controls, all of whom were admitted to one of three ICUs from January 2020 to December 2021, were included.

Endpoints

The primary endpoint was all-cause death within 28 days after hospitalization for sepsis, and the secondary endpoint was in-hospital death at any time after hospitalization for sepsis.

Data collection

Blood samples were collected from each enrolled patient and sent for HDL2b analysis using microfluidic chip technology [10]; for analysis, the HDL2b results were presented as the molar percentage of total HDL-C: (HDL2b/HDL-Ctotal)×100%. Other laboratory data were measured using an automated analyzer. All demographic characteristics, vital signs, underlying illnesses, laboratory data, SOFA score, and Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation (APACHE) II score were recorded within 24 h after admission. Treatments and complications during the ICU stay were recorded for all patients. The combined HDL2b level and SOFA score (HDL2b + SOFA) or HDL2b level and APACHE II score (HDL2b + APACHE II) was calculated by assigning 1 point to patients with HDL2b levels less than or equal to 6.2%, following its ROC-derived cutoff. This point was added to the original SOFA or APACHE II score as a pragmatic approximation to facilitate clinical applicability [11, 12].

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS version 22.0 (IBM, USA). Baseline values are presented as means ± standard deviations (SDs), medians and interquartile ranges (IQRs), or numbers and percentages, as appropriate. A t-test was used to determine the significance of differences of continuous variables that had normal distributions. Analysis of variance (ANOVA) or the Kruskal-Wallis test was used to determine the significance of differences of continuous variables that had non-normal distributions. Categorical data were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was performed, and optimal cut-off values were determined by Youden’s J statistic. Univariate and multivariate analyses were used to determine the association of different variables with the odds ratio (OR) for 28-day mortality. The Kaplan-Meier method was used for analysis of 28-day mortality and total in-hospital mortality in different groups.

Results

Characteristics of patients with sepsis and non-septic controls

A total of 58 septic patients admitted to ICUs from January 2020 to December 2021 were screened, and 47 of them, as well as 11 non-septic controls, were included in this study (Fig. 1). Overall, there was no significant differences in gender, age, body mass index (BMI), total cholesterol, and small dense LDL-C (Sd-LDL-C) (Table 1) between the two cohorts. Notably, our data also indicated that patients with sepsis had markedly lower levels of HDL-C (0.57 vs. 1.13 mmol/L, p < 0.001), LDL-C (1.01 vs. 1.60 mmol/L, p = 0.015), and HDL2b (10.95% vs. 23.78%, p < 0.001) compared to non-septic controls (Table 1).

Characteristics of sepsis patients who were survivors and non-survivors

We then analyzed the baseline characteristics of sepsis patients who were survivors (36/47) and non-survivors (11/47) at 28 days after ICU admission (Table 2). The results show that there was no significant difference in age (64.33 ± 18.87 vs. 70.64 ± 16.79 years, p = 0.727), sex distribution, systolic blood pressure (SBP), heart rate (HR), and underlying diseases between the two groups. Our data also showed that the percents of hematogenous spread (13.89% vs. 45.46%, p = 0.027), acute kidney injury (AKI; 52.78% vs. 90.91%, p = 0.024), liver dysfunction (36.11% vs. 81.82%, p = 0.009), pleural effusion (55.56% vs. 90.91%, p = 0.035), and use of vasopressive agents (61.11% vs. 100%, p = 0.015) of survival patients were lower than those of non-survivor patients. The laboratory data indicated that non-survivors showed increased levels of white blood cells (WBCs, 18.09 vs. 13.21 × 109/L, p = 0.021), neutrophil-lymphocyte ratio (NLR; 89.62% vs. 86.42%, p = 0.003), C-reactive protein (CRP; 267.11 vs. 179.04 mg/L, p = 0.036) and lactate (5.65 vs. 3.38 mmol/L, p = 0.040), and decreased levels of hemoglobin (95.73 ± 18.17 vs. 113.68 ± 26.51 g/L, p = 0.043) and plasma HDL2b (6.74% [4.63, 8.08] vs.11.78% [7.20, 13.40], p = 0.002) compared to survivors (Table 2).

We also compared disease severity in the two groups patients using three different scoring systems (Table 2). The non-survivor group had significantly higher APACHE II score (22.82 [18.75, 25.75] vs. 17.92 [14.00, 22.00], p = 0.019), SOFA score (12.27 [10.50, 14.75] vs. 8.58 [5.00, 11.50], p = 0.004], and HDL2b + SOFA score (13.00 [10.75, 15.00] vs. 8.75 [5.50, 11.50], p = 0.001).

Predicting 28-day mortality in sepsis patients using different scoring systems

To assess the utilities of plasma HDL2b level, SOFA score, and HDL2b + SOFA score for prognosis of 28-day mortality, receiver-operator characteristic curve (ROC) analysis was conducted (Fig. 2; Table 3). Our data showed that the plasma HDL2b level had an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.755, sensitivity of 72.73%, and specificity of 83.33%; the SOFA score had an AUC of 0.782, a sensitivity of 72.73%, and a specificity of 75.00%; the HDL2b + APACHE II score had an AUC of 0.739, sensitivity of 72.70%, and specificity of 69.4%, and the HDL2b + SOFA score had an AUC of 0.806, sensitivity of 72.73%, and specificity of 83.33%.

Univariate and multivariate analyses of factors associated with 28-day mortality

Table 4 presented the univariate and multivariate analyses of factors associated with 28-day mortality. In univariate analysis, patients with haematogenous infection had a 4.17-fold increased risk of 28-day mortality compared to those without (OR = 5.167, 95% CI: 1.334–23.549, p = 0.034), and those with AKI had a 7.95-fold increased risk (OR = 8.947, 95% CI: 1.035–77.374, p = 0.047). Liver dysfunction was also associated with a 6.96-fold higher risk (OR = 7.962, 95% CI: 1.489–42.561, p = 0.015). For each 1 g/dL increase in hemoglobin, the risk of 28-day mortality decreased by 69.5% (OR = 0.305, 95% CI: 0.105–0.887, p = 0.028). Each 1 mmol/L increase in lactate was associated with a 12.2-fold increase in risk (OR = 13.200, 95% CI: 2.473–70.470, p = 0.023), and each 1 mg/dL increase in HDL2b was associated with a 78.1% reduction in risk (OR = 0.219, 95% CI: 0.025–0.517, p = 0.005). A 1-point increase in APACHE II and SOFA scores was associated with 4.33-fold (OR = 5.333, 95% CI: 1.194–23.830, p = 0.028) and 7.00-fold (OR = 8.000, 95% CI: 1.739–36.812, p = 0.008) increases in mortality risk, respectively. The HDL2b + SOFA index was associated with a 42% increased risk of 28-day mortality 1-point increase (OR = 1.418, 95% CI: 1.102–1.823, p = 0.007). In the multivariate model including liver dysfunction, HDL2b, and SOFA did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.077) (Supplementary Table S1). By contrast, the composite HDL2b + SOFA index remained an independent predictor of 28-day mortality (adjusted OR = 1.321, 95% CI = 1.028–1.698, p = 0.029), corresponding to per score increase in 32.1% risk of 28-day mortality.

Relationship of HDL2b + SOFA score with total in-hospital all-cause death

Among the 47 sepsis patients, 11 (23.4%) died within 28 days after sepsis onset and 16 (34.0%) died during hospitalization. Kaplan-Meier analysis indicated that sepsis patients with HDL2b + SOFA scores less than 13 had a significantly better probability of survival after 28 days (p = 0.001) (Fig. 3A) and a significantly better probability of total in-hospital survival (p = 0.001) (Fig. 3B).

Discussion

No previous clinical studies evaluated the use of HDL2b combined with SOFA score for predicting 28-day and total in-hospital mortality of sepsis patients. Our study had two major findings. First, among patients admitted to an ICU, patients with sepsis had a significantly lower level of HDL2b than non-septic controls. Second, an elevated HDL2b level combined with the SOFA score was associated with increased 28-day mortality and total in-hospital mortality.

Traditionally, the main function of HDL was thought to promote reverse cholesterol transport to the liver for excretion. However, recent studies also demonstrated that HDL can binds with endotoxins, regulates serum complement levels, improves endothelial function, and has anti-inflammatory and antioxidant activities [13]. Several clinical studies reported that the level of HDL was decreased by approximately 50% in patients with sepsis, which is closely related to the severity of sepsis and poor prognosis [6, 14–17]. In agreement, our data showed that the HDL-C level was significantly decreased in patients with sepsis.

HDL2 and HDL3 are the two main types of HDL [18]. HDL2b is a subtype of HDL2, and gradient-gel electrophoresis indicated it has a greater density and size than the other known subtypes (9.7–12.0 nm) [19]. Li et al. [8] constructed a mouse model of sepsis and found that the plasma HDL2b levels in the sepsis group were significantly lower than those in the control group. Another study by Tang et al. [20] showed that the HDL2b level was negatively correlated with inflammatory factors (IL-1β and TNF-α), suggesting that HDL2b might has anti-inflammatory effects. In sepsis, the rapid depletion of HDL2b, observed in both studies, may impair these protective mechanisms, exacerbating endothelial dysfunction, organ damage, and immune dysregulation. These findings are consistent with our data that the HDL2b levels in patients with sepsis were significantly lower than those in non-septic controls. The multivariate analysis also showed that HDL2b was significantly and independently associated with 28-day mortality in sepsis patients. HDL2b can be easily measured in clinical settings, suggesting it may be useful in real-world settings as a prognostic indicator for sepsis patients. Our multivariate analysis also found that hemoglobin and lactate levels measured within 24 h of ICU admission, and the presence of complications of acute liver failure were also reliable prognostic indicators in patients with sepsis.

Our ROC analysis of 28-day mortality indicated the AUC for HDL2b was 0.755, lower than the AUC for the SOFA score (0.782) but higher than the AUC for the APACHE II score (0.716). SOFA is a reliable prognostic indicator because it is based on assessment of multiple organ systems. In particular, the SOFA score assesses function of the respiratory, hematological, hepatic, cardiovascular, neurological, and renal systems, and the resulting score correlates significantly with in-hospital morbidity and mortality [21]. Other studies of sepsis patients indicated that a 2-point increase in the SOFA score significantly increased the risk of total mortality [4, 22, 23]. Toker et al. showed that the SOFA score provided better predictions of in-hospital mortality than the SIRS score or the qSOFA score in patients with sepsis [24]. Therefore, we examined the combined use of HDL2b level with SOFA score as a prognostic indicator. Our ROC analysis of 28-day mortality indicated the HDL-2b combined with the SOFA score (AUC = 0.806, sensitivity: 72.73%, specificity: 83.33%) was a valuable indicator of patient risk, and had a better AUC value than HDL2b or the SOFA score alone. Our survival curve analysis demonstrated that a low HDL2b level combined with a high SOFA score was associated with significantly increased risk of 28-day mortality and total in-hospital mortality. Although HDL2b alone did not remain statistically significant in multivariate analysis, its combination with SOFA retained independent predictive value. This suggests that HDL2b serves as a complementary rather than a standalone predictor, enhancing the discriminatory power of the SOFA score.

Previous researchers have used many tools to predict 28-day and total in-hospital deaths in sepsis patients, including the Nutrition Risk in Critically ill score [25], Mortality in Emergency Department Sepsis score [26], Sepsis Patient Evaluation Emergency Department score [27], APACHE II score [28], and SOFA score [29]. In contrast, using HDL2b combined with SOFA score to predict the prognosis of patients with sepsis is more concise, easy to operate, and has acceptable reliability and accuracy. We believe that use of HDL2b combined with SOFA score will greatly assist clinicians in assessing the prognosis of sepsis patients using measurements performed at admission. Clinicians can also use the HDL2b + SOFA score to better inform family members of a patient’s current condition and to describe the evolution of a patient’s condition.

There is still a need for further research on this topic due to several limitations of our study. First, the relatively small sample size in our study is a limitation that may affect the generalizability of our findings. This constraint also restricts our ability to adjust for potential confounders comprehensively. Second, although all samples were collected from patients within 24 h after admission for sepsis, there were some differences among patients in time since the onset of symptoms to sample collection. Third, we did not monitor the dynamics of HDL2b in sepsis patients and did not examine the possible mechanism by which it promoted the onset or progression of sepsis. Additionally, this study did not perform subgroup analyses due to the small sample size and insufficient cases in certain subgroups, which may introduce risks of multiple comparison bias and false positives. Finally, we acknowledge the methodological simplification in constructing the HDL2b + SOFA score. HDL2b was dichotomized at its ROC-derived threshold and assigned a unit weight of one point to ensure bedside applicability, similar to qSOFA. While this pragmatic choice enhances usability, it sacrifices the dose-response detail of HDL2b and should be viewed as an approximation rather than a precise statistical equivalent to SOFA subscores. Future studies, multicenter studies are needed to validate and this scoring system, potentially by modeling HDL2b as a continuous variable or incorporating coefficient-based weighting alongside detailed clinical classifications (e.g., infection sources, immune status).

Conclusion

The HDL2b + SOFA composite score was a reliable predictor of 28-day and in-hospital mortality in sepsis patients, showing better discriminatory ability than SOFA alone.

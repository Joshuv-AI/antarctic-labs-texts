Cauchy-Jacobi orthogonal polynomials and the discrete CKP equation
Shi-Hao Li, Satoshi Tsujimoto, Ryoto Watanabe, Guo-Fu Yu

Abstract
. This paper intends to construct discrete spectral transformations for Cauchy-Jacobi orthogonal polynomials, and find its corresponding discrete integrable systems. It turns out that the normalization factor of Cauchy-Jacobi orthogonal polynomials acts as the τ \tau -function of the discrete CKP equation, which has applications in Yang-Baxter equation, integrable geometry, cluster algebra, and so on.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
39A36, 15A15

1.
Introduction

The relation between orthogonal polynomials and integrable systems has been studied for many years, due to its deep connection with conformal field theory, random matrix theory and numerical algorithms, etc. On the one hand, orthogonal polynomials play crucial roles in the characterization of spectral problems, thus making connections with Lax representations for discrete integrable equations.
On the other hand, new orthogonal polynomials were proposed when solving the spectral problem of classical integrable systems. Cauchy bi-orthogonal polynomials is one of the examples proposed during the study of Hermite-Padé approximation problem of Degasperis-Procesi peakon equation
[
15
]
. Later on, classical properties of Cauchy bi-polynomials were investigated
[
2
,
3
]
, including its zeros distribution, four-term recurrence relation, Riemann-Hilbert problem and so on. Besides, such a polynomial is related to random matrix theory. In
[
4
]
, the Cauchy two-matrix model with specific Laguerre weight was considered, with limiting behavior expressed in terms of Meijer-G function. To be precise, the inner product of Cauchy-Laguerre weight is defined from
\mathbb{R}[x]\times\mathbb{R}[y]\to\mathbb{R}
, by

\displaystyle\langle f(x),g(y)\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}\frac{x^{a}y^{b}e^{-x-y}}{x+y}f(x)g(y)dxdy.

To evaluate the related bi-moments
\langle x^{i},y^{j}\rangle
with Cauchy-Laguerre weight, we can introduce a one-parameter generalization of the inner product
[
8
, lemma 2.3]

\displaystyle\langle f(x),g(y)\rangle_{t}=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}\frac{x^{a}y^{b}e^{-t(x+y)}}{x+y}f(x)g(y)dxdy.

This inner product plays a dominant role when we connect Cauchy bi-orthogonal polynomials with Toda-type equation. In
[
12
]
, it has been shown that with
a=b
, the one-parameter deformed Cauchy-Laguerre weight is related to the CKP hierarchy, as well as a semi-discrete Toda equation of CKP type (C-Toda for brevity).
If
a\neq b
, there is also a related discrete integrable system, with
\tau
-function expressed by an asymmetric Gram determinant
[
6
]
. Besides, in
[
16
]
, a full-discrete equation was constructed by introducing a discrete evolution of the inner product, while in
[
14
]
, a non-abelian C-Toda lattice was obtained by using matrix-valued Cauchy bi-orthogonal polynomials.

In this paper, we mainly consider a deformed Cauchy bi-orthogonal polynomials with Jacobi weight, and its corresponding discrete integrable system. By introducing a two-parameter symmetric inner product

\displaystyle\langle f(x),g(y)\rangle_{s,t}=\int_{[0,1]\times[0,1]}\frac{x^{s}y^{s}}{x+y}f(x)g(y)\left(\frac{1-x}{1+x}\right)^{t}\left(\frac{1-y}{1+y}\right)^{t}dxdy,\quad s,t\in\mathbb{N},

we show the corresponding orthogonal polynomials
\{P_{n}^{s,t}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
not only have the four-term recurrence relation, but some spectral transformations with respect to parameters
s
and
t
. Different with the discrete time evolutions considered in
[
16
]
, we consider an evolution with respect to variables
x
and
y
simultaneously.

In Section
2
, we construct different spectral transformations in
s
-direction and
t
-direction, which, compatible with four-term recurrence relation, leading to an algebraic identity and nonlinear difference equations. By noting the normalization factor could be simply expressed by a discrete Gram determinant, in Section
3
, we simplify nonlinear equations by using spectral transformations, and decouple nonlinear equations into bilinear equations. It was finally found that these bilinear equations have also been derived in
[
11
]
from hexahedron recurrence relations. Besides, the bilinear equations could be expressed in terms of a single quartic equation, governed by the normalization factor of Cauchy-Jacobi orthogonal polynomials. It is nothing but the discrete CKP equation, equivalent to the star-triangle move in the Ising model studied by Kashaev
[
10
]
.

To conclude, there are some highlights of this paper: (1) We introduce a two-parameter deformation for Cauchy orthogonal polynomials in Jacobi type, and study its related discrete spectral transformations; (2) We provide a determinant solution for the discrete CKP equation. Different from the soliton solution, this is a molecule solution determined by its initial values; (3) We construct a Lax pair for the discrete CKP equation. The integrability of discrete CKP equation was obtained by Yang-Baxter equation, and we show its Lax integrability by using orthogonal polynomial method.

2.
Two-parameter generalization of Cauchy orthogonal polynomials

Let’s consider an inner product with Cauchy weight by defining a symmetric bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\mathbb{R}[x]\times\mathbb{R}[y]\to\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\langle f(x),g(y)\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{1}{x+y}f(x)g(y)\omega(x)\omega(y)dxdy.

Here we assume that the weight function
\omega(x)
is supported on a subset of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
to avoid the singularity brought by the Cauchy kernel
1/(x+y)
.
With this inner product, we could obtain a family of monic orthogonal polynomials
\{P_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
by requiring the orthogonality

\displaystyle\langle P_{n}(x),P_{m}(y)\rangle=h_{n}\delta_{n,m},

where
\delta_{n,m}=0
if
n\neq m
and
\delta_{n,m}=1
if
n=m
.
In this paper, we focus on a two-parameter deformation of the Cauchy inner product, reading as

\displaystyle\langle f(x),g(y)\rangle_{s,t}=\int_{(0,1)\times(0,1)}\frac{x^{s}y^{s}}{x+y}f(x)g(y)\left(\frac{1-x}{1+x}\right)^{t}\left(\frac{1-y}{1+y}\right)^{t}dxdy,\quad s,t\in\mathbb{N}.

In other words, we choose a deformed Jacobi weight function
\omega(x)=x^{s}(1-x)^{t}(1+x)^{-t}
, supported on
(0,1)
.
With this deformed inner product, we could introduce a family of monic two-parameter Cauchy orthogonal polynomials
\{P^{s,t}_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
by orthogonality
\langle P_{n}^{s,t}(x),P_{m}^{s,t}(y)\rangle_{s,t}=h_{n}^{s,t}\delta_{n,m}
, where
h_{n}^{s,t}
is a non-zero constant.

Proposition 2.1
.

The two-parameter deformed monic Cauchy orthogonal polynomials have the following determinant expression

\displaystyle P_{n}^{s,t}(x)=\frac{1}{\tau_{n}^{s,t}}\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}m_{0,0}^{s,t}&\cdots&m_{0,n-1}^{s,t}&1\\
\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots\\
m_{n-1,0}^{s,t}&\cdots&m_{n-1,n-1}^{s,t}&x^{n-1}\\
m_{n,0}^{s,t}&\cdots&m_{n,n-1}^{s,t}&x^{n}\end{array}\right|,\quad\tau_{n}^{s,t}=\det\left(m_{i,j}^{s,t}\right)_{i,j=0}^{n-1},

where

\displaystyle m_{i,j}^{s,t}=\langle x^{i},y^{j}\rangle_{s,t}=\int_{(0,1)^{2}}\frac{x^{s+i}y^{s+j}}{x+y}\left(\frac{1-x}{1+x}\right)^{t}\left(\frac{1-y}{1+y}\right)^{t}dxdy.

Using the determinant formula, it is not difficult to verify that
h_{n}^{s,t}=\tau_{n+1}^{s,t}/\tau_{n}^{s,t}
, and the non-zero property of normalization factor is guaranteed by the Andréief formula (for reference, see
[
2
, Equation (2.5)]
)

\displaystyle\tau_{n}^{s,t}=\int_{0<x_{1}<\cdots<x_{n}<1\atop 0<y_{1}<\cdots<y_{n}<1}\prod_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{x_{i}^{s}y_{j}^{s}}{x_{i}+y_{j}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}(y_{i}-y_{j})^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{1-x_{i}}{1+x_{i}}\right)^{t}\left(\frac{1-y_{i}}{1+y_{i}}\right)^{t}dx_{i}dy_{i}.

(2.1)

Noting that every term in the integration is positive within the interval
(0,1)
, we can define a probability measure on the configuration space. However, different from the Cauchy-Laguerre case, it is still unknown how to evaluate this partition function in terms of hypergeometric functions.

Moreover, we have the following properties of the deformed inner product.

Proposition 2.2
.

The two-parameter deformed inner product has the following evolution properties:

(1)

\langle f(x),g(y)\rangle_{s+1,t}=\langle xf(x),yg(y)\rangle_{s,t};

(2)

\langle f(x),g(y)\rangle_{s,t+1}=\langle f(x),g(y)\rangle_{s,t}-\mathcal{L}_{s,t}(f)\mathcal{L}_{s,t}(g)
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{s,t}(f)=\sqrt{2}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{x^{s}}{1+x}f(x)\left(\frac{1-x}{1+x}\right)^{t}dx.

As a direct result, we know that moments
\{m_{i,j}^{s,t}\}_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfy relations

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} m_{i,j}^{s+1,t}=m_{i+1,j+1}^{s,t},\quad m_{i,j}^{s,t+1}=m_{i,j}^{s,t}-\phi_{i}^{s,t}\phi_{j}^{s,t}\end{aligned}

(2.2)

with
\phi_{i}^{s,t}=\mathcal{L}_{s,t}(x^{i})
.
Similar to ordinary Cauchy orthogonal polynomials, we have the following four-term recurrence relation for
\{P_{n}^{s,t}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

For any
s,\,t\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} x(P_{n}^{s,t}(x)&+a_{n}^{s,t}P_{n-1}^{s,t}(x))\\
&=P_{n+1}^{s,t}(x)+(a_{n}^{s,t}-b_{n}^{s,t})P_{n}^{s,t}(x)+(-c_{n}^{s,t}+a_{n}^{s,t}b_{n-1}^{s,t})P^{s,t}_{n-1}(x)-a_{n}^{s,t}c_{n-1}^{s,t}P^{s,t}_{n-2}(x)\end{aligned}

(2.3)

for some non-zero coefficients

a_{n}^{s,t}
,
b_{n}^{s,t}
and
c_{n}^{s,t}
,
where initial values are given by
P_{-1}^{s,t}(x)=0
and
P_{0}^{s,t}(x)=1
.

It has been noticed in
[
12
]
that variables
a_{n}^{s,t}
,
b_{n}^{s,t}
and
c_{n}^{s,t}
have determinant expressions. By introducing

\displaystyle\tilde{\sigma}_{n}^{s,t}=\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}m^{s,t}_{0,0}&\cdots&m^{s,t}_{0,n-1}&m^{s,t}_{0}\\
m^{s,t}_{1,0}&\cdots&m^{s,t}_{1,n-1}&m^{s,t}_{1}\\
\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots\\
m^{s,t}_{n,0}&\cdots&m^{s,t}_{n,n-1}&m_{n}^{s,t}\end{array}\right|,\quad m_{i}^{s,t}=\int_{0}^{1}x^{s+i}\left(\frac{1-x}{1+x}\right)^{t}dx

and denoting
p_{n}^{s,t}
as the coefficient of
x^{n-1}
in
P_{n}^{s,t}(x)
, we have

\displaystyle a_{n}^{s,t}=-\frac{\tilde{\sigma}^{s,t}_{n}\tau^{s,t}_{n-1}}{\tilde{\sigma}^{s,t}_{n-1}\tau^{s,t}_{n}},\quad b_{n}=p_{n+1}^{s,t}-p_{n}^{s,t},\quad c_{n}^{s,t}=\frac{\tau_{n-1}^{s,t}\tau_{n+1}^{s,t}}{(\tau_{n}^{s,t})^{2}}.

(2.8)

It should be remarked that
p_{n}^{s,t}
has determinant expression as well. If we introduce

\displaystyle{\tilde{\tau}}_{n}^{s,t}=\left|\begin{array}[]{ccc}m_{0,0}^{s,t}&\cdots&m_{0,n-1}^{s,t}\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
m_{n-2,0}^{s,t}&\cdots&m_{n-2,n-1}^{s,t}\\
m_{n,0}^{s,t}&\cdots&m_{n,n-1}^{s,t}\end{array}\right|,

then we have
p_{n}^{s,t}=-{\tilde{\tau}}_{n}^{s,t}/\tau_{n}^{s,t}
.

Remark 2.4
.

The four-term recurrence relation is based on the fact that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left(P_{n}^{s,t}(x)+a_{n}^{s,t}P_{n-1}^{s,t}(x)\right)dx=0

rather than

\mathcal{L}_{s,t}\left(P_{n}^{s,t}(x)+a_{n}^{s,t}P_{n-1}^{s,t}(x)\right)=0.

Therefore, we need to introduce single moments
m_{i}^{s,t}
instead of
\phi_{i}^{s,t}
to express the coefficient
a_{n}^{s,t}
.

2.1.
Discrete spectral transformation I

The first discrete spectral transformation is related to another family of polynomials
\{Q_{n}^{s,t}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
Let’s define monic polynomials

\displaystyle Q_{n}^{s,t}(x)=\frac{1}{\xi_{n}^{s,t}}\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}m_{0,1}^{s,t}&\cdots&m_{0,n}^{s,t}&1\\
\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots\\
m_{n,1}^{s,t}&\cdots&m_{n,n}^{s,t}&x^{n}\\
\end{array}\right|,\quad\xi_{n}^{s,t}=\det\left(m_{i,j+1}^{s,t}\right)_{i,j=0}^{n-1}.

In the theory of orthogonal polynomials, this family of polynomials is called the adjacent family of polynomials
\{P_{n}^{s,t}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Similar to the normalization factor
\tau_{n}^{s,t}
, we can show that

\displaystyle\xi_{n}^{s,t}=\int_{0<x_{1}<\cdots<x_{n}<1\atop 0<y_{1}<\cdots<y_{n}<1}\prod_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{x_{i}^{s}y_{j}^{s+1}}{x_{i}+y_{j}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}(y_{i}-y_{j})^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{1-x_{i}}{1+x_{i}}\right)^{t}\left(\frac{1-y_{i}}{1+y_{i}}\right)^{t}dx_{i}dy_{i},

which is a non-zero partition function.
By using Jacobi determinant identity to
\tau_{n+1}^{s,t}P_{n+1}^{s,t}
on its first and last columns and the first and last rows, we have the following discrete transformation.

Proposition 2.5
.

There is a discrete transformation

\displaystyle P_{n}^{s+1,t}(x)=\frac{1}{x}\left(P_{n+1}^{s,t}(x)+\frac{\xi_{n}^{s,t}\xi_{n+1}^{s,t}}{\tau_{n}^{s+1,t}\tau_{n+1}^{s,t}}Q_{n}^{s,t}(x)\right).

Unlike the ordinary Christoffel transformation connecting
P_{n}^{s,t}(x)
and
P_{n}^{s+1,t}(x)
, here we need to use an auxiliary polynomial
Q_{n}^{s,t}(x)
. In the next proposition, we demonstrate a Christoffel transformation for
Q_{n}^{s,t}(x)
.

Proposition 2.6
.

For auxiliary polynomials
\{Q_{n}^{s,t}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, we have the discrete spectral transformation

\displaystyle Q_{n}^{s,t}(x)=xP_{n-1}^{s+1,t}(x)-\frac{\xi_{n-1}^{s,t}\tau_{n}^{s+1,t}}{\xi_{n}^{s,t}\tau_{n-1}^{s+1,t}}Q_{n-1}^{s,t}(x).

Proof.

This transformation could also be derived by the property of
Q_{n}^{s,t}(x)
.
Although
Q_{n}^{s,t}(x)
can’t form an orthogonal system, they have the relation

\displaystyle\langle Q_{n}^{s,t}(x),y^{i}\rangle_{s,t}=0,\quad 1\leq i\leq n.

By expanding
xP_{n-1}^{s+1,t}(x)
in terms of
Q_{n}^{s,t}(x)
, we have

\displaystyle xP_{n-1}^{s+1,t}(x)=Q_{n}^{s,t}(x)+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\zeta_{n,k}^{s,t}Q_{k}^{s,t}(x).

Taking the inner product
\langle\cdot,y^{i}\rangle_{s,t}\,(i=1,\cdots,n)
, we have

\displaystyle\langle xP_{n-1}^{s+1,t}(x),y^{i}\rangle_{s,t}=\langle P_{n-1}^{s+1,t},y^{i-1}\rangle_{s+1,t}=0,\quad 1\leq i\leq n-1,

and thus

\displaystyle\zeta_{n,k}^{s,t}=0,\quad 0\leq k\leq n-2,\quad\zeta_{n,n-1}^{s,t}=\langle xP_{n-1}^{s+1,t},y^{n}\rangle_{s,t}\langle Q_{n-1}^{s,t},y^{n}\rangle_{s,t}^{-1}=\frac{\xi_{n-1}^{s,t}\tau_{n}^{s+1,t}}{\xi_{n}^{s,t}\tau_{n-1}^{s+1,t}}.

∎

Using Propositions
2.5
and
2.6
, we can conclude the following discrete spectral transformation for
\{P_{n}^{s,t}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Theorem 2.7
.

(s,t)
-deformed Cauchy orthogonal polynomials
\{P_{n}^{s,t}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfy the following spectral transformation

\displaystyle x\left(P_{n}^{s+1,t}(x)+\alpha_{n}^{s,t}P_{n-1}^{s+1,t}(x)\right)=P_{n+1}^{s,t}(x)+\beta_{n}^{s,t}P_{n}^{s,t}(x),

(2.9)

where

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}^{s,t}=\frac{\xi_{n+1}^{s,t}\tau_{n}^{s,t}}{\tau_{n+1}^{s,t}\xi_{n}^{s,t}}-\frac{\xi_{n+1}^{s,t}\xi_{n}^{s,t}}{\tau_{n+1}^{s,t}\tau_{n}^{s+1,t}},\quad\beta_{n}^{s,t}=\frac{\xi_{n+1}^{s,t}\tau_{n}^{s,t}}{\tau_{n+1}^{s,t}\xi_{n}^{s,t}}.

2.2.
Discrete spectral transformation II

This part is devoted to the discrete evolution on index
t
. Firstly, we have the following discrete transformation by introducing a family of auxiliary polynomials
\{R_{n}^{s,t}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. We first define monic polynomials

\displaystyle R_{n}^{s,t}(x)=\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{\sigma_{n-1}^{s,t}}\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\phi^{s,t}_{0}&m^{s,t}_{0,0}&\cdots&m^{s,t}_{0,n-2}&1\\
\phi^{s,t}_{1}&m^{s,t}_{1,0}&\cdots&m^{s,t}_{1,n-2}&x\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots\\
\phi^{s,t}_{n}&m^{s,t}_{n,0}&\cdots&m^{s,t}_{n,n-2}&x^{n}\end{array}\right|,

where

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}^{s,t}=\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}m_{0,0}^{s,t}&\cdots&m_{0,n-1}^{s,t}&\phi_{0}^{s,t}\\
m_{1,0}^{s,t}&\cdots&m_{1,n-1}^{s,t}&\phi_{1}^{s,t}\\
\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots\\
m_{n,0}^{s,t}&\cdots&m_{n,n-1}^{s,t}&\phi_{n}^{s,t}\end{array}\right|.

It should be noted that
\sigma_{n}^{s,t}
given above is different from
\tilde{\sigma}_{n}^{s,t}
given in (
2
).
In fact,
\sigma_{n}^{s,t}
is defined by

\sigma_{n}^{s,t}=\tau_{n}^{s,t}\mathcal{L}_{s,t}(P_{n}^{s,t})
.
By using auxiliary polynomials
\{R_{n}^{s,t}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, we can claim the following proposition.

Proposition 2.8
.

There is a discrete transformation

\displaystyle P_{n}^{s,t+1}(x)=R_{n}^{s,t}(x)-d_{n}^{s,t}P_{n-1}^{s,t+1}(x),

(2.10)

where

\displaystyle d_{n}^{s,t}=-\frac{\sigma_{n}^{s,t}\tau_{n-1}^{s,t+1}}{\sigma_{n-1}^{s,t}\tau_{n}^{s,t+1}}.

Proof.

By substituting
m_{i,j}^{s,t+1}=m_{i,j}^{s,t}-\phi_{i}^{s,t}\phi_{j}^{s,t}
, we know

\displaystyle P_{n}^{s,t+1}(x)=\frac{1}{\tau_{n}^{s,t+1}}\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccc}1&\phi^{s,t}_{0}&\cdots&\phi^{s,t}_{n-1}&0\\
\phi^{s,t}_{0}&m^{s,t}_{0,0}&\cdots&m^{s,t}_{0,n-1}&1\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots\\
\phi^{s,t}_{n}&m^{s,t}_{n,0}&\cdots&m^{s,t}_{n,n-1}&x^{n}\end{array}\right|.

An application of Jacobi identity to the above determinant for the first and last rows and last two columns gives the result.
∎

In fact,
R_{n}^{s,t}(x)
can be uniquely determined by relations

\displaystyle\langle R_{n}^{s,t}(x),y^{i}\rangle_{s,t}=0,\quad 0\leq i\leq n-2,\qquad\mathcal{L}_{s,t}(R_{n}^{s,t})=0.

(2.11)

By making the use of
R_{n}^{s,t}(x)
, we have the following discrete spectral transformation.

Theorem 2.9
.

We have the discrete spectral transformation

\displaystyle P_{n}^{s,t+1}(x)+d_{n}^{s,t}P_{n-1}^{s,t+1}(x)=P_{n}^{s,t}(x)+e_{n}^{s,t}P_{n-1}^{s,t}(x),

(2.12)

where

\displaystyle e_{n}^{s,t}=-\frac{\sigma_{n}^{s,t}\tau_{n-1}^{s,t}}{\sigma_{n-1}^{s,t}\tau_{n}^{s,t}}.

Proof.

It should be noted that
\{P_{n}^{s,t}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
form a set of basis for polynomials, and thus

\displaystyle R_{n}^{s,t}(x)=P_{n}^{s,t}(x)+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\alpha_{k}^{s,t}P_{k}^{s,t}(x).

The property of
R_{n}^{s,t}
(c.f equation (
2.11
)) leads to the result

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}^{s,t}=0,\quad 0\leq k\leq n-2,\qquad\alpha_{n-1}^{s,t}=\langle R_{n}^{s,t}(x),y^{n-1}\rangle_{s,t}(h_{n-1}^{s,t})^{-1}.

Moreover, by comparing the coefficient, we obtain

\displaystyle R_{n}^{s,t}(x)=P_{n}^{s,t}(x)+e_{n}^{s,t}P_{n-1}^{s,t}(x),

which results in the result by using Proposition
2.8
.
∎

2.3.
Algebraic construction of an integrable hierarchy

In this part, we first reformulate the above discrete spectral transformations, and then give a discrete integrable hierarchy by using compatibility conditions. Let
\Lambda
be a shift operator and
\Phi^{s,t}
be a semi-infinite column vector generated by polynomials
P_{0}^{s,t}(x)
,
P_{1}^{s,t}(x)
,
\cdots
. With these notations, the four-term recurrence relation could be reformulated as

\displaystyle x\Phi^{s,t}=\mathcal{L}^{s,t}\Phi^{s,t},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=(I+A^{s,t}\Lambda^{-1})^{-1}(\Lambda+A^{s,t}-B^{s,t}-C^{s,t}\Lambda^{-1}+A^{s,t}\Lambda^{-1}B^{s,t}-A^{s,t}\Lambda^{-1}C^{s,t}\Lambda^{-1}),

where

I
is the identity matrix and
A^{s,t}=\text{diag}(a_{0}^{s,t},a_{1}^{s,t},\cdots)
,
B^{s,t}=\text{diag}(b_{0}^{s,t},b_{1}^{s,t},\cdots)
and
C^{s,t}=\text{diag}(c_{0}^{s,t},c_{1}^{s,t},\cdots)
. Similarly, discrete spectral transformations (
2.9
) and (
2.12
) could be written as

\displaystyle x\Phi^{s+1,t}=\mathcal{N}^{s,t}\Phi^{s,t},\quad\Phi^{s,t+1}=\mathcal{M}^{s,t}\Phi^{s,t},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{s,t}=(I+\alpha^{s,t}\Lambda^{-1})^{-1}(\Lambda+\beta^{s,t}),\quad\mathcal{M}^{s,t}=(I+E^{s,t}\Lambda^{-1})^{-1}(I+D^{s,t}\Lambda^{-1}),

and

\displaystyle\alpha^{s,t}=\text{diag}(\alpha_{0}^{s,t},\alpha_{1}^{s,t},\cdots),\quad\beta^{s,t}=\text{diag}(\beta_{0}^{s,t},\beta_{1}^{s,t},\cdots),

\displaystyle D^{s,t}=\text{diag}(d_{0}^{s,t},d_{1}^{s,t},\cdots),\quad E^{s,t}=\text{diag}(e_{0}^{s,t},e_{1}^{s,t},\cdots).

By using compatibility conditions, we have the following integrable hierarchy.

Theorem 2.10
.

There is an integrable hierarchy given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{s+1,t}\mathcal{N}^{s,t+1}={\mathcal{N}}^{s,t}{\mathcal{M}}^{s,t},\quad{\mathcal{L}}^{s+1,t}{\mathcal{N}}^{s,t}={\mathcal{N}}^{s,t}{\mathcal{L}}^{s,t},\quad{\mathcal{M}}^{s,t}{\mathcal{L}}^{s,t+1}={\mathcal{L}}^{s,t}{\mathcal{M}}^{s,t}.

(2.13)

By recognizing that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}^{s,t}=\Lambda-B^{s,t}-C^{s,t}\Lambda^{-1}+O(\Lambda^{-2}),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}^{s,t}=I+(D^{s,t}-E^{s,t})\Lambda^{-1}+E^{s,t}\Lambda^{-1}(E^{s,t}-D^{s,t})\Lambda^{-1}+O(\Lambda^{-3}),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}^{s,t}=\Lambda+(\beta^{s,t}-\alpha^{s,t})+\alpha^{s,t}\Lambda^{-1}(\alpha^{s,t}-\beta^{s,t})+O(\Lambda^{-2}),

we can obtain the following nonlinear difference equation from (
2.13
) which reads

\displaystyle g_{n}^{s+1,t}+f_{n}^{s,t+1}=f_{n}^{s,t}+g_{n+1}^{s,t},

\displaystyle f_{n+1}^{s,t}-b_{n}^{s+1,t}=f_{n}^{s,t}-b_{n+1}^{s,t},

\displaystyle g_{n}^{s,t}-b_{n}^{s,t+1}=g_{n+1}^{s,t}-b_{n}^{s,t}

\displaystyle(g_{n}^{s+1,t}-\alpha_{n}^{s,t+1})f_{n-1}^{s,t+1}-e_{n}^{s+1,t}g_{n-1}^{s+1,t}=(g_{n-1}^{s,t}-\alpha_{n}^{s,t})f_{n-1}^{s,t}-e_{n}^{s,t}g_{n-1}^{s,t},

\displaystyle c_{n}^{s+1,t}+b_{n}^{s+1,t}f_{n}^{s,t}-\alpha_{n+1}^{s,t}f_{n}^{s,t}=c_{n+1}^{s,t}-f_{n}^{s,t}b_{n}^{s,t}-\alpha_{n}^{s,t}f_{n-1}^{s,t},

\displaystyle c_{n-1}^{s,t+1}-g_{n}^{s,t}b_{n-1}^{s,t+1}+e_{n}^{s,t}g_{n-1}^{s,t}=c_{n-1}^{s,t}-g_{n-1}^{s,t}b_{n-1}^{s,t}+e_{n-1}^{s,t}g_{n-1}^{s,t},

where we use notations
f_{n}^{s,t}=\beta_{n}^{s,t}-\alpha_{n}^{s,t}
and
g_{n}^{s,t}=d_{n}^{s,t}-e_{n}^{s,t}
.
These equations with unknown variables
b_{n}^{s,t},\,c_{n}^{s,t},\,d_{n}^{s,t},\,e_{n}^{s,t},\alpha_{n}^{s,t},\,\beta_{n}^{s,t}
form an integrable system from compatibility conditions. However, the determinant expressions of these variables are only related to
\tau_{n}^{s,t},\,{\tilde{\tau}}_{n}^{s,t},\,\xi_{n}^{s,t},\,\sigma_{n}^{s,t}
. It means that there are some variables could be eliminated, from which a simpler equation could be obtained. In the subsequent, we would derive an equivalent equation expressed by
\tau_{n}^{s,t}
and show that the corresponding discrete integrable system is exactly the discrete CKP equation.

Remark 2.11
.

Note that in the semi-discrete C-Toda equation, the nonlinear variables are expressed by
a_{n}\,b_{n}
and
c_{n}
. Therefore, we can see the differences between continuous time evolutions and discrete time evolutions in the discrete setting.

3.
The discrete CKP equation

In last section, we showed an overdetermined system from the compatibility conditions of spectral transformations. In this section, we show that there are some simpler relations between variables which could be obtained directly from discrete spectral transformations.
The first relation between
\tau_{n}^{s,t}
and
\xi_{n}^{s,t}
comes from the discrete spectral transformation (
2.9
).

Proposition 3.1
.

We have the formula

\displaystyle\tau_{n+1}^{s,t}\tau_{n-1}^{s+1,t}=\tau_{n}^{s,t}\tau_{n}^{s+1,t}-(\xi_{n}^{s,t})^{2}.

(3.1)

Proof.

By acting an inner product
\langle\cdot,y^{n}\rangle_{s,t}
on both sides of (
2.9
), we could get
\alpha_{n}^{s,t}h_{n-1}^{s+1,t}=\beta_{n}^{s,t}h_{n}^{s,t}
. Then the equation (
3.1
) is a direct result by substituting
\alpha^{s,t}_{n}
and
\beta_{n}^{s,t}
into this formula.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

By using this proposition,
\alpha_{n}^{s,t}
in (
2.9
) could be simplified as

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}^{s,t}=\frac{\xi_{n+1}^{s,t}\tau_{n-1}^{s+1,t}}{\xi_{n}^{s,t}\tau_{n}^{s+1,t}}.

Moreover, we have the following bilinear relations from discrete spectral transformations (

Proposition 3.3

We have equations

\displaystyle\tau_{n+1}^{s,t+1}\tau_{n}^{s,t}-\tau_{n}^{s,t+1}\tau_{n+1}^{s,t}=(\sigma_{n}^{s,t})^{2},

\displaystyle\tau_{n}^{s,t}\tau_{n}^{s+1,t+1}-\tau_{n+1}^{s,t}\tau_{n-1}^{s+1,t+1}=\xi_{n}^{s,t+1}\xi_{n}^{s,t}-\sigma_{n-1}^{s+1,t}\sigma_{n}^{s,t}.

(3.2b)

Proof.

From equation (

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{s,t}\left(xP_{n}^{s+1,t}(x)\right)=\mathcal{L}_{s+1,t}\left(P_{n}^{s+1,t}(x)\right)=\frac{\sigma_{n}^{s+1,t}}{\tau_{n}^{s+1,t}},\quad\mathcal{L}_{s,t}\left(P_{n}^{s,t}(x)\right)=\frac{\sigma_{n}^{s,t}}{\tau_{n}^{s,t}},

then the equality could be written in terms of a trilinear equation

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}^{s+1,t}\xi_{n}^{s,t}\tau_{n+1}^{s,t}+\xi_{n+1}^{s,t}\sigma_{n-1}^{s+1,t}\tau_{n+1}^{s,t}=\sigma_{n+1}^{s,t}\tau_{n}^{s+1,t}\xi_{n}^{s,t}+\xi_{n+1}^{s,t}\sigma_{n}^{s,t}\tau_{n}^{s+1,t}.

(3.3)

Theorem 3.4
.

The normalization factor
\tau_{n}^{s,t}
satisfies the following discrete CKP equation

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &4\left(\tau_{n}^{s+1,t}\tau_{n}^{s,t}-\tau_{n+1}^{s,t}\tau_{n-1}^{s+1,t}\right)\left(\tau_{n}^{s+1,t+1}\tau_{n}^{s,t+1}-\tau_{n+1}^{s,t+1}\tau_{n-1}^{s+1,t+1}\right)\\
&\qquad=\left(\tau_{n}^{s+1,t}\tau_{n}^{s,t+1}+\tau_{n}^{s+1,t+1}\tau_{n}^{s,t}-\tau_{n+1}^{s,t+1}\tau_{n-1}^{s+1,t}-\tau_{n+1}^{s,t}\tau_{n-1}^{s+1,t+1}\right)^{2}.\end{aligned}

(3.8)

This theorem implies that the discrete CKP equation admits the following multiple integral solution

\displaystyle\tau_{n}^{s,t}=\int_{0<x_{1}<\cdots<x_{n}<1\atop 0<y_{1}<\cdots<y_{n}<1}\prod_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{x_{i}^{s}y_{j}^{s}}{x_{i}+y_{j}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}(y_{i}-y_{j})^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{1-x_{i}}{1+x_{i}}\right)^{t}\left(\frac{1-y_{i}}{1+y_{i}}\right)^{t}dx_{i}dy_{i}.

Moreover, from bilinear relations, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 3.5
.

The discrete CKP equation (
3.8

\displaystyle\tau_{n}^{s,t}=\det(m_{i,j}^{s,t})_{i,j=0}^{n-1},

where

\displaystyle m_{i,j}^{s+1,t}=m_{i+1,j+1}^{s,t},\quad m_{i,j}^{s,t+1}=m_{i,j}^{s,t}-\phi_{i}^{s,t}\phi_{j}^{s,t}

and
\phi_{i}^{s+1,t}=\phi_{i+1}^{s,t}
.

In fact, there are some other simpler relations satisfied by these variables, whose compatibility condition would lead to the discrete CKP equation as well.

4.
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we derived the discrete CKP equation by using deformed Cauchy orthogonal polynomials. By introducing a deformed Jacobi weight, we demonstrated the special Cauchy orthogonal polynomials satisfied two different spectral transformations, which are compatible with four-term recurrence relation satisfied by general Cauchy bi-orthogonal polynomials. Therefore, we obtain some algebraic systems from Lax pairs. It is our belief that these complicated nonlinear system should have a simpler form, since there is a simple
\tau
-function expressed by determinant. Then we use bilinear equations to simplify the above-mentioned algebraic equations, and find the equation governed by a single
\tau
-function. This is nothing but a discrete CKP equation.

However, it is still of interest to know more about the Jacobi-deformed Cauchy orthogonal polynomials and related matrix model. Although we have the multiple integral representation for the normalization and polynomials, we’d like to know their exact expressions in terms of hypergeometric function and their limiting behavior when the degree of polynomials tend to infinity.

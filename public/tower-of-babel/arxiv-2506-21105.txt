On regularity of profinite isomorphisms between cusped hyperbolic 3-manifolds and the A-polynomial
Xiaoyu Xu

Abstract
. We prove that any isomorphism between the profinite completions of the fundamental groups of two cusped finite-volume hyperbolic 3-manifolds is regular and peripheral regular. As an application, we show that the A A -polynomial of prime knots in S 3 S^{3} is a profinite invariant, up to possible mirror image.



1.
Introduction

Profinite rigidity questions to which extent a finitely generated, residually finite group is determined by its finite quotient groups. These data are equivalently encoded in an algebraic construction named profinite completion according to
[
15
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
G
be a group, the
profinite completion
of
G
is defined as

\widehat{G}=\varprojlim\,\raise 2.58334pt\hbox{$G$}\big/\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$N$}

where
N
ranges over all finite-index normal subgroups of
G
.

The question becomes more accessible when we restrict ourselves to 3-manifold groups. Since 3-manifolds are largely determined by their fundamental groups, it is natural to ask which topological or geometrical properties of a compact 3-manifold
M
is determined by the isomorphism type of
\widehat{\pi_{1}M}
.

Convention
.

In this article, all 3-manifolds are orientable unless otherwise stated.

Proven in a series of works by Wilton–Zalesskii
[
46
,
47
,
48
]
, as well as the works of Wilkes
[
41
,
44
]
for the bounded case, the profinite completion of the fundamental group determines whether a compact 3-manifold
M
is geometric (in the sense of Thurston); it determines the geometry type in the geometric case, and determines the prime decomposition and the JSJ-decomposition in the non-geometric case. Many examples of 3-manifolds are proven to be profinitely rigid, that is, uniquely distinguished among compact 3-manifolds by the profinite completion of its fundamental group, see
[
4
,
10
,
40
,
42
,
51
]
. However, the general question for profinite rigidity in 3-manifolds is still widely open. In
[
50
]
, the author proved that the the profinite completion of the fundamental group determines the homeomorphism type of a compact 3-manifold
M
with empty or toral boundary up to finitely many possibilities, which we refer to as the ‘profinite almost rigidity’, and the remaining challenging part is the case of hyperbolic 3-manifolds (including closed and cusped manifolds).

1.1.
Regularity and peripheral regularity

The first part of this paper characterizes a useful feature of isomorphisms between the profinite completion of the fundamental groups of hyperbolic 3-manifolds.
The notion of regularity for a profinite isomorphism was first introduced by Boileau–Friedl
[
3
]
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
G
and
H
be two groups. An isomorphism
f:\widehat{G}\to\widehat{H}
is called
regular
if the induced isomorphism
\widehat{{G}^{\mathrm{ab}}}\to\widehat{{H}^{\mathrm{ab}}}
is the profinite completion of an isomorphism
{G}^{\mathrm{ab}}\to{H}^{\mathrm{ab}}
, where ‘
\mathrm{ab}
’ denotes the abelianization.

In the study of profinite rigidity among 3-manifold groups, peripheral subgroups in 3-manifold groups plays an important role. A related notion named peripheral regularity was introduced by the author in
[
50
,
51
]
.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
M
and
N
be compact 3-manifolds with incompressible toral boundaries, and let
f:\widehat{\pi_{1}M}\to\widehat{\pi_{1}N}
be an isomorphism.

(1)

We say that
f

respects the peripheral structure
if there is a one-to-one correspondence between the boundary components of
M
and
N
, denoted by
\partial_{i}M\longleftrightarrow\partial_{i}N
, such that
f(\overline{\pi_{1}\partial_{i}M})=g_{i}^{-1}\cdot\overline{\pi_{1}\partial_{i}N}\cdot g_{i}
for some
g_{i}\in\widehat{\pi_{1}N}
, where
\pi_{1}\partial_{i}M\leq\pi_{1}M
and
\pi_{1}\partial_{i}N\leq\pi_{1}N
are conjugacy representatives of the peripheral subgroups.

(2)

We say that
f
is
peripheral regular
if
f
respects the peripheral structure (so we follow the notation in (
1
)), and for each
i
, the isomorphism
C_{g_{i}}\circ f|_{\overline{\pi_{1}\partial_{i}M}}:\widehat{\pi_{1}\partial_{i}M}\cong\overline{\pi_{1}\partial_{i}M}\longrightarrow\overline{\pi_{1}\partial_{i}N}\cong\widehat{\pi_{1}\partial_{i}N}
is regular, where
C_{g_{i}}(x)=g_{i}xg_{i}^{-1}
denotes the conjugation.

The main result of this article is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose
M
and
N
are cusped hyperbolic 3-manifolds and
f:\widehat{\pi_{1}M}\to\widehat{\pi_{1}N}
is an isomorphism. Then
f
is regular and peripheral regular.

We remark that in general, a profinite isomorphism between 3-manifold groups may not be regular. This is demonstrated by the example of Hempel pairs
[
20
]
, as well as easily constructable examples for the 3-torus
T^{3}
or the product of the once-punctured torus with
S^{1}
. Thus,
Theorem 1.4
is particularly special for hyperbolic 3-manifolds.

Weaker versions of
Theorem 1.4
have been proven. Liu
[
23
]
proved that any isomorphism between the profinite completion of the fundamental groups of finite-volume hyperbolic 3-manifolds (including closed and cusped) is
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}
-regular, that is, the induced isomorphism
\widehat{H_{1}(M;\mathbb{Z})}\to\widehat{H_{1}(N;\mathbb{Z})}
is the profinite completion of an isomorphism
H_{1}(M;\mathbb{Z})\to H_{1}(N;\mathbb{Z})
composed with a scalar multiplication by some element
\lambda\in\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}
, see
Theorem 3.7
. The author proved in
[
50
]
that any isomorphism between the profinite completion of the fundamental groups of cusped hyperbolic 3-manifolds is peripheral
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}
-regular, that is, replacing ‘regular’ in
Definition 1.3
with ‘
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}
-regular’, see
Theorem 3.9
.

The proof of
Theorem 1.4
is an improvement of these two weaker results. The key technique we utilize is the relative cohomology theory for profinite groups introduced by Wilkes
[
44
]
. In fact, for an isomorphism
f
between the profinite completion of the fundamental groups of finite-volume hyperbolic 3-manifolds, we can define a profinite mapping degree
\mathbf{deg}(f)\in\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}
witnessed in the third relative homology groups. The coefficient
\lambda\in\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}
that appears in the
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}
-regularity statement is denoted by
\mathbf{coef}(f)
. We establish two relations between
\mathbf{coef}(f)
and
\mathbf{deg}(f)
. On one hand, through passing onto the peripheral subgroups, we prove that
\mathbf{coef}(f)^{2}=\pm\mathbf{deg}(f)
. On the other hand, using the techinque of Dehn filling introduced in
[
51
]
, we can also pass these invariants to a profinite isomorphism between closed hyperbolic 3-manifolds and prove that
\mathbf{coef}(f)^{3}=\pm\mathbf{deg}(f)
based on a formula of Liu
[
24
, Lemma 5.3]
. These two methods demonstrate a dimension shift, which in turn implies that
\mathbf{coef}(f)=\pm 1
.

Remark 1.5
.

When
M
and
N
are closed hyperbolic 3-manifolds and
f:\widehat{\pi_{1}M}\to\widehat{\pi_{1}N}
is an isomorphism, Liu
[
24
, Lemma 7.1]
showed that
\mathbf{coef}(f)^{2}=1
. This is currently the closest result towards regularity in the closed case. Our proof for the cusped case relies on the boundary structure, and it cannot be extended directly to the closed case.

1.2.
The
A
-polynomial

Knot complements in
S^{3}
provide a delightful testing ground for profinite rigidity in 3-manifolds. For instance, Ueki
[
37
]
proved that knots in
S^{3}
with profinitely isomorphic knot groups have identical Alexander polynomials.
Torus knot complements and prime graph knot complements are profinitely rigid among all knot complements in
S^{3}
, according to Boileau–Friedl
[
3
]
and Wilkes
[
43
]
respectively.
In
[
51
, Theorem C]
, the author proved that the complement of hyperbolic knots or hyperbolic-type satellite knots in
S^{3}
are distinguished from compact orientable 3-manifolds by the profinite completion of their fundamental groups. In addition, in
[
51
]
, the author also showed the profinite rigidity of many hyperbolic knot complements, such as the complement of full twist knots, including a new proof for the figure-eight knot
[
6
]
.

The
A
-polynomial of a knot
K
is a two-variable polynomial
A_{K}(M,L)
defined by Cooper, Culler, Gillet, Long, and Shalen in
[
12
]
. This is a knot invariant related to the
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
-representations of 3-manifold groups. An enhanced version of the
A
-polynomial is also defined and used in many literatures containing slightly more information than the
A
-polynomial, which we denote as
\widetilde{A}_{K}(M,L)
, see
§ 10.1
for complete definition.
Briefly speaking, when defining the enhanced version, the component in the character variety of the knot complement given by reducible representations is excluded.
The relation between these two versions is simple. In fact,

A_{K}(M,L)=\mathrm{Red}((L-1)\widetilde{A}_{K}(M,L))
,
where ‘
\mathrm{Red}
’ means reducing repeating factors. In particular, the enhanced
A
-polynomial completely determines the
A
-polynomial.

For a knot
K
in
S^{3}
, we denote
X(K)=S^{3}-\text{int}(N(K))
as the exterior of
K
. An application of
Theorem 1.4
is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.6
.

Suppose
J
and
K
are knots in
S^{3}
, and
f:\widehat{\pi_{1}(X(J))}\to\widehat{\pi_{1}(X(K))}
is an isomorphism. Suppose either

respects the peripheral structure
†
†

†

assuming
J
and
K
are non-trivial.
, or one of

and

is a prime knot. Then, up to possibly replacing

with its mirror image, the enhanced

-polynomials of

and

are identical:
\widetilde{A}_{J}(M,L)\doteq\widetilde{A}_{K}(M,L)
, and consequently their

The assumption that either the isomorphism respects the peripheral structure, or one of the knots is a prime knot is analogous to the condition in distinguishing knots in
S^{3}
through the fundmental group of their exteriors. In fact, given knots
J
and
K
in
S^{3}
, suppose
f:\pi_{1}(X(J))\to\pi_{1}(X(K))
is an isomorphism; then,

and
K
are equivalent (including mirror image) if either

respects the peripheral structure
[
38
]
or one of

and

is prime
[

]
. Moreover, this assumption is necessary in

. For non-prime knots

and

, where
\overline{K_{2}}
denotes the mirror image of

, we always have
\pi_{1}(X(J))\cong\pi_{1}(X(K))
. However, in this case
A_{J}(M,L)
and
A_{K}(M,L)
may not coincide, see
[
13
]
for the example of reef knot and granny knot.

The core behind
Theorem 1.6
can be stated in the fashion of character varieties. For a compact connected manifold
Y
, let
\mathscr{X}_{n}(Y)
denote the
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C})
-character variety of
\pi_{1}Y
, where
n\in\mathbb{N}
. For a compact disconnected manifold
Z
consisting of components
Z_{1},\cdots,Z_{k}
, we denote
\mathscr{X}_{n}(Z)=\mathscr{X}_{n}(Z_{1})\times\cdots\times\mathscr{X}_{n}(Z_{k})
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Suppose
M
and
N
are cusped hyperbolic 3-manifolds, and
\widehat{\pi_{1}M}\cong\widehat{\pi_{1}N}
. Then there exists a homeomorphism
\Psi:\partial M\to\partial N
such that the images of the restriction maps
\mathscr{X}_{n}(M)\to\mathscr{X}_{n}(\partial M)
and
\mathscr{X}_{n}(N)\to\mathscr{X}_{n}(\partial N)
are identical through

2.
Preliminaries on profinite groups

2.1.
Profinite groups

A
profinite group
is an inverse limit of finite groups indexed over a directed partially ordered set, equipped with the subspace topology inherited from the product topology. A direct characterisation for profinite groups is shown by the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
32
, Theorem 2.1.3]
)
.

A topological group
\Pi
is a profinite group if and only if
\Pi
is compact, Hausdorff, and totally disconnected.

The profinite completion of an abstract group
G
, defined as
\widehat{G}=\varprojlim_{N\lhd_{f.i.}G}G/N
in
Definition 1.1
, is thus a profinite group.

2.2.
Residual finiteness

There is a canonical homomorphism.

\displaystyle\iota_{G}:\;G

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\widehat{G}

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle\longmapsto(gN)_{N\lhd_{f.i.}G}

It is easy to see that
\iota_{G}
is injective if and only if
G
is residually finite. Thus, in the following context, when
G
is residually finite, we always identify
G
as its image in
\widehat{G}
, and elements in
G
are also viewed as elements in
\widehat{G}
.

Hempel’s theorem for residual finiteness
[
19
]
, together with the virtual Haken theorem
[
1
]
, yields the following result.

Proposition 2.2
.

The fundamental group of any compact 3-manifold is residually finite.

2.5.
The profinite integers

The profinite completion of
\mathbb{Z}
, denoted as
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
, is called the ring of
profinite integers
. By definition,

\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}=\varprojlim\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}

where
n\in\mathbb{N}
is partially ordered by divisiblity.
Two more equivalent definitions for
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
are also useful. First, we can take a cofinal system
n!\in\mathbb{N}
, so that we can express
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
as an inverse limit over a sequence

\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}=\varprojlim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{Z}/n!.

Second, we can decompose
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
into its prime components, and find

\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}=\prod_{p\text{: prime}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

In particular, this shows that
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
is torsion-free.

The group of multiplicatively invertible elements in
\mathbb{Z}
is denoted by
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}
. Indeed,

\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}=\varprojlim(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}

is a profinite group.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\lambda,\mu\in\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}
and
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
n\neq 0
. Suppose
m\lambda=n\mu
. Then
\lambda=\pm\mu
and
m=\pm n
.

Proof.

m=n(\mu\lambda^{-1})
, so
m\equiv 0\pmod{n}
. Since
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, there exists
a\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
m=na
. Then
na=n(\mu\lambda^{-1})
; in other words,
n(a-\mu\lambda^{-1})=0
. Since
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
is torsion-free, it follows that
a-\mu\lambda^{-1}=0
. In particular,
a=\mu\lambda^{-1}\in\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}
. Thus,
a=\pm 1
, for otherwise,
a\notin(\mathbb{Z}/a)^{\times}
. Therefore,
\mu=\pm\lambda
and
m=\pm n
.
∎

3.2.

-regularity

Let

(see also

.

A series of works by Wilton–Zalesskii
[

]
imply that any profinite isomorphism between finite-volume hyperbolic 3-manifolds respects the peripheral structure, see

and

Peripheral

4.1.
The group-level explanation

This subsection is supplementary. Though it will not be used in the subsequent sections, it might provide some help in understanding the reasonableness of the definition of homology coefficient. Indeed, we show that
there is another way to determine the homology coefficient, focusing only on the group level instead of the abelianization (homology) level.
Let us start with some preparations.

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose
M
and
N
are finite-volume hyperbolic 3-manifolds, and
f:\widehat{\pi_{1}M}\to\widehat{\pi_{1}N}
is an isomorphism. Let
\gamma\in\widehat{\pi_{1}N}
, and let
C_{\gamma}:\widehat{\pi_{1}N}\to\widehat{\pi_{1}N}
be the conjugation by
\gamma
on the left. Then
\mathbf{coef}(f)=\mathbf{coef}(C_{\gamma}\circ f)
.

Proof.

For simplicity of notation, we assume
\mathbf{coef}(f)=\pm\lambda
.

Case 1:

b_{1}(M)=b_{1}(N)>0
.

In fact, the conjugation has no effect on the (profinite) abelianization level, that is to say
f_{\ast}:\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Z}}H_{1}(M;\mathbb{Z})\to\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Z}}H_{1}(N;\mathbb{Z})
and
(C_{\gamma}\circ f)_{\ast}:\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Z}}H_{1}(M;\mathbb{Z})\to\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Z}}H_{1}(N;\mathbb{Z})
are the same map. Thus, the assumption that
f
is
\lambda
-regular implies that
C_{\gamma}\circ f
is also
\lambda
-regular. In this case,
b_{1}(M)=b_{1}(N)>0
, so there is no need to pass on to finite covers. Thus,
\mathbf{coef}(C_{\gamma}\circ f)=\pm\lambda
.

Case 2:

b_{1}(M)=b_{1}(N)=0
.

Let
M^{\prime}
and
N^{\prime}
be a pair of
f
–corresponding finite regular covers of
M
and
N
with
b_{1}(M^{\prime})=b_{1}(N^{\prime})>0
, that is,
\pi_{1}M^{\prime}
and
\pi_{1}N^{\prime}
are normal subgroups of
\pi_{1}M
and
\pi_{1}N
respectively. Then
(C_{\gamma}\circ f)(\widehat{\pi_{1}M^{\prime}})=C_{\gamma}(\widehat{\pi_{1}N^{\prime}})=\widehat{\pi_{1}N^{\prime}}
. Denote
f^{\prime}=f|_{\widehat{\pi_{1}M^{\prime}}}
and
(C_{\gamma}\circ f)^{\prime}=(C_{\gamma}\circ f)|_{\widehat{\pi_{1}M^{\prime}}}
. Then
f^{\prime}
is
\lambda
-regular.

We choose
\eta\in\widehat{\pi_{1}N^{\prime}}\lhd\widehat{\pi_{1}N}
and

such that

. Since
\pi_{1}N^{\prime}\lhd\pi_{1}N
, the conjugation map
C_{h}:\pi_{1}N\to\pi_{1}N
restricts to an isomorphism
\varphi:\pi_{1}N^{\prime}\to\pi_{1}N^{\prime}
. Then it is easy to verify that the following diagram commutes.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\pi_{1}N^{\prime}}}

For a profinite group

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
M
and
N
be finite-volume hyperbolic 3-manifolds. Suppose

is an isomorphism. If

and

are non-trivial elements such that

is conjugate to
\beta^{\lambda}
in
\widehat{\pi_{1}N}
for some

. Then,
\mathbf{coef}(f)=\pm\lambda
.

Proof.

By

, we can compose

with a conjugation and assume that

.

Since
\pi_{1}M
and
\pi_{1}N
are torsion-free, the subgroups

and

are isomorphic to

.
According to
[

]
, any abelian subgroup of a virtually compact special group is a virtual retract. Combined with the virtual specialization theorem

]
, there exists a finite-index subgroup of

corresponding to a finite cover

such that

. By

, the map

represents a non-trivial class in

. In particular,

.

Let

be the

–corresponding finite cover of

that corresponds to

. Then

, and

represents a class in

.

Let

. Then,

is an isomorphism of

sends the non-trivial class

is an isomorphism,

is a non-trivial class in

is

, so

.
∎

Moreover, the candidates

are cusped, one can choose

,

5.
(Co)homology theory and relative (co)homology theory for profinite groups

The (co)homology theory for profinite groups was first defined by Serre
[
33
]
, and a relative version of this theory was defined and studied in depth by Wilkes
[
44
]
, which is essential to the proof in this paper.
In this section, we briefly review this theory and sketch some important properties that will be used in the following sections.

5.1.
Definitions

We start by reviewing the definition for relative (co)homology theory for discrete groups. We refer the readers to
[
2
,
35
]
for general references.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
G
be a discrete group, and let
M
be a
G
-module.

(1)

The
homology and cohomology of
G
with coefficients in
M
are defined as

H_{\ast}(G;M)=\mathrm{Tor}^{G}_{\ast}(\mathbb{Z},M),\;\text{and}\;H^{\ast}(G;M)=\mathrm{Ext}^{\ast}_{G}(\mathbb{Z},M).

(2)

Let
\mathcal{H}=\{H_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a non-empty collection of subgroups in
G
, and let
\Delta_{G,\mathcal{H}}
be the kernel of the augmentation homomorphism
\mathop{\oplus}_{i\in I}\mathbb{Z}[G/H_{i}]\to\mathbb{Z}
. The
homology and cohomology of
G
relative to
\mathcal{H}
with coefficients in
M
are defined as

H_{\ast+1}(G,\mathcal{H};M)=\mathrm{Tor}^{G}_{\ast}(\Delta_{G,\mathcal{H}}^{\perp},M),\;\text{and}\;H^{\ast+1}(G,\mathcal{H};M)=\mathrm{Ext}_{G}^{\ast}(\Delta_{G,\mathcal{H}},M),

where
\Delta_{G,\mathcal{H}}^{\perp}
denotes
\Delta_{G,\mathcal{H}}
with the right
G
-action
\delta\cdot g=g^{-1}\delta
.

With the above functors replaced by their continuous version, a similar construction is adapted to profinite groups that applies for specific coefficient modules.

Notation 5.2
.

Let
\Pi
be a profinite group.
\mathfrak{C}(\Pi)
denotes the category of profinite
\Pi
-modules,
\mathfrak{D}(\Pi)
denotes the category of discrete
\Pi
-modules, and
\mathfrak{F}(\Pi)=\mathfrak{C}(\Pi)\cap\mathfrak{D}(\Pi)
denotes the category of finite
\Pi
-modules.

For a profinite group
\Pi
,
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}[\![\Pi]\!]
denotes the complete group algebra of
\Pi
, which is a profinite ring, see
[
32
, Section 5.3]
.
When applying the continuous version of Tor, Ext, and (co)homology functors, it is sometimes more convenient to restrict ourselves to the category of
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}[\![\Pi]\!]
-modules rather than the category of
\Pi
-modules. The next proposition points out their relations.

Proposition 5.3

(
[
32
, Proposition 5.3.6]
)
.

Let
\Pi
be a profinite group.

(1)

The category of profinite
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}[\![\Pi]\!]
-modules
\mathfrak{C}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}[\![\Pi]\!])
is identical with
\mathfrak{C}(\Pi)
.

(2)

The category of discrete
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}[\![\Pi]\!]
-modules
\mathfrak{D}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}[\![\Pi]\!])
is identical with the subcategory of torsion modules in
\mathfrak{D}(\Pi)
.

(3)

\mathfrak{F}(\Pi)=\mathfrak{C}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}[\![\Pi]\!])\cap\mathfrak{D}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}[\![\Pi]\!])
.

Though the cohomology of a profinite group
\Pi
is actually defined for coefficients in
\mathfrak{D}(\Pi)
, see
[
32
, Section 6.4]
, only those in
\mathfrak{D}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}[\![\Pi]\!])
will be used in this article. Thus, we introduce the definitions following
[
32
, Section 6.2–6.3]
.

Definition 5.4
.

Let
\Pi
be a profinite group.

(1)

For

, the
profinite homology of
\Pi
with coefficients in
M
is defined as

\mathbf{H}_{\ast}(\Pi;M)=\mathbf{Tor}_{\ast}^{\Pi}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}},M)\in\mathfrak{C}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}).

(2)

For
A\in\mathfrak{D}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}[\![\Pi]\!])
, the
profinite cohomology of
\Pi
with coefficients in
A
is defined as

\mathbf{H}^{\ast}(\Pi;A)=\mathbf{Ext}^{\ast}_{\Pi}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}},A)\in\mathfrak{D}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}).

Here
\mathbf{Tor}
and
\mathbf{Ext}
are the continuous versions of
\mathrm{Tor}
and
\mathrm{Ext}
defined in
[
32
, Section 6.1]
.

Most generally, relative profinite (co)homology is defined for a profinite group relative to a collection of closed subgroups continuously indexed over a profinite space, see
[
44
]
. In this article, we shall only consider the profinite (co)homology of a profinite group relative to a finite collection of closed subgroups. We abbreviate this setting into the following terminology.

Definition 5.5
.

(1)

We say that
(G,\mathcal{H})
is a
discrete group pair
if
G
is a discrete group, and
\mathcal{H}=\{H_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is an ordered, non-empty, finite collection of subgroups in
G
.

(2)

We say that
(\Pi,\mathcal{S})
is a
profinite group pair
if
\Pi
is a profinite group, and
\mathcal{S}=\{S_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is an ordered, non-empty, finite collection of closed subgroups in
\Pi
.

We now define the relative profinite (co)homology following
[
44
, Definition 2.1]
.

Definition 5.6
.

Given a profinite group pair
(\Pi,\mathcal{S})
, let
\mathbf{\Delta}_{\Pi,\mathcal{S}}
be the kernel of the augmentation homomorphism

\mathop{\oplus}_{i=1}^{n}\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}[\![\Pi/S_{i}]\!]\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}.

Then
\mathbf{\Delta}_{\Pi,\mathcal{S}}\in\mathfrak{C}(\Pi)=\mathfrak{C}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}[\![\Pi]\!])

(1)

For

, the
profinite homology of
\Pi

\mathcal{S}
with coefficients in
M

\mathbf{H}_{\ast+1}(\Pi,\mathcal{S};M)=\mathbf{Tor}_{\ast}^{\Pi}(\mathbf{\Delta}_{\Pi,\mathcal{S}}^{\perp},M)\in\mathfrak{C}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}).

(2)

For
A\in\mathfrak{D}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}[\![\Pi]\!])
, the
profinite cohomology of
\Pi
relative to
\mathcal{S}
with coefficients in
A

\mathbf{H}^{\ast+1}(\Pi,\mathcal{S};A)=\mathbf{Ext}^{\ast}_{\Pi}(\mathbf{\Delta}_{\Pi,\mathcal{S}},A)\in\mathfrak{D}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}).

5.2.
The long exact sequence

The following proposition demonstrates the long exact sequence for relative profinite homology, analogous to the one for relative homology of discrete groups.

The short exact sequence

in

induces a long exact sequence

\cdots\to\mathbf{Tor}_{k}^{\Pi}(\mathbf{\Delta}_{\Pi,\mathcal{S}}^{\perp},M)\to\mathop{\oplus}\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{Tor}_{k}^{\Pi}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}[\![\Pi/S_{i}]\!]^{\perp},M)\to\mathbf{Tor}_{k}^{\Pi}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}},M)\to\mathbf{Tor}_{k-1}^{\Pi}(\mathbf{\Delta}_{\Pi,\mathcal{S}}^{\perp},M)\to\cdots

by the homological functors

, see
[

Note that

by the profinite version of Shapiro’s lemma
[

. Thus, this implies the long exact sequence stated in the proposition.

5.4.
Cohomological goodness

Cohomological goodness was first defined by Serre in
[
33
]
, which can also be generalized to the relative version.

For a discrete group
G
, let
\mathfrak{F}(G)
denote the category of finite
G
-modules. It is easy to see that
\mathfrak{F}(G)=\mathfrak{F}(\widehat{G})
.

Definition 5.13
.

(1)

A discrete group
G
is
cohomologically good
(in the sense of Serre) if for any
A\in\mathfrak{F}(G)=\mathfrak{F}(\widehat{G})
, the map
\iota^{\ast}:\mathbf{H}^{\ast}(\widehat{G};A)\to H^{\ast}(G;A)
is an isomorphism.

(2)

A discrete group pair
(G,\mathcal{H})
is
cohomologically good
if for any
A\in\mathfrak{F}(G)=\mathfrak{F}(\widehat{G})
, the map
\iota^{\ast}:\mathbf{H}^{\ast}(\widehat{G},\overline{\mathcal{H}};A)\to H^{\ast}(G,\mathcal{H};A)
is an isomorphism.

Example 5.14
.

The following groups are cohomologically good.

(1)

Finite groups.

(2)

Finitely generated abelian groups,
[
33
, p. 16, Exercise 2]
.

(3)

Finitely generated free groups and surface groups,
[
17
, Proposition 3.6]
.

(4)

Finitely generated 3-manifold groups,
[
9
, Theorem 3.5.1 and Lemma 3.7.1]
.

A combination of
Proposition 2.4
and
[
44
, Proposition 6.3]
yields the following proposition, which passes the cohomological goodness from groups to a group pair.

Proposition 5.15
.

Let
(G,\mathcal{H}=\{H_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n})
be a discrete group pair. Suppose that
G
is cohomologically good, each
H_{i}
is cohomologically good, and the profinite topology on
G
induces the full profinite topology on each
H_{i}
. Then the discrete group pair
(G,\mathcal{H})
is cohomologically good.

The cohomological goodness can be transformed into the homological version by Pontryagin duality.

Definition 5.16
.

A discrete group
G
is of
type
\text{FP}_{\infty}
if the
G
-module
\mathbb{Z}
is of type
\text{FP}_{\infty}
. A discrete group pair
(G,\mathcal{H})
is of
type
\text{FP}_{\infty}
if the
G
-module
\Delta_{G,\mathcal{H}}
is of type
\text{FP}_{\infty}
.

Proposition 5.17

(
[
44
, Proposition 6.2]
)
.

(1)

Suppose
G
is a cohomologically good discrete group of type
\text{FP}_{\infty}
. Then for any
M\in\mathfrak{F}(G)=\mathfrak{F}(\widehat{G})
, the map
\iota_{\ast}:H_{\ast}(G;M)\to\mathbf{H}_{\ast}(\widehat{G};M)
is an isomorphism.

(2)

Suppose
(G,\mathcal{H})
is a cohomologically good discrete group pair of type
\text{FP}_{\infty}
. Then for any
M\in\mathfrak{F}(G)=\mathfrak{F}(\widehat{G})
, the map
\iota_{\ast}:H_{\ast}(G,\mathcal{H};M)\to\mathbf{H}_{\ast}(\widehat{G},\overline{\mathcal{H}};M)
is an isomorphism.

Convention 5.18
.

In the remaining part of this article,
\mathbb{Z}
or
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
appearing as coefficient modules of a certain (relative) (profinite) homology is assumed to have trivial action by the group unless otherwise stated.

Proposition 5.19
.

(1)

Let
G
be a cohomologically good discrete group of type
\text{FP}_{\infty}
. Then, there are isomorphisms of
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-modules

\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Z}}H_{\ast}(G;\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\;\;\cong\;\;}H_{\ast}(G;\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})\xrightarrow[\iota_{\ast}]{\;\;\cong\;\;}\mathbf{H}_{\ast}(\widehat{G};\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}).

(2)

Let
(G,\mathcal{H})
be a cohomologically good discrete group pair of type
\text{FP}_{\infty}
. Then, there are isomorphisms of
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-modules

\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Z}}H_{\ast}(G,\mathcal{H};\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\;\;\cong\;\;}H_{\ast}(G,\mathcal{H};\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})\xrightarrow[\iota_{\ast}]{\;\;\cong\;\;}\mathbf{H}_{\ast}(\widehat{G},\overline{\mathcal{H}};\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}).

Proof.

We only prove part (
2
), and the proof for part (
1
) is similar. The first isomorphism follows from the universal coefficient theorem. Since
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
has trivial
G
-action, there is a short exact sequence

0\to\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Z}}H_{\ast}(G,\mathcal{H};\mathbb{Z})\to H_{\ast}(G,\mathcal{H};\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})\to\mathrm{Tor}^{\mathbb{Z}}_{1}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}},H_{\ast-1}(G,\mathcal{H};\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}))\to 0,

see
[
7
, p. 60, Exercise 3]
.
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
is torsion-free, and is hence a flat
\mathbb{Z}
-module. Thus,
\mathrm{Tor}^{\mathbb{Z}}_{1}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}},H_{\ast-1}(G,\mathcal{H};\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}))=0
, which implies the first isomorphism.

Moving on to the second isomorphism, we first deal with the profinite side. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathbb{Z}/n!
be the finite
\widehat{G}
-module with trivial
\widehat{G}
-action, and the quotient maps
\mathbb{Z}/(n+1)!\to\mathbb{Z}/n!
are also homomorphisms of
\widehat{G}
-modules. Then in
\mathfrak{C}(\widehat{G})
, we still have
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}=\varprojlim_{n}\mathbb{Z}/n!
. The functor
\mathbf{Tor}_{\ast}^{\widehat{G}}
commutes with inverse limits
[
32
, Corollary 6.1.10]
, so there is an isomorphism

\mathbf{H}_{\ast}(\widehat{G},\overline{\mathcal{H}};\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})=\mathbf{Tor}_{\ast-1}^{\widehat{G}}(\mathbf{\Delta}_{\widehat{G},\overline{\mathcal{H}}}^{\perp},\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}\varprojlim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathbf{Tor}_{\ast-1}^{\widehat{G}}(\mathbf{\Delta}_{\widehat{G},\overline{\mathcal{H}}}^{\perp},\mathbb{Z}/n!)=\varprojlim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathbf{H}_{\ast}(\widehat{G},\overline{\mathcal{H}};\mathbb{Z}/n!).

As for the discrete side, the relative homology of a group pair may not, in general, commute with the inverse limit taken in the coefficient modules. However, in this specific case, we show that

(5.20)

H_{\ast}(G,\mathcal{H};\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})\longrightarrow\varprojlim\limits_{n\to\infty}H_{\ast}(G,\mathcal{H};\mathbb{Z}/n!)

is an isomorphism.

Since
(G,\mathcal{H})
is of type
\text{FP}_{\infty}
, by
[
7
, Chapter VIII, Proposition 4.5]
, we can take a free resolution
F_{\bullet}\to\Delta_{G,\mathcal{H}}^{\perp}\to 0
of finite type in the category of right
\mathbb{Z}[G]
-modules. Let
C^{n}_{\bullet}=F_{\bullet}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[G]}\mathbb{Z}/n!
. Then,
C^{0}_{\bullet}\leftarrow C^{1}_{\bullet}\leftarrow C^{2}_{\bullet}\leftarrow\cdots
is a tower of chain complexes of abelian groups.
Note that
\mathbb{Z}/(n+1)!\to\mathbb{Z}/n!
is surjective and the tensor product
F_{k}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[G]}-
is right exact, so
C^{n+1}_{k}\to C^{n}_{k}
is surjective for any
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
. Consequently, the tower
C^{0}_{\bullet}\leftarrow C^{1}_{\bullet}\leftarrow\cdots
satisfies degree-wise the Mittag-Leffler condition. Then, according to
[
39
, Theorem 3.5.8]
, there is a short exact sequence

0\to{\varprojlim\limits_{n}}^{1}H_{\ast+1}(C^{n}_{\bullet})\to H_{\ast}(\varprojlim\limits_{n}C^{n}_{\bullet})\to\varprojlim\limits_{n}H_{\ast}(C^{n}_{\bullet})\to 0.

By construction
H_{\ast}(C^{n}_{\bullet})=\mathrm{Tor}_{\ast}^{G}(\Delta^{\perp}_{G,\mathcal{H}},\mathbb{Z}/n!)=H_{\ast+1}(G,\mathcal{H};\mathbb{Z}/n!)
. Note that
C^{n}_{k}\cong(\mathbb{Z}/n!)^{\oplus r_{k}}
, where
F_{k}=\mathbb{Z}[G]^{\oplus r_{k}}
. Thus,
\varprojlim_{n}C^{n}_{k}\cong\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{\oplus r_{k}}\cong F_{k}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[G]}\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
, and
H_{\ast}(\varprojlim_{n}C^{n}_{\bullet})\cong H_{\ast}(F_{\bullet}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[G]}\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})=\mathrm{Tor}_{\ast}^{G}(\Delta^{\perp}_{G,\mathcal{H}},\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})=H_{\ast+1}(G,\mathcal{H};\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
. Thus, we actually have a short exact sequence

0\to{\varprojlim\limits_{n}}^{1}H_{\ast}(C^{n}_{\bullet})\to H_{\ast}(G,\mathcal{H};\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})\xrightarrow{\;\phi\;}\varprojlim\limits_{n}H_{\ast}(G,\mathcal{H};\mathbb{Z}/n!)\to 0.

The map
\phi
is exactly the one in (
5.20
), since it is induced by the maps between the coefficient modules
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathbb{Z}/n!
.

Note that for each
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
,
C^{n}_{k}\cong(\mathbb{Z}/n!)^{\oplus r_{k}}
is finite, so
H_{k}(C^{n}_{\bullet})
is also finite. As a consequence, the tower of abelian groups
H_{k}(C^{n}_{\bullet})
satisfies the Mittag-Leffler condition, and
\varprojlim^{1}_{n}H_{k}(C^{n}_{\bullet})=0
according to
[
39
, Proposition 3.5.7]
. Therefore, the map
\phi
is indeed an isomorphism.

We can now relate the two sides.
Since
(G,\mathcal{H})
is cohomologically good and of type
\text{FP}_{\infty}
,
Proposition 5.17
implies that for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the map
\iota_{\ast}:H_{\ast}(G,\mathcal{H};\mathbb{Z}/n!)\to\mathbf{H}_{\ast}(\widehat{G},\overline{\mathcal{H}};\mathbb{Z}/n!)
is an isomorphism. In addition, these isomorphisms are natural with respect to the coefficient modules
\mathbb{Z}/n!
. Thus, we have a commutative diagram of abelian groups

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H_{\ast}(G,\mathcal{H};\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbf{H}_{\ast}(\widehat{G},\overline{\mathcal{H}};\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\varprojlim\limits_{n}H_{\ast}(G,\mathcal{H};\mathbb{Z}/n!)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\varprojlim\limits_{n}\mathbf{H}_{\ast}(\widehat{G},\overline{\mathcal{H}};\mathbb{Z}/n!)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{\ast}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{\ast}}

where the two vertical maps and the bottom map are all proven to be isomorphisms. Thus,
\iota_{\ast}:{H_{\ast}(G,\mathcal{H};\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})}\to{\mathbf{H}_{\ast}(\widehat{G},\overline{\mathcal{H}};\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})}
is also an isomorphism (of abelian groups). Note that
\iota_{\ast}
is obviously a homomorphism of
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-modules, so the proof completes.

(Co)homology of groups are related to (co)homology of their Eilenberg-MacLane spaces, possibly with respect to local coefficents. For simplicity, we make the statements for

Definition 5.25

Suppose

Remark 5.31
.

The switch of basepoints in
Theorem 5.29
and

induces possible conjugations on the subgroups
H_{i}
and

. However, these do not affect the homology groups up to canonical isomorphisms similar as

.

In order to apply this construction to 3-manifolds, let us first make some preparations.

Proposition 5.32

(
[
44
, Proposition 6.15]
)
.

Suppose
(X,Y)
is an Eilenberg-MacLane pair for a discrete group pair
(G,\mathcal{H})
, and
X
has finitely many cells in each dimension. Then
(G,\mathcal{H})
is of type
\text{FP}_{\infty}
.

Proposition 5.33
.

Let
M
be a compact, orientable, aspherical, boundary-irreducible 3-manifold, and let
\Sigma
be a properly embedded, incompressible and boundary-incompressible surface in
M
. Then the profinite topology on
\pi_{1}M
induces the full profinite topology on
\pi_{1}\Sigma
.

Proof.

We divide the proof into three cases.

Case 1:

M
is closed, and then
\Sigma
is also closed.

A sufficient condition for
\pi_{1}M
to induce the full profinite topology on
\pi_{1}\Sigma
is that any finite-index subgroup of
\pi_{1}\Sigma
is separable in
\pi_{1}M
, since this will imply that any closed subset in
\pi_{1}\Sigma
under its profinite topology is closed under the subspace topology of
\pi_{1}\Sigma
inherited from the profinite topology of
\pi_{1}M
. In other words, for any finite cover
\widetilde{\Sigma}
of
\Sigma
, there is a
\pi_{1}
-injective immersion
f:\widetilde{\Sigma}\to\Sigma\hookrightarrow M
, and it suffices to show that
f_{\ast}(\pi_{1}\widetilde{\Sigma})
is separable in
\pi_{1}M
.

The criteria for separability is established by Przytycki–Wise
[
30
]
.
[
30
, Theorem 1.1]
showed that given a
\pi_{1}
-injective immersed closed surface
f:S\looparrowright M
,
f_{\ast}(\pi_{1}S)
is separable in
\pi_{1}M
if
f
lifts to an embedding in a finite cover
M^{\prime}
of
M
. Liu
[
22
]
provides a characterization for virtual embedding. In fact, for an essentially immersed closed surface
f:S\looparrowright M
, he
defined a spirality character
s(f)\in H^{1}(\Phi(S);\mathbb{Q}^{\times})
, in which
\Phi(S)
denotes the almost fiber part of
S
.
[
22
, Theorem 1.1]
showed that
S
virtually embeds if and only if
s(f):H_{1}(\Phi(S);\mathbb{Z})\to\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
takes value in
\{\pm 1\}
, for which Liu called that
S
is aspiral in the almost fiber part. In our case, if we denote
\iota:\Sigma\to M
as the embedding, then
s(\iota)
takes value in
\{\pm 1\}
.

Moreover,
[
22
, Proposition 4.1]
showed that the spirality character is natural. To be concrete, for a finite cover
\kappa:\widetilde{\Sigma}\to\Sigma
, the spirality character of the immersion
f:\widetilde{\Sigma}\xrightarrow{\kappa}\Sigma\xrightarrow{\iota}M
satisfies
s(f)=\kappa^{\ast}s(\iota)
. In other words, we have

s(f):H_{1}(\Phi(\widetilde{\Sigma});\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\;\kappa_{\ast}\;}H_{1}(\Phi(\Sigma);\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\;s(\iota)\;}\mathbb{Q}^{\times}.

Therefore,
s(f)
also takes value in
\{\pm 1\}
. Consequently,
f
lifts to an embedding in a finite cover of
M
according to
[
22
, Theorem 1.1]
, and
f_{\ast}(\pi_{1}\widetilde{\Sigma})
is separable according to the criteria of Przytycki–Wise
[
30
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Case 2:

M
has non-empty boundary, while
\Sigma
is closed.

Let
D(M)
be the double of
M
along its boundary, which is a closed 3-manifold. Then the retraction
D(M)\to M
implies that
\pi_{1}M
injects into
\pi_{1}D(M)
through the inclusion map. Thus,
\Sigma
is an essentially embedded surface in
D(M)
. Since
M
is aspherical and boundary-incompressible,
D(M)
is also aspherical. Thus, by
Case 1
, the profinite topology on
\pi_{1}D(M)
induces the full profinite topology on
\pi_{1}\Sigma
. Note that
\pi_{1}\Sigma<\pi_{1}M<\pi_{1}D(M)
, so the profinite topology on
\pi_{1}M
also induces the full profinite topology on
\pi_{1}\Sigma
, since the profinite topology on the subgroup
\pi_{1}M
is always finer than the subspace topology inherited from the profinite topology of
\pi_{1}D(M)
.

Case 3:

M
has non-empty boundary, and
\Sigma
also has non-empty boundary.

Let
D(\Sigma)
be the double of
\Sigma
along its boundary, which embeds into
D(M)
. Since
\Sigma
is incompressible and boundary-incompressible, the double
D(\Sigma)
is incompressible in
D(M)
. According to
Case 1
,
\pi_{1}D(M)
induces the full profinite topology on
\pi_{1}D(\Sigma)
. There is a retraction map
r:\pi_{1}D(\Sigma)\to\pi_{1}\Sigma
induced by the folding, that is,
r|_{\pi_{1}\Sigma}=id
. For any finite-index subgroup
H<\pi_{1}\Sigma
,
r^{-1}(H)
is a finite-index subgroup in
\pi_{1}D(\Sigma)
satisfying
r^{-1}(H)\cap\pi_{1}\Sigma=H
. Thus, the profinite topology on
\pi_{1}D(\Sigma)
induces the full profinite topology on
\pi_{1}\Sigma
. Combining these two steps, the profinite topology on
\pi_{1}D(M)
induces the full profinite topology on
\pi_{1}\Sigma
. Then, by the same reasoning as
Case 2
, the profinite topology on
\pi_{1}M
induces the full profinite topology on
\pi_{1}\Sigma
.
∎

.

Let

]

6.
The profinite mapping degree

6.1.
The closed case

The mapping degree of a map between closed oriented manifolds is defined through its induced map on the top homology. We extend this definition into the setting of profinite groups.

Let
M
and
N
be closed, orientable, aspherical 3-manifolds, and suppose
f:\widehat{\pi_{1}M}\to\widehat{\pi_{1}N}
is a continuous homomorphism. Then,
f
induces a homormophism of
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-modules

f_{\ast}:\;\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes H_{3}(M;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbf{H}_{3}(\widehat{\pi_{1}M};\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})\longrightarrow\mathbf{H}_{3}(\widehat{\pi_{1}N};\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})\cong\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes H_{3}(N;\mathbb{Z}),

where the canonical isomorphisms
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes H_{3}(M;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbf{H}_{3}(\widehat{\pi_{1}M};\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
and
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes H_{3}(N;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbf{H}_{3}(\widehat{\pi_{1}N};\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
are given by
Corollary 5.27
.

Definition 6.1
.

Suppose
M
and
N
are closed, oriented, aspherical 3-manifolds. Let
[M]\in H_{3}(M;\mathbb{Z})
and
[N]\in H_{3}(N;\mathbb{Z})
be the fundamental classes representing the orientation. For a continuous homomorphism
f:\widehat{\pi_{1}M}\to\widehat{\pi_{1}N}
,
the
profinite mapping degree
of
f
is the unique element
\mathbf{deg}(f)\in\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

f_{\ast}(1\otimes[M])=\mathbf{deg}(f)\otimes[N]
.

In particular, when
f
is an isomorphism,
\mathbf{deg}(f)\in\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
M
and

be closed, oriented, aspherical 3-manifolds, and let

be a homomorphism. Then
\widehat{p}:\widehat{\pi_{1}M}\to\widehat{\pi_{1}N}
is a continuous homomorphism, and
\mathbf{deg}(\widehat{p})=\mathrm{deg}(p)

We claim that the following diagram commutes.

(6.3)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes H_{3}(\pi_{1}N;\mathbb{Z})}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{3}(\pi_{1}M;\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{3}(\pi_{1}N;\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})}
(b)
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{H}_{3}(\widehat{\pi_{1}M};\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1\otimes p_{\ast}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{\ast}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{\ast}}

The profinite mapping degree of a profinite isomorphism is invariant when passing to finite covers.

Lemma 6.4
.

Suppose
M
and
N
are closed, oriented, aspherical 3-manifolds, and suppose

and
N

Let

6.2.
The cusped hyperbolic case

Mapping degrees can also be defined in the case of manifolds with boundary.
Let
M
and
N
be compact, oriented, aspherical 3-manifolds with non-empty boundary. The mapping degree of a proper map

(i.e.

However, when we replace

8.
Calculation by Dehn filling

In this section we prove the second relation between the profinite mapping degree and the homology coefficient for a profinite isomorphism between cusped hyperbolic 3-manifolds. The method is to apply the Dehn filling construction established in
[
51
]
so as to pass the calculation to a profinite isomorphism between closed hyperbolic manifolds, and then deduce the relation according to
Proposition 6.5
.

8.1.
Dehn filling

Let us first specify our notions for Dehn fillings. Let
M
be a compact 3-manifold with non-empty boundary consisting of tori
\partial_{1}M,\cdots,\partial_{n}M
. The
slopes
on
\partial_{i}M
is denoted as

\text{slp}(\partial_{i}M)=\{\text{free homotopy classes of unoriented essential simple closed curves on }\partial_{i}M\cong T^{2}\}

which is equipped with discrete topology. Indeed, an element in
\text{slp}(\partial_{i}M)
can be viewed as (a
\pm
-pair of) primitive integral points in
H_{1}(\partial_{i}M;\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The one-point compactification of
\text{slp}(\partial_{i}M)
is denoted as
\text{slp}^{\ast}(\partial_{i}M)=\text{slp}(\partial_{i}M)\cup\{\infty\}
. In fact,
\text{slp}^{\ast}(\partial_{i}M)
can be viewed as a subset of
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}}/(x\sim-x)\cong S^{2}/\sim\cong S^{2}
. For simplicity of notation, we denote
\text{slp}(\partial M)=\text{slp}(\partial_{1}M)\times\cdots\times\text{slp}(\partial_{n}M)
and
\text{slp}^{\ast}(\partial M)=\text{slp}^{\ast}(\partial_{1}M)\times\cdots\times\text{slp}^{\ast}(\partial_{n}M)
.

Definition 8.1
.

Let

be a compact 3-manifold with non-empty boundary consisting of
n

as follows. For each

so that the meridian of the solid torus is attached to
c_{i}
; if
c_{i}=\infty
, we do nothing and skip this boundary component.

The key result in
[
51
]
is that a profinite isomorphic pair of orientable cusped hyperbolic 3-manifolds carries profinite isomorphisms between their Dehn fillings. For the application in the following subsections, let us succinctly explain this result. We first introduce a notation.

Let

be a compact 3-manifold with non-empty boundary consisting of
n

(

8.4.
Conclusions

Now we can summarize the various relations between the homology coefficient and the profinite mapping degree.

Proposition 8.10
.

Suppose
(M,N,f,\lambda,\Psi)
is a cusped hyperbolic setting of profinite isomorphism, with
M
and
N
oriented. Then
\mathbf{coef}(f)^{3}=\pm\mathbf{deg}(f)
.

Proof.

Let
U
be the open neighbourhood of
(\infty,\cdots,\infty)
in
\text{slp}^{\ast}(\partial M)
given by
Lemma 8.9
, and let
\mathbf{c}\in U\cap\text{slp}(\partial M)
.
Then according to
Corollary 8.8
and
Lemma 8.9
respectively,
\mathbf{coef}(f^{\mathrm{Df}(\mathbf{c})})=\mathbf{coef}(f)
and
\mathbf{deg}(f^{\mathrm{Df}(\mathbf{c})})=\mathbf{deg}(f)
. Note that

and
N_{\Psi(\mathbf{c})}
are closed hyperbolic 3-manifolds, so by

,
\mathbf{coef}(f^{\mathrm{Df}(\mathbf{c})})^{3}=\pm\mathbf{deg}(f^{\mathrm{Df}(\mathbf{c})})
. Combining these equations, we have
\mathbf{coef}(f)^{3}=\mathbf{coef}(f^{\mathrm{Df}(\mathbf{c})})^{3}=\pm\mathbf{deg}(f^{\mathrm{Df}(\mathbf{c})})=\pm\mathbf{deg}(f)
.

as the following corollary.

Definition 9.1

M=X(J)
and
N=X(K)
are homeomorphic. It then follows from
[
16
]
that
J
and
K
are equivalent.
∎

10.3.
Matching up
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C})
representations

In a series of works by Spitler and Bridson–McReynolds–Reid–Spitler
[
4
,
5
,
34
]
, a standard method has been established to study the interplay between linear representations and the profinite completion. We shall explain how peripheral regularity plays its role in this process. Firstly, let us reintroduce the construction in
[
4
,
5
,
34
]
.

Lemma 10.6
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group, and let
\rho:G\to\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C})
be a representation, where
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then, there exists a prime number
p
, a field isomorphism
\varsigma:\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\to\mathbb{C}
(which is not continuous in the classical topology), and a continuous homomorphism
\Phi:\widehat{G}\to\mathrm{SL}(n,\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}})
such that the following diagram commutes.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{SL}(n,\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{G}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi}
(continuous)
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varsigma_{\ast}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

Proof.

It is shown in
[
4
, Lemma 4.2]
(though only stated for
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
representations, but also works for
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C})
-representations) that there exist some prime number
p
and a field isomorphism
\theta:\mathbb{C}\to\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
such that the representation

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta_{\ast}\rho:\;G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{SL}(n,\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

is bounded, that is, the matrix entries in
\mathrm{im}(\theta_{\ast}\rho)
is bounded with respect to the unique
p
-adic norm.

Since
G
is finitely generated, there exists a finite extension
K
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
such that
\mathrm{im}(\theta_{\ast}\rho)\subseteq\mathrm{SL}(n,K)
.
Since
K
is finite over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
,
K
is a complete and locally compact metric space, and so is
\mathrm{SL}(n,K)
. Thus, the boundness of
\theta_{\ast}\rho
implies that the closure
\overline{\mathrm{im}(\theta_{\ast}\rho)}
in
\mathrm{SL}(n,K)
is compact. Moreover,
K
is totally disconnected, and so is
\mathrm{SL}(n,K)
. Therefore, the topological group
\overline{\mathrm{im}(\theta_{\ast}\rho)}
is compact, Hausdorff, and totally disconnected, which is thus a profinite group according to
Proposition 2.1
.

Consider the homomorphism

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi:\;G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{im}(\theta_{\ast}\rho)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\mathrm{im}(\theta_{\ast}\rho)}\subseteq\mathrm{SL}(2,K).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta_{\ast}\rho}

The universal property of profinite completion
[
32
, Lemma 3.2.1]
implies that the homomorphism
\psi
extends to a continuous homomorphism
\Psi:\widehat{G}\to\overline{\mathrm{im}(\theta_{\ast}\rho)}\subseteq\mathrm{SL}(2,K)
such that
\Psi\circ\iota_{G}=\psi
.
Then,
we define a continuous homomorphism

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi:\;\widehat{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\mathrm{im}(\theta_{\ast}\rho)}\subseteq\mathrm{SL}(2,K)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{SL}(2,\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Psi}
(continuous)

Denote
\varsigma=\theta^{-1}:\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\to\mathbb{C}
, then
\varsigma_{\ast}\circ\Phi\circ\iota_{G}=\varsigma_{\ast}\circ\theta_{\ast}\rho=\rho
, which yields the commutative diagram in the statement of this lemma.
∎

Lemma 10.6
enables us to ‘match up’
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C})
-representations of a profinitely isomorphic pair of finitely generated groups in a weak sense. Indeed, the construction of
\phi
depends on the choice of
p
and
\varsigma
; however, some properties of
\phi
are independent with the choice, for instance, regarding the ingredients of the discrete groups that are preserved under the profinite isomorphism. We apply this observation to a peripheral regular profinite isomorphism between bounded 3-manifolds.

As a reminder for the definition of peripheral regularity, we reformulate
Definition 1.3
into the following setting.

Convention 10.7
.

A
standard peripheral regular setting

(\!(M,N,f)\!)
consists of the following ingredients.

(1)

M
and
N
are compact 3-manifolds with incompressible toral boundaries, and
f:\widehat{\pi_{1}M}\to\widehat{\pi_{1}N}
is a peripheral regular isomorphism.

(2)

Denote the boundary components of
M
and
N
as
\partial_{1}M,\cdots,\partial_{k}M
and
\partial_{1}N,\cdots,\partial_{k}N
respectively, and choose conjugacy representatives of peripheral subgroups
\pi_{1}\partial_{i}M\leq\pi_{1}M
and
\pi_{1}\partial_{i}N\leq\pi_{1}N
.

(3)

Up to a reordering, assume
f(\overline{\pi_{1}\partial_{i}M})=\overline{\pi_{1}\partial_{i}N}^{g_{i}}
for
g_{i}\in\widehat{\pi_{1}N}
.

(4)

There are isomorphisms
\psi_{i}:\pi_{1}\partial_{i}M\to\pi_{1}\partial_{i}N
such that
C_{g_{i}}\circ f|_{\overline{\pi_{1}\partial_{i}M}}:\widehat{\pi_{1}\partial_{i}M}\to\widehat{\pi_{1}\partial_{i}N}
is the profinite completion of
\psi_{i}
.

Proposition 10.8
.

Let
(\!(M,N,f)\!)
be a standard peripheral regular setting (
Convention 10.7
), and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Given any representation
\theta:\pi_{1}N\to\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C})
, there exists a representation
\phi:\pi_{1}M\to\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C})
such that for each
1\leq i\leq k
, the two representations

\begin{gathered}\phi_{i}:\pi_{1}\partial_{i}M\hookrightarrow\pi_{1}M\xrightarrow{\phi}\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C})\quad\text{and}\quad\psi_{i}^{\ast}\theta_{i}:\pi_{1}\partial_{i}M\xrightarrow{\psi_{i}}\pi_{1}\partial_{i}N\hookrightarrow\pi_{1}N\xrightarrow{\theta}\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C})\end{gathered}

are equivalent (by conjugation in
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C})
).
Moreover, when
\theta
is non-abelian (resp. irreducible),
\phi
is also non-abelian (resp. irreducible).

Proof.

According to
Lemma 10.6
, there exists a field isomorphism
\varsigma:\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
\theta
extends to a representation

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\pi_{1}N}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{SL}(n,\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Theta}
(continuous)
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varsigma_{\ast}}

Let
\Phi=\Theta\circ f:\widehat{\pi_{1}M}\to\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, which is continuous, and let

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi:\;\pi_{1}M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\pi_{1}M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{SL}(n,\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi=\Theta\circ f}
(continuous)
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varsigma_{\ast}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

Then, for any
1\leq i\leq k
and any
\alpha\in\pi_{1}\partial_{i}M
,

\displaystyle\phi_{i}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\varsigma_{\ast}\Theta(f(\alpha))=\varsigma_{\ast}\Theta(g_{i}^{-1}\psi_{i}(\alpha)g_{i})

\displaystyle(\text{\autoref{convsprs}~(\ref{convsprs4})})

\displaystyle=\varsigma_{\ast}\Theta(g_{i})^{-1}\cdot\theta(\psi_{i}(\alpha))\cdot\varsigma_{\ast}\Theta(g_{i})

\displaystyle=\varsigma_{\ast}\Theta(g_{i})^{-1}\cdot(\psi_{i}^{\ast}\theta_{i})(\alpha)\cdot\varsigma_{\ast}\Theta(g_{i}).

Note that the element
\varsigma_{\ast}\Theta(g_{i})\in\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C})
that induces the conjugation is irrelavent with
\alpha
. Therefore,
{\phi_{i}}
and
{\psi_{i}^{\ast}\theta_{i}}
are equivalent.

Next, we prove the two extra properties. First, we show that if the presentation
\phi
in this construction is abelian, then
\theta
is also abelian.
In this case,
[\pi_{1}M,\pi_{1}M]\subseteq\ker(\phi)
; since
\Phi
is continuous, we have
\overline{[\pi_{1}M,\pi_{1}M]}\subseteq\ker(\Phi)
.
According to
[
50
, Lemma 2.8]
,
\overline{[\pi_{1}M,\pi_{1}M]}=\overline{[\widehat{\pi_{1}M},\widehat{\pi_{1}M}]}
.
Since
f
is an isomorphism of profinite groups,
f(\overline{[\widehat{\pi_{1}M},\widehat{\pi_{1}M}]})=\overline{[\widehat{\pi_{1}N},\widehat{\pi_{1}N}]}
.
Therefore,
\overline{[\widehat{\pi_{1}N},\widehat{\pi_{1}N}]}\subseteq\ker(\Phi\circ f^{-1})=\ker(\Theta)
. Since
[\pi_{1}N,\pi_{1}N]\subseteq\overline{[\widehat{\pi_{1}N},\widehat{\pi_{1}N}]}
, we finally derive that
[\pi_{1}N,\pi_{1}N]\subseteq\ker(\theta)
, which is to say that
\theta
is also abelian.

Second, we show that if the presentation
\phi
in this construction is reducible, then
\theta
is also reducible. In this case,
\Phi(\pi_{1}M)
keeps invariant a non-trivial linear subspace
W\subsetneq\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}^{n}
. Since
\pi_{1}M
is dense in
\widehat{\pi_{1}M}
and
\Phi
is continuous,
\Phi(\widehat{\pi_{1}M})
actually keeps
W
invariant. Note that
\Phi(\widehat{\pi_{1}M})=\Theta(\widehat{\pi_{1}N})
, so
\Theta(\widehat{\pi_{1}N})
also keeps
W
invariant. Consequently,
\theta(\pi_{1}N)
keeps the non-trivial linear subspace
\varsigma(W)\subsetneq\mathbb{C}^{n}
invariant, which is to say
\theta
is also reducible.
∎

For a finitely generated group
G
, let
\mathscr{Y}_{n}(G)
be the set of equivalence classes of
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C})
representations of
G
. There is a standard quotient map
\mathscr{Y}_{n}(G)\to\mathscr{X}_{n}(G)
. When
G=\pi_{1}Y
is the fundamental group of a compact, connected manifold
Y
, we abbreviate
\mathscr{Y}_{n}(G)
into
\mathscr{Y}_{n}(Y)
. When
Z
is a disconnected manifold consisting of components
Z_{1},\cdots,Z_{k}
, we denote
\mathscr{Y}_{n}(Z)=\mathscr{Y}_{n}(Z_{1})\times\cdots\times\mathscr{Y}_{n}(Z_{k})
. Any continuous map
Z\to Y
between compact manifolds induces a map
\mathscr{Y}_{n}(Y)\to\mathscr{Y}_{n}(Z)
by pulling back.

Corollary 10.9
.

Let
(\!(M,N,f)\!)
be a standard peripheral regular setting (
Convention 10.7
). Then, the images of the restriction maps
\mathscr{Y}_{n}(M)\to\mathscr{Y}_{n}(\partial M)
and
\mathscr{Y}_{n}(N)\to\mathscr{Y}_{n}(\partial N)
are identical through
\Psi=(\psi_{1},\cdots,\psi_{k})
.

Proof.

According to
Proposition 10.8
,
\Psi^{\ast}(\mathrm{im}(\mathscr{Y}_{n}(N)\to\mathscr{Y}_{n}(\partial N)))\subseteq\mathrm{im}(\mathscr{Y}_{n}(M)\to\mathscr{Y}_{n}(\partial M))
.
By symmetry, we consider
f^{-1}:\widehat{\pi_{1}N}\to\widehat{\pi_{1}M}
. Let
h_{i}=f^{-1}(g_{i}^{-1})\in\widehat{\pi_{1}M}
. Then
f^{-1}(\overline{\pi_{1}\partial_{i}N})={\overline{\pi_{1}\partial_{i}M}}^{h_{i}}
, and
C_{h_{i}}\circ f^{-1}|_{\overline{\pi_{1}\partial_{i}N}}
is the profinite completion of
\psi_{i}^{-1}
. Again by
Proposition 10.8
,
(\Psi^{-1})^{\ast}(\mathrm{im}(\mathscr{Y}_{n}(M)\to\mathscr{Y}_{n}(\partial M))\subseteq\mathrm{im}(\mathscr{Y}_{n}(N)\to\mathscr{Y}_{n}(\partial N))
. Combining the two sides yields the conclusion.
∎

Corollary 10.9
, in particular, holds for cusped hyperbolic 3-manifolds according to
Theorem 1.4
, in which the boundary homeomorphism
\Psi:\partial M\to\partial N
is either orientation-preserving on all components or orientation-reversing on all components according to
Proposition 7.1
(
1
). This proves
Theorem 1.7
.

We can now prove the first part of
Theorem 1.6
. Note that
\mathbb{Z}
, the group of the unknot, is profinitely rigid among all finitely generated residually finite groups, so we are justified to stay with non-trivial knots in the proof of
Theorem 1.6
.

Theorem 10.10
.

Let
J
and
K
be non-trivial knots in
S^{3}
. Suppose
f:\widehat{\pi_{1}(X(J))}\to\widehat{\pi_{1}(X(K))}
is an isomorphism that respects the peripheral structure. Then, up to possibly replacing
K
with its mirror image, the enhanced
A
-polynomials of
J
and
K
coincide:
\widetilde{A}_{J}(M,L)\doteq\widetilde{A}_{K}(M,L)
.

Proof.

By the profinite detection of geometrization and JSJ-decomposition (
Lemma 9.7
),
J
and
K
falls in the same class of torus knots, graph knots, hyperbolic knots, and satellite knots containing hyperbolic JSJ-pieces. When
J
and
K
are torus knots or graph knots,
[
3
, Theorem 1.5]
for the former case and
Lemma 10.5
for the latter case imply that
J
and
K
are equivalent. Hence, in these two cases, the (enhanced)
A
-polynomials of
J
and
K
certainly coincide up to a possible mirror image.

Now we assume that
J
and
K
are hyperbolic knots or satellite knots containing hyperbolic JSJ-pieces. Then, by
Theorem 1.4
for the former case, and by
Theorem 9.2
for the latter case,
f
is peripheral regular.
We follow the standard peripheral regular setting in
Convention 10.7
for
(\!(X(J),X(K),f)\!)
. Then, according to
Lemma 10.4
, the isomorphism
\psi:\pi_{1}(\partial X(J))\to\pi_{1}(\partial X(K))
is defined by
\psi(m_{J})=\pm m_{K}
and
\psi(m_{K})=\pm m_{K}
. Up to possibly replacing
K
with its mirror image, possibly changing the orientation of
l_{K}
, we can assume that
\psi(m_{J})=m_{K}
and
\psi(l_{J})=l_{K}
.

By
Definition 10.1
, in order to show
\widetilde{A}_{J}\doteq\widetilde{A}_{K}
, it suffices to show that
\xi^{\ast}(J)=\xi^{\ast}(K)
. We first show that
\xi^{\ast}(J)\supseteq\xi^{\ast}(K)
. For any
(M,L)\in\xi^{\ast}(K)
, let
\theta:\pi_{1}(X(K))\to\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
be a non-abelian representation such that

\tau(m_{K})=\begin{pmatrix}M&0\\
0&M^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\tau(l_{K})=\begin{pmatrix}L&0\\
0&L^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.

According to
Proposition 10.8
, there exists a non-abelian representation
\phi:\pi_{1}(X(J))\to\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
such that the two representations
\phi|:\pi_{1}(\partial X(J))\to\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
and
\tau|\circ\psi:\pi_{1}(\partial X(J))\to\pi_{1}(\partial X(K))\to\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
are equivalent. Thus, we can compose
\phi
with a conjugation in
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
to obtain a representaion
\phi^{\prime}:\pi_{1}(X(J))\to\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
, such that
\phi^{\prime}|:\pi_{1}(\partial X(J))\to\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
is equal to
\tau\circ\psi|:\pi_{1}(\partial(X(J))\to\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
. In particular,

\phi^{\prime}(m_{J})=\tau(\psi(m_{J}))=\tau(m_{K})=\begin{pmatrix}M&0\\
0&M^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\;\text{ and }\;\phi^{\prime}(l_{J})=\tau(\psi(l_{J}))=\tau(l_{K})=\begin{pmatrix}L&0\\
0&L^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.

Therefore,
(M,L)\in\xi^{\ast}(J)
.

By symmetry, replacing
f
with
f^{-1}
also implies
\xi^{\ast}(J)\subseteq\xi^{\ast}(K)
. Thus,
\xi^{\ast}(K)=\xi^{\ast}(J)
, which finishes the proof.
∎

10.4.
Getting rid of the peripheral structure

Now we prove the second part of
Theorem 1.6
. Most cases here can be restored to
Theorem 10.10
, such as the case of hyperbolic knots and hyperbolic-type satellite knots (i.e. satellite knots whose outermost JSJ-piece is hyperbolic), as profinite isomorphisms between them always respect the peripheral structure. The most tricky case here is cabled knots, for which we utilize the cabling formula by Ni–Zhang
[
28
]
to determine the
A
-polynomials.

Definition 10.11
.

Let
K
be a knot in
S^{3}
, and let
p,q
be a coprime pair of integers, where
\left|q\right|\geq 2
. The
(p,q)
-cable of
K
is a knot
C_{p,q}(K)
lying on
\partial N(K)
that represents the homotopy class
p[m_{K}]+q[l_{K}]
on
\partial N(K)
, where
(m_{K},l_{K})
is the preferred meridian-longitude pair.

Usually, the sign of
q
determines whether the orientation of
C_{p,q}(K)
follows or reverses that of
K
. However, the
A
-polynomial is not sensible to orientations on the knot (see
Proposition 10.3
), so in the discussion of this article, we may always assume
q>0
.

Now we state the cabling formula for the (enhanced)
A
-polynomial introduced by Ni–Zhang.

Theorem 10.12

(
[
28
, Theorem 2.8]
)
.

Let
K
be a non-trivial knot, and let
p,q
be a pair of coprime integers such that
q\geq 2
. Let
\widetilde{A}_{K}(M,L)
be the normalized enhanced
A
-polynomial. Then

\widetilde{A}_{C_{p,q}(K)}(M,L)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathrm{Red}\left(F_{p,q}(M,L)\cdot\mathrm{Res}_{y}(\widetilde{A}_{K}(M^{q},y),y^{q}-L)\right),&\text{if }\mathrm{deg}_{L}(\widetilde{A}_{K})\neq 0\\
&F_{p,q}(M,L)\cdot\widetilde{A}_{K}(M^{q}),&\text{if }\mathrm{deg}_{L}(\widetilde{A}_{K})=0\end{aligned}\right.

where
\mathrm{Res}_{y}
is the resultant of two polynomials in
y
, and
F_{p,q}
is defined by

F_{p,q}(x,y)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1+x^{2p}y,&\text{if }q=2\text{ and }p>0,\\
&x^{-2p}+y,&\text{if }q=2\text{ and }p<0,\\
&-1+x^{2pq}y^{2},&\text{if }q>2\text{ and }p>0,\\
&-x^{-2pq}+y^{2},&\text{if }q>2\text{ and }p<0.\end{aligned}\right.

Though
Theorem 10.12
is stated for the enhanced
A
-polynomial, one can deduce from the relation (
10.2
) and the formula
\mathrm{Res}_{y}(f(y)g(y),h(y))=\mathrm{Res}_{y}(f(y),h(y))\mathrm{Res}_{y}(g(y),h(y))
that for any non-trivial knot
K
,

A_{C_{p,q}(K)}(M,L)=\mathrm{Red}\left(F_{p,q}(M,L)\cdot\mathrm{Res}_{y}({A}_{K}(M^{q},y),y^{q}-L)\right),

where
A_{K}
is normalized.

In particular,
\widetilde{A}_{C_{p,q}(K)}
only relies on
\widetilde{A}_{K}
and
(p,q)
, and
{A}_{C_{p,q}(K)}
only relies on
{A}_{K}
and
(p,q)
.

Theorem 10.13
.

Suppose
J
and
K
are knots in
S^{3}
and
\widehat{\pi_{1}(X(J))}\cong\widehat{\pi_{1}(X(K))}
. Assume one of
J
and
K
is a prime knot. Then, up to possibly replacing
K
with its mirror image,
\widetilde{A}_{J}(M,L)\doteq\widetilde{A}_{K}(M,L)
.

Proof.

As in
Theorem 10.10
, we may also assume that
J
and
K
are non-trivial knots.
According to
Theorem 9.4
and
Lemma 9.7
(
2
), which does not rely on the peripheral structure,
J
and
K
belong to the same class of torus knots, graph knots, hyperbolic knots, or satellite knots with hyperbolic pieces. When they are torus knots, again by
[
3
]
,
J
and
K
are equivalent; and when they are graph knots, Wilkes
[
43
, Theorem B]
proved that
J
and
K
are equivalent whenever one of them is prime. Thus, in these two cases, obviously
\widetilde{A}_{J}\doteq\widetilde{A}_{K}
up to a possible mirror image. When
J
and
K
are hyperbolic knots, it follows from
Proposition 3.8
that any isomorphism
\widehat{\pi_{1}(X(J))}\cong\widehat{\pi_{1}(X(K))}
respects the peripheral structure. Hence, the conclusion for this case follows from
Theorem 10.10
.

In what follows, we assume that
J
and
K
are satellite knots with hyperbolic pieces.
For simplicity of notation, let
M=X(J)
and
N=X(K)
. The JSJ-graphs
\Gamma^{M}
and
\Gamma^{N}
are rooted trees, where the roots
r\in\Gamma^{M}
and
s\in\Gamma^{N}
correspond to the outermost JSJ-pieces. Let
f:\widehat{\pi_{1}M}\to\widehat{\pi_{1}N}
be an isomorphism, and let
f_{\bullet}:(\widehat{\mathcal{G}^{M}},\Gamma^{M})\xrightarrow{\cong}(\widehat{\mathcal{G}^{N}},\Gamma^{N})
be the congruent isomorphism given by
Theorem 9.4
. Then by
Lemma 9.7
(
1
),
f^{\dagger}(r)=s
. In particular,
M_{r}
and
N_{s}
are either both hyperbolic or both Seifert fibered by (
2
) of the same lemma.

When
M_{r}
and
N_{s}
are hyperbolic, it follows from
[
51
, Proposition 3.3]
that
f
respects the peripheral structure. This can also be deduced from
Lemma 9.7
(
4
). Thus, in this case, the conclusion follows from
Theorem 10.10
.

Now we assume that
M_{r}
and
N_{s}
are Seifert fibered. Then, according to
[
8
, Theorem 4.18]
, the outermost Seifert fibered piece of a satellite knot can only be a cable space
C_{p,q}=(S_{0,2};p/q)
(
\left|q\right|\geq 2
) or a composite space
S_{0,n}\times S^{1}
(

), where

denotes
S^{2}
removing
n
open disks. Note that when the outermost piece is a cable space, the degree of the root in the JSJ-graph is
1
, and when the outermost piece is a composite space, the degree of the root in the JSJ-graph is greater than
1
. Thus, the graph isomorphism
f^{\dagger}:\Gamma^{M}\to\Gamma^{N}
implies that
M_{r}
and
N_{s}
are either both cable spaces or both composite spaces. When they are both composite spaces, both
J
and
K
are non-prime. Thus, by assumption,
M_{r}=C_{p,q}
and
N_{s}=C_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}
are cable spaces.

Consequently,
J
is the
(p,q)
-cable of the companion knot
c(J)
, and
K
is the
(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})
-cable of the companion knot
c(K)
. We may assume with out loss of generality that
q>0
and
q^{\prime}>0
. By assumption,
c(J)
and
c(K)

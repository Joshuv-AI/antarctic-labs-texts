Energy and photon centroids of spatiotemporal light pulses and consequences for their intrinsic orbital angular momentum
Titouan Gadeyne, Thierry Ruchon

Abstract
An ongoing debate surrounding the intrinsic transverse orbital angular momentum (OAM) attributed to spatiotemporal optical vortex (STOV) light pulses has raised the energy and photon-density centroids as two alternative frameworks to define the center of a free electromagnetic wavepacket. We herein derive, within a single formalism, lowest-order expressions for the positions and velocities of both centroids, directly applicable to an arbitrary scalar, near-paraxial and quasi-monochromatic pulse envelope formulated in space-time. Examining pulses with structures including temporal chirp, wavefront rotation, Gaussian-type STOVs and tilted lobulated profiles, we illustrate general principles underlying the shifts and propagation dynamics of the two centroids, and discuss how these properties underpin the value and conservation of intrinsic OAM referenced to either centroid. Finally, despite widespread use of the notion of intrinsic OAM per photon , we argue that in neither framework could individual STOV photons be said to carry a well-defined quantum of intrinsic transverse OAM, as such quantities do not write as expectation values of a single operator for which STOV states could form a basis of orthogonal eigenmodes. This contrasts with the well-established quantization scheme for the total longitudinal OAM of light in spatial vortex beams, bearing implications for the interpretation of future experiments involving STOV pulses in quantum optics, nonlinear wave-mixing or light-matter interactions.

;
Bliokh 2021
;

;

One difficulty lies in the fact that the OAM
\mathbf{L}
is defined with respect to the frame origin: displacing this origin as
\mathbf{r}\to\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{0}
changes the OAM as
\mathbf{L}\to\mathbf{L}-\mathbf{r}_{0}\crossproduct\mathbf{P}
, where
\mathbf{P}
is the total linear momentum of the field
Bliokh and Nori 2015
. While this leaves the component of
\mathbf{L}
parallel to
\mathbf{P}
unchanged, its transverse part can be considerably affected, even by subwavelength shifts of the origin. This motivates the splitting
of optical OAM into
extrinsic
(whose value does depend on the choice of origin) and
intrinsic
(whose value does not) parts, as

\mathbf{L}=\mathbf{L}_{e}+\mathbf{L}_{i}
.

Broadly speaking, the extrinsic OAM
\mathbf{L}_{e}
of an optical pulse is due to the lever arm the propagation of its center creates with respect to the origin, which can be non-zero even for simple (
e.g.
plain Gaussian) wavepackets. In contrast, an intrinsic part
\mathbf{L}_{i}
only arises for wavepackets endowed with a more complex internal structure, inside which electromagnetic energy flows with a sense of rotation around a center point. Unfortunately, many procedures could be imagined to construct an origin-independent quantity from the total OAM
\mathbf{L}

Bliokh and Nori 2015
. In recent years, two main strategies have been put forward, which we now briefly review.

In a first approach (explored rigorously by Porras in

), the splitting is achieved by referencing OAM to the
energy centroid

\mathbf{r}_{\text{EC}}
of the pulse, as

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}_{i,\text{EC}}=\mathbf{L}-\mathbf{r}_{\text{EC}}\crossproduct\mathbf{P},

(1)

where
\mathbf{r}_{\text{EC}}

As a displacement of the origin by

\mathbf{r}_{\text{EC}}\to\mathbf{r}_{\text{EC}}-\mathbf{r}_{0}

\hbar\ell/2

Since

, the quantity
\mathbf{L}_{i,\text{EC}}
is itself conserved. In this respect, it was successfully applied by Hancock
et al.
to describe experiments involving spatiotemporally perturbed pulses, detected after some propagation

;
Hancock et al. 2025
.

An alternative strategy proposed by Bliokh

to the so-called

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}_{i,\text{PC}}=\mathbf{L}-\mathbf{r}_{\text{PC}}\crossproduct\mathbf{P}.

(2)

where
\mathbf{r}_{\text{PC}}
is obtained as the expectation value of the position operator against a
photon wavefunction

;
Bialynicki-Birula 1996
;
Smirnova et al. 2018
;
Bliokh 2023a
. It is an optical analogue to the “particle-number” centroid of a material body, which has already been used alongside the energy centroid to gain insight into the description of angular momentum phenomena

.
In this picture, STOV pulses with circular symmetry have
integer
intrinsic t-OAM per photon

, and the dependence of t-OAM on the space-time eccentricity of the pulse acquires an elegantly general form, coinciding with that for the longitudinal OAM of regular vortex beams

.
For an arbitrary wavepacket the photon centroid may not travel in the direction of
\mathbf{P}

;
Bliokh 2025
, hence this splitting of
\mathbf{L}
is generally not time-independent.
If the conserved nature of
\mathbf{L}_{i,\text{EC}}
makes it more convenient for describing free-space propagation, little is known about the possible relevance of either framework as a tool to interpret
e.g.
interactions of STOVs with matter. Up to this point, the t-OAM of STOVs has only been detected indirectly, by computing it from a measurement of the light field itself: in this case, both formalisms (
1
) and (
2
) could equivalently be applied, yielding very different yet self-consistent pictures.

Since these proposals, attempts at comparing energy and photon centroids have remained difficult, partly due to the distinct mathematical formalisms they involve: while
\mathbf{r}_{\text{EC}}
is commonly defined as an integral over space
(x,y,z)

Zangwill 2012
, expressing
\mathbf{r}_{\text{PC}}
requires a reciprocal-space formulation (
i.e.
plane-wave expansion) of the fields – for this reason, photon-centroid quantities have been obtained for theoretical fields with simple wavevector spectra, such as Bessel pulses
Smirnova et al. 2018
;
Bliokh 2023a
;
Bliokh 2023b
. Both centroids can prove cumbersome to compute however, as structured pulses are commonly prescribed with a complex amplitude envelope known in space-time
(x,y,t)
at a single coordinate
z
along their axis of propagation. Such a space-time formulation is arguably more in line with experimental manipulation of ultrashort pulses

, which are typically shaped in the frequency domain (thus controlling their
temporal
structure more directly than their
longitudinal
one), and probed with detectors set at a fixed
z
position.

In this work, we give a rigorous procedure to express electromagnetic quantities defined in reciprocal space directly in terms of a near-paraxial, quasi-monochromatic and linearly polarized pulse envelope specified in space-time. We obtain, within a single formalism, formulae for the two centroids, their velocities and associated intrinsic OAM – ample details on our calculations are provided in the Supplementary Material (SM)
sup
. These general expressions allow us to illustrate centroid trajectories and their consequences for intrinsic OAM on a variety of pulses with structures including temporal chirp, wavefront rotation, Gaussian-type STOVs and tilted lobulated profiles.
Lastly, we reevaluate the notion of intrinsic OAM
per photon
, observing that these constructions – regardless of the chosen centroid – are not quantities that individual photons in spatial or spatiotemporal optical vortices could carry in well-defined quanta: intrinsic OAM (transverse or longitudinal) is not generated by a single operator for which vortex states could be the eigenmodes. This differs from how the total longitudinal OAM of light is quantized in spatial vortex beams, which has offered an
unprecedented platform for quantum experiments
Mair et al. 2001
;
Molina-Terriza et al. 2007
;
Erhard et al. 2017
. From the perspective of quantum optics, STOV pulses should not be expected to function as analogues of regular vortex beams.

III.2
Propagation of the centroids

As captured by Eqs. (
8
) and (
10
), the two centroids always propagate with constant velocities along rectilinear trajectories. Centroid velocities underpin conservation properties of intrinsic OAM in time: while
\mathbf{v}_{\text{EC}}
is always aligned with
\mathbf{P}
, resulting in
\partial_{t}\mathbf{L}_{i,\text{EC}}=0
,
\mathbf{v}_{\text{PC}}
may not be, such that
\mathbf{L}_{i,\text{PC}}
is not conserved in general. Although this fact has been emphasized by advocates of (
1
) as a reason to discard this construction
Hancock et al. 2024
, to our understanding it could still find relevance as a tool to describe certain OAM phenomena. In fact, because the space-time envelope of
e.g.
a STOV wavepacket severely deforms upon propagation, it seems natural that its way of interacting with matter could change accordingly along the propagation axis: the interaction may then be more adequately quantified by a time-dependent parameter. Unlike the energy centroid, the photon centroid also has the property of being sensitive to frequency inhomogeneities within the pulse, which could prove important when dealing with dispersive materials.

Limiting our calculations to first order in
\kappa

\mathbf{v}_{\text{PC}}\approx\mathbf{v}_{\text{EC}}=c^{2}\mathbf{P}/U

U

\mathbf{P}

14

16

Bliokh 2023b

\kappa^{2}

\expectationvalue{i\partial_\tau}

e.g.

III.3
Intrinsic OAM per photon and optical vortices

Recent literature on STOVs has emphasized the notion of intrinsic OAM
per photon
.
In our formalism, the intrinsic OAM per photon (in units of
\hbar
) with respect to either centroid read, to lowest order,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{L}_{i,\text{EC}}^{\text{ph}}=\frac{\mathbf{L}_{i,\text{EC}}}{N}\approx&\expectationvalue{
[\vb{R}-\expval{\vb{R}}] \cross\begin{pmatrix}
-i\grad_\perp\\
0
\end{pmatrix}
}\end{split}

(21)

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}_{i,\text{PC}}^{\text{ph}}=\frac{\mathbf{L}_{i,\text{PC}}}{N}\approx

\displaystyle\expectationvalue{
[\vb{R}-\expval{\vb{R}}] \cross\begin{pmatrix}
-i\grad_\perp\\
i\partial_\tau/c
\end{pmatrix}
}.

(22)

They are manifestly independent of the overall position
\expectationvalue{\vb{R}}
of the prescribed pulse envelope, and therefore intrinsic. Both quantities write as the difference of one term of the form
\expectationvalue{\vb{R} \cross(-i\grad)}
and the analogous term where the expectation values are taken separately,
\expectationvalue{\vb{R}}\crossproduct\expectationvalue{-i\grad}
.

The energy-centroid construction only involves transverse momentum components
\gradient_{\perp}
. Indeed, to first order the internal structure of a pulse will only contribute transverse components to the momentum density (see Eq. (
16
)) – this is well illustrated by STOVs, for which it is known that the spatiotemporal phase vortex does not add additional momentum along the propagation direction
Hancock et al. 2021a
;
Porras 2023a
. In contrast, the photon-centroid construction features a complete (space-time) gradient
\gradient_{\text{st}}=(\gradient_{\perp},-\partial_{\tau}/c)
. Yet, in obtaining this result we did not have to postulate the existence of an additional longitudinal momentum density
Hancock et al. 2024
: inclusion of the temporal gradient is instead brought by the process of referencing to the photon centroid.

The terminology
per photon
may suggest that this is a property quantized at the level of single photons, in similar fashion to how the longitudinal OAM of regular vortex beams is quantized in integer steps
Molina-Terriza et al. 2007
. This tempting interpretation could be reinforced by recent studies on the upconversion of STOVs in harmonic or sum-frequency generation, which showed that their topological charge gets multiplied in accordance with the number of photons upconverted by the nonlinear process
Hancock et al. 2021b
;
Gui et al. 2021
;
Fang et al. 2021
;
Gao et al. 2023
. Yet, we can show with a simple argument that such intrinsic constructions are not quantities that photons in optical vortices carry in well-defined quanta. Consider a superposition of two STOV pulses, symmetric in space-time and with topological charges
\ell_{A}
and
\ell_{B}
:

\displaystyle\psi(x,t)

\displaystyle=\psi_{\ell_{A}}+\psi_{\ell_{B}}e^{i\Phi},

\displaystyle\psi_{\ell}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2^{\absolutevalue{\ell}}}{\absolutevalue{\ell}!}}e^{-(x/x_{0})^{2}-(ct/x_{0})^{2}}\left[\frac{ct}{x_{0}}-i\,\text{sgn}(\ell)\frac{x}{x_{0}}\right]^{\absolutevalue{\ell}},

where
\Phi
is an arbitrary dephasing between the two pulses, and the normalization ensures both have the same energy
\innerproduct{\psi_{\ell_A} }{\psi_{\ell_A} }=\innerproduct{\psi_{\ell_B} }{\psi_{\ell_B} }
. For
\ell_{A}\neq\ell_{B}
they are orthogonal,
\innerproduct{\psi_{\ell_A} }{\psi_{\ell_B} }=0
and individually their intrinsic OAM per photon read

\displaystyle L_{i,\text{EC}}^{\text{ph}}|_{y}(\psi_{\ell})=\ell/2,\;\;\;\;\;L_{i,\text{PC}}^{\text{ph}}|_{y}(\psi_{\ell})=\ell.

If individual STOV photons all carried the same quantum of intrinsic OAM, a photon in the superposition should have the same probability of being detected with either of these two values, hence the average intrinsic OAM per photon of the superposition should be the average of that of each STOV taken separately. Yet, it can be shown that when
\absolutevalue{\ell_A - \ell_B}=1
, this law does not hold. This is illustrated in
Figure 3
for
(\ell_{A},\ell_{B})=(1,2)
. In
Figure 3
(a), we plot the space-time envelope of the superposition for the particular case
\Phi=\pi/4
. Although by symmetry each constituent STOV has its apparent center located at the origin
\expectationvalue{\vb{R}}=\mathbf{0}
, the same is not true for their superposition: this center gets shifted towards the region where they interfere constructively, which depends on their relative phase. In parallel, the superposition also has an average phase gradient,
\expectationvalue{-i \grad_\text{st}}\neq 0
. As a result, an extrinsic contribution to OAM is generated which must be subtracted to isolate the intrinsic part: terms of the form
\expectationvalue{\vb{R}}\crossproduct\expectationvalue{-i \grad}
in (
21
) and (
22
) contribute. In panels (b) and (c), we plot the corresponding intrinsic OAM per photon (full lines) as a function of
\Phi
, in the energy-centroid and photon-centroid frameworks, respectively. We immediately observe that their values match neither the naive
(\ell_{A}+\ell_{B})/4=0.75
nor
(\ell_{A}+\ell_{B})/2=1.5
in the respective cases. Each time this coincides with a non-vanishing term
\expectationvalue{\vb{R}}\crossproduct\expectationvalue{-i \grad}
(dotted lines). The effect is particularly striking in the energy-centroid picture, as keeping only the transverse phase gradient renders this term sensitive to the dephasing between the two STOVs.

Most importantly, the same reasoning applies to regular,
spatial
optical vortices (which could be obtained by replacing
-ct
with
y
in expressions above): there also,
intrinsic
longitudinal OAM does not behave additively under a superposition. However, in this case the
total
longitudinal OAM does: from Eq. (
17
), we recall that it is generated by the operator
x(-i\partial_{y})-y(-i\partial_{x})
, for which vortex states are eigenmodes ; it then becomes trivial to show that the OAM per photon of the superposition is always the average of that for the two constituent vortex modes.

Our formalism hence highlights fundamental differences between transverse and longitudinal OAM of propagating pulses, and how they relate to optical vortices.
The total longitudinal OAM can be said to be carried in integer quanta by photons, as the field can be quantized on a basis of orthogonal eigenmodes of the corresponding operator with integer eigenvalues. These modes are spatial vortices, and for a given pure mode the OAM is naturally intrinsic, as
\mathbf{L}\propto\mathbf{P}
.
Meanwhile, as apparent in Eq. (
17
) the total transverse OAM of a pulse does not involve the analogous operator, and is also always origin-dependent – the same observations were
made in theories of the t-OAM per unit length of monochromatic beam-like states of light
Aiello et al. 2009
;
Aiello et al. 2010
. The various intrinsic constructions (
21
), (
22
) that could be envisioned do not take the form of expectation values of a single operator for which vortex states would be the eigenmodes. As mentioned in
Bliokh 2025
, such quantities do not appear to be observables measured by any physical process, and may be better considered as tools to gain intuitive insight into the OAM of optical fields. The “quantization” of STOV t-OAM mentioned in earlier studies
Hancock et al. 2021a
stems merely from the fact that a basis of vortex states is itself indexed by a discrete parameter, the topological charge
\ell
– it appears meaningless from the standpoint of quantum optics and properties of single photons.

Interestingly, the STOVs
\psi_{\ell}
would be eigenmodes of
[\mathbf{R}\crossproduct\expectationvalue{-i\grad_\text{st}}]|_{y}=-c\tau\partial_{x}-x\partial_{\tau}/c
, which is involved in the photon-centroid picture (
22
) and matches the “canonical” OAM operator originally used by Bliokh
et al.
in early studies of STOVs
Bliokh and Nori 2012a
;
Bliokh 2021
. However, we emphasize that in the general case where
\expectationvalue{\vb{R}}\crossproduct\expectationvalue{-i\grad_\text{st}}\neq\mathbf{0}
, applying this operator alone to a pulse envelope neither generates its intrinsic nor its total t-OAM.

IV
Conclusions

We have developed a rigorous theory of the energy and photon centroids of light pulses and the corresponding intrinsic orbital angular momenta, providing formulae conveniently applicable to an arbitrary pulse envelope.
We have illustrated them on fields with simple structures to best showcase the principles dictating the trajectories of both centroids, but they could readily be applied to other pulses of recent interest, such as STOVs of higher radial orders
Liu et al. 2024
, with arbitrary orientation of their singular lines
Wang et al. 2021
;
Zang et al. 2022
;
Porras and Jolly 2023
, wavepackets threaded by several singularities
Wan et al. 2022a

or optical hopfions
Wan et al. 2022b
, as well as experimentally measured pulses. Though we have focused here on near-paraxial fields, we note that exact, non-paraxial expressions for STOV pulses have recently been reported
Vo et al. 2024
, which may serve as a basis for post-paraxial investigations of t-OAM. We stress that the present theory is concerned with
propagating
pulses, and does not describe the OAM that may come with
static
transverse phase vortices, of the sort that can appear in simple superpositions of two monochromatic laser beams
Pas’ko et al. 2001
;
Zhong et al. 2024
and of which the t-OAM has recently been detected mechanically
Hu et al. 2023
– these structures are distinctly different from STOV pulses.

We hope our formalism can help revealing fundamental differences between the total and intrinsic
OAM of light pulses. Despite widespread use of the notion of intrinsic OAM
per photon
, we observe that this quantity – regardless of the centroid chosen to define it – could not be carried in well-defined quanta by photons in optical vortices, be they spatial or spatiotemporal. Indeed, intrinsic OAM is not an observable associated to an operator for which optical vortices would be the eigenmodes. It should be noted that this conclusion is not a consequence of prescribing our pulse envelopes in space-time as
\psi(x,y,t)
: it could also be reached using a spatial formulation
\psi(x,y,z)
as in
Porras 2024c
. The situation contrasts sharply with the quantization of the total longitudinal OAM in spatial vortex beams, on which stands some of their celebrated applications
Erhard et al. 2017
. This distinction may prove crucial for the proper interpretation of future experiments exploring the behaviour of STOVs in quantum optics, in the driving of electronic transitions
Babiker et al. 2002
;
Schmiegelow et al. 2016
or in nonlinear optical processes, for which the upconversion of angular momenta quanta alongside photons frequently offers an invaluable framework
Roger et al. 2013
;
Zhdanova et al. 2015
;
Kong et al. 2017
;
Luttmann et al. 2023
.

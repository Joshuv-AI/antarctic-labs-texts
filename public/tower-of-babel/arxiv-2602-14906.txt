Probing Quark Electric Dipole Moment with Topological Anomalies
Chao-Qiang Geng, Xiang-Nan Jin, Chia-Wei Liu, Bin Wu

Abstract

A nonzero T-odd asymmetry A T A_{T} requires anomalous couplings descending from a five-dimensional Chern–Simons construction. Modeling the running of F A F_{A} and F V F_{V} with vector-meson dominance, we estimate sensitivities to d s d_{s} at the level of 𝒪 ⁡ ( 10 − 16 ) ​ e ⋅ cm \mathcal{O}(10^{-16})\,e\cdot\mathrm{cm} in e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} data at CMD–3 and 𝒪 ⁡ ( 10 − 18 ) ​ e ⋅ cm \mathcal{O}(10^{-18})\,e\cdot\mathrm{cm} using existing J / ψ J/\psi samples at BESIII. Future experiments at the Super Tau–Charm Facility and Belle II can further improve the reach by an order of magnitude.

II
CP-odd observables

Consider
\gamma^{*}(q)\to K^{+}(p_{+})\,K^{-}(p_{-})\,\pi^{0}(p_{0})
.
We employ the symmetric set of kinetic variables

s=q^{2},
and
s_{\pm}=(p_{\pm}+p_{0})^{2}.

The total amplitude is parameterized as

{\cal M}(\gamma^{*}\to K^{+}K^{-}\pi^{0})=\epsilon_{\mu}(q)\,J^{\mu},

(2)

where
\epsilon
is the polarization vector of the virtual photon.
Using a gauge-invariant decomposition, the hadronic current can be parameterized as

J^{\mu}=F_{V}\Big(s,s_{+},s_{-}\Big)V^{\mu}\!+\!F_{A}\!\Big(s,s_{+},s_{-}\Big)A^{\mu},

(3)

with the two transverse basis vectors

\displaystyle V^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q\!\cdot\!p_{0})\,(p_{+}-p_{-})^{\mu}-\big(q\!\cdot\!(p_{+}-p_{-})\big)\,p_{0}^{\mu},

\displaystyle A^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\,p_{+\nu}\,p_{-\alpha}\,p_{0\beta},

(4)

which satisfy
q_{\mu}V^{\mu}=q_{\mu}A^{\mu}=0
.
Invariance under C-parity demands
F_{V}
and
F_{A}
to be invariant under exchanging
s_{+}
and
s_{-}
; see the figures in Ref.
BESIII:2019apb
. Since the final state has negative intrinsic parity, one sees that
F_{V}
and
F_{A}
are CP-even and CP-odd, respectively.

In the
\gamma^{*}
centre-of-mass frame,
q^{\mu}=(\sqrt{s},\vec{0})
and

p_{i}^{\mu}=(E_{i},\vec{p}_{i})
.
The amplitude can be written as

{\cal M}=\vec{\epsilon}\cdot\Big(F_{V}\vec{V}+F_{A}\vec{A}\Big),

(5)

with

\vec{V}=\sqrt{s}\,\big[E_{0}\,(\vec{p}_{+}-\vec{p}_{-})+(E_{+}-E_{-})\,\big(\vec{p}_{+}+\vec{p}_{-}\big)\big]

and

\vec{A}=\sqrt{s}\,(\vec{p}_{+}\times\vec{p}_{-})
.
Taking the unpolarized
e^{+}e^{-}\to\gamma^{*}
case, we sum over the two transverse photon polarizations and
obtain

\overline{|{\cal M}|^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\lambda=\pm}|{\cal M}|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\Big|\big(F_{V}\,\vec{V}+F_{A}\,\vec{A}\big)_{\perp}\Big|^{2},

(6)

where
\vec{X}_{\perp}\equiv\vec{X}-(\vec{X}\cdot\hat{z})\hat{z}
denotes the component transverse
to the beam axis.
The interference term proportional to

\Re(F_{V}F_{A}^{*})\,\vec{V}_{\perp}\cdot\vec{A}_{\perp}

is
C
-even and CP-odd, and will be shown to be induced by
d_{s}
.

The fully differential decay distribution for
\gamma^{*}(q)\to K^{+}(p_{+})\,K^{-}(p_{-})\,\pi^{0}(p_{0})
in the
\gamma^{*}
rest frame is

\frac{d\Gamma}{d\Phi}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{4}}\,\frac{|\vec{p}_{0}|\,|\vec{p}_{+}^{\,*}|}{16\,s}\;\overline{|{\cal M}|^{2}},

(7)

where

d\Phi\equiv dm_{KK}\,d\cos\theta_{0}\,d\Omega_{+}^{*}.

Here,

\theta_{0}
is the inclined angle of the three-momentum of
\pi^{0}

and
\gamma^{*}
polarization
in the
\gamma^{\ast}
rest frame, while

\Omega_{+}^{\ast}=(\theta^{\ast},\phi)
and
\vec{p}_{+}^{\,\ast}
are those of
K^{+}
in the
K^{+}K^{-}
rest frame; see FIG.
2
.

7
).
The angle

e^{+}
and
\pi^{0}
in the center-of-mass frame.
The angle

.

To isolate the contribution linear in
F_{A}
without assuming a specific kinematic
dependence, we define the signed interference observable

\vec{V}_{\perp}\cdot\vec{A}_{\perp}

and construct the counting asymmetry

A_{T}\equiv\frac{N\big(\vec{V}_{\perp}\!\cdot\!\vec{A}_{\perp}>0\big)-N\big(\vec{V}_{\perp}\!\cdot\!\vec{A}_{\perp}<0\big)}{N\big(\vec{V}_{\perp}\!\cdot\!\vec{A}_{\perp}>0\big)+N\big(\vec{V}_{\perp}\!\cdot\!\vec{A}_{\perp}<0\big)}\,.

(8)

Only
the interference term

\Re(F_{V}F_{A}^{*})\,(\vec{V}_{\perp}\cdot\vec{A}_{\perp})
contributes, providing a robust probe of
\Re(F_{V}F_{A}^{*})
.

Finally, we note that
the framework here can be straightforwardly extended to the one with
J/\psi
as the initial state, as its
J^{PC}
is identical to
\gamma^{*}
.

III
Mapping
d_{s}
to
\chi
PT

Starting from the flavor-conserving CP-violating strange-quark dipole operator,

\mathcal{L}_{\text{eff}}=\frac{d_{s}}{2}\,\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}\,\bar{s}\,\sigma_{\mu\nu}\,s,

(9)

where
d_{s}
parametrizes the strange-quark electric dipole operator induced by new physics.
The QCD Lagrangian enjoys a global
SU(3)_{L}\otimes SU(3)_{R}
flavor symmetry, with

q_{L}\to g_{L}\,q_{L}
,
q_{R}\to g_{R}\,q_{R}
and
q=(u,d,s)^{T}
.
To make chiral symmetry manifest in the presence of
d_{s}
, we introduce spurion fields

t_{\pm}
that transform as

t_{+}\to g_{L}\,t_{+}\,g_{R}^{\dagger},\qquad t_{-}\to g_{R}\,t_{-}\,g_{L}^{\dagger}.

(10)

With these spurions, the effective Lagrangian can be rewritten as

\mathcal{L}_{\text{eff}}=\frac{d_{s}}{2}\,\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}\,\Big(\bar{q}_{L}\,\sigma_{\mu\nu}\,t_{+}\,q_{R}+\bar{q}_{R}\,\sigma_{\mu\nu}\,t_{-}\,q_{L}\Big).

(11)

Matching to Eq. (
9
), one sets

t_{\pm}=\mathrm{diag}(0,0,1)

in flavor space. With the spurion transformation rules above,

\mathcal{L}_{\text{eff}}
is formally invariant under
SU(3)_{L}\otimes SU(3)_{R}
.

To match onto chiral fields, we note that
U=\exp(2iP/f_{P})
with

\displaystyle P\!=\!\!\!\ \left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{\eta_{0}}{\sqrt{3}}+\frac{\eta_{8}}{\sqrt{6}}+\frac{\pi^{0}}{\sqrt{2}}&\pi^{+}&K^{+}\\
\pi^{-}&\frac{\eta_{0}}{\sqrt{3}}+\frac{\eta_{8}}{\sqrt{6}}-\frac{\pi^{0}}{\sqrt{2}}&K^{0}\\
K^{-}&\bar{K}^{0}&\frac{\eta_{0}}{\sqrt{3}}-\frac{2\eta_{8}}{\sqrt{6}}\end{array}\right]\!,

which transforms as

U\to g_{L}\,U\,g_{R}^{\dagger}.

The lowest-order chiral realization of the strange-quark tensor currents is

\displaystyle{\cal L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d_{s}}{2}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}\frac{\Lambda_{2}}{8}T_{\mu\nu}(t_{+},t_{-})

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}(t_{+},t_{-})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\mathrm{Tr}\!\Big[(t_{+}U^{\dagger}+Ut_{-})\,[\alpha_{\mu},\alpha_{\nu}]\Big]

(16)

\displaystyle\,-\,\epsilon_{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}\,\mathrm{Tr}\!\Big[(t_{+}U^{\dagger}-Ut_{-})\,\alpha^{\lambda}\alpha^{\sigma}\Big]\,,

where

\alpha_{\mu}=-i\,\partial_{\mu}U\,U^{\dagger}

and
\Lambda_{2}
is a low-energy constant.
The tensor

T_{\mu\nu}(t_{+},t_{-})
is constructed to satisfy

\tfrac{1}{2}\,\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}T_{\alpha\beta}(t_{+},t_{-})=-\,i\,T^{\mu\nu}(t_{+},-t_{-})
,
mirroring
\tfrac{1}{2}\,\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\sigma_{\alpha\beta}=-\,i\,\sigma^{\mu\nu}\gamma_{5}
.
The relevant piece reads

\displaystyle{\cal L}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\frac{i2\sqrt{2}\,d_{s}\,\Lambda_{2}}{f_{K}^{2}f_{\pi}}F^{\mu\nu}\Big[K^{+}\,(\partial_{\mu}K^{-})\,(\partial_{\nu}\pi^{0})\Big],

(17)

where

F^{\mu\nu}=\big(\partial^{\mu}A^{\nu}-\partial^{\nu}A^{\mu}\big).

We focus on this channel because
K^{\pm}
is on shell and
\pi^{0}
can be reconstructed from two photons in relevant experiments.

In the SM, the leading contribution is fixed by the
WZW term. Since this term is the
descendant of a five-dimensional Chern–Simons form, it is naturally written in
the language of differential forms
Wess:1971yu
;
Witten:1983tw

{\cal L}_{\chi{\rm PT}}^{\rm(WZW)}=\frac{\,N_{c}}{48\pi^{2}}\,{\rm Tr}\!\left[eQ\,A\wedge\alpha^{3}\right].

(18)

Here
A
stands for the photon field.
The overall normalization is fixed by the
underlying chiral anomaly. Equivalently, it is topologically quantized by the
five-dimensional Chern–Simons construction, so the coefficient is an integer, which is
N_{c}
in QCD.
Expanding to
{\cal O}(f_{P}^{-3})
yields the familiar local

\gamma^{\ast}\to K^{+}K^{-}\pi^{0}
vertex

{\cal L}_{\chi{\rm PT}}^{\gamma KK\pi^{0}}=-\frac{N_{c}\,e}{12\pi^{2}\,f_{K}^{2}f_{\pi}}\,A\wedge d\pi^{0}\wedge dK^{+}\wedge dK^{-}.

(19)

The WZW-induced amplitude
therefore provides the irreducible odd-parity baseline for

\gamma^{\ast}\to K^{+}K^{-}\pi^{0}
.
The interference between the WZW term and the EDM naturally occurs because both induce odd-intrinsic-parity processes. In contrast, ordinary
\chi
PT conserves intrinsic parity and does not contribute.

In the chiral limit,
\chi
PT yields

\!\!\!\!F_{V}(0,0,0)=\frac{-N_{c}\,e}{12\pi^{2}\,f_{K}^{2}f_{\pi}},~~F_{A}(0,0,0)=\frac{\sqrt{2}\,d_{s}\,\Lambda_{2}}{f_{K}^{2}f_{\pi}}\,.

(20)

At
\sqrt{s}>2m_{K}+m_{\pi}
, resonances such as
K^{*+}\to K\pi^{0}
induce
structures in

F_{V}
and
F_{A}
.
In principle, the form factors
F_{V}
and
F_{A}
can be determined directly from the measured kinematic distributions; a dedicated Monte-Carlo study of this extraction will be reported elsewhere. To leading order,

and

can be modeled by the sequential decays of

\displaystyle\!\!\!F_{V,A}\Big(s,s_{+},s_{-}\Big)=F_{V,A}(s,0,0)

(21)

\displaystyle\!\!\!\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{m_{K^{*}}^{2}}{m_{K^{*}}^{2}-s_{+}-i\Gamma_{K^{*}}\sqrt{s_{+}}}+\frac{m_{K^{*}}^{2}}{m_{K^{*}}^{2}-s_{-}-i\Gamma_{K^{*}}\sqrt{s_{-}}}\right),

where

is a nonperturbative input to be fitted from the experiments.
The
determination of the ratio

V
Conclusion

We evaluate the amplitude of
\gamma^{\ast}\to K^{+}K^{-}\pi^{0}
using a gauge-invariant basis of transverse vectors, introducing CP-even and CP-odd form factors
F_{V}
and
F_{A}
. Assuming C-parity invariance, we express the amplitude in the
\gamma^{\ast}
rest frame and identify the T-odd interference observable
\vec{V}_{\perp}\cdot\vec{A}_{\perp}
. Within
\chi
PT, the CP-even form factor arises from the WZW term, while a strange-quark electric dipole moment contributes via axial vector currents.
We match the underlying operators onto chiral fields and obtain
F_{V}
and
F_{A}
in the chiral limit.
The interference between the topological anomaly and
d_{s}
is a foreseeable result, since both break intrinsic parity.

We define a signed interference asymmetry
A_{T}
to isolate
d_{s}
, and calculate the statistical sensitivity
\delta d_{s}
. Numerical estimates are performed for
e^{+}e^{-}\to K^{+}K^{-}\pi^{0}
at CMD-3, showing sensitivities at the
10^{-16}\,e\cdot\mathrm{cm}
level with current data and better with higher luminosity. We extend the analysis to
J/\psi\to K^{+}K^{-}\pi^{0}
and note that existing BESIII samples could reach the order of
\delta d_{s}\sim 10^{-18}\,e\cdot\mathrm{cm}
. The asymmetry construction cancels overall normalization and many detector effects, providing a robust strategy to probe CP-violating strange-quark dipole moments.

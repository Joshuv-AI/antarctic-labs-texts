q q -Series Invariants of Three-Manifolds and Knots-Quivers Correspondence
Sachin Chauhan

Abstract
The Gukov-Pei-Putrov-Vafa (GPPV) conjecture is a relationship between two three-manifold invariants: the Witten-Reshetikhin-Turaev (WRT) invariant and the Z ^ \widehat{Z} (“Z-hat”) invariant. In fact, WRT invariant is defined at roots of unity, 𝕢 ⁡ ( exp ⁡ ( 2 ​ π ​ i k + 2 ) , k ∈ ℤ + , for ​ S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) ) {\mathbbm{q}}\left(\exp\left(\frac{2\pi i}{k+2}\right),~k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},~\text{for}~SU(2)\right) , and is generally a complex number, whereas Z ^ \widehat{Z} -invariant is a q q -series with integer coefficients such that | q | < 1 |q|<1 . Therefore, Z ^ \widehat{Z} -invariant can be obtained from WRT-invariant by performing a particular analytic continuation, 𝕢 → q {\mathbbm{q}}\rightarrow q . In this thesis, we first examine this conjecture for S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) SO(3) and the ortho-symplectic supergroup O ​ S ​ p ​ ( 1 | 2 ) OSp(1|2) . This is done by setting up the WRT invariant for the respective groups and then performing the particular analytic continuation to extract Z ^ \widehat{Z} . As a result of this exercise, we found that Z ^ S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) = Z ^ S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) \widehat{Z}^{SU(2)}=\widehat{Z}^{SO(3)} and identified a relation between Z ^ S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) \widehat{Z}^{SU(2)} and Z ^ O ​ S ​ p ​ ( 1 | 2 ) \widehat{Z}^{OSp(1|2)} . Motivated by the equality of Z ^ \widehat{Z} for S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) and S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) SO(3) groups, we study this conjecture for S ​ U ​ ( N ) / ℤ m SU(N)/\mathbb{Z}_{m} groups, where ℤ m \mathbb{Z}_{m} is a subgroup of ℤ N \mathbb{Z}_{N} , in our second paper. We subsequently found that Z ^ S ​ U ​ ( N ) / ℤ m = Z ^ S ​ U ​ ( N ) \widehat{Z}^{SU(N)/\mathbb{Z}_{m}}=\widehat{Z}^{SU(N)} . Another theme of the thesis is to study a conjecture between knot theory and quiver representation theory. More precisely, this conjecture relates the generating function of the symmetric r r -colored HOMFLY-PT polynomial with the motivic generating series associated with a symmetric quiver. In particular, we obtain a quiver representation for a family of knots called double twist knots K ⁡ ( p , − m ) K(p,-m) . Primarily, we exploit the reverse engineering of Melvin-Morton-Rozansky (MMR) formalism to deduce the pattern of the matrix for these quivers.

Chapter 1   Introduction

Over the past few decades, the interplay between mathematics and string theory has proven to be highly fruitful, leading to significant advancements in both fields. String dualities have facilitated the prediction of new conjectures across diverse areas of mathematics, while mathematical conjectures have, in turn, informed the discovery of novel dualities in string theory
[
1
,
2
]
.

One area that has particularly benefited from this interdisciplinary approach is low-dimensional topology, which has seen remarkable progress driven by insights from quantum field theory. This collaboration has introduced a wealth of new topological invariants for knots and 3-manifolds, collectively referred to as quantum invariants. These developments have given rise to the field known as quantum topology.

1.1
Knot Theory

Knot theory, a key component of this field, focuses on the study and classification of knots as mathematical objects. A knot is defined as the image of a smooth (or piecewise smooth) embedding
S^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Further, topologically equivalent knots
K
and
K^{\prime}
can be converted into each other via Reidemeister moves RI, RII, and RIII
[
3
]
. Figure
1.1

Figure 1.1:
Diagrams of knots shown via their projection onto a plane.

A collection of knots that do not intersect but may be linked or knotted together is called a link. A link consisting of a single component is simply a knot. The framing of a knot
K
is defined as a continuous choice of a vector field normal to the knot
K
. This framing is often visualized by specifying a ribbon-like band around the knot, representing the normal vector field. A framed link

[\mathcal{L},\mathbf{f}]

\mathbf{f}=(f_{1},f_{2},\ldots)
for each of its components.

Among the significant classes of knot invariants are polynomial knot invariants, such as the Alexander polynomial

[

[

[
9

1.1.1
Knot homologies

We will first review the developments on homological invariants of knots accounting for these integers

(

\mathcal{H}^{K}

Homological Invariants of Knots:

The pioneering work of Khovanov
[
11
]
on bi-graded homology theory led to categorification of the Jones polynomial. This was extended to colored

J_{r}^{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(K;{\mathbbm{q}},t)=\sum_{i,j}t^{j}{\mathbbm{q}}^{i}{\rm dim}\mathcal{H}^{K;\mathfrak{sl}_{2};r}_{i,j}~.

(1.9)

1.2
Three-Manifold

A three-manifold is a mathematical space that locally resembles three-dimensional Euclidean space

Closed 3-manifolds can be described using framed links in

. Specifically, there exists a procedure to construct a 3-manifold

[\mathcal{L},\mathbf{f}]
inside

Theorem 1.1
(Lickorish-Wallace
[
39
,
40
]

Any closed, connected, orientable 3-manifold can be obtained from

Knot theory has attracted attention from both mathematicians and physicists during the last 40 years. The seminal work of Witten

Chapter 3   Gukov-Pei-Putrov-Vafa Conjecture for
SU(N)/\mathbb{Z}_{m}

In chapter
2
, the
q
-series valued invariant called
\widehat{Z}
for

Therefore, it raises the question of whether the equality

), there exists gauge groups between

and

of

-invariant for

-invariant depends on

.

While the physics perspective suggests that

But in certain cases, compactified theories do have Lie group dependence instead of Lie algebra
[

We will briefly highlight the steps involved in the reverse engineering formalism of MMR expansion

:

i.

We rewrite the Alexander polynomial in new variable

\Delta(x)=1-\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}\frac{(1-x)^{2i}}{x^{i}}\equiv 1-g(X)

are integers, and

is a positive integer.

ii.

Now, we use the following inverse binomial theorem

\frac{1}{(1-u)^{n}}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+m-1}{m}u^{m}

to write the first term of MMR expansion (
) as follows:

\displaystyle{}\frac{1}{\Delta(x)^{N-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\binom{N+m-2}{m}g(X)^{m}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}X^{k}

(4.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k!c_{k})\frac{X^{k}}{k!}

iii.

We make the following quantum deformation to get the quantum-deformed polynomial:

\frac{X^{k}}{k!}=\frac{(1-x)^{2k}}{k!x^{k}}\rightsquigarrow\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{r}{k}_{\mathbbm{q}}{\mathbbm{q}}^{-rk}(-a{\mathbbm{q}}^{r}t^{3};{\mathbbm{q}})_{k}.

{\mathbbm{q}}
-Pochhammer, while square brackets correspond to the
{\mathbbm{q}}
-binomials, which are defined as:

\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{r}{k}_{\mathbbm{q}}=\frac{({\mathbbm{q}}^{2};{\mathbbm{q}}^{2})_{r}}{({\mathbbm{q}}^{2};{\mathbbm{q}}^{2})_{k}({\mathbbm{q}}^{2};{\mathbbm{q}}^{2})_{r-k}}~~~;~~~(x;{\mathbbm{q}})_{k}=\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(1-x{\mathbbm{q}}^{i}).

4.2

Knot-Quiver Correspondence of double twist knots

We observe that the quiver matrix has a block structure by performing a similar analysis of the previous section for other examples of the double twist knots

Proposition:

The
r
-colored Jones polynomial for double twist knots

\displaystyle{}J_{r}(K(p,-m);{\mathbbm{q}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{d_{1}+d_{2}+\ldots+d_{4pm+1}=r}(-1)^{\Lambda_{(p,-m)}}{\mathbbm{q}}^{\Xi_{(p,-m)}}

, phase factor

.

for some examples lead to the following conjecture:

The generic structure of the quiver matrix will take the form

\hline\cr\tilde{F}_{k}^{\top}&\tilde{R}_{1}^{\top}&\cdots&R_{i}^{\top}&\cdots&&\tilde{U}_{i}&\cdots&T_{i}&\tilde{T}_{i}\\
\hline\cr\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\

Chapter 5   Conclusions and Future Outlooks

5.1

Conclusions

We have focussed on WRT invariant for negative-definite plumbed 3-manifold
M(\Gamma)
for
SO(3)
and
OSp(1|2)
group, involving framed link invariants. One of the reasons is that these invariants can be computed in
SU(2)
Chern-Simons theory. Particularly, the colored link invariants for the
SO(3)
and
OSp(1|2)
are obtainable by a change of polynomial variable of colored Jones invariants. The
q
-series form of the
\widehat{Z}
for these two groups could be systematically deduced.
We find a neat relation between
\widehat{Z}^{OSp(1|2)}_{b}[M(\Gamma),q]
and
\widehat{Z}^{SU(2)}_{b}[M(\Gamma),q]
(
2.87
,
2.86
)
, which was subsequently proved by Costantino et al. in ref.
[
66
]
.
Also, we show that
\widehat{Z}^{SO(3)}_{b}[M(\Gamma);q]=\widehat{Z}^{SU(2)}_{b}[M(\Gamma);q]
. Such a result forced us to investigate the
\widehat{Z}
for factor groups
SU(N)/\mathbb{Z}_{m}
and find its relation to
\widehat{Z}^{SU(N)}
.

Inspired by the result of our first work
[
59
]
, we investigated the
\widehat{Z}
-invariant for
SU(N)/\mathbb{Z}_{m}
group. For this, we followed the route of GPPV conjecture, which required us to first fix the WRT invariant for
SU(N)/\mathbb{Z}_{m}
group. After fixing it, we extracted the
\widehat{Z}
-invariant for
SU(N)/\mathbb{Z}_{m}
group. Interestingly, we found
\widehat{Z}^{SU(N)/\mathbb{Z}_{m}}=\widehat{Z}^{SU(N)}
(
3.37
), showing
\widehat{Z}
is Lie algebra dependent.

In the final piece of work, we concentrated on rewriting the colored Jones as a motivic series and extracting the quiver data for the double twist knot family,
K(p,-m)
. We found a pattern in quiver matrices for
K(p,-2)
knots, where
p\geq 2
(
4.2.2
). Further, we found the quiver matrix for
K(3,-3)
(Appendix
D
).

5.2

Future Outlooks

Some of the challenging problems we hope to pursue in future are:

(a)

The brane setup in string theory for

gauge group gives a natural interpretation for these
q
-series as partition function of the theory

involving orientifolds. It will be worth investigating such a construction to obtain

for
SO(N)
group and compare with our

(b)

We have conjectured the relation between WRT invariant for

\widehat{Z}^{\mathfrak{su}(N)}
. It would be interesting to study the
w
-refined version of
SU(N)

WRT invariant, a

-refined WRT invariant was introduced in Ref.

.

(c)

Explore the explicit form of
\widehat{Z}
-invariant for other Lie groups (type

) and their Langlands dual.

(d)

The work on GPPV conjecture in this thesis was restricted to negative-definite plumbed 3-manifolds. It would be nice to study the GPPV conjecture and
\widehat{Z}
-invariant for more general 3-manifolds.

(e)

It would be interesting to check the superconformal index for

suggests that the superconformal index is insensitive to global aspects provided

(f)

It will be worth investigating whether our conjectured form for the block structure in

There is a pretzel family of knots whose

Quantum resources in resource management systems
Iskandar Sitdikov, M. Emre Sahin, Utz Bacher, Aleksander Wennersteen, Andrew Damin, Mark Birmingham, Philippa Rubin, Stefano Mensa, Matthieu Moreau, Aurelien Nober, Hitomi Takahashi, Munetaka Ohtani

Abstract
Quantum computers are beginning to operate in high-performance computing (HPC) environments. Quantum can complement classical resources for specific workloads, but their adoption depends on integration into existing HPC infrastructure. Treating quantum devices as first-class resources allows for unified scheduling, improved usability, and support for hybrid quantum-classical applications. This paper presents the design architecture and reference implementation for quantum resources control using existing workload management systems. We introduce a suite of plugins for Slurm that enable integration of on-prem and cloud quantum computing resources into existing high-performance computing centers. The paper details the interface design, plugin concept and implementation, operational aspects for heterogeneous compute clusters, as well as considerations for other resource management systems.



I
Introduction

The advent of quantum computing necessitates the integration of quantum resources into traditional HPC environments. Hybrid quantum–classical applications increasingly run on large HPC systems. Production sites prefer to manage compute resources via single workload management system and avoid problem of multiple scheduling systems. The gap between workload management compute resource abstractions and local or cloud-hosted quantum computers motivates a thin compatibility layer. Previous efforts relied on wrapper scripts, while our contribution is, to the best of our knowledge, the first to integrate at native to resource management systems, making quantum computers
first-class
scheduler entities.

For reference implementation we choose Slurm. Slurm, a widely adopted workload manager, offers the SPANK framework to extend its capabilities. This work introduces SPANK plugins

II
Background

II.1
Resource management systems

Resource management systems are multi-tenant systems responsible for resource allocation for given tasks based on predetermined policies.
Slurm is an open-source workload manager designed for Linux clusters of all sizes. SPANK is a plugin framework within Slurm that allows for the extension of its functionalities without modifying the core source code. SPANK plugins can intercept and modify job submission and execution behaviors, making it suitable for integrating quantum resource management features.

II.2
Quantum Resource Management Interface (QRMI)

The Quantum Resource Management Interface (QRMI
1
) is a conceptual framework that abstracts the complexities of interacting with various quantum computing platforms. It provides a standard interface for job submission, monitoring, and result retrieval, facilitating the integration of quantum resources into the existing HPC ecosystem.
QRMI is distributed as a Rust library with bindings to C for HPC ecosystem integration.

III
High Level Structure

At large, there are three personas involved in quantum-enabled workload management systems:

•

HPC users
, consuming resources in the compute cluster and using access to Quantum Computers through these resources.

•

HPC admins
, configuring the workload management system and managing access and mapping to available Quantum Computers.

•

Quantum Computer providers
, offering access to Quantum Computer.

Figure
2
shows how this concept maps to the Slurm workload manager.

This separation of responsibilities leads to architectural tenets:

•

A Slurm-level quantum resource directly maps to a physical quantum device.

•

Back-end selection is a configuration detail of the quantum resource, not of user-level code.

•

Parallelism abstractions (e.g., DA API lanes) are hidden behind the single quantum resource.

IV
Design and Implementation

It is important to consider how the needs of all stakeholders intersect when designing the SPANK-based quantum resource layer. Within this ecosystem, three constituencies must interact seamlessly. HPC users submit jobs that mix conventional CPU/GPU workloads with quantum tasks and expect the scheduler to hide complexity of connecting and communicating with system while retaining familiar Slurm commands. HPC administrators install and tune Slurm, register each quantum device as a GRES, and maintain the mapping between those scheduler objects and the physical quantum computers so that routing, accounting, and security remain consistent across the cluster. Quantum providers of cloud services, such as IBM Quantum, provide access to the actual processors through system APIs; their gateways must integrate with the plugin at run time to allocate, monitor, and release devices without disrupting the Slurm job life-cycle.

IV.2
Quantum resource

Custom resource definition for quantum system is QPU, which maps resource management system’s resource notion to physical quantum system.
Additional resource definition might be needed depending on implementation from hardware vendors. Some vendors expose to parallelism within quantum computer as execution lanes, threads, parts of devices (qubits, partitions), etc. Therefore we define quantum resource as an abstract that composed out of physical device and parallelism notion.
Our architecture and implementation allows this flexibility as our definition of quantum resource is described in QRMI and abstracts away implementation details for vendors.

All quantum resources are treated as a Slurm GRES named “QPU.” Interaction follows the pattern:

\text{Acquire Resource}\rightarrow\text{Execute Quantum Task}

\rightarrow\text{Release Resource}

This is implemented via SPANK hooks to lock the quantum computer for the job’s duration and inject credentials or middleware as required, avoiding the overhead of spawning separate Slurm jobs for primitive calls.

IV.3
SPANK plugin for quantum resources

The SPANK plugins are designed to operate in different contexts within Slurm: allocator, local, remote, and job_script. Each plugin implements specific callback functions corresponding to these contexts, enabling fine-grained control over job behavior.

The SPANK quantum plugin leverages Slurm’s event-driven callbacks:

•

Prologue:
Handle secrets, acquire quantum resource (QRMI call), set up network middleware.

•

Task Init:
Parse plugin options, configure environment variables for Qiskit primitives (e.g. devices identifiers).

•

Epilogue:
Tear down middleware, release quantum resource (QRMI call).

This sequence ensures quantum back-end selection is bound at job start and that Qiskit-level code uses the Slurm-defined resource identity instead of hard-coding back-end names.

IV.4
Quantum GRES plugin

Converting SPANK plugin to GRES plugin would be straightforward as majority of quantum resource control logic lives within QRMI library. GRES plugin, similar to SPANK plugin, will be using imported QRMI library to control resource.

V
Usage Scenarios

V.1
Hybrid Quantum-Classical Workflows

Users can submit jobs that involve both classical and quantum computations.
The SPANK plugins manage the allocation and execution of quantum tasks, ensuring synchronization with classical components. In most cases, an SDK like Qiskit
3
3

3

https://github.com/Qiskit/qiskit
is used to drive quantum jobs from the classical part of the job.
This classical job can be integrated into a higher level workflow as is common in HPC projects.

V.2
Quantum-Only Jobs

For jobs that solely involve quantum computations, the plugins handle the lifecycle of the quantum job, from resource allocation to result retrieval, providing a seamless user experience. These jobs are per se independent of classical processing tied to a slurm job. They can be called in the context of a workflow to ensure consistency in data flow.

VI
Quantum resources for cloud and container management systems

Cloud and container technologies are standard in commercial environments, not just for business applications, but also for compute-heavy purposes.
One example is the large area of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML) tasks, and increasingly AI- and compute-centric tasks become parts of business workflows.
A popular operating environment in this space is Kubernetes (including distributions of it such as the Red Hat OpenShift Container Platform). Kubernetes has become the de-facto standard for larger containerized solutions and provide good portability between on-premises and cloud environments.

There are approaches to combine Kubernetes with Slurm to retain a Slurm experience on top of Kubernetes, such as Slinky

In such cases, resources exposed to users are still managed through Slurm, and only the connection to physical quantum resources that is not visible to a Slurm user needs to be re-framed.

However, a compute-heavy workload increasingly also uses plain cloud tooling and operating environments like Kubernetes. Similarlyly to the general HPC approach described in this paper, integration of quantum resources into operating environments is the base for consistent manageability, overarchinobservability,ty, and integrated operations.
While this paper does not discuss the integration of quantum resources in Kubernetes in detail, there are similar considerations for native cloud tooling that we laid out for Slurm:

•

Quantum resource definition:
an administrator can create quantum resources made available in the operating environment. Integration in the resource schema of the operating environment, such as (custom) resource definitions, unifies configuration management. Moving the responsibility of quantum resource provisioning and access to the administrator scope allows for a consistent access and resource management, and adds to a stable governance posture of the hybrid environment. Applications can use these resources and create actual quantum jobs that are executed on quantum systems. This responsibility split ensures a very controlled use of quantum resources.

•

Scheduling of quantum tasks:
applications submit quantum jobs for execution based on cluster-provided quantum resources. This is an abstraction level that allows the operating environment to control scheduling and execution of such quantum jobs on physical resources, consistent with control over classical (compute, storage, and networking) resources. As a result, business objectives can be pursued from a cluster-global view, including the ordering and scheduling of quantum jobs.

•

Hybrid workflow tooling:
once resources and workloads are under control, the orchestration following overarching workflows become relevant. With clean integration of quantum resources and jobs, existing tooling for the operating environment can start to create hybrid workflows, without or with minimal extensions to consider the resources and artifacts introduced by quantum. Eventually, hybrid workflows are key to approach business objectives across the hybrid environment.

VII
Conclusion

The integration of SPANK plugins for quantum resource management within Slurm represents a significant step towards unifying classical and quantum computing workflows. The presented architecture bridges today’s HPC schedulers and emerging quantum accelerators with minimal disruption. By implementing a resource interface in user space and reusing Slurm’s plugin hooks, the solution offers a pragmatic path towards connecting quantum computing and supercomputing. By abstracting the complexities of quantum resource interactions, these plugins enable users to leverage quantum computing capabilities within familiar HPC environments.

A.1
Definitions

•

Quantum Processing Unit (QPU)

A QPU comprises all hardware responsible for accepting quantum instruction sets (or circuits) and returning measured results, including quantum chips, amplifiers, control electronics, and instrumentation .

•

Quantum Computer

A Quantum Computer consists of a QPU plus the classical compute infrastructure that accepts API requests and orchestrates job execution .

•

SPANK Plugins

SPANK (Slurm Plug-in Architecture for Node and job Kontrol) is a generic, stackable framework enabling runtime modification of Slurm’s job launch path without altering core Slurm code .

•

SPANK Quantum Plugin

A SPANK plugin tailored to manage quantum-related resources and job workflows in Slurm, advertising quantum resources, handling allocation, and interfacing with back-end quantum platforms .

•

Qiskit Primitives

High-level constructs—
Sampler
for bitstring sampling and
Estimator
for expectation-value computation—abstract low-level quantum hardware details, enabling users to focus on computational results rather than hardware orchestration .

Tumor metabolic heterogeneity based on 18F-FDG PET/CT is a predictor of outcome in diffuse large B-cell lymphoma
Authors: Wenchong Xin, Fei Wang, Luo Lu, Haoyu Zang, Bai He, Weiying Gu, Yuetao Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTumor metabolic heterogeneity (MH) assessed by 18-fluorine fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography computed tomography (18F-FDG PET/CT) is established as a marker of drug resistance in solid tumors. Its prognostic significance in diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) remains inadequately explored. This study aims to investigate the prognostic value of tumor MH assessed by 18F-FDG PET/CT in DLBCL.MethodsThis study retrospectively included 297 patients diagnosed with DLBCL at the Third Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University from August 2012 to December 2022. We evaluated tumor MH using the area under the curve of cumulative standardized uptake value-volume histogram (AUC-CSH). In addition to obtaining conventional PET metabolic metrics, including maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax), mean standardized uptake value (SUVmean), total metabolic tumor volume (TMTV), and total lesion glycolysis (TLG), we also collected baseline clinical data. This study developed a survival prediction model based on Cox regression analysis and compared its prognostic performance with the National Comprehensive Cancer Network-International prognostic index (NCCN-IPI). We evaluated the model’s calibration, discrimination, and clinical utility, and conducted an internal validation.ResultsMultivariable Cox regression analysis indicated age (hazard ratio [HR], HR,1.62, 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.13 ~ 2.34, P = 0.01; HR, 2.15, 95% CI, 1.35 ~ 3.42, P = 0.001), TMTV (HR, 3.24, 95% CI, 1.56 ~ 6.73, P = 0.002; HR, 3.35, 95% CI, 1.30 ~ 8.62, P = 0.012), and AUC-CSH (HR, 0.64, 95% CI, 0.45 ~ 0.92, P = 0.015; HR, 0.59, 95% CI, 0.38 ~ 0.92, P = 0.018) as independent predictive factors for both progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS). Lower AUC-CSH value corresponded to higher tumor MH, indicating poorer survival outcome. The combined model outperformed the NCCN-IPI, achieving C-index of 0.688, 0.630 for PFS and 0.721, 0.688 for OS. The calibration curve showed good consistency, and the decision curve analysis (DCA) also confirmed its potential application value in individualized treatment decisions.ConclusionsTumor MH, assessed by 18F-FDG PET/CT, demonstrates potential as a prognostic biomarker in DLBCL. Its incorporation into predictive models may improve individualized prognostic evaluation and facilitate more precise treatment stratification for these patients.Trial registrationThis research has been retrospectively registered in the Ethics Committee institutional of the Third Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University [approval number (2022) KD155].Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-025-15149-x.

Introduction

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is the most prevalent pathological subtype of aggressive non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma and accounts for the largest share of lymphoma-related deaths worldwide [1, 2].The standard first-line chemoimmunotherapy of rituximab combined with cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone (R-CHOP) has significantly improved patient prognosis, achieving long-term disease control in nearly 90% of patients with limited-stage disease and up to 60% of patients with advanced-stage disease [3]. However, patients who fail R-CHOP treatment typically have poor outcomes, with a median overall survival (OS) of only 6 months [4]. Furthermore, treatment options for patients with relapsed or refractory (R/R) disease are limited. While high-dose chemotherapy combined with autologous stem cell transplantation can provide the best curative opportunity for this population, their prognosis remains discouraging, with only a minority achieving remission through salvage therapy [5, 6]. Patients who did not experience R/R within two years after initial treatment have a survival rate similar to that of the general population. This underscores the critical importance of timely and accurate identification of R/R patients before treatment, as well as the optimization of treatment regimens [7]. Currently, widely used models based on clinical data, such as the International Prognostic Index (IPI) and the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN)-IPI, are insufficient in effectively identifying high-risk subsets with poor outcomes to chemoimmunotherapy.

18-fluorine fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography computed tomography (18F-FDG PET/CT) has become an increasingly irreplaceable tool for the assessment of disease burden, staging, and treatment response in DLBCL [8, 9]. The prognostic significance of quantitative PET metrics, including metabolic tumor volume (MTV) and total lesion glycolysis (TLG), in DLBCL has been substantiated by previous validation studies [10–12]. Indeed, relying solely on PET volumetric parameters such as MTV and TLG−which reflect tumor burden−may be insufficient for predicting prognosis in DLBCL. This is because these parameters fail to account for the critical feature of tumor heterogeneity, which has been demonstrated to be closely associated with tumor aggressiveness and metastatic potential, potentially contributing to disease progression and treatment failure [13]. Tumor heterogeneity refers to the diversity of molecular biological or genetic alterations that emerge during tumor progression. However, conventional invasive detection methods (e.g., genetic analysis, molecular pathological testing, cytological evaluation, etc.) can only provide localized information about the tumor, which to some extent limits our understanding of its overall complexity. El Naqa et al. [14] first proposed the area under the curve of cumulative standardized uptake value-volume histogram (AUC-CSH) method, a quantitative approach to non-invasively characterize tumor metabolic heterogeneity (MH) based on 18F-FDG PET/CT imaging. By analyzing the spatial distribution characteristics of radiopharmaceuticals, this method can effectively reflect glucose metabolic activity and other pathophysiological processes of tumor tissues. Multiple studies [15–19] have demonstrated its significance for predicting treatment response and prognosis in various solid malignancies. Notably, it also has exhibited preliminary but encouraging prognostic potential in lymphoma [20–22]. Currently, research on the specific prognostic impact of tumor MH in DLBCL remains limited. Tumor MH may provide a novel perspective on prognosis stratification for DLBCL patients. Therefore, further validation and exploration are still necessitated to determine the optimal method for predicting the prognosis of DLBCL patients utilizing tumor MH.

This study aims to (1) assess the prognostic significance of tumor MH, as determined by 18F-FDG PET/CT imaging, on progression-free survival (PFS) and OS in patients with DLBCL receiving R-CHOP or R-CHOP-like first-line treatment regimens; (2) construct a simple, economical, and stable comprehensive prediction model; and (3) evaluate the model’s calibration, discrimination, and clinical utility and employ the bootstrap method to conduct internal validation.

Materials and methods

Study population

A total of 297 individuals with DLBCL who underwent baseline 18F-FDG PET/CT scans at the Third Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University between August 2012 and December 2022 were retrospectively analyzed in this study. The Ethics Committee of the Third Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University granted ethical approval for this study [approval number (2022) KD155], and informed consent was waived since it’s retrospective. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) patients with a histopathological diagnosis of DLBCL; (2) availability of a baseline 18F-FDG PET/CT scan; (3) initial treatment with R-CHOP or an R-CHOP-like regimen. Exclusion criteria included: (1) concurrent primary central nervous system lymphoma (PCNSL) or other malignancies; (2) treatment with regimens other than R-CHOP or R-CHOP-like protocols; (3) absence of baseline 18F-FDG PET/CT examination; (4) surgical removal of the primary lesion; (5) incomplete clinical data or loss to follow-up. The research strictly adheres to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. We collected baseline clinical data for DLBCL by reviewing electronic medical records, which included the following: age, gender, the number of extranodal involvements (EN), B symptom, Ann Arbor stage, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS) score, β2 microglobulin (β2 MG), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) level, cell-of-origin (COO), and NCCN-IPI score.

18F-FDG PET/CT acquisition

Image acquisition was performed using a Biograph mCT (64) PET/CT scanner (Siemens Healthcare, Erlangen, Germany). According to the standard scanning protocol, each patient fasted for at least 6 h and ensured that their blood glucose concentration was <11 mmol/L. Patients were intravenously administered 18F-FDG based on their body weight at a dose of 3.70–5.70.55MBq/kg, with a radiochemical purity of > 95%. The imaging agent was provided by Nanjing Jiangyuan Andico Positron Research and Development Co. For the process of image acquisition, each patient was required to lie on the scanning bed in a supine position with both hands clasped behind their head. Following the enhance CT scans using Care dose four-dimensional (4D) technology, a PET scan was conducted using a three-dimensional acquisition mode. The acquisition range encompassed from the base of the skull to the upper end of the femur, with a 2-minute acquisition time per bed position and approximately 6–8 bed positions, and CT data was applied for attenuation correction. The acquired images from the PET and CT scans were registered and fused on the Syngo TrueD workstation. PET data were reconstructed using an iterative reconstruction algorithm (2 iterations and 21 subsets), which incorporates point spread function correction with time-of-flight information (TrueX + TOF, ultraHD-PET). Upon completion of this process, attenuation-corrected PET images, CT images, and fused PET/CT images became available for review in axial, coronal, and sagittal planes, as well as in maximum intensity projections and three-dimensional cine mode.

18F-FDG PET/CT image analysis

Two experienced nuclear medicine physicians independently analyzed the images using Lifex software (version 5.10, http://www.lifexsoft.org), without knowledge of the patient’s clinical outcomes or other information. For disputed results, the final diagnosis was determined by a consensus among the reviewing physicians. Based on a fixed threshold of maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax) 4.0, the system automatically generates the boundary contour of the target lesion and calculates its metabolic volume. Visual assessment was performed to manually remove physiological uptake in the brain, heart, bladder, and kidneys. Additionally, false-positive lesions resulting from inflammation or infection were also excluded. This process ensured that only true pathological lesions were retained for further analysis. Traditional semi-quantitative PET/CT parameters, including SUVmax, mean standardized uptake value (SUVmean), and total MTV (TMTV) were measured, and TLG was calculated by multiplying SUVmean by TMTV. The tumor MH of the hottest lesion in each patient was assessed using the AUC-CSH method, consistent with previous studies [20, 22, 23]. The CSH is obtained by plotting the volumetric percentage of the tumor that exceeds a certain SUVmax threshold, which ranges from 0% to 100%. AUC-CSH is a quantitative index of tracer uptake distribution heterogeneity in which lower values correspond to higher MH. All these indicators were independently measured by two physicians, and the final values were established by averaging their measurements.

Follow-up

Survival status, including disease progression, relapse, or death, and survival time were determined by reviewing electronic medical records or telephone calls, and the follow-up period concluded on August 31, 2024. Patients were followed up for a minimum of 12 months or until death. Since PFS and OS are considered reliable statistical endpoints in survival analysis, they were selected as the primary endpoints for this study. PFS was defined as the period from the baseline PET/CT scan to the first disease relapse or progression, death from any cause, or the end of follow-up. OS was defined as the time from the baseline PET/CT scan to death from any cause or the end of follow-up. Patients who did not experience an event by the end of follow-up were censored.

Development, evaluation, and validation of the nomogram

Based on the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve of PFS and OS, the optimal cutoff values for continuous variables including TMTV, TLG, SUVmax, SUVmean, and AUC-CSH were determined. The study also checked the multicollinearity for each variable using the variance inflation factor (VIF), and only variables with a VIF < 10 was included in the final model. Subsequently, Cox regression analysis was employed to identify independent prognostic factors associated with PFS and OS, upon which predictive models were constructed to evaluate patient survival outcomes. Variables with a P value < 0.1 in the univariate Cox regression analysis were incorporated into the multivariate Cox regression analysis. Based on the multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression model, we constructed a combined prediction model incorporating independent predictors. Calibration curves were used to graphically assess the calibration of the model, while the Harrell concordance index (C-index) and time-dependent area under the ROC curve (tdROC) were calculated to quantify its discrimination. The performance of the prediction model was subsequently compared with that of the conventional NCCN-IPI model. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was employed to assess the model’s clinical utility by calculating the net benefit at various threshold probabilities. The final model’s predictive performance was internally validated using the bootstrap resampling. The simplified analysis flowchart for the study is exhibited in Fig. 1.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using the R software package (version 4.4.2) and IBM SPSS (version 24.0). Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies (percentages), while continuous variables were represented by mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median (minimum, maximum). The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was employed to evaluate the normality of the variables. To analyze the consistency of measurements between two observers, a random sample of 145 cases was selected for repeated measurements, and the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) was used for analysis. Cox regression analysis was then applied to identify potential independent predictors and to establish a predictive model. Kaplan-Meier analysis was employed to estimate survival distributions in different risk groups, and the log-rank test was used to compare survival probability. A P value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient characteristics

Among the 297 patients who met the inclusion criteria, there were 141 (47.5%) males and 156 (52.5%) females. The patients’ ages ranged from 13 to 87 years, with an average age of 60.63 ± 13.92 years. The median follow-up time was 32 months, during which 130 (43.8%) patients experienced disease progression and 89 (30.0%) patients died. The 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year PFS and OS rates were 73.4%, 57.9%, 54.1%, and 87.2%, 72.7% and 69.6%, respectively. 25 (8.4%) patients with missing β2 MG values, which were replaced using the mean value imputation. The baseline clinical data and semi-quantitative PET/CT metabolic parameters of the study population are summarized in Table 1. The statistical results demonstrated good consistency for all measurements between the two observers, with ICC ranging from 0.840 to 0.999 (see Additional file for details).

Patients with a high degree of tumor MH experience poorer survival outcomes

The AUC-CSH values of this study ranged from 0.14 to 0.72, with a mean ± SD of 0.38 ± 0.10. Generally, a lower value of AUC-CSH indicates a higher level of tumor heterogeneity and poorer survival outcomes. Patients with a high degree of tumor MH (AUC-CSH ≤ 0.32) exhibited significantly lower 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year PFS rates (65.6% vs. 77.0%, 41.4% vs. 65.4%, and 34.3% vs. 62.9%, respectively; P < 0.0001) and OS rates (82.8% vs. 89.2%, 62.8% vs. 77.3%, and 56.4% vs. 75.6%, respectively; P = 0.00032) compared to those with low tumor MH (AUC-CSH > 0.32), as shown in Additional file. Furthermore, Pearson correlation analysis revealed a weak or very weak negative correlation between AUC-CSH and the MTV of the hottest lesion (r = −0.189, P = 0.001) or TMTV (r = −0.263, P < 0.0001). Suggesting that tumor MH is independent of MTV, which represents tumor burden. These results are detailed in the Additional file.

Univariate and multivariate cox regression analysis

Univariate Cox regression analysis indicated that age, Ann Arbor stage, B symptom, EN, ECOG PS, LDH, TMTV, TLG, SUVmean, and AUC-CSH are potential prognostic factors for PFS (all P < 0.05). However, no significant correlation was observed between PFS and gender, β2 MG, or COO (all P > 0.05). Similarly, OS significantly correlated with age, Ann Arbor stage, B symptom, EN, ECOG PS, LDH, β2MG, COO, TMTV, TLG, and AUC-CSH (all P < 0.05), while gender and SUVmean showed no significant correlation with OS (all P > 0.05), as presented in Table 3. Development and validation of the combined prediction model

Variables with independent prognostic significance in the multivariate Cox regression analysis were utilized to construct a combined prediction model. Given that ECOG PS and COO did not exhibit in dependent prognostic value for PFS, we chose age, TMTV, and AUC-CSH as indicators to construct a combined model for individualized prediction of PFS and OS at 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year (visualized as a nomogram, as shown in Fig. 2a and d). Each predictive variable was assigned a score based on a predefined scoring system. By summing the scores of these variables and mapping the total score to a survival probability scale, we were able to estimate the probabilities of 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year PFS and OS. The accuracy of the combined model in predicting 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year PFS and OS was evaluated by ROC curves, with AUC values of 0.748, 0.744, and 0.741 for PFS and 0.746, 0.775, and 0.751 for OS, respectively (Fig. 3). Discussion

This study confirmed the predictive significance of tumor MH, assessed by 18F-FDG PET/CT, for patients newly diagnosed with DLBCL. Survival analysis identified age, TMTV, and tumor MH as independent predictors of both PFS and OS. A combined model constructed based on these three factors stratified patients into three risk groups, among which the high-risk group exhibited 5-year PFS and OS rates of only 27.8% and 44.1%, respectively.

Our study systematically analyzed the independent predictive value of tumor MH in the prognosis of DLBCL and evaluated its clinical application potential. Patients exhibiting higher degree of tumor MH had a significantly worse prognosis (PFS: HR, 0.64, 95% CI, 0.45 ~ 0.92, P = 0.015 and OS: HR, 0.59, 95% CI, 0.38 ~ 0.92, P = 0.018). A prospective study demonstrated that both tumor MH (HR, 12.8; 95% CI: 3.3 ~ 49.9, P < 0.001) and TLG (HR, 46.5; 95% CI: 5.8 ~ 373.8, P < 0.001) were independent predictors of PFS. Based on baseline tumor MH and TLG, the investigators constructed a prognostic model for PFS, which confirmed that patients with both high TLG and high MH had significantly worse clinical outcomes (HR, 31.2; 95% CI, 9.9 ~ 97.8, P < 0.001), with a 5-year PFS rate of only 11% (95% CI, 1 ~ 39%). This study is the first to establish the clinical significance of tumor MH as a potential prognostic biomarker in primary mediastinal B-cell lymphoma (PMBCL) [20]. Subsequent studies conducted by Senjo H et al. and Ceriani Let al. similarly confirmed the adverse prognostic impact of higher degree of tumor MH in DLBCL, further establishing the status of tumor MH as a prognostic biomarker for DLBCL [21, 22]. Although tumor MH did not persist as a significant risk factor in multivariate analysis, it remained valuable for stratifying patients with high TMTV. This observation may be attributed to the more prominent prognostic significance of TMTV compared with MH. Our data and the findings of the previous studies collectively support the viewpoint that tumor MH holds promise as a biomarker for individualized prognostic prediction and treatment stratification in DLBCL patients.

Tumor heterogeneity may be one of the underlying mechanisms contributing to increased tumor aggressiveness and therapeutic resistance, thereby partly explaining inter-individual differences in treatment response [24, 25]. Reinfeld BI et al. [26] founded that 18F-FDG is taken up not only by tumor cells but also by immune infiltrating cells within tumor lesions. Consequently, 18F-FDG uptake heterogeneity can more comprehensively reflect the heterogeneity of both tumor cells and immune cells within tumor lesions. Analysis of intra-tumoral metabolic activities enables more precise characterization of tumors, providing profound imaging evidence for clinical treatment and aiding in our understanding of this complex phenomenon. Nevertheless, these results necessitate verification via pertinent mechanistic studies.

TMTV serves as a robust predictor of prognosis in DLBCL and other lymphoma subtypes, regardless of the measurement methodology employed., which align with our finding [10, 11, 27]. PET-derived TMTV offers a more thorough assessment of disease burden than anatomical imaging [28–31], as it incorporates both metabolic activity and anatomical features of the tumor. Compared to traditional prognostic models - which indirectly reflect tumor burden through Ann Arbor stage and LDH levels-MTV provides a more accurate representation of tumor burden and may even partially replace these parameters [31, 32]. This could potentially explain why, in our study, apart from age, other clinical indicators within the NCCN-IPI failed to demonstrate significant prognostic value in multivariate analysis. Conversely, Mikhaeel NG et al. [10]. found that the Ann Arbor stage retained independent prognostic value, providing additional prognostic information beyond MTV alone. This discrepancy may stem from the variations in the methodology. They added age and stage as continuous variables to the international metabolic prognostic index (IMPI) model, unlike the dichotomous variables applied in our study. A further reason for the inconsistent results could be the difference in the proportion of advanced disease (Ann Arbor stage III–IV): 65.2% in their cohort versus 55.2% in ours. Our study exhibited a more balanced stage distribution, while their cohort consisted predominantly of advanced stage patients, contributing to an overall unfavorable outcome and a relatively higher number of events. The Hans algorithm-based classification into GCB and non-GCB subtypes revealed no significant association with PFS. This finding underscores the limitations of the Hans classification as a standalone tool for risk stratification in DLBCL. Unlike MTV, tumor MH highlights information such as spatial distribution, heterogeneity, and morphological characteristics. We found that tumor MH is independent of both the MTV of the hottest lesion and the TMTV. These two metrics are complementary, and their combination can provide a more comprehensive prognostic evaluation in DLBCL.

It is common knowledge that age serves as a crucial indicator reflecting the biological reserve function of cancer patients. Elderly individuals typically exhibit reduced tolerance to treatments such as chemotherapy and radiotherapy, and they are more likely to experience adverse effects from these therapies. MTV was reported to be highly correlated with all factors in IPI except for age [28]. By incorporating age into the combined model, the prediction accuracy of the model can be further improved.

In the current study, we had several limitations. First, its retrospective nature may introduce potential selection bias. Prospective, multicenter and large-sample studies are still necessary to support the prognostic significance of tumor MH in DLBCL. Second, the analysis relied on a single dataset with a relatively small sample size, which may affect the generalizability of the findings. The absence of external dataset verification also restricts the model’s applicability in real-world settings. Third, maximum distance between lesions (Dmax), an emerging indicator which reflects the spatial distribution of the disease, provided prognostic information independent of MTV [33, 34]. However, it has not yet been routinely adopted in clinical practice. We will quantitatively measure the Dmax in the future to evaluate its application in DLBCL. Moreover, incorporating additional prognostic biomarkers such as the incidence of double-hit or triple-hit lymphomas and the expression frequencies of MYC, BCL2, and BCL6, as well as interim PET (iPET) data (e.g., Deauville score (DS) or ΔSUVmax) remains valuable for DLBCL prognostic. Although these features were not analyzed in the current study due to data limitations. In a follow-up study, multiple prognostic indicators will be integrated into the model to identify those with the greatest impact on clinical outcomes to assist in further risk stratification of DLBCL and guide individualized treatment decisions. Last, further integration of radiomics and advanced artificial intelligence (AI) - based image analysis may also help build a more robust and comprehensive prognostic evaluation system.

Conclusions

Tumor MH holds promise as a biomarker for individualized prognostic prediction and treatment stratification in DLBCL patients. By revealing the information regarding the intralesional heterogeneity and spatial complexity, tumor MH complements DLBCL tumor burden measured by TMTV.

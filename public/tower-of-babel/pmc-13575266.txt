Response to the letter regarding “Genetic diagnosis of hereditary kidney disease in pediatric patients through whole-exome sequencing and mitochondrial DNA analysis”
Authors: Jiyoung Oh, Keumwha Lee, Dongju Won, Young-Mock Lee, Jae Il Shin
Published: 2026

We thank Mehri and Finsterer [1] for the comments and the opportunity to clarify these points.

As described in Table 2 in the original article, variant classification in our study was performed according to the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics and Association for Molecular Pathology (ACMG/AMP) guidelines using multiple lines of evidence. In silico prediction was considered as one component of the overall assessment and was interpreted together with clinical phenotype, population frequency data, segregation analysis when available, and other ACMG/AMP evidence categories. Trio-based analysis was performed in a subset of families and contributed to variant interpretation where applicable [2].

Regarding the patient with the m.3243A>G variant, there was no history of seizures, stroke-like episodes, developmental delay, or other neurological symptoms. At the time of evaluation, the patient underwent clinical assessment for extra-renal involvement. As described in the manuscript, neurological examination, hearing and visual assessments, brain magnetic resonance imaging, and cardiac echocardiography revealed no significant abnormalities. Post-diagnostic serum lactate was 2.6 mmol/L (reference range, 0.5–2.2 mmol/L).

Considering the variable penetrance and age-dependent manifestations associated with the m.3243A>G variant, ongoing clinical follow-up has been recommended for the patient and family members [3].

Left bundle branch optimized cardiac resynchronization therapy with leadless LV endocardial pacing (LOT-endo CRT)
Authors: Hiro Kawata, Ramakota Reddy, Sreekanth Karanam
Published: 2026

Introduction

Cardiac resynchronization therapy (CRT) with biventricular pacing (BVP) using a coronary sinus (CS) lead is an effective treatment for patients with reduced left ventricular (LV) ejection fraction (LVEF) and intraventricular conduction delays, especially left bundle branch block.1 However, approximately one-third of patients with BVP using CS vein pacing are nonresponders.

Conduction system pacing using left bundle branch area pacing (LBBAP) has emerged as an alternative to BVP-CRT and shown to achieve better clinical results than does BVP with a CS lead.2,3

More recently, LBBAP-optimized CRT (LOT-CRT) has been reported to be feasible and more effective than BV-CRT.4,5

The Wireless Stimulation Endocardially for Cardiac Resynchronization (WiSE-CRT) system (EBR Systems Inc., Sunnyvale, CA) pacing represents a novel approach to delivering CRT by deploying a leadless electrode within the LV endocardium and is expected to provide a more physiologic pattern of ventricular activation compared with conventional epicardial pacing.6,7

We successfully performed LBBAP and WiSE CRT pacing in a patient who was a nonresponder to conventional CRT, resulting in a significant improvement in heart failure status (Figure 1). To our knowledge, this is the first description of LOT-CRT with endocardial LV pacing, a novel approach that integrates LBBAP with LV endocardial pacing to enhance electrical resynchronization.

Case report

A 63-year-old man with a history of hypertension, nonischemic cardiomyopathy, chronic systolic heart failure (LVEF 20%–25%), obstructive sleep apnea treated with bilevel positive airway pressure (BiPAP), and moderate pulmonary hypertension was referred for heart failure management.

He had a long-standing left bundle branch block and LV systolic dysfunction; thus, he underwent implantation of a CRT with defibrillator (CRT-D) in December 2016 for primary prevention. In 2021, because of progressive worsening of heart failure symptoms, he was referred to our institution for further evaluation and management. Given persistent heart failure symptoms despite conventional BVP, alternative strategies for resynchronization were considered. As a potential option to improve electrical synchrony, endocardial LV pacing using the WiSE-CRT system was evaluated.

In April 2022, the patient underwent implantation of the WiSE-CRT system. After implantation, the QRS duration shortened to 140 ms (Figure 2B). At 8-month follow-up, his LVEF had improved to 45% (Supplemental Movie 2) and functional status improved to NYHA functional class I.

However, approximately 12 months after implantation, premature battery depletion occurred as a result of malfunction of the ultrasound transmitter component of the WiSE-CRT system. As a result, ventricular activation reverted to right ventricular apical pacing with QRS prolongation to 190 ms (Figure 2C). TTE showed worsening dyssynchrony and an LVEF that dropped to 35% (Supplemental Movie 3). Because the initial WiSE-CRT system implantation had been performed as part of a clinical trial, replacement of the transmitter and battery was not possible until regulatory approval by the US Food and Drug Administration was obtained. After LV endocardial pacing with the WiSE-CRT system was discontinued, heart failure symptoms worsened; therefore, we decided to upgrade to an LBBAP system. In January 2024, a 3830 lead (Medtronic, Minneapolis, MN) was successfully implanted. The QRS duration shortened to 140 ms with an LV activation time (LVAT) of 80 ms (Figure 2D).

In June 2024, he developed decompensated heart failure in the setting of atrial flutter and atrial fibrillation (AF) and required multiple hospitalizations. He also experienced ventricular fibrillation and received 3 appropriate implantable cardioverter-defibrillator shocks. During atrial flutter or AF, the QRS duration and LVAT were prolonged to 154 and 110 ms, respectively (Figure 1E). Because AF persisted at that time, radiofrequency ablation for cavotricuspid isthmus and pulsed field ablation for pulmonary vein isolation were performed. Sinus rhythm was successfully restored and maintained.

In September 2024, although TTE demonstrated improved dyssynchrony after LBBAP, LV systolic function remained low with LVEF 30% (Supplemental Movie 4). The patient remained in NYHA functional class II and continued to experience exertional dyspnea during daily activities. In August 2025, he was again hospitalized for heart failure. Thereafter, he continued to experience persistent heart failure symptoms consistent with NYHA functional class III status. After US Food and Drug Administration approval of the WiSE-CRT system, battery replacement was performed in October 2025 as a final attempt at resynchronization optimization.

Postprocedural clinical course

After replacement of the WiSE-CRT transmitter and battery, the QRS duration shortened to 136 ms, with an improvement in LVAT to 76 ms (Figures 2F and 4) Postoperatively, his heart failure symptoms improved to NYHA functional class I. Discussion

CRT using a CS lead has been the mainstay of CRT for many years. However, conventional BVP with a CS lead has several limitations, including anatomic constraints, phrenic nerve stimulation, and nonphysiologic ventricular activation resulting from an epicardial pacing wavefront. In addition, CS lead implantation may be unsuccessful in 5%–7% of patients owing to anatomic challenges, high pacing thresholds, or phrenic nerve stimulation despite the availability of multipolar leads.8

The SOLVE-CRT (Stimulation of the Left Ventricular Endocardium for Cardiac Resynchronization Therapy) trial demonstrated the efficacy and safety of leadless LV endocardial pacing. LV endocardial pacing has several advantages over epicardial pacing via a CS lead.7 It allows greater freedom in pacing site selection, unconstrained by coronary venous anatomy or phrenic nerve capture. Furthermore, LV endocardial stimulation provides a more physiologic pattern of myocardial depolarization.

Leadless LV endocardial pacing using the WiSE-CRT system represents a novel approach that enables LV stimulation without the need for a transvenous lead, thereby avoiding some of the limitations associated with conventional epicardial CS leads. However, as a relatively new technology, several limitations should be acknowledged.

Because the system relies on ultrasound energy transmitted from a subcutaneous transmitter to the endocardial receiver electrode, effective energy delivery may be affected by patient positioning, thoracic anatomy, and lung interference, potentially resulting in intermittent loss of capture. In addition, device-related issues have been reported, including premature battery depletion, which in some cases has been attributed to insulation breaches in the transmitter (model 4100), leading to increased power consumption as in our case. Although this system has only recently received US Food and Drug Administration approval and long-term outcomes, including battery longevity, remain to be clarified, it may represent an effective therapeutic option for patients in whom conventional CRT has failed.

In recent years, conduction system pacing, including LBBAP, has emerged as an alternative to conventional CRT and has demonstrated favorable electrical and clinical outcomes compared with CS-based BVP.2,3,9,10 As a result, LBBAP has increasingly become the first-line strategy for CRT.

In the randomized study reported by Chen et al,9 LBBP-CRT resulted in a narrower QRS duration and greater improvement in LVEF than did BVP using a CS lead. However, the overall proportion of responders did not significantly differ between the 2 groups, with nonresponse rates of 14.2% and 19.6%, respectively.3,9

One possible explanation is that delayed activation of the LV lateral wall in patients with heart failure does not always arise from a discrete proximal left bundle branch lesion that can be bypassed by LBBAP. Rather, it may reflect diffuse or distal conduction system disease. In addition, electrical uncoupling, myocardial scar formation, or functional conduction block within the ventricular myocardium itself may contribute to persistent delay in lateral wall activation despite conduction system pacing.

In our case, the onset of AF and atrial flutter was associated with worsening heart failure, accompanied by marked prolongation of QRS duration and LVAT during LBBAP. These findings suggest that conduction in the distal left bundle branch may become delayed or even blocked in the setting of rapid atrial rates or ventricular dilation related to heart failure. In such cases, pacing from the LV lateral wall may be more effective. A certain proportion of patients fail to achieve adequate LV resynchronization with either conventional CS pacing or LBBAP. In such patients, LOT-CRT with endocardial LV pacing may provide complementary mechanisms of activation, allowing more complete ventricular resynchronization through fusion of activation wavefronts.

Conclusion

This case illustrates that LBBAP alone may not always achieve adequate ventricular resynchronization in patients with advanced heart failure and complex conduction system disease. In our patient, recurrent atrial arrhythmias and progressive heart failure were associated with deterioration of QRS duration and LVAT despite successful LBBAP, suggesting dynamic or distal conduction system impairment beyond the reach of proximal conduction system pacing.

The addition of leadless LV endocardial pacing to LBBAP, forming a novel LOT-CRT with endocardial LV pacing, resulted in significant electrical resynchronization, reverse remodeling, and marked clinical improvement.

Although long-term durability and battery performance require further investigation after recent regulatory approval, LOT-CRT with endocardial LV pacing may represent a promising therapeutic option for selected patients who remain nonresponders to conventional CRT or conduction system pacing alone. Further prospective studies are warranted to define patient selection criteria and long-term outcomes of this combined resynchronization approach.

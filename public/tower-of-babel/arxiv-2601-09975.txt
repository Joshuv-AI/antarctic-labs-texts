Einstein & Yang–Mills implies Conformal Yang–Mills X
Samuel Blitz ♢ , A. Rod Gover ♡ , Jarosław Kopiński ♣ , & Andrew Waldron ♠

Abstract
. There exist conformally invariant, higher-derivative, variational analogs of the Yang–Mills condition for connections on vector bundles over a conformal manifold of even dimension greater than or equal to six. We give a compact formula for these analogs and prove that they are a strict weakening of the Yang–Mills condition with respect to an Einstein metric. We also show that the conformal Yang–Mills condition for the tractor connection of an even dimensional conformal manifold is equivalent to vanishing of its Fefferman–Graham obstruction tensor. This result uses that the tractor connection on a Poincaré–Einstein manifold is itself Yang–Mills. Keywords: Conformal geometry, Yang–Mills theory, Einstein manifolds, Poincaré–Einstein manifolds, conformally invariant conditions on connections, tractor calculus.

1.
Introduction

The problem of finding distinguished linear connections
A
on a vector bundle

V\to VM\to M

has myriad applications
ranging from fundamental physics
[
26
,
1
]
to four-manifold theory
[
7
,
8
]
. When the base manifold
M
is equipped with a (possibly indefinite signature) metric
g
, a natural criterion on
A
is the
Yang–Mills condition

[
26
]

j_{a}[g,A]:=g^{bc}\nabla^{A,g}_{b}F^{A}_{ca}=0\,,

where
F^{A}
is the curvature of
A
and
\nabla^{A,g}
is the corresponding Levi–Civita coupled covariant derivative.
When
M
is a four-manifold, any
A
whose
Yang–Mills current

j[g,A]
vanishes also solves

j[\Omega^{2}g,A]=0\,,\>\mbox{ where $0<\Omega\in C^{\infty}(M)$.}

In other words, the four dimensional Yang–Mills condition is conformally invariant.
Consider now a space of connections on
VM
such that the
natural pairing on endomorphisms of
\Gamma(VM)
induces a non-degenerate pairing
on the corresponding subspace of endormorphisms inhabited by curvatures
F
,
or in short “the space of connections has a non-degenerate trace-form”.
Then
the current

j
is the functional gradient
of an energy

E_{\rm YM}\big[g,A]:=-\frac{1}{4}\int_{M}{\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}{\rm Vol}(g)\operatorname{Tr}F_{ab}F^{ab}\,.

When
M
is a four-manifold,
E[\Omega^{2}g,A]=E[g,A]
so the energy functional depends only on the conformal class of metrics
c:=[g]
.
In dimensions other than four, this is no longer the case.

Remarkably, there exists a variational, conformally invariant analog of the Yang–Mills condition for six-manifolds
[
3
,
12
,
10
,
11
]
, namely

(1.1)

k_{a}[g,A]:=\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla^{c}\big(\nabla_{[c}{j}_{a]}-4P_{[c}{}^{b}F_{a]b}-JF_{ca}\big)+\tfrac{1}{2}[j^{b},F_{ba}]=0\,.

The above current
k
is the functional gradient of

E_{\rm CYM}\big[[g],A\big]:=\frac{1}{4}\int_{M}{\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}{\rm Vol}(g)\operatorname{Tr}\big(j_{a}j^{a}+JF_{ab}F^{ab}+4{F}^{ab}P_{a}{}^{c}F_{bc}-\nabla^{a}v_{a}\big)\,,

where
v_{a}:=\frac{1}{4}\nabla_{a}F^{2}+2F_{ab}j^{b}
. Moreover

k[\Omega^{2}g,A]=\Omega^{-4}k[g,A]\,,

so we term
k[g,A]=0
the (dimension six)
conformal Yang–Mills condition
. Our conventions for tensors on manifolds
are detailed in Section
1.1
.

It not difficult to find an exact 2-form
F={\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}A
in Euclidean space
({\mathbb{R}}^{6},\delta)
that is not coclosed but such that
k[\delta,A]=0
.

Viz.
, there exist conformal Yang–Mills connections that are themselves not Yang–Mills. Conversely,
if
A_{\rm YM}
is a Yang–Mills connection, it need not be that
k[g,A_{\rm YM}]=0
. This is almost evident from Equation (
1.1
), but we give explicit examples in Section
3.1
.
However, if additionally the metric
g
is an
Einstein metric

g_{\rm E}
, meaning that its trace-free Schouten tensor
P
vanishes, then one immediately sees from Equation (
1.1
) that

k[g_{\rm E},A_{\rm YM}]=0\,,

so Einstein and Yang–Mills implies conformal Yang–Mills for six-manifolds.

This suggests the following two questions:

(i)

Do there exist variational, higher derivative, conformal Yang–Mills conditions in higher dimensions?

(ii)

If so, are any of these
weakenings of the Einstein and Yang–Mills condition?

The first question has been answered in the affirmative for all even dimensions greater than or equal to four.

Theorem 1.1

(from
[
10
]
)
.

Let
n\in 2{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geqslant 2}
and let
(M^{n},c)
be a conformal
n
-manifold. In addition let
VM
be a vector bundle with a connection
A
.
Then there exists a 1-form
k
on
M
valued in endomorphisms of
VM
such that, for any
g\in c
and
0<\Omega\in C^{\infty}(M)
,

(1.2)

k[\Omega^{2}g,A]=\Omega^{2-n}k[g,A]\,.

If in addition the space of connections is equipped with a non-degenerate trace-form, then

k
is the functional
gradient of a
corresponding energy

E_{\rm CYM}\big[[g],A\big]:=-\frac{1}{4}\int_{M}{\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}{\rm Vol}(g)\mathcal{E}\big[g,A]\,,

where

\mathcal{E}[\Omega^{2}g,A]=\Omega^{-n}\mathcal{E}[g,A]\,.

Equation (
1.2
) implies that we may view
k
as a weight
2-n
conformal density determined only by the conformal class itself; when doing so we then write
k[c,A]
—see Section
2
for details.

The proof of Theorem
1.1

in
[
10
]
relies on “geometric holography”, where one embeds a conformal manifold as a hypersurface boundary of some
n+1
dimensional manifold,

M^{n}\hookrightarrow X^{n+1}\,.

The data of a conformal class of metrics
c
on
M
defines a complete metric
g_{+}
on the interior
X_{+}
of

subject to the asymptotic Einstein condition

P^{g_{+}}+\frac{1}{2}{\hskip 0.85358pt}g_{+}={\mathcal{O}}(s^{n-2})\,,

where

is any defining function for the embedding
M\hookrightarrow X
and
s^{2}g_{+}
smoothly extends as a metric to
X
. Moreover
g_{+}
is determined uniquely modulo terms of sufficiently high order in
s

[
9
]
. In turn, given a connection
A
on
VM
, this can be extended to a Yang–Mills connection
{\bm{A}}
over
X_{+}
with respect to
g_{+}
, at least up to a certain order, namely

In fact,

s^{2-n}j[g_{+},{\bm{A}}]\big|_{M}=k[g,A]\,,

where the metric
g\in c
is the smooth extension of
s^{2}g_{+}
to the boundary
M

[
10
]
. The proof of Theorem
1.1
establishes that the obstruction

to smooth Yang–Mills solutions on
X
defines a conformally invariant and variational 1-form on
M
valued in endomorphisms of
VM
. Here we shall call the so-obtained tensor

conformal Yang–Mills current
of the connection
A
.

While the work of
[
10
]
gave both a compact holographic formula for the energy functional

as well as a recursion for computing explicitly the corresponding higher conformal Yang–Mills currents

, higher dimensional analogs of Equation (

However, tractor calculus methods allow for a simple universal formula.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
n\in 2{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geqslant 3}
and
(M^{n},c)
be a conformal manifold. If

with curvature tractor

{\mathcal{F}}=q_{\sf w}(F)

, then

(1.3)

\cancel{P}_{n-4}{\mathcal{F}}=2(n-3)(n-4)[(n-5)!]^{2}\,q_{\sf s}(k)\,.

The tractor bundle
{\mathcal{T}}M
for an
n

is a rank
n+2

. In the above,

is a tractor extension of the curvature

, and

is a tractor algebraically in isomorphism with the conformal Yang–Mills current

. The operator

is a derivative-order

]
.

An important invariant of even dimensional conformal manifolds of dimension greater than or equal to six is the Fefferman–Graham obstruction tensor

[
9
]
, which may be viewed as a higher dimensional analog of the Bach tensor; see Section
3

itself comes canonically equipped with a connection

called the tractor connection.
Our next result relies on holographic methods—see Theorem

—to show that
the Fefferman–Graham obstruction tensor is equivalent to the conformal Yang–Mills current of the tractor connection. This extends what was known in dimensions

[

,
13
,
12
]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M^{n},c)
be a conformal manifold of dimension

of

q_{\sf s}:\Gamma(T^{*}M[2-n]\otimes T^{*}M)\longrightarrow\Gamma(\wedge^{2}{\mathcal{T}}M[2-n]\otimes T^{*}M)\,,

and such that

k[c,A^{\mathcal{T}}]=2(n-1)(n-2)q_{\sf s}({\mathcal{B}})\,.

Our next result answers Question (

Let

(M^{n},c)

.
Then the Einstein and Yang–Mills conditions imply the conformal Yang–Mills condition. In other words,

is a Yang–Mills connection with respect to

Prescient elements of the tractor calculus

1.1.
Convention center

We employ the abstract index
notation of Penrose
[
24
]
to handle tensor bundles and sections thereof.
For that we denote by
{\mathcal{E}}M
, or simply
{\mathcal{E}}
, the trivial line bundle over
M
with smooth section space
C^{\infty}(M)
.
Tensor bundles are then described by attaching appropriate indices to
{\mathcal{E}}
.
Round and square brackets are then used to denote totally symmetric
and antisymmetric subbundles.
Thus
{\mathcal{E}}_{a}
and
{\mathcal{E}}^{a}
are respective notations for the
cotangent and tangent bundles
T^{*}M
and
TM
while
v^{a}
stands for a section of the latter and
{\mathcal{E}}_{(ab)}
denotes
the subbundle
\odot^{2}T^{*}M
of symmetric tensors in
T^{*}M\otimes T^{*}M
.
Given a metric
g
(say), then
g_{ab}\cdot{\mathcal{E}}
is a line-subbundle of
{\mathcal{E}}_{(ab)}
.
The contraction
\omega(v)=:\iota_{v}\omega
of a
1-form
\omega
with a tangent vector
v^{a}
is denoted by a repeated index
v^{a}\omega_{a}
.
We will in addition use the notation
\omega_{v}
for this, and along the same lines, symbols such as
R_{avcd}
denote
v^{b}R_{abcd}
.
The interior multiplication operator
\iota
also makes sense for
v^{\alpha}\in\Gamma(VM)
and
\omega_{\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{k}}\in\Gamma(\wedge^{k}V^{*}M)
, where
VM
is any vector bundle, so that

\iota_{v}\omega:=v^{\alpha}\omega_{\alpha\beta_{2}\cdots\beta_{k}}\,.

The above notation will also be applied to the case when
v
is some linear operator taking values in
\Gamma(VM)
.

If
X^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}
is an endomorphism of
\Gamma(VM)
, we may denote the action of
X
by

X^{\sharp}v^{\alpha}:=X^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}v^{\beta}\>\mbox{ and }\>X^{\sharp}\mu_{\beta}:=-X^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}\mu_{\alpha}\,,

where
\mu_{\alpha}\in\Gamma(V^{*}M)
; the
hash action
on higher tensor products is defined by the Leibniz rule, while the hashed action
X^{\sharp}
annihilates scalars.
If
A
is a linear connection on
VM
and
v^{\alpha}
a section thereof, then the curvature
F^{A}
is defined in terms of a commutator by coupling
\nabla^{A}
to any torsion-free affine connection
\gamma
and writing

[\nabla_{a}^{A,\gamma},\nabla^{A,\gamma}_{b}]v^{\alpha}=2\nabla_{[a}\nabla_{b]}v^{\alpha}=F^{A}_{ab}{}^{\sharp}v^{\alpha}=F_{ab}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}v^{\beta}\,.

Throughout, we will suppress identifying sub and superscripts where context allows and simply write
\nabla
and
F
for the respective connection-coupled gradient and curvature.
We may of course write
X^{\sharp}=:X^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}\,\sharp^{\beta}{}_{\alpha}
in order to define an operator
\sharp^{\beta}{}_{\alpha}
. In this way more complicated operators such as
X^{\sharp\sharp}:=X^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}{}^{\gamma}{}_{\delta}\,\sharp^{\beta}{}_{\alpha}\sharp^{\delta}{}_{\gamma}

may be introduced.

Our convention for the
Riemann tensor
R_{ab}{}^{c}{}_{d}

of the Levi–Civita connection
\nabla_{a}^{g}
of a metric
g_{ab}
,
is such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left[\nabla_{a},\nabla_{b}\right]v^{c}\,&=R_{ab}{}^{c}{}_{d}v^{d}=R_{ab}{}^{\sharp}\,v^{c},\\
\left[\nabla_{a},\nabla_{b}\right]{\hskip 0.85358pt}\omega_{c}&=R_{abc}{}^{d}\omega_{d}=R_{ab}{}^{\sharp}\,\omega_{c}\,,\end{split}

where

v
is any tangent vector field and
\omega
any 1-form. Using the
metric to raise and lower indices we have, for example,

R_{abcd}=g_{ce}R_{ab}{}^{e}{}_{d}
and
|v|^{2}=v_{a}v^{a}=g_{ab}v^{a}v^{b}
. The Riemann tensor can be decomposed as

R_{abcd}=:W_{abcd}+2\left(g_{c[a}P_{b]d}+g_{d[b}P_{a]c}\right),

where the
Weyl tensor

\,W_{abcd}
is
completely trace-free
and
P_{ab}
is the
Schouten tensor
. It follows, when
\operatorname{dim}M=:n\geqslant 3
, that

P_{ab}:=\frac{1}{n-2}\left(R_{ab}-\frac{R}{2\left(n-1\right)}g_{ab}\right)\,,

where
R_{bc}:=R_{ab}{}^{a}{}_{c}
is the
Ricci tensor
and its metric trace
R:=g^{ab}R_{ab}
is the
scalar curvature
. We will use
J
to denote the metric trace of Schouten,
i.e.

J:=g^{ab}P_{ab}
.
The identity endomorphism of
{\mathcal{E}}^{a}
is denoted
\delta^{a}{}_{b}
so that
g^{ac}g_{cb}=\delta^{a}{}_{b}
. The metric trace-free part of a symmetric group of indices is denoted by a subscript
\circ
so that, for example,
X_{(abc)_{\circ}}=X_{(abc)}-\frac{3}{d+2}g_{(ab}X_{c)de}g^{de}
.
Importantly, the trace and trace-free parts of the Schouten tensor obey Bianchi-type identities

\displaystyle\nabla^{a}P_{ab}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{b}J\,,

(1.5)

\displaystyle\nabla_{c}P_{ab}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{c}P_{(ab)_{\circ}}+{\hskip 0.85358pt}\frac{1}{d-1}{\hskip 0.85358pt}{\hskip 0.85358pt}g_{ab}\nabla^{d}P_{(dc)_{\circ}}\,.

The
Cotton
and
Bach
tensors are respectively given by

(1.6)

\displaystyle C_{abc}

\displaystyle:=2\nabla_{[a}P_{b]c}\,,

(1.7)

\displaystyle B_{ab}\,

\displaystyle:=\nabla^{c}C_{cab}+P^{cd}W_{cadb}\,.

We everywhere assume that metrics
g_{ab}
are Riemannian, but many of our results extend easily to pseudo-Riemannian structures.
Throughout the article, all structures are assumed to be smooth unless otherwise stated, and all manifolds are taken to be connected. The notation
{\mathcal{O}}(s^{\ell})
stands for
s^{\ell}{\mathcal{X}}
where
{\mathcal{X}}
is some (suitable) smooth quantity.

2.
Tractor Calculus

Here we review conformal tractor calculus—see

[
2
,
5
,
6
,
14
]
for details—and extend those methods to handle couplings to general linear connections.
A conformal manifold
(M^{n},c)
is a smooth manifold of
dimension
n\geqslant 3
equipped with an equivalence class
c
of Riemanian
metrics, where
g_{ab}
,
\widehat{g}_{ab}\in c
means that

\widehat{g}_{ab}=\Omega^{2}g_{ab}\,,

for some smooth positive function

\Omega
.
Oftentime, calculations will be
performed with respect to some
generic
g\in c
.

There is no
distinguished connection on the tangent bundle of a general conformal manifold. However there is an invariant and canonical tractor connection on the closely related standard tractor bundle. To describe these ingredients we first need the notion of a conformal density:
The top exterior power of the tangent bundle
\Lambda^{n}TM
is a line bundle. Thus its square

\mathcal{V}:=(\Lambda^{n}TM)^{\otimes 2}
is
canonically oriented and so one can take oriented roots thereof. Given

w\in\mathbb{R}
we set

(2.1)

{\mathcal{E}}[w]:=\mathcal{V}^{\frac{w}{2n}},

and refer to this as the
bundle of conformal densities
. For any vector
bundle
VM
, we write
VM[w]:=VM\otimes{\mathcal{E}}[w]
, for example

{\mathcal{E}}_{(ab)}[w]=\odot^{2}T^{*}M\otimes{\mathcal{E}}[w]
and
VM[0]=VM
.
On a fixed Riemannian manifold, the line bundle
{\mathcal{V}}
is canonically
trivialized by the square of the volume form.
Since each metric in a conformal class determines a trivialization of
{\mathcal{V}}
, it follows easily that on a conformal structure there is a
canonical section
{\sf g}_{ab}\in\Gamma({\mathcal{E}}_{(ab)}[2])
termed the
conformal metric
. This has
the property that any strictly positive section
\tau\in\Gamma({\mathcal{E}}[1])
determines a metric

g_{ab}:=\tau^{-2}{\sf g}_{ab}\in c\,.

For this reason we call such a density
\tau
a
scale
.
Moreover, the Levi–Civita connection of
g_{ab}
preserves
the corresponding scale
\tau
and therefore
{\sf g}_{ab}
. Thus it makes sense to use the
conformal metric to raise and lower indices, even when we are choosing
a particular metric representative
g_{ab}\in c
and its Levi–Civita connection
for calculations.
Since each
g\in c
trivializes
{\mathcal{V}}
, it also follows that a section
\Gamma({\mathcal{E}}[w])
may be understood as an equivalence class
[g;f]
of pairs
g\in c
and
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, under the relation

(g,f)\sim(\Omega^{2}g,\Omega^{w}f),\qquad 0<\Omega\in C^{\infty}(M)\,.

For this reason, given a bundle
VM[w]
, we call the number
w
its
weight
.

From Taylor series of sections of
{\mathcal{E}}[1]
one recovers the jet exact sequence at 2-jets,

(2.2)

0\to{\mathcal{E}}_{(ab)}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sf i}}{{\longrightarrow}}J^{2}{\mathcal{E}}[1]\to J^{1}{\mathcal{E}}[1]\to 0\,.

The bundle
J^{2}{\mathcal{E}}[1]
and its sequence (
2.2
) are canonical objects on any smooth manifold. Given a
conformal structure
c
, we have the orthogonal decomposition of
{\mathcal{E}}_{(ab)}[1]
into trace-free and trace parts

{\mathcal{E}}_{(ab)}[1]={\mathcal{E}}_{(ab)_{0}}[1]\oplus{\sf g}_{ab}\cdot{\mathcal{E}}[-1]\,.

Thus we can canonically quotient
J^{2}{\mathcal{E}}[1]
by the image of

{\mathcal{E}}_{(ab)_{0}}[1]
under
{\sf i}
as given in (
2.2
). The resulting
quotient bundle is denoted
{\mathcal{T}}^{*}M
, or
{\mathcal{E}}_{A}
in abstract indices,
and called the
conformal cotractor bundle
.
Its dual is termed the
standard tractor bundle

{\mathcal{T}}M
.

Since the
jet exact sequence at 1-jets of
{\mathcal{E}}[1]
is

0\to{\mathcal{E}}_{a}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sf i}}{{\longrightarrow}}J^{1}{\mathcal{E}}[1]\to{\mathcal{E}}[1]\to 0\,,

we see that
{\mathcal{T}}^{*}M
has a composition series (or filtration structure)

{\mathcal{T}}^{*}M={\mathcal{E}}[1]\mbox{$\begin{picture}(-0.5,-1.0)\put(2.0,0.2){$+$}\put(6.2,2.8){\oval(8.0,8.0)[l]}\end{picture}$}{\mathcal{E}}_{a}[1]\mbox{$\begin{picture}(-0.5,-1.0)\put(2.0,0.2){$+$}\put(6.2,2.8){\oval(8.0,8.0)[l]}\end{picture}$}{\mathcal{E}}[-1]\,.

What this notation means is that
{\mathcal{E}}[-1]
is a subbundle of
{\mathcal{T}}^{*}M
,
and the quotient of
{\mathcal{E}}_{A}={\mathcal{T}}^{*}M
by
{\mathcal{E}}[-1]
(which is
J^{1}{\mathcal{E}}[1]
) has

{\mathcal{E}}_{a}[1]
as a subbundle, and there is a canonical
injection

X_{A}:{\mathcal{E}}[-1]\to{\mathcal{E}}_{A}
.
We also employ an abstract index notation for
tensor tractor bundles

{\mathcal{T}}^{\Phi}M
and their sections,
obtained by tensoring
{\mathcal{T}}M
and
{\mathcal{T}}^{*}M
.
Here the label
\Phi
stands for some collection of abstract tractor indices.

Given a choice of metric
g\in c
, the formula

(2.3)

{\mathcal{E}}[1]\ni\sigma\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}\sigma\\
\nabla_{a}\sigma\\
-\frac{1}{n}\left(\Delta+J\right)\sigma\end{pmatrix}\,,

where
\Delta
is the Laplacian
\nabla^{a}\nabla_{a}={\sf g}^{ab}\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}
, gives a second-order differential operator on
{\mathcal{E}}[1]
which is a linear map
J^{2}{\mathcal{E}}[1]\to{\mathcal{E}}[1]\oplus{\mathcal{E}}_{a}[1]\oplus{\mathcal{E}}[-1]
.
This map factors through
{\mathcal{T}}^{*}M
, and a choice of metric
g
determines an isomorphism

(2.4)

{\mathcal{T}}^{*}M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}{[{\mathcal{T}}^{*}M]}_{g}={\mathcal{E}}[1]\oplus{\mathcal{E}}_{a}[1]\oplus{\mathcal{E}}[-1]\,.

We will often use this isomorphism to “split”
tractor bundles. Thus, given
g\in c
, a section
V_{A}
of
{\mathcal{E}}_{A}
may be labeled by a triple

(\sigma,\mu_{a},\rho)
of sections of the bundles in the right hand side of the above display. Equivalently we may write

(2.5)

V_{A}=\sigma Y_{A}+\mu_{a}Z_{A}{}^{a}+\rho X_{A}\,.

Here the algebraic splitting operators

Y_{A}:{\mathcal{E}}[1]\to{\mathcal{E}}_{A}
and
Z_{A}{}^{a}:{\mathcal{E}}_{a}[1]\to{\mathcal{E}}_{A}
are determined by the
choice
g\in c
and can be viewed as sections of
{\mathcal{E}}_{A}[-1]
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{A}{}^{a}[-1]
, respectively, while the tractor
X_{A}
is a section of
{\mathcal{E}}_{A}[1]
determined by
(M,c)
alone. We call
the three sections
Y_{A}
,
Z_{A}{}^{a}
and
X_{A}

tractor projectors
. We will also sometimes use respective monikers “top/north”, “middle/west” and “bottom/south” for the “slots”
\sigma
,
\mu
and
\rho
of
V
.

By construction the tractor bundle is conformally invariant,
i.e.
determined by
(M,c)
and independent of any choice of
g\in c
. Evidently the splitting (
2.5
) is not. Considering the transformation of the operator (
2.3
) determining the splitting upon moving to a conformally related metric
\widehat{g}=\Omega^{2}g
,
one sees that the slots of an invariant section of
{\mathcal{T}}^{*}M
must be related by

(2.6)

[{\mathcal{T}}^{*}M]_{\widehat{g}}\ni\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\widehat{\sigma}\\
\widehat{\mu}_{b}\\[2.84526pt]
\widehat{\rho}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\
\Upsilon_{b}&\delta^{c}_{b}&0\\[2.84526pt]
-\frac{1}{2}|\Upsilon|_{g}^{2}&-\Upsilon^{c}&1\end{array}\right)\!\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\sigma\\
\mu_{c}\\
\rho\end{array}\right)\sim\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\sigma\\
\mu_{b}\\[2.84526pt]
\rho\end{array}\right)\in[{\mathcal{T}}^{*}M]_{g}\,,

where

Lemma 2.3

{\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}\bm{s}

3.1.

Our results apply to connections on any vector bundle
VM
, so in particular when
VM
is a line bundle. Our first example addresses our claim in the introduction that not every conformal Yang–Mills connection is itself Yang–Mills.

Example 3.9
.

Let
V{\mathbb{R}}^{6}
be a line bundle over 6-dimensional Euclidean space
({\mathbb{R}}^{6},\delta)
.
Then we may describe a connection by a 1-form
A
and its curvature by
F={\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}A
. In those terms, the conformally invariant Yang–Mills condition (
1.1
) becomes

\Delta j=0\,,

where the current is given by the 1-form
j={\EXT\hskip 0.02843pt}^{*}F
.
Let
\lambda
be any parallel vector field and
E
be the standard Euler vector field defined such that
{\mathcal{L}}_{E}{\hskip 0.85358pt}\delta=2\delta
. Then consider the 1-form

A=E^{\flat}(\lambda)\,E^{\flat}\,,

where
E^{\flat}=\delta(E,{\bm{\cdot}})
. A simple computation shows

j=-5\lambda^{\flat}\,,

which is clearly in the kernel of
\Delta
. Hence we have found a connection that is not Yang–Mills but is conformal Yang–Mills.

Finally we give a Yang–Mills connection that is not conformal Yang–Mills. By dint of Theorem
1.4
, for that we must consider Riemannian manifolds that are not conformal to Einstein.

Example 3.10
.

We wish to consider a metric

admitting a non-vanishing, parallel 2-form

so that

is both closed and co-closed and therefore obeys the source-free Maxwell’s equations.
In that case the conformal Yang–Mills Condition (

) on a 6-manifold reduces to

(3.18)

We now search for a geometry such that
the left hand side of the above display does not vanish. For that, on
{\mathbb{R}}^{6}\ni(u,v,x,y,z,w)
, we consider a metric ansatz

(3.19)

and a 2-form

F=f(u+v){\hskip 0.85358pt}du\wedge dv\,.

Rudimentary computations show that

, the metric

is not conformal to an Einstein metric and nor is the left hand side of Equation (

) zero.
However
the 2-form

is covariantly constant.
We note that it is even possible to choose

such that Equation (
3.19
) globally defines a metric on

.

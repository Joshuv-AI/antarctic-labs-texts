Regularity of Einstein 5 5 -manifolds via 4 4 -dimensional gap theorems
Yiqi Huang and Tristan Ozuch

Abstract
. We refine the regularity of noncollapsed limits of 5 5 -dimensional manifolds with bounded Ricci curvature. In particular, for noncollapsed limits of Einstein 5 5 -manifolds, we prove that • tangent cones are unique of the form ℝ × ℝ 4 / Γ \mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{4}/\Gamma on the top stratum, hence outside a countable set of points, • the singular set is entirely contained in a countable union of Lipschitz curves and points, • away from a nowhere dense subset, these Lipschitz curves consist of smooth geodesics, • the interior of any geodesic is removable: limits of Einstein manifolds are real-analytic orbifolds with singularities along geodesic and bounded curvature away from their extreme points, and • if an asymptotically Ricci-flat 5 5 -manifold with Euclidean volume growth has one tangent cone at infinity that splits off a line, then it is the unique tangent cone at infinity. These results prompt the question of the orbifold regularity of noncollapsed limits of Einstein manifolds off a codimension 5 5 set in arbitrary dimension. The proofs rely on a new result of independent interest: all spherical and hyperbolic 4 4 -orbifolds are isolated among Einstein 4 4 -orbifolds in the Gromov-Hausdorff sense. This yields various gap theorems for Einstein 4 4 -orbifolds, which do not extend to higher dimensions.



1.
Introduction

An
Einstein manifold

(M,g)
satisfies the fundamental curvature condition

{\rm Ric}(g)=\Lambda g,\quad\Lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

(1.1)

Such metrics are distinguished not only by having a curvature constant but also as stationary points of significant geometric functionals. Another curvature condition considered in this article is that of
bounded Ricci curvature
, which we normalize in dimension
n
as

-(n-1)g\leqslant{\rm Ric}\leqslant(n-1)g.

In dimensions greater than or equal to four, compact manifolds with uniform diameter bounds and noncollapsing conditions, sequences of Einstein metrics may develop singularities. Understanding these singularities and their formation has been a central question since the 1970’s. By the end of the 1980’s, noncollapsed limits of
4
-manifolds were roughly understood. More recently, the
4
-dimensional picture has been further clarified and a basic structure is emerging in higher-dimensions.

In the present article, we refine our understanding of limits in dimension
4
and deduce several gap theorems for spherical and hyperbolic orbifolds, only valid in this dimension. This leads to substantial applications to the regularity of limits of Einstein
5
-manifolds, towards an orbifold regularity theorem away from a codimension
5
singular set.

1.1.
Background

1.1.1.
Noncollapsed degeneration of Einstein
4
-manifolds

A central objective in four-dimensional differential geometry is to understand the structure and topology of the moduli space of Einstein metrics on a given compact manifold
M^{4}
, i.e. the set of Einstein metrics modulo the action of the diffeomorphism group, denoted
\mathbf{E}(M^{4})
. When equipped with the Gromov-Hausdorff metric
d_{GH}
, the completion of
\mathbf{E}(M^{4})
includes Einstein orbifolds, as proven in
[
And89
,
BKN89
,
Tia90
]
. The need for a common background manifold
M^{4}
was weakened to a noncollapsing assumption in
[
CN15
]
. The structure of this completion has recently been improved substantially in
[
Biq13
,
Biq16
,
Ozu22a
,
Ozu22b
,
Ozu20
]
. It has been shown in particular that the moduli space of Einstein metrics
GH
-close to a given Einstein
4
-orbifold
(M_{o},g_{o})
was embedded in finite-dimensional set obtained by a gluing construction. Its dimension is controlled by the noncollapsedness.

It has long been an open question, explicitly posed in
[
And94
,
And10
]
, whether Einstein orbifolds can be approximated or resolved by sequences of smooth Einstein metrics. Such approximations have been obtained by gluing-perturbation, see e.g.
[
Biq13
,
Don12
,
LS94
]
. On the other hand, in
[
Ozu21b
]
, it was proven that spherical and hyperbolic orbifolds with the simplest singularities,
\mathbb{R}^{4}/\pm
, could never be limits of smooth Einstein
4
-manifolds in the Gromov-Hausdorff sense. In the present article, we show that
all
hyperbolic and spherical orbifolds are
GH
-isolated, even within the class of Einstein
4
-
orbifolds
. This is not a mere extension of
[
Ozu21b
]
and requires completely new ideas in order to deal with the deeply technical case of multiscale trees of singularities and singular metrics. The resulting isolation theorem implies a series of new surprising quantitative gap phenomena for Einstein
4
-orbifolds. These results are genuinely
4
-dimensional: they have no analogue in higher dimensions
n\geqslant 5
, even with isolated singularities.

1.1.2.
Regularity of noncollapsed Einstein
n
-manifolds for
n\geqslant 5

The higher-dimensional Einstein manifolds have been extensively studied over the last four decades. The modern compactness theory originates with the Cheeger–Gromov program of pointed
C^{\infty}
convergence under curvature control.
A foundational leap was the structure theory for spaces with lower Ricci curvature bounds

[
CC96
,
Col97
,
CC97
,
CC00a
,
CC00b
]
, which introduced the stratification of the singular set and established the regularity of the limit space.

Uniqueness of tangent cones.

One of the key problems in the regularity theory is the uniqueness of the tangent cones. For a noncompact manifold with nonnegative Ricci curvature and Euclidean volume growth, it was proved in
[
CC96
]
that the tangent cone at infinity is a metric cone. A priori, however, different rescaling sequences may lead to different tangent cones. A long-standing well-known question asks whether the cross-section of the tangent cone at infinity for noncollapsed Ricci-flat manifolds depends on the rescaling sequence of the blow-downs. It is known that uniqueness may fail if assuming merely the lower bound on the Ricci curvature
[
Per97
,
CC96
,
CoN13
]
and for collapsed Ricci-flat metrics
[
Hat17
]
.

There is a substantial literature on this uniqueness problem of geometric PDEs. For minimal surface, the uniqueness was established assuming the integrability and smoothness of the cross-sections in
[
AA81
]
, and the integrability assumption was later removed in the seminal work of
[
Sim81
]
. For more general nonsmooth cross-sections, the uniqueness of cones
\mathds{R}\times C(\mathbb{S}^{p}\times\mathbb{S}^{q})
for
p+q\geq 7
was proved in
[
Sim94
]
. Only very recently was the uniqueness of the cylindrical Simons cone,
\mathds{R}\times C(\mathbb{S}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{3})
established in
[
Sze20a
]
. The case
\mathds{R}\times C(\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{4})
was subsequently addressed in
[
FTW25
]
. See also the uniqueness for two-dimensional area-minimizing integral
currents
[
Whi83
]
. Analogous uniqueness problems arise in other geometric PDEs, for example, mean curvature flow
[
Sch14
,
CIM15
,
CM15
,
CS21
,
CM23
,
LZ24
]
, and Ricci flow
[
SW15
,
CMZ24
,
LW24
,
CM25
,
FL25a
]
and the references therein.

For noncollapsed Ricci-flat manifolds, Cheeger-Tian
[
CT94
]
proved uniqueness under the assumption that the cross-sections of all tangent cones are smooth and integrable; later Colding-Minicozzi
[
CM14
]
established uniqueness assuming only that one tangent cone is smooth. See also the Kähler-Einstein setting
[
DS17
]
and related study
[
KSz24
,
YZ25
]
.
When the cone has a nonsmooth cross-section, for instance a cone that splits off a line
\mathbb{R}\times C(Z)
, much less is known.

As our first result in higher dimensions, we show that in dimension 5, if one tangent cone splits off a line, then it is the
unique
tangent cone. To the best of authors’ knowledge, this is the first uniqueness result for a given general Ricci-flat cone with a nonsmooth cross-section. It yields a clearer understanding of the asymptotic geometry and provides curvature decay estimates for Ricci-flat
5
-manifolds.

Regularity of limit spaces and singular sets.

Through a series of works
[
And89
,
BKN89
,
Tia90
,
CN15
]
, it was shown that limits of
4
-manifolds with bounded Ricci curvature and uniform lower volume bounds are Riemannian orbifolds with locally finitely many singularities.
A natural question is whether such structural results extend to higher dimensions.
Key recent milestones include the metric geometry structure of the regular set, the resolution of the codimension-four conjecture and the finiteness of
\mathcal{H}^{n-4}
-measure of the singular set
[
CoN12
,
CN15
,
CJN21
,
JN21
]
.
Moreover, the
rectifiability
of the singular set was established in
[
CJN21
,
JN21
]
: up to an
\mathcal{H}^{n-4}
-measure zero
subset,
\mathcal{S}
is contained in a countable union of bi-Lipschitz images of subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n-4}
.

Several issues remained largely open, notably the treatment of the exceptional
\mathcal{H}^{n-4}
-measure zero
subset and the possible emergence of an orbifold structure in higher dimensions.
In dimension
5
we prove:

(1)

Refined rectifiability.

We prove that the entire singular set is contained in a countable union of points and bi-Lipschitz images of subsets of
\mathbb{R}
, without discarding any
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-measure zero
subset.
This refinement relies on the uniqueness of local tangent cones: while
[
CJN21
,
JN21
]
established uniqueness
\mathcal{H}^{n-4}
-almost everywhere, in dimension
5
we prove uniqueness on the
entire
top stratum, hence away from a countable collection of points.

(2)

Orbifold regularity.

We extend the orbifold regularity to dimension
5
and formulate a
codimension-five orbifold conjecture
for Einstein limits.
In particular, the singular curves, away from a
nowhere-dense
subset
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{orb}}
, are geodesic and possess a real-analytic
orbifold
structures with
bounded curvature
. By classifying all geodesics in the limit space
(X,d)
, we also obtain more metric geometry properties of the limit space, generalizing
[
CoN12
]
. Namely, the orbifold-regular set
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{orb}}:=X\setminus\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{orb}}
is an open
convex
orbifold, and the orbifold-singular set
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{orb}}
is the set of
extreme points
of the limit space. Since Einstein metrics are real-analytic, we further conjecture – by unique continuation – that
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{orb}}
is countable.
This will be discussed in detail in Section
1.3
.

Many of the results above
require only bounded Ricci curvature, and less central statements hold in all dimensions
n\geqslant 5
. See
[
Col96a
,
Col96b
,
CT06
,
Col12
,
CN13
]
for more developments in this direction.

1.2.
Main results

1.2.1.
Gap theorems for Einstein
4
-manifolds

We first show the isolation of all hyperbolic or spherical
4
-orbifolds, i.e. spaces with isolated flat conical singularities and sectional curvatures constant equal to
\pm 1
.

Theorem 1.1

(Isolation theorem)
.

For any compact hyperbolic or spherical
4
-
orbifold

(M_{o},g_{o})
with isolated singularities, there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that if an Einstein
orbifold

(M,g)
with
{\rm Ric}(g)=\pm 3g
satisfies

d_{GH}\left((M,g),(M_{o},g_{o})\right)<\delta_{0},

then
(M,g)
is
isometric
to
(M_{o},g_{o})
.

This result extends that of
[
Ozu21b
]
that was only dealing with smooth metrics close to orbifolds with simplest singularities
\mathbb{R}^{4}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. The present article deals with much more challenging general case which allows formation of
trees of singularities
which substantially complicate the problem and require new ideas.

Remark 1.2
.

There are Ricci-flat
4
-manifolds approaching flat orbifolds
[
LS94
]
on
K3
, but not on any topology
[
BK17
,
Ozu21a
]
.

Theorem
1.1
yields gap theorems for Einstein
4
-orbifolds with no extension to higher dimensions.

Corollary 1.3

(Gap theorems)
.

For all
v>0
, there exists
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(v)>0
such that if an Einstein
4
-orbifold
(M,g)
with
{\rm Ric}(g)=3g
and
{\rm Vol}(M,g)>v
satisfies one of the following:

(1)

(Almost Bonnet-Myers theorem)

{\rm diam}(M,g)>\pi-\varepsilon_{0}
,

(2)

(Almost Obata-Lichnerowicz theorem)
the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
of the Laplacian satisfies
\lambda_{1}<4+\varepsilon_{0}
,

(3)

(Almost conformally flat)

\|W_{g}\|_{L^{1}}<\varepsilon_{0}
,

(4)

(Almost Lévy–Gromov theorem)
there exists some
t\in(17^{-17},1-17^{-17})
with
\big|I_{(M,g)}(t)-I_{\mathbb{S}^{4}}(t)\big|\leqslant\varepsilon_{0}
, where
I
denotes the isoperimetric profile
I_{(M,g)}(t):=\inf\big\{\mathrm{Per}(\Omega):\Omega\subset M,{\rm Vol}_{g}(\Omega)=t\,{\rm Vol}_{g}(M)\big\},

(5)

(Almost Bishop-Gromov theorem)

(M,g)
has a singularity modeled on
\mathbb{R}^{4}/\Gamma
and
{\rm Vol}(M)>\frac{|\mathbb{S}^{4}|}{|\Gamma|}-\varepsilon_{0}
,

then
(M,g)
is a spherical
4
-orbifold. In all cases but (3),
(M,g)
is isometric to a spherical suspension over
\mathbb{S}^{3}/\Gamma
for some finite
\Gamma\subset O(4)
.

Remark 1.4
.

From
[
CTZ12
]
, spherical 4-orbifolds are spherical suspensions over
\mathbb{S}^{3}/\Gamma
and their
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-quotient.

Remark 1.5
.

Each gap theorem above strengthens well-known rigidity theorems for spherical suspensions. For (1) use the maximal-diameter theorem of Cheng
[
Che75
]
, for (2) use the Lichnerowicz–Obata theorem
[
Lic58
,
Oba62
]
, for (4) use the Lévy–Gromov estimate
[
Gro81
]
(see a survey
[
Ant25
]
), for (5) use Bishop–Gromov based at the singular point
[
BC64
,
Gro81
]
. In the RCD setting, equality yields a spherical suspension; see for instance
[
Ket15
,
CaM17
]
. Note also that the
L^{1}
smallness in (3) is implied by the
L^{p}
smallness with any
p\geqslant 1
and that gap theorems for smooth Einstein
n
-manifolds with small
L^{\frac{n}{2}}
norm of their Weyl tensors are proven in
[
Sin92
,
She90
,
IS02
]
.

Remark 1.6
.

Generalizations of Corollary
1.3
fail in dimension
5
and higher: in
[
Böh98
]
, Böhm constructed a sequence of Einstein
5
-metrics
(S^{5},g_{i})
with
{\rm Ric}(g_{i})=4g_{i}
and volume uniformly bounded below such that
(S^{5},g_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
converges in the Gromov-Hausdorff sense to a spherical suspension where the diameter and the first eigenvalue of the
(S^{5},g_{i})
are arbitrarily close to
\pi
and
5
respectively, and supposedly, since the singularities forming along the sequence are conical, we should have: for
1\leqslant p<\frac{5}{2}
,
\|W\|_{L^{p}}\to 0
.

Remark 1.7
.

Imposing loose bounds on
{\rm Ric}
is not sufficient to obtain (almost) rigidity: by
[
Ozu22b
, Remark 51]
, there exist smooth metrics
(\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2},g_{t})_{t\in(0,1)}
with
3g_{t}\leqslant{\rm Ric}(g_{t})\leqslant 6.1g_{t}
converging to a spherical orbifold.
The metrics
g_{t}
satisfy
{\rm diam}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2},g_{t})\to\pi
, one can show that the first eigenvalue tends to
4
, and for any
p\in[1,+\infty)
the
L^{p}
-norm of the Weyl curvature converges to zero as
t\to 0
.

Remark 1.8
.

The constants in our gap theorems of Corollary
1.3
are not explicit since they are proven by contradiction. Gap theorems with explicit constant, such as
[
Gur00
]
are exceptionally rare. See related discussion of gap theorems in
[
HM25
]
.

1.2.2.
Tangent cones of Einstein
5
-manifolds

Our main new tool to study the regularity of noncollapsed limits of Einstein
5
-manifolds is the following result.

Theorem 1.9

(Isolation of
1
-symmetric Ricci-flat cones)
.

For all
v>0
, there exists
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(v)>0
such that the following holds.

Let
(C(Y),d_{C(Y)},y)
and
(C(Z),d_{C(Z)},z)
be two metric cones that are limits of sequences of pointed 5-manifolds
(M_{i},g_{i},p_{i})
and
(N_{i},\tilde{g}_{i},q_{i})
both satisfying
|{\rm Ric}_{i}|\to 0
and that the balls centered at
p_{i},q_{i}
of radius
1
have volume bounded below by
v>0
. Suppose that
(C(Z),d_{C(Z)},z)
isometrically splits off a line and

d_{GH}(Y,Z)<\varepsilon_{0}.

Then the cones are isometric:
(C(Y),d_{C(Y)},y)\cong(C(Z),d_{C(Z)},z)\cong(\mathds{R}\times C(\mathbb{S}^{3}/\Gamma),g_{0},0)
for
\Gamma\subset O(4)
finite.

Remark 1.10
.

Once again, the higher-dimensional analogous statement cannot hold by considering cones over the examples of
[
Böh98
]
.

In particular, if these two cones arise as tangent cones at some point, this quantitative isolation directly implies the uniqueness of tangent cones splitting off a line.

Theorem 1.11

(Uniqueness of tangent cones)
.

Let
(M_{i},g_{i},p_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of pointed
5
-manifolds with
|{\rm Ric}|<4
and
{\rm Vol}(B_{1}(p_{i}))>v>0

d_{GH}
-converging to
(X,d,p)
. Assume that
(\mathbb{R}\times C(Y),d_{\mathbb{R}\times C(Y)})
is a tangent cone at
x\in X
, then it is the
unique
tangent cone at
x
. In particular, except for countably many points, the tangent cone is uniquely isometric to
\mathds{R}\times C(\mathbb{S}^{3}/\Gamma)
where
\Gamma\subset O(4)
acts freely.

Moreover, we obtain quantitative regularity on a wedge region
\mathcal{W}(x)
around any point
x\in X
whose tangent cone splits off a line.
The wedge region, defined in (
4.3
), consists of a neighborhood of
x
with a scaling-invariant tubular neighborhood of the splitting axis removed.

Corollary 1.12
.

If one tangent cone at
x\in X
splits, then there exists some wedge region
\mathcal{W}(x)
around
x
and constant
\rho>0
such that the harmonic radius (see for instance
[
And90
]
for a definition),
r_{h}(y)
, at the point
y\in\mathcal{W}(x)
has a lower bound
r_{h}(y)\geq\rho\cdot d(x,y)
. If we further assume that the sequence
M_{i}
is
Einstein
, then we have the curvature estimate
|{\rm Rm}|(y)=o\big(d(x,y)^{-2}\big)
in
\mathcal{W}(x)
.

We next describe the asymptotic geometry of complete, noncollapsed, asymptotically Ricci-flat
5
-manifolds with Euclidean volume growth.
When a tangent cone at infinity splits off a line, then it is
unique
.

Theorem 1.13
.

Let
(M,g,p)
be a 5-manifold with Euclidean volume growth and satisfy
\sup_{B_{2R}(p)\setminus B_{R}(p)}|{\rm Ric}|=o(R^{-2})
. If one of the tangent cones at infinity splits, then this is the
unique
tangent cone at infinity.

Finally, we obtain curvature control near infinity.

Corollary 1.14
.

Let
(M,g,p)
be a 5-manifold with Euclidean volume growth and satisfy
\sup_{B_{2R}(p)\setminus B_{R}(p)}|{\rm Ric}|=o(R^{-2})
. If one of the tangent cones at infinity splits, then there exists some wedge region around infinity
\mathcal{W}(\infty)
and constant
\rho>0
such that the harmonic radius at the point
y\in\mathcal{W}(\infty)
has a lower bound
r_{h}(y)\geq\rho\cdot d(p,y)
. If we further assume that the manifold
M
is
Ricci-flat
, then we have the curvature estimate
|{\rm Rm}|(y)=o\big(d(p,y)^{-2}\big)
in
\mathcal{W}(\infty)
.

1.2.3.
Singular set of noncollapsed limits of Einstein
5
-manifolds, and orbifold structure

We refine the structure of the singular set
\mathcal{S}
, and the regularity of the Gromov-Hausdorff limit space
X
. By the uniqueness of
1
-symmetric tangent cones
1.11
, we can further stratify the singular set
\mathcal{S}(X)
as follows:

Theorem 1.15
.

There exists some
N_{0}=N_{0}(v)
depending on the noncollapsing constant
v
such that

\mathcal{S}(X)=\mathcal{S}^{0}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{2\leqslant|\Gamma|\leqslant N_{0}}\mathcal{S}^{1}_{\Gamma},

(1.2)

where
\mathcal{S}_{\Gamma}^{1}
is the set of points whose unique tangent cone is
\mathds{R}\times\mathds{R}^{4}/\Gamma
,
\Gamma\subset O(4)
and
\mathcal{S}^{0}
is a set of countably many points where any tangent cone never splits.

Our first result refines the classical rectifiability statement of
[
CJN21
,
JN21
]
, eliminating the need to discard an
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-measure zero
subset:

Theorem 1.16

(Singular set contained in Lipschitz manifolds)
.

Let
(M_{i},g_{i},p_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of pointed
5
-manifolds with
|{\rm Ric}|<4
and
{\rm Vol}(B_{1}(p_{i}))>v>0
. Let
(X,d,p)
be a Gromov-Hausdorff limit of the sequence. Then the singular set
\mathcal{S}
satisfies: There exist countably many points
q_{j}
and measurable subsets
Z_{i}\subset\mathcal{S}^{1}_{\Gamma_{i}}
with bi-Lipschitz maps
\phi_{i}:Z_{i}\to\mathds{R}
such that

\mathcal{S}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}Z_{i}\,\,\cup\,\,\bigcup_{j=0}^{\infty}\{q_{j}\}.

(1.3)

Remark 1.17
.

This resolves, in dimension
5
and under a two-sided Ricci bound, the analogue of
[
Nab20
, Conjecture 2.15]
. The original conjecture concerns lower Ricci bounds.

The points
\{q_{j}\}
in fact coincide with the set of
0
-symmetric singularities
\mathcal{S}^{0}
. Theorem
1.16
says that all of the
1
-symmetric singular points lie on Lipschitz curves. It turns out that we understand the singularities completely when they exhaust such a curve in
\mathcal{S}^{1}_{\Gamma}
: they are orbifold singularities along geodesics and the curvature stays bounded. In other words: the curve of singularities in
\mathcal{S}^{1}_{\Gamma}
is
removable
. More generally, given
any
curve inside the singular set
\mathcal{S}
, we have the following structure theorem:

Theorem 1.18

(Structure of curves of singularities)
.

Let
(M_{i},g_{i},p_{i})
be a sequence of pointed
Einstein

5
-manifolds with
|{\rm Ric}|<4
and
{\rm Vol}(B_{1}(p_{i}))>v>0
. Let
(X,d,p)
be a Gromov-Hausdorff limit of the sequence. Assume that there exists a unit-speed curve
\gamma:I=(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\to X
contained in the singular set,
\gamma(I)\subset\mathcal{S}(X)
, for some
\varepsilon>0
. Then, there exists a relatively closed
nowhere dense
subset
\mathcal{I}\subset I
such that
I=\mathcal{I}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{\infty}(s_{j},s_{j+1})
satisfies:

•

on each
(s_{j},s_{j+1})
, there exists a finite group
\Gamma_{j}\subset O(4)
such that at all of the points in
\gamma(s_{j},s_{j+1})
, the tangent cone is unique and isometric to
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{4}/\Gamma_{j}
,

•

there exist an open sets
U_{j}\subset X
with
\gamma\big((s_{j},s_{j+1})\big)\subset U_{j}
such that on
U_{j}\setminus\gamma
the Einstein metric extends
real-analytically
across
\gamma
as a real-analytic
5
-orbifold metric with singularities
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{4}/\Gamma_{j}
along
\gamma
. In particular, the curvature stays bounded.

•

\gamma_{|(s_{j},s_{j+1})}
is a unit-speed geodesic of
X
.

If
\gamma(I)\subset\mathcal{S}^{1}_{\Gamma}
, then
\mathcal{I}=\emptyset
,
\gamma
is a geodesic and the metric extends as a real-analytic orbifold across it.

Theorem
1.18
characterizes any curve of singularities up to a nowhere dense subset: they are open geodesic segments. We now turn to the converse and completely characterize neighborhoods of open geodesic segments of our limit spaces.

By establishing the sharp Hölder continuity of tangent cones along geodesics in a seminal work
[
CoN12
]
, Colding-Naber, and later generalized by Deng
[
Den25
]
, classified the geodesics (meaning locally minimizing, in the metric sense) with one regular point: the whole interior part is regular. Combining Theorems
1.11
,
1.18
and their estimates, we can now fully classify the geodesics by one of their interior points:

Theorem 1.19

(Classification of geodesics)
.

Let
\sigma:[0,l]\to X
be a geodesic in a noncollapsed Einstein
5
-dimensional limit space
(X,d)
with
|{\rm Ric}_{M_{i}}|\leqslant 4
. Then either
\sigma\big((0,l)\big)
is regular, or the entire
\sigma\big((0,l)\big)\subset\mathcal{S}^{1}_{\Gamma}
for some
\Gamma\subset O(4)
and the interior of the curve is removable in the sense of Theorem
1.18
.

This implies that the entire interior of a geodesic has isometric tangent cones
\mathds{R}\times\mathds{R}^{4}/\Gamma
for some fixed
\Gamma\subset O(4)

Finally we discuss the orbifold regularity of the limit space

Combining the refined rectifiability Theorem

The orbifold-regular subset

•

1.4.
Strategy and Organization

The article is divided into two parts, the first one concerns gap theorems for Einstein orbifolds, and the second the regularity of noncollapsed Einstein
5
-manifolds.

1.4.1.
Isolation theorem for Einstein
4
-manifolds

We first outline the proof of the isolation theorem for spherical and hyperbolic Einstein
4
-orbifolds, Theorem
1.1
.

Setup and refined approximation.

Assume towards a contradiction that there exists a sequence of Einstein manifolds
(M_{i},g_{i})_{i}
converging in the Gromov-Hausdorff (GH) sense to a spherical or hyperbolic Einstein
4
-orbifold
(M_{o},g_{o})
. By the GH-to-smooth theory of
[
Ozu22a
,
Ozu22b
]
, each
g_{i}
is smoothly approximated by a naïve gluing metric
g_{i}^{D}
capturing all bubble trees of ALE Ricci-flat pieces. A key new input is a
refined
obstruction space adapted to these trees. We first glue and perturb every bubble tree to a
Ricci-flat modulo obstructions
configuration, and then upgrade
g_{i}^{D}
to an
Einstein modulo obstructions
approximation
g_{i}^{A}
with an explicit refined obstruction
w_{i}^{A}
supported on the bubble region.

Quantitative control.

In appropriate, divergence-free gauges, the linearization of the Einstein (mod. obstructions) map is invertible between specific weighted Hölder spaces, hence, by inverse function theorem,

\|g_{i}^{A}-g_{i}\|+\|w_{i}^{A}\|\;\lesssim\;\|{\rm Ric}(g_{i}^{A})-\Lambda g_{i}^{A}+w_{i}^{A}\|,

(1.5)

where
\Lambda\in\{\pm 3\}
is the Einstein constant of
(M_{o},g_{o})
. Thus, to reach a contradiction, it suffices to prove that
\|w_{i}^{A}\|
cannot be dominated by the right-hand side.

Decomposition of the obstruction.

A precise analysis of the refined obstruction shows a canonical splitting

w_{i}^{A}=u_{i}^{B}+u_{i}^{o},

where
u_{i}^{B}
is the obstruction to perturbing the bubble trees toward Ricci-flat metrics, and
u_{i}^{o}
is the obstruction created when matching the trees to the core orbifold
(M_{o},g_{o})
. When
(M_{o},g_{o})
is spherical or hyperbolic, then
u_{i}^{o}
is nonvanishing. The main conceptual step of the proof then consists in proving that if
u_{i}^{B}
compensates
u_{i}^{o}
, then it contains a much larger piece orthogonal to
u_{i}^{o}
. In either case this contradicts (
1.5
):

\|w_{i}^{A}\|\ \gg\ \|{\rm Ric}(g_{i}^{A})-\Lambda g_{i}^{A}+w_{i}^{A}\|.

Conclusion.
No Einstein orbifold can be GH-close to
(M_{o},g_{o})
unless it is isometric to it.

1.4.2.
Regularity of Einstein
5
-manifolds

We then focus on the regularity of Einstein
5
-manifolds.

Uniqueness of tangent cones.

In the 5-dimensional limit space, we first establish an isolation theorem for
1
-symmetric cones, building on the gap theorems for Einstein
4
-orbifolds.
This, in particular, yields the uniqueness of
1
-symmetric tangent cones.
Combined with
\varepsilon
-regularity, we then obtain quantitative curvature estimates on wedge regions around such points, and analogous results hold for tangent cones at infinity and their asymptotic curvature behavior. These results are proved in Section
4.2
.

Refined rectifiability.

The isolation theorem implies that
1
-symmetry at a given scale propagates to
uniform symmetry
across all smaller scales.
Using the cone-splitting principle and
\varepsilon
-regularity, this uniform symmetry allows the construction of almost splitting maps whose restrictions to the singular set are bi-Lipschitz onto intervals.
A countable selection of such maps yields a covering of
\mathcal{S}^{1}
by bi-Lipschitz images of subsets of
\mathbb{R}
, thereby establishing a
refined rectifiability
of the singular set without discarding any exceptional subsets.
Moreover, we show that along each curve with constant volume density, the tangent cones remain identical. The proof is given in Section
4.3
.

Orbifold structure.

Along a Lipschitz curve of singularity type
\mathds{R}\times\mathds{R}^{4}/\Gamma
, GH-close model charts to
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{4}/\Gamma
can be
glued horizontally
along the direction parallel to the singular curve. Controlling the gluing error uniformly is subtle: it requires compatibility of the local
\Gamma
-actions on adjacent charts. Crucially, the transition maps are constrained to be approximated only by the reflection or translation along the singular axis
\mathds{R}
and rotation preserving the
\Gamma
-action on
\mathds{R}^{4}/\Gamma
– a property that fails in flat
\mathds{R}^{5}
. These glued charts then yield
C^{0}
orbifold-type coordinates on punctured neighborhoods.

To control the curvature in these necks, we apply Hardy-type inequalities and a Moser iteration for subsolutions of the following equation

\Delta u\gtrsim-|y|^{-2}u

(1.6)

with
|y|
the distance to the singular curve. Equation (
1.6
) is satisfied by
u=|{\rm Rm}|^{1-\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0
by 1)
\varepsilon
-regularity and our isolation theorem for
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{4}/\Gamma
cones, 2) the differential equation satisfied by curvature on Einstein metrics, 3) an improved Kato inequality. This gives uniform
pointwise
curvature bounds. Once curvature is uniformly bounded, we upgrade the coordinate regularity to
C^{1,\alpha}
, and then to analyticity via the Einstein equations.
This establishes the analytic orbifold structure along the singular curves. Thanks to these coordinates, the singular curve, which is the fixed-point set of the
\Gamma
-action, is a geodesic. The details are presented in Section
5
.

5.1.

In the first step, we establish the

In the case of isolated singularities, one typically constructs a diffeomorphism between an annulus surrounding the singular point and a corresponding annulus in the tangent cone. These diffeomorphisms are then glued together along the radial direction. The uniqueness of tangent cone then ensures that the error decays as the radial scale tends to zero.

Several key new difficulties have to be addressed in the case of a higher-dimensional singular set. At each fixed radial scale, one must first glue together the various local blocks along the directions parallel to the singular set. As the radial scale tends to zero, the number of such blocks increases, making uniform estimates essential to prevent error accumulation. Moreover, the gluing procedure must be carefully implemented to avoid shrinking the annuli in proportion to the number of blocks. We will overcome these difficulties in Lemma

In this section we prove that the curvature is bounded around the singular curve

and rescaling, we can simply assume that there exists some small

d_{GH}(B_{r}(x),B_{r}(0,x^{\prime}))\leqslant\eta r,\text{ for any }x\in\gamma([-1,1])\text{ and any }r\leqslant 1.

(5.6)

The main theorem of this section is the following uniform
L^{\infty}
curvature bound:

Theorem 5.6
.

If
\eta
is small enough, then for any integer
k\geq 0
, there exists some constant
C_{k}(\eta)
such that

\sup_{B_{1}(x_{0})}|\nabla^{k}{\rm Rm}|\leqslant C_{k}(\eta).

(5.7)

By Proposition

, we have a
C^{0}
coordinate chart for the ball
B_{1}(x_{0})
and the metric is
C^{0}
-close to the standard metric in
\mathds{R}\times\mathds{R}^{4}/\Gamma
. Since we will focus on the curvature estimate in this section, without loss of generality we study the universal cover of the ball
B_{1}(x_{0})
. The sobolev inequalities hold in
B_{1}(x_{0})
thanks to
[
Cro80
]
. To keep track of the dimensional constants, we will work in any dimension
n\geq 5
in this section.

We define
B\equiv B_{1}^{x}(0^{n-4})\times B_{1}^{y}(0^{4})
and the ball minus a segment
B^{*}\equiv B\setminus(B_{1}^{x}(0^{n-4})\times\{0\})
. Also, we let
\nabla_{x},\nabla_{y}
denote the gradient along
x,y
direction respectively.

In the following we consider the equation which holds in
B^{*}
:

\Delta u\geqslant-fu.

(5.8)

It will be applied to
f=|{\rm Rm}|
and both
u=|\nabla^{k}{\rm Rm}|
or
u=|{\rm Rm}|^{1-\alpha}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
and some dimensional
\alpha
.

We will adapt Moser’s iteration argument to prove that
|{\rm Rm}|
is bounded. To achieve this, we first prove the following Caccioppoli type inequality, motivated by
[
Sib85
]
.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
u\in C^{1}(B^{*})
and
u\geqslant 0
satisfying the equation (
5.8
) in the sense of distributions. Suppose
u\in L^{2q}
for some
q\geqslant 1+\varepsilon>1
and
|f(x,y)|\leqslant\delta|y|^{-2}
for some
\delta\leqslant\delta_{0}(\varepsilon,q)
. Then for any
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B)
we have

\displaystyle\int_{B}\eta^{2}|\nabla u^{q}|^{2}\leqslant C(\varepsilon)q\int_{B}|\nabla\eta|^{2}u^{2q}.

(5.9)

Proof.

By integration by parts, the following holds for any function
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B^{*})
with compact support in
B^{*}

\int_{B}\nabla\chi\cdot\nabla u\leqslant\int_{B}\chi fu.

(5.10)

We define the following test function for some large constant
K>0

F(t)=\frac{t^{q}}{(1+\frac{t}{K})^{q/2}}

(5.11)

whose motivation is that if
u\in L^{2q}
, then
F(u)\in L^{4}
since we temper the large values of the function.

Note that the function
F
is
C^{\infty}
for
t>0
. We define
G(t)\equiv F(t)F^{\prime}(t)
. Then the following properties hold by direct computation:

\displaystyle F(t)

\displaystyle\leqslant K^{q/2}t^{q/2};

(5.12)

\displaystyle tG(t)

\displaystyle\leqslant qF(t)^{2};

(5.13)

\displaystyle G^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\geqslant c(\varepsilon)F^{\prime}(t)^{2}.

(5.14)

Now we pick
\eta,\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B)
and further let
\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon}
vanish in
B_{1}^{x}\times B_{\varepsilon}^{y}
. Choose the cutoff function
\chi\equiv(\eta\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon})^{2}G(u)
and by (
5.10
) and (
5.13
) we have

\displaystyle\int_{B}\nabla\Big((\eta\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon})^{2}G(u)\Big)\cdot\nabla u\leqslant\int_{B}(\eta\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon})^{2}G(u)fu\leqslant q\int_{B}(\eta\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon})^{2}|f||F(u)|^{2}.

(5.15)

Note that
\nabla F(u)=F^{\prime}(u)\nabla u
and
\nabla G(u)=G^{\prime}(u)\nabla u
. Hence by (
5.14
) we have
\nabla G(u)\cdot\nabla u=|\nabla u|^{2}G^{\prime}(u)\geqslant c(\varepsilon)F^{\prime}(u)^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}=c(\varepsilon)|\nabla F(u)|^{2}
. Hence

\displaystyle\int_{B}\nabla\Big((\eta\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon})^{2}G(u)\Big)\cdot\nabla u

\displaystyle=\int_{B}(\eta\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon})^{2}\nabla G(u)\cdot\nabla u+\int_{B}G(u)\nabla(\eta\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon})^{2}\cdot\nabla u

(5.16)

\displaystyle\geqslant c(\varepsilon)\int_{B}(\eta\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon})^{2}|\nabla F(u)|^{2}-\int_{B}|2\eta\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon}F^{\prime}(u)\nabla u|\cdot|F(u)\nabla(\eta\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon})|

(5.17)

\displaystyle=c(\varepsilon)\int_{B}(\eta\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon})^{2}|\nabla F(u)|^{2}-\int_{B}|2\eta\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon}\nabla F(u)|\cdot|F(u)\nabla(\eta\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon})|

(5.18)

Combining these and (
5.15
) we obtain

\displaystyle c(\varepsilon)\int_{B}(\eta\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon})^{2}|\nabla F(u)|^{2}\leqslant\int_{B}|2\eta\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon}\nabla F(u)|\cdot|\nabla(\eta\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon})F(u)|+q\int_{B}(\eta\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon})^{2}|f|F(u)^{2}\equiv I_{1}+I_{2}.

(5.19)

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, for some
c^{\prime}(\varepsilon)
we have

I_{1}\leqslant\frac{c(\varepsilon)}{2}\int_{B}(\eta\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon})^{2}|\nabla F(u)|^{2}+c^{\prime}(\varepsilon)\int_{B}|\nabla(\eta\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon})|^{2}F(u)^{2}.

(5.20)

To estimate
I_{2}
, we apply Hardy inequality to get

I_{2}\leqslant\delta q\int_{B_{1}^{x}}\int_{B_{1}^{y}}(\eta\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon})^{2}F(u)^{2}|y|^{-2}dydx\leqslant 4\delta q\int_{B_{1}^{x}}\int_{B_{1}^{y}}|\nabla_{y}(\eta\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon}\cdot F(u))|^{2}(x,y)dydx.

(5.21)

Note that
|\nabla_{y}F(u)|^{2}\leqslant|\nabla F(u)|^{2}
. Hence by choosing
\delta\leqslant\delta_{0}(\varepsilon,q)
small, we can absorb all terms involving
|\nabla F|
into the left hand side of (
5.19
) and thus obtain

\int_{B}(\eta\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon})^{2}|\nabla F(u)|^{2}\leqslant C(\varepsilon)q\int_{B}|\nabla(\eta\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon})|^{2}F^{2}(u).

(5.22)

Here is the only place where we require
\delta
to be small depending on
q
.

Note that we can choose
\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon}
such that
\eta\cdot\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon}\to\eta
and
\int_{B\cap\mathrm{supp}(\eta)}|\nabla\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon}|^{4}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
since
B\setminus\mathrm{supp}(\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon})\subset B_{1}^{x}\times B_{2\varepsilon}^{y}\to B_{1}^{x}\times\{0\}\subset\mathds{R}^{n-4}\times\{0\}
. Combining
F(u)\leqslant K^{q/2}u^{q/2}
and
u\in L^{2q}
, we have
F(u)\in L^{4}
and thus

\int_{B}|\nabla\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\eta^{2}F^{2}(u)\leqslant C(K)\Bigg(\int_{B}|\nabla\bar{\eta}_{\varepsilon}|^{4}\Bigg)^{1/2}\cdot\Bigg(\int_{B}F(u)^{4}\Bigg)^{1/2}\to 0,\text{ as }\varepsilon\to 0.

(5.23)

Combining all, we can now conclude that

\int_{B}\eta^{2}|\nabla F(u)|^{2}\leqslant C(\varepsilon)q\int_{B}|\nabla\eta|^{2}F^{2}(u).

(5.24)

Finally we let
K\to\infty
. Note that
F(u)\to u^{q}
. The proof is finished by Fatou’s Lemma. ∎

As a consequence, we get higher integrability of
u
whenever
\delta
is small enough.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
u\in C^{1}(B^{*})
and
u\geqslant 0
satisfying the equation (

) in the sense of distributions. Suppose

for some

for some

\displaystyle||u^{q}||_{L^{2N}(B_{1/2})}\leqslant C(n,\varepsilon,N)||u^{q}||_{L^{2}(B_{1})}

(5.25)

Proof.

We will prove it using Sobolev inequality and iteration argument. We assume

. Otherwise, there is nothing to prove.

Let
v_{0}=u^{q}
. Define
v_{k}=v_{k-1}^{\frac{n}{n-2}}=u^{q\cdot(\frac{n}{n-2})^{k}}
. We further define the radii

. Then we can define the cutoff functions
\eta_{k}\equiv 1

and vanishing outside

By Sobolev inequality in

and (

\displaystyle||v_{0}||_{L^{2^{*}}(B_{r_{1}})}\leqslant||\eta_{0}v_{0}||_{L^{2^{*}}(B_{1})}\leqslant C||\nabla(\eta_{0}v_{0})||_{L^{2}(B_{1})}\leqslant C(||\eta_{0}\nabla v_{0}||_{L^{2}(B_{1})}+||v_{0}\nabla\eta_{0}||_{L^{2}(B_{1})})\leqslant C_{0}(n,\varepsilon,N)||v_{0}\nabla\eta_{0}||_{L^{2}(B_{1})}.

This implies that
v_{0}\in L^{2^{*}}(B_{r_{1}})
, equivalently

. If

, we finish the proof. Otherwise, we can apply (

) and Sobolev inequality again to

5.3.
Analytic orbifold coordinates

Recall that in the previous section
5.1
, we construct the
C^{0}
coordinates around curves of singularities by gluing techniques. In this section, we will improve the regularity of the coordinate charts using the curvature estimate
|\nabla^{k}{\rm Rm}|\leqslant C
following
[
DS14
]
.

First let us recall our setting: Let
(X,p)
be a 5-dimensional noncollapsed limit space. Let
\gamma:[-2,2]\subset\mathds{R}\to X
be a 1-Lipschitz curve with
\gamma(0)=p
parametrized by arc length. Suppose each
\gamma(t)
have the same tangent cone
\mathds{R}\times\mathds{R}^{4}/\Gamma
with
\Gamma\neq id
. Then by Proposition
5.5
we know
B_{1}(p)\setminus\gamma
is a smooth manifold with metric
g
.

Let
\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}_{j}\equiv\{x\in X:d(x,\gamma)=j^{-1}\}
be the cylindrical hypersurface surrounding
\gamma
. The regularity of such cylinders is not good enough for higher order derivative estimates. So we need to construct a better set of cylinders. We define
\mathcal{C}_{0}\equiv(-1,1)\times\mathbb{S}^{3}\subset\mathds{R}\times\mathds{R}^{4}
to be the standard cylinder in
\mathds{R}^{5}
.

According to Lemma
5.4
, we have the following immediate corollary.

Lemma 5.11
.

There exists a sequence of smooth embeddings
f_{j}:\mathcal{C}_{0}=(-1,1)\times\mathbb{S}^{3}\to X
such that the following holds:

(1)

d_{GH}(\mathcal{C}_{f_{j}},\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}_{j})\cap B_{1}(p))\leqslant\varepsilon_{j}
, where
\mathcal{C}_{f_{j}}\equiv f_{j}(\mathcal{C}_{0})
;

(2)

\big|j^{2}f_{j}^{*}g-h_{0}\big|_{C^{4}_{h_{0}}}\leqslant\varepsilon_{j}
;

(3)

\big|j^{-1}A_{\mathcal{C}_{j}}-id\big|_{C^{3}_{h_{0}}}\leqslant\varepsilon_{j}
.

Then we can construct the analytic coordinate around the curve.

Theorem 5.12
.

There exists an analytic diffeomorphism
F:B^{*}\to B_{1}(p)\setminus\gamma
such that
F^{*}g
extends to an analytic metric on the whole
B
.

Proof.

First we define the maps
F_{j}:\mathcal{C}_{0}\times[j^{-1},1]
as follows:

F_{j}(z,r)=\exp_{f_{j}(z)}\Big((r-j^{-1})N(f_{j}(z))\Big).

where
z=(t,\theta)\in\mathcal{C}_{0}=(-1,1)\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
and
N(f_{j}(z))
is the unit outward normal vector of
\mathcal{C}_{f_{j}}
at
f_{j}(z)
.

We want to show that each
F_{j}
is a diffeomorphism from
\mathcal{C}_{0}\times[j^{-1},1]
to its image. To prove this, let
J(r)
a Jacobi field along the geodesic
\alpha_{z}(r)\equiv F_{j}(z,r)
. Since we have
|{\rm Rm}|\leqslant C
in
B_{1}(p)\setminus\gamma
by Theorem
5.6
, by Rauch comparison theorem there exists some constant
C>0
and

C^{-1}|J(j^{-1})|_{g}\leqslant|J(r)|_{g}\leqslant Cj|J(j^{-1})|_{g}.

Hence
F_{j}
has no critical point in
\mathcal{C}_{0}\times[j^{-1},1]
.

We can write the metric
F_{j}^{*}g=dr^{2}+r^{2}h_{j}(z,r)
. Let
h_{0}
be the standard metric on
\mathcal{C}_{0}=(-1,1)\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
and we will prove
h_{j}\to h_{0}
as
j\to\infty
in
C^{1,\alpha}
.

Given
z\in(-1,1)\times\mathbb{S}^{3}
and a unit tangential vector
\xi
at
z
. Consider the Jacobi field
J(r)
along the geodesic
\alpha_{z}(r)=F_{j}(z,r)
satisfying

(1)

J(j^{-1})=df_{j}(\xi)
;

(2)

J^{\prime}(j^{-1})=A_{\mathcal{C}_{j}}(J(j^{-1}))
.

This implies that
J(r)=dF_{j}|_{(z,r)}(\xi)
. Moreover, by Lemma
5.11
we have

(1)

\big||J(j^{-1})|_{g}-j^{-1}\big|\leqslant j^{-1}\varepsilon_{j}
;

(2)

\big||J^{\prime}(j^{-1})|_{g}-j|J(j^{-1})|_{g}\big|\leqslant 2\varepsilon_{j}
.

Let
\{e_{1}(r),...,e_{5}(r)=\alpha^{\prime}(r)\}
be the parallel orthonormal frame along
\alpha_{z}(r)
and write
J(r)=\sum_{k=1}^{5}J_{k}(r)e_{k}(r)
. Then by the Jacobi field equation we have

J_{k}^{\prime\prime}(r)+\sum_{l}R_{k5l5}|_{\alpha_{z}(r)}J_{l}(r)=0,

where
R_{k5l5}=\langle{\rm Rm}(e_{k},e_{5})e_{l},e_{5}\rangle
. Then

\begin{split}J_{k}(r)&=J_{k}(j^{-1})+J^{\prime}_{k}(j^{-1})\cdot(r-j^{-1})-\int_{j^{-1}}^{r}\int_{j^{-1}}^{s}R_{k5l5}|_{\alpha_{z}(t)}J_{k}(t)dtds.\end{split}

By Theorem
5.6
, we have
|{\rm Rm}|\leqslant C_{1}
. This implies that for any
r\in[j^{-1},1]
we have

\Big||J(r)|_{g}-r\Big|\leqslant C_{2}(j^{-1}+\varepsilon_{j}r+r^{3}).

Hence, at the metric level,

\Big|h_{j}-h_{0}\Big|_{C^{0}_{h_{0}}}\leqslant C_{2}(j^{-1}r^{-1}+\varepsilon_{j}+r^{2}).

By differentiating the Jacobi equation and the bound
|\nabla^{k}{\rm Rm}|\leqslant C(k)
we obtain the high order derivative estimates

\begin{split}r^{-1}\Big|\nabla^{g_{0}}(h_{j}-h_{0})\Big|_{C^{0}_{h_{0}}}\leqslant C_{3}(j^{-1}r^{-2}+\varepsilon_{j}r^{-1}+r);\\
r^{-2}\Big|\nabla^{g_{0}}\nabla^{g_{0}}(h_{j}-h_{0})\Big|_{C^{0}_{h_{0}}}\leqslant C_{4}(j^{-1}r^{-2}+\varepsilon_{j}r^{-1}+1).\end{split}

For any fixed
r\ll 1
, we let
j\to\infty
. Then the metric
F^{*}_{j}
converges in
C^{3}
to some map on
[r,1]\times\mathcal{C}_{0}
. Then we let
r\downarrow 0
and thus we obtain a limit map
F:(0,1]\times\mathcal{C}_{0}
such that the metric
F^{*}g
satisfies

r^{-2}\Big|F^{*}g-g_{0}\Big|_{C^{0}_{g_{0}}}+r^{-1}\Big|F^{*}g-g_{0}\Big|_{C^{1}_{g_{0}}}+\Big|F^{*}g-g_{0}\Big|_{C^{2}_{g_{0}}}\leqslant C_{5}.

This implies that
F^{*}g
extends to a
C^{1,1}
metric on the whole ball
B_{1}
. It is classical that if an Einstein metric is
C^{1,\alpha}
in some coordinates, then they can be upgraded to a better set of coordinates where the metric is real-analytic. This can be proven thanks to harmonic coordinates, or coordinates in Bianchi gauge or divergence-free gauge with respect to a background flat metric.
∎

5.4.
Structure for curves of singularities

We now prove Theorem
1.18
for curves of singularities.

Proof of Theorem
1.18
.

Let
\gamma:I\to\mathcal{S}(X)
be a singular curve and
\mathcal{V}(t)
be the volume density at the point
\gamma(t)
for
t\in I
. Since
\mathcal{V}
is lower semi-continuous with values in a finite set, then there exists a nowhere dense subset
\mathcal{I}\subset I
such that

(1)

I=\mathcal{I}\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}(s_{j},s_{j+1})
;

(2)

\mathcal{V}
is constant on each interval
(s_{j},s_{j+1})
.

By Corollary
4.16
, for each
j
there exists some
\Gamma_{j}
such that the tangent cones on each segment
\gamma((s_{j},s_{j+1}))
are all isometric to
\mathds{R}\times\mathds{R}^{4}/\Gamma_{j}
. Combining Theorem
5.6
and
5.12
, we can prove (3). To prove (2), note that the metric is invariant under the nontrivial group
\Gamma_{j}
acting by isometry and fixing a curve. It is a classical consequence that this curve must be a geodesic.
∎

5.5.
Geodesics on limits of Einstein
5
-manifolds

We conclude by classifying all geodesics in the limit space
X
.

In a seminal work
[
CoN12
]
, Colding and Naber proved the Hölder continuity of tangent cones along any
limit geodesic
that arises as the limit of geodesics in the sequence. More recently Deng
[
Den25
]
generalized the estimate to
any
geodesic in
X
, that is a curve locally minimizing the distance
d
.

Theorem 5.13
.

Let
\sigma:[0,l]\to X
be a geodesic in the limit space
X
. Then there exists
\alpha,C>0
such that given any
\delta>0
with
\delta l<s<t<l-\delta l
and
Y_{\sigma(s)},Y_{\sigma(t)}
tangent cones from the same sequence of rescalings, then we have

d_{GH}(B_{1}(Y_{\sigma(s)}),B_{1}(Y_{\sigma(t)}))<\frac{C}{\delta l}|s-t|^{\alpha}.

As an immediate consequence, if one point of the geodesic lies in the regular set
\mathcal{R}
, then the entire interior of the geodesic lies in
\mathcal{R}

[
CoN12
]
. In particular the regular set
\mathcal{R}
is convex. Using Theorem
5.13
together with our isolation Theorem
1.9
, we can now classify all geodesics in
X
:

Proof of Theorem
1.19
.

It suffices to consider the case where the entire
\sigma((0,l))\in\mathcal{S}
. By the stratification Theorem
1.15
, there must be some
\Gamma
and
t_{0}\in(0,l)
such that
\sigma(t_{0})\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{\Gamma}
since
\mathcal{S}^{0}
is countable. Then by Theorem
5.13
and isolation Theorem
1.9
, there exists some
\theta_{0}>0
such that the tangent cone at
\sigma(t)
is isometric to
\mathds{R}\times\mathds{R}^{4}/\Gamma
for any
t\in(t_{0}-\theta_{0},t_{0}+\theta_{0})
. By connectedness this implies that any interior point has the unique tangent cone
\mathds{R}\times\mathds{R}^{4}/\Gamma
. Therefore we can use Theorem
1.16
to prove that the interior of this geodesic is removable in the sense of Theorem
1.16
.
∎

Theorem
1.19
therefore provides a complete classification of the interior of all geodesics. We say that a geodesic is of
\Gamma
-type
if all the tangent cones are
\mathds{R}\times\mathds{R}^{4}/\Gamma
(we allow
\Gamma=\{id\}
). We now turn to the behavior of the end points. We prove that the tangent cones at end points can only have worse singularities. Similar results can be formulated for any curves thanks to Theorem
1.16
.

Proposition 5.14
.

Let
\sigma:[0,l]\to X
be a geodesic of
\Gamma
-type in
X
. Let
C(Z)
be a tangent cone at
\sigma(0)
. Then
Z
has at least one singularity of the type
\mathds{R}^{4}/\Gamma^{\prime}
with
|\Gamma^{\prime}|\geq|\Gamma|
.

Proof.

Let
C(Z)
be the tangent cone at
\sigma(0)
from the rescaling sequence
s_{i}
. By passing to a subsequence, we have
\sigma(s_{i})\to z
for some point
z\in Z
. Since
\sigma
is of
\Gamma
-type, by Bishop-Gromov monotonicity and Colding’s volume convergence theorem, the volume density at
z\in Z\subset C(Z)
cannot be larger than that of
\mathds{R}^{4}/\Gamma
. This completes the proof.
∎

Appendix A
Function spaces

For a tensor
s
, a point
x
,
\alpha>0
and a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
. The Hölder seminorm is defined as

[s]_{C^{\alpha}(g)}(x):=\sup_{\{y\in T_{x}M,|y|<\textup{inj}_{g}(x)\}}\Big|\frac{s(x)-s(\exp^{g}_{x}(y))}{|y|^{\alpha}}\Big|_{g}.

For orbifolds, we consider a norm which is bounded for tensors decaying at the singular points.

Definition A.1

(Weighted Hölder norms on an orbifold)
.

Let
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<\alpha<1
and
(M_{o},g_{o})
an orbifold and
S_{o}
a subset of its singularities. Then, for any tensor
s
on

, we define

\displaystyle\|s\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{\beta}(g_{o})}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{M_{o}}r_{o}^{-\beta}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{k}r_{o}^{i}|\nabla_{g_{o}}^{i}s|_{g_{o}}+r_{o}^{k+\alpha}[\nabla_{g_{o}}^{k}s]_{C^{\alpha}(g_{o})}\Big).

For ALE manifolds, we will consider a norm which is bounded for tensors decaying at infinity.

Definition A.2

(Weighted Hölder norms on an ALE orbifold)
.

Let
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<\alpha<1

Appendix B
Duality for vector fields

We will have to carefully understand the duality between elements of the kernel of
\delta_{e}\delta_{e}^{*}
, where we identify
1
-forms and vector fields through
X\sim g(X,\cdot)
. From
[
Ozu22b
, Section 3.1]
, which essentially follows
[
CT94
, Section 2]
, the homogeneous
1
-forms in
\ker\delta_{e}\delta_{e}^{*}
are of the form

(1)

r^{a^{\pm}_{j}}\psi
with
a^{\pm}_{j}:=\pm(1+j)
,
j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, where
\psi
is an eigenvector the Hodge Laplacian,

(2)

r^{b^{\pm}_{j}}d_{\mathbb{S}^{3}/\penalty\Gamma}\phi\;+\;b^{\pm}_{j}r^{b^{\pm}_{j}-1}\phi dr
,
b_{j}^{\pm}=-1\pm(1+j)
or

(3)

2r^{c^{\pm}_{j}+2}d_{\mathbb{S}^{3}/\penalty\Gamma}\phi\;+\;c^{\mp}_{j}r^{c^{\pm}_{j}+1}\phi dr
, with
c_{j}^{\pm}=-1\pm(1+j)
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
\phi
an eigenfunction of the Laplacian.

Consider for each of the above tensors,
(\phi_{i}^{j})_{i}
and
(\psi_{i}^{j})_{i}
orthonormal bases of eigentensors of the eigenspaces above. Denote
X_{a_{j}^{\pm},i}
,
X_{b_{j}^{\pm},i}
and
X_{c_{j}^{\pm},i}
the associated vector fields in
\ker\delta_{e}\delta_{e}^{*}
.

Among these tensors, those whose norm is in
r
correspond to:
a_{1}^{+}
,
b_{2}^{+}
and
c^{+}_{0}
. Those with a norm in
r^{-3}
correspond to
and those whose norm grows like
r^{-3}
are:
a_{1}^{-}
,
b_{0}^{-}
and
c^{-}_{2}
.

An application of
[
Ozu22b
, Lemma 3.4]
yields the following result.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
(N,g^{B})
be a tree of ALE orbifold. Then, for any linear combination
X_{1}:=\alpha X_{a_{1}^{+}}+\beta X_{b_{2}^{+}}+\gamma X_{c_{0}^{+}}
for
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists a unique vector field
\mathcal{X}_{1}
on
N
such that: for a cut-off
\chi
supported in a neighborhood of infinity of
N
where
g^{B}
has ALE coordinates,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\delta_{g^{B}}\delta_{g^{B}}^{*}\mathcal{X}_{1}=0,\\
&\mathcal{X}_{1}-\chi X_{1}\in r_{B}C^{3,\alpha}_{\delta},\text{ for }0<\delta<4\end{aligned}\right.

(B.1)

We additionally have:

\|\mathcal{X}_{1}-\chi X_{1}\|_{r_{B}C^{3,\alpha}_{\delta}}\leqslant C\|r^{-1}X_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(g_{e})}

(B.2)

There is a duality between tensors in
\ker\delta_{e}\delta^{*}_{e}
whose norms are growing in
r
and in
r^{-3}
. Namely, when integrating by parts
\int_{M}\delta\delta^{*}X(Y)-\delta\delta^{*}Y(X)
on an ALE manifold
M
, one gets a boundary term

B(X,Y):=\lim_{s\to\infty}\int_{\{r=s\}}\left(\delta^{*}X(Y,\partial_{r})-\delta^{*}Y(X,\partial_{r})\right).

It turns out that if
Y=Y^{3}+o(r^{-3})
and
X=X_{1}+o(r)
for
|Y^{3}|\propto r^{-3}
and
|X_{1}|\propto r
, then
B(X,Y)=B(X_{1},Y^{3})
defines an important coupling. Explicit computations show that for three positive constants
C_{a}
,
C_{b}
and
C_{c}
, one has: for any
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i},\gamma_{i},\alpha^{\prime}_{k},\beta^{\prime}_{k},\gamma^{\prime}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\sum_{i,k}B\left(\alpha_{i}X_{a_{1}^{+},i}+\beta_{i}X_{b_{2}^{+},i}+\gamma_{i}X_{c_{0}^{+},i}\,,\,\,\alpha^{\prime}_{k}X_{a_{1}^{-},k}+\beta^{\prime}_{k}X_{c_{2}^{-},k}+\gamma^{\prime}_{k}X_{b_{0}^{-},k}\right)=\sum_{i}C_{a}\alpha_{i}\alpha^{\prime}_{i}+C_{b}\beta_{i}\beta^{\prime}_{i}+C_{c}\gamma_{i}\gamma^{\prime}_{i}.

(B.3)

In other words, the bilinear form
B
essentially diagonalizes in the above basis. This is a tool to precisely estimate how large the terms in
r^{-3}
are in the expansion of vector fields in
r_{B}C^{3,\alpha}_{\delta,**}
.

Proposition B.2
.

Let
Y=Y^{3}+o(r^{-3})\in r_{B}C^{3,\alpha}_{\delta,**}
for
\delta>0
, with a decomposition
Y^{3}=\alpha^{\prime}_{k}X_{a_{1}^{-},k}+\beta^{\prime}_{k}X_{c_{2}^{-},k}+\gamma^{\prime}_{k}X_{b_{0}^{-},k}
, consider
X_{1}=\alpha_{i}X_{a_{1}^{+},i}+\beta_{i}X_{b_{2}^{+},i}+\gamma_{i}X_{c_{0}^{+},i}
and the associated
\mathcal{X}_{1}
from Lemma
B.1
. Then, one has

\int_{M}\delta\delta^{*}Y(\mathcal{X}_{1})=-\sum_{i}C_{a}\alpha_{i}\alpha^{\prime}_{i}+C_{b}\beta_{i}\beta^{\prime}_{i}+C_{c}\gamma_{i}\gamma^{\prime}_{i}.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of the following integration by parts: using (
B.3
) in the last line,

\displaystyle-\int_{M}\delta\delta^{*}Y(\mathcal{X}_{1})

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\delta\delta^{*}\mathcal{X}_{1}(Y)-\delta\delta^{*}Y(\mathcal{X}_{1})=B(Y^{3},X_{1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}C_{a}\alpha_{i}\alpha^{\prime}_{i}+C_{b}\beta_{i}\beta^{\prime}_{i}+C_{c}\gamma_{i}\gamma^{\prime}_{i}.

∎

Amplifying the antiferromagnetic spin Seebeck effect through topological magnons
Feodor Svetlanov Konomaev, Kjetil M. D. Hals

Abstract
Topological magnons emerge as topologically protected spin wave states at the edges of magnets. Here, we theoretically explore how these surface states can be harnessed to amplify the spin Seebeck effect (SSE) in antiferromagnets (AFMs) interfaced with normal metals (NMs). Based on a microscopic model of a kagome AFM, we demonstrate that broken mirror symmetry, combined with the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction (DMI), drives the system into a topological phase hosting spin-polarized magnons at the boundaries. Notably, linear response calculations reveal that in AFM/NM heterostructures, the topological magnons exhibit strong coupling to the metal’s charge carriers, resulting in a substantial enhancement of the SSE. The relative contribution of the topological magnons is found to be 4-5 times greater than that of the trivial magnon bands. Moreover, our results show that this enhancement is highly sensitive to the strength of the DMI.

A key implication of the SSE is its ability to convert heat into electricity through the spin degree of freedom. This heat-to-electricity conversion was first demonstrated in heterostructures composed of ferromagnetic insulators (FIs) and heavy metals (HMs)

First, they exhibit excellent scalability, where the power output can be increased by expanding the area of the thin-film heterostructure. Second, heterostructures are considerably simpler to fabricate than conventional thermocouple-based modules. However, despite these promising features, the efficiency of the spin-based generators remains too low for large-scale commercial applications

In the topologically nontrivial phase, the topological magnons manifest as coherent, propagating spin waves along the sample’s surface, offering energy-efficient channels for transporting spin currents. The topological spin excitations can be experimentally observed through the magnon thermal Hall effect

Since the topological magnons are localized at the boundaries, they are expected to strongly couple to the itinerant charge carriers of a metal interfaced with the magnetic insulator. This strong coupling can notably amplify interface phenomena, such as the SSE, significantly improving the overall heat-to-electricity conversion efficiency of the magnetic heterostructures.

In this work, we theoretically explore how spin-polarized topological manginess enhance the SSE in a kagome AFM interfaced with a normal metal (NM).
We demonstrate that the combination of broken mirror symmetry and a finite in-plane DMI is crucial for inducing both a net spin polarization of the magnons and driving the AFM into a topological phase.
Additionally, we present a comprehensive microscopic theory for calculating the SSE in heterostructures composed of coupled layers of kagome AFMs and NMs. Using this framework, we show that the topologically protected edge states strongly couple to the charge carriers at the AFM/NM interface, leading to a substantial enhancement of the SSE. Specifically, the contribution of the topological magnons to the spin current is typically 4–5 times greater than that of trivial states.
To date, few experiments have investigated the SSE in noncollinear AFMs
[
16
]
, such as kagome AFMs. Our findings show that these largely unexplored spin systems exhibit a distinct topological SSE, which offers a promising mechanism for optimizing the performance of spin-based thermoelectric generators.

II
Theory

The kagome AFM is described by the Hamiltonian
[
47
]

\mathcal{H}_{\text{AF}}=\mathcal{H}_{\text{E}}+\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{A}}+\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{DM}}\text{.}

(1)

Here,
\mathcal{H}_{\text{E}}=J\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}{\boldsymbol{S}}_{i}\cdot{\boldsymbol{S}}_{j}
represents the exchange interaction between the nearest neighbour spins
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{i}
and
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{j}
with the coupling strength
J>0

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{A}}=\sum_{i}\left(K_{\perp}({\boldsymbol{S}}_{i}\cdot\hat{{\boldsymbol{z}}})^{2}-K({\boldsymbol{S}}_{i}\cdot\hat{{\boldsymbol{n}}}_{i})^{2}\right)
characterizes the easy plane (
K_{\perp}>0
) and easy axes (
K>0

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{DM}}=\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}{\boldsymbol{D}}_{ij}\cdot({\boldsymbol{S}}_{i}\times{\boldsymbol{S}}_{j})
is the DMI between the nearest neighbor lattice sites
\langle ij\rangle
. The vectors
\hat{{\boldsymbol{n}}}_{i}
define the local easy axis at each lattice point
i
. In kagome AFMs, the three sublattice spins within a unit cell experience distinct easy axes, denoted as

\hat{{\boldsymbol{n}}}_{A}=[-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2},-\frac{1}{2},0]^{T}
,
\hat{{\boldsymbol{n}}}_{B}=[\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2},-\frac{1}{2},0]^{T}
and
\hat{{\boldsymbol{n}}}_{C}=[0,1,0]^{T}
(see Fig.
1

The DMI vectors

\begin{split}&{\boldsymbol{D}}_{AB}=D_{\perp}\hat{{\boldsymbol{z}}}+D_{\parallel}(\hat{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\times\hat{{\boldsymbol{z}}})\text{,}\\
&{\boldsymbol{D}}_{BC}=D_{\perp}\hat{{\boldsymbol{z}}}+D_{\parallel}(\hat{{\boldsymbol{b}}}\times\hat{{\boldsymbol{z}}})\text{,}\\
&{\boldsymbol{D}}_{CA}=D_{\perp}\hat{{\boldsymbol{z}}}+D_{\parallel}(\hat{{\boldsymbol{c}}}\times\hat{{\boldsymbol{z}}})\text{,}\end{split}

(2)

where
{\boldsymbol{a}}=a[-1,0,0]^{T}
is the vector that connects site
B
to site

,
{\boldsymbol{b}}=a[\frac{1}{2},-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2},0]^{T}

Due to the parallel component of the DMI, the spins acquire an out-of-plane tilting by an angle

\begin{split}\theta=-\frac{1}{2}\arctan\left(\frac{2\sqrt{3}D_{\parallel}}{3J+K_{\perp}+K-\sqrt{3}D_{\perp}}\right)\text{,}\end{split}

(3)

which is found by minimizing the energy of Eq. (
1

Consequently, in equilibrium, the sublattice spins are given by

For a mirror symmetric kagome lattice plane, the in-plane component
D_{\parallel}
vanishes, leaving only the out-of-plane component
D_{\perp}
.
In this case, the spins lie in

III
Results and discussion

The thermally excited magnons must, on average, carry a net spin angular momentum in order to pump a DC spin current into an adjacent metallic layer.
Thus, we begin by examining the spin polarization of the magnons.

The statistical average of the magnons’ spin polarization at temperature
T
is given by

\langle{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\mathrm{SW}}(T)\rangle=\sum_{n,{\boldsymbol{k}}}\langle{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\mathrm{SW};n,{\boldsymbol{k}}}\rangle n_{\mathrm{B}}(\varepsilon_{n}({\boldsymbol{k}}),T)\text{,}

(6)

where
n_{\mathrm{B}}(\varepsilon,T)=(e^{\varepsilon/k_{B}T}-1)^{-1}
is the Bose-Einstein distribution and
the quantity
\langle{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\mathrm{SW};n,{\boldsymbol{k}}}\rangle=-[\langle n,{\boldsymbol{k}}|\sum_{i}{\boldsymbol{S}}_{i}|n,{\boldsymbol{k}}\rangle-\langle{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\rm vac}\rangle]
represents the average spin carried by the magnon mode
|n,{\boldsymbol{k}}\rangle=\gamma_{n,{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\dagger}|0\rangle
. Here,
\langle{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\rm vac}\rangle
is the spin of the the vacuum state
|0\rangle
.
For further analysis, it is convenient to express
\langle{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\mathrm{SW};n,{\boldsymbol{k}}}\rangle
in terms of the elements of the paraunitary matrix
\mathbf{T}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{-1}
(see Appendix
C
):

\langle{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\mathrm{SW};n,{\boldsymbol{k}}}\rangle=\hbar\sum_{l}(\absolutevalue{U_{ l n, \vect{k}}}^{2}+\absolutevalue{V_{l n, \vect{k}}}^{2})\hat{{\boldsymbol{z}}}_{l}\text{.}

(7)

Fig.
2
a shows the
x
,
y
and
z
components of the average spin polarization
\langle{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\mathrm{SW}}(T)\rangle
as a function of
D_{\parallel}
for a kagome AFM at a temperature of
T=300
K.
Notably, the magnon system exhibits a net spin polarization along the z-axis when
D_{\parallel}\neq 0
. This polarization arises from the out-of-plane tilting in Eq. (
3
), which results in a weak ferromagnetic phase and a corresponding net polarization of the thermally excited magnons. Additionally, we observe that the spin polarization reaches its peak value for a finite in-plane DMI .
The reason the polarization does not increase monotonically with
D_{\parallel}

is that the in-plane DMI also elevates the magnon state energies. This is depicted in the inset of Fig.
2
b, which shows the average energy of the first, second, and third bands, and the average energy of all bands, for different
D_{\parallel}
. Thus,
D_{\parallel}
induces two counteracting effects that influence the polarization: (1) the increased tilting angle, which enhances
\langle{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\mathrm{SW};n,{\boldsymbol{k}}}\rangle
for each magnon state, and (2) the rise in magnon energy, which reduces the Bose-Einstein occupation number
n_{\mathrm{B}}(\varepsilon,T)
, leading to a lower overall
\langle{\boldsymbol{S}}_{\mathrm{SW}}(T)\rangle
. The maximum spin polarization occurs at the
D_{\parallel}
-value where these two effects are balanced.

In addition to generating spin-polarized magnons, the in-plane DMI is pivotal in driving the spin system into a topological phase characterized by magnonic edge states. We investigate this transition by numerically calculating the Chern numbers for the three magnon bands, expressed as the normalized integrals of the Berry curvatures
B_{n}({\boldsymbol{k}})
(
n\in\{1,2,3\}
) over the first Brillouin zone:

C_{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d{\boldsymbol{k}}B_{n}({\boldsymbol{k}})\text{.}

(8)

Here,
B_{n}({\boldsymbol{k}})=\epsilon_{\mu\nu}\partial_{k_{\mu}}A_{n,\nu}({\boldsymbol{k}})
, where

A_{n,\nu}({\boldsymbol{k}})=i[\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{3}(\mathbf{T}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{-1})^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{3}\partial_{k_{\nu}}\mathbf{T}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{-1}]_{nn}
(
\nu\in\{x,y\}
) is the gauge connection,

\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{3}=\mathrm{diag}(1,1,1,-1,-1,-1)
, and
\varepsilon_{\mu\nu}
denotes the 2D antisymmetric Levi-Civita symbol
[
20
]
.

Our numerical analysis shows that an in-plane DMI exceeding 3.04 meV is required to obtain well-defined Chern numbers with a global gap between the two lowest magnon bands, as illustrated in Fig.
2
c for
D_{\parallel}=5
.
In this regime, the Chern numbers are
C_{1}=-1
,
C_{2}=0
, and
C_{3}=1
, signifying the presence of a single edge state band within the global gap between the two lowest magnon bands for a finite system with boundaries.
Additionally, in a finite system, an edge state band forms between the second and third bands. However, this band does not reside within a global gap (see Fig.
2
c).
For
D_{\parallel}<3.04
meV, narrow regions in parameter space exhibit nonzero Chern numbers (different from those mentioned above), but without a global gap between neighboring magnon bands.
Consequently, in what follows, we focus on the regime where
D_{\parallel}>3.04
meV, as this ensures a clear identification of the contribution of the nontrivial edge states to the SSE.

Next, we explore how the surface states influence the SSE.

\displaystyle=

{\boldsymbol{r}}_{\alpha}^{\pm z}={\boldsymbol{r}}_{\alpha}^{\pm}\times\hat{{\boldsymbol{z}}}

c_{\alpha}^{\pm\pm}={\boldsymbol{r}}_{\alpha}^{\pm}\cdot{\boldsymbol{r}}^{\pm}

c_{\alpha}^{\pm z}={\boldsymbol{r}}_{\alpha}^{\pm}\cdot\hat{{\boldsymbol{z}}}
, where

{\boldsymbol{r}}_{\alpha}^{\pm}=\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}_{\alpha}\pm i\hat{{\boldsymbol{y}}}_{\alpha}
and
{\boldsymbol{r}}^{\pm}=\hat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\pm i\hat{{\boldsymbol{y}}}
.

Fig.

\hbar^{2}
. We focus on the

[

]

\lambda=70

\tau=1

[
7

\coth\frac{\varepsilon_{n}(q)}{2k_{B}T_{\mathrm{AF}}}-\coth\frac{\varepsilon_{n}(q)}{2k_{B}T_{\mathrm{N}}}
at
k_{B}T_{\rm AF}\sim\varepsilon_{n}/4.5
. Beyond these peaks, the contributions remain considerable, reaching 21–28% at room temperature. The significant contribution to the spin current is characteristic of topological magnons and arises from their strong coupling to the itinerant charge carriers at the AFM/NM interface. This is demonstrated in Fig.
3

D_{\parallel}=5
meV/
\hbar^{2}
at 300 K. It is noteworthy that the mid-range contribution from the trivial magnon states is about 5%.
Thus, the contribution from the topological magnon band is 4–5 times higher than that of the trivial bands, underscoring the potential of edge states to greatly amplify the antiferromagnetic SSE.
It should be emphasized that while edge states are typically associated with topological magnon bands, non-topological edge magnons are also expected to produce a similar enhancement of the SSE.

Theoretical investigations have demonstrated that the population of edge modes can be tuned and enhanced using a driving electromagnetic field
[
57
,
58
]
.
This effect provides a promising mechanism for exploring the influence of topological magnons on the SSE and could potentially be utilized to amplify thermal spin pumping.

In our investigations, we have disregarded the effects of inelastic relaxation mechanisms – such as magnon-magnon and magnon-phonon interactions – on the robustness of the topological magnon states. These relaxation mechanisms could influence the SSE, particularly at high temperatures. It is generally believed that the topological states remain intact as long as the linewidth of the magnon excitations is smaller than the gap between neighboring magnon bands. However, a recent theoretical study demonstrates that the edge states remain robust even when the damping rates are large enough to close the gap, thus indicating that the topological magnons exhibit resilience against substantial inelastic relaxation mechanisms
[
59
]
.

Our study focused on a 2D sample where the charge carriers couple to the AFM spins along a line. However, we anticipate that the impact of topological magnons could be even more pronounced in 3D systems, where the magnons and charge carriers interact across a 2D AFM/NM interface. Recent experiments have already reported evidence of topological magnons in 3D AFMs
[
45
,
46
]
, making it an exciting prospect for future research.

{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathrm{s}}=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}},q_{x},j}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{4\pi\hbar}{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{j;q_{x}}\delta_{k_{x}q_{x}}\mathfrak{Re}\left(\underline{G_{\mathrm{AF}}}_{j,q_{x}}(\omega)\underline{G_{\mathrm{N}}}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}(\omega)\right)_{12}

(39)

where

{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{j;q_{x}}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in E}{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}_{\alpha j;q_{x}}^{+}\Gamma_{\beta j;q_{x}}^{-,*}+{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}_{\alpha j;q_{x}}^{-}\Gamma_{\beta j;q_{x}}^{+,*}+2{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}_{\alpha j;q_{x}}^{z}\Gamma_{\beta j;q_{x}}^{z,*}\text{.}

(40)

Using the bosonic fluctuation-dissipation theorem
G^{K}(\omega)=[G^{R}(\omega)-G^{A}(\omega)]\coth(\beta\hbar\omega/2)
(where
\beta=1/k_{B}T
), the real part of the Keldysh component of the Green’s function product can be expressed as

\begin{split}&\mathfrak{Re}\left(\underline{G_{\mathrm{AF}}}_{j,q_{x}}(\omega)\underline{G_{\mathrm{N}}}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}(\omega)\right)_{12}\\
&=\mathfrak{Re}\big(G_{\mathrm{AF},j,q_{x}}^{R}(\omega)G_{\mathrm{N},{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{K}(\omega)+G_{\mathrm{AF},j,q_{x}}^{K}(\omega)G_{\mathrm{N},{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{A}(\omega)\big)\\
&=2\mathfrak{Im}G_{\mathrm{AF},j,q_{x}}^{R}(\omega)\mathfrak{Im}G_{\mathrm{N},{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{R}(\omega)\mathcal{F}(\omega,T_{\mathrm{AF}},T_{\mathrm{N}}),\end{split}

(41)

where

\mathcal{F}(\omega,T_{\mathrm{AF}},T_{\mathrm{N}})=\coth\frac{\hbar\omega}{2k_{B}T_{\mathrm{AF}}}-\coth\frac{\hbar\omega}{2k_{B}T_{\mathrm{N}}}.

(42)

Here,
T_{\mathrm{AF}}
(
T_{\mathrm{N}}
) is the temperature of the AFM (NM). The retarded Green’s function of the spin-density correlations (i.e., the spin susceptibility) in the NM is given by
[
7
,
10
]

\begin{split}&G_{\mathrm{N},{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{R}(\omega)=\frac{\chi_{\mathrm{N}}}{1+\lambda^{2}{\boldsymbol{k}}^{2}-i\omega\tau}\quad\text{and}\\
&\mathfrak{Im}G_{\mathrm{N},{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{R}(\omega)=\frac{\chi_{\mathrm{N}}\omega\tau}{(1+\lambda^{2}{\boldsymbol{k}}^{2})^{2}+(\omega\tau)^{2}}\text{,}\end{split}

(43)

where
\chi_{\mathrm{N}}
denotes the paramagnetic susceptipility of the NM,
\lambda
is the spin-diffusion length and
\tau
is the spin-flip relaxation time.
The spin-flip relaxation time arises from the SOC in the NM region.
The retarded Green’s function for the magnons in the AFM is

\begin{split}&G_{\mathrm{AF},j,q_{x}}^{R}(\omega)=\frac{1}{\omega-\omega_{j}(q_{x})+i\alpha_{G}\omega}\quad\text{and}\\
&\mathfrak{Im}G_{\mathrm{AF},j,q_{x}}^{R}(\omega)=\frac{-\alpha_{G}\omega}{(\omega-\omega_{j}(q_{x}))^{2}+(\alpha_{G}\omega)^{2}}\text{,}\end{split}

(44)

where
\hbar\omega_{j}(q_{x})=\varepsilon_{j}(q_{x})
is the eigenvalue of the
j
th spin-wave mode with momentum
q_{x}
, and
\alpha_{G}
is the Gilbert damping of the spins.
Substituting Eq. (
41
) into Eq. (
39
), we arrive at

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathrm{s}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{x}=q_{x},k_{y},j}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{2\pi\hbar}{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{j;q_{x}}\mathfrak{Im}G_{\mathrm{AF},j,q_{x}}^{R}(\omega)\mathfrak{Im}G_{\mathrm{N},{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{R}(\omega)

(45)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\left(\coth\frac{\hbar\omega}{2k_{B}T_{\mathrm{AF}}}-\coth\frac{\hbar\omega}{2k_{B}T_{\mathrm{N}}}\right)\text{.}

Note that
\mathfrak{Im}G_{\mathrm{AF},j,q_{x}}^{R}(\omega)
is strongly peaked at the frequency
\omega>0
corresponding to the eigenfrequency
\omega_{j}(q_{x})
of the magnon.
We can, therefore, simplify the above result further by considering the low damping limit
\alpha_{G}\rightarrow 0
. In this limit, the imaginary part of the magnon Green’s function can be approximated by

\lim_{\alpha_{G}\rightarrow 0}\mathfrak{Im}G_{\mathrm{AF},j,q_{x}}^{R}(\omega)\approx-\pi\delta(\omega-\omega_{j}(q_{x}))
.
Thus, we can perform the integration over
\omega
in Eq. (
45
), which yields

\begin{split}\lim_{\alpha_{G}\rightarrow 0}{\boldsymbol{I}}_{\mathrm{s}}&=-\sum_{k_{x}=q_{x},k_{y},j}\frac{1}{2\hbar}{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{j;q_{x}}\mathfrak{Im}G_{\mathrm{N},{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{R}(\omega_{j}(q_{x}))\\
&\times\left(\coth\frac{\hbar\omega_{j}(q_{x})}{2k_{B}T_{\mathrm{AF}}}-\coth\frac{\hbar\omega_{j}(q_{x})}{2k_{B}T_{\mathrm{N}}}\right)\text{.}\end{split}

(46)

The quantity
{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{j;q_{x}}
does not depend on
k_{y}
, which occurs only in the NM Green’s function.
Considering the continuum limit
a\rightarrow 0
, this allows us to integrate out the
k_{y}
-dependency of the NM Green’s function

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{y}}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Im}G_{\mathrm{N},{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{R}(\omega_{j}(q_{x}))

(47)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{L_{y}}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk_{y}\frac{\chi_{\mathrm{N}}\omega_{j}(q_{x})\tau}{(1+\lambda^{2}q_{x}^{2}+\lambda^{2}k_{y}^{2})^{2}+(\omega_{j}(q_{x})\tau)^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{L_{y}\chi_{\mathrm{N}}}{2\lambda}\sqrt{\frac{\sqrt{(1+(\lambda q_{x})^{2})^{2}+(\omega_{j}(q_{x})\tau)^{2}}-1-(\lambda q_{x})^{2}}{2\left((1+(\lambda q_{x})^{2})^{2}+(\omega_{j}(q_{x})\tau)^{2}\right)}}

where
L_{y}
is the length of the NM sample in
y
-direction.
Substituting the result from Eq. (
47
) into Eq. (
46
) and renaming
q_{x}\rightarrow q
and
j\rightarrow n
, we arrive at the final expression for the thermally-induced spin current in the NM region from Eq. (

) in the main text.

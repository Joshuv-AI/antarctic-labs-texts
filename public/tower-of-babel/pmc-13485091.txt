Neuro ophthalmology matters in India
Authors: Shikha Talwar Bassi
Published: 2026

“When you have eliminated the obvious ocular causes, whatever remains—however complex—deserves neuro-ophthalmic thought.”

Indian ophthalmology has achieved extraordinary success in restoring vision, particularly through cataract surgery and advances in retinal care.[12345] These accomplishments have rightly shaped training priorities and service delivery models across the country. However, as ophthalmology as a speciality evolves, so too must our understanding of visual disease. A growing proportion of patients present with visual symptoms that are not purely ocular, not surgically remediable, and not confined to a single speciality. In this increasingly complex clinical landscape, neuro-ophthalmology has emerged as a discipline that anchors diagnosis and coordinates care across specialities—quietly, thoughtfully, and indispensably.

Evidence from a Tertiary Institute

The All India Institute of Medical Sciences Sentinel Surveillance Report, led by Prof. Praveen Vashist, signalled that our national focus must move from the quantity of surgery to the quality of visual outcomes, identifying that unrecognized non-surgical comorbidities are a primary cause of poor visual recovery.[6] Dhiman et al.[7] in their landmark study at a tertiary center in India, profiled the vast array of neuro-ophthalmic disorders, proving that these cases are a significant and complex part of tertiary care.

The Specialist as Lead Coordinator

Neuro-ophthalmology sits at a unique intersection. It connects the eye to the brain, vision to neurology, and symptoms to systems. Patients often present with nonspecific complaints—blurred vision, headache, transient visual loss, diplopia—yet the underlying diagnosis may range from benign to life-threatening. In such scenarios, the neuro-ophthalmologist is frequently the first clinician to localize disease accurately, determine urgency, and chart the appropriate diagnostic and management pathway. As Nair and Gandhi noted in their seminal editorial, neuro-ophthalmology in India is no longer a niche luxury but “an idea whose time has come.” They argued that as the cataract backlog is addressed, the focus must shift toward these complex, often life-saving, diagnostic challenges.[8]

Neuro-ophthalmology rarely functions in isolation. Its strength lies in coordination. Neurologists rely on neuro-ophthalmic localization to narrow differentials. Neurosurgeons depend on visual pathway assessment to guide operative decisions and prognostication. Physicians managing inflammatory, infectious, or autoimmune disease use visual function as a sensitive marker of disease activity and response to therapy. Even outside medicine, neuro-ophthalmic input influences rehabilitation, occupational planning, and long-term quality-of-life decisions.

In chronic conditions—idiopathic intracranial hypertension, demyelinating disease, compressive optic neuropathies, inflammatory optic neuropathies—the neuro-ophthalmologist often remains the most consistent clinician over time. While other specialties may enter and exit the patient’s journey, neuro-ophthalmology provides continuity, tracking functional vision, and coordinating care as the disease evolves. This longitudinal involvement is one of the specialty’s greatest strengths. Spitze et al.[9] highlighted the profound responsibility of the career, noting that the neuro-ophthalmologist often leads the management of patients with complex systemic illnesses.

Anchoring Diagnosis in a Complex System

What distinguishes neuro-ophthalmology is not a procedure or a device, but a way of thinking. Careful history-taking, detailed examination, and anatomical localization form the foundation of every consultation. Before imaging is ordered or referrals are made, the neuro-ophthalmologist answers the most important question: Where is the problem? Eye, optic nerve, chiasm, tract, cortex, neuromuscular junction, or systemic disease.

Strengthening Mainstream Ophthalmology

Within ophthalmology itself, neuro-ophthalmology plays a critical supportive role. Cataract surgeons encounter patients with unexpectedly poor visual outcomes despite technically perfect surgery. Pediatric ophthalmologists, glaucoma specialists, and oculoplastic surgeons frequently confront neuro-ophthalmic overlap. In each of these situations, neuro-ophthalmology does not compete—it clarifies.

Institutions with integrated neuro-ophthalmology services consistently demonstrate improved diagnostic confidence, better counselling, and more appropriate use of investigations. The specialty elevates the overall standard of care without demanding the spotlight.

The Cost of Missed Guidance

Equally important is the recognition that failure to seek or integrate neuro-ophthalmic input carries consequences. When diagnosis is delayed or incomplete, outcomes may be poor despite best intentions. Patients often cannot distinguish between disease progression and missed opportunity; that responsibility rests with the treating clinician. Neuro-ophthalmology, therefore, acts as a safeguard—ensuring that foreseeable diagnostic pitfalls are addressed early, and that outcomes, even when unfavorable, are at least understood and explained.

The Option of Excellence

Despite its importance, India lacks sufficient numbers of trained specialists.[6] We must recognize that giving the best to the patient is an option that every clinician can exercise by taking an opinion from a trained neuro-ophthalmologist. Too often, patients undergo unnecessary surgical paths because the neurological “anchor” was missing. Every clinician should feel empowered to seek a neuro-ophthalmic consultation, ensuring the patient receives a diagnosis that is “quiet and thoughtful.”

Be a Neuro-ophthalmologist!

One of the most compelling reasons to choose neuro-ophthalmology is what happens as diagnostic capability matures. With experience, the neuro-ophthalmologist becomes a trusted reference point—not only within ophthalmology but also across specialties. Over time, your value is defined not by the number of procedures performed, but by the clarity you bring to uncertainty.

This professional credibility extends beyond institutional boundaries. Neuro-ophthalmologists are consulted by neurologists, neurosurgeons, physicians, and intensivists alike. They are invited into multidisciplinary discussions because their perspective integrates anatomy, function, and prognosis.

Training Future Clinicians Who Think

India has made encouraging strides in developing structured neuro-ophthalmology fellowships, producing clinicians skilled in diagnosis, imaging interpretation, and multidisciplinary care. Yet the number of trained specialists remains small relative to national need.[67] Encouraging more trainees to enter neuro-ophthalmology is therefore not simply a matter of career choice—it is a strategic investment in the future of Indian healthcare.

For residents and young ophthalmologists, neuro-ophthalmology offers a path that is intellectually rigorous, clinically relevant, and professionally sustainable. It allows one to remain a thinking physician within a procedural specialty.

Conclusion

Neuro-ophthalmology matters in India because it anchors diagnosis, coordinates care, and restores coherence to complex clinical pathways. It matters because visual symptoms are often the earliest indicators of neurological disease, and because timely interpretation can alter the course of both sight and life. Most importantly, it matters because it reinforces the central role of thoughtful clinical medicine in an increasingly fragmented healthcare environment.

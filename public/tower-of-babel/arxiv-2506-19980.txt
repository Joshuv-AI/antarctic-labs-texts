Properties of the symmetric difference in lattices with complementation
Václav Cenker, Ivan Chajda, Helmut Länger

Abstract
The symmetric difference in Boolean lattices can be defined in two different but equivalent forms. However, it can be introduced also in every bounded lattice with complementation where these two forms need not coincide. We study lattices with complementation and the variety of such lattices where these two expressions coincide and point out explicitly some interesting subvarieties. Using a result of J. Berman we estimate the size of free algebras in these subvarieties. It is well-known that the symmetric difference is associative in every Boolean lattice. We prove that it is just the property of Boolean lattices, namely the symmetric difference in a lattice with complementation is associative if and only if this lattice is Boolean. Similarly, we prove that a lattice with complementation is Boolean if and only if the symmetric difference satisfies a certain simple identity in two variables. We also characterize lattices with a unary operation satisfying De Morgan’s laws.

Introduction

The aim of this paper is to show several important properties of the symmetric difference in complemented lattices where the complementation is a unary operation. Because the symmetric difference can be defined in two different ways which coincide in Boolean lattices, we are interested in lattices which need not be distributive but these two forms of symmetric difference coincide. It is well-known that in a Boolean algebra the symmetric difference is associative. We show that also, conversely, associativity of the symmetric difference yields distributivity of the complemented lattice in question.

We start with definitions of several basic concepts.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a lattice with complementation satisfying one of the following identities:

(i)

x\wedge y\approx x\wedge(x^{\prime}\vee y)
,

(ii)

x\vee y\approx x\vee(x^{\prime}\wedge y)
.

Then
\mathbf{L}
satisfies the identity
x^{\prime\prime}\approx x
.

Proof.

Assume (i). Then

x\wedge x^{\prime\prime}\approx x\wedge(x^{\prime}\vee x^{\prime\prime})\approx x\wedge 1\approx x.

(1)

Hence
x^{\prime}\approx x^{\prime}\wedge x^{\prime\prime}
and

x^{\prime\prime\prime}\vee x^{\prime}\approx x^{\prime\prime\prime}\vee(x^{\prime}\wedge x^{\prime\prime\prime})\approx x^{\prime\prime\prime}

whence

x\vee x^{\prime\prime\prime}\approx x\vee x^{\prime\prime\prime}\vee x^{\prime}\approx 1.

Using this, (
1
) and (i) we obtain

x\approx x\wedge x^{\prime\prime}\approx x^{\prime\prime}\wedge x\approx x^{\prime\prime}\wedge(x^{\prime\prime\prime}\vee x)\approx x^{\prime\prime}\wedge 1\approx x^{\prime\prime}.

By dualizing the preceding proof we see that (ii) implies
x^{\prime\prime}\approx x
. ∎

\displaystyle x+_{1}y

\displaystyle:=(x^{\prime}\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge y^{\prime}),

\displaystyle x+_{2}y

\displaystyle:=(x\vee y)\wedge(x^{\prime}\vee y^{\prime}).

(x^{\prime}\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge y^{\prime})\approx(x\vee y)\wedge(x^{\prime}\vee y^{\prime}).

(2)

The following lemma presents two identities holding in any ortholattice satisfying identity (

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be an ortholattice satisfying identity
(
2

. Then it satisfies the identities

\displaystyle(x\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge y^{\prime})\vee(x^{\prime}\wedge y)\vee(x^{\prime}\wedge y^{\prime})

\displaystyle\approx 1,

\displaystyle(x\vee y)\wedge(x\vee y^{\prime})\wedge(x^{\prime}\vee y)\wedge(x^{\prime}\vee y^{\prime})

\displaystyle\approx 0.

Proof.

We have

(x\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge y^{\prime})\vee(x^{\prime}\wedge y)\vee(x^{\prime}\wedge y^{\prime})\approx(x+_{1}y)\vee(x+_{2}y)^{\prime}\approx(x+_{1}y)\vee(x+_{1}y)^{\prime}\approx 1.

The second identity is dual to the first one.
∎

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a non-trivial lattice with complementation satisfying identity

If there would exist some

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=a\vee 0\vee(a\wedge b^{\prime})=a\vee(a^{\prime}\wedge b)\vee(a\wedge b^{\prime})=a\vee\big((a\vee b)\wedge(a^{\prime}\vee b^{\prime})\big)=

\displaystyle=a\vee\big(1\wedge(a^{\prime}\vee b^{\prime})\big)=a\vee a^{\prime}\vee b^{\prime}=1

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be an ortholattice having the property that for all

Let

If

a+_{1}b=(a^{\prime}\wedge b)\vee(a\wedge b^{\prime})=a^{\prime}\vee b^{\prime}=(b\vee a)\wedge(a^{\prime}\vee b^{\prime})=(a\vee b)\wedge(a^{\prime}\vee b^{\prime})=a+_{2}b.

Recall that the
horizontal sum
of a family
(C_{i},\leq_{i},0,1),i\in I,
of bounded chains with

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\mathbf{C}_{1}=(C_{1},\leq,0,1)
and
\mathbf{C}_{2}=(C_{2},\leq,0,1)
be bounded chains satisfying
C_{1}\cap C_{2}=\{0,1\}
, let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,0,1)
denote the horizontal sum of
\mathbf{C}_{1}
and
\mathbf{C}_{2}
and let
\,{}^{\prime}
be a unary operation on
L
. Then the following holds:

(i)

The operation
\,{}^{\prime}
is a complementation if and only if
0^{\prime}=1
,
1^{\prime}=0
,
(C_{1}\setminus\{0,1\})^{\prime}\subseteq C_{2}\setminus\{0,1\}
and
(C_{2}\setminus\{0,1\})^{\prime}\subseteq C_{1}\setminus\{0,1\}
,

(ii)

if
\,{}^{\prime}
is a complementation then
\mathbf{L}
satisfies identity
(
2
)
,

(iii)

if
\,{}^{\prime}
is a complementation then
\mathbf{L}
satisfies De Morgan’s laws if and only if
\,{}^{\prime}
is antitone.

Proof.

Let
a,b\in L
.

(i)

First assume
\,{}^{\prime}
to be a complementation. As mentioned in the beginning, we have
0^{\prime}=1
and
1^{\prime}=0
. Moreover, if
a\in C_{1}\setminus\{0,1\}
then
a^{\prime}\in L\setminus C_{1}=C_{2}\setminus\{0,1\}
showing
(C_{1}\setminus\{0,1\})^{\prime}\subseteq C_{2}\setminus\{0,1\}
. By symmetry,
(C_{2}\setminus\{0,1\})^{\prime}\subseteq C_{1}\setminus\{0,1\}
. Conversely, if
0^{\prime}=1
,
1^{\prime}=0
,
(C_{1}\setminus\{0,1\})^{\prime}\subseteq C_{2}\setminus\{0,1\}
and
(C_{2}\setminus\{0,1\})^{\prime}\subseteq C_{1}\setminus\{0,1\}
then
\,{}^{\prime}
is a complementation, namely the equalities
a\vee a^{\prime}=1
and
a\wedge a^{\prime}=0
can be seen by distinguishing the cases
a=0
,
a=1
,
a\in C_{1}\setminus\{0,1\}
and
a\in C_{2}\setminus\{0,1\}
.

(ii)

If
a\in\{0,1\}
or
b\in\{0,1\}
then
a+_{1}b=a+_{2}b
. If
a,b\in C_{1}\setminus\{0,1\}
then
a\vee b\in C_{1}\setminus\{0,1\}
and
a^{\prime}\vee b^{\prime}\in C_{2}\setminus\{0,1\}
and hence

a+_{1}b=(a^{\prime}\wedge b)\vee(a\wedge b^{\prime})=0\vee 0=0=(a\vee b)\wedge(a^{\prime}\vee b^{\prime})=a+_{2}b.

If
a\in C_{1}\setminus\{0,1\}
and
b\in C_{2}\setminus\{0,1\}
then
a^{\prime}\wedge b\in C_{2}\setminus\{0,1\}
and
a\wedge b^{\prime}\in C_{1}\setminus\{0,1\}
and hence

a+_{1}b=(a^{\prime}\wedge b)\vee(a\wedge b^{\prime})=1=1\wedge 1=(a\vee b)\wedge(a^{\prime}\vee b^{\prime}).

The remaining cases can be treated similarly.

(iii)

Assume
\,{}^{\prime}
to be a complementation. If
\mathbf{L}
satisfies De Morgan’s laws and
a\leq b
then
b^{\prime}=(a\vee b)^{\prime}=a^{\prime}\wedge b^{\prime}\leq a^{\prime}
. This shows that
\,{}^{\prime}
is antitone. Conversely, assume
\,{}^{\prime}
to be antitone. If
a,b\in C_{1}
and
a\leq b
then
(a\vee b)^{\prime}=b^{\prime}=b^{\prime}\wedge a^{\prime}
. If
a\in C_{1}\setminus\{0,1\}
and
b\in C_{2}\setminus\{0,1\}
then
(a\vee b)=1^{\prime}=0=a^{\prime}\wedge b^{\prime}
. The other cases can be treated similarly. Hence
\mathbf{L}
satisfies the identity
(x\vee y)^{\prime}\approx x^{\prime}\wedge y^{\prime}
. Dual arguments show that
\mathbf{L}
satisfies also the identity
(x\wedge y)^{\prime}\approx x^{\prime}\vee y^{\prime}
.

∎

3
Identities of symmetric differences

As mentioned in the introduction, it is well-known (see e.g.
[
2
]
) that in a Boolean lattice
(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime},0,1)
the term operations
+_{1}
and
+_{2}
coincide and these operations are associative, i.e. they satisfy the identities

(x+_{1}y)+_{1}z\approx x+_{1}(y+_{1}z)\text{ and }(x+_{2}y)+_{2}z\approx x+_{2}(y+_{2}z),

respectively. The question arises whether, conversely, in a lattice with complementation, associativity of
+_{1}
or
+_{2}
implies lattice distributivity. We will show that this is indeed the case.

At first we prove an auxiliary result using identites similar to those studied in
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a lattice with complementation. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

x\wedge y\approx x\wedge(x^{\prime}\vee y)
,

(ii)

x\vee y\approx x\vee(x^{\prime}\wedge y)
.

Proof.

Assume (i). Then, by Lemma
1.1
we have
x^{\prime\prime}\approx x
, and from (i) we get

x^{\prime}\wedge y\approx x^{\prime}\wedge(x^{\prime\prime}\vee y)\approx x^{\prime}\wedge(x\vee y).

(4)

Using this we obtain

(x\vee y)^{\prime}\wedge x^{\prime}\approx(x\vee y)^{\prime}\wedge\big((x\vee y)\vee x^{\prime}\big)\approx(x\vee y)^{\prime}\wedge 1\approx(x\vee y)^{\prime}.

Thus

(x\vee y)^{\prime}\wedge x^{\prime}\approx(x\vee y)^{\prime}

(5)

and hence

x\vee(x^{\prime}\vee y)^{\prime}\approx x\vee\big((x^{\prime}\vee y)^{\prime}\wedge x^{\prime\prime}\big)\approx x\vee\big((x^{\prime}\vee y)^{\prime}\wedge x\big)\approx x

showing

x\approx x\vee(x^{\prime}\vee y)^{\prime}.

(6)

Using (i) we get

x\wedge\big(y\vee(y\vee x^{\prime})^{\prime}\big)\approx x\wedge\big(x^{\prime}\vee y\vee(y\vee x^{\prime})^{\prime}\big)\approx x\wedge 1\approx x

and hence

x\approx x\wedge\big(y\vee(y\vee x^{\prime})^{\prime}\big).

Using this and (
6
) we obtain

x\vee(x\vee y^{\prime})^{\prime}\approx\big(x\vee(x\vee y^{\prime})^{\prime}\big)\vee\Big(\big(x\vee(x\vee y^{\prime})^{\prime}\big)\wedge y\Big)\approx\big(x\vee(x\vee y^{\prime})^{\prime}\big)\vee y\approx x\vee\big((x\vee y^{\prime})^{\prime}\vee y\big)\approx x\vee y.

Thus

x\vee y\approx x\vee(x\vee y^{\prime})^{\prime}.

(7)

Using this and (
4
) we get

(x\vee y)\wedge x^{\prime}\approx\big(x\vee(x\vee y^{\prime})^{\prime}\big)\wedge x^{\prime}\approx(x\vee y^{\prime})^{\prime}\wedge x^{\prime}.

Thus

(x\vee y)\wedge x^{\prime}\approx(x\vee y^{\prime})^{\prime}\wedge x^{\prime}.

From this, (
5
) and (
4
) we obtain

(x\vee y)^{\prime}\approx(x\vee y)^{\prime}\wedge x^{\prime}\approx(x\vee y^{\prime\prime})^{\prime}\wedge x^{\prime}\approx(x\vee y^{\prime})\wedge x^{\prime}\approx x^{\prime}\wedge y^{\prime}.

Thus

(x\vee y)^{\prime}\approx x^{\prime}\wedge y^{\prime}.

Finally, using this and (
7
) we get

x\vee y\approx x\vee(x\vee y^{\prime})^{\prime}\approx x\vee(x^{\prime}\wedge y^{\prime\prime})\approx x\vee(x^{\prime}\wedge y).

Therefore (ii) holds. On the other hand, if (ii) holds, then again
x^{\prime\prime}\approx x
by Lemma
1.1
. The proof that (ii) implies (i) follows by dualizing the preceding arguments.
∎

Now we get a connection of the afore mentioned identities with distributivity of the lattice in question. This is an essential result for the proof of our next theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a lattice with complementation satisfying one of the following identities

(i)

x\wedge y\approx x\wedge(x^{\prime}\vee y)
,

(ii)

x\vee y\approx x\vee(x^{\prime}\wedge y)
.

Then
\mathbf{L}
is distributive and hence Boolean.

Proof.

According to Lemmas
1.1
and
3.1
we have
x^{\prime\prime}\approx x
, and (i) and (ii) are equivalent. Therefore we may assume that both identities are satisfied. Using these identities we get

x\wedge(y\vee z)\approx x\wedge(x^{\prime}\vee y\vee z)\approx x\wedge\big(x^{\prime}\vee(x\wedge y)\vee z\big)\approx x\wedge\big((x\wedge y)\vee z\big).

Thus

x\wedge(y\vee z)\approx x\wedge\big((x\wedge y)\vee z\big).

(8)

Using this we obtain

\displaystyle x\wedge(y\vee z)

\displaystyle\approx\big(x\wedge(y\vee z)\big)\vee\big(x\wedge(y\vee z)\wedge y\big)\approx

\displaystyle\approx\big(x\wedge(y\vee z)\big)\vee\Big(x\wedge\big((x\wedge y)\vee z\big)\wedge y\Big)\approx

\displaystyle\approx\big(x\wedge(y\vee z)\big)\vee\Big((x\wedge y)\wedge\big((x\wedge y)\vee z\big)\Big)\approx\big(x\wedge(y\vee z)\big)\vee(x\wedge y)

and hence

x\wedge(y\vee z)\approx\big(x\wedge(y\vee z)\big)\vee(x\wedge y).

Finally, using this, (ii), (
8
) and (i) we get

\displaystyle x\wedge(y\vee z)

\displaystyle\approx\big(x\wedge(y\vee z)\big)\vee(x\wedge y)\approx(x\wedge y)\vee\Big((x\wedge y)^{\prime}\wedge\big(x\wedge(y\vee z)\big)\Big)\approx

\displaystyle\approx(x\wedge y)\vee\Big((x\wedge y)^{\prime}\wedge x\wedge\big((x\wedge y)\vee z\big)\Big)\approx

\displaystyle\approx(x\wedge y)\vee\Big((x\wedge y)^{\prime}\wedge\big((x\wedge y)\vee z\big)\wedge x\Big)\approx(x\wedge y)\vee\big((x\wedge y)^{\prime}\wedge z\wedge x\big)\approx

\displaystyle\approx(x\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge z).

∎

Our main result concerning the associativity of the symmetric difference is as follows.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a lattice with complementation. Then
\mathbf{L}
is Boolean if and only if either
+_{1}
or
+_{2}
is associative.

Proof.

If
\mathbf{L}
is Boolean then
+_{1}=+_{2}
and this operation is associative (see
[
4
]
). Conversely, assume either
+_{1}
or
+_{2}
to be associative. Let
+\in\{+_{1},+_{2}\}
. Then

x+0\approx x,x+1\approx x^{\prime}\text{ and }x+x\approx 0

and hence

(x+y)^{\prime}\approx x+(y+1)\approx x+y^{\prime}\approx x^{\prime}+y\text{ and }x^{\prime\prime}\approx x+1+1\approx x+0\approx x.

Thus

(x+y)^{\prime}\approx x+y^{\prime}\approx x^{\prime}+y\text{ and }x^{\prime\prime}\approx x.

For
+_{1}
we have

x\approx(x+_{1}y)+_{1}y\approx\big(y\wedge(x+_{1}y)^{\prime}\big)\vee\big(y^{\prime}\wedge(x+_{1}y)\big).

(9)

Using this we obtain

\displaystyle x\wedge(x+_{1}y)^{\prime}

\displaystyle\approx x\wedge(x+_{1}y)^{\prime}\wedge\Big(\big(x\wedge(x+_{1}y)^{\prime}\big)\vee\big(x^{\prime}\wedge(x+_{1}y)\big)\Big)\approx x\wedge(x+_{1}y)^{\prime}\wedge y\approx

\displaystyle\approx(x\wedge y)\wedge(x^{\prime}+_{1}y)\approx(x\wedge y)\wedge\big((x\wedge y)\vee(x^{\prime}\wedge y^{\prime})\big)\approx x\wedge y.

Thus

x\wedge(x+_{1}y)^{\prime}\approx x\wedge y.

From this and (
9
) we get

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\approx\big(y\wedge(x+_{1}y)^{\prime}\big)\vee\big(y^{\prime}\wedge(x+_{1}y)\big)\approx(x\wedge y)\vee\big(y^{\prime}\wedge(x+_{1}y^{\prime\prime})\big)\approx

\displaystyle\approx(x\wedge y)\vee\big(y^{\prime}\wedge(x+_{1}y^{\prime})^{\prime}\big)\approx(x\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge y^{\prime}).

Thus

x\approx(x\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge y^{\prime}).

Using this we obtain

x^{\prime}\vee y\approx(x^{\prime}\wedge y)\vee(x^{\prime}\wedge y^{\prime})\vee(x\wedge y)\vee(x^{\prime}\wedge y)\approx(x^{\prime}\wedge y)\vee(x^{\prime}\wedge y^{\prime})\vee(x\wedge y)\approx x^{\prime}\vee(x\wedge y).

Thus
x^{\prime}\vee y\approx x^{\prime}\vee(x\wedge y)
and, consequently,
x\vee y\approx x\vee(x^{\prime}\wedge y)
. By Theorem
3.2
this implies that
\mathbf{L}
is Boolean. For
+_{2}
we can use similar arguments. We have

x\approx(x+_{2}y)+_{2}y\approx\big(y\vee(x+_{2}y)\big)\wedge\big(y^{\prime}\vee(x+_{2}y)^{\prime}\big).

(10)

Using this we get

\displaystyle x\vee(x+_{2}y)

\displaystyle\approx\big(x\vee(x+_{2}y)\big)\vee\Big(\big(x\vee(x+_{2}y)\big)\wedge\big(x^{\prime}\vee(x+_{2}y)^{\prime}\big)\Big)\approx x\vee(x+_{2}y)\vee y\approx

\displaystyle\approx(x\vee y)\wedge(x+_{2}y)\approx(x\vee y)\vee\big((x\vee y)\wedge(x^{\prime}\vee y^{\prime})\big)\approx x\vee y.

Thus

x\vee(x+_{2}y)\approx x\vee y.

From this and (
10
) we obtain

x\approx\big(y\vee(x+_{2}y)\big)\wedge\big(y^{\prime}\vee(x+_{2}y)^{\prime}\big)\approx(x\vee y)\wedge\big(y^{\prime}\vee(x+_{2}y^{\prime})\big)\approx(x\vee y)\wedge(x\vee y^{\prime}).

Thus

x\approx(x\vee y)\wedge(x\vee y^{\prime})

and hence

x\wedge y\approx(x\vee y)\wedge(x\vee y^{\prime})\wedge(x\vee y)\wedge(x^{\prime}\vee y)\approx(x\vee y)\wedge(x\vee y^{\prime})\wedge(x^{\prime}\vee y)\approx x\wedge(x^{\prime}\vee y).

This shows
x\wedge y\approx x\wedge(x^{\prime}\vee y)
which, by Theorem
3.2
, implies that
\mathbf{L}
is Boolean.
∎

As shown above, associativity of the symmetric difference in a lattice
\mathbf{L}
with complementation yields distributivity of
\mathbf{L}
. Surprisingly, also a simple identity in only two variables formulated for the symmetric difference yields the same, see the following result.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a lattice with complementation satisfying identity
(
2
)
. Then
\mathbf{L}
is a Boolean algebra if and only if
+
satisfies the identity

(x+y)+y\approx x

where either
+=+_{1}
or
+=+_{2}
.

Proof.

Of course, if
\mathbf{L}
is a Boolean algebra then according to Theorem
3.3
the symmetric difference is associative and hence it satisfies
(x+y)+y\approx x+(y+y)\approx x+0\approx x
. Conversely, assume
\mathbf{L}
to satisfy the identity
(x+y)+y\approx x
. If
\mathbf{L}
satisfies the identity
(x+_{1}y)+_{1}y\approx x
then

\displaystyle x^{\prime}\wedge y

\displaystyle\approx(x^{\prime}\wedge y)\wedge\big((x^{\prime}\wedge y)\vee(x\wedge y^{\prime})\big)\approx x^{\prime}\wedge y\wedge(x+_{1}y)\approx

\displaystyle\approx x^{\prime}\wedge\big((y+_{1}x)+_{1}x\big)\wedge(x+_{1}y)\approx

\displaystyle\approx\big(x^{\prime}\wedge(x+_{1}y)\big)\wedge\Big(\big((y+_{1}x)^{\prime}\wedge x\big)\vee\big((y+_{1}x)\wedge x^{\prime}\big)\Big)\approx x^{\prime}\wedge(x+_{1}y)

and hence

x\vee y\approx x\vee\big((y+_{1}x)+_{1}x\big)\approx x\vee\big((y+_{1}x)^{\prime}\wedge x\big)\vee\big((y+_{1}x)\wedge x^{\prime}\big)\approx x\vee\big(x^{\prime}\wedge(x+_{1}y)\big)\approx x\vee(x^{\prime}\wedge y)

which implies that
\mathbf{L}
is Boolean according to Theorem
3.2
. If
\mathbf{L}
satisfies the identity
(x+_{2}y)+_{2}y\approx x
then

\displaystyle x\vee y

\displaystyle\approx(x\vee y)\vee\big((x\vee y)\wedge(x^{\prime}\vee y^{\prime})\big)\approx(x\vee y)\vee(x+_{2}y)\approx

\displaystyle\approx x\vee\big((y+_{2}x)+_{2}x\big)\vee(x+_{2}y)\approx

\displaystyle\approx x\vee(x+_{2}y)\vee\Big(\big(y+_{2}x)\vee x\big)\wedge\big((y+_{2}x)^{\prime}\vee x^{\prime}\big)\Big)\approx x\vee(x+_{2}y)

and hence

\displaystyle x^{\prime}\wedge y

\displaystyle\approx x^{\prime}\wedge\big((y+_{2}x)+_{2}x\big)\approx x^{\prime}\wedge\big((y+_{2}x)\vee x\big)\wedge\big((y+_{2}x)^{\prime}\vee x^{\prime}\big)\approx

\displaystyle\approx x^{\prime}\wedge\big(x\vee(x+_{2}y)\big)\approx x^{\prime}\wedge(x\vee y)

which because of

\displaystyle x^{\prime}

\displaystyle\approx 1\wedge(x^{\prime}\vee 0)\approx(x\vee 1)\wedge(x^{\prime}\vee 1^{\prime})\approx x+_{2}1,

\displaystyle x^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\approx(x+_{2}1)+_{2}1\approx x

implies
x\wedge y\approx x^{\prime\prime}\wedge y\approx x^{\prime\prime}\wedge(x^{\prime}\vee y)\approx x\vee(x^{\prime}\vee y)
proving
\mathbf{L}
to be Boolean according to Theorem
3.2
.
∎

\,{}^{\prime}

x^{\prime}\vee(x\wedge y)\approx x^{\prime}\vee y\text{ and }x^{\prime}\wedge(x\vee y)\approx x^{\prime}\wedge y.

In

4
De Morgan’s laws

It is well-known that a lattice
(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime})
with an antitone involution
\,{}^{\prime}
satisfies De Morgan’s laws

(x\vee y)^{\prime}\approx x^{\prime}\wedge y^{\prime}\text{ and }(x\wedge y)^{\prime}\approx x^{\prime}\wedge y^{\prime}.

However, De Morgan’s laws may be satisfied also in the case that
\,{}^{\prime}
is not an involution.

Now we are interested in conditions under which a lattice with a unary operation
\,{}^{\prime}
satisfies De Morgan’s laws. At first, we see that the lattice
\mathbf{N}_{5}
with the complementation defined in Example
2.8
satisfies De Morgan’s laws despite the fact that the complementation
\,{}^{\prime}
is not an involution.

Example 4.1
.

The complementation
\,{}^{\prime}
of the non-modular lattices
\mathbf{N}_{5}
and
\mathbf{N}_{5}^{*}
is antitone, but not an involution, but these lattices still satisfy De Morgan’s laws. The complementation
\,{}^{\prime}
of the non-modular lattice
\mathbf{O}_{5}
is antitone and an involution and satisfies De Morgan’s laws. The complementation
\,{}^{\prime}
of the non-modular lattice
\mathbf{O}_{6}^{*}
is not antitone, but it is an involution, and does not satisfy De Morgan’s laws.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime})
be a lattice with a unary operation
\,{}^{\prime}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

\mathbf{L}
satisfies the identities
(x\vee y)^{\prime}\approx x^{\prime}\wedge y^{\prime}
and
(x\wedge y)^{\prime}\approx x^{\prime}\vee y^{\prime}
,

(ii)

the operation
\,{}^{\prime}
is antitone and
\mathbf{L}
satisfies the identities
(x\vee y)^{\prime}\wedge x^{\prime}\approx x^{\prime}\wedge y^{\prime}
and
(x\wedge y)^{\prime}\vee x^{\prime}\approx x^{\prime}\vee y^{\prime}
.

Proof.

We prove that the following are equivalent:

(iii)

\mathbf{L}
satisfies the identity
(x\vee y)^{\prime}\approx x^{\prime}\wedge y^{\prime}
,

(iv)

the operation
\,{}^{\prime}
is antitone and
\mathbf{L}
satisfies the identity
(x\vee y)^{\prime}\wedge x^{\prime}\approx x^{\prime}\wedge y^{\prime}
.

The rest follows by duality. Let
a,b\in L
.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(iv):

If
a\leq b
then
b^{\prime}=(a\vee b)^{\prime}=a^{\prime}\wedge b^{\prime}\leq a^{\prime}
showing that
\,{}^{\prime}
is antitone. Moreover,
(a\vee b)^{\prime}\wedge a^{\prime}=a^{\prime}\wedge b^{\prime}\wedge a^{\prime}=a^{\prime}\wedge b^{\prime}
proving (iv).

(iv)
\Rightarrow
(iii):

Because of
a\leq a\vee b
and
b\leq a\vee b
we have
(a\vee b)^{\prime}\leq a^{\prime}
and
(a\vee b)^{\prime}\leq b^{\prime}
and hence
(a\vee b)^{\prime}\leq a^{\prime}\wedge b^{\prime}
. Conversely, (iv) implies
a^{\prime}\wedge b^{\prime}=(a\vee b)^{\prime}\wedge a^{\prime}\leq(a\vee b)^{\prime}
which together with
(a\vee b)^{\prime}\leq a^{\prime}\wedge b^{\prime}
yields
(a\vee b)^{\prime}=a^{\prime}\wedge b^{\prime}
.
∎

Example 4.3
.

One can easily check that condition
(ii)
of Theorem
4.2
is satisfied in the lattices
\mathbf{N}_{5}
,
\mathbf{N}_{5}^{*}
and
\mathbf{O}_{6}
, but not in the lattice
\mathbf{O}_{6}^{*}
.

DeepDive: A deep dive into the physics of the first massive quiescent galaxies in the Universe
K. Ito, F. Valentino, G. Brammer, M. L. Hamadouche, K. E. Whitaker, V. Kokorev, P. Zhu, T. Kakimoto, P.-F. Wu, J. Antwi-Danso, W. M. Baker, D. Ceverino, A. L. Faisst, M. Farcy, S. Fujimoto, A. Gallazzi, S. Gillman, R. Gottumukkala, K. E. Heintz, M. Hirschmann, C. K. Jespersen, M. Kubo, M. Lee, G. Magdis, M. Onodera, R. Shimakawa, M. Tanaka, S. Toft, J. R. Weaver

Abstract
We present the DeepDive program, in which we obtained deep ( 1 − 3 1-3 hours) JWST/NIRSpec G235M/F170LP spectra for 10 primary massive ( log ⁡ ( M ⋆ / M ⊙ ) = 10.8 − 11.5 \log{(M_{\star}/M_{\odot})}=10.8-11.5 ) quiescent galaxies at z ∼ 3 − 4 z\sim 3-4 . A novel reduction procedure extends the nominal wavelength coverage of G235M beyond H α \alpha and [N II ] at z ∼ 4 z\sim 4 , revealing weak, narrow H α \alpha lines indicative of low star formation rates ( SFR ∼ 0 − 5 ​ M ⊙ ​ yr − 1 {\rm SFR}\sim 0-5\,M_{\odot}\,{\rm yr^{-1}} ). Two out of 10 primary targets have broad H α \alpha lines, indicating the presence of AGNs. We also conduct an archival search of quiescent galaxies observed with NIRSpec gratings in the DAWN JWST Archive, which provides a statistical context for interpreting the DeepDive targets. This archival search provides a spectroscopic sample of 140 quiescent galaxies spanning 1 < z < 5 1<z<5 and covering more than an order of magnitude in stellar mass. We revisit the selection of quiescent galaxies based on rest-frame U ​ V ​ J UVJ colors, specific star formation rates, and the detection of the 4000 Å spectral break, finding ∼ 90 % \sim 90\% overlap between these criteria. The sample of a total of 150 quiescent galaxies constructed in this study shows that those at z ∼ 3 − 5 z\sim 3-5 , including the DeepDive targets, typically exhibit weaker 4000 Å breaks and bluer colors than their lower-redshift counterparts, indicating generally younger stellar populations. Stacked spectra of sources grouped by the D n ​ 4000 D_{n}4000 index reveal faint Iron and Magnesium absorption line features in the stellar continuum even for the low D n ​ 4000 D_{n}4000 ( D n ​ 4000 D_{n}4000 < 1.35 <1.35 ) subsample at high redshift ( z ∼ 3 z\sim 3 ). In addition, higher D n ​ 4000 D_{n}4000 subsamples show fainter nebular emission lines. These results demonstrate that medium-resolution NIRSpec spectroscopy is essential for robustly characterizing the diversity and evolution of early quiescent galaxies. All photometric and spectroscopic data from this study will be made publicly available.

Introduction

In the past two decades, a surge of studies has shed light on several aspects of the formation and evolution of the first massive quiescent galaxies, characterized by early and intense star formation activity, sudden “quenching”, and followed by a long passive evolution of their stellar populations. The same concept of “first” quiescent systems has been revisited each time new and powerful instruments became available. Only 20 years ago, the numerous population of red and morphologically compact galaxies at
z\sim 1.5-2
was indicated as prototype early massive quiescent galaxies
(
Daddi et al. 2005
;
Trujillo et al. 2006
;
Toft et al. 2007
, e.g.,)
, later confirmed when the first near-infrared spectrographs installed on 8-10m ground-based telescopes were built
(
Kriek et al. 2006
;
van Dokkum et al. 2009
;
Kriek et al. 2009
)
. Spectroscopy has been instrumental in confirming the redshifts of massive quiescent galaxies, establishing the existence of old stellar populations, constraining their star formation histories, and estimating their dynamical masses
(
Belli et al. 2014
;
Belli et al. 2017
)
. Since then, the quiescent frontier has been pushed to
z\sim 3-4
, the limit at which the brightest (
K\lesssim 22-23\,{\rm mag}
) and most massive systems could be spectroscopically confirmed in the last transparent window in the near-infrared offered by the atmosphere
(
Glazebrook et al. 2017
;
Schreiber et al. 2018a
;
Tanaka et al. 2019
;
Valentino et al. 2020
;
Forrest et al. 2020
;
Forrest et al. 2022
;
Esdaile et al. 2021
;
Kakimoto et al. 2024
;
Antwi-Danso et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
. Recently, the advent of the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) has allowed for a spectacular jump forward in the exploration of this topic. Massive systems with suppressed star formation rate (SFR) have been spectroscopically confirmed up to
z\sim 5-7
, posing a serious challenge to our models and simulations
(
Carnall et al. 2024
;
de Graaff et al. 2024b
;
Onoue et al. 2024
;
Lagos et al. 2024
;
De Lucia et al. 2024
;
Weibel et al. 2025
)
. The unprecedented sensitivity of JWST now also reveals the presence of pockets of outflowing ionized and neutral gas, possibly powered by residual SF or AGN activity
(
Belli et al. 2024
;
Davies et al. 2024
;
D’Eugenio et al. 2024
;
Wu 2024
;
Valentino et al. 2025
)
, the standard agents invoked to explain the abrupt interruption of the intense star formation
(
Lagos et al. 2024
;
De Lucia et al. 2025
, for recent overviews)
. Tighter constraints on the SFHs, showing the existence of short star formation periods
(
Carnall et al. 2024
;
Park et al. 2024a
;
Baker et al. 2024
;
Nanayakkara et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
, and, at lower redshifts, on the history of metal enrichment
(
Gallazzi et al. 2014
;
Beverage et al. 2021
;
Beverage et al. 2024
;
Beverage et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
can now constitute excellent tests for the next-generation hydrodynamical simulations and have prompted the creation of ever novel spectral models to reproduce and explain the observed spectra
(
Park et al. 2024b
, e.g.,)
.

Throughout this journey, research has typically proceeded in two phases: an initial “discovery” phase, followed by “population studies”. The early phase focused on identifying remarkable systems, while the later stage aims to characterize galaxies on a statistical basis. Since the launch of JWST, the emphasis has been on finding exceptional systems – the most distant, most massive, or oldest galaxies – to demonstrate the capabilities of the instruments and push the limits of prior knowledge. However, only statistical studies can ultimately reveal what is typical versus exceptional and establish representative samples for meaningful comparisons with theoretical models.

In this work, we introduce the
“A deep dive into the physics of the first massive quiescent galaxies in the Universe”
(DeepDive) program, a spectroscopic campaign with JWST/NIRspec aimed at characterizing the properties and underlying physics of massive quiescent galaxies at
z=3-4
. New observations are placed in context by leveraging all equivalent JWST/NIRSpec observations taken within the first three years of telescope operations, retrieved from the DAWN JWST Archive (DJA). Our ultimate goal is to provide the basis for the first robust statistical census of this population at
1<z<5
and to offer a complementary spectroscopic analysis to the photometric archival approach presented in
Valentino et al. 2023
and
Baker et al. 2025
. Here we introduce the selection, observations, and data reduction of the DeepDive program (Section
2
). We then describe our archival search for grating spectra of quiescent galaxies (Section
3
) and present their basic properties, such as stellar masses, SFR, colors, and indicators of the strength of the 4000Å stellar break (Section
4
). In the same section, we present average spectra obtained by stacking subsamples grouped by the
D_{n}4000
index and explore their properties. The analysis of specific scientific topics, including the histories of star formation and metal enrichment, stellar dynamics and structures, gas outflows, and ISM properties, is deferred to dedicated forthcoming works (Section
5
). All spectroscopic and photometric data will be released in conjunction with this paper, with the aim of accelerating progress in our understanding of how massive quiescent galaxies form and evolve, and to better inform future time allocation requests. Throughout this work, we make use of the AB system
(
Oke & Gunn 1983
)
to report magnitudes. We adopt a
\Lambda
CDM cosmology with
\Omega_{\rm m}=0.3
,
\Omega_{\Lambda}=0.7
, and
H_{0}=70\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}
.

2
The DeepDive program

The DeepDive program (PID #3567, PI: Valentino) is a JWST General Observer (GO) program targeting quiescent galaxies at
z\sim 3-4
with the NIRSpec and NIRCam instruments. Its primary goals are to derive fundamental physical properties of these galaxies, such as star formation histories, metallicities, and stellar velocity dispersions, from deep grating spectra and photometry (see Section
5
for more details). This section describes the target selection, observational data, and the procedures for data reduction.

2.1
Primary target selection

The primary targets of DeepDive are 11 bright (
K<23
mag), and therefore massive (
M_{\star}\sim 10^{11}\,M_{\odot}
), quenched galaxies with known spectroscopic redshifts from ground-based observations at
z=3.4-4

(
Glazebrook et al. 2017
;
Schreiber et al. 2018a
;
Schreiber et al. 2018b

Tanaka et al. 2019

Esdaile et al. 2021
;
Forrest et al. 2020
;
Valentino et al. 2020

3
. The target selection is based on an extension of the rest-frame
UVJ
colors that includes more recently quenched, post-starburst-like objects
(
Williams et al. 2009
;
Schreiber et al. 2018b
;
Belli et al. 2019
)
. The targets are scattered among different cosmological fields, only partially covered by the existing JWST and HST imaging previously, as detailed in Section
2.2
: PRIMER-UDS and COSMOS
(
Donnan et al. 2024
)
, COSMOS-Web
(
Casey et al. 2023
)
, CEERS-EGS
(

;
Finkelstein et al. 2023
)
, XMM-VIDEO
(
Jarvis et al. 2013
)

2.2
Imaging

As part of the DeepDive program, we acquired NIRCam imaging for the five targets previously lacking it (ID 78, 96, 115, 170, 179). We observed each field with four filters (F150W, F200W, F356W, and F444W) for a total integration time of
1,460
s each. We implemented a 4-point INTRAMODULE dithering pattern for all targets, except for ID 179, where we used a more compact INTRAMODULEBOX configuration. The observations were split into seven groups, each with one integration, for a total of 4 integrations per dithering position. The raw data were reduced as described in
Valentino et al. 2023
. Briefly, we retrieved level-2 products processed with the JWST pipeline and processed them with
grizli

(
Brammer 2023a
)
, where we used the
jwst_0989.pmap
calibration data and the most recent sky flats. All images are aligned to Gaia DR3
(
Gaia Collaboration et al. 2021
)
, coadded, and drizzled
(
Fruchter & Hook 2002
)
onto a common pixel scale of
0\aas@@fstack{\prime\prime}04
. This allowed us to reach an average
5\sigma
depth of 28.3 mag in the F444W image within
0\aas@@fstack{\prime\prime}15
circular radius apertures (without further aperture corrections). The
1\sigma
depth was estimated by randomly placing 2000 apertures in source-free regions and computing
1.4826\times\mathrm{MAD}
, where
\mathrm{MAD}
is the median absolute deviation.

2.3
Spectroscopy

Eight of the ten main targets have been observed in eight JWST/NIRSpec Micro-Shutter Assembly (MSA,
Jakobsen et al. 2022
) pointings as part of the DeepDive program. In each case, we used the G235M/F170LP medium resolution (
R\sim 1000
) grating and filter combination to cover wavelengths around the Balmer break for targets at
z>3.4
. Each observation is tailored to the brightness of the main target to ensure a similar S/N ratio on the stellar continuum emission at rest-frame optical wavelengths, resulting in different total integration times. The total integration time ranged from 2,300 s to 10,500 s (Table
9
). For all masks, we adopted a 3-point nodding pattern. We allocated at least five microshutters to the main targets in order to enclose enough background emission for an optimal subtraction. The rest of the fillers were allocated an average of three microshutters. During the mask preparation phase, we did not allow for any spectral overlap between the primary targets and other bright continuum emitters (e.g., dusty star-forming galaxies, Gillman et al., in prep.). However, lower-priority fillers, mostly comprised of line emitters, could have overlapping spectral traces. Here we focus on the main massive, quenched targets. Therefore, the overlap of spectral traces does not constitute an issue. The remaining two main targets (ID 80 and 111) had been previously observed as part of GO PID #3543
(
Carnall et al. 2024
, the Early eXtragalactic Continuum and Emission Line Science, EXCELS, PI: A. Carnall,)
. We thus used the raw data from this program and reduced their spectra as described for the rest of the spectra acquired in DeepDive. These targets, common to EXCELS, have been observed for
14,706
s with the G140M and G395M gratings, and for
19,958
s with the G235M grating, respectively. Besides these two targets, we searched for and added coverage of prism and medium resolution gratings from DJA whenever available for our main sample. This resulted in using the PRISM/CLEAR and G395M/F290LP spectra from the GO PID #4223
(
de Graaff et al. 2024a
, the Red Unknowns: Bright Infrared Extragalactic Survey, RUBIES, PIs: A. de Graaff and G. Brammer,)
for ID 196 and the PRISM/CLEAR spectrum from GO PID #2565 (PI: K. Glazebrook) for ID 80 and 134. All spectra have been homogeneously reduced using
MSAExp

(
Brammer 2023b
;
Heintz et al. 2025
;
de Graaff et al. 2024a
)
. The pipeline also determines the spectroscopic redshifts of the sources. It has been recently updated to recover the full red extension of the spectra allowed by the combination of the long-pass filter and the projection on the detector, as detailed in
Valentino et al. 2025
and
Pollock et al. 2025
(
v4.0
, Brammer et al. in prep.).

2.4
Flux correction

We corrected the grating spectra for residual flux losses by anchoring the spectroscopy to the photometry. We applied a polynomial correction function, with an order adapted to the number of available photometric data points in the observed spectral range (
n=0
,
n=1
, and
n=2
for
1-2
,
3-4
, or
>4
filters available, respectively). For the correction, we primarily considered the photometry from JWST and HST (Section
2.2
and Table
9
). However, due to the limited filter coverage of their fields, we also included the available ground-based photometry for IDs 78, 96, 115, 170, and 179. For sources in the COSMOS field not covered by the PRIMER survey, the SXDS field, and the XMM-LSS field, we adopted the ground-based measurements in the catalogs by
Weaver et al. 2022
,
Kubo et al. 2018
, and
Desprez et al. 2023
, respectively. We considered the available total, aperture-corrected photometry in all cases. Sources with IDs 80, 134, and 196 were observed with PRISM/CLEAR as part of other programs
(
de Graaff et al. 2024a
;
Nanayakkara et al. 2024
;
Nanayakkara et al. 2025
)
. In these cases, we first anchored the prism spectra to the photometry and then the grating spectra to the corrected prism spectra. Possible amplitude differences between prism and grating spectra can indeed arise from different shutter positions.

Finally, we introduced a second-order wavelength-dependent correction for the portion of the v4.0 grating spectra extended beyond the nominal cutoff. The correction is necessary to compensate for an imperfect calibration due to the lack of dedicated calibration spectra at certain wavelengths. We derived such a correction by modeling with a spline the ratio between the grating spectra and available prism spectra for a subsample of the DeepDive main targets (ID 80, 134, and 196) and in the archival collection (Section
3
), and we applied it to all available medium-resolution spectra. The corrections derived for each grating are shown in Appendix
C
. In the case of G235M/F170LP spectra as those collected in DeepDive, the correction amounts to up to 20 percent at
\lambda>3.1\,{\rm\mu m}
. The flux-corrected spectra of all primary targets in our program are shown in Figure
1
.

2.5
SED fitting

We derived stellar masses, SFRs, dust attenuation, and rest-frame colors of our targets by modeling their Spectral Energy Distributions (SEDs). For the purpose of presenting the general properties of our sample, we opted to model the photometry under common assumptions widely used in the literature for a straightforward comparison. More sophisticated modeling of the combined spectroscopic and photometric data will be reported in future work and adapted to the specific science goals (e.g., Hamadouche et al. in prep.). Here we use two SED fitting codes,
Eazy-py

(
Brammer et al. 2008
;
Brammer 2021
)
and
Bagpipes

(
Carnall et al. 2018
)

3
Archival search for grating spectra of quiescent galaxies up to
z\sim 5

To put our DeepDive survey in a broader context and systematically trace the redshift evolution of the properties of massive quenched systems, we compiled all publicly available JWST/NIRSpec grating spectra of galaxies with suppressed star formation. These were selected from the 67,099 spectra available in DJA (as of March 26, 2025), as well as from spectra of filler sources observed as part of the DeepDive program. We assigned different archival spectra to the same source by allowing for a maximum projected separation of
0.2\arcsec
between the slit positions. Since our aim is to study the stellar spectra of quiescent sources in the rest-frame optical, we selected only those sources with grating spectra covering wavelengths shorter than
\lambda_{\rm rest}<0.5\,{\rm\mu m}
, in order to focus on spectra comparable to those of the main DeepDive targets. We further imposed a minimum threshold on the median signal-to-noise ratio per pixel at
0.4\,{\rm\mu m}<\lambda_{\rm rest}<0.5\,{\rm\mu m}
greater than five to ensure that the stellar continuum emission was reliably detected.

Next, we compiled the available HST and JWST/NIRCam photometry for each target in the archive. Aperture-corrected total photometry measured within
0\aas@@fstack{\prime\prime}5
apertures is adopted, as derived with
SEP
using the original images without PSF matching. Ground-based photometry is also included for sources observed in the same MSA masks as the main DeepDive targets DD-ID
7
7

7

The ID in the DeepDive program.
78, 96, 115, 170, and 179. Using this photometry, flux corrections were applied to the spectra in the same way as for the primary DeepDive targets. Stellar population synthesis modeling of the photometry for all archival sources was also performed using the setup described in Section
2.5
.

We then classified galaxies in this continuum-selected sample as “quiescent” according to the following three traditional, yet distinct criteria:

1.

Valentino et al. 2023

\displaystyle(U-V)

\displaystyle>1.3,

\displaystyle(V-J)

\displaystyle<1.8,

\displaystyle(U-V)

\displaystyle>0.88\times(V-J)+0.29

Specific star formation rate (sSFR) selection. Sources were selected as quiescent galaxies if they have an sSFR from the SED fitting more than one dex below the star formation main sequence (SFMS) of
Schreiber et al. 2015

Popesso et al. 2023

Schreiber et al. 2015
SFMS are retained, except for a single source. Similarly, even if we apply the alternative criterion which

4.3
Composite spectra as a function of
D_{n}4000

In the previous sections, we introduced our DeepDive program and the archival search, and we assembled a large sample of quiescent galaxies with high-quality grating spectra from JWST/NIRSpec. By construction, each spectrum is characterized by high S/N on the stellar continuum, which will allow us to study their physical parameters (Section
5
). Here we showcase what can be achieved when we push even further the capabilities of the instrument, and stack tens of spectra to derive the average spectral shapes of massive quiescent galaxies at different times after their main quenching epoch.

We divide our combined sample based on their
D_{n}4000
index values. We, thus, remove sources whose spectra do not cover the
0.385\,{\rm\mu m}<\lambda_{\rm rest}<0.45\,{\rm\mu m}
range, retaining a final sample of 110 sources. We bin these galaxies into a high (
D_{n}4000>1.5
), an intermediate (
1.35<D_{n}4000<1.5
), and a low (
D_{n}4000<1.35
)
D_{n}4000
samples. These bins contain 26, 40, and 44 sources, respectively. We show in Figure
8
the distributions of the
D_{n}4000
index, stellar mass, and redshifts. The low
D_{n}4000
sample primarily consists of sources at higher redshift (with a median of
z\sim 3
) than the other samples. 16 out of 44 (
36\%
) spectra of the low
D_{n}4000
sample were obtained as part of DeepDive, highlighting again how this program complements other efforts to characterize massive, quenched systems in the distant Universe by covering relatively recently quenched systems. The high
D_{n}4000
sample consists of more massive galaxies, partially because they are at a lower redshift. Nonetheless, the difference in stellar masses among the three subsamples is marginal (within 0.5 dex).

To create the average spectra, we followed the method presented in
Onodera et al. 2012
. The spectra were first de-redshifted and normalized by the average flux at
0.44\,{\rm\mu m}<\lambda_{\rm rest}<0.48\,{\rm\mu m}
. They were then resampled on a 1Å rest-frame wavelength grid by linearly interpolating the original spectra. We then computed the average spectra by optimally weighting each spectrum by the inverse square of its noise array. The uncertainties (
\sigma
) on the average spectra were estimated by applying the jackknife method:

\sigma^{2}=\frac{N-1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(f-f_{i})^{2},

(2)

where
N
is the number of spectra used,
f
is the average spectrum determined by using all
N
spectra, and
f_{i}
is the average spectrum determined by using the
N-1
spectra removing
i
-th spectrum. The possible bias due to the limited sample size is corrected by the following equation:

f^{\prime}=f-(N-1)\left(\langle f_{i}\rangle-f\right),

(3)

where
f^{\prime}
is the bias-corrected average spectrum and
\langle f_{i}\rangle
is the mean of the
f_{i}
spectra. We use the
f^{\prime}
as the average spectrum of each subsample.

Figure
8
shows the stacked spectra of the galaxies grouped according to their
D_{n}4000
index. Thanks to the large number of objects in each subsample, a high signal-to-noise ratio is achieved for the stacked spectra. The median signal-to-noise ratio at
0.4\,{\rm\mu m}<\lambda_{\rm rest}<0.5\,{\rm\mu m}
of the stacked spectra of low-
D_{n}4000
, mid-
D_{n}4000
, and high-
D_{n}4000
sample is
{\rm S/N}=87
,
84
, and
77
, respectively. The spectra clearly become progressively redder with increasing
D_{n}4000
index, reflecting the older stellar populations in higher
D_{n}4000
samples. Additionally, the absorption line strengths differ among the three stacked spectra. In particular, the CaII absorption lines are stronger in the higher-
D_{n}4000
samples, consistent with their older stellar populations. Conversely, the brightest [O
II
], [O
III
], and H
\alpha
emission lines are found in the lowest-
D_{n}4000
bin. One possible explanation is AGN activity, as recently quenched galaxies with low
D_{n}4000
values may have a higher likelihood of hosting AGNs – similar to what is observed in post-starburst quiescent galaxies at high redshift
(
Belli et al. 2019
;

5
Science objectives of DeepDive

The purpose of this work is to introduce our program and to demonstrate how the DeepDive sample compares with a homogeneously selected set of similarly quenched galaxies, all benefiting from comparable data quality and coverage. While detailed analyses of the formation and evolution of massive quenched galaxies will be presented in forthcoming studies, we take this opportunity to briefly outline the main science cases that the DeepDive spectra will inform.

5.1
Star formation and metal enrichment histories

Recent JWST studies of massive quiescent galaxies suggest that their formation and quenching timescales are short, consistent with forming very early in the Universe and quenching rapidly via abrupt feedback mechanisms
(
Carnall et al. 2023
;
Carnall et al. 2024
;
Slob et al. 2024
;
Park et al. 2024a
;
de Graaff et al. 2024b
, see e.g)
. These formation timescales are encapsulated in the chemical abundance patterns of galaxies through powerful diagnostics such as
\alpha
-enhancement [
\alpha
/Fe]
(
Kriek et al. 2019
;
Jafariyazani et al. 2024
;
Beverage et al. 2021
;
Beverage et al. 2024
;
Beverage et al. 2025
, e.g.)
, due to the short timescales on which
\alpha
elements (e.g. Mg, O, Ne, Si) are produced in Type II (core-collapse) supernovae relative to iron-peak elements, which are produced mainly in Type Ia supernovae
(
Woosley & Weaver 1995
;
Thomas et al. 2003
, see e.g.)
. Owing to the high-quality spectroscopy afforded by the DeepDive program, we will be able to extract robust star-formation histories and metal abundances (e.g., [Mg/Fe]) at
z>3
to directly constrain these evolutionary timescales for statistically significant samples.

5.2
Stellar dynamics

The high S/N detection of stellar absorption features allows us to derive the stellar velocity dispersion. This parameter is known to be correlated with various fundamental properties of galaxies in the local Universe, as seen in the mass fundamental relation
(
Djorgovski & Davis 1987
)
. The stellar velocity dispersion has been measured in a handful of quiescent galaxies at
z\sim 3-4
using ground-based telescopes
(
Tanaka et al. 2019
;
Saracco et al. 2020
;
Esdaile et al. 2021
;
Forrest et al. 2022
, e.g.,)
, but the estimates are affected by large uncertainties, and the overall number of measurements is still severely limited. The DeepDive program will double the number of measurements of accurate stellar velocity dispersions at these redshifts. While still affected by uncertainties due to the geometry of the systems and possibly unresolved stellar rotation in compact objects
(
Slob et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
, the estimate of the stellar velocity dispersion will allow for studying the existence of a fundamental plane of massive quenched system up to
1.5
Gyr after the Big Bang, and to trace its time evolution with the rest of the archival sample
(
Mendel et al. 2020
;
Stockmann et al. 2021
;
van der Wel et al. 2022
, e.g.,)
. Thanks to NIRCam imaging, from which we can determine the half-light radius
(e.g.,
Ito et al. 2024
;
Kawinwanichakij et al. 2025
;
Yang et al. 2025
, for the size measurement of quiescent galaxies)
, we will also be in the position to estimate dynamical masses and put a constraint on the initial mass function by comparing it with stellar masses from SED modeling.

5.3
Ionized ISM properties

As visible in both the individual and average spectra presented above, we detect significant emission lines from the ionized interstellar medium in several massive quenched galaxies. The presence of such emission lines, powered by AGN, residual SF, shocks, or a combination of these mechanisms, is now a rather well-established finding allowed by JWST observations
(
Carnall et al. 2023
;
D’Eugenio et al. 2024
;
Baker et al. 2024
;
Park et al. 2024a
;
Bugiani et al. 2025
)
. Interestingly, the stacked spectra in bins of
D_{n}4000
seem to suggest the presence of stronger emission in more recently quenched systems (Figure
8
), and the line ratios might point at a significant contribution of AGN activity to the powering of the emission. The large available sample compiled in this work will allow for the exploration of trends on a solid statistical basis.

5.4
Outflow

Recent observations indicate that neutral gas outflows are common in quenched galaxies beyond the local universe
(
Davies et al. 2024
;
Belli et al. 2024
;
Sun et al. 2025
)
. Gas outflows could be powered by AGN, supernovae, and stellar winds from young stars. They play a crucial role in the baryon cycle of galaxies, regulating their evolution by removing gas and redistributing energy and momentum into the surrounding medium. Half of the DeepDive main targets (and more in the secondary targets) show signs of blue-shifted Na
i
D
\lambda\lambda
5,891, 5,897 absorption, a standard tracer of such outflows. The DeepDive sample will unlock the study of neutral gas outflows in uncharted redshift and mass regimes (Figure
5
), the missing transition phase between ancient star-forming galaxies and present-day quiescent galaxies.

6
Summary

We presented an overview of the DeepDive program, which aims to characterize the physical mechanisms driving the formation and evolution of early massive quiescent galaxies using medium-resolution spectra obtained with JWST/NIRSpec, along with an archival search for additional grating spectra of quiescent galaxies. In this work, we discussed the following points:

•

we introduced the primary DeepDive sample of 10 color-selected quenched systems at
z\sim 3-4
, whose large stellar masses (
\mathrm{log}(M_{\star}/M_{\odot})>10.7
) and young stellar ages fill a portion of the parameter space so far scarcely explored with deep JWST spectra.

•

we focused on the presentation of newly acquired four-band NIRCam images and G235M/F170LP NIRSpec spectra as part of DeepDive, and we delved into a few technical details of the data reduction. In particular, we concentrated on a careful flux calibration of the newly extended wavelength ranges covered by v4.0 spectra from the latest version of the
MSAExp
pipeline, developed as part of the DJA initiative. The wavelength extension results are crucial to cover spectral features such as the H
\alpha
+[N
II
] complex in all DeepDive primary targets.

•

based on measurements of the H
\alpha
emission, inaccessible from the ground for this redshift, we confirmed the quiescence of our main targets (
\mathrm{SFR}\sim 0-5

M_{\odot}
yr
-1
), and highlighted the presence of AGNs for two sources as revealed by broad wings in the emission lines.

•

to place the DeepDive targets in a broader context, we carried out an extensive search for archival quiescent galaxies with data quality and coverage comparable to those of the main DeepDive sample. Using three classical selection methods – the strength of the 4000Å break (quantified via the
D_{n}4000
index), the
UVJ
color diagram, and a specific star formation rate (sSFR) threshold – we identified
\sim 140
unique sources with high-S/N NIRSpec grating spectra. These archival quiescent galaxies are primarily located at
z\sim 2
. Combined with the DeepDive targets, the resulting sample spans a redshift range of
1<z<5
and covers 1.5 dex in stellar mass. All spectra were reduced and calibrated using the same methodology as for the DeepDive primary targets, allowing for consistent and systematic comparisons.

•

we find approximately a
90
% overlap between these three selection criteria for quiescent galaxies, while
70
% of the spectroscopic sample simultaneously satisfies all selections.

•

we map the
D_{n}4000
indices measured from the spectra onto
UVJ
rest-frame colors from the photometric modeling. As a natural consequence of the shorter time available to assemble and quench and selection criteria privileging bright sources, high-redshift quenched systems are caught closer to their main quenching epoch, thus displaying bluer colors and lower
D_{n}4000
index. The parameterization in Eq.
1
is a useful tool for comparing the classical
UVJ
selection, commonly used at
z\lesssim 2-3
, with the break strength criteria, which is increasingly popular for defining quenched systems at higher redshifts.

•

finally, we generate average spectral templates for massive quiescent galaxies at different post-quenching stages as tracked by
D_{n}4000
. The overall spectra are redder for higher
D_{n}4000
samples. Also, absorption line features change along with the
D_{n}4000
value, as expected from the correlation between age and
D_{n}4000
index. The emission lines are stronger for the lower
D_{n}4000
value sample, possibly due to residual star formation or AGN activity in more recently quenched galaxies. The achieved S/N is such that we detect various faint metal absorption features that were previously detectable only at low redshift, showing that we can constrain the metallicity even for quiescent galaxies at
z\geq 3
.

Such a large statistical sample, encompassing wide intervals of stellar masses, redshifts, ages, and several other properties, will be key to propelling research on the formation and evolution of massive quenched galaxies—following the approach described in Section
5
– beyond the current “discovery” phase and into one of statistical consolidation, where robust statistics will reveal the underlying physics. To help foster new studies, all homogeneously reduced and analyzed data used in this work, both from DeepDive and the archival search, will be made public.

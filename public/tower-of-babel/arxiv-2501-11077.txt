Isolated vertices in two duplication-divergence models with edge deletion
Tiffany Y. Y. Lo, Gesine Reinert, Ruihua Zhang

Abstract
Duplication-divergence models are a popular model for the evolution of gene and protein interaction networks. However, existing duplication-divergence models often neglect realistic features such as loss of interactions. Thus, in this paper we present two novel models that incorporate random edge deletions into the duplication-divergence framework. As in protein-protein interaction networks, with proteins as vertices and interactions as edges, by design isolated vertices tend to be rare, our main focus is on the number of isolated vertices; our main result gives lower and upper bounds for the proportion of isolated vertices, when the network size is large. Using these bounds we identify the parameter regimes for which almost all vertices are typically isolated; and also show that there are parameter regimes in which the proportion of isolated vertices can be bounded away from 0 and 1 with high probability. In addition, we find regimes in which the proportion of isolated vertices tends to be small. The proof relies on a standard martingale argument, which in turn requires a careful analysis of the first two moments of the expected degree distribution. The theoretical findings are illustrated by simulations, indicating that as the network size tends to infinity, the proportion of isolated vertices can converge to a limit that is neither 0 or 1.

Introduction

Networks are fundamental structures for describing complex systems. One example is given by physical interactions between proteins in an organism, which can be represented by a protein-protein interaction (PPI) networks with proteins as vertices and edges representing physical interactions. In the light of evolution of species, a natural question is how to model the evolution of such networks. For this purpose, duplication-divergence (DD) models have been explored

[
7
;
20
;

The standard duplication-divergence model
[

;
5
]

runs as follows. Starting from the initial graph
G_{m_{0}}
on
m_{0}
vertices,

[

first observed that a birth-catastrophe process is embedded in the evolution of the expected degree distributions over time

[

;

For

,

When

]
proves that the degree distribution of the connected component has a limit that can be characterised by the stationary distribution of a birth-catastrophe process, and this is used in
[

to prove a power-law behaviour for the degree distribution. The

case is also studied in detail in
[

]
, proving a central limit theorem for the logarithm of the degree of a uniformly chosen vertex. Other features of the model, such as the count of cliques and the degree of a fixed vertex, are studied in
[

;
12
]

Modifications to the model include that new vertices may connect to vertices which are not involved in the duplication-divergence step

Evolving PPI networks may exhibit not only divergence but also edge loss, for example by protein interactions losing functionality

;

. Random edge deletions can significantly affect network behaviour; for instance,

]
shows that such a mechanism can lead to different asymptotic degree distributions in preferential attachment models. DD models with edge deletions are also considered in

, but in a continuous-time setting. Both

Here we generalise the duplication-divergence model in

The paper is structured as follows. Section

•

With probability

With probability

The motivation for adding an isolated vertex at the deletion-and-addition step is to keep the total number of vertices fixed at

With probability

4
Recursion Formulas

The analysis of the two models in this paper relies heavily on recursion formulas for the expectations. First we state the recursion formulas for
N_{m,k}
in both models. Then, we show that in Model A,
\mathbbm{E}[(N_{m,k}-N_{m-1,k})^{2}]
can be bounded in terms of the second moment of the expected degree distribution, a finding which underpins our martingale argument in Section
6
, and we give an upper bound for the latter quantity.
The proofs are straightforward, and are deferred to
Section
7
.

Proposition 4.1
.

The recursion formulas for
N_{m,k}
,
k\geq 1
in both models are follows.

1.

For Model A, we have

\mathbb{E}(N_{m,0}-N_{m-1,0}\mid G_{m-1})=q(1-2r)\frac{N_{m-1,0}}{N_{m-1}}+q(1-r)\sum^{m-2}_{j=1}\frac{N_{m-1,j}}{N_{m-1}}p^{j}\\
+(1-q)\left(2+\frac{N_{m-1,1}-N_{m-1,0}}{N_{m-1}}\right),

(16)

whereas for
k\geq 1,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(N_{m,k}-N_{m-1,k}\mid G_{m-1})=\frac{N_{m-1,k-1}}{N_{m-1}}q\big(r+r(1-p)^{k-1}+(1-p)(k-1)\big)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{N_{m-1,k}}{N_{m-1}}q(r+(1-p)k)+q(1-r)\sum^{m-2}_{j=k}\frac{N_{m-1,j}}{N_{m-1}}{j\choose k}p^{j-k}(1-p)^{k}

\displaystyle\quad+qr\sum^{m-2}_{j=k}\frac{N_{m-1,j}}{N_{m-1}}{{j}\choose{k-1}}p^{j-k+1}(1-p)^{k-1}+(1-q)(k+1)\frac{N_{m-1,k+1}-N_{m-1,k}}{N_{m-1}}.

(17)

2.

For Model B, (
17
) holds with
p,q,r
replaced with
p_{m-1},q_{m-1},r_{m-1}
, and instead of (
16
) we have

\mathbb{E}(N_{m,0}-N_{m-1,0}\mid G_{m-1})=q_{m-1}(1-2r_{m-1})\frac{N_{m-1,0}}{N_{m-1}}+q_{m-1}(1-r_{m-1})\sum^{m-2}_{j=1}\frac{N_{m-1,j}}{N_{m-1}}p_{m-1}^{j}\\
+(1-q_{m-1})\left(1+\frac{N_{m-1,1}-N_{m-1,0}}{N_{m-1}}\right).

(18)

Proposition 4.2
.

For Model A,
we have

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}[(N_{m,0}-N_{m-1,0})^{2}\mid G_{m-1}]

\displaystyle\leq 5(1-q)\sum^{m-2}_{j=0}j^{2}\frac{N_{m-1,j}}{N_{m-1}}+5;

(19)

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}[(N_{m,k}-N_{m-1,k})^{2}\mid G_{m-1}]

\displaystyle\leq(3+2q)\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}j^{2}\frac{N_{m-1,j}}{N_{m-1}}+4,\quad\quad\mbox{ for }k\geq 1.

(20)

In view of Proposition
4.2
, an upper bound on the second moment of the expected degree distribution at time step
m-1
immediately yields an upper bound on
\mathbbm{E}[(N_{m,k}-N_{m-1,k})^{2}]
.
We denote
k^{th}
moment of the expected degree distribution at time
m
for Model A by

\displaystyle\psi_{k}(m)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}j^{k}\frac{\mathbbm{E}N_{m,j}}{m}.

(21)

Lemma 4.3
.

Let

\psi_{j}(m)
be as above,
\tau=6q-4pq+p^{2}q-3
and

) and (

).
Then, for Model A, there is a constant

\displaystyle\psi_{1}(m)\leq\begin{cases}C&\text{if $\kappa<0$}\\

Similarly, for

,

\displaystyle\psi_{2}(m)\leq\begin{cases}C&\quad\text{if $\tau<0$,}\\
C\log m&\quad\text{if $\tau=0$,}\\
Cm^{\tau}&\quad\text{if $\tau>0$.}\end{cases}

For

,

\displaystyle\psi_{2}(m)\leq\begin{cases}C\log m&\quad\text{if $\tau<0$,}\\
C\log^{2}m&\quad\text{if $\tau=0$,}\\

Finally, for

Cm^{\max\{\tau,\kappa\}}&\quad\text{if $\tau>0$ and $\tau\neq\kappa$,}\\

5
A Martingale Tool

A key tool for proving Theorems
3.2
and
3.4
is a modified version of
Lemma 1 in
[
1
]
, as stated below.

Lemma 5.1
.

Given
m_{0}\geq 0
,
\xi_{m_{0}}\geq 0
, let
(\xi_{m})_{m\geq m_{0}}
be a non-negative integrable process adapted to the filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{m})_{m\geq m_{0}}
, with
\mathcal{F}_{m_{0}}
being the trivial
\sigma
-algebra. Assume that for all
m\geq m_{0}+1
,

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}[\xi_{m}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m-1}]\geq\Bigl(1-{\frac{u}{m-1}}\Bigr)\xi_{m-1}+v_{m}

(26)

for some
0\leq u\leq m_{0}
, and
(v_{m})_{m\geq m_{0}}
is a non-negative predictable process such that almost surely, for some constant
v\geq 0
,

v_{m}\geq v,\quad m\geq m_{0}.

(27)

Suppose also there are constants
\widetilde{C},\delta>0
not depending on
m
such that

\mathbbm{E}[(\xi_{m}-\xi_{m-1})^{2}]\leq\widetilde{C}m^{1-\delta},\quad m\geq m_{0}.

(28)

Let

\displaystyle\alpha_{\delta}

\displaystyle:=\mathbbm{1}[\delta<2]\cdot\delta/4+\mathbbm{1}[\delta\geq 2]\cdot 1/2;

(29)

\displaystyle\beta_{m,\delta}

\displaystyle:=\mathbbm{1}[\delta<2]\cdot m^{-\delta/2}+\mathbbm{1}[\delta=2]\cdot m^{-1}\log m+\mathbbm{1}[\delta>2]\cdot m^{-1}.

(30)

Then there exists a constant
C=C(m_{0},u)>0

such that with probability at least
1-C\beta_{m,\delta}
,

\frac{\xi_{m}}{m}\geq\frac{v}{u+1}-Cm^{-\alpha_{\delta}}.

(31)

If (
26
) holds with strict equality, and instead (or in addition) of (
27
), there is a constant
V>0
such that

v_{m}\leq V,\quad m\geq m_{0},

(32)

then there exists a constant
C=C(\widetilde{C},m_{0},u,\delta)>0
such that with probability at least
1-C\beta_{m,\delta}
,

\frac{\xi_{m}}{m}\leq\frac{V}{u+1}+Cm^{-\alpha_{\delta}}.

(33)

Remark 5.2
.

For Model A, the inequality (
28
),
with
\xi_{m}=N_{m,k}
, is much easier to verify than the analogous condition set out in Lemma 1 of
[
1
]
, which is

\mathbb{E}[(N_{m,k}-N_{m-1,k})^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m-1}]\leq O\big(m^{1-\delta}\big)\quad\text{for some $\delta>0$.}

(34)

The trade-off is the weaker conclusion than that obtained in
[
1
]
, where (
31
) is replaced with
\liminf_{m\to\infty}\xi_{m}/m\geq v/(u+1)
almost surely (and similarly for the upper bound). In the proof of Theorem
3.6
, we show that (
34
) can be easily verified at least when
p=q=1
. We also note that
0\leq N_{m,k}\leq m
and hence
N_{m,k}
is integrable.

Proof of Lemma
5.1
.

Define
c_{m_{0}}=1
and

c_{m}:=\prod^{m}_{i=m_{0}+1}\Bigl\{1-\frac{u}{i-1}\Bigr\}^{-1}=\frac{\Gamma(m)\Gamma(m_{0}-u)}{\Gamma(m-u)\Gamma(m_{0})},\quad m\geq{m}_{0},

(35)

which is positive for all
m\geq{m}_{0}
, since
u<i
for all
i\geq{m}_{0}
.
By (
26
) and (
35
), for
m\geq m_{0}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[c_{m}\xi_{m}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m-1}]\geq c_{m}\left(1-{\frac{u}{m-1}}\right)\xi_{m-1}+c_{m}v_{m}=c_{m-1}\xi_{m-1}+c_{m}v_{m}.

(36)

Since
v_{m}
are non-negative,
(c_{m}\xi_{m}{-c_{m_{0}}\xi_{m_{0}}})_{m\geq m_{0}}
is therefore a submartingale with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{m-1}
.
Moreover, since
\xi_{m}
is square integrable by (
28
), so is
c_{m}\xi_{m}
. Now, write the Doob decomposition of the submartingale
(c_{m}\xi_{m}{-c_{m_{0}}\xi_{m_{0}}})_{m\geq m_{0}}
, so that

c_{m}\xi_{m}{-c_{m_{0}}\xi_{m_{0}}}=M_{m}+A_{m},

(37)

where

M_{m}=\sum^{m}_{i=m_{0}+1}(c_{i}\xi_{i}-\mathbbm{E}[c_{i}\xi_{i}\mid\mathcal{F}_{i-1}])
, with
{M_{m_{0}}=0},

is a mean zero martingale and

A_{m}=\sum_{i=m_{0}+1}^{m}(\mathbbm{E}[c_{i}\xi_{i}\mid\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]-c_{i-1}\xi_{i-1}),
with
{A_{m_{0}}=0},

is predictable with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{m}
. By (
36
),

A_{m}-A_{m-1}=\mathbbm{E}[c_{m}\xi_{m}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m-1}]-c_{m-1}\xi_{m-1}\geq c_{m}v_{m}\geq 0,\quad m\geq m_{0}+1,

(38)

implying that
(A_{m})_{m\geq m_{0}}
is a non-negative and (weakly) increasing. Moreover, a computation similar to
[
10
, Lemma 13]
(taking
a=0
and
b=-u
there) yields

\displaystyle\sum^{m}_{i=m_{0}+1}c_{i}=\sum^{m}_{i=m_{0}+1}\frac{\Gamma(m_{0}-u)\Gamma(i)}{\Gamma(m_{0})\Gamma(i-u)}=\frac{\Gamma(m_{0}-u)}{\Gamma(m_{0})}\frac{m^{u+1}}{u+1}+O(m^{u}).

(39)

Thus by (
38
), (
27
) and (
39
),

\displaystyle A_{m}\geq\sum^{m}_{i=m_{0}+1}c_{i}v_{i}\geq v\sum^{m}_{i=m_{0}+1}c_{i}\geq\frac{\Gamma(m_{0}-u)}{\Gamma(m_{0})}\frac{vm^{u+1}}{u+1}+O(m^{u}).

(40)

We now show that when
m
is large,
|M_{m}|
is typically much smaller than
A_{m}
. Let
B_{m}
be the increasing process associated with the Doob decomposition of the submartingale
M_{m}^{2}
. Since
M_{m}
is a mean zero martingale, a straightforward computation gives

B_{m}=\sum_{i=m_{0}+1}^{m}\big(\mathbbm{E}\big[M_{i}^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}\big]-M_{i-1}^{2}\big)=\sum_{i=m_{0}+1}^{m}\mathbbm{E}\big[(M_{i}-M_{i-1})^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}\big]=\sum^{m}_{i=m_{0}+1}\mathrm{Var}(c_{i}\xi_{i}\mid\mathcal{F}_{i-1}).

(41)

Thus,

\displaystyle B_{m}=\sum^{m}_{i=m_{0}+1}\mathrm{Var}(c_{i}(\xi_{i}-\xi_{i-1})\mid\mathcal{F}_{i-1})

\displaystyle\leq D_{m}:=\sum^{m}_{i=m_{0}+1}c^{2}_{i}\mathbbm{E}[(\xi_{i}-\xi_{i-1})^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{i-1}].

Taking expectation and using the fact that
c_{m}
is increasing,

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}D_{m}

\displaystyle=\sum^{m}_{i=m_{0}+1}\mathbbm{E}[c_{i}^{2}(\xi_{i}-\xi_{i-1})^{2}]\leq\sum^{m}_{i=m_{0}+1}c_{m}^{2}\mathbbm{E}[(\xi_{i}-\xi_{i-1})^{2}].

(42)

Below,
C>0
is a constant that may vary, and depends on
\widetilde{C}
,
m_{0}
,
u
and
\delta
. By applying (
28
) to (
42
),

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}D_{m}\leq c_{m}^{2}\sum^{m}_{i=m_{0}+1}\widetilde{C}i^{1-\delta}\leq\begin{cases}Cm^{2-\delta}c_{m}^{2}&\text{if $\delta<2$;}\\
\widetilde{C}(\log m)c_{m}^{2}&\text{if $\delta=2$;}\\
Cm_{0}^{2-\delta}c_{m}^{2}&\text{if $\delta>2$.}\end{cases}

(43)

Let
d_{m}:=m^{1-\alpha_{\delta}}c_{m}
,
and note
d_{m}=m^{1+u-\alpha_{\delta}}(1+O(m^{-1}))
. By (
41
),
\mathbbm{E}B_{m}=\mathbbm{E}M_{m}^{2}
.
So by Markov’s inequality, (
29
), (
43
), and separately considering the cases,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|M_{m}|\geq d_{m})=\mathbb{P}(M^{2}_{m}\geq d_{m}^{2})\leq\frac{\mathbbm{E}M_{m}^{2}}{d_{m}^{2}}=\frac{\mathbbm{E}B_{m}}{d_{m}^{2}}\leq\frac{\mathbbm{E}D_{m}}{d_{m}^{2}}\leq\begin{cases}Cm^{-\delta/2}&\text{if $\delta<2$;}\\
\widetilde{C}m^{-1}\log m&\text{if $\delta=2$;}\\
Cm^{-1}&\text{if $\delta>2$.}\end{cases}

(44)

Since
c_{m_{0}}\xi_{m_{0}}\geq 0
, by (
37
) we also have

\displaystyle c_{m}\xi_{m}\geq{A_{m}}-|M_{m}|.

(45)

In view of (
40
) and (
44
), we can deduce that there is a constant
C>0
such that with probability at least
1-Cm^{-\beta_{m,\delta}}
,

\displaystyle A_{m}-|M_{m}|\geq\frac{\Gamma(m_{0}-u)}{\Gamma(m_{0})}\frac{vm^{u+1}}{u+1}-d_{m}+O\big(m^{u}\big).

(46)

Furthermore, by Stirling’s formula,

c_{m}=\frac{\Gamma(m_{0}-u)}{\Gamma(m_{0})}m^{u}\big(1+O\big(m^{-1}\big)\big);

combining this with (
45
) and (
46
), and noting that
0<\alpha_{\delta}\leq 1/2
,

\displaystyle\frac{\xi_{m}}{m}\geq\frac{A_{m}-|M_{m}|}{mc_{m}}\geq\frac{v}{u+1}-Cm^{-\alpha_{\delta}},

which is (
31
).

Similarly, assume that instead of (or in addition to) (
27
), we have (
32
), and (
26
) holds with equality.
Then,
(c_{m}\xi_{m}-c_{m_{0}}\xi_{m_{0}})_{m\geq m_{0}}
is still a submartingale.
Thus, using
c_{m}\xi_{m}\leq A_{m}+|M_{m}|+c_{m_{0}}\xi_{m_{0}},

we have from (
44
) that
there is a constant
C>0
such that with probability at least
1-Cm^{-\beta_{m,\delta}}
,

\displaystyle c_{m}\xi_{m}\leq A_{m}+d_{m}+c_{m_{0}}\xi_{m_{0}}.

Furthermore, it follows from (
37
) that
A_{m}-A_{m-1}=c_{m}v_{m}
, and
(
32
)
further implies that
A_{m}=\sum_{i=m_{0}}^{m}c_{i}v_{i}\leq V\sum_{i=m_{0}}^{m}c_{i}.

Hence the assertion (
33
) follows from a computation similar to (
40
).
∎

Proofs of Theorems
3.1
,
3.2
and

With the previous preparation, we now set out to prove the main results. We take

\displaystyle u=1+2q(r-1)\quad\text{and}\quad v_{m}=(1-q)\Bigl(2+\frac{N_{m-1,1}}{m-1}\Bigr)+q(1-r)\sum^{m-2}_{j=1}\frac{N_{m-1,j}}{m-1}p^{j},

(47)

and note that almost surely, for all
m\geq m_{0}
,

\displaystyle v:=2(1-q)\leq v_{m}\leq V:=3(1-q)+pq(1-r).

(48)

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Taking expectations in (
16
) yields

\mathbbm{E}N_{m,0}=\mathbbm{E}N_{m-1,0}\left(1-\frac{u}{m-1}\right)+v_{m}\leq\mathbbm{E}N_{m-1,0}\left(1-\frac{u}{m-1}\right)+V.

Dividing by
m
and taking the upper bound gives a recursion of the form

g(m+1)\leq g(m)\left(1-\frac{u}{m}\right)+\frac{V}{m},

where
g(m)=\mathbbm{E}(N_{m,0})/m
. This is equivalent to

g(m+1)-\frac{V}{u}\leq\left(1-\frac{u}{m}\right)\left(g(m)-\frac{V}{u}\right),

which has as solution

g(m+1)-\frac{V}{u}\leq\left(g(m_{0})-\frac{V}{u}\right)\prod^{m}_{i=m_{0}}\left(1-\frac{u}{m}\right)=O(m^{-u}),

where
u>0
by assumption.
The stated upper bound on
\mathbbm{E}(N_{m})/m
thus follows immediately. The lower bound can be proved similarly using
v
in (
48
) and we therefore omit the details.
∎

The next proof again relies on the recurrence formulas.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

We prove the lower bound claimed in (
31
) by applying Lemma
5.1
to the recursion formula (
16
) for
N_{m,0}
, taking
u
and
v_{m}
as in (
47
), and
\xi_{m,0}=N_{m,0}
.
Then noting that
N_{m}=m
for Model A, (
16
) reads

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\xi_{m,0}\mid G_{m-1})=\xi_{m-1,0}\left(1-\frac{u}{m-1}\right)+v_{m}.

First we verify the conditions for applying Lemma
5.1
.
Observe that
u\geq 0
when
r=1
or
r<1
with
q\leq 1/(2(1-r))
, and at least one of these conditions holds under the theorem. Additionally, we have that
u\leq 1<m_{0}
for all
m\geq m_{0}\geq 2
; where
m_{0}\geq 2
by assumption. We choose
p,q
such that
\tau<1
, so that
\kappa=4q-2pq-2<1
; see Remark
4.4
. By (
21
) and combining the bounds in Lemma
4.3
for different values of
\tau,\kappa
, there is a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\psi_{2}(m)

\displaystyle\leq C\log^{2}m\cdot\mathbbm{1}[\tau\leq 0,\kappa\leq 0]+Cm^{\max\{\tau,\kappa\}}\log m\cdot\big(1-\mathbbm{1}[\tau\leq 0,\kappa\leq 0]\big).

Thus, by the last display, Proposition
4.2
, and choosing
0<\delta<\min\{1,1-\max\{\tau,\kappa\}\}
, for
m
large enough,
\mathbbm{E}[(\xi_{m,0}-\xi_{m-1,0})^{2}]\leq Cm^{1-\delta}
.
Furthermore, almost surely, (
48
) holds for all
m\geq m_{0}
.
Thus, the conditions set out in Lemma
5.1
are met, including (
26
) with strict equality, and with
\alpha_{\delta}=\delta/4
and
\beta_{m,\delta}=m^{-\delta/2}
in (

) and (
30
). It follows
that there exists a constant
C>0
such that with probability at least
1-Cm^{-\delta/2}
,

\displaystyle\frac{v}{u+1}-Cm^{-\delta/4}\leq\frac{N_{m,0}}{m}\leq\frac{V}{u+1}+Cm^{-\delta/4},

with
v/(u+1)=\rho_{0}
and
V/(u+1)=\rho_{1}
, as in (
3
) and (
4
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.

The proof is similar to the one of Theorem
3.2
.
When
p=1
, then we have that
\sum_{j\geq 1}p^{j}N_{m,j}/m=1-N_{m,0}/m
and so the recursion formula (
16
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}(N_{m,0}\mid G_{m-1})

\displaystyle=N_{m-1,0}\Bigl(1+\frac{q(1-2r)-q(1-r)-(1-q)}{m-1}\Bigr)+q(1-r)+(1-q)\Bigl(2+\frac{N_{m-1,1}}{m-1}\Bigr).

We take
\xi_{m}=N_{m,0}
as before, and

\displaystyle u^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q(1-r)+(1-q)-q(1-2r)=1-q+qr,

\displaystyle v^{\prime}_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q(1-r)+(1-q)\Bigl(2+\frac{N_{m-1,1}}{m-1}\Bigr).

Clearly,
u^{\prime}\geq 0
for all
q,r\in[0,1]
, and
u^{\prime}\leq 1<m_{0}
. We have, almost surely for all

{N_{m,j}}\leq m
,
\widetilde{N}^{(1)}_{m,k}=\sum^{m-1}_{j=k}|\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{(1)}_{m,k}\cap\mathcal{N}_{m,j}|
and
(x+1)^{2}\leq 5x^{2}+4
for non-negative integers

, we find

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}[(N_{m,0}-N_{m-1,0})^{2}\mid G_{m-1}]

\displaystyle\leq(1-q)\sum^{m-2}_{k=0}\sum^{m-2}_{j=0}(k+2)^{2}\frac{|\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{(1)}_{{m-1},k}\cap\mathcal{N}_{{m-1},j}|}{{m-1}}\mathbf{1}[j\geq k]+1

\displaystyle\leq(1-q)\sum^{m-2}_{j=0}\sum^{m-2}_{k=0}(j+2)^{2}\frac{|\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{(1)}_{{m-1},k}\cap\mathcal{N}_{{m-1},j}|}{{m-1}}+1

\displaystyle\leq(1-q)\sum^{m-2}_{j=0}(j+2)^{2}\frac{N_{{m-1},j}}{{m-1}}+1

\displaystyle\leq 5(1-q)\sum^{m-2}_{j=0}j^{2}\frac{N_{{m-1},j}}{{m-1}}+5,

which is (
19
). For
k\geq 2
, we again consider the cases of duplication-and-divergence and deletion-and-addition separately. Denote by
V
the parent chosen at time
m
, and recall that the parent-child link is formed independently of the other edges of the child. Hence,

\displaystyle q\mathbbm{E}[(N_{m,k}-N_{m-1,k})^{2}\mid G_{m-1};\mbox{duplication-divergence}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q\sum_{l=0}^{m-2}\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}\sum_{s=0}^{m-2}\mathbbm{P}\big(V\in\mathcal{N}_{m-1,l}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{m-1,j}^{(k)}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{m-1,s}^{(k-1)}\mid G_{m-1}\big)\mathbf{1}[j+s\leq l]

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\mathbbm{E}\big[(N_{m,k}-N_{m-1,k})^{2}\mid G_{m-1};\mbox{duplication-divergence};V\in\mathcal{N}_{m-1,l}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{m-1,j}^{(k)}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{m-1,s}^{(k-1)}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q\sum_{l=0}^{m-2}\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}\sum_{s=0}^{m-2}\frac{\big|\mathcal{N}_{m-1,l}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{m-1,j}^{(k)}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{m-1,s}^{(k-1)}\big|}{{m-1}}\mathbf{1}[j+s\leq l]\sum_{a=0}^{j}\sum_{b=0}^{s}\sum_{c=0}^{l-j-s}p(a,b,c)

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\big\{r\big[\mathbf{1}(l=k-1)-\mathbf{1}(l=k)+\mathbf{1}(a+b+c+1=k)-a+b\big]^{2}+(1-r)\big[\mathbf{1}(a+b+c=k)-a+b\big]^{2}\big\}.

where
p(a,b,c)
is the probability of the event that the child retains
a
edges to neighbours of
V
with degree
k
,
b
edges to those with degree
k-1
, and
c
edges to other neighbours of
V
.
For
0\leq a,b\leq l
,

\displaystyle\big(\mathbf{1}(l=k-1)-\mathbf{1}(l=k)+\mathbf{1}(a+b+c+1=k)-a+b\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(b-a)^{2}+4|b-a|+4

\displaystyle\leq l^{2}+4l+4\leq 5l^{2}+4;

where the last inequality is valid for non-negative integers
l
. Similarly,
\big(\mathbf{1}(a+b+c=k)-a+b\big)^{2}\leq 3l^{2}+1.
Thus, noting that
\sum^{j}_{a=0}\sum^{s}_{b=0}\sum^{l-j-s}_{c=0}p(a,b,c)=1
,

\displaystyle q\mathbbm{E}[(N_{m,k}-N_{m-1,k})^{2}|G_{m-1};\mbox{duplication}]

(71)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle q\sum_{l=0}^{m-2}(5l^{2}+4)\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}\sum_{s=0}^{m-2}\frac{\big|\mathcal{N}_{m-1,l}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{m-1,j}^{(k)}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{m-1,s}^{(k-1)}\big|}{{m-1}}\mathbf{1}[j+s\leq l]\cdot

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 5q\sum_{l=0}^{m-2}l^{2}\frac{N_{m-1,l}}{{m-1}}+4q.

On the other hand, the overall change in the count of vertex of degree
k
is
s-j-\mathbf{1}[l=k]
, if a vertex in
\mathcal{N}_{m-1,l}\cap\mathcal{\widetilde{N}}^{(k)}_{m-1,j}\cap\mathcal{\widetilde{N}}^{(k+1)}_{m-1,s}
is selected for deletion-addition. So by a similar argument, we obtain

\displaystyle(1-q)\mathbbm{E}[(N_{m,k}-N_{m-1,k})^{2}\mid G_{m-1};\mbox{deletion}]

(72)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-q)\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{s=0}^{m}\frac{\big|\mathcal{N}_{m-1,l}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{m-1,j}^{(k)}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{m-1,s}^{(k+1)}\big|}{{m-1}}\mathbf{1}[j+s\leq l]\cdot(s-j-\mathbf{1}[l=k])^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-q)\sum_{l=0}^{m-2}(3l^{2}+1)\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\sum_{s=0}^{m-1}\frac{\big|\mathcal{N}_{m-1,l}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{m-1,j}^{(k)}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{m-1,s}^{(k+1)}\big|}{{m-1}}\mathbf{1}[j+s\leq l]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 3(1-q)\sum_{l=0}^{m-2}l^{2}\frac{N_{m-1,l}}{{m-1}}+1-q.

By (
71
) and (
72
),

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}[(N_{m,k}-N_{m-1,k})^{2}\mid G_{m-1}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(3+2q)\sum_{l=0}^{m-2}l^{2}\frac{N_{m-1,l}}{{m-1}}+4,

proving (
20
).
∎

Proof of Proposition
4.3
.

We start by proving the claim for

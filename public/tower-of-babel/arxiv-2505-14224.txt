1.1
Notations and Preliminaries

The Poincaré ball model of hyperbolic space, denoted by
\mathbb{B}^{N}
, is defined as
B(0,1)\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
equipped with the Riemannian metric:

\left(g_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}\right)_{ij}=\left(\frac{2}{1-|x|^{2}}\right)^{2}\delta_{ij}.
Another commonly used model for hyperbolic space is the upper half space model, denoted
\mathbb{H}^{N}
, which consists of the upper half space
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}=\left\{\left(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:x_{N}>0\right\}
, endowed with the Riemannian metric
\left(g_{\mathbb{H}^{N}}\right)_{ij}=\left(\frac{1}{x_{N}}\right)^{2}\delta_{ij}.

An isometry
M:\mathbb{B}^{N}\to\mathbb{H}^{N}
between these two models is given by

\displaystyle M\left(x\right)=M\left((x^{\prime},x_{N})\right)=\left(\frac{2x^{\prime}}{\left|x^{\prime}\right|^{2}+\left(1+x_{N}\right)^{2}},\frac{1-\left|x\right|^{2}}{\left|x^{\prime}\right|^{2}+\left(1+x_{N}\right)^{2}}\right),

(4)

with the property
M=M^{-1}
. We denote the hyperbolic distance between two points
x,y\in\mathbb{B}^{N}
as
d_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x,y)
and has the form

\displaystyle d_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=\cosh^{-1}\left(1+\frac{2|x-y|^{2}}{\left(1-|x|^{2}\right)\left(1-|y|^{2}\right)}\right).

(5)

We denote by
B\left(x,r\right):=\left\{y\in\mathbb{B}^{N}:d_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x,y)<r\right\}
, the hyperbolic ball centered at
x\in\mathbb{B}^{N}
with radius
r>0
, and the corresponding hyperbolic sphere by
S\left(x,r\right):=\left\{y\in\mathbb{B}^{N}:d_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}(x,y)=r\right\}.
Here we remark that, if the center is at the origin, the hyperbolic and Euclidean spheres coincide, i.e.,
S\left(0,r\right)=S_{E}\left(0,\tanh\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)\right),
where
S_{E}\left(0,\tanh\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)\right)
denotes a Euclidean sphere of radius
\tanh\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)
centered at the origin.

Exponential map on
\mathbb{B}^{N}
:
Given that hyperbolic space is a complete manifold, it follows from the Hopf-Rinow theorem that
\mathbb{B}^{N}
is geodesically complete. Consequently, the exponential map at the origin
\mathrm{exp}_{0}:T_{0}(\mathbb{B}^{N})\to\mathbb{B}^{N}
is well defined and expressed as

\displaystyle\mathrm{exp}_{0}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sinh\left(2|z|\right)}{1+\cosh\left(2|z|\right)}\frac{z}{|z|},\quad\forall z\in T_{0}\left(\mathbb{B}^{N}\right),

(6)

where
|z|
is the Euclidean norm in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Since the injectivity radius of
\mathbb{B}^{N}
at the origin is infinite,
\mathrm{exp}_{0}
is a diffeomorphism from the tangent space
T_{0}\left(\mathbb{B}^{N}\right)
onto
\mathbb{B}^{N}
. Furthermore, for any
r>0
, the map
\mathrm{exp}_{0}:B_{T_{0}\left(\mathbb{B}^{N}\right)}\left(z,r\right)\to B\left(\mathrm{exp_{0}}(z),r\right)
is also a diffeomorphism, where
B_{T_{0}\left(\mathbb{B}^{N}\right)}\left(z,r\right)
refers to a ball with center at
0
and radius
r
inside the tangent space
T_{0}\left(\mathbb{B}^{N}\right)
. The tangent space
T_{0}\left(\mathbb{B}^{N}\right)
can be identified as the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and the metric in tangent space is the same as Euclidean distance. For convenience, we denote
B_{T_{0}\left(\mathbb{B}^{N}\right)}\left(z,r\right)
as
B_{E}(z,r)
. We also use the following notations:

\displaystyle\overline{B\left(\mathrm{exp_{0}}(z),r\right)}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{exp_{0}}\left(\overline{B_{E}(z,r)}\right),\quad\text{for}\;r>0.

\displaystyle A_{E}\left(z;r_{2},r_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=B_{E}(z,r_{1})\setminus\overline{B_{E}(z,r_{2})},\quad\text{for}\;r_{1}>r_{2}>0.

\displaystyle A\left(\mathrm{exp_{0}}(z);r_{2},r_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{exp_{0}}\left(A_{E}\left(z;r_{2},r_{1}\right)\right),\quad\text{for}\;r_{1}>r_{2}>0.

Proof.

For every

\displaystyle u\circ\tau_{b}(gx)

\displaystyle=u\left(\frac{\left(1-|b|^{2}\right)(gx)+\left(|gx|^{2}+2(gx)\cdot b+1\right)b}{|b|^{2}|gx|^{2}+2(gx)\cdot b+1}\right)

\displaystyle=u\left(\frac{\left(1-|b|^{2}\right)(gx)+\left(|x|^{2}-2x_{N}\cdot b_{N}+1\right)\left(g\left(0,\cdots,0,-b_{N}\right)\right)}{|b|^{2}|x|^{2}-2x_{N}\cdot b_{N}+1}\right)

\displaystyle=-u\circ\tau_{-b}(x).

∎

A cohomological invariant for algebras of degree 8 and exponent 2 in characteristic 2
Ahmed Laghribi, Nico Lorenz

Abstract
. Our aim in this paper is to extend a work of Sivatski [ Siv22 ] to characteristic 2. More precisely, for F F a field of characteristic 2 2 and a central simple algebra A A of exponent 2 that splits over a triquadratic extension of F F of separability degree at least 4, we attach a cohomological invariant 𝗂𝗇𝗏 ⁡ ( A ) ∈ H 2 3 ​ ( F ) / G \mathsf{inv}(A)\in H_{2}^{3}(F)/G , where H 2 3 ​ ( F ) H_{2}^{3}(F) is the third Kato-Milno cohomology group and G G is a subgroup of H 2 3 ​ ( F ) H_{2}^{3}(F) divisible by the Brauer class of A A . As an application, we will relate the decomposability of the algebra in degree 8 to the vanishing of 𝗂𝗇𝗏 ⁡ ( A ) \mathsf{inv}(A) . Moreover, we will use this invariant to prove some descent results for central simple algebras and quadratic forms over biquadratic extensions.

1.
Introduction

Throughout this article,
F
denotes a field of characteristic 2.
Given a central simple algebra
A
over
F
, it is a difficult problem to know whether it is decomposable or not, i.e. whether there are nontrivial central simple algebras
A_{1},A_{2}
over
F
such that
A\cong A_{1}\otimes_{F}A_{2}
.
Recall that for
i\in\{1,2\}
, every central simple algebra of degree
2^{i}
and exponent
2
is Brauer equivalent to a product of
i
quaternion algebras.
The situation is more complicated for degree at least
8
and exponent
2
.
In this case, Amitsur-Rowen-Tignol
[
ART79
, Theorem 5.1]
in characteristic different from 2 and Rowen
[
Row84
, Section 3]
in characteristic 2 provided examples of indecomposable algebras of degree 8 and exponent 2.
Later, Karpenko produced examples of indecomposable algebras of degree
p^{n}
and exponent
p
except for the case
p=2=n
, see
[
Kar98
, Corollary 5.4]
.

Another important topic of a somewhat different flavour is the descent problem: given a field extension
K/F
and a central simple algebra
A
over
K
, determine whether there is an
F
-algebra
A^{\prime}
such that
A\cong A^{\prime}_{K}
.
Recently, in the spirit of what was done before by Barry in
[
Bar14
]
, Barry, Chapman and Laghribi
[
BCL20
]
studied the descent problem over separable quadratic extensions
K/F
for biquaternion algebras with trivial corestriction.
They introduced an invariant
\delta_{K/F}
that vanishes for such a
K
-algebra
B
if and only if
B
is defined over
F
up to isomorphism.
Moreover they applied this invariant to construct an indecomposable algebra of degree
8
and exponent
2
over a field of cohomological
2
-dimension 3, demonstrating a connection between decomposability and descent.

In the spirit of finding invariants that encode information on the algebra, Sivatski introduced in
[
Siv22
]
, for fields of characterstic not 2, an invariant for any central simple algebra of exponent
2
split by a triquadratic extension.
In particular, when the degree is
8
, this invariant vanishes if and only if the algebra decomposes into a tensor product of three quaternion algebras related to the triquadratic extension.
It has been also applied to prove results about the descent for algebras and quadratic forms.

Our aim in this paper is to extend most of
[
Siv22
]
to fields of characteristic 2.
We will not restrict ourselves to separable triquadratic extensions but will consider more generally the the case of extensions of the form
L=K(\wp^{-1}(a),\wp^{-1}(b))
, where
K/F
is an arbitrary quadratic extension.
In the next section, we will recall some background on quadratic forms and differential forms in characteristic 2.
After that, in
Section

3
we construct the invariant

\mathsf{inv}(A)\in H_{2}^{3}(F)/[A]\wedge\mathrm{dlog}(\operatorname{im}(\mathrm{N}_{K(\wp^{-1}(a+b))/F})\setminus\{0\}),

where
H_{2}^{3}(F)
is the third Kato-Milne cohomology group,
[A]
is the class of
A
in
\mathrm{Br}_{2}(F)
and
\mathrm{dlog}(\alpha)=\frac{\mathrm{d}\alpha}{\alpha}\in\Omega^{1}_{F}
for any
\alpha\in F^{\ast}
with
\Omega^{1}_{F}
the space of 1-differential forms, see
Section

2
for details.

In
Section

4
, we prove that this invariant
\mathsf{inv}(A)
is trivial if and only if
[A]=[Q\otimes[a+b,v)\otimes Q^{\prime}]
, where
Q,Q^{\prime}
are quaternion such that
Q^{\prime}
splits over
K
.
These conditions are further equivalent to
[A_{K(\wp^{-1}(a+b))}]
being defined over
F
, see
Proposition

4.3
.
In
Example

4.6
, we construct a biquaternion
F
-algebra
B
such that
\mathsf{inv}(B)\neq 0
but
\delta_{K/F}(B)=0
.
This examples combines involved arguments relying on the construction of Rowen of an indecomposable algebra of degree 8 and exponent 2, residue maps introduced in
[
BCL20
, Proposition 6.3]
and the nonexcellence of biquadratic separable extensions
[
LM25b
, Example 6.1]
, which is also based on Rowen’s example cited before.

In
Section

5
we will combine the results about the invariant with deep results from E. Peyre and N. Karpenko to construct an indecomposable algebra of index
8
such that there is a nontrivial element in the torsion part of the second Chow group of its Severi-Brauer variety.
Moreover, we can describe this element explicitly.

Section

6
will be devoted to applications of the invariant.
More precisely for an odd degree extension
M/F
, mixed biquadratic extension
M^{\prime}/F
and a central simple
F
-algebra
D
such that
D_{MM^{\prime}}
is Brauer equivalent to
Q_{MM^{\prime}}
for an
M
-quaternion
Q
, we show that
D_{M^{\prime}}
comes from an
F
-quaternion algebra, see
Theorem

6.1
.

We apply this descent to get a similar result for quadratic forms.
For an
F
-quadratic form
\varphi
such that
\varphi_{MM^{\prime}}
is Witt equivalent to
\psi_{MM^{\prime}}
for an
M
-quadratic form
\psi
of dimension at most 3 or similar to a two-fold Pfister form, we show that
\varphi_{M^{\prime}}
is Witt equivalent to some
F
-quadratic form
\psi^{\prime}
of the same shape as
\psi
over
M^{\prime}
, see
Theorem

6.6
.

2.
Preliminaries and Notation

2.1.
Quadratic forms

For quadratic forms, our standard reference will be
[
EKM08
]
.
To keep our paper self contained, we recall some definitions and notations.
Over a field of characteristic 2, any quadratic form
\varphi
decomposes as

(2.1)

\displaystyle\varphi\cong[a_{1},b_{1}]\perp\ldots\perp[a_{r},b_{r}]\perp\langle c_{1},\ldots,c_{s}\rangle,

where
[a,b]
denotes the two-dimensional quadratic form given by
aX^{2}+XY+bY^{2}
and
\langle c_{1},\ldots,c_{s}\rangle
denotes the
s
-dimensional quadratic form given by
c_{1}Z_{1}^{2}+\ldots+c_{s}Z_{s}^{2}
.
The tuple
(r,s)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
is unique and called the
type
of
\varphi
.
The form
\langle c_{1},\ldots,c_{s}\rangle
is unique up to isometry and called the
quasilinear part
of
\varphi
.
We denote it by
\mathrm{ql}\left(\varphi\right)
.
The form
\varphi
is called
nonsingular
if
s=0
and
totally singular
if
r=0
.
In this case, the
Arf-invariant
of
\varphi
as in (
2.1
) is defined to be
\Delta(\varphi):=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}b_{i}\in F/\wp(F)
, where
\wp(F)=\{x^{2}+x\mid x\in F\}
.

A quadratic form
\varphi
is called
isotropic
if there is a nonzero vector
v
with
\varphi(v)=0
; otherwise, it is called
anisotropic
.
The quadratic form
\mathbb{H}=[0,0]
is called the
hyperbolic plane
.
Up to isometry it is the unique nonsingular isotropic form of dimension 2.
A form isometric to a sum of hyperbolic planes is called
hyperbolic
.

The Witt decomposition asserts that any form
\varphi
uniquely decomposes as

\varphi\cong\varphi_{\mathrm{an}}\perp\mathcal{H}\perp\langle 0,\ldots,0\rangle,

where
\varphi_{\mathrm{an}}
is anisotropic and
\mathcal{H}
is hyperbolic.
The integer
\frac{1}{2}\dim\mathcal{H}
is called the
Witt index
of
\varphi
and denoted by
\mathfrak{i}_{\mathrm{W}}(\varphi)
.
We call
\varphi_{\mathrm{an}}
the
anisotropic part
of
\varphi
.

Two forms
\varphi,\psi
are
Witt equivalent
, if there exist
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\varphi\perp n\times\mathbb{H}\cong\psi\perp m\times\mathbb{H}.

In this case, we write
\varphi\sim\psi
.

We denote the
(quadratic) Witt group
of
F
by
\mathsf{W}_{q}(F)
, and the
(bilinear) Witt ring
of
F
by
\mathsf{W}(F)
.
It is well known that we have an action of
\mathsf{W}(F)
on
\mathsf{W}_{q}(F)
induced by the tensor product, see
[
EKM08
, Section 8.B]
.
For any
n\geq 1
, let
\mathsf{I}^{n}(F)
denote the
n
-th power of the fundamental ideal
\mathsf{I}(F)
of
\mathsf{W}(F)
(where
\mathsf{I}^{0}(F)=\mathsf{W}(F)
).
Similarly, denote by
\mathsf{I}^{n+1}_{q}(F)
the submodule of
\mathsf{W}_{q}(F)
given by
\mathsf{I}^{n}(F)\otimes\mathsf{W}_{q}(F)
(note that
\mathsf{I}^{1}_{q}(F)=\mathsf{W}_{q}(F)
).
It is well known that
\mathsf{I}^{n}(F)
is additively generated by the bilinear forms

\langle 1,a_{1}\rangle_{b}\otimes\ldots\otimes\langle 1,a_{n}\rangle_{b}=:\langle\!\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\rangle\!\rangle_{b}

for some
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in F^{\ast}
that we call an
n
-fold bilinear Pfister form
.
Similarly,
\mathsf{I}^{n+1}_{q}(F)
is additively generated by scalar multiples of forms of the shape

\langle\!\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\rangle\!\rangle_{b}\otimes[1,a]=:\langle\!\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},a]\!]

which are called
(n+1)
-fold quadratic Pfister forms
.

Recall the Arason-Pfister Hauptsatz, which states that if
\varphi
is a nonzero anisotropic quadratic Pfister form lying in
\mathsf{I}^{n}(F)
, then
\dim(\varphi)\geq 2^{n}
.

Given a field extension
K/F
of finite degree and a nonzero
F
-linear map
s:K\to F
and a (quadratic or bilinear) form
\varphi
over
K
, we associate the form, denoted by
s_{\ast}(\varphi):=s\circ\varphi
, that we call the
transfer
of
\varphi
with respect to
s
.
This new form has dimension
[K:F]\cdot\dim(\varphi)
, where
\dim(\varphi)
denotes the dimension of
\varphi
.
A well known fact that we will regularly use is the
Frobenius reciprocity
, stating that for a bilinear form
\beta
over
F
resp. over
K
and a quadratic form
\varphi
over
K
resp. over
F
, we have

s_{\ast}(\beta_{K}\otimes\varphi)=\beta\otimes s_{\ast}(\varphi)\quad\text{ resp. }\quad s_{\ast}(\beta\otimes\varphi_{K})=s_{\ast}(\beta)\otimes\varphi.

2.2.
Central simple algebras

Our standard reference for central simple algebras is
[
GS06
]
.
Let
\mathrm{Br}(F)
denote the Brauer group of
F
.
For any central simple algebra
A
over
F
, let
\deg(A)=\sqrt{\dim_{F}(A)}
denote the
degree
of
A
, and let
\mathrm{ind}(A)
be the
index
of
A
, i.e. the degree of the division algebra
D
Brauer equivalent to
A
.
An algebra
A
is called
split
if it is Brauer equivalent to
F
.

An
F
-quaternion algebra
, denoted by
[a,b)
, is an
F
-algebra of dimension
4
with a basis
1,u,v,w
subject to the relations

u^{2}+u=a\in F,\quad v^{2}=b\in F^{\ast},\quad uv=w=vu+v.

For any quaternion algebra
Q=[a,b)
, we can associate its
norm form
given by
\langle\!\langle b,a]\!]
.
Recall the following relations in
\mathrm{Br}_{2}(F)
that will be used frequently:

[a,b)+[a,c)=[a,bc)\quad\text{ and }\quad[x,y)+[z,y)=[x+z,y),

where
a,x,z\in F
and
b,c,y\in F^{\ast}
.

Note that
Q
contains an inseparable and a separable quadratic field extensions of
F
, namely
F(v)
and
F(u)
respectively and
Q
splits over these two extensions.
Conversely, if a central simple algebra
A
of exponent 2 splits over a quadratic extension
F(x)
given by
x^{2}=d\in F
(resp.
x^{2}+x=d\in F
) then
A
is Brauer equivalent to
[c,d)
(resp.
Q\cong[d,c)
) for a suitable
c\in F
(resp.
c\in F^{\ast}
), i.e. we have

\mathrm{Br}_{2}(F(x)/F):=\ker(\mathrm{Br}_{2}(F)\to\mathrm{Br}_{2}(F(x)))=\begin{cases}\{[[c,d)]\mid c\in F\},&\text{ if $x^{2}=d$}\\
\{[[d,c)]\mid c\in F^{\ast}\},&\text{ if $x^{2}+x=d$}.\end{cases}

Every central simple algebra of degree 4 and exponent 2 is a biquaternion algebra, i.e. it is isomorphic to the tensor product of two quaternion algebras (see
[
Alb32
, p. 369]
or
[
KMRT98
, (16.1) Theorem]
for a modern treatment).
An
Albert form
is a form of dimension
6
with trivial Arf invariant.
It is thus of the shape
x_{1}[1,a]\perp x_{2}[1,b]\perp x_{3}[1,a+b]
for some
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},a,b\in F
.

Given a biquaternion algebra
A=[a_{1},b_{1})\otimes[a_{2},b_{2})
, we can attach an Albert form
\alpha=[1,a_{1}+a_{2}]\perp b_{1}[1,a_{1}]\perp b_{2}[1,a_{2}]
.
This form is unique up to similarity, see
[
MS89
]
.
The isotropy behaviour of the
\alpha
encodes the index of
A
in that
\mathrm{ind}(A)=4
if and only if
\mathfrak{i}_{\mathrm{W}}(\alpha)=0
,
\mathrm{ind}(A)=2
if and only if
\mathfrak{i}_{\mathrm{W}}(\alpha)=1
and
\mathrm{ind}(A)=1
if and only if
\mathfrak{i}_{\mathrm{W}}(\alpha)=3
, see
[
KMRT98
, (16.5) Theorem]
.

Let
C(\varphi)
denote the Clifford algebra of the quadratic form
\varphi
.
If
\varphi
is nonsingular, then
C(\varphi)
is a central simple algebra.
Explicitly, for
\varphi\cong b_{1}[1,a_{1}]\perp\ldots\perp b_{r}[1,a_{r}]
with
b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}\neq 0
, we have

C(\varphi)=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}[a_{i},b_{i})

by
[
Sch85
, p. 342]
.
The
Clifford invariant

c(\varphi)
of
\varphi
is defined as the class of
C(\varphi)
in
\mathrm{Br}_{2}(F)
, where
\mathrm{Br}_{2}(F)
denotes the 2-torsion part of the Brauer group of
F
.

2.3.
Differential forms

Let
\Omega^{1}_{F}
denote the
F
vector space of 1-dimensional differentials generated by symbols
\mathrm{d}a
, for
a\in F
, subject to the relations

\mathrm{d}(a+b)=\mathrm{d}(a)+\mathrm{d}(b)\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathrm{d}(ab)=a\mathrm{d}b+b\mathrm{d}a.

For an integer
n\geq 1
, let
\Omega_{F}^{n}=\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}\Omega_{F}^{1}
.
We take
\Omega_{F}^{0}=F
and for
n<0
, we take
\Omega_{F}^{n}=0
.
The space
\Omega_{F}^{n}
is generated as an
F
vector space by symbols of the shape
a\mathrm{d}b_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge b_{n}
.

Let
\mathrm{d}:\Omega_{F}^{n}\to\Omega_{F}^{n+1}
be the
differential operator
defined by

a\mathrm{d}b_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge b_{n}\mapsto\mathrm{d}a\wedge\mathrm{d}b_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge b_{n}.

The
Artin-Schreier operator

\wp:\Omega_{F}^{n}\to\Omega_{F}^{n}/\mathrm{d}\Omega_{F}^{n-1}
is defined on generators by

a\frac{\mathrm{d}b_{1}}{b_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge\frac{\mathrm{d}b_{n}}{b_{n}}\mapsto(a^{2}-a)\frac{\mathrm{d}b_{1}}{b_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge\frac{\mathrm{d}b_{n}}{b_{n}}.

Its cokernel is denoted by
H_{2}^{n+1}(F)
.
Explicitly we have

H_{2}^{n+1}(F)=\Omega_{F}^{n}/(\mathrm{d}\Omega_{F}^{n-1}+\wp(\Omega_{F}^{n})).

It is easy to see that
H_{2}^{1}(F)\cong F/\wp(F)
and a well known result by Sah
[
Sah72
, Theorem 2 (ii)]
asserts that
H_{2}^{2}(F)\cong\mathrm{Br}_{2}(F)\cong\mathsf{I}_{q}^{2}(F)/\mathsf{I}_{q}^{3}(F)
.
More generally, for each
n\geq 1
, we have a celebrated result by Kato
[
Kat82
]
stating that we have isomorphisms

f^{n}:\mathsf{I}_{q}^{n}(F)/\mathsf{I}_{q}^{n+1}(F)\to H_{2}^{n}(F)

mapping
\overline{\langle\!\langle b_{1},\ldots,b_{n-1},a]\!]}
to
\overline{a\frac{\mathrm{d}b_{1}}{b_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge\frac{\mathrm{d}b_{n}}{b_{n}}}
.
For any
\varphi\in\mathsf{I}^{n}(F)
, let
e^{n}(\varphi)=f^{n}(\overline{\varphi})
.
To slim down the notation, for a central simple algebra
A
over
F
with
[A]\in\mathrm{Br}_{2}(F)
, we also use the notation
[A]
for the corresponding element in
H_{2}^{2}(F)
.

One of the results we will repeatedly use concerns the kernel
H_{2}^{n+1}(K/F):=\ker(H_{2}^{n+1}(F)\to H_{2}^{n+1}(K))
of the restriction map, where
K/F
is a biquadratic extension.
More precisely, if
K_{1},K_{2}
are quadratic extensions of
F
with
K_{1}K_{2}=K
, this kernel is equal to

H_{2}^{n+1}(K_{1}/F)+H_{2}^{n+1}(K_{2}/F),

see
[
AJ12
, Theorem 20]
for the separable case and
[
AL13
, Proposition 2 and 3]
for all other cases.

For
n=1
, since
\mathrm{Br}_{2}(F)\cong H_{2}^{2}(F)
, we obtain

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Br}_{2}(K/F)=\mathrm{Br}_{2}(K_{1}/F)+\mathrm{Br}_{2}(K_{2}/F).

3.
Construction of the invariant

Throughout this section, let
K/F
be a quadratic field extension.
Then
K=F(\delta)
, where either
\delta^{2}+\delta=d\in F^{\ast}
or
\delta^{2}=d\in F^{\ast}
, i.e.
\delta=\wp^{-1}(d)
or
\delta=\sqrt{d}

K/F
is separable or inseparable respectively.
We fix the notation for

and
\delta

Let
s:K\to F

F

and

In the case of a separable extension,

We denote by

(and thus also on the level of Brauer classes in the 2-torsion part).
Let
\mathrm{N}_{K/F}
denote the norm map corresponding to the extension

\mathrm{N}_{K/F}(x)=\begin{cases}x\overline{x}&\text{ if $K/F$ is separable}\\

where
\overline{x}
denotes the conjugate of

in the separable case.

[

.

Odd Degree Descent for Quadratic Forms

A field extension
K/F
is called
excellent
if for any quadratic form over
F
, the anisotropic part
(\varphi_{K})_{\mathrm{an}}
is defined over
F
, i.e. there is a quadratic form
\psi
over
F
such that
(\varphi_{K})_{\mathrm{an}}\cong\psi_{K}
.

It is known that quadratic separable extensions and purely inseparable multiquadratic field extensions are excellent, see
[
EKM08
, Corollary 22.12]
resp.
[
Hof15
, Theorem 3.4]
.
On the other hand, biquadratic extensions are not excellent in general, see
[
LM25b
, Example 6.1]
and
[
BCL25
, Example 3.3]
for examples of biquadratic separable resp. biquadratic mixed field extensions that are not excellent.
For a biquadratic field extension
K/F
which is not purely inseparable and a given quadratic form
\varphi
over
F
, it is thus desirable to have criteria predicting whether
(\varphi_{K})_{\mathrm{an}}
is defined over
F
.

Using our invariant
\mathsf{inv}
, especially the descent result for central simple algebras proved in
Theorem

6.1
, we develop a criterion relative to an odd degree extension for the case of a mixed biquadratic field extension.
More precisely, we have the following:

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
L/F
be a finite field extension of odd degree and
\varphi
be a quadratic form.
Let
E/F
be a biquadratic extension of degree 4 such that
(\varphi_{LE})_{\mathrm{an}}
is defined over
L
.
Then
(\varphi_{E})_{\mathrm{an}}
is defined over
F
in the following situations:

(i)

E=F(\wp^{-1}(b),\sqrt{d})
,
\varphi
is nonsingular,
\dim(\varphi_{E})_{\mathrm{an}}=4
and
\Delta(\varphi_{E})=0
;

(ii)

E=F(\wp^{-1}(b),\sqrt{d})
and
\dim(\varphi_{E})_{\mathrm{an}}=3
;

(iii)

\dim(\varphi_{E})_{\mathrm{an}}\leq 2
.

Proof.

(i)
:
Since
\Delta(\varphi_{E})=0
, we already have
\Delta(\varphi_{F(\wp^{-1}(b))})=0
, so
\Delta(\varphi)\in\{0,b\}
.
Since the anisotropic part of
\varphi_{E}
is the same as the anisotropic part of
(\varphi\perp[1,b])_{E}
, we may assume
\Delta(\varphi)=0
, i.e.
\varphi\in\mathsf{I}^{2}_{q}(F)
.

Let
[D]
denote its Clifford invariant.
Since
(\varphi_{L(\wp^{-1}(b),\sqrt{d})})_{\mathrm{an}}
is defined over
L
and of dimension
4
by assumption, its Clifford invariant
[D_{L(\wp^{-1}(b),\sqrt{d})}]
is equal to
[Q_{L(\wp^{-1}(b),\sqrt{d})}]
, where
Q
is a quaternion algebra defined over
L
.
By
Theorem

6.1
, this property descents to
F
, i.e. there is a quaternion algebra
\widehat{Q}
over
F
such that
[D_{F(\wp^{-1}(b),\sqrt{d})}]=[\widehat{Q}_{F(\wp^{-1}(b),\sqrt{d})}]
.

We now deduce
Q_{L(\wp^{-1}(b),\sqrt{d})}\cong\widehat{Q}_{L(F(\wp^{-1}(b),\sqrt{d})}
.
Since the norm forms of quaternion algebras are unique up to isometry, we find
\lambda\in L(\wp^{-1}(b),\sqrt{d})
such that
(\varphi_{L(\wp^{-1}(b),\sqrt{d})})_{\mathrm{an}}\cong\lambda\widehat{\pi}
, where
\widehat{\pi}
is the norm form of
\widehat{Q}
.
Using the roundness of Pfister forms we can suppose
\lambda\in L
because
(\varphi_{LE})_{\mathrm{an}}
is defined over
L
.

Using
[
Ahm04
, Theorem 2.1 (ii)]
, we obtain

\varphi_{L}=\lambda\widehat{\pi}+\psi_{1}\otimes[1,b]+\langle\!\langle d\rangle\!\rangle_{b}\otimes\psi_{2}\in\mathsf{W}_{q}(L)

for a bilinear form
\psi\in\mathsf{W}(L)
and a quadratic form
\psi_{2}\in\mathsf{W}_{q}(L)
.

Since
L/F
is separable, there is some
\gamma\in L
such that
L=F(\gamma)
.
Let
s:L\to F
be the linear map with
s(1)=1
and
s(\gamma^{k})=0
for
1\leq k<[L:F]
.
Using Frobenius reciprocity, we have

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle=s_{\ast}(\lambda\widehat{\pi}+\psi_{1}\otimes[1,b]+\langle\!\langle d\rangle\!\rangle_{b}\otimes\psi_{2})

\displaystyle=s_{\ast}(\langle\lambda\rangle_{b})\widehat{\pi}+s_{\ast}(\psi_{1})\otimes[1,b]+\langle\!\langle d\rangle\!\rangle_{b}\otimes s_{\ast}(\psi_{2}).

It is easy to see that
s_{\ast}(\langle\lambda\rangle_{b})
is Witt equivalent to
\langle\mathrm{N}_{F(\lambda)/F}(\lambda)\rangle_{b}
.
The conclusion now follows since
(\varphi_{E})_{\mathrm{an}}\cong s_{\ast}(\langle\lambda\rangle_{b})\otimes\widehat{\pi}
.

(ii)
:
Since by
[
HL04
, Lemma 2.2]
, for any quadratic form
\varphi
(of arbitrary type) over
F
and any field extension
K/F
,
(\mathrm{ql}\left(\varphi_{K}\right))_{\mathrm{an}}
is defined over
F
, it is enough to consider the case in which
(\varphi_{E})_{\mathrm{an}}
is of type
(1,1)
.
More precisely we can write
\varphi=\varphi_{r}\perp\mathrm{ql}\left(\varphi\right)
with
\varphi_{r}
nonsingular such that
\mathrm{ql}\left(\varphi_{LE}\right)\cong\langle s,0,\ldots,0\rangle
, where
s\in F^{\ast}
.
Using
[
HL04
, Lemma 2.6]
and the assumption, we get
((\varphi_{r}\perp\langle s\rangle)_{LE})_{\mathrm{an}}\cong x[1,a]\perp\langle s\rangle
for some
x,a\in L^{\ast}
.

We choose the nonsingular completion with trivial Arf invariant.
Then we get

((\varphi_{r}\perp s[1,\Delta(\varphi_{r})])_{LE})_{\mathrm{an}}\cong s\langle\!\langle xs,a]\!].

In particular, we can apply
(i)
to
\widehat{\varphi}:=\varphi_{r}\perp s[1,\Delta(\varphi_{r})]
and deduce the existence of
\alpha,\beta\in F
such that

(\widehat{\varphi}_{E})_{\mathrm{an}}\cong s\langle\!\langle\alpha,\beta]\!].

Adding
\langle s\rangle
on both sides and using the isometries
(\mathrm{ql}\left(\varphi\right)_{E})_{\mathrm{an}}\cong\langle s\rangle_{E}
and
s[1,t]\perp\langle s\rangle\cong\mathbb{H}\perp\langle s\rangle
, we obtain the Witt equivalence

\displaystyle\varphi_{E}

\displaystyle\sim(\varphi_{r}\perp\mathrm{ql}\left(\varphi\right))_{E}

\displaystyle\sim(\varphi_{r}\perp s[1,\Delta(\varphi_{r})]\perp\langle s\rangle\perp\langle 0,\ldots,0\rangle)_{E}

\displaystyle\sim(\widehat{\varphi}_{E})_{\mathrm{an}}\perp\langle s\rangle_{E}\perp\langle 0,\ldots,0\rangle

\displaystyle\cong s\langle\!\langle\alpha,\beta]\!]\perp\langle s\rangle_{E}\perp\langle 0,\ldots,0\rangle\sim\langle s\rangle_{E}\perp s\alpha[1,\beta]_{E}\perp\langle 0,\ldots,0\rangle

which yields the assertion by taking the anisotropic part.

(iii)
:
Similarly as in
(ii)
, it is enough to consider the case in which
(\varphi_{E})_{\mathrm{an}}
is of type
(1,0)
.
Let
a=\Delta(\varphi)
.
By assumption, there is
x\in L^{\ast}
such that
(\varphi_{LE})_{\mathrm{an}}=x[1,a]
.
In
\mathsf{W}_{q}(L)
we thus have

\varphi_{L}=x[1,a]+\psi_{1}+\psi_{2},

where, depending on the separability degree of
E/F
, the forms
\psi_{i}
are divisible either by
\langle\!\langle a_{i}\rangle\!\rangle_{b}
or
\langle\!\langle a_{i}]\!]
for a suitable
a_{i}\in L^{\ast}
, see
[
Ahm04
, Theorems 2.1, 2.2]
.

Now, we can conclude as in
(i)
.
∎

Super-Heisenberg Scaling Using Nonlinear Quantum Scrambling
Dong Xie, Chunling Xu

Abstract
Super-Heisenberg scaling, which scales as N − β N^{-\beta} with β > 1 \beta>1 in terms of the number of particles N N or T − β T^{-\beta} in terms of the evolution time T T , is better than Heisenberg scaling in quantum metrology. It has been proven that super-Heisenberg scaling can be achieved when the Hamiltonian of the system involves many-body interactions or the time-dependent terms. We demonstrate that nonlinear quantum scrambling facilitates the achievement of super-Heisenberg scaling T − β T^{-\beta} when the generator of the parameter is time-independent. More importantly, in dissipative systems, we can still obtain super-Heisenberg scaling in the friction model. In the optical cavity system, an exponential improvement in measurement precision over time can be achieved by combining injected external squeezing and intracavity squeezing. Our work provides an optimal method for leveraging nonlinear resources to enhance the measurement precision of the driving field.

This result shows that the super-Heisenberg scaling

When

\displaystyle(\lambda)=

.-
When the system suffers from dissipation due to interaction with noise, the dynamics can be described by Langevin equations.

Dissipation in the cavity model
.-In the cavity model, the dynamics of the cavity model
a
is described by the Langevin equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}a=i[H,a]-\gamma a+\sqrt{2\gamma}a_{in}(t),

(32)

where the cavity suffers from a squeezed vacuum reservoir with the noise operator
a_{in}(t)

;
lab36

\displaystyle\langle a^{\dagger}_{in}(t)a_{in}(t^{\prime})\rangle=\sinh^{2}(r)\delta(t-t^{\prime}),

(33)

\displaystyle\langle a_{in}(t)a^{\dagger}_{in}(t^{\prime})\rangle=\cosh^{2}(r)\delta(t-t^{\prime}),

(34)

\displaystyle\langle a_{in}(t)a_{in}(t^{\prime})\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}\sinh(2r)\delta(t-t^{\prime}),

(35)

\displaystyle\langle a^{\dagger}_{in}(t)a^{\dagger}_{in}(t^{\prime})\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}\sinh(2r)\delta(t-t^{\prime}),

(36)

where

Switching to the position and momentum operators, we can obtain the corresponding Langevin equations,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}X=i[H,X]-\gamma X+A_{x}(t),

(37)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}P=i[H,P]-\gamma P+A_{p}(t),

(38)

where the noise operators are

Since both the momentum and position operators decay over time, it is impossible to directly obtain the super-Heisenberg scaling as in the friction model.
In order to obtain the super-Heisenberg scaling, a two-photon driving (i.e., squeezed case) can be employed to resist the dissipation rate. The Hamiltonian for the two-photon driving is given by
lab37

\displaystyle H_{d}=i\mu(a^{\dagger 2}-a^{2}),

(39)

where
\mu
denotes the driving strength.

When
\mu^{\prime}=\mu-\gamma=0
, the expectation value of the measurement operator
P
is given by

\displaystyle\langle P\rangle

\displaystyle\approx\frac{-MG\lambda^{M-1}T^{M-1}}{2\gamma}.

(40)

When the squeezing parameter
r
satisfies

\displaystyle e^{2r}=\frac{M(M-1)G\lambda^{M-2}T^{M-3/2}}{\sqrt{\gamma}},

(41)

the variance of the momentum operator reaches its minimum,

\displaystyle\langle\delta^{2}P\rangle\geq\frac{M(M-1)G\lambda^{M-2}T^{M-3/2}}{2\sqrt{\gamma}}.

(42)

At this point, the minimum measurement uncertainty of the parameter
\lambda
is

\displaystyle\delta\lambda=\frac{\sqrt{2}\gamma^{3/4}}{\sqrt{M(M-1)G\lambda^{M-2}}T^{(M-2)/4}}.

(43)

From the above equation, it can be seen that the super-Heisenberg scaling can be obtained when
M>2
. This indicates that the combination of injected external squeezing and intracavity squeezing can counteract the dissipation process in the cavity model.

When
\mu>\gamma
and
T\gg 1
, we can obtain the expectation value of the measurement operator
P
as follows:

\displaystyle\langle P\rangle

\displaystyle\approx\frac{-MG(\lambda/\mu_{-}+\langle X_{0}\rangle)^{M-1}\exp[(M-1)(\mu_{-}T)]}{\mu_{+}}.

(44)

When the squeezing parameter is given by

\displaystyle e^{2r}=\frac{[M(M-1)G(\langle X_{0}\rangle+\lambda/\mu_{-})^{M-2}]e^{(M-1)\mu_{-}T}}{\sqrt{\mu_{-}{\mu_{+}}}},

(45)

the optimal measurement uncertainty of
\lambda
is

\displaystyle\delta\lambda\geq\frac{\gamma^{1/2}\mu_{+}^{1/4}\mu_{-}^{3/4}}{\sqrt{M(M-1)G}(\lambda/\mu_{-}+\langle X_{0}\rangle)^{(M-2)/2}e^{(M-1)\mu_{-}T/2}}.

(46)

Based on the above equation, we can see that when
M=2
, the measurement precision is independent of the initial position state. When
M>2
, increasing the initial expected value of
X
can improve the measurement precision. This is mainly achieved through higher-order nonlinearity, which amplifies the information of parameter
\lambda
in the expectation value of
P
.
What’s more, the above equation demonstrates that when
M
is greater than or equal to 2, the measurement uncertainty of the parameters decreases exponentially over time. At this point, the amplification factor of the signal is much greater than that of the noise. This is achieved through the application of nonlinear quantum scrambling with the assistance of injected external squeezing and intracavity squeezing.

Conclusion
.-We have conducted a thorough investigation into the role of nonlinear quantum scrambling in enhancing parameter precision, and determined the theoretical limit of the achievable measurement precision. In a closed system, the super-Heisenberg scaling can be achieved under general conditions (i.e., when the nonlinear coupling coefficient
G
is independent of the linear coupling coefficient
\lambda
to be measured). An interesting point is that when the nonlinear coupling coefficient is proportional to the linear coupling coefficient being measured, it actually causes the super-Heisenberg scaling to vanish. The optimal measurement operator is obtained, and it is proven that the optimal measurement precision can be achieved. An appropriate squeezing process can be employed to resist the effects of detuning between the frequency of the free Hamiltonian and the driving frequency. In the dissipative friction model, super-Heisenberg scaling can still be obtained by using a dissipation-free position operator to encode information.
In the dissipative cavity model, the measurement precision can be enhanced exponentially over time through the combined use of injected external squeezing and intracavity squeezing.
In both dissipative systems, measuring only the momentum is sufficient to obtain the super-Heisenberg scaling. Our results have opened up a pathway for the effective utilization of nonlinear quantum scrambling to enhance the measurement precision.

(S20)

where
G^{\prime}=\partial_{\lambda}G
.
From the above equation, one can clearly see that the coefficient of the higher-order terms of time (
G-G^{\prime}\lambda
) is exactly cancelled out when
G
is proportional to
\lambda
:
G=\xi\lambda
. When
G^{\prime}>0
, the coefficient
G-G^{\prime}\lambda<0
. At this point, if the slope of
G
with respect to
\lambda
is greater than 0, the quantum Fisher information of the system can be reduced. Conversely, the quantum Fisher information about
\lambda
can
be improved to a certain extent, but it does not affect the exponential relationship with time.

S3. Calculation of Quantum Fisher Information:
G
and
\lambda
are functions of
y

When both
G
and
\lambda
are functions of
y
, the quantum Fisher information with respect to
y
can be derived by the following process

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}=4(\langle\partial_{\lambda}\psi(T)|\partial_{\lambda}\psi(T)\rangle-|\langle\psi|\partial_{\lambda}\psi(T)\rangle|^{2})

(S21)

\displaystyle=4\langle\{\lambda^{\prime}XT+\sum_{n=2}^{M}\lambda^{\prime}G(-\lambda)^{n-1}\frac{M!}{n!(M-n)!}P^{M-n}T^{n+1}

\displaystyle+MG^{\prime}P^{M-1}T+\sum_{n=2}^{M+1}[G^{\prime}(-\lambda)^{n-1}-(n-1)\lambda^{\prime}G(-\lambda)^{n-2}]

\displaystyle\times\frac{M!}{n!(M-n+1)!}P^{M-n+1}T^{n}+\lambda^{\prime}MGP^{M-1}T^{2}\}^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle-4|\langle\lambda^{\prime}XT+\sum_{n=2}^{M}\lambda^{\prime}G(-\lambda)^{n-1}\frac{M!}{n!(M-n)!}P^{M-n}T^{n+1}

\displaystyle+MG^{\prime}P^{M-1}T+\sum_{n=2}^{M+1}[G^{\prime}(-\lambda)^{n-1}-(n-1)\lambda^{\prime}G(-\lambda)^{n-2}]

\displaystyle\times\frac{M!}{n!(M-n+1)!}P^{M-n+1}T^{n}+\lambda^{\prime}MGP^{M-1}T^{2}\rangle|^{2}

(S22)

\displaystyle=4\langle[\lambda^{\prime}XT+MP^{M-1}T^{2}(G\lambda^{\prime}-G^{\prime}\lambda)/2+MG^{\prime}P^{M-1}T

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=3}^{M+1}(G\lambda^{\prime}-G^{\prime}\lambda)(-\lambda)^{n-2}\frac{M!}{n!(M-n+1)!}P^{M-n+1}T^{n}]^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle-|\langle[XT+MP^{M-1}T^{2}(G\lambda^{\prime}-G^{\prime}\lambda)/2+MG^{\prime}P^{M-1}T

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=3}^{M+1}(G\lambda^{\prime}-G^{\prime}\lambda)(-\lambda)^{n-2}\frac{M!}{n!(M-n+1)!}P^{M-n+1}T^{n}]\rangle|^{2},

(S23)

where
G^{\prime}=\partial_{y}G
and
\lambda^{\prime}=\partial_{y}\lambda
.
When the condition
G\lambda^{\prime}-G^{\prime}\lambda\neq 0
and for long time
T\gg 1
, the quantum Fisher information can be reduced to the following form

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}\approx 4[(G\lambda^{\prime}-G^{\prime}\lambda)(-\lambda)^{M-2}T^{M}]^{2}\langle\Delta^{2}P\rangle.

(S24)

S4. The optimal measurement operator

The expectation value of the position operator
X
is

\displaystyle\langle X\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\psi_{0}|\exp[i(\lambda X+gP^{M})T]X\exp[-i(\lambda X+gP^{M})T]|\psi_{0}\rangle

(S25)

\displaystyle=\langle X+MGP^{M-1}T+\sum_{n=2}^{M}\frac{M!(-\lambda)^{n-1}}{n!(M-n)!}GP^{M-n}T^{n}\rangle

(S26)

\displaystyle=\langle X-G/\lambda(P-\lambda T)^{M}+G/\lambda P^{M}\rangle.

(S27)

The expectation value of the measurement operator
M_{e}
is

\displaystyle\langle M_{e}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\psi|X+G/\lambda_{c}P^{M}-G/\lambda_{c}(P+\lambda_{c}t)^{M}|\psi\rangle

(S28)

\displaystyle=\langle\psi_{0}|X-G/\lambda(P-\lambda t)^{M}+G/\lambda_{c}(P-\lambda t)^{M}

\displaystyle+G/\lambda P^{M}-G/\lambda_{c}[P+(\lambda_{c}-\lambda)t]^{M}|\psi_{0}\rangle.\

(S29)

According to the error propagation formula, the measurement uncertainty of
\lambda
is

\displaystyle\delta\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\langle M_{e}^{2}\rangle-\langle M_{e}\rangle^{2}}}{|\partial_{\lambda}\langle M_{e}\rangle|}

(S30)

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle\delta X\rangle}{G\lambda^{M-2}t^{M}}

(S31)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2G\lambda^{M-2}t^{M}\langle\delta P\rangle}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathcal{F}(\lambda)}}.

(S32)

S5. The influence of detuning

The detuning Hamiltonian is described by the following form

\displaystyle\Delta H=\Omega a^{\dagger}a,

(S33)

where the detuning
\Omega=\omega-\omega_{d}
.
The evolution equations of the position operator and the momentum operator are:

\displaystyle\dot{X}

\displaystyle=MGP^{M-1}+\Omega P,

(S34)

\displaystyle\dot{P}

\displaystyle=-\lambda-\Omega X.

(S35)

When
M=2
, the analytical solutions of the above equations are given by

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=\cos[\sqrt{\Omega(2G+\omega)}T]X_{0}+\lambda\frac{\cos[\sqrt{\Omega(2G+\Omega)}T]-1}{\Omega}

\displaystyle+\frac{\sin[\sqrt{\Omega(2G+\omega)}T]\sqrt{2G+\Omega}}{\sqrt{\Omega}}P_{0},

(S36)

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=\frac{-\sqrt{\Omega}\sin[\sqrt{\Omega(2G+\Omega)}T]}{\sqrt{2G+\Omega}}X_{0}+\cos[\sqrt{\Omega(2G+\Omega)}T]P_{0}

\displaystyle-\lambda\frac{\sin[\sqrt{\Omega(2G+\Omega)}T]}{\sqrt{(2G+\Omega)\Omega}}.

(S37)

When
\sqrt{2\Omega(2G+\Omega)}T\ll 1
, the optimal measurement operator is
M_{e}=X+\frac{G}{\lambda_{c}}P^{2}-\frac{G}{\lambda_{c}}(P+\lambda_{c}T)^{2}
.
The corresponding uncertain relationship is

\displaystyle\delta\lambda=\frac{\langle\delta X\rangle}{GT^{2}}.

(S38)

S6. Dissipation in the friction model

In the friction model, the quantum-Langevin eqautions are given by

\displaystyle\dot{X}

\displaystyle=MGP^{M-1}+\eta_{x},

(S39)

\displaystyle\dot{P}

\displaystyle=-\lambda-\gamma P+\eta_{p}.

(S40)

As a result, the analytical forms of the momentum operator and the position operator are

\displaystyle{P}

\displaystyle=\exp(-\gamma T)P_{0}+\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}\exp[-\gamma(T-t^{\prime})][-\lambda+\eta_{p}(t^{\prime})],

(S41)

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=X_{0}+\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}(MGP^{M-1}+\eta_{x}).

(S42)

When
\gamma T\gg 1
, the above solutions can be further simplified as

\displaystyle{P}

\displaystyle=-\lambda/\gamma+\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}\exp[-\gamma(T-t^{\prime})]\eta_{p}(t^{\prime}),

(S43)

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=X_{0}+MG[-\lambda/\gamma+\int_{0}^{\infty}dt^{\prime}\exp(-\gamma t^{\prime})\eta_{y}(t^{\prime})]^{M-1}T.

(S44)

By exchanging the positions of
X
and
P
, the Hamiltonian is described by

\displaystyle H=\lambda P+GX^{M}.

(S45)

Under the control of this Hamiltonian, the corresponding quantum-Langevin equations are

\displaystyle\dot{X}

\displaystyle=\lambda+\eta_{x},

(S46)

\displaystyle\dot{P}

\displaystyle=-MGX^{M-1}+\eta_{p}-\gamma P.

(S47)

By the same derivation, when
\gamma T\gg 1
, the solutions at time
T
are given by

\displaystyle{X}=X_{0}+\lambda T+\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}\eta_{x}(t^{\prime})

(S48)

\displaystyle{P}=\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}\exp(-\gamma t^{\prime})\eta_{p}(t^{\prime})-

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}\exp[-\gamma(t-t^{\prime})]MG[X_{0}+\lambda t^{\prime}+\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}dt^{\prime\prime}\eta_{x}(t^{\prime\prime})]^{M-1}.

(S49)

The expectation values are given by

\displaystyle{\langle P\rangle}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}\exp[-\gamma(T-t^{\prime})]MG(X_{0}+\lambda t^{\prime})^{M-1}

(S50)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{MG\lambda^{M-1}T^{M-1}}{\gamma}.

(S51)

\displaystyle{\langle\delta^{2}P\rangle}=\langle[\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}\exp[-\gamma(T-t^{\prime})]MG(X_{0}+\lambda t^{\prime})^{M-1}]^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle-|\langle\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}\exp[-\gamma(T-t^{\prime})]MG(X_{0}+\lambda t^{\prime})^{M-1}\rangle|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}

(S52)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{2}.

(S53)

S7. Dissipation in the cavity model

In the dissipative cavity model, the quantum-Langevin equations are

\displaystyle\dot{X}

\displaystyle=-\gamma X+\lambda+A_{x},

(S54)

\displaystyle\dot{P}

\displaystyle=-MGX^{M-1}+A_{p}-\gamma P.

(S55)

The solutions is given by

\displaystyle{X}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{\gamma}(1-\exp(-\gamma T))+\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}\exp[-\gamma(T-t^{\prime})]A_{x}(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle+\exp(-\gamma T)X_{0},

(S56)

\displaystyle{P}

\displaystyle=\exp(-\gamma T)P_{0}+\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}\exp(-\gamma t^{\prime})A_{p}(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}\exp[-\gamma(T-t^{\prime})]MGX(t^{\prime})^{M-1}.

(S57)

The expectation value of the momentum operator is given by

\displaystyle\langle P\rangle=\frac{GM\lambda^{M-1}}{\gamma^{M}}.

(S58)

Including the two-photon driving, the quantum-Langevin equations are rewritten as

\displaystyle\dot{X}

\displaystyle=(\mu-\gamma)X+\lambda+A_{x},

(S59)

\displaystyle\dot{P}

\displaystyle=-MGX^{M-1}+A_{p}+(-\mu-\gamma)P,

(S60)

where
\mu
denotes the two-photon driving strength.
By solving the above equations, the general forms of the position and momentum operators at time
T
are given by

\displaystyle{X}=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{\mu_{-}}[\exp(\mu_{-}T)-1]+\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}\exp[\mu_{-}(T-t^{\prime})]A_{x}(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle+\exp(\mu_{-}T)X_{0},

(S61)

\displaystyle{P}=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}\exp[-\mu_{+}(T-t^{\prime})]MGX(t^{\prime})^{M-1}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}\exp(-\mu_{+}t^{\prime})A_{p}(t^{\prime})+\exp(-\mu_{+}T)P_{0},

(S62)

where
\mu_{\pm}=\mu\pm\gamma
.

When
\mu=\gamma
and
T\gg 1
, the expectation values are given by

\displaystyle\langle X\rangle

\displaystyle\approx\lambda T+\langle X_{0}\rangle,

(S63)

\displaystyle\langle P\rangle

\displaystyle\approx\frac{-MG\lambda^{M-1}T^{M-1}}{2\gamma}.

(S64)

By using the correlations for the noise operator
a_{in}(t)
, we can obtain the correlations for
A_{x}(t)
and
A_{p}(t)

\displaystyle\langle A_{x}(t)A_{x}(t^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=\gamma e^{-2r}\delta(t-t^{\prime}),

(S65)

\displaystyle\langle A_{p}(t)A_{p}(t^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=\gamma e^{2r}\delta(t-t^{\prime}).

(S66)

We further obtain that the variance of the momentum operator
P
is

\displaystyle\langle\delta^{2}P\rangle

\displaystyle\approx\frac{(M-1)^{2}M^{2}G^{2}\lambda^{2(M-2)}T^{2(M-2)}}{(2\gamma)^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\langle\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}A_{x}(t^{\prime})\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime\prime}A_{x}(t^{\prime\prime})\rangle+\gamma e^{2r}/4

(S67)

\displaystyle=\frac{(M-1)^{2}M^{2}G^{2}\lambda^{2(M-2)}T^{(2M-3)}e^{-2r}}{4\gamma}+e^{2r}/4

(S68)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{M(M-1)G\lambda^{M-2}T^{M-3/2}}{2\sqrt{\gamma}},

(S69)

where the condition for setting the equality in the last equation is the squeezing parameter

\displaystyle e^{2r}=\frac{M(M-1)G\lambda^{M-2}T^{M-3/2}}{\sqrt{\gamma}}.

(S70)

Based on the error propagation formula, the measurement uncertainty of
\lambda
is derived

\displaystyle\delta\lambda=\frac{\sqrt{\langle\delta^{2}P\rangle}}{|\partial_{\lambda}\langle P\rangle|}\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}\gamma^{3/4}}{\sqrt{M(M-1)G\lambda^{M-2}}T^{(M-2)/4}}.

(S71)

When
\mu>\gamma
and
T\gg 1
, we can obtain the expectation values

\displaystyle\langle X\rangle

\displaystyle\approx(\lambda/\mu_{-}+\langle X_{0}\rangle)\exp(\mu_{-}T),

(S72)

\displaystyle\langle P\rangle

\displaystyle\approx\frac{-MG(\lambda/\mu_{-}\exp(\mu_{-}T)+\exp(\mu_{-}T)\langle X_{0}\rangle)^{M-1}}{\mu_{+}}.

(S73)

Utilizing the the correlations of the noise, we further achieve the variance of the momentum operator

\displaystyle\langle\delta^{2}P\rangle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{[M(M-1)G\langle X\rangle^{M-2}]^{2}\langle[\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}e^{\mu_{-}(T-t^{\prime})}A_{x}(t^{\prime})]^{2}\rangle}{{\mu_{+}}^{2}}+\frac{\gamma e^{2r}}{2\mu_{+}}

(S74)

\displaystyle=\frac{[M(M-1)G\langle X\rangle^{M-2}]^{2}\gamma e^{2\mu_{-}T-2r}}{2\mu_{-}{\mu_{+}}^{2}}+\frac{\gamma e^{2r}}{2\mu_{+}}

(S75)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{[M(M-1)G(\langle X_{0}\rangle+\lambda/\mu_{-})^{M-2}]{\gamma}e^{(M-1)\mu_{-}T}}{\mu_{+}\sqrt{\mu_{-}{\mu_{+}}}}.

(S76)

where the condition for the equality to hold is that the squeezing parameter satisfies

\displaystyle e^{2r}=\frac{[M(M-1)G(\langle X_{0}\rangle+\lambda/\mu_{-})^{M-2}]e^{(M-1)\mu_{-}T}}{\sqrt{\mu_{-}{\mu_{+}}}}.

(S77)

Based on the error propagation formula, the measurement uncertainty of the parameter
\lambda
is derived by the following equation

\displaystyle\delta\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\langle\delta^{2}P\rangle}}{|\partial_{\lambda}\langle P\rangle|}

(S78)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\gamma^{1/2}\mu_{+}^{1/4}\mu_{-}^{3/4}}{\sqrt{M(M-1)G}(\lambda/\mu_{-}+\langle X_{0}\rangle)^{(M-2)/2}e^{(M-1)\mu_{-}T/2}}.

(S79)

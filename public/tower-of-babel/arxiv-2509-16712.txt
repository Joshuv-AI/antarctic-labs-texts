Compactness and least energy solutions to the super-Liouville equation on the sphere The second author was partially supported by NSFC Grant 12031012 and STCSM Grant 24ZR1440700.
Mingyang Han

Abstract
In this work, we study the super-Liouville equation on the sphere with positive coefficient functions. We begin by deriving estimates for the spinor component of the equation, thereby finding that the energy of the spinor part of solutions is uniformly bounded. We then analyze the compactness of the solution space in two aspects: the compactness for solutions with small energy and the compactness with respect to the conformal transformation group of the sphere. Finally, by introducing a new natural constraint, the Nehari manifold, and employing variational methods, we obtain the existence of the least energy solutions when the coefficient functions are even.



Keywords:
Super-Liouville equation, sphere, compactness results, existence results

In this paper, we focus on the existence of solutions to the following equation on the standard sphere
(\mathbb{S}^{2},g_{0})

\left\{{\begin{array}[]{*{20}{l}}{-\Delta u={h_{1}}(x){e^{2u}}-1+{h_{2}(x)}{e^{u}}{{\left|\psi\right|}^{2}},}&{{\text{on}}\;{\mathbb{S}^{2}},}\\
{D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\psi=h_{2}{e^{u}}\psi,}&{{\text{on}}\;{\mathbb{S}^{2}}.}\end{array}}\right.

(4)

Here

is the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
and

are nonnegative functions. As pointed out in Proposition
4.1

The second difficulty arises from the nature of the Dirac operator. Unlike the Laplace operator, the Dirac operator is strongly indefinite, and its expression in local coordinates is not as straightforward as that of the Laplace operator. Many of its analytical properties are inherent in the algebraic structure used in the definition of the Dirac operator. However, the strongly indefinite nature of the Dirac operator may also provide some additional information. For instance, by exploiting the strongly indefinite nature, we derive the first result, which concerns a priori estimate for the spinor component of the solution. Specifically, we show that the Sobolev norm of spinors, see Section
2.3
, of all solutions is bounded. Here, we present a result on a more general sphere

\left\{{\begin{array}[]{*{20}{l}}{-{\Delta_{g}}u=h_{1}(x){e^{2u}}-{K_{g}}+h_{2}(x){e^{u}}{{\left|\psi\right|}^{2}},}&{{\text{on}}\;{\mathbb{S}^{2}},}\\
{{D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}_{g}}\psi=h_{2}{e^{u}}\psi,}&{{\text{on}}\;{\mathbb{S}^{2}}.}\end{array}}\right.

.

Suppose
h_{i}(x)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
are positive smooth functions on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
with
h_{i}(-x)=h_{i}(x),\ \forall x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
for

. Then there is a ground state solution
(u,\psi)\in H_{even}^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})\times H^{1/2}(\mathcal{S}\mathbb{S}^{2})

. Let
(\Sigma,g)
be a Riemann surface of genus
\gamma
equipped with a fixed spin structure. The spinor bundle
\mathcal{S}\Sigma
on
\Sigma
is equipped with a natural Hermitian product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{h}
induced by the metric
g
. A spinor
\psi\in\Gamma(\mathcal{S}\Sigma)
is a section of the spinor bundle
\mathcal{S}\Sigma
.

The Clifford algebra
\text{Cl}(T_{x}\Sigma,g_{x})
is generated by the tangent space
T_{x}\Sigma
and the metric
g_{x}
at any point
x\in\Sigma
, satisfying the Clifford relation:

X_{i}\cdot X_{j}+X_{j}\cdot X_{i}=-2g_{x}(X_{i},X_{j}).

We denote the bundle of Clifford algebras over
\Sigma
by

\text{Cl}(\Sigma,g)=\coprod_{x\in\Sigma}\text{Cl}(T_{x}\Sigma,g_{x}).

There exists a representation

\rho:T\Sigma\to\text{End}(\mathcal{S}\Sigma),

which can be extended to
\text{Cl}(\Sigma,g)
:

\rho:\text{Cl}(\Sigma,g)\otimes\mathcal{S}\Sigma\to\mathcal{S}\Sigma,\hskip 10.00002pt\sigma\otimes\psi\mapsto\rho(\sigma)\cdot\psi.

We will use the notation
X\cdot\psi
to represent
\rho(X)\cdot\psi
. The representation
\rho
is compatible with the connection
\nabla
and with the Hermitian metrics

{\left\langle{\cdot,\cdot}\right\rangle_{h}}
. Specifically, if
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
is a local orthonormal frame on
T\Sigma
satisfying

e_{i}\cdot e_{j}\cdot\psi+e_{j}\cdot e_{i}\cdot\psi=-2\delta_{ij}\psi,

then the following properties hold:

\nabla_{e_{i}}(e_{j}\cdot\psi)=(\nabla_{e_{i}}e_{j})\cdot\psi+e_{j}\cdot(\nabla_{e_{i}}\psi),

\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle_{h}=\langle e_{i}\cdot\varphi,e_{i}\cdot\psi\rangle_{h},

and

{\left\langle{X\cdot{\psi_{1}},X\cdot{\psi_{2}}}\right\rangle_{h}}={g}\left({X,X}\right){\left\langle{{\psi_{1}},{\psi_{2}}}\right\rangle_{h}},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall X\in\Gamma\left({T\Sigma}\right),\forall{\psi_{1}},\ {\psi_{2}}\in\Gamma\left({S\Sigma}\right).

The Dirac operator
D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}
is defined by

D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\psi:=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{2}e_{\alpha}\cdot\nabla_{e_{\alpha}}\psi.

For further details on spinors, we refer the reader to
[
10
,
37
,
41
]
.

2.2
Conformal symmetry and transformations

For clarity, we explicitly label various geometric objects with the metric
g
. The material that follows is primarily sourced from
[
7
, Section 2]
. First, we introduce the behavior of the Dirac operator under a pointwise conformal transformation, i.e., when
g_{v}=e^{2v}g
with
v\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
. Then there is an isometric map

\begin{split}b:(T\Sigma,g)&\to(T\Sigma,e^{2v}g)\\
X&\mapsto e^{-v}X\end{split}

between
(T\Sigma,g)
and
(T\Sigma,e^{2v}g)
. The map
b
induces an isomorphic isometric map on the spinor bundle

\beta\equiv\beta_{g,g_{v}}:(\mathcal{S}_{g}\Sigma,h)\to(\mathcal{S}_{g_{v}}\Sigma,h_{v}).

The map
\beta
is independent of the spinor connection. Under this map, there is a well-known two-dimensional conformal transformation formula as follows

D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}_{g_{v}}\beta\left(e^{-\frac{1}{2}v}\psi\right)=e^{-\frac{3}{2}v}\beta(D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}_{g}\psi).

Next, we introduce the transformation induced by a conformal diffeomorphism from surfaces. Let
\phi:\Sigma\to\Sigma
be a diffeomorphism that preserves the orientation and the spinor structure. Let
\phi^{*}g
denote the pull-back metric on
T\Sigma
. Then the tangent map
T\phi:(T\Sigma,\phi^{*}g)\to(T\Sigma,g)
is an isometric map, and it also preserves the Levi-Civita connection. There exists an induced map
\Phi
, which also covers the map
\phi
, in the sense that the following diagram is commutative:

\begin{array}[]{*{20}{c}}{({\mathcal{S}_{{{\phi}^{*}g}}}\Sigma,{\phi}^{*}h)}&{\xrightarrow{\Phi}}&{({\mathcal{S}_{g}}\Sigma,{h})}\\
\downarrow&&\downarrow\\
{(\Sigma,{{\phi}^{*}g})}&{\xrightarrow{{\phi}}}&{(\Sigma,g)}\end{array}

The map
\Phi
preserves the spinor connection and is an isometric map of vector bundles, and thus preserves the Dirac operator. Therefore for any
\psi\in\Gamma(\mathcal{S}_{g}\Sigma)
, denoting
\phi^{*}\psi=\Phi^{-1}\circ\psi\circ\phi\in\Gamma(\mathcal{S}_{\phi^{*}g}\Sigma)
as the pulled-back spinor, then

\Phi\left(D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}_{\phi^{*}g}(\phi^{*}\psi)(x)\right)=D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}_{g}\psi(\phi(x)),\hskip 10.00002ptx\in\Sigma.

(10)

and

{\left\langle{{\phi^{*}}\psi,{\phi^{*}}\psi}\right\rangle_{\phi^{*}h}}={\left\langle{\psi\circ\phi,\psi\circ\phi}\right\rangle_{h\circ\phi}}.

(11)

For the sake of notational convenience in the sequel, we omit the subscript in the Hermitian inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{h}
for spinors.

2.3
Sobolev spaces of spinors

We now introduce the fractional Sobolev spaces
H^{1/2}(\mathcal{S}\Sigma)
for spinors on general Riemann surfaces. For foundational material on Sobolev spaces and fractional Sobolev spaces, we refer to [4, 20].

We first describe the spectrums of Dirac operator
D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}
, donated by Spec(
D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}
).
In
L^{2}({\mathcal{S}\Sigma})
we can write
\operatorname{Spec}(D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss})
as
\operatorname{Spec}(D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss})={\left\{{{\lambda_{k}}}\right\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}_{*}}}}\cup\left\{0\right\}
, where
\mathbb{Z}_{*}
=
\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
.
\lambda_{k}(k\in{\mathbb{Z}_{*}})
are the non-zero eigenvalues. A spinor corresponding to the eigenvalue 0 is referred to as a harmonic spinor.
We denote that the dimension of vector space of harmonic spinors is
h^{0}
, and put the non-zero eigenvalues in an increasing order (in absolute value) and counted with multiplicities to get

-\infty\leftarrow\cdots\leq\lambda_{-l-1}\leq\lambda_{-l}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{-1}<0<\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{k}\leq\lambda_{k+1}\leq\cdots\rightarrow+\infty.

Let
\varphi_{k}
be the eigenspinors corresponding to
\lambda_{k},k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}
with
\left\|\varphi_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}=1
. These eigenspinors together form a complete orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathcal{S}\Sigma)
: any spinor
\psi\in\Gamma(\mathcal{S}\Sigma)
can be expressed in terms of this basis as

\psi=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}}a_{k}\varphi_{k}+\sum_{1\leq j\leq h^{0}}a_{0,j}\varphi_{0,j},

and the Dirac operator acts as

D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\psi=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}}\lambda_{k}a_{k}\varphi_{k}.

For any
s>0
, the operator
|D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}|^{s}:\Gamma(\mathcal{S}\Sigma)\rightarrow\Gamma(\mathcal{S}\Sigma)
is defined as

|D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}|^{s}\psi=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}}\left|\lambda_{k}\right|^{s}a_{k}\varphi_{k},

provided that the right-hand side belongs to
L^{2}(\mathcal{S}\Sigma)
. In particular,
|D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}|^{1}
defined in
L^{2}(\mathcal{S}\Sigma)
is abbreviated as
|D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}|
with

\operatorname{Spec}(|D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}|)=\left\{\left|\lambda_{k}\right|:k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}\right\}.

Similarly, the spectrum of
|D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}|^{1/2}
is

\operatorname{Spec}\left(|D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}|^{1/2}\right)=\left\{\left|\lambda_{k}\right|^{1/2}:k\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}\right\}.

Let

{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}(\mathcal{S}\Sigma)=Dom\left({{{\left|D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\right|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}}\right)=\left\{{\psi\in{L^{2}}(\mathcal{S}\Sigma)\left|{\int_{\Sigma}{\left\langle{{{\left|D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\right|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\psi,{{\left|D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\right|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\psi}\right\rangle}{\text{d}{v}}<\infty}\right.}\right\}

denote the domain of the operator
|D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}|^{1/2}
. It is clear that
|D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}|^{1/2}
is self-adjoint in
L^{2}(\mathcal{S}\Sigma)
. Define an inner product

{\langle\psi,\phi\rangle_{{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}}}={\langle\psi,\phi\rangle_{{L^{2}}}}+{\left\langle{|D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}{|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\psi,|D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}{|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\phi}\right\rangle_{{L^{2}}}}.\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\psi,\varphi\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathcal{S}\Sigma).

(12)

Let
\|\cdot\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathcal{S}\Sigma)}=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}(\mathcal{S}\Sigma)}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Then
(H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathcal{S}\Sigma),\|\cdot\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathcal{S}\Sigma)})
is a Hilbert space. For this space and
q\leq 4
, there exists a continuous embedding

H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathcal{S}\Sigma)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\mathcal{S}\Sigma).

(13)

Furthermore, for
q<4
, the embedding is compact, for more details, see, for instance, reference
[
39
,
40
]
.

Let
L^{2,+}(\mathcal{S}\Sigma)
and
L^{2,-}(\mathcal{S}\Sigma)
denote the subspaces spanned by
\left\{{{\varphi_{k}}:k>0}\right\}
and
\left\{{{\varphi_{k}}:k<0}\right\}
, respectively. If the spin structure of
\Sigma
is chosen such that
0\notin\operatorname{Spec}(D_{g}\hskip-13.2094pt\hbox to13.2094pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss})
, we can split spinors into the positive and negative parts according to the spectrum of
D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}=D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}_{g}
, i.e we have the decomposition

H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathcal{S}\Sigma)=H^{\frac{1}{2},{+}}(\mathcal{S}\Sigma)\oplus H^{\frac{1}{2},{-}}(\mathcal{S}\Sigma),

(14)

where

H^{\frac{1}{2},{+}}(\mathcal{S}\Sigma)=H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathcal{S}\Sigma)\cap L^{2,+}(\mathcal{S}\Sigma),\hskip 10.00002ptH^{\frac{1}{2},{-}}(\mathcal{S}\Sigma)=H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathcal{S}\Sigma)\cap L^{2,-}(\mathcal{S}\Sigma).

Furthermore, if we write
\psi=\psi^{+}+\psi^{-}
, which is decomposed according to (
14
), then we have

\int_{\Sigma}\left\langle D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\psi^{+},\psi^{+}\right\rangle{\text{d}{v}}=\int_{\Sigma}\left\langle|D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi^{+},|D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi^{+}\right\rangle{\text{d}{v}}\geq\lambda_{1}\left(D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}_{g}\right)\left\|\psi^{+}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2},

(15)

where
\lambda_{1}
is the first positive eigenvalue of
D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}=D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}_{g}
. Hence

\displaystyle\left\|\psi^{+}\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\psi^{+}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\||D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi^{+}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\left(\lambda_{1}\left(D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}_{g}\right)^{-1}+1\right)\left\||D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi^{+}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(16)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\lambda_{1}\left(D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}_{g}\right)^{-1}+1\right)\left\|\psi^{+}\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}.

That is, for a given
g
, the integral
\int_{\Sigma}\left\langle D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\psi^{+},\psi^{+}\right\rangle{\text{d}{v}}
defines a norm on
H^{\frac{1}{2},+}(\mathcal{S}\Sigma)
which is equivalent to the Hilbert’s. Similarly, on
H^{\frac{1}{2},-}(\mathcal{S}\Sigma)
there is an equivalent norm given by

-\int_{\Sigma}\left\langle D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\psi^{-},\psi^{-}\right\rangle{\text{d}{v}}=\left\||D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi^{-}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Consequently,

\int_{\Sigma}\left[\left\langle D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\psi^{+},\psi^{+}\right\rangle-\left\langle D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\psi^{-},\psi^{-}\right\rangle\right]{\text{d}{v}}=\left\||D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi^{+}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\||D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi^{-}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(17)

defines a norm equivalent to the
H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathcal{S}\Sigma)
-norm.

It is worthy to mention that the functional
E:{H^{1}}({\mathbb{S}^{2}})\times{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}(\mathcal{S}{\mathbb{S}^{2}})\to\mathbb{R}
,

E\left({u,\psi}\right)=\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{\left[{{{\left|{\nabla u}\right|}^{2}}+2u-h_{1}{e^{2u}}+2\left({\left\langle{D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}\psi,\psi}\right\rangle-h_{2}{e^{u}}{{\left|\psi\right|}^{2}}}\right)}\right]{\text{d}{v}_{g_{0}}}}+4\pi

is well defined and is of class
C^{1}
. By using the similar arguments in
[
42
]
, the critical points of
E(u,\psi)
, which are weak solutions of (
4
), are actually smooth.

2.4
Spinors on the sphere

Finally, in particular, we provide a brief introduction to spinors on the sphere. Consider the spin bundle
\mathcal{S}\mathbb{S}^{2}\to\mathbb{S}^{2}
associated with the unique spin structure on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, and let
D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}_{g_{0}}
denote the Dirac operator on
(\mathbb{S}^{2},g_{0})
. The spectrum of the Dirac operator
D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}_{g_{0}}
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
consists of the eigenvalues
\pm(k+1)
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, with the (real) multiplicity of the eigenvalues
\pm(k+1)
being
4(k+1)
. Regarding harmonic spinors on the sphere, the Lichnerowicz formula yields the following celebrated result.

Theorem 2.1

(Corollary 8.9,
[
52
]
)
.

Any compact spin manifold of positive scalar curvature admits no harmonic spinors. In fact, the same conclusion holds if the scalar curvature is
\geq
0 and
>
0 at some point.

In particular, there are no harmonic spinor fields on
\left(\mathbb{S}^{2},g_{0}\right)
hence

{h^{0}}=\dim{\text{ker}}(D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}_{g_{0}})=0.
And the first positive eigenvalue is
1
, with the corresponding eigen-spinors given by Killing spinors of constant length. According to Friedrich
[
26
]
, the existence of such spinors with constant length is related to the embedding of the surface into three-dimensional Euclidean space. For further details, we refer to
[
2
,
27
,
31
,
52
]
.

3
Bounds and estimates for compactness

In this section, we primarily perform estimates on the solutions and utilize them to establish the compactness results. We first employ the spectral properties of the Dirac operator to derive estimates for solutions of the equation (
5
), with particular attention to the positive and negative parts of the spinor separately, thereby obtaining a uniform bound for the spinor component of solutions.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Notice that the weak solution of (
5
) is smooth. Integrate both sides of the first equation

{-{\Delta_{g}}u=h_{1}(x){e^{2u}}-{K_{g}}+h_{2}(x){e^{u}}{{\left|\psi\right|}^{2}}}

), and apply Gauss-Bonnet formula to yield

\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{\left({{h_{1}}{e^{2u}}+h_{2}{e^{u}}{{\left|\psi\right|}^{2}}}\right)}{\text{d}{v}}=4\pi.

(18)

Therefore we have

0\leq\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}h_{2}{{e^{u}}}{\left|\psi\right|^{2}}{\text{d}}v\leq 4\pi.

(19)

Multiply both sides of the second equation

by the spinor
\psi

\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{\left\langle{D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}_{g}\psi,\psi}\right\rangle}{\text{d}{v}}=\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}h_{2}{{e^{u}}{{\left|\psi\right|}^{2}}}{\text{d}{v}}.

and

(21)

Similarly, there exist positive constants
B_{1}
and

such that

-{B_{1}}\left\|{{\psi^{-}}}\right\|_{{H^{1/2}}}^{2}\leq\int_{{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}}{\left\langle{D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}_{g}{\psi^{-}},{\psi^{-}}}\right\rangle}{\text{d}}v\leq{-B_{2}}\left\|{{\psi^{-}}}\right\|_{{H^{1/2}}}^{2}.

\displaystyle{A_{1}}\left\|{{\psi^{+}}}\right\|_{{H^{1/2}}}^{2}-{B_{1}}\left\|{{\psi^{-}}}\right\|_{{H^{1/2}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}}{\left\langle{D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}_{g}{\psi^{+}},{\psi^{+}}}\right\rangle}{\text{d}}v+\int_{{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}}{\left\langle{D\hskip-8.55695pt\hbox to8.55695pt{\hss\sl/\/\hss}_{g}{\psi^{-}},{\psi^{-}}}\right\rangle}{\text{d}}v

\displaystyle A_{1}{\left\|{{\psi^{+}}}\right\|^{2}_{{H^{1/2}}}}

The coupling term
{e^{u}}{\left|\psi\right|^{2}}
in the super-Liouville equation plays a crucial role in connecting the scalar and spinor components, and this is also reflected in the concrete calculations and estimates. For the convenience of subsequent discussions, we list the indices related to the coupling term here.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
\left(u,\psi\right)
is a solution to (

) and
{\left\|{{e^{u}}}\right\|_{{L^{\alpha}}\left({{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}\right)}}\leq C,\;\alpha>1
, then there exists a

such that for any

{\left\|{{e^{u}}{{\left|\psi\right|}^{2}}}\right\|_{{L^{\beta}}\left({{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}\right)}}\leq C^{\prime}.

In particular, by Stokes’ theorem, we have
\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{{e^{2u}}}\mathrm{d}v\leq\frac{1}{{{h_{\min}}}}
, and consequently,
\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{{e^{u}}{{\left|\psi\right|}^{2}}}{\mathrm{d}v}\leq C
.

Proof.

According to the embedding theorem of spinors, the spinor parts of all solutions are bounded in
L^{4}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
:

{\left\|\psi\right\|_{{L^{4}({\mathbb{S}^{2}})}}}\leq C.

Let
p=\frac{{2+\alpha}}{2}
and
q=\frac{{2+\alpha}}{\alpha}

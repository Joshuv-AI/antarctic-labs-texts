LLM-Guided Dynamic-UMAP for Personalized Federated Graph Learning
Sai Puppala, Ismail Hossain, Md Jahangir Alam, Tanzim Ahad, Sajedul TalukderComputer Science

Abstract
We present a novel approach that uses large language models (LLMs) to assist graph machine learning (GML) under personalization and privacy constraints. Our approach integrates (i) LLM-assisted data augmentation for sparse graphs, (ii) prompt and instruction tuning to adapt foundation models to graph tasks, and (iii) in-context learning to provide few-shot graph reasoning signals. These signals parameterize a Dynamic-UMAP manifold of client-specific graph embeddings inside a Bayesian variational objective for personalized federated learning. The method supports node classification and link prediction in low-resource settings and aligns LLM latent representations with graph structure via a cross-modal regularizer. We provide a convergence sketch for our variational aggregation, detail a privacy threat model with moments-accountant DP, and report applications to knowledge graph completion, recommendation-style link prediction, and citation/product graphs, along with proposed evaluation protocols for LLM-augmented GML.



1
Introduction

Graphs power applications from recommendation and scientific discovery to knowledge graph (KG) completion and question answering (QA)
[
12
,
10
]
. In practice, graphs are heterogeneous, private, and sparse across clients (e.g., institutions or edge devices). Meanwhile, LLMs encode extensive world knowledge and can be prompted or instruction-tuned to provide structural priors, textual context, and few-shot signals
[
5
]
. We ask:
How can LLMs assist personalized federated GML when labeled edges/nodes are scarce and data cannot leave clients?

We propose
LLM-Guided Dynamic-UMAP (LG-DUMAP)
, a Gaussian variational framework that: (1) builds client-specific graph manifolds using Dynamic-UMAP over graph embeddings; (2) injects LLM guidance via prompt/instruction-tuned text encoders and in-context exemplars; (3) performs
personalized
federated variational inference with privacy-preserving aggregation of
similarity prototypes (markers)
summarizing local similarity structures; and (4) aligns LLM and graph latent spaces to improve low-resource performance.

Contributions.

•

A parametric UMAP-style manifold objective coupled with variational
marker
aggregation for personalized federation.

•

Cross-modal alignment to LLM text space and calibrated pseudo-edges with abstention for robustness.

•

A concrete privacy threat model with secure aggregation, per-client clipping, and moments-accountant DP; and an attack-driven evaluation via membership inference.

•

Expanded baselines (Per-FedAvg, pFedMe, Ditto, FedProx; LightGCN/RotatE/ComplEx/CompGCN) with systems evidence (cost/latency, partial participation) and worst-client/percentile reporting.

Related Work

2.1
LLM-Augmented Graph Learning

Hybrid pipelines use language encoders to guide graph learning by supplying semantic priors, pseudo-labels, and retrieval-style context
[
5
]
. In text-rich graphs (e.g., citation networks, product catalogs, and KGs), LLM embeddings can act as additional node features, enable label/edge proposals under extreme sparsity, and furnish rationales. We leverage these capabilities with a
calibrated
admission policy for pseudo-edges (temperature scaling, confidence thresholding) to mitigate overconfident hallucinations
[
9
]
. Compared to text-only or late-fusion baselines
[
10
,
12
]
, we integrate language supervision
within
a geometric objective, coupling cross-modal alignment with a parametric manifold learner.

2.2
Personalized Federated Learning for (Graph) Models

FedAvg assumes homogeneous objectives and suffers on non-IID clients; remedies include FedProx
[
15
]
and personalization strategies such as Per-FedAvg
[
7
]
, pFedMe
[
6
]
, and Ditto
[
14
]
. Most operate at the level of
model parameters
. In contrast, we personalize through a lightweight
variational prototype layer
(markers) that summarizes local similarity structure and is safe to share under privacy budgets. This differs from prior federated GNN work that aggregates entire GNN weights/gradients, incurring larger communication and privacy footprints. We also incorporate partial participation and report worst-client/percentile metrics to characterize non-IID regimes.

2.3
Parametric Manifold Learning and UMAP

UMAP is a widely used non-linear dimensionality reduction technique built on fuzzy simplicial sets
[
16
]
; parametric variants learn an explicit mapping that can be amortized across data and clients
[
18
]
. Unlike purely unsupervised application of (parametric) UMAP to graph embeddings, our Dynamic-UMAP is optimized jointly with downstream tasks and a cross-modal regularizer tying the manifold to LLM embeddings. We additionally introduce
markers
—a KDE-style similarity model on interpoint distances in the learned space—which (i) acts as a compact, aggregatable representation for federation and (ii) improves link modeling in low-resource regimes. We report manifold trustworthiness/continuity and representation-similarity diagnostics (CKA, Procrustes) to probe geometry and modality alignment
[
13
,
8
]
.

2.4
Knowledge-Graph and Recommendation Baselines

For KG completion, translational and rotational families (TransE/RotatE) and bilinear approaches (ComplEx) remain strong baselines
[
3
,
20
,
21
]
. Structure-aware GNNs such as CompGCN add relation composition into message passing
[
22
]
. In recommendation, LightGCN shows that stripped-down propagation without non-linearities is competitive on collaborative filtering
[
11
]
. Our evaluation situates LG-DUMAP against these methods and emphasizes few-shot/cold-start conditions where text priors help most. We also include heterophily stress tests (e.g., Chameleon, Squirrel) where neighborhood homophily is weak and geometric signals matter
[
17
]
.

Methodology

Each client
k\in\{1,\dots,N\}
owns a private graph
G_{k}=(V_{k},E_{k},X_{k},T_{k})
with node features
X_{k}
, optional node/edge texts
T_{k}
, and tasks: node classification or link prediction. Clients cannot share raw data. The server maintains global priors but aggregates only privacy-preserving statistics.

LLM guidance.

An LLM
\mathcal{L}
provides (a) text embeddings for
T_{k}
, (b) instruction/prompt-tuned decoding to propose edges or node labels in low-resource regimes, and (c) in-context exemplars for few-shot behaviors. We denote the LLM text encoder by
h_{\theta}(\cdot)
and a lightweight adapter/prompt
P
.

We integrate UMAP, graph encoders, and LLM guidance within a variational federated objective.

3.1
Graph and Text Fusion Embeddings

We compute node embeddings by fusing a GNN encoder
\phi_{k}
with LLM text embeddings:

\tilde{X}_{k}=\mathrm{Fuse}\big(\mathrm{GNN}_{\phi_{k}}(G_{k}),\ h_{\theta}(T_{k};P)\big)\in\mathbb{R}^{|V_{k}|\times m}.

(1)

3.2
Dynamic-UMAP Manifold

We learn a low-dimensional manifold preserving local graph structure and LLM priors
[
16
,
18
]
:

Z_{k}=g_{\beta_{k}}(\tilde{X}_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{|V_{k}|\times d}.

(2)

The UMAP objective uses neighborhood probabilities
(p_{ij},q_{ij})
with

\displaystyle L_{\text{UMAP}}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=-\!\!\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}_{k}^{\mathrm{nn}}}\Big[p_{ij}\log q_{ij}+(1-p_{ij})\log(1-q_{ij})\Big],

(3)

\displaystyle q_{ij}

\displaystyle=\big(1+a\|z_{i}-z_{j}\|^{2b}\big)^{-1},

(4)

where
(a,b)
follow standard UMAP settings
[
16
]
. We optimize
g_{\beta_{k}}
jointly with downstream losses.

3.3
LLM-Augmented Similarity and Markers

Let
s_{ij}=\lVert z_{i}-z_{j}\rVert_{2}
in
Z_{k}
.
Markers
are scalar prototypes in the
distance
space (not vectors in
Z_{k}
). We model a soft assignment of pairwise distances to
M=\{m_{1},\dots,m_{|M|}\}\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
via a KDE-style mixture:

p_{eij}=\frac{\exp\!\big(-\lVert s_{ij}-m_{e}\rVert^{2}/(2\sigma_{\text{kern}}^{2})\big)}{\sum_{m^{\prime}\in M}\exp\!\big(-\lVert s_{ij}-m^{\prime}\rVert^{2}/(2\sigma_{\text{kern}}^{2})\big)}.

(5)

Markers are initialized from labeled edges and
calibrated
LLM pseudo-edges (accepted if confidence
c\geq\tau
after temperature scaling).

3.4
Cross-Modal Alignment

We align
Z_{k}
with LLM embeddings using cosine similarity with explicit normalization:

L_{\text{align}}^{(k)}=\sum_{i\in V_{k}}\!\Big(1-\frac{\langle z_{i},h_{\theta}(T_{k,i};P)\rangle}{\|z_{i}\|_{2}\,\|h_{\theta}(T_{k,i};P)\|_{2}}\Big).

(6)

We assess representation similarity with CKA and Procrustes analyses
[
13
,
8
]
.

3.5
Client Variational Objective

For client
k
, the loss is

L_{k}=\mathbb{E}_{q_{\phi_{k}}(M)}\!\Big[J_{k}(Z_{k})\Big]+\lambda\,\mathrm{KL}\!\big(q_{\phi_{k}}(M)\,\|\,s(M)\big)+\gamma L_{\text{align}}^{(k)}+\eta L_{\text{UMAP}}^{(k)},

(7)

where
J_{k}
includes classification NLL and link prediction BCE using
p_{eij}
. The prior
s(M)
is a simple Gaussian mixture over marker locations;
q_{\phi_{k}}(M)
is the client’s variational posterior over markers.

3.6
Server Aggregation (Personalized)

The server aggregates markers only:

M_{t+1}=M_{t}+\frac{\sum_{k}w_{k}\,\mathbb{E}_{q_{\phi_{k}}(M)}[M_{k}-M_{t}]}{\sum_{k}w_{k}}.

(8)

No raw data, node embeddings, or GNN weights are shared.

Calibration and Safety for LLM Proposals

We calibrate LLM pseudo-edges via temperature scaling (
T
) on a validation slice and report ECE (15 bins) and Brier scores
[
9
,
4
]
. Pseudo-edges with confidence
\hat{p}\geq\tau
are admitted; we study F1 vs.
\tau\in\{0.6,0.7,0.8,0.9\}
and acceptance counts.

Calibration Metrics

Expected Calibration Error (ECE):
\mathrm{ECE}=\sum_{b}\frac{|B_{b}|}{n}\,|\mathrm{acc}(B_{b})-\mathrm{conf}(B_{b})|
. Brier:
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}(\hat{p}_{i}-y_{i})^{2}
.

6
Theory

Assume each client loss
L_{k}
is
L
-smooth and the update variance is bounded. The aggregated marker update is a stochastic approximation to a stationary point of
L(M)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k}L_{k}(M)
.

Proposition 1 (Convergence of Marker Averaging).

Under
L
-smoothness, bounded variance, and diminishing step size (or Polyak averaging) with partial participation rate
p>0
, we have
\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\lVert\nabla L(M_{t})\rVert=0
, with an
O(1/\sqrt{t})
rate under standard assumptions.

Lemma 1 (Alignment improves local curvature).

If LLM embeddings form
\delta
-separated clusters consistent with labels and
\gamma>0
, the alignment term adds a positive semidefinite component to the neighborhood Hessian, reducing variance of
p_{eij}
and accelerating convergence.

Appendix A
Convergence Sketch (Extended)

Let
L(M)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k}L_{k}(M)
be
L
-smooth and let the aggregated update be
M_{t+1}=M_{t}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k}\mathbb{E}_{q_{\phi_{k}}}[M_{k}-M_{t}]
with bounded variance. With client sampling rate
p>0
and diminishing step size or Polyak averaging, standard stochastic approximation results yield
\lim_{t}\lVert\nabla L(M_{t})\rVert\to 0
at
O(1/\sqrt{t})
. The KL term stabilizes around Gaussian mixture priors; alignment adds local positive curvature when semantic clusters are separable.

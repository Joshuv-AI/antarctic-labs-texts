The implications of LncRNAs and premature ovarian insufficiency
Authors: Qian Zhao, Xuan-ling Li, Guang-yao Lin, Chen-lin Sui, Bao-fang Zhang, Yu-quan Bai, Lian-wei Xu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPremature ovarian insufficiency (POI) is a complex disorder characterized by hypoestrogenism, elevated gonadotropins, and menstrual irregularity or amenorrhea. Effective treatments remain limited, but advances in reproductive biology have highlighted long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs) as pivotal regulators of granulosa cell function and follicular development. Growing evidence shows that lncRNAs contribute to POI pathogenesis and hold promise as diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets. This review summarizes recent findings on the lncRNA-mediated regulation of granulosa cell proliferation, autophagy, apoptosis, hormone signalling, mitochondrial stress, glycolysis, and DNA damage and discusses current limitations—including small sample sizes, limited in vivo validation, and translational challenges—that hinder clinical application. Although lncRNAs represent an emerging frontier in POI research, their diagnostic and therapeutic value requires further validation before clinical integration.Keywords: LncRNAs, Correlation, Premature ovarian insufficiency, Biomarkers, Review

Introduction

Premature ovarian insufficiency (POI) is characterized by chronic hypoestrogenism resulting from the premature loss of ovarian function in women under 40 years of age [1]. The condition was first described as “primary ovarian insufficiency” by Fuller Albright et al. in 1942 [2]. POI typically arises from a diminished ovarian follicle pool or a failure of follicles to respond to normal hormonal stimulation, ultimately leading to premature menopause [3]. It affects approximately 1–3% of women in the general population [4]. During assisted reproductive technology (ART) treatment, patients with POI often experience reduced fertility and pregnancy rates, higher miscarriage rates, and lower live birth outcomes. Prolonged estrogen deficiency also exerts systemic effects, including decreased bone mineral density, impaired cardiovascular function, and elevated neurological risk, as well as compromised overall well-being and sexual health [3, 5, 6]. Since this disease may take years to manifest, early identification of accurate diagnostic biomarkers is crucial for improving clinical diagnosis, guiding treatment decisions, assessing disease risk, and predicting prognosis in patients with POI.

Noncoding RNAs (ncRNAs), a class of functional RNAs that do not encode proteins, have attracted considerable attention in recent years [7]. They are increasingly recognized as key regulators in the pathogenesis of numerous diseases [8], including cardiovascular [9] and neurological disorders [10]. On the basis of their length, ncRNAs can be broadly categorized into long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs) and small noncoding RNAs (sncRNAs). LncRNAs, typically longer than 200 nucleotides, exert endocrine, paracrine, and autocrine regulatory effects [11]. Moreover, they play essential roles in a wide range of biological processes, including cellular proliferation, adhesion, apoptosis, angiogenesis, and migration [12]. Studies have also demonstrated that alterations in lncRNA expression levels, whether through depletion, overexpression, or mutation, are closely associated with various forms of dysfunction [13, 14]. Emerging evidence suggests that lncRNAs participate in the pathogenesis of POI and may serve as novel diagnostic biomarkers. However, the diversity of lncRNAs and the scarcity of mechanistic investigations in POI highlight substantial gaps in our current understanding. To date, no comprehensive systematic review has synthesized these findings.

To address this knowledge gap, we conducted a comprehensive literature search across nine databases, encompassing six English databases (PubMed, Web of Science, Springer, Embase, Scopus, EBSCO Medicine) and three Chinese databases (China National Knowledge Infrastructure, Wanfang, VIP). We systematically reviewed clinical, in vitro, and in vivo studies investigating the roles of lncRNAs in POI. The key lncRNAs involved in POI regulation are summarized in Table 1, with details on their expression patterns, molecular targets, biological functions, study species, and study model. This review integrates current evidence on lncRNA-mediated regulation in POI to evaluate its potential as a diagnostic biomarker and explore its possible application in disease prediction and therapeutic intervention.

LncRNAs associated with POI

Possible potential mechanisms between LncRNAs and POI occurrence

LncRNAs participating in the proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis of GCs

Granulosa cells (GCs) are a layer of parietal cells that surround the follicle surface, support the formation and development of follicles, and secrete hormones associated with ovarian function [23]. GC is associated with many ovarian-related diseases, including polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) [24] and POI [25]. Dysfunction of GCs is a major contributor to POI, and recent studies have indicated that lncRNAs play crucial roles in modulating gene expression through diverse mechanisms. Additionally, lncRNAs serve as essential regulatory factors in the proper development of GCs, follicles, and ovaries [26]. Wang et al. [27] reported that lncRNAs can act synergistically as selective ligands, recruiting and regulating RNA-binding coregulatory factors in response to specific signals. This ncRNA/RNA-binding protein interaction integrates transcriptional programs and thereby influences GC proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis. Li et al. [20] demonstrated that downregulation of the expression of the lncRNA granule cell associated transcript 1 (GCAT1) leads to ovarian dysfunction. P27 functions as an effector of GCAT1 in regulating GC proliferation, and inhibiting p27 expression in GCs may be beneficial for restoring GC proliferation and preventing GCAT1-related POI. Wang et al. [16] confirmed that downregulation of the lncRNA PVT1 can promote GC apoptosis by inducing Foxo3a, while its overexpression can restore ovarian function. Similarly, studies [18] have revealed that the lncRNA ZNF674-AS1 is downregulated in the GCs of POI patients, demonstrating its role in regulating GC proliferation, glycolysis, and AMPK activation via interaction with ALDOA. In addition, Sun et al. [22] were the first to report that the lncRNA DANCR is involved in the negative regulation of granulosa cell aging and POI development. Knockout of DANCR weakened the interaction between hNRNPC and p53, thereby increasing p53 protein levels and inducing granulosa cell aging, which exacerbated follicular atresia and led to POI. Luo et al. confirmed the downregulation of HOTAIR expression in ovarian tissue and granulosa cells through microarray dataset analysis and qRT‒PCR experiments and demonstrated the promoting effect of HOTAIR in POI and its potential as a biomarker for POI [28]. They proposed that HOTAIR promotes proliferation by regulating the miR-148b-3p/ATG14-mediated autophagy pathway. In summary, multiple studies have shown that regulating the expression of lncRNAs can promote ovarian granulosa cell proliferation and improve POI through various mechanisms, suggesting that lncRNAs are promising biomarkers for prognosis. To date, all clinical studies involving lncRNAs and POI, including the lncRNA GCAT1, the lncRNA PVT1, the lncRNA ZNF674-AS1, the lncRNA DANCR, and the lncRNA HOTAIR, have shown significant downregulation, leading to pathological manifestations of ovarian GC in terms of proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis. The overexpression of these lncRNAs can restore ovarian function in POI and may help prevent GCAT1-related POI. Notably, the lncRNA HOTAIR shows therapeutic potential in alleviating cisplatin-induced POI toxicity. Despite ongoing challenges, these innovative discoveries of lncRNAs highlight scientific prospects for expanding their predictive and therapeutic applications in POI ovarian GC proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis.

LncRNAs regulating the expression of hormone-related genes and their receptors

Hormones are circulating messengers that mediate the long-distance regulation of specific target organ activities [29]. LncRNAs can exhibit autocrine hormone-like behavior and regulate cellular activity by controlling transcription. For example, they can alter cellular signalling indirectly by interacting with hormone-coding genes or hormone antagonists/receptors [30]. Steroid receptor RNA activator (SRA) is among the earliest lncRNAs linked to steroid receptor signalling and functions as a prototypical RNA coactivator [31]. Anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH), produced by GCs, regulates follicular growth. AMH is closely related to ovarian function and has important value for the diagnosis and prediction of POI [32, 33], with Amhr2 serving as the main determinant of AMH action [32, 34]. One study [15] revealed that lncRNA-Amhr2 can activate the Amhr2 gene in GC by increasing promoter activity, thereby regulating AMH levels and ovarian function by controlling AMH signalling. Moreover, Li et al. [20] reported a noteworthy association between decreased expression of the lncRNA GCAT1 and the serum levels of follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) and AMH. Another study [18] also confirmed that the serum levels of AMH and estradiol in POI patients were positively correlated with the expression of the lncRNA ZNF674-AS1, whereas the serum level of FSH was negatively correlated. Together, these studies indicate that the expression of lncRNAs in GC is correlated with the serum levels of clinical ovarian function indicators. However, the concept that lncRNAs may act in hormone autocrine manners remains challenging to verify owing to current technical limitations, and existing knowledge remains incomplete. Further research is ongoing to elucidate the physiological functions of lncRNA-Amhr2 in regulating the endocrine system [15].

LncRNAs regulating mitochondrial function and affect oxidative stress homeostasis in the ovary

Mitochondria are key intracellular organelles that play essential roles in regulating energy production, oxidative stress, calcium homeostasis, and apoptosis [35]. They are also the primary site for maintaining redox homeostasis between the generation and clearance of reactive oxygen species (ROS) [36]. Mitochondrial dysfunction can lead to an increase in oxidative stress, which may cause DNA damage, gene mutations, and apoptosis, thereby contributing to the occurrence of POI [37]. LncRNAs can regulate the transcription and translation of multiple genes and thus participate in various physiological and pathological activities, including oxidative stress, the inflammatory response, and apoptosis [38]. Chen et al. reported that downregulating the expression of Lnc Maternally Expressed 3 (MEG3) can inhibit the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway, reduce autophagy in GCs, and normalize their proliferation [39]. Further research by Yang et al. revealed that the lncRNA MEG3 may inhibit autophagy and mitigate H2O2-induced oxidative stress damage in human ovarian GCs by regulating the adenosine 5‘-monophosphate (AMP)-activated protein kinase (AMPK) mTOR pathway [38]. Moreover, another study revealed that metastasis-associated lung adenocarcinoma transcript 1 (MALAT1) can affect mitochondrial function and promote mitochondrial depolarization and ROS production, thereby activating the mitochondrial pathway and inducing apoptosis [40]. Yang et al. studied the ovarian damage induced by T-2 toxin exposure in sows and reported that T-2 toxin increases the level of the lncRNA CUFF.253988.1, inhibits the activation of the SIRT3/PGC-1α pathway, leads to mitochondrial dysfunction and increased apoptosis, and ultimately results in ovarian damage [41]. Multiple studies had shown that lncRNAs, important messengers that mediate communication between the nucleus and mitochondria, display abnormal regulatory functions and can mediate aging and the occurrence and development of tumors [42, 43]. However, there is limited evidence suggesting the presence of nuclear-encoded lncRNAs in mitochondria. The impact and regulatory mechanisms of lncRNAs in mitochondria on mitochondrial genome function may provide new directions for the early diagnosis and precise treatment of POI.

LncRNAs promoting glycolysis to regulate ovarian function

Glycolysis is an inefficient way of providing energy, but it sustains rapid ATP production under energy stress [44]. Because cell proliferation requires rapid production of ATP, activation of glycolytic flux is accompanied by the production of 3-phosphoglycerate and dihydroxyacetone phosphate, which maintain the production of amino acids, triglycerides, and phospholipids required for accelerated proliferation [45]. In addition, glycolysis generates fewer reactive oxygen species (ROS) than does mitochondrial oxidative metabolism, thereby reducing aging and apoptosis [45]. Several researchers have reported that lncRNAs are closely associated with the occurrence of POI by regulating glycolysis in GCs. Chen et al. [46] confirmed that Lx8-SINE B2 deficiency affects multiple metabolic pathways, particularly glycolysis. Its interaction with the glycolytic enzyme Eno1 regulates the expression of metabolic genes and affects glycolysis. The lncRNA Lx8-SINE B2 has also been identified as a novel regulator of ESC proliferation, the cell cycle, and metabolism. Similarly, another study [47] revealed that the lncRNA Lncenc1 maintains the immature state of ESCs by promoting glycolysis. Nevertheless, the potential mechanism by which lncRNAs play a role in aerobic glycolysis is complex and remains poorly explored in the context of POI. Further systematic and comprehensive studies are needed to clarify their roles.

LncRNAs regulating DNA damage repair in the ovary

Genomic integrity is crucial to life. To maintain genomic stability, eukaryotic cells have developed the ability to detect DNA damage, transmit signals indicating damage, and initiate cellular responses to eliminate it. This coordinated process is known as the DNA damage response (DDR) [48, 49]. When damage is severe and cannot be repaired, the DDR usually causes stagnation of the cell cycle during DNA repair or leads to apoptosis [50]. The inability to effectively reverse DNA damage in cells often results in severe clinical consequences, such as neurodegenerative lesions [51], infertility [52], immunodeficiency [53], and cancer [54]. Abnormal GC function can result in follicular atresia and POI in mammals [55]. If DNA damage is not repaired promptly or accurately, cell death may occur, leading to premature ovarian failure [56, 57]. To date, minichromosome maintenance 8 homologous recombination repair factor (MCM8) [58], MutS homologue 5 (MSH5) [59], breast cancer gene 2 (BRCA2) [60, 61] and other variants of DNA repair genes have been demonstrated to be pathogenic factors for POI. Liu et al. [62] suggested that GCs from women with biochemical premature ovarian insufficiency (bPOI) [63] differ significantly from those from healthy women in terms of the expression of 191 lncRNAs. A total of 22 lncRNAs with differential expression were detected in the GC expression data of bPOI patients through network analysis and validation of genes that interact with hub miRNAs in the ceRNA network, providing further research into POI pathogenic mechanisms and the possibility of related genes. In another study [17], microarray analysis revealed low expression of the lncRNA HCP5, which is located near the known POI pathogenic gene MSH5, in the GCs of bPOI patients. This study also demonstrated the role of HCP5 in regulating MSH5 expression through YB1, thereby impacting the DDR process in GCs. These findings emphasize that HCP5 is a new MSH5 transcription activator and suggest a new epigenetic mechanism for POI. Li et al. [21] investigated the expression of the lncRNA RP4-545C24.1 in the GCs of bPOI patients and named it DDGC. Silencing DDGC significantly inhibited DNA damage repair ability, confirming that DDGC can improve etoposide-induced DNA damage and apoptosis in vivo. The involvement of lncRNAs in regulating ovarian DNA damage repair provides a new epigenetic perspective on the pathogenesis of POI. Unfortunately, little is known about the involvement of dysfunctional lncRNAs in the development of POI; therefore, more in-depth research on DNA damage repair is needed.

LncRNAs regulating fragile X-related premature ovarian insufficiency (FXPOI)

Fragile X-associated premature ovarian insufficiency (FXPOI) is one of several disorders caused by the CGG trinucleotide repeat extension located in the 5’ untranslated region (UTR) of the fragile X messenger ribonucleoprotein 1 (FMR1) gene on the X chromosome. Women with FXPOI have a reduced number of follicles, manifested as amenorrhea, estrogen deficiency, and loss of fertility before the age of 40 [64]. Studies have shown that women with fragile X chromosomes have an average of 5 years earlier menopause than their peers do [65, 66]. In FXPOI patients, the levels of FMR1 mRNA are abnormally increased, and the toxic effects of FMR1 mRNA lead to the occurrence of FXPOI [67]. Excess FMR1 mRNA forms abnormal secondary structures that bind to cellular proteins, generating RNA–protein inclusions that persist in brain and ovarian tissues and resist degradation [68]. Some proteins within these inclusion bodies are involved in regulating ovarian function, such as Sam68, which can modulate the mRNA levels of FSH and LH receptors [69]. Loss of Sam68 can lead to ovarian antagonism towards FSH and LH at the receptor level, impairing female fertility and follicular development. In addition, the toxic effects of mRNAs may trigger mitochondrial dysfunction even before the formation of RNA protein inclusion bodies [70]. A study of PM mice revealed that the mitochondrial function of oocytes and GCs in a PM mouse model was abnormal, including reduced mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) and decreased mitochondrial mass [71]. However, there is currently no research observing mitochondrial dysfunction in the GCs of human PM carriers. Researchers have studied newly discovered lncRNAs, namely, FMR4, FMR5, and FMR6, in recent years. Among them, FMR4 is downregulated in patients with fragile X syndrome and upregulated in PM carriers, mirroring the expression pattern of FMR1. The expression levels of FMR5 appear consistent across normal individuals, individuals with complete mutations, and PM carriers, whereas FMR6 has been found to have a regulatory effect on FMR1 mRNA. Both FMR4 and FMR6 are believed to be related to FXPOI [72–75]. Nevertheless, there is still limited research on the etiology and regulatory mechanisms of FXPOI. Large-scale clinical, animal, and cellular studies related to FXPOI are needed to explore deeper mechanisms, discover new therapeutic targets, and benefit FXPOI and POI patients.

Discussion

Comprehensive analysis revealed that lncRNAs may regulate the development of POI through several pathways (Fig. 1). First, lncRNAs appear to participate in glycolysis and glucose metabolism in GCs, regulating cell-cycle-related genes (Fig. 1: Top left path). Preliminary evidence from studies on the lncRNA Lx8 SINE B2 and the lncRNA ZNF674-AS1 supported this mechanism. Second, several in vivo or in vitro validated lncRNAs, including lncRNA-PVT1, lncRNA-GCAT1, lncRNA DDGC, and lncRNA DANCR, indicated that the abnormal expression of aberrant lncRNA genes may influence proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis in the ovarian GCs of POI patients (Fig. 1: Middle Path). Third, studies on the lncRNAs MEG3 and CUFF253988.1 suggested that lncRNAs may affect ovarian mitochondrial function, thereby disrupting oxidative stress homeostasis (Fig. 1: Bottom left path). Fourth, small-scale microarray identification revealed that lncRNA HCP5 and DDGC may be involved in gene transcription or translation regulation and are closely related to the DNA damage response, thereby contributing to POI pathogenesisv (Fig. 1: Upper right path). Finally, lncRNAs may be involved in regulating the expression of hormone-related genes and their receptors (Fig. 1: Bottom right path), thus regulating hormone levels to affect ovarian function. Related clinical studies on lncRNA-Amr2, lncRNA GCAT1, and lncRNA ZNF674-AS1 had supported this finding. In addition, lncRNAs have been implicated in the pathogenesis of fragile X-associated premature ovarian failure (FXPOI) (Fig. 1: Bottom Path).

Possible potential mechanism of lncRNA-induced POI pathogenesis (By Figdraw.).

This figure summarizes six possible pathways through which lncRNAs regulate ovarian function and trigger premature ovarian insufficiency (POI). These pathways interact with one another and may converge on common cellular outcomes, leading to POI. Annotation: GCAT1, PVT1, and DANCR regulate translation factors (PTBP1, FOXO3a, and hnRNPC). ZNF674-AS1 and Lx8 SINE B2 affect glycolysis and mitochondrial function. MEG3 and CUFF.253988.1 regulate autophagy. HCP5 and DDGC modulate DNA repair. The accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) induces oxidative stress, which may trigger excessive autophagy; both processes can lead to DNA damage and promote GC apoptosis or senescence. lncRNAs derived from Amhr2 exhibit hormone-like activity, activating the Amhr2 gene and regulating AMH signalling. These hormone-like autocrine actions may contribute to dysregulated hormone profiles and ovarian dysfunction. In addition, FMR1, FMR4, and FMR6 have been implicated in fragile X–related POI (FXPOI)

To date, robust evidence has demonstrated that lncRNAs play critical roles in granulosa cell (GC) proliferation and apoptosis [76], as well as in regulating hormone-related genes and receptors that directly influence ovarian function [77]. LncRNA-mediated mitochondrial dysfunction [78–80] and oxidative stress [80, 81] have also been consistently reported, although the underlying mechanisms remain to be fully elucidated. In contrast, the roles of lncRNAs in glycolysis [82, 83], the DNA damage response [84], and FXPOI [85] have been supported mainly by preliminary findings and remain speculative. Importantly, biological processes such as energy metabolism, mitochondrial activity, GC survival, and hormone production are highly interconnected rather than isolated. Therefore, lncRNAs may exert both individual and synergistic regulatory effects across these pathways. However, whether dysfunction of a single lncRNA, independent of cooperative interactions, is sufficient to trigger POI remains unclear.

Despite recent progress, research on lncRNAs as diagnostic biomarkers for POI remains at an early stage. To date, only one study [86] has quantitatively evaluated the diagnostic value of the lncRNAs FMR4, FMR5, and FMR6 in FXPOI via receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. The results showed that FMR4 could differentiate premutation carriers from FXPOI patients (AUC = 0.67), whereas FMR5 and FMR6 showed no diagnostic association. Overall, most clinical studies have focused only on correlation rather than causation, with small sample sizes, heterogeneous lncRNA targets, limited in vivo validation, and a lack of cross-species confirmation. The upstream regulators, downstream effectors, and detailed molecular mechanisms of dysregulated lncRNAs in POI remain largely undefined. Future studies should therefore integrate advanced technologies such as single-cell sequencing, multiomics analysis, and genetic animal models, alongside large-scale multicenter clinical studies, to further elucidate lncRNA functions and validate their clinical utility. The selective disruption of a single lncRNA in the ovary via precise genetic tools may further clarify its causal roles in POI. Additionally, constructing predictive models that incorporate lncRNA biomarkers (e.g., odds ratios or AUC values) will be essential for assessing diagnostic and therapeutic potential and accelerating clinical translation.

Conclusions

POI has profound physiological and psychological consequences for young women of reproductive age. The abnormal expression of lncRNAs is closely related to the pathogenesis of POI, suggesting that lncRNAs may be potential biomarkers for diagnosing POI. A more comprehensive understanding of the regulatory roles of lncRNAs will provide valuable insights and open new directions for the diagnosis and treatment of idiopathic POI.

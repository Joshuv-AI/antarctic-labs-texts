A categorical account of the Metropolis–Hastings algorithm
Rob Cornish, Andi Q. Wang

Abstract
. Metropolis–Hastings (MH) is a foundational Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithm. In this paper, we ask whether it is possible to formulate and analyse MH in terms of categorical probability, using a recent involutive framework for MH-type procedures as a concrete case study. We show how basic MCMC concepts such as invariance and reversibility can be formulated in Markov categories, and how one part of the MH kernel can be analysed using standard CD categories. To go further, we then study enrichments of CD categories over commutative monoids. This gives an expressive setting for reasoning abstractly about a range of important probabilistic concepts, including substochastic kernels, finite and σ \sigma -finite measures, absolute continuity, singular measures, and Lebesgue decompositions. Using these tools, we give synthetic necessary and sufficient conditions for a general MH-type sampler to be reversible with respect to a given target distribution.



1.
Introduction

Markov kernels
are a fundamental concept in probability theory.
Recall that a Markov kernel
P:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
encodes a family of probability distributions
P(x,\mathrm{d}y)
on the space
\mathcal{Y}
indexed by points
x\in\mathcal{X}
.
Recent work on
copy-discard (CD) categories
and
Markov categories

(
Cho2019
;
Fritz2020
)
has sought to develop a synthetic framework for reasoning about systems of Markov kernels and their unnormalised counterparts algebraically.
This approach has been applied successfully to a number of classical results from probability and statistics, yielding streamlined and more conceptual proofs than traditional measure-theoretic arguments
(
Fritz2021
;
Rischel2020
;
Fritz2020
;
Moss2023
)
.

In applied settings, Markov kernels are often regarded as “stochastic functions” and used to model
conditional probability distributions
, which in turn have a huge number of applications in computational statistics, machine learning, and computer science generally.
It is therefore natural to ask: to what extent are Markov and CD categories ready for reasoning about contemporary methodology in these domains?
Are they usable “as-is”, or are additional structures or concepts needed to handle the complexities of modern algorithms?

We consider this question in the context of
Markov chain Monte Carlo
(MCMC) methods, and specifically the
Metropolis–Hastings
(MH) algorithm
(
metropolis1953equation
;
Hastings1970
)
, which is a foundational tool for sampling from complex probability distributions.
We focus on a recent line of work which studies MH through the lens of
involutions

(
Neklyudov2020
;
Andrieu2020
;
Glatt-Holtz2023
;
Glatt-Holtz2024
;
cusumano2020automating
)
, building on earlier foundational work
(
Tierney1998
;
Green1995
)
.
This perspective has already lead to a range of new methodology of interest to computational statistics practitioners
(
Bou-Rabee2024
;
Liu2024
;
Xu2025
)
.

A major contribution to the involutive approach is the framework of
(
Andrieu2020
)
.
This considers a Markov kernel
\Pi_{\mathrm{MH}}:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}
defined

(1)

\Pi_{\mathrm{MH}}(\xi,\mathrm{d}\xi^{\prime})\coloneqq\alpha(\xi)\,\delta_{\phi(\xi)}(\mathrm{d}\xi^{\prime})+(1-\alpha(\xi))\,\delta_{\xi}(\mathrm{d}\xi^{\prime}),

where

is a measurable function regarded as an

,

is a measurable

(i.e.
\phi=\phi^{-1}
), and

is the Dirac delta.
Intuitively, given an input

, this kernel transitions to

with probability

, and otherwise remains at

.
By combining

with a suitable

(see Section

(

)
that a very wide variety of MH-type algorithms can be recovered as special cases (see also Appendix

The main theoretical result of
(

)
is their Theorem 3, which gives sufficient conditions under which

is

Reversibility in turn constitutes a key correctness guarantee for many MCMC algorithms.
We state a summarised version of this result as follows (see Appendix

Suppose

The kernel

\alpha(\xi)=\begin{cases}\alpha(\phi(\xi))\,r(\xi)&\text{if $\xi\in S$,}\\

Summary of main results

Given the important implications of Theorem
1.1
for MH-type procedures, it serves as a natural test case for the applicability of categorical probability.
Our goal in this paper is to obtain this result synthetically using Markov and CD categories.
For this, we find that three levels of structure are needed:

•

In Section
2
, we use Markov categories to formulate generic aspects of MCMC algorithms, such as invariance, reversibility and state-space augmentation.

•

In Section
3
, we use CD categories to reason about the first summand in (
1
): we prove reversibility of this term, and recover part of the condition (
2
).

•

However, to study the entire kernel
\Pi_{\mathrm{MH}}
, we need additional structure: for example, we need a way to “add” morphisms, and we need a way to reason about decompositions of morphisms into disjoint “parts”.
In Section
4
, we therefore consider an
enriched
extension of CD categories.
This allows us to provide a synthetic generalisation of Theorem
1.1
in the form of Theorems
4.27
and
4.43
below.

The appendix contains some additional background and proofs, as well as two additional examples of MCMC-related techniques (the systematic-scan Gibbs sampler, and importance sampling) within categorical probability, which may be of interest to practitioners.

We believe these results are interesting in their own right, as they involve arguments that are both more conceptual and more general than their measure-theoretic counterparts.
We also hope our work illustrates how categorical tools may be used to analyse contemporary methodology in computational statistics more generally.

Technical contributions

In order to consider the full MH kernel
\Pi_{\mathrm{MH}}
, we study CD categories
enriched
over commutative monoids, which provides a minimal structure for reasoning about addition of morphisms.
This enrichment is expressive, and permits formulating and proving results about abstract versions of the following important concepts from measure theory:

•

Substochastic kernels
and
probabilities
(i.e. values in
[0,1]
).

•

Finite
and
\sigma
-finite
kernels.

•

Pointwise
absolute continuity
between kernels.

•

Singular measures
and
Lebesgue decompositions
.

The enrichment combines well with string diagrams, allowing for straightforward graphical reasoning.
It also integrates naturally with existing categorical approaches to probabilistic choice: when suitable coproducts exist, the subcategory of normalised morphisms carries the structure of a
distributive Markov category

(
ackerman2024probabilistic
)
, relating our “algebraic” treatment of convex combinations to the “sampling” perspective.

Importantly, we show that these tools are not merely descriptive, but may be used to obtain interesting results about probabilistic concepts.
In the context of Theorem
1.1
, this enrichment allows us to reason about decompositions of
\mu
into parts supported on
S
and
S^{c}
, and to obtain the condition (
2
) via purely algebraic arguments.
It also supplies a converse to Theorem
1.1
, which was not given in
(
Andrieu2020
)
, and permits a straightforward extension to the historically more complex
skew-reversible
setting (Corollary
4.29
).
We believe these results suggest this enrichment may be useful in applications beyond MCMC also.

4.

4.2.1.

Statisticians are trained automatically to associate a convex combination like (

In turn, this means they cannot be used to interpret convex combinations like (

For our goal of proving Theorem

However, for general probability theory, it seems useful to have a setting that supports both these perspectives.

Proposition 4.14.

Let
\mathbf{C}
be a semiadditive CD category with an initial object and binary coproducts whose inclusions are normalised and deterministic.

4.3.
Cancellative and finite morphisms

It is often useful to know that a commutative monoid
\mathcal{M}
is
cancellative
.
This property says that if
P+Q=P+R
holds in
\mathcal{M}
, then also
Q=R
.
Unfortunately, due to the possibility of obtaining infinite mass, kernels are not cancellative in general.
For example, if
P(x,A)=\infty
for all
x
and
A
, then
P+Q=P
for any
Q
.
This motivates the following elementwise definition instead:

Definition 4.15.

Let
P:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
be a morphism in a category
\mathbf{C}
equipped with a
\mathbf{CMon}
-enrichment.
We say that
P
is
cancellative
if for all
Q,R:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
such that
P+Q=P+R
, it holds that
Q=R
.

Note that we define this purely in terms of a
\mathbf{CMon}
-enrichment (not a CD structure).
It therefore applies to the category
\mathbf{Kern}
(Example
3.2
) as well as to
\mathbf{sfKern}
.

The following basic result holds for cancellative morphisms in any
\mathbf{CMon}
-enriched category.

Proposition 4.16.

Let
P,Q:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
be morphisms in a
\mathbf{CMon}
-enriched category
\mathbf{C}
.
If
P\leq Q
and
Q
is cancellative, then so is
P
.

Proof.

By assumption, there is some
R:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
such that
P+R=Q
.
If we have
P+S=P+T
for some
S,T:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
, then

Q+S=(P+R)+S=(P+R)+T=Q+T,

and so
S=T
by cancellativity of
Q
.
∎

For kernels, cancellativity recovers the notion of
\sigma
-finiteness
.
Recall that a measure
\mu
on a measurable space
\mathcal{X}
is
\sigma
-finite if there exists disjoint measurable

such that
\mathcal{X}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}
and each
\mu(A_{n})<\infty
.
The following result is proven in Appendix

.

Proposition 4.17.

Let
K:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
be a kernel (i.e. a morphism in
\mathbf{Kern}
).
If
K
is pointwise
\sigma
-finite (i.e. each

is
\sigma
-finite), then

is cancellative.
The converse holds if all singleton subsets of

are measurable.

The following basic result would be tedious to show in terms of
\sigma
-finiteness, but is straightforward in terms of cancellativity.

Proposition 4.18.

Cancellative morphisms in a
\mathbf{CMon}
-enriched category are preserved under composition with isomorphisms.

Proof.

Let
P:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
be cancellative, and let
\varphi:\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{X}
and
\psi:\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{Z}
be isomorphisms.
Suppose
Q,R:\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{Z}
satisfy

\psi\circ P\circ\varphi+Q=\psi\circ P\circ\varphi+R.

Bilinearity of composition (Definition
4.1
) implies that

P+\psi^{-1}\circ Q\circ\varphi^{-1}=P+\psi^{-1}\circ R\circ\varphi^{-1},

and hence
\psi^{-1}\circ Q\circ\varphi^{-1}=\psi^{-1}\circ R\circ\varphi^{-1}
by cancellativity of
P
.
Applying
\psi
and
\varphi
on either side then gives
Q=R
.
∎

A closely related notion to cancellativity is
finiteness
.
Our terminology here is motivated by the case of kernels (see Proposition
4.20
below, proven in Appendix

).

Definition 4.19.

A morphism
P:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
in a semiadditive CD category
\mathbf{C}

if the effect

is cancellative.

Proposition 4.20.

Let
P:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
be a kernel (i.e. a morphism in
\mathbf{Kern}
).
Then
P
is finite if and only if
P(x,\mathcal{Y})<\infty

In

It is therefore natural to ask whether this relationship holds generally.
We conjecture that it does not always hold, and so introduce the following definition to capture the cases where it does.

Definition 4.21.

A semiadditive CD category
\mathbf{C}
is

if all finite morphisms in
\mathbf{C}
are cancellative.

Finiteness interacts with substochasticity in a natural way, as the following result shows.

Proposition 4.22.

Let

4.5.
Absolute continuity

From Remark
4.7
, the hom-sets of a
\mathbf{CMon}
-enriched category
\mathbf{C}
admit a preorder
\leq
.
They also admit a separate preorder related to
absolute continuity
.
Recall that a measure
\nu
is absolutely continuous with respect to another measure
\mu
if
\mu(A)=0
implies
\nu(A)=0
.
Intuitively, this says that
\nu
assigns mass only to regions where
\mu
does.
When both are absolutely continuous with respect to each other, they are said to be
equivalent
.
The following extends these ideas to arbitrary morphisms in a
\mathbf{CMon}
-enriched category.

Definition 4.30.

Let
P,Q:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
be morphisms in a
\mathbf{CMon}
-enriched category
\mathbf{C}
.
We write
P\ll Q
if for all
R:\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{X}
and
S:\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{Z}
in
\mathbf{C}
we have

(35)

S\circ Q\circ R=0\implies S\circ P\circ R=0.

We write
P\equiv Q
if both
P\ll Q
and
Q\ll P
hold.

Note that this does not require a CD structure: only a
\mathbf{CMon}
-enrichment.
In particular, we can ask what happens when
\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{Kern}
(Example
3.2
).
This is answered by the following result, proven in Section
D.3.1
of the Appendix.

Proposition 4.31.

Let
P,Q:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
be kernels (i.e. morphisms in
\mathbf{Kern}
).
Then
P\ll Q
if and only if
P(x,\mathrm{d}y)
is absolutely continuous with respect to
Q(x,\mathrm{d}y)
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
.
Accordingly,
P\equiv Q
if and only if
P(x,\mathrm{d}y)
and
Q(x,\mathrm{d}y)
are equivalent as measures for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
.

It is straightforward to check that
\ll
is reflexive and transitive and is therefore a preorder.
Like for
\leq
in Remark

, it also induces an enrichment over preorders, as the following result shows.
We omit the proof, which is immediate from Definition

Proposition 4.32.

\ll
is preserved by pre- and post-composition.

The following shows that

has

Proposition 4.33.

It holds that

Moreover, if
P\ll 0

Proof.

Recall that
S\circ 0\circ R=0

This immediately gives the first claim.
If

Taking

4.6.
Lebesgue decompositions

A fundamental concept in measure theory is the notion of a

Given measures
\nu

\nu=\nu_{\mathrm{ac}}+\nu_{\mathrm{s}},

is another measure that is

Here singular means there exists a measurable set

\nu_{\mathrm{s}}(S)=0
and

Intuitively,

and

It is a classical result that a Lebesgue decomposition always exists whenever

and

are

(

We now establish an abstract version of this concept in the setting of

-enriched categories.

Let

is a greatest lower bound of

\ll

Meets do not need always need to exist.
Moreover, when they do, they are usually not unique, since
\ll

Proposition 4.37.

Meets are preserved by pre- and post-composition with isomorphisms.

Proof.

Suppose
R
is a meet of
P

Given compatible isomorphisms

\psi

immediately gives that

and

with respect to

.
Now suppose that

is some other lower bound.
Then
\psi^{-1}\circ S\circ\varphi^{-1}
is a lower bound of

and
Q
, and so
\psi^{-1}\circ S\circ\varphi^{-1}\ll R
.
Proposition

then gives

∎

It remains to show the first part of this result, which involves a decomposition of the target measure

Let

(2)

(1) By Proposition

5.
Conclusion and future work

In this work we developed an enrichment of CD categories over commutative monoids, providing a minimal setting for reasoning about sums of morphisms, and used it to study the involutive MH framework of
(
Andrieu2020
)
.
This gave a purely algebraic derivation of their “balancing condition” in Theorem
1.1
, together with a converse result and an immediate extension to the skew-reversible setting.

An interesting direction for future work is to give general conditions under which Lebesgue decompositions exist in this setting, rather than only isolating the properties that such decompositions ought to satisfy.
This appears to require a suitable version of the Radon–Nikodym theorem, which we conjecture may be attainable by assuming that the preorder
\leq
induced by the enrichment is complete.
We also expect that making this precise will involve an explicit notion of
predicates
, suggesting an interesting connection with
effectus theory

(
cho2015introduction
)
, which we leave to future work.

B.1.

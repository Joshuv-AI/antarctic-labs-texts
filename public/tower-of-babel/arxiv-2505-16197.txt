Hypoellipticity of the Asymptotic Bismut Superconnection on Contact Manifolds
Jesus Sanchez Jr, Andres Franco Valiente

Abstract
Given a contact sub-Riemannian manifold, one obtains a non-integrable splitting of the tangent bundle into the directions along the contact distribution and the Reeb field. We generalize the construction of the Bismut superconnection to this non-integrable setting and show that although singularities appear within the superconnection, if one extracts the finite part then the resulting operator is hypoelliptic. We then discuss the explicit form of the operator on principal 𝕊 1 \mathbb{S}^{1} -bundles and discuss possible directions for generalizations and open questions; in particular, a possible relationship between our hypoelliptic operator and a constrained supersymmetric sigma model.



1
Introduction

On a Riemannian manifold
\left(M,g\right)
, the Dirac-type operators have proven to be the golden operators of study among the class of differential operators. They have demonstrated a wide range of applicability and have been at the center of connections relating analysis, topology, and geometry on
M
. For example, they play a fundamental role in the various proofs of the Atiyah-Singer index theorems, obstructions to positive scalar curvature, Connes noncommutative geometry program, and appear as essential terms within the nonlinear equations of gauge theory. Recently the subject of index theory and microlocal analysis has seen a huge expansion given by the work of van Erp-Yuncken
[
6
]
, van Erp
[
5
]
, and Mohsen
[
4
]
into the realm of hypoelliptic operators on filtered manifolds. The techniques rely heavily on noncommutative harmonic analysis and the tangent groupoid machinery developed by Connes and his contemporaries and is perhaps the most recent success story of noncommutative geometry. In a recent paper of Dave-Haller
[
2
]
, a plethora of natural hypoelliptic operators has been supplied to a given filtered manifold, thus opening up the range of applicability of these new hypoelliptic index theorems. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a filtration, if one equips a filtered manifold
\left(M,\mathcal{F}\right)
with a sub-Riemannian metric
g_{\mathcal{F}}
, a natural question arises: is there a canonical class of first order operators associated to
\left(M,\mathcal{F},g_{\mathcal{F}}\right)
which plays the role of the Dirac-type operators in sub-Riemannian geometry? This question has already been approached from the spectral triple perspective in the work of Hasselmann
[
3
]
, the verdict being that the naive choice of horizontal Dirac operator does record the sub-Riemannian distance but also has undesirable analytic properties such as the lack of hypoellipticity and having an infinite dimensional kernel even when the manifold is closed.

Our aim in this paper is to explore this question with the construction of a new differential operator
\mathbb{D}
associated to a sub-Riemannian manifold
\left(M,\mathcal{F},g_{\mathcal{F}}\right)
equipped with a transverse distribution
\mathcal{T}
which splits the tangent bundle
TM=\mathcal{F}\oplus\mathcal{T}
. The idea is inspired from Bismut’s construction of Levi-Civita superconnection in the proof of the local families index theorem
[
1
]
. We begin with an auxiliary transverse metric
g_{\mathcal{T}}
and obtain an ordinary Riemannian metric
g_{M}=g_{\mathcal{F}}\oplus g_{\mathcal{T}}
on M. Following Bismut, we apply an adiabatic scaling to
g_{\mathcal{T}}
and consider the one-parameter family of metrics
g_{M,u}=g_{\mathcal{F}}\oplus u^{-1}g_{\mathcal{T}}
. Using a projection, one then obtains a pair of one-parameter families of metric-compatible connections
\nabla^{\mathcal{F},u}
and
\nabla^{\mathcal{T},u}
and equips the covariant Euclidean vector bundle
\left(\mathcal{F},g_{\mathcal{F}},\nabla^{\mathcal{F},u}\right)
with a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded Clifford module

C\ell^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\left(\mathcal{F}^{*},g^{*}_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\circlearrowright\left(E^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ },h_{E},\nabla^{E,u}\right)

which is tensored with the Clifford module

C\ell^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\left(\mathcal{T}^{*},ug^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}\right)\circlearrowright\left(\wedge^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathcal{T}^{*},\wedge ug^{*}_{\mathcal{T}},\nabla^{\mathcal{T},u}\right)

to obtain the Clifford module

C\ell\left(T^{*}M,g^{*}_{M,u}\right)\circlearrowright_{m_{u}}\left(E^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\hat{\otimes}\wedge^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathcal{T}^{*},h_{E}\otimes\wedge ug^{*}_{\mathcal{T}},\nabla^{E,u}\otimes I+I\otimes\nabla^{\mathcal{T},u}\right).

Bismut then introduces a modified connection
\nabla^{E\otimes\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*},u}
which is compatible with the Levi-Civita connection of
g_{M,u}
and proceeds to investigate the behavior of the one-parameter family of Dirac operators

D_{u}:=m_{u}\circ\nabla^{E\otimes\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*},u}

in the limit as
u\rightarrow 0^{+}
. When the distribution
\mathcal{F}
is the vertical distribution associated to a locally trivial fibration
\pi:M\rightarrow B
, then one has that the limit as
u\rightarrow 0^{+}
exists

\mathbb{A}:=\lim_{u\rightarrow 0^{+}}D_{u}

and is the Bismut superconnection
\mathbb{A}
, up to a zeroth order term. However, when
\mathcal{F}
is a
non-integrable
distribution, singularities will appear in the limit as
u\rightarrow 0^{+}
thus initially warranting one to dismiss the non-integrable setting. In this paper, we show that if one extracts the finite part of the limit of
D_{u}
as
u\rightarrow 0^{+}
, then there are situations in which the operator

\mathbb{D}:=\lim^{F.P.}_{u\rightarrow 0^{+}}D_{u}

is a
hypoelliptic operator
. The simplest example of a non-trivial non-integrable filtered manifold is a contact manifold
\left(M,\theta\right)
in which case the filtration is given by the kernel of the contact 1-form
\mathcal{F}=\operatorname{Ker}\theta
and in this case we show that for
any choice of sub-Riemannian metric
, there are natural Clifford modules which admit hypoelliptic
asymptotic Bismut superconnections
. We have the following theorem, the first is for the Clifford module of
\mathcal{F}
-spinors

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
\left(M,\theta,g_{\mathcal{F}}\right)
is a contact sub-Riemannian manifold such that
\left(\mathcal{F},g_{\mathcal{F}}\right)
is orientable and admits a spin structure. Then for any choice of complex hermitian spinor bundle
\left(\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}},h_{\mathscr{S}}\right)
, the operator

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}

\displaystyle:=\lim_{u\rightarrow 0^{+}}^{F.P.}D^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}_{u}

is hypoelliptic. If
M
is closed, then
\textnormal{Ker}\mathbb{D}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}
is a finite dimensional space of smooth sections.

and the second is for the Clifford module of
\mathcal{F}
-differential forms

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
\left(M,\theta,g_{\mathcal{F}}\right)
is a contact sub-Riemannian manifold. Then the operator

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}

\displaystyle:=\lim_{u\rightarrow 0^{+}}^{F.P.}D^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}_{u}

is hypoelliptic. If
M
is closed, then
\textnormal{Ker}\mathbb{D}^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}
is a finite dimensional space of smooth sections.

If one is given a geometric structure on a closed manifold
M
and can naturally associate a hypoelliptic differential operator then one can hope that the dimension of the kernel of this operator provides an invariant for this geometric structure. We show that there are simple examples where the dimension of the kernel of our operator is non-trivial as well as the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-dimension.

This article is organized as follows: We first give the construction of the asymptotic Bismut superconnection for a contact manifold and give two exemplary situations where one can consider this construction i.e. the setting of
\mathcal{F}
-spinors and
\mathcal{F}
-differential forms. In the next section, we demonstrate the hypoellipticity of our operators using the methods of noncommutative harmonic analysis as developed in
[
6
]
and
[
2
]
. We then give a few indications that the construction is sensitive to the choice of Clifford compatible connections
\nabla^{E,u}
as well as compute the kernel in the case of principal bundles over
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. We end with a discussion of what the general situation for filtered manifolds may look like as well as further discussion on open problems with regards to invariance properties of
\textnormal{Ker}\mathbb{D}
, extension of the work to the equivariant setting and a potential setting where this new operator may be employed in the path integral formalism.

2
Construction of the Asymptotic Bismut Superconnection

Let
\left(M^{2n+1},\theta\right)
be a contact manifold and
\mathcal{F}=\operatorname{Ker}\theta\leqslant TM
the corresponding contact distribution equipped with sub-Riemannian metric
g_{\mathcal{F}}
. The contact 1-form comes with a unique vector field
T
satisfying

\iota_{T}\theta=1,\quad\iota_{T}d\theta=0,

which is called the
Reeb vector field
. Let
\mathcal{T}
be the smooth subbundle of
TM
spanned by
T
and
g_{\mathcal{T}}
be the Euclidean fiber metric on
\mathcal{T}
which gives the Reeb field length
1
.
For
u>0
we can extend the metric
g_{\mathcal{F}}
to a Riemannian metric
g_{M,u}
by giving the Reeb field
T
length
u^{-1/2}
and making
\mathcal{F}\perp\mathcal{T}
. The metric on
M
then takes the form

\displaystyle g_{M,u}\left(X,Y\right)

\displaystyle=g_{\mathcal{F}}\left(X,Y\right),\quad X,Y\in\Gamma\left(\mathcal{F}\right),

\displaystyle g_{M,u}\left(X,T\right)

\displaystyle=0,\quad X\in\Gamma\left(\mathcal{F}\right),

\displaystyle g_{M,u}\left(T,T\right)

\displaystyle=u^{-1},

which in terms of
g_{\mathcal{F}}
and
g_{\mathcal{T}}
can be expressed as

g_{M,u}=g_{\mathcal{F}}\oplus u^{-1}g_{\mathcal{T}},

and we denote the
u=1
metric by
g_{M}
. The Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{g_{u}}
projects to metric compatible connections on
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{T}
respectively which we denote by
\nabla^{\mathcal{F},u}
and
\nabla^{\mathcal{T},u}
.
Suppose
\left(E^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ },h^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }_{E}\right)\rightarrow M
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded
\mathbb{C}\ell\left(\mathcal{F}^{*},g^{*}_{\mathcal{F}}\right)
-bundle and that
\nabla^{E^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ },u}
is a one-parameter family of hermitian connections for
\left(E^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ },h^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }_{E}\right)\rightarrow M
compatible with the connection
\nabla^{\mathcal{F},u}
for each
u>0
. If we give
\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}
the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading given by even/odd forms and the natural induced connection
\nabla^{\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*},u}
from
\nabla^{\mathcal{T},u}
, then we can take the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-tensor product with
\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}
and obtain a new Clifford module

\mathbb{C}\ell\left(\mathcal{F}^{*},g^{*}_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\hat{\otimes}\mathbb{C}\ell\left(\mathcal{T}^{*},ug^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}\right)\circlearrowright\left(E^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}\right).

Observe that under the natural isomorphism

\mathbb{C}\ell\left(\mathcal{F}^{*},g^{*}_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\hat{\otimes}\mathbb{C}\ell\left(\mathcal{T}^{*},ug^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}\right)\simeq\mathbb{C}\ell\left(T^{*}M,g^{*}_{M,u}\right),

we obtain a Clifford module for the cotangent bundle of
\left(M,g_{M,u}\right)

\mathbb{C}\ell\left(T^{*}M,g^{*}_{M,u}\right)\circlearrowright\left(E^{\bullet}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}\right).

We denote the Clifford action of differential forms on
M
by
m_{u}\left(\tau\right)\in\textnormal{End}\left(E\otimes\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}\right)
, for
\tau\in\Gamma\left(\wedge T^{*}M\right)
. Note that the tensor product connection
\nabla^{E^{\bullet},u}\otimes I+I\otimes\nabla^{\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*},u}
on the Clifford bundle
E^{\bullet}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}
is compatible with the direct sum connection
\nabla^{\mathcal{F},u}\oplus\nabla^{\mathcal{T},u}
which unfortunately may not be the Levi-Civita connection of
g_{M,u}
. Bismut provides a remedy to this situation in
[
1
]
by considering the shape
1
-form

\nabla^{g_{M,u}}=\nabla^{\mathcal{F},u}\oplus\nabla^{\mathcal{T},u}+S_{u},\quad S_{u}\in\Omega^{1}\left(M,\mathfrak{so}\left(TM,g_{M,u}\right)\right),

and letting
\omega_{u}\in\Omega^{1}\left(M,\wedge^{2}T^{*}M\right)
be given as

\iota_{X}\omega_{u}\left(Y,Z\right)=g_{M,u}\left(\iota_{X}S_{u}Y,Z\right),\quad X,Y,Z\in\Gamma\left(TM\right).

Then observe that the modified connection

\nabla^{\mathbb{E}^{\bullet},u}:=\nabla^{E,u}\hat{\otimes}I+I\hat{\otimes}\nabla^{\mathcal{T}^{*},u}+\frac{1}{2}m_{u}\omega_{u},

is compatible with the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{g_{M,u}}
. Using these constructions, we form the corresponding Dirac operator

D_{u}:=m_{u}\circ\nabla^{\mathbb{E}^{\bullet},u}:\Gamma\left(E^{\bullet}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}\right)\rightarrow\Gamma\left(E^{\bullet}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}\right).

Our main interest is in the asymptotic behavior of
D_{u}
in the limit as
u\rightarrow 0^{+}
. In particular, we are interested in the finite part of the limiting operator
D_{u}
as
u\rightarrow 0^{+}

\mathbb{D}:=\lim_{u\rightarrow 0^{+}}^{F.P.}D_{u}.

Before doing so, we make a few observations concerning the connections
\nabla^{\mathcal{F},u}
and
\nabla^{\mathcal{T},u}
as well as the dual shape
1
-form
\omega_{u}
. The first is that the connections
\nabla^{\mathcal{T},u}
are constant in
u
and in fact are the canonical flat connection for the real line bundle
\mathcal{T}
. To see this, just recall that under the Koszul identity one has

\displaystyle 2g_{M,u}\left(\nabla^{g_{M,u}}_{X}Y,Z\right)

\displaystyle=Xg_{M,u}\left(Y,Z\right)-Zg_{M,u}\left(X,Y\right)+Yg_{M,u}\left(Z,X\right),

\displaystyle\quad-g_{M,u}\left(Y,[X,Z]\right)+g_{M,u}\left(X,[Z,Y]\right)-g_{M,u}\left(Z,[Y,X]\right).

which when we plug in
Y=Z=T
we obtain

2g_{M,u}\left(\nabla^{g_{M,u}}_{X}T,T\right)=0.

This implies that
T
is globally
\nabla^{\mathcal{T},u}
-parallel for all
u>0
, demonstrating that
\nabla^{\mathcal{T},u}=d
when we trivialize
\mathcal{T}\simeq\underline{\mathbb{R}}_{M}
using
T
. The connections
\nabla^{\mathcal{F},u}
are
not
constant in
u
but their variation is easy to keep track of. First, note that if
X,Y,Z\in\Gamma\left(\mathcal{F}\right)
, then the Koszul identity implies that the quantity

g_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\nabla^{\mathcal{F},u}_{X}Y,Z\right)=g_{M,u}\left(\nabla^{g_{M,u}}_{X}Y,Z\right),

does not vary in
u
. Now suppose that
Y,Z\in\Gamma\left(\mathcal{F}\right)
and observe that the Koszul identity gives

\displaystyle 2g_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\nabla^{\mathcal{F},u}_{T}Y,Z\right)=2g_{M,u}\left(\nabla^{g_{M,u}}_{T}Y,Z\right)

\displaystyle=Tg_{\mathcal{F}}\left(Y,Z\right)-g_{M,u}\left(Y,[T,Z]\right)+g_{M,u}\left(T,[Z,Y]\right)-g_{M,u}\left(Z,[Y,T]\right)

\displaystyle=Tg_{\mathcal{F}}\left(Y,Z\right)-g_{\mathcal{F}}\left(Y,[T,Z]^{\mathcal{F}}\right)+g_{M,u}\left(T,[Z,Y]\right)-g_{\mathcal{F}}\left(Z,[Y,T]^{\mathcal{F}}\right).

Observe that the only term that has variation in
u
is the term

g_{M,u}\left(T,[Z,Y]\right)=u^{-1}g_{M}\left(T,[Z,Y]\right).

Because of the non-integrability of
\mathcal{F}
, this term will be non-zero in general and thus the one-parameter family of connections
\nabla^{\mathcal{F},u}
will not converge as
u\rightarrow 0^{+}
. Furthermore observe that by definition of
g_{M}
one has that

g_{M}\left(T,[Z,Y]\right)=-\theta\left([Y,Z]\right)=d\theta\left(Y,Z\right).

Observe that we may restrict
d\theta
to
\mathcal{F}
and obtain a symplectic form
\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\in\Gamma\left(\wedge^{2}\mathcal{F}^{*}\right)
which in turn induces a skew-symmetric operator
\mathfrak{so}\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\in\Gamma\left(\mathfrak{so}\left(\mathcal{F},g_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\right)
which we use to construct the
1
-form
\theta\otimes\mathfrak{so}\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\in\Omega^{1}\left(M,\mathfrak{so}\left(\mathcal{F},g_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\right)
. This
1
-form with values in skew-symmetric operators is constructed so that if we define the
g_{\mathcal{F}}
-compatible connection

\nabla^{\mathcal{F},0}:=\nabla^{\mathcal{F},u}-\frac{u^{-1}}{2}\theta\otimes\mathfrak{so}\Omega_{\mathcal{F}},

then
\nabla^{\mathcal{F},0}

does not vary with
u
.

To analyze the shape form
\omega_{u}
, the Koszul identity will again play the key role. When
X,Y,Z\in\Gamma\left(\mathcal{F}\right)
we have

\iota_{X}\omega_{u}\left(Y,Z\right)=g_{M,u}\left(\left(\nabla^{g_{M,u}}_{X}-\nabla^{\mathcal{F},u}_{X}\right)Y,Z\right)=0,

whereas if
X=T
and
Y,Z\in\Gamma\left(\mathcal{F}\right)
we have

\iota_{T}\omega_{u}\left(Y,Z\right)=g_{M,u}\left(\left(\nabla^{g_{M,u}}_{T}-\nabla^{\mathcal{F},u}_{T}\right)Y,Z\right)=0,

and lastly for
X,Y\in\Gamma\left(\mathcal{F}\right)
and
Z=T
, we have

\displaystyle\iota_{X}\omega_{u}\left(Y,T\right)

\displaystyle=g_{M,u}\left(\left(\nabla^{g_{M,u}}_{X}-\nabla^{\mathcal{F},u}_{X}\right)Y,T\right)

\displaystyle=g_{M,u}\left(\nabla^{g_{M,u}}_{X}Y,T\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}Tg_{M,u}\left(X,Y\right)-\frac{1}{2}g_{M,u}\left(Y,[X,T]\right)+\frac{1}{2}g_{M,u}\left(X,[T,Y]\right)-\frac{1}{2}g_{M,u}\left(T,[Y,X]\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}Tg_{\mathcal{F}}\left(X,Y\right)-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mathcal{F}}\left(Y,[X,T]^{\mathcal{F}}\right)+\frac{1}{2}g_{\mathcal{F}}\left(X,[T,Y]^{\mathcal{F}}\right)-\frac{u^{-1}}{2}g_{M}\left(T,[Y,X]\right).

The last term is again related to
d\theta

-\frac{1}{2}g_{M}\left(T,[Y,X]\right)=-\frac{1}{2}d\theta\left(X,Y\right).

If we view
d\theta
as a
1
-form with values in
T^{*}M
, then we can wedge on the right with
\theta
and obtain

d\theta\wedge_{R}\theta\in\Omega^{1}\left(M,\wedge^{2}T^{*}M\right).

If we extend
g_{\mathcal{F}}
to a symmetric rank
2
-covariant tensor field on
M
by declaring it vanishes on
T
, then for
X,Y\in\Gamma\left(TM\right)
, the Lie derivative acts as

L_{T}g_{\mathcal{F}}\left(X,Y\right)=Tg_{\mathcal{F}}\left(X^{\mathcal{F}},Y^{\mathcal{F}}\right)-g_{\mathcal{F}}\left(X^{\mathcal{F}},[T,Y]^{\mathcal{F}}\right)-g_{\mathcal{F}}\left([T,X]^{\mathcal{F}},Y^{\mathcal{F}}\right),

where
X^{\mathcal{F}}
and
Y^{\mathcal{F}}
denote their respective projections onto
\mathcal{F}
. We can also view
L_{T}g_{\mathcal{F}}
as a
1
-form on
M
with values in
T^{*}M
and wedge on the right with
\theta
obtaining

L_{T}g_{\mathcal{F}}\wedge_{R}\theta\in\Omega^{1}\left(M,\wedge^{2}T^{*}M\right).

The above calculations demonstrate that we have

\omega_{u}=-\frac{1}{2}L_{T}g_{\mathcal{F}}\wedge_{R}\theta-\frac{u^{-1}}{2}d\theta\wedge_{R}\theta.

This is as far as we can go before specifying the one-parameter covariant Clifford modules

\mathbb{C}\ell\left(\mathcal{F}^{*},g^{*}_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\circlearrowright\left(E,h_{E},\nabla^{E,u}\right).

2.1
The Case of Spinors

Suppose the Riemannian vector bundle
\left(\mathcal{F},g_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\rightarrow M
is orientable and admits a spin structure. In this case let
\left(\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}},h_{\mathscr{S}}\right)\rightarrow M
be the bundle of complex hermitian
\mathcal{F}
-spinors corresponding to the choice of spin structure. The bundle of complex spinors comes equipped with an irreducible action of the
\mathcal{F}^{*}
-Clifford bundle, denoted by
c_{\mathcal{F}}
, and is naturally
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded by the volume form of
\mathcal{F}

\mathbb{C}\ell\left(\mathcal{F}^{*},g^{*}_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\circlearrowright_{c_{\mathcal{F}}}\left(\mathscr{S}^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }_{\mathcal{F}},h^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }_{\mathscr{S}}\right).

In this case we equip
\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}
with the spin connection corresponding to the one-parameter family
metric compatible connections
\nabla^{\mathcal{F},u}
, denoted
\nabla^{\mathscr{S},u}
. Note that because

\nabla^{\mathcal{F},u}=\nabla^{\mathcal{F},0}+\frac{u^{-1}}{2}\theta\otimes\mathfrak{so}\Omega_{\mathcal{F}},

if
\nabla^{\mathscr{S},0}
denotes the spin connection induced by the metric compatible connection
\nabla^{\mathcal{F},0}
, then

\nabla^{\mathscr{S},u}=\nabla^{\mathscr{S},0}+\frac{u^{-1}}{4}\theta\otimes c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right).

In this case, the operator of interest is

D^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}_{u}:=m_{u}\circ\left(\nabla^{\mathscr{S},u}\hat{\otimes}I+I\hat{\otimes}d\right)+\frac{1}{2}m_{u}\circ m_{u}\omega_{u}.

Given the decomposition of
\nabla^{\mathscr{S},u}
above we can write

m_{u}\circ\left(\nabla^{\mathscr{S},u}\hat{\otimes}I+I\hat{\otimes}d\right)=m_{u}\circ\left(\nabla^{\mathscr{S},0}\hat{\otimes}I+I\hat{\otimes}d\right)+\frac{u^{-1}}{4}m_{u}\circ\theta\otimes c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\hat{\otimes}I.

The first Clifford contraction can be split into a sum of a Clifford contraction in the
\mathcal{F}
-direction along with a Clifford contraction in the
\mathcal{T}
-directions. We write this as

m_{u}\circ\left(\nabla^{\mathscr{S},0}\hat{\otimes}I+I\hat{\otimes}d\right)=m_{u}\big|_{\mathcal{F}}\circ\left(\nabla^{\mathscr{S},0}\hat{\otimes}I+I\hat{\otimes}d\right)+m_{u}\big|_{\mathcal{T}}\circ\left(\nabla^{\mathscr{S},0}\hat{\otimes}I+I\hat{\otimes}d\right)

and label the
\mathcal{F}
-Clifford contraction by

D^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}:=m_{u}\big|_{\mathcal{F}}\circ\left(\nabla^{\mathscr{S},0}\hat{\otimes}I+I\hat{\otimes}d\right).

Observe that the operator
D^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}
is an odd-
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded operator which is
constant in
u
due to the fact that
m_{u}\left(\alpha\right)=c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\alpha\right)\hat{\otimes}I
for any
\alpha\in\Gamma\left(\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}\right)
. The Clifford contracted component in the
\mathcal{T}
direction is

m_{u}\big|_{\mathcal{T}}\circ\left(\nabla^{\mathscr{S},0}\hat{\otimes}I+I\hat{\otimes}d\right)=I\hat{\otimes}\left(\varepsilon_{\theta}-u\iota_{T}\right)\left(\nabla^{\mathscr{S},0}_{T}\hat{\otimes}I+I\hat{\otimes}\partial_{T}\right).

The Clifford contraction of
\theta\otimes c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\hat{\otimes I}
is concentrated in the
\mathcal{T}
-directions and is given by

\displaystyle m_{u}\circ\theta\otimes c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\hat{\otimes}I

\displaystyle=I\hat{\otimes}\left(\varepsilon_{\theta}-u\iota_{T}\right)c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\hat{\otimes}I

\displaystyle=c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\hat{\otimes}\left(\varepsilon_{\theta}-u\iota_{T}\right).

Thus, the Clifford contracted covariant derivative takes the form

m_{u}\circ\left(\nabla^{\mathscr{S},u}\hat{\otimes}I+I\hat{\otimes}d\right)=D^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}+I\hat{\otimes}\left(\varepsilon_{\theta}-u\iota_{T}\right)\left(\nabla^{\mathscr{S},0}_{T}\hat{\otimes}I+I\hat{\otimes}\partial_{T}\right)+\frac{u^{-1}}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\hat{\otimes}\left(\varepsilon_{\theta}-u\iota_{T}\right).

Given the general form of the shape form
\omega_{u}
we see that the Clifford contraction of
\omega_{u}
is concentrated in the
\mathcal{F}
-directions. However a simplification within the contraction of
L_{T}g_{\mathcal{F}}
appears. Suppose that
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{2n}
is a local orthonormal framing of
\left(\mathcal{F},g_{\mathcal{F}}\right)
with dual framing
\{\varphi^{j}\}_{j=1}^{2n}
and note that

m_{u}\circ m_{u}L_{T}g_{\mathcal{F}}\wedge_{R}\theta=L_{T}g_{\mathcal{F}}\left(e_{i},e_{j}\right)c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi^{i}\right)\hat{\otimes}I\cdot c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi^{j}\right)\hat{\otimes}I\cdot I\hat{\otimes}\left(\varepsilon_{\theta}-u\iota_{T}\right).

However
L_{T}g_{\mathcal{F}}
is symmetric whereas
c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi^{i}\right)c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi^{j}\right)+c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi^{j}\right)c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi^{i}\right)=-2\delta^{ij}
, thus we have

\displaystyle m_{u}\circ m_{u}L_{T}g_{\mathcal{F}}\wedge_{R}\theta

\displaystyle=-\delta_{ij}L_{T}g_{\mathcal{F}}\left(e_{i},e_{j}\right)I\hat{\otimes}\left(\varepsilon_{\theta}-u\iota_{T}\right)

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{Tr}L_{T}g_{\mathcal{F}}I\hat{\otimes}\left(\varepsilon_{\theta}-u\iota_{T}\right).

The divergent term within
\omega_{u}
has Clifford contraction

m_{u}\circ m_{u}d\theta\wedge_{R}\theta=2c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\hat{\otimes}\left(\varepsilon_{\theta}-u\iota_{T}\right).

All together this gives

m_{u}\circ m_{u}\omega_{u}=-u^{-1}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\hat{\otimes}\left(\varepsilon_{\theta}-u\iota_{T}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr}L_{T}g_{\mathcal{F}}I\hat{\otimes}\left(\varepsilon_{\theta}-u\iota_{T}\right).

which gives a global form of the
u
-dependent Dirac operator

\displaystyle D^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}_{u}

\displaystyle=D^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}+I\hat{\otimes}\left(\varepsilon_{\theta}-u\iota_{T}\right)\left(\nabla^{\mathscr{S},0}_{T}\hat{\otimes}I+I\hat{\otimes}\partial_{T}\right)+\frac{u^{-1}}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\hat{\otimes}\left(\varepsilon_{\theta}-u\iota_{T}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt-\frac{u^{-1}}{2}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\hat{\otimes}\left(\varepsilon_{\theta}-u\iota_{T}\right)+\frac{1}{4}\operatorname{Tr}L_{T}g_{\mathcal{F}}I\hat{\otimes}\left(\varepsilon_{\theta}-u\iota_{T}\right).

As explained earlier, observe that this Dirac operator has clear divergences as
u\rightarrow 0^{+}
, however as shown above, they are mild and can be grouped together in a single term of order
u^{-1}
. If we denote

\nabla^{0}_{T}:=\nabla^{\mathscr{S},0}_{T}\hat{\otimes}I+I\hat{\otimes}\partial_{T},

then collecting the constant terms in
u
we find that

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}

\displaystyle:=\lim_{u\rightarrow 0^{+}}^{F.P.}D^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}_{u}

\displaystyle=D^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}+I\hat{\otimes}\varepsilon_{\theta}\nabla^{0}_{T}+\frac{1}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\hat{\otimes}\iota_{T}+\frac{1}{4}\operatorname{Tr}L_{T}g_{\mathcal{F}}I\hat{\otimes}\varepsilon_{\theta}

The operator
\mathbb{D}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}
is odd with respect to the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading on
\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}
.

2.2
The Case of Differential Forms

Consider the Clifford bi-module of
\mathcal{F}
-forms

C\ell\left(\mathcal{F}^{*},g^{*}_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\circlearrowright_{c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}}\left(\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*},g_{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}\right)_{c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}}\circlearrowleft C\ell\left(\mathcal{F}^{*},g^{*}_{\mathcal{F}}\right).

Given the decomposition

\nabla^{\mathcal{F},u}=\nabla^{\mathcal{F},0}+\frac{u^{-1}}{2}\theta\otimes\mathfrak{so}\Omega_{\mathcal{F}},

the natural induced connection on
\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}
is given by

\wedge\nabla^{\mathcal{F},u}=\wedge\nabla^{\mathcal{F},0}+\frac{u^{-1}}{4}\theta\otimes\text{ad}\Omega_{\mathcal{F}},

where

\text{ad}\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}=c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)-c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right).

What we will do is remove the right Clifford multiplication and opt for using the one-parameter family of Clifford connections

\nabla^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*},u}:=\wedge\nabla^{\mathcal{F},0}+\frac{u^{-1}}{4}\theta\otimes c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right).

We will discuss the reason for this choice in the section on hypoellipticity. The operator of interest then is

D^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}_{u}:=m_{u}\circ\left(\nabla^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}\hat{\otimes}I+I\hat{\otimes}d\right)+\frac{1}{2}m_{u}\circ m_{u}\omega_{u}.

If we define

\displaystyle D^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}

\displaystyle:=m_{1}\big|_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\wedge\nabla^{\mathcal{F},0}\hat{\otimes}I+I\hat{\otimes}d\right),

\displaystyle\nabla^{0}_{T}

\displaystyle:=\wedge\nabla^{\mathcal{F},0}_{T}\hat{\otimes}I+I\hat{\otimes}\partial_{T},

then the same analysis as in the case of
\mathcal{F}
-spinors gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}

\displaystyle:=\lim_{u\rightarrow 0^{+}}^{F.P.}D^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}_{u}

\displaystyle=D^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}+I\hat{\otimes}\varepsilon_{\theta}\nabla^{0}_{T}+\frac{1}{4}c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\hat{\otimes}\iota_{T}+\frac{1}{4}\operatorname{Tr}L_{T}g_{\mathcal{F}}I\hat{\otimes}\varepsilon_{\theta},

which is an odd operator if
\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}
is equipped with the standard even/odd form grading.

3
Hypoellipticity in the Heisenberg Calculus

On a contact manifold
\left(M^{2n+1},\theta\right)
there is a microlocal calculus, called the
Heisenberg Calculus
which one can use to investigate problems of hypoellipticity when dealing with non-elliptic operators on
\left(M^{2n+1},\theta\right)
such as a sub-Laplacian. For differential operators acting on the sections of a complex vector bundle
E\rightarrow M
, the description of the calculus is fairly straightforward and we will follow the description given in
[
2
]
. To describe the calculus, first note that at
x\in M
there is a natural skew-symmetric bilinear form

\displaystyle[\cdot,\cdot]_{x}:\mathcal{F}_{x}\times\mathcal{F}_{x}\rightarrow T_{x}M/\mathcal{F}_{x}

\displaystyle\left(X_{x},Y_{x}\right)\rightarrow[\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}]_{x}+\mathcal{F}_{x}

where
\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}
denote local sections of
\mathcal{F}
which extend
X_{x}
and
Y_{x}
. If we extend the bracket to
\mathfrak{t}_{\mathcal{F}}M_{x}:=\mathcal{F}_{x}\oplus T_{x}M/\mathcal{F}_{x}
by demanding that elements of
T_{x}M/\mathcal{F}_{x}
are central then one checks that this bracket satisfies the Jacobi identity. By defining the grading degree of
\mathcal{F}_{x}
to be
1
and that of
T_{x}M/\mathcal{F}_{x}
to be
2
, one obtains a
graded two-step nilpotent Lie algebra
. We denote the
osculating group at
x
by
T_{\mathcal{F}}M_{x}:=\exp\mathfrak{t}_{\mathcal{F}}M_{x}
which is defined to be the simply connected exponential of
\mathfrak{t}_{\mathcal{F}}M_{x}
. In this case

\mathfrak{t}_{\mathcal{F}}M:=\bigsqcup_{x\in M}\mathfrak{t}_{\mathcal{F}}M_{x},\quad T_{\mathcal{F}}M:=\bigsqcup_{x\in M}T_{\mathcal{F}}M_{x},

become bundles of Lie algebras and Lie groups, respectively.

Fix a point
x\in M
and recall that Darboux’s theorem guarantees the existence of a neighborhood
U
of
x
and a
contact framing
of
\mathcal{F}\big|_{U}
of the form
\{Q_{i},P_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
which satisfies

[Q_{i},Q_{j}]=[P_{i},P_{j}]=0,\quad[Q_{i},P_{j}]=\delta_{ij}T.

This directly implies that the osculating groups are all the Heisenberg group
T_{\mathcal{F}}M_{x}\simeq\mathcal{H}
which is the smooth manifold
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
with group structure

\left(\vec{z},t\right)\cdot\left(\vec{w},s\right):=\left(\vec{z}+\vec{w},t+s+\frac{1}{2}\text{Im}\overline{\vec{z}}\cdot\vec{w}\right).

As a basis for the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
\mathcal{H}
one can take the left-invariant vector fields

Q_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}-\frac{y_{i}}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\quad P_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}+\frac{x_{j}}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\quad T=\frac{\partial}{\partial t},

which satisfy the commutation relations

[Q_{i},Q_{j}]=[P_{i},P_{j}]=0,\quad[Q_{i},P_{j}]=\delta_{ij}T.

If we assume that
U
is chosen so that
E\rightarrow M
trivializes along
U
,
E\big|_{U}\simeq\underline{\mathbb{C}^{r}}_{U}
, then any differential operator
L\in\text{Diff}\left(M,E\right)
will take the form

L=\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|+\gamma\leq l}a_{\alpha\beta\gamma}Q_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}P_{j}^{\beta_{j}}T^{\gamma},\quad a_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\in C^{\infty}\left(U,M_{r}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)\right).

Given that the order of vector fields tangent to
\mathcal{F}
are
1
and the order of vector fields tangent to
\mathcal{T}\simeq TM/\mathcal{F}
are
2
, in this case the
Heisenberg co-symbol
of
L
at
x
is defined by taking the highest order components i.e. the components for which
|\alpha|+|\beta|+2\gamma
is maximal within the local form of
L
, and one evaluates the matrix components at
x
. The cosymbol then is

\sigma^{co}\left(L\right)_{x}:=\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|+2\gamma=max}a_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\left(x\right)Q_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}P^{\beta_{j}}_{j}T^{\gamma}.

One checks that for different choices of local contact framing at
x
and local trivializations of
E
at
x
the Heisenberg cosymbol transforms like a section of the bundle
\mathcal{U}\left(\mathfrak{t}_{\mathcal{F}}M\right)\otimes\textnormal{End}E
, where
\mathcal{U}\left(\mathfrak{t}_{\mathcal{F}}M\right)
denotes the bundle of universal enveloping algebras of the osculating Lie algebras for the non-integrable distribution
\mathcal{F}
. For
L\in\text{Diff}\left(M,E\right)
we let
|L|^{H.O.}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
be the minimal integer for which in any local contact framing and local trivialization of
E
, the Heisenberg order of all terms appearing within the local expansion of
L
are bounded by
|L|^{H.O.}
. By definition, if
E
is given a connection
\nabla
then we have

\displaystyle X\in\Gamma\left(\mathcal{F}\right)\Rightarrow|\nabla_{X}|^{H.O.}=1,\quad\sigma^{co}\left(\nabla_{X}\right)_{x}=X_{x}\otimes I_{E_{x}},

\displaystyle Z\in\Gamma\left(\mathcal{T}\right)\Rightarrow|\nabla_{Z}|^{H.O.}=1,\quad\sigma^{co}\left(\nabla_{Z}\right)_{x}=Z_{x}\otimes I_{E_{x}}.

The main use of this new notion of differential order is the following theorem relating the representation theory of the osculating Lie groups
T_{\mathcal{F}}M
to the hypoellipticity of differential operators. To set up notation, if
x\in M
we let
\Pi_{x}:T_{\mathcal{F}}M_{x}\rightarrow U\left(H\right)
denote a unitary representation and
\pi_{x}:\mathfrak{t}_{\mathcal{F}}M_{x}\rightarrow\textnormal{End}H^{\infty}
the induced Lie algebra representation on the smooth vectors
H^{\infty}
. If
V
is a fixed complex vector space then we can inflate the Lie algebra representation

\pi^{V}_{x}:\mathfrak{t}_{\mathcal{F}}M_{x}\otimes\mathfrak{gl}\left(V\right)\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}\left(H^{\infty}\otimes V\right)

by defining

\pi^{V}_{x}\left(X\otimes T\right):=\pi_{x}\left(X\right)\otimes I_{V}+I_{H^{\infty}}\otimes T.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
2
]
,
[
6
]
)
.

Suppose that
\left(M^{2n+1},\theta\right)
is a contact manifold and
E\rightarrow M
is a complex vector bundle with differential operator
L
. Then
L
is hypoelliptic if for every
x\in M
and every irreducible unitary representation
\Pi_{x}:T_{\mathcal{F}}M_{x}\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(H)
, the induced operator on smooth vectors of the inflated representation
\pi^{E_{x}}_{x}:\mathfrak{t}_{\mathcal{F}}M_{x}\otimes\mathfrak{gl}\left(E_{x}\right)\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}\left(H^{\infty}\otimes E_{x}\right)

\pi^{E_{x}}_{x}\left(\sigma^{co}\left(L\right)_{x}\right):H^{\infty}\otimes E_{x}\rightarrow H^{\infty}\otimes E_{x}

is injective. If
M
is closed, then
\textnormal{Ker}L
is a finite dimensional space of smooth sections.

One says that a differential operator
L\in\text{Diff}\left(M,E\right)
is
Rockland
if it satisfies the above criteria for hypoellipticity. For a general filtered manifold the above theorem reduces the determination of hypoellipticity to the study of the action of the cosymbol of the operator on the irreducible unitary representations of the simply connected nilpotent Lie group
T_{\mathcal{F}}M_{x}
, for each
x\in M
. It is here that the simplicity of the contact assumption enters: on a contact manifold the osculating groups are all the Heisenberg group
T_{\mathcal{F}}M_{x}\simeq\mathcal{H}
. The irreducible representations of
\mathcal{H}
fall into two types: a
2n
-parameter family of one-dimensional complex representations and a one-parameter family of infinite dimensional representations. For
\vec{\xi}=\left(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{2n}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}\setminus{\vec{0}}
, the one-dimensional representations are given at the Lie algebraic level as

\pi_{\vec{\xi}}\left(Q_{i}\right)=2\pi i\xi_{i},\quad\pi_{\vec{\xi}}\left(P_{j}\right)=2\pi i\xi_{n+j},\quad\pi_{\vec{\xi}}\left(T\right)=0.

The infinite dimensional representations are parametrized by
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\setminus{0}
and are the standard Schrödinger representations of
\mathcal{H}
acting on
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{q}\right)
. The smooth vectors of these representations are the Schwartz class functions and the representation at the Lie algebraic level is given by

\pi_{\lambda}\left(Q_{i}\right)=\frac{\lambda}{i}q_{i},\quad\pi_{\lambda}\left(P_{i}\right)=\frac{\partial}{\partial q_{i}},\quad\pi_{\lambda}\left(T\right)=i\lambda.

A quick look at our operator both in the spinor or differential form context demonstrates that it will
not
be a Rockland operator. The operator
D^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}
will only differentiate along the
\mathcal{F}
-directions and thus locally will have Heisenberg order
1
, where as the terms with no derivatives will be order zero. Thus the highest order component of
\mathbb{D}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}
will be the component involving differentiation along the Reeb directions
T
and its cosymbol at
x\in M
is the operator

\sigma^{co}\left(\mathbb{D}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\right)_{x}=1\hat{\otimes}\varepsilon_{\theta_{x}}T_{x}\otimes I_{E_{x}}.

However the element
T_{x}\in\mathfrak{t}_{\mathcal{F}}M_{x}
is zero within any of the one-dimensional representations of
\mathcal{H}
. Our method for determining the hypoellipticity of
\mathbb{D}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}
(resp.
\mathbb{D}^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}
) will be to look at its square

\bm{\Delta}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}:=\left(\mathbb{D}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\right)^{2}.

We have the following theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that
\left(M,\theta,g_{\mathcal{F}}\right)
is a contact sub-Riemannian manifold such that
\left(\mathcal{F},g_{\mathcal{F}}\right)
is orientable and admits a spin structure. Then for any choice of complex hermitian spinor bundle
\left(\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}},h_{\mathscr{S}}\right)
, the operator

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}

\displaystyle:=\lim_{u\rightarrow 0^{+}}^{F.P.}D^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}_{u}

\displaystyle=D^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}+I\hat{\otimes}\varepsilon_{\theta}\nabla^{0}_{T}+\frac{1}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\hat{\otimes}\iota_{T}+\frac{1}{4}\operatorname{Tr}L_{T}g_{\mathcal{F}}I\hat{\otimes}\varepsilon_{\theta}

is hypoelliptic. If
M
is closed, then
\textnormal{Ker}\mathbb{D}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}
is a finite dimensional space of smooth sections.

Proof.

We first make the observation that a differential operator
A
is hypoelliptic if and only if
A^{2}
is hypoelliptic. Turning to our operator
\mathbb{D}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}
we study its square
\bm{\Delta}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}
. Observe that by our global formula for
\mathbb{D}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}
we have

\displaystyle\bm{\Delta}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}=\left(D^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\right)^{2}+

\displaystyle\quad[D^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}},I\hat{\otimes}\varepsilon_{\theta}\nabla^{0}_{T}+\frac{1}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\hat{\otimes}\iota_{T}+\frac{1}{4}\operatorname{Tr}L_{T}g_{\mathcal{F}}I\hat{\otimes}\varepsilon_{\theta}]+[I\hat{\otimes}\varepsilon_{\theta}\nabla^{0}_{T},\frac{1}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\iota_{T}]+\frac{1}{16}\operatorname{Tr}L_{T}g_{\mathcal{F}}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)

where the commutators above are supercommutators of odd operators.
Observe that the distribution
\mathcal{F}
is left invariant under the flow of
T
which implies that for any
X\in\Gamma\left(\mathcal{F}\right)
one has
[T,X]\in\Gamma\left(\mathcal{F}\right)
. The presence of
\nabla^{0}_{T}
in the second commutator tells us that the co-symbol of
\bm{\Delta}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}
at a given
x\in M
will be at least of order
2
. Moreover, because
\theta\big|_{\mathcal{T}}\in\Gamma\left(\mathcal{T}^{*}\right)
is parallel, we have

D^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}I\hat{\otimes}\varepsilon_{\theta}=-I\hat{\otimes}\varepsilon_{\theta}D^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\Rightarrow[D^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}},I\hat{\otimes}\varepsilon_{\theta}\nabla^{0}_{T}]=-I\hat{\otimes}\varepsilon_{\theta}[D^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}},\nabla^{0}_{T}].

Thus all terms within the commutator involving
D^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}
have Heisenberg order
\leq 1
and we can discard the commutator involving
D^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}
and the zeroth order term
\operatorname{Tr}L_{T}g_{\mathcal{F}}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)
. To analyze the
\left(D^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\right)^{2}
term first let
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{2n}
be a local orthonormal framing of
\left(\mathcal{F},g_{\mathcal{F}}\right)
with dual framing
\{\varphi^{i}\}_{i=1}^{2n}
. Then locally we have

\displaystyle\left(D^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi^{i}\right)\hat{\otimes}I\nabla^{\mathscr{S},0}_{e_{i}}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi^{j}\right)\hat{\otimes}I\nabla^{\mathscr{S}_{0}}_{e_{j}}

\displaystyle=c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi^{i}\right)c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\nabla^{\mathcal{F}^{*},0}_{e_{i}}\varphi^{j}\right)\hat{\otimes}I\nabla^{\mathscr{S},0}_{e_{j}}+c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi^{i}\right)c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi^{j}\right)\hat{\otimes}I\nabla^{\mathscr{S},0}_{e_{i}}\nabla^{\mathscr{S},0}_{e_{j}}

The terms involving a single covariant derivative have Heisenberg order
1
and thus can be ignored. The terms involving second order derivatives can be re-expressed as

\displaystyle c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi^{i}\right)c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi^{j}\right)\hat{\otimes}I\nabla^{\mathscr{S},0}_{e_{i}}\nabla^{\mathscr{S},0}_{e_{j}}=

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\delta_{ij}\nabla^{\mathscr{S},0}_{e_{i}}\nabla^{\mathscr{S},0}_{e_{j}}+c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi^{i}\right)c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi^{j}\right)\hat{\otimes}I_{i<j}K_{\nabla^{\mathscr{S},0}}\left(e_{i},e_{j}\right)+c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi^{i}\right)c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi^{j}\right)\hat{\otimes}I_{i<j}\nabla^{\mathscr{S},0}_{[e_{i},e_{j}]}.

The curvature terms can be discarded as they are zero’th order. Now for a fixed
x\in M
let
\{u_{i},v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be an orthonormal basis of
\left(\mathcal{F}_{x},g_{\mathcal{F}_{x}}\right)
which diagonalizes
\Omega_{\mathcal{F}_{x}}

\Omega_{\mathcal{F}_{x}}\left(u_{i},v_{i}\right)=\delta_{ij}\alpha_{i},\quad\alpha_{i}>0.

Let
\{U_{i},V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be a local orthonormal framing of
\left(\mathcal{F},g_{\mathcal{F}}\right)
which extends the framing
\{u_{i},v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
. Then at
x
we have

[U_{i},U_{j}]_{x}=[V_{i},V_{j}]_{x}=0,\quad[U_{i},V_{j}]_{x}=-\delta_{ij}\alpha_{i}T_{x}+E_{x},

where
E_{x}\in\mathcal{F}_{x}
.
Thus, the co-symbol of
\left(D^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\right)^{2}
at
x
is

\displaystyle\sigma^{co}\left(\left(D^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\right)^{2}\right)_{x}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(u_{i}\otimes I_{\mathscr{S}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}_{x}}\right)^{2}+\left(v_{i}\otimes I_{\mathscr{S}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}_{x}}\right)^{2}-\alpha_{i}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{u_{i}}\right)c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{v_{j}}\right)\hat{\otimes}IT_{x}\otimes I_{\mathscr{S}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}_{x}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\left(u_{i}\otimes I_{\mathscr{S}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}_{x}}\right)^{2}+\left(v_{i}\otimes I_{\mathscr{S}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}_{x}}\right)^{2}\right)-c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}_{x}}\right)\hat{\otimes}IT_{x}\otimes I_{\mathscr{S}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}_{x}}.

To conclude, note that

\sigma^{co}\left([I\hat{\otimes}\varepsilon_{\theta}\nabla^{0}_{T},\frac{1}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\iota_{T}]\right)_{x}=\frac{1}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}_{x}}\right)\hat{\otimes}IT_{x}\otimes I_{\mathscr{S}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}_{x}}.

Putting our calculations together we find that

\sigma^{co}\left(\bm{\Delta}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\right)_{x}=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\left(u_{i}\otimes I_{\mathscr{S}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}_{x}}\right)^{2}+\left(v_{i}\otimes I_{\mathscr{S}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}_{x}}\right)^{2}\right)-\frac{3}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}_{x}}\right)\hat{\otimes}IT_{x}\otimes I_{\mathscr{S}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}_{x}}.

We now must check that the above operator is injective in every irreducible unitary representation of
\mathfrak{t}_{\mathcal{F}}M_{x}
. First note that if we define
e_{i}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{i}}}v_{i}
and
f_{j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{j}}}u_{j}
, then we have

[e_{i},e_{j}]=[f_{i},f_{j}]=0,\quad[e_{i},f_{j}]=\delta_{ij}T_{x},

and the cosymbol takes the form

\sigma^{co}\left(\bm{\Delta}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\right)_{x}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\left(-\left(e_{i}\otimes I_{\mathscr{S}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}_{x}}\right)^{2}-\left(f_{i}\otimes I_{\mathscr{S}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}_{x}}\right)^{2}-\frac{3}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{u_{i}}\varphi_{v_{i}}\right)\hat{\otimes}IT_{x}\otimes I_{\mathscr{S}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}_{x}}\right),

For
\vec{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus{\vec{0}}
, the inflated finite dimensional representation gives

\pi^{\mathscr{S}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}}_{\vec{\xi}}\left(\sigma^{co}\left(\bm{\Delta}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\right)_{x}\right)=(2\pi)^{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\right)|\vec{\xi}|^{2},

which is injective on
\mathscr{S}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}_{x}
for any
\xi\neq 0
. For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\setminus{0}
we have

\pi_{\lambda}^{\mathscr{S}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}}\left(\sigma^{co}\left(\bm{\Delta}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\right)_{x}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\left(-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial q_{i}^{2}}+\lambda^{2}q_{i}^{2}-\frac{3\lambda i}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{u_{i}}\varphi_{v_{i}}\right)\hat{\otimes}I\right).

Observe that the operator
c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{u_{i}}\varphi_{v_{i}}\right)
squares to
-1
and is skew-adjoint and thus has eigenvalues
\pm i
. With respect to a diagonalizing basis for
c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{u_{i}}\varphi_{v_{i}}\right)
, the
i
-th term in the above sum is

\alpha_{i}\left(-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial q_{i}^{2}}+\lambda^{2}q^{2}\pm\frac{3}{4}\lambda\right)

The operator
-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial q_{i}^{2}}+\lambda^{2}q^{2}
is bounded below by
|\lambda|
and thus we have for a Schwartz class function
\psi
with values in
\mathscr{S}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}_{x}
that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{\mathscr{S}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}_{x}}\left(\alpha_{i}\left(-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial q_{i}^{2}}+\lambda^{2}q_{i}^{2}-\frac{3\lambda i}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{u_{i}}\varphi_{v_{i}}\right)\hat{\otimes}I\right)\psi,\psi\right)dq\geq\frac{|\lambda|\alpha_{i}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{\mathscr{S}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}_{x}}\left(\psi,\psi\right)dq

where
h_{\mathscr{S}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}*}=h_{\mathscr{S}}\otimes\wedge g_{\mathcal{T}^{*}}
. Summing over
i
gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{\mathscr{S}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}_{x}}\left(\pi_{\lambda}^{\mathscr{S}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}}\sigma^{co}\left(\bm{\Delta}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\right)_{x}\psi,\psi\right)\geq\frac{|\lambda|}{4}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h_{\mathscr{S}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}_{x}}\left(\psi,\psi\right)dq.

Thus
\sigma^{co}\left(\bm{\Delta}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\right)_{x}
is injective in every inflated irreducible unitary representation of
T_{\mathcal{F}}M_{x}
and by Theorem 3.1 above, the operator
\bm{\Delta}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}
is hypoelliptic implying that
\mathbb{D}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}
is hypoelliptic.

∎

The same proof goes through for the operator acting on forms and gives the following theorem.

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose that
\left(M,\theta,g_{\mathcal{F}}\right)
is a contact sub-Riemannian manifold. Then the operator

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}

\displaystyle:=\lim_{u\rightarrow 0^{+}}^{F.P.}D^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}_{u}

\displaystyle=D^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}+I\hat{\otimes}\varepsilon_{\theta}\nabla^{0}_{T}+\frac{1}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\hat{\otimes}\iota_{T}+\frac{1}{4}\operatorname{Tr}L_{T}g_{\mathcal{F}}I\hat{\otimes}\varepsilon_{\theta}

is hypoelliptic. If
M
is closed, then
\textnormal{Ker}\mathbb{D}^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}
is a finite dimensional space of smooth sections.

Remark 3.4
.

When working with spinors, the natural choice of connections grants a hypoelliptic operator. However, the situation is a bit more subtle on the differential form side. In this case, we may be tempted into taking our covariant Clifford module to be the horizontal differential forms
\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}
with the natural induced connection
\nabla^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*},u}
coming from
\nabla^{\mathcal{F},u}
. Let us see the problem with this connection on the three dimensional Heisenberg group.

Example 3.5
.

Let us see what we have computed in the case of the
3
dimensional Heisenberg group
\left(\mathcal{H},\theta,g_{\mathcal{F}}\right)
. We let
Q,P
be the standard contact vector fields satisfying

[Q,P]=T,\quad g_{\mathcal{F}}\left(Q,P\right)=0,\quad g_{\mathcal{F}}\left(Q,Q\right)=g_{\mathcal{F}}\left(P,P\right)=1.

We let
\{\varphi_{Q},\varphi_{P},\theta\}
denote the dual framing to
\{Q,P,T\}
.
In this case, for
u>0
we have

\displaystyle A^{u}

\displaystyle=\theta\otimes\begin{bmatrix}0&u^{-1}/2\\
-u^{-1}/2&0\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle\omega_{u}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{Q}\otimes\frac{1}{2}u^{-1}\varphi_{P}\wedge\theta-\varphi_{P}\otimes\frac{1}{2}u^{-1}\varphi_{Q}\wedge\theta.

In which case our Dirac operator
D_{u}
is

\displaystyle D_{u}=c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)\left(Q+\frac{1}{4}u^{-1}c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)\hat{\otimes}\left(\varepsilon^{L}_{\theta}-u\partial^{L}_{\theta}\right)\right)+c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)\left(P-\frac{1}{4}u^{-1}c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)\hat{\otimes}\left(\varepsilon^{L}_{\theta}-u\partial^{L}_{\theta}\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\varepsilon^{L}_{\theta}-u\partial^{L}_{\theta}\right)\left(T-\frac{1}{4}u^{-1}\left(c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)-c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)\right)\right).

Collecting the terms constant in
u
gives the limiting operator
\mathbb{D}=\frac{d}{du}\big|_{u=0}\left(uD_{u}\right)
below

\mathbb{D}=c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)Q+c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)P+\varepsilon^{L}_{\theta}T-\frac{1}{4}\left(c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)+c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)\right)\partial^{L}_{\theta},

which when squared gives

\bm{\Delta}=-Q^{2}-P^{2}+c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\varphi_{P}\right)T-\frac{1}{4}\left(c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)+c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)\right)T+\mathcal{O}_{\Psi^{\theta}}\left(1\right).

The unfortunate feature of the above operator is that when we restrict to forms in
\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}
with odd degree the term

c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)+c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right),

vanishes, and thus at any
x\in H
, up to highest order our operator is

\sigma^{co}\left(\bm{\Delta}\right)=\begin{bmatrix}-Q^{2}-P^{2}+\frac{1}{2}c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\varphi_{P}\right)T&0\\
0&-Q^{2}-P^{2}+c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\varphi_{P}\right)T\end{bmatrix},

and the lower block is
not
Rockland in the Heisenberg calculus.

∎

While the above calculation is quite depressing, we can take a step back and see what went wrong. If
A^{\mathcal{F}}\in\Omega^{1}\left(P_{O}\left(\mathcal{F},g_{\mathcal{F}}\right),\mathfrak{o}(2n)\right)
denotes the connection
1
-form, then we have that the induced connection
1
-form on
\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}
is given by

A^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}=\frac{1}{2}[A^{\mathcal{F}},\cdot],

where the bracket is with respect to Clifford multiplication on
C\ell\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
. This brought in the pesky
c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)
above. It is here that we have to remember that there is a bit of freedom in our choice of covariant Clifford structure
\nabla^{E,u}
. We see here that there actually
two other natural choices
for
\nabla^{E,u}
. The first is to note that the above connection
\nabla^{u}
on
\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}
is
not
the Levi-Civita connection on
\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}\simeq\wedge T^{*}M
. To obtain the Levi-Civita connection we would need to add in the term
-\frac{1}{2}\omega^{R}_{u}
to
\nabla^{u}
. However, in this case the addition of extra right multiplication does not remedy the issue of being Rockland as we show below.

Example 3.6
.

If we instead add in the right multiplication by
-\frac{1}{2}\omega_{u}^{R}
, we obtain for
u>0

\displaystyle D_{u}=c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)\left(Q+\frac{1}{4}u^{-1}\left(c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)\left(\varepsilon^{L}_{\theta}-u\partial^{L}_{\theta}\right)-\left(\varepsilon^{R}_{\theta}-u\partial^{R}_{\theta}\right)c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle+c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)\left(P-\frac{1}{4}u^{-1}\left(c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)\left(\varepsilon^{L}_{\theta}-u\partial^{L}_{\theta}\right)-\left(\varepsilon^{R}_{\theta}-u\partial^{R}_{\theta}\right)c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\varepsilon^{L}_{\theta}-u\partial^{L}_{\theta}\right)\left(T-\frac{1}{4}u^{-1}\left(c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)-c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)\right)\right).

Taking the finite part gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}=c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)\left(Q-\frac{1}{4}\left(c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)\partial^{L}_{\theta}-\partial^{R}_{\theta}c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)\right)\right)+c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)\left(P+\frac{1}{4}\left(c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)\partial^{L}_{\theta}-\partial^{R}_{\theta}c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon^{L}_{\theta}T-\frac{1}{4}\left(c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)-c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)\right)\partial^{L}_{\theta}

\displaystyle=c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)Q+c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)P+\varepsilon_{\theta}^{L}T

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{4}\left(c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)+c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)\right)\partial^{L}_{\theta}+\frac{1}{4}\left(c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)\partial^{R}_{\theta}c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)-c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)\partial^{R}_{\theta}c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)\right)

which when squared gives

\displaystyle\bm{\Delta}=

\displaystyle-Q^{2}-P^{2}+c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\varphi_{P}\right)T-\frac{1}{4}\left(c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)+c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)\right)T

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{4}[\varepsilon^{L}_{\theta},c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)\partial^{R}_{\theta}c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)-c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)\partial^{R}_{\theta}c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)]T+\mathcal{O}_{\Psi^{\theta}}\left(1\right)

\displaystyle=-Q^{2}-P^{2}+c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\varphi_{P}\right)T-\frac{1}{4}\left(c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)+c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)\right)T

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(-1)^{H}}{4}\left(c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)-c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)\right)T+\mathcal{O}_{\Psi^{\theta}}\left(1\right)

where
(-1)^{H}
is the horizontal form degree operator on
\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}
. Once again we run into the same issue, which is that both terms

c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)+c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right),\quad c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)-c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{P}\right)c^{R}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\right)

vanish on odd elements of
\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}
, leaving up to highest order the term
-Q^{2}-P^{2}+c^{L}_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi_{Q}\varphi_{P}\right)T
which isn’t Rockland.

∎

From the above example, we see that we may rule out the Levi-Civita connection on
\wedge T^{*}M
as a choice of Clifford compatible connection.

Remark 3.7
.

When one selects
\nabla^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*},u}
as the Clifford connection and adds in
-\frac{1}{2}\omega^{R}_{u}
, then the Dirac operator
D_{u}
is the de Rham-Dirac operator
d_{M}+d_{M}^{*,u}
on
\Omega^{*}\left(M\right)
, where the adjoint is taken relative to
g_{M,u}
. One may be a bit suspicious that the construction does not behave well for this one-parameter family of supercharges. However, within the framework of the families index theorem, when one takes the vertical Dirac operators
D^{\mathcal{V}}
to be the de Rham-Dirac operators of the fibers, then the one-parameter family of Dirac operators
D_{u}\circlearrowright\Gamma\left(\wedge\mathcal{V}^{*}\hat{\otimes}\pi^{*}\wedge T^{*}B\right)
are
not
the de Rham-Dirac operators corresponding to the metrics
g_{\mathscr{M},u}
on
\mathscr{M}
. As we have not done the calculation for ourselves, it is unclear how the one-parameter family of de Rham-Dirac operators
d_{\mathscr{M}}+d_{\mathscr{M}}^{*,u}
will behave with regards to local index theoretic considerations.

4
An Example: Principal
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-bundles over
\mathbb{S}^{2}

Given that on a general closed contact sub-Riemannian submanifold
\left(M,\theta,g_{\mathcal{F}}\right)
we have natural associated operators
\mathbb{D}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}
and
\mathbb{D}^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}
, we are interested in what lies within their kernel. We will focus first on the case of
\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}
. Perhaps the simplest example of compact contact manifolds are supplied by the principal
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-bundles over an integral symplectic manifold
\left(P^{2n+1},\theta\right)\circlearrowleft\mathbb{S}^{1}\xrightarrow{\pi}\left(\Sigma^{2n},\bm{\Omega}\right)
. In this case
i\theta\in\Omega^{1}\left(P,i\mathbb{R}\right)
is the connection
1
-form and it satisfies

d\theta=\pi^{*}\bm{\Omega}.

If we equip
\Sigma
with a Riemannian metric
g_{\Sigma}
then we can pullback this metric to
\mathcal{F}=\textnormal{Ker}\theta\simeq\pi^{*}T\Sigma
thus obtaining
g_{\mathcal{F}}:=\pi^{*}g_{\Sigma}
. Given that
\theta
is a connection
1
-form for a principal
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-bundle, this implies that the Reeb vector field is simply the rotation vector field

T_{p}=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}p\cdot e^{it}.

The fact that
g_{\mathcal{F}}
is a pullback of a metric along
\Sigma
gives us that the Lie derivative of
g_{\mathcal{F}}
along
T
vanishes, thus giving the first simplification

\mathbb{D}^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}=D^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}+I\hat{\otimes}\varepsilon_{\theta}\nabla^{0}_{T}+\frac{1}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\hat{\otimes}\iota_{T}.

Moreover, under the Euclidean bundle isomorphism

\pi^{*}\left(\wedge T^{*}\Sigma,g_{\wedge T^{*}\Sigma}\right)\simeq\left(\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*},g_{\mathcal{F}}^{*}\right)

we see that the connection
\nabla^{\mathcal{F},0}
corresponds with the pullback of the Levi-Civita connection giving the isomorphism of covariant Euclidean bundles

\pi^{*}\left(\wedge T^{*}\Sigma,g_{\wedge T^{*}\Sigma},\nabla^{g_{\Sigma}}\right)\simeq\left(\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*},g_{\mathcal{F}}^{*},\wedge\nabla^{\mathcal{F},0}\right).

Under this isomorphism we have

\nabla^{0}_{T}=\partial_{T},

and for
\eta\in\Omega\left(\Sigma\right)
, if
\tilde{\eta}\in\Gamma\left(\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}\right)
denotes
\tilde{\eta}=\pi^{*}\eta\big|_{\mathcal{F}}
, then

D^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}\tilde{\eta}=\widetilde{\left(d_{\Sigma}+d_{\Sigma}^{*}\right)\eta}.

Note that
\nabla^{\mathcal{F},0}
has no curvature within the
T
-directions. Thus if
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{2n}
is a local orthonormal framing of
\left(T\Sigma,g_{\Sigma}\right)
with pullback framing
\{e^{\mathcal{F}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{2n}
then we have

\displaystyle D^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}\nabla^{\mathcal{F},0}_{T}

\displaystyle=c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi^{i}_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\nabla^{\mathcal{F},0}_{e^{\mathcal{F}}_{i}}\nabla^{\mathcal{F},0}_{T}

\displaystyle=c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi^{i}_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\left(\nabla^{\mathcal{F},0}_{T}\nabla^{\mathcal{F},0}_{e^{\mathcal{F}}_{i}}+\nabla^{\mathcal{F},0}_{[e^{\mathcal{F}}_{i},T]}\right)

\displaystyle=\nabla^{\mathcal{F},0}_{T}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\varphi^{i}_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\nabla^{\mathcal{F},0}_{e_{i}}

\displaystyle=\nabla^{\mathcal{F},0}_{T}D^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}.

Lastly, we have

\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}=\widetilde{\bm{\Omega}_{\Sigma}}.

From the above considerations one observation is evidently apparent: The harmonic forms of
\mathcal{H}_{\left(\Sigma,g_{\sigma}\right)}
naturally embed under pullback as a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded subspace of
\textnormal{Ker}\mathbb{D}^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\left(\Sigma,g_{\sigma}\right)}

\displaystyle\lhook\joinrel\rightarrow\textnormal{Ker}\mathbb{D}^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle\mapsto\tilde{\eta}.

Let us compute the full kernel for the simplest possible principal
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-bundles one can consider: the case of
\Sigma\simeq\mathbb{S}^{2}
. In this case we say that a metric
g
and symplectic form
\bm{\Omega}
are conformally compatible if there is a constant
\alpha>0
for which
\alpha\cdot g
and
\bm{\Omega}
give rise to a Kähler structure.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose we equip
\mathbb{S}^{2}
with the round metric
g
of radius
1/2
and a conformally compatible symplectic form
\bm{\Omega}
, and consider a covariant principal
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-bundle
\left(P,\theta\right)\circlearrowleft\mathbb{S}^{1}\xrightarrow{\pi}\mathbb{S}^{2}
with
d\theta=\pi^{*}\bm{\Omega}
. Then we have that

\textnormal{Ker}\mathbb{D}^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}\simeq H^{*}_{dR}\left(\mathbb{S}^{2}\right)

where the isomorphism is at the level of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector spaces.

Proof.

We first consider the case of the Hopf fibration
\mathbb{S}^{3}\circlearrowleft\mathbb{S}^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{2}
. We first compute the even part of the kernel of
\mathbb{D}^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}
. Note that even sections of
\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}
are of the form

\eta_{\mathcal{F}}+\tau_{\mathcal{F}}\theta

where
\eta_{\mathcal{F}}\in\Gamma\left(\wedge^{ev}\mathcal{F}^{*}\right)
and
\tau\in\Gamma\left(\wedge^{1}\mathcal{F}^{*}\right)
. Now suppose

\mathbb{D}^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}\left(\eta_{\mathcal{F}}+\tau_{\mathcal{F}}\theta\right)=0.

Then we obtain the system

D^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}\eta_{\mathcal{F}}-\frac{1}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\tau_{\mathcal{F}}=0,\quad D^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}\tau_{\mathcal{F}}+\partial_{T}\eta_{\mathcal{F}}=0.

Because
\bm{\Omega}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}=-2dV_{g}
we see that
D^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}

anti-commutes
with
c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)
. Applying
D^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}
to the first equation and denoting
\Delta^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}=\left(D^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}\right)^{2}
gives

\Delta^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}\eta_{\mathcal{F}}+\frac{1}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)D^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}\tau_{\mathcal{F}}=0

and using the second equation we obtain

\Delta^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}\eta_{\mathcal{F}}-\frac{1}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\partial_{T}\eta_{\mathcal{F}}=0.

Observe that solutions to the above equation determine solutions to the original system since we can solve for
\tau_{\mathcal{F}}
in the first equation by using
c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)^{2}=-4
giving

\tau_{\mathcal{F}}=-c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)D^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}\eta_{\mathcal{F}}.

The choice of metric of radius
1/2
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
guarantees that the induced metric

g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}=g_{\mathcal{F}}\oplus g_{\mathcal{T}}

is the round metric of radius
1
. Furthermore recall that for the Hopf fibration
\mathbb{S}^{3}\circlearrowleft\mathbb{S}^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{2}
there exists a global orthonormal framing
X,Y\in\Gamma\left(\mathcal{F}\right)
such that

[X,Y]=2T,\quad[T,Y]=2X,\quad[X,T]=2Y.

The above bracket relations imply, via the Koszul identity, that we also have that

\nabla^{\mathcal{F},0}_{X}Y=\nabla^{\mathcal{F},0}_{Y}X=0.

Lastly, we have
\wedge^{2}\mathcal{F}^{*}\simeq\underline{\mathbb{C}}_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
where the isomorphism is given by the global non-vanishing section
dV_{\mathcal{F}}
of
\wedge^{2}\mathcal{F}^{*}
. Under this isomorphism
\wedge\nabla^{\mathcal{F},0}
becomes the canonical flat connection. Thus under the isomorphism
\wedge^{ev}\mathcal{F}^{*}\simeq\underline{\mathbb{C}^{2}}_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
the connection
\wedge\nabla^{\mathcal{F},0}
becomes the canonical flat connection on
\underline{\mathbb{C}^{2}}_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
.
Thus we have the following global formula

\Delta^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}=-X^{2}-Y^{2}-c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)T,

which when combined with
\frac{1}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)T
gives

\Delta^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}-\frac{1}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)T=-X^{2}-Y^{2}-\frac{5}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)T.

If we use
\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}=-2dV_{\mathcal{F}}
and a hidden zero we obtain

\Delta^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}+\frac{1}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)T=\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}+T^{2}+\frac{5}{2}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(dV_{\mathcal{F}}\right)T.

Here we
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
denotes the Laplacian on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
with the round metric of radius
1
. The operator
c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(dV_{\mathcal{F}}\right)
squares to
-1
and is constant on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
. If we use the homogeneous polynomials as a basis for
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{S}^{3}\right)
then if
V_{m}
denotes the space of homogeneous polynomials, the operator
\Delta^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}-\frac{1}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)T
leaves each of these subspaces invariant and is diagonalizable on each such subspace. The spectrum is given as

\sigma\left(\Delta^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}-\frac{1}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)T\big|_{V_{m}}\right)=\{m(m+2)-k^{2}\pm\frac{5}{2}k\}_{k=-m}^{m}.

Thus the only subspace where
\Delta^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}+\frac{1}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)T
are those subspaces for which there is an
m\geq 0
and
0\leq|k|\leq m
such that

m(m+2)-k^{2}\pm\frac{5}{2}k=0.

Since
k
is already assumed to lie within
-m\leq k\leq m
we can absorb the
\pm
into
k
and simply solve

m(m+2)-k^{2}+\frac{5}{2}k=0.

The integer solutions to the above quadratic equation are

\left(m,k\right)=(-2,0),(-1,2),(0,0).

Because
m\geq 0
, we see that the kernel of
\Delta^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}-\frac{1}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)T
is the constant functions of
\mathbb{S}^{3}
. But these are precisely the functions on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
which are pullbacks of constant functions on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, hence

\textnormal{Ker}^{+}\mathbb{D}^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}\simeq H^{ev}_{dR}\left(\mathbb{S}^{2}\right).

Suppose now that we have an odd element
\chi_{\mathcal{F}}+\xi_{\mathcal{F}}\theta
, with
\chi_{\mathcal{F}}\in\Gamma\left(\mathcal{F}^{*}\right)
and
\xi_{\mathcal{F}}\in\Gamma\left(\wedge^{ev}\mathcal{F}^{*}\right)
satisfying

\mathbb{D}^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}\left(\chi_{\mathcal{F}}+\xi_{\mathcal{F}}\theta\right)=0.

Once again we obtain a system

D^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}\chi_{\mathcal{F}}+\frac{1}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\xi_{\mathcal{F}}=0,\quad D^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}\xi_{\mathcal{F}}-\partial_{T}\chi_{\mathcal{F}}=0,

for which the same trick gives

\Delta^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}\chi_{\mathcal{F}}-\frac{1}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\partial_{T}\chi_{\mathcal{F}}=0,\quad\xi_{\mathcal{F}}=c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)D^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}\chi_{\mathcal{F}}.

Now observe that if we apply
D^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}
to the first equation and let
\alpha_{\mathcal{F}}=D^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}\chi_{\mathcal{F}}\in\Gamma\left(\wedge^{ev}\mathcal{F}^{*}\right)
, then

\Delta^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}\alpha_{\mathcal{F}}+\frac{1}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)T\alpha_{\mathcal{F}}=0.

Thus we must now compute the kernel of the operator

\Delta^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}+\frac{1}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)T=\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}+T^{2}-\frac{3}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(dV_{\mathcal{F}}\right)T.

When we restrict to
V_{m}
and compute the spectrum we find

\sigma\left(\Delta^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}+\frac{1}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)T\big|_{V_{m}}\right)=\{m(m+2)-k^{2}\pm\frac{3}{2}k\}_{k=-m}^{m}.

In this case one obtains the lower bound

\left(\Delta^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}+\frac{1}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\right)\big|_{V_{m}}\geq\frac{m}{2}.

Thus we have that
\alpha_{\mathcal{F}}
must be the pullback of a constant function on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. Hence

0=D^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}\alpha=\Delta^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}\chi_{\mathcal{F}}.

which implies
\partial_{T}\chi_{\mathcal{F}}=0
, i.e. that
\chi_{\mathcal{F}}
is the pullback of a
1
-form on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
and that this
1
-form is harmonic on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
and thus is zero by the Hodge Theorem. All together, what we have found is that
\chi_{\mathcal{F}}=0
and hence so is
\xi_{\mathcal{F}}
. Thus

\textnormal{Ker}^{-}\mathbb{D}^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}=0.

Now recall that if
\pi_{P}:P\circlearrowleft\mathbb{S}^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}
is a principal
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-bundle then there is a covering map
\rho:\mathbb{S}^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}
and a covering map
p:\mathbb{S}^{3}\rightarrow P
which fit into a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{S}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{S}^{3}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{S}^{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P\times\mathbb{S}^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{S}^{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p\times\rho}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Id}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{P}}

where
R
denotes the right multiplication map. We choose the contact form
\theta_{P}
so that
p^{*}\theta_{P}=\theta_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
and sub-Riemannian metric
g_{\mathcal{F}_{P}}
so that
p^{*}g_{\mathcal{F}_{P}}=g_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}
. In this case, if
\alpha\in\Gamma\left(\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}_{P}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}_{P}\right)
satisfies

\mathbb{D}^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}_{P}}\alpha=0

then we immediately have that

\mathbb{D}^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}p^{*}\alpha=0.

As discussed above, pullbacks of harmonic forms on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
give a subspace of
\textnormal{Ker}\mathbb{D}^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}_{P}}
. Because
p^{*}
is an injective operator and
\textnormal{Ker}\mathbb{D}^{\mathcal{F}^{*}_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}
is precisely pullbacks of harmonic forms on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, we see that
\textnormal{Ker}\mathbb{D}^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}_{P}}
must consist precisely of pullbacks of harmonic forms on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
as well, thus

\textnormal{Ker}^{*}\mathbb{D}^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}_{P}}\simeq H^{*}_{dR}\left(\mathbb{S}^{2}\right).

∎

5
Generalizations and Future Directions

There are two generalizations of our construction in the setting of contact sub-Riemannian manifolds that are worth mentioning. The first is what occurs when one twists the
u
-dependent Clifford module
C\ell\left(\mathcal{F}^{*},g^{*}_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\circlearrowright\left(E,h_{E},\nabla^{E,u}\right)
by an auxiliary covariant hermitian vector bundle
\left(G,h_{G},\nabla^{G}\right)
. The second is what occurs in the equivariant setting. To the initiated these will seem like direct consequences of the arguments presented above.

5.1
Twisting by Auxiliary Bundles

Suppose that
\left(G^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ },h_{G},\nabla^{G}\right)\rightarrow M
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded covariant hermitian vector bundle. Then in the spinor and differential form context we can twist the bundles by the covariant data and obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\ell\left(\mathcal{F}^{*},g^{*}_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\circlearrowright\left(\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}\hat{\otimes}G,h_{\mathscr{S}}\hat{\otimes}h_{G},\nabla^{\mathscr{S},u}\otimes I+I\otimes\nabla^{G}\right)

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\ell\left(\mathcal{F}^{*},g^{*}_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\circlearrowright\left(\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}\hat{\otimes}G,\wedge g^{*}_{\mathcal{F}}\hat{\otimes}h_{G},\nabla^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*},u}\otimes I+I\otimes\nabla^{G}\right)

and obtain twisted versions of the asymptotic Bismut superconnections. In this case we have the following extensions of the theorems above.

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose that
\left(M,\theta,g_{\mathcal{F}}\right)
is a contact sub-Riemannian manifold such that
\left(\mathcal{F},g_{\mathcal{F}}\right)
is orientable and admits a spin structure. Then for any choice of complex hermitian spinor bundle
\left(\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}},h_{\mathscr{S}}\right)
, and auxiliary twisting bundle
\left(G^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ },h_{G},\nabla^{G}\right)
, the operator

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}\hat{\otimes}G}

\displaystyle:=\lim_{u\rightarrow 0^{+}}^{F.P.}D^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}\hat{\otimes}G}_{u}

\displaystyle=D^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}\hat{\otimes}G}+I\hat{\otimes}\varepsilon_{\theta}\nabla^{0}_{T}+\frac{1}{4}c_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Omega_{\mathcal{F}}\right)\hat{\otimes}\iota_{T}+\frac{1}{4}TrL_{T}g_{\mathcal{F}}I\hat{\otimes}\varepsilon_{\theta}

is hypoelliptic. If
M
is closed, then
\textnormal{Ker}\mathbb{D}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}\hat{\otimes}G}
is a finite dimensional space of smooth sections. The same statements hold if one replaces
\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}
with
\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}
.

5.2
The Equivariant Setting

Suppose that a Lie group
G
acts on
M
in such a way that it preserves the contact sub-Riemannian structure
G\circlearrowright\left(M,\theta,g_{\mathcal{F}}\right)
. If
G
is a compact Lie group that acts by contactomorphisms
G\circlearrowright\left(M,\theta\right)
then a sub-Riemannian metric
g_{\mathcal{F}}
exists by averaging an auxiliary metric. Then
G
preserves the splitting
TM=\mathcal{F}\oplus\mathcal{T}
as well as the one-parameter family of metrics
g_{M,u}=g_{\mathcal{F}}\oplus u^{-1}g_{\mathcal{T}}
. This in turn implies that
G
preserves the Levi-Civita connections
\nabla^{g_{M,u}}
and thus the induced connections
\nabla^{\mathcal{F},u}
and
\nabla^{\mathcal{T},u}
as well as the dual shape
1
-form
\omega_{u}
. This implies that
G
preserves that connection
\nabla^{\wedge\mathcal{F},u}
for all
u>0
and thus the asymptotic Bismut superconnection is
G
-equivariant

\mathbb{D}^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}g\cdot\eta=g\cdot\mathbb{D}^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}\eta,\quad\eta\in\Gamma\left(\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}\right).

If
G
preserves a choice of orientation and spin structure for
\left(\mathcal{F},g_{\mathcal{F}}\right)
, then this amounts to equivariance of
\mathbb{D}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}
with respect to the
G
-action

\mathbb{D}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}g\cdot\alpha=g\cdot\mathbb{D}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}\alpha,\quad\alpha\in\Gamma\left(\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}\hat{\otimes}\wedge\mathcal{T}^{*}\right).

Of course if one is handed a
G
-equivariant
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded covariant hermitian vector
G\circlearrowright\left(E^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ },h_{E},\nabla^{E}\right)\rightarrow G\circlearrowright\left(M,\theta,g_{\mathcal{F}}\right)
, then we can twist the asymptotic Bismut superconnection and obtain an equivariant operator. In this case we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 5.2
.

Suppose that
G
is a Lie group and we are given
G
-equivariant data
G\circlearrowright\left(E^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ },h_{E},\nabla^{E}\right)\rightarrow G\circlearrowright\left(M,\theta,g_{\mathcal{F}}\right)
. Then we have that
\mathbb{D}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}\hat{\otimes}E}
and
\mathbb{D}^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}\hat{\otimes}E}
are
G
-equivariant hypoelliptic operators and if
M
is closed then their kernels are finite dimensional
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded representations of
G

G\circlearrowright\textnormal{Ker}^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathbb{D}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}\hat{\otimes}E},\quad G\circlearrowright\textnormal{Ker}^{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\mathbb{D}^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}\hat{\otimes}E}.

5.3
Future Directions

There are two pressing questions that this construction demands be answered. The first is on the stability of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded invariants

\displaystyle\chi\left(\mathbb{D}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}\hat{\otimes}G}\right)

\displaystyle:=\text{dim}\textnormal{Ker}^{+}\mathbb{D}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}\hat{\otimes}G}-\text{dim}\textnormal{Ker}^{-}\mathbb{D}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}\hat{\otimes}G}

\displaystyle\chi\left(\mathbb{D}^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}\hat{\otimes}G}\right)

\displaystyle:=\text{dim}\textnormal{Ker}^{+}\mathbb{D}^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}\hat{\otimes}G}-\text{dim}\textnormal{Ker}^{-}\mathbb{D}^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}\hat{\otimes}G}.

and whether or not the above invariants are sensitive to the contact structure or only on the topology of the underlying manifold
M
. The second question regards the generality of the construction. More precisely, if we are given an arbitrary filtered manifold
\left(M,\mathcal{F}\right)
along with a subRiemnannian metric
g_{\mathcal{F}}
and splitting
TM=\mathcal{F}\oplus\mathcal{T}
, then the construction of the operators
\mathbb{D}^{\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}}
and
\mathbb{D}^{\wedge\mathcal{F}^{*}}
go through and the question of their hypoellipticity is currently open. There are of course simple instances one can consider, like that of a finite product of contact manifolds
\left(M_{1},\theta_{1}\right)\times\cdots\left(M_{k},\theta_{k}\right)
and where one takes the filtration to be
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{1}\boxplus\cdots\boxplus\mathcal{F}_{k}
. The reason for the direct generalization comes from the fact that the osculating groups in this case will be a finite product of Heisenberg groups, whose irreducible unitary reps can be explicitly computed using the Krillov orbit method. However, once the osculating groups leave the simple world of Heisenberg groups, the orbit method quickly becomes a difficult method to practically implement and it is clear that we will need a better understanding of
why
we obtained a hypoelliptic operator from this construction.

5.4
The Dream: A Path Integral Approach

In this last section we will formally describe the ideas which led to the consideration of our operator. If one considers the loop space of a Riemannian manifold
\left(M,g\right)
, denoted
\mathcal{L}M:=C^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z},M\right)
, then
\mathcal{L}M
inherits a natural action of
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
given by the rotation of a loop. In this case, the fixed point set of this action is seen to be the set of constant loops which are naturally identified as
M
. Witten has shown in
[
7
]
that the integral of the reciprocal equivariant Euler characteristic of the normal bundle
\mathscr{N}_{\mathcal{L}M}M\rightarrow M
can be regularized in an appropriate sense and computes the
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
-genus of
M

\int_{M}\chi^{-1}_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\left(\mathscr{N}_{\mathcal{L}M}M\right)=\int_{M}\hat{\mathcal{A}}\left(M\right).

Witten then applies a supersymmetry argument to relate the reciprocal of the regularized equivariant Euler characteristic to the path integral of the
\mathcal{N}=1
non-linear sigma model with source
\mathbb{R}^{1,1}
and target
\left(M,g\right)

\int_{M}\chi^{-1}_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\left(\mathscr{N}_{\mathcal{L}M}M\right)=\int_{\mathcal{L}M}e^{-S},

where
S
is locally given as

S=\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}dt\hskip 2.84544pt\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\dot{x}^{\mu}\dot{x}^{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\left(\frac{D}{dt}\psi^{\mu}\right)\psi^{\nu}.

From this perspective, the spinor Dirac operator
\not{D}
emerges naturally as the quantized Noether charge of the
\mathcal{N}=1
supersymmetric sigma model action
S
.
By using the correspondence between operator methods and the path integral formalism, Witten then relates the path integral to the supertrace of the heat flow of the corresponding quantum Hamiltonian
\hat{H}=\frac{1}{2}\not{D}^{2}
to equate the path integral to the index of
\not{D}

\text{Ind $\not{D}$}=\operatorname{STr}e^{-\beta\hat{H}}.

From here, the index theorem for the spinor Dirac operator follows. If one is now equipped with a filtered manifold
\left(M,\mathcal{F}\right)
, then one can still consider the
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
-manifold of smooth loops which are tangent to
\mathcal{F}
, denoted
\mathcal{L}^{\mathcal{F}}M
, and note that the fixed point set of the
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
action is still the set of constant loops. Thus one is confronted with the question of whether one can make sense of the quantity

\int_{M}\chi^{-1}_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\left(\mathscr{N}_{\mathcal{L}^{\mathcal{F}}M}M\right)

and equate it to the index of a corresponding hypoelliptic operator
\mathbb{D}

\int_{M}\chi^{-1}_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\left(\mathscr{N}_{\mathcal{L}^{\mathcal{F}}M}M\right)=\text{Ind $\mathbb{D}$}.

One could then interpret the regularized reciprocal equivariant Euler characteristic as the supersymmetric localization of an action functional
S_{\mathcal{F}}
corresponding to a constrained supersymmetric non-linear sigma model with target the sub-Riemannian manifold
\left(M,\mathcal{F},g_{\mathcal{F}}\right)
, the constraint corresponding to the condition that the bosonic velocity of a map
\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{1,1}\rightarrow M
be tangent to
\mathcal{F}
. The analytic difficulties here would be in giving a rigorous meaning of the constrained path integral

\int_{\mathcal{L}^{\mathcal{F}}M}e^{-S_{\mathcal{F}}}.

However, one can approach this problem directly from the operator side and instead try to cook up the quantized Noether current corresponding to the constrained action functional
S_{\mathcal{F}}
. One can gain a bit of a clue for how this could be practically implemented by studying the work of Bismut on the local families index theorem
[
1
]
, for there one considers a distribution
\mathcal{F}
which is the vertical distribution of a locally trivial fibration
\pi:\mathscr{M}\rightarrow B
equipped with an Ehresmann connection
\nabla
and vertical fiber metric
g_{\mathcal{F}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\mathscr{M},g_{\mathcal{F}},\nabla\right)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

One can again consider the smooth manifold of loops which are tangent to the distribution
\mathcal{L}^{\mathcal{F}}\mathscr{M}
and note that it sits within the loop space
\mathcal{L}\mathscr{M}
as a
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
-submanifold. The loop space of
\mathscr{M}
maps to the loop space of
B
and one obtains an equivariant diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\circlearrowright\mathcal{L}M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\circlearrowright\mathcal{L}^{\mathcal{F}}\mathscr{M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\circlearrowright\mathcal{L}\mathscr{M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\circlearrowright\mathcal{L}B.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{L}^{\mathcal{F}}\left(\pi\right)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{L}\left(\pi\right)}

If one formally applies families equivariant localization to the path integral for the
\mathcal{N}=1
sigma model with target
\left(\mathscr{M},g_{\mathcal{F}}\oplus\pi^{*}g_{B}\right)
, one would expect that one has the following relationship between fibered path-integrals

\int^{!}_{\mathcal{L}\mathscr{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}B}e^{-S}\Big|_{B}=\int^{!}_{\mathcal{L}^{\mathcal{F}}\mathscr{M}\rightarrow B}e^{-S_{\mathcal{F}}}.

Rather than attempt to give a rigorous meaning to the fibered path integral of action functionals on
\mathcal{L}\mathscr{M}
over
\mathcal{L}B
, Bismut argues in
[
1
]
that the Chern character of the Bismut superconnection
\mathbb{A}
is the analogue of the path-integration along the fiber on the operator theory side

\int^{!}_{\mathcal{L}\mathscr{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}B}e^{-S}\Big|_{B}=\text{STr }e^{-\mathbb{A}^{2}}.

Given the success of the Bismut superconnection in the local families index theorem, one is lead to explore its behavior in the non-integrable setting, in hopes that it will give a rigorous meaning to the reciprocal equivariant Euler characteristic of
\mathscr{N}_{\mathcal{L}^{\mathcal{F}}M}M
. The
hypoellipticity
that arises within the asymptotic superconnection, which is
not
present in the classic story given above, adds a new layer of interest in pursuing these ideas further.

Shock waves in classical dust collapse
Viqar Husain, Hassan Mehmood

Abstract
During gravitational collapse of dust in spherical symmetry, matter particles may collide forming shell crossing surfaces (SCS) on which the Einstein equations become indeterministic. We show that there is a unique evolution beyond SCS such that a propagating shock wave forms, the metric remains continuous, and the stress-energy tensor dynamically becomes that of a thin shell. We give numerical simulations that exhibit this result.



One of the most interesting problems in classical general relativity is the dynamics of gravitational collapse. The simplest setting for studying this problem is in spherical symmetry because the long-time static limit of gravitational collapse is expected to be the Kerr black hole, the first exact solution of Einstein’s equation. This solution is the universal attractor provided matter satisfies physically reasonable conditions, such as positive energy density and timelike or null matter flux.

How matter behaves as it undergoes gravitational collapse is also an important question. The details depend on the type of matter, and whether or not it has internal pressure. There are two well-studied exact solutions where the answer is known to some extent: for null fluid, the solution is the Vaidya metric
Vaidya 1951
and its generalization to include pressure
Husain 1996
; for timelike dust, the solution is the Lemaitre-Tolman-Bondi (LTB) spacetimes
Bondi 1947
;
Tolman 1934
. In the former case, particles of the fluid follow infalling lightlike trajectories until all the fluid’s mass-energy enters its Schwarzschild radius and a black hole forms; in the latter case there is the additional interesting possibility that timelike dust trajectories intersect at a location outside the Schwarzschild radius, a situation known as “shell crossing.”

For a brief review of the problem let us begin with the spherically symmetric metric

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+X(R,t)^{2}dR^{2}+r^{2}(R,t)d\Omega^{2}.

(1)

Then the Einstein equations with pressureless dust
G_{ab}=8\pi\rho\ u_{a}u_{b}

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r^{\prime 2}}{1+E(R)}

(2)

\displaystyle E(R)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dot{r}^{2}-\frac{2M(R)}{r}

(3)

where
\dot{r}=\partial r/\partial t,\ r^{\prime}=\partial r/\partial R

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{M^{\prime}}{4\pi r^{2}r^{\prime}}.

There is a significant literature on the nature of SCS. It is known that at an SCS the curvature singularity can be naked
Yodzis et al. 1973
, and is gravitationally weak in the sense that tidal forces
Newman 1986
;
Nolan 1999
and redshifts are bounded
Hellaby and Lake 1985
. It has been shown that if initial data are suitably restricted that SCS do not occur
Lake 1984
. However this “cure” appears too restrictive from the physical perspective of permitting all regular initial data.

There have also been attempts to extend the LTB spacetime beyond an SCS: the first such attempt was for a specific class of initial data
Papapetrou 1973
, followed by a more general proposal for extension
C.J.S. and N 1992
; it was shown in
Frauendiener and Klein 1995
that additional information about shell interaction is required for spacetime extension. Another approach used the fact that the LTB equations may be written as a conservation law that may be used to find solutions of the integrated version of the equations, called weak solutions. Such solutions are known to lead to shock waves in fluid mechanical systems. An issue is that weak solutions are not unique, they depend on the choice of variables used. For the LTB system this approach yields a shock wave such that the metric is bounded but discontinuous at the shock
Nolan 2003
;
Nolan 2004
. A more recent method suggests excising the SCS, and replacing it with a thin shell
Tippett and Booth 2014
using Israel junction conditions
Israel 1966
;
Israel 1967
;
Poisson 2009
;
Papapetrou and Hamoui 1968
; this yields a continuous metric throughout spacetime.

As may be gathered by this summary highlighting the main approaches to extending LTB metrics beyond SCS, there is no consensus on what is the unique physics. The approach that uses weak solutions appears to be the most natural as it is well established in fluid mechanics and similar systems; the issue of their non-uniqueness is settled for the more familiar cases by determining what is the natural physical observable that undergoes the shock, such as density in fluid flow.

Here we show that (i) there are weak solutions of the LTB system in which a shock wave forms at SCS such that the metric is continuous at the shock and the dynamics extends naturally beyond SCS, (ii) the variable for which this occurs is unique, and (iii) the dynamical equation for the shock is different from that of the thin shell derived using the junction conditions. These results settle the long-standing question of extending the LTB metric beyond SCS. We also present numerical examples of evolution that lead to shock wave formation using techniques applicable to hyperbolic conservation laws
LeVeque 2002
;
LeVeque 1992
. To arrive at these results we formulate the gravity-dust Einstein equations in the canonical theory in the dust time gauge
Lasky et al. 2006
;
Husain and Pawlowski 2012
.

Weak solution
Continuing a solution beyond the SCS requires consideration of
weak solutions
, which are solutions of the integrated version of eqn.(
18
). For radial integration domain
[r_{1},r_{2}]
this is

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}P_{\Lambda}(r,t)\ dr

\displaystyle=M(P_{\Lambda}(r_{2}),r_{2},t)-M(P_{\Lambda}(r_{1}),r_{1},t),

(22)

i.e. that the change of
P_{\Lambda}
in the radial interval is due to the net flux of
M
through its boundaries. To see what solutions this yields, let us apply it to a thin dust shell such that
P_{\Lambda}
are constants
P_{\Lambda}^{L},P_{\Lambda}^{R}
on either side of the shell. This could be the situation where two shells collide at a characteristic crossing
r=S
. Then, at time
t=0
the initial data is

\displaystyle P_{\Lambda}(0,r)=\Theta(r-S)P^{R}_{\Lambda}+\Theta(S-r)P^{L}_{\Lambda},

(23)

We expect dynamics to be such that the surface of discontinuity is propagating, i.e.
S=S(t)
, therefore we can write

P_{\Lambda}(t,r)=\Theta(r-S(t))P^{R}_{\Lambda}+\Theta(S(t)-r)P^{L}_{\Lambda}.

(24)

It remains to find the shock speed
dS(t)/dt
. This is done by substituting (
24

22

r_{1}=S(t)-\delta r
and
r_{2}=S(t)+\delta r

\dot{\Theta}(g(t))=\dot{g}(t)\delta(g(t))

\displaystyle\frac{dS(t)}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{M(S(t),P^{R}_{\Lambda})-M(S(t),P^{L}_{\Lambda})}{P^{R}_{\Lambda}-P^{L}_{\Lambda}}

(25)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{P^{R}_{\Lambda}+P^{L}_{\Lambda}}{2S(t)},

where the second equality follows from (
17
), namely
M=P^{2}_{\Lambda}/2r
. The first of (

P_{\Lambda}

Metric continuity
We now ask what is the implication of the shock velocity (
25
) for the metric (
20

\Sigma
from both sides by substituting

\displaystyle dr|_{\Sigma}=dS(t)=-\frac{P^{R}_{\Lambda}(t)+P^{L}_{\Lambda}(t)}{2S(t)}dt

\displaystyle ds^{2}|_{\Sigma_{R}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left[1-\left(\frac{P^{R}_{\Lambda}-P^{L}_{\Lambda}}{2S}\right)^{2}\right]dt^{2}+S^{2}d\Omega^{2}

(27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ds^{2}|_{\Sigma_{L}}.

Thus the metric is continuous at the shock for the dynamical equation (

derived
from the weak solution, unlike the case of Israel junction conditions where it is imposed.

Uniqueness
We now show a uniqueness property: There is no shock speed other than (
25
) that leads to continuity of the metric at the shock. This question is useful to address because the dynamical equation may be written as a conservation law in more than one variable, as noted above in
eqns. (
18

\displaystyle\frac{dS(t)}{dt}=F(S,P_{\Lambda}^{R},P_{\Lambda}^{L})

\displaystyle\left(\frac{P_{\Lambda}}{S}+F\right)^{2}_{|L}=\left(\frac{P_{\Lambda}}{S}+F\right)^{2}_{|R}.

The unique solution for

As an example, one can compute the shock speed that arises from the alternative dynamical equation (

\displaystyle\frac{dS(t)}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3\sqrt{S(t)}}\frac{(M^{R})^{3/2}-(M^{L})^{3/2}}{M^{R}-M^{L}}

(30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{3S(t)}\frac{(P^{R}_{\Lambda})^{3}-(P^{L}_{\Lambda})^{3}}{(P^{R}_{\Lambda})^{2}-(P^{L}_{\Lambda})^{2}},

By a similar calculation it follows that the metric is not continuous at the shock.

Comparison with junction conditions
Let us now compare the shock equation (
34
) with that of the thin shell obtained from the junction conditions. The usual treatment of the thin shell is in Schwarzschild coordinates with the following result
Poisson 2009
;
Israel 1967
for the marginally bound case (where the shell velocity is zero at spatial infinity):

\left(\frac{dR(\tau)}{d\tau}\right)^{2}=\left(1+\frac{m}{2R(\tau)}\right)^{2}-1,

(36)

where
R(\tau)
is the radius of the shell and
\tau
is the shell’s proper time for the induced metric on the shell,
ds^{2}|_{\text{shell}}=d\tau^{2}+R(\tau)^{2}d\Omega^{2}
. To compare with the shock equation (
34
), we transform (
34

to the proper time

[1-m/2S(t)]dt^{2}=d\tau^{2}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{dS(\tau)}{d\tau}\right)^{2}=\left(\frac{2S(\tau)}{m}-1\right)^{-1}.

(37)

Comparison of the last two equations shows that the weak solution and the junction conditions give significantly different dynamics, although the metric is continuous at the shock in the first case and at the shell in the second case. To understand the difference, let us note that the ADM momentum in the chosen gauge is

\displaystyle\tilde{\pi}^{ab}=\frac{P_{\Lambda}}{2}\ r^{a}r^{b}+\frac{rP_{\Lambda}^{\prime}}{4}\ (e^{ab}-r^{a}r^{b}),

(38)

where

is the unit radial vector and

is the Euclidean 3-metric. Now, the second junction condition (the discontinuity in the extrinsic curvature on the timelike shell surface), when projected on the PG spatial slice, gives a discontinuity in the radial component of the ADM momentum, and continuity in the angular directions

P_{\Lambda}

and
continuity in
P_{\Lambda}^{\prime}
. In contrast, the weak solution requires only the former, with no condition on
P_{\Lambda}^{\prime}

Discussion
We have presented three results that provide new insight into the classical problem of dust collapse initiated by the LTB solution. These are: (i) a unique extension of the metric beyond SCS such that a shock wave forms with the metric continuous at the shock; this comes from weak solutions of eqn. (
18
); no other form of the equation gives metric continuity at the shock; (2) the weak dynamics provided by (
18
) is different from the junction conditions where metric continuity is imposed rather than derived from the shock speed; (3) numerical simulations using the Godunov method exhibit our analytical results showing how a smooth initial density dynamically forms a shock wave accompanied by a discontinuity in the variable
P_{\Lambda}
.

These results show that there is no reason to exclude initial data that lead to SCS outside the horizon as advocated in
Lake 1984
, since the formation of shock waves with continuity of the metric beyond SCS follows from the weak solutions of (
18
). Our results also suggest that there is no reason to propose ad hoc extensions beyond SCS. We considered only marginally bound solutions; the
E\neq 0
case (
3
) requires an analysis of the coupled equations (
12
-
13
). This is a more challenging task because neither equation is in the conservation law form in the chosen variables.

In closing, we note that effective quantum LTB models have been the focus of several recent studies

;
Husain et al. 2022b
;
Giesel et al. 2023
;
Giesel et al. 2024
;
Cipriani et al. 2024
;

. A feature of these models is the formation of a shock wave followed by its bounce near

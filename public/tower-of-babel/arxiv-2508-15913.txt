Gaussian filters in quantum lattice systems: Applications to spectral flow, local perturbations, clustering, and the quantum Hall effect
Sven Bachmann, Zhiqian (Simon) Du, Martin Fraas, Tom Wessel

Abstract
We consider the locality and spectral properties of the smearing τ f ​ ( A ) = ∫ − ∞ ∞ ⁡ d ​ t ​ f ​ ( t ) ​ τ t ​ ( A ) \tau_{f}(A)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\,f(t)\,\tau_{t}(A) when applied to the dynamics τ t \tau_{t} of quantum spin systems. While recent applications of this map have used superpolynomially but not exponentially decaying functions f f to ensure exact spectral properties, we use here Gaussian filters. This improves the locality at the expense of errors on the spectral side. We propose a number of concrete applications, from quasi-adiabatic continuation to correlation decay, and exponential stability away from impurities. Finally, we discuss an application to the quantum Hall effect.

1
Introduction

Physical time evolution flows
t\mapsto\tau_{t}(A)={\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}Ht}\,A\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-\mathrm{i}Ht}
are local, where the precise meaning of locality depends on the particular setup.
Propagation is strictly within the light cone when the underlying equation is the wave equation, it is diffusive for the heat equation, and the Lieb-Robinson bound
[
17
]
provides an effective ballistic propagation bound for the Schrödinger equation on sufficiently regular lattices.
Among others, these results are essential in proving well-posedness for these equations.
The locality that originates in the Lieb-Robinson bound has proved crucial to understand both thermal phases and (topologically ordered) ground state phases, see
[
hastings2010locality
]
for many examples.
In this context, the smearing map
1
1

1

When the subscript of
\tau
is a real number, it denotes the time evolution and when the subscript is a function, it denotes this integral.

\tau_{f}(A)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\,f(t)\,\tau_{t}(A)

(1)

has proved particularly useful, since the decay of
f
controls the locality of
A\mapsto\tau_{f}(A)
while its Fourier transform
\widehat{f}
controls the spectral properties of the map.
In fact, the (Arveson) spectrum of
\tau
is defined as the smallest subset
\sigma\subset\mathbb{R}
such that
\tau_{f}
vanishes for all
f
such that
\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{f})\cap\sigma=\varnothing
, see
[
6
, Definition A.1]
.

Although it was not phrased exactly as such in its original formulation
[
hastings2004locality
]
, smearing of the type (
1
) is central to quasi-adiabatic continuation, also known as the spectral flow
[
hastings2010locality
,
bachmann2012automorphic
]
, a tool that has become one of the cornerstones of the analysis and classification of topological phases of matter, and of the related questions of adiabaticity and linear response theory
[
bachmann2018adiabatic
,
monaco2019adiabatic
,
teufel2020non
]
.
Understanding quasi-particle excitations of topologically ordered states also relies on this technique
[
6
,
bachmann2020many
]
.
In another guise, such smearings are instrumental in proving rapid decay of correlations for gapped quantum lattice systems
[
hastings2006spectral
,
nachtergaele2006lieb
]
, and to relate this to stability of ground state
[
bachmann2022stability
]
.

In the example of the spectral flow, the Fourier transform
\widehat{f}
is required to be discontinuous, which implies that
f
cannot decay exponentially at infinity.
While stretched-exponential or even superpolynomial decay is often sufficient and convenient for locality arguments
[
nachtergaele2019quasi
]
, one may wish to use an exponentially decaying function.
In fact, the original formulation of quasi-adiabatic continuation used a Gaussian filter.
The price to pay is that the spectral properties are not exact anymore.
Exponentially decaying filter functions have also proved useful in treating thermal states, see e.g.
[
14
,
11
,
9
]
.

In this paper, we consider the use of Gaussian filter functions, providing exact bounds both on the spatial locality and on the spectral errors.
In particular, we define a spectral flow which is exponentially local and almost exact.
Our Ansatz is similar to the one used in
[
hastings2005quasiadiabatic
]
.
Here, the smearing is applied term-by-term on an interaction and the width of the Gaussian must be chosen in a spatially inhomogeneous way.
As expected, better locality yield worse spectral mapping properties.
The same methods allow us to provide an exponential version of the
“local perturbations perturb locally”
property.
What is more, a properly chosen Gaussian filter convolved with a step function yields exponential decay of correlations for gapped spectral patches whose width does not need to vanish in the thermodynamic limit.
As a corollary of all the above, we conclude that the Hall conductance is quantized in finite systems up to errors that are exponentially small in the system size.

This paper is organized as follows.
After a brief review of the Lieb-Robinson bound, we introduce in Section
3
the almost inverse Liouvillian built using a Gaussian filter.
We prove bounds characterizing both its quasi-locality properties and its spectral property.
In Section
4
, we use it to construct an exponentially local almost spectral flow and show that local perturbations that do not close the gap have exponentially small effect away from the perturbation.
Parallel ideas are developed in Section
5
to prove exponential clustering for finite volume spectral patches of finite width.
Finally, Section
6
discusses the application of these tools to the quantum Hall effect.

2
Mathematical setup

We consider spin systems on finite
D
-regular graphs.
Therefore, let
\Lambda
be a finite set and denote by
d
the graph distance.
Clearly, there exist constants
D\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{vol}}>0
such that the volume of all balls

\displaystyle B_{x}(r):=\{\nonscript\,z\in\Lambda\nonscript\,|\allowbreak\nonscript\,\mathopen{}d\lparen z,x\rparen\leq r\nonscript\,\}

scales such that

\displaystyle\lvert B_{x}(r)\rvert\leq\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{vol}}\,(r+1)^{D}\qquad\text{for all $x\in\Lambda$ and $r\geq 0$.}

The set of graphs with this scaling for fixed
D
and
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{vol}}
is denoted
\mathcal{G}(D,\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{vol}})
.
With this definition, all finite subsets
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{D}
are in the same
\mathcal{G}(D,\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{vol}})
, and they can even have periodic boundary conditions in one or more directions.

For later purposes, we note that there exists a constant
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{vol},b,k}\geq 1
such that for all sets
Z\subset\Lambda

\lvert Z\rvert^{k}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-b\operatorname{diam}\lparen Z\rparen}\leq\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{vol},b,k}.

If
b\leq D
, then

\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{vol},b,k}\leq\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{vol}}^{k}\,\big\lparen\tfrac{k\,D}{{\mathrm{e}}}\big\rparen^{kD}\,b^{-kD}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{b}.

(2)

Indeed, for any
z\in Z
, we have

\lvert Z\rvert^{k}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-b\operatorname{diam}\lparen Z\rparen}\leq\lvert B_{z}(\operatorname{diam}\lparen Z\rparen)\rvert^{k}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-b\operatorname{diam}\lparen Z\rparen}\leq\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{vol}}\,\sup_{n\geq 0}\,(n+1)^{kD}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-bn},

and the bound (
2
) follows since the supremum is attained at
n=\min\{0,k\,D/b-1\}
.

With every site
x\in\Lambda
one associates a finite-dimensional local Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}_{x}:=\mathbb{C}^{q}
with the corresponding space of linear operators denoted by
\mathcal{A}_{x}:=\mathcal{B}\left\lparen\mathbb{C}^{q}\right\rparen
.
Moreover, we define the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}_{\Lambda}:=\bigotimes_{x\in\Lambda}{\mathcal{H}}_{x}
, and denote the algebra of bounded linear operators on
{\mathcal{H}}_{\Lambda}
by
\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}:=\mathcal{B}({\mathcal{H}}_{\Lambda})
.
Due to the tensor product structure, we have
\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}=\bigotimes_{x\in\Lambda}\mathcal{A}_{x}
.
Hence, for
X\subset\Lambda
, any
A\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
can be viewed as an element of
\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}
by identifying
A
with
A\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda\backslash X}\in\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}
, where
\mathbf{1}_{\Lambda\backslash X}
denotes the identity in
\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda\backslash X}
.
This identification is always understood implicitly and for
B\in\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}
we denote by
\supp(B)
the smallest
Y\subset\Lambda
such that
B\in\mathcal{A}_{Y}
.

Remark 1
.

While we formulate all our results for spin systems, one can use the same approach for fermionic lattice systems provided the algebra is that of even elements of the CAR, see for example
[
22
]
.
In particular,
Theorems

12
,
13
and
17
also hold for fermionic lattice systems.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be a compact interval.
A (time-dependent) interaction on
\Lambda
is a function

\Phi\colon I\times\{Z\subset\Lambda\}\to\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda},\quad(t,Z)\mapsto\Phi(t,Z)\in\mathcal{A}_{Z}\quad\text{with}\quad\Phi(t,Z)=\Phi(t,Z)^{*}.

(3)

For
b\geq 0
, we define interaction norms

\lVert\Phi\rVert_{b}:=\sup_{t\in I}\sup_{z\in\Lambda}\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z\subset\Lambda\mathpunct{\ordinarycolon}\\
z\in Z\end{subarray}}\,\lVert\Phi(t,Z)\rVert\,{\mathrm{e}}^{b\operatorname{diam}\lparen Z\rparen}.

(4)

An interaction
\Phi
gives rise to the corresponding operator

H(t):=\sum_{Z\subset\Lambda}\Phi(t,Z),

which generates the Heisenberg time-evolution
\tau_{t,s}
defined as the solution of

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\,\tau_{s,t}(A)=\tau_{s,t}\big\lparen\mathrm{i}\,[H(t),A]\big\rparen,\qquad\tau_{s,s}(A)=A,

(5)

for any
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}
.

An important property of the time-evolution is its locality as captured by Lieb-Robinson bounds, which originated in
[
17
]
and were generalized in
[
21
]
and many other works.
We here state a version for the norm (
4
), whose time-independent version appeared in
[
bachmann2022trotter
, Theorem A.1]
, and the time-dependent one is in
[
18
, Theorem 7.3.3]
.

Lemma 2

(Lieb-Robinson bound)
.

Let
D\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{vol}}>0
,
\Lambda\in\mathcal{G}(D,\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{vol}})
be finite, and
b^{\prime}>b>0
.
Then, for all intervals
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, time-dependent interactions
\Phi
such that
\lVert\Phi\rVert_{b^{\prime}}<\infty
, disjoint subsets
X
,
Y\subset\Lambda
, observables
A\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
and
B\in\mathcal{A}_{Y}
, and
s
,
t\in I
it holds that

\big\lVert\big[\tau_{s,t}(A),B\big]\big\rVert\leq 2\,\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{vol},1,b^{\prime}-b}^{-1}\,\lVert A\rVert\,\lVert B\rVert\,\big\lparen{\mathrm{e}}^{b\,v\,\lvert t-s\rvert}-1\big\rparen\,D(X,Y),

(6)

where
v=2\,\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{vol},1,b^{\prime}-b}\,\lVert\Phi\rVert_{b^{\prime}}/b
is the Lieb-Robinson velocity and

\displaystyle D(X,Y)

\displaystyle:=\min\bigg\{\sum_{x\in X}{\mathrm{e}}^{-bd\lparen x,Y\rparen},\sum_{y\in Y}{\mathrm{e}}^{-bd\lparen y,X\rparen}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\phantom{:}\leq\min\big\{\lvert X\rvert,\lvert Y\rvert\big\}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-bd\lparen X,Y\rparen}.

In this statement and the rest of the paper, the interaction norm and the evolution implicitly depend on
\Lambda
and this dependence is understood from the context.
Importantly, all the constants will be independent of the specific
\Lambda\in\mathcal{G}(D,\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{vol}})
and only depend on the lattice through
D
and
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{vol}}
.
In fact, one can define an interaction
\Phi
and the interaction norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{b}
on an infinite lattice
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}^{D}
and the restrictions
\Phi\rvert_{\Lambda}
to finite lattices
\Lambda\subset\Gamma
satisfy
\lVert\Phi\rvert_{\Lambda}\rVert_{b}\leq\lVert\Phi\rVert_{b}
.
Our results are then uniform in
\Lambda\subset\Gamma
.

3
The almost inverse Liouvillian

For
\beta>0
let

\varphi_{\beta}(t):=\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{\uppi}}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta^{2}t^{2}}.

It is such that
\int\varphi_{\beta}=1
and the Fourier transform is

\hat{\varphi}_{\beta}(\omega):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\uppi}}\int\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\,\varphi_{\beta}(t)\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-\mathrm{i}t\omega}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\uppi}}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{\omega^{2}}{4\beta^{2}}}

(7)

for which
\int\hat{\varphi}_{\beta}=\beta\sqrt{\frac{2}{\uppi}}
.
Then, for any Hamiltonian
H
and observable
A
we define the
almost inverse Liouvillian

\mathcal{I}_{H,\beta}(A):=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\,\varphi_{\beta}(t)\,\int_{0}^{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\,\tau_{s}^{H}(A),

(8)

where
\tau_{s}^{H}(A)={\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}Hs}\,A\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-\mathrm{i}Hs}
.
To make contact with the previous section, we imagine here a time-dependent interaction and the corresponding Hamiltonian in a finite volume
\Lambda
.

To quantify the properties of the almost inverse Liouvillian, it will be helpful to define the exact inverse Liouvillian
\mathcal{I}_{H}
along the same lines, see
[
8
]
.
Instead of
\varphi_{\beta}
, the map
\mathcal{I}_{H}
uses a non-negative function
w
with Fourier transform
\hat{w}\in C^{1}_{0}((-\delta,\delta))
and
\int w=1
.
Note that
w
can be chosen to decay faster than any inverse power but not exponentially.
Whenever
\mathcal{I}_{H}
involves a gapped Hamiltonian with gap
\gamma
, we implicitly choose
\delta<\gamma
.

Using standard techniques, we see that the almost inverse Liouvillian has good locality properties.

Lemma 3

(Locality of the almost inverse Liouvillian)
.

Let
D\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{vol}}>0
and
\Lambda\in\mathcal{G}(D,\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{vol}})
be finite.
Let
b^{\prime}>b>0
,
\Phi
be an interaction such that
\lVert\Phi\rVert_{b^{\prime}}<\infty
and
H
the corresponding Hamiltonian.
Then, for all disjoint
X
,
Y\subset\Lambda
,
A\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
and
B\in\mathcal{A}_{Y}
,

\big\lVert\big[\mathcal{I}_{H,\beta}(A),B\big]\big\rVert\leq 2\,\min\{\lvert X\rvert,\lvert Y\rvert\}\,\lVert A\rVert\,\lVert B\rVert\,\inf_{T>0}\,\bigg\lparen\frac{2\,\beta}{\sqrt{\uppi}\,b^{2}\,v^{2}}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{b(vT-d\lparen X,Y\rparen)}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\uppi}\,\beta}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta^{2}T^{2}}\bigg\rparen,

(9)

where
v
is the Lieb-Robinson velocity from
Lemma

2
.

One may choose
T=\frac{d(X,Y)}{2v}
and then use
d\lparen X,Y\rparen^{2}\geq d\lparen X,Y\rparen
to get

\big\lVert\big[\mathcal{I}_{H,\beta}(A),B\big]\big\rVert\leq 2\,\min\{\lvert X\rvert,\lvert Y\rvert\}\,\lVert A\rVert\,\lVert B\rVert\bigg\lparen\frac{2\,\beta}{\sqrt{\uppi}\,b^{2}\,v^{2}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\uppi}\,\beta}\bigg\rparen{\mathrm{e}}^{-b(\beta)d(X,Y)},

(10)

where
b(\beta)=\min\big\{\frac{b}{2},\frac{\beta^{2}}{4v^{2}}\big\}
.

Remark 4
.

The last expression (
10
), in particular
b(\beta)
, emphasizes the double origin of the quasi-locality of the map
\mathcal{I}_{H,\beta}
, namely the locality of the Hamiltonian through the Lieb-Robinson bound yielding the dependence on
b
and the locality expressed by the Gaussian filter yielding the dependence on
\beta
.
In particular, the locality cannot be improved further than
\beta
of the order of
\sqrt{b}
, and we shall later restrict our attention to this situation, see (
16
) below.

Proof.

We bound

\big\lVert\big[\mathcal{I}_{H,\beta}(A),B\big]\big\rVert\leq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\,\varphi_{\beta}(t)\,\int_{0}^{\mathrlap{t}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\,\big\lVert\big[\tau_{s}^{H}(A),B\big]\big\rVert=\inf_{T>0}(I_{<T}+I_{\geq T}),

and split the integral into a part
I_{<T}
where
\lvert t\rvert<T
and the rest
I_{\geq T}
where
\lvert t\rvert\geq T
.
For the first part we use
\varphi_{\beta}(t)\leq\beta/\sqrt{\uppi}
and the Lieb-Robinson bound for
\tau^{H}
,
Lemma

2
, where we bound
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{vol},1,b^{\prime}-b}^{-1}\leq 1
, to obtain

\displaystyle I_{<T}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{\uppi}}\,4\,\min\{\lvert X\rvert,\lvert Y\rvert\}\,\lVert A\rVert\,\lVert B\rVert\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-bd\lparen X,Y\rparen}\int_{0}^{\mathrlap{T}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\,\int_{0}^{\mathrlap{t}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\,\big\lparen{\mathrm{e}}^{bvs}-1\big\rparen

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{\uppi}}\,4\,\min\{\lvert X\rvert,\lvert Y\rvert\}\,\lVert A\rVert\,\lVert B\rVert\,\frac{1}{(bv)^{2}}\big\lparen{\mathrm{e}}^{bvT}-1-bvT-\tfrac{1}{2}(bvT)^{2}\big\rparen\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-bd\lparen X,Y\rparen}.

For the second term we use the decay of
\varphi_{\beta}
and the trivial bound

\big\lVert\big[\tau_{s}^{H}(A),B\big]\big\rVert\leq 2\,\lVert A\rVert\,\lVert B\rVert

to get

I_{\geq T}\leq 4\,\lVert A\rVert\,\lVert B\rVert\,\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{\uppi}}\int_{T}^{\mathrlap{\infty}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta^{2}t^{2}}\,t\leq 2\,\lVert A\rVert\,\lVert B\rVert\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{\uppi}\,\beta}\,\big\lparen{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta^{2}T^{2}}-1\big\rparen.

Combining both bounds concludes the proof.
∎

Assumption 5

(Gap Assumption)
.

Let
\Lambda
be finite.
Let
\Phi
be an interaction satisfying
\lVert\Phi\rVert_{b^{\prime}}<\infty
for some
b^{\prime}>0
, and let
H
be the corresponding Hamiltonian.
We assume that the spectrum of
H
is of the form

\sigma\lparen H\rparen=\sigma_{0}\cup\sigma_{1}

with
\gamma=d\lparen\sigma_{1},\sigma_{0}\rparen>0
.

From here onwards, we shall drop the subscript
\Lambda
for notational clarity.
We denote
P=\chi_{\sigma_{0}}(H)
the spectral projection of
H
corresponding to the patch
\sigma_{0}
.

Proposition 6
.

Let
H
satisfy the
Assumption 5

(Gap Assumption)
.
and assume that
\sigma_{0}=\{E_{0}\}
is a single eigenvalue.
Then

\mathcal{I}_{H,\beta}(P\,A\,P)=0,

for all
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}
.

Proof.

One calculates

\mathcal{I}_{H,\beta}(P\,A\,P)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\,\varphi_{\beta}(t)\,\int_{0}^{\mathrlap{t}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\,\tau_{s}^{H}(P\,A\,P)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\,\varphi_{\beta}(t)\,t\,P\,A\,P=0,

because the time-evolution is trivial and
\varphi_{\beta}
is an even function.
∎

Remark 7
.

The fact that
\mathcal{I}_{H,\beta}
vanishes exactly on the range of
P
depends on the assumption
\sigma_{0}=\{E_{0}\}
.
For spectral patches with
\delta=\operatorname{diam}\lparen\sigma_{0}\rparen>0
sufficiently small with respect to
\gamma
, a similar result could be obtained, although with an error bound, by replacing
\hat{\varphi}_{\beta}
with two Gaussians centred at
\pm\tfrac{\delta+\gamma}{2}
.

Proposition 8
.

Let
H
satisfy the
Assumption 5

(Gap Assumption)
.
.
Then

is an almost inverse of the Liouvillian

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\in\sigma_{0}\end{subarray}}\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\in\sigma_{1}\end{subarray}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\,\big\lparen w(t)-\varphi_{\beta}(t)\big\rparen\,{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}t(\mu-\nu)}\,P_{\mu}\,A\,P_{\nu}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2\uppi}\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\in\sigma_{0}\end{subarray}}\,\lVert P_{\mu}\rVert_{q}\,\lVert A\rVert\,\Big\lVert\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\in\sigma_{1}\end{subarray}}\hat{\varphi}_{\beta}(\nu-\mu)\,P_{\nu}\Big\rVert

With (

Remark 9
.

Since we are dealing with a fixed finite volume here, the rank of

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\in\sigma_{0}\end{subarray}}\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\in\sigma_{1}\end{subarray}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\,\big\lparen\varphi_{\beta}(t)-w(t)\big\rparen\,\int_{0}^{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\,{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}(\mu-\nu)s}\,P_{\mu}\,A\,P_{\nu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\in\sigma_{0}\end{subarray}}\,P_{\mu}\,A\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\in\sigma_{1}\end{subarray}}\,\frac{\sqrt{2\uppi}}{\mathrm{i}(\mu-\nu)}\,\hat{\varphi}_{\beta}(\nu-\mu)\,P_{\nu},

by the
Assumption 5

(Gap Assumption)
.
.
It follows that

\big\lVert\mathcal{I}_{H,\beta}(A)-\mathcal{I}_{H}(A)\big\rVert_{q}\leq\sqrt{2\uppi}\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\in\sigma_{0}\end{subarray}}\,\lVert P_{\mu}\rVert_{q}\,\lVert A\rVert\,\Big\lVert\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\in\sigma_{1}\end{subarray}}\frac{\hat{\varphi}_{\beta}(\nu-\mu)}{\nu-\mu}\,P_{\nu}\Big\rVert\leq\sqrt{2\uppi}\,\lVert P_{\mu}\rVert_{1}\,\lVert A\rVert\,\frac{\hat{\varphi}_{\beta}(\gamma)}{\gamma}.

The second statement follows as in

4
The almost spectral flow and LPPL

Under the
Assumption 11

(Uniform Gap assumption)
.
below,
\mathcal{I}_{H(t)}\big\lparen\dot{H}(t)\big\rparen
generates an automorphism
\alpha_{0,s}
which provides a mapping between the instantaneous ground states of
H(0)
and
H(s)
, namely

\omega_{s}(A)=\omega_{0}\circ\alpha_{0,s}(A)

(11)

for all
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}
, where
\omega_{s}(A)=\Tr(P_{s})^{-1}\Tr(P_{s}\,A)
, see
[
8
]
.
However, as explained in the introduction, this automorphism
\alpha
cannot be exponentially local.
Instead, we shall use the almost inverse Liouvillian through
\mathcal{I}_{H(t),\beta}\big\lparen\dot{H}(t)\big\rparen
to obtain an exponentially local almost spectral flow
\alpha^{\beta}
.

Similarly to the locality of
\mathcal{I}_{H(t),\beta}(A)
for strictly local operators
A\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
discussed in
Lemma

3
, standard arguments show that
\mathcal{I}_{H(t),\beta}\big\lparen\dot{H}(t)\big\rparen
is given by an exponentially local interaction if
H
and
\dot{H}
are, see
Lemma

15
.
We first concentrate to the spectral mapping properties of
\alpha^{\beta}_{0,s}
.

Assumption 11

(Uniform Gap assumption)
.

Let
H(s)
with
s\in[0,1]
be a Hamiltonian given by a smooth time-dependent interaction
s\mapsto\Phi(s)
satisfying
\sup_{s\in[0,1]}\lVert\Phi(s)\rVert_{b^{\prime}}<\infty
for some
b^{\prime}>0
.
We assume that
H(s)
satisfies the
Assumption 5

(Gap Assumption)
.
.
Moreover, we assume that there exists compact intervals
I(s)
with endpoints depending smoothly on
s
such that
\sigma_{0}(s)\subset I(s)\subset\mathbb{R}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\setminus}{\textstyle\setminus}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\setminus$}}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\setminus$}}}\sigma_{1}(s)
.

To characterize the almost spectral flow
\alpha^{\beta}
, we wish to compare it to the exact spectral flow
\alpha
, using the standard identity

\alpha^{1}_{s,t}(A)-\alpha^{2}_{s,t}(A)=\sum_{Z\subset\Lambda}\int_{s}^{t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda\,\alpha^{1}_{s,\lambda}\Big\lparen\Big[\Psi_{1}(\lambda,Z)-\Psi_{2}(\lambda,Z),\alpha^{2}_{\lambda,t}(A)\Big]\Big\rparen,

(12)

for any two automorphisms generated by interactions
\Psi_{1}
and
\Psi_{2}
.
One then uses the Lieb-Robinson bound to obtain an estimate which grows in
\supp(A)
but is not extensive in
\Lambda
.
As outlined above, the locality of the interaction
\mathcal{I}_{H(t)}\big\lparen\dot{H}(t)\big\rparen
is insufficient for our purpose as
\mathcal{I}_{H(t)}
is not exponentially local.
An exponential bound can be obtained directly using
Lemma

10
with
A
being replaced by the full
\dot{H}(t)
, but the resulting bound on
\lVert\alpha_{0,s}(A)-\alpha^{\beta}_{0,s}(A)\rVert

is proportional to
\sup_{s\in[0,1]}\lVert\dot{H}(s)\rVert
, which in general is extensive in
\Lambda
.

The solution we propose below is to construct another flow
\alpha^{\beta,X}_{t,s}
where
X=\supp(A)
, which is adapted to the support of the observable, and use it as an intermediate to compare the exact spectral flow
\alpha
with its exponentially local but approximate cousin
\alpha^{\beta}
.
The generator of
\alpha^{\beta,X}
is given by

\sum_{Z\subset\Lambda}\mathcal{I}_{H(t),\beta_{X,Z}}\big\lparen\dot{\Phi}(t,Z)\big\rparen,

(13)

where the width of the Gaussian is modulated as follows

\frac{1}{\beta_{X,Z}^{2}}=\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}+\mathbf{1}_{d\lparen X,Z\rparen\geq\ell}\,d\lparen X,Z\rparen,

and the parameter

will be chosen appropriately later.
We note that a very similar choice was also proposed in the original
[
16
]
.
With this, we shall prove the following.

Theorem 12
.

Let

,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{vol}}>0

and

such that the following holds.
For all

finite, smooth Hamiltonians

that satisfy the

(Uniform Gap assumption)
.
with gap

and are given by interactions

\big\lvert\omega_{s}(A)-\omega_{0}\circ\alpha^{\beta}_{s,0}(A)\big\rvert\leq C\,\lvert X\rvert^{2}\,\lVert A\rVert\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-c\beta^{-2}},

for all

and

Besides proving automorphic equivalence (

.
The strategy sketched above will yield the following result.

.

Let

,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{vol}}>0
,

,

,

and

such that the following holds.

(Uniform Gap assumption)
.

Remark 14
.

A few remarks are in order.

(i)

The uniform gap assumption is of course crucial for the result and in general very difficult to verify, except for weak perturbations of frustration-free systems, see
[
michalakis2013stability
,
nachtergaele2022quasi
]
.

(ii)

An LPPL for ground states of gapped quantum spin systems was originally proposed in

]

An exponential decay was obtained in

We refer to the discussion in
[

(iii)

In particular, in the scenario of an increasing sequence of volumes

Fix

y\in Y\end{subarray}}\,\big\lVert\Delta_{k}\big\lparen\dot{\Phi}(Y)\big\rparen\big\rVert\,{\mathrm{e}}^{p\operatorname{diam}\lparen Y\rparen}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{2pk}

Exponential clustering revisited

Finally, Gaussian filters were already used in previous proofs of the exponential clustering theorem: Ground state correlations decay in the presence of a spectral gap.
In the present context of quantum spin systems, exponential decay of correlations were proved in
[
21
]
in the infinite-volume limit (the gap refers in this case to the gap of the GNS Hamiltonian), in the limit of finite volumes in
[
15
]

[
2

but the decay is only superpolynomial.
In this section, we prove that correlations decay indeed exponentially under the sole assumption of a spectral gap, even if there is eigenvalue splitting in the ground state.

Assumption 16
.

Let
\Lambda

\Phi

As above, we denote by

the spectral projection associated with the spectral patch
\sigma_{0}
.
Note that the condition

What is needed in the proof is that

Theorem 17
.

Let

,

,

,

,

Then there exist constants

,

, such that the following holds.
For all

Proof.

For the proof, we define the filter function

The Fourier transform of

\hat{g}_{\beta}(\omega)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\beta}\,\lparen\Theta_{\gamma/2}\star\hat{\varphi}_{\beta}\rparen(\omega)=\frac{1}{2\beta\sqrt{\uppi}}\,\int_{-\infty}^{\omega-\gamma/2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{\xi^{2}}{4\beta^{2}}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\uppi}}\,\int_{-\infty}^{\frac{\omega-\gamma/2}{2\beta}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-x^{2}},

Before we continue the proof, we note some fact about the Gaussian error function.
We have

Hence, the Fourier transform of the filter function satisfies

\displaystyle\hat{g}_{\beta}(\omega)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{\uppi}\,\lvert\omega-\gamma/2\rvert}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{(\omega-\gamma/2)^{2}}{4\beta^{2}}}\qquad\text{for $\omega<\gamma/2$}

\displaystyle 1-\hat{g}_{\beta}(\omega)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{\uppi}\,(\omega-\gamma/2)}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-\frac{(\omega-\gamma/2)^{2}}{4\beta^{2}}}\qquad\text{for $\omega>\gamma/2$.}

We now decompose the correlation into three terms

\displaystyle\langle\Omega,A\,B\Omega\rangle-\langle\Omega,A\,P\,B\,\Omega\rangle={}

\displaystyle\langle\Omega,A\,P^{\perp}\,B\,\Omega\rangle

\displaystyle\big\langle\Omega,[\mathcal{J}_{H,\beta}(A),B]\,\Omega\big\rangle

\displaystyle+\big\langle\Omega,B\,\mathcal{J}_{H,\beta}(A)\,\Omega\big\rangle

Finally, the commutator

We briefly recall the setting of

The lattice is a sequence of discrete tori

The inverse Liouvillian is used to construct a unitary

across a fiducial line of the torus.
Replacing the exact inverse Liouvillian by the almost inverse Liouvillian introduced in Section

By charge conservation and again the locality lemma, the operator
(U^{\beta})^{*}\,Q_{\mathrm{R}}\,U^{\beta}-Q_{\mathrm{R}}
decomposes in two contributions at each boundary of the right half-torus.
We define the operator of charge transport as the left one

T^{\beta}=\left\lparen(U^{\beta})^{*}\,Q_{\mathrm{R}}\,U^{\beta}-Q_{\mathrm{R}}\right\rparen_{\mathrm{left}}.

(34)

More precisely,
\Tr\big\lparen P_{L}\,T_{L}^{\beta}\big\rparen
measures the amount of charge transported in the ground state across one fiducial line across the torus after threading one unit of flux in the torus – up to exponentially small errors in
L
, because the splittings we used are only unique up to exponentially small terms.

With these definitions, the proof of quantization of this quantity follows exactly the original argument of
[
2
, Section IV.B]
.
We only recall the main steps and illustrate where the results of the previous sections yield improved bounds.
The unitary

Z^{\beta}(\varphi)=(U^{\beta})^{*}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi\overline{Q}_{\mathrm{R}}^{\beta}}\,U^{\beta}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-\mathrm{i}\varphi\overline{Q}_{\mathrm{R}}^{\beta}}

factorizes as
Z^{\beta}(\varphi)=Z^{\beta}(\varphi)_{\mathrm{left}}\,Z^{\beta}(\varphi)_{\mathrm{right}}
as in the discussion above.
Note that the
“
-
”
and
“
+
”
in
[
2
]
are the analogues of
“
\mathrm{left}
”
and
“
\mathrm{right}
”
here, respectively.
Since
\mathcal{I}_{H,\beta}
is an almost inverse Liouvillian, Proposition
8
,
Z^{\beta}(\varphi)
commutes with
P
(in operator norm) up to exponentially small errors in
\beta^{-2}=L
.
By exponential clustering, Theorem
17
, it follows that
[Z^{\beta}(\varphi)_{\mathrm{left}},P]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\exp L}}{{=}}0
, where we use the notation
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\exp L}}{{=}}
to indicate equality up to exponentially small errors in
L
.
From there on, the argument runs without a change, but the errors are always exponential rather than superpolynomial, yielding that

P\,Z^{\beta}(\varphi)_{\mathrm{left}}\,P\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\exp L}}{{=}}{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi\Big\lparen P\big\lparen T^{\beta}-((U^{\beta})^{*}K^{\beta}_{\mathrm{left}}U^{\beta}-K^{\beta}_{\mathrm{left}})\big\rparen P+\overline{Q}_{\mathrm{R}}^{\beta}\Big\rparen}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi\overline{Q}_{\mathrm{R}}^{\beta}}\,P,

(35)

where
K^{\beta}_{\mathrm{left}}=\mathcal{I}_{H,\beta}\circ\big\lparen\mathcal{L}_{H}(Q_{\mathrm{R}})_{\mathrm{left}}\big\rparen
.
At
\varphi=2\uppi
, an independent argument yields that
\det_{P}Z^{\beta}(2\uppi)_{\mathrm{left}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\exp L}}{{=}}1
, where the determinant is on the range of
P
.
With this, the claim that
\Tr\big\lparen P\,T^{\beta}\big\rparen
is exponentially close to an integer follows from (
35
) by computing the trace of the exponent and using the unitary invariance of the trace.

Finally, if the sequence of states is convergent, then
p^{-1}\,\Tr\lparen P_{L}\,T_{L}\rparen
is convergent because
T_{L}
is a sufficiently local operator, see
[
bachmann2020many
, Corollary 2.3]
.
Note that
T_{L}
is obtained with the exact inverse Liouvillian here.
The Laughlin argument then implies that the limit of

is equal to
2\,\uppi\,\kappa
where

.
By Lemma

\lVert T^{\beta}_{L}-T_{L}\rVert\to 0

\beta^{-2}=L

We conclude that
p^{-1}\,\Tr\big\lparen P_{L}(T^{\beta}_{L}-T_{L})\big\rparen

0

p^{-1}\,\Tr\lparen P_{L}\,T^{\beta}_{L}\rparen

In particular the sequence
\big\lparen\frac{n_{L}}{p}\big\rparen_{L}
is eventually constant and equal to

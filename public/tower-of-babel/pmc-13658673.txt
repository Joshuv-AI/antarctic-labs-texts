Impact of Fostemsavir in Individuals with Multidrug-Resistant HIV-1, Stratified by Baseline Viral Load
Authors: Manyu Prakash, Alftan Dyson, Fangfang Du, Bo Li, Marcia Wang, Michelle Moorhouse, Bruce Gilliam, Bryn Jones
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionIn populations with undetectable or low viral load (VL) and limited treatment options, antiretrovirals with a new mechanism of action may support HIV-1 treatment success. We evaluated efficacy and safety of fostemsavir-based regimens in participants with limited treatment options and an undetectable or low baseline VL from the phase 3 BRIGHTE study.MethodsBRIGHTE included adults with multidrug-resistant HIV-1 on failing regimens with ≤ 2 fully active antiretrovirals remaining. Participants with 1–2 fully active antiretrovirals entered the randomized cohort and received open-label fostemsavir + optimized background therapy after an 8-day placebo-controlled period. This post hoc analysis assessed virologic suppression (VL < 40 copies/mL; missing = excluded), immunologic outcomes, and safety through week 240 by baseline VL (< 40, 40 to < 400, 400 to < 1000, and ≥ 1000 copies/mL).ResultsIn the randomized cohort (N = 272), 21 (8%) participants had baseline VL < 400 copies/mL, 2 with < 40 copies/mL. By week 24, 13/18 (72%), 8/10 (80%), and 121/219 (55%) participants in the < 400, 400 to < 1000, and ≥ 1000 copies/mL subgroups, respectively, had virologic suppression. At week 240, 14/14 (100%) participants with baseline VL < 400 copies/mL were suppressed. In the overall randomized cohort, CD4+ T cell count (P < 0.001) and CD4+/CD8+ ratio (P < 0.001) demonstrated a significant increase from baseline through week 240. Mean change from baseline to week 240 in CD4+ T cell count was + 294 and + 141 cells/mm3 in the < 40 and 40 to < 400 copies/mL subgroups, respectively. Mean CD4+/CD8+ ratio was 0.62 at baseline and 0.79 at week 240 in the < 40 copies/mL subgroup and 0.34 at baseline and 0.62 at week 240 in the 40 to < 400 copies/mL subgroup. Few adverse events leading to withdrawal/discontinuation were observed in the < 40 copies/mL (0/2) or 40 to < 400 copies/mL (1/19 [5%]) subgroups.ConclusionFindings support considering fostemsavir for regimen optimization in individuals with limited treatment options and undetectable/low VL, especially if safety/tolerability may be a concern.Trial RegistrationClinicalTrials.gov, NCT02362503.

Introduction

Antiretroviral therapy (ART) has significantly improved life expectancy for people with HIV-1, making it comparable to the general population [1–4]. However, a considerable comorbidity gap remains, with adults with HIV-1 living approximately 16 fewer comorbidity-free years than those without HIV-1 [5]. Incomplete immune recovery, particularly with lower CD4+ T cell counts, can contribute to an increase in comorbidities and mortality [1, 2]. Between 9% and 45% of people with HIV-1 do not achieve satisfactory immune recovery despite viral suppression [6]. This proportion may be even higher among people with multidrug-resistant (MDR) HIV-1 who are already at a higher risk of comorbidities and are faced with challenges in constructing suppressive regimens [6–8].

Low-level viremia refers to detectable but low levels of HIV-1 RNA, typically < 1000 copies/mL, despite being on ART [9]. It can contribute to ongoing immune activation, disease progression, and drug resistance development [1, 9, 10]. Therefore, promptly addressing detectable virus is important to help reduce comorbidity risk.

Effective management of people with HIV-1 can help minimize the risk of morbidity and mortality [11]. Prompt adjustment to ART upon confirmed virologic failure or persistent low-level viremia can decrease subsequent management challenges, such as a progressive deterioration of the immune landscape [9, 12, 13]. Individuals who are heavily treatment-experienced and virologically suppressed may benefit from ART optimization to help reduce toxicity and drug–drug interactions [11]. Focusing ART management on improving immune recovery could also reduce healthcare costs and utilization [14]. The availability of various antiretroviral options with distinct mechanisms of action allows for personalized treatment regimens that offer clinical advantages [11].

In the phase 3 BRIGHTE study, fostemsavir demonstrated durable virologic and immunologic responses over approximately 7 years (336 weeks) [15, 16]. Temsavir, the active form of fostemsavir, works to bind both membrane-associated and soluble gp120, preventing CD4 receptor attachment on host cells [17, 18]. This dual targeting may not only improve viral control but also reduce CD4+ T cell death, supporting immune recovery [17]. Fostemsavir-based regimens are also linked to a reduction in anti-gp120 CD4-induced antibodies compared with control ART [19].

Fostemsavir-based regimens are indicated for adults with MDR HIV-1 on a failing current regimen due to resistance, intolerance, or safety considerations [20–22]. In individuals with undetectable or low viral load and limited treatment options, inclusion of an appropriate agent with a new mechanism of action could support treatment success and bolster the immune landscape. Here, we report long-term efficacy and safety outcomes through week 240 in participants with undetectable or low viral load at baseline who received fostemsavir-based regimens.

Methods

Study Design

BRIGHTE is a large (N = 371), ongoing, multicenter, two-cohort, phase 3 trial including 113 centers in 22 countries. BRIGHTE evaluates the efficacy and safety of fostemsavir (600 mg twice daily) + optimized background therapy (OBT) in adults with MDR HIV-1 on a failing regimen with ≤ 2 fully active and approved antiretrovirals available at screening. Complete methods for the BRIGHTE study have been previously published [15, 23]. Participants were enrolled between February 23, 2015, and May 27, 2016 [24].

BRIGHTE consists of two cohorts: participants with 1 or 2 active antiretrovirals remaining were enrolled in the randomized cohort (n = 272) and received open-label fostemsavir + OBT after an 8-day blinded placebo-controlled period, and participants with no fully active antiretrovirals remaining were enrolled in the non-randomized cohort (n = 99) and received open-label fostemsavir + OBT starting on day 1. This post hoc analysis evaluated efficacy and safety among all participants recruited into the randomized cohort by baseline viral load subgroups through week 240. The analysis included participants from the randomized cohort who had a viral load ≥ 400 copies/mL at screening but had a recorded viral load of < 40 or < 400 copies/mL at baseline. The screening period was up to 60 days in duration.

Study Population

Participants were adults (aged ≥ 18 years) on failing ART, with confirmed HIV-1 RNA ≥ 400 copies/mL at screening. Confirmed HIV-1 RNA ≥ 400 copies/mL was defined as two measurements of HIV-1 RNA ≥ 400 copies/mL, the first obtained within 6 months of screening and the second obtained at screening. Participants had no viable ART regimens available due to the exhaustion of at least four of six antiretroviral classes. Temsavir susceptibility was not an inclusion criterion for the study.

Ethics

BRIGHTE was performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Study protocols, amendments, and other required documents were reviewed and approved by the Western Institutional Review Board (Puyallup, WA). All participants provided written informed consent before study initiation.

Endpoints

The primary and key secondary endpoints of BRIGHTE have previously been published [23, 24]. In this post hoc analysis, we evaluated the proportion of participants with virologic suppression (HIV-1 RNA < 40 copies/mL; missing = excluded), change from baseline in CD4+ T cell count and CD4+/CD8+ ratio, and change from baseline in biomarker concentrations (sCD14, sCD163, and d-dimer). Virologic suppression was analyzed through week 240 according to baseline viral load (HIV-1 RNA < 400, 400 to < 1000, and ≥ 1000 copies/mL). CD4+ T cell count and CD4+/CD8+ ratio were assessed in the overall cohort and according to baseline viral load (HIV-1 RNA < 40, 40 to < 400, 400 to < 1000, and ≥ 1000 copies/mL) through week 240. Cumulative safety was assessed in the safety population (all participants who received ≥ 1 dose of study drug) and by baseline viral load (HIV-1 RNA < 40, 40 to < 400, 400 to < 1000, and ≥ 1000 copies/mL).

Procedures

Plasma HIV-1 RNA, CD4+ and CD8+ T cell counts, adverse events (AEs), and laboratory chemistry and hematology were assessed at screening; baseline (day 1); day 8; and weeks 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 36, 48, and every 12 weeks thereafter in the randomized cohort. Viral load was measured at central laboratory facilities using the RealTime HIV-1 viral load assay (Abbott Molecular Inc, Des Plaines, IL). CD4+ and CD8+ T cell counts were measured using flow cytometry. CD4+/CD8+ ratio is an independent marker of clinical progression [25]. A ratio below 0.30 is associated with a significantly increased risk of severe non-AIDS-defining events or death compared with a ratio above 0.45 [25]. Therefore, improvements in mean CD4+/CD8+ ratio were informed by a cutoff of ≥ 0.30.

Biomarker concentrations were analyzed as previously described [26]. Briefly, serum or plasma samples were sent to ICON Laboratory Services (sCD14 and d-dimer) and Myriad RBM (sCD163) for biomarker testing. We measured sCD14 using the Quantikine Human sCD14 Immunoassay (R&D Systems Inc, Minneapolis, MN) and sCD163 using xMAP Technology (Luminex Corporation, Austin, TX). We used STA-Liatest D-Di (Diagnostica Stago SAS, Parsippany, NJ) to measure d-dimer.

BRIGHTE was planned to continue beyond week 96 until participants were able to access fostemsavir by other means. The study extended into the COVID-19 pandemic, affecting data collection between weeks 192 and 240. Eighty participants gained commercial access to fostemsavir and completed the study before the week 240 data cutoff.

Statistics

Efficacy and safety through week 240 were summarized across baseline viral load subgroups using descriptive statistics. Mean change from baseline was used to assess CD4+ T cell count, CD4+/CD8+ ratio, and biomarker concentrations. CD4+/CD8+ ratio was categorized as low risk (> 0.45), medium risk (0.30–0.45), and high risk (< 0.30) based on findings showing that lower CD4+/CD8+ ratio was associated with higher risk of clinical progression [25]. A one-sample t test was used to determine whether mean changes from baseline in CD4+ T cell count and CD4+/CD8+ ratio in the overall randomized cohort were statistically significant (P < 0.001) at weeks 24, 48, 96, 144, and 240 compared with baseline values; values were capped at P < 0.001.

Safety

Fostemsavir-based regimens had a favorable long-term safety and tolerability profile in people with MDR HIV-1 in the 240-week interim analysis of the overall randomized and non-randomized cohorts of BRIGHTE [27]. Across baseline viral load subgroups, 84% to 100% of participants reported an AE (Table 2). Although numbers of participants were small, the proportions experiencing a serious AE were 1/2 (50%) in the < 40 copies/mL subgroup and 6/19 (32%) in the 40 to < 400 copies/mL subgroup. Among the baseline viral load 400 to < 1000 copies/mL and ≥ 1000 copies/mL subgroups, the proportions of participants experiencing a serious AE were 6/10 (60%) and 109/241 (45%), respectively. Neither of the two participants with baseline viral load < 40 copies/mL had a drug-related AE. Through week 240, 9/19 (47%) participants with baseline viral load 40 to < 400 copies/mL experienced a drug-related AE. Constipation and headache were the drug-related AEs reported in > 10% of participants in this subgroup. In the baseline viral load 400 to < 1000 copies/mL and ≥ 1000 copies/mL subgroups, 4/10 (40%) and 100/241 (41%) participants had a drug-related AE, respectively. As reported previously, through week 240, there were 15 deaths in the randomized cohort [15]. Discussion

In individuals with undetectable or low viral load and limited treatment options, adding an antiretroviral with a new mechanism of action provides a viable option to sustain virologic suppression and prevent resistance-driven rebound. These individuals often have complex treatment histories, including multidrug resistance or intolerance, which can restrict future options [7]. A novel agent can circumvent existing resistance pathways, enhance regimen durability, and improve long-term outcomes. This aligns with clinical guidelines that emphasize individualized therapy to optimize long-term virologic and immunologic outcomes, minimize toxicity, and preserve future treatment choices [28].

In this analysis, a small number of individuals with limited treatment options and an undetectable or low viral load at baseline had high rates of virologic suppression through approximately 5 years on fostemsavir-based regimens. Significant improvements in immune parameters were consistently observed in the overall cohort. Numerical improvements were also consistently observed regardless of baseline viral load. Inflammatory biomarkers independently associated with increased risk of morbidity and mortality [29–31] remained generally stable in participants with undetectable or low viral load at baseline. Numerical improvements in sCD14 were observed in participants with baseline viral load ≥ 1000 copies/mL.

Fostemsavir-based regimens were generally well tolerated, with few discontinuations across baseline viral load subgroups. These results align with the overall 240-week BRIGHTE safety analysis, demonstrating the favorable long-term safety and tolerability of fostemsavir-based regimens, with no new safety trends after week 96 [27]. These safety findings are consistent with those observed in other trials including similar populations with limited treatment options and advanced HIV-1 [27]. Serious AEs in BRIGHTE included seven hospitalizations due to COVID-19, which occurred around the week 192 and week 240 windows, with no COVID-19-related deaths observed [27, 32]. The interpretation of serious AEs in the present analysis is limited by the small sample sizes within some of the subgroups, notably the lower viral load subgroups.

Long-term safety and tolerability are important factors when constructing or modifying ART regimens for individuals with limited treatment options who may also have comorbidities and adherence challenges [27]. Furthermore, potential drug–drug interactions between antiretroviral agents and over-the-counter or prescription medications must be considered [27]. Based on extensive data, fostemsavir has a drug–drug interaction profile that supports co-administration with most medications prescribed for HIV-1 and associated comorbidities, with no need for dose adjustment [20, 21], and demonstrates favorable tolerability in people with renal or hepatic impairment [33].

Participants with undetectable or low baseline viral load demonstrated sustained numerical improvements in CD4+ T cell count. Mean baseline CD4+ T cell counts in these subgroups were moderately high at study start (> 400 cells/mm3) and continued to improve over time. Outcomes reported here align with real-world data from the OPERA and TRIO cohorts, which found that most people starting fostemsavir-based regimens while virologically suppressed experienced immunologic improvements [34, 35]. Subgroups with baseline viral load ≥ 400 copies/mL also showed robust numerical improvements in CD4+ T cell count, consistent with previous BRIGHTE data [15]. The sustained and robust CD4+ T cell recovery observed is likely a consequence of the unique mechanism of action of fostemsavir, which effectively suppresses HIV-1 replication and reduces circulating sgp120, which has been shown to be a driver of bystander infection [36, 37]. This may explain the durable numerical immunologic gains observed among participants with any level of circulating HIV-1 viral loads.

In the randomized cohort, the proportion of participants with CD4+ T cell count ≥ 200 cells/mm3 increased from 73/272 (27%) at baseline to 118/139 (85%) at week 240. Prior analyses demonstrated that achieving CD4+ T cell count ≥ 200 cells/mm3 was associated with clinically meaningful benefits, including no new AIDS-defining events after week 48 [27]. This improvement in CD4+ T cell count occurred across populations and was observed regardless of baseline viral load and demographic characteristics including age, race, and sex [38]. Higher CD4+ T cell counts are associated with a lower long-term risk of comorbidities (including cardiovascular disease) [39–41], lower healthcare costs and resource utilization, and meaningful improvements in quality of life and survival [14, 42].

Functional improvements were also characterized by significant improvements in CD4+/CD8+ ratio over approximately 5 years in the overall randomized cohort, with numerical improvements in participants with undetectable or low baseline viral load, further supporting the positive impact on the immune landscape. A CD4+/CD8+ ratio < 0.30 vs > 0.45 has been associated with a twofold increased risk of clinical progression in people with HIV-1 [25]. By week 240, CD4+/CD8+ ratio was above the 0.30 and 0.45 thresholds in all baseline viral load subgroups. A CD4+/CD8+ ratio < 0.5 has also been independently associated with a twofold increased risk of non-AIDS-related cancers compared with a ratio ≥ 0.5 (adjusted hazard ratio 2.13; 95% CI 1.32–3.45) [43]. Subgroups with baseline viral load 40 to < 400, 400 to < 1000, and ≥ 1000 copies/mL had a CD4+/CD8+ ratio < 0.5 at baseline, which was > 0.5 by week 240.

While resistance to fostemsavir-based ART was not evaluated in this subanalysis, we have previously reported resistance patterns in the broader cohort at week 240 [15]. Accurately quantifying the role of gp120 mutations in cases of virologic failure with fostemsavir-based regimens is complex [44]. Indeed, some participants in the BRIGHTE study re-suppressed after virologic failure despite treatment-emergent genotypic and/or phenotypic evidence of reduced temsavir susceptibility [44].

Interpretation of these findings is limited by the post hoc nature of the analysis and few participants in subgroups with undetectable or low viral load at baseline, limiting our ability to draw definitive conclusions. The small number of participants in subgroups with undetectable or low viral load at baseline also precluded reliable inference; therefore, formal statistical testing within individual subgroups was not conducted.

The immune recovery observed in participants from BRIGHTE with low or undetectable baseline viral load suggests that fostemsavir may provide benefits beyond virologic suppression and supports consideration of fostemsavir when optimizing ART regimens.

Conclusion

In this post hoc analysis, we found high rates of virologic suppression and persistent improvements in the immune landscape with fostemsavir-based regimens among people with undetectable or low viral load at baseline. These observations in a small number of participants with limited treatment options are encouraging and consistent with the overall BRIGHTE study results [15]. Our findings indicate that for some individuals with undetectable or low viral load, optimizing their regimen with fostemsavir may be beneficial, especially if they have concerns about the safety or tolerability of their current regimen.

900 patients evaluated within a median of 1 day from referral in a very early arthritis clinic in France
Authors: Esther Ebstein, Pierre-Antoine Juge, Marine Forien, Leslie Benattar, Marie Péan de Ponfilly, Madeleine Franc, Bastien Léger, Anne Dupont, Manon Lemee, Chloé Jessen, Margaux Moret, Léa Bastard, Laurinda Carré, Sirine Mahjoub, Monika Klimek, Anna Molto, Marie-Christine Gallais, Mylène Dousset, Sébastien Ottaviani, Philippe Dieudé
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveIn order to fit with National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) guidelines on arthritis, we established a ‘very early arthritis clinic’ (v-EAC) to evaluate patients within 48 h of a medical referral for any suspected arthritis of <1-month duration. We describe the characteristics of patients referred to the v-EAC from 2 October 2023 to 1 October 2024.MethodsDelay of referral was recorded. A systematic and standardized clinical, biological and ultrasound evaluation of all consecutive patients was conducted. Patients and disease characteristics are described.ResultsA total of 900 patients were evaluated within a median delay of 1 day after referral: 51% were women and the median age was 55 years (IQR 42–67). Among these patients, 37% were referred by emergency departments, 28% by rheumatologists, 18% by general practitioners and 17% by other specialists. For 46% of patients, it was the first episode of a swollen joint, with a median symptom duration of 14 days (IQR 5–44) and a mean number of 1.3 (s.d. 1.9) swollen joints. Inflammatory arthritis was confirmed in 63% of cases and diagnoses were flare of immune-mediated inflammatory disease (IMID)-related arthritis in 35% of cases (half of which represented the first manifestation of an IMID-related arthritis), crystal-related arthritis in 27% (mostly gout) and infectious disease in 8.0% (including 34 bursitis and 13 septic arthritis). Joint aspiration was performed in 414 patients (46%). Corticosteroid-derivative injections were administered to 31% of patients and hospitalization was required in 10% of cases [median stay 6 days (IQR 4–8)].ConclusionA dedicated care pathway can make the NICE guidelines regarding management of arthritis reachable.Keywords: early arthritis clinic, care pathway, inflammatory rheumatism, crystal-related arthritis, NICE guidelines

Introduction

In 2018, rheumatology consultation delays in France were 45 days. Due to the current workforce crisis in rheumatology in France, and the ageing population, this delay may increase and make it necessary to optimize the care pathway.

The European League Against Rheumatism states in its first recommendation for the management of early arthritis that patients should be referred to and seen by a rheumatologist within 6 weeks after symptom onset [1]. Moreover, the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) guidelines provide evidence-based, standardized recommendations for the diagnosis, treatment and long-term management of patients with arthritis. These help reduce variability in clinical practice, promote early intervention and optimal use of therapies and ultimately improve patient outcomes and quality of care. These guidelines support cost-effective decision-making within healthcare systems while ensuring high standards of patient-centred care.

In rheumatology, the NICE guidelines recommend that adults with suspected persistent synovitis affecting more than one joint, or the small joints of the hands and feet, should be referred to rheumatology services within 3 working days of presenting in primary care [2, 3] and adults with RA and disease flares should receive advice within 1 working day of contacting rheumatology services [2].

Several early arthritis clinics (EACs) have been established worldwide to detect and treat early inflammatory arthritis [4, 5]. Despite an expected increase in outpatient costs (visits and diagnostic tests) and pharmaceutical costs, the reduction in the number and length of hospitalizations and the greater adherence to international standards of care support the effectiveness of the EAC model [6]. However, such clinics do not exist yet in France, delays in referrals differ from one EAC to another and none of this structure addresses the recommendation of particularly short delay. To our knowledge, none of these clinics reported the management of flares of a known rheumatic disease. Within this context, we established a very early arthritis clinic (v-EAC) at Bichat University Hospital [Paris; the Centre de l’Arthrite Aigue de Bichat (CAAB)].

We aimed to describe the characteristics of patients received at the EAC during the first year after opening and evaluate the feasibility of addressing NICE guidelines.

Methods

Study design, setting and participants

A longitudinal single-centre observational cohort study was conducted in our v-EAC (the CAAB). All patients with suspected arthritis of any suspected aetiology, lasting for <1 month were seen within 48 h after medical referral. Patients could be referred by any medical doctor by phone, e-mail or via a contact online form on our website (https://www.rhumatologie-bichat.com/centre-de-larthrite-aigue/) and an appointment was scheduled by a nurse dedicated to the v-EAC (M.D.). Consecutive patients from 2 October 2023 through 1 October 2024 are presented here.

All data were collected prospectively on a standardized form and reported in the institutional electronic medical record. Baseline characteristics included age, sex, BMI, history of any cardiovascular disease, chronic kidney disease, diabetes, immunosuppression, cancer, inflammatory rheumatic disease (IRD, i.e. RA, PsA, SpA), gout or calcium pyrophosphate disease, type of medical referee, clinical characteristics of the current episode (localization of arthritis, symptom duration, inflammatory local sign, fever, extra-articular symptoms, number of tender and swollen joints), previous treatment history [urate-lowering therapy (ULT), conventional DMARDs, biologic and targeted synthetic DMARDs (bDMARDs and tsDMARDs), diuretics, NSAIDs, glucocorticoids, previous intra-articular injection] and lab test results. Knee arthrocentesis was performed by our dedicated nurse (M.D.), within the framework of a dedicated collaborative practice protocol. Other joint arthrocentesis was performed by a rheumatologist. If arthrocentesis was performed, inflammatory arthritis was defined by a synovial fluid leucocyte count >1500/mm3. A trained rheumatologist systematically performed a microscopic synovial fluid analysis to identify crystal deposits [i.e. monosodium urate (MSU) or calcium pyrophosphate deposits (CPPD)].

Ultrasonography was systematically performed by an expert rheumatologist using a linear probe (4–15 mHz) with a Mylab X8 echograph (Esaote, Genoa, Italy). The presence of effusion, tenosynovitis or synovitis of symptomatic joints was systematically assessed. According to the suspected diagnosis, other US features were evaluated:

Presence of US calcifications of fibrocartilage and hyaline cartilage of the wrists and knees for CPPD [10, 11].

We prospectively collected the data about final diagnosis of the current episode, treatment received and need for subsequent hospitalization and/or follow-up. The final diagnosis of inflammatory arthritis relied on synovial fluid analysis or US evaluation.

Statistical analysis

All samples were de-identified and assigned an identification number. Sample processing and data analyses were performed with all study analysts being blinded to information concerning patients and samples. De-identified data were exported from Excel for Microsoft 365 MSO (version 2211, build 16.0.15831.20098; Microsoft, Redmond, WA, USA) to SPSS Statistics for Windows (version 25.0; IBM, Armonk, NY, USA) for statistical analysis.

Descriptive statistics were expressed as frequencies and percentages for categorical variables and as means, s.d.s, medians and interquartile ranges (IQRs) for continuous variables. Associations with occurrence of a first arthritis were tested using univariable analysis (c2 or Student’s test) and multivariable analyses (logistic regression model). The multivariable model included variables with an unadjusted P-value <0.20.

Ethics

All patients consented to participate in the study (via non-opposition, in accordance with the French reference methodology MR-003 of the Commission nationale de l’informatique et des libertés). The study was specifically approved by the Comité d’Evaluation de l’Ethique des projets de Recherche Biomédicale (CEERB) Paris Nord (Institutional Review Board of Paris North Hospitals, AP-HP, no. 12-081).

Results

Participant characteristics

Overall, 900 patients were evaluated in our EAC. Among them, 455 (51%) were women, the median age was 55 years (IQR 42–67), the median BMI was 26.4 kg/m2 (IQR 23–30), a personal history of IRD was present in 208 (23%) and gout in 89 (10%) (Table 1). A total of 334 patients (37%) were referred to our EAC by emergency departments, 256 (28%) by rheumatologist, 18% by GPs and 17% by other specialized doctors. The median delay between referral and evaluation was 1 day (IQR 0–3).

Characteristics of current early arthritis

It was the first episode of joint swelling for 415 patients (46%), lasting for a median of 14 days (IQR 4.7–44) (Table 2). Clinical examination found joint swelling in 665 patients (74%). Among them, 459 patients (69%) had monoarthritis, 144 (22%) had oligoarthritis and 64 patients (9%) had polyarthritis. Only 68 (8%) had a self-reported fever, confirmed in 6 cases at the EAC. A total of 164 patients (18%) reported at least one extra-articular manifestation, mostly cutaneous [104 patients, (63%): 47 psoriasis, 15 non-specific rash, 12 subcutaneous tophi, 11 Raynaud’s phenomenon, 4 lymphangitis, 4 erythema nodosum, 3 RA, 3 hidradenitis suppurativa, 3 skin wound, 2 urticarian rash, 1 morphoea, 1 sarcoid lesion], while 15 (2%) reported high-risk sexual behaviours and 15 (2%) reported a recent vaccination. Prior to evaluation in our EAC, 339 (38%) patients had received prior treatments for their current arthritis condition, such as NSAIDs, systemic glucocorticoids or local steroid injections.

Biological and US results

Of the 900 patients, 414 underwent arthrocentesis, involving mainly the knee (62%) and ankle (13%) joints; a leucocyte count was available for 347 samples. Based on the analysis of the synovial fluid, an inflammatory arthritis was identified in 194 (56%) patients. In the arthritis group, the median leucocyte count was 7821/mm3 (IQR 2720–100 000), with 68% neutrophils. Crystals were identified in 98 samples (25%) with 62 MSU, 30 CPPD and 6 other types (4 corticosteroid crystals and 2 lipid crystal–induced arthritis). In the non-arthritis group, the median leucocyte count was 392/mm3 (IQR 170–555), with 3.5% neutrophils.

A total of 469/803 patients had an elevated CRP >5 mg/l (58%) and 34/798 (4%) had an acute or chronic kidney disease defined by an estimated glomerular function rate ≤30 ml/min. Patients with a gouty arthritis had a median uric acid level of 459 µmol/l (IQR 386–538).

Ultrasonographic evaluation identified specific findings in 247/900 (27%) patients, including gout in 104 (12%), CPPD in 48 (5%), PMR in 23 (3%), PsA in 16 (2%), hydroxyapatitis deposit in 14 (2%) and RA in 9 (1%).

Diagnosis

After evaluation, a final diagnosis of inflammatory arthritis was confirmed in 563 patients (63%). The final diagnoses for the current flare-up were IMID-related arthritis in 311 patients (35%), evenly split between acute exacerbation of a known IMID and first-time acute IMID; crystal-related arthritis with mostly gout in 239 patients (27%); infection in 70 patients (8%), including 13 septic arthritis, 34 bursitis and 11 soft tissue infections; and 281 other conditions (31%), such as OA, tendinopathy, stress fractur, and haemarthrosis (Fig. 1).

Final diagnosis after medical evaluation of 900 patients at the v-EAC. CRD: crystal-related disease

Among the 70 patients with an infectious condition, 12 (17%) had received NSAIDs or glucocorticoids (n = 9 and 3, respectively) prior to their evaluation at the EAC.

Management of early arthritis

A total of 351 patients received articular/peri-articular steroid injection (31%), 176 patients (20%) were treated with NSAIDs and 183 (20%) with oral glucocorticoids, with a median dose of 17.5 mg/day (IQR 10–25).

Among the patients with IMID-related arthritis, conventional synthetic DMARDs were initiated or optimized in 62 patients (20%) and bDMARDs or tsDMARDs were initiated in 53 patients (17%).

For the patients with crystal-related arthritis, 160 (67%) received colchicine, 49 (20%) received oral glucocorticoids and 22 (9%) received subcutaneous anakinra (100 mg/day). Among the 162 patients with gouty arthritis, 89 had a history of gout and 36 of them were not treated with a ULT. ULT was initiated or modified in 127 patients (78%).

After their evaluation, 337 (62%) patients were scheduled for ongoing rheumatology follow-up in our rheumatology department and 88 (10%) patients required hospitalization within a few days of their v-EAC assessment, with a median hospital stay of 6 days (IQR 4–8).

Factors associated with the onset of new arthritis

For the 398 patients without any history of IMID-related disease or crystal-related diseases, inflammatory arthritis was confirmed in 214 patients (54%). Compared with patients with no inflammatory arthritis, those with confirmed inflammatory arthritis more often had an inflammatory local sign (54% vs 38%, P = 0.008), fever (17% vs 7%, P = 0.03), extra-articular manifestations (22% vs 12%, P = 0.05), high-risk sexual behaviour (5% vs 1%, P = 0.04) and a higher median CRP level [14 mg/l (IQR 3–11) vs 3 (IQR 1–11), P < 0.001] (Table 3).

In the multivariate model, factors associated with new-onset inflammatory arthritis were the presence of inflammatory local signs [odds ratio (OR) 2.76 (95% CI 1.46, 5.33)], fever [OR 8.50 (95% CI 1.94, 61.99)] and a higher CRP level [OR/5 mg/l 1.34 (95% CI 1.22, 1.54)].

Discussion

During a 1-year period, 900 patients were recruited and evaluated in our v-EAC, with an exceptionally short delay of 1 day between referral and evaluation and a median duration of symptoms of 14 days, thus meeting NICE guidelines. To our knowledge, our v-EAC is the clinic with the shortest referral delay, classically ≈2 weeks in previously reported EACs [14, 15].

The EAC model can be beneficial in terms of diagnostic accuracy, long-term patient outcomes and cost-effectiveness [16]. We aimed to develop our structure to align with this model.

Diagnosing arthritis and its underlying cause can be challenging due to factors like rapid spontaneous symptom resolution, palindromic presentations [17, 18], patient phenotypes [19] and limited access to appropriate laboratory testing (e.g. for crystal identification) [20, 21]. To optimize our pathway, we opted for direct medical referral without any prior biologic testing or imaging. Our nurse (M.D.) played a central role in this organization, by scheduling appointments, welcoming patients, performing blood test analyses and carrying out knee arthrocentesis, which is still a relatively new practice in France and should be encouraged. A rheumatology resident (A.D., M.L., C.J., M.M., L.B., L.C., S.M., M.K.) and a trained rheumatologist (S.O., M.F., E.E., L.B., M.P.D.P., P.A.J., B.L.) were assigned to the EAC every working day.

Even if we did not restrict referrals to cases of persistent synovitis, our rate of IMID-related arthritis is in line with previous reports and emphasises access to a rheumatologist for microcrystalline conditions.

Given the current challenges in healthcare access, v-EAC represents an important solution to help patients promptly receive specialized care, in line with the EULAR recommendation to evaluate suspected inflammatory arthritis within 6 weeks of symptom onset and the NICE guidelines of within 3 working days after primary care referral [1, 2]. Although France does not have a strict system of medical sectorization, ≈622 000 inhabitants were covered by our clinic.

The NICE guidelines also recommend advising patients with suspected flare of RA within 1 working day. In line with this recommendation, we made the choice to also receive patients with flare of their underlying IMID and to reinforce the treat-to-target strategy.

Early evaluation by a rheumatologist has a beneficial impact on long-term outcomes. Studies [4, 22, 23] have shown that seeing a rheumatologist within 6 weeks of symptom onset for RA, rather than 7–12 weeks (Leiden EAC and ESPOIR cohorts), was associated with a higher rate of sustained DMARD-free remission over a median 7-year follow-up. Although this was not the primary aim of our study, it may be hypothesized that the median symptom duration of 14 days in our cohort increased the likelihood of achieving remission.

From a health economic standpoint, early diagnosis of RA, within the first 12 weeks, is now proven to be associated with lower costs, regardless of antibody status [24]. While we have less data on the cost-effectiveness of such management of non-IMID conditions, we decided to also see patients with suspected crystal arthropathy, predominantly gout. Globally, this condition is largely managed in primary care [25]. However, several studies have shown that acute gout flare management adheres to treatment recommendations in only about half of cases with GPs and chronic tophaceous gout management in <20% [26]. Furthermore, prescribing ULT is cost-effective [27]. Our findings are consistent with previous data, as we were able to initiate or optimize ULT in nearly 80% of these patients. Therefore rheumatologists have a crucial role to play in the management of this condition and this type of specialized care structure could be essential to support these patients and improve their medication adherence.

EAC evaluation was followed by hospitalization in 10% of patients, mainly for infection or for pain relief, demonstrating that this structure must be associated with an inpatient service.

Our study has some limitations. First, we did not compare our population with a standard-of-care group. Second, we did not evaluate the cost-effectiveness of our structure. It is only a descriptive analysis, without follow-up evaluation.

Additional studies are needed to evaluate the long-term outcomes of these patients. What is the impact of an early diagnosis on outcome? Is remission without treatment achievable? Does it improve patient-related outcomes, and is the implementation of these structures associated with decreased sick leave? We need to evaluate the cost-effectiveness of v-EAC compared with EAC and standard care in terms of medications, pharmaceutical costs and reductions in the number and lengths of hospitalizations.

Moving forward, we aim to expand allied health training and collaborative practice protocols to further involve our nurses in the care of patients assessed in our v-EAC.

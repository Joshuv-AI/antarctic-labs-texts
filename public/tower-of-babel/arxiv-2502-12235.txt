Canted magnetism and ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} fractionalization in metallic states of the Lieb lattice Hubbard model near quarter filling
Alexander Nikolaenko, Pietro M. Bonetti, Anant Kale, Martin Lebrat, Markus Greiner, Subir Sachdev

Abstract
A recent experiment has examined ultracold, fermionic, spin-1/2 6 Li atoms in the Lieb lattice at different Hubbard repulsion U U and filling fractions ν \nu (Lebrat et al. arXiv:2404.17555 ). At ν = 1 / 2 \nu=1/2 and small U U , they observe an enhanced compressibility on the p x , y p_{x,y} sites, pointing to a flat band near the Fermi energy. At ν = 1 / 2 \nu=1/2 and large U U they observe an insulating ferrimagnet. Both small and large U U observations at ν = 1 / 2 \nu=1/2 are consistent with theoretical expectations. Surprisingly, near ν = 1 / 4 \nu=1/4 and large U U , they again observe a large p x , y p_{x,y} compressibility, pointing to a flat p x , y p_{x,y} band of fermions across the Fermi energy. Our Hartree-Fock computations near ν = 1 / 4 \nu=1/4 find states with canted magnetism (and related spiral states) at large U U , which possess nearly flat p x , y p_{x,y} bands near the Fermi level. We employ parton theories to describe quantum fluctuations of the magnetic order found in Hartree-Fock. We find a metallic state with ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} fractionalization possessing gapless, fermionic, spinless ‘chargons’ carrying ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} gauge charges which have a nearly flat p x , y p_{x,y} band near their Fermi level: this fractionalized metal is also consistent with observations. Our DMRG study does not indicate the presence of magnetic order, and so supports a fractionalized ground state. Given the conventional ferrimagnetic insulator at ν = 1 / 2 \nu=1/2 , the ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} fractionalized metal at ν = 1 / 4 \nu=1/4 represents a remarkable realization of doping-induced fractionalization.

In this section, we apply the Hartree-Fock approximation to the Hubbard model on the Lieb lattice, which reads

H=-t\sum_{\langle j,j^{\prime}\rangle,\sigma}c^{\dagger}_{j,\sigma}c_{j^{\prime},\sigma}+U\sum_{j}n_{j,\uparrow}n_{ij,\downarrow}\,,

(20)

where

(
c_{j,\sigma}
) creates (annihilates) a fermion on Lieb lattice site
j
with spin projection
\sigma
, the notation
\langle j,j^{\prime}\rangle
restricts the sum to those pairs of sites

that are nearest neighbors,
U>0
is a contact repulsive interaction, and
n_{j,\sigma}=c^{\dagger}_{j,\sigma}c_{j,\sigma}\equiv\sum_{\sigma}n_{j,\sigma}
. In Fig.
2

c_{j,\sigma}
fermions live. This is composed of three sublattices, which we name

,
p_{x}
, and
p_{y}

and

orbitals on

and
p_{x,y}
sublattices, respectively. In the noninteracting limit (

) we can diagonalize (

) exactly and obtain the band structure shown in Fig.
2
(b). We get three bands: a perfectly flat band at zero energy and two particle-hole symmetric bands linearly touching the flat one at
\mathbf{k}=(\pi,\pi)

Within the Hartree-Fock approximation, we mean-field decouple the Hubbard interaction in a spin-rotationally invariant way as
[
31
,
32
,
33
]

Un_{j,\uparrow}n_{j,\downarrow}\simeq-\delta\mu_{j}\,c^{\dagger}_{j}c_{j}+\bm{\Delta}_{j}\cdot c^{\dagger}_{j}\bm{\sigma}c_{j}+\frac{\delta\mu_{j}^{2}}{U}-\frac{|\bm{\Delta}_{j}|^{2}}{U}\,,

(21)

where
\bm{\sigma}=(\sigma^{1},\sigma^{2},\sigma^{3})
are the Pauli matrices. The following self-consistency relations hold

\displaystyle\delta\mu_{j}=-\frac{U}{2}\langle c^{\dagger}_{j}c_{j}\rangle\,,

(22a)

\displaystyle\bm{\Delta}_{j}=\frac{U}{2}\langle c^{\dagger}_{j}\bm{\sigma}c_{j}\rangle\,.

(22b)

The expectation values are computed using the (quadratic) mean-field Hamiltonian

H_{\mathrm{MF}}=-t\sum_{\langle j,j^{\prime}\rangle}c^{\dagger}_{j}c_{j^{\prime}}+\sum_{j}\left[-(\mu+\delta\mu_{j})\,c^{\dagger}_{j}c_{j}+\bm{\Delta}_{j}\cdot c^{\dagger}_{j}\bm{\sigma}c_{j}\right]\,.

(23)

In the above Hamiltonian we have added a chemical potential
\mu
to fix the total filling
\nu=\frac{1}{2N_{s}}\sum_{j}\langle c^{\dagger}_{j}c_{j}\rangle
, where

is the total number of sites.

From this point on, we relabel the sites
j
as

, where

labels a Bravais vector, and

,
p_{x}
,
p_{y}
the three sites in the unit cell. The coordinates of site

, with

III.1
Canted Ferromagnetic Higgs phase

III.2
Ferromagnetic Higgs phase

In the ferromagnetic Higgs phase, we find
\langle\bm{H}_{\mathfrak{j},\alpha}\rangle=H_{0}(0,0,1)
, with
\delta\mu_{p_{x}}=\delta\mu_{p_{y}}=\delta\mu_{d}\equiv\delta\mu
. The chargon action is obtained from action (
31

\theta=0
,
H_{d}=H_{p_{x}}=H_{p_{y}}=H_{0}
and
\delta\mu_{p_{x}}=\delta\mu_{p_{y}}=\delta\mu_{d}=\delta\mu

\psi_{j}\to e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}\sigma^{3}}\psi_{j}

\chi^{ss^{\prime}}_{jj^{\prime}}

33
) by setting
Q=0
. We furthermore observe
\chi_{\tau}=\chi_{0}\,\mathrm{diag}(1,1,1,-1,-1,-1)
, with
\chi_{0}\simeq\frac{1}{2}
. This implies that in this case we have a U(1) spin liquid.

Within this gauge choice, the PSG acts trivially, that is, all symmetries can be implemented without resorting to a gauge transformation.

It is worthwhile to remark that in this case the spinon action simply describes free bosons, which will realize a Bose-Einstein condensate at
T=0
, rendering the ferromagnetic Higgs phase always unstable to ferromagnetic long range order.

III.5
(q,0)
Spiral Higgs phase

The spiral Higgs phase is characterized by a Higgs condensate of the form

\langle\bm{H}_{\mathfrak{j},\alpha}\rangle=\begin{cases}H_{d}\,(\sin(\mathbf{Q}\cdot\mathbf{R}_{\mathfrak{j},d}),0,\cos(\mathbf{Q}\cdot\mathbf{R}_{\mathfrak{j},d}))&\alpha=d\,,\\

V_{j}=e^{-i\frac{\phi_{j}}{2}\sigma^{2}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\psi}[\psi,\bar{\psi}]=-T\sum_{\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbf{k}}\,\Psi^{\dagger}_{k}\left[i\omega_{n}\mathbbm{1}_{6}-\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}A^{\mathbf{Q}}_{\mathbf{k}}-H&B_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mathbf{Q}}\\

0&0&0\end{array}\right)\,,

\displaystyle H=\mathrm{diag}(H_{d},H_{p_{x}},H_{p_{y}})\,.

(40i)

Evaluating the coefficients in Eq. (

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{z}[z,z^{*}]=

\hline\cr Q_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}&P_{-\mathbf{k}}^{T}\end{array}\right)\right]Z_{k}\,,

\displaystyle P_{\mathbf{k}}=

\displaystyle\chi_{\tau}=

(41i)

where, similarly to the previous sections,
\bar{\lambda}_{d}

Schwinger boson theory

We start this section by introducing a
t
-
J
Hamiltonian, corresponding to the large-
U
limit of Eq. (
20
)

H=-tP\sum_{\langle j,j^{\prime}\rangle,\sigma}c^{\dagger}_{j,\sigma}c_{j^{\prime},\sigma}P+J\sum_{\langle j,j^{\prime}\rangle}\bm{S}_{j}\cdot\bm{S}_{j^{\prime}},

(42)

where
t
is the hopping term and
J>0
is a Heisenberg interaction between the spins, and
P
are the projection operators on the basis with no double occupancy. The fermions described by
c_{j,\sigma}
are subject to the constraint
\sum_{\sigma}c^{\dagger}_{j,\sigma}c_{j,\sigma}\leq 1
. To enforce this constraint, we use the decomposition (
19
) of the fermionic operator
c_{i\sigma}=b_{i\sigma}f^{{\dagger}}_{i}
in terms of the Schwinger boson and the fermionic spinless chargon, with a constraint at each site
b_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}b_{i\sigma}+f^{{\dagger}}_{i}f_{i}=1
. By introducing auxiliary fields and treating them with the saddle-point approximation, we arrive at the following mean-field Hamiltonian:

H_{mf}=\sum_{\langle j,j^{\prime}\rangle,\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\left(-Q_{j,j^{\prime}}\epsilon_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}b_{j\sigma}^{\dagger}b_{j^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}+(P_{j,j^{\prime}}+R_{j,j^{\prime}})\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}b_{j\sigma}^{\dagger}b_{j^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}+\frac{2t}{J}P_{j,j^{\prime}}f^{{\dagger}}_{j}f_{j^{\prime}}+c.c.\right)+\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}(b_{j\sigma}^{\dagger}b_{j\sigma}+f^{{\dagger}}_{j}f_{j}-1).

(43)

The mean-field parameters are defined in the following way:

\begin{cases}&Q_{j,j^{\prime}}=\frac{J}{2}\epsilon_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\langle b_{j\sigma}b_{j^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle\\
&P_{j,j^{\prime}}=\frac{J}{2}\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\langle b_{j\sigma}^{\dagger}b_{j^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}\rangle\\
&R_{j,j^{\prime}}=t\langle f_{j}^{\dagger}f_{j}^{\prime}\rangle.\\
\end{cases}

(44)

In the most general case, the mean-field parameters depend on the position indexes
(j,j^{\prime})
. However, in the rest of the paper, we assume the system does not break any translational invariance. This allows us to drop indexes. We also note that
Q_{j,j^{\prime}}=-Q_{j^{\prime},j}
so we choose the directions of the field within the unit cell as shown in Fig.
8
. There are three distinct Lagrange multipliers
\lambda_{d},\lambda_{p_{x}},\lambda_{p_{y}}
on each site in the unit cell.

\end{array}\right),

and

.

has the same form as the

P_{2}e^{-ik_{y}/2}+P_{4}e^{ik_{y}/2}&0&\lambda_{p_{y}}+\mu\\
\end{array}\right)\psi_{k}.

(48)

We also assume that there is no nematic phase, so mean-field parameters in different directions have the same absolute value:
|Q_{1}|=|Q_{2}|=|Q_{3}|=|Q_{4}|
and the same for

and

\begin{cases}&Q=\sum_{k}\left(\langle b_{d,\uparrow}(k)b_{p_{x},\downarrow}(-k)\rangle e^{-ik_{x}/2}-\langle b_{d,\downarrow}(-k)b_{p_{x},\uparrow}(k)\rangle e^{ik_{x}/2}\right)\\

The bosonic Hamiltonian is more subtle to diagonalize. Using the similar transformation

by taking the corresponding eigenvectors of

VI
Discussion

This paper has introduced a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
fractionalized metallic state for the Lieb lattice Hubbard model at large Hubbard repulsion near filling fraction
\nu=1/4
which preserves all the symmetries of the lattice model. This is proposed as an attractive candidate to explain observations on ultracold atoms
[
5
]
, including the unexpected enhanced compressibility on the
p_{x,y}
sites near
\nu=1/4
. This fractionalized metal is obtained by doping a conventional ferrimagnetic insulator at
\nu=1/2
, and so realizes the long-sought phenomenon of doping-induced fractionalization.

The structure of the fractionalized insulator is most easily understood from the parton construction in Section
IV
, which fractionalizes the spin-1/2 fermion
c_{\sigma}
into a bosonic spinon
b_{\sigma}
and a fermionic spinless ‘chargon’
f
.
The nearest-neighbor exchange prefers a condensate of
Q_{ij}\sim\epsilon_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}b_{i\sigma}b_{j\sigma^{\prime}}
, where
i,j
are nearest-neighbors. On the other hand, the hopping term prefers a condensate of
P_{ij}\sim b_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}b_{j\sigma}
. The presence of both
Q_{ij}
and
P_{ij}
condensate leaves only the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
gauge symmetry
b_{i\sigma}\rightarrow\pm b_{i\sigma}
unhiggsed, leading to the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
fractionalized state with the same anyon sectors as the toric code. The
b_{\sigma}
excitations then represent the gapped spinon sector (the
e
particles) of the fractionalized metal. When the
b_{\sigma}
condense, all fractionalized excitations are confined in the state with canted magnetic order in Fig.
1
. In the fractionalized state, the chargons have effective filling fraction
\nu_{f}=1-2\nu
, and so
\nu_{f}=1/2
when
\nu=1/4
. The chargon band structure is shown in Fig.
9
, and there is a flat
p_{x,y}
flat band near the Fermi level at
\nu=1/4
, as hinted in Ref.
[
5
]
.

Establishing the existence of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
fractionalized state in future experiments requires lower temperatures and the observation of a spin gap, along with the absence of a ferromagnetic moment present in the canted magnetic state.
Recent experimental work
[
38
]
has demonstrated progress towards achieving temperatures comparable to the expected spin gap.
The spiral states can be detected by measuring the susceptibility to Ising-nematic ordering.

The nearly flat
p_{x,y}
band near the Fermi level in the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
fractionalized metal indicates that this phase is likely not stable down to zero temperature. Eventual instabilities to charge density wave order and superconductivity appear likely and are interesting topics for further study.

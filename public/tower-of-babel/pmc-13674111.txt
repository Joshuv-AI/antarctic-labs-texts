Comparison of outcomes after DA‐EPOCH‐R or GMALL high‐intensity therapy for front‐line treatment of Burkitt lymphoma: A retrospective cohort
Authors: Eduard Schulz, Florian Posch, Kristina Friedrich, Peter Neumeister, Katharina T. Prochazka
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In this retrospective cohort of adult Burkitt lymphoma, DA‐EPOCH‐R showed no signal of inferior survival or relapse risk compared with GMALL‐based high‐intensity therapy. Outcomes were driven mainly by established prognostic factors, particularly age, advanced stage and Burkitt lymphoma International Prognostic Index.

To the Editor,

Burkitt lymphoma (BL) is a rapidly proliferative mature B‐cell lymphoma for which cure depends on prompt initiation of effective multiagent immunochemotherapy. 1 In Central Europe, newly diagnosed adult BL has traditionally been treated with German Multicenter Study Group for Adult Acute Lymphoblastic Leukeamia (GMALL) based high‐intensity protocols, supported by the GMALL‐B‐ALL/NHL2002 trial, the largest prospective study conducted in adult BL/leukaemia and a benchmark dataset for outcomes with intensive chemoimmunotherapy. 2 By contrast, dose‐adjusted etoposide, prednisone, vincristine, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin and rituximab (DA‐EPOCH‐R) has emerged as an alternative frontline strategy based on prospective single‐arm data showing high cure rates with a less dose‐intensive approach. 3 , 4 However, DA‐EPOCH‐R has not been widely adopted in German‐speaking countries, where GMALL‐based therapy remains an established standard.

In the randomized Dutch‐Belgian Haemato‐Oncology Cooperative Group/Swiss Group for Clinical Cancer Research (HOVON/SAKK) trial, outcomes did not differ significantly between R‐CODOX‐M/R‐IVAC, an intensive alternating chemoimmunotherapy regimen comprising rituximab plus cyclophosphamide, vincristine, doxorubicin, and high‐dose methotrexate (R‐CODOX‐M), alternating with rituximab plus ifosfamide, etoposide, and high‐dose cytarabine (R‐IVAC), and DA‐EPOCH‐R among patients without central nervous system (CNS) involvement. 5 Similarly, in the original BL International Prognostic Index (BL‐IPI) study, progression‐free survival (PFS) did not differ significantly between high‐intensity regimens and DA‐EPOCH‐R; outcomes were more strongly determined by BL‐IPI risk group. 6 In that analysis, 3‐year PFS rates for the low‐, intermediate‐ and high‐risk BL‐IPI groups were 88%, 67% and 61% after R‐CODOX‐M/R‐IVAC, and 87%, 73% and 51% after DA‐EPOCH‐R respectively. Direct comparative data between DA‐EPOCH‐R and GMALL‐based therapy are limited, 7 leaving uncertainty about whether DA‐EPOCH‐R provides comparable disease control in this treatment context.

We performed a retrospective single‐centre cohort study of consecutive adult patients with BL treated at the Division of Haematology, Medical University of Graz, Austria, between May 2000 and July 2022. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Medical University of Graz (No. 34‐429 ex21/22 1233‐2022). Data were extracted from a centralized electronic medical record system. Of 54 identified patients, five (9%) did not receive antineoplastic therapy, mainly because of palliative intent, and were excluded from outcome analyses. The final cohort comprised 49 patients treated with either GMALL‐based therapy or DA‐EPOCH‐R as frontline treatment. One human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) positive patient received the lower dose short‐course EPOCH‐R regimen with double‐dose rituximab described by Dunleavy et al. 3

The primary analysis compared outcomes between patients receiving GMALL‐based therapy and those receiving DA‐EPOCH‐R. Overall survival (OS) and PFS were estimated by Kaplan–Meier methods and compared by log‐rank tests. Cumulative incidences of progression/relapse, lymphoma‐related death and death from other causes were estimated with competing‐risk methods and compared by Gray tests. Follow‐up was truncated at 10 years because GMALL was used predominantly in earlier calendar years, resulting in longer potential follow‐up than for DA‐EPOCH‐R. Cox regression was used for OS, with multivariable models restricted a priori to avoid overfitting. Additional statistical details are provided in the Supporting Information.

Twenty‐seven patients (55%) received GMALL‐based therapy and 22 (45%) received DA‐EPOCH‐R. Treatment practice changed substantially over time: GMALL was used almost exclusively in the 2000s, whereas DA‐EPOCH‐R became the dominant institutional regimen after approximately 2014 (Figure S1). Baseline characteristics were well balanced between groups (Table S1). Median age was 55 (interquartile range [IQR], 38–72) years in the GMALL group and 57 (IQR, 43–65) years in the DA‐EPOCH‐R group. Stage III/IV disease was present in 17/27 (63%) and 15/22 (68%) patients, respectively. The BL‐IPI distribution was similar between groups, with low‐, intermediate‐ and high‐risk disease observed in 1 (5%), 10 (48%) and 10 (48%) GMALL‐treated patients, compared with 4 (20%), 8 (40%) and 8 (40%) patients treated with DA‐EPOCH‐R respectively. CNS involvement at diagnosis was uncommon, occurring in one patient in the DA‐EPOCH‐R group. Bone marrow involvement was numerically more frequent among GMALL‐treated patients (12/25 [48%] vs. 4/22 [18%]; p = 0.063).

After a median follow‐up of 6.3 years, 19 deaths and 10 progression/relapse events had occurred. Estimated 5‐year OS was 65% in the overall cohort (Figure 1; Table S2) and did not differ significantly between treatment groups (Table S3). Five‐year OS was 61% after GMALL‐based therapy and 75% after DA‐EPOCH‐R (log‐rank p = 0.344). PFS was also similar, with estimated 1‐, 3‐ and 5‐year PFS rates of 74%, 69% and 61% after GMALL‐based therapy compared with 75%, 75% and 75% after DA‐EPOCH‐R respectively (log‐rank p = 0.312).

Competing‐risk analyses (Figure 1C,D; Table S3) did not show a significant difference in progression/relapse incidence between GMALL‐based therapy and DA‐EPOCH‐R (Gray p = 0.897). Progression/relapse occurred exclusively in patients with stage III/IV disease and was uniformly followed by lymphoma‐related death. The cumulative incidence of death from other causes was higher after GMALL‐based therapy (Gray p < 0.0001), whereas no such deaths were observed in the DA‐EPOCH‐R group during available follow‐up (Figure 1D). This finding should be interpreted cautiously because of the retrospective design, treatment‐era imbalance and limited number of events.

In exploratory univariable analyses (Table S4), DA‐EPOCH‐R was not associated with inferior OS (hazard ratio [HR] = 0.61, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.21–1.72; p = 0.349) or higher progression/relapse incidence (subdistribution HR = 0.88, 95% CI: 0.25–3.12; p = 0.839). Adverse predictors of OS included increasing age (HR = 1.20 [per 5‐year increase], 95% CI: 1.03–1.39; p = 0.020), Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status ≥2 (HR = 4.70, 95% CI: 1.31–16.88; p = 0.018), stage III/IV disease (HR = 4.36, 95% CI: 1.26–15.06; p = 0.020) and higher BL‐IPI (HR = 2.07 per point increase, 95% CI: 1.18–3.65; p = 0.012).

To limit model overfitting given the low number of events, we evaluated two predefined multivariable Cox models with similar performance (Figure 1E; Table S5): one including treatment, age and disease stage and another including treatment and the continuous BL‐IPI score. In both models, DA‐EPOCH‐R was not associated with inferior OS compared with GMALL‐based therapy. In model 1, increasing age (HR = 1.22 [per 5‐year increase], 95% CI: 1.04–1.42; p = 0.012) and stage III/IV disease (HR = 4.67, 95% CI: 1.34–16.27; p = 0.016) remained independently associated with worse OS, while DA‐EPOCH‐R showed no adverse effect on survival (HR = 0.50, 95% CI: 0.18–1.41; p = 0.191). Similarly, in model 2, higher BL‐IPI retained independent prognostic significance (HR = 1.91 [per point increase], 95% CI: 1.09–3.38; p = 0.025), whereas DA‐EPOCH‐R again was not associated with inferior OS (HR = 0.40, 95% CI: 0.11–1.45; p = 0.165).

These data address a practical treatment question for centres in which GMALL‐based therapy remains familiar and DA‐EPOCH‐R is less commonly used. In this real‐world cohort, consistent with prior comparisons of DA‐EPOCH‐R with other high‐intensity approaches such as R‐CODOX‐M/R‐IVAC, DA‐EPOCH‐R was not associated with inferior survival or higher relapse risk compared with GMALL‐based therapy. Outcomes were instead driven by established prognostic factors, particularly age, advanced stage and BL‐IPI. The absence of an apparent efficacy disadvantage is clinically relevant because DA‐EPOCH‐R omits several components of highly dose‐intensive BL regimens and may therefore represent a more manageable treatment approach for selected older or less fit patients. 1 However, primary CNS involvement remains a distinct high‐risk clinical scenario that has not been sufficiently evaluated for DA‐EPOCH‐R and continues to pose a major therapeutic challenge even with high‐intensity regimens. 5 , 6 , 7

Compared with the prospective GMALL‐B‐ALL/NHL2002 trial, 2 our cohort was older (median age 55 vs. 42 years) but had a broadly similar proportion of advanced‐stage disease (stage III/IV: 65% vs. 71%). CNS involvement was less frequent in our series (2% vs. 10%), precluding meaningful subgroup comparison. In GMALL‐B‐ALL/NHL2002, 5‐year OS/PFS were 80%/75% overall but declined to 62%/60% in patients >55 years despite dose‐reduced therapy. In this context, the 5‐year OS/PFS estimates of 65%/66% observed in our older real‐world cohort appear clinically consistent with the GMALL experience, and both datasets identify age and advanced disease as major adverse prognostic factors rather than providing evidence that regimen selection alone determines outcome.

This study is limited by its retrospective single‐centre design, small sample size and non‐randomized treatment allocation. The small number of events also limited statistical modelling; exploratory univariable analyses included multiple candidate variables and were not adjusted for multiplicity, and should therefore be interpreted descriptively rather than as confirmatory evidence of independent prognostic effects. The comparison is also partly confounded by calendar time, because DA‐EPOCH‐R was introduced later in institutional practice. Improvements in diagnostics, supportive care, infection management, imaging, CNS assessment and general lymphoma care over the 22‐year period may have influenced outcomes and represent an important limitation of the study. We therefore regard these findings as hypothesis‐generating rather than definitive. Nevertheless, the balanced baseline characteristics, long follow‐up and consistency of univariable and multivariable analyses in line with previous studies support the conclusion that DA‐EPOCH‐R is a credible frontline alternative to GMALL‐based therapy, especially for adult patients with BL without primary CNS involvement, including selected older or less fit patients. Prospective studies and larger registry‐based analyses should specifically evaluate DA‐EPOCH‐R against GMALL‐based approaches, because this is the comparison most relevant to practice in Germany, Austria and other Central European settings.

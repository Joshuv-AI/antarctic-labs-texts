Non-abelian Geometric Quantum Energy Pump
Yang Peng

Abstract
We introduce a non-abelian geometric quantum energy pump realized by a transitionless geometric quantum drive–a time-dependent Hamiltonian supplemented by a counterdiabatic term generated by a prescribed trajectory on a smooth control manifold–that coherently transports states within a degenerate subspace. When the coordinates of the trajectory are independently addressable by external drives, the net energy transferred between drives is set by the non-abelian Berry-curvature tensor. The trajectory-averaged pumping power is separately controlled by the initial state and by the Hamiltonian topology through the Euler class. We outline an implementation with artificial atoms, which are realizable on various platforms including trapped atoms/ions, superconducting circuits, and semiconductor quantum dots. The resulting energy pump can serve as a quantum transducer or charger, and as a metrological tool for measuring phase coherences in quantum states.

Energy pumping

Note that the driving trajectory

depends on the initial driving phases

,
where we introduced instantaneous pumping power (averaged over initial phases) from drive

to drive
\mu

\overline{P}_{\nu\mu}(t)=\sum_{\alpha\beta}c^{*}_{\alpha}c_{\beta}\overline{F}^{\alpha\beta}_{\nu\mu}\dot{f}^{\nu}(t)\dot{f}^{\mu}(t).

Here, we have introduced the averaged non-abelian Berry curvature
\overline{F}^{\alpha\beta}_{\mu\nu}=\int d\varphi^{\mu}d\varphi^{\nu}\,F^{\alpha\beta}_{\mu\nu}/(4\pi^{2})

(\varphi^{\mu},\varphi^{\nu})

Since

is an instantaneous eigenstate, we can apply the Hellmann–Feynman theorem, and write the integrand as

. This term averages to zero over random initial phases if one takes the parametrization in Eq. (

Conclusions and outlook.
—
In this work, we introduced the non-abelian geometric quantum energy pump, by exploiting a transitionless geometric quantum drive that evolves a quantum state within its degenerate subspace. The pump provides a steady, persistent and highly tunable pumping power, which satisfies the criteria being an ideal pump. We showed that the pumping power has a quantum geometric meaning as it is related to the non-abelian Berry curvature tensor. We proposed experimental realization of such a pump based on an artificial atom controlled by multiple independent driving fields, which has already been realized in various physical systems like trapped atoms/ions, superconducting circuits, and semiconductor quantum dots.

We used a two-tone drive example to compute the pumped energy into one of the drive explicitly, and demonstrated both analytically and numerically that the pumping power is controlled independently by the initial state property, and the Euler class, the topological property, of the system Hamiltonian
H_{0}
.

The quantum energy pump we proposed is potentially useful in quantum technology, as a quantum transducer or a quantum charger. Alternatively, since the pumping power is sensitive to the phase coherence property of the initial state inside the degenerate manifold, the quantum energy pump setup could be also used to extract phase coherence information of a quantum state. These practical applications require connecting the quantum energy pump to external devices, such as cavities, qubits etc., which requires explicit modeling and is beyond the scope of this work. We shall explore these directions in future.

, and
consider the Schrödinger equation

i\partial_{t}\ket{\psi(t)}=H(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t})\ket{\psi(t)},

(19)

under the initial condition
\ket{\psi(0)}=\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{0}}^{n}\ket{\psi(0)}
,
then
\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t}}^{n}\ket{\psi(t)}=\ket{\psi(t)}

at any time
t
. Thus,
\mathcal{A_{t}}
is the counterdiabatic term.

Proof.

We can consider an infinitesimal
\delta t\to 0
time evolution
(neglecting terms
o(\delta t^{2})
)

\ket{\psi(t+\delta t)}=\ket{\psi(t)}-i\delta t\left[H_{0}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t})+\mathcal{A}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t})\right]\ket{\psi(t)},

(20)

and compute

\displaystyle\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t+\delta t}}^{n}\ket{\psi(t+\delta t)}

\displaystyle=\left[\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t}}^{n}+\delta t\partial_{t}\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t}}^{n}\right]\left[1-i\delta tH_{0}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t})-i\delta t\mathcal{A}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t})\right]\ket{\psi(t)}

(21)

\displaystyle=\left[\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t}}^{n}-\delta t\left(i\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t}}^{n}H_{0}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t})+i\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t}}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t})-\partial_{t}\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t}}^{n}\right)\right]\ket{\psi(t)}.

(22)

Note that
[H_{0}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t}),\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t}}^{n}]=0

and
\mathcal{A}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t})
has no matrix elements
between states at the same energy, namely
\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t}}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{t}(\varphi_{t})\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t}}^{n}=0
, by Theorem
1
.
Assuming
\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t}}^{n}\ket{\psi(t)}=\ket{\psi(t)}
,
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{t}(\varphi_{t})\ket{\psi(t)}

\displaystyle=i(\partial_{t}\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t}}^{n})\ket{\psi(t)}.

and thus

\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t+\delta t}}^{n}\ket{\psi(t+\delta t)}=\left[1-i\delta t\left(H_{0}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t})+\mathcal{A}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t})\right)\right]\ket{\psi(t)}=\ket{\psi(t+\delta t)}.

(23)

Hence,
\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t}}\ket{\psi(t)}=\ket{\psi(t)}

whenever
\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{0}}^{n}\ket{\psi(0)}=\ket{\psi(0)}
.
∎

Because of Theorem
2
, if one takes
\{\ket{n\alpha}\}
as a basis for the eigensubspace of
H_{0}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{0})
at energy
\epsilon_{n}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{0})
, we can define a parallelly transported basis along the trajectory
\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t}
as
\{\ket{n\alpha(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t})}\}
, with
\ket{n\alpha(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{0})}\equiv\ket{n\alpha}
. Explictly, we can write

\ket{n\alpha(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t})}=\mathcal{W}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t}\leftarrow\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{0})\ket{n\alpha(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{0})},

(24)

with Wilson line operator generated by the KGP
\mathcal{A}_{t}

\mathcal{W}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t}\leftarrow\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{0})=\mathcal{T}\exp(-i\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\,\mathcal{A}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t^{\prime}}))=\mathcal{P}\exp(-i\int_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t}}d\boldsymbol{\varphi}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{\varphi})),

(25)

where
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}=(\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{d})
. This means that from an initial state
\ket{\psi(0)}
in this subpsace, parametrized as

\ket{\psi(0)}=\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}\ket{n\alpha(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{0})},

(26)

the time-evolved state can be written as

\ket{\psi(t)}=e^{-i\int_{0}^{t}ds\,\epsilon_{n}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{s})}\mathcal{W}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t}\leftarrow\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{0})=e^{-i\int_{0}^{t}ds\,\epsilon_{n}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{s})}\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}\ket{n\alpha(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t})}.

(27)

This implies that the expectation value of any operator
\mathcal{F}
, the expectation value can be computed as

\bra{\psi(s)}\mathcal{F}\ket{\psi(s)}=\sum_{\alpha\beta}c_{\alpha}^{*}c_{\beta}F^{\alpha\beta},\quad F^{\alpha\beta}=\bra{\alpha}\mathcal{F}\ket{\beta}.

(28)

The following two theorems are used to derive the energy pumping formula (Eq. (6)) of the main text. Particularly, Theorem
4
relates the derivative
\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{t}
to the non-abelian Berry curvature tensor
\mathcal{F}_{t\mu}
.

Theorem 3
.

Let us construct a generic projector
\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}=\sum_{m\in S}\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{m}

as a partial sum of indices over a set
S
. Then

\partial_{\mu}\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}=-i[\mathcal{A}_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}),\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}].

(29)

Proof.

Let us compute the right hand side of the above equation

\displaystyle-i[\mathcal{A}_{\mu},\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n}\sum_{m\in S}\left[[\partial_{\mu}\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{n},\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{n}],\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{m}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n}\sum_{m\in S}\left((\partial_{\mu}\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{n})\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{n}\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{m}-\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{n}(\partial_{\mu}\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{n})\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{m}-\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{m}(\partial_{\mu}\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{n})\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{n}+\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{m}\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{n}(\partial_{\mu}\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{n})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m\in S}\left[(\partial_{\mu}\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{m})\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{m}+\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{m}(\partial_{\mu}\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{m})\right]-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m\in S}\sum_{n\neq m}\left[\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{n}(\partial_{\mu}\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{n})\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{m}+\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{m}(\partial_{\mu}\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{n})\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{n}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m\in S}\sum_{n\neq m}\left[\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{n}(\partial_{\mu}\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{m})+(\partial_{\mu}\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{m})\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{n}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m\in S}\left[(1-\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{m})(\partial_{\mu}\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{m})+(\partial_{\mu}\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{m})(1-\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{m})\right]

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}.

(30)

Note that in the third line, we have used the property that
\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{n}(\partial_{\mu}\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{n})\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}^{n}=0.

∎

Theorem 4
.

Define the subspace projected Kato potential

\mathcal{A}_{\mu}^{(\Pi)}(\boldsymbol{\varphi})=\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}\mathcal{A}_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{\varphi})\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}.

(31)

Then

\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}(\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{\nu}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}))\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}=\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}(\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{\nu}^{(\Pi)}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}))\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}-\mathcal{F}_{\mu\nu}^{(\Pi)}(\boldsymbol{\varphi})

(32)

where the last term is the non-abelian Berry curvature defined on
this subspace

\mathcal{F}_{\mu\nu}^{(\Pi)}(\boldsymbol{\varphi})=i\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}[\partial_{\mu}\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}},\partial_{\nu}\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}]\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}.

(33)

Proof.

Let us compute the projected derivative of the Kato gauge potential
(we drop the notation of explicit
\boldsymbol{\varphi}
-dependence
in the following for simplicity)

\displaystyle\Pi(\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{\nu})\Pi

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}\left(\Pi\mathcal{A}_{\nu}\Pi\right)-(\partial_{\mu}\Pi)\mathcal{A}_{\nu}\Pi-\Pi\mathcal{A}_{\nu}(\partial_{\mu}\Pi)

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{\nu}^{(\Pi)}+i[\mathcal{A}_{\mu},\Pi]\mathcal{A}_{\nu}\Pi+i\Pi\mathcal{A}_{\nu}[\mathcal{A}_{\mu},\Pi]

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{\nu}^{(\Pi)}+i\mathcal{A}_{\mu}\Pi\mathcal{A}_{\nu}\Pi-i\Pi\mathcal{A}_{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{\nu}\Pi+i\Pi\mathcal{A}_{\nu}\mathcal{A}_{\mu}\Pi-i\Pi\mathcal{A}_{\nu}\Pi\mathcal{A}_{\mu}.

Applying projector
\Pi
on from left and right on both sides of
the above equation, we obtain

\Pi(\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{\nu})\Pi=\Pi(\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{\nu}^{(\Pi)})\Pi-i\left[\Pi\mathcal{A}_{\mu}(1-\Pi)\mathcal{A}_{\nu}\Pi-\Pi\mathcal{A}_{\nu}(1-\Pi)\mathcal{A}_{\mu}\Pi\right]

(34)

From the non-abelian Berry curvature defined in terms of the projector in Eq. (
33
),
and using Theorem
3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\mu\nu}^{(\Pi)}

\displaystyle=i\Pi[\partial_{\mu}\Pi,\partial_{\nu}\Pi]\Pi=-i\Pi\left[[\mathcal{A}_{\mu},\Pi],[\mathcal{A}_{\nu},\Pi]\right]\Pi

\displaystyle=-i\Pi\left[\mathcal{A}_{\mu}\Pi\mathcal{A}_{\nu}\Pi-\Pi\mathcal{A}_{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{\nu}\Pi-\mathcal{A}_{\mu}\Pi\Pi\mathcal{A}_{\nu}+\Pi\mathcal{A}_{\mu}\Pi\mathcal{A}_{\nu}\right]\Pi-(\mu\leftrightarrow\nu)

\displaystyle=-i(\Pi\mathcal{A}_{\mu}\Pi\mathcal{A}_{\nu}\Pi-\Pi\mathcal{A}_{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{\nu}\Pi)-(\mu\leftrightarrow\nu)

\displaystyle=i\Pi\mathcal{A}_{\mu}(1-\Pi)\mathcal{A}_{\nu}\Pi-i\Pi\mathcal{A}_{\nu}(1-\Pi)\mathcal{A}_{\mu}\Pi,

(35)

which is exactly the second term in Eq. (
34
).
Note that
\Pi\mathcal{F}_{\mu\nu}^{(\Pi)}\Pi=\mathcal{F}_{\mu\nu}^{(\Pi)}
.
∎

In the last theorem, we show that the non-abelian Berry curvature defined in terms of the projectors in Eq. (
33
) coincide with definition of Wilczek and Zee
43
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\{\ket{\alpha}\}
be a basis of a subspace with projector
\Pi
,
namely
\Pi=\sum_{\alpha}\ket{\alpha}\bra{\alpha}
. Let us denote
the matrix elements
F_{\mu\nu}^{\alpha\beta}\equiv\bra{\alpha}\mathcal{F}_{\mu\nu}^{(\Pi)}\ket{\beta}
.
Then this matrix
F_{\mu\nu}
can be computed from

F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}-i[A_{\mu},A_{\nu}],

(36)

where
A_{\mu}
is the non-abelian Wilczek-Zee connection, generalizing
abelian Berry connection, defined as
A_{\mu}^{\alpha\beta}=\bra{\alpha}i\partial_{\mu}\ket{\beta}.

Proof.

Using the definition of the non-abelian Berry curvature, we have

\displaystyle F_{\mu\nu}^{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=i\bra{\alpha}[\partial_{\mu}\Pi,\partial_{\nu}\Pi]\ket{\beta}

(37)

\displaystyle=i\bra{\partial_{\mu}\alpha}(1-\Pi)\ket{\partial_{\nu}\beta}-(\mu\leftrightarrow\nu)

(38)

\displaystyle=\left[i\braket{\partial_{\mu}\alpha|\partial_{\nu}\beta}-i\sum_{\gamma}\braket{\partial_{\mu}\alpha|\gamma}\braket{\gamma|\partial_{\nu}\beta}\right]-(\mu\leftrightarrow\nu)

(39)

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}^{(\alpha\beta)}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}^{(\alpha\beta)}-i\sum_{\gamma}\left[A_{\mu}^{\alpha\gamma}A_{\nu}^{\gamma\beta}-A_{\nu}^{\alpha\gamma}A_{\mu}^{\gamma\beta}\right]

(40)

∎

III
Derivation of the energy pumping formula

The energy pumped into drive
\mu
can be computed via

E_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t})=\int_{0}^{t}ds\,\dot{\varphi}_{s}^{\mu}\bra{\psi(s)}\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{s})\ket{\psi(s)}.

(41)

Since
\ket{\psi(s)}=\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{s}}^{n}\ket{\psi(s)}
, we have

\bra{\psi(s)}\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{s})\ket{\psi(s)}=\bra{\psi(s)}\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{s}}^{n}\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{s})\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{s}}^{n}\ket{\psi(s)}.

(42)

Using Theorems
1
and
4
, we have

\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{s}}^{n}\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{s})\Pi_{\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{s}}^{n}=-\mathcal{F}_{\mu t}^{n}=\mathcal{F}_{t\mu}=\sum_{\nu}\dot{\varphi}_{t}^{\nu}\mathcal{F}_{\nu\mu}^{n}.

(43)

Noting that
\mathcal{F}_{\mu\mu}^{n}=0
, we arrive at the energy pumping formula of the main text

E_{\mu}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t})=\int_{0}^{t}ds\,\dot{\varphi}_{s}^{\nu}\dot{\varphi}_{s}^{\mu}\bra{\psi(s)}\mathcal{F}_{\nu\mu}^{n}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{s})\ket{\psi(s)}.

(44)

IV
Derivation of the KGP in the tripod system

The dark state projector
\Pi=1-P
, where
P=\ket{\tilde{g}}\bra{\tilde{g}}
, with

\ket{\tilde{g}}=\sum_{j}\Omega_{j}\ket{g_{i}}/\Omega,\quad\Omega=\sqrt{\sum_{i}\Omega_{i}^{2}}

(45)

The KGP can be computed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\left\{[\partial_{t}P,P]+[\partial_{t}(1-P),(1-P)]\right\}

\displaystyle=i(\dot{P}P-P\dot{P})

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{\Omega^{2}}\sum_{jk}(\dot{\Omega}_{j}\Omega_{k}+\Omega_{j}\dot{\Omega}_{k})\left[\ket{g_{j}}\bra{g_{k}}P-P\ket{g_{j}}\bra{g_{k}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{\Omega^{4}}\sum_{jkl}\left(\dot{\Omega}_{j}\Omega_{k}^{2}\Omega_{l}\ket{g_{j}}\bra{g_{l}}-\Omega_{l}\Omega_{j}^{2}\dot{\Omega}_{k}\ket{g_{l}}\bra{g_{k}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{\Omega^{2}}\sum_{jk}(\dot{\Omega}_{j}\Omega_{k}-\dot{\Omega}_{k}\Omega_{j})\ket{g_{j}}\bra{g_{k}}.

(46)

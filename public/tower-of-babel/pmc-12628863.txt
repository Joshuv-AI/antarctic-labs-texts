Systemic immune-inflammation index predicts acute histologic chorioamnionitis pathologic staging and neonatal respiratory distress syndrome in women with preterm premature rupture of membranes: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Hui Chen, Qiu Mo, Mei-Ting Huang, Qing-Lan Lin, Qiao-Hong Lai, Yan-Ping Yu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundWe aimed to investigate the predictive value of the systemic immune-inflammation index (SII) for acute histologic chorioamnionitis (HCA) pathologic staging and neonatal respiratory distress syndrome (NRDS) in women with preterm premature rupture of membranes (PPROM).MethodsWe retrospectively evaluated 135 pregnant women with PPROM at a gestational age of 20+ 0 ~ 33+ 6 weeks. The women based on placental histopathology were categorized into No/Mild and Moderate-Severe group. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was conducted to assess the factors influencing Moderate-Severe HCA and to develop a nomogram model. The model was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, calibration curves, and decision curves.ResultsOf the 135 women, 63 were included in the No/Mild HCA group and 72 were included in the Moderate-Severe HCA group. Among inflammatory markers, the SII showed the highest area under the curve (AUC) for predicting Moderate-Severe HCA. The Moderate-Severe HCA group displayed lower gestational age at PPROM, lower birth weight and higher SII compared with the No/Mild HCA group. Multivariate logistic regression analysis identified SII, C-reactive protein (CRP) and gestational age at PPROM as independent prognostic factors affecting Moderate-Severe HCA, our nomogram model incorporating these three factors had the highest AUC of 0.853 (95%CI: 0.788–0.918), followed by gestational age at PPROM, CRP and SII with AUCs of 0.786 (95%CI: 0.705–0.764), 0.697 (95%CI: 0.619–0.776) and 0.674 (95%CI: 0.584–0.764), respectively. The sensitivity and specificity of the nomogram model, gestational age at PPROM, CRP, and SII were 0.708 and 0.889, 0.681 and 0.714, 0.653 and 0.905, 0.583 and 0.667, respectively. The model showed well-fitted calibration curves and the clinical benefit. The model and birth weight are independent risk factors affecting NRDS.ConclusionsSII was identified as an inflammatory biomarker in both Moderate-Severe HCA and NRDS can serve as a useful tool to guide clinical treatment decisions.

Introduction

Preterm premature rupture of membranes (PPROM) is defined as the spontaneous rupture of membranes occurring prior to 37 weeks of gestation at least 1 h before the onset of contractions [1]. PPROM is the main cause of preterm births, accounting for about 25–30 per cent of preterm births [1–3]. Preterm birth is a major cause of perinatal mortality and long-term morbidity. An important complication experienced by approximately 50–60% of women with PPROM is acute histological chorioamnionitis (HCA) [4–7]. HCA is present in more than 90% of placentas delivered between 21 and 24 weeks of gestation, compared to an incidence of only 3–5% of placentas delivered at term [1, 8–10]. Although antibiotics can reduce intraamniotic inflammation in some cases [11–13], they often fail to prevent HCA-associated morbidities due to persistent inflammatory mediators causing fetal and maternal injury [14–16].

Several studies have shown that the presence of HCA in pregnancies complicated by PPROM may increase the number of adverse maternal and neonatal outcomes such as neonatal respiratory distress syndrome (NRDS), postnatal sepsis, necrotizing enterocolitis, bronchopulmonary dysplasia, intraventricular hemorrhage, cerebral white matter injury, and cerebral palsy [17–22]. Recent studies have shown that pathological staging of Moderate-Severe HCA is associated with the development of early onset sepsis, structural brain abnormalities, severe ventricular haemorrhage, cognitive impairment and cerebral palsy [23, 24]. In addition, several studies have shown a significant increase in neonatal morbidity as well as mortality that accompanies an increase in the severity of HCA pathological staging [25–27]. Therefore, early diagnosis of pathological staging of HCA is critical to improving pregnancy and neonatal outcomes and preventing related complications.

The use of serum markers of inflammation has been extensively studied in various diseases. C-reactive protein (CRP), Delta Neutrophil Index (DNI), monocyte-lymphocyte ratio (MLR), neutrophil-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), and procalcitonin (PCT) have all been linked to HCA [28–33]. Inflammatory markers such as NLR are also strongly associated with pre-eclampsia and gestational diabetes, affecting neonatal prognosis [34, 35]. However, there is currently limited research on its predictive value in patients with pathological staging of HCA. The systemic immune-inflammation index (SII) is a relatively new inflammatory marker, which is calculated from peripheral blood neutrophil, platelet and lymphocyte counts by a formula that reflects the balance between host immune function and inflammation [36, 37]. Although SII has been explored as a prenatal diagnosis of HCA, the relationship with the pathological staging of HCA remains understudied.

In this study, we aimed to evaluate whether the SII serves as a predictive marker for the pathological stages of HCA in women experiencing PPROM. Additionally, we sought to investigate the impact of a model based on maternal SII on NRDS.

Materials and methods

Study population

A total of 135 women with singleton gestation who were aged ≥ 18 years and were diagnosed with PPROM at a gestational age between 20+ 0 and 33+ 6 weeks from January 1, 2020 to October 30, 2023 at Xiaolan People’s Hospital of Zhongshan were included in this study. Pregnant women with signs of clinical chorioamnionitis or any other evidence of infection, abruptio placentae, placenta previa, missing placental pathologic results, and medical disorders such as gestational or pre-gestational diabetes, gestational hypertension, or a history of cervical cerclage were excluded.

The study adhered to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki, with ethical approval granted by the Ethics Committee of the Xiaolan People’s Hospital of Zhongshan (approval number: ZSXL-LL2024-012) The requirement for written informed consent was waived because all patients, on admission, consented for their anonymized medical data to be analyzed and published for research purposes.

Definition

An examination conducted by experienced pathologists in the Department of Pathology identified the presence of HCA based on inflammatory changes observed in the placental membranes. Fresh placental specimens were fixed in formalin and stained with haematoxylin and eosin, and the presence of HCA was determined by the pathologist on the basis of the presence or absence of an inflammatory reaction in the placental membranes, which was then categorized according to the inflammatory reaction. The inflammation was further categorized into maternal inflammatory response (MIR) and fetal inflammatory response (FIR) according to specific diagnostic criteria [38]. MIR was characterized by three stages: stage 1 involving neutrophil presence in the chorion or subchorionic space, stage 2 with neutrophil infiltration of chorionic connective tissue or amnion, and stage 3 manifested as necrotizing chorioamnionitis. FIR was identified by the presence of vasculitis in the umbilical cord or chorionic plate, with three stages ranging from chorionic vasculitis to necrotizing funisitis. Mild HCA was defined only if both FIR and MIR were stage 1. Moderate-Severe HCA was defined when there was any stage 2 or stage 3.

Diagnostic criteria for NRDS were based on the European Consensus Guidelines on Respiratory Distress Syndrome:2022 Update. These criteria encompassed: (1) clinical symptoms such as shallow breathing, dyspnea, and expiratory moans, as well as physical signs like cyanosis, nasal flaring, and decreased breath sounds. (2) Blood gas analysis indicated hypoxemia, hypercapnia, and metabolic/respiratory acidosis, (3) chest X-rays revealed decreased lung transparency, ground-glass opacities, bronchial inflation, and white lung formation in severe cases. NRDS can be diagnosed by any of the above 3 criteria.

Inflammatory markers were calculated as follows: SII: neutrophil/lymphocyte ratio × platelet count; PIV: neutrophil count × platelet count × monocyte count/lymphocyte count; NLR: neutrophil count/lymphocyte count; PLR: platelet count/lymphocyte count; MLR: monocyte count/lymphocyte count; PNI: lymphocyte count × 0.005 + albumin × 10; SIRI: neutrophil count × monocyte count/lymphocyte count.

Clinical factors

Patient demographics and laboratory indicators were extracted from electronic medical record system. Comprehensive perinatal information collection included body mass index (BMI); type of birth; weeks of gestation at diagnosis; neutrophil, lymphocyte, monocyte, and platelet count, ALB, CRP; birth weights of newborns; 1 st and 5th minute APGAR scores and so on.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are expressed as means and standard deviations (SDs) or medians and ranges. The chi-squared test or Fisher’s exact test was used for categorical variables, and the two-sample t-test or Wilcoxon rank sum test was used for continuous variables. We utilized Sankey diagrams to visualize the evolution or transition from one state to another or from one time point to another. The best cut-off values to predict the outcome were determined by the Youden index. The predictive values of single laboratory indicators and the multivariate model were evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis and the area under the curve (AUC). Multiple logistic regression analysis was performed to estimate the odds ratio of NRDS and Moderate-Severe HCA prediction with adjustment for confounders. The combination of significant parameters was used to generate a predictive equation. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 26.0 (IBM, Chicago, IL, USA) and R software version 4.1.2 (http://www.R-project.org). The rms package was used to plot nomogram and calibrate curves, the sankeyplotr package and decisioncurve package were used to plot Sankey diagrams and decision curves, respectively. All statistical tests were two-sided and p < 0.05 was considered to indicate a statistically significant result.

Model evaluation and risk stratification

ROC curves were plotted to analyze and evaluate the accuracy of serum CRP, SII, gestational age at PPROM and the nomogram model in the prediction of Moderate-Severe HCA, the AUC of them were 0.786 (95%CI: 0.705–0.764), 0.674 (95%CI: 0.584–0.764), 0.697 (95%CI: 0.619–0.776) and 0.853 (95%CI: 0.788–0.918), respectively. Their sensitivity and specificity are 0.583 and 0.667, 0.653 and 0.905, 0.681 and 0.714, 0.708 and 0.889, respectively. The nomogram model had the greatest AUC (Fig. 3). The calibration curve shows that the nomogram model have good consistency between the predicted and actual results of Moderate-Severe HCA. Additionally, DCA was used to calculate the net benefit of the nomogram, and results showed that the nomogram had higher net benefit than other independent predictors. For the prediction of Moderate-Severe HCA, the cut-off value of the nomogram model was 0.57. Based on the optimum cut-off value, patients were divided into a low risk group (nomogram value < 0.57) and a high risk group (nomogram value ≥ 0.57). We found that a higher proportion of women (87.9%) in high risk group were diagnosed with Moderate-Severe HCA compared to low risk group (27.3%) (P < 0.001).

Discussion

In this retrospective study, we developed a nomogram to predict Moderate-Severe HCA in women with PPROM, based on SII, CRP, and gestational age at PPROM. Further, we demonstrated the predictive value of the nomogram using the ROC curve, calibration curve and DCA curve. Importantly, we also demonstrated that the nomogram could identify women whose infants are at risk of NRDS.

Prior studies have focused primarily on the diagnosis of HCA, neglecting to investigate the significance of HCA pathological stages. In our study, we used the placental pathology stage to assess the severity of HCA, A system for quantifying the staging of placental inflammation indicates the extent of neutrophil infiltration in a given area, which directly reflecting the progression of the disease [39, 40]. In our study, the Moderate-Severe HCA group exhibited a trend of higher neutrophil infiltration compared to the No/Mild HCA group. NLR, an inflammatory marker based on neutrophils and lymphocytes, is widely used to predict the diagnosis of HCA rather than its pathological stage [29, 41]. Our study is the first to use inflammatory markers to predict the pathological staging of HCA. Previous studies on SII were used to predict the diagnosis of HCA rather than its staging. Additionally, compared to other individual markers, the nomogram based on SII demonstrated the highest predictive performance, with an area under the ROC curve of 0.853. Based on the nomogram score, we categorized it into two groups, high risk and low risk. We found that almost 90% of the patients in the high risk group had Moderate-Severe HCA. Risk stratification prior to delivery helped us to make risk assessment of the patients and could guide the physicians in clinical decision-making and early intervention in the perinatal period for high risk patients.

CRP is a plasma protein synthesised by hepatocytes and is considered a key marker of response to acute inflammation. CRP levels rise rapidly within 48 h of tissue injury or infection, and thus have been used to predict infection [42, 43]. Although it is not specific for infectious diseases, it is a frequently used laboratory variable in the diagnosis of HCA [28, 44]. CRP has demonstrated its value in predicting the diagnosis of HCA, and our study further show the importance of CRP in predicting the pathological stage of HCA. In our study, the Moderate-Severe HCA group showed a trend towards lower gestational age at PPROM and birth weight compared to the No/Mild HCA group. Previous studies have shown that gestational age have good predictive value of HCA [28, 45]. Our study suggests that gestational age at PPROM is an independent risk factor for predicting moderate to severe HCA. The above results suggest that during clinical management, we should pay more attention to the lower Gestational age at PPROM.

Some studies reveal that low birth weight and Gestational age increase the hazard of NRDS [46, 47], our research also shows that birth weight is an independent predictor of NRDS, because low birth weight newborns may experience lung immaturity leading to a lack of surfactants, and another reason is that as birth weight decreases, the risk of exposure/susceptibility to various medical issues increases, such as hypoglycemia and sepsis. Our findings showed that nomogram was an independent risk factor for predicting NRDS and had an OR as high as 4.691. Since gestational age was included in the nomogram, our results are in agreement with most of the results. The nomogram also contains SII, demonstrating that SII also has a role in predicting NRDS. There is a long-standing consensus that prenatal steroids reduce respiratory disease and other complications of pregnancy in newborns born before 34 weeks [48, 49]. However, our study does not support this view. The small sample size and the fact that most pregnant women use antenatal steroids are responsible for the inconsistent results of the study. In premature infants, NRDS is a common respiratory condition resulting from decreased production or increased destruction of pulmonary surfactant (PS) secreted by alveolar cells. Furthermore, there is extensively debatable that the association between severe HCA and the incidence of NRDS. Some scholars have suggested that the foetal lung can only mature when inflammation is not severe, whereas severe inflammation may increase the vascular permeability of the foetal lung, leading to leakage of serum proteins into the alveoli, thus damaging the foetal lung tissue [50–52]. It has been suggested that infants exposed to Mild HCA and Moderate have a reduced incidence of NRDS (protective defect), while Severe HCA is not associated with NRDS. Moreover, this protective defect is more likely to occur in preterm infants born at less than 32 weeks of gestation [53]. Our results suggest that Moderate-Severe HCA is not associated with NRDS, which partially overlaps with their findings. Possible reasons for this are the different stages of HCA and the different gestational ages of the pregnant women at the time of enrolment. For the different grade of NRDS, no study provided relevant data.

The same gestational age may lead to disparate neonatal outcomes. The main reason for this is that preterm birth is a complex and highly heterogeneous syndrome with multiple risk factors and etiologically related conditions that contribute to different gestational, postnatal and early childhood phenotypic outcomes [54–57]. It has been suggested that preterm labour should not be defined solely by gestational age, but that risk factors and possible causative factors should be included to differentiate between the different phenotypes, which is more conducive to the prediction of adverse maternal and neonatal outcomes [58].

The study has some limitations that should be mentioned. First, this study had a retrospective design, a small sample size, and was based on single-center experience; a larger, multicentric, prospective study needs to be conducted. Second, we have not classified the severity of NRDS and lack an assessment of different severity levels of NRDS using HCA pathological staging. Third, maternal antibiotic therapy may have an impact on NRDS and should be further included in subsequent studies. Finally, we did not count the effect of inflammatory markers after antibiotics or antenatal steroids on Moderate-Severe HCA and NRDS, as dynamic changes in SII may also have some predictive value.

In conclusion, Maternal serum SII and CRP, Gestational age at PPROM could be used as predictors for pathological staging of HCA in women with PPROM. The nomogram model may become a useful tool in clinical practice for predicting NRDS. Risk stratification based on nomogram may help screen high-risk patients and make early interventions for potential risks.

On large Sidon sets
Ingo Czerwinski ​, Alexander Pott

Abstract
A (binary) Sidon set M M is a subset of 𝔽 2 t \mathbb{F}_{2}^{t} such that the sum of four distinct elements of M M is never 0. The goal is to find Sidon sets of large size. In this note we show that the graphs of almost perfect nonlinear (APN) functions with high linearity can be used to construct large Sidon sets. Thanks to recently constructed APN functions 𝔽 2 8 → 𝔽 2 8 \mathbb{F}_{2}^{8}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{8} with high linearity, we can construct Sidon sets of size 192 in 𝔽 2 15 \mathbb{F}_{2}^{15} , where the largest sets so far had size 152. Using the inverse and the Dobbertin function also gives larger Sidon sets as previously known. Each of the new large Sidon sets M M in 𝔽 2 t \mathbb{F}_{2}^{t} yields a binary linear code with t t check bits, minimum distance 5, and a length not known so far. Moreover, we improve the upper bound for the linearity of arbitrary APN functions.



Keywords
Sidon sets, vectorial boolean functions, almost perfect nonlinear (APN) functions, linear binary codes.

Primary: 11B13, 94D10.

Secondary: 94B05.

Introduction

The following question is easy to state but seems difficult to answer:
What is the largest number of elements
in a set, in which an appropriate addition is defined,
so that no sum appears twice?

In the early 1930s Sidon
[
32
,
33
]
was the first who discussed
such sets in the integers.
Later Babai and Sós
[
1
]
generalised
such sets to arbitrary groups and called them

Sidon sets
. In this note, we only discuss binary Sidon sets, that are
Sidon sets in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}
, the
t
-dimensional vector space over the binary field
\mathbb{F}_{2}
:

Definition 1.1

(
[
1
]
)
.

A subset
M
of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}
is called a
Sidon
set
if
m_{1}+m_{2}\neq m_{3}+m_{4}
for all distinct
m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},m_{4}\in M
.
Equivalently, the sum of any four distinct elements of
M
is different from
0
.

We emphasise that, by definition, every subset of a Sidon set

M
and every coset
M+b=\{m+b:m\in M\}
,
b\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}
,
is also a Sidon set.

The main research question on Sidon sets in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}
is
to find sets of large size.
For dimensions
t\geq 11
, the maximum size is unknown (see Table
2
).
Consequently, it is interesting to improve lower bounds by constructions,
as well as upper bounds by theoretical arguments.

The trivial upper bound (already mentioned in
[
1
]
)
arises from the fact that the sums of two distinct elements
of a Sidon set
M
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}
are distinct and non-zero,
hence

\binom{\left\lvert M\right\rvert}{2}=\frac{\left\lvert M\right\rvert(\left\lvert M\right\rvert-1)}{2}\leq\left\lvert\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}\setminus\{0\}\right\rvert.

So far, progress on the upper bound has only been made in terms of coding theory.
This uses a one-to-one correspondence between Sidon sets that contain zero and linear codes with minimum distance
\geq 5

(
[
26
, p. 33]
,
[
20
]
,
[
21
]
).

The first improvement of the trivial bound was made in 1993 by
Brouwer and Tolhuizen
[
7
]
. They improved the case
t
odd.
The case
t
even was recently improved by the authors
in
[
21
]
. We state here only the Brouwer-Tolhuizen version.

Proposition 1.2
.

For any
t\geq 6
, an upper bound for the maximum size of a Sidon set
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}
is given by

s_{\max{}}(\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t})\leq\begin{cases}\sqrt{2^{t+1}}-2&\text{ for $t$ odd},\\
\left\lfloor\sqrt{2^{t+1}}+0.5\right\rfloor&\text{ for $t$ even}.\end{cases}

(1)

The improvement in
[
21
]
is that we may subtract

1
or
2
in the case that
t
is even, depending on some technical conditions.

For small dimensions
t\leq 14
, better upper bounds are known derived from coding theory
(see e.g Table 2 of
[
21
]
or Table
2
in this paper).

On the construction side, the following is known.
In the case of dimension
t=2n
even, the best
known infinite families are Sidon sets of size

2^{n}+1
if
n
is odd and
2^{n}+2
if
n
is even.
Sidon sets of size
2^{n}+1
are obtained from non-primitive BCH codes
which are constructed as shortenings of codes with parameters

[2^{n}+1,2^{n}+1-2n,5]
(see
[
26
, p. 586]
or (C2) construction in
[
27
]
).
Another construction is by Carlet, Mesnager and Picek

[
17
,
18
]
.
They have shown that the multiplicative subgroup
M
of size
2^{n}+1

in the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{2^{2n}}
is a Sidon set
in the additive group of the field.
This set can also be constructed from a certain Goppa code,
see
[
27
]
.
If
n
is even, then
0\notin M
and
M\cup\{0\}
is Sidon too.
These Sidon sets are of maximum size for
t=4,6,8
.
For
t=10
the construction leads to
33

but the largest possible Sidon set in dimension
10
has size
34
,
see Table
2
.

In the case of odd dimension
t=2n-1
, less is known on infinite families of Sidon sets.
There is only one construction which uses the shortening of a BCH code
(see
[
26
, p. 586]
or
construction (C3) in
[
27
]
).
It leads to Sidon sets in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n-1}

of size
2^{n-1}+2^{n/2}
for
n
even and
of size
2^{n-1}+2^{\frac{n-1}{2}}
for
n
odd.
For
t=7
, this construction gives a Sidon set of maximum possible size.
For
t>7
and
t
odd, several sporadic examples are known which give
larger Sidon sets, see, for instance, Table
2
.
To summarise:

Remark 1.3
.

The best infinite families of Sidon sets in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}
for
t\geq 6

known so far lead to Sidon sets of size

\begin{cases}2^{n}+2&\text{ for $t=2n$ and $n$ even},\\
2^{n}+1&\text{ for $t=2n$ and $n$ odd},\\
2^{n-1}+2^{n/2}&\text{ for $t=2n-1$ and $n$ even},\\
2^{n-1}+2^{\frac{n-1}{2}}&\text{ for $t=2n-1$ and $n$ odd}.\\
\end{cases}

We call these the
classical
parameters of large Sidon sets.

Another important class of Sidon sets in even dimensions
t=2n

are Sidon sets of size
2^{n}
and
stems from almost perfect nonlinear functions.
These functions are quite important from a cryptographic perspective.

Definition 1.4

(
[
29
]
)
.

A function
F\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
is called
almost perfect nonlinear
(APN)
if for every
a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
the equation

F(x)+F(x+a)=b

has at most
2
solutions in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
.

Several classes of APN functions are known: there are some infinite families,
see
[
14
]
for a summary, but also many sporadic constructions
(which are quite important for us), see

[
3
]
.

As early as 1969, Lindström used the graph of the cube function
x^{3}
,
which is APN, as an example of a Sidon set
[
25
]
.
The relationship between APN functions and Sidon sets
is as follows (see e.g
[
17
,
18
]
):

Proposition 1.5
.

A function
F\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
is APN
if and only if its graph
G_{F}=\{(x,F(x)):x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\}

is a Sidon set in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n}
.

Note that the graph of an APN function is in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n}
and
has size
2^{n}
which is slightly worse
than the other constructions mentioned above.
But they will play an important role in Section
4

to construct large Sidon sets in odd dimensions.

Now, that we have seen constructions of large Sidon sets,
we will introduce a new construction of large Sidon sets in the next section.
In its general form, an existing Sidon set is intersected with subspaces.
In Section
3
we will then show that the linearity of an APN function
gives us information about the largest intersection of its graph with hyperplanes.
In addition, we improve the upper bound for the linearity of an arbitrary APN function.
In Section
4
we then explicitly construct large Sidon sets
from APN functions with high linearity,
and in Section
5
we translate them into coding theory terms.
Finally, in Section
6
, we summarise all results and give an overview table
about the maximum size of Sidon sets in small dimensions and related bounds and constructions.

2
Sidon sets in subspaces

The idea of constructing large Sidon sets
is based on the fact that every subset of a Sidon set is also
a Sidon set.
If
M
is a Sidon set in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}
and
U
an
s
-dimensional
linear subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}
, then

M\cap U
is a Sidon subset of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}

and therefore a Sidon subset in the
s
-dimensional vector space
U
.

Also the intersection with affine subspaces can be used to construct Sidon sets,
since with
M
also
M+b
is Sidon for
b\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}
.
Hence, the intersection of
M
with an
affine subspace
U+b
leads to Sidon sets in
U

by intersecting
M+b
with
U
(note
|M\cap(U+b)|=|(M+b)\cap U|
).

Our goal is to construct large Sidon sets using this procedure.
In order to get a large Sidon set in this way, you need a subspace
U

which contains “many” elements of the Sidon set
M
.
But one expects that large Sidon sets are “equally” distributed
within
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}
, hence
\left\lvert M\cap U\right\rvert\approx|M|/2^{t-s}
is the expectation.
In other words: if we intersect a large Sidon set with a
hyperplane
,
we expect that the size of the intersection is in the range of
|M|/2
.
However, the upper bound reduces the size only by a factor
1/\sqrt{2}
and
we present examples where the intersection size of
M
with a hyperplane is in the range
|M|/\sqrt{2}
(see Section
4
).

Claude Carlet pointed out that the construction in Theorem 4.1 of

[
18
]
may be also reformulated as a construction
of a large Sidon set as the intersection of the graph of an APN function
with an appropriate subgroup (not a hyperplane).

3
Linearity and hyperplanes

Throughout the paper
\cdot
denotes the usual inner product in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}
,
which is
a\cdot b=a_{1}b_{1}+\dots+a_{t}b_{t}
for

a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{t}),b=(b_{1},\dots,b_{t})\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}
.

Let
F\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
be a function.
The
Walsh transform
of
F
is the function

W_{F}\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\times\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\to\mathbb{Z}
defined by

W_{F}(a,b)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{a\cdot x+b\cdot F(x)}.

The
linearity
of
F
is defined by

\mathcal{L}(F)=\max_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n},b\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\setminus\{0\}}\left\lvert W_{F}(a,b)\right\rvert.

In fact, the Walsh transform and linearity of
F
can also be formulated
as properties of the graph
G_{F}
inside its ambient space
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n+m}

(see
[
14
, p. 71]
):

Definition 3.1
.

Let
M
be a subset of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}
. The Fourier transform
of
M
is the mapping
\mathcal{F}_{M}\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}\to\mathbb{Z}

defined by

\mathcal{F}_{M}(a)=\sum_{m\in M}(-1)^{a\cdot m}

and the linearity of
M
is defined by

\mathcal{L}(M)=\max_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}\setminus\{0\}}\left\lvert\mathcal{F}_{M}(a)\right\rvert.

We remark that
\mathcal{F}_{M}(a)
and
\mathcal{L}(M)
are even if and only if
\left\lvert M\right\rvert
is even,
and that the linearity of a function equals the linearity of its graph
(see
[
14
, p. 71]
):

Proposition 3.2
.

If
F\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
is a function and
G_{F}
its graph
then
\mathcal{L}(F)=\mathcal{L}(G_{F})
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(F)

\displaystyle=\max_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n},b\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\setminus\{0\}}\left\lvert W_{F}(a,b)\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\max_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n},b\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\setminus\{0\}}\left\lvert\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{a\cdot x+b\cdot F(x)}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\max_{(a,b)\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\times(\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\setminus\{0\})}\left\lvert\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{(a,b)\cdot(x,F(x))}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\max_{(a,b)\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\times\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\setminus{(0,0)}}\left\lvert\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{(a,b)\cdot(x,F(x))}\right\rvert=\mathcal{L}(G_{F}).

The last equality holds because of

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{a\cdot x}=0

and therefore

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{(a,0)\cdot(x,F(x))}=0

for
a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
.
∎

The following Proposition is well known
using the terminology of functions (see
[
19
]
).
The interpretation in the terminology of sets seems to be folklore.
We include it here for the convenience of the reader.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
M
be a subset of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}
,

a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}\setminus\{0\}
,

H_{a}=\{g\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}:a\cdot g=0\}

and let
b\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}\setminus H_{a}
.
Then
\mathcal{F}_{M}(a)
has different representations using intersection numbers with affine hyperplanes:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{M}(a)

\displaystyle=\left\lvert M\cap H_{a}\right\rvert-\left\lvert(M+b)\cap H_{a}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert M\right\rvert-2\cdot\left\lvert(M+b)\cap H_{a}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=2\cdot\left\lvert M\cap H_{a}\right\rvert-\left\lvert M\right\rvert

and

\displaystyle\left\lvert M\cap H_{a}\right\rvert\qquad=\frac{\left\lvert M\right\rvert+\mathcal{F}_{M}(a)}{2}

\displaystyle\left\lvert(M+b)\cap H_{a}\right\rvert=\frac{\left\lvert M\right\rvert-\mathcal{F}_{M}(a)}{2}.

Proof.

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{M}(a)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in M}(-1)^{a\cdot m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in M\cap H_{a}}1\quad+\sum_{m\in(M+b)\cap H_{a}}-1

\displaystyle=\left\lvert M\cap H_{a}\right\rvert-\left\lvert(M+b)\cap H_{a}\right\rvert

The remaining follows from
\left\lvert M\right\rvert=\left\lvert M\cap H_{a}\right\rvert+\left\lvert(M+b)\cap H_{a}\right\rvert
.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

If
M
is a subset of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}
then there exists a linear hyperplane
H
such that either

\left\lvert M\cap H\right\rvert=\frac{\left\lvert M\right\rvert+\mathcal{L}(M)}{2}\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad

or

\left\lvert(M+b)\cap H\right\rvert=\frac{\left\lvert M\right\rvert+\mathcal{L}(M)}{2}\qquad\text{ for every }b\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}\setminus H.

Proof.

From the definition of
\mathcal{L}(M)
it follows
that
\mathcal{L}(M)=\left\lvert\mathcal{F}_{M}(a)\right\rvert
for some
a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}\setminus\{0\}
.
Let
H_{a}=\{g\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}:a\cdot g=0\}
and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}\setminus H_{a}
as above.
Proposition
3.3
shows the follwing:

\left\lvert M\cap H_{a}\right\rvert=\frac{\left\lvert M\right\rvert+\mathcal{F}_{M}(a)}{2}=\frac{\left\lvert M\right\rvert+\mathcal{L}(M)}{2}
if
\mathcal{F}_{M}(a)>0
,
and

\left\lvert(M+b)\cap H_{a}\right\rvert=\frac{\left\lvert M\right\rvert-\mathcal{F}_{M}(a)}{2}=\frac{\left\lvert M\right\rvert+\mathcal{L}(M)}{2}
otherwise.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
M
be a Sidon set in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}
.
Then there exists a Sidon set in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t-1}

of size
\frac{\left\lvert M\right\rvert+\mathcal{L}(M)}{2}
.

Proof.

Use Corollary
3.4
and note that
M+b

is Sidon too.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
F:\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
be an APN function.
Then there exists a Sidon set in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n-1}
of size

2^{n-1}+\frac{\mathcal{L}(F)}{2}
.

Proof.

Direct consequence of Proposition
1.5
,
Proposition
3.2
,
and Corollary
3.5
.
∎

In the next section, we will apply Corollary
3.6
to several highly linear APN functions.
But first, as an interesting application,
we use it to give an upper bound on the linearity of APN functions.

Not so much is known on that topic (see
[
13
]
)
apart of that an arbitrary APN function
F\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}

cannot have linearity
2^{n}
for
n\geq 3
(
[
12
, p. 424]
).
For certain classes of APN functions smaller bounds are known.
In
[
15
]
a bound is given,
that holds for all
known
APN functions.
But it is open if it is valid for
all
APN functions.
If
F
is a quadratic APN function then its
linearity is
\leq 2^{n-1}
(see
[
13
]
).
Here quadratic means that all component functions have degree
2
,
or, in other words, the function

x\mapsto F(x+a)+F(x)+F(a)+F(0)
is linear for
a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
.
Let us formulate a general upper bound:

Corollary 3.7
.

If
F\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
is APN then
\mathcal{L}(F)\leq 2^{n}-4
for
n\geq 3
.

Proof.

Assume
n\geq 4
. Since
\left\lvert G_{F}\right\rvert
is even,
it follows that
\mathcal{L}(F)
is also even.
Assuming
\mathcal{L}(F)=2^{n}-2
would lead to a Sidon set
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n-1}
of size
2^{n-1}+2^{n-1}-1=2^{n}-1
due to Corollary
3.6
.
But this is not possible because of Proposition
1.2

and the case
n\geq 4
is shown.
For
n=3
it is an exercise to show that only one
APN function exists up to CCZ-equivalence
(see
[
14
, p. 28]
for the definition of CCZ-equivalence),
and that it is quadratic with linearity 4, hence
\mathcal{L}(F)=4
.
∎

The connection between Sidon sets and APN functions also shows
that no component function of an APN function can have weight
1
,
i.e. no component can have all its values 0 with only one exception.
To see this, let
F\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
be a function
and let
a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
. Then the
component function

F_{a}\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}
of
F

is defined as
x\mapsto a\cdot F(x)
.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
F\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
be an APN function,

a\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{2}

and define
w_{a,\lambda}=\left\lvert\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}:F_{a}(x)=\lambda\}\right\rvert
.
Then there exists a Sidon set of size
2^{n}-w_{a,\lambda}
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n-1}
.

Proof.

The set
H_{a}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n}:(0,a)\cdot(x,y)=0\}

defines a hyperplane in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n}
.
Therefore the sets
G_{F}\cap H_{a}
and
(G_{F}+b)\cap H_{a}
are
Sidon sets, where
b\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n}\setminus H_{a}
.
If
\lambda=0
, we have
|G_{F}\cap H_{a}|=w_{a,\lambda}
,
and if
\lambda=1
, we have
|(G_{F}+b)\cap H_{a}|=w_{a,\lambda}
,
see also Proposition
3.3
.
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

For
n\geq 3
there is no APN function
F\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}

with component function
x_{1}x_{2}\dotsc x_{n}
.

Proof.

Assume
n\geq 4
.
Note that
x_{1}x_{2}\dotsc x_{n}
gives a component function with
w_{a,1}=1
,
which results in a Sidon set of size
2^{n}-1
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n-1}

due to Corollary
3.8
.
But this Sidon set cannot exist because of Proposition
1.2

and the case
n\geq 4
is shown.
The case
n=3
follows again from the
uniqueness of such an APN function.
∎

4
New construction

It follows from Corollary
3.6
that it is sufficient
to know the linearity of an APN function
in order to know the best possible size
when intersecting its graph with a hyperplane.
Therefore we discuss in this section the known linearities of APN functions.
There are many good research papers and books which give
a good overview about APN functions and their Walsh spectra, we refer to

[
9
,
14
,
30
]
.

A classical result from Chabaud and Vaudenay
[
19
]
shows that the
linearity of a function
F\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
is lower bounded by
2^{(n+1)/2}

and that can be reached for
n
odd only.
Functions reaching this bound are called
almost bent
.
They are APN and are characterised
by having only the values
0
,
2^{(n+1)/2}
and
-2^{(n+1)/2}
as their Walsh coefficients
[
19
]
.
Let us begin with quadratic APN functions
F\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
.
Several infinite families of quadratic APN functions as well as sporadic ones are known.

If
n
is odd, quadratic APN functions are almost bent, see
[
14
]
.
We may apply Corollary
3.6
to these APN functions and obtain Sidon sets
of size
2^{n-1}+2^{\frac{n-1}{2}}
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n-1}
for
n
odd.
This size matches the size of Sidon sets with classical parameters
(see Remark
1.3
).
Hence we do not obtain Sidon sets larger than the known ones
but we remark that all APN functions for
n
odd which are not almost bent
will lead to improvements.

The situation is more interesting if
n
is even.
The linearities of the known
infinite
families of
quadratic APN functions for
n
even are
2^{n/2+1}
.
Applying Corollary
3.6
results in Sidon sets of size

2^{n-1}+2^{n/2}
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n-1}
for
n
even.
Again, this size matches the size of Sidon sets with classical parameters
(see Remark
1.3
).
Many
sporadic
quadratic APN functions for

n=6
and
n=8
are known, see

[
3
,
8
,
36
,
37
]
.
It is interesting that some of them have the best possible linearity

Remark 4.2
.

No infinite family of APN functions on
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}

with linearity
2^{n-1}
is known so far,
and there is also no proof that such a family cannot exist,
see
[
2
]
for a thorough investigation
of such APN functions. If APN functions on
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}

with linearity
2^{n-1}
exist for all even
n
,
we would have Sidon sets of size

2^{n-1}+2^{n-2}=3\cdot 2^{n-2}
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n-1}
.

Next we discuss the inverse function
F(x)=x^{-1}
on
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
(here
F(0)=0
).
This is an APN function if and only if
n
is odd
[
28
]
.
The Walsh coefficients are the elements
\equiv 0\bmod 4

in the interval

[-2^{n/2+1}+1,2^{n/2+1}+1],

see
[
24
]
, and therefore:

Theorem 4.3
.

The linearity of the inverse function equals to

\left\lfloor 2^{n/2+1}+1\right\rfloor-\left(\left\lfloor 2^{n/2+1}+1\right\rfloor\bmod 4\right)

and leads to a Sidon set of size

2^{n-1}+\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\left\lfloor 2^{n/2+1}+1\right\rfloor-\left(\left\lfloor 2^{n/2+1}+1\right\rfloor\bmod 4\right)\Bigr)

in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2n-1}
for odd
n\geq 3
.

Proof.

The upper bound for the Walsh coefficients would give a linearity of

\left\lfloor 2^{n/2+1}+1\right\rfloor-\left(\left\lfloor 2^{n/2+1}+1\right\rfloor\bmod 4\right)

(2)

and the lower bound of

\left\lvert\left\lfloor-2^{n/2+1}+1\right\rfloor+4-\left(\left\lfloor-2^{n/2+1}+1\right\rfloor\bmod 4\right)\right\rvert=\\
-\left\lfloor-2^{n/2+1}+1\right\rfloor-4+\left(\left\lfloor-2^{n/2+1}+1\right\rfloor\bmod 4\right).

(3)

We define
\varepsilon=2^{n/2+1}+1-\left\lfloor 2^{n/2+1}+1\right\rfloor
.
Then

\left\lfloor-2^{n/2+1}+1\right\rfloor=-2^{n/2+1}+1-(1-\varepsilon)=-2^{n/2+1}+\varepsilon.

Hence

\left\lfloor 2^{n/2+1}+1\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor-2^{n/2+1}+1\right\rfloor=2^{n/2+1}+1-\varepsilon+-2^{n/2+1}+\varepsilon=1

(4)

and

\left(\left\lfloor 2^{n/2+1}+1\right\rfloor\bmod 4\right)\neq\left(\left\lfloor-2^{n/2+1}+1\right\rfloor\bmod 4\right).

(5)

Subtracting (3) from (2) and using (4) gives

5-\left(\left\lfloor 2^{n/2+1}+1\right\rfloor\bmod 4\right)-\left(\left\lfloor-2^{n/2+1}+1\right\rfloor\bmod 4\right)

which is always
\geq 0
because of (5).
This shows the linearity of the inverse.
The existence of the Sidon set follows from Corollary
3.6
.
∎

for odd
n\geq 5

1.3

5
Linear Codes

In this short section we want to point out explicitly the consequences of our Sidon sets to codes.
We use the well known one-to-one correspondence
between Sidon sets that contain zero and linear codes with minimum distance
\geq 5

(
[
26
, p. 33]
,
[
20
]
,
[
21
]
), see
also
[
16
]
for the APN case.

A (binary)
linear code

\mathcal{C}
of
length

m

and
dimension

k
is a
k
-dimensional linear subspace
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}

and
c\in\mathcal{C}
is called a
code word
of
\mathcal{C}
.
We consider all vectors to be row vectors.
If the
minimum distance
of
\mathcal{C}
is
d
,
that is the minimum number of non-zero entries
of all non-zero code words of
\mathcal{C}
, then
\mathcal{C}
is called a
[m,k,d]
-code.
We call a
[m,m-t,d]
-code, a linear code of length
m
, with
t

check bits

and with minimum distance
d
.
Such a
[m,m-t,d]
-code
\mathcal{C}
can be defined as the kernel of a matrix
\mathcal{H}

with
t
rows,
m
columns and rank
t
which is called
parity-check matrix

of
\mathcal{C}
, i.e
\mathcal{C}=\{v\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}:\mathcal{H}\cdot v^{\intercal}=0\}
.
Then a Sidon set
M
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}
with
0\in M
,
\left\lvert M\right\rvert\geq t+2

and
\dim\langle M\rangle=t

(that is the dimension of the linear span of
M
)
leads to a linear code
\mathcal{C}_{M}
of length
\left\lvert M\right\rvert-1
,
with
t
check bits and with minimum distance
\geq 5
.
Thus the
\left\lvert M\right\rvert-1
non-zero elements
M\setminus\{0\}

form the columns of the parity-check matrix of
\mathcal{C}_{M}
.
Since every Sidon set contains, without loss of generality, the zero vector, we obtain:

Every Sidon set
M
in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}
with
\left\lvert M\right\rvert\geq t+2
and
\dim\langle M\rangle=t

leads to a
[\left\lvert M\right\rvert-1,\left\lvert M\right\rvert-t-1,\geq 5]
-code.

In the following theorem, we summarise the consequences of the results of the previous section
to coding theory.

Theorem 5.1
.

(a)

There exists a
[191,176,5]
-code.

(b)

There exist
[m-1,m-2n-2,5]
-codes for

m=2^{n-1}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\lfloor 2^{n/2+1}+1\right\rfloor-\left(\left\lfloor 2^{n/2+1}+1\right\rfloor\bmod 4\right)\right)

and odd
n\geq 3
.

(c)

There exist
[m-1,m-2n-2,5]
-codes for

m=2^{n-1}+2^{3n/5-1}+2^{2n/5-1}

and
n=5,10,15,20,25,30,35
.

The
[191,176,5]
-code from Theorem
5.1
(a) improves the best previously known length 151 of a linear code with
15
check bits and with minimum distance
5
(see
[
5
,
23
]
).
The codes from Theorem
5.1
(b)
provide an infinite family of codes
with better length for the same odd number of check bits
t
and minimum distance 5
than the codes from the Sidon sets with classical parameters (see Remark
1.3
).

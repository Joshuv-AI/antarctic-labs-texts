Corrigendum for Han’s Corrigendum
Laurence Boxer

Abstract
S.-E. Han’s paper, ”Remarks on Pseudocovering Spaces in a Digital Topological Setting: A Corrigendum”, is meant to address errors in previous papers. However, this paper is also marked by errors in its mathematics, as well as improprieties in its citations. We address these flaws in the current work. Key words and phrases: digital topology, digital image, covering map MSC: 54B20, 54C35

S.-E. Han’s paper
[

]

[

The notion of a covering map has been adapted from

•

Much of

•

Han doesn’t even cite his own papers correctly.

2.1
Digital images

We use
{\mathbb{N}}
for the set of natural numbers,

{\mathbb{Z}}
for the set of integers, and

\#X
for the number of distinct members of
X
. Let
{\mathbb{N}}^{*}={\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
.

We typically denote a (binary) digital image
as
(X,\kappa)
, where
\emptyset\neq X\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
for some

n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\kappa
represents an adjacency
relation of pairs of points in
X
. Thus,

(X,\kappa)
is a graph, in which members of
X
may be
thought of as black points, and members of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\setminus X

as white points, typically of a picture of some “real world”
object or scene.

Let
u,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
1\leq u\leq n
.
Han’s papers use “
k
-adjacency” sometimes to mean
an arbitrary adjacency, sometimes as an abbreviation
for what he calls “
k(u,n)
-adjacency,” where the
digital image
(X,k)
satisfies
X\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and

x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),~y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in X

are
k(u,n)
-adjacent if and only if

•

x\neq y
, and

•

for at most
u
indices
i
,

\mid x_{i}-y_{i}\mid=1
, and

•

for all indices
j
such that

\mid x_{j}-y_{j}\mid\neq 1
, we have

x_{j}=y_{j}
.

Generally,
n
is either explicitly given (e.g.,
n=2
) or is understood
as an abstract positive integer.
Therefore, other authors refer to this adjacency as
c_{u}
-adjacency.
We will prefer the latter notation in the current paper.
The
c_{u}
adjacencies are the adjacencies most used
in digital topology, especially
c_{1}
and
c_{n}
.

In low dimensions, it is also common to denote a

c_{u}
adjacency by the number of points that can
have this adjacency with a given point in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. E.g.,

•

For subsets of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{1}
,
c_{1}
-adjacency is 2-adjacency.

•

For subsets of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
,
c_{1}
-adjacency is 4-adjacency and

c_{2}
-adjacency is 8-adjacency.

•

For subsets of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}
,
c_{1}
-adjacency is 6-adjacency,

c_{2}
-adjacency is 18-adjacency, and

c_{3}
-adjacency is 26-adjacency.

We use the notations
y\leftrightarrow_{\kappa}x
, or, when
the adjacency
\kappa
can be assumed,
y\leftrightarrow x
, to mean

x
and
y
are
\kappa
-adjacent.
The notations
y\leftrightarroweq_{\kappa}x
, or, when

\kappa
can be assumed,
y\leftrightarroweq x
, mean either

y=x
or
y\leftrightarrow_{\kappa}x
.
For
x\in X
, let

N(X,x,\kappa)=\{\,y\in X\mid x\leftrightarrow_{\kappa}y\,\}.

When the image
(X,\kappa)
under discussion is clear, we
will use the notations
N(x)
or
N_{\kappa}(x)

as follows.

N(x)=\{\,y\in X\mid y\leftrightarroweq_{\kappa}x\,\}=N(X,x,\kappa)\cup\{x\}.

A sequence
P=\{y_{i}\}_{i=0}^{m}
in a digital image
(X,\kappa)
is
a
\kappa
-path from
a\in X
to
b\in X
if

a=y_{0}
,
b=y_{m}
, and
y_{i}\leftrightarroweq_{\kappa}y_{i+1}

for
0\leq i<m
.

X
is
\kappa
-connected

[
24
]
,
or
connected
when
\kappa

is understood, if for every pair of points
a,b\in X
there
exists a
\kappa
-path in
X
from
a
to
b
.

A
(digital)
\kappa
-closed curve
is a
path
S=\{s_{i}\}_{i=0}^{m}
such that
s_{0}=s_{m}
,
and
0<|i-j|<m

implies
s_{i}\neq s_{j}
. If, also,
0\leq i<m
implies

N(S,x_{i},\kappa)=\{x_{(i-1)\mod n},~x_{(i+1)\mod m}\}

then
S
is a
(digital)

\kappa
-simple closed curve
.

2.2
Digitally continuous functions

Material in this section is largely quoted or paraphrased from
[
6
]
.

Digital continuity is defined
to preserve connectedness, as at
Definition
2.1
below. By
using adjacency as our standard of “closeness,” we
get Theorem
2.2
below.

Definition 2.1
.

[
2
]
(generalizing a definition of
[
24
]
)
Let
(X,\kappa)
and
(Y,\lambda)
be digital images.
A function
f:X\rightarrow Y
is

(\kappa,\lambda)
-continuous
if for
every
\kappa
-connected
A\subset X
we have that

f(A)
is a
\lambda
-connected subset of
Y
.

If either of
X
or
Y
is a subset of the
other, we use the abbreviation

\kappa
-continuous for
(\kappa,\kappa)
-continuous.

When the adjacency relations are understood, we will simply say that
f
is
continuous
. Continuity can be expressed in terms of adjacency of points:

Theorem 2.2
.

[
24
,
2
]

A function
f:X\to Y
is continuous if and only if
x\leftrightarrow x^{\prime}
in
X

implies
f(x)\leftrightarroweq f(x^{\prime})
.

Han’s papers generally use the equivalent formulation that

f
is continuous if and only for every
x\in X
,

f(N_{\kappa}(x))\subset N_{\lambda}(f(x))
.

See also
[
8
,
9
]
, where similar notions are referred to as
immersions
,
gradually varied operators
,
and
gradually varied mappings
.

A digital
isomorphism
(called
homeomorphism

in
[
1
]
) is a
(\kappa,\lambda)
-continuous
surjection
f:X\to Y
such that
f^{-1}:Y\to X
is

(\lambda,\kappa)
-continuous.

The literature uses
path
polymorphically: a

(c_{1},\kappa)
-continuous function
f:[0,m]_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\to X

is a
\kappa
-path if
f([0,m]_{{\mathbb{Z}}})
is a
\kappa
-path
as described above from
f(0)
to
f(m)
.

Definition 2.3
.

[
3
]

Let
p:(E,\kappa)\to(B,\lambda)
be a continuous
surjection of digital images. The map
p
is a

(\kappa,\lambda)
-covering (map)
if and only if

1.

for every
b\in B
, there is an index set
M

such that

p^{-1}(N_{\lambda}(b))=\bigcup_{i\in M}N_{\kappa}(e_{i}),\mbox{ where }e_{i}\in p^{-1}(b);

2.

if
i,j\in M
,
i\neq j
, then

N_{\kappa}(e_{i})\cap N_{\kappa}(e_{j})=\emptyset
; and

3.

p|_{N_{\kappa}(e_{i})}:N_{\kappa}(e_{i})\to N_{\lambda}(b)
is a

(\kappa,\lambda)
-isomorphism.

We find the following definition in Han’s
paper
[
17
]
(not in
[
12
]

to the contrary in
[

,

]

Definition 2.4
.

A digitally continuous map
h:(X,\kappa)\to(Y,\lambda)

is a
pseudo-local (PL) isomorphism
if for every

x\in X
,
h(N_{\kappa}(x))\subset Y
is

\lambda
-isomorphic to

N_{\lambda}(h(x))\subset Y
.

In his paper
[

]
, Han gives the following.

Definition 2.5
.

A digitally continuous map
h:(X,\kappa)\to(Y,\lambda)

is a

[in more recent

h|_{N_{\kappa}(x)}
is a

(\kappa,\lambda)
-homeomorphism

Han defines a digital pseudo-covering as follows.

Definition 3.1
.

[
16
]

Let
p:(E,\kappa)\to(B,\lambda)
be a surjection
such that for every
b\in B
,

1.

there is an index set
M
such that

p^{-1}(N_{\kappa}(b,1))=\bigcup_{i\in M}N_{\kappa}(e_{i},1)
, where

e_{i}\in p^{-1}(b)
;

2.

if
i,j\in M
and
i\neq j
, then

N_{\kappa}(e_{i},1)\cap N_{\kappa}(e_{j},1)=\emptyset
; and

3.

p|_{N_{\kappa}(e_{i},1)}:N_{\kappa}(e_{i},1)\to N_{\lambda}(b,1)
is a WL-isomorphism for
all
i\in M
.

Then
p
is a
pseudo-covering
map.

However, A. Pakdaman shows in
[
21
]
that Han’s definition
does not effectively give us a new object of study.
In particular, Pakdaman shows the following.

Theorem 3.2
.

[
21
]

A digital pseudo-covering map as defined in
Definition
3.1
is in fact a digital covering map.

Definition 3.3
.

[
13
]
Let
p:(E,\kappa)\to(B,\lambda)

be
(\kappa,\lambda)
-continuous. Let

f:[0,m]_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\to B
be
(c_{1},\lambda)
-continuous.
A
(c_{1},\kappa)
-continuous function

\tilde{f}:[0,m]_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\to E
such that

p\circ\tilde{f}=f
is a

(digital) path lifting of
f
. If for every

b_{0}\in B
, every
e_{0}\in p^{-1}(b_{0})
, and every
path
f
such that
f(0)=b_{0}
, there is a unique
lifting
\tilde{f}
such that
\tilde{f}(0)=e_{0}
,
then
p
has the
unique path lifting property
.

Han’s variants of covering maps don’t all give us new notions, as
shown by the following expansion of Theorem
3.2
.

Theorem 3.4
.

[
6
]

Let
p:(X,\kappa)\to(Y,\lambda)
be a continuous
surjection. Then the following are equivalent.

•

p
is a digital covering map.

•

p
is a local isomorphism.

•

p
is a pseudo-covering in the sense of

3.1
.

•

p
is a WL-isomorphism with the unique

In order to obtain a notion of a digital pseudo-cover that is not equivalent to
a digital cover, Pakdaman gave the following definition.

Definition 3.5
.

[
21
]

Let
p:(E,\kappa)\to(B,\lambda)
be a surjection
of digital images. Suppose for all
b\in B
we
have the following.

1.

for some index set
M

\bigcup_{i\in M}N_{\kappa}(e_{i},1)\subset p^{-1}(N_{\lambda}(b,1))

N_{\kappa}(e_{i},1)\cap N_{\kappa}(e_{i},1)=\emptyset

4
Other flaws in
[
19
]

In this section, we take
p:{\mathbb{N}}^{*}\to C_{n}
, where
C_{n}
is a digital
simple closed curve of
n
points
\{c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1}\}
indexed
circularly, and
p
is defined by

p(z)=c_{z\!\!\mod n}.

In Remark 3.10(1) of
[
19
]
, Han claims

Assertion 4.1
.

There exists
b\in C_{n}
such that

\bigcup_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}N(e_{i})\neq p^{-1}(N(b)),~~~\mbox{ where }~~~p^{-1}(b)=\{e_{i}\mid i\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}\}.

Proposition 4.2
.

Assertion
4.1
is false.

Proof.

Consider the following cases.

•

Suppose
b=c_{0}
. We have

p^{-1}(N(c_{0}))=\{0,1\}\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}[i\cdot n-1,i\cdot n+1]_{{\mathbb{Z}}}=\bigcup_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}N(e_{i}),

where
e_{i}=i\cdot n
and
p^{-1}(0)=\{e_{i}\mid i\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}\}
.

•

Suppose
b=c_{j}
such that
j\neq 0\mod n
. We have

p^{-1}(N(c_{j}))=\bigcup_{i=0}^{\infty}[i\cdot j-1,i\cdot n+1]_{{\mathbb{Z}}}=\bigcup_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}N(e_{i}),

where
i\mod n=j
and
p^{-1}(c_{j})=\{e_{i}\mid i\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}\}
.

Therefore we have
p^{-1}(N(b))=\bigcup_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}}N(e_{i})
in both
cases. This completes the proof.
∎

Han claims that the following Corollary
4.3
(stated
in
[
19
]
as Corollary 3.11) follows from his Remark 3.10.
However, since we see in Proposition

that Han’s

Corollary 4.3
.

A pseudo-covering map in the sense of Definition
3.1
is
a WL-isomorphic surjection, but the converse does not obtain.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.4
, a pseudo-covering map in the sense of

is a WL-isomorphic surjection.

The map

assumed for this section is easily seen to be a WL-isomorphic

]

from

∎

An injective-type norm and integral bilinear forms defined by sequence classes
Jamilson R. Campos, Lucas Nascimento, Luiz Felipe P. Sousa

Abstract
In this work we define a class of injective-type norm on tensor products through the environment of sequence classes. Examples and results on this norm will be presented and the duality is studied in this context. As a byproduct, we present the definition of the associated integral-type bilinear forms and also a tensor characterization for a class of sequence spaces.

Introduction

The study of the duals
(E\hat{\otimes}_{\alpha}F)^{\prime}
(here
E
and
F
are Banach spaces and
\alpha
is a reasonable crossnorm) as classes of linear operators or bilinear forms is a fundamental part in the theory of tensor products (see
[
17
,
35
]
) and establishes its close relationship with the parallel theory of operator ideals (see
[
18
,
31
]
and
[
32
, 6.3]
). Issues such as the maximality and minimality of ideals and the approximation property, for example, are involved in this scope and recent developments in these subjects can be seen in
[
2
,
16
,
20
,
21
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
27
,
36
]
.

A number of important operator ideals are defined, or characterized, by the transformation of vector-valued sequences and a unifying approach to this kind of operators ideals was proposed in
[
4
]
using the concept of Sequence Classes (cf. Definition
1.1
). For sequence classes
X
and
Y
, a linear operator
T\colon E\longrightarrow F
is
(X;Y)
-summing if
T((x_{j}))_{j=1}^{\infty}\in Y(F)
whenever
(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in X(E)
and the Banach operator ideal of such operators is denoted by
{\cal L}_{X;Y}
. This approach has shown to be quite fruitful (see
[
3
,
5
,
7
,
8
,
11
,
14
,
33
,
34
]
) and the maximality of the Banach operator ideals of the type
{\cal L}_{X;Y}
was recently studied, using a tensor approach, in
[
6
]
.

It is well known that since the injective norm
\varepsilon
on
E\otimes F
is smaller than the projective norm
\pi
, every bounded linear functional on
E\hat{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}F
is the linearization of a unique bounded bilinear form on
E\times F
. A complete description of the dual space of
E\hat{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}F
leads to the definition of Integral Bilinear Forms and Integral Operators (see
[
35
, Section 3.4]
). The purpose of this paper is to define and study a class of injective-type norms on tensor products, which generalizes the norm
\varepsilon
in a certain sense, using the environment of sequence classes. We establish necessary results on these norms as well as present the duality theory for such normed tensor products. As an application, we present in the last section of the paper a tensor characterization for the space
(\ell_{p}^{\rm mid})^{u}(E)
.

Banach spaces over
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
are denoted by
E
and
F
. The symbol
E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1}}{{\hookrightarrow}}F
means that
E
is a linear subspace of
F
and
\|x\|_{F}\leq\|x\|_{E}
for every
x\in E
; and
E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1}}{{=}}F
means that
E
is isometrically isomorphic to
F
. We denote by
E^{\prime}
the topological dual of E,
B_{E}
denotes the closed unit ball of
E
and
{\cal L}(E;F)
the Banach space of bounded linear operators from
E
to
F
with the usual operator norm. For
x\in E
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
, the symbol
x\cdot e_{j}
denotes the sequence
(0,\ldots,0,x,0,0,\ldots)\in E^{\mathbb{N}}
, where
x
is placed at the
j
-th coordinate. The symbol
(x_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
, where
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in E
, stands for the sequence
(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n},0,0,\ldots)\in E^{\mathbb{N}}
. By
\widehat{T}
we mean the induced operator

\widehat{T}\colon E^{\mathbb{N}}\longrightarrow F^{\mathbb{N}}~,~\widehat{T}((x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty})=(T(x_{j}))_{j=1}^{\infty}.

Restrictions of
\widehat{T}
to subspaces of
E^{\mathbb{N}}
are still denoted by
\widehat{T}
. The other notations and symbols used here are usual in Functional Analysis.

The theory, nomenclature and symbology on the sequence class environment are present in
[
4
]
and the additional associated elements in
[
5
,
6
,
10
]
. However, for the convenience of the reader, we will present below the definitions and essential tools on this topic.

Definition 1.1
.

According to
[
4
]
, a
Sequence Class
is a rule
X
that assigns to each Banach space
E
a Banach space
X(E)
of
E
-valued sequences, that is
X(E)
is a vector subspace of
E^{\mathbb{N}}
with the coordinatewise operations, such that:

(i)
c_{00}(E)\subseteq X(E)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1}}{{\hookrightarrow}}\ell_{\infty}(E)
for every Banach space
E
,

(ii)
\|x\cdot e_{j}\|_{X(E)}=\|x\|_{E}
for every Banach space
E
, every
x\in E
and every
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

To avoid ambiguities we shall eventually denote a sequence classe
X
by
X(\cdot)
. For example, we write just
\ell_{p}^{w}
instead of
\ell_{p}^{w}(\cdot)
, but we write
\ell_{p}(\cdot)
so that there is no confusion with the scalar sequence space
\ell_{p}
.

Given sequence classes
X
and
Y
, we say that an operator
T\in{\cal L}(E;F)
is
(X;Y)
-summing
if
T((x_{j}))_{j=1}^{\infty}\in Y(F)
whenever
(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in X(E)
. In this case, the induced linear operator
\widehat{T}\colon X(E)\longrightarrow Y(F)
, is continuous and
\|T\|_{X;Y}:=\|\widehat{T}\|
is a norm that makes the space
{\cal L}_{X;Y}(E;F)
of
(X;Y)
-summing operators a Banach space. Whenever we refer to
{\cal L}_{X;Y}(E;F)
we assume that it is endowed with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{X;Y}
.

A sequence class
X
is
linearly stable
if, regardless of the Banach spaces
E
and
F
, every operator
T\in{\cal L}(E;F)
is
(X;X)
-summming and
\|T\|_{X;X}=\|T\|
, that is,
\mathcal{L}_{X;X}(E;F)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1}}{{=}}\mathcal{L}(E;F)
. If the sequence classes
X
and
Y
are linearly stable and
X(\mathbb{K})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1}}{{\hookrightarrow}}Y(\mathbb{K})
, then
\mathcal{L}_{X;Y}
is a Banach operator ideal
[
4
, Theorem 3.6]
.

We need to recall two more definitions from
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
, respectively. A sequence class
X
is said to be:

(i)
finitely determined
if for all sequence
(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in E^{\mathbb{N}}
, it follows that
(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in X(E)
if and only if
\sup_{k}\|(x_{j})_{j=1}^{k}\|_{X(E)}<+\infty
and, in this case,
\|(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\|_{X(E)}=\sup_{k}\|(x_{j})_{j=1}^{k}\|_{X(E)}.

(ii)
spherically complete
if
(\lambda_{j}x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in X(E)
and
\|(\lambda_{j}x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\|_{X(E)}=\|(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\|_{X(E)}
whenever
(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in X(E)
and
(\lambda_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\mathbb{K}^{\mathbb{N}}
is such that
|\lambda_{j}|=1
for every
j
.

Example 1.2
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
p^{*}
defined by
1/p+1/{p^{*}}=1
, where we set
p^{*}=\infty
if

. The following rules define spherically complete and linearly stable sequence classes, endowed with their usual norms:

\bullet
The class
E\mapsto c_{0}(E)
of norm null sequences.

\bullet
The class
E\mapsto\ell_{\infty}(E)
of bounded sequences.

\bullet
The class
E\mapsto\ell_{p}(E)
of absolutely
p
-summable sequences.

\bullet
The class
E\mapsto\ell_{p}^{w}(E)
of weakly
p
-summable sequences

\ell_{p}^{w}(E):=\left\{(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in E^{\mathbb{N}}:\|(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\|_{w,p}=\sup_{\varphi\in B_{E^{\prime}}}\|(\varphi(x_{j}))_{j=1}^{\infty}\|_{p}<\infty\right\}.

\bullet
The class
E\mapsto\ell_{p}^{u}(E)
of unconditionally
p
-summable sequences
[
17
, Section 8.2]
:

\ell_{p}^{u}(E):=\left\{(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\ell_{p}^{w}(E):\lim_{k\to\infty}\|(x_{j})_{j=k}^{\infty}\|_{w,p}\to 0\right\},

with the norm inherited from
\ell_{p}^{w}(E)
.

\bullet
The class
E\mapsto\ell_{p}\langle E\rangle
of Cohen strongly
p
-summable sequences
[
15
]
:

\ell_{p}\langle E\rangle:=\left\{(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in E^{\mathbb{N}}:\|(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\|_{\ell_{p}\langle E\rangle}=\sup_{(\varphi_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in B_{\ell_{p^{*}}^{w}(E^{\prime})}}\|(\varphi_{j}(x_{j}))_{j=1}^{\infty}\|_{1}<\infty\right\}.

\bullet
The class
E\mapsto\ell_{p}^{\rm mid}(E)
of mid
p
-summable sequences
[
7
]
:

\ell_{p}^{\rm mid}(E)=\left\{(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in E^{\mathbb{N}}:\|(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\|_{p,{\rm mid}}=\sup\limits_{(\varphi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in B_{\ell_{p}^{w}(E^{\prime})}}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|\varphi_{n}(x_{j})|^{p}\right)^{1/p}<\infty\right\}.

(1)

All sequence classes presented above are finitely determined except
c_{0}(\cdot)
and
\ell_{p}^{u}
.

The dual of a sequence class
X
was introduced in
[
5
]
in the following fashion:

X^{\rm dual}(E)=\left\{(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\ \mathrm{in\ }E:\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{j}(x_{j})\ \mathrm{converges\ }\text{for every}\ (\varphi_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\ \mathrm{in\ }X(E^{\prime})\right\}.

If
X
is a linearly stable and spherically complete sequence class, the expression

\left\|(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\right\|_{X^{\rm dual}}:=\sup_{(\varphi_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in B_{X(E^{\prime})}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left|\varphi_{j}(x_{j})\right|=\sup_{(\varphi_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in B_{X(E^{\prime})}}\left|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{j}(x_{j})\right|

makes
X^{\rm dual}(E)
a Banach space and
X^{\rm dual}
a finitely determined, linearly stable and spherically complete sequence class (see
[
5
, Proposition 2.5]
).

Example 1.3
.

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we have
(\ell_{p}^{w})^{\rm dual}=\ell_{p^{*}}\langle\cdot\rangle
and
(\ell_{p})^{\rm dual}=\ell_{p^{*}}(\cdot)
. In particular,
(\ell_{\infty})^{\rm dual}=\ell_{1}(\cdot)

[
5
, Example 2.6(d)]
, which is somewhat surprising because
(\ell_{\infty})^{\prime}\neq\ell_{1}
.

2
Dual classes

In
[
5
]
, conditions on a sequence class
X
were established so that we have
X^{\rm dual}(E^{\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1}}{{=}}X(E)^{\prime}
, by the application

\displaystyle J\colon X^{\rm dual}(E^{\prime})\longrightarrow X(E)^{\prime}~,~J\left((\varphi_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\right)\left((x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{j}(x_{j}).

(2)

More specifically, this holds (
[
5
, Theorem 2.10]
) if
X
is
dual-representable
, i.e.,
X
is linearly stable, finitely determined, spherically complete, finitely injective and such that
c_{00}(E)
is dense in
X(E)
. By the way, a sequence class
X
is said to be
injective
if

\|(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\|_{X(E)}\leq\|(i(x_{j}))_{j=1}^{\infty}\|_{X(F)}

whenever
i\colon E\longrightarrow F
is a metric injection and
(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in E^{\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence that fulfills
(i(x_{j}))_{j=1}^{\infty}\in X(F)
. If we have only
\|(x_{j})_{j=1}^{k}\|_{X(E)}\leq\|(i(x_{j}))_{j=1}^{k}\|_{X(F)}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in E
, we say that
X
is
finitely injective
.

Example 2.1
.

The sequence classes
c_{0}(\cdot),\ell_{\infty}(\cdot),\ell_{p}(\cdot),\ell_{p}^{w}
and
\ell_{p}^{u}
are injective.

Some facts presented below are immediate consequences of the involved definitions.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
X
be a sequence class. It follows that:

a)

If
X
is injective, then it is also finitely injective;

b)

If
X
is finitely injective and finitely determined, then it is also injective;

c)

If
X
is linearly stable and injective, then
\|\cdot\|_{X(G)}=\|\cdot\|_{X(E)}\ {\rm in}\ X(G)
regardless of the subspace
G
of
E
.

The next example shows that, even though it is not possible to use the result
[
5
, Theorem 2.10]
to obtain the duality
X^{\rm dual}(E^{\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1}}{{=}}X(E)^{\prime}
, there may exist a sequence class
X^{\prime}
such that
X^{\prime}(E^{\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1}}{{=}}X(E)^{\prime}
.

Example 2.3
.

For
1<p<\infty
, the sequence class
X=\ell_{p}\langle\cdot\rangle
is not injective (this is not difficult to prove and is done in
[
29
, Proposição 4.4.17]
) and since it is finitely determined, from Proposition
2.2
b), the only option left is that it is not finitely injective too. So we cannot use the result
[
5
, Theorem 2.10]
to obtain the isomorphism in (
2
) for the sequence class
X^{\rm dual}
. However, as
{\cal L}(\ell_{p^{*}};E)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1}}{{=}}\ell_{p}^{w}(E)
(see
[

, Proposition 2.2]
) and
\ell_{p}\langle E\rangle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1}}{{=}}E\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}\ell_{p}
(see
[

, Section 2]
), we have

\ell_{p^{*}}^{w}(E^{\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1}}{{=}}{\cal L}(\ell_{p};E^{\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1}}{{=}}(\ell_{p}\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}E)^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1}}{{=}}(\ell_{p}\langle E\rangle)^{\prime}

where the second isomorphism comes from tensor product theory (see
[
35
, Section 2.2]
).

Motivated by the above circumstance, we are invited to explore sequence classes of type
X^{\prime}
for which, as the dual

of a class

, provide an isomorphism as in (

Definition 2.4
.

Let

be a sequence class. We say that a sequence class

if
X^{\prime}(E^{\prime})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1}}{{=}}X(E)^{\prime}
for any Banach space

\Psi:X^{\prime}(E^{\prime})\longrightarrow X(E)^{\prime}\ ,\ \Psi\left((\varphi_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\right)\left((x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{j}(x_{j}),

(3)

for all
(\varphi_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in X^{\prime}(E^{\prime})
and all
(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in X(E)
.

.

a) The sequence class

c) If

Definition 2.6
.

We say that a sequence class

has the
extension property
if for any Banach space

, every
M
subspace of

(\varphi_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in X(M^{\prime})

\|(\widetilde{\varphi}_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\|_{X(E^{\prime})}\leq\|(\varphi_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\|_{X(M^{\prime})}

Example 2.7
.

The sequence classes
c_{0}(\cdot),h_{M}(\cdot),\ell_{M}(\cdot)
and
\ell_{p}(\cdot)

Proposition 2.8
.

If
X
is a linearly stable and injective sequence class, then every dual class
X^{\prime}
for

has the extension property.

Proof.

Let
M
be a subspace of
E
and
(\varphi_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in X^{\prime}(M^{\prime})
. Our hypothesis and Proposition
2.2
c) allow us to take
X(M)
as a normed subspace of

and we obtain the following diagram

(\varphi_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in X^{\prime}(M^{\prime})\longmapsto\varphi\in X(M)^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}\widetilde{\varphi}\in X(E)^{\prime}\longmapsto(\widetilde{\varphi}_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in X^{\prime}(E^{\prime}),

where
\widetilde{\varphi}
is a Hahn-Banach extension of

\|(\varphi_{j})^{\infty}_{j=1}\|_{X^{\prime}(M^{\prime})}=\|\varphi\|=\|\widetilde{\varphi}\|=\|(\widetilde{\varphi}_{j})^{\infty}_{j=1}\|_{X^{\prime}(E^{\prime})}

and also

\varphi((x_{j})^{\infty}_{j=1})=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{j}(x_{j})\textrm{ and }\widetilde{\varphi}((x_{j})^{\infty}_{j=1})=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\widetilde{\varphi}_{j}(x_{j}),

for all

and for all

, we have

and so
\widetilde{\varphi}_{j}(x)=\widetilde{\varphi}(x\cdot e_{j})=\varphi(x\cdot e_{j})=\varphi_{j}(x).

∎

3
Injective-type norms

Let us now introduce a class of reasonable norms, involving sequence classes, on the tensor product
E\otimes F
. These norms are, from a certain point of view, generalizations of the injective norm
\varepsilon
.

We need to use certain definitions and results on the scalar component
X(\mathbb{K})
of a sequence class
X
and so we present some of the required theory of scalar-valued sequence spaces. Next, for the convenience of the reader, we present some definitions in the scope of tensor norms.

Definition 3.1
.

[
10
, Definition 3.1]
A
scalar sequence space

\lambda
is a Banach space formed by scalar-valued sequences, that is,
\lambda\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{\mathbb{N}}
, endowed with the usual coordinatewise algebraic operations, satisfying the following conditions:
(i)
c_{00}\subseteq\lambda\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1}}{{\hookrightarrow}}\ell_{\infty}
.
(ii)
(e_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
is a Schauder basis for
\lambda
.
(iii)
\|e_{j}\|_{\lambda}=1
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

Defining also the sequence space

\lambda_{*}:=\{(\varphi(e_{j}))_{j=1}^{\infty}:\varphi\in\lambda^{*}\}\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{\mathbb{N}}

we have the following

Proposition 3.2
.

[
10
, Proposition 3.3]
Let
\lambda
be a scalar sequence space. Then
\lambda_{*}
is a linear space with the usual algebraic operations, the map

\|\cdot\|_{\lambda_{*}}\colon\lambda_{*}\to[0,\infty)~,~\|(\varphi(e_{j}))_{j=1}^{\infty}\|_{\lambda_{*}}=\|\varphi\|,

is a norm on
\lambda_{*}
and the correspondence

\varphi\in\lambda^{\prime}\mapsto(\varphi(e_{j}))_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\lambda_{*}

is an isometric isomorphism. In particular,
\lambda_{*}
is a Banach space. Besides that, we have:

a)
for every
(\alpha_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\lambda
,
(\alpha_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{j}e_{j}
and
b)

c_{00}\subseteq\lambda_{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1}}{{\hookrightarrow}}\ell_{\infty}
and
\|e_{j}\|_{\lambda_{*}}=1
,
\forall j\in\mathbb{N}
.

Example 3.3
.

The following are scalar sequence spaces:

\lambda=\ell_{p}
with
\lambda_{*}=\ell_{p^{*}}
for every
1<p<\infty
,
\lambda=c_{0}
with
\lambda_{*}=\ell_{1}
,
\lambda=\ell_{1}
with
\lambda_{*}=\ell_{\infty}
,
\lambda=h_{M}
with
\lambda_{*}=\ell_{M^{*}}
(see
[
10
, Examples 3.2]
) and
\lambda=\ell_{M}
with
\lambda_{*}=\ell_{M^{*}}
if
M
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition at zero.

The norms
\varepsilon
and
\pi
on
E\otimes F
are given by (see
[
35
, Chapters 2 and 3]
)

\varepsilon(u)=\sup\left\{\left|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\varphi(x_{j})\psi(y_{j})\right|:\varphi\in B_{E^{\prime}},\psi\in B_{F^{\prime}}\right\},

where
\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}\otimes y_{j}
is any representation ou
u
, and

\pi(u)=\inf\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{k}\|x_{j}\|\|y_{j}\|\right\}
,
the infimum being taken over all representations of
u
. We need to use the symbol
B(E,F)
to represent the space of all bilinear forms on
E\times F
.

We say that a norm
\alpha
on
E\otimes F
is
a reasonable crossnorm
if: (i)
\alpha(x\otimes y)\leq\|x\|\cdot\|u\|
for all
x\in E
and
y\in F
. (ii) The linear functional
\varphi\otimes\psi
on
E\otimes F
is continuous and
\|\varphi\otimes\psi\|\leq\|\varphi\|\|\psi\|
for all
\varphi\in E^{\prime}
and
\psi\in F^{\prime}
. A characterization given in
[
35
, Proposition 6.1]
ensures that
\alpha
is a reasonable crossnorm if and only if
\varepsilon(u)\leq\alpha(u)\leq\pi(u)
for every
u\in E\otimes F
.

We say that a correspondence that assigns, for all Banach spaces
E
and
F
, a norm
\alpha
on
E\otimes F
is
a tensor norm
if:

\bullet

\alpha
is a reasonable crossnorm.

\bullet

\alpha
is
uniform
, that is, for all Banach spaces
E_{1},E_{2},F_{1},F_{2}
and all operators
T_{i}\in{\cal L}(E_{i},F_{i})
,
i=1,2
, holds
\|T_{1}\otimes T_{2}\colon E_{1}\otimes_{\alpha}E_{2}\longrightarrow F_{1}\otimes_{\alpha}F_{2}\|\leq\|T_{1}\|\cdot\|T_{2}\|
.

\bullet

\alpha
is
finitely generated
, that is, for all Banach spaces
E,F
and any
u\in E\otimes F
,

\alpha(u;E\otimes F)=\inf\left\{\alpha(u;M\otimes N):u\in M\otimes N,M\in{\cal F}(E),N\in{\cal F}(F)\right\},

where
{\cal F}(E)
is the collection of all finite dimensional subspaces of
E
.

We can now introduce our class of norms.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
E
and
F
be Banach spaces and
\lambda
be a scalar sequence space. Then, the expression

\alpha_{\lambda}(u)=\sup\left\{\left|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(x_{j})\psi_{n}(y_{j})\right|:(\varphi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in B_{X(E^{\prime})},(\psi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in B_{Y(F^{\prime})}\right\}

defines a reasonable crossnorm on
E\otimes F
for any linearly stable sequence classes
X
and
Y
that meet the conditions
X(\mathbb{K})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1}}{{\hookrightarrow}}\lambda
and
Y(\mathbb{K})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1}}{{\hookrightarrow}}\lambda_{*}
.

Proof.

For
x\in E
and
y\in F
, we define the form

\displaystyle B_{x,y}:

\displaystyle X(E^{\prime})\times Y(F^{\prime})\longrightarrow\mathbb{K}

\displaystyle((\varphi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty},(\psi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty})\longmapsto B_{x,y}((\varphi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty},(\psi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(x)\psi_{n}(y).

Note that for any
x\in E
the linear operator
T_{x}:E^{\prime}\to\mathbb{K}
given by
T_{x}(\varphi)=\varphi(x)
is continuous and
\|T_{x}\|=\|x\|
. So, the linear stability of
X
gives us

\|(\varphi_{n}(x))^{\infty}_{n=1}\|_{X(\mathbb{K})}=\|(T_{x}(\varphi_{n}))_{n=1}^{\infty}\|_{X(\mathbb{K})}\leq\|x\|\left\|(\varphi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\right\|_{X(E^{\prime})},

for all
(\varphi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in X(E^{\prime})
. Thus, as
X(\mathbb{K})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1}}{{\hookrightarrow}}\lambda
and
Y(\mathbb{K})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1}}{{\hookrightarrow}}\lambda_{*}
, the application
B_{x,y}
is well-defined since

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(x)\psi_{n}(y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|(\varphi_{n}(x))^{\infty}_{n=1}\|_{\lambda}\|(\psi_{n}(y))^{\infty}_{n=1}\|_{\lambda_{*}}

(4)

\displaystyle\leq\|(\varphi_{n}(x))^{\infty}_{n=1}\|_{X(\mathbb{K})}\|(\psi_{n}(y))^{\infty}_{n=1}\|_{Y(\mathbb{K})}

\displaystyle\leq\|x\|\|y\|\left\|(\varphi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\right\|_{X(E^{\prime})}\left\|(\psi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\right\|_{Y(F^{\prime})}<\infty,

where the first inequality comes from a Hölder-type inequality for scalar sequence spaces (see
[
10
, Lemma 3.9]
). It easy to see that
B_{x,y}
is bilinear and its continuity follows from (
4
). It is also easy to show that the application
A:E\times F\longrightarrow B(X(E^{\prime}),Y(F^{\prime}))
given by
A(x,y)=B_{x,y}
is bilinear and we can take its linearization

A_{L}:E\otimes F\longrightarrow B(X(E^{\prime}),Y(F^{\prime}))\ ,\ u=\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}\otimes y_{j}\longmapsto\sum_{j=1}^{k}B_{x_{j},y_{j}},

for which
\sum_{j=1}^{k}B_{x_{j},y_{j}}((\varphi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty},(\psi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(x_{j})\psi_{n}(y_{j})
. Following in a similar way to the calculus made in (
4
), we get

\displaystyle\left|A_{L}(u)((\varphi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty},(\psi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(x_{j})\psi_{n}(y_{j})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|(\varphi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\right\|_{X(E^{\prime})}\left\|(\psi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\right\|_{Y(F^{\prime})}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\|x_{j}\|\|y_{j}\|,

that is,
A_{L}
maps
E\otimes F
into continuous bilinear forms. A straightforward calculus shows that
A_{L}
is injective and all this allows us to define a norm
\alpha_{\lambda}
on
E\otimes F
by setting

\alpha_{\lambda}(u):=\left\|A_{L}(u)\right\|=\sup\left\{\left|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(x_{j})\psi_{n}(y_{j})\right|:(\varphi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in B_{X(E^{\prime})},(\psi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in B_{Y(F^{\prime})}\right\}.

It is immediate that
\varepsilon\leq\alpha_{\lambda}
and, reasoning as in (
4
), we obtain

\alpha_{\lambda}(x\otimes y)=\sup\left\{\left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(x)\psi_{n}(y)\right|:(\varphi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in B_{X(E^{\prime})},(\psi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in B_{Y(F^{\prime})}\right\}\leq\left\|x\right\|\left\|y\right\|.

This and the fact that
\alpha_{\lambda}
satisfies the triangle inequality by its definition yield that
\alpha_{\lambda}\leq\pi
. Thus
\alpha_{\lambda}
is a reasonable crossnorm on
E\otimes F
.
∎

The symbol
E\otimes_{\alpha_{\lambda}}F
denotes the tensor product
E\otimes F
endowed with the norm
\alpha_{\lambda}
and its completion will be denoted by
E\widehat{\otimes}_{\alpha_{\lambda}}F
.

Proposition 3.6
.

The norm
\alpha_{\lambda}
is uniform regardless of the scalar sequence space
\lambda
.

Proof.

In what follows the operator
T^{\prime}:F^{\prime}\to E^{\prime}
is the adjoint operator of
T\in\mathcal{L}(E;F)
.

Let
E_{i}
and
F_{i}
be Banach spaces and
T_{i}\in\mathcal{L}(E_{i};F_{i}),i=1,2
. Consider the linear operator
T_{1}\otimes T_{2}:E_{1}\otimes_{\alpha_{\lambda}}E_{2}\longrightarrow F_{1}\otimes_{\alpha_{\lambda}}F_{2}
given by
T_{1}\otimes T_{2}(x\otimes y)=T_{1}(x)\otimes T_{2}(y)
. Since
X
and
Y
are linearly stable, we get

\left\|(\varphi_{n}\circ T_{1})_{n=1}^{\infty}\right\|_{X(E_{1}^{\prime})}=\left\|(T_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi_{n}))_{n=1}^{\infty}\right\|_{X(E_{1}^{\prime})}\leq\left\|T_{1}\right\|\left\|(\varphi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\right\|_{X(F_{1}^{\prime})}

whenever
(\varphi_{n})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in X(F_{1}^{\prime})
, and the same can be done for
Y
with the operator
T_{2}
. So, for
u\in E_{1}\otimes_{\alpha_{\lambda}}E_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\alpha_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\left(T_{1}\otimes T_{2}(u),F_{1}\otimes F_{2}\right)=

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\left|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(\varphi_{n}\circ T_{1})(x_{j})(\psi_{n}\circ T_{2})(y_{j})\right|:(\varphi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in B_{X(F_{1}^{\prime})},(\psi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in B_{Y(F_{2}^{\prime})}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\|T_{1}\right\|\left\|T_{2}\right\|\cdot

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\sup\left\{\left|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{T_{1}^{\prime}}{\left\|T_{1}\right\|}(\varphi_{n})(x_{j})\frac{T_{2}^{\prime}}{\left\|T_{2}\right\|}(\psi_{n})(y_{j})\right|:(\varphi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in B_{X(F_{1}^{\prime})},(\psi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in B_{Y(F_{2}^{\prime})}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|T_{1}\right\|\left\|T_{2}\right\|\sup\left\{\left|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\overline{\varphi}_{n}(x_{j})\overline{\psi}_{n}(y_{j})\right|:(\overline{\varphi}_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in B_{X(E_{1}^{\prime})},(\overline{\psi}_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in B_{Y(E_{2}^{\prime})}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\|T_{1}\right\|\left\|T_{2}\right\|\alpha_{\lambda}(u,E_{1}\otimes E_{2})

and therefore
T_{1}\otimes T_{2}
is continuous and
\left\|T_{1}\otimes T_{2}\right\|\leq\left\|T_{1}\right\|\left\|T_{2}\right\|.

∎

Proposition 3.7

Proof.

subspaces of

, respectively. Since

4
The dual of the space
E\widehat{\otimes}_{\alpha_{\lambda}^{\prime}}F

In the context of the

and

and

The following lemma shows that
E\widehat{\otimes}_{\alpha_{\lambda}^{\prime}}F
can be identified as a subspace of the Banach space
\left(C(B_{X(E)^{\prime}}\times B_{Y(F)^{\prime}}),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\infty}\right)
, where
B_{X(E)^{\prime}}
and
B_{Y(F)^{\prime}}
carry their weak
∗

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
E
and
F
be Banach spaces and
\lambda
be a scalar sequence space. The map

\varPhi:E\otimes_{\alpha_{\lambda}^{\prime}}F\longrightarrow C(B_{X(E)^{\prime}}\times B_{Y(F)^{\prime}})\ ,\ u=\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}\otimes y_{j}\longmapsto\varPhi_{u},

where
\varPhi_{u}
is given by
\varPhi_{u}(\varphi,\psi)=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(x_{j})\psi_{n}(y_{j})
, is a linear isometry. In particular, its extension
\widetilde{\varPhi}:E\widehat{\otimes}_{\alpha_{\lambda}^{\prime}}F\longrightarrow C(B_{X(E)^{\prime}}\times B_{Y(F)^{\prime}})
is also a embedding.

Proof.

Let us first show that
\varPhi
is well-defined. Let
(\varphi^{\gamma},\psi^{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
a net in
B_{X(E)^{\prime}}\times B_{Y(F)^{\prime}}
such that
(\varphi^{\gamma},\psi^{\gamma})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\gamma}}{{\longrightarrow}}(\varphi,\psi)\in B_{X(E)^{\prime}}\times B_{Y(F)^{\prime}}.
Since the projections are continuous, we have
\varphi^{\gamma}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\gamma}}{{\longrightarrow}}\varphi
and
\psi^{\gamma}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\gamma}}{{\longrightarrow}}\psi
in the respective weak
∗
topologies and thus

\varphi^{\gamma}((x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\gamma}}{{\longrightarrow}}\varphi((x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty})\text{\ \ \ and \ \ }\psi^{\gamma}((y_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\gamma}}{{\longrightarrow}}\psi((y_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty})

(5)

for all
((x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty},(y_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty})\in X(E)\times Y(F)
.

For each
\gamma\in\Gamma
, we can write
\varphi^{\gamma}=(\varphi_{n}^{\gamma})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in X^{\prime}(E^{\prime})
,
\psi^{\gamma}=(\psi_{n}^{\gamma})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in Y^{\prime}(F^{\prime})
and also
\varphi=(\varphi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in X^{\prime}(E^{\prime})
and
\psi=(\psi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in Y^{\prime}(F^{\prime})
. From the isomorphism in (
3
) and by (
5
), we get

\varphi_{n}^{\gamma}(x)=\varphi^{\gamma}(x\cdot e_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\gamma}}{{\longrightarrow}}\varphi(x\cdot e_{n})=\varphi_{n}(x)\ \text{ and }\ \psi_{n}^{\gamma}(y)=\psi^{\gamma}(y\cdot e_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\gamma}}{{\longrightarrow}}\psi(y\cdot e_{n})=\psi_{n}(y)

for all
x\in E
,
y\in F
and all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore,

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{n=1}^{l}\varphi_{n}^{\gamma}(x_{j})\psi_{n}^{\gamma}(y_{j})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\gamma}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{n=1}^{l}\varphi_{n}(x_{j})\psi_{n}(y_{j}),

(6)

for all
l\in\mathbb{N}
. On the other hand, by the well-definition of the norm
\alpha_{\lambda}^{\prime}
, we have

\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{n=1}^{l}\varphi_{n}^{\gamma}(x_{j})\psi_{n}^{\gamma}(y_{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}^{\gamma}(x_{j})\psi_{n}^{\gamma}(y_{j})\ \text{for all}\ \gamma\in\Gamma,

(7)

and

\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{n=1}^{l}\varphi_{n}(x_{j})\psi_{n}(y_{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(x_{j})\psi_{n}(y_{j}).

(8)

Using (
6
) and (
8
) it is not difficult to show that

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{n=1}^{l}\varphi_{n}^{\gamma}(x_{j})\psi_{n}^{\gamma}(y_{j})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(l,\gamma)}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(x_{j})\psi_{n}(y_{j})

(9)

and from (
6
), (
7
) and (
9
) it follows that the iterated limits are the same and

\lim\limits_{l\rightarrow\infty}\lim\limits_{\gamma}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{n=1}^{l}\varphi_{n}^{\gamma}(x_{j})\psi_{n}^{\gamma}(y_{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(x_{j})\psi_{n}(y_{j})=\lim\limits_{\gamma}\lim\limits_{l\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{n=1}^{l}\varphi_{n}^{\gamma}(x_{j})\psi_{n}^{\gamma}(y_{j}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\gamma}\varPhi_{u}(\varphi^{\gamma},\psi^{\gamma})

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{\gamma}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}^{\gamma}(x_{j})\psi_{n}^{\gamma}(y_{j})=\lim\limits_{\gamma}\lim_{l\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{n=1}^{l}\varphi_{n}^{\gamma}(x_{j})\psi_{n}^{\gamma}(y_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(x_{j})\psi_{n}(y_{j})=\varPhi_{u}(\varphi,\psi)

and this shows us that
\varPhi_{u}
is continuous and
\varPhi
is well-defined. Let us now prove that
\varPhi
is linear. If
u,v\in E\otimes F
,
\beta\in\mathbb{K}
,
\varphi\in B_{X(E)^{\prime}}
and
\psi\in B_{Y(F)^{\prime}}
, then

\varPhi_{\beta u+v}(\varphi,\psi)=A_{L}(\beta u+v)(\varphi,\psi)=\beta A_{L}(u)(\varphi,\psi)+A_{L}(v)(\varphi,\psi)=\beta\varPhi_{u}(\varphi,\psi)+\varPhi_{v}(\varphi,\psi),

where
A_{L}
is the linearization of
A:E\times F\longrightarrow B(X^{\prime}(E^{\prime}),Y^{\prime}(F^{\prime}))
given by
A(x,y)=B_{x,y}
(cf. Proposition
3.4
).
Therefore
\Phi
is linear and it is immediate that
\left\|\varPhi_{u}\right\|_{\infty}=\alpha_{\lambda}^{\prime}(u)
for all
u\in E\otimes F
.
∎

Now we are in a position to characterize all continuous linear functional on

.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
E
and
F
be Banach spaces,
B:E\times F\longrightarrow\mathbb{K}
a bilinear form and
\lambda
a scalar sequence space. Then,
B_{L}:E\widehat{\otimes}_{\alpha_{\lambda}^{\prime}}F\longrightarrow\mathbb{K}
is a continuous linear functional if and only if there exists a regular Borel measure
\mu
on the compact
B_{X(E)^{\prime}}\times B_{Y(F)^{\prime}}
such that

B(x,y)=\int_{B_{X(E)^{\prime}}\times B_{Y(F)^{\prime}}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(x)\psi_{n}(y)d\mu(\varphi,\psi)

for all
x\in E
and
y\in F
. Furthermore,
\left\|B_{L}\right\|=\inf\left\|\mu\right\|
, where
\mu
ranges over the set of all measures that correspond to
B
in this way, and this infimum is attained.

Proof.

Suppose that
B_{L}:E\widehat{\otimes}_{\alpha_{\lambda}^{\prime}}F\longrightarrow\mathbb{K}
is a continuous linear functional. From the Lemma
4.1
, we can identify
E\widehat{\otimes}_{\alpha_{\lambda}^{\prime}}F
as a subspace of
C(B_{X(E)^{\prime}}\times B_{Y(F)^{\prime}})
and the Hahn-Banach Theorem ensures the existence of a continuous linear functional
\widetilde{B_{L}}:C(B_{X(E)^{\prime}}\times B_{Y(F)^{\prime}})\longrightarrow\mathbb{K}
that extends
B_{L}
and satisfies
\left\|B_{L}\right\|=\|\widetilde{B_{L}}\|.

By the Riesz Representation Theorem, the functional
\widetilde{B_{L}}
is given by a single regular complex Borel measure
\mu
in the form

\widetilde{B_{L}}(f)=\int_{B_{X(E)^{\prime}}\times B_{Y(F)^{\prime}}}f(\varphi,\psi)\ d\mu(\varphi,\psi),

for each
f\in C(B_{X(E)^{\prime}}\times B_{Y(F)^{\prime}})
and
\|\widetilde{B_{L}}\|=\left\|\mu\right\|.
In particular, using Lemma
4.1
for
f=x\otimes y
we get

B(x,y)=B_{L}(x\otimes y)=\widetilde{B_{L}}(x\otimes y)=\int_{B_{X(E)^{\prime}}\times B_{Y(F)^{\prime}}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(x)\psi_{n}(y)d\mu(\varphi,\psi)

for all
x\in E
and
y\in F.

Conversely, since
B_{L}
is the linearization of
B
, if
u\in E\otimes F
and
\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}\otimes y_{j}
is a representation of
u
, we have

\displaystyle B_{L}\left(u\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}B_{L}(x_{j}\otimes y_{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}B(x_{j},y_{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\int_{B_{X(E)^{\prime}}\times B_{Y(F)^{\prime}}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(x_{j})\psi_{n}(y_{j})d\mu(\varphi,\psi)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{X(E)^{\prime}}\times B_{Y(F)^{\prime}}}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(x_{j})\psi_{n}(y_{j})d\mu(\varphi,\psi).

From Lemma
4.1
we known that
u=\Phi_{u}\in C(B_{X(E)^{\prime}}\times B_{Y(F)^{\prime}})
and so
u
is a Borel-measurable function. Moreover, since
\mu
is a complex measure then
|\mu|
is a positive finite measure and so
u\in L_{1}(|\mu|)
because

\displaystyle\int_{B_{X(E)^{\prime}}\times B_{Y(F)^{\prime}}}\left|u\right|\ d|\mu|(\varphi,\psi)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{X(E)^{\prime}}\times B_{Y(F)^{\prime}}}\left|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(x_{j})\psi_{n}(y_{j})\right|d|\mu|(\varphi,\psi)

\displaystyle\leq\|\Phi_{u}\|_{\infty}\int_{B_{X(E)^{\prime}}\times B_{Y(F)^{\prime}}}d|\mu|(\varphi,\psi)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{\lambda}^{\prime}(u)\left|\mu\right|\left(B_{X(E)^{\prime}}\times B_{Y(F)^{\prime}}\right)=\alpha_{\lambda}^{\prime}(u)\left\|\mu\right\|<\infty.

Thus, from the Radon-Nikodym Theorem, there exists a
|\mu|
-integrable function
h
on
B_{X(E)^{\prime}}\times B_{Y(F)^{\prime}}
such that
|h|=1
and

\int_{B_{X(E)^{\prime}\times Y(F)^{\prime}}}u\ d\mu(\varphi,\psi)=\int_{B_{X(E)^{\prime}\times Y(F)^{\prime}}}uh\ d|\mu|(\varphi,\psi),

which brings us to

\displaystyle\left|B_{L}(u)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{B_{X(E)^{\prime}}\times B_{Y(F)^{\prime}}}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(x_{j})\psi_{n}(y_{j})d\mu(\varphi,\psi)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{B_{X(E)^{\prime}}\times B_{Y(F)^{\prime}}}u\ d\mu(\varphi,\psi)\right|=\left|\int_{B_{X(E)^{\prime}}\times B_{Y(F)^{\prime}}}uh\ d|\mu|(\varphi,\psi)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{X(E)^{\prime}}\times B_{Y(F)^{\prime}}}\left|u\right|\ d|\mu|(\varphi,\psi)\leq\alpha_{\lambda}^{\prime}(u)\left\|\mu\right\|

and therefore
B_{L}:E\otimes_{\alpha_{\lambda}^{\prime}}F\longrightarrow\mathbb{K}
is continuous. It follows that the extension
B_{L}:E\widehat{\otimes}_{\alpha_{\lambda}^{\prime}}F\longrightarrow\mathbb{K}
(here we use the same symbol) is continuous and
\left\|B_{L}\right\|\leq\left\|\mu\right\|.
Finally, from what we did now and in the previous implication, we have
\left\|B_{L}\right\|=\inf\left\|\mu\right\|
and that this infimum is attained.
∎

We have just characterized the elements of
(E\widehat{\otimes}_{\alpha_{\lambda}^{\prime}}F)^{\prime}
in terms of certain bilinear forms. Let us define the Banach space formed by these applications.

Definition 4.3
.

We say that a bilinear form
B:E\times F\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
is
\lambda
-integral if
B_{L}:E\widehat{\otimes}_{\alpha_{\lambda}^{\prime}}F\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
is continuous. We denote the space of all bilinear
\lambda
-integral forms by
\mathcal{B}_{I,\lambda}(E,F)
, endowed with the norm

\left\|B\right\|_{{\cal B}_{I,\lambda}}=\inf\left\|\mu\right\|=\left\|B_{L}\right\|.

From what we proved in the previous theorem we have

\left(E\widehat{\otimes}_{\alpha_{\lambda}^{\prime}}F\right)^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1}}{{=}}\mathcal{B}_{I,\lambda}(E,F)
.

Remark 4.4
.

a) A noteworthy fact: since
\varepsilon\leq\alpha_{\lambda}^{\prime}
, one has

\mathcal{B}_{I}(E,F)\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{I,\lambda}(E,F),

for all Banach
E
and
F
, i.e., every integral bilinear form (see
[
35
, Section 3.4]
) is
\lambda
-integral.

b) The only requirement on the norm
\alpha_{\lambda}^{\prime}
in Theorem
4.2
is that it be a reasonable norm. For the cases where this norm is injective and if
M
and
N
are subspaces of
E
and
F
, respectively, it follows from the Hahn-Banach Theorem that every
\lambda
-integral form defined on
M\times N
can be extended to a
\lambda
-integral form defined on
E\times F
, with the same norm.

The
\lambda
-integral forms satisfy a characteristic property of multi-ideals and the proof of this fact will be omitted for its simplicity.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
E,F,G
and
H
be Banach spaces,
T\in\mathcal{L}(G;E)
,
S\in\mathcal{L}(H;F)
and
\lambda
be a scalar sequence space. If
B\in\mathcal{B}_{I,\lambda}(E,F)
, then the bilinear form bilinear
B^{\prime}=B\circ(T,S)
is
\lambda
-integral and

\left\|B^{\prime}\right\|_{{\cal B}_{I,\lambda}}\leq\left\|B\right\|_{{\cal B}_{I,\lambda}}\left\|T\right\|\left\|S\right\|.

5
Tensor characterization for
(\ell_{p}^{\rm mid})^{u}(E)

Tensor characterizations for sequence spaces are common in tensor product theory and operator theory. Distinguished examples of what has been said are the characterizations
\ell_{p}\langle E\rangle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1}}{{=}}E\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}\ell_{p}
(cf. Example
2.3
) and
\ell_{p}^{u}(E)=E\widehat{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}\ell_{p}
(see
[
17
, Section 8.2]
), for
1\leq p<\infty
.

To establish our characterization for the space
(\ell_{p}^{\rm mid})^{u}(E)
we first need to present the procedure
X\mapsto X^{u}
. This procedure was first studied in the thesis
[
28
]
(not published) and improved and detailed in
[
9
]
. A few definitions and results from this last reference will be enough.

A sequence class
X
is said to be
finitely shrinking
if, regardless of the Banach space
E
, the sequence
(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in X(E)
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, it holds

(x_{j})_{j\neq k}:=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k-1},x_{k+1},\ldots)\in X(E)\textrm{ and }\|(x_{j})_{j\neq k}\|_{X(E)}\leq\|(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\|_{X(E)}.

Suppose that
X
is finitely shrinking. If
(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in X(E)
, then
(x_{n},x_{n+1},\ldots)\in X(E)
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. For a Banach space
E
, we define

X^{u}(E):=\{(x_{j})_{j=1}\in X(E):\lim_{n}\|(x_{n},x_{n+1},\ldots)\|_{X(E)}=0\},

endowed with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{X(E)}
. It is proved in
[
9
, Proposition 4.5]
that the rule
E\mapsto X^{u}(E)
defines a sequence class and if
X
is linearly stable, then so is
X^{u}(\cdot)
.

Example 5.1
.

a) For every
1\leq p<\infty
it is immediate that
(\ell_{p}^{w})^{u}=\ell_{p}^{u}
and
(\ell_{p})^{u}=\ell_{p}(\cdot)
. We also have
(\ell_{\infty})^{u}=c_{0}(\cdot)
and by
[
22
, Theorem 3.10]
we get
(\ell_{p}\left\langle\cdot\right\rangle)^{u}=\ell_{p}\left\langle\cdot\right\rangle
.

b) For
1\leq p<\infty
, it is easy to check that the sequence class
\ell_{p}^{\rm mid}
is finitely shrinking. Furthermore, we have
(\ell_{p}^{\rm mid})^{u}\neq\ell_{p}^{\rm mid}
and
(\ell_{p}^{\rm mid})^{u}\neq\ell_{p}^{u}
(
[
9
, Proposition 4.9]
).

For the results of this section, which comprise the next theorem and its immediate corollary, it is necessary to precisely specify the sequence classes
X
and
Y
involved in the definition of our appropriate
\alpha_{\lambda}
. Here it is also necessary to use the fact (see (
1
)): for
(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in E^{\mathbb{N}}
, we have

(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\ell_{p}^{\rm mid}(E)\ \ \Leftrightarrow\ \ ((\varphi_{n}(x_{j}))_{n=1}^{\infty})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\ell_{p}(\ell_{p}),\ \forall\ (\varphi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell_{p}^{w}(E^{\prime}).

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
E
be a Banach space and
\lambda=\ell_{p}
,
1\leq p<\infty
. Taking
X=\ell_{p}^{w}(\cdot)
and
Y=\ell_{p^{*}}(\cdot)
we obtain the isometric isomorphism

(\ell_{p}^{\rm mid})^{u}(E)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1}}{{=}}E\widehat{\otimes}_{\alpha_{\ell_{p}}}\ell_{p}.

Proof.

Considering the operator

J\colon E\times\ell_{p}\to(\ell_{p}^{\rm mid})^{u}(E)\ ,\ (x,(a_{k})^{\infty}_{k=1})\mapsto(a_{k}x)^{\infty}_{k=1},

we have

\displaystyle\|J((x,(a_{k})^{\infty}_{k=1}))\|_{p,\rm mid}

\displaystyle=\sup_{(\varphi_{n})^{\infty}_{n=1}\in B_{\ell_{p}^{w}(E^{\prime})}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\varphi_{n}(a_{k}x)|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{(\varphi_{n})^{\infty}_{n=1}\in B_{\ell_{p}^{w}(E^{\prime})}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|^{p}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\varphi_{n}(x)|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\|(a_{k})^{\infty}_{k=1}\|_{p}\sup_{(\varphi_{n})^{\infty}_{n=1}\in B_{\ell_{p}^{w}(E^{\prime})}}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\varphi_{n}(x)|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\|(a_{k})^{\infty}_{k=1}\|_{p}\|x\cdot e_{1}\|_{p,\rm mid}=\|(a_{k})^{\infty}_{k=1}\|_{p}\|x\|<\infty

and as

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|(a_{k}x)^{\infty}_{k=n}\|_{p,\rm mid}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|(a_{k})^{\infty}_{k=n}\|_{p}\|x\|=0

it follows that
J
is well-defined. It is easy to prove that
J
is bilinear and its continuity follows from above calculus. We prove now that the linearization

J_{L}\colon E\otimes_{\alpha_{\ell_{p}}}\ell_{p}\to(\ell_{p}^{\rm mid})^{u}(E)\ ,\ u=\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}\otimes a_{i}\mapsto\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i_{k}}x_{i}\right)^{\infty}_{k=1}

of
J
is an isometry, where
\alpha_{\ell_{p}}
is given by

\alpha_{\ell_{p}}(u)=\sup\left\{\left|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(x_{j})\psi_{n}(y_{j})\right|:(\varphi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in B_{\ell_{p}^{w}(E^{\prime})},(\psi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in B_{\ell_{p^{*}}(\ell_{p^{*}})}\right\}.

Indeed, from the well-definition of
J_{L}
and the definition of
\ell_{p}^{\rm mid}(E)
, for any
u=\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}\otimes a_{i}\in E\otimes_{\alpha_{\ell_{p}}}\ell_{p}
, we have

\left(\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i_{k}}\varphi_{n}(x_{i})\right)^{\infty}_{n=1}\right)^{\infty}_{k=1}\in\ell_{p}(\ell_{p}),

for all
(\varphi_{n})^{\infty}_{n=1}\in\ell_{p}^{w}(E^{\prime})
. Thus,

\displaystyle\|J_{L}(u)\|_{p,\rm mid}

\displaystyle=\sup_{(\varphi_{n})^{\infty}_{n=1}\in B_{\ell_{p}^{w}(E^{\prime})}}\left\|\left(\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i_{k}}\varphi_{n}(x_{i})\right)^{\infty}_{n=1}\right)^{\infty}_{k=1}\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\sup_{(\varphi_{n})^{\infty}_{n=1}\in B_{\ell_{p}^{w}(E^{\prime})}}\left\|\left(\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i_{k}}\varphi_{n}(x_{i})\right)^{\infty}_{k=1}\right)^{\infty}_{n=1}\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\sup_{(\varphi_{n})^{\infty}_{n=1}\in B_{\ell_{p}^{w}(E^{\prime})}}\sup_{(b_{n})^{\infty}_{n=1}\in B_{\ell_{p*}(\ell_{p*})}}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}\left(\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i_{k}}\varphi_{n}(x_{i})\right)^{\infty}_{k=1}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\sup_{(\varphi_{n})^{\infty}_{n=1}\in B_{\ell_{p}^{w}(E^{\prime})}}\sup_{(b_{n})^{\infty}_{n=1}\in B_{\ell_{p*}(\ell_{p*})}}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{n_{k}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i_{k}}\varphi_{n}(x_{i})\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\sup_{(\varphi_{n})^{\infty}_{n=1}\in B_{\ell_{p}^{w}(E^{\prime})}}\sup_{(b_{n})^{\infty}_{n=1}\in B_{\ell_{p*}(\ell_{p*})}}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(x_{i})\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{n_{k}}a_{i_{k}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\sup_{(\varphi_{n})^{\infty}_{n=1}\in B_{\ell_{p}^{w}(E^{\prime})}}\sup_{(b_{n})^{\infty}_{n=1}\in B_{\ell_{p*}(\ell_{p*})}}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(x_{i})b_{n}(a_{i})\right|=\alpha_{\ell_{p}}(u)

and
J_{L}
is an isometry and extends to an isometry
J_{L}:E\widehat{\otimes}_{\alpha_{\ell_{p}}}\ell_{p}\to(\ell_{p}^{\rm mid})^{u}(E)
. Furthermore, it is easy to verify that
J_{L}(E\otimes\ell_{p})
is a dense subspace in
(\ell_{p}^{\rm mid})^{u}(E)
and, by the definition of completion, we can conclude that
J_{L}
is an isometric isomorphism.
∎

As far as we know, the class of sequences
\ell_{p}^{w}
is not a dual class for any class
X
if
p=1
. For the other choices of
1<p<\infty
, as
\ell_{p}^{w}
is dual for
\ell_{p^{*}}\langle\cdot\rangle
and
\ell_{p^{*}}(\cdot)
is dual for
\ell_{p}(\cdot)
, the norm
\alpha_{\ell_{p}}
can be interpreted as an
\alpha_{\ell_{p}}^{\prime}
norm and we immediately obtain the following

Corollary 5.3
.

The isometric isomorphisms

((\ell_{p}^{\rm mid})^{u}(E))^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1}}{{=}}(E\widehat{\otimes}_{\alpha_{\ell_{p}}^{\prime}}\ell_{p})^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1}}{{=}}\mathcal{B}_{I,\ell_{p}}(E,F)

hold for
1<p<\infty
.

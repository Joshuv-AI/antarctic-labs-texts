Close points on a modular hyperbola Dedicated to Melvyn Nathanson and Carl Pomerance on their 80th birthday
Tsz Ho Chan

Abstract
In this paper, we continue the study of small squares containing at least two points on a modular hyperbola x ​ y ≡ c ( mod p ) xy\equiv c\pmod{p} . We deduce a lower bound for its side length. We also investigate what happens if the “distances” between two such points are special type of numbers like prime numbers, squarefree numbers or smooth numbers as well as more general multiplicatively closed sets or almost dense sets.



1
Introduction and main results

Let
p>2
be a prime number and
c
be any integer such that
\gcd(c,p)=1
. The main object of study is the modular hyperbola:

\mathcal{H}_{p}^{c}:=\{(x,y):xy\equiv c\pmod{p}\}.

We exclude
p=2
as the the modular hyperbola
xy\equiv 1\pmod{2}
has one point only. Many people have studied the distribution of points on
\mathcal{H}_{p}^{c}
using exponential sum, Kloosterman sum or character sum methods. A very nice survey was written by Igor Shparlinski
[
6
]
. Recently, the author
[
1
]
considered small squares

B_{X,Y}(H):=\{(x,y):x\equiv X+i\;(\bmod\;p),y\equiv Y+j\;(\bmod\;p)\text{ for some }0\leq i,j\leq H\}

containing two points on
\mathcal{H}_{p}^{c}
. It was proved that, given any
\epsilon>0
and any
\gcd(c,p)=1
, there exists a constant
C_{\epsilon}>0
such that when
H=C_{\epsilon}p^{1/4+\epsilon}
,

|\mathcal{H}_{p}^{c}\cap B_{X,Y}(H)|\geq 2\;\text{ for some }\;0\leq X,Y\leq p-1.

(1)

Moreover, the above has connection with the least positive quadratic non-residue
n_{p}\,(\bmod\;p)
, namely, for some constant
C_{\epsilon}>0
, either

|\mathcal{H}_{p}^{c}\cap B_{X,Y}(C_{\epsilon}p^{1/6+\epsilon})|\geq 2\;\text{ for some }\;0\leq X,Y\leq p-1,

or

n_{p}\leq C_{\epsilon}p^{1/(6\sqrt{e})+\epsilon}.

In this paper, we deduce the lower bound
H\gg\sqrt{\log p}
to ensure (
1
) for all
c
.

Theorem 1
.

There exist infinitely many prime numbers
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
such that, for some
\gcd(c_{p},p)=1
,

|\mathcal{H}_{p}^{c_{p}}\cap B_{X,Y}(\sqrt{\lfloor 0.1\log p\rfloor})|\leq 1

for all
0\leq X,Y\leq p-1
.

Furthermore, we are interested in the situation where the “distances” between two such close points are some specific type of integers such as prime numbers, squarefree numbers, or smooth numbers.

Question 1
.

Given any infinite set
\mathcal{A}
of positive integers, what is the smallest
H
that guarantees the existence of
h,k\in\mathcal{A}\cap[1,H]
such that

(x+h,y+k)\in\mathcal{H}_{p}^{c}\;\;\text{ for some }\;\;(x,y)\in\mathcal{H}_{p}^{c}\;?

Towards this, we have the following results. First, we need some definitions.

Definition 1
.

A set
\mathcal{A}
is multiplicatively closed if
a\cdot b\in\mathcal{A}
whenever
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
.

Definition 2
.

An infinite set
\mathcal{A}
of positive integers has positive lower density
\delta>0
if

\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}\cap[1,X]}1\geq\delta X

for all sufficiently large
X
.

Definition 3
.

An infinite set
\mathcal{A}
of positive integers is
“almost dense”
if, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
c_{\epsilon,\mathcal{A}}>0
such that

\#\{X\leq n\leq 2X\,:\,n\in\mathcal{A}\}\geq c_{\epsilon,\mathcal{A}}X^{1-\epsilon}

(2)

for all sufficiently large
X
(in term of
\epsilon
).

Theorem 2
.

Given any real number
\epsilon>0
, prime number
p>2
, and integer
c
with
\gcd(c,p)=1
. Let
\mathcal{M}
be a multiplicative closed set with positive lower density
\delta
. Then there exists a constant
C_{\delta,\epsilon}>0
such that

(x+h,y+k)\in\mathcal{H}_{p}^{c}

for some
(x,y)\in\mathcal{H}_{p}^{c}
and some
h,k\in\mathcal{M}\cap[1,C_{\delta,\epsilon}\,p^{1/4}\exp((\log p)^{1/2+\epsilon})]
.

Recall that an integer is
y
-smooth
if all of its prime factors are less than or equal to
y
. It is well-known that
p^{\epsilon}
-smooth numbers have positive density in intervals
[1,p^{C}]
for any fixed
C>0
. Thus, Theorem
2
applies to
p^{\epsilon}
-smooth “distances” and improves on
H=C_{\epsilon}p^{1/4+\epsilon}
. Although squarefree numbers are not multiplicatively closed, we have a similar result.

Theorem 3
.

Given any real number
\epsilon>0
, prime number
p>2
, and integer
c
with
\gcd(c,p)=1
. Let
\mathcal{S}
be the set of squarefree numbers. Then there exists a constant
C_{\epsilon}>0
such that

(x+h,y+k)\in\mathcal{H}_{p}^{c}

for some
(x,y)\in\mathcal{H}_{p}^{c}
and some
h,k\in\mathcal{S}\cap[1,C_{\epsilon}\,p^{1/4}\exp((\log p)^{1/2+\epsilon})]
. Note: One can further restrict
h
to be a prime number and
k
to be a product of two distinct prime numbers.

Theorem 4
.

Given any real number
\epsilon>0
, prime number
p>2
, and integer
c
with
\gcd(c,p)=1
. Then, for any “almost dense” set of positive integers
\mathcal{A}
, there exists a constant
C_{\epsilon,\mathcal{A}}>0
such that

(x+h,y+k)\in\mathcal{H}_{p}^{c}

for some
(x,y)\in\mathcal{H}_{p}^{c}
and some
h,k\in\mathcal{A}\cap[1,C_{\epsilon,\mathcal{A}}\,p^{11/34+\epsilon}]
.

Thus, Theorem
4
applies to prime “distances” for example. The readers are encouraged to study other interesting integer “distances” or consider rectangles instead of squares.

The paper is organized as follows. First, we will transform the problem into a character sum problem as in
[
1
]
. Then we will prove Theorem
1
using Weil’s bound on character sums. Next, we will prove Theorems
2
and
3
using Burgess’s method. Finally, we will prove Theorem
4
using a double character sum estimate.

Notation.
Throughout the paper,
|\mathcal{A}|
stands for the cardinality of a set
\mathcal{A}
. The flooring function
\lfloor x\rfloor
stands for the largest integer less than or equal to
x
. The function
\exp(x)=e^{x}
. When an integer
a
divides another integer
b
, we abbreviate it as
a\mid b
. Given a prime
p
and
\gcd(n,p)=1
, we use
\overline{n}
to denote the multiplicative inverse of
n\,(\bmod\,p)
(i.e.,
n\cdot\overline{n}\equiv 1\pmod{p}
). The symbols
f(x)=O(g(x))
,
f(x)\ll g(x)
, and
g(x)\gg f(x)
are equivalent to
|f(x)|\leq Cg(x)
for some constant
C>0
. Also,
f(x)=O_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}}(g(x))
,
f(x)\ll_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}}g(x)
and
g(x)\gg_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}}f(x)
mean that the implicit constant may depend on the parameters
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}
.

2
Some preliminaries

Suppose
(x,y)
and
(x+h,y+k)
are two points on
\mathcal{H}_{p}^{c}
for some
1\leq h,k\leq H
. We have
(x+h)(y+k)\equiv xy\equiv c\pmod{p}
which is equivalent to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{r}kx^{2}+hc+hkx\equiv 0\pmod{p},\\
xy\equiv c\pmod{p}.\end{array}\right.

After completing the square of the first equation, we get
(2kx+hk)^{2}\equiv h^{2}k^{2}-4hkc\pmod{p}
. Hence, the statement (
1
) can be reduced to finding
1\leq h,k\leq H
such that

\Bigl(\frac{hk(hk-4c)}{p}\Bigr)=1\;\;\text{ or }\;\;\Bigl(\frac{k}{p}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{k-4c\overline{h}}{p}\Bigr)=1.

(3)

Here
(\frac{\cdot}{p})
is the Legendre symbol modulo
p
. Note that once we have
h
and
k
satisfying (
3
), we can reverse the above to find
x
and, hence, the two points
(x,y)
and
(x+h,y+k)
on
\mathcal{H}_{p}^{c}
. A key tool we need is the following Weil’s bound on character sums (see Theorem 11.23 in
[
3
]
for example).

Theorem 5
.

Let
\chi
be a non-principal multiplicative character modulo
p
of order
d>1
. Suppose
f\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
has
m
distinct roots and
f
is not a
d
-th power. Then, we have

\Big|\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\chi(f(x))\Big|\leq(m-1)\sqrt{p}.

(4)

3
Proof of Theorem
1

Suppose
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, we want to find
L/4<c<p-L/4
such that

\Bigl(\frac{l-4c}{p}\Bigr)=\Bigl(\frac{l+4c}{p}\Bigr)=-1\;\text{ for all }\;1\leq l\leq L

(5)

with some positive integer
L<n_{p}\ll p^{1/2}\log p
by Pólya-Vinogradov inequality. This would imply

\Bigl(\frac{hk}{p}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{hk-4c}{p}\Bigr)=-1\;\;\text{ for all }\;1\leq|h|,|k|\leq\sqrt{L}.

This together with (
1
) and (
3
) implies that
|\mathcal{H}_{p}^{c}\cap B(X,Y,\sqrt{L})|\leq 1
for all
0\leq X,Y\leq p-1
. To deduce (
5
), we consider the following sum

\Sigma:=\sum_{L/4\,<c<\,p-L/4}\,\prod_{k=1}^{L}\Bigl(\frac{1-(\frac{k-4c}{p})}{2}\Bigr)\cdot\prod_{l=1}^{L}\Bigl(\frac{1-(\frac{l+4c}{p})}{2}\Bigr).

Note that the contribution from the missing terms
1\leq c\leq L/4
and
p-L/4\leq c\leq p
is at most
L
. Inserting these missing terms, expanding things out and applying Weil’s bound (
4
), we get

\displaystyle\Sigma=

\displaystyle\frac{p}{2^{2L}}+\frac{1}{2^{2L}}\mathop{\sum_{0\leq r,s\leq L}}_{r+s>0}(-1)^{r+s}\sum_{1\leq k_{1}<k_{2}<\ldots<k_{r}\leq L}\,\sum_{1\leq l_{1}<l_{2}<\ldots<l_{s}\leq L}

\displaystyle\sum_{c=1}^{p}\Bigl(\frac{4c-k_{1}}{p}\Bigr)\cdots\Bigl(\frac{4c-k_{r}}{p}\Bigr)\cdot\Bigl(\frac{4c+l_{1}}{p}\Bigr)\cdots\Bigl(\frac{4c+l_{s}}{p}\Bigr)+O(L)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{p}{2^{2L}}+O(2^{2L}Lp^{1/2})\geq\frac{p}{2^{2L+1}}\geq p^{0.7}

with
L=\lfloor 0.1\log p\rfloor
when
p
is sufficiently large. This shows that (
5
) holds for some
L/4<c<p-L/4
. Finally, we have
n_{p}\gg\log p\cdot\log\log\log p>L
for infinitely many primes
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
by Graham and Ringrose
[
5
]
. Hence, we have Theorem
1
.

4
Proof of Theorem
2

Set

H:=p^{1/4}\exp(0.5(\log p)^{1/2+\epsilon}),\;\;T:=\exp(0.25(\log p)^{1/2+\epsilon})(\log p)^{1/2-\epsilon},\;\text{ and }\;K:=H\cdot T.

We want to find integers
h\in\mathcal{M}\cap[1,H]
and
k\in\mathcal{M}\cap[1,K]
satisfying

\Bigl(\frac{k}{p}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{k-4c\overline{h}}{p}\Bigr)=1.

(6)

Let
\mathcal{Q}_{p}
be the set of quadratic residues
(\bmod\;p)
. By pigeonhole principle and positive lower density of
\mathcal{M}
,

\Big|[1,T]\cap\mathcal{Q}_{p}\Big|\;\;\text{ or }\;\;\Big|[1,T]\backslash\mathcal{Q}_{p}\Big|\geq\frac{\delta T}{2}.

We pick
\mathcal{Z}_{0}
to be the bigger of these two sets. Next, we pick
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{Z}_{1}=\{1\leq z_{1}\leq H:z_{1}\in\mathcal{M}\}
. Then
|\mathcal{A}|,|\mathcal{Z}_{1}|\geq\delta H
by positive lower density.

Suppose (
6
) is false for all numbers
h\in\mathcal{M}\cap[1,H]
and
k\in\mathcal{M}\cap[1,K]
. Then

\Bigl(\frac{z_{0}-4c\overline{hz_{1}}}{p}\Bigr)=-\Bigl(\frac{z_{0}}{p}\Bigr),\text{ having the same value for all }h\in\mathcal{A},z_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}_{0},z_{1}\in\mathcal{Z}_{1},

apart from those tuples
(h,z_{0},z_{1})
with
hz_{0}z_{1}=r_{4c}
, the remainder of
4c
when divided by
p
. This yields

|S|:=\bigg|\mathop{\sum_{h\in\mathcal{A}}}_{z_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}_{0},z_{1}\in\mathcal{Z}_{1}}\Bigl(\frac{z_{0}-4c\overline{hz_{1}}}{p}\Bigr)\bigg|\geq|\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{Z}_{0}||\mathcal{Z}_{1}|-2d_{3}(r_{4c})\gg|\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{Z}_{0}||\mathcal{Z}_{1}|\gg_{\delta}H^{2}T.

(7)

Here
d_{3}(n)
stands for the number of ways to write
n
as a product of
3
positive integers. Let

w(n):=\#\{(h,z_{1})\in\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{Z}_{1}:hz_{1}n\equiv 4c\pmod{p}\}.

Clearly,
\sum_{n=1}^{p-1}w(n)=|\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{Z}_{1}|
. Define

W:=\sum_{n=1}^{p-1}w(n)^{2}=\#\{(h_{1},h_{2},z_{1},z_{2}):h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathcal{A},\,z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathcal{Z}_{1},\,h_{1}z_{1}=h_{2}z_{2}\}.

Given
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
, then any
z_{1}
determines
z_{2}
uniquely and vice versa. Also, observe that
\frac{h_{1}}{\gcd(h_{1},h_{2})}z_{1}=\frac{h_{2}}{\gcd(h_{1},h_{2})}z_{2}
and, hence,

\frac{h_{1}}{\gcd(h_{1},h_{2})}\,\Big|\,z_{2}\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\frac{h_{2}}{\gcd(h_{1},h_{2})}\,\Big|\,z_{1}.

Thus,

W=\sum_{h_{1}\leq h_{2}\in\mathcal{A}}\mathop{\sum_{z_{1}\in\mathcal{Z}_{1}}}_{\frac{h_{2}}{\gcd(h_{1},h_{2})}\mid z_{1}}1+\sum_{h_{2}<h_{1}\in\mathcal{A}}\mathop{\sum_{z_{2}\in\mathcal{Z}_{1}}}_{\frac{h_{1}}{\gcd(h_{1},h_{2})}\mid z_{2}}1=:W_{1}+W_{2}.

By
\sum_{m\leq x,d\mid m}1\leq\frac{x}{d}
and
\sum_{n\leq x}d(n)\ll x\log x
,

\displaystyle W_{1}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{h_{1}\leq h_{2}\in\mathcal{A}}\frac{H}{h_{2}/\gcd(h_{1},h_{2})}\leq H\sum_{h_{2}\in\mathcal{A}}\frac{1}{h_{2}}\sum_{\delta\mid h_{2}}\delta\sum_{h_{1}\leq h_{2},\;\delta\mid h_{1}}1\leq H\sum_{h_{2}\in\mathcal{A}}d(h_{2})

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle H^{2}\log H.

(8)

We have a similar bound for
W_{2}
and
W
as well. By Hölder’s inequality, (
4
) and (
4
), we have

\displaystyle|S|

\displaystyle=\Big|\sum_{n}w(n)\sum_{z_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}_{0}}\Bigl(\frac{z_{0}-n}{p}\Bigr)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl(\sum_{n}w(n)^{\frac{2R}{2R-1}}\Bigr)^{\frac{2R-1}{2R}}\Bigl(\sum_{n}\Bigl(\sum_{z_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}_{0}}\Bigl(\frac{z_{0}-n}{p}\Bigr)\Bigr)^{2R}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2R}}

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\sum_{n}w(n)^{\frac{2R}{2R-1}}\Bigr)^{\frac{2R-1}{2R}}\Bigl(\sum_{z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{2R}\in\mathcal{Z}_{0}}\sum_{n}\Bigl(\frac{n-z_{1}}{p}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{n-z_{2}}{p}\Bigr)\cdots\Bigl(\frac{n-z_{2R}}{p}\Bigr)\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2R}}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl(\sum_{n}w(n)\Bigr)^{\frac{2R-2}{2R}}\Bigl(\sum_{n}w(n)^{2}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2R}}\cdot\Bigl(\frac{(2R)!}{2^{R}R!}|\mathcal{Z}_{0}|^{R}p+2R|\mathcal{Z}_{0}|^{2R}\sqrt{p}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2R}}

\displaystyle\ll(H^{2})^{\frac{2R-2}{2R}}\bigl(H^{2}\log H\bigr)^{\frac{1}{2R}}\cdot\Bigl(\sqrt{R}\sqrt{T}\cdot p^{\frac{1}{2R}}+T\cdot(\sqrt{p})^{\frac{1}{2R}}\Bigr).

(9)

The term
\frac{(2R)!}{2^{R}R!}|\mathcal{Z}_{0}|^{R}p
comes from the diagonal contributions when
z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{2R}
can be partitioned into
R
distinct pairs
z_{i},z_{j}
with
z_{i}=z_{j}
. Pick
R=\lfloor 2(\log p)^{1/2-\epsilon}+1\rfloor
and recall
T=\exp(0.25(\log p)^{1/2+\epsilon})(\log p)^{1/2-\epsilon}
. One can check that

T\cdot(\sqrt{p})^{\frac{1}{2R}}\gg_{\epsilon}\sqrt{R}\sqrt{T}p^{\frac{1}{2R}}.

Hence, (
4
) yields

|S|\ll_{\epsilon}H^{2}T\cdot\Bigl(\frac{H^{2}}{p^{1/2}\log H}\Bigr)^{-\frac{1}{2R}}

which contradicts (
7
) as
H=p^{1/4}\exp(0.5(\log p)^{1/2+\epsilon})
. This proves the existence of some solution to (
6
) and, hence, Theorem
2
.

Proof of Theorem
3

Since this proof is very similar to that of Theorem
2
, we will highlight the necessary modifications only. We use the same choices for
H
and
T
but set
K:=2H\cdot T
. Let

\mathcal{A}:=\{1\leq h\leq H:h\text{ is prime}\}.

We have
|\mathcal{A}|\gg H/\log H
by Tchebyshev’s estimate. By pigeonhole principle,

\Big|\{1\leq z_{0}\leq T:z_{0}\text{ is prime}\}\cap\mathcal{Q}_{p}\Big|\;\text{ or }\;\Big|\{1\leq z_{0}\leq T:z_{0}\text{ is prime}\}\backslash\mathcal{Q}_{p}\Big|\gg\frac{T}{\log T},

and we pick
\mathcal{Z}_{0}
to be the bigger of these two sets. Finally, set

\mathcal{Z}_{1}:=\{H<z_{1}\leq 2H:z_{1}\text{ is prime}\}

and
|\mathcal{Z}_{1}|\gg H/\log H
. Suppose (
6
) is false for all squarefree numbers
1\leq h\leq H
and
1\leq k=z_{0}z_{1}\leq K
. Then

\Bigl(\frac{z_{0}-4c\overline{hz_{1}}}{p}\Bigr)=-\Bigl(\frac{z_{1}}{p}\Bigr),\text{ having the same value for all }h\in\mathcal{A},z_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}_{0},z_{1}\in\mathcal{Z}_{1},

apart from those tuples
(h,z_{0},z_{1})
such that
hz_{0}z_{1}=r_{4c}
. Similar to (
7
), we have

|S|:=\bigg|\mathop{\sum_{h\in\mathcal{A}}}_{z_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}_{0},z_{1}\in\mathcal{Z}_{1}}\Bigl(\frac{z_{0}-4c\overline{hz_{1}}}{p}\Bigr)\bigg|\gg|\mathcal{A}||\mathcal{Z}_{0}||\mathcal{Z}_{1}|\gg\frac{H^{2}}{\log^{2}H}\cdot\frac{T}{\log T}.

(10)

Recall

w(n)=\#\{(h,z_{1})\in\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{Z}_{1}:hz_{1}n\equiv 4c\pmod{p}\},

and

\displaystyle W=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{p-1}w(n)^{2}=\#\{(h_{1},h_{2},z_{1},z_{2}):h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathcal{A},\,z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathcal{Z}_{1},\,h_{1}z_{1}=h_{2}z_{2}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{z_{1}=z_{2}\in\mathcal{Z}_{1}}\,\sum_{h_{1}=h_{2}\in\mathcal{A}}1\ll\frac{H^{2}}{\log^{2}H}.

Following the same argument as in Theorem
2
, we have

|S|\ll_{\epsilon}\Bigl(\frac{H^{2}}{\log^{2}H}\Bigr)^{\frac{2R-2}{2R}}\Bigl(\frac{H^{2}}{\log^{2}H}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2R}}\cdot\frac{T}{\log T}\cdot(\sqrt{p})^{\frac{1}{2R}}\ll\frac{H^{2}}{\log^{2}H}\cdot\frac{T}{\log T}\cdot\Bigl(\frac{H^{2}}{\sqrt{p}\log^{2}H}\Bigr)^{-\frac{1}{2R}}

which contradicts (
10
) by the choice of
H
. This gives Theorem
3
.

6
Proof of Theorem
4

Karatsuba
[
4
]
studied double character sums and proved that, for some
\epsilon^{\prime}=\epsilon^{\prime}(\epsilon)>0
,

\sum_{x\in I}\Big|\sum_{y\in S}\chi(y+x)\Big|<p^{-\epsilon^{\prime}}|I||S|

(11)

for any interval
I\subset[1,p]
with
|I|=p^{\beta}
and any arbitrary subet
S\subset[1,p]
with
|S|=p^{\alpha}
satisfying

\alpha,\beta>\epsilon\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\alpha+2\beta>1+\epsilon

(12)

for some given
\epsilon>0
. Mei-Chu Chang
[
2
]
improved the above condition (
12
) to

\epsilon<\beta\leq\frac{1}{k}\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\Bigl(1-\frac{2}{3k}\Bigr)\alpha+\frac{2}{3}\Bigl(1+\frac{2}{k}\Bigr)\beta>\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{3k}+\epsilon

(13)

for some
\epsilon>0
and some integer
k>0
.

Suppose
\alpha=\beta=11/34+\epsilon
. One can check that (
13
) is satisfied with
k=3
. Let
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{A}\cap[1,p^{\alpha}]
. By (
11
),

\Big|\sum_{k,h\in\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}\Bigl(\frac{k}{p}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{k-4c\overline{h}}{p}\Bigr)\Big|\leq\sum_{k\in\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}\Big|\sum_{h\in\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}\Bigl(\frac{k-4c\overline{h}}{p}\Bigr)\Big|\leq\sum_{k\leq p^{\alpha}}\Big|\sum_{h\in\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}}\Bigl(\frac{-4c\overline{h}+k}{p}\Bigr)\Big|<p^{\alpha-\epsilon^{\prime}}|\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}|.

(14)

If
(\frac{k}{p})(\frac{k-4c\overline{h}}{p})\neq 1
for all
h,k\in\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
, then
(\frac{k}{p})(\frac{k-4c\overline{h}}{p})=0
for at most
|\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}|
of the
h
’s, and the rest satisfy
(\frac{k}{p})(\frac{k-4c\overline{h}}{p})=-1
. Thus, inequality (
14
) implies
|\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}|^{2}-|\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}|<p^{\alpha-\epsilon^{\prime}}|\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}|
which contradicts
|\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}|\geq c_{\epsilon^{\prime}/2,\mathcal{A}}\cdot p^{\alpha-\epsilon^{\prime}/2}
as
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
is “almost dense”. Therefore,
(\frac{k}{p})(\frac{k-4c\overline{h}}{p})=1
for some
h,k\in\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
and, hence, Theorem
4
is true.

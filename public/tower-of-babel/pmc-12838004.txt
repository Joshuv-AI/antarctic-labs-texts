Cadonilimab plus chemotherapy compared to chemotherapy as first-line treatment for persistent, recurrent, or metastatic cervical cancer in China: a cost-effectiveness analysis
Authors: Longfeng Zhang, Qingsheng Yang, Huide Zhu, Zhiwei Zheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo assess the cost-effectiveness of cadonilimab plus chemotherapy compared to chemotherapy alone as first-line treatment for metastatic cervical cancer from the Chinaese medical healthcare perspective.MethodsWe developed a partitioned survival mode to simulate the costs and outcomes associated with the two treatment strategies over a lifetime horizon. Clinical data, cost resource utilization, and health-related quality of life data were obtained from the COMPASSION-16 trial and published literature. The model’s robustness was tested through sensitivity analyses.ResultsThe total cost of treatment for the cadonilimab group in this study was $86,839.48, whereas the cost for the chemotherapy group was $36,770.38. The cadonilimab group demonstrated an incremental gain of 0.70 quality-adjusted life years, resulting in an additional cost of $50,069.10. The incremental cost-effectiveness ratio for the cadonilimab group was determined to be $71,527.28 per QALY, which surpasses the willingness to pay threshold of $40,343.68. Sensitivity analyses demonstrated that the results were robust across varying assumptions of key parameters.ConclusionThe use of cadonilimab plus chemotherapy as a first-line treatment for metastatic cervical cancer was not a cost-effective strategy compared to chemotherapy alone in China.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12913-025-13871-7.Keywords: Cost-effectiveness analysis, Cadonilimab, Chemotherapy, Metastatic cervical cancer, Quality-adjusted life year, Incremental cost-effectiveness ratio

Introduction

Cancer has become an increasingly critical and pressing public health issue, commanding substantial attention due to its substantial consequences on individual health, national economies, and societal advancement.In 2021, cervical cancer continues to pose a significant public health challenge globally, with an estimated 670,000 new cases reported worldwide [1]. Over the past three decades, there has been a worrisome increase in the total number of cervical cancer cases. Notably, disparities persist between regions, with low Socio-Demographic Index (SDI) areas shouldering the greatest burden of the disease [2]. In the year 2022, China experienced a concerning surge in the incidence and mortality rates of cancer. The estimated number of new cancer cases reached a staggering 4,824,700, while the number of deaths attributed to this disease amounted to approximately 2,574,200 [3]. Despite current efforts to promote the human papillomavirus (HPV) vaccine and implement preventive measures, cervical cancer continues to pose a significant burden globally. It is estimated that approximately 604,000 new cases of cervical cancer and 342,000 deaths occur each year [4]. Patients diagnosed with metastatic cervical cancer requires systemic therapy, as their cancer has spread beyond the cervix and cannot be effectively treated with local interventions such as surgery or radiation therapy. This advanced stage of cervical cancer carries a grim prognosis, with limited treatment options available and a lower chance of long-term survival [5]. Platinum-based chemotherapy is currently considered the standard of care for patients with metastatic cervical cancer, but the addition of novel targeted therapies has shown potential to improve outcomes [6, 7]. One such targeted therapy is bevacizumab, a drug that specifically targets the vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) pathway. Clinical studies have shown that the addition of bevacizumab to chemotherapy significantly improves the clinical survival rate in patients with metastatic cervical cancer [8]. Another potential target for metastatic cervical cancer is the programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1)/programmed death-ligand 1 (PDL-1) pathway [9]. Several clinical trials have demonstrated the significant efficacy and safety of PD-1 or PDL-1 inhibitors when used in combination with standard chemotherapy for the treatment of metastatic cervical cancer.For instance, the KEYNOTE-826 trial investigated the combination of pembrolizumab with chemotherapy in patients with metastatic cervical cancer. The trial demonstrated a significant improvement in objective response rates compared to chemotherapy alone. Furthermore, the combination therapy led to improved progression-free survival and overall survival rates, suggesting a potential breakthrough in the management of metastatic cervical cancer [10]. Another notable example is the BEATcc trial, which performed a comprehensive evaluation of atezolizumab in combination with chemotherapy for patients with metastatic cervical cancer. The trial exhibited highly promising results, demonstrating a substantial enhancement in objective response rates and prolonged duration of response compared to patients solely treated with chemotherapy [11]. In addition, there have been promising results observed with bispecific antibodies, such as PD-1/CTLA-4 and PD-L1/TGF-β (YM-101, M7824), in the treatment of advanced cervical cancer [12]. These bispecific antibodies have demonstrated a degree of effectiveness and safety in clinical studies.These bispecific antibodies function by simultaneously targeting multiple immune checkpoint pathways, leading to enhanced antitumor immunity. By binding to both PD-1 and CTLA-4 or PD-L1 and TGF-β, these antibodies disrupt the inhibitory signals that suppress the immune system, thereby promoting an immune response against cancer cells.The development of bispecific antibodies, specifically targeting immune checkpoints, provides a novel therapeutic approach for advanced cervical cancer.Recently, the COMPASSION-16 phase 3 clinical trial investigated the efficacy of cadonilimab [13], a novel PD-1 and CTLA-4 bispecific antibody, in patients with metastatic cervical cancer. The results of this trial demonstrated a significant improvement in both progression-free survival and overall survival(OS) in participants who received cadonilimab.The median progression-free survival(PFS) in the cadonilimab group was 12.7 months, compared to 8.1 months in the placebo group. This indicates that patients receiving cadonilimab had a longer duration of time without their cancer progressing. Furthermore, the median overall survival was not reached in the cadonilimab group, with the range estimated to be at least 27.0 months. In contrast, the placebo group had a median overall survival of 22.8 months [14].

The COMPASSION-16 phase 3 clinical trial has shown promising outcomes for the treatment of metastatic cervical cancer with the use of cadonilimab. Despite these positive findings, it is essential to evaluate the cost effectiveness of integrating cadonilimab into the current treatment regimen. The cost effectiveness analysis provides crucial insights that can aid in informed decision-making by healthcare providers and policymakers.Therefore, the primary aim of this study was to assess the cost effectiveness of incorporating cadonilimab into the standard chemotherapy regimen compared to chemotherapy alone as the treatment for metastatic cervical cancer.This analysis was conducted from the perspective of the Chinese medical healthcare system.

Method

The cost effectiveness evaluation model

Based on the COMPASSION-16 clinical data, we constructed a partitioned survival model to evaluate the cost-effectiveness of different treatment strategies for metastatic cervical cancer. To capture the long-term outcomes, we simulated the model for a 10-year time horizon, as it has been reported that 99% of patients in the chemotherapy arm reached death within this timeframe.

The model study population is consistent with the enrolled population of clinical trial patients. The initial state is the diagnosis state of untreated persistent, recurrent, or metastatic cervical cancer. Patients enter the model from the initial treatment stage, and after receiving treatment, they will transition to the progression-free state with a certain probability. In the progression-free disease state, they may further progress or maintain or die, that is, they may transition to the " progression-free disease”,“progression disease” or “death” state after treatment. The structure of the model is illustrated in Fig. 1.The statistical analysis and modeling were performed using TreeAge Pro 2022.

Clinical and input date

Our model assumes a study population consistent with the COMPASSION-16 study population.A total of 445 patients were included and randomly assigned to two treatment groups, with 222 patients assigned to the cadonilimab group and 223 patients assigned to the placebo group. The median age of the participants was 56 years, with an interquartile range of 50 to 62. Among the participants, 287 (64%) had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance score of 1, indicating relatively good functional status. The majority of patients had squamous cell carcinoma (83%), and approximately half of the participants had received previous concurrent chemoradiotherapy (48%). The level of programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1), a protein associated with immune responses, was also assessed in the patients. Of the participants, 116 (26%) had a PD-L1 combined positive score (CPS) lower than 1, indicating a relatively low expression of PD-L1. On the other hand, 312 (70%) had a PD-L1 CPS of 1 or higher, indicating a higher expression of PD-L1. Additionally, 180 (40%) of the participants had a PD-L1 CPS of 10 or higher, indicating a particularly high expression of PD-L1.

According to the dosing regimen in the COMPASSION-16 clinical trial, The patients were randomly assigned to receive cadonilimab at a dose of 10 mg/kg or placebo in combination with platinum-based chemotherapy, with or without bevacizumab, administered every 3 weeks. The chemotherapy consisted of either cisplatin at a dosage of 50 mg/m² or carboplatin at a dosage calculated based on an area under the curve (AUC) of 5, along with paclitaxel at a dosage of 175 mg/m². The use of bevacizumab was optional and administered at a dosage of 15 mg/kg.Conversely, the chemotherapy group was administered a placebo in combination with the same chemotherapy regimen, including cisplatin or carboplatin plus paclitaxel, with the optional addition of bevacizumab.Both the cadonilimab and placebo treatments were administered every three weeks on the first day of each treatment cycle for a total of six cycles. Following the completion of these initial cycles, patients in both groups received either cadonilimab or placebo, with or without bevacizumab, as maintenance therapy every three weeks.Our model considered the costs associated with managing adverse drug reactions, specifically focusing on grade 3–5 serious adverse events that have an incidence rate greater than 5%.

The treatment duration assumptions of our model are consistent with those of clinical trials, which was 10.4 months (interquartile range [IQR] 4.2–22.5) for patients in the cadonilimab group, while it was 8.5 months (IQR 4.6–15.0) for those in the placebo group.

We have established the WTP threshold for this study as $40,343.68 per QALY. This threshold has been determined based on the criterion of three times the Gross Domestic Product (GDP) per capita in 2024 [15, 16]. All costs related to this study have been converted from Renminbi (RMB) to United States dollars (USD) using the average official exchange rate of RMB 7.12 to USD 1 in 2024 [16].

Model transfer probability and input date

The model transition probabilities used in the model were derived from data reconstruction and simulation of the COMPASSION-16 clinical trial. Survival curves from the trial were extracted using GetData Graph Digitizer software, and an R software(version 4.4.2) was utilized to reconstruct a model of the data and generate simulation curves [17]. To identify the appropriate distribution for the reconstructed model, several distributions including weibull, lognormal, loglogistic, gompertz, exponential, and gamma distributions were considered. The goodness of fit for each distribution was evaluated by determining the minimum values of the Akaike information criterion (AIC) and Bayesian information criterion (BIC) obtained through the model simulation.A lower value of AIC and BIC indicates a better fit of the distribution to the data [18]. Visual inspection of the simulated survival curves was also performed to further evaluate the appropriateness of each distribution(Table 1; Fig. 2).

The result revealed that log-logistic distributions exhibited the most suitable fit to survival curves observed in clinical trials. To quantify the transition probability between different states in this study, we employed a methodology that involves the estimation of survival data and the consideration of time dependence. During the course of the follow-up period in the COMPASSION-16 clinical trial, the survival and transition probabilities of the model were collected directly from the reconstructed survival curve and computed within the TreeAge Pro 2022 software. Subsequently, these points were transformed into the respective transition probabilities.Once the model progressed through the follow-up period, we employed the log-logistic distribution that best matched the data to estimate survival rates and transition probabilities. The calculation of the survival function relies on the time transition probability, which adheres to the expression S(t) = 1/(1 + λtγ) [19]. The parameters of the γ and λ are presented in Table 2.

Input costs and utility

In conducting pharmacoeconomic evaluations, it is crucial to comprehensively consider the various components of costs involved. These costs typically encompass direct costs, indirect costs, and hidden costs. However, for the purposes of this study within the context of China’s health system, the focus will be solely on direct costs. Direct costs primarily include the costs related to the use of cadonilimab and chemotherapy drugs, follow-up care, best supportive care, and the management of adverse reactions.The drug costs utilized in this study were sourced from Yaozhi.com (https://data.yaozh.com/). These costs were calculated based on the median bid prices of drugs across various provinces in China.For the costs associated with follow-up care, best supportive care, and management of adverse reactions are based on data from relevant references. Sensitivity analysis will be conducted to assess the robustness of our cost estimates and to explore the potential impact of uncertainty in key input parameters.Furthermore, in order to calculate the appropriate chemotherapy dosages, we made assumptions based on the average characteristics of a patient, specifically an average weight of 59.0 kg, height of 160 cm, and a creatinine clearance rate of 70 µmol/L [20].

Health-related quality of life is an important measure used to evaluate the impact of an individual’s health on their daily functioning. It is commonly quantified using utility values, which range from 0 (representing the worst health state) to 1 (representing the best health state). In the COMPASSION-16 clinical trial, however, there were no reports of health-related utility values. Therefore, in this study, we have relied on utility values obtained from the existing literature. In the present study, utility scores for PFS and disease progression (PD) were determined as 0.85 and 0.52, respectively, by analyzing data from the EuroQol five-dimensional questionnaire (EQ-5D) within the KEYNOTE-158 trial.

Sensitivity analyses

In this study, we conducted a comprehensive analysis to assess the stability of our model. This analysis involved two types of sensitivity analysis: one-way sensitivity analysis and probabilistic sensitivity analysis.During the one-way sensitivity analysis, we varied the input parameters, within a range of ± 25% from their base values, to examine their impact on the study outcomes. This approach allowed us to assess the potential influence of these parameters on the ICER. By performing this analysis, we gained insights into the robustness of the results and the sensitivity of the model to the input parameters.The outcomes of the one-way sensitivity analysis were presented using tornado plots.

Additionally, we performed a probabilistic sensitivity analysis to capture the uncertainty associated with the input parameters. This approach involved randomly sampling values for these parameters from their respective probability distributions. By conducting numerous iterations of this process, we were able to generate a distribution of ICER values, offering a comprehensive understanding of the range and probability of different outcomes.The results of the probabilistic sensitivity analysis are presented in a scatter plot.

Result

The base case results

The total cost for the cadonilimab group in this study was determined to be $86,839.48, while the cost for the chemotherapy group was $36,770.38. The cadonilimab group achieved an incremental gain of 0.70 QALYs, resulting in an additional cost of $50,069.10. Consequently, the ICER for the cadonilimab group was calculated to be $71,527.28 per QALY.

These findings suggest that the use of the cadonilimab regimen may not be deemed cost-effective within the Chinese healthcare system, as the ICER surpasses the WTP threshold of $40,343.68. Consequently, the additional benefits provided by cadonilimab in terms of QALYs gained might not justify the substantial increase in costs compared to chemotherapy alone.Table 3 provides a summary of these results.

Sensitivity analyses result

The results of the one-way sensitivity analysis, as shown in Fig. 3, revealed several crucial factors that affect the ICER in this study. Among these factors, the cost of the drug cadonilimab, the utility value of progressive disease, and the utility value of progression-free disease were found to have the most significant impacts.It is important to highlight that the cost of drug cadonilimab plays a crucial role in determining the cost-effectiveness of the intervention. The sensitivity analysis showed that even when adjusting the cost of the drug, the ICER remained above the WTP threshold. This suggests that the cost of cadonilimab would need to be substantially lower in order for the intervention to be considered cost-effective.Additionally, the utility values assigned to both progressive disease and progression-free disease also impact the ICER. Modifying these utility values within a range of ± 25% did not result in significant changes to the ICER outcomes. This suggests that the impact of these utility values on the cost-effectiveness of the intervention is relatively stable.

Discussion

Cervical cancer is a significant global health issue, particularly in low- and middle-income countries, where it ranks as the fourth most common cancer among women [28]. In these countries, the burden of cervical cancer is disproportionately high, with higher rates of morbidity and mortality compared to high-income countries. This disparity can be attributed to factors such as limited access to HPV vaccination, low prevalence of screening, and inadequate medical resources.In recent years, there has been a notable increase in the risk factors for cervical cancer, including early marriage, engaging in sexual activity with multiple partners, and a lack of barrier contraception use. These behavioral changes have contributed to the rising incidence of cervical cancer among young women. It is important to note that approximately 99% of cervical cancer cases are caused by persistent infection with high-risk types of HPV, with HPV16 and HPV18 being the most prevalent types [29]. Low rates of screening among women in areas with limited education and scarce medical resources further exacerbate the problem. This leads to a high proportion of cervical cancer cases being diagnosed at advanced stages, resulting in poorer outcomes for affected individuals.Despite the promotion and popularization of the HPV vaccine, cervical cancer remains a significant threat to women’s health in China. The ICO/IARC China HPV and Related Diseases Report in 2023 reveals that cervical cancer ranked third among female tumors in terms of incidence and mortality rates among women aged 15 to 44 [30]. While early-stage cervical cancer can often be successfully treated with surgical intervention or chemoradiotherapy, patients with metastatic disease face a much grimmer prognosis [31]. These advanced cases pose substantial challenges for both patients and healthcare providers.Patients with metastatic cervical cancer often exhibit more aggressive disease characteristics and are at a higher risk of treatment failure and disease progression. Moreover, traditional therapies, such as surgery, radiation, or chemotherapy, have limited efficacy in controlling the progression of advanced cervical cancer [32]. In recent years, there has been a significant advancement in the field of cancer treatment with the emergence of immunotherapy.Studies have shown that pembrolizumab and nivolumab can effectively activate the immune system to target and destroy cancer cells in metastatic cervical cancer [33]. The recent COMPASSION-16 study has demonstrated significant and clinically meaningful improvements in progression-free survival and overall survival in first-line metastatic cervical cancer by adding cadonilimab to chemotherapy, with or without bevacizumab. However, it is essential to evaluate its pharmacoeconomic impact to determine if the benefits provided by the drug outweigh its costs.Assessing the pharmacoeconomic impact of cadonilimab from a pharmaceutical perspective is of utmost importance in order to understand its value in the overall healthcare system.

This cost-effectiveness analysis aims to assess the economic implications of adopting cadonilimab as a first-line treatment option for metastatic cervical cancer. The analysis considers both the costs and benefits associated with this therapeutic approach.In this study, the group treated with cadonilimab demonstrated a statistically significant improvement in QALYs of 0.70 compared to the group receiving chemotherapy. However, this intervention incurred an additional cost of $50,069.10. The ICER was determined to be $71,527.28 per QALY gained. The ICER represents the additional cost required to gain one additional QALY. In this case, the ICER for cadonilimab exceeded the WTP threshold of $40,343.68 .Therefore, the cadonilimab regimen may not be considered cost-effective within the Chinese healthcare system.

The sensitivity analyses were conducted to assess the robustness of the results.

Although no parameters led to substantial changes in the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER) outcomes, our analysis unveiled several critical factors that impact the ICER in this study.The influence of the cost of cadonilimab on the ICER highlights the importance of considering the pricing of the drug in the overall cost-effectiveness analysis. A higher drug cost directly affects the ICER, making the treatment less economically viable. Similarly, the utility values assigned to progressive disease and progression-free disease also play a significant role in shaping the ICER. Utility values reflect the preference or quality of life associated with different health states, and assigning lower values to progressive disease or lower values to progression-free disease may result in higher ICERs.

It is crucial to highlight our strong objection to utilizing the findings of the pharmacoeconomic analysis, specifically the cost-effectiveness evaluation from this study, to impose restrictions on the utilization of cadonilimab. Instead, we firmly advocate for the utilization of these results to inform policy-making decisions, guide health insurance policies, and provide up-to-date cost-effectiveness information to pharmaceutical companies.We discovered that a substantial reduction in the price of cadonilimab to $3.50 per mg, equivalent to 50% of the current price, would result in an ICER of $38,351.72 per QAL). This ICER approaches the WTP threshold of $40,343.68 per QALY. These findings suggest that decreasing the price of cadonilimab could make treatment options more economically viable.Furthermore, performing a probability sensitivity analysis for cadonilimab at a 50% price reduction revealed a 57.10% advantage in cost-benefit analysis when considering it as an option at the WTP threshold of $40,343.68 per QALY. These results indicate that cadonilimab becomes a more cost-effective choice as its price decreases. Lowering the price of cadonilimab may enhance its economic viability and improve its standing in cost-effectiveness assessments in China.

Currently in China, the implementation of a centralized drug procurement system has proven to be an effective strategy in addressing the financial burden stemming from the high prices of drugs, particularly high-value drugs [34]. This approach allows the Chinese government to directly negotiate with pharmaceutical companies, leveraging the country’s immense medical demands as leverage for ensuring the survival of enterprises, while also serving as a crucial means for pharmaceutical companies to reduce drug prices [35]. Over the span of several years of implementation, numerous high-value oncology drugs, including gefitinib, have experienced price reductions of up to 50% [36]. Furthermore, Chinese pharmaceutical enterprises also produce drugs with comparable pharmacological effects, though at significantly lower prices, through consistency evaluation, thereby meeting the clinical requirements. Through these methods, the Chinese government has successfully lowered drug prices and improved drug accessibility for all stakeholders involved.

Currently, there is some published studies examining the cost-effectiveness of cadonilimab specifically for the treatment of metastatic cervical cancer. One such study by Wang et al. evaluated the cost-effectiveness of cadonilimab plus bevacizumab and chemotherapy for persistent, recurrent, or metastatic cervical cancer.The analysis found that cadonilimab plus bevacizumab and chemotherapy was not a cost-effective option compared to chemotherapy alone. The ICER was $88,533.51/QALY [37]. This suggests that cadonilimab plus bevacizumab and chemotherapy may not provide sufficient value for the additional cost incurred.Similarly, Ding et al. assessed the cost-effectiveness of cadonilimab plus chemotherapy as first-line treatment for persistent, recurrent, or metastatic cervical cancer.The analysis showed that cadonilimab combination therapy was not cost-effective, with an ICER of $75,944.56/QALY per QALY gained [38]. However, we have to note that there are new updates and reference functions available for our research. This is because the previously mentioned articles were published early and used exchange rates and WTP values from 2023. Our study, on the other hand, employs the most recent data from 2024 for the prices of cadonilimab, among other factors, thereby enhancing the accuracy and relevance of the cost-effectiveness analysis. Secondly, we introduced a different hypothesis regarding subsequent treatment therapy compared to the existing literature. This allowed us to explore the cost-effectiveness analysis of cadonilimab from various perspectives.Thirdly, our methodology involved a distinct calculation of transition probabilities, enabling a comprehensive examination of the cost-effectiveness analysis of cadonilimab from diverse angles. Finally, we discussed the cost-effectivenessadvantage of cadonilimab in the treatment of metastatic cervical cancer after price fluctuation, and provided further guidance for medical decision and price.

This study has the following limitations.Firstly, this study is based on a long-term economic model utilizing data from the COMPASSION-16 clinical trial. However, the applicability of the model to short-term data from COMPASSION-16 is challenging to verify, leading to extrapolation results that may either overestimate or underestimate long-term survival benefits.Secondly, Economic models assume sustained efficacy over the long term, such as the persistence of immune memory effects of catunilumab. If issues such as drug resistance or delayed toxicity exist in real-world practice, it could introduce bias in the calculation of the ICER.Thirdly, there may be differences between the trial population and real-world patients. COMPASSION-16 employed stringent inclusion criteria, such as having an ECOG score of 0–1 and lacking serious comorbidities. Real-world patient characteristics may vary significantly, leading to uncertainties in extrapolation. This could result in an overestimation of the duration of effective treatment in the model, thereby potentially overestimating the QALY benefit.Fourthly, our study only considered treatment-related serious adverse reactions of grade 3 and above, neglecting other adverse events. This limitation might introduce bias in our results compared to real-world clinical practice. The exclusion of certain adverse events could influence the overall cost-effectiveness assessment. However, our sensitivity analysis shows that fluctuations in costs associated with grade 3 or higher adverse events do not significantly affect the incremental cost-effectiveness ratios. The consistent finding of ICERs above the established WTP threshold further supports the reliability and robustness of our conclusions.Despite these limitations, our study provides valuable insights into the cost-effectiveness of the treatment under evaluation. Future research should aim to address these limitations and further refine our understanding of the economic implications of the treatment.

Conclusion

According to the results of this cost-effectiveness analysis, the addition of cadonilimab to chemotherapy as a first-line treatment for metastatic cervical cancer is not considered cost-effective when compared to chemotherapy alone. Therefore, decision-makers should take into account the evidence of cost-effectiveness in order to make informed decisions regarding the allocation of healthcare resources for metastatic cervical cancer.

Pion generalized parton distributions at zero skewness
Fernando Chandra, Parada T. P. Hutauruk, Terry Mart

Abstract
In this paper, we systematically study the generalized parton distributions (GPDs) of the pion Goldstone Boson at zero skewness ( ξ = 0 \xi=0 ) in the framework of the covariant Nambu–Jona-Lasinio model with the help of the proper time regularization scheme to cure the divergence simultaneously to simulate the confinement. To this end, we evaluate the generalized form factors and parton distribution functions derived, respectively, from the first Mellin moments and the forward limit pion GPDs, in comparison to existing experimental data, recent lattice QCD simulations, and JAM global QCD analyses. We find that the pion parton distribution functions derived from the pion GPDs have excellent agreement with the experimental data and JAM analysis at renormalization scale μ 2 = \mu^{2}= 4 and 27 GeV 2 . In addition, our results for the pion generalized form factors involving the scalar, vector, and tensor dressed form factors are consistent with recent lattice data and existing data. We then compute the charge radii for those dressed generalized form factors, and we obtain r S π = r_{S}^{\pi}= 0.56 fm, r V π = r_{V}^{\pi}= 0.63 fm, and r T π = r_{T}^{\pi}= 0.83 fm for the pion scalar, vector, and tensor form factors, respectively.



keywords
Nambu–Jona-Lasinio model; generalized parton distributions; proper-time regularization scheme, pseudo-Goldstone boson; generalized form factors.

Introduction

The internal structure of hadrons, as a playground of quantum chromodynamics (QCD), provides crucial information about the properties of QCD such as spontaneous chiral symmetry breaking

This makes the pion still interesting and challenging for further study. However, the pion’s internal structure still cannot be directly computed from the first principles of QCD, even though we believe QCD is the underlying theory of strong interaction. Therefore, to overcome this obstacle, we need to construct a theoretical model that mimics the properties of QCD, known as the QCD-inspired model. In the literature, several QCD-inspired models have been performed to investigate the pion internal structure, as well as to understand properties of QCD

These observable quantities provide a deeper insight into the internal structure of the hadrons, specifically their spatial, transverse, and longitudinal momentum fractions of the valence quark inside the pion. However, in the present study, we concentrate on the pion GPDs at zero skewness, which provides information about the quark longitudinal momentum fraction, skewness parameter, and transverse momentum in terms of the pion parent momentum.

Among the observable quantities mentioned above, GPDs are one of the powerful and essential tools to understand the internal structure of the pion in three-dimensional (3D) tomography. GPDs unify the information contained in PDFs and EMFFs. The pion GPDs provide information about the one-dimensional PDF and EMFF, in addition to the pion 3D tomography. Unfortunately, experimental data for the pion GPDs are not yet available in the literature. However, limited data exist for the pion PDFs, and some experimental measurements of the pion EMFFs have been reported. Hence, we will extract the pion PDFs and EMFFs from the pion GPDs and compare them with the existing experimental data

\displaystyle i\int\frac{d^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\mathrm{Tr}\big[\gamma_{5}\tau_{a}S_{q}(p+k)\gamma_{5}\tau_{b}S_{q}(k)\big].

(5)

\displaystyle 1+2G_{\pi}\Pi_{\pi}(k^{2}=m_{\pi}^{2})

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle g_{\pi qq}^{-2}

3
Generalized parton distributions

In this section, we present the calculation of the vector, tensor, and scalar pion GPDs in the covariant NJL model, starting from the definition and generic expression of the GPDs. A generic formulation of the GPDs in the nonlocal matrix element, non-diagonal momentum space (
\xi\neq 0
), and the quark field separated by a light-like distance can be defined as follows:

\mathcal{H}^{q}(x,\xi,t)=\int\frac{dz^{-}}{4\pi}e^{\big[ixP^{+}z^{-}\big]}\big<\pi^{+}(p^{\prime})\mid\bar{\psi}_{q}(-\frac{z^{-}}{2})\gamma^{+}\psi_{q}(\frac{z^{-}}{2})\mid\pi^{+}(p)\big>_{z^{+}=0,\mathbf{z}=0},

(10a)

\mathcal{P}E^{q}(x,\xi,t)=\int\frac{dz^{-}}{4\pi}e^{\big[ixP^{+}z^{-}\big]}\big<\pi^{+}(p^{\prime})\mid\bar{\psi}_{q}(-\frac{z^{-}}{2})i\sigma^{+j}\psi_{q}(\frac{z^{-}}{2})\mid\pi^{+}(p)\big>_{z^{+}=0,\mathbf{z}=0},

(10b)

\frac{M_{u}}{P^{+}}\mathcal{H}^{q}_{S}(x,\xi,t)=\int\frac{dz^{-}}{4\pi}e^{\big[ixP^{+}z^{-}\big]}\big<\pi^{+}(p^{\prime})\mid\bar{\psi}_{q}(-\frac{z^{-}}{2})1\psi_{q}(\frac{z^{-}}{2})\mid\pi^{+}(p)\big>_{z^{+}=0,\mathbf{z}=0},

(10c)

where
\mathcal{P}={(P^{+}q^{j}-P^{j}q^{+})}/{(m_{\pi}P^{+})}
,
\psi_{q}
is the quark field with flavor
q=(u,d)
,
x
is the Bjorken
x
or the quark longitudinal momentum,
t=Q^{2}=-q^{2}=\Delta^{2}=(p^{\prime}-p)^{2}
is the momentum transfer or photon virtual momentum, and
\xi={(p^{+}-p^{\prime+})}/{(p^{+}+p^{\prime+})}=-{\Delta^{+}}/{2P^{+}}
stands for the skewness parameter with the
p
and
p^{\prime}
are the initial and final pion momentum, respectively. Here, we define the average momentum of the pion
P={(p+p^{\prime})}/{2}
, where
P
shows the average momentum carried by the initial and final pion momenta.

The pion has leading twist vector (no spin flip)
\mathcal{H}^{q}(x,\xi,t)
and tensor (spin flip)
E^{q}(x,\xi,t)
quark GPDs. The vector and tensor quark GPDs can be defined in the isoscalar and isovector isospin projections with the isospin matrix equal unity for the isoscalar and
\tau_{3}
for the isovector. The relation between the quark GPDs and the isoscalar and isovector GPDs can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{q}(x,\xi,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big[\mathcal{H}^{I=0}(x,\xi,t)+\mathcal{H}^{I=1}(x,\xi,t)\big],

(11)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\bar{q}}(x,\xi,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big[\mathcal{H}^{I=0}(x,\xi,t)-\mathcal{H}^{I=1}(x,\xi.t)\big],

(12)

or it can also be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{I=0}(x,\xi,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{q}(x,\xi,t)+\mathcal{H}^{\bar{q}}(x,\xi,t),

(13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{I=1}(x,\xi,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{q}(x,\xi,t)-\mathcal{H}^{\bar{q}}(x,\xi,t),

(14)

where
\mathcal{H}^{q}(x,\xi,t)
supports the regime
x\in\big[0,1\big]
, while
\mathcal{H}^{\bar{q}}(x,\xi,t)=-\mathcal{H}^{q}(-x,\xi,t)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{q}(x,0,0)=\frac{1}{2}\big[\mathcal{H}^{I=0}(x,0,0)+\mathcal{H}^{I=1}(x,0,0)\big]=u_{v}^{\pi}(x),

Based on the polynomiality condition, a general relation between the pion GPDs and the generalized form factors can be introduced by

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}dxx^{n}\mathcal{H}^{q}(x,\xi,t)

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}dxE^{q}(x,\xi=0,t)

\displaystyle B_{1,0}^{q}(t=-Q^{2})=F^{q}_{T}(Q^{2}),

(19)

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{-1}dx\mathcal{H}^{q}_{S}(x,\xi=0,t)

5
Summary and conclusion

To summarize, we have systematically investigated the parton distribution functions (PDFs) and generalized form factors (GFFs) of the pion, derived from the pion generalized parton distributions (GPDs) at zero skewness, within the covariant Nambu–Jona-Lasinio (NJL) model. The Schwinger proper-time regularization scheme was employed to control ultraviolet divergences and, at the same time, to simulate quark confinement. We computed the vector, tensor, and scalar pion GPDs and subsequently examined the pion PDFs obtained from the forward limit of the GPDs, as well as the vector and tensor pion form factors extracted from the first Mellin moments of the pion GPDs and the scalar pion form factor obtained from the twist-3 pion GPDs.

We found that the shapes of the scalar, vector, and tensor pion GPDs slightly change with increasing the values of
t
, as shown in Figs.
1
-
3
. Such a change will be expected to affect the properties of the pion PDFs and GFFs in the forward limit. Regarding the pion PDFs, we find that, after evolving from the initial scale
\mu_{0}^{2}=0.18~\text{GeV}^{2}
to
\mu^{2}=27~\text{GeV}^{2}
, our results exhibit excellent agreement with the available experimental data
[
14
]
and the JAM global analysis
[
15
]
. For completeness, we also evolved the pion PDFs to
\mu^{2}=4~\text{GeV}^{2}
to enable a direct comparison with lattice QCD results and the JAM global analysis. At this scale, our pion PDFs remain consistent with the JAM determination.

From the Mellin moment of the vector and tensor pion GPDs, we obtained the vector and tensor form factors, while the scalar pion form factor was derived from the twist-3 scalar pion GPDs. We found that the vector pion form factor is consistent with the experimental and lattice QCD data, followed by the tensor pion form factor, which is consistent with the lattice QCD data. Surprisingly, we found that the scalar pion form factor underestimates the lattice QCD data, as shown in the bottom center panel of Fig.
5
.

Finally, we computed the scalar, vector, and tensor pion charge radii. We obtained the
r_{V}^{\pi}=
0.63 fm,
r_{T}^{\pi}=
0.83 fm, and
r_{S}^{\pi}=
0.56 fm. Using these values, we find that the charge radii follow the ordering:
r_{T}^{\pi}\geq r_{V}^{\pi}\geq r_{S}^{\pi}
, which is consistent with the results reported by lattice QCD in Ref.
[
22
]
, and theoretical model calculations in Refs.
[
43
,
44
]
. It is worth mentioning that the results of the present study are very challenging to verify with the experimental data from future experiments, such as the Electron-Ion Collider (EIC) at BNL
[
16
]
, the Electron-ion collider in China (EicC)
[
17
]
, J-PARC
[
18
]
, the upgraded JLAB 22 GeV
[
19
]
, and the COMPASS/AMBER++ at CERN
[
20
]
.

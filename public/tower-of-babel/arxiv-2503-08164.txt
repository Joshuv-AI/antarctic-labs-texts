Simple Jordan superalgebras with the even parts of Clifford type
Ivan Shestakov, Efim Zelmanov

Abstract
The purpose of this paper is a partial progress towards classification of simple infinite dimensional Jordan superalgebras. First, we prove that the only simple infinite dimensional Jordan superalgebras with finite dimensional even parts are the superalgebras of superforms. Then we consider the superalgebras whose even parts are infinite dimensional algebras of “Clifford type”, that is, direct sums of algebras of bilinear forms. The results of [ 18 ] show that the number of summonds in these sums is 1 or 2. We prove that the second case is impossible and that the simple infinite dimensional Jordan superalgebras of the first type are the superalgebras of superforms.

The purpose of this paper is a partial progress towards classification of simple infinite dimensional Jordan superalgebras.
The first theorem shows that we may restrict ourself to superalgebras with infinite dimensional even part.
We consider the superalgebras whose even parts are infinite dimensional algebras of “Clifford type”, that is, direct sums of algebras of bilinear form. The results of

Let

Proof of Theorem 1

Let
S=S_{\bar{0}}+S_{\bar{1}}
be a simple (not, necessary Jordan) superalgebra over a field
F
. We start with discussion of the centroid
Cent\,S
. This part may be folklorically known. We do it for the benefit of a reader.

Let
M(S)
be the
multiplication algebra
of
S
, that is, the algebra of linear transformations of the vector space
M
generated by all left and right multiplications by elements from
S_{\bar{0}}\cup S_{\bar{1}}
. The algebra
M(S)
is also
\mathbf{Z}_{2}
-graded:

M(S)=M(S)_{\bar{0}}+M(S)_{\bar{1}}.

The centroid
Cent\,(S)
is the subalgebra of all even linear transformations of
S
that commute with all elements form
M(S)
.
Standard arguments show that the centroid of a simple superalgebra is a field extension of
F
. We call the superalgebra
S

central
if
Cent\,(S)=F
.

For
i=0
or
1
let
Cent\,(S_{\bar{i}})
be the algebra of linear transformations of the space
S_{\bar{i}}
that commute with all elements from the restriction of
M(S)_{\bar{0}}
to
S_{\bar{i}}
. For an arbitrary transformation
\varphi
from
Cent\,(S)
the restriction of
\varphi
to
S_{\bar{i}}
lies in
Cent\,(S_{\bar{i}})
. We denote this restriction as
\pi_{i}(\varphi)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

\pi_{i}:Cent\,(S)\rightarrow Cent(S_{\bar{i}}),\ i=0,1
is an isomorphism.

Proof.
We need to show that the mapping
\pi_{i}
is surjective. Let
\psi\in Cent\,(S_{\bar{i}})
and let
a\in S_{\overline{1-i}}
. Since the superalgebra
S
is simple, there exist elements
a_{k}\in S_{\bar{i}}
and operators
W_{k}\in M(S)_{\bar{1}}
such that

a=\sum_{k}a_{k}W_{k}.

We will define the extension
\tilde{\psi}
of
\psi
to
S_{\overline{1-i}}
via

\tilde{\psi}(a)=\sum_{k}\psi(a_{k})W_{k}

It remains to prove that
\sum_{k}a_{k}W_{k}=0
implies
\sum_{k}\psi(a_{k})W_{k}=0
. If
\sum_{k}\psi(a_{k})W_{k}\neq 0
then

(\sum_{k}\psi(a_{k})W_{k})M(S)_{\bar{1}}=S_{\bar{0}},

because of simplicity of the superalgebra
S
.

In particular, there exists an operator
U\in M(S)_{\bar{1}}
such that

(\sum_{k}\psi(a_{k})W_{k})U\neq 0.

Since
W_{k}U\in M(S)_{\bar{0}}
and
\psi\in Cent\,(S_{\bar{i}})
, it follows that

\sum_{k}\psi(a_{k})(W_{k}U)=\psi(\sum_{k}a_{k}W_{k}U)=0,

a contradiction.

The construction above implies that for an arbitrary element
b\in S_{\bar{i}}
and an arbitrary operator
W\in M(S)_{\bar{1}}
we have

\tilde{\psi}(bW)=\psi(b)W.

Now let
b\in S_{\overline{1-i}},\,W\in M(S)_{\bar{0}}
. We need to show that
\tilde{\psi}(bW)=\tilde{\psi}(b)W
.
Let
b=\sum_{k}a_{k}W_{k},\,a_{k}\in S_{\bar{i}},\,W_{k}\in M(S)_{\bar{1}}
. Then

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}(bW)=\tilde{\psi}(\sum_{k}a_{k}W_{k}W)=\sum_{k}\psi(a_{k})W_{k}W=(\sum_{k}\tilde{\psi}(a_{k}W_{k}))W=\tilde{\psi}(b)W.

Finally, let
b\in S_{\overline{1-i}},\,W\in M(S)_{\bar{1}},\,b=\sum_{k}a_{k}W_{k},\,a_{k}\in S_{\bar{i}},\,W_{k}\in M(S)_{\bar{1}}.
Then

bW=\sum_{k}a_{k}W_{k}W,\,W_{k}W\in M(S)_{\bar{0}}
. Hence

\tilde{\psi}(b)=\sum_{k}\psi(a_{k})W_{k},\,\ \psi(bW)=\sum_{k}\psi(a_{k})W_{k}W=\tilde{\psi}(b)W.

We proved that the mapping
\pi_{i}
is injective, hence it is an isomorphism. This completes the proof of the lemma.

[\tilde{M},\tilde{M}]=(0).

Choose arbitrary elements
x_{1}\in M_{1},\,y_{2}\in M_{2}
. The subspace

\tilde{V}=\{v\in V\,|\,x_{1}\cdot v\in\tilde{M},\,y_{2}\cdot v\in\tilde{M},\ (v_{0}\,|\,v)=0\}

has finite codimension in
V
. Since
\dim_{F}V=\infty
, the subspace above contains an element
v^{\prime}
such that
v^{\prime 2}=e
, it follows from nondegeneracy of the form. Observe that
M_{2}\cdot v^{\prime}\in\{f,M,e_{2}\}\cdot\{e_{1},J,e_{2}\}\subseteq\{f,M,e_{1}\}=M_{1}
. Similarly,
M_{1}\cdot v^{\prime}\subseteq M_{2}
.

It follows from (
1.4
) that

((y_{2}v^{\prime})\cdot v^{\prime})\cdot e_{1}+((e_{1}v^{\prime})\cdot v^{\prime})y_{2}=2(y_{2}v^{\prime})(v^{\prime}e_{1}),

which implies
(y_{2}\cdot v^{\prime})\cdot v^{\prime}=\tfrac{1}{4}y_{2}
.

Now, let
g,h
be orthogonal idempotents in a Jordan algebra
J
, then we have
JU(g,h)U(h)=(0)
and hence

JU(g,h)U(J(U(h)))\subseteq JU(g,h)U(h)U(J)U(h)=(0)

or
\{\{h,J,h\},\{g,J,h\},\{h,J,h\}\}=(0)
.
Taking
h=f+e_{1},\,g=e_{2}
in the superalgebra
J
, we have
M_{1}\subseteq\{h,J,h\},\ v^{\prime}\in\{g,J,h\}
, hence
\{M_{1},v^{\prime},M_{1}\}=(0)

and

0=\{x_{1},v^{\prime},y_{2}\cdot v^{\prime}\}=[x_{1}\cdot v^{\prime},y_{2}\cdot v^{\prime}]-[(y_{2}\cdot v^{\prime})\cdot v^{\prime},x_{1}]-[x_{1},y_{2}\cdot v^{\prime}]\cdot v^{\prime\prime}.

We have
[x_{1},y_{2}\cdot v^{\prime}]\in[M_{1},M_{1}]\subseteq Ff+Fe_{1},\ (Ff+Fe_{1})\cdot v^{\prime}\subseteq Fv^{\prime}
. Also,
[x_{1}\cdot v^{\prime},y_{2}\cdot v^{\prime}]\in[\tilde{M},\tilde{M}]=(0)
, which implies

[x_{1},y_{2}]\in Fv^{\prime}.

There is another element
v^{\prime\prime}\in\tilde{V}
, such that
(v^{\prime\prime})^{2}=e,\ v^{\prime\prime}
is not collinear with
v^{\prime}
. Arguing as above, we get
[x_{1},y_{2}]\in Fv^{\prime\prime}
. Hence
[x_{1},y_{2}]=0,\ [M_{1},M_{2}]=(0)
.

Now we have

[M,M]=[M_{1},M_{1}]+[M_{2},M_{2}]\subseteq Ff+Fe+Fv_{0}.

Choosing another element
v_{0}^{\prime}
with the same properties, we get

[M,M]\subseteq Ff+Fe+Fv_{0}^{\prime}.

Therefore,

[M,M]\subseteq Ff+Fe.

Let
x,y\in M,\ [x,y]=\alpha f+\beta e,\,\beta\neq 0
. The subspace

\{v\in V\,|\,[x\cdot v,y]=[x,y\cdot v]=0\}

has a finite codimension in
V
. Let
v
be a nonzero element from this subspace. Then by (
4.1
)

\{x,v,y\}=[x\cdot v,y]-[y\cdot v,x]-[x,y]v\in\{f,J,f\}=Ff.

This implies
[x,y]\cdot v=\beta v=0
, a contradiction. Hence
[M,M]\subseteq Ff
.

We showed that
\tilde{M}=\{x\in M\,|\,\langle x,M\rangle=(0)\}
is an
A
-subbimodule of
M
. Since
[\tilde{M},M]=\langle\tilde{M},M\rangle=(0)
, it follows that
\tilde{M}
is an ideal of
J
. Suppose that
\tilde{M}\neq M
and factor the ideal
\tilde{M}
out. Then without loss of generality we can assume that
M\neq(0)
and
\dim_{F}M\leq 4
.

The subspace
\{v\in V\,|\,M\cdot v=(0)\}
has finite codimension in

, hence it contains an element
v
such that
(v,v)=1
. For an arbitrary element

we have

4(x\cdot v)\cdot v=x=0.

We showed that
\tilde{M}=M,\ [M,M]=(0)
. This completes the proof of the lemma.

Proof of Theorem 3.

Suppose that
A=J(V_{1})\oplus J(V_{2});\ \dim_{F}V_{i}\geq 2;\,i=1,2.

The algebra
A
is infinite dimensional, hence at least one of the spaces
V_{1},V_{2}
is infinite dimensional.
Suppose that
\dim_{F}V_{2}=\infty
. Let
f,e
be the identity elements of the algebras
J(V_{1}),J(V_{2})
, respectively.
By Lemma
4.1
,
M=\{e,M,f\}
.
The identity
f
is a sum of two orthogonal idempotents,
f=f_{1}+f_{2}
. The subsuperalgebra
J_{1}=\{f_{1}+e,J,f_{1}+e\}
satisfies the assumptions of Lemma
4.5
,
(J_{1})_{\bar{0}}=Ff_{1}+J(V_{2}),\ (J_{1})_{\bar{1}}=M_{1}=\{f_{1},M,e\}
. By Lemma
4.5

[M_{1},M_{1}]=(0)
.
Consider also the subsuperalgebra
J_{2}=\{f_{2}+e,J,f_{2}+e\}=Ff_{2}+J(V_{2})+M_{2},\ M_{2}=\{f_{2},M,e\},\ M=M_{1}+M_{2}
. As before, we have
[M_{2},M_{2}]=(0)
, hence
[M,M]=[M_{1},M_{2}]=\{f_{1},J(V_{1}),f_{2}\}\subseteq J(V_{1})
.

The superalgebra
J(V_{1})+M
satisfies the assumptions of Lemma
4.3
, hence by this lemma
[M,M]=(0)
, which contradicts the simplicity of the superalgebra
J
. This completes the proof.

The lived experience of mask wearing among patients with chronic illnesses during the COVID-19 pandemic
Authors: Parvaneh Aghajari, Mina Hosseinzadeh, Zahra Sheikhalipour
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe COVID-19 pandemic necessitated widespread mask-wearing as a key preventive measure. While masks played a crucial role in limiting virus transmission, their implications for individuals living with chronic illnesses remain underexplored. This study aimed to explore the lived experiences of patients with chronic conditions regarding mask-wearing during and after the COVID-19 pandemic, with a focus on physical, psychological, and social aspects. A qualitative phenomenological approach was employed to explore the lived experiences of individuals with chronic illnesses regarding mask-wearing during the COVID-19 pandemic. In-depth semi-structured interviews were conducted with a purposive sample of 15 participants. Data were analyzed following Colaizzi’s method to identify key themes and ensure validation through participant feedback. The central theme identified was “a revolution in lifestyle” encompassing two main categories: “behavioral adaptations for survival” and “emerging challenges in everyday life.” Mask-wearing during and after the pandemic generated complex, multifaceted experiences for patients with chronic conditions. Healthcare providers and policymakers should recognize these challenges and develop tailored interventions such as personalized guidance, improved mask design, and psychosocial support to enhance compliance and improve quality of life for this vulnerable population.Keywords: COVID-19, Chronic disease, Face masks, Qualitative researchSubject terms: Health care, Psychology, Psychology

Introduction

The COVID-19 pandemic brought substantial lifestyle changes worldwide1,2, including the widespread adoption of protective behaviors deemed both effective and feasible by the public3. Wearing a mask is considered one of the key methods for preventing the spread of COVID-19, especially among the elderly and individuals with chronic or underlying conditions, and it plays an important role in their self-care4. The results of the study by Lyu (2020) showed that the use of face masks among the general public can help reduce the spread of COVID-195. Moreover, the use of masks, in combination with other preventive measures, can help prevent the transmission of the virus from infected individuals to others and plays a critical role as one of the key factors in reducing the spread and transmission of the disease6. Wearing a mask, regardless of its type or design, can reduce the risk of COVID-19 infection. Maintaining a distance of 1 to 2 m in indoor spaces and ensuring proper ventilation, along with wearing a mask, is essential7.

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) recommends that even after receiving two doses of the COVID-19 vaccine, and a booster dose, preventive measures, such as wearing an appropriate mask in public places or around individuals at high risk of COVID-19, should still be followed due to the rapid transmission of the virus and the potential for infection8. Moreover, the duration of immunity provided by COVID-19 vaccines has not yet been clearly determined9; therefore, vaccinated individuals should continue to follow health protocols even after receiving the vaccine. As a result, the possibility of infection among vaccinated individuals, especially those with chronic illnesses, remained a significant concern in various communities.

Before the COVID-19 pandemic, the effectiveness of mask use in reducing the spread of respiratory infections was a controversial topic, as there was insufficient evidence to support this practice10. However, with the onset of the pandemic, scientific evidence gradually increased, and compelling data demonstrated that wearing masks and practicing social distancing served as effective non-pharmaceutical interventions in reducing the spread of such infections11. In particular, masks were recognized both as a tool for source control—preventing virus transmission from infected individuals—and as a barrier to reduce exposure to the infection for others10–12.

A recent meta-analysis showed that high-filtration respirators (e.g., N95 masks) offer significantly greater protection than surgical or cloth masks. Mandated mask use reduces respiratory pathogen transmission at the community level. Beyond their protective function, masks have also taken on socio-cultural meanings, and resistance to mask-wearing has often been tied to misinformation and political beliefs. While most people tolerate masks well, those with specific health conditions or disabilities, such as hearing impairments, may encounter difficulties. Furthermore, the widespread use of disposable masks raises environmental concerns, including microplastic pollution13. Despite these concerns, the correct use of masks can play a crucial role in limiting the spread of the virus and may help reduce disease severity in case of infection14. These findings clearly emphasize the importance of mask-wearing in the community and the necessity of adhering to health protocols to protect public health. Although the World Health Organization declared the end of the COVID-19 pandemic on May 5, 2023, it warned that humans will coexist with the virus for the long term15.

Patients with chronic illnesses often have weakened immune systems or other health conditions that increase their susceptibility to infections, making preventive measures such as mask-wearing especially critical for reducing their risk of illness. Exploring their lived experiences with mask usage during COVID-19 can reveal unique challenges and needs. Using a phenomenological approach, this study provides valuable insights for healthcare providers and policymakers to develop effective support programs. The findings contribute to improving pandemic preparedness, shaping health policies, and enhancing community education tailored to this population’s needs.

Methods

Design

A descriptive phenomenological approach was employed to explore the lived experiences of individuals with chronic illnesses related to mask-wearing during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Participants and setting

Participants were selected through purposive sampling from among patients with chronic illnesses attending healthcare centers affiliated with the Faculty of Medical Sciences in Maragheh, Iran. A total of 15 participants were included based on the following criteria: a minimum two-year history of a chronic illness (including cardiovascular conditions such as hypertension, heart failure, myocardial infarction, and open-heart surgery; respiratory conditions such as asthma, COPD, and allergies; diabetes; and renal failure), age over 18 years, consistent use of face masks during healthcare visits, willingness to participate, and the ability to articulate their experiences (Table 1).

Data collection

Data collection took place between June 2023 and December 2023, using in-depth semi-structured interviews and field notes. A total of 24 interviews were conducted with 15 participants, each lasting approximately 45 min. Notably, nine participants were interviewed a second time to gain deeper insights and clarify emerging themes. These follow-up interviews enriched the data and helped ensure a more comprehensive understanding of their lived experiences.

Interviews were held in private or staff rest areas within healthcare centers to ensure comfort and confidentiality. Each session began with open-ended questions such as, “Please describe a typical day of wearing a mask,” and “What does mask-wearing mean to you?” Probing questions were used to elicit further detail, and non-verbal cues, such as nodding and supportive gestures, were employed to encourage deeper reflection.

Prior to each interview, participants were informed of the study’s objectives, and written informed consent was obtained for participation and audio recording. Verbal reconfirmation was also sought immediately before recording began. Interviews were recorded using an MP3 device, and the interviewer took observational notes on participants’ tone, emotions, and signs of fatigue. Contextual notes were also made regarding the participants’ environments and their mask-wearing behavior during interactions. All notes were treated as data and were transcribed, coded, and analyzed alongside the interview content. Sampling continued until data saturation was reached, meaning that no new themes, insights, or variations were emerging from subsequent interviews. Saturation was assessed through ongoing review and analysis of the interview transcripts. After the 15th interview, and especially through the second interviews with 9 participants, the research team observed that responses became repetitive and consistent, indicating that the major themes had been thoroughly explored.

Data analysis

Data were analyzed using Colaizzi’s seven-step method. First, all transcripts were read several times for a holistic understanding of participants’ lived experiences. In the second step, significant statements related to the phenomenon were extracted. Next, explicit meanings were formulated from these statements. In step four, these meanings were clustered into themes and sub-themes. Then, a comprehensive description of the phenomenon was developed. In step six, the fundamental structure of the participants’ experiences was identified. Finally, to validate the findings, summaries of the results were reviewed with five participants either in person or via telephone. Feedback from participants was incorporated into the final analysis, with minor clarifications integrated to ensure that the thematic description accurately reflected their lived experiences16. The first author performed this step with the participants.

Rigor

Scientific rigor was ensured based on Guba and Lincoln’s criteria: credibility, dependability, confirmability, and transferability17. Credibility was enhanced through member-checking, with five participants reviewing and confirming that the themes accurately reflected their experiences. To establish dependability, the findings—including subthemes and main themes—were reviewed and revised through consultation with two external qualitative experts. Confirmability was ensured by practicing reflexivity and setting aside researcher biases throughout the process. The research team recognized that personal experiences with chronic illness and mask-wearing during the COVID-19 pandemic could influence data interpretation. To address potential biases, researchers engaged in reflective journaling throughout data collection and analysis, regularly discussing interpretations among the team to ensure that findings were grounded in participants’ accounts rather than personal assumptions. These procedures helped minimize subjective influence and maintain the credibility of the thematic analysis. Transferability was supported by using maximum variation sampling to capture a wide range of perspectives and ensuring thick descriptions of context and participant characteristics.

Results

Fifteen patients with chronic diseases participated in this study. The demographic and social characteristics of the participants are presented in Table 1.

The lived experiences of these patients regarding mask-wearing revealed a main theme titled “A Revolution in Lifestyle,” which comprised two sub-themes: “Behavioral Changes for Survival” and “New Challenges in Life.”

The sub-theme “Behavioral Changes for Survival” included aspects such as knowledge enhancement, attitude modification, and adaptation to new practices. The sub-theme “New Challenges in Life” encompassed physical discomforts, social difficulties, and a novel form of waste generation associated with mask use. Behavioral changes for survival

Following the emergence of COVID-19 and widespread reports about the high risk of infection and severe complications among individuals with chronic illnesses, preventive efforts became a top priority for this population. Participants in this study emphasized that survival required fundamental lifestyle changes, transformations that, for many, to redefine their lives and priorities, and reshaped the meaning of their lives. Accordingly, they followed government health guidelines with extreme care and vigilance, striving to adapt their daily routines to align with preventive measures.

For these individuals, behavior change during the pandemic functioned not only as a survival strategy but also as an opportunity to re-evaluate life priorities and adopt more adaptive coping strategies. Participants reported that consistent mask-wearing and adherence to other preventive measures enhanced their sense of safety in public, allowed continuation of daily and social activities, and supported the fulfillment of personal responsibilities. By increasing their perceived control over health risks, these behaviors reduced anxiety and stress, supported adaptive coping, and strengthened personal resilience. Collectively, these adaptations contributed to improved overall quality of life during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Information seeking

With the emergence of novel and unfamiliar diseases, the need for accurate and up-to-date information about various aspects of these illnesses increased significantly. In this context, the World Health Organization and national health authorities continuously gathered, analyzed, and updated data to develop effective guidelines for disease prevention and control. This ongoing process of information gathering meant that, as the situation evolved, new decisions were made and previous recommendations were revised and communicated to the public.

Among those most in need of reliable and timely information were individuals with underlying chronic conditions, who faced higher risks of infection. For these patients, continuous access to trustworthy health information, especially through modern information technologies, was essential for managing their condition effectively.

“Every morning when I woke up, I looked for any new updates about the disease or anything new related to prevention.” (Participant 2)

They reported that each day, they actively searched for new information about the disease through social media and television, hoping to alleviate some of their anxiety about infection and death. In a time when even healthcare professionals acknowledged their limited understanding of the virus, staying informed with up-to-date knowledge was seen as the only viable means of self-protection during this unfamiliar emergency. However, participants also encountered misinformation regarding self-care practices and unscientific treatment methods.

Despite the spread of misleading content in the media and the promotion of anti-mask messages by certain groups, these factors did not deter participants from wearing masks. The illness, deaths, and complications experienced by those around them triggered a deep sense of fear and stress, making the risk of infection and death the most compelling reasons for adhering to mask use. This behavior continued until vaccines were developed and their effectiveness became clear. Participants described vaccination as the only reliable solution to relieve their fear and anxiety, and as a justification for eventually reducing their strict adherence to protective measures, including mask-wearing.

“At first, I was very strict about wearing mask. I looked for new information about how to protect myself and my family from everywhere: TV, doctor’s visits, even on Telegram and Instagram. I became really sensitive about it. In the beginning, I never forgot to wear my mask; I was very careful, because every day we heard about so many people dying from COVID—it made us afraid. Later on, sometimes I would forget to wear it, and my kids kept reminding me, ‘Don’t forget your mask, put your mask on.” (Participant 14)

Change in attitude toward mask-wearing

The global spread of COVID-19, its high infection and mortality rates, and the resulting crisis especially for individuals with underlying health conditions, heightened people’s sensitivity to staying informed through media and social networks. As they became more aware of the situation, many individuals actively sought to prevent infection in themselves and others. Protective behaviors such as mask-wearing, maintaining physical distance, avoiding family gatherings, and even refraining from visiting their own children became essential coping strategies. These practices were no longer viewed as temporary measures but as necessary components of daily life.

When family members contracted COVID-19, those with chronic illnesses who had consistently worn masks and either avoided infection or experienced only mild symptoms without requiring hospitalization, came to believe that mask-wearing was likely the key factor in preserving their physical well-being. Consequently, they became even more committed to preventive measures.

Initially, participants adopted mask-wearing out of necessity for survival. However, as the pandemic continued, it evolved into a sense of social responsibility, a moral obligation not only to protect oneself but also to safeguard others from transmission. Over time, adherence to public health guidelines became a daily effort to protect both personal and community health.

“Wearing a mask is a human and ethical necessity. A person must also consider others.You can’t just ignore people around you.” (Participant 9)

“I always wear a mask for myself, so I don’t catch anything from others, and so I don’tpass anything on to anyone else.” (Participant 15)

“Now, wearing a mask is more of a social issue than a personal one. It’s not just about me avoiding COVID—my loved ones and those around me matter. If I get it and pass it to someone else, it might not affect me seriously, but it could be much worse for them. That’s why this is a social issue… it makes me feel obligated to wear it.” (Participant 8)

Habit of continuous mask use

During the COVID-19 pandemic, individuals with chronic illnesses were warned that, if infected, they faced a higher risk of mortality compared to the general population. Many had either personally experienced severe illness or witnessed its impact on others around them. As a result, the majority consistently wore masks in their daily activities and limited their presence in public spaces. Whether for grocery shopping, administrative tasks, or going to work, mask-wearing became an integral and inseparable part of their daily lives.

Several participants described a profound shift in their values and attitudes toward health and self-protection. Some participants stated that even after the official end of the emergency phase, they continued to wear masks—often more consistently than before—not only due to lingering fear of infection but also as a precaution against respiratory issues caused by influenza or air pollution. Interestingly, several admitted that prior to the pandemic, despite medical advice, they rarely used masks; however, their habits had since changed significantly.

“After going through COVID, I truly understand the value of health. I prefer or maybe I should say, I’m convinced that I need to always wear a mask outside the house to protect my health.” (Participant 8)

Another recommendation from the Ministry of Health was the frequent replacement of masks to avoid using dirty or damp ones. Participants reported adhering to this guideline and stated that they would change their masks whenever necessary. Most said they always carried an extra mask with them and replaced it as needed. In situations where an extra mask was unavailable, some improvised by covering their nose and mouth with a scarf, shawl, or even tissue paper.

“Sometimes you notice it’s gotten damp, so I pull my scarf over the mask. Now that it’s winter, I pull my shawl or scarf over it and hold it in place so there won’t be any problem.” (Participant 3)

Despite being aware of the necessity of changing their masks, participants sometimes used a single mask for extended hours, wearing it continuously from morning until bedtime without replacement. They only pulled it down to their chin when it was necessary to eat or drink. In some cases, a single mask was used for several consecutive days. To store the mask at home or at the workplace, it was either hung on a clothes hanger available in the setting or placed inside a plastic bag or handbag for later reuse.

“I take off my mask along with my clothes in the stairwell and leave it there. Later, when I need to go out, I just pick it up from the same place, put it on, and leave.” (Participant 12)

“When I come home, I take off the mask and put it somewhere. I don’t throw it away after just one use—I don’t stay in crowded places much. I just leave it in a corner. I use one mask two or three times; I go out, come back, and leave it in the hallway again.” (Participant 8)

During daily activities, many participants reported that they often carried extra masks with them because they would sweat, causing the mask to become wet and require replacement. In situations where they did not have an extra mask, they tried to keep the wet mask on their face or cover their mouth and nose with other items they had with them.

“While shopping in the summer heat, I was sweating so badly that I had to pull the mask away from my mouth and use my shawl to cover my nose and mouth to protect myself.” (Participant 2)

Some participants also mentioned that due to mask shortages or the high cost of masks, it was sometimes difficult to obtain them. As a result, they occasionally used cloth masks.

“I had sewn a double-layered cloth mask. I would wash it, dry it under the sun, then iron it and use it again.” (Participant 4)

New challenges in life

The new lifestyle required during the COVID-19 pandemic made it necessary for people, especially those with chronic illnesses, to follow new routines in their daily lives. This significant change brought various challenges and led to both temporary and persistent physical problems for patients with chronic conditions. In addition, restrictions in communication with loved ones, such as children, relatives, and friends, caused feelings of isolation. The frequent use of disposable masks also contributed to a new source of environmental waste, which could eventually have negative consequences for everyone.

Physical side effects

Participants stated that while they had no problem with wearing a clean mask, they experienced difficulties keeping it on for extended periods. These difficulties were sometimes temporary and included shortness of breath, excessive sweating, and impaired vision for those wearing glasses. Despite understanding the necessity of wearing a mask in crowded places, especially during peak periods of the pandemic, many participants reported experiencing breathlessness while wearing it. They described a sense of suffocation, prompting them to quickly pull the mask down, take a deep breath, and then pull it back up. This action was often done instinctively, regardless of the presence of others.

“When I walk outside, I feel like I can’t breathe. I just can’t catch my breath.” (Participant 7)

“While walking, I feel like I’m suffocating. I pull the mask down for a few minutes so I don’t lose my breath, and then I pull it back up.” (Participant 14)

“In summer, when I go to the market, I sweat a lot. I feel short of breath and have to pull down my mask, take a few deep breaths, and then continue walking. I don’t even pay attention to whether the area is crowded or not, because I feel like I’m choking…” (Participant 7)

Some participants reported persistent physical side effects related to mask use. These effects included skin sensitivity, respiratory irritation, eye inflammation, and injuries behind the ears caused by mask straps. These symptoms persisted for a longer duration and, in some cases, led participants to stop wearing masks altogether.

“It makes my face a bit red. When I sweat, my face gets irritated and sometimes I get rashes or pimples, especially in summer… In such cases, I stop wearing a mask for a while, even if I risk getting sick.” (Participant 2)

“When I wear it for a long time, I notice dry, flaky skin around my nose, and the area under my eyes becomes completely red. It even reached my eyes. I went to an eye doctor and he gave me drops, saying my eye was infected.” (Participant 13)

Difficulties in social interactions

Most participants reported experiencing communication problems following the onset of the pandemic and the widespread use of masks. Difficulties in speaking with others often led to interruptions in interactions. Moreover, the limited ability to understand facial expressions hindered participants’ communication with others.

“When I talk to someone while wearing a mask, I feel suffocated. Talking makes me breathe faster, and that makes it much worse.” (Participant 1)

“In some places where mask-wearing is mandatory, you have to speak loudly for the other person to hear you. For example, in offices or banks, there is a glass barrier and the staff says, ‘Speak louder,’ or ‘Pull your mask down so I can hear you.’” (Participant 13)

Another challenge related to mask use was the economic burden on families. Participants acknowledged that due to the societal conditions caused by the pandemic, many did not have a stable financial situation, and the additional expense of purchasing masks for daily use took up a portion of the family income.

“It was an extra cost we had to pay. We used to buy 50 masks and would use them for about 10 to 15 days at most.” (Participant 13)

“Sometimes the cost of masks became very high. It was really a terrible time, (said with choking emotion). Before the announcement of the pandemic, masks were everywhere, but from the very next day, all masks disappeared from the market and prices skyrocketed. I would stand in line at pharmacies just to get one mask, and after hours, they only gave one per person. That one mask would last only a few hours, even though you might have an illness and a high risk of infection. We went through the hardest days.” (Participant 8)

Environmental issues

Participants reported that despite receiving training on mask use, they had incomplete knowledge about proper mask disposal. They believed that improper disposal of masks contributed to the spread of disease. Additionally, due to the high cost of masks, discarding a clean or slightly used mask outdoors was viewed as wasteful and inappropriate by some participants. Consequently, masks that were used and potentially contaminated were sometimes repurposed for other daily uses, such as cleaning surfaces. Participants regarded masks as non-biodegradable plastic items that could have a significant environmental impact, and the pandemic had created conditions that intensified this problem.

“At home, I used to put the mask inside a plastic bag before throwing it away. Throwing masks carelessly outside during the pandemic caused pollution, especially in our community. In my opinion, there was no proper education on how to use and dispose of masks correctly.” (Participant 11)

“You see people don’t even throw masks in trash bins in public places or streets… This is harmful. Even if there was no COVID-19, if someone had a cold, it would still spread.” (Participant 6)

“Sometimes you see me cleaning the mirror with a mask at home (laughs), wiping dust off my shoes, or it just becomes a kind of decoration in the house. I hang it on the door handle. I spent a lot of money; I can’t just tear it and throw it away; it feels like a waste.” (Participant 7)

“The problem is that it pollutes the environment because we have no control, and sometimes people take off used masks and throw them on the streets. From this perspective, it is very dangerous.” (Participant 8)

Discussion

This study aimed to explore the experiences of patients with chronic conditions regarding mask-wearing during the COVID-19 pandemic. The qualitative findings revealed a central theme of A Revolution in Lifestyle, encompassing two subthemes: behavioral changes for survival and new challenges in daily life. Participants emphasized that consistent mask-wearing and adherence to preventive measures helped reduce the risk and potential severity of COVID-19, particularly for individuals with chronic illnesses. These adaptations also allowed participants to maintain daily activities, fulfill personal responsibilities, and engage in social interactions. By enhancing their perceived control over health risks, these behavioral adjustments supported adaptive coping, reduced anxiety, and strengthened personal resilience.

The theme of behavioral change for survival revealed that chronic patients made significant adjustments in their behaviors to protect themselves against COVID-19. These changes were primarily driven by fear of contracting the virus and its potentially severe complications. Findings of a recent study demonstrated that chronic patients made considerable efforts to preserve their lives and prevent infection18. Such efforts led to fundamental changes in their lifestyles and reshaped their meaning of life.

Emerging evidence strongly supports that persistent mask-wearing reduces not only the risk of contracting COVID-19 but also the severity of disease, particularly among vulnerable populations such as individuals with chronic illnesses. Masks limit viral exposure, which can mitigate disease progression, leading to milder symptoms and fewer complications. This protective effect is critical for reducing morbidity and mortality in high-risk groups and underscores the importance of maintaining mask use alongside other preventive measures19,20.

In our study participants diligently and meticulously adhered to government guidelines for disease prevention and wholeheartedly tried to smooth their path in life. In our study middle-aged and older adults described heightened vigilance and stricter adherence to mask use, reflecting a sense of higher vulnerability, compared with younger participants. This aligns with studies that show perceived risk and coping strategies often differ meaningfully by age group21. Such adherence also enhanced participants’ confidence in managing their health, reinforcing a sense of control and promoting sustained protective behaviors over time. Given the small sample size of 15 participants, the findings regarding gender and educational differences should be interpreted cautiously. Nonetheless, preliminary insights suggest female participants were generally more attentive to mask use and preventive behaviors, and those with higher education levels sought more reliable information and adhered more consistently to guidelines. These observations offer valuable contextual understanding but are not generalized; further research with larger and more diverse samples is needed. Consistent with our findings, previous research has shown that behavioral changes during crises like the COVID-19 pandemic serve not only as survival strategies but also as opportunities to redefine life and foster a positive approach to challenges22. These changes can enhance quality of life and individual empowerment, ultimately supporting survival. Three subthemes emerged within this domain:

Regarding the subtheme of information seeking, individuals with chronic illnesses actively sought updated information about COVID-19 and preventive strategies. They relied on a variety of sources, including television, radio, social media platforms, and healthcare professionals to obtain the information they needed. Notably, participants’ searches frequently focused on mask-related information. Repeated exposure to scientific explanations, personal experiences reported by other patients, and expert guidance led them to internalize masks as personal protective tools, transforming mask-wearing from an externally imposed requirement into a routine practice aligned with their values and identity. This behavior underscores the critical role of awareness and information literacy in adopting protective measures. This finding aligns with previous studies, which have demonstrated that during times of crisis, people tend to actively pursue new knowledge in order to protect themselves23.

Participants in this study reported that they regularly turned to television and social media each day in search of the latest updates on the pandemic, in an attempt to reduce their anxiety about contracting the virus or dying from it. Similarly, a recent study exploring information-seeking behaviors among cancer patients during the pandemic identified television programs as their primary source of COVID-related information24.

In another study conducted in Bangladesh, only 10% of participants expressed strong agreement that government-issued information was accurate, while 16.4% reported complete distrust in such sources25. These findings underscore that, during public health crises, trust in information sources, in addition to access, plays a significant role in shaping individuals’ health-related decisions.

Regarding the subtheme of attitude change, the outbreak of COVID-19 significantly influenced the attitudes of individuals with chronic illnesses toward mask-wearing. Initially, they accepted wearing masks out of fear and a sense of compulsion due to the risk of infection. However, as the pandemic persisted, mask-wearing gradually came to be viewed as a social responsibility and moral obligation. Participants reported that beyond protecting their own health, they saw masks as a means to prevent transmission to others. Similar findings in other studies have shown that public attitudes toward mask use shifted during the pandemic, with many individuals perceiving it as an essential public duty26. Mask-wearing, when recognized as a socially accepted behavior, encouraged broader public adherence. Moreover, increased awareness of the dangers of COVID-19 and an understanding of the effectiveness of masks in preventing transmission helped promote acceptance of this protective behavior27,28.

Regarding the subtheme of habit of continuous mask use, the findings revealed that individuals with chronic conditions gradually adapted to the new circumstances by incorporating regular mask-wearing into their daily routines. Whether shopping, managing administrative tasks, or going to work, they consistently wore masks, which became an inseparable aspect of their daily lives. This aligns with findings from other studies, which suggest that in times of crisis, people often exhibit a remarkable capacity to adjust to new norms and environmental demands29,30.

The second major theme identified in this study was “new challenges in daily life.” This theme illustrates how mask-wearing introduced new difficulties for individuals with chronic illnesses during the COVID-19 pandemic. These challenges were categorized into three sub-themes: physical side effects, social difficulties, and waste generation.

About Physical side effects, wearing masks resulted in both temporary and long-term physical issues for chronically ill participants. Temporary problems included shortness of breath, excessive sweating, and fogging of eyeglasses. Long-term adverse effects involved skin irritation, respiratory sensitivities, eye inflammation, and ear sores caused by prolonged pressure from mask straps. These findings are consistent with other studies reporting that extended mask usage can lead to dermatological, respiratory, and ocular problems. For instance, recent research has indicated that wearing N95 masks for prolonged periods may elevate CO₂ levels, causing symptoms such as headaches, dizziness, and fatigue. One study noted that after 12 h of continuous N95 mask use, participants commonly reported elevated CO₂ levels alongside these symptoms19. However, a recent systematic review by Wangsan et al. (2022) indicates that significant changes in CO₂ and oxygen levels occur mainly during high-intensity physical activities in healthy individuals, while no meaningful changes are observed during low-to-moderate activities. These changes are generally subclinical, suggesting that routine mask use in chronic patients is unlikely to pose significant health risks20.

About difficulties in Social Interactions our findings showed that the participants experienced communication barriers due to mask-wearing and pandemic-related restrictions. The inability to read facial expressions and subtle cues disrupted face-to-face communication, reducing the effectiveness of interpersonal interactions. A recent study similarly found that in clinical settings, masks can serve as a physical barrier to effective communication, limiting emotional expression and nonverbal understanding31. In broader contexts, mask use combined with social distancing may lead to feelings of isolation and decreased social interaction, potentially contributing to heightened levels of stress and depression, particularly among individuals with limited social support32.

Frequent use of disposable face masks generated a new wave of environmental waste with potential long-term impacts on public health and ecological sustainability. Beyond the general harms of single-use plastics33–35, improper disposal of masks in public areas such as streets and parks has become a notable concern, contributing to pollution and potentially facilitating viral transmission36,37. To mitigate these risks, raising public awareness about proper mask disposal, promoting biodegradable or reusable alternatives, and integrating waste management strategies into public health policies are essential steps toward balancing infection prevention with environmental protection.

Limitations

Our study has some limitations. First, due to its qualitative and phenomenological design, the sample size was relatively small and may not represent the full diversity of chronic patients. Second, data were collected through self-reported experiences, which are susceptible to recall bias and may be influenced by current health status, attitudes, and social desirability. Memories of past events, symptoms, and care experiences may be reconstructed or biased, potentially affecting the accuracy and completeness of participants’ accounts. Finally, the findings are specific to chronic patients and may not be generalizable to other populations or those with different health conditions.

Conclusion

The findings of this study reveal that mask-wearing during the COVID-19 pandemic generated complex and multifaceted experiences for individuals with chronic illnesses. While wearing a mask was widely perceived as a protective measure essential for survival, it also introduced new challenges into their daily lives. These insights can inform policymakers and healthcare professionals in gaining a deeper understanding of the unique needs and struggles of chronically ill patients during public health crises. It is imperative that healthcare stakeholders develop strategies to mitigate the negative impacts of prolonged mask use among this vulnerable population.

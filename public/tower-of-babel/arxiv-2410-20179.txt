Cubic Siegel polynomials and the bifurcation measure
Matthieu Astorg, Davoud Cheraghi, Arnaud Chéritat

Abstract
. We prove that cubic polynomial maps with a fixed Siegel disk and a critical orbit eventually landing inside that Siegel disk lie in the support of the bifurcation measure μ bif \mu_{\mathrm{bif}} . This answers a question of Dujardin in positive. Our result implies the existence of holomorphic disks in the support of μ bif \mu_{\mathrm{bif}} , and also implies that the set of rigid parameters is not closed in the moduli space of cubic polynomials.

1.
Introduction

Let
\textup{Poly}_{d}
denote the space of monic centered polynomial maps of degree
d\geq 2
, and let

\mathcal{M}_{d}
denote the moduli space of rational maps of degree
d\geq 2
on the Riemann sphere.
In general, we let
M=\textup{Poly}_{d}
or
\mathcal{M}_{d}
.
By definition, the
bifurcation locus

\mathrm{Bif}\subset M
is the complement of the set of

J
-stability, that is,
\mathrm{Bif}
is the set of parameters around which the Julia set does not locally move holomorphically.
The celebrated results of Mañé-Sad-Sullivan
[
MSS83
]
and Lyubich
[
Lyu83
]
provide several other
equivalent characterisations of the bifurcation locus, in terms of the behaviour of the critical orbits or in terms
of periodic cycles changing from repelling to attracting or vice-versa.

In
[
DeM01
]
, DeMarco introduced a
bifurcation current

T_{\mathrm{bif}}
, which is a closed
positive current of bidegree
(1,1)
whose support is equal to
\mathrm{Bif}
.
Bassanelli and Berteloot
[
BB07
]
studied the higher degree currents

T_{\mathrm{bif}}^{k}:=T_{\mathrm{bif}}\wedge\ldots\wedge T_{\mathrm{bif}}
,
1\leq k\leq\dim M
, which detect higher codimensional
bifurcation phenomena.
When
k=\dim M
,
T_{\mathrm{bif}}^{k}
is called the
bifurcation measure
and is denoted by
\mu_{\mathrm{bif}}
.
This is a finite positive measure, which is the Monge-Ampère measure of the plurisubharmonic potential

M\ni f\mapsto L(f)
, where
L(f)
is the Lyapunov exponent of the unique measure of maximal entropy.
The support of
\mu_{\mathrm{bif}}
is also called the maximal bifurcation locus, in the sense that it
detects maximal codimension bifurcation phenomena.

In recent years, the study of the bifurcation measure
\mu_{\mathrm{bif}}
and its support has attracted considerable attention.
For instance, by the works of Bassanelli-Berteloot
[
BB07
]
,
Buff-Epstein
[
BE09
]
and Dujardin-Favre
[
DF08
]
, it is known
that the support of
\mu_{\mathrm{bif}}
coincides with the closure of the set of parameters with the maximal number of
non-repelling cycles (namely
d
in the case of
\textup{Poly}_{d}
or
2d-2
in the case of
\mathcal{M}_{d}
), and also with the
closure of the set of parameters for which all critical points are strictly preperiodic to repelling cycles.

For parameters in the support of
\mu_{\mathrm{bif}}
, all critical points must be active.
However, this is not a sufficient condition. For instance, the following example is due to Douady

[
DF08
, Example 6.13]
.
The polynomial map
f(z):=z+z^{2}/2+z^{3}\in\textup{Poly}_{3}
has one parabolic fixed point which attracts both critical points,
so they are both active at
f
in the family
\textup{Poly}_{3}
. However,
f
is
parabolic attracting
,
which means that any small perturbation of
f
possesses either a parabolic or an attracting fixed point.
It follows that
f\notin\mathrm{supp}\,\,\mu_{\mathrm{bif}}
.
A more subtle example is given in
[
IM20
]
by Inou and Mukherjee, where they
construct a real-analytic family
(f_{t})_{t\in I}
of
parabolic repelling
cubic polynomials,
for which both critical points are attracted to the parabolic fixed point, but
none of the
f_{t}
is in the support of
\mu_{\mathrm{bif}}
.
Loosely speaking, these examples show that in general a certain independence of
the critical orbits is required for a map
f\in M
to be in the support of
\mu_{\mathrm{bif}}
.

In this paper, we address the following question stated by Dujardin in
[
Duj09
]
:
Given a cubic polynomial map with a Siegel disk and a critical orbit which eventually lands inside the Siegel disk,
does that cubic polynomial belong to the support of
\mu_{\mathrm{bif}}
?
Note that the second critical orbit must accumulate on the boundary of the Siegel disk, so that both critical points
are active in
\textup{Poly}_{3}
but are related through the Siegel disk.
Such maps also have a one-parameter family of quasi-conformal deformations, similar to the examples of Douady
and Inou-Mukherjee. However, in our case the parameter family is complex.

We work with the family of cubic polynomials with a marked fixed point placed at
0
,
parametrised as

(1)

f_{\lambda,a}(z)=\lambda z+az^{2}+z^{3},\quad\lambda,a\in\mathbb{C}.

Let
\lambda=e^{2i\pi\theta}
, for an irrational number
\theta
.
The map
f_{\lambda,a}
is
linearisable
near the origin if there is a conformal change
of coordinate
\phi:V\to\mathbb{D}(0,r)
defined on a neighbourhood
V
of the origin such that

\phi\circ f_{\lambda,a}=\lambda\phi
.
The maximal domain of that linearisation is called the
Siegel disk
of
f_{\lambda,a}
.
By the classic work of Siegel
[
Sie42
]
and Brjuno
[
Brj71
]
,
if
\theta
satisfies the so-called
Brjuno condition

\sum_{n\geq 0}q_{n}^{-1}\log q_{n+1}<+\infty,

then for every
a\in\mathbb{C}
,
f_{\lambda,a}
is linearisable near the origin.
Here
(p_{n}/q_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is the sequence of best rational approximants of
\theta
.

Let us say that a cubic polynomial
f_{\lambda,a}
is a
Siegel polynomial of capture type
,
if
f_{\lambda,a}
has a Siegel disk containing the origin and a critical point whose orbit
eventually lands inside that Siegel disk.
Our main theorem is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Any Siegel polynomial of capture type
f_{\lambda,a}

is contained in the support of
\mu_{\mathrm{bif}}
.

For a fixed
\lambda=e^{2i\pi\theta}
, it is not difficult to prove that the bifurcation locus
of the slice
\{f_{\lambda,a}:a\in{\mathbb{C}}\}
is contained in the support of
\mu_{\mathrm{bif}}
.
However, if
\theta
is a Brjuno number and
f_{e^{2i\pi\theta},a}
is a Siegel polynomial of capture type,
then it belongs to the stability locus of this slice.
This implies the following two consequences:

Corollary 1.2
.

There are holomorphic disks in the support of
\mu_{\mathrm{bif}}
in
\textup{Poly}_{3}
.

Recall that a polynomial
P
is called conformally rigid if any polynomial which
is quasi-conformally conjugate to
P
is affinely conjugate to
P
.
Following the pioneering works of Sullivan
[
Sul85
]
and Thurston
[
DH93
]
, now there is substantial body of work related to the
quasi-conformal and conformal rigidity conjectures in complex dynamics (MLC conjecture and higher degree analogues).

Corollary 1.3
.

The set of conformally rigid cubic polynomials is not closed in
\textup{Poly}_{3}
.

Indeed, Siegel polynomials of capture type are not conformally rigid (see Section
2.1
),
but strictly post-critically finite maps are conformally rigid
[
DH93
]
, and form a dense subset of
\mathrm{supp}\,\mu_{\mathrm{bif}}
by
[
BE09
]
.

2.
From elliptic to parabolic

Within Section
2
we assume that
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}
is a bounded type number.
In particular,
\theta
is a Brjuno number.

Let us fix
\lambda=e^{2i\pi\theta}
and consider the family of maps

f_{\lambda,a}(z)=\lambda z+az^{2}+z^{3},\quad a\in\mathbb{C}.

We shall denote the Siegel disk of
f_{\lambda,a}
with the notation
\Delta_{\theta}(a)
.

2.1.
Capture components

Definition 2.1
.

By a capture component we mean a connected component of the set

\{a\in{\mathbb{C}}:f_{\lambda,a}\text{ is a polynomial of capture type }\}.

By
[
Zak99
, Theorem 7.3]
, capture components are (open) stability components in the slice of maps
\{f_{\lambda,a}:a\in{\mathbb{C}}\}
,
and are contained in the connectedness locus.
In particular, they are simply connected (by the maximum principle).
By
[
Zak99
, Lemma 7.4]
, each capture component
V
contains a unique
centre
, that is, a parameter
a_{0}\in V
where a critical point of
f_{\lambda,a_{0}}

is pre-periodic to
0
.
Moreover, all cubic polynomials in a capture component minus its center point are quasi-conformally conjugate to each other on
the Riemann sphere (
[
Zak99
, Theorem 7.5 (a)]
).

2.2.
Asymptotic size and conformal radius

Let
p_{n}/q_{n}
, for
n\geq 1
, be the sequence of the best rational approximants of
\theta
.
Define
\lambda_{n}=e^{2i\pi p_{n}/q_{n}}
.

By
[
Che20
]
, for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
there is a degree
q_{n}
polynomial map
b_{n}
such that

(2)

f_{\lambda_{n},a}^{\circ{q_{n}}}(z)=z+b_{n}(a)z^{q_{n}+1}+O(z^{q_{n}+2}).

Let us say that an open set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}}
is a non-degenerate parabolic locus, if for all sufficiently large
n\geq 1
,

b_{n}
is non-zero on
\Omega
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
denote the Zakeri curve
[
Zak99
]
, i.e.

\mathcal{C}_{\theta}=\{a\in{\mathbb{C}}:\text{ both critical points of $f_{\lambda,a}$ belong to $\partial\Delta_{\theta}(a)$}\}.

Let
r_{\theta}(a)
denote the conformal radius of the Siegel disk of
f_{\lambda,a}
at
0
.

The following result will be crucial to our argument:

Theorem 2.2

(
[
Che20
]
)
.

For every irrational number of bounded type
\theta
and every
a\in\mathbb{C}
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}|b_{n}(a)|^{1/q_{n}}=1/r_{\theta}(a).

Moreover, the convergence is uniform on compact subsets of any non-degenerate parabolic locus in
{\mathbb{C}}
.

The first part of the above theorem is readily presented in
[
Che20
]
.
The latter part also follows from that paper.
Indeed, if
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}}
is a non-degenerate parabolic locus, for large
n
, the sequence of maps
q_{n}^{-1}\log|b_{n}(a)|
are harmonic on
\Omega
,
and by
[
Che20
, Proposition 54]
, converge to
-\log r_{\theta}(a)
in
L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
.
Then, the
L^{1}_{loc}
convergence implies uniform convergence on compact sets for harmonic functions.

2.3.
Parabolic rays converge to Siegel internal rays

Let
U\subset{\mathbb{C}}
be an arbitrary capture component. There exists a critical point
c_{a}
and an integer

k\geq 1
such that for all
a\in U
,
f_{{\lambda},a}^{\circ k}(c_{a})\in\Delta_{\theta}(a)
.
We let
U^{*}:=\{a\in U:f_{{\lambda},a}^{\circ k}(c_{a})\neq 0\}
.
For
a\in U
, let
\phi_{a}:\Delta_{\theta}(a)\to\mathbb{D}_{r_{\theta}(a)}
denote the linearising coordinate,
normalised by
\phi_{a}^{\prime}(0)=1
.
We make use of the following lemma of Jellouli
[
Jel94
]
, which is obtained through
direct calculations, a compactness argument, and the bound
|\theta_{0}-p_{n}/q_{n}|\leq 1/q_{n}^{2}
.

Lemma 2.3

(Jellouli)
.

For every
r_{0}>0
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
a\in U
, all
z\in\mathbb{D}(0,r_{0}r_{\theta}(a))
, all
n\geq 1
, and all

0\leq k\leq q_{n}
:

|\phi_{a}\circ f_{\lambda_{n},a}^{\circ k}\circ\phi_{a}^{-1}(z)-\lambda_{n}^{k}z|\leq Ck|z|/q_{n}^{2}.

There is a holomorphic motion of the boundary of
\Delta_{\theta}(a)
over
U
.
The map
(a,z)\mapsto\phi_{a}(z)
is holomorphic over
a\in U
and
z\in\Delta_{\theta}(a)
,
and the map
a\mapsto\log r_{\theta}(a)
is harmonic on
U
,
see
[
Sul
]
or
[
Zak16
]
.
In particular, there exists a holomorphic function
u:U\to{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\mathrm{Re}\,u=\log r_{\theta}
.
We let
v:=e^{u}
, so that
v
is also holomorphic and
|v|=r_{\theta}
.

For
a\in U
and
n\geq 1
, consider the map

\tilde{G}_{n,a}=\phi_{a}\circ f_{\lambda_{n},a}^{\circ q_{n}}\circ\phi_{a}^{-1},

which is defined on a neighbourhood of
0
.
We also consider the normalised map

G_{n,a}(z):=v(a)^{-1}\tilde{G}_{n,a}(v(a)z).

From (
2
), it is not difficult to see that near
0
,

(3)

G_{n,a}(z)=z+b_{n}(a)v(a)^{\circ q_{n}}z^{q_{n+1}}+O(z^{q_{n}+2}).

For any
r_{1}\in(0,1)
, there exists
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for all
a\in U
and all
n\geq N
,
G_{n,a}
is
defined and univalent on
\mathbb{D}(0,r_{1})
.
In particular, we may write

G_{n,a}(z)=ze^{g_{n,a}(z)}

for some function
g_{n,a}:\mathbb{D}(0,{r_{1}})\to{\mathbb{C}}
.
Then, the map

H_{n,a}(w):=w+g_{n,a}(e^{w})

is defined and univalent on the left half-plane
\mathrm{Re}\,w<\log r_{1}
, and satisfies

\exp\circ H_{n,a}=G_{n,a}\circ\exp.

We do not know if
U
is a non-degenerate parabolic locus.
Let
\Omega\subseteq U^{*}
be a simply connected non-degenerate parabolic locus. In particular, the convergence in Theroem
2.2

is uniform on compact subsets of
\Omega
.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
Che01
, Lem 4.2]

Fix an arbitrary
r_{1}\in(0,1)
. For
n\geq 1
and
a\in U
let
N(n,a)
denote the number of fixed points of
G_{n,a}
in
\mathbb{D}(0,r_{1})
.
Then, as
n\to\infty
,
N(n,a)/q_{n}
converges to
0
uniformly on compact subsets of
\Omega
.

Proof.

Let
\Lambda
be a compact set in
\Omega
.
For each
n\geq 1
,
N(n,a)
is uniformly bounded from above independent of
a\in\overline{U}
.
Let us choose
a_{n}\in\Lambda
so that
N(n,a_{n})=\max_{a\in\Lambda}N(n,a)
.
By Lemma
2.3
, there exists a sequence
r_{n}\to 1^{-}
such that for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

G_{n,a_{n}}
is defined and univalent on
\mathbb{D}(0,r_{n})
, and
\sup_{|z|\leq r_{n}}|G_{n,a_{n}}(z)-z|\to 0
.
We consider the normalised map
F_{n}(z):=r_{n}^{-1}G_{n,a_{n}}(r_{n}z)
, so that for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
,

F_{n}
is defined and univalent on
\mathbb{D}
, and
\sup_{|z|\leq 1}|F_{n}(z)-z|\to 0
.
In particular, we will always assume in what follows that
n
is large enough so that

\sup_{|z|\leq 1}|F_{n}(z)-z|\leq 2.

Moreover, we have

F_{n}(z)=z+C_{n}z^{q_{n}+1}+O(z^{q_{n}+2})

where
C_{n}:=b_{n}(a_{n})v(a_{n})^{q_{n}}r_{n}^{q_{n}}
.
By Theorem
2.2
and the definition of
v
, we have

(4)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{q_{n}}\log|C_{n}|=0.

For each
r\in(0,1]
, let
N_{n}(r)
denote the number of fixed points of
F_{n}
in
\mathbb{D}(0,r)\setminus\{0\}
,
counted with multiplicity.
Let
\zeta_{n,j}
, for
1\leq j\leq N_{n}(1)
, denote the fixed points of
F_{n}
in
\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
, repeated
with multiplicity.
There exists a holomorphic function
\sigma_{n}:\mathbb{D}\to{\mathbb{C}}
with no zeroes in
\mathbb{D}
such that for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
,

F_{n}(z)=z+z^{q_{n}+1}{\sigma_{n}(z)}\prod_{j=1}^{N_{n}(1)}\frac{z-\zeta_{j,n}}{1-\overline{\zeta_{j,n}}z}.

Clearly
\max_{|z|=1}|{\sigma_{n}(z)}|\leq\max_{|z|=1}|F_{n}(z)-z|\leq 2
, thus by the maximum principle,
|{\sigma_{n}(0)}|\leq 2
.
Moreover,
C_{n}={\sigma_{n}(0)}(-1)^{N_{n}(1)}\prod_{j=1}^{N_{n}(1)}\zeta_{j,n}
.
Let
r\in(r_{1},1)
.
Then

|C_{n}|=|{\sigma}_{n}(0)|\prod_{|\zeta_{j,n}|<r}|\zeta_{j,n}|\prod_{|\zeta_{j,n}|\geq r}|\zeta_{j,n}|\leq 2\cdot r^{N_{n}(r)}\cdot 1.

Therefore,

0\leq\frac{N_{n}(r)}{q_{n}}\leq\frac{\log 2-\log|C_{n}|}{q_{n}|\log r|}.

Combining with (
4
), we get

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N_{n}(r)}{q_{n}}=0.

Since
N_{n}(r)\geq N(n,a_{n})
for large enough
n
, we are done.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
r_{1}\in(0,1)
, and
\Lambda
be a compact set in
\Omega
.
There exists
N_{1}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for all
n\geq N_{1}
and all
a\in\Lambda
,
G_{n,a}
has no fixed points
in
\mathbb{D}(0,r_{1})
except for
z=0
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.3
, there exists
C=C(r_{1})>0
such that for all
n\geq 1
, all
1\leq k\leq q_{n}
, all
a\in\Lambda

and all
|z|\leq r_{1}r_{\theta}(a)
, we have

|\phi_{a}\circ f_{\lambda_{n},a}^{\circ k}\circ\phi_{a}^{-1}(z){-\lambda_{n}^{k}z}|\leq Ck|z|/q_{n}^{2}.

Let us consider
L_{n,a}(z):=v(a)^{-1}\phi_{a}\circ f_{\lambda_{n},a}\circ\phi_{a}^{-1}\left(zv(a)\right)
.
We have
L_{n,a}^{\circ q_{n}}=G_{n,a}
, and for all
n\geq 1
, all
1\leq k\leq q_{n}
and all
z\in\mathbb{D}(0,r_{1})
,

|L_{n,a}^{\circ k}(z)-\lambda_{n}^{k}z|\leq Ck|z|/q_{n}^{2}.

Let
s:=\min(1,1/C)>0
.
By Lemma
2.4
, there exists
N_{1}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for all
n\geq N_{1}
and all
a\in\Lambda
,

(5)

N(n,a)<sq_{n}/2.

Assume for a contradiction that there exists
n\geq N_{1}
,
a\in\Lambda
and
z\in\mathbb{D}(0,r_{1})\setminus\{0\}
such that

G_{n,a}(z)=z
.
Then for all
1\leq k\leq q_{n}
,
L_{n,a}^{\circ k}(z)
is also a fixed point of
G_{n,a}
.
We will show that the set
\{L_{n,a}^{\circ k}(z):1\leq k\leq q_{n}\}
contains at least
sq_{n}
elements,
which contradicts (
5
).

First, observe that if
1\leq k_{1}<k_{2}\leq q_{n}
, then

|\lambda_{n}^{k_{1}}-\lambda_{n}^{k_{2}}|\geq 4/q_{n}.

Then, for all
1\leq k_{1}<k_{2}\leq sq_{n}
:

\displaystyle|L_{n,a}^{\circ k_{1}}(z)-L_{n,a}^{\circ k_{2}}(z)|

\displaystyle\geq|\lambda_{n}^{k_{1}}z-\lambda_{n}^{k_{2}}z|-|L_{n,a}^{\circ k_{1}}(z)-\lambda_{n}^{k_{1}}z|-|L_{n,a}^{\circ k_{2}}(z)-\lambda_{n}^{k_{2}}z|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{4}{q_{n}}|z|-\frac{2Csq_{n}|z|}{q_{n}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2|z|}{q_{n}}.

In particular,
L_{n,a}^{\circ k_{1}}(z)\neq L_{n,a}^{\circ k_{2}}(z)
.
This completes the proof of the claim.
∎

In the following lemma we assume that
r_{1}\in(0,1)
,
\Lambda\subset\Omega
and
N_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
satisfy
Lemma
2.5
.

Lemma 2.6
.

For all
r_{1}\in(0,1)
, all
n\geq N_{1}
and all
a\in\Lambda
, on the left half-plane
\mathrm{Re}\,w<\log r_{1}
, we may write

g_{n,a}(e^{w})=e^{\ell_{n,a}(w)}

where

\ell_{n,a}(w)=\log b_{n}(a)+q_{n}u(a)+q_{n}w+k_{n,a}(e^{w})

for some holomorphic function
k_{n,a}:\mathbb{D}(0,r_{1})\to\mathbb{C}
satisfying
k_{n,a}(0)=0
.

Note that
\log b_{n}(a)
is well-defined on
\Omega
for large enough
n
, because
\Omega
is simply connected and
b_{n}(a)

becomes non-zero on
\Omega
.

Proof.

Recall that by definition, we have
g_{n,a}(z)=\log\frac{G_{n,a}(z)}{z}
, well-defined
on
\mathbb{D}(0,r_{1})
for all
n\geq N_{1}
and
a\in\Lambda
.
Moreover, by (
3
) and the definition of
v
, we have

G_{n,a}(z)=z+b_{n}(a)e^{q_{n}u(a)}z^{q_{n}+1}+O(z^{q_{n}+2}).

Therefore,

(6)

g_{n,a}(z)=\log\left(1+b_{n}(a)e^{q_{n}u(a)}z^{q_{n}}+O(z^{q_{n}+1})\right)=b_{n}(a)e^{q_{n}u(a)}z^{q_{n}}h_{n,a}(z)

for some function
h_{n,a}:\mathbb{D}(0,{r_{1}})\to{\mathbb{C}}
such that
h_{n,a}(0)=1
.
Moreover, because by Lemma
2.6
,
G_{n,a}
has no fixed points inside
\mathbb{D}(0,{r_{1}})\setminus\{0\}
,

h_{n,a}
does not vanish on
\mathbb{D}(0,{r_{1}})
.
Thus,
h_{n,a}(z)=\exp\circ k_{n,a}(z)
for some function
k_{n,a}:\mathbb{D}(0,{r_{1}})\to\mathbb{C}

with
k_{n,a}(0)=0
.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

For every
r_{0}\in(0,1)
and every compact set
\Lambda\subset\Omega
, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{a\in{\Lambda}}\sup_{|z|\leq r_{0}}\frac{\mathrm{Re}\,k_{n,a}(z)}{q_{n}}=0,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{a\in{\Lambda}}\sup_{|z|\leq r_{0}}\left|\frac{k_{n,a}^{\prime}(z)}{q_{n}}\right|=0.

Proof.

Let us fix arbitrary
r_{0}\in(0,1)
, compact set
\Lambda\subset\Omega
and
\epsilon>0
.
We choose
r_{1}\in(r_{0},1)
such that
-\log r_{1}<\epsilon/2
.
By Lemma
2.3
, if we let

\epsilon_{n}:=\sup_{a\in U}\sup_{|z|\leq r_{1}}|G_{n,a}(z)-z|,

then we have
\epsilon_{n}\leq C(r_{1})/q_{n}
, and hence
\epsilon_{n}\to 0
.

It follows from (
6
) that for all
(z,a)\in\mathbb{D}(0,r_{1})\times{\Lambda}
, we have

|b_{n}(a)e^{q_{n}u(a)}z^{q_{n}+1}e^{k_{n,a}(z)}|\leq\epsilon_{n}.

Let
(y_{n},a_{n})\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}(0,r_{1})\times{\Lambda}
be such that

By the maximum principle, we have
|y_{n}|=r_{1}
and
a_{n}\in\partial U
. Therefore,

\displaystyle r_{\theta}(a_{n})^{q_{n}}|b_{n}(a_{n})|r_{1}^{{q_{n}}}e^{\mathrm{Re}\,k_{n,a_{n}}(y_{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon_{n}/r_{1},

using
{|}e^{q_{n}u(a)}{|}=r_{\theta}(a)^{q_{n}}
.
Applying
\frac{1}{q_{n}}\log
to both sides of the above inequality, we obtain

{\log}r_{\theta}(a_{n})+\frac{1}{q_{n}}\log|b_{n}(a_{n})|+\log r_{1}+\frac{\mathrm{Re}\,k_{n,a_{n}}(y_{n})}{q_{n}}\leq\frac{1}{q_{n}}\log\frac{\epsilon_{n}}{r_{1}}

and hence

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{Re}\,k_{n,a_{n}}(y_{n})}{q_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{q_{n}}\log\frac{\epsilon_{n}}{r_{1}}-\log r_{1}-\frac{1}{q_{n}}\log|b_{n}(a_{n})|-\log r_{\theta}(a_{n}).

Using
\epsilon_{n}\leq C(r_{1})/q_{n}
, we have
\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{q_{n}}\log\frac{\epsilon_{n}}{r_{1}}\leq 0

and by Theorem

Therefore, there exists

such that for all

Then, by our choice of

, for all

,

\frac{1}{q_{n}}\mathrm{Re}\,k_{n,a_{n}}(y_{n})\leq-\log r_{1}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\leq\epsilon.

On the other hand,
k_{n,a}(0)=0
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
a\in{\Lambda}
.
Combining with the above argument, we conclude that for all
n\geq N
,

0\leq\sup_{a\in{\Lambda}}\sup_{|z|\leq r_{0}}\frac{\mathrm{Re}\,k_{n,a}(z)}{q_{n}}\leq\frac{1}{q_{n}}\mathrm{Re}\,k_{n,a_{n}}(y_{n})\leq\epsilon

The first inequality implies the second inequality, as a general property.
Fix arbitrary
r_{0}
,
r_{1}\in(r_{0},1)
and
\epsilon>0
.
By the first inequality, there is
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for all
n\geq N
all
a\in\Lambda
and all
|z|<r_{1}
,

\mathrm{Re}\,k_{n,a}(z)/(\epsilon q_{n})<1
. Thus,

maps the disk
\mathbb{D}(0,r_{1})
into the left
half-plane
\mathrm{Re}\,w<1
, with
k_{n,a}(0)/(\epsilon q_{n})=0

Post-composing the map

with a Möbius transformation and applying the Schwarz lemma,
we conclude that there is a constant

but not on

,
\epsilon
and
a

such that on the disk
\mathbb{D}(0,r_{0})
,

.
As
\epsilon
was arbitrary, this implies the second inequality. ∎

Remark 3.4
.

By the seminal work of Yoccoz
[
Yoc95
]
, the Brjuno condition is sharp (necessary and sufficient)
for the linearisability of a quadratic polynomial with an irrationally indifferent fixed point.
That optimality remains open for cubic polynomials in general.
However, it follows from a general result Perez-Marco that if
f_{e^{2i\pi\theta,a_{0}}}
is a Siegel polynomial of capture type,
for some
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}
and
a_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}
, then
\theta
must be a Brjuno number.
That is because if
f_{e^{2i\pi\theta,a}}
is of capture type, there is an open set
U\subset{\mathbb{C}}
containing
a_{0}
such that for all
a\in U
,

f_{e^{2i\pi\theta,a}}
has a Siegel disk.
But by
[
PM01
]
, if
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}
is non-Brjuno, then

\{a\in{\mathbb{C}}:f_{e^{2i\pi\theta,a}}\text{ has a Siegel disk }\}

must be a polar set, which is impossible here.

The same result of Perez-Marco
[
PM01
]
implies that if
\theta
is not Brjuno but
f_{e^{2i\pi\theta},a_{0}}
has a Siegel disk containing
0
,
then
a_{0}
is in the bifurcation locus of the slice
\{f_{e^{2i\pi\theta,a}},a\in{\mathbb{C}}\}
.
Then by
[
MSS83
]
or
[
Lyu83
]
, arbitrarily close to
a_{0}
, there must be some
a_{1}\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that
f_{e^{2i\pi\theta,a_{1}}}
has a neutral cycle
which is non-persistent in the family
\{f_{e^{2i\pi\theta,a}},a\in{\mathbb{C}}\}
.
In particular,
f_{e^{2i\pi\theta},a_{1}}
has 2 neutral cycles, therefore by
[
BE09
]
it is in the support of
\mu_{\mathrm{bif}}
.
This proves that if there exists cubic polynomial maps with Siegel disks with non-Brjuno rotation numbers (which are conjectured not to exist),
then they must also be in
\mu_{\mathrm{bif}}
.

We have now completed the ingredients we need to prove Theorem
1.1
for rotation numbers of bounded type.
In order to generalise it to all Brjuno numbers, we shall use the following result due to Avila, Buff and Chéritat.

Theorem 3.5

(
[
ABC04
]
, Theorem 3, p. 11)
.

Let
\theta
be a Brjuno number, and let
\Phi
denote the Brjuno-Yoccoz function. Let
\theta_{n}\to\theta
, where
\theta_{n}
are Brjuno numbers and

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\Phi(\theta_{n})\to\Phi(\theta)+C

for some constant
C\geq 0
.
Let
f_{n}(z)=e^{2\pi i\theta_{n}}z+O(z^{2})
, for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, be a sequence of holomorphic maps on
\mathbb{D}
such that

f_{n}\to R_{\theta}
(the rigid rotation of angle
\theta
) locally uniformly on
\mathbb{D}
.
Then

\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\log r(\Delta_{f_{n}})\geq e^{-C}.

In the above statement,
r(\Delta_{f_{n}})
denotes the conformal radius of the Siegel disk
\Delta_{f_{n}}
of
f_{n}
centred at
0
.

We now present the proof of the main result.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
a_{0}
be an arbitrary parameter in
U
, which is not the centre of
U
.
We consider two cases, based on whether
a_{0}
lies in a non-degenerate parabolic locus or not.

Case i) The parameter
a_{0}
does not belong to a non-degenerate parabolic locus.

Let us first show that if
b_{n}(c)=0
for some
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
c\in{\mathbb{C}}
, then
(\lambda_{n},c)\in\mathrm{supp}\,\mu_{\mathrm{bif}}
.
Indeed, by definition of
b_{n}
, the map
f_{\lambda_{n},c}
has a parabolic fixed point of parabolic multiplicity 2 at the origin.
The immediate parabolic basin is therefore comprised of
2q_{n}
Fatou components.
On the other hand, when
b_{n}(a)\neq 0
, the parabolic multiplicity is
1
and there are only
q_{n}
Fatou components in the immediate parabolic basin of
0
.
Therefore, the map
f_{\lambda_{n},c}
cannot be
J
-stable in the slice family
\{f_{\lambda_{n},a}:a\in{\mathbb{C}}\}
.
The classical results of
[
MSS83
]
and
[
Lyu83
]
imply the existence of
a_{n}^{\prime}
arbitrarily close to
c
such that

f_{\lambda_{n},a_{n}^{\prime}}
has a neutral cycle which is non-persistent in the slice
\{f_{\lambda_{n},a}:a\in{\mathbb{C}}\}
.
Therefore
f_{\lambda_{n},a_{n}^{\prime}}
has exactly 2 neutral cycles, including the parabolic fixed point at the origin.
By
[
BE09
]
,
(\lambda_{n},a_{n}^{\prime})\in\mathrm{supp}\,\mu_{\mathrm{bif}}
, and since we may choose
a_{n}^{\prime}
arbitrarily close to
c
, so is
(\lambda_{n},c)
.

By definition, if
a_{0}
does not belong to any non-degenerate parabolic locus, there is a sequence of parameters
(a_{k})_{k\geq 1}
in
U

such that
a_{k}\to a_{0}
,
b_{n_{k}}(a_{k})=0
for some
n_{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and
\lim_{k\to\infty}n_{k}=\infty
.
By the above paragraph, each
(\lambda_{n_{k}},a_{k})\in\mathrm{supp}\,\mu_{\mathrm{bif}}
, and hence
(\lambda,a)\in\mathrm{supp}\,\mu_{\mathrm{bif}}
.

Case ii) The parameter
a_{0}
belongs to a non-degenerate parabolic locus, say
\Omega
.

Let us first assume that
\theta
is bounded type. Fix an arbitrary
\epsilon>0
.
We will find some
a_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\|(\lambda_{n},a_{n})-(\lambda,a_{0})\|\leq\epsilon
and
(\lambda_{n},a_{n})\in\mathrm{supp}\,\mu_{\mathrm{bif}}
.

Applying Proposition
3.3
with
V:=\mathbb{D}(a_{0},\epsilon/2)
, we conclude that there exists
n

such that
|\lambda_{n}-\lambda|<\epsilon/2
and
a_{n}\in\mathbb{D}(a_{0},\epsilon/2)
such that
f_{\lambda_{n},a_{n}}
has a neutral cycle besides the fixed point
0
.
By
[
BE09
, Main Theorem]
,
(\lambda_{n},a_{n})\in\mathrm{supp}\,\mu_{\mathrm{bif}}
, and by construction
\|(\lambda_{n},a_{n})-(\lambda,a_{0})\|\leq\epsilon
.
Therefore
a_{0}\in\mathrm{supp}\,\mu_{\mathrm{bif}}
.

Now let
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}
be Brjuno number and
a\in{\mathbb{C}}
be such that
f_{e^{2i\pi\theta},a}
is of capture type
(we do not assume anymore that
\theta
has bounded type).
Let
[a_{0}:a_{1}:\ldots]
denote the entries of its continued fraction expansion.
For all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
\theta_{n}
be the unique irrational number whose continued fraction expansion has entries

[a_{0}:\ldots a_{n}:1:1\ldots].

We have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Phi(\theta_{n})=\Phi(\theta)

where
\Phi
is the Brjuno-Yoccoz function.
Let
\phi:\Delta_{\theta}(a)\to\mathbb{D}
denote the linearising coordinate of the Siegel disk of
f_{e^{2i\pi\theta},a}
, normalised to map

\Delta_{\theta}(a)
to the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
.
Let
h_{n}(z):=\phi\circ f_{e^{2i\pi\theta_{n}},a}\circ\phi^{-1}(z)
.

The sequence
h_{n}
satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
3.5
with
C=0
.
It follows that for every compact
K\subset\Delta_{\theta}(a)
, there exists
n_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
,
K\subset\Delta_{\theta_{n}}(a)
.
By assumption, there exists
k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{*}
such that
f_{e^{2i\pi\theta},a}^{\circ k}(c_{a})\in\Delta_{\theta}(a)
, where
c_{a}
is a critical point of

f_{e^{2i\pi\theta},a}
; by taking
K:=\overline{\mathbb{D}}(c_{a},\delta)
for
\delta>0
small enough that
K\subset\Delta_{\theta}(a)
,
we deduce that for all
n
large enough,
f_{e^{2i\pi\theta_{n}},a}^{\circ k}(c_{a})\in\Delta_{\theta_{n}}(a)
.
In other words, for all
n
large enough,
f_{e^{2i\pi\theta_{n}},a}
is a Siegel polynomial of capture type, with bounded type rotation number.
By the above,
(e^{2i\pi\theta_{n}},a)\in\mathrm{supp}\,\mu_{\mathrm{bif}}
, and since
e^{2i\pi\theta_{n}}\to e^{2i\pi\theta}
,
(e^{2i\pi\theta},a)
must be in
\mathrm{supp}\,\mu_{\mathrm{bif}}
as well.
∎

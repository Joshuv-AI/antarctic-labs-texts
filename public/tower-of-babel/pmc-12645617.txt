Dietary Intervention for Systemic Lupus Erythematosus patients based on HLA-DQ2 and DQ8 genotyping (DISH): a protocol for a randomised controlled study
Authors: Alvina Widhani, Wahyu Ika Wardhani, Selvi Nafisa Shahab, Mentari Kasih, Rahmat Cahyanur, Evy Yunihastuti, Ardhi Rahman Ahani
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionSLE is a chronic autoimmune disease that is influenced by both genetic and environmental factors, one of which is diet. Dietary intervention plays a role in the management of SLE, and precision nutrition based on genetic data may enhance its effectiveness. The human leukocyte antigen (HLA)-DQ2 and DQ8 genotypes are strongly associated with coeliac disease (CD), and emerging evidence suggests a possible link between SLE and CD. However, no published studies have explored the use of HLA-DQ2 and DQ8 genotyping to guide dietary intervention in patients with SLE. This study aims to investigate the impact of dietary intervention, tailored to HLA-DQ2 and DQ8 genotyping, on disease activity and quality of life of patients with SLE.Methods and analysis90 patients with SLE with positive HLA-DQ2 or DQ8 genotyping will be randomised in a 1:1 ratio to either the intervention group or a control group. Participants in the intervention group will receive intervention in the form of a low-gluten dietary modification for 12 weeks. Participants in both groups will continue their usual SLE medications. During three research visits (weeks 0, 4 and 12), all participants will complete a clinical interview, blood sample collection, dietary assessment and a questionnaire. Disease activity and quality of life will be assessed by the Mexican Systemic Lupus Erythematosus Disease Activity Index score and Lupus Quality of Life questionnaire. Other secondary outcomes that will also be assessed are fatigue score, sleep quality, anxiety and depression, and physical activity.Ethics and disseminationThis study has obtained ethical approval from the Ethics Committee of the Faculty of Medicine, University of Indonesia–Cipto Mangunkusumo Hospital (KET948/UN2F1/ETIK/PPM0002/2025). Findings will be published in peer-reviewed journals and submitted for intellectual property rights.Trial registration numberNCT07183007.Keywords: Lupus Erythematosus, Systemic; Polymorphism, Genetic; Health-Related Quality Of Life

WHAT THIS STUDY ADDS

The Dietary Intervention for Systemic Lupus Erythematosus patients based on human leukocyte antigen (HLA)-DQ2 and DQ8 genotyping is a randomised controlled trial that can examine the effects of dietary intervention conducted according to HLA-DQ2 and DQ8 genotypes on the disease activity and quality of life of patients with SLE. Adult patients with SLE who are HLA-DQ2 or DQ8 positive will be randomised to a control group or the intervention group. The intervention group will receive a low-gluten dietary modification intervention for 12 weeks. Disease activity and quality of life will be assessed at weeks 0, 4 and 12.

Introduction

SLE is a chronic autoimmune disease characterised by autoantibodies against nuclear antigens, immune complex deposition and inflammation.1 2 There are approximately 5 million patients with lupus worldwide.1 Development and progression of SLE are influenced by both genetic and environmental factors, including ultraviolet radiation, cigarette smoke, vitamin D deficiency, hormonal factors, diet and gut microbiota.1 Diet influences the composition of gut bacteria, mucosal barrier integrity and systemic inflammation, which in turn affect autoimmune conditions.3

The goals of SLE treatment are to prevent relapse and organ damage, improve chances of survival and enhance quality of life (QoL). A comprehensive management of SLE should also include dietary interventions as it is known that modifications in diet can improve the health and QoL of patients with SLE.1 4

Diet plays a role in epigenetic changes through DNA methylation, which may affect gene expression, disease course and progression.4 Diet also has an effect on the dysregulation of the immune system and chronic inflammation.1 Some patients have benefited from dietary interventions and lifestyle modifications as a part of their SLE management, in addition to pharmacological therapy.2 5 6 The Mediterranean diet, omega-3 supplementation and vitamin D3 supplementation have been shown to be able to reduce disease activity in patients with SLE.4 5 Synbiotic supplementation has also shown the ability to decrease systemic inflammation and disease activity.6 However, to date, no dietary intervention has consistently proven effective, nor are there any evidence-based recommendations for personalised dietary management in SLE.1 4

Precision nutrition, a part of precision medicine, is a potential approach in SLE management for the optimisation of the benefits of dietary intervention through the consideration of genetic, epigenetic and environmental factors.4 Nutrigenomic testing can provide insight into individual responses to dietary intervention.7 For example, human leukocyte antigen (HLA)-DQ2/DQ8 genotypes are strongly associated with coeliac disease (CD). Gluten, a protein present in wheat, can trigger CD in genetically predisposed individuals.8 In patients with SLE, the prevalence of CD is higher compared with the general population.9 However, because it is often asymptomatic, CD is frequently underdiagnosed.10

A gluten-free diet has been shown to improve disease course in CD and its associated autoimmune conditions. However, evidence of effectivity in non-coeliac autoimmune diseases is still limited. Some studies suggest that a low-gluten diet can serve as an adjunctive therapy for other autoimmune conditions.11 In patients with Hashimoto’s thyroiditis, a low-gluten diet has been shown to improve thyroid function and decrease inflammation.12 While the mechanism by which a gluten-free diet benefits non-coeliac autoimmune diseases remains unclear, it is thought to offer potential benefits.13

Dietary interventions based on HLA-DQ2/DQ8 genotyping which are related to gluten sensitivity are, thus, expected to improve disease activity and the QoL of patients with SLE. However, no studies to date have evaluated dietary intervention based on HLA-DQ2 and DQ8 genotyping in patients with SLE.6

Aims

Primary: To investigate the effect of a 12-week dietary intervention based on HLA-DQ2 and DQ8 genotypes on SLE disease activity and QoL.

Secondary: To examine whether a 12-week dietary intervention based on HLA-DQ2 and DQ8 genotypes will affect fatigue score, sleep quality, anxiety and depression, and physical activity.

Methods and analysis

Trial design and randomisation

This experimental study will be a single-blind randomised clinical trial. Patients with SLE with positive HLA-DQ2 and/or DQ8 genes will be randomised into an intervention group, which will receive dietary intervention, or a control group, which will not receive any intervention. A computer-generated, blocked allocation sequence will be used to assign participants in a 1:1 ratio to these two groups. To minimise bias, the study assistants assessing the primary outcomes will remain uninformed of the group allocation until the statistical analysis is complete. The intervention group will complete a 12-week low-gluten diet programme, while the control group will continue their usual diet. Both groups will partake in three research visits (at weeks 0, 4 and 12) during which the participants will undergo a clinical interview, a physical examination and blood sample collection to assess disease activity. During these visits, participants will also complete the same set of questionnaires each time to track changes over time. Setting and recruitment

Study participants will be recruited using consecutive sampling from the outpatient clinic of Dr Cipto Mangunkusumo General Hospital, Jakarta, Indonesia, from September 2025 until the minimal sample size is met.

Sample size

The required sample size was calculated for primary outcomes, QoL and disease activity using the formula for comparing two independent means (intervention vs control), with a significance level (Zα) of 1.96 and a statistical power of 80%. In the absence of previous similar studies, the sample size estimation was based on clinical judgement. A difference of 3 points in the Mexican Systemic Lupus Erythematosus Disease Activity Index (MEX-SLEDAI) score and 10 points in the Lupus Quality of Life (LupusQoL) score was considered clinically meaningful. The SDs were assumed to be 4 for MEX-SLEDAI and 15 for LupusQoL. The minimum size obtained was 36 participants per group, giving a total of 72 participants. Factoring in a potential 20% drop-out rate, the minimum sample size required was determined to be 90 participants.

Participant selection

Patients will be considered eligible if they meet the following inclusion criteria: (1) diagnosed with SLE according to the 2019 European Alliance of Associations for Rheumatology/American College of Rheumatology criteria and registered as a patient at Dr Cipto Mangunkusumo Hospital; (2) above 18 years of age; and (3) has HLA-DQ2 and/or DQ8 positive. Exclusion criteria include: (1) presence of active infection; (2) refusal to participate in the study; (3) pregnancy; (4) breastfeeding; (5) already on a specific autoimmune-related diet; (6) existence of a severe medical condition (active cancer, stage V chronic kidney disease or acute heart failure); and (7) taking corticosteroids equivalent to more than 20 mg/day prednisone.

Intervention and comparator

Dietary education and counselling on low-gluten diets from a clinical nutritionist will be given to participants in the intervention group. This is to be delivered via a mobile-messaging platform (eg, WhatsApp or SMS). Education on the low-gluten diets will involve instructions on which foods to consume and which to avoid throughout the intervention period. These participants will also receive weekly reminders of the low-gluten diets. Meanwhile, participants in the control group will be instructed to maintain their usual diet. Participants in both groups will continue their usual standard-of-care pharmacological therapy for the duration of the study. Maintenance therapy may include medications such as corticosteroids and other immunosuppressants. Any adjustments to a participant’s medication regimen during the study period will be carefully documented. No considerable adverse effects are expected to result from this dietary modification intervention.

Measure of outcomes

Feasibility and acceptability assessment

Potential participants will first be screened to confirm they meet all study eligibility criteria. Subsequently, written informed consent will be obtained from all eligible individuals prior to initiation of any study-related procedures. Study participants will undergo a series of assessments including a structured interview, a physical examination and blood sample collection at every research visit (weeks 0, 4 and 12). Participants will be withdrawn from the study at any point if they meet any of the following criteria: (1) a change to a different immunosuppressant is required, or the corticosteroid dose is increased to more than 20 mg/day (prednisone equivalent); (2) pregnancy is detected during the study period; (3) consent to participate is withdrawn; and (4) hospitalisation occurs during the intervention period.

Blood sampling

At each screening, blood samples will be collected to determine the presence of HLA-DQ2 and/or HLA-DQ8 alleles. Eligibility for the study will be limited to participants with confirmed positivity for one or both of the specified HLA alleles. Genotyping for the HLA-DQ2 and HLA-DQ8 will be performed via real-time PCR method following the protocol developed by Mashayekhi et al.14 First, venous blood will be collected from each participant in EDTA tubes. DNA will be extracted from the peripheral blood leucocytes using a commercial extraction kit according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The concentration of the extracted DNA will then be quantified using a Qubit high-sensitivity DNA assay. For each reaction, 1 µL of sample DNA was used. Each sample will undergo three separate PCR reactions to detect the HLA-DQ2.2, HLA-DQ2.5 and HLA-DQ8 alleles. The BM2 gene will be amplified in parallel as an internal control. In addition, a negative control (nuclease-free water) will be included in each PCR run to monitor for reagent contamination or on specific amplification. Blood samples will also be collected to measure SLE disease activity at weeks 0, 4 and 12.

Participant questionnaires

Participants will complete a battery of validated self-report questionnaires (online supplemental file), including the LupusQoL, Fatigue Severity Scale (FSS), International Physical Activity Questionnaire, Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI) and Hospital Anxiety Depression Scale (HADS).

QoL will be measured using the LupusQoL, a disease-specific, 34-item instrument that assesses eight domains: physical health, pain, planning, intimate relationships, emotional health, burden to others, body image and fatigue. The validated Indonesian language version of this questionnaire will be used for this study.15

Fatigue is measured using the FSS, a nine-item self-report questionnaire that evaluates the extent to which fatigue interferes with daily activities and its perceived severity. Each item is rated on a 7-point Likert scale, where 1 indicates ‘strongly disagree’ and 7 indicates ‘strongly agree’.” The total score ranges from 9 to 63, with higher scores reflecting greater fatigue severity. The validated Indonesian version is used in this study.16

Physical activity is assessed using the short form of the International Physical Activity Questionnaire. This instrument captures information on time spent walking, engaging in moderate and vigorous-intensity activities and sedentary behaviour over the past 7 days. The data are used to estimate total physical activity in metabolic equivalent (MET)-minutes per week, as well as the duration of time spent sitting.17

Sleep quality is assessed using PSQI, which distinguishes between ‘poor’ and ‘good’ sleep based on seven domains: subjective sleep quality, sleep latency, sleep duration, habitual sleep efficiency, sleep disturbances, use of sleep medication and daytime dysfunction over the past month. Each item is scored on a 0–3 Likert scale, with a score of 3 indicating the most severe level of difficulty. A global score of 5 or greater is indicative of poor sleep quality. The validated Indonesian version is used in this study.18

Anxiety and depression levels are assessed using HADS, a 14-item questionnaire comprising two subscales: one for anxiety (HADS-A) and one for depression (HADS-D), each containing seven items. Respondents rate their feelings over the past week using a 4-point Likert scale: 0=Not at all; 1=Occasionally; 2=Often; 3=Most of the time. Each subscale yields a score ranging from 0 to 21. Score interpretation is as follows: 0–7=Normal; 8–10=Mild symptoms; 11–21=Moderate to severe symptoms. The validated Indonesian version is used in this study.19

MEX-SLEDAI is used to assess SLE disease activity, with a minimum score of 0 and a maximum score of 32. Scores were interpreted as follows: 0–1, remission; 2–5, mild disease activity; 6–9, moderate disease activity; 10–13, severe disease activity; and ≥14, very severe disease activity.20

Anthropometric assessment

Anthropometric measurements, which include height, weight and waist-hip circumferences, will also be taken. These data will be used to calculate each participant’s body mass index (BMI) and waist-to-hip ratio. Height and weight will be measured using a GEA Stature Meter and a GEA Electronic Personal Scale, respectively, according to the manufacturer’s instructions. BMI will then be calculated, and the participants will be classified based on WHO BMI criteria.21

Waist and hip circumferences will be measured with a non-stretchable measuring tape according to the standardised procedure outlined by WHO. Waist circumference will be measured at the midpoint between the iliac crest and the last palpable rib, at a level parallel to the floor. Hip circumference will be measured at the widest point of the buttocks, at a level parallel to the floor.22 The waist-to-hip ratio will be calculated for each participant to assess their risk of metabolic complications. Patients will be classified as having a substantially increased risk based on WHO cut-off points.22 All measurements will be conducted by the same researcher.

Dietary assessment

Dietary assessments will be conducted by nutritionists using 24-hour food recall and food record to estimate average daily energy intake, a modified Food Frequency Questionnaire (FFQ) to estimate average daily gluten intake and Dietary Inflammatory Index. All dietary assessments will be conducted at each of the three visits, with week 0 serving as the baseline.

During visits, a nutritionist will conduct the 24-hour food recall covering the participant’s intake on the previous day. An additional 2 days’ food record will be reported by each participant on one subsequent weekday and one weekend. Participants will be instructed to submit documentation on all consumed items in real time via WhatsApp chats on agreed days. Data from these 3 days of intake will then be analysed and averaged to estimate the participants’ daily energy and nutrient intake.23 24 The modified FFQ will be used to assess the intake of specific foods and food groups over the past month.25 This instrument will be adapted to include common local foods, particularly those with significant gluten content. Standard portion sizes will then be used to quantify the average daily consumption of key nutrients, with a primary focus on gluten. The gluten value for each food item has been sourced from existing literature, including published data from ELISA gluten analyses.26,28 For food items where published data are unavailable, gluten values have been estimated based on the amount of wheat flour in standard recipes.26

Adherence to the low-gluten diet for participants in the intervention group will be formally evaluated at weeks 4 and 12 using the Biagi score, which categorises compliance as poor (0–1 points), moderate (2 points) or good (3–4 points).29 Participants in the control group, who will be instructed to maintain their usual diet, will also receive dietary assessments in parallel with the intervention group.

Study monitoring and data analysis

Study monitoring

All participants will receive their usual standard of care alongside study procedures. Regular communication will be established between the study team and all participants to ensure patient safety and to address any concerns. Any adverse events will be recorded. The study will be monitored by the Ethics Committee of the Faculty of Medicine, University of Indonesia–Cipto Mangunkusumo Hospital. Decisions regarding early termination of the trial—due to safety concerns, lack of efficacy or other ethical considerations—will be made collectively by the principal investigators in consultation with the institutional ethics committee. Access to interim results will be restricted to the analysis team. Any protocol modification will be informed to the Ethics Committee of the Faculty of Medicine, University of Indonesia–Cipto Mangunkusumo Hospital.

Data analysis plan

Intention-to-treat analysis will include all eligible subjects meeting the inclusion criteria who underwent treatment allocation. The per-protocol analysis includes subjects from the intention-to-treat population and excludes patients with major protocol violations. Statistical analysis will be performed using SPSS V.20.0 (IBM). The threshold for statistical significance will be set at p<0.05. To assess for normality, the Shapiro-Wilk test will be performed. An independent samples t-test will be used to compare numerical variables (disease activity, QoL, fatigue and physical activity) between the intervention and control groups. If the assumption of normality is violated, the Mann-Whitney U test will be used. A paired samples t-test will be used to compare preintervention and postintervention data within each group. Should the normality assumption be violated, the Wilcoxon signed-rank test will be used. Categorical variables (sleep quality and anxiety and depression score) will be analysed using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate.

Ethics and dissemination

Permission was received to conduct the study at the Outpatient Clinic of the Department of Internal Medicine, Cipto Mangunkusumo Hospital. This study has also been preregistered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT07183007). Participants will be required to sign a written informed consent prior to inclusion in the study. The risk of adverse events occurring as a result of the dietary modification intervention is minimal. Regardless, adverse events and corresponding treatments will be systematically logged, monitored and reviewed by the study team. The findings of this study will be published in peer-reviewed journals and submitted for intellectual property rights.

Discussion

While some clinical research has examined dietary interventions in patients with SLE, none have focused on personalised interventions based on genetic data.56 30,32 This study will be the first to use HLA-DQ2 and DQ8 genetic data as a basis for dietary intervention in patients with SLE. Furthermore, this study will evaluate the effects of a low-gluten diet on the disease activity and QoL of patients with SLE. A target of 90 individuals with positive HLA-DQ2.2, DQ2.5 or DQ8 genotypes will be enrolled as participants in this study, who will then be randomised to either the intervention group or a control group in equal quantities.

In the general population, the prevalence of HLA-DQ2/DQ8 ranges from 30% to 40%.33 Among patients with liver disease, organic upper gastrointestinal diseases or functional upper gastrointestinal diseases, the prevalence may reach 45.7%, 46.3% and 53.4%, respectively.34 In patients with CD, HLA-DQ2 is present in approximately 90% of cases, while the remaining 10% are HLA-DQ8 positive.35 Although the meta-analysis showed that the prevalence of biopsy-verified CD among patients with SLE is similar to that in the general population, it may be underdiagnosed, as some CD cases can be asymptomatic and the symptoms may overlap with those of SLE.36 Unfortunately, epidemiological data on HLA-DQ2 and DQ8 in Indonesia are currently unavailable. We plan to screen 100 patients with SLE for HLA-DQ2 and DQ8, aiming to identify 90 individuals who test positive for either allele. These subjects will then be randomised into either an intervention or a control group.

Data from screening, questionnaire responses and assessments from week 0 will serve as a baseline prior to dietary intervention. Individuals who refuse to adhere to the dietary modification will be withdrawn from the study given the reliance on continuous dietary assessment. This study is expected to demonstrate how genetic-based, low-gluten dietary intervention, as a part of a healthy lifestyle strategy for SLE management, may impact disease activity and patients’ QoL. Findings from this study are also expected to serve as a foundation for future studies with larger sample sizes, ultimately leading to a recommendation for implementing genetic-based dietary interventions in the clinical management of SLE.

The limitation of this study is its open-label design, which should be considered when interpreting the results. However, to minimise bias in the assessment of disease activity, the examiner will remain blinded to the patients’ randomisation status.

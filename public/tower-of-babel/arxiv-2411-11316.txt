On the Density of naturals n n coprime to ⌊ P ⁡ ( n ) ⌋ \lfloor P(n)\rfloor for certain Classes of Polynomials
Aahan Chatterjee

Abstract
We obtain asymptotic bounds on the number of natural numbers less than X X satisfying gcd ⁡ ( n , ⌊ P ⁡ ( n ) ⌋ ) = 1 \gcd{\left(n,\lfloor P(n)\rfloor\right)}=1 , under some diophantine conditions on the coefficient of x x in P P , and show that the density of such naturals is exactly 1 ζ ⁡ ( 2 ) \dfrac{1}{\zeta{\left(2\right)}} .



1
Introduction

If
m
and
n
are two natural numbers chosen at random, the probability that they are coprime is known to be
\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}
. This result extends beyond independent integers and can hold when
m
and
n
are functionally related. For instance, Watson proved in
[
2
]
that if
\alpha
is irrational, the density of natural numbers
n
satisfying
\gcd(n,\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor)=1
is precisely
\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}
. Similarly, Lambek and Moser demonstrated in
[
3
]
that if
f(1),f(2),\dots
is a non-decreasing sequence of non-negative integers that grows slowly to infinity, and if the intervals over which
f(m)=n
grow slowly with
n
, then the probability that
f(n)
is relatively prime to
n
is also
\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}
.

In this paper, we extend Watson’s result to a broader class of functional relationships. Roughly speaking, we show that if
P
is a polynomial with real coefficients and the coefficient of
x
in
P
is not too well-approximable, then the probability that
n
and
\lfloor P(n)\rfloor
are coprime is exactly
\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}
.

Main Result

More specifically:

Theorem
.

Let
P\in\mathbb{R}[x]
be a polynomial such that the coefficient of
x
in
P
is
non-Liouville
(we shall define this in
Definition
1
). Then we have that:

\begin{split}S(X)&=\dfrac{X}{\zeta{\left(2\right)}}+O\left(\dfrac{X}{\log{\log{X}}^{1/\penalty 3}}\right)\quad\text{and hence}\quad\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{S(X)}{X}=\frac{1}{\zeta(2)}\\
\text{where}\quad S(X)&=\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\{x\leq X\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\gcd{\left(x,\lfloor P(x)\rfloor\right)}=1\}.\end{split}

Heuristic

For a prime
p
, the probability that
p\mid\gcd{\left(n,\lfloor P(n)\rfloor\right)}
is equal to
\frac{1}{p}
times the probability that
\left\{\frac{P(pn)}{p}\right\}<\frac{1}{p}
, which is
\frac{1}{p^{2}}
. Therefore, the probability that
p
does not divide
\gcd{\left(n,\lfloor P(n)\rfloor\right)}
is
1-\frac{1}{p^{2}}
.

Heuristically, we expect that the density of natural numbers
n

satisfying
\gcd{\left(n,\lfloor P(n)\rfloor\right)}=1
is

\prod_{p\;\text{prime}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right).

This product is well-known to evaluate to
\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}
, consistent with earlier results for simpler cases. This motivates us to proceed with sieving to establish the result rigorously. However, precise bounds require good control over the error terms, which arise when applying Weyl’s equidistribution theorem. To manage these errors effectively, we impose certain Diophantine conditions on the coefficient of
x
in
P
.

2
Diophantine Approximation

Definition 1
.

Let
\omega\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
B>0
. We say that a number
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
is Liouville if we have that
\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N},\exists
a pair of integers
(p,q)
with
q>1
such that

\left|\alpha-\dfrac{p}{q}\right|\leq q^{-n}.

4
Sieving

4.1
Controlling Error Terms

We will now apply the bounds on Weyl sums derived in the previous section along with the Erdos–Turan Discrepancy Theorem to estimate the error terms in the sieving process.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let us define:

\mathcal{A}_{d}(X)=\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\{x\leq X\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}d\mid gcd(x,\lfloor P(x)\rfloor\}.

Then
\exists
constants
\mu,C,C_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{A}_{d}(X)-\dfrac{X}{d^{2}}\right|\leq C_{0}\left(\dfrac{X}{d}\right)^{1-\mu}

Proof.

By
Theorem
3.1
, we know that
\exists\rho,\tau>0
such that for large enough
X
and for all
m<X^{\rho}
, we have that
s_{m}(X)\leq X^{1-\tau}
. Thus we can find a positive constant
C
such that whenever
\frac{X}{d}>C
, we have that

s_{m}\left(X/\penalty d\right)\leq\left(\dfrac{X}{d}\right)^{1-\tau}\quad\text{for all}\quad m<\left(\dfrac{X}{d}\right)^{\rho}.

Now by Erdos-Turan-Discrepancy Theorem, we know that:

\displaystyle\left|\left|\mathcal{A}_{d}(X)\right|-\frac{X}{d^{2}}\right|\leq C_{0}\left(\dfrac{X}{dT}+\displaystyle\sum_{m\leq T}\dfrac{\left|s_{m}\left(X/\penalty d\right)\right|}{m}\right).

Finally choosing
T=\left(\dfrac{X}{d}\right)^{\rho}
and
\mu\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}={\rm max}\{1-\rho,1-\tau/\penalty 2\}
, we obtain the desired bound.
∎

4.2
Doing The Sieving

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
P\in\mathbb{R}[x]
be a polynomial such that the coefficient of
x
in
P
is
non-Liouville
. Then we have that:

\begin{split}S(X)&=\dfrac{X}{\zeta{\left(2\right)}}+O\left(\dfrac{X}{(\log{\log{X}})^{1/\penalty 3}}\right)\quad\text{and hence}\quad\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{S(X)}{X}=\frac{1}{\zeta(2)}\\
\text{where}\quad S(X)&=\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\#\{x\leq X\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\gcd{\left(x,\lfloor P(x)\rfloor\right)}=1\}.\end{split}

Proof.

Let us define

S(X,z)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\#\{x\leq X\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\gcd{\left(\gcd{\left(x,\lfloor P(x)\rfloor\right)},P_{z}\right)}=1\}.

Let
\epsilon,A>0
be arbitrary. Then we notice that

\begin{split}S(X)-S(X,z)&\leq\displaystyle\sum_{(d,P_{z})=1;\,d\leq X}|\mathcal{A}_{d}|\\
\quad&=\displaystyle\sum_{(d,P_{z})=1;\,d\leq X/\penalty\log^{\epsilon}{z}}|\mathcal{A}_{d}|+\displaystyle\sum_{(d,P_{z})=1;\,d\geq X/\penalty\log^{\epsilon}{z}}|\mathcal{A}_{d}|\end{split}

Now we shall estimate the the sums on the RHS seperately.

\begin{split}\displaystyle\sum_{(d,P_{z})=1;\,d\leq X/\penalty\log^{\epsilon}{z}}|\mathcal{A}_{d}|&\leq\displaystyle\sum_{(d,P_{z})=1;\,d\leq X/\penalty\log^{\epsilon}{z}}\dfrac{X}{d^{2}}+O\left(\left(\dfrac{X}{d}\right)^{1-\mu}\right)\\
\quad&\leq\displaystyle\sum_{d>z}\dfrac{X}{d^{2}}+O\left(\dfrac{X}{\log^{\epsilon\mu}{z}}\right)\\
\quad&=O\left(\dfrac{X}{\log^{\epsilon\mu}{z}}\right)\end{split}

Furthermore, we also have

\begin{split}\displaystyle\sum_{(d,P_{z})=1;\,d\geq X/\penalty\log^{\epsilon}{z}}|\mathcal{A}_{d}|&\leq{\log^{\epsilon}{z}}\displaystyle\sum_{(d,P_{z})=1;\,d\geq X/\penalty\log^{\epsilon}{z}}1\\
\quad&=\log^{\epsilon}{z}\left(X\left(\displaystyle\prod_{p<z}\left(1-\dfrac{1}{p}\right)+O\left(\dfrac{1}{\log^{A}{X}}\right)\right)\right)\\
(\text{for}\quad z\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}&=X^{c/\penalty\log{log{X}}}\quad\text{where}\quad c=1/\penalty(2(A+1)))\\
\quad&\leq\dfrac{X}{\log^{1-\epsilon}{z}}+O\left(\dfrac{X}{\log^{A}{X}}\right)\end{split}

Thus in conclusion, we have that

\begin{split}S(X)-S(X,z)&=O\left(\dfrac{X}{\log^{1-\epsilon}{z}}\right)+O\left(\dfrac{X}{\log^{\epsilon\mu}{z}}\right)+O\left(\dfrac{X}{\log^{A}{X}}\right)\\
\text{where}\quad z&=X^{c/\penalty\log{\log{X}}}.\end{split}

So now it just remains for us to estimate
S(X,z)
.
For this, we observe that

\begin{split}S(X,z)&=\sum_{d\mid P_{z};\,d<X}\mu(d)|\mathcal{A}_{d}|\\
\quad&=X\left(\displaystyle\prod_{p<z}\left(1-\dfrac{1}{p^{2}}\right)+O\left(\dfrac{1}{X}\right)\right)+O\left(\displaystyle\sum_{d<X/\penalty\log^{\epsilon}{z}}\left(\dfrac{X}{d}\right)^{1-\mu}\right)\\
\quad&+\displaystyle\sum_{d\mid P_{z};\,X/\penalty\log^{\epsilon}{z}<d\leq X}|\mathcal{A}_{d}|\end{split}

(2)

Now if
d\mid P_{z}
with
w(d)<\log{\log{d}}+(\log{\log{d}})^{2/\penalty 3}
and
d>\dfrac{X}{\log{z}}
,  then we can find a prime factor of
d
of size atleast
M=d^{1/\penalty{2\log{\log{d}}}}
.
We claim
M>z
. To see this, we note that

\begin{split}\log{M}&=\dfrac{\log{d}}{2\log{\log{d}}}\\
\quad&\geq\dfrac{\log{X}}{\log{\log{X}}}\left(\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{c}{2\log{\log{X}}}\right)\\
\quad&>\dfrac{\log{X}}{2(A+1)\log{\log{X}}}>\log{z}\quad(\text{for large enough}\quad X)\end{split}

Thus
M>z.
But then this means that
d
has a prime factor
>z
and thus
d\not|P_{z}
, a contradiction.
Therefore the contribution of the last sum in
\left(\ref{f}\right)
is atmost

\begin{split}\#\{n<X\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}|\omega(n)&-\log{\log{n}}|>{(\log{\log{n})}}^{2/\penalty 3}\}<\dfrac{X}{(\log{\log{X})^{1\slash 3}}}\\
\quad&\,(\text{Hardy-Ramanujan Theorem})\end{split}

So in conclusion we have that

S(X,z)=\dfrac{X}{\zeta{\left(2\right)}}+O\left(\dfrac{X}{\log^{\epsilon\mu}{z}}\right)+O\left(\dfrac{X}{(\log{\log{X})}^{1/\penalty 3}}\right)

Combining this with the estimate for
S(X)-S(X,z)
we obtained earlier gives us

S(X)=\dfrac{X}{\zeta{\left(2\right)}}+O\left(\dfrac{X}{(\log{\log{X})}^{1/\penalty 3}}\right)\quad\text{as desired.}

∎

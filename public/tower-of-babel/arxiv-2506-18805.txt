Contact loci of semihomogeneous singularities
Eduardo de Lorenzo Poza, Jiahui Huang

Abstract
. Two of the main open problems in the theory of contact loci are the arc-Floer conjecture, which states that the compactly supported cohomology of the restricted m m -contact locus and the fixed-point Floer cohomology of the m m -th iterate of the Milnor monodromy are isomorphic up to a shift; and the embedded Nash problem, which asks for a description of the irreducible components of the unrestricted m m -contact locus in terms of an embedded resolution of singularities. In this paper we study the geometry of contact loci of semihomogeneous singularities, and use our results to give an affirmative answer to the arc-Floer conjecture (under some conditions on the dimension and the degree) and a complete solution to the embedded Nash problem.



1.
Introduction

Given an integer
m\geq 1
, the
m
-contact locus
\mathcal{X}_{m}
associated to a hypersurface singularity is the subscheme of the jet space formed by jets that have order of contact
m
with the singularity.
Contact loci were first defined by Denef and Loeser
[
DL02
]
in the context of motivic integration, where they appeared as the coefficients of the motivic zeta function, see
[
CNS18
, §7.4]
.

While the geometry of contact loci is interesting in its own right because the spaces
\mathcal{X}_{m}
may be seen as invariants of the singularity, its study is also related to a number of open problems in singularity theory, such as the monodromy conjecture, the arc-Floer conjecture, and the embedded Nash problem.
For example, contact loci of plane curves are well understood —their connected components are smooth and the topology of each component is completely described
[
BL23
, Thm. 3.5]
—, and this is a key ingredient in the proof of the arc-Floer conjecture for plane curves
[
BL23
, Thm. 1.1]
and the resolution of the embedded Nash problem for plane curves
[
Bud+24
, Thm. 1.22]
.

In this paper, we study the geometry of contact loci associated to semihomogeneous singularities, see Definition
2.3
.
Our main result in this direction is a description of the graded pieces of the filtration induced by the order of the jets, see Proposition
3.1
.
Even though each graded piece is relatively simple to describe, the relationship between the different pieces is quite intricate.
In contrast to the case of plane curves, we show that contact loci of semihomogeneous singularities may have several irreducible components and the irreducible components could be singular, see Example
5.6
.
Nevertheless, we also find that the cohomology of each contact locus is the direct sum of the cohomologies of its irreducible components.

As corollaries to our study of the geometry of contact loci, we show that the arc-Floer conjecture holds for semihomogeneous singularities under some conditions on the degree, and we give a complete solution to the embedded Nash problem for this kind of singularities.
Let us now give a brief overview of both problems and the techniques that we use in their resolution.

1.1.
The arc-Floer conjecture

The
arc-Floer conjecture
is a conjectural relationship between two invariants of an isolated hypersurface singularity: one of symplectic origin —Floer cohomology of Milnor monodromy iterates—, and one of algebraic origin —compactly supported cohomology of the contact loci.
Its origin goes back to an observation by Seidel about a result by Denef and Loeser that we will now recall.

Let
f:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\rightarrow(\mathbb{C},0)
be a holomorphic function germ with an isolated singularity at the origin.
A classical result by Milnor (see
[
Sea19
, §4]
for a complete list of references) says that for small
\varepsilon,\delta>0
, the restriction
f|:f^{-1}(\partial\mathbb{D}_{\delta})\cap\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{\varepsilon}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{D}_{\delta}
is a locally trivial fibration, whose monodromy (well defined up to isotopy) we call the
Milnor monodromy
and denote by
\phi
.
On the other hand, Denef and Loeser considered the
(restricted) contact loci

\mathcal{X}_{m}
of
f
, which are defined for each integer
m\geq 1
as the subscheme of the
m
-th jet scheme
\mathcal{L}_{m}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
formed by jets through the origin that have contact order
m
with
f
.

The aforementioned result of Denef and Loeser
[
DL02
, Thm. 1.1]
states that the compactly supported Euler characteristic of the
m
-contact locus
\chi_{c}(\mathcal{X}_{m})
is equal to the Lefschetz number of the
m
-th iterate of the monodromy
\Lambda(\phi^{m})
.
Seidel observed that this Lefschetz number is also an Euler characteristic, namely the Euler characteristic of the Floer cohomology groups
\HF^{\bullet}(\phi^{m},+)
.
The arc-Floer conjecture predicts that the numerical equality
\chi_{c}(\mathcal{X}_{m})=\Lambda(\phi^{m})
actually comes from an isomorphism between the underlying the cohomology groups
[
B+22
, Conj. 1.5]
, see (
AFC
).

Aside from Denef and Loeser’s result, there is further evidence in favor of the arc-Floer conjecture.
First, there are two spectral sequences with isomorphic first pages (up to a shift) that converge to either of the two cohomology groups.
They were introduced by McLean
[
McL16
, Thm. 1.2]
and Budur, Fernández de Bobadilla, Lê and Nguyen
[
B+22
, Thm. 1.1]
, respectively.
Furthermore, it is known that the isomorphism predicted by the arc-Floer conjecture exists when
m
is the multiplicity of
f

[
B+22
, Prop. 1.6]
.
Moreover, the conjecture has been shown to hold for all
m
in the case of plane curves by de la Bodega and the first author
[
BL23
, Thm. 1.1]
.

Here we show that the arc-Floer conjecture holds for semihomogeneous singularities of degree
d\geq 2
in
n\geq 3
variables, under some conditions on the degree (see Theorem
4.2
for the proof and the sharpest conditions we are able to get, also Figure
4.1
).

Theorem A
.

Let
f:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\rightarrow(\mathbb{C},0)
be a semihomogeneous germ of degree
d
in
n\geq 3
variables. If
2\leq d<n/2
or
d>2n-2
, then (
AFC
) holds.

The proof of this theorem involves a careful study of the spectral sequences by McLean and Budur et al.
Since the relationship between the
E_{1}
pages of both spectral sequences is already known, we need to study the differentials.
The two spectral sequences depend on a choice of embedded resolution of singularities.
By considering a particularly simple
m
-separating resolution, we show in Theorem
2.11
and Theorem
3.6
that both spectral sequences degenerate at the first page (in general for the cohomology of contact loci, and under some conditions on
d
and
n
in the case of the McLean spectral sequence).
Note that this
E_{1}
-degeneration is somewhat unexpected because of our results on the geometry of the contact locus — we show that the irreducible components of
\mathcal{X}_{m}
are not disjoint, but
E_{1}
-degeneration means that
H^{\bullet}_{c}(\mathcal{X}_{m})
is a direct sum of the cohomologies of the irreducible components.

1.2.
The embedded Nash problem

The
embedded Nash problem
, first proposed in
[
ELM04
, Rmk. 2.8]
, consists in determining the irreducible components of
\mathcal{X}_{m}
in terms of an (
m
-separating) resolution of singularities, see (
ENP
) for the precise statement.
It inherits its name from the classical Nash problem, which asks for an analogous description of the irreducible components of the subset of arcs that pass through the singular locus.

The embedded Nash problem has been solved for several families of singularities, such as
toric invariant ideals given by a cone inside an affine toric variety
[
Ish04
, Thm. 5.11]
,
hyperplane arrangements
[
BT22
, §7]
,
[
Bud+24
, §4]
,
plane curves
[
Bud+24
, §6, §7]
,
[
BL23
, Thm. 3.5]
,
and curves in an arbitrary surface
[
Bod25
, Thm. 3.4]
.

As a corollary to our study of the geometry of contact loci, we give a complete solution to the embedded Nash problem for semihomogeneous singularities of degree
d
in arbitrary dimension
n\geq 2
.
In fact, we not only describe the divisorial
m
-valuations that correspond to irreducible components of the
m
-contact locus, but also the dlt and essential
m
-valuations, see Definition
5.2
. These are the divisorial valuations that appear in
[
Bud+24
, Thm. 1.13]
, an analog of de Fernex and Docampo’s result for the Nash problem
[
FD16
, Thm. 1.1]
.

Theorem B
.

Let
f:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\rightarrow(\mathbb{C},0)
be an semihomogeneous germ of degree
d
in
n\geq 2
variables.
Then we have the following solution to (
ENP
):

(i)

If
d<n
, then the triple
(\mathbb{C}^{n},f^{-1}(0),0)
has no dlt
m
-valuations; it has no contact valuations if
m<d
, and exactly one contact
m
-valuation if
m\geq d
; and it has
\lfloor m/d\rfloor
essential
m
-valuations.

(ii)

If
d\geq n
, then the dlt, contact and essential
m
-valuations of the triple
(\mathbb{C}^{n},f^{-1}(0),0)
coincide, and there are exactly
\lfloor m/d\rfloor
of them.

Proof.

See Proposition
5.4
, Proposition
5.11
and Corollary
5.13
.
∎

2.
Floer cohomology of monodromy iterates

Let
f:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\rightarrow(\mathbb{C},0)
be a holomorphic function germ with an isolated critical point at
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
The goal of this section is to study the fixed-point Floer cohomology groups of monodromy iterates
\HF^{\bullet}(\phi^{m},+)
in the case where
f
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
d
.

2.1.
Preliminaries

A
log resolution
of a hypersurface singularity germ
f:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\rightarrow(\mathbb{C},0)
is a proper birational morphism
\mu:(X,(f\circ\mu)^{-1}(0))\rightarrow(\mathbb{C}^{n},f^{-1}(0))
from a smooth variety
X
such that the total transform
(f\circ\mu)^{-1}(0)
is a divisor with simple normal crossings.
We denote by
\mathcal{E}
the set of irreducible components of the total transform, so
(f\circ\mu)^{-1}(0)=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}}N_{E}E
, where each number
N_{E}\coloneqq\operatorname{ord}_{E}f
is called the
multiplicity
of
f\circ\mu
along the irreducible divisor
E
.
We denote by
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathcal{E}
the subset of irreducible components of the strict transform of
(f\circ\mu)^{-1}(0)
under
\mu
.
We also define the
log discrepancy
of each
E\in\mathcal{E}
by
\nu_{E}\coloneqq\operatorname{ord}_{E}(K_{X/\mathbb{C}^{n}})+1
.

Given an integer
m\geq 1
, the divisors

such that

divides

are called
m
-divisors
. If, whenever

and

, we have

, then the resolution

is said to be
m
-separating
.
It is always possible to perform further blow-ups on a given log resolution to make it

-separating
[

, Lemma 2.9]
.

For every divisor

we denote

2.2.
An

\mathcal{N}_{Z/E_{(\kappa^{\prime},r^{\prime})}}\cong\mathscr{O}_{S}(\kappa^{\prime}+r^{\prime}d)\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{N}_{Z/E_{(\kappa^{\prime\prime},r^{\prime\prime})}}\cong\mathscr{O}_{S}(-\kappa^{\prime\prime}-r^{\prime\prime}d).

We prove (
5
) by induction on the number of blow-ups. The base case is
(\kappa^{\prime},r^{\prime})=(0,1)
and
(\kappa^{\prime\prime},r^{\prime\prime})=(1,0)
.
Note that the tangent cone of
f^{-1}(0)
is given by the vanishing of
h
, so it is the cone over the smooth projective hypersurface
S
. Therefore, after blowing up the origin, the normal bundle of the exceptional divisor inside the strict transform is
\mathcal{N}_{Z/E_{(1,0)}}\cong\mathscr{O}_{S}(-1)
.
On the other hand,
E_{(0,1)}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
, and under this isomorphism the inclusion
Z\hookrightarrow E_{(0,1)}
is mapped to the inclusion
S\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
.
Hence its normal bundle is
\mathcal{N}_{Z/E_{(0,1)}}\cong\mathscr{O}_{S}(d)
.
This finishes the proof of the base case.

Now suppose that (
5
) holds.
Recall that the exceptional divisor
E_{(\kappa,r)}
of the blow-up

\mu^{(i+1)}:X^{(i+1)}=\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X^{(i)}\rightarrow X^{(i)}

is isomorphic to the projectivization of the normal bundle, i.e
E_{(\kappa,r)}\cong\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{N}_{Z/X^{(i)}})
, see
[
Vak25
, 22.3.D.(a)]
,
[
Ful98
, B.6.3]
.
The normal bundle
\mathcal{N}_{Z/X^{(i)}}
has two canonical rank-one subbundles, namely
\mathcal{N}_{Z/E_{(\kappa^{\prime},r^{\prime})}}\oplus 0
and
0\oplus\mathcal{N}_{Z/E_{(\kappa^{\prime\prime},r^{\prime\prime})}}
.
By definition of projectivization of a vector bundle, these give two sections of
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{N}_{Z/X^{(i)}})\rightarrow Z
.
The images of these sections are sent under the isomorphism
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{N}_{Z/X^{(i)}})\cong E_{(\kappa,r)}
to the intersections
E_{(\kappa,r)}\cap E_{(\kappa^{\prime},r^{\prime})}
and
E_{(\kappa,r)}\cap E_{(\kappa^{\prime\prime},r^{\prime\prime})}
respectively.

Recall that tensoring by a line bundle does not affect the projectivization
[
Har77
, Lemma II.7.9]
, so tensoring with
\mathscr{O}_{S}(-\kappa^{\prime}-r^{\prime}d)
and recalling that
(\kappa,r)=(\kappa^{\prime},r^{\prime})+(\kappa^{\prime\prime},r^{\prime\prime})
, we get

\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{O}_{S}(\kappa^{\prime}+r^{\prime}d)\oplus\mathscr{O}_{S}(-\kappa^{\prime\prime}-r^{\prime\prime}d))\cong\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{O}_{S}\oplus\mathscr{O}_{S}(-\kappa-rd)).

The subspace
0\oplus\mathscr{O}_{S}(-\kappa^{\prime\prime}-r^{\prime\prime}d)
is sent by the tensor product to
0\oplus\mathscr{O}_{S}(-\kappa-rd)
, which is the hyperplane at infinity of the projective completion
\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{O}_{S}\oplus\mathscr{O}_{S}(-\kappa-rd))
of
\mathscr{O}_{S}(-\kappa-rd)
, see
[
Ful98
, B.5.2]
. Removing this hyperplane at infinity we are left with the line bundle
\mathscr{O}_{S}(-\kappa-rd)
, whose zero section now corresponds to the intersection of
E_{(\kappa,r)}
with
E_{(\kappa^{\prime},r^{\prime})}
. In conclusion, the normal bundle of the intersection
E_{(\kappa^{\prime},r^{\prime})}\cap E_{(\kappa,r)}
in
E_{(\kappa,r)}
is

\mathcal{N}_{E_{(\kappa^{\prime},r^{\prime})}\cap E_{(\kappa,r)}/E_{(\kappa,r)}}=\mathscr{O}_{S}(-\kappa-rd).

Analogously, we find

\mathcal{N}_{E_{(\kappa^{\prime\prime},r^{\prime\prime})}\cap E_{(\kappa,r)}/E_{(\kappa,r)}}=\mathscr{O}_{S}(\kappa+rd).

This concludes the induction, proving (
5
) and finishing the proof of (ii).
∎

Now that we have understood the topology of the divisors
E_{(\kappa,r)}
, we move on to the study of the Denef-Loeser coverings
c_{(\kappa,r)}:\widetilde{E}_{(\kappa,r)}^{\circ}\rightarrow E_{(\kappa,r)}^{\circ}
.
The proof of the following lemma is standard, but we include it for completeness.

Lemma 2.6
.

For
(\kappa,r)\neq(0,1),(1,0)
, the space
\widetilde{E}_{(\kappa,r)}^{\circ}
is path connected.

Proof.

Write
E=E_{(\kappa,r)}
and let
E^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}
be such that
E\cap E^{\prime}\neq\varnothing
.
Then either
E
has appeared during the resolution as the exceptional divisor of the blow-up of the intersection of
E^{\prime}
with another divisor, or vice versa.
Denoting by
N,N^{\prime}
the multiplicities of
E,E^{\prime}
respectively, a straightforward induction shows that
\gcd(N,N^{\prime})=\gcd(N_{(1,0)},N_{(0,1)})=\gcd(1,d)=1
.

Consider an open affine
U\subset X
around a point in
E\cap E^{\prime}
such that
f\circ\mu=uv^{N}(v^{\prime})^{N^{\prime}}
, with
u\in\mathscr{O}^{\times}(U)
and
v,v^{\prime}\in\mathscr{O}(U)
, which exists because the total transform
(f\circ\mu)^{-1}(0)
has normal crossings.
Note that
U\setminus E^{\prime}
is an affine open containing
E^{\circ}\cap U
. Furthermore,
v^{\prime}\in\mathscr{O}^{\times}(U\setminus E^{\prime})
and hence
u(v^{\prime})^{N^{\prime}}\in\mathscr{O}^{\times}(U\setminus E^{\prime})
.
By construction of the covering
c_{E}
we have a description of
c_{E}^{-1}(E^{\circ}\cap U)
as a pullback (
1
), namely

c_{E}^{-1}(E^{\circ}\cap U)\cong\{(z,x)\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times(E^{\circ}\cap U)\mid u(x)v^{\prime}(x)^{N^{\prime}}=z^{N}\}.

Consider a loop
x(t)
in
E^{\circ}\cap U
such that
v^{\prime}(x(t))=e^{2\pi\imath t}
.
Note that
u(x(t))
is a loop in
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
which does
not
wind around the origin (because
u
does not vanish along
E^{\prime}
).
Therefore we may lift
u(x(t))
through the covering
\mathbb{C}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}:z\mapstochar\rightarrow z^{N}
, obtaining a
loop

\hat{u}(t)
in
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
such that
\hat{u}(0)=\hat{u}(1)
and
\hat{u}(t)^{N}=u(x(t))
.
Define

z(t)\coloneqq\hat{u}(t)e^{2\pi\imath tN^{\prime}/N}.

Then,

z(t)^{N}=\hat{u}(t)^{N}e^{2\pi\imath tN^{\prime}}=u(x(t))v^{\prime}(x(t))^{N^{\prime}},

which shows that
(z(t),x(t))
is a loop in
c_{E}^{-1}(E^{\circ}\cap U)
lifting
x(t)
.
Furthermore, as
t
varies through the integers,
z(t)
runs through all
N
-th roots of
u(x(0))
because
N
and
N^{\prime}
are coprime.
In other words, the path
(z(t),x(t))
passes through all of the
N
preimages of
u(x(0))
under the covering
c_{E}
, which shows that
c_{E}^{-1}(E^{\circ}\cap U)
is path connected.
Since any point in
\widetilde{E}^{\circ}
may be connected to a point in
c_{E}^{-1}(E^{\circ}\cap U)
by a path, we conclude that
\widetilde{E}^{\circ}
is path connected.
∎

Now that we have described the topology of the Denef-Loeser coverings, we can compute their homology groups.
For the next results, we need to recall the structure of the cohomology ring of the smooth degree
d
hypersurface
S
, see
[

]
. We have

H^{k}(S)\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}\langle h^{k/2}\rangle&\text{if }k<n-2\text{ and $k$ is even},\\
\mathbb{Z}^{b}&\text{if }k=n-2,\\
\mathbb{Z}\langle\tfrac{1}{d}h^{k/2}\rangle&\text{if }k>n-2\text{ and $k$ is even},\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where
h\in H^{2}(S)
is the hyperplane class coming from the embedding in
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
, and
\tfrac{1}{d}h^{k/2}
is just notation for an integral class
\beta\in H^{k}(S)
such that
d\beta=h^{k/2}
. The rank of the middle cohomology group is given by

(6)

b=(-1)^{n-1}2\left\lceil\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rceil+\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}d^{n-1-k}.

From here it is immediate that the Lefschetz map
H^{k}(S)\xrightarrow{\smile h}H^{k+2}(S)
is an isomorphism for

.
Furthermore, if
n
is odd then the maps
H^{n-4}(S)=0\xrightarrow{\smile h}H^{n-2}(S)
and
H^{n-2}(S)\xrightarrow{\smile h}H^{n}(S)=0
are zero, and the map
H^{n-3}(S)\cong\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{\smile h}H^{n-1}(S)\cong\mathbb{Z}
is multiplication by
d
.
On the other hand, if
n
is even then
H^{n-3}(S)=0\xrightarrow{\smile h}H^{n-1}(S)=0
is zero, the map
H^{n-4}(S)\cong\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{\smile h}H^{n-2}(S)
is injective (because the composition
H^{n-4}(S)\xrightarrow{\smile h^{2}}H^{n}(S)
is multiplication by

and hence injective), and
H^{n-2}(S)\xrightarrow{\smile h}H^{n}(S)\cong\mathbb{Z}
is surjective by the following lemma.

Lemma 2.8
.

The Lefschetz map
H^{n-2}(S)\xrightarrow{\smile h}H^{n}(S)
is surjective.

Proof.

The following argument is due to
[
Saw25
]
. If
n
is odd then
H^{n}(S)=0
and there is nothing to prove, so let us assume that
n
is even. Since any two smooth hypersurfaces of degree
d
are diffeomorphic, and the induced isomorphism in cohomology respects the cup product and the hyperplane class, we may assume that
S
is the Fermat hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
, i.e. the zero locus of
\sum_{i=1}^{n/2}(x_{i}^{d}-y_{i}^{d})
. Then
S
contains a linear subspace

of dimension
\tfrac{n}{2}-1
, namely the one defined by the ideal
(x_{1}-y_{1},\ldots,x_{n/2}-y_{n/2})
.

Let
\alpha\in H^{n-2}(S)
be the Poincaré dual of
[L]\in H_{n-2}(S)
. Then,
\alpha\smile h^{(n-2)/2}
is the Poincaré dual of the intersection of
L
with
(n-2)/2
generic hyperplanes, which is a point, and therefore it generates
H^{2n-4}(S)
. Since
S
has degree

, we have

. This implies that
\alpha\cup h
is a generator of

, which finishes the proof.
∎

, Prop. II.7.10]
,
H^{\prime}
is ample if
a
is large enough. Furthermore, for large enough
a
we also have

\frac{ab_{E}}{N_{E}}<\frac{-1+a(b_{E}+b_{F})}{N_{E}+N_{F}}<\frac{ab_{F}}{N_{F}},\text{ i.e. }\frac{b^{\prime}_{E}}{N_{E}}<\frac{b^{\prime}_{E^{\prime}}}{N_{E^{\prime}}}<\frac{b^{\prime}_{F}}{N_{F}},

as we wanted. This concludes the induction and the proof.
∎

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
n\geq 3,d\geq 1,m\geq 1
be integers. Suppose that

(7)

2k(d-n)+1\notin\{\pm 1,\pm(n-1),\pm(n-2),\pm(2n-3)\}\quad\text{for all }k\in[1,m/d)\cap\mathbb{Z}.

Then the McLean spectral sequence (
2
) associated to the minimal
m
-separating log resolution of a semihomogeneous singularity of degree
d
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
degenerates at the first page. In particular, if
2\leq d<n/2
or
d>2n-2
then the spectral sequence degenerates for all
m\geq 1
.

Proof.

By (
4
), the
m
-divisors in the resolution
\mu:X\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}
are in one-to-one correspondence with coprime pairs
(\kappa,r)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{2}
such that
\kappa+rd
divides
m
.
In turn, these are in one-to-one correspondence with (not necessarily coprime) pairs
(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{2}
such that
a+bd=m
(the bijection is given by
(a,b)\mapstochar\rightarrow(\kappa,r)=(a,b)/\gcd(a,b)
).
The pairs satisfying this latter condition are precisely

(8)

\{(a,b)=(m+id,-i)\mid i\in[-m/d,0]\cap\mathbb{Z}\}.

Let
E_{-\lfloor m/d\rfloor},\ldots,E_{-1},E_{0}
be the corresponding
m
-divisors, so that
E_{i}=E_{(m+id,-i)/\gcd}
.
Note that
E_{0}
is the strict transform
E_{(1,0)}
, and whenever
d
divides
m
we have
E_{-m/d}=E_{(0,1)}
.
For a cleaner notation, denote by
N_{i},\nu_{i},b_{i}
the constants
N_{E_{i}},\nu_{E_{i}},b_{E_{i}}
associated to
E_{i}
.
The indexing of the
E_{i}
has been chosen so that
i<j
if and only if
E_{i}
is closer to
E_{(0,1)}
than
E_{j}
, and hence
b_{i}/N_{i}<b_{j}/N_{j}
by Lemma
2.10
.
Since we know the coprime pair associated to each divisor
E_{i}
, we can compute explicitly using (
4
) that

(9)

2m\left(\frac{\nu_{i}}{N_{i}}-1\right)=2m\left(\frac{(m+id)-in}{(m+id)-id}-1\right)=2i(d-n).

Let
p_{i}=mb_{i}/N_{i}
, so that the homology of
\widetilde{E}_{i}^{\circ}
appears in the column
p_{i}
of the first page of the McLean spectral sequence (
2
).
Let
\ell\geq 1
be an integer, and suppose that we have already shown that all the differentials of the McLean spectral sequence are zero up to the
\ell
-th page, so
E_{\ell}^{p,q}\cong E_{1}^{p,q}
for all
p,q
.
Suppose that there is a nonzero differential on the page
E_{\ell}
, which necessarily goes from a column
p_{i}
to a column
p_{j}
with
i<j
, say
0\neq d_{\ell}^{p_{i},p_{j}}:E_{\ell}^{p_{i},q_{i}}\rightarrow E_{\ell}^{p_{j},q_{j}}
.
By definition of the differentials, we have

(10)

p_{j}=p_{i}+\ell\qquad\text{and}\qquad q_{j}=q_{i}-\ell+1.

Using Theorem
2.1
and (
9
), we have

E_{\ell}^{p_{i},q_{i}}\cong E_{1}^{p_{i},q_{i}}=H_{n-1-(p_{i}+q_{i})-2i(d-n)}(\widetilde{E}_{i}^{\circ}),

and analogously for
E_{\ell}^{p_{j},q_{j}}
. Since we are assuming that the differential is nonzero, both the domain and codomain must be nonzero.
By Corollary
2.9
, this implies
-\lfloor m/d\rfloor\leq i<j\leq-1
and

\begin{cases}n-1-(p_{i}+q_{i})-2i(d-n)\in\{0,n-2,n-1,2n-3\},\\
n-1-(p_{j}+q_{j})-2j(d-n)\in\{0,n-2,n-1,2n-3\}.\end{cases}

Note that this condition covers both cases
(\kappa,r)=(0,1)
and
(\kappa,r)\neq(0,1)
. Subtracting both terms and using (
10
), we get

2(j-i)(d-n)+1\in\{0,\pm 1,\pm(n-1),\pm(n-2),\pm(2n-3)\}.

To finish the proof, put
k=j-i
and observe that
1\leq k\leq\lfloor m/d\rfloor-1
and that
2k(d-n)+1
is an odd integer, and therefore is never zero.

Now suppose
d>2n-2
. Then for
1\leq k\leq\lfloor m/d\rfloor-1
we have

2k(d-n)+1>2\cdot 1\cdot(2n-2-n)+1=2n-3,

and therefore condition (
7
) for having zero differentials is always satisfied.
Similarly, if
d<n/2
then

2k(d-n)+1<2k(-n/2)+1=-kn+1.

Hence for
k\geq 2
this is strictly smaller than
-2n+3
and condition (
7
) is satisfied. If
k=1
then
2k(d-n)+1<-n+1
, so the only option for (
7
) to not hold is
2(d-n)+1=-2n+3
, which implies
d=1
.
∎

3.
Cohomology of contact loci

3.1.
Preliminaries

Let
m
be a positive integer.
Recall that an
m
-jet
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a morphism

\gamma:\allowbreak\Spec\mathbb{C}[t]/(t^{m+1})\rightarrow\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}=\Spec\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}].

The morphism
\gamma
is uniquely determined by the images of the variables
x_{i}
under the associated ring map
\gamma^{\sharp}:\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[t]/(t^{m+1})
.
We denote those images by

\gamma^{i}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\gamma^{i}_{j}t^{j}\coloneqq\gamma^{\sharp}(x_{i})\in\mathbb{C}[t]/(t^{m+1}),\quad\text{ where }\gamma^{i}_{j}\in\mathbb{C}.

We group the coefficients of each power of
t
in a vector
\gamma_{j}\coloneqq(\gamma_{j}^{1},\ldots,\gamma_{j}^{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and identify the
m
-jet
\gamma
with the corresponding truncated power series with coefficients in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, i.e.
\gamma(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\gamma_{j}t^{j}
.
Similarly, an
arc
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a morphism
\gamma:\allowbreak\Spec\mathbb{C}\llbracket t\rrbracket\rightarrow\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}=\Spec\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}].

Recall that there is a
\mathbb{C}
-scheme
\mathcal{L}_{m}\coloneqq\mathcal{L}_{m}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\coloneqq\Spec\mathbb{C}[\{\gamma^{i}_{j}\}^{i=1,\ldots,n}_{j=0,\ldots,m}]
, called the
m
-jet space
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, whose closed points are precisely the
m
-jets in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, see
[
ELM04
]
,
[
Ish07
]
.
For
m^{\prime}<m
there is a natural truncation map
\pi^{m}_{m^{\prime}}:\mathcal{L}_{m}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{m^{\prime}}
.
The inverse limit exists, and we denote it by
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}\coloneqq\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\coloneqq\varprojlim_{m}\mathcal{L}_{m}
and call it the
arc space
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
By
[
Bha16
, Thm. 1.1]
, the closed points of
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}
are in bijection with the arcs in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

For any polynomial
f\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, the
restricted
m
-contact locus
associated to
f
is

\mathcal{X}_{m}\coloneqq\mathcal{X}_{m}(f,0)\coloneqq\{\gamma\in\mathcal{L}_{m}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\mid\gamma(0)=0,f(\gamma(t))\equiv t^{m}\ (\text{mod }t^{m+1})\}.

Note that the contact loci are equipped with the analytic topology coming from the inclusion
\mathcal{X}_{m}\subset\mathcal{L}_{m}\cong\mathbb{C}^{n(m+1)}
.
Whenever we refer to the cohomology of
\mathcal{X}_{m}
(e.g. in the statement of the arc-Floer conjecture) we are referring to the space equipped with the analytic topology.
Unless otherwise stated, all cohomology groups should be understood to have integral coefficients.

3.2.
Filtering by the order

We are interested in the case where
f\in\mathbb{C}\llbracket x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\rrbracket
is a semihomogeneous power series of degree
d
, i.e.
f=h+F
with
h
homogeneous of degree
d
and having
0
as an isolated critical point and
F
of order strictly greater than
d
, see Definition
2.3
.
The initial term
h
defines a smooth hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
which we denote by
S
, just as we did in Section
2
.
We consider the
increasing
filtration
F_{\bullet}\mathcal{L}_{m}
on the jet space by the
negative
order of the jets, i.e.

(11)

F_{p}\mathcal{L}_{m}\coloneqq\{\gamma\in\mathcal{L}_{m}\mid\operatorname{ord}_{t}(\gamma)\geq-p\},

where
\operatorname{ord}_{t}(\gamma)\coloneqq\min\{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\mid\gamma_{i}\neq 0\}
. The “graded pieces” of this filtration are

F_{(p)}\mathcal{L}_{m}\coloneqq F_{p}\mathcal{L}_{m}\setminus F_{p-1}\mathcal{L}_{m}=\{\gamma\in\mathcal{L}_{m}\mid\operatorname{ord}_{t}(\gamma)=-p\}.

Since the filtration
F_{\bullet}\mathcal{L}_{m}
is concentrated in negative degrees, we will denote
\rho\coloneqq-p
to simplify the notation, so that the jets in
F_{(p)}
are those of order
\rho
.
Consider the filtration
F_{\bullet}\mathcal{X}_{m}
on the contact locus induced by
F_{\bullet}\mathcal{L}_{m}
, whose graded pieces are
F_{(p)}\mathcal{X}_{m}=\mathcal{X}_{m}\cap F_{(p)}\mathcal{L}_{m}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The graded pieces
F_{(p)}\mathcal{X}_{m}
have the following description:

(i)

If
p\notin[-m/d,-1]\cap\mathbb{Z}
, then
F_{(p)}\mathcal{X}_{m}=\varnothing
. Otherwise, (ii)-(iv) hold.

(ii)

For
\gamma\in F_{(p)}\mathcal{X}_{m},\ p\in[-m/d,-1]\cap\mathbb{Z}
, the variables
\gamma_{0},\ldots,\gamma_{\rho-1}
are zero and the variables
\gamma_{m-(d-1)\rho+1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}
are not subject to any equation. Formally,

\pi^{m}_{\rho-1}(F_{(p)}\mathcal{X}_{m})=\{0\},\quad F_{(p)}\mathcal{X}_{m}=(\pi^{m}_{m-(d-1)\rho})^{-1}(\pi^{m}_{m-(d-1)\rho}(F_{(p)}\mathcal{X}_{m})).

(iii)

The variable
\gamma_{\rho}
is only constrained by one equation; namely
f(\gamma_{\rho})=0
if
p\in(-m/d,-1]
, and
f(\gamma_{\rho})=1
if
p=-m/d
. Formally,

\pi^{m}_{\rho}(F_{(p)}\mathcal{X}_{m})\cong\begin{cases}CS^{\circ}&\text{ if }-m/d<p\leq-1,\\
M_{h}&\text{ if $d$ divides $m$ and }p=-m/d,\end{cases}

where
CS^{\circ}=\{x\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\mid f(x)=0\}\setminus\{0\}
is the affine cone over the projective hypersurface
S
with the origin removed, and
M_{h}=\{x\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\mid h(x)=1\}
is the affine Milnor fiber of
h
.

(iv)

For
a\in[\rho+1,m-(d-1)\rho]\cap\mathbb{Z}
, the variable
\gamma_{a}
is restricted to a hyperplane that depends holomorphically in the lower-order variables and in fact this hyperplane is, after translating it to the origin, the tangent hyperplane of
\pi^{m}_{\rho}(F_{(p)}\mathcal{X}_{m})
at
\gamma_{\rho}
.
Formally, the restriction of the truncation map

\pi^{a}_{a-1}|:\pi^{m}_{a}(F_{(p)}\mathcal{X}_{m})\rightarrow\pi^{m}_{a-1}(F_{(p)}\mathcal{X}_{m})

is a complex affine bundle, whose model vector bundle is the pullback by
\pi^{a-1}_{\rho}
of the tangent bundle of
\pi^{m}_{\rho}(F_{(p)}\mathcal{X}_{m})
.

Proof.

Note that by definition there are no jets
\gamma\in\mathcal{L}_{m}
of negative degree.
If
\operatorname{ord}_{t}\gamma=0
then
\gamma(0)\neq 0
and hence
\gamma\notin\mathcal{X}_{m}
.
And if
\operatorname{ord}_{t}\gamma=\alpha>m/d
then
\gamma(t)=t^{\alpha}\delta(t)
for a jet
\delta
with
\delta(0)\neq 0
, thus

\operatorname{ord}_{t}f(\gamma(t))=\operatorname{ord}_{t}(t^{d\alpha}h(\delta(t))+F(t^{\alpha}\delta(t)))\geq\min\{d\alpha,\operatorname{ord}_{t}F(t^{\alpha}\delta(t))\}=d\alpha>m

by homogeneity of
h
, concluding that
\gamma\notin\mathcal{X}_{m}
. This proves (i).

Recall that the jets
\gamma\in F_{(p)}\mathcal{L}_{m}
are of the form
\gamma(t)=\gamma_{\rho}t^{\rho}+\cdots+\gamma_{m}t^{m}
with
\gamma_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
for
i\in\{\rho,\ldots,m\}
and
\gamma_{\rho}\neq 0
.
Consider the Taylor expansion for
f(\gamma(t))
around
\gamma_{\rho}t^{\rho}
:

f(\gamma(t))=\sum_{k=0}^{d}\dfrac{1}{k!}D^{k}f(\gamma_{\rho}t^{\rho})\cdot(\gamma_{\rho+1}t^{\rho+1}+\cdots+\gamma_{m}t^{m},\ldots,\gamma_{\rho+1}t^{\rho+1}+\cdots+\gamma_{m}t^{m}).

Using linearity of the derivative, the fact that
D^{k}h(x)\cdot(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})
is homogeneous of degree
d-k
in the variable
x
and linear in each variable
v_{i}
, and the fact that
\operatorname{ord}_{t}D^{k}F(\gamma_{\rho}t^{\rho})>(d-k)\rho
, we can group the coefficients of each power of
t
in the expression above.
Doing this, we obtain

\mathrm{Coef}(f(\gamma(t)),t^{d\rho})=h(\gamma_{\rho})

and for every
d\rho<\alpha\leq m
we get

(12)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Coef}(f(\gamma(t)),t^{\alpha})

\displaystyle=Dh(\gamma_{\rho})\cdot\gamma_{\alpha-(d-1)\rho}+\text{(terms involving only $\gamma_{\rho},\ldots,\gamma_{\alpha-(d-1)\rho-1}$).}

The defining equation of the contact locus, which is
f(\gamma(t))\equiv t^{m}(\mathrm{mod}\ t^{m+1})
, may therefore be split into
m-d\rho+1
non-trivial equations in the variables
\gamma_{k}
, namely

\mathrm{Coef}(f(\gamma(t)),t^{\alpha})=0\text{ for }d\rho\leq\alpha<m\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathrm{Coef}(f(\gamma(t)),t^{m})=1.

Observe that the variables
\gamma_{0},\ldots,\gamma_{\rho-1}
are zero by definition of
F_{(p)}\mathcal{X}_{m}
, and that the variables
\gamma_{m-(d-1)\rho+1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
do not appear in the equations. This proves (ii).

If
m=d\rho
, then the only non-trivial equation is
\mathrm{Coef}(f(\gamma(t)),t^{m})=1
, i.e.
h(\gamma_{\rho})=1
.
This equation defines the global Milnor fiber
M_{h}
, see
[
Dim92
, Exercise 3.1.13]
.
On the other hand, if
m>d\rho
then the first non-trivial equation is
\mathrm{Coef}(f(\gamma(t)),t^{d\rho})=0
, i.e.
h(\gamma_{\rho})=0
, which defines the affine cone over
S
.
Since
\gamma\in F_{(p)}\mathcal{X}_{m}
we must also have
\gamma_{\rho}\neq 0
, so putting both equations together we obtain
\gamma_{\rho}\in CS^{\circ}
. As we will see next, all the other equations have solutions for every
\rho\in CS^{\circ}
, and then we will have proved (iii).

Finally, we see from (
12
) that for every
a\in[\rho+1,m-(d-1)\rho]
the first equation in which
\gamma_{a}
appears is
\mathrm{Coef}(f(\gamma(t)),t^{a+(d-1)\rho})=0
or
1
.
This is an affine equation in
\gamma_{a}
whose coefficients depend holomorphically on
\gamma_{\rho},\ldots,\gamma_{a-1}
.
Furthermore, the linear form
Dh(\gamma_{\rho})
is nonzero because
\gamma_{\rho}\neq 0
is a smooth point of
h
, and the equation
Dh(\gamma_{\rho})=0
defines the tangent bundle of
\pi^{m}_{\rho}(F_{(p)}\mathcal{X}_{m})
. This proves (iv).
∎

3.3.
Comparing filtrations

We are going to prove that the filtration
F_{\bullet}\mathcal{X}_{m}
is equivalent to the filtration described in
[
B+22
, Lemma 2.3]
, for the resolution
\mu:X\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}
introduced in §
2.2
and an ample divisor
H
with the properties of Lemma
2.10
.
Let us start by reviewing the definition of this other filtration.

Recall that we are denoting by
\mathcal{E}
the set of irreducible components of the exceptional divisor
(f\circ\mu)^{-1}(0)
.
Recall also that given an
arc

\sigma\in\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
whose generic point is not contained in
0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, there exists a unique lift
\widetilde{\sigma}\in\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(X)
(i.e.
\sigma=\mu\circ\widetilde{\sigma}
) by the valuative criteria for properness and separatedness.
Finally, recall that, since
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is smooth, the truncation morphism
\pi_{m}:\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{m}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
is locally trivial.
For any
E\in\mathcal{E}
let
\mathcal{X}_{m,E}
be the set of
\gamma\in\mathcal{X}_{m}
such that there exists an arc
\sigma\in\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
with
\pi_{m}(\sigma)=\gamma
and such that
\widetilde{\sigma}(0)\in E
. Since the log resolution
\mu
is
m
-separating, we have a decomposition

\mathcal{X}_{m}=\bigsqcup_{E\in\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{X}_{m,E},

compare with
[
B+22
, Lemma 2.1]
. Fix an ample divisor
H=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}}b_{E}E
on
X
with the properties of Lemma
2.10
, and define an
increasing
filtration
G_{\bullet}\mathcal{X}_{m}
by

G_{p}\mathcal{X}_{m}\coloneqq\bigsqcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}E\in\mathcal{E},\ N_{E}\mid m\\
mb_{E}/N_{E}\leq p\end{subarray}}\mathcal{X}_{m,E}.

Proposition 3.2
.

The filtrations
F_{\bullet}\mathcal{X}_{m}
and
G_{\bullet}\mathcal{X}_{m}
are equivalent in the following sense:
there exists an increasing function
\iota:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
such that
F_{p}\mathcal{X}_{m}=G_{\iota(p)}\mathcal{X}_{m}
for all
p\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Recall from the proof of Theorem
2.11
that we may label the
m
-divisors in the resolution
\mu
(i.e. the
E\in\mathcal{E}
such that
N_{E}
divides
m
) by
E_{-\lfloor m/d\rfloor},\ldots,E_{-1},E_{0}
in such a way that
i<j
implies
b_{i}/N_{i}<b_{j}/N_{j}
.
Therefore the function
\iota(i)=mb_{i}/N_{i}
for
i\in[-m/d,0]\cap\mathbb{Z}
is increasing, and we may extend it arbitrarily to an increasing function
\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
.
All we need to show is that the graded pieces of
F_{\bullet}
and
G_{\bullet}
are equal, i.e.
F_{(p)}\mathcal{X}_{m}=G_{(\iota(p))}\mathcal{X}_{m}
for all
p\in\mathbb{Z}
.
This amounts to showing that an jet
\gamma\in\mathcal{X}_{m}
has order
\rho
if and only if there exists an arc
\sigma\in\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
such that
\pi_{m}(\sigma)=\gamma
and
\widetilde{\sigma}(0)\in E_{-\rho}
.

Recall that
\operatorname{ord}_{t}\gamma
is the interection multiplicity of
\gamma
with a general hyperplane
L\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
through the origin. Note that a hyperplane
L
meets the origin with multiplicity
1
. An easy induction then shows that

\mu^{*}L=\widetilde{L}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}E_{(\kappa,r)}\in\mathcal{E}\\
(\kappa,r)\neq(1,0)\end{subarray}}rE_{(\kappa,r)},

where
\widetilde{L}
is the strict transform of
L
, see §
2.2
for the notation
E_{(\kappa,r)}
.
Let
\sigma
be any arc such that
\pi_{m}(\sigma)=\gamma
and let
E
be the unique divisor such that
\widetilde{\sigma}(0)\in E
, whose associated coprime pair we denote by
(\kappa_{E},r_{E})
. Then

\operatorname{ord}_{t}\gamma=\gamma\cdot L=\sigma\cdot L=\widetilde{\sigma}\cdot(\mu^{*}L)=r_{E}(\widetilde{\sigma}\cdot E)

because for a general
L
we have
\widetilde{\sigma}\cdot\widetilde{L}=0
. On the other hand,

m=\gamma\cdot f^{-1}(0)=\sigma\cdot f^{-1}(0)=\widetilde{\sigma}\cdot(\mu^{*}(f^{-1}(0)))=N_{E}(\widetilde{\sigma}\cdot E).

In particular,
E
is an
m
-divisor, so there is an
i\in\{-\lfloor m/d\rfloor,\ldots,-1,0\}
such that
E=E_{i}
.
Recall from (
8
) that the coprime pair corresponding to the divisor
E_{i}
is
(m+id,-i)/\mathrm{gcd}
.
Putting the previous two equations together and using (
4
) we conclude

\operatorname{ord}_{t}\gamma=\frac{r_{E}m}{N_{E}}=\frac{r_{E}m}{\kappa_{E}+r_{E}d}=\frac{-im}{(m+id)-id}=-i.

In other words,
\operatorname{ord}_{t}\gamma=\rho
if and only if
\sigma
lifts to
E_{-\rho}
, as we wanted to show.
∎

3.4.
Degeneration of the order spectral sequence

Since
F_{\bullet}\mathcal{X}_{m}
is an increasing filtration by closed subsets, there is a spectral sequence in cohomology with compact support

(13)

\phantom{}_{F_{\bullet}\mathcal{X}_{m}}E_{1}^{p,q}=H^{p+q}_{c}(F_{(p)}\mathcal{X}_{m})\implies H^{p+q}_{c}(\mathcal{X}_{m}),

see
[
Stacks
,
Tag 012K
]
or
[
Ara05
, §3-§4]
.
In fact, by Proposition
3.2
this spectral sequence coincides, up to a relabeling of the entries, with the spectral sequence
\{\phantom{}_{\text{BFLN}}E_{\ell}^{p,q}\}_{\ell\geq 0}
given by Budur et al.
[
B+22
, Thm. 1.1]
.
In turn, the first page of the latter spectral sequence is isomorphic, up to a shift, to the first page of the McLean spectral sequence (
2
), see
[
B+22
, Rmk. 1.4]
.
More precisely, using the increasing function
\iota
from Proposition
3.2
and taking into account the index considerations of Remark
2.2
, we have

(14)

\phantom{}_{\text{McLean}}E_{1}^{\iota(p),q}\cong\phantom{}_{\text{BFLN}}E_{1}^{\iota(p),q+(n-1)(2m+1)}\cong\phantom{}_{F^{\bullet}\mathcal{X}_{m}}E_{1}^{p,q+(n-1)(2m+1)+\iota(p)-p}.

Taking this into account, the
arc-Floer conjecture
was stated in
[
B+22
, Conj. 1.5]
. With the corrected shift, the conjecture is

(AFC)

\HF^{\bullet}(\phi^{m},+)\cong H_{c}^{\bullet+(n-1)(2m+1)}(\mathcal{X}_{m})\quad\text{for every integer }m\geq 1.

Since the proof of degeneration in Theorem
2.11
only uses the fact that specific terms in the first page are nonzero, we could use exactly the same proof to prove the degeneration of (
13
) in some cases.
Nevertheless, we can exploit the topology of the contact loci that we computed in Proposition
3.1
to prove degeneration in all cases.
Another setting in which
\phantom{}{}_{\text{BFLN}}E_{1}^{p,q}
is known to degenerate at the first page is the case of hyperplane arrangements
[
BT22
, Prop. 7.4]
; note that this is the case of a homogeneous singularity of degree
d=1
without the assumption of having an isolated critical point at

4.
The arc-Floer conjecture

We are still considering a semihomogeneous germ
f:(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\rightarrow(\mathbb{C},0)
of degree
d
, see Definition
2.3
.
As we mentioned previously in (
14
), the McLean spectral sequence (
2
) and the spectral sequence associated to the order filtration (
13
) have the same first page up to a relabeling of the columns and a shift.
Furthermore, in Theorem
2.11
we have shown that McLean’s spectral sequence (
2
) degenerates at the first page under some conditions on the degree
d
and the number of variables
n
; while in Theorem
3.6
we have shown that (
13
) degenerates at the first page unconditionally (and hence so does Budur–Fernández de Bobadilla–Lê–Nguyen’s spectral sequence).

While this allows us to conclude that the
E_{\infty}
pages of both spectral sequences are isomorphic (under the conditions on
d
and
n
and accounting for the shift as in (
14
)), it is not enough to conclude that the groups to which they converge are isomorphic.
This is a general fact about spectral sequences: a filtered complex
F^{\bullet}C^{\bullet}
induces a filtration in cohomology
F^{\bullet}H^{\bullet}
, and (under mild finiteness assumptions that hold in our setting, see
[
Stacks
,
Tag 012W
]
) the graded pieces are given by the
E_{\infty}
page of the associated spectral sequence

F^{(p)}H^{p+q}\cong E_{\infty}^{p,q}.

Hence,
reconstructing
H^{\bullet}
from the
E_{\infty}
page requires understanding some extensions.
Since we are working with integer coefficients, and by Corollary
2.9
there are non-free terms in the
E_{1}
page, these extensions might be nontrivial.
Therefore, knowing that the
E_{\infty}
pages coincide only gives us a partial result.

5.1.

Recall once again that an arc in

is a morphism
\gamma:\allowbreak\Spec\mathbb{C}\llbracket t\rrbracket\rightarrow\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}=\Spec\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}].

There is a
\mathbb{C}

\mathcal{L}_{\infty}\coloneqq\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, called the

of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, whose closed points are arcs in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
It is equipped with canonical

\pi^{\infty}_{m}:\mathcal{L}_{\infty}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{m}
for every
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
For a polynomial
f\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
we define the
unrestricted
m
-contact locus
as

\mathcal{X}_{m}^{\infty}=\{\gamma\in\mathcal{L}_{\infty}\mid\gamma(0)=0,\ \mathrm{ord}_{t}(f(\gamma(t)))=m\}.

We now give the precise statement of the embedded Nash problem.
Let
\mu:X\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}
be an
m
-separating log resolution of the triple
(\mathbb{C}^{n},f,0)
(by definition this is an
m
-separating log resolution of the pair
(\mathbb{C}^{n},f)
with the additional requirement that
\mu^{-1}(0)
be a normal crossing divisor), and denote the total transform by
(f\circ\mu)^{-1}(0)=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}}N_{E}E
as we did in Section

For any arc
\gamma\in\mathcal{X}_{m}^{\infty}
, the image of its generic point is not contained in
f^{-1}(0)
, and therefore we may lift
\gamma
to a unique arc in the resolution
\widetilde{\gamma}\in\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(X)
, meaning
\gamma=\mu\circ\widetilde{\gamma}
.
Note that

m=\mathrm{ord}_{t}(f(\gamma(t)))=\mathrm{ord}_{t}(f(\mu(\widetilde{\gamma}(t))))=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}}N_{E}\cdot\mathrm{ord}_{E}(\widetilde{\gamma}).

Since the values
\mathrm{ord}_{E}(\widetilde{\gamma})
are a non-negative integers, the
m
-separating condition

N_{E}+N_{F}\leq m\implies E\cap F=\varnothing

implies that there is a unique

such that
\mathrm{ord}_{E}(\widetilde{\gamma})>0
, and furthermore
N_{E}
divides
m
, i.e.
E
is an
m
-divisor.
Following
[

]
, we define

\mathcal{X}_{m,E}^{\infty}\coloneqq\{\gamma\in\mathcal{X}_{m}^{\infty}\mid\mathrm{ord}_{E}(\widetilde{\gamma})>0\}=\{\gamma\in\mathcal{X}_{m}^{\infty}\mid\widetilde{\gamma}(0)\in E\},

and obtain the following decomposition (compare with §

)

\mathcal{X}_{m}^{\infty}=\bigsqcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}E\in\mathcal{E}\\

Proposition 5.1

(
[

, Thm. 2.1]
,
[

, Lemma 2.6]
)
.

For every

such that

,

is irreducible and has codimension

such that

. The embedded nash problem is the following:

(ENP)

describe the

such that

is an irreducible component of
\mathcal{X}_{m}^{\infty}

In analogy to de Fernex and Docampo’s solution to the classical Nash problem

]
, the authors of
[

]
gave a result that gives “bounds” for the components of the contact locus.
Before stating it, we need to introduce some definitions.

Definition 5.2
.

Let

be a smooth complex algebraic variety,

a nonzero effective divisor on

, and

(i)

An

-valuation

on

given by an
m
-divisor on some

-separating log resolution of the triple – that is,

for some prime divisor

on an

-separating log resolution

with

.

(ii)

Assume

is reduced. A

-valuation
of

is an

-valuation given by a prime divisor lying over

in a dlt modification of

, see
[

, Def. 2.4]
.

(iii)

A

-valuation

is an
m
-valuation

such that

is an irreducible component of the contact locus

.

(iv)

-valuation
of

is an
m
-valuation

.

Proposition 5.3

(
[

, Thm. 1.13]
)
.

Let

be as before, and

.
Then:

5.2.
Dlt, contact and essential valuations of semihomogeneous singularities

Our goal is to describe the three sets of Proposition
5.3
in the case where
Y=\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
D=\mathrm{div}(f)
is a semihomogeneous singularity and
\Sigma=\{0\}
.
To do so, we will use the
m
-separating resolution
\mu:X\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}
that we introduced in §
2.2
.
The key facts about
\mu
we need to recall are: that we have constructed it as an explicit sequence of blow-ups (
3
); that the dual graph of
\mu
is a chain; and that if we label its
m
-divisors as
E_{-\lfloor m/d\rfloor},\ldots,E_{-1},E_{0}
, where
E_{0}
is the strict transform and the order of the indices coincides with the order of the chain, then we have explicit formulas for the multiplicities and log discrepancies.
Let us denote
\mathcal{X}_{m,i}^{\infty}\coloneqq\mathcal{X}_{m,E_{i}}^{\infty}
.

Proposition 5.4
.

The contact
m
-valuations of a semihomogeneous singularity of degree
d
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
have the following description in terms of the
m
-separating resolution
\mu:X\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}
:

(i)

If
d<n
, then the only contact
m
-valuation is the one given by the divisor
E_{-1}
(note that this means there are no
m
-valuations if
m<d
).

(ii)

If
d\geq n
, then every
m
-divisor on
\mu:X\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}
gives a contact
m
-valuation.

Proof.

Recall from the proof of Proposition
3.2
that
\mathcal{X}_{m,i}^{\infty}=\{\gamma\in\mathcal{X}_{m}^{\infty}\mid\mathrm{ord}_{t}\gamma=-i\}.
By semicontinuity of the order, we conclude that
\overline{\mathcal{X}_{m,i}^{\infty}}\cap\mathcal{X}_{m,j}^{\infty}=\varnothing
if
i<j
.
Using (
4
) and (
8
), the codimension formula of Proposition
5.1
becomes

\mathrm{codim}(\mathcal{X}_{m,i}^{\infty})\coloneqq\mathrm{codim}(\mathcal{X}_{m,i}^{\infty}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(\mathbb{C}^{n}))=m\dfrac{\nu_{i}}{N_{i}}=m\dfrac{(m+id)-in}{(m+id)-id}=m+i(d-n).

If
d\geq n
, we see that
\mathrm{codim}(\mathcal{X}_{m,i}^{\infty})\leq\mathrm{codim}(\mathcal{X}_{m,j}^{\infty})
for every
i<j
, which shows that
\mathcal{X}_{m,i}^{\infty}\not\subset\overline{\mathcal{X}_{m,j}^{\infty}}
for
i<j
. This proves (ii).

On the other hand, note that
\mathcal{X}_{m}^{\infty}
is defined by
m-d+n
equations inside
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
:
n
equations to impose that
\gamma(0)=0
(one for each of the the constant terms of components of the arc
\gamma
) and
m-d
equations to impose that
\mathrm{ord}_{t}(f(\gamma(t)))=m
(one for each of the coefficients of
t^{d},\ldots,t^{m-1}
of
f(\gamma(t))
).
Thus any irreducible component of
\mathcal{X}_{m}^{\infty}
has codimension at most
m-d+n
.
If
d<n
, then for any
j<-1
we have

\mathrm{codim}(\mathcal{X}_{m,j}^{\infty})>\mathrm{codim}(\mathcal{X}_{m,-1}^{\infty})=m-(d-n)

and hence the only irreducible component of
\mathcal{X}_{m}^{\infty}
is
\overline{\mathcal{X}_{m,-1}^{\infty}}
. This proves (i).
∎

Remark 5.5
.

The previous proof shows that if
d\geq n
, then
\overline{\mathcal{X}_{m,i}^{\infty}}\cap\mathcal{X}_{m,j}^{\infty}=\varnothing
and
\mathcal{X}_{m,i}^{\infty}\not\subset\overline{\mathcal{X}_{m,j}^{\infty}}
for
i<j
, which implied that each
\overline{\mathcal{X}_{m,i}^{\infty}}
is an irreducible component of
\mathcal{X}_{m}^{\infty}
.
Nevertheless, in contrast with what happens for plane curves
[
BL23
, Thm. 3.5]
, the irreducible components of
\mathcal{X}_{m}^{\infty}
are
not
disjoint.
In other words, it may happen that
\mathcal{X}_{m,i}^{\infty}\cap\overline{\mathcal{X}_{m,j}^{\infty}}\neq\varnothing
for
i<j
. This means that the decomposition (
22
) is in general not a stratification.

Example 5.6
.

(i)

Consider the polynomial
f=x^{4}+y^{4}+z^{4}
(i.e.

,

) and the family of arcs

We have

.
In particular,

(ii)

Consider the polynomial

(i.e.

,

).
By Proposition

, there is only one contact

-valuation, meaning that

is irreducible.
However, given an arc

it is a straightforward computation to show that the Jacobian matrix of the defining equations has maximal rank at

if and only if

-valuations.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
W
be an algebraic variety,
\pi:\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{F})\rightarrow W
the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle associated to a rank 2 vector bundle
\mathcal{F}
, and
\sigma:W\rightarrow\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{F})
a section. Then, for every curve
C\subset\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{F})
, we have an equality of rational cycles

[C]=\mathrm{deg}(\pi|_{C})\cdot[\sigma(\pi(C))]+\pi^{*}\alpha_{0},

for some rational
0
-cycle
\alpha_{0}\in A_{0}(W)
.

Proof.

By
[
Ful98
, Thm. 3.3]
, any
1
-cycle
\beta\in A_{1}(\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{F}))
can be written as

\beta=(c_{1}(\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{F})}(1))\frown\pi^{*}\alpha_{1})+\pi^{*}\alpha_{0},

for unique
\alpha_{1}\in A_{1}(W)
and
\alpha_{0}\in A_{0}(W)
.
Applying
\pi_{*}
to both sides, we find
\pi_{*}\beta=\alpha_{1}
.
If we take
\beta=[C]-\mathrm{deg}(\pi|_{C})\cdot[\sigma(\pi(C))]
, we have
\pi_{*}\beta=0
and by uniqueness we get the desired result.

∎

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
W
be a smooth variety,
D
a smooth divisor on
W
,
Z
a smooth subvariety contained in the support of
D
, and
\beta:\Bl_{Z}W\rightarrow W
the blow-up of

at

.
Let
E
be the exceptional divisor of
\beta
and let
\widetilde{D}
be the strict transform of
D
under
\beta
. Then,

\mathcal{N}_{\widetilde{D}/\Bl_{Z}W}\cong\beta^{*}\mathcal{N}_{D/W}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{\widetilde{D}}(-E\cap\widetilde{D}).

Proof.

Note that

\beta^{*}\mathscr{O}_{W}(D)\cong\mathscr{O}_{\Bl_{Z}W}(\widetilde{D}+E)\cong\mathscr{O}_{\Bl_{Z}W}(\widetilde{D})\otimes\mathscr{O}_{\Bl_{Z}W}(E),

so tensoring by

and restricting to

we obtain the desired result, since
\mathcal{N}_{\widetilde{D}/\Bl_{Z}W}\cong\mathscr{O}_{\Bl_{Z}W}(\widetilde{D})|_{\widetilde{D}}

[
Vak25
, 21.2.J]
.
∎

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
\mu:X\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}
be the embedded resolution introduced in §

, and let

be the divisor associated to the coprime pair
(\kappa,r)\neq(0,1)

\mathcal{N}_{E/X}\cong\mathscr{O}_{E}(-1)\otimes\Big(\mathscr{O}_{E}(1)\otimes\pi^{*}\mathscr{O}_{S}(-\kappa^{\prime\prime}-r^{\prime\prime}d)\Big)^{\otimes-n^{\prime}}\otimes\Big(\mathscr{O}_{E}(1)\otimes\pi^{*}\mathscr{O}_{S}(\kappa^{\prime}+r^{\prime}d)\Big)^{\otimes-n^{\prime\prime}}.

Our strategy is to study how the normal bundle of

Recall that we denoted by

the image of

\mu^{(i+1)}\circ\cdots\circ\mu^{(\ell)}

There is a smallest

Then, by construction,

The center of every further blow-up

∎

Remark 5.10
.

The coprime pairs
(\kappa^{\prime},r^{\prime})
and
(\kappa^{\prime\prime},r^{\prime\prime})
are not extra data that one needs to remember from the resolution process: they may be easily obtained from the continued fraction expansion of

\kappa^{\prime}/r^{\prime}=[q_{0};q_{1},\ldots,q_{k-1}]\quad\text{ and }\quad\kappa^{\prime\prime}/r^{\prime\prime}=[q_{0};q_{1},\ldots,q_{k-1},q_{k}-1].

Proposition 5.11
.

The dlt
m
-valuations of a semihomogeneous singularity of degree

have the following description in terms of the

-separating resolution
\mu:X\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}

(i)

If

m
-valuations.

(ii)

If

, then every
m
-divisor on
\mu:X\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}
gives a dlt
m
-valuation.

Proof.

Recall that
D=\mathrm{div}(f)
.
Let

.
By
[

, to find all the dlt

-divisors appear on the dlt modifications of

Recall that for any exceptional

Now suppose

To show that

\displaystyle(K_{X}+\Delta)\cdot C

\displaystyle=\mathrm{deg}_{C}\mathscr{O}_{X}(\nu_{(0,1)}E_{(0,1)}+\nu_{(1,r)}E_{(1,r)})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\eqref{eq:N-nu-formulas}}}}{{=}}n\cdot\mathrm{deg}_{C}\mathscr{O}_{X}(E_{(0,1)})+(1+rn)\cdot\mathrm{deg}_{C}\mathscr{O}_{X}(E_{(1,r)})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phantom{a}}}{{=}}n\cdot\mathrm{deg}_{C}\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(-1)+(1+rn)\cdot\mathrm{deg}_{C}\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(d)

. We have

\displaystyle\mathscr{O}_{X}(E)|_{E}\cong\mathcal{N}_{E/X}

\displaystyle\cong\mathscr{O}_{E}(-1-n^{\prime}-n^{\prime\prime})\otimes\pi^{*}\mathscr{O}_{S}(a),\quad\text{for some $a\in\mathbb{Z}$}

\displaystyle\mathscr{O}_{X}(E_{(\kappa^{*},r^{*})})|_{E}

\displaystyle\cong\mathscr{O}_{E}(1)\otimes\pi^{*}\mathscr{O}_{S}(\kappa^{*}+r^{*}d),

\displaystyle\mathscr{O}_{X}(E_{(\kappa^{\prime\prime},r^{\prime\prime})})|_{E}

\displaystyle\cong\mathscr{O}_{E}(1)\otimes\pi^{*}\mathscr{O}_{S}(-\kappa^{\prime\prime}-r^{\prime\prime}d),

where the first line comes from Lemma
5.9
and the second and third lines come from (
23
). We have
\mathrm{deg}_{F}\mathscr{O}_{E}(1)=1
and
\mathrm{deg}_{F}\pi^{*}\mathscr{O}_{S}(k)=0
for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then,

\displaystyle(K_{X}+\Delta)\cdot F

\displaystyle=(\nu_{(\kappa^{*},r^{*})}E_{(\kappa^{*},r^{*})}+\nu_{(\kappa,r)}E_{(\kappa,r)}+\nu_{(\kappa^{\prime\prime},r^{\prime\prime})}E_{(\kappa^{\prime\prime},r^{\prime\prime})})\cdot F

\displaystyle=\nu_{(\kappa^{*},r^{*})}+\nu_{(\kappa,r)}(-1-n^{\prime}-n^{\prime\prime})+\nu_{(\kappa^{\prime\prime},r^{\prime\prime})}

\displaystyle=(\kappa^{*}+r^{*}n)-(\kappa^{\prime}+r^{\prime}n)(1+n^{\prime}+n^{\prime\prime})-(\kappa^{\prime\prime}+r^{\prime\prime}n)=0.

∎

Finally, we move on to the essential
m
-valuations.

Proposition 5.12
.

The map
\mu:X\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}
is the minimal
m
-separating log resolution of the triple
(\mathbb{C}^{n},f,0)
, in the sense that any other
m
-separating log resolution factors through
\mu
.

Proof.

Let
\nu:Y\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}
be an arbitrary
m
-separating log resolution of the triple
(\mathbb{C}^{n},f,0)
.
We are going to see that
\nu
factors through each of the blow-ups in the sequence (
3
) that defines
\mu
.

By definition of log resolution of a triple, we know that
\nu^{-1}(0)
is a normal crossing divisor.
Hence, by the universal property of the blow-up,
\nu
factors through the blow-up of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
at the origin, i.e. through
\mu^{(1)}
.

Now suppose that we have already shown that
\nu
factors through
\mu^{(i)}
for some
i<\ell
.
We want to apply once again the universal property of the blow-up to conclude that
\nu
factors through
\mu^{(i+1)}
, which is the blow-up of
X^{(i)}
at the intersection of two divisors
E
and
F
.
To be able to do that, we just need to show that
\nu^{-1}(E\cap F)
is a divisor on
Y
.

Suppose that
\nu^{-1}(E\cap F)
is not a divisor on
Y
, so there is some irreducible component
C
of
\nu^{-1}(E\cap F)
with
\mathrm{codim}_{Y}C\geq 2
.
Then there is some point
y\in C
such that the restriction of
\nu
to a neighborhood of
y
is an isomorphism (this is Zariski’s Main Theorem, see
[
Mum99
, Prop. III.9.1]
).
In particular, that the strict transforms of
E
and
F
on
Y
intersect at
\nu(y)
.

On the other hand, we know that
N_{E}+N_{F}\leq m
, since otherwise we would not have blown up the intersection of
E
and
F
in the construction of
\mu
.
Hence, since
\nu
is
m
-separating, the strict transforms of
E
and
F
on
Y
(which exist because we are assuming that
\nu
factors through
\mu^{(i)}
) do not intersect. This is a contradiction, so
\nu^{-1}(E\cap F)
must be a divisor on
Y
and we are done.
∎

Corollary 5.13
.

The essential
m
-valuations of a semihomogeneous singularity of degree
d
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
are precisely the ones given by
m
-divisors on the
m
-separating resolution
\mu:X\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Investigating the relationship between electronic learning and emotional and cultural intelligence of 2021–2022 incoming nursing students at Shiraz University of Medical Sciences
Authors: Nasrin Shokrpour, Zeinab Soleymani, AmirYusef Farahmandi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe present research aimed to investigate the relationship between electronic learning and emotional and cultural intelligence in Shiraz University of Medical Sciences nursing students who entered the university in the years 2021 and 2022.MethodsThis is a cross-sectional and correlational study conducted in the School of Nursing in Shiraz University of Medical Sciences. 172 nursing students who were studying in the academic year 2021–2022 participated in this study. 90 nursing students in 2021 and 82 in 2022 entered the university. Using Morgan’s sampling table, we selected 118 nursing students as the statistical sample. Due to the emergence of the Covid-19, available sampling was used. To collect the data, we used three emotional intelligence, cultural intelligence, and e-learning questionnaires. Pearson’s correlation coefficient and multiple linear regression tests were employed to analyze the data.ResultsIt was shown that there was a significant relationship between emotional intelligence and e-learning with students’ cultural intelligence. In addition, there was a significant relationship between electronic learning and emotional intelligence dimensions. Also, there was a positive and significant relationship between e-learning and the dimensions of cultural intelligence. The optimism, strategic, and motivational components could predict electronic learning in students.Discussion and conclusionAccording to the findings of the research, e-learning is a new teaching method that was used in Iran following the conditions of the COVID-19 pandemic in the world and provides many educational opportunities for the educational system of the country. It exists, prompting experts and curriculum specialists to examine e-learning and its influencing factors seriously and comprehensively. When students are at a favorable level in terms of emotional intelligence and cultural intelligence, they can accept electronic learning and improve and develop it.

Introduction

The integration of electronic technologies in the teaching and learning process is necessary and inevitable. E-learning, or electronic learning, refers to the use of digital technologies to deliver educational content and facilitate learning experiences. E-learning has become increasingly popular in recent years due to its flexibility, accessibility, and scalability; it simulates the students and provides the possibility of learning lessons instead of participating in the classroom or, in addition to that, through other methods such as video conference, chat, e-mail, and share documents [1]. The effectiveness of e-learning is influenced by various factors, including the design of the learning materials, the quality of instruction, and the engagement of learners. In this respect, e-learning, cultural intelligence, and emotional intelligence are all interconnected concepts that play a significant role in shaping students’ success in a diverse and globalized world. Some underlying theories underpin the relationship between these three constructs. Cultural intelligence (CQ) refers to an individual’s ability to function effectively in culturally diverse settings by understanding and adapting to different cultural norms and practices. The concept of cultural intelligence is based on the work of Earley and Ang (2003), who proposed a four-dimensional model of CQ comprising metacognitive, cognitive, motivational, and behavioral components. According to this model, individuals with high CQ possess the knowledge, skills, and attitudes necessary to interact successfully with people from different cultural backgrounds [2].

Emotional intelligence (EI) is another essential factor in students’ success, as it influences their ability to manage emotions, empathize with others, and navigate social interactions effectively. The concept of emotional intelligence was popularized by Daniel Goleman (1995), who proposed that EI consists of four main components: self-awareness, self-management, social awareness, and relationship management. Individuals with high EI can regulate their emotions, communicate effectively, and build strong relationships with others [3].

The relationship between cultural intelligence, emotional intelligence, and e-learning can be understood through the lens of social cognitive theory, which emphasizes the reciprocal interactions between personal factors, behavior, and the environment. According to Bandura (1986), individuals learn and develop new skills through observation, imitation, and reinforcement. In the context of e-learning, students with high CQ and EI are more likely to engage actively with the learning materials, collaborate effectively with their peers, and adapt to the demands of a culturally diverse and dynamic online environment [4].

Numerous studies have been conducted on these three concepts in various cultures. It is believed that conventional intelligence is a limited definition, and emotional intelligence shows how successful we are in work and play [5]. People with higher emotional intelligence regulate emotions and react meaningfully under stress [6], quickly and accurately mix competencies [7], and solve problems with logical explanations [8, 9]. Preston et al. emphasize that emotional intelligence is a person’s ability to coordinate with his and others’ emotions and guide their behavior. People with higher emotional intelligence are empathetic to others [10] and tend to reduce and resolve conflicts; they are calm under pressure, listen, and reflect and respond to constructive criticisms better than their peers [11]. Emotional intelligence requires a person’s competence in identifying and understanding emotions and allows people to understand and manage those emotions successfully; it has three assumptions: emotions play a fundamental role in people and in their ability to understand and practice different emotions, it can be developed and improved during a person’s life, and these variances cause individual adaptation to different contexts [12]. Davis and Nichols stated that emotional intelligence, such as emotional awareness, could negatively affect a person’s capacity to cope with emotionally related situations, leading to adverse intrapersonal effects such as overreaction to stress, including manipulative behaviors and becoming antisocial [13].

In this regard, Neg et al. state that the concept of cultural intelligence which was first introduced by Dieterman and Sternberg (1986) is one of the dimensions of multidimensional intelligence. Cultural intelligence refers to personal capacities that enable a person to interact with other people from different backgrounds and cultural backgrounds. It is a sign of the ability to be effective between different cultures and this skill can be taught to people. In short, cultural intelligence can be defined as a person’s ability to successfully adapt to a new cultural environment, which also has areas of cross-cultural education [14]. It seems that cultural intelligence is an inevitable necessity in medical professions and contexts. It helps the employees of medical departments to perform their duties with people who have different cultures and different ethnic and racial backgrounds and have work and social interaction [15]. Shakurzadeh in a study revealed that there was a strong relationship between emotional intelligence and digital literacy and e-learning with the resilience of Azad University students during the Covid-19 pandemic. In general, the variables of emotional intelligence, digital literacy, and electronic learning have a significant relationship with students’ resilience, and these variables predict the resilience variable [16]. Rahgozar and Abadian in their research showed that there was a significant relationship between training flow and emotional intelligence as well as training and motivation. There was also a significant relationship between emotional intelligence and motivation, as well as between emotional intelligence and the success of virtual education based on computer games [17]. Nikomanesh and Shabani in another study indicated that there was a significant relationship between distance education and emotional intelligence [18]. Embaye et al. (2022) in their research suggested that there were three important groups of students: those with “average characteristics, average performance, those with “slightly above average trait emotional intelligence, high performers,” and those with “trait emotional intelligence, outstanding performers.” The characteristics that distinguish most of the high-functioning group from the others are the extent to which the cluster members have a high score of well-being, self-control, emotions, sociability, and, in general, a higher record of global profiles of trait emotional intelligence [19]. Even, Wahab et al. in 2022 in their study showed that there was a relationship between students’ perception of instructors’ emotional intelligence and students’ performance during online learning at a public university in Malaysia [20]. The positive impact of emotional intelligence on education has also received widespread attention from practitioners around the world [21]. In a more recent study, Albalawee et al. concluded that cultural intelligence and language learners’ strategy could directly predict the language achievement of Iraqi EFL learners. Emo-sensory intelligence (ESQ) could only predict language learners’ achievement through the mediation of language learning strategies [22].

Learners, especially in higher education, are the most important part of the education system in a country, and it is essential to pay attention to their educational and learning issues and, accordingly, academic progress and career future. Due to the COVID-19 pandemic and its complications that threatened the mental and physical health of those affected, health protocols were compiled and implemented, and one of the cases included in this framework was the virtualization of education and the closing of schools and universities. Changes in teaching methods and educational system and adaptation to the crisis conditions and tensions that we have faced in this field are among the serious challenges for learners, higher education, professors, and families. Therefore, it is important to pay attention to the factors that are effective in e-learning. In this research, two important aspects of emotional and cultural intelligence are discussed. There is a research gap in the fact that no study has been conducted in the field of e-learning and cultural intelligence. Most of the research conducted in the field of e-learning and emotional intelligence is on the statistical population of students. Less has been done in the field of medical contexts.

Methods

This is a descriptive-correlation study conducted using Pearson’s correlation coefficient and linear regression method which examined the relationship between electronic learning and emotional intelligence and cultural intelligence of students. The participants were nursing students of Shiraz University of Medical Sciences, who were 172 individuals studying at this university in the academic year 2021–2022. The number of incoming nursing students in the year 2021 was equal to 90, and that of the year 2020 was equal to 82. Using Morgan’s sampling table, we selected 118 nursing students as the statistical sample. According to the number of the research community, this value in the statistical sample column in Morgan’s sampling table is equal to 118 subjects. The available sampling method was used in this research. To collect information, we used electronic learning, emotional intelligence, and cultural intelligence questionnaires.

The e-learning questionnaire was developed and designed by Watkins et al. in 2004 [23] and contains 27 questions and 6 dimensions related to access to technology (1 to 3), motivation, (13 to 15), continuous communication skills (4 to 11), ability to learn through media (16 to 18), Internet group discussions (19 to 22), and important issues for success in e-learning (23 to 27); it measures the students’ abilities in the use of e-learning and is scored based on a five-point Likert scale ranging from “completely agree” to “completely disagree”, using questions such as “I am equipped with the basic skills of Internet search and access to information.” The students’ score is calculated by the sum of scores to each question. The minimum score is 27 and the maximum 135. The more the score, the higher the student’s ability in the use of online learning. In his project, Ahangar Saleh Bani evaluated the validity of the content, form, and criteria of this questionnaire. Cronbach’s alpha coefficient calculated in Ahangar Saleh Bani’s research for this questionnaire was estimated to be above 0.7 [24].

The second questionnaire used was devised by Petrides et al. and is a 30-question questionnaire called Emotional Intelligence of Students; it measures the students’ emotions when faced with different situations and includes 15 subscales with 4 factors called well-being, self-control, irritability, and warm-bloodedness. The range of scores is between 30 and 210, and higher scores indicate more emotional intelligence. The items are scored based on a 7-point Likert scale ranging from “Completely agree = 7” to “Completely disagree = 1” [25]. The minimum score is 30 and the maximum score is 210. Scores between 30 and 60 indicate poor emotional intelligence, between 60 and 90 moderate emotional intelligence, and higher than 90 strong emotional intelligence. The validity and reliability of this questionnaire were estimated by Bayani as acceptable [26]. Petrides and Farnham reported 0.84 and 0.89 in men and women, respectively, for the validity of the questionnaire using the internal consistency method [25]. Cooper and Petrides confirmed the validity and reliability of the questionnaire [27]. Ahmadi et al. (2015) reported the validity of the questionnaire using the internal consistency method in the sample of students as 0.76 and 0.71, respectively [28].

The cultural intelligence questionnaire was devised by Eng et al. in 2004 and includes 20 items. It measures the students’ ability to behave appropriately in different cultural situations. The questions are scored using a seven-point Likert scale and have four dimensions of cultural intelligence: strategic (1 to 5), cognitive (6 to 10), motivational (11 to 15), and behavioral (16 to 20) [29]. The scores range from 20 to 140; the higher the scores, the higher the students’ cultural intelligence. In Ballou’s research, by using the opinion of experts and experienced professors in the field of cultural affairs management, the formal and content validity of the measurement tool was approved in measuring the research variables, and their opinions were applied. The reliability of the questionnaire using Cronbach’s alpha method in Balue’s research for strategic, cognitive, motivational, and behavioral components was obtained as 0.75, 0.75, 0.74, and 0.74, respectively [30]. Also, in the research of Mousavi Chalak et al., Cronbach’s alpha coefficient was equal to 0.80 [31].

For data analysis, SPSS version 25 was used, and descriptive statistics methods (mean, standard deviation, frequency) and Pearson correlation coefficient statistical methods were applied to answer the first research question; also, regression analysis was used to test the second research question of the study. The significance level for all tests was considered less than 0.05. This study was approved by the ethics committee of Shiraz University of Medical Sciences with the code of IR.SUMS.REC.1402-028, and the participants’ informed consent was obtained before they filled out the questionnaires.

The relationship between electronic learning and emotional intelligence and cultural intelligence of students

According to the results, there was a positive and significant relationship between the dimensions of e-learning and emotional intelligence with cultural intelligence. In other words, with the increase in emotional intelligence and electronic learning, the level of cultural intelligence of students also increases.

To further analyze the results, we also used Pearson correlation tests to determine whether there is a significant relationship between electronic learning and different dimensions of emotional intelligence, and then between electronic learning and dimensions of cultural intelligence separately.

The results showed that there was a positive and significant relationship between all dimensions of emotional intelligence and electronic learning. In other words, with the increase in optimism, understanding, control, and social skills, the amount of electronic learning by students also increased. As to the relationship between electronic learning and all the dimensions of cultural intelligence, it was revealed that there was a positive and significant relationship between e-learning and the components of cultural intelligence. In other words, with the increase in strategic, cognitive, motivational, and behavioral skills, the students’ amount of electronic learning also increased.

Discussion

In the present study, we first sought to answer whether there is a significant relationship between e-learning and emotional intelligence with students’ cultural intelligence. It was found that there was a positive and significant relationship between the dimensions of e-learning and emotional intelligence with cultural intelligence. In other words, with the increase in emotional intelligence and electronic learning, the level of cultural intelligence of students also increased. In further analysis, it was shown that all the dimensions of emotional and cultural intelligence were correlated with electronic learning. In the same line, Alam et al. in 2021 worked on the effect of e-learning and emotional intelligence on psychological intentions. It was found that online classes and EI can affect the students’ stress, burnout, and performance [32]. Also, the role of emotional intelligence was investigated by Viscue et al., concluding that emotional intelligence, as a personality trait, is important in online success, being able to predict the students’ performance [33].

Studies have shown that emotional intelligence has an important role in influencing.and controlling different variables of the learning process.Studies have shown that emotional intelligence has an important role in influencing.and controlling different variables of the learning process.Emotional intelligence, as a personality trait, has also been considered important in.online success, being able to predict the student’s performance.

In another study conducted in 2024 by Das, the role of emotional intelligence (EI) in e-learning was discussed. He explains that components of emotional intelligence include self-awareness, self-control, inner motivation, and social consciousness; similar to our results, it was indicated that EI helps to enhance one’s performance in online learning [34]. Also, Hendra et al. concluded that self-efficacy, emotional intelligence, and learning motivation have a positive and significant impact on students’ academic performance [35]. The findings of some other studies also confirm those of other studies [16, 19, 20].

In this regard, it can be said that the relationship between emotional intelligence and electronic learning or virtual education is a very serious and challenging issue that has led to numerous studies in this field. Emotional intelligence is an ability that can be improved by students by using self-awareness, self-control skills, and self-management. Understanding its effectiveness with compassion and empathy and behaving with proper regulation and management of relationships in such a way that they develop their motivation and performance are of high importance. Today, due to the increasing need of people for education and learning, lack of economic facilities professional trainers, and access to educational centers as well as the high costs, education planners and specialists have suggested that a curriculum in which, with the help of new educational technologies, new methods that are both economical and cost-effective and train many people at the same time should be designed. Virtual education or e-learning in our country has not received due attention due to lack of acceptance of technology and culture, lack of awareness and recognition and positive and negative consequences, and lack of educational facilities, technology, and technological infrastructure. Thus, this teaching method is more difficult for families and educational system than for students. One of the factors that can play a constructive and practical role is the development of cultural intelligence, which is different according to the social and cultural values and norms of each country and requires the attention of authorities’ compassion, planning, and basic measures in this field.

It was also found in this study that there was a significant relationship between electronic learning and different components of emotional intelligence. The results are consistent with those of the studies conducted by Shakurzadeh [16], Rahgozar and Abadian [17], Embay et al. [19], and Viscue [33]. According to the findings of this research, it can be said that for academic success, learners must learn skills to face stressful situations, maintain a positive attitude, and interact with professors. By learning and applying emotional intelligence techniques, students’ psychological tensions can be dealt with and their performance can be increased. Emotional intelligence increases academic performance and reduces psychological stress among students, and this is more common in nursing students who experience a lot of psychological stress in their work and academic environment. It also increases emotional intelligence skills, communication skills, and people’s ability to create and maintain social networks, which also contributes to mental health, academic and career success, and motivation. People who have higher emotional intelligence show social and individual adaptation as well as social skills, resilience, psychological pressure, well-being, and better academic performance. E-learning is the most important teaching method, which has received a lot of attention in the education system of the country after the days of Corona. Virtual education in the universities in our country has many problems and challenges, including lack of awareness, acceptance of new teaching methods, lack of familiarity with technology, educational facilities and technological infrastructures, lack of students’ compatibility and interaction with professors and e-learning environment, and tension and psychological pressure. Emotions, methods of self-awareness, management and control over behavior, thoughts and feelings, optimism, critical thinking, development of communication skills, and personality traits are effective in improving and developing electronic learning or virtual education.

In this study, we also found that there was a significant relationship between electronic learning and the components of cultural intelligence of students. Albalawee et al. targeted toward examining the relationship between cultural intelligence (CQ), emo-sensory intelligence (ESQ), language learning strategies (LLS), and students’ language achievement (LA). They indicated that there was a significant relationship between ESQ, CQ, learning strategies, and students’ language achievement. In addition, it was found that CQ and language learners’ strategy could directly predict the achievement of Iraqi EFL learners [22], which is consistent with our results.

Omidyan and Alipour state that educational systems such as universities should plan to promote students’ personality and cultural intelligence so that they can increase their academic achievement and social skills. According to them, this can be done through conferences, seminars, workshops and plans such as cultural and scientific camping research, competition, and teamwork for university students [36].

One of the most important reasons for the success of e-learning is the level of cultural intelligence. In fact, acculturation and provision of a suitable platform provide the grounds for acceptance, planning, and adaptation to new teaching and learning methods, using communication technologies. It is necessary to comply with the cultural standards appropriate to the values, norms, educational criteria, and content of textbooks at the discretion of the planners and authors of the textbooks, as well as the religious beliefs and educational system of any given country. To develop and popularize virtual education, the educational system in the country must, in the first step, believe in the necessity and effectiveness of virtual education or e-learning to be able to talk about its application and use by students and professors, and then design the necessary platform for continuation and provide the stability of e-learning in the higher education. The higher the level of cultural intelligence of students and professors and the education system of the country in general, the more and better the tendency toward electronic learning.

It was also revealed in this study that emotional intelligence could predict the students’ electronic learning. Embaye et al. also propose a model for categorizing students into specific groups while considering their emotional intelligence that can provide better adaptivity in e-education. In addition, the study can contribute to building more effective educational strategies in an e-learning environment [19]. In this regard, studies carried out by Shakurzadeh, Rahgozar and Abadian, and Ahmadi et al. are consistent with those of the present study. According to them, emotional intelligence instills higher skills in students in the field of personal management, emotional self-awareness, and understanding of emotions and feelings. Accordingly, people are suggested to deal with the problems and challenges of life. Given that virtual education and learning environments are stressful and students experience a lot of stress, they need mutual understanding [16, 17, 28]. As found the optimism component positively and significantly predicts e-learning. Therefore, it is important to pay attention to this issue. Optimism is one of the most important factors and components of emotional intelligence, which will play a very important role in the development of e-learning and its effectiveness. It refers to positive and hopeful beliefs about issues and situations and related challenges and leads to the quality of learning, education and teaching, academic progress, academic and career motivation, job and academic satisfaction, performance, academic and career success, commitment and loyalty, critical thinking, reduction of job burnout, improvement of virtual education, etc. One of the factors that can be effective in solving the problems related to e-learning and its development is the positive insight and perspective in this field and awareness of the positive effects of e-learning on learning and education and the level of motivation and enthusiasm of students, professors, and e-learning presenters. The higher the level of self-awareness, control of emotions, and emotions, the greater the effectiveness and optimism towards e-learning.

In this phase of the study, it was also found that cultural intelligence could predict the students’ electronic learning as a part of their achievements, the result which has been confirmed in some other studies. For example, Albalawee et al. in 2024 state that cultural intelligence (CQ) and language learners’ strategy could directly predict the achievement of Iraqi EFL learners, but emo-sensory intelligence (ESQ) could only predict the learners’ achievement through the mediation of learning strategies [22].

Khan et al. in a study which aimed to study the effects of emotional intelligence and cultural intelligence in undergraduate, graduate, and post-graduate students studying abroad and how it affects their academic performance, with cultural adjustment mediating their educational achievements. Their findings indicated that all variables were statistically significant, positive and moderately correlated with each other and cultural adjustment significantly mediated the effect between emotional intelligence, cultural intelligence and academic performance of international students [37].

The results of this research are in the same line with those of the studies conducted by Hajizadeh (2021) and Dalili Saleh (2019) [38, 39]. In explaining the findings of the research, it can be said that cultural intelligence is in line with emotional intelligence and has a complementary and strategic role. The basis of emotional intelligence is that students are familiar with their culture and values. The importance of the role of cultural intelligence is revealed when emotional intelligence shows its weaknesses and limitations, and cultural intelligence, with the foundation and strategies it provides, not only eliminates the weaknesses of emotional intelligence but also causes the improvement and development of emotional intelligence and provides the fields of strengthening and virtual training. The motivational element helps people to persevere in the face of obstacles so that they can adapt to other cultures. This section may be the most difficult or ambiguous component of cultural intelligence. Being motivated, having perseverance, and having strong belief are very effective in this way. Cultures differ from each other in many ways. Without sufficient motivation, cultural intelligence is meaningless. This element considers the ability to empathize. The desire to communicate and persist on the way to the goal covers the biggest cultural gaps. Also, the strategic component is effective in students’ electronic learning. The cognitive or strategic element refers to the general thinking skills that people use to understand how and why to operate in new environments. In addition to people’s opinions and values, this knowledge also includes the methods and procedures that others use to do work. For many people, it is difficult to find an opening to foreign cultures; however, a person who has a strong cultural intelligence from the mental and cognitive aspects will quickly find some common concepts. The relationship between cultural intelligence and e-learning is not hidden from anyone. Electronic learning, which is named by other titles such as electronic education, virtual education, and online or online education plays a very important role in the learning and education of students. To realize e-learning, it is necessary to create a culture and stabilize its position in the educational system. The requirement for this is the development of cultural intelligence in higher education to promote the electronic learning of students so that its challenges and disadvantages can be solved, and educational opportunities can arise.

Among the applications of emotional intelligence and cultural intelligence in e-learning can be the development of academic progress, continuous and effective learning, the ability to understand one’s own and other’s emotions, the development of compatible emotions and reduction of incompatible emotions, the development of self-efficacy and communication skills, the development of academic and career motivation, reduction of academic stress and anxiety, development of psychological resilience, social responsibility and competence perception, and development of creative and critical thinking. To this end, Mirmoghtadaie et al. (2020) in an article entitled” Virtual education based on emotional intelligence components and E-learning readiness” devised a course. According to them, since the professors’ and students’ face-to-face communication is reduced compared to the traditional methods used, while if these influential factors are categorized, some cases can be mentioned whose traces can be found in the emotional intelligence of individuals and their readiness ratio for e-learning [38]. Also, Embaye et al. introduced a model for adaptive e-education based on trait emotional intelligence. Using Trait Emotional Intelligence (TraitEI) as a model and agglomerative hierarchical cluster analysis technique, they identified segments of students attending the online course. According to them, using this model for segmenting students into related groups based on emotional intelligence enhances adaptivity in e-education. Moreover, their study contributes to building more effective educational strategies in an e-learning context [19]. As suggested by Karami and Izadpanah, the EFL students have more cultural intelligence compared to medical students. Thus, based on the results, the authorities in medical environments should consider the significance of cultural factors in their classes [39], which is true with our students in health-related fields like medicine and nursing.

Among the limitations of the present research is that we conducted our study on nursing students in Shiraz, so its generalization to other educational levels and students of other disciplines, and cultures is limited. This research is of correlation type and only the relationship between the variables has been examined, so it is not possible to understand the cause-and-effect relationships between the variables.

Conclusion

According to the findings of the current research, following the COVID-19 pandemic, e-learning is a new teaching method used in Iran and the world and has provided many educational opportunities for the educational system in the countries. It exists, prompting experts and curriculum specialists to examine e-learning and its influencing factors seriously and comprehensively. When students are at a favorable level in terms of emotional intelligence and cultural intelligence, they can accept electronic learning and improve and develop it. The authorities in the education system are suggested to address these areas and provide more comprehensive insights and support for the effective implementation of e-learning strategies while considering the students’ emotional and cultural intelligence.

Suggestions for further research

Further research could replicate this study with a broader sample size and include a diverse sample from various student populations and disciplines. Also, a mixed-methods study using both quantitative and qualitative research tools should be conducted to gain a deeper understanding of the variables.

Moreover, comparative studies on emotional and cultural intelligence in the context of electronic learning in different universities merit further investigation. University authorities can hold seminars to familiarize students with the emotional environment of the university, aiding acceptance of technological and virtual education changes and highlighting the positive aspects of electronic education through lectures, posters, and incentives to foster a supportive psychological environment. Further research can also look at implementing emotional and cultural intelligence training courses to enhance performance, adaptability, and attitudes toward change and innovation in e-learning.

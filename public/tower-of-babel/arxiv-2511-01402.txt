Scalar self-force effects in neutral W W -soliton backgrounds
Massimo Bianchi 1 , Donato Bini 2 , Giorgio Di Russo 3

Abstract
We investigate several geometrical and physical properties of the recently found W W -soliton solution (neutral case). We discuss both the genuine 5d solution and its reduction to 4d and highlight similarities and differences. In both cases, we study scattering processes of massless and massive particles in the background, reconstructing the gauge-invariant scattering angle, either with exact expressions or with large-angular momentum expansion expressions, which we show how to resum in a useful form. Finally, we analyze the propagation of a test scalar field in the W W -soliton background and compute the spectrum of Quasi Normal Modes in the case of (non-)minimal coupling and the radiated energy in the case of minimal coupling. Our result for the energy loss is fully analytic and presented in a Post-Newtonian expansion, following the approach termed gravitational self force.

IV
Scalar field in 5d

A massless scalar field in the
W
-soliton spacetime, in the minimal coupling case, evolves according the following wave equation

\Box\psi=-4\pi\rho_{S}\,,

(119)

where

\rho_{S}=q_{S}\int\frac{d\tau}{\sqrt{-g}}\delta^{(5)}(x-x_{p}(\tau))\,.

(120)

is a scalar charge density, with
q_{S}
a scalar ‘charge’, describing the tri-linear coupling of a massive probe to the massless scalar field.
The latter is taken as a proxy for the more involved case of gravitational waves and may represent one of the two scalar fields sourcing the
W
-soliton. In particular setting
{\rm Im}(z)=\exp(\sqrt{2}\psi/\sqrt{3})
gives rise to a canonically normalized scalar field minimally coupled to the gravitational field.

Eq. (
119
) can be reduced by expanding in spherical harmonics and Fourier transforming the time

\psi(t,r,\theta,\phi,y)=\sum_{\ell mn}Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi)\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}e^{-i\omega t}e^{i\frac{n}{R_{y}}y}R_{\ell m\omega n}(r)\,.

(121)

Notice that
R_{\ell m\omega n}(r)
is actually
m
independent in the homogeneous case, but it acquires a non-trivial dependence on
m
in presence of a (generic) source.

IV.1
Solutions of the homogeneous radial equation

Taking
n=0=P_{y}
for simplicity, the homogeneous radial equation is then given by

\displaystyle r(r-4M)R_{\ell\omega}^{\prime\prime}(r)+2(r-2M)R_{\ell\omega}^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle+[r^{2}\omega^{2}-\ell(\ell+1)]R_{\ell\omega}(r)=0\,,

(122)

and admits two independent solutions, labeled below as “in-solutions” (regular at
r=4M
) and “up-solutions” (purely outgoing at
r\to+\infty

We will find these solutions in Post-Newtonian (PN) sense, determining

1.

pure PN in- and up-solutions:
R_{\ell\omega}^{\rm in,PN}(r)
and

\ell

2.

MST-type in- and up-solutions:
R_{\ell\omega}^{\rm in,MST}(r)
and

\ell=0,1,2,3,\ldots

They are discussed below where we also display the beginning of the power series representing them.

IV.2
PN-type solutions

The in-solution is given by

\displaystyle R_{\ell\omega}^{\rm in,PN}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{\ell}-\eta^{2}r^{\ell-1}\frac{2\ell M+r^{3}\omega^{2}}{2(3+2\ell)}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.\frac{8((\ell-1)\ell-4)Mr^{3}\omega^{2}}{(\ell+1)(2\ell+3)}\right)+O(\eta^{6})\,,

IV.4
The inhomogeneous radial equation

As in Ref.
Bianchi:2024vmi
, we assume the source moving on a timelike circular equatorial geodesic, with parametric equations

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t_{p}(\tau)=\Gamma\tau\,,\quad r=r_{p}(\tau)=r_{0}\,,\quad\theta=\theta_{p}(\tau)=\frac{\pi}{2}\,,

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{p}(\tau)=\Gamma\Omega\tau\,,\quad y_{p}(\tau)=0\,,\qquad

(134)

with
r_{0}>4M
and

\Omega=\sqrt{\frac{M}{r_{0}^{3}-Mr_{0}^{2}}}\,,\qquad\Gamma=\frac{\sqrt{1-\frac{M}{r_{0}}}}{1-\frac{2M}{r_{0}}}\,,

(135)

and 5-velocity

U^{\mu}=\frac{dx^{\mu}_{p}(\tau)}{d\tau}=\Gamma(\delta^{\mu}_{t}+\Omega\delta^{\mu}_{\phi})\,.

(136)

The scalar charge density
\rho_{S}
, Eq. (

\displaystyle\rho_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q_{S}\int\frac{d\tau}{r(r-2M)\sin\theta}\delta^{(5)}(x-x_{p}(\tau))\,,

(137)

where now
\sqrt{-g}=r(r-2M)\sin\theta=r^{2}f_{s}(r)\sin\theta
(notice the presence of the factor

in comparison with the familiar Schwarzschild spacetime) and

\displaystyle\delta^{(5)}(x-x_{p}(\tau))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta(t-\Gamma\tau)\delta(r-r_{0})\delta(\theta-\frac{\pi}{2})\times

(138)

\displaystyle\times\delta(\phi-\Omega t)\delta(y)\,.

The integral over
\tau
is immediate and gives

\displaystyle\rho_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q_{S}}{r_{0}^{2}f_{s}(r_{0})\Gamma}\delta(r-r_{0})\delta(\theta-\frac{\pi}{2})\delta(\phi-\Omega t)\delta(y)

(139)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q_{S}}{r_{0}^{2}f_{s}(r_{0})\Gamma}\sum_{\ell mn}\delta(r-r_{0})Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi)Y_{\ell m}^{*}(\frac{\pi}{2},0)\times

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}e^{-i\omega t}\delta(\omega-m\Omega)\frac{e^{in\frac{y}{R_{y}}}}{2\pi R_{y}}\,,

where we have used a Fourier series representation for
\delta(y)

\delta(y)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{in\frac{y}{R_{y}}}}{2\pi R_{y}}\,,

(140)

(instead of an integral representation) since

is a periodic variable,
besides the identity

\displaystyle\delta(\theta-\frac{\pi}{2})\delta(\phi-\phi_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell m}Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi)Y_{\ell m}^{*}(\frac{\pi}{2},\phi_{0})\,.\qquad

(141)

Consequently, the scalar charge density
\rho_{S}

\displaystyle\rho_{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell mn}{\mathcal{S}}_{\ell mn}(t,r)e^{in\frac{y}{R_{y}}}Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi)

(142)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell mn}\int_{\omega}e^{-i\omega t}\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\ell mn}(\omega,r)e^{in\frac{y}{R_{y}}}Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi)\,,

where we have introduced the notation

\int_{\omega}=\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\,.

(143)

Furthermore, for dimensional reasons and to ease comparison with 4d spacetimes (i.e., the Schwarzschild spacetime), it is convenient to define

q_{\rm W}=\frac{q_{S}}{2\pi R_{y}}\,.

(144)

Therefore,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{\ell mn}(t,r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\omega}e^{-i\omega t}\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\ell mn}(\omega,r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q_{W}}{r_{0}^{2}f_{s}(r_{0})\Gamma}\delta(r-r_{0})e^{-im\Omega t}Y_{\ell m}^{*}(\frac{\pi}{2},0)\,,

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\ell mn}(\omega,r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dte^{i\omega t}{\mathcal{S}}_{\ell mn}(t,r)

(145)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q_{W}}{r_{0}^{2}f_{s}(r_{0})\Gamma}\delta(r-r_{0})Y_{\ell m}^{*}(\frac{\pi}{2},0)\times

\displaystyle 2\pi\delta(\omega-m\Omega)\,.

In the present case
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\ell mn}(\omega,r)
does not depend explicitly on
n

Let us then look for massless scalar waves
\psi

Eq. (

\displaystyle\Box\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell mn}\int_{\omega}e^{-i\omega t}e^{in\frac{y}{R_{y}}}Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi)\left[\frac{f^{2}}{f_{s}}R_{\ell mn\omega}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{2}{r}R_{\ell mn\omega}^{\prime}+\left(\frac{\omega^{2}}{f^{2}}-\frac{n^{2}f_{s}^{2}}{R_{y}^{2}f^{2}}-\frac{L}{r^{2}f_{s}}\right)R_{\ell mn\omega}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\pi\sum_{\ell mn}\int_{\omega}e^{-i\omega t}e^{in\frac{y}{R_{y}}}\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\ell mn}(\omega,r)Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi)\,,

\sum_{\ell mn}Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi)\int_{\omega}e^{-i\omega t}e^{in\frac{y}{R_{y}}}{\mathcal{E}}_{\ell mn\omega}(r)=0\,,

(147)

where

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{\ell mn\omega}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{f^{2}}{f_{s}}R_{\ell mn\omega}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{2}{r}R_{\ell mn\omega}^{\prime}+\left(\frac{\omega^{2}}{f^{2}}-\frac{n^{2}f_{s}^{2}}{R_{y}^{2}f^{2}}-\frac{L}{r^{2}f_{s}}\right)R_{\ell mn\omega}+4\pi\widehat{\mathcal{S}}_{\ell mn}(\omega,r)\,,\qquad

\displaystyle\frac{f^{2}}{f_{s}}R_{\ell mn\omega}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{2}{r}R_{\ell mn\omega}^{\prime}+\left(\frac{\omega^{2}}{f^{2}}-\frac{n^{2}f_{s}^{2}}{R_{y}^{2}f^{2}}-\frac{L}{r^{2}f_{s}}\right)R_{\ell mn\omega}

\displaystyle-\frac{q_{W}}{r_{0}^{2}f_{s}(r_{0})\Gamma}\delta(r-r_{0})Y_{\ell m}^{*}(\frac{\pi}{2},0)2\pi\delta(\omega-m\Omega)\,.

(149)

\displaystyle S_{\ell mn\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{4\pi q_{\rm W}}{\Gamma\Delta(r_{0})}Y_{\ell m}^{*}(\frac{\pi}{2},0)2\pi\delta(\omega-m\Omega)

\displaystyle\bar{S}_{\ell mn\omega}2\pi\delta(\omega-m\Omega)\,,

\displaystyle-q_{\rm W}K(r_{0})Y_{\ell m}^{*}(\frac{\pi}{2},0)\,,

(153)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{(r)}R_{\ell mn\omega}

(156)

\displaystyle+

Finally,

V
Concluding remarks

We have studied the so-called neutral
W
-soliton solution, recently discovered in
Chakraborty:2025ger
;
Dima:2025tjz
, which is an exact solution of the Einstein-Maxwell equations in 5 dimensions with source two electromagnetic fields and, after reduction to 4 dimensions, a complex scalar field.
The interesting feature is that its reduced version in d=4 is also an exact solution which contains a naked singularity and its source is a (complex) scalar field.

In both cases, 4d and 5d, we have studied a scattering process of massless and massive particle on the background, reconstructing the scattering angle either with exact expressions or with large-angular momentum expansion expressions (which we have shown how to resum in some useful form).
Finally we have analized the case of a scalar field evolving on the background, characterizing Quasi-Normal-Modes in case of (non-)minimal coupling and the radiated energy in the case of minimal coupling. Our result for the energy loss is fully analytic and presented in PN expanded form, following the approach termed gravitational self force, although we focussed on massless scalar waves as a proxy of gravitational waves. This result has been obtained by generalizing to this context the procedure developed by Mano-Suzuki-Takasugi for black holes and checking its consistency with the modern approach based on qSW and CFT.
This nontrivial accomplishment has allowed us to compare and contrast with similar situations in the case of a Schwarzschild black hole and that of a topological star.

It will be a challenge left to future works to extend these type of studies to the case of a charged
W
-soliton solution and to genuine GWs.

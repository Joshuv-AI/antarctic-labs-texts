Reanalyzing DESI DR1: 4. Percent-Level Cosmological Constraints from Combined Probes and Robust Evidence for the Normal Neutrino Mass Hierarchy Preprint: MIT-CTP/5993
Mikhail M. Ivanov, James M. Sullivan, Shi-Fan Chen, Anton Chudaykin, Mark Maus, Oliver H. E. Philcox

Abstract
We present cosmological parameters measurements from the full combination of DESI DR1 galaxy clustering data described with large-scale structure effective field theory. By incorporating additional datasets (photometric galaxies and CMB lensing cross-correlations) and extending the bispectrum likelihood to smaller scales using a consistent one-loop theory computation, we achieve substantial gains in constraining power relative to previous analyses. Combining with the latest DESI baryon acoustic oscillation data and using cosmic microwave background (CMB) priors on the power spectrum tilt and baryon density, we obtain tight constraints on the Λ \Lambda CDM model, finding the Hubble constant H 0 = 69.08 ± 0.37 ​ km ​ s − 1 ​ Mpc − 1 H_{0}=69.08\pm 0.37~\mathrm{km}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1} , the matter density fraction Ω m = 0.2973 ± 0.0050 \Omega_{m}=0.2973\pm 0.0050 , and the mass fluctuation amplitude σ 8 = 0.815 ± 0.016 \sigma_{8}=0.815\pm 0.016 (or the lensing parameter S 8 ≡ σ 8 ​ Ω m / 0.3 = 0.811 ± 0.016 S_{8}\equiv\sigma_{8}\sqrt{\Omega_{m}/0.3}=0.811\pm 0.016 ), corresponding to 0.6 % 0.6\% , 1.7 % 1.7\% , and 2 % 2\% precision respectively. Adding the Pantheon+ supernova sample (SNe), we find a preference of 2.6 ​ σ 2.6\sigma for the w 0 ​ w a w_{0}w_{a} dynamical dark energy model from low-redshift data alone, which increases to 2.8 ​ σ 2.8\sigma when exchanging the SNe with Planck CMB data. Combining full-shape data with BAO, CMB, and SNe likelihoods, we improve the dark energy figure-of-merit by 18 % 18\% and bound the sum of the neutrino masses to M ν < 0.057 M_{\nu}<0.057 eV in Λ \Lambda CDM and M ν < 0.095 M_{\nu}<0.095 eV in the w 0 ​ w a w_{0}w_{a} dynamical dark energy model (both at 95% CL). This represents an improvement of 25 % 25\% over the background expansion constraints and the strongest bound on neutrino masses in w 0 ​ w a w_{0}w_{a} CDM to date. Our results suggest that the preference for the normal ordering of neutrino mass states holds regardless of the cosmological background model, and is robust in light of tensions between cosmological datasets.

Concordance between cosmological datasets and neutrino oscillation experiments can be resolved by introducing a dynamical dark energy (DDE) component,
i.e.
replacing
\Lambda
CDM

In either case, inferring

In this work, we carry out a new precision analysis of the public DESI data, combining spectroscopic statistics with photometric clustering, CMB lensing, primary CMB anisotropies, BAO, and supernovae. For the first time, we find some evidence for the normal hierarchy of neutrino mass states within the DDE model (as well as the
\Lambda
CDM model, as found in previous works).
To achieve this, we perform a full-shape analysis of galaxy clustering data
Ivanov et al. 2020b
;
D’Amico et al. 2019
;
Chen et al. 2022a
, which leverages a precision theoretical description of galaxy clustering utilizing EFT concepts drawn from particle physics
Baumann et al. 2012
;
Carrasco et al. 2012
;
Ivanov 2022
. Our treatment goes beyond that of the official DESI collaboration
Adame et al. 2024a
in a number of ways:

•

Three-Point Functions
: In all spectroscopic analyses, we analyze both the galaxy power spectrum and bispectrum (as in
(
Chudaykin et al. 2025b
;
Chudaykin et al. 2025a
)
), novelly including the bispectrum quadrupole. This utilizes the quasi-optimal bispectrum estimators introduced in
Chudaykin et al. 2025b
(building on
(
Philcox 2024
)
), which accurately account for the survey window and fiber collision effects via a novel stochastic scheme. We find that the bispectrum offers significant additional constraining power on cosmological parameters.

•

One-Loop Predictions
: We extend the bispectrum likelihood to smaller scales by utilizing a consistent one-loop EFT theory prediction in combination with a robust analysis pipeline. Our computation has been recently presented in
Bakx et al. 2025a
(see
Sefusatti 2009
;

;

;

(which has not previously been applied to data). This facilitates percent-level-accuracy one-loop bispectrum computations for different cosmological models.

•

Combined Probes
: In addition to the two- and three-point functions of the DESI DR1 galaxy samples, we analyze the cross-correlation of both photometric and spectroscopic DESI samples with CMB lensing maps, as well as the photometric auto-correlations. Sailer et al. 2024

(
Maus et al. 2025a

•

Two-dimensional correlators
: We use the angular auto- and cross-correlations

6

6

We apply a normalization correction computed from mocks to the galaxy-CMB cross-correlation measurements due to mode coupling from masking and filtering in the latter

Three-dimensional correlators
:
We measure the power spectrum and bispectrum of each spectroscopic DESI chunk using the quasi-optimal estimators contained in the
PolyBin3D
code
(
Philcox 2024
)
. Our power spectrum measurements are obtained similarly to those of
48

.
We compute the bispectrum across a wider range of scales than in previous works, due to our use of a one-loop theoretical model, and additionally
compute both the monopole and quadrupole moments (
B_{0,2}
), following
(
Ivanov et al. 2023
;
Bakx et al. 2025a
)
. In all cases, we combine measurements from the Northern and Southern Galactic caps, weighting by the survey areas. To compute the statistics, we require a fiducial cosmology – as in
Chudaykin et al. 2025b
, we use the
Planck
2018 best-fit parameters
\{h=0.6736,\omega_{b}=0.02237,\omega_{\rm cdm}=0.1200,N_{\rm ur}=2.0328,\omega_{\nu}=0.00064420\}
assuming a single massive neutrino with
M_{\nu}=0.06
eV
(
Aghanim et al. 2018
)
.

As discussed in
48
, our power spectrum and bispectrum estimators correct for a number of known systematic effects. In particular, we account for the mask utilizing quasi-unwindowed estimators (plus a theory convolution matrix for the power spectrum, akin to the pseudo-
C_{\ell}
approach of
Alonso 2019
), imaging systematics via particle weights (following
(
Adame et al. 2024b
)
), radial integral constraints (based on
(
de Mattia and Ruhlmann-Kleider 2019
)
), and wide-angle effects in the power spectrum (as in
(
Beutler and McDonald 2021
)
). Finally, we account for fiber collisions by removing pairs of points with angular separations below
0.05
degrees, working deterministically for the power spectrum (following
(
Pinon et al. 2025
)
), and stochastically for the bispectrum, using the efficient algorithm introduced in
48
. When computing the bispectrum, we reweight the input data such that the statistic has the same effective redshift as the two-point function following
(
Chen et al. 2024b
)
, which simplifies the theoretical model.

Finally, we compute theoretical covariance matrices for the power spectrum and bispectrum using the FFT-based algorithms in
PolyBin3D
. These account for the survey geometry as well as inhomogeneous noise properties, imaging systematics marginalization (via linear marginalization), and integral constraints, though work in the Gaussian limit (e.g., ignoring trispectrum contributions to the power spectrum covariance). The code requires a fiducial power spectrum, which is obtained via a fit to a power spectrum obtained from an initial analysis run. All spectra, normalizations, fiber collision corrections, and covariances are computed on a high-performance computing platform, with the total computation requiring
\mathcal{O}(10^{4})
CPU-hours.

2.3
Likelihood

Our full DESI likelihood is a product of four contributions:

L_{\rm EFT-FS}=L_{P_{\ell}}\times L_{B_{\ell}}\times L_{\kappa g}^{\rm spec-z}\times L_{\kappa g,gg}^{\rm photo-z}\,.

(3)

The likelihoods for the three-dimensional power spectrum and bispectrum multipoles are given by

\begin{split}&-2\mathop{\rm ln}\nolimits L_{P_{\ell}}=\sum_{X}\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}=0,2,4}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N_{\rm bins}}\mathbb{C}_{ij,\ell\ell^{\prime},XX}^{-1}\\
&(P^{{\rm th},X}_{\ell}(k_{i})-P^{{\rm d},X}_{\ell}(k_{i}))(P^{{\rm th},X}_{\ell^{\prime}}(k_{j})-P^{{\rm d},X}_{\ell^{\prime}}(k_{j}))\,,\\
&-2\mathop{\rm ln}\nolimits L_{B_{\ell}}=\sum_{X}\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}=0,2}\sum_{I,J=1}^{N_{\rm triangles}}\mathbb{C}_{IJ,\ell\ell^{\prime}}^{-1}\\
&\,\times\,(B^{{\rm th},X}_{\ell}(T_{I})-B^{{\rm d},X}_{\ell}(T_{I}))(B^{{\rm th},X}_{\ell^{\prime}}(T_{J})-B^{{\rm d},X}_{\ell^{\prime}}(T_{J}))\,,\end{split}

(4)

where ‘th’ and ‘d’ represent theory and data respectively,
\mathbb{C}
is a covariance matrix,
T_{I}=(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})
is a triplet of wavenumbers satisfying the triangle conditions, and
X
indexes the spectroscopic sample with

X\in\{\rm{BGS},\rm{LRG1},\rm{LRG2},\rm{LRG3},\rm{ELG2},\rm{QSO}\}\,.

(5)

We do not include the cross-covariance between the power spectra and bispectra. As argued in
48
, this is justified as long as our analysis is restricted to large enough scales. In particular,
Ivanov et al. 2022a
explicitly checked that the cross-covariance can be ignored for the tree-level bispectrum likelihood restricted to
k_{\rm max}=0.08~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}
. Similarly, simulation studies found that the use of a joint power and bispectrum likelihood without the cross-covariance does not bias parameter recovery from the tree-level bispectrum
Krause et al. 2024
for
k_{\rm max}=0.08~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}
,
and for the one-loop bispectrum at
k_{\rm max}=0.15~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}

Bakx et al. 2025a
, which match our fiducial scale cuts (see Section
2.4
). Furthermore, our bispectrum covariances ignore non-Gaussian contributions, which can be important in the squeezed limit
(
Biagetti et al. 2021
;
Salvalaggio et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
. However, the squeezed triangles have both a small signal (in the models probed herein) and a large noise and hence do not contribute significantly to the cosmological parameter constraints
Ivanov et al. 2022a
.

To analyze the projected angular statistics, we use the following spectroscopic galaxy-CMB lensing likelihood:

\begin{split}&-2\mathop{\rm ln}\nolimits L_{\kappa g}^{\rm spec-z}=\sum_{X,X^{\prime}}\sum_{\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}}\sum_{L,L^{\prime}=1}^{N_{\rm bins}}\mathbb{C}_{X\kappa,X^{\prime}\kappa^{\prime},LL^{\prime}}^{-1}\\
&\quad\,\times\,(C^{{\rm th},X\kappa}_{L}-C^{{\rm d},X\kappa}_{L})(C^{{\rm th},X^{\prime}\kappa^{\prime}}_{L^{\prime}}-C^{{\rm d},X^{\prime}\kappa^{\prime}}_{L^{\prime}})\end{split}

where
X
and
\kappa
refer to spectroscopic and CMB lensing samples, with

\begin{split}&X\in\{\rm{BGS},\rm{LRG1},\rm{LRG2},\rm{LRG3}\}\,\\
&\kappa\in\{\kappa_{\rm Planck\,PR4},\kappa_{\rm ACT\,DR6}\}\,.\end{split}

(6)

where
L
encodes the harmonic bins. Here, the covariance matrix
\mathbb{C}
accounts for the correlations between the different CMB experiments and galaxy samples. We ignore the cross-covariance between these two-dimensional cross-spectra and our three-dimensional measurements due to the negligible overlap between the sampled modes, following
Taylor and Markovič 2022
. As in
Maus et al. 2025a
,
we do not use the spectroscopic
C_{\ell}^{gg}
data
in our fiducial analysis due to its non-negligible correlation with the three-dimensional power spectra,
P_{\ell}(k)
.

Finally, our photometric likelihood is given by

\begin{split}&-2\mathop{\rm ln}\nolimits L_{gg,\kappa g}^{\rm photo-z}=\sum_{XK,X^{\prime}K^{\prime}}\sum_{L,L^{\prime}=1}^{N_{\rm bins}}\mathbb{C}_{XK,X^{\prime}K^{\prime},LL^{\prime}}^{-1}\\
&\quad\,\times\,(C^{{\rm th},XK}_{L}-C^{{\rm d},XK}_{L})(C^{{\rm th},X^{\prime}K^{\prime}}_{L^{\prime}}-C^{{\rm d},X^{\prime}K^{\prime}}_{L^{\prime}})\,,\end{split}

(7)

where
XK
runs over the six galaxy auto-spectra constructed from the photometric of Table
1
, and the twelve cross-spectra formed with the
Planck
PR4 and ACT DR6 lensing maps.
Note that our photometric likelihood includes both auto- and cross-correlation statistics, unlike the two-dimensional spectroscopic likelihood.
The covariance matrix
\mathbb{C}
includes all relevant Gaussian cross-covariances,
i.e.

\mathrm{Cov}[C^{\kappa_{a}g_{i}},C^{\kappa_{b}g_{j}}]
,

\mathrm{Cov}[C^{g_{i}g_{i}},C^{\kappa_{a}g_{j}}]
, and
\mathrm{Cov}[C^{g_{i}g_{i}},C^{g_{j}g_{j}}]
, where
i,j
and
a,b
indicate the photometric and lensing samples respectively.

We do not analyze the cross-correlations between different spectroscopic or photometric galaxy samples (
i.e.

C_{\ell}^{g_{i}g_{j}}
for
i\neq j
). In principle, these were
measured in
Sailer et al. 2024
;
Sailer et al. 2025b
, and could be used in cosmological analyses. In practice, this would require additional assumptions on the time evolution of EFT parameters, and raise the issue of small-scale sensitivity, as these correlations receive significant contributions from magnification, which is challenging to model within EFT. Since the signal from cross-correlations is significantly smaller than that from auto-correlations, we do not expect that their excision will significantly degrade cosmological constraints
Sailer et al. 2024
.

Finally, we note that we do not combine the lensing auto-spectrum (
C_{\ell}^{\kappa\kappa}
) with the galaxy auto- or lensing cross-spectra (
C_{\ell}^{gg}
or
C_{\ell}^{\kappa g}
), since the covariance
between these probes is not readily available. To estimate the impact of this effect, we performed a linear theory Fisher forecast for the
\sigma_{8}
inference from the full combination of DESI photometric galaxy and
Planck
lensing two-point correlations:
C_{\ell}^{gg},C_{\ell}^{g\kappa}
and
C_{\ell}^{\kappa\kappa}
. The joint forecast yields
\approx 1\%
error on
\sigma_{8}
, consistent
with that seen in the actual data. When neglecting the cross-covariance
between
C_{\ell}^{\kappa\kappa}
and [
C_{\ell}^{gg},C_{\ell}^{g\kappa}
], we find that the forecasted error on
\sigma_{8}
is overestimated by
12\%
, representing most of the statistical improvements from adding
C_{\ell}^{g\kappa}
. Though this error is not very significant, particularly when one combines with the CMB primary likelihood, we omit the
C_{\ell}^{\kappa\kappa}
likelihood when analyzing [
C_{\ell}^{gg},C_{\ell}^{g\kappa}
] to be rigorous.
In Appendix
B
we check that
a joint analysis of
C_{\ell}^{\kappa\kappa}
and
[
C_{\ell}^{gg},C_{\ell}^{g\kappa}
]
data without the cross-covariance
leads to only marginal improvements
over our fiducial analysis.

We sample our likelihoods using the Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) technique, implemented via the
Montepython
code
Audren et al. 2013
;
Brinckmann and Lesgourgues 2019
.
We consider the chains converged when they satisfy the Gelman-Rubin
convergence criterion
Gelman and Rubin 1992
with
R-1<0.02
for all sampled parameters. For frequentist tests, we compute best-fit parameters by minimizing the likelihood with a Jeffreys prior, which is exactly equivalent to minimizing the full unmarginalized likelihood.

.

. These scale cuts have been calibrated with various suites of simulations covering LRG, ELG and QSO-like tracers

Ivanov et al. 2020b
;
Chudaykin et al. 2021a
;
Chudaykin et al. 2021b
;
Ivanov et al. 2022a

Ivanov 2021

;
Ivanov et al. 2023
;

Our scale cuts ensure that systematic errors due to two-loop corrections to the power spectrum and bispectrum are subdominant. {k_{\text{max}}}

Chudaykin et al. 2024
;
Bakx et al. 2025a

This was previously demonstrated in analyses of BOSS-like LRG galaxies in synthetic PTChallenge and Nseries simulations, with

(4-5)\%

From a theoretical standpoint, the shifts in

3
Theoretical Model

In this section, we describe our theoretical model of the galaxy power spectrum and bispectrum, as well as the cross-correlation with lensing. Our treatment of the former follows
48
, but is extended to consistently include the one-loop bispectrum, following
Bakx et al. 2025a
. Whilst the theoretical description of each observable has already been presented in the literature
(
Ivanov et al. 2020b
;
Chudaykin et al. 2020
;
Ivanov et al. 2022a
;
Philcox and Ivanov 2022
;
Chen et al. 2024b
;
Bakx et al. 2025a
;
D’Amico et al. 2025
, e.g.,)
, we find it useful to recapitulate the relevant predictions in a unified framework, since we are undertaking a joint analysis of multiple different large-scale structure statistics for the first time. This ensures a unified theoretical framework, which is particularly important for the treatment of nuisance parameters entering the various statistics. In addition, the inclusion of the one-loop bispectrum requires a
new basis of nuisance parameters, which differs from that used in the previous analyses based on the one-loop power spectrum and tree-level bispectrum.

Spectroscopic Power Spectra

We utilize the one-loop EFT prediction for the anisotropic galaxy clustering in redshift space, as implemented in the public

We model the effects of neutrinos on galaxy clustering via the Einstein-de-Sitter approximation,
supplemented with the so-called “cb” prescription
Chudaykin and Ivanov 2019

Ivanov et al. 2020a

Villaescusa-Navarro et al. 2014

of the baryons and cold dark matter fluid (which depends implicitly on

(
Aviles et al. 2021
;
Noriega et al. 2022

; given the current error-bars on

and

A_{s}
and
M_{\nu}
in the LSS data
Ivanov et al. 2020a
.
To further test this assumption, we obtained measurements of

(\Omega_{m},H_{0},\sigma_{8})
in two analyses: (a) assuming a single

eV); (b) assuming a

Compared to previous literature, we use a different basis for the redshift-space counterterms

Philcox et al. 2022
;
D’Amico et al. 2022a

\begin{split}P^{{\rm(s)}}_{\rm ctr}({\bm{k}})=&2Z_{1}^{\rm ctr}({\bm{k}})Z_{1}({\bm{k}})P_{\rm lin}(k)\,,\\
Z_{1}^{\rm ctr}({\bm{k}})=&\frac{k^{2}}{k_{\rm NL}^{2}}\bigg(-b_{\nabla^{2}\delta}+\left(e_{1}-\frac{1}{2}c_{1}f\right)f\mu^{2}-\frac{1}{2}c_{2}f^{2}\mu^{4}\bigg),\end{split}

(16)

;
Chudaykin et al. 2025a

Finally, we include a higher-order counterterm
\tilde{c}
as in previous works:

Z_{1}^{\rm ctr}({\bm{k}})\supset-\frac{\tilde{c}}{2}\frac{k^{4}}{k_{\rm NL}^{4}}f^{4}\mu^{4}f^{4}Z_{1}({\bm{k}})\,.

(17)

Though formally next-order in perturbation theory, this is introduced to account
for the large velocity dispersion (

“fingers-of-God”

) of
massive galaxies such as the LRG sample. The importance of this counterterm for

(
Ivanov et al. 2020b
;

;

The stochasticity contribution includes both scale-independent and scale-dependent parts,

P^{{\rm(s)}}_{\rm stoch}({\bm{k}})=\frac{1}{\bar{n}}\left(P_{\rm shot}+a_{0}\frac{k^{2}}{k^{2}_{\rm NL}}+a_{2}\mu^{2}\frac{k^{2}}{k^{2}_{\rm NL}}\right)~\,,

(18)

for mean number density
\bar{n}
, where we have subtracted the Poisson contribution, such that
P_{\rm shot}
has a prior-mean of zero.

To account for non-perturbative long-wavelength displacements, we apply infrared (IR) resummation using the approach based on time-sliced perturbation theory
Blas et al. 2016a
;
Blas et al. 2016b
;
Ivanov and Sibiryakov 2018
;
Vasudevan et al. 2019

(k_{\rm true},\mu_{\rm true})

\begin{split}k_{\rm true}^{2}&=k_{\rm obs}^{2}\left[q_{\parallel}^{-2}\mu_{\rm obs}^{2}+q_{\perp}^{-2}(1-\mu_{\rm obs}^{2})\right]\,,\\
\mu_{\rm true}^{2}&=q_{\parallel}^{-2}\mu_{\rm obs}^{2}\left[q_{\parallel}^{-2}\mu_{\rm obs}^{2}+q_{\perp}^{-2}(1-\mu_{\rm obs}^{2})\right]^{-1}\,,\end{split}

(19)

where the Alcock-Paczynski parameters are defined as
(

q_{\parallel}=\frac{H_{\rm fid}(z)}{H_{\rm true}(z)}\frac{H_{0,\rm true}}{H_{0,\rm fid}}\,,\quad q_{\perp}=\frac{D_{{\rm true},A}(z)}{D_{{\rm fid},A}(z)}\frac{H_{0,\rm true}}{H_{0,\rm fid}}\,.

(20)

Here

(21)

\displaystyle Z_{2}({\bm{k}}_{1},{\bm{k}}_{2})=\frac{b_{2}}{2}+b_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}\left(\frac{({\bm{k}}_{1}\cdot{\bm{k}}_{2})^{2}}{k_{1}^{2}k_{2}^{2}}-1\right)+b_{1}F_{2}({\bm{k}}_{1},{\bm{k}}_{2})+f\mu^{2}G_{2}({\bm{k}}_{1},{\bm{k}}_{2})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad~~+\frac{f\mu k}{2}\left(\frac{\mu_{1}}{k_{1}}(b_{1}+f\mu_{2}^{2})+\frac{\mu_{2}}{k_{2}}(b_{1}+f\mu_{1}^{2})\right)\,,\quad\text{where}

(24a)

\displaystyle F_{2}({\bm{k}}_{1},{\bm{k}}_{2})=\frac{5}{7}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{({\bm{k}}_{1}\cdot{\bm{k}}_{2})}{k_{1}^{2}}+\frac{({\bm{k}}_{1}\cdot{\bm{k}}_{2})}{k_{2}^{2}}\right)+\frac{2}{7}\frac{({\bm{k}}_{1}\cdot{\bm{k}}_{2})^{2}}{k_{1}^{2}k_{2}^{2}}\,,

(24b)

\displaystyle G_{2}({\bm{k}}_{1},{\bm{k}}_{2})=\frac{3}{7}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{({\bm{k}}_{1}\cdot{\bm{k}}_{2})}{k_{1}^{2}}+\frac{({\bm{k}}_{1}\cdot{\bm{k}}_{2})}{k_{2}^{2}}\right)+\frac{4}{7}\frac{({\bm{k}}_{1}\cdot{\bm{k}}_{2})^{2}}{k_{1}^{2}k_{2}^{2}}\,.

(24c)

B^{\rm tree~(s)}_{\rm stoch}

captures the three-point stochasticity:

B^{\rm tree~(s)}_{\rm stoch}=\frac{A_{\rm shot}}{\bar{n}^{2}}\,,

(25)

while
B_{\rm mixed}
is the mixed deterministic-stochastic contribution, with a structure similar to the Poissonian prediction
\propto P/\bar{n}

Peebles 1980
;
Baldauf et al. 2016
:

B^{\rm tree~(s)}_{\rm mixed}=\frac{b_{1}B_{\rm shot}+f\mu^{2}P_{\rm shot}}{\bar{n}}P_{\rm lin}(k_{1})Z_{1}({\bm{k}}_{1})+\text{cyc.}

(26)

Finally,
B_{\rm ctr}
is a phenomenological term that acts as a proxy
for the one-loop counterterms, whose contribution is important
even on large scales. This is obtained by adding a fingers-of-God contribution to the
linear theory kernel,
i.e.
using

Z^{\rm FoG}_{1}({\bm{k}})=(b_{1}+f\mu^{2})-\tilde{c}_{1}\mu^{2}\frac{k^{2}}{k_{\rm NL}^{2}}

(27)

instead of
Z_{1}({\bm{k}})
in the deterministic contribution, with
k_{\rm NL}=0.45~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}

Ivanov et al. 2022b

\tilde{c}_{1}
. Note that we include the
\tilde{c}_{1}
term only when performing tree-level bispectrum analyses, since our one-loop likelihood has EFT counterterms that consistently account for all fingers-of-God effects at order
\mathcal{O}(k^{2}/k_{\rm FoG}^{2})
.

We account for tree-level IR resummation in the bispectrum using the formalism of time-sliced perturbation theory
Ivanov and Sibiryakov 2018
(see also
Chen et al. 2024c
).
To compare theory and data, we compute the multipoles via the continuous definition
(
Scoccimarro et al. 1999b
;
Scoccimarro 2015
;
Ivanov et al. 2023
)
,

\begin{split}&B_{\ell}^{\rm ideal}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})=(2\ell+1)\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{d\mu_{1}}{2}\\
&\times B(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3})\mathcal{L}_{\ell}(\mu_{1})\,,\end{split}

(28)

where we order the wavenumbers in triangle configurations as
k_{1}\geq k_{2}\geq k_{3}
, such that the the angular integration is performed with respect to the cosine between the line-of-sight and the largest wavevector. The angles
\mu_{2},\mu_{3}
are defined by the polar and azimuthal angles
\mu
and
\phi
through
\mu_{1}=\mu
,

\mu_{2}=\mu\cos\zeta-\chi\sin\zeta,\qquad\mu_{3}=-\frac{k_{1}}{k_{3}}\mu_{1}-\frac{k_{2}}{k_{3}}\mu_{2}

(29)

where
\chi\equiv\sqrt{1-\mu^{2}}\cos\phi
,
\cos\zeta\equiv\hat{{\bm{k}}}_{1}\cdot\hat{{\bm{k}}}_{2}

(
Scoccimarro 2015
;
Philcox et al. 2022
;
Bakx et al. 2025a
, e.g.,)
.

As discussed in
(
Philcox et al. 2022
)
, it is important to account for the effects of finite bin widths in the bispectrum. This is achieved by computing the binned statistic following the approach of
Ivanov et al. 2022a
;
Ivanov et al. 2023
, recently streamlined in
Bakx et al. 2025a
:

\begin{split}B_{\ell}(\bar{k}_{1},\bar{k}_{2},\bar{k}_{3})&=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}(\bar{k}_{1},\bar{k}_{2},\bar{k}_{3})}\frac{2\ell+1}{\alpha_{\parallel}^{2}\alpha_{\perp}^{4}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\left[\int_{\bar{k}_{i}-\Delta k_{i}/2}^{\bar{k}_{i}+\Delta k_{i}/2}k_{i}dk_{i}\right]\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{d\mu_{1}}{2}\\
&\mathcal{I}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},\mu,\phi)\,B(k_{1}^{\prime},k_{2}^{\prime},k_{3}^{\prime},\mu_{1}^{\prime},\mu_{2}^{\prime},\mu_{3}^{\prime})\mathcal{L}_{\ell}(\mu_{1})\,,\quad\text{where}\\
\mathcal{N}(\bar{k}_{1},\bar{k}_{2},\bar{k}_{3})&=\prod_{i=1}^{3}\left[\int_{\bar{k}_{i}-\Delta k/2}^{\bar{k}_{i}+\Delta k_{i}/2}k_{i}dk_{i}\right]\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{d\mu_{1}}{2}\mathcal{I}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},\mu_{1},\phi)\,.\end{split}

(30)

k_{i}\in[\bar{k}_{i}-\Delta k_{i}/2,\bar{k}_{i}+\Delta k_{i}/2)
are linearly spaced bins, whose centers satisfy the triangle conditions,
i.e.

|\bar{k}_{1}-\bar{k}_{2}|\leq\bar{k}_{3}\leq\bar{k}_{1}+\bar{k}_{2}
, and we restrict to
\bar{k}_{1}\geq\bar{k}_{2}\geq\bar{k}_{3}
as before. Here,
\mathcal{I}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},\mu,\phi)
is a weighting function equal to one if
{\bm{k}}_{1},{\bm{k}}_{2},{\bm{k}}_{3}
obey the triangle conditions and zero otherwise. In
Ivanov et al. 2022a
;
Ivanov et al. 2023
, the
\mathcal{I}
function was not included, and weights were introduced to account for the differences between the continuous integral of (
30
) and the discrete computation on a grid of wavenumbers. When
\mathcal{I}
is included, these weights are equal to one with negligibly small corrections (for
B_{0}
and
B_{2}
), thus do not need to be included
Bakx et al. 2025a
.

Finally, we account for coordinate distortions in the bispectrum by treating
all observed momenta and angles in (
30
) as functions of the true
momenta and angles as in (

) (see see
Ivanov et al. 2022a
;

;

3.3
Spectroscopic Bispectra at One-Loop

At one-loop order, the redshift-space galaxy bispectrum is given by

\begin{split}B_{\rm 1-loop}&=B^{\rm tree~(s)}_{\rm det}[b_{1},b_{2},b_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}]+B^{\rm 1-loop~(s)}_{\rm det}[b_{1},b_{2},b_{\mathcal{G}_{2}},b_{\Gamma_{3}},b_{3},\gamma_{2}^{\times},\gamma_{3},\gamma_{21}^{\times},\gamma_{211},\gamma_{22},\gamma_{31}]\\
&+B^{\rm ctr~(s)~I}[b_{1},b_{\nabla^{2}\delta},b_{\nabla^{2}\delta^{2}},b_{\nabla^{2}\mathcal{G}_{2}},b_{(\nabla\delta)^{2}},b_{(\nabla t)^{2}},e_{1},e_{5},c_{1},...,c_{7}]+B^{\rm ctr~(s)~II}[b_{1},b_{2},b_{\mathcal{G}_{2}},b_{\nabla^{2}\delta},e_{1},c_{1},c_{2}]\\
&+B_{\rm mixed}^{\rm tree~(s)}[b_{1},P_{\rm shot},B_{\rm shot}]+B_{\rm mixed}^{\rm 1-loop~(s)}[b_{1},b_{2},b_{\mathcal{G}_{2}},b_{\Gamma_{3}}P_{\rm shot},B_{\rm shot},d_{2},d_{\mathcal{G}_{2}},d_{\Gamma_{3}}]\\
&+B_{\rm mixed}^{\rm ctr~(s)~I}[b_{1},a_{0},a_{3},...,a_{5},a_{7},...,a_{12}]+B_{\rm mixed}^{\rm ctr~(s)~II}[b_{1},P_{\rm shot},B_{\rm shot},b_{\nabla^{2}\delta},e_{1},c_{1},c_{2},\tilde{c}]\\
&+B_{\rm stoch}[A_{\rm shot},a_{1},a_{6}]\,.\end{split}

(31)

Bakx et al. 2025a
(see also
Eggemeier et al. 2018
;
Eggemeier et al. 2021
;
Philcox et al. 2022
;
D’Amico et al. 2022a
). Here,
B^{\rm tree~(s)}_{\rm det}
and
B^{\rm 1-loop~(s)}_{\rm det}
refer to mode-coupling contributions,
B^{\rm ctr~(s)~I,II}
are deterministic higher-derivative corrections,
B^{\rm tree~(s)}_{\rm mixed}
and
B^{\rm 1-loop~(s)}_{\rm mixed}
are the mixed deterministic-stochastic terms at leading- and next-to-leading order respectively,
B^{\rm ctr~(s)~I,II}_{\rm mixed}
are mixed terms involving higher derivative deterministic and stochastic corrections,
while
B_{\rm stoch}
is a purely stochastic contribution, which now includes scale-dependent bispectrum stochasticity.
The explicit expressions for these terms are given in Appendix
A
. Together with the one-loop power spectrum prediction, this involves 45 free parameters, which parametrize all relevant baryonic and galaxy formation effects at one-loop order. These are treated as independent for each spectroscopic DESI redshift bin, and, upon marginalization, effectively induce a smooth correlated covariance in the bispectrum likelihood. To account for long-wavelength displacements, we use the efficient one-loop IR resummation technique outlined in
Ivanov and Sibiryakov 2018
and implemented in
Philcox et al. 2022
. In particular, we apply the full anisotropic damping to the tree-level bispectrum expressions and use an isotropic damping approximation for the one-loop components. We treat the binning effects in the one-loop bispectrum via (
30
), as in the tree-level analysis.

Due to the large number of convolution integrals contained within
B^{\rm 1-loop~(s)}_{\rm det}
and the many triangle configurations analyzed, computing the one-loop corrections to the bispectrum is much more expensive than for the power spectrum, even with analytic methods such as
fftlog

Simonovic et al. 2018
. This prohibits full cosmological studies, where one wishes to scan over non-linear cosmological parameters, which requires many evaluations of the bispectrum model. To ameliorate this, one can decompose the bispectrum into a suitable cosmology-independent basis, such that the expensive pieces of the calculation can be pre-computed and the cosmology-dependent result can be assembled as a matrix multiplication. Here, we use the
cobra
technique of
Bakx et al. 2025b
(see also
(
D’Amico et al. 2022a
;
Anastasiou et al. 2024
)
), which employs a singular-value decomposition of the input IR-resumed tree-level power spectrum
Blas et al. 2016b
(see also
Philcox et al. 2021
;
Chen et al. 2024c
), allowing the full cosmological parameter prior to be expressed using
N_{\rm cobra}=4
basis functions. After pre-computing the
N_{\rm cobra}^{3}
loop integrals using
fftlog

(
Simonovic et al. 2018
)
and applying the bin integration, we can compute the full one-loop bispectrum in less than a second. To implement the coordinate-distortion effect
on the one-loop corrections, we perform a linear-order expansion in
q_{\parallel},q_{\perp}
, pre-computing all terms. We refer the reader to
Bakx et al. 2025a
for further details on this procedure.

3.4
Projected Galaxy Clustering & CMB Lensing Cross-Correlations

To predict the angular power spectrum of galaxies and their cross-correlation with CMB lensing, we use the one-loop EFT models implemented in
CLASS-PT

Chudaykin et al. 2020
.
Specifically, the galaxy auto-spectrum depends on the real-space galaxy-galaxy power spectrum:

\begin{split}&P^{\rm(r)}_{\rm gg}(k)=b_{1}^{2}P_{\rm lin}(k)+P^{\rm(r)}_{\rm 1-loop,gg}[b_{1},b_{2},b_{\mathcal{G}_{2}},b_{\Gamma_{3}}](k)\\
&+P^{\rm(r)}_{\rm ctr,gg}[b_{\nabla^{2}\delta}](k)+P^{\rm(r)}_{\rm stoch,gg}[P_{\rm shot},a_{0}](k)\,,\end{split}

(32)

where the superscript (r) indicates real space. The real-space model can be obtained from the redshift-space formulae defined above by setting all redshift-space quantities to zero,
i.e.
with
f=a_{2}=0
. In this limit, the counterterm and stochastic contributions simplify significantly:

\begin{split}&P^{\rm(r)}_{\rm ctr,gg}(k)=-2b_{\nabla^{2}\delta}\frac{k^{2}}{k_{\rm NL}^{2}}b_{1}P_{\rm lin}(k)\,,\\
&P^{\rm(r)}_{\rm stoch,gg}(k)=\frac{1}{\bar{n}}\left(P_{\rm shot}+a_{0}\frac{k^{2}}{k_{\rm NL}^{2}}\right)~\,.\end{split}

(33)

where
b_{\nabla^{2}\delta}
is a higher-derivative bias, which absorbs the dark matter sound-speed term
c_{s}/k_{\rm NL}^{2}
(which itself contains corrections due to baryonic
feedback
Lewandowski et al. 2015
).

To model the cross-correlation of galaxies and both CMB lensing and magnification bias, we require the cross-spectrum of galaxies and matter at one-loop order:

\begin{split}&P^{\rm(r)}_{\rm gm}(k)=b_{1}P_{\rm lin}(k)+P^{\rm(r)}_{\rm 1-loop,gm}[b_{1},b_{2},b_{\mathcal{G}_{2}},b_{\Gamma_{3}}](k)\\
&+P^{\rm(r)}_{\rm ctr,gm}[b_{\nabla^{2}\delta},c_{s}](k)+P_{\rm stoch,gm}^{\rm(r)}[a^{\rm gm}_{0}](k)\,.\end{split}

(34)

The deterministic one-loop piece is given in
Chudaykin et al. 2020
;
Chudaykin et al. 2021b
, and the counterterm contribution is

P^{\rm(r)}_{\rm ctr,gm}(k)=-(b_{\nabla^{2}\delta}+c_{s}b_{1})\frac{k^{2}}{k_{\rm NL}^{2}}P_{\rm lin}(k)\,.

(35)

where we use a different convention to previous works in order to match the one-loop bispectrum counterterm parametrization
Bakx et al. 2025a
. Note that the degeneracy between
b_{\nabla^{2}\delta}
and
c_{s}
is broken when we analyze both the galaxy cross- and auto-spectrum.
The stochastic contribution is obtained by cross-correlating the matter and galaxy stochastic density components. Due to mass and momentum conservation, the latter scales like
k^{2}
in the
k\to 0
limit
Baumann et al. 2012
;
Carrasco et al. 2012
;
Ivanov 2022
, while the former goes as
k^{0}

Assassi et al. 2014
;
Desjacques et al. 2018
, implying that the cross-spectrum
in the
k\to 0
limit
must be given by

P_{\rm stoch,gm}^{\rm(r)}=a^{\rm gm}_{0}\frac{k^{2}}{k_{\rm NL}^{2}}\frac{1}{\bar{n}}\,,

(36)

where
a^{\rm gm}_{0}
is an order-one dimensionless constant.

An important contribution to the galaxy cross-spectrum is sourced by magnification and its cross-correlation with CMB lensing. To describe these effects, we require a
theoretical model for matter clustering,
i.e.
the real-space matter power spectrum
P_{mm}
.
Due to the wide redshift-extent of the magnification and lensing kernels, this necessitates modeling
P_{mm}
down to small scales, where the EFT description does not provide a well-controlled approximation. Even on large-scales, the contributions depend on the time-evolution of
P_{mm}
, which, in EFT models, is not known
a priori
.
To ameliorate this, we adopt a phenomenological model for the matter power spectrum,
which acts as a Padé resummed version of the one-loop EFT prediction, supplemented with higher order counterterms
Chen et al. 2024b
(see also
(
Philcox et al. 2020a
)

for an alternative approach):

\begin{split}P^{\rm(r)}_{\rm mm}(k,z)=&P_{\rm lin}(k,z)+P^{\rm(r)}_{\rm 1-loop,mm}(k,z)\\
&+\frac{2\alpha(z)k^{2}P_{\rm lin}(k,z)}{1+\beta(z)k^{4}}\,,\end{split}

(37)

where we have restored the explicit time-dependence of the counterterm coefficients. In the
k\to 0
limit this reduces to the usual one-loop EFT prediction
(
Carrasco et al. 2012
;
Chudaykin et al. 2020
, e.g.,)
,

P^{\rm(r)}_{\rm mm}(k,z)=P_{\rm lin}(k,z)+P^{\rm(r)}_{\rm 1-loop,mm}(k,z)+2\alpha(z)k^{2}P_{\rm lin}(k)\,.

(38)

When computing the one-loop term, we use the Einstein-de-Sitter approximation to compute the time-dependence (ignoring IR resummation effects, which violate this scaling, though are largely irrelevant for projected statistics):

P^{\rm(r)}_{\rm 1-loop,mm}(k,z)=D^{4}_{+}(z)P^{\rm(r)}_{\rm 1-loop,mm}(k,z=0)\,

(39)

where
D_{+}(z)
is the growth factor. We fit the parameters
\alpha(z)
and
\beta(z)
appearing in (
37
) to the
HMcode
output
Mead et al. 2015
, using a redshift grid spanning
z\in[0,1]
.
Chen et al. 2024b
found that a simple two-parameter model for each fitting parameter:

f=f_{0}\times[D_{+}(z)]^{N_{f}}\,,

(40)

for
f\in\{\alpha,\beta\}
, provides a
\lesssim 3\%
accurate match to
HMcode
matter power spectrum on non-linear scales
0.4\leq k/(h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1})\leq 1
across this redshift range – this will be adopted henceforth to describe the magnification bias contributions. Whilst our simple model clearly does not fully capture the complex physics of dark matter clustering on small scales, this is not a concern for our analysis, given that our main goal to have a rough model that does not bias the extraction of cosmological parameters from the angular clustering observables.
We further note that the galaxy magnification is not used to model cross-spectra between different photometric samples since we do not include the galaxy cross-spectra
(
C_{\ell}^{g_{i}g_{j}}
for
i\neq j

in

;

Under the Limber-Kaiser approximation
Limber 1953
;
Kaiser 1992
;
Kaiser 1998

statistics from their three-dimensional real-space counterparts, by first forming the building blocks

\hat{C}_{\ell}^{XY}=\int d\chi\frac{W^{X}(\chi)W^{Y}(\chi)}{\chi^{2}}P_{XY}\left(k=\frac{\ell+1/2}{\chi},z(\chi)\right)\,,

(41)

where
\chi
is the comoving distance,
W^{X,Y}

\begin{split}&W^{g}(\chi)=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}H(z)\frac{dN}{dz}~\,,\\

(42)

\begin{split}&C_{\ell}^{\kappa g}=\hat{C}_{\ell}^{\kappa g}+\hat{C}_{\ell}^{\kappa\mu}\,,\\
&C_{\ell}^{gg}=\hat{C}_{\ell}^{gg}+2\hat{C}_{\ell}^{g\mu}+\hat{C}_{\ell}^{\mu\mu}\,,\end{split}

(44)

\begin{split}&P_{\kappa g}=P_{\mu g}=P_{\rm gm}\,,\quad P_{\kappa\mu}=P_{\kappa\kappa}=P_{\mu\mu}\equiv P_{\rm mm}\,.\end{split}

(45)

In practice, we can adopt the effective redshift approximation for the galaxy auto- and cross-correlations, due to a narrow width of the

\begin{split}&P_{\rm gg/gm}\left(k=\frac{\ell+1/2}{\chi},z(\chi)\right)\\
&~~~~~~~~\approx P_{\rm gg/gm}\left(k=\frac{\ell+1/2}{\chi},z_{\rm eff}\right)\,,\end{split}

5
Discussion and Conclusions

In this work we have presented the most complete analysis of the public DESI galaxy
clustering data to date, incorporating the three-dimensional galaxy power spectrum and bispectrum
multipoles, the projected angular two-point correlations of DESI photometric galaxies
and the cross-correlations of both photometric and spectroscopic galaxies with CMB lensing maps from
Planck
and ACT. Notably, ours is the first analysis of DESI data to feature a consistent one-loop model for both the galaxy power spectrum and bispectrum, utilizing the systematic EFT prescription based on power counting rules.
On the analysis side, we carefully account for a wide variety of systematic effects including the survey geometry, fiber collisions, integral constraints, and binning in all relevant statistics. We also vary the one-loop EFT predictions as we scan over cosmology using the
cobra
technique – this allows us to achieve an unprecedented subpercent accuracy in the one-loop EFT computations at minimal computational cost.

Our work provides some of the strongest constraints on both the late-time matter abundance and the growth of structure. Specifically, in the
\Lambda{\rm CDM}
model we find a 2% limit on the mass fluctuation amplitude
\sigma_{8}=0.815\pm 0.016
, which is consistent and
competitive with the
\Lambda{\rm CDM}
bounds derived the from primary CMB data, e.g.,
\sigma_{8}=0.811\pm 0.006
from
Planck
2018
Planck Collaboration et al. 2020
or
\sigma_{8}=0.8113\pm 0.005
from
Planck
+ACT
Louis et al. 2025
.
Our bound compares favorably to previous constraints from large-scale galaxy clustering, though remains somewhat shy of the CMB lensing - unWISE galaxy correlation result
\sigma_{8}=0.814\pm 0.010

Farren et al. 2025
.
Other notable literature constraints include the lensing amplitude measurements from
3\times 2
-point analyses (including cosmic shear, galaxy clustering, and galaxy-galaxy lensing) using the DESI DR1 full-shape dataset combined with weak lensing maps from KiDS, DES, and HSC
Semenaite et al. 2025

adopt a simulation-based model for small-scale projected galaxy clustering and galaxy-galaxy lensing, finding

Our result is also consistent

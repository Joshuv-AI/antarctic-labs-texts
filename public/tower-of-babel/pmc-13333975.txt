Association between early postoperative coefficient of variation of heart rate and delayed atrial fibrillation following sublobar lung resection: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Haiyang Hu, Haiyang Guo, Jiang Fu, Haining Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSublobar resection is increasingly established as a standard of care for early-stage non-small cell lung cancer, yet predictors of delayed postoperative atrial fibrillation (POAF) in this specific population remain understudied. This retrospective cohort study utilized the MIMIC-IV database to investigate whether early postoperative coefficient of variation of heart rate (HR-CV) independently predicts delayed POAF (> 24 h) in 348 patients requiring intensive care after wedge resection or segmentectomy. Unlike traditional static risk factors, continuous monitoring data were analyzed to capture dynamic autonomic fluctuations. The incidence of delayed POAF was 16.7%. Multivariable logistic regression demonstrated that elevated HR-CV in the first 24 postoperative hours was significantly associated with delayed POAF (p = 0.003), whereas coefficient of variation of systolic blood pressure (SBP-CV) showed no significant association. Adding HR-CV to a clinical baseline model (age, hypertension, and heart failure) improved the Area Under the Curve (AUC) from 0.690 to 0.722. These findings suggest that autonomic dysregulation, rather than systemic hemodynamic instability, acts as a primary driver of arrhythmia in this cohort. Introduction

Postoperative atrial fibrillation (POAF) is the most common sustained arrhythmia after non-cardiac thoracic surgery, with a reported incidence ranging from 10% to 20% depending on the extent of resection and patient characteristics1–3. Although the incidence of POAF after sublobar resection is generally lower than that after lobectomy or pneumonectomy, it remains clinically significant because even short-lived episodes of POAF are associated with a 2- to 4-fold increased risk of stroke, prolonged hospital length of stay, and increased long-term mortality4–6.

The pathophysiology of POAF is multifactorial, involving oxidative stress, inflammation, and sympathetic hyperactivation induced by surgical trauma7,8. Traditional risk prediction models have largely relied on static preoperative variables such as age, sex, and history of hypertension9. However, these static factors fail to capture the dynamic physiological changes occurring in the acute postoperative phase—specifically, the fluctuations in autonomic tone that often precede the onset of arrhythmia10. Emerging evidence suggests that autonomic dysregulation, characterized by an imbalance between sympathetic and parasympathetic activity, plays a pivotal role in the initiation of atrial fibrillation11,12.

Heart rate variability (HRV), derived from continuous electrocardiogram monitoring, is a well-established non-invasive marker of autonomic nervous system function13. Previous studies in cardiac surgery have suggested that reduced or aberrant HRV predicts POAF14,15. However, traditional HRV analysis (e.g., frequency-domain spectral analysis) necessitates high-fidelity beat-to-beat R-R interval data and complex artifact removal, which restricts its utility to specialized research settings requiring Holter monitors. In the fast-paced intensive care unit (ICU) environment, such granular data is often unavailable or impractical for real-time risk stratification. In contrast, the coefficient of variation of heart rate (HR-CV)—derived from standard continuous telemetry logs—has emerged as a pragmatic and accessible “bedside” surrogate16. Unlike complex metrics that require retrospective signal processing, HR-CV utilizes routinely available vital sign data to capture macroscopic autonomic instability, offering superior translational potential for widespread clinical adoption17.

This study aimed to investigate whether early postoperative HR-CV, as a surrogate for autonomic instability, independently predicts delayed-onset POAF in patients undergoing sublobar lung resection. Additionally, we sought to compare the predictive value of HR-CV against the coefficient of variation of systolic blood pressure variability (SBP-CV) to determine whether autonomic dysregulation or hemodynamic fluctuation is the predominant driver of POAF in this specific population.

Methods

Study design and data source

This retrospective cohort study was conducted using the Medical Information Mart for Intensive Care IV (MIMIC-IV, version 3.1) database18. MIMIC-IV is a large, single-center, publicly available critical care database containing de-identified electronic health records of patients admitted to the Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center (Boston, MA) between 2008 and 2019. Access to the database was granted after completion of the National Institutes of Health Protecting Human Research Participants web-based training course (Certification Number: 74066696). The requirement for informed consent was waived by the Institutional Review Boards of the Massachusetts Institute of Technology and Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center due to the retrospective, de-identified nature of the data. This study is reported in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines19.

Study population

We identified adult patients (aged ≥ 18 years) who underwent sublobar lung resection (wedge resection or segmentectomy) using International Classification of Diseases, Ninth and Tenth Revision (ICD-9 and ICD-10) procedure codes.

Patients were excluded if they:

Underwent major resection (lobectomy or pneumonectomy) during the same hospitalization;

Underwent major resection (lobectomy or pneumonectomy) during the same hospitalization;

Had a documented history of preoperative atrial fibrillation or atrial flutter;

Had a documented history of preoperative atrial fibrillation or atrial flutter;

Had an ICU length of stay < 24 h, preventing the collection of adequate monitoring data;

Had an ICU length of stay < 24 h, preventing the collection of adequate monitoring data;

Developed POAF within the first 24 h after ICU admission (early-onset POAF).

Developed POAF within the first 24 h after ICU admission (early-onset POAF).

The exclusion of early-onset cases was implemented to strictly separate the exposure monitoring window from the outcome event.

Data extraction and variable definition

The primary exposure was early postoperative HR-CV. We extracted continuous heart rate (HR) and systolic blood pressure (SBP) measurements recorded in minute-by-minute resolution during the first 24 h of ICU admission. For each patient, the coefficient of variation (CV) was calculated as:

To rigorously establish a temporal sequence and avoid “reverse causality” (i.e., the inclusion of arrhythmic episodes in variability calculations), the calculation of HR-CV and SBP-CV was strictly limited to this initial 24-hour window. In our final analytical cohort, the earliest documented onset of POAF occurred at 34 h postoperatively. This observed temporal interval naturally precludes the overlap of the monitoring window with the arrhythmia event, ensuring that the calculated variability metrics reflect baseline sinus rhythm rather than intra-arrhythmic fluctuations.

Covariates

Baseline characteristics included age, gender, body mass index (BMI), and surgical approach (Video-Assisted Thoracoscopic Surgery [VATS] vs. Open). Comorbidities were defined using the Charlson Comorbidity Index, specifically identifying hypertension, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), congestive heart failure (CHF), and diabetes mellitus. Postoperative laboratory markers (hemoglobin, creatinine, white blood cell count) were extracted from the first 24-hour window.

Outcome definition

The primary outcome was delayed POAF, defined as new-onset atrial fibrillation occurring > 24 h after ICU admission. Unlike administrative datasets relying solely on discharge diagnosis codes, we utilized high-resolution chart events (ItemID: 220048, “Heart Rhythm”) to identify POAF. This approach allowed for the precise identification of the arrhythmia onset time. Patients were flagged as having POAF if the heart rhythm documentation indicated “Atrial Fibrillation”, “A-Fib”, or “AF” during the monitoring period. The onset time was defined as the timestamp of the first documentation of atrial fibrillation.

Missing data handling

Missing data were present in covariates such as BMI (22.7%) and laboratory values (~ 20%). To minimize selection bias associated with complete-case analysis, we employed Multiple Imputation by Chained Equations (MICE)20. We assumed data were missing at random (MAR) and generated five imputed datasets. Importantly, the primary exposure HR-CV, the secondary comparator SBP-CV, and the primary outcome delayed POAF were complete (0% missingness) in the final study cohort. Consequently, these critical variables were not subjected to imputation, ensuring that the core associations examined in this study relied entirely on observed physiological data. Variables with excessive missingness (> 30%, e.g., serum lactate, albumin) were excluded from the analysis to preserve model stability. All subsequent multivariable analyses were performed on the pooled imputed dataset.

Continuous variables were compared between the POAF and No-POAF groups using the Mann-Whitney U test, and categorical variables using the Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test.

Results

Study population and baseline characteristics

The selection process is summarized in Fig. 1. From an initial cohort of patients undergoing lung resection, 348 patients met the inclusion criteria for sublobar resection (wedge or segmentectomy) and had sufficient high-resolution monitoring data. Baseline characteristics are presented in Table 1. The incidence of delayed POAF was 16.7% (58/348). Patients who developed delayed POAF were significantly older (67.1 vs. 60.2 years, p < 0.001) and had a higher prevalence of hypertension (53.4% vs. 37.9%, p = 0.040) and congestive heart failure (22.4% vs. 11.0%, p = 0.032). There were no significant differences in gender, BMI, surgical approach (VATS vs. Open), or extent of resection (wedge vs. segmentectomy) between the two groups. Intraoperative profiles and postoperative laboratory values, including hemoglobin and electrolytes, were comparable. The POAF group exhibited significantly higher HR-CV than the non-POAF group (13.7 ± 7.4 vs. 11.0 ± 6.0; p = 0.006). In contrast, there was no significant difference in SBP-CV between the groups (p = 0.095). This dissociation suggests that autonomic instability, rather than systemic hemodynamic fluctuation, is the predominant pathophysiological feature preceding delayed POAF in this population.

Predictors of delayed POAF

Univariate and multivariable logistic regression analyses are detailed in Table 2. In univariate analysis, age, hypertension, congestive heart failure, and HR-CV were significantly associated with delayed POAF. SBP-CV was not a significant predictor (p = 0.289) and was therefore excluded from the primary multivariable model. In the multivariable model adjusted for age, hypertension, and congestive heart failure, early postoperative HR-CV remained an independent predictor of delayed POAF (Adjusted OR 1.06; 95% CI 1.02–1.11; p = 0.003). This indicates that for every 1% increase in HR-CV, the risk of delayed POAF increases by approximately 6%. Age (p = 0.004) and history of congestive heart failure (p = 0.029) were also independent predictors. To strictly account for the potential confounding effects of cardiovascular medications, we performed further sensitivity analyses incorporating the administration of beta-blockers and common vasoactive agents during the initial 24-hour ICU monitoring window (Supplementary Table S1). Given the limited number of delayed POAF events, these medications were added individually to the base multivariable clinical model to prevent statistical overfitting. As demonstrated in Supplementary Table S2, early postoperative HR-CV remained a robust and independent predictor of delayed POAF even after independently adjusting for the administration of beta-blockers (Adjusted OR 1.06; 95% CI 1.02–1.11; p = 0.004), norepinephrine (Adjusted OR 1.06; 95% CI 1.02–1.11; p = 0.008), or phenylephrine (Adjusted OR 1.05; 95% CI 1.01–1.10; p = 0.012).

Incremental predictive value and risk calculation

We evaluated the added value of HR-CV to a standard clinical risk model consisting of age, hypertension, and congestive heart failure. The inclusion of HR-CV significantly improved the model’s discriminative ability, increasing the Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (AUC) from 0.690 (Clinical Model) to 0.722 (Clinical + HR-CV Model) (Fig. 2). Based on the final multivariable model, a web-based risk calculator was developed (available at: https://dochu.shinyapps.io/POAF-Riskcalc/), allowing clinicians to estimate the individual probability of delayed POAF by inputting standard clinical variables and the calculated HR-CV.

Subgroup analysis

Subgroup analyses demonstrated a consistent association between HR-CV and delayed POAF across key clinical strata (Fig. 3). The direction of the effect (OR > 1.0) was maintained in patients aged < 65 and ≥ 65 years, males and females, and those with or without hypertension. Notably, the association appeared most pronounced in patients undergoing VATS (OR 1.11, 95% CI 1.02–1.20; p = 0.013) and those with hypertension (OR 1.09, 95% CI 1.03–1.16; p = 0.004), suggesting robust predictive value in high-risk subsets.

Discussion

In this retrospective cohort study utilizing high-resolution data from the MIMIC-IV database, we identified early postoperative HR-CV as a robust and independent predictor of delayed-onset POAF following sublobar lung resection. The addition of HR-CV to a standard clinical model (age, hypertension, and CHF) significantly improved risk stratification performance (AUC increased from 0.690 to 0.722). Furthermore, our analysis revealed a distinct physiological pattern: while HR-CV was strongly associated with POAF, SBP-CV was not. This observation suggests that autonomic dysregulation may play a more prominent role than systemic hemodynamic fluctuations in the pathogenesis of POAF within this specific surgical population.

The relationship between the autonomic nervous system and atrial arrhythmias is well-established, which posits that an autonomic imbalance acts as a critical trigger on a vulnerable atrial substrate12,21. Our findings align with pioneering work by Hogue et al. and Amar et al., who demonstrated that temporal fluctuations in vagal and sympathetic tone frequently precede the onset of POAF in cardiac and major thoracic surgery10,14. Interestingly, while Böhm et al. have previously highlighted the prognostic value of SBP variability in cardiovascular outcomes22, our study did not find a significant association between SBP-CV and delayed POAF in the multivariable analysis. This discrepancy may be attributed to the specific pathophysiology of sublobar resection. Unlike major vascular surgery where hemodynamic shifts are profound, sublobar resection is relatively hemodynamically stable. In this context, HR-CV likely serves as a more sensitive “biosignal” of intrinsic cardiac autonomic stress, reflecting the erratic “tug-of-war” between sympathetic and parasympathetic inputs on the sinoatrial node, rather than generalized vascular instability23.

As sublobar resection becomes a standard treatment approach for early-stage NSCLC24–26, understanding its specific complication profile is crucial. In our cohort, the observed POAF incidence was 16.7%, which is notably higher than the 2%–5% typically reported in general thoracic cohorts27–29. This discrepancy is attributable to the nature of the MIMIC-IV database, which exclusively captures patients requiring ICU admission. Consequently, our study population represents a “selected high-risk” subset with greater comorbidity burdens than routine ward admissions. Our subgroup analysis offers a nuanced explanation: the predictive value of HR-CV was most pronounced in elderly patients (≥ 65 years) and those with hypertension. In younger, healthier patients, the cardiovascular system can tolerate autonomic fluctuations without degenerating into arrhythmia. However, in elderly patients with stiffer ventricles and atrial fibrosis, even minor autonomic instability (manifested as elevated HR-CV) may be sufficient to trigger POAF. This identifies a specific “high-risk phenotype” that warrants closer surveillance. Currently, our risk calculator provides a static prediction based on the first 24-hour window (https://dochu.shinyapps.io/POAF-Riskcalc/). However, looking forward, we envision integrating this algorithm into bedside monitors for real-time risk stratification. As the patient’s heart rate variability fluctuates dynamically, the risk score could be updated instantaneously to alert clinicians of impending arrhythmia onset. A conceptual simulation of such a real-time ‘POAF Monitor’ is provided in Supplementary Video 1. This transition from static scoring to dynamic surveillance represents the next frontier in postoperative precision medicine.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, as a single-center retrospective study utilizing the MIMIC-IV database, our current model lacks an independent external validation cohort. Consequently, the external validity of the proposed risk calculator remains unconfirmed. The current model and the associated web-based tool should be regarded as a preliminary risk stratification tool. Future multicenter, prospective studies with independent cohorts are strictly necessary to rigorously evaluate the model’s predictive performance and generalizability before it can be recommended for routine clinical use. Second, we utilized time-domain HRV metrics (CV) due to the nature of the available data; future studies using frequency-domain analysis (e.g., LF/HF ratio) could provide deeper insights into the specific sympathovagal balance30. Third, although we adjusted for key confounders, residual confounding from unmeasured variables such as intraoperative pain scores or specific anesthetic agents cannot be ruled out31. Fourth, while our sensitivity analyses rigorously adjusted for the binary administration (yes/no) of beta-blockers and vasoactive agents, our current risk model does not incorporate the precise cumulative doses or dynamic titrations of these medications. In the intensive care setting, vasoactive drugs are frequently titrated on a minute-to-minute basis to maintain hemodynamic stability. Calculating a reliable cumulative dose equivalent that perfectly aligns with the specific 24-hour monitoring window used for our HR-CV calculations is highly susceptible to documentation artifacts and noise within a retrospective database like MIMIC-IV. Future prospective studies with automated, high-resolution medication tracking are warranted to evaluate the nuanced dose-response relationship of these medications on postoperative autonomic fluctuations and arrhythmia risk.

Conclusions

Early postoperative heart rate variability is an independent predictor of delayed atrial fibrillation in patients undergoing sublobar lung resection. In this cohort, HR-CV demonstrated superior predictive value compared to systolic blood pressure variability, highlighting the importance of autonomic monitoring in the early recovery phase. These findings support the integration of dynamic physiological markers into postoperative risk stratification, potentially aiding clinicians in identifying high-risk patients who may benefit from extended telemetry monitoring.

BKP and CKP hierarchies via orbifold Saito theory
Alexey Basalaev

Abstract
. Semisimple Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds can be used to construct integrable hierarchies, following the work of Dubrovin–Zhang and Buryak. Examples of such hierarchies include the Kac–Wakimoto hierarchies, the KP hierarchy, among others. In all these examples, the Saito theory of isolated singularities played a crucial role. In this note, we show that the BKP and CKP hierarchies can likewise be constructed from Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds. This new construction, however, utilizes the orbifold version of Saito theory for isolated singularities endowed with a symmetry group. Keywords: integrable systems, Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds. MSC codes: 14H70, 14N35.



1.
Introduction

Tense connection between the cohomological field theories and integrable systems has been known since the last decade of XX. This was first conjectured by Witten (cf.
[
W93
]
) that the partition functions of some cohomological field theories are tau–functions of the integrable hierarchies. Conjectures of Witten were proved by Kontsevich
[
K92
]
, Faber-Shadrin-Zvonkine
[
FSZ06
]
and Fan-Jarvis-Ruan
[
FJR13
]
.

The genus
0
potential of every cohomological field theory defines a formal Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold. Conversely if a Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold is semisimple, it can be used to reconstruct uniquely all genera potentials of some cohomological field theory uniquely.

It was later observed that Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds can be used to construct integrable systems themselves rather then just tau-functions (cf.
[
DVV91
]
,
[
D96
]
[Lecture 6] and other).

The general constructions of Dubrovin-Zhang
[
DZ01
]
and Buryak
[
Bu15
]
associate an integrable hierarchy to any semisimple Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold. Several special Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds are known to have there specific constructions of the integrable hierarchies (cf.
[
G03
,
FGM10
,
GM05
,
LRZ15
,
P06
,
BDbN21
,
B24
,
B25
]
). Some of these constructions were also extended to the flat F–manifolds (cf.
[
ABLR21
,
LPR11
,
B22
]
).

Both constructions of Dubrovin–Zhang and Buryak applied to the ADE type Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds give the hierarchy, equivalent to Kac–Wakimoto hierarchy of the same ADE type.
The full series of A–type Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds can be used to construct the famous KP hierarchy.

It was essential to ask what Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds provide BCFG Kac-Wakimoto hierarchies. This question was answered by Liu, Ruan and Zhang in
[
LRZ15
]
. In particular, they’ve found that BCFG Kac-Wakimoto hierarchies are given by the special subflows of the Dubrovin–Zhang hierarchies.

Next to KP hierarchy well–known are BKP and CKP hierarchies (cf.
[
DJKM82
]
). Both are easy to define in the Lax form using the Lax operator of KP hierarchy and imposing just one additional constraint.
This is the second essential question to ask — how to construct BKP and CKP hierarchies via the Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds.

If such construction would exist, the corresponding Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds would be isomorphic because BKP and CKP hierarchies are known to coincide dispersionless.

In this paper we make use of the orbifold Saito theories of A and D type singularities in order to construct BKP and CKP hierarchies.

Orbifold Saito theory

ADE type Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds have several equivalent definitions. One of them is via the Saito theory of the ADE type singularity (cf.
[
S83
,
H02
]
). In particular, one considers the unfolding of the polynomial
f\in{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}]
defining the singularity in question. For
A_{N}
and
D_{N}
types the polynomials
f
are given by
f_{A_{N}}:=\frac{1}{N+1}x_{1}^{N+1}+x_{2}^{2}
and
f_{D_{N}}:=\frac{1}{N-1}x_{1}^{N-1}+x_{1}x_{2}^{2}
.

Mirror symmetry sparkled investigation of the isolated singularities endowed with the symmetry groups. Following the physicists notation, the pairs
(f,G)
where
f\in{\mathbb{C}}[{\bf x}]
defines an isolated singularity and
G
is its symmetry groups are now called
Landau–Ginzburg orbifolds
.

Saito theory of the Landau–Ginzburg orbifolds has been under investigation of many researchers (cf.
[
T11
,
T93
,
BT22
,
BR25
]
). The corresponding Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold is
G
–graded. Its
\mathrm{id}
–graded submanifold is fixed by the
G
–invariants of the “classical” Saito theory and therefore called
invariant sector
.

In this note we show that BKP and CKP hierarchies are constructed essentially via the Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds of the Landau–Ginzburg orbifolds.
We consider the hierarchies of the orbifold Saito theory Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds and show that restriction to the invariant sector gives a subhierarchy.

Our main theorem is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

BKP hierarchy is the invariant sector hierarchy of the series of Landau–Ginzburg orbifolds
(A_{2N+1},\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
.
CKP hierarchy is the invariant sector hierarchy of the series of Landau–Ginzburg orbifolds
(D_{N},\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
.

Proof of this theorem is given in Section
5.1
.

Compared to
[
LRZ15
]
we work with Saito theory Landau–Ginzburg orbifolds while the authors of loc.cit. work with the FJR theory. From the point of view of mirror symmetry we work on the B side while
[
LRZ15
]
work on the A side. Another difference is that we construct the BKP and CKP hierarchies rather then the Kac–Wakimoto hierarchies.
We also distinguish the subhierarchies inside the bigger hierarchies as
[
LRZ15
]
do. However in our case this subhierarchies are given essentially by the invariant sector whereas Liu, Ruan, Zhang do the specific research to sort out the suitable flows.

2.
Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds

Assume
M
to be an open full–dimensional subspace of
{\mathbb{C}}^{l}
. We say that it’s endowed with a structure of Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold if there is a regular function
{\mathcal{F}}={\mathcal{F}}(t_{1},\dots,t_{l})
on
M
, s.t. the following conditions hold (cf.
[
D96
]
).

•

There is a distinguished variable
t_{1}
, such that:

\frac{{\partial}{\mathcal{F}}}{{\partial}t_{1}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{l}\eta_{\alpha,\beta}t_{\alpha}t_{\beta},

and
\eta_{\alpha,\beta}
are components of a non-degenerate bilinear form
\eta
(which does not depend on
t_{\bullet}
). In what follows denote by
\eta^{\alpha,\beta}
the components of
\eta^{-1}
.

•

The function
{\mathcal{F}}
satisfies a large system of PDEs called the WDVV equations:

\sum_{\mu,\nu=1}^{l}\frac{{\partial}^{3}{\mathcal{F}}}{{\partial}t_{\alpha}{\partial}t_{\beta}{\partial}t_{\mu}}\eta^{\mu,\nu}\frac{{\partial}^{3}{\mathcal{F}}}{{\partial}t_{\nu}{\partial}t_{\gamma}{\partial}t_{\sigma}}=\sum_{\mu,\nu=1}^{l}\frac{{\partial}^{3}{\mathcal{F}}}{{\partial}t_{\alpha}{\partial}t_{\gamma}{\partial}t_{\mu}}\eta^{\mu,\nu}\frac{{\partial}^{3}{\mathcal{F}}}{{\partial}t_{\nu}{\partial}t_{\beta}{\partial}t_{\sigma}},

which should hold for every given
1\leq\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\sigma\leq l
.

•

There is a vector field
E
called the
Euler vector field
, such that modulo quadratic terms in
t_{\bullet}
we have
E\cdot{\mathcal{F}}=(3-\delta){\mathcal{F}}
for some fixed complex number
\delta
. We will assume
E
to have the following simple form

E=\sum_{i=1}^{l}d_{i}t_{i}\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t_{i}}

for some fixed numbers
d_{1},\dots,d_{l}
.

Given such a data
(M,{\mathcal{F}},E)
one can endow every tangent space
T_{p}M
with a structure of commutative associative product
\circ
(depending on
{\bf t}
) defined as follows:

\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t_{\alpha}}\circ\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t_{\beta}}=\sum_{\delta,\gamma=1}^{l}\frac{{\partial}^{3}{\mathcal{F}}}{{\partial}t_{\alpha}{\partial}t_{\beta}{\partial}t_{\delta}}\eta^{\delta\gamma}\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t_{\delta}}.

The unit of this product is the vector field
e=\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t_{1}}
.
It follows that
\eta(a\circ b,c)=\eta(a,b\circ c)
for any vector fields
a,b,c
.

Let

To introduce the Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold structure on
M_{W}

{\mathcal{A}}_{v}:={\mathbb{C}}[x,y]\,\Big/\left(\frac{{\partial}\Lambda_{W}}{{\partial}x},\frac{{\partial}\Lambda_{W}}{{\partial}y}\right).

It’s endowed with the quotient-ring product structure, and the classical singularity theory arguments assure that
{\mathcal{A}}_{v}

N

{\mathbb{C}}
–vector space. Let

\displaystyle A_{N}:\quad\frac{{\partial}\Lambda_{W}}{{\partial}v_{k}}=x^{k-1},\ 1\leq k\leq N,

\displaystyle D_{N}:\quad\frac{{\partial}\Lambda_{W}}{{\partial}v_{k}}=x^{k-1},\ 1\leq k\leq N-1,\quad\frac{{\partial}\Lambda_{W}}{{\partial}v_{N}}=y.

The product
\circ:T_{v}M\otimes T_{v}M\to T_{v}M\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[v_{1},\dots,v_{N}]

\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}v_{a}}\circ\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}v_{b}}:=\sum_{k=1}^{N}c_{ab}^{k}(v)\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}v_{k}}.

Obviously,
{\partial}/{\partial}v_{1}

In particular, we have for

(2.1)

\displaystyle\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}v_{a}}\circ\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}v_{b}}=\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}v_{a+b-1}}\quad\forall a+b\leq N+1,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}v_{a}}\circ\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}v_{N+2-a}}=-\sum_{k=2}^{N}(k-1)v_{k}\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}v_{k-1}}.

Introduce the non-degenerate
{\mathbb{C}}[v]

\eta:T_{v}M\otimes T_{v}M\to{\mathbb{C}}[v_{1},\dots,v_{N}]

\displaystyle\eta(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}v_{a}},\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}v_{b}}):=c_{a,b}^{N},\quad W=A_{N},

\displaystyle\eta(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}v_{a}},\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}v_{b}}):=c_{a,b}^{N-1},\quad W=D_{N}.

2.5.
Dubrovin–Frobenius submanifold

This topic was investigated thoroughly by I. Strachan in
[
St01
]
. Consider a Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold
(M,\circ,\eta)
. Let
\iota:M^{\prime}\hookrightarrow M
be a submanifold. Then
M^{\prime}
has an induced bilinear form
\eta^{\prime}
on it. Moreover, the pairing
\eta
gives us the orthogonal projector
\mathrm{pr}:TM\to TM^{\prime}
that allows one to introduce the product
\circ^{\prime}
for
M^{\prime}

a\circ^{\prime}b:=\mathrm{pr}(a\circ b),\quad\forall a,b\in T_{p}M^{\prime}\subset T_{p}M.

It follows immediately that
(T_{p}M^{\prime},\circ^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})
satisfies the Frobenius algebra property for any
p\in M^{\prime}
. Denote also
e^{\prime}:=\mathrm{pr}(e)
and
E^{\prime}:=\mathrm{pr}(E)
. Note that these vectors can even turn out to be zero.

We will call
M^{\prime}
a Dubrovin–Frobenius submanifold if
(M^{\prime},\circ^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})
is a Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold by itself. In particular, this assumes
\eta^{\prime}
to be non–degenerate and the product
\circ^{\prime}
to have a Frobenius potential (see
[
St01
]
for the details).

Assume also the product
\circ^{\prime}
above to be such that
a\circ^{\prime}b=a\circ b
in
T_{p}M
for any
p\in M^{\prime}
. This is equivalent to the property
((a\circ b)\mid_{M^{\prime}})^{\perp}=0
. The Dubrovin–Frobenius submanifolds satisfying this property will be called
natural
.

Assume Dubrovin–Frobenius submanifold
M^{\prime}
to be obtained by setting
t_{k}=0
for all

with
{\mathcal{R}}\subset\{1,\dots,N\}
. If

are structure constants of
\circ
, the property of being natural for

means

\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t_{\alpha}}\circ^{\prime}\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t_{\beta}}=\sum_{\gamma\not\in{\mathcal{R}}}\left(c_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}\mid_{t_{k}=0\ \forall k\in{\mathcal{R}}}\right)\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t_{\gamma}}.

2.6.
B type Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold

It was shown by Zuber in

]

Consider
\iota:M_{B_{N}}\hookrightarrow M_{A_{2N-1}}

Introduce the

–action on

3.
Integrable systems

3.1.
Stabilizing potentials

Let
\{{\mathcal{F}}_{N}\}_{N\geq N_{min}}
be an infinite series of
N
–dimensional Frobenius potentials.
Assume
{\mathcal{F}}_{N}\in{\mathbb{C}}[[t_{1},\dots,t_{N}]]
. We will say that this series
stabilize
if the following holds.

For any
N\geq N_{min}
, all indices
1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq N
, satisfying the
index stabilization condition

\alpha+\beta\ll N
and any
L>N
the the following equality holds in
{\mathbb{C}}[[s_{1},\dots,s_{L}]]

\frac{{\partial}^{2}{\mathcal{F}}_{N}}{{\partial}t_{\alpha}{\partial}t_{\beta}}\mid_{t_{\alpha}=\eta^{\alpha\gamma}s_{\gamma}}=\frac{{\partial}^{2}{\mathcal{F}}_{L}}{{\partial}t_{\alpha}{\partial}t_{\beta}}\mid_{t_{\alpha}=\eta^{\alpha\gamma}s_{\gamma}}

Note that the substitution
t_{\alpha}=\eta^{\alpha\gamma}s_{\gamma}
on the both sides is taken with its own pairing
\eta
, different for the two sides.

The index stabilization condition can be made more precise in the particular examples.
It was proven in Theorem 4.1 of
[
BDbN21
]
that
{\mathcal{F}}_{N}={\mathcal{F}}_{A_{N}}
with
N_{min}=1
and index stabilization condition
\alpha+\beta\leq N

Another stabilizing series of potentials is given by the D type Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds.
According to Theorem 4.9 of loc.cit. the series
{\mathcal{F}}_{N}={\mathcal{F}}_{D_{N}}
stabilize with

\alpha+\beta\leq N-1

\alpha\leq N-1

\beta=N
.

3.2.
Dispersionless system

To any stabilizing series
\{{\mathcal{F}}_{N}\}_{N\geq N_{min}}
as above associate the system of PDEs on the function
f=f(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},\dots)
.

(3.1)

{\partial}_{\alpha}{\partial}_{\beta}f=\left(\frac{{\partial}^{2}{\mathcal{F}}_{N}}{{\partial}t_{\alpha}{\partial}t_{\beta}}\right)\mid_{t_{\gamma}=\eta^{\gamma\delta}{\partial}_{1}{\partial}_{\delta}f},

where we denote
{\partial}_{\alpha}:={\partial}/{\partial}t_{\alpha}
and the number
N
of the right hand side is taken such that the index stabilization condition for
\alpha,\beta
and
N
holds.

Note that the right hand side of Eq. (
3.1
) is a complex coefficients power series in
{\partial}_{1}{\partial}_{\bullet}f
.
This system of equations expresses the second order derivatives of
f
with respect to the higher index variables via the set
\{{\partial}_{1}{\partial}_{k}f\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
that should be understood as a
Cauchy data
.

The compatibility of this system of PDEs follows from WDVV equation on the potentials
{\mathcal{F}}_{N}
(cf. Proposition 2.1 of
[
BDbN21
]
and proof of Theorem 1.2 in
[
B24
]
).

It was shown in
[
BDbN21
]
that Eq. (
3.1
) for the stabilizing system of A type Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds is equivalent to dispersionless KP hierarchy. To be more precise Eq. (
3.1
) coincides with the Fay form of dispersionless KP hierarchy after some rescaling.

For the stabilizing system of D type Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds Eq. (
3.1
) is equivalent to dispersionless limit of 1–component reduced 2–component BKP hierarchy. To be more precise Eq. (
3.1
) has two types of flows that originate from the specific structure of the potential. The first class of flows coincides with the Fay form of the dispersionless BKP hierarchy after some rescaling. The second class of flows coincides with the Fay form of the reduced dispersionless BKP hierarchy (cf. Section 7 of loc.cit.).
We will comment on this more in what follows.

3.3.
Dispersionfull systems

There are several ways to associate to a semisimple Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold a dispersionfull hierarchy (cf.
[
DZ01
,
Bu15
]
). In particular, the approach of Dubrovin and Zhang is to construct a dispersionless hierarchy first and extend it further to the dispersionfull one.

Our primary interest in this paper concerns A and D type Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds.
In these special cases there are specific constructions of the dispersionfull hierarchies
[
FGM10
,
GM05
,
G03
,
MT08
]
.

3.4.
Dubrovin–Frobenius submanifolds and subflows

Let
\{{\mathcal{F}}_{N}\}
be a stabilizing series of potentials. Assume
{\mathcal{F}}_{N}
to introduce the structure of Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold on
M_{N}
. Assume also
M^{\prime}_{N}\hookrightarrow M_{N}
to be a natural Dubrovin–Frobenius submanifold, given by

M_{N}^{\prime}=\{(t_{1},\dots,t_{N})\in M_{N}\ |\ t_{\alpha}=0\ \forall\alpha\in{\mathcal{R}}_{N}\}

for some index set
{\mathcal{R}}_{N}\subset\{1,\dots,N\}
. Assume also
{\mathcal{R}}_{N}\subseteq{\mathcal{R}}_{N+1}
for all
N\geq N_{min}
.

In particular, for
B_{N}\hookrightarrow A_{2N-1}
we have
{\mathcal{R}}_{2N-1}=\{2,4,\dots,2N-2\}
and for
B_{N}\hookrightarrow D_{N+1}
we have
{\mathcal{R}}_{N+1}=\{N+1\}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let the series
\{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{N}\}
of Frobenius potentials stabilize. Then the system Eq. (
3.1
) for it are subflows of the system Eq. (
3.1
) for the series
\{{\mathcal{F}}_{N}\}
obtained by taking
{\partial}_{1}{\partial}_{k}f=0
for all
k\in\cup_{N}{\mathcal{R}}_{N}
.

Proof.

It follows from the construction that for all
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\not\in{\mathcal{R}}_{N}
we have

\frac{{\partial}^{3}{\mathcal{F}}_{N}^{\prime}}{{\partial}t_{\alpha}{\partial}t_{\beta}{\partial}t_{\gamma}}=\frac{{\partial}^{3}{\mathcal{F}}_{N}}{{\partial}t_{\alpha}{\partial}t_{\beta}{\partial}t_{\gamma}}\mid_{t_{\delta}=0,\ \forall\delta\in{\mathcal{R}}_{N}}.

Because
{\mathcal{F}}_{N}^{\prime}
is a Frobenius potential of a Dubrovin–Frobenius submanifold, for any
\alpha\in{\mathcal{R}}_{N}
and any other index
\beta
, such that
\eta^{\alpha\beta}\neq 0
we have
\beta\in{\mathcal{R}}_{N}
.
Then Eq. (
3.1
) for
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{N}
gives

\displaystyle{\partial}_{\alpha}{\partial}_{\beta}f

\displaystyle=\frac{{\partial}^{2}{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{N}}{{\partial}t_{\alpha}{\partial}t_{\beta}}\mid_{t_{\nu}=\eta^{\nu\delta}{\partial}_{1}{\partial}_{\delta}f}=\frac{{\partial}^{2}{\mathcal{F}}_{N}}{{\partial}t_{\alpha}{\partial}t_{\beta}}\mid_{t_{\delta}=0,\ \forall\delta\in{\mathcal{R}}_{N}}\mid_{t_{\nu}=\eta^{\nu\delta}{\partial}_{1}{\partial}_{\delta}f}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{{\partial}^{2}{\mathcal{F}}_{N}}{{\partial}t_{\alpha}{\partial}t_{\beta}}\mid_{t_{\nu}=\eta^{\nu\delta}{\partial}_{1}{\partial}_{\delta}f}\right)\mid_{{\partial}_{1}{\partial}_{\delta}f=0,\ \forall\delta\in{\mathcal{R}}_{N}}.

∎

The proposition above shows an interesting connection between the natural Dubrovin–Frobenius submanifolds and subflows of the integrable systems. Recall that for the submanifolds we where considering the products
{\partial}/{\partial}t_{\alpha}\circ{\partial}/{\partial}t_{\beta}
of the small number of the basis vectors - only those satisfying
\alpha,\beta\not\in{\mathcal{R}}_{N}
. On the PDEs side this translates to considering the flows
{\partial}_{\alpha}{\partial}_{\beta}f
of an unknown function
f
only with respect to the restricted set of variables.

Also, for the submanifolds, the coefficients of the product
{\partial}/{\partial}t_{\alpha}\circ{\partial}/{\partial}t_{\beta}
, when computed in the ambient Dubrovin–Frobenius structure, had to be restricted to
t_{k}=0
for
k\in{\mathcal{R}}_{N}
. On the PDEs side this translates to setting the respective Cauchy data to zero:
{\partial}_{1}{\partial}_{k}f=0
.

4.
KP, BKP and CKP

In this section we gather all the data we need about the KP, BKP and CKP hierarchies. We do not give full account of them referencing an interested reader to
[
DJKM82
,
NZ16
]
and
[
Z05
,
Z21
]
. We closely follow the work of Zabrodin below.

4.1.
KP hierarchy

Consider the operators

D(z):=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{z^{-n}}{n}{\partial}_{n},\quad\Delta(z):=\frac{\exp(2\hbar D(z))-1}{\hbar}.

Let
{\bf t}=t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4},\dots
.
For a function
\tau=\tau({\bf t})
denote

\tau({\bf t}+[z^{-1}]):=\tau(t_{1}+\frac{1}{z},t_{2}+\frac{1}{2z^{2}},t_{3}+\frac{1}{3z^{3}},t_{4}+\frac{1}{4z^{4}},\dots)=\exp(D(z))\cdot\tau({\bf t}).

The function
\tau=\tau({\bf t})
is said to be a tau–function of KP hierarchy if it satisfies

{\partial}_{1}\log\frac{\tau({\bf t}+[z_{1}^{-1}]-[z_{2}^{-1}])}{\tau({\bf t})}=(z_{2}-z_{1})\left(\frac{\tau({\bf t}+[z_{1}^{-1}])\tau({\bf t}-[z_{2}^{-1}])}{\tau({\bf t})\tau({\bf t}+[z_{1}^{-1}]-[z_{2}^{-1}])}-1\right).

KP hierarchy can be equivalently rewritten in a Fay form on the function
f=\hbar^{2}\log\tau
.
The Fay form of KP hierarchy is the following equation in the ring of formal power series in
z_{1}^{-1},z_{2}^{-1}
.

\exp(\Delta(z_{1})\Delta(z_{2})f)=1-\frac{\Delta(z_{1}){\partial}_{1}f-\Delta(z_{2}){\partial}_{1}f}{z_{1}-z_{2}}.

Assume also
f
to have an
\hbar
–expansion
f=\sum_{g\geq 0}\hbar^{g}f_{g}
. The dispersionless limit of KP hierarchy in the Fay form is the following equation

\exp(D(z_{1})D(z_{2})f_{0})=1-\frac{D(z_{1}){\partial}_{1}f_{0}-D(z_{2}){\partial}_{1}f_{0}}{z_{1}-z_{2}}.

4.2.
BKP hierarchy

In what follows we need to distinguish between the even and odd–indexed variables. Denote
{\bf t}_{o}:=t_{1},t_{3},t_{5},\dots
.

Consider the operators

D^{\mathrm{B}}(z):=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{z^{-2n-1}}{2n+1}{\partial}_{2n+1},\quad\Delta^{\mathrm{B}}(z):=\frac{\exp(2\hbar D^{\mathrm{B}}(z))-1}{\hbar}.

For a function
\tau=\tau({\bf t}_{o})
denote

\tau({\bf t}_{o}+2[z^{-1}]_{o}):=\tau(t_{1}+\frac{2}{z},t_{3}+\frac{2}{3z^{3}},t_{5}+\frac{2}{5z^{5}},\dots)=\exp(2D^{\mathrm{B}}(z))\cdot\tau({\bf t}_{o})

and

\tau^{[z]_{o}}:=\tau({\bf t}_{o}+2[z^{-1}]_{o}),\quad\tau^{[z_{1}z_{2}]_{o}}:=\tau({\bf t}_{o}+2[z_{1}^{-1}]_{o}+2[z_{2}^{-1}]_{o}).

The function
\tau=\tau({\bf t}_{o})
is said to be a tau–function of BKP hierarchy if it satisfies (see Section 3.2 of
[
Z21
]
)

\tau\tau^{[z_{1}z_{2}]_{o}}\left(1+\frac{1}{z_{1}+z_{2}}{\partial}_{1}\log\frac{\tau}{\tau^{[z_{1}z_{2}]_{o}}}\right)=\tau^{[z_{1}]_{o}}\tau^{[z_{2}]_{o}}\left(1+\frac{1}{z_{1}-z_{2}}{\partial}_{1}\log\frac{\tau^{[z_{1}]_{o}}}{\tau^{[z_{2}]_{o}}}\right).

BKP hierarchy can be rewritten in a Fay form on the function
f=\hbar^{2}\log\tau
.

\displaystyle\left(z_{1}+z_{2}-{\partial}_{1}\hbar\Delta^{\mathrm{B}}(z_{1})\Delta^{\mathrm{B}}(z_{2})f-{\partial}_{1}(\Delta^{\mathrm{B}}(z_{1})f+\Delta^{\mathrm{B}}(z_{2})f)\right)\exp(\Delta^{\mathrm{B}}(z_{1})\Delta^{\mathrm{B}}(z_{2})f)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad=\frac{z_{1}+z_{2}}{z_{1}-z_{2}}\left(z_{1}-z_{2}-{\partial}_{1}(\Delta^{\mathrm{B}}(z_{1})f-\Delta^{\mathrm{B}}(z_{2})f)\right).

Assume also
f
to have an
\hbar
–expansion
f=\sum_{g\geq 0}\hbar^{g}f_{g}
. The dispersionless limit of BKP hierarchy in the Fay form is the following equation

(4.1)

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{{\partial}_{1}(2D^{\mathrm{B}}(z_{1})+2D^{\mathrm{B}}(z_{2}))f_{0}}{z_{1}+z_{2}}\right)e^{2D^{\mathrm{B}}(z_{1})\cdot 2D^{\mathrm{B}}(z_{2})f_{0}}=1-\frac{{\partial}_{1}(2D^{\mathrm{B}}(z_{1})-2D^{\mathrm{B}}(z_{2}))f_{0}}{z_{1}-z_{2}}.

4.3.
CKP hierarchy

The function
\tau=\tau({\bf t}_{o})
is said to be a tau–function of CKP hierarchy if it satisfies (see Section 4.3 of
[
Z21
]
)

\displaystyle\frac{z_{1}+z_{2}}{z_{1}-z_{2}}\Bigg[\left(z_{1}-{\partial}_{1}\log\frac{\tau^{[z_{1}z_{2}]_{o}}}{\tau^{[z_{2}]_{o}}}\right)^{1/2}\left(z_{1}-{\partial}_{1}\log\frac{\tau^{[z_{1}]_{o}}}{\tau}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad-\left(z_{2}-{\partial}_{1}\log\frac{\tau^{[z_{1}z_{2}]_{o}}}{\tau^{[z_{1}]_{o}}}\right)^{1/2}\left(z_{2}-{\partial}_{1}\log\frac{\tau^{[z_{2}]_{o}}}{\tau}\right)^{1/2}\Bigg]

(4.2)

\displaystyle\qquad=\left(z_{1}+z_{2}-{\partial}_{1}\log\frac{\tau^{[z_{1}z_{2}]_{o}}}{\tau}\right)\frac{\tau\tau^{[z_{1}z_{2}]_{o}}}{\tau^{[z_{1}]_{o}}\tau^{[z_{2}]_{o}}}.

The dispersionless limit of CKP hierarchy coincides with the dispersionless limit of BKP hierarchy — Eq. (
4.1
). This perfectly reflects the fact that the invariant sectors of
M_{A_{2N-1},\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}}
and
M_{D_{N+1},\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}}
are isomorphic as the Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds.

Equation on the tau–function above is more complicated than the respective equation for the BKP hierarchy. Working with the CKP hierarchy in what follows we use the following result of Krichever and Zabrodin. Essentially it shows that CKP hierarchy can be understood as the restriction of KP (see Section 2.3 of
[
KZ21
]
).

Theorem 4.1

(Krichever, Zabrodin)
.

For any tau–function
\tau^{\mathrm{CKP}}
of the CKP hierarchy there is a unique tau–function
\tau^{\mathrm{KP}}
of the KP hierarchy, such that

0={\partial}_{1}{\partial}_{2k}\tau^{\mathrm{KP}}\mid_{t_{2m}=0,\ m\geq 1},\quad\forall k\geq 1

and

\left(\tau^{\mathrm{CKP}}(t_{1},t_{3},t_{5},\dots)\right)^{2}=\tau^{\mathrm{KP}}(t_{1},0,t_{3},0,t_{5},\dots).

This theorem is a part of Theorem 2.2 of
[
KZ21
]
together with some ingredients of its proof that are important in our exposition (Lemma 2.1 and Appendix A of loc.cit.).

4.4.
A type Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold hierarchy

The system (
3.1
) for the stabilizing series
\{{\mathcal{F}}_{A_{N}}\}
was investigated in
[
BDbN21
]
.
It was proved in Theorem 6.2 of
[
BDbN21
]
that the system (
3.1
) coincides with the dispersionless limit of KP hierarchy after the substitution
t_{k}\mapsto t_{k}/k
.

Consider the differential operators
{\partial}_{k}^{\hbar}
defined by the equality

\Delta(z)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{z^{-k}}{k}{\partial}_{k}^{\hbar}.

In particular, we have
{\partial}_{k}^{\hbar}={\partial}_{k}+O(\hbar)
.
It follows from the results of Natanzon–Zabrodin
[
NZ16
, Lemma 3.2]
that full KP hierarchy is obtained from the dispersionless one by the substitution
{\partial}_{k}\mapsto{\partial}_{k}^{\hbar}
. This gives us that the full KP hierarchy is obtained from the system (
3.1
) for the stabilizing series
\{{\mathcal{F}}_{A_{N}}\}
via this substitution.

D type Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold hierarchy

The system (
3.1
) for the stabilizing series
\{{\mathcal{F}}_{D_{N}}\}
was investigated in
[
BDbN21
]

t_{N}
enters the potential

As a consequence, this system of PDEs distributes in the following two

(dlD-1)

(dlD-2)

\displaystyle{\partial}_{\alpha}{\partial}_{0}f

\displaystyle=\frac{{\partial}^{2}{\mathcal{F}}_{D_{N}}}{{\partial}t_{\alpha}{\partial}t_{N}}\mid_{t_{\gamma}=\eta^{\gamma\delta}{\partial}_{1}{\partial}_{\delta}f},\quad N\geq\alpha,

on the unknown function
f=f(t_{0},t_{1},t_{2},\dots)
with the Cauchy data
{\partial}_{1}{\partial}_{0}f,{\partial}_{1}{\partial}_{1}f,{\partial}_{1}{\partial}_{2}f,\dots

This is important to note that due to the special form of a D type Frobenius potential, the right hand side of Eq. (

{\partial}_{1}{\partial}_{0}f

It was proved in Theorem 7.1 of

. The flows (
dlD-2

4.5.1.
Dispersionfull hierarchy

Similarly to the case of the stabilizing series

and the KP hierarchy, we expect that BKP hierarchy can be obtained from its dispersionless limit via some
\hbar

5.
Orbifold Saito theory

In this section consider the polynomials
f_{A_{2N-1}}
and
f_{D_{N}}
introduced in Section
2.1
together with two different symmetry groups
G
both homeomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.

For
f_{A_{2N-1}}
let
G
be generated by
g:(x,y)\mapsto(-x,-y)
. And for and
f_{D_{N}}
let
G
be generated by
g:(x,y)\mapsto(x,-y)
.
One notes immediately that such transformations preserve the polynomials given.

Mirror symmetry suggests existence of orbifold Saito theory associated with the pairs
(f_{A_{2N-1}},G)
and
(f_{D_{N}},G)
. The attempts to construct an orbifold Saito theory were made in
[
Tu1
,
Tu2
,
BT22
,
BR25
]
.

Like the classical Saito theory, orbifold Saito theory should produce a Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold. Stemming from the orbifold theory this Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold should satisfy the certain special properties. In particular, its potential should depend on the variables
t_{g,\alpha}
with
g\in G
and
\alpha
— integer index.

The following theorem can be used as the definition of the Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds of the Landau–Ginzburg orbifolds we are interested in.

Theorem 5.1
.

Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold of the Landau–Ginzburg orbifold
(f_{A_{2N-1}},G)
depends on the variables
t_{\mathrm{id},1},\dots,t_{\mathrm{id},N},t_{g,1}
and is given by

{\mathcal{F}}_{(A_{2N-1},G)}(t_{\mathrm{id},1},\dots,t_{\mathrm{id},N},t_{g,1})={\mathcal{F}}_{D_{N+1}}(t_{\mathrm{id},1},\dots,t_{\mathrm{id},N},t_{g,1}).

Dubrovin–Frobenius manifold of the Landau–Ginzburg orbifold
(f_{D_{N+1}},G)
depends on the variables
t_{\mathrm{id},1},\dots,t_{\mathrm{id},N},t_{g,1}
and is given by

{\mathcal{F}}_{(D_{N+1},G)}(t_{\mathrm{id},1},\dots,t_{\mathrm{id},N},t_{g,1},\dots,t_{g,N-1})={\mathcal{F}}_{A_{2N-1}}(t_{\mathrm{id},1},t_{g,1},t_{\mathrm{id},2},t_{g,2},\dots,t_{\mathrm{id},N}).

This theorem follows from
[
Kau03
,
Kau06
,
BTW23
,
BT17
]
on the level of Frobenius algebras at the origin and from
[
BR25
]
on the level of Saito theories.

Main idea behind this theorem is that there is an orbifold equivalence
(A_{2N-1},G)\sim D_{N+1}
and
(D_{N+1},G)\sim A_{2N-1}
that can be obtained via the crepant resolution argument (see Section 5 of
[
BTW23
]
and Section 6 of
[
BT17
]
). Derived categories of the orbifold equivalent pairs are equivalent what suggests (but not proves) that the corresponding Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds are isomorphic. Finally in
[
BR25
]
it was computed that the Saito theories match under this equivalence exaclty with the variable substitution as above.

Denote by
M_{(f_{A_{2N-1}},G),\mathrm{id}}
and
M_{(f_{D_{N+1}},G),\mathrm{id}}
the submanifolds of
M_{(f_{A_{2N-1}},G)}
and
M_{(f_{D_{N+1}},G)}
respectively, obtained by setting all non–trivially graded variables to zero:
t_{g,\bullet}=0
. We will call these submanifolds
invariant sectors
.

General theory constitutes that invariant sectors are Dubrovin–Frobenius submanifolds. In the two cases we investigate in this paper this fact was introduced in Section
2.6
. Proposition
3.1
shows existence of the subhierarchies associated to these submanifolds. In order to prove Theorem
1.1
it remains to show that these subhierarchies are exactly BKP and CKP respectively.

5.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

By the isomorphism
M_{(A_{2N-1},\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})}\cong M_{D_{N+1}}
we have to investigate the system (
3.1
) for the D type Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds. Under this isomorphism
M_{(A_{2N-1},\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}),\mathrm{id}}
is identified with
M^{\prime}_{D_{N+1}}:=\{t_{N+1}=0\}\subset M_{D_{N+1}}
.

By the isomorphism
M_{(D_{N+1},\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})}\cong M_{A_{2N-1}}
we have to investigate the system (
3.1
) for the A type Dubrovin–Frobenius manifolds. Under this isomorphism
M_{(D_{N+1},\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}),\mathrm{id}}
is identified with
M^{\prime}_{A_{2N-1}}:=\{t_{2}=\dots=t_{2N-2}=0\}\subset M_{A_{2N-1}}
.

By Proposition
2.2
, both
M^{\prime}_{D_{N+1}}
and
M^{\prime}_{A_{2N-1}}
are natural submanifolds and by Proposition
3.1
their flows are given exactly by Eq. (
dlD-1
). Theorem 7.1 of
[
BDbN21
]
completes the proof for the dispersionless case.

Dispersionfull statement for A type Landau–Ginzburg orbifolds follows immediately because in the terminology of Section
4.5.1
restricting to the invariant sector we consider only the positive flows on BKP hierarchy.

Dispersionfull statement for D type Landau–Ginzburg orbifolds and CKP hierarchy follows from Theorem
4.1
.

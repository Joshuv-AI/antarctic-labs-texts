Abstract
. In this paper, we study the long-time existence result for small data solutions of quasilinear wave equations exterior to star-shaped regions in two space dimensions. The key novelty is that we establish a Morawetz type energy estimate for the perturbed inhomogeneous wave equation in the exterior domain, which yields t − 1 2 t^{-\frac{1}{2}} decay inside the cone. In addition, two new weighted L 2 L^{2} product estimates are established to produce t − 1 2 t^{-\frac{1}{2}} decay close to the cone. We then show that the existence lifespan T ε T_{\varepsilon} for the quasilinear wave equations with general quadratic nonlinearity satisfies ε 2 ​ T ε ​ ln 3 ​ T ε = A , \varepsilon^{2}T_{\varepsilon}\ln^{3}T_{\varepsilon}=A, for some fixed positive constant A A , which is almost sharp (with some logarithmic loss) comparing to the known result of the corresponding Cauchy problem.

3.

In this section, we will study the following system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lllll}\Box_{h}u=F,\\
u|_{\partial\mathcal{K}}=0,\end{array}\right.

where

\displaystyle\Box_{h}u=\Box u+h^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}u.

We shall assume that

satisfy the symmetry
conditions

(3.2)

\displaystyle h^{\alpha\beta}=h^{\beta\alpha},

as well as the size conditions

(3.3)

\displaystyle|h|=\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{2}|h^{\alpha\beta}|\ll 1.

Define the scaling operator by

S=t\partial_{t}+r\partial_{r}=t\partial_{t}+x\cdot\nabla
. First, we
prove the following scaling Morawetz energy estimate on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\mathcal{K}
for the perturbed wave equation.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be bounded and star-shaped with respect to the origin.
Assume that the perturbation terms
h^{\alpha\beta}
satisfy conditions (
3.2
) and (
3.3

Then for any smooth function

(3.4)

\displaystyle\quad+C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\mathcal{K}}|\partial h||\partial u|\big(|Su|+|u|\big)dx.

Proof.

By multiplying
\Box u

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}(u_{t}^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2})-\nabla\cdot(u_{t}\nabla u),

\Box u

\displaystyle(x\cdot\nabla u)\Box u

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}(u_{t}x\cdot\nabla u)+\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot(x|\nabla u|^{2}-xu_{t}^{2})+u_{t}^{2}

(3.7)

\displaystyle\quad-\nabla\cdot(\nabla ux\cdot\nabla u).

By multiplying
\Box u
by
u/2

(3.8)

\displaystyle(u/2)\Box u

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}(uu_{t})-\frac{1}{2}u_{t}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot(u\nabla u)+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}.

The combination of equalities (

3

\displaystyle\Box u(tu_{t}+ru_{r}+u/2)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot(x|\nabla u|^{2}-xu_{t}^{2})-\nabla\cdot(tu_{t}\nabla u)

\displaystyle\quad-\nabla\cdot(\nabla ux\cdot\nabla u)-\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot(u\nabla u).

Now let’s calculate the perturbation terms. By using the symmetry conditions (
3.2
), for any
\gamma=0,1,2

\displaystyle\big(h^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}u\big)\partial_{\gamma}u

\displaystyle=\partial_{\alpha}\big(h^{\alpha\beta}u_{\gamma}u_{\beta}\big)-\big(\partial_{\alpha}h^{\alpha\beta}\big)u_{\gamma}u_{\beta}-h^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\gamma}\partial_{\alpha}u\partial_{\beta}u

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}(Sh^{\alpha\beta})u_{\alpha}u_{\beta}+\frac{1}{2}h^{\alpha\beta}u_{\alpha}u_{\beta}.

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}(Sh^{\alpha\beta})u_{\alpha}u_{\beta}-\frac{1}{2}\big(\partial_{\alpha}h^{\alpha\beta}\big)uu_{\beta}.

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}(tu_{t}^{2}+t|\nabla u|^{2}+2ru_{t}u_{r}+uu_{t}+h^{0\beta}(2Su+u)u_{\beta}-th^{\alpha\beta}u_{\alpha}u_{\beta})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot(x|\nabla u|^{2}-xu_{t}^{2})-\nabla\cdot(tu_{t}\nabla u)-\nabla\cdot(\nabla ux\cdot\nabla u)-\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot(u\nabla u)

\displaystyle\quad-\big(\partial_{\alpha}h^{\alpha\beta}\big)\big(Su\big)u_{\beta}+\frac{1}{2}(Sh^{\alpha\beta})u_{\alpha}u_{\beta}-\frac{1}{2}\big(\partial_{\alpha}h^{\alpha\beta}\big)uu_{\beta}.

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\mathcal{K}}(\Box_{h}u)(Su+u/2)dx+C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\mathcal{K}}|Sh||\partial u|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\quad+C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\mathcal{K}}|\partial h||\partial u|\big(|Su|+|u|\big)dx.

Lemma 3.4

|b|+j\leqslant k

\displaystyle C\|\Delta\partial_{t}^{j}\tilde{u}\|_{H^{|b|-2}(|x|\leqslant 5/2)}+C\sum_{j\leqslant k-1}\|\partial\partial_{t}^{j}u\|_{L^{2}(|x|\leqslant 5/2)}

\displaystyle C\sum_{|a|\leqslant k-2}\|\partial^{a}\Box u\|_{L^{2}(|x|\leqslant 5/2)}+C\sum_{j\leqslant k-1}\|\partial\partial_{t}^{j}u\|_{L^{2}(|x|\leqslant 5/2)}

\displaystyle+C\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|c|+j\leqslant k\\

Lemma 3.6

(Elliptic Regularity Estimates)
.

Let
\Omega=\mathbb{B}_{t}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\mathcal{K})

where

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leqslant C_{0}\big(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big),

(3.29)

\displaystyle\sum_{|a|=2}^{k}\|\nabla^{a}w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leqslant C_{k}\big(\|f\|_{H^{k-2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big).

Proof.

When
t\leqslant 6
, by the standard elliptic regularity estimates, we
have

\displaystyle\|w\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\big(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\big).

By Poincaré inequality, we have
\|w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

3.28

\widetilde{w}(x)=36/t^{2}w(tx/6)

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}\widetilde{w}\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{\Omega})}\leqslant C\big(\|f(tx/6)\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{\Omega})}+\|\nabla\widetilde{w}\|_{L^{2}(\widetilde{\Omega})}\big),

Similarly, for

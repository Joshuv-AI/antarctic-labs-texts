Weighted wave envelope estimates for the parabola
Jongchon Kim, Hyerim Ko

Abstract
. In this paper, we extend Fefferman’s classical square function estimate for the parabola to a weighted setting. Our weighted square function estimate is derived from a weighted wave envelope estimate for the parabola. The bounds are formulated in terms of families of multiscale tubes together with weight parameters that quantify the distribution of the weight. As an application, we obtain some weighted L p L^{p} -estimates for a class of Fourier multiplier operators and for solutions to free Schrödinger equation.

Introduction

The paper is concerned with weighted square function estimates for the parabola and some of its applications. Let
\mathcal{P}
denote the truncated parabola

\mathcal{P}=\{(t,t^{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|t|\leq 1\}

and
N_{R^{-1}}\mathcal{P}=\{(t,t^{2}+\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|t|\leq 1,\;|\eta|\leq R^{-1}\}
denote its
R^{-1}
-neighborhood for a large
R\geq 1
. We consider the canonical covering of
N_{R^{-1}}\mathcal{P}
by finitely overlapping parallelograms
\theta
of dimensions
R^{-1/2}\times R^{-1}
. Given a function
f
whose Fourier transform is supported on
N_{R^{-1}}\mathcal{P}
, we decompose
f=\sum_{\theta}f_{\theta}
, where
\widehat{f}_{\theta}
is supported on
\theta
.
This can be done, for example, by using a smooth partition of unity subordinate to a covering of the interval
[-1,1]
by finitely overlapping intervals of length
\sim R^{-1/2}
(see e.g. the proof of
Theorem

2.1
).

By Plancherel’s theorem, these functions
\{f_{\theta}\}
are orthogonal on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
:
\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\sum_{\theta}\|f_{\theta}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
. Moreover, the family exhibits certain
L^{p}
orthogonality due to the curvature properties of the parabola for some
p
larger than
2
. For instance, the classical square function estimate for the parabola (see
[
9
]
) states that

(1)

\|f\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\big\|\big(\sum_{\theta}|f_{\theta}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\big\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

This inequality relies on the geometric observation by Fefferman
[
14
]
that the algebraic differences
\theta-\theta^{\prime}
overlap only finitely often as
\theta\neq\theta^{\prime}
vary. See also
[
17
,
30

20
]
for extensions to non-degenerate curves in higher dimensions. Square function estimates of the form (
1
) have several important applications in harmonic analysis. The sharp square function estimate (
1
) is known to imply sharp results for the Kakeya maximal function, the Bochner-Riesz multipliers, the Fourier restriction operator, and local smoothing estimates for the Schrödinger equation; see
[
6
,
41
]
and references therein. In higher dimensions, it is conjectured that (
1
) holds with
L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
replaced by
L^{\frac{2d}{d-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, which remains wide open.

Let
H:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,\infty)
be a bounded function on
\mathbb{R}^{2}

\|f\|_{L^{p}(Hdx)}\leq C_{p,H}(R)\big\|\big(\sum_{\theta}|f_{\theta}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

for
2\leq p\leq 4

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

and
H:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,1]
be a function. For any function

whose Fourier transform is supported on
N_{R^{-1}}(\mathcal{P})
, we have,

(4)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}(Hdx)}\lessapprox\big(\max_{U\in\mathbb{U}}\kappa_{p,H}(U)+R^{-100}\big)\big\|\big(\sum_{\theta}|f_{\theta}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Here we mean by
A\lessapprox B

A\leq C_{p}(\log R)^{O(1)}B
. The term
R^{-100}
on the right-hand side of (

\max_{U\in\mathbb{U}}\kappa_{p,H}(U)\gg R^{-100}
whenever
H
is the characteristic function of a union of unit balls.
We also use the notation

to denote

with an absolute constant

Example 1

(

\displaystyle\langle H\rangle_{\alpha}:=\sup_{(z,\rho)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,\infty]}\rho^{-\alpha}H(B_{\rho}(z))\lesssim 1.

\max_{U\in\mathbb{U}}\kappa_{p,H}(U)\;\lesssim\;R^{-(2-\alpha)\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{4}\right)}.

\displaystyle H(T)

\displaystyle\lesssim s^{-1}s^{-\alpha},

\displaystyle H(U)

On the other hand,

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
2\leq p\leq 4
and
H:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\{0\}\cup[R^{-400},1]
be a weight. If
\widehat{f}
is supported on
N_{R^{-1}}(\mathcal{P})
, we have

(8)

\|f\|_{L^{p}(Hdx)}^{p}\lessapprox\sum_{R^{-1/2}\leq s\leq 1}\sum_{|\tau|=s}\sum_{U\in\mathbb{U}_{\tau}}\kappa_{p,H}(U)^{p}|U|^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\big\|(\sum_{\theta\subset\tau}|f_{\theta}|^{2})^{1/2}\big\|_{L^{2}(w_{U})}^{p}.

Here
w_{U}
denotes an
L^{\infty}
-normalized weight which decays rapidly away from
U
.

The estimate (
8
) is sharp for the unit ball example in
2
, where the term
s=1
on the right-hand side of (
8
) dominates. We present an example where
\|f\|_{L^{p}(Hdx)}^{p}
essentially matches the contribution from
s=R^{-1/2}
.

Example 3
.

Let
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
H=1_{Y}
. Consider
f=\sum_{\theta}f_{\theta}
such that
\{f_{\theta}\}
have essentially disjoint supports on
Y
. Then

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}(Y)}^{p}\sim\sum_{\theta}\|f_{\theta}\|_{L^{p}(Y)}^{p}=\sum_{\theta}\sum_{U\in\mathbb{U}_{\theta}}\|f_{\theta}\|_{L^{p}(U\cap Y)}^{p}.

We further assume that
|f_{\theta}|
is essentially constant on each
U\in\mathbb{U}_{\theta}
, which is natural in view of the uncertainty principle. Then

(9)

\|f_{\theta}\|_{L^{p}(U\cap Y)}^{p}\sim\frac{|U\cap Y|}{|U|}\|f_{\theta}\|_{L^{p}(U)}^{p}\sim\frac{|U\cap Y|}{|U|}|U|^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\|f_{\theta}\|_{L^{2}(U)}^{p}.

Hence,

\|f\|_{L^{p}(Y)}^{p}\;\sim\;\sum_{|\theta|=R^{-1/2}}\sum_{U\in\mathbb{U}_{\theta}}\frac{|U\cap Y|}{|U|}\,|U|^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\,\big\|f_{\theta}\big\|_{L^{2}(U)}^{p}.

We note that
\mathbb{U}_{\theta}=\mathbb{T}_{\theta}
forms an identical tiling of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by parallelepipeds of dimensions
R^{1/2}\times R
. Therefore,

(10)

\displaystyle\kappa_{p,H}(U)^{p}=\frac{|U\cap Y|}{|U|},\;\;U\in\mathbb{U}_{\theta}.

Thus,
\|f\|_{L^{p}(Y)}^{p}
is comparable to the
s=R^{-1/2}
term on the right-hand side of (
8
).

Wave envelope estimates, namely estimates of the form (
8

Their wave envelope estimate for
\Gamma
implies, among other consequences, the sharp
L^{4}
square function estimate for
\Gamma
and the sharp local smoothing estimate for the wave equation in 2+1 dimension.
The

1.4
recovers the

[

We note that
[

established more refined versions of the

wave envelope estimates, termed amplitude-dependent wave envelope estimates, for both the parabola
\mathcal{P}
and the cone
\Gamma

For the proof of

2.1.
Weighted wave envelope estimates imply weighted square function estimates

In this section, we prove that
Theorem

1.4
implies
Theorem

1.1
.

Let
2\leq p\leq 4
. We first verify (
4
) for weights
H:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[R^{-400},1]
.
By Hölder’s inequality,
Theorem

1.4
yields

\|f\|_{L^{p}(Hdx)}^{p}\lessapprox\sum_{R^{-1/2}\leq s\leq 1}\sum_{|\tau|=s}\sum_{U\in\mathbb{U}_{\tau}}\kappa_{p,H}(U)^{p}\big\|\big(\sum_{\theta\subset\tau}|f_{\theta}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\big\|_{L^{p}(w_{U})}^{p}.

After dominating
\kappa_{p,H}(U)^{p}
by
\sup_{U\in\mathbb{U}}\kappa_{p,H}(U)^{p}
, we sum over all
U
. This yields, for each
s
,

\displaystyle\sum_{|\tau|=s}\sum_{U\in\mathbb{U}_{\tau}}\big\|\big(\sum_{\theta\subset\tau}|f_{\theta}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\big\|_{L^{p}(w_{U})}^{p}\lesssim\sum_{|\tau|=s}\big\|\big(\sum_{\theta\subset\tau}|f_{\theta}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\big\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\leq\big\|\big(\sum_{\theta}|f_{\theta}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\big\|_{L^{p}}^{p}.

For the last inequality, we use embedding
\ell^{2}\subset\ell^{p}
for
p\geq 2
.
Since
s
ranges over dyadic numbers in
[R^{-\frac{1}{2}},1]
, this gives
Theorem

1.1
when
H:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[R^{-400},1]
.

For the case
H:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,1]
, we decompose
H=H_{1}+H_{2}
, where
0\leq H_{1}\leq R^{-400}
and
R^{-400}\leq H_{2}\leq 1
. For
H_{2}
, we have already obtained a bound which involves
\sup_{U\in\mathbb{U}}\kappa_{p,H}(U)
. For
H_{1}
, we use the unweighted case (
H\equiv 1
) to get

\|f\|_{L^{p}(H_{1})}\leq R^{-100}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lessapprox R^{-100}\big\|\big(\sum_{\theta}|f_{\theta}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\big\|_{L^{p}}.

Combining these estimates yields
Theorem

1.1
.
∎

2.2.
Weighted estimates for a radial Fourier multiplier

Let
\psi
be a smooth bump function supported on
[-1,1]
.
We consider the Fourier multiplier transformation
S_{R}
defined by

\widehat{S_{R}f}(\xi)=\psi(R(1-|\xi|))\widehat{f}(\xi),

which plays a critical role in the theory of Bochner-Riesz means. It is well-known that

(11)

\|S_{R}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lessapprox\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},\;\;2\leq p\leq 4,

which follows from an interpolation of the trivial
L^{2}
-bound and the sharp
L^{4}
-bound due to Córdoba
[
9
]
which relies on the square function estimate (
1
) and bounds for the Nikodym maximal function.

We present a weighted version of (
11
). For each dyadic scale
R^{-1/2}\leq s\leq 1
, we cover
N_{R^{-1}}\mathbb{S}^{1}
by finitely overlapping rectangles
\tau
of dimensions
s\times s^{2}
and define
\mathbb{T}_{\tau}
,
\mathbb{U}_{\tau}
and
\mathbb{U}
, accordingly. With this minor modification in mind, we obtain the following.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
2\leq p\leq 4
and
H:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,1]
be a function. Then

\|S_{R}f\|_{L^{p}(Hdx)}\lessapprox\big(\max_{U\in\mathbb{U}}\kappa_{p,H}(U)+R^{-100}\big)\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Proof.

The proof is essentially the same as the proof of (
11
) by Córdoba
[
9
]
, so we only sketch the argument. We divide
\mathbb{R}^{2}
into four sectors by lines
y=\pm x
. Without loss of generality, we may replace
S_{R}
by a smooth frequency projection to the part of
N_{R^{-1}}\mathbb{S}^{1}
contained in one of the four sectors which includes the point
(0,-1)
.

Next, we cover
[-1,1]
by finitely overlapping intervals
I
of length
\sim R^{-1/2}
, and let
\{\chi_{I}\}
be a smooth partition of unity adapted to this covering. Then we have
S_{R}f=\sum_{I}S_{R}f_{I}
, where
\widehat{f_{I}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=\chi_{I}(\xi_{1})\widehat{f}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
. By
Theorem

1.1
, we have

\|S_{R}f\|_{L^{p}(Hdx)}\lessapprox\big(\max_{U\in\mathbb{U}}\kappa_{p,H}(U)+R^{-100}\big)\big\|\big(\sum_{I}|S_{R}f_{I}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

By duality and the boundedness of the Nikodym maximal function, for any
2\leq p\leq 4
,

\big\|\big(\sum_{I}|S_{R}f_{I}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lessapprox\big\|\big(\sum_{I}|f_{I}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Finally, by the Littlewood-Paley inequality for equally spaced intervals, we have

(12)

\displaystyle\big\|\big(\sum_{I}|f_{I}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},\;\;\;p\geq 2,

which completes the proof.
∎

2.3.
Weighted estimates for the Schrödinger equation

Let

e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}f(x)=(2\pi)^{-1}\int e^{ix\xi}e^{it\xi^{2}}\widehat{f}(\xi)\,d\xi

denote the solution to the free Schrödinger equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u=\partial_{x}^{2}u,&(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\\
u(x,0)=f(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

Let
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
be such that
\widehat{\eta}
is compactly supported on
[-1,1]
and
|\eta(t)|\sim 1
on
[-1,1]
. We define the operator
\mathbf{U}_{R}
by

(13)

\mathbf{U}_{R}f(x,t)=\eta(R^{-1}t)e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}f(x).

If
\widehat{f}
is supported on
[-1,1]
, then
\widehat{\mathbf{U}_{R}f}
is supported on
N_{R^{-1}}\mathcal{P}
.

As another consequence of
Theorem

1.1
, we establish a weighted estimate for the Schrödinger propagator.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
2\leq p\leq 4
and
H:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,1]
be a function. For any function
f
whose Fourier transform is supported on
[-1,1]
, we have

\|\mathbf{U}_{R}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2},Hdxdt)}\lessapprox\big(\max_{U\in\mathbb{U}}\kappa_{p,H}(U)+R^{-100}\big)R^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}.

Proof.

Let
\{\chi_{I}\}
be the smooth partition of unity given in
Theorem

2.1
. We decompose
f=\sum_{I}f_{I}
, where
\widehat{f_{I}}=\chi_{I}\widehat{f}
. By
Theorem

1.1
, we have

\|\mathbf{U}_{R}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2},Hdxdt)}\lessapprox\big(\max_{U\in\mathbb{U}}\kappa_{p,H}(U)+R^{-100}\big)\big\|\big(\sum_{I}|\mathbf{U}_{R}f_{I}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Thus, it suffices to verify that

(14)

\displaystyle\Big\|\big(\sum_{I}|\mathbf{U}_{R}f_{I}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lessapprox R^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},\;\;2\leq p\leq 4.

A detailed proof of (
14
) can be found in a note by Yung
[
41
, Proof of Theorem 2]
. It follows from a standard duality argument similar to the one used in
[
9
]
and
[
33
]
. For completeness, we sketch the argument in
Section

5.3
.
∎

As a corollary, we state a special case for
\alpha
-dimensional measures.
For
0<\alpha<1
and a measure
\mu
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we set

(15)

\displaystyle\langle\mu\rangle_{\alpha}:=\sup_{(z,\rho)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,\infty)}\rho^{-\alpha}\mu(B_{\rho}(z)).

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
2\leq p\leq 4
and
0\leq\alpha\leq 2
.
For any function
f
whose Fourier transform is supported on
[-1,1]
and
\mu
satisfying
\langle\mu\rangle_{\alpha}\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\big\|\mathbf{U}_{R}f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,R],\mu)}\lessapprox R^{\frac{1}{p}-(2-\alpha)\big(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{4}\big)}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}.

Proof.

Recall (
13
).
By the Fourier localization property of
\mathbf{U}_{R}f
, we may write
\mathbf{U}_{R}f=\mathbf{U}_{R}f*\varphi
for a Schwartz function
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
Consequently, by Hölder’s inequality,

\big|\mathbf{U}_{R}f\ast\varphi\big|^{p}\lesssim|\mathbf{U}_{R}f|^{p}\ast|\varphi|

for
p\geq 1
. It follows that

\int\big|\mathbf{U}_{R}f(x,t)\big|^{p}\,d\mu(x,t)\lesssim\int\big|\mathbf{U}_{R}f(x,t)\big|^{p}H(x,t)\,dxdt

where

H=\mu\ast|\varphi|.

We check that
H
is
\alpha
-dimensional, using the dyadic decomposition
|\varphi|\lesssim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{-10j}1_{B_{2^{j}}}
. By using the decay and the assumption that
\langle\mu\rangle_{\alpha}\lesssim 1
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}(z)}H\lesssim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{-8j}\mu(B_{\rho}(z)+B_{2^{j}})\lesssim\rho^{\alpha}.

Thus,
\langle H\rangle_{\alpha}\lesssim 1
. A similar computation shows that
\|H\|_{\infty}\lesssim 1
. Consequently,
Theorem

2.2
and (
5
) give the desired estimate.

2.4.
Fractal local smoothing estimates relative to parabolic balls

The estimate (
16
) can be regarded as an analogue of the local smoothing phenomenon for the wave equation, first discovered by Sogge
[
37
]
. Indeed, comparing (
16
) with the sharp fixed-time estimate due to Miyachi
[
32
]
,

\|e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim_{p,\gamma}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R})},\;\;p\in(1,\infty)\;\text{and}\;\gamma\geq\Big|1-\frac{2}{p}\Big|,

it follows that averaging over a compact time interval yields a gain of
2/p
derivatives whenever
p>4
. In the context of the Schrödinger equation, local smoothing estimates generally refer to such derivative gains obtained by averaging over a compact space-time region (see e.g.
[
36
]
).

Rogers
[
35
]
proved (
16
) by connecting it to the Fourier restriction estimate for the parabola. See
[
41
]
for a proof of (
16
) which relies on the square function estimate (
1
).

We seek to extend estimates of the form (
16
) to general measures on
\mathbb{R}\times[0,1]
that satisfy suitable size conditions. We refer to these as
fractal local smoothing estimates
for the Schrödinger equation.

For
0\leq\beta\leq 3
, we consider a class of Borel measures on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
for which

\displaystyle[\mu]_{\beta,\text{par}}:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\rho>0}\rho^{-\beta}\mu\big(B_{\rho,\text{par}}(z)\big)\lesssim 1,

where
B_{\rho,\textit{par}}(z)
denotes the parabolic “ball”
(z_{1}-\rho,z_{1}+\rho)\times(z_{2}-\rho^{2},z_{2}+\rho^{2})
for
z=(z_{1},z_{2})
.
This class of measures naturally arises in view of the parabolic rescaling associated with the Schrödinger equation. For such measures, we consider the estimate

(18)

\displaystyle\big\|e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,1],\mu)}\leq C[\mu]_{\beta,\text{par}}^{1/p}\|f\|_{L_{\gamma}^{p}(\mathbb{R})}.

2.5.
Fractal local smoothing estimates

We now consider fractal local smoothing estimates for the Schrödinger operator with
\alpha
-dimensional measures in the standard (non-parabolic) sense. Let
0\leq\alpha\leq 2
and consider a class of Borel measures on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
for which

(21)

\displaystyle[\mu]_{\alpha}:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\;\rho>0}\rho^{-\alpha}\mu\big(B_{\rho}(z)\big)<\infty.

Here, the condition (
21
) differs slightly from
\langle\mu\rangle_{\alpha}
defined in (
15
), in that the supremum is taken over all
\rho>0
, rather than
\rho>1
.

In the
L^{2}
setting, a variety of results for general fractal measures are known (see, e.g.,
[
40
,
31
,
34
,
12
]
, but much less is understood beyond the
L^{2}
framework.
For product measures, however, optimal results have been obtained by Lee–Lee–Roncal
[
28
]
,
with further developments connected to Assouad dimensions.
Suppose
\nu
is supported on
[0,1]
and satisfies

\nu((t-\rho,t+\rho))\leq C\rho^{\alpha},\quad(t,\rho)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}.

In
[
28
, Theorem 1.6]
, it is shown that if
\gamma\geq-\frac{\alpha}{2}
, then
H^{\gamma}
–
L_{t}^{2}(d\nu;L_{x}^{2}(-1,1))
estimates holds for
e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}f
.
Moreover, optimal results in higher dimensions and weighted Strichartz-type estimates of the form
H^{\gamma}
–
L_{t}^{q}(d\nu;L_{x}^{r})
for fractional Schrödinger operators are established in
[
28
]
(see also
[
4
]
for analogous results for the wave operator).

For the wave equation, the situation is better understood: not only are the product-type estimates optimal (see
[
4
]
), but there are also extensive results on
L^{p}-L^{q}
estimates with respect to more general fractal measures (see
[
34
,
8
,
25
,
23
,
7
]
; see also
[
24
,
22
]
for related results in the case of product measures).

We consider the local smoothing type estimates for the Schrödinger operator relative to fractal measure
\mu
:

(22)

\displaystyle\big\|e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,1],\mu)}\leq C[\mu]_{\alpha}^{1/p}\|f\|_{L_{\gamma}^{p}(\mathbb{R})}

for some
C=C_{\alpha,p,\gamma}
.
By combining the weighted square function estimates in
Theorem

1.1
with the standard strategy, we obtain the following.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
0\leq\alpha\leq 2
, and let
\mu
be a Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
[\mu]_{\alpha}\leq 1
.

(i)

(Sufficiency)
For
2\leq p\leq 4
, the estimate (
22
) holds whenever

\gamma>\gamma(\alpha,p):=\begin{cases}\frac{2-\alpha}{2},&\alpha\in[1,2],\\[3.44444pt]
\frac{2-\alpha}{2}+\frac{\alpha-1}{p},&\alpha\in[0,1].\end{cases}

(ii)

(Necessity)
Conversely, (
22
) can hold only if

\gamma\geq\begin{cases}\max\!\big(1-\frac{2\alpha}{p},\,\frac{2-\alpha}{p}\big),&\alpha\in[1,2],\\[3.44444pt]

The sufficient conditions
\gamma(\alpha,p)
are essentially sharp for
p=4
for all
\alpha\in[0,2]
and
p=2
for
\alpha\in[1,2]
.
We prove the sufficiency part of
Theorem

2.4
in
Section

5
and the necessity part in
Section

A lower bound for the weighted square function estimate

We give a lower bound for the weighted square function estimate by using an example from

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\Big(\max_{R^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq s\leq R^{-\kappa}}R^{-\frac{3\kappa}{4}}(s^{-1}R^{-(\frac{1}{2}+3\kappa)})^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{4}},\max_{R^{-\kappa}\leq s\leq 1}s^{\frac{3}{4}}(s^{-1}R^{-(\frac{1}{2}+3\kappa)})^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{4}}\Big).

Recall that
2\leq p\leq 4
. Substituting
\kappa=(2-\alpha)/6
yields

\displaystyle\max_{U\in\mathbb{U}}\kappa_{p,H}(U)

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\Big(R^{-\frac{3\kappa}{p}},R^{-(\frac{1}{2}+3\kappa)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{4})}\Big)=\max\Big(R^{-\frac{2-\alpha}{2p}},~R^{-\frac{3-\alpha}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{4})}\Big),

which is equivalent to (
25
).
∎

4.
Weighted wave envelope estimates

In this section, we prove

4.1.
Reductions

Let

We first reduce

when

Assume that (

By the assumption on

and a dyadic pigeonholing, there exists a dyadic number

and a subset

Using (

For the rest of the section, we prove (

4.2.
Decomposition

For
R\geq 1
, let
K
be a dyadic number such that
K\sim\log R
. We choose

such that
K^{m}\sim R^{1/2}
.
We consider larger canonical covering
\tau

|\tau|=s
for each scale

At the smallest scale for

of size

For each block
\tau
at an intermediate scale, we define

f_{\tau}=\sum_{\theta\subset\tau}f_{\theta}.

We use the following pointwise estimate from
[

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, there is an absolute constant

(independent of

(32)

|f(x)|^{p}\leq C^{m}\sum_{\theta}|f_{\theta}(x)|^{p}+C^{m}K^{p}\sum_{R^{-1/2}<s\leq 1}\sum_{|\tau|=s}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\subset\tau\\
|\tau_{1}|=|\tau_{2}|=K^{-1}s\\
d(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\geq\frac{1}{2}K^{-1}s\end{subarray}}|f_{\tau_{1}}(x)f_{\tau_{2}}(x)|^{\frac{p}{2}},

where

and

The inequality follows from the Bourgain–Guth argument

By integrating (

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}(Y)}^{p}\!\leq\!C^{m}\sum_{\theta}\|f_{\theta}\|_{L^{p}(Y)}^{p}+C^{m}K^{p}\sum_{R^{-1/2}<s\leq 1}\sum_{|\tau|=s}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\subset\tau\\

By the computation (

This corresponds to the contribution at the smallest scale

in (

Thus it remains to control the bilinear terms, which reduces to the following proposition.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let

and
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\subset\tau

,

Summing (

Since for each fixed

Proof of

We claim that

Here, by definition (

Having established

4.4.

It remains to establish (

For each

For any unit cube

\int_{B\cap\widetilde{Y}}|g_{\tau_{1}}g_{\tau_{2}}|^{2}\,\lessapprox\max_{q\subset B}\frac{|q\cap\widetilde{Y}|}{|q|}|B|^{-1}\|g_{\tau_{1}}\|_{L^{2}(w_{B})}^{2}\|g_{\tau_{2}}\|_{L^{2}(w_{B})}^{2}

It remains to prove

Theorem 4.4

Let

and
B

and
\tau_{2}

Proof of

4.3
.

Since

More precisely, we may choose
\phi=\phi_{N}:=(1+|\cdot|)^{-N}

|g_{\tau_{1}}|^{2}|g_{\tau_{2}}|^{2}\lesssim|g_{\tau_{1}}|^{2}|g_{\tau_{2}}|^{2}*\phi.

|g_{\tau_{1}}|^{2}|g_{\tau_{2}}|^{2}*\phi(x)\sim|g_{\tau_{1}}|^{2}|g_{\tau_{2}}|^{2}*\phi(y)

The integral on the right-hand side of (

\int|g_{\tau_{1}}g_{\tau_{2}}|^{2}\phi*1_{B},

where

is a
L^{\infty}
-normalized weight which decays rapidly away from

5.1.
Rescaling and reductions

We reduce
Theorem

2.4
and
Theorem

2.5
to
2.3

[\mu]_{\alpha}=1
and
[\mu]_{\beta,\text{par}}=1
for the proofs of

2.4
and

2.5

Fix

. Given a measure
\mu
, we need to verify

\big\|e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,1];\mu)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R})}

for all

and

respectively. By a standard Littlewood–Paley reduction, it suffices to show that

\|e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,1];\mu)}\lessapprox R^{\gamma}\|f\|_{p}

for any function

whose Fourier transform is supported on
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}:|\xi|\leq R\}
.

For the purpose, we define
f_{R}(x)=f(R^{-1}x)
so that
\widehat{f_{R}}
is supported on

and
\|f_{R}\|_{p}=R^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{p}
. A change of variable
\xi\rightarrow R\xi
gives

\displaystyle|e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}f(x)|\sim|\mathbf{U}_{R^{2}}f_{R}(Rx,R^{2}t)|,\;\;(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times[0,1],

where
\mathbf{U}_{R^{2}}
is defined in (

We also define the rescaled measure
\mu_{R}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by
\mu_{R}(E)=\int 1_{E}(Rx,R^{2}t)d\mu(x,t)
so that we have

\int|\mathbf{U}_{R^{2}}f_{R}|^{p}(x,t)\,d\mu_{R}(x,t)=\int|\mathbf{U}_{R^{2}}f_{R}|^{p}(Rx,R^{2}t)\,d\mu(x,t).

Thus, we have

(42)

\big\|e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,1];\mu)}\lesssim\big\|\mathbf{U}_{R^{2}}f_{R}\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mu_{R})}.

Lemma 5.1
.

For
\mu_{R}
defined as above, we have

(1)

If
[\mu]_{\beta,\text{par}}\leq 1
, then

\displaystyle\langle\mu_{R}\rangle_{(\beta+1)/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-\beta},\;\;\;\beta\in[1,3],

\displaystyle\langle\mu_{R}\rangle_{\beta}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-\beta},\;\;\;\beta\in[0,1].

(2)

If
[\mu]_{\alpha}\leq 1
, then

\langle\mu_{R}\rangle_{\alpha}\lesssim\begin{cases}R^{1-2\alpha},&\alpha\in[1,2],\\
R^{-\alpha},&\alpha\in[0,1].\end{cases}.

Proof.

Consider the ball
B_{\rho}(z)
of radius
\rho\geq 1
for
z=(z_{1},z_{2})
.
By the definition of
\mu_{R}
, we have

\mu_{R}\big(B_{\rho}(z)\big)\leq\mu\Big(\big(\frac{z_{1}-\rho}{R},\frac{z_{1}+\rho}{R}\big)\times\big(\frac{z_{2}-\rho}{R^{2}},\frac{z_{2}+\rho}{R^{2}}\big)\Big).

Suppose that
[\mu]_{\beta,\text{par}}=1
.
We may cover the rectangle

\big(\frac{z_{1}-\rho}{R},\frac{z_{1}+\rho}{R}\big)\times\big(\frac{z_{2}-\rho}{R^{2}},\frac{z_{2}+\rho}{R^{2}}\big)

by

\mu_{R}\big(B_{\rho}(z)\big)\lesssim\min\big(\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}/R\big)^{\beta},(\rho/R)^{\beta}\big)=R^{-\beta}\min(\rho^{\frac{1+\beta}{2}},\rho^{\beta}),

which verifies the claim.

Next, assume that
[\mu]_{\alpha}=1

\big(\frac{z_{1}-\rho}{R},\frac{z_{1}+\rho}{R}\big)\times\big(\frac{z_{2}-\rho}{R^{2}},\frac{z_{2}+\rho}{R^{2}}\big)

,
we observe that

|K_{J}^{t}(x)|=(2\pi R)^{-1}\Big|\eta(R^{-2}t)\int e^{iR^{-1}(x+2tc_{J})\xi+iR^{-2}t\xi^{2}}\tilde{\psi}(2\xi)\,d\xi\Big|.

Integration by parts yields the decay estimate

(45)

\displaystyle|K_{J}^{t}(x)|\leq C_{N}R^{-1}(1+R^{-1}|x+2tc_{J}|+R^{-2}|t|)^{-N},\quad N\geq 1,

which, in particular, implies
\|K_{J}^{t}\|_{L_{x}^{1}}\lesssim 1
.
Consequently, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

|\mathbf{U}_{R^{2}}f_{J}(x,t)|^{2}\lesssim|K_{J}^{t}|*|f_{J}|^{2}(x).

For
q=(p/2)^{\prime}
, let
g\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
\|g\|_{L^{q}}=1
. Thus we obtain

\int\sum_{J}|\mathbf{U}_{R^{2}}f_{J}(x,t)|^{2}g(x,t)\,dxdt\lesssim\int\sum_{J}|f_{J}(y)|^{2}\mathfrak{M}g(y)\,dy,

where
\mathfrak{M}g
is defined by

\mathfrak{M}g(y)=\sup_{J}\int|K_{J}^{t}(x-y)g(x,t)|\,dxdt,\quad y\in\mathbb{R}.

By duality and Hölder, we find that

\Big\|\big(\sum_{J}|\mathbf{U}_{R^{2}}f_{J}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L^{p}}^{2}\lesssim\big\|\big(\sum_{J}|f_{J}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\big\|_{p}^{2}\,\,\big\|\mathfrak{M}\big\|_{L^{q}\to L^{q}}.

By the one-dimensional analogue of (
12
), for
2\leq p\leq\infty
,

\big\|\big(\sum_{J}|f_{J}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}.

Thus in order to establish (
44
), it remains to prove that for
2\leq p\leq 4
,

(46)

\displaystyle\big\|\mathfrak{M}\big\|_{L^{q}\to L^{q}}\lessapprox R^{2\cdot\frac{2}{p}},\;\;q=\Big(\frac{p}{2}\Big)^{\prime}.

To show this, for
w\in[-1,1]
we set

T_{w}=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|x+2tw|\leq R^{-1},~|t|\leq 1\},

and define the Nikodym maximal function by

\mathfrak{N}g(y):=\sup_{w\in[-1,1]}\frac{1}{|T_{w}|}\int_{T_{w}+(y,0)}|g(x,t)|\,dxdt,\quad y\in\mathbb{R}.

The Nikodym maximal function satisfies the following bounds; see e.g.
[
33
,
41
]
.

Proposition 5.3
.

For
2\leq q\leq\infty
and
R\geq 1
, we have

\|\mathfrak{N}g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R})}\lessapprox\|g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R})}.

In view of (
45
), the operator
\mathfrak{M}
can be dominated by a rescaled version of
\mathfrak{N}
. Indeed, after the rescaling
y\rightarrow R^{2}y
and
(x,t)\rightarrow(R^{2}x,R^{2}t)
and noting
|T_{w}|=R^{-1}
, we have

\|\mathfrak{M}\|_{q\rightarrow q}\lesssim R^{2}R^{-\frac{2}{q}}\|\mathfrak{N}\|_{q\rightarrow q}\lessapprox R^{\frac{2}{q^{\prime}}}=R^{\frac{4}{p}},

which verifies (
46
).

6.
Examples for lower bounds

In this section, we discuss the lower bounds for the regularity
\zeta
and
\gamma
in
2.3
,
Theorem

2.4
, and
Theorem

2.5
.

6.1.
Lower bounds for
\zeta
in
2.3

We show that (
17
) holds only if

(47)

\displaystyle\zeta\geq\zeta(\alpha,p):=\max\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p},~\min\Big(\frac{\alpha}{2p},\frac{2\alpha-1}{2p}\Big)\Big).

(i)
Proof of
\zeta\geq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}
.

To show this, we use an example from
[
35
]
. Let
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([1/4,4])
be such that
\psi=1
on
[1/2,2]
. We take
\widehat{f}(\xi)=e^{-iR\xi^{2}}\psi(\xi)
so that

f(x)=(2\pi)^{-1}\int e^{ix\xi-iR\xi^{2}}\psi(\xi)\,d\xi.

By integration by parts, we obtain
|f(x)|\lesssim(R+|x|)^{-N}
for
|x|\geq 10R

while the stationary phase method gives
|f(x)|\lesssim R^{-1/2}
for
|x|\leq 10R
. Hence

\|f\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim R^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{p}}.

Note that

|\mathbf{U}_{R}f(x)|\geq(2\pi)^{-1}\Big|\int e^{ix\xi+i(t-R)\xi^{2}}\psi(\xi)\,d\xi\Big|.

In particular, one has
|\mathbf{U}_{R}f(x)|\gtrsim 1
on the set

F=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|x|\leq c,~|t-R|\leq c\}

for some small constant
c>0
. Thus

\displaystyle\mu(F)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim\big\|\mathbf{U}_{R}f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,R],\mu)}\lesssim\langle\mu\rangle_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{p}}R^{\zeta}R^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{p}}

follows.
If we take
\mu=1_{F}(x,t)dxdt
, then
\langle\mu\rangle_{\alpha}\leq 1
for any
\alpha\in[0,2]
. Since
\mu(F)\sim 1
, we obtain
\zeta\geq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}
as desired.

(ii)
Proof of
\zeta\geq\min\big(\frac{\alpha}{2p},\frac{2\alpha-1}{2p}\big)
.

For a smooth function
\psi
as above, choose
g
such that
\widehat{g}(\xi)=\psi(R^{\frac{1}{2}}(\xi+1))
.
Then
\|g\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim R^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2p}}
.

By the change of variable
\xi\rightarrow R^{-\frac{1}{2}}\xi-1
, ignoring the extra oscillatory factor independent of
\xi
, we obtain

\big|\mathbf{U}_{R}g(x)\big|=(2\pi)^{-1}\Big|\int e^{ix\xi+it\xi^{2}}\psi(R^{\frac{1}{2}}(\xi+1))\,d\xi\Big|=(2\pi)^{-1}R^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big|\int e^{iR^{-1/2}(x-2t)\xi+itR^{-1}\xi^{2}}\psi(\xi)\,d\xi\Big|.

Thus
|\mathbf{U}_{R}g(x)|\gtrsim R^{-\frac{1}{2}}
for
(x,t)\in G
where

(48)

\displaystyle G=\{(x,t):|x-2t|\leq cR^{1/2},~|t|\leq R\}

for a small constant
c>0
. Thus

(49)

\displaystyle R^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mu(G)^{\frac{1}{p}}\lesssim\big\|e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}g\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,R],\mu)}\lesssim\langle\mu\rangle_{\alpha}^{1/p}R^{\zeta-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2p}}.

For a set
G
given in (
48
), cover
G
by union of
O(R^{\frac{1}{2}})
disjoint balls of radius
R^{\frac{1}{2}}
. When
\alpha\in[1,2]
we obtain

\displaystyle|G\cap B_{\rho}(z)|\lesssim\begin{cases}\rho^{2}\leq R^{\frac{2-\alpha}{2}}\rho^{\alpha},\quad~&\rho\in[1,R^{1/2}],\\[3.44444pt]
R^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho\leq R^{\frac{2-\alpha}{2}}\rho^{\alpha},\quad~&\rho\in[R^{1/2},\infty).\end{cases}

When
\alpha\in[0,1]
, we have

|G\cap B_{\rho}(z)|\lesssim\begin{cases}\rho^{2}\leq R^{\frac{2-\alpha}{2}}\rho^{\alpha}\leq R^{\frac{3}{2}-\alpha}\rho^{\alpha},\quad~&\rho\in[1,R],\\[3.44444pt]
R^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho\leq R^{\frac{3}{2}-\alpha}\rho^{\alpha},\quad~&\rho\in[R^{1/2},R],\\[3.44444pt]
R^{\frac{3}{2}}\leq R^{\frac{3}{2}-\alpha}\rho^{\alpha},\quad~&\rho\in[R,\infty).\end{cases}

We now take
\mu=\min\big(R^{\frac{\alpha-2}{2}},R^{\alpha-\frac{3}{2}}\big)1_{G}(x,t)dxdt
. Then
\langle\mu\rangle_{\alpha}\leq 1
follows.
Since
|G|\sim R^{\frac{3}{2}}
, we have
\mu(G)\sim\min\big(R^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}},R^{\alpha}\big)
.
Thus (
49
) yields the lower bounds
\zeta\geq\min\big(\frac{\alpha}{2p},\frac{2\alpha-1}{2p}\big)
.

Appendix A
Proof of
Lemma

4.1

In this section, we prove
Lemma

4.1
, motivated by the argument in
[
5
]
.
The following elementary lemma will be used repeatedly.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a sequence of non-negative real number indexed by a finite set
I
. For each
i\in I
, let
I_{i}\subset I
be a subset containing
i
such that
|I_{i}|\leq C_{1}
for all
i\in I
for some
constant
C_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
. Then there exists
C=C(C_{1},p)
such that for
p\geq 1
,

\Big(\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}\Big)^{p}\leq C\Big(\max_{i\in I}a_{i}^{p}+(\#I)^{p}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I,\\
j\notin I_{i}\end{subarray}}a_{i}^{\frac{p}{2}}a_{j}^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big).

Proof.

Let
*,**\in I
be the indices for which
a_{*}=\max_{i\in I}a_{i}
and
a_{**}=\max_{j\notin I_{*}}a_{j}
. Then, for any
i\notin I_{*}
we have
a_{i}\leq a_{*}^{1/2}a_{**}^{1/2}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I_{*}}a_{i}+\sum_{i\notin I_{*}}a_{i}\leq C_{1}a_{*}+(\#I)a_{*}^{1/2}a_{**}^{1/2}.

Taking
p
-th power and using the inequality
(x+y)^{p}\lesssim_{p}x^{p}+y^{p}
for
x,y>0
, we obtain the desired bound.
∎

Now we prove
Lemma

4.1
.

Proof of
Lemma

4.1
.

Let
R\geq 1
. Recall that
K
is a dyadic number chosen so that

1\leq K\leq\dots\leq K^{m}\sim R^{1/2}.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{0}=\{\tau_{0}\}
denote collection of the unit cube covering the parabola
\mathcal{P}
.

At the first stage, we decompose
\tau_{0}
into a collection
\mathcal{T}_{1}=\mathcal{T}_{1}(\tau_{0})
of
K^{-1}\times K^{-2}
boxes
\tau_{1}
covering the
K^{-2}
-nbd of the parabola.
For each
\tau_{1}\in\mathcal{T}_{1}
, let
\mathcal{T}_{2}(\tau_{1})
be a collection of
K^{-2}\times K^{-4}
boxes
\tau_{2}\subset\tau_{1}
covering
K^{-4}
-nbd of
\mathcal{P}
, and define
\mathcal{T}_{2}=\cup_{\tau_{1}\in\mathcal{T}_{1}}\mathcal{T}_{2}(\tau_{1})
.

Proceeding inductively, for
2\leq j\leq m
, we define
\mathcal{T}_{j}(\tau_{j-1})
as the collection of boxes
\tau_{j}\subset\tau_{j-1}
of dimension
K^{-j}\times K^{-2j}
covering
K^{-2j}
-neighborhood of
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{T}_{j}
similarly.
Finally, denote
\mathcal{T}_{m}=\{\theta\}
and for each
j=1,\dots,m
set

f_{\tau_{j}}=\sum_{\theta\subset\tau_{j}}f_{\theta}.

For each
\tau_{1}\in\mathcal{T}_{1}
, let

\mathcal{N}_{1}(\tau_{1})=\{\tau_{1}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{1}:~\tau_{1}^{\prime}\cap 2\tau_{1}\neq\emptyset\}.

It is clear that
\mathcal{N}_{1}(\tau_{1})
consists of only
O(1)
many elements, and if
\tau_{1}^{\prime}\notin\mathcal{N}_{1}(\tau_{1})
then
d(\tau_{1},\tau_{1}^{\prime})\geq 1/K
.
Since
\mathcal{T}_{1}
is covered by such neighborhood
\mathcal{N}_{1}(\tau_{1})
,
applying
Lemma

A.1
to
|f|^{p}\leq(\sum_{\tau_{1}\in\mathcal{T}_{1}}|f_{\tau_{1}}|)^{p}
, and using that
\#\mathcal{T}_{1}\lesssim K
, we have

|f(x)|^{p}\leq C\max_{\tau_{1}\in\mathcal{T}_{1}}|f_{\tau_{1}}(x)|^{p}+CK^{p}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau_{1},\tau_{1}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{1};\\
d(\tau_{1},\tau_{1}^{\prime})\geq\frac{1}{2K}\end{subarray}}|f_{\tau_{1}}(x)|^{\frac{p}{2}}|f_{\tau_{1}^{\prime}}(x)|^{\frac{p}{2}}

for some absolute constant
C
.

Applying
Lemma

A.1
again to the first term
|f_{\tau_{1}}|^{p}=|\sum_{\tau_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{2}(\tau_{1})}f_{\tau_{2}}|^{p}

\displaystyle|f(x)|^{p}

\mathcal{T}:=\mathcal{T}_{m}
, we get

\begin{split}|f(x)|^{p}&\leq C^{m}\max_{\theta\in\mathcal{T}}|f_{\theta}(x)|^{p}+C^{m}K^{p}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{\tau_{j-1}\in\mathcal{T}_{j-1}}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau_{j},\tau_{j}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{j}(\tau_{j-1});\\

.

Orbitmesy and promotion on self-dual posets
Esther Banaian, Emily Barnard, Sunita Chepuri, Jessica Striker

Abstract
. We introduce the notion of orbitmesy, which is related to homomesy, a central phenomenon in dynamical algebraic combinatorics. An orbit 𝒪 \mathcal{O} is said to be orbitmesic with respect to a statistic if the orbit’s average statistic value is equal to the global average. We particularly focus on the action of promotion on increasing labelings of certain fence posets called zig-zag posets, and two statistics, the antipodal sum statistic and the total sum statistic. We classify all of the orbitmesic promotion orbits for the zig-zag poset with four elements. Along the way, we investigate how homomesy of one action can be used to find orbitmesic orbits for another action, for the same fixed statistic. We prove several general results which can be used to find infinite families of orbitmesic orbits for any self-dual poset.

Introduction

Let
X
be a finite set, let
\phi:X\to X
be a bijective action on
X
, and let
\eta:X\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
be a statistic on
X
.

Homomesy
is a central phenomenon in dynamical algebraic combinatorics in which the average statistic value of each orbit of
\phi
equals the global average
(equivalently, each orbit has the same average statistic value).
Defined by Propp and Roby in
[
PR15
]
, where they studied its occurrence with respect to
rowmotion
on order ideals of posets, homomesy has been found in many other contexts (see e.g. the survey articles
[
Rob16
,
Str17
]
). Recently, the study of orbit averages has expanded to instances in which homomesy does not occur, but there is still a notable pattern to the orbit averages. In
[
EPRS23
]
, the authors define
homometry
, in which the average statistic value on an orbit is the same as the average value on all other orbits of the same size.
The authors of
[
EPRS23
]
study rowmotion of order ideals of
fence posets
with respect to the statistic
\hat{\chi}
which measures the size of an order ideal.
A fence poset is a poset of the form
x_{1}\lessdot x_{2}\lessdot\cdots\lessdot x_{a}\gtrdot x_{a+1}\gtrdot\cdots\gtrdot x_{a+b}\lessdot x_{a+b+1}\cdots
, as pictured below.
x_{1}
x_{2}
\iddots
x_{a}
x_{a+1}
\ddots
x_{a+b}
x_{a+b+1}
\iddots

Fence posets are prominent in several areas of mathematics, including the study of gentle and string algebras and cluster algebras
[
ÇS18
]
. Fence posets have been of particular interest lately due to a conjecture of Morier-Genoud and Ovsienko
[
MGO20
]
, later proven by Oğuz and Ravichandran
[
OR23
]
, which stated that the rank generating function of the lattice of order ideals of a fence poset is unimodal.
Importantly, rowmotion of order ideals of fence posets is
not
generally homomesic.
One of the main results of
[
EPRS23
]
shows that for certain fence posets, rowmotion of order ideals is homometric with respect to
\hat{\chi}
(see
[
EPRS23
, Theorem 4.5]
).

In this paper, we continue the study of fence posets initiated by
[
EPRS23
]
, this time focusing on the action of

on the set increasing labelings of a family of fence posets called

, which we denote
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
, with respect to two statistics, the antipodal sum and total sum statistics.
More precisely,
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
is the fence poset with

x_{1}\lessdot x_{2}\gtrdot x_{3}\lessdot x_{4}\gtrdot\cdots
.
For any finite poset, we define an increasing labeling of

to be a function

such that whenever

in

. Denote the set of all increasing labelings of

with values in

.
See
Definition

3.1
.
The total sum statistic of

simply sums the labels

for all

.
If

is self-dual with order-reversing involution

, the

, and is denoted

.
See

The promotion action on

was defined in
[

]
as a generalization of

[

]
and

on
[

]

increasing tableaux
. These are themselves generalizations of promotion and jeu de taquin on standard and semistandard Young tableaux
[

]
. Increasing labeling promotion was shown in
[
DSV19
]
to be equivariant with
rowmotion
of order ideals of a related poset they called the

; this is a natural analogue of results on increasing tableaux
[
DPS17
]
and two-row standard Young tableaux
[

]
.

Homomesy has been studied with respect to both promotion and rowmotion, and occurs in the following instances, among many others: cardinality under rowmotion on order ideals of the product of two chains poset
[
PR15
]
, other minuscule posets
[
RW15
]
, and the product of three chains where one is of size
2

[
Vor19
]
; sums of centrally symmetric entries under promotion of rectangular semistandard Young tableaux
[
BPS16
]
; and sums of centrally symmetric entries of the first/last row/column under

-promotion of rectangular increasing tableaux
[

,
Pec17
]
.
The interplay between promotion and rowmotion make the study of promotion on fence posets a natural next step from
[
EPRS23
]
.

Just as rowmotion of order ideals is generally not homomesic for fence posets, promotion also generally fails to be homomesic (with respect to the antipodal sum and total sum statistics).
Rather than focusing on homometry, we introduce a different weakening of homomesy called
orbitmesy
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be an orbit of a bijective action on a finite set
X
and
\eta:X\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
be a statistic on
X
. Then the orbit
\mathcal{O}
exhibits
orbitmesy
(or is
orbitmesic
) with respect to the statistic
\eta
if

\dfrac{1}{|\mathcal{O}|}\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathcal{O}}\eta(x)=\dfrac{1}{|X|}\displaystyle\sum_{x\in X}\eta(x).

Note that an orbit is trivially orbitmesic if
\mathcal{O}=X
, that is, if the action has only one orbit.
If all orbits are orbitmesic, then the orbitmesy is a homomesy.
So orbitmesy allows one to move beyond the binary question of whether or not a set, statistic, and action exhibits homomesy to the aim of characterizing which orbits exhibit orbitmesy.
In our main result, we classify all of the orbitmesic orbits of the promotion action on increasing labelings of the zig-zag poset
\mathcal{Z}_{4}
with respect to the antipodal sum statistic
\mathcal{A}_{x}
, where
x
is any element of
\mathcal{Z}_{4}
.

Theorem

7.8
.

Let
P=\mathcal{Z}_{4}
and
x\in P
. Let
\mathcal{O}
be a promotion orbit of
\Inc^{q}(P)
. Then
\mathcal{O}
exhibits orbitmesy with respect to the antipodal sum statistic
\mathcal{A}_{x}
if and only if
\mathcal{O}
avoids
1324
or is balanced.

Both the pattern avoidance condition and the balanced condition are easy to check (see
Definitions

7.3
and
7.5
).

In order to prove this result, we develop two general tools which can be applied to any finite poset
P
. The first allows us to understand the infinite set of all increasing labelings
\bigcup_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}\Inc^{q}(P)
through a finite set of
packed
increasing labelings. An increasing labeling is packed if it has no “holes.” The key point is if
f\in\bigcup_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}\Inc^{q}(P)
is packed then,
q
is at most equal to the cardinality of
P
. We can relate any increasing labeling to a packed labeling through a process known as
deflation
.

[
MP18
, Theorem 6.1]
computed the order of the promotion action on any increasing labeling in terms of packed labelings for miniscule posets.
The next theorem extends
[
MP18
, Theorem 6.1]
to any finite poset.

Theorem

3.17
.

Let
P
be any finite poset.
Let
f\in\Inc^{q}(P)
such that
\Con(f)
has period
\ell
. Let
r
denote the smallest integer such that the deflation
\overline{f}
is in
\Inc^{r}(P)
, and suppose the size of the promotion orbit containing
\overline{f}
is
\tau
. Then, the promotion orbit of
f
has size

\frac{\tau\ell}{\gcd(r\ell/q,\tau)}.

The above theorem tells us that the order of promotion on the set
\Inc^{q}(P)
is divisible by
q
, as long as
q>|P|
(see
Corollary

3.18
).
For small
n
, we use
Theorem

3.17
to explicitly compute the order of promotion for zig-zag posets (see
Corollaries

4.1
,
4.3
and
4.4
).
We also use the ideas of packed labelings and deflation in
Propositions

6.2
and
6.4
, where we provide conditions under which orbitmesy of a deflated promotion orbit
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
can be lifted to orbitmesy of
\mathcal{O}
for the total sum statistic and the antipodal sum statistic, respectively.

Our second general tool is to analyze the relationship between promotion and an involution which we call
\emph{swap}
.
The swap map is inspired by order ideal complementation and its role in the study of rowmotion on fence posets.
As in
[
EPRS23
]
, the swap action requires our poset
P
to be self-dual.
The key observation is that the swap action is clearly homomesic with respect to the total sum statistic and the antipodal sum statistic
\mathcal{A}_{x}
, for any
x
in
P
.
We use this fact to show the following.

Theorem

5.14
.

Let
P
be a self-dual poset and let
\mathcal{O}
be a promotion orbit of elements of
\Inc^{q}(P)
such that
\textnormal{swap}(\mathcal{O})=\mathcal{O}
. Then,
\mathcal{O}
exhibits orbitmesy with respect to the antipodal sum statistic
\mathcal{A}_{x}
for all
x\in P
and with respect to the total sum statistic
\mathrm{Tot}
.

Furthermore, we show that swap behaves well with deflation and “anticommutes” with promotion (see
Lemma

5.4
and
Proposition

5.8
).
This leads to the following result, which allows us to lift the orbitmesy of a deflated orbit
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
up to
\mathcal{O}
.

Corollary

5.15
.

Let
P
be a self-dual poset. Let
f\in\Inc^{q}(P)
and let
\mathcal{O}
be the promotion orbit of
f
in
\Inc^{q}(P)
. Let
r
be the largest label in
\overline{f}
and
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
be the orbit of
\overline{f}\in\Inc^{r}(P)
. Suppose the following hold:

\rho^{i}(\Con(f))=\mathrm{rev}(\Con(f))

Then the orbit
\mathcal{O}

for all

and the total sum statistic
\mathrm{Tot}
.

We combine all of these tools to classify orbitmesic promotion orbits of
\Inc^{q}(\mathcal{Z}_{4})
, with respect to the antipodal sum statistic.
Surprisingly, even though the antipodal sum statistic is not homomesic, the total sum statistic is homomesic.

Promotion on

2.
Orbitmesy

In this short section, we give two initial examples of orbitmesy (see
Definition

1.1
) and prove
Proposition

2.3
relating homomesic and orbitmesic actions. Then starting in the next section through the end of the paper, we study promotion on increasing labelings, which is our main application of orbitmesy and the context in which we isolated this phenomenon.

Example 2.1
.

Consider the reverse action
\mathcal{R}:S_{n}\rightarrow S_{n}
which acts on a permutation
\pi
as
\mathcal{R}(\pi_{1}\pi_{2}\ldots\pi_{n})=\pi_{n}\ldots\pi_{2}\pi_{1}
; for example,
\mathcal{R}(316524)=425613
.
Consider the statistic given by the first entry of the permutation. The global average of this statistic is
\frac{n+1}{2}
, since this is the average of the numbers
1
through
n
and each number is equally likely to be in the first entry. Thus the
\mathcal{R}
-orbit of
\pi\in S_{n}
is orbitmesic with respect to the first entry statistic if and only if the first and last entries of
\pi
sum to
n+1
, as in the case of the example orbit.

We now move to an example of orbitmesy involving rowmotion, which is a fundamental action in dynamical algebraic combinatorics (see e.g.
[
SW12
,
Str17
]
for further information).

Example 2.2
.

Let
P
be a self-dual poset and fix an order-reversing involution
\kappa:P\rightarrow P
. An
order ideal
of
P
is a subset
I
of
P
such that if
y\in I
and
x<y
then
x\in I
; the set of order ideals of
P
is denoted
J(P)
. The
dual order ideal
of
I
(called the
ideal complement
in
[
EPRS23
, p.4]
) is the image of its complement
P\setminus I
under
\kappa
.
The
rowmotion
of
I\in J(P)
is the order ideal generated by the minimal elements of
P\setminus I
. We call a rowmotion orbit
\mathcal{O}

self-dual
if for all
I\in\mathcal{O}
, the dual order ideal of
I
is also in
\mathcal{O}
. The following is a restatement of
[
EPRS23
, Corollary 3.3(a)]
in the language of orbitmesy:
Given a self-dual poset,
any self-dual rowmotion orbit exhibits orbitmesy with respect to the cardinality statistic.

Note that in the above, the map from an order ideal to its dual order ideal is clearly homomesic with respect to the cardinality statistic.
This is an example of the following general phenomenon which we describe explicitly in the next proposition. For a fixed statistic (such as cardinality), if one action,
\phi
, (such as order ideal dual) is homomesic, this can give rise to orbitmesies for another action,
\psi
(such as rowmotion).
One must simply check that a
\psi
orbit is “covered” by
\phi
-orbits. More precisely, given
\phi,\psi:X\rightarrow X
, an orbit
\mathcal{O}
of
\psi
is
\phi
-closed
if for all
x\in\mathcal{O}
,
\phi(x)\in\mathcal{O}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
X
be a finite set and
\eta:X\to\mathbb{Z}
be a statistic on
X
.
Suppose
\phi
and
\psi
are bijective actions on
X
. If
(X,\eta,\phi)
exhibits homomesy, then any orbit of
\psi
which is
\phi
-closed exhibits orbitmesy with respect to
\eta
.

Proof.

Suppose that the global average of the
\eta
-statistic on
X
is equal to
n
.
Then for every
\phi
-orbit
\mathcal{O}
we have
\sum_{x\in\mathcal{O}}\eta(x)=n|\mathcal{O}|
.
Suppose that
\mathcal{O}_{\psi}
is an orbit of the
\psi
action which is closed under the action of
\phi.

This implies that
\mathcal{O}_{\psi}
can be partitioned by the orbits of
\phi
.
That is,
\mathcal{O}_{\psi}=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{O}_{\phi,i}
where each
\mathcal{O}_{\phi,i}
is an orbit of
\phi
. Then we have

\sum_{x\in\mathcal{O}_{\psi}}\eta(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\sum_{x\in\mathcal{O}_{\phi,i}}\eta(x)\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}n|\mathcal{O}_{\phi,i}|=n|\mathcal{O}_{\psi}|.

Thus the average value of
\eta
on
\mathcal{O}_{\psi}
is equal to the global average.
∎

We later use this proposition to prove an orbitmesy involving promotion on increasing labelings of self-dual posets (see
Theorem

5.14
).

The following lemma shows that the sliding subposets of an increasing labeling and its deflation coincide.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let

be such that

.

Proof.

We use the characterization of the sliding subposet from

The following proposition shows that promotion and deflation interact nicely. This proposition was proven for miniscule posets (using only the assumption that there is a unique maximal element) in

(see also

, Sec. 3]
) using the Bender-Knuth definition of promotion; we use a different proof method involving the sliding subposet to prove it for arbitrary posets.

Proposition 3.13
.

Let

and
\overline{f}
be
r
-packed. If the first entry in
\Con(f)
is
1
, we have
\overline{\Pro(f)}=\Pro(\overline{f})
. Otherwise, we have
\overline{\Pro(f)}=\overline{f}
.

Proof.

First assume the first entry in
\Con(f)
is 1. Let the labels used by

be
\{1=i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{r}\}
so that
\overline{f}\in\Inc^{r}(P)
. From

, we know
\Pro(f)
has labels
\{i_{2}-1<i_{3}-1<\cdots<i_{r}-1<q\}
.
Pick

and let

so that
\overline{f}(x)=j
.

•

If
x\notin S(f)
, then

and
\overline{Pro(f)}(x)=j-1
. Since

by

•

If

If

z
covered by
\kappa(y)
since
P
is self-dual and
\kappa
is an involution.

At this point we have shown
x\in S_{i}(\textnormal{swap}(f))
if and only if there exists
y\lessdot x
such that

and
f(\kappa(x))=\max_{\kappa(y)\gtrdot z}f(z)
.
By our inductive hypothesis,
y\in\cup_{j=1}^{i-1}\kappa(S^{-1}_{j}(f))
, and equivalently
\kappa(y)\in\cup_{j=1}^{i-1}S^{-1}_{j}(f)
. Therefore, since
\kappa(y)\gtrdot\kappa(x)
, if

, then
\kappa(x)\in S^{-1}_{i}(f)
. In conclusion, we have shown that
x\in S_{i}(\textnormal{swap}(f))
if and only if
\kappa(x)\in S^{-1}_{i}(f)
, which is true if and only if
x\in\kappa(S^{-1}_{i}(f))
.

Proposition 5.8
.

Given

,
\textnormal{swap}(\Pro^{-1}(f))=\Pro(\textnormal{swap}(f))
.

Proof.

Let
x\in P
. There are three possibilities for

:
x\in S(\textnormal{swap}(f))
and
x
is maximal,
x\in S(\textnormal{swap}(f))
and
x
is not maximal, or
x\notin S(\textnormal{swap}(f))
. For each case, we show
\Pro(\textnormal{swap}(f))(x)=\textnormal{swap}(\Pro^{-1}(f))(x)
.

First, let
x\in S(\textnormal{swap}(f))
and suppose
x
is maximal. Then,
\Pro(\textnormal{swap}(f))(x)=q
. If
x\in S(\textnormal{swap}(f))
then, by
Lemma

5.7
,
\kappa(x)\in S^{-1}(f)
, and if
x
is maximal then
\kappa(x)
is minimal. Therefore,
\Pro^{-1}(f)(\kappa(x))=1
and by definition
\textnormal{swap}(\Pro^{-1})(f)(x)=q+1-\Pro^{-1}(f)(\kappa(x))=q=\Pro(\textnormal{swap}(f))(x)
.

Next, if
x\in S(\textnormal{swap}(f))
and
x
is not maximal, then
\Pro(\textnormal{swap}(f))(x)=\min_{z\gtrdot x}\textnormal{swap}(f)(z)-1=q-\max_{z\gtrdot x}f(\kappa(z))=q-\max_{z\lessdot\kappa(x)}f(z)
. From
Lemma

5.7
, we know that if
x\in S(\textnormal{swap}(f))
and
x
is not maximal, then
\kappa(x)\in S^{-1}(f)
and
\kappa(x)
is not minimal. Therefore,
\textnormal{swap}(\Pro^{-1}(f))(x)=q+1-\Pro^{-1}(f)(\kappa(x))=q+1-(\max_{z\lessdot\kappa(x)}f(z)+1)=\Pro(\textnormal{swap}(f))(x)
as desired.

Finally, suppose

, so that equivalently
x\notin\kappa(S^{-1}(f))
by
Lemma

5.7
and
\kappa(x)\notin S^{-1}(f)
. We know
\Pro(\textnormal{swap}(f))(x)=\textnormal{swap}(f)(x)-1=q+1-f(\kappa(x))-1=q+1-(f(\kappa(x))+1)=q+1-\Pro^{-1}(f)(\kappa(x))=\textnormal{swap}(\Pro^{-1}(f))(x)
.

We have shown
\Pro(\textnormal{swap}(f))(x)=\textnormal{swap}(\Pro^{-1}(f))(x)
for each
x\in P
, so that these are the same labelings of
P
.
∎

Definition 5.11

Proposition 5.12

on

When an orbit of

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{x}(f)+\mathcal{A}_{x}(\textnormal{swap}(f))

\displaystyle=f(x)+f(\kappa(x))+(q+1-f(\kappa(x)))+(q+1-f(x))

\displaystyle=2(q+1).

6.
Orbitmesy and Deflation

In
Corollary

5.15
we saw that we could prove an increasing labeling promotion orbit was orbitmesic using information about the deflation of the orbit. In this section we show some other instances where an orbit’s deflation allows us to prove orbitmesy.

We begin with a lemma about the global average of the statistics
\mathcal{A}_{x}
and
\mathrm{Tot}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
P
be a self-dual poset and consider all elements of
\Inc^{q}(P)
.

(1)

For all
x\in P
, the global average of the antipodal sum statistic with respect to
x
is
q+1
. That is,

\frac{1}{|P|}\sum_{f\in\Inc^{q}(P)}\mathcal{A}_{x}(f)=q+1.

(2)

The global average of the total sum statistic is
\frac{|P|}{2}(q+1)
. That is,

\frac{1}{|P|}\sum_{f\in\Inc^{q}(P)}\mathrm{Tot}(f)=\frac{|P|}{2}(q+1).

Proof.

From the proof of
Proposition

5.12
we know that the average of
\mathcal{A}_{x}
is
q+1
on every orbit of
swap
on
\Inc^{q}(P)
. This means that the average of
\mathcal{A}_{x}
on
\Inc^{q}(P)
is
q+1
. The second statement follows from the first statement and the fact that for all
f\in\Inc^{q}(P)
,
\sum_{x\in P}\mathcal{A}_{x}(f)=2\mathrm{Tot}(f)
.
∎

Proposition 6.2
.

Given a self-dual poset
P
, let
f\in\Inc^{q}(P)
such that
\overline{f}
is a linear extension. Moreover, given
r,\ell,\tau
as in
Theorem

3.17
, suppose
\gcd(r\ell/q,\tau)=1
. If
\mathcal{O}
is the promotion orbit of
\Inc^{q}(P)
containing
f
, then the orbit
\mathcal{O}
is orbitmesic with respect to the total sum statistic
\mathrm{Tot}
.

Proof.

Let
f
have content word
\Con(f)
. The condition on the
\gcd
of these values means that each cyclic rotation of
\Con(f)
appears
\tau
times when we view elements of
\mathcal{O}
as pairs of deflations and content words. Since
\overline{f}
is a linear extension, we in particular know that every label in

the same is true for all

. Therefore, computing the total sum of

. If we sum the vectors

\sum_{f\in\mathcal{O}}\mathrm{Tot}(f)=\frac{r\ell\tau(q+1)}{2}
.
The size of the orbit is

so the average total sum is

. Since

must be

, by

6.1
, this is equal to the global average. In other words, this orbit is orbitmesic.
∎

Proposition 6.4

\mathcal{A}_{x}
.

Proof.

As in the proof of
Proposition

6.2
, the condition on the gcd implies that
\mathcal{O}
contains every possible pair of a labeling in
\overline{\mathcal{O}}
and a cyclic rotation of
\Con(f)
.
We begin by assuming that
\ell
, the period of
\Con(f)
, is equal to
q
.
We can use the description of
\mathcal{O}
as pairs of content words and packed labelings to break up the computation of
\mathcal{A}_{x}
for all labelings in
\mathcal{O}
.

We assume we have chosen
f
so that
\Con(f)=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{q})
has
c_{q}=1
. Define
s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}
to be the nonnegative integers such that the
r
values of

in
\Con(f)
occur at
s_{1}+1,s_{1}+s_{2}+2,\ldots,s_{1}+\cdots+s_{r-1}+s_{r}+r
. That is,
\Con(f)
is
s_{1}
0’s followed by a 1, then
s_{2}
0’s followed by a 1, and so on.

Let
j
and
k
be such that
\overline{f}(x)=j
and
\overline{f}(\kappa(x))=k
. With our notation, this means
f(x)=(\sum_{i=1}^{j}s_{i})+j
and

. For all
t\leq s_{1}
(equivalently, for all

), from

3.13
we know
\Pro^{t}(f)(x)=(\sum_{i=1}^{j}s_{i})+j-t

\Pro^{t}(f)(\kappa(x))=(\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i})+k-t

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{s_{1}}\mathcal{A}_{x}(\Pro^{i}(f))

\displaystyle=(s_{1}+1)(\sum_{i=1}^{j}s_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}+j+k)-2-4-\cdots-2s_{1}

\displaystyle=(s_{1}+1)(\sum_{i=1}^{j}s_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}+j+k)-s_{1}(s_{1}+1)

\displaystyle=(s_{1}+1)(\sum_{i=1}^{j}s_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}+j+k-s_{1})

\displaystyle=(s_{1}+1)(s_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{j}s_{i}+\sum_{i=2}^{k}s_{i}+j+k).

The computation is similar for all other possible deflations. Let
f_{2},\ldots,f_{\tau}
be the
\tau-1
other labelings in
\mathcal{O}
with content word equal to
\Con(f)
. Let

be such that

and

. Let

be our original labeling

so that

and

from the previous summation. Then, we compute

(1)

\displaystyle\sum_{z=1}^{\tau}\sum_{i=0}^{s_{1}}\mathcal{A}_{x}(\Pro^{i}(f_{z}))=(s_{1}+1)\big(a_{1}s_{1}+a_{2}s_{2}+\cdots+a_{r}s_{r}+\sum_{z=1}^{\tau}j_{z}+k_{z})\big)

where

and for

,

is equal to the number of values in the multiset

which are at least

.
Note that the stable condition implies

. This means

(2)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}=\tau+\sum_{i=2}^{r}a_{i}=\tau+\sum_{z=1}^{\tau}((j_{z}-1)+(k_{z}-1))=\sum_{z=1}^{\tau}(j_{z}+k_{z})-\tau=\tau(r+1)-\tau=\tau r.

Let
g
be the labeling which deflates to
\overline{f}
and has content word
\rho^{s_{1}+1}(\Con(f))
. As noted in the first sentence of this proof, we know

is in the orbit
\mathcal{O}
. By the definition of the numbers

, we know that
\mathcal{A}_{x}(g)=(\sum_{i=2}^{j+1}s_{i})+j+(\sum_{i=2}^{k+1}s_{i})+k
.

We can compute
\sum_{i=s_{1}+1}^{s_{1}+s_{2}+1}\mathcal{A}_{x}(\Pro^{i}(g))
analogously to the computation of
\sum_{i=0}^{s_{1}}\mathcal{A}_{x}(\Pro^{i}(f))
, but with the values

cyclically rotated. Therefore,
\sum_{h\in\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{A}_{x}(h)
is the result of summing the

terms given by all cyclic shifts of
Equation

1
. Explicitly, this is

\displaystyle\sum_{h\in\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{A}_{x}(h)

\displaystyle=(s_{1}+1)(\tau s_{1}+a_{2}s_{2}+\cdots+a_{r-1}s_{r-1}+a_{r}s_{r}+\tau(r+1))

\displaystyle+(s_{2}+1)(\tau s_{2}+a_{2}s_{3}+\cdots+a_{r-1}s_{r}+a_{r}s_{1}+\tau(r+1))

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle+(s_{r}+1)(\tau s_{r}+a_{2}s_{1}+\cdots+a_{r-1}s_{r-2}+a_{r}s_{r-1}+\tau(r+1)).

Treating this as a polynomial in the variables

and analyzing the coefficients, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{h\in\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{A}_{x}(h)

\displaystyle=\tau(s_{1}^{2}+\cdots+s_{r}^{2})+(a_{2}+a_{r})(s_{1}s_{2}+s_{2}s_{3}+\cdots+s_{r}s_{1})

\displaystyle+(a_{3}+a_{r-1})(s_{1}s_{3}+s_{2}s_{4}+\cdots+s_{r-1}s_{1})+\cdots+\tau(2r+1)(s_{1}+\cdots+s_{r})+\tau r(r+1),

where we use

2
to analyze for the coefficient of linear terms. As

which are equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{h\in\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{A}_{x}(h)

\displaystyle=\tau(s_{1}^{2}+\cdots+s_{r}^{2})+2\tau(s_{1}s_{2}+s_{1}s_{3}+\cdots+s_{r-1}s_{r})+(\tau(2r+1))(s_{1}+\cdots+s_{r})

\displaystyle+\tau r(r+1)

\displaystyle=\tau(s_{1}+s_{2}+\cdots+s_{r}+r)(s_{1}+s_{2}+\cdots+s_{r}+r+1)

Since the size of

Now, if

In this section, we prove an orbitmesy for the antipodal sum statistics of increasing labelings of the poset
\mathcal{Z}_{4}
under promotion. Here there are only two antipodal sum statistics, so we give them their own notation.

Definition 7.1
.

Given an increasing labeling
f\in\Inc^{q}(\mathcal{Z}_{n})
, we define the exterior antipodal sum
\mathcal{A}_{e}(f)
as the sum of the labels on the leftmost and rightmost elements. Given
f\in\Inc^{q}(\mathcal{Z}_{4})
, we define the interior antipodal sum
\mathcal{A}_{i}(f)
as the sum of the labels on the other two elements.

We next define a pattern avoidance condition on labelings of fence posets. This is analogous to the idea of pattern avoidance in permutations.

Definition 7.3

Consider an increasing labeling of fence poset

Further, we say a subset of

Proposition 7.7
.

Given an orbit of
\Inc^{q}(\mathcal{Z}_{4})
under promotion, either all labelings in the orbit are imbalanced or all labelings in the orbit are balanced.

Proof.

Let
f\in\Inc^{q}(\mathcal{Z}_{4})
with labels
w\leq x\leq y\leq z
and suppose
f
is imbalanced.
Recall from
Lemma

3.9
that promotion shifts the binary content word. Thus, if
w\neq 1
then the spacing between non-zero entries in the binary content word of
\Pro(f)
is the same as in the binary content word of
f
.
If
w=1
, then
\Pro(f)
has labels
x-1\leq y-1\leq z-1\leq q
.
Since
x-w\neq z-y
and
w-z+q\neq y-x
and
w=1
, we know
y-x\neq q-z+1
and
x-1\neq z-y
. This means that in either case,
\Pro(f)
is imbalanced.
Since applying promotion to an imbalanced labeling results in another imbalanced labeling, if an orbit contains any imbalanced labelings, it must contain only imbalanced labelings.
∎

Following the above proposition, we call an orbit of
\Inc^{q}(\mathcal{Z}_{4})
under promotion
imbalanced
when all its elements are imbalanced.

Proposition 7.10
.

Let
P=\mathcal{Z}_{4}
and
x\in P
. If
\mathcal{O}
is a promotion orbits of
\Inc^{q}(P)
that contains
1324
then the average value of the antipodal sum statistic
\mathcal{A}_{x}
equals
q+1
if and only if
\mathcal{O}
is balanced.

Proof.

By
Proposition

3.13
, there exists
f\in\mathcal{O}
such that
\bar{f}=1324
and if
\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{q})=Con(f)
, then
c_{q}=1
. We set
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
to be the nonnegative integers such that
c_{\alpha+1}=c_{\alpha+\beta+2}=c_{\alpha+\beta+\gamma+3}=c_{q}=1
and
\alpha+\beta+\gamma+3<q
. Let
\delta=q-4-\alpha-\beta-\gamma
.
With this notation,
\mathcal{O}
being balanced is equivalent to having
\beta=\delta
or
\alpha=\gamma
.

First assume
\mathcal{A}_{x}
is the exterior antipodal sum statistic
\mathcal{A}_{e}
. Our proof method will be similar to that of
Proposition

6.4
. We will begin by adding up the exterior antipodal sums of
\{(1324,\rho^{i}(\mathbf{c})):0\leq i\leq\alpha\}
where we are identifying each ordered pair
(g,v)
with the increasing labeling
f
such that
\overline{f}=g
and
\Con(f)=v
. Note that for these values of
i
,
\Pro^{i}(f)=\Pro^{i}(\overline{f},\mathbf{c})=(\overline{f},\rho^{i}(\mathbf{c}))
from
Lemma

3.9
and
Proposition

3.13
. To compute
\mathcal{A}_{e}(1324,\rho^{i}(\mathbf{c}))
where
0\leq i\leq\alpha
, we add the index of the first and last nonzero entries of
\rho^{i}(\mathbf{c})
. That is,
\mathcal{A}_{e}(1324,\rho^{i}(\mathbf{c}))=(\alpha+1-i)+(q-i)
. Then, the sum of all these exterior antipodal sums is given by

\displaystyle[(\alpha+1)+(q)]+[(\alpha)+(q-1)]+\cdots+[(1)+(q-\alpha)]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\alpha}(q-\alpha+1)+2j

\displaystyle=(q-\alpha+1)(\alpha+1)+(\alpha+1)\alpha

\displaystyle=(q+1)(\alpha+1)

\displaystyle=(\alpha+\beta+\gamma+\delta+5)(\alpha+1).

Since
\mathbf{c}
is a binary word with four 1’s, it could have no symmetry, 2-fold symmetry, or 4-fold symmetry. If
\mathbf{c}
has 2-fold or 4-fold symmetry then
\alpha=\gamma
,
\beta=\delta
and, applying
Lemma

3.9
and
Proposition

3.13
, we see
\mathcal{O}=\{(1324,\rho^{i}(\mathbf{c})):0\leq i\leq\alpha\}\cup\{(2413,\rho^{i}(\mathbf{c})):\alpha+1\leq i\leq\alpha+\beta+1\}
.
The exterior antipodal sums of labelings in the first set is computed above while for the second set it is
(\beta+2\gamma+\delta+5)(\beta+1)
by a similar computation.
Therefore, in this case the sum of
\mathcal{A}_{e}
over all elements of
\mathcal{O}
is
(2\alpha+2\beta+5)(\alpha+\beta+2)
, which is equal to
(q+1)(\frac{q}{2})=(q+1)|\mathcal{O}|
since
\alpha+\beta+2=\frac{q}{2}=|\mathcal{O}|
.

In the case when
\mathbf{c}
has no symmetry, by
Lemma

3.9
and
Proposition

3.13
,
\mathcal{O}=\{(1324,\rho^{i}(\mathbf{c}):0\leq i\leq\alpha\}\cup\{2413,\rho^{i}(\mathbf{c}:\alpha+1\leq i\leq\alpha+\beta+1\}\cup\{(1324,\rho^{i}(\mathbf{c}):\alpha+\beta+2\leq i\leq\alpha+\beta+\gamma+2\}\cup\{2413,\rho^{i}(\mathbf{c}:\alpha+\beta+\gamma+3\leq i\leq q-1\}

which simplifies to

(3)

\displaystyle(\alpha+\beta+\gamma+\delta+4)(\alpha+\beta+\gamma+\delta+5)+(\alpha-\gamma)(\delta-\beta)=q(q+1)+(\alpha-\gamma)(\delta-\beta).

Since

on the elements of
\mathcal{O}
is

exactly when
\alpha=\gamma
or
\beta=\delta

Considering both cases, we see that the average value of
\mathcal{A}_{e}

\mathcal{O}

\mathcal{A}_{e}

\mathcal{O}
is

\mathcal{O}
is balanced.

Now let
\mathcal{A}_{x}

Since the interior antipodal sum of

Promotion on

8.
Future work

This work raises several natural questions and suggests promising directions for further investigation.

Overall, we would like to find other, natural instances of orbitmesy. A natural starting point would be to look for remarks in the literature, such as
[
Vor19
, Propositions 38 and 39]
of non-homomesic pairs of maps and statistics and try to characterize which subset of orbits exhibits orbitmesy. One could also undertake a systematic search
using FindStat
[
RS+
]
, as was done
[
ELM+24
,

]
for other phenomena in dynamical algebraic combinatorics.

It would also be interesting to extend our results on small zig-zag posets. For instance, is there a pattern to the order of promotion on
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
as
n
grows? As we mentioned in

4.5

\Inc^{q}(\mathcal{Z}_{6})

One could similarly ask to classify orbits exhibiting total sum orbitmesy for

Finally, in order to characterize promotion orbits of
\Inc^{q}(\mathcal{Z}_{4})

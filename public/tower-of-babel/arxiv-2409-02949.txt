To avoid a circular definition, we give an alternative characterization of the exponential integral using the Cauchy principal value, which assigns finite values to divergent integrals. Defining

Definition 1

Let

(6)

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int_{a}^{b}f(t)dt\vcentcolon=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\left(\int_{c+\varepsilon}^{b}f(t)dt+\int_{a}^{c-\varepsilon}f(t)dt\right).

Defining
\mathbb{R}^{\times}\vcentcolon=\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}

Proof

First, we introduce a series formula for
\int_{1}^{x}f(t)dt
which may be obtained simply by infinite integration by parts.

Definition 3

(Harmonic numbers)
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define
H_{n}\vcentcolon=\sum_{k=1}^{n}1/k
.

Lemma 1
.

Suppose
f
is analytic at
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
1
. Then

(11)

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{x}f(t)dt=\sum_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}g^{(n)}(x)\left(\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\left(\log|x|-H_{n}\right)\right)+\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{n}H_{n}g^{(n)}(1)}{n!},

where
g(x)=xf(x)
.

Proof.

Apply integration by parts repeatedly to

(12)

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{x}f(t)dt=\int_{1}^{x}\frac{tf(t)}{t}dt,

integrating
1/t
and differentiating
tf(t)
. Note that the
n
-fold antidifferentiation of
1/x
yields

(13)

\displaystyle\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\left(\log|x|-H_{n}\right),

which may be verified inductively. Since
f
is analytic at
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
1
, the series converge.
∎

Lemma 2
.

We have

(14)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-e^{1-t}}{1-t}dt=\gamma-\text{Ei}(1).

Proof.

By
[
10
, pg.243]
,

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-e^{-t}-e^{-1/t}}{t}dt=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-e^{-t}}{t}dt-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{-1/t}}{t}dt

(15)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-e^{-t}}{t}dt-\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t}}{t}dt=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-e^{-t}}{t}dt+\text{Ei}(-1).

Then by (
2
),

\displaystyle\gamma-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-e^{t}}{t}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-e^{-t}}{t}dt-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-e^{t}}{t}dt+\text{Ei}(-1)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{t}-e^{-t}}{t}dt+\text{Ei}(-1)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\left(\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}\frac{e^{t}}{t}dt-\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}\frac{e^{-t}}{t}dt\right)+\text{Ei}(-1)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\left(\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}\frac{e^{t}}{t}dt+\int_{-1}^{-\varepsilon}\frac{e^{t}}{t}dt\right)+\text{Ei}(-1)

\displaystyle=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{e^{t}}{t}dt+\text{Ei}(-1)

\displaystyle=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{-\infty}^{1}\frac{e^{t}}{t}dt-\int_{-\infty}^{-1}\frac{e^{t}}{t}dt+\text{Ei}(-1)

(16)

\displaystyle=\text{Ei}(1)-\text{Ei}(-1)+\text{Ei}(-1)=\text{Ei}(1).

Consequently,

(17)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-e^{1-t}}{1-t}dt=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-e^{t}}{t}dt=\gamma-\text{Ei}(1),

as desired.
∎

Theorem 1
.

The exponential integral yields the Puiseux representation

(18)

\displaystyle\text{Ei}(x)=\gamma+\log|x|+\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{x^{n}}{n(n!)},

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}
.

Proof.

Noting that the exponential function is entire, by Lemma
1
,

\displaystyle\text{Ei}(x)-\text{Ei}(1)=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{-\infty}^{x}\frac{e^{t}}{t}dt-\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{-\infty}^{1}\frac{e^{t}}{t}dt=\int_{1}^{x}\frac{e^{t}}{t}dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}e^{x}\left(\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\left(\log|x|-H_{n}\right)\right)+e\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{n}H_{n}}{n!}

(19)

\displaystyle=\log|x|+e^{x}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}H_{n}x^{n}}{n!}+e\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{n}H_{n}}{n!}.

By a special case of
[
2
, Corollary 3]
,

(20)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}H_{k}(-1)^{k+1}=\frac{1}{n}

for
n>0
.
3
3

3

This relation may be alternatively verified using the integral form of the Harmonic numbers
[
4
]
, the binomial coefficients’ recurrence relation
[
6
]
, or binomial inversion
[
8
]
.
Thus, by convolution of coefficients,

(21)

\displaystyle e^{x}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}H_{n}x^{n}}{n!}=\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\right)\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}H_{n}x^{n}}{n!}\right)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{x^{n}}{n(n!)}.

Putting (
2
) and (
21
) together, we obtain

(22)

\displaystyle\text{Ei}(x)=\log|x|+\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{x^{n}}{n(n!)}+\text{Ei}(1)+e\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{n}H_{n}}{n!}.

Lastly, using the usual integral form of the Harmonic numbers, we have

\displaystyle e\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{n}H_{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=e\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-t^{n}}{1-t}dt=e\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\frac{1-t^{n}}{1-t}dt

(23)

\displaystyle=e\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{-1}-e^{-t}}{1-t}dt=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-e^{1-t}}{1-t}dt=\gamma-\text{Ei}(1)

by Lemma
2
. The interchange of the summation and integral in (
2

(24)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{n\geq 0}\left|\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\frac{1-t^{n}}{1-t}\right|dt=\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1-t^{n}}{n!(1-t)}dt=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e-e^{t}}{1-t}dt<\infty.

Therefore

(25)

\displaystyle\text{Ei}(x)=\gamma+\log|x|+\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{x^{n}}{n(n!)},

as desired.
∎

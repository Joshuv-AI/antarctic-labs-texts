The Strict 2-Category Structure of Distorted Monoidal Categories
Joaquim Reizi Higuchi

Abstract
We develop a typed, constructive framework for distorted monoidal categories —monoidal categories equipped with a unit distortion Λ : Id ⇒ Id \Lambda:\mathrm{Id}\Rightarrow\mathrm{Id} and a binatural distortion σ X , Y : X ⊗ Y → Y ⊗ X \sigma_{X,Y}:X\otimes Y\to Y\otimes X satisfying hexagon–style axioms without assuming invertibility. With σ \sigma -lax monoidal functors required to satisfy strict compatibilities with Λ \Lambda and σ \sigma , and monoidal natural transformations as 2-cells, these structures assemble into a strict 2-category. Strictness holds on the nose : for composable F , G , H F,G,H , μ X , Y H ⁡ ( G ​ F ) = μ X , Y ( H ​ G ) ​ F and μ 0 H ⁡ ( G ​ F ) = μ 0 ( H ​ G ) ​ F , \mu^{H(GF)}_{X,Y}=\mu^{(HG)F}_{X,Y}\quad\text{and}\quad\mu^{H(GF)}_{0}=\mu^{(HG)F}_{0}, and the interchange law for 2-morphisms is an equality. Conceptually, we identify a 2-monad presentation whose strict algebras, strict morphisms, and 2-cells instantiate the foregoing data; our contribution is the explicit, type-safe calculus that realizes these general facts at the level of concrete diagrammatic proofs. We give robust existence schemes for non-invertible distortions via normalized idempotent twists of a braiding and for nontrivial unit distortions via graded characters, and we exhibit a sharp test case showing that monoidality of Λ \Lambda is independent of the other axioms. The resulting calculus generalizes braided monoidal categories ( Λ = Id \Lambda=\mathrm{Id} , σ \sigma invertible) and supports irreversible or resource-sensitive tensorial reasoning, with immediate applicability to directed homotopy, categorical quantum processes with measurement, and non-symmetric operadic situations. The constructions and proofs are designed for formal verification.

Introduction

Monoidal categories form the common language for tensorial phenomena across algebra, topology, quantum theory, and computer science. The symmetric case, where a symmetry
\sigma:X\otimes Y\xrightarrow{\cong}Y\otimes X
is invertible, is classical
[
1
,
3
]
. Many natural systems, however, are intrinsically
directed
: they admit canonical ways to
reorder
tensor factors that need not be reversible, while still supporting coherent tensor calculus. Typical sources include concurrency and directed homotopy
[
6
]
, irreversible processes in categorical quantum mechanics, and algebraic situations where filtrations or truncations introduce loss of information.

From braidings to distortions.

We propose a flexible but type
-
safe abstraction we call a
distorted monoidal category
: a monoidal category

(\mathcal{C},\otimes,\mathbf{I},\alpha,\lambda,\rho)

equipped with a
binary distortion

\sigma_{X,Y}:X\otimes Y\to Y\otimes X
obeying
typed
hexagon axioms and a
unit distortion

\Lambda:\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{C}}\Rightarrow\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{C}}
obeying monoidality and unit normalizations (Definition
2.1
). Unlike braidings,
\sigma
is not assumed invertible, and
\Lambda
may be nontrivial. This relaxes symmetry just enough to capture irreversible interchange while preserving a workable coherence calculus.

Conceptual position and novelty

Our main structural result (Theorem

-
categorical paradigm.
Let
\mathsf{S}
be the 2
-
monad on
\mathbf{Cat}
whose strict algebras are

]

By freely adjoining to a

Distorted monoidal categories,

The novelty of this paper is therefore

2
Preliminaries and Notation

We work in the category
\mathbf{Cat}
of (locally small) categories. All functors and natural transformations are assumed to be well-defined in this context. We adopt standard conventions from
[
1
,
2
]
for monoidal categories, functors, and natural transformations.

Definition 2.1

(Distorted monoidal category)
.

A
distorted monoidal category
is a tuple

(\mathcal{C},\otimes,\mathbf{I},\alpha,\lambda,\rho,\Lambda,\sigma)

where
(\mathcal{C},\otimes,\mathbf{I},\alpha,\lambda,\rho)
is a monoidal category (not assumed strict) with associator
\alpha
and unitors
\lambda,\rho
, together with

•

a natural transformation
\Lambda:\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{C}}\Rightarrow\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{C}}
(
unit distortion
);

•

a natural transformation
\sigma_{X,Y}:X\otimes Y\to Y\otimes X
(
binary distortion
);

subject to the following
distorted coherence axioms
:

(D1)

Binaturality:
for all morphisms
f:X\to X^{\prime}
and
g:Y\to Y^{\prime}
,

(g\otimes f)\circ\sigma_{X,Y}\;=\;\sigma_{X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}}\circ(f\otimes g).

(D2)

Unit normalization:

\sigma_{X,\mathbf{I}}=\lambda_{X}^{-1}\circ\rho_{X}
and
\sigma_{\mathbf{I},X}=\rho_{X}^{-1}\circ\lambda_{X}
; moreover
\Lambda_{\mathbf{I}}=\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbf{I}}
.

(D3)

Hexagons (typed, two forms):
for all
X,Y,Z
,

\displaystyle\sigma_{X\otimes Y,\,Z}\;=\;

\displaystyle\alpha_{Z,X,Y}\circ(\sigma_{X,Z}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{Y})\circ\alpha^{-1}_{X,Z,Y}\circ(\mathrm{Id}_{X}\otimes\sigma_{Y,Z})\circ\alpha_{X,Y,Z},

\displaystyle\sigma_{X,\,Y\otimes Z}\;=\;

\displaystyle\alpha^{-1}_{Y,Z,X}\circ(\mathrm{Id}_{Y}\otimes\sigma_{X,Z})\circ\alpha_{Y,X,Z}\circ(\sigma_{X,Y}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{Z})\circ\alpha^{-1}_{X,Y,Z}.

(D4)

Monoidality of
\Lambda
:

\Lambda_{X\otimes Y}=\Lambda_{X}\otimes\Lambda_{Y}
for all
X,Y
.

No symmetry or invertibility of
\sigma
is required.

Remark 2.2
.

The hexagon axioms (D3) are carefully typed to ensure that domains and codomains match. Each equality is an identity of morphisms with the displayed source and target. When
\sigma
is invertible, these axioms reduce to the standard braiding hexagons
[
3
]
.

Remark 2.3
.

The commutation of
\Lambda
with
\sigma
,

(\Lambda_{Y}\otimes\Lambda_{X})\circ\sigma_{X,Y}\;=\;\sigma_{X,Y}\circ(\Lambda_{X}\otimes\Lambda_{Y}),

is
not
an axiom: it follows directly from binaturality (D1) by taking
f=\Lambda_{X}
and
g=\Lambda_{Y}
. We therefore retain only
\Lambda_{\mathbf{I}}=\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbf{I}}
(in (D2)) and the multiplicativity
\Lambda_{X\otimes Y}=\Lambda_{X}\otimes\Lambda_{Y}
(in (D4)) as axioms.

-lax monoidal functor)

Remark 2.5

(Scope of (S
\Lambda
) and why we keep it strict)
.

Throughout we impose the strict condition

F(\Lambda_{Z})=\Lambda^{\prime}_{FZ}\qquad(Z\in\mathcal{C}).

(3)

This delivers a
strict
2
-
category in which composite laxators agree on the nose and all 2

A tempting “bicategorical weakening” is to replace (

are endo

(2

together with invertible unit comparisons

and

The strict law (

(Monoidal natural transformation)

F
and
G
satisfy the distortion compatibility

F(\sigma_{X,Y})\circ\mu^{F}_{X,Y}=\mu^{F}_{Y,X}\circ\sigma^{\prime}_{FX,FY}

(8)

for all
X,Y\in\mathcal{C}
, and

G(\sigma^{\prime}_{A,B})\circ\mu^{G}_{A,B}=\mu^{G}_{B,A}\circ\sigma^{\prime\prime}_{GA,GB}

(9)

for all
A,B\in\mathcal{D}
.
Recall the composite laxator

\mu^{GF}_{X,Y}\ :=\ G\!\big(\mu^{F}_{X,Y}\big)\circ\mu^{G}_{FX,FY}.

We must show, for all
X,Y\in\mathcal{C}
,

(GF)(\sigma_{X,Y})\circ\mu^{GF}_{X,Y}\;=\;\mu^{GF}_{Y,X}\circ\sigma^{\prime\prime}_{GFX,GFY}.

Compute in

\displaystyle(GF)(\sigma_{X,Y})\circ\mu^{GF}_{X,Y}

\displaystyle=G\!\big(F(\sigma_{X,Y})\big)\ \circ\ \Big(G\!\big(\mu^{F}_{X,Y}\big)\circ\mu^{G}_{FX,FY}\Big)

\displaystyle=G\!\big(F(\sigma_{X,Y})\circ\mu^{F}_{X,Y}\big)\ \circ\ \mu^{G}_{FX,FY}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:Ssigma-F}}{=}\ G\!\big(\mu^{F}_{Y,X}\circ\sigma^{\prime}_{FX,FY}\big)\ \circ\ \mu^{G}_{FX,FY}

\displaystyle=\ G\!\big(\mu^{F}_{Y,X}\big)\circ G\!\big(\sigma^{\prime}_{FX,FY}\big)\circ\mu^{G}_{FX,FY}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:Ssigma-G}}{=}\ G\!\big(\mu^{F}_{Y,X}\big)\circ\mu^{G}_{FY,FX}\circ\sigma^{\prime\prime}_{GFX,GFY}

\displaystyle=\ \Big(G\!\big(\mu^{F}_{Y,X}\big)\circ\mu^{G}_{FY,FX}\Big)\ \circ\ \sigma^{\prime\prime}_{GFX,GFY}

\displaystyle=\ \mu^{GF}_{Y,X}\ \circ\ \sigma^{\prime\prime}_{GFX,GFY}.

This is exactly the required

Lemma 3.6

(Vertical composition of monoidal transformations)
.

If
\theta:F\Rightarrow G
and
\phi:G\Rightarrow H
are monoidal, then
\phi\circ\theta:F\Rightarrow H
is monoidal.

Proof.

Let
F,G,H:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
be
\sigma
-lax monoidal with structure maps

\mu^{F}_{X,Y}:FX\otimes^{\prime}FY\to F(X\otimes Y),\quad\mu^{G}_{X,Y}:GX\otimes^{\prime}GY\to G(X\otimes Y),

\mu^{H}_{X,Y}:HX\otimes^{\prime}HY\to H(X\otimes Y),

and units
\mu^{F}_{0}:\mathbf{I}^{\prime}\to F\mathbf{I}
,
\mu^{G}_{0}:\mathbf{I}^{\prime}\to G\mathbf{I}
,
\mu^{H}_{0}:\mathbf{I}^{\prime}\to H\mathbf{I}
.
Assume
\theta:F\Rightarrow G
and
\phi:G\Rightarrow H
are monoidal, i.e.

\displaystyle\mu^{G}_{X,Y}\circ(\theta_{X}\otimes^{\prime}\theta_{Y})

\displaystyle=\theta_{X\otimes Y}\circ\mu^{F}_{X,Y},

(10)

\displaystyle\mu^{G}_{0}

\displaystyle=\theta_{\mathbf{I}}\circ\mu^{F}_{0},

\displaystyle\mu^{H}_{X,Y}\circ(\phi_{X}\otimes^{\prime}\phi_{Y})

\displaystyle=\phi_{X\otimes Y}\circ\mu^{G}_{X,Y},

(11)

\displaystyle\mu^{H}_{0}

\displaystyle=\phi_{\mathbf{I}}\circ\mu^{G}_{0}.

We show that the vertical composite
(\phi\circ\theta):F\Rightarrow H
is monoidal by verifying the tensor and unit axioms.

Tensor axiom.

For any
X,Y\in\mathcal{C}
,

\displaystyle\mu^{H}_{X,Y}\circ\big((\phi\circ\theta)_{X}\otimes^{\prime}(\phi\circ\theta)_{Y}\big)

\displaystyle=\mu^{H}_{X,Y}\circ(\phi_{X}\otimes^{\prime}\phi_{Y})\circ(\theta_{X}\otimes^{\prime}\theta_{Y})

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:mon-phi-V}}{=}\phi_{X\otimes Y}\circ\mu^{G}_{X,Y}\circ(\theta_{X}\otimes^{\prime}\theta_{Y})

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:mon-theta-V}}{=}\phi_{X\otimes Y}\circ\theta_{X\otimes Y}\circ\mu^{F}_{X,Y}

\displaystyle=(\phi\circ\theta)_{X\otimes Y}\circ\mu^{F}_{X,Y}.

Unit axiom.

\mu^{H}_{0}\overset{\eqref{eq:mon-phi-V}}{=}\phi_{\mathbf{I}}\circ\mu^{G}_{0}\overset{\eqref{eq:mon-theta-V}}{=}\phi_{\mathbf{I}}\circ\theta_{\mathbf{I}}\circ\mu^{F}_{0}=(\phi\circ\theta)_{\mathbf{I}}\circ\mu^{F}_{0}.

Both required equalities hold, hence
\phi\circ\theta
is monoidal.
∎

Lemma 3.7

(Horizontal composition of monoidal transformations)
.

Let
F_{1},F_{2}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
and
G_{1},G_{2}:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{E}
be
\sigma
-lax monoidal functors and let
\theta:F_{1}\Rightarrow F_{2}
,
\phi:G_{1}\Rightarrow G_{2}
be monoidal. Then their horizontal composite

\phi\ast\theta:G_{1}F_{1}\Rightarrow G_{2}F_{2},\qquad(\phi\ast\theta)_{X}:=\phi_{F_{2}X}\circ G_{1}(\theta_{X})=G_{2}(\theta_{X})\circ\phi_{F_{1}X},

is monoidal.

Proof.

We use the monoidal structures

\mu^{F_{i}}_{X,Y}:F_{i}X\otimes^{\prime}F_{i}Y\to F_{i}(X\otimes Y),\quad\mu^{G_{j}}_{A,B}:G_{j}A\otimes^{\prime\prime}G_{j}B\to G_{j}(A\otimes^{\prime}B),

and units
\mu^{F_{i}}_{0}:\mathbf{I}^{\prime}\to F_{i}\mathbf{I}
,
\mu^{G_{j}}_{0}:\mathbf{I}^{\prime\prime}\to G_{j}\mathbf{I}^{\prime}

(i,j\in\{1,2\})
.
The composite laxators and unit are

\mu^{G_{j}F_{i}}_{X,Y}:=G_{j}\!\big(\mu^{F_{i}}_{X,Y}\big)\circ\mu^{G_{j}}_{F_{i}X,F_{i}Y},

\mu^{G_{j}F_{i}}_{0}:=G_{j}(\mu^{F_{i}}_{0})\circ\mu^{G_{j}}_{0}.

Well-definedness of
(\phi\ast\theta)_{X}
.

By naturality of
\phi
at
m=\theta_{X}:F_{1}X\to F_{2}X
,

\phi_{F_{2}X}\circ G_{1}(\theta_{X})=G_{2}(\theta_{X})\circ\phi_{F_{1}X}.

Tensor axiom.

For any
X,Y\in\mathcal{C}
,

\displaystyle\mu^{G_{2}F_{2}}_{X,Y}\circ\big((\phi\ast\theta)_{X}\otimes^{\prime\prime}(\phi\ast\theta)_{Y}\big)

\displaystyle=\Big(G_{2}(\mu^{F_{2}}_{X,Y})\circ\mu^{G_{2}}_{F_{2}X,F_{2}Y}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad\circ\Big((\phi_{F_{2}X}\circ G_{1}(\theta_{X}))\otimes^{\prime\prime}(\phi_{F_{2}Y}\circ G_{1}(\theta_{Y}))\Big)

\displaystyle=G_{2}(\mu^{F_{2}}_{X,Y})\circ\Big(\mu^{G_{2}}_{F_{2}X,F_{2}Y}\circ(\phi_{F_{2}X}\otimes^{\prime\prime}\phi_{F_{2}Y})\Big)

\displaystyle\quad\circ\big(G_{1}(\theta_{X})\otimes^{\prime\prime}G_{1}(\theta_{Y})\big)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{mon}(\phi)}{=}G_{2}(\mu^{F_{2}}_{X,Y})\circ\Big(\phi_{F_{2}X\otimes^{\prime}F_{2}Y}\circ\mu^{G_{1}}_{F_{2}X,F_{2}Y}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad\circ\big(G_{1}(\theta_{X})\otimes^{\prime\prime}G_{1}(\theta_{Y})\big)

\displaystyle=G_{2}(\mu^{F_{2}}_{X,Y})\circ\phi_{F_{2}X\otimes^{\prime}F_{2}Y}

\displaystyle\quad\circ\Big(\mu^{G_{1}}_{F_{2}X,F_{2}Y}\circ\big(G_{1}(\theta_{X})\otimes^{\prime\prime}G_{1}(\theta_{Y})\big)\Big)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{nat }(\mu^{G_{1}}_{-,-})}{=}G_{2}(\mu^{F_{2}}_{X,Y})\circ\phi_{F_{2}X\otimes^{\prime}F_{2}Y}

\displaystyle\quad\circ G_{1}(\theta_{X}\otimes^{\prime}\theta_{Y})\circ\mu^{G_{1}}_{F_{1}X,F_{1}Y}

\displaystyle=\phi_{F_{2}(X\otimes Y)}\circ G_{1}\!\big(\mu^{F_{2}}_{X,Y}\circ(\theta_{X}\otimes^{\prime}\theta_{Y})\big)

\displaystyle\quad\circ\mu^{G_{1}}_{F_{1}X,F_{1}Y}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{mon}(\theta)}{=}\big(\phi_{F_{2}(X\otimes Y)}\circ G_{1}(\theta_{X\otimes Y})\big)

\displaystyle\quad\circ G_{1}(\mu^{F_{1}}_{X,Y})\circ\mu^{G_{1}}_{F_{1}X,F_{1}Y}

\displaystyle=(\phi\ast\theta)_{X\otimes Y}\circ\mu^{G_{1}F_{1}}_{X,Y}.

Unit axiom.

We must show

\mu^{G_{2}F_{2}}_{0}\;=\;(\phi\ast\theta)_{\mathbf{I}}\circ\mu^{G_{1}F_{1}}_{0}.

Starting from the right-hand side and using the definitions:

\displaystyle(\phi\ast\theta)_{\mathbf{I}}\circ\mu^{G_{1}F_{1}}_{0}

\displaystyle=\big(\phi_{F_{2}\mathbf{I}}\circ G_{1}(\theta_{\mathbf{I}})\big)\circ\big(G_{1}(\mu^{F_{1}}_{0})\circ\mu^{G_{1}}_{0}\big)

\displaystyle=\phi_{F_{2}\mathbf{I}}\circ G_{1}(\theta_{\mathbf{I}}\circ\mu^{F_{1}}_{0})\circ\mu^{G_{1}}_{0}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{unit mon}(\theta)}{=}\phi_{F_{2}\mathbf{I}}\circ G_{1}(\mu^{F_{2}}_{0})\circ\mu^{G_{1}}_{0}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{nat }(\phi)}{=}\big(G_{2}(\mu^{F_{2}}_{0})\circ\phi_{\mathbf{I}^{\prime}}\big)\circ\mu^{G_{1}}_{0}

\displaystyle=G_{2}(\mu^{F_{2}}_{0})\circ\big(\phi_{\mathbf{I}^{\prime}}\circ\mu^{G_{1}}_{0}\big)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{unit mon}(\phi)}{=}G_{2}(\mu^{F_{2}}_{0})\circ\mu^{G_{2}}_{0}\;=\;\mu^{G_{2}F_{2}}_{0}.

Here, “nat
(\phi)
” is naturality of
\phi
at the arrow
\mu^{F_{2}}_{0}:\mathbf{I}^{\prime}\to F_{2}\mathbf{I}
, and “unit mon
(\phi)
” is the unit axiom
\phi_{\mathbf{I}^{\prime}}\circ\mu^{G_{1}}_{0}=\mu^{G_{2}}_{0}
.

Both tensor and unit axioms hold, so
\phi\ast\theta
is monoidal.
∎

4
Main Theorem: Strict 2-Category Structure

We now state and prove the main result.

Theorem 4.1

(Strict 2-category structure)
.

Let
\mathbf{DistMon}_{\sigma}
denote the structure consisting of:

•

Objects (0-cells):
distorted monoidal categories

(\mathcal{C},\otimes,\mathbf{I},\alpha,\lambda,\rho,\Lambda,\sigma)
;

•

1-morphisms (1-cells):

\sigma
-lax monoidal functors
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
;

•

2-morphisms (2-cells):
monoidal natural transformations
\theta:F\Rightarrow G
.

Then
\mathbf{DistMon}_{\sigma}
forms a
strict 2-category
, meaning:

1.

Composition of
\sigma
-lax monoidal functors is
\sigma
-lax monoidal.

2.

Vertical and horizontal compositions of monoidal natural transformations are monoidal.

3.

Identity 1-morphisms
\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{C}}
and identity 2-morphisms
\mathrm{Id}_{F}
are monoidal.

4.

Horizontal composition of 2-morphisms is strictly associative and unital: for all composable
\theta,\eta
and
\phi,\psi
,

(\psi\ast\phi)\ast(\eta\ast\theta)=(\psi\ast(\phi\ast\eta))\ast\theta

and

\mathrm{Id}_{G}\ast\theta=\theta=\theta\ast\mathrm{Id}_{F}.

5.

The
interchange law
holds as an equality: for composable 2-morphisms
\eta,\theta,\phi,\psi
,

(\psi\circ\phi)\ast(\theta\circ\eta)\;=\;(\psi\ast\theta)\circ(\phi\ast\eta).

6.

Strictness of triple composition:
For functors
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
,
G:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{E}
,
H:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{F}
, the composite laxators and units satisfy the
equalities

\displaystyle\mu^{H(GF)}_{X,Y}

\displaystyle=\mu^{(HG)F}_{X,Y},

(12)

\displaystyle\mu^{H(GF)}_{0}

\displaystyle=\mu^{(HG)F}_{0}.

(13)

Proof.

We verify each clause constructively.

(1) Closure of 1-morphisms under composition.

Let

\displaystyle F:(\mathcal{C},\otimes,\mathbf{I},\alpha,\lambda,\rho,\Lambda,\sigma)

\displaystyle\quad\to(\mathcal{D},\otimes^{\prime},\mathbf{I}^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime},\rho^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}),

\displaystyle G:(\mathcal{D},\otimes^{\prime},\mathbf{I}^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime},\rho^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})

\displaystyle\quad\to(\mathcal{E},\otimes^{\prime\prime},\mathbf{I}^{\prime\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime},\rho^{\prime\prime},\Lambda^{\prime\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime})

be
\sigma
-lax. Define

\mu^{GF}_{X,Y}:=G\!\big(\mu^{F}_{X,Y}\big)\circ\mu^{G}_{FX,FY},

\mu^{GF}_{0}:=G(\mu^{F}_{0})\circ\mu^{G}_{0}.

By Lemmas
3.1
,
3.2
,
3.3
,
3.4
, and
3.5
,
(GF,\mu^{GF}_{-,-},\mu^{GF}_{0})
is
\sigma
-lax monoidal.

(2) Closure of 2-morphisms under composition.

Vertical composition: If
\theta:F\Rightarrow G
and
\phi:G\Rightarrow H
are monoidal, then
\phi\circ\theta:F\Rightarrow H
is monoidal by Lemma
3.6
.

Horizontal composition: If
\theta:F_{1}\Rightarrow F_{2}
and
\phi:G_{1}\Rightarrow G_{2}
are monoidal, define

(\phi\ast\theta)_{X}:=\phi_{F_{2}X}\circ G_{1}(\theta_{X})=G_{2}(\theta_{X})\circ\phi_{F_{1}X}.

Then
\phi\ast\theta:G_{1}F_{1}\Rightarrow G_{2}F_{2}
is monoidal by Lemma
3.7
.

(3) Identity 1- and 2-morphisms.

For each
\mathcal{C}
, equip
\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{C}}
with
\mu^{\mathrm{Id}}_{X,Y}:=\mathrm{Id}_{X\otimes Y}
and
\mu^{\mathrm{Id}}_{0}:=\mathrm{Id}_{\mathbf{I}}
. Then
\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{C}}
is
\sigma
-lax by Lemma
3.12
. For any
F
, the identity
\mathrm{Id}_{F}
is monoidal by Lemma
3.13
.

(4) Associativity and unitality of horizontal composition.

Horizontal composition is defined componentwise via functor composition and whiskering. Since functor composition in
\mathbf{Cat}
is strictly associative and unital, horizontal composition inherits this property. Explicitly, for
\theta:F_{0}\Rightarrow F_{1}
and
\phi:G_{0}\Rightarrow G_{1}
,

(\mathrm{Id}_{G}\ast\theta)_{X}=\mathrm{Id}_{GF_{1}X}\circ G(\theta_{X})=G(\theta_{X})

and

(\phi\ast\mathrm{Id}_{F})_{X}=\phi_{FX}\circ\mathrm{Id}_{G_{0}FX}=\phi_{FX}.

The associativity of horizontal composition follows from the associativity of functor composition.

(5) Interchange law.

Take

\eta:F_{0}\Rightarrow F_{1},\quad\theta:F_{1}\Rightarrow F_{2},\qquad\phi:G_{0}\Rightarrow G_{1},\quad\psi:G_{1}\Rightarrow G_{2}.

For each
X\in\mathcal{C}
, compute:

\displaystyle\big((\psi\circ\phi)\ast(\theta\circ\eta)\big)_{X}

\displaystyle=(\psi\circ\phi)_{F_{2}X}\circ G_{0}\big((\theta\circ\eta)_{X}\big)

\displaystyle=\big(\psi_{F_{2}X}\circ\phi_{F_{2}X}\big)\circ\big(G_{0}(\theta_{X})\circ G_{0}(\eta_{X})\big)

\displaystyle=\psi_{F_{2}X}\circ\big(\phi_{F_{2}X}\circ G_{0}(\theta_{X})\big)\circ G_{0}(\eta_{X}).

By naturality of
\phi
at the morphism
\theta_{X}:F_{1}X\to F_{2}X
(Lemma
3.10
),

\phi_{F_{2}X}\circ G_{0}(\theta_{X})=G_{1}(\theta_{X})\circ\phi_{F_{1}X}.

Thus

\displaystyle\big((\psi\circ\phi)\ast(\theta\circ\eta)\big)_{X}

\displaystyle=\psi_{F_{2}X}\circ G_{1}(\theta_{X})\circ\phi_{F_{1}X}\circ G_{0}(\eta_{X})

\displaystyle=\big(\psi_{F_{2}X}\circ G_{1}(\theta_{X})\big)\circ\big(\phi_{F_{1}X}\circ G_{0}(\eta_{X})\big)

\displaystyle=(\psi\ast\theta)_{X}\circ(\phi\ast\eta)_{X}

\displaystyle=\big((\psi\ast\theta)\circ(\phi\ast\eta)\big)_{X}.

Hence the interchange law holds as an equality componentwise, and thus as an equality of natural transformations.

(6) Strictness of triple composition.

Let
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
,
G:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{E}
,
H:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{F}
be
\sigma
-lax monoidal functors. We verify (
12
) and (
13
).

For the laxators, compute for any
X,Y\in\mathcal{C}
:

\displaystyle\mu^{H(GF)}_{X,Y}

\displaystyle=H\!\big(\mu^{GF}_{X,Y}\big)\circ\mu^{H}_{(GF)X,(GF)Y}

\displaystyle=H\!\big(G(\mu^{F}_{X,Y})\circ\mu^{G}_{FX,FY}\big)\circ\mu^{H}_{GFX,GFY}

\displaystyle=H\!\big(G(\mu^{F}_{X,Y})\big)\circ H\!\big(\mu^{G}_{FX,FY}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\circ\mu^{H}_{GFX,GFY}

\displaystyle=(HG)\!\big(\mu^{F}_{X,Y}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\circ\underbrace{\Big(H\!\big(\mu^{G}_{FX,FY}\big)\circ\mu^{H}_{GFX,GFY}\Big)}_{=\,\mu^{HG}_{FX,FY}}

\displaystyle=\mu^{(HG)F}_{X,Y}.

For the units:

\displaystyle\mu^{H(GF)}_{0}

\displaystyle=H\!\big(\mu^{GF}_{0}\big)\circ\mu^{H}_{0}

\displaystyle=H\!\big(G(\mu^{F}_{0})\circ\mu^{G}_{0}\big)\circ\mu^{H}_{0}

\displaystyle=H\!\big(G(\mu^{F}_{0})\big)\circ H(\mu^{G}_{0})\circ\mu^{H}_{0}

\displaystyle=(HG)(\mu^{F}_{0})\circ\underbrace{\big(H(\mu^{G}_{0})\circ\mu^{H}_{0}\big)}_{=\,\mu^{HG}_{0}}

\displaystyle=\mu^{(HG)F}_{0}.

Thus both (
12
) and (
13
) hold as
equalities
of morphisms, establishing the strict associativity of functor composition in
\mathbf{DistMon}_{\sigma}
.

From clauses (1)–(6),
\mathbf{DistMon}_{\sigma}
is a strict 2-category.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

The strictness established in Theorem
4.1
is a consequence of our explicit choices of composite laxator structures. In a more general setting allowing lax or pseudo
\Lambda
-compatibility, one would obtain a weak 2-category (bicategory) structure with coherence isomorphisms. Our framework prioritizes simplicity and computability at the cost of excluding certain natural functors (see Remark
2.5
).

Example 5.3

(Parity projector that violates
\beta
–equivariant sliding)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\mathbf{SVect}_{k}
be the symmetric monoidal category of
\mathbb{Z}/2
–graded
k
–vector spaces with the Koszul braiding
\beta
.
For
V,W\in\mathcal{C}
, write
V=V_{0}\oplus V_{1}
and
W=W_{0}\oplus W_{1}
.
Define a binatural idempotent

to be the projector acting as the identity on the three parity summands

V_{0}\otimes W_{0},\quad V_{0}\otimes W_{1},\quad V_{1}\otimes W_{0},

and zero on
V_{1}\otimes W_{1}
(equivalently,
e_{V,W}=\mathrm{Id}_{V\otimes W}-\pi_{V_{1}\otimes W_{1}}

What holds.

1.

(E0) Idempotent and binatural.

By construction

2.

(E1) Normalization.

The unit

.

3.

(E2) Typed multiplicativity.

For homogeneous
v\in V_{a}
,
w\in W_{b}
,
z\in Z_{c}
with

, a direct check shows

e_{V\otimes W,\,Z}\circ(e_{V,W}\otimes\mathrm{Id})=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ (a\wedge b)\ \vee\ ((a\oplus b)\wedge c),

and

(\mathrm{Id}\otimes e_{W,Z})\circ e_{V,\,W\otimes Z}=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ (b\wedge c)\ \vee\ (a\wedge(b\oplus c)).

Both conditions are equivalent to “at least two of
a,b,c
equal

{}^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{L}}
) and (E2
{}^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{R}}
) hold after inserting the associators
\alpha,\alpha^{-1}
.

What fails.

The
\beta
–equivariant sliding axioms (B

) and (B
{}^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{R}}
)

hold for this

Working in a strict monoidal skeleton (Mac Lane coherence; then reinsert

T:=v_{1}\otimes w_{1}\otimes z_{0}\in V_{1}\otimes W_{1}\otimes Z_{0}\quad(v_{1}\in V_{1},\ w_{1}\in W_{1},\ z_{0}\in Z_{0}\ \text{nonzero}).

For (B
{}^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{L}}

(\mathrm{Id}_{V}\otimes\beta_{W,Z})\circ(\mathrm{Id}_{V}\otimes e_{W,Z})\circ e_{V,\,W\otimes Z}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{=}}(e_{V,Z}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{W})\circ(\mathrm{Id}_{V}\otimes\beta_{W,Z})\circ(\mathrm{Id}_{V}\otimes e_{W,Z}),

we obtain:

•

Left-hand side:

\mathrm{LHS}(T)=0

•

Right-hand side:

6.1

Adjoining the distortion operations.

The axiom (S

Proposition 6.4

\mathrm{Id}

6.4
Coherence program

A Mac Lane
-
style coherence theorem for distorted monoidal categories would state that every well
-
typed diagram built from
\alpha,\lambda,\rho,\sigma,\Lambda
and the axioms (D1)–(D4) commutes. Even in the strict monoidal case (
\alpha=\lambda=\rho=\mathrm{Id}
) this is nontrivial because
\sigma
need not be invertible. The braided hexagons are classically equivalent to the Yang–Baxter equation when
\beta
is an isomorphism
[
3
]
; in our non
-
invertible setting the typed hexagons furnish
oriented
Yang–Baxter relations. A promising route is to view (D3) as a terminating, locally confluent rewriting system on string diagrams enriched by the idempotent data that appear in Proposition
5.2
. Establishing global confluence would yield normal forms and hence coherence. We expect that additional mild hypotheses (e.g. stability of chosen idempotents under tensor, as in (E2)) suffice to prove such a result.

The constructions of Section

•

•

.

These case studies indicate that distorted monoidal categories provide a disciplined middle ground between symmetric flexibility and directed irreversibility, with axioms designed to keep diagrammatic reasoning intact.

6.6

1.

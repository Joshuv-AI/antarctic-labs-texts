UMAP Is Spectral Clustering on the Fuzzy Nearest-Neighbor Graph
Yang Yang

Abstract
UMAP (Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection) is one of the most widely used algorithms for nonlinear dimension reduction and data visualization. Despite its popularity and its presentation through the lens of algebraic topology, the precise relationship between UMAP and classical spectral methods has remained informal. In this work, we prove that UMAP performs spectral clustering on the fuzzy k k -nearest neighbor graph . Our proof proceeds in three steps: (1) we show that UMAP’s stochastic optimization with negative sampling is a contrastive learning objective on the similarity graph; (2) we invoke the result of 8 establishing that contrastive learning on a similarity graph is equivalent to spectral clustering; and (3) we verify that UMAP’s spectral initialization computes the exact linear solution to this spectral problem. The equivalence is exact for Gaussian kernels, and holds as a first-order approximation for UMAP’s default Cauchy-type kernel. Our result unifies UMAP, contrastive learning, and spectral clustering under a single framework and provides theoretical grounding for several empirical observations about UMAP’s behavior.

Background

2.1
UMAP: Construction and Optimization

Given data
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{D}
and a number of neighbors
k
, UMAP proceeds as follows.

Step 1:
k
-NN graph construction.

For each point
x_{i}
, compute the
k
nearest neighbors
\{x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{k}}\}
and define:

\rho_{i}=\min_{j:d(x_{i},x_{j})>0}d(x_{i},x_{j}),\quad\sum_{j=1}^{k}\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{\max(0,d_{ij}-\rho_{i})}{\sigma_{i}}\Bigr)=\log_{2}k,

(1)

where
d_{ij}=d(x_{i},x_{i_{j}})
and
\sigma_{i}
is found by binary search. The directed edge weights are:

v_{j|i}=\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{\max(0,d(x_{i},x_{j})-\rho_{i})}{\sigma_{i}}\Bigr).

(2)

Step 2: Symmetrization.

The directed graph is symmetrized via the probabilistic
t
-conorm:

v_{ij}=v_{j|i}+v_{i|j}-v_{j|i}\cdot v_{i|j}.

(3)

This produces the adjacency matrix
V=(v_{ij})\in[0,1]^{n\times n}
.

Step 3: Low-dimensional embedding.

Define the smooth kernel:

\Phi(y_{i},y_{j})=\bigl(1+a\|y_{i}-y_{j}\|^{2b}\bigr)^{-1},

(4)

where
a,b>0
are fitted to a target curve determined by the
min-dist
parameter. The default fit yields
a\approx 1.929
,
b\approx 0.7915
; setting
a=b=1
recovers the Cauchy kernel used in
t
-SNE.

UMAP minimizes the
fuzzy set cross-entropy
between the high-dimensional graph
V
and low-dimensional similarities
W=(\Phi(y_{i},y_{j}))
:

\mathcal{L}(Y)=\sum_{i\neq j}\bigl[-v_{ij}\log\Phi(y_{i},y_{j})-(1-v_{ij})\log(1-\Phi(y_{i},y_{j}))\bigr].

(5)

Step 4: Optimization via negative sampling SGD.

Algorithm 5 of
12
optimizes (
5
) by:

•

Positive step:
Sample edge
(a,b)
with probability
v_{ab}
; update
y_{a}\leftarrow y_{a}+\alpha\,\nabla_{y_{a}}\log\Phi(y_{a},y_{b})
.

•

Negative step:
For
n_{\text{neg}}
uniform random samples
c
, update
y_{a}\leftarrow y_{a}+\alpha\,\nabla_{y_{a}}\log(1-\Phi(y_{a},y_{c}))
.

•

The learning rate decays:
\alpha=1-e/n_{\text{epochs}}
.

Initialization.

UMAP initializes the embedding via spectral decomposition of the normalized graph Laplacian
\tilde{L}(V)=D_{V}^{-1/2}(D_{V}-V)D_{V}^{-1/2}
, taking the eigenvectors corresponding to the
d
smallest non-zero eigenvalues (Algorithm 4 of
12
).

Spectral Clustering

Definition 2.1

(Graph Laplacian)
.

For a weighted adjacency matrix
W\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n\times n}
, the
combinatorial Laplacian
is
L(W)=D_{W}-W
where
D_{W}=\diag(\sum_{j}W_{ij})
. The
normalized Laplacian
is
\tilde{L}(W)=D_{W}^{-1/2}L(W)D_{W}^{-1/2}

]
.

Definition 2.2

(Spectral Clustering
[
21
]
)
.

Given an adjacency matrix
W

, the spectral clustering problem is:

\min_{Z\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}}\;\tr\!\bigl(Z^{\top}L(W)\,Z\bigr)+E(Z).

(6)

Without regularization and with
Z^{\top}Z=I
, the solution is given by the bottom eigenvectors of

A key identity connecting the Laplacian quadratic form to pairwise distances is:

\tr\!\bigl(Z^{\top}L(W)\,Z\bigr)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}W_{ij}\,\|Z_{i}-Z_{j}\|^{2}.

(7)

Contrastive Learning as Spectral Clustering

3.1
Proof of
Theorem

3.1

The proof proceeds in four steps.

Step 1: UMAP’s negative sampling is contrastive learning.

UMAP’s Algorithm 5 decomposes the full loss (
5
) into stochastic updates. At each step, a positive pair
(a,b)
is drawn from the graph with probability proportional to
v_{ab}
, and
n_{\text{neg}}
negative pairs
(a,c_{i})
are drawn uniformly at random. The per-step stochastic loss is:

\ell(a)=-\log\Phi(y_{a},y_{b})-\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\text{neg}}}\log\bigl(1-\Phi(y_{a},y_{c_{i}})\bigr),

(12)

where
b
is sampled from the neighbors of
a
(with probability
\propto v_{ab}
) and
c_{i}\sim\text{Uniform}(\mathcal{V})
.

This is precisely
noise-contrastive estimation
(NCE)
[
7
]
, the same optimization paradigm underlying the negative sampling variant of contrastive learning
[
13
]
. The first term attracts positive pairs (graph neighbors); the second term repels uniformly sampled negatives. This structure is shared with InfoNCE
[
19
]
, which also draws positives from a similarity distribution and negatives uniformly.

Taking the expectation over sampling, the effective loss minimized over one epoch is:

\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}]=-\!\!\sum_{(a,b)\in\mathcal{E}}\!\!v_{ab}\log\Phi(y_{a},y_{b})-\frac{n_{\text{neg}}}{n}\sum_{a}d_{a}\sum_{c}\log(1\!-\!\Phi(y_{a},y_{c})),

(13)

where
d_{a}=\sum_{b}v_{ab}
is the weighted degree. The first sum is the
attraction
(pull neighbors together) and the second is the
repulsion
(push random pairs apart)—the canonical structure of contrastive objectives.

Step 2: The attractive term is a Laplacian quadratic form.

We analyze the attractive term
\mathcal{L}_{\text{attract}}=-\sum_{i\neq j}v_{ij}\log\Phi(y_{i},y_{j})
for two kernel choices.

Case (a): Gaussian kernel.
With
\Phi_{G}(y_{i},y_{j})=\exp(-\|y_{i}-y_{j}\|^{2}/2\tau)
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{attract}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i\neq j}v_{ij}\log\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{\|y_{i}-y_{j}\|^{2}}{2\tau}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\tau}\sum_{i\neq j}v_{ij}\|y_{i}-y_{j}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau}\,\tr\!\bigl(Y^{\top}L(V)\,Y\bigr),

(14)

where the last step uses the Laplacian identity (
7
). This is
exactly
the spectral clustering objective.

Case (b): Cauchy kernel.
With
\Phi(y_{i},y_{j})=(1+a\|y_{i}-y_{j}\|^{2})^{-1}
(setting
b=1
):

\mathcal{L}_{\text{attract}}=\sum_{i\neq j}v_{ij}\log(1+a\|y_{i}-y_{j}\|^{2}).

(15)

For neighboring pairs where
a\|y_{i}-y_{j}\|^{2}\ll 1
(which holds for graph-connected pairs in a well-optimized embedding), the first-order Taylor expansion
\log(1+x)\approx x
gives:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{attract}}\approx a\sum_{i\neq j}v_{ij}\|y_{i}-y_{j}\|^{2}=2a\,\tr\!\bigl(Y^{\top}L(V)\,Y\bigr).

(16)

Remark 3.2
.

Even without the Taylor approximation,
Equation

15
defines a
kernelized spectral objective
:
\sum_{ij}v_{ij}\,\phi(\|y_{i}-y_{j}\|^{2})
with
\phi(t)=\log(1+at)
. Since
\phi
is concave and monotonically increasing, it shares the same minimization landscape as the quadratic form—the objective is minimized when high-weight edges have small embedding distances. The concavity of
\phi
penalizes large distances less severely, providing the well-known “crowding problem” mitigation that motivates heavy-tailed kernels
[
20
]
.

Step 3: The repulsive term prevents collapse.

The repulsive term in UMAP’s loss (
5
) is:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{repel}}=-\sum_{i\neq j}(1-v_{ij})\log(1-\Phi(y_{i},y_{j})).

(17)

Since
v_{ij}
is nonzero only for
k
-nearest neighbors and zero for the vast majority of pairs (
v_{ij}=0
for non-neighbors), this simplifies to:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{repel}}\approx-\sum_{i\neq j}\log(1-\Phi(y_{i},y_{j})).

(18)

This term is minimized when
\Phi(y_{i},y_{j})\to 0
for non-neighbor pairs, i.e., non-neighbors are pushed apart.

In the contrastive framework of
Theorem

2.3
, the analogous term is the log-partition function
\log R(Z)=\sum_{i}\log\sum_{j\neq i}k(Z_{i}-Z_{j})
. While the functional forms differ (UMAP uses binary cross-entropy per-edge; InfoNCE uses softmax normalization), both serve the identical structural role: they are
barrier functions
that penalize embedding collapse (
y_{i}=y_{j}
for all
i,j
), ensuring the spectral clustering solution is non-degenerate. Without this term, the trivial solution
Y=\mathbf{0}
would minimize the attractive term.

Step 4: Spectral initialization is the exact linear solution.

UMAP initializes the embedding via Algorithm 4 of
12
:

1.

Form the adjacency matrix
A=V
from the
k
-NN graph.

2.

Compute
\tilde{L}=D^{-1/2}(D-A)D^{-1/2}
.

3.

Set
Y_{\text{init}}
to the bottom
d
eigenvectors of
\tilde{L}
.

This computes:

Y_{\text{init}}=\arg\min_{Z^{\top}Z=I}\;\tr\!\bigl(Z^{\top}\tilde{L}(V)\,Z\bigr),

(19)

which is the exact solution to the
normalized spectral clustering
problem on
V

[
15
,
14
]
. This is equivalently the Laplacian Eigenmaps embedding
[
3
]
of the fuzzy
k
-NN graph.
\square

4.3
Why Spectral Initialization Matters

Theorem

3.1
(c) provides a theoretical explanation for the empirical finding of
10

t
-SNE. Since UMAP’s SGD phase performs a

More precisely, the spectral initialization

lies near the global minimizer of the linearized (Gaussian-kernel) version of UMAP’s loss. The subsequent SGD with the Cauchy kernel refines the local geometry while the repulsive term maintains separation, preserving the global topology established by the eigenvectors.

4.5
Implications for Practitioners

The spectral clustering interpretation of UMAP has several practical implications:

1.

Choice of
k
:
Since UMAP performs spectral clustering on the
k
-NN graph, the choice of
k
determines the graph topology and thus the spectral properties. Larger
k
produces denser graphs with smoother eigenvectors; smaller
k
produces sparser graphs that capture finer local structure.

2.

Kernel choice:
The choice between Gaussian and Cauchy kernels affects only the
refinement
phase, not the fundamental spectral structure. The Cauchy kernel provides better low-dimensional visualization (via crowding mitigation) but does not change the underlying clustering.

3.

Number of epochs:
More SGD epochs produce embeddings further from the initial spectral solution, trading global structure for local fidelity. Early stopping preserves more of the spectral (global) structure.

4.

Negative samples:
The number of negative samples
n_{\text{neg}}
controls the strength of the repulsive regularizer relative to the attractive Laplacian term, analogous to the regularization strength in
Equation

6
.

5
Conclusion

We have proven that UMAP performs spectral clustering on the fuzzy
k
-nearest neighbor graph. The proof bridges UMAP’s cross-entropy optimization to the contrastive learning framework and invokes the spectral equivalence established by
8
. The equivalence is exact for Gaussian kernels and a first-order approximation for the default Cauchy kernel.

This result places UMAP, contrastive self-supervised learning, and spectral clustering within a unified theoretical framework: all three minimize cross-entropy divergences between fixed similarity graphs and learned embeddings, yielding objectives dominated by the graph Laplacian quadratic form. The main difference lies in how the similarity graph is constructed—
k
-NN distances for UMAP, augmentation probabilities for contrastive learning, and kernel similarities for spectral clustering.

We hope this result helps demystify UMAP’s remarkable empirical success and provides practitioners with a spectral perspective for understanding and tuning the algorithm.

Appendix A
Proof Details

A.1
The Laplacian Identity

Lemma A.1
.

For any symmetric
W\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n\times n}
and
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
:

\sum_{i,j}W_{ij}\|Z_{i}-Z_{j}\|^{2}=2\,\tr(Z^{\top}L(W)Z).

Proof.

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}W_{ij}\|Z_{i}-Z_{j}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}W_{ij}(Z_{i}^{\top}Z_{i}-2Z_{i}^{\top}Z_{j}+Z_{j}^{\top}Z_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}d_{i}Z_{i}^{\top}Z_{i}+\sum_{j}d_{j}Z_{j}^{\top}Z_{j}-2\sum_{i,j}W_{ij}Z_{i}^{\top}Z_{j}

\displaystyle=2\,\tr(Z^{\top}D_{W}Z)-2\,\tr(Z^{\top}WZ)

\displaystyle=2\,\tr(Z^{\top}(D_{W}-W)Z)=2\,\tr(Z^{\top}L(W)Z).\qed

A.2
Taylor Approximation Error Bound

For the Cauchy kernel approximation in
Equation

16
, we bound the error. Let
t_{ij}=a\|y_{i}-y_{j}\|^{2}
. The approximation error per edge is:

|\log(1+t_{ij})-t_{ij}|=\frac{t_{ij}^{2}}{2(1+\xi_{ij})^{2}}\leq\frac{t_{ij}^{2}}{2}

for some
\xi_{ij}\in(0,t_{ij})
, by the mean value theorem. Thus the total error is bounded by:

\bigl|\mathcal{L}_{\text{attract}}-2a\,\tr(Y^{\top}L(V)Y)\bigr|\leq\frac{a^{2}}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}v_{ij}\|y_{i}-y_{j}\|^{4}.

(21)

This bound is small when the embedding is well-optimized, since
v_{ij}>0
only for
k
-nearest neighbors, and the attractive force drives
\|y_{i}-y_{j}\|\to 0
for such pairs. In practice, for the default UMAP parameters (
a\approx 1.929
,
min-dist
=0.1
), the typical inter-neighbor distance in the embedding is
\|y_{i}-y_{j}\|\approx 0.1
–
0.5
, giving
t_{ij}\approx 0.02
–
0.48
and relative errors below
25\%
.

A.3
Connection to the Normalized Cut

UMAP’s spectral initialization (
Equation

19
) solves the normalized spectral clustering problem, which is the relaxation of the
normalized cut
(NCut) objective
[
15
]
:

\text{NCut}(S,\bar{S})=\frac{\text{cut}(S,\bar{S})}{\text{vol}(S)}+\frac{\text{cut}(S,\bar{S})}{\text{vol}(\bar{S})},

where
\text{cut}(S,\bar{S})=\sum_{i\in S,j\in\bar{S}}v_{ij}
and
\text{vol}(S)=\sum_{i\in S}d_{i}
.

The relaxed continuous problem
\min_{Z^{\top}DZ=I}\tr(Z^{\top}L(V)Z)
is equivalent to
\min_{Z^{\top}Z=I}\tr(Z^{\top}\tilde{L}(V)Z)
via the substitution
Z\to D^{-1/2}Z
, whose solution is the generalized eigenvectors of
(L,D)
or equivalently the ordinary eigenvectors of
\tilde{L}
—precisely what UMAP computes.

This means that UMAP’s initialization partitions the data according to the minimum normalized cut of the
k
-NN graph, and the subsequent SGD refinement adjusts the within-cluster geometry using the nonlinear kernel.

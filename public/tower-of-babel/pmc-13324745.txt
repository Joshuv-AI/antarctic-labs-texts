Adapted two-phase capacitance–resistance modeling of polymer flooding in synthetic and benchmark reservoirs with varying degree of heterogeneity
Authors: Meruyet Zhanabayeva, Peyman Pourafshary
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Polymer flooding is a widely applied enhanced oil recovery method that improves sweep efficiency by increasing injected water viscosity and achieving a more favorable mobility ratio. Field-scale performance evaluation remains challenging when detailed reservoir data are unavailable, motivating the use of data-driven approaches such as Capacitance–Resistance Model (CRM). However, conventional single-phase CRM formulations neglect two-phase flow effects, limiting their applicability to polymer flooding. This study presents a two-phase CRM that explicitly incorporates polymer-induced viscosity variations and saturation-dependent mobility. The proposed approach was validated using two case studies, including a benchmark reservoir model (PUNQ-S3) and a synthetic reservoir using CMG STARS simulator. Results demonstrate that the two-phase CRM consistently improves predictive accuracy in synthetic reservoir cases, reducing forecast errors from 10 to 22% with the conventional CRM to consistently below 4% across both homogeneous and heterogeneous cases, demonstrating nearly a tenfold reduction in prediction error. Similarly, in the benchmark PUNQ-S3 case, forecast error decreased from 18% with the conventional CRM to about 9% with two-phase CRM. The two-phase CRM successfully captured polymer-induced mobility control, displacement dynamics, and interwell connectivity, whereas the conventional CRM failed under strong heterogeneity. The proposed two-phase CRM significantly outperforms the conventional formulation, offering a physics-informed, computationally efficient, and field-relevant framework offering strong potential for polymer flood evaluation under realistic reservoir conditions.

Introduction

Polymer flooding (PF) is a well-established EOR technique in which high molecular weight polymers are added to the injected water to increase its viscosity, thereby reducing water mobility and improving sweep efficiency during the displacement of viscous oil1–3,42. The fundamentals and applications of polymer flooding have been extensively reviewed in the literature4–11. In recent years, significant technical advancements and economic benefits have further stimulated widespread implementation of polymer flooding at the field scale. In polymer flooding, the injection of water-soluble polymers increases the effective viscosity of the displacing phase, thereby achieving a more favorable mobility ratio and delaying breakthrough especially in heterogeneous reservoirs2,5,6,9,10,12–19. Despite these successes, the prediction and evaluation of polymer flooding performance remain challenging. Full-physics numerical simulators can accurately represent these processes, although they are computationally demanding and require detailed reservoir characterization, which is often unavailable at the field scale. This has motivated the use of simplified models such as the CRM, which provides a reduced-physics framework to describe dynamic connectivity between injectors and producers using production and injection data4,20–27.

Conventional CRM formulations have been successfully applied to waterflooding, offering fast and reliable history matching and forecasting capabilities28–31. However, they assume single-phase flow and neglects the impact of multiphase phenomena such as saturation redistribution and viscosity variations32. These simplifications limit their applicability to polymer flooding, where polymer injection alters the displacement front and the effective mobility in the reservoir6,8,10,12,13,33–36. To address these limitations, a two-phase CRM formulation is adopted that explicitly accounts for saturation changes and mobility variations induced by polymer flooding while retaining the computational efficiency of the CRM framework.

The objective of this study is to evaluate the performance of the developed two-phase CRM when applied to polymer flooding and compare it against the conventional CRM formulation. The models were benchmarked by comparing their results with synthetic polymer flood cases generated using CMG STARS. Reservoir heterogeneity is systematically varied and represented by the Dykstra–Parsons coefficient (VDP) to assess model robustness across different heterogeneity scenarios37. Through this comparative analysis, we aim to establish the applicability and advantages of the adopted two-phase CRM as a rapid, physics-based tool for polymer flood performance evaluation.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section “Methodology” describes the methodology, including the development of the two-phase CRM and its implementation. Model performance is evaluated using two case studies: a synthetic Cartesian reservoir model and the benchmark PUNQ-S3 field-scale reservoir. Section “Results and discussion” presents the results and discussion. Finally, section “Conclusion and recommendations” summarizes the main conclusions of the study.

Development of the two-phase CRM for polymer flooding

The CRM has proven to be an efficient tool for predicting reservoir performance using available injection and production data. However, the classical CRM formulation, originally developed by27 is based on total fluid material balance and therefore treats the reservoir as a single-phase system. In this formulation, saturation changes are not explicitly coupled to production, which limits its ability to capture the dynamic variations in oil and water mobilities. While this assumption may be acceptable in late-stage waterfloods38 where water cut is high, it reduces model accuracy during processes in which phase behavior and mobility evolve more strongly.

This limitation is particularly critical in polymer flooding of heterogeneous reservoirs, where mobility control is strongly dependent on both saturation distribution and polymer-induced viscosity changes39. Polymer injection increases the effective viscosity of the injected phase, and the resulting saturation redistribution modifies the local mobility ratio between oil and water. Consequently, CRM that neglects saturation and viscosity variations may underestimate the polymer’s influence on sweep efficiency and oil recovery. By incorporating saturation profiles into the CRM framework, the model can dynamically update mobility and provide a more accurate representation of polymer flood performance. This extension is essential for predicting oil rates under polymer injection and for optimizing injection strategies especially in heterogeneous reservoirs.

Study case 1: development of a synthetic Cartesian reservoir model

In line with the research objectives, the methodology is designed to examine whether the developed two-phase CRM model can accurately capture oil production behavior under varying degrees of reservoir heterogeneity during polymer flooding. The CRM analysis requires injection and production histories under varying reservoir conditions as input data. To obtain this, we constructed a model to generate such histories. A three-dimensional synthetic reservoir model (Fig. 2) was developed in the CMG-STARS simulator, where polymer flooding scenarios were carried out for several reservoir cases with different heterogeneities. Study case 2: PUNQ-S3 benchmark model

To further assess the robustness of the proposed polymer flood model, its performance was validated using the benchmark PUNQ-S3 reservoir case, which is widely recognized in the literature for evaluating reservoir simulation and data-driven modeling approaches (F. J. T. Floris | M. D. Bush | M. Cuypers | F. Roggero | A-R. Syversveen, 2001). For this benchmark study, the same fluid and polymer properties, along with the main reservoir characteristics and injection data, were preserved to ensure a consistent comparison framework with the initial synthetic model. This setup enables a direct assessment of the model’s generalization capability under more realistic reservoir conditions.

PUNQ-S3 is a 5-layer heterogeneous reservoir model, discretized using a corner-point grid. The reservoir model contains 19 × 28x5 grid block of which 1761 blocks are active blocks. The grid size of each block is 180 m in horizontal directions. The reservoir model is completed with a corner point grid. Figure below shows the top structure of the reservoir model. By adopting this assumption, the analysis isolates the role of polymer-induced mobility control on CRM predictions while avoiding the complexities of detailed polymer transport modeling.

Conclusion and recommendations

In this study, a two-phase CRM formulation was modified and applied to PF in order to improve the predictive accuracy of production dynamics under varying reservoir heterogeneities. The results demonstrate that incorporating two-phase flow effects and mobility variation is essential for accurately capturing polymer flood behavior. Across all cases, the two-phase CRM consistently outperformed the conventional CRM, reducing prediction errors by nearly an order of magnitude. For example, in the highly channelized streak case, the average MAPE decreased from 21.45% with the conventional CRM to only 3.18% with the two-phase CRM. Similarly, in reservoirs with different levels of heterogeneity, the error of the conventional CRM ranged from 10–22%, while the two-phase CRM maintained errors below 2% in all cases, indicating near-perfect history matching even under unfavorable reservoir conditions. In addition, the two-phase CRM demonstrated superior predictive accuracy over the conventional CRM in the PUNQ-S3 polymer flood case, reducing the average MAPE from 18 to 9%, consistent with the improvements observed in the synthetic reservoir cases.

The analysis of interwell connectivity maps further confirmed that the two-phase CRM provides more realistic characterization of injector–producer communication during polymer flooding. Moreover, the CRM analysis results confirm that higher \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${K}_{v}$$\end{document}​ values are indicative of greater reservoir heterogeneity, thereby validating the consistency of the simulated cases.

Overall, this work highlights the novelty and importance of explicitly accounting for two-phase flow and mobility control in CRM modeling of polymer floods. The proposed approach significantly enhances predictive capability, offering a more robust framework for analyzing PF in both homogeneous and highly heterogeneous reservoirs. This contribution provides a foundation for extending CRM applications to chemical EOR processes and serves as a valuable step toward integrating polymer transport dynamics in future developments.

Future work should focus on coupling the two-phase CRM with a polymer transport model to account for concentration variation, adsorption and retention mechanisms to enhance its physical representativeness and predictive capability. Field-scale validation is also essential to establish the applicability of the proposed approach beyond synthetic reservoir models. Moreover, exploring the adaptability of the two-phase CRM to other chemical EOR processes, such as surfactant–polymer or alkaline–polymer flooding, represents an important direction for future research.

A homotopy theorem for incremental stability
Thomas Chaffey, Andrey Kharitenko, Fulvio Forni, Rodolphe Sepulchre

Abstract
A theorem is proved to verify incremental stability of a feedback system via a homotopy from a known incrementally stable system. A first corollary of that result is that incremental stability may be verified by separation of Scaled Relative Graphs, correcting two assumptions in [ 1 , Theorem 2] . A second corollary provides an incremental version of the classical IQC stability theorem.



I
Introduction

There are two standard approaches to verifying absolute stability of a feedback
interconnection: the first, introduced by
[
2
]
, is to work in an
extended space, which includes unstable signals, and show that all signals are, in
fact, stabilized by the feedback. The second, pioneered by
[
3
]
,
is based upon a
homotopy
argument: it is shown that a known
stable system can be perturbed continuously to produce the desired feedback
interconnection, in such a way that stability is never lost. Such an argument has
two ingredients: firstly, one shows that a system remains stable
under a small perturbation, provided a gain bound is satisfied. Secondly, one must
verify such a gain bound along the entire path of perturbations. In the
original theorems of Megretski and Rantzer
[
3
,
4
]
, it was shown that
perturbations which were small in the gap metric
[
5
]
preserved
stability, and (soft)
Integral Quadratic Constraints (IQCs)
were
used to verify gain bounds.

In this paper, we offer an alternative approach: an
incremental
homotopy
argument is developed (Theorem
2
), where gain bounds are
replaced by incremental gain bounds, and the gap metric is replaced by a minor
modification of the incremental small gain theorem
(Theorem
1
). In a similar vein to the
incremental versions of the small
gain theorem
[
6
]
, the stronger assumption of incremental
gain allows well-posedness and causality assumptions to be weakened. While in a
standard homotopy argument, well-posedness and causality of the feedback
interconnection must be assumed along the entire path, requiring reference to an
extended space, in the incremental setting,
only incremental boundedness of the operators must be assumed, which may be verified
without any extended space. The setting of Theorem
2
is
more general than the typical IQC setting, as neither operator is assumed to be
linear nor time-invariant.

Theorem
2
is a standalone and improved version of an
incremental stability theorem recently proved by the authors in

[
1
]
, in the context of a graphical stability criterion based on
the
Scaled Relative Graph (SRG)
, a graphical representation of an operator
introduced by
[
7
]
. In
[
1
]
, it was shown that the
SRG generalizes the Nyquist diagram of an LTI operator to an arbitrary nonlinear
operator, and a generalization of the Nyquist criterion was given in

[
1
, Theorem 2]
. As a first corollary of
Theorem
2
, we reprove
[
1
, Theorem 2]
,
correcting two technical assumptions. This theorem has also been recently
generalized by
[
8
]
, removing a technical assumption, however that
generalization relies on an extended space, which is not required here.

As a second corollary to Theorem
2
, we obtain an incremental version of the IQC stability theorem of

[
3
]
. This corollary is closely related to
[
9
, Theorem 7.40]
, but does not make any assumptions of causality, nor rely on an
extended space.
It has been shown that incremental gain bounds can
be verified using closely related
differential
IQCs
[
10
]
.
Incremental IQCs are used in the study of periodic solutions by

[
11
]
, in the study of neural networks by

[
12
]
and in system identification by
[
13
]
. It has recently been
shown that well-posedness and causality assumptions can be relaxed in the
non-incremental setting
[
14
]
.

The remainder of this note is structured as follows. In Section
II
,
we introduce necessary notation and preliminary results. In
Section
III
, we prove our main result
(Theorem
2
), a general incremental homotopy theorem. In
Section
IV
, it is shown how incremental stability may be verified using separation of
SRGs, and in Section
V
, an incremental IQC stability theorem is given.
Finally, in Section
VI
, it is shown how assumptions of incremental
boundedness may be relaxed in exchange for well-posedness and causality, giving a
middle ground between the incremental approach of
Theorem
2
and standard non-incremental arguments.

II
Preliminaries

II-A

Signals and systems

Let
\mathcal{F}
denote the space of all functions mapping the interval
[0,\infty)
into
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Let
L_{2}^{n}
be the space of equivalence classes of trajectories
u\in\mathcal{F}
and satisfying

\displaystyle\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert:=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}u(t)^{\mkern-1.5mu\top}u(t)\;\mathrm{d}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}<\infty,

under the equivalence
u\sim y\iff\left\lVert{u-y}\right\rVert=0
. We will abuse terminology
in the usual way and say that a trajectory
u\in\mathcal{F}

belongs to
L_{2}^{n}
when
it belongs to an equivalence class in
L_{2}^{n}
. For the remainder of this note, we
will drop the dimension
n
, and simply denote
L_{2}^{n}
by
L_{2}
, where
n
is
arbitrary. Given an element
x\in L_{2}
, we let
\hat{x}
denote its Fourier
transform.

By an
operator
on a domain
D\subseteq L_{2}
we will mean a single-valued map

H:D\to L_{2}
. The domain
D
will also be denoted
\operatorname{dom}\left({H}\right)
. We will associate an operator
H
with its
relation
or
graph
,
defined as
\{(u,y)\;|\;y=H(u)\}\subseteq L_{2}\times L_{2}
, and denote the two in the same way.
Scalar multiplication, summation, and inversion of relations are defined as follows:

\displaystyle\alpha H

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{(u,\alpha y)\;|\;y=H(u)\}

\displaystyle H_{1}+H_{2}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{(u,y+z)\;|\;y=H_{1}(u),z=H_{2}(u)\}

\displaystyle H^{-1}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{(y,u)\;|\;y=H(u)\}.

We note that the inverse of an operator may be multivalued, and therefore not
necessarily an operator. However, these relational operations are always well
defined.

Given an operator
H:L_{2}\to L_{2}
, we define the
gain
of
H
on
L_{2}
, denoted
\left\lVert{H}\right\rVert
, to be the smallest
\gamma>0
such
that there exists
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
such that, for all inputs
u\in L_{2}
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert{H(u)}\right\rVert\leq\gamma\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert+\beta.

If the gain of an operator is finite, the operator is said to be
bounded
. If

\beta=0
, the operator is said to have
finite gain with zero offset
.

The
incremental gain
of
H
on
L_{2}
is defined to be

\displaystyle\left\lVert{H}\right\rVert_{\Delta}:=\sup_{u_{1},u_{2}\in L_{2},u_{1}\neq u_{2}}\frac{\left\lVert{H(u_{1})-H(u_{2})}\right\rVert}{\left\lVert{u_{1}-u_{2}}\right\rVert}.

If the incremental gain of an operator is finite, the operator is said to be

incrementally bounded
. If an operator derives from a dynamical system,
incremental boundedness is equivalent to asymptotic stability of any input/output
trajectory, under reachability and observability assumptions
[
15
]
.

Consider the negative feedback interconnection of two operators,
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
, defined by
the equations

\displaystyle e

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u-H_{2}(y)

(1)

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{1}(e)

(2)

and illustrated in Figure
1
.
We make the standing assumption that
this negative feedback interconnection defines a (single-valued) operator from some (possibly empty) domain
D\subseteq L_{2}
to
L_{2}
, mapping
u
to
y
. We denote the relation of this operator
by
[H_{1},H_{2}]:=\{(u,y)\;|;\ \text{there exits a unique }e\text{ s.t. }\eqref{eq:feedback_one}--\eqref{eq:feedback_two}\text{ are satisfied}\}
.

Fig. 1:
Negative feedback interconnection of
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
.

We will make use of the following two technical lemmas.

Lemma 1
.

Given operators
H_{1},H_{2}:L_{2}\to L_{2}
,

\displaystyle[H_{1},H_{2}]=(H_{1}^{-1}+H_{2})^{-1}.

Proof.

Applying the definitions of relational inverse and sum, we arrive at

\displaystyle(H_{1}^{-1}+H_{2})^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{(e+z,y)\;|\;y=H_{1}(e),u-e=H_{2}(y)\}.

Setting
u=e+z
, we arrive at the definition of
[H_{1},H_{2}]
.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Given
\tau,\nu\geq 0
and operators
H_{1},H_{2}:L_{2}\to L_{2}
,

\displaystyle[H_{1},(\tau+\nu)H_{2}]=[[H_{1},\tau H_{2}],\nu H_{2}].

(3)

Proof.

Note that
(H^{-1})^{-1}=H
, and, given a single-valued operator,
(\tau+\nu)H=\tau H+\nu H
. We then have:

\displaystyle[H_{1},(\tau+\nu)H_{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(H_{1}^{-1}+(\tau+\nu)H_{2})^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(H_{1}^{-1}+\tau H_{2}+\nu H_{2})^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((H_{1}^{-1}+\tau H_{2})^{-1})^{-1}+\nu H_{2})^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[[H_{1},\tau H_{2}],\nu H_{2}].

∎

The following theorem is a modified version of the classical incremental small gain
theorem
[
6
, Theorem 30, p. 184]
. It differs from the classical statement of the
theorem in that the operators are defined only on
L_{2}
, rather than an extended
space, and are not required to map
0
to
0
. It is closely related to the incremental gap robustness result of
[
5
, Theorem 1]
.

Theorem 1

(Incremental small gain theorem)
.

Let
H_{1},H_{2}:L_{2}\to L_{2}
be operators with incremental gain bounds of

\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
respectively. If
\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}<1
, then for any

u\in L_{2}
, there exist unique
e,y\in L_{2}
satisfying the feedback
interconnection (
1
)–(
2
).

Proof.

Fix
u\in L_{2}
. Substituting (
2
) in (
1
)
gives
e=u-H_{2}(H_{1}(e))
. Define

K_{u}(x):=u-H_{2}(H_{1}(x))
. Since
H_{1},H_{2}:L_{2}\to L_{2}
,
K_{u}:L_{2}\to L_{2}
. We claim that
K_{u}
is a contraction on
L_{2}
. Indeed, letting
x,\bar{x}\in L_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert{K_{u}(x)-K_{u}(\bar{x})}\right\rVert

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\lVert{u-H_{2}(H_{1}(x))-u+H_{2}(H_{1}(\bar{x}))}\right\rVert

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\lVert{H_{2}(H_{1}(\bar{x})-H_{2}(H_{1}(x))}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}\left\lVert{H_{2}(\bar{x})-H_{2}(x)}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\left\lVert{x-\bar{x}}\right\rVert.

Therefore, by the Banach fixed point theorem, there exists a unique solution to
e=K_{u}(e)
for
each
u\in L_{2}
. Furthermore, we have
y=H_{1}(e)
, so existence and uniqueness of

y
is guaranteed.
∎

II-B

Scaled Relative Graphs

The Scaled Relative Graph (SRG) is a graphical representation of the gain and phase
of an operator. Phase is given by the angle between two signals. For
u,y\in L_{2}
, this is defined as

\displaystyle\angle(u,y):=\arccos\frac{\operatorname{Re}\left\langle{u},{y}\right\rangle}{\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert\left\lVert{y}\right\rVert}\in[0,\pi].

We define the SRG for an arbitrary relation, allowing us to talk about the SRG of an
operator and its relational inverse on the same footing.
Let
R\subseteq L_{2}\times L_{2}
. We write
u\in R(y)
if
(u,y)\in R
. Given
u_{1},u_{2}\in L_{2}
,
u_{1}\neq u_{2}
, define the set of complex numbers
z_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})
by

\displaystyle z_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})\coloneqq

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{\left\lVert{y_{1}-y_{2}}\right\rVert}{\left\lVert{u_{1}-u_{2}}\right\rVert}e^{\pm j\angle(u_{1}-u_{2},y_{1}-y_{2})}\right.

\displaystyle\bigg|\;y_{1}\in R(u_{1}),y_{2}\in R(u_{2})\bigg\}.

If
u_{1}=u_{2}
and there are corresponding
outputs
y_{1}\neq y_{2}
, then

z_{R}(u_{1},u_{2})
is defined to be
\{\infty\}
. If
R
is single valued at
u_{1}
,

z_{R}(u_{1},u_{1})
is the empty set.
The
Scaled Relative Graph
(SRG) of
R
is then given by

\displaystyle\operatorname{SRG}_{\,}({R}):=\bigcup_{u_{1},u_{2}\in\,L_{2}}z_{R}(u_{1},u_{2}).

The SRG of an operator
H:L_{2}\to L_{2}
is defined to be the SRG of its relation.
We refer the reader to
[
1
]
for the relationship between the SRG,
the Nyquist diagram of a transfer function and the incremental disc of a static
nonlinearity, and to
[
16
]
for the relationship to the numerical range
of a linear operator.

The incremental gain of an operator (on its domain) is the maximum radius of its SRG. The proof is
immediate from the definition of the SRG.

Lemma 3
.

Given an operator
H:D\to L_{2}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{u_{1},u_{2}\in D}\frac{\left\lVert{H(u_{1})-H(u_{2})}\right\rVert}{\left\lVert{u_{1}-u_{2}}\right\rVert}=\sup_{z\in\operatorname{SRG}_{\,}({H})}|z|.

Given an interconnection of operators, the SRG of the interconnection can be
estimated from the SRGs of the individual operators. We recall a couple of necessary
results in the following lemma, and refer the reader to
[
7
]
for the full theory.

An SRG
\mathcal{G}
is said to satisfy the
chord property
if, for each
z\in\mathcal{G}
,
\lambda z+(1-\lambda)\bar{z}\in\mathcal{G}
for all
\lambda\in[0,1]
.

Lemma 4
.

Given
A,B\subseteq L_{2}\times L_{2}
, we have:

1.

\operatorname{SRG}_{\,}({A^{-1}})=\{z^{-1}\;|\;z\in\operatorname{SRG}_{\,}({A})\}
;.

2.

if
\bar{A}\supseteq\operatorname{SRG}_{\,}({A})
is any set satisfying the chord property, then

\operatorname{SRG}_{\,}({A+B})\subseteq\bar{A}+\operatorname{SRG}_{\,}({B})
.

Proof.

The property
1
is proved in
[
7
, Theorem 5]
. The
proof of Property
2
proceeds as follows. Let
(u_{1},y_{A}),(u_{2},z_{A})\in A,(u_{1},y_{B}),(u_{2},z_{B})\in B
. Then
(u_{1},y_{A}+y_{B}),(u_{2},z_{A}+z_{B})\in A+B
, and

\displaystyle w=\frac{\left\lVert{y_{A}+y_{B}-z_{A}-z_{B}}\right\rVert}{\left\lVert{u_{1}-u_{2}}\right\rVert}

\displaystyle\exp(j\angle(y_{A}+y_{B}-z_{A}-z_{B},u_{1}-u_{2}))\in\operatorname{SRG}_{\,}({A+B}),

\displaystyle w_{A}=\frac{\left\lVert{y_{A}-z_{A}}\right\rVert}{\left\lVert{u_{1}-u_{2}}\right\rVert}\exp(j\angle{y_{A}-z_{A},u_{1}-u_{2}})\in\operatorname{SRG}_{\,}({A}),

\displaystyle w_{B}=\frac{\left\lVert{y_{B}-z_{B}}\right\rVert}{\left\lVert{u_{1}-u_{2}}\right\rVert}\exp(j\angle{y_{B}-z_{B},u_{1}-u_{2}})\in\operatorname{SRG}_{\,}({B}).

Then, by direct calculation,
\operatorname{Re}(w)=\operatorname{Re}(w_{A})+\operatorname{Re}(w_{B})
and
\operatorname{Im}(w_{B})-\operatorname{Im}(w_{A})\leq\operatorname{Im}(w)\leq\operatorname{Im}(w_{B})+\operatorname{Im}(w_{A})
, that is,
w\in w_{B}+[w_{A},\overline{w_{A}}]
.
Since
[w_{A},\overline{w_{A}}]\subseteq\bar{A}
, the claim follows.
∎

III
Incremental homotopy

The following theorem gives a method for verifying finite incremental gain of a
feedback interconnection using a homotopy from a known incrementally bounded
operator. The proof proceeds by
using the incremental small gain theorem to show small perturbations in the feedback
preserve stability, and applies this idea inductively to scale the feedback from
0

to
1
.

Theorem 2

(Incremental homotopy)
.

Let
H_{1},H_{2}:L_{2}\to L_{2}
be operators such that

(i)

H_{1},H_{2}
have finite incremental gain;

(ii)

there exists
\gamma>0
such that, for all
\tau\in[0,1]
and all

u_{1},u_{2}\in\operatorname{dom}\left({[H_{1},\tau H_{2}]}\right)
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert{y_{1}-y_{2}}\right\rVert\leq\gamma\left\lVert{u_{1}-u_{2}}\right\rVert,

where
y_{i}=[H_{1},\tau H_{2}](u_{i}),i=1,2
.

Then
\operatorname{dom}\left({[H_{1},H_{2}]}\right)=L_{2}
and
[H_{1},H_{2}]
has an incremental
gain bound of
\gamma
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
be incremental gain bounds for
H_{1}
and
H_{2}

respectively. We begin by showing that there exists
\nu>0
such that
\operatorname{dom}\left({[H_{1},\nu H_{2}]}\right)=L_{2}
. Indeed, setting
\nu<1/(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})
, we
have that
\nu\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}<1
, so it follows from
Theorem
1
that
\operatorname{dom}\left({[H_{1},\nu H_{2}]}\right)=L_{2}
.

By assumption,
\gamma
is an incremental gain bound
for
[H_{1},\nu H_{2}]
. It then follows from Theorem
1
that,
for all
\tau\in[0,1/\gamma\gamma_{2})
,
\operatorname{dom}\left({[[H_{1},\nu H_{2}],\tau H_{2}]}\right)=L_{2}
. From Lemma
2
,
[[H_{1},\nu H_{2}],\tau H_{2}]=[H_{1},(\nu+\tau)H_{2}]
. Again, by assumption, this operator has an incremental
gain bound of
\gamma
.

Proceeding inductively, we have that
\operatorname{dom}\left({[H_{1},(\nu+k\tau)H_{2}]}\right)=L_{2}

for all
\tau\in[0,1/\gamma\gamma_{2})
and positive integers
k
such that

\nu+k\tau\leq 1
, so, in particular,
\operatorname{dom}\left({[H_{1},H_{2}]}\right)=L_{2}
. The
incremental gain bound of
\gamma
then follows from Condition (
(ii)
)
in the theorem statement.
∎

We note that (
(ii)
) implies single-valuedness of
[H_{1},\tau H_{2}]
for
all
\tau\in[0,1]
.

Theorem
2
allows incremental stability of a feedback
interconnection to be verified using only information about
L_{2}
signals, without
any reference to an extended space containing unbounded signals. This is useful
as it allows the use of operator theoretic tools to verify the incremental gain bound of
condition
(ii)
. In the following section, we show this may be done
graphically using the SRG, and in Section
V
we verify the incremental
gain bound using IQCs.

IV
SRG separation

Given an SRG
\mathcal{G}
, let
\overline{\mathcal{G}}
denote the smallest SRG
containing
\mathcal{G}
and satisfying the chord property. We begin with a
graphical condition which guarantees finite incremental gain of a feedback
interconnection, on its domain (which may not, in general, be all of
L_{2}
).
Given two regions
X_{1},X_{2}
in the extended complex plane, we let

\operatorname{dist}(X_{1},X_{2})
denote
\inf_{x_{1}\in X_{1},x_{2}\in X_{2}}|x_{1}-x_{2}|
.

Lemma 5
.

Let
H_{1},H_{2}
be operators, and suppose there exists
\gamma>0
such that

\operatorname{dist}(\operatorname{SRG}_{\,}({H_{1}})^{-1},-\overline{\operatorname{SRG}_{\,}({H_{2}})})\geq 1/\gamma
. Then, for
any
u_{i}\in\operatorname{dom}\left({[H_{1},H_{2}]}\right)
and
y_{i}=(H_{1}^{-1}+\tau H_{2})^{-1}(u_{i})
, we have

\left\lVert{y_{1}-y_{2}}\right\rVert\leq\gamma\left\lVert{u_{1}-u_{2}}\right\rVert
.

Proof.

Since
\overline{\operatorname{SRG}_{\,}({H_{2}})^{-1}}
satisfies the chord property by construction,
we can apply the SRG sum rule. The result then follows from
Lemmas
3
and
4
and the following
geometry.

∎

The separation condition of Lemma
5
guarantees that an
operator has an incremental bound on its domain. Guaranteeing that this domain is,
in fact,
L_{2}
, requires a stronger separation property: the SRGs
must remain separated as the feedback is gradually increased from zero. We formalize
this separation property as follows.

Definition 1
.

Two operators
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are said to have
strictly separated SRGs with
margin
r_{\min}>0
if

\operatorname{dist}(\operatorname{SRG}_{\,}({H_{1}})^{-1},-\tau\overline{\operatorname{SRG}_{\,}({H_{2}})})\geq r_{\min}
for all
\tau\in(0,1]
.

Corollary 1
.

Suppose
H_{1},H_{2}:L_{2}\to L_{2}
have bounded incremental gain and strictly separated SRGs. Then the feedback
interconnection
[H_{1},H_{2}]
maps
L_{2}
to
L_{2}
and has bounded incremental
gain.

Proof.

It follows from strict separation of the SRGs of
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
, and
Lemma
5
, that
1/r_{\min}
is an incremental gain bound
for
[H_{1},\nu H_{2}]
. The result then follows from
Theorem
2
.
∎

Corollary
1
corrects the assumptions of
[
1
, Theorem
2, Corollary 1, Corollary 2]
in two ways: in

[
1
]
, only
H_{1}
was was assumed to be incrementally bounded, and
the separation of the SRGs was not required to be strict.
Strict separation must also be assumed in
[
1
, Theorem 1]
. The additional assumptions are
satisfied in all the examples of
[
1
]
. Furthermore, the recent
result of
[
8
, Theorem 1]
removes these two assumptions, but
at the price of a weaker form of incremental stability.
The following example illustrates why strict separation of the SRGs
is required to guarantee incremental boundedness.

Example 1
.

Consider the operator
N:L_{2}\to L_{2}
given by

\displaystyle(Nu)(t)=\phi(u(t))

where
\phi(x)=-\arctan(x)
.

\phi
is
1
-Lipschitz continuous, so
N
has an incremental gain bound of

1
. It follows that its SRG is contained in the closed unit disc. We now
observe the following: the SRG of
N
is, in fact, contained in the open unit
disc
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\;|\;|z|<1\}
, and therefore does not contain
the point
-1
. However, the operator
formed by putting
N
in unity gain negative feedback has infinite incremental
gain.

To see that
\operatorname{SRG}_{\,}({N})\subseteq\mathbb{D}
, suppose that there exist
u_{1},u_{2}\in L_{2}
,
u_{1}\neq u_{2}
, such that

\displaystyle\frac{\left\lVert{N(u_{1})-N(u_{2})}\right\rVert}{\left\lVert{u_{1}-u_{2}}\right\rVert}=1.

(4)

Since
u_{1}\neq u_{2}
, the set
E=\{t\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;u_{1}(t)\neq u_{2}(t)\}
has positive
measure. Equation (
4
) implies

\displaystyle\int_{E}|u_{1}(t)-u_{2}(t)|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}{t}=\int_{E}|\phi(u_{1}(t))-\phi(u_{2}(t))|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{E}\left|\frac{\phi(u_{1}(t))-\phi(u_{2}(t))}{u_{1}(t)-u_{2}(t)}\right|^{2}|u_{1}(t)-u_{2}(t)|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}{t},

or equivalently

\displaystyle\int_{E}\left(1-\left|\frac{\phi(u_{1}(t))-\phi(u_{2}(t))}{u_{1}(t)-u_{2}(t)}\right|^{2}\right)|u_{1}(t)-u_{2}(t)|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}{t}=0.

Hence we must have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\phi(u_{1}(t))-\phi(u_{2}(t))}{u_{1}(t)-u_{2}(t)}\right|=1

for almost all
t\in E
. But this is impossible, since

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\phi(x)-\phi(y)}{x-y}\right|<1

for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
with
x\neq y
. Thus indeed
\operatorname{SRG}_{\,}({N})\subseteq\mathbb{D}
.

We now show the
(N^{-1}+I)^{-1}
does not have a finite incremental gain. Indeed, on its
domain, we have

\displaystyle(N^{-1}+I)^{-1}(u)(t)=\psi^{-1}(u(t)),

where
\psi(x)=x-\tan(x)
. However, the function
\psi^{-1}
is not
Lipschitz continuous at
x=0
, since
\psi^{\prime}(0)=0
so

(\psi^{-1})^{\prime}(0)
does not exist. The ratio

\displaystyle\frac{\left\lVert{(N^{-1}+I)^{-1}(u_{1})-(N^{-1}+I)^{-1}(u_{2})}\right\rVert}{\left\lVert{u_{1}-u_{2}}\right\rVert}

can be made arbitrarily large, for example by taking
u_{1}(t)=au(t)
,
u_{2}(t)=u(t)
for
a,b\neq 0
small
enough, where

\begin{gathered}u(t):=\begin{cases}1&t\in[0,1]\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}\\
\end{gathered}

V
Incremental IQCs

In this section, we give a second method of verifying condition
(ii)
of
Theorem
2
: satisfaction of an incremental IQC. This
gives a incremental version of the classical IQC stability theorem

[
3
, Theorem 1]
. Our theorem is closely related to
[
9
, Theorem
7.40]
, but does not rely on an extended space or assumptions of
causality. The developments closely follow the non-incremental theory of

[
4
,
14
]
.

Corollary 2
.

Let
H_{1}:L_{2}\to L_{2}
be a bounded LTI operator, and
H_{2}:L_{2}\to L_{2}
be
incrementally bounded. Let
\Pi:j\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be a Hermitian-valued
function with
L_{\infty}
entries. Given
y_{1},y_{2}\in L_{2}
, define
\Delta\hat{y}(j\omega):=\hat{y}_{1}(j\omega)-\hat{y}_{2}(j\omega)
and
\Delta\hat{H}_{2}(y)(j\omega):=\hat{H}_{2}(y_{1})(j\omega)-\tau\hat{H}_{2}(y_{2})(j\omega)
. Suppose that, for every

\tau\in[0,1]
and every
y_{1},y_{2}\in L_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle{\footnotesize\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\begin{pmatrix}\Delta\hat{y}(j\omega)\\
\tau\Delta\hat{H}_{2}(y)(j\omega)\end{pmatrix}^{*}\Pi(j\omega)\begin{pmatrix}\Delta\hat{y}(j\omega)\\
\tau\Delta\hat{H}_{2}(y)(j\omega)\end{pmatrix}\;\mathrm{d}{\omega}\geq 0,}

(5)

and that there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\hat{H_{1}}(j\omega)\\
I\end{pmatrix}^{*}\Pi(j\omega)\begin{pmatrix}\hat{H_{1}}(j\omega)\\
I\end{pmatrix}\leq-\varepsilon I

(6)

for all
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
. Then
[H_{1},H_{2}]
maps
L_{2}
to
L_{2}
and has bounded
incremental gain.

In contrast to
[
3
, Theorem 1]
, Corollary
2
does not require any well-posedness assumptions along
the homotopy path.
Before giving the proof of Corollary
2
, we make a technical definition
and prove two lemmas, on which the proof relies.

Definition 2
.

A functional
\sigma:L_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be
quadratically continuous

if, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\sigma(y)\leq\sigma(x)+\varepsilon\left\lVert{x}\right\rVert^{2}+C\left\lVert{x-y}\right\rVert^{2}

for all
x,y\in L_{2}
.

The class of quadratically continuous functionals that we will use are characterized
in the following lemma, which is a special case of
[
14
, Lem. 3.18]
.

Lemma 6
.

Let
\Pi:j\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be a Hermitian-valued function with
L_{\infty}

entries. Then the functional
\sigma:L_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\displaystyle\sigma(x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left\langle{x},{x}\right\rangle_{\Pi}

\displaystyle\left\langle{x},{y}\right\rangle_{\Pi}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\hat{x}^{*}(j\omega)\Pi(j\omega)\hat{y}(j\omega)\;\mathrm{d}{\omega}

is quadratically continuous.

Proof.

Note that
\left\langle{\cdot},{\cdot}\right\rangle_{\Pi}
defines a bounded Hermitian form.

\displaystyle\sigma(x)-\sigma(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle{y},{y}\right\rangle_{\Pi}-\left\langle{x},{x}\right\rangle_{\Pi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle{y},{y}\right\rangle_{\Pi}-\left\langle{x},{y}\right\rangle_{\Pi}+\left\langle{x},{y}\right\rangle_{\Pi}-\left\langle{x},{x}\right\rangle_{\Pi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle{y-x},{y}\right\rangle_{\Pi}-\left\langle{x},{y-x}\right\rangle_{\Pi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle{y-x},{y}\right\rangle_{\Pi}-\left\langle{y-x},{x}\right\rangle_{\Pi}

\displaystyle+\left\langle{y-x},{y}\right\rangle_{\Pi}+\left\langle{x},{y-x}\right\rangle_{\Pi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle{y-x},{y-x}\right\rangle_{\Pi}+\left\langle{y-x},{x}\right\rangle_{\Pi}+\left\langle{x},{y-x}\right\rangle_{\Pi}.

Since
\left\langle{\cdot},{\cdot}\right\rangle_{\Pi}
is bounded, there exists
M\geq 0
such that
\left\langle{x},{y}\right\rangle\leq M\left\lVert{x}\right\rVert\left\lVert{y}\right\rVert
for all
x,y\in L_{2}
. We therefore have

\displaystyle\sigma(x)-\sigma(y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M\left\lVert{x-y}\right\rVert^{2}+2M\left\lVert{x}\right\rVert\left\lVert{x-y}\right\rVert.

(7)

Furthermore, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left(M\left\lVert{x-y}\right\rVert-\varepsilon\left\lVert{x}\right\rVert\right)^{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon}M^{2}\left\lVert{x-y}\right\rVert^{2}-2M\left\lVert{x}\right\rVert\left\lVert{x-y}\right\rVert+\varepsilon\left\lVert{x}\right\rVert^{2}\geq 0

\displaystyle\text{so}\quad 2M\left\lVert{x}\right\rVert\left\lVert{x-y}\right\rVert\geq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}M^{2}\left\lVert{x-y}\right\rVert^{2}+\varepsilon\left\lVert{x}\right\rVert^{2}.

Combining with (
7
), we have

\displaystyle\sigma{x}-\sigma{y}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(M+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}M^{2}\right)\left\lVert{x-y}\right\rVert^{2}+\varepsilon\left\lVert{x}\right\rVert^{2}.

∎

We now show that a quadratically continuous functional can be used to verify incremental
boundedness.

Lemma 7
.

Consider
H_{1},H_{2}:L_{2}\to L_{2}
and assume
H_{1}
has an incremental gain bound
of
\lambda
. Let
\sigma:L_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be quadratically continuous with constant
C
. For
u_{i}\in L_{2}
, let
y_{i}\in[H_{1},H_{2}](u_{i})
and
e_{i}
denote
u_{i}-H_{2}(y_{i})
.
Suppose that, for all
u_{1},u_{2}\in\operatorname{dom}\left({[H_{1},H_{2}]}\right)
,
we have

\displaystyle\sigma(h_{1})\leq-2\varepsilon\left\lVert{h_{1}}\right\rVert^{2}

(8)

\displaystyle\sigma(h_{2})\geq 0,

(9)

where
h_{1}:=(y_{1}-y_{2},e_{1}-e_{2})
,
h_{2}:=(y_{1}-y_{2},H_{2}(y_{1})-H_{2}(y_{2}))
. Then there exists
\lambda>0
such that, for
all
u_{1},u_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert{y_{1}-y_{2}}\right\rVert\leq\sqrt{\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{\lambda^{2}}{1+\lambda^{2}}\right)}\left\lVert{u_{1}-u_{2}}\right\rVert.

Proof.

Given two signals
x_{1},x_{2}
, let
\Delta x:=x_{1}-x_{2}
.
We have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sigma(h_{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sigma(h_{1})+\varepsilon\left\lVert{h_{1}}\right\rVert^{2}+C\left\lVert{h_{2}-h_{1}}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\left\lVert{h_{1}}\right\rVert^{2}+C\left\lVert{h_{2}-h_{1}}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\varepsilon(\left\lVert{\Delta y}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert{\Delta e}\right\rVert^{2})+C(\left\lVert{\Delta H_{2}(y)-\Delta e}\right\rVert^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\varepsilon(\left\lVert{\Delta y}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert{\Delta e}\right\rVert^{2})+C\left\lVert{\Delta u}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\left(1+\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\right)\left\lVert{\Delta y}\right\rVert+C\left\lVert{\Delta u}\right\rVert^{2},

from which the result follows.
∎

Proof of Corollary
2
.

Define
\left\langle{\cdot},{\cdot}\right\rangle_{\Pi}
as in Lemma
6
. It
follows from that lemma that
\sigma(x):=\left\langle{x},{x}\right\rangle_{\Pi}
is quadratically
continuous. Equation (
5
) gives condition (
9
)
of Lemma
7
. We now show that Equation (
6
)
gives condition (
8
). Indeed, pre- and post-multiplying with

\hat{u}^{*}(j\omega)
and
\hat{u}(j\omega)
, respectively, and integrating over
\omega
, gives

\displaystyle{\small\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{H_{1}}(j\omega)\hat{u}(j\omega)\\
\hat{u}(j\omega)\end{pmatrix}^{*}\Pi(\omega)\begin{pmatrix}\hat{H_{1}}(j\omega)\hat{u}(j\omega)\\
\hat{u}(j\omega)\end{pmatrix}\;\mathrm{d}{\omega}\leq-\varepsilon\left\lVert{\hat{u}}\right\rVert^{2}.}

Now let
\alpha>0
be a gain bound for
H_{1}
. Then we can write
-\varepsilon\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert^{2}\leq-2\bar{\varepsilon}(\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert{H_{1}(u)}\right\rVert^{2})
for some

\bar{\varepsilon}\leq\varepsilon/(2(1+\alpha))
. Since
H_{1}
is LTI, this
non-incremental condition is then equivalent to the incremental condition
(
8
).

We finally note that the gain bound given by Lemma
7
depends
only on
\sigma
and the incremental gain of
H_{1}
, and not on
\tau
. The

2
.
∎

VI
Relaxing the assumption of incremental boundedness

Much of the existing stability literature focuses on the verification of
non-incremental stability, and assumes weaker non-incremental boundedness properties
for the components. In exchange, systems must be assumed to be well-posed, in the
sense of causality and existence of solutions in an appropriate ambient space,
containing
L_{2}
but allowing unbounded signals. In this section, we show that incremental stability can also be
verified under these weaker assumptions, subject to the same well-posedness
assumptions as in a typical non-incremental analysis. We begin by introducing the extended
L_{2}
space.

Given
T>0
, denote by
P_{T}:\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}
the truncation operator

\displaystyle P_{T}(u)(t):=\begin{cases}u(t)&t<T\\
0&t\geq T.\end{cases}

The
extended
L_{2}
space
,
L_{2e}
, is defined as the subset of

\mathcal{F}
such that
P_{T}u\in L_{2}
for all
T
. An operator
H:L_{2}\to L_{2}
,
or
H_{e}:L_{2e}\to L_{2e}
, is said to be
causal
if
P_{T}HP_{T}=P_{T}H

for all
T>0
.

A negative feedback interconnection is said to be

well-posed
if, for any
u\in L_{2e}
, there
exist unique
e,y\in L_{2e}
satisfying (
1
)–(
2
).

Theorem 3
.

Suppose

(i)

H_{1},H_{2}:L_{2}\to L_{2}
have finite gain with zero offset
and are causal;

(ii)

[H_{1},\tau H_{2}]

is well-posed and causal for all
\tau\in(0,1]
;

(iii)

there exists
\gamma>0
such that, for all
\tau\in[0,1]
and all

u_{1},u_{2}\in\operatorname{dom}\left({[H_{1},\tau H_{2}]}\right)
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert{y_{1}-y_{2}}\right\rVert\leq\gamma\left\lVert{u_{1}-u_{2}}\right\rVert,

where
y_{i}=[H_{1},\tau H_{2}](u_{i}),i=1,2
.

Then
[H_{1},H_{2}]
maps

L_{2}
to
L_{2}
and has finite incremental gain.

This theorem provides a middle ground between the incremental
Theorem
2
and classical homotopy results such as

[
3
, Theorem 1]
.

Proof of Theorem

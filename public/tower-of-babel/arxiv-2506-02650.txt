Weighted L 2 L^{2} estimates with applications to L p L^{p} problems
Shukun Wu

Abstract
. We establish some weighted L 2 L^{2} estimates for the Fourier extension operator in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} , and discuss several applications to L p L^{p} problems. These include estimates for the maximal Schrödinger operator and the maximal extension operator, decay of circular L p L^{p} -means of Fourier transform of fractal measures, and an L p L^{p} analogue of the Mizohata–Takeuchi conjecture.



1.
Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to prove certain weighted
L^{2}
estimates for the Fourier extension operator and to present a couple of applications to problems related to
L^{p}
estimates.
Previously, these problems shared similar obstructions arising from limitations of the bilinear restriction theorem.

1.1.
Overview

We begin with a gentle introduction to our weighted
L^{2}
estimate.
Let
S
be a compact curve in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with non-zero curvature.
Typical examples are the unit circle and the truncated parabola
\{(\xi,\xi^{2}):|\xi|\leq 1\}
.
For this curve
S
, let
\sigma_{S}
be the surface measure on
S
, and we define the extension operator
E_{S}:S\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
as

(1.1)

E_{S}f(x)=\int e^{ix\cdot\xi}f(\xi)d\sigma_{S}(\xi).

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a set.
We are interested in controlling
\|E_{S}f\|_{L^{2}(X)}
in terms of
\|f\|_{2}
and the information encoded in the weight
X
.
For instance, when considering
L^{p}
inequality,
X
often represents an upper level set, and an estimate of the form

(1.2)

\|E_{S}f\|_{L^{2}(X)}\lesssim|X|^{\alpha}\cdot\|f\|_{2}

for some
\alpha>0
would be desirable.
This is because, by Hölder’s inequality, such an estimate is essentially equivalent to an
(L^{2},L^{p})
estimate

(1.3)

\|E_{S}f\|_{p}\lesssim\|f\|_{2},

where
p
is chosen so that
1/2-1/p=\alpha
.
In other words, questions about
L^{p}
estimates can often be reduced to the study of weighted
L^{2}
estimates.

The classical Stein-Tomas estimate shows that (
1.2
) can only be true if
\alpha\geq 1/3
.
Via the bilinear restriction theorem, this lower bound can be improved to
\alpha\geq 1/4
if
E_{S}
is replaced by its bilinear analog
\text{Bil}E_{S}
.
That is, we have

(1.4)

\|{\rm Bil}E_{S}f\|_{L^{2}(X)}\lesssim|X|^{1/4}\cdot\|f\|_{2},

which we also refer to as the bilinear restriction estimate.

The bilinear restriction theorem has proven useful in various problems.
However, when it comes to problems related to weighted
L^{p}
estimates (e.g., Proposition
3.2
), it is often inefficient.
This is because (
1.4
) is sharp only when
X
is a union of
r
-balls for some
r
much larger than
1
, exhibiting a full-dimensional structure (since each
r
-ball has full dimensions), whereas in weighted
L^{p}
problems, the weight is usually a union of unit balls obeying certain fractal conditions.

In this paper, we prove a weighted
L^{2}
estimate that improves (
1.4
) when
X
is a 1-dimensional fractal set, and when the bilinear operator
\text{Bil}E_{S}
is replaced by a slightly smaller operator, the “broad” operator
{\rm{Br}}_{A}E_{S}
.
Weighted
L^{2}
estimates involving
{\rm{Br}}_{A}E_{S}
were investigated in
[
LW24
]
as a tool for the study of the convergence of the Bochner-Riesz means.
The broad operator
{\rm{Br}}_{A}E_{S}
was introduced by Guth in
[
Gut16
]
.
Below we recall its definition and a notion for fractal sets.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
K\geq 1
and
A\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
\Sigma=\{\sigma\}
be a set of finite-overlapping
K^{-1}
-arcs that covers
S
.
Let
f_{\sigma}=f{\bf 1}_{\sigma}
.
For any
f:S\to\mathbb{C}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, define

(1.5)

{\rm{Br}}_{A}E_{S}f(x):=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Sigma^{\prime}\subset\Sigma,\\
\#\Sigma^{\prime}=A\end{subarray}}\min_{\sigma\in\Sigma^{\prime}}|E_{S}f_{\sigma}(x)|.

Definition 1.2

(Katz-Tao
(\delta,s,C)
-set)
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
be a small number.
For
s\in(0,n]
, a finite set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called a
Katz-Tao
(\delta,s,C)
-set
(or simply a
Katz-Tao
(\delta,s)
-set
if
C
is not important in the context) if

(1.6)

\#(E\cap B(x,r))\leq C(r/\delta)^{s},\hskip 8.5359pt\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,r\in[\delta,1].

A similar definition applies when
E
is a union of
\delta
-balls.

Now we can state our main theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
R\geq 1
,
\varepsilon>0
, and
K\leq R^{\varepsilon^{4}}
.
Let
X\subset B_{R}
be a union of unit balls such that the
R^{-1}
-dilate of
X
is a Katz-Tao
(R^{-1},1)
-set.
Let
\varepsilon^{\prime}\in(0,\log K/\log R)
.
Then when
A\geq R^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}
, we have

(1.7)

\|{\rm{Br}}_{A}E_{S}f\|_{L^{2}(X)}\leq C_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}R^{\varepsilon}|X|^{2/9}\cdot\|f\|_{2}.

The proof of Theorem
1.3
uses the two-ends Furstenberg estimate established in
[
WW24
]
.
It also uses induction on scales and the refined decoupling theorem.

It is appealing to determine the smallest
\alpha>0
such that

(1.8)

\|{\rm{Br}}_{A}E_{S}f\|_{L^{2}(X)}\lessapprox|X|^{\alpha}\cdot\|f\|_{2}

holds for all weights
X
whose
R^{-1}
-dilate is a Katz-Tao
(R^{-1},1)
-set.
The following example demonstrates that
\alpha
cannot be smaller than
1/6
.
Let
S=\{(\xi,\xi^{2}):\xi\in[-1,1]\}
be the truncated parabola.
Assume that
R^{1/2}
is an integer.
Let
f
be the characteristic function of the
(100R)^{-1}
-neighborhood of
R^{-1/2}\mathbb{Z}\cap[-1,1]
.
By the uncertainty principle, when
(x_{1},x_{2})\in B_{R}
, we have

(1.9)

|E_{S}f(x_{1},x_{2})|\sim R^{-1}\Big|\sum_{k=1}^{R^{1/2}}e\big(x_{1}\frac{k}{R^{1/2}}+x_{2}\frac{k^{2}}{R}\big)\Big|,

Let
X
be the
10^{-2}
-neighborhood of the set

(1.10)

\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in B_{R}:x_{1}=R^{1/2}(\frac{a}{q}+\mathbb{Z}),\,x_{2}=R\frac{b}{q},\,\text{ $a,b,q\sim R^{1/6}$ are integers}\},

so
|X|\sim R
.
Also, by evaluating the Gauss sum in (
1.9
), we have
{\rm{Br}}_{A}E_{S}f(x)\sim|E_{S}f(x)|\gtrsim R^{-7/12}
for all
x\in X
.
Thus,
\|{\rm{Br}}_{A}E_{S}f\|_{L^{2}(X)}\sim R^{-7/12}R^{1/2}\sim|X|^{1/6}\|f\|_{2}
, which shows
\alpha\geq 1/6
.

1.2.
Applications

We give three applications to our weighted
L^{2}
estimate (
1.7
), with the help of the standard broad-narrow analysis.

1.2.1.
L^{p}
circular decay of Fourier transform of fractal measures

Let
\mu
be a probability Frostman measure supported in
[0,1]^{2}
such that
\mu(B_{r})\lesssim r
for all
B_{r}\subset[0,1]^{2}
.
Let
\sigma
be the surface measure on the unit circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}
.
Wolff
[
Wol99
]
studied the decay of the circular
L^{2}
-means of
\widehat{\mu}
and prove that, for any such
1
-dimensional probability Frostman measure, the following is true for all
\varepsilon>0
and
R\geq 1
:

(1.11)

\Big(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\widehat{\mu}(R\xi)|^{2}d\sigma(\xi)\Big)^{1/2}\leq C_{\varepsilon}R^{\varepsilon}R^{-1/4}

In the same paper, he posted a similar question regarding the circular
L^{p}
-means (see also the discussion in
[
Gut25
, Section 10.2]
), which can be formulated using the following definition.

Definition 1.4
.

Define
\sigma_{p}(1)
be the supremum of all
\sigma
such that for all probability Frostman measure
\mu
supported in
[0,1]^{2}
with
\mu(B_{r})\lesssim r
for all
B_{r}\subset[0,1]^{2}
, it holds

(1.12)

\Big(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\widehat{\mu}(R\xi)|^{p}d\sigma(\xi)\Big)^{1/p}\leq C_{p}R^{-\sigma}.

Wolff’s question is about identifying
\sigma_{p}(1)
.
In particular, (
1.11
) is equivalent to
\sigma_{2}(1)\geq 1/4
.
As an application of Theorem
1.3
, we prove the following result, generalizing Wolff’s estimate (
1.11
) to circular
L^{p}
-means.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\mu
be a probability Frostman measure supported in
[0,1]^{2}
such that
\mu(B_{r})\lesssim r
for all
B_{r}\subset[0,1]^{2}
.
Then
\sigma_{p}(1)\geq 1/2p
for all
p\in[9/5,2]
.

The lower bound
\sigma\geq 1/2p
is sharp in (
1.12
) for fixed
R
.
That is, for fixed
R
, there exists a measure
\mu
such that the L.H.S. of (
1.12
) is
\gtrsim R^{-1/2p}
.
In fact, we can just take
d\mu(x)=Re^{iRx_{2}}\varphi(x_{1})\varphi(Rx_{2})dx_{1}dx_{2}
, where
\varphi
is a bump function on
[-1,1]
.
In this case,
\widehat{\mu}
is essentially supported on a horizontal
R\times 1
-rectangle with height
R
, so
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\widehat{\mu}(R\xi)|^{p}d\sigma(\xi)\sim R^{-1/2}
, yielding
(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\widehat{\mu}(R\xi)|^{p}d\sigma(\xi))^{1/p}\gtrsim R^{-1/2p}
.

Wolff’s estimate (
1.11
) is connected to Falconer distance set problem.
For this connection and higher dimensional generalizations of (
1.11
), we refer to
[
DZ19
]
.

1.2.2.
Estimates for the maximal Schrödinger operator and the maximal extension operator

Let
I
be a compact interval, and let

(1.13)

e^{it\Delta}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i(x\xi+t\xi^{2})}\widehat{f}(\xi)d\xi

be the solution of the Schrödinger equation on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times I

(1.14)

iu_{t}=\Delta u,\hskip 14.22636ptu(\cdot,0)=f.

We are interested in the maximal Schrödinger operator
\sup_{t\in I}|e^{it\Delta}f(x)|
, which has been studied to prove pointwise convergence of
e^{it\Delta}f
as
t\to 0
(See
[
DK82
,
DGL17
,
DZ19
]
, for example).
In 1+1 dimensions with initial date in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
, this problem was answered in
[
DK82
]
.
From a modern perspective, it is a corollary of the bilinear restriction estimate (
1.4
) (take
q=4,\,p=2
in Proposition
3.4
).
In fact, the pointwise convergence problem is often addressed by establishing the
L^{p}
-boundedness of
\sup_{t\in I}|e^{it\Delta}f(x)|
.

Regarding the
L^{p}
behavior of
\sup_{t\in I}|e^{it\Delta}f(x)|
in the plane, the following conjecture was raised in
[
LRS13
]
[Conjecture 1.6].

Conjecture 1.6
.

Let
I
be a compact interval.
Then for
q>3
, the following is true for
\alpha=1-2/q
.

(1.15)

\|e^{it\Delta}f\|_{L^{q}_{x}L^{\infty}_{t}(\mathbb{R}\times I)}\leq C_{p,\alpha}\|f\|_{B^{q}_{\alpha,q}(\mathbb{R})}.

Here,
\|f\|_{L^{p}_{x}L^{q}_{t}}=\|\int|f(\cdot,t)|^{q}dt)^{1/q}\|_{p}
, and
B^{p}_{\alpha,q}
is the Besov space with

(1.16)

\|f\|_{B^{p}_{\alpha,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\big(\sum_{k\geq 0}2^{k\alpha q}\|P_{k}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{q}\big)^{1/q},

where
P_{k}
is the Fourier projection to the annulus
\{|\xi|\sim 2^{k}\}
.
Note that
B^{p}_{\alpha,p}
agrees with the Sobolev-Slobodecki space
W^{\alpha,p}
when
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

In the restricted range
q\in(3,4]
, the following (up to endpoint) stronger conjecture could also be true.

Conjecture 1.7
.

Let
I
be a compact interval.
Then for
q\in(3,4]
and
2/q+1/p<1
, the following is true for
\alpha=1-1/p-1/q
.

(1.17)

\|e^{it\Delta}f\|_{L^{q}_{x}L^{\infty}_{t}(\mathbb{R}\times I)}\leq C_{p,\alpha}\|f\|_{B^{p}_{\alpha,q}(\mathbb{R})}.

Via the broad-narrow analysis, the case
q=4
in both Conjectures
1.6
and
1.7
follows from the bilinear restriction estimate (
1.4
).
As an application of Theorem
1.3
, we verify both conjectures in a wider range.

Theorem 1.8
.

Conjectures
1.6
and
1.7
are true when
q>18/5
.

As an
L^{2}
method, Theorem
1.3
is robust and applies equally well to the sister problem of Conjecture
1.7
: the
L^{p}
-boundedness of the maximal extension operator.
This problem was mentioned in
[
Wu21
, (1.17)]
.
In two dimensions, the Knapp example suggests the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.9
.

For
q\in(3,4]
and
2/q+1/p<1
, we have

(1.18)

\|E_{S}f\|_{L^{q}_{x_{1}}L^{\infty}_{x_{2}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{p}.

Similar to Conjectures
1.6
and
1.7
, the bilinear restriction theorem shows that (
1.18
) is true when
p=4
.
As an application of Theorem
1.3
, we have

Theorem 1.10
.

Conjecture
1.18
is true when
q>18/5
.

1.2.3.
L^{p}
variant of the Mizohata–Takeuchi conjecture

The Mizohata–Takeuchi conjecture asks

Conjecture 1.11
.

For a set
X\subset B_{R}
, define a weight

(1.19)

w_{R}(X)=\sup_{T:\text{ a $1\times R$-tube}}|T\cap X|.

Then for all
\varepsilon>0
,

(1.20)

\|Ef\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}\leq C_{\varepsilon}R^{\varepsilon}w_{R}(X)\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

In order to answer Conjecture
1.11
, one likely needs to explore the algebraic properties of the weight
X
, which seems quite challenging.
However, this difficulty disappears when the
L^{2}
norm
\|Ef\|_{L^{2}(X)}
is replaced by a smaller quantity
\|Ef\|_{L^{p}(X)}
for some
p>2
.
The case
p=4
for (
1.21
) was considered by Carbery, Iliopoulou, and Shayya in
[
Obe23
]
.

As an application of Theorem
1.3
, we show the following
L^{p}
variant of (
1.20
), extending the result by Carbery, Iliopoulou, and Shayya.

Theorem 1.12
.

For all
\varepsilon>0
and
p\geq 18/5
,

(1.21)

\|Ef\|_{L^{p}(X)}^{p}\leq C_{\varepsilon}R^{\varepsilon}w_{R}(X)\|f\|_{2}^{p}.

Wave packet decomposition and lemmas

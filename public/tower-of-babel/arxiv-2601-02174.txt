Lamperti Operators, Dilation Theory, and Applications in Noncommutative Ergodic Theory
Guixiang Hong, Wei Liu, Samya Kumar Ray, Bang Xu

Abstract
. In this paper, we develop a novel framework for quantitative mean ergodic theorems in the noncommutative setting, with a focus on actions of amenable groups and semigroups. We prove square function inequalities for ergodic averages arising from actions of groups of polynomial volume growth on a fixed noncommutative L p L_{p} -space for 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty . To achieve this, we establish two endpoint estimates for a noncommutative square function on non-homogeneous space. Our approach relies on semi-commutative non-homogeneous harmonic analysis, including the non-doubling Calderón-Zygmund arguments for non-smooth kernels and BMO \mathrm{BMO} space theory, operator-valued inequalities related to balls and cubes in groups equipped with non-doubling measures, and a noncommutative generalization of the classical transference method for amenable group actions. As an application, we establish a quantitative ergodic theorem for the ergodic averages associated with the positive power of modulus representation arising from a Lamperti representation on noncommutative L p L_{p} -spaces, extending some results in [ 81 ] . To obtain quantitative ergodic theorem for semigroups of operators, in this paper, we address the open question of extending dilation theorem of Fackler-Glück from single operators to commuting tuples on Banach spaces including noncommutative L p L_{p} -spaces. Indeed our approach provides genuine joint N N -dilations for commuting families, unifying and extending the classical dilation theorems of Sz.-Nagy–Foiaş and Akçoglu–Sucheston for a natural class of commuting tuple of contractions extending the abstract dilation theorem of of Fackler-Glück for commuting tuple of contractions. This enables us to obtain a quantitative ergodic theorem for a large class of semigroups of operators on ℝ + d \mathbb{R}^{d}_{+} .

Introduction

The study of ergodic theory for group actions has followed a remarkable trajectory since its inception in the 1930s. The foundational works of Birkhoff
[
9

85

15

Motivated by Calderón’s result, ergodic theorems for various group actions have been extensively investigated. A notable example is the work of Breuillard
[
11

[

established the pointwise ergodic theorem for averages along tempered Følner sequences. We refer the reader to the survey works

2.
Preliminaries and some technical lemmas

2.1.
Noncommutative
L_{p}
-spaces

Throughout this paper, let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra equipped with a normal semifinite faithful (
n.s.f.
) trace
\tau
and
\mathcal{M}_{+}
be the positive part of
\mathcal{M}
. Let
\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{M}}
denote the unit element in
{\mathcal{M}}
. Given
x\in\mathcal{M}_{+}
, the support of
x
, denoted by
s(x)
, is defined to be the smallest projection
a
in
\mathcal{M}
such that
xa=x=ax
. We denote by
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{M}_{+}}
the set of all
x\in\mathcal{M}_{+}
such that
\tau(s(x))<\infty
, and by
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{M}}
the linear span of
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{M}_{+}}
. Then
\mathcal{S}_{{\mathcal{M}}}
is a
w^{*}
-dense
\ast
-subalgebra of
{\mathcal{M}}
. Let
1\leq p<\infty
, the noncommutative
L_{p}
-space
L_{p}({\mathcal{M}},\tau)
is defined by the completion of
\mathcal{S}_{{\mathcal{M}}}
with respect to the norm

\|x\|_{p}=\tau(|x|^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}},~\qquad~\mbox{where}~|x|=(x^{*}x)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For
p=\infty
, we set
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M},\tau)=\mathcal{M}
equipped with the operator norm.

Let
L_{0}({\mathcal{M}})
be the set of the
\ast
-algebra of
\tau
-measurable
operators. The noncommutative weak
L_{p}
-space
L_{p,\infty}(\mathcal{M})
is defined as the set of all
x\in L_{0}(\mathcal{M})
equipped the following finite quasi-norm

\|x\|_{p,\infty}=\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda\tau(\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(|x|))^{\frac{1}{p}}.

In particular, for weak
L_{1}
-space, one has the basic fact (cf.
[
79
]
): for any
x_{1},x_{2}\in L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{M})
and
\lambda>0
,

(2.1)

\tau(\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(|x_{1}+x_{2}|))\leq\tau(\chi_{(\lambda/2,\infty)}(|x_{1}|))+\tau(\chi_{(\lambda/2,\infty)}(|x_{2}|)).

For more explanation of noncommutative
L_{p}
-spaces and weak
L_{p}
-spaces we refer to
[
73
,
23
]
.

2.2.
Noncommutative Hilbert-valued
L_{p}
-spaces

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
(x_{n})
be a finite sequence in
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
. Define

\|(x_{n})\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{r})}=\Big\|\Big(\sum_{n}|x^{\ast}_{n}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{p},\quad\|(x_{n})\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}=\Big\|\Big(\sum_{n}|x_{n}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{p}.

Let
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{r})
(resp.
{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}
) be the completion of all finite sequences in
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{r})}}
(resp.
\|\cdot\|_{{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}}
). The mixed space
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})
is defined as follows:

•

If
1\leq p<2
, let

L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})=L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{r})+L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})

equipped with the sum norm

\|(x_{n})\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})}=\inf\{\|(y_{n})\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{r})}+\|(z_{n})\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}\},

where the infimun runs over all decompositions
x_{n}=y_{n}+z_{n}
in
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
.

•

If
2\leq p\leq\infty
, let

L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})=L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{r})\cap L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})

equipped with the intersection norm

\|(x_{n})\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})}=\max\{\|(x_{n})\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{r})},\|(x_{n})\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}\}.

Clearly,
L_{2}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{r})=L_{2}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})=L_{2}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})
. Replacing the
L_{p}
-norm with the weak
L_{p}
-norm, one can also use the above procedure to define the noncommutative Hilbert-valued weak
L_{p}
-spaces.
For instance, the
L_{1,\infty}({\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{rc})
is defined by

\|(x_{n})\|_{L_{1,\infty}({\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{rc})}=\inf_{x_{n}=y_{n}+z_{n}}\{\|(y_{n})\|_{L_{1,\infty}({\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{r})}+\|(z_{n})\|_{L_{1,\infty}({\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{c})}\}.

We refer the reader to Pisier-Xu’s book
[
73
]
for the properties of such spaces. In particular, the following noncommutative Khintchine inequalities will be frequently used, see
[
57
,
58
,
70
,
12
]
for the proof.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(\varepsilon_{n})
be a Rademarcher sequence on probability space
(\Omega,P)
. Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
(x_{n})
be a sequence in
L_{p}({\mathcal{M}};\ell^{rc}_{2})
. Then there exist two positive constants
c_{p}
and
c^{\prime}_{p}
such that

c_{p}\|(x_{n})\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell^{rc}_{2})}\leq\bigg\|\sum_{n}\varepsilon_{n}x_{n}\bigg\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\Omega)\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}))}\leq c^{\prime}_{p}\|(x_{n})\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell^{rc}_{2})}.

The above estimate also holds if one replaces the
L_{p}
-spaces by
L_{1,\infty}
-space.

We also need another class of non-commutative Hilbert-valued
L_{p}
-spaces. Let
(\Sigma,\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite measurable space and
0<p\leq\infty
. The column space
L_{p}({\mathcal{M}};L^{c}_{2}(\Sigma))
consists of operator-valued functions
f
for which the norm (or
p
-norm, when
0<p<1
) is finite. The norm is defined as follows

\|f\|_{L_{p}({\mathcal{M}};L^{c}_{2}(\Sigma))}=\left\|\int_{\Sigma}f^{*}(w)f(w)d\mu(w)\right\|_{p}.

The key property for our purpose is the following Hölder’s inequality (see, e.g.,
[
59
, Proposition 1.1]
).

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
0\leq p,q,r\leq\infty
satisfy
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}
. Then for any
f\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M};L^{c}_{2}(\Sigma))
and
g\in L_{q}(\mathcal{M};L^{c}_{2}(\Sigma))
, the following inequality holds

\left\|\int_{\Sigma}f^{*}(w)g(w)\,d\mu(w)\right\|_{r}\leq\left\|\left(\int_{\Sigma}|f(w)|^{2}\,d\mu(w)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{p}\left\|\left(\int_{\Sigma}|g(w)|^{2}\,d\mu(w)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{q}.

2.3.
‘Dyadic cubes’ and boundary properties

In this subsection, we revisit the ‘dyadic cubes’ constructed on the metric space
(G,d,m)
that satisfy the geometric doubling condition (
1.7
) and the
(\epsilon,r_{0})
-annular decay property (
1.8
). Note that
(G,d,m)
may not necessarily be a measure doubling space. As a result, the ’dyadic cubes’ lose the boundary property (cf.
[
17
, Theorem 11 (3.6)]
) of Christ-type dyadic cubes constructed in doubling metric measure spaces. By leveraging the refined lemmas from
[
30
]
, which address boundary conditions in dyadic cubes, balls, and their specific configurations, we derive key technical lemmas to establish Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
. These lemmas could be regarded as results in vector-valued non-homogeneous harmonic analysis with the underlying Banach spaces being noncommutative
L_{p}
spaces.
Since the estimates are delicate, we will fix throughout the paper several constants—including
k_{1},n_{0},n_{1},c_{0},C_{0},C_{1},L_{0},L_{1},\delta
—that depend on conditions (
1.7
), (
1.8
), and the construction.

As is well-known that the geometrically doubling property extends the doubling measure property (cf.
[
19
]
). Consequently, the metric space
(G,d,m)
satisfying conditions (
1.7
) and (
1.8
) may constitute a non-doubling measure space. However, a straightforward calculation reveals that (
1.8
) implies the following property: For any
h\in G
and
r_{0}<r\leq R<\infty
,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\frac{m(B(h,R))}{m(B(h,r))}\lesssim\Big(\frac{R}{r}\Big)^{\epsilon}.

This estimation will be applied subsequently. Furthermore, the geometrically doubling condition immediately implies the following property.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
(G,d)
satisfy the geometrically doubling property, namely condition (
1.7
). For any ball
B(h,R)
with radius
R>0
and arbitrary
0<r\leq R
, there exists a finite covering of
B(h,R)
by at most
D^{[\log_{2}(R/r)]+1}
balls of radius
r
.

The following system of ‘dyadic cubes’ in geometrically doubling space was constructed in
[
38
, Theorem 2.2]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
(G,d)
satisfy the geometrically doubling property, namely condition (
1.7
). Fix constants
0<c_{0}<C_{0}<\infty
and
\delta>1
such that

18C_{0}\delta^{-1}\leq c_{0}.

Let
I_{k}
be an index set for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\{z_{\alpha}^{k}\in G:\alpha\in I_{k},k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
an associated collection of points with the properties that

(2.3)

d(z_{\alpha}^{k},z_{\beta}^{k})\geq c_{0}\delta^{k}~(\alpha\neq\beta),~\min_{\alpha}d(w,z_{\alpha}^{k})<C_{0}\delta^{k},~\forall~w\in G,~k\in\mathbb{Z}.

Set
a_{0}:=c_{0}/3
and
C_{1}:=2C_{0}
. Then there exists a sets
\big\{Q_{\alpha}^{k}\big\}_{\alpha\in I_{k}}
, associating with the index set
\{z_{\alpha}^{k}\}_{\alpha\in I_{k}}
, satisfies the following properties:

(i)

\forall~k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\cup_{\alpha\in I_{k}}Q_{\alpha}^{k}=G
;

(ii)
if
k\geq l
then either
Q_{\alpha}^{l}\subset Q_{\beta}^{k}
or
Q_{\alpha}^{l}\cap Q_{\beta}^{k}=\emptyset
;

(iii)
for each
(k,\alpha)
and each
n>k
, there exists a unique
Q_{\beta}^{n}
such that
Q_{\alpha}^{k}\subset Q_{\beta}^{n}
, and for
n=k+1
, the element
Q_{\beta}^{k+1}

is designated as the parent of
Q_{\alpha}^{k}
, denoted by the symbol
\widehat{Q}_{\alpha}^{k}
.

(iv)

B(z_{\alpha}^{k},a_{0}\delta^{k})\subseteq Q_{\alpha}^{k}\subseteq B(z_{\alpha}^{k},C_{1}\delta^{k})
.

We emphasize that the geometric doubling property ensures the validity of the second inequality in (
2.3
), while the small boundary property (see
[
17
, Theorem 11 (3.6)]
) of dyadic cubes constructed via Proposition
2.4
does not hold universally. Furthermore, Hong and Liu
[
30
]
established distinct boundary properties of these cubes. To achieve our objectives, we synthesize the following four propositions which are established in
[
30
, Lemmas 2.6–2.7 and 2.9–2.10]
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Fix
0<c_{0}<C_{0}<\infty
and
\delta>1
in Proposition
2.4
. Define

\displaystyle L_{0}=[\log_{\delta}(12/c_{0})]+1,L_{1}=[\log_{\delta}(36r_{0}/c_{0})]+1.

Let
k,L\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfy
L_{0}<L<k+L_{0}-L_{1}
and
\alpha\in I_{k}
. There exists a constant
\eta>0
independent of
k
,
L
and ‘dyadic cubes’
Q_{\alpha}^{k}
such that

\begin{split}&m\big(\{g\in Q_{\alpha}^{k}:d(h,G\setminus Q_{\alpha}^{k})\leq\delta^{k-L}\}\big)\lesssim\delta^{-L\eta}m(Q_{\alpha}^{k}),\\
&m\big(\{g\in G\setminus Q_{\alpha}^{k}:d(h,Q_{\alpha}^{k})\leq\delta^{k-L}\}\big)\lesssim\delta^{-L\eta}m(Q_{\alpha}^{k}).\end{split}

Proposition 2.6
.

Given a ball
B_{r}
and a ‘dyadic cube’
Q^{k}_{\alpha}
, define

\mathcal{H}(B_{r},Q^{k}_{\alpha})=\{h\in Q_{\alpha}^{k}:B(h,r)\cap(Q^{k}_{\alpha})^{c}\neq\emptyset\}.

Set
n_{0}=\max\{L_{1}-L_{0},0\}
. Then for every
n>n_{0}
,
k>L_{0}
and
Q_{\alpha}^{n+k}
, we have

m(\mathcal{H}(B_{\delta^{n}},Q^{n+k}_{\alpha}))\lesssim\delta^{-k\eta}m(Q^{n+k}_{\alpha}).

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
r\geq 2r_{0}
and
s\in(0,r]
, then

\begin{split}m(B(h,r+s)\setminus B(h,r-s))\lesssim\bigg(\frac{s}{r}\bigg)^{\epsilon}m(B(h,r)).\end{split}

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
A
be a measurable set on
G
, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1}(A,k)=\bigcup_{{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in I_{k}\\
\partial A\cap Q_{\alpha}^{k}\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}}Q_{\alpha}^{k}.

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(A,k)=\bigcup_{{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in I_{k}\\
\partial A\cap Q_{\alpha}^{k}\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}}Q_{\alpha}^{k}\cap A,

where
\partial A
stands for the boundary of
A
. Set

n_{1}=\min\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\delta^{n}\geq 2r_{0}\},k_{1}=\max\{k\in\mathbb{Z}:2C_{1}\delta^{k}\leq 1\}.

Assume that
n>n_{1}
and
k<k_{1}
, then for any
h\in G
, we have

\begin{split}&\sup_{r\in[\delta^{n},\delta^{n+1}]}\frac{m(\mathcal{I}_{1}(B(h,r),n+k))}{m(B(h,r))}\lesssim\delta^{\epsilon k}\\
&\sup_{r\in[\delta^{n},\delta^{n+1}]}\frac{m(\mathcal{I}(B(h,r),n+k))}{m(B(h,r))}\lesssim\delta^{\epsilon k}.\end{split}

Based on the four propositions above, we obtain the following technical lemmas.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
(G,d)
satisfy the geometric doubling property (
1.7
).
Then for
f\in L_{p}({\mathcal{N}})
with
p\in[1,\infty]
,

\sup_{r>0}\|M_{r}f\|_{p}\leq D^{1/p}\|f\|_{p},

where
M_{r}
was defined in (
1.5
).

Proof.

For
p=1
, we have

\|M_{r}f\|_{L_{1}({\mathcal{N}})}=\|M_{r}f\|_{L_{1}(G;L_{1}(\mathcal{M}))}\leq\int_{G}\frac{1}{m(B(h,r))}\int_{B(h,r)}\|f(g)\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{M})}dm(g)dm(h).

Observe that by
[
1
, Theorem 3.5]

\int_{G}\frac{1}{m(B(h,r))}\int_{B(h,r)}\|f(g)\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{M})}dm(g)dm(h)\leq D\|f\|_{L_{1}({\mathcal{N}})}.

Thus
\|M_{r}\|_{L_{1}({\mathcal{N}})\rightarrow L_{1}({\mathcal{N}})}\leq D
for all
r>0
. On the other hand,
\|M_{r}\|_{L_{\infty}({\mathcal{N}})\rightarrow L_{\infty}({\mathcal{N}})}\leq 1
for all
r>0
. It then follows from interpolation argument (cf.
[
73
]
) that
\|M_{r}\|_{L_{p}({\mathcal{N}})\rightarrow L_{p}({\mathcal{N}})}\leq D^{1/p}
for all
p\in[1,\infty]
.
∎

Lemma 2.10
.

Define

\displaystyle M_{r,n+k}f(h)=\frac{1}{m(B(h,r))}\int_{\mathcal{I}(B(h,r),n+k)}f(g)dm(g)

and

\displaystyle M^{1}_{r,n+k}f(h)=\frac{1}{m(B(h,r))}\int_{\mathcal{I}_{1}(B(h,r),n+k)}f(g)dm(g).

Let
p\in[1,2]
. Assume that
n>n_{r_{0}}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, then

(2.4)

\displaystyle\sup_{r\in[\delta^{n},\delta^{n+1}]}\Big(\|M_{r,n+k}f\|_{p}+\|M^{1}_{r,n+k}f\|_{p}\Big)\lesssim D^{1/p}(1+\delta^{k})^{\epsilon/p}\|f\|_{p}.

Furthermore, if
n>n_{1}
,
k<k_{1}
, then

(2.5)

\displaystyle\sup_{r\in[\delta^{n},\delta^{n+1}]}\Big(\|M_{r,n+k}f\|_{p}+\|M^{1}_{r,n+k}f\|_{p}\Big)\lesssim D^{1/p}\delta^{\epsilon k/p}\|f\|_{p}.

Proof.

We begin with the first inequality. For
p=1
,
n>n_{r_{0}}
and
r\in[\delta^{n},\delta^{n+1}]
, since
\mathcal{I}_{1}(B(h,r),n+k)\subseteq B(h,r+2C_{1}\delta^{n+k})
, we have from (
2.2
) that

(2.6)

\frac{m(\mathcal{I}_{1}(B(h,r),k+n))}{m(B(h,r))}\leq\frac{m(B(h,r+2C_{1}\delta^{n+k}))}{m(B(h,r))}\lesssim(1+\delta^{k})^{\epsilon}.

We then apply
[
1
, Theorem 3.5]
again to deduce that

(2.7)

\begin{split}\|M^{1}_{r,n+k}f\|_{1}&\lesssim\int_{G}\frac{1}{m(B(h,r))}\int_{B(h,r+2C_{1}\delta^{n+k})}\|f(g)\|_{L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})}dm(g)dm(h)\\
&\lesssim\int_{G}\frac{(1+\delta^{k})^{\epsilon}}{m(B(h,r+2C_{1}\delta^{n+k}))}\int_{B(h,r+2C_{1}\delta^{n+k})}\|f(g)\|_{L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})}dm(g)dm(h)\\
&\leq D(1+\delta^{k})^{\epsilon}\|f\|_{1}.\end{split}

For
p=2
, by the Minkowski and Cauchy-Schwarz inequalities, we have

(2.8)

\begin{split}&\Big\|\int_{\mathcal{I}_{1}(B(h,r),n+k)}f(g)dm(g)\Big\|^{2}_{L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})}\leq\Big(\int_{\mathcal{I}_{1}(B(h,r),n+k)}\|f(g)\|_{L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})}dm(g)\Big)^{2}\\
&\leq m(\mathcal{I}_{1}(B(h,r),n+k))\int_{\mathcal{I}_{1}(B(h,r),n+k)}\|f(g)\|^{2}_{L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})}dm(g).\end{split}

According to (
2.6
) and the proof of Lemma
2.9
, we finally obtain

(2.9)

\begin{split}\|M^{1}_{r,n+k}f\|^{2}_{2}&\leq\int_{G}\frac{m(\mathcal{I}_{1}(B(h,r),n+k))}{m(B(h,r))^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{I}_{1}(B(h,r),n+k)}\|f(g)\|^{2}_{L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})}dm(g)dm(h)\\
&\lesssim\int_{G}\frac{(1+\delta^{k})^{\epsilon}}{m(B(h,r+2C_{1}\delta^{n+k}))}\int_{B(h,r+2C_{1}\delta^{n+k})}\|f(g)\|^{2}_{L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})}dm(g)dm(h)\\
&\lesssim D(1+\delta^{k})^{\epsilon}\|f\|^{2}_{L_{2}({\mathcal{N}})}.\end{split}

The above two estimates are also valid for
M_{r,n+k}f
. So by interpolation argument (cf.
[
73
]
), the first inequality is proved.

For
n>n_{1}
and
k<k_{1}
, a more refined analysis is required compared to the case
n>n_{r_{0}}
. Observe that for
r\in[\delta^{n},\delta^{n+1}]
, the inclusion
\mathcal{I}_{1}(B(h,r),n+k)\subseteq B(h,r+2C_{1}\delta^{n+k})\setminus B(h,r-2C_{1}\delta^{n+k})
holds. Define
A(h)
as the annulus
A(h)={B(h,r+2C_{1}\delta^{n+k+1})\setminus B(h,r-2C_{1}\delta^{n+k+1})}
.

When
p=1
, applying the argument of (
2.7
) and Fubini theorem, we obtain

(2.10)

\begin{split}\|M^{1}_{r,n+k}f\|_{1}&\lesssim\int_{G}\frac{1}{m(B(h,r))}\int_{A(h)}\|f(g)\|_{L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})}dm(g)dm(h)\\
&=\int_{G}\int_{G}\frac{\chi_{A(g)}(h)}{m(B(h,r))}\|f(g)\|_{L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})}dm(h)dm(g).\end{split}

From estimate (4.6) in
[
30
]
, it follows that

(2.11)

\int_{G}\frac{\chi_{A(g)}(h)}{m(B(h,r))}dm(h)\lesssim Dr^{\epsilon k}.

Combining (
2.10
) with (
2.11
), we conclude

(2.12)

\begin{split}\sup_{r\in[\delta^{n},\delta^{n+1}]}\|M^{1}_{r,n+k}f\|_{1}\lesssim D\delta^{\epsilon k}\|f\|_{1}.\end{split}

We consider the case when
p=2
. From (
2.9
), we obtain

(2.13)

\|M^{1}_{r,n+k}f\|^{2}_{2}\leq\int_{G}\frac{m(\mathcal{I}_{1}(B(h,r),n+k))}{m(B(h,r))^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{I}_{1}(B(h,r),n+k)}\|f(g)\|^{2}_{L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})}dm(g)dm(h)

Proposition
2.8
shows that

(2.14)

\frac{m(\mathcal{I}_{1}(B(h,r),k+n))}{m(B(h,r))}\lesssim\delta^{\epsilon k}.

Combining (
2.13
) with (
2.14
) and applying the reasoning from (
2.9
), we obtain

\|M^{1}_{r,n+k}f\|^{2}_{2}\lesssim D\delta^{\epsilon k}\|f\|^{2}_{2}.

The same argument applies to
M_{r,n+k}f
since
\mathcal{I}(B(h,r),k+n)\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{1}(B(h,r),k+n)
. Therefore, the interpolation argument gives the second inequality.
∎

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
n>n_{r_{0}}
. Let
(r_{i})_{i}
be an increasing sequence positive numbers belonging to
[\delta^{n},\delta^{n+1}]
. Then

\displaystyle\|\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}(M_{r_{i}}f-M_{r_{i+1}}f)\|_{L_{1}(L_{\infty}(\Omega)\overline{\otimes}{\mathcal{N}})}\lesssim D\delta^{\epsilon}\|f\|_{L_{1}({\mathcal{N}})},

where
(\varepsilon_{i})
is the Rademacher sequence on a probablity space
(\Omega,P)
.

Proof.

Note that

(2.15)

\begin{split}M_{r_{i}}f(h)-M_{r_{i+1}}f(h)&=\Big(\frac{1}{m(B(h,r_{i}))}-\frac{1}{{\mu}(B(h,r_{i+1}))}\Big)\int_{B(h,r_{i})}f(g)dm(y)\\
&\ \ +\frac{1}{m(B(h,r_{i+1}))}\int_{B(h,r_{i+1})\setminus B(h,r_{i})}f(g)dm(y).\end{split}

Therefore, by the Minkowski inequality,

\begin{split}&\|M_{r_{i}}f-M_{r_{i+1}}f\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{N})}\\
&\leq\int_{G}\Big(\frac{1}{m(B(h,r_{i}))}-\frac{1}{m(B(h,r_{i+1}))}\Big)\int_{B(h,r_{i})}\|f(g)\|_{L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})}dm(g)dm(h)\\
&\ \ +\int_{G}\frac{1}{m(B(h,r_{i+1}))}\int_{B(h,r_{i+1})\setminus B(h,r_{i})}\|f(y)\|_{L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})}dm(g)dm(h).\end{split}

Then summing over all
i
, one gets

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\|M_{r_{i}}f-M_{r_{i+1}}f\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{N})}\leq\int_{G}\frac{2}{m(B(h,\delta^{n}))}\int_{B(h,\delta^{n+1})}\|f(g)\|_{L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})}dm(g)dm(h).

Combining (
2.2
) with Lemma
2.9
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{G}\frac{1}{m(B(h,\delta^{n}))}\int_{B(h,\delta^{n+1})}\|f(g)\|_{L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})}dm(g)dm(h)

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{\epsilon}\int_{G}\frac{1}{m(B(h,\delta^{n+1}))}\int_{B(h,\delta^{n+1})}\|f(g)\|_{L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})}dm(g)dm(h)

\displaystyle\lesssim D\delta^{\epsilon}\|f\|_{1},

and the lemma is proved.
∎

For abbreviation, we write

According to the argument given in
[
18
]
, we assume that
m(G)=\infty
. Define a dense subset of

\mathcal{N}_{c,+}=L_{1}(\mathcal{N})\cap\{f|f\in\mathcal{N}_{+},~\overrightarrow{\mbox{supp}}f~\mbox{is compact}\},

Lemma 2.12

More specifically, we have the following expression of

.

With the notations given in the Cuculescu construction and let

(i) and (iii) immediately yield the following two inequalities:

\|(M_{\delta^{n}}f-\mathsf{E}_{n}f)_{n>n_{r_{0}}}\|_{L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{N};\ell_{2}^{rc})}\lesssim\|f\|_{1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|(M_{\delta^{n}}f-\mathsf{E}_{n}f)_{n>n_{r_{0}}}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N};\ell_{2}^{rc})}\lesssim\|f\|_{p}.

We also note that while Theorem
3.1
(ii) does not provide the
\mathrm{BMO}
estimate for the ‘long one’
(M_{\delta^{n}}f-\mathsf{E}_{n}f)_{n>n_{r_{0}}}
. Although this estimate is similar to the one in Theorem
3.2
(ii), a detailed analysis will be presented in Subsection
5.1
.

Theorem
3.1
was first established by Jones and Rosenblatt
[
42
, Theorem 2]
in the commutative setting. Later, the fourth author extended this result to the operator-valued setting for the translation action of
\mathbb{R}^{d}

[
87
]
. Theorem
3.2
originated from the work of the first, second, and fourth authors on quantitative mean ergodic inequalities
[
31
]
, building on Jones et al.’s study of square function inequalities for ergodic averaging
[
41
]
. These two theorems significantly refine and generalize these earlier results.

The proof strategy for Theorem
3.1
and
3.2
draws on ideas from
[
31
,
87
]
. However, conditions (
1.8
) and (
1.7
) do not guarantee that the space
(G,d,m)
is a doubling metric measure space, which introduces new challenges. For instance, the dual argument used to establish
L_{p}
estimation for high-index
2<p<\infty
in
[
87
]
is ineffective.
The ‘dyadic cubes’ constructed in Proposition
2.4
may lack the small boundary property (cf.
[
17
, Theorem 11]
. Moreover, the noncommutative Calderón-Zygmund decomposition for homogeneous spaces becomes insufficient. Consequently, our analysis necessitates a careful geometric treatment of cubes and balls, in conjunction with the noncommutative non-doubling Calderón-Zygmund theory.

4.
Proof of Theorem
3.1
and
3.2
: Weak type
(1,1)
estimates

In this section, we prove the weak type
(1,1)
estimates for Theorem
3.1
and
3.2
. We start by proving the estimate for the ‘long one’, namely Theorem
3.1
, as the corresponding result for Theorem
3.2
(the ‘short one’) can be derived analogously to the ‘long one’ via the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(\varepsilon_{i})_{i\in\mathcal{S}}
be a Rademacher sequence on a fixed probability space
(\Omega,P)
and
T_{i}
and
\mathcal{S}
be defined in Theorem
3.2
. Define

\mathcal{T}f=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}\varepsilon_{i}T_{i}f.

Then

\|(T_{i}f)_{i\in\mathcal{S}}\|_{L_{1,\infty}(\mathcal{N};\ell_{2}^{cr})}\thickapprox\|\mathcal{T}f\|_{L_{1,\infty}(L_{\infty}(\Omega)\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})},

where
L_{\infty}(\Omega)\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N}
is a tensor von Neumann algebra equipped with trace
\widetilde{\varphi}=\varphi\otimes P
.

The aforementioned lemma is an immediate consequence of the noncommutative Khintchine inequalities (Proposition
2.1
).

The procedure to show weak type
(1,1)
estimates is now standard. Let
f\in L_{1}({\mathcal{N}})
. Since
f
can be decomposed into with linear combination four positive elements, namely
f=f_{1}-f_{2}+i(f_{3}-f_{4})
with
f_{j}\geq 0
and
\|f_{j}\|_{1}\leq\|f\|_{1}
for
j=1,2,3,4
, it is enough to prove weak type
(1,1)
estimates by assuming that
f
is positive. Moreover,
{\mathcal{N}}_{c,+}
is dense in
L_{1}({\mathcal{N}})_{+}
, so we may just consider
f\in{\mathcal{N}}_{c,+}
. Now fix
f\in{\mathcal{N}}_{c,+}
and
\lambda>0
. In what follows, the symbol
T
will denote either the operator
L
or
\mathcal{T}
, while
{\varphi}_{1}
will denote either the trace
\varphi
or
\widetilde{\varphi}
. By combining the distribution inequality (
2.1
) with the noncommutative Calderón-Zygmund decomposition (Proposition
2.13
), we conclude that

{\varphi}_{1}(\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(|Tf|))\leq{\varphi}_{1}(\chi_{(\lambda/3,\infty)}(|Tg|))+{\varphi}_{1}(\chi_{(\lambda/3,\infty)}(|Tb_{d}|))+{\varphi}_{1}(\chi_{(\lambda/3,\infty)}(|Tb_{\it{off}}|)).

Therefore, by Lemma
4.1
, the weak type
(1,1)
estimate of
(T_{i}f)_{i}
reduces to prove

(4.1)

\begin{split}&{\varphi}_{1}(\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(|Tb_{d}|))\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{1}}{\lambda}\\
&{\varphi}_{1}(\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(|Tb_{\it{off}}|))\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{1}}{\lambda}\\
&{\varphi}_{1}(\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(|Tg|))\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{1}}{\lambda}.\end{split}

4.1.
Proof of Theorem
3.1
(i)

We begin with the following estimate: Let
L_{n}=M_{\delta^{n+n_{r_{0}}}}-\mathsf{E}_{n+n_{r_{0}}}
and
n_{2}=\max\{n_{r_{0}},n_{1},k_{2}\}
, where
k_{2}
is the constant defined in Lemma
2.14
. Then we have

(4.2)

\begin{split}\Big\|\sum_{0<n\leq n_{2}-n_{r_{0}}}v_{n}L_{n}f\Big\|_{L_{1,\infty}(L_{\infty}(\Omega)\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}\lesssim\|f\|_{1}.\end{split}

It is clear that

\Big\|\sum_{0<n\leq n_{2}-n_{r_{0}}}v_{n}L_{n}f\Big\|_{L_{1,\infty}(L_{\infty}(\Omega)\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}\leq\Big\|\sum_{0<n\leq n_{2}-n_{r_{0}}}v_{n}L_{n}f\Big\|_{L_{1}(L_{\infty}(\Omega)\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})},

and using the triangle inequality for
L_{1}
norm
\|\cdot\|_{L_{1}(L_{\infty}(\Omega)\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}
, we get

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{0<n\leq n_{2}-n_{r_{0}}}v_{n}L_{n}f\Big\|_{L_{1}(L_{\infty}(\Omega)\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle\leq\|(v_{n})_{n}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\sum_{0<n\leq n_{2}-n_{r_{0}}}\|L_{n}f\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq(n_{2}-n_{r_{0}})(D+1)\|(v_{n})_{n}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\|f\|_{1},

where the last inequality follows from Lemma
2.9
and (
2.17
). Hence, (
4.2
) is proved.

By (
4.2
), we can restrict our analysis to the operator
Lf=\sum_{n>n_{2}}v_{n}(M_{\delta^{n}}f-\mathsf{E}_{n}f)
. Furthermore, according to (
4.1
), it remains to demonstrate that

(4.3)

\displaystyle{\varphi}(\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(|Lb_{d}|))\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{1}}{\lambda}

(4.4)

\displaystyle{\varphi}(\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(|Lb_{\it{off}}|))\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{1}}{\lambda}

(4.5)

\displaystyle{\varphi}(\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(|Lg|))\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{1}}{\lambda}.

4.1.1.
Weak type estimate for diagonal terms
b_{d}
: (
4.3
)

Using the projection
\zeta
introduced in Lemma
2.14
, we decompose
Lb_{d}
in the following way

Lb_{d}=(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{N}}-\zeta)Lb_{d}(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{N}}-\zeta)+\zeta Lb_{d}(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{N}}-\zeta)+(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{N}}-\zeta)Lb_{d}\zeta+\zeta Lb_{d}\zeta.

Lemma
2.14
(i) shows

(4.6)

\begin{split}{\varphi}\big(\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(|Lb_{d}|)\big)\lesssim&\ \varphi(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{N}}-\zeta)+{\varphi}\big(\chi_{(\lambda/4,\infty)}(|\zeta Lb_{d}\zeta|)\big)\\
\lesssim&\ \frac{\|f\|_{1}}{\lambda}+{\varphi}\big(\chi_{(\lambda/4,\infty)}(|\zeta Lb_{d}\zeta|)\big).\end{split}

Consequently, it suffices to establish the following bound

(4.7)

\displaystyle{\varphi}\big(\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(|\zeta Lb_{d}\zeta|)\big)\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{1}}{\lambda}.

Proof of estimate (
4.7
).

Observe that

\displaystyle{\varphi}\big(\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(|\zeta Lb_{d}\zeta|)\big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\zeta Lb_{d}\zeta\|_{L_{1}(L_{\infty}(\Omega)\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}}{\lambda}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|(v_{n})_{n}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\sum_{n>n_{2}}\|\zeta(M_{\delta^{n}}b_{d}-\mathsf{E}_{n}b_{d})\zeta\|_{1}}{\lambda}.

To complete the proof, it is sufficient to demonstrate the equality

(4.8)

\sum_{n>n_{2}}\|\zeta(M_{\delta^{n}}b_{d}-\mathsf{E}_{n}b_{d})\zeta\|_{1}\lesssim\|f\|_{1}.

Before establishing (
4.8
), we first claim that

(4.9)

\forall n>n_{2},~\zeta\mathsf{E}_{n}b_{\it{d}}\zeta=0.

Indeed, applying Proposition
2.13
, we write
b_{\it{d}}=\sum_{k}b_{\it{d},k}=\sum_{k}\big(p_{k}fp_{k}-\mathsf{E}_{k+1}(p_{k}fp_{k})\big)
. Hence if we proved that
\forall k,\zeta\mathsf{E}_{n}b_{\it{d,k}}\zeta=0
, then the claim is proved. To prove this fact, we split
k
into two cases:
k<n
and
k\geq n
. For
k<n
, applying the conditional expectation property (see Subsection
2.4
) directly gives
\mathsf{E}_{n}b_{\it{d,k}}=\mathsf{E}_{n}(p_{k}fp_{k})-\mathsf{E}_{n}(\mathsf{E}_{k+1}(p_{k}fp_{k}))=\mathsf{E}_{n}(p_{k}fp_{k})-\mathsf{E}_{n}(p_{k}fp_{k})=0
. For
k\geq n
, it follows from the definition of
n_{2}
that
k>k_{2}
. By the definition of conditional exception
\mathsf{E}_{n}
(see (
2.16
)), we observe that

(4.10)

\begin{split}&\zeta(h)\mathsf{E}_{n}(b_{\textit{d,k}})(h)\zeta(h)\\
&=\sum_{\alpha\in I_{n}}\frac{1}{m(Q_{\alpha}^{n})}\int_{Q_{\alpha}^{n}}\zeta(h)b_{\textit{d,k}}(g)\zeta(h)dm(g)\chi_{Q_{\alpha}^{n}}(h).\end{split}

Observe that for any
h\in Q_{\alpha}^{n}
, Proposition
2.4
(iv) combined with a straightforward calculation calculation shows that for all
g\in Q_{\alpha}^{n}
,
d(g,h)\leq d(g,z_{\alpha}^{n})+d(z_{\alpha}^{n},h)\leq 2C_{1}\delta^{n}
. This directly yields the containment relation
Q_{\alpha}^{n}\subseteq B(h,2C_{1}\delta^{n})
. By invoking Lemma
2.14
(ii), we immediately establish the vanishing property
\zeta\mathsf{E}_{n}b_{\it{d,k}}\zeta=0
, which completes the proof of claim (
4.9
).

Applying the claim (
4.9
), (
4.8
) reduces to establishing the inequality

(4.11)

\sum_{n>n_{2}}\|\zeta M_{\delta^{n}}b_{d}\zeta\|_{1}\lesssim\|f\|_{1}.

Following the decomposition in Proposition
2.13
, we write
b_{d}=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{\alpha\in I_{k}}b_{d,Q_{\alpha}^{k}}
. By applying the trigonometric inequality of
L_{1}
-norm, we demonstrate that the proof of (
4.11
) can be reduced to verifying

(4.12)

\sum_{n>n_{2}}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{\alpha\in I_{k+n}}\|\zeta M_{\delta^{n}}b_{d,Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}}\zeta\|_{1}\lesssim\|f\|_{1}.

Define

k_{3}=\min\{k\in\mathbb{N}:~C_{1}\delta^{k+1}>2~\&~2C_{1}\delta^{-k+1}<1\},~k_{4}=\max\{k_{2},k_{3}\}.

We will prove that

(4.13)

\|\zeta M_{\delta^{n}}b_{d,Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}}\zeta\|_{1}\lesssim a(k)\|b_{d,Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}}\|_{1},

where

a(k)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\hbox{$k>k_{4}$;}\\
{D},&\hbox{$-k_{4}\leq k\leq k_{4}$;}\\
{D\delta^{\epsilon k}},&\hbox{$k<-k_{4}$.}\end{array}\right.

Temporarily assuming this result, we can immediately derive (
4.12
) by combining (
4.13
) with Lemma
2.13
(iii). More precisely,

\displaystyle\sum_{n>n_{2}}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{\alpha\in I_{k+n}}\|\zeta M_{\delta^{n}}b_{d,Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}}\zeta\|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n>n_{2}}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{\alpha\in I_{k+n}}a(k)\|b_{d,Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a(k)\bigg)\bigg(\sum_{n}\sum_{\alpha\in I_{n}}\|b_{d,Q_{\alpha}^{n}}\|_{1}\bigg)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{1}.

We now proceed to prove (
4.13
).

Case

k>k_{4}
. From the proof of Lemma
2.14
(ii), we have
\zeta(h)b_{d,Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}}(g)\chi_{B(h,2C_{1}\delta^{k+n+1})}(g)\zeta(h)=0
. This immediately
implies

\displaystyle\zeta(h)M_{\delta^{n}}(b_{\textit{d},Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}})(h)\zeta(h)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(B(h,\delta^{n}))}\int_{B(h,\delta^{n})}\zeta(h)b_{\textit{d},Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}}(g)\chi_{B(h,2C_{1}\delta^{k+n+1})}(g)\zeta(h)dm(g)

\displaystyle=0.

The desired result is established.

Case

-k_{4}\leq k\leq k_{4}
. By Lemma
2.9
, we have

\displaystyle\|\zeta M_{\delta^{n}}b_{d,Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}}\zeta\|_{1}\leq D\|b_{d,Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}}\|_{1}.

Case

k<-k_{4}
. Set
\mathcal{I}(B(h,r),Q)=\{Q\cap B(h,r):Q\cap\partial B(h,r)\neq\emptyset\}
. Observe that
b_{d,Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}}
has support contained in
\widehat{Q}^{k+n}_{\alpha}
, and satisfies the vanishing property
\int_{\widehat{Q}^{k+n}_{\alpha}}b_{d,Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}}=0
. Then

M_{\delta^{n}}(b_{d,Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}})(h)=\frac{1}{m(B(h,\delta^{n}))}\int_{\mathcal{I}(B(h,\delta^{n}),\widehat{Q}^{k+n}_{\alpha})}b_{d,Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}}(g)dm(g).

Note that
\mathcal{I}(B(h,\delta^{n}),\widehat{Q}^{k+n}_{\alpha})\subseteq\{g:\delta^{n}-2C_{1}\delta^{n+k+1}\leq d(g,h)\leq\delta^{n}+2C_{1}\delta^{n+k+1}\}
. It follows from (
2.12
) that

\displaystyle\|\zeta M_{\delta^{n}}(b_{d,Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}})\zeta\|_{L_{1}({\mathcal{N}})}\leq\|M_{\delta^{n}}(b_{d,Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}})\|_{L_{1}({\mathcal{N}})}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{G}\frac{1}{m(B(h,\delta^{n}))}\int_{\delta^{n}-2C_{1}\delta^{n+k+1}\leq d(g,h)\leq\delta^{n}+2C_{1}\delta^{n+k+1}}\|b_{d,Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}}(g)\|_{L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})}dm(g)dm(h)

\displaystyle\lesssim D\delta^{\epsilon k}\|b_{d,Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}}\|_{L_{1}({\mathcal{N}})},

which completes the proof.
∎

4.1.2.
Weak type estimate for off-diagonal terms
b_{\it{off}}
: (
4.4
)

Our objective is to establish the inequality

(4.14)

\displaystyle{\varphi}\big(\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(|\zeta Lb_{\it{off}}\zeta|)\big)\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{1}}{\lambda}.

We are now prepared to prove the estimate (
4.14
).

Proof of estimate (
4.14
).

Similar to the estimate of
(
4.7
)
, it suffices to show

(4.15)

\sum_{n>n_{2}}\|\zeta(M_{\delta^{n}}b_{\it{off}}-\mathsf{E}_{n}b_{\it{off}})\zeta\|_{1}\lesssim\|f\|_{1}.

Prior to establishing (
4.15
), we introduce an analogous claim similar to (
4.9
)

(4.16)

\forall n>n_{2},~\zeta\mathsf{E}_{n}b_{\text{off}}\zeta=0.

This claim follows directly from the proof of (
4.9
) by decomposing
b_{\text{off}}
as
b_{\it{off}}=\sum_{k}b_{\textit{off},k}=\sum_{k}\big(q_{k}fp_{k}+p_{k}fq_{k}\big)
, where the detailed verification is omitted for brevity.

Given the expression
b_{\it{off}}=\sum_{k}b_{\it{off},k}=\sum_{k}\big(q_{k}fp_{k}+p_{k}fq_{k}\big)
and Claim (
4.16
), by using a similar approach as in (
4.11
), we conclude that the estimate (
4.15
) reduces to proving the inequality

(4.17)

\sum_{n>n_{2}}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{\alpha\in I_{k+n}}\|\zeta M_{\delta^{n}}b_{\it{off},k+n}\zeta\|_{1}\lesssim\|f\|_{1}.

Observing that (
2.20
) implies

(4.18)

\sum_{n}\|p_{n}fp_{n}\|_{1}\leq\|f\|_{1},

we conclude that to prove (
4.17
), it suffices to show

(4.19)

\|\zeta M_{\delta^{n}}b_{\it{off},k+n}\zeta\|_{1}\lesssim a(k)\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\|p_{k+n}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{1}\|p_{k+n}fp_{k+n}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{1},

where

a(k)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\hbox{$k>k_{4}$;}\\
D(1+\delta^{k_{4}})^{\epsilon},&\hbox{$-k_{4}\leq k\leq k_{4}$;}\\
D\delta^{\epsilon k},&\hbox{$k<-k_{4}$.}\end{array}\right.

Assume this result momentarily. Combining (
2.20
) and (
2.18
) with inequality (
4.18
), one can immediately obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n>n_{2}}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\|\zeta M_{\delta^{n}}b_{\it{off},k+n}\zeta\|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n>n_{2}}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a(k)\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\|p_{k+n}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{1}\|p_{k+n}fp_{k+n}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(\sum_{n>n_{2}}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a(k)\|p_{k+n}\|_{1}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(\sum_{n>n_{2}}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a(k)\|p_{k+n}fp_{k+n}\|_{1}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\|f\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{1}}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|f\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{1}

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{1}.

We now proceed to prove (
4.19
).

Case

k>k_{4}
. By Proposition
2.13
(iii), we express
b_{\textit{off},k+n}=\sum_{\alpha\in I_{k+n}}b_{\textit{off},Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}}
, noting that for each
b_{\textit{off},Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}}
,

\displaystyle\zeta(h)M_{\delta^{n}}(b_{\textit{off},Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}})(h)\zeta(h)=\frac{1}{m(B(h,\delta^{n}))}\int_{B(h,\delta^{n})}\zeta(h)b_{\textit{off},Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}}(g)\chi_{B(h,2C_{1}\delta^{k+n+1})}(g)\zeta(h)dm(g).

By applying Lemma
2.14
(ii), we obtain the identity
\zeta(h)b_{\it{off},Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}}(g)\chi_{B(h,2C_{1}\delta^{k+n+1})}(g)\zeta(h)=0
. Combining this with the previous result, we derive
\zeta(h)M_{\delta^{n}}(b_{\textit{off},Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}})(h)\zeta(h)=0
, which consequently implies
\zeta M_{\delta^{n}}b_{\it{off},k+n}\zeta=0
. Thus, the desired result is established.

Case

-k_{4}\leq k\leq k_{4}
. Recall that
b_{\textit{off},k+n}=q_{k+n}fp_{k+n}+p_{k+n}fq_{k+n}
, and by symmetry, we only need to consider the term
\|\zeta M_{\delta^{n}}(p_{k+n}fq_{k+n})\zeta\|_{1}
. Note that

M_{\delta^{n}}(p_{k+n}fq_{k+n})(w)=\frac{1}{m(B(h,\delta^{n}))}\int_{\mathcal{I}_{1}(B(h,\delta^{n}),k+n)}\mathsf{E}_{k+n}(p_{k+n}f\chi_{B(w,\delta^{n})}q_{k+n})(g)dm(g).

Set
g(y)=f(y)\chi_{B(w,\delta^{n})}(y)
, then by (
2.4
) for
p=1
, we have

(4.20)

\|M_{\delta^{n}}(p_{k+n}fq_{k+n})\|_{1}\leq D(1+\delta^{k_{4}})^{\epsilon}\|p_{k+n}g_{k+n}q_{k+n}\|_{1}.

Applying the fact that the conditional expectation
\mathsf{E}_{n}
is a positive operator and Lemma
2.12
(ii), we get

(4.21)

\begin{split}|q_{{k+n}}g_{{k+n}}p_{{k+n}}|&\leq(p_{{k+n}}g_{{k+n}}p_{{k+n}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\|q_{{k+n}}g_{{k+n}}q_{{k+n}}\|_{{\mathcal{M}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq(p_{{k+n}}f_{{k+n}}p_{{k+n}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\|q_{{k+n}}f_{{k+n}}q_{{k+n}}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{{\mathcal{M}}}\\
&\leq\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(p_{{k+n}}f_{{k+n}}p_{{k+n}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{split}

From the equality
\|p_{k+n}g_{k+n}q_{k+n}\|_{1}=\|q_{k+n}g_{k+n}p_{k+n}\|_{1}
, together with (
4.20
), (
4.21
) and Proposition
2.2
, we derive

\displaystyle\|\zeta M_{\delta^{n}}(p_{k+n}fq_{k+n})\zeta\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq D(1+\delta^{k_{4}})^{\epsilon}\|p_{k+n}g_{k+n}q_{k+n}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq D(1+\delta^{k_{4}})^{\epsilon}\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\|p_{{k+n}}f_{{k+n}}p_{{k+n}}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq D(1+\delta^{k_{4}})^{\epsilon}\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\|p_{k+n}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{1}\|p_{{k+n}}f_{{k+n}}p_{{k+n}}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{1}.

By the trace-preserving property of
\mathsf{E}_{k+n}
, we have
\|p_{{k+n}}f_{{k+n}}p_{{k+n}}\|_{1}=\|p_{{k+n}}fp_{{k+n}}\|_{1}
, thereby proving the desired estimate.

Case

k<-k_{4}
. By extending the argument used for the case
-k_{4}\leq k\leq k_{4}
, we can adapt the proof by substituting (
2.4
) with (
2.5
) for
p=1
, which ultimately yields

\displaystyle\|M_{\delta^{n}}(q_{k+n}fp_{k+n})\|_{1}\leq D\delta^{\epsilon k}\|q_{k+n}g_{k+n}p_{k+n}\|_{1}.

Then (
4.19
) is proved.
∎

4.1.3.
Weak type estimate for the good function

: (

)

To establish the estimate for the good part, we require the following proposition.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
h\in L_{2}(\mathcal{N})
. Then

\|Lh\|_{L_{2}(L_{\infty}(\Omega)\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}\lesssim\|h\|_{2}.

The proof strategy employed here closely follows
[

, Lemma 3.1]
. It should be emphasized that the boundary conditions stipulated in Propositions

constitute indispensable elements of this proof framework. For brevity, we omit the detailed proof steps and refer to the referenced works.

).

Using the Chebychev inequality, Proposition

(ii), we have

{\varphi}(\chi_{(\lambda/2,\infty)}(|Lg|))\leq\frac{\|Lg\|^{2}_{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\lesssim\frac{\|g\|_{2}^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{1}}{\lambda},

As noted earlier, by Lemma
4.1
, the proof of Theorem
3.2
(i) largely follows the framework of Theorem
3.1
(i) with slight adjustments for the specific assumptions. However, the treatment of the term

differs significantly from that of (

). Below we present the detailed derivation and outline the key steps.

4.2.
Proof of Theorem
3.2
(i)

Define
\mathcal{S}_{n}=\{i:[r_{i},r_{i+1})\subseteq[\delta^{n},\delta^{n+1})\}
. It is clear that
\mathcal{S}=\cup_{n>n_{r_{0}}}\mathcal{S}_{n}
. Then using Lemma
2.11
, one can see that

\sum_{n_{r_{0}}<n\leq n_{2}}\|\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{n}}\varepsilon_{i}T_{i}f\|_{L_{1}(L_{\infty}(\Omega)\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}\leq(n_{2}-n_{r_{0}})D\|f\|_{1}.

Based on the preceding estimation, it suffices to consider the operator
\mathcal{T}f=\sum_{n>n_{2}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{n}}\varepsilon_{i}T_{i}f
. Moreover, by (

), the proof will be complete once we prove

(4.23)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\varphi}(\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(|\mathcal{T}b_{\it{off}}|))\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{1}}{\lambda}

(4.24)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\varphi}(\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(|\mathcal{T}g|))\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{1}}{\lambda}.

We begin with the term (

).

4.2.1.
Weak type estimate for diagonal terms

: (

Observe that (

Furthermore, by applying the triangle inequality for the

).

Following analogous reasoning to that in (

), to establish (
4.25
), it is sufficient to verify

(4.26)

\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{n}}\|\zeta(M_{r_{i}}b_{d,Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}}-M_{r_{i+1}}b_{d,Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}})\zeta\|_{1}\lesssim a(k)\|b_{d,Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}}\|_{1},

{D\delta^{\epsilon}},&\hbox{$-k_{4}\leq k\leq k_{4}$;}\\

We now proceed to establish (
4.26
).

Case

. Following the proof for the case
k>k_{4}
in estimate (

), we obtain that for all

,

.

Case

-k_{4}\leq k\leq k_{4}

2.11
.

Case

. Since

is supported on
\widehat{Q}_{\alpha}^{k+n}
and

Additionally, (
2.15

(4.27)

\begin{split}&\|\zeta(h)(M_{r_{i}}b_{d,Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}}-M_{r_{i+1}}b_{d,Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}})(h)\zeta(h)\|_{L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})}\\

i_{0}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
such that

(if such an

. Summing over all

in (

), we get

\begin{split}&\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{n}}\|\zeta(M_{r_{i}}b_{d,Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}}-M_{r_{i+1}}b_{d,Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}})\zeta\|_{L_{1}({\mathcal{N}})}\\
&\lesssim\int_{G}\frac{1}{m(B(h,\delta^{n}))}\int_{B(h,r_{i_{0}}+2C_{1}\delta^{n+k+1})\setminus B(h,r_{i_{0}}-2C_{1}\delta^{n+k+1})}\|b_{d,Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}}(g)\|_{L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})}dm(g)dm(h)\\
&\lesssim D\delta^{\epsilon k}\|b_{d,Q_{\alpha}^{k+n}}\|_{L_{1}({\mathcal{N}})},\end{split}

where the last inequality follows from (
2.2
) and the proof of (

∎

4.2.2.
Weak type estimate for off-diagonal terms
b_{\it{off}}
: (
4.23
)

The approach for (
4.23
) differs significantly from (
4.4
). In contrast to (
4.4
), our analysis employs the
L_{2}
-norm method. The proof hinges on the following version of the almost orthogonality principle (see, e.g.,
[
30
,
32
]
).

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(T_{n,i})_{n\in\mathbb{N},i\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence of bounded linear operators on
L_{2}(\mathcal{N})
. Let
(u_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
and
(v_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
are two sequences of operators in
L_{2}(\mathcal{N})
such that
f=\sum_{k}u_{k}
and
\sum_{k}\|v_{k}\|_{2}^{2}<\infty
. Assume that for every
n>N
, there exists a positive sequence
(\sigma(k))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\sigma=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a(k)<\infty
such that

\|(T_{n,i}(u_{k+n}))_{i}\|^{2}_{L_{2}({\mathcal{N}};\ell_{2}^{rc})}\leq a(k)\|v_{k+n}\|_{1},

then

\sum_{n>N}\|(T_{n,i}(f))_{i}\|^{2}_{L_{2}({\mathcal{N}};\ell_{2}^{rc})}\leq\sigma^{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|v_{k}\|_{1}.

Proof.

With the conditions in hand, using the triangle inequality for
L_{2}(\mathcal{N};\ell_{2}^{rc})
-norm and the Young inequality for
\ell_{2}
-norm, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n>N}\|(T_{n,i}(f))_{i}\|^{2}_{L_{2}({\mathcal{N}};\ell_{2}^{rc})}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n>N}\bigg(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\|(T_{n,i}(u_{k+n}))_{i}\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{N};\ell_{2}^{rc})}\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n>N}\bigg(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a(k)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v_{k+n}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{1}\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \bigg(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a(k)\bigg)^{2}\bigg(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\|v_{k}\|_{1}\bigg),

which finishes the proof.
∎

We now proceed to prove (
4.23
).

Proof of (
4.23
).

Applying Chebyshev’s inequality along with the orthogonality of
\varepsilon_{i}
, (
4.23
) reduces to showing that

\begin{split}\widetilde{\varphi}\big(\chi_{(\lambda,\infty)}(|\zeta Tb_{\it{off}}\zeta|)\big)&\leq\frac{\sum_{n>n_{2}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{n}}\|\zeta T_{i}b_{\it{off}}\zeta\|^{2}_{2}}{\lambda^{2}}.\end{split}

It suffices to show

(4.28)

\sum_{n>n_{2}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{n}}\|\zeta T_{i}b_{\it{off}}\zeta\|^{2}_{2}\lesssim\lambda\sum_{n}\|p_{n}fp_{n}\|_{1}

since by (
2.20
)

\sum_{n}\|p_{n}fp_{n}\|_{1}\leq\|f\|_{1}.

To establish (
4.28
), as in Proposition
2.13
, we first express
b_{\it{off}}=\sum_{k}b_{\it{off},k}=\sum_{k}\big(q_{k}fp_{k}+p_{k}fq_{k}\big)
. Letting
(T_{n,i})_{n,i}=(M_{r_{i+1}}-M_{r_{i}})_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{n}}
,
N=n_{2}
,
u_{k}=b_{\it{off},k}
and
v_{k}=p_{k}fp_{k}
in Lemma
4.3
, it is sufficient to show that

(4.29)

\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{n}}\|M_{r_{i+1}}b_{\it{off},k+n}-M_{r_{i}}b_{\it{off},k+n}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\lambda a(k)\|p_{k+n}fp_{k+n}\|_{1},

where

a(k)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\hbox{$k>k_{4}$;}\\
D(1+\delta^{k_{4}})^{\epsilon},&\hbox{$-k_{4}\leq k\leq k_{4}$;}\\
D\delta^{\epsilon k},&\hbox{$k<-k_{4}$.}\end{array}\right.

We now turn to proving (
4.29
).

Case

k>k_{4}
. The desired estimate follows directly from the proof of the case
k>k_{4}
presented in estimate (
4.19
).

Case

-k_{4}\leq k\leq k_{4}
. Recall that
b_{\textit{off},k+n}=q_{k+n}fp_{k+n}+p_{k+n}fq_{k+n}
, by symmetry, we only consider to the term
p_{k+n}fq_{k+n}
. According to (
2.15
), we first set

\displaystyle M^{1}_{i}f(h)=\Big(\frac{1}{m(B(h,r_{i}))}-\frac{1}{m(B(h,r_{i+1}))}\Big)\int_{B(h,r_{i})}f(g)dm(g)

\displaystyle M^{2}_{i}f(h)=\frac{1}{m(B(h,r_{i+1}))}\int_{B(h,r_{i+1})\setminus B(h,r_{i})}f(g)dm(g).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|M_{r_{i+1}}p_{k+n}fq_{k+n}-M_{r_{i}}p_{k+n}fq_{k+n}\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2\|M^{1}_{i}p_{k+n}fq_{k+n}\|_{2}^{2}+2\|M^{2}_{i}p_{k+n}fq_{k+n}\|^{2}_{2}.

We first focus on the term
\|M^{1}_{i}p_{k+n}fq_{k+n}\|_{2}^{2}
. Observing that

\int_{B(h,r_{i})}p_{k+n}(g)f(g)q_{k+n}(g)dm(g)=\int_{\mathcal{I}_{1}(B(h,r_{i}),k+n)}\mathsf{E}_{k+n}(p_{k+n}f\chi_{B(h,r_{i})}q_{k+n})(g)dm(g),

and by analogy with (
4.21
), we derive

\begin{split}|q_{{k+n}}g_{{k+n}}p_{{k+n}}|^{2}&\leq p_{{k+n}}g_{{k+n}}p_{{k+n}}\|q_{{k+n}}g_{{k+n}}q_{{k+n}}\|_{{\mathcal{M}}}\\
&\leq p_{{k+n}}f_{{k+n}}p_{{k+n}}\|q_{{k+n}}f_{{k+n}}q_{{k+n}}\|_{{\mathcal{M}}}\\
&\leq\lambda p_{{k+n}}f_{{k+n}}p_{{k+n}}.\end{split}

Combining (
2.6
) and (
2.8
) with the observations above, we derive

(4.30)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{m(B(h,\delta^{n}))}\Big\|\int_{B(h,r_{i})}p_{k+n}(g)f(g)q_{k+n}(g)dm(g)\Big\|^{2}_{L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})}\\
&\lesssim\lambda(1+\delta^{k_{4}})^{\epsilon}\int_{\mathcal{I}_{1}(B(h,r_{i}),k+n)}\|\mathsf{E}_{k+n}(p_{k+n}f\chi_{B(w,r_{i})}p_{k+n})(g)\|_{L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})}dm(g).\end{split}

Since
f
is positive and
\mathcal{I}_{1}(B(h,r_{i}),k+n)\in\mathcal{F}_{k+n}
, then by the definition of
\mathsf{E}_{k+n}
, one can see that

(4.31)

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathcal{I}_{1}(B(h,r_{i}),k+n)}\|\mathsf{E}_{k+n}(p_{k+n}f\chi_{B(h,r_{i})}p_{k+n})(g)\|_{L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})}dm(g)\\
&\leq\int_{B(h,r_{i})}p_{k+n}(g)f(g)p_{k+n}(g)dm(g).\end{split}

The combination of (
2.2
), (
4.30
), and (
4.31
) leads to

\displaystyle\|M^{1}_{i}p_{k+n}fq_{k+n}(h)\|^{2}_{L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda(1+\delta^{k_{4}})^{\epsilon}\Big(\frac{1}{m(B(h,r_{i}))}-\frac{1}{m(B(h,r_{i+1}))}\Big)

\displaystyle\times\int_{B(h,\delta^{n+1})}\|p_{k+n}(g)f(g)p_{k+n}(g)\|_{L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})}dm(g).

Summing
i\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
over the above inequality, and applying (
2.2
) and Lemma

, we obtain

\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{n}}\|M^{1}_{i}p_{k+n}fq_{k+n}\|^{2}_{2}\lesssim\lambda D(1+\delta^{k_{4}})^{\epsilon}\|p_{k+n}fp_{k+n}\|_{1}.

The preceding arguments remain valid when applied to the term

. Let us explain it briefly. The main observations are
\mathcal{I}_{1}(B(h,r_{i})\setminus B(h,r_{i-1}),k+n)\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{1}(B(h,r_{i}),k+n)\cup\mathcal{I}_{1}(B(h,r_{i-1}),k+n)
and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m(B(h,\delta^{n}))}\Big\|\int_{B(h,r_{i})\setminus B(h,r_{i-1})}p_{k+n}(g)f(g)q_{k+n}(g)dm(g)\Big\|^{2}_{L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda(1+\delta^{k_{4}})^{\epsilon}\int_{\mathcal{I}_{1}(B(h,r_{i})\setminus B(h,r_{i-1}),k+n)}\|\mathsf{E}_{k+n}(p_{k+n}f\chi_{B(h,r_{i})\setminus B(h,r_{i-1})}p_{k+n})(g)\|_{L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})}dm(g).

So, summing

over the above inequality, we have

\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{n}}\|M^{2}_{i}p_{k+n}fq_{k+n}\|^{2}_{2}\lesssim\lambda D(1+\delta^{k_{4}})^{\epsilon}\|p_{k+n}fp_{k+n}\|_{1}.

Case

k<-k_{4}
. The argument for the case
-k_{4}\leq k\leq k_{4}
remains valid here. Observing that using (
2.14
), (
2.8
) and (
4.21
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m(B(h,\delta^{n}))}\Big\|\int_{B(h,r_{i})}p_{k+n}(y)f(g)q_{k+n}(g)dm(g)\Big\|^{2}_{L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda\delta^{k\epsilon}\int_{\mathcal{I}_{1}(B(h,r_{i}),k+n)}\|\mathsf{E}_{k+n}(p_{k+n}f\chi_{B(h,r_{i})}p_{k+n})(g)\|_{L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})}dm(g).

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m(B(h,\delta^{n}))}\Big\|\int_{B(h,r_{i})\setminus B(h,r_{i-1})}p_{k+n}(y)f(g)q_{k+n}(g)dm(g)\Big\|^{2}_{L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda\delta^{k\epsilon}\int_{\mathcal{I}_{1}(B(h,r_{i})\setminus B(h,r_{i-1}),k+n)}\|\mathsf{E}_{k+n}(p_{k+n}f\chi_{B(h,r_{i})\setminus B(h,r_{i-1})}p_{k+n})(g)\|_{L_{1}({\mathcal{M}})}dm(g).

We have therefore established (

), which completes the proof.∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
3.1
and
3.2
:
(L_{\infty},\mathrm{BMO})
and strong type

In this section, we show the
(L_{\infty},\mathrm{BMO})
and strong type
(p,p)
estimates stated in Theorem
3.1
and
3.2
, respectively.

5.1.
(L_{\infty},\mathrm{BMO})
estimate

Let
\mathcal{A}
be the von Neumann algebra equipped with the trace
\varphi
. Let
({\mathcal{N}}_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
be a filtration of
{\mathcal{N}}
given by Subsection
2.4
. It is clear that
({\mathcal{N}}_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
is a filtration of
\mathcal{A}
. By abuse of notation, we continue to let
\mathsf{E}_{k}
stand for the conditional expectation with respect to
\mathcal{A}_{k}
. Let us recall the definition of noncommutative
\mathrm{BMO}
space associated with the filtration
(\mathcal{A}_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
.

\displaystyle\mathrm{BMO}_{c}(\mathcal{A})=\{f\in L_{\infty}(\mathcal{A}):\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{c}(\mathcal{A})}=\sup_{k}\|\mathsf{E}_{k}(|f-\mathsf{E}_{k+1}f|^{2})\|_{\infty}^{1/2}<\infty\}

\displaystyle\mathrm{BMO}_{r}(\mathcal{A})=\{f\in L_{\infty}(\mathcal{A}):\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{r}(\mathcal{A})}=\|f^{*}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{c}(\mathcal{A})}<\infty\}.

The space
\mathrm{BMO}(\mathcal{A})
is defined as

\mathrm{BMO}(\mathcal{A})=\mathrm{BMO}_{c}(\mathcal{A})\cap\mathrm{BMO}_{r}(\mathcal{A}),

equipped with the intersection norm

(5.1)

\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}(\mathcal{A})}=\max\{\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{c}(\mathcal{A})},\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{r}(\mathcal{A})}\}.

A direct computation establishes the following expressions for the
\mathrm{BMO}
norms

(5.2)

\begin{split}&\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{c}(\mathcal{A})}=\sup_{Q_{\alpha}^{k}\in\mathcal{F}}\Big\|\frac{1}{m(Q_{\alpha}^{k})}\int_{Q_{\alpha}^{k}}\Big|f(h)-f_{\widehat{Q}_{\alpha}^{k}}\Big|^{2}dm(h)\Big\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{{\mathcal{M}}},\\
&\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{r}(\mathcal{A})}=\sup_{Q_{\alpha}^{k}\in\mathcal{F}}\Big\|\frac{1}{m(Q_{\alpha}^{k})}\int_{Q_{\alpha}^{k}}\Big|f^{*}(h)-f^{*}_{\widehat{Q}_{\alpha}^{k}}\Big|^{2}dm(h)\Big\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{{\mathcal{M}}}.\end{split}

We now proceed to prove Theorem
3.1
(ii).

Proof of Theorem
3.1
(ii).

Let
f\in L_{\infty}(\mathcal{A})
. By the definition of
\mathrm{BMO}
space, it is enough to establish

(5.3)

\|Lf\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{c}(\mathcal{A})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\infty}

and

(5.4)

\|Lf\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{r}(\mathcal{A})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\infty}.

It suffices to prove (
5.3
). To see this, suppose (
5.3
) holds. Then, by the fact that
\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{r}(\mathcal{A})}=\|f^{*}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{c}(\mathcal{A})}
, we obtain

\|Lf\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{r}(\mathcal{A})}=\|(Lf)^{*}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{c}(\mathcal{A})}=\|Lf^{*}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{c}(\mathcal{A})}\lesssim\|f^{*}\|_{\infty}=\|f\|_{\infty},

which establishes (
5.4
).

We establish (
5.3
) in the following. Exploiting the operator convexity of the square function
w\mapsto|w|^{2}
, we obtain for any
c\in\mathcal{A}

\begin{split}\frac{1}{m(Q_{\alpha}^{k})}&\int_{Q_{\alpha}^{k}}\Big|f(h)-f_{\widehat{Q}_{\alpha}^{k}}\Big|^{2}dm(h)\leq\frac{2}{m(Q_{\alpha}^{k})}\int_{Q_{\alpha}^{k}}\Big|f(h)-c\Big|^{2}d{\mu}(h)+2|f_{\widehat{Q}_{\alpha}^{k}}-c|^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{2}{m(Q_{\alpha}^{k})}\int_{Q_{\alpha}^{k}}\Big|f(h)-c\Big|^{2}dm(h)+\frac{2}{m(\widehat{Q}_{\alpha}^{k})}\int_{\widehat{Q}_{\alpha}^{k}}\Big|f(h)-c\Big|^{2}dm(h).\end{split}

Combining the above estimate with (
5.2
), we conclude

(5.5)

\begin{split}\|Lf\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{c}(\mathcal{A})}&\lesssim\sup_{Q_{\alpha}^{k}\in\mathcal{F}}\inf_{c\in\mathcal{A}}\Big(\Big\|\frac{1}{m(Q_{\alpha}^{k})}\int_{Q_{\alpha}^{k}}|Lf-c|^{2}dm(h)\Big\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{{\mathcal{M}}}\\
&+\Big\|\frac{1}{m(\widehat{Q}_{\alpha}^{k})}\int_{\widehat{Q}_{\alpha}^{k}}|Lf-c|^{2}dm(h)\Big\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{{\mathcal{M}}}\Big).\end{split}

Fixing a dyadic cube’
Q^{k}_{\beta}
, we recall that
\widehat{Q}_{\beta}^{k}
is the parent of
Q_{\beta}^{k}
and
k_{2}=\min\{k:a_{0}\delta^{k}>r_{0}\}
. Let
\widetilde{Q}_{\beta}^{k}=\{h\in G:d(h,z_{\beta}^{k})\leq 4C_{1}\delta^{k+1}\}
. We then define

{Q^{k}_{\beta}}^{*}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\widetilde{{Q}}^{k}_{\beta},&\hbox{$k>k_{2}$;}\\
{Q}^{k}_{\beta},&\hbox{$k\leq k_{2}$,}\end{array}\right.

and decompose
f
as

f=f\mathds{1}_{{Q^{k}_{\beta}}^{*}}+f\mathds{1}_{G\setminus{Q^{k}_{\beta}}^{*}}:=f_{1}+f_{2}.

By applying the operator convexity of square function
w\mapsto|w|^{2}
again, we obtain

(5.6)

\begin{split}|Lf-c|^{2}\leq 2|Lf_{1}|^{2}+2|Lf_{2}-c|^{2}.\end{split}

Combining (
5.5
) with (
5.6
), (
5.3
) reduces to proving the two equalities

(5.7)

\begin{split}\sup_{Q_{\alpha}^{k}\in\mathcal{F}}\inf_{c\in\mathcal{A}}&\Big(\Big\|\frac{1}{m(Q_{\alpha}^{k})}\int_{Q_{\alpha}^{k}}|Lf_{1}(h)|^{2}dm(h)\Big\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{{\mathcal{M}}}+\Big\|\frac{1}{m(\widehat{Q}_{\alpha}^{k})}\int_{\widehat{Q}_{\alpha}^{k}}|Lf_{1}(h)|^{2}dm(h)\Big\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{{\mathcal{M}}}\Big)\\
&\lesssim\|f\|_{\infty}\end{split}

and

(5.8)

\begin{split}\sup_{Q_{\alpha}^{k}\in\mathcal{F}}\inf_{c\in\mathcal{A}}&\Big(\Big\|\frac{1}{m(Q_{\alpha}^{k})}\int_{Q_{\alpha}^{k}}|Lf_{2}(h)-c|^{2}dm(h)\Big\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{{\mathcal{M}}}+\Big\|\frac{1}{m(\widehat{Q}_{\alpha}^{k})}\int_{\widehat{Q}_{\alpha}^{k}}|Lf_{2}(h)-c|^{2}dm(h)\Big\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{{\mathcal{M}}}\Big)\\
&\lesssim\|f\|_{\infty}.\end{split}

We now turn our attention to (
5.7
). It is known that the elements of the von Neumann algebra
{\mathcal{M}}
can be seen as bounded linear operators on
L_{2}(M)
by right or left multiplication. By Proposition
4.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Big(

\displaystyle\Big\|\frac{1}{m(Q_{\alpha}^{k})}\int_{Q_{\alpha}^{k}}\Big|\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}T_{i}(f^{*}_{1})(h)\otimes e_{1i}\Big|^{2}dm(h)\Big\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{{\mathcal{M}}\accentset{{\cc@style\underline{\mskip 10.0mu}}}{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2})}

Proof of Theorem

In this section, we investigate the noncommutative analogue of the classical transference principle established by Coifman and Weiss
[
20
]
. Recall that a sequence of compact sets

with positive Haar measures in a locally compact group

is called a F
{\o}
lner sequence if, for every

,

\lim_{i}\frac{m((F_{n}g)\triangle F_{n})}{m(F_{n})}=0,

or equivalently
for all compact set

\lim_{n}\frac{m(F_{n}K)}{m(F_{n})}=1.

A group

is said to be amenable if it admits a F

lner sequence.

6.1.
Strong type inequalities.

The transference principle for strong type
(p,p)
inequalities requires careful consideration of bounded linear operator extensions. This foundational result follows directly from noncommutative Khintchine inequalities (Proposition
2.1
), a standard tool familiar to specialists in functional analysis. We omit the detailed proof as it constitutes an exercise for advanced readers.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let

. Assume that

is a bounded linear operator on

extends to a bounded operator on
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell^{rc}_{2})

Proof of Theorem
1.5
(i).

Let

and

. By the standard approximation argument stated in Remark
1.8
, it suffices to show for any fixed integer
i_{0}
,

\|(A_{r_{i}}x-A_{r_{i+1}}x)_{1\leq i\leq i_{0}}\|_{L_{p}({\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{rc})}\lesssim\|x\|_{L_{p}({\mathcal{M}})}.

Let
\alpha
be a strongly continuous and uniformly bounded action of
G
on
L_{p}(M)
. Fix
x\in L_{p}({\mathcal{M}})
and a compact set
A
. Let
K
be a compact set such that
B_{r_{i_{0}+1}}\subseteq K
. Define
L_{p}({\mathcal{M}})
-valued function

on

F_{AK}(g)=\chi_{AK}(g)\alpha_{g}x.

By the metric invariance property of

is a Haar measure, it follows that

. This leads to the following results for

\alpha_{h}A_{r_{i}}x=\frac{1}{m(B_{r_{i}})}\int_{B_{r_{i}}}\alpha_{h}\alpha_{g}xdm(g)=\frac{1}{m(B_{r_{i}})}\int_{B_{r_{i}}}F_{AK}(hg)dm(g)=M_{r_{i}}F_{AK}(h).

By combining Lemma

with the uniform boundedness assumption of

\begin{split}\|(A_{r_{i}}x-A_{r_{i+1}}x)_{1\leq i\leq i_{0}}\|_{L_{p}({\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{rc})}&\lesssim\|(\alpha_{g}A_{r_{i}}x-\alpha_{g}A_{r_{i+1}}x)_{1\leq i\leq i_{0}}\|_{L_{p}({\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{rc})}\\

Furthermore, applying the strong type

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{m(A)}\int_{A}\|(M_{r_{i}}F_{AK}(g)-M_{r_{i+1}}F_{AK}(g))_{1\leq i\leq i_{0}}\|^{p}_{L_{p}({\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{rc})}dm(g)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(a_{i})_{1\leq i\leq i_{0}}\|^{p}_{L_{p,\infty}(L_{\infty}(G)\overline{\otimes}{\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{c})}+\|(a^{*}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq i_{0}}\|^{p}_{L_{p,\infty}(L_{\infty}(G)\overline{\otimes}{\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{c})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(f_{i})_{1\leq i\leq i_{0}}\|^{p}_{L_{p,\infty}(L_{\infty}(G)\overline{\otimes}{\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{c})}+\|(g_{i})_{1\leq i\leq i_{0}}\|^{p}_{L_{p,\infty}(L_{\infty}(G)\overline{\otimes}{\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{c})}

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\|(f^{*}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq i_{0}}\|^{p}_{L_{p,\infty}(L_{\infty}(G)\overline{\otimes}{\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{c})}+\|(g^{*}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq i_{0}}\|^{p}_{L_{p,\infty}(L_{\infty}(G)\overline{\otimes}{\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{c})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|(a_{i})_{1\leq i\leq i_{0}}\big\|^{p}_{L_{p,\infty}(L_{\infty}(G)\overline{\otimes}{\mathcal{M}};\ell_{2}^{rc})}+\varepsilon^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|F_{AK}\|^{p}_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(G)\overline{\otimes}{\mathcal{M}})}+\varepsilon^{p}.

By the arbitrariness of

which completes the proof.
∎

7.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
.
A bounded linear operator

on

is said to be a

if for all
\tau
-finite projections

with

, one has

(Te)^{*}(Tf)=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad(Te)(Tf)^{*}=0.

Theorem 7.2
.

[
33
]

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and let
T:L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau)\;\to\;L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau)

be a Lamperti operator of norm
\|T\|_{p\to p}^{p}=C
.
Then there exist uniquely:
a partial isometry
w\in\mathcal{M}
,
a positive self-adjoint operator
b
affiliated with
\mathcal{M}
,
and a normal Jordan
*
-homomorphism
J:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
, such that the following hold:

(1)

w^{*}w=J(1)=s(b)
, where
s(b)
denotes the support of
b
.
Moreover, if
T
is positive, then
w=J(1)=s(b)
.

(2)

Every spectral projection of
b
commutes with
J(x)
for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
.

(3)

For all

, one has

(7.1)

(4)

For all

\tau\!\bigl(b^{p}\,J(x)\bigr)\;\leq\;C^{p}\,\tau(x).

Remark 7.3
.

In Theorem (
7.2
) the decomposition in (
7.1
) will be referred to as Lamperti decomposition of the operator
T.

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and let
T:L_{p}(\mathcal{M})\to L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
be a Lamperti operator with decomposition
T=wbJ
.
Define the operator
|T|
by

|T|(x):=bJ(x),\qquad x\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{M}}.

Then
|T|
extends to a positive Lamperti operator on
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
, and one has
|Tx|=||T|x|=|T||x|
for all
x\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M}).

Moreover,

\|T\|_{p\to p}=\||T|\|_{p\to p}.

Proof.

Take
x\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{M}}
. Then

(Tx)^{*}(Tx)=J(x)^{*}bw^{*}wbJ(x)=J(x)^{*}b^{2}J(x)=|bJ(x)|^{2}=\bigl(|T|x\bigr)^{*}\bigl(|T|x\bigr).

Here, the second equality uses parts (1) and (2) of Theorem
7.2
.
By uniqueness of the square root, it follows that

(7.2)

|Tx|=|\,|T|x\,|=|T||x|,\qquad x\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{M}}.

Consequently,

\|Tx\|_{p}=\||T|x\|_{p},\qquad x\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{M}}.

Hence
|T|
extends uniquely to a bounded operator on
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
.
By construction,
|T|
is positive, and it is again a Lamperti operator (see
[
33
, Remark 3.4]
for details) with
\|T\|_{p\to p}=\||T|\|_{p\to p}.

Finally, to prove
7.2
for arbitrary
x\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
, choose a sequence
(x_{n})\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{M}}
with
\|x_{n}-x\|_{p}\to 0
.
By continuity of the map
x\mapsto|x|
in
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})

[
50
,
16
]
, we deduce

|Tx|=\lim_{n\to\infty}|Tx_{n}|=\lim_{n\to\infty}||T|x_{n}|=||T|x|.

This proves the claim.
∎

Remark 7.5
.

For a Lamperti operator

on
L_{p}(\mathcal{M}),
the operator
|T|
defined in Proposition (

) is called the modulus of

Let
G
be a locally compact group and

Let

be a strongly continuous uniformly bounded action of

on

We say

is (positive) Lamperti if

is a (positive) Lamperti operator for all

Proposition 7.6
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and let
\alpha:G\to\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\mathcal{M}))
be a Lamperti representation. Then the map

g\mapsto|\alpha|_{g}:=|\alpha_{g}|

is again a Lamperti representation.

Proof.

First, recall that
\alpha
is a representation, so for all
g,h\in G
and
x\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
,

\alpha_{g}(\alpha_{h}(x))=\alpha_{gh}(x).

By Proposition (
7.4
), for every positive element
x\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M})_{+}
, we have

|\alpha_{g}(\alpha_{h}(x))|=|\alpha_{g}|\,|\alpha_{h}|\,x,

and

|\alpha_{gh}(x)|=|\alpha_{gh}|\,x.

Combining these two equalities gives

|\alpha_{g}|\,|\alpha_{h}|\,x=|\alpha_{gh}|\,x,\qquad\forall\,x\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M})_{+}.

Since positive elements span
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
, this identity extends to all
x\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
, and we conclude that

|\alpha_{g}|\,|\alpha_{h}|=|\alpha_{gh}|,

showing that
g\mapsto|\alpha_{g}|
is a representation.

It remains to check strong continuity. Since
\alpha
is strongly continuous, for any
x\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
,

\|\alpha_{g}(x)-x\|_{p}\to 0\quad\text{as }g\to e.

In particular, this holds for
x\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M})_{+}
. But the continuity of the map
x\mapsto|x|
we get

\big\||\alpha_{g}(x)|-x\big\|_{p}\to 0\quad\text{as }g\to e.

Since
|\alpha_{g}(x)|=|\alpha_{g}|(x)
, we obtain

\||\alpha_{g}|(x)-x\|_{p}\to 0,\ \text{for all}x\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M})_{+}.

Thus
g\mapsto|\alpha_{g}|
is strongly continuous.
This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Proposition 7.9
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a finite von Neumann algebra, let

,

Qy:=\sum_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})}\sum_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})}\left(\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Lambda(\alpha_{j})}{N^{n}}\right)^{1/q}\,y_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})},

for

y=\Big(y_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})}\Big)_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathcal{A}^{n},\,(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in[N]^{n}}.

We show now that
Q
is a contraction.

By the triangle inequality, we have

\|Qy\|\leq\sum_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})}\sum_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})}\left(\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Lambda(\alpha_{j})}{N^{n}}\right)^{1/q}\big\|y_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})}\big\|.

Applying Hölder’s inequality with exponents
p
and
q
yields

\|Qy\|\leq\Bigg(\sum_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})}\sum_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Lambda(\alpha_{j})}{N^{n}}\Bigg)^{1/q}\Bigg(\sum_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})}\sum_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})}\big\|y_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})}\big\|^{p}\Bigg)^{1/p}.

Now observe that by (
8.3
)

\sum_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})}\sum_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Lambda(\alpha_{j})}{N^{n}}=\sum_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Lambda(\alpha_{j})}{N^{n}}\cdot N^{n}=\sum_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Lambda(\alpha_{j})=1.

Therefore, we obtain that

\|Qy\|\leq\Bigg(\sum_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})}\sum_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})}\big\|y_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})}\big\|^{p}\Bigg)^{1/p}=\|y\|_{Y}.

Thus
Q
is a contraction.

For
1\leq r\leq n
, define
U_{r}:Y\to Y
by

(8.4)

\big(U_{r}x\big)_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})}:=T_{r,\alpha_{r}(i_{r})}\,x_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),(i_{1},\dots,\sigma(i_{r}),\dots,i_{n})},

where

x=\Big(x_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})}\Big)_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{A}_{n},\;\,(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\{1,\dots,N\}^{n}}\in Y,

and
\sigma:\{1,\dots,N\}\to\{1,\dots,N\}
denotes the
N
-cycle
\sigma=(1\;2\;\cdots\;N).

Fix
r\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. Linearity of
U_{r}
is immediate from the linearity of each
T_{r,j}
and the definition of
U_{r}
as in (
8.4
). We check that
U_{r}
is an isometry. Recall

\|x\|_{Y}^{p}\;=\;\sum_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})}\sum_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})}\big\|x_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})}\big\|_{X}^{p},

and that each
T_{r,j}
is an isometry on
X
by hypothesis for all
1\leq j\leq m
. Hence

\displaystyle\|U_{r}x\|_{Y}^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})}\sum_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})}\big\|\big(U_{r}x\big)_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})}\big\|_{X}^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})}\sum_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})}\big\|T_{r,\alpha_{r}(i_{r})}\,x_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),(i_{1},\dots,\sigma(i_{r}),\dots,i_{n})}\big\|_{X}^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})}\sum_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})}\big\|x_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),(i_{1},\dots,\sigma(i_{r}),\dots,i_{n})}\big\|_{X}^{p},

where we used that
T_{r,\alpha_{r}(i_{r})}
is an isometry to drop it inside the norm. Now observe that the map

(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n},\;i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\longmapsto(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n},\;i_{1},\dots,\sigma(i_{r}),\dots,i_{n})

is a bijection of the finite index set
\mathcal{A}^{n}\times[N]^{n}
as it is the identity on all coordinates except the
r
-th
i
-coordinate where it applies the permutation
\sigma
. Therefore the double sum above is equal to

\sum_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})}\sum_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})}\big\|x_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})}\big\|_{X}^{p}=\|x\|_{Y}^{p}.

Thus
\|U_{r}x\|_{Y}=\|x\|_{Y}
for all
x\in Y
, so
U_{r}
is an isometry.

We now prove that
U_{r}
and
U_{s}
commute for
r\neq s
. Fix
r\neq s
and an index

((\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),(i_{1},\dots,i_{n}))
. We compute the corresponding coordinate of
U_{r}U_{s}x
.
First,

(U_{s}x)_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})}=T_{s,\alpha_{s}(i_{s})}\,x_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),(i_{1},\dots,\sigma(i_{s}),\dots,i_{n})}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\big(U_{r}(U_{s}x)\big)_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})}

\displaystyle=T_{r,\alpha_{r}(i_{r})}\big(U_{s}x\big)_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),(i_{1},\dots,\sigma(i_{r}),\dots,i_{n})}

\displaystyle=\big(T_{r,\alpha_{r}(i_{r})}\,T_{s,\alpha_{s}(i_{s})}\big)\,x_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),(i_{1},\dots,\sigma(i_{r}),\dots,\sigma(i_{s}),\dots,i_{n})}.

Interchanging the roles of
r
and
s
gives

\big(U_{s}(U_{r}x)\big)_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})}=\big(T_{s,\alpha_{s}(i_{s})}T_{r,\alpha_{r}(i_{r})}\big)\,x_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),(i_{1},\dots,\sigma(i_{r}),\dots,\sigma(i_{s}),\dots,i_{n})}.

By the hypothesis that the families
\mathcal{T}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{T}_{n}
are pairwise commuting, every operator from
\mathcal{T}_{r}
commutes with every operator from
\mathcal{T}_{s}
. In particular,

T_{r,\alpha_{r}(i_{r})}T_{s,\alpha_{s}(\sigma(i_{r}))}\;=\;T_{s,\alpha_{s}(\sigma(i_{r}))}T_{r,\alpha_{r}(i_{r})}.

Thus the two coordinate values

\big(U_{r}(U_{s}x)\big)_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})}\quad\text{and}\quad\big(U_{s}(U_{r}x)\big)_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})}

are equal for every index. Since this holds for every index, we conclude
U_{r}U_{s}x=U_{s}U_{r}x
for all
x\in Y
, i.e.
U_{r}U_{s}=U_{s}U_{r}
.

Next we prove that for any
0\leq j_{1},\dots,j_{n}\leq N
such that
j_{1}+\dots+j_{n}=N,
we have the identity

(8.5)

T_{1}^{j_{1}}\dots T_{n}^{j_{n}}x=QU_{1}^{j_{1}}\dots U_{n}^{j_{n}}Jx,\text{for all}\ x\in X.

To describe the action of the dilating operators, let

x=\bigl(x_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),\,(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})}\bigr).

Then, for a fixed
r\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, the iterates of
U_{r}
act as

(8.6)

\bigl(U_{r}^{\,j_{r}}x\bigr)_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),\,(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})}=\Biggl(\prod_{l_{r}=1}^{j_{r}}T_{r,\,\alpha_{r}\!\bigl(\sigma^{\,i_{r}-1}(l_{r})\bigr)}\Biggr)\,x_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),\,(i_{1},\dots,\sigma^{j_{r}}(i_{r}),\dots,i_{n})}.

In above we have used the fact that
\sigma^{k-1}(j)=\sigma^{j-1}(k)
for all
j,k\in\{1,\dots,N\}.

\displaystyle QU_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots U_{n}^{j_{n}}Jx

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}^{n}}\;\sum_{i\in[N]^{n}}\left(\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Lambda(\alpha_{j})}{N^{n}}\right)^{1/q}\left(\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Lambda(\alpha_{j})}{N^{n}}\right)^{1/p}\Biggl(\prod_{r=1}^{n}\prod_{l_{r}=1}^{j_{r}}T_{r,\,\alpha_{r}\!\bigl(\sigma^{\,i_{r}-1}(l_{r})\bigr)}\Biggr)x

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}^{n}}\;\sum_{i\in[N]^{n}}\left(\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Lambda(\alpha_{j})}{N^{n}}\right)\Biggl(\prod_{r=1}^{n}\prod_{l_{r}=1}^{j_{r}}T_{r,\,\alpha_{r}\!\bigl(\sigma^{\,i_{r}-1}(l_{r})\bigr)}\Biggr)x

\displaystyle=\prod_{r=1}^{n}\Big(\sum_{\alpha_{r}\in\mathcal{A}}\sum_{i_{r}\in[N]}\frac{\Lambda(\alpha_{r})}{N}\prod_{l=1}^{j_{r}}T_{r,\,\alpha_{r}\!\bigl(\sigma^{\,i_{r}-1}(l)\bigr)}\Big)x

(8.7)

\displaystyle=T_{1}^{j_{1}}\dots T_{n}^{j_{n}}x.

In above we have used Lemma (
8.7
) to obtain (
8.7
).
∎

Corollary 8.8
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and and
(\mathcal{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{U}_{n})
be a tuple of pairwise commuting families of isometries on
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
consisting of isomteries. Then, every tuple of operators
\mathbf{T}=(T_{1},\dots,T_{n})
where
T_{i}
belonging to the convex hull of
\mathcal{U}_{i},
where
1\leq i\leq n,
also admits a joint
N
-dilation for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
on a bigger noncommutative
L_{p}
-space.

Remark 8.9
.

As in
[
24
]
, one can use an ultraproduct argument to obtain joint dilations for SOT-limits of such commuting tuples; this will be addressed elsewhere.

9.
Quantitative ergodic theorem for semigroup of operators

Let
1\leq p<\infty.
Let us denote
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}:=\{(t_{1},\dots,t_{d}):t_{i}\geq 0,\ 1\leq i\leq d\}.
Let
\alpha:\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}\to\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\mathcal{M}))
be a strongly continuous uniformly bounded semigroup of operators. Define for any
t>0,

A_{t}(\alpha)x:=\frac{1}{t^{d}}\int_{0}^{t}\dots\int_{0}^{t}\alpha_{(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})}(x)\,ds_{1}\dots ds_{d},\quad x\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M}).

For any commuting tuple of operators
\mathbf{T}=(T_{1},\dots,T_{d})
in
\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\mathcal{M}))
we define

A_{n}(\mathbf{T})x:=\frac{1}{n^{d}}\sum_{j_{1}=0}^{n-1}\dots\sum_{j_{d}=0}^{n-1}T_{1}^{j_{1}}\dots T_{d}^{j_{d}}(x),\quad x\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M}).

Proposition 9.1
.

Let
\alpha:\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}\to\mathcal{L}(L_{p}(\mathcal{M}))
be a strongly continuous uniformly bounded semigroup of operators such that there exists a positive constant
C>0
so that for all nonnegative integers
\mathbf{k}:=(k_{1},\dots,k_{d})

(9.1)

\sup_{(n_{i})_{i}}\Big\|\big(A_{n_{i+1}}(\mathbf{T}_{\frac{1}{\mathbf{k}}})x-A_{n_{i}}(\mathbf{T}_{\frac{1}{\mathbf{k}}})x\big)_{i}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})}\leq C\,\|x\|_{p},\quad\forall x\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M}).

where the supremum is being taken over all increasing sequences of positive integers
(n_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,\infty)
and

\mathbf{T}_{\frac{1}{\mathbf{k}}}:=(\alpha_{(\frac{1}{k},\dots,0)},\dots,\alpha_{(0,\dots,\frac{1}{k}})).
Then

(9.2)

\sup_{(t_{i})_{i}}\Big\|\big(A_{t_{i+1}}(\alpha)x-A_{t_{i}}(\alpha)x\big)_{i}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})}\leq C\,\|x\|_{p},\quad\forall x\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M}).

where the supremum being taken over all increasing sequences
(t_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,\infty)

Proof.

Let
0<t_{1}<\dots<t_{m}
be a finite increasing sequence.
It suffices to prove (
9.2
) with a constant independent of the choice of the sequence. For any
t>0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, set
n_{k}(t):=\lfloor kt\rfloor,
where
\lfloor s\rfloor
denotes the greatest integer not exceeding
s
.

We first prove the following approximation.
For every
t>0
and
x\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
, we claim that

(9.3)

\big\|A_{n_{k}(t)}(\mathbf{T}_{1/\mathbf{k}})x-A_{t}(\alpha)x\big\|_{p}\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as }k\to\infty.

Indeed, by the strong continuity and uniform boundedness of
\alpha
,
the Riemann integral

\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\cdots\!\int_{0}^{t}\alpha_{(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})}(x)\,ds_{1}\cdots ds_{d}

is approximated by the Riemann sums

\frac{1}{k^{d}}\sum_{j_{1}=0}^{n_{k}(t)-1}\!\cdots\!\sum_{j_{d}=0}^{n_{k}(t)-1}\alpha_{\left(\frac{j_{1}}{k},\dots,\frac{j_{d}}{k}\right)}(x)=A_{n_{k}(t)}(\mathbf{T}_{1/\mathbf{k}})x.

Since
|n_{k}(t)/k-t|\to 0
as
k\to\infty
, the convergence in (
9.3
) follows.

To this end, we observe by using the triangle inequality, we have

\displaystyle\Big\|\big(A_{t_{i+1}}(\alpha)x-A_{t_{i}}(\alpha)x\big)_{i=0}^{m-1}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\Big\|\big(A_{t_{i+1}}(\alpha)x-A_{t_{i}}(\alpha)x-\big(A_{n_{k}(t_{i+1})}(\mathbf{T}_{1/\mathbf{k}})x-A_{n_{k}(t_{i})}(\mathbf{T}_{1/\mathbf{k}})x\big)\big)_{i=0}^{m-1}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})}

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big\|\big(A_{n_{k}(t_{i+1})}(\mathbf{T}_{1/\mathbf{k}})x-A_{n_{k}(t_{i})}(\mathbf{T}_{1/\mathbf{k}})x\big)_{i=0}^{m-1}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})}.

By the discrete assumption of the proposition, i.e. (
9.2
) the second term is bounded by
C\|x\|_{p}
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\Big\|\big(A_{t_{i+1}}(\alpha)x-A_{t_{i}}(\alpha)x\big)_{i=0}^{m-1}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\Big\|\big(A_{t_{i+1}}(\alpha)x-A_{t_{i}}(\alpha)x-\big(A_{n_{k}(t_{i+1})}(\mathbf{T}_{1/\mathbf{k}})x-A_{n_{k}(t_{i})}(\mathbf{T}_{1/\mathbf{k}})x\big)\big)_{i=0}^{m-1}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})}+C\|x\|_{p}.

We now handle the error term.
By the noncommutative Khintchine inequality and the convergence (
9.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\Big\|\big(A_{t_{i+1}}(\alpha)x-A_{t_{i}}(\alpha)x-\big(A_{n_{k}(t_{i+1})}(\mathbf{T}_{1/\mathbf{k}})x-A_{n_{k}(t_{i})}(\mathbf{T}_{1/\mathbf{k}})x\big)\big)_{i=0}^{m-1}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\Big\|\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}\otimes\Big(A_{t_{i+1}}(\alpha)x-A_{t_{i}}(\alpha)x-\big(A_{n_{k}(t_{i+1})}(\mathbf{T}_{1/\mathbf{k}})x-A_{n_{k}(t_{i})}(\mathbf{T}_{1/\mathbf{k}})x\big)\Big)\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\Omega)\,\overline{\otimes}\,\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sum_{i}\Big\|A_{t_{i+1}}(\alpha)x-A_{t_{i}}(\alpha)x-\big(A_{n_{k}(t_{i+1})}(\mathbf{T}_{1/\mathbf{k}})x-A_{n_{k}(t_{i})}(\mathbf{T}_{1/\mathbf{k}})x\big)\Big\|_{p}\;\longrightarrow\;0,\quad\text{as }k\to\infty.

Combining the above estimates and letting
k\to\infty
, we obtain

\Big\|\big(A_{t_{i+1}}(\alpha)x-A_{t_{i}}(\alpha)x\big)_{i=0}^{m-1}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{rc})}\leq C\|x\|_{p}.

Since the constant
C
is independent of the finite sequence
(t_{i})_{i=0}^{m}
, the desired supremum bound in (
9.1
) follows.
This completes the proof.
∎

, Lemma 7.3]

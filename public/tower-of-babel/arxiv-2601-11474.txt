The resultant divisor is negative
Olivier Benoist

Abstract
. Fix two integers 1 ≤ d < e 1\leq d<e . We study the birational geometry of a parameter space for pairs of homogeneous polynomials of degrees d d and e e in two variables (in which the higher degree polynomial is well defined only up to a multiple of the lower degree polynomial). We show that one can run the MMP on this space, and that it eventually contracts the resultant divisor.



1.
Introduction

Studying the birational geometry of moduli or parameter spaces, especially by running the minimal model program (MMP) on them, is now a classical theme in algebraic geometry. The archetypal example is the Hassett–Keel program for running the MMP on the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
of stable curves of genus
g
, which has recently been completed for
g\leq 4
(see
[
23
,
24
,
10
,
1
,
3
]
).

In this article, we consider and solve a similar problem for pairs of homogeneous polynomials of distinct degrees in two variables. We prove that one can run the MMP on an appropriate parameter space for such pairs, and that this MMP eventually contracts the resultant divisor parameterizing noncoprime polynomials. This demonstrates the existence of projective curves avoiding the resultant divisor.

1.1.
The resultant divisor

Let
k
be a field. For
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
V_{l}:=H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}}(l))
be the space of degree
l
homogeneous polynomials in two variables. Let
{P_{l}:=\mathbb{P}(V_{l})}
be its projectivization. We let
\langle F\rangle\in P_{l}
be the point induced by a nonzero
F\in V_{l}
. For
F\in V_{l}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
\langle F\rangle_{m}\subset V_{m}
be the space of degree
m
multiples of
F
.

Fix two integers
1\leq d<e
. Let
\widetilde{\Delta}_{d,e}\subset P_{d}\times P_{e}
be the subvariety parameterizing those
(\langle F\rangle,\langle G\rangle)
such that
F
and
G
are not coprime (i.e., such that
F
and
G
have a common zero on
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
). This subvariety is the zero locus of the classical
resultant polynomial

R_{d,e}\in H^{0}(P_{d}\times P_{e},\mathcal{O}_{P_{d}\times P_{e}}(e,d))
(a polynomial in the coefficients of
F
and
G
vanishing exactly when
F
and
G
are not coprime). It follows that
\widetilde{\Delta}_{d,e}
is an ample divisor in
P_{d}\times P_{e}
. In particular, any projective curve in
P_{d}\times P_{e}
meets
\widetilde{\Delta}_{d,e}
.

The purpose of this article is to show that, in spite of this, the resultant has strong negativity properties. It turns out that
P_{d}\times P_{e}
is not the optimal parameter space to consider, because the coprimality of a pair
{(\langle F\rangle,\langle G\rangle)\in P_{d}\times P_{e}}
depends on
G
only up to a multiple of
F
. To take this into account, we introduce the projective bundle
P_{d,e}\to P_{d}
whose fiber over
\langle F\rangle
is the projectivization
\mathbb{P}(V_{e}/\langle F\rangle_{e})
of the space of degree
e
polynomials up to a multiple of
F
. The point of
P_{d,e}
associated with a nonzero
G\in V_{e}/\langle F\rangle_{e}
will be denoted by
[F,G]
. The divisor
\widetilde{\Delta}_{d,e}
induces a divisor
\Delta_{d,e}\subset P_{d,e}
, which we call the
resultant divisor
. Set
U_{d,e}:=P_{d,e}\setminus\Delta_{d,e}
.

The most striking consequence of our results is the following theorem:

Theorem 1.1

(Corollary
5.9
)
.

The variety
U_{d,e}
admits a projective compactification
X_{d,e}
whose boundary
X_{d,e}\setminus U_{d,e}
has codimension
2
in
X_{d,e}
.

Taking appropriate linear sections of
X_{d,e}
, we deduce the following.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
6.1
)
.

The variety
U_{d,e}
is covered by projective curves.

We do not know how to construct projective curves avoiding the resultant divisor
\Delta_{d,e}
directly (except in particular cases, e.g. when
e=d+1
, see Remark
6.2
). Let us emphasize the concrete meaning of Theorem
1.2
. It states that one can find many one-parameter algebraic families of pairs
[F,G]
of polynomials of degrees
d
and
e
, such that
F
and
G
do not acquire a common root under any degeneration.

1.2.
Running the MMP

Let us now explain the strategy of our proof of Theorem
1.1
.
To construct
X_{d,e}
from
P_{d,e}
, we need to somehow contract the resultant divisor
\Delta_{d,e}
. However, the birational map
P_{d,e}\dashrightarrow X_{d,e}
is not a morphism in general: the divisor
\Delta_{d,e}
can only be contracted after an appropriate birational modification of
X_{d,e}
. The best one can hope for is to perform a series of flips (birational modifications in codimension
\geq 2
) on
P_{d,e}
, after which it may become possible to contract the strict transform of
\Delta_{d,e}
.

Such a sequence of flips should be the outcome of running the MMP on
P_{d,e}
. As
P_{d,e}
has Picard rank
2
(except when
d=1
, see §
2.3
), there are two directions in which one could run the MMP (this is a so-called
2
-ray game, see e.g.
[
13
, §2.2]
or
[
9
, §2.F]
). One of the two directions yields the projection
P_{d,e}\to P_{d}
, and it is the other direction that could eventually contract the resultant divisor.

The MMP cannot be applied to all smooth projective varieties. In our situation, it is possible to run it successfully on
P_{d,e}
if and only if
P_{d,e}
is a Mori dream space in the sense of
[
25
, Definition 1.10]
(see
[
25
, Proposition 1.11]
). Our main theorem states that this is the case, gives a full description of the loci flipped by the MMP applied to
P_{d,e}
, and shows that the divisor
\Delta_{d,e}
eventually gets contracted.

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
5.11
)
.

The following assertions hold.

(i)

The variety
P_{d,e}
is a Mori dream space.

(ii)

The effective cone
\mathrm{Eff}(P_{d,e})
of
P_{d,e}
is generated by the pullback
\mathcal{O}_{P_{d,e}}(1,0)
of
\mathcal{O}_{P_{d}}(1)
to
P_{d,e}
and by the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{P_{d,e}}(\Delta_{d,e})
.

(iii)

The MMP for
P_{d,e}
flips
W_{d,e}^{i}:=\{[F,G]\in P_{d,e}\mid\deg(\gcd(F,G))\geq d-i\}
for
1\leq i\leq d-2
and eventually contracts
W_{d,e}^{d-1}=\Delta_{d,e}
.

(iv)

The output of the MMP for
P_{d,e}
is a projective compactification
X_{d,e}
of
U_{d,e}
, whose boundary has codimension
2
, and that admits a stratification whose strata are isomorphic to
(U_{d-j,e+j})_{0\leq j<d}
.

The low dimension and inductive structure of the boundary of
X_{d,e}
is reminiscent of Baily–Borel compactifications of hermitian locally symmetric spaces.

1.3.
Constructing birational models

We take a concrete approach to Theorem
1.1
, giving direct constructions of the varieties appearing in the MMP for
P_{d,e}
.

In Section
3
, we introduce the variety
\widehat{P}_{d,e}
obtained from
P_{d,e}
by blowing up successively the strict transforms of the
(W_{d,e}^{u})_{1\leq u<d}
. The strict transform
{\widehat{E}_{d,e}^{u}\subset\widehat{P}_{d,e}}
of the exceptional divisor of the
u
-th blow-up is isomorphic to
\widehat{P}_{d-u,e+u}\times\widehat{P}_{u,e-d+u}
(see Theorem
3.1

(ii)
and Remark
3.6
(i)). This surprising structural result for
\widehat{E}_{d,e}^{u}
allows for arguments by induction on
d
that are at the heart of our proofs.

Associating with
[F,G]\in U_{d,e}
the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-invariant subscheme
\{F=G=0\}
of
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}
yields a rational map
\rho_{d,e}:\widehat{P}_{d,e}\dashrightarrow\mathrm{Hilb}_{d,e}
to a multigraded Hilbert scheme
\mathrm{Hilb}_{d,e}
in the sense of
[
20
]
(see §
4.1
). In Section
4
, we study the variety
\mathrm{Hilb}_{d,e}
in detail. For each
l\geq 0
, sending
Z\in\mathrm{Hilb}_{d,e}
to the space of degree
l
equations of
Z
yields a morphism from
\mathrm{Hilb}_{d,e}
to a Grassmannian, hence to a semi-ample bundle on
\mathrm{Hilb}_{d,e}
which we compute (see Proposition
4.8
). Using these semi-ample line bundles and leveraging our excellent understanding of the geometry of
\widehat{P}_{d,e}
, we deduce that the rational map
\rho_{d,e}:\widehat{P}_{d,e}\dashrightarrow\mathrm{Hilb}_{d,e}
is an isomorphism (see Theorem
4.11
).

The pull-backs by
\rho_{d,e}
of our semi-ample line bundles give rise to birational models of
P_{d,e}
constructed as contractions of
\widehat{P}_{d,e}
. In Section
5
, we study these birational models and we show that they realize the MMP for
P_{d,e}
(see (
5.8
)). This leads to a proof of Theorem
1.3
.

1.4.
The Mori dream space property and invariant theory

More conceptual criteria exist for determining whether a given smooth projective variety is a Mori dream space. We have been unable to use them to establish Theorem
1.3

(i)
.

First, any log Fano variety (say, over
k=\mathbb{C}
) is a Mori dream space by
[
8
, Corollary 1.3.2]
.
We have been unable to use such a criterion to prove Theorem
1.3

(i)
. This is related to the fact that we need to run the MMP backwards: the small contractions to be flipped contract
K
-positive instead of
K
-negative curves.

Second, a smooth projective variety
X
(say, with torsion-free Picard group) is a Mori dream space
if and only if its Cox ring
\mathrm{Cox}(X):=\bigoplus_{\mathcal{L}\in\mathrm{Pic}(X)}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})
is finitely generated (see
[
25
, Proposition 2.9]
). The Cox ring of
P_{d,e}
is easy to describe. Recall that
V_{l}=H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}}(l))
for
l\geq 0
. Let the additive group
V_{e-d}
act on the affine algebraic variety
V_{d}\times V_{e}
by
H\cdot(F,G)=(F,G+HF)
. At least when
{d>1}
(see Remark
6.4
), the natural rational map
{V_{d}\times V_{e}\dashrightarrow P_{d,e}}
identifies
\mathrm{Cox}(P_{d,e})
with the ring of invariants
{\mathcal{O}(V_{d}\times V_{e})^{V_{e-d}}}
. This gives a reformulation of Theorem
1.3

(i)
in the framework of Hilbert’s
14
th problem.

We have been unable to use this criterion to prove Theorem
1.3

(i)
. Instead, we deduce from Theorem
1.3

(i)
the following result pertaining to invariant theory.

Theorem 1.4

(Theorem
6.3
)
.

The
k
-algebra of invariants of the action of
V_{e-d}
on
{V_{d}\times V_{e}}
given by
H\cdot(F,G)=(F,G+HF)
is finitely generated.

Algebras of invariants of reductive group actions are always finitely generated. Theorem
1.4
features an additive group action, for which no general result is available (see Nagata’s counterexample to Hilbert’s
14
th problem
[
31
]
).

1.5.
A comparison with
(\mathbb{P}^{2}_{k})^{[n]}

We briefly compare the birational geometry of
P_{d,e}
and of the Hilbert scheme
(\mathbb{P}^{2}_{k})^{[n]}
of length
n
subschemes of
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{k}
(when
k=\mathbb{C}
). These varieties are related because the modification
\widehat{P}_{d,e}
of
P_{d,e}
introduced in §
1.3
can be realized as a closed subvariety of
(\mathbb{P}^{2}_{k})^{[de]}
(see Theorem
4.11
and Remark
4.3
).

Unlike
P_{d,e}
in general, the variety
(\mathbb{P}^{2}_{k})^{[n]}
is always log Fano (see
[
2
, Theorem 2.5]
).
It therefore follows from
[
8
, Corollary 1.3.2]
that
(\mathbb{P}^{2}_{k})^{[n]}
is a Mori dream space. As explained in §
1.4
, we are not aware of such a direct argument to prove Theorem
1.3

(i)
according to which
P_{d,e}
is a Mori dream space.

As
(\mathbb{P}^{2}_{k})^{[n]}
has Picard rank
2
by
[
17
]
, one can run its MMP in two directions. One of the two directions yields a divisorial contraction: the Hilbert–Chow morphism
[
16
, p. 516]
. It is the other direction that is interesting (it is described in great detail in
[
2
, §10]
when
n\leq 9
, see
[
28
, Theorem 1.2]
for general
n
).

Finally, the nontrivial boundary of
\mathrm{Eff}(P_{d,e})
is generated by the resultant divisor
\Delta_{d,e}
(see Theorem
1.3

(ii)
). In contrast, the nontrivial boundary of
\mathrm{Eff}((\mathbb{P}^{2}_{k})^{[n]})
is difficult to describe and depends on
n
in a complicated fashion (see
[
26
, Theorem 1.4 and Table 1 p. 59]
).

1.6.
The higher-dimensional case

The questions that are solved in this article for homogeneous polynomials in two variables are also interesting in more variables.

Fix
n\geq 1
and
1\leq d<e
. Set
P^{(n)}_{d}:=\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}}(d)))
. Let
{P^{(n)}_{d,e}\to P^{(n)}_{d}}
be the projective bundle whose fiber over

\langle F\rangle
is
\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k},\mathcal{O}(e))/\langle F\rangle_{e})
. As above, we use the notation
[F,G]
for points of
P^{(n)}_{d,e}
. We let
\Delta^{(n)}_{d,e}\subset P^{(n)}_{d,e}
be the
discriminant divisor
parameterizing those
[F,G]\in P^{(n)}_{d,e}
such that
\{F=G=0\}
is not a smooth complete intersection of codimension
2
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}
.
Define
U^{(n)}_{d,e}:=P^{(n)}_{d,e}\setminus\Delta^{(n)}_{d,e}
.

When
n=1
, the discriminant divisor is exactly the resultant divisor. It therefore makes sense to ask if our main results admit generalizations for arbitrary
n\geq 1
.

Question 1.5
.

Is some Zariski-open subset
{V\subset U^{(n)}_{d,e}}
covered by projective curves?

Question 1.6
.

Is the variety
P^{(n)}_{d,e}
a Mori dream space?

Question 1.7
.

Is the divisor
\Delta^{(n)}_{d,e}
on the boundary of the effective cone of
P^{(n)}_{d,e}
?

A more optimistic version of Question
1.5
would require that
V=U^{(n)}_{d,e}
.

When
n=3
and
(d,e)=(2,3)
, Questions
1.6
and
1.7
were first considered in
[
10
, §1.3]
in relation with the Hassett–Keel program in genus
4
.

In the direction of Question
1.7
, it is known that the divisor
\Delta^{(n)}_{d,e}
is never ample (see
[
6
, Remarque 2.9]
).

Positive answers to Questions
1.6
and
1.7
would give rise to a positive answer to Question
1.5
.
To see it, use Question
1.6
to run the MMP on
P^{(n)}_{d,e}
. If the last step of this MMP is a divisorial contraction, then Question
1.7
implies that it contracts (the strict transform of)
\Delta_{d,e}^{(n)}
. Curves constructed as general linear sections of the resulting variety then answer Question
1.5
. If the last step of this MMP is a fibration, then Question
1.7
implies that this fibration is induced by the line bundle associated with (the strict transform of)
\Delta_{d,e}^{(n)}
. Curves constructed as linear sections in a general fiber of this fibration then answer Question
1.5
.

Questions
1.5
to
1.7
have positive answers when
n=1
(see Theorem
1.3
) and when
{d=1}
and
n\geq 2
(the MMP for
P_{d,e}^{(n)}
can then be realized as a variation of GIT in the sense of
[
32
]
for the diagonal action of
\mathrm{SL}_{n}
on
P^{(n-1)}_{e}\times\mathbb{P}(M_{n+1,n})
).

When
d\geq 2
, we expect that the last step of the MMP for
P^{(n)}_{d,e}
(whose existence is predicted by Question
1.6
) is a divisorial contraction (contracting the discriminant divisor in view of Question
1.7
). This would answer positively the next question.

Question 1.8
.

Assume that
d\geq 2
.
Does some Zariski-open subset
V\subset U^{(n)}_{d,e}
admit a projective compactification whose boundary has codimension
\geq 2
?

Again, one could even hope that choosing
V=U^{(n)}_{d,e}
in Question
1.8
works.

When
n=3
, a positive answer to Question
1.8
would show the existence of nonisotrivial complete families of smooth curves in
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{k}
, thereby solving a classical problem (see
[
11
,
12
]
,
[
21
, p. 57]
and
[
7
]
).

1.7.
Other values of the degrees

One can consider further extensions of these questions. Fix degrees
{1\leq d_{1}\leq\dots\leq d_{c}}
. Let
U^{(n)}_{d_{1},\dots,d_{c}}
be the variety parameterizing those
(F_{1},\dots,F_{c})
in
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{c}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}},\mathcal{O}(d_{i}))
such that
\{F_{1}=\dots=F_{c}=0\}
is smooth of codimension
c
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}
, where
F_{i}
is well defined up to multiples of the
F_{j}
of degree
\leq d_{i}
and up to scalar multiplication. (The variety
U^{(n)}_{d_{1},\dots,d_{c}}
can be realized as an open subset of a multigraded Hilbert scheme parameterizing
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-invariant subschemes of
\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{k}
, by adapting the construction explained in §
1.3
.)

When the
d_{i}
are all equal with common value
d
(e.g. in the
{c=1}
case), the variety
U^{(n)}_{d_{1},\dots,d_{c}}
is the complement of the discriminant locus in the Grassmannian
{\mathrm{Grass}\big(c,H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}}(d))\big)}
. Unless
d=1
and
c<n+1
, this locus is a nonempty divisor (see
[
5
, Remarque 1.1]
), hence an ample divisor, so
U^{(n)}_{d_{1},\dots,d_{c}}
is affine. In particular, the variety
U^{(n)}_{d_{1},\dots,d_{c}}
cannot contain projective curves. This contrasts with Theorem
1.2
. As far as we know, whenever the degrees are not all equal, the variety
U^{(n)}_{d_{1},\dots,d_{c}}
might be covered by projective curves.

The case
c=2
and
d_{1}<d_{2}
is particular because the variety
U^{(n)}_{d_{1},d_{2}}
admits a very simple compactification
P^{(n)}_{d_{1},d_{2}}
with which one can work. In general, we are not aware of the existence of such a concrete compactification.

2.
Generalities and notation

In this section, we introduce the variety
P_{d,e}
which is our main object of study.

2.1.
Conventions

We fix a field
k
. A
variety
over
k
is a separated scheme of finite type over
k
. A
curve
is a variety of pure dimension
1
over
k
.

2.2.
The variety
P_{d,e}

Set
V_{l}:=H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}}(l))
for
l\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
P_{l}:=\mathbb{P}(V_{l})
be its projectivization. If
F\in V_{l}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, we let
\langle F\rangle_{m}\subset V_{m}
be the space of multiples of
F
of degree
m
. If
F
is nonzero, we write
\langle F\rangle
instead of
\langle F\rangle_{l}
for the line spanned by
F
, which we view as a point of
P_{l}
.

For
1\leq d<e
, the vector bundle
\mathcal{E}_{d,e}
over
P_{d}
whose fiber over
\langle F\rangle
is
V_{e}/\langle F\rangle_{e}
fits into a short exact sequence

(2.1)

0\to V_{e-d}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{P_{d}}(-1)\xrightarrow{\cdot F}V_{e}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{P_{d}}\to\mathcal{E}_{d,e}\to 0

(see e.g.
[
6
, (1) p. 1753]
). Let
P_{d,e}\to P_{d}
be the associated projective bundle.
We let
[F,G]\in P_{d,e}
be the point associated with nonzero
F\in V_{d}
and
G\in V_{e}/\langle F\rangle_{e}
.

Let
\Delta_{d,e}\subset P_{d,e}
be the subset of all
[F,G]\in P_{d,e}
such that
F
and
G
are not coprime. It is an irreducible divisor, which is empty if and only if
d=1
.

2.3.
Line bundles on
P_{d,e}

The Picard group
\mathrm{Pic}(P_{d,e})
of
P_{d,e}
is generated by the pull-back
\mathcal{O}_{P_{d,e}}(1,0)
of the ample tautological bundle on
P_{d}
and by the relatively ample tautological bundle
\mathcal{O}_{P_{d,e}}(0,1)
of the projective bundle
P_{d,e}\to P_{d}
. All line bundles on
P_{d,e}
are therefore of the form
\mathcal{O}_{P_{d,e}}(l,m)
for some
l,m\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Unless
d=1
, the line bundles
\mathcal{O}_{P_{d,e}}(1,0)
and
\mathcal{O}_{P_{d,e}}(0,1)
are linearly independent, and the smooth projective variety
P_{d,e}
has Picard rank
2
.

If
d=1
, the morphism
{P_{1,e}\to P_{1}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}}
is an isomorphism,
so
P_{1,e}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
has Picard rank
1
. To compute the relation between
\mathcal{O}_{P_{1,e}}(1,0)
and
\mathcal{O}_{P_{1,e}}(0,1)
in this case, note that
\mathcal{O}_{P_{1,e}}(0,-1)\simeq\mathcal{E}_{1,e}
and take the determinant of (
2.1
) to show that

(2.2)

\mathcal{O}_{P_{1,e}}(0,-1)\simeq\mathcal{E}_{1,e}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{P_{1,e}}(e,0)\textrm{ \hskip 3.00003ptin\hskip 3.00003pt }\mathrm{Pic}(P_{1,e}).

2.4.
Multiplication maps

For
0\leq u\leq d<e
, we define the multiplication map

(2.3)

\mu_{d,e}^{u}:P_{d-u}\times P_{u,e-d+u}\to P_{d,e}

by
\mu_{d,e}^{u}(\langle A\rangle,[B,C])=[AB,AC]
. Let
W_{d,e}^{u}\subset P_{d,e}
be the image of
\mu_{d,e}^{u}
endowed with its reduced structure. One has
W^{0}_{d,e}=\varnothing
and
W^{d}_{d,e}=P_{d,e}
. Note that
W_{d,e}^{d-1}=\Delta_{d,e}
.

It will sometimes be easier to work on
P_{d}\times P_{e}
rather than on
P_{d,e}
. For this reason, we introduce the morphisms
\widetilde{\mu}_{d,e}^{u}:P_{d-u}\times P_{u}\times P_{e-d+u}\to P_{d}\times P_{e}

given by
\widetilde{\mu}_{d,e}^{u}(\langle A\rangle,\langle B\rangle,\langle C\rangle)=(\langle AB\rangle,\langle AC\rangle)
and define
\widetilde{W}_{d,e}^{u}
to be the image of
\widetilde{\mu}_{d,e}^{u}
.

3.
Blowing up
P_{d,e}

In this section, we construct and describe a modification
\widehat{P}_{d,e}
of

3.1.
The variety
\widehat{P}_{d,e}

Fix
1\leq d<e
. For
0\leq u<d
, we let
\beta^{u}P_{d,e}
be the variety obtained by blowing up in
P_{d,e}
first
W_{d,e}^{1}
, then the strict transform of
W_{d,e}^{2}
, etc., and lastly the strict transform of
W_{d,e}^{u}
. We define
\widehat{P}_{d,e}:=\beta^{d-1}P_{d,e}
.

For
0\leq u<v<d
, we let
\beta^{u}W_{d,e}^{v}
be the strict transform of
W_{d,e}^{v}
in
\beta^{u}P_{d,e}
. We also let
\beta^{u}\mu_{d,e}^{v}:P_{d-v}\times\beta^{u}P_{v,e-d+v}\dashrightarrow\beta^{u}P_{d,e}
be the rational map induced by
\mu_{d,e}^{v}
(see (

)). It will be shown to be a morphism in Proposition

below. We define
\widehat{\mu}_{d,e}^{\,v}:=\beta^{v-1}\mu_{d,e}^{v}
.

For
1\leq u<d
, we let
\alpha^{u}_{d,e}:\beta^{u}P_{d,e}\to\beta^{u-1}P_{d,e}
be the blow-up of
\beta^{u-1}W^{u}_{d,e}
, with exceptional divisor
E_{d,e}^{u}\subset\beta^{u}P_{d,e}
. For
1\leq v\leq u<d
, we let
\beta^{u}E^{v}_{d,e}
be the strict transform of
E^{v}_{d,e}
in
\beta^{u}P_{d,e}
. We define
{\widehat{E}_{d,e}^{u}:=\beta^{d-1}E_{d,e}^{u}\subset\widehat{P}_{d,e}}

When this cannot cause any confusion, we write
\mathcal{O}_{X}(l,m)
or
\mathcal{O}(l,m)
for the pull-back of the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{P_{d,e}}(l,m)
on any blow-up

of

(such as

).

Our goal is the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Fix
1\leq d<e
.

(i)

The variety
\widehat{P}_{d,e}
is smooth and the
(\widehat{E}_{d,e}^{u})_{1\leq u<d}
form a strict normal crossings divisor in it.

(ii)

For
1\leq u<d
, there is a canonical isomorphism

(3.1)

\varphi_{d,e}^{u}:\widehat{P}_{d-u,e+u}\times\widehat{P}_{u,e-d+u}{\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}\widehat{E}_{d,e}^{u}.

Let
p_{1}:\widehat{E}_{d,e}^{u}\to\widehat{P}_{d-u,e+u}
and
p_{2}:\widehat{E}_{d,e}^{u}\to\widehat{P}_{u,e-d+u}
be the projections.

(iii)

If
v<u
, then
\widehat{E}_{d,e}^{v}|_{\widehat{E}_{d,e}^{u}}=p_{2}^{*}\widehat{E}_{u,e-d+u}^{v}
as Cartier divisors.

(iv)

If
v>u
, then
\widehat{E}_{d,e}^{v}|_{\widehat{E}_{d,e}^{u}}=p_{1}^{*}\widehat{E}_{d-u,e+u}^{v-u}
as Cartier divisors.

(v)

One has
\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{P}_{d,e}}(\widehat{E}_{d,e}^{u})|_{\widehat{E}_{d,e}^{u}}\simeq p_{1}^{*}\mathcal{O}(1,-1)\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(1,1)(-\sum_{w=1}^{u-1}\widehat{E}_{u,e-d+u}^{w})

(vi)

One has
\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{P}_{d,e}}(l,m)|_{\widehat{E}_{d,e}^{u}}\simeq p_{1}^{*}\mathcal{O}(l+m,0)\otimes p_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}(l,m)

Proposition 3.2

For

\displaystyle(BH+AI,CH+AJ)

Lemma 3.4

Fix

We resume the proof of Proposition

).

and

∎

4.
The multigraded Hilbert scheme

In this section, we give an interpretation of
\widehat{P}_{d,e}
as a mutigraded Hilbert scheme in the sense of
[
20
]
(see Theorem
4.11
).

4.1.
The variety
\mathrm{Hilb}_{d,e}

Let
\mathbb{G}_{m}
act on
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}
by homotheties.
Let
Z\subset\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}
be a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-invariant closed subscheme.
The Hilbert function
l\mapsto\mathrm{HF}_{Z}(l)
of
Z
associates with
l\in\mathbb{Z}
the dimension of the subspace of
V_{l}
consisting of equations of
Z
. Our convention differs from
[
20
]
, which considers the dimension of the quotient (it is more convenient for us to manipulate equations of
Z
rather than functions on
Z
).

For
1\leq d<e
, we consider the function
l\mapsto\mathrm{HF}_{d,e}(l)
defined by:

(4.1)

\mathrm{HF}_{d,e}(l)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}0&\textrm{ if }\hskip 3.00003ptl\leq d-1,\\
l+1-d&\textrm{ if }\hskip 3.00003ptd\leq l\leq e-1,\\
2l+2-d-e&\textrm{ if }\hskip 3.00003pte\leq l\leq d+e-1,\\
l+1&\textrm{ if }\hskip 3.00003ptd+e\leq l.\end{array}\right.

Lemma 4.1
.

Fix
1\leq d<e
. Choose
[F,G]\in U_{d,e}
. Set
Z:=\{F=G=0\}\subset\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}
.
Then
\mathrm{HF}_{Z}(l)=\mathrm{HF}_{d,e}(l)
for all
l\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

As
[F,G]\in U_{d,e}
, one can consider the Koszul exact sequence associated with the regular sequence
(F,G)
on
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}
. It reads:

(4.2)

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}}\xrightarrow{(G,-F)}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}}^{\oplus 2}\xrightarrow{(F,G)}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}}\to\mathcal{O}_{Z}\to 0.

Taking global sections in (
4.2
) and retaining the terms of appropriate homogeneous degrees shows that
\mathrm{HF}_{Z}(l)=\dim_{k}(V_{l-d})+\dim_{k}(V_{l-e})-\dim_{k}(V_{l-d-e})
for all
l\in\mathbb{Z}
. Computing this function shows that it coincides with
\mathrm{HF}_{d,e}(l)
.
∎

Let
\mathrm{Hilb}_{d,e}
be the multigraded Hilbert scheme parameterizing
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-invariant subschemes
of
\mathbb{A}_{k}^{2}
with Hilbert function
\mathrm{HF}_{d,e}
, as defined and constructed in
[
20
, Theorem 1.1]
. We use the same notation for a point of
\mathrm{Hilb}_{d,e}
and for the associated subscheme of
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}
.
Let
\sigma_{d,e}:U_{d,e}\to\mathrm{Hilb}_{d,e}
be the morphism associating with
[F,G]\in U_{d,e}
the subscheme
\{F=G=0\}\subset\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}
(see Lemma
4.1
).

Proposition 4.2
.

Fix
1\leq d<e
. The morphism
\sigma_{d,e}
realizes
\mathrm{Hilb}_{d,e}
as a smooth projective compactification of
U_{d,e}
. There exists a morphism
{\pi_{d,e}:\mathrm{Hilb}_{d,e}\to P_{d,e}}
that restricts to the identity on
U_{d,e}
.

Proof.

The variety
\mathrm{Hilb}_{d,e}
is projective by
[
20
, Corollary 1.2]
and smooth and connected by

[
15
, Theorem 1]
or
[
29
, Theorem 1.1]
(these facts appear in
[
27
, Theorems 2.9 and 3.13]
with the caveat that Iarrobino ignores nilpotents).

For
Z\in\mathrm{Hilb}_{d,e}
, it follows from (
4.1
) that
Z
satisfies a unique nonzero equation
F\in V_{d}
(up to multiplication by scalar), and a unique nonzero equation
G\in V_{e}
(up to a multiple of
F
and multiplication by a scalar). One can therefore define
\pi_{d,e}:\mathrm{Hilb}_{d,e}\to P_{d,e}
by the formula
\pi_{d,e}(Z)=[F,G]
.

If
\pi_{d,e}(Z)=[F,G]
is in
U_{d,e}
, then
Z=\{F=G=0\}
(because
Z\subset\{F=G=0\}
and
\mathrm{HF}_{Z}=\mathrm{HF}_{\{F=G=0\}}
by Lemma
4.1
). The section
\sigma_{d,e}
of
\pi_{d,e}
above
U_{d,e}
therefore shows that
\pi_{d,e}
is an isomorphism above
U_{d,e}
. It follows that
\pi_{d,e}
is birational and that
\mathrm{Hilb}_{d,e}
is a smooth compactification of
U_{d,e}
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

The subschemes of
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}
parameterized by
\mathrm{Hilb}_{d,e}
satisfy all the equations of degree
\geq d+e
by (
4.1
), so they are supported on the origin. We may thus view them as finite subschemes of
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{k}
. The induced morphism
{\mathrm{Hilb}_{d,e}\to\mathrm{Hilb}(\mathbb{P}^{2}_{k})}
is a monomorphism, as one sees from the description of the functors of points of these two schemes, hence a closed immersion by
[
19
, Corollaire 18.12.6]
.

4.2.

Proposition 5.5

Proof.

The middle vertical arrow of (

We used the following lemma.

Lemma 5.6

6.1.
Projective curves avoiding the resultant divisor

Using Proposition

Theorem 6.1
.

The variety

Proof.

By Corollary

, the variety

Remark 6.2
.

I do not know how to construct curves as in Theorem

\geq 2

(6.1)

\mathcal{O}(V_{d}\times V_{e})^{V_{e-d}}{\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}\mathcal{O}(V_{d}^{*}\times V_{e})^{V_{e-d}}\simeq\mathcal{O}(V_{d,e}).

be the complement of the zero section in

. As

(6.2)

\mathcal{O}(V_{d,e}){\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}\mathcal{O}(V_{d,e}^{*})

\mathrm{Cox}(P_{d,e}){\mathrel{\to}^{\,\sim\,}}\mathcal{O}(V_{d,e}^{*}).

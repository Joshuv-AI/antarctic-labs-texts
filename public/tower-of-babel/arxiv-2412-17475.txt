Strange shadows of ℓ p \ell_{p} -balls
Zakhar Kabluchko, Mathias Sonnleitner

Abstract
. We prove a large deviations principle for orthogonal projections of the unit ball 𝔹 p n \mathbb{B}_{p}^{n} of ℓ p n \ell_{p}^{n} onto a random k k -dimensional linear subspace of ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} as n → ∞ n\to\infty in the case 2 < p ≤ ∞ 2<p\leq\infty and for the intersection of 𝔹 p n \mathbb{B}_{p}^{n} with a random k k -dimensional subspace in the case 1 ≤ p < 2 1\leq p<2 . The corresponding rate function is finite only on L q L_{q} -zonoids and their duals, respectively, and given in terms of the maximum entropy over suitable measures generating the L q L_{q} -zonoid, where 1 p + 1 q = 1 \frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1 . In particular, we obtain that the renormalized projections/sections almost surely tend to a k k -dimensional Euclidean ball of certain radius. Moreover, we identify the asymptotic probability that the random orthogonal projection remains within a ball of smaller radius. As a byproduct we obtain an interesting inequality for the Gamma function.

1.
Introduction and main results

Let
\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|^{p}\leq 1\}
be the unit ball of
\ell_{p}^{n}
,
1\leq p<\infty
, and
\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}=[-1,1]^{n}
. Via orthogonal projection onto a
k
-dimensional subspace,
k\leq n
, the
\ell_{p}
-ball
\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}
casts a
k
-dimensional shadow. This terminology is also used in Ball’s
Shadows of Convex Bodies

[
7
]
, where the author bounds the volume of a convex body by an average of volumes of some of its shadows. By a theorem of Dvoretzky-Milman
[
5
, Cor. 7.24]
, as the dimension
n
grows, most such shadows tend to be very close to the Euclidean ball
\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}
, after suitable renormalization. Thus, we might say that
\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}
is the typical shadow of
\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}
. We are interested in shadows which are unlikely,
strange
so to speak, occurring with exponentially small (in
n
) probability. This can be investigated within the framework of large deviations theory.

A classical result, which goes back to Maxwell, Borel and Poincaré, states that the coordinates of a random vector
X_{n}
which is uniformly distributed in
n^{1/2}\mathbb{B}_{2}^{n}
become approximately Gaussian as
n\to\infty
. Due to
[
46
,
47
]
this also holds true for
n^{1/p}\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}
but in this case the limit density is proportional to
e^{-|x|^{p}/p}
for
p<\infty
and the uniform density on
[-1,1]
for
p=\infty
. In contrast, Klartag’s central limit theorem for convex bodies
[
24
]
shows that for any fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
most
k
-dimensional projections of
X_{n}
become approximately Gaussian. Thus, in some sense, if
p\neq 2
, coordinate projections of
X_{n}
are atypical. From a large deviations perspective this atypicality has been investigated in
[
15
]
and
[
16
]
, which motivated large deviation theorems for norms of
X_{n}
in
[
2
,
19
]
and triggered a body of work, partly summarized in the surveys
[
43
,
45
]
. For large deviations of random projections of the cube
\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}
, two more perspectives in terms of random measures have been considered in
[
17
,
20
]
. On a related note, in
[
41
]
a central limit theorem is proven for the volume of random projections of
\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n}
. This has recently been extended to
p>1
in
[
44
]
where also large deviation principles are proven. In the following, we provide yet another large deviation perspective of random projections of
\ell_{p}
-balls, which is in terms of the shape of the random projections of
\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}
itself, rather than a distribution defined on it. This allows for a detailed description of the set of strange shadows of
\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}
.

More precisely, we are interested in large deviations of the sequence of random
k
-dimensional sets

Z_{n,p}:=n^{1/p-1/2}\Pi_{n,k}^{\top}\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n},\qquad n\geq k,

where
k\in\mathbb{N}
is fixed and
\Pi_{n,k}
is a random
n\times k
-matrix whose columns form an orthonormal
k
-frame uniformly distributed on the Stiefel manifold
\mathbb{V}_{n,k}
, the set of all such frames in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with respect to Haar measure. That is, the first column
u_{1}
of
\Pi_{n,k}
is uniformly distributed on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, the second column
u_{2}
is uniformly distributed on
u_{1}^{\perp}\cap\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, and so on. Then
E_{n,k}=\Pi_{n,k}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
is a random
k
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
uniformly distributed on the Grassmannian
\mathbb{G}_{n,k}
, the set of all such subspaces, with respect to Haar measure. Moreover, the composition of the isometry
\Pi_{n,k}^{\top}\colon E_{n,k}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
and the orthogonal projection
P_{E_{n,k}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to E_{n,k}
onto
E_{n,k}
satisfies
\Pi_{n,k}^{\top}P_{E_{n,k}}=\Pi_{n,k}^{\top}
. Thus, the action of
\Pi_{n,k}^{\top}
can indeed be understood as taking orthogonal projection onto a random subspace and then mapping isometrically to
\mathbb{R}^{k}
.

The object
Z_{n,p}
is a random element of the space
\mathcal{K}^{k}
of convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
, that is, nonempty compact convex sets, which is equipped with the Hausdorff metric

\delta_{H}(K,L)=\max\Big\{\sup_{x\in K}\inf_{y\in L}\|x-y\|,\sup_{x\in L}\inf_{y\in K}\|x-y\|\Big\},\quad K,L\in\mathcal{K}^{k}.

As a random linear image of
\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}
, the set
Z_{n,p}
is a random compact set taking values in
\mathcal{K}^{k}
in the sense of
[
33
]
and
[
37
]
. Note that for our purposes a convex body is a nonempty compact convex set which may have empty interior, see
[
48
]
.

The following class of convex bodies will be essential for the study of the random convex body
Z_{n,p}
. Let
1<p\leq\infty
and let
1\leq q<\infty
be the Hölder-conjugate index with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
, where
\frac{1}{\infty}=0
. An
L_{q}
-zonoid (also sometimes called
L_{q}
-centroid body) is a convex body
K\in\mathcal{K}^{k}
with support function

h(K,u):=\sup_{x\in K}\langle x,u\rangle=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}|\langle x,u\rangle|^{q}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big)^{1/q},\qquad u\in\mathbb{R}^{k},

(1)

for some Borel probability measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
, where

\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})=\Big\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{k})\colon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\|x\|_{2}^{q}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)<\infty\Big\}

and
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
is the set of all Borel probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. In this case, we write
K=Z_{q}(\mu)
for the
L_{q}
-zonoid generated by
\mu
and we denote by

\mathrm{Zon}_{k,q}=\{Z_{q}(\mu)\colon\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})\}

the set of all
L_{q}
-zonoids in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Then the random set
Z_{n,p}
is a random
L_{q}
-zonoid, i.e. takes values in
\mathrm{Zon}_{k,q}
, see Proposition
20
below.

We will prove a large deviation principle (LDP) for
k
-dimensional random projections and sections of
\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}
in the complete separable metric space
(\mathcal{K}^{k},\delta_{H})
. Here, a sequence of random convex bodies
(K_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies an LDP with lower semicontinuous rate function
I\colon\mathcal{K}^{k}\to[0,\infty]
if for all Borel sets
A\subset\mathcal{K}^{k}
it holds that

\displaystyle-\inf_{K\in\Int A}I(K)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}n^{-1}\log\mathbb{P}[K_{n}\in A]

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}n^{-1}\log\mathbb{P}[K_{n}\in A]\leq-\inf_{K\in\cl A}I(K),

where
\Int A
(respectively,
\cl A
) denotes the interior (respectively, closure) of
A
and
\inf\emptyset=\infty
. A rate function is good if it additionally has compact sublevel sets. We refer to
[
12
]
for a background on the theory of large deviations. The rate functions in our main results involve the (differential) entropy which, for
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
, is given by

{\rm Ent}(\mu)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\log(f(x))f(x)\mathrm{d}x,

whenever
\mu
admits a Lebesgue density
f
and
{\rm Ent}(\mu)=-\infty
otherwise. Our main result for random projections of
\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}
reads as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Let
2<p\leq\infty
and let
1\leq q<2
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
. The sequence
Z_{n,p}=n^{1/p-1/2}\Pi_{n,k}^{\top}\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}
,
n\geq k
, satisfies an LDP in
\mathcal{K}^{k}
with good rate function

I_{p}(K)=\inf\{\mathrm{Ent}(\gamma^{\otimes k})-\mathrm{Ent}(\mu)\colon\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})\text{ with }Z_{q}(\mu)=K\text{ and }Z_{2}(\mu)\subset\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}\},

where
K\in\mathcal{K}^{k}
and
\gamma^{\otimes k}
denotes the
k
-dimensional standard Gaussian distribution.

Large deviation principles for Minkowski sums of i.i.d. random compact sets have been proven in
[
8
]
, and more recently in
[
27
,
35
,
36
]
. In contrast, the setting studied here is related to
q
-sums of random convex sets, see Remark
21
. Our approach to prove Theorem
1
is based on a large deviation principle for the empirical measure of the rows of the random matrix
\Pi_{n,k}^{\top}
, recently established by Kim and Ramanan in
[
23
, Thm. 2.8]
.

Remark 2
.

Theorem
1
yields a description of the set of strange shadows. Let
2<p\leq\infty
and
K\in\mathcal{K}^{k}
with
0<I_{p}(K)<\infty
with
I_{p}
as in Theorem
1
. Then
K
belongs to
\mathrm{Zon}_{k,q}
and admits an absolutely continuous generating measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
with
Z_{q}(\mu)=K
and
Z_{2}(\mu)\subset\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}
. The set of all such
K
can be interpreted as the set of all possible shadows of the ball
\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}
that occur with probability exponentially small in
n
. (Shadows with
I_{p}(K)=\infty
occur with probability super-exponentially small in
n
.) Further, the body
K
has nonempty interior, see e.g. Proposition
17
below. By Lyapunov’s inequality, we have
Z_{q}(\mu)\subset Z_{2}(\mu)
and thus
K\subset\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}
. Additionally, if
K=\alpha\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}
for some
\alpha\in(0,1]
, then it holds that
\alpha\leq a_{k,q}^{1/q}\sqrt{k}<1
, where
a_{k,q}
is as in (
22
) in Section
5
below, see Remark
28
.

The geometric condition
Z_{2}(\mu)\subset\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}
in Theorem
1
is equivalent to

\mathrm{Cov}(\mu):=[\mathrm{Cov}_{X\sim\mu}(X_{i},X_{j})]_{i,j=1}^{k}\leq I_{k\times k},

(2)

where
X\sim\mu
means that
X
has distribution
\mu
,
\leq
denotes the Loewner order on the cone of positive semi-definite matrices and
I_{k\times k}
the identity matrix. It is well known that
\gamma^{\otimes k}
is the unique maximum entropy distribution given (
2
) and that
\mathrm{Ent}(\gamma^{\otimes k})=\frac{k}{2}\log(2\pi e)
, see also Section
5
. Therefore, for
K\in\mathcal{K}^{k}
,

I_{p}(K)=\frac{k}{2}\log(2\pi e)-\sup\{\mathrm{Ent}(\mu)\colon\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})\text{ with }Z_{q}(\mu)=K,Z_{2}(\mu)\subset\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}\},

(3)

and it holds that
I_{p}(K)\geq 0
with
I_{p}(K)=0
if and only if
K=Z_{q}(\gamma^{\otimes k})
. Rotational invariance of
\gamma^{\otimes k}
implies that
Z_{q}(\gamma^{\otimes k})=m_{q}\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}
with

m_{q}:=(\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\gamma}|X|^{q})^{1/q}=\sqrt{2}\bigg(\frac{\Gamma(\frac{q+1}{2})}{\sqrt{\pi}}\bigg)^{1/q},

(4)

where
\gamma
is the one-dimensional standard Gaussian distribution and
\Gamma(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{x-1}e^{-t}\mathrm{d}t
,
x>0
, denotes the Gamma function.

Together with the LDP in Theorem
1
we obtain the following corollary on the convergence of the random shadows
Z_{n,p}
,
n\geq k
, to the typical shadow
m_{q}\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}
.

Corollary 3
.

Let
2<p\leq\infty
. The sequence
Z_{n,p}=n^{1/p-1/2}\Pi_{n,k}^{\top}\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}
,
n\geq k
, satisfies

Z_{n,p}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\rm a.s.}m_{q}\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k},

(5)

where almost sure convergence is in Hausdorff distance and
m_{q}
is as in (
4
).

Remark 4
.

In the case of
p=\infty
, Corollary
3
was obtained in
[
20
, Thm. 1.1]
, where in Rem. 1.2 a proof using the Dvoretzky-Milman theorem in the form of
[
5
, Cor. 7.24]
is sketched. The proof of
[
20
, Rem. 1.2]
can be extended to show that (
5
) holds also for
1<p<\infty
. In this respect, note that the Dvoretzky-Milman theorem yields
c,c^{\prime}>0
such that for any convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with nonempty interior,
\varepsilon>0
and
k\leq c\varepsilon^{2}k_{*}
, it holds that, with probability at least
1-e^{-c^{\prime}\varepsilon^{2}k_{*}}
,

(1-\varepsilon)w(K)\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}\subset\Pi_{n,k}^{\top}K\subset(1+\varepsilon)w(K)\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k},

Here,
k_{*}
is the critical/Dvoretzky dimension of the polar of
K
and
w(K)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h(K,u)\mathrm{d}\sigma(u)
is the half-mean width, where
\sigma
is the rotation invariant probability measure on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. It is known (see e.g.
[
5
, Sec. 7.2.4]
) that, for
g_{1},\dots,g_{n}\overset{\rm i.i.d.}{\sim}\gamma
,

w(n^{1/p-1/2}\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n})=\frac{\mathbb{E}(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|g_{i}|^{q})^{1/q}}{\mathbb{E}(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|g_{i}|^{2})^{1/2}}\to\frac{m_{q}}{m_{2}}=m_{q},

(6)

as
n\to\infty
and that in this case
k_{*}
grows like
n^{\min\{1,2/q\}}
. For
1<p<\infty
and fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we can thus take
\varepsilon>0
arbitrarily small as
n\to\infty
, and we obtain (
5
) with a Borel-Cantelli argument.

By duality, we can obtain a result for the intersection of
\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}
with a random
k
-dimensional subspace
E_{n,k}
, uniformly distributed on the Grassmannian
\mathbb{G}_{n,k}
with respect to Haar measure. Let
\mathcal{K}^{k}_{(o)}\subset\mathcal{K}^{k}
be the set of convex bodies containing the origin in their interior. For any
K\in\mathcal{K}^{k}_{(o)}
, the polar or dual body is defined by

K^{\circ}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{k}\colon\langle x,y\rangle\leq 1\text{ for all }y\in K\}.

By Proposition
23
below, it holds that

K_{n,q}:=\Pi_{n,k}^{\top}(n^{1/q-1/2}\mathbb{B}_{q}^{n}\cap E_{n,k})\overset{\rm d}{=}Z_{n,p}^{\circ},\qquad n\geq k,

(7)

where
\Pi_{n,k}^{\top}\colon E_{n,k}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
is an isometry and the polar
Z_{n,p}^{\circ}
is well-defined almost surely. The following LDP is our main result for random sections.

Theorem 5
.

Let
1\leq q<2
and
2<p\leq\infty
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
. The sequence
K_{n,q}=\Pi_{n,k}^{\top}(n^{1/q-1/2}\mathbb{B}_{q}^{n}\cap E_{n,k})
,
n\geq k
, satisfies an LDP in
\mathcal{K}^{k}_{(o)}
with good rate function

J_{q}(K)=I_{p}(K^{\circ}),

where
K\in\mathcal{K}^{k}_{(o)}
with polar
K^{\circ}\in\mathcal{K}^{k}_{(o)}
.

As a consequence of
(m_{q}\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k})^{\circ}=m_{q}^{-1}\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}
we obtain the following corollary on the convergence of the random sections
K_{n,q}
,
n\geq k
, to the typical section
m_{q}^{-1}\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}
.

Corollary 6
.

Let
1\leq q<2
. The sequence
K_{n,q}=\Pi_{n,k}^{\top}(n^{1/q-1/2}\mathbb{B}_{q}^{n}\cap E_{n,k})
,
n\geq k
, satisfies

K_{n,q}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\rm a.s.}m_{q}^{-1}\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k},

where almost sure convergence is in Hausdorff distance and
m_{q}
is as in (
4
).

Again, we may deduce this from the Dvoretzky-Milman theorem stated in its dual form for random sections. Note that a refinement for random sections of
\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}
is given in
[
42
]
.

Remark 7
.

We can infer from the LDPs in Theorems
1
and
5
as well as the strong laws of large numbers in Corollaries
3
and
6
corresponding results for continuous functionals
L\colon\mathcal{K}^{k}_{(o)}\to\mathbb{R}
, via the contraction principle and the continuous mapping theorem, respectively. Examples include intrinsic volumes or diameter. Note that independently of us large deviations results for functionals of random projections and sections of
\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}
are obtained in
[
44
]
using different techniques. Central limit theorems for the volume of random sections of fixed codimension have been established in
[
1
]
.

Remark 8
.

Comparing the volume of random with extremal sections, it is due to Nazarov and proved in
[
9
]
that, for every
2
-dimensional linear subspace
H_{n,2}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\mathrm{vol}_{2}(n^{1/2}\mathbb{B}_{1}^{n}\cap H_{n,2})\geq\frac{n^{3}\sin^{3}(\frac{\pi}{2n})}{\cos(\frac{\pi}{2n})},

with equality when
\mathbb{B}_{1}^{n}\cap H_{n,2}
is a regular
2n
-gon. From above, we have the bound
\mathrm{vol}_{2}(\mathbb{B}_{1}^{n}\cap H_{n,2})\leq\mathrm{vol}_{2}(\mathbb{B}_{1}^{2})=2
, see
[
34
, Thm. II.2]
. As
n\to\infty
, a regular
2n
-gon tends to an Euclidean ball and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{vol}_{2}(n^{1/2}\mathbb{B}_{1}^{n}\cap H_{n,2})\geq\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)^{3}.

It follows from Corollary
6
and the continuous mapping theorem that

\mathrm{vol}_{2}(n^{1/2}\mathbb{B}_{1}^{n}\cap E_{n,2})\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\rm a.s.}\mathrm{vol}_{2}(m_{1}^{-1}\mathbb{B}_{2}^{2})=\frac{\pi^{2}}{2},

which makes the volume of random sections only slightly larger than for the minimal section. There is a vast literature on extremal sections and projections of convex bodies, in particular the
\ell_{p}
-balls
\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}
, see the survey
[
39
]
.

Remark 9
.

In case of
k=1
and
1<p\leq\infty
, the random projection
Z_{n,p}
is given by

Z_{n,p}=[-W_{n,p},W_{n,p}]\quad\text{for }W_{n,p}=\frac{(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|g_{i}|^{q})^{1/q}}{(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|g_{i}|^{2})^{1/2}},

(8)

where
g_{1},\dots,g_{n}\overset{\rm i.i.d.}{\sim}\gamma
and
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
, see Proposition
20
below. In
[
19
, Sec. 5]
it is proven that the sequence
(W_{n,p})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies an LDP on
\mathbb{R}
. Namely, if
p>2
and thus
q<2
, then the rate function is given by virtue of Cramér’s theorem in terms of the Legendre-Fenchel transform of the log-moment generating function
\Lambda(t_{1},t_{2})=\log\mathbb{E}e^{t_{1}|g_{1}|^{q}+t_{2}|g_{1}|^{2}},t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
. If
1<p<2
and thus
2<q<\infty
, then
(W_{n,p})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, satisfies an LDP with speed
n^{2/q}
(replacing the prefactor
n
in the above definition of LDP) and rate function

I_{p}^{(1)}(z)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}(z^{q}-m_{q}^{q})^{2/q}&\colon z\geq m_{q},\\
\infty&\colon\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where
m_{q}
is as in (
4
). If
p=1
, then (
8
) holds with
W_{n,1}=\frac{\max_{1\leq i\leq n}|g_{i}|}{(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|g_{i}|^{2})^{1/2}}
. Similarly as in
[
6
]
one can show that
(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\log n}}W_{n,1})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies an LDP with speed
\log n
and rate function

I_{1}^{(1)}(z)=\begin{cases}z^{2}-1&\colon z\geq 1,\\
\infty&\colon\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Also in case of
k>1
, it seems possible to prove an LDP directly for the support function of
Z_{n,p}
, at least for
p>2
, however, with a less explicit rate function than in Theorem
1
.

Remark 10
.

Our results for random projections and sections hold for
2<p\leq\infty
and
1\leq q<2
, respectively. The case
p=q=2
is trivial since
Z_{n,2}=\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}
,
n\geq k
. In the complementary ranges
1<p<2
and
2\leq q<\infty
, respectively, we expect different behavior in the form of large deviations with speed
n^{2/q}
. This is suggested by the Dvoretzky-Milman theorem (see Remark
4
), the one-dimensional case (see Remark
9
) or LDPs for functionals such as
[
2
, Thm. 1.2]
. The cases
p=1
and
p=\infty
, respectively, closely resemble the setting of Gaussian polytopes but with only asymptotically independent points. This seems to require yet another approach.

As an application of the LDP in Theorem
1
we compute the asymptotic probability that the rescaled projection
Z_{n,p}
of
\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}
onto a random
k
-dimensional subspace lies within a Euclidean ball
\beta\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}
of small radius
\beta>0
. By Corollary
3
we have
Z_{n,p}\to m_{q}\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}
and thus for
\beta<m_{q}
the event
\{Z_{n,p}\subset\beta\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}\}
is rare and occurs with exponentially small probability as specified by the following theorem.

Theorem 11
.

Let
2<p\leq\infty
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
1\leq q<2
with
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p}=1
and

\beta_{k,q}=m_{q}\bigg(\frac{\frac{k}{q}\Gamma(\frac{k}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{k+q}{2})}\bigg)^{1/q}\bigg(\frac{\frac{k}{2}\Gamma(\frac{k}{q})}{\Gamma(\frac{k+2}{q})}\bigg)^{1/2}=\delta_{k,q}^{1/q}\bigg(\frac{k\Gamma(\frac{k}{q})}{\Gamma(\frac{k+2}{q})}\bigg)^{1/2},

where
m_{q}
is as in (
4

\delta_{k,q}
is defined by the above equation. Then, it holds that
\beta_{k,q}<m_{q}
and for every

\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{-1}\log\mathbb{P}[Z_{n,p}\subset\beta\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}]=c_{k,q,\beta},

where for

, we have

, while for
\beta\leq\beta_{k,q}
we have

where
\omega_{k}=\frac{2\pi^{k/2}}{\Gamma(\frac{k}{2})}
is the surface area of
\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}
.

In fact, the probability
\mathbb{P}[Z_{n,p}\subset\beta\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}]
is also exponentially vanishing for
\beta\in(\beta_{k,q},m_{q})
but the value of

remains undetermined, see Section

for the limit distribution of coordinates of a random vector uniformly distributed in an

-sphere and conditioned to lie in a small Euclidean ball. Note that

as

, and so the gap

.

-zonoids and their generating measures. Note that in

2.
L_{q}
-zonoids

In the following, we collect facts about
L_{q}
-zonoids and refer to
[
48
, Sec. 10.14]
and
[
4
, Sec. 10.4.2]
for more information and references. In particular, we refer to
[
26
]
for an approach through Fourier analysis, to
[
32
]
for volume estimates of
L_{q}
-zonoids and their polars and to
[
38
]
for a probabilistic interpretation of
L_{q}
-zonoids. If
q=1
, then
L_{1}
-zonoids are simply known as zonoids, see also
[
48
, Ch. 3.5]
, and for
q=\infty
one can define
Z_{\infty}(\mu)
as the convex hull of the support of
\mu
.

Let
1\leq q<\infty
. Recall that the set of all
L_{q}
-zonoids is denoted by

\mathrm{Zon}_{k,q}=\{Z_{q}(\mu)\colon\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})\}\subset\mathcal{K}^{k},

and for
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
the convex body
Z_{q}(\mu)
has support function

T_{q}(\mu)\colon\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R},\quad u\mapsto\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}|\langle x,u\rangle|^{q}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big)^{1/q},

(10)

whose
q
-th power is also called the
L_{q}
-cosine transform of
\mu
, see e.g.
[
31
]
. It is well-defined since
T_{q}(\mu)(u)\leq\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\|x\|^{q}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\big)^{1/q}\|u\|<\infty
for
u\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
. In fact, allowing only measures in
\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
is no restriction, which we show for completeness.

Lemma 14
.

Let
1\leq q<\infty
and
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
. If
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}|\langle x,u\rangle|^{q}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)<\infty
holds for every
u\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, then
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
.

Proof.

Take a maximal set
\mathcal{E}\subset\mathbb{S}^{k-1}
such that
\langle u,v\rangle\leq\frac{1}{2}
for every
u,v\in\mathcal{E}
with
u\neq v
, i.e. a spherical code of minimal angle
\arccos(\frac{1}{2})
, see
[
10
, Ch. 1, 2.3]
. For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{k}\setminus\{0\}
we find
v\in\mathcal{E}
such that
\langle\frac{x}{\|x\|},v\rangle>\frac{1}{2}
. Consequently, it holds that

\frac{1}{2^{q}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\|x\|^{q}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)<\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\sup_{v\in\mathcal{E}}|\langle x,v\rangle|^{q}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\leq\sum_{v\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}|\langle x,v\rangle|^{q}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)<\infty,

which gives
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
.
∎

In the following, we equip
\mathrm{Zon}_{k,q}
with the induced Hausdorff metric and
\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
with the
q
-Wasserstein metric, for which
\mu_{n}\to\mu
in
\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
if and only if
\mu_{n}\to\mu
weakly and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\|x\|_{2}^{q}\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\|x\|_{2}^{q}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)
.

Proposition 15
.

Let
1\leq q<\infty
. The map

Z_{q}\colon\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})\to\mathrm{Zon}_{k,q},\qquad\mu\mapsto Z_{q}(\mu),

is well-defined and continuous.

For the proof of Proposition
15
we need the following lemma, where we recall that a function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
is sublinear if it is positively homogenous and subadditive.

Lemma 16
.

Let
1\leq q<\infty
. For every
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
the function
T_{q}(\mu)\colon\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
defined in (
10
) is Lipschitz continuous and sublinear.

Proof.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
. Positive homogenity of
T_{q}(\mu)
is immediate. By the reverse triangle inequality in
L_{q}(\mu)
, for
u_{1},u_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
,

|T_{q}(\mu)(u_{1})-T_{q}(\mu)(u_{2})|\leq T_{q}(\mu)(u_{1}-u_{2})\leq\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\|x\|^{q}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big)^{1/q}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|,

which establishes subadditivity and Lipschitz continuity of
T_{q}(\mu)
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
15
.

Denote by
C(\mathbb{S}^{k-1})
the space of continuous functions on
\mathbb{S}^{k-1}
equipped with the uniform metric and by
C_{s}(\mathbb{S}^{k-1})
the set of
f\in C(\mathbb{S}^{k-1})
for which the extension
\tilde{f}(x)=\|x\|f(\frac{x}{\|x\|})
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{k}\setminus\{0\}
,
\tilde{f}(0)=0
, is sublinear. By Lemma
16
, for every
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
the restriction
T_{q}(\mu)|_{\mathbb{S}^{k-1}}
belongs to
C_{s}(\mathbb{S}^{k-1})
. Thus, the map
\tilde{T}_{q}\colon\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})\to C_{s}(\mathbb{S}^{k-1})
taking
\mu
to
T_{q}(\mu)|_{\mathbb{S}^{k-1}}
is well-defined.

We can then write
Z_{q}=I^{-1}\circ\tilde{T}_{q}
, where

\displaystyle I\colon\mathcal{K}^{k}\to C_{s}(\mathbb{S}^{k-1}),\qquad K\mapsto h(K,\cdot)|_{\mathbb{S}^{k-1}},

is an isometric bijection, see e.g.
[
48
, Ch. 1]
. Therefore, the composition
Z_{q}=I^{-1}\circ\tilde{T}_{q}
is well-defined and it only remains to prove continuity of
\tilde{T}_{q}\colon\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})\to C_{s}(\mathbb{S}^{k-1})
.

Let
\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\dots
with
\mu_{n}\to\mu_{0}
in
\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
, i.e.
\mu_{n}\to\mu_{0}
weakly and moreover the convergence
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\|x\|^{q}\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\|x\|^{q}\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}(x)
holds. Moreover, for every
u\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1}
, the pointwise convergence
\tilde{T}_{q}(\mu_{n})(u)\to\tilde{T}_{q}(\mu_{0})(u)
holds, see e.g.
[
49
, Ch. 6.8]
. By Lemma
16
, the functions
\tilde{T}_{q}(\mu_{0}),\tilde{T}_{q}(\mu_{1}),\dots
are Lipschitz-continuous on
\mathbb{S}^{k-1}
(with respect to the Euclidean metric) with Lipschitz-constant at most
M^{1/q}
where
M:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\|x\|^{q}\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)
is finite. By the Arzelà-Ascoli theorem, the sequence
(\tilde{T}_{q}(\mu_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is relatively compact in
C(\mathbb{S}^{k-1})
and uniform convergence
\tilde{T}_{q}(\mu_{n})\to\tilde{T}_{q}(\mu_{0})
follows from pointwise convergence. This proves Proposition
15
.
∎

We note the following consequence of Lemma
16
. Recall that for our purposes a convex body may have empty interior.

Proposition 17
.

For a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\mu
is not supported on a hyperplane,

(ii)

Z_{q}(\mu)
contains the origin in its interior,

(iii)

Z_{q}(\mu)
is a symmetric convex body with nonempty interior.

Proof.

(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii)
\colon
Suppose that
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
is supported on a hyperplane
H_{u}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{k}\colon\langle x,u\rangle=0\}
. By definition, the support function satisfies then
h(Z_{q}(\mu),\pm u)=0
, implying that
Z_{q}(\mu)\subset H_{u}
. Consequently, the interior of
Z_{q}(\mu)
is empty.

If, on the other hand,
Z_{q}(\mu)
does not contain the origin in its interior, then for arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
there is
u\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1}
with
h(Z_{q}(\mu),u)<\varepsilon
. This gives
\inf_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1}}h(Z_{q}(\mu),u)=0
. By Lemma
16
, the function
h(Z_{q}(\mu),\cdot)
is continuous and attains its infimum at some point
u_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1}
. Then
\mu
is supported in
H_{u_{0}}
. This completes the proof of the first equivalence.

(ii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii)
\colon
By Lemma
16
, the function
T_{q}(\mu)
is sublinear and thus the support function of a convex body given by
Z_{q}(\mu)
, which is symmetric. By symmetry, the
L_{q}
-zonoid
Z_{q}(\mu)
has nonempty interior if and only if
0\in\Int Z_{q}(\mu)
.
∎

The set
\mathrm{Zon}_{k,q}
of
L_{q}
-zonoids arises from the closure of the set of
L_{q}
-zonotopes, where
K\in\mathcal{K}^{k}
is called an
L_{q}
-zonotope if its support function is of the form

h(K,u)=\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\langle a_{i},u\rangle|^{q}\Big)^{1/q},\qquad u\in\mathbb{R}^{k},

(11)

for some
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
. This is a special case of (
1
) with discrete generating measure
\mu
. We can also interpret
K
as the
q
-sum of the line segments
[-a_{i},a_{i}]
,
i=1,\dots,n
, where the
q
-sum of convex bodies
K,L
containing the origin is the unique convex body with support function
u\mapsto(h(K,u)^{q}+h(L,u)^{q})^{1/q}
,
u\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, see
[
14
]
and
[
48
, Ch. 9.1]
. We observe that
L_{q}
-zonotopes correspond to linear images of
\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}
.

Lemma 18
.

Let
1<p\leq\infty
and
1\leq q<\infty
with
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p}=1
and
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
. For every
A\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times n}
the image
A\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}
is a convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
with support function of the form (
11
), where
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
are the columns of
A
. In particular, it is an
L_{q}
-zonoid with generating measure
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{n^{1/q}a_{i}}
.

Proof.

The support function of
A\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}
at
u\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
is given by

h(A\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n},u)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}}\langle x,A^{\top}u\rangle=\|A^{\top}u\|_{q}.

Denoting the columns of
A
by
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
, it follows for
\mu_{A}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{n^{1/q}a_{i}}
that

h(A\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n},u)=\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\langle a_{i},u\rangle|^{q}\Big)^{1/q}=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}|\langle x,u\rangle|^{q}{\rm d}\mu_{A}(x)\Big)^{1/q}.

∎

Remark 19
.

Lemma
18
admits a partial converse. Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
with

and

. If for some

-zonoid if and only if

or

, where

. In particular, for

, the set

is an

-zonoid if and only if

. This follows from the embedding results in
[

, Thm. 2.1]
together with
[

and Remark

.

3.
Large deviations in the Stiefel manifold and the proof of Theorem
1

In the following, we derive Theorem
1
from an LDP for the empirical measure of rescaled rows of the random Stiefel matrix
\Pi_{n,k}
. The following is a special case of Lemma
18
.

Proposition 20
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
n\geq k
. Every realization of
Z_{n,p}=n^{1/p-1/2}\Pi_{n,k}^{\top}\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}
is an
L_{q}
-zonoid with generating measure

L_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{\sqrt{n}v_{i}},

(12)

where
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
are the rows of
\Pi_{n,k}
.

Remark 21
.

Proposition
20
can be interpreted as
Z_{n,p}
being the
q
-sum of the random line segments
[-\sqrt{n}v_{i},\sqrt{n}v_{i}]
,
i=1,\dots,n
. Since these segments are identically distributed, one approach to an LDP for
(Z_{n,p})_{n\geq k}
may consist in extending existing LDPs for Minkowski sums to
q
-sums. However, the dependence between the rows
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
complicates such an approach.

Kim and Ramanan established in
[
23
]
that for every
q\in(0,2)
the sequence
(L_{n})_{n\geq k}
in (
12
) satisfies an LDP in the
q
-Wasserstein space
\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
equipped with the
q
-Wasserstein metric. Note that an LDP directly for the sequence
(\Pi_{n,k})_{n\geq k}
was proven in
[
18
]
. It is well-known that
\Pi_{n,k}\overset{\rm d}{=}G_{n,k}^{\top}(G_{n,k}G_{n,k}^{\top})^{-1/2}
, where
G_{n,k}
is a Gaussian random matrix with columns
g_{1},\dots,g_{n}\overset{\rm i.i.d.}{\sim}\gamma^{\otimes k}
, see e.g.
[
20
, Lem. 3.1]
. By the law of large numbers, the random matrix
\frac{1}{n}G_{n,k}G_{n,k}^{*}
tends almost surely to the identity matrix
I_{k\times k}
, as
n\to\infty
, and thus one may compare the behavior of
L_{n}
as in (
12
) to that of
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{g_{i}}
. By Sanov’s theorem, the latter sequence of empirical measures satisfies an LDP in
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
, equipped with the weak topology, with rate function at
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
given by the relative entropy

H(\mu|\gamma^{\otimes k})=\begin{cases}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\log(\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}\gamma^{\otimes k}})\mathrm{d}\mu&\colon\mu\ll\gamma^{\otimes k},\\
\infty&\colon\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

In contrast, the rate function corresponding to
L_{n}
is given by

\mathbb{H}_{k}(\mu)=\begin{cases}H(\mu|\gamma^{\otimes k})+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}(I_{k\times k}-\mathrm{Cov}(\mu))&\colon\mathrm{Cov}(\mu)\leq I_{k\times k},\\
\infty&\colon\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(13)

where
\mathrm{tr}(A)
denotes the trace of a matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
.

Proposition 22

(
[
23
, Thm. 2.8]
)
.

Let
q\in(0,2)
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, let
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
be the rows of a random matrix
\Pi_{n,k}
sampled according to the Haar measure on
\mathbb{V}_{n,k}
. Then, the sequence
(L_{n})_{n\geq k}
as in (
12
) satisfies an LDP in
\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
(equipped with the
q
-Wasserstein metric) with strictly convex good rate function
\mathbb{H}_{k}
.

We shall infer the LDP in Theorem
1
from Proposition
22
using continuity of the map
Z_{q}
taking the random measure
L_{n}
to the random convex body
Z_{q}(L_{n})=Z_{n,p}
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Let
2<p\leq\infty
and
1\leq q<2
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. Using the contraction principle (e.g.
[
12
, Thm. 4.2.1]
), we derive from Propositions
15
and
22
that the sequence
(Z_{n,p})_{n\geq k}
satisfies a large deviation principle in
\mathrm{Zon}_{k,q}
with rate function

I_{p}(K)=\inf\{\mathbb{H}_{k}(\mu)\colon K=Z_{q}(\mu)\},\qquad K\in\mathrm{Zon}_{k,q}.

(14)

Since
\mathrm{Zon}_{k,q}
is closed and
I_{p}(K)=\infty
for
K\in\mathcal{K}^{k}\setminus\mathrm{Zon}_{k,q}
, the LDP extends to
\mathcal{K}^{k}
, see
[
12
, Lem. 4.1.5]
.

In the following, we rewrite the rate function in (
14
) to the one in Theorem
1
. Plugging in the definition of
\mathbb{H}_{k}
from (
13
), we have
I_{p}(K)<\infty
precisely when
K=Z_{q}(\mu)
for a probability measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
which admits a Lebesgue density
f
and satisfies
\mathrm{Cov}(\mu)\leq I_{k\times k}
as well as
H(\mu|\gamma^{\otimes k})<\infty
. In this case, we compute

\mathbb{H}_{k}(\mu)=-\mathrm{Ent}(\mu)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\|x\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)+\frac{k}{2}(\log(2\pi)+1)-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{Cov}(\mu)).

Further, we note that

\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{Cov}(\mu))=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\|x\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}x_{i}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\Big)^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\|x\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)-\|\mathrm{bar}(\mu)\|^{2},

where
\mathrm{bar}(\mu)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}x\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)
is the barycenter of
\mu
. Together with
\mathrm{Ent}(\gamma^{\otimes k})=\frac{k}{2}\log(2\pi e)
we arrive at

\mathbb{H}_{k}(\mu)={\rm Ent}(\gamma^{\otimes k})-{\rm Ent}(\mu)+\frac{1}{2}\|\mathrm{bar}(\mu)\|^{2}.

The symmetrized version
\tilde{\mu}
of
\mu
has Lebesgue density
\tilde{f}(x)=\frac{f(x)+f(-x)}{2}
, satisfies
K=Z_{q}(\tilde{\mu})
and is centered, i.e.
\mathrm{bar}(\tilde{\mu})=0
. Further, by convexity of
x\mapsto x\log x
, it holds that

-{\rm Ent}(\tilde{\mu})\leq-{\rm Ent}(\mu),

and consequently
\mathbb{H}_{k}(\tilde{\mu})\leq\mathbb{H}_{k}(\mu)
. Thus, we can put

I_{p}(K)=\inf\{\mathrm{Ent}(\gamma^{\otimes k})-{\rm Ent}(\mu)\colon\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})\text{ with }Z_{q}(\mu)=K\text{ and }\mathrm{Cov}(\mu)\leq I_{k\times k}\}.

Finally, rewrite
\mathrm{Cov}(\mu)\leq I_{k\times k}
to
Z_{2}(\mu)\subset\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1
.
∎

4.
Duality and the proof of Theorem
5

We use basic facts about duality and functions representing convex sets, see also
[
48
]
. Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a convex body such that
0\in\Int K
, that is
K\in\mathcal{K}^{k}_{(o)}
. Then, its dual
K^{\circ}
is also a convex body with
0\in\Int K^{\circ}
. Recall that the support function of
K
is given by

h(K,u)=\sup_{x\in K}\langle x,u\rangle,\qquad u\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1},

and the radial function of
K
is defined by

\rho(K,u)=\sup\{r\geq 0:ru\in K\},\qquad u\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1}.

It is known that there is a duality between the support function and the reciprocal radial function, namely

h(K^{\circ},u)=1/\rho(K,u),\qquad u\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1}.

It is clear that the radial function determines
K
uniquely. As a consequence of the above duality, the support function also determines
K
uniquely. We have the following duality between sections and projections, see also
[
5
, Sec. 1.1.4]
.

Proposition 23
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a convex body such that
0\in\Int K
. Let also
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a linear subspace and
P_{L}
be the orthogonal projection onto
L
. Then, the convex bodies
K^{\circ}\cap L
and
P_{L}K
, considered within the ambient space
L
, are dual to each other.

Proof.

If we want to indicate that the support function is considered with respect to the ambient space
L
, we will denote it by
h_{L}
.
For
u\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1}\cap L
we have

\displaystyle h_{L}(P_{L}K,u)=\sup_{x\in P_{L}K}\langle x,u\rangle=\sup_{x\in K}\langle x,u\rangle=h_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}(K,u).

It follows that

h_{L}(P_{L}K,u)=h_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}(K,u)=1/\rho(K^{\circ},u)=1/\rho(K^{\circ}\cap L,u)=h_{L}((K^{\circ}\cap L)^{\circ}_{L},u),

where in the last term the subscript
L
indicates that the dual is taken within
L
.
Since the support function determines the convex body uniquely, we conclude that
P_{L}K
and
K^{\circ}\cap L
are dual to each other within
L
.
∎

We recall that since the random
k
-dimensional subspace
E_{n,k}
is uniformly distributed on the Grassmannian
\mathbb{G}_{n,k}
, we can put
E_{n,k}=\Pi_{n,k}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
and then
\Pi_{n,k}^{\top}E_{n,k}=\mathbb{R}^{k}
, where
\Pi_{n,k}
is uniformly distributed on
\mathbb{V}_{n,k}
. Almost surely,
\Pi_{n,k}
is of full rank, which we shall assume in the following.

Let
1\leq q<2
. Proposition
23
and the duality between
\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}
and
\mathbb{B}_{q}^{n}
imply

(n^{1/q-1/2}\mathbb{B}_{q}^{n}\cap E_{n,k})^{\circ}=n^{1/p-1/2}P_{E_{n,k}}\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n},

where the dual is taken with respect to the ambient space
E_{n,k}
. Since
(\Pi_{n,k}(\mathbb{R}^{k}))^{\perp}=\ker\Pi_{n,k}^{\top}
, it holds that
\Pi_{n,k}^{\top}P_{E_{n,k}}=\Pi_{n,k}^{\top}
, and we can apply the isometry
\Pi_{n,k}^{\top}\colon E_{n,k}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
, which commutes with duality, to both sides and arrive at

(\Pi_{n,k}^{\top}(n^{1/q-1/2}\mathbb{B}_{q}^{n}\cap E_{n,k}))^{\circ}=n^{1/p-1/2}\Pi_{n,k}^{\top}\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}=Z_{n,p}.

Polarity being an involution, we obtain (
7
), i.e., that
K_{n,q}=Z_{n,p}^{\circ}
, where
K_{n,q}
is as in Theorem
5
.

In the following, we establish continuity of the polar operation, and for this we need that convergence in Hausdorff distance implies uniform convergence of radial functions.

Lemma 24
.

Let
K,K_{1},K_{2},\dots\in\mathcal{K}^{k}_{(o)}
. If
K_{n}\to K
, then the uniform convergence
\rho(K_{n},\cdot)\to\rho(K,\cdot)
holds on
\mathbb{S}^{k-1}
.

Proof.

Let
0<r<R<\infty
be such that
B(0,2r)\subset K\subset B(0,R/2)
. Then for
n\geq n_{0}
we have
B(0,r)\subset K_{n}\subset B(0,R)
. Let
\varepsilon>0
be small and
\delta=\delta(r,R,\varepsilon)
be such that for any
\alpha\in(\varepsilon,1)
and
x\in B(0,R)
we have

B\Big(x-\alpha\frac{x}{\|x\|},2\delta\Big)\subset C_{x}:={\rm conv}(\{x\}\cup B(0,r)),

see e.g.
[
50
, Ch. 3.2]
. Intuitively, the cone
C_{x}
is not too narrow away from its apex. Assume that
|\rho(K_{n},u_{n})-\rho(K,u_{n})|>\varepsilon
for some
u_{n}\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1}
and
n\geq n_{0}
. It remains to show
\delta_{H}(K_{n},K)\geq\delta
.

In fact, suppose without loss of generality that
r\leq\rho(K_{n},u_{n})<\rho(K,u_{n})\leq R
. Further,
x_{n}:=\rho(K,u_{n})u_{n}\in\partial K
and
C_{x_{n}}\subset K
as well as
y_{n}:=\rho(K_{n},u_{n})u_{n}\in\partial K_{n}
. Moreover,
y_{n}=x_{n}-\alpha_{n}\frac{x_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|}
for some
\alpha_{n}=\rho(K,u_{n})-\rho(K_{n},u_{n})>\varepsilon
. The ball
B(y_{n},\delta)
is contained in the interior of
C_{x_{n}}
and thus of
K
. Therefore,
\delta_{H}(K_{n},K)=\delta_{H}(\partial K_{n},\partial K)\geq\delta
.
∎

Lemma 25
.

Polarity
\cdot^{\circ}\colon\mathcal{K}^{k}_{(o)}\to\mathcal{K}^{k}_{(o)}
is continuous with respect to Hausdorff distance.

Proof.

Assume
K,K_{1},K_{2},\dots
are in
\mathcal{K}^{k}_{(o)}
and that
K_{n}\to K
in Hausdorff distance. By Lemma
24
we have the uniform convergence
\rho(K_{n},\cdot)\to\rho(K,\cdot)
. Since
K\in\mathcal{K}^{k}_{(o)}
, we find
r>0
such that for large enough
n
and all
u\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1}
, we have
\rho(K_{n},u)\geq r
. Thus, we have uniform convergence of

h(K_{n}^{\circ},\cdot)=\rho(K_{n},\cdot)^{-1}\to\rho(K,\cdot)^{-1}=h(K^{\circ},\cdot),

showing that
K_{n}^{\circ}\to K^{\circ}
in Hausdorff distance.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
5
.

Proof of Theorem
5
.

Theorem
1
implies that for
2<p\leq\infty
the sequence
(Z_{n,p})_{n\geq k}
satisfies an LDP in
\mathcal{K}^{k}
with good rate function
I_{p}
as defined in Theorem
1
. As noted in Remark
2
, Proposition
17
implies that
\{K\in\mathcal{K}^{k}\colon I_{p}(K)<\infty\}\subset\mathcal{K}^{k}_{(o)}
. Further,
\mathcal{K}^{k}_{(o)}\subset\mathcal{K}^{k}
is measurable by way of
\mathcal{K}^{k}_{(o)}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\{K\colon B(0,\frac{1}{n})\subset K\}
, where the sets in the union are open (see the proof of
[
48
, Lem. 1.8.18]
). Thus, by
[
12
, Lem. 4.1.5]
the sequence
(Z_{n,p})_{n\geq k}
satisfies an LDP in
\mathcal{K}^{k}_{(o)}
with the same good rate function
I_{p}
.

Let
1\leq q<2
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. Then by (
7
) it holds that
K_{n,q}=Z_{n,p}^{\circ}
. The contraction principle implies that the sequence
(K_{n,q})_{n\geq k}
satisfies an LDP on
\mathcal{K}^{k}_{(o)}
with good rate function
J_{q}\colon\mathcal{K}^{k}_{(o)}\to[0,\infty]
defined by

J_{q}(K)=\inf\{I_{p}(L)\colon L\in\mathcal{K}^{k}_{(o)}\text{ with }L^{\circ}=K\}=I_{p}(K^{\circ}).

This completes the proof of Theorem
5
.
∎

5.
Maximum entropy and the proof of Theorem
11

Let
2<p\leq\infty
and thus
1\leq q<2
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. For
\beta>0
define the set

A_{\beta}=\{K\in\mathcal{K}^{k}\colon K\subseteq\beta\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}\}.

(15)

Observe that
A_{\beta}
is closed with interior
\Int A_{\beta}=\{K\in\mathcal{K}^{k}\colon K\subset\beta\Int\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}\}
. Once we can show that, for
I_{p}
as in Theorem
1
and
c_{k,q,\beta}
as in Theorem
11
,

\inf_{K\in\Int A_{\beta}}I_{p}(K)=\inf_{K\in A_{\beta}}I_{p}(K)=-c_{k,q,\beta},

(16)

then the limit in Theorem
11
follows from the large deviation principle in Theorem
1
applied to the Borel set
A_{\beta}\subset\mathcal{K}^{k}
. In the course of the proof of (
16
), we will give the proof of Proposition
13
, which implies the inequality
\beta_{k,q}<m_{q}
claimed in Theorem
11
.

By the alternative representation (
3
) of the rate function
I_{p}
we have

\inf_{K\in A_{\beta}}I_{p}(K)=\frac{k}{2}\log(2\pi e)-\sup_{\mu\in M_{\beta}}\mathrm{Ent}(\mu),

(17)

where

M_{\beta}=\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})\colon Z_{q}(\mu)\subset\beta\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}\text{ and }Z_{2}(\mu)\subset\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}\},

(18)

which may also be written as the set of probability measures
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}|\langle x,u\rangle|^{q}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\beta^{q},\qquad\text{for all }u\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1},

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}|\langle x,u\rangle|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq 1,\qquad\text{for all }u\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1}.

(19)

Analogously, we have

\inf_{K\in\Int A_{\beta}}I_{p}(K)=\frac{k}{2}\log(2\pi e)-\sup_{\mu\in M_{\beta}^{\prime}}\mathrm{Ent}(\mu),

(20)

with

M_{\beta}^{\prime}=\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})\colon Z_{q}(\mu)\subset\beta\Int\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}\text{ and }Z_{2}(\mu)\subset\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}\},

which can be written in terms of the constraints (
19
) with the first inequality replaced by a strict inequality.

The proof strategy for showing (
16
) is to compute the supremum
\sup_{\mu\in M_{\beta}}\mathrm{Ent}(\mu)
and to show that this value does not change if
M_{\beta}
is replaced by
M_{\beta}^{\prime}
. The following proposition shows that the supremum of
\mathrm{Ent}(\mu)
over
\mu\in M_{\beta}
is actually attained and the maximizer is unique if the supremum is finite. In the latter case we call the maximizer the maximum entropy distribution in
M_{\beta}
.

Proposition 26
.

For any
\beta>0
the set
M_{\beta}
is compact in
q
-Wasserstein distance and the supremum
\sup_{\mu\in M_{\beta}}\mathrm{Ent}(\mu)
is attained at some
\mu^{*}
. If the supremum is finite, then the maximizer is unique.

Proof.

For compactness, we observe that
M_{\beta}
is the intersection of

C=\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})\colon Z_{2}(\mu)\subset\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}\}=\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})\colon\mathrm{Cov}(\mu)\leq I_{k\times k}\}

with the preimage of the closed set
A_{\beta}
under the continuous map
Z_{q}\colon\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})\to\mathcal{K}^{k}
(see Proposition
15
). Thus, it is sufficient to establish compactness of
C
. This is a straightforward generalization of
[
22
, Lem. 3]
(recall that
q<2
). In particular, for tightness observe that any
\mu\in C
satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\|x\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{Cov}(\mu))\leq k.

Consequently, the set
M_{\beta}
is compact. Considering entropy as a function

\mathrm{Ent}\colon\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\},

it is upper semi-continuous with respect to
q
-Wasserstein topology, see e.g.
[
3
, Rem. 9.3.8]
, and therefore attains its maximum on the compact convex set
M_{\beta}
.

Let
\mu_{1}^{*},\mu_{2}^{*}\in M_{\beta}
be two maximizers of the entropy with
\mathrm{Ent}(\mu_{1}^{*})=\mathrm{Ent}(\mu_{2}^{*})<\infty
. Then
\mu_{1}^{*}
and
\mu_{2}^{*}
have Lebesgue densities
f_{1}^{*}
and
f_{2}^{*}
. Consider a measure
\mu_{3}^{*}\in M_{\beta}
with density
\frac{1}{2}(f_{1}^{*}+f_{2}^{*})
. By strict concavity of
x\mapsto-x\log x
we have
\mathrm{Ent}(\mu_{3}^{*})>\mathrm{Ent}(\mu_{1}^{*})
, which is a contradiction. Hence, the maximizer is unique.
∎

In the following, we compute the maximum entropy distribution
\mu^{*}
in
M_{\beta}
for
\beta>0
and the corresponding entropy, which will be shown to be finite. Using rotational symmetry of
M_{\beta}
, the computation of
\sup_{\mu\in M_{\beta}}\mathrm{Ent}(\mu)
will reduce to a one-dimensional problem with an explicit solution if
\beta\leq\beta_{k,q}
or
\beta\geq m_{q}
.

More precisely, for any orthogonal matrix
O
in the orthogonal group
\mathbb{O}_{k}
, we have
\mu\in M_{\beta}
if and only if
O\cdot\mu\in M_{\beta}
, where the rotated measure
O\cdot\mu
assigns to a Borel set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
the mass

(O\cdot\mu)(A):=\mu(\{O^{-1}x\colon x\in A\}).

Moreover, the rotation invariance of
M_{\beta}
transfers to the rotation invariance of the maximum entropy distribution
\mu^{*}
.

Lemma 27
.

If a compact convex set
M\subset\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
is invariant under rotations, then so is the maximum entropy distribution in
M
, provided that it has finite entropy.

Proof.

Since
M
is compact and convex, the maximizer of the entropy is unique, see the proof of Proposition
26
. If
\mu^{*}
is a maximizer, then for every
O\in\mathbb{O}_{k}
the measure
O\cdot\mu^{*}
is also a maximizer, proving that
O\cdot\mu^{*}=\mu^{*}
.
∎

By Lemma
27
, provided that its entropy is finite, the maximum entropy distribution
\mu^{*}\in M_{\beta}
is rotationally invariant, i.e. has a Lebesgue density
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
of the form
f\colon x\mapsto g(\|x\|)
for
g\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
with

1=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}f(x)\mathrm{d}x=\omega_{k}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{k-1}g(r)\mathrm{d}r=\int_{0}^{\infty}v(r)\mathrm{d}r,

where
v(r)=\omega_{k}r^{k-1}g(r)
. Again using polar integration, for any
q\geq 1
and
u\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}|\langle x,u\rangle|^{q}\mathrm{d}\mu^{*}(x)=a_{k,q}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{q}v(r)\mathrm{d}r,

(21)

where

a_{k,q}:=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{k-1}}|\langle z,u\rangle|^{q}\mathrm{d}\sigma(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{q+1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{k}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{k+q}{2})},

(22)

see e.g.
[
4
, p. 337]
,
[
26
, Lem. 3.12]
or
[
32
, p. 178]
.

Remark 28
.

We can now justify the final statement in Remark
2
. As a special case of the above, if
I_{p}(\alpha\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k})<\infty
for some
\alpha\in(0,1]
, then
\sup_{\mu\in\widetilde{M}_{\alpha}}\mathrm{Ent}(\mu)
is finite, where the set
\widetilde{M}_{\alpha}=\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})\colon Z_{q}(\mu)=\alpha\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}\text{ and }Z_{2}(\mu)\subset\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}\}
is compact, convex and invariant under rotations. By Lemma
27
, we find a maximum entropy distribution
\mu^{*}\in\widetilde{M}_{\alpha}
which gives rise to a density
v
of the above form. It follows from (
21
) that
\alpha^{q}=a_{k,q}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{q}v(r)\mathrm{d}r
and
1\geq a_{k,2}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2}v(r)\mathrm{d}r
. Since
a_{k,2}=\frac{1}{k}
, Lyapunov’s inequality gives
\alpha<a_{k,q}^{1/q}\sqrt{k}=\overline{a}_{k,q}m_{q}
, where
m_{q}
is as in (
4
) and

\overline{a}_{k,q}:=\bigg(\frac{\Gamma(\frac{k}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{k+q}{2})}\bigg)^{1/q}\Big(\frac{k}{2}\Big)^{1/2}=\bigg(\frac{\Gamma(\frac{k}{2}+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{k}{2}+\frac{q}{2})}\bigg)^{1/q}\Big(\frac{k}{2}\Big)^{1/2-1/q}>1,

by Gautschi’s inequality for the Gamma function. Note that Lyapunov’s inequality is strict since
v
is a density with respect to Lebesgue measure.

Rewriting the optimization problem
\sup_{\mu\in M_{\beta}}\mathrm{Ent}(\mu)
in terms of
v
, we want to find a density
v
on
[0,\infty)
solving

\displaystyle\max\qquad

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\infty}\log(v(r))v(r)\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{\infty}v(r)\log(r^{k-1})\mathrm{d}r+\log(\omega_{k})

subject to

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{q}v(r)\mathrm{d}r\leq\frac{\beta^{q}}{a_{k,q}}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2}v(r)\mathrm{d}r\leq k,

(23)

since
a_{k,2}=\frac{1}{k}
. The solution
v^{*}
to this optimization problem is known to be of the form

v^{*}(r)=\frac{1}{Z}r^{k-1}\exp(-\lambda_{1}r^{q}-\lambda_{2}r^{2}),\qquad r>0,

(24)

where
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\geq 0
are such that
v^{*}
satisfies the constraints in (
23
) and
Z
is a normalizing constant. Moreover, we have the ‘complementary slackness’ conditions

\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{q}v^{*}(r)\mathrm{d}r-\frac{\beta^{q}}{a_{k,q}}\Big)\lambda_{1}=0\quad\text{ and }\quad\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2}v^{*}(r)\mathrm{d}r-k\Big)\lambda_{2}=0.

(25)

This follows for example from the proof of
[
11
, Thm. 12.1.1]
(see also
[
22
, Lem. 6]
). We refer to
[
22
]
to a related geometric problem giving rise to a similar optimization problem.

Analogously, the optimization problem (
23
) without the second constraint is solved by
v^{*}
of the form (
24
) with
\lambda_{2}=0
and
\lambda_{1}>0
chosen in such a way that the first constraint is satisfied with equality. The formula

\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{a}e^{-br^{c}}\mathrm{d}r=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{a+1}{c})}{cb^{\frac{a+1}{c}}},\qquad\text{ for }a\geq 0\text{ and }b,c>0,

yields that the optimal
v^{*}
solving (
23
) without the second constraint is of the form

v^{*}(r)=\frac{1}{Z_{k,q,\beta}}r^{k-1}e^{-\lambda_{k,q,\beta}r^{q}}\quad\text{ with }Z_{k,q,\beta}=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{k}{q})}{q\lambda_{k,q,\beta}^{k/q}}\text{ and }\lambda_{k,q,\beta}=\frac{ka_{k,q}}{q\beta^{q}}.

(26)

Moreover, it holds that

\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2}v^{*}(r)\mathrm{d}r=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{k+2}{q})}{\Gamma(\frac{k}{q})}\lambda_{k,q,\beta}^{-2/q}=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{k+2}{q})}{\Gamma(\frac{k}{q})}\Big(\frac{ka_{k,q}}{q}\Big)^{-2/q}\beta^{2},

which is increasing in
\beta
and equal to
k
for the choice of
\beta=\beta_{k,q}
as in Theorem
11
. Note that
\delta_{k,q}
as in Theorem
11
satifies
\delta_{k,q}=\frac{k}{q}a_{k,q}
. In particular, if
\beta\leq\beta_{k,q}
, then
\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2}v^{*}(r)\mathrm{d}r\leq k
and
v^{*}
satisfies the constraints in (
23
) as well as maximizes entropy over all densities
v
satisfying the first constraint with equality. It also solves the original problem in (
23
) since any solution
v^{*}
to (
23
) with
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
satisfies, by (
25
), both constraints with equality. The corresponding maximum entropy is given by

-\int_{0}^{\infty}\log(v^{*}(r))v^{*}(r)\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{\infty}v^{*}(r)\log(r^{k-1})\mathrm{d}r+\log(\omega_{k})=\frac{k}{q}+\log(Z_{k,q,\beta})+\log(\omega_{k}).

Thus, we obtain the following partial solution to
\sup_{\mu\in M_{\beta}}\mathrm{Ent}(\mu)
.

Lemma 29
.

For any
0<\beta\leq\beta_{k,q}
, the maximum entropy distribution
\mu^{*}
in
M_{\beta}
has Lebesgue density

f^{*}\colon\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R},\qquad x\mapsto\frac{1}{\omega_{k}Z_{k,q,\beta}}e^{-\lambda_{k,q,\beta}\|x\|^{q}},

with
Z_{k,q,\beta}
and
\lambda_{k,q,\beta}
as above. It holds that

\mathrm{Ent}(\mu^{*})=k\log\beta+\log\Gamma\Big(\frac{k}{q}\Big)+\frac{k}{q}(1-\log\delta_{k,q})-\log q+\log\omega_{k},

where
\delta_{k,q}=\frac{k}{q}a_{k,q}
is as in Theorem
11
. Moreover, the set
A_{\beta}
defined in (
15
) is an
I_{p}
-continuity set, that is, the first equality in (
16
) holds.

Proof.

The form of the density of the maximum entropy distribution
\mu^{*}
in
M_{\beta}
is a consequence of the above discussion and the identity
f^{*}(x)=g(\|x\|)=\omega_{k}^{-1}\|x\|^{1-k}v^{*}(\|x\|)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{k}\setminus\{0\}
. We compute

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ent}(\mu^{*})

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{q}+\log(Z_{k,q,\beta})+\log(\omega_{k})

\displaystyle=k\log\beta+\log\Gamma\Big(\frac{k}{q}\Big)+\frac{k}{q}\Big(1-\log\frac{ka_{k,q}}{q}\Big)-\log q+\log\omega_{k},

which is continuous and increasing as a function of
\beta\in(0,\beta_{k,q}]
. This gives

\sup_{\mu\in M_{\beta}^{\prime}}\mathrm{Ent}(\mu)=\sup_{\gamma<\beta}\sup_{\mu\in M_{\gamma}}\mathrm{Ent}(\mu)=\sup_{\mu\in M_{\beta}}\mathrm{Ent}(\mu)=\mathrm{Ent}(\mu^{*}),

where
M_{\beta}^{\prime}
is as in (
20
). This proves the first equality in (
16
) via (
17
) and (
20
).
∎

We can now set, for
\beta\leq\beta_{k,q}
,

c_{k,q,\beta}:=k\log\beta-\frac{k}{2}\log(2\pi e)+\log\Gamma\Big(\frac{k}{q}\Big)+\frac{k}{q}(1-\log\delta_{k,q})-\log q+\log\omega_{k}

to obtain, by (
17
),

\inf_{K\in A_{\beta}}I_{p}(K)=\frac{k}{2}\log(2\pi e)-\mathrm{Ent}(\mu^{*})=-c_{k,q,\beta}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
11
in the case of
\beta\leq\beta_{k,q}
.

Recall that the standard Gaussian distribution
\gamma^{\otimes k}
maximizes entropy over the set
\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{k})\colon Z_{2}(\mu)\subset\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}\}
with
\mathrm{Ent}(\gamma^{\otimes k})=\frac{k}{2}\log(2\pi e)
. For
\beta\geq m_{q}
, it follows from
Z_{q}(\gamma^{\otimes k})=m_{q}\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}\subset\beta\mathbb{B}_{2}^{k}
with
m_{q}
as in (
4
) that
\gamma^{\otimes k}
also maximizes entropy over
M_{\beta}
. Thus,

\inf_{K\in A_{\beta}}I_{p}(K)=0\quad\text{for }\beta\geq m_{q},

which proves Theorem
11
in the case of
\beta\geq m_{q}
.

Before we prove Theorem
11
in the remaining case

which implies that indeed
\beta_{k,q}<m_{q}

Proof of Proposition
13
.

Let
x,y>0
and set
x=\frac{1}{q}
and
y=\frac{1}{p}
with
0<q,p<\infty
. Let
\beta>0
. Consider the problem of finding a density
v
on
[0,\infty)
solving

\displaystyle\max\qquad

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ent}(v)

subject to

\displaystyle m_{q}(v)\leq\beta

(P(
q,\beta
))

where
\mathrm{Ent}(v)=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\log(v(r))v(r)\mathrm{d}r
and
m_{q}(v):=\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{q}v(r)\mathrm{d}r
. We say that
v
is feasible for P(
q,\beta
) if it satisfies the constraint
m_{q}(v)\leq\beta
. Similar to before, the solution
v^{*}
to the problem P(
q,\beta
) is of the form

v^{*}(r)=\frac{\lambda^{1/q}}{\Gamma(\frac{q+1}{q})}e^{-\lambda r^{q}},\qquad r>0,

(27)

where
\lambda>0
is such that
v^{*}
satisfies
m_{q}(v^{*})=\beta
. Similar computations as above show that

v_{1}^{*}(r)=\frac{1}{q^{1/q}\Gamma(\frac{q+1}{q})\beta^{1/q}}e^{-\frac{r^{q}}{q\beta}}\text{ and }v_{2}^{*}(r)=\frac{1}{p^{1/p}\Gamma(\frac{p+1}{p})}e^{-\frac{r^{p}}{p}},\qquad r>0,

solve the problems P(
q,\beta
) and P(
p,1
), respectively. It holds that

m_{p}(v_{1}^{*})=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{p+1}{q})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{q})}q^{p/q}\beta^{p/q}\quad\text{and}\quad m_{q}(v_{2}^{*})=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{q+1}{p})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{p})}p^{q/p}

and we see that
m_{p}(v_{1}^{*})\leq 1
holds precisely when

\beta\leq\beta_{p,q}:=\frac{1}{q}\bigg(\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{q})}{\Gamma(\frac{p+1}{q})}\bigg)^{q/p}.

In particular, the solution
v_{1}^{*}
to P(
q,\beta_{p,q}
) is feasible for P(
p,1
) and satisfies
\mathrm{Ent}(v_{1}^{*})\leq\mathrm{Ent}(v_{2}^{*})
. Suppose that
m_{q}(v_{2}^{*})\leq\beta_{p,q}
. Then
v_{2}^{*}
is feasible for P(

) and satisfies
\mathrm{Ent}(v_{2}^{*})\leq\mathrm{Ent}(v_{1}^{*})
. Consequently, we must have that
\mathrm{Ent}(v_{1}^{*})=\mathrm{Ent}(v_{2}^{*})
and both
v_{1}^{*}
and
v_{2}^{*}
solve both P(
q,\beta_{p,q}
) and P(
p,1
). This can only be true if

and then in fact
m_{q}(v_{2}^{*})=\beta_{p,q}
. Thus, if
p\neq q
we must have
\beta_{p,q}<m_{q}(v_{2}^{*})
, i.e. that

\frac{\frac{1}{q}\Gamma(\frac{1}{p})}{\Gamma(\frac{q+1}{p})}\bigg(\frac{\frac{1}{p}\Gamma(\frac{1}{q})}{\Gamma(\frac{p+1}{q})}\bigg)^{q/p}<1,

which is the inequality in Proposition
13
for

∎

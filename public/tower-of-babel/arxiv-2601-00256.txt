Equivariant Cohomology, BRST Quantization, and Analytic Localization: A Unified Framework
Lixin Xu

Abstract
This paper provides a detailed exposition of the two main models for equivariant cohomology—the Cartan and Weil models—and their explicit isomorphism via the Kalkman (Mathai–Quillen) transformation. We then connect this framework to the BRST quantization of gauge theories, showing how the BRST complex can be identified with the Cartan model. Viewing both the Kalkman transformation and Witten’s Morse-theoretic deformation as gauge-fixing procedures leads naturally to the equivariant Witten deformation . This combined perspective yields a transparent analytic proof of the Atiyah–Bott–Berline–Vergne (ABBV) localization formula for integrals of equivariantly closed forms.The theory is richly illustrated with computations on ℂ ​ ℙ 1 \mathbb{CP}^{1} and ℂ ​ ℙ n \mathbb{CP}^{n} , supplemented by explicit coordinate calculations.

Introduction

Equivariant cohomology represents a sophisticated algebraic-topological invariant for spaces equipped with group actions, encoding both the topology of the space and the symmetries of the acting group. Its development has been deeply intertwined with progress in differential geometry, symplectic geometry, and mathematical physics.

Historically, equivariant cohomology emerged from the study of transformation groups and found profound applications in the localization of path integrals in topological field theories
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. The theory provides a powerful framework for understanding Hamiltonian group actions, moment maps, and the Duistermaat–Heckman formula
[
4
,
5
]
.

Two complementary algebraic models dominate the literature: the
Cartan model
, which employs polynomial maps from the Lie algebra to differential forms, and the
Weil model
, constructed from the Weil algebra of the group. Their isomorphism, established via the Kalkman transformation (also known as the Mathai–Quillen isomorphism
[
6
]
), reveals deep connections with algebraic topology and homological algebra.

Parallel developments in theoretical physics led to the BRST (Becchi–Rouet–Stora–Tyutin) formalism
[
7
,
8
]
, a cohomological approach to quantizing gauge theories with constraints. Remarkably, the BRST complex naturally identifies with the Cartan model when the gauge group acts on the field space, establishing a fundamental bridge between mathematical structures in geometry and physics.

On the analytic front, Witten’s deformation of the de Rham differential using a Morse function
[
2
]
provides a mechanism for localizing integrals to critical points, offering connections between differential topology, quantum mechanics, and supersymmetry.

The central contribution of this work is the synthesis of these perspectives through the observation that both the Kalkman transformation and Witten deformation can be interpreted as
gauge-fixing
procedures within a unified BRST/BV framework. Their combination yields the
equivariant Witten deformation
, which we employ to give an analytic derivation of the celebrated Atiyah–Bott–Berline–Vergne (ABBV) localization formula
[
3
,
5
]
.

We begin by recalling the definitions and properties of the Cartan and Weil models, establishing the Kalkman isomorphism with detailed proofs (§
2
). We then develop the precise correspondence with the BRST formalism, including the full BRST/BV complex (§
3
). After reviewing Witten’s deformation and its gauge-fixing interpretation (§
4
), we construct the equivariant Witten deformation (§
4.3
) and employ it to prove the ABBV formula with rigorous estimates (§
5
). Detailed examples on
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
and
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
illustrate the abstract machinery (§
6
). We conclude with broader remarks on applications and future directions (§
7
).

2
The Cartan and Weil Models of Equivariant Cohomology

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional smooth manifold and
G
a compact Lie group acting smoothly on
M

\Phi:G\times M\longrightarrow M,\qquad(g,x)\longmapsto g\cdot x.

(1)

Denote by
\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Lie}(G)
the Lie algebra of
G
. The infinitesimal action gives a Lie algebra homomorphism

\rho:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{X}(M),\qquad\xi\longmapsto X_{\xi},

(2)

where
X_{\xi}
is the fundamental vector field defined by

X_{\xi}(x)=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\exp(t\xi)\cdot x.

(3)

The map
\rho
satisfies
[X_{\xi},X_{\eta}]=X_{[\xi,\eta]}
for all
\xi,\eta\in\mathfrak{g}
.

2.1
Cartan Model

For compact
G
, the
Cartan model
provides a computationally tractable description of equivariant cohomology.

Definition 2.1

(Cartan Complex)
.

The Cartan complex is defined as

\Omega^{\bullet}_{G}(M)\coloneqq\bigl(S(\mathfrak{g}^{*})\otimes\Omega^{\bullet}(M)\bigr)^{G},

(4)

where
S(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
denotes the symmetric algebra on the dual Lie algebra, and the superscript
G
indicates
G
-invariant elements under the diagonal action: the coadjoint action on
S(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
and the pullback action on
\Omega^{\bullet}(M)
. Elements
\alpha\in\Omega^{k}_{G}(M)
are equivariant polynomial maps

\alpha:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\Omega^{\bullet}(M),\qquad\xi\longmapsto\alpha(\xi)=\sum_{i\geq 0}\alpha_{i}(\xi),

(5)

where
\alpha_{i}(\xi)
is homogeneous of degree
i
in
\xi
with values in
\Omega^{\bullet}(M)
, and
\deg_{\text{total}}(\alpha)=\deg_{\text{polynomial}}+\deg_{\text{form}}=k
.

The
equivariant differential

d_{G}:\Omega^{k}_{G}(M)\to\Omega^{k+1}_{G}(M)
is

(d_{G}\alpha)(\xi)\coloneqq\mathrm{d}\bigl(\alpha(\xi)\bigr)-\iota_{X_{\xi}}\bigl(\alpha(\xi)\bigr).

(6)

Theorem 2.2

(Nilpotency of
d_{G}
)
.

The operator
d_{G}
satisfies
d_{G}^{2}=0
on
\Omega_{G}^{\bullet}(M)
.

Proof.

For
\alpha\in\Omega_{G}^{\bullet}(M)
, compute

\displaystyle(d_{G}^{2}\alpha)(\xi)

\displaystyle=d_{G}\bigl((d_{G}\alpha)(\xi)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}\bigl((d_{G}\alpha)(\xi)\bigr)-\iota_{X_{\xi}}\bigl((d_{G}\alpha)(\xi)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}\bigl(\mathrm{d}\alpha(\xi)-\iota_{X_{\xi}}\alpha(\xi)\bigr)-\iota_{X_{\xi}}\bigl(\mathrm{d}\alpha(\xi)-\iota_{X_{\xi}}\alpha(\xi)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\mathrm{d}^{2}\alpha(\xi)}_{=0}-\mathrm{d}\iota_{X_{\xi}}\alpha(\xi)-\iota_{X_{\xi}}\mathrm{d}\alpha(\xi)+\underbrace{\iota_{X_{\xi}}\iota_{X_{\xi}}\alpha(\xi)}_{=0}

\displaystyle=-(\mathrm{d}\iota_{X_{\xi}}+\iota_{X_{\xi}}\mathrm{d})\alpha(\xi)

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{L}_{X_{\xi}}\alpha(\xi).

Since
\alpha
is
G
-invariant,
\mathcal{L}_{X_{\xi}}\alpha(\xi)=0
for all
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
.
∎

The cohomology of
(\Omega^{\bullet}_{G}(M),d_{G})
is the
equivariant cohomology

H^{\bullet}_{G}(M;\mathbb{R})
.

Remark 2.3
.

The Cartan model is particularly suited for computations because it works directly with forms on
M
. The term
-\iota_{X_{\xi}}
can be viewed as a ”twist” of the de Rham differential encoding the group action. For
G
compact, this model computes the Borel equivariant cohomology
H^{\bullet}_{G}(M)\cong H^{\bullet}(EG\times_{G}M)
.

2.2
Weil Model

The Weil algebra provides a universal model for the differential forms on the classifying space
BG
.

Definition 2.4

(Weil Algebra)
.

The Weil algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
is the graded commutative algebra

W(\mathfrak{g})\coloneqq\Lambda(\mathfrak{g}^{*})\otimes S(\mathfrak{g}^{*}),

(7)

where
\Lambda(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
is the exterior algebra (generators
\theta^{a}
of degree
1
, ”connection forms”) and
S(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
is the symmetric algebra (generators
\phi^{a}
of degree
2
, ”curvature forms”).

Choose a basis
\{e_{a}\}
of
\mathfrak{g}
with dual basis
\{\theta^{a}\}
of
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, and structure constants
f^{a}_{bc}
defined by
[e_{b},e_{c}]=f^{a}_{bc}e_{a}
. The differential
d_{W}
is

\displaystyle d_{W}\theta^{a}

\displaystyle=\phi^{a}-\frac{1}{2}f^{a}_{bc}\,\theta^{b}\theta^{c},

(8)

\displaystyle d_{W}\phi^{a}

\displaystyle=-f^{a}_{bc}\,\theta^{b}\phi^{c}.

(9)

Theorem 2.5

(Structure of Weil Algebra)
.

(W(\mathfrak{g}),d_{W})
is a commutative differential graded algebra with
d_{W}^{2}=0
, and it is acyclic:
H^{k}(W(\mathfrak{g}))=0
for
k>0
,
H^{0}(W(\mathfrak{g}))=\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Compute
d_{W}^{2}\theta^{a}=d_{W}(\phi^{a}-\frac{1}{2}f^{a}_{bc}\theta^{b}\theta^{c})=-f^{a}_{bc}\theta^{b}\phi^{c}-\frac{1}{2}f^{a}_{bc}(d_{W}\theta^{b})\theta^{c}+\frac{1}{2}f^{a}_{bc}\theta^{b}(d_{W}\theta^{c})
.
Substituting
d_{W}\theta^{b}=\phi^{b}-\frac{1}{2}f^{b}_{de}\theta^{d}\theta^{e}
, and using the Jacobi identity for the structure constants, one verifies that all terms cancel. Similarly for
d_{W}^{2}\phi^{a}
. Acyclicity follows from the existence of a contracting homotopy.
∎

The
Weil model
for
G
-equivariant cohomology uses the complex

\bigl(W(\mathfrak{g})\otimes\Omega^{\bullet}(M)\bigr)_{\text{basic}},

(10)

where ”basic” means elements that are

1.

Horizontal
:
\iota_{e_{a}}\alpha=0
for all
a
, where
\iota_{e_{a}}
acts by contraction on the
\theta^{a}
factor.

2.

Invariant
:
\mathcal{L}_{e_{a}}\alpha=0
for all
a
, where
\mathcal{L}_{e_{a}}=[d_{W},\iota_{e_{a}}]
.

The differential is
d_{W}\otimes 1+1\otimes\mathrm{d}
.

2.3
Mathai–Quillen Isomorphism (Kalkman Transformation)

The Cartan and Weil models are isomorphic via an explicit transformation.

Definition 2.6

(Kalkman Transformation)
.

Define
\kappa:W(\mathfrak{g})\otimes\Omega^{\bullet}(M)\to W(\mathfrak{g})\otimes\Omega^{\bullet}(M)
by

\kappa\coloneqq\exp(-\cont{\theta}),\quad\text{where }\iota_{\theta}\coloneqq\theta^{a}\otimes\iota_{X_{a}},

(11)

with
X_{a}=X_{e_{a}}
. This is a graded algebra isomorphism.

Theorem 2.7

(Intertwining Property)
.

\kappa
intertwines the differentials

\kappa\circ(d_{W}\otimes 1+1\otimes\mathrm{d})\circ\kappa^{-1}=d_{G},

(12)

and restricts to an isomorphism between the basic subcomplex of the Weil model and the Cartan complex.

Proof.

Since
\iota_{\theta}
is a derivation, we use the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula:

e^{-\iota_{\theta}}(d_{W}+\mathrm{d})e^{\iota_{\theta}}=d_{W}+\mathrm{d}+[d_{W}+\mathrm{d},-\iota_{\theta}]+\frac{1}{2}[[d_{W}+\mathrm{d},-\iota_{\theta}],-\iota_{\theta}]+\cdots.

(13)

Compute
[d_{W},\iota_{\theta}]=-\mathcal{L}_{\theta}
and
[\mathrm{d},\iota_{\theta}]=0
(since
\mathrm{d}
and contractions commute). The higher commutators vanish due to
[\mathcal{L}_{\theta},\iota_{\theta}]=0
. The result simplifies to
d_{W}+\mathrm{d}-\mathcal{L}_{\theta}
. After identifying
\phi^{a}
with elements of
S(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
and noting that on basic elements the
\theta^{a}
terms vanish, we recover
d_{G}
.
∎

Hence
\kappa
induces an isomorphism of cochain complexes and, consequently, an isomorphism
H^{\bullet}_{G,\text{Weil}}(M)\simeq H^{\bullet}_{G,\text{Cartan}}(M)
.

Remark 2.8
.

The Kalkman transformation eliminates the connection-like generators
\theta^{a}
in favor of curvature-like generators
\phi^{a}
, introducing the contraction term into the differential. This mirrors the passage from connection to curvature in differential geometry.

3.2
Batalin–Vilkovisky Formalism

The full BV complex extends this structure

\Phi_{\text{BV}}=(\text{fields})\oplus(\text{anti-fields})\oplus(\text{ghosts})\oplus(\text{anti-ghosts}).

(15)

The BV differential
s_{\text{BV}}
squares to zero and encodes both the gauge symmetry and the equations of motion.

Theorem 3.1

(BRST as Equivariant Cohomology)
.

The BRST cohomology of a gauge theory is isomorphic to the
G
-equivariant cohomology of the field space
\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

Identify
\delta
with
\mathrm{d}
,
c^{a}\mathcal{L}_{X_{a}}
with
-\iota_{X_{c}}
(up to conventions), and note the Chevalley–Eilenberg term matches. This gives
s\simeq d_{G}
on the appropriate complexes.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

This identification explains why topological field theories (Chern–Simons, Donaldson–Witten) often have BRST-exact actions: they integrate equivariantly exact forms, whose evaluation localizes via ABBV.

4
Witten Deformation and its Equivariant Counterpart

4.1
Witten Deformation of de Rham Complex

Given
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
smooth, Witten introduced a deformation

d_{t}\coloneqq e^{-tf}\,\mathrm{d}\,e^{tf}=\mathrm{d}+t\,\mathrm{d}f\wedge.

(16)

Theorem 4.1

(Properties of
d_{t}
)
.

1.

d_{t}^{2}=0
for all
t\geq 0

2.

(\Omega^{\bullet}(M),d_{t})
has cohomology isomorphic to
H^{\bullet}(M;\mathbb{R})

3.

For
f
Morse, as
t\to\infty
, the Witten Laplacian
\Delta_{t}=d_{t}d_{t}^{\dagger}+d_{t}^{\dagger}d_{t}
localizes eigenforms near critical points

Proof.

(1)
\mathrm{d}^{2}=0
,
\mathrm{d}f\wedge\mathrm{d}f=0
, and
[\mathrm{d},\mathrm{d}f\wedge]=\mathrm{d}^{2}f\wedge=0
.

(2) Conjugation by
e^{tf}
gives chain isomorphism.

(3) Follows from semiclassical analysis of
\Delta_{t}
.
∎

4.2
Gauge-Fixing Interpretation

In BV formalism, gauge-fixing is implemented via a canonical transformation generated by a gauge-fixing fermion
\Psi

s_{\text{BV}}\;\longrightarrow\;e^{\{\Psi,\;\cdot\}}s_{\text{BV}}e^{-\{\Psi,\;\cdot\}}.

(17)

For supersymmetric quantum mechanics with
Q
the supersymmetry charge, choosing
\Psi=tf
yields
Q_{t}=e^{tf}Qe^{-tf}
, recovering Witten’s deformation.

4.3
Equivariant Witten Deformation

Assume
f
is
G
-invariant:
\mathcal{L}_{X_{\xi}}f=0
for all
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
. Define

Definition 4.2

(Equivariant Witten Deformation)
.

The
Kalkman–Witten transformation
is

\kappa_{t}\coloneqq e^{-tf}\,\kappa\,e^{tf}=e^{-tf-\iota_{\theta}},

(18)

where the simplification uses
[f,\iota_{\theta}]=0
due to
G
-invariance.

Applying
\kappa_{t}
to the Weil differential gives

\displaystyle d_{W,t}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\kappa_{t}\,d_{W}\,\kappa_{t}^{-1}

\displaystyle=e^{-tf}d_{G}e^{tf}

(19)

\displaystyle=d_{G}+t\,\mathrm{d}f\wedge.

We denote
d_{G,t}\coloneqq d_{G}+t\,\mathrm{d}f\wedge
.

Theorem 4.3

(Properties of
d_{G,t}
)
.

1.

d_{G,t}^{2}=0
on
\Omega_{G}^{\bullet}(M)

2.

(\Omega_{G}^{\bullet}(M),d_{G,t})
has cohomology isomorphic to
H_{G}^{\bullet}(M)

3.

The map
\alpha\mapsto e^{-tf}\alpha
gives an isomorphism
(\Omega_{G}^{\bullet}(M),d_{G})\cong(\Omega_{G}^{\bullet}(M),d_{G,t})

Proof.

(1) Compute:

d_{G,t}^{2}=d_{G}^{2}+t[d_{G},\mathrm{d}f\wedge]+t^{2}(\mathrm{d}f\wedge)^{2}.

(20)

Since
d_{G}^{2}=0
,
(\mathrm{d}f\wedge)^{2}=0
, and
[d_{G},\mathrm{d}f\wedge]=\mathrm{d}(d_{G}f)\wedge=0
(as
d_{G}f=\mathrm{d}f-\iota_{X}f=0
by invariance).
∎

5
Proof of the ABBV Localization Formula via Equivariant Witten Deformation

We present an analytic proof of the ABBV localization formula using the equivariant Witten deformation.

5.1
Setting and Assumptions

Let
M
be compact, oriented,
n
-dimensional without boundary, with
G=S^{1}
action. Assume

1.

Fixed point set
M^{G}=\{p_{1},\dots,p_{N}\}
consists of isolated points

2.

\omega\in\Omega^{n}_{G}(M)
is equivariantly closed:
d_{G}\omega=0

3.

f:M\to\mathbb{R}
is
G
-invariant Morse with critical points exactly at
M^{G}

4.

f\geq 0
,
f(p_{i})=0
,
f>0
away from fixed points

Lemma 5.1

(Existence of Invariant Morse Function)
.

For a compact Lie group
G
acting on compact
M
with isolated fixed points, there exists a
G
-invariant Morse function
f
with critical points at
M^{G}
.

Proof.

Take generic
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
such that
X_{\xi}
has isolated zeros. Define
f(x)=\frac{1}{2}g(X_{\xi},X_{\xi})
for a
G
-invariant metric
g
. This is
G
-invariant, and its critical points are zeros of
X_{\xi}
.
∎

5.2
Deformation and Independence of
t

Consider the deformed form

\omega_{t}\coloneqq e^{-tf}\,\omega.

(21)

Since
d_{G}\omega=0
, we have

d_{G,t}\,\omega_{t}=e^{-tf}d_{G}\omega=0.

(22)

Define the integral as a function of the deformation parameter

I(t)\coloneqq\int_{M}[\omega_{t}]_{n}=\int_{M}e^{-tf}[\omega]_{n}.

(23)

Proposition 5.2

(
t
-independence)
.

I(t)
is independent of
t
:
I(t)=I(0)
for all
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

The map

\Phi_{t}:\alpha\mapsto e^{-tf}\alpha,

(24)

is an isomorphism of complexes from
(\Omega_{G}^{\bullet}(M),d_{G})
to
(\Omega_{G}^{\bullet}(M),d_{G,t})
, because
d_{G,t}=e^{-tf}d_{G}e^{tf}
. Specifically,
\Phi_{t}
is a chain map because

d_{G,t}(\Phi_{t}\alpha)=(d_{G}+t\,df\wedge)(e^{-tf}\alpha)=e^{-tf}d_{G}\alpha-t(df\wedge e^{-tf}\alpha)+t(df\wedge e^{-tf}\alpha)=e^{-tf}d_{G}\alpha=\Phi_{t}(d_{G}\alpha),

(25)

where we used that
d_{G}(e^{-tf}\alpha)=e^{-tf}d_{G}\alpha+(d_{G}e^{-tf})\alpha andd_{G}e^{-tf}=-tdfe^{-tf}
. The map is invertible, with inverse
\Phi_{t}^{-1}\alpha=e^{tf}\alpha
.

Therefore, it induces an isomorphism on equivariant cohomology. In particular, the cohomology class of
e^{-tf}\omega
in the
d_{G,t}
-cohomology is the image of the fixed class
[\omega]_{d_{G}}
. The integral
I(t)=\int_{M}[e^{-tf}\omega]_{n}
depends only on this cohomology class, as the integral of the top-degree component of an equivariantly closed form is a well-defined functional on equivariant cohomology (since equivariantly exact forms integrate to zero over compact
M
without boundary). Hence,
I(t)
is constant in
t
,
I(t)\equiv I(0)=\int_{M}[\omega]_{n}
.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

The invariance of the integral under the deformation is a consequence of the cohomological nature of the pairing with the fundamental class in the equivariant setting.

5.3
Localization as
t\to\infty

By
t
-independence:

\int_{M}[\omega]_{n}=\lim_{t\to\infty}I(t)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{M}e^{-tf}[\omega]_{n}.

(26)

Lemma 5.4

(Exponential decay away from fixed points)
.

For any compact
K\subset M\setminus M^{G}
,
\int_{K}e^{-tf}[\omega]_{n}\to 0
exponentially as
t\to\infty
.

Proof.

Since
f>0
on
K
, let
\delta=\min_{K}f>0
. Then

\left|\int_{K}e^{-tf}[\omega]_{n}\right|\leq e^{-t\delta}\int_{K}|[\omega]_{n}|\to 0.

(27)

∎

Thus contributions localize to neighborhoods of fixed points

\int_{M}[\omega]_{n}=\sum_{p\in M^{G}}\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{U_{p}}e^{-tf}[\omega]_{n}.

(28)

5.4
Local Computation at an Isolated Fixed Point

Fix
p\in M^{G}
. By equivariant tubular neighborhood theorem, a
G
-invariant neighborhood
U_{p}
is equivariantly diffeomorphic to
T_{p}M\cong\mathbb{C}^{m}
(assuming
n=2m
even). The linear isotropy action has weights
w_{1},\dots,w_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
.

Choose coordinates
(z_{1},\dots,z_{m})
on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
with

\displaystyle f(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}|z_{j}|^{2}+O(|z|^{3}),

(29)

\displaystyle[\omega]_{2m}(z)

\displaystyle=\omega_{0}(p)\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{i}{2}\mathrm{d}z_{j}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{z}_{j}+O(|z|).

(30)

Here
\omega_{0}(p)
is the
0
-form component of
\omega
at
p
, and
\lambda_{j}>0
after possible orientation adjustment.

Lemma 5.5

(Gaussian asymptotics)
.

As
t\to\infty

\int_{U_{p}}e^{-tf}[\omega]_{2m}=\omega_{0}(p)\left(\frac{2\pi}{t}\right)^{m}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}}+O(t^{-m-1}).

(31)

Proof.

Change to polar coordinates
z_{j}=r_{j}e^{i\theta_{j}}

\frac{i}{2}\mathrm{d}z_{j}\wedge\mathrm{d}\bar{z}_{j}=r_{j}\mathrm{d}r_{j}\wedge\mathrm{d}\theta_{j}.

(32)

The leading contribution comes from the Gaussian integral

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2m}}e^{-\frac{t}{2}\sum\lambda_{j}r_{j}^{2}}\omega_{0}(p)\prod r_{j}\mathrm{d}r_{j}\mathrm{d}\theta_{j}=\omega_{0}(p)(2\pi)^{m}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{t}{2}\lambda_{j}r^{2}}r\mathrm{d}r.

(33)

Each radial integral equals
\frac{1}{t\lambda_{j}}
. Higher order terms contribute
O(t^{-m-1})
by stationary phase.
∎

The weights
w_{j}
relate to
\lambda_{j}
via the moment map:
\lambda_{j}=|w_{j}|
with appropriate sign determined by orientation.

5.5
The ABBV Localization Formula

Combining contributions from all fixed points:

Theorem 5.6

(Atiyah–Bott–Berline–Vergne Localization)
.

For
\omega\in\Omega_{G}^{n}(M)
with
d_{G}\omega=0

\int_{M}[\omega]_{n}=(2\pi)^{m}\sum_{p\in M^{G}}\frac{\omega_{0}(p)}{\prod_{j=1}^{m}w_{j}(p)},

(34)

where
n=2m
, and
w_{1}(p),\dots,w_{m}(p)
are the weights of the isotropy representation at
p
.

Remark 5.7

(Equivariant Euler class interpretation)
.

The denominator
\prod_{j=1}^{m}w_{j}(p)
is (up to
(2\pi)^{m}
) the equivariant Euler class
e_{G}(T_{p}M)
of the tangent space. Thus

\int_{M}\omega=\sum_{p\in M^{G}}\frac{i_{p}^{*}\omega}{e_{G}(N_{p})},

(35)

where
N_{p}
is the normal bundle at
p
and
i_{p}^{*}\omega
denotes the restriction of the 0-form component.

6
Examples and Applications

6.1
Localization on
\mathbb{CP}^{1}

Consider
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
with homogeneous coordinates
[z_{0}:z_{1}]
and

Conclusion

We have presented a unified framework connecting equivariant cohomology, BRST quantization, and analytic localization:

•

Detailed exposition of Cartan and Weil models with explicit proofs

•

Clarification of the BRST/equivariant cohomology correspondence

•

Construction of equivariant Witten deformation as a gauge-fixing procedure

•

Rigorous analytic proof of ABBV localization with error estimates

•

Comprehensive examples illustrating the theory

This framework unifies algebraic, geometric, and analytic perspectives, revealing deep interconnections between topology, group actions, and supersymmetric quantum mechanics. The ABBV formula itself is a cornerstone of modern symplectic geometry and topological field theory, with applications ranging from Duistermaat–Heckman formulas to the computation of partition functions in supersymmetric gauge theories.

The equivariant Witten deformation provides a powerful analytic tool that makes the localization phenomenon transparent: as the deformation parameter
t
tends to infinity, the integral receives contributions only from infinitesimal neighborhoods of the fixed points, where Gaussian approximations become exact. This is a rigorous manifestation of the saddle-point method in an infinite-dimensional setting, underpinning many results in supersymmetric localization.

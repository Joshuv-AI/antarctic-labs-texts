Pioneering Radial Forearm Free Flap Reconstruction for Lip Cancer in a Young Patient in Ethiopia
Authors: Lealem T. Gelaw, Zenebe T. Gebremariyam, Nebyat A. Woldemicael, Mieraf S. Habtemariam
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Summary:Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) of the lip is rare in young individuals, usually associated with chronic sun exposure, tobacco use, or genetic predisposition. We report a 20-year-old woman with epilepsy who developed lower lip SCC. This case is unique for its early onset and is the first lower lip reconstruction with a radial forearm free flap in Ethiopia. The patient underwent wide local excision, bilateral selective neck dissection (levels I–III), and radial forearm free flap reconstruction with a palmaris longus tendon sling. Postoperatively, recovery was uneventful with complete flap survival, improved oral competence, and restored speech. This case highlighted invasive SCC in a young patient and demonstrated the feasibility of free flap reconstruction in a resource-limited setting.

Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) of the lip usually affects older adults with a history of ultraviolet exposure, smoking, or alcohol use. SCC in young adults is rare, presenting unique challenges. We present a 20-year-old woman with lower lip SCC, managed with radial forearm free flap (RFFF) reconstruction—the first reported in Ethiopia.

CASE PRESENTATION

A 20-year-old woman presented with a foul-smelling, nonhealing lower lip ulcer for 1 month. She had a 12-year history of epilepsy, well controlled on medication. The wound began after minor trauma and worsened with herbal treatment (Fig. 1).

Preoperative clinical photograph showing an extensive fungating and ulcerated mass involving the lower lip.

She reported difficulty in speech, drooling, and feeding, causing social embarrassment and withdrawal. She denied alcohol, smoking, or a family history of similar conditions.

Examination revealed a large, ulcerated lower lip mass extending to both commissures and the chin, with indurated rolled edges, necrosis, friability, and purulent discharge. The upper lip was unaffected. There was bilateral level I–II cervical lymphadenopathy, with multiple firm, mobile nodes. Systemic examination was normal.

Baseline laboratory investigations showed mild anemia (hemoglobin 10 g/dL); other tests and imaging were normal. Histopathology confirmed poorly differentiated SCC. No distant metastasis was detected.

SURGICAL MANAGEMENT

Our microsurgical unit is led by 3 plastic surgeons, supported by trained staff. Operation Smile provided operating microscopes, instruments, and consumables. Support from the nongovernmental organization helped address infrastructure and staffing issues. We operate on 5 patients per week during mission with Swedish collaborators and 10–15 patients independently per year.

SURGICAL TECHNIQUE AND TECHNICAL CONSIDERATIONS

A 2-team approach was used. The tumor was marked with a 2-cm margin. The entire lower lip, including the commissures to the chin, was excised in full thickness. The specimen was labeled and sent for histopathological examination.

One team performed a bilateral neck dissection, and the other harvested the RFFF. A degloving neck incision extending from angle to angle, 2 cm below the mandible, allowed for nodal clearance and recipient vessel preparation. Level I–III lymph nodes were dissected bilaterally. The facial artery and vein were preserved for microvascular anastomosis without additional incisions for vessel exposure.

The RFFF was designed using a template matching the defect. The radial artery and veins and the cephalic vein were preserved with the flap. The palmaris longus tendon was harvested and kept hydrated. The tendon was anchored to the modiolus at the commissures and slightly overcorrected.

The harvested flap folded over the tendon onto itself to replicate the vermilion–mucosal interface, covering the inner and outer lip. Inset was performed in layers with commissural alignment and symmetry. The flap filled the defect, matching the lower facial contours.

After inset and tensioning, the radial artery was anastomosed end-to-end to the facial artery, and the vein to the facial vein, using 9-0 Prolene under the microscope. Patency was confirmed with Doppler and clinical examination. Flap viability was monitored intra- and postoperatively (Figs. 2, 3).

POSTOPERATIVE COURSE

Postoperative recovery was smooth, with complete flap survival (Fig. 4). Nasogastric feeding was continued for 1 week; the diet was advanced without complications. Drooling resolved, and chewing and speech improved, restoring oral competence.

One-month postoperative appearance after wide local excision, neck dissection, and RFFF reconstruction.

The patient was discharged for outpatient follow-up. Follow-up biopsy confirmed clear margins and a poorly differentiated, high-grade invasive SCC. She was referred to oncology for possible adjuvant radiotherapy and remains under joint follow-up for recurrence or revision.

DISCUSSION

Lower lip SCC usually affects older adults with chronic sun exposure, tobacco use, or immunosuppression.1 Its occurrence in young adults without these factors is rare. This 20-year-old patient with bilateral neck metastasis raises questions about early-onset pathogenesis and risk factors.

Young-onset SCC may relate to genetic predisposition, human papillomavirus infection, and chronic inflammations.1,2 The role of human papillomavirus in oral or lip SCC remains controversial, but may contribute in patients without traditional risk factors.3 Immunosuppression from disease or medication can predispose young patients to aggressive SCC.4Our patient had no such history.

Advanced lip SCC surgery with an RFFF is well established but rare in young patients. The RFFF offers pliability, vascularity, and minimal donor-site morbidity.5 We chose the RFFF due to complete lower lip involvement and the limited local options for functional and aesthetic reconstruction. Studies highlight its advantages for large tissue replacement.6,7

This is the first reported case of lower lip SCC managed with an RFFF in Ethiopia. Early diagnosis and radical surgery were key in managing this aggressive case. Free tissue transfer demonstrates microsurgical advances enabling superior function and aesthetics, even in young patients. Complex microsurgical procedures, such as free transverse rectus abdominis myocutaneous flaps, can succeed in resource-limited settings, proving feasibility beyond high-income countries.8

CONCLUSIONS

Early intervention is crucial, as delays due to traditional healer use can worsen disease progression. Awareness of suspicious lesions is vital for timely diagnosis and better outcomes. SCC in a 20-year-old is rare, warranting further study of potential genetic or viral causes.

This first RFFF lower lip reconstruction in Ethiopia was successful for a large lower lip defect, demonstrating both functional and aesthetic value. It highlights microsurgical advances enabling complex oncologic reconstructions in resource-limited settings.

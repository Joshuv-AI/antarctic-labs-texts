Evaluation of Virtual Surgical Planning in Accuracy and Efficiency of Complex Maxillofacial Trauma Reconstruction
Authors: Jigar Prahelad Bhai Thakkar, Padmanabha Kumar Govindaraj, Pratesh Nitin Dholabhai, Pooja Arora, Sushree Arpita Priyadarsani, Sameer Gupta
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Complex maxillofacial trauma reconstruction presents significant challenges due to anatomical intricacies and the need for precise spatial restoration. Virtual surgical planning (VSP) has emerged as a promising technology to enhance surgical accuracy and efficiency.Objective:This study aimed to evaluate the accuracy and intraoperative efficiency of Virtual Surgical Planning in the reconstruction of complex maxillofacial trauma compared to conventional planning methods.Materials and Methods:A prospective comparative study was conducted on 40 patients with complex maxillofacial fractures, randomly divided into two groups: Group A (VSP, n = 20) and Group B (conventional planning, n = 20). Pre- and postoperative CT scans were used to assess accuracy based on bony alignment and symmetry. Surgical duration, intraoperative complications, and postoperative outcomes were also analyzed.Results:Group A showed significantly improved alignment accuracy (2.1 ± 0.4 mm deviation) compared to Group B (3.7 ± 0.8 mm; P < 0.001). Average surgical duration was reduced in Group A (142.3 ± 11.7 minutes) versus Group B (176.5 ± 13.2 minutes; P < 0.01). Symmetry scores, based on postoperative imaging, were superior in the VSP group (mean score 4.7/5 vs. 3.9/5; P = 0.004). No major complications were reported in either group, though minor adjustments were less frequent in Group A.Conclusion:Virtual Surgical Planning significantly enhances the accuracy and efficiency of complex maxillofacial trauma reconstruction, supporting its integration into routine surgical practice.

INTRODUCTION

Maxillofacial trauma involving complex fractures presents a significant reconstructive challenge due to the functional and aesthetic importance of craniofacial anatomy. Restoring precise anatomical alignment is critical for optimal outcomes, yet traditional methods of surgical planning often rely heavily on surgeon experience and intraoperative judgment.[1]

Recent advancements in imaging, computer-assisted design, and 3D printing have revolutionized the surgical landscape. Virtual surgical planning (VSP) enables surgeons to simulate procedures preoperatively, design cutting guides, and manufacture patient-specific implants. These technologies have shown promising outcomes in orthognathic and oncologic surgeries[23] but limited data exist regarding their application in acute trauma scenarios.

A growing body of literature supports the use of VSP in improving accuracy and reducing surgical time, particularly in elective cases. However, its role in complex trauma—where pre-existing asymmetry, comminution, and soft tissue swelling are common—remains underexplored.[45]

This study aims to evaluate the accuracy and intraoperative efficiency of Virtual Surgical Planning in complex maxillofacial trauma reconstruction compared to conventional planning techniques.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Sample size and selection

A total of 40 patients aged 18–65 years, presenting with complex midface or mandible fractures requiring open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF), were enrolled. Patients with panfacial trauma, pathological fractures, or pre-existing deformities were excluded. Participants were randomly allocated into two equal groups using a computer-generated randomization table:

Procedure

For Group A, CT DICOM data were processed through VSP software to perform virtual reduction and plan osteosynthesis. Custom cutting guides and 3D models were printed. Group B underwent traditional planning using radiographs and intraoperative judgment.

All surgeries were performed by two senior maxillofacial surgeons using standardized protocols. Surgical time was recorded from incision to wound closure.

Outcome measures

Accuracy: Postoperative CT scans were superimposed on preoperative virtual plans. Linear deviation was measured at key anatomical landmarks (e.g., zygomatic arches, mandibular angles).

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS version 25. Mean ± SD was used for continuous variables. Independent t-tests and Chi-square tests assessed differences between groups. A P -value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

Baseline characteristics

The two groups were comparable in age, sex, and fracture type (P > 0.05). Mean patient age was 34.2 ± 10.5 years, with a male predominance (70%).

Surgical accuracy

Mean linear deviation from the planned position was significantly lower in Group A (2.1 ± 0.4 mm) compared to Group B (3.7 ± 0.8 mm; P < 0.001).

Surgical time

Group A demonstrated reduced operative time (142.3 ± 11.7 minutes) relative to Group B (176.5 ± 13.2 minutes; P = 0.008).

Facial symmetry

Symmetry scores were higher in the VSP group (4.7 ± 0.3) than in the conventional group (3.9 ± 0.5; P = 0.004), indicating improved postoperative facial alignment.

Intraoperative adjustments

Only 3 patients (15%) in Group A required intraoperative plate modifications versus 12 patients (60%) in Group B (P = 0.002).

Postoperative complications

No major complications were observed. Minor complications (e.g., mild malocclusion) occurred in two patients in Group B and one patient in Group A, which was not statistically significant (P > 0.05) [Tables 1 and 2].

DISCUSSION

The present study demonstrates that VSP significantly enhances both accuracy and efficiency in the reconstruction of complex maxillofacial trauma. These findings are consistent with prior studies that have reported the benefits of VSP in craniofacial surgery.[67]

Our results showed a significantly reduced deviation from planned outcomes in the VSP group. This aligns with the findings of Mazzoni et al.,[8] who reported sub-millimeter deviations in orthognathic procedures using VSP. Similarly, Zhang et al.[9] observed improved anatomical restoration in orbital fracture reconstructions using VSP.

Reduced surgical time observed in this study may be attributed to preoperative planning, guide-assisted osteotomies, and minimized intraoperative adjustments. This efficiency is crucial in trauma cases, where prolonged procedures may increase infection risk and blood loss.[10]

Although the benefits are clear, VSP also poses limitations. It requires specialized software, equipment, and a trained technical team. Moreover, in emergency trauma scenarios, the time required for preoperative planning may not always be feasible.[11] Our study mitigated this limitation by focusing on secondary reconstructions or cases with delayed definitive management.

Strengths of this study include its prospective design, standardized surgical protocol, and objective radiological assessment. Limitations involve the small sample size and single-center setting, which may affect generalizability.

Further research with multicentric trials and cost-benefit analyses is needed to establish VSP as a routine component in trauma workflows.

CONCLUSION

This study concludes that Virtual Surgical Planning significantly improves the accuracy of bony realignment and reduces operative time in complex maxillofacial trauma cases. The findings support the integration of VSP into reconstructive protocols, especially in centers equipped for digital planning. Adoption of this technology can contribute to enhanced patient outcomes, surgical predictability, and efficient resource utilization.

Effect of Tirzepatide on Health‐Related Quality of Life in Japanese Patients With Obesity Disease: Patient‐Reported Outcomes From the SURMOUNT‐J Study
Authors: Hiroaki Masuzaki, Hirotaka Nagashima, Tomotaka Shingaki, Tomonori Oura, Ichiro Tatsuno
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAimsWe evaluated the impact of tirzepatide on health‐related quality of life (HR‐QoL) in Japanese individuals with obesity disease from the phase 3 SURMOUNT‐J trial.Materials and MethodsJapanese adults with obesity disease without diabetes were randomized 1:1:1 to tirzepatide (10 or 15 mg) or placebo for 72 weeks as an adjunct to lifestyle modifications. Prespecified analyses assessed changes from baseline to Week 72 in HR‐QoL using the Impact of Weight on Quality of Life‐Lite‐Clinical Trials Version (IWQOL‐Lite‐CT) and 36‐Item Short‐Form Health Survey Version 2.0 (SF‐36v2), and assessed subgroup differences by sex (male, female), baseline body mass index (< 35, ≥ 35 kg/m2) and age (< 65, ≥ 65 years). Post hoc analyses examined correlations between HR‐QoL scores and percentage weight loss.ResultsAmong 201 participants (tirzepatide 10 mg: n = 63; tirzepatide 15 mg: n = 69; placebo: n = 69), both tirzepatide doses significantly improved IWQOL‐Lite‐CT Physical Function composite, Physical composite, Psychosocial composite and Total scores and the SF‐36v2 Physical Functioning domain score at Week 72 versus placebo. Subgroup analyses showed no major variations in treatment effect by baseline characteristics. Across treatment groups, weight loss was correlated with IWQOL‐Lite‐CT improvements at Week 72 (r values up to −0.32), with several correlations reaching statistical significance.ConclusionsIn Japanese adults with obesity disease, tirzepatide significantly improved HR‐QoL over 72 weeks compared with placebo in both physical and psychosocial domains. These findings align with results from the SURMOUNT clinical program, indicating that tirzepatide may improve HR‐QoL across different populations with obesity or overweight. Trial Registration: ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT04844918

Introduction

Obesity in Japan is defined by a body mass index (BMI) ≥ 25 kg/m2, reflecting evidence that Japanese populations are at higher risk of cardiometabolic disorders above this BMI threshold [1]. Compared with non‐Asian populations, Asian populations are more susceptible to visceral adiposity–related metabolic risk, with higher visceral fat levels linked to cardiometabolic complications [2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7]. Accordingly, the Japanese Society for the Study of Obesity (JASSO) defines ‘obesity disease’ as BMI ≥ 25 kg/m2 (or ≥ 35 kg/m2 for ‘high‐degree’ obesity disease) accompanied by one or more obesity‐related health disorders (ORHDs) or a visceral fat area of ≥ 100 cm2, and defines improvement in health‐related quality of life (HR‐QoL) is a key treatment goal [1, 8].

Japanese individuals with obesity experience a high socioeconomic burden, including greater risk of comorbidity and premature mortality, higher healthcare costs and greater impairment of HR‐QoL compared with individuals of normal weight [9]. Obesity negatively affects physical, functional and social dimensions of daily life, contributing to limitations on activities of daily living, reduced work productivity and diminished social engagement [10]. Weight loss achieved through surgical and non‐surgical interventions has been shown to improve HR‐QoL [11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18], but data on patient‐reported outcomes in Japanese adults with obesity disease, particularly during long‐term pharmacological treatment, remain limited.

Tirzepatide, a once‐weekly, glucose‐dependent insulinotropic polypeptide receptor and glucagon‐like peptide‐1 receptor agonist (GLP‐1RA), is approved in Japan for the treatment of type 2 diabetes (T2D) and obesity disease. Participants treated with tirzepatide have shown substantial weight loss and improvements in metabolic parameters in the global phase 3 SURMOUNT program [19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24]. In the SURMOUNT‐J trial, tirzepatide 10 and 15 mg produced significant reductions in body weight and visceral fat in Japanese adults with obesity disease but without T2D [24]. However, the impact of tirzepatide on HR‐QoL in this population has not been previously described. Given that improvement in HR‐QoL is the ultimate treatment goal suggested by the JASSO guidelines, this analysis assessed the HR‐QoL of Japanese participants in SURMOUNT‐J following 72 weeks of tirzepatide treatment. We also explored the relationship between weight loss and changes in HR‐QoL and assessed whether baseline characteristics influenced HR‐QoL outcomes.

Materials and Methods

Study Design and Participants

The study design and primary outcomes of the SURMOUNT‐J clinical trial have been previously published [24]. Briefly, SURMOUNT‐J was a randomized, double‐blind, placebo‐controlled phase 3 trial that evaluated the efficacy and safety of tirzepatide 10 and 15 mg versus placebo over 72 weeks in conjunction with a reduced calorie diet and increased physical activity for weight management. The study enrolled Japanese adults (age ≥ 20 years) without T2D who met the JASSO criteria for obesity requiring pharmacological treatment (BMI ≥ 27 and < 35 kg/m2 with ≥ 2 ORHDs, or BMI ≥ 35 kg/m2 with ≥ 1 ORHD). ORHDs were defined based on the JASSO diagnostic criteria for obesity disease [8] and included impaired glucose tolerance (IGT; oral glucose tolerance test 0‐h glucose 110–125 mg/dL and/or 2‐h glucose 140–199 mg/dL, inclusive of ‘borderline type’ impaired fasting serum glucose [25]), hyperlipidaemia (fasting triglycerides ≥ 150 mg/dL) and metabolic dysfunction‐associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD; hepatic fat fraction ≥ 5% as measured by magnetic resonance imaging‐proton density fat fraction). Key exclusion criteria included a diagnosis of diabetes per Japanese clinical practice guidelines [25], treatment with drugs that promote weight change within 3 months prior to screening (semaglutide prohibited within 12 months of randomization), and liver disease other than MASLD.

The study was conducted according to international ethical standards, including the Declaration of Helsinki and Council for International Organizations of Medical Sciences International Ethical guidelines and Good Clinical Practice guidelines, and was approved by ethical review boards at each study site (full list [24]). Written informed consent was provided by all participants prior to participation in the study.

Procedures and Assessments

Participants were randomized 1:1:1 to receive once‐weekly subcutaneous tirzepatide (10 or 15 mg) or placebo. Randomization was stratified by baseline IGT (yes, no), baseline hyperlipidaemia (yes, no), baseline MASLD (yes, no) and sex (male, female). Dosing was initiated at 2.5 mg and escalated by 2.5 mg every 4 weeks until the target dose was reached.

Participants completed HR‐QoL assessments at randomization and Week 72 using questionnaires that had been translated into Japanese and linguistically validated. Assessments included the Impact of Weight on Quality of Life‐Lite‐Clinical Trials Version (IWQOL‐Lite‐CT) and the 36‐Item Short‐Form Health Survey Version 2.0 (SF‐36v2) acute form. The IWQOL‐Lite‐CT is a 20‐item, obesity‐specific measure developed for clinical trials to assess the impact of body weight changes [26, 27]. It assesses two domains of obesity‐related HR‐QoL (7 items physical and 13 items psychosocial) and provides three composite scores (Physical, Psychosocial, Physical Function) and a Total score. Each item is rated on 5‐point frequency (‘never’ to ‘always’) or truth (‘not at all true’ to ‘completely true’) scales. Composite and Total scores range from 0 to 100, with improved HR‐QoL indicated by higher scores [26, 27]. The SF‐36v2 is a 36‐item questionnaire measuring functional health status. The SF‐36v2 assesses 8 health domains (Physical Functioning, Role‐Physical, Bodily Pain, General Health, Vitality, Social Functioning, Role‐Emotional, Mental Health) that are scored individually and combined into Physical and Mental Component Summary scores [28, 29]. Scoring is norm‐based, derived from the mean score in the United States general population (mean set to 50, with a standard deviation [SD] of 10), with higher scores indicating better HR‐QoL or function [28, 29].

Prespecified secondary endpoints included mean change from baseline at Week 72 in the SF‐36v2 Physical Functioning domain score and the IWQOL‐Lite‐CT Physical Function composite score. Prespecified exploratory endpoints included mean change from baseline at Week 72 in scores other than Physical Functioning for SF‐36v2 and IWQOL‐Lite‐CT indices. Prespecified subgroup analyses included the assessment of HR‐QoL at Week 72 by selected baseline characteristics, including sex (male, female), baseline BMI (< 35, ≥ 35 kg/m2, corresponding to the cut‐off for the Japanese criteria for high‐degree obesity disease) and age (< 65, ≥ 65 years). Post hoc analyses included assessment of the correlations between percentage body weight loss and improvements in IWQOL‐Lite‐CT and SF‐36v2 scores.

Statistical Analyses

Data from 17 medical research centres were included in this analysis [24]. Efficacy was evaluated in the modified intention‐to‐treat population, which included all randomized participants who received at least one dose of study drug. The prespecified estimand for efficacy was the efficacy estimand, using the efficacy analysis set, comprising data obtained during active treatment and excluding data after permanent study drug discontinuation. Only participants with a non‐missing baseline value and at least one non‐missing post‐baseline value were included in the analysis.

Post‐baseline measures were assessed using an analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) model with baseline value as covariate and hyperlipidaemia at screening, MASLD at screening, IGT at screening, sex and treatment as factors. Missing values at Week 72 were imputed by last observation carried forward (LOCF). Estimated treatment differences (ETDs) were reported as least‐squares mean with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). p values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. Subgroup analyses were conducted at Week 72 using the primary ANCOVA model with the addition of subgroup and treatment‐by‐subgroup interaction terms. Sex was included as a covariate for age and BMI subgroups only. The treatment‐by‐subgroup interaction was used to assess whether treatment effects differed by subgroup and was deemed significant if p values were < 0.1.

Post hoc analyses were conducted to assess the correlation between percentage change from baseline in body weight and change from baseline in SF‐36v2 and IWQOL‐Lite‐CT scores at 72 weeks. Pearson correlation coefficients were derived and two‐sided p values were calculated to test the null hypothesis of no correlation.

No multiplicity adjustment was applied. The domain‐level, item‐level and summary score analyses, as well as the subgroup and interaction analyses, were exploratory in nature. Statistical analyses were conducted using SAS (version 9.4) or R (version 4.2.2) [30].

Results

Demographics and Baseline Characteristics

Demographics and clinical characteristics have been previously reported for SURMOUNT‐J [24]. Overall, 225 participants were randomly assigned to treatment (placebo, n = 75; tirzepatide 10 mg, n = 73; tirzepatide 15 mg, n = 77). The overall study population included 133 male participants (59.1%) and 92 female participants (40.9%) and had a mean (SD) age of 50.8 (10.7) years and body weight of 92.0 (14.9) kg (Table 1). Overall mean (SD) BMI was 33.5 (4.4) kg/m2, comprising 148 (65.8%) participants with a BMI < 35 kg/m2 and 77 (34.2%) participants with a BMI ≥ 35 kg/m2. A total of 201 participants provided IWQOL‐Lite‐CT assessment data, and 200 provided SF‐36v2 assessment data. Baseline scores for the IWQOL‐Lite‐CT and SF‐36v2 were well balanced across treatment arms (Table 1).

Change From Baseline in HR‐QoL at Week 72

The least‐squares mean (standard error) changes from baseline in the IWQOL‐Lite‐CT Physical Function composite score at Week 72 were 15.2 (2.1) and 13.0 (2.0) in the tirzepatide 10‐ and 15‐mg groups, respectively, compared with 2.2 (2.0) in the placebo group, representing ETDs (95% CI) of 13.0 (7.4, 18.7) for tirzepatide 10 mg and 10.8 (5.4, 16.3) for tirzepatide 15 mg (p < 0.001 for both comparisons; Figure 1). The Physical composite, Psychosocial composite and Total scores were also all significantly improved with both doses of tirzepatide compared with placebo (Figure 1). At Week 72, tirzepatide significantly improved several individual item scores on the IWQOL‐Lite‐CT compared with placebo, including physical items (‘trouble bending over’, ‘walking up flight of stairs’, ‘unable to stand for 20–30 minutes’, ‘not as physically active as would like’ and ‘unable to walk far or quickly’) and psychosocial items (‘less confident because of weight’, ‘others judge me based on weight’, ‘frustrated shopping for clothes’, ‘feel bad seeing myself in pictures’, ‘feel down or depressed about weight’, ‘worried about my health’, ‘self‐conscious about my weight’, ‘frustrated with myself about weight’, ‘avoid social gatherings because of weight’ and ‘uncomfortable in public places’; Figure S1).

Associations of HR‐QoL Improvements With Weight Loss and Baseline Characteristics

Statistically significant weight reductions were previously reported for tirzepatide compared with placebo across age, sex and BMI subgroups at Week 72 in the SURMOUNT‐J trial [31]. Statistically significant improvements in IWQOL‐Lite‐CT Physical Function composite, Physical composite and Total scores were also observed at Week 72 with both tirzepatide dose levels compared with placebo across all sex and BMI subgroups and in the < 65 years subgroup, with the exception of Total score for the tirzepatide 10 mg female subgroup (Figure 3). Statistically significant improvements in Psychosocial composite scores were observed for one or both tirzepatide dose levels in the male, BMI < 35 kg/m2, and < 65 years subgroups, with similar trends evident in the female and BMI ≥ 35 kg/m2 subgroups. The benefit of tirzepatide was lower and did not reach statistical significance for any IWQOL‐Lite‐CT scores in the ≥ 65 years subgroup, which had a small sample size and higher variability than the < 65 years subgroup. Overall, treatment effects on IWQOL‐Lite‐CT scores did not vary significantly by subgroup.

Statistically significant improvements in the SF‐36v2 Physical Functioning domain score were observed at Week 72 with one or both tirzepatide dose levels compared with placebo across all subgroups except for the ≥ 65 years subgroup (Figures [Link], [Link]). Treatment effects were generally similar between subgroups for SF‐36v2 component summary and domain scores, although some subgroup differences were observed. Interaction p values did not reach significance for the sex subgroups for any SF‐36v2 score (Figure S3). For the BMI subgroups, the interaction p value reached significance (p = 0.014) for the Role‐Physical domain score, although the directionality of the treatment effect was inconsistent (Figure S4). For the age subgroups, interaction p values reached significance for the Physical Component summary score (p = 0.005) and the Role‐Physical (p < 0.001), Bodily Pain (p = 0.069) and Role‐Emotional (p = 0.002) domain scores, with the ≥ 65 years subgroup showing a less favourable treatment effect for these items with tirzepatide compared with the < 65 years subgroup (Figure S5).

Discussion

In SURMOUNT‐J, tirzepatide was generally well‐tolerated and produced substantial weight reduction and improvements in cardiometabolic parameters in Japanese adults with obesity disease [24]. Consistent with these effects, the current analysis found that tirzepatide 10 and 15 mg were associated with improvements in HR‐QoL at Week 72 compared with placebo based on patient‐reported outcomes on the IWQOL‐Lite‐CT and SF‐36v2, with significant improvements observed for both physical and psychosocial functioning. Additionally, SF‐36v2 Mental Health domain scores improved with tirzepatide treatment, reaching significance in the 10 mg group. Interpreted within an on‐treatment framework, these findings suggest that tirzepatide treatment improved multiple aspects of weight‐related HR‐QoL and functional health status. Unlike the clear dose–response relationship observed for body weight reduction in SURMOUNT‐J [24], IWQOL‐Lite‐CT outcomes did not exhibit a monotonic dose–response pattern. This observation is consistent with the complex and often nonlinear relationship between weight loss and HR‐QoL [32]. Additionally, differences in tolerability between the two doses may have influenced individual participants' reported experience.

Interpretation of these improvements in HR‐QoL should consider thresholds for clinically meaningful within‐patient improvement. A recent analysis of the STEP 6 clinical study, which examined East Asian adults with obesity receiving semaglutide, defined clinically meaningful thresholds of ≥ 3.7 points for the SF‐36v2 Physical Functioning score and ≥ 14.6, ≥ 13.5, ≥ 16.2 and ≥ 16.6 points for the IWQOL‐Lite‐CT Physical Function, Physical, Psychosocial and Total scores, respectively [18]. In the current analysis, some mean improvements met these thresholds (e.g., 15.2‐point improvement from baseline in IWQOL‐Lite‐CT Physical Function with tirzepatide 10 mg), whereas many did not, and within‐patient analysis was not conducted. Therefore, the statistically significant differences observed between the tirzepatide and placebo groups in SURMOUNT‐J may not necessarily translate into clinically meaningful benefit for individual patients.

Long‐term HR‐QoL data associated with tirzepatide treatment in Asian populations with obesity or overweight are limited. The current findings align with those reported for selective GLP‐1RAs [18, 33] and tirzepatide [13, 14, 15, 16, 23] for weight management. SURMOUNT‐CN evaluated tirzepatide in Chinese adults with obesity plus obesity‐related complications, demonstrating significant improvements in the SF‐36v2 Physical Functioning domain score and the IWQOL‐Lite‐CT Physical Function composite score after 52 weeks compared with placebo [23]. Improvements in both physical and psychosocial HR‐QoL measures were also observed with tirzepatide across the global SURMOUNT‐1‐4 studies [13, 14, 15, 16]. Consistent with the findings from SURMOUNT‐J, all IWQOL‐Lite‐CT scores improved significantly with tirzepatide versus placebo at Week 72 in participants with obesity or overweight, both with T2D (SURMOUNT‐2 [15]) and without T2D (SURMOUNT‐1 [16]). Comparable benefits were seen at Week 72 in participants with obesity or overweight without T2D who had previously achieved ≥ 5% weight loss following a 12‐week intensive lifestyle intervention (SURMOUNT‐3 [13]) and in participants with obesity or overweight during an 88‐week randomized withdrawal study (SURMOUNT‐4 [14]). The similar improvements in IWQOL‐Lite‐CT scores across SURMOUNT‐J and global SURMOUNT trials provide evidence that tirzepatide may deliver consistent weight‐related HR‐QoL benefits during treatment across different populations, regardless of cultural context or healthcare settings.

Compared with the current findings in Japanese participants, tirzepatide treatment was associated with broader benefits across multiple SF‐36v2 domains (e.g., Social Functioning, Role‐Emotional) in the global SURMOUNT studies. Potential explanations for this observation include ethnic differences in metabolic risk factors, such as the predisposition of Japanese individuals to visceral adiposity [2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7], and sociocultural factors. People with obesity in Japan frequently experience weight‐related self‐stigma and self‐blame [34], which may attenuate improvements in mental and emotional health domains despite clinically significant weight loss. It should also be noted that baseline mental HR‐QoL was relatively high in SURMOUNT‐J, and this may have precluded observable improvements in mental health and social functioning domains.

With the exception of the ≥ 65 years subgroup, which included few participants and exhibited high variability, IWQOL‐Lite‐CT scores generally improved across subgroups with at least one of the tirzepatide dose levels. SF‐36v2 Physical Functioning showed the most consistent improvements across subgroups, with limited evidence of subgroup interactions. Tirzepatide‐mediated HR‐QoL improvements did not meaningfully differ across BMI subgroups, despite the association of higher BMI with worsening HR‐QoL in people with obesity [10], including those with BMI ≥ 35 kg/m2 in real‐world data from Japan [34]. These findings are consistent with those from the STEP 6 trial, where semaglutide 2.4 and 1.7 mg similarly improved weight and HR‐QoL across BMI categories [18]. Collectively, the data suggest that the beneficial effects of selective GLP‐1RAs and tirzepatide on HR‐QoL may be largely independent of baseline BMI.

Across SURMOUNT‐1‐4, participants achieving greater body weight reduction with tirzepatide consistently reported numerically greater gains in HR‐QoL [13, 14, 15, 16]. In SURMOUNT‐J, correlations were observed between weight loss and improvements on the IWQOL‐Lite‐CT. This suggests that greater weight loss was associated with HR‐QoL improvement, although the correlations were modest, likely reflecting substantial individual variability. Importantly, correlation coefficients were similar between the placebo (lifestyle modifications alone) and tirzepatide groups, although tirzepatide produced significantly greater mean improvements in both weight loss and IWQOL‐Lite‐CT scores compared with placebo. These findings suggest that the magnitude of weight reduction achievable during tirzepatide treatment may enable gains in HR‐QoL that are unlikely to be achieved through lifestyle modifications alone. Additionally, the HR‐QoL outcomes may reflect not only the direct impact of weight loss on daily physical function and mental health but also the indirect impact of improvement in cardiometabolic health and reduction of visceral fat levels and OHRD burden seen with tirzepatide in SURMOUNT‐J [24]. Overall, these findings suggest that tirzepatide treatment is associated with improvements in HR‐QoL in Japanese individuals with obesity disease, a key JASSO treatment goal [8].

In the current analysis, HR‐QoL was examined via two patient‐reported measures which assessed multiple aspects of psychosocial and mental health alongside physical functioning to provide a more comprehensive picture of the impact of tirzepatide on HR‐QoL in Japanese participants with obesity disease. This study adds to the limited data available on HR‐QoL outcomes for anti‐obesity medications in Japan. However, this study had several limitations that should be considered when interpreting the data. First, the study was designed to evaluate long‐term outcomes at Week 72, and the absence of intermediate assessments prevents the characterization of the full time course of HR‐QoL changes. Second, missing data at Week 72 were handled using LOCF, which could underestimate variability and bias results if discontinuation is related to tolerability or treatment response. In SURMOUNT‐J, treatment discontinuation rates were 11% for placebo, 18% for tirzepatide 10 mg and 16% for tirzepatide 15 mg [24]. Given the dose‐escalation design of the study and the known tolerability profile of tirzepatide, a substantial proportion of these discontinuations would be expected to occur before participants achieved sustained exposure to maintenance dosing, when HR‐QoL improvements are anticipated to be limited. Accordingly, the application of LOCF is likely to yield conservative rather than inflated estimates of treatment effects. Generalizability of the current findings may also be limited. SURMOUNT‐J only enrolled Japanese individuals with obesity disease with BMI ≥ 27 kg/m2 who had a subset of predefined ORHDs (hyperlipidaemia, MASLD, or IGT/impaired fasting glucose), excluding individuals with T2D. Therefore, the current findings may not extend to individuals with more mild obesity or with T2D or other ORHDs. Few participants had impaired physical function at baseline (n = 8 with mean [SD] baseline IWQOL‐Lite‐CT Physical Function composite score 51.3 [28.8]), precluding meaningful analysis of HR‐QoL outcomes in this subgroup, despite other SURMOUNT studies showing larger benefits in participants with greater baseline limitations [13, 14, 15, 16]. Additionally, subgroup analyses included a low number of participants aged ≥ 65 years, limiting conclusions regarding age‐related differences in tirzepatide‐mediated HR‐QoL improvements. Finally, SF‐36v2 scores were calculated using United States norm‐based methods, consistent with other SURMOUNT trials, but cultural differences in health perception may affect interpretation of absolute norm‐based scores.

Conclusion

The current analysis demonstrates that Japanese participants experienced similar HR‐QoL improvements to those observed in the global SURMOUNT clinical program, supporting tirzepatide's potential to enhance HR‐QoL across different populations with obesity or overweight. These findings align with JASSO treatment goals for obesity disease and high‐degree obesity disease, with tirzepatide associated with benefits in body weight, ORHD prevalence, cardiometabolic parameters and HR‐QoL for Japanese participants with obesity disease.

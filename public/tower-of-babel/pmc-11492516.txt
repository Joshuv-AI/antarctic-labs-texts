The epidemiology of asthma in Mainland China: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Jiarui Yu, Liang Xu, Azhu Han, Maomao Xie
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundReliable estimates of asthma prevalence are essential for developing effective public health policies. However, discrepancies in published results have posed challenges for public health planning in China. To address this issue, we conducted this study to provide a comprehensive assessment of the epidemiology of asthma prevalence in China.MethodsWe conducted a thorough systematic review across multiple databases including PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), Wanfang Data, and Weipu database. The search was limited to studies published in either Chinese or English up to August 31, 2023, that reported on asthma prevalence in China. We extracted relevant study characteristics and performed meta-analyses. The study protocol was registered with PROSPERO (CRD42023397949).ResultsA literature search yielded 192 studies that met the inclusion criteria out of 17,152 articles. The overall prevalence of asthma in Mainland China was found to be 2.20% (95% confidence interval, CI: 1.96-2.47%; I2 = 99.8%), with the prevalence of ever asthma at 2.21%, current asthma at 2.16%, ever asthma-like symptoms at 14.36%, and current asthma-like symptoms at 3.01%. Using a random-effects model, the prediction interval ranged from 0.44 to 10.27%. Meta-analysis subgroups indicated that men or children and the elderly generally have a higher prevalence of asthma. In addition, the prevalence of asthma is higher in urban areas and in the East China. The prevalence of asthma has been on the rise over the past three decades, from 0.69% in 1984 to 5.30% in 2021. Projections suggest that the prevalence could reach 9.76% by 2050 for the Chinese population.ConclusionsAsthma remains less prevalent in China than in western countries, however its prevalence has been gradually increasing over the past thirty years. The disease burden shows notable variations based on sex, age, and geographic regions, indicating significant demographic and regional disparities. To guide decision-making priorities and direct future research, conducting high-quality epidemiological studies on asthma is crucial.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-024-20330-1.

Background

Asthma, a common chronic airway disease affecting children and adults worldwide, has become a significant public health concern [1–3]. Symptoms include wheezing, shortness of breath, chest tightness, and cough, and these symptoms may vary in intensity and duration, leading to airflow limitation. The prevalence of asthma has risen globally, particularly in industrialized countries, affecting between 1 and 18% of populations across various countries [4]. In 2017, asthma resulted in 495,100 deaths and 22.8 million disability-adjusted life-years [5, 6]. Over the past three decades, trends in prevalence and severity of asthma symptoms have varied by age group, country income, and region [7, 8]. An increasing number of studies indicated that interactions between infections, atopy, genetic susceptibility and environmental exposures (such as pollen, farmyard environment, air pollution and tobacco smoke exposure) influence the development of wheezing illness and the risk of progression to asthma [9, 10]. China, a rapidly industrializing country with dense populations in megacities, is experiencing changes in the incidence and prevalence of asthma due to rapid changes in lifestyle and environmental exposures.

Asthma is a heterogeneous disease. The diagnosis of asthma is primarily based on respiratory symptoms of wheezing, coughing, chest tightness, and/or difficulty breathing, as well as physiological evidence of variable expiratory airflow limitation. In China, existing epidemiological studies indicate that the prevalence of asthma varies widely, with estimates ranging from 0.22 to 20.2% across different regions [11–13]. However, many of these studies have limitations, such as small sample sizes or a focus on specific geographic regions, which make them less representative of the general Chinese population [14–17]. Additionally, variations in reported asthma prevalence can be attributed to differences in sampling methods, data collection techniques, and criteria for diagnosing asthma [18–20]. Despite China’s vast population, there has yet to be a comprehensive study on the national prevalence of asthma throughout the entire life-course. From the perspective of public health, reliable estimates are crucial to adequately prevent and treat asthma, as well as allocate health resources and make evidence-based policies.

Although previous systematic reviews have attempted to determine the prevalence of asthma in children or adults [21–23], the reported results were inconsistent. The inconsistency may be due to various factors such as individual, geographical, and environmental influences. Therefore, a comprehensive review of asthma prevalence across different characteristics is necessary. This review aims to fill the gap by providing recent epidemiology evidence for the prevalence of asthma in China. It will also involve stratification by sex, age, residence, region, and time period to provide a comprehensive understanding of the asthma burden in China. In addition, we further project the trends in asthma prevalence in China by the mid-21st century, especially 2050, to provide valuable insights into the specific patterns of asthma prevalence in China.

Methods

This review was registered with PROSPERO (CRD42020150945) and conducted according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 guidelines [24] (Supplementary Table 2).

Search strategy

A thorough literature search was conducted on databases, including PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Chinese National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), Wanfang Data (Chinese database), and Weipu database (Chinese database). The literature search included studies published from the inception of each database until August 31, 2023. The search terms included: (asthma) AND (prevalence OR incidence OR rate OR risk factors OR epidemi*) AND (China or Chinese). The search strategy details can be found in the Supplementary Table 1.

Eligibility criteria

The included articles on the quantified prevalence of asthma must meet specific eligibility criteria. These criteria primarily focus on research based on Chinese population and provide numerical estimates of asthma prevalence. In cases where duplicates were identified, we excluded the study that with the smallest sample size. For instance, if Study A and Study B were published as part of a multi-center research project, with Study A having a sample size of 5,000 while Study B having a sample size of 3000, we excluded Study B to ensure that each participant was counted only once in our analysis. However, if different features or subgroups of the same research were reported in separate studies, all studies were included. Additionally, if two or more publications reported the same incidence or prevalence of asthma from the same location during an overlapping period, only the first study identified was included. Data extraction

After eliminating duplicates from different bibliographic databases, two researchers (JY and LX) independently conducted a thorough screening of titles and abstracts from all retrieved articles in the literature search. Their goal was to identify potentially relevant articles based on the pre-defined selection criteria. When a disagreement arise, a third researcher (AH) was consulted to resolve it. After screening, the researchers examined the full text of the identified articles and extracted relevant data into an electronic spreadsheet. The data included information such as the first author’s name, publication date, region, period, sample size, overall prevalence, study design, sampling method, and diagnostic criteria. Examples of diagnostic criteria include standardized questionnaires such as the American Thoracic Society (ATS), European Community Respiratory Health Survey (ECRHS), and International Study of Asthma and Allergies in Childhood Questionnaire (ISAACQ). Additionally, the outcome of asthma were recorded, including ever asthma, current asthma, ever asthma-like symptoms, and current asthma-like symptoms. Subgroup analysis covered factors like sex, age, residence, region, period, sampling methods, sample size, and study quality.

Quality evaluation

The evidence quality for the included studies in this research was assessed using the critical appraisal checklist for analytical cross-sectional studies, developed by the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) Critical Appraisal tool [25] (Supplementary Table 4). This checklist evaluates the methodological quality of studies and identify potential biases in their design, conduct, and analysis. It consists of nine criteria, and each rated as follows: Yes (1 point), No (0 points), Not Applicable (0 points), or Unclear (0 points). The cumulative score for each study can range from 0 to 9, higher scores indicating studies of higher quality.

Statistical analysis

Before pooling asthma prevalence estimates, we used Logit transformation to stabilize the variance of the raw prevalence from each included study [26, 27]. In the meta-analysis, we retrieved the sample size and events for asthma in China. All pooled prevalence of asthma rates, incidences with 95% CIs were calculated using random-effects meta-analyses, as effect sizes were sampled from a wide variety of studies with different populations and protocols. Statistical heterogeneity is expressed as I² as well as τ², and prediction intervals were calculated where possible [28, 29]. Subgroup analyses were conducted to identify potential sources of heterogeneity. Therefore, we conducted a number of subgroup analyses as follows: (1) sex (female, male); (2) age (children aged ≤ 14 years, adults aged from 15 to 64 years, elderly aged ≥ 65 years); (3) residence (rural, urban); (4) regions (south, east, north, southwest, northwest, central, and northeast); (5) periods (before 2000, 2000–2005, 2006–2010, 2011–2015, and after 2016); (6) sampling methods (random sampling, cluster sampling, stratified cluster sampling, stratified sampling, multistage sampling, probability proportional sampling, and others); (7) sample size (2,000–10,000; 10,000–50,000; 50,000-100,000; ≥100,000); (8) study quality (score of 5, 6, 7, 8, and 9); (9) outcome (ever asthma, current asthma, ever asthma-like symptoms, current asthma-like symptoms). The definitions used in this review are as follows: (a) ever asthma: a history of asthma diagnosis by a physician; (b) current asthma: individuals with a physician diagnosis of asthma who have experienced symptoms or used asthma medication within the past 12 months; (c) ever asthma-like symptoms: a history of wheezing, shortness of breath, chest tightness, and cough; (d) current asthma-like symptoms: clinical manifestations such as wheezing, shortness of breath, chest tightness, and cough occurring within the past 12 months.

We also conducted sensitivity to evaluate the robustness of the pooled results. To ascertain whether any individual study exerted a disproportionally large influence, we implemented a leave-one-out sensitivity analysis for each meta-analysis. Additionally, we have conducted additional sensitivity analyses focusing on methodological subgroups, including sampling methods, sample size, and study quality. Publication bias was assessed qualitatively through visual inspection of funnel plots and quantitatively using the Egger linear regression test [30] and the Begg rank correlation test, applicable when more than 10 estimates were available in a single analysis.

In addition, we conducted a future asthma prevalence projection for the general population in China. To accurately describe and fit the asthma prevalence data, we employed a hierarchical Bayesian approach [31, 32]. To account for the heterogeneity across studies included in the systematic review and meta-analysis reporting asthma prevalence, we incorporated a random effects parameter. The overall prevalence of asthma was calculated by dividing the number of individuals diagnosed with asthma by the total number of samples reported. Within the Bayesian model framework, the prevalence is treated as a random variable with an unknown parameter. Therefore, we applied the Logit transformation to the asthma prevalence estimates. Considering the observed heterogeneity among the studies, we included a random effects parameter using the conjugate gamma distribution: Gamma (0.01, 0.01). The Gamma distribution is commonly used for modeling unknowns within the range from 0 to infinity. We assigned noninformative normal priors, represented as ß ~ Normal (0, 0.0001), to all coefficients and the intercept in the model. The posterior distributions were estimated using the Just Another Gibbs Sampler (R2JAGS) and Markov-chain Monte Carlo procedure (MCMC pack). We assessed the convergence of our model based on the Gelman-Rubin convergence statistics.

The management of literature was conducted using Endnote, version 20 (Thomson ResearchSoft, Connecticut, United States). All data analyses were performed using R software, version 4.3.1 (The R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). The meta-analysis was conducted using the “meta” package, and the two-sided P-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Study characteristics

The search process is illustrated in Fig. 1. The initial literature search retrieved 17,152 citations, of which 3,386 were excluded due to duplications and irrelevant. After screening the titles and abstracts, a further 13,315 records were excluded, with 439 reviewed in full-text. Ultimately, 192 articles met the eligibility criteria and were included in this review. These articles collectively involved 6,865,401 participants, with individual study sizes ranging from 2,011 to 984,131 participants. The selected articles spanned a publication period from1987 to 2023 and covered all 31 provinces, autonomous regions, or municipalities of China. The majority of these studies were cross-sectional surveys, with most JBI scores exceeding 6 points. Supplementary Table 3 provides further details on the characteristics of included studies.

Prevalence of asthma

A random effects model was utilized to calculate the pooled prevalence of asthma in the general Chinese population, given the significant heterogeneity. The pooled prevalence of asthma was estimated to be 2.20% (95% CI: 1.96-2.47%; I2 = 99.8%). The prediction interval, derived from the random effects model, ranged from 0.44 to 10.27%, indicating that the true prevalence in future studies may fall within this range and reflecting a certain degree of heterogeneity across studies. Further analysis revealed that the pooled prevalence for current asthma, ever asthma, current asthma-like symptoms, and ever asthma-like symptoms were 2.16% (95% CI: 1.59-2.91%; I2 = 99.2%), 2.21% (95% CI: 1.96-2.49%; I2 = 99.8%), 3.01% (95% CI: 2.30-3.92%; I2 = 96.9%), and 14.36% (95% CI: 2.54-51.86%; I2 = 99.8%), respectively (Fig. 2).

Fig. 2Prevalence of asthma among Chinese according to different subgroups

Prevalence of asthma among Chinese according to different subgroups

Figure 2 illustrates the asthma prevalence rates by sex and age group, based on an analysis 129 studies. The results indicate that males have a higher prevalence of asthma at 2.28% (95% CI: 2.00-2.61%; I2 = 99.5%) compared to females at 1.73% (95% CI: 1.51-1.97%; I2 = 99.3%). Among the age groups, the elderly exhibits the highest prevalence at 2.37% (95% CI: 1.85-3.02%; I2 = 96.3%), followed by children aged 0–14 years with a prevalence of 2.27% (95% CI: 1.98-2.60%; I2 = 99.7%). In contrast, adults had a notably lower asthma prevalence of 1.09% (95% CI: 0.92-1.30%; I2 = 99.0%). In addition, we further divided the age groups in the supplementary materials and found that the asthma prevalence rates for the age ranges of 0–5 years, 6–14 years, 15–24 years, 25–45 years, 46–65 years, and over 65 years were 2.49%, 2.98%, 0.91%, 0.78%, 1.51%, and 2.37%, respectively (see Supplementary Fig. 4).

The findings of our subgroup meta-analysis, illustrated in Fig. 3, reveal significant geographical disparities in asthma prevalence among various regions and provinces. East China shows the highest asthma prevalence at 2.65% (95% CI: 2.25-3.13%), whereas Northeast China reports the lowest at 1.25% (95% CI: 0.91-1.70%). The provincial data indicates that the four provinces with the highest overall prevalence rates are Chongqing, Fujian, Shanghai, and Anhui, with rates of 3.83% (95% CI: 2.48-5.85%), 3.35% (95% CI: 2.73-4.11%), 3.33% (95% CI: 2.21-5.23%), and 3.27% (95% CI: 2.19-4.85%), respectively. In contrast, the four provinces with the lowest overall prevalence rates are Tibet, Qinghai, Ningxia, and Yunnan, with rates of 0.39% (95% CI: 0.15-1.05%), 0.55% (95% CI: 0.36-0.84%), 0.73% (95% CI: 0.58-0.92%), and 1.00% (95% CI: 0.82-1.21%), respectively (Supplementary Fig. 6). Furthermore, our study reveals a disparity between urban and rural areas, with urban regions showing a higher asthma prevalence of 2.40% (95% CI: 1.78-3.22%) compared 1.52% (95% CI: 1.12-2.05%) in rural area.

Prevalence of asthma in Mainland China by geographic region

Figure 4 and Supplementary Fig. 7 illustrate the changing pattern and future projections of asthma prevalence in the general population of China. Over the past three decades, the graph shows a clear upward in asthma prevalence, with the rate rising from 0.69% in 1984 to 5.30% in 2021 based on the trend analysis. This growth corresponds to an average annual growth rate of approximately 0.07%. The surge in asthma prevalence in China in the past few decades has caused concern. Before 2000, the prevalence was 1.25% (95% CI: 0.96-1.63%; I2 = 99.9%). However, by 2016, the prevalence had more than doubled to 3.33% (95% CI: 2.41-4.50%; I2 = 99.9%). If this trajectory continues unabated, projections suggest that by 2050, asthma may affect nearly one-tenth of the population in China, equivalent to 9.76% (95% CI:5.11-16.46%) of the population (Fig. 4).

Sensitivity analysis and publication bias

In this study, we assessed heterogeneity, conducted subgroup analyses, and evaluated publication bias. Significant heterogeneity was observed in the pooled estimates concerning sex, age, residents, and geographic regions (I2 range 98.9–99.9%; all P < 0.05) (Fig. 2). Sensitivity analyses indicated that the pooled prevalence of asthma among Chinese varied from 2.18 to 2.22% after removing a single study at one time (Supplemental Fig. 4). Notably, no individual study exerted an undue influence on the overall pooled prevalence. Additionally, sensitivity analysis focusing on methodological subgroups, including sampling methods, sample size, and research quality, showed that small sample sizes and multistage sampling may increase the prevalence rates of asthma. However, the presence of publication bias was suggested both by the funnel plot (Supplemental Fig. 3) and by Egger’s regression test (P < 0.001).

Discussion

In this study, we conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis of 192 researched across 31 provinces in China from 1984 to 2021. Our findings indicated that the overall prevalence of asthma in the general Chinese population was 2.20%, with males exhibiting a significantly higher prevalence than females. Using a random-effects model, the prediction interval ranged from 0.44 to 10.27%. Additionally, children and the elderly exhibited higher prevalence rates than adults. The study also highlighted geographic disparities, with the eastern regions of China demonstrating a higher prevalence of asthma. Trend analysis revealed a steady increase in the prevalence of asthma from 0.69% in 1984 to 5.30% in 2021, which translates to an annual increase of 0.07%. Based on these trends, we estimate that the prevalence of asthma in China will reach 9.76% by 2050.

The global prevalence of asthma varies significantly, with China reporting lower rates compared to most Western countries. In China, asthma prevalence is highest among children aged 6–14, with a rate of 2.98%, and is generally higher in children and the elderly than in adults. Notably, asthma prevalence is higher in males than females during childhood, a trend observed globally [14, 33, 34]. This difference may be attributed to factors such as sex hormones, genetic and epigenetic variations, and environmental influences [35]. However, this trend reverses in adulthood, where asthma becomes more prevalent in women, likely due to hormonal changes and differences in airway anatomy and physiology [36, 37]. Understanding these sex-specific differences is crucial for tailoring asthma therapies, as boys tend to experience more severe asthma symptoms before puberty, while women report worse symptoms up to menopause [36]. The distinct patterns of asthma prevalence and severity across age and gender underscore the importance of considering these factors in both clinical practice and public health strategies.

This review highlights the significant variation in asthma prevalence across different regions of China, with rates varying widely from 0.22 to 20.20% [38–41]. The eastern regions of China exhibited the highest asthma prevalence at 2.65%, while the Northeast had the lowest at 1.25%. Recent studies have shown that atmospheric conditions can directly and indirectly influence the prevalence of asthma, and extreme ambient temperatures can heighten the risk of asthma attacks [42]. In addition to climate factors, air pollution, overcrowding in cities, diet, and genetics can also affect asthma prevalence rates. Moreover, research suggest that asthma prevalence is higher in large central metropolitan areas compared to micropolitan or noncore regions [16, 43–46]. Interestingly, rural areas in China have report lower rates of asthma-like symptoms and cases, supporting the “hygiene hypothesis” and indicating lower asthma in farming communities.

Over the previous three decades, there has been a noticeable increase in the prevalence rates of asthma in China, rising from 0.69% in 1984 to 5.30% in 2021. The Global Asthma Network Phase I cross-sectional study indicated that children in lower-middle-income countries experienced an increase in ever having asthma and night cough, while rates remained stable in upper-middle-income and high-income countries [7]. It is widely acknowledged that asthma is influenced by a combination of genetic and environmental factors [47]. However, the significant rise in asthma prevalence during this relatively short period suggests that environmental factors may play a more crucial role than genetic predispositions [48, 49]. These environmental factors could include changes in societal lifestyles, increased exposure to environmental triggers, advancements in detection technologies, and improved medical standards. Considering the potential future epidemic of asthma in developing countries, it is likely that the prevalence rate of asthma will also increase in these regions. This study further employs the hierarchical Bayesian method to make predictions, and forecasts that by 2050, the prevalence of asthma in China will approach one-tenth. This finding emphasizes the implementing of taking protective measures in China to prevent and manage asthma effectively.

To effectively manage and reduce the health burden caused by asthma, it is essential that we focus on several key aspects: Firstly, asthma is an ongoing challenge, so a through exploration of the phenotypes and endotypes of asthma is necessary [50]. This requires innovative approaches to identify, diagnose, and accurately classify asthma at early ages. Secondly, combining cohort studies can refine the spectrum of risk factors that induce asthma, and improve our understanding of the long-term significance of early-stage symptoms. This sheds light on the “black box” of the development and onset of asthma from a life-course perspective. Thirdly, the use of Natural Language Processing (NLP) algorithms, a computational phenotyping approach, to extract information from electronic health records is innovative and may lead to early diagnosis of asthma and improved epidemiological studies, enhancing the early warning system for asthma. Finally, further research is necessary to understand the pathophysiological mechanisms of asthma onset and progression, focusing on identifying novel therapeutic targets through the molecular triggers of airway remodeling.

Our meta-analysis provides the most comprehensive evaluation to date regarding the prevalence of asthma in China, incorporating the largest sample of Chinese population ever analyzed in a systematic review focused on this condition. However, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, due to the availability of the Chinese database and English database, this study mainly summarizes literature on asthma prevalence in Mainland China, excluding regions such as Taiwan, Hong Kong and Macao, where relevant Chinese literature retrieval databases are not accessible. Second, we performed the meta-analysis by directly extracting asthma prevalence rates without standardizing adjustments based on the demographic characteristics of the study areas. This method may result in heterogeneity in the study outcomes. Additionally, the prevalence of asthma is highly heterogeneous, which could impact the accuracy of future asthma prevalence predictions. Third, most of the studies included were cross-sectional and lacked detailed phenotypes, clinical evaluations, lung function data, and information on illness severity. Furthermore, even within our subgroup analyses, we observed high levels of heterogeneity. Lastly, although our findings indicate potential publication bias and significant heterogeneity among the included studies, the rate values derived from the sensitivity analysis remained consistently stable.

In conclusion, this study presents the first meta-analysis on the overall prevalence of asthma in China. The findings reveal an increasing trend in asthma prevalence in China in recent years, particularly in Eastern China, although it remains considerably lower than that in Western countries. Additionally, there is considerable variation in asthma prevalence across different regions within China. Given that the pathophysiology of asthma is influenced by environmental exposures, it is crucial to focus on trends in asthma prevalence and prevalence in susceptible areas. By developing innovative approaches for early identification and diagnosis of asthma, we can significantly enhance management strategies and reduce the public health burden associated with this condition.

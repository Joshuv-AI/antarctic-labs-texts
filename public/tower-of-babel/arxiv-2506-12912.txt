Logit Dynamics in Softmax Policy Gradient Methods
Yingru Li

Abstract
We analyzes the logit dynamics of softmax policy gradient methods. We derive the exact formula for the L2 norm of the logit update vector: ‖ Δ ​ 𝐳 ‖ 2 ∝ 1 − 2 ​ P c + C ⁡ ( P ) \|\Delta\mathbf{z}\|_{2}\propto\sqrt{1-2P_{c}+C(P)} This equation demonstrates that update magnitudes are determined by the chosen action’s probability ( P c P_{c} ) and the policy’s collision probability ( C ⁡ ( P ) C(P) ), a measure of concentration inversely related to entropy. Our analysis reveals an inherent self-regulation mechanism where learning vigor is automatically modulated by policy confidence, providing a foundational insight into the stability and convergence of these methods.



1
Introduction

Policy gradient methods
[
2
,
1
]
update parameters based on the product of three factors: learning rate, advantage estimate, and score function. In softmax policies, the score function has a specific structure that creates rich, self-regulating dynamics. This analysis dissects these dynamics by focusing on the logits—the raw outputs of a model before the softmax normalization.

By focusing on the dynamics of the logits themselves, we can gain fundamental insights into the learning process that are independent of the specific policy parameterization
\theta
(e.g., whether the policy is a linear model or a deep neural network). This analysis reveals the intended change in the policy’s output distribution, offering a clear view of the adaptive mechanisms inherent in the softmax function.

2
Mathematical Foundation

This section outlines the core mathematical concepts, clarifying how the general policy gradient update translates into specific, structured updates for the policy’s logits.

2.1
Policy Gradient Framework

The policy gradient theorem
[
2
]
provides the gradient of the expected return
J(\theta)
with respect to policy parameters
\theta
:

\nabla_{\theta}J(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{s,a}\left[\nabla_{\theta}\log\pi_{\theta}(a|s;\theta)A(s,a)\right]

(1)

Here,
A(s,a)
is the advantage function, and
\pi_{\theta}(a|s;\theta)
is the policy.

For softmax policies, the probability of selecting action
a_{i}
given state
s
is determined by logits
z_{k}(s,\theta)
:

\pi_{\theta}(a_{i}|s;\theta)=\frac{e^{z_{i}(s,\theta)}}{\sum_{k}e^{z_{k}(s,\theta)}}\triangleq P_{i}

(2)

The term
P_{i}
serves as a shorthand for this probability.

2.2
From Parameter Gradients to Logit Updates

The update to the parameters
\theta
is driven by the gradient
\nabla_{\theta}\log\pi_{\theta}
. Using the chain rule, we can decompose this gradient’s effect on the logits
\mathbf{z}
:

\nabla_{\theta}\log\pi_{\theta}=\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{z}}{\partial\theta}\right)\left(\nabla_{\mathbf{z}}\log\pi_{\theta}\right)

(3)

The term
\frac{\partial\mathbf{z}}{\partial\theta}
is the Jacobian of the logits with respect to the parameters, which depends on the specific architecture of the policy network. The second term,
\nabla_{\mathbf{z}}\log\pi_{\theta}
, captures the desired change in the logits to increase the probability of an action.

This paper analyzes the dynamics of this second term. We define the
logit update
,
\Delta z_{j}
, as the effective change to the logit
z_{j}
that the algorithm seeks to induce for a given experience (state
s
, chosen action
a_{c}
, advantage
A
), scaled by a learning rate
\eta
. This provides a parameterization-agnostic view of the learning update.

2.3
The Logit Score Function and Update Equations

The gradient of the log-policy with respect to the logits, which we can call the logit score function, takes a remarkably simple form:

Fundamental Score Function for Softmax Logits

\frac{\partial\log\pi_{\theta}(a_{i}|s;\theta)}{\partial z_{j}(s,\theta)}=\delta_{ij}-P_{j}

(4)

where
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta (1 if
i=j
, 0 otherwise), and
P_{j}=\pi_{\theta}(a_{j}|s;\theta)
.

The logit update,
\Delta z_{j}(s,\theta)
, follows from applying the principle of stochastic gradient ascent to the objective. For a single experience
(s,a_{c},A)
, the update is:

\Delta z_{j}(s,\theta)=\eta\cdot A\cdot\left(\frac{\partial\log\pi_{\theta}(a_{c}|s;\theta)}{\partial z_{j}(s,\theta)}\right)

(5)

Substituting the score function from Equation
4
(with
i=c
) into Equation
5
yields the detailed logit update equations:

Logit Update Equations

For action
a_{c}
chosen with advantage
A\equiv A(s,a_{c})
:

\displaystyle\Delta z_{c}(s,\theta)

\displaystyle=\eta(1-P_{c})A

(chosen action’s logit)

(6)

\displaystyle\Delta z_{o}(s,\theta)

\displaystyle=-\eta P_{o}A

\displaystyle\text{(other actions' logits, }o\neq c\text{)}

(7)

An important consequence of these update rules is the conservation of the sum of logit changes:

Conservation Property

The sum of logit updates is zero:

\sum_{j}\Delta z_{j}(s,\theta)=\eta A\left((1-P_{c})+\sum_{o\neq c}(-P_{o})\right)=\eta A\left(1-\sum_{j}P_{j}\right)=\eta A(1-1)=0

(8)

This zero-sum property is significant. It shows that the updates adjust the
relative
strengths of the logits without altering their sum. This ensures that changes to the probability distribution arise from a
structured redistribution
of probability mass, rather than from a uniform shift across all logits (which the softmax function would render irrelevant to the final probabilities anyway).

4
Update Magnitude and Collision Probability

4.1
The Update Magnitude Formula

The total magnitude of the logit update vector for an experience is:

\|\Delta z\|_{2}=\sqrt{\sum_{j}(\Delta z_{j})^{2}}=\sqrt{(\eta(1-P_{c})A)^{2}+\sum_{o\neq c}(-\eta P_{o}A)^{2}}=\eta|A|\sqrt{(1-P_{c})^{2}+\sum_{o\neq c}P_{o}^{2}}

(9)

By expanding
(1-P_{c})^{2}=1-2P_{c}+P_{c}^{2}
and noting that
P_{c}^{2}+\sum_{o\neq c}P_{o}^{2}=\sum_{a}P_{a}^{2}
, this can be rewritten in a more revealing form:

Logit Update Magnitude Formula

\|\Delta z\|_{2}=\eta|A|\sqrt{1-2P_{c}+C(P)}

(10)

where
C(P)=\sum_{a}P_{a}^{2}
is the collision probability.

4.2
Understanding Collision Probability

The collision probability
C(P)
is a fundamental measure of distribution concentration:

Key Insight

Collision Probability Properties:

•

Definition
:
C(P)=\sum_{a}P_{a}^{2}
- the probability that two independent samples from
P
are identical

•

Range
:
1/n\leq C(P)\leq 1
for
n
actions

•

Minimum
:
C(P)=1/n
when
P
is uniform (maximum entropy/spread)

•

Maximum
:
C(P)=1
when
P
is deterministic (minimum entropy/concentration)

•

Information theoretic
:
C(P)=e^{-H_{2}(P)}
where
H_{2}
is the Rényi entropy of order 2

4.3
How Collision Probability Shapes Learning

The logit update magnitude formula reveals how
C(P)
controls learning dynamics:

\|\Delta z\|_{2}=\eta|A|\underbrace{\sqrt{1-2P_{c}+C(P)}}_{\text{sensitivity factor}}

(11)

This sensitivity factor depends on two components:

1.

Action-specific term
:
(1-2P_{c})
- larger when choosing an unlikely action (
P_{c}\to 0
).

2.

Distribution-wide term
:
C(P)
- larger for more concentrated (low-entropy) distributions.

Key Dynamics:

•

When
P_{c}\approx 0
(exploring):
\|\Delta z\|_{2}\approx\eta|A|\sqrt{1+C(P)}
. The update is large, especially if the policy is already concentrated elsewhere (high
C(P)
).

•

When
P_{c}\approx 1
(exploiting):
\|\Delta z\|_{2}\approx\eta|A|\sqrt{1-2+1}=0
, since
P_{c}\approx 1
implies
C(P)\approx 1
. Confident choices lead to minimal adjustments.

•

Maximum possible:
\|\Delta z\|_{2}\approx\eta|A|\sqrt{2}
when
P_{c}\to 0
and another action is deterministic, so
C(P)\to 1
.

5
Conclusion

The mathematical structure of softmax policy gradients, when analyzed through its effect on logits, reveals an elegant self-regulating system for learning. Our detailed analysis of these logit dynamics highlights several key aspects:

1.

Probability-Dependent Individual Logit Updates:
The change to the chosen action’s logit (
\Delta z_{c}
) is scaled by
(1-P_{c})
, ensuring that rarely-chosen actions (low
P_{c}
) receive proportionally larger updates, facilitating rapid learning from surprising experiences. Conversely, highly probable actions (
P_{c}\approx 1
) see minimal logit changes, promoting stability. Updates to other logits (
\Delta z_{o}
) are scaled by their own probabilities
P_{o}
, ensuring a coherent redistribution of probability mass.

2.

Conservation of Relative Logit Strength:
The property
\sum_{j}\Delta z_{j}=0
is a cornerstone of the logit dynamics. It guarantees that updates are a zero-sum game, redistributing influence among actions rather than shifting all logits up or down uniformly. This is the mechanism that forces a structured change in the resulting probability distribution.

3.

Update Magnitude Governed by Chosen Action and Distribution Concentration:
The L2 norm of the entire logit update vector,
\|\Delta z\|_{2}=\eta|A|\sqrt{1-2P_{c}+C(P)}
, reveals a direct link to the probability of the chosen action
P_{c}
and the collision probability
C(P)=\sum_{a}P_{a}^{2}
.

•

C(P)
is a measure of distribution concentration, inversely related to entropy (e.g.,
C(P)=e^{-H_{2}(P)}
). Low entropy implies high
C(P)
.

•

When exploring (chosen action
P_{c}\approx 0
), the update magnitude becomes large:
\|\Delta z\|_{2}\approx\eta|A|\sqrt{1+C(P)}
. This reflects a significant attempt to shift the policy.

•

When exploiting a dominant action (
P_{c}\approx 1
, so
C(P)\approx 1
), the update magnitude vanishes:
\|\Delta z\|_{2}\approx 0
. This shows that confident choices lead to minimal overall changes in the logit vector, promoting convergence.

This shows that the overall ”vigor” of the logit updates is modulated by both the specific action chosen (
P_{c}
) and the overall concentration (
C(P)
) of the policy, which is intrinsically linked to policy entropy.

4.

Natural Learning Trajectory:
The interplay between
P_{c}
and
C(P)
facilitates a natural transition from exploration to exploitation. Initially, when the policy is uncertain (high entropy, low
C(P)
), it can explore and adapt readily. As it learns and becomes confident (low entropy, high
C(P)
), the updates for those confident actions diminish, leading to stability.

These properties emerge directly from the mathematical definition of the softmax function and the policy gradient theorem. Understanding these precise logit dynamics, especially the role of collision probability in modulating the overall update strength, provides crucial insights for designing algorithms, tuning hyperparameters, and diagnosing issues like premature convergence or insufficient exploration.

Appendix A
Collision Probability, Rényi Entropy, and Shannon Entropy

A.1
Definitions and Relationships

Understanding the exploration-exploitation characteristics of a policy often involves measuring the uncertainty or concentration of its action probability distribution. Several related concepts are key:

For a discrete probability distribution
P=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
:

•

Collision Probability (
C(P)
):
This is the probability that two independent samples drawn from the distribution
P
will be the same.

C(P)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}^{2}

(12)

•

Rényi Entropy of order
\alpha
(
H_{\alpha}(P)
):
This is a family of entropy measures. For
\alpha>0,\alpha\neq 1
:

H_{\alpha}(P)=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}^{\alpha}

(13)

(We assume base
e
for the logarithm, for consistency with
\log
meaning natural logarithm.)

•

Rényi Entropy of order 2 (
H_{2}(P)
):
A special case of Rényi entropy that directly relates to the collision probability:

H_{2}(P)=\frac{1}{1-2}\log\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}^{2}=-\log\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}^{2}=-\log C(P)

(14)

From this, it’s clear that the collision probability can be expressed as:

C(P)=e^{-H_{2}(P)}

(15)

•

Shannon Entropy (
H(P)
):
This is the standard measure of uncertainty in information theory. It is also the limit of the Rényi entropy as
\alpha\to 1
:

H(P)=\lim_{\alpha\to 1}H_{\alpha}(P)=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}\log p_{i}

(16)

Derivation of the Shannon entropy as the limit of Rényi entropy:
The limit
\lim_{\alpha\to 1}\frac{\log\sum p_{i}^{\alpha}}{1-\alpha}
is of the form
\frac{0}{0}
. Applying L’Hôpital’s Rule:

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\to 1}\frac{\frac{d}{d\alpha}\left(\log\sum_{i}p_{i}^{\alpha}\right)}{\frac{d}{d\alpha}(1-\alpha)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\alpha\to 1}\frac{\frac{1}{\sum_{i}p_{i}^{\alpha}}\sum_{i}(p_{i}^{\alpha}\log p_{i})}{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{1}{\sum_{i}p_{i}^{1}}\sum_{i}(p_{i}^{1}\log p_{i})}{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{i}p_{i}\log p_{i}}{-1}=-\sum_{i}p_{i}\log p_{i}

A.2
Qualitative Relationships and the Entropy Ordering Theorem

While individual action probabilities
p_{i}
directly influence logit update magnitudes, the collision probability
C(P)
as a summary statistic provides insight into the distribution’s concentration. It relates to Shannon entropy via the following theorem:

Theorem 1

(Entropy Ordering)
.

For any probability distribution
P
:

H_{2}(P)\leq H(P)

(17)

This implies:

C(P)\geq e^{-H(P)}

(18)

Equality holds in both inequalities if and only if
P
is uniform over its support.

Proof.

The definition of Rényi entropy of order 2 is
H_{2}(P)=-\log C(P)\quad(*)
. A fundamental property of Rényi entropies is that
H_{\alpha}(P)
is a non-increasing function of
\alpha
. That is, for
0<\alpha<\beta
,
H_{\alpha}(P)\geq H_{\beta}(P)
. Since Shannon entropy
H(P)
is the limit as
\alpha\to 1
, we can consider it
H_{1}(P)
. As
1<2
, it follows that
H_{1}(P)\geq H_{2}(P)
, so
H(P)\geq H_{2}(P)
. Substituting equation
(*)
into this inequality:

H(P)\geq-\log C(P)
.
Multiplying by -1 reverses the inequality:
-H(P)\leq\log C(P)
. Exponentiating both sides preserves the inequality:
e^{-H(P)}\leq C(P)
.
∎

This theorem establishes that
H_{2}(P)
provides a lower bound on
H(P)
, and conversely,
e^{-H(P)}
provides a lower bound on
C(P)
.

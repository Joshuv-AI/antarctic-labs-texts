Existence and stability of the Riemann solutions for a non-symmetric Keyfitz–Kranzer type model
Rahul Barthwal, Christian Rohde, Anupam Sen

Abstract
In this article, we develop a new hyperbolic model governing the first-order dynamics of a thin film flow under the influence of gravity and solute transport. The obtained system turns out to be a non-symmetric Keyfitz-Kranzer type system. We find an entire class of convex entropies in the regions where the system remains strictly hyperbolic. By including delta shocks, we prove the existence of unique solutions of the Riemann problem. We analyze their stability with respect to the perturbation of the initial data and to the gravity and surface tension parameters. Moreover, we discuss the large time behaviour of the solutions of the perturbed Riemann problem and prove that the initial Riemann states govern it. Thus, we confirm the structural stability of the Riemann solutions under the perturbation of initial data. Finally, we validate our analytical results with well-established numerical schemes for this new system of conservation laws.



Key words.
Non-symmetric Keyfitz-Kranzer systems, thin film flows, structural stability, measure valued solutions, Riemann problem, nonlinear wave interactions.

Introduction

Keyfitz-Kranzer type systems
[
20
]
are an important class of hyperbolic systems of conservation laws given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle u_{i_{t}}+\big(u_{i}(\phi(u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{n}))\big)_{x}=0,~i=1,2,\ldots,n,~n\in\mathbb{N}.

(1.1)

In recent years, the system (
1.1
) has been analyzed extensively for different choices of
\displaystyle\phi
. Some of the particular choices of
\displaystyle\phi
include
\displaystyle\phi(u)=\phi(|u|)

[
14
,
15
]
for
\displaystyle(n\times n)
systems while
\displaystyle\phi(u_{1},u_{2})=\phi(\alpha u_{1}+\beta u_{2})
,
\displaystyle\phi(u_{1},u_{2})=\phi(u_{1}/u_{2})

[
38
,
39
]
and
\displaystyle\phi(u_{1},u_{2})=\phi(u_{1}u_{2})

[
32
]
for two-component systems. Actually, the different choices of
\displaystyle\phi
describe quite distinct physical phenomena. For instance, the choice of
\displaystyle\phi(u)=\phi(|u|)
describes a simplified magnetohydrodynamics model
[
13
,
14
]
or the elastic string model
[
20
]
, while
\displaystyle\phi(u)=k_{i}u_{i}/(1+\sum u_{i})
describes multi-component chromatography processes
[
17
]
.

For the particular
\displaystyle(2\times 2)
case with
\displaystyle\phi(u)=f(u_{1})
, Karlsen et al.
[
18
]
developed a semi-Godunov scheme based on Riemann solutions for general triangular systems, including the system of the form

\displaystyle u_{1,t}+(u_{1}f(u_{1}))_{x}

\displaystyle=0,

(1.2)

\displaystyle u_{2,t}+(u_{2}f(u_{1}))_{x}

\displaystyle=0.

The system (
1.2
) represents the simplest
\displaystyle(2\times 2)
triangular system of conservation laws.

Another important Keyfitz-Kranzer type system was recently introduced in Conn et al.
[
9
]
. It takes the form

\displaystyle h_{t}+(h^{2}b/2)_{x}

\displaystyle=0,

(1.3)

\displaystyle b_{t}+(hb^{2}/2)_{x}

\displaystyle=0.

Clearly, the system (
1.3
) is a Keyfitz-Kranzer type system with
\displaystyle\phi(h,b)=hb/2
. The system (
1.3
) governs the first-order dynamics of a surface tension-driven thin film flow with an anti-surfactant solute in the absence of gravity. The system (
1.3
) has been analyzed by Sen&Raja Sekhar and Minhajul et al. for classical and nonclassical wave interactions in 1-D

while Barthwal et al. and Pandey et al.
[

,
3
,
28

considered multi-D cases.

A central question in the theory of Cauchy problems for (
1.1
) concerns the stability of Riemann solutions under small perturbations of the initial data or flux parameters. Perturbed Riemann problems involving nonlinear wave interactions have been studied extensively to establish the stability of these solutions (see, e.g.,
[
16
,
22
,
33
,
40
,
41
]
and references therein). We also refer the interested readers to a recent work
[
36
]
for structural stability of Riemann solutions with flux perturbations.

Recent progress in this area leads us to study the structure of the system (
1.3
), when we include the gravity effects. Is the new system well-posed and has unique and stable Riemann solutions, especially in the domains where the system becomes weakly hyperbolic? Furthermore, we analyze the effects of the gravity and surface tension parameters and perturbation of initial data on the stability of these solutions.

In this article, we develop a mathematical model which governs the first-order dynamics of a thin film flow under the influence of gravity and anti-surfactant solute. It turns out to be a non-symmetric Keyfitz-Kranzer type system (

) with
\displaystyle\phi(u_{1},u_{2})=\alpha u_{1}u_{2}+\kappa u_{1}^{2}/3
for
\displaystyle\alpha,\kappa\geq 0
. The model obtained in this article extends the mathematical model considered in
[
3
,
10
]
by considering gravity effects. Precisely, we consider the following system

\displaystyle h_{t}+(\alpha h^{2}b+\kappa h^{3}/3)_{x}

\displaystyle=0,

(1.4)

\displaystyle b_{t}+(\alpha hb^{2}+\kappa h^{2}b/3)_{x}

\displaystyle=0,

where
\displaystyle h(x,t)
is the dimensionless film thickness and
\displaystyle b(x,t)
is the concentration gradient and
\displaystyle\alpha
and
\displaystyle\kappa
are two nonnegative parameters defining the surface tension and gravity effects. Further, the state space is defined as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{U}=\{(h,b)^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}|~~h,b\geq 0\}.

(1.5)

Remark 1.1
.

In this article, we focus on the state space (

\displaystyle h>0

In the second part of the article, we consider the local well-posedness of the Cauchy problem with initial data

3
Hyperbolicity of the system (
1.4
) and the local wellposedness of Cauchy problem

In this section, we discuss the basic properties of the system (
1.4
), which is the one-dimensional version of (
2.7
) and develop a class of strictly convex entropies for it.

3.1
Hyperbolicity and Riemann invariants of the system (
1.4
)

For smooth solutions, (
1.4
) can be written in the primitive form

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbf{U}_{t}+\mathbf{DF(U)}~\mathbf{U}_{x}=0,

(3.1)

where

\mathbf{U}=\begin{bmatrix}h\\
b\end{bmatrix},~\text{and}~\mathbf{DF(U)}=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha hb+\kappa h^{2}&\alpha h^{2}/2\\
\alpha b^{2}/2+2\kappa hb/3&\alpha hb+\kappa h^{2}/3\end{bmatrix}.

By a direct calculation, the eigensystem of the system (
1.4
) is given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(h,b)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\alpha hb+\dfrac{\kappa h^{2}}{3},\quad

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbf{r}_{1}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=(-3\alpha h,3\alpha b+2\kappa h)^{\top},

(3.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(h,b)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=3\alpha hb+\kappa h^{2},\quad

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbf{r}_{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=(h,b)^{\top}.

(3.3)

A straightforward calculation leads us to
\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{U}}\lambda_{1}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{1}=0
, while

\nabla_{\mathbf{U}}\lambda_{2}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{2}=h(3\alpha b+2\kappa h)\neq 0~\mathrm{for}~(h,b)^{\top}\in\mathcal{U}^{\circ}.

This implies that the associated characteristic field of
\displaystyle\lambda_{1}
is linearly degenerate while that of
\displaystyle\lambda_{2}
is either genuinely nonlinear in the interior or linearly degenerate on the borderlines of the state space
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}
. Moreover, we have a clear ordering of the eigenvalues given by

\lambda_{1}(h,b)<\lambda_{2}(h,b)=3\lambda_{1}(h,b),~{\rm{for}}~(h,b)^{\top}\in\mathcal{U}^{\circ}

and

\lambda_{1}(h,b)=\lambda_{2}(h,b)=0,~{\rm{for}}~(h,b)^{\top}\in\partial\mathcal{U}.

This implies that the system (
1.4
) is strictly hyperbolic in the interior of the state space (
1.5
) while for
\displaystyle h=0
(at the boundaries), the system (
1.4
) becomes weakly hyperbolic.

From the characteristic analysis, it follows that the first characteristic field is always linearly degenerate. Consequently, the associated wave is a contact discontinuity
\displaystyle J
. On the other hand, the second characteristic field is genuinely nonlinear. Therefore, the corresponding wave is either a rarefaction wave
\displaystyle R
or a shock wave
\displaystyle S
whenever the state variables
\displaystyle(h,b)^{\top}\in\mathcal{U}^{\circ}
. For states on the boundary of the admissible set, i.e., when
\displaystyle(h,b)^{\top}\in\partial\mathcal{U}
, the wave structure is no longer purely classical. In this case, one may encounter measure-valued solutions or composite waves. We discuss these situations in Section
4.2.2
.

Remark 3.1
.

For the case when
\displaystyle\alpha=0
,
\displaystyle b=0
and
\displaystyle\kappa=1
, the system (
1.4
) collapses to the thin film equation under the influence of gravity, which has been analyzed as a prototype example of scalar conservation laws with nonconvex flux for which the existence of nonclassical waves such as undercompressive shock waves is a well-known result, see e.g.,
[
6

7
]
and references cited therein. Therefore, it would be interesting to analyze whether the formal limit

3.1.1
Riemann invariants of the system (

By definition, the Riemann invariants
\displaystyle w_{i}(h,b)
are parallel to the left eigenvectors such that for
\displaystyle i\in\{1,2\}

\nabla_{\mathbf{U}}w_{i}\cdot r_{i}=0,

where
\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{U}}=(\partial_{h},\partial_{b})^{\top}
. Therefore, one can easily obtain the Riemann invariants given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle w_{1}=\alpha hb+\dfrac{\kappa h^{2}}{3},\qquad w_{2}=\dfrac{b}{h}.

(3.4)

In view of explicit Riemann invariants, we can obtain a class of strictly convex entropies for the system (
1.4
) defined in the regions where the system remains strictly hyperbolic.

3.2
Entropy-entropy pairs for the system (
1.4
) in
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{\circ}

In this section, we obtain a class of entropy-entropy pairs
\displaystyle(\eta,q)
for the system (
1.4
). Moreover, we provide the sufficient conditions under which the obtained entropies are strictly convex. Precisely, we prove the following.

Theorem 3.2

(Strictly convex entropies)
.

Let
\displaystyle\Psi
and
\displaystyle\Theta
be arbitrary functions in
\displaystyle C^{1}(0,\infty)
. There exists a class of entropy/entropy-flux pairs
\displaystyle(\eta,q)
for the system (
1.4
) defined in
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{\circ}
. They are given by

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\eta[h,b]&=&\Psi(w_{1})+\sqrt{w_{1}}\Theta\left(p\right),\\[4.30554pt]
q[h,b]&=&3\left(w_{1}\Psi(w_{1})-\displaystyle\int^{w_{1}}\Psi(w_{1})\,dw_{1}\right)+(w_{1})^{3/2}\Theta(p),\end{array}

(3.5)

where
\displaystyle w_{1}
and
\displaystyle w_{2}
are the Riemann invariants of the system (
1.4
) defined in (
3.4
) and
\displaystyle p
is a function of
\displaystyle w_{2}
defined by
\displaystyle p=3\alpha w_{2}+\kappa
.

Moreover, a sufficient condition for the entropy
\displaystyle\eta[h,b]
to be strictly convex is that the functions
\displaystyle\Psi
and
\displaystyle\Theta
satisfy

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Psi^{\prime\prime}(w_{1})>0,~\Psi^{\prime}(w_{1})<0,~2w_{1}\Psi^{\prime\prime}(w_{1})+\Psi^{\prime}(w_{1})>0,~\Theta(p)=A\sqrt{p}+\dfrac{B}{\sqrt{p}},

(3.6)

for some arbitrary nonnegative constants
\displaystyle A
and
\displaystyle B
.

Proof.

Since
\displaystyle w_{1}=\alpha hb+\dfrac{\kappa h^{2}}{3}
and
\displaystyle w_{2}=3\alpha hb+\kappa h^{2}
are the Riemann invariants of the system (
1.4
), we must have

\displaystyle(w_{1})_{t}+3w_{1}(w_{1})_{x}=0,

(3.7)

\displaystyle(w_{2})_{t}+w_{1}(w_{2})_{x}=0,

where we have used the fact that
\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(w_{1},w_{2})=3\lambda_{1}(w_{1},w_{2})=3w_{1}
.

Thus by comparing the mixed derivatives of
\displaystyle q
, any entropy pair
\displaystyle(\eta,q)[w_{1},w_{2}]
must satisfy

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2w_{1}\eta_{w_{1}w_{2}}-\eta_{w_{2}}=0,

which implies that any entropy
\displaystyle\eta
should be of the form

\displaystyle\displaystyle\eta[w_{1},w_{2}]=\Psi(w_{1})+\sqrt{w_{1}}\Theta(w_{2}),

(3.8)

along with the entropy flux given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle q[w_{1},w_{2}]=3\left(w_{1}\Psi(w_{1})-\displaystyle\int\Psi(w_{1})\,dw_{1}\right)+(w_{1})^{3/2}\Theta(w_{2}).

(3.9)

In view of the diagonal system (
3.7
), a necessary and sufficient condition
[
11
]
for
\displaystyle\eta
to be strictly convex is that

\mathbf{r_{i}^{\top}}\nabla^{2}_{(h,b)}\eta(w_{1},w_{2})\mathbf{r_{i}}>0~\text{for}~i\in\{1,2\},

where
\displaystyle\mathbf{r}_{i}
are the eigenvectors of the system defined in (
3.2
) and
\displaystyle\nabla^{2}_{(h,b)}
is the Hessian operator w.r.t.
\displaystyle(h,b)
.

First, we use the Hessian identity

\displaystyle\displaystyle\nabla^{2}_{(h,b)}\eta(w_{1},w_{2})=D^{\top}\nabla^{2}_{(w_{1},w_{2})}\eta(w_{1},w_{2})D+\eta_{w_{1}}\nabla^{2}_{(h,b)}w_{1}+\eta_{w_{2}}\nabla^{2}_{(h,b)}w_{2},

(3.10)

where
\displaystyle D
denotes the Jacobian of
\displaystyle(w_{1},w_{2})
w.r.t.
\displaystyle(h,b)
.

An easy computation gives

D\mathbf{r_{1}}=(2w_{1},0)^{\top}~\text{and}~D\mathbf{r_{2}}=(0,6\alpha(3\alpha w_{2}+\kappa))^{\top}=(0,6\alpha p)^{\top},

where
\displaystyle p=3\alpha w_{2}+\kappa
. Therefore, for
\displaystyle i\in\{1,2\}
we multiply (
3.10
) by
\displaystyle\mathbf{r_{i}^{\top}}
from the left and by
\displaystyle\mathbf{r_{i}}
from the right, and obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{r_{1}^{\top}}\nabla^{2}_{(h,b)}\eta(w_{1},w_{2})\mathbf{r_{1}}=4w_{1}^{2}\eta_{w_{1}w_{1}}+\eta_{w_{1}}\mathbf{r_{1}^{\top}}\nabla^{2}_{(h,b)}w_{1}\mathbf{r_{1}}+\eta_{w_{2}}\mathbf{r_{1}^{\top}}\nabla^{2}_{(h,b)}w_{2}\mathbf{r_{1}},\vskip 5.69046pt

(3.11)

\displaystyle\mathbf{r_{2}^{\top}}\nabla^{2}_{(h,b)}\eta(w_{1},w_{2})\mathbf{r_{2}}=36\alpha p^{2}\eta_{w_{2}w_{2}}+\eta_{w_{1}}\mathbf{r_{2}^{\top}}\nabla^{2}_{(h,b)}w_{1}\mathbf{r_{2}}+\eta_{w_{2}}\mathbf{r_{2}^{\top}}\nabla^{2}_{(h,b)}w_{2}\mathbf{r_{2}}.

A tedious but straightforward calculation then leads to

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbf{r_{1}^{\top}}\nabla^{2}_{(h,b)}\eta(w_{1},w_{2})\mathbf{r_{1}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=2w_{1}(2w_{1}\Psi^{\prime\prime}(w_{1})+\Psi^{\prime}(w_{1})),\vskip 5.69046pt

(3.12)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbf{r_{2}^{\top}}\nabla^{2}_{(h,b)}\eta(w_{1},w_{2})\mathbf{r_{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=9\alpha^{2}\sqrt{w_{1}}\left(4p^{2}\Theta^{\prime\prime}(p)+4p\Theta^{\prime}(p)-\Theta(p)-2w_{1}\Psi^{\prime}(w_{1})\right).

(3.13)

Clearly, in view of positivity of
\displaystyle w_{1}
and
\displaystyle w_{2}
in
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{\circ}
, a sufficient condition for the positivity of
\displaystyle\mathbf{r_{1}^{\top}}\nabla^{2}_{(h,b)}\eta(w_{1},w_{2})\mathbf{r_{1}}
and
\displaystyle\mathbf{r_{2}^{\top}}\nabla^{2}_{(h,b)}\eta(w_{1},w_{2})\mathbf{r_{2}}
is

4p^{2}\Theta^{\prime\prime}(p)+4p\Theta^{\prime}(p)-\Theta(p)=0,~\Psi^{\prime}(w_{1})<0,~\Psi^{\prime\prime}(w_{1})>0,~2w_{1}\Psi^{\prime\prime}(w_{1})+\Psi^{\prime}(w_{1})>0.

This concludes the proof of the theorem.

4.1
Elementary classical waves of system (

\displaystyle(h,b)^{\top}\in\mathcal{U}^{\circ}

For a given left state

in the interior of the state space (

which can be linked to

by a contact
discontinuity
\displaystyle J(h_{l},b_{l})

4.1.1
Rarefaction waves.

Since the
\displaystyle 2^{\mathrm{nd}}
characteristic field is genuinely nonlinear, the wave associated with this field is either a rarefaction or a shock wave. Moreover, the 2-Riemann invariant remains constant across the fans of 2-rarefaction wave. This then leads to the expression of the 2-rarefaction wave given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle R_{2}:=\begin{cases}\dfrac{dx}{dt}=\lambda_{2}=3\alpha hb+\kappa h^{2},\vskip 5.69046pt\\
\dfrac{b}{h}=\dfrac{b_{l}}{h_{l}},\vskip 5.69046pt\\
h_{l}\leq h,~b_{l}\leq b.\end{cases}

(4.1)

Clearly,
\displaystyle R_{2}
is a curve in the
\displaystyle(h,b)
-space emanating from
\displaystyle\mathbf{U}_{l}=(h_{l},b_{l})^{\top}

4.1.2
Discontinuous solutions: shock and contact waves.

For a given left state
\displaystyle\mathbf{U}_{l}=(h_{l},b_{l})^{\top}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbf{U}(x,t)=\begin{cases}\mathbf{U}_{l}=(h_{l},b_{l})^{\top}\in{\mathcal{U}}&:~x<\sigma t,\\

(4.2)

Moreover, (
4.2

1.4

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sigma[\![\mathbf{U}]\!]=[\![\mathbf{F(U)}]\!]

(4.3)

\displaystyle[\![\mathbf{V}]\!]=\mathbf{V}_{r}-\mathbf{V}_{l}

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}_{l},\mathbf{V}_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

In view of the system (

\begin{array}[]{rclrcl}\sigma[\![h]\!]&=&\!\!\!\!\Big[\!\!\Big[h\left(\alpha hb+\dfrac{\kappa h^{2}}{3}\right)\Big]\!\!\Big],\\
\sigma[\![b]\!]&=&\!\!\!\!\Big[\!\!\Big[b\left(\alpha hb+\dfrac{\kappa h^{2}}{3}\right)\Big]\!\!\Big].\end{array}

For nonconstant solutions and a given left state

\displaystyle 1

The
\displaystyle 2

\displaystyle\mathbf{U_{l}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle S_{2}:=\begin{cases}\sigma_{2}((h_{l},b_{l});(h,b))=(h+h_{l})\left(\alpha b+\dfrac{\kappa h}{3}\right)+h_{l}\left(\alpha b_{l}+\dfrac{\kappa h_{l}}{3}\right),\\

5
Stability of the Riemann solutions with perturbation in initial data

In what follows, we develop the solutions of the Cauchy problem (
1.4
)-(
1.7
) via nonlinear wave interactions and prove Theorem
1.6
. The initial data (
1.7
) can be understood as two local Riemann problems located at
\displaystyle(-\varepsilon,0)
and
\displaystyle(\varepsilon,0)
and thus the solution of (
1.4
)-(
1.7

\displaystyle i
th Riemann problem, the solution is denoted by either
\displaystyle J_{i}+S_{i}/R_{i}
or
\displaystyle\delta S_{i}
,
\displaystyle i=1,2,\ldots,N,~N\geq 2
, where “
\displaystyle+
” denotes “followed by” a nonlinear wave. These nonlinear interactions give rise to new emerging nonlinear classical and nonclassical hyperbolic waves. We compute all these possible points and times of interaction and provide the expression of solution curves explicitly. Moreover, during the process of interaction, the strength of each delta shock wave is also computed. All these nonlinear quantities depend on the perturbation parameter
\displaystyle\varepsilon
explicitly. By analyzing each of these interaction points, nonlinear wave curves and the strength of delta shocks, we
prove that the solution of (
1.4
) and (
1.7
) given by
\displaystyle(h_{\varepsilon},b_{\varepsilon})(x,t)
converges to the Riemann solution of (
1.4
)-(
1.6
) as
\displaystyle\varepsilon\to 0
.

Depending upon the different choices of initial data, there are exactly seven possibilities for the solution of the Cauchy problem (
1.4
)-(
1.7
). The possible cases of nonlinear wave interactions are listed as follows:

\displaystyle 1.\;J_{1}+S_{1}\text{ and }J_{2}+S_{2},\quad 2.\;J_{1}+S_{1}\text{ and }J_{2}+R_{2},\quad 3.\;J_{1}+R_{1}\text{ and }J_{2}+R_{2},

\displaystyle 4.\;J_{1}+R_{1}\text{ and }J_{2}+S_{2},\quad 5.\;\delta S_{1}\text{ and }J_{2}R_{2},\quad 6.\;J_{1}+S_{1}\text{ and }\delta S_{2},\quad 7.\;J_{1}+R_{1}\text{ and }\delta S_{2}.

where
\displaystyle J_{2}R_{2}
is the composite wave solution of the second Riemann problem defined in (
4.18
). Moreover, the notation for e.g.
\displaystyle J_{1}+S_{1}
and
\displaystyle J_{2}+R_{2}
implies that the state
\displaystyle(h_{-},b_{-})
is connected to the state
\displaystyle(h_{m},b_{m})
by a contact discontinuity followed by a shock wave arising from the first Riemann problem while the state the
\displaystyle(h_{m},b_{m})
is connected to the state
\displaystyle(h_{+},b_{+})
by a contact discontinuity followed by a rarefaction wave arising from second Riemann problem.

For the sake of brevity, here we consider only the first two cases of classical wave interactions, as the other two cases can be analyzed in a similar manner. In particular, we consider the non-trivial cases of classical wave interactions where the shock/contact or delta shock may interact with the rarefaction wave. For the delta shock wave interaction, we consider all possible cases.

Remark 5.1

In the current state space
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}

7
Conclusions and future outlook

In this article, we analyzed the Riemann problem for a newly developed
\displaystyle(2\times 2)
hyperbolic system, which governs the dynamics of thin film flow under the influence of gravity and solute transport. We were able to provide a novel set of classes of entropy-entropy pairs with sufficient conditions for strictly convex entropies. Also, we prove the existence and uniqueness of the Riemann problem and analyzed the stability of the constructed solutions via flux perturbation and perturbation in the initial data.

A natural next step would be to prove the existence of global
\displaystyle L^{\infty}
solutions for the system using the compensated compactness approach, where the rich entropy structure of the system can play a big role. However, it will not be straightforward to obtain global bounds due to the nonconvexity of the first Riemann invariant and may need flux perturbation methods to obtain a bounded invariant region and thus induced global bounds. Also, it would be interesting to analyze whether the one-layer case could be extended to its multi-layer counterpart while maintaining the rich mathematical structure of the underlying dynamics.

Is metformin therapy in conjunction with lifestyle modifications more effective than lifestyle modifications alone in lowering the risk of gestational diabetes mellitus in pregnant women with metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD)? A protocol for parallel-arm superiority randomised controlled trial in tertiary care centres, Puducherry, South India
Authors: Venkatachalam Jayaseelan, Murali Subbaiah, Kalaiselvy Arikrishnan, Ramkumar Govindarajalu, Sabita Pulavarthi, Nivedita Nanda, Khadeeja Nasreen Vadakkepeediyakkal, Pazhanivel Mohan, Gayathri Murugesan Sivagurunathan, Mohammed Kais Musthafa, Mahadevan Duraiswamy, Susmitha Pulikkot Suragi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMetabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) and gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) are prevalent metabolic disorders in pregnancy, posing significant risks to maternal and fetal health. This study evaluates the effectiveness of metformin, in combination with lifestyle modifications, compared with lifestyle modifications alone, in reducing the incidence of diabetes, pro-inflammatory liver markers, adverse maternal and neonatal outcomes and total gestational weight gain in pregnant women diagnosed with MASLD in the first trimester.MethodsThis parallel-arm, randomised controlled trial will recruit pregnant women (≤14 weeks of gestation) with confirmed MASLD from antenatal clinics of tertiary care public hospitals in Puducherry, India. Participants will be consecutively enrolled until a sample size of 296 is reached. Block randomisation will ensure balanced group allocation, with allocation concealment maintained using sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes. The intervention group will receive oral metformin (500 mg two times per day) alongside structured lifestyle modification counselling, while the control group will receive lifestyle modification counselling alone. Primary outcomes include GDM incidence, changes in pro-inflammatory markers, MASLD grading (assessed via liver function tests and ultrasound) and adverse maternal outcomes such as hypertensive disorders, polyhydramnios, genitourinary infections, caesarean delivery and postpartum haemorrhage. Neonatal outcomes assessed include macrosomia, stillbirth, intrauterine death, birth injury, shoulder dystocia, respiratory distress and neonatal hypoglycaemia. The secondary outcome is total gestational weight gain. Participants will be followed at 24–28 weeks, 34–36 weeks and post partum (within 6 weeks of delivery). Data collection will be conducted using a pretested structured questionnaire, with data entry and management performed using REDCap software. Statistical analysis will be conducted using STATA V.4, applying both intention-to-treat and per-protocol analyses. Effect sizes will be reported as proportions and relative risks with 95% CIs, ensuring robust statistical inference.ConclusionsThis study provides a rigorous framework to assess metformin’s role in managing MASLD and preventing GDM, thereby promoting favourable maternal and neonatal outcomes. Findings will contribute to improved clinical management, public health strategies and policy recommendations.Ethics and disseminationThe study was approved by the JIPMER Institutional Ethics Committee (JIP/AEC/2023/01/011), and the findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed journals and academic conferences.Trial registration numberCTRI/2023/12/060930.

Introduction

Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) and gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) are among the most notable metabolic disorders of pregnancy, posing significant risks to maternal and child health. Globally, the reported prevalence of MASLD and GDM during pregnancy is approximately 10% and 10–14%, respectively.1 However, in India, MASLD prevalence is recorded to be higher at 26%, driven by metabolic risk factors, including lifestyle practices and ethnicity. MASLD has gained much attention in recent times, due to its substantial burden and adverse short-term and long-term maternal and neonatal outcomes, such as urinary tract infections, abortions, stillbirth, caesarean delivery, macrosomia, low birth weight, respiratory distress and neonatal hypoglycaemia.2 3

MASLD has been identified as an independent predictor of GDM, even after adjusting for other components of metabolic syndrome, including obesity, hyperlipidaemia and hypertension. Although the association between MASLD and GDM is bidirectional, substantial evidence suggests that pregnant women diagnosed with MASLD in the first trimester are at a twofold to fourfold increased risk of developing GDM in the second trimester, specifically between 24 and 28 weeks of gestation.4 5 Despite this evidence, no standardised medical protocol currently exists for the diagnosis or management of MASLD and its progression to complications. This gap in clinical practice raises significant concerns about the dual metabolic burden posed by these conditions, particularly among vulnerable populations such as pregnant women and their neonates, in the absence of effective interventions and management strategies.

Metformin therapy has shown several beneficial effects in the management of MASLD and the potential prevention of GDM.68 Its mechanisms of action include reducing hepatic gluconeogenesis, enhancing insulin sensitivity, inhibiting lipogenesis, accelerating fatty acid oxidation and alleviating the necro-inflammatory processes and fibrosis associated with MASLD.9 Additionally, metformin has been demonstrated to stabilise elevated liver enzyme levels by improving liver histopathology and function.10 It also contributes to a reduction in body mass index (BMI), thereby supporting optimal gestational weight gain.11

Metformin is generally recognised as a safe and effective option for managing GDM, polycystic ovary syndrome-related infertility and pre-existing diabetes in pregnancy.12 However, mild side effects, such as nausea, vomiting and diarrhoea, have been reported, with rare risks including hypoglycaemia, vitamin B12 deficiency and lactic acidosis, particularly in individuals with renal or hepatic impairment.13 14

Proinflammatory markers, including C reactive protein (CRP), interleukin-6 (IL-6), tumour necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) and interleukin-1 beta (IL-1β), are valuable for diagnosing and monitoring liver inflammation. In contrast, selenoprotein P (SeP) and adiponectin are more specific to MASLD, reflecting oxidative stress and insulin sensitivity. These markers are common to both metabolic disorders, easily measurable and have been extensively studied in MASLD, making them ideal for research compared with less organ-specific markers like CRP and interleukin.15

Considering these mechanisms, this study aims to assess the effectiveness of metformin therapy combined with lifestyle modifications on the incidence of GDM, pro-inflammatory marker levels and grading of MASLD, adverse pregnancy outcomes, including both maternal and neonatal complications, as well as total gestational weight gain. The study will compare these outcomes against those achieved through lifestyle modifications alone.

Methodology

Study design and setting

This multicentre, parallel-arm superiority randomised controlled trial will be conducted in the antenatal clinics of the Obstetrics and Gynaecology departments at the Jawaharlal Institute of Postgraduate Medical Education and Research (JIPMER) and the Indira Gandhi Medical College and Research Institute (IGMCRI) in Puducherry, India, from August 2023 to August 2026. Puducherry, a Union Territory in India, has an estimated population of approximately 1.7 million as of 2024,16 with an annual birth rate of 13.3 per 1000 populations.17 The region is characterised by a diverse ethnic composition, predominantly Tamil-speaking Hindus, and is supported by a well-developed healthcare infrastructure, including 81 subcentres, 39 primary health centres (PHCs), 4 community health centres (CHCs) and 9 hospitals.18

South Asians are known to have a higher metabolic risk for GDM, with a reported prevalence of 27%.19 JIPMER and IGMCRI, with approximately 12 000 and 3000 annual deliveries, respectively, provide healthcare services to underserved and economically disadvantaged populations. These centres are equipped with advanced diagnostic and therapeutic facilities, multidisciplinary expertise and operate daily antenatal clinics that offer comprehensive care, including free routine check-ups, imaging services and integrated dietary counselling. The selection of JIPMER and IGMCRI as study sites is further justified by their established mechanisms for participant recruitment, follow-up and data collection, as well as their strict adherence to ethical standards. These attributes collectively ensure a robust research environment suitable for conducting this trial.

Sample size estimation

The sample size for this study was calculated using OpenEpi V.3.01, based on an assumed prevalence of GDM of 3% among pregnant women without MASLD and 15% among those with MASLD.20 With a power of 90% and a 95% CI, the required sample size was estimated to be 268 participants. To account for an anticipated 10% attrition rate, the final sample size was adjusted to 296 participants, with 148 participants allocated to each group in a 1:1 ratio.

Sampling technique

Two out of the three tertiary care public hospitals were selected as study sites using a purposive sampling technique to ensure representation of diverse populations from Puducherry and Tamil Nadu. These sites were deliberately chosen due to their high patient turnover and the availability of diagnostic facilities for MASLD.

Participant recruitment will be conducted using a consecutive sampling approach, wherein all eligible individuals identified during the study period at both centres will be included until the required sample size is achieved. No predetermined quotas will be set for each hospital; instead, sample representation from both hospitals will be proportionate to their respective caseloads and annual delivery rates, as outlined in the study setting.

Study procedure

Eligible participants meeting the inclusion criteria and providing informed consent will undergo abdominal ultrasound imaging for MASLD screening during the first trimester. Ultrasound examinations will be performed at the Department of Radio-diagnosis by qualified radiologists following standardised imaging protocols. A scoring system will be used to subjectively quantify hepatic fat accumulation, categorising participants into grades 0, 1, 2 or 3 based on sonographic findings.

Evidence from prior studies suggests that ultrasound has a sensitivity of 84.8% for the diagnosis of MASLD.21 Given that the prevalence of MASLD among pregnant women in the first trimester is estimated at 18%–20%,22 approximately six to seven women need to be screened to identify one positive case of MASLD.

Screening and baseline assessment

Participants diagnosed with MASLD will undergo a 75 g oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) the following day, with three-point blood glucose measurements obtained using a glucometer. Those meeting the International Association of Diabetes and Pregnancy Study Groups (IADPSG) criteria for GDM will be excluded from the study. Participants with MASLD and normal glycaemic levels will then undergo baseline assessments of dietary practices, using a 24-hour dietary recall method, and physical activity levels, assessed through the Global Physical Activity Questionnaire (GPAQ).

Data collection and biochemical analysis

Sociodemographic, obstetric and clinical data will be collected using a pretested structured questionnaire. Baseline body weight will be measured using a standardised digital weighing scale. Fasting venous blood samples (5 mL) will be collected to measure pro-inflammatory markers, including plasma adiponectin and selenoprotein P. Plasma will be isolated by centrifugation at 3000 rpm for 10 min and stored in cryovials at −37°C to −40°C until analysis. To ensure stability and minimise inter-sample variability, plasma samples will be analysed in batches of 80 using ELISA. All assays will be conducted in a single central laboratory using validated methods to ensure analytical consistency and reliability.

Randomisation

Participants will be randomised into either the intervention or control group using block randomisation to ensure balanced group allocation. Random block sizes of 4, 6 and 8 will be generated using randomisation software. The randomisation process will be conducted by an independent individual not involved in the study, ensuring confidentiality of the allocation sequence. To maintain allocation concealment, the sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelope technique will be employed. on identifying an eligible participant in the clinic, the corresponding envelope will be assigned in consecutive order. The investigator will verify the intactness of the envelope seal before allocation and then proceed with study procedures. Given the non-blinded nature of the intervention, only the outcome assessor will be blinded to group assignments to minimise bias in outcome evaluation. Study groups

Intervention arm (study group 1): Participants randomised to the intervention group will receive oral metformin therapy (500 mg two times per day) in combination with structured counselling on lifestyle modifications

Control arm (study group 2): Participants in the control group will receive only structured counselling on lifestyle modifications

Procedure for administering intervention

All participants will receive standardised face-to-face counselling sessions at baseline (30–45 min) covering healthy dietary practices, physical activity and weight management. These sessions will be delivered by experts from the Departments of Community Medicine and Obstetrics & Gynaecology, with dietary plans tailored to participants’ socioeconomic status, dietary preferences, locally available foods and caloric needs based on Medical Nutritional Therapy for GDM management. The dietary approach follows the healthy plate method, emphasising the consumption of 400 g of vegetables, 100 g of fruits, 250 g of cereals (eg, brown rice, millets), 27 g of healthy fats (eg, sunflower or groundnut oil), 35 g of nuts, 85 g of pulses and protein-rich foods such as fish, chicken and paneer. Dairy intake will be encouraged at 300 mL of milk or curd daily, while red meat, processed foods and excess salt (>5 g/day) will be discouraged. Participants will be advised to stay well-hydrated, consuming 2–3 L of water daily and prioritising low-sugar fruits and vegetables. Meals will be balanced and distributed across three meals and three snacks per day, incorporating complex carbohydrates and low-GI foods to support appropriate gestational weight gain and normoglycaemia.23 Physical activity recommendations will include mild to moderate-intensity exercises for at least 30 min per day, totalling a minimum of 150 min per week, incorporating walking, abdominal strengthening (eg, four-point kneeling, side plank), stretching (eg, shoulder stretch, seated trunk twist) and lower body exercises (eg, hip stretch). For participants new to exercise, activity duration will be gradually increased, with trimester-specific guidance on optimal gestational weight gain based on baseline BMI. In addition to lifestyle counselling, participants in study group 1 will receive metformin therapy (500 mg two times per day) until delivery to reduce the risk of GDM, with detailed instructions provided on potential minor side effects. To reinforce the counselling provided, all participants will receive a standardised educational booklet developed according to clinical guidelines and reviewed by experts. Adherence to lifestyle modifications and Metformin therapy will be monitored through self-reported logs and follow-up visits.

Follow-up and end line assessment

All participants in both study groups will undergo uniform assessments. Follow-up OGTT will be conducted at 24–28 weeks and 32–36 weeks of gestation to evaluate the incidence of GDM. Within 6 weeks post partum, blood samples will be collected to assess changes in the levels of pro-inflammatory markers associated with MASLD. Additionally, a repeat abdominal ultrasound will be performed to evaluate the progression of MASLD based on standardised grading criteria.

Data on gestational weight gain and adverse pregnancy outcomes, including both maternal and neonatal complications, will be collected through patient interviews and by reviewing documented information in hospital case sheets. Data collection will be conducted systematically and consistently across both study groups. Trained data collectors to ensure data accuracy and reliability will gather information on adverse pregnancy outcomes during the final follow-up period, extending from immediately after delivery to 2 weeks post partum. Data will be obtained through self-reports from study participants and corroborated by reviewing hospital case sheets or discharge summaries. All outcomes will be recorded in strict accordance with the operational definitions.

Adherence assessment

A week-wise dietary chart is provided to participants in both study groups to assess dietary adherence using the Pregnancy Dietary Adherence Questionnaire (PDAQ) and the 24-hour dietary recall method. Dietary adherence is evaluated at baseline and during follow-up visits at 28 weeks and 36 weeks of gestation, prior to delivery. During these follow-up visits, adherence to physical activity is also assessed using the GPAQ.2426 Additionally, BMI and blood pressure are measured according to standard guidelines.27 In the intervention group, adherence to metformin therapy is evaluated using the Medication Adherence Rating Scale-10 (MARS-10) at 18 weeks, 26 weeks and 34 weeks during antenatal follow-up. Participants are considered adherent if they follow the recommended dietary intervention for at least 5 days per week. To enhance adherence monitoring and support, participants in both groups receive biweekly follow-up phone calls throughout the study period.

Study tool

The study will use a structured, validated questionnaire to comprehensively collect data on demographic characteristics, clinical and metabolic parameters, lifestyle habits, intervention adherence and study outcomes. The severity of MASLD will be evaluated through liver imaging and the quantification of pro-inflammatory markers. A standardised health education booklet, developed in consultation with subject matter experts and in accordance with established guidelines, will be pretested on 10% of the estimated sample size, drawn from comparable hospital populations. The pretesting process will assess language clarity, comprehensibility, content adequacy and the feasibility of data collection. Based on participant feedback and expert recommendations, necessary refinements will be made before implementation. To ensure uniformity in data collection, investigators and field staff will undergo structured training on the standardised administration of the questionnaire and booklet during baseline and follow-up visits. The questionnaire, Participant Information Sheet, Informed Consent Form and Exclusion Form are available as online supplemental file 1 for reference.

PDAQ: Healthful eating habits will be assessed over the past 7 days using a nine-item questionnaire, with each item scored from 0 to 7 based on adherence. Items 4 and 9 will be reverse-scored to account for unhealthy dietary practices. The total score ranges from 0 to 63, with higher scores reflecting greater adherence to recommended dietary guidelines.

GPAQ: Physical activity will be assessed across occupational, travel-related and recreational domains, including sedentary behaviour, using a 16-item questionnaire. Frequency (days per week) and duration (minutes or hours per day) will be recorded for each domain. Total physical activity will be expressed in metabolic equivalent of task (MET) minutes per week and classified into low, moderate or high activity levels based on standardised criteria.

MARS-10: Adherence to prescribed medication regimens will be assessed using a 10-item questionnaire with yes/no responses. The total score ranges from 0 to 10, with higher scores indicating better adherence to the prescribed treatment.

Operational definitions

MASLD in pregnancy will be graded based on imaging findings: Mild (score 1) is defined by diffuse liver echogenicity greater than the right kidney cortex; Moderate (score 2) is characterised by impaired visualisation of the portal vein radicle walls and Severe (score 3) involves a marked increase in hepatic echogenicity, obscuring the diaphragm’s clear delineation.

GDM will be diagnosed using the IADPSG criteria with a 75 g OGTT, where any one of the following abnormal glucose values confirms the diagnosis: fasting ≥92 mg/dL, 1 hour ≥180 mg/dL or 2 hour≥153 mg/dL.

Hypertensive disorders in pregnancy will be defined as blood pressure ≥140/90 mm Hg on two occasions, at least 4 hours apart, after 20 weeks of gestation, with or without proteinuria, end-organ damage, or a history of hypertension or ongoing anti-hypertensive treatment.

Chronic liver disease will be defined as a progressive deterioration of liver functions persisting for more than 6 months, affecting clotting factor synthesis, protein metabolism, detoxification and bile excretion.28

Polyhydramnios is a condition marked by an excessive amount of amniotic fluid, commonly due to increased fetal urine output or impaired fetal swallowing.

Postpartum haemorrhage is defined as a cumulative blood loss of 1000 mL or more accompanied by signs or symptoms of hypovolaemia, regardless of the mode of delivery.

Intrauterine death is the death of a fetus at or after 20 weeks of gestation or with a weight ≥350 g, with no signs of life at delivery.

Macrosomia and microsomia will be classified based on birth weight percentiles: macrosomia is defined as birth weight at or above the 90th percentile, while microsomia is below the 10th percentile, indicating fetal growth restriction.29

Shoulder dystocia: After the delivery of the fetal head, the anterior shoulder becomes impacted behind the maternal pubic symphysis, preventing delivery of the rest of the body.

Respiratory distress will be identified by the onset of respiratory difficulty within the first 2 hours of birth, characterised by radiologic findings and oxygen dependence. Severe cases will be defined by the need for invasive or non-invasive mechanical ventilation with FiO₂ >0.25 for at least 24 hours and neonatal intensive care unit(NICU) admission.30

Neonatal hypoglycaemia is characterised by a blood glucose concentration typically below 47 mg/dL, occurring within the early neonatal period and associated with an increased risk of adverse neurodevelopmental outcomes

Data management plan

This study will employ structured case report forms (CRFs) to systematically record clinical records, laboratory results, and participant-reported data. Physical CRFs will be securely stored in locked cabinets, while electronic data will be maintained in a password-protected database with encrypted backups to ensure security and integrity. To protect participant confidentiality, all personal identifiers will be replaced with unique participant codes. Data quality assurance measures will include comprehensive staff training, double-entry verification and independent monitoring to minimise errors and missing data. Access to the final dataset will be restricted to authorised personnel only, and deidentified data will be shared exclusively with the approval of the principal investigator and the Ethics Committee. In accordance with ethical guidelines set by the Indian Council of Medical Research and relevant regulatory standards, data will be retained for 5 years following study completion and securely disposed of thereafter.

Data will be entered into REDCap and analysed using STATA V.14. The study will test the null hypothesis (H₀) that metformin combined with lifestyle modifications is not more effective than lifestyle modifications alone in preventing GDM, excessive gestational weight gain and adverse pregnancy outcomes, against the alternative hypothesis that metformin is more effective. A one-tailed test will be employed for hypothesis testing. The primary outcomes will include the incidence of GDM, levels of pro-inflammatory liver markers and adverse pregnancy outcomes, while secondary outcomes will encompass total gestational weight gain. Both intention-to-treat (ITT) and per-protocol (PP) analyses will be performed to ensure the robustness of findings. Effect sizes will be reported as proportions and relative risks with corresponding 95% CIs. Continuous variables will be summarised using mean (SD) or median (IQR), as appropriate. Missing data will be systematically assessed and reported, with values ignored if less than 10% is missing and determined to be Missing Completely at Random. For missing data exceeding 10%, advanced statistical methods, such as multiple imputation or mean substitution, will be employed. Comparative analyses for primary outcomes will be conducted using the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test, as applicable, while gestational weight gain and inflammatory marker levels will be analysed using the unpaired t-test or Wilcoxon rank-sum test, depending on data distribution. Logistic regression modelling will be employed to identify factors associated with primary outcomes, adjusting for potential confounders, with model robustness evaluated using the coefficient of determination (R²). Statistical significance will be set at p<0.05, and subgroup analyses will be conducted to explore variations in primary outcomes across sociodemographic categories and adherence levels, providing insights into potential effect modifiers.

Ethics and dissemination

This study has received ethical approval from the Institutional Ethics Committee (JIP/AEC/2023/01/011) and is registered with the Clinical Trial Registry of India (CTRI/2023/12/060930). Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants following a comprehensive explanation of the study’s objectives, procedures, potential risks and benefits. Participation will be entirely voluntary, with participants retaining the right to withdraw at any stage without repercussions. In the event of any harm or injury directly related to study participation, appropriate compensation will be provided in accordance with ethical guidelines, and these provisions will be explicitly outlined in the patient information sheet to ensure transparency and informed decision-making. The ethical review board will oversee and monitor adherence to these protocols.

On study completion, a summary of the findings will be disseminated to participants through health informational sessions. Study outcomes will be shared with healthcare providers, policymakers, programme managers and the general public via peer-reviewed journal publications, presentations at scientific conferences, ClinicalTrials.gov and press releases. To promote transparency and facilitate future research, the complete study protocol will be made accessible in reputable repositories. Additionally, a deidentified dataset, appropriately coded to maintain participant confidentiality, will be made available on request to qualified researchers, supporting further investigation and validation of the study findings.

Protocol violation policy

Protocol deviations and violations will be systematically documented, reported and managed in accordance with the International Council for Harmonisation Good Clinical Practice guidelines and relevant local regulations. Serious deviations with potential implications for participant safety will be promptly reported to the Institutional Ethics Committee and the funding agency, while all other deviations will be documented and reported periodically as required. Corrective and preventive actions will be implemented to mitigate recurrence and ensure protocol adherence. Throughout the reporting and management process, strict confidentiality will be maintained in compliance with local data protection regulations, safeguarding participant privacy and study integrity.

Patient and public involvement

Patients or members of the public were not directly involved in formulating the research question, designing the study, or determining outcome measures for this protocol. However, during the pretesting phase, feedback was sought from pregnant women (10% of the estimated sample size) from similar populations across different hospitals to evaluate comprehension, language clarity and content adequacy of the study tools and educational booklet. This input was carefully incorporated into the final versions of the study materials to enhance their relevance and accessibility.

Discussion

This study aims to assess the efficacy of Metformin combined with lifestyle modifications in reducing the incidence of GDM, preventing the progression of MASLD, improving maternal and neonatal outcomes, and regulating maternal weight gain among pregnant women with MASLD. Given the rising prevalence of MASLD and its associated metabolic risks, particularly among vulnerable populations such as pregnant women in India, this study addresses a critical gap in managing the coexistence of MASLD and GDM, which significantly increases the risk of pregnancy complications. Metformin therapy has demonstrated beneficial effects by enhancing insulin sensitivity, reducing hepatic gluconeogenesis and promoting fatty acid oxidation, thereby targeting the underlying pathophysiology of both MASLD and GDM. Its safety profile in pregnancy is well established. The multicentric, randomised controlled design of this study enhances its methodological rigour, with implementation at tertiary care public hospitals equipped with essential radiological facilities, including first-trimester screening, provided at no cost. Abdominal ultrasound for MASLD screening will be conducted by dedicated radiology experts, ensuring feasibility due to the proximity of radiological services to the antenatal clinics. All participants will receive standard care in the form of structured lifestyle modification counselling, a recognised intervention for reducing GDM risk. This approach ensures ethical treatment, reflects real-world management of metabolic disorders, effectively controls for confounding variables and isolates the additional benefits of metformin. A placebo will not be administered to the control group to avoid exposing participants to an inert treatment without therapeutic value, thereby addressing ethical concerns. Both IITT and PP analyses will be performed to evaluate the study outcomes. ITT analysis preserves the benefits of randomisation and reflects real-world scenarios, while PP analysis assesses the intervention’s efficacy under ideal conditions, enhancing the validity and robustness of the findings in accordance with Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials guidelines. The study’s strengths include its multicentric, randomised controlled design, the combination of metformin and lifestyle modifications as a well-defined intervention, comprehensive MASLD screening and ethical treatment provision to all participants. The use of validated questionnaires, standardised health education materials and trained field staff contributes to the study’s internal validity. Additionally, the availability of radiological facilities supports feasibility. However, the study has certain limitations, including the potential for performance bias due to the absence of a placebo in the control arm, which may lead to unmasking of the intervention. Adherence data, collected through self-reported measures, may be prone to social desirability and misclassification biases, potentially leading to information bias. Nevertheless, the risk of outcome ascertainment bias is minimised by blinding the analyst responsible for outcome assessment. Generalisability may be limited to the study population, necessitating further research to explore the applicability of findings to other ethnic groups. If proven effective, this intervention could provide robust evidence supporting the development of a standardised MASLD management protocol during pregnancy, potentially leading to the widespread adoption of metformin for managing metabolic risks among pregnant women in India, thereby improving maternal and neonatal health outcomes. Future research may explore the long-term effects and sustained impact of Metformin and lifestyle interventions on maternal and neonatal health. This study protocol is developed in accordance with Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT) guidelines.

Drug safety monitoring plan

This academic trial assesses the efficacy of a well-established, low-risk drug that is already widely used by the target population for other medical indications. Given its well-documented safety profile, no additional safety monitoring beyond standard clinical practice is required.

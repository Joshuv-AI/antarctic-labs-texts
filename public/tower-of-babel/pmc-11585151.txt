Behavioral risk assessment of work-related musculoskeletal disorders among workers of petrochemical industries: protocol of a mixed method study
Authors: Zohreh Moradi, Sedigheh Sadat Tavafian, Fazlollah Ahmadi, Omran Ahmadi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMusculoskeletal disorders (MSDs) are one of the most common occupational diseases and the main cause of disability worldwide. Work-related musculoskeletal disorders (WMSDs) are one of the common health risks and the most important cause of absenteeism due to disability in various industries, including the petrochemical industry, in developed and developing countries. These disorders have important social economic, and significant financial consequences due to direct and hidden costs. Health behaviors play a role in both creating and preventing musculoskeletal disorders in employees. Therefore, by identifying the influencing factors on these behaviors, it is possible to strengthen and improve the preventive behaviors of musculoskeletal disorders through educational intervention programs. This study aims to assess the behavioral risk of work-related musculoskeletal disorders, and design and implement an educational intervention to teach effective behaviors in the prevention of musculoskeletal disorders in petrochemical industry workers.MethodsThis study is a mixed-method study implemented in four stages involving the qualitative study, the design and evaluation of an instrument, the design of an experimental randomized clinical trial, and the psychometric evaluation of the instrument and the evaluation of the program. The research community consists of employees working in the petrochemical industry. The volume of samples in the qualitative study with the purposeful sampling method, in the instrument design stage based on the available sampling method, and also in the experimental study, the samples are employees suffering from work-related musculoskeletal disorders, who were selected based on a simple random method from among the employees of the petrochemical industry. Then they will be divided into intervention and control groups. The instruments of this research include a demographic questionnaire, a researcher-made questionnaire for measuring behavior, and two auxiliary instruments including the visual analog scale (VAS) and the Quebec Disability Scale. Evaluation is done in 4 stages: pre-test, immediately, 3, and 6 months after the intervention of both groups. The obtained data will be analyzed using SPSS software.DiscussionMusculoskeletal disorders related (WMSDs) to work can harm employees’ health in various industries, including the petrochemical industry. Background

Despite the technological advances and the decrease in the amount of manpower in various industries [1], the prevalence of musculoskeletal disorders related to work (WMSDs) is still one of the biggest health problems in the working population in developed and developing countries, which has the highest number of different complaints among the working population [2, 3]. Carrying out work tasks in inappropriate physical, psychological, and ergonomic conditions constantly puts working people under muscle tension and vibration, which can lead to WMSDs [4]. These disorders can cause pain or damage to the body’s musculoskeletal system, including bones, tendons, nerves, ligaments, joints, cartilage, blood vessels, and intervertebral discs of the spine [1, 5].

WMSDs have a significant impact on social functions and health status, which leads to the inability to perform daily activities and as a result, reduces the quality of life and imposes health costs on individuals, organizations, and governments [4, 6]. The social and economic consequences of these disorders include reduced productivity due to absenteeism, leave and early retirement, loss of working days, significant financial consequences due to direct and hidden costs, and destructive effects on the quality of life, health, and job satisfaction. Individuals, organizations, and communities have been confirmed by numerous studies [6–9].

Statistics show that MSDs are the second most common occupational disease among 160 million occupational disorders in the world [10]. These disorders account for 7% of society’s diseases and 19% of hospitalizations [11, 12]. The prevalence of musculoskeletal disorders among workers has been reported to vary between 40 and 90% worldwide [13]. At the global level, musculoskeletal disorders are considered one of the main causes of health complaints among workers in various industries and the second cause of physical disability in developed countries [14, 15]. The prevalence of these disorders varies in different occupations. Global statistics show that the prevalence of WMSDs among petrochemical workers is about 41.5% [16].

In developing countries, insufficient understanding of occupational hazards by workers, lack of control measures on occupational hazards, and poorly designed workstations increase the burden of these disorders among workers [17]. In the petrochemical industry where products are continuously produced, employees are exposed to various musculoskeletal disorders risk factors such as long hours of sitting activity with high mental workload among control room employees, highly dynamic repetitive activities in repair and maintenance operations, turning valves Large, operating other large production line machinery, placing, holding or moving objects, frequent use of instruments, frequent rotation of valves, applying force and pressure on the hard surface [11, 18, 19].

Various studies show that MSDs are multifactorial. Physical factors are as follows: doing heavy, static, or monotonous work, intense or restricted posture, repetitive movements, ergonomics of the work environment, improper equipment, forces, exposure to vibration, manual handling of materials, excessive energy consumption, poor posture, bending excess, and rotation of the spine [4, 20, 21]. Psychosocial factors are as follows: organizational, financial, and social aspects, interpersonal relationships, work-rest cycle, rotational shift, unfavorable training, support of colleagues, and job dissatisfaction [22]. Demographic and individual factors are as follows: age, gender, body mass index, work experience, general health, sports activity, individual lifestyle, individual behavioral habits, individual psychological characteristics, and capacities [20], as risk factors in causing disorders musculoskeletal injuries caused by work play a role in employees of various industries, including the petrochemical industry [21].

According to the conducted studies, most of the reasons stated regarding MSDs are rooted in the causes of unhealthy and risky behavior performed by petrochemical industry employees [22, 23]. The reduction of these disorders will be possible if the workers of the petrochemical industry can observe and maintain the correct behaviors during their daily activities [24].

Based on the studies, it seems that the main obstacles in performing preventive behaviors of MSDs in the petrochemical industry are the lack of local and appropriate training for workers during work, the lack of ergonomic equipment suitable for the anthropometry of workers at work, the lack of cycle work is the right break in the workplace and health care costs [25, 26].

Health education is the most important principle in changing people’s behavior and health education planners can change people’s knowledge, attitudes, and beliefs by giving correct information and awareness [27]. With comprehensive reviews and evaluations of social, epidemiological, behavioral, individual, environmental, ecological, and educational dimensions and a detailed review of supporting and inhibiting policies in the work environment regarding existing health problems, planning, designing, and implementing suitable educational interventions, and providing practical suggestions and local will discuss various topics including the preventive behaviors of musculoskeletal disorders to eliminate and correct undesirable health behaviors and create, maintain and promote desirable behaviors in employees of various industries, including petrochemical industry workers [24, 28], and lead them to make decisions in the direction of adopting and carrying out good behavior [29].

Several studies show that health education and health promotion interventions include interventions with an emphasis on ergonomic principles and control measures in various productive work environments, including training workshops and re-designing workstations [2, 11, 30, 31], improving the condition of workstations, providing collaborative training programs (discussion and problem-solving practice) [8, 32], implementing participatory ergonomic intervention programs from passive training techniques (such as lectures, presentation of training videos, and preparation of pamphlets) to performance-based techniques (such as workstation redesign by encouraging workers to participate in the process of identifying risk factors) [2, 26, 30], implementing interventions by maintaining a balance between work and sufficient rest time during work and using stress reduction techniques [33, 34], appropriate design of work environments according to the characteristics of working people and based on the type of their work [35], and can play an important role in preventing and reducing MSDs among workers in various industries, including petrochemicals.

Several questionnaires such as the Nordic Musculoskeletal Disorders Questionnaire (NMDQ) [36], McGill Pain Questionnaire (MPQ) [37], Chronic Pain Rating Scale (CPGS) [38], and Rowland-Morris Disability Questionnaire [37] are used to understand how MSDs affect a person’s ability to perform normal activities.

However, these questionnaires cannot evaluate the causes and obstacles of performing or not performing preventive behaviors of MSDs. Therefore, to develop any intervention for the prevention of MSDs caused by work in different jobs, especially petrochemical industry employees, we will need a behavior measurement instrument so that we can understand the related causes and make appropriate interventions accordingly.

Objective

Therefore, according to the elimination of these limitations, the purpose of this multi-step and structured protocol is to identify behavioral risk factors that cause WMSDs, develop a tool, and design and implement an educational intervention to teach effective behaviors in the prevention of MSDs in petrochemical industry employees. To achieve the overall goal, the following four stages will be implemented.

Stage 1: Conducting a qualitative study with conventional content analysis method.

Stage 2: Design and validation of an instrument for measuring preventive behaviors of MSDs based on content analysis.

Stage 3: Designing and compiling the educational program (experimental randomized clinical trial).

Stage 4: Implementation and evaluation of the educational program and measurement of the stability of the program and psychometrics of the instrument.

Trial methods/design

The design of this protocol is a mixed-method study of the type of qualitative study and a randomized experimental clinical trial with a control group, which will be done in four stages. Its final goal is to design, validate, and evaluate an instrument for measuring preventive behaviors of MSDs of petrochemical industry employees. This protocol was developed and reported according to the recommendations of the Standard Protocol Items Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT) [39] and will be conducted and reported following the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) (Figs. 1, 2, and 3). The study overview

The first stage of the study: the qualitative study of conventional content analysis (Fig. 1)

Qualitative studies are the best type of study to obtain behaviors, feelings, attitudes, characteristics, values, meanings, and perceptions that determine the health status and lifestyles of certain individuals or groups [40]. When the purpose of a study is to describe a phenomenon or when the theory or research literature on the phenomenon under study is limited, a qualitative study with conventional content analysis will be used.

The purpose of this stage

The purpose of this stage is to obtain the experiences of employees suffering from MSDs, discover their needs and experiences, determine the history of MSDs in employees, and determine the behavioral and non-behavioral causes of MSDs, the cause is doing or not doing behaviors that lead to the creation or reduction of musculoskeletal disorders in petrochemical industry employees.

Characteristics of the research community and participants in the qualitative study

At this stage, based on the available studies [41, 42], the researcher will conduct in-depth interviews with people who are a rich source of information (employees and managerial and executive agents of the petrochemical industry) willing to participate.

Sample size

In this stage of the research, the primary participants with maximum diversity in terms of (age, gender, education level, socio-economic level, work experience, cultural level, variety of job duties, and full-time, part-time, and contract workers) are selected using purposive sampling, which is a suitable method for qualitative research [41]. The sample size is based on the available studies [29, 42]. There will be 25 to 30 people, and the interview will continue until reaching information saturation and will continue until the participants do not add new data to the previous data and in this case, the sampling will stop [43].

Place and community of research at this stage

This study will be carried out in the Petrochemical Company of Asalouye City which is located in the south of Iran. The researcher will try to choose samples with maximum diversity (in terms of age, gender, socio-economic status, level of education, job level place of residence, etc.). Therefore, based on the criteria for entering the research, the participants will gradually enter the research and an individual and face-to-face interview will be conducted, this process will continue until the data reaches saturation and no new code is extracted.

Entry criteria for the qualitative study

Entry criteria for the qualitative study are as follows: being of Iranian origin, having a medical diagnosis of MSDs caused by work for at least 6 months (the patient to have passed the crisis period and be able to express his experiences in this field to the researcher), work experience of more than 2 years, age more than 18 years (age less than 18 years is adolescence and usually has its conditions), and able to express experiences and interested in participating in research.

Qualitative study data collection method

The data collection method in this stage of the research will be an unstructured interview. At this stage of the research, the researcher will be present in the petrochemical industry (with maximum environmental diversity from different centers of the industry) and after introducing himself and explaining the importance and objectives of the research and ensuring the confidentiality of the information and gaining the trust of the participants, he received a written informed consent signed by them. Will coordinate the time and place of the interview with them.

The interviews will be recorded by the researcher. The interview will be conducted individually and in a private and quiet place approved by the participants. At first, open questions will be used at the beginning of the interview to make it possible for the participants to fully describe their experiences regarding the phenomenon in question. Then, in the continuation of the interview process, the questions will be directed toward specialized questions and in line with the goals. After the participant’s answer, probing questions will be asked according to the answers.

The duration of the interviews will depend on the tolerance of the participants, the amount of information, and their willingness and agreement. Interviews continue until data saturation is reached. All recorded interviews will be typed in Word software.

Then the content analysis of the qualitative data is done based on the stage of analyzing the qualitative data obtained from the contracted qualitative study based on the steps proposed by Granheim and Lundman [41].

In the first stage of the research, the concepts related to the behavioral and non-behavioral causes of musculoskeletal disorders in petrochemical industry workers will be carefully defined. Then, based on the characteristics, similarities, and differences, they will be placed in different classes. Finally, the dimensions and areas that make up the concept of MSDs in petrochemical workers and questionnaire structures are determined.

Analysis of the data from the qualitative study

Then the information from the in-depth interviews will be analyzed by the researcher based on the Graneheim and Landman algorithm (a simple algorithm for classifying qualitative data) in qualitative content analysis in the following order [42].

At first, the entire recorded file of the interview will be written in Farsi. Second, the entire interview text will be considered as a unit of analysis. Third, semantic units will be extracted and the coding process will be done. The coding process and the extracted codes will be reviewed and approved by the experts of the research team. Fourth, similar codes will be placed under the main floors and floors. Fifth, finally emerging categories will be formed [44].

In this study, validity, confirmability, transferability, and reliability criteria will be used to evaluate the accuracy of the obtained results [43, 45].

Validity: with supporting evidence, the researcher ensures that the findings represent what is being studied [46].

Confirmability: the researcher ensures that the extracted findings are based on the participant’s answers and not the researcher’s interpretation and bias [46].

Transferability: with contextual information, the researcher confirms whether the results can be used in similar situations or not [46, 47].

Reliability: the researcher describes the study in detail so that the work can be repeated [45].

The second phase: instrument validation

This stage of the study will be methodological research and in it, we will determine the validity and reliability of the instrument for measuring the preventive behaviors of MSDs.

Validity

In this study, content validity, face validity, and construct validity (factor analysis) are used to determine the validity of the instrument for measuring preventive behaviors of MSDs in petrochemical industry workers.

Content validity

According to Grant and Davis [49], content validity will be measured qualitatively and quantitatively. In the validity of qualitative content from the point of view of the panel of experts (health education, physiotherapy, occupational health, ergonomics, and safety), it will be used for grammatical points, proper use of words, correct place of phrases, scoring, transparency and simplicity of the items [42, 45].

In quantitative content validity, content validity ratio (C.V.R) and content validity index (C.V.I) will be used. For the content validity ratio, 10 experts (health education, physiotherapy, occupational health, ergonomics, and safety) are asked to comment on the necessity of the questionnaire items based on the three-part spectrum “necessary, useful but not necessary, not necessary.” Then the content validity ratio will be calculated. According to Lawshe et al. [50], scores above 0.62 are considered appropriate.

To calculate the content validity index, the evaluators check the degree of relevance of each item with the desired structure with four statements “not relevant, relatively relevant, relevant, and completely relevant” and then the score of the content validity index will be calculated for each item. Scores higher than 0.79 are considered appropriate, and scores between 0.7 and 0.8 will need to be revised and corrected [51, 52].

Face validity

The face validity of the instrument will be evaluated quantitatively and qualitatively.

In qualitative formal validity, 10 participants in the research are asked to comment on (the level of difficulty, comprehensibility, and ambiguity of each item), and after summarizing and analyzing, necessary changes are applied.

In quantitative formal validity, the participants are also asked to (specify the importance of each item based on a Likert scale of 1 to 5), and then the impact score of each item is calculated. Items with an impact factor greater than 1.5 are appropriate and items with a lower impact factor are removed (Formula 1) [44, 53].

Construct validity (factor analysis)

For the construct validity of the factor analysis method, which includes 4 basic steps, including the preparation of the correlation matrix of all the variables used in the analysis, estimation of commonality, extraction of factors, selection and rotation of factors to simplify and make the factor structure more understandable and interpret the results [41] will be used.

Exploratory factor analysis will be used to identify relationships between items and their relationship with the main variable under investigation, i.e., musculoskeletal disorders in petrochemical industry workers and instrument structures.

Kaiser–Meyer–Olkin (KMO) test and Bartlett’s sphericity test will be used to check sampling adequacy. After calculating the correlation matrix between the variables, the factors will be extracted and the variables that have a high correlation will be placed in a category or factor [41, 54].

According to McCallum and colleagues, the required sample size for factor analysis to determine the construct validity of the instrument is considered to be at least five times the number of instrument items [55].

Reliability

The concept of the reliability of an instrument is that if we use the instrument again under completely similar conditions to measure the characteristic to be measured, how similar and reliable the results will be [56]. To check the reliability of the researcher-made questionnaire in this study, two retest methods will be used to measure external reliability and Cronbach’s alpha to measure internal reliability.

External reliability measurement

To measure external reliability, the test-re-test method (test questions are given twice to a single group under the same conditions) and the scores are compared. To find the correlation between scores, the Spearman or Pearson correlation coefficient is calculated. If the correlation value is 0.74 or more, it can be said that the instrument has a reliable feature [57].

Cronbach’s alpha

The internal reliability of the instrument is calculated through the coefficient of internal consistency (Cronbach’s alpha), which varies between 0 and 1. According to Bland and Altman’s theory, scales whose internal consistency coefficient is greater than 0.7 can be used in research [55, 58, 59].

Scoring

After obtaining the items and coordinating them with each other, the scoring rules will be selected in a 5-mode Likert spectrum. Formula 2 will be used to standardize the scoring based on the linear transformation standard of 100 [60]. Finally, a score between 0 and 100 is obtained.

The fourth stage of the study: implementation and evaluation of the educational intervention program and evaluation of the sustainability of the program (Fig. 3).

Educational interventions

At this stage, the participants will be divided into two intervention and control groups based on the simple randomization sampling method. The intervention group will receive the educational intervention program and the control group will not receive any educational intervention program. The educational intervention will be designed to address specific hazards associated with MSDs in petrochemical workers. The content of the interventions includes the understanding of musculoskeletal disorders (information about common types of MSDs, causes, initial symptoms of pressure or damage, and their long-term consequences), principles of ergonomics (training of ergonomic practices appropriate to specific job tasks in the petrochemical industry), use of tools and equipment (instructions on proper use of tools and equipment to minimize strain), stress management (techniques for stress management that can help with muscle tension, such as mindfulness exercises, breathing techniques, and breaks to promote mental well-being), preventive behaviors MSDs (stretching and corrective movements to strengthen muscles and increase flexibility). These materials will be prepared and compiled through the needs assessment of the target group and the areas obtained from the WMSD preventive behavior measurement tool and using the opinions of experts in education and health promotion, occupational health, ergonomics, physiotherapy, and authentic scientific texts.

Presentation methods

These contents will be conducted including face-to-face education sessions(by a qualified ergonomist or occupational health professional, allowing for hands-on demonstrations and direct interaction), online methods ( e-learning platforms that workers can access at their convenience including videos, interactive quizzes, and downloadable resources, accommodating various learning styles), Practical sessions in the workplace setting (ergonomic assessments), poster presentations, pamphlets, infographics, short health messages, podcasts, animations, by the main researcher under the supervision of the research team.

Quarterly evaluations: Quarterly follow-ups to assess progress and check the performance of learned behaviors.

In this study, the data collection instrument is a demographic questionnaire, a researcher-made questionnaire for measuring preventive behaviors of MSDs, and two auxiliary instruments, including the visual analog scale (VAS) [69], and The Quebec Back Pain Disability Scale [70]. Data analysis

In this stage of the study, the data collected from the four stages of the pre-test, immediately, 3 months, and 6 months after the intervention will be coded and analyzed with SPSS software.

Kolmogorov–Smirnov test is used to check the normality of data distribution. If the data obtained from the research has a normal distribution, central indices, dispersion, and parametric statistical tests will be used, otherwise non-parametric statistical tests will be used.

Statistical tests of squares will be used to check the relationship between nominal variables. If the data is normal, the paired T-test will be used to compare and check the variables before and after the implementation of the educational intervention in a group, and if the data is not normally distributed, the Wilcoxon test will be used.

Repeated measurement tests will be used to compare variables several times and in several groups. Statistical tests of analysis of variance will be used to determine the effect of educational interventions on the employees participating in the research. To control the effect of confounding variables, the statistical method of co-variance analysis will be used.

Discussion

One of the features of this study is to pay attention to health promotion in industrial workplaces. This study will investigate the effect of educational intervention programs in creating preventive behaviors of MSDs in petrochemical industry workers.

The strengths of this study include conducting unstructured in-depth interviews, the mixed-method design, conducting qualitative research, focusing on behavioral risks, and development of a novel measurement instrument, psychometrics of the questionnaire, designing an educational intervention program based on the needs of the target group, and implement and evaluate the educational intervention program, in-person and face-to-face education, The easy access of the participants pointed to the researcher and experts of the research team.

The limitation of the current research is the self-reporting of the participants in completing the questionnaires, which the researcher and the experts of the research team used to solve this limitation and reduce potential bias, from objective ergonomic evaluations, three-dimensional motion recording systems to analyze situations, movements and performance of workers during work, standardized tools such as Rapid Upper Limb Assessment (RULA) [71], Rapid Whole Body Assessment (REBA) [72], field visits, and observations will be used.

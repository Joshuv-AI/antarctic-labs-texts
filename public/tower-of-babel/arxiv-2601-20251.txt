Efficient Evaluation of LLM Performance with Statistical Guarantees
Skyler Wu, Yash Nair, Emmanuel J. Candès

Abstract
Exhaustively evaluating many large language models (LLMs) on a large suite of benchmarks is expensive. We cast benchmarking as finite-population inference and, under a fixed query budget, seek tight confidence intervals (CIs) for model accuracy with valid frequentist coverage. We propose Factorized Active Querying (FAQ), which (a) leverages historical information through a Bayesian factor model; (b) adaptively selects questions using a hybrid variance-reduction/active-learning sampling policy; and (c) maintains validity through Proactive Active Inference —a finite-population extension of active inference ( 49 ) that enables direct question selection while preserving coverage. With negligible overhead cost, FAQ delivers up to 5 × 5\times effective sample size gains over strong baselines on two benchmark suites, across varying historical-data missingness levels: this means that it matches the CI width of uniform sampling while using up to 5 × 5\times fewer queries. We release our source code and our curated datasets to support reproducible evaluation and future research.

Problem description
Consider a benchmark suite of
N_{q}

z_{j}\in\{0,1\}

\theta:=\frac{1}{N_{q}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}z_{j}\in[0,1].

(1)

Such valid-coverage intervals provide uncertainty-quantified performance estimates to support deployment decisions alongside considerations such as cost and latency.

Related Work

Probabilistic factor modeling
FAQ is inspired by probabilistic factor modeling
(
31
;
9
;
6
)
, which assumes a (Bayesian) generative factor model for a partially-observed binary matrix—here, the matrix of model-question correctness outcomes—and learns low-dimensional latent factors from the data. Importantly, any uncertainty guarantees for these approaches are tied to correct specification of the factor model (and any prior assumptions). In contrast, FAQ uses the Bayesian factor model only as a
mental model
to guide adaptive query selection, while delivering valid frequentist coverage regardless of factor-model fidelity. If the data
is
well-described by the factor model, FAQ automatically adapts and delivers tighter CIs.

Active statistical inference
For model-free frequentist coverage, FAQ builds on active statistical inference
(
49
;
18
)
, which uses machine learning predictions over unlabeled covariates/features to adaptively select labels under a fixed budget and construct CIs. Our benchmark setting, however, provides no explicit covariates, so we use the factor model above to construct latent features to guide adaptive query selection. Moreover, unlike sequential active inference—which, in a pre-specified “nature’s order” stream, can only make label/skip decisions for the next item and enforces the budget constraints only in expectation—PAI/FAQ directly selects the next question from the full benchmark bank using all available information and enforces the query budget exactly.

LLM evaluations
FAQ contributes to a growing literature on evaluating LLMs using only a fraction of benchmark questions

By targeting finite-bank accuracy with model-free coverage, FAQ provides uncertainty quantification that remains meaningful over such drifts.

3.2
Proactive Active Inference

Motivation and desiderata
The factor model is only a working model and offers no coverage guarantee on its own. Moreover, we make no i.i.d. assumptions on benchmark questions: we treat the labels
z_{j}
as fixed-but-unknown, since curated suites often intentionally include adversarial/edge cases and are not necessarily meant to represent a superpopulation of user queries. Thus, we want a
model-free
inference layer that wraps around the factor model and yields valid coverage for the finite-bank estimand
\theta
.

Sequential active inference
(
49
)
provides coverage, but differs from our approach in two key ways. First, it follows
nature’s order
: at time
t
, one observes only the next item in a fixed stream and can only make a label/skip decision via an adaptive Bernoulli policy. In contrast, in our benchmark-bank setting, we have access to the full pool of question factors
\{v_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N_{q}}
at every step and wish to leverage this global information to
proactively select
the next question. Second, we desire a workflow that is deterministic conditional on selection: once a question is chosen, it is labeled with probability
1
(no coin flip).

These desiderata are particularly important for FAQ because the new model factor
u
is initialized from historical models and may be inaccurate (especially as models evolve over time). Proactively selecting informative questions early accelerates learning of
u
(c.f. Section
3.4
), improving subsequent query choices and inference. Practically, our desiderata better matches typical evaluation workflows: one selects a subset of questions under a budget, rather than streaming through the entire bank with probabilistic labeling decisions.

3.5
A Practical Hybrid Sampling Policy

In practice, we trade off exploitation (variance-reduction using the current
\hat{p}^{(t-1)}
) and exploration (querying informative questions to quickly refine
\hat{u}^{(t)}
and hence
\hat{p}^{(t)}
). We implement this via a time-varying hybrid sampling policy. Inspired by Sections
3.3
-
3.4

, define unnormalized oracle (o) and active-learning (a) scores as follows:

\displaystyle s^{(t)}_{\text{o}}(j):=\sqrt{\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)}\left(1-\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)}\right)},\;s^{(t)}_{\text{a}}(j):=d^{(t)}(j).

Define
\text{Norm}(x)(j)=x(j)/\sum_{j^{\prime}}x(j^{\prime})
. We convert each score to a probability vector via
h^{(t)}_{\text{o}}=\text{Norm}(s^{(t)}_{\text{o}})
and
h^{(t)}_{\text{a}}=\text{Norm}(s^{(t)}_{\text{a}})
.
Given hyperparameters
\rho,\gamma,\beta_{0},\tau\in(0,1)

\alpha_{t}
and tempering exponent

\displaystyle\alpha_{t}=\max\left(0,1-\frac{t}{\rho n_{b}}\right),\;\beta_{t}=\beta_{0}\min\left(1,\frac{t}{\gamma n_{b}}\right).

We mix the normalized scores and apply exponential tempering to form our combined-and-tempered score

\displaystyle h_{\text{cat}}^{(t)}(j)=\left(\left(1-\alpha_{t}\right)h^{(t)}_{\text{o}}(j)+\alpha_{t}h^{(t)}_{\text{a}}(j)\right)^{\beta_{t}}.

(9)

Finally, we normalize

To isolate the gains of PAI and our hybrid sampling (beyond the factor model), we compare FAQ to a “traditional” active inference ablation that follows

Three mechanisms likely drive the shift. First, FAQ can proactively target informative questions early on via its active-learning component, whereas traditional active inference can only label/skip items in a fixed stream and its

6
Discussion and Conclusion

We introduced
Factorized Active Querying
(FAQ), a finite-bank framework for LLM evaluation that, under fixed query budgets and with negligible overhead cost, achieves up to
5\times
higher ESS than uniform sampling and
2.4\times
higher ESS than the strongest baselines—all while maintaining model-free frequentist coverage. FAQ also supports
continuous monitoring
: each new evaluation both leverages and expands the historical data, enabling progressively more cost-effective uncertainty-quantified evaluation over time.

Future work is motivated by a few concrete limitations. First, we treat question factors as fixed from historical data: periodically updating them could better track drift and improve efficiency. Second, richer, metadata-informed priors (e.g., parameter count or base model family) may improve low-budget performance. Third, our ablations suggest budget-dependent regimes, motivating three-stage hybrid designs that incorporate traditional active inference at larger budgets. Finally, extending FAQ to non-binary feedback and batched querying would broaden applicability and enable parallel evaluation across adjudicators and inference workers.

Impact Statement

Our work provides practitioners with a statistically-principled method for efficiently and accurately evaluating an LLM’s performance using only a fraction of queries compared to traditional methods. This can support more reliable model selection and monitoring by making statistically-rigorous evaluation feasible at scale. At the same time, cheaper evaluation may lower the barrier to not only rapid iteration but also benchmark gaming, so we encourage using FAQ with held-out test suites, transparent and good-faith reporting of configurations and uncertainty, and safety frameworks. We urge the reader to deploy our methods or variants thereof towards the general benefit of society.

Full Historical Factor Model Negative Log-Likelihood Objective

O

\displaystyle L\left(\{u_{i^{\prime}}\}_{i^{\prime}=1}^{N_{\text{old}}},\{v_{j^{\prime}}\}_{j^{\prime}=1}^{N_{q}}\right)=-\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{old}}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}O_{ij}\left(H_{ij}\log\sigma\left(u_{i}^{\top}v_{j}\right)+\left(1-H_{ij}\right)\log\left(1-\sigma\left(u_{i}^{\top}v_{j}\right)\right)\right),

(13)

We implement using PyTorch’s binary cross-entropy function for numerical stability. In practice,
\{u_{i^{\prime}}\}_{i^{\prime}=1}^{N_{\text{old}}}

A.2
Derivation of Online Logistic Regression Laplace Approximation Update

This derivation was inspired by Ch. 4 of
(
5
)
’s discussion on Laplace approximations and Bayesian logistic regression. The key idea is to Taylor expand the log-posterior around the current mean, which yields approximate closed-form update formulas while preserving approximate Gaussian conjugacy.

Suppose at time
t-1
we have
u\sim\mathcal{N}(m,S)
. At time
t
, we queried
z
with corresponding question factor
v
.
13
13

13

For notational brevity, we omit random indices
I_{t}
, superscripts, and subscripts. The derivations are left exactly unchanged.
Because under our factor model,
z\sim\text{Bern}\left(\sigma\left(u^{\top}v\right)\right)
, by Bayes’ Rule we have the following, where “
\sim
” refers to equality up to an additive constant that does not depend on
u
, and
\sigma(x):=\frac{1}{1+\exp(-x)}=\frac{\exp(x)}{1+\exp(x)}
:

\displaystyle p(u\mid z)\propto p(z\mid u)p(u)\propto\sigma\left(u^{\top}v\right)^{z}\left(1-\sigma\left(u^{\top}v\right)\right)^{1-z}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left(u-m\right)^{\top}S^{-1}\left(u-m\right)\right)

\displaystyle\implies\log p(u\mid z)\sim z\log\left(\sigma\left(u^{\top}v\right)\right)+\left(1-z\right)\log\left(1-\sigma\left(u^{\top}v\right)\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(u-m\right)^{\top}S^{-1}\left(u-m\right)

\displaystyle=zu^{\top}v-z\log\left(1+\exp\left(u^{\top}v\right)\right)-(1-z)\log\left(1+\exp(u^{\top}v)\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(u-m\right)^{\top}S^{-1}\left(u-m\right)

\displaystyle=zu^{\top}v-\log\left(1+\exp(u^{\top}v)\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(u-m\right)^{\top}S^{-1}\left(u-m\right).

To apply Laplace’s approximation, we will require a second-order Taylor expansion of
\log p(u\mid z)
about
u=m
. For now, we proceed by computing the gradient and Hessian of the exact log-posterior:

\displaystyle\nabla_{u}\log p(u\mid z)=zv-\frac{\exp(u^{\top}v)}{1+\exp(u^{\top}v)}v-S^{-1}(u-m)=zv-\sigma\left(u^{\top}v\right)v-S^{-1}(u-m),

and, noting that
\nabla_{x}\sigma(x)=\sigma(x)(1-\sigma(x))
, we have

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}_{u}\log p(u\mid z)=-S^{-1}-\sigma\left(u^{\top}v\right)\left(1-\sigma\left(u^{\top}v\right)\right)vv^{\top}.

Assembling our second-order Taylor expansion and defining
\hat{p}:=\sigma\left(u^{\top}v\right)
, let

\displaystyle b=\nabla_{u}\log p(u\mid z)\mid_{u=m}=zv-\sigma\left(u^{\top}v\right)v:=\left(z-\hat{p}\right)v,

and

\displaystyle A=\nabla^{2}_{u}\log p(u\mid z)\mid_{u=m}=-S^{-1}-\hat{p}\left(1-\hat{p}\right)vv^{\top}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\log p(u\mid z)\approx\log p(m\mid z)+b^{\top}\left(u-m\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(u-m\right)^{\top}A\left(u-m\right)

\displaystyle\sim b^{\top}u+\frac{1}{2}u^{\top}Au-u^{\top}Am=\frac{1}{2}\left(u^{\top}Au-2\left(Am-b\right)^{\top}u\right).

After completing the square, we obtain the following

\displaystyle\log p(u\mid z)\sim\frac{1}{2}\left(u^{\top}Au-2\left(Am-b\right)^{\top}u\right)\sim-\frac{1}{2}\left(\left(u-A^{-1}\left(Am-b\right)\right)^{\top}\left(-A\right)\left(u-A^{-1}\left(Am-b\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\left(u-\left(m-A^{-1}b\right)\right)^{\top}\left(-A\right)\left(u-\left(m-A^{-1}b\right)\right)\right).

Pattern-matching to the multivariate normal distribution, we deduce that

\displaystyle u\mid z\overset{\cdot}{\sim}\mathcal{N}\left(m-A^{-1}b,-A^{-1}\right)\overset{d}{=}\mathcal{N}\left(m+\left(S^{-1}+\hat{p}\left(1-\hat{p}\right)vv^{\top}\right)^{-1}\left(z-\hat{p}\right)v,\left(S^{-1}+\hat{p}\left(1-\hat{p}\right)vv^{\top}\right)^{-1}\right).

For compute-efficient updates, the Sherman-Morrison formula tells us that

\displaystyle\left(S^{-1}+\hat{p}\left(1-\hat{p}\right)vv^{\top}\right)^{-1}=S-\frac{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})Svv^{\top}S}{1+\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})v^{\top}Sv}.

Putting everything together,

\displaystyle u\mid z\overset{\cdot}{\sim}\mathcal{N}\left(m+\left(S-\frac{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})Svv^{\top}S}{1+\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})v^{\top}Sv}\right)\left(z-\hat{p}\right)v,S-\frac{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})Svv^{\top}S}{1+\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})v^{\top}Sv}\right).

We enumerate the technical assumptions/conditions required to prove the asymptotic normality stated in Theorem

Assumption A.1

(Variance stabilization)
.

The average conditional variance converges in probability to a positive deterministic limit

(which does not depend on
(m)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{n_{b}}}\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\mbox{Var}\left(\Delta_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right)\overset{p}{\to}\sigma^{2}.

Assumption A.2

The triangular array of problem instances in combination with sampling policies obey the Lindeberg condition. Specifically, for every

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{n_{b}}}\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{t}^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$1$}(|\Delta_{t}|>\epsilon\sqrt{{n_{b}}})\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]\overset{p}{\to}0.

Assumption A.3

(Conditional variance control)
.

The average conditional variance of the inverse-propensity-weighted predicted probabilities satisfies

\frac{1}{n_{b}}\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\mbox{Var}\left(\frac{1}{N_{q}}\cdot\frac{\hat{p}^{(t-1)}_{I_{t}}}{q_{t}(I_{t})}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right)=o_{p}(n_{b}).

A.3.3
Asymptotic Normality via Martingale CLT

By substitution, we begin with the following:

\sqrt{{n_{b}}}\left(\hat{\theta}_{n_{b}}-\theta\right)=\sqrt{{n_{b}}}\left(\frac{1}{{n_{b}}}\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\phi_{t}-\frac{1}{N_{q}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}z_{j}\right).

Simplifying, we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{{n_{b}}}\left(\hat{\theta}_{n_{b}}-\theta\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{{n_{b}}}}\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\phi_{t}-\frac{\sqrt{{n_{b}}}}{N_{q}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}z_{j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{{n_{b}}}}\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\phi_{t}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{{n_{b}}}N_{q}}\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}z_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{{n_{b}}}}\left(\phi_{t}-\frac{1}{N_{q}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}z_{j}\right)=\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{b}}}\Delta_{t}.

Next, we will show that the
\Delta_{t}
are martingale differences—i.e., that their cumulative sums form a martingale.

Proposition A.4

(Martingale differences and unbiasedness)
.

Define
M_{t}
as follows:

M_{t}:=\sum_{s=1}^{t}\left(\phi_{s}-\frac{1}{N_{q}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}z_{j}\right):=\sum_{s=1}^{t}\Delta_{s}.

(14)

Then,
(M_{t},\mathcal{F}_{t})
is a martingale. In particular,
\hat{\theta}_{n_{b}}
is unbiased for
\theta
for every finite
n_{b}
.

Proof.

Substituting, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\phi_{t}-\frac{1}{N_{q}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}z_{j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{q}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)}+\frac{z_{I_{t}}-\hat{p}_{I_{t}}^{(t-1)}}{q_{t}(I_{t})}-\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}z_{j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{q}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\left(\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)}-z_{j}\right)+\frac{z_{I_{t}}-\hat{p}_{I_{t}}^{(t-1)}}{q_{t}(I_{t})}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{q}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\left(\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)}-z_{j}\right)+\frac{1}{N_{q}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{z_{I_{t}}-\hat{p}_{I_{t}}^{(t-1)}}{q_{t}(I_{t})}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{q}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\left(\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)}-z_{j}\right)+\frac{1}{N_{q}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\frac{z_{j}-\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)}}{q_{t}(j)}P(I_{t}=j\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{q}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\left(\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)}-z_{j}\right)+\frac{1}{N_{q}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\frac{z_{j}-\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)}}{q_{t}(j)}q_{t}(j)=0,

thus confirming that we have a martingale. As a corollary, by summing and taking expectations, we find that
\mathbb{E}[\hat{\theta}_{n_{b}}]=\theta
: i.e., that
\hat{\theta}_{n_{b}}
is unbiased for
\theta
at any finite
{n_{b}}
.
∎

Given that we have a martingale, we now proceed to obtain the asymptotic variance of
\hat{\theta}_{n_{b}}
. To do, so, we need to first compute the conditional variances of our martingale increments. Because the
z_{j}
are treated as fixed, by substitution we have

\displaystyle\mbox{Var}\left(\Delta_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right)=\frac{1}{N_{q}^{2}}\mbox{Var}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)}+\frac{z_{I_{t}}-\hat{p}_{I_{t}}^{(t-1)}}{q_{t}(I_{t})}-\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}z_{j}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right)=\frac{1}{N_{q}^{2}}\mbox{Var}\left(\frac{z_{I_{t}}-\hat{p}_{I_{t}}^{(t-1)}}{q_{t}(I_{t})}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{q}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{z_{I_{t}}-\hat{p}_{I_{t}}^{(t-1)}}{q_{t}(I_{t})}\right)^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]-\frac{1}{N_{q}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{z_{I_{t}}-\hat{p}_{I_{t}}^{(t-1)}}{q_{t}(I_{t})}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{q}^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\left(\frac{z_{j}-\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)}}{q_{t}(j)}\right)^{2}P(I_{t}=j\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1})-\frac{1}{N_{q}^{2}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\frac{z_{j}-\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)}}{q_{t}(j)}P(I_{t}=j)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{q}^{2}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\frac{\left(z_{j}-\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)}\right)^{2}}{q_{t}(j)}-\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\left(z_{j}-\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)}\right)\right)^{2}\right).

Under Assumptions
A.1
-
A.2
, the martingale central limit theorem (c.f.,
19
) implies

\sqrt{{n_{b}}}\left(\hat{\theta}_{n_{b}}-\theta\right)\overset{d}{\to}\mathcal{N}\left(0,\sigma^{2}\right).

(15)

Lemma
A.5
below shows that
\hat{\sigma}_{n_{b}}^{2}\overset{p}{\rightarrow}\sigma^{2}
, and thus the result follows by Slutsky’s theorem.

Lemma A.5
.

Define

\hat{\sigma}_{n_{b}}^{2}=\frac{1}{{n_{b}}N_{q}^{2}}\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\frac{\left(z_{I_{t}}-\hat{p}_{I_{t}}^{(t-1)}\right)^{2}}{q_{t}(I_{t})^{2}}-\frac{1}{{n_{b}}N_{q}^{2}}\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\left(\frac{1}{{n_{b}}}\sum_{s=1}^{n_{b}}\frac{z_{I_{s}}}{q_{s}(I_{s})}-\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)}\right)^{2}.

(16)

Then
\hat{\sigma}_{n_{b}}^{2}\overset{p}{\rightarrow}\sigma^{2}
.

Proof.

Define

\hat{A}_{n_{b}}:=\frac{1}{{n_{b}}N_{q}^{2}}\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\frac{\left(z_{I_{t}}-\hat{p}_{I_{t}}^{(t-1)}\right)^{2}}{q_{t}(I_{t})^{2}},\hat{B}_{n_{b}}:=\frac{1}{{n_{b}}N_{q}^{2}}\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\left(\frac{1}{{n_{b}}}\sum_{s=1}^{n_{b}}\frac{z_{I_{s}}}{q_{s}(I_{s})}-\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)}\right)^{2}

as well as

A:=\frac{1}{n_{b}}\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\frac{1}{N_{q}^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\frac{\left(z_{j}-\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)}\right)^{2}}{q_{t}(j)},\;B:=\frac{1}{n_{b}}\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\frac{1}{N_{q}^{2}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\left(z_{j}-\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)}\right)\right)^{2}

To show that
\hat{\sigma}_{n_{b}}^{2}\overset{p}{\rightarrow}\sigma^{2}
, it suffices to show that (a)
\hat{A}_{n_{b}}=A+o_{p}(1)
and (b)
\hat{B}_{n_{b}}=B+o_{p}(1)
. We show these below:

(a)

Define
\tilde{\theta}^{(t-1)}:=\frac{1}{N_{q}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\hat{p}^{(t-1)}_{j}
. Under Assumptions
A.1
–
A.2
,
19
guarantees that

\displaystyle o_{p}(1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n_{b}}\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\left[\left(\frac{1}{N_{q}}\frac{z_{I_{t}}-\hat{p}^{(t-1)}_{I_{t}}}{q_{t}(I_{t})}+\tilde{\theta}^{(t-1)}-\theta\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{N_{q}^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\frac{\left(z_{j}-\hat{p}^{(t-1)}_{j}\right)^{2}}{q_{t}(j)}-(\tilde{\theta}^{(t-1)}-\theta)^{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\hat{A}_{n_{b}}-A+\frac{1}{n_{b}}\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\left[2(\tilde{\theta}^{(t-1)}-\theta)^{2}-2(\theta-\tilde{\theta}^{(t-1)})\left(\frac{1}{N_{q}}\frac{z_{I_{t}}-\hat{p}^{(t-1)}_{I_{t}}}{q_{t}(I_{t})}\right)\right].

To show that
\hat{A}_{n_{b}}-A=o_{p}(1)
it suffices to show that

\frac{1}{n_{b}}\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\left(\theta-\tilde{\theta}^{(t-1)}\right)\left(\theta-\tilde{\theta}^{(t-1)}-\frac{1}{N_{q}}\frac{z_{I_{t}}-\hat{p}^{(t-1)}_{I_{t}}}{q_{t}(I_{t})}\right)=o_{p}(1).

(17)

To show (
17
), we apply the Lenglart inequality (c.f., e.g.,
43
or
22
) to obtain

P\left(\left|\frac{1}{n_{b}}\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\left(\theta-\tilde{\theta}^{(t-1)}\right)\left(\theta-\tilde{\theta}^{(t-1)}-\frac{1}{N_{q}}\frac{z_{I_{t}}-\hat{p}^{(t-1)}_{I_{t}}}{q_{t}(I_{t})}\right)\right|\geq\epsilon\right)\leq\\
\frac{\eta}{\epsilon}+P\left(\frac{1}{n_{b}^{2}}\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\theta-\tilde{\theta}^{(t-1)}\right)^{2}\left(\theta-\tilde{\theta}^{(t-1)}-\frac{1}{N_{q}}\frac{z_{I_{t}}-\hat{p}^{(t-1)}_{I_{t}}}{q_{t}(I_{t})}\right)^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]\geq\eta\right)

for all
\epsilon,\eta>0
. Hence, it suffices to show that
\frac{1}{n_{b}^{2}}\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\theta-\tilde{\theta}^{(t-1)}\right)^{2}\left(\theta-\tilde{\theta}^{(t-1)}-\frac{1}{N_{q}}\frac{z_{I_{t}}-\hat{p}^{(t-1)}_{I_{t}}}{q_{t}(I_{t})}\right)^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]=o_{p}(1)
which is true by virtue of the fact that
(\theta-\tilde{\theta}^{(t-1)})^{2}\leq 1
in combination with Assumption
A.1
.

(b)

Expanding both squares, we find that

\displaystyle\hat{B}_{n_{b}}-B

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{n_{b}}\sum_{s=1}^{n_{b}}\frac{1}{N_{q}}\cdot\frac{z_{I_{s}}}{q_{s}(I_{s})}\right)^{2}-\theta^{2}-2\tilde{\theta}^{(t-1)}\left[\frac{1}{n_{b}}\sum_{s=1}^{n_{b}}\frac{1}{N_{q}}\cdot\frac{z_{I_{s}}}{q_{s}(I_{s})}-\theta\right]

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{n_{b}}\sum_{s=1}^{n_{b}}\frac{1}{N_{q}}\cdot\frac{z_{I_{s}}}{q_{s}(I_{s})}-\theta\right)^{2}+2\theta\left(\frac{1}{n_{b}}\sum_{s=1}^{n_{b}}\frac{1}{N_{q}}\cdot\frac{z_{I_{s}}}{q_{s}(I_{s})}-\theta\right)-2\tilde{\theta}^{(t-1)}\left[\frac{1}{n_{b}}\sum_{s=1}^{n_{b}}\frac{1}{N_{q}}\cdot\frac{z_{I_{s}}}{q_{s}(I_{s})}-\theta\right].

As
\theta,\tilde{\theta}^{(t-1)}\in[0,1]
, it suffices to show that
\frac{1}{n_{b}}\sum_{s=1}^{n_{b}}\frac{1}{N_{q}}\cdot\frac{z_{I_{s}}}{q_{s}(I_{s})}-\theta\overset{p}{\rightarrow}0.
Since
\hat{\theta}_{n_{b}}-\theta\overset{p}{\rightarrow}0
it further suffices to show that
\frac{1}{n_{b}}\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\frac{1}{N_{q}}\frac{\hat{p}^{(t-1)}_{I_{t}}}{q_{t}(I_{t})}-\frac{1}{N_{q}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\hat{p}^{(t-1)}_{j}=o_{p}(1)
. Following the same argument as in the previous item, this is implied by

\frac{1}{n_{b}^{2}}\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\mbox{Var}\left(\frac{1}{N_{q}}\cdot\frac{\hat{p}^{(t-1)}_{I_{t}}}{q_{t}(I_{t})}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right)\overset{p}{\rightarrow}0,

which is precisely Assumption
A.3
.

∎

A.4
Proof of Theorem
3.2

Replacing
\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)}
with our non-time-varying oracle
p_{j}
’s, our estimator after
n_{b}
sampling rounds becomes

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}_{{n_{b}},\text{oracle}}=\frac{1}{{n_{b}}}\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\left(\theta^{*}+\frac{1}{N_{q}}\frac{z_{I_{t}}-p_{I_{t}}}{q_{t}(I_{t})}\right)=\theta^{*}+\frac{1}{{n_{b}}N_{q}}\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\frac{z_{I_{t}}-p_{I_{t}}}{q_{t}(I_{t})}.

Because
\theta^{*}
is deterministic, to minimize the variance of
\hat{\theta}_{{n_{b}},\text{oracle}}
, it suffices to minimize the variance of
M_{n_{b}}=\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\frac{z_{I_{t}}-p_{I_{t}}}{q_{t}(I_{t})}
. Define the filtration
\mathcal{F}_{t}=\sigma(I_{1},z_{I_{1}},\dots,I_{t},z_{I_{t}})
. Writing out the variance, we have the following, noting that because
\hat{\theta}_{{n_{b}},\text{oracle}}
is unbiased for
\theta
, it must follow that
\mathbb{E}[M_{n_{b}}]=0
and thus

\displaystyle\mbox{Var}(M_{n_{b}})=\mathbb{E}\left[M_{n_{b}}^{2}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[M_{n_{b}}\right]^{2}=\mathbb{E}[M_{n_{b}}^{2}]=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{s=1}^{n_{b}}\frac{z_{I_{s}}-p_{I_{s}}}{q_{s}(I_{s})}\right)\left(\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\frac{z_{I_{t}}-p_{I_{t}}}{q_{t}(I_{t})}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{s=1}^{n_{b}}\left(\frac{z_{I_{s}}-p_{I_{s}}}{q_{s}(I_{s})}\right)^{2}+2\sum_{1\leq s<t\leq{n_{b}}}\left(\frac{z_{I_{s}}-p_{I_{s}}}{q_{s}(I_{s})}\right)\left(\frac{z_{I_{t}}-p_{I_{t}}}{q_{t}(I_{t})}\right)\right].

Now, for any
s<t
, we deduce the following, because
\frac{z_{I_{s}}-p_{I_{s}}}{q_{s}(I_{s})}
and
q_{t}(\cdot)
are
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
-measurable:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{z_{I_{s}}-p_{I_{s}}}{q_{s}(I_{s})}\right)\left(\frac{z_{I_{t}}-p_{I_{t}}}{q_{t}(I_{t})}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{z_{I_{s}}-p_{I_{s}}}{q_{s}(I_{s})}\right)\left(\frac{z_{I_{t}}-p_{I_{t}}}{q_{t}(I_{t})}\right)\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{z_{I_{s}}-p_{I_{s}}}{q_{s}(I_{s})}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{z_{I_{t}}-p_{I_{t}}}{q_{t}(I_{t})}\right)\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{z_{I_{s}}-p_{I_{s}}}{q_{s}(I_{s})}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{z_{I_{t}}-p_{I_{t}}}{q_{t}(I_{t})}\right)\mid I_{t}=j,\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]P(I_{t}=j\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{z_{I_{s}}-p_{I_{s}}}{q_{s}(I_{s})}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{z_{j}-p_{j}}{q_{t}(j)}\right)\mid I_{t}=j,\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]q_{t}(j)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{z_{I_{s}}-p_{I_{s}}}{q_{s}(I_{s})}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\mathbb{E}\left[z_{j}-p_{j}\mid I_{t}=j,\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]\right]=0,

because for all
j
,
\mathbb{E}\left[z_{j}-p_{j}\mid I_{t}=j,\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[z_{j}-p_{j}\right]=0
by our oracle setup assumptions. Thus, by linearity, conditional independences, and variance of the Bernoulli, we have the following, noting that
q_{t}(\cdot)
is
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}
-measurable:

\displaystyle\mbox{Var}(M_{n_{b}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{z_{I_{t}}-p_{I_{t}}}{q_{t}(I_{t})}\right)^{2}\right]=\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{z_{I_{t}}-p_{I_{t}}}{q_{t}(I_{t})}\right)^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{z_{I_{t}}-p_{I_{t}}}{q_{t}(I_{t})}\right)^{2}\mid I_{t}=j,\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]P(I_{t}=j\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1})\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{z_{j}-p_{j}}{q_{s}(j)}\right)^{2}\mid I_{t}=j,\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]q_{t}(j)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\left(z_{j}-p_{j}\right)^{2}\mid I_{t}=j,\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]}{q_{t}(j)}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\frac{p_{j}(1-p_{j})}{q_{t}(j)}\right].

At this point, note that we are minimizing a sum over
t
of quantities that are nonnegative, as the
p_{j}
are nonnegative and the
q_{t}(\cdot)
are strictly positive by definition. From our final expression, there is also no temporal dependency between
q_{s}(\cdot)
and
q_{t}(\cdot)
for
s<t
. Finally, once provided with our choice of candidate
q_{t}(\cdot)
, the quantities inside the expectations are now deterministic. Combined, these three points tell us that (a) minimizing
\mbox{Var}(M_{n_{b}})
entails minimizing the instantaneous variance contributions
\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}p_{j}(1-p_{j})/q_{t}(j)
at each time step
s
; and (b) this instantaneous variance contribution minimization is the same across all timesteps. Specifically, at each time step
t
, we want to solve the following minimization problem, which is strictly convex because
p_{j}(1-p_{j})
are all nonnegative, and the
q_{t}(j)
are all strictly positive:

\displaystyle\min_{q_{t}(\cdot)}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\frac{p_{j}(1-p_{j})}{q_{t}(j)}\quad\text{s.t.}\quad q_{t}(j)>0\;\forall j,\;\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}q_{t}(j)=1.

Treating
q_{t}(\cdot)
as a vector and setting up the Lagrangian, we have

\displaystyle L(q_{t}(\cdot),\lambda)=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\frac{p_{j}(1-p_{j})}{q_{t}(j)}+\lambda\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}q_{t}(j)-1\right).

Partial-differentiating to obtain first-order conditions, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial L(q_{t}(\cdot),\lambda)}{\partial q_{t}(j)}=-\frac{p_{j}(1-p_{j})}{q_{t}(j)^{2}}+\lambda=0\implies{q_{t}^{*}(j)}^{2}=\frac{p_{j}(1-p_{j})}{\lambda}\implies q_{t}^{*}(j)\propto\sqrt{p_{j}(1-p_{j})},

exactly as desired, and emphasizing that the oracle-optimal
q_{t}^{*}(j)
are not time-varying. Because of the strict convexity of our minimization problem, such a solution is the unique minimizer.

A.5
Derivation of (Approximate) Maximal Variance-Reducing Index

Under the idealized assumptions of Section
3.4
, suppose at time
t
that

\displaystyle u\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\sim\mathcal{N}\left(\hat{u}^{(t-1)},\hat{\Sigma}^{(t-1)}\right),

and recall that

\displaystyle\theta^{*}:=g(u)=\frac{1}{N_{q}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\sigma\left(u^{\top}v_{j}\right).

Under our Laplace approximation update derivation setup in Appendix
A.2
, we know that with
\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)}:=\sigma\left(\hat{u}^{(t-1)}{}^{\top}v_{j}\right)
,

\displaystyle u\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1},z_{j}\overset{\cdot}{\sim}\mathcal{N}\bigg(\hat{u}^{(t-1)}+\left(\hat{\Sigma}^{(t-1)}-\frac{\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)}(1-\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)})\hat{\Sigma}^{(t-1)}v_{j}v_{j}^{\top}\hat{\Sigma}^{(t-1)}}{1+\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)}(1-\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)})v_{j}^{\top}\hat{\Sigma}^{(t-1)}v_{j}}\right)\left(z_{j}-\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)}\right)v_{j},

(18)

\displaystyle\hat{\Sigma}^{(t-1)}-\frac{\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)}(1-\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)})\hat{\Sigma}^{(t-1)}v_{j}v_{j}^{\top}\hat{\Sigma}^{(t-1)}}{1+\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)}(1-\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)})v_{j}^{\top}\hat{\Sigma}^{(t-1)}v_{j}}\bigg).

(19)

Define
\hat{w}^{(t-1)}_{j}:=\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)}\left(1-\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)}\right)=\sigma\left(\hat{u}^{(t-1)}{}^{\top}v_{j}\right)\left(1-\sigma\left(\hat{u}^{(t-1)}{}^{\top}v_{j}\right)\right)
. Then,

\displaystyle\mbox{Var}\left(u\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1},z_{j}\right)=\hat{\Sigma}^{(t-1)}-\frac{\hat{w}^{(t-1)}_{j}\hat{\Sigma}^{(t-1)}v_{j}v_{j}^{\top}\hat{\Sigma}^{(t-1)}}{1+\hat{w}^{(t-1)}_{j}v_{j}^{\top}\hat{\Sigma}^{(t-1)}v_{j}}.

Now, differentiating and recalling that
\sigma^{\prime}(z)=\sigma(z)\left(1-\sigma(z)\right)
, we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{u}g(u)=\frac{1}{N_{q}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\sigma\left(u^{\top}v_{j}\right)\left(1-\sigma\left(u^{\top}v_{j}\right)\right)v_{j}.

(20)

By the normal approximation associated with the multivariate Delta method, it follows that

\displaystyle\mbox{Var}\left(\theta^{*}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1},z_{j}\right)\approx\left(\nabla_{u}g(u)\mid_{u=\hat{u}^{(t-1)}}\right)^{\top}\mbox{Var}\left(u\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1},z_{j}\right)\left(\nabla_{u}g(u)\mid_{u=\hat{u}^{(t-1)}}\right).

Plugging in
\hat{u}^{(t-1)}
instead of using the mean parameter update in Equation
18
because we do not have access to
z_{j}
yet (as we have not queried our next point yet), by a similar argument as above we have

\displaystyle\mbox{Var}\left(\theta^{*}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right)\approx\left(\nabla_{u}g(u)\mid_{u=\hat{u}^{(t-1)}}\right)^{\top}\mbox{Var}\left(u\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right)\left(\nabla_{u}g(u)\mid_{u=\hat{u}^{(t-1)}}\right).

Subtracting, we obtain the following approximate variance reduction after substituting
\hat{u}^{(t-1)}
into Equation
20
:

\displaystyle\mbox{Var}\left(\theta^{*}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right)-\mbox{Var}\left(\theta^{*}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1},z_{j}\right)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{N_{q}^{2}}\left(\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{N_{q}}\hat{w}^{(t-1)}_{j^{\prime}}v_{j^{\prime}}\right)^{\top}\left(\frac{\hat{w}^{(t-1)}_{j}\hat{\Sigma}^{(t-1)}v_{j}v_{j}^{\top}\hat{\Sigma}^{(t-1)}}{1+\hat{w}^{(t-1)}_{j}v_{j}^{\top}\hat{\Sigma}^{(t-1)}v_{j}}\right)\left(\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{N_{q}}\hat{w}^{(t-1)}_{j^{\prime}}v_{j^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{q}^{2}}\frac{\hat{w}^{(t-1)}_{j}\left(v_{j}^{\top}\hat{\Sigma}^{(t-1)}\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{N_{q}}\hat{w}^{(t-1)}_{j^{\prime}}v_{j^{\prime}}\right)^{2}}{1+\hat{w}^{(t-1)}_{j}v_{j}^{\top}\hat{\Sigma}^{(t-1)}v_{j}}.

Thus, the index
I_{t}
that, upon being queried, most reduces the (approximate) posterior variance of
\theta^{*}
is

\displaystyle I_{t}=\operatornamewithlimits{argmax}_{j}\frac{\hat{w}^{(t-1)}_{j}\left(v_{j}^{\top}\hat{\Sigma}^{(t-1)}\frac{1}{N_{q}}\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{N_{q}}\hat{w}^{(t-1)}_{j^{\prime}}v_{j^{\prime}}\right)^{2}}{1+\hat{w}^{(t-1)}_{j}v_{j}^{\top}\hat{\Sigma}^{(t-1)}v_{j}}.

Appendix B
Investigating Sampling With/Without Replacement

A natural question is whether we can sample questions
without replacement
, since re-querying the same question appears wasteful. An instinctive ad-hoc implementation is to “zero out” the sampling probabilities of previously-queried questions and renormalize before drawing the next index.

However, making this change while keeping the same PAI estimator and variance estimate is not theoretically supported and can be empirically harmful at moderate-to-large budgets. Recall that Theorem
3.1
was proven via a martingale CLT and that our proof treated
\Delta_{t}:=\phi_{t}-\theta
, with

\displaystyle\phi_{t}=\frac{1}{N_{q}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{q}}\hat{p}_{j}^{(t-1)}+\frac{1}{N_{q}}\frac{z_{I_{t}}-\hat{p}_{I_{t}}^{(t-1)}}{q_{t}(I_{t})},

as martingale differences/increments with respect to the filtration
\mathcal{F}_{t}
. With with-replacement sampling, at each round we draw our next question
I_{t}\sim q_{t}(\cdot)
(c.f. our hybrid sampling policy in Eq.
10
) over the full question bank, and the inverse-probability correction in
\phi_{t}
uses
q_{t}(I_{t})
. This alignment is what enables our martingale structure and makes Assumptions
A.1
-
A.2
plausible: as our model-factor estimate
(\hat{u}^{(t)},\hat{\Sigma}^{(t)})
stabilizes as we observe more question labels, the predicted probabilities
\hat{p}^{(t)}
and, by extension, the sampling policy
q_{t}(\cdot)
, also stabilize. This is important because now it is reasonable that the average conditional variance
n_{b}^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{n_{b}}\mbox{Var}(\Delta_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1})
converges to a finite limit
v^{2}
—the crux of Assumption
A.1
.

Empirically, Figure

For these reasons, we recommend sampling with replacement when using FAQ/PAI to preserve theoretically-guaranteed uncertainty quantification, with minimal loss in point-estimation performance.

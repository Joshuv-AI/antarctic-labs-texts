Rate–Distortion Analysis of Compressed Query Delegation with Low-Rank Riemannian Updates
Faruk Alpay, Buğra Kılıçtaş

Abstract
. Bounded-context agents fail when intermediate reasoning exceeds an effective working-memory budget. We study compressed query delegation (CQD) : (i) compress a high-dimensional latent reasoning state into a low-rank tensor query, (ii) delegate the minimal query to an external oracle, and (iii) update the latent state via Riemannian optimization on fixed-rank manifolds. We give a math-first formulation: CQD is a constrained stochastic program with a query-budget functional and an oracle modeled as a noisy operator. We connect CQD to classical rate–distortion and information bottleneck principles, showing that spectral hard-thresholding is optimal for a natural constrained quadratic distortion problem, and we derive convergence guarantees for Riemannian stochastic approximation under bounded oracle noise and smoothness assumptions. Empirically, we report (A) a 2,500-item bounded-context reasoning suite (BBH-derived tasks plus curated paradox instances) comparing CQD against chain-of-thought baselines under fixed compute and context; and (B) a human “cognitive mirror” benchmark ( N = 200 N=200 ) measuring epistemic gain and semantic drift across modern oracles.

4.
CQD as Constrained Rate–Distortion / Information Bottleneck

CQD compresses a latent state to a query under a budget, then uses an oracle to reduce error/uncertainty. This aligns with rate–distortion:

(1)

\min_{Q(\cdot)}\ \mathbb{E}\big[d(\mathcal{X},\widehat{\mathcal{X}})\big]\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{B}(Q)]\leq\tau,

where
d
is a distortion and
\widehat{\mathcal{X}}
is the representation used for delegation. A standard Lagrangian form is

(2)

\min_{Q(\cdot)}\ \mathbb{E}[d(\mathcal{X},\widehat{\mathcal{X}})]+\lambda\,\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{B}(Q)].

In IB, one minimizes
I(\mathcal{X};Q)
subject to preserving task-relevant information
I(Q;Y)

[
3
]
. CQD can be viewed as producing
Q
that preserves task-salient components (dominant spectral content) while minimizing communication cost.

In this work we specialize to a deterministic encoder based on spectral truncation (ASM), where
\mathcal{B}(Q)
is proportional to retained multilinear ranks (Section
5.5
).

5.
Low-Rank Tensor Model and Adaptive Spectral Masking

5.1.
Tucker/HOSVD representation

We parameterize
\mathcal{X}
by Tucker factors
[
10
,
9
]
:

(3)

\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{G}\times_{1}U^{(1)}\times_{2}U^{(2)}\times_{3}U^{(3)},

with
U^{(n)}\in\mathrm{St}(r_{n},I_{n})
and core
\mathcal{G}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{1}\times r_{2}\times r_{3}}
. This constrains
\mathcal{X}
to a fixed multilinear rank manifold (under standard regularity).

5.2.
ASM definition

Let
\mathbf{X}_{(n)}=U^{(n)}\Sigma^{(n)}V^{(n)\top}
be an SVD of the mode-
n
unfolding.

Definition 5.1

(Adaptive Spectral Masking (ASM))
.

Given
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, define

M^{(n)}=\diag\!\big(\mathbb{I}(\sigma_{i}^{(n)}\geq\epsilon\,\sigma_{1}^{(n)})\big),\qquad r_{n}=\tr(M^{(n)}).

Define the masked tensor (a multilinear projection)

(4)

\displaystyle\Psi_{\mathrm{ASM}}(\mathcal{X})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{X}\times_{1}\big(U^{(1)}M^{(1)}U^{(1)\top}\big)\times_{2}\big(U^{(2)}M^{(2)}U^{(2)\top}\big)\times_{3}\big(U^{(3)}M^{(3)}U^{(3)\top}\big).

5.3.
Optimality of spectral hard-thresholding under quadratic distortion

We formalize the key claim: under a constrained quadratic distortion objective, keeping the top singular directions is optimal (matrix case), and ASM inherits this optimality mode-wise.

Theorem 5.2

(Eckart–Young–Mirsky
[
4
,
5
]
)
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
and
A_{r}
be its truncated SVD at rank
r
. Then

(5)

A_{r}\in\argmin_{\rank(B)\leq r}\|A-B\|_{F},

and the minimizer is achieved by keeping the top
r
singular values/vectors.

Proposition 5.3

(Mode-wise quadratic optimality)
.

Fix mode
n
. Let
P
be an orthogonal projector of rank
r_{n}
acting on the row space of
\mathbf{X}_{(n)}
(i.e.,
P=UU^{\top}
with
U\in\mathrm{St}(r_{n},I_{n})
). Then

(6)

P^{\star}\in\argmin_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rank(P)=r_{n},\\
P^{2}=P=P^{\top}\end{subarray}}\ \|\mathbf{X}_{(n)}-P\mathbf{X}_{(n)}\|_{F}

is the projector onto the top-
r_{n}
left singular subspace of
\mathbf{X}_{(n)}
.

5.4.
Approximation bound for truncated HOSVD

Lemma 5.4

(Truncated HOSVD tail bound
[
9
,
10
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}_{\bm{r}}
be truncated HOSVD reconstruction with ranks
\bm{r}=(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})
. Then

(7)

\|\mathcal{X}-\mathcal{X}_{\bm{r}}\|_{F}^{2}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{3}\sum_{i>r_{n}}\big(\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{X}_{(n)})\big)^{2}.

5.5.
Query-budget scaling

If the query encodes only the masked core and small metadata, then a natural budget proxy is

(8)

\mathcal{B}(Q)\propto r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}.

Thus, for
r_{n}\ll I_{n}
, CQD can be orders-of-magnitude smaller than transmitting raw traces.

6.
Oracle Model, Learning Dynamics, and Riemannian Updates

6.1.
Oracle as noisy operator

Given a query
Q
, the oracle returns

(9)

R=\mathcal{O}(Q)=\bar{R}(Q)+\xi(Q),

where
\mathbb{E}[\xi(Q)\mid Q]=0
and
\mathbb{E}\|\xi(Q)\|^{2}\leq\sigma^{2}
.

6.2.
Learning objective

Let
f(\mathcal{X};R)
be a differentiable loss reflecting task performance after integrating response
R
. CQD solves

(10)

\min_{\mathcal{X}\in\mathcal{M}_{\bm{r}}}\ F(\mathcal{X})\quad\text{where}\quad F(\mathcal{X})=\mathbb{E}\big[f\big(\mathcal{X};\mathcal{O}(Q(\mathcal{X}))\big)\big],

subject to
\mathcal{B}(Q(\mathcal{X}))\leq\tau
. One may handle the constraint via a Lagrangian multiplier
\lambda\geq 0
:

(11)

\min_{\mathcal{X}\in\mathcal{M}_{\bm{r}}}\ \mathbb{E}\big[f(\mathcal{X};\mathcal{O}(Q(\mathcal{X})))\big]+\lambda\,\mathcal{B}(Q(\mathcal{X})).

6.3.
Riemannian update rule

We perform updates on
\mathcal{M}_{\bm{r}}
using a retraction-based step:

(12)

\mathcal{X}_{k+1}=\Retr_{\mathcal{X}_{k}}\!\left(-\eta_{k}\,\grad\tilde{f}_{k}(\mathcal{X}_{k})\right),

where
\tilde{f}_{k}
is the stochastic realization induced by oracle response at iteration
k
.

Assumption 6.1

(Smoothness and bounded variance)
.

F
is
L
-smooth on
\mathcal{M}_{\bm{r}}
and stochastic gradients satisfy

\mathbb{E}\|\grad\tilde{f}_{k}(\mathcal{X})-\grad F(\mathcal{X})\|^{2}\leq\sigma^{2}
.

Theorem 6.2

(Riemannian stochastic approximation convergence
[
16
,
14
,
17
]
)
.

Under Assumption
6.1
, and step sizes
\eta_{k}>0
satisfying
\sum_{k}\eta_{k}=\infty
and
\sum_{k}\eta_{k}^{2}<\infty
, the iterates of (
12
) satisfy

(13)

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\ \mathbb{E}\|\grad F(\mathcal{X}_{k})\|^{2}=0.

Moreover, if
F
has isolated critical sets and the retraction is locally well-defined, the iterates converge to the set of stationary points almost surely.

Full proofs are in Appendix
A
.

9.
Discussion

CQD can be read as a rate–distortion constrained communication scheme: the query must fit a budget, so optimal encoders preserve dominant spectral structure. Theoretical results (Proposition
5.3
, Lemma
5.4
, Theorem
6.2
) formalize why low-rank spectral masking plus Riemannian updates yields stable learning dynamics under oracle noise.

10.
Conclusion

We presented a math-first CQD framework grounded in rate–distortion and low-rank geometry. We proved spectral retention optimality for quadratic distortion and gave convergence guarantees for Riemannian stochastic approximation. Benchmarks show CQD yields improved bounded-context performance and strong drift–gain coupling.

A.1.
Proof of Proposition
5.3

Let
A=\mathbf{X}_{(n)}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{n}\times M}
where
M
is the product of the other dimensions. Let
P
be an orthogonal projector of rank
r
. We minimize
\|A-PA\|_{F}^{2}
over rank-
r
projectors
P
.

First,

(14)

\displaystyle\|A-PA\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=\tr\big((A-PA)^{\top}(A-PA)\big)

(15)

\displaystyle=\tr(A^{\top}A)-2\tr(A^{\top}PA)+\tr(A^{\top}P^{\top}PA)

(16)

\displaystyle=\tr(A^{\top}A)-2\tr(A^{\top}PA)+\tr(A^{\top}PA)

(17)

\displaystyle=\tr(A^{\top}A)-\tr(A^{\top}PA)

(18)

\displaystyle=\tr(A^{\top}A)-\tr(PAA^{\top}).

Thus minimizing
\|A-PA\|_{F}^{2}
is equivalent to maximizing
\tr(PAA^{\top})
.

Let
AA^{\top}=U\Lambda U^{\top}
be an eigendecomposition with eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{I_{n}}\geq 0
(note
\lambda_{i}=\sigma_{i}(A)^{2}
). Any rank-
r
orthogonal projector can be written as
P=VV^{\top}
with
V\in\mathrm{St}(r,I_{n})
. Then

\tr(PAA^{\top})=\tr\big(V^{\top}(AA^{\top})V\big)=\tr\big(V^{\top}U\Lambda U^{\top}V\big).

Let
\widetilde{V}=U^{\top}V\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{n}\times r}
; since
U
is orthogonal,
\widetilde{V}\in\mathrm{St}(r,I_{n})
. Then

(19)

\tr\big(\widetilde{V}^{\top}\Lambda\widetilde{V}\big)=\sum_{i=1}^{I_{n}}\lambda_{i}\,\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{r}\widetilde{v}_{ij}^{2}\Big)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i},

where we used
\sum_{j=1}^{r}\widetilde{v}_{ij}^{2}\leq 1
and the ordering of
\lambda_{i}
. Equality is achieved by choosing
\widetilde{V}
to select the first
r
standard basis vectors, i.e.,
V=U_{(:,1:r)}
. Therefore, the maximizer is the projector onto the top-
r
eigenspace of
AA^{\top}
, equivalently the top-
r
left singular subspace of
A
. By (
18
), this also minimizes
\|A-PA\|_{F}^{2}
. ∎

A.2.
Proof of Lemma
5.4

Let
P_{n}=U^{(n)}_{(:,1:r_{n})}U^{(n)\top}_{(:,1:r_{n})}
be the orthogonal projector onto the top-
r_{n}
left singular subspace of
\mathbf{X}_{(n)}
. Define the multilinear projection

(20)

\mathcal{X}_{\bm{r}}=\mathcal{X}\times_{1}P_{1}\times_{2}P_{2}\times_{3}P_{3}.

Write the residual as a telescoping decomposition:

(21)

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}-\mathcal{X}_{\bm{r}}

\displaystyle=\big(\mathcal{X}-\mathcal{X}\times_{1}P_{1}\big)+\big(\mathcal{X}\times_{1}P_{1}-\mathcal{X}\times_{1}P_{1}\times_{2}P_{2}\big)

(22)

\displaystyle\quad+\big(\mathcal{X}\times_{1}P_{1}\times_{2}P_{2}-\mathcal{X}\times_{1}P_{1}\times_{2}P_{2}\times_{3}P_{3}\big).

Using orthogonality of projections and Pythagorean-type inequalities for successive orthogonal projections,

(23)

\|\mathcal{X}-\mathcal{X}_{\bm{r}}\|_{F}^{2}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{3}\|\mathcal{X}-\mathcal{X}\times_{n}P_{n}\|_{F}^{2}.

For mode

,

(24)

\|\mathcal{X}-\mathcal{X}\times_{n}P_{n}\|_{F}=\|\mathbf{X}_{(n)}-P_{n}\mathbf{X}_{(n)}\|_{F}.

By Proposition
5.3
,

is the optimal rank-

(25)

\|\mathbf{X}_{(n)}-P_{n}\mathbf{X}_{(n)}\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{i>r_{n}}\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{X}_{(n)})^{2}.

Substituting into (
23
) yields (
7
). ∎

A.3.
Proof of Theorem
6.2

Let
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}_{\bm{r}}
and
F:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}
be
L
-smooth: for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
and tangent vectors
\eta\in\mathrm{T}_{x}\mathcal{M}
, the pullback along a retraction satisfies

(26)

F(\Retr_{x}(\eta))\leq F(x)+\langle\grad F(x),\eta\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|\eta\|^{2}.

Let
g_{k}=\grad\tilde{f}_{k}(\mathcal{X}_{k})
be the stochastic Riemannian gradient estimator at iteration
k
, so that
\mathbb{E}[g_{k}\mid\mathcal{X}_{k}]=\grad F(\mathcal{X}_{k})
and
\mathbb{E}\|g_{k}-\grad F(\mathcal{X}_{k})\|^{2}\leq\sigma^{2}
.

Set the update direction in (
26
) to
\eta=-\eta_{k}g_{k}
and
x=\mathcal{X}_{k}
:

(27)

F(\mathcal{X}_{k+1})\leq F(\mathcal{X}_{k})-\eta_{k}\langle\grad F(\mathcal{X}_{k}),g_{k}\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\eta_{k}^{2}\|g_{k}\|^{2}.

Take conditional expectation given
\mathcal{X}_{k}
.
Using
\mathbb{E}[g_{k}\mid\mathcal{X}_{k}]=\grad F(\mathcal{X}_{k})
,

(28)

\mathbb{E}\big[\langle\grad F(\mathcal{X}_{k}),g_{k}\rangle\mid\mathcal{X}_{k}\big]=\|\grad F(\mathcal{X}_{k})\|^{2}.

Moreover,

(29)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|g_{k}\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{X}_{k}]

\displaystyle=\|\grad F(\mathcal{X}_{k})\|^{2}+\mathbb{E}\|g_{k}-\grad F(\mathcal{X}_{k})\|^{2}

(30)

\displaystyle\leq\|\grad F(\mathcal{X}_{k})\|^{2}+\sigma^{2}.

Plugging (
28
) and (
30
) into (
27
) yields

(31)

\mathbb{E}[F(\mathcal{X}_{k+1})\mid\mathcal{X}_{k}]\leq F(\mathcal{X}_{k})-\Big(\eta_{k}-\frac{L}{2}\eta_{k}^{2}\Big)\|\grad F(\mathcal{X}_{k})\|^{2}+\frac{L}{2}\eta_{k}^{2}\sigma^{2}.

For all sufficiently large
k
,
\eta_{k}
is small and
\eta_{k}-\frac{L}{2}\eta_{k}^{2}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\eta_{k}
. Taking total expectation and summing from
k=0
to
T-1
gives

(32)

\mathbb{E}[F(\mathcal{X}_{T})]\leq F(\mathcal{X}_{0})-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\eta_{k}\,\mathbb{E}\|\grad F(\mathcal{X}_{k})\|^{2}+\frac{L}{2}\sigma^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\eta_{k}^{2}.

Assuming

is lower bounded and using
\sum_{k}\eta_{k}^{2}<\infty
, we obtain
\sum_{k}\eta_{k}\,\mathbb{E}\|\grad F(\mathcal{X}_{k})\|^{2}<\infty
. Because
\sum_{k}\eta_{k}=\infty
, it follows that
\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\|\grad F(\mathcal{X}_{k})\|^{2}=0
.

Almost sure convergence to the stationary set follows by applying the Robbins–Siegmund supermartingale theorem to (
31
) as in
[
16
]
, using local properties of the retraction. ∎

Appendix B
CQD learning procedure and query encoding

This appendix records the CQD learning process in a self-contained way, matching Algorithms
1
–
2
. The goal is to make explicit (i) what information is sent in the query, (ii) how that information respects the budget proxy (
8
), and (iii) how the oracle response is integrated into the next iterate.

B.1.
Query contents

Let
\widehat{\mathcal{X}}=\Psi_{\mathrm{ASM}}(\mathcal{X};\epsilon)
. The encoder
\mathrm{Enc}(\cdot)
maps
\widehat{\mathcal{X}}
to a compact query
Q
consisting of:

•

the retained multilinear ranks
(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})
;

•

the masked core tensor entries of
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
in the Tucker form (
3
) (serialized);

•

optional side information required by the task loss (e.g., task identifier, constraint flags, or a short natural-language instruction describing the oracle contract);

•

a checksum or seed so that oracle-side stochasticity can be controlled when needed.

The communication cost is dominated by the core size
r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}
; metadata costs are lower order.

B.2.
Response integration

We model the oracle response as
R=\bar{R}(Q)+\xi(Q)
, cf. (
9
). The integration map
\mathsf{Upd}
produces a stochastic gradient surrogate
\grad\tilde{f}(\mathcal{X};R)
used in (
12
). Concretely,
\mathsf{Upd}
extracts from
R
either (a) a completion of missing components of the compressed state, or (b) a corrective direction (e.g., a residual, constraint violation, or a low-dimensional edit) expressed in the same factorization coordinates. The update is then projected to the tangent space and retracted back to
\mathcal{M}_{\bm{r}}
.

B.3.
Budget control

Given a target budget
\tau
, one can control ranks by:

(1)

choosing
\epsilon
so that the induced ranks satisfy
r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}\leq\tau
;

(2)

or adapting
\epsilon_{k}
online by increasing it when
r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}>\tau
and decreasing it otherwise.

This realizes a primal feasibility mechanism compatible with the constrained objective (

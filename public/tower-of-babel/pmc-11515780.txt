Combined effect of anion gap and red cell distribution width on the risk of acute kidney injury after cardiac surgery
Authors: Bin Zheng, Shanshan Li, Yinglin Peng, Lixian Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThis study was to analyze the roles of red blood cell distribution width (RDW), anion gap (AG) levels and their combined effects on the risk of acute kidney injury (AKI) following cardiac surgery.MethodsThis cohort study extracted the data of 1951 participants aged ≥ 18 years with the assessment of AKI after cardiac surgery during ICU stay from MIMIC-IV database between 2008 and 2019. Receiver operator characteristic (ROC) curve was used to determine the optimal cut-off value AG and RDW. The associations between AG, RDW, and the combined effects of AG and effects were evaluated via univariable and multivariable Logistic models. Odds ratio (OR) with 95% confidence interval (CI) were imputed.ResultsAmong all the participants, 831 participants had AKI, and 1120 did not have AKI. ROC curves revealed that the optimum cut of points of AG and RDW were 12.75 mmol/L, and 13.65%, respectively. Increased risk of AKI was found in patients after cardiac surgery with AG > 12.75 mmol/L (OR = 1.44, 95%CI 1.15–1.80) or RDW > 13.65% group (OR = 1.23, 95%CI 1.01–1.50). In comparison to subjects with AG ≤ 12.75 mmol/L and RDW ≤ 13.65%, AG > 12.75 mmol/L and RDW ≤ 13.65% (OR = 1.42, 95%CI 1.07–1.89), and AG > 12.75 mmol/L and RDW > 13.65% (OR = 1.75, 95%CI 1.24–2.47) were associated with increased odds of AKI in patients after cardiac surgery.ConclusionsAG and RDW had combined effects on risk of AKI in patients after cardiac surgery, which might offer an insight for the management of patients after cardiac surgery.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13019-024-03100-3.

Background

Acute kidney injury (AKI) is one of the most serious perioperative complications for patients who received cardiac surgery [1]. AKI affected about 22.3% of cardiac surgery patients and is increasingly recognized as a source of poor patient outcomes after cardiac surgery [2]. AKI results in increased length of hospital stay and mortality, and the mortality rate can be as high as 50% in 2%-5% of cardiac surgery patients who develop severe cardiac surgery associated-AKI that requires renal replacement therapy [3, 4]. Therefore, the identification of patients at high risk of postoperative AKI is critical to the clinical management of these patients undergoing cardiac surgery.

Some possible pathophysiological mechanisms underlying the increased risk of postoperative AKI in patients undergoing cardiac surgery have been proposed, such as ischemia–reperfusion injury, inflammation, microembolism, decreased renal perfusion, and the use of nephrotoxic drugs [5, 6]. The prediction of AKI based on routine clinical indicators is a practical and easy method, and red blood cell distribution width (RDW) is a parameter reflecting the variability of red blood cell size in the complete blood count of routine clinical tests, which was initially used to distinguish different types of anemia and has attracted attention as an inflammatory marker in recent years [7]. Studies have found that higher levels of RDW were associated with a higher risk of AKI in coronary artery disease care units [8]. Acid–base imbalance, especially metabolic acidosis, was considered a common problem in ICU patients and was associated with morbidity and mortality [9]. The anion gap (AG) is a measure of the difference between undetermined anion and undetermined cation concentrations, which was used to detect and evaluate metabolic acidosis and mixed acid–base imbalance [10]. Increased AG levels were reported to be associated with elevated risk of AKI in patients with cardiogenic shock [11]. In clinic, multiple abnormalities are often present after surgery. However, no studies have explored whether the coexistence of multiple abnormal states poses additional risks to patients.

The purpose of this study was to analyze the effects of RDW, AG levels and their combined effects on the risk of postoperative AKI based on Medical Information Mart for Intensive Care-IV (MIMIC-IV) database. We also performed subgroup analysis in terms of age, types of surgery, and treatments.

Methods

Study design and population

This cohort study extracted the data of 5201 participants aged ≥ 18 years with the assessment of AKI after cardiac surgery during ICU stay from MIMIC-IV database between 2008 and 2019. The MIMIC-IV database offers unrestricted access to a comprehensive collection of over 50,000 admissions in the intensive care unit (ICU) at Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center in Boston, Massachusetts. The data spans from 2008 to 2019 [12]. The MIMIC-IV database encompasses a vast array of information, encompassing demographics, vital signs, test results, and diagnoses classified using codes from both the International Classification of Diseases, Ninth Revision (ICD-9) and Tenth Revision (ICD-10). In our study, patients aged ≥ 18 years who admitted to ICU after cardiac surgery, and with assessment of AKI after surgery during this ICU stay were included. Patients who underwent cardiac surgery were screened from MIMIC-IV by ICD codes [GABG (ICD-9: 3610–3619, and ICD-10: first four values were 0210–0213); valvular surgery (ICD-9: 3599, 3500, 3501, 3502, 3503, and 3504); repair of septal defect of heart (ICD-9: 3550–3554, 3560, 3570, 3571, and 3598); aortic aneurysm surgery (ICD-9: 3732); and others (ICD-9: 3710, 3539, 3542, 3733, and 3737)]. AKI was calculated every time a creatinine or urine output measurement occurs, and baseline creatinine was defined as the lowest creatinine in the past 7 days. Patients with length of ICU stay less than 24 h, who diagnosed with AKI or end-stage renal disease (ICD-9: 5856 or ICD-10: N186 or eGFR < 15 mL/min/1.73 m2) upon ICU admission, or those without data on key variables were excluded. The requirement of ethical approval for this was waived by the Institutional Review Board of The Second Affiliated Hospital, School of Medicine, South China University of Technology, because the data was accessed from MIMIC-IV database (a publicly available database). The need for written informed consent was waived by the Institutional Review Board of The Second Affiliated Hospital, School of Medicine, South China University of Technology due to retrospective nature of the study. All methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations.

Potential covariates

Age (years), gender (female or male), race (White, Black or other), heart rate (beat/min), systolic blood pressure (mmHg), diastolic blood pressure, central venous pressure (CVP) (mmHg), respiratory rate (beat/min), temperature (℃), oxygen saturation (SpO2) (%), partial pressure of carbon dioxide (PCO2) (mmHg), partial pressure of oxygen (PO2) (mmHg), PH, operation type [coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) or other], Simplified Acute Physiologic Score II (SAPSII), Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA), Glasgow coma scale (GCS), white blood cell (WBC) (K/uL), hematocrit (%), platelet (K/uL), hemoglobin (g/dL), glucose (mg/dL), International normalized ratio (INR), blood urea nitrogen (BUN) (mg/dL), lactate (mmol/L), calcium (mmol/L), estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) (mL/min/1.73 m2), urine output (mL), diuretic (yes or no), transfusion (yes or no), base excess, anemia (yes or no), other diseases of blood and blood-forming organs (yes or no), and intraoperative use of extracorporeal circulation auxiliary (yes or no) were potential covariates analyzed in this study. The variables were measured at the baseline of postoperative ICU admission, except IUECA, which was measured during surgery.

Main and outcome variables

AG and RDW were main variables. We used the baseline data of AG and RDW which were measured at the baseline of postoperative ICU admission.

The occurrence of AKI was the outcome, which was determined on the basis of Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes Clinical Practice Guidelines [13]. People with increase in SCr by ≥ 0.3 mg/dl (≥ 26.5 μmol/l) within 48 h; or increase in SCr to ≥ 1.5 times baseline, which is known or presumed to have occurred within the prior 7 days; or urine volume < 0.5 ml/kg/hour for 6 h were defined as AKI. Patients were followed up during ICU stay, and the end point of follow-up was the occurrence of AKI or discharge from the ICU.

Statistical analysis

The normality of quantitative data was tested by skewness and kurtosis methods, and the homogeneity of variance was tested by Levene’s test. Normal distribution measurement data were described as Mean and standard deviation [Mean (± SD)]. The t test was used for comparison between groups with equal variance, and t’ test was used for unequal variance. Non-normal data were described by median and quartiles [M (Q1, Q2)], and Wilcoxon rank sum test was used for comparison between groups. Enumeration data were described as the number and ratio of cases [n (%)], and chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test was used for comparison between groups. Missing values of variables were shown in Supplementary Table 1. Variables with missing values more than 20% were deleted, otherwise, multiple imputation were applied. Sensitivity analysis was applied for comparing data before and after missing value manipulation (Supplementary Table 2). Potential confounders were identified by backward screening using univariable and multivariable Logistic regression models. The interaction of AG and RDW on the risk of AKI was evaluated, and relative excess risk due to interaction (RERI), attributable proportion due to interaction (AP), and the synergy index (S) were calculated. The associations between AG, RDW, and the combined effects of AG and effects were evaluated via univariable and multivariable Logistic models. All the subjects were divided into AG ≤ 12.75 mmol/L and RDW ≤ 13.65%, AG ≤ 12.75 mmol/L and RDW > 13.65%, AG > 12.75 mmol/L and RDW ≤ 13.65%, and AG > 12.75 mmol/L and RDW > 13.65% (Fig. 1 and Fig. 2). Subgroup analysis was performed according to age (< 60 or ≥ 60), type of surgery, and treatments. Odds ratio (OR) with 95% confidence interval (CI) were imputed. Alpha = 0.05. Results

Comparisons of characteristics of participants with or without AKI

In total, 5201 participants aged ≥ 18 years with the assessment of AKI after cardiac surgery during ICU stay from MIMIC-IV database between 2008 and 2019 were identified. Patients with length of ICU stay less than 24 h (n = 210), participants who diagnosed with AKI upon ICU admission (n = 3036), subjects diagnosed with end-stage renal disease upon ICU admission (n = 3), and people without data on sodium (n = 1) were excluded. Finally, 1951 participants were included. Among them, 831 participants had AKI, and 1120 did not have AKI. Discussion

The current study evaluated the effects of RDW, AG levels, especially their combined effects on the risk of AKI in patients after cardiac surgery. We found that AG > 12.75 mmol/L or RDW > 13.65% was correlated with increased risk of AKI in patients after cardiac surgery. AG > 12.75 mmol/L and RDW ≤ 13.65%, as well as AG > 12.75 mmol/L and RDW > 13.65% were associated with increased risk of AKI in patients after cardiac surgery. The combined effect of AG and RDW on the risk of AKI in those receiving cardiac surgery might offer an insight for the management of patients after cardiac surgery.

Kidney is the primary organ responsible for electrolyte retention and excretion, as well as maintaining acid–base homeostasis in healthy individuals [15]. The presence of elevated AG levels is also significantly correlated with increased mortality following CABG [16]. In acute coronary syndrome patients after CABG, the serum AG level exhibited a J-shaped curve in the ICU, indicating its association with the risk of AKI [17]. A retrospective analysis of the MIMIC-IV database indicated that the serum AG had a predictive value for 28-day in-hospital all-cause mortality in sepsis patients with AKI [18]. Also, the serum AG level was a predictor for all-cause mortality in critically ill patients with AKI [19]. AG reflects the concentration of unmeasured anions and can be easily calculated using conventional clinical chemical analysis [20]. The elevation of serum AG is typically attributed to the excessive production of organic acid anions and/or impaired excretion of anions [21] or increased serum lactate and ketoanions [22]. Metabolic acidosis is not only a consequence of the progression of renal dysfunction, but also associated with an increased risk of AKI due to its vasoconstrictive effect on renal blood flow and promotion of inflammatory mediator releases [23]. These findings supported the results of our study, which demonstrated that AG > 12.75 mmol/L was correlated with increased risk of AKI in patients after cardiac surgery.

This study also found that RDW > 13.65% was associated with elevated risk of AKI in patients after cardiac surgery. The findings might be allied with the results from previous studies. High level of RDW was a valuable biomarker for all-cause death in critical diabetic patients with AKI [24]. RDW was previously identified to be an independent predictor of AKI in patients in the Coronary Care Unit [8]. A retrospective analysis from MIMIC-IV database using propensity score matching demonstrated that RDW improved the prediction of 28-day mortality for patients with sepsis-induced AKI [25]. RDW predicted long-term mortality in critically ill patients with acute kidney injury: a retrospective database study [26]. RDW was also reported to be associated with ischemia–reperfusion injury [27]. The mechanisms might be associated with inflammation [28] and oxidative stress [29]. Inflammation inhibited iron metabolism and bone marrow function, as well as the proliferation and maturation of erythrocytes, resulting in an elevation of RDW values. In addition, AKI was a result of systematic and intrarenal inflammation [30]. In this study, we also observed the combined effect of AG and RDW on the risk of AKI in patients. These findings suggested that in patients following cardiac surgery, monitoring of AG as well as RDW was necessary.

The different types of cardiac surgery were associated with the risk of AKI [31], and in our study, AG > 12.75 mmol/L and RDW ≤ 13.65% as well as AG > 12.75 mmol/L and RDW > 13.65% were related to increased risk of AKI in patients after cardiac surgery who receiving CABG. In patients receiving other operation rather than CABG, AG ≤ 12.75 mmol/L and RDW > 13.65% as well as AG > 12.75 mmol/L and RDW > 13.65% were correlated with increased risk of AKI in patients after cardiac surgery. and development of acute kidney disease with or without AKI is associated with a higher risk of major adverse kidney events and hospitalization after acute aortic dissection surgery [32].

This study was the first to investigate the combined effect of AG and RDW levels on the risk of AKI after cardiac surgery. AG and RDW were easily detected, which might be quickly obtained. For patients receiving cardiac surgery, routinely assessment of AG and RDW might be essential. For those with high AG as well as high RDW, special interventions might be provided. We also found some limitations in our study. Firstly, this study was a retrospective cohort study, there might be some selection bias. Secondly, some clinical data, and cardiac markers associated with the risk of AKI, such as comorbidities [33], and a novel PPARδ modulator (ASP1128) [34], albumin [35], neutrophil to lymphocyte ratio [36] and/or prognostic nutritional index [37] were included or with high missing values in the MIMIC-IV database, which could not be analyzed. Thirdly, the sample sizes concerning mortality and patients with dialysis-requiring AKI were small, which could not be analyzed in the present study. The results in this study still need validation in more future studies.

Conclusions

The effects of RDW, AG levels, as well as their combined effects on the risk of AKI in patients after cardiac surgery were evaluated in the present study. The results depicted that higher AG and RDW levels were associated with increased risk of AKI in patients after cardiac surgery. AG and RDW had combined effects on risk of AKI in patients after cardiac surgery, which might offer an insight for the management of patients after cardiac surgery.

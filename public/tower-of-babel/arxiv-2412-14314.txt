Derived equivalence for the simple flop of type G 2 † G_{2}^{\dagger} via tilting bundles
Wahei Hara

Abstract
. The aim of this article is to prove the derived equivalence for a local model of the simple flop of type G 2 † G_{2}^{\dagger} , which was found by Kanemitsu [ 9 ] . This flop is the only known simple flop that comes from a non-homogeneous roof. The proof of the derived equivalence is done by using tilting bundles, and also produces a noncommutative crepant resolution of the singularity that is derived equivalent to both sides of the flop.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
14F08, 14E05, 14E16.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

The study of the derived categories of coherent sheaves over algebraic varieties is one of recent central topics in algebraic geometry,
and it is also related to mathematical physics, representation theory, and many other fields of mathematics.
In the context of algebraic geometry, the relation between derived categories and birational geometry is actively studied,
and many of those works are motivated by the following conjecture,
which suggests that derived categories and birational geometry would be parallel in some sense.

Conjecture 1.1

(DK conjecture
[
2
,
12
]
)
.

Assume that two smooth varieties
X_{+}
and
X_{-}
are K-equivalent, i.e. there exist another smooth variety
\widetilde{X}
and two projective birational morphisms
\phi_{\pm}\colon\widetilde{X}\to X_{\pm}
such that
\phi_{+}^{*}K_{X_{+}}\sim\phi_{-}^{*}K_{X_{-}}
, where
K_{X_{\pm}}
are the canonical divisors.
Then
X_{+}
and
X_{-}
are D-equivalent, i.e. there exists an exact equivalence
\Db(\coh X_{+})\simeq\Db(\coh X_{-})
of triangulated categories.

A flop between two smooth varieties gives an example of a K-equivalent pair.
The DK conjecture is still widely open, even for flops.
It is known by
[
1
]
that, the most standard example of a flop called an Atiyah flop satisfies the DK conjecture.

Generalising the construction of the Atiyah flops,
Kanemitsu
[
9
]
found a large list of flops called simples flops, and labeled them using the Dynkin data.
The aim of this article is to prove the DK conjecture for an exceptional example of a simple flop, called the simple flop of type
G_{2}^{\dagger}
.

1.2.
The simple flop of type
G_{2}^{\dagger}
and the main result

This section explains the geometry of the simple flop of type
G_{2}^{\dagger}
and then states the main result.

Let
H_{+}=\mathbb{Q}^{5}
be the five dimensional quadric.
It admits the (dual) spinor bundle
\mathcal{S}^{\vee}
of rank
4
.
It is known by Ottaviani
[
15
]
that a general section
\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{S}^{\vee}
is nowhere vanishing,
and hence it has a rank three quotient bundle
\mathcal{G}
that lies in an exact sequence
0\to\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{S}^{\vee}\to\mathcal{G}\to 0
.
The bundle
\mathcal{G}
is a stable bundle with Chern classes
(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})=(2,2,2)
.
It is also known that all stable rank three bundles over
\mathbb{Q}^{5}
with these Chern classes are obtained in the way above.
These rank three bundles are sometimes called
Ottaviani bundles
over
\mathbb{Q}^{5}
.
Ottaviani bundles form a non-trivial moduli, but the group
\Aut(\mathbb{Q}^{5})
acts on this moduli space transitively
[
15
,
16
]
.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{+}
be an Ottaviani bundle over
H_{+}
, and consider the total space

X_{+}:=\Tot_{H_{+}}(\mathcal{G}_{+}^{\vee}(-1))=\mathbb{V}_{H_{+}}(\mathcal{G}_{+}(1))=\Spec_{H_{+}}\Sym^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G}_{+}(1)).

The above fact about the moduli space says, as an abstract variety,
X_{+}
does not depend on the choice of Ottaviani bundles.
Let
H_{+}^{0}\subset X_{+}
be the zero section,

\phi_{+}\colon\widetilde{X}:=\Bl_{H_{+}^{0}}X_{+}\to X_{+}
the blowing-up of
X_{+}
along
H_{+}^{0}
,
and
E\subset\widetilde{X}
the exceptional divisor.
By construction,
E\simeq\mathbb{P}_{H_{+}}(\mathcal{G}_{+}(1)):=\Proj\Sym^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G}_{+}(1))
.
It is known that
E
admits another
\mathbb{P}^{2}
-bundle structure
E\to H_{-}
, and the base
H_{-}
is also isomorphic to
\mathbb{Q}^{5}

(see Section
2.1
).
Under this,
E
is identified with the projectivization
\mathbb{P}_{H_{-}}(\mathcal{G}_{-}(1))
of an Ottaviani bundle over
H_{-}\simeq\mathbb{Q}^{5}
.
This geometry also yields another smooth blow-down
\phi_{-}\colon\widetilde{X}\simeq\Bl_{H_{-}^{0}}X_{-}\to X_{-}
with the same exceptional divisor
E
, where

X_{-}=\Tot_{H_{-}}(\mathcal{G}_{-}^{\vee}(-1))
and
H_{-}^{0}\subset X_{-}
is the zero-section.
Put
R:=H^{0}(X_{+},\mathcal{O}_{X_{+}})\simeq H^{0}(X_{-},\mathcal{O}_{X_{-}})
.
Then two natural morphisms
f_{\pm}\colon X_{\pm}\to\Spec R
are flopping contractions,
and the birational map
X_{+}\dashrightarrow X_{-}
is the associated flop.
This is an example of
a simple flop
, i.e. a flop connected by one smooth blow-up and one smooth blow-down.
The following is the main theorem of this article.

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists an exact equivalence of
R
-linear triangulated categories

\Phi\colon\Db(\coh X_{+})\xrightarrow{\sim}\Db(\coh X_{-})

such that
R{f_{+}}_{*}\simeq R{f_{-}}_{*}\circ\Phi
.

Remark 1.3
.

Since
X_{+}
and
X_{-}
are isomorphic to each other as abstract varieties,
the existence of a
\mathbb{C}
-linear equivalence
\Db(\coh X_{+})\simeq\Db(\coh X_{-})
is obvious.
Thus the question in this case is the existence of an equivalence that satisfies the compatibility with the flop,
i.e. the
R
-linear property and the commutativity
R{f_{+}}_{*}\simeq R{f_{-}}_{*}\circ\Phi
.

The classification of simple flops is related to the classification of
roofs
, i.e. Fano manifolds of Picard rank two and index
n+1
, admitting two different
\mathbb{P}^{n}
-bundle structures, for some
n
.
A roof appears in the geometry of a simple flop as the exceptional divisor of the blow-ups.
Roofs are partially classified in
[
9
]
, and most of them are rational homogeneous manifolds of Picard rank two with two projections to those of Picard rank one.
The roof
E
(with two projections
E\to H_{\pm}
) is the only known example of a roof such that
E
is not a rational homogeneous manifold.
The roof
E
and the associated flop are called of type
G_{2}^{\dagger}
in
[
9
]
.
In this terminology, the flop
X_{+}\dashrightarrow X_{-}
this article studies is a local model of the
simple flop of type
G_{2}^{\dagger}
.
The non-homogeneous property makes the simple flop of this type the most remarkable among all known simple flops.

1.3.
The method using tilting bundles

The proof of the main theorem is done by using
tilting bundles
(see Definition
3.2
).
Some previous works
[
6
,
7
,
18
]
also prove the derived equivalence for local models of the Atiyah flops, the Mukai flops, and the simple flops of type
C_{2}
and
G_{2}
by constructing tilting bundles.

Recall that, if a tilting bundle
\mathcal{T}
exists over a variety
Z
that is projective over an affine variety,
then it yields an exact equivalence of triangulated categories

\RHom_{Z}(\mathcal{T},-)\colon\Db(\coh Z)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Db(\modu A),

where
A:=\End_{Z}(\mathcal{T})
and
\modu A
is the category of finitely generated right modules over
A

(cf.
[
19
, Lemma 2.3]
).
Thus, in order to prove the derived equivalence
\Db(\coh X_{+})\simeq\Db(\coh X_{-})
,
it is enough to show that there exist tilting bundles
\mathcal{T}_{\pm}
over
X_{\pm}
such that
\Lambda:=\End_{X_{+}}(\mathcal{T}_{+})\simeq\End_{X_{-}}(\mathcal{T}_{-})
as
R
-algebras.

Sections
3.2
and
3.3
give a construction of such tilting bundles, which can be summarized as follows.
The tilting bundles constructed in this article are closely related to Kapranov’s tilting bundle over
\mathbb{Q}^{5}

[
10
]
; however, the pull-back of Kapranov’s bundle to
X_{\pm}
does not yield a tilting bundle.
More precisely, computations using the Borel–Bott–Weil theorem reveal that the bundle has non-vanishing
\Ext^{1}
, which violates the tilting property (Lemma
3.4
).
However, the only non-vanishing extension appears as the
\Ext^{1}
between two indecomposable summands, and thus taking the corresponding extension produces tilting bundles (Theorem
3.5
).
Later, it turns out that the constructed tilting bundle admits a natural description from the geometry of rational homogeneous manifolds of Dynkin type
D_{4}
(Proposition
3.7
).
In fact, this final step is the most crucial ingredient of this article,
and allows us to compare the endomorphism rings
\End_{X_{\pm}}(\mathcal{T}_{\pm})
on both sides.

1.4.
Further remarks and discussions

1.4.1.
The associated K3 surfaces

Let
s_{+}
be a regular section of the twisted Ottaviani bundle
\mathcal{G}_{+}(1)
over
H_{+}\simeq\mathbb{Q}^{5}
.
Then its zero-locus
S_{+}\subset H_{+}
is a K3 surface of degree
12
.
Under the flop
X_{+}\dashrightarrow X_{-}
, there exists a corresponding section

s_{-}\in H^{0}(H_{-},\mathcal{G}_{-}(1))
whose zero-locus
S_{-}\subset H_{-}
is also a K3 surface of degree
12
(cf.
[
20
, Section 5]
).
If one chooses
s_{+}
general enough,
S_{+}
and
S_{-}
give a non-isomorphic pair of K3 surfaces (
[
11
, Corollary 4.10]
).
The derived equivalence in Theorem
1.2
also produces a derived equivalence
\Db(\coh S_{+})\simeq\Db(\coh S_{-})
(cf.
[
20
, Section 5]
), which gives a new proof for the D-equivalence of
S_{\pm}
.
More detailed study of those K3 surfaces from the points of view of Hodge theory and motive theory
was already done in
[
11
]
.
The result in this article adds a new aspect from the flop to the study of those K3 surfaces.

1.4.2.
The generalised McKay correspondence

The proof of the derived equivalence using tilting bundles fits into the picture of the generalised McKay correspondence.
Indeed, if tilting bundles
\mathcal{T}_{\pm}
as in Section
1.3
exist,
the noncommutative
R
-algebra
\Lambda=\End_{X_{+}}(\mathcal{T}_{+})=\End_{X_{-}}(\mathcal{T}_{-})
is a
noncommutative crepant resolution (NCCR)
of
R
(cf.
[
21
,
22
]
).

It is widely expected that, if an affine scheme
\Spec S
admits both (projective) crepant resolutions and an NCCR
A
,
any (projective) crepant resolution
X
of
S
can be realised as the King’s moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\theta}(\mathbf{v})
of
\theta
-stable finite length right
A
-modules of a fixed dimension vector
\mathbf{v}
for some stability condition
\theta
.
This kind of expectation is called the Craw–Ishii type problem (cf.
[
23
, Conjecture 1.7]
).

If the expectation actually holds for our
R
and an NCCR
A
of
R
,
then the universal bundles
\mathcal{U}_{\pm}
over
X_{\pm}
should be tilting bundles with
\End_{X_{\pm}}(\mathcal{U}_{\pm})\simeq A
,
and hence
\mathcal{U}_{\pm}
give a pair of tilting bundles with the desired property.

In order to establish the Craw–Ishii type problem for the NCCR
\Lambda
of
R
that is constructed in this article, one needs more detailed information about the algebra
\Lambda
, like an explicit description as the path algebra of a quiver with relations.
However, a question of that kind is not easy in general, and we leave it as a future work.

1.4.3.
Possible alternative proofs for the derived equivalence

The previous works
[
13
,
17
,
20
]
proved the derived equivalence for some simple flops (the Mukai flops and simple flops of types
A^{G}_{4}
,
C_{2}
,
D_{5}
and
G_{2}
) using
mutations of SODs
of
\Db(\coh\widetilde{X})
.
The work
[
25
]
, which was announced around the same time as this article,
applies the strategy using mutation to the simple flop of type
G_{2}^{\dagger}
(and
D_{4}
) to give an alternative proof of the derived equivalence.
On one hand,
the resulting derived equivalence
\Psi\colon\Db(\coh X_{+})\xrightarrow{\sim}\Db(\coh X_{-})
from the mutation method is very complicated in general.
For example, it is unclear if one can modify the equivalence
\Psi
so that
R{f_{+}}_{*}\simeq R{f_{-}}_{*}\circ\Psi
holds.
On the other hand, the advantage of the mutation method is that it works in any models of the flop and in the relative settings.

Other possible methods to prove the derived equivalences are the method finding an explicit Fourier–Mukai kernel and the method using the variation of GIT.
For the first method, the structure sheaf of the fiber product
X_{+}\times_{\Spec R}X_{-}
is a candidate of the Fourier–Mukai kernel, and it actually gives an equivalence if a simple flop is an Atiyah flop, a Mukai flop, or of type
C_{2}

[
1
,
8
,
12
,
14
]
.
The method using the variation of GIT is also expected to work for all simple flops,
but at the time of writing, this strategy is known to work only for the Atiyah flops and the Mukai flops
[
3
,
5
]
.
One problem is that, for most of simple flops including of type
G_{2}^{\dagger}
, a nice GIT picture has not been found yet.

2.
The geometry of the roof

The present section explains the geometry of roofs,
and constructs a key vector bundle that will be used in the proof of the main theorem.

\OGr(4,8)

\mathbb{Q}^{6}

2.2.
The key bundle and the exchange diagram over
\OGr(3,8)
and
W

The aim of this section is to produce a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
over
W
, which plays an important role during the proof of the main theorem.
Put
Z=\OGr(3,8)
and
\mathcal{O}_{Z}(a,b):=p_{+}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\OGr_{+}(4,8)}(a)\otimes p_{-}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\OGr_{-}(4,8)}(b)
.
Let
\mathcal{V}
be the rank three universal subbundle over
Z
.
Then there are two (dual of the) tautological sequences

0\to\mathcal{O}_{Z}(1,-1)\to p_{+}^{*}\mathcal{S}_{+}^{\vee}\to\mathcal{V}^{\vee}\to 0
and

0\to\mathcal{O}_{Z}(-1,1)\to p_{-}^{*}\mathcal{S}_{-}^{\vee}\to\mathcal{V}^{\vee}\to 0
.

Taking the fiber product of two bundle
p_{+}^{*}\mathcal{S}_{+}^{\vee}
and
p_{-}^{*}\mathcal{S}_{-}^{\vee}
over
\mathcal{V}^{\vee}
gives a rank five vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
Z
and a commutative diagram

.
Note that the restriction of

to

is the pull-back of the dual of the spinor bundle over

by the projection
\pi_{\pm}\colon W\to H_{\pm}

H_{\pm}\simeq\mathbb{Q}^{5}
is also denoted by
\mathcal{S}_{\pm}
, and the restriction of
\mathcal{E}
to

is also denoted by
\mathcal{E}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Two exact sequences

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{O}_{W}(-1,1)\to\mathcal{E}\to\pi_{+}^{*}\mathcal{S}_{+}^{\vee}\to 0,~\text{and}

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{O}_{W}(1,-1)\to\mathcal{E}\to\pi_{-}^{*}\mathcal{S}_{-}^{\vee}\to 0

over
W
do not split.

Proof.

First, observe that

\displaystyle\Hom_{W}(\mathcal{O}_{W}(1,-1),\mathcal{O}_{W}(-1,1))

\displaystyle\simeq H^{0}(W,\mathcal{O}_{W}(-2,2))

\displaystyle{}\simeq{}H^{0}(H_{-},\mathcal{O}_{H_{-}}(2)\otimes(\pi_{-})_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\pi_{-}}(-2))

\displaystyle{}={}0.

Thus, the natural homomorphism
\Ext_{W}^{1}(\mathcal{V}^{\vee},\mathcal{O}_{W}(-1,1))\to\Ext_{W}^{1}(\pi_{+}^{*}\mathcal{S}^{\vee},\mathcal{O}_{W}(-1,1))
,
which sends the class
[0\to O_{W}(-1,1)\to\pi_{-}^{*}\mathcal{S}_{-}^{\vee}\to\mathcal{V}^{\vee}|_{W}\to 0]

to the class
[0\to O_{W}(-1,1)\to\mathcal{E}\to\pi_{+}^{*}\mathcal{S}_{+}^{\vee}\to 0]
,
is injective.
Since the dual spinor bundle
\mathcal{S}_{-}^{\vee}
is indecomposable, the exact sequence
0\to O_{W}(-1,1)\to\pi_{-}^{*}\mathcal{S}_{-}^{\vee}\to\mathcal{V}^{\vee}|_{W}\to 0
does not split, and hence the corresponding class in
\Ext_{W}^{1}(\pi_{+}^{*}\mathcal{S}^{\vee},\mathcal{O}_{W}(-1,1))
is non-zero.
Thus the extension
0\to\mathcal{O}_{W}(-1,1)\to\mathcal{E}\to\pi_{+}^{*}\mathcal{S}_{+}^{\vee}\to 0
does not split.

Swapping the roles of
\mathcal{S}_{-}
and
\mathcal{S}_{+}
in the above argument proves that the second sequence does not split, either.
∎

The above two exact sequences and the diagram will be used to exchange two tilting bundles over both sides of the flop in Section
3.3
.
The diagram of the exact sequences over
W
will be referred as the
exchange diagram
.
The bundle
\mathcal{E}
over
W
will be referred as the
key bundle
.

3.
Proof of the derived equivalence

This section gives the proof of the main theorem.

3.1.
Borel–Bott–Weil computations

Let
\mathcal{S}
be the spinor bundle over
\mathbb{Q}^{5}
.
The five dimensional quadric
\mathbb{Q}^{5}
is isomorphic to a rational homogeneous manifold
\Spin(7)/P(\alpha_{1})
of Dynkin type
B_{3}
, where
P(\alpha_{1})
is the parabolic subgroup of
\Spin(7)
corresponding to the simple root
\alpha_{1}
(cf.
[
16
, Section 1.4]
).
The weight lattice for the representations of
\Spin(7)
is spanned by three weights
L_{1},L_{2},L_{3}
and an additional weight
(L_{1}+L_{2}+L_{3})/2
(cf.
[
4
, Section 19.3]
).
Thus a weight vector can be identified with
(k_{1}/2,k_{2}/2,k_{3}/2)
such that
k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}
are all odd integers or all even integers.

Fix an identification
\mathbb{Q}^{5}\simeq\Spin(7)/P(\alpha_{1})
.
If
\mathcal{F}
is an irreducible homogeneous vector bundle over
\mathbb{Q}^{5}\simeq\Spin(7)/P(\alpha_{1})
,
it corresponds to a weight
(k_{1}/2,k_{2}/2,k_{3}/2)
.
Conversely, a weight uniquely determines an irreducible homogeneous bundle, if it exists.
Thus it is allowed to denote as

\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}\left(\frac{k_{1}}{2},\frac{k_{2}}{2},\frac{k_{3}}{2}\right).

Under this notation,

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}^{5}}(a)=\mathcal{F}(a,0,0)
and
\mathcal{S}^{\vee}=\mathcal{F}\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The following hold.

(1)

H^{i}(\mathbb{Q}^{5},\Sym^{k}\mathcal{S}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}^{5}}(k+j))=0
for all
i>0
,
k\geq 0
and
j\geq-4
.

(2)

H^{i}(\mathbb{Q}^{5},\Sym^{k}\mathcal{S}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{S}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}^{5}}(k+j))=0
for all
i>0
,
k\geq 0
and
j\geq-3
except
(i,j,k)=(1,-3,1)
.

(3)

H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}^{5},\mathcal{S}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{S}^{\vee}(-2))\simeq\mathbb{C}
.

(4)

H^{i}(\mathbb{Q}^{5},\Sym^{k}\mathcal{S}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{S}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{S}\otimes\mathcal{O}(k+j))=0
for all
i>0
,
k\geq 0
and
j\geq 0
.

Proof.

Basically this lemma is a consequence of the Borel–Bott–Weil theorem (cf.
[
24
, Section 4.3]
).
For simplicity, let
\left(\frac{k_{1}}{2},\frac{k_{2}}{2},\frac{k_{3}}{2}\right)
denote the irreducible homogeneous bundle
\mathcal{F}\left(\frac{k_{1}}{2},\frac{k_{2}}{2},\frac{k_{3}}{2}\right)
.

For (1), note that
\Sym^{k}\mathcal{S}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}^{5}}(k+j)=\left(\frac{3k+2j}{2},\frac{k}{2},\frac{k}{2}\right)
.
If
k\geq 0
and
j\geq-4
, the weight is dominant except when

(k,j)\in\{(0,-4),(0,-3),(0,-2),(0,-1),(1,-4),(1,-3),(1,-2),(2,-4),(2,-3),(3,-4)\}.

If
(k,j)
is one of the above ten, then the bundle is one of

(-4,0,0)
,
(-3,0,0)
,
(-2,0,0)
,
(-1,0,0)
,
\left(-\frac{5}{2},\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)
,
\left(-\frac{3}{2},\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)
,
\left(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)
,
(-1,1,1)
,
(0,1,1)
, and
\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2}\right)
.

Under the dotted action of the Weyl group on the space of weights (cf.
[
24
, Section 4.3]
), all the above weights have non-trivial stabilizers,
and hence the corresponding vector bundles are acyclic.
This shows (1).

For (2) and (3), the Littlewood–Richardson rule (cf.
[
4
, Section 25.3]
) yields

\displaystyle\Sym^{k}\mathcal{S}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{S}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}^{5}}(k+j)

\displaystyle\simeq\begin{cases}\left(\frac{2j+1}{2},\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)&\text{if $k=0$}\\
\left(\frac{3k+2j+1}{2},\frac{k+1}{2},\frac{k+1}{2}\right)\oplus\left(\frac{3k+2j+1}{2},\frac{k+1}{2},\frac{k-1}{2}\right)\oplus\left(\frac{3k+2j+1}{2},\frac{k-1}{2},\frac{k-1}{2}\right)&\text{if $k\geq 1$.}\end{cases}

If
k\geq 0
and
j\geq-3
,
as for (1), one can see that all weights are dominant or give acyclic bundles except when
(k,j)=(1,-3)
.
In this exceptional case, the bundle we consider is
\mathcal{S}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{S}^{\vee}(-2)
.
Now it is well-known that

\RG(\mathbb{Q}^{5},\mathcal{S}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{S}^{\vee}(-2))=\RHom_{\mathbb{Q}^{5}}(\mathcal{S}^{\vee},\mathcal{S})\simeq\mathbb{C}[-1].

(Of course, one can also deduce this from the Borel–Bott–Weil theorem.)
This completes the proof of (2) and (3).

It remains to show (4).
First, note that

\mathcal{S}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{S}\simeq(0,1,1)\oplus(0,1,0)\oplus(0,0,0).

It holds that
(0,1,1)\simeq\Sym^{2}\mathcal{S}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{O}(-1)
and
that
(0,1,0)\simeq T_{\mathbb{Q}^{5}}(-1)
, where
T_{\mathbb{Q}^{5}}
is the tangent bundle.
The Littlewood–Richardson rule gives

\displaystyle\Sym^{k}\mathcal{S}^{\vee}\otimes\Sym^{2}\mathcal{S}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}^{5}}(k+j-1)

\displaystyle\simeq\begin{cases}(j,1,1)&\text{if $k=0$}\\
\left(\frac{3+2j}{2},\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2}\right)\oplus\left(\frac{3+2j}{2},\frac{3}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)\oplus\left(\frac{3+2j}{2},\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)&\text{if $k=1$}\\
\left(\frac{3k+2j}{2},\frac{k+2}{2},\frac{k+2}{2}\right)\oplus\left(\frac{3k+2j}{2},\frac{k+2}{2},\frac{k}{2}\right)\oplus\left(\frac{3k+2j}{2},\frac{k+2}{2},\frac{k-2}{2}\right)&\\
\oplus\left(\frac{3k+2j}{2},\frac{k}{2},\frac{k}{2}\right)\oplus\left(\frac{3k+2j}{2},\frac{k}{2},\frac{k-2}{2}\right)\oplus\left(\frac{3k+2j}{2},\frac{k-2}{2},\frac{k-2}{2}\right)&\text{if $k\geq 2$}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\Sym^{k}\mathcal{S}^{\vee}\otimes T_{\mathbb{Q}^{5}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}^{5}}(k+j-1)

\displaystyle\simeq\begin{cases}(j,1,0)&\text{if $k=0$}\\
\left(\frac{3+2j}{2},\frac{3}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)\oplus\left(\frac{3+2j}{2},\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)&\text{if $k=1$}\\
\left(\frac{3k+2j}{2},\frac{k+2}{2},\frac{k}{2}\right)\oplus\left(\frac{3k+2j}{2},\frac{k}{2},\frac{k}{2}\right)\oplus\left(\frac{3k+2j}{2},\frac{k}{2},\frac{k-2}{2}\right)&\text{if $k\geq 2$}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\Sym^{k}\mathcal{S}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}^{5}}(k+j)\simeq\left(\frac{3k+2j}{2},\frac{k}{2},\frac{k}{2}\right).

If
k\geq 0
and
j\geq 0
, one can see that all above weights are dominant or give acyclic bundles.
This shows (4).
∎

3.2.
Construction of tilting bundles

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an Ottaviani bundle over
\mathbb{Q}^{5}
, and put

X=\Tot_{\mathbb{Q}^{5}}(\mathcal{G}^{\vee}(-1))=\Spec_{\mathbb{Q}^{5}}\Sym^{\bullet}\mathcal{G}(1).

The variety
X
is a local Calabi–Yau eightfold with a flopping contraction
f\colon X\to\Spec H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
.

The aim of the present section is to construct tilting bundles over the variety
X
.
Recall that the notion of tilting bundle is defined as follows.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
Z
be a scheme that is projective over an affine Noetherian scheme.

(1)

A
pretilting
bundle over
Z
is a locally free sheaf
\mathcal{E}
of finite rank such that
\Ext^{i}_{Z}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})=0
for all
i>0
.

(2)

A
tilting
bundle over
Z
is a pretilting bundle
\mathcal{T}
that generates the (unbounded) derived category
\D(\Qcoh Z)
of quasi-coherent sheaves over
Z
, i.e. if
F\in\D(\Qcoh Z)
satisfies
\RHom_{Z}(\mathcal{T},F)=0
, then
F=0
.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{X}(a)
and
\mathcal{S}_{X}
denote the pull-back of the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}^{5}}(a)
and the spinor bundle
\mathcal{S}
on
\mathbb{Q}^{5}
by the projection
X\to\mathbb{Q}^{5}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

The following hold.

(1)

H^{i}(\mathbb{Q}^{5},\Sym^{k}(\mathcal{G}(1))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}^{5}}(j))=0
for all
i>0
,
k\geq 0
and
j\geq-4
.

(2)

H^{i}(\mathbb{Q}^{5},\Sym^{k}(\mathcal{G}(1))\otimes\mathcal{S}^{\vee}(j))=0
for all
i>0
,
k\geq 0
and
j\geq-3
, except when
(i,j,k)=(1,-3,1)
.

(3)

H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}^{5},\mathcal{G}(1)\otimes\mathcal{S}^{\vee}(-3))=\mathbb{C}
.

(4)

H^{i}(\mathbb{Q}^{5},\Sym^{k}(\mathcal{G}(1))\otimes\mathcal{S}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{S})=0
for all
i>0
and
k\geq 0
.

Proof.

Recall that an Ottaviani bundle
\mathcal{G}
fits in an exact sequence
0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}^{5}}\to\mathcal{S}^{\vee}\to\mathcal{G}\to 0
.
Taking the symmetric power gives exact sequences

0\to(\Sym^{k-1}\mathcal{S}^{\vee})(k)\to(\Sym^{k}\mathcal{S}^{\vee})(k)\to\Sym^{k}(\mathcal{G}(1))\to 0

for all
k\geq 0
.
Now the result follows from Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

The following holds.

(1)

H^{i}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(j))=0
for all
i>0
and
j\geq-4
.

(2)

H^{i}(X,\mathcal{S}_{X}^{\vee}(j))=0
for all
i>0
and
j\geq-3
except when
(i,j)=(1,-3)
.

(3)

H^{1}(X,\mathcal{S}_{X}^{\vee}(-3))=\mathbb{C}
.

(4)

\Ext_{X}^{i}(\mathcal{S}_{X},\mathcal{S}_{X})=0
for all
i>0
.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{F}
is a vector bundle over
\mathbb{Q}^{5}
and
\mathcal{F}_{X}
denotes the pull-back of
\mathcal{F}
by
X\to\mathbb{Q}^{5}
,
the adjunction gives

H^{i}(X,\mathcal{F}_{X})=H^{i}\left(\mathbb{Q}^{5},\mathcal{F}\otimes\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\Sym^{k}(\mathcal{G}(1))\right).

Thus the result follows from Lemma
3.3
.
∎

By the above lemma, it follows that
\Ext_{X}^{1}(\mathcal{S}_{X}^{\vee},\mathcal{O}_{X}(-2))\simeq H^{1}(X,\mathcal{S}_{X}^{\vee}(-3))=\mathbb{C}
.
Let

0\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(-2)\to\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{S}_{X}^{\vee}\to 0

be the unique non-trivial extension.

Theorem 3.5
.

The bundles

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{O}_{X}(-2)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}(2)\oplus\mathcal{P},~\text{and}

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{\vee}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}_{X}(-2)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}(2)\oplus\mathcal{P}^{\vee}

are tilting bundles over
X
.

Proof.

By Kapranov
[
10
]
, it is known that the bundle

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}^{5}}(-2)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}^{5}}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{S}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}^{5}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}^{5}}(1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}^{5}}(2)

is a tilting bundle over
\mathbb{Q}^{5}
, and in particular it generates
\D(\Qcoh\mathbb{Q}^{5})
.
Thus, by
[
7
, Lemma 3.1]
, the bundle

\mathcal{U}:=\mathcal{O}_{X}(-2)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{S}_{X}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}(2)

also generates
\D(\Qcoh X)
.

Consider the collection of vector bundles appearing as direct summands of
\mathcal{U}
, taken in the order given above.
Then, by Lemma
3.4
, this collection consists of pretilting bundles.
Furthermore, there are no non-trivial higher
\Ext
’s to the right,
and when viewed to the left, no non-trivial
\Ext^{i}
with
i\geq 2
appear.
Therefore, it is able to apply
[
7
, Lemma 2.4]
to construct a tilting bundle.

Moreover, the only nontrivial higher extension is
\Ext_{X}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(2),\mathcal{S}_{X})=\mathbb{C}
,
and the corresponding exact sequence is

0\to\mathcal{S}_{X}\to\mathcal{P}^{\vee}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(2)\to 0
.
Thus the proof of
[
7
, Lemma 2.4]
shows that the resulting tilting bundle over
X
is the bundle

\mathcal{T}^{\vee}=\mathcal{O}_{X}(-2)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}(2)\oplus\mathcal{P}^{\vee}.

The dual of this bundle
\mathcal{T}

is also a pretilting bundle, and since
\mathcal{T}
can generate the shifts of
\mathcal{S}_{X}^{\vee}
and
\mathcal{S}_{X}
by taking cones,

\mathcal{T}
is also a generator of
\D(\Qcoh X)
, and hence is tilting.
∎

3.3.
Derived equivalence

This section completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
Recall from Section
1.2
that the diagram of the flop is

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E=W}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{+}^{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{X}=\Tot_{W}(\mathcal{O}_{W}(-1,-1))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{-}^{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{+}=\Tot_{H_{+}}(\mathcal{G}_{+}^{\vee}(-1))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{-}=\Tot_{H_{-}}(\mathcal{G}_{-}^{\vee}(-1))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{+}=\mathbb{Q}^{5}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Spec R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{-}=\mathbb{Q}^{5}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{+}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{-}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi_{+}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Bl_{H_{+}^{0}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi_{-}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Bl_{H_{-}^{0}}}
flop
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{+}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{-}}

As in Section
3.2
, let
\mathcal{S}_{X_{\pm}}
be the pull-back of the spinor bundle over
H_{\pm}
by the projection
X_{\pm}\to H_{\pm}
,
and
\mathcal{P}_{\pm}
the bundles that lie in the unique non-trivial extensions

0\to\mathcal{O}_{X_{\pm}}(-2)\to\mathcal{P}_{\pm}\to\mathcal{S}_{X_{\pm}}\to 0.

Put

\mathcal{T}_{\pm}:=\mathcal{O}_{X_{\pm}}(-2)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X_{\pm}}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X_{\pm}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X_{\pm}}(1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X_{\pm}}(2)\oplus\mathcal{P}_{\pm}.

By Theorem
3.5
,
\mathcal{T}_{\pm}
and
\mathcal{T}_{\pm}^{\vee}
are tilting bundles over
X_{\pm}
.

Theorem 3.6
.

There are isomorphisms

{f_{+}}_{*}\mathcal{T}_{+}\simeq{f_{-}}_{*}\mathcal{T}_{-}
and
{f_{+}}_{*}\mathcal{T}_{+}^{\vee}\simeq{f_{-}}_{*}\mathcal{T}_{-}^{\vee}

of
R
-modules.

The proof of this theorem requires the observation from Section
2.2
.
Let
\mathcal{E}
be the key bundle over
W
.
Let
\mathcal{E}_{\widetilde{X}}
denote the pull-back of the key bundle
\mathcal{E}
to
\widetilde{X}
by the projection
\widetilde{X}=\Tot_{W}(\mathcal{O}_{W}(-1,-1))\to W
.
The pull-back of the exchange diagram over
W
to
\widetilde{X}
by the projection gives two exact sequences

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(-1,1)\to\mathcal{E}_{\widetilde{X}}\to\phi_{+}^{*}\mathcal{S}_{X_{+}}^{\vee}\to 0,

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(1,-1)\to\mathcal{E}_{\widetilde{X}}\to\phi_{-}^{*}\mathcal{S}_{X_{-}}^{\vee}\to 0.

By Lemma
2.1
, these sequences do not split.
Indeed, if the above sequences split, their restriction to the zero-section, which coincide with the sequences in Lemma
2.1
, remain split, but this is a contradiction.

Proposition 3.7
.

There exists an isomorphism
{\phi_{\pm}}_{*}(\mathcal{E}_{\widetilde{X}}(E))\simeq\mathcal{P}_{\pm}
for each
+
and
-
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(-1,-1)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(E)
, twisting the above exact sequences by
\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(E)
gives

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(-2,0)\to\mathcal{E}_{\widetilde{X}}(E)\to\phi_{+}^{*}\mathcal{S}_{X_{+}}^{\vee}(E)\to 0,

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(0,-2)\to\mathcal{E}_{\widetilde{X}}(E)\to\phi_{-}^{*}\mathcal{S}_{X_{-}}^{\vee}(E)\to 0.

By the projection formula,
R{\phi_{+}}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(-2,0)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X_{+}}(-2)
and
R{\phi_{-}}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(0,-2)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X_{-}}(-2)
.
In addition, since
\phi_{\pm}
are smooth blowing-ups,
\mathcal{O}_{E}(E)
is the universal line bundle for
both
\pi_{\pm}\colon E\to H_{\pm}^{0}
, and hence
R{\phi_{\pm}}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{E}(E)=0
holds.
Therefore, the standard exact sequence
0\to\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}\to\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(E)\to\mathcal{O}_{E}(E)\to 0
shows that
R{\phi_{\pm}}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(E)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X_{\pm}}
.
In particular, the projection formula yields an isomorphism

R{\phi_{\pm}}_{*}\left(\phi_{\pm}^{*}\mathcal{S}_{X_{\pm}}^{\vee}(E)\right)\simeq\mathcal{S}_{X_{\pm}}^{\vee}

for each
+
and
-
.
Thus,
R{\phi_{\pm}}_{*}(\mathcal{E}_{\widetilde{X}}(E))\simeq{\phi_{\pm}}_{*}(\mathcal{E}_{\widetilde{X}}(E))
, and this sheaf lies in an exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{X_{\pm}}(-2)\to{\phi_{\pm}}_{*}(\mathcal{E}_{\widetilde{X}}(E))\to\mathcal{S}_{X_{\pm}}^{\vee}\to 0.

We claim that this exact sequence does not split for each
+
and
-
.
To see this, it is enough to show that the two associated homomorphisms

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{+}\colon

\displaystyle\Ext^{1}_{\widetilde{X}}(\phi_{+}^{*}\mathcal{S}_{X_{+}}^{\vee}(E),\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(-2,0))\to\Ext_{X_{+}}^{1}(\mathcal{S}_{X_{+}}^{\vee},\mathcal{O}_{X_{+}}(-2)),

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{-}\colon

\displaystyle\Ext^{1}_{\widetilde{X}}(\phi_{-}^{*}\mathcal{S}_{X_{-}}^{\vee}(E),\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(0,-2))\to\Ext_{X_{-}}^{1}(\mathcal{S}_{X_{-}}^{\vee},\mathcal{O}_{X_{-}}(-2))

are injective.
Since

\Ext_{X_{+}}^{1}(\mathcal{S}_{X_{+}}^{\vee},\mathcal{O}_{X_{+}}(-2))\simeq\Ext_{\widetilde{X}}^{1}(\phi_{+}^{*}\mathcal{S}_{X_{+}}^{\vee},\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(-2,0)),

the homomorphism
\varepsilon_{+}
is obtained by applying the functor
\Ext^{1}_{\widetilde{X}}(-,\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(-2,0))
to the
counit morphism

\eta\colon\phi_{+}^{*}\mathcal{S}_{X_{+}}^{\vee}\simeq\phi_{+}^{*}{\phi_{+}}_{*}\phi_{+}^{*}\mathcal{S}_{X_{+}}^{\vee}(E)\to\phi_{+}^{*}\mathcal{S}_{X_{+}}^{\vee}(E).

Note that this
\eta
is injective and has the cokernel
\phi_{+}^{*}\mathcal{S}_{X_{+}}^{\vee}(E)|_{E}
.
Now

\displaystyle\Ext^{1}_{\widetilde{X}}(\phi_{+}^{*}\mathcal{S}_{X_{+}}^{\vee}(E)|_{E},\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(-2,0))

\displaystyle\simeq\Ext^{1}_{E}(\phi_{+}^{*}\mathcal{S}_{X_{+}}^{\vee}(E)|_{E},\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(-2,0)|_{E}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{E}}\mathcal{O}_{E}(E)[-1])

\displaystyle\simeq\Hom_{W}(\pi_{+}^{*}\mathcal{S}_{+}^{\vee},\mathcal{O}_{W}(-2,0))

\displaystyle\simeq\Hom_{H_{+}}(\mathcal{S}_{+}^{\vee},\mathcal{O}_{H_{+}}(-2))=0.

Therefore,
\varepsilon_{+}
is injective.
The same argument shows that
\varepsilon_{-}
is also injective.
Finally, the uniqueness of the non-trivial extensions yields

{\phi_{\pm}}_{*}(\mathcal{E}_{\widetilde{X}}(E))\simeq\mathcal{P}_{\pm}

as desired.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.6
.

Let
\refl(Y)
denote the category of reflexive coherent sheaves over a normal scheme
Y
.
Since
f_{\pm}\colon X_{\pm}\to\Spec R
are flopping contractions, they are isomorphic in codimension one,
and hence the push-forwards
{f_{\pm}}_{*}
give equivalences of categories

\refl(X_{+})\simeq\refl(\Spec R)\simeq\refl(X_{-}).

Since
X_{+}\dashrightarrow X_{-}
is a flop,
{f_{+}}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X_{+}}(a)\simeq{f_{-}}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X_{-}}(-a)\in\refl(\Spec R)

for all
a\in\mathbb{Z}
.
In addition, Proposition
3.7
gives

{f_{+}}_{*}\mathcal{P}_{+}\simeq(f_{+}\circ\phi_{+})_{*}(\mathcal{E}_{\widetilde{X}}(E))\simeq(f_{-}\circ\phi_{-})_{*}(\mathcal{E}_{\widetilde{X}}(E))\simeq{f_{-}}_{*}\mathcal{P}_{-}\in\refl(\Spec R).

Since the equivalences commute with duals,
{f_{+}}_{*}\mathcal{P}_{+}^{\vee}\simeq{f_{-}}_{*}\mathcal{P}_{-}^{\vee}
also holds.
This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

The tilting bundles
\mathcal{T}_{\pm}
give equivalences

\tau_{\pm}:=\RHom_{X_{\pm}}(\mathcal{T}_{\pm},-)\colon\Db(\coh X_{\pm})\xrightarrow{\sim}\Db(\modu\End_{X_{\pm}}(\mathcal{T}_{\pm})).

The equivalences of categories
\refl(X_{+})\simeq\refl(\Spec R)\simeq\refl(X_{-})
together with Theorem
3.6
imply that

\Lambda:=\End_{X_{+}}(\mathcal{T}_{+})\simeq\End_{R}({f_{+}}_{*}\mathcal{T}_{+})\simeq\End_{R}({f_{-}}_{*}\mathcal{T}_{-})\simeq\End_{X_{-}}(\mathcal{T}_{-})

as
R
-algebras.
This yields the equivalences of
R
-linear triangulated categories

\Db(\coh X_{+})\xrightarrow[\tau_{+}]{\sim}\Db(\modu\Lambda)\xrightarrow[\tau_{-}^{-1}]{\sim}\Db(\coh X_{-}).

The derived equivalence
\tau_{+}
sends
\mathcal{O}_{X_{+}}
to the projective
\Lambda
-module
P_{0}
corresponding to the summand
R={f_{+}}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X_{+}}
of
{f_{+}}_{*}\mathcal{T}_{+}
, and then
\tau_{-}^{-1}
sends
P_{0}
to
\mathcal{O}_{X_{-}}
.
If

\Phi:=\tau_{-}^{-1}\circ\tau_{+}\colon\Db(\coh X_{+})\xrightarrow{\sim}\Db(\coh X_{-})

denotes the composited derived equivalence,
then for any
\mathcal{F}\in\Db(\coh X_{+})
,

\displaystyle R{f_{+}}_{*}\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle\simeq\RHom_{X_{+}}(\mathcal{O}_{X_{+}},\mathcal{F})\simeq\RHom_{X_{-}}(\Phi\mathcal{O}_{X_{+}},\Phi\mathcal{F})\simeq\RHom_{X_{-}}(\mathcal{O}_{X_{-}},\Phi\mathcal{F})\simeq(R{f_{-}}_{*}\circ\Phi)(\mathcal{F}).

All the above isomorphisms are functorial, and hence there is a functor isomorphism
R{f_{+}}_{*}\simeq R{f_{-}}_{*}\circ\Phi
.
This completes the proof of the main theorem.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

Using
\mathcal{T}_{\pm}^{\vee}
instead of
\mathcal{T}_{\pm}
gives another equivalence

\Psi\colon\Db(\coh X_{+})\xrightarrow{\sim}\Db(\coh X_{-}).

The composition
\Psi^{-1}\circ\Phi
gives a non-trivial autoequivalence of

.
It would be interesting to study the relation between this autoequivalence and spherical twists.

During the proof of the main theorem, the following corollary also had been proved.
Note that, for any local model

of a flopping contraction of type
G_{2}^{\dagger}
, the completion
\mathfrak{R}
of
\mathcal{R}
along the maximal ideal that corresponds to the singularity does not depend on the choice of local models, and
\Spec\mathfrak{R}
has exactly two crepant resolutions.

.

Two
R
-modules

and

give NCCRs of

(cf.

,

]
).

Monotonicity in the parameter of the Mittag-Leffler function and determining the fractional exponent of the subdiffusion equation
Ravshan Ashurov, Ilyoskhuja Sulaymonov

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove the strict monotonicity in the parameter ρ \rho of the Mittag-Leffler functions E ρ ​ ( − t ρ ) E_{\rho}(-t^{\rho}) and t ρ − 1 ​ E ρ , ρ ​ ( − t ρ ) t^{\rho-1}E_{\rho,\rho}(-t^{\rho}) . Then, these results are applied to solve the inverse problem of determining the order of the fractional derivative in subdiffusion equations, where the available measurement is given at one point in space-time. In particular, we find the missing conditions in the previously known work in this area. Moreover, the obtained results are valid for a wider class of subdiffusion equations than those considered previously. An example of an initial boundary value problem constructed by Sh.A. Alimov is given, for which the inverse problem under consideration has a unique solution. We also point out the application of the monotonicity of the Mittag-Leffler functions to solving some other inverse problems of determining the order of a fractional derivative. Keywords : The Caputo fractional derivative, monotonicity in the parameter of the Mittag-Leffler function, inverse problems.



1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega
be an arbitrary
N
- dimensional domain with a sufficiently smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. Consider the following
Initial-boundary value problem
:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &D_{t}^{\rho}u(x,t)-\Delta u(x,t)=0,\hskip 9.24994ptx\in\Omega,\hskip 9.24994pt0<t\leq T,\\
&u{(x,t)}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\\
&u(x,0)=\varphi(x),\hskip 9.24994ptx\in\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\varphi(x)
is a continuous function,
\Delta
is the Laplace operator,
\rho\in(0,1)
and
D_{t}^{\rho}
is the fractional Caputo derivative defined as (see, for example,
[
1
]
, p. 91):

D_{t}^{\rho}u(x,t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\rho)}\frac{d}{dt}\int\limits_{0}^{t}\frac{u(x,\xi)-u(x,0)}{(t-\xi)^{\rho}}d\xi,\hskip 9.24994ptt>0,\,\,x\in\Omega.

When modeling various processes, the order of the fractional derivative
\rho
is often unknown. Unfortunately, in the processes under consideration there is no device for measuring this parameter. One of the effective methods for finding the unknown order of the derivative is, by setting some additional conditions for a solution
u(x,t)
of problem (
1.1
), to solve analytically the corresponding inverse problem.

In recent years, significant advances have been made in the study of this type of inverse problems (see Liu et al.
[
2
]
and references therein for a comprehensive review of the work published up to 2019). Moreover, numerous studies (e.g.
[
3
]
-
[
10
]
) published after 2019 have proposed additional conditions for efficiently treating and solving these inverse problems.

A very interesting paper
[
11
]
by G. Li, Z. Wang, X. Jia, Y. Zhang was recently published, in which the order of the fractional derivative
\rho
in the equation
D_{t}^{\rho}u(x,t)-c\Delta u(x,t)=0
(
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},d=1,2,3
and
c
is a sufficiently small positive number) was reconstructed from information on the solution of the initial-boundary value problem at only one point of space-time
(x_{0},t_{0})
(see Theorem 2). However, in our opinion, this result is questionable in the sense that the conditions of this theorem are not sufficient to guarantee its assertion. Note also that the proof relies on the condition
d\leq 3
. However, the paper contains an interesting auxiliary result (see Theorem 1): the authors proved that the function
g(\rho)=E_{\rho}(-ct^{\rho})
has a negative derivative,
g^{\prime}(\rho)<0
, if
t
is large enough and
c
is small enough.

Motivated by the results and interesting ideas of
[
11
]
, we continued our research in this direction. We managed to find the missing condition in Theorem 2 and remove some restrictions mentioned in
[
11
]
. In particular:

1) we have proved that the Mittag-Leffler functions
t^{\rho-1}E_{\rho,\rho}(-t^{\rho})
and
E_{\rho}(-t^{\rho})
increase monotonically in parameter
\rho
for sufficiently small
t
. This is the main result of this work;

2) the requirement of a sufficiently small coefficient
c
was removed from the considered equation;

3) the restriction on the dimension of the domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},d=1,2,3
was removed. Of course, the transition to the domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, with
N\geq 1
, does not cause any particular difficulty, but we would like to draw the attention of readers to one fundamental work by V.A. Ilyin
[
12
]
;

4) it is shown that the conditions of Theorem 2 of
[
11
]
is not complete and an updated version of this theorem with a proof is presented.

5) other applications of the monotonicity in parameter of Mittag-Leffler functions are given.

The remainder of the paper is structured as follows.

Section 2 introduces the necessary auxiliary concepts. Section 3 proves the monotonicity of the Mittag-Leffler functions in the parameter
\rho
. This result, which is naturally of independent interest, can be applied to the solution of various inverse problems of determining the order of a fractional derivative; some applications are noted at the end of this section. Section 4 studies the initial-boundary value problem (
1.1
). Section 5 discusses the inverse problem of determining the fractional order using information on the solution of the initial-boundary value problem at only one point of space-time
(x_{0},t_{0})
. This requires rather strict conditions on the corresponding eigenfunctions and Fourier coefficients of the initial function. In the next section an example of an initial boundary value problem constructed by Sh.A. Alimov is given, for which this inverse problem has always a unique solution. In the final section, using the monotonicity of the Mittag-Leffler function, a new proof of one result of A.V. Pskhu is presented.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we recall the fundamental result of V.A. Ilyin
[
12
]
on the convergence of Fourier coefficients and present an estimate of the Mittag-Leffler function.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
N
-dimentional domain with a sufficiently smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
and
\{v_{k}(x)\}
denote the complete system of orthonormal in
L_{2}(\Omega)
eigenfunctions and
\{\lambda_{k}\}
the set of positive eigenvalues of the spectral problem:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta v(x)=\lambda v(x),\hskip 9.24994ptx\in\Omega,\\
&v(x)\big|_{\partial\Omega}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Let us present some assertions about eigenfunctions
v_{k}(x)
and eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}
proved by V.A.Ilyin
[
12
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The series
\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{-\left(\left[\frac{N}{2}\right]+1\right)}v_{k}^{2}(x)
converges uniformly in a closed domain
\overline{\Omega}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let the function
g(x)
satisfy the conditions

(1)

g(x)\in C^{p}(\overline{\Omega}),\,\,\frac{\partial^{p+1}g(x)}{\partial x_{1}^{p_{1}}\dots\partial x_{n}^{p_{n}}}\in L_{2}(\Omega),\,\,p+1=p_{1}+p_{2}+\dots p_{n},p\geq 1,

(2)

g(x)|_{\partial\Omega}=\Delta g(x)|_{\partial\Omega}=\dots=\Delta^{\left[\frac{p}{2}\right]}g(x)|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

Then the number series
\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}g_{k}^{2}\lambda_{k}^{p+1}
converges, where
g_{k}=(g,v_{k})
.

Let
A
be a self-adjoint extension in
L_{2}(\Omega)
of the Laplace operator with the homogeneous Dirichlet condition. Let
v_{k}(x)
be the eigenfunctions and
\lambda_{k}
the corresponding eigenvalues of this operator. For any real number
\tau
we define the power of the operator
A
by the formula

A^{\tau}f(x)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{\tau}f_{k}v_{k}(x),\hskip 9.24994ptf_{k}=(f,v_{k}),

the domain of definition has the form

D(A^{\tau})=\{f\in L_{2}(\Omega),\hskip 9.24994pt\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{2\tau}|f_{k}|^{2}<\infty\}.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
M
be a fixed number. We will say that
f\in I_{M}(\Omega)
if

(1)

satisfy conditions Lemma
2.2
with the exponent
p=\left[\frac{N}{2}\right]

(2)

||A^{\frac{1}{2}\left([\frac{N}{2}]+1\right)}f||_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\leq M.

For
0<\rho<1
, let
E_{\rho,\mu}(z)
denote the Mittag-Leffler function defined as:

E_{\rho,\mu}(z)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{\Gamma(\rho k+\mu)},\hskip 9.24994pt\mu,z\in\mathbb{C}.

If
\mu=1
, then the Mittag-Leffler function is called the one-parameter or classical Mittag-Leffler function and is denoted by
E_{\rho}(z)=E_{\rho,1}(z)
.

Recall the following estimate of the Mittag-Leffler functions (see, e.g.
[
13
]
, p. 29).

Lemma 2.4
.

For any
t\geq 0
one has

|E_{\rho,\mu}(-t)|\leq\frac{C}{1+t},\hskip 9.24994pt\mu\in\mathbb{C},

where constant
C
does not depend on
t
.

3.
Monotonicity of the Mittag-Leffler functions

This paragraph uses some original ideas from the paper
[
11
]
by G. Li, Z. Wang, X. Jia, Y. Zhang.

Let us first introduce some important concepts to prove the strict monotonicity of the Mittag-Leffler function
E_{\rho}(-t^{\rho})
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\rho\in(0,1)
. Then the following equality holds:

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\Gamma(\rho n)}{\Gamma(\rho n+\rho)}=0.

This lemma was proved in
[
11
]
. For the convenience of the reader, we recall this proof.

Proof.

Using the Stirling approximation of the gamma function we have:

\frac{\Gamma(\rho n)}{\Gamma(\rho n+\rho)}\sim\frac{\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{\sqrt{\rho n}}\left(\frac{\rho n}{e}\right)^{\rho n}}{\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{\sqrt{\rho n+\rho}}\left(\frac{\rho n+\rho}{e}\right)^{\rho n+\rho}}\sim e^{\rho}\left(\frac{\rho n}{\rho n+\rho}\right)^{\rho n}\frac{1}{(\rho n+\rho)^{\rho}}

\times\sqrt{\frac{\rho n+\rho}{\rho n}}\sim e^{\rho}\left[\left(\frac{1}{1+1/n}\right)^{n}\right]^{\rho}\frac{1}{(\rho n+\rho)^{\rho}}\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{n}}\hskip 9.24994pt(n\rightarrow\infty).

According to
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{1}{1+1/n}\right)^{n}=e^{-1}
, we get:

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\Gamma(\rho n)}{\Gamma(\rho n+\rho)}=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}e^{\rho}e^{-\rho}\frac{1}{(\rho n+\rho)^{\rho}}\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{n}}=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{(\rho n+\rho)^{\rho}}=0.

Lemma
3.1
is proved.
∎

Now we calculate the derivative of the Mittag-Leffler function
E_{\rho}(-t^{\rho})
. Let
\Phi(\rho)
be the logarithmic derivative of the gamma function
\Gamma(\rho)
(see, for example
[
14
]
). Then
\Gamma^{\prime}(\rho)=\Gamma(\rho)\Phi(\rho)
and therefore, we have:

\frac{d}{d\rho}E_{\rho}(-t^{\rho})=\frac{d}{d\rho}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\frac{t^{\rho n}}{\Gamma(\rho n+1)}

(3.1)

=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}nt^{\rho n}\frac{\ln{t}-\Phi(\rho n+1)}{\Gamma(\rho n+1)}=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}y_{n},

where

\Phi(\rho n+1)=-\gamma-\frac{1}{\rho n+1}+\sum\limits_{s=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{s+\rho n+1}\bigg),

(3.2)

y_{n}=nt^{\rho n}\frac{\ln{t}-\Phi(\rho n+1)}{\Gamma(\rho n+1)},

and
\gamma\approx 0.57722
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\rho\in(0,1)
. Then the following two statements hold:

1)

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\Phi(\rho n+\rho+1)}{\Phi(\rho n+1)}=1.

2)
\Phi(\rho n+1)>-\frac{1}{\rho+1}
for all
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

Part 1 of this lemma was proved in
[
11
]
. We give a slightly different proof.
Consider the function
f(z)=z(\ln{z}-\Phi(z))
. Function
f(z)
is strictly decreasing and strictly convex on
(0,\infty)
(see,
[
15
]
) and the following relationships are fulfilled:

(3.3)

\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow 0}f(z)=1\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994pt\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow\infty}f(z)=\frac{1}{2}.

From (
3.3
) and the monotonicity of
f(z)
we get:

(3.4)

\frac{1}{2}\leq f(z)\leq 1,\hskip 9.24994ptz\in(0,\infty).

Using estimate (
3.4
) and definition of function
f(z)
we obtain

(3.5)

\ln{z}-\frac{1}{z}\leq\Phi(z)\leq\ln{z}-\frac{1}{2z}.

Apply estimate (
3.5
) to have:

(3.6)

\ln{(\rho n+1)}-\frac{1}{\rho n+1}\leq\Phi(\rho n+1)\leq\ln{(\rho n+1)}-\frac{1}{2(\rho n+1)}.

Therefore,

\frac{\ln{(\rho n+\rho+1)}-\frac{1}{\rho n+\rho+1}}{\ln{(\rho n+1)}-\frac{1}{2(\rho n+1)}}\leq\frac{\Phi(\rho n+\rho+1)}{\Phi(\rho n+1)}\leq\frac{\ln{(\rho n+\rho+1)}-\frac{1}{2(\rho n+\rho+1)}}{\ln{(\rho n+1)}-\frac{1}{\rho n+1}}.

Firstly we will show that

(3.7)

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln{(\rho n+\rho+1)}}{\ln{(\rho n+1)}}=1.

Using Lopital’s theorem we get

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln{(\rho n+\rho+1)}}{\ln{(\rho n+1)}}=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\frac{\rho}{\rho n+\rho+1}}{\frac{\rho}{\rho n+1}}=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\rho n+1}{\rho n+\rho+1}=1

Apply Squeeze theorem and (
3.7
) to obtain

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\Phi(\rho n+\rho+1)}{\Phi(\rho n+1)}=1.

Part 1 of the Lemma
3.2
is proved.

The proof of part 2 of the lemma obviously follows from (
3.6
):

\Phi(\rho n+1)\geq\ln{(\rho n+1)}-\frac{1}{\rho n+1}>-\frac{1}{\rho+1},\hskip 9.24994ptn\geq 1.

Lemma
3.2
is completely proved.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\rho_{0}\in(0,1)
and
t_{1}>0
. Then series (
3.1
) is uniformly convergent with respect to
t\in[t_{1},T]
and
\rho\in[\rho_{0},1]
.

Proof.

Since
t\in[t_{1},T]
and
\rho\in[\rho_{0},1]
we get

\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|(-1)^{n}nt^{\rho n}\frac{\ln{t}-\Phi(\rho n+1)}{\Gamma(\rho n+1)}\right|\leq\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}nT^{n}\frac{|\ln{t}-\Phi(\rho n+1)|}{\Gamma(\rho_{0}n+1)}.

Using inequality (
3.6
), we have

\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{nT^{n}}{\Gamma(\rho_{0}n+1)}\left(\max\limits_{t\in\{t_{1},T\}}|\ln{t}|+\ln{(n+1)}+\frac{1}{2}\right)=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}z_{n},

where

z_{n}=\frac{nT^{n}}{\Gamma(\rho_{0}n+1)}\left(\max\limits_{t\in\{t_{1},T\}}|\ln{t}|+\ln{(n+1)}+\frac{1}{2}\right).

Let us consider the following proportion

\frac{z_{n+1}}{z_{n}}=\frac{(n+1)T^{n+1}\Gamma(\rho_{0}n+1)}{nT^{n}\Gamma(\rho_{0}n+\rho_{0}+1)}\frac{\max\limits_{t\in\{t_{1},T\}}|\ln{t}|+\ln{(n+2)}+\frac{1}{2}}{\max\limits_{t\in\{t_{1},T\}}|\ln{t}|+\ln{(n+1)}+\frac{1}{2}}.

By the equality
\Gamma(\rho n+1)=\rho n\Gamma(\rho n)
, we have

\frac{z_{n+1}}{z_{n}}=T\frac{\Gamma(\rho_{0}n)}{\Gamma(\rho_{0}n+\rho_{0})}\frac{\max\limits_{t\in\{t_{1},T\}}|\ln{t}|+\ln{(n+2)}+\frac{1}{2}}{\max\limits_{t\in\{t_{1},T\}}|\ln{t}|+\ln{(n+1)}+\frac{1}{2}}.

Apply Lemma
3.1
, to get

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{z_{n+1}}{z_{n}}=0.

Thus, by D’Alembert’s Ratio Test, we conclude that the series
\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}z_{n}
is convergent. Therefore, by the Weierstrass M-Test, we deduce that the series (
3.1
) is uniformly convergent.

Lemma
3.3
is proved.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
0<\rho<1
. Then for
t\in\left(0,\min{\left(\frac{1}{2^{\frac{1}{\rho}}},\frac{1}{e^{\frac{7}{2}}}\right)}\right]
the inequalities
y_{n+1}>y_{n},\,\,n\geq 1
, hold.

Proof.

Let us consider the following proportion

\frac{y_{n+1}}{y_{n}}=t^{\rho}\frac{(n+1)\Gamma(\rho n+1)}{n\Gamma(\rho n+\rho+1)}\frac{\ln(t)-\Phi(\rho n+\rho+1)}{\ln(t)-\Phi(\rho n+1)}.

According to equality
\Gamma(\rho n+1)=\rho n\Gamma(\rho n)
we have

\frac{y_{n+1}}{y_{n}}=t^{\rho}\frac{\Gamma(\rho n)}{\Gamma(\rho n+\rho)}\frac{\ln(t)-\Phi(\rho n+\rho+1)}{\ln(t)-\Phi(\rho n+1)}

(3.8)

=t^{\rho}\frac{\Gamma(\rho n)}{\Gamma(\rho n+\rho)}\left(1+\frac{\Phi(\rho n+1)-\Phi(\rho n+\rho+1)}{\ln(t)-\Phi(\rho n+1)}\right)

Now we will show that

\left|\frac{\Phi(\rho n+1)-\Phi(\rho n+\rho+1)}{\ln(t)-\Phi(\rho n+1)}\right|<1.

Based on estimate (
3.6
), we examine four cases to prove the estimate above.

We can rewrite the expression
\Phi(z)
according to the estimate (
3.5
) in the following form:

\Phi(z)=\ln(z)-\frac{\theta(z)}{z},\hskip 9.24994ptz\in(0,\infty),

where function
\theta(z)
is continuous and satisfies
\frac{1}{2}\leq\theta(z)\leq 1
for all
z\in(0,\infty)
.
From this we get:

\left|\frac{\Phi(\rho n+1)-\Phi(\rho n+\rho+1)}{\ln{t}-\Phi(\rho n+1)}\right|

=\left|\frac{\ln{(\rho n+1)}-\frac{\theta(\rho n+1)}{\rho n+1}-\ln{(\rho n+\rho+1)}+\frac{\theta(\rho n+\rho+1)}{\rho n+\rho+1}}{\ln{t}-\ln{(\rho n+1)}+\frac{\theta(\rho n+1)}{\rho n+1}}\right|

=\left|\frac{\ln{\left(\frac{\rho n+1}{\rho n+\rho+1}\right)}-\frac{(\rho n+1)(\theta(\rho n+1)-\theta(\rho n+\rho+1))+\rho\theta(\rho n+1)}{(\rho n+1)(\rho n+\rho+1)}}{\ln{t}-\ln{(\rho n+1)}+\frac{\theta(\rho n+1)}{\rho n+1}}\right|

\leq\left|\frac{\ln{\left(\frac{\rho n+1}{\rho n+\rho+1}\right)}}{\ln{t}-\ln{(\rho n+1)}+\frac{\theta(\rho n+1)}{\rho n+1}}\right|+\left|\frac{\frac{(\rho n+1)(\theta(\rho n+1)-\theta(\rho n+\rho+1))+\rho\theta(\rho n+1)}{(\rho n+1)(\rho n+\rho+1)}}{\ln{t}-\ln{(\rho n+1)}+\frac{\theta(\rho n+1)}{\rho n+1}}\right|

\leq\frac{1}{|\ln{t}-\ln{(\rho n+1)}|-\frac{\theta(\rho n+1)}{\rho n+1}}+\frac{\left|\frac{\theta(\rho n+1)-\theta(\rho n+\rho+1)}{(\rho n+\rho+1)}+\frac{\rho\theta(\rho n+1)}{(\rho n+1)(\rho n+\rho+1)}\right|}{|\ln{t}-\ln{(\rho n+1)}|-\frac{\theta(\rho n+1)}{\rho n+1}}.

According to
\theta(\rho n+1),\theta(\rho n+\rho+1)\in[1/2,1]
we have

\left|\frac{\Phi(\rho n+1)-\Phi(\rho n+\rho+1)}{\ln{t}-\Phi(\rho n+1)}\right|\leq\frac{1}{|\ln{t}|-1}+\frac{\frac{1}{2}+1}{|\ln{t}|-1}=\frac{\frac{5}{2}}{|\ln{t}|-1}.

If
\ln{t}<-\frac{7}{2}
then we have

(3.9)

\left|\frac{\Phi(\rho n+1)-\Phi(\rho n+\rho+1)}{\ln{t}-\Phi(\rho n+1)}\right|<1.

Since
\Gamma(\rho n)\leq\Gamma(\rho n+\rho)
, the equality (
3.8
) and the inequality (
3.9
) we have the following inequality

\frac{y_{n+1}}{y_{n}}\leq t^{\rho}\left(1+\frac{\Phi(\rho n+1)-\Phi(\rho n+\rho+1)}{\ln(t)-\Phi(\rho n+1)}\right)<2t^{\rho}.

If we select
t\in\left(0,\min{\left(\frac{1}{2^{\frac{1}{\rho}}},\frac{1}{e^{\frac{7}{2}}}\right)}\right]
, then we have
\frac{y_{n+1}}{y_{n}}<1
. For such
t
, according to assertion 2 of Lemma
3.2
, the sequence
y_{n}
, defined by the equality (
3.2
), is negative for all
n\geq 1
. Therefore, we get the inequality
y_{n+1}>y_{n}
.
Lemma
3.4
is proved.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\rho_{0}\in(0,1)
. Then, for any
t\in\left(0,\min\left(\frac{1}{2^{\frac{1}{\rho_{0}}}},\frac{1}{e^{\frac{7}{2}}}\right)\right]
, the Mittag-Leffler function
E_{\rho}(-t^{\rho})
is monotonically increasing in
\rho\in[\rho_{0},1]
.

Proof.

According to Lemma
3.3
the series (
3.1
) converges absolutely. Let us divide this series into groups as follows

\frac{d}{d\rho}E_{\rho}(-t^{\rho})=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}y_{n}=-(y_{1}-y_{2})-(y_{3}-y_{4})-\dots

Then by Lemma
3.4
it follows that
\frac{d}{d\rho}E_{\rho}(-t^{\rho})>0
for all
t
and
\rho
from the conditions of the lemma.

Theorem
3.5
is proved.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\rho_{0}\in(0,1)
. Then, for any
t\in\left(0,\min\left(\frac{1}{2^{\frac{1}{\rho_{0}}}},\frac{1}{e^{\frac{13}{6}}}\right)\right]
, the function
t^{\rho-1}E_{\rho,\rho}(-t^{\rho})
is monotonically decreasing in
\rho\in[\rho_{0},1]
.

The theorem is proved in the same way as Theorem
3.5
.

Using Theorems
3.5
and
3.6
, we can update the results presented in several previous works. In particular, in
[
4
,
7
]
, the inverse problem of determining the order of the fractional derivative is solved under the over-determination condition

(3.10)

||u(x,t_{0})||_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=d_{0},

where
t_{0}
is a sufficiently large number. Our results show that under the over-determination condition (
3.10
), the time instant
t_{0}
can also be chosen sufficiently small, which is quite useful for real-life applications.

4.
Initial-boundary value problem

Let us first give the definition of the solution to the problem (
1.1
).

Definition 4.1
.

A function
u(x,t)
with the properties

(1)

u(x,t)\in C(\overline{\Omega}\times[0.T])
,

(2)

D_{t}^{\rho}u(x,t),\Delta u(x,t)\in C(\overline{\Omega}\times(0.T])
,

and satisfying conditions
(
1.1
) is called
the solution
of the problem (
1.1
).

Now we present theorem about the solution of problem (
1.1
).

Theorem 4.2
.

Let function
\varphi(x)\in I_{M}(\Omega)
. Then problem (
1.1
) has a unique solution:

(4.1)

u(x,t)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{k}E_{\rho}(-\lambda_{k}t^{\rho})v_{k}(x),

where
\varphi_{k}
are the Fourier coefficients of function
\varphi(x)
.

Proof.

According to the Fourier method, we will seek the solution to problem (
1.1
) in the form

u(x,t)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}T_{k}(t)v_{k}(x),

where
T_{k}(t)=(u(\cdot,t),v_{k})
are the Fourier coefficients of the function
u(x,t)
and are unknown.

Multiply the orthonormal eigenfunctions
v_{k}(x)
to the equation in problem (
1.1
), to get

(D_{t}^{\rho}u(\cdot,t),v_{k})+(-\Delta u(\cdot,t),v_{k})=0.

We have
(D_{t}^{\rho}u(\cdot,t),v_{k})=D_{t}^{\rho}T_{k}(t)
and since operator
A
is self-adjoint, then
(-\Delta u(\cdot,t),v_{k})=\lambda_{k}T_{k}(t)
. Therefore, to determine
T_{k}(t)
we obtain the following Cauchy problem

D_{t}^{\rho}T_{k}(t)+\lambda_{k}T_{k}(t)=0,\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994ptT_{k}(0)=\varphi_{k}.

This problem has a unique solution (see, for example,
[
13
]
, p. 174):

T_{k}(t)=\varphi_{k}E_{\rho}(-\lambda_{k}t^{\rho}).

Let us show that the operators
(-\Delta)
and
D_{t}^{\rho}
can be applied term-by-term to series (
4.1
) and the resulting series converges uniformly in
(x,t)\in(\overline{\Omega}\times(0,T])
:

-\Delta u(x,t)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}\varphi_{k}E_{\rho}(-\lambda_{k}t^{\rho})v_{k}(x).

Using Lemma
2.4
and applying the Cauchy-Bunyakovsky inequality we get

|-\Delta u(x,t)|\leq\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{k}\varphi_{k}E_{\rho}(-\lambda_{k}t^{\rho})v_{k}(x)|\leq\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}|\varphi_{k}||v_{k}(x)|\frac{C}{1+\lambda_{k}t^{\rho}}

\leq Ct^{-\rho}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}|\varphi_{k}||v_{k}(x)|=Ct^{-\rho}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}|\varphi_{k}|(\sqrt{\lambda_{k}})^{\left(\left[\frac{N}{2}\right]+1\right)}(\sqrt{\lambda_{k}})^{-\left(\left[\frac{N}{2}\right]+1\right)}|v_{k}(x)|

\leq Ct^{-\rho}\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}|\varphi_{k}|^{2}\lambda_{k}^{\left(\left[\frac{N}{2}\right]+1\right)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{-\left(\left[\frac{N}{2}\right]+1\right)}v_{k}^{2}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

Therefore, if
\varphi(x)\in I_{M}(\Omega)
, then the series
\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}|\varphi_{k}|^{2}\lambda_{k}^{\left(\left[\frac{N}{2}\right]+1\right)}
converges.
Furthermore, according to Lemma
2.1
the series
\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{-\left(\left[\frac{N}{2}\right]+1\right)}v_{k}^{2}(x)
converges uniformly in a closed domain
\overline{\Omega}
. Hence,
-\Delta u(x,t)\in C(\overline{\Omega}\times(0,T])
.

From equation (
1.1
) one has

D_{t}^{\rho}u(x,t)=\Delta u(x,t)
,
t>0
, and hence we get
D_{t}^{\rho}u(x,t)\in C(\overline{\Omega}\times(0,T])
.

The uniqueness of the solution is proved in the standard way (see, for example,
[
3
]
).
∎

5.
Inverse problem

Before moving on to the inverse problem, let us examine the uniform convergence of the following series

(5.1)

\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{k}v_{k}(x)\frac{d}{d\rho}E_{\rho}(-\lambda_{k}t^{\rho}),

where
\varphi_{k}=(\varphi,v_{k})
. One has

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\rho_{0}\in(0,1)
and
t_{1}>0
. If the function
\varphi(x)\in I_{M}(\Omega)
. Then the series (
5.1
) is uniformly convergent with respect to
x\in\overline{\Omega},\,\,t\in[t_{1},T],\,\,\rho\in[\rho_{0},1]
.

Proof.

In Lemma
3.3
the uniform convergence for all
t\in[t_{1},T]
and
\rho\in[\rho_{0},1]
of the series (
3.1
) for
\frac{d}{d\rho}E_{\rho}(-\lambda_{k}t^{\rho})
is proved. Therefore there exists a number
C_{0}
such that
\left|\frac{d}{d\rho}E_{\rho}(-\lambda_{k}t^{\rho})\right|\leq C_{0}
. Next, the uniform convergence of the series (
5.1
) is proved using the same arguments as in the proof of Theorem
4.2
.
Lemma
5.1
is proved.
∎

From Lemma
5.1
we get the following corollary.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
\rho_{0}\in(0,1)
and
t_{1}>0
. Then for any number
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
n_{0}=n_{0}(\varepsilon,M,\rho_{0},t_{1})
such that the following inequality holds for all
x\in\overline{\Omega},\,\,t\in[t_{1},T],\rho\in[\rho_{0},1]
and
\varphi(x)\in I_{M}(\Omega)
:

\sum\limits_{k=n_{0}+1}^{\infty}\left|\varphi_{k}v_{k}(x)\frac{d}{d\rho}E_{\rho}(-\lambda_{k}t^{\rho})\right|<\varepsilon.

Now we move on to the study of the inverse problem. Let
x_{0}\in\Omega
be fixed and we have the measured data at one time instant
t_{0}>0
given as

(5.2)

u(x_{0},t_{0})=d_{0},

where
d_{0}
is a fixed number. If we introduce the following nonlinear function

(5.3)

G(\rho)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{k}E_{\rho}(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho})v_{k}(x_{0}),

then the equation (
5.2
) for determining the unknown parameter
\rho
can be written as
G(\rho)=d_{0}
.

Based on Corollary
5.2
, we introduce the following definition of the solution of the Inverse Problem (
1.1
), (
5.2
).

Definition 5.3
.

The pair
\{u(x,t),\rho\}
of the function
u(x,t)
and the parameter
\rho
with the properties:
\rho\in[\rho_{0},1]
,
\rho_{0}\in(0,1)
,
u(x,t)
satisfies the conditions of Definition
4.1
and together with
\rho
satisfies the over-determination condition (
5.2
) is called a
solution
of the Inverse Problem (
1.1
), (
5.2
).

Now we present the theorem about the solution of Inverse Problem (
1.1
), (
5.2
).

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be a fixed number and

t_{0}\in\left(0,\min\left(\frac{1}{2^{1/\rho_{0}}},\frac{1}{e^{7/2}}\right)\right]
.
Denote
n_{0}=n_{0}(\varepsilon,M,\rho_{0},t_{0})
as in Corollary
5.2
. Suppose that for some
x_{0}\in\Omega
the quantities
v_{k}(x)
and
\varphi_{k}
of
\varphi(x)\in I_{M}(\Omega)
satisfy the following conditions:

(1)

\varphi_{k}\geq 0
and
v_{k}(x_{0})\geq 0
for
k=1,2,\ldots,n_{0}
;

(2)

\varphi_{k_{0}}
and
v_{k_{0}}(x_{0})
satisfying following inequality for some
k_{0}\in\{1,2,\ldots,n_{0}\}

\varphi_{k_{0}}v_{k_{0}}(x_{0})>\frac{\varepsilon}{M_{k_{0}}},

where
M_{k_{0}}=\frac{d}{d\rho}E_{\rho}(-\lambda_{k_{0}}t_{0}^{\rho})
.

Then the nonlinear function
G(\rho)
, defined by (
5.3
), is strictly monotonically increasing on
\rho\in[\rho_{0},1]
. Moreover, Inverse Problem (
1.1
), (
5.2
) has a unique solution if and only if

(5.4)

d_{0}\in[G(\rho_{0}),G(1)].

Proof.

First, we will show that the function
G(\rho)
is strictly monotonic. To demonstrate the monotonicity of the function
G(\rho)
, it is sufficient to show that for any fixed
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in[\rho_{0},1]
with
\rho_{2}>\rho_{1}
, the inequality
G(\rho_{2})-G(\rho_{1})>0
holds.
So

G(\rho_{2})-G(\rho_{1})=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{k}v_{k}(x_{0})[E_{\rho_{2}}(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{2}})-E_{\rho_{1}}(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}})].

Let
\varepsilon>0
be an arbitrary given number, and
n_{0}
be a number from Corollary
5.2
. Then we may write

G(\rho_{2})-G(\rho_{1})=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n_{0}}\varphi_{k}v_{k}(x_{0})[E_{\rho_{2}}(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{2}})-E_{\rho_{1}}(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}})]

+\sum\limits_{k=n_{0}+1}^{\infty}\varphi_{k}v_{k}(x_{0})[E_{\rho_{2}}(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{2}})-E_{\rho_{1}}(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}})]=I_{1}+I_{2}.

Now we estimate the following expression

|I_{2}|=\left|\sum\limits_{k=n_{0}+1}^{\infty}\varphi_{k}v_{k}(x_{0})[E_{\rho_{2}}(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{2}})-E_{\rho_{1}}(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}})]\right|

\leq\sum\limits_{k=n_{0}+1}^{\infty}\left|\varphi_{k}v_{k}(x_{0})[E_{\rho_{2}}(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{2}})-E_{\rho_{1}}(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}})]\right|.

Since
E_{\rho}(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho})
is continuously differentiable with respect to
\rho
at each point
\rho>0
, by Lagrange theorem, there exists
q_{1}=q_{1}(k)\in[\rho_{1},\rho_{2}]
such that (
k>n_{0}
)

E_{\rho_{2}}(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{2}})-E_{\rho_{1}}(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}})=\frac{d}{d\rho}(E_{\rho}(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho}))\bigg|_{\rho=q_{1}}(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1})

:=\frac{d}{d\rho}(E_{q_{1}}(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{q_{1}}))(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1}).

From this we get:

|I_{2}|\leq\sum\limits_{k=n_{0}+1}^{\infty}\left|\varphi_{k}v_{k}(x_{0})\frac{d}{d\rho}E_{q_{1}}(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{q_{1}})\right|(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1})

=(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1})\sum\limits_{k=n_{0}+1}^{\infty}\left|\varphi_{k}v_{k}(x_{0})\frac{d}{d\rho}E_{q_{1}}(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{q_{1}})\right|.

Apply Corollary
5.2
to obtain:

|I_{2}|\leq(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1})\varepsilon.

Let us consider
I_{1}
:

I_{1}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n_{0}}\varphi_{k}v_{k}(x_{0})[E_{\rho_{2}}(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{2}})-E_{\rho_{1}}(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}})].

Again, by Lagrange theorem, there exists
q_{2}=q_{2}(k)\in[\rho_{1},\rho_{2}]
such that (
k\leq n_{0}
)

E_{\rho_{2}}(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{2}})-E_{\rho_{1}}(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho_{1}})=\frac{d}{d\rho}(E_{\rho}(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{\rho}))\bigg|_{\rho=q_{2}}(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1})

:=\frac{d}{d\rho}(E_{q_{2}}(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{q_{2}}))(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1}).

From this and the fact that
\frac{d}{d\rho}E_{q_{2}}(-\lambda_{k}t_{0}^{q_{2}})=M_{k}
, we have

I_{1}=(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1})\sum_{k=1}^{n_{0}}\varphi_{k}v_{k}(x_{0})M_{k}.

If
\varphi_{k}\geq 0
and
v_{k}(x_{0})\geq 0
for
k=1,2,\ldots,n_{0}
, and for some
k_{0}\in(1,2,\ldots,n_{0})
,
\varphi_{k_{0}}>0
and
v_{k_{0}}(x_{0})>0
, then we get

I_{1}>(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1})\varphi_{k_{0}}v_{k_{0}}(x_{0})M_{k_{0}}.

Since
\varphi_{k_{0}}v_{k_{0}}(x_{0})>\frac{\varepsilon}{M_{k_{0}}}
we have
G(\rho_{2})-G(\rho_{1})>0
. Therefore,
G(\rho)
is monotonically increasing.

Obviously, if condition (
5.4
) is not met, then the solution to the Inverse Problem does not exist.

Theorem is proved.
∎

Remark 1.

Let
M_{0}=\min\limits_{1\leq k\leq n_{0},\rho\in[\rho_{0},1]}M_{k}(\rho)
. Then from the given proof it follows that condition (2) of Theorem
5.4
can be replaced by the condition

(5.5)

\sum_{k=1}^{n_{0}}\varphi_{k}v_{k}(x_{0})>\frac{\varepsilon}{M_{0}}.

Note that in Theorem 2 of the paper
[
11
]
by G. Li, Z. Wang, X. Jia, Y. Zhang, in particular, condition (2) is missing. From the proof of this theorem it becomes obvious that if condition (2) (or the condition (
5.5
) in Remark
5
) is missing, then it is easy to construct a counterexample showing that the assertion of the theorem is not satisfied.

6.
An example of Sh.A. Alimov

It should be noted that the formulation of the inverse problem considered in the work
[
11
]
by G. Li, Z. Wang, X. Jia, Y. Zhang is interesting, but Theorem
5.4
requires rather strict conditions on the corresponding eigenfunctions and coefficients of the initial function. In this connection, a natural question arises: does an initial-boundary value problem for the subdiffusion equation exist for which the inverse problem with condition (
5.2
) has a unique solution? The following simple example by Sh.A. Alimov provides a positive answer to this question.

Let
A
be the operator
Av(x,y)=-\Delta v(x,y)
with boundary conditions

(6.1)

\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}=0,\hskip 9.24994pt(x,y)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,H),

and

(6.2)

\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}(x,0)=0,\hskip 9.24994pt\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}(x,H)+hv(x,H)=0,\hskip 9.24994ptx\in\Omega.

Here
\Delta=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{k}^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y^{2}}
is the Laplace operator,
\nu
is an outer normal to
\Omega
and
h>0
.

Condition (
6.1
) means that the boundary of domain
\Omega
is reliably isolated. Condition (
6.2
) means that penetration and diffusion is possible only through one of the bases of the cylindrical region
D=\Omega\times(0,H)
, and the greater the concentration on this base, the greater the penetration flow.

Consider the Cauchy type problem:

(6.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}^{\rho}u(x,y,t)+Au(x,y,t)=0,\hskip 9.24994pt0<t\leq T,\hskip 9.24994pt(x,y)\in D,\\
&\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0}\partial^{\rho-1}u(x,y,t)=\phi(x,y),\hskip 9.24994pt(x,y)\in D,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\phi
is a given continuous function and
\partial^{\rho}_{x}
is the fractional Riemann-Liouville derivative defined as (see, for example,
[
1
]
, p. 70):

\partial_{x}^{\rho}u(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\rho)}\frac{d}{dx}\int\limits_{0}^{x}\frac{u(\xi)}{(x-\xi)^{\rho}}d\xi,\hskip 9.24994ptx>0.

The first eigenvalue of operator
A
is negative:
\lambda_{1}=-\mu^{2}
, where
\mu=\mu(hH)
is the unique positive solution to the equation

\mu\tanh\mu\ =\ hH,

and the corresponding first eigenfunction is positive:
u_{1}(x,y)>0
.

Further reasoning is valid for an arbitrary initial-boundary value problem (
6.3
), in which the operator
A
has these two properties, i.e.

\lambda_{1}<0,\hskip 9.24994pt\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}\leq\lambda_{3}\leq\cdots

and

(6.4)

u_{1}(x,y)>0.

Under certain conditions on the initial function, the unique solution to problem (
6.3
) has the form (see Theorem
4.2
):

(6.5)

u(x,y,t)\ =\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}t^{\rho-1}E_{\rho,\rho}(-\lambda_{m}t^{\rho})(\phi,u_{m})u_{m}(x,y),

which absolutely and uniformly converges on
(x,y)\in\overline{D}

for each
t\in(0,T]
.

Let us consider the inverse problem with the over-determination condition:

(6.6)

U(\rho;t_{0})\equiv u(x_{0},y_{0},t_{0})=d_{0},

at a fixed time instant
t_{0}
and a point
(x_{0},y_{0})\in D
.

One has

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
(x_{0},y_{0})
be a completely arbitrary point in domain
D
and the Fourier coefficient
\phi_{1}=(\phi,u_{1})
of
\phi(x,y)
is not zero. Then there is a number
T_{0}=T_{0}(\rho,\lambda^{*})
,
\lambda^{*}=\min\limits_{j}|\lambda_{j}|
, such that for all
t_{0}\geq T_{0}
the function
U(\rho;t_{0})
is strictly monotone: if
\phi_{1}<0
, then
U(\rho;t_{0})
increases, and if

\phi_{1}>0
, then
U(\rho;t_{0})
decreases as a function of
\rho
.

Naturally, equation (
6.6
) with respect to
\rho
does not have a solution for every
d_{0}
: a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of a solution to this equation is the fulfillment of inequality

(6.7)

\inf\limits_{\rho\in(0,1)}U(\rho,t_{0})<d_{0}<\max\limits_{\rho\in(0,1)}U(\rho,t_{0})

The following theorem gives a positive answer to the above question.

Theorem 6.2
.

Suppose the conditions of Lemma
6.1
are satisfied. Let
d_{0}
satisfy condition (
6.7
) and let
t_{0}\geq T_{0}
. Then inverse problem (
6.3
), (
6.6
) has a unique solution
\{u(x,y,t),\rho\}
.

First, we present a proof of Lemma
6.1
.

Proof.

By virtue of formula (
6.5
), function
U(\rho;t_{0})
has the form

(6.8)

U(\rho;t_{0})\ =\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}t_{0}^{\rho-1}E_{\rho,\rho}(-\lambda_{m}t_{0}^{\rho})\phi_{m}u_{m}(x_{0},y_{0}).

Let us recall the asymptotic formulas (see
[
16
]
, p. 134)

(6.9)

E_{\rho,\rho}(z)=\frac{1}{\rho}z^{\frac{1}{\rho}-1}e^{z^{\frac{1}{\rho}}}+O\bigg(\frac{1}{z}\bigg),\hskip 9.24994ptz>1,

(6.10)

E_{\rho,\rho}(-r)=\frac{r^{-2}}{\Gamma(-\rho)}+O(r^{-3}),\hskip 9.24994ptr>1.

Taking into account the properties of eigenvalues, we conclude that in the sum (
6.8
) in the first few terms the argument of the function
E_{\rho,\rho}
is positive, and then all the rest are negative. Therefore, using estimates (
6.9
) and (
6.10
), it is not hard to see that for
t^{\rho}_{0}\lambda^{*}>1
,
\lambda^{*}=\min\limits_{j}|\lambda_{j}|
,

U(\rho;t_{0})=\phi_{1}u_{1}(x_{0},y_{0})\ \frac{1}{\rho}|\lambda_{1}|^{\frac{1}{\rho}-1}e^{t_{0}|\lambda_{1}|^{\frac{1}{\rho}}}\bigg(1+O(1)e^{-\epsilon_{0}t_{0}^{\rho}}\bigg),\,\,\,\epsilon_{0}=\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}>0.

Since function
f(z)=z|\lambda_{1}|^{z}e^{t_{0}|\lambda_{1}|^{z}}
is increasing and eigenfunction
u_{1}(x,y)
is positive (see (
6.4
)), then function
U(\rho;t_{0})
increases if
\phi_{1}<0
and decreases if
\phi_{1}>0
. Lemma
6.1
is proved.
∎

Theorem
6.2
is a consequence of this lemma.

7.
On a result of A.V. Pskhu

Let us note an interesting result by A.V. Pskhu
[
17
]
, published in 2002, which, as far as we know, represents the first inverse problem for determining the order of fractional derivatives.

Consider the following equation

(7.1)

\partial^{\rho}_{x}u(x)-\lambda u(x)=0,

where,
x\in(0,a],\,\,\rho\in(0,1],\,\,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
\partial^{\rho}_{x}
is the fractional Riemann-Liouville derivative.

Inverse problem for equation (
7.1
).
Find the value of order
\rho,\,\,\rho\in(0,1]
, and solution
u(x)
, of equation (
7.1
) which obeys conditions

(7.2)

u(x_{0})=u_{0},\hskip 9.24994ptu(x_{1})=u_{1},

where
x_{0},\,\,x_{1}\in(0,a],\,\,u_{0},\,\,u_{1}
are given real numbers.

As the author proved, the problem has a unique solution if
x_{0}\neq x_{1}
and

\frac{x_{0}}{x_{1}}<\frac{u_{1}}{u_{0}}\leq e^{\lambda(x_{1}-x_{0})}.

We adopted a slightly different approach to the inverse problem (
7.1
), (
7.2
) and derived the following result.

Let
\rho\in(0,1]
. Consider the following Cauchy problem

(7.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial^{\rho}_{x}u(x)-\lambda u(x)=0,\hskip 9.24994ptx\in(0,1],\\
&\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow 0}\partial_{x}^{\rho-1}u(x)=\varphi,\end{aligned}\right.

where,
\varphi,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
are given numbers.

Inverse problem for problem (
7.3
).
Find the value of order
\rho,\,\,\rho\in(0,1]
, and solution
u(x)
, of problem (
7.3
) which obeys condition

(7.4)

u(x_{0})=u_{0},

where
x_{0}\in(0,1]
and
u_{0}
are given real number.

Note the unique solution to problem (
7.3
) has the form (see, for example,
[
1
]
, p. 224)

(7.5)

u(x)=\varphi x^{\rho-1}E_{\rho,\rho}(\lambda x^{\rho}).

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
x\in(0,1]
and
\lambda>0
. The function
x^{\rho-1}E_{\rho,\rho}(\lambda x^{\rho})
decreases monotonically with respect to
\rho\in(0,1]
.

Proof.

Let
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in(0,1],\,\,\rho_{1}<\rho_{2}
. We have

x^{\rho_{1}-1}E_{\rho_{1},\rho_{1}}(\lambda x^{\rho_{1}})-x^{\rho_{2}-1}E_{\rho_{2},\rho_{2}}(\lambda x^{\rho_{2}})=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{k}x^{\rho_{1}(k+1)-1}}{\Gamma(\rho_{1}(k+1))}

-\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{k}x^{\rho_{2}(k+1)-1}}{\Gamma(\rho_{2}(k+1))}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{k}x^{-1}\left(\frac{x^{\rho_{1}(k+1)}}{\Gamma(\rho_{1}(k+1))}-\frac{x^{\rho_{1}(k+1)}}{\Gamma(\rho_{2}(k+1))}\right).

Now consider the following proportion

Q=\frac{\frac{x^{\rho_{2}(k+1)}}{\Gamma(\rho_{2}(k+1))}}{\frac{x^{\rho_{1}(k+1)}}{\Gamma(\rho_{1}(k+1))}}=x^{(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1})(k+1)}\frac{\Gamma(\rho_{1}(k+1))}{\Gamma(\rho_{2}(k+1))}.

Since
\Gamma(\rho_{1}(k+1))\leq\Gamma(\rho_{2}(k+1))
and
x\in(0,1]
we have
Q\leq 1
for all
k\geq 0
. Therefore,
x^{\rho_{1}-1}E_{\rho_{1},\rho_{1}}(\lambda x^{\rho_{1}})>x^{\rho_{2}-1}E_{\rho_{2},\rho_{2}}(\lambda x^{\rho_{2}})
for all
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in(0,1],\,\,\rho_{1}<\rho_{2}
.

Lemma
7.1
is proved.
∎

The following result is true.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
x_{0}\in(0,1]
and
\lambda>0
. Then the inverse problem (
7.3
), (
7.4
) has a unique solution
\{u(x),\rho\}
if and only if

e^{\lambda x_{0}}\leq\frac{u_{0}}{\varphi}<+\infty.

Proof.

Using (
7.5
) and equality (
7.4
) we get

\varphi x_{0}^{\rho-1}E_{\rho,\rho}(\lambda x_{0}^{\rho})=u_{0}.

Obviously, according to Lemma
7.1
, the inverse problem (
7.3
), (
7.4
) has a unique solution.

Theorem
7.2
is proved.
∎

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
\rho_{0}\in(0,1)
,
x_{0}\in\left(0,\min\left(\frac{1}{2^{\frac{1}{\rho_{0}}}},\frac{1}{e^{\frac{13}{6}}}\right)\right]
and
\lambda<0
. Then the inverse problem (
7.3
), (
7.4
) has a unique solution
\{u(x),\rho\}
if and only if

e^{\lambda x_{0}}\leq\frac{u_{0}}{\varphi}<+\infty.

The proof of this theorem follows from Theorem
3.6
.

A numerical Bernstein splines approach for nonlinear initial value problems with Hilfer fractional derivative
Niels Goedegebure, Kateryna Marynets

Abstract
The Hilfer fractional derivative interpolates the commonly used Riemann-Liouville and Caputo fractional derivative. In general, solutions to Hilfer fractional differential equations are singular for t ↓ 0 t\downarrow 0 and are difficult to approximate with high numerical accuracy. We propose a numerical Bernstein splines technique to approximate solutions to generalized nonlinear initial values problems with Hilfer fractional derivatives. Convergent approximations are obtained using an efficient vectorized solution setup with few convergence requirements for a wide range of nonlinear fractional differential equations. We demonstrate efficiency of the developed method by applying it to the fractional Van der Pol oscillator, a system with applications in control systems and electronic circuits.



Keywords:

fractional initial value problems,
splines,
numerical approximation,
Hilfer fractional derivative,
fractional Van der Pol oscillator.

Introduction

Fractional calculus offers extension of classical differential and integral operators to the non-integer orders.
Particularly of interest are fractional derivatives, generalizing
\alpha
-order differentiation for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
Fractional differential equations (FDEs) involving these fractional derivatives have gained considerable interest from applications in control theory
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
and physical systems
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
.

In the focus of this paper will be a
d
-dimensional fractional initial value problem (FIVP):

\displaystyle\begin{cases}{{}_{0}}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{{\alpha},{\beta}}\mathbf{y}(t)=\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{y}(t)),\quad 0<t\leq T,\\
{{}_{0}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{1-{\gamma}}\mathbf{y}(0)=\mathbf{\tilde{y}}_{0},\end{cases}

(1)

where
\mathbf{y}:(0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
are the unknown vector-valued solution functions for initial values
\mathbf{\tilde{y}}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathbf{f}:(0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a given vector-valued function, in general nonlinear with respect to both of its arguments.
Here,
{{}_{0}}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha,\beta}
denotes the Hilfer fractional derivative of order
\alpha\in(0,1)
and type
\beta\in[0,1]
as introduced in
[
4
]
, and

{{}_{0}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{1-\gamma}
stands for the fractional Riemann-Liouville integral with
\gamma=\alpha+\beta-\alpha\beta
.
Note that in this work, operations
{{}_{0}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}
,
{{}_{0}}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha,\beta}
,
|\cdot|
and
\leq
are applied component-wise.

A general challenge for the modeling and interpretation of FDEs is the type of required initial conditions.
Depending on the chosen fractional differential operator, FDEs can result in fractional derivative or integral initial values, as discussed in
[
7
]
.
The Hilfer fractional differential operator parametrizes this operator choice by interpolating between two commonly used fractional differential operators: Riemann-Liouville for
\beta=0
and Caputo for
\beta=1
.
These operators and their properties are extensively discussed for instance in
[
7
,
8
]
.
Here, the Riemann-Liouville operator results in fractional initial values whereas the Caputo derivative operator yields integer order initial conditions.

We highlight the results of
Furati et al. 2012
, where the authors prove existence and uniqueness of solutions of FIVP (
1
) with Hilfer fractional derivatives for a scalar-valued setting
(d=1)
.
This results can be also expended to the higher dimensional systems for general
d\in\mathbb{N}
.
For
\beta\not=1
and
\mathbf{\tilde{y}}_{0}\not=\mathbf{0}
however, the solutions to this system are in general singular for
t\downarrow 0
.
This provides challenges in implementation for the numerical approximations to solutions compared to the Caputo case of
\beta=1
, for which numerous established methods exist
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
.
Recently, some numerical methods have been proposed to approximate solutions to the general case for Hilfer fractional differential equations.

[
13
]
provide an approach yielding
mild
solutions under conditions for integro-differential equations.

Admon et al. 2023
proposes a method for linear relaxation-oscillations, applying integration on both sides of (
1
) and taking a Caputo approach.
Another linear Hilfer integro-differential is considered by
Naveen et al. 2023
, taking a Lagrange polynomial approach.

In this paper, we propose a new numerical method to efficiently approximate solutions to the nonlinear FIVP (
1
) with Hilfer fractional derivatives.
The method is expanded from thesis work
[
16
]
, using a Bernstein splines approach as motivated by recent results in
[
17
,
18
]
.
To overcome numerical difficulties and singularities, we apply a time-domain transformation of solutions using a
weighing
approach as it was done in
[
9
]
for its weighed norm, together with a time domain transformation resulting in solutions starting from
\varepsilon>0
small.
The new method shows highly satisfactory results with an efficient vectorized implementation and allows for convergence for a wide range of nonlinear problems.

2
Preliminary results on fractional calculus

Definition 2.1

(Riemann-Liouville fractional integral,
[
11
]
)
.

For
\alpha\geq 0
and
a\leq t\leq b
, define the Riemann-Liouville fractional integral as:

\displaystyle{{}_{a}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}f(t)={\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}}\int_{a}^{t}\left(t-s\right)^{\alpha-1}f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

(2)

Furthermore, we use the notational convention
{{}_{a}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}f(a):=\raisebox{2.15277pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\displaystyle\lim_{t\downarrow a}\;$}}{{}_{a}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}f(t)
.

This operator has a number of useful properties:

Proposition 2.1

(Semigroup property of fractional integration,
[
11
]
)
.

For
\alpha,\beta>0
, if
f\in L^{p}[a,b]
with
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we have:

\displaystyle({{}_{a}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}{{}_{a}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\beta})f(t)={{}_{a}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha+\beta}f(t),

(3)

almost everywhere for
t\in[a,b]
.
Furthermore, if
\alpha+\beta>1
, the equality (
3
) holds pointwise.

The following boundedness and continuity result will be useful for further results.

Proposition 2.2

(
L^{p}
boundedness in
f
,
[
11
]
)
.

The Riemann-Liouville integral is bounded in
L^{p}[a,b]
for
f\in L^{p}[a,b]
, with
1\leq p\leq\infty
, satisfying:

\displaystyle||{{}_{a}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}f||_{L^{p}[a,b]}\leq\frac{(b-a)^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}||f||_{L^{p}[a,b]}.

(4)

By linearity of integration, this result also provides continuity in
L^{p}[a,b]
with regards to
f
.

Now, let us introduce two special functions.

Definition 2.2

(Beta function,
[
11
]
)
.

For
a,b>0
, denote the (complete) beta function as:

\displaystyle\mathrm{B}(a,b)=\int_{0}^{1}\vartheta^{a-1}(1-\vartheta)^{b-1}\,\mathrm{d}\vartheta.

(5)

Definition 2.3

(Incomplete beta function,
[
22
]
)
.

For
a,b>0
and
0\leq z\leq 1
, denote the incomplete beta function as:

\displaystyle\mathrm{B}_{z}(a,b)=\int_{0}^{z}\vartheta^{a-1}(1-\vartheta)^{b-1}\,\mathrm{d}\vartheta.

(6)

We now use the beta functions as introduced above to explicitly calculate the fractional integral of a locally supported monomial.

Proposition 2.3

(Fractional integral of monomials)
.

For
\alpha>0
,
k>-1
and
0<b\leq t
, the
\alpha
-fractional integral of a monomial with local support
\mathds{1}_{[0,b]}s^{k}:[0,b]\to\mathbb{R}
is given by:

\displaystyle{{}_{0}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}\{\mathds{1}_{[0,b]}s^{k}\}(t)=\frac{t^{\alpha+k}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\mathrm{B}_{b/t}(k+1,\alpha).

(7)

Proof.

Substituting
z:=\frac{s}{t}
gives:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{b}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}s^{k}\,\mathrm{d}s=\frac{t^{\alpha+k}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{b/t}(1-z)^{\alpha-1}z^{k}\,\mathrm{d}z=\frac{t^{\alpha+k}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\mathrm{B}_{b/t}(k+1,\alpha).

(8)

∎

Remark 2.1
.

Note that by Proposition
2.3
we have:

\displaystyle{{}_{0}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}\mathds{1}_{[0,b]}s^{k}=\begin{cases}\frac{t^{\alpha+k}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\mathrm{B}_{1}(k+1,\alpha)\quad&\textrm{if}\quad t\leq b\\
\frac{t^{\alpha+k}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\mathrm{B}_{b/t}(k+1,\alpha)\quad&\textrm{if}\quad t>b\end{cases}=\,\frac{t^{\alpha+k}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\mathrm{B}_{1\land b/t}(k+1,\alpha).

(9)

Here,
x\land y:=\min(x,y)
.
Hence, the fractional integral of a monomial with local support is not constant after its support, in contrast to the integer-order integration case.

Now, let us establish fractional differentiation.

Definition 2.4

(Hilfer derivative,
[
4
]
)
.

Define the Hilfer fractional derivative of order
0<\alpha<1
and type
0\ \leq\beta\leq 1
for
t\geq 0
as:

\displaystyle{{}_{0}}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha,\beta}={{}_{0}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\beta(1-\alpha)}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}{{}_{0}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{(1-\beta)(1-\alpha)}.

(10)

Remark 2.2

(Interpolation of Caputo and RL-derivative)
.

The Hilfer fractional derivative interpolates two of the most commonly used fractional derivative operators:

\displaystyle{{}_{0}}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\alpha,\beta}=\begin{cases}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}{{}_{0}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{1-\alpha},\quad\text{for}\quad\beta=0,\quad\text{(Riemann-Liouville derivative)},\\
{{}_{0}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{1-\alpha}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t},\quad\text{for}\quad\beta=1,\quad\text{(Caputo derivative)}.\end{cases}

(11)

To obtain solutions to Hilfer fractional differential equations,
Furati et al. 2012
introduce the following space of functions continuous when
weighed
:

C_{1-\gamma}[0,T]=\{f:(0,T]\to\mathbb{R}:t^{\gamma-1}f\in C[0,T]\},

together with the following fractionally differentiable subspace:

\displaystyle C^{\zeta}_{1-\gamma}[0,T]=\{f\in C_{1-\gamma}[0,T],\,{{}_{0}}\mathrm{D}_{t}^{\zeta,0}f(t)\in C_{1-\gamma}[0,T]\}.

(12)

For IVP (
1
), the following existence and uniqueness result holds:

Theorem 2.1

(Existence and uniqueness
[
9
]
)
.

If
\mathbf{f}:(0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfies:

1.

\mathbf{f}(\cdot,\mathbf{y}(\cdot))\in C^{{\beta}(1-{\alpha})}_{1-{\gamma}}[0,T]
for any
\mathbf{y}\in C_{1-{\gamma}}[0,T]
,

2.

\mathbf{f}
is Lipschitz in its second argument.
For some nonnegative matrix
K\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{d\times d}
:

\displaystyle|\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{y}_{a}(t))-\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{y}_{b}(t))|\leq K|\mathbf{y}_{a}(t)-\mathbf{y}_{b}(t)|,

(13)

for all
t\in(0,T]
,
\mathbf{y}_{a},\mathbf{y}_{b}\in G\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Then, there exists a unique solution
\mathbf{y}\in C_{1-\gamma}^{\gamma}[0,T]
to IVP (
1
).
Furthermore,
\mathbf{y}
is a solution if and only if it satisfies the equivalent integral equation:

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}(t)=\frac{\mathbf{\tilde{y}}_{0}}{\Gamma(\gamma)}t^{\gamma-1}+{{}_{0}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{y}(t)),\quad t>0.

(14)

Proof.

The proof follows from Theorem 23 and 25 of
Furati et al. 2012
, applied component-wise for vector-valued functions in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
∎

3
Auxiliary spline theory results

In this section, we establish splines approximation operator results from Bernstein polynomials (Section
3.1
) extending to locally supported Bernstein polynomials as splines (Section
3.2
).

3.1
Bernstein polynomials

Definition 3.1

(Bernstein polynomial operator
[
23
]
)
.

For a function
f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
, define the Bernstein operator of order
q\in\mathbb{N}
as:

\displaystyle B^{q}f(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{q}a_{j}b_{j,q}(s_{[a,b]}(t)),

(15)

with coefficients

a_{j}=f(a+{j(b-a)}/{q}),

Bernstein basis functions
b_{j,q}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
given by
b_{j,q}(s)=\sum_{\ell=j}^{q}{\binom{q}{\ell}}{\binom{\ell}{j}}(-1)^{\ell-j}s^{\ell}

and basis domain map
s_{[a,b]}:[a,b]\to[0,1]
given by
s_{[a,b]}(t)=\frac{t-a}{b-a}
.

Remark 3.1

(Bernstein operator at endpoints)
.

For a function
f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
,
direct calculation using Definition
3.1
gives:

\displaystyle B^{q}f(a)=f(a),\quad\mathrm{and}\quad B^{q}f(b)=f(b).

(16)

To assess the accuracy of approximations for all
t\in(a,b)
, let us consider the modulus of continuity.

Definition 3.2

(Modulus of continuity)
.

For a continuous function
f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
, the modulus of continuity
\omega
is defined as:

\displaystyle\omega\,(f;\delta)=\sup_{|t-s|\leq\delta}|f(t)-f(s)|,\quad\textrm{ for }t,s\in[a,b].

(17)

We can now obtain a bound on the accuracy of the approximation of a continuous function
f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 3.1

(Bernstein operator error bound
[
23
]
)
.

For a continuous function
f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
, the following error estimate holds:

\displaystyle|f(t)-B^{q}f(t)|\leq\frac{5}{4}\,\omega\left(f;\frac{(b-a)}{\sqrt{q}}\right),\quad\textrm{for all }t\in[a,b].

(18)

3.2
Bernstein splines

To obtain a polynomial approximation for longer time intervals without increasing order
q
, a splines approach is taken.
We subdivide the time domain:

Definition 3.3

(Knot collection)
.

Given a closed interval
[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R}
, take a knot collection
\mathcal{A}
of size
k+1
as the collection of disjoint intervals:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\{A_{0},...,A_{k}\}=\{[t_{0},t_{1}),...,[t_{k-1},t_{k}),[t_{k},t_{k+1}]\},

(19)

such that
\bigcup_{A_{i}\in\mathcal{A}}A_{i}=[a,b].

Now, using Definition
3.1
, we establish the Bernstein splines operator as follows:

Definition 3.4

(Bernstein spline operator)
.

For a function
f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
, and a knot collection
\mathcal{A}
of size
k+1
, the order
q
Bernstein spline operator is given by:

\displaystyle S^{q}_{\mathcal{A}}f(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\mathds{1}_{A_{i}}\,B^{q}\,\{\mathds{1}_{\bar{A}_{i}}f\}(t),

(20)

where
\bar{A}_{i}
denotes the closure of
A_{i}
.

Proposition 3.1

(Continuity of the Bernstein splines operator)
.

If
f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous, then
S^{q}_{\mathcal{A}}f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous.

Proof.

First, note that for every
t\in A_{i}\in\mathcal{A}
, we have that
S^{q}_{\mathcal{A}}f:A_{i}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous by continuity of the Bernstein polynomials as can quickly be seen from Definition
3.1
.
Now, to prove
S^{q}_{\mathcal{A}}f
is not merely piecewise continuous but continuous on the whole interval
[a,b]
, take an arbitrary internal knot point
t_{j}
with
j\in\{1,...,k\}
.
Then, taking
t\in A_{j-1}=[t_{j-1},t_{j})
gives:

\displaystyle\lim_{t\uparrow t_{j}}S^{q}_{\mathcal{A}}f(t)=\lim_{t\uparrow t_{j}}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\mathds{1}_{A_{i}}\,B^{q}\,\{\mathds{1}_{\bar{A}_{i}}f\}(t)=\lim_{t\uparrow t_{j}}\,B^{q}\,\{\mathds{1}_{[t_{j-1},t_{j}]}f\}(t)=f(t_{j}).

(21)

Here, the last equality follows from the result of Remark
3.1
.
Similarly, taking
t\in A_{j}=[t_{j},t_{j+1})
, we have:

\lim_{t\downarrow t_{j}}S^{q}_{\mathcal{A}}f(t)=\lim_{t\downarrow t_{j}}\,B^{q}\,\{\mathds{1}_{[t_{j},t_{j+1}]}f\}(t)=f(t_{j})

and hence

\lim_{t\downarrow t_{j}}S^{q}_{\mathcal{A}}f(t)=\lim_{t\uparrow t_{j}}S^{q}_{\mathcal{A}}f(t),

proving continuity at internal knot points, showing continuity on the whole interval
[a,b]
.
∎

We can now give an error bound in approximation using the splines operator:

Theorem 3.2

(Bernstein splines error bound)
.

Let
f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function with a defined modulus of continuity
\omega
.
Let
h=\sup_{A_{i}\in\mathcal{A}}|A_{i}|
be the largest knot size of knot collection
\mathcal{A}
.
Then, for the Bernstein spline operator of order
q
, it holds that for
a\leq t\leq b
:

\displaystyle|f(t)-S^{q}_{\mathcal{A}}f(t)|=

\displaystyle\leq\frac{5}{4}\,\omega\left(f;\frac{h}{\sqrt{q}}\right).

(22)

Proof.

The proof follows from combining the Bernstein splines operator of Definition
3.4
with the polynomial error bound of Theorem
3.1
.
∎

Definition 3.5

(Spline fractional integration operator)
.

For
u:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
continuous and knot collection
\mathcal{A}
,
introduce the order
0<\alpha<1
splines fractional integration operator
{{}_{a}^{q}}\Phi_{t}^{\alpha}
as:

\displaystyle{{}_{a}^{q}}\Phi_{t}^{\alpha}u(t)=S^{q}_{\mathcal{A}}\,{{}_{a}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}u(t),\quad\textrm{ for }a\leq t\leq b,

(23)

where
S^{q}_{\mathcal{A}}
is the spline operator as defined in Definition
3.4
.

To provide error bounds, we can use the results as obtained above:

Lemma 3.1

(Error of the spline integration operator)
.

Take
u:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
continuous. Then, for
a\leq t\leq b
:

\displaystyle||{{}_{a}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}u-{{}_{a}^{q}}\Phi_{t}^{\alpha}u||_{\infty}\leq\left(\frac{h}{\sqrt{q}}\right)^{\alpha}\frac{5\,||u||_{\infty}}{2\,\Gamma(\alpha+1)},

(24)

where
h
is the maximum knot size
h=\sup_{A_{i}\in\mathcal{A}}|A_{i}|
and
||\cdot||_{\infty}=\sup_{t\in[a,b]}|\cdot|
.

Proof.

By the Definition
3.5
and Theorem
3.2
we have:

\displaystyle|{{}_{a}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}u(t)-{{}_{a}^{q}}\Phi_{t}^{\alpha}u(t)|=\left|{{}_{a}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}\,u(t)-S^{q}_{\mathcal{A}}\,{{}_{a}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}u(t)\right|\leq\frac{5}{4}\,\omega\left({{}_{a}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}u(t);\frac{h}{\sqrt{q}}\right).

(25)

Now, let us bound the modulus of continuity.
Take
a\leq t<t^{\prime}\leq b
. Then:

\displaystyle|{{}_{a}}\mathrm{I}_{t^{\prime}}^{\alpha}u(t^{\prime})-{{}_{a}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}u(t)|=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\bigg|\int_{a}^{t^{\prime}}u(s)(t^{\prime}-s)^{\alpha-1}\,\mathrm{d}s-\int_{a}^{t}u(s)(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg|

(26)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\bigg|\int_{a}^{t}u(s)\left[(t^{\prime}-s)^{\alpha-1}-(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\right]\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}u(s)(t^{\prime}-s)^{\alpha-1}\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg|.

(27)

Since
a-1\in(-1,0)
and
(t^{\prime}-s)>(t-s)
, we have
(t^{\prime}-s)^{\alpha-1}-(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\geq 0
for
a\leq s\leq t
.
Furthermore,
(t^{\prime}-s)^{\alpha-1}\geq 0
.
Hence, taking
||u||_{\infty}:=\sup_{s\in[a,b]}|u(s)|
, we can apply the triangle inequality and Hölder’s inequality twice, yielding:

\displaystyle|{{}_{a}}\mathrm{I}_{t^{\prime}}^{\alpha}u(t^{\prime})-{{}_{a}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}u(t)|

(28)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{a}^{t}\left((t^{\prime}-s)^{\alpha-1}-(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\right)\mathrm{d}s\,||u||_{\infty}+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}(t^{\prime}-s)^{\alpha-1}\mathrm{d}s\,||u||_{\infty}

(29)

\displaystyle=\frac{-\left((t^{\prime}-a)^{\alpha}-(t-a)^{\alpha}\right)+2(t^{\prime}-t)^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}||u||_{\infty}\leq\frac{2(t^{\prime}-t)^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}||u||_{\infty}.

(30)

Here, the final inequality follows since
t^{\prime}>t
gives
(t^{\prime}-a)^{\alpha}-(t-a)^{\alpha}>0
.
Finally, taking the maximum distance
t^{\prime}-t=h
gives:

\displaystyle\omega\left({{}_{a}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}u(t);\frac{h}{\sqrt{q}}\right)=\frac{2(hq^{-1/2})^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}||u||_{\infty},

(31)

resulting in the error bound and concluding the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.2

(Bound of the spline integration operator)
.

Take a continuous function
u:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
.
Then, for
a\leq t\leq b
,
{{}_{a}^{q}}\Phi_{t}^{\alpha}
is bounded. In particular,

\displaystyle||{{}_{a}^{q}}\Phi_{t}^{\alpha}u||_{\infty}\leq\Psi||u||_{\infty},

(32)

with
\Psi=\frac{\frac{5}{2}\left(\frac{h}{\sqrt{q}}\right)^{\alpha}+(t-a)^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}
.
Here,
||\,\cdot\,||_{\infty}:=\sup_{t\in[a,b]}|\,\cdot\,|
,
h
is the maximum knot size
h=\sup_{A_{i}\in\mathcal{A}}|A_{i}|
and
q
the order of Bernstein basis polynomial splines.
Furthermore, since
{{}_{a}^{q}}\Phi_{t}^{\alpha}
is linear, this also gives continuity in
u
.

Proof.

First, write:

\displaystyle||{{}_{a}^{q}}\Phi_{t}^{\alpha}u||_{\infty}\leq||{{}_{a}^{q}}\Phi_{t}^{\alpha}u-{{}_{a}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}u+{{}_{a}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}u||_{\infty}\leq||{{}_{a}^{q}}\Phi_{t}^{\alpha}u-{{}_{a}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}u||_{\infty}+||{{}_{a}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}u||_{\infty}.

(33)

Then, by the bounds of Lemma
3.1
and Proposition
2.2
for
a\leq t
we have:

\displaystyle||{{}_{a}^{q}}\Phi_{t}^{\alpha}u||_{\infty}\leq\frac{\frac{5}{2}\left(\frac{h}{\sqrt{q}}\right)^{\alpha}+(t-a)^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}||u||_{\infty},

(34)

providing the operator bound.
∎

4
Spline approximation results

4.1
Convergence results

In this section, we prove convergence results of the splines approximations for FIVP (
1
).
Note that for vector-valued functions, operators
{{}_{0}^{q}}\Phi_{t}^{\alpha}
and
S^{q}_{\mathcal{A}}
are considered component-wise.
To establish a numerical method to approximate solutions of FIVP (
1
) for all Hilfer derivative type values
0\leq\beta\leq 1
, we first consider the Caputo-derivative case of
\beta=1
:

Lemma 4.1

(Caputo-derivative splines approximations)
.

Consider FIVP (
1
) for
0\leq t\leq T
and
\beta=1
and assume that the requirements of existence and uniqueness of Theorem
2.1
hold.
Then, for a knot collection
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{0},...,A_{k}\}
, we can construct an order
q\in\mathbb{N}
Bernstein spline approximation
\mathbf{y}^{q}_{j,\mathbf{n}}:[0,t_{j}]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
as:

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}^{q}_{j,\mathbf{n}}(t)=\mathbf{\tilde{y}}_{0}+{{}_{0}^{q}}\Phi_{t}^{\alpha}\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{y}^{q}_{j,\mathbf{n}}(t)),\quad 0\leq t\leq t_{j},\quad\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{j},

(35)

converging to
\mathbf{y}^{q}:=\raisebox{2.15277pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\displaystyle\lim_{\mathbf{n}\to\infty}\;$}}\mathbf{y}_{k,\mathbf{n}}^{q}
if:

•

||\Psi{K}||_{\infty}<1
, where

\Psi=\frac{\frac{5}{2}\left(\frac{h}{\sqrt{q}}\right)^{\alpha}+h^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}

and
h=\sup_{A_{i}\in\mathcal{A}}|A_{i}|
.

Furthermore, we have:

•

Continuity of intermediate approximations.
For all iterations we have
\mathbf{y}^{q}_{j,\mathbf{n}}\in C[0,t_{j}]
for all
j
and
\mathbf{n}
.

•

Convergence in polynomial order.
The limit
\lim_{q\to\infty}\mathbf{y}^{q}

converges to analytical solution
\mathbf{y}
with order:

\displaystyle\quad\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\mathbf{y}^{q}(t)-\mathbf{y}(t)\right|=\mathcal{O}({q}^{-{\alpha}/{2}})

(36)

•

Convergence in knot size.
The limit
\lim_{h\to 0}\mathbf{y}^{q}

converges to analytical solution
\mathbf{y}
with order:

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\mathbf{y}^{q}(t)-\mathbf{y}(t)\right|=\mathcal{O}(h^{\alpha})

(37)

Proof.

Construct the solution spline approximation sequence
\mathbf{y}^{q}_{j,\mathbf{n}}:[0,t_{j}]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
as:

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}^{q}_{j,\mathbf{n}}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{j}\mathbf{u}^{q}_{i,n_{i}}\mathds{1}_{A_{i}}(t)=\begin{cases}\mathbf{u}^{q}_{0,n_{0}}(t),\quad\textrm{if}\quad&t\in A_{0},\\
\mathbf{u}^{q}_{1,n_{1}}(t),\quad\textrm{if}\quad&t\in A_{1},\\
\quad\vdots\\
\mathbf{u}^{q}_{j,n_{j}}(t),\quad\textrm{if}\quad&t\in A_{j}.\\
\end{cases}

(38)

Here,
\mathbf{u}_{i,n}:A_{i}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfies:

\displaystyle(\mathbf{u}^{q}_{i,{n+1}}(t))_{n\geq 0}=\mathbf{\tilde{y}}_{0}+{{}_{0}^{q}}\Phi_{t}^{\alpha}\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{y}^{q}_{i,\mathbf{n}}(t)),\quad\mathbf{n}_{i}=n,\quad t\in A_{i},

(39)

where
{{}_{0}^{q}}\Phi_{t}^{\alpha}:=S_{\mathcal{A}}^{q}{{}_{0}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}
is the splines integration operator as defined in Definition
3.5
.
For initial iteration values, take:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathbf{u}^{q}_{i,0}:={\mathbf{\tilde{y}}}_{0},\quad&\textrm{for}\quad i=0,\\
\mathbf{u}^{q}_{i,0}:=\raisebox{2.15277pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\displaystyle\lim_{t\uparrow t_{i}}\;$}}\mathbf{u}^{q}_{i-1,\mathbf{n}_{i-1}}(t),\quad&\textrm{for}\quad i\geq 1.\end{cases}

(40)

Then, the limit of the splines approximation sequence on the whole interval
[0,T]
is given by:

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}^{q}(t):=\mathbf{y}^{q}_{k,\infty}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbf{u}^{q}_{i,n}\mathds{1}_{A_{i}}(t).

(41)

To prove convergence of (
41
), we first need to show that all iterations
\mathbf{y}^{q}_{j,\mathbf{n}}
are continuous for all
q,j,\mathbf{n}
.
Take
q\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then, for
j=0
, we know that
\mathbf{y}^{q}_{0,{n}_{0}}
is continuous for all
{n}_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
since
\mathbf{y}^{q}_{0,0}
is continuous by (
40
) as it is a constant function, and for
{n}_{0}\geq 1
, continuity is obtained by the (Lipschitz) continuity of
\mathbf{f}
and Lemma
3.2
.
Then, for
\mathbf{y}^{q}_{1,{n}_{1}}
, the initial iteration
\mathbf{y}^{q}_{1,0}
is again continuous by (
40
) since:

\displaystyle\lim_{t\downarrow t_{1}}\mathbf{y}^{q}_{1,0}(t)=\mathbf{u}^{q}_{1,0}(t_{1})=\lim_{t\uparrow t_{1}}\mathbf{u}^{q}_{0,n_{0}}(t)=\lim_{t\uparrow t_{1}}\mathbf{y}^{q}_{1,0}(t).

(42)

Then, for
t\in[t_{1},t_{2})
, first iteration
\mathbf{y}^{q}_{1,0}(t)
is continuous by the continuity of the constant function
\mathbf{u}^{q}_{1,0}
.
Repeating the same argument as above, we obtain continuity of
\mathbf{y}^{q}_{1,\mathbf{n}}
for
n_{1}\geq 1
.
Finally, repeating the same argumentation for
j\geq 2
, we obtain continuity for all
\mathbf{y}^{q}_{j,\mathbf{n}}
.

Now we can show the convergence of the limit inside the summation of (
41
).
Lemma
3.2
gives a bound for
{{}_{a}^{q}}\Phi_{t}^{\alpha}
for
a\leq t\leq b
such that for
t\in A_{i}
with
h_{i}:=t_{i+1}-t_{i}
we have:

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in A_{i}}\left|{{}_{t_{i}}^{q}}\Phi_{t}^{\alpha}u(t)\right|\leq\Psi_{i}\sup_{t\in A_{i}}\left|u(t)\right|,\quad\text{where}\quad\Psi_{i}=\frac{\frac{5}{2}\left(\frac{h_{i}}{\sqrt{q}}\right)^{\alpha}+h_{i}^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}.

(43)

Now, extending this inequality component-wise to vector-valued functions, we have for
i\in\{0,...,k\}
:

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in A_{i}}\left|\mathbf{u}^{q}_{i,n+1}(t)-\mathbf{u}^{q}_{i,n}(t)\right|

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\in A_{i}}\left|{{}_{0}^{q}}\Phi_{t}^{\alpha}\{\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{y}^{q}_{i,\mathbf{n}}(t))-\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{y}^{q}_{i,\mathbf{n}-1}(t))\}\right|

(44)

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\in A_{i}}\left|{{}_{t_{i}}^{q}}\Phi_{t}^{\alpha}\{\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{u}^{q}_{i,{n}}(t))-\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{u}^{q}_{i,{n}-1}(t))\}\right|

(45)

\displaystyle\leq\Psi_{i}\sup_{t\in A_{i}}\left|\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{u}^{q}_{i,{n}}(t))-\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{u}^{q}_{i,{n}-1}(t))\right|

(46)

\displaystyle\leq\Psi K\sup_{t\in A_{i}}\left|\mathbf{u}^{q}_{i,{n}}(t)-\mathbf{u}^{q}_{i,{n}-1}(t)\right|.

(47)

Since
||\Psi K||_{\infty}<1
, Banach’s fixed point theorem gives uniform convergence.
Hence, given
i\in\{0,...,k\}
, we have convergence for the sequence
(\mathbf{u}^{q}_{i,n})_{n\geq 0}
as
n\to\infty
.
From (
38
) and (
39
) it now follows that
\mathbf{y}^{q}:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfies:
\mathbf{y}^{q}(t)=\mathbf{\tilde{y}}_{0}+{{}_{0}^{q}}\Phi_{t}^{\alpha}\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{y}^{q}(t)).

Furthermore,
\mathbf{y}^{q}\in C[0,T]
since it is the uniform limit of a sequence of continuous functions.

We now use the above results to obtain a convergence order estimate in
q
and
h
.
Taking an analytical solution
\mathbf{y}:(0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
of the IVP (
1
) with equivalent integral equation (
14
), write:

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}^{q}(t)-\mathbf{y}(t)=({{}_{0}^{q}}\Phi_{t}^{\alpha}-{{}_{0}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha})\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{y}^{q}(t))+{{}_{0}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}\{\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{y}^{q}(t))-\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{y})\}.

(48)

And thus:

\displaystyle\overline{|\mathbf{y}^{q}(t)-\mathbf{y}(t)|}\leq\left(\frac{h}{\sqrt{q}}\right)^{\alpha}\frac{5\,\overline{||\mathbf{f}||}_{\infty}}{2\,\Gamma(\alpha+1)}+||K||_{\infty}\,{{}_{0}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}\overline{|\mathbf{y}^{q}(t)-\mathbf{y}(t)|},

(49)

where
\overline{\mathbf{v}}:=\sup_{i\in\{1,...,d\}}v_{i}
denotes the supremum over all dimension vector components and
||K||_{\infty}
denotes the supremum-matrix norm as the sum over each row, making (
49
) a scalar equation.
Furthermore, since
\left(\frac{h}{\sqrt{q}}\right)^{\alpha}\frac{5\,\overline{||\mathbf{f}||}_{\infty}}{2\,\Gamma(\alpha+1)}
does not depend on
t
, we can apply the fractional Grönwall’s inequality (Corollary 2,
[
24
]
):

\displaystyle\overline{|\mathbf{y}^{q}(t)-\mathbf{y}(t)|}\leq\left(\frac{h}{\sqrt{q}}\right)^{\alpha}\frac{5\,\overline{||\mathbf{f}||}_{\infty}}{2\,\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\mathrm{E}_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha}||K||_{\infty}),

(50)

where
\mathrm{E}_{\alpha}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{j}}{\Gamma(j\alpha+1)}
denotes the Mittag-Leffer function.
Then, since
\mathrm{E}_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha}||K||_{\infty})
is independent of
q
and
h
, and since the supremum is taken over all dimensions, we obtain convergence orders of:

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\mathbf{y}^{q}(t)-\mathbf{y}(t)\right|=\mathcal{O}(h^{\alpha}),\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\mathbf{y}^{q}(t)-\mathbf{y}(t)\right|=\mathcal{O}({q}^{-{\alpha}/{2}}).

(51)

This concludes the proof by providing a proof for the final two statements.
∎

We can now construct approximations for the general case of the Hilfer derivative:

Theorem 4.1

(General Hilfer derivative spline approximations)
.

Consider FIVP (
1
) for
0\leq t\leq T
and
0\leq\beta<1
.
Assume the requirements of existence and uniqueness of Theorem
2.1
hold.
Then, for a knot collection
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{0},...,A_{k}\}
on the interval
[\varepsilon,T]
we can construct an order
q\in\mathbb{N}
Bernstein spline approximation
\mathbf{y}^{q,\varepsilon}_{j,\mathbf{n}}:[\varepsilon,t_{j}]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
as:

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}^{q,\varepsilon}_{j,\mathbf{n}}(t)=\mathbf{\tilde{y}}_{0}+{{}_{\varepsilon}^{q}}\Phi_{t}^{\alpha}\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{y}^{q,\varepsilon}_{j,\mathbf{n}}(t)),\quad 0\leq t\leq t_{j},\quad\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{j},

(52)

converging to
\mathbf{y}^{q,\varepsilon}:=\raisebox{2.15277pt}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\displaystyle\lim_{\mathbf{n}\to\infty}\;$}}\mathbf{y}_{k,\mathbf{n}}^{q,\varepsilon}
if:

•

||(1+\frac{h_{i}}{t_{i}})^{1-\gamma}\,\Psi_{i}\,K||_{\infty}<1
, where
\Psi_{i}=\frac{\frac{5}{2}\left(\frac{h_{i}}{\sqrt{q}}\right)^{\alpha}+h_{i}^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}
, with
h_{i}:=t_{i+1}-t_{i}
,
i\in\{0,...,k\}
.

Furthermore, the approximations satisfy:

•

Continuity of intermediate approximations.
For all iterations we have
\mathbf{y}^{q,\varepsilon}_{j,\mathbf{n}}\in C[\varepsilon,t_{j}]
for all
j
and
\mathbf{n}
.

•

Convergence in polynomial order.
The limit
\lim_{q\to\infty}\mathbf{y}^{q,\varepsilon}

converges to
\mathbf{y}^{\varepsilon}
with order:

\displaystyle\quad\sup_{t\in[\varepsilon,T]}\left|t^{1-\gamma}\mathbf{y}^{q,\varepsilon}(t)-t^{1-\gamma}\mathbf{y}^{\varepsilon}(t)\right|=\mathcal{O}({q}^{-{\alpha}/{2}}).

(53)

•

Convergence in knot size.
The limit
\lim_{h\to 0}\mathbf{y}^{q,\varepsilon}

converges to
\mathbf{y}^{\varepsilon}
with order:

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[\varepsilon,T]}\left|t^{1-\gamma}\mathbf{y}^{q,\varepsilon}(t)-t^{1-\gamma}\mathbf{y}^{\varepsilon}(t)\right|=\mathcal{O}(h^{\alpha}).

(54)

Furthermore, if
\alpha+\gamma>1
, we have:

•

Convergence to the analytical solution.

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[\varepsilon,T]}|\mathbf{y}^{\varepsilon}(t)-\mathbf{y}(t)|=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{\alpha+\gamma-1}).

(55)

Proof.

First, we will show that by the assumption of
\mathbf{f}\in C_{1-\gamma}[0,T]
, we also have
\mathbf{f}\in C[\varepsilon,T]
.
Since
\mathbf{f}\in C_{1-\gamma}[0,T]
, we have
\mathbf{h}:=t^{1-\gamma}\mathbf{f}\in C[0,T]
.
Then, for
\varepsilon\leq t\leq T
, write
\mathbf{f}:=t^{\gamma-1}\mathbf{h}
.
Since
t^{\gamma-1}\in C[\varepsilon,T]
because a singularity only appears for
t\downarrow 0
and since
\mathbf{h}\in C[\varepsilon,T]
, we have that the product must be in
C[\varepsilon,T]
as well.
Hence,
\mathbf{f}\in C[\varepsilon,T]
.

Now, consider the equivalent integral equation (
14
) of IVP (
1
).
Taking the transformation
\mathbf{v}(t):=t^{1-\gamma}\mathbf{y}(t)
then gives:

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}(t)=\mathbf{\tilde{v}_{0}}+{{}_{0}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}\mathbf{g}(t,s,\mathbf{v}(s)),\quad t>0.

(56)

Here,
\mathbf{\tilde{v}}_{0}:=\frac{\mathbf{\tilde{y}}_{0}}{\Gamma(\gamma)}
and
\mathbf{g}(t,s,\mathbf{v}(s)):=t^{1-\gamma}\mathbf{f}(s,s^{\gamma-1}\mathbf{v}(s))
, where
s\leq t
is introduced as the variable of integration for notational clarity.
Now, consider the
\varepsilon
-shifted integral equation:

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}^{\varepsilon}(t)=\mathbf{\tilde{v}_{0}}+{{}_{\varepsilon}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}\mathbf{g}(t,s,\mathbf{v}^{\varepsilon}(s)),\quad t\geq\varepsilon.

(57)

Note that
\mathbf{g}
is Lipschitz in
\mathbf{v}^{\varepsilon}
for
\varepsilon\leq s\leq t
since
\mathbf{f}
is Lipschitz.
Furthermore,
\mathbf{g}\in C[\varepsilon,T]
since
\mathbf{f}\in C[\varepsilon,T]
.
Then, existence and uniqueness of
\mathbf{v}^{\varepsilon}\in C[\varepsilon,T]
in equation (
57
) can then be obtained by Theorem 23 and 25 of
Furati et al. 2012
in the Caputo case of
\beta=1
and taking
a=\varepsilon
.

To obtain approximations for
\mathbf{v}^{\varepsilon}
of (
57
), we follow the same proof as in Lemma
4.1
.
We are left to show we can obtain a fixed point contraction mapping for the transformed system.
Write:

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in A_{i}}\left|\mathbf{u}^{q,\varepsilon}_{i,n+1}(t)-\mathbf{u}^{q,\varepsilon}_{i,n}(t)\right|

(58)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in A_{i}}\left|{{}_{0}^{q}}\Phi_{t}^{\alpha}\{\mathbf{g}(t,s,\mathbf{v}^{q,\varepsilon}_{i,\mathbf{n}}(t))-\mathbf{g}(t,s,\mathbf{v}^{q,\varepsilon}_{i,\mathbf{n}-1}(t))\}\right|

(59)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in A_{i}}\left|{{}_{t_{i}}^{q}}\Phi_{t}^{\alpha}\{\mathbf{g}(t,s,\mathbf{u}^{q,\varepsilon}_{i,{n}}(t))-\mathbf{g}(t,s,\mathbf{u}^{q,\varepsilon}_{i,{n}-1}(t))\}\right|

(60)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Psi_{i}\sup_{s,t\in A_{i}}\left|\mathbf{g}(t,s,\mathbf{u}^{q,\varepsilon}_{i,{n}}(s))-\mathbf{g}(t,s,\mathbf{u}^{q,\varepsilon}_{i,{n}-1}(s))\right|

(61)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi_{i}\sup_{s,t\in A_{i}}\left|t^{1-\gamma}\mathbf{f}(s,s^{\gamma-1}\mathbf{u}^{q,\varepsilon}_{i,{n}}(s))-t^{1-\gamma}\mathbf{f}(s,s^{\gamma-1}\mathbf{u}^{q,\varepsilon}_{i,{n}-1}(s))\right|

(62)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Psi_{i}{t}_{i+1}^{1-\gamma}\sup_{s\in A_{i}}\left|\mathbf{f}(s,s^{\gamma-1}\mathbf{u}^{q,\varepsilon}_{i,{n}}(s))-\mathbf{f}(s,s^{\gamma-1}\mathbf{u}^{q,\varepsilon}_{i,{n}-1}(s))\right|

(63)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Psi_{i}{t}_{i+1}^{1-\gamma}K\sup_{s\in A_{i}}\left|s^{\gamma-1}\mathbf{u}^{q,\varepsilon}_{i,{n}}(s)-s^{\gamma-1}\mathbf{u}^{q,\varepsilon}_{i,{n}-1}(s)\right|

(64)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Psi_{i}\left(1+\frac{{h}_{i}}{t_{i}}\right)^{1-\gamma}K\sup_{s\in A_{i}}\left|\mathbf{u}^{q,\varepsilon}_{i,{n}}(s)-\mathbf{u}^{q,\varepsilon}_{i,{n}-1}(s)\right|.

(65)

Since by assumption
||\Psi_{i}\left(1+\frac{{h}_{i}}{t_{i}}\right)^{1-\gamma}K||_{\infty}<1
, we thus have convergence of approximations
\mathbf{u}^{q,\varepsilon}\in C[\varepsilon,T]
to the shifted equation (
57
), satisfying convergence in
{h}
and
q
as specified in the Caputo case of Lemma
4.1
.
Furthermore, by the same argument as stated above for continuity of
\mathbf{f}\in C[\varepsilon,T]
, we know intermediate approximations
\mathbf{y}^{q,\varepsilon}_{j,\mathbf{n}}\in C[\varepsilon,t_{j}]
since
\mathbf{v}^{q,\varepsilon}_{j,\mathbf{n}}\in C[\varepsilon,t_{j}]
.

Finally, to establish the connection to the analytical solution
\mathbf{y}
, consider the back-transformed shifted solution
\mathbf{y}^{\varepsilon}:=t^{\gamma-1}\mathbf{v}^{\varepsilon}:[\varepsilon,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Hence, by (
57
),
\mathbf{y}^{\varepsilon}
satisfies:

\mathbf{y}^{\varepsilon}(t)=\frac{\mathbf{\tilde{y}}_{0}}{\Gamma(\gamma)}t^{\gamma-1}+{{}_{\varepsilon}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{y}^{\varepsilon}(t))

for
t\geq\varepsilon
.
Now consider analytical solution
\mathbf{y}
satisfying the equivalent integral equation (
14
).
Then, for
\varepsilon\leq t\leq T
write:

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}(t)-\mathbf{y}^{\varepsilon}(t)

\displaystyle={{}_{0}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{y}(t))-{{}_{\varepsilon}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{y}^{\varepsilon}(t)),

(66)

\displaystyle={{}_{0}}\mathrm{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{y}(t))+{{}_{\varepsilon}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}[\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{y}(t))-\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{y}^{\varepsilon}(t))].

(67)

By the Lipschitz condition on
\mathbf{f}
and the triangle inequality, we have:

\displaystyle|\mathbf{y}(t)-\mathbf{y}^{\varepsilon}(t)|\leq|{{}_{0}}\mathrm{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{y}(t))|+{{}_{\varepsilon}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}K|\mathbf{y}(t)-\mathbf{y}^{\varepsilon}(t)|.

(68)

By making use of the H
\ddot{\text{o}}
lder inequality,
the first term in (
68
) can be written as follows:

\displaystyle|{{}_{0}}\mathrm{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{y}(t))|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}|(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{y}(t))|\,\mathrm{d}s

(69)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}|(t-s)^{\alpha-1}s^{\gamma-1}\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{y}(t))s^{1-\gamma}|\,\mathrm{d}s

(70)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}s^{\gamma-1}\,\mathrm{d}s\,||\mathbf{f}||_{1-\gamma}.

(71)

By Proposition
2.3
, we have

\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}s^{\gamma-1}\,\mathrm{d}s=\frac{t^{\alpha+\gamma-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\mathrm{B}_{\varepsilon/t}(\gamma,\alpha).

Hence, we can write:

\displaystyle|{{}_{0}}\mathrm{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{y}(t))|\leq\frac{t^{\alpha+\gamma-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\mathrm{B}_{\varepsilon/t}(\gamma,\alpha)||\mathbf{f}||_{1-\gamma}.

(72)

Furthermore, as will be used later in the proof, this function is decreasing in
t>\varepsilon
, as:

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\biggl(\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}s^{\gamma-1}\,\mathrm{d}s\biggr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}s^{\gamma-1}\,\mathrm{d}s

(73)

\displaystyle=(\alpha-1)\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}(t-s)^{\alpha-2}s^{\gamma-1}\,\mathrm{d}s<0,

(74)

since
(t-s)^{\alpha-2}s^{\gamma-1}\geq 0
for
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\gamma\in(0,1]
,
0\leq s\leq\varepsilon
and furthermore
(\alpha-1)<0
.
Thus, the supremum must be attained at
t=\varepsilon
, and hence:

\displaystyle|{{}_{0}}\mathrm{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}\mathbf{f}(t,\mathbf{y}(t))|\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{\alpha+\gamma-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\mathrm{B}(\gamma,\alpha)||\mathbf{f}||_{1-\gamma},=\varepsilon^{\alpha+\gamma-1}\underbrace{\frac{\Gamma(\gamma)}{\Gamma(\alpha+\gamma)}||\mathbf{f}||_{1-\gamma}.}_{:=\Theta}

(75)

Then, substituting back to (
68
), we have:

\displaystyle|\mathbf{y}(t)-\mathbf{y}^{\varepsilon}(t)|\leq\varepsilon^{\alpha+\gamma-1}\Theta+{{}_{\varepsilon}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}K|\mathbf{y}(t)-\mathbf{y}^{\varepsilon}(t)|.

(76)

Now, writing
\mathbf{z}(t):=|\mathbf{y}(t)-\mathbf{y}^{\varepsilon}(t)|\mathds{1}_{[\varepsilon,T]}(t)
, we have

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbf{z}}(t)\leq\varepsilon^{\alpha+\gamma-1}\Theta+\frac{||K||_{\infty}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\overline{\mathbf{z}}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

(77)

Here,
\overline{\mathbf{z}}:=\sup_{i\in\{1,...,d\}}z_{i}
denotes the supremum over all dimension vector components and
||K||_{\infty}
denotes the supremum-matrix norm as the sum over each row.
Now, by the fractional Grönwall inequality as proven in Corollary 2 of
Ye et al. 2007
, we have:

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbf{z}}(t)\leq\varepsilon^{\alpha+\gamma-1}\Theta\mathrm{E}_{\alpha}(t^{\alpha}||K||_{\infty}),

(78)

where
\mathrm{E}_{\alpha}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{j}}{\Gamma(j\alpha+1)}
denotes the Mittag-Leffler function.
Writing this back as an upper bound to the components of
\mathbf{y}
and taking the limit over the supremum now gives:

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\biggl(\sup_{t\in[\varepsilon,T]}|\mathbf{y}(t)-\mathbf{y}^{\varepsilon}(t)|\biggr)\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{\alpha+\gamma-1}\Theta\mathrm{E}_{\alpha}(T^{\alpha}||K||_{\infty})=\mathbf{0},

(79)

since
\alpha+\gamma-1>0
.
Furthermore, since
\Theta\mathrm{E}_{\alpha}(T^{\alpha}||K||_{\infty})
is independent of
\varepsilon
, we know:

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[\varepsilon,T]}|\mathbf{y}(t)-\mathbf{y}^{\varepsilon}(t)|=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{\alpha+\gamma-1}),

(80)

concluding the proof.
∎

4.2
Implementation

To construct a numerical method constructing the approximations to the solutions of FIVP (
1
), we numerically implement the splines and integration operators used in Lemma
4.1
and Theorem
4.1
.
Given a function
f:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
and a knot collection
\mathcal{A}
, the splines approximation operator can directly be implemented from Definition
3.1
and
3.4
, yielding:

\displaystyle S^{q}_{\mathcal{A}}f(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{q}\sum_{\ell=j}^{q}a_{i,j}{\binom{q}{\ell}}{\binom{\ell}{j}}(-1)^{\ell-j}\,\mathds{1}_{A_{i}}\,s_{i}(t)^{\ell},

(81)

where
a_{i,j}=f(t_{i}+j(t_{i+1}-t_{i})/q)
for
i\in\{0,...,k\}
and
j\in\{0,...,q\}
.
Furthermore,
s_{i}:A_{i}\to[0,1]
is given by
s_{i}(t):=\frac{t-t_{i}}{t_{i+1}-t_{i}}
.

Now, for a given Bernstein spline
S:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
with Bernstein coefficients
a_{i,j}
, we want to establish an expression for fractional integration.
First, consider the monomial part of the expression.
Note that for
t<t_{i}
, the integral must be zero as the fractional integral of the zero function is zero.
Now, for
t\geq t_{i}
, the integral can be written out as follows:

\displaystyle{{}_{0}}\mathrm{I}_{t}^{\alpha}\{\mathds{1}_{A_{i}}(t)\,s_{i}(t)^{\ell}\}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-x)^{\alpha-1}\mathds{1}_{A_{i}}(x)\,s_{i}(x)^{\ell}\,\mathrm{d}x

(82)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{t_{i}}^{t\land t_{i+1}}(t-x)^{\alpha-1}s_{i}(x)^{\ell}\,\mathrm{d}x

(83)

\displaystyle=\frac{(t_{i+1}-t_{i})}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{s_{i}(t)\land 1}(t-s_{i}^{-1}(z))^{\alpha-1}z^{\ell}\,\mathrm{d}z

(84)

6
Conclusions

In summary, the proposed method shows high accuracy, good convergence and provides a flexible framework to approximate solutions to a wide range of nonlinear FIVPs.
In future research, improvements can be made in computational efficiency for the memory and computational contributions of past solution values on every knot.
Furthermore, an adapted spline basis formulation can be provided to give fractionally differentiable approximations satisfying regularity requirements of the analytical solution for every intermediate iteration.

Lean and Mean Adaptive Optimization via Subset-Norm and Subspace-Momentum with Convergence Guarantees
Thien Hang Nguyen, Huy Le Nguyen

Abstract
We introduce two complementary techniques for efficient optimization that reduce memory requirements while accelerating training of large-scale neural networks. The first technique, Subset-Norm step size, generalizes AdaGrad-Norm and AdaGrad(-Coordinate) through step-size sharing. Subset-Norm (SN) reduces AdaGrad’s memory footprint from O ⁡ ( d ) O(d) to O ⁡ ( d ) O(\sqrt{d}) , where d d is the model size. For non-convex smooth objectives under coordinate-wise sub-gaussian noise, we show a noise-adapted high-probability convergence guarantee with improved dimensional dependence of SN over existing methods. Our second technique, Subspace-Momentum , reduces the momentum state’s memory footprint by restricting momentum to a low-dimensional subspace while performing SGD in the orthogonal complement. We prove a high-probability convergence result for Subspace-Momentum under standard assumptions. Empirical evaluation on pre-training and fine-tuning LLMs demonstrates the effectiveness of our methods. For instance, combining Subset-Norm with Subspace-Momentum achieves Adam’s validation perplexity for LLaMA 1B in approximately half the training tokens (6.8B vs 13.1B) while reducing Adam’s optimizer-states memory footprint by more than 80% with minimal additional hyperparameter tuning.

2.2
Assumptions and Notations

For our theoretical analysis, we consider the unconstrained non-convex stochastic optimization problem

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)
where
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is the objective function.
We assume access to an history independent, non-biased gradient estimator

\widehat{\nabla}f(x)
for any
x\in\mathcal{X}

\mathbb{\mathbb{E}}\left[\widehat{\nabla}f(x)\mid x\right]=\nabla f(x)

Furthermore, we assume that

is an
L
-smooth
function:

\left\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\right\|\leq L\left\|x-y\right\|,\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Smoothness implies the following quadratic
upperbound that we will extensively utilize: for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have
f(y)-f(x)\leq\left\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\|y-x\right\|^{2}.

Subset-size heuristics to avoid additional hyperparameters.

Providing a useful and robust default setting for an algorithm is important to justify claims of reduced costs. We provide
a simple partitioning scheme for SN for 2D parameters
p\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
: we simply group along the smaller dimension. For example, if
p
is of shape
(2048,1024)
, we group by rows to get a state of size

This is a natural grouping scheme that groups the latent dimension together and aims for the rough

subset size discussed in the previous paragraph.
Another simplification is that subset-norm is applied only on

in transformers. This means we compress all the attention, MLP, and
final LM head weights. This implementation is presented in more details
in Appendix

shows that this heuristic grouping, while simple, is not optimal and can
be improved by tuning the subset size, but we opt for simplicity over performance-tuning in our experiments.

at time

a low-dimensional version
c_{t}:=P\widehat{\nabla}f(x_{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
is computed
that is used to update the states before projecting back to
\mathbb{R}^{d}

for update:

\displaystyle m_{t}

\displaystyle=\beta_{1}m_{t-1}+\left(1-\beta_{1}\right)c_{t}

\displaystyle v_{t}^{2}

\displaystyle=\beta_{2}v_{t-1}^{2}+\left(1-\beta_{2}\right)c_{t}^{2}

(4)

\displaystyle x_{t+1}

\displaystyle=x_{t}-P^{*}\left(m_{t}/v_{t}\right).

This update performs adaptive optimization in the row

U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
of

with

We propose Subspace Momentum (SM) – presented in Algorithm

4.1
High-Probability Convergence of Subspace-Momentum

We show that Subspace-Momentum, Algorithm
3
,
converges with high-probability under the standard assumptions of smoothness
and
\sigma
-subgaussian gradient noise:

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is

L
-smooth and lower bounded by
f_{*}
. Assume unbiased stochastic
gradients
\widehat{\nabla}f(x_{t})
with
\sigma
-subgaussian stochastic
gradient noise. Then, the iterates
x_{t}
given by SGD with Subspace-Momentum
(Algorithm
3
) with step size
\eta=\min\left\{\frac{1}{2\alpha};\sqrt{\frac{\Delta_{1}}{\sigma^{2}\alpha T}}\right\}

for
\alpha:=\frac{(3-\beta)L}{2\left(1-\beta\right)}
satisfies
the following with probability at least
1-\delta

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}\left\lVert\nabla_{t}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{8\Delta_{1}\alpha}{T}+\frac{7\sigma\sqrt{\alpha\Delta_{1}}}{\sqrt{T}}+\frac{48\sigma^{2}\log\left(1/\delta\right)}{T},

where
\Delta_{1}:=f(x_{1})-f_{*}
is the initial function gap.

We observe that Theorem
4.1
has a similar
rate to vanilla SGD. Unlike adaptive algorithms, we need to know the problem parameters to get an adaptive convergence rate. The proof is presented in Appendix
D.2

5.1.1
Discussions

Subset-Norm (SN) improves upon all existing adaptive methods while reducing memory.
Modifying Adam, AdaGradm, AdaGrad, and RMSProp with the SN adaptive step size not only reduces memory footprint but improves their performance across different scales. Notably, AdaGrad and AdaGradm benefit the most from the SN step size, providing empirical support for the theoretical benefits of SN presented in Section
3
.

Combining Subspace-Momentum (SM) with SN further improves performance while saving additional memory.
Perhaps surprisingly, limiting the use of momentum to a subspace
improves
performance in SN-adaptive step sizes rather than degrading it. Our experiments show that SNSM, combining SN and SM, gives the best performance for the least amount of memory across model sizes. While adding SM introduces additional hyperparameters, Section
B.7
suggests that these parameters are not too sensitive.

Furthermore, Section
B.8
shows that the choice of the subspace matters i.e. the subspace spanned by a top-
k
singular vectors of a snapshot of a stochastic gradient seems to be the most beneficial for momentum as opposed to simpler choices like a random subspace. Our current guarantee for SM, presented in Section
D
, does not yet explain why or when subspace momentum is useful, and theoretical understanding of (EMA style) momentum in stochastic optimization is still limited
(
Kidambi et al., 2018
)
. We believe this could be related to how momentum is beneficial when noise is low (and harmful when noise is high) and the choice of the subspace could correlate to the amount of gradient noise or optimization landscape that harm or benefit momentum
(
Wang et al., 2024
;
Gitman et al., 2019
)
.

Hyperparameter robustness.
In Table
3
, the best learning rate (LR) found via grid search is displayed and is highlighted in red as the best LR changes across scales. This indicates potential sensitivity to tuning for each respective algorithm. We see that Adam requires smaller LR for larger models, but using SN and SNSM does not. AdaGradm seems less sensitive to the base LR overall.

Closing the theory-practice gap.
While there is a non-trivial performance gap between Adam and AdaGrad(m) for larger models, using the SN step size closes this gap across scales. This shows that AdaGrad style algorithms can be competitive to Adam when using the SN step size. Interestingly, vanilla AdaGrad seems to perform well as model size increases. This is important because AdaGrad enjoys stronger theoretical understanding than Adam and has one fewer parameter –
\beta_{2}
– to tune.

Subset-Norm’s subset size ablation.

While we use a simple scheme to compress the adaptive step size
of linear modules in the previous experiments, Table
2
suggests that there is an optimal subset
size that depends on the noise. Figure
5
shows performance for various subset-size selection. Since the step size scales with the subset size, the optimal base LR should be decreased as we decrease the subset size closer towards Adam. We include additional results for 130M model in Figure
9
.

Figure 5:
Subset size ablation for AdamSN on LLaMA 60M trained for 1.38B tokens
(batch size of 512 of max length 256 for 10,000 steps). The higher
the subset size, the smaller the memory footprint of the second moment
optimizer state.

While one can use the heuristics discussed on models where linear modules make up the vast majority, for arbitrary models with
weights of
d
elements, we found that a subset size of
\sqrt{d}/2
is probably
a reasonable choice. If more resources are available, the subset
size can also be tuned.

Subspace selection.
While the top-
k
singular vectors of stochastic gradients gives a subspace with strong performance, performing SVD can be expensive for larger models and storing the dense projection consumes non-negligible memory for large ranks. Gradient-independent projections
like random gaussian as in FLORA
(
Hao et al., 2024
)
avoids
SVD and can save memory by storing the pseudorandom seed
(at the cost of recomputating the projection at every step). One can
further speed up the random projection by using a fast
subspace embedding like the Subsampled-Randomized Hadamard Transform
(SRHT) used in the Fast-JL transform
(
Ailon & Chazelle, 2009
)
. Random
projections like SRHT can also be used to approximate SVD (Appx-SVD)
computation
(
Halko et al., 2011
)
that can be much faster than
full SVD. Finally, the cheapest projection is a subspace
of random standard bases. Recently, GRASS
(
Muhamed et al., 2024
)

explores this idea and tests sampling random bases with large gradient
norms. We examine different choices for the subspace and
compare their time, space, and performance in Appendix
B.8
(Table
13
).

Related Works

As model sizes grow, memory-efficient training techniques have become
crucial. Following up on AdaFactor
(
Shazeer & Stern, 2018
)
, low-rank
methods like Galore
(
Zhao et al., 2024
)
, LoRA
(
Hao et al., 2024
)
,
and ReLORA
(
Lialin et al., 2023
)
reduce memory usage by approximating
large weight matrices with low-rank representations. Projection-based
approaches, such as GRASS
(
Muhamed et al., 2024
)
and FLORA
(
Hao et al., 2024
)
,
compress gradients or combine low-rank ideas with projections to reduce
memory requirements. Recently, AdaMeM
(
Vyas et al., 2024a
)
proposes to incorporate the orthogonal subspace to the AdaFactor optimizer; this is related to but different from our simpler SM algorithms, where we use subspace decompositions to decouple the momentum and SGD.
BAdam
(
Luo et al., 2024
)
,
a block coordinate descent method that utilizes Adam as an inner solver,
has been proposed for fine-tuning large language models.
In contrast to our proposed methods, these methods are largely heuristic-driven and often lack convergence
guarantees under standard assumptions.
On the other hand, methods like SM3
(
Anil et al., 2019
)
,
which uses subset (cover) statistics to show convergence in online
learning, and MicroAdam
(
Modoranu et al., 2024
)
, which provides
convergence guarantees for a gradient compression scheme with error
correction, offer theoretical guarantees.

Additional approaches to reducing memory during training include optimizer
quantization
(
Li et al., 2024a
;
Dettmers et al., 2021
;
Dettmers et al., 2024
)
,
attention computation compression/optimization
(
Wu et al., 2022
;
Dao et al., 2022
;
Dao, 2023
;
Shah et al., 2024
)
,
activation checkpointing
(
Chen et al., 2016
)
, and distributed
training
(
Rajbhandari et al., 2020
)
. For inference, compression techniques
are also actively being explored
(
Sakr & Khailany, 2024
;
Dettmers et al., 2022
;
Xiao et al., 2024
;
Lin et al., 2024
;
Frantar et al., 2023
)
. These are orthogonal directions to our work and can be combined.
Another orthogonal direction is approximated second-order optimization, where one aims to approximate the Hessian preconditioner using only first-order information in order to achieve faster convergence. Some works in this area include
(
Gupta et al., 2018
;
Liu et al., 2023a
;
Vyas et al., 2024b
)
. These methods typically demonstrate faster training but at the cost of super-linear memory and additional computational overhead.

Convergence analysis of non-convex optimization methods has seen significant
progress, with recent works providing convergence proofs for adaptive
algorithms like Adam
(
Li et al., 2024b
;
Défossez et al., 2022
)
.
Numerous studies have explored convergence properties of various adaptive
and stochastic gradient methods
(
Chen et al., 2018
;
Défossez et al., 2022
;
Ene & Nguyen, 2021
;
Liu et al., 2023c
;
Liu et al., 2023b
;
Ward et al., 2019
;
Zou et al., 2019
;
Reddi et al., 2018
;
Nesterov, 1983
)
,
while lower bound analyses
(
Arjevani et al., 2023
)
have highlighted
fundamental limits in non-convex optimization. Here, obtaining convergence results for EMA updates (Adam style) for subset-norm and under further relaxed assumptions like affine smoothness
(
Wang et al., 2023
;
Attia & Koren, 2023
)
, affine noise
(
Hong & Lin, 2024
;
Faw et al., 2022
)
, heavy-tailed noise
(
Zhang et al., 2019
;
Zhang et al., 2020
;
Nguyen et al., 2023a
;
Nguyen et al., 2023b
)
are of great interest.

Comparison with Adam-mini.
Very recently, Adam-mini
(
Zhang et al., 2024
)
also uses shared step sizes as Subset-Norm; however, the partition strategy is quite different from ours. While Adam-mini also employs a grouping strategy for the adaptive step size, it is primarily motivated empirically and lacks a general grouping strategy for general parameters.
In contrast, our theory results show that grouping by noise magnitude leads to improvement.
In experiments, our AdamSNSM uses less memory than Adam-mini, due to the fact that Adam-mini uses full momentum while we use momentum only in a subspace (which outperforms full momentum in many cases given a good choice of subspace). Furthermore, in terms of perplexity, Adam-mini performs very closely to AdamW while our methods outperform Adam (which performs similarly to AdamW) on a range of language tasks and model sizes.

7
Conclusion and Future Works

In this paper, we introduce two principled optimizer states’ memory reduction methods —Subset-Norm
(SN) and Subspace-Momentum (SM)—designed to address the high memory
costs associated with adaptive optimizers in large-scale deep learning. SN and SM achieve memory savings without
compromising performance and admit high-probability convergence guarantees under relaxed
assumptions. Extensive experiments pre-training and fine-tuning LLMs validate our methods’ effectiveness and efficiency.

Future works.

Promising directions include exploring SN and SM on additional domains like Reinforcement Learning, where high memory and high noise are also bottlenecks when scaling up to large models. Fully generalizing our methods involves developing general projections (beyond matrix decomposition) for SM on higher order tensors for use on additional architectures like CNNs. A more in-depth investigation to more optimal subset partition strategies for SN is also an interesting open question, since our analysis in Section
3.2
only applies to equipartition subsets. Further theoretical understanding for SM’s subspace dependency for improving the subspace selection for SM is desirable. Furthermore, the convergence of SNSM is still unknown. Finally, the benefits of momentum in stochastic optimization in general is still a mystery; using SM to study effects of momentum on particular subspaces could open doors to obtain a more fine-grained understanding for why or when momentum helps.

Projection types.

Note that the choice of the projection is important as some projections are more computationally and memory expensive than other, although trading other qualities for given the cost. Simple projections like selecting a subset of coordinates for momentum (Subset-Momentum) are not only faster but enables simple distributed training like FSDP unlike more complex subspace selection mechanism that requires additional priors about the parameters (shape, low-rank, etc.) that might not always satisfied.

C.2
Convergence Rate Derivation

We derive the dimensional dependency of convergence rates for different
AdaGrad variants below.

AdaGrad-Coordinate.

For
c=d
(AdaGrad-Coordinate), we get
\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\left\lVert\sigma_{\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert=\alpha d^{\beta}
,

\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\alpha^{2}d^{\beta}
, and
\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\left\lVert\sigma_{\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{4}=\alpha^{4}d^{\beta}
,
so the bound from Theorem
3.1
becomes

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\lVert\nabla_{t}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{O}\left(\alpha^{4}d^{\beta}+\alpha^{3}d^{\beta}+dL+d^{1.5}\alpha\right)\cdot\tilde{O}\left(\frac{\alpha d^{\beta}}{\sqrt{T}}+\frac{\alpha^{2}d^{\beta}+\alpha d^{\beta}+Ld}{T}\right).

The dependency on
d
for the slow term
O(1/\sqrt{T})
is
d^{1.5}d^{\beta}=d^{1.5+\beta}
.
The dependency on
d
for the fast term
O(1/T)
is
d^{1.5}d=d^{2.5}
.
Note that there is an inherent
d^{1.5}
dependency for the slow
term that does not reduce as the coordinate-noise density decrease.

AdaGrad-Norm

For
c=1
(AdaGrad-Norm), we get
\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\left\lVert\sigma_{i}\right\rVert^{2}=\alpha^{2}d^{\beta}
,

\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}=\alpha d^{\beta/2}
, and
\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert^{4}=\alpha^{4}d^{2\beta}
.
This means that our bound from Theorem
3.1

becomes

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\lVert\nabla_{t}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\tilde{O}\left(\alpha^{4}d^{2\beta}+\alpha^{3}d^{\beta}+L+\alpha\right)\cdot\tilde{O}\left(\frac{\alpha d^{\beta/2}}{\sqrt{T}}+\frac{\alpha^{2}d^{\beta}+\alpha d^{\beta/2}+L}{T}\right).

The dependency on
d
for the slow term
O(1/\sqrt{T})
is
d^{2\beta}\cdot d^{\beta/2}=d^{2.5\beta}
.
The dependency on
d
for the fast term
O(1/T)
is
d^{2\beta}\cdot d^{\beta}=d^{3\beta}
.
Note that when
\beta=0
, or when all the noise is on a single coordinate,
we recover the dimension-free results of previous works.

AdaGrad-Subset-Norm.

Now, consider the following partition strategy, where we divide the
coordinates into
c=d^{1-\beta}k
subsets of size
d^{\beta}/k

each with the
d^{\beta}
noisy coordinates into just
k
subsets
so that the rest of the
c-k
subsets do not contain any noisy coordinate. This is a reasonable choice due to the empirical validation from Section
C.1
: The noisy parameters seem to cluster in groups corresponding to the architecture.

With this strategy, we have
\left\lVert\sigma_{\Psi_{j}}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\alpha^{2}d^{\beta}/k\implies\left\lVert\sigma_{\Psi_{j}}\right\rVert_{2}=\alpha d^{\beta/2}/k^{0.5}

if
j
is a noisy subset. We can compute
\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\left\lVert\sigma_{\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert=\alpha d^{\beta/2}k^{0.5}
,

\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\left\lVert\sigma_{\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\alpha^{2}d^{\beta}
,
and
\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\left\lVert\sigma_{\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{4}=\alpha^{4}d^{2\beta}/k
.
From Theorem
3.1
, we get a bound of

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\lVert\nabla_{t}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{O}\left(\alpha^{4}d^{2\beta}/k+\alpha^{3}d^{\beta}+d^{1-\beta}kL+\left(d^{1-\beta}k\right)^{3/2}\alpha\right)\cdot

\displaystyle\quad\quad\tilde{O}\left(\frac{\alpha d^{\beta/2}k^{0.5}}{\sqrt{T}}+\frac{\alpha^{2}d^{\beta}+\alpha d^{\beta/2}k^{0.5}+Ld^{1-\beta}k}{T}\right).

Set
k=d^{7\beta/5-3/5}
so that
\left(d^{1-\beta}k\right)^{3/2}=d^{2\beta}/k=d^{3\beta/5+3/5}
.
Then we can simplify

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\lVert\nabla_{t}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{O}\left(\alpha^{4}d^{3(\beta+1)/5}+\alpha^{3}d^{\beta}+d^{2(\beta+1)/5}L+d^{3(\beta+1)/5}\alpha\right)\cdot

\displaystyle\quad\quad\tilde{O}\left(\frac{\alpha d^{(12\beta-3)/10}}{\sqrt{T}}+\frac{\alpha^{2}d^{\beta}+\alpha d^{(12\beta-3)/10}+Ld^{2(\beta+1)/5}}{T}\right).

The dependency on
d
for the slow term
O(1/\sqrt{T})
is
d^{3(\beta+1)/5}\cdot d^{(12\beta-3)/10}=d^{3(1+6\beta)/10}=d^{0.3+1.8\beta}
.
The dependency on
d
for the fast term
O(1/T)
is a bit more complicated:
For
\beta\in[0,\frac{2}{3}]
, we have the dependency on
d
is

d^{3(\beta+1)/5}\cdot d^{2(\beta+1)/5}=d^{\beta+1}
. For
\beta\in[\frac{2}{3},1]
,
we have the dependency on
d
is
d^{3(\beta+1)/5}\cdot d^{\beta}=d^{3(\beta+1)/5+\beta}=d^{1.6\beta+0.6}
.
Note that this is only a possible partition strategy where the subset
sizes are of equal size (which is probably the most natural and easiest
to implement). There, the optimal subset size is
k=d^{1.4\beta-0.6}
,
for which if we plug in
\beta\in[0,1]
we get a range from
1

to
d^{0.8}
.

C.3
From Theory to Practice

Our theory provides an optimal grouping strategy that depends on the noise density. However, in practice, we must trade off the cost to figure out a good grouping and the performance gain from it.
The key from the theory improvement is to group the coordinates with similar noise magnitudes together. However, any expensive method to figure out these groups (e.g. the Hessian in Adam-mini) would have detrimental effects on the wall clock time and memory. Instead, our heuristic as in Section
3.2
is meant to be a simple method to capture most of these groups.

Intuitively, coordinates in the same row/column either act on the same input or are used to compute the same output. The noise and normalization on each input and output would affect coordinates in the same row/columns in a correlated way. To provide some evidence, we perform the experiments in Section
C.1
again in Figure
18
, but with the noise grouped by the corresponding dimension according to the heuristics.

Figure 18:
Coordinate noise grouped by the corresponding dimension according to the heuristics

There we see most groups have very low noise (very close to 0, namely less than
10^{-12}
) while a small number of groups (top 1 percentile in the annotation) have much larger noises.
Overall, our heuristics aim to capture the similar noise coming from the same inputs and outputs. Our experiments suggest that this is a major part of the gain. There might be other simple sources of correlation in the noise magnitudes, which we leave for future work.

Appendix D
Subspace-Momentum Convergence Proof

In this section, we provide a high-probability convergence proof for SGD
with Subspace-Momentum for non-convex smooth objective under sub-gaussian gradient noise.

D.1
Setup and intuition

Notations.

Given a linear operator
P:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{k}
, we have
P^{*}:\mathbb{R}^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}

is
P
’s adjoint
16
16

16

In
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the adjoint
P^{*}
of a linear operator
P
is the linear operator given by the
transpose
of the matrix representation of
P
. We can also generalize Subspace-Momentum to general Hilbert spaces.
, and we consider
P^{*}P:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}

is a projection operator i.e.
P^{*}P
is a bounded linear operator
such that
\left(P^{*}P\right)^{2}=P^{*}P
. Given a space
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
we denote its orthogonal subspace by
V^{\perp}:=\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\left\langle v,u\right\rangle=0,\ \forall u\in V\right\}
.

Let
U=\text{row}\left(P\right)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the row span of

P
. Let
\Psi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow U
be
\Psi(x)=P^{*}Px
and
\Psi^{\perp}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow U^{\perp}

be
\Psi^{\perp}(x)=x-P^{*}Px
. Then for any vector
x
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
have the orthogonal decomposition

x=\Psi(x)+\Psi^{\perp}(x).

SGD with Subspace Momentum.

Let
g_{t}:=\widehat{\nabla}f(x_{t})
denotes the stochastic gradient at time

t
. Let
\hat{c}_{t}=Pg_{t}
,
g_{t}^{U}=\Psi g_{t}=P^{*}Pg_{t}\in U
,
and
g_{t}^{\perp}=g_{t}-g_{t}^{U}\in U^{\perp}
. Let
\nabla_{t}:=\nabla f(x_{t})

be a short hand for the gradient at time
t
and let
\nabla_{t}^{U}:=\Psi\left(\nabla f(x_{t})\right)\in U

and
\nabla_{t}^{\perp}:=\Psi^{\perp}\left(\nabla f(x_{t})\right)\in U^{\perp}

be the orthogonal decomposition of
\nabla f(x_{t})
with respect
to
U
and
U^{\perp}
, so that
\nabla_{t}=\nabla_{t}^{U}+\nabla_{t}^{\perp}
.
Note that the superscript of a variable tries to suggest the space
that it lives in (either
U
or
U^{\perp}
). We have the following
update rule for subspace momentum:

\displaystyle\hat{m}_{t}

\displaystyle=\beta\hat{m}_{t-1}+(1-\beta)Pg_{t}

\displaystyle g_{t}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=g_{t}-P^{*}Pg_{t}

\displaystyle m_{t}

\displaystyle=P^{*}\hat{m}_{t}

\displaystyle x_{t+1}

\displaystyle=x_{t}-\eta\left(m_{t}+g_{t}^{\perp}\right).

Note that

\displaystyle m_{t}

\displaystyle=\beta P^{*}\hat{m}_{t-1}+(1-\beta)P^{*}Pg_{t}

\displaystyle=\beta m_{t-1}+(1-\beta)g_{t}^{U}.

Expanding the terms, we see that this is just momentum in
U

\displaystyle m_{t}

\displaystyle=\beta P^{*}\hat{m}_{t-1}+(1-\beta)P^{*}Pg_{t}

\displaystyle=\beta P^{*}\hat{m}_{t-1}+(1-\beta)g_{t}^{U}

\displaystyle=\beta^{2}P^{*}\hat{m}_{t-2}+(1-\beta)\beta g_{t-1}^{U}+(1-\beta)g_{t}^{U}

\displaystyle=(1-\beta)\sum_{i=0}^{t}\beta^{i}g_{t-i}^{U}.

(5)

Hence, we can think of the update of SGD-SM as performing two separate algorithms
in the orthogonal subspaces: momentum in the subspace
U
and SGD in
the subspace
U^{\perp}
(see also Figure
4
) i.e. if we decompose
x_{t}
into its orthogonal
components
x_{t}=x_{t}^{U}+x_{t}^{\perp}
, then

\displaystyle x_{t+1}^{U}

\displaystyle=x_{t}^{U}-\eta m_{t}

\displaystyle=x_{t}^{U}-\eta(1-\beta)\sum_{i=0}^{t}\beta^{i}g_{t-i}^{U}

\displaystyle x_{t+1}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=x_{t}^{\perp}-\eta g_{t}^{\perp}.

For our analysis, let
\xi_{t}:=g_{t}-\nabla_{t}
denote the stochastic
gradient error at time
t
. We can further decompose the error into
its subspace components:

\displaystyle\xi_{t}

\displaystyle=\xi_{t}^{U}+\xi_{t}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\left(g_{t}^{U}-\nabla_{t}^{U}\right)+\left(g_{t}^{\perp}-\nabla_{t}^{\perp}\right).

Basic facts.

We establish some facts for subspace momentum.

1.

Pythagorean:
\left\lVert g_{t}\right\rVert^{2}=\left\lVert g_{t}^{U}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert g_{t}^{\perp}\right\rVert^{2}

and
\left\lVert\nabla_{t}\right\rVert^{2}=\left\lVert\nabla_{t}^{U}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{t}^{\perp}\right\rVert^{2}

and so on for these decompositions.

2.

Subspace smoothness: If
f
is smooth
\left\lVert\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\right\rVert\leq L\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert
,
then due to contraction property of the projection operator, we have
that the projected gradients of
f
are also
L
-Lipschitz:

\displaystyle\left\lVert P^{*}P\nabla f(x)-P^{*}P\nabla f(y)\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\right\rVert

(6)

\displaystyle\leq L\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert.

3.

Subspace non-bias:

\displaystyle\mathbb{\mathbb{E}}\left[g_{t}^{U}-\nabla_{t}^{U}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{\mathbb{E}}\left[\xi_{t}^{U}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{\mathbb{E}}\left[P^{*}P\left(g_{t}-\nabla_{t}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=0,

and similarly for the orthogonal subspace

\displaystyle\mathbb{\mathbb{E}}\left[g_{t}^{\perp}-\nabla_{t}^{\perp}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{\mathbb{E}}\left[\xi_{t}^{\perp}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{\mathbb{E}}\left[\xi_{t}-\xi_{t}^{U}\right]

\displaystyle=0.

4.

Subspace bounded variance: if the stochastic gradient’s variance is
bounded, then its subspace components are also bounded
\mathbb{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\xi_{t}\right\rVert^{2}\right]
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert g_{t}^{U}-\nabla_{t}^{U}\right\rVert^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\xi_{t}^{U}\right\rVert^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\xi_{t}\right\rVert^{2}-\left\lVert\xi_{t}^{\perp}\right\rVert^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sigma^{2}-\mathbb{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\xi_{t}^{\perp}\right\rVert^{2}\right],

and similarly,

\mathbb{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\xi_{t}^{\perp}\right\rVert^{2}\right]\leq\sigma^{2}-\mathbb{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert\xi_{t}^{U}\right\rVert^{2}\right].

D.2
Subspace-Momentum convergence proof

Suppose that
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is
L
-smooth and stochastic
gradients
\widehat{\nabla}f(x_{t})=g_{t}
is unbiased, i.e.
\mathbb{\mathbb{E}}\left[g_{t}\right]=\nabla f(x_{t})
,
and has
\sigma
-sub-gaussian noise, i.e.
\mathbb{\mathbb{E}}[\exp(\lambda^{2}\left\lVert g_{t}-\nabla f(x_{t})\right\rVert^{2})]\leq\exp(\lambda^{2}\sigma^{2})
for all
\lambda
s.t.
|\lambda|\leq 1/\sigma
.
First, we will show an error bound that is a starting point for the high-probability convergence results.

Lemma D.1
.

If
f
is
L

(Algorithm

f(x_{T+1})-f(x_{1})\leq-\eta\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\lVert\nabla_{t}\right\rVert^{2}-\eta\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\langle\nabla_{t},\xi_{t}\right\rangle+\frac{(3-\beta)L\eta^{2}}{2\left(1-\beta\right)}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\lVert g_{t}\right\rVert^{2}.

Remark D.2
.

Lemma
D.1
shows that the optimization error of SGD-SM is quite similar
to SGD-M.

Proof.

Note that
m_{t}\in U
and
r_{t}\in U^{\perp}
. Starting with smoothness,
we have

\displaystyle f(x_{t+1})

\displaystyle\leq f(x_{t})+\left\langle\nabla f(x_{t}),x_{t+1}-x_{t}\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert x_{t+1}-x_{t}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=f(x_{t})-\eta\left\langle\nabla f(x_{t}),m_{t}+g_{t}^{\perp}\right\rangle+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}\left\lVert m_{t}+g_{t}^{\perp}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=f(x_{t})-\eta\left\langle\nabla f(x_{t}),m_{t}\right\rangle-\eta\left\langle\nabla f(x_{t}),g_{t}^{\perp}\right\rangle+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}\left\lVert m_{t}+g_{t}^{\perp}\right\rVert^{2}.

We have

\displaystyle f(x_{t+1})-f(x_{t})

\displaystyle\leq-\eta\left\langle\nabla_{t}^{U},m_{t}\right\rangle-\eta\left\langle\nabla_{t}^{\perp},g_{t}^{\perp}\right\rangle+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}\left\lVert m_{t}+g_{t}^{\perp}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=-\eta\left\langle\nabla_{t}^{U},m_{t}\right\rangle-\eta\left\langle\nabla_{t}^{\perp},g_{t}^{\perp}\right\rangle+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}\left\lVert m_{t}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}\left\lVert g_{t}^{\perp}\right\rVert^{2}.

(Pythagorean)

Summing it up, we get

f(x_{T+1})-f(x_{1})\leq\underbrace{-\eta\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\langle\nabla_{t}^{U},m_{t}\right\rangle+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\lVert m_{t}\right\rVert^{2}}_{\text{SGD with momentum error in $U$}}\underbrace{-\eta\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\langle\nabla_{t}^{\perp},g_{t}^{\perp}\right\rangle+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\lVert g_{t}^{\perp}\right\rVert^{2}}_{\text{vanilla SGD error in $U^{\perp}$}}.

(7)

We analyze

and

separately. Intuitively, the error within each subspace is controlled
by their respective algorithm. Investigating the momentum term, we
have

\displaystyle-\left\langle\nabla_{t}^{U},m_{t}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\nabla_{t}^{U},\beta m_{t-1}+(1-\beta)g_{t}^{U}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\beta\left\langle\nabla_{t}^{U},m_{t-1}\right\rangle-(1-\beta)\left\langle\nabla_{t}^{U},g_{t}^{U}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\beta\left\langle\nabla_{t}^{U}-\nabla_{t-1}^{U},m_{t-1}\right\rangle-\beta\left\langle\nabla_{t-1}^{U},m_{t-1}\right\rangle-(1-\beta)\left\langle\nabla_{t}^{U},g_{t}^{U}\right\rangle.

We examine

\displaystyle-\left\langle\nabla_{t}^{U}-\nabla_{t-1}^{U},m_{t-1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\nabla_{t}-\nabla_{t-1},m_{t-1}\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla_{t}^{\perp}-\nabla_{t-1}^{\perp},m_{t-1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\nabla_{t}-\nabla_{t-1},m_{t-1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\nabla_{t}-\nabla_{t-1}\right\rVert\left\lVert m_{t-1}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq L\left\lVert x_{t}-x_{t-1}\right\rVert\left\lVert m_{t-1}\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\eta L\left\lVert m_{t-1}+g_{t-1}^{\perp}\right\rVert\left\lVert m_{t-1}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\eta L\left\lVert m_{t-1}+g_{t-1}^{\perp}\right\rVert^{2}.

Now we have

\displaystyle-\left\langle\nabla_{t}^{U},m_{t}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\eta L\beta\left\lVert m_{t-1}+g_{t-1}^{\perp}\right\rVert^{2}-\beta\left\langle\nabla_{t-1}^{U},m_{t-1}\right\rangle-(1-\beta)\left\langle\nabla_{t}^{U},g_{t}^{U}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\eta L\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\beta^{t-i}\left\lVert m_{i}+g_{i}^{\perp}\right\rVert^{2}-(1-\beta)\sum_{i=1}^{t}\beta^{t-i}\left\langle\nabla_{i}^{U},g_{i}^{U}\right\rangle.

\displaystyle=-\eta\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\nabla_{t,j}\widehat{\nabla}_{t,j}}{b_{t,i}}+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\widehat{\nabla}_{t,j}^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\eta\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\nabla_{t,j}^{2}}{b_{t,i}}-\eta\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\nabla_{t,j}\xi_{t,j}}{b_{t,i}}+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\widehat{\nabla}_{t,j}^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\eta\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\nabla_{t,j}^{2}}{b_{t,i}}-\eta\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\nabla_{t,j}\xi_{t,j}}{a_{t,i}}+\eta\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\left(\frac{1}{a_{t,i}}-\frac{1}{b_{t,i}}\right)\nabla_{t,j}\xi_{t,j}+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\widehat{\nabla}_{t,j}^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}.

(13)

Now, we analyze
\frac{1}{a_{t,i}}-\frac{1}{b_{t,i}}
for
i=0,1,\dots,c-1
:

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{a_{t,i}}-\frac{1}{b_{t,i}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{b_{t,i}-a_{t,i}}{a_{t,i}b_{t,i}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{b_{t,i}^{2}-a_{t,i}^{2}}{a_{t,i}b_{t,i}\left(b_{t,i}+a_{t,i}\right)}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{b_{t-1,i}^{2}+\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{\Psi_{i}}f(x_{t})\right\rVert^{2}-b_{t-1,i}^{2}-\left\lVert\nabla_{\Psi_{i}}f(x_{t})\right\rVert^{2}}{a_{t,i}b_{t,i}\left(b_{t,i}+a_{t,i}\right)}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{\Psi_{i}}f(x_{t})\right\rVert^{2}-\left\lVert\nabla_{\Psi_{i}}f(x_{t})\right\rVert^{2}}{a_{t,i}b_{t,i}\left(b_{t,i}+a_{t,i}\right)}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\left(\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{\Psi_{i}}f(x_{t})\right\rVert-\left\lVert\nabla_{\Psi_{i}}f(x_{t})\right\rVert\right)\left(\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{\Psi_{i}}f(x_{t})\right\rVert+\left\lVert\nabla_{\Psi_{i}}f(x_{t})\right\rVert\right)}{a_{t,i}b_{t,i}\left(b_{t,i}+a_{t,i}\right)}\right|.

Since
b_{t,i}=\sqrt{b_{t-1,i}^{2}+\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{\Psi_{i}}f(x_{t})\right\rVert^{2}}\geq\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{\Psi_{i}}f(x_{t})\right\rVert

and
a_{t,i}=\sqrt{b_{t-1,i}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{\Psi_{i}}f(x_{t})\right\rVert^{2}}\geq\left\lVert\nabla_{\Psi_{i}}f(x_{t})\right\rVert
,
we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{a_{t,i}}-\frac{1}{b_{t,i}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{\left(\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{\Psi_{i}}f(x_{t})\right\rVert-\left\lVert\nabla_{\Psi_{i}}f(x_{t})\right\rVert\right)\left(\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{\Psi_{i}}f(x_{t})\right\rVert+\left\lVert\nabla_{\Psi_{i}}f(x_{t})\right\rVert\right)}{a_{t,i}b_{t,i}\left(\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{\Psi_{i}}f(x_{t})\right\rVert+\left\lVert\nabla_{\Psi_{i}}f(x_{t})\right\rVert\right)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{\Psi_{i}}f(x_{t})\right\rVert-\left\lVert\nabla_{\Psi_{i}}f(x_{t})\right\rVert}{a_{t,i}b_{t,i}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{\Psi_{i}}f(x_{t})-\nabla_{\Psi_{i}}f(x_{t})\right\rVert}{a_{t,i}b_{t,i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert}{a_{t,i}b_{t,i}}.

Hence, we have

\left|\frac{1}{a_{t,i}}-\frac{1}{b_{t,i}}\right|\leq\frac{\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert}{a_{t,i}b_{t,i}}.

Then from
13
, taking the absolute value of
\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\left(\frac{1}{a_{t,i}}-\frac{1}{b_{t,i}}\right)\nabla_{t,j}\xi_{t,j}
,
we can bound:

\displaystyle\Delta_{t+1}-\Delta_{t}

\displaystyle\leq-\eta\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\nabla_{t,j}^{2}}{b_{t,i}}-\eta\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\nabla_{t,j}\xi_{t,j}}{a_{t,i}}+\eta\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\left|\frac{1}{a_{t,i}}-\frac{1}{b_{t,i}}\right|\left|\nabla_{t,j}\xi_{t,j}\right|+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\widehat{\nabla}_{t,j}^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq-\eta\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\nabla_{t,j}^{2}}{b_{t,i}}-\eta\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\nabla_{t,j}\xi_{t,j}}{a_{t,i}}+\eta\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\frac{\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert}{a_{t,i}b_{t,i}}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\left|\nabla_{t,j}\xi_{t,j}\right|+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\widehat{\nabla}_{t,j}^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}

\displaystyle\overset{(1)}{\leq}-\eta\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\nabla_{t,j}^{2}}{b_{t,i}}-\eta\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\nabla_{t,j}\xi_{t,j}}{a_{t,i}}+\eta\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\frac{\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert}{a_{t,i}b_{t,i}}\left\lVert\nabla_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\widehat{\nabla}_{t,j}^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq-\eta\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\nabla_{t,j}^{2}}{b_{t,i}}-\eta\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\nabla_{t,j}\xi_{t,j}}{a_{t,i}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\eta\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert\left(\frac{\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{2b_{t,i}^{2}}+\frac{\left\lVert\nabla_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{2a_{t,i}^{2}}\right)+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\widehat{\nabla}_{t,j}^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}},

where (1) is due to
\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\left|\nabla_{t,j}\xi_{t,j}\right|=\left\langle\left|\nabla_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right|,\left|\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right|\right\rangle\leq\left\lVert\nabla_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert

and
\left|\cdot\right|
denotes coordinate-wise absolute value when
we apply to vectors. The last inequality is due to
2ab\leq a^{2}+b^{2}
.
Now, we can sum both sides for
t=1,\dots,T
to telescope the LHS:

\displaystyle\Delta_{T+1}-\Delta_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\Bigl(-\eta\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\nabla_{t,j}^{2}}{b_{t,i}}-\eta\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\nabla_{t,j}\xi_{t,j}}{a_{t,i}}

\displaystyle+\eta\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert\left(\frac{\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{2b_{t,i}^{2}}+\frac{\left\lVert\nabla_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{2a_{t,i}^{2}}\right)+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\widehat{\nabla}_{t,j}^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}\Bigr).

Rearranging gives

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\nabla_{t,j}^{2}}{b_{t,i}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{T+1}}{\eta}-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\underbrace{\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\nabla_{t,j}\xi_{t,j}}{a_{t,i}}}_{A}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\underbrace{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert\left(\frac{\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{2b_{t,i}^{2}}+\frac{\left\lVert\nabla_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{2a_{t,i}^{2}}\right)}_{B}+\frac{\eta L}{2}\underbrace{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\widehat{\nabla}_{t,j}^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}}_{C}.

On the LHS, we note that

\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\nabla_{t,j}^{2}}{b_{t,i}}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\frac{\left\lVert\nabla_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}}.

We now bound each term separately. It’s easiest to bound
C
:
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\widehat{\nabla}_{t,j}^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}
:

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\widehat{\nabla}_{t,j}^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\widehat{\nabla}_{t,j}^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{b_{t,i}^{2}-b_{t-1,i}^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{d}2\log\frac{b_{T,i}}{b_{0,i}}.

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{b_{t,i}^{2}-b_{t-1,i}^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}1-\frac{b_{t-1,i}^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\log\frac{b_{t,i}^{2}}{b_{t-1,i}^{2}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\log\prod_{t=1}^{T}\frac{b_{t,i}}{b_{t-1,i}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\log\frac{b_{T,i}}{b_{0,i}}.

We now have a useful inequality

\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}\leq 2\log\frac{b_{T,i}}{b_{0,i}},\ \forall i=0,\dots,c-1.

(14)

Next, we deal with
-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\nabla_{t,j}\xi_{t,j}}{a_{t,i}}

via a martingale argument. Let
\mathcal{F}_{t}:=\sigma\left(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{t-1}\right)

denote the natural filtration. Note that
x_{t}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable.
For any
w>0
, we have for each
i\in[c]
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{\mathbb{E}}\left[\exp\left(-w\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\nabla_{t,j}\xi_{t,j}}{a_{t,i}}-2w^{2}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\sigma_{j}^{2}\nabla_{t,j}^{2}}{a_{t,i}^{2}}\right)\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-2w^{2}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\sigma_{j}^{2}\nabla_{t,j}^{2}}{a_{t,i}^{2}}\right)\mathbb{\mathbb{E}}\left[\exp\left(-w\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\nabla_{t,j}\xi_{t,j}}{a_{t,i}}\right)\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 1.

Then a simple inductive argument and using Markov’s inequality gives
with probability at least
1-\delta
:

-w\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\nabla_{t,j}\xi_{t,j}}{a_{t,i}}\leq 2w^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\sigma_{j}^{2}\nabla_{t,j}^{2}}{a_{t,i}^{2}}+\log\frac{1}{\delta}.

By a union bound across all
c
subsets, we have w.p. at least
1-c\delta
:

-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\nabla_{t,j}\xi_{t,j}}{a_{t,i}}\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{w\sigma_{j}^{2}\nabla_{t,j}^{2}}{a_{t,i}^{2}}+\frac{c}{w}\log\frac{1}{\delta}.

(15)

Let’s call the event that (
15
)
happens
E_{1}
. Now, consider
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\nabla_{t,j}^{2}}{a_{t,i}^{2}}
.
We have

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\nabla_{t,j}^{2}}{a_{t,i}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left\lVert\nabla_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{a_{t,i}^{2}}=\frac{\left\lVert\nabla_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t-1,i}^{2}+\left\lVert\nabla_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}

\displaystyle\overset{(*)}{\leq}\frac{2\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}+2\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t-1,i}^{2}+2\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}+2\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}

\displaystyle\frac{\left\lVert\nabla_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{a_{t,i}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\frac{\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}+2\frac{\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}.

For
(*)
we use the fact that
\frac{x}{c+x}
is an increasing
function and
\left\lVert\nabla_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}=\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{t,\Psi_{i}}+\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}\leq 2\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}+2\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}
.
Let
\sigma_{\max}:=\max_{i\in[d]}\sigma_{i}
, then under event
E_{1}
,
we have with probability at least
1-c\delta
:

\displaystyle-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\nabla_{t,j}\xi_{t,j}}{a_{t,i}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{w\sigma_{j}^{2}\nabla_{t,j}^{2}}{a_{t,i}^{2}}+\frac{c}{w}\log\frac{1}{\delta}

\displaystyle\leq w\sigma_{\max}^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\nabla_{t,j}^{2}}{a_{t,i}^{2}}+\frac{c}{w}\log\frac{1}{\delta}

\displaystyle\leq w\sigma_{\max}^{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\left(2\frac{\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}+2\frac{\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}\right)+\frac{c}{w}\log\frac{1}{\delta}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\sigma_{\max}\sqrt{c\log\frac{1}{\delta}}}_{=:\alpha}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\left(2\frac{\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}+2\frac{\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}\right)+\sigma_{\max}\sqrt{c\log\frac{1}{\delta}}

(set
w:=\frac{\sqrt{c\log\frac{1}{\delta}}}{\sigma_{\max}}
)

\displaystyle=2\alpha\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\left(\frac{\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}+\frac{\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}\right)+\alpha.

where the second to last equality is due to choosing
w=\frac{\sqrt{c\log\frac{1}{\delta}}}{\sigma_{\max}}

and the last equality is letting
\alpha:=\sigma_{\max}\sqrt{c\log\frac{1}{\delta}}

for readability.

Let
M_{T,i}=\max_{t\leq T}\left|\xi_{t,i}\right|
. Using our notation,
we can define
M_{T,\Psi_{i}}:=\max_{t\leq T}\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert
.
Under event
E_{1}
(and our new bound for
C
), we have that with
probability at least
1-c\delta
:

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\frac{\left\lVert\nabla_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{(C)}}{\leq}\frac{\Delta_{1}}{\eta}-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\nabla_{t,j}\xi_{t,j}}{a_{t,i}}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert\left(\frac{\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{2b_{t,i}^{2}}+\frac{\left\lVert\nabla_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{2a_{t,i}^{2}}\right)+\eta L\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\log\frac{b_{T,i}}{b_{0,i}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Delta_{1}}{\eta}-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\nabla_{t,j}\xi_{t,j}}{a_{t,i}}

(16)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}M_{T,\Psi_{i}}\left(\frac{\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{2b_{t,i}^{2}}+\frac{\left\lVert\nabla_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{2a_{t,i}^{2}}\right)+\eta L\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\log\frac{b_{T,i}}{b_{0,i}}

\displaystyle\overset{\left(E_{1}\right)}{\leq}\frac{\Delta_{1}}{\eta}+2\alpha\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\left(\underbrace{\frac{\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}}_{\text{bound with (C)}}+\frac{\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}\right)+\alpha+

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}M_{T,\Psi_{i}}\left(\frac{\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{2b_{t,i}^{2}}+\frac{\left\lVert\nabla_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{2a_{t,i}^{2}}\right)+\eta L\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\log\frac{b_{T,i}}{b_{0,i}}

(17)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{(C)}}{\leq}\frac{\Delta_{1}}{\eta}+2\alpha\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\frac{\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}+\alpha+

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}M_{T,\Psi_{i}}\left(\frac{\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{2b_{t,i}^{2}}+\frac{\left\lVert\nabla_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{2a_{t,i}^{2}}\right)+\left(\eta L+4\alpha\right)\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\log\frac{b_{T,i}}{b_{0,i}}

(18)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Delta_{1}}{\eta}+2\alpha\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\frac{\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}+\alpha+

(19)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}M_{T,\Psi_{i}}\frac{\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{2b_{t,i}^{2}}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}M_{T,\Psi_{i}}\frac{\left\lVert\nabla_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{2a_{t,i}^{2}}+\left(\eta L+4\alpha\right)\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\log\frac{b_{T,i}}{b_{0,i}}.

(20)

Let us turn our attention to
M_{T,\Psi_{i}}:=\max_{t\leq T}\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert
.
Note that

\displaystyle\Pr\left[\max_{t\in[T]}\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}\geq A\right]

\displaystyle=\Pr\left[\exp\left(\frac{\max_{t\in[T]}\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{w}\right)\geq\exp\left(\frac{A}{w}\right)\right]

(for
w>0
)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\frac{A}{w}\right)\mathbb{\mathbb{E}}\left[\exp\left(\frac{\max_{t\in[T]}\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{w}\right)\right]

(Markov)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{A}{w}\right)\mathbb{\mathbb{E}}\left[\max_{t\in[T]}\exp\left(\frac{\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{w}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\frac{A}{w}\right)\sum_{t\in[T]}\mathbb{\mathbb{E}}\left[\exp\left(\frac{\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{w}\right)\right].

We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{\mathbb{E}}\left[\exp\left(\frac{\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{w}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{\mathbb{E}}\left[\exp\left(\frac{\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\xi_{t,j}^{2}}{w}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{\mathbb{E}}\left[\exp\left(\frac{\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\xi_{t,j}^{2}}{w}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{\mathbb{E}}\left[\prod_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\exp\left(\frac{\xi_{t,j}^{2}}{w}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\prod_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\mathbb{\mathbb{E}}\left[\exp\left(\frac{\xi_{t,j}^{2}}{w}\right)\right].

(independence)

Since sub-gaussianity give us

\mathbb{\mathbb{E}}\left[\exp\left(\lambda^{2}\xi_{t,i}^{2}\right)\right]\leq\exp\left(\lambda^{2}\sigma_{i}^{2}\right),\forall\left|\lambda\right|\leq\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}},\forall i\in\left[d\right],

we have
\mathbb{\mathbb{E}}\left[\exp\left(\frac{\xi_{t,j}^{2}}{w}\right)\right]\leq\exp\left(\frac{\sigma_{j}^{2}}{w}\right)

if
\sqrt{\frac{1}{w}}\leq\frac{1}{\sigma_{j}}
. We pick
w:=\left\lVert\sigma_{\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}=\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\sigma_{j}^{2}\geq\sigma_{j}^{2},\ \forall j\in\Psi_{i}

. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{\mathbb{E}}\left[\exp\left(\frac{\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{\left\lVert\sigma_{\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\exp\left(\frac{\sigma_{j}^{2}}{\left\lVert\sigma_{\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\frac{\left\lVert\sigma_{\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{\left\lVert\sigma_{\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}\right)=1.

(21)

We have actually shown that
\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}
is a
\left\lVert\sigma_{\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}
-subgaussian
random variable in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
(see Proposition 2.5.2 in
(

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert b_{T}\right\rVert_{1}\left(\frac{\Delta_{1}}{\eta}+cH(\delta)+\left(\ln T/\delta\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+c\eta L+4c\alpha\right)\log\frac{\left\lVert b_{T}\right\rVert_{1}}{b_{0,\min}}\right)

(35)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert b_{T}\right\rVert_{1}\left(\frac{\Delta_{1}}{\eta}+cH(\delta)+\left(\ln T/\delta\left\lVert\sigma\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+c\eta L+4c\alpha\right)\log\frac{\left\lVert b_{T}\right\rVert_{1}}{b_{0,\min}}\right).

(36)

It remains to bound
\left\lVert b_{T}\right\rVert_{1}
. We start again from smoothness
of
f
:

\displaystyle\Delta_{t+1}-\Delta_{t}

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle\nabla_{t},x_{t+1}-x_{t}\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert x_{t+1}-x_{t}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=-\eta\left\langle\nabla_{t},\frac{\widehat{\nabla}_{t}}{b_{t}}\right\rangle+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}\left\lVert\frac{\widehat{\nabla}_{t}}{b_{t}}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=-\eta\left\langle\widehat{\nabla}_{t}-\xi_{t},\frac{\widehat{\nabla}_{t}}{b_{t}}\right\rangle+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\widehat{\nabla}_{t,\Psi_{j}}^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\eta\left\langle\widehat{\nabla}_{t},\frac{\widehat{\nabla}_{t}}{b_{t}}\right\rangle+\eta\left\langle\xi_{t},\frac{\widehat{\nabla}_{t}}{b_{t}}\right\rangle+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\frac{\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\eta\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\widehat{\nabla}_{t,j}^{2}}{b_{t,i}}+\eta\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\xi_{t,j}\widehat{\nabla}_{t,j}}{b_{t,i}}+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\frac{\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\eta\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\frac{\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}}+\frac{\eta^{2}L}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\frac{\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}+\eta\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\xi_{t,j}\widehat{\nabla}_{t,j}}{b_{t,i}}.

(37)

Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\xi_{t,j}\widehat{\nabla}_{t,j}}{b_{t,i}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\xi_{t,j}^{2}}{b_{t,i}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\widehat{\nabla}_{t,j}^{2}}{b_{t,i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{j\in\Psi_{i}}\frac{\xi_{t,j}^{2}}{b_{t,i}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\frac{\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}}.

Plugging back in, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{t+1}-\Delta_{t}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\frac{\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}}+\eta^{2}L\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\frac{\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}+\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\frac{\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}}.

Summing over
T
and rearranging, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\frac{\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\Delta_{1}}{\eta}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\frac{\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}}+2\eta L\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\frac{\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}

\displaystyle\implies\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\frac{\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\Delta_{1}}{\eta}+2\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\frac{\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\left(\frac{4\eta L}{b_{t,i}^{2}}-\frac{1}{b_{t,i}}\right)\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}.

We can bound
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\left(\frac{4\eta L}{b_{t,i}^{2}}-\frac{1}{b_{t,i}}\right)\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}

as follows. Consider
i\in[c]
. Let
\tau_{i}=\max\left\{t\leq T\mid b_{t,i}\leq 4\eta L\right\}

so that

implies
b_{t,i}>4\eta L\iff\frac{4\eta L}{b_{t,i}^{2}}<\frac{1}{b_{t,i}}

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(\frac{4\eta L}{b_{t,i}^{2}}-\frac{1}{b_{t,i}}\right)\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{i}}\left(\frac{4\eta L}{b_{t,i}^{2}}-\frac{1}{b_{t,i}}\right)\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}+\sum_{t=\tau_{i}+1}^{T}\left(\underbrace{\frac{4\eta L}{b_{t,i}^{2}}-\frac{1}{b_{t,i}}}_{<0}\right)\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{i}}\left(\frac{4\eta L}{b_{t,i}^{2}}-\frac{1}{b_{t,i}}\right)\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\eta L\sum_{t=1}^{\tau_{i}}\frac{\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 8\eta L\log\frac{b_{\tau_{i},i}}{b_{0,i}}\leq 8\eta L\log\frac{4\eta L}{b_{0,i}}.

Hence, we have

\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\frac{\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}}\leq\frac{4\Delta_{1}}{\eta}+2\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\frac{\left\lVert\xi_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}}+8\eta L\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\log\frac{4\eta L}{b_{0,i}}.

Consider the LHS

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\frac{\left\lVert\widehat{\nabla}_{t,\Psi_{i}}\right\rVert^{2}}{b_{t,i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\frac{b_{t,i}^{2}-b_{t-1,i}^{2}}{b_{t,i}}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}b_{t,i}-\frac{b_{t-1,i}^{2}}{b_{t,i}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}b_{t,i}-\frac{b_{t-1,i}^{2}}{b_{t-1,i}}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}b_{t,i}-b_{t-1,i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}b_{t,i}-b_{t-1,i}=\sum_{i=0}^{c-1}b_{T,i}-b_{0,i}

Hence, we have

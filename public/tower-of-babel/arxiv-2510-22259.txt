Infinitely many families of distance-optimal binary linear codes with respect to the sphere packing bound
Hao Chen, Conghui Xie, Cunsheng Ding

Abstract
R. W. Hamming published the Hamming codes and the sphere packing bound in 1950. In the past 75 years, infinite families of distance-optimal linear codes over finite fields with minimum distance at most 8 with respect to the sphere packing bound have been reported in the literature. However, it is a 75-year-old open problem in coding theory whether there is an infinite family of distance-optimal linear codes over finite fields with arbitrarily large minimum distance with respect to the sphere packing bound. This main objective of this paper is to settle this long-standing open problem in coding theory. As by-products, several infinite families of distance-optimal binary codes with small minimum distances are presented. Two infinite families of binary five-weight codes are reported. Some open problems are also proposed.

Introduction

1-A

Linear codes, cyclic codes and BCH codes

Let
q
be a power of a prime and let
n
be a positive integer.
An
[n,k,d]
linear code
{\mathcal{C}}
over
{\mathrm{GF}}(q)
is a
k
-dimensional linear subspace of
{\mathrm{GF}}(q)

with minimum Hamming distance
d
. An
[n,k,d]
linear code
{\mathcal{C}}
over
{\mathrm{GF}}(q)
is said to
be distance-optimal if there is no
[n,k]
linear code
{\mathcal{C}}
over
{\mathrm{GF}}(q)
with distance at least

d+1
. Throughout this paper, we use
\dim({\mathcal{C}})
and
d({\mathcal{C}})
to denote the dimension and
minimum distance of the linear code
{\mathcal{C}}
, respectively. Let
A_{i}({\mathcal{C}})
denote the number of
codewords with Hamming weigh
i
in
{\mathcal{C}}
. The polynomial
\sum_{j=0}^{n}A_{i}({\mathcal{C}})z^{j}
is
called the weight enumerator of
{\mathcal{C}}
.

Let
\textbf{b}=\mathbf{(}b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{n-1})
and
\textbf{c}=\mathbf{(}c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1})
be two vectors in
{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{n}
. The standard inner product of the two vectors
b
and
c
is
defined by

\textbf{b}\textbf{c}^{T}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}{b_{i}}{c_{i}}.

The (Euclidean) dual code of
\mathcal{C}
, denoted by
\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
, is defined by

\mathcal{C}^{\perp}=\{\textbf{b}\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{n}:\textbf{b}\textbf{c}^{T}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}{b_{i}}{c_{i}}=0\ \ \forall\ \textbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}\}.

An
[n,k,d]
linear code
{\mathcal{C}}
over
{\mathrm{GF}}(q)
is said to be
cyclic
if

(c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1})\in{\mathcal{C}}
implies
(c_{n-1},c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-2})\in{\mathcal{C}}
.
By identifying a vector
(c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1})\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{n}

with the polynomial

c_{0}+c_{1}x+c_{2}x^{2}+\cdots+c_{n-1}x^{n-1}\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle,

a code
{\mathcal{C}}
of length
n
over
{\mathrm{GF}}(q)
corresponds to a subset of the quotient ring

{\mathrm{GF}}(q)[x]/

\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
.
It is well known that
a linear code
{\mathcal{C}}
is cyclic if and only if the corresponding subset in
{\mathrm{GF}}(q)[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle

is an ideal of the quotient ring
{\mathrm{GF}}(q)[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
.
Notice that every ideal of
{\mathrm{GF}}(q)[x]/\langle x^{n}-1\rangle
is principal. Let
{\mathcal{C}}=\langle g(x)\rangle
be a
cyclic code, where
g(x)
is monic and has the smallest degree among all the
generators of
{\mathcal{C}}
. Then
g(x)
must be unique and a divisor of
x^{n}-1
, and is called the
generator polynomial

of
{\mathcal{C}}

and
h(x)=(x^{n}-1)/g(x)
is referred to as the
parity-check
polynomial of
{\mathcal{C}}
.
The dual code
{\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}
of the cyclic code
{\mathcal{C}}
is also cyclic and has the generator polynomial

h^{*}(x):=h_{k}^{-1}x^{k}h(x^{-1})
, where
h_{k}
is the coefficient of the term
x^{k}
in
h(x)
.

Recall that the generator polynomial of a cyclic code
{\mathcal{C}}
of length
n
must be a divisor of the
polynomial
x^{n}-1
over
{\mathrm{GF}}(q)
. To study cyclic codes of length
n
over
{\mathrm{GF}}(q)
, we must
study the factorization of
x^{n}-1
over
{\mathrm{GF}}(q)
. To this end, we must define and deal with
the
q
-cyclotomic cosets modulo
n
.

Let
\gcd(n,q)=1
.
Let
\mathbb{{Z}}_{n}
denote the set
\{0,1,2,\ldots,n-1\}
. Let
s
be an integer with
0\leq s<n
. The
q
-cyclotomic coset of
s
modulo
n
is defined by

C_{s}=\{s,sq,sq^{2},\ldots,sq^{\ell_{s}-1}\}\bmod n\subseteq\mathbb{{Z}}_{n},

where
\ell_{s}
is the smallest positive integer such that
s\equiv sq^{\ell_{s}}\pmod{n}
, and is the size of the

q
-cyclotomic coset
C_{s}
. The smallest integer in
C_{s}
is named as the
coset leader
of
C_{s}
.
Let
\Gamma_{(n,q)}
be the set of all the coset leaders. We have then
C_{s}\cap C_{t}=\emptyset
for any two
distinct elements
s
and
t
in
\Gamma_{(n,q)}
, and

\displaystyle\bigcup_{s\in\Gamma_{(n,q)}}C_{s}=\mathbb{{Z}}_{n}.

(1)

Consequently, the distinct
q
-cyclotomic cosets modulo
n
partition
\mathbb{{Z}}_{n}
.

Let
m={\mathrm{ord}}_{n}(q)
be the order of
q
modulo
n
, which is the smallest positive integer such that

q^{m}\equiv 1\pmod{n}
, and let
\alpha
be a primitive element of
{\mathrm{GF}}(q^{m})
. Put
\beta=\alpha^{(q^{m}-1)/n}
.
Then
\beta
is a primitive
n
-th root of unity in
{\mathrm{GF}}(q^{m})
. The minimal polynomial
{\mathsf{M}}_{\beta^{s}}(x)

of
\beta^{s}
over
{\mathrm{GF}}(q)
is the monic polynomial of the smallest degree over
{\mathrm{GF}}(q)
with
\beta^{s}

as a root. It is easily seen that this polynomial is given by

{\mathsf{M}}_{\beta^{s}}(x)=\prod_{i\in C_{s}}(x-\beta^{i})\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)[x],

which is irreducible over
{\mathrm{GF}}(q)
. It then follows from (
1
) that

\displaystyle x^{n}-1=\prod_{s\in\Gamma_{(n,q)}}{\mathsf{M}}_{\beta^{s}}(x),

(2)

which is the factorization of
x^{n}-1
into irreducible factors over
{\mathrm{GF}}(q)
. This canonical factorization of
x^{n}-1

over
{\mathrm{GF}}(q)
is fundamental for the study of cyclic codes.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a cyclic code of length
n
with generator polynomial
g(x)
.
The set
T=\{0\leq i\leq n-1:g(\beta^{i})=0\}
is called the

defining set
of
\mathcal{C}
with respect to
\beta
.

Let
\delta
be an integer with
2\leq\delta\leq n
and let
b
be an integer.
A
BCH code
over
{\mathrm{GF}}(q)

with length
n
and
designed distance

\delta
, denoted by
{\mathcal{C}}_{(q,n,\delta,b)}
, is the cyclic code with
generator polynomial

\displaystyle g_{(q,n,\delta,b)}(x)={\mathrm{lcm}}({\mathsf{M}}_{\beta^{b}}(x),{\mathsf{M}}_{\beta^{b+1}}(x),\ldots,{\mathsf{M}}_{\beta^{b+\delta-2}}(x)),

(3)

where the least common multiple is computed over
{\mathrm{GF}}(q)[x]
.

BCH codes over finite fields form a subclass of cyclic codes with many interesting properties and are closely related to many
areas of mathematics. In many cases BCH codes are the best linear codes.
For example, among all binary cyclic codes of odd length
n
with
n\leq 125
the best cyclic code is always a BCH code
except for two special cases
[
6
]
. However, certain BCH codes have bad parameters also.
Reed-Solomon codes are also BCH codes and are widely used in communication
devices and consumer electronics. This shows the importance of BCH codes in practice.

Binary BCH codes were invented by Hocquenghem
[
18
]
and independently by Bose and Ray-Chaudhuri
[
2
]
.
They were generalized to BCH codes over finite fields by Gorenstein and Zierler in 1961
[
10
]
.
In the past sixty-five years, a lot of progress on the study of BCH codes has been made
[
7
]
.
However, the parameters of most BCH codes are open.

1-B

The motivation of this paper

The
sphere packing bound
, also called the
Hamming
bound,
states that

\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\rfloor}\binom{n}{i}(q-1)^{i}\leq q^{n-k}

for any
[n,k,d]
linear code over
{\mathrm{GF}}(q)
.
This bound was published in 1950 by R. W. Hamming
[
13
]
.

A code meeting the sphere packing bound is said to be
perfect
.
Since all perfect codes are known and very rare, it is very interesting to look for distance-optimal linear codes
with respect to the sphere packing bound.
In the literature there are some infinite families of distance-optimal linear codes with minimum distance
at most 8 with respect to the sphere packing bound (see Section
2-A
).
However, it is a 75-year-old open problem in coding theory whether there is an infinite family of distance-optimal linear codes over finite fields with arbitrarily large minimum distance with respect to the sphere packing bound.
The motivation of this paper is this 75-year-old open problem in coding theory.

1-C

The objectives and methodology of this paper

The key objective of this paper is to settle the long-standing open problem in coding theory introduced
in Section
1-B
. Another objective is to construct infinite families of distance-optimal binary codes with small minimum distances with respect to the sphere packing bound.

To achieve these objectives in this paper,
we will study binary BCH codes with length
n
of the following forms:

•

n=(2^{2s}+1)(2^{s}-1)
for positive integer
s
.

•

n=2^{2s}+2^{s}+1
for positive integer
s
.

•

n=(2^{s}-1)/\lambda
for positive integer
s
, where
\lambda
is a constant divisor of
2^{s}-1
.

We will also study their related codes.

1-D

The achievements of this paper

The main achievement of this paper is the settlement of the 75-year-old open problem in coding theory introduced
in Section
1-B
by presenting infinitely many families of distance-optimal binary codes with arbitrarily large minimum distance.
In addition, several infinite families of distance-optimal binary codes with small minimum distances are presented.
Two infinite families of binary five-weight codes are documented in this paper. Some research problems are proposed.

3
Preliminaries

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a cyclic code of length
n
over
{\mathrm{GF}}(q)
with generator polynomial

g(x)=\prod_{i\in T}(x-\beta^{i}),

where
\beta
is an
n
-th root of unity over an extension field
{\mathrm{GF}}(q^{m})
,
T
is the union of some
q
-cyclotomic cosets modulo
n
, and is called
the
defining set
of
{\mathcal{C}}
with respect to
\beta
.
We say that
T
contains a set of
s
consecutive integers if there is some
integer
a
such that
\{a,a+1,\ldots,a+s-1\}\bmod n\subseteq T
.
The following
is a simple but very useful lower bound (see
[
2
]
and
[
18
]
).

Theorem 3.1

(BCH bound)
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a cyclic code of length
n
over
{\mathrm{GF}}(q)
with defining set
T
and minimum distance
d
. Assume that
T

contains
\delta-1
consecutive integers for some integer
\delta
. Then
d\geq\delta
.

The BCH bound depends on the choice of the primitive
n
-th root of unity
\beta
.
Different choices of the primitive root may yield different lower bounds. When applying
the BCH bound, it is important to choose the right primitive root so that the best lower bound is obtained. However, it is open how
to choose such a primitive root.
By definition, for a BCH code
{\mathcal{C}}_{(q,n,\delta,b)}
we have

d({\mathcal{C}}_{(q,n,\delta,b)})\geq\delta.

The following result is useful in some cases
[
20
]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

The BCH code
{\mathcal{C}}_{(q,n,\delta,b)}
has minimum distance
d=\delta
if
\delta
divides
\gcd(n,b-1)
.

The following lemma was proved in
[
1
]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
n
be a positive integer such that
q^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}<n\leq q^{m}-1
, where

m={\mathrm{ord}}_{n}(q)
. Then the
q
-cyclotomic coset
C_{s}=\{sq^{j}\bmod{n}:0\leq j\leq m-1\}
has cardinality

m
for all
s
in the range
1\leq s\leq nq^{\lceil m/2\rceil}/(q^{m}-1)
. In addition, every
s
with

s\not\equiv 0\pmod{q}
in this range is a coset leader.

4-A

The parameters of the codes
{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,3,1)}
and their duals

In this subsection, we study the codes
{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,3,1)}
and their duals and do some preparations for investigating the codes
\overline{{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,3,1)}}
in Section
4-B
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
s\geq 2
. Then code
{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,3,1)}
has parameters
[n,n-4s,3]
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,3,1)}^{\perp}
has parameters

[n,4s,2^{3s-1}-2^{2s-1}]
and weight enumerator

1+(2^{4s}-1-n)z^{2^{3s-1}-2^{2s-1}}+nz^{2^{3s-1}}.

Proof.

By definition and Lemma
4.1
,
{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,3,1)}
is the binary cyclic code of length
n
with generator polynomial

\mathsf{M}_{\beta}(x)
. Then by Lemma
4.1
we have

\dim({\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,3,1)})=n-|C_{1}|=n-4s

and

\dim({\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,3,1)}^{\perp})=4s.

By definition,
{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,3,1)}^{\perp}
is an irreducible cyclic code.

Let
\gamma=\beta^{-1}
. Then
\gamma
is an
n
-th primitive root of unity.
Then the trace representation of
{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,3,1)}^{\perp}
is given by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,3,1)}^{\perp}=\{{\mathbf{c}}_{a}=({\mathrm{Tr}}(a\gamma^{i}))_{i=0}^{n-1}:a\in{\mathrm{GF}}(2^{4s})\},

(5)

where
{\mathrm{Tr}}
is the absolute trace function. Then
{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,3,1)}^{\perp}
is an irreducible cyclic code
and its weight enumerator follows from Theorem 23 in
[
9
]
.

It follows from the BCH bound that the minimum distance of
{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,3,1)}
is at least
3
.
By the fourth Pless power moment
[
19
]
, we have

\displaystyle 8\sum_{j=0}^{n}j^{3}A_{j}({\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,3,1)}^{\perp})=2^{4s}(n^{2}(n+3)-6A_{3}({\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,3,1)})).

Solving the equation above yields that

A_{3}({\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,3,1)})=\frac{(u-2)(u-1)(u^{2}+1)}{6}>0,

where
u=2^{s}
. As a result,
d({\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,3,1)})=3.

∎

4-B

The extended codes
\overline{{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,3,1)}}
of the codes
{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,3,1)}

Our first infinite family of distance-optimal binary codes is given in the following theorem.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
s\geq 2
. Then the extended code
\overline{{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,3,1)}}
has parameters
[n+1,n-4s,4]

and is distance-optimal with respect to the sphere packing bound.
Furthermore,

\overline{{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,3,1)}}^{\perp}
has parameters

[n+1,4s+1,n+1-2^{3s-1}]
and weight enumerator

\displaystyle 1+nz^{n+1-2^{3s-1}}+(2^{4s}-1-n)z^{2^{3s-1}-2^{2s-1}}+

\displaystyle(2^{4s}-1-n)z^{n+1-2^{3s-1}+2^{2s-1}}+nz^{2^{3s-1}}+z^{n+1}.

Proof.

Since
3
is the minimum distance of
{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,3,1)}
and is odd,
the parameters of
\overline{{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,3,1)}}
follow from the parameters of

{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,3,1)}
directly.

We now prove the distance-optimality of the code
\overline{{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,3,1)}}
.
Note that
s\geq 2

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{2}\binom{n+1}{i}=\frac{n^{2}+3n+4}{2}>2^{4s+1}.

is

7
Infinitely many families of distance-optimal binary cyclic codes with arbitrarily large minimum distance

Let
s\geq 3
be a positive integer and
n=(2^{s}-1)/\lambda
, where
\lambda<2^{\lfloor s/2\rfloor}
and
\lambda
is a constant divisor of
2^{s}-1
. Hence,
\lambda
does not vary according to
s
. Let
\alpha
be a primitive element of
{\mathrm{GF}}(2^{s})
, and let
\beta=\alpha^{\lambda}
.
Then
\beta
is an
n
-th primitive root of unity.
Let
\mathsf{M}_{\beta}(x)
denote the minimal polynomial of
\beta
over
{\mathrm{GF}}(2)
. Throughout this section, we fix the notation. We will need the following lemma in this section.

Lemma 7.1
.

Recall that
C_{i}
denotes the
2
-cyclotomic coset modulo
n
containing
i
.
Then the following hold.

•

{\mathrm{ord}}_{n}(2)=s
.

•

For each odd
i
with
1\leq i\leq 2^{\lceil s/2\rceil}/\lambda
, we have
|C_{i}|=s
.

•

All the odd
i
with
1\leq i\leq 2^{\lceil s/2\rceil}/\lambda
are the coset leaders of these
C_{i}
.

Proof.

The conclusion of the first part is known (see
[
24
]
for example).
Then the desired conclusions of the remaining two parts follow from Lemma
3.3
.
∎

For any fixed integer
\ell\geq 2
, define

s_{2}(\ell,\lambda)
to be the smallest positive integer such that
(2\ell-1)\lambda\leq 2^{\lceil s_{2}(\ell,\lambda)/2\rceil}
.
We consider the BCH code
{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,2\ell,0)}
. If
s\geq s_{2}(\ell,\lambda)
, by definition and Lemma

7.1
,

\displaystyle\dim({\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,2\ell,0)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}\deg(\mathsf{M}_{\beta^{2i-1}}(x))-1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}|C_{2i-1}|-1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n-(\ell-1)s-1.

By the BCH bound we have

d({\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,2\ell,0)})\geq 2\ell
.
Note that

\ell!\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\binom{n}{i}-2(\lambda n+1)^{\ell-1}\right)=n^{\ell}+\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}a_{i}(\ell,\lambda)n^{i},

where these
a_{i}(\ell,\lambda)
are integers depending on
\ell
and
\lambda
only.
Define

a(\ell,\lambda)=\max_{0\leq i\leq\ell-1}\{|a_{i}(\ell,\lambda)|\}

and

s_{1}(\ell,\lambda)
to be the smallest positive integer such that
1+\ell\lambda a(\ell,\lambda)<2^{\lceil s_{1}(\ell,\lambda)/2\rceil}
.
If
s\geq s_{1}(\ell,\lambda)
, we have

\displaystyle n^{\ell}+\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}a_{i}(\ell,\lambda)n^{i}\geq n^{\ell}-\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}|a_{i}(\ell,\lambda)|n^{i}\geq n^{\ell}-a(\ell,\lambda)\ell n^{\ell-1}>0.

Define
s(\ell,\lambda)=\max\{s_{1}(\ell,\lambda),s_{2}(\ell,\lambda)\}
.
It then follows from the sphere packing bound that
d({\mathcal{C}}_{(2,2^{s}-1,2\ell,0)})\leq 2\ell
if
s\geq s(\ell,\lambda)
.
Consequently,
d({\mathcal{C}}_{(2,2^{s}-1,2\ell,0)})=2\ell
if
s\geq s(\ell,\lambda)
.
We have now proved the following theorem.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let notation be the same as before. If
s\geq s(\ell,\lambda)
, then the BCH code
{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,2\ell,0)}
has parameters
[n,n-1-(\ell-1)s,2\ell]
and is distance-optimal with respect to the sphere packing bound.

Remark 7.3
.

Theorem
7.2
is a generalisation of some known results in the literature. For example,
when
\lambda=1
and
\ell\in\{1,2,3,4\}
, the parameters of the codes
{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,2\ell,0)}
are known in
the literature (see
[
8
]
for example), although the distance-optimality with respect to
the sphere packing bound may not have been mentioned in the literature.
When
\ell=2
and
\ell=4
, the code
{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,2\ell,0)}
was known to be distance-optimal
[
25
]
.
When
\ell=3
, the code
{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,2\ell,0)}
was known to be distance-optimal
[
5
]

Remark 7.4
.

For each constant divisor
\lambda
of
2^{s}-1
and each
\ell\geq 2
, there is a constant

s(\ell,\lambda)
such that
\{{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,2\ell,0)}:s\geq s(\ell,\lambda)\}
is an infinite family of
distant-optimal binary cyclic codes with respect to the sphere packing bound, where each code

{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,2\ell,0)}
has parameters

[n,n-1-(\ell-1)s,2\ell]
. This shows that Theorem
7.2
has given infinitely many families of
distant-optimal binary cyclic codes with respect to the sphere packing bound. Hence,
Theorem
7.2
has made a 75-year breakthrough in coding theory in the sense that
it gives the first infinite family of distance-optimal binary cyclic codes with arbitrarily large minimum distance
with respect to the sphere packing bound.

Open problems

For the three types of binary BCH codes
{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,\delta,b)}
, where

•

n=(2^{2s}+1)(2^{s}-1)
for positive integer
s
, or

•

n=2^{2s}+2^{s}+1
for positive integer
s
, or

•

n=(2^{s}-1)/\lambda
for positive integer
s
and
\lambda>1
being a constant divisor of

2^{s}-1
,

we have the following open problems.

Open Problem 8.1
.

Determine the automorphism groups of code
{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,\delta,b)}
and code
\overline{{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,\delta,b)}}
.

Open Problem 8.2
.

Does
\overline{{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,\delta,1)}}
support
2
-designs for certain
\delta

[
8
]
?

Open Problem 8.3
.

Determine
d({\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,\delta,b)})
for
\delta\geq 7
and
d(\overline{{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,\delta,b)}})
for
\delta\geq 8
.

Open Problem 8.4
.

Are there
\delta
and
b
such that
\dim({\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,\delta,b)})\geq(n-1)/2
and

d({\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,\delta,b)})\geq\sqrt{n}/2
?

Open Problem 8.5
.

Determine
d({\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,\delta,b)}^{\perp})
and
d(\overline{{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,\delta,b)}}^{\perp})

Open Problem 8.6
.

Determine the covering radii of the distance-optimal binary codes presented in this paper.

It would be interesting to settle some of the open problems under certain conditions.

Summary and concluding remarks

The following infinite families of distance-optimal binary linear codes were presented in this paper.

The infinite family of the binary codes

This paper has made a 75-year breakthrough in coding theory in the sense that
it presents the first infinite family of distance-optimal linear codes with arbitrarily large minimum distance
with respect to the sphere packing bound.

The reader is informed that the idea for constructing distance-optimal binary cyclic codes with
respect to the sphere packing bound in Section
7
does not work for the code
{\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,2\ell,0)}
for
n
being
of the following firms:

•

n=(2^{2s}+1)(2^{s}-1)=\frac{2^{4s}-1}{2^{s}+1}
for positive integer
s
and
\ell\geq 4
(it works only for
\ell=2
and
\ell=3
).

•

n=2^{2s}+2^{s}+1=\frac{2^{3s}-1}{2^{s}-1}
for positive integer
s
and
\ell\geq 3
(it works only for
\ell=2
).

In the two cases
\lambda=2^{s}+1
and
\lambda=2^{s}-1
, the value of
\lambda
depends on
s
and
is not a constant divisor of
2^{4s}-1
and
2^{3s}-1
. When
n=\frac{2^{4s}-1}{2^{s}+1}
and
\ell\geq 4
, and when
n=\frac{2^{3s}-1}{2^{s}-1}
and
\ell\geq 3
, the sphere packing bound cannot be used to prove that
d({\mathcal{C}}_{(2,n,2\ell,0}))\leq 2\ell
for
s
being large enough.
This justifies that we need to treat the BCH codes of length
n
of different forms separately.

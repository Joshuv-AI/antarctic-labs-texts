Deformed Schur indices and Macdonald polynomials
Yasuyuki Hatsuda

Abstract
The Schur index in four-dimensional 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 super Yang-Mills theory with U ⁡ ( N ) U(N) gauge group has a natural two-parameter deformation. We find that a matrix integral in such a deformed Schur index can be exactly evaluated by using Macdonald polynomials. The resulting expression is a simple combinatorial summation over partitions. An extension to line operator indices is straightforward. In particular, for an anti-symmetric representation, the line operator index has a relatively simple form. We further discuss infinite N N analysis and finite N N giant graviton expansions.

Introduction

Superconformal indices
[
1
,
2
]
are a powerful tool to probe BPS spectra in superconformal field theories.
They can be used to test the AdS/CFT correspondence as well as to study strongly coupled non-Lagrangian theories. In four-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=4
super Yang-Mills theory with
U(N)
gauge group, the superconformal index is well-known, and results in a unitary matrix integral
[
2
]
.

The
\mathcal{N}=4
superconformal index has four independent fugacities. There are many specializations.
In this work, we focus on one of them, in which three of four independently remain.
More concretely, we analyze the following reduced index:

\displaystyle I_{N}(t,u;q)=\int_{U(N)}dU\exp\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\frac{t^{n}+u^{n}-t^{n}u^{n}-q^{n}}{1-q^{n}}\Tr(U^{n})\Tr[(U^{\dagger})^{n}]\right),

(1)

where
dU
is the (normalized) Haar measure of
U(N)
.
An advantage of this reduction is that the integrand can be rewritten in terms of the
q
-Pochhammer symbol, while the original superconformal index is rewritten in terms of the elliptic gamma function. The analysis of the former gets simpler.
On the one hand this index is regarded as a reduction of the full superconformal index, but on the other hand it is also regarded as a two-parameter deformation of the Schur index
[
3
,
4
]
, which is a very special limit of the superconformal index.
We refer to
I_{N}(t,u;q)
as the
deformed Schur index
.
1
1

1

From the perspective in the next section, it seems natural to refer to it as “Macdonald index”. However this terminology has already been used in a similar but slightly different context in
[
4
]
. To avoid confusion, we do not use this terminology.

Note that the similar limit was considered (but not analyzed in detail) for other gauge groups in
[
5
]
.

Clearly, the deformed Schur index (
1
) also takes the form of the unitary matrix integral. Its evaluation is still far from obvious.
A powerful approach to evaluate it is the character expansion method
[
6
]
.
Another approach is the so-called Fermi-gas formalism
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
.
2
2

2

Precisely speaking, to apply the Fermi-gas formalism, we need an additional constraint that
q=tu
.

In this work, we find the third approach based on less familiar symmetric orthogonal polynomials,
Macdonald polynomials
.
By using them, the unitary matrix integral (
1
) can be directly performed for arbitrary
N
. We find the following surprisingly simple sum representation:

\displaystyle I_{N}(t,u;q)=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(t;t)_{N}(t^{N}q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}u^{|\lambda|}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}\frac{(t^{N-i+1};q)_{\lambda_{i}}}{(t^{N-i}q;q)_{\lambda_{i}}}.

(2)

where
\lambda
is a partition whose length
\ell(\lambda)
is less than or equal to
N
(see Appendix
A
).
We stress that the result (
2
) is perturbative in
u
but exact in
t
and
q
.
As a consequence, it is particularly useful in the study of finite
N
corrections, also known as giant graviton expansions
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
8
]
.
A similar evaluation is possible for line operator indices, in which we insert characters of the
U(N)
gauge group into (
1
).

The organization of this paper is as follow. In the next section, we derive (
2
) by using the Macdonald polynomials. We use known mathematical results on the Macdonald polynomials. All the ingredients needed in this work are reviewed in Appendix
A
. We consider various special limits, and confirm the validity of (
2
). We also comment on advantages of our result (
2
), compared to the result obtained by the character expansion method. We can extend the similar computation to line operator insertions. In particular, the line operator index for an anti-symmetric representation has a simple expression. In Section
3
, we propose a new systematic way to deal with the large
N
analysis. We also explore finite
N
corrections to the indices. We find some new analytic results on the giant graviton expansions. Section
4
is devoted to future directions. In Appendix
B
, we show additional results on half-indices of interfaces with
U(N)
and
U(M)
gauge groups.

2
Deformed Schur indices from Macdonald polynomials

2.1
Superconformal indices and various limits

We start with a matrix integral representation of the
\mathcal{N}=4
superconformal index for the
U(N)
gauge group. As shown in
[
2
]
, it is given by

\displaystyle I_{N}(t,u,v;p,q)=\int_{U(N)}dU\exp\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{f(t^{n},u^{n},v^{n};p^{n},q^{n})}{n}\Tr(U^{n})\Tr[(U^{\dagger})^{n}]\right),

(3)

where
f(t,u,v;p,q)
is the single letter index of the theory. In our convention, it is given by

\displaystyle f(t,u,v;p,q)=1-\frac{(1-t)(1-u)(1-v)}{(1-p)(1-q)}.

(4)

It is important to note that five parameters
(t,u,v;p,q)
are not independent. We have a constraint

\displaystyle pq=tuv.

(5)

Therefore four of five are actually independent.
Since the integrand in (
3
) is a class function of the unitary matrix
U
, we can use Weyl’s integration formula (see
[
13
]
for instance). It reduces to an integral over the maximal torus
\mathbb{T}^{N}
. The resulting integral takes the form

\displaystyle I_{N}(t,u,v;p,q)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!}\oint_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{dx_{i}}{2\pi ix_{i}}\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}\left(1-\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}}\right)

(6)

\displaystyle\times\exp\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{f(t^{n},u^{n},v^{n};p^{n},q^{n})}{n}p_{n}(x)p_{n}(x^{-1})\right),

where
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})
are eigenvalues of
U
, and
p_{n}(x)
is the power sum symmetric polynomial (see Appendix
A
). The integration contour for each
x_{i}
goes around a unit circle counterclockwise.

It is not easy to perform this
N
-dimensional integral for the full superconformal index exactly. Instead, we explore a special case in the fugacity configuration so that the exact evaluation is possible. In this work, we focus on the following slice of the fugacity space:

\displaystyle v=p=0.

(7)

In this special case, the fugacity constraint (
5
) is automatically satisfied, and we can independently change
(t,u,q)
.
The resulting single letter index is now reduced to

\displaystyle f(t,u;q)=1-\frac{(1-t)(1-u)}{1-q}=\frac{t+u-tu-q}{1-q}.

(8)

The matrix integral is thus given by (
1
).
There are several interesting specializations of this index.

For
t=q
or
u=q
, things are dramatically simplified. In this case, the single letter index reduces to
f(q,u;q)=u
or
f(t,q;q)=t
, and it does not depend on
q
.
In this very special limit, the index counts the
1/2
BPS operators. It is well-known that the index is exactly given by

\displaystyle I_{N}(q,u;q)=\frac{1}{(u;u)_{N}},\qquad I_{N}(t,q;q)=\frac{1}{(t;t)_{N}}.

(9)

If taking the limit
q\to 0
, we have
f(t,u;0)=t+u-tu
. This case counts
1/4
BPS operators.
The index in this limit is more non-trivial than the
1/2
BPS case.
We will derive an exact form of its index in the next subsection.

Also taking the limit
u\to 0
, the single letter index is given by

\displaystyle f(t,0;q)=\frac{t-q}{1-q}.

(10)

In this case, the corresponding index is known as the “half-index” (of Neumann boundary condition)
[
14
]
.
Due to an obvious symmetry between
t
and
u
, the limit
t\to 0
is essentially same as
u\to 0
.
However, in the analysis in the next subsection, these two limits look different, and lead to the equivalent result non-trivially.

Finally, if we set
u=q/t
, the resulting index is known as the flavored Schur index. The single letter index is now given by

\displaystyle f(t,q/t;q)=\frac{t+q/t-2q}{1-q}.

(11)

For the further specialization to
t=q^{1/2}
(
i.e.
,
u=q^{1/2}
), the index
I_{N}(q^{1/2},q^{1/2};q)
is nothing but the original Schur index
[
3
,
4
]
.

We stress that all of them are obtained from the index (
12
) as special limits.
The reduced index
I_{N}(t,u;q)
is regarded as a two-parameter deformation of the Schur index
I_{N}(q^{1/2},q^{1/2};q)
. As mentioned in the introductory section, we refer to
I_{N}(t,u;q)
as the deformed Schur index.

2.2
Exact evaluation of deformed Schur indices

In this subsection, we evaluate the matrix integral of the deformed Schur index exactly.
When
v=p=0
, we can rewrite the integral representation (
6
) as a more convenient form in terms of the
q
-Pochhammer symbol:

\displaystyle I_{N}(t,u;q)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!}\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}^{N}(tu;q)_{\infty}^{N}}{(t;q)_{\infty}^{N}(u;q)_{\infty}^{N}}\oint_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{dx_{i}}{2\pi ix_{i}}\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}\frac{(x_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}(tux_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(tx_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}(ux_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}.

(12)

This is a starting point of our analysis. The
q
-Pochhammer symbol is defined by

\displaystyle(x;q)_{\infty}=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}(1-xq^{k}),\qquad(x;q)_{n}=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(1-xq^{k}),\qquad(x;q)_{0}=1,

(13)

and we have used an identity,

\displaystyle(qx;q)_{\infty}=\frac{(x;q)_{\infty}}{1-x},

(14)

to derive (
12
).
A method to perform the integral (
12
) is simple. The computation consists of three steps.

In the first step, we recognize that the integrand of (
12
) includes a weight function of Macdonald polynomials of type
A_{N-1}
. In Appendix
A
, we review basics on the Macdonald polynomials of type A, based on
[
15
,
16
]
, for the reader’s convenience. The weight function of the Macdonald polynomials of type
A_{N-1}
is given by

\displaystyle w(x)=\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}\frac{(x_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(tx_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}.

(15)

The integral (
12
) is then written as

\displaystyle I_{N}(t,u;q)=\frac{1}{N!}\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}^{N}}{(t;q)_{\infty}^{N}}\oint_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{dx_{i}}{2\pi ix_{i}}w(x)\prod_{i,j=1}^{N}\frac{(tux_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(ux_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}.

(16)

In the second step, we use the Cauchy formula for the Macdonald polynomials:

\displaystyle\prod_{i,j=1}^{N}\frac{(tx_{i}y_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(x_{i}y_{j};q)_{\infty}}=\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}b_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}(x;q,t)P_{\lambda}(y;q,t),

(17)

where

\displaystyle b_{\lambda}=\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq\ell(\lambda)}\frac{(t^{j-i+1}q^{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}};q)_{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{j+1}}}{(t^{j-i}q^{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}+1};q)_{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{j+1}}}.

(18)

The summation in (
17
) is taken over all the partitions whose length is less than or equal to
N
.
Setting
y_{j}=u/x_{j}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\prod_{i,j=1}^{N}\frac{(tux_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(ux_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}=\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}u^{|\lambda|}b_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}(x;q,t)P_{\lambda}(x^{-1};q,t),

(19)

where we have used
P_{\lambda}(ux^{-1};q,t)=u^{|\lambda|}P_{\lambda}(x^{-1};q,t)
.

In the final step, we perform the integral by using the norm formula of the Macdonald polynomial:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N!}\oint_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{dx_{i}}{2\pi ix_{i}}w(x)P_{\lambda}(x;q,t)P_{\lambda}(x^{-1};q,t)=\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,N},

(20)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,N}=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\frac{(t^{j-i}q^{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}+1};q)_{\infty}(t^{j-i}q^{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}};q)_{\infty}}{(t^{j-i+1}q^{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}};q)_{\infty}(t^{j-i-1}q^{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}+1};q)_{\infty}}.

(21)

We arrive at an exact expression

\displaystyle I_{N}(t,u;q)=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}^{N}}{(t;q)_{\infty}^{N}}\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}u^{|\lambda|}b_{\lambda}\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,N}.

(22)

This result is already simple compared with the original integral expression. After some computations, we can further simplify the product
b_{\lambda}\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,N}
as

\displaystyle b_{\lambda}\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,N}=\frac{(t;q)_{\infty}^{N}}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{N-1}(t;t)_{N}(t^{N}q;q)_{\infty}}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}\frac{(t^{N-i+1};q)_{\lambda_{i}}}{(t^{N-i}q;q)_{\lambda_{i}}}.

(23)

Therefore we find a more compact form:

\displaystyle I_{N}(t,u;q)=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(t;t)_{N}(t^{N}q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}u^{|\lambda|}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}\frac{(t^{N-i+1};q)_{\lambda_{i}}}{(t^{N-i}q;q)_{\lambda_{i}}}.

(24)

This is one of the main results in this work.
In the original integral (
12
), the symmetric structure for
t
and
u
is manifest. However, our result (
24
) is not. The symmetry is cured in a quite non-trivial way. This fact causes some interesting consequences.

To check the validity of our result (
24
), let us take various limits, mentioned in the previous subsection.
We first consider
t=q
.
In this limit, the Macdonald polynomials reduce to the Schur polynomials. In fact, the integral (
12
) is written as

\displaystyle I_{N}(q,u;q)=\frac{1}{N!}\oint_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{dx_{i}}{2\pi ix_{i}}\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}\left(1-\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}}\right)\prod_{i,j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{1-ux_{i}/x_{j}},

(25)

and we can use the more familiar Cauchy formula for the Schur polynomials:

\displaystyle\prod_{i,j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{1-x_{i}y_{j}}=\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}s_{\lambda}(x)s_{\lambda}(y).

(26)

The prefactor and the summand in (
24
) get trivial, and as was shown in
[
6
]
, the index finally becomes

\displaystyle I_{N}(q,u;q)=\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}u^{|\lambda|}=\frac{1}{(u;u)_{N}},

(27)

as expected. As we mentioned before, the deformed Schur index has the symmetry between
t
and
u
. This means that for
u=q
, we have

\displaystyle I_{N}(t,q;q)=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(t;t)_{N}(t^{N}q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}q^{|\lambda|}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}\frac{(t^{N-i+1};q)_{\lambda_{i}}}{(t^{N-i}q;q)_{\lambda_{i}}}=\frac{1}{(t;t)_{N}}.

(28)

Therefore we obtain a quite non-trivial summation identity

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}q^{|\lambda|}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}\frac{(t^{N-i+1};q)_{\lambda_{i}}}{(t^{N-i}q;q)_{\lambda_{i}}}=\frac{(t^{N}q;q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}.

(29)

We do not have a direct proof of this identity. It would be nice to find it.

Let us consider the limit
q\to 0
. In this case, the Macdonald polynomials reduce to the Hall-Littlewood polynomials.
From (
24
), we have

\displaystyle I_{N}(t,u;0)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(t;t)_{N}}\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}u^{|\lambda|}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}(1-t^{N-i+1})

(30)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(t;t)_{N}}\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}u^{|\lambda|}(t^{N};t^{-1})_{\ell(\lambda)}.

We can perform the sum as follows.

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}u^{|\lambda|}(t^{N};t^{-1})_{\ell(\lambda)}=1+\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}(t^{N};t^{-1})_{\ell}\sum_{\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{\ell}\geq 1}u^{\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{\ell}}

(31)

By changing the variables as

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}=n_{1}+n_{2}+\cdots+n_{\ell},\qquad\lambda_{2}=n_{2}+\cdots+n_{\ell},\qquad\dots,\qquad\lambda_{\ell}=n_{\ell},

(32)

we find

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{\ell}\geq 1}u^{\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{\ell}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n_{1}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n_{2}=0}^{\infty}\cdots\sum_{n_{\ell-1}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n_{\ell}=1}^{\infty}u^{n_{1}+2n_{2}+\cdots+(\ell-1)n_{\ell-1}+\ell n_{\ell}}

(33)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n_{1}=0}^{\infty}u^{n_{1}}\sum_{n_{2}=0}^{\infty}u^{2n_{2}}\cdots\sum_{n_{\ell-1}=0}^{\infty}u^{(\ell-1)n_{\ell-1}}\sum_{n_{\ell}=1}^{\infty}u^{\ell n_{\ell}}

\displaystyle=\frac{u^{\ell}}{(1-u)(1-u^{2})\cdots(1-u^{\ell})}

\displaystyle=\frac{u^{\ell}}{(u;u)_{\ell}}

We also have

\displaystyle(t^{N};t^{-1})_{\ell}=\frac{(t;t)_{N}}{(t;t)_{N-\ell}}.

(34)

Combining these results, we finally arrive at a very simple expression of the
1/4
BPS index,

\displaystyle I_{N}(t,u;0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{N}\frac{u^{\ell}}{(t;t)_{N-\ell}(u;u)_{\ell}}.

(35)

The symmetric structure is not manifest, but one can confirm it.

The limit
u\to 0
is also interesting. The integral (
12
) reduces to

\displaystyle I_{N}(t,0;q)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!}\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}^{N}}{(t;q)_{\infty}^{N}}\oint_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{dx_{i}}{2\pi ix_{i}}\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}\frac{(x_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(tx_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}.

(36)

We can directly apply the norm formula for the trivial Macdonald polynomial
P_{\emptyset}(x;q,t)=1
.

\displaystyle I_{N}(t,0;q)

\displaystyle=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}^{N}}{(t;q)_{\infty}^{N}}\mathcal{N}_{\emptyset,N}=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(t;t)_{N}(t^{N}q;q)_{\infty}}.

(37)

where we have used (
23
) for
\lambda=\emptyset
and
b_{\emptyset}=1
.
This is an exact result on the half-index.
3
3

3

Note that an essentially equivalent analytic result was obtained in
[
5
]
. The same result is also observed in
[
14
]
by using a duality on half-indices of Neumann boundary condition and Nahm pole boundary condition.

Of course, the result agrees with (
24
) for
u=0
.
On the other hand, if we consider
t\to 0
, the Macdonald polynomials reduce to the
q
-Whittaker polynomials.
The sum in (
24
) remains non-trivial,

\displaystyle I_{N}(0,u;q)=(q;q)_{\infty}\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}\frac{u^{|\lambda|}}{(q;q)_{\lambda_{N}}}.

(38)

The symmetry between
t
and
u
requires
I_{N}(0,u;q)=I_{N}(u,0;q)
, and
we get a sum formula,

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}\frac{u^{|\lambda|}}{(q;q)_{\lambda_{N}}}=\frac{1}{(u;u)_{N}(u^{N}q;q)_{\infty}}.

(39)

We can show it directly as follows. Changing the variables as in (
32
) for
\ell=N
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}\frac{u^{|\lambda|}}{(q;q)_{\lambda_{N}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n_{1}=0}^{\infty}\cdots\sum_{n_{N}=0}^{\infty}\frac{u^{n_{1}+2n_{2}+\cdots+Nn_{N}}}{(q;q)_{n_{N}}}

(40)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-u)(1-u^{2})\cdots(1-u^{N-1})}\frac{1}{(u^{N};q)_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(u;u)_{N-1}(u^{N};q)_{\infty}}=\frac{1}{(u;u)_{N}(u^{N}q;q)_{\infty}}.

All of these computations validate the result (
24
).

Let us consider the Schur limit
(t,u,q)\to(x,x,x^{2})
. In this case, (
24
) reduces to

\displaystyle I_{N}(x,x;x^{2})=\frac{(x^{2};x^{2})_{\infty}}{(x;x)_{N}(x^{N+2};x^{2})_{\infty}}\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}x^{|\lambda|}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}\frac{(x^{N-i+1};x^{2})_{\lambda_{i}}}{(x^{N-i+2};x^{2})_{\lambda_{i}}}.

(41)

Unfortunately, we do not have a way to deal with this summation over
\lambda
. Interestingly, there is another much simpler formula for the Schur index
[
17
]
,

\displaystyle I_{N}(x,x;x^{2})=\frac{(x^{2};x^{2})_{\infty}}{(x;x)_{\infty}^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\biggl[\binom{N+n}{N}+\binom{N+n-1}{N}\biggr]x^{nN+n^{2}}.

(42)

Combining these two expressions, the following identity should hold,

\displaystyle\frac{(x;x)_{\infty}(x^{N};x)_{\infty}}{(x^{N};x^{2})_{\infty}}\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}x^{|\lambda|}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}\frac{(x^{N-i+1};x^{2})_{\lambda_{i}}}{(x^{N-i+2};x^{2})_{\lambda_{i}}}

(43)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\biggl[\binom{N+n}{N}+\binom{N+n-1}{N}\biggr]x^{nN+n^{2}}.

We have checked it by computing the
x
-series of the both sides for various
N
.
It is quite interesting to prove it rigorously.

To close this subsection, we comment on an equivalence to a previous conjecture proposed in
[
18
]
.
The formula (
24
) is rewritten as

\displaystyle I_{N}(t,u;q)

\displaystyle=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(t;t)_{N}(t^{N}q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{n_{1}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n_{2}=0}^{\infty}\cdots\sum_{n_{N}=0}^{\infty}u^{n_{1}+2n_{2}+\cdots Nn_{N}}

(44)

\displaystyle\times\frac{(t^{N};q)_{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{N}}(t^{N-1};q)_{n_{2}+\cdots+n_{N}}\cdots(t;q)_{n_{N}}}{(t^{N-1}q;q)_{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{N}}(t^{N-2}q;q)_{n_{2}+\cdots+n_{N}}\cdots(q;q)_{n_{N}}},

by using (
32
) for
\ell=N
. Using (
14
) and

\displaystyle(x;q)_{n}=\frac{(x;q)_{\infty}}{(xq^{n};q)_{\infty}},

(45)

and imposing
tu=q
, this expression is equivalent to Eq. (3.55) in
[
18
]
. In other words, we rigorously derived the earlier conjecture on the flavored Schur index by using known results on the Macdonald polynomials.

2.3
Comparison with character expansion method

In the previous subsection, we have shown that the matrix integral of the deformed Schur index is beautifully evaluated with the help of the Macdonald polynomials. There is another powerful tool to evaluate matrix integrals, a.k.a. the character expansion method. We compare these two methods.

Let us recall the character expansion method
[
6
]
. The idea is simple. We start with the matrix integral (
6
). We denote
f_{n}=f(t^{n},u^{n},v^{n};p^{n},q^{n})
for short, and then the integrand is expanded as

\displaystyle\exp\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{f_{n}}{n}p_{n}(x)p_{n}(x^{-1})\right)=\sum_{\mu}\frac{f_{\mu}}{z_{\mu}}p_{\mu}(x)p_{\mu}(x^{-1})

(46)

where for a partition
\mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\dots)=(1^{m_{1}}2^{m_{2}}\dots)
, we define

\displaystyle f_{\mu}=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\mu)}f_{\mu_{i}},\qquad p_{\mu}(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\mu)}p_{\mu_{i}}(x),\qquad z_{\mu}=\prod_{i\geq 1}i^{m_{i}}m_{i}!\,.

(47)

See Appendix
A
for the notation on partitions.
We can translate the power sum symmetric polynomials
p_{\mu}(x)
into the Schur polynomials
s_{\lambda}(x)
by the so-called Frobenius formula:

\displaystyle p_{\mu}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\vdash n\\
\ell(\lambda)\leq N\end{subarray}}\chi_{\mu}^{\lambda}s_{\lambda}(x),

(48)

where
n=|\mu|
, and the sum is taken over all the partitions
\lambda
of
n
with
\ell(\lambda)\leq N
.
\chi_{\mu}^{\lambda}
is the character of the symmetric group
S_{n}
for the representation
\lambda
and the conjugacy class
\mu
. Then we can perform the torus integral by using the orthonormality of the Schur polynomials, and finally obtain

\displaystyle I_{N}(t,u,v;p,q)=\sum_{\mu}\frac{f_{\mu}}{z_{\mu}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\vdash n\\
\ell(\lambda)\leq N\end{subarray}}(\chi_{\mu}^{\lambda})^{2}.

(49)

This character expansion method is powerful to evaluate the superconformal index.
However, for
p=v=0
, our result (
24
) has several advantages. The first one is that our formula contains the single partition sum, while (
49
) has the double partition sums. The computational cost should be saved in our result. The second one is that the values of the character of the symmetric group does not have a general explicit formula. One needs character tables, the Murnaghan-Nakayama rule or the Frobenius formula to obtain them. In our formula, all the ingredients are written in an explicit way. The third and biggest one is that in the character expansion method, it is hard to find exact expressions of the index.

To see the third point more concretely, let us consider the
1/4
BPS index:
f(t,u;0)=t+u-tu
for
N=3
. The character expansion method yields the double series expansion for both
t
and
u
:

\displaystyle I_{3}(t,u;0)=1+(t+u)+(2t^{2}+tu+2u^{2})+(3t^{3}+2t^{2}u+2tu^{2}+3u^{3})+\cdots

(50)

It is quite non-trivial to resum this expansion exactly, and find the analytic form (
35
).
Therefore to explore the analytic structure of indices, the character expansion method is not very useful. This point is also important to explore finite
N
corrections in Section
3.2
.

2.4
Line operator indices

We can extend the previous calculation to insertions of characters of
U(N)
.
These are called (Wilson or electric) line operator indices. Let us consider an insertion

\displaystyle I_{\lambda,\rho,N}(t,u;q)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!}\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}^{N}(tu;q)_{\infty}^{N}}{(t;q)_{\infty}^{N}(u;q)_{\infty}^{N}}\oint_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{dx_{i}}{2\pi ix_{i}}

(51)

\displaystyle\times\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}\frac{(x_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}(tux_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(tx_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}(ux_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}s_{\lambda}(x)s_{\rho}(x^{-1}).

Recall that the Schur polynomial
s_{\lambda}(x)
is the
U(N)
character for the representation
\lambda
. We also use a short-hand notation
I_{\lambda,N}(t,u;q)=I_{\lambda,\lambda,N}(t,u;q)
.

We first focus on an anti-symmetric representation
\lambda=\rho=(1^{r})
. In this case, the Schur polynomial becomes the elementary symmetric polynomial:
s_{(1^{r})}(x)=e_{r}(x)
. We start with

\displaystyle I_{(1^{r}),N}(t,u;q)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!}\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}^{N}}{(t;q)_{\infty}^{N}}\oint_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{dx_{i}}{2\pi ix_{i}}w(x)

(52)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}u^{|\lambda|}b_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}(x;q,t)P_{\lambda}(x^{-1};q,t)e_{r}(x)e_{r}(x^{-1}).

We can use the Pieri formula:

\displaystyle e_{r}(x)P_{\lambda}(x;q,t)=\sum_{\mu\in V_{N}^{r}(\lambda)}\psi_{\mu/\lambda}^{\prime}(q,t)P_{\mu}(x;q,t),

(53)

where
V_{N}^{r}(\lambda)
is defined by (
156
),
and

\displaystyle\psi_{\mu/\lambda}^{\prime}(q,t)

\displaystyle=\psi_{\mu^{\prime}/\lambda^{\prime}}(t,q),

(54)

\displaystyle\psi_{\mu/\lambda}(q,t)

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq\ell(\lambda)}\frac{(t^{j-i+1}q^{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}};q)_{\mu_{i}-\lambda_{i}}(t^{j-i}q^{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j+1}+1};q)_{\mu_{i}-\lambda_{i}}}{(t^{j-i}q^{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}+1};q)_{\mu_{i}-\lambda_{i}}(t^{j-i+1}q^{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j+1}};q)_{\mu_{i}-\lambda_{i}}}.

(55)

As explained in Appendix
A
,
\lambda^{\prime}
denotes the conjugate partition of
\lambda
.
With the help of this formula, we can immediately evaluate the torus integral, and obtain

\displaystyle I_{(1^{r}),N}(t,u;q)=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}^{N}}{(t;q)_{\infty}^{N}}\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}u^{|\lambda|}b_{\lambda}\sum_{\mu\in V_{N}^{r}(\lambda)}\mathcal{N}_{\mu,N}\psi_{\mu/\lambda}^{\prime}(q,t)^{2}.

(56)

We further rewrite it, by using (
23
), as

\displaystyle I_{(1^{r}),N}(t,u;q)=\frac{(q;q)}{(t;t)_{N}(t^{N}q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}u^{|\lambda|}\sum_{\mu\in V_{N}^{r}(\lambda)}\varphi_{\mu/\lambda}^{\prime}(q,t)\psi_{\mu/\lambda}^{\prime}(q,t)

(57)

\displaystyle\times\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\mu)}\frac{(t^{N-i+1};q)_{\mu_{i}}}{(t^{N-i}q;q)_{\mu_{i}}}

where

\displaystyle\varphi_{\mu/\lambda}^{\prime}(q,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{b_{\lambda}}{b_{\mu}}\psi_{\mu/\lambda}^{\prime}(q,t)=\varphi_{\mu^{\prime}/\lambda^{\prime}}(t,q),

(58)

\displaystyle\varphi_{\mu/\lambda}(q,t)

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq\ell(\mu)}\frac{(t^{j-i+1}q^{\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}};q)_{\mu_{j}-\lambda_{j}}(t^{j-i}q^{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j+1}+1};q)_{\mu_{j+1}-\lambda_{j+1}}}{(t^{j-i}q^{\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}+1};q)_{\mu_{j}-\lambda_{j}}(t^{j-i+1}q^{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j+1}};q)_{\mu_{j+1}-\lambda_{j+1}}}.

(59)

The combinatorial sums in (
57
) may be implemented in a symbolic computational system.

One way to evaluate the line operator indices for general representations is to use the Jacobi-Trudi formula:

\displaystyle s_{\lambda}(x)=\det(e_{\lambda_{i}^{\prime}-i+j}(x))_{1\leq i,j\leq l(\lambda^{\prime})}.

(60)

Then we repeatedly apply the Pieri formula. The resulting formulae are however terribly complicated.
To sketch it, let us consider two-column repsentations
\lambda=(1^{r_{1}}2^{r_{2}})
and
\rho=(1^{r_{1}^{\prime}}2^{r_{2}^{\prime}})
in (
51
).
Since the Schur polynomial for
\lambda=(1^{r_{1}}2^{r_{2}})
is given by

\displaystyle s_{\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=\begin{vmatrix}e_{r_{1}+r_{2}}(x)&e_{r_{1}+r_{2}+1}(x)\\
e_{r_{2}-1}(x)&e_{r_{2}}(x)\end{vmatrix}

(61)

\displaystyle=e_{r_{1}+r_{2}}(x)e_{r_{2}}(x)-e_{r_{1}+r_{2}+1}(x)e_{r_{2}-1}(x),

we have

\displaystyle s_{\lambda}(x)s_{\rho}(x^{-1})

(62)

\displaystyle=e_{r_{1}+r_{2}}(x)e_{r_{2}}(x)e_{r_{1}^{\prime}+r_{2}^{\prime}}(x^{-1})e_{r_{2}^{\prime}}(x^{-1})-e_{r_{1}+r_{2}}(x)e_{r_{2}}(x)e_{r_{1}^{\prime}+r_{2}^{\prime}+1}(x^{-1})e_{r_{2}^{\prime}-1}(x^{-1})

\displaystyle-e_{r_{1}+r_{2}+1}(x)e_{r_{2}-1}(x)e_{r_{1}^{\prime}+r_{2}^{\prime}}(x^{-1})e_{r_{2}^{\prime}}(x^{-1})

\displaystyle+e_{r_{1}+r_{2}+1}(x)e_{r_{2}-1}(x)e_{r_{1}^{\prime}+r_{2}^{\prime}+1}(x^{-1})e_{r_{2}^{\prime}-1}(x^{-1}).

Since
e_{r}(x)e_{s}(x)P_{\mu}(x;q,t)
is expanded as
P_{\nu}(x;q,t)
by using the Pieri formula twice, we can evaluate the integral of
I_{\lambda,\rho,N}(t,u;q)
. In the next section, we see that in the large
N
limit, the computation is drastically simplified.

3
Large
N
limit

3.1
Analysis at infinite
N

In the context of the AdS/CFT correspondence, we are usually interested in the large
N
limit and finite
N
corrections to it.
It is not obvious to take the limit
N\to\infty
in the matrix integral (
3
) or (
6
).
One standard way to do so is to use the technique of random matrices,
i.e.
, to use the saddle-point analysis of matrix integrals.
In this subsection, we develop another way to treat the strictly infinite
N
analysis, based on the theory of symmetric functions.

In combinatorics, it is often useful to consider “symmetric polynomials with an infinite number of variables”. Such are usually referred to as symmetric functions. The basic philosophy of the famous book
[
15
]
is to develop the theory of symmetric functions rather than symmetric polynomials. Many results on symmetric polynomials of
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})
are obtained from those on symmetric functions by projection
x_{N+1}=x_{N+2}=\dots=0
.
Inverting the logic, we easily obtain results at
N=\infty
in an algebraic way. Note that most of the results in this section are quoted from Chapter VI in
[
15
]
.

Let us define an expectation value and an inner product by

\displaystyle\langle A(x)\rangle_{N}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!}\oint_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{dx_{i}}{2\pi ix_{i}}w(x)A(x),

(63)

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{N}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!}\oint_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{dx_{i}}{2\pi ix_{i}}w(x)f(x)g(x^{-1})=\langle f(x)g(x^{-1})\rangle_{N}^{\prime}

(64)

In this notation, the deformed Schur index and the half-index are written as

\displaystyle I_{N}(t,u;q)

\displaystyle=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}^{N}}{(t,q)_{\infty}^{N}}\biggl\langle\prod_{i,j=1}^{N}\frac{(tux_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(ux_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}\biggr\rangle_{N}^{\prime},

(65)

\displaystyle I_{N}(t,0;q)

\displaystyle=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}^{N}}{(t,q)_{\infty}^{N}}\langle 1\rangle_{N}^{\prime}.

(66)

Now we define the expectation value and the inner product at
N=\infty
by

\displaystyle\langle A(x)\rangle_{\infty}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\langle A(x)\rangle_{N}^{\prime}}{\langle 1\rangle_{N}^{\prime}},\qquad\langle f,g\rangle_{\infty}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\langle f,g\rangle_{N}^{\prime}}{\langle 1,1\rangle_{N}^{\prime}}.

(67)

In these expressions, the functions on the left hand sides have an infinite number of variables.
The inner product
\langle f,g\rangle_{\infty}
has a very nice property. The power sum symmetric functions now satisfy an orthogonal relation,

\displaystyle\langle p_{\lambda},p_{\mu}\rangle_{\infty}=\delta_{\lambda,\mu}z_{\lambda}(q,t),

(68)

where

\displaystyle z_{\lambda}(q,t)=z_{\lambda}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}\frac{1-q^{\lambda_{i}}}{1-t^{\lambda_{i}}}

(69)

Of course, this is not the case for finite
N
. The power sum symmetric polynomials are not orthogonal for the inner product (
64
).

Let us consider a ratio

\displaystyle\frac{I_{\infty}(t,u;q)}{I_{\infty}(t,0;q)}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{I_{N}(t,u;q)}{I_{N}(t,0;q)}=\biggl\langle\prod_{i,j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(tux_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(ux_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}\biggr\rangle_{\infty}.

(70)

We can easily evaluate it. Using

\displaystyle\prod_{i,j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(tux_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(ux_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\exp\biggl(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{u^{n}}{n}\frac{1-t^{n}}{1-q^{n}}p_{n}(x)p_{n}(x^{-1})\biggr)

(71)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda}\frac{u^{|\lambda|}}{z_{\lambda}(q,t)}p_{\lambda}(x)p_{\lambda}(x^{-1}),

we find

\displaystyle\frac{I_{\infty}(t,u;q)}{I_{\infty}(t,0;q)}=\sum_{\lambda}\frac{u^{|\lambda|}}{z_{\lambda}(q,t)}\langle p_{\lambda},p_{\lambda}\rangle_{\infty}=\sum_{\lambda}u^{|\lambda|}=\frac{1}{(u;u)_{\infty}}.

(72)

Since the large
N
limit of the half-index is easily obtained from the exact result (
37
) as

\displaystyle I_{\infty}(t,0;q)=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(t;t)_{\infty}},

(73)

we find

\displaystyle I_{\infty}(t,u;q)=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(t;t)_{\infty}(u;u)_{\infty}}.

(74)

We can also re-derive the same result from our exact formula (
24
).
However, we should be careful when taking the large
N
limit.
First,
b_{\lambda}
is written as
4
4

4

See Eq. (4.11) in
[
15
]
.

\displaystyle b_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{\langle P_{\lambda},P_{\lambda}\rangle_{\infty}}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{N}_{\emptyset,N}}{\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,N}}.

(75)

Using (
23
), we find

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}\frac{(t^{N-i+1};q)_{\lambda_{i}}}{(t^{N-i}q;q)_{\lambda_{i}}}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{b_{\lambda}\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,N}}{b_{\emptyset}\mathcal{N}_{\emptyset,N}}=1.

(76)

Therefore, from (
24
), we obtain

\displaystyle I_{\infty}(t,u;q)=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(t;t)_{\infty}}\sum_{\lambda}u^{|\lambda|}=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(t;t)_{\infty}(u;u)_{\infty}}.

(77)

To see line operator indices, let us start with an insertion of the power sum symmetric polynomials,

\displaystyle I_{\mu,\nu,N}^{\text{p.s.}}(t,u;q)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!}\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}^{N}(tu;q)_{\infty}^{N}}{(t;q)_{\infty}^{N}(u;q)_{\infty}^{N}}\oint_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{dx_{i}}{2\pi ix_{i}}

(78)

\displaystyle\times\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}\frac{(x_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}(tux_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(tx_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}(ux_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}p_{\mu}(x)p_{\nu}(x^{-1}).

We would like to know
I_{\mu,\nu,\infty}^{\text{p.s.}}(t,u;q)
. To do so, we start with

\displaystyle\frac{I_{\mu,\nu,\infty}^{\text{p.s.}}(t,u;q)}{I_{\infty}(t,0;q)}=\biggl\langle\prod_{i,j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(tux_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(ux_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}p_{\mu}(x)p_{\nu}(x^{-1})\biggr\rangle_{\infty}.

(79)

We can still evaluate it as follows. Considering

\displaystyle\prod_{i,j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(tux_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(ux_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}p_{\mu}(x)p_{\nu}(x^{-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda}\frac{u^{|\lambda|}}{z_{\lambda}(q,t)}p_{\lambda}(x)p_{\lambda}(x^{-1})p_{\mu}(x)p_{\nu}(x^{-1})

(80)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda}\frac{u^{|\lambda|}}{z_{\lambda}(q,t)}p_{\lambda\cup\mu}(x)p_{\lambda\cup\nu}(x^{-1}),

where
\lambda\cup\mu
is a union of two partitions
\lambda
and
\mu
,
5
5

5

For example, if
\lambda=(3,1)
and
\mu=(2,2,1)
, then
\lambda\cup\mu=(3,2,2,1,1)
.

we find

\displaystyle\frac{I_{\mu,\nu,\infty}^{\text{p.s.}}(t,u;q)}{I_{\infty}(t,0;q)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda}\frac{u^{|\lambda|}}{z_{\lambda}(q,t)}\langle p_{\lambda\cup\mu},p_{\lambda\cup\nu}\rangle_{\infty}

(81)

\displaystyle=\delta_{\mu,\nu}\sum_{\lambda}\frac{u^{|\lambda|}}{z_{\lambda}(q,t)}z_{\lambda\cup\mu}(q,t).

For
\lambda=(1^{k_{1}}2^{k_{2}}\dots)
and
\mu=(1^{m_{1}}2^{m_{2}}\dots)
, we have
\lambda\cup\mu=(1^{k_{1}+m_{1}}2^{k_{2}+m_{2}}\dots)
and

\displaystyle z_{\lambda\cup\mu}(q,t)=z_{\lambda}(q,t)z_{\mu}(q,t)\prod_{i\geq 1}\binom{k_{i}+m_{i}}{k_{i}}.

(82)

Then, the sum over
\lambda
is performed,

\displaystyle\frac{I_{\mu,\nu,\infty}^{\text{p.s.}}(t,u;q)}{I_{\infty}(t,0;q)}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\mu,\nu}z_{\mu}(q,t)\sum_{\lambda}\prod_{i\geq 1}\binom{k_{i}+m_{i}}{k_{i}}u^{ik_{i}}

(83)

\displaystyle=\delta_{\mu,\nu}z_{\mu}(q,t)\prod_{i\geq 1}\frac{1}{(1-u^{i})^{m_{i}+1}}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\mu,\nu}z_{\mu}(q,t)\frac{1}{(u;u)_{\infty}}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\mu)}\frac{1}{1-u^{\mu_{i}}}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\mu,\nu}\frac{z_{\mu}}{(u;u)_{\infty}}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\mu)}\frac{1-q^{\mu_{i}}}{(1-t^{\mu_{i}})(1-u^{\mu_{i}})}.

Therefore

\displaystyle I_{\mu,\nu,\infty}^{\text{p.s.}}(t,u;q)=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(t;t)_{\infty}(u;u)_{\infty}}\delta_{\mu,\nu}z_{\mu}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\mu)}\frac{1-q^{\mu_{i}}}{(1-t^{\mu_{i}})(1-u^{\mu_{i}})}.

(84)

The same result was obtained in
[
19
,
20
]
by the Fermi-gas formalism and in
[
21
]
by the character expansion method.
Using the Frobenius formula:

\displaystyle s_{\lambda}(x)=\sum_{\mu\vdash|\lambda|}\frac{\chi_{\mu}^{\lambda}}{z_{\mu}}p_{\mu}(x),

(85)

we finally arrive at the general line operator index at
N=\infty
,

\displaystyle\frac{I_{\lambda,\rho,\infty}(t,u;q)}{I_{\infty}(t,u;q)}=\sum_{\mu\vdash|\lambda|}\frac{\chi_{\mu}^{\lambda}\chi_{\mu}^{\rho}}{z_{\mu}}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\mu)}\frac{1-q^{\mu_{i}}}{(1-t^{\mu_{i}})(1-u^{\mu_{i}})}.

(86)

If
\lambda=\rho=(1^{r})
or
\lambda=\rho=(r)
, we have
\chi_{\mu}^{\lambda}\chi_{\mu}^{\rho}=1
for any
\mu
. We then find

\displaystyle\frac{I_{(1^{r}),\infty}(t,u;q)}{I_{\infty}(t,u;q)}=\frac{I_{(r),\infty}(t,u;q)}{I_{\infty}(t,u;q)}=\sum_{\mu\vdash r}\frac{1}{z_{\mu}}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\mu)}\frac{1-q^{\mu_{i}}}{(1-t^{\mu_{i}})(1-u^{\mu_{i}})}.

(87)

If taking
N\to\infty
in (
57
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{I_{(1^{r}),\infty}(t,u;q)}{I_{\infty}(t,u;q)}=(u;u)_{\infty}\sum_{\lambda}u^{|\lambda|}\sum_{\mu\in V_{\infty}^{r}(\lambda)}\varphi^{\prime}_{\mu/\lambda}(q,t)\psi^{\prime}_{\mu/\lambda}(q,t).

(88)

These two must be equal.

3.2
Finite
N
corrections: giant graviton expansions

One of the most remarkable properties on superconformal indices is that their finite
N
corrections are also generated by (analytically continued) superconformal indices
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
8
]
(see also
[
22
,
23
,
24
]
). From a perspective of the AdS/CFT correspondence, this property is often referred to as a giant graviton expansion. In this section, we study such a surprising structure, particularly found by Gaiotto and Lee in
[
8
]
because this type of expansion is well suited for our formula (
24
).

Their basic claim is that the finite
N
corrections to the superconformal index is given by

\displaystyle\frac{I_{N}(t,u,v;p,q)}{I_{\infty}(t,u,v;p,q)}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t^{kN}\hat{I}_{k}(t,u,v;p,q),

(89)

where
\hat{I}_{k}(t,u,v;p,q)
is another index for gauge group
U(k)
, whose single letter index
\hat{f}(t,u,v;p,q)
is determined by the condition:

\displaystyle(1-f)(1-\hat{f})=(1-t)(1-t^{-1}).

(90)

It is very easy to see that
\hat{f}(t,u,v;p,q)
is given by

\displaystyle\hat{f}(t,u,v;p,q)=1-\frac{(1-t^{-1})(1-p)(1-q)}{(1-u)(1-v)}.

(91)

This means

\displaystyle\hat{I}_{k}(t,u,v;p,q)=I_{k}(t^{-1},q,p;v,u).

(92)

Note that from (
5
) we have
t^{-1}pq=uv
.
To the author’s knowledge, this proposal is yet to be proved, but has been confirmed in various limits.

In our interested case
v=p=0
, we have
\hat{I}_{k}(t,u,0;0,q)=I_{k}(t^{-1},q,0;0,u)
.
Therefore the giant graviton expansion for the deformed Schur index is given by

\displaystyle\frac{I_{N}(t,u;q)}{I_{\infty}(t,u;q)}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t^{kN}\hat{I}_{k}(t,u;q),\qquad\hat{I}_{k}(t,u;q)=I_{k}(t^{-1},q;u),

(93)

where
\hat{I}_{0}(t,u;q):=1
. There are two subtleties to check this highly non-trivial claim.

One is that the “giant graviton index”
\hat{I}_{k}(t,u;q)=I_{k}(t^{-1},q;u)
should be understood as an analytic continuation of the original index because the first fugacity
t^{-1}
satisfies
|t^{-1}|>1
when
|t|<1
, which is a condition for the convergence of the matrix integral of the original index.
This problem is not a problem in our formula (
24
) because it is exact in
t
. We can analytically continue it to
|t|>1
regime.
Note that in the character expansion method, one has to resum the power series of
t
in (
49
) for the analytic continuation. This resummation is non-trivial.
For the 1/4 BPS index
I_{N}(t,u;0)
, the giant graviton index
\hat{I}_{k}(t,u;0)=I_{k}(t^{-1},0;u)
is the analytic continuation of the half-index.
For the half-index
I_{N}(t,0;q)
, the giant graviton index
\hat{I}_{k}(t,0;q)=I_{k}(t^{-1},q;0)
is conversely the analytic continuation of the 1/4 BPS index. In these cases, we can prove the giant graviton expansions analytically
[
18
]
.

The other is the exchange between
u
and
q
. Our formula (
24
) is given by a power series of
u
but exact in
t
and
q
. On the other hand,
I_{k}(t^{-1},q;u)
has a power series of
q
, not
u
. To resolve this mismatch of the expansion regimes, we scale both
u
and
q
simultaneously. For example, we set

\displaystyle q=\alpha u.

(94)

In the following analysis, we consider this parametrization. Note that the flavored Schur index corresponds to
\alpha=t
.

We follow the argument in
[
8
]
.
We first expand
I_{N}(t,u;\alpha u)
with respect to
u
. Using our formula (
24
), we find

\displaystyle I_{1}(t,u;\alpha u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-t}+(1-\alpha)u+(1-\alpha)(1+\alpha+\alpha t)u^{2}+O(u^{3}),

(95)

\displaystyle I_{2}(t,u;\alpha u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-t)(1-t^{2})}+\frac{1-\alpha}{1-t}u+\frac{(1-\alpha)(2+\alpha-t+\alpha t^{2})}{1-t}u^{2}+O(u^{3}),

(96)

\displaystyle I_{3}(t,u;\alpha u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-t)(1-t^{2})(1-t^{3})}+\frac{1-\alpha}{(1-t)(1-t^{2})}u

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt+\frac{(1-\alpha)(2+\alpha-t^{2}+\alpha t^{3})}{(1-t)(1-t^{2})}u^{2}+O(u^{3}).

(97)

In general,
I_{N}(t,u;\alpha u)
has the following nice structure:

\displaystyle I_{N}(t,u;\alpha u)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}g_{N,j}^{(\alpha)}(t)u^{j},\qquad g_{N,j}^{(\alpha)}(t)=\frac{1-\alpha}{(t;t)_{N-1}}G_{N,j}^{(\alpha)}(t),

(98)

where the explicit forms of
G_{N,j}^{(\alpha)}(t)
for
j=0,1,2
are given by

\displaystyle G_{N,0}^{(\alpha)}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-\alpha)(1-t^{N})},

(99)

\displaystyle G_{N,1}^{(\alpha)}(t)

\displaystyle=1,

(100)

\displaystyle G_{N,2}^{(\alpha)}(t)

\displaystyle=2+\alpha+t^{N}\biggl(-\frac{1}{t}+\alpha\biggr).

(101)

We observe that
G_{N,j}^{(\alpha)}(t)
for
j\geq 1
is a “polynomial” of degree
j-1
in
t^{N}
.

If we assume the giant graviton expansion (
93
), we can fix
G_{N,j}^{(\alpha)}(t)
recursively.
Let us introduce

\displaystyle I_{\infty}(t,u;\alpha u)=\frac{(\alpha u;\alpha u)_{\infty}}{(t;t)_{\infty}(u;u)_{\infty}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}g_{\infty,j}^{(\alpha)}(t)u^{j},\qquad g_{\infty,j}^{(\alpha)}(t)=\frac{1-\alpha}{(t;t)_{\infty}}G_{\infty,j}^{(\alpha)}.

(102)

Note that
I_{k}(t^{-1},\alpha u;u)
has the following expansion:

\displaystyle I_{k}(t^{-1},\alpha u;u)=I_{k}(t^{-1},\alpha u;\alpha^{-1}\cdot\alpha u)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}g_{k,j}^{(\alpha^{-1})}(t^{-1})\alpha^{j}u^{j}.

(103)

Plugging (
98
), (
102
) and (
103
) into (
93
), we obtain

\displaystyle g_{N,j}^{(\alpha)}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t^{kN}\sum_{m=0}^{j}\alpha^{m}g_{\infty,j-m}^{(\alpha)}(t)g_{k,m}^{(\alpha^{-1})}(t^{-1})\qquad(j\geq 1),

(104)

where
g_{0,m}^{(\alpha^{-1})}(t^{-1})=1
.

We further translate (
104
) into that for
G_{N,j}^{(\alpha)}(t)
and assume that
G_{N,j}^{(\alpha)}(t)
is a polynomial of degree
j-1
in
t^{N}
.
We finally obtain

\displaystyle G_{N,j}^{(\alpha)}(t)

\displaystyle=G_{\infty,j}^{(\alpha)}+\sum_{n=1}^{j-1}t^{nN}\biggl[\frac{G_{\infty,j}^{(\alpha)}}{(t;t)_{n}}

(105)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{j}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}t^{\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}\alpha^{m}(1-\alpha^{-1})}{(t;t)_{n-k}(t;t)_{k-1}}G_{\infty,j-m}^{(\alpha)}G_{k,m}^{(\alpha^{-1})}(t^{-1})\biggr]\quad(j\geq 1).

To fix
G_{N,j}^{(\alpha)}(t)
recursively from this relation, we need inputs
G_{k,j}^{(\alpha^{-1})}(t^{-1})
for
k=1,2,\dots j-1
. For low values of
j
, this is easily done by using (
24
). For example, for
j=2
, we need only
G_{1,2}^{(\alpha^{-1})}(t^{-1})
:

\displaystyle G_{N,2}^{(\alpha)}(t)=2+\alpha+t^{N}\bigl(1+2\alpha-\alpha G_{1,2}^{(\alpha^{-1})}(t^{-1})\bigr).

(106)

Using
G_{1,2}^{(\alpha)}(t)=1+\alpha+\alpha t
, this reproduces the previous result (
101
).
Pushing this computation, we further find

\displaystyle G_{N,3}^{(\alpha)}(t)

\displaystyle=3+\alpha+t^{N}\biggl(-\frac{1}{t^{2}}+\frac{-2+\alpha}{t}+\alpha+\alpha^{2}\biggr)+t^{2N}\biggl(\frac{1}{t^{3}}-\frac{\alpha}{t^{2}}-\frac{\alpha}{t}+\alpha^{2}\biggr),

(107)

\displaystyle G_{N,4}^{(\alpha)}(t)

\displaystyle=5+2\alpha+t^{N}\biggl(-\frac{1}{t^{3}}+\frac{-2+\alpha}{t^{2}}+\frac{-4+\alpha+\alpha^{2}}{t}+\alpha+2\alpha^{2}+\alpha^{3}\biggr)

\displaystyle+t^{2N}\biggl(\frac{1}{t^{5}}+\frac{1-\alpha}{t^{4}}+\frac{2-2\alpha}{t^{3}}+\frac{-3\alpha+\alpha^{2}}{t^{2}}-\frac{\alpha}{t}+\alpha^{2}+\alpha^{3}\biggr)

\displaystyle+t^{3N}\biggl(-\frac{1}{t^{6}}+\frac{\alpha}{t^{5}}+\frac{\alpha}{t^{4}}+\frac{\alpha-\alpha^{2}}{t^{3}}-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{t^{2}}-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{t}+\alpha^{3}\biggr).

The high-
j
computations are straightforward.
It would be interesting to find the general structure of
G_{N,j}^{(\alpha)}(t)
.

We can repeat the same computation for line operator indices.
The giant graviton expansions (or brane expansions) of the line operator indices were studied in
[
25
,
26
,
27
,
21
]
.
Here we focus on the fundamental representation. From (
57
), we have

\displaystyle I_{(1),1}(t,u;\alpha u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-t}+(1-\alpha)u+(1-\alpha)(1+\alpha+\alpha t)u^{2}+O(u^{3}),

(108)

\displaystyle I_{(1),2}(t,u;\alpha u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-t)^{2}}+\frac{1-\alpha}{1-t}(2+t)u

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1-\alpha}{1-t}\Bigl(3+2\alpha t+(-1+2\alpha)t^{2}+\alpha t^{3}\Bigr)u^{2}+O(u^{3}),

(109)

\displaystyle I_{(1),3}(t,u;\alpha u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-t)^{2}(1-t^{2})}+\frac{1-\alpha}{(1-t)(1-t^{2})}(2+2t+t^{2})u

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1-\alpha}{(1-t)(1-t^{2})}\Bigl(4+3t+(-1+2\alpha)t^{2}+(-2+3\alpha)t^{3}

\displaystyle\quad+(-1+2\alpha)t^{4}+\alpha t^{5}\Bigr)u^{2}+O(u^{3}).

(110)

There is the following nice structure:

\displaystyle I_{(1),N}(t,u;\alpha u)=\frac{1-\alpha}{(1-t)(t;t)_{N-1}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}u^{j}G_{(1),N,j}^{(\alpha)}(t),

(111)

where

\displaystyle G_{(1),N,0}^{(\alpha)}(t)=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}.

(112)

We would like to determine
G_{(1),N,j}^{(\alpha)}(t)
for
j\geq 1
from the giant graviton expansion.
The giant graviton expansion for the fundamental line operator index was proposed in

,

\displaystyle\frac{I_{(1),N}(t,u;q)}{I_{(1),\infty}(t,u;q)}=\frac{I_{N}(t,u;q)}{I_{\infty}(t,u;q)}-\frac{(1-t^{-1})(1-q)}{1-u}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}t^{kN}I_{(1),k}(t^{-1},q;u),

(113)

where

\displaystyle I_{(1),\infty}(t,u;q)=\frac{1-q}{(1-t)(1-u)}I_{\infty}(t,u;q).

(114)

Plugging the ansatz (

) into (

), we find an analytic form of

(
j=1,2,3
),

\displaystyle G_{(1),N,1}^{(\alpha)}(t)

\displaystyle=2-t^{N}\biggl(\frac{1}{t}+1\biggr),

(115)

\displaystyle G_{(1),N,2}^{(\alpha)}(t)

\displaystyle=4+t^{N}\biggl(-\frac{1}{t^{2}}+\frac{2(-2+\alpha)}{t}-2+\alpha\biggr)

\displaystyle\quad+t^{2N}\biggl(\frac{1}{t^{3}}+\frac{1-\alpha}{t^{2}}+\frac{1-\alpha}{t}-\alpha\biggr),

(116)

\displaystyle G_{(1),N,3}^{(\alpha)}(t)

\displaystyle=7-\alpha-\alpha^{2}+t^{N}\biggl(-\frac{1}{t^{3}}+\frac{-4+3\alpha}{t^{2}}+\frac{-9+7\alpha}{t}-3+\alpha+\alpha^{2}\biggr)

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt+t^{2N}\biggl(\frac{1}{t^{5}}+\frac{2-\alpha}{t^{4}}+\frac{5(1-\alpha)}{t^{3}}+\frac{4-7\alpha+2\alpha^{2}}{t^{2}}+\frac{2-4\alpha+\alpha^{2}}{t}-\alpha+\alpha^{2}\biggr)

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt+t^{3N}\biggl(-\frac{1}{t^{6}}+\frac{-1+\alpha}{t^{5}}+\frac{-1+2\alpha}{t^{4}}-\frac{(1-\alpha)^{2}}{t^{3}}+\frac{(2-\alpha)\alpha}{t^{2}}+\frac{(1-\alpha)\alpha}{t}-\alpha^{2}\biggr).

(117)

4
Conclusion

In this work, we found a new technique to evaluate the unitary matrix integral in the two-parameter deformation of the Schur index. We used known mathematical results on the Macdonald polynomials. The resulting expression (
24
) is quite simple, and it is particularly useful in the study of finite
N
corrections.

There are several directions to future works. It is desirable to extend the formalism in this work to the full superconformal index (
3
). It is known that the matrix integral (
3
) can be rewritten in terms of the elliptic gamma function
[
28
]
, which is a one-parameter deformation of the
q
-Pochhammer symbol. To evaluate the matrix integral (
3
) along this line, we probably need an “elliptic deformation” of the Macdonald polynomials. It would be interesting to develop it.

It is also intriguing to explore the S-duality between Wilson line operators and ’t Hooft line operators. For instance, it is known that the Wilson line operator index (
57
) for the anti-symmetric representation has the S-dual description by the ’t Hooft line operator. According to
[
29
]
, the corresponding ’t Hooft line operator index in the flavored Schur limit is given by

\displaystyle I_{(1^{r},0^{N-r})}^{\text{'t Hooft}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r!(N-r)!}\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}^{2N}}{(t;q)_{\infty}^{N}(u;q)_{\infty}^{N}}\oint_{\mathbb{T}^{r}}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\frac{dx_{i}}{2\pi ix_{i}}\oint_{\mathbb{T}^{N-r}}\prod_{j=1}^{N-r}\frac{dy_{j}}{2\pi iy_{j}}

(118)

\displaystyle\times\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq r}\frac{(x_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}(qx_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(tx_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}(ux_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N-r}\frac{(y_{i}/y_{j};q)_{\infty}(qy_{i}/y_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(ty_{i}/y_{j};q)_{\infty}(uy_{i}/y_{j};q)_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{i=1}^{r}\prod_{j=1}^{N-r}\frac{(q^{1/2}x_{i}/y_{j};q)_{\infty}(q^{1/2}y_{j}/x_{i};q)_{\infty}(q^{3/2}x_{i}/y_{j};q)_{\infty}(q^{3/2}y_{j}/x_{i};q)_{\infty}}{(tq^{1/2}x_{i}/y_{j};q)_{\infty}(tq^{1/2}y_{j}/x_{i};q)_{\infty}(uq^{1/2}x_{i}/y_{j};q)_{\infty}(uq^{1/2}y_{j}/x_{i};q)_{\infty}},

where
q=tu
. So far, we do not have a nice way to evaluate this integral exactly. It would be interesting to find it and to prove the equivalence.

An extension to other gauge groups is also interesting. The Schur (line operator) indices and their giant graviton expansions of type BCD are extensively studied in
[
5
,
30
,
31
,
32
,
33
,
34
]
. For general root systems, the Macdonald polynomials can be also defined
[
35
]
. They are unified by the so-called Koornwinder polynomials
[
36
]
. It would be nice to use the Macdonald-Koornwinder polynomials to evaluate the deformed Schur indices for general root systems.

Appendix A
Review of Macdonald polynomials

In this appendix, we quickly review Macdonald polynomials of type A. We basically follow the notation in
[
15
,
16
]
.

Partitions.

Let
\lambda
be a partition. We denote it by

\displaystyle\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots),\qquad\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq 0,

(119)

or by

\displaystyle\lambda=(1^{m_{1}}2^{m_{2}}\dots),\qquad m_{i}\geq 0.

(120)

Here
\lambda_{i}
are called parts of
\lambda
, and
m_{i}
multiplicity of
i
.
The number of non-zero parts
\lambda_{i}
is called length, denoted by
\ell(\lambda)
.
The weight
|\lambda|
is the sum of the parts,

\displaystyle|\lambda|=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\cdots=m_{1}+2m_{2}+\cdots.

(121)

If
|\lambda|=n
, then
\lambda
is called a partition of
n
. We denote it by
\lambda\vdash n
.
A partition has a one-to-one correspondence to a Young diagram.
We sometimes identify a partition with its corresponding Young diagram.
Let us consider a Young diagram for
\lambda
. The partition for the transposed Young diagram is called the conjugate partition of
\lambda
, which is denoted by
\lambda^{\prime}
. For example, if
\lambda=(7,5,4,1)
, then
\ell(\lambda)=4
,
|\lambda|=17
and
\lambda^{\prime}=(4,3,3,3,2,1,1)
.

Let
\lambda
and
\mu
be two partitions. If
\lambda_{i}\geq\mu_{i}
for any
i=1,2,\dots
, we denote
\lambda\supset\mu
. In this case, the Young diagram for
\lambda
includes that for
\mu
. We can subtract the diagram
\mu
from
\lambda
. The remaining one is referred to as a skew diagram, denoted by
\lambda/\mu
. If two partitions
\lambda
and
\mu
satisfy

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\geq\mu_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\lambda_{3}\geq\dots,

(122)

then the skew diagram
\lambda/\mu
is called a horizontal strip. It also satisfies
\lambda_{i}^{\prime}-\mu_{i}^{\prime}\leq 1
for any
i=1,2,\dots
.
Similarly, if
\lambda
and
\mu
satisfy

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{\prime}\geq\mu_{1}^{\prime}\geq\lambda_{2}^{\prime}\geq\mu_{2}^{\prime}\geq\lambda_{3}^{\prime}\geq\dots,

(123)

then the skew diagram
\lambda/\mu
is called a vertical strip. For the vertical strip,
\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i}\leq 1
holds for any
i=1,2,\dots
. For example, if
\lambda=(3,3,1)
and
\mu=(3,1)
, then
\lambda/\mu
is a horizontal strip, but not a vertical strip.

We also introduce the dominance ordering of partitions:

\displaystyle\mu\leq\lambda\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad|\mu|=|\lambda|\quad\text{and}\quad\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{i}\leq\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{i},\quad\forall i=1,2,\dots.

(124)

Then
\mu<\lambda
means
\mu\leq\lambda
and
\mu\neq\lambda
. Note that the dominance ordering is not a total ordering.

Symmetric polynomials.

We are interested in symmetric polynomials of
n
-variable
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
.
We first introduce elementary and completely symmetric polynomials by

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}z^{r}e_{r}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+zx_{i}),

(125)

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}z^{r}h_{r}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{1-zx_{i}}.

(126)

Obviously, the elementary symmetric polynomials are non-trivial only for
r\leq n
.
Also, power sum symmetric polynomials are defined by

\displaystyle p_{r}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{r}.

(127)

To define the Macdonald polynomials, the following monomial symmetric polynomial is important:

\displaystyle m_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\sum_{\alpha\in S_{n}.\lambda}x^{\alpha},

(128)

where
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})
in the sum runs over all distinct permutations of the partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
, and
x^{\alpha}=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
.
We show some explicit forms for
n=3
:

\displaystyle m_{(3)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})

\displaystyle=x_{1}^{3}+x_{2}^{3}+x_{3}^{3},

(129)

\displaystyle m_{(2,1)}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})

\displaystyle=x_{1}^{2}x_{2}+x_{1}x_{2}^{2}+x_{1}^{2}x_{3}+x_{1}x_{3}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}x_{3}+x_{2}x_{3}^{2},

\displaystyle m_{(1^{3})}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})

\displaystyle=x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}.

Clearly, we have
m_{(r)}(x)=p_{r}(x)
and
m_{(1^{r})}(x)=e_{r}(x)
.

Macdonald polynomials.

Following
[
16
]
, we introduce the Macdonald polynomials. Let us consider the following
q
-difference operator:

\displaystyle D_{x}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{1\leq j(\neq i)\leq n}\frac{tx_{i}-x_{j}}{x_{i}-x_{j}}T_{q,x_{i}},

(130)

where

\displaystyle T_{q,x_{i}}f(x_{1},\dots,x_{i},\dots,x_{n})=f(x_{1},\dots,qx_{i},\dots,x_{n}).

(131)

The Macdonald polynomial

\displaystyle P_{\lambda}(x;q,t)=m_{\lambda}(x)+\sum_{\mu<\lambda}u_{\mu}^{\lambda}(q,t)m_{\mu}(x)

(132)

is defined as an eigenfunction of
D_{x}
. More precisely, it satisfies the following eigenvalue equation,

\displaystyle D_{x}P_{\lambda}(x;q,t)=d_{\lambda}(q,t)P_{\lambda}(x;q,t),

(133)

where the eigenvalue is given by

\displaystyle d_{\lambda}(q,t)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}t^{n-i}q^{\lambda_{i}}.

(134)

For each partition
\lambda
, the Macdonald polynomial
P_{\lambda}(x;q,t)
is uniquely fixed by this definition.
For the reader’s convenience, we show its explicit forms for
n\geq 3
and
|\lambda|\leq 3
:

\displaystyle P_{(1)}=m_{(1)},\qquad P_{(2)}=m_{(2)}+\frac{(1-q^{2})(1-t)}{(1-q)(1-qt)}m_{(1^{2})},\qquad P_{(1^{2})}=m_{(1^{2})},

(135)

and

\displaystyle P_{(3)}

\displaystyle=m_{(3)}+\frac{(1-q^{3})(1-t)}{(1-q)(1-q^{2}t)}m_{(2,1)}+\frac{(1-q^{2})(1-q^{3})(1-t)^{2}}{(1-q)^{2}(1-qt)(1-q^{2}t)}m_{(1^{3})},

(136)

\displaystyle P_{(2,1)}

\displaystyle=m_{(2,1)}+\frac{(1-t)(2+q+t+2qt)}{1-qt^{2}}m_{(1^{3})},

\displaystyle P_{(1^{3})}

\displaystyle=m_{(1^{3})}.

By definition, we have

\displaystyle P_{(1^{r})}(x;q,t)=m_{(1^{r})}(x)=e_{r}(x).

(137)

The polynomials
P_{(r)}(x;q,t)
are generated by

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{(tx_{i}y;q)_{\infty}}{(x_{i}y;q)_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}g_{r}(x;q,t)y^{r},

(138)

\displaystyle P_{(r)}(x;q,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{(q;q)_{r}}{(t;q)_{r}}g_{r}(x;q,t).

(139)

The Macdonald polynomials have the two parameters
(q,t)
, and there are various interesting specializations.
The case
t=q
is particularly important. In this case, it is well-known that the Macdonald polynomials reduce to the Schur polynomials,

\displaystyle P_{\lambda}(x;q,q)=s_{\lambda}(x),

(140)

where the Schur polynomials are defined by

\displaystyle s_{\lambda}(x)=\frac{\det(x_{i}^{\lambda_{j}+n-j})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}}{\det(x_{i}^{n-j})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}}.

(141)

Also in the limit
t\to 1
, the Macdonald polynomials reduce to the monomial symmetric polynomials:
P_{\lambda}(x;q,1)=m_{\lambda}(x)
.
In
q\to 0
,
P_{\lambda}(x;0,t)
is the Hall-Littlewood polynomials. When
t\to 0
,
P_{\lambda}(x;q,0)
is called the
q
-Whittaker polynomials. See Fig. 1.1 in
[
16
]
for other specializations.

The Macdonald polynomials are symmetric orthogonal polynomials. The weight function of them is given by

\displaystyle w(x)=\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\frac{(x_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(tx_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}.

(142)

The orthogonality relation is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n!}\oint_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{dx_{i}}{2\pi ix_{i}}w(x)P_{\lambda}(x;q,t)P_{\mu}(x^{-1};q,t)=\delta_{\lambda,\mu}\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,n},

(143)

where
\mathbb{T}^{n}=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}|\;|x_{i}|=1\}
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,n}=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\frac{(t^{j-i}q^{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}+1};q)_{\infty}(t^{j-i}q^{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}};q)_{\infty}}{(t^{j-i+1}q^{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}};q)_{\infty}(t^{j-i-1}q^{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}+1};q)_{\infty}}.

(144)

The orthogonality is shown by the self-adjointness of the difference operator
D_{x}
, but a derivation of the formula on the norm
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,n}
is highly non-trivial.

In our analysis, the Cauchy formula plays a crucial role. Let us define

\displaystyle\Pi(x,y;q,t)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{(tx_{i}y_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(x_{i}y_{j};q)_{\infty}}.

(145)

The Cauchy formula claims that

\displaystyle\Pi(x,y;q,t)=\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq\min(n,m)}b_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}(x;q,t)P_{\lambda}(y;q,t),

(146)

where

\displaystyle b_{\lambda}=\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq\ell(\lambda)}\frac{(t^{j-i+1}q^{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}};q)_{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{j+1}}}{(t^{j-i}q^{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}+1};q)_{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{j+1}}}.

(147)

When
q=t
, the Cauchy formula reduces to

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{1-x_{i}y_{j}}=\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq\min(n,m)}s_{\lambda}(x)s_{\lambda}(y).

(148)

A key point to show the Cauchy formula is that
\Pi(x,y;q,t)
is a kernel function of the difference operators
D_{x}
and
D_{y}
:

\displaystyle D_{x}\Pi(x,y;q,t)=D_{y}\Pi(x,y;q,t).

(149)

In the analysis of line operator indices, we need the Pieri formula. Let us explain it.
Since the Macdonald polynomials form a basis of symmetric polynomials, the product of two Macdonald polynomials are also expanded by the Macdonald polynomials:

\displaystyle P_{\lambda}(x;q,t)P_{\rho}(x;q,t)=\sum_{\mu}c_{\lambda\rho}^{\mu}(q,t)P_{\mu}(x;q,t).

(150)

When
t=q
,
c_{\lambda\rho}^{\mu}=c_{\lambda\rho}^{\mu}(q,q)
is nothing but the Littlewood-Richardson coefficient for the Schur polynomials. Unlike the Schur polynomials, the coefficient
c_{\lambda\rho}^{\mu}(q,t)
for the Macdonald polynomials are much more complicated. Fortunately, for
\rho=(1^{r})
, the coefficient is explicitly known. This is referred to as the Pieri formula:

\displaystyle e_{r}(x)P_{\lambda}(x;q,t)=\sum_{\mu\in V_{n}^{r}(\lambda)}\psi_{\mu/\lambda}^{\prime}(q,t)P_{\mu}(x;q,t)

(151)

where

\displaystyle V_{n}^{r}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\{\mu\vdash|\lambda|+r\;|\;\ell(\mu)\leq n\;\;\text{and}\;\;\mu/\lambda\;\;\text{is a vertical strip}\},

(152)

\displaystyle\psi_{\mu/\lambda}(q,t)

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq\ell(\lambda)}\frac{(t^{j-i+1}q^{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}};q)_{\mu_{i}-\lambda_{i}}(t^{j-i}q^{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j+1}+1};q)_{\mu_{i}-\lambda_{i}}}{(t^{j-i}q^{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}+1};q)_{\mu_{i}-\lambda_{i}}(t^{j-i+1}q^{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j+1}};q)_{\mu_{i}-\lambda_{i}}},

(153)

\displaystyle\psi_{\mu/\lambda}^{\prime}(q,t)

\displaystyle=\psi_{\mu^{\prime}/\lambda^{\prime}}(t,q).

(154)

There is also another type of the Pieri formula for
\rho=(r)
:

\displaystyle g_{r}(x;q,t)P_{\lambda}(x;q,t)=\sum_{\mu\in H_{n}^{r}(\lambda)}\varphi_{\mu/\lambda}(q,t)P_{\mu}(x;q,t),

(155)

where

\displaystyle H_{n}^{r}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\{\mu\vdash|\lambda|+r\;|\;\ell(\mu)\leq n\;\;\text{and}\;\;\mu/\lambda\;\;\text{is a horizontal strip}\},

(156)

\displaystyle\varphi_{\mu/\lambda}(q,t)

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq\ell(\mu)}\frac{(t^{j-i+1}q^{\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}};q)_{\mu_{j}-\lambda_{j}}(t^{j-i}q^{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j+1}+1};q)_{\mu_{j+1}-\lambda_{j+1}}}{(t^{j-i}q^{\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}+1};q)_{\mu_{j}-\lambda_{j}}(t^{j-i+1}q^{\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j+1}};q)_{\mu_{j+1}-\lambda_{j+1}}}.

(157)

For example, if
n=4
,
r=2
and
\lambda=(3,1,1)
, then
V_{4}^{2}((3,1,1))
and
H_{4}^{2}((3,1,1))
are explicitly given by

\displaystyle V_{4}^{2}((3,1,1))

\displaystyle=\{(4,2,1),(4,1,1,1),(3,2,2),(3,2,1,1)\},

(158)

\displaystyle H_{4}^{2}((3,1,1))

\displaystyle=\{(5,1,1),(4,2,1),(4,1,1,1),(3,3,1),(3,2,1,1)\}.

Appendix B
Half-indices of interfaces

In this appendix, we show additional exact results on half-indices of the
U(N)|U(M)
interface, introduced in
[
14
]
.
Without loss of generality, we can assume
N\leq M
.

The matrix integral for the half-index for NS5-type interface between
U(N)
and
U(M)
gauge theories is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{II}_{\mathcal{N}}^{U(N)|U(M)}=\frac{1}{N!M!}\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}^{N+M}}{(t;q)_{\infty}^{N+M}}\oint_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{dx_{i}}{2\pi ix_{i}}\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}\frac{(x_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(tx_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}

(159)

\displaystyle\times\oint_{\mathbb{T}^{M}}\prod_{i=1}^{M}\frac{dy_{i}}{2\pi iy_{i}}\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq M}\frac{(y_{i}/y_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(ty_{i}/y_{j};q)_{\infty}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{j=1}^{M}\frac{(tu^{1/2}x_{i}/y_{j};q)_{\infty}(tu^{1/2}y_{j}/x_{i};q)_{\infty}}{(u^{1/2}x_{i}/y_{j};q)_{\infty}(u^{1/2}y_{j}/x_{i};q)_{\infty}}.

We use the Cauchy formula as

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{j=1}^{M}\frac{(tu^{1/2}x_{i}/y_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(u^{1/2}x_{i}/y_{j};q)_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}u^{|\lambda|/2}b_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}(x;q,t)P_{\lambda}(y^{-1};q,t),

(160)

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{j=1}^{M}\frac{(tu^{1/2}y_{j}/x_{i};q)_{\infty}}{(u^{1/2}y_{j}/x_{i};q)_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell(\mu)\leq N}u^{|\mu|/2}b_{\mu}P_{\mu}(x^{-1};q,t)P_{\mu}(y;q,t).

Then we can perform the torus integrals:

\displaystyle\mathbb{II}_{\mathcal{N}}^{U(N)|U(M)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}^{N+M}}{(t;q)_{\infty}^{N+M}}\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}\sum_{\ell(\mu)\leq N}u^{|\lambda|/2+|\mu|/2}b_{\lambda}b_{\mu}\delta_{\lambda,\mu}\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,N}\delta_{\mu,\lambda}\mathcal{N}_{\mu,M}

(161)

\displaystyle=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}^{N+M}}{(t;q)_{\infty}^{N+M}}\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}u^{|\lambda|}b_{\lambda}\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,N}b_{\lambda}\mathcal{N}_{\lambda,M}.

Using (
23
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{II}_{\mathcal{N}}^{U(N)|U(M)}=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}^{2}}{(t;t)_{N}(t^{N}q;q)_{\infty}(t;t)_{M}(t^{M}q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}u^{|\lambda|}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}\frac{(t^{N-i+1};q)_{\lambda_{i}}(t^{M-i+1};q)_{\lambda_{i}}}{(t^{N-i}q;q)_{\lambda_{i}}(t^{M-i}q;q)_{\lambda_{i}}}.

(162)

This result is equivalent to the previous conjecture in
[
18
]
.

On the other hand, the matrix integral of the half-index of the D5-type
U(N)|U(M)
interface is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{II}_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}^{U(N)|U(M)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N!}\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}^{N}(tu;q)_{\infty}^{N}}{(t;q)_{\infty}^{N}(u;q)_{\infty}^{N}}\oint_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{dx_{i}}{2\pi ix_{i}}\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}\frac{(x_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}(tux_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(tx_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}(ux_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}

(163)

\displaystyle\times\prod_{k=1}^{M-N}\frac{(t^{k-1}q;q)_{\infty}}{(t^{k};q)_{\infty}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(t^{(M-N+1)/2}ux_{i};q)_{\infty}(t^{(M-N+1)/2}ux_{i}^{-1};q)_{\infty}}{(t^{(M-N+1)/2}x_{i};q)_{\infty}(t^{(M-N+1)/2}x_{i}^{-1};q)_{\infty}}.

The evaluation of this integral turns out to be more involved. We rewrite it as

\displaystyle\mathbb{II}_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}^{U(N)|U(M)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}^{N}}{(u;q)_{\infty}^{N}}\prod_{k=1}^{M-N}\frac{(t^{k-1}q;q)_{\infty}}{(t^{k};q)_{\infty}}\frac{1}{N!}\oint_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{dx_{i}}{2\pi ix_{i}}\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq N}\frac{(x_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(ux_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}

(164)

\displaystyle\times\prod_{i,j=1}^{N}\frac{(tux_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(tx_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(t^{(M-N+1)/2}ux_{i};q)_{\infty}(t^{(M-N+1)/2}ux_{i}^{-1};q)_{\infty}}{(t^{(M-N+1)/2}x_{i};q)_{\infty}(t^{(M-N+1)/2}x_{i}^{-1};q)_{\infty}}.

Note that we need to consider the Macdonald polynomials with two parameters
(q,u)
.
We use the Cauchy formula:

\displaystyle\prod_{i,j=1}^{N}\frac{(tux_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}{(tx_{i}/x_{j};q)_{\infty}}=\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}t^{|\lambda|}b_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}(x;q,u)P_{\lambda}(x^{-1};q,u),

(165)

and the generating function (
138
):

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(t^{(M-N+1)/2}ux_{i};q)_{\infty}}{(t^{(M-N+1)/2}x_{i};q)_{\infty}}=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}g_{r}(x;q,u)t^{(M-N+1)r/2}.

(166)

Moreover we use another Pieri formula (
155
). We finally obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{II}_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}^{U(N)|U(M)}=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}^{2}}{(t;t)_{M-N}(t^{M-N}q,q)_{\infty}(u;u)_{N}(u^{N}q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{\ell(\lambda)\leq N}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}t^{|\lambda|+(M-N+1)r}

(167)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\mu\in H_{N}^{r}(\lambda)}\varphi_{\mu/\lambda}(q,u)\psi_{\mu/\lambda}(q,u)\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\mu)}\frac{(u^{N-i+1};q)_{\mu_{i}}}{(u^{N-i}q;q)_{\mu_{i}}},

where we have used

\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{M-N}\frac{(t^{k-1}q;q)_{\infty}}{(t^{k};q)_{\infty}}=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(t;t)_{M-N}(t^{M-N}q;q)_{\infty}}.

(168)

For
q=tu
, these two half-indices are exactly the same,

\displaystyle\mathbb{II}_{\mathcal{N}}^{U(N)|U(M)}=\mathbb{II}_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}^{U(N)|U(M)}\qquad(q=tu)

(169)

To check it, we consider a slice
t=\beta q^{1/2}
and
u=\beta^{-1}q^{1/2}
for instance, and expand both indices around
q=0
.
We find a perfect agreement.

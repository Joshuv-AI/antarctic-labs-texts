del Pezzo surfaces with one bad prime over cyclotomic ℤ ℓ \mathbb{Z}_{\ell} -extensions Thanks: Nowroozi is supported by the EPSRC studentship.
Maryam Nowroozi

Abstract
. Let K K be a number field and S S a finite set of primes of K K . Scholl proved that there are only finitely many K K -isomorphism classes of del Pezzo surfaces of any degree 1 ≤ d ≤ 9 1\leq d\leq 9 over K K with good reduction away from S S . Let instead K K be the cyclotomic ℤ 5 \mathbb{Z}_{5} -extension of ℚ \mathbb{Q} . In this paper, we show, for d = 3 d=3 , 4 4 , that there are infinitely many ℚ ¯ \overline{\mathbb{Q}} isomorphism classes of del Pezzo surfaces, defined over K K , with good reduction away from the unique prime above 5 5 .

1.
Introduction

Let
\ell
be a rational
prime and
r
a positive integer.
Write
\mathbb{Q}_{r,\ell}
for the unique degree
\ell^{r}
totally real subfield
of
\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{n})
, where
\mu_{n}
denotes
the set of
\ell^{n}
-th roots of
1
. We let

\mathbb{Q}_{\infty,\ell}=\cup_{r}\mathbb{Q}_{r,\ell}
;
this is called the
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-cyclotomic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
,
and
\mathbb{Q}_{r,\ell}
is called the
r
-th layer of
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty,\ell}
.
Now let
K
be a number field, and write

K_{\infty,\ell}=K\cdot\mathbb{Q}_{\infty,\ell}

and
K_{r,\ell}=K\cdot\mathbb{Q}_{r,\ell}
.
To ease notation we shall sometimes write
K_{\infty}

for
K_{\infty,\ell}
.
Many theorems and conjectures regarding the arithmetic
of curves and varieties over number fields have
analogues over
K_{\infty}
. We mention some examples.

(1)

The Mordell–Weil theorem asserts that for
an abelian variety
A
over a number field
K
,
the Mordell–Weil group
A(K)
is finitely generated.
A celebrated conjecture of Mazur
[
4
]
asserts that
A(K_{\infty})

is finitely generated for an abelian variety
A
over
K_{\infty}
.

(2)

Let
X
be a curve of genus
\geq 2
over a number field
K
.
Faltings’ theorem (previously known as the Mordell conjecture)
[
2
]
asserts that
X(K)
is finite. A conjecture
of Parshin and Zarhin
[
10
, page 91]

asserts that
X(K_{\infty})
is finite, for a curve
X
of genus
\geq 2

over
K_{\infty}
.

(3)

A theorem of Zarhin
[
11
, Corollary 4.2]
,
asserts that the Tate homomorphism conjecture
(also a theorem of Faltings
[
2
]
over number fields)
continues to hold over
K_{\infty}
.

The Shafarevich conjecture for abelian varieties asserts that for a number field
K
, a finite set of primes
S
of
K
, and a given dimension
d\geq 1
,
there are only finitely many principally polarized
abelian varieties
A
defined over
K

of dimension
d
and good reduction away from
S
.
The Shafarevich conjecture was proved by Faltings
[
2
]

in the same paper in which he proved the Tate homomorphism conjecture
and the Mordell conjecture. In a recent paper
[
8
]
,
Siksek and Visser show that the Shafarevich conjecture for abelian varieties does not hold over
K_{\infty}
. For example, they construct infinitely many principally polarized abelian surfaces
defined over
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty,11}
with good reduction away from

2
,
11
, that are pairwise non-isomorphic over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.

In view of the above theorems, conjectures and counterexamples,
it is interesting to test whether Shafarevich-type
conjectures for other families of varieties continue to hold
over
K_{\infty}
.
In 1985 Scholl proved the anaologue of the Shafarevich conjecture
for del Pezzo surfaces. More precisely,
Scholl
[
6
]
proved that, for any
1\leq d\leq 9
,
and for any number field
K
and for
S
a finite set of primes of
K
,
then there are only finitely many
K
-isomorphism classes of del Pezzo surfaces
of degree
d
over
K
with good reduction away from
S
.
In this paper, we construct infinite families of del Pezzo surfaces over

\mathbb{Q}_{\infty,5}
with good reduction away from
5
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
r\geq 1
. Then there is a del Pezzo surface
X_{r}
of degree
3

defined over
\mathbb{Q}_{r,5}
with good reduction away from the unique
prime above
5
. Moreover, within the family
\{X_{r}\;:\;r\geq 1\}
,
there is an infinite subfamily whose members are pairwise non-isomorphic
over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
r\geq 1
. Then there is a del Pezzo surface
Y_{r}
of degree
4

defined over
\mathbb{Q}_{r,5}
with good reduction away from the unique
prime above
5
. Moreover, within the family
\{Y_{r}\;:\;r\geq 1\}
,
there is an infinite subfamily whose members are pairwise non-isomorphic
over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.

We quickly sketch the construction of the surfaces
X_{r}
and
Y_{r}
.
Let
\zeta=\zeta_{5^{r+1}}=\exp(2\pi i/5^{r+1})
.
The extension
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)/\mathbb{Q}
is cyclic with Galois group

\Gal(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)/\mathbb{Q})\;\cong\;\left(\mathbb{Z}/5^{r+1}\mathbb{Z}\right)^{\times}\;\cong\;\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/5^{r}\mathbb{Z}.

Let
\tau:\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)
be the field automorphism
given by

\tau(\zeta)=\zeta^{\delta}

where
\delta\in(\mathbb{Z}/5^{r+1}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
is the element satisfying

\delta^{2}\equiv-1\pmod{5^{r+1}},\qquad\delta\equiv 2\pmod{5};

such
\delta
exists and is unique by Hensel’s lemma.
We note that
\tau
is an automorphism of order
4
.
Let

H=\langle\tau\rangle
be the subgroup of
\Gal(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)/\mathbb{Q})

generated by
\tau
, and let
\mathbb{Q}_{r,5}

by the fixed field of
H
which has degree
5^{r}
and
Galois group isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/5^{r}\mathbb{Z}
. We note that

\mathbb{Q}_{\infty,5}=\bigcup_{r=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{Q}_{r,5}

Since
5
is totally ramified in
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)
, it is totally ramified
in
\mathbb{Q}_{r,5}
.

Let
P=(\zeta^{2}:\zeta:1)\in\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta))
.
We let
X_{r}
be the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
in the six points

(1)

\{\sigma(P):\sigma\in H\}\cup\{(1:0:0),(0:1:0)\}.

Let
Y_{r}
be the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
in the five points

(2)

\{\sigma(P):\sigma\in H\}\cup\{(1:0:0)\}.

Since these sets of points are both stable
under the action of
H
, the surfaces

X_{r}
and
Y_{r}
are defined over
\mathbb{Q}_{r,5}
.

Lemma 3
.

The six points in (
1
) are in general position. Moreover,
for any prime ideal
\mathfrak{q}\subset\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}_{r,5}}
with
\mathfrak{q}\nmid 5
,
the reduction of the six points in (
1
) modulo

\mathfrak{q}
are in general position.

Lemma
3
is proved in Section
3
.
Since the six points in (
1
) are in general position,
the surface
X_{r}
is a del Pezzo surface of degree
9-6=3
.
Moreover,
for a prime
\mathfrak{q}\nmid 5
, as
the six points remain in general position modulo
\mathfrak{q}
,
it follows
[
6
, Section 3]

that
X_{r}
has good reduction modulo
\mathfrak{q}
.
To complete the proof of Theorem
1

we need to show that among the
X_{r}
there are infinitely
many that are pairwise non-isomorphic over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.
We do this in Section
4
by using
the Clebsch–Salmon
invariants of the degree
3
del Pezzo surfaces
X_{r}
.

The five points in (
2
) are a subset
of the six points in (
1
) and it follows
that they are in general position, and their reduction
modulo any
\mathfrak{q}\nmid 5
are in general position.
We conclude immediately that the
Y_{r}

are degree
9-5=4
del Pezzo surfaces with good reduction
away from the unique prime above
5
. In Section
4

we again use invariants to show that among the
Y_{r}
there
are infinitely many that are pairwise non-isomorphic over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.

All the computations are done in Magma
[
1
]
and SageMath
[
9
]
. You can find the code here:
https://github.com/maryam-nowroozi/del-pezzo-surfaces

2.
background

In this section, we review some of the definitions we will need in further sections.
Let
k
be a field.

Definition 3
.

Let
1\leq r\leq 8
be an integer and let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{r}
be
r
distinct points in
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{k}
. We say that
P_{1},\ldots,P_{r}
are in
general position
if the following hold:

(1)

no three of the points lie on a line;

(2)

no six of the points lie on a conic;

(3)

no eight of the points lie on a singular cubic, with one of these eight points at the singular point.

Definition 4
.

Let
k
be a field. A surface
X
over
k
is a del Pezzo surface if its anticanonical divisor
-K_{X}
is ample.

Note that if
-K_{X}
is very ample, then it determines an embedding of
X
into

\mathbb{P}^{n}
for some
n
, under which
-K_{X}
corresponds to a hyperplane
section
H
. The degree of this embedding is
H^{2}
, which explains why the
integer
(-K_{X})^{2}
is called the degree of a del Pezzo surface
X
.

Example 4
.

Let
X=Q_{1}\cap Q_{2}
be the intersection of two non-singular quadric hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{4}_{k}
. Then
X
is a Del Pezzo surface of degree 4 (see
[
5
]
, Example 4.5).

Example 5
.

Let
X
be a cubic surface over
k
, i.e. a non-singular projective variety of dimension
2
and degree
3
,
then
X
is a Del Pezzo surface of degree
3
(see
[
5
]
, Example 4.6).

Proposition 6
.

Let
k
be a field. If
X
is a blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{k}
in
r\leq 8

k
-rational points in general position, then
X
is a del Pezzo surface of
degree
9-r
over
k
.

Proof.

See
[
5
]
, Proposition 4.34.
∎

Remark 7
.

If
k
is an algebraically closed field, then a del Pezzo surface
X
is either isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
or

X
arises as a blowing up of
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{k}
in
r\leq 8
points in general position and
K_{X}^{2}=9-r
(see
[
5
]
, Theorem 4.22).
In general, this does not hold over an arbitrary field.

3.
Proof of Lemma
3

The goal of this section is to prove Lemma
3
.
Let
k
be a field and let
u
,
v\in k^{\times}
. Let

T_{u,v}\;=\;\{(u^{2}:u:1),~(u^{-2}:u^{-1},1),~(v^{2}:v:1),~(v^{-2}:v^{-1}:1),~(1:0:0),~(0:1:0)\}.

We note that the set of six points in (
1
) is simply
T_{\zeta,\zeta^{\delta}}
.

Lemma 8
.

Let

f(u,v)=(u^{4}-1)(v^{4}-1)(u^{2}-v^{2})(u^{2}v^{2}-1).

The set of six points
T_{u,v}
is in general position if and only if
f(u,v)\neq 0
.

Proof.

Let
M_{u,v}
be the
6\times 6
determinant with rows
(x^{2},y^{2},z^{2},xy,xz,yz)
with

(x,y,z)
running through the elements of
T_{u,v}
. We see that
the six points of
T_{u,v}
lie on a conic if and if this determinant is
0
.
A quick
Magma
computation shows that

M_{u,v}=(u+1)(u-1)(v+1)(v-1)(u-v)^{2}(uv-1)^{2}/u^{3}v^{3}.

Moreover, three points
(x_{i}:y_{i}:z_{i})
, with
i=1,2,3
, lie on a line if and only if the

3\times 3
determinant with rows
(x_{i},y_{i},z_{i})
is zero. We computed this determinant
for the
\binom{6}{3}=20
possible choices of triples of points from among six points
of
T_{u,v}
. All these determinants have numerators with factors
among
u\pm 1
,
u^{2}+1
,
v\pm 1
,
v^{2}+1
,
u\pm v
,
uv\pm 1
.
The lemma follows.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3

Let
r\geq 1
and
\zeta=\exp(2\pi i/5^{r+1})
.
Write
u=\zeta
,
v=\zeta^{\delta}
. Then the six points in (
1
)
form the set
T_{u,v}
.
We consider the elements

u^{4}-1,v^{4}-1,u^{2}-v^{2},u^{2}v^{2}-1.

By Lemma
8
, the six points are in general position provided
these quantities are non-zero.
All of these, up to multiplication by a root of unity, have the form
\zeta^{\mu}-1
,
where
\mu
is among
4
,
4\delta
,
2\delta-2
,
2\delta+2
.
Since
\delta\equiv 2\pmod{5}
, we see that
\mu\equiv 4
,
3
,
2
,
1\pmod{5}
respectively.
Thus in all cases
\zeta^{\mu}
is a primitive
5^{r+1}
-th root of unity, and it follows that
the six points in (
1
) are in general position.

Next we consider the six points modulo a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}_{r,5}}
.
We note that, for the above four values of
\mu
, that the element
\zeta^{\mu}-1
generates the unique
prime ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)}
above
5
. Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}_{r,5}}

satisfying
\mathfrak{p}\nmid 5
.
Let
k=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}_{r,5}}/\mathfrak{p}
. Then the reduction of
\zeta^{\mu}-1
in
\overline{k}
is non-zero.
Thus the reduction of six points in (
1
) in
\mathbb{P}^{2}(\overline{k})
are in general position.
This completes the proof.

4.
Isomorphism of del Pezzo surfaces

4.1.
Degree 3

The goal of this section is to prove that there exists an infinite subfamily of
\{X_{r}:r\geq 1\}
whose members are pairwise non-isomorphic. We will confirm this by using the Clebsch-Salmon invariants for cubic surfaces.

We now recall the definition of Clebsch-Salmon invariants. Let
X
be a cubic surface given by a system of equations of the form

\displaystyle a_{0}x_{0}^{3}+a_{1}x_{1}^{3}+a_{2}x_{2}^{3}+a_{3}x_{3}^{3}+a_{4}x_{4}^{3}=0

\displaystyle x_{0}+x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4}=0

which is also called its
pentahedral form
. The coefficients
a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}
are called
pentahedral coefficients
of the surface.

Definition 5

(Clebsch-Salmon invariants)
.

Let
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{5}
be the elementary symmetric functions. We have the following invariants of degrees 8,16,24,32, and 40, respectively

\displaystyle I_{8}=\sigma_{4}^{2}-4\sigma_{3}\sigma_{5},

\displaystyle I_{16}=\sigma_{1}\sigma_{5}^{3},

\displaystyle I_{24}=\sigma_{4}\sigma_{5}^{4},

\displaystyle I_{32}=\sigma_{2}\sigma_{5}^{6},

\displaystyle I_{40}=\sigma_{5}^{8}.

Let
X_{u,v}
be the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
in
T_{u,v}
and let
[I_{8}(X_{u,v}):I_{16}(X_{u,v}):I_{24}(X_{u,v}):I_{32}(X_{u,v}):I_{40}(X_{u,v})]\in\mathbb{P}^{8,16,24,32,40}
be the invariants of
X_{u,v}
. The following lemma proves that there are at most finitely many del Pezzo surfaces isomorphic over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
to
X_{u,v}
.

Lemma 9
.

Let
(u,v)\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
,
f(u,v)\neq 0
. Then, there are at most finitely many pairs
(r,s)\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
,
f(r,s)\neq 0
such that
X_{r,s}
is isomorphic over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
to
X_{u,v}

Proof.

Suppose
X_{r,s}\cong_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}X_{u,v}
. Then

\frac{I_{16}(X_{u,v})}{I_{8}^{2}(X_{u,v})}=\frac{I_{16}(X_{r,s})}{I_{8}^{2}(X_{r,s})}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \frac{I_{24}(X_{u,v})}{I_{8}^{3}(X_{u,v})}=\frac{I_{24}(X_{r,s})}{I_{8}^{3}(X_{r,s})}

Let

\displaystyle F(r,s)=I_{16}(X_{u,v})-\alpha I_{8}^{2}(X_{u,v})\in\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)[r,s]

\displaystyle G(r,s)=I_{24}(X_{u,v})-\beta I_{8}^{3}(X_{u,v})\in\mathbb{Q}(\beta)[r,s]

Then,
F(r,s)=G(r,s)=0
. Let
h_{1}(r)=\operatorname{Res}_{s}(F(r,s),G(r,s))
and
h_{2}(s)=\operatorname{Res}_{r}(F(r,s),G(r,s))
. Then
h_{1}(r)=h_{2}(s)=0
. So, the number of possibilities for the pair
(r,s)
is at most
\deg(h_{1})\cdot\deg(h_{2})
. A quick
Magma
computation shows that
\deg(h_{1})\cdot\deg(h_{2})=518400
.
∎

This proves that within the family
\{X_{r}\;:\;r\geq 1\}
,
there is an infinite subfamily whose members are pairwise non-isomorphic
over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.

4.2.
degree 4

Recall that a Del Pezzo surface of degree 4 is a complete intersection of two quadrics in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
. Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
be a smooth surface defined by the intersection of two quadrics over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic different from 2. We know that
X
is charactrized upto isomorphism by the degeneracy locus of the pencil of quadrics containing
X
, i.e., by the form

h(t,w)=\det(tA_{F}+wA_{G}),

where
A_{F}
and
A_{G}
are symmetric
5\times 5
matrices whose associated quadric forms define
X
(see
[
3
]
for more details).

The two-dimensional space of binary quintic forms with non-vanishing discriminant up to linear change of variables serves as a moduli space of smooth del Pezzo surface of degree 4.

The Del Pezzo surface defined by blowing up the five points

\displaystyle P_{1}=(u^{2}:u:1),

\displaystyle P_{2}=(u^{-2}:u^{-1}:1),

\displaystyle P_{3}=(v^{2}:v:1),

\displaystyle P_{4}=(v^{-2}:v^{-1}:1),

\displaystyle P_{5}=(1:0:0).

is a complete intersection of two quadrics in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
given below

\displaystyle F(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})=-x_{1}x_{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{0}x_{3}+ax_{2}x_{3}+bx_{3}^{2}+ax_{3}x_{4}+x_{4}^{2},

\displaystyle G(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})=-x_{1}x_{3}-ax_{3}^{2}+x_{0}x_{4}+ax_{2}x_{4}.

where
a=\frac{-u^{2}v-uv^{2}-u-v}{uv}

and
b=\frac{u^{2}v^{2}+u^{2}+2uv+v^{2}+1}{uv}
.

The defined matrices
A_{F}
and
A_{G}
are as follows:

A_{F}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0&0&1/2&0\\
0&0&-1/2&0&0\\
0&-1/2&1&a/2&0\\
1/2&0&a/2&b&a/2\\
0&0&0&a/2&1\end{bmatrix},\ A_{G}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0&0&0&1/2\\
0&0&0&-1/2&0\\
0&0&0&0&a/2\\
0&-1/2&0&-a&0\\
1/2&0&a/2&0&0\end{bmatrix}

We have

h(t,w)=\det(tA_{F}+wA_{G})=\begin{vmatrix}0&0&0&\frac{1}{2}t&\frac{1}{2}w\\
0&0&-\frac{1}{2}t&-\frac{1}{2}w&0\\
0&-\frac{1}{2}t&t&\frac{a}{2}t&\frac{a}{2}w\\
\frac{1}{2}t&-\frac{1}{2}w&\frac{a}{2}t&-aw+bt&\frac{a}{2}t\\
\frac{1}{2}w&0&\frac{a}{2}w&\frac{a}{2}t&t\end{vmatrix}

So, we get
h(t,w)=\frac{1}{16}(t^{5}-at^{4}w+bt^{3}w^{2}-at^{2}w^{3}+tw^{4})
which is a binary quintic. The ring
SL_{2}
-invariants of binary quintics is generated by four invariants,
I_{4},I_{8},I_{12}
, and,
I_{18}
(see
[
7
]
).

Let
Y_{u,v}
be the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
in five points
P_{1},\ldots,P_{5}
and let
[I_{4}:I_{8}:I_{12}:I_{18}]\in\mathbb{P}^{4,8,12,18}
be the invariants of the degeneracy locus of the pencil of quadrics containing
Y_{u,v}
, defined above. The following lemma proves that there are at most finitely many del Pezzo surfaces isomorphic over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
to
Y_{u,v}
.

Lemma 10
.

Let
(u,v)\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
,
f(u,v)\neq 0
. Then, there are at most finitely many pairs
(r,s)\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
,
f(r,s)\neq 0
such that
Y_{r,s}
is isomorphic over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
to
Y_{u,v}

Proof.

Suppose
Y_{r,s}\cong_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}Y_{u,v}
. Then

\frac{I_{8}(Y_{u,v})}{I_{4}^{2}(Y_{u,v})}=\frac{I_{8}(Y_{r,s})}{I_{4}^{2}(Y_{r,s})}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \frac{I_{12}(Y_{u,v})}{I_{4}^{3}(Y_{u,v})}=\frac{I_{12}(Y_{r,s})}{I_{4}^{3}(Y_{r,s})}

Let

\displaystyle F(r,s)=I_{8}(Y_{u,v})-\alpha I_{4}^{2}(Y_{u,v})\in\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)[r,s]

\displaystyle G(r,s)=I_{12}(Y_{u,v})-\beta I_{4}^{3}(Y_{u,v})\in\mathbb{Q}(\beta)[r,s]

Then,
F(r,s)=G(r,s)=0
. Let
h_{1}(r)=\operatorname{Res}_{s}(F(r,s),G(r,s))
and
h_{2}(s)=\operatorname{Res}_{r}(F(r,s),G(r,s))
. Then
h_{1}(r)=h_{2}(s)=0
. So, the number of possibilities for the pair
(r,s)
is at most
\deg(h_{1})\cdot\deg(h_{2})
. A quick
Magma
computation shows that
\deg(h_{1})\cdot\deg(h_{2})=518400
.
∎

This proves that within the family
\{Y_{r}\;:\;r\geq 1\}
,
there is an infinite subfamily whose members are pairwise non-isomorphic
over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.

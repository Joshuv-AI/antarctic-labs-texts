Inclusions of Operator Algebras from Tensor Categories: beyond irreducibility
Lucas Hataishi, Roberto Hernández Palomares

Abstract
We derive faithful inclusions of C*-algebras from a coend-type construction in unitary tensor categories. This gives rise to different potential notions of discreteness for an inclusion in the non-irreducible case, and provides a unified framework that encloses the theory of compact quantum group actions. We also provide examples coming from semi-circular systems and from factorization homology. In the irreducible case, we establish conditions under which the C*-discrete and W*-discrete conditions are equivalent.

Introduction

Inclusions of operator algebras provide a flexible framework to study classical and quantum symmetries of noncommutative spaces. Jones’ landmark discovery of the Index Rigidity Theorem for subfactors
[
Jon83
]
launched the construction and classification of numerous new examples of subfactors, and fueled interactions with other fields such as low-dimensional topology, statistical mechanics, and quantum field theory. (See
[
JS97
]
for a survey).

Subsequently,
discrete subfactors
were introduced in
[
ILP98
]
as a broad class of inclusions
N\subset M
sharing properties of crossed products by outer actions of discrete groups on factors. They generally do not have finite index, but can be characterized either by a generating property in terms of the
quasi-normalizer
of
N
in
M
, or by a decomposition of the space of bounded
N{\hbox{-}}N
-bimodular operators on
L^{2}(M)
as a product of type I factors.

Associated to a subfactor
N\subset M
is its
standard invariant
,
{\mathcal{C}}_{N\subset M}
, that can be axiomatized using Jones’ planar algebras
[
Jon22
]
or Popa’s
\lambda
-lattices
[
Pop95
]
. The standard invariant classifies amenable subfactors of the hyperfinite
\rm II_{1}
-factor
\mathcal{R}

[
Pop94
]
. The formulation of the standard invariant that is relevant to us follows the philosophy advocated in
[
JP17
,
Müg03a
,
Müg03b
]
, and goes roughly as follows.

Let
N
be a a
\II_{1}
-factor
N
, and denote by
{\sf Bim}(N)
the category of
N{\hbox{-}}N
-Hilbert bimodules. Suppose we are given a subfactor
N\subset M
. Consider the Hilbert space completion
L^{2}(M)
with respect to the trace. Looking at it as a
N{\hbox{-}}N
-bimodule, it decomposes into a direct sum of irreducible ones. Let
{\mathcal{C}}_{N\subset M}
be the subcategory of
{\sf Bim}(N)
generated by those irreducible bimodules, their direct sums and their fusion products. By construction,
{\mathcal{C}}_{N\subset M}
is a C
∗
-tensor subcategory of
{\sf Bim}(N)
. The factor
M
induces a functor
{\mathbb{M}}:({\mathcal{C}}_{N\subset M})^{\op}\to{\sf Vec}
, defined on objects by
{\mathbb{M}}(K)=\Hom_{N-M}(K\underset{N}{\boxtimes}L^{2}(M),L^{2}(M))
. Then the standard invariant of
N\subset M
is the pair
({\mathcal{C}}_{N\subset M},{\mathbb{M}})
. The main result in
[
JP19
]
is that the standard invariants
({\mathcal{C}}_{N\subset M},{\mathbb{M}})
classify extremal irreducible discrete extensions
N\subset M
. We say that
\ {\mathcal{C}}_{N\subset M}
acts on
N
by means of the inclusion functor
{\mathcal{C}}_{N\subset M}\hookrightarrow{\sf Bim}(N)
. The procedure by which
M
is reconstructed from the standard invariant is called the realization or
crossed product
of
N
by
{\mathbb{M}}
, and it is denoted by
N\rtimes{\mathbb{M}}
. We say that
{\mathbb{M}}
is the
C
∗
-algebra object
, in the algebraic ind-completion
{\sf Vec}({\mathcal{C}}_{N\subset M}):=\{\text{linear functors}\ F:{\mathcal{C}}_{N\subset M}^{\op}\to{\sf Vec}\}
of
{\mathcal{C}}_{N\subset M}
, associated to the subfactor
N\subset M
.

In
[
HPN23
]
, the second-named author and Nelson introduced the abstract properties of discreteness and projective-quasi-regularity for unital irreducible inclusions of C*-algebras
A\overset{E}{\subset}B
.
Akin to discrete subfactors, irreducible C*-discrete inclusions are characterized by a standard invariant comprising an action of a UTC and a connected C*-algebra object in its algebraic ind-completion.
The class of C*-discrete inclusions encompasses all finite Watatani-index, certain semicircular systems, and cores of Cuntz algebras, as well as crossed products by actions of discrete (quantum) groups.

A main objective of this manuscript is to extend the standard invariant to non-irreducible inclusions of C*-algebras. A prominent class of examples comes from the Morita theory of compact quantum group actions, the relevant inclusions beings
B^{\mathbb{G}}\subset B
, where
B
is a C*-algebra acted by a compact quantum group and
B^{\mathbb{G}}
is the fixed point C*-subalgebra. Such inclusions have a well understood categorical description based on Tannaka-Krein duality
[
DCY13
,
DCY15
,
Nes14
]
. Such an approach to quantum group actions has been very useful in classification problems, e.g.
[
NY17
]
. In this context, irreducibility corresponds to ergodicity of the action of the compact quantum group. Cores of Cuntz algebra fall into this class of examples.

Another source of motivation for this work comes from
factorization homology
with values in C*-categories (cf
[
Hat23
]
).
Starting with a unitarily braided unitary tensor category
{\mathcal{C}}
, factorization homology gives a functorial construction associating a C*-tensor category
F_{\mathcal{C}}(\Sigma)
to every compact oriented surface
\Sigma
. When
\Sigma
has non-empty boundary,
F_{\mathcal{C}}(\Sigma)
is described by a C
∗
-algebra object that is generally not connected.

We shall now describe the contents of this article. Section 2 is a preliminary section where we gather some background material, fix notations and conventions. In Section 3 we introduce the
coend realization
construction that will serve as main tool in this paper. Given a UTC
{\mathcal{C}}
, C*-algebra objects
{\mathbb{A}}
in
{\sf Vec}({\mathcal{C}}^{\op})
and
{\mathbb{D}}
in
{\sf Vec}({\mathcal{C}})
, it follows from
[
JP19
, §4.4]
, the existence of a C*-algebra
C^{*}_{u}|{\mathbb{A}}\odot{\mathbb{D}}|
. We show that there is also a minimal completion, which we denote by
{\mathbb{A}}\bowtie{\mathbb{D}}
, and call it the
coend realization
of the pair
({\mathbb{A}},{\mathbb{D}})
. Let
\mathbbm{1}
be a tensor unit in
{\mathcal{C}}
. We then prove the following.

Theorem A

(Theorem
3.15
)
.

There is a canonical faithful conditional expectation
{\mathbb{E}}:{\mathbb{A}}\bowtie{\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathbb{A}}(1)\otimes{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1})
.

The proof of the theorem relies on the existence of canonical expectations of finite Pimsner-Popa indices
{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{A}}}_{X}:{\mathbb{A}}(\overline{X}X)\to{\mathbb{A}}(1)
and
{\mathbb{E}}^{\mathbb{D}}_{X}:{\mathbb{D}}(\overline{X}X)\to{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1})
, which allow for a local comparison between operator norms and Hilbert C*-module norms.

We then consider the above construction to the case when
{\mathbb{A}}
is the action of a UTC on a unital C*-algebra
A
with trivial center. This means that
{\mathbb{A}}
is a unitary tensor functor
{\mathbb{A}}:{\mathcal{C}}\to{\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A)
from
{\mathcal{C}}
to the UTC of finitely generated projective bimodules of
A
. In this context, we write
A\rtimes{\mathbb{D}}
for
{\mathbb{A}}\bowtie{\mathbb{D}}
, and call it the crossed product of
A
by
{\mathbb{D}}
under the action of
{\mathcal{C}}
.
Then, by applying Theorem A, since
{\mathbb{A}}(1)=A
, we obtain the inclusions

A\subset A\otimes{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1})\overset{{\mathbb{E}}}{\subset}A\rtimes{\mathbb{D}},

where the last inclusion is faithful. It is then easy to see, assuming
{\mathcal{Z}}(A)\simeq{\mathbb{C}}
, that the possible descents of
{\mathbb{E}}:A\rtimes{\mathbb{D}}\to A\otimes{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1})
to a conditional expectation
E:A\rtimes{\mathbb{D}}\to A
are parametrized by states on the C
∗
-algebra
{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1})
.

As mentioned earlier, in the case of a
\rm II_{1}
-factor
N
, a discrete extension
N\subset M
can be characterized in two ways; either in terms of the quasi-normalizer or in terms of
\End_{N-N}(L^{2}(M))
being a discrete von Neumann algebras. For extensions of C
∗
-algebras, it is yet not known if the two, accordingly adapted to the C
∗
-context, are equivalent.

Taking this into account, in Section 4, we make the distinction between flavors of discreteness for a faithful inclusion
A\overset{E}{\subset}D
. Using the faithful conditional expectation
E
, we can complete
D
to an
A{\hbox{-}}A
-correspondence
{\mathcal{E}}
. Discreteness and their variations are related to whether
D
or
{\mathcal{E}}
can be reconstructed from
{\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A)
. The analog concepts in the case of irreducible extensions of type
\rm II_{1}
-factors all coincide.

We call a faithful inclusion
A\overset{E}{\subset}D
discrete if the
*
-subalgebra of
D
generated by irreducible finitely generated projective
A{\hbox{-}}A
-subbimodules of
D
is dense in the operator norm.
There is then a category
{\sf C^{*}Disc}(A)
whose objects are discrete inclusions
A\overset{E}{\subset}D
and whose morphisms
(A\overset{E_{1}}{\subset}D_{1})\to(A\overset{E_{2}}{\subset}D_{2})
are expectation-preserving ucp maps
D_{1}\to D_{2}
.
Now the crossed product construction we introduce in Section 4 takes a C*-algebra object
{\mathbb{D}}\in{\sf Vec}({\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A))
, together with a faithful state
\omega\in{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1})
, and produces a discrete inclusion
A\subset A\rtimes{\mathbb{D}}
. We then prove the following reconstruction theorem.

Theorem B

(Theorem
4.15
)
.

Let
{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{\Omega}({\sf Vec}({\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A)))
be the category of C*-algebra objects in
{\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A)
with prescribed faithful states. Morphisms are ucp maps (in the sense of
[
JP17
]
, Section 4) preserving the prescribed states. There is an equivalence

\displaystyle{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{\Omega}({\sf Vec}({\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A)))\simeq{\sf C^{*}Disc}(A).

In other words, a discrete extension of
A
is classified by a pair
({\mathbb{D}},\omega)\in{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{\Omega}({\sf Vec}({\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A)))
.

Consider, as above, a faithful inclusion
A\overset{E}{\subset}D
, with corresponding C
∗
-algebra object
{\mathbb{D}}
defined in the algebraic ind-completion of
{\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A)
. We say that
A\overset{E}{\subset}D
is
projective-quasi-regular
, short PQR, the images of the canonical evaluation maps
K\odot{\mathbb{D}}(K)\to D
generate, as
K
ranges over the irreducibles in
{\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A)
, a dense subspace of the
completion
{\mathcal{E}}
of
D
as an
A{\hbox{-}}A
-correspondence. In Section 5 we show that the corresponding relative commutant
A^{\prime}\cap\End_{{\mathbb{C}}{\hbox{-}}A}({\mathcal{E}})=\End_{A{\hbox{-}}A}({\mathcal{E}})
of a PQR inclusion
A\overset{E}{\subset}D
indeed decomposes as a direct product of type
\rm I
factors, but in principle such a decomposition does not characterize projective quasi-regularity in the non-irreducible case. Indeed, such a decomposition characterizes
{\mathcal{E}}
as an object in
{\sf Hilb}({\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A))
, i.e., as a unitary ind-object. In that case, we say that
A\overset{E}{\subset}D
is an
ind-inclusion
, and prove the following generalization of Proposition 2.10 in
[
HPN23
]
.

Theorem C

(Theorem
4.18
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}:=\overline{D}^{\|\cdot\|_{A}}
. The inclusion
A\overset{E}{\subset}D
is an ind-inclusion if and only if there are Hilbert spaces
\{H_{i}\}_{i\in I}
such that

\End_{A{\hbox{-}}A}({\mathcal{E}})\simeq\prod_{i\in I}^{\ell^{\infty}}{\mathcal{B}}(H_{i}).

Section 5 is dedicated to examples, starting with a few considerations explaining how the theory of compact quantum group actions on C
∗
-algebras fit into our framework, a discussion that will be no novelty to the expert.

We characterize when a covariance matrix
\eta
on a C*-algebra
A
with trivial center leads to an ind-inclusion
A\subset\Phi(\eta)
, the latter being the C*-algebra of the corresponding
A
-valued semi-circular system
[
Shl99
]
.
We also explain how factorization homology gives rise to C*-algebra objects, and therefore to discrete inclusions through Theorem
B
, which come with canonical actions of mapping class groups of surfaces.
We mention there are other known families of not necessarily irreducible C*-discrete inclusions arising from Cuntz-Pimsner algebras of dualizable correspondences, which were studied in
[
HP25
]
.

When compared C
∗
-discreteness to W
∗
-discreteness, a central improvement is that the former is defined without making reference to states, as opposed to subfactor discreteness, which carries substantial spacial data compatible with the unique trace on a
\rm{II}_{1}
-factor.
It is then meaningful to ask to what extent are these notions of discreteness compatible.
We address this question in the case where the base C*-algebra
A
is strongly dense in
N
, and the discrete extensions
(A\subset B,E_{A})
and
((N,\tr))\subset M,E_{B})
are related by a
compatible action
of
{\mathcal{C}}
, which is compatible with the spacial data carried by the tracial state on
N
and the associated spherical bimodules.
In this context, we prove that the double commutant of an irreducible C*-discrete inclusion gives an extremal irreducible discrete subfactor:

Theorem D

(Theorem
6.7
)
.

Given a unitary tensor category
{\mathcal{C}}
and compatible actions
F:{\mathcal{C}}\to{\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A)
and
F^{\prime\prime}:{\mathcal{C}}\to{\sf Bim_{bf}^{sp}}(N).

Then, for any C*-discrete extension
A\overset{E}{\subset}B
supported on
F[{\mathcal{C}}]
, there is a corresponding extremal discrete subfactor
N\overset{E^{\prime\prime}}{\subset}M
obtained as a double commutant in a compatible representation.

As a consequence, in Corollary
6.9
we obtain a partial comparison between the lattices of intermediate subalgebras
A\subset D\subset B
and
N\subset P\subset M,
which restricts to a bijection when considering only those C*-discrete
A\subset D
.

2.2
Unitary tensor categories

A linear category
{\mathcal{C}}
is a locally small category such that for every pair
X,Y\in{\mathcal{C}}
, the space
{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)
of morphisms from
X
to
Y
is equipped with a structure of a vector space over
{\mathbb{C}}
. The model example is the category
{\sf Mod}(A)
of left
A
-modules for an algebra
A
over
{\mathbb{C}}
, where the morphisms between two modules are linear maps between them intertwining the corresponding actions.
A dagger, or
*
-category
, is a linear category
{\mathcal{C}}
equipped with natural involutions
(-)^{*}:{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)\to{\mathcal{C}}(Y,X)
. Naturality means that
(-)^{*}
can be seen as a funtcor

{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{C}}^{\op},

acting as the identity function on the set of objects. In the category
{\sf Hilb}_{fd}
of finite dimensional Hilbert spaces, for instance, the standard
*
-structure is given by the adjoint operation on the spaces of bounded linear maps.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a
*
-category, and suppose that
{\mathcal{C}}
admits finite direct sums. Then
{\mathcal{C}}
is a C
∗
-category if
{\mathcal{C}}(X):={\mathcal{C}}(X,X)
is a C
∗
-algebra for every
X\in{\mathcal{C}}
.

Definition 2.5
.

A C
∗
-tensor category is a C
∗
-category
{\mathcal{C}}
equipped with a tensor structure
\boxtimes:{\mathcal{C}}\times{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
such that

((-)\boxtimes(-))^{*}=(-)^{*}\boxtimes(-)^{*}

on morphisms.

We shall make use of the following terminology. For tensor categories
{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
, a tensor functor
F:{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
is understood to have natural isomorphisms as tensor structure. A lax functor
F:{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
is understood to be a functor equipped with associators and unitors, but they are not required to be natural isomorphisms.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
be C
∗
-tensor categories. A tensor functor
F:{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
is a
unitary tensor functor
if it is
*
-preserving and the tensor structure consist of unitaries.

We will assume throughout the paper all tensor categories of interest to have been strictified.

Examples of C
∗
-tensor categories include: the category
{\sf Rep}({\mathbb{G}})
of unitary representations of a locally compact (quantum) group
{\mathbb{G}}
, the category
{\sf Bim}(N)
of bimodules over a von Neumann algebra
N
and the category
{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}
of non-degenerate right C
∗
-correspondences over a C
∗
-algebra
A
.

We will find latter in this paper unitary functors
{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}
that are almost unitary tensor functors, Definition
2.6
being broken due to the associators not being adjointable morphisms.

Definition 2.7
.

A functor
F:{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}
is a
weak unitary tensor functor
if it is unitary and if it is equipped with isometries

F^{2}=\{F^{2}_{X,Y}:F(X)\boxtimes F(Y)\to F(X\boxtimes Y)\ |\ X,Y\in{\mathcal{C}}\},

not necessarily adjointable, satisfying pentagon coherence.

Remark 2.8
.

Weak unitary tensor functors are the key to Tannaka-Krein duality for compact quantum group actions
[
Nes14
]
.

We wish to introduce now the concept of duality in C
∗
-tensor categories. As a motivating example, let us fix a unital C
∗
-algebra
A
, and consider the subcategory
{\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A)
of
{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}
consisting of bi-Hilbertian, finitely generated projective correspondences over

, it admits a right Pimsner-Popa basis, consisting of a finite set

Thus, if
{\mathbb{D}}
is a C*-algebra object in a UTC
{\mathcal{C}}
, then
{\mathbb{D}}
has the structure of a lax tensor functor
{\mathbb{D}}:{\mathcal{C}}^{\op}\to{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1}){\hbox{-}}{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1})}
. It can be deduced moreover that the components of the lax structure
{\mathbb{D}}^{2}
are isometries, possibly non-adjointable, i.e.,
{\mathbb{D}}^{2}
is a
weak unitary tensor functor
as introduced in the previous Section.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
{\mathbb{D}}
be a C
∗
-algebra object in
{\sf Vec}({\mathcal{C}})
. Then
{\mathbb{D}}^{2}
induces isometric linear maps

{\mathbb{D}}(X)\underset{{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1})}{\boxtimes}{\mathbb{D}}(Y)\to{\mathbb{D}}(X\boxtimes Y)

for
X,Y\in{\mathcal{C}}
.

Proof.

Fix
X,Y\in{\mathcal{C}}
. It follows from the axioms of
{\mathbb{D}}^{2}
that
{\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{X,Y}:{\mathbb{D}}(X)\odot{\mathbb{D}}(Y)
is
{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1})
-balanced. Using the right
{\mathcal{C}}
-module C
∗
-category
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{D}}
associated to
{\mathbb{D}}
, given
f\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{D}}({\mathbb{D}}\odot X,{\mathbb{D}})
and
g\in{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathbb{D}}}({\mathbb{D}}\odot Y,{\mathbb{D}})
, we have

{\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{X,Y}(f\odot g)=g(f\odot\id_{Y}):{\mathbb{D}}\odot(X\boxtimes Y)\simeq({\mathbb{D}}\odot X)\odot Y\to{\mathbb{D}},

and that

\displaystyle\langle{\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{X,Y}(f\odot g),{\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{X,Y}(f\odot g)\rangle_{{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1})}

\displaystyle=g(f\odot\id_{Y})(f^{*}\odot\id_{Y})g^{*}

\displaystyle=\langle g,\langle f,f\rangle_{{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1})}\triangleright g\rangle_{{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1})}

\displaystyle=\langle f\boxtimes g,f\boxtimes g\rangle_{{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1})}.

∎

We summarize our conclusions in the following Corollary.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a UTC and let
{\mathbb{D}}
be a C
∗
-algebra object in
{\sf Vec}({\mathcal{C}})
. Then the functor
{\mathbb{D}}:{\mathcal{C}}^{\op}\to{\sf Vec}
can be endowed with a canonical structure of a weak unitary tensor functor from
{\mathcal{C}}^{\op}
to
{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1}){\hbox{-}}{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1})}
.

To finish the preliminary section, we have one final observation. This appears in Appendix A of
[
HY22
]
.

Lemma 3.8
.

If
{\mathbb{D}}
is a C*-algebra object as above, for each
X\in{\mathcal{C}}
,
{\mathbb{D}}(X)
has a canonical structure of right Hilbert
{\mathbb{D}}(\overline{X}\boxtimes X)
-module.

Proof.

The action is defined, for
a\in{\mathbb{D}}(X)
and
x\in{\mathbb{D}}(\overline{X}\boxtimes X)
, by

a\triangleleft x:={\mathbb{D}}(R_{X}\boxtimes\id_{X})\left({\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{X,\overline{X}\boxtimes X}(a\odot x)\right).

The
{\mathbb{D}}(\overline{X}\boxtimes X)
-valued inner product is given by

\langle a,a^{\prime}\rangle_{{\mathbb{D}}(\bar{X}\boxtimes X)}:={\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{\overline{X},X}(j^{\mathbb{D}}(a)\odot a^{\prime}),

which is shown to be
{\mathbb{D}}(\overline{X}\boxtimes X)
-linear by means of the following computation:

\displaystyle\langle a,a^{\prime}\triangleleft x\rangle_{{\mathbb{D}}(\bar{X}\boxtimes X)}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{\overline{X},X}\left(j^{\mathbb{D}}(a)\odot{\mathbb{D}}(R_{X}\boxtimes\id_{X})({\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{X,\overline{X}\boxtimes X}(a^{\prime}\odot x))\right)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{D}}(\id_{\overline{X}}\boxtimes R_{X}\boxtimes\id_{X}){\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{\overline{X},X\boxtimes\overline{X}\boxtimes X}\left(j^{\mathbb{D}}(a)\odot{\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{X,\overline{X}\boxtimes X}(a^{\prime}\odot x)\right)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{D}}(\id_{\overline{X}}\boxtimes R_{X}\boxtimes\id_{X}){\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{\overline{X}\boxtimes X,{\overline{X}\boxtimes X}}({\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{\overline{X},X}(j^{\mathbb{D}}(a)\odot a^{\prime})\odot x)

\displaystyle=\langle a,a^{\prime}\rangle_{{\mathbb{D}}(\bar{X}\boxtimes X)}\cdot x.

The right Hilbert
{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1})
-module structure of
{\mathbb{D}}(X)
is induced from the Hilbert
{\mathbb{D}}(\overline{X}\boxtimes X)
-module structure together with the conditional expecation
{\mathbb{E}}^{\mathbb{D}}_{X}:{\mathbb{D}}(\overline{X}\boxtimes X)\to{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1})
, meaning

\langle x,y\rangle_{{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1})}={\mathbb{E}}_{X}^{\mathbb{D}}(\langle x,y\rangle_{{\mathbb{D}}(\bar{X}\boxtimes X)}).

Thus the Banach space structure of
{\mathbb{D}}(X)
also coincides with the one coming from the right Hilbert
{\mathbb{D}}(\overline{X}\boxtimes X)
-module structure. From this it follows that the topology on
{\mathbb{D}}(X)
induced by the
{\mathbb{D}}(\bar{X}\boxtimes X)
-valued inner product is the same as the one induced by the
{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1})
-valued inner product, due to finiteness of the index of
{\mathbb{E}}_{X}^{\mathbb{D}}
. In particular,
{\mathbb{D}}(X)
is closed for the former topology.
∎

3.2
Coend realization

Given an arbitrary UTC
{\mathcal{C}}
and C*-algebra objects
{\mathbb{A}}
and
{\mathbb{B}}
in
{\sf Vec}({\mathcal{C}}^{\op})
and
{\sf Vec}({\mathcal{C}})
, respectively, it was shown in
[
JP19
]
that the
*
-algebra

|{\mathbb{A}}\times{\mathbb{B}}|:=\bigoplus_{X\in\Irr({\mathcal{C}})}{\mathbb{A}}(X)\odot{\mathbb{B}}(X)

has a universal C*-completion.
We show in this section that there is a canonical minimal C*-completion
{\mathbb{A}}\bowtie{\mathbb{B}}
of
|{\mathbb{A}}\times{\mathbb{B}}|
, analogous to regular representations, featuring a faithful conditional expectation
{\mathbb{A}}\bowtie{\mathbb{B}}\to{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbbm{1})\otimes{\mathbb{B}}(\mathbbm{1})
. This is analogous to the comparison between universal and reduced group C
∗
-algebras.

For
X\in{\mathcal{C}}
, as in Definition
3.1
we write
{\mathbb{E}}^{\mathbb{A}}_{X}
and
{\mathbb{E}}^{\mathbb{B}}_{X}
for the conditional expectations
{\mathbb{A}}(\overline{X}\boxtimes X)\to{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbbm{1})
and
{\mathbb{B}}(\overline{X}\boxtimes X)\to{\mathbb{B}}(\mathbbm{1})
, respectively. Let
{\mathbb{A}}(X)\otimes{\mathbb{B}}(X)
be the exterior tensor product of the
{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbbm{1})
-
{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbbm{1})
-correspondence
{\mathbb{A}}(X)
with the
{\mathbb{B}}(\mathbbm{1})
-
{\mathbb{B}}(\mathbbm{1})
-correspondence
{\mathbb{B}}(X)
, and define

{\mathcal{E}}:=\bigoplus_{X\in\Irr({\mathcal{C}})}^{\ell^{2}}{\mathbb{A}}(X)\otimes{\mathbb{B}}(X)

to be the completion of
\bigoplus_{X\in\Irr({\mathcal{C}})}{\mathbb{A}}(X)\otimes{\mathbb{B}}(X)
as a
({\mathbb{A}}(\mathbbm{1})\otimes{\mathbb{B}}(\mathbbm{1}))
-
({\mathbb{A}}(\mathbbm{1})\otimes{\mathbb{B}}(\mathbbm{1}))
-correspondence.

Given irreducible objects
X_{0},X_{1},X_{2}\in{\mathcal{C}}
, there is a Hilbert space structure on
{\mathcal{C}}(X_{0},X_{1}\boxtimes X_{2})
given by
\langle x,y\rangle:=x^{*}y
. Let us fix, for each triple
(X_{0},X_{1},X_{2})
of irreducible objects, a choice of an orthonormal basis
O(X_{0},X_{1}\boxtimes X_{2})
for this Hilbert space.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a UTC and
{\mathbb{D}}
a C*-algebra object in
{\sf Vec}({\mathcal{C}})
. For
X_{0},X_{1},X_{2}\in\Irr({\mathcal{C}})
, given
\xi_{0}\in{\mathbb{D}}(X_{0})
, the linear map
\xi_{0}\triangleright-:{\mathbb{D}}(X_{1})\to{\mathbb{D}}(X_{2})
given by

\xi_{0}\triangleright\xi_{1}:=\sum_{v\in O(X_{2},X_{0}\boxtimes X_{1})}{\mathbb{D}}(v)\left({\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{X_{0},X_{1}}(\xi_{0}\odot\xi_{1})\right)

is an adjointable map of
{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1})
-Hilbert C*-modules. Its adjoint is then given by
j^{\mathbb{D}}_{X_{0}}(\xi_{0})\triangleright(-)
.

Proof.

Given
X\in{\mathcal{C}}
, the definition of the
{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1})
-valued inner product on
{\mathbb{D}}(X)
is

\langle\xi,\xi^{\prime}\rangle_{{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1})}:={\mathbb{D}}(R_{X})\left({\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{\overline{X},X}(j^{\mathbb{D}}(\xi)\odot\xi^{\prime})\right).

Then, for
\xi_{i}\in{\mathbb{D}}(X_{i})
,
i\in\{0,1,2\}
,

\displaystyle\langle\xi_{0}\triangleright\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\rangle_{{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in O(X_{2},X_{0}\boxtimes X_{1})}\langle{\mathbb{D}}(v)\left({\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{X_{0},X_{1}}(\xi_{0}\odot\xi_{1})\right),\xi_{2}\rangle_{{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in O(X_{2},X_{0}\boxtimes X_{1})}{\mathbb{D}}(R_{X_{2}})\left({\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{\overline{X}_{2},X_{2}}\left(j^{\mathbb{D}}_{X_{2}}(\left(\left({\mathbb{D}}(v)({\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{X_{0},X_{1}}(\xi_{0}\odot\xi_{1})\right)\right))\odot\xi_{2}\right)\right).

Let
(v^{*})^{\vee}:=(\bar{R}_{X_{2}}^{*}\boxtimes\id_{\bar{X}_{1}\boxtimes\bar{X}_{0}})(\id_{\bar{X}_{2}}\boxtimes v^{*}\boxtimes\id_{\bar{X}_{1}\boxtimes\bar{X}_{0}})(\id_{\bar{X}_{2}}\boxtimes R_{X_{0}\boxtimes X_{1}})
. From the axioms in the definition of C
∗
-algebra objects, it follows that

j^{\mathbb{D}}_{X_{2}}(\left(\left({\mathbb{D}}(v)({\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{X_{0},X_{1}}(\xi_{0}\odot\xi_{1})\right)\right))=\left({\mathbb{D}}((v^{*})^{\vee}){\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{\overline{X}_{1},\overline{X}_{0}}(j^{\mathbb{D}}_{X_{1}}(\xi_{1})\odot j^{\mathbb{D}}_{X_{0}}(\xi_{0}))\right).

Consequently,

\displaystyle\langle\xi_{0}\triangleright\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\rangle_{{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in O(X_{2},X_{0}\boxtimes X_{1})}{\mathbb{D}}(R_{X_{2}})\left({\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{\overline{X}_{2},X_{2}}\left({\mathbb{D}}((v^{*})^{\vee}){\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{\overline{X}_{1},\overline{X}_{0}}(j^{\mathbb{D}}_{X_{1}}(\xi_{1})\odot j^{\mathbb{D}}_{X_{0}}(\xi_{0}))\right)\odot\xi_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in O(X_{2},X_{0}\boxtimes X_{1})}{\mathbb{D}}(R_{X_{2}})({\mathbb{D}}((v^{*})^{\vee})\odot\id)\left({\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{\overline{X}_{1}\boxtimes\overline{X}_{0},X_{2}}\left({\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{\overline{X}_{1},\overline{X}_{0}}(j^{\mathbb{D}}_{X_{1}}(\xi_{1})\odot j^{\mathbb{D}}_{X_{0}}(\xi_{0}))\odot\xi_{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in O(X_{2},X_{0}\boxtimes X_{1})}{\mathbb{D}}(R_{X_{2}})\left({\mathbb{D}}((v^{*})^{\vee}\boxtimes\id)\left({\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{\overline{X}_{1},\overline{X}_{0}\boxtimes X_{2}}\left(j^{\mathbb{D}}_{X_{1}}(\xi_{1})\odot{\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{\overline{X}_{0},X_{2}}(j^{\mathbb{D}}_{X_{0}}(\xi_{0})\odot\xi_{2})\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in O(X_{2},X_{0}\boxtimes X_{1})}{\mathbb{D}}(R_{X_{2}})\left({\mathbb{D}}(\id\boxtimes v^{*})\left({\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{\overline{X}_{1},\overline{X}_{0}\boxtimes X_{2}}\left(j^{\mathbb{D}}_{X_{1}}(\xi_{1})\odot{\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{\overline{X}_{0},X_{2}}(j^{\mathbb{D}}_{X_{0}}(\xi_{0})\odot\xi_{2})\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\overset{(*)}{=}\sum_{w\in O(X_{1},\overline{X}_{0}\boxtimes X_{2})}{\mathbb{D}}(R_{X_{1}})\left({\mathbb{D}}(\id\boxtimes w)\left({\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{\overline{X}_{1},\overline{X}_{0}\boxtimes X_{2}}\left(j^{\mathbb{D}}_{X_{1}}(\xi_{1})\odot{\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{\overline{X}_{0},X_{2}}(j^{\mathbb{D}}_{X_{0}}(\xi_{0})\odot\xi_{2})\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in O(X_{1},\overline{X}_{0}\boxtimes X_{2})}{\mathbb{D}}(R_{X_{1}})\left(\left({\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{\overline{X}_{1},\overline{X}_{0}\boxtimes X_{2}}\left(j^{\mathbb{D}}_{X_{1}}(\xi_{1})\odot{\mathbb{D}}(w){\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{\overline{X}_{0},X_{2}}(j^{\mathbb{D}}_{X_{0}}(\xi_{0})\odot\xi_{2})\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\langle\xi_{1},j^{\mathbb{D}}_{X_{0}}(\xi_{0})\triangleright\xi_{2}\rangle.

In the fifth equality marked with
(*)
, we are making use of the canonical isomorphism

{\mathcal{C}}(X_{2},X_{0}\boxtimes X_{1})\overset{\text{Frob.}}{\simeq}{\mathcal{C}}(\overline{X}_{0}\boxtimes X_{2},X_{1})\overset{*}{\simeq}{\mathcal{C}}(X_{1},\overline{X}_{0}\boxtimes X_{2}).

∎

Remark 3.10
.

As a consequence of the above computation, each map

\xi_{1}\mapsto{\mathbb{D}}(\omega)\left({\mathbb{D}}^{2}_{X_{0},X_{1}}(\xi_{0}\odot\xi_{1})\right),

with
\omega\in O(X_{2},X_{0}\boxtimes X_{1})
, is adjointable. We shall make use of this observation later.

Applying the Lemma above to the C
∗
-algebra objects
{\mathbb{A}}
and
{\mathbb{B}}
simultaneously, we obtain the following.

Corollary 3.11
.

|{\mathbb{A}}\times{\mathbb{B}}|
is canonically and faithfully represented on
{\mathcal{L}}_{{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbbm{1})\otimes{\mathbb{B}}(\mathbbm{1})}({\mathcal{E}})
. Moreover, the vector
\Omega:=1_{{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbbm{1})}\otimes 1_{{\mathbb{B}}(\mathbbm{1})}
is a cyclic vector for
|{\mathbb{A}}\times{\mathbb{B}}|
.

Definition 3.12
.

Define
{\mathbb{A}}\bowtie{\mathbb{B}}
to be the norm closure of
|{\mathbb{A}}\times{\mathbb{B}}|
in
{\mathcal{L}}_{{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbbm{1})\otimes{\mathbb{B}}(\mathbbm{1})}({\mathcal{E}})
.

Using the notation
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
for the
{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbbm{1})\otimes{\mathbb{B}}(\mathbbm{1})
-valued inner product on
{\mathcal{E}}
, the formula

{\mathbb{A}}\bowtie{\mathbb{B}}\ni T\mapsto{\mathbb{E}}(T):=\langle T\Omega,\Omega\rangle

defines a conditional expectation
{\mathbb{A}}\bowtie{\mathbb{B}}\to{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbbm{1})\otimes{\mathbb{B}}(\mathbbm{1})

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\odot b_{i}\in{\mathbb{A}}(X)\odot{\mathbb{B}}(X)
. Then

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}{\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{\overline{X},X}(j^{\mathbb{A}}_{X}(a_{i})\odot a_{j})\otimes{\mathbb{B}}^{2}_{\overline{X},X}(j^{\mathbb{B}}_{X}(b_{i})\odot b_{j})

is positive in
{\mathbb{A}}(\overline{X}\boxtimes X)\otimes{\mathbb{B}}(\overline{X}\boxtimes X)
.

Proof.

Consider on
{\mathbb{A}}(X)
the canonical right Hilbert
{\mathbb{A}}(\overline{X}\boxtimes X)
-C*-module structure from Lemma
3.8
. Observe that

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}{\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{\overline{X},X}(j^{\mathbb{A}}_{X}(a_{i})\odot a_{j})\otimes{\mathbb{B}}^{2}_{\overline{X},X}(j^{\mathbb{B}}_{X}(b_{i})\odot b_{j})

is the
{\mathbb{A}}(\overline{X}\boxtimes X)\otimes{\mathbb{B}}(\overline{X}\boxtimes X)
-valued inner product of
\sum_{i}a_{i}\otimes b_{i}
with itself in the exterior tensor product
{\mathbb{A}}(X)\otimes{\mathbb{B}}(X)
.
∎

Lemma 3.14
.

For
T\in{\mathbb{A}}(X)\odot{\mathbb{B}}(X)\subset{\mathbb{A}}\bowtie{\mathbb{B}}
, it holds

\|T\Omega\|\leq\|T\|_{{\mathbb{A}}\bowtie{\mathbb{B}}}\leq d_{X}^{2}\|T\Omega\|

.

Proof.

Writing
T=\sum_{i}a_{i}\odot b_{i}\in{\mathbb{A}}(X)\odot{\mathbb{B}}(X)
, we have

{\mathbb{E}}(T^{*}T)=(E^{\mathbb{A}}_{X}\otimes E^{\mathbb{B}}_{X})\left(\sum_{i,j}{\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{\overline{X},X}(j^{\mathbb{A}}_{X}(a_{i})\odot a_{j})\otimes{\mathbb{B}}^{2}_{\overline{X},X}(j^{\mathbb{B}}_{X}(b_{i})\odot b_{j})\right).

Since both
E^{\mathbb{A}}_{X}
and
E^{\mathbb{B}}_{X}
are conditional expectations with Pimsner-Popa index at most
d_{X}^{2}
,
E^{\mathbb{A}}_{X}\otimes E^{\mathbb{B}}_{X}
has Pimsner-Popa index at most
d_{X}^{4}
. Moreover, the argument of
(E^{\mathbb{A}}_{X}\otimes E^{\mathbb{B}}_{X})
in the right-hand-side of the above inequality is positive by Lemma
3.13
. Thus,

\|T\Omega\|^{2}=\|{\mathbb{E}}(T^{*}T)\|\leq\|T^{*}T\|_{{\mathbb{A}}\bowtie{\mathbb{B}}}\leq d_{X}^{4}\|{\mathbb{E}}(T^{*}T)\|=d_{X}^{4}\|T\Omega\|^{2}.

∎

Now we prove Theorem
A
, which generalizes Proposition 3.7 in
[
HPN23
]
to arbitrary C
∗
-algebra objects instead of just connected ones.
In that case, the proof relied on the fibers being finite-dimensional, while for a general C*-algebra objects, we use the fact that its fibers are finite-index over the base algebra.

Theorem 3.15

(Theorem
A
)
.

The conditional expectation
{\mathbb{E}}:{\mathbb{A}}\bowtie{\mathbb{B}}\to{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbbm{1})\otimes{\mathbb{B}}(\mathbbm{1})
is faithful.

Proof.

Let
T\in{\mathbb{A}}\bowtie{\mathbb{B}}
be such that
{\mathbb{E}}(T^{*}T)=0
. Let
(T_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be a net in
|{\mathbb{A}}\times{\mathbb{B}}|
converging to
T
in norm. Write

T_{\lambda}=\sum_{X\in\Irr({\mathcal{C}})}T_{\lambda}^{(X)},

with
T_{\lambda}^{(X)}\in{\mathbb{A}}(X)\odot{\mathbb{B}}(X)
. Observe that
E(T^{*}T)=0
if and only if
T\Omega=0
. Let
P_{X}:{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathbb{A}}(X)\otimes{\mathbb{B}}(X)
be the canonical projection. Then we have

T_{\lambda}^{(X)}\Omega=P_{X}T_{\lambda}\Omega\to P_{X}T\Omega=0.

From Lemma
3.14
,
T_{\lambda}^{(X)}\Omega\to 0
implies
T_{\lambda}^{(X)}\to 0
in the operator norm.
Let
Y,Z\in\Irr({\mathcal{C}})
. For
S\in{\mathbb{A}}(Y)\odot{\mathbb{B}}(Y)
,
T_{\lambda}^{(X)}S\Omega
is supported on the direct summands
{\mathbb{A}}(W)\otimes{\mathbb{B}}(W)
of
{\mathcal{E}}
for which
W\leq X\boxtimes Y
, meaning
{\mathcal{C}}(W,X\boxtimes Y)\neq 0
. Using Frobenius reciprocity in
{\mathcal{C}}
, we have then

P_{Z}T_{\lambda}S\Omega=\sum_{X\leq Z\boxtimes\overline{Y}}P_{Z}T_{\lambda}^{(X)}S\Omega.

Observe that the sum above is over a finite index set. It follows that

P_{Z}TS\Omega=\lim_{\lambda}P_{Z}T_{\lambda}\Omega=\sum_{X\leq Z\boxtimes\overline{Y}}\lim_{\lambda}P_{Z}T_{\lambda}^{(X)}S\Omega=0.

Since

TS\Omega=\sum_{Z\in\Irr({\mathcal{C}})}P_{Z}TS\Omega,

it follows that
TS\Omega=0
. From the density of
\{S\Omega\ |S\in{\mathbb{A}}(Y)\odot{\mathbb{D}}(Y),\ Y\in\Irr({\mathcal{C}})\}
in
{\mathcal{E}}
, we conclude that
T=0
.
∎

Definition 3.16
.

An action of a unitary tensor category on a unital C
∗
-algebra
A
is a unitary tensor functor
\alpha:{\mathcal{C}}\to{\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A)
.

Remark 3.17
.

In the above definition,
\alpha:{\mathcal{C}}\to{\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A)
is said to be an
outer action
if it is fully faithful. Such outer actions are of major interest in the literature, but our results can be proven in greater generality.

Recall that an action
{\mathcal{C}}\curvearrowright A
of a unitary tensor category
{\mathcal{C}}
on a C
∗
-algebra
A
can in particular be seen as a C
∗
-algebra object in
{\mathcal{C}}^{\op}
.

Definition 3.18
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a unitary tensor category and
A
a unital C
∗
-algebra, and let
\alpha:{\mathcal{C}}\to{\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A)
be an action. Let
{\mathbb{D}}
be a C
∗
-algebra object in
{\sf Vec}({\mathcal{C}})
. The crossed product of
A
by the pair
(\alpha,{\mathbb{D}})
is defined to be the coend realization

\displaystyle A\rtimes{\mathbb{D}}:=\alpha\bowtie{\mathbb{D}}.

By Theorem
3.15
, there is a canonical faithful conditional expectation
{\mathbb{E}}:A\underset{\alpha}{\rtimes}{\mathbb{D}}\to A\otimes{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1})
.

3.3
Conditional expectations down to
A

We say that a conditional expectation
E:A\rtimes{\mathbb{D}}\to A
is compatible with the canonical conditional expectation
{\mathbb{E}}:A\rtimes{\mathbb{D}}\to A\otimes{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1})
if it is the composite of
{\mathbb{E}}
with a conditional expectation
E:A\otimes{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1})\to A
. The following Lemma will allow us to characterize such conditional expectations by means of states on the ground C
∗
-algebra
{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1})
.

Lemma 3.19
.

Let
A
and
D
be unital C*-algebras and assume
A
has trivial center. Then there is a one-to-one correspondence between ucp
A{\hbox{-}}A
-bimodular maps
E:A\otimes D\to A
and states on
D
, where
A
acts on
A\otimes D
via the embedding
A\mapsto A\otimes 1_{D}\subset A\otimes D
.

Proof.

Given a state
\omega
on
D
, the corresponding ucp map is
E_{\omega}:=\id_{A}\otimes\omega
. Now, if
E:A\otimes D\to A
is a ucp
A{\hbox{-}}A
-bimodular map, for any
d\in D
, the map

A\ni a\mapsto E_{d}(a)=E(a\otimes d)

is a bounded
A{\hbox{-}}A
-bimodular map
A\to A
. Since
\End_{A{\hbox{-}}A}(A)\simeq{\mathcal{Z}}(A)\simeq{\mathbb{C}}
, there is
\omega_{E}:D\to{\mathbb{C}}
such that

E_{d}=\omega_{E}(d)\id_{A}.

From the fact that
E
is ucp one deduces that
\omega_{E}
is a state. Indeed, for all
d\in D
,

\omega_{E}(d^{*}d)1_{A}=E_{d^{*}d}(1_{A})=E(1_{A}\otimes d^{*}d)\geq 0,

and
\omega_{E}(1_{D})1_{A}=E(1_{A}\otimes 1_{D})=1
.
It is immediate that the constructions
\omega\mapsto E_{\omega}
and
E\mapsto\omega_{E}
are mutually inverses.
∎

Proposition 3.20
.

Let
\alpha:{\mathcal{C}}\to{\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A)
be an action of a unitary tensor category on a unital C*-algebra
A
with trivial center. Let
{\mathbb{D}}
be a C*-algebra object in
{\sf Vec}({\mathcal{C}})
. Then there is an order preserving one-to-one correspondence between conditional expectations
A\rtimes{\mathbb{D}}\to A
compatible with the canonical conditional expectation
A\rtimes{\mathbb{D}}\to A\otimes{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1})
and states on the C*-algebra
{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1})
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.19
, such conditional expectations are in bijections with
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1}))
. It is easy to see that if
\omega_{1}\leq\omega_{2}
in
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1}))
, then
E_{\omega_{1}}\leq E_{\omega_{2}}
.
∎

Observe that, in the Proposition above, the conditional expectation
E_{\omega}:A\rtimes{\mathbb{D}}\to A
is faithful if and only if the corresponding state
\omega
on
{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbbm{1})
is faithful.

4
Flavors of discreteness

4.1
Discrete, PQR and Ind inclusions

Let
A

be the right
A

-correspondence obtained as the completion of

in the

Theorem 4.1

(
[
HPN23
]
, Theorem 2.9)
.

Let

be as above. Let
{\mathcal{E}}=\overline{D}^{\|\cdot\|_{A}}

in

are mutually inverses. They are moreover natural in

\Psi:{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}(({\hbox{-}}),D)^{\diamondsuit}\leftrightarrow{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}D}(({\hbox{-}})\underset{A}{\boxtimes}D,D):\Phi

The isomorphism

-norm in the GNS-construction for C
∗
-algebras.

Corollary 4.2

(
[
HPN23
]
, Theorem 2.7)

The functor
{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}(({\hbox{-}}),D)^{\diamondsuit}:{\mathcal{C}}^{\op}\to{\sf Vec}
has a canonical structure of a C

-algebra object.

C

-discreteness will be characterized by the fact that the images of the maps in

as

Suppose we are given a Hilbert space object
{\mathcal{H}}
in the unitary ind-completion

Definition 4.3
.

The realization

of

-correspondence

Proposition 4.4
.

Realization of Hilbert space objects extends to a fully-faithful unitary tensor functor
|-|:{\sf Hilb}({\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A))\to{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}
, realizing
{\sf Hilb}({\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A))

{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}

Proof.

If

given on the
K

defines the action of
|-|
on morphisms. We have thus a functor
{\sf Hilb}({\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A))\to{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}

Let us show that this functor is a unitary tensor functor. Given Hilbert space objects
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{H}}_{1}\boxtimes{\mathcal{H}}_{2}|

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{K\in\Irr(A)}^{\ell^{2}}K\otimes({\mathcal{H}}_{1}\boxtimes{\mathcal{H}}_{2})(K)

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{K\in\Irr(A)}^{\ell^{2}}K\otimes\left(\bigoplus_{K_{1},K_{2}\in\Irr(A)}^{\ell^{2}}{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}(K,K_{1}\underset{A}{\boxtimes}K_{2})\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{1}(K_{1})\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{2}(K_{2})\right)

It remains to show that

\displaystyle{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}(|{\mathcal{H}}_{1}|,|{\mathcal{H}}_{2}|)

\displaystyle={\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}\left(\bigoplus_{K_{1}\in\Irr(A)}^{\ell^{2}}K_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{1}(K_{1}),\bigoplus_{K_{2}\in\Irr(A)}^{\ell^{2}}K_{2}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{2}(K_{2})\right)

∎

The third ind-object we consider is the fiberwise closure of

Lemma 4.6

Let

Proof.

We have the

The restriction of the conditional expectation

for a unique state

∎

Definition 4.7

(

]
, Section 4.5.)

on

The fibers of

Definition 4.8
.

Let

(1)

(2)

For each

-Frobenius reciprocity

Lemma 4.9
.

The closure of
{\mathbb{D}}(K)={\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}(K,{\mathcal{E}})^{\diamondsuit}
in the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{E}}(K)
coincides with
L^{2}_{\omega}{\mathbb{D}}(K)
.

Proof.

Given

, its

-norm

is defined by

f^{*}f(\xi)=\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{E}}(K)}^{2}\xi\qquad\forall\ \xi\in K,

or equivalently
\langle f(\xi),f(\xi)\rangle=\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{E}}(K)}^{2}\|\xi\|^{2}
for all
\xi\in K
. But

\displaystyle\langle f(\xi),f(\xi)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\check{f}(\xi)\Omega,\check{f}(\xi)\Omega\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(\check{f}(\xi))^{*}\check{f}(\xi)\Omega,\Omega\rangle

\displaystyle=E((\check{f}(\xi))^{*}\check{f}(\xi))

\displaystyle=(\id\otimes\omega_{E}){\mathbb{E}}^{\mathbb{D}}(\check{f}(\xi))^{*}\check{f}(\xi)).

In the identification of
A\rtimes{\mathbb{D}}
with a C
∗
-subalgebra of

, the element
\check{f}(\xi))^{*}\check{f}(\xi)
corresponds to

\left(\bar{\xi}\odot j^{\mathbb{D}}(\check{f})\right)\cdot\left(\xi\odot\check{f}\right),

and therefore

(\id\otimes\omega_{E}){\mathbb{E}}^{\mathbb{D}}(\check{f}(\xi))^{*}\check{f}(\xi))=\|\xi\|^{2}\omega_{E}({\mathbb{E}}^{\mathbb{D}}(j^{\mathbb{D}}(\check{f})\cdot\check{f})),

where we are writing

for short. Since this must hold for every

\|f\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{E}}(K)}^{2}=\omega_{E}({\mathbb{E}}^{\mathbb{D}}(j^{\mathbb{D}}(\check{f})\cdot\check{f})).

∎

Proposition 4.10

In Definition

(1)

Proof.

The closure of

in

. Since

4.2
Characterization of C*-discrete inclusions

In the spirit of categorical duality for compact quantum group actions
[
NT13
]
and of subfactor reconstruction, in this Section we characterize C
∗
-discrete inclusions by means of categorical data.

Definition 4.12
.

Given a unitary tensor category
{\mathcal{C}}
, let
{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{\Omega}({\sf Vec}({\mathcal{C}}))
be the category whose objects are pairs
({\mathbb{D}},\omega)
, where
{\mathbb{D}}
is a C*-algebra object and
\omega
is a faithful state on
{\mathbb{D}}
and whose morphisms are state-preserving ucp maps
\theta:({\mathbb{D}}_{1},\omega_{1})\to({\mathbb{D}}_{2},\omega_{2})
is a morphism iff
\theta
is a ucp map
{\mathbb{D}}_{1}\to{\mathbb{D}}_{2}
and
\omega_{2}\circ\theta=\omega_{1}
.

Definition 4.13
.

Given a unital C
∗
-algebra
A
with trivial center, let
{\sf C^{*}Disc}(A)
denote the category whose objects are discrete inclusions
A\overset{E}{\subset}D
. Morphisms between objects
(A\subset D_{1},E_{1})
and
(A\subset D_{2},E_{2})
are
A
-
A
-bimodular ucp-maps
\phi:D_{1}\to D_{2}
mapping the conditional expectation
E_{1}
to
E_{2}
, i.e.,
E_{2}\circ\phi=E_{1}
.

Remark 4.14
.

In Section
5
, the symbol
\Omega
will also be used to denote cyclic vectors for correspondences. Below, a state on a C
∗
-algebra object will allow for a
realization
of the abstract categorical data as concrete operator algebraic data. This is analogous to the GNS-construction, justifying the overuse of the symbol
\Omega
.

The following theorem is an extension of
[
HPN23
, Theorem 4.3]
to non-connected C*-algebra objects.

Theorem 4.15

(Theorem
B
)
.

There is an equivalence

{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{\Omega}({\sf Vec}({\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A)))\simeq{\sf C^{*}Disc}(A).

Proof.

At the level of objects, the functor
{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{\Omega}({\sf Vec}({\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A)))\to{\sf C^{*}Disc}(A)
is given by taking the crossed-product. Functoriality is proven as in Theorem 5.7 in
[
Hat23
]
, but we give a sketch of the proof for completeness.

Suppose
\theta:({\mathbb{D}}_{1},\omega_{1})\to({\mathbb{D}}_{2},\omega_{2})
is a morphism in
{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{\Omega}({\sf Vec}({\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A)))
. Define

\displaystyle|\theta|:

\displaystyle|{\mathbb{D}}_{1}|\to|{\mathbb{D}}_{2}|

\displaystyle\xi\odot\eta\mapsto\xi\odot\theta(\eta).

It extends to a ucp-map
\id_{A}\rtimes\theta:A\rtimes{\mathbb{D}}_{1}\to A\rtimes{\mathbb{D}}_{2}
. Let
\pi_{2}:{\mathbb{D}}_{2}\to{\mathbb{B}}(L^{2}_{\omega_{2}}{\mathbb{D}}_{2})
be the GNS-representation. The composite
\pi_{2}\circ\theta
is a ucp map
{\mathbb{D}}_{1}\to{\mathbb{B}}(L^{2}_{\omega_{2}}{\mathbb{D}}_{2})
. For
\xi\odot\eta\in K\odot{\mathbb{D}}_{1}(K)
, write

|\pi_{2}\circ\theta|(\xi\odot\eta):=\xi\odot\pi_{2}(\theta(\eta)).

Then
|\pi_{2}\circ\theta|
extends to a ucp-map
A\rtimes{\mathbb{D}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{A}(|L^{2}_{\omega_{2}}{\mathbb{D}}_{2}|)
in such a way that
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A\rtimes{\mathbb{D}}_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A\rtimes{\mathbb{D}}_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{L}}_{A}(|L^{2}_{\omega_{2}}{\mathbb{D}}_{2}|)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\id_{A}\rtimes\theta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces|\pi_{2}\circ\theta|}
can.

commutes; in the diagram,
can.
denotes the canonical representation of
A\rtimes{\mathbb{D}}_{2}
on
|L^{2}_{\omega_{2}}{\mathbb{D}}_{2}|
. Denoting by
E_{i}:A\rtimes{\mathbb{D}}_{i}\to A
the conditional expectations corresponding to
\omega_{i}
, since
\omega_{2}\circ\theta_{1}=\omega_{1}
, it follows

E_{2}\circ(\id_{A}\rtimes\theta)=E_{1}.

We remark that, in contrast with
[
Hat23
]
and
[
JP19
]
, the above proof of functoriality is shorter because complete positivity implies norm continuity. In the cited papers, the context was a W
∗
-context, and normality of the realization of the maps had to be shown.

Let us construct the functor
{\sf C^{*}Disc}(A)\to{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{\Omega}({\sf Vec}({\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A)))
. At the level of objects, it is given by taking the underlying C*-algebra object associated to the discrete inclusion, together with the faithful state on it provided by the faithful conditional expectation (Proposition
3.20
). We are left to check functoriality with respect to morphisms. Suppose
\phi:(A\subset D_{1},E_{1})\to(A\subset D_{2},E_{2})
is a morphism of discrete inclusions. Using that
\phi
sends
E_{1}
to
E_{2}
and Kadisons’ inequality, we obtain

E_{2}(\phi(x^{*})\phi(x))\leq E_{2}(\phi(x^{*}x))=E_{1}(x^{*}x)\qquad\forall\ x\in D_{1}.

As a consequence,
\phi
extends to a bounded
A
-
A
-bimodular map
\Phi:{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
, where
{\mathcal{E}}_{i}:=\overline{D_{i}}^{\|\cdot\|_{A_{i}}}
. Let
{\mathbb{D}}_{i}
be the underlying C*-algebra object of the discrete inclusion
(A\subset D_{i},E_{i})
, and let
\omega_{i}
be the faithful state on
{\mathbb{D}}_{i}
provided by the conditional expectation
E_{i}
. Recall that

{\mathbb{D}}_{i}(K)={\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}(K,{\mathcal{E}}_{i})^{\diamondsuit},

and, since
A\subset D_{i}
is assumed C
∗
-discrete, which implies PQR,

L^{2}_{\omega_{i}}{\mathbb{D}}_{i}(K)={\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}(K,{\mathcal{E}}_{i}),

where
K\in{\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A)
. For every such
K
, and for every
f\in{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A}(K,{\mathcal{E}}_{1})
,
\Phi\circ f
is a bounded
A{\hbox{-}}A
-bimodular map, and therefore adjointable since
K
is fgp. We have thus, for every
K\in{\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A)
, a well defined linear map

{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}(K,{\mathcal{E}}_{1})\to{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}(K,{\mathcal{E}}_{2})

given by
f\mapsto\Phi\circ f
.
Since
\Phi
extends
\phi:D_{1}\to D_{2}
, it preserves the diamond spaces, and defines therefore a natural transformation
{\mathbb{D}}_{1}\to{\mathbb{D}}_{2}
. Since
\phi
is an
A{\hbox{-}}A
-bimodular ucp map, the resulting morphism
{\mathbb{D}}_{1}\to{\mathbb{D}}_{2}
is easily seen to be a ucp map of C*-algebra objects.

It is straightforward to check that the functors
{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{\Omega}({\sf Vec}({\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A)))\leftrightarrow{\sf C^{*}Disc}(A)
are mutually quasi-inverses.
∎

4.3
Characterization of ind-inclusions

Let
A\overset{E}{\subset}D
be an ind-inclusion of unital C*-algebras, where
A
is assumed to have trivial center. Our goal in this section is to characterize the algebra

{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}({\mathcal{E}})

where
{\mathcal{E}}
is as in Section 4, i.e., the right
A{\hbox{-}}A
correspondence obtained from
D
and the conditional expectation
E:D\to A
. We will show in this section that a decomposition of
{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}({\mathcal{E}})
into a direct product of type I factors characterizes
A\overset{E}{\subset}D
as being an ind-inclusion.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{E}}
be the object in
{\sf Hilb}({\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A))
associated to
{\mathcal{E}}
. Recall
A\overset{E}{\subset}D
being an ind-inclusion means

{\mathcal{E}}\simeq|{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{E}}|:=\bigoplus_{K\in\Irr(A)}^{\ell^{2}}K\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{E}}(K)\ \,

as a right
A{\hbox{-}}A
-correspondence. In particular,
K\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{E}}(K)
is a complemented
A{\hbox{-}}A
-subcorrespondence of
{\mathcal{E}}
for all
K\in\Irr(A)
. Let us denote by
P_{K}
the corresponding orthogonal central projection. The net of projections which associates to every finite subset
\Lambda\subset\Irr(A)
the projection

\sum_{K\in\Lambda}P_{K}

converges strictly to the identity
\id_{\mathcal{E}}
.

Lemma 4.16
.

For each
K\in\Irr(A)
,

P_{K}{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}({\mathcal{E}})\simeq{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{E}}(K)).

Proof.

It follows from the above orthogonal decomposition of
{\mathcal{E}}
that

P_{K}{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}({\mathcal{E}})=P_{K}{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}({\mathcal{E}})P_{K}\simeq{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}(K\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{E}}(K)).

Since
K
is a simple
A{\hbox{-}}A
-bimodule, it follows that
the latter is
{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{E}}(K))
.
∎

Corollary 4.17
.

There is an isomorphism

{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}({\mathcal{E}})\simeq\prod_{K\in\Irr(A)}^{\ell^{\infty}}{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{E}}(K)).

Proof.

It follows from the discussion above that there is an embedding of
{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}({\mathcal{E}})
into
\prod_{K}^{\ell^{\infty}}{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{E}}(K))
.
Now an element
x\in\prod_{K}^{\ell^{\infty}}{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{E}}(K))
is a bounded family
(x_{K})_{K\in\Irr(A)}
where
x_{K}\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{E}}(K))
. There is a unique
\tilde{x}\in{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}({\mathcal{E}})
defined by the condition that

P_{k}\tilde{x}=(\id_{K}\otimes x_{K})P_{K}\qquad\forall\ K\in\Irr(A).

Then the image of
\tilde{x}
under the embedding
{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}({\mathcal{E}})\hookrightarrow\prod_{K}^{\ell^{\infty}}{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{E}}(K))
coincides with
x
.
∎

We now show the results above characterize ind-inclusions.

Theorem 4.18

(Theorem
C
)
.

The inclusion
A\overset{E}{\subset}D
is an ind-inclusion if and only if there exists a family of mutually orthogonal central projections
\{P_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subset{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}({\mathcal{E}})
such that

(1)

\sum_{i\in I}P_{i}
converges strictly to
\id_{\mathcal{E}}
;

(2)

for each
i\in I
there is a Hilbert space
H_{i}
such that
P_{i}{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}({\mathcal{E}})\simeq{\mathcal{L}}(H_{i})
;

(3)

for every projection
P\in{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}({\mathcal{E}})
,
P({\mathcal{E}})\in{\sf Hilb}({\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A))
.

Proof.

We already know that
A\subset D
being and ind-inclusion implies the existence of
\{P_{i}\}_{i\in I}
with the properties above. Let us prove the converse.

Given
i\in I
,
(3)
implies that

P_{i}({\mathcal{E}})=\bigoplus_{K\in\Irr(A)}^{\ell^{2}}K\otimes H_{i}(K),

where
H_{i}\in{\sf Hilb}({\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A))
. Since for
i\neq j
in
I
we have
P_{i}P_{j}=P_{j}P_{i}=0
, the supports of
H_{i}
and
H_{j}
in
\Irr(A)
must be disjoint. (
K\in\Irr(A)
belongs to the support of
H_{i}
means that
H_{i}(K)\neq 0
.) Thus, if
K
maps non-trivially into
{\mathcal{E}}
as an
A{\hbox{-}}A
-bimodule, there is a unique
i\in I
such that
K
belongs to the support of
H_{i}
. For
i,j\in I
,

{\sf Hilb}({\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A))(H_{i},H_{j})=\prod_{K\in\Irr(A)}^{\ell^{\infty}}{\mathcal{L}}(H_{i}(K),H_{j}(K)).

Now the orthogonality of the family
\{P_{i}\}_{i}
together with item
(2)
imply that for each
i
there is a unique
K_{i}
in the support of
H_{i}
. Moreover, there is an embedding

\bigoplus_{i\in I}K_{i}\otimes H_{i}(K_{i})\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{E}}.

Item
(1)
implies that this embedding has dense range, that is,

{\mathcal{E}}\simeq\bigoplus_{i\in I}^{\ell^{2}}K_{i}\otimes H_{i}(K_{i}).

Thus, defining the Hilbert space object
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{E}}
by
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{E}}(K_{i})=H_{i}(K_{i})
,
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{E}}(K)=0
if
K
is not in the support of any of the
H_{i}
’s, it follows

{\mathcal{E}}\simeq|{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{E}}|.

∎

5.1
Compact quantum group actions

Now we explain how the theory of continuous actions of compact quantum groups fits into our framework, providing examples of C
∗
-discrete inclusions. For the fundamentals of the theory of compact quantum groups and their representation categories, see
[
NT13
]
.

Let
{\mathbb{G}}
be a compact quantum group with reduced algebra of functions
C({\mathbb{G}})
, i.e., we consider the Haar state to be faithful on
C({\mathbb{G}})
. Consider the unitary tensor category
{\sf Rep}({\mathbb{G}})
of finite dimensional unitary representations of
{\mathbb{G}}
, together with its fiber functor
F:{\sf Rep}({\mathbb{G}})\to{\sf Hilb}_{fd}
, which is in particular a C
∗
-algebra object in
{\sf Vec}({\sf Rep}({\mathbb{G}})^{\op})
. Then the coend realization

F\bowtie(-):{\mathbb{A}}\mapsto F\rtimes{\mathbb{A}}=:A

can be equipped with the structure of a functor from the category of C
∗
-algebra objects in
{\sf Vec}({\sf Rep}({\mathbb{G}}))
to the category of C
∗
-algebras equipped with a
continuous
action of
{\mathbb{G}}
(
[
Nes14
]
). There is a canonical isomorphism

{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbbm{1})\simeq A^{{\mathbb{G}}},

where
A^{{\mathbb{G}}}
denotes the fixed point subalgebra of
A
under the
{\mathbb{G}}
-action, and
\mathbbm{1}\in{\sf Rep}({\mathbb{G}})
stands for the trivial representation. The canonical conditional expectation

{\mathbb{E}}:A=F\bowtie{\mathbb{A}}\to F(\mathbbm{1})\otimes{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbbm{1})\simeq{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbbm{1})\simeq A^{{\mathbb{G}}}

corresponds to averaging along the
{\mathbb{G}}
-action on
A
by means of the Haar state.

5.2
Semi-circular systems

In this section we describe when an
A
-valued semi-circular system gives rise to an ind-inclusion. This is equivalent to asking when the Fock space
{\mathcal{F}}(\eta)
of the given covariance matrix is an ind-object in
{\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A)
. Our main result here is a characterization of that property in terms of the covariance matrix
\eta
.

Definition 5.1
.

A covariance matrix on a C*-algebra
A
is a completely positive map
\eta:A\to A\otimes{\mathcal{L}}(\ell^{2}(I))
,
I
being an arbitrary index set.

Covariance matrices can be produced by means of the following Lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose
{\mathcal{E}}
is a right
A{\hbox{-}}A
-correspondence. Suppose
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a family of vectors in
{\mathcal{E}}
with the property that there exists
C\geq 0
such that

\sum_{j\in I}\|\langle\xi_{i},a\triangleright\xi_{j}\rangle\|^{2}\leq C\|a\|^{2}\ \forall\ a\in A,\ \forall\ i\in I.

Denoting by
\{e_{i,j}\}_{i,j\in I}
a choice of a system of matrix units in
{\mathcal{L}}(\ell^{2}(I))
, it follows that

\displaystyle\eta:A

\displaystyle\to A\otimes{\mathcal{L}}(\ell^{2}(I))

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{i,j\in I}\langle\xi_{i},a\triangleright\xi_{j}\rangle\otimes e_{i,j}

is a covariance matrix on
A
.

Proof.

In the canonical representation
A\otimes{\mathcal{L}}(\ell^{2}(I))\subset{\mathcal{L}}_{A}(A\otimes\ell^{2}(I))
, it is straightforward to see that

\sum_{j\in I}\|\langle\xi_{i},a\triangleright\xi_{j}\rangle\|^{2}\leq C\|a\|^{2}

for all
i\in I
implies
\|\eta(a)\|\leq C^{1/2}\|a\|
, and that
\eta(a^{*}a)
is positive for all
a
. To show that
\eta
is completely positive, observe that
\eta\otimes\id_{{\mathbb{M}}_{n}}
can be written in terms of the correspondence
{\mathcal{E}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
as

(\eta\otimes\id_{{\mathbb{M}}_{n}})(a\otimes m_{kl})=\sum_{i,j\in I}\sum_{k^{\prime},l^{\prime}=1}^{n}\langle\xi_{i}\otimes v_{k^{\prime}},(a\otimes m_{k,l})\triangleright(\xi_{j}\otimes v_{l^{\prime}}\rangle\otimes m_{k^{\prime},l^{\prime}}\otimes e_{i,j},

where
\{v_{k}\}_{k}
is an orthonormal basis for
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
and
\{m_{k,l}\}_{k,l}
the corresponding system of matrix units.
∎

The discussion to follow shows in particular that every covariance matrix can be obtained in this way.

Associated to a covariance matrix
\eta
, there is an
A{\hbox{-}}A\otimes{\mathcal{L}}(\ell^{2}(I))
-correspondence

A\underset{\eta}{\otimes}(A\otimes{\mathcal{L}}(\ell^{2}(I))).

It is the separation-completion of the algebraic tensor product
A\odot(A\otimes{\mathcal{L}}(\ell^{2}(I)))
with respect to the inner product

\langle a\otimes x,b\otimes y\rangle:=x^{*}\eta(a^{*}b)y.

Fix
i_{0}\in I
. There is an associated embedding of C*-algebras

A\simeq A\otimes{\mathbb{C}}e_{i_{0},i_{0}}\subset A\otimes{\mathcal{L}}(\ell^{2}(I)),

It is clear then that

{\mathcal{X}}:=[A\underset{\eta}{\otimes}(A\otimes{\mathcal{L}}(\ell^{2}(I)))]\triangleleft(1\otimes e_{i_{0},i_{0}})

inherits the structure of a right
A{\hbox{-}}A
-correspondence. Observe that, if we write

\eta(a)=\sum_{i,j}\eta_{i,j}(a)\otimes e_{i,j},

and define
\xi_{i}:=1\otimes e_{i,i_{0}}\in{\mathcal{X}}
, then

\langle\xi_{i},a\triangleright\xi_{j}\rangle=\eta_{i,j}(a).

Definition 5.3
.

The Fock space associated to the covariance matrix
\eta
above is

{\mathcal{F}}(\eta):=\bigoplus_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}}^{\ell^{2}}{\mathcal{X}}^{\underset{A}{\boxtimes}n},

where
{\mathcal{X}}^{\underset{A}{\boxtimes}0}:=A
.

Given
\xi\in{\mathcal{X}}
, let
T_{\xi}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{A}({\mathcal{F}}(\eta))
be the creation operator associated to
\xi
:

T_{\xi}(\eta)=\xi\boxtimes\eta\ \forall\ \eta\in{\mathcal{F}}(\eta).

Definition 5.4
.

The semi-circular operator
X_{\xi}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{A}({\mathcal{F}}(\eta))
associated to a vector
\xi\in{\mathcal{X}}
is given by

X_{\xi}:=T_{\xi}+T_{\xi}^{*}.

For
i\in I
, write
X_{i}:=X_{\xi_{i}}
. Let

\Phi(\eta):=C^{*}(A,\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I})\subset{\mathcal{L}}_{A}({\mathcal{F}}(\eta)).

The image
\Omega
of the unit
1_{A}\in A
under the embedding
A\to{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{F}}(\eta)
is a cyclic vector for the action of
\Phi(\eta)
. It induces a conditional expectation
E:\Phi(\eta)\to A
via

E(x):=\langle x\Omega,\Omega\rangle.

From
[
HPN24
, Theorem 5.2]
, a sufficient condition for the conditional expectation
E
to be faithful is that
A
admits a faithful tracial state
\tau
such that

\tau(\eta_{ij}(x)y)=\tau(x\eta_{ji}(y))
for all
x,y\in A
.

Assume from now on that
E
is faithful. Let
{\mathbb{D}}_{\eta}:{\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A)^{\op}\to{\sf Vec}
be given by

{\mathbb{D}}_{\eta}(K):={\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}(K,{\mathcal{F}}(\eta))^{\diamondsuit}:=\{f\in{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}(K,{\mathcal{F}}(\eta))\ |\ f[K]\subset\Phi(\eta)\Omega\}.

Due to C*-Frobenius reciprocity,
{\mathbb{D}}_{\eta}\in{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}({\sf Vec}({\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A)))
. There is a canonical embedding
A\rtimes{\mathbb{D}}_{\eta}\hookrightarrow\Phi(\eta)
, given by

\displaystyle K\otimes{\mathbb{D}}_{\eta}(K)

\displaystyle\to\Phi(\eta)

\displaystyle\zeta\otimes f

\displaystyle\mapsto\check{f}(\zeta),

where, for
K
a simple
A{\hbox{-}}A
-bimodule,
\check{f}
denotes the unique factorization of
f
through
\Phi(\eta)\ni x\mapsto x\triangleright\Omega\in{\mathcal{F}}(\eta)
.

Lemma 5.5
.

We have

\overline{\spann\{f(\xi)\ |f\in{\mathbb{D}}_{\eta}(K),\xi\in K,K\in\Irr(A)\}}^{\|\cdot\|_{A}}\simeq|L^{2}_{\omega}{\mathbb{D}}_{\eta}|,

as
A
-
A
correspondences, where
\omega
is the state associated to the conditional expectation
A\rtimes{\mathbb{D}}\to A
obtained by restriction of the conditional expectation on
\Phi(\eta)
.

Proof.

Recall the
*
-algebra

|A\times{\mathbb{D}}_{\eta}|:=\bigoplus_{K\in\Irr(A)}K\odot{\mathbb{D}}_{\eta}(K),

and the faithful conditional expectation
{\mathbb{E}}:|A\times{\mathbb{D}}_{\eta}|\to A\odot{\mathbb{D}}_{\eta}(\mathbbm{1})\subset A\otimes{\mathbb{D}}_{\eta}(\mathbbm{1})
. The state
\omega\in{\mathbb{D}}_{\eta}(\mathbbm{1})
is determined by
E=(\id_{A}\otimes\omega)\circ{\mathbb{E}}
.
Let
x_{\lambda}
be a net in
|A\times{\mathbb{D}}|
such that
x_{\lambda}\triangleright\Omega\to 0
in
{\mathcal{F}}(\eta)
. This is the case if and only if

E(x_{\lambda}^{*}x_{\lambda})=(\id\otimes\omega)({\mathbb{E}}(x_{\lambda}^{*}x_{\lambda}))\to 0.

The right hand side is exactly the
A
-valued inner product on
|L^{2}_{\omega}{\mathbb{D}}_{\eta}|
.
∎

Theorem 5.6
.

Suppose the conditional expectation
E:\Phi(\eta)\to A
is faithful. Then the inclusion
A\subset\Phi(\eta)
is an ind-inclusion if and only if
A\underset{\eta_{ii}}{\otimes}A\in{\sf Hilb}({\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A))
.

Proof.

From
4.4
, we deduce that
A\underset{\eta_{ii}}{\otimes}A\in{\sf Hilb}({\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A))
if and only if

A\underset{\eta_{ii}}{\otimes}A\simeq|{\mathsf{C^{*}Alg}}_{A{\hbox{-}}A}((-),A\underset{\eta_{ii}}{\otimes}A)|.

We also have that
A\underset{\eta_{ii}}{\otimes}A\simeq\overline{A\triangleright\xi_{i}\triangleleft A}^{\|\cdot\|_{A}}=\overline{A\triangleright(X_{i}\triangleright\Omega)\triangleleft A}^{\|\cdot\|_{A}}
. Thus, A
\underset{\eta_{ii}}{\otimes}A\in{\sf Hilb}({\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A))
if and only if the
A{\hbox{-}}A
-correspondence generated by
X_{i}\triangleright\Omega
is a Hilbert space object. A simple computation show that

\overline{A\triangleright X_{j}X_{i}\triangleright\Omega\triangleleft A}^{\|\cdot\|_{A}}\subset(A\underset{\eta_{jj}}{\otimes}A)\underset{A}{\boxtimes}(A\underset{\eta_{ii}}{\otimes}A)\oplus A.

Since realization is a unitary tensor functor (Proposition
4.4
), the right hand side of the above inclusion is a Hilbert space object, and therefore the left hand side also is. By iteration, we conclude that the
A{\hbox{-}}A
-correspondence generated by
P\triangleright\Omega
, where
P
is a polynomial in the semi-circular elements, is a Hilbert space object if and only if the
A\underset{\eta_{ii}}{\otimes}A
’s are Hilbert space objects. Passing to the limit, we have that
\overline{\Phi(\eta)\triangleright\Omega}^{\|\cdot\|_{A}}
is a Hilbert space object if and only if the
A\underset{\eta_{ii}}{\otimes}A
’s are.
∎

Corollary 5.7
.

Suppose the conditional expectation
E:\Phi(\eta)\to A
is faithful. Then the
A
-valued semi-circular system
A\subset\Phi(\eta)
is a ind-inclusion if and only if
\eta
can be written

\eta_{ij}(a)=\langle\xi_{i},a\triangleright\xi_{j}\rangle

for a family
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of vectors in an ind-object
H\in{\sf Hilb}({\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A))
, satisfying

\sum_{j\in I}\|\langle\xi_{i},a\triangleright\xi_{j}\rangle\|^{2}\leq C\|a\|^{2}\ \forall\ a\in A,\ \forall\ i\in I,

for some
C\geq 0
.

Example 5.8
.

Let
\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a countable collection of automorphisms on a unital C*-algebra
A
with faithful unique trace
\tau
, and consider the covariance matrix

\eta=\mathsf{diag}(\eta_{i}),

where
\eta_{i}=\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{i}^{-1}
.
Then, by direct computation we see that for every
i\in I
and all
x,y\in A

\tau(x\eta_{i}(y))=\tau(\eta_{i}(x)y),

fulfilling the hypotheses of
[
HPN24
, Theorem 5.2]
.
Then, the inclusion
A\subset\Phi(\eta)
has a faithful conditional expectation and is C*-discrete.

5.3
C*-algebraic factorization homology

Factorization homology, as developed in
[
AF15
]
, is a generalized homology theory for topological manifolds. It is a homology theory in the sense that it is functorial and satisfy an excision property, and is generalized in the sense that it assigns categories as invariants of manifolds. In
[
Hat23
]
, it was proven that as a target for factorization homology one can take the symmetric monoidal category
{\mathsf{C^{*}lin}}
, a certain category of C
∗
-categories, equipped with the max tensor product
\underset{\max}{\boxtimes}
as introduced in
[
AV20
]
. Our goal here is not to give a detailed account on that subject, but briefly explain how it can provide examples of non-connected C
∗
-algebra objects.

We will be interested in factorization homology for surfaces. A factorization homology theory with values in
({\mathsf{C^{*}lin}},\underset{\max}{\boxtimes})
is a monoidal functor
{\mathcal{F}}:{\mathsf{Surf}}\to{\mathsf{C^{*}lin}}
, where the source category is the category of surfaces with embeddings as morphisms and disjoint unions as monoidal structure. The functoriality of
{\mathcal{F}}
is expressed in 2-categorical terms: smooth oriented embeddings correspond to unitary functors between C*-tensor categories, and isotopies between embeddings correspond to unitary natural isomorphisms between unitary functors. This implies, in particular, that if
D
denotes the compact oriented disk, then

{\mathcal{F}}(D)=:{\mathcal{C}}

is a unitarily braided C*-tensor category. Also,
{\mathcal{F}}
must be functorial with respect to smooth oriented embeddings
\Sigma_{1}\to\Sigma_{2}
, and due to the co-completeness of
{\mathsf{C^{*}lin}}
(see
[
AV20
]
),
{\mathcal{F}}
is completely determined by
{\mathcal{C}}
. For this reason, we call
{\mathcal{F}}
the factorization homology with coefficients in
{\mathcal{C}}
, and the notation

{\mathcal{F}}(\Sigma)=:\int_{\Sigma}{\mathcal{C}}

is used.

The values of factorization homology are determined by universal properties, and can in principle be difficult to compute. But assuming that
{\mathcal{C}}
is a unitary tensor category equipped with a unitary braiding
\tau
, there are very concrete computations.

Theorem 5.9

(
[
Hat23
]
, Theorem 4.5)
.

Let
\Sigma
be an oriented compact surface with non-empty boundary. Each choice of boundary component
B\subset\Sigma
induces canonically a structure of cyclic right
{\mathcal{C}}
-module C*-category on
\int_{\Sigma}{\mathcal{C}}
. Thus, there is a C*-algebra object
{\mathbb{D}}_{\Sigma}\in{\sf Vec}({\mathcal{C}})
such that

\int_{\Sigma}{\mathcal{C}}\simeq{\mathbb{D}}_{\Sigma}{\hbox{-}}{\sf Mod}_{\mathcal{C}},

as cyclic right
{\mathcal{C}}
-module C*-categories.

Example 5.10
.

If
\Sigma=Ann
is the annulus, then

{\mathbb{D}}_{Ann}=\bigoplus_{X\in\Irr({\mathcal{C}})}^{c_{0}}\overline{X}\boxtimes X,

i.e., as a functor
{\mathcal{C}}^{\op}\to{\sf Vec}
,

{\mathbb{D}}_{Ann}(Y)=\bigoplus_{X\in\Irr({\mathcal{C}})}^{c_{0}}{\mathcal{C}}(Y,\overline{X}\boxtimes X).

The algebra structure of
{\mathbb{D}}_{Ann}
can be described as follows. It is determined by the morphisms

\overline{X}\boxtimes X\boxtimes\overline{Y}\boxtimes Y\overset{\tau_{\overline{X}\boxtimes X,\overline{Y}}\boxtimes\id_{Y}}{\longrightarrow}(\overline{Y}\boxtimes\overline{X})\boxtimes(X\boxtimes Y)\longrightarrow\overline{W}\boxtimes W,

for
X,Y,W\in\Irr({\mathcal{C}})
, where the last arrow is given by irreducible decomposition. Observe that the vector space dimension of
{\mathbb{D}}_{Ann}(\mathbbm{1})
is the cardinality of
\Irr({\mathcal{C}})
, so that
{\mathbb{D}}_{Ann}
is in general not connected nor locally finite. More generally, the C
∗
-algebra objects
{\mathbb{D}}_{\Sigma}
, for
\partial\Sigma\neq\emptyset
, can be described as sort of twisted tensor products of copies of
{\mathbb{D}}_{Ann}
.

For
\Sigma
, as above, with a fixed non empty boundary component
B\subset\partial\Sigma
, consider the associated mapping class group
\Gamma(\Sigma,B)
. It is the group of oriented self-diffeomorphisms of
\Sigma
fixing
B
pointwise, modulo isotopies.

Given now an action
{\mathcal{C}}\to{\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A)
on a unital C*-algebra
A
that has trivial center, we have the associated crossed products
A\rtimes{\mathbb{D}}_{\Sigma}
.

Theorem 5.11
.

There is a canonical action of
\Gamma(\Sigma,B)
on
A\rtimes{\mathbb{D}}_{\Sigma}
by
*
-automorphisms such that

A\subset(A\rtimes{\mathbb{D}}_{\Sigma})^{\Gamma(\Sigma,B)}.

Theorem
5.11
follows from
[
Hat23
, Proposition 5.13]
, and functoriality of the crossed products. It is analogous to
[
Hat23
, Corollary 5.14]
.

The conditional expectations
A\rtimes{\mathbb{D}}_{\Sigma}\to A
of interest would be the
\Gamma(\Sigma,B)
-invariant ones. Those correspond to
\Gamma(\Sigma,B)
-invariant states on
{\mathbb{D}}_{\Sigma}(\mathbbm{1})
. We do not know if they exist in general, but we have an example; for the annulus, the corresponding mapping class group is then isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Theorem 5.12

(
[
Hat23
]
, Theorem 5.15)
.

The projection

{\mathbb{D}}_{Ann}\simeq\bigoplus_{X\in\Irr({\mathcal{C}})}^{c_{0}}\overline{X}\boxtimes X\to\mathbbm{1}

induces a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-equivariant state on
{\mathbb{D}}_{Ann}(\mathbbm{1})
.

6
von Neumann vs C*-discreteness

The discrete property of a unital inclusion of operator algebras
(C\subset D,E)
have been formulated in terms of a quasi-regularity condition for both subfactors and inclusions of C*-algebras. Here,
E:D\to C
is a conditional expectation; ie a (normal) ucp map onto
C
. Namely, an inclusion is quasi-regular if certain intermediate
*
-algebra whose structure is inherited from a unitary tensor category (UTC) is dense in the appropriate sense. In this section, we develop the framework for which a direct comparison of these two notions—von Neumann-discreteness and C*-discreteness—imply one another. See
[
HPN23
]
and
[
JP19
]
for complete details about discrete inclusions.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a UTC,
A
be a unital C*-algebra, and
N
be a
\rm{II}_{1}
-factor with its unique trace
\tr.
Recall that an action of
{\mathcal{C}}
on
(N,\tr)
(or on
A
) is given by a unitary tensor functor
F^{\prime\prime}:N\to{\sf Bim_{bf}^{sp}}(N)
(or
F:{\mathcal{C}}\to{\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A)
).
Here,
{\sf Bim_{bf}^{sp}}(N)
are the finitely generated projective (
aka
bifinite)
N
-
N
bimodules which are
spherical
; ie whose left and right von Neumann dimensions match. (See
[
HHP20
, Example 2.10]
for a detailed description of
{\sf Bim_{bf}^{sp}}(N)
.) We say that
these actions are compatible
if and only if the following conditions hold:

(
{\mathcal{H}}
1)

There exists a fully faithful unitary tensor functor

{\mathcal{H}}:{\sf Bim_{fgp}}(A)\to{\sf Bim_{bf}^{sp}}(N)

such that

{\mathcal{H}}\circ F\overset{\eta}{\cong}F^{\prime\prime},

where
\eta
is some monoidal natural unitary isomorphism. (Later in this manuscript, we will often suppress
\eta
and assume it is the identity.) Here, the monoidality is with respect to
\{({\mathcal{H}}F)^{2}_{X,Y}:={\mathcal{H}}(F^{2}_{X,Y})\circ{\mathcal{H}}^{2}_{FX,FY}\}_{X,Y\in{\mathcal{C}}}
. Namely,

\eta_{X\otimes Y}\circ({\mathcal{H}}F)^{2}_{X,Y}=(F^{\prime\prime}_{X,Y})^{2}\circ(\eta_{X}\boxtimes\eta_{Y}).

(
{\mathcal{H}}
2)

There is a collection of contractive
A
-
A
bimodular injective maps

\{\Omega^{X}:F(X)\hookrightarrow\left(({\mathcal{H}}\circ F)(X)\right)^{\circ}\}_{X\in{\mathcal{C}}}

with
\Omega^{X}[F(X)]\subset({\mathcal{H}}\circ F)(X)
dense for every
X\in{\mathcal{C}}.

Here,
\left(({\mathcal{H}}\circ F)(X)\right)^{\circ}
denotes the dense subspace of left (equivalently right)
N

bounded vectors

\xi\in({\mathcal{H}}\circ F)(X)
, which are defined by the property that the map
N\ni n\mapsto n\rhd\xi\in({\mathcal{H}}\circ F)(X)
extends to a bounded map on
L^{2}(N,\tr)

[
Bis97
]
.

(
{\mathcal{H}}
3)

The family
\{\eta_{Z}\circ\Omega^{Z}:F(Z)\to F^{\prime\prime}(Z)^{\circ}\}_{Z\in{\mathcal{C}}}
of contractive
A
-
A
bimodular maps is compatible with the actions
F
and
F^{\prime\prime}
. This is, for all
X,Y\in{\mathcal{C}}
the following identity holds:

(F^{\prime\prime}_{X,Y})^{2}\circ(\eta_{X}\boxtimes\eta_{Y})\circ(\Omega^{X}\boxtimes\Omega^{Y})=\eta_{X\otimes Y}\circ\Omega^{X\otimes Y}\circ F^{2}_{X,Y},

the equality holding at the level of bounded vectors.

(
{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle\mapsto D^{\prime\prime}.

Here,
{\mathbb{P}}
is the connected W*/C* algebra object underlying
N\subseteq P
, and the commutants are taken within
\End(L^{2}(B,\varphi)).

Furthermore, these maps satisfy

\Phi\circ\Psi=\id.

Proof.

We shall systematically omit the maps
\Omega^{a}
and
\eta^{a}
from the definition of compatibility to ease notation and assume they are canonical inclusions.
Furthermore, the difference between taking double commutators in
L^{2}|{\mathbb{B}}|_{{\mathbb{F}}^{\prime\prime}}
or
L^{2}(B,\varphi)
is immaterial by Lemma
6.5
.

Notice that the underlying algebra object
{\mathbb{P}}
is well-defined by
[
JP19
, §7.3]
, which is moreover connected with
\mathbbm{1}\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}\subseteq{\mathbb{B}}
.
And so, the definition of
\Psi
is meaningful, giving a clearly injective map.

By compatibility of actions, for each
Z\in{\mathcal{C}}
the inclusion
F(Z)\subseteq F^{\prime\prime}(Z)^{\circ}
is dense.
Now, we claim

P=\overline{\oplus_{Z}F^{\prime\prime}(Z)^{\circ}\otimes{\mathbb{P}}(Z)}^{\sf{WOT}}\supset\overline{\oplus_{Z}F(Z)\otimes{\mathbb{P}}(Z)}^{\|\cdot\|_{\sf{op}}^{L^{2}(B,\varphi)}}=:D

is
WOT
-dense.
Otherwise, underlying
D^{\prime\prime}
there is a connected W*-algebra object
\mathbbm{1}\subseteq{\mathbb{D}}\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}
by the aforementioned Galois correspondence.
But if
{\mathbb{D}}\neq{\mathbb{P}}
it would contradict that
F(Z)\subset F^{\prime\prime}(Z)^{\circ}
is dense for all
Z\in{\mathcal{C}}.

Thus, the W*-algebra object
{\mathbb{P}}
is the same as the C*-algebra object
{\mathbb{D}}.

It follows that
\Phi\circ\Psi(P)=P
and also that
\Phi
is necessarily surjective.
∎

Remark 6.10
.

Weather
\Psi\circ\Phi=\id
is still unknown to us in this generality, as there might be intermediate C*-algebras
A\subseteq D\subseteq B
which are not C*-discrete.
However, these has promising progress in this direction, where reasonable restraints have been found on the underlying actions making the Galois correspondence tight; cf
[
Muk24
, §4]
.

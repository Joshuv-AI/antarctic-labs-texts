Predictors of 1-year fibrosis in treatment-naïve neovascular age-related macular degeneration: the role of baseline OCTA-derived MNV vessel density
Authors: Yi Lin, Xianggui Zhang, Zhen Huang, Ya Ye, Ming Yan, Yanping Song
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT

Background

Subretinal fibrosis is a common and vision-threatening end-stage sequela of neovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD) and represents a major cause of irreversible vision loss [1–4]. According to a recent epidemiological report, the prevalence of fibrosis in patients with incident nAMD was 13% at baseline and increased to 32%, 36%, and 56% at 12, 24, and 60 months, respectively [5]. Once fibrosis develops, permanent damage to the retinal pigment epithelium and photoreceptors may occur, leading to poor long-term visual outcomes [6]. Although intravitreal anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (anti-VEGF) therapy effectively suppresses exudative activity and macular neovascularization (MNV), it cannot fully prevent fibrotic scar formation [7–9]. As a result, fibrosis remains an important unmet challenge in the management of nAMD.

Early identification of eyes at high risk for fibrosis is therefore of considerable clinical importance. Previous studies have identified several baseline clinical and imaging features associated with fibrosis development in nAMD, including worse visual acuity, intraretinal fluid (IRF), retinal hemorrhage, subretinal hyperreflective material (SHRM), and type 2 macular neovascularization (MNV) [10–14]. These findings suggest that fibrosis is related, at least in part, to baseline lesion severity and phenotype. However, most of these predictors are derived from conventional clinical or structural imaging assessments, and quantitative biomarkers that more directly reflect the microvascular characteristics of MNV remain insufficiently studied [12].

Optical coherence tomography angiography (OCTA) enables noninvasive visualization of the neovascular microvascular network and allows quantitative assessment of vascular morphology [15, 16]. These parameters may provide complementary biomarkers beyond conventional structural OCT. However, evidence regarding OCTA-derived predictors of fibrosis in nAMD remains limited. The potential significance of early post-treatment OCTA changes, particularly after completion of the loading phase, has not been fully established.

Accordingly, this study aimed to compare OCTA quantitative parameters between eyes with and without fibrosis at baseline and at the post-loading visit, and to evaluate whether baseline clinical characteristics combined with OCTA-derived biomarkers could predict 1-year fibrosis in treatment-naïve nAMD. Baseline imaging was used to identify predictive markers present at diagnosis, whereas post-loading OCTA was included to explore early post-loading-phase vascular remodeling and short-term temporal changes in lesion phenotype. Through this combined cross-sectional and short-term follow-up assessment, we sought to identify imaging markers that may improve fibrosis risk stratification and support more individualized follow-up.

Methods

Study design and participants

This retrospective, single-center study enrolled treatment-naïve patients with neovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD) diagnosed between 2023 and 2025. The study was approved by the institutional ethics committee (Approval No. 2024015-01) and adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki.

Eligible eyes met the following criteria: active treatment-naïve nAMD with type 1 or type 2 macular neovascularization (MNV) confirmed by multimodal imaging; receipt of intravitreal anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (anti-VEGF) therapy using a 3 + PRN regimen; completion of the three-injection loading phase; follow-up of at least 12 months; and availability of high-quality OCTA images at both baseline and the post-loading visit. The post-loading visit was defined as an OCTA assessment performed within 1 week after completion of the three-injection anti-VEGF loading phase.

Eyes were excluded if they had fibrosis or scarring at baseline, polypoidal choroidal vasculopathy, high myopia, diabetic retinopathy, other retinal diseases that might interfere with image interpretation, or poor-quality OCTA images, defined as signal strength < 6/10 or severe artifacts. If both eyes of the same patient met the eligibility criteria, both eyes were included in the analysis.

A total of 91 patients (115 eyes) were screened. After exclusion of 6 patients (6 eyes) with baseline fibrosis, 9 patients (15 eyes) with follow-up < 12 months, and 4 patients (6 eyes) with unavailable high-quality OCTA images at baseline or at the post-loading visit or with severe image artifacts, 72 patients (88 eyes) were included in the final analysis.

Clinical and imaging evaluation

All patients underwent comprehensive ophthalmic examination, including best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA), color fundus photography, structural optical coherence tomography (OCT), fluorescein angiography (FFA), indocyanine green angiography (ICGA), and OCT angiography (OCTA). Baseline demographic and clinical data, including age, sex, and the number of anti-VEGF injections within 1 year, were obtained from the medical records.

Treatment-naïve active nAMD was diagnosed using multimodal imaging findings from color fundus photography, OCT, FFA, and ICGA. MNV type, pigment epithelial detachment (PED) type, subretinal fluid (SRF), intraretinal fluid (IRF), subretinal hyperreflective material (SHRM), and retinal hemorrhage were assessed primarily on OCT and fundus imaging. SHRM was defined as hyperreflective material located between the neurosensory retina and the retinal pigment epithelium on structural OCT. In this study, SHRM was assessed as present or absent and was not further differentiated into exudative and non-exudative components. All qualitative image assessments were independently evaluated by two readers, and disagreements were adjudicated by a senior retinal specialist.

Definition of fibrosis

The primary outcome was fibrosis at 1 year. Fibrosis was determined by multimodal imaging, including color fundus photography, OCT, FFA, and ICGA. An eye was considered fibrotic when multimodal imaging demonstrated a scar-like lesion, characterized by a relatively well-demarcated yellowish-white or grayish lesion on color fundus photography and dense, homogeneous, plaque-like subretinal hyperreflective material with relatively clear borders on OCT, often accompanied by retinal pigment epithelium alteration and outer retinal disruption. Angiographic findings on FFA/ICGA were used as supportive evidence when consistent with fibrotic scarring rather than predominantly active exudative leakage. OCTA acquisition and image processing

OCTA was performed using the BM-400 K BMizar system (TowardPi Medical Technology, Beijing, China). A 6 × 6 mm scan centered on the fovea was acquired at baseline and at the post-loading visit. Quantitative analysis was performed on en face MNV flow images generated from the avascular slab, which was defined by the device as the region extending from the outer plexiform layer to Bruch’s membrane. For each eye, the segmentation boundaries were reviewed on the corresponding B-scans and manually adjusted when necessary to ensure inclusion of the full MNV complex, including the sub-RPE component in type 1 MNV. Images with signal strength < 6/10, severe motion artifacts, or uncorrectable segmentation errors were excluded.

For each OCTA image, the MNV lesion was manually delineated as the MNV region of interest (MNV ROI), and all subsequent analyses were performed within the MNV ROI. Image preprocessing within the MNV ROI included contrast-limited adaptive histogram equalization (CLAHE), bilateral filtering for denoising while preserving edges, top-hat morphological transformation to enhance vascular structures, Otsu automatic thresholding for binarization, morphological opening and closing to remove small noise and reconnect fragmented vessels, and removal of connected components smaller than 30 pixels.

Quantitative OCTA parameters

The final binarized vascular image within the MNV ROI was used for parameter extraction. Fractal dimension (FrD) was calculated in ImageJ using the FracLac plug-in with the box-counting method. Briefly, the number of grid squares containing vascular pixels was repeatedly counted across multiple box sizes, and the slope of the linear fit between log N(r) and log (1/r) was taken as the fractal dimension, reflecting vascular structural complexity.

After skeletonization of the binarized image, branch points were defined as skeleton pixels with at least three neighboring skeleton pixels within a 3 × 3 neighborhood, with adjacent branch points merged to avoid duplicate counting. Endpoints were defined as skeleton pixels with one neighboring skeleton pixel. Segments were defined as the number of 8-connected components in the skeletonized image. MNV vessel density was calculated as the ratio of flow-positive vascular pixels to the total number of pixels within the MNV ROI.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range), as appropriate, and categorical variables as counts and percentages. Between-group comparisons were performed using Welch’s t test, the Mann–Whitney U test, the chi-square test, or Fisher’s exact test. Within-group comparisons of OCTA parameters between baseline and the post-loading visit were performed using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Univariate and multivariable logistic regression analyses were used to identify factors associated with fibrosis. ROC curves were used to evaluate the predictive performance of individual variables and the combined model for 1-year fibrosis. A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient enrollment and baseline characteristics

A total of 91 patients (115 eyes) with treatment-naïve nAMD were screened. After excluding 6 patients (6 eyes) with baseline fibrosis, 9 patients (15 eyes) with follow-up < 12 months, and 4 patients (6 eyes) with unavailable high-quality OCTA images at baseline or at the post-loading visit or with severe image artifacts, 72 patients (88 eyes) were included in the final analysis. Of these, 31 eyes developed fibrosis at 1 year and 57 eyes did not.

Baseline characteristics are summarized in Table 1. Compared with eyes without fibrosis, eyes that developed fibrosis had significantly worse baseline BCVA (logMAR) (0.82 [0.60, 1.26] vs. 0.52 [0.30, 0.70], P < 0.001), greater baseline CMT (344.00 [258.50, 498.00] µm vs. 267.00 [209.00, 382.00] µm, P = 0.019), and a higher prevalence of SHRM, retinal hemorrhage, type 2/mixed MNV, high PED, and IRF (all P < 0.05). No significant between-group differences were observed in age, sex, number of injections within 1 year, or SRF.

Univariate and multivariable logistic regression analyses for 1-year fibrosis

As shown in Table 3, univariate logistic regression identified several factors associated with 1-year fibrosis, including worse baseline BCVA, greater baseline CMT, SHRM, IRF, and higher baseline MNV vessel density. Retinal hemorrhage differed significantly between the fibrosis and non-fibrosis groups, but its odds ratio was not estimable in logistic regression because of complete separation. In addition, MNV type and PED type were significantly associated with fibrosis in the overall univariate comparison.

In the multivariable model, baseline BCVA (OR 1.162, 95% CI 1.024–1.319, P = 0.020), baseline CMT (OR 1.032, 95% CI 1.001–1.064, P = 0.043), and baseline MNV vessel density (OR 1.073, 95% CI 1.009–1.142, P = 0.025) remained independently associated with 1-year fibrosis, whereas SHRM was no longer significant (OR 2.850, 95% CI 0.770–10.547, P = 0.117). These findings suggest that worse baseline visual function, greater baseline macular thickness, and higher baseline MNV vessel density are independent predictors of fibrosis in treatment-naïve nAMD.

Predictive performance of the combined model

Figure 3 shows the ROC curves of baseline BCVA, baseline CMT, SHRM, baseline MNV vessel density, and the combined model for predicting 1-year fibrosis. The combined model achieved the best discriminative performance, with an AUC of 0.787 (95% CI 0.688–0.874), exceeding that of baseline BCVA (AUC 0.737, 95% CI 0.627–0.841), baseline CMT (AUC 0.653, 95% CI 0.523–0.765), SHRM (AUC 0.681, 95% CI 0.591–0.764), and baseline MNV vessel density (AUC 0.678, 95% CI 0.565–0.789). These results suggest that combining baseline clinical and OCTA-derived parameters may provide better discriminative performance for fibrosis risk stratification in treatment-naïve nAMD.

Discussion

In this retrospective study of treatment-naïve nAMD, eyes that developed fibrosis within 1 year showed worse baseline disease status and distinct OCTA microvascular characteristics. In the final multivariable model, baseline BCVA, baseline CMT, and baseline MNV vessel density were independently associated with 1-year fibrosis, whereas SHRM did not remain significant after adjustment. In addition, the combined model showed better discrimination than any single predictor alone. Overall, these findings support the concept that fibrosis is linked to baseline lesion severity and suggest that an OCTA-derived vascular biomarker may provide complementary information beyond conventional clinical and structural features [12, 13, 17, 18].

Our results regarding baseline clinical and structural factors are broadly consistent with previous studies. Prior work has identified worse baseline visual acuity, IRF, SHRM, retinal hemorrhage, and type 2 MNV as factors associated with subsequent fibrosis in nAMD [5, 12]. In our cohort, eyes that later developed fibrosis had worse baseline BCVA and a higher prevalence of SHRM, IRF, retinal hemorrhage, and type 2 or mixed MNV in univariate analyses. Worse baseline BCVA likely reflects more advanced lesion involvement and greater outer retinal dysfunction at presentation [19]. It may also indicate that the outer retina has already sustained more substantial injury, potentially predisposing the lesion to subsequent fibrotic scar formation [10]. Similarly, greater baseline CMT may indicate a higher exudative or structural burden, consistent with previous studies showing that thicker retina is associated with subsequent scar development [5]. Persistent retinal thickening may therefore be viewed as a marker of more active and structurally disruptive disease. In parallel, long-term cohort data have shown that scar formation is associated with poorer visual outcomes [13, 17].

A key finding of the present study is that baseline MNV vessel density emerged as an independent OCTA biomarker associated with fibrosis. Because this parameter was calculated within the manually delineated MNV ROI, it reflects the proportion of flow-positive vascular pixels within the neovascular lesion rather than retinal vascular density. Previous OCTA studies have suggested that eyes developing fibrosis tend to show larger lesion- or vessel-related metrics and more complex vascular morphology at baseline, although the specific quantitative parameters retained across studies have not been entirely consistent [12, 20, 21]. In this context, our finding that baseline MNV vessel density remained independently associated with fibrosis extends the current evidence by suggesting that vascular filling burden at presentation may have predictive value beyond conventional structural markers. Because MNV vessel density reflects the proportion of flow-positive vascular signal within the lesion, it may provide a quantitative estimate of the vascular burden of MNV that is not fully captured by structural OCT alone [22]. Biologically, a higher baseline MNV vessel density may reflect a more active or more heavily vascularized neovascular complex that is predisposed to subsequent fibrovascular transformation [23]. From a clinical perspective, higher baseline MNV vessel density may therefore represent an imaging sign of a lesion phenotype that already carries an increased risk of fibrosis at presentation and may warrant closer monitoring during follow-up.

Another notable observation is that between-group OCTA differences were more evident at the post-loading visit than at baseline. The post-loading visit is clinically relevant because it corresponds to the completion of the loading phase, when lesion response to initial anti-VEGF treatment is commonly reassessed and early vascular remodeling may already have occurred [24]. In our study, the fibrosis group showed significantly higher fractal dimension, branch points, endpoints, segments, and MNV vessel density than the non-fibrosis group at the post-loading visit, indicating that eyes at higher risk of fibrosis continued to exhibit a denser and more complex neovascular network even after initial treatment. However, within-group comparisons between baseline and the post-loading visit did not reveal statistically significant changes in either group. Taken together, these findings suggest that the post-loading visit OCTA parameters may provide supportive phenotypic information regarding early post-treatment lesion behavior, but that the principal predictive information in our cohort remained concentrated in baseline variables. Thus, the post-loading visit OCTA results should be interpreted as complementary rather than primary predictors of fibrosis.

SHRM warrants specific discussion. In our cohort, SHRM was significantly associated with fibrosis in the baseline comparison and in univariate regression, which is consistent with previous studies and reviews that have linked SHRM to fibrovascular tissue and scar formation in nAMD [5, 10, 13, 25, 26]. In the study by Liu et al., SHRM was also identified as a baseline risk factor in univariate analysis but did not remain independently significant in the multivariable model [12]. A similar pattern in our data suggests that SHRM may overlap with other baseline markers of lesion severity, particularly baseline CMT and OCTA-derived vascular burden, rather than acting as a fully independent predictor once these variables are considered together. This may reflect the fact that SHRM is itself a structural manifestation of more advanced lesion activity and tissue damage. Accordingly, when baseline CMT and OCTA-derived vascular burden are simultaneously included in the multivariable model, the independent contribution of SHRM may be attenuated. However, SHRM was assessed as a binary feature in the present study and was not further differentiated into exudative and non-exudative components, which may partly explain why its independent contribution was attenuated in the multivariable model.

From a clinical perspective, our findings highlight the potential value of integrating conventional clinical and structural markers with an OCTA-derived vascular biomarker for fibrosis risk assessment in treatment-naïve nAMD. In particular, the inclusion of baseline MNV vessel density in the final model represents a key strength of our study, as it suggests that OCTA may provide quantitative vascular information that is not fully captured by routine clinical examination or structural OCT alone. By combining BCVA and CMT, which reflect functional impairment and structural burden, with MNV vessel density, which reflects lesion vascular burden, our model offers a more comprehensive characterization of baseline disease status. Although the discriminative performance of the combined model was moderate, its AUC exceeded that of each individual predictor, supporting the concept that integrating clinical manifestations with imaging-derived vascular features may improve fibrosis risk stratification. Such an approach may help refine baseline risk stratification and support closer follow-up and more individualized prognostic counseling for eyes considered at higher risk.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this was a retrospective, single-center study with a relatively limited sample size, which may restrict the generalizability of the findings. Second, OCTA image processing involved manual delineation of the MNV lesion and several semi-automated preprocessing steps, which may introduce measurement variability. Third, although fibrosis was assessed using multimodal imaging and adjudicated by experienced readers, some degree of subjectivity in image interpretation is unavoidable. Future prospective, multicenter studies with larger cohorts are needed to confirm these findings and to determine whether baseline OCTA-based vascular biomarkers can be incorporated into clinically useful prediction tools. In addition, SHRM was assessed only as present or absent and was not further differentiated into exudative and non-exudative components, which may have limited the interpretation of its independent predictive value.

Conclusions

Eyes with treatment-naïve nAMD that developed fibrosis within 1 year showed worse baseline disease status and distinct OCTA microvascular characteristics. Baseline BCVA, baseline CMT, and baseline MNV vessel density were independently associated with fibrosis, and the combined model provided the best predictive performance. By integrating conventional clinical and structural features with an OCTA-derived vascular biomarker, our study suggests that baseline MNV vessel density may offer useful complementary information for fibrosis risk stratification in nAMD.

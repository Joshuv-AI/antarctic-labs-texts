1.
Introduction

1.1.
Historical Context

The study of the distribution of divisor functions in arithmetic progressions has a rich history, with significant breakthroughs occurring in recent decades.

Definition 1.1
.

For any
n,\ k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, the
k
-fold divisor function

d_{k}(n)
counts the number of ways to write
n
as a product of
k
positive integers,

d_{k}(n)\ :=\ \sum_{n_{1}n_{2}\dots n_{k}=n}1,

where each
n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
n
be a fixed positive integer and consider the sum of the
n
th powers of the primitive
q
th roots of unity. This sum is known as
Ramanujan’s sum
and is denoted by

c_{q}(n)\ :=\ \sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{m\ (q)}e\Big(\frac{mn}{q}\Big),

where
e(z):=e^{2\pi iz}
for any
z\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}\limits_{m\ (q)}
indicates that
m
runs over residues coprime to
q
.

It is known that this sum reduces to the Möbius function when
n
is coprime to
q
, and
c_{q}(n)=\mu(q)
.

Classical results by Selberg, Linnik, and Hooley (independently, 1950s, unpublished) established that the usual divisor function
d(n)
has an exponent of distribution
2/3
. For the ternary divisor function
d_{3}(n)
, early progress was much harder: Friedlander and Iwaniec
[
FI
]
achieved the first development beyond
1/2
, attaining an exponent of distribution
1/2+1/230
. This was subsequently improved to
1/2+1/82
by Heath-Brown
[
HB
]
, and to
1/2+1/46
by Fouvry, Kowalski, and Michel
[
FKM
]
(in the case of prime moduli).

A different line of development was the use of averaging over arithmetic progressions to extend the range of distribution. In 2005, Banks, Heath-Brown, and Shparlinski
[
BHS
]
showed that by averaging over all coprime residue classes modulo
q
, one can obtain a power-saving error term for divisor sums for the full range of moduli
q
. Notably, Nguyen
[
Ng
]
applied the Banks, Heath-Brown, Shparlinski method to the 3-fold divisor function and obtained an averaged level of distribution of
2/3
for
d_{3}(n)
. In concrete terms, Nguyen showed that when one averages over all coprime residue classes
a\ (\bmod\ q)
, the error term in the sum of
d_{3}(n)
up to
X
in the progression
n\equiv a\ (\bmod\ q)
exhibits cancellation for all moduli
q\ll X^{2/3-\varepsilon}
.This result matched the

2/3
distribution level on average, corresponding to the classical Selberg–Hooley bound in an averaged setting. Blomer
[
Bl
]
introduced a simplified, more analytic approach using the Voronoi summation formula, which streamlined the analysis of divisor sums in progressions.
Parry
[
Pa1
]
revisited the distribution of
d_{3}(n)
in arithmetic progressions and gave a proof same as Blomer that
d_{3}
has an exponent of distribution
2/3
on average.

Recently, for
k=4
, Parry
[
Pa2
]
used the Petrow-Young
[
PY
]
subconvexity bound for Dirichlet
L
-functions to show that
d_{4}(n)
has an exponent of distribution
4/7
when allowing an average over
a\ (\bmod q)
, for prime
q
.

1.2.
Statement of Main Results

In this paper, we push the averaged level of distribution for the ternary divisor function
d_{3}(n)
to
8/11
by using the Petrow–Young
[
PY
]
subconvexity bound. This work was motivated by a problem originally suggested to us by Parry.

We extend the result of Parry
[
Pa1
]
on
d_{3}(n)
, which has an exponent of distribution
2/3
, to
8/11
for primes by following the method of Parry in
[
Pa2
]
and we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

\displaystyle E_{a/q}(s)\

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{d_{3}(n)}{n^{s}}e\left(\frac{na}{q}\right),

\displaystyle f_{a/q}(x)\

\displaystyle=\ \underset{s=1}{\operatorname{Res}}\left\{\frac{E_{a/q}(s)\left((2x)^{s}-x^{s}\right)}{s}\right\},

\displaystyle\Delta(a/q)\

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{x\leq n\leq 2x}d_{3}(n)e\left(\frac{na}{q}\right)\ -\ f_{a/q}(x).

Then for a prime
q\leq x^{8/11}
and any
\varepsilon>0
,

\sum_{a=1}^{q}|\Delta(a/q)|^{2}\ \ll_{\varepsilon}\ x^{3/2+\varepsilon}q^{11/16}.

Let

\theta_{n}(q)=\frac{\mu(q/(q,n))}{\phi(q/(q,n))}.

It is straightforward to establish that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
d|n\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{d_{3}(n)\chi(n/d)}{n^{s}}

is holomorphic past
s=1
for non-principal Dirichlet characters so, after breaking the sum according to the residue
r\bmod q
, then according to
d=(r,q)
, and then using Dirichlet characters, we get

f_{a/q}(x)=\mathrm{Res}_{s=1}\left\{\frac{(2x)^{s}-x^{s}}{s}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{d_{3}(n)\theta_{n}(q/(q,a))}{n^{s}}\right\}=:F_{x}\left(\frac{q}{(q,a)}\right).

Then letting

\mathcal{M}_{x}(q,a)=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{d|q}c_{d}(a)F_{x}(d)\qquad\quad E_{x}(q,a)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x<n\leq 2x\\
n\equiv a(q)\end{subarray}}d_{3}(n)-\mathcal{M}_{x}(q,a)

we get

\sum_{N=1}^{q}\mathcal{M}_{x}(q,N)e\left(\frac{Nr}{q}\right)=F_{x}\left(\frac{q}{(q,r)}\right)

so

E_{x}(q,a)=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{r=1}^{q}e\left(-\frac{ar}{q}\right)\left(\sum_{x<n\leq 2x}d_{3}(n)e\left(\frac{nr}{q}\right)-\sum_{N=1}^{q}\mathcal{M}_{x}(q,N)e\left(\frac{Nr}{q}\right)\right)=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{b=1}^{q}e\left(-\frac{ar}{q}\right)\Delta(r/q)

and therefore

(1.1)

\sum_{a=1}^{q}|E_{x}(q,a)|^{2}=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{r=1}^{q}|\Delta(r/q)|^{2}.

Applying Cauchy-Schwarz to the above equality, which leads to the following theorem,

Theorem 1.2
.

For prime
q\leq x^{8/11}

(1.2)

\sum_{a=1}^{q}|E_{x}(q,a)|\ \ll_{\varepsilon}\ x^{3/4+\varepsilon}q^{11/32},

which shows equidistribution on average up to
q\leq x^{8/11}
.

In Section
2
we collect the auxiliary results needed for the proof of Theorem
1.1
. A key point is Lemma
6
, where we establish bounds for the third moment of Dirichlet
L
-functions using the Petrow–Young
[
PY
]
subconvexity result. These bounds are then applied in Lemmas
8
and
9
to obtain cancellation in sums involving Ramanujan sums, which are crucial in our analysis. Finally, in Section
3
, we combine these ingredients to complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The following Lemma concerns the bound for
A_{h/q}(n)
from Ivić-Voronoi formula.

Lemma 2
.

Let
A_{h/q}(n)
be as in Lemma
1
with prime
q
and
(h,q)=1
. If
q\mid n
, then

A_{h/q}(n)\;\ll_{\varepsilon}\;n^{\varepsilon}\,(q^{2},n).

for any
\varepsilon>0
. Moreover, if
q\nmid m
, then

\sum_{h=1}^{q-1}A_{h/q}(n)\,\overline{A_{h/q}(m)}\;\ll_{\varepsilon}\;(mn)^{\varepsilon}q(q^{2},n),

for any
\varepsilon>0
.

Proof.

By definition,

A_{h/q}(n)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{abc=n}R_{a,b,c}(h/q)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{abc=n}\sum_{x,y,z=1}^{q}e\left(\frac{ax+by+cz-hxyz}{q}\right).

Since
q\mid n
, at least one of
a,b,c
must be divisible by
q
. First, let’s assume that
q\mid c
. We have

R_{a,b,c}(h/q)=\sum_{x,y=1}^{q}e\left(\frac{ax+by}{q}\right)\sum_{z=1}^{q}e\left(\frac{(c-hxy)z}{q}\right).

If
c\not\equiv hxy\pmod{q}
, then it is well-known that the inner sum is
0
. So, the only case that matters is
xy\equiv c\bar{h}\equiv 0\pmod{q}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle R_{a,b,c}(h/q)=q\sum_{\underset{q\mid xy}{x,y=1}}^{q}e\left(\frac{ax+by}{q}\right)

\displaystyle=q\left(1+\sum_{x=1}^{q-1}e\left(\frac{ax}{q}\right)+\sum_{y=1}^{q-1}e\left(\frac{by}{q}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=q(1+c_{q}(a)+c_{q}(b))

\displaystyle=q(2+c_{q}(a)+c_{q}(b)+c_{q}(c)-q).

By the symmetry, the same formula holds whenever
q\mid a
or
q\mid b
, hence for all triples with
q\mid abc
. We have

R_{a,b,c}(h/q)=q(2+c_{q}(a)+c_{q}(b)+c_{q}(c)-q)=k_{a,b,c}q^{2}-(q^{2}+q)

where
q^{k_{a,b,c}}=(q,a)(q,b)(q,c)
, or equivalently,
k_{a,b,c}
equals the number of elements among
a,b,c
divisible by
q
. Therefore,

\displaystyle A_{h/q}(n)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{abc=n}R_{a,b,c}(h/q)

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{2}}{2}\sum_{abc=n}k_{a,b,c}-\frac{q^{2}+q}{2}\sum_{abc=n}1

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{2}}{2}\log_{q}\left[\prod_{abc=n}(q,a)(q,b)(q,c)\right]-\frac{q^{2}+q}{2}d_{3}(n).

Let
v_{q}(n)=\alpha\geq 1
and
n=q^{\alpha}\ell
. Then, we can change the variables as
(a,b,c)=(q^{x}a^{\prime},q^{y}b^{\prime},q^{z}c^{\prime})
where
(q,a^{\prime})=(q,b^{\prime})=(q,c^{\prime})=1
.

\displaystyle\prod_{abc=n}(q,a)(q,b)(q,c)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\underset{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}c^{\prime}=\ell}{x+y+z=\alpha}}(q,q^{x}a^{\prime})(q,q^{y}b^{\prime})(q,q^{z}c^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\prod_{\underset{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}c^{\prime}=\ell}{x+y+z=\alpha}}(q,q^{x})(q,q^{y})(q,q^{z})

\displaystyle=\left[\prod_{x+y+z=\alpha}(q,q^{x})(q,q^{y})(q,q^{z})\right]^{d_{3}(\ell)}

\displaystyle=q^{\frac{3\alpha(\alpha+1)d_{3}(\ell)}{2}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle A_{h/q}(n)

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{2}}{2}\log_{q}\left[\prod_{abc=n}(q,a)(q,b)(q,c)\right]-\frac{q^{2}+q}{2}d_{3}(n)

\displaystyle=\frac{3q^{2}\alpha(\alpha+1)d_{3}(\ell)}{4}-\frac{(q^{2}+q)d_{3}(n)}{2}.

Since
d_{3}
is multiplicative,
d_{3}(n)=d_{3}(\ell)d_{3}(q^{\alpha})=\frac{(\alpha+2)(\alpha+1)}{2}d_{3}(\ell)
.

\displaystyle A_{h/q}(n)

\displaystyle=\frac{3q^{2}\alpha d_{3}(n)}{2(\alpha+2)}-\frac{(q^{2}+q)d_{3}(n)}{2}

\displaystyle=d_{3}(n)\left[q^{2}\left(1-\frac{3}{\alpha+2}\right)-\frac{q}{2}\right]

\displaystyle=d_{3}(n)\cdot\begin{cases}-\frac{q}{2}&\text{if }\alpha=1,\\
C_{\alpha}q^{2}-\frac{q}{2}&\text{if }\alpha\geq 2,\end{cases}\quad\text{where }C_{\alpha}:=1-\frac{3}{\alpha+2}\neq 0\text{ and bounded}.

Notice that
A_{h/q}(n)
does not depend on
h
. Since
d_{3}(n)\ll_{\varepsilon}n^{\varepsilon}
for any
\varepsilon>0
,
A_{h/q}(n)\ll_{\varepsilon}n^{\varepsilon}(q^{2},n).
For the second bound, if we use
(q,m)=1
,

\displaystyle\overline{A_{h/q}(m)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{abc=m}\overline{R_{a,b,c}(h/q)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{abc=m}\sum_{x,y,z=1}^{q}e\left(\frac{hxyz-ax-by-cz}{q}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{abc=m}\sum_{x,y=1}^{q}e\left(\frac{-ax-by}{q}\right)\sum_{z=1}^{q}e\left(\frac{(hxy-c)z}{q}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{q}{2}\sum_{abc=m}\sum_{\underset{xy\equiv c\bar{h}\pmod{q}}{1\leq x,y\leq q}}e\left(\frac{-ax-by}{q}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{q}{2}\sum_{abc=m}\sum_{x=1}^{q-1}e\left(\frac{-ax-bc\overline{hx}}{q}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{q}{2}\sum_{abc=m}\sum_{u=1}^{q-1}e\left(\frac{-u-m\overline{hu}}{q}\right),

\displaystyle=\frac{qd_{3}(m)\overline{K(1,m\bar{h};q)}}{2},

where
K(n,m;q)=\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}\limits_{r=1}^{q}e\left(\frac{nr+m\bar{r}}{q}\right)
is Kloosterman’s sum. Therefore,

\sum_{h=1}^{q-1}A_{h/q}(n)\,\overline{A_{h/q}(m)}=\frac{qd_{3}(m)A_{1/q}(n)}{2}\sum_{h=1}^{q-1}\overline{K(1,m\bar{h};q)}

because
A_{h/q}(n)
does not depend on
h
. Now,

\sum_{h=1}^{q-1}\overline{K(1,m\bar{h};q)}=\sum_{h=1}^{q-1}\sum_{u=1}^{q-1}e\left(\frac{-u-m\overline{hu}}{q}\right)=\sum_{u=1}^{q-1}e\left(\frac{-u}{q}\right)\sum_{h=1}^{q-1}e\left(\frac{-m\overline{uh}}{q}\right)=-\sum_{u=1}^{q-1}e\left(\frac{-u}{q}\right)=1.

Therefore, we have

\sum_{h=1}^{q-1}A_{h/q}(n)\overline{A_{h/q}(m)}=\frac{qd_{3}(m)A_{1/q}(n)}{2}\ll_{\varepsilon}(mn)^{\varepsilon}q(q^{2},n).

∎

Hence, we can write this result as

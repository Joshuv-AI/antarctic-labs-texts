Analysis of unstable block by discrete element method during blasting excavation of fractured rock mass in underground mine
Authors: Zhongyuan Gu, Miaocong Cao
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractDuring the excavation process in fractured rock masses in underground mines, blasting excavation can cause dangerous block collapses on surrounding rock. The applicable blasting method can effectively reduce the damage of surrounding rock caused by blasting disturbance. In order to investigate the impact of blasting disturbances of dangerous blocks in fractured rock mass drift, this study focuses on the instability characteristics of dangerous blocks during the excavation process in a mine located in Jilin Province. Based on detailed detection of discontinuities characteristics in the study area, the damage effects of shaped charge hydraulic blasting and conventional blasting on surrounding rock structure are analyzed and compared by using equivalent static method. The 3DEC discrete element software was used to analyze the instability characteristics of surrounding rock dangerous blocks under these two different blasting methods. The results indicate that using shaped charge hydraulic blasting can effectively reduce the impact of blasting disturbances on the excavated rock mass and minimize dangerous block collapses during drift excavation.Keywords: Fractured rock mass, Blasting disturbance, Dangerous block, 3DEC

Introduction

The construction of major underground rock engineering projects, especially the excavation of underground metal mine drift, excavation of underground chambers, and construction of shafts, involves complex engineering geological issues [[1], [2], [3]]. The presence of discontinuities with different orientations and sizes within the rock mass is one of the main factors affecting the stability of underground mines [4]. The existence of discontinuities weakens the local rock mass, deteriorates its mechanical properties, and significantly reduces its stability [[5], [6], [7]]. Under stress disturbance conditions, the rock mass experiences compression and dynamic disturbances, leading to the expansion of discontinuities and the generation of new fractures. This results in the formation of a large number of rock blocks within the surrounding rock, triggering collapse and damage of unstable rock masses [[8], [9], [10], [11], [12], [13]].

The development of a precise model for predicting the collapse of hazardous rock blocks and conducting stability analysis has emerged as a pressing scientific and technological challenge in underground mining operations involving fractured rock formations [14]. In recent years, significant progress has been made in theoretical models, numerical calculations, and stability analysis related to the collapse hazards of underground rock masses. Through extensive research and field verification, it has been found that the main cause of instability in drift dangerous rock masses is the expansion of joint fractures in the rock mass due to blasting excavation. After excavation, the rock mass collapses under the action of its own gravity and other traction forces [[15], [16], [17], [18], [19], [20], [21], [22], [23]]. Therefore, reducing the disturbance on the surrounding rock during blasting excavation is an effective measure to mitigate the instability of drift dangerous rock masses [24].

Some scholars have conducted research on the impact of blasting disturbance during drift excavation, including the propagation laws of blasting vibration waves, blasting techniques, construction equipment and technology, etc [[25], [26], [27], [28]]. One technique, known as shaped charge hydraulic blasting (SCHB) [29], has received widespread attention. The use of SCHB in excavation can reduce the number of surrounding boreholes and the amount of explosive charge, resulting in less vibration in the surrounding rock mass and contributing to the stability of the rock mass after blasting. Currently, energy accumulation water pressure blasting technology is primarily used in drift construction, while there is limited research on its application in underground mining excavation. In fact, due to differences in processes, the cross-sections of underground mine tunnels are smaller than those of tunnels constructed for other purposes. However, there is a higher demand for excavation speed in underground mining (due to mineral production requirements), making it difficult to achieve timely support for every segment of the excavation. Therefore, the collapse of hazardous blocks within underground mine drift is characterized by smaller volume, higher frequency, and stronger instantaneous nature [30,31]. Conducting such research on the stability analysis of the drift surrounding rock in underground mines holds strong engineering practical significance.

For this, the study takes an underground mine in a certain location in Jilin Province in China as the case study. Based on the detailed detection of joint and fracture characteristics in the research area, it analyzes and compares the disturbance effects of SCHB and Conventional blasting methods on the surrounding rock structure, and explores the instability characteristics of hazardous rock blocks under these two different blasting methods.

Analysis of blasting vibration in tunnel shaped charge hydraulic blasting

Construction technology

Blasting disturbance is the main cause of hazardous block collapse in fractured rock tunneling [35]. The primary reason is the excessive density of blastholes and the complex loading structure in conventional blasting methods, which results in inefficient utilization of explosive energy. The concentrated energy formation in the explosive charge region greatly affects the stability of the surrounding rock, leading to cave-ins. At the same time, over excavation or under excavation phenomena are prone to occur, resulting in increased time and effort in subsequent construction and increased construction costs.

The design of slotting holes and auxiliary holes for SCHB follows the same principles as conventional blasting in terms of slotting form, blasthole layout, blasthole quantity and depth, initiation sequence, and time intervals. The main difference lies in the charging structure of peripheral holes (Fig. 3). SCHB effectively reduces the problem of energy concentration caused by conventional blasting in excavation, thereby reducing the disturbance of blasting vibration to surrounding rock and ensuring the safety.

Analysis of blasting vibration effect

By reducing the arrangement of surrounding boreholes and effectively concentrating the blasting energy, SCHB reduces the interference of blasting vibrations on the surrounding rock mass, thereby decreasing the likelihood of dangerous block collapses [29]. Construction of discrete block model

In order to further compare and analyze the disturbance effect of blasting vibrations on the surrounding rock between SCHB and conventional blasting, the 3DEC discrete element software is used to simulate the instability of hazardous blocks during drift excavation. The equivalent static acceleration of the surrounding rock particles caused by the two blasting methods is assigned to the simulation model of the study area. This allows for a comparison of the unstable characteristics of hazardous blocks during excavation using the two blasting methods. In the 3DEC discrete element software [39], the rock mass is treated as individual blocks of different shapes, with the contacts between blocks representing the fractures. This is known as the discrete block model [40]. The steps for conducting the simulation of hazardous block instability in tunneling using 3DEC are as follows:

In order to make the model calculation closer to the actual situation, the boundary conditions of the numerical model need to be set. Considering that the research focuses on the analysis of rock block failure in the region of block instability, the boundary condition of displacement 0 is set in the X, Y and Z axes of the model. In addition, the influence of ground stress is also considered in this study. The average buried depth of the drift under study is about 450 m, and ground stress load of 450 m buried depth is applied to the model, and the initial stress of the model is balanced.

Block instability criterion

During the process of excavation, dangerous blocks may be exposed in the surrounding rocks and undergo sudden falling and destruction through self-weight action. However, for some blocks that meet the mobility conditions, they do not suddenly become unstable, the interaction force between the block and the contact surface continuously weakens with mining disturbance until sliding failure occurs.

In order to distinguish different modes of instability for hazardous blocks, this study divides them based on block displacement (Fig. 6). Blocks with a displacement greater than 45 mm are defined as completely unstable blocks B1. These blocks experience sliding failure along the sliding plane during or after excavation. Blocks with a displacement between 25 mm and 45 mm are defined as delayed-type unstable blocks B2. These blocks have not undergone complete sliding failure but may further deteriorate under subsequent construction disturbances. Unstable blocks with displacements between 0 mm and 25 mm are defined as potential unstable blocks B3. These blocks have not experienced significant sliding and may transform into delayed-type unstable blocks B2 or completely unstable blocks B1 under subsequent construction disturbances, as shown in Table 4.

Numerical analysis result

Using the 3DEC software, two different blasting methods were employed to assess the instability characteristics of rock masses during excavation. The blasting effect was considered as a static mass directly imposed on the rock blocks (Fig. 7). It can be observed that when using the conventional blasting method for excavation, the face, sidewalls, and roof mostly consist of completely unstable block B1 (blue region) after excavation, while the remaining blocks are predominantly delayed unstable block B2 (green region). There is a potential risk of block collapse during subsequent construction. In contrast, when employing the SCHB for excavation, only partial complete unstable block B1 is present on the face and roof, while most of the blocks are classified as potentially unstable block B3 (yellow region). This indicates that the disturbance caused by SCHB is smaller, reducing the possibility of block collapse during excavation.

Numerical results.

From the statistical results of block quantities (Fig. 8(a)), it can be observed that compared to the conventional blasting method, when using the SCHB method for tunnel excavation, there are less completely unstable blocks B1 and delayed unstable blocks B2. This indicates that the SCHB method can effectively reduce the risk of rockfall in tunnel excavation, although the influence of different rock types and fragmentation degrees on the two blasting methods cannot be ruled out.

Block statistics.

From the statistical results of block volumes (Fig. 8(b)), when using the conventional blasting method and the SCHB, the maximum volume of potential unstable block B3 is smaller for the former. It can be considered that the conventional blasting method has a greater disturbance, leading to increased fracturing of the surrounding rock. The rate of delayed unstable blocks B2 is higher than that of the latter, indicating that the conventional blasting method causes greater disturbance to primary joint cracks, and the utilization of blasting energy is not concentrated, resulting in an increased possibility of unstable rock blocks in the surrounding rock.

Conclusion

(1)Detection of joint fractures was conducted using a 3D laser scanning instrument. Three dominant sets of fractures were identified, and Schmid tensor analysis was performed using DIPS software. The results indicate that the three dominant sets of fractures are prone to intersect with the walls, forming hazardous blocks that satisfy the conditions for movement. This provides a data foundation for the subsequent analysis of hazardous block formation in jointed rock masses in tunnels.

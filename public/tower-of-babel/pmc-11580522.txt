Characterization of Single-Cell Cis-regulatory Elements Informs Implications for Cell Differentiation
Authors: Ying-Ying Ren, Zhen Liu, George Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Abstract Cis-regulatory elements govern the specific patterns and dynamics of gene expression in cells during development, which are the fundamental mechanisms behind cell differentiation. However, the genomic characteristics of single-cell cis-regulatory elements closely linked to cell differentiation during development remain unclear. To explore this, we systematically analyzed ∼250,000 putative single-cell cis-regulatory elements obtained from snATAC-seq analysis of the developing mouse cerebellum. We found that over 80% of these single-cell cis-regulatory elements show pleiotropic effects, being active in 2 or more cell types. The pleiotropic degrees of proximal and distal single-cell cis-regulatory elements are positively correlated with the density and diversity of transcription factor binding motifs and GC content. There is a negative correlation between the pleiotropic degrees of single-cell cis-regulatory elements and their distances to the nearest transcription start sites, and proximal single-cell cis-regulatory elements display higher relevance strengths than distal ones. Furthermore, both proximal and distal single-cell cis-regulatory elements related to cell differentiation exhibit enhanced sequence-level evolutionary conservation, increased density and diversity of transcription factor binding motifs, elevated GC content, and greater distances from their nearest genes. Together, our findings reveal the general genomic characteristics of putative single-cell cis-regulatory elements and provide insights into the genomic and evolutionary mechanisms by which single-cell cis-regulatory elements regulate cell differentiation during development.Keywords: CRE, pleiotropy, cell differentiation, genomic characteristics

Significance

Although cis-regulatory elements (CREs) are well known to play crucial roles in cell differentiation, it remains elusive which genomic characteristics of CREs contribute to cell differentiation. Our systematical characterization of ∼250,000 putative single-cell CREs (scCREs) reveals that most scCREs are active in 2 or more cell types. The pleiotropic degrees of scCREs are positively correlated with the density and diversity of TF binding motifs and GC content, but negatively correlated with the distances to their nearest genes. scCREs related to cell differentiation show greater evolutionary conservation, higher density and diversity of TF binding motifs, and higher GC content and are located further from their potential target genes.

Introduction

Multicellular organisms consist of numerous cell types that share the same genomic sequence but differ in structure and function. The diversity in cell types is regulated by differences in the accessibility of cis-regulatory elements (CREs) during development, which control the spatiotemporal patterns of gene expression through interactions with transcription factors (TFs) (Spitz and Furlong 2012; Sahu et al. 2022). TFs recognize specific motifs within the CREs, forming unique combinations that determine the timing and location of target gene expression (Zinzen et al. 2009; Gasperini et al. 2019). As a result, CREs play a pivotal role in dynamically regulating gene expression during cell differentiation, which is indispensable for organogenesis and overall organismal development (Gorkin et al. 2020; Sarropoulos et al. 2021).

Several comprehensive studies, such as those conducted by the Encyclopedia of DNA Elements (ENCODE) consortium, have extensively profiled the epigenomes of various tissues, primary cells, and cell lines (Encode Project Consortium, Moore et al. 2020). Millions of CREs have been identified, significantly enhancing our understanding of their genomic characteristics. For example, growing evidence challenges the traditional view that CREs have strictly tissue-specific activities by suggesting their potential to be active in multiple tissues and developmental contexts, thereby serving diverse functions (Fuqua et al. 2020; Xin et al. 2020). This phenomenon is referred to as pleiotropy (Zhang 2023). Furthermore, comprehensive analyses have revealed the widespread presence of pleiotropic CREs in the human genome, which are associated with various crucial genomic features, including the density and diversity of TF binding motifs, GC content, and sequence conservation (Fish et al. 2017; Singh and Yi 2021; Laiker and Frankel 2022).

However, the catalogs of bulk CREs have 2 main limitations. First, these data sets were derived from bulk tissues or organs, which means that they predominantly represent the most abundant cell types and rare cell types may not be adequately captured in bulk epigenomic profiling (Calderon et al. 2019). Second, creating a comprehensive catalog of CREs through bulk epigenomic profiling is challenging due to the varying activity patterns of CREs across different cell types during various developmental stages or physiological states (Preissl et al. 2023). Fortunately, the emergence of single-cell epigenomic techniques, such as single-cell assay for transposase-accessible chromatin using sequencing (scATAC-seq), offers a viable solution to overcome these limitations. These advanced methodologies facilitate the generation of a more comprehensive collection of potential CREs at the single-cell level, providing deeper insights into the variations and dynamics of CREs (Satpathy et al. 2019; Xu et al. 2021). Despite the rapid accumulation of putative single-cell CRE (scCRE) data, our understanding of their pleiotropy and genomic features remains limited. Additionally, the genomic characteristics of scCREs with varying degrees of pleiotropy and their effects on cell differentiation during development are poorly understood.

To address these questions, we conducted a systematic analysis of ∼250,000 putative scCREs across 12 distinct cell types in the developing mouse cerebellum. Our findings revealed that most scCREs are active in 2 or more cell types, indicating the widespread presence of pleiotropic effects in scCREs. We further observed positive correlations between the degrees of pleiotropy and several genomic features in proximal and distal scCREs, including the density and diversity of TF binding motifs and GC content. Importantly, scCREs associated with cell differentiation in both proximal and distal regions exhibited more TF binding motifs, increased GC content, and greater sequence conservation. Our findings illuminate previously unknown genomic characteristics of putative scCREs and establish a general connection between these characteristics and their potential roles in cell differentiation, providing insights into the regulatory mechanisms of scCREs involved in cell differentiation during development.

Results

Most scCREs Display Pleiotropic Effects

To investigate the pleiotropy and genomic characteristics of scCREs, we gathered high-quality data on 249,674 putative scCREs from the developing mouse cerebellum, covering 11 developmental stages and 12 distinct cell types (supplementary fig. S1, Supplementary Material online) (Sarropoulos et al. 2021). The pleiotropic degree of scCREs was determined by assessing the number of cell types in which a scCRE is active, following established principles outlined in previous studies (Singh and Yi 2021; Laiker and Frankel 2022). The pleiotropic degree of a scCRE indicates its activity in multiple cell types, ranging from 1 to 12 in our data set, which includes a total of 12 cell types. A pleiotropic degree of 1 signifies specificity to a particular cell type, while a degree of 12 suggests activity across all cell types in the data set. Based on this definition, we found that ∼20% of the putative scCREs were specific to individual cell types, while the remaining ∼80% exhibited pleiotropy (Fig. 1a), indicating activity in at least 2 cell types. Furthermore, we observed a trend in the distribution of scCRE numbers based on their pleiotropic degrees, initially decreasing and then increasing as the degree of pleiotropy increased (Fig. 1a).

Due to distinct biological functions primarily as promoters or enhancers based on distances from the transcription start site (TSS), we classified the putative scCREs into proximal elements, which were within the 3 kb region upstream and downstream of the TSS and covered most promoter regions (Goni et al. 2007; Lake et al. 2023; Li et al. 2023), and distal elements, which were located outside this region. The proportions of proximal scCREs increased with higher pleiotropic degrees (Fig. 1b), indicating that more proximal scCREs exhibited activity in multiple cell types than distal scCREs. Furthermore, we observed a negative correlation between the pleiotropic degrees of proximal scCREs and the distances from their nearest TSSs (ρ = −0.48, P < 2.2 × 10−16; Fig. 1c). By contrast, the negative correlation between the pleiotropic degrees of distal scCREs and the distances from their nearest TSSs was weaker, although still statistically significant (ρ = −0.03, P < 2.2 × 10−16; Fig. 1d). Proximal scCREs are more likely to function as promoters, whereas distal scCREs are more likely to function as enhancers. This discrepancy in the correlation between pleiotropic degrees and distances to the nearest TSSs suggests that promoter-like scCREs closer to TSSs are more likely to exhibit functional activity across multiple cell types compared to enhancer-like scCREs.

The Pleiotropic Degree of scCREs Is Positively Correlated with the Abundance and Diversity of TF Binding Motifs, as well as with GC Content

The regulatory function of CREs is primarily attributed to the complex nature of TF binding motifs within these elements. The combinatorial binding of TFs to CREs allows for precise spatiotemporal regulation of gene expression, forming the foundation underlying gene regulatory networks (Davidson 2010). To evaluate the complexity of TF binding motifs in scCREs, we scanned proximal and distal scCREs using the JASPAR database (Castro-Mondragon et al. 2022) and found that, on average, each proximal scCRE contained a significantly larger number of total TF binding motifs compared to distal scCREs (P < 2.2 × 10−16, Mann–Whitney U test; Fig. 2a). We observed positive correlations between the numbers of total TF binding motifs and the pleiotropic degrees for both proximal and distal scCREs (ρ = 0.30, P < 2.2 × 10−16; ρ = 0.14, P < 2.2 × 10−16; Fig. 2b). Additionally, we found that each proximal scCRE contained an average of 108 unique TF binding motifs, which was significantly larger than the number observed in distal scCREs (P < 2.2 × 10−16, Mann–Whitney U test; Fig. 2c). Similarly, the numbers of unique TF binding motifs showed positive correlations with the pleiotropic degrees for both proximal and distal scCREs (ρ = 0.20, P < 2.2 × 10−16; ρ = 0.13, P < 2.2 × 10−16; Fig. 2d). Consistent findings were obtained when we performed the same analyses using different databases of TF binding motifs, including CIS-BP (Weirauch et al. 2014), HOCOMOCO (Kulakovskiy et al. 2018), and UniPROBE (Hume et al. 2015) (supplementary fig. S2, Supplementary Material online).

In addition to the density and diversity of TF binding motifs, previous studies have highlighted a strong association between the regulatory activity of bulk CREs and their GC content. High GC content has been shown to be an indicative factor for CRE activity (Erwin et al. 2014; Chen et al. 2018). Therefore, we examined the correlation between GC content and the pleiotropic degree of proximal and distal scCREs. After calculating and summarizing the GC content for each scCRE, we found that proximal scCREs had a significantly higher average GC content of 52% compared to distal scCREs (P < 2.2 × 10−16, Mann–Whitney U test; Fig. 2e). Furthermore, significant positive correlations were found between GC contents and the pleiotropic degrees of both proximal and distal scCREs (ρ = 0.48, P < 2.2 × 10−16; ρ = 0.21, P < 2.2 × 10−16; Fig. 2f). These results suggest that both proximal and distal scCREs with higher pleiotropic degrees may exhibit enhanced regulatory activity compared to those with lower pleiotropic degrees.

scCREs Exhibit a Bimodal Distribution of Evolutionary Conservation

Several studies have shown that mutations occurring in CREs can have functional impacts on morphology, physiology, and behavior (Drapeau et al. 2006; Jeong et al. 2006). These mutations may lead to more phenotypic changes than those affecting protein-coding sequences, suggesting that CREs are subject to evolutionary constraints (Wray 2007). To evaluate the impact of evolutionary constraints on scCREs, we obtained phastCons scores for proximal and distal scCREs (Sarropoulos et al. 2021). Overall, we found positive correlations between the phastCons scores and the pleiotropic degrees for both proximal and distal scCREs (ρ = 0.18, P < 2.2 × 10−16; ρ = 0.15, P < 2.2 × 10−16; Fig. 3a). These results suggest that highly pleiotropic scCREs tend to exhibit greater evolutionary conservation. Interestingly, we identified a consistent bimodal distribution in the phastCons scores of proximal and distal scCREs across different pleiotropic degrees (Fig. 3b). This bimodal distribution is likely caused by different evolutionary constraints acting on both proximal and distal scCREs. Some scCREs may have minimal functional roles and have evolved faster than others, while some may have important regulatory roles and thus have experienced stronger evolutionary constraints. Additionally, the evolutionarily nonconserved peaks were more prominent than the evolutionarily conserved peaks, while the evolutionarily conserved peaks appeared sharper than the evolutionarily nonconserved peaks. This suggests that the number of scCREs that evolved faster was larger than that of scCREs under stronger evolutionary constraints.

To further evaluate the selection pressure acting on the 2 kinds of peaks, we conducted a simulation analysis using nucleic acid fragments equivalent in length to scCREs. These fragments consisted of 4-fold degenerate sites (4Dsites), which are commonly regarded as neutral because substitutions at these sites do not affect amino acids (Nei and Kumar 2000). We calculated the average phyloP scores for these fragments, as well as for conserved and nonconserved scCREs. The average phyloP scores of the 4Dsite fragments and nonconserved scCREs were predominately clustered around 0, indicating that nonconserved scCREs were largely unaffected by selection pressure. In contrast, conserved scCREs exhibited positive average phyloP scores (Fig. 3c), suggesting that they were subject to purifying selection. These findings were consistent for both proximal and distal scCREs, implying that conserved scCREs may play more significant biological functions compared to nonconserved ones.

Next, we investigated the associations between evolutionary conservation and genomic characteristics of scCREs. Specifically, we focused on the conserved proximal scCREs with phastCons scores ranging from 0.645 to 1.00 (peak value ± standard deviation [SD]) and the nonconserved proximal scCREs with phastCons scores ranging from 0 to 0.408. Similarly, we examined the conserved distal scCREs with phastCons scores ranging from 0.646 to 1.00 and the nonconserved distal scCREs with phastCons scores ranging from 0 to 0.393. Comparatively, the conserved scCREs exhibited significantly higher abundance and diversity of TF binding motifs compared to the nonconserved scCREs across various pleiotropic degrees for both proximal and distal scCREs (P < 0.05, Mann–Whitney U tests; Fig. 4a and b). Notably, we found a consistent elevation in GC contents within conserved scCREs compared to nonconserved scCREs across different pleiotropic degrees for proximal scCREs (P < 0.0001, Mann–Whitney U tests; Fig. 4c). By contrast, the GC contents were lower in conserved distal scCREs than in nonconserved ones when the pleiotropic degrees were less than 9; however, this trend reversed for pleiotropic degrees greater than 10 (P < 0.0001, Mann–Whitney U tests; Fig. 4c). In addition, the distances from the nearest TSSs were significantly larger for nonconserved proximal scCREs than for conserved ones across various pleiotropic degrees (P < 0.05, Mann–Whitney U tests; Fig. 4d). Notably, the distances from the nearest TSSs were smaller for nonconserved distal scCREs than for conserved ones across different levels of pleiotropy (P < 0.0001, Mann–Whitney U tests; Fig. 4d). This contrasting result between proximal and distal scCREs may largely be attributed to their distinct functional roles. Proximal scCREs typically act in a promoter-like capacity. Their closer proximity to TSSs and stronger evolutionary constraints may enhance gene transcription stability and efficiency. In contrast, distal scCREs primarily function as enhancers, engaging in complex 3-dimensional interactions with target genes. The greater distance from TSSs and weaker evolutionary constraints may allow distal scCREs to respond rapidly and flexibly to the dynamic demands of biological activities. Collectively, these findings suggest that, in addition to pleiotropic degrees, the evolutionary constraint on scCREs may also be closely associated with their genomic characteristics, including the density and diversity of TF binding motifs, GC content, and proximity to neighboring genes.

Evolutionarily Conserved scCREs are More Likely to Regulate Genes Associated with Cell Differentiation

To investigate the correlation between the evolutionary conservation of scCREs and their biological significance, we performed functional enrichment analysis for the nearest genes associated with conserved and nonconserved scCREs. It is worth noting that although the nearest genes may not always be targets for CREs, there is a higher likelihood for CREs to regulate nearby genes (Shen et al. 2012; Rickels and Shilatifard 2018; Gasperini et al. 2019). Therefore, the analysis results are valuable in highlighting the biological significance of the evolutionary conservation of scCREs. In comparison, we observed that conserved scCREs across different pleiotropic degrees were significantly associated with genes enriched in Gene Ontology (GO) terms related to cell differentiation compared to nonconserved scCREs, regardless of their proximity to the TSSs (supplementary fig. S3, Supplementary Material online). These findings suggest that conserved scCREs have a greater tendency to regulate genes involved in cell differentiation than nonconserved scCREs.

To further examine the association between the evolutionary conservation of scCREs and cell differentiation, we curated 2,713 genes associated with cell differentiation from the GO database (supplementary table S1, Supplementary Material online) and retrieved their corresponding scCREs. To ensure a fair comparison, we randomly selected 2,713 genes unrelated to cell differentiation and identified their respective scCREs. This process was repeated 1,000 times. Upon analysis, we observed that both proximal and distal scCREs adjacent to genes involved in cell differentiation exhibited significantly higher levels of evolutionary conservation compared to the scCREs near genes not associated with cell differentiation (P < 0.001, permutation test; Fig. 5a). These findings provide further evidence for stronger evolutionary constraints on the scCREs related to cell differentiation.

Genomic Features of scCREs Associated with Cell Differentiation

To further investigate the genomic features of scCREs associated with cell differentiation, we collected 2,984 cell stemness maintenance genes (supplementary table S2, Supplementary Material online) from the StemChecker database (Pinto et al. 2015) and 3,149 housekeeping genes (supplementary table S3, Supplementary Material online) from the HRT Atlas database (Hounkpe et al. 2021). Subsequently, we identified the scCRE for each gene. Our analysis revealed that both proximal and distal scCREs associated with cell differentiation exhibited significantly larger numbers of total and unique TF binding motifs compared to those associated with cell stemness maintenance and housekeeping functions (P < 0.0001, Mann–Whitney U tests; Fig. 5b). Moreover, the levels of GC content were significantly higher in both proximal and distal scCREs related to cell differentiation than those related to cell stemness maintenance and housekeeping functions (P < 0.0001, Mann–Whitney U tests; Fig. 5b). Additionally, we observed that the distances to TSSs were significantly greater for both proximal and distal scCREs related to cell differentiation compared to those related to the cell stemness maintenance and housekeeping functions (P < 0.0001, Mann–Whitney U tests; Fig. 5b).

To confirm these results, we randomly selected an equal number of genes unrelated to cell differentiation as the number of cell differentiation genes. Subsequently, we identified their corresponding scCREs. This process was repeated 1,000 times. Similarly, our analysis revealed that both proximal and distal scCREs related to cell differentiation exhibited larger numbers of total and unique TF binding motifs, increased GC content, and were located at a greater distance from TSSs compared to scCREs unrelated to cell differentiation (Fig. 5c). We also applied similar analyses for scCREs involved in cell stemness maintenance and housekeeping functions, respectively. Interestingly, the results showed contrasting patterns for proximal and distal scCREs related to cell stemness maintenance or housekeeping functions compared with scCREs related to cell differentiation (Fig. 5c).

Discussion

In this study, we investigated the pleiotropy and genomic characteristics of 250,000 putative scCREs derived from 12 different cell types within the developing mouse cerebellum. Our findings revealed that over 80% of these scCREs exhibit activity in 2 or more cell types, highlighting the prevalence of pleiotropic scCREs. Notably, certain cell types demonstrate closer developmental relationships with each other than with others. While this phenomenon may influence the proportions of scCREs among cell types with varying developmental proximities, it does not introduce bias in comparing genomic features across degrees of pleiotropy. For example, it is acknowledged that GABAergic interneurons exhibit a closer developmental relationship with astroglia compared to Purkinje cells within the mouse cerebellum (Vladoiu et al. 2019). Quantitative analyses of various genomic features, such as the number of total motifs, the number of unique motifs, GC content, phastCons score, and distance to TSS in scCREs shared between GABAergic interneurons and astroglia, did not show significant differences when compared to those shared between GABAergic interneurons and Purkinje cells or between astroglia and Purkinje cells (supplementary fig. S4, Supplementary Material online).

Despite the difference in the definition of pleiotropy, our study revealed consistent correlations between pleiotropic degrees of and various genomic features in both scCREs and bulk CREs, including positive correlations with the abundance and diversity of TF binding motifs, GC content, and evolutionary conservation (Villar et al. 2015; Singh and Yi 2021). When a CRE functions across multiple contexts, it is expected to fulfill diverse regulatory roles. Consequently, such a CRE likely undergoes stronger evolutionary pressures on its sequences, potentially resulting in the accumulation of a dense and diverse array of TF binding motifs to ensure effective regulatory roles on its targets (Rebeiz et al. 2015). The consistency of these observations across different resolutions of CREs suggests that our findings capture reliable genomic features of scCREs that are pertinent to their regulatory functions during developmental processes. This implies that these genomic features may apply to other contexts, even though they were derived from a single data set of scCREs from the developing mouse cerebellum. Notably, we observed an increase in the number of scCREs at higher levels of pleiotropy (Fig. 1a), which contrasts with previous findings from bulk CREs across tissues (Singh and Yi 2021). Bulk CREs represent the averaged gene regulatory activities across various cell types within a tissue, likely reflecting the specific function of that tissue. As a result, the numbers of bulk CREs consistently decrease as pleiotropic degrees increase. By contrast, scCREs capture gene regulatory activities for specific cell types, probably more accurately reflecting the specific function of the cell type and their regulatory roles in fundamental cellular activities. The increased number of scCREs at higher levels of pleiotropy may be associated with these fundamental cellular activities shared among different cell types. Indeed, the genes nearest to the scCREs with the highest pleiotropic degree of 12 were enriched in fundamental functional categories, such as histone modification, cell cycle, and transcription (supplementary fig. S5, Supplementary Material online).

In addition to elucidating the general genomic characteristics of scCREs, we also focused on unraveling the genomic features of scCREs associated with cell differentiation during development. The pleiotropic degrees of these scCREs related to cell differentiation ranged from 1 to 12. Notably, among this subset of scCREs, the cell type-specific scCREs (pleiotropic degree: 1) exhibited the highest proportions across various degrees of pleiotropy (Fig. 6a) and a significantly higher proportion compared to other cell type-specific scCREs unrelated to cell differentiation, such as those involved in cell stemness maintenance and fundamental functions (P < 0.001, χ2 tests; Fig. 6a). These results suggest that cell type-specific scCREs may play more crucial regulatory roles in cell differentiation during development compared to other pleiotropic scCREs. It is worth noting that the prevalence of scCREs functioning in all 12 cell types was particularly prominent in housekeeping functions, as opposed to those associated with cell differentiation and cell stemness maintenance (Fig. 6a). This observation aligns well with the fundamental roles of housekeeping genes, which are indispensable for various cellular activities shared by diverse cell types. Moreover, our findings indicate that scCREs related to cell differentiation showed a higher abundance and diversity of TF binding motifs, increased GC content, and are subject to stronger evolutionary constraints (Fig. 6b). However, they displayed lower degrees of pleiotropy. Although these observations seem to contradict the generally positive correlation between pleiotropic degrees and the density and diversity of TF binding motifs, as well as GC content, across all scCREs examined (Fig. 2), they suggest that scCREs related to cell differentiation have enhanced gene regulatory activities and greater cell type specificity compared to other scCREs during development. The evolutionary conservation analyses showed that the scCREs related to cell differentiation underwent stronger evolutionary pressures on its sequences, which may drive the accumulation of a dense and diverse array of TF binding motifs and the increased GC content to ensure effective regulatory roles on its targets. Additionally, we examined the number of scCREs per cell differentiation gene. We would expect a larger number of scCREs involved in cell differentiation genes because the presence of multiple enhancers proximal to the same gene enhances functional and phenotypic robustness (Osterwalder et al. 2018). Indeed, the median count of scCREs per cell differentiation gene was estimated to be 9, which was significantly larger than the median counts of scCREs per gene related to cell stemness maintenance and housekeeping functions, respectively (P < 0.001; Mann–Whitney U tests; Fig. 6c).

The emergence of single-cell epigenomic profiling has greatly improved our understanding of scCREs involved in cell differentiation during development, covering a wide range of both common and rare cell types across various tissues and species. Recently, an increasing number of data sets have been developed, revealing distinct scCRE profiles within predominant cell types in various tissues such as brain (Liu et al. 2021), blood (Satpathy et al. 2019), lung (Duong et al. 2022), and others (Muto et al. 2021). These data sets allow for systematic explorations of the collection of scCREs and the transcriptional regulatory mechanisms that control target genes crucial for orchestrating cellular state transitions during development. Building on this foundation, our study provides new and valuable insights into understanding the regulatory framework and genomic characteristics of scCREs involved in the developmental emergence and diversification of diverse cell types.

Materials and Methods

The Data Sets of Putative scCREs and Their Reliability

The data sets of putative scCREs from the developing mouse cerebellum were obtained from Sarropoulos et al. (2021). These data sets included 249,674 scCREs, each 500 bp in length, derived from snATAC-seq analysis of ∼90,000 cells representing 12 primary cell types across 11 developmental stages. Sarropoulos et al. implemented necessary controls and filters to minimize the noise in the snATAC-seq data and reduce false-positive scCREs. They retained cells with high sequencing depth (unique fragments > 5,000), preserved ATAC-seq peaks with high signal-to-background ratios (TSS enrichment score ≥ 3), and eliminated doublets. Additionally, they ensured that each identified ATAC-seq peak was validated by at least 2 biological samples and exhibited activity in at least 5% of the cells within a cluster. Finally, they evaluated the reliability of their putative scCREs by cross-referencing them with previously identified CREs from both bulk and single-cell sequencing methodologies. These comparisons encompassed data sets such as ENCODE chromHMM (Gorkin et al. 2020), experimentally validated hindbrain enhancers (Visel et al. 2007), transcribed enhancers across a diverse range of mouse samples (doi:10.5281/zenodo.1411211), and a single-cell atlas of chromatin accessibility in adult mouse organs (Cusanovich et al. 2018). Their scCREs consistently showed an overlapping ratio exceeding 80% with these data sets. Furthermore, we also compared these putative scCREs with putative CREs from the ENCODE project (https://www.encodeproject.org/), which include promoter-like signature (PLS), proximal enhancer-like signature (pELS), and distal enhancer-like signature (dELS). The observed overlapping ratios were 82.8%, 67.2%, and 42.2% for PLS, pELS, and dELS. Additionally, we calculated the overlapping ratios of these scCREs with H3K27ac and H3K4me3 data obtained from 25 mouse tissues in the ENCODE database, which consistently exceeded 50%. Notably, the overlapping ratios reached 85.6% and 83.2% for the H3K4me3 and H3K27ac data of the mouse cerebellum, respectively. It was observed that nearly half of the transcribed bidirectional regions in FANTOM5 (https://fantom.gsc.riken.jp/5/) overlapped with these putative scCREs (supplementary table S4, Supplementary Material online). Based on these reliable putative scCREs, Sarropoulos et al. (2021) defined a total of 12 distinct cell types during mouse cerebellum development by applying Louvain clustering on snATAC-seq data and using gene scores as a proxy for gene expression to assign clusters to potential cell types based on previously identified marker genes, along with manual examination of in situ hybridization data from the Allen Developing Mouse Brain Atlas resource.

Annotation of scCREs and the Definition of Their Pleiotropic Degrees

We categorized these putative scCREs based on their presence across the 12 cell types using the BEDtools software (Quinlan and Hall 2010). The degree of pleiotropy of a scCRE was determined by the number of cell types in which it was active. For the annotation of putative scCREs, we used the ChIPseeker R package (Wang et al. 2022), which provide details such as the distance between a scCRE and the TSS of its nearest gene, potential target genes, and the chromosomal positioning. The promoter region was defined as a 3 kb region upstream and downstream of the TSS (Goni et al. 2007; Lake et al. 2023; Li et al. 2023). Based on their relative location to this region, putative scCREs were categorized into proximal and distal elements.

Identification of Genomic Features of scCREs

We used the FIMO software (Grant et al. 2011) with default settings to identify TF binding motifs within scCREs based on the following databases: nonredundant JASPAR (Castro-Mondragon et al. 2022), CIS-BP (Weirauch et al. 2014), HOCOMOCO (Kulakovskiy et al. 2018), and UniPROBE (Hume et al. 2015). Additionally, the GC content for each scCRE was determined using the SeqKit tool (Shen et al. 2016).

Identification of Conserved and Nonconserved scCREs

We computed the peak values and corresponding SDs of phastCons scores for both proximal and distal scCREs. We then extracted representative conserved and nonconserved scCREs whose phastCons scores were within the range of the peak value ± SD for further analyses. Specifically, based on the density distribution of conservation among all proximal scCREs and all distal scCREs, the SD of conservation among all proximal scCREs was determined to be 0.330, with double-peak values at 0.078 and 0.975, respectively. The selection range for nonconserved scCREs in the proximal region was 0.078 ± 0.330, ranging from [0, 0.408). The selection range for conserved scCREs in the proximal region was 0.975 ± 0.330, ranging from (0.645, 1]. For all distal scCREs, the SD was calculated as 0.341, with double-peak values at 0.052 and 0.987, respectively. Therefore, the selection range for nonconserved scCREs in the distal region was determined to be 0.052 ± 0.341, i.e. [0, 0.393), while the selection range for conserved scCREs in the distal region was 0.987 ± 0.341, i.e. (0.646, 1].

Evolutionary Conservation of scCREs

For the analysis of evolutionary conservation in scCREs, we obtained their phastCons scores from Sarropoulos et al. (2021). To retrieve the 4Dsites, we downloaded the mouse genomic sequences (version mm10) along with their corresponding annotations from the UCSC database (Nassar et al. 2023). We then used the GffRead tool (Pertea and Pertea 2020) to convert the mouse genome GTF file into a GFF file, which served as input data for ReSeqtools (He et al. 2013). ReSeqtools facilitated the extraction of 4Dsites within the codons by providing their genomic positions. The phyloP scores corresponding to these 4Dsites were obtained from the UCSC database based on their genomic coordinates. To assess evolutionary conservation, we randomly selected 500 4Dsites to create a fragment of equal length to the scCREs and calculated the average phyloP score for this fragment. This process was repeated 1,000 times. Subsequently, we randomly selected 1,000 conserved and 1,000 nonconserved scCREs from the proximal and distal scCREs, respectively, and applied the same methodology to determine the average phyloP scores of these scCREs.

Functional Enrichment Analyses

We calculated the peak values and corresponding SDs of phastCons scores for both proximal and distal scCREs. We then extracted representative conserved and nonconserved scCREs whose phastCons scores fell within the range of peak value ± SD for further analyses. Functional enrichment analyses were performed for the nearest genes associated with these representative conserved and nonconserved scCREs using clusterProfiler (version 4.6.2) (Wu et al. 2021). Specifically, we focused on significantly enriched GO terms within the biological process (adjusted P < 0.01), determined using the Benjamini–Hochberg algorithm implemented in pAdjustMethod for multiple testing corrections. The results of the functional enrichment analyses were summarized and visualized using the simplifyEnrichment tool (version 1.8.0) (Gu and Hübschmann 2023). To enhance the presentation and comprehension of the enrichment analysis results, we organized the data based on the number of GO terms and selected the top 5 representative summary words for presentation.

Identification of Genes Associated with Cell Differentiation, Cell Stemness Maintenance, and Housekeeping Functions

We used the R packages org.Mm.eg.db and GO.db to retrieve all GO terms and their detailed descriptions. We then screened genes related to cell differentiation based on their functional annotations. By intersecting these genes with the total of 19,873 nearest genes to each scCRE, we identified a total of 2,713 cell differentiation genes (supplementary table S1, Supplementary Material online). Similarly, we obtained genes related to cell stemness maintenance from the StemChecker database (Pinto et al. 2015) and housekeeping genes from the HRT Atlas database (Hounkpe et al. 2021). By overlapping these gene sets with 19,873 nearest genes to scCREs, we identified 2,984 genes related to cell stemness maintenance (supplementary table S2, Supplementary Material online) and 3,194 housekeeping genes (supplementary table S3, Supplementary Material online).

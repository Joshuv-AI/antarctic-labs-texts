Clinical Efficacy of Sodium-Glucose Cotransporter-2 Inhibitors After Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement in Patients With Diabetes: A Global Retrospective Study
Authors: Muhammad Hanif, Waasay H. Khan, Shiva P. Sah, Jai S. Nagarajan, Jenish Bhandari, Stephen J. Knohl, Cynthia C. Taub, Andrew M. Weinberg, Ankur Kalra
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTranscatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR), a standard therapy for aortic stenosis, is preferred for older or high-risk patients. Various complications, including heart failure (HF), resulting in increased mortality, have been reported in the literature. Sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) are recommended in HF, irrespective of ejection fraction; however, their role in reducing post-TAVR cardiovascular complications remains underexplored. We conducted a retrospective study to evaluate cardiovascular outcomes associated with the use of SGLT2i in post-TAVR patients.MethodsThis retrospective cohort was based on a global database of electronic health records of more than 160 million patients. After propensity-score matching, the study population included 9930 post-TAVR diabetic patients divided into 2 cohorts based on their exposure to SGLT2i. Exposure to SGLT2i was defined as exposure after 1 day of TAVR.ResultsIn this study of 9930 post-TAVR diabetic patients, the primary outcome of major adverse cardiovascular events and secondary outcomes, ie, cardiovascular mortality, ischemic stroke, and HF exacerbation, were significantly reduced in SGLT2i-exposed post-TAVR patients at the 1-month and 1-year follow-up. Hemorrhagic stroke was comparable at the 1-month follow-up; however, it was significantly lower in SGLT2i-exposed cohorts at the 1-year follow-up. The risk of new-onset myocardial infarction was significantly reduced at 1 month; however, it was comparable between the 2 cohorts at the 1-year follow-up. Our study also demonstrated a significant reduction in the risk of end-stage renal disease in the SGLT2i-exposed cohort at the 1-year follow-up. The periprocedural risk of acute kidney injury (within 7 days of TAVR) as well as at the 1-month and 1-year follow-up was significantly lower in the SGLT2i-exposed cohort.ConclusionsIn this study, post-TAVR SGLT2i use in diabetic patients was associated with reduced cardiovascular morbidity and mortality for up to 1 year.

Introduction

Transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) has emerged as a standard therapy for severe aortic stenosis (AS), with its global focus expanding beyond symptomatic severe AS to include asymptomatic severe and even moderate AS cases.1 Several randomized controlled trials demonstrate that TAVR is noninferior, and in some aspects, superior to surgical aortic valve replacement (SAVR) for low-risk AS patients.2,3 TAVR is preferred for older or high-risk patients, whereas SAVR remains the standard for relatively younger, low-risk patients, or those requiring concomitant cardiac surgery.4 With TAVR now expanding to low-risk patients, Reddy et al5 reaffirmed its noninferiority to SAVR, demonstrating a 20% reduction in 5-year all-cause mortality, alongside sustained advantages in procedural safety and recovery.

Despite recent advancements in TAVR, this procedure has many early and late complications. Post-TAVR periprocedural acute kidney injury (AKI) and heart failure (HF) readmissions have been reported as the strongest independent predictors of increased mortality and diminished quality of life postprocedure.6,7 Similarly, literature has reported a 2-fold increased risk of stroke in post-TAVR patients compared with matched controls.8 These high-impact post-TAVR complications exhibit greater prognostic significance than other procedural risks, emphasizing the critical role of perioperative risk stratification and management.9

Sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors (SGLT2i), initially developed for diabetes management and later repurposed for HF and chronic kidney disease (CKD) to slow disease progression, have evolved in their therapeutic applications.10 SGLT2i have redefined HF management, with robust clinical trial data supporting their benefits in reducing morbidity and mortality.11 Consequently, both US and European clinical guidelines now universally recommend incorporating SGLT2i into HF treatment protocols, irrespective of ejection fraction or diabetes status.12,13 However, the role of SGLT2i is still underexplored in new-onset HF post-TAVR. This gap persists despite the well-documented burden of cardiac complications after TAVR, where approximately 40% of readmissions are cardiovascular in origin, with HF accounting for over 50% of these events.14 Furthermore, HF not only drives the rehospitalization rate but also represents the leading cause of mortality in TAVR patients, emphasizing its central role in postprocedural outcomes.15

In this study, we aimed to investigate the impact of SGLT2i in post-TAVR patients, with major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) as the primary outcome. MACE were defined as a composite of acute myocardial infarction (AMI), ischemic stroke, hemorrhagic stroke, and cardiovascular mortality. Secondary outcomes included the individual components of the composite end-point, HF exacerbation, end-stage renal disease (ESRD), and AKI. The study also sought to clarify the therapeutic potential of SGLT2i in reducing post-TAVR morbidity and mortality, thereby addressing a critical evidence gap in current TAVR management.

Methods

Data source

We used TriNetX for this study, a global network of health care organizations (HCO) aggregating data from electronic health records (EHR). This study was conducted using the Global Collaborative Network, where 170 HCO and over 160 million patients were queried. TriNetX provides access to comprehensive electronic medical records, including demographics, diagnoses coded using the International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision, Clinical Modification (ICD-10-CM), and procedures coded using the International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision, Procedure Coding System or Current Procedural Terminology, laboratory values standardized with Logical Observation Identifiers Names and Codes, and genomic data. The research network's data are sourced from academic and nonacademic medical centers, community hospitals, and specialty units, encompassing data from both insured and noninsured patients. The HCO are located in 30 countries across the US, Latin America, Europe, and the Asia-Pacific region. Despite the anonymized nature of the data, the integrated analytics enable the creation of patient-level data for cohort selection and matching, analysis of incidence and prevalence within a cohort, and comparison of characteristics and outcomes between matched cohorts. More details of the platform can be found elsewhere.16 This study was exempt from institutional review board review and written informed consent from patients because of the deidentified nature of the TriNetX clinical database.

Patient population

We conducted this study on adults with type 2 diabetes (T2D) (aged ≥18 years) who underwent TAVR between January 2014 and December 2024. We created 2 cohorts based on exposure to SGLT2i after undergoing a TAVR procedure: one with post-TAVR diabetic patients on SGLT2i and a control group of post-TAVR diabetic patients without SGLT2i. SGLT2i exposure was identified in the TriNetX platform using RxNorm and anatomical therapeutic chemical classification codes for empagliflozin, dapagliflozin, canagliflozin, ertugliflozin, or the SGLT2i drug class. Patients were classified into the exposure cohort if an SGLT2i medication record was documented after TAVR. The index date was defined as the date of the first documented post-TAVR SGLT2i exposure occurring after the first day following TAVR, using an intention-to-treat framework. Patients with documented SGLT2i exposure prior to TAVR were not included in the exposure cohort. Patients with outcomes occurring before the index event or before the beginning of the specified outcome window were excluded from the corresponding outcome analyses. TriNetX captures EHR-documented medication exposure; however, detailed information regarding medication adherence, prescription fill history, dose intensity, treatment duration, or discontinuation was not available.

Patients were followed until the occurrence of the outcome of interest, death, last recorded encounter, or completion of the predefined follow-up window, whichever occurred first. Before propensity-score matching (PSM), 23,989 patients in the control cohort and 5587 patients in the SGLT2i-exposed cohort met the eligibility criteria. After matching, 4965 patients were included in each cohort for the final analysis. Both cohorts were followed for 1 year after the index event. ICD-10-CM and RxNorm codes for diabetic mellitus and TAVR are defined in Supplemental Table S1.

Outcomes

The primary outcome was a relative risk (RR) of MACE, which was defined as a composite of AMI, ischemic stroke, hemorrhagic stroke, and cardiovascular mortality. Secondary outcomes included the individual components of the composite end-point, HF exacerbation, and ESRD. Additionally, we evaluated safety outcomes of new-onset periprocedural AKI at 1-month, and 1-year follow-up.

All the outcomes were analyzed at 1 month and 1 year after the index point, ie, after initiating SGLT2i in post-TAVR diabetic patients. Patients with an outcome of interest before the window period were excluded from the main analysis.

Outcomes were identified using structured ICD-10-CM coding algorithms within the TriNetX database, a large health care network containing diagnostic, procedural, medication, laboratory, and demographic data derived from EHR. Primary clinical data, including symptoms, biomarkers, electrocardiographic findings, imaging studies, and direct chart-level adjudication, were not independently reviewed. Outcome definitions were based on predefined ICD-10-CM diagnosis codes and standardized coding approaches that have been used in prior TriNetX-based observational studies. The ICD-10 codes used for outcome definitions are provided in Supplemental Table S2.

Statistical analysis

The patient population was divided into 2 groups based on exposure to SGLT2i. Continuous variables are presented as mean ± SD, and categorical variables are reported as n (%). To ensure comparability of the cohorts, PSM was performed with the baseline demographics (age, sex, race), body mass index, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, CKD, HF, ischemic heart disease, tobacco use, and medication use like beta-blockers, angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers, antiarrhythmic agents, glucagon-like peptide receptor agonists, loop diuretics, thiazides, and HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors, and laboratory tests like hemoglobin level, low-density lipoprotein level, serum creatinine, glomerular filtration rate, glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), natriuretic B peptide, and left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF). PSM was performed using logistic regression based on the previously mentioned covariates and one-to-one greedy nearest-neighbor matching without replacement, and a caliper of 0.1 of the SD of the logit propensity score was used. Covariate balance was assessed using standardized mean differences (SMD).

Most covariates achieved acceptable balance after matching (SMD < 0.1); however, mild residual imbalance persisted for selected variables, including HF, HbA1c, creatinine, estimated glomerular filtration rate, and body mass index. Therefore, these variables were interpreted cautiously during outcome analysis.

All baseline characteristics were assessed within the 12 months preceding the index date, defined as the date of the first SGLT2i exposure in patients with T2D who underwent TAVR. For laboratory variables, the most recent available values within that 12-month period were used. Post-TAVR diabetic patients exposed/not exposed to SGLT2i were matched 1:1 using a built-in PSM algorithm. The risk ratio, along with the P value, was reported. For comparison between 2 groups, we used χ2 for categorical variables and t-tests for continuous variables.

Propensity-score matching was performed using clinically relevant covariates, including demographics, cardiovascular comorbidities, medication use, laboratory parameters, and cardiac function variables, to reduce baseline confounding prior to outcome analysis. Risk ratios reported in the outcomes tables were derived from the matched cohorts and do not represent separate multivariable regression-adjusted estimates.

All hypothesis tests were 2-tailed, with statistical significance defined as P <.05. Two-tailed tests were chosen to account for the possibility of differences in either direction, reflecting an unbiased approach to hypothesis testing, and 95% CI were reported to indicate the precision of the estimates. The database version utilized in this study included data available up to December 2025. Cohort construction, PSM, and outcome analyses were conducted using the integrated analytic tools within TriNetX. Statistical analyses were performed using the TriNetX online platform.16

Sensitivity analysis

E-values were calculated during the sensitivity analysis to evaluate the robustness of the findings to potential biases from unmeasured confounding or omitted covariates in both primary and secondary outcomes. E-value was calculated using the formula: E=RR+RR(RR–1). A higher E-value indicates that a more potent unmeasured cofounder would be necessary to invalidate the observed relationship and suggest a robust, causal link between the exposure and the outcome.

Several sensitivity analyses were conducted to assess the robustness of the findings. First, SGLT2i exposure was redefined as initiation at least 30 days post-TAVR in T2D patients, excluding patients who began therapy earlier, while maintaining identical follow-up and analytic methods. Second, a 3-month landmark analysis was implemented to reduce exposure misclassification, requiring patients to be alive and under follow-up at landmark, with outcomes assessed from that point forward. Additional subgroup analyses were performed in: (1) post-TAVR patients with HF and type 2 diabetes mellitus, and (2) post-TAVR HF patients without diabetes to evaluate whether the observed associations remained consistent across diabetic and nondiabetic HF populations. PSM and outcome analyses were performed using the same analytic framework as the primary analysis. Additionally, for falsification analysis, we evaluated the RR of new-onset hypothyroidism, vitamin D deficiency, osteoarthritis, and dermatologic diseases.

Primary outcome

Propensity-score matching analysis showed that the primary outcome of MACE was significantly lower in the SGLT2i-exposed post-TAVR patients compared with control, both at the 1-month (RR, 0.57; 95% CI, 0.43-0.75; P < .01) and 1-year follow-up (RR, 0.72; 95% CI, 0.63-0.82; P < .01) (Tables 2 and 3). The corresponding E-value for the 1-year outcome was 2.12, suggesting that an unmeasured confounder would need to be associated with both the exposure and the outcome by a risk ratio of 2.12 to fully explain this association. Secondary outcomes

At the 1-month follow-up, secondary outcomes, ie, cardiovascular mortality (RR, 0.66; 95% CI, 0.51-0.85; P < .01), HF exacerbation (RR, 0.10; 95% 0.07-0.15; P < .01), AMI (RR, 0.41; 95% CI, 0.28-0.61; P < .01), and ischemic stroke (RR, 0.60; 95% CI, 0.37-1.00; P = .04) were found to be significantly lower in SGLT2i-exposed post-TAVR diabetic patients compared with controls (Table 2). The risk of hemorrhagic stroke was found to be comparable between the 2 cohorts at the 1-month follow-up (Table 2).

Similarly, a significant reduction in cardiovascular mortality (RR, 0.72; 95% CI, 0.64-0.81; P < .01), HF exacerbation (RR, 0.60; 95% CI, 0.43-0.83; P < .01), ischemic stroke (RR 0.72, 95% CI, 0.57-0.91; P < .03), and hemorrhagic stroke (RR, 0.52; 95% CI, 0.32-0.82; P < .01) were found in SGLT2i-exposed post-TAVR patients at the 1-year follow-up. The risk of MI was found to be comparable between the 2 cohorts at the 1-year follow-up (RR, 0.83; 95% CI, 0.67-1.03; P = .09) (Table 3). The risk of ESRD was significantly lower in the SGLT2i-exposed cohort at the 1-year follow-up (RR, 0.69; 95% CI, 0.47-0.99; P = .04) (Table 3). However, ESRD outcomes at the 1-month follow-up could not be reliably assessed because of insufficient event numbers (<10 events in each cohort after PSM).

Safety outcome

The RR of periprocedural AKI (within 7 days of TAVR) was found to be significantly less in the SGLT2i-exposed cohort (RR, 0.35; 95% CI, 0.24-0.51; P < .01). Similar risk reduction in AKI was found at both 1-month (RR, 0.61; 95% CI, 0.43-0.86; P < .01) and 1-year follow-up (RR, 0.80; 95% CI, 0.67-0.94; P = .07) in SGLT2i cohort compared with controls (Tables 2 and 3).

Subgroup analyses in HF populations

Subgroup analyses were additionally performed in post-TAVR patients with HF and type 2 diabetes mellitus, as well as in post-TAVR HF patients without diabetes. Among post-TAVR HF patients with diabetes, SGLT2i exposure was additionally associated with significantly lower risks of MACE, cardiovascular mortality, HF exacerbation, AMI, and ischemic stroke (Supplemental Table S3). Similarly, in post-TAVR HF patients without diabetes, SGLT2i exposure remained associated with a lower risk of MACE and HF exacerbation, supporting potential cardiovascular benefits beyond diabetic populations (Supplemental Table S4).

Sensitivity analyses

Sensitivity analyses using alternative SGLT2i exposure definitions

In sensitivity analyses redefining SGLT2i exposure as initiation occurring ≥30 days after TAVR in T2D patients, the associations between SGLT2i use and study outcomes remained consistent and statistically significant at the 1-year follow-up, with effect estimates comparable to those observed in the primary analysis (Supplemental Table S5).

Landmark analysis at 3 months

In a 3-month landmark analysis performed to reduce potential exposure misclassification related to medication adherence, SGLT2i use remained associated with significantly lower risks of the primary and most secondary outcomes compared with matched controls (Supplemental Table S6).

Falsification outcomes

We found no significant differences in any falsification outcomes, suggesting that unmeasured confounders were likely balanced between the 2 cohorts (Supplemental Table S7).

Discussion

In this study, we evaluated the cardiovascular outcomes associated with SGLT2i use in post-TAVR diabetic patients. Our results showed that the primary outcome of MACE was significantly lower in SGLT2i-exposed post-TAVR patients at the 1-month and 1-year follow-up. Secondary outcomes, ie, cardiovascular mortality, ischemic stroke, and HF exacerbation, were significantly lower in SGLT2i-exposed post-TAVR patients compared with controls at the 1-month and the 1-year follow-up. The risk of MI was significantly reduced in the SGLT2i-exposed cohort at the 1-month follow-up. However, this risk was comparable between the 2 cohorts at the 1-year follow-up. Periprocedural AKI (within 7 days of TAVR) was also significantly less in the SGLT2i-exposed cohort. Similar risk reduction in AKI was found at the 1-month and 1-year follow-up in the SGLT2i-exposed cohort compared with the controls. Our study also demonstrated a significant reduction in the risk of ESRD in the SGLT2i-exposed cohort at the 1-year follow-up. However, the renal findings in our study should be interpreted cautiously. Although AKI reduction remained significant in the primary analysis, this association was attenuated in certain sensitivity analyses, potentially reflecting reduced statistical power after application of stricter exposure definitions and landmark analysis criteria. Additionally, periprocedural AKI after TAVR is multifactorial and may be influenced by procedural and hemodynamic factors not fully captured within EHR-based data sets.

Paolisso et al17 conducted a study on the clinical efficacy of SGLT2i in post-TAVR patients. They reported significant improvement in LVEF, all-cause death, MACE, and HF hospitalization (HFH) in SGLT2i-exposed post-TAVR patients compared with SGLT2i-nonexposed post-TAVR patients, findings consistent with our results. However, their study population comprised patients with severe AS and LVEF <50%, whereas ours included all AS patients, irrespective of severity and LVEF. Recently, a randomized controlled trial (DAPA-TAVI trial) showed similar significantly reduced all-cause mortality and cardiovascular mortality in dapagliflozin-exposed post-TAVR patients compared with controls, consistent with our findings.18 However, their study population included patients with a history of HF along with one or more of the following conditions: renal insufficiency, diabetes, or LV systolic dysfunction. A recent meta-analysis conducted by Dimitriadis et al19 revealed significant periprocedural AKI risk reduction among chronic SGLT2i users undergoing coronary procedures. Our study supports similar AKI risk reduction in post-TAVR patients. Additionally, post-TAVR periprocedural AKI has been reported to be a strong predictor for both short-term and long-term mortality.20 In addition to reductions in AKI, SGLT2i exposure was also associated with a significantly lower risk of progression to ESRD at the 1-year follow-up in our study. These findings are consistent with prior cardiovascular and renal outcome trials demonstrating the reno-protective effects of SGLT2i across a broad spectrum of high-risk patients.21 Given that CKD and renal dysfunction are highly prevalent among post-TAVR patients and are strongly associated with adverse long-term outcomes, the observed reduction in ESRD may have important clinical implications in this vulnerable population. However, these findings should be interpreted cautiously, given the observational design and limited granularity regarding longitudinal renal function trajectories within EHR-based data sets.

Similarly, our study demonstrated significantly lower risks of ischemic stroke and hemorrhagic stroke at the 1-year follow-up in the SGLT2i-exposed cohort. The mechanism underlying the observed reduction in hemorrhagic stroke remains incompletely understood. Potential explanations include SGLT2i-mediated reductions in blood pressure, arterial stiffness, oxidative stress, systemic inflammation, and endothelial dysfunction, which may collectively improve vascular stability and reduce susceptibility to intracerebral hemorrhage.22,23 However, given the relatively lower event rates and observational nature of the study, these findings should be interpreted cautiously and considered hypothesis-generating.

Despite substantial evidence supporting TAVR in AS patients, the literature has reported a significant incidence of HFH in post-TAVR patients. HFH post-TAVR has been associated with an increased risk of long-term all-cause mortality.24 A spectrum of preprocedural clinical and structural factors, including the complex pathophysiology of AS, which is no longer considered an isolated valvular disease but rather a combination of valvular and myocardial disease, has been independently linked to heightened HF risk post-TAVR. These include the presence or absence of extravalvular cardiac damage, lower baseline aortic valve mean gradient, persistent ventricular remodeling, myocardial fibrosis, diastolic dysfunction, multivalvular disease, pulmonary hypertension, coronary artery disease, and atrial fibrillation.25,26 In addition, procedural and device-related complications such as significant paravalvular regurgitation, severe prosthesis-patient mismatch, and conduction disturbances have been shown to exacerbate HF outcomes further.25,26 These findings highlight the importance of a comprehensive, multidisciplinary approach to TAVR with emphasis on optimization of medical therapy to mitigate HF-related morbidity and mortality.

Sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors, the latest addition to guideline-directed medical therapy for HF, reduce cardiac preload through natriuresis and glycosuria, thereby lowering ventricular filling pressure. The reduction in plasma volume subsequently reduces blood pressure and arterial stiffness, reducing afterload and improving subendocardial blood flow.27 Additionally, SGLT2i increase sodium delivery to the macula densa, thereby activating tubuloglomerular feedback. This activation leads to renal arteriolar afferent vasoconstriction, resulting in reduced intraglomerular pressure and subsequently offering a reno-protective effect.28 SGLT2i-induced glycosuria also mediates a uricosuric effect via the GLUT9 transporter, which may be cardio-protective, as increased plasma uric acid levels have been associated with cardiovascular complications and congestive HF.29,30 With a combination of cardio-protective and reno-protective mechanisms, SGLT2i have significantly reduced morbidity and mortality in patients with chronic use, highlighting their pivotal role in current approaches to cardiovascular and kidney disease management.

Importantly, although the primary analysis was limited to diabetic patients to reduce clinical heterogeneity, additional subgroup analyses demonstrated consistent benefits in post-TAVR patients with HF, both with and without diabetes. In these exploratory analyses, SGLT2i exposure remained associated with significant reductions in HF exacerbations and favorable trends in cardiovascular outcomes, suggesting that SGLT2i benefits may extend beyond diabetic populations in the post-TAVR setting. These findings are consistent with the contemporary HF literature, which demonstrates cardiovascular and renal benefits of SGLT2i irrespective of diabetes status.

Strengths and limitations

The sample size of 4965 post-TAVR patients with SGLT2i exposure is one of the largest cohorts. PSM reduces confounding bias by controlling confounding variables, balancing observed covariates, and reducing selection bias. Because the data are from TriNetX, an extensive global network database that procures data from EHR of various HCO from different countries, including various demographics, there is better generalizability and external validity. This study is further strengthened by extensive sensitivity analyses, including a 3-month landmark analysis to address exposure persistence, alternative exposure definitions, and falsification outcomes. Results were consistent across analyses, supporting the robustness of the findings.

There was a lack of granular data on dosing and the type of administration of SGLT2i. Because these analyses were based on EHR-recorded active prescriptions within TriNetX, detailed information regarding medication adherence, refill history, exact initiation or discontinuation dates, and long-term compliance was unavailable. Therefore, SGLT2i exposure should be interpreted as documented prescription exposure rather than confirmed medication adherence. Detailed information regarding the exact interval between TAVR and SGLT2i initiation, longitudinal adherence, and treatment duration was not consistently available within TriNetX.

Unobserved confounders and the need for granular data on key covariates may have led to imbalanced matching. Although extensive PSM was performed to mitigate the influence of confounders, residual confounding remains possible, for example, variations in health care standards across different countries. Despite robust PSM, potential biases from unmeasured confounders may remain. To address this, we conducted a sensitivity analysis using E-values. The high E-values suggest that unmeasured confounders are unlikely to account for the observed effects. The retrospective nature of the study prevented the establishment of causality.

Outcomes were identified using ICD-10-CM coding data within TriNetX and were not independently adjudicated using primary clinical records, biomarkers, electrocardiographic findings, imaging studies, or symptom-level data. Errors while reporting health conditions as ICD-10 codes may have existed because of inaccurate or incomplete data, which remains an intrinsic limitation of TriNetX.

Clinical implications and future directions

Our study suggests that SGLT2i in post-TAVR diabetic patients may significantly reduce cardiovascular mortality and other cardiovascular complications, including HF exacerbation, both in the short- and long-term follow-up. These findings highlight the potential role of SGLT2i as adjunctive therapy in the peri-TAVR period, particularly in patients with diabetes who remain at elevated cardiovascular risk despite valve intervention. Although the results are promising, they require further validation in prospective, randomized clinical trials to clarify the ideal timing of initiation of SGLT2i, identify the appropriate patient population, and assess the potential advantages in nondiabetic individuals undergoing TAVR. Additionally, future studies should compare the relative efficacy of different SGLT2i to support personalized therapeutic strategies in post-TAVR settings.

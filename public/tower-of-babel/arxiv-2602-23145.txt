Stochastic Differential Inclusions driven by Maximal Monotone Operators with empty interiors
Juan Guillermo Garrido, Pedro Pérez-Aros, Mathias Staudigl

Abstract
This paper studies the long-time behavior of stochastic differential inclusions driven by maximal monotone operators, motivated by continuous-time models of first-order optimization methods under noisy or approximate operator information. We first address well-posedness and show that existence and uniqueness can be established without the customary requirement that the operator’s domain has nonempty interior, by adopting an appropriate notion of solution. We then analyze asymptotic properties of the resulting stochastic dynamics, extending convergence guarantees beyond previously studied settings that rely on smooth potentials, full-domain subdifferentials, or Lipschitz monotone operators. In addition, we consider a Tikhonov-type regularization of the stochastic inclusion and prove corresponding well-posedness and long-time convergence results.



Keywords.

Stochastic differential inclusion, Maximal monotone operator, Asymptotic analysis, Convergence Rates, Tikhonov regularization

AMS subject classifications.

60H10, 37N40, 90C25, 49J52, 34F05, 47H05

1
Introduction

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
be a proper, convex, and lower semicontinuous function.
A central continuous-time model in convex optimization is the
subgradient flow

(1.1)

-x^{\prime}(t)\in\partial f(x(t)),\qquad x(0)=x_{0}\in\operatorname{dom}f,

where
\partial f
denotes the convex subdifferential. In finite dimension, solutions of (
1.1
)
are globally defined and, whenever the function attains its minimum, converge as
t\to\infty
to minimizers of
f
.
More broadly, (
1.1
) is a particular instance of evolution inclusions governed by maximal monotone
operators, whose well-posedness and long-time behavior have been extensively studied
(see, e.g.,
[
4
,
9
,
26
]
).

This dynamical viewpoint is also tightly connected to numerical algorithms from mathematical optimization. Indeed, classical time discretizations of
maximal-monotone evolutions yield fundamental first-order methods, such as implicit schemes and splitting procedures
based on resolvents and proximal mappings. In turn, qualitative properties of the continuous-time trajectories
(Lyapunov decrease, asymptotic convergence, stability) provide a useful blueprint for interpreting convergence
mechanisms and, in favorable regimes, rates for broad classes of descent algorithms (see, e.g.,

[
2
,
3
]
, and the references therein).

Modern large-scale applications often involve uncertainty, where the driving first-order information is noisy,
incomplete, or obtained through sampling. In such settings, exact evaluations of
\partial f
(or, more generally, of a
monotone operator) may be prohibitively expensive, while stochastic approximations are computationally viable.
This motivates stochastic counterparts of maximal-monotone dynamics, which can be viewed as continuous-time version
of stochastic first-order schemes (see, e.g.,
[
12
]
).

In this work we study the long-time behavior of solutions to the stochastic differential inclusion (SDI)

(1.2)

dX_{t}\in-A(X_{t})\,dt+\sigma(t,X_{t})\,dB_{t},\qquad X_{0}\in{\text{cl }}\operatorname{dom}A,

where
A\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a maximal monotone operator,
\sigma\colon[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times\ell}

is a given diffusion coefficient, and
B
denotes an
\ell
-dimensional Brownian motion.
A first fundamental issue in this multivalued setting is well-posedness. Existing approaches typically impose structural
conditions that allow one to adapt classical SDI techniques; in particular, it is customary to assume that
\operatorname{dom}A
has
nonempty interior (see, e.g.,
[
6
,
11
,
17
]
).
Our first contribution is to show that this interiority assumption can be removed by adopting a modified notion of
solution. This yields existence and uniqueness results for general SDI without requiring any additional assumption of the maximal monotone operator
A
.

A second, and central, question concerns the asymptotic behaviour of (
1.2
).
Recent works have established convergence results primarily in settings where the operator has additional structure.
Most contributions address the smooth case
A=\nabla f
, with
f
convex and differentiable, and show that several
convergence properties of the deterministic dynamics extend to the stochastic setting (see, e.g.,

[
20
,
18
]
).
In the nonsmooth regime,
[
19
]
treats the case
A=\partial g
for convex, continuous
functions
g
with full domain, while related results for Lipschitz continuous monotone operators are obtained in

[
8
]
. The aim of the present work is to go beyond these frameworks and establish convergence results
for (
1.2
) driven by a general maximal monotone operator.

Finally, we also analyze a Tikhonov-type regularization of (
1.2
), namely

(1.3)

dX_{t}\in-A(X_{t})\,dt-\varepsilon(t)X_{t}\,dt+\sigma(t,X_{t})\,dB_{t},\qquad X_{0}\in{\text{cl }}\operatorname{dom}A,

where
\varepsilon(\cdot)
is a nonnegative parameter function. This additional term plays the role of a vanishing
regularization that is relevant both for asymptotic selection mechanisms and for algorithmic stabilization.
Building on our well-posedness theory for general SDIs, we establish existence and uniqueness for
(
1.3
) and derive its long-time convergence properties alongside those of
(
1.2
).

The recent paper
[
16
]
is very much related to this work. They study a penalty-regulated stochastic differential inclusion and prove that the trajectories of the constructed dynamical system exhibit asymptotic convergence towards a solution of constrained variational inequality. This result is obtained under the standard setting, assuming that the driving maximally monotone operator in the drift has non-empty interior. The present paper complements thus the findings of
[
16
]
by proving existence and uniqueness of solution for a much more general class of operators. Moreover, we perform in this paper the analysis of the Tikhonov regularized dynamical system, which behaves dynamically very differently to penalty-governed dynamics.

The rest of the work is organized as follows: In Section
2
, we introduce the notation, definitions, and auxiliary lemmas used throughout the work. Section
3
extends the existence and uniqueness theory for a general SDI, without any assumption on the maximal monotone operator (in fact, we not require the non-emptiness of the interior of the domain). In Section
4
, we establish general convergence results for the solutions of (
1.2
) under mild decaying assumptions on
\sigma
, which extends the convergence results obtained in
[
18
]
. In particular, we prove convergence of the averaged process and show that, whenever the associated deterministic dynamics (i.e., when
\sigma=0
in (
1.2
)) converges to a zero of
A
, the stochastic system (
1.2
) converges as well. We also provide rates of convergence in some special cases. Section
5
focuses on the case
A=\partial\varphi
, with
\varphi
being a proper, convex, and lower semicontinuous function, and provides convergence rates in general settings, extending the study of
[
18
,
20
]
in the smooth case (
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{1,+}
or
\mathcal{C}^{2}
) to the nonsmooth setting. Finally, in Section
6
, we study the convergence of solutions to (
1.3
) without additional assumptions on
A
and under mild hypotheses on the parameter function
\varepsilon
.

Notation and Preliminaries in Convex Analysis

In this section we introduce the notation and some preliminary results that will be used throughout the paper.
The Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is equipped with the standard inner product and the associated norm

\langle x,y\rangle=x^{\top}y,\qquad\|x\|=\sqrt{\langle x,x\rangle}.

For a matrix
P\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, its
adjoint
(transpose) is denoted by
P^{\ast}
.
We write
\|P\|
for the Frobenius norm of
P
.

2.1
Notions in Convex Analysis

For a set
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the
affine hull
of
C
is defined as

\operatorname{aff}(C):=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}x_{i}:x_{i}\in C,\ \lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\ \sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}=1\right\},

that is, the smallest affine subspace containing
C
. Equivalently, if
x_{0}\in C
, then

\operatorname{aff}(C)=x_{0}+\operatorname{span}(C-x_{0}),

where
C-x_{0}:=\{x-x_{0}:x\in C\}
and
\operatorname{span}(C-x_{0})
denotes the linear subspace generated by
C-x_{0}
. It follows by definition that

(2.1)

\operatorname{aff}(C)-a=\operatorname{aff}(C-C)=\operatorname{span}(C-C)\quad\forall a\in C.

The
convex hull
of a set
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is defined as

\operatorname{conv}(C):=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}x_{i}:x_{i}\in C,\ \lambda_{i}\geq 0,\ \sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}=1\right\},

that is, the smallest convex set containing
C
. For a set
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
(not necessarily convex), the
relative interior
of
C
is defined as the interior of
C
relative to its affine hull, namely,

\operatorname{rint}(C):=\{x\in C:\exists\,\varepsilon>0\text{ such that }\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(x)\cap\operatorname{aff}(C)\subseteq C\}.

It is well-known that every convex set in finite dimensions has a nonempty relative interior (see, e.g.,
[
27
, Proposition 2.40]
). More generally, if a set
U
is nearly convex in the sense that there exists a convex set
C
with
C\subseteq U\subseteq{\text{cl }}C
, then
\operatorname{rint}(U)\neq\varnothing
. In particular, for our purposes, the domains of maximal monotone operators are nearly convex sets, and hence their relative interiors are nonempty (see, e.g.,
[
27
, Theorem 12.41]
).

If
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a nonempty subset and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we say that
v
is
orthogonal
to
C
, denoted
v\perp C
, if

\langle v,x-y\rangle=0,\qquad\forall\,x,y\in C,

that is,
v
is orthogonal to the space generated by
C-C
. If
L\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a linear operator, its
range
(or image) is defined as

\operatorname{rge}(L):=\{Lx:x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}.

Given a set
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and a point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we denote
d(x;C)
the distance from
x
to
C
.
For a function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
the
effective domain
of
f
is given by

\operatorname{dom}f:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:f(x)<\infty\}.

The
subdifferential
of a convex function
f
at
x\in\operatorname{dom}f
is defined as

\partial f(x):=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:f(y)\geq f(x)+\langle v,y-x\rangle\ \ \forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}.

Any element
v\in\partial f(x)
is called a
subgradient
of
f
at
x
.

An operator
A\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{d}
is called
monotone
if

\langle u-v,x-y\rangle\geq 0,\qquad\forall(x,u),(y,v)\in\operatorname{gph}A,

where the
graph
of
A
is

\operatorname{gph}A:=\{(x,u)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}:u\in A(x)\}.

The operator
A
is said to be
maximal monotone
if it is monotone and its graph cannot be properly extended while preserving monotonicity. The resolvent of
A
with parameter
\lambda>0
is defined as
J_{\lambda A}(x):=(\text{Id}+\lambda A)^{-1}
. When
A
is maximal monotone, we know that
J_{\lambda A}
is single-valued on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(see, e.g.,
[
5
, Theorem 21.1]
). For
x\in\operatorname{dom}A
,
A(x)
is convex and closed (see
[
5
, Proposition 20.36]
) and we denote by
A^{0}(x)
to the element of
A(x)
with minimal norm.

For the sake of simplicity of the presentation, all the previous definitions and notations are understood to hold in any arbitrary finite-dimensional Hilbert space.

We recall the following well-known result, which provides a sufficient condition for the preservation of maximal monotonicity under affine transformations (see, e.g.,
[
27
, Theorem 12.43]
,
[
28
, Theorem 4]
,
[
29
, Theorem 6]
, or
[
25
, Corollary 4.4]
).

Theorem 2.1

(Maximal monotonicity under composition)
.

Let
X,Y
be two finite dimensional Hilbert spaces, and
A\colon Y\rightrightarrows Y
be maximal monotone mapping,
P\colon X\to Y
a be a linear mapping. Define
T\colon X\rightrightarrows X
by

T(x):=P^{\ast}A(Px).

If
\operatorname{rge}P\cap\operatorname{rint}\left(\operatorname{dom}A\right)\neq\emptyset
, then
T
is maximal monotone.

2.2
Measure theory and stochastic processes

In what follows we work on a filtered probability space

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})

satisfying the standard conditions: the filtration is right-continuous
(
\mathcal{F}_{t}=\bigcap_{u>t}\mathcal{F}_{u}
for all
t\geq 0
) and complete
(every
\mathbb{P}
-null set in
\mathcal{F}
belongs to
\mathcal{F}_{0}
). Following standard conventions and notation, we suppress the dependence on

\omega\in\Omega
for convenience when dealing with random variables or
stochastic processes.

A stochastic process with values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(equipped with its Borel

\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
) is a family
X=(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
such that

X_{t}\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is measurable for each
t\geq 0
.

A process
X
is said to be
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
-adapted if
X_{t}
is

\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable for all
t\geq 0
.
It is
progressively measurable
(with respect to
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
) if,
for every
t\geq 0
, the map

(s,\omega)\mapsto X_{s}(\omega),\qquad(s,\omega)\in[0,t]\times\Omega,

is
\mathcal{B}([0,t])\otimes\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable.

A random time
\tau\colon\Omega\to[0,\infty]
is a
stopping time

(with respect to
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
) if
\{\tau\leq t\}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}
for all

t\geq 0
. Given a process
X
, the
stopped process
is defined by

X_{t}^{\tau}:=X_{t\wedge\tau},\qquad t\geq 0.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
. we denote by
L_{\mathbb{P}}^{p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})

the set of all (equivalence classes of)
\mathcal{F}
-measurable functions

\xi\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\int_{\Omega}\|\xi(\omega)\|^{p}\,d\mathbb{P}(\omega)<\infty.

Particularly, we denote by
L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R})

the space of locally
p
-integrable functions, that is,

L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R}):=\left\{f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}\ \text{measurable}:\int_{0}^{T}|f(t)|^{p}\,dt<\infty,\ \forall T>0\right\}.

Finally, if
f\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is continuous and

g\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is of bounded variation, we define the

Riemann–Stieltjes integral
of
f
with respect to
g
by

\int_{a}^{b}\langle f(t),dg(t)\rangle:=\lim_{\|\pi\|\to 0}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\langle f(t_{i}),g(t_{i+1})-g(t_{i})\rangle,

where the limit is taken over partitions

\pi=\{a=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}=b\}
of
[a,b]
, provided it exists.
It is well known that this limit exists for every continuous

f\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and every bounded variation function

g\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
. We refer to
[
21
]
for more details.

Integral average and like-Opial’s Lemma

For every locally integrable function

x\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define its integral average as

(2.2)

\overline{x}_{t}:=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}x(s)ds.

The following lemma is in the spirit of Opial’s Lemma for the integral average defined in (

[

.

,

belongs to

. Then there is
x^{\star}\in\mathcal{S}
such that
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\overline{x}_{t}=x^{\star}
.

The final three lemmas in this section follow from standard arguments, so we omit their proofs.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
T_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and

be a function such that

, then
\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}f(t)=0
.

.

Consider

and

. Consider an lsc function

3
The SDI Model for Monotone Inclusions and Definition of Solutions

In this section, we introduce the stochastic differential inclusion (SDI) model that will be the focus of this work, together with the notion of solution that we shall adopt.

Let
A\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a maximal monotone operator, and let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t}),\mathbb{P})
be a filtered probability space. We consider the following stochastic differential inclusion:

(3.1)

dX_{t}\in-A(X_{t})\,dt+F(t,X_{t})\,dt+\sigma(t,X_{t})\,dB_{t},\qquad X_{0}\in{\text{cl }}\operatorname{dom}A,

where
\sigma\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times\ell}
,
F\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
are mappings, and
B
is an
\ell
-dimensional Brownian motion adapted to the filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.

We now introduce the concept of solution that will be used in this work.

Definition 3.1
.

A triplet
(X,Y,M)
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued processes is called a
solution
of (
3.1
) if:

a)

X
,
Y
, and
M
are continuous progressively measurable processes with
Y_{0}=0
and
M_{0}=0
.

b)

X_{t}\in\overline{\operatorname{dom}A}
for all
t\geq 0
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s., the mapping
(\omega,t)\mapsto\sigma(\omega,t,X_{t}(\omega))
is progressively measurable, and for all
T>0

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}F(t,X_{t})dt<\infty\text{ and }\int_{0}^{T}\|\sigma(t,X_{t})\|^{2}dt<\infty\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

c)

Y
has paths of bounded variation on compact intervals. Moreover, for every pair of continuous
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
-adapted processes
(\alpha,\beta)
with
(\alpha_{t},\beta_{t})\in\operatorname{gph}A
for all
t\geq 0
, the process

(3.2)

\langle X_{t}-\alpha_{t},dY_{t}-\beta_{t}dt\rangle

defines a nonnegative measure on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Equivalently, for all
0\leq a\leq b
,

(3.3)

\int_{a}^{b}\langle X_{t}-\alpha_{t},dY_{t}\rangle\geq\int_{a}^{b}\langle X_{t}-\alpha_{t},\beta_{t}\rangle dt,\qquad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

d)

M
is a continuous local martingale such that

(3.4)

M_{t}\perp\operatorname{dom}A,\qquad\forall t\geq 0,\ \mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

e)

The decomposition

(3.5)

X_{t}=X_{0}-Y_{t}+M_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}F(s,X_{s})ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,X_{s})dB_{s},\qquad t\geq 0,

holds
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

When there is no risk of ambiguity, we will simply say that
X
is a solution to (
3.1
).

Stochastic differential inclusions has been investigated by several authors (see, e.g.,
[
23
]
and the references therein). To the best of our knowledge, however, all existing works rely on the assumption that
\operatorname{dom}A
has nonempty interior. In that case, the decomposition in (
3.5
) involves only the bounded-variation process
Y_{t}
.

Our approach relaxes this assumption by allowing the presence of the martingale term
M
in (
3.5
). From a geometric perspective,
M
captures random fluctuations in directions orthogonal to the affine hull of
\operatorname{dom}A
, induced by the noise in the system. If we denote by

L:=\operatorname{span}(\operatorname{dom}A-\operatorname{dom}A),

then
M_{t}\in L^{\perp}
. Furthermore, since
X_{t}-X_{0}\in L
, it follows from (
3.5
) that for every
v\in L^{\perp}
,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\langle Y_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}F(s,X_{s})ds,v\rangle=\langle M_{t},v\rangle+\left\langle\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,X_{s})\,dB_{s},v\right\rangle.

In (
3.6
), the left-hand side is a process of bounded variation, while the right-hand side is a local martingale. By
[
15
, Theorem 4.8]
, we deduce that
\langle Y_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}F(s,X_{s})ds,v\rangle\equiv 0
. Since
v\in L^{\perp}
was arbitrary and
L^{\perp}
is separable, we conclude that

Y_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}F(s,X_{s})ds\in L^{\perp\perp}=L.

Particularly, using (
2.1
), we conclude that

(3.7)

\displaystyle Y_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}F(s,X_{s})ds\in\aff(\operatorname{dom}A)-X_{0}\quad\forall t\geq 0,\;\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

Furthermore, multiplying (
3.5
) by
P^{\ast}
, the orthogonal projection onto
L
, we obtain

(3.8)

X_{t}=X_{0}-Y_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}F(s,X_{s})\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}P^{\ast}\sigma(s,X_{s})\,dB_{s}.

We will use this auxiliary identity throughout the paper, since it allows us to apply Itô’s formula to the process
X_{t}
.

It is worth emphasizing that if
\operatorname{dom}A
has nonempty interior, then condition (
3.4
) directly enforces
M_{t}\equiv 0
, thereby recovering the classical definition adopted in the literature (see, e.g.,
[
23
]
). On the other hand, the following example shows that in the general case the martingale component cannot be omitted.

Example 3.1
.

Consider the convex function

f(x):=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }x=0,\\
\infty,&\text{if }x\neq 0,\end{cases}

and let
A:=\partial f
, so that
\operatorname{dom}A=\{0\}
. Take
F\equiv 0
, and the diffusion coefficient
\sigma(t,x)\equiv 1
.
In this case, any solution must satisfy
X_{t}\in{\text{cl }}{\operatorname{dom}A}=\{0\}
for all
t\geq 0
. Then (
3.5
) reduces to

0=-Y_{t}+M_{t}+B_{t}.

By
[
15
, Theorem 4.8]
, this implies
M_{t}=-B_{t}
and
Y_{t}\equiv 0
.

Now, we turn into the existence and uniqueness of (
3.1
).

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
A\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a maximal monotone operator. Consider
\sigma\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times\ell}
and
F\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be mapping such that
\sigma(\cdot,\cdot,x)\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times\ell}
and
F(\cdot,\cdot,x)\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
are progressively measurable stochastic process for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Furthermore, let us suppose that there exists
\kappa\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R})
and
\zeta\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R})
such that
d\mathbb{P}\otimes dt
-almost every
(\omega,t)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+}

a)

x\mapsto F(t,x)
is continuous.

b)

\langle F(t,x)-F(t,y),x-y\rangle\leq\zeta(t)\|x-y\|^{2},\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

c)

, we have
\sup_{t\geq 0}\|X_{t}\|^{2}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, then by Fatou’s Lemma

\mathbb{E}(\|X^{\star}\|^{2})\leq\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}(\|\overline{X}_{t}\|^{2})\leq\mathbb{E}(\sup_{s\geq 0}\|X_{s}\|^{2})<\infty,

thus
X^{\star}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then, the proof of
(d)
is done.

\blacksquare

Remark 4.1
.

It is worth noting that assertions
(a)
of Theorem
4.1
can be stated when
x^{\star}\in\mathcal{S}
is replaced by a random variable
X^{\star}\colon\Omega\to\mathcal{S}
which is
\mathcal{F}_{s}
-measurable and
X^{\star}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

In the following result, we formally prove that the solution
(X_{t})
of the SDI (
1.2
) converges to a measurable function taking values in
\mathcal{S}
, provided that the solution of the deterministic differential inclusion

(4.5)

\displaystyle\dot{x}(t)\in-A(x(t)),\qquad x(0)=x_{0},

converges to an equilibrium point of
A
for any initial condition
x_{0}\in{\text{cl }}(\operatorname{dom}A)
.

It is worth recalling that the existence of solutions for this class of differential inclusions has a long history (see, e.g.,
[
4
]
and the references therein). Moreover, it is well known that such solutions do not necessarily converge to an equilibrium of
A
. Nevertheless, several authors have investigated sufficient conditions ensuring that the solution of the differential inclusion converges to an equilibrium point (see, e.g.,
[
10
,
24
]
).

Theorem 4.2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
4.1
, suppose that
A
satisfies that every trajectory of the deterministic system (
4.5
) starting from any
x_{0}\in\operatorname{cl}(\operatorname{dom}A)
converges to an equilibrium. Then there exists a random variable
X^{\star}\colon\Omega\to\mathcal{S}
such that

\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s. }\lim_{t\to\infty}X_{t}=X^{\star}\text{ and }\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}(\|X_{t}-X^{\star}\|^{2})=0.

Proof.

For every
T>0
, consider
x_{T}\colon[T,\infty[\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the unique absolutely continuous function satisfying (
4.5
), with initial condition
x_{T}(T)=X_{T}
. By our assumption, we have
\displaystyle x_{T}^{\ast}:=\lim_{t\to\infty}x_{T}(t)\in\mathcal{S}
. Moreover, if
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{S}
, we have
\|x_{T}(t)-\bar{x}\|\leq\|X_{T}-\bar{x}\|,\forall t\geq T
. Since
X_{T}\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it follows that
x_{T}(t)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
x_{T}^{\ast}\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. By using
[
23
, Proposition 6.17]
, we have

\int_{a}^{b}\langle X_{t}-x_{T}(t),dY_{t}\rangle\geq\int_{a}^{b}\langle X_{t}-x_{T}(t),-x^{\prime}_{T}(t)\rangle dt,\forall a\geq b\geq T.

The above conclusion holds
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.. Moreover, by using Itô’s Lemma we have for
t\geq T

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|X_{t}-x_{T}(t)\|^{2}=

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{t}\langle X_{s}-x_{T}(s),-dY_{s}\rangle+\int_{T}^{t}\langle X_{s}-x_{T}(s),-x_{T}^{\prime}(s)\rangle ds

\displaystyle+\int_{T}^{t}\langle X_{s}-x_{T}(s),P^{\ast}\sigma(s,X_{s})dB_{s}\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{T}^{t}\|P^{\ast}\sigma(s,X_{s})\|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{t}\langle X_{s}-x_{T}(s),P^{\ast}\sigma(s,X_{s})dB_{s}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{T}^{t}\sigma_{\infty}(s)^{2}ds.

Taking expectation, we conclude

\forall t\geq T:\mathbb{E}(\|X_{t}-x_{T}(t)\|^{2})\leq\int_{T}^{t}\mathbb{E}(\sigma_{\infty}(s)^{2})ds.

Since the expectation of martingale part of the right hand side is zero. On the other hand, observe that if
t\geq T

d(X_{t};\mathcal{S})^{2}\leq\|X_{t}-x^{\ast}_{T}\|^{2}\leq 2\|X_{t}-x_{T}(t)\|^{2}+2\|x_{T}(t)-x_{T}^{\ast}\|^{2},

where
d(X_{t};\mathcal{S})
denotes the distance form
X_{t}
to
\mathcal{S}
. By taking expectation, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(d(X_{t};\mathcal{S})^{2})

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}(\|X_{t}-x_{T}(t)\|^{2})+2\mathbb{E}(\|x_{T}(t)-x_{T}^{\ast}\|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{T}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}(\sigma_{\infty}(s)^{2})ds+2\mathbb{E}(\|x_{T}(t)-x_{T}^{\ast}\|^{2}).

Since
\forall t\geq T:\|x_{T}(t)-x_{T}^{\ast}\|^{2}\leq\|X_{T}-x_{T}^{\ast}\|^{2}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
and
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\|x_{T}(t)-x_{T}^{\ast}\|^{2}=0
, by using dominated convergence theorem we have
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}(\|x_{T}(t)-x_{T}^{\ast}\|^{2})=0
. Therefore,

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}(d(X_{t};\mathcal{S})^{2})\leq 2\int_{T}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}(\sigma_{\infty}(s)^{2})ds.

Since
T>0
was arbitrary and
\sigma_{\infty}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, we conclude that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}(d(X_{t};\mathcal{S})^{2})=0.

It follows that there is
(t_{n})\to\infty
such that
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}d(X_{t_{n}};\mathcal{S})^{2}=0
. Take the set
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
such that for all
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
,
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}d(X_{t_{n}}(\omega);\mathcal{S})^{2}=0
and for all
\displaystyle x^{\star}\in\mathcal{S}:\lim_{t\to\infty}\|X_{t}(\omega)-x^{\star}\|
exists, we know that
\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{\prime})=1
. Fix
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
, since
(X_{t_{n}}(\omega))
is bounded, we have up to a subsequence
X_{t_{n}}(\omega)\to x^{\star}
and given that
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}d(X_{t_{n}}(\omega);\mathcal{S})=0
, we have
x^{\star}\in\mathcal{S}
. Since
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\|X_{t}(\omega)-x^{\star}\|
exists, we conclude that
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\|X_{t}(\omega)-x^{\star}\|=0
. Since
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
was arbitrary, we conclude that
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
\displaystyle X^{\star}:=\lim_{t\to\infty}X_{t}\in\mathcal{S}
. Finally, by Fatou’s lemma we have
\mathbb{E}(\|X^{\star}\|^{2})\leq\mathbb{E}(\sup_{t\geq 0}\|X_{t}\|^{2})
and by Theorem
4.1
we conclude that
X^{\star}\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and by dominated convergence theorem we conclude that
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}(\|X_{t}-X^{\star}\|^{2})=0
.

\blacksquare

Perhaps the most important case in which global convergence of solutions to (
4.5
) applies is the
subgradient evolution problem, that is,
A=\partial f
, where
f
is a proper, convex, and lower semicontinuous
function; see, for instance,
[
4
]
. Nonetheless, an equally important and more general setting
is provided by the notion of
demipositive
maximal monotone operators, which in particular covers
the case of subdifferentials of convex functions, cocoercive operators, strongly maximal monotone operators among others (see, e.g.,
[
26
, Proposition 6.2]
).

We recall that a maximal monotone operator
A
is said to be
demipositive
if there exists

w\in\mathcal{S}
such that, for every sequence
(u_{n})\subset\operatorname{dom}A
converging to
u
, and every
bounded sequence
(v_{n})
with
v_{n}\in A(u_{n})
, the implication

\langle v_{n},u_{n}-w\rangle\to 0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad u\in\mathcal{S}

holds. This notion was introduced in
[
10
]
to establish the convergence of solutions of
the deterministic system (
4.5
) to equilibrium points of the maximal monotone operator.

Corollary 4.3
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
4.1
suppose that
A
is demipositive. Then, there is some random variable
X^{\star}\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s. }\lim_{t\to\infty}X_{t}=X^{\star}\text{ and }\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}(\|X_{t}-X^{\star}\|^{2})=0.

Proof.

As a consequence of
[
9
, Corollary III.3.1]
, the system (
4.5
) has an unique solution for all
x_{0}\in{\text{cl }}(\operatorname{dom}A)
. The result follows from
[
26
, Theorem 6.3]
and Theorem
4.2
.

\blacksquare

Remark 4.2
.

The above corollary ensures that when
A
is either strongly maximal monotone or cocoercive, the conclusion of Theorem
4.2
holds since in both cases
A
is demipositive. Particularly, Corollary
4.3
generalizes the results obtained in
[
18
, Theorem 5.1 & 5.2]
, where only cocoercive operators where considered.

The next results provides an error bound in terms of a suitably defined gap function associated with the maximally monotone operator. Consider the Brézis-Haraux function

H_{A}(x,y)=\sup\{\langle x-u,v-y\rangle:(u,v)\in\operatorname{gph}(A)\}.

It is well known
[
1
]
that
H_{A}
can be interpreted as an exterior penalty for
\operatorname{gph}(A)
, in the sense that
H_{A}(x,u)=0
if and only if
(x,u)\in\operatorname{gph}(A)
. Moreover, it holds true that

H_{A}(x,y)\geq||x-(\text{Id}+A)^{-1}(x+y)||^{2}\geq 0\qquad\forall(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Given these properties, a standard merit function for solving the monotone inclusion problem is provided in terms of the gap function
G_{A}(x):=H_{A}(x,0)
, or its localized version

(4.6)

G_{A}(x|K)=\sup\left\{\langle x-u,v\rangle:u\in\operatorname{dom}(A)\cap K,\;v\in Au\right\},

where
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a given compact set. In
[
7
]
, the authors show that
\Gamma(x)
is the minimal translation invariant gap function for the problem of finding an element of
A^{-1}(0)
. The localized version defined in (
4.6
) is used to obtain a well-defined merit function for equilibrium problems having an unbounded domain (see e.g.
[
22
]
).

Proposition 4.4
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
4.1
, consider the process
X
solving (
1.2
). Then, for all compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have that

\mathbb{E}[G_{A}(\overline{X}_{t}|K)]\leq\mathcal{O}(1/t).

In addition, assume that
A
is
\rho
-strongly maximal monotone with
\mathcal{S}=\{x^{\star}\}
, then

(4.7)

\mathbb{E}(\|X_{t}-x^{\star}\|^{2})\leq e^{-2\rho t}\mathbb{E}(\|X_{0}-x^{\star}\|^{2})+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2\rho(t-s)}\mathbb{E}(\sigma_{\infty}(s)^{2})ds.

Proof.

Take a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, note that in the proof of Theorem
1.2
it is proved that for all
(u,v)\in\operatorname{gph}A
and
x^{\star}\in\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle\left\langle v,\overline{X}_{t}-u\right\rangle\leq\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\langle X_{s}-x^{\star},dY_{s}\rangle+\frac{1}{t}\langle u-x^{\star},X_{t}-X_{0}-N_{t}\rangle.

where
N_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(t,X_{t})dB_{t}
. Then, taking the supremum and then expectation it yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[G_{A}(\overline{X}_{t}|K)]\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle X_{s}-x^{\star},dY_{s}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{t}(\sup_{w\in K}\|w\|+\|x^{\star}\|)(2\mathbb{E}(\sup_{s\geq 0}\|X_{s}\|^{2})^{1/2}+\sup_{s\geq 0}\mathbb{E}(\|N_{s}\|^{2})^{1/2}),

which gets the desired error bound.

Now, define the function
\psi(t):=\mathbb{E}(\|X_{t}-x^{\star}\|^{2})
. By Fatou’s Lemma,
\psi
is lsc. According to Theorem
4.1
we have that for all
t,s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
t\geq s

\displaystyle\psi(t)

\displaystyle\leq\psi(s)-2\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{s}^{t}\langle X_{\tau}-x^{\star},dY_{\tau}\rangle\right)+\int_{s}^{t}\mathbb{E}(\sigma_{\infty}(\tau)^{2})d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\psi(s)-2\rho\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{s}^{t}\|X_{\tau}-x^{\star}\|^{2}d\tau\right)+\int_{s}^{t}\mathbb{E}(\sigma_{\infty}(\tau)^{2})d\tau

\displaystyle=\psi(s)-2\rho\int_{s}^{t}\psi(\tau)d\tau+\int_{s}^{t}\mathbb{E}(\sigma_{\infty}(\tau)^{2})d\tau.

By using Lemma
2.5
we have that

\psi(t)\leq e^{-2\rho t}\psi(0)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2\rho(t-s)}\mathbb{E}(\sigma_{\infty}(s)^{2})ds,

which proves (
4.7
).

\blacksquare

5
Stochastic Subdifferential Inclusion

In this section, we consider a proper, convex, and lower semicontinuous function

\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
, and study the asymptotic behavior of the solution to the SDI (
1.2
) in the particular case
A=\partial\varphi
. This analysis builds on the previous results and in this setting we can derive additional convergence estimates, including estimates on the values of
\varphi
along the trajectory. For related results in the smooth convex setting, we refer the reader to
[
18
,
20
]
.

First, let us recall the following lemma presented in
[
23
, Proposition 6.36]
.

Proposition 5.1
.

For all continuous and adapted process
\alpha\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it holds
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

\forall a,b\in\mathbb{R}_{+},a\leq b:\int_{a}^{b}\varphi(\alpha_{t})dt\geq\int_{a}^{b}\varphi(X_{t})dt+\int_{a}^{b}\langle\alpha_{t}-X_{t},dY_{t}\rangle.

We now state the main result on convergence in the setting of stochastic subdifferential inclusions.

Theorem 5.2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
4.1
, suppose that
A=\partial\varphi
for given proper, convex, and lower semicontinuous function

\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
. Then,

(a)

\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}(\varphi(X_{s})-\min\varphi)ds\right)<\infty
. Consequently,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

\int_{0}^{\infty}(\varphi(X_{t})-\min\varphi)dt<\infty\text{ and }\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\varphi(X_{t})=\min\varphi.

(b)

There is a random variable
X^{\star}\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}X_{t}=X^{\star}
,
\mathbb{P}(X^{\star}\in\mathcal{S})=1
and
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}(\|X_{t}-X^{\star}\|^{2})=0
.

(c)

Suppose that

(5.1)

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}(\|\partial^{0}\varphi(X_{t})\|^{2})<\infty,

then
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}(\varphi(X_{t}))=\min\varphi
.

(d)

It holds
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.,
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\varphi(\overline{X}_{t})=\min\varphi
. Furthermore,

\mathbb{E}(\varphi(\overline{X}_{t})-\min\varphi)\leq\frac{1}{2t}\mathbb{E}(d(X_{0};\mathcal{S})^{2})+\frac{1}{2t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}(\sigma_{\infty}(s)^{2})ds.

(e)

Suppose that
\varphi
is
\mu
-strongly convex, then
\mathcal{S}=\{x^{\star}\}
and we have

(5.2)

\mathbb{E}(\|\overline{X}_{t}-x^{\star}\|^{2})\leq\frac{1}{\mu t}\mathbb{E}(\|X_{0}-x^{\star}\|)+\frac{1}{\mu t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}(\sigma_{\infty}(s)^{2})ds.

Proof.

In this setting, the set of equilibrium points is given by
\mathcal{S}=\operatorname{argmin}\varphi
. Let us take
\widehat{X}:=\proj_{\mathcal{S}}(X_{0})
. Observe that
\widehat{X}\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and it is
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-measurable, by using Proposition
5.1
we have
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

\int_{0}^{t}(\varphi(X_{s})-\min\varphi)ds\leq\int_{0}^{t}\langle X_{s}-\widehat{X},dY_{s}\rangle.

Taking expectation, and by using Theorem
4.1
(see Remark
4.1
) we have

(5.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}(\varphi(X_{s})-\min\varphi)ds\right)\leq\mathbb{E}(d(X_{0};\mathcal{S})^{2})+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}(\sigma_{\infty}(s)^{2})ds.

By Beppo Levi’s convergence Theorem we have that

\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}(\varphi(X_{s})-\min\varphi)ds\right)\leq\mathbb{E}(d(X_{0};\mathcal{S})^{2})+\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}(\sigma_{\infty}(s)^{2})ds<\infty.

In particular, we have that
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
\int_{0}^{\infty}(\varphi(X_{s})-\min\varphi)ds<\infty
, thus by using Lemma
2.3
we have
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\varphi(X_{t})=\min\varphi
, then
(a)
is proved.

To check
(b)
, we note that
\partial\varphi
is demipositive (see, e.g.,
[
26
, Proposition 6.2]
), then by Corollary
4.3
,
(b)
holds.

To prove
(c)
, we simply note that the condition
\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}(\|\partial^{0}\varphi(X_{t})\|^{2})<\infty
implies that there is
T>0
such that for a.e.
t\geq T
,
\partial^{0}\varphi(X_{t})
is well-defined. Hence, for a.e.
t\geq T

\varphi(X_{t})-\varphi(x^{\star})\leq\langle\partial^{0}\varphi(X_{t}),X_{t}-x^{\star}\rangle\leq\|\partial^{0}\varphi(X_{t})\|\|X_{t}-x^{\star}\|

By taking expectation and using Hölder inequality we have

\mathbb{E}(\varphi(X_{t})-\min\varphi)\leq\mathbb{E}(\|\partial^{0}\varphi(X_{t})\|^{2})^{1/2}\mathbb{E}(\|X_{t}-x^{\star}\|^{2})^{1/2}.

Thus,
(c)
holds by taking
t\to\infty
since
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}(\|X_{t}-x^{\star}\|^{2})=0
.
On the other hand, by Lemma
2.4
, for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}

\varphi(\overline{X}_{t})-\min\varphi\leq\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}(\varphi(X_{s})-\min\varphi)ds\leq\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}(\varphi(X_{s})-\min\varphi)ds.

It follows that
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\varphi(\overline{X}_{t})=\min\varphi
. Moreover, dividing by
t
the inequality (
5.3
) yields

\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\varphi(X_{s})ds-\min\varphi\right)\leq\frac{1}{2t}\mathbb{E}(d(X_{0};\mathcal{S})^{2})+\frac{1}{2t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}(\sigma_{\infty}(s)^{2})ds

and by Lemma
2.4
, we conclude that

(5.4)

\mathbb{E}(\varphi(\overline{X}_{t})-\min\varphi)\leq\frac{1}{2t}\mathbb{E}(d(X_{0};\mathcal{S})^{2})+\frac{1}{2t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}(\sigma_{\infty}(s)^{2})ds.

Hence,
(d)
holds. Finally, suppose that
\varphi
is
\mu
-strongly convex, then
\mathcal{S}=\{x^{\star}\}
and

\frac{\mu}{2}\|\overline{X}_{t}-x^{\star}\|^{2}+\varphi(x^{\star})\leq\varphi(\overline{X}_{t}).

Taking expectation and using inequality (
5.4
) we obtain directly (
5.2
), thus
(e)
is done.

\blacksquare

Remark 5.1
.

A sufficient condition to ensure (
5.1
) is to assume that the function
\varphi
satisfies
\operatorname{dom}\partial\varphi
is closed and there is
c>0
such that
\|\partial^{0}\varphi(x)\|\leq c(\|x\|+1),\forall x\in\operatorname{dom}\partial\varphi
.

We close this section with establishing a concentration bound of the ergodic averaged trajectory in terms of the objective function value gap

\Delta\varphi(x):=\varphi(x)-\min\varphi.

Let
x^{\star}
be a solution, and define
E_{x^{\star}}(t):=\frac{1}{2}||X(t)-x^{\star}||^{2}
. Itô’s formula yields

\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\Delta\varphi(X(s))ds\leq\frac{E_{x^{\star}}(0)}{t}+\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\langle X_{s}-x^{\star},P^{\ast}\sigma(s,X_{s})dB_{s}\rangle+\frac{1}{2t}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{\infty}(s)^{2}ds.

Applying Jensen’s inequality, this yields

\Delta\varphi(\overline{X}_{t})\leq\frac{E_{x^{\star}}(0)}{t}+\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\langle X_{s}-x^{\star},P^{\ast}\sigma(s,X_{s})dB_{s}\rangle+\frac{1}{2t}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{\infty}(s)^{2}ds.

To control the martingale part in the middle of the above expression, we introduce Wiener process
(W_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, defined by

dW_{t}=-P^{\ast}\sigma(t,X_{t})dB_{t},\quad W_{0}=X_{0}.

Applying Itô’s formula to the process
t\mapsto\pi(t)=\frac{1}{2}||W_{t}-x^{\star}||^{2}
, we obtain

\int_{0}^{t}\langle W_{s}-x^{\star},P^{\ast}\sigma(s,X_{s})dB_{s}\rangle\leq E_{x^{\star}}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{2}_{\infty}(s)ds.

This allows us to decompose the continuous martingale term as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\langle X_{s}-x^{\star},P^{\ast}\sigma(s,X_{s})dB_{s}\rangle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\langle X_{s}-W_{s},P^{\ast}\sigma(s,X_{s})dB_{s}\rangle

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\langle W_{s}-x^{\star},P^{\ast}\sigma(s,X_{s})dB_{s}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle E_{x^{\star}}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{2}_{\infty}(s)ds+U_{t}

where
U_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}\langle X_{s}-W_{s},P^{\ast}\sigma(s,X_{s})dB_{s}\rangle
. Hence,

\Delta\varphi(\overline{X}_{t})\leq\frac{1}{t}(S_{t}+U_{t}),\text{where }S_{t}:=2E_{x_{{}^{*}}}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{2}_{\infty}(s)ds.

For
\varepsilon>0
, we thus see

\mathbb{P}(\Delta\varphi(\overline{X}_{t})\geq\varepsilon)\leq\mathbb{P}(U_{t}\geq t\varepsilon-S(t)).

This estimate is the basis for the following large-deviations bound.

Theorem 5.3
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
5.2
, consider
x^{\star}
an arbitrary solution. The, for all
\varepsilon>0
and
t>0
, we have

(5.5)

\mathbb{P}\left(\Delta\varphi(\bar{X}_{t})\geq Q_{0}(t)+\varepsilon Q_{1}(t)\right)\leq\exp(-\varepsilon^{2}/4),

where

(5.6)

\displaystyle Q_{0}(t)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{t}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{2}_{\infty}(s)ds+2E_{x^{\star}}(0)\right),

(5.7)

\displaystyle Q_{1}(t)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{t}\sqrt{\delta(t)},\text{ where }\delta(t):={\mathbb{E}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{2}_{\infty}(s)\cdot||W_{s}-X_{s}||^{2}ds\right).

Proof.

Let be
\rho_{t}:=[U,U]_{t}
the quadratic variation process of the continuous semimartingale
\{(W_{t},\mathcal{F}_{t});t\geq 0\}
. We have

\displaystyle\rho_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\|(P^{\ast}\sigma(s,X_{s}))^{\top}(W_{s}-X_{s})\|^{2}ds\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|\sigma(s,X_{s})\|^{2}\cdot\|W_{s}-X_{s}\|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{2}_{\infty}(s)\|W_{s}-X_{s}\|^{2}ds=:\hat{\delta}(t),

it follows that
\delta(t)=\mathbb{E}(\hat{\delta}(t))
. We next bound the stochastic exponential
t\mapsto\exp(\theta U_{t}),\theta\in\mathbb{R}
via the Cauchy-Schwarz-Bunyakowski inequality as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\exp(\theta U_{t})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp(\theta U_{t}-b\rho_{t})\exp(b\rho_{t})\right]\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[\exp(2\theta U_{t}-2b\rho_{t})\right]}\cdot\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[\exp(2b\rho_{t})\right]}.

Setting
b=\theta^{2}
, the expression inside the first expected values is just the stochastic exponential of the process
t\mapsto 2\theta U_{t}
. Hence,
t\mapsto\exp(2\theta U_{t})
is a submartingale, and we conclude

(5.8)

\mathbb{E}\left[\exp(\theta U_{t})\right]\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[\exp(2\theta^{2}\rho_{t})\right]}\leq{\exp(\theta^{2}\mathbb{E}(\rho_{t}))\leq\exp(\theta^{2}\delta(t)).}

where is used Jensen’s inequality. It follows, for
a>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[U_{t}\geq a]

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-\theta a)\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\exp(\theta U_{t})\right]\leq\exp(-\theta a)\exp(\theta^{2}\delta(t))=\exp(-\theta a+\theta^{2}\delta(t)),

where the first inequality is just Markov’s inequality, and the second line uses (
5.8
). Minimizing the expression on the right-hand side with respect to
\theta
, yields
\theta=\frac{a}{2\delta(t)}
, resulting in the upper bound

\mathbb{P}[U_{t}\geq a]\leq\exp\left(-\frac{a^{2}}{4\delta(t)}\right)

From these computations, we deduce that

\mathbb{P}(U_{t}\geq a)\leq\exp(-\varepsilon^{2}/4),\text{whenever $a\geq\varepsilon\sqrt{\delta(t)}$.}

This leads us the inequalities

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\Delta\varphi(\bar{X}_{t})\geq Q_{0}(t)+\varepsilon Q_{1}(t)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(U_{t}\geq tQ_{0}(t)+t\varepsilon Q_{1}(t)-S_{t})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(U_{t}\geq\varepsilon\sqrt{\delta(t)})\leq\exp(-\varepsilon^{2}/4),

which concludes the proof.

\blacksquare

6
Tikhonov Regularization

The final section of this paper concerns a stochastic Tikhonov regularization of the SDI
(
1.2
), given by (
1.3
), where the parameter function

\varepsilon\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}\setminus\{0\}
satisfies

\lim_{t\to\infty}\varepsilon(t)=0
.
The additional drift term
-\varepsilon(t)X_{t}
plays the role of a Tikhonov regularizer for
(
1.2
). Such perturbations have attracted considerable interest, as they often
improve the asymptotic behavior of trajectories (see, e.g.,

[
13
,
2
]
).
The result below shows that every trajectory of (
1.3
) converges to the
minimum-norm zero of
A
, namely
\proj_{\mathcal{S}}(0)
, without imposing further
structural assumptions on
A
.

For every
\eta>0
, we define
x_{\eta}:=J_{\frac{1}{\eta}A}(0)
. By
[
5
, Theorem 23.48]
,
we know that
\displaystyle\lim_{\eta\to 0}x_{\eta}=\proj_{\mathcal{S}}(0)=:x^{\star}
. Moreover, it
can be shown that the mapping
\eta\mapsto x_{\eta}
is locally Lipschitz on

\mathbb{R}_{+}\setminus\{0\}
and that, almost everywhere,

(6.1)

\left\|\frac{d}{d\eta}x_{\eta}\right\|\leq\frac{\|x^{\star}\|}{\eta}

(see, e.g.,
[
2
, p. 529]
).

Recently,
[
19
, Theorem 14]
established a related result in the setting
where
A=\nabla F+\partial g
on a Hilbert space, with
F
a convex function of class

\mathcal{C}^{1,+}
and
g
a continuous convex function. Their analysis requires the curve

t\mapsto x_{\varepsilon(t)}
to satisfy a certain compatibility condition with the parameter function

\varepsilon
. In contrast, we do not impose any assumption on the trajectory
t\mapsto x_{\varepsilon(t)}
;
instead, we only assume the standard conditions on
\varepsilon
, which allows us to recover and extend
the deterministic result of
[
13
, Proposition 5]
to the stochastic
setting in finite dimension. Formally, we need to compare the stochstic dynamic give by the solution of (
1.3
) to its deterministic counterpart. Formally, for the initial condition
x_{0}\in\operatorname{dom}A
, we take the absolutely continuous trajectory
x\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
which satisfies

(6.2)

x^{\prime}(t)\in-A(x(t))-\varepsilon(t)x(t),\ x(0)=x_{0}.

On the parameter function
\varepsilon
, we now assume that it does not depend on
\Omega
, and only satisfies

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
A\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a maximal monotone operator with
\mathcal{S}\neq\emptyset
. Consider
\sigma\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times\ell}
satisfying Assumption
1
and consider a parameter function
\varepsilon:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}

such that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\varepsilon(t)=0
and
\int_{0}^{\infty}\varepsilon(t)dt=\infty
. Let
X_{0}
be an
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-measurable, square-integrable random variable, and consider the solution
x
of (
6.2
) starting from any
x_{0}\in\operatorname{dom}A
. Then, the Tikhonov regularized SDI (
1.3
) has an unique solution
X
and it satisfies that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}(\sup_{s\geq t}\|X_{s}-x(s)\|^{2})=0.

Proof.

First, the existence of the solution
(X,Y,M)
is given by Theorem
3.2
by symply consider
F(t,x)=\varepsilon(t)x
. Let us consider
L:=\operatorname{span}(\operatorname{dom}A-\operatorname{dom}A)
by
P^{\ast}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to L
the linear proyection onto
L
. Second, by Itô’s Lemma we have

(6.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|X_{t}-x(t)\|^{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|X_{s}-x(s)\|^{2}+\int_{s}^{t}\langle X_{\tau}-x(\tau),dX_{\tau}-dx(\tau)\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\|P^{\ast}\sigma(\tau,X_{\tau})\|^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|X_{s}-x(s)\|^{2}+\int_{s}^{t}\langle X_{\tau}-x(\tau),P^{\ast}\sigma(\tau,X_{\tau})dB_{\tau}\rangle

\displaystyle-\int_{s}^{t}\varepsilon(\tau)\|X_{\tau}-x(\tau)\|^{2}d\tau+\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\sigma_{\infty}(\tau)^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{s}^{t}\langle X_{\tau}-x(\tau),-dY_{\tau}+(-x^{\prime}(\tau)-\varepsilon(\tau)x(\tau))d\tau\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|X_{s}-x(s)\|^{2}+\int_{s}^{t}\langle X_{\tau}-x(\tau),P^{\ast}\sigma(\tau,X_{\tau})dB_{\tau}\rangle

\displaystyle-\int_{s}^{t}\varepsilon(\tau)\|X_{\tau}-x(\tau)\|^{2}d\tau+\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\sigma_{\infty}(\tau)^{2}d\tau.

where we have used the monotonicity by virtue of
[
23
, Proposition 6.17]
. Then, taking expectation we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\|X_{t}-x(t)\|^{2})\leq\mathbb{E}(\|X_{s}-x(s)\|^{2})-2\int_{s}^{t}\varepsilon(\tau)\mathbb{E}(\|X_{\tau}-x(\tau)\|^{2})d\tau+\int_{s}^{t}\mathbb{E}(\sigma_{\infty}(\tau)^{2})d\tau.

Then, by using Lemma
2.5
, for all
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
t\geq t_{0}

\displaystyle{\mathbb\displaystyle E}(\|X_{t}-x(t)\|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(-2\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\varepsilon(\tau)d\tau\right)\left[\mathbb{E}(\|X_{t_{0}}-x(t_{0})\|^{2})+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\exp\left(2\int_{t_{0}}^{s}\varepsilon(\tau)d\tau\right)\mathbb{E}(\sigma_{\infty}(s)^{2})ds\right]

Replacing
t_{0}=0
in the above inequality yields that
\mathbb{E}(\|X_{t}-x(t)\|^{2})<\infty,\forall t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Now, take
\eta>0
, then take
t_{0}
big enough such that
\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}(\sigma_{\infty}(\tau)^{2})d\tau\leq\eta
, it follows that

\mathbb{E}(\|X_{t}-x(t)\|^{2})\leq\exp\left(-2\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\varepsilon(\tau)d\tau\right)\mathbb{E}(\|X_{t_{0}}-x(t_{0})\|^{2})+\eta,\forall t\geq t_{0}.

It follows that
\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}(\|X_{t}-x(t)\|^{2})\leq\eta
, and given that
\eta>0
is arbitrary, we conclude that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}(\|X_{t}-x(t)\|^{2})=0.

Now, fix
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, then define

{M}_{t}:=\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\langle X_{\tau}-x(\tau),P^{\ast}\sigma(\tau,X_{\tau})dB_{\tau}\rangle

Now, consider the stopping time
\tau_{n}:=\inf\{\,t\geq r:\|X_{t}\|\geq n\,\}
. It follows by (
6.3
) that

(6.4)

\frac{1}{2}\|X_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}-x(t\wedge\tau_{n})\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|X_{t_{0}}-x(t_{0})\|^{2}+\sup_{t\geq t_{0}}|{M}_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}|+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}\sigma_{\infty}(\tau)^{2}d\tau.

By the same argument as in (
4.3
), there exists a constant
\mathcal{K}>0
such that, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(6.5)

\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{t\geq t_{0}}|{M}_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}|\Big)\leq\frac{1}{4}\,\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{t\geq t_{0}}\|X_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}-x(t\wedge\tau_{n})\|^{2}\Big)+\mathcal{K}\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}(\sigma_{\infty}(\tau)^{2})\,d\tau.

Thus, taking expectation in (
6.4
) and using (
6.5
) yields

\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{t\geq t_{0}}\|X_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}-x(t\wedge\tau_{n})\|^{2}\Big)\leq 2\mathbb{E}(\|X_{t_{0}}-x(t_{0})\|^{2})+(4\mathcal{K}+2)\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}(\sigma_{\infty}(\tau)^{2})d\tau.

Sending
n\to\infty
in the above inequality, we can see that
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}(\sup_{s\geq t}\|X_{s}-x(s)\|^{2})=0
.

\blacksquare

Although the proof of Theorem
6.1
is based on estimating the discrepancy between
X_{t}
and an auxiliary trajectory
x(t)
, the bounds obtained in the argument are not sharp enough to provide a quantitative rate for
\|X_{t}-x(t)\|
. As a consequence, even if one could derive a rate for
\|x(t)-x^{\star}\|
, this information cannot be directly converted into a rate of convergence for
\|X_{t}-x^{\star}\|
, nor for its expected value.

For this reason, by imposing a stronger assumption on the parameter function
\varepsilon
, it becomes possible to compare the solution of the SDI (
1.3
) with the curve
t\mapsto x_{\varepsilon(t)}
, leading to a more explicit rate of convergence.

Theorem 6.2
.

In the setting of Theorem
6.1
let us suppose that the parameter function
\varepsilon
is
\mathcal{C}^{1}
and satisfies
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\left|\varepsilon^{\prime}(t)\right|}{\varepsilon(t)^{2}}=0.
Then, the solution
X
of the Tikhonov regularized SDI (
1.3
) satisfies that there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
r\geq 0

(6.6)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{s\geq r}\|X_{s}-x_{\varepsilon(s)}\|^{2}\Big)\leq C\left(\sup_{t\geq r}\frac{|\varepsilon^{\prime}(t)|}{\varepsilon(t)^{2}}+\int_{r}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}(\sigma_{\infty}(\tau)^{2})\,d\tau\right).

Proof.

Let us consider
L:=\operatorname{span}(\operatorname{dom}A-\operatorname{dom}A)
by
P^{\ast}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to L
the linear proyection onto
L
. By Itô’s Lemma, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|X_{t}-x_{\varepsilon(t)}\|^{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|X_{s}-x_{\varepsilon(s)}\|^{2}+\int_{s}^{t}\langle X_{\tau}-x_{\varepsilon(\tau)},-dY_{\tau}\rangle

\displaystyle-\int_{s}^{t}\varepsilon(\tau)\langle X_{\tau}-x_{\varepsilon(\tau)},X_{\tau}\rangle\,d\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{s}^{t}\langle X_{\tau}-x_{\varepsilon(\tau)},-\frac{d}{d\tau}x_{\varepsilon(\tau)}\rangle\,d\tau+\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\|P^{\ast}\sigma(\tau,X_{\tau})\|^{2}\,d\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{s}^{t}\langle X_{\tau}-x_{\varepsilon(\tau)},P^{\ast}\sigma(\tau,X_{\tau})\,dB_{\tau}\rangle.

Since
-\varepsilon(\tau)x_{\varepsilon(\tau)}\in A(x_{\varepsilon(\tau)})
and
\tau\mapsto x_{\varepsilon(\tau)}
is continuous, we have

\int_{s}^{t}\langle X_{\tau}-x_{\varepsilon(\tau)},-dY_{\tau}\rangle\leq\int_{s}^{t}\varepsilon(\tau)\langle X_{\tau}-x_{\varepsilon(\tau)},x_{\varepsilon(\tau)}\rangle\,d\tau.

Now, fix
r>0
and take
t,s>r
. Then,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|X_{t}-x_{\varepsilon(t)}\|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|X_{s}-x_{\varepsilon(s)}\|^{2}-\int_{s}^{t}\varepsilon(\tau)\|X_{\tau}-x_{\varepsilon(\tau)}\|^{2}\,d\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{s}^{t}\|X_{\tau}-x_{\varepsilon(\tau)}\|\,\Big\|\frac{d}{d\tau}x_{\varepsilon(\tau)}\Big\|\,d\tau+\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\sigma_{\infty}(\tau)^{2}\,d\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{s}^{t}\langle X_{\tau}-x_{\varepsilon(\tau)},P^{\ast}\sigma(\tau,X_{\tau})\,dB_{\tau}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|X_{s}-x_{\varepsilon(s)}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{s}^{t}\varepsilon(\tau)\|X_{\tau}-x_{\varepsilon(\tau)}\|\,\big(\mathcal{L}_{r}-\|X_{\tau}-x_{\varepsilon(\tau)}\|\big)\,d\tau

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\sigma_{\infty}(\tau)^{2}\,d\tau+\int_{s}^{t}\langle X_{\tau}-x_{\varepsilon(\tau)},P^{\ast}\sigma(\tau,X_{\tau})\,dB_{\tau}\rangle,

where we have used (
6.1
), and

\mathcal{L}_{r}:=\|x^{\star}\|\sup_{t\geq r}\frac{|\varepsilon^{\prime}(t)|}{\varepsilon(t)^{2}}.

Define

\mathcal{O}_{r}=\{\,t>r:\|X_{t}-x_{\varepsilon(t)}\|>\mathcal{L}_{r}\,\}.

Since
t\mapsto\|X_{t}-x_{\varepsilon(t)}\|
is continuous,
\mathcal{O}_{r}
is open, then there is a countable family of pairwise disjoints open intervals
\{]a_{i},b_{i}[\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\mathcal{O}_{r}=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}]a_{i},b_{i}[
.
For
t\notin\mathcal{O}_{r}
we have
\|X_{t}-x_{\varepsilon(t)}\|\leq\mathcal{L}_{r}
, while for
t\in]a_{i},b_{i}[
the condition
\|X_{a_{i}}-x_{\varepsilon(a_{i})}\|\leq\mathcal{L}_{r}
implies

\frac{1}{2}\|X_{t}-x_{\varepsilon(t)}\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{r}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{r}^{t}\sigma_{\infty}(\tau)^{2}\,d\tau+\int_{a_{i}}^{t}\langle X_{\tau}-x_{\varepsilon(\tau)},P^{\ast}\sigma(\tau,X_{\tau})\,dB_{\tau}\rangle.

Moreover, for all
t>a_{i}
,

\int_{a_{i}}^{t}\langle X_{\tau}-x_{\varepsilon(\tau)},P^{\ast}\sigma(\tau,X_{\tau})\,dB_{\tau}\rangle\leq 2\sup_{s\in[r,t]}|{M}_{s}|,

where

{M}_{s}:=\int_{r}^{s}\langle X_{\tau}-x_{\varepsilon(\tau)},P^{\ast}\sigma(\tau,X_{\tau})\,dB_{\tau}\rangle.

Thus, for all
t>r
,

(6.7)

\frac{1}{2}\|X_{t}-x_{\varepsilon(t)}\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{r}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{r}^{t}\sigma_{\infty}(\tau)^{2}\,d\tau+2\sup_{s\in[r,t]}|{M}_{s}|.

Now, consider the stopping time
\tau_{n}:=\inf\{\,t\geq r:\|X_{t}\|\geq n\,\}
. It follows by (
6.7
) that

(6.8)

\frac{1}{2}\|X_{t\wedge\tau_{n}}-x_{\varepsilon(t\wedge\tau_{n})}\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{r}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{r}^{\infty}\sigma_{\infty}(\tau)^{2}\,d\tau+2\sup_{s\geq r}|{M}_{s\wedge\tau_{n}}|.

By the same argument as in (
4.3
), there exists a constant
\mathcal{K}>0
such that, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(6.9)

\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{s\geq r}|{M}_{s\wedge\tau_{n}}|\Big)\leq\frac{1}{8}\,\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{s\geq r}\|X_{s\wedge\tau_{n}}-x_{\varepsilon(s\wedge\tau_{n})}\|^{2}\Big)+2\mathcal{K}\int_{r}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}(\sigma_{\infty}(\tau)^{2})\,d\tau.

Taking expectation in (
6.8
), and then using (

such that, for all

,

\frac{1}{4}\,\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{s\geq r}\|X_{s\wedge\tau_{n}}-x_{\varepsilon(s\wedge\tau_{n})}\|^{2}\Big)\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{r}^{2}+C\int_{r}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}(\sigma_{\infty}(\tau)^{2})\,d\tau.

\blacksquare

Let us emphasize that the estimate obtained in (
6.6
) shows that the convergence rate of the stochastic trajectory
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is
comparable
to that of the deterministic curve
x_{\varepsilon(t)}
, up to constants that are intrinsic to the dynamics.
In particular, since
x_{\varepsilon(t)}
is deterministic, its rate of convergence toward
x^{\star}
is independent of the random initial condition
X_{0}
.
This motivates the final corollary, where we derive explicit convergence rates as consequences of the above theorem in special cases of the operator

.

Corollary 6.3
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
6.2
, define
z_{t}=\sup_{s\geq t}\frac{|\varepsilon^{\prime}(s)|}{\varepsilon(s)^{2}}
. Then, the following assertions hold

(a)

Suppose that
A\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
L
-Lipschitz with
L>0
. Then, the solution
(X_{t})
of satisfies the following

(6.10)

\mathbb{E}\left(\|A(X_{t})\|^{2}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon(t)^{2}+z_{t}^{2}+\int_{t}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}(\sigma_{\infty}(s)^{2})ds\right).

(b)

Suppose that
A=\partial\varphi
, for a proper, convex and lower semicontinuous function
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
and it satisfies the following error bound inequality on
\mathcal{S}
:

\exists p\geq 1,\gamma>0,r>\min\varphi:\varphi(x)-\min\varphi\geq\gamma d(x;\mathcal{S})^{p},\forall x\in[\varphi\leq r].

Thus, we have

(6.11)

\mathbb{E}\left(\|X_{t}-x^{\star}\|^{2}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon(t)^{1/p}+z_{t}^{2}+\int_{t}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}(\sigma_{\infty}(s)^{2})ds\right)

Proof.

Observe that, by (
6.6
) we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{s\geq t}\|X_{s}-x_{\varepsilon(s)}\|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(z_{t}^{2}+\int_{t}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}(\sigma_{\infty}(\tau)^{2})\,d\tau\right)

(a)
: Since
-\varepsilon(s)x_{\varepsilon(s)}\in A(x_{\varepsilon(s)})
, we have that

\|A(x_{\varepsilon(s)})\|=\varepsilon(s)\|x_{\varepsilon(s)}\|\leq\varepsilon(s)\|x^{\star}\|,\forall s\geq 0.

Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\|A(X_{t})\|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}(\|A(X_{t})-A(x_{\varepsilon(t)})\|^{2})+2\mathbb{E}(\|A(x_{\varepsilon(t)})\|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq 2L^{2}\mathbb{E}(\|X_{t}-x_{\varepsilon(t)}\|^{2})+2\|x^{\star}\|^{2}\varepsilon(t)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2L^{2}C\left(z_{t}^{2}+\int_{t}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}(\sigma_{\infty}(\tau)^{2})\,d\tau\right)+2\|x^{\star}\|^{2}\varepsilon(t)^{2},

which allows to conclude (

).

(b)
: By using
[
19
, Proposition 23]
we have there are constants

such that
\mathbb{P}

\|x_{\varepsilon(t)}-x^{\star}\|^{2}\leq C^{\ast}\varepsilon(t)^{1/p},\forall t\geq T^{\ast}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\|X_{t}-x^{\star}\|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}\Big(\|X_{t}-x_{\varepsilon(t)}\|^{2}\Big)+2\mathbb{E}\Big(\|x_{\varepsilon(t)}-x^{\star}\|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 2C\left(z_{t}^{2}+\int_{t}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}(\sigma_{\infty}(\tau)^{2})\,d\tau\right)+2C^{\ast}\varepsilon(t)^{1/p}

and then (

\blacksquare

7
Conclusions

In this paper we were concerned with the asymptotic analysis of stochastic differential inclusions designed to solve monotone inclusion problems. Abandoning the classical non-empty interior assumption of the driving maximally monotone operator, we prove existence and uniqueness of solutions via a detailed constructed of orthogonal martingale processes. We then establish a finite time rate statement on the ergodic average of the process, in terms of gap functions associated with the monotone inclusion. Particularizing our model to the subgradient evolution case, asymptotic convergence to the minimum, as well as probabilistic concentration bounds are derived. Lastly, we study the strong convergence of the Tikhonov regularized subgradient flow. Our analysis reveals a slightly weakened Tikhonov regularization strategy is sufficient for ensuring strong convergence of the stochastic process to the least norm element.

Many interesting directions for future research remain. First, it would be very interesting to study the long-time behavior of trajectories when the inclusion is not necessarily monotone, but rather hypomonotone
[
27
, Example 12.28]
. Additionally, understanding the multiscale aspects of the dynamical systems, and extension to the infinite-dimensional Hilbert space case are important next steps.

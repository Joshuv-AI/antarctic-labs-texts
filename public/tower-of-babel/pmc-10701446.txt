Protocol for generating mutant zebrafish using CRISPR-Cas9 followed by quantitative evaluation of vascular formation
Authors: Juanjuan Luo, Chunjiao Lu, Maya Wang, Xiaojun Yang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
SummaryThe use of vascular-specific transgenic zebrafish provides advantages for identifying new mutations affecting angiogenesis and vascular development. Here, we present a protocol for establishing, screening, and phenotyping CRISPR-Cas9-based mutagenesis in fluorescently labeled transgenic zebrafish. We describe steps for designing single-guide RNA (sgRNA) oligos, synthesizing sgRNA and Cas9 mRNA, and microinjection and generation of mutant lines. We then detail procedures for visualizing dynamic vasculature and quantitatively evaluating vascular formation in transgenic zebrafish.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Luo et al.1

Before you begin

Zebrafish were raised according to the guidelines for zebrafish care2 before the experiment. This protocol describes methods for establishing specific gene knockout fish and evaluating vasculature formation in this model. The first part is the generation of CRISPR-Cas-9-based mutagenesis, which is moderately modified from the previous report,3 including target selection, synthesis of Cas9 mRNA and single-guide RNA, microinjection, and genotyping of homozygous strains. The second part is the evaluation of angiogenic activity by quantifying specific gene mutation-affected vasculature formation, including the area of vessels, vessel length, number of vessels, density, branching, sprouting, and lacunarity in transgenic zebrafish.

Design of single guide RNA oligos

Timing: 1–2 h

Timing: 1–2 h

Effective sgRNAs, which are responsible for recruiting Cas9 to specific genomic loci, are critical for successful CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing. Generally, the CRISPR targeting sequences, located in the adjacent motif of the protospacer adjacent motif (PAM) site, include a single guide (usually 20 base pairs in length), which can be specifically recognized and cleaved by Cas9. In addition, when using T7 or SP6 to synthesize sgRNA, target sequences must start with GG (T7) or GA (SP6). We recommend selecting the GG targets if possible, as T7 polymerase is more efficient than SP6 for RNA synthesis. There are many web-based sgRNA design tools that have different features and advantages.4.Utilize the web tool CHOPCHOP to design sgRNAs.a.Search RefSeq/ENSEMBL/gene ID/genomic coordinates or retrieve the desired target sequence from a database and subsequently choose a genome assembly, such as danRer10 or danRer11.b.Select: Find Target Sites.c.Show the sequences on a new web page, according to the sgRNA design setting (Figure 1B).d.The GC content of the designed sgRNA sequence should be 40%–60%, which might be more effective.6e.Considering secondary structure (self-complementarity), we choose a self-complementarity of 0, which does not form a secondary structure.f.Considering off-target effects (MM0, MM1, MM2, MM3), select MM0 = 1 and MM1, MM2 and MM3 = 0 (MM represents mismatch), indicating that there is no similar target with 1, 2, or 3 base mismatches, so that the possibility of off-target effects is relatively low.CRITICAL: To effectively generate a genomic deletion in the target region of interest, the most efficient guide will be separately identified from 3 potential candidates and selected for the following experiment.g.Analyze the off-target events using the web-based application CCTop (https://cctop.cos.uni-heidelberg.de:8043/).h.Input the sgRNA sequence and the corresponding species genome. The website will display the coordinates of off-target sites and gene IDs.i.Verify these off-target sites by PCR, T7EI and sequencing.Note: In our experiment, by analyzing the sequence of the zebrafish cgasa gene at http://chopchop.cbu.uib.no/, we selected a sgRNA (sgcgasa) to target the cgasa sequence. Generation of the specific gene knockout homozygous mutation in zebrafish

Timing: approximately 7–8 months

Timing: approximately 7–8 months

After injection, although most mutagenesis is degraded during gastrulation, some mutagenesis survives the gastrula stage and has a likelihood of affecting somatic and germline cells in a mosaic way.8 Mutagenesis is heritable only if it occurs in the germ cells, and these initial individuals are termed founders (F0). To isolate mutations, the injected embryos are raised to adulthood. We usually perform PCR on genomic DNA obtained from caudal fin clips to screen F0 fish for germline mutations. Every F0 individual is mated with a wild-type fish, and subsequently, their offspring from each mating pair are examined for mutations in the resulting F1 generation. Due to potential variations in the recovered mutations at the sequence level, it is necessary to perform genotyping and sequencing to identify F1-positive fish lines. In the context of CRISPR-Cas9, the mechanism induces double-strand breaks (DSBs) at predetermined cleavage sites, which are subsequently repaired through the nonhomologous end-joining pathway (NHEJ).9 NHEJ is a system characterized by its propensity for errors, which can lead to the deletion or insertion of a small number of base pairs. This phenomenon, referred to as "indel" formation, introduces a level of randomness in each individual mutagenesis event.10 In F1 generations from such positive parents, approximately 20% of embryos inherit mutagenesis. F1-positive individuals will produce 50% F2-positive embryos. Expected outcomes

Using this protocol, we successfully established a homozygous mutant line of cgas by CRISPR-Cas9 and out-crossed it with homozygous Tg(fli1a:eGFP) fish harboring stable and strong expression of eGFP in the endothelial cells of vessels to generate the Tg(fli1a:eGFP);cgasa−/− line, which allowed us to easily identify the cgas mutant affecting vascular development by a transgenic-based reverse genetic approach and visualize vascular formation in vivo.1 Here, the results indicate that Cgasa deficiency in zebrafish can lead to the obvious disruption of vascular development, which is analyzed by the coverage of the fluorescence-labeled brain cortical vasculature of 3-month-old Tg(fli1a:eGFP);cgasa−/− and Tg(fli1a:eGFP) homozygotes (n = 16 fish/group; Figure 7).Figure 7AngioTool was used to analyze brain cortical vasculature formation in Tg(fli1a:eGFP);cgasa−/− and Tg(fli1a:eGFP) fish lines(A) Representative images of brain cortical vasculature in 3-month-old Tg(fli1a:eGFP);cgasa−/− and Tg(fli1a:eGFP) fish. Limitations

Transparent zebrafish only provide an advantage for dynamically visualizing angiogenesis and vascular development in vivo at the early development stage. However, observing vasculature formation in adult transgenic zebrafish in vivo is still challenging.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

The incorrect size of the assembled oligo.

Potential solution

The oligos failed to properly assemble. Check the sequences of oligonucleotides and repeat the experiment.

Problem 2

The concentration of synthesized RNA is low.

Potential solution

There are several potentials in this situation. Elongating the reaction time and/or increasing the amount of the transcriptional template according to the standard manual might be helpful for increasing RNA products. In addition, the absence of degradation induced by nuclease contamination or failure of transcription in the reaction was ensured.

Problem 3

No somatic mutation was identified.

Potential solution

The CRISPR-Cas9 system might not work effectively. It is common that not all sgRNAs work well. It is recommended to check the integrity of the top-stand oligo and bottom strand Ultramer or redesign the new sgRNAs if the problem still exists.

Problem 4

The homozygous Tg(fli1a:eGFP) mutant and the specific gene mutation cannot be simultaneously identified.

Potential solution

If the mutation of the specific gene and the fli1 promoter-driven eGFP in the Tg(fli1a:eGFP) fish line is located on the same chromosome, there should be no chance to screen the homozygote simultaneously containing homozygous Tg(fli1a:eGFP) and the specific gene mutation.

Problem 5

The downloaded AngioTool software does not function properly.

Potential solution

Confirm that your computer is running the appropriate java version JDK7 at http://www.java.com/en/download/installed.jsp. The test should confirm "You have the recommended Java installed (Version 7 Update XX). Make sure the download AngioToolSetup.exe matches your computer version (32- or 64-bit versions).

Problem 6

AngioTool cannot automatically detect and analyze vessel profiles.

Potential solution

Establish clear morphological criteria of vessels prior to engaging in the automated assessment of vascular networks with AngioTool.

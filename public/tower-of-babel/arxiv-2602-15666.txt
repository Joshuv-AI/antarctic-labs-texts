The stability of Yang-Mills connections on δ \delta -pinched manifolds
Xiaoli Han, Yang Wen

Abstract
. In this article, we establish pinching conditions under which all weakly stable Yang-Mills connections on compact manifolds are flat. As a corollary, we provide a dimension-dependent constant δ ⁡ ( n ) \delta(n) and prove that there exist no non-flat weakly stable Yang-Mills connections on δ ⁡ ( n ) \delta(n) -pinched compact simply-connected Riemannian manifolds.



Keywords:

Yang-Mills connection, stability, variation

58E15, 53C07, 53C21

1.
Introduction

Let
(M,g)
be an n-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold and
E
be a vector bundle of rank
r
over
M
with structure group
G
, where
G
is a compact Lie group. The classical Yang-Mills functional is given by

\displaystyle YM(\nabla)=\int_{M}|R^{\triangledown}|^{2}dV,

where
\nabla
is a connection on
E
and
R^{\triangledown}
is its curvature. We denote
d^{\triangledown}
to be the exterior differential operator on
E
induced by
\nabla
and
\delta^{\triangledown}
is its adjoint operator. The Euler-Lagrange equation of
YM
is

\displaystyle\delta^{\triangledown}R^{\triangledown}=0

and the critical point of
YM
is called Yang-Mills connection.

We are interested in the stability of a Yang-Mills connection. We call
\nabla
is weakly stable if the second variation of
YM
is non-negative at
\nabla
, i.e.

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}YM(\nabla^{t})\mid_{t=0}\geq 0

for any curve of connections
\nabla^{t}
such that
\nabla^{0}=\nabla
.

The study of geometric stability originated in minimal surface theory with the seminal Lawson-Simons conjecture
[
1
]
, which established the nonexistence of stable minimal
k
-submanifolds (
2\leq k\leq n-2
) in
\frac{1}{4}
-pinched compact Riemannian manifolds. Advancing the conjecture, Howard
[
2
]
proved that there exists
\delta(n,p)\in(\frac{1}{4},1)
such that there is no
p
-dimensional stable minimal surface in
n
-dimensional
\delta(n,p)
-pinched compact Riemannian manifolds. As a special case, Hu-Wei
[
3
]
proved that there is no stable minimal surface in a compact simply connected hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
or
S^{n+1}
with
\frac{1}{5}
-pinched curvature. Considering the perturbation of metrics, Franz-Trinca
[
4
]
established non-existence of closed stable minimal
k
-submanifolds in generic conformal spheres
(S^{n},\tilde{g})
for dimensions
2\leq k\leq n-\delta^{-1}
, where
\delta
dependents on
\tilde{g}
.

Motivated by the fundamental analogies between minimal surfaces, harmonic maps, and Yang-Mills fields, significant efforts have been made to adapt techniques from minimal surface theory to investigate harmonic maps and Yang-Mills configurations. The harmonic mapping conjecture posits that every stable harmonic map from an arbitrary Riemannian manifold into a compact simply connected Riemannian manifold with
\frac{1}{4}
-pinched curvature must be constant. Building on foundational rigidity theorems for harmonic maps, Leung
[
5
]
established the non-existence of non-constant stable harmonic maps from arbitrary Riemannian domains to the standard sphere
S^{n}
for
n\geq 3
. This result was significantly extended by Okayasu
[
6
]
through the introduction of a dimension-independent curvature constraint. Specifically, Okayasu proved that for any simply connected compact
Riemannian manifold with
\delta
-pinched sectional curvature satisfying
\delta\geq\frac{5}{6}
, all stable harmonic maps into such target manifolds must be constant.

A natural conjecture arises: whether there can exist no non-flat weakly stable Yang-Mills connections on compact simply connected Riemannian manifolds with
\frac{1}{4}
-pinched curvature? Bourguignon-Lawson
[
7
]
proved that any weakly stable Yang-Mills connections on
S^{n}
(
n\geq 5
) must be constant. They also proved that if the structure group
G=SU(2),SU(3)
, or
U(2)
, then the weakly stable Yang-Mills connection on
S^{4}
is self-dual or anti self-dual. Kobayashi-Ohnita-Takeuchi
[
8
]
proved that the Yang-Mills connection on any compact irreducible symmetric space other than
S^{n}
(
n\geq 5
),
P^{2}(\mathbb{C}ay)
,
E_{6}/F_{4}
and compact simple Lie groups is a weakly stable Yang-Mills connection. Utilizing Lawson’s constructive method, Ni
[
9
]
proved that the Morse index of
\operatorname{YM}
on
S^{n}(n\geq 5)
is larger than
m+1
, and the smallest eigenvalue of the Jacobi operator of
\operatorname{YM}
is not greater than
4-n
.

The principal aim of this investigation is to determine a curvature pinching threshold that precludes the existence of non-flat weakly stable Yang-Mills connections. Inspired by Howard’s
[
2
]
variational construction, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M
be a compact
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with
n\geq 5
and
\operatorname{inj}(M)\geq c_{n}\pi
for some
c_{n}\in(0,1]
. There exists
\delta=\delta(n,\operatorname{inj}(M))>0
such that if the sectional curvature satisfies
\delta<K\leq 1
, then every weakly stable Yang-Mills connection on
M
is flat.

If
(M,g)
be a simply connected compact Riemannian manifold with dimensional
n>4
and sectional curvature
K\in(\delta K_{0},K_{0}]
for constants
K_{0},\delta>0
. After the homothety
\tilde{g}=K_{0}g
, we may assume the sectional curvature satisfies
K\in(\delta,1]
. By Klingenberg injectivity radius estimate (see
[
10
]
2.6.A.1), we have
c_{n}=1
and thus
\operatorname{inj}(M)=\pi
. Therefore, we have the following deduction.

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
M
be a simply connected compact Riemannian manifold with dimensional
n\geq 5
. Then there exists
\delta(n)\in(0,1)
such that if
M
is
\delta(n)
-pinched, then any weakly stable Yang-Mills connection on
M
is flat.

Remark 1.1
.

Ohnita-Pan
[
11
]
established results analogous to Corollary
1.1
, but their approach differs from ours, consequently yielding distinct constants
\delta(n)
.

2.
Preliminary

2.1.
The connections and curvatures on vector bundles

Assume
(M,g)
be a
n
dimensional compact Riemannian manifold and
\mathscr{X}(M)
be the collection of vector fields on
M
. Let
E\to M
be a rank
r
vector bundle over
M
with a compact Lie group
G
as its structure group. We also assume
\langle\ ,\ \rangle
be the Riemannian metric on
E
compatible with the action of
G
and
\mathfrak{g}_{E}
be the adjoint bundle of
E
. Let
\nabla:\Omega^{0}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})\to\Omega^{1}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
be the connection on
E
compatible with the metric
\langle\ ,\ \rangle
. Locally,
\nabla
takes the form

\displaystyle\nabla=d+A,

where
A\in\Omega^{1}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
.

For any connection
\nabla
on
E
, the corresponding curvature
R^{\nabla}
is given by

\displaystyle R^{\nabla}=dA+\frac{1}{2}[A\wedge A],

where

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[A\wedge A](X,Y)=[A(X),A(Y)].

The induced inner product on
\Omega^{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
is given by

\displaystyle\langle\phi,\psi\rangle=\frac{1}{2}Tr(\phi^{T}\psi)

for any
\phi,\psi\in\Omega^{0}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
and

\displaystyle\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{g}=\frac{1}{p!}\sum_{1\leq i_{1},...,i_{p}\leq p}\langle\phi(e_{i_{1}},...,e_{i_{p}}),\psi(e_{i_{1}},...,e_{i_{p}})\rangle,

for any
\phi,\psi\in\Omega^{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
, where
\{e_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}
is an orthogonal basis of
TS^{n}
respect to the metric
g
. After integrating, we get the global inner product of
\Omega^{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
, that is

\displaystyle(\phi,\psi)_{g}=\int_{S^{n}}\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{g}dV_{g}.

The connection
\nabla
induces a connection
d^{\nabla}:\Omega^{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})\to\Omega^{p+1}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
on
\Omega^{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
, and we assume
\delta^{\nabla}:\Omega^{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})\to\Omega^{p-1}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
be the formal adjoint operator of
d^{\nabla}
. On local coordinates, we have

\displaystyle d^{\nabla}\phi(X_{1},...,X_{p+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{p+1}(-1)^{i+1}\nabla_{X_{i}}\phi(X_{1},...,\hat{X_{i}},...,X_{p+1}),

\displaystyle\delta^{\nabla}\phi(X_{1},...,X_{p-1})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla_{e_{i}}\phi(e_{i},X_{1},...,X_{p-1})

for any
\phi\in\Omega^{p}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
.

We can define the Laplace–Beltrami operator
\Delta^{\nabla}
by

\displaystyle\Delta^{\nabla}\phi=d^{\nabla}\delta^{\nabla}\phi+\delta^{\nabla}d^{\nabla}\phi

and the rough Laplacian operator
\nabla^{\ast}\nabla
by

\displaystyle\nabla^{\ast}\nabla\phi=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\nabla_{e_{i}}\nabla_{e_{i}}\phi-\nabla_{D_{e_{i}}e_{i}}\phi).

For
\phi\in\Omega^{1}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
and
\psi\in\Omega^{2}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
, define

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}_{g}^{\nabla}(\phi)(X)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}[R^{\nabla}(e_{i},X),\phi(e_{i})],

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}_{g}^{\nabla}(\psi)(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}[R^{\nabla}(e_{i},X),\psi(e_{i},Y)]-[R^{\nabla}(e_{i},Y),\psi(e_{i},X)].

Then we have the following Bochner–Weizenböck formula first introduced by Bourguignon-Lawson.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
7
]

For any
\phi\in\Omega^{1}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
and
\psi\in\Omega^{2}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta^{\nabla}\phi=\nabla^{\ast}\nabla+\phi\circ\operatorname{Ric}+\mathfrak{R}_{g}^{\nabla}(\phi),

\displaystyle\Delta^{\nabla}\psi=\nabla^{\ast}\nabla+\psi\circ(\operatorname{Ric}\wedge Id+2R_{M})+\mathfrak{R}_{g}^{\nabla}(\psi),

where
R_{M}
is the curvature tensor of
M
and

•

\operatorname{Ric}:TM\to TM
is the Ricci transformation defined by

\operatorname{Ric}\left(X\right)=\sum_{j}R_{M}(X,e_{j})e_{j},

•

\operatorname{Ric}\wedge\operatorname{Id}
is the extension of the Ricci transformation
\operatorname{Ric}
to
\wedge^{2}TM
given by

\operatorname{Ric}\wedge\operatorname{Id}(X,Y)=\operatorname{Ric}\wedge\operatorname{Id}(X\wedge Y)=\operatorname{Ric}(X)\wedge Y+X\wedge\operatorname{Ric}(Y),

•

The composite map
\psi\circ R_{M}:\wedge^{2}TM\to\Omega^{0}\left(\mathfrak{g}_{E}\right)
is defined by

\psi\circ R_{M}(X,Y)=\psi\circ R_{M}(X\wedge Y)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\psi(e_{j},R_{M}(X,Y)e_{j}).

3.
Estimate for the second variation of Yang-Mills functional

3.1.
Setting and Notation

In this section, we will compute the second variation of the Yang-Mills functional along special variational directions. Let
M
be a compact
n
-dimensional (
n\geq 5
) Riemannian manifold with sectional curvature
K\in(\delta,1]
for some constant
\delta\in(0,1)
and
R:=\operatorname{inj}(M)\geq c_{n}\pi
for some
c_{n}\in(0,1]
. Assume
\nabla
be a Yang-Mills connection and
\nabla^{t}=\nabla+tB
be a smooth curve in the affine space of connections for some
B\in\Omega^{1}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
. The second variation is

(1)

\mathscr{L}(B):=\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}YM(\nabla+tB)|_{t=0}=\int_{S^{n}}\langle\delta^{\triangledown}d^{\triangledown}B+\mathfrak{R}^{\triangledown}(B),B\rangle dV.

By direct calculation, we can prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any vector field
V\in\mathscr{X}(M)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}(i_{V}R^{\triangledown})=\int_{M}

\displaystyle\langle R^{\triangledown}(R(e_{i},V)e_{i},e_{j})+R^{\triangledown}(e_{i},R(e_{i},V)e_{j})-\nabla_{D_{e_{i}}V}R^{\triangledown}(e_{i},e_{j})-\nabla_{e_{i}}R^{\triangledown}(D_{e_{i}}V,e_{j})+R^{\triangledown}(D^{\ast}DV,e_{j})

\displaystyle+R^{\triangledown}(D^{2}_{e_{i},e_{j}}V,e_{i}),R^{\triangledown}(V,e_{j})\rangle dV.

Fix a point
y\in M
, The geodesic distance function
\rho_{y}(x):=dist(x,y)
is smooth when
x\notin Cut(y)
, the cut locus of
y
. We also define

\displaystyle f_{R}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\cos(\frac{\pi}{R}t),&|t|\leq R,\\
1,&|t|\geq R\end{array}\right.

be a
C^{2}
function. Let
V^{R}_{y}
be the vector field defined by

(2)

\displaystyle V^{R}_{y}(x)=\operatorname{grad}(f_{R}\circ\rho_{y})={f_{R}}^{\prime}(\rho_{y})\operatorname{grad}(\rho_{y}).

Leveraging continuity in the second variation
\mathscr{L}(i_{V_{y}^{R}})
with respect to
R
, we always assume
R=\pi
in the following calculations. Let
f:=f_{\pi}
and
V_{y}:=V_{y}^{\pi}
.

Note that
\operatorname{supp}(V_{y})\subset\overline{B_{\pi}}(y)
and
V_{y}\in W^{2,2}(M)
, which ensures that
\mathscr{L}(i_{V_{y}}R^{\nabla})
is well-defined. Let
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
be a local orthonormal frame for
TM
over
B_{\pi}(y)
. Expressing
V_{y}
in this frame:

(3)

\begin{split}V_{y}&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}e_{i},\\
D_{e_{i}}V_{y}&=\sum_{j=1}^{n}V_{j,i}e_{j},\\
D_{e_{j}}D_{e_{i}}V_{y}&=\sum_{k=1}^{n}V_{k,ji}e_{k}.\end{split}

The symmetry
V_{i,j}=V_{j,i}
holds for all
i,j
, as verified by:

\displaystyle V_{i,j}=\langle D_{e_{j}}\operatorname{grad}(f\circ\rho_{y}),e_{i}\rangle=\operatorname{Hess}(f\circ\rho_{y})(e_{i},e_{j})=V_{j,i}.

Differentiating this relation with respect to
e_{k}
yields
V_{i,jk}=V_{j,ik}
for all
i,j,k
. Using the geodesic equation
D_{\operatorname{grad}(\rho_{y})}\!\operatorname{grad}(\rho_{y})=0
and the relation
V=f^{\prime}(\rho_{y})\operatorname{grad}(\rho_{y})
, we derive

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}V_{j,i}=\langle D_{V}V,e_{j}\rangle=f^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{y})V_{j}

for any index
j
. Differentiating with respect to
e_{l}
and applying the curvature commutator formula

[D_{e_{l}},D_{e_{i}}]=R(e_{i},e_{l})+D_{[e_{i},e_{l}]}
yields:

(4)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}V_{j,li}

\displaystyle=\frac{f^{\prime\prime\prime}(\rho_{y})}{f^{\prime}(\rho_{y})}V_{j}V_{l}+f^{\prime\prime}(\rho_{y})V_{j,l}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i,j}V_{i,l}-\sum_{i,k=1}^{n}V_{i}V_{k}R_{jkli}

where the curvature components are defined as
R_{jkli}=\langle R(e_{l},e_{i})e_{k},e_{j}\rangle
. On
B_{\pi}(y)\backslash\{y\}
with
f(\rho)=\sin\rho
, this simplifies to:

(5)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}V_{j,li}

\displaystyle=-V_{j}V_{l}+\cos\rho_{y}V_{j,l}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i,j}V_{i,l}-\sum_{i,k=1}^{n}V_{i}V_{k}R_{jkli}.

3.2.
First-order derivative estimate for the vector field
V_{y}

In this section, we shall estimate those terms in the second variation
\mathscr{L}(i_{V_{y}}R^{\triangledown})
that involve first derivatives of
V_{y}
. For any
x\in B_{\pi}(y)\backslash\{y\}
with
\rho_{y}(x)=\rho
, let
\gamma(t)
be the geodesic curve on
B_{\pi}(y)
such that
\gamma(0)=y
and
\gamma(\rho)=x
. Define projection operators
P_{1},P_{2}:T_{x}M\to T_{x}M
to be

\displaystyle P_{1}(X)=\langle X,\gamma^{\prime}(\rho)\rangle\gamma^{\prime}(\rho),\ P_{2}(X)=X-P_{1}(X).

Let
\mathcal{Q}:X\mapsto D_{X}V_{y}
be a linear map on
T_{x}M
. By the definition of
V_{y}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(X)=f^{\prime\prime}(\rho)X(\rho)\operatorname{grad}(\rho_{y})+f^{\prime}(\rho)D_{X}\operatorname{grad}(\rho_{y})=\cos\rho P_{1}(X)+\sin\rho\sum_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{Hes}(\rho_{y})(P_{2}(X),e_{i})e_{i}.

Suppose
\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n}
be the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{Q}
and
e_{1},...,e_{n}
be the corresponding eigenvectors, where
\lambda_{1}=\cos\rho
and
e_{1}=\operatorname{grad}(\rho_{y})
. Then we have
V_{i,j}=\lambda_{i}\delta_{ij}
. Under the assumption that the sectional curvature satisfies
K\in(\delta,1]
and applying the Hessian comparison theorem (Theorem A, [GH]), we derive

(6)

\begin{split}\cos\rho\leq\lambda_{i}&\leq\sqrt{\delta}\sin\rho\cot(\sqrt{\delta}\rho)+\sin\rho(\cot\rho-\sqrt{\delta}\cot(\sqrt{\delta}\rho))P_{2}\\
&\leq\sqrt{\delta}\sin\rho\cot(\sqrt{\delta}\rho)\end{split}

for any
i=2,...,n
, where we use
P_{2}
is a non-negative operator and
t\cot(t\rho)
is decreasing respect to
t
for any
\rho\in(0,\pi)
. Let
\rho_{\delta}\in\left(\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{\delta}}\right)
be the unique solution to
\tan\bigl(\sqrt{\delta}\,\rho_{\delta}\bigr)+\sqrt{\delta}\,\tan\rho_{\delta}=0
and define

\displaystyle f_{1}(\rho)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\cos^{2}\rho,&\rho\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]\\
\sqrt{\delta}\cos\rho\sin\rho\cot(\sqrt{\delta}\rho),&\rho\in[\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{\delta}}]\\
\delta\sin^{2}\rho\cot^{2}(\sqrt{\delta}\rho),&\rho\in[\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{\delta}},\pi)\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle f_{2}(\rho)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\delta\sin^{2}\rho\cot^{2}(\sqrt{\delta}\rho),&\rho\in(0,\rho_{\delta}]\\
\cos^{2}\rho,&\rho\in[\rho_{\delta},\pi)\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle g_{1}(\rho)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\cos^{2}\rho,&\rho\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]\\
0,&\rho\in[\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{\delta}}]\\
\delta\sin^{2}\rho\cot^{2}(\sqrt{\delta}\rho),&\rho\in[\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{\delta}},\pi)\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle g_{2}(\rho)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\delta\sin^{2}\rho\cot^{2}(\sqrt{\delta}\rho),&\rho\in(0,\rho_{\delta}]\\
\cos^{2}\rho,&\rho\in[\rho_{\delta},\pi)\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle h_{1}(\rho)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\cos^{2}\rho,&\rho\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]\\
\sqrt{\delta}\sin\rho\cos\rho\cot(\sqrt{\delta}\rho),&\rho\in[\frac{\pi}{2},\pi)\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle h_{2}(\rho)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sqrt{\delta}\sin\rho\cos\rho\cot(\sqrt{\delta}\rho),&\rho\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]\\
\cos^{2}\rho,&\rho\in[\frac{\pi}{2},\pi)\end{array}\right.

Based on the eigenvalue estimate (
6
) for
\mathcal{Q}
, we immediately obtain the following lemma:

Lemma 3.2
.

For each
x\in B_{\pi}(y)\backslash\{y\}
with
\rho_{x}(y)=\rho
, let
\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n}
denote the eigenvalues of the operator
\mathcal{Q}_{x}:T_{x}M\to\mathbb{R}
defined by
X\mapsto D_{X}V_{y}
.
We have
\lambda_{1}=\cos\rho
and

(7)

\begin{split}&f_{1}(\rho)\leq\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}\leq f_{2}(\rho),\\
&g_{1}(\rho)\leq\lambda_{i}^{2}\leq g_{2}(\rho),\\
&h_{1}(\rho)\leq\cos\rho\lambda_{i}\leq h_{2}(\rho)\end{split}

for any
i=2,...,n
.

3.3.
The second variation formula

In this section, we will rewrite all terms involving second derivatives of
V_{y}
in the second variation to match the form on the left-hand side of (
5
). We extend
V_{i},V_{i,j},V_{i,jk}
to
L^{2}
-functions on
M
by setting them to vanish outside
B_{\pi}(y)\backslash\{y\}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume
R^{\triangledown}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}F_{ij}\omega^{i}\wedge\omega^{j}
on
B_{\pi}(y)
. The last 4 terms in
\mathscr{L}(i_{V_{y}}R^{\triangledown})
are given by:

(i)

(8)

\begin{split}&-\int_{M}\langle\nabla_{D_{e_{i}}V_{y}}R^{\triangledown}(e_{i},e_{j}),R^{\triangledown}(V_{y},e_{j})\rangle dV\\
=&\int_{M}\frac{1}{2}|R^{\triangledown}|^{2}(V_{k,kl}V_{l}+V_{k,k}V_{l,l})-\frac{3}{2}V_{k,k}V_{l,i}\langle F_{ij},F_{lj}\rangle+V_{k}V_{l}R_{ik}\langle F_{ij},F_{lj}\rangle+V_{k,i}V_{l,k}\langle F_{ij},F_{lj}\rangle\\
&+\frac{1}{2}V_{k,il}V_{l}\langle F_{ij},F_{jk}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}V_{s}V_{l}R_{ksji}\langle F_{ij},F_{kl}\rangle+V_{k,i}V_{l,j}\langle F_{ij},F_{kl}\rangle dV.\end{split}

(ii)

(9)

\begin{split}&-\int_{M}\langle\nabla_{e_{i}}R^{\triangledown}(D_{e_{i}}V_{y},e_{j}),R^{\triangledown}(V_{y},e_{j})\rangle dV\\
=&\int_{M}\frac{1}{2}|R^{\triangledown}|^{2}(V_{k,kl}V_{l}+V_{k,k}V_{l,l})-\frac{3}{2}V_{k,k}V_{l,i}\langle F_{ij},F_{lj}\rangle+V_{k}V_{l}R_{ik}\langle F_{ij},F_{lj}\rangle+V_{k,i}V_{l,k}\langle F_{ij},F_{lj}\rangle\\
&+\frac{1}{2}V_{k,il}V_{l}\langle F_{ij},F_{jk}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}V_{s}V_{l}R_{ksji}\langle F_{ij},F_{kl}\rangle+V_{k,i}V_{l,j}\langle F_{ij},F_{kl}\rangle dV.\end{split}

(iii)

(10)

\begin{split}\int_{M}\langle R^{\triangledown}(D^{\ast}DV_{y},e_{j}),R^{\triangledown}(V_{y},e_{j})\rangle dV=\int_{M}V_{i,i}V_{l,k}\langle F_{kj},F_{lj}\rangle-\frac{1}{2}|R^{\triangledown}|^{2}(V_{i,il}V_{l}+V_{i,i}V_{l,l})-V_{i}V_{l}R_{ik}\langle F_{kj},F_{lj}\rangle dV.\end{split}

(iv)

(11)

\begin{split}\int_{M}\langle R^{\triangledown}(D^{2}_{e_{i},e_{j}}V_{y},e_{i}),R^{\triangledown}(V_{y},e_{j})\rangle dV=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}V_{s}V_{l}R_{jsik}\langle F_{ki},F_{jl}\rangle dV,\end{split}

where
R_{ik}=\sum_{j}R_{ijik}
be the Ricci curvature, and we omit the summation symbol for repeated indices in all expressions.

Proof.

(i) We will perform pointwise computations on
B_{\pi}(y)\backslash\{y\}
. Using Bianchi identity, we have

\displaystyle-\langle\nabla_{D_{e_{i}}V_{y}}R^{\triangledown}(e_{i},e_{j}),R^{\triangledown}(V_{y},e_{j})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-V_{k,i}V_{l}\langle F_{ij,k},F_{lj}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\operatorname{div}(g(D_{e_{i}}V_{y},\cdot)V_{l}\langle F_{ij},F_{lj}\rangle)+V_{k,ik}V_{l}\langle F_{ij},F_{lj}\rangle+V_{k,i}V_{l,k}\langle F_{ij},F_{lj}\rangle+V_{k,i}V_{l}\langle F_{ij},F_{lj,k}\rangle

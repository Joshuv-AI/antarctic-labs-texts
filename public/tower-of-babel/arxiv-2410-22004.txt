Dynamical metastability and re-entrant localization of trapped active elements with speed and orientation fluctuations
Manish Patel, Amir Shee, Debasish Chaudhuri

Abstract
We explore the dynamics of active elements performing persistent random motion with fluctuating active speed and in the presence of translational noise in a d d -dimensional harmonic trap, modeling active speed generation through an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process. Our approach employs an exact analytic method based on the Fokker-Planck equation to compute time-dependent moments of any dynamical variable of interest across arbitrary dimensions. We analyze dynamical crossovers in particle displacement before reaching the steady state, focusing on three key timescales: speed relaxation, persistence, and dynamical relaxation in the trap. Notably, for slow active speed relaxation, we observe an intermediate time metastable saturation in the mean-squared displacement before reaching the final steady state. The steady-state distributions of particle positions exhibit two types of non-Gaussian departures based on control parameters: bimodal distributions with negative excess kurtosis and heavy-tailed unimodal distributions with positive excess kurtosis. We obtain detailed steady-state phase diagrams using the exact calculation of excess kurtosis, identifying Gaussian and non-Gaussian regions and possible re-entrant transitions.



Keywords
: active Brownian particles, speed fluctuation, harmonic trap, exact moments, phase diagram

II
Model and Calculation of Moments

We consider an Active Brownian Particle (ABP) with fluctuating active speed in a
d
-dimensional harmonic trap of strength
k
. The overdamped dynamics are defined by the position
{\bf r}^{\prime}=(r^{\prime}_{1},r^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,r^{\prime}_{d})
and active speed
v^{\prime}
along the orientation
\hat{\bm{u}}=(u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{d})
over time
t^{\prime}
. The active speed follows an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process, relaxing to a mean speed
v^{\prime}_{0}
over a timescale
\tau^{\prime}_{v}
. The dynamics are influenced by Gaussian white noise with strengths
D
,
D_{r}
, and
D_{v}
for translation, orientation, and active speed, respectively. We define
l=\sqrt{D/D_{r}}
as the unit of length and
\tau=1/D_{r}
as the unit of time, expressing all other quantities accordingly. Speed and velocity are measured in units of
\bar{v}=\sqrt{DD_{r}}
.
Using the dimensionless position
{\bf r}={\bf r}^{\prime}/l
, active speed
v=v^{\prime}/\bar{v}
and time
t=t^{\prime}/\tau
, we write the dimensionless form of Langevin dynamics in
d
dimension in the following Ito form
[
81
]
:

\displaystyle dr_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle vu_{i}dt-\beta r_{i}dt+\sqrt{2}\,dB^{t}_{i}(t)\,,

(1)

\displaystyle dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[(v-{\rm Pe})/\tau_{v}]dt+\sqrt{2A_{v}}\,dB^{s}(t)\,,

(2)

\displaystyle du_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\delta_{ij}-u_{i}u_{j})\sqrt{2}\,dB^{r}_{j}(t)-(d-1)u_{i}dt\,.

(3)

In the above, we used the following dimensionless parameters: trap strength
\beta=\mu k\tau
, mean activity
{\rm Pe}=v_{0}/\bar{v}
, relaxation time
\tau_{v}=\tau^{\prime}_{v}/\tau
and the strength of speed fluctuation
A_{v}=\tau D_{v}/\bar{v}^{2}
.
The Gaussian noise in translation, rotation, and speed relaxation dynamics are uncorrelated.
The components of noise in translation and rotation obey
\langle dB^{t}_{i}\rangle=\langle dB^{r}_{i}\rangle=0
and
\langle dB^{t}_{i}dB^{t}_{j}\rangle=\langle dB^{r}_{i}dB^{r}_{j}\rangle=\delta_{i,j}\,dt
, while for speed relaxation they obey
\langle dB^{s}\rangle=0
and
\langle dB^{s}dB^{s}\rangle=dt
.
Note that the first term in equation (
3
) projects the noise onto a (
d-1)
-  dimensional surface, while the second term ensures the normalization of the unit vector after displacement.
The numerical integration of the Langevin equation can be performed using the direct Euler-Maruyama scheme.

Noting that the position
{\bf r}
and active speed
v
perform drift-diffusion, while the orientation
\hat{\bm{u}}
undergoes a diffusion on a
(d-1)
-dimensional hypersurface, we can write the corresponding Fokker-Planck equation for the probability distribution
P({\bf r},v,\hat{\bm{u}},t)
as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}P({\bf r},v,\hat{\bm{u}},t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}P+\nabla_{u}^{2}P+A_{v}\partial_{v}^{2}P-v\,\hat{\bm{u}}\cdot\nabla P

(4)

\displaystyle+\beta dP+\beta\,{\bf r}\cdot\nabla P+(1/\tau_{v})P+[(v-{\rm Pe})/\tau_{v}]\partial_{v}P\,,

where
\nabla
is the
d
-dimensional gradient operator and
\nabla_{u}^{2}
is the spherical Laplacian in (
d-1
) dimensional orientation space. It can be expressed as
\nabla^{2}_{u}=z^{2}{\sum_{i=1}^{d}}\partial_{z_{i}}^{2}-[z^{2}\partial_{z}^{2}+(d-1)z\partial_{z}]
in the
d
-dimensional Cartesian coordinates with
u_{i}=z_{i}/z
and
z=|\bf{z}|.

With the help of the Laplace transform of time
\tilde{P}({\bf r},v,\hat{\bm{u}},s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,e^{-st}P({\bf r},v,\hat{\bm{u}},t)
, the Fokker-Planck equation can be written as,

\displaystyle-P({\bf r},v,\hat{\bm{u}},0)+s\tilde{P}({\bf r},v,\hat{\bm{u}},s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\tilde{P}+\nabla_{u}^{2}\tilde{P}+A_{v}\partial_{v}^{2}\tilde{P}-v\,\hat{\bm{u}}\cdot\nabla\tilde{P}

(5)

\displaystyle+{\nabla\cdot[\beta{\bf r}\tilde{P}]}+(1/\tau_{v})\tilde{P}+[(v-{\rm Pe})/\tau_{v}]\partial_{v}\tilde{P}\,,

where
P({\bf r},v,\hat{\bm{u}},0)
sets the initial condition.
This enables the formulation of the equation describing the mean value of any arbitrary observable in the Laplace domain defined as
\langle\psi\rangle_{s}=\int d{\bf r}\,dv\,d\hat{\bm{u}}\,\psi({\bf r},v,\hat{\bm{u}})\tilde{P}({\bf r},v,\hat{\bm{u}},s)
.
Multiplying the above equation by
\psi({\bf r},v,\hat{\bm{u}})
and integrating over all possible
({\bf r},v,\hat{\bm{u}})
, we get the moment equation

\displaystyle-\langle\psi\rangle_{0}+s\langle\psi\rangle_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\nabla^{2}\psi\rangle_{s}+\langle\nabla_{u}^{2}\psi\rangle_{s}+A_{v}\langle\partial_{v}^{2}\psi\rangle_{s}+\langle v\hat{\bm{u}}\cdot\nabla\psi\rangle_{s}-\beta\langle{\bf r}\cdot\nabla\psi\rangle_{s}

(6)

\displaystyle-(1/\tau_{v})\langle(v-{\rm Pe})\partial_{v}\psi\rangle_{s}\,,

where
\langle\psi\rangle_{0}=\int d{\bf r}\,dv\,d\hat{\bm{u}}\,\psi({\bf r},v,\hat{\bm{u}})P({\bf r},v,\hat{\bm{u}},0)
. Without any loss of generality, the initial condition can be chosen as
P({\bf r},v,\hat{\bm{u}},0)=\delta({\bf r}-{\bf r}_{0})\delta(v-v_{1})\delta(\hat{\bm{u}}-\hat{\bm{u}}_{0})
.
This represents an initial state where the system is precisely located at position
{\bf r}_{0}
, moving at speed
v_{1}
, and oriented in the direction
\hat{\bm{u}}_{0}
.
We utilize equation (
6
) to derive the exact form of the moments as a function of time. For example, the orientation autocorrelation obtained using equation (
6
) gives the expected result
\langle\hat{\bm{u}}(t)\cdot\hat{\bm{u}}(0)\rangle=e^{-(d-1)t}
for persistent random walker. In the following, we calculate several dynamic moments and investigate the intriguing steady-state results using
\lim_{t\to\infty}\langle\psi\rangle=\lim_{s\to 0}s\langle\psi\rangle_{s}=\langle\psi\rangle_{\rm st}
.

\displaystyle\frac{(v_{1}-{\rm Pe})\hat{\bm{u}}_{0}}{(s+\beta)(s+d-1+\tau_{v}^{-1})}+\frac{{\rm Pe}\,\hat{\bm{u}}_{0}+{\bf r}_{0}(s+(d-1))}{(s+\beta)(s+(d-1))}\,.

\displaystyle\langle\bm{v}\rangle(t)=(v_{1}-{\rm Pe})\hat{\bm{u}}_{0}\,e^{-((d-1)+\tau_{v}^{-1})t}+{\rm Pe}\,\hat{\bm{u}}_{0}\,e^{-(d-1)t}\,,

A_{v}=0
, the initial choice of active speed
v_{1}={\rm Pe}
ensures that the active speed remains constant at
v={\rm Pe}
for equation (
16
), thereby restoring the result for constant speed ABPs in the harmonic trap
[
40
]
.
Furthermore, in the absence of both harmonic trap and speed fluctuations, MSD in the equation (
16
) restores the result for free ABPs
[
39
]
.

In the asymptotic long time limit, MSD saturates to the steady state value

\displaystyle\langle{\bf r}^{2}\rangle_{\rm st}=\frac{d}{\beta}+\frac{{\rm Pe}^{2}}{\beta(d-1+\beta)}+\frac{\tau_{v}A_{v}}{\beta[(d-1+\beta)+\tau_{v}^{-1}]}\,,

(17)

which depends on fluctuation strength and the two time scales
\tau_{v}
and
\beta^{-1}
.
The steady-state MSD increases monotonically with
\tau_{v}
and
A_{v}
. It diverges for very large values of either parameter. With
\beta
, it monotonically decreases and vanishes as
1/\beta^{2}
in the large
\beta
limit.

IV.3
Effective Diffusion

The fluctuation in position at equilibrium satisfies
\langle{\bf r}^{2}_{\rm st}\rangle=dD/\beta D_{r}
, where
D=k_{B}T/\gamma
is defined by fluctuation-dissipation relation (FDR).
Away from equilibrium, the FDR no longer holds in its conventional form. However, by extending the equilibrium notion, we can obtain an estimate of the effective diffusivity

\displaystyle D_{\rm eff}:=\frac{\beta\langle{\bf r}^{2}\rangle_{\rm st}}{d}=1+\frac{{\rm Pe}^{2}}{d(d-1+\beta)}+\frac{\tau_{v}\,A_{v}}{d[(d-1+\beta)+\tau_{v}^{-1}]}\,,

(18)

which underscores the critical role of
\tau_{v}
,
A_{v}
and interaction
\beta
. Clearly, the effective diffusivity is an increasing function of the speed relaxation time
\tau_{v}
and speed fluctuation
A_{v}
.
A sanity check can be obtained in the free particle limit of
\beta\to 0
, in which the diffusivity reduces to
D_{\rm eff}=1+{\rm Pe}^{2}/d(d-1)+\tau_{v}A_{v}/d[(d-1)+\tau_{v}^{-1}]
, a result established before for free ABPs with speed fluctuation
[
79
]
.

IV.4
Dynamical Crossovers and Probability Distribution

Here, we analyze the series expansion of
\langle{\bf r}^{2}\rangle(t)
to identify the crossovers in the dynamics and to relate them to the dynamical change in probability distribution as obtained from direct numerical simulations.
The nature and occurrence of these crossovers highly depend on the timescales
\tau_{v}
and
\beta^{-1}
.

The series expansion of MSD with initial conditions
v_{1}={\rm Pe}
and
{\bf r}=\bf{0}
from equation (
16
) around
t=0
gives

\displaystyle\langle{\bf r}^{2}\rangle(t)=2d\,t+({\rm Pe}^{2}-2d\beta)t^{2}+\frac{1}{3}\left(2A_{v}+4d\beta^{2}-(d-1+3\beta){\rm Pe}^{2}\right)t^{3}+{\cal O}(t^{4})\,.

(19)

The first term in the expansion suggests a diffusive scaling at a short time
\langle{\bf r}^{2}\rangle\sim t
, set by thermal diffusivity. It then crosses over to a ballistic scaling
\langle{\bf r}^{2}\rangle\sim t^{2}
at time
t_{I}=2d/({\rm Pe}^{2}-2d\beta)
if
{\rm Pe}^{2}>2d\beta
.

Beyond this, further crossovers in MSD show qualitative differences depending on the competition of two timescales
\tau_{v}
and
\beta^{-1}
, which results in four possibilities at intermediate time
t
: (a)
t\ll(\tau_{v},\beta^{-1})
, (b)
\beta^{-1}\ll t\ll\tau_{v}
, (c)
\tau_{v}\ll\tau\ll\beta^{-1}
and (d)
(\tau_{v},\beta^{-1})\ll t
.
In the first regime of slow speed relaxation and weak trap,
t\ll(\tau_{v},\beta^{-1})
, the system can spend a significantly long time in the initial diffusive regime before crossing over to ballistic behavior in the intermediate time in a manner described by equation (
19
). In the subsequent sections, we explore the intermediate time dynamics for the remaining three cases in further detail.

(i) Strong trap strength and slow speed relaxation,
\beta^{-1}\ll t\ll\tau_{v}
, shows metastability:

Here, MSD exhibits an intermediate time crossover to a quasi-steady state, as shown in figure
1
(
a
).
This transition occurs as the strong trap influences ABPs to relax to it more quickly before active speed can relax to the steady state value.
The nature of this crossover at an intermediate time
\beta^{-1}\ll t\ll\tau_{v}
can be obtained explicitly using
e^{-2\beta t}\approx 0
,
e^{-(d-1+\beta+1/\tau_{v})t}\approx 0
,
e^{-(d-1+\beta)t}\approx 0
and
e^{-2t/\tau_{v}}\approx 1-2t/\tau_{v}
in equation (
16
).
Using these we get the following form of MSD:
\langle{\bf r}^{2}\rangle_{\rm int}={\cal A}+{\cal B}t
, where

\displaystyle{\cal A}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{\beta}+\frac{{\rm Pe}^{2}}{\beta(d-1+\beta)}+\frac{A_{v}[(d-1+3\beta)-\tau_{v}^{-1}]}{\beta(\beta-\tau_{v}^{-1})[(d-1+\beta)^{2}-\tau_{v}^{-2}]}\,,

(20)

\displaystyle{\cal B}

\displaystyle=\frac{2A_{v}}{(\beta-\tau_{v}^{-1})[(d-1+\beta)-\tau_{v}^{-1}]}\,.

(21)

As noted before, for
{\rm Pe}^{2}>2d\beta
, MSD cross overs from initial thermal diffusion
\langle{\bf r}^{2}\rangle\sim t
to ballistic scaling
\langle{\bf r}^{2}\rangle\sim t^{2}
at
t_{I}
. In a strong trap, the ABP with slow speed relaxation shows an intermediate time crossover from ballistic to a quasi-steady state
\langle{\bf r}^{2}\rangle\sim t^{0}
at
t_{II}=[{\cal A}/({\rm Pe}^{2}-2d\beta)]^{1/2}
, which is estimated by comparing the second term in the right-hand side of equation (
19
) with the
\mathcal{A}
term in the expression of
\langle{\bf r}^{2}\rangle_{\rm int}
.
Further, the quasi-steady state behavior crosses over to a subsequent diffusive scaling at
t_{III}={\cal A}/{\cal B}
provided
t_{III}>t_{II}
.
At late time, this diffusive scaling crosses over to the final steady state at
t_{IV}=\langle{\bf r}^{2}\rangle_{\rm st}/{\cal B}
, estimated by the comparison of the second term in the right-hand side of
\langle{\bf r}^{2}\rangle_{\rm int}
with the equation (
17
).

Note that, for small activity
{\rm Pe}^{2}<2d\beta
, the MSD directly crosses over from thermal diffusive scaling at the shortest time to the quasi-steady state at
t_{I}^{{}^{\prime}}={\cal A}/2d
, obtained by comparing the first term in the right-hand side of equation (
19
) with the first term in the right-hand side of
\langle{\bf r}^{2}\rangle_{\rm int}
.
This quasi-steady state crosses over to subsequent diffusive scaling at
t_{III}
obtained above. As before, in the asymptotic long-time, the diffusive scaling crosses over to the final steady state at
t_{IV}
.

and
\gamma=1/\tau_{v}
.

(ii) Shallow trap strength and fast speed relaxation:
\tau_{v}\ll t\ll\beta^{-1}

\tau_{v}\ll t\ll\beta^{-1}

\displaystyle=-\left[\frac{2{\rm Pe}^{2}}{(d-1)^{2}-\beta^{2}}+\frac{A_{v}\tau_{v}^{3}[3+(d-1-\beta)\tau_{v}]}{(1-\beta\tau_{v})(1-(d-1-\beta)\tau_{v})(1+(d-1+\beta)\tau_{v})}\right]\,,

(22)

\displaystyle{\cal D}

\displaystyle=\frac{2[\beta d(d-1-\beta)+\beta{\rm Pe}^{2}-A_{v}]}{\beta(d-1-\beta)}+\frac{2A_{v}}{\beta(d-1)(1-\beta\tau_{v})}+\frac{2A_{v}}{(d-1)(d-1-\beta)[1+(d-1-\beta)\tau_{v}]}\,.

(23)

.

(iii) Strong trap strength and fast speed relaxation:

In the limit of fast speed relaxation and strong trap strength

and

approach 0. The MSD reaches the steady-state value described by equation (

t_{I}

VI
Outlook

In this study, we presented and used an exact analytic method to explore the behavior of non-interacting active particles undergoing persistent random motion with fluctuating speeds in a
d
-dimensional harmonic trap in the presence of translational diffusion. This method enables the calculation of all time-dependent dynamical moments in any dimension. We presented explicit results for several moments, including second and fourth displacement moments, that closely match numerical simulations.

We examined dynamical crossovers in the mean squared displacement (MSD) under varying regimes of speed relaxation and trap strength, identifying crossover times between different scaling behaviors. Notably, under strong trap strength and slow speed relaxation, we observed a remarkable intermediate time metastability in the MSD, followed by a transition to a diffusive regime and eventually a steady state. This progression involves the evolution from an initial Gaussian distribution centered at the trap to an intermediate bimodal distribution with a spherically symmetric ring around the trap center, culminating in a heavy-tailed distribution peaking back at the center. The analytic results for excess kurtosis illustrate a non-monotonic change from zero (Gaussian) to negative values (bimodal) at intermediate times, then increasing to a positive value (heavy-tailed) in the steady state.

In steady state, swim pressure increases with activity as
{\rm Pe}^{2}
and decreases with trap stiffness as
\beta^{-1}
. The effective diffusivity increases with activity
{\rm Pe}
, speed relaxation time
\tau_{v}
, and speed fluctuation strength
A_{v}
. We characterized the steady state using excess kurtosis, distinguishing non-equilibrium distributions from Gaussian ones, and presented detailed phase diagrams for four control parameters: speed relaxation time, speed fluctuation strength, activity, and trap strength. These diagrams reveal three distinct phases based on kurtosis: positive (heavy-tailed), negative (bimodal), and zero (Gaussian).

Our findings show that slow speed relaxation combined with large speed fluctuations favors positive excess kurtosis, while fast relaxation with low fluctuation strength corresponds to a simple active Brownian particle (ABP) and leads to negative kurtosis. The excess kurtosis also exhibited non-monotonic behavior with varying trap strength, showing possibilities of re-entrant transitions between Gaussian and non-Gaussian behaviors.

The analytical predictions from our study can be experimentally validated using artificial active particles, such as Janus colloids in harmonic traps. Additionally, our results can provide a foundation for understanding the dynamics of motile cells with speed and directional fluctuations in complex environments
[
89
,
90
,
55
]
. In this paper, we employed Fokker-Planck equations to provide exact calculations for dynamical moments. However, deriving analytic expressions for the distribution functions presents an intriguing challenge that we believe would be a valuable direction for future research.

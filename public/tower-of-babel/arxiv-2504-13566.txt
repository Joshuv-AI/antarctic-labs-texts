1.
introduction

Local cohomology is a fundamental tool in algebraic geometry and commutative algebra. So vanishing results are very useful. Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be either a Noetherian local ring or a standard graded Noetherian ring with
\mathfrak{m}
its maximal homogeneous ideal with
\dim A=d
. Let
I\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
be an ideal (it is homogeneous if
A
is graded). The first vanishing result is that
H^{i}_{I}(A)=0
for all
i>d
; see
[
1
, 6.1.2]
. Perhaps the next result is the famous Hartshorne-Lichtenbaum theorem, which states that if
A
is a complete local domain (in the graded case
A_{0}
is a complete domain and
A
is a graded domain) then
H^{d}_{I}(A)=0
if and only if
\dim A/I>0
; see
[
3
, 14.1]
(for the graded case see
[
1
, 14.1.16]
). There is a version of this result which is applicable to all Noetherian local rings but it is complicated to state; see
[
3
, 14.6]
. The next case is when
A
is a complete regular local ring containing a separably closed residue field then
H^{j}_{I}(A)=0
for
j\geq d-1
if and only id
\dim A/I\geq 2
and

\operatorname{Spec}(A)\setminus\{\mathfrak{m}\}
is connected, see
[
3
, 14.7]
. Analogous result holds in the graded case when
A=k[X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}]
, see
[
2
, 7.5]
.

Usually local cohomology behaves well when the underlying rings are nice. In this paper we prove vanishing theorems for certain rings containing nilpotents. We stress that these results are completely unexpected.

We first prove

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be complete Noetherian local ring of dimension
d
and let
P
be a prime ideal with
G_{P}(A)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}P^{n}/P^{n+1}
a domain. Fix
r\geq 1
. If
J
is a homogeneous ideal of
G_{P^{r}}(A)
with
\dim G_{P^{r}}(A)/J>0
then the local cohomology module
H^{d}_{J}(G_{P^{r}}(A))=0
.

Next we show

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a regular local ring with
k=A/\mathfrak{m}
separably closed. Fix
r\geq 1
. Let
J
be a homogeneous ideal of
G_{\mathfrak{m}^{r}}(A)
.
If
\dim G_{\mathfrak{m}^{r}}(A)/J\geq 2
and
\operatorname{Proj}G_{\mathfrak{m}^{r}}(A)/J
is connected then
the local cohomology modules
H^{j}_{J}(G_{\mathfrak{m}^{r}}(A))=0
for
j\geq d-1
.

Remark 1.3
.

Note that in Theorem
1.2
we
do not
assume
A
is equi-characteristic. Also note that for
r\geq 2
the ring
G_{\mathfrak{m}^{r}}(A)
may not contain a field.

It is perhaps worthwhile to investigate converse of Theorem
1.2
. We prove:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be an equi-characteristic regular local ring with
k=A/\mathfrak{m}
separably closed. Fix
r\geq 1
. Let
J
be a homogeneous ideal of
G_{\mathfrak{m}^{r}}(A)
.
If the local cohomology modules
H^{j}_{J}(G_{\mathfrak{m}^{r}}(A))=0
for
j\geq d-1
,
then
\dim G_{\mathfrak{m}^{r}}(A)/J\geq 2
and
\operatorname{Proj}G_{\mathfrak{m}^{r}}(A)/J
is connected.

We now describe in brief the contents of this paper. In section two we discuss a construction that we need. In section three we discuss an infinitely generated
\mathcal{R}=A[It]
-module
W^{I}(A)
that plays a crucial role in our proofs. In section four we prove Theorem
1.1
. In section five we prove Theorem
1.2
. In the next section we discuss some preliminary results that is needed to prove
Theorem
1.4
. In section seven we discuss
F
-module or
D
-module structure on
W^{\mathfrak{m}}(A)
. In section eight we give proof of Theorem
1.4
.

2.
A construction

In this section
A
is a Noetherian local ring,
I
an ideal of
A
. Let
\mathcal{R}(I)=A[It]
be the Rees algebra of
I
. Let
G_{I}(A)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}I^{n}/I^{n+1}
be the associated graded ring of
I
. Fix
r\geq 1
. In this section we discuss a construction of to relate homogeneous ideals in
G_{I^{r}}(A)
with a certain ideal in
G_{I}(A)
.
The strategy is to use the Rees algebra. We note that ideals in
G_{I}(A)
correspond to ideals of
\mathcal{R}(I)
containing
It^{0}
.
Throughout
\ {}^{<r>}
denotes the
r^{th}
-Veronese functor. We note that the associated graded ring does not behave well with respect to the Veronese functor. However the Rees algebra does.
We have
\mathcal{R}(I)^{<r>}=\mathcal{R}(I^{r})
.

Construction 2.1
.

Let
Q
be a homogeneous ideal in
G_{I^{r}}(A)
. Let
\mathcal{R}(I^{r})=A[I^{r}u]
. Let
\eta\colon\mathcal{R}(I^{r})\rightarrow G_{I^{r}}(A)
be the natural map. Let
K=\eta^{-1}(Q)
.
Say
K=(\mathfrak{q}u^{0},k_{1}u^{a_{1}},k_{2}u^{a_{2}},\cdots,k_{s}u^{a_{s}})
. Here
k_{i}\in I^{a_{i}r}
(with
a_{i}\geq 1
) and
\mathfrak{q}
is an ideal of
A
containing
I^{r}
.
Let
a=lcm\{a_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq s}
. Consider
the following ideal of
\mathcal{R}(I^{r})

\widetilde{K}=(\mathfrak{q}u^{0},c_{1}u^{a},c_{2}u^{a},\cdots,c_{s}u^{a})\quad\text{where}\ c_{i}=k_{i}^{a/a_{i}}\ \text{for}\ i=1,\ldots,s.

Consider the following ideal of
\mathcal{R}(I)=A[It]
;

K^{\sharp}=(\mathfrak{q}+I,c_{1}t^{ar},c_{2}t^{ar},\cdots,a_{s}t^{a_{s}r}).

Our construction has the following properties:

Proposition 2.2
.

(with hypotheses as in
2.1
). We have

(1)

\sqrt{\widetilde{K}}=\sqrt{K}

(2)

(K^{\sharp})^{<r>}=(\mathfrak{q}+I,c_{1}u^{a},c_{2}u^{a},\cdots,c_{s}u^{a})
.

(3)

\sqrt{(K^{\sharp})^{<r>}}=\sqrt{\widetilde{K}}=\sqrt{K}
.

(4)

\dim A[It]/K^{\sharp}=\dim A[I^{r}u]/K
.

(5)

\operatorname{Proj}A[It]/K^{\sharp}
is connected if and only if
\operatorname{Proj}A[I^{r}u]/K
is connected.

(6)

K^{\sharp}
contains
It^{0}
. Let
\xi\colon A[It]\rightarrow G_{I}(A)
be the natural map. Then

A[It]/K^{\sharp}=G_{I}(A)/\xi(K^{\sharp})
.

Proof.

(1) This is clear.

(2) Set
J=(\mathfrak{q}+I,c_{1}u^{a},c_{2}u^{a},\cdots,c_{s}u^{a})
. Clearly
J\subseteq(K^{\sharp})^{<r>}
.
Let
\xi\in(K^{\sharp})^{<r>}_{n}=K^{\sharp}_{nr}
. We have

\xi=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}w_{i}t^{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{l}\beta_{j}c_{j}t^{ar},

where
w\in q+I
and
\beta_{j}\in I^{nr-ar}
. Note
\beta_{j}\in I^{r(n-a)}
. It follows that

\xi=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}w_{i}u^{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{l}\beta_{j}c_{j}u^{a}\in J.

The result follows.

(3) We note that
\sqrt{\mathfrak{q}+I}=\sqrt{\mathfrak{q}}
as
\mathfrak{q}\supseteq I^{r}
. It follows that
\sqrt{(K^{\sharp})^{<r>}}=\sqrt{\widetilde{K}}
. The second equality follows from (1).

(4), (5). Let
S=A[It]/K^{\sharp}
. Them
S^{<r>}=A[I^{r}u]/(K^{\sharp})^{<r>}
. We note that
S^{<r>}\subseteq S
is a finite integral extension. It follows that

\dim A[It]/K^{\sharp}=\dim A[I^{r}u]/(K^{\sharp})^{<r>}=\dim A[I^{r}u]/K.

Here the second equality follows from (3).

We also have
\operatorname{Proj}S\cong\operatorname{Proj}S^{<r>}
. We also have that by (3) we get as topological spaces (but not as schemes)

\operatorname{Proj}A[I^{r}u]/(K^{\sharp})^{<r>}=\operatorname{Proj}A[I^{r}u]/K.

The result follows.

(6) This is clear.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section we give

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
J
be an ideal of
G_{P^{r}}(A)
with
\dim G_{P^{r}}(A)/J>0
. Let
\eta\colon A[P^{r}u]\rightarrow G_{P^{r}}(A)
be the natural map. Let
K=\eta^{-1}(J)

2.1
.
By
2.2

\dim A[Pt]/K^{\sharp}>0
. Let
\xi\colon A[Pt]\rightarrow G_{P}(A)
be the natural map. Set
I=\xi(K^{\sharp})
. Then by
2.2
;

A[Pt]/K^{\sharp}=G_{P}(A)/I
. So we have
H^{d}_{I}(G_{P}(A))=0
. The exact sequence
(\dagger)
induces exact sequence in cohomology

\cdots\rightarrow H^{i}_{I}(G_{P}(A))\rightarrow H^{i}_{K^{\sharp}}(W^{P}(A))(1)\xrightarrow{t^{-1}}H^{i}_{K^{\sharp}}(W^{P}(A))\rightarrow H^{i+1}_{I}(G_{P}(A))\rightarrow\cdots.

As
H^{d}_{I}(G_{P}(A))=0
we get the maps

(1)

H^{d-1}_{K^{\sharp}}(W^{P}(A))_{n+1}\xrightarrow{t^{-1}}H^{d-1}_{K^{\sharp}}(W^{P}(A))_{n}
is surjective for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
; and

(2)

H^{d}_{K^{\sharp}}(W^{P}(A))_{n+1}\xrightarrow{t^{-1}}H^{d}_{K^{\sharp}}(W^{P}(A))_{n}
is injective for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

As local cohomology behaves well with respect to the Veronese functor we have
for all
i\geq 0

H^{i}_{K^{\sharp}}(W^{P}_{A})^{<r>}=H^{i}_{(K^{\sharp})^{<r>}}(W^{P^{r}}_{A}).

So we have

H^{i}_{(K^{\sharp})^{<r>}}(W^{P^{r}}_{A})_{n}=H^{i}_{K^{\sharp}}(W^{P}_{A})_{nr}.

Also note that

H^{i}_{(K^{\sharp})^{<r>}}(W^{P^{r}}_{A})(1)_{n}=H^{i}_{(K^{\sharp})^{<r>}}(W^{P^{r}}_{A})_{n+1}=H^{i}_{K^{\sharp}}(W^{P}_{A})_{(n+1)r}.

We have a surjective map

H^{d-1}_{K^{\sharp}}(W^{P}(A))_{nr+r}\xrightarrow{t^{-r}}H^{d-1}_{K^{\sharp}}(W^{P}(A))_{nr},\quad\text{for all}\ n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Notice that multiplication by
t^{-r}
is same as multiplication by
u^{-1}
. So we have surjective maps

H^{d-1}_{(K^{\sharp})^{<r>}}(W^{P^{r}}(A))_{n+1}\xrightarrow{u^{-1}}H^{d-1}_{(K^{\sharp})^{<r>}}(W^{P^{r}}(A))_{n},\quad\text{for all}\ n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Similarly we have injective maps

H^{d}_{(K^{\sharp})^{<r>}}(W^{P^{r}}(A))_{n+1}\xrightarrow{u^{-1}}H^{d}_{(K^{\sharp})^{<r>}}(W^{P^{r}}(A))_{n},\quad\text{for all}\ n\in\mathbb{Z}.

We have a short exact sequence of
A[P^{r}u]
-modules

0\rightarrow G_{P^{r}}(A)\rightarrow W^{P^{r}}(A)(1)\xrightarrow{u^{-1}}W^{P^{r}}(A)\rightarrow 0.

Taking local cohomology with respect to
(K^{\sharp})^{<r>}
and the above discussion we have

H^{d}_{(K^{\sharp})^{<r>}}(G_{P^{r}}(A))=0.

By
2.2
, we get that

H^{i}_{J}(G_{P^{r}}(A))\cong H^{i}_{(K^{\sharp})^{<r>}}(G_{P^{r}}(A))\quad\text{for all}\ i\geq 0.

The result follows.

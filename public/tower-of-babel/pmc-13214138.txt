Development of a nomogram with machine learning-assisted feature selection for predicting left atrial appendage thrombosis and severe spontaneous echo contrast in patients with non-paroxysmal atrial fibrillation
Authors: Heng Wang, Jiali Fan, Bingyuan Zhou, Changsheng Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe left atrial appendage (LAA) is the primary source of thromboembolic events in patients with non-valvular atrial fibrillation (NVAF). Therefore, detection of LAA thrombosis and severe spontaneous echo contrast (SEC) is crucial for risk stratification and management of patients with NVAF.MethodsWe retrospectively enrolled 327 patients with non-paroxysmal NVAF and collected 34 clinical and echocardiographic variables. Three machine learning approaches (SVM-RFE, Boruta, and LASSO) were employed for feature selection to construct logistic regression models. Model performance was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, calibration curves, decision curve analysis (DCA), and clinical impact curves (CIC). Reclassification analysis was performed using net reclassification improvement (NRI) and integrated discrimination improvement (IDI).ResultsLAA thrombosis or severe SEC was detected in 40 (12.2%) patients. The three machine learning algorithms demonstrated comparable area under the curve (AUC) performance, with values of 0.88 for SVM-RFE, 0.89 for LASSO, and 0.89 for Boruta. The final nomogram was constructed using variables selected by all three machine learning models. It achieved an AUC of 0.88 (95% CI: 0.83–0.93), which was significantly higher than that of the CHA2DS2-VASc score (AUC = 0.68, p < 0.001). DCA and CIC analyses showed high net benefits across a wide range of threshold probabilities. Reclassification analysis revealed an NRI of 0.957 and IDI of 0.254 (both p < 0.001) when compared with the CHA2DS2-VASc score.ConclusionsThis exploratory study suggests that the proposed nomogram may help estimate the risk of LAA thrombosis/severe SEC in patients with non-paroxysmal NVAF. The model may be useful for risk stratification and may inform clinical decision-making regarding the necessity of TEE in patients with non-paroxysmal NVAF, but its clinical reliability requires further validation in larger, independent, prospective cohorts.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12872-026-05805-w.Keywords: Atrial fibrillation, Left atrial appendage, Spontaneous echo contrast, Machine learning, Nomogram

Introduction

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the most common sustained cardiac arrhythmia in adults worldwide, affecting approximately 2–4% of adults and significantly increasing the risk of stroke [1]. According to the Global Burden of Disease 2019 study, over 59 million individuals lived with atrial fibrillation in 2019, representing a marked increase in global prevalence from 33.5 million in 2010 [2]. Notably, the left atrial appendage (LAA) has been identified as the primary source of thromboembolic events in non-valvular atrial fibrillation (NVAF) patients, with over 90% of cardiac thrombi originating from this anatomical structure [3].

Transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) remains the gold standard for detecting thrombi in patients with AF [4]. Beyond thrombus detection, TEE examination also enables assessment of spontaneous echo contrast (SEC), which serves as an important marker of blood stasis and thrombogenic potential. SEC within the LAA is conventionally graded from 0 to 4 using TEE based on echogenicity intensity and swirling pattern characteristics [5]. An additional classification of severe SEC is sludge, often defined as dense, viscous, gelatinous echo density that forms a meniscus level but not yet solid, representing an intermediate stage between SEC and formed thrombus [6]. LAA thrombosis and sludge represent the primary sources of stroke and systemic embolism in patients with NVAF [7, 8]. Compared with patients without LAA thrombi or sludge who had a 1.6% annual mortality rate, patients with these conditions demonstrated an increased mortality risk of 23%, while the annual ischemic stroke rate increased from 1% to 17% [7]. However, despite the sludge appearing denser than SEC, the two often coexist, and accurate differentiation remains challenging due to the unavailability of ultrasound contrast agents [9]. Consequently, sludge can be integrated with SEC grade 4 and assessed using a 5-point scale in clinical practice.

Existing clinical scoring systems, such as the CHA2DS2-VASc and CHADS2 scores, have significant limitations in predicting thromboembolic risk. These scoring systems, which are primarily based on clinical risk factors, demonstrated modest predictive performance for thromboembolic risk, with C-statistics of 0.68 and 0.65, respectively [10]. In contrast, impaired left atrial function has been identified as a strong predictor for the presence of LAA thrombosis in patients with NVAF [11]. Kim et al. demonstrated that incorporating left atrial emptying fraction into the CHADS2 and CHA2DS2-VASc scores significantly improved predictive accuracy for detecting dense SEC or thrombosis [10]. Therefore, non-invasive assessment of atrial function may serve as a valuable screening tool for identifying LAA thrombosis and SEC or provide important supplementary information to enhance clinical scoring systems.

Although predictive models for LAA thrombosis and SEC have been established, including the nomogram for LAA thrombosis and SEC prediction and the CLOTS-AF scoring system for dense SEC and thrombosis risk stratification, predictive models specifically targeting the combined risk of LAA thrombosis and severe SEC (grade 4) remain underdeveloped [12, 13]. Moreover, prior studies enrolled mixed AF subtypes without separating paroxysmal from non-paroxysmal AF, resulting in heterogeneity among populations with different thrombotic risks [12–14]. Therefore, we focused on patients with non-paroxysmal AF and applied machine-learning algorithms to identify the most important predictors from clinical characteristics, laboratory variables, and readily obtainable transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) parameters, and then translated these predictors into a nomogram to facilitate individualized risk estimation.

Methods

Study population

We retrospectively enrolled patients with NVAF who were hospitalized at the First Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University between October 2018 and May 2022. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) age ≥ 18 years and (2) complete TTE and TEE examinations. The exclusion criteria included: (1) paroxysmal atrial fibrillation and (2) poor echocardiographic image quality. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University (No. 20251032).

Definition

The primary outcome of this study was the presence of LAA thrombosis and severe SEC (grade 4) detected by TEE. SEC was graded according to the classification system established by Fatkin et al., which categorizes SEC from grade 0 to 4 based on echogenic intensity and pattern characteristics [5]. SEC grade 4 is characterized by intense echodensity and very slow swirling patterns in the LAA, usually with similar density in the main cavity. Representative TEE images demonstrating SEC grade 0 and grade 4 are shown in Fig. 1. According to the 2025 American Society of Echocardiography (ASE) Guidelines for the Evaluation of Left Ventricular Diastolic Function, we categorized left atrial pressure (LAP) in patients with AF into elevated and non-elevated LAP (including normal or indeterminate LAP) [15].

Transthoracic echocardiography

All patients underwent TTE using Vivid E95 with an M5Sc transducer (GE Healthcare). Standard views were acquired according to the guideline [16]. Right atrial diameter was measured in the apical four-chamber view at ventricular end-systole, and left atrial diameter, interventricular septal thickness, left ventricular end-diastolic diameter, and left ventricular posterior wall end-diastolic thickness were measured in the parasternal long-axis view. Left ventricular ejection fraction was calculated using the biplane Simpson method. Mitral inflow early diastolic velocity (E wave) and E-wave deceleration time were obtained by pulsed-wave Doppler.

For left atrial (LA) volume and strain assessment, dedicated non-foreshortened apical four-chamber (A4C) and two-chamber (A2C) views were acquired, with the left atrial endocardial border clearly visualized. LA maximum volume was measured at left ventricular end-systole using the biplane area-length method (A-L method) from A4C and A2C views, and indexed to body surface area to derive the LA volume index (LAVI). For LA reservoir strain (LASr), two-dimensional speckle-tracking echocardiography was performed offline using A4C and A2C views. The LA endocardial border was manually traced, with the pulmonary veins and the LA appendage excluded from the region of interest, and the software automatically generated strain curves. LASr was defined as the peak positive LA strain during the reservoir phase and was calculated as the average of measurements from the A4C and A2C views.

Transesophageal echocardiography

TEE was performed immediately after completion of the TTE examination. Patients fasted for at least 4 h before TEE. A multiplane TEE probe was used to obtain focused LAA images in the mid-esophageal view by rotating the imaging plane to optimize the LAA long-axis visualization. SEC was defined as an echogenic swirling pattern of blood flow within the LA/LAA on echocardiography, and was graded from 0 to 4 according to Fatkin et al. [5].

Feature selection and modeling

We collected 34 clinical, TTE, and laboratory variables from patients, and we generated a correlation heatmap for the continuous variables (Fig. S1). To identify optimal predictors for LAA thrombosis and severe SEC, we employed three machine learning approaches for feature selection: support vector machine-recursive feature elimination (SVM-RFE), Boruta, and least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO). SVM-RFE is an iterative algorithm that systematically eliminates the least important features by recursively training SVM models and ranking variables based on their contribution to classification [17]. The Boruta algorithm compares the importance of real predictor variables with those of random so-called shadow variables using statistical testing and several runs of RFs, where shadow variables are generated by permuting the original values across observations and therefore destroying the relationship with the outcome, enabling identification of all relevant variables within a classification framework [18]. LASSO is a variable selection method that shrinks some coefficients and sets others to 0, enabling it pick out the variables most associated with the response variable [19]. The overlapping variables selected by the three machine-learning methods were used to construct the nomogram.

Validation

The performance of the nomogram was evaluated based on discrimination, calibration, and clinical utility. We employed receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and calibration curves to assess model discrimination and calibration, respectively. Decision curve analysis (DCA) and clinical impact curves (CIC) were utilized to assess the clinical benefit of the nomogram. The calibration curves were generated using 1000 bootstrap resamples. In addition, the area under the ROC curve (AUC) of the nomogram was internally validated using 1000 bootstrap resamples. DCA was performed using 5-fold cross-validation.

Analysis workflow

The overall analysis workflow was as follows: data cleaning and missing-value handling were performed for the 34 clinical, TTE, and laboratory variables. SVM-RFE, Boruta, and LASSO were then applied for feature selection, and variables concurrently selected by all three methods were retained as the final feature set. A multivariable logistic regression model was developed based on this feature set and presented as a nomogram. Discrimination was evaluated by ROC curves and AUC. Calibration was assessed by calibration curves. Clinical utility was examined using DCA and CIC. Internal validation was conducted using bootstrap resampling and 5-fold cross-validation.

Statistical analysis

Patients were stratified into two groups based on the presence or absence of LAA thrombosis or severe SEC. Continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation for normally distributed data or median (interquartile range) for non-normally distributed data and were compared between groups using Student’s t-test or the Mann-Whitney U test, respectively. Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies (percentages) and compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Missing values were imputed using the random forest imputation method. All statistical analyses were two-tailed, and P-values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using R software version 4.5.1.

Results

Baseline characteristics

Between October 2018 and May 2022, we retrospectively enrolled 598 patients. After excluding patients with paroxysmal AF (n = 262) and those with poor echocardiographic image quality (n = 9), a total of 327 patients with non-paroxysmal AF were included in the final analysis.

Table 1 showed the baseline characteristics and clinical parameters of the study population stratified by the presence or absence of LAA thrombosis or severe SEC. Demographic characteristics were overall balanced between the two groups. Echocardiographic assessment revealed that patients with LAA thrombosis/severe SEC had significantly impaired cardiac structure and function, including enlarged left ventricular end-diastolic dimension (53 mm vs 50 mm, p < 0.001), reduced left ventricular ejection fraction (51% vs 59%, p < 0.001), lower septal and lateral e’ velocities (5 cm/s vs 8 cm/s, p < 0.001, and 8 cm/s vs 11 cm/s, p < 0.001, respectively), elevated E/e’ ratio (14.4 vs. 9.2, p < 0.001), higher pulmonary artery systolic pressure (31 mmHg vs. 27 mmHg, p = 0.003), increased left atrial volume index (LAVI) (70.6 mL/m2 vs 51.5 mL/m2, p < 0.001), and impaired LASr (5.78% vs 9.26%, p < 0.001).

Regarding medical history and laboratory parameters, patients with LAA thrombosis/severe SEC had a significantly higher prevalence of congestive heart failure (32.5% vs. 10.5%, p < 0.001) and stroke history (20.0% vs. 7.8%, p = 0.020), had elevated D-dimer levels (0.32 mg/L vs. 0.22 mg/L, p = 0.001), showed a greater proportion of patients with elevated LAP (65% vs. 31.36%, p < 0.001), and had higher CHA₂DS₂-VASc scores (3 vs. 2, p < 0.001).

Development of the nomogram

To develop a robust predictive model for LAA thrombosis/severe SEC, we employed three machine learning approaches for feature selection and model development (Fig. 2). Performance evaluation revealed comparable discriminative ability across all three methods, with AUC values of 0.88 (95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.83–0.93) for SVM-RFE, 0.89 (95% CI: 0.83–0.93) for LASSO, and 0.89 (95% CI: 0.84–0.94) for Boruta (Fig. 3A).

Development of the nomogram for predicting LAA thrombosis/severe SEC. A, ROC curves of SVM-RFE, LASSO, and Boruta models; B, nomogram for predicting LAA thrombosis/severe SEC risk

To enhance model generalizability and clinical applicability, the final nomogram was constructed using the overlapping variables selected by three machine-learning feature-selection methods, which were also the variables selected by SVM-RFE. The constructed nomogram incorporated three key parameters (LASr, LAVI, and septal e’), establishing a point-based scoring system with corresponding probability estimates for LAA thrombosis/severe SEC (Fig. 3B).

Validation of the nomogram for predicting LAA thrombosis/severe SEC

To assess the performance and clinical utility of the nomogram, we conducted validation analyses (Figs. 4A-D). Due to the small sample size (40 cases and 287 controls), we did not perform random assignment to training and validation sets. Discriminative ability was evaluated using ROC curve analysis. The nomogram demonstrated superior discriminative ability with an AUC of 0.88 (95% CI: 0.83–0.93), which was significantly higher than the CHA2DS2-VASc score (AUC = 0.68, 95% CI: 0.60–0.76, P < 0.001) (Fig. 4A). Meanwhile, the nomogram exhibited significantly better discriminative ability than its individual constituent variables, including LAVI, septal e’, and LASr (P = 0.005, 0.006, and 0.010, respectively) (Table S1). To assess the robustness of the nomogram’s discrimination, we further performed internal validation using 1000 bootstrap resamples. The bootstrap-corrected AUC remained unchanged (AUC = 0.88) (Fig. S2A).

Performance of the nomogram for predicting LAA thrombosis/severe SEC. A, ROC curves of the CHA2DS2-VASc score and the nomogram; B, DCA curves of the CHA2DS2-VASc score and the nomogram; C, CIC curves of the nomogram; D, calibration curves of the nomogram

The clinical utility of the nomogram was evaluated using DCA and CIC (Fig. 4B and C). DCA demonstrated substantial net benefits, with the model outperforming the CHA2DS2‑VASc score and both “treat-all” and “treat-none” strategies across a wide range of threshold probabilities. The net benefit curve derived from 5-fold cross-validation closely overlapped with the apparent performance curve (Fig. S2B). Calibration curves were shown in Fig. 4D, demonstrating good agreement between predicted and observed values using 1000 bootstrap replicates. Reclassification analysis demonstrated significant improvements in predictive performance, with a continuous net reclassification improvement (NRI) of 0.957 (95% CI: 0.698–1.201, p < 0.001) and integrated discrimination improvement (IDI) of 0.254 (95% CI: 0.167–0.340, p < 0.001) compared with the CHA2DS2-VASc score (Table 2).

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, although several predictive models for LAA thrombosis/SEC have been reported, our study extends the existing literature by using machine-learning based feature selection to identify key predictors and by developing and visualizing a predictive nomogram specifically for LAA thrombosis/severe SEC in patients with non-paroxysmal NVAF. Our findings demonstrated that the nomogram achieved superior discriminative performance compared with the widely used CHA2DS2-VASc score, with good calibration and substantial clinical utility.

In the current study, we combined LAA thrombosis and severe SEC as a composite endpoint. A previous study demonstrated that both LAA thrombosis and sludge are independently associated with adverse cardiovascular outcomes in patients with AF, with comparable risks for thromboembolic events and all-cause mortality, and patients with either condition fared significantly worse compared with those with SEC alone [8]. From a diagnostic perspective, distinguishing between LAA thrombosis, sludge, and SEC grade 4 remains challenging with TEE, often requiring additional techniques such as ultrasound enhancing agents or isoproterenol infusion for accurate differentiation [6, 9, 20]. Considering that LAA thrombosis and sludge exhibit comparable prognostic outcomes and given the diagnostic difficulties in distinguishing these conditions with TEE, combining LAA thrombosis and severe SEC as a composite endpoint provides a clinically meaningful and practical approach for risk stratification.

We included only patients with non-paroxysmal AF, based on both clinical characteristics of the disease and methodological considerations. Patients with non-paroxysmal AF demonstrated substantially higher rates of LA thrombus formation compared with those with paroxysmal AF, with approximately 4-fold higher prevalence [14]. Therefore, developing predictive models for LAA thrombosis and severe SEC in patients with non-paroxysmal AF has greater clinical significance. Additionally, machine learning-based variable selection requires consistent parameter availability across all patients, whereas certain echocardiographic parameters, such as the A wave, are only measurable in patients with paroxysmal AF during sinus rhythm episodes.

TEE remains the gold standard test for detecting LAA thrombus and SEC [21]. However, as a semi-invasive procedure requiring esophageal intubation, TEE may cause patient discomfort and carries a risk of procedure-related complications [22]. In contrast, TTE is non-invasive and readily available in routine practice, and thus may be used as a risk-stratification tool before proceeding to TEE [23]. Our nomogram incorporated three TTE variables that have been established as predictors of LAA thrombosis in patients with AF. LAVI serves as a robust predictor of LAA thrombosis formation, with a prospective multicenter study demonstrating that higher LAVI is independently associated with increased risk of LAA thrombosis [24]. Septal e’ velocity represents another well-established predictor that reflects diastolic function. Doukky et al. demonstrated that diastolic function indices, including E/e’ ratio and e’ velocity, are significantly associated with LAA thrombosis in patients with NVAF [25]. The underlying pathophysiological mechanism involves impaired diastolic function with consequent elevation of left ventricular filling pressures, resulting in left atrial blood stasis and subsequent increased risk of LAA thrombus formation [25, 26]. LASr provides additional discriminative value by quantifying atrial mechanical function beyond traditional parameters. Recent studies demonstrated that LASr can effectively identify patients at higher risk of LAA thrombosis and SEC, with impaired left atrial strain providing significant incremental diagnostic value beyond traditional clinical risk scores such as the CHA2DS2-VASc score [27, 28].

Several studies have developed predictive models for LAA thrombosis and SEC in patients with AF. Sun et al. developed a nomogram for predicting LAA thrombosis and SEC risk in patients with NVAF, achieving an AUC of 0.886 [13]. Their model demonstrated superior performance compared with traditional risk scores, with the CHA2DS2-VASc score achieving an AUC of only 0.579 in the same population. Similarly, Huang et al. developed a machine learning model to predict left atrial thrombosis (LAT)/SEC risk in NVAF patients, achieving an AUC of 0.831, which was significantly better than the CHA2DS2-VASc score (AUC = 0.650) [29]. Another study developed a nomogram incorporating hemodynamic parameters from TTE for predicting LAT/SEC risk in NVAF patients, achieving a C-index of 0.878 in the training set and 0.872 in the validation set, which significantly outperformed the CHA2DS2-VASc score (AUC = 0.678 and 0.674, respectively) [30]. In contrast to these studies that included heterogeneous AF populations, our study specifically targeted patients with non-paroxysmal AF, a population with substantially higher LAT prevalence [14]. Moreover, prior models generally used LA/LAA thrombosis and SEC as a composite endpoint, whereas we focused on the clinically more severe outcome of LAA thrombosis or SEC grade 4. In our study, the CHA2DS2-VASc score demonstrated limited discriminative ability for LAA thrombosis/severe SEC prediction, with an AUC of 0.68. In contrast, our nomogram achieved superior discriminative performance with an AUC of 0.88, representing a substantial improvement over the conventional risk stratification approach. This advantage is readily interpretable, as the CHA2DS2-VASc score was originally developed for long-term stratification of stroke and thromboembolic risk in patients with atrial fibrillation, rather than for specifically identifying the presence of LAA thrombosis/severe SEC [31]. Moreover, our nomogram outperformed each individual TTE variable (LAVI, septal e’ velocity, and LASr), supporting the concept that integrating complementary information from left atrial structure and function provides incremental predictive value beyond any single parameter alone. Importantly, our nomogram relies on three readily obtainable TTE parameters. The combination of superior predictive performance and clinical accessibility makes our nomogram a potentially useful tool for risk stratification and may help inform clinical decision-making regarding the necessity of TEE in patients with non-paroxysmal NVAF.

Limitations

There were several limitations in the current study. First, our study had a small sample size with only 40 patients diagnosed with LAA thrombosis/severe SEC, which precluded the division of our dataset into separate training and validation cohorts. The small sample size may also increase the risk of overfitting and lead to potential overestimation of model performance. To mitigate this, we selected only the overlapping predictors identified by three machine-learning models to minimize the number of variables included in the final model. Nevertheless, the model’s performance should be confirmed in larger samples and independent external validation cohorts. Second, as a retrospective study, we only included patients who underwent both TEE and TTE, which may have introduced indication bias. In addition, our study was conducted at a single center, which may limit the generalizability of our findings to broader populations. Moreover, our study was restricted to patients with non-paroxysmal AF, and we did not differentiate between persistent and long-standing persistent AF, which may limit the applicability of our nomogram across different non-paroxysmal AF subtypes and requires further validation in paroxysmal AF. Furthermore, the study lacked data on patients’ medication adherence, specific NOAC types, and therapeutic INR levels, which may substantially limit the clinical reliability of the model. Finally, the outcome in this study was based on TEE-detected LAA thrombosis/severe SEC rather than clinically thromboembolic events. Therefore, other models are needed to predict patients’ long-term clinical thromboembolic risk.

Conclusions

This exploratory study demonstrated that a nomogram constructed using three TTE parameters (LAVI, septal e’, and LASr) exhibits good discriminative ability for LAA thrombosis/severe SEC in patients with non-paroxysmal NVAF. Therefore, the model may be useful for risk stratification and may inform clinical decision-making regarding the necessity of TEE in patients with non-paroxysmal NVAF, but its clinical reliability requires further validation in larger, independent, prospective cohorts.

Hidden zeros for higher-derivative YM and GR amplitudes at tree-level
Kang Zhou

Abstract
We extend the recently discovered phenomenon of hidden zeros to tree amplitudes for Yang-Mills (YM) and general relativity (GR) theories with higher-derivative interactions. This includes gluon amplitudes with a single insertion of the local F 3 F^{3} operator, as well as graviton amplitudes at sub-leading and sub-sub-leading orders in the low-energy expansion of bosonic closed string amplitudes—referred to as R 2 R^{2} and R 3 R^{3} amplitudes, respectively. The kinematic condition for hidden zeros leads to unavoidable propagator singularities in unordered graviton amplitudes. We investigate in detail the systematic cancellation of these divergences, which resolves ambiguities in the proof of hidden zeros. Our approach is based on universal expansions that express tree amplitudes as linear combinations of bi-adjoint scalar amplitudes.

An important subtlety in the study of hidden zeros within higher-derivative GR amplitudes concerns the emergence of divergences from propagators. The kinematic condition that determines hidden zeros includes, as a key element, the requirement that
k_{a}\cdot k_{b}=0

For graviton amplitudes, which are devoid of color ordering, these singularities are inescapable and present a paramount concern for the consistency of the hidden zeros. Fortunately, as we will elucidate, a systematic cancellation of these divergences occurs under the requisite kinematic conditions. This results in an effective expansion of amplitudes that is manifestly finite, thereby placing the proof of hidden zeros on a firm and unambiguous foundation.

2.1
The BAS basis

The bi-adjoint scalar (BAS) theory describes the cubic interaction of massless scalar field
\phi^{Aa}
, with the Lagrangian

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\rm BAS}={1\over 2}\,\partial_{\mu}\phi^{Aa}\,\partial^{\mu}\phi^{Aa}+{\lambda\over 3!}\,F^{ABC}f^{abc}\,\phi^{Aa}\phi^{Bb}\phi^{Cc}\,,

(1)

where
F^{ABC}={\rm tr}([T^{A},T^{B}]T^{C})
and
f^{abc}={\rm tr}([T^{a},T^{b}]T^{c})
are usual structure constants of two Lie groups, respectively.

Each tree amplitudes in this theory with coupling constants stripped off consists solely of propagators for massless scalars, and the usual decomposition of group factors leads to

\displaystyle A^{\rm BAS}_{n}=\sum_{\sigma\in{\cal S}_{n}\setminus Z_{n}}\,\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}\in{\cal S}^{\prime}_{n}\setminus Z^{\prime}_{n}}\,{\rm tr}[T^{A_{\sigma_{1}}},\cdots T^{A_{\sigma_{n}}}]\,{\rm tr}[T^{a_{\sigma^{\prime}_{1}}}\cdots T^{a_{\sigma^{\prime}_{n}}}]\,{\cal A}^{\rm BAS}_{n}(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n}|\sigma^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,\sigma^{\prime}_{n})\,,

(2)

where
A_{n}
represents the full
n
-point tree amplitude. The summation is over all un-cyclic permutations
{\cal S}_{n}\setminus Z_{n}
and
{\cal S}^{\prime}_{n}\setminus Z^{\prime}_{n}
. Each partial amplitude
{\cal A}^{\rm BAS}_{n}(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n}|\sigma^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,\sigma^{\prime}_{n})
is planar with respect to both orderings
(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n})
and
(\sigma^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,\sigma^{\prime}_{n})
. For example, the
5
-point amplitude
{\cal A}^{\rm BAS}_{5}(1,2,3,4,5|1,2,4,5,3)
includes only one term, i.e.,

\displaystyle{\cal A}^{\rm BAS}_{5}(1,2,3,4,5|1,2,4,5,3)={1\over s_{12}}\,{1\over s_{45}}\,,

(3)

up to an overall
\pm
sign, since other Feynman diagrams are not compatible with two orderings
(1,2,3,4,5)
and
(1,2,4,5,3)
simultaneously.
In the above, each Mandelstam variable
s_{{\alpha}}
is defined as usual

\displaystyle s_{{\alpha}}\equiv k_{{\alpha}}^{2}\,,~~~{\rm with}~k_{{\alpha}}\equiv\sum_{\ell\in{\alpha}}\,k_{\ell}\,,~~~~

(4)

where

is the momentum of the external particle
\ell
, while
{\alpha}
is a subset of external particles
\{1,\cdots,n\}
.
For simplicity, sometimes we denote an
n
-point partial amplitude as

, where
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{n}
and
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{\prime}_{n}
stand for two orderings.

The anti-symmetry of structure constants

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\rm BAS}_{n}(\cdots,p,q,\cdots|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{n})=-\Gamma^{\rm BAS}_{n}(\cdots,q,p,\cdots|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{n})\,,~~

(5)

where

, from an individual Feynman diagram, denotes the product of massless scalar propagators excluding the
\pm

3
Hidden zeros for higher-derivative YM amplitudes

In this section, we employ the universal expansion of
F^{3}
tree amplitudes (see section
2.2
) to establish the existence of their hidden zeros. As our approach traces these zeros back to those in bi-adjoint scalar (BAS) amplitudes, we begin with a brief review of hidden zeros for BAS amplitudes in subsection
3.1
. We then present the corresponding hidden zeros for
F^{3}
amplitudes in subsection
3.2
, where we also explain the core reason for this phenomenon from the perspective of the universal expansion—namely, the reduced expansion (
37
) and the related discussion. The supporting examples and a general proof are provided in subsections
3.3
through
3.5
.

3.1
Hidden zeros for BAS amplitudes

A special set of double ordered BAS amplitudes vanish on certain loci in kinematic space. Such hidden zeros can be achieved as follows. For
n
-point BAS amplitudes, one can choose two external legs
\hat{i}
and
\hat{j}
, and divide the remaining external legs into two subsets
A
and
B
, namely
A\cup B=\{1,\cdots,n\}\setminus\{\hat{i},\hat{j}\}
.
The amplitudes
{\cal A}^{\rm BAS}_{n}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{n}|\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\prime}_{n})
with two orderings satisfying

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{n}=\boldsymbol{A},\hat{i},\boldsymbol{B},\hat{j}\,,~~~~{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{\prime}_{n}=\boldsymbol{A}^{\prime},\hat{i},\boldsymbol{B}^{\prime},\hat{j}\,,~~~~{\rm up~to~cyclic~permutations}\,,~~

(29)

vanish as

\displaystyle{\cal A}^{\rm BAS}_{n}(\boldsymbol{A},\hat{i},\boldsymbol{B},\hat{j}|\boldsymbol{A}^{\prime},\hat{i},\boldsymbol{B}^{\prime},\hat{j})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{(\ref{condi0-momentum})}

\displaystyle 0\,,~~

(30)

where the kinematic condition is given as

\displaystyle k_{a}\cdot k_{b}=0\,,~~~~{\rm for}~~\forall\,a\in A\,,~~\forall\,b\in B\,.~~

(31)

In (
29
),
\boldsymbol{A}
and
\boldsymbol{A}^{\prime}
are two ordered sets obtained by imposing orderings on elements in
A
, while
\boldsymbol{B}
and
\boldsymbol{B}^{\prime}
are ordered sets obtained by imposing orderings on elements in
B
. The hidden zeros in (
30
) can be proved via either the CHY formula or Feynman rules
Zhang:2024efe
;
Huang:2025blb
.

It is worth emphasizing that not all BAS amplitudes exhibit hidden zeros. For a given choice of
A
and
B
, the hidden zeros exist in BAS amplitudes with two orderings compatible with the choice of
A
and
B
. That is, in either
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{n}
or
\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\prime}_{n}
, elements in
A
and
B
are separated by
\hat{i}
and
\hat{j}
as in (
29
).

As the simplest examples, let us consider two
4
-point BAS amplitudes. We choose
\hat{i}
,
\hat{j}
,
A
and
B
as

\displaystyle\hat{i}=1\,,~~\hat{j}=3\,,~~A=\{2\}\,,~~B=\{4\}\,.~~

(32)

The first example is the amplitude
{\cal A}^{\rm BAS}_{4}(1,2,3,4|1,4,3,2)
. In this example, both two orderings are compatible with the choice (
32
), therefore the amplitude exhibits hidden zero. Such hidden zero can be verified by directly calculating this amplitude,

\displaystyle{\cal A}^{\rm BAS}_{4}(1,2,3,4|1,4,3,2)={1\over s_{12}}+{1\over s_{14}}=-{s_{13}\over s_{12}s_{14}}\,,

(33)

which vanishes when
s_{13}=2k_{2}\cdot k_{4}=0
. The second example is the amplitude
{\cal A}^{\rm BAS}_{4}(1,2,3,4|1,3,2,4)
. It does not exhibit hidden zero, since the right ordering
(1,3,2,4)
is not compatible with the choice (
32
). As can be seen, the explicit formula

\displaystyle{\cal A}^{\rm BAS}_{4}(1,2,3,4|1,3,2,4)=-{1\over s_{14}}

(34)

is manifestly nonzero when
k_{2}\cdot k_{4}=0
.

3.2
Hidden zeros for
F^{3}
amplitudes

As we will prove, the
F^{3}
amplitudes has the following hidden zeros

\displaystyle{\cal A}^{F^{3}}_{n}(\hat{i},\boldsymbol{A},\hat{j},\boldsymbol{B})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{(\ref{condi0-YM})}

\displaystyle 0\,,~~

(35)

on the special loci in kinematic space determined by

\displaystyle k_{a}\cdot k_{b}=k_{a}\cdot\epsilon_{b}=\epsilon_{a}\cdot k_{b}=\epsilon_{a}\cdot\epsilon_{b}=0\,.~~

(36)

The above kinematic condition is obtained by extending (
31

A
and
B

A
and
B
are separated by
\hat{i}
and
\hat{j}

To prove the behavior (
35

amplitudes described in section
2.2
, as well as the hidden zeros for BAS amplitudes in (
30

B

36

30

10
). More explicitly, on the specific loci (
36
), the expansion of

amplitudes can be reduced to

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{n}^{F^{3}}(\hat{i},\boldsymbol{A},\hat{j},\boldsymbol{B})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{(\ref{condi0-YM})}

\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{A}^{\prime}}\,\sum_{\boldsymbol{B}^{\prime}}\,C_{F^{3}}(\epsilon,\boldsymbol{A}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{B}^{\prime})\,\sum_{\shuffle}\,{\cal A}^{\rm BAS}_{n}(\hat{i},\boldsymbol{A}^{\prime}\shuffle\boldsymbol{B}^{\prime},\hat{j}|\hat{i},\boldsymbol{A},\hat{j},\boldsymbol{B})\,,~~

(37)

where
\boldsymbol{A}^{\prime}
and
\boldsymbol{B}^{\prime}
are ordered amplitudes obtained by imposing orderings on elements in
A
and
B
, respectively. Since each

is independent of the shuffles
\shuffle
, the KK relation (
10

amplitudes then vanish as in (
35

30

The above discussion shows that the reduced expansion (
37

amplitudes.

4.2
Cancellations of divergences and effective expansions

In this subsection, we show the main mechanism and conclusion of the cancellations of divergences, and the reason why such cancellations ensure the validity of the reduced expansions of
R^{2}
and
R^{3}

To describe degrees of divergences, let us parameterize each Lorentz invariant in the kinematic condition (

\displaystyle k_{a}\cdot k_{b}\to\tau\,k_{a}\cdot k_{b}\,,~~~~k_{a}\cdot\epsilon_{b}\to\tau\,k_{a}\cdot\epsilon_{b}\,,~~~~\epsilon_{a}\cdot k_{b}\to\tau\,\epsilon_{a}\cdot k_{b}\,,~~~~\epsilon_{a}\cdot\epsilon_{b}\to\tau\,\epsilon_{a}\cdot\epsilon_{b}\,,~~~~~~~~\tau\to 0\,,~~

\tau

which contains divergent propagators
1/s_{a_{1}b_{1}}
,
1/s_{a_{2}b_{2}}

\tau^{-t}

As in (
5

6

For each partial BAS amplitude which contains the particular

\tau^{-t}

with

should be adjacent to each other in either of two orderings

28

Each

27
) or (
28

\displaystyle C^{\rm eff}(\epsilon,\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}_{n-2})\sim{\cal O}(\tau^{c})\,,~~~~C^{\rm eff}(\widetilde{\epsilon},\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}^{\prime}_{n-2})\sim{\cal O}(\tau^{c^{\prime}})\,,~~~~{\rm with}~c\geq t\,,~c^{\prime}\geq t\,.~~~~

and
\sum_{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}_{n-2}}
(or
C(\widetilde{\epsilon},\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}^{\prime}_{n-2})

The first type of cancellation can be traced to kinematic factors like

{\rm E}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{f}}

,
\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{f}

\boldsymbol{\pi}

71

If

and
b_{\ell}
are not adjacent in any of these ordered sets, or, they are separated into different ordered sets,
there are two manners of cancellations. The first one,
a_{\ell}
and
b_{\ell}

{\rm E}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{f}}

r_{1}=a_{\ell},Y_{r_{1}}=k_{b_{\ell}}
or
r_{1}=b_{\ell},Y_{r_{1}}=k_{a_{\ell}}
, where

denotes the first element in
\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{f}

\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{f}=\{r_{1},\cdots\}
. The divergence from

{\rm E}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{f}}

\Gamma^{\rm BAS}_{n}(\cdots,a_{\ell},b_{\ell},\cdots|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{n})
and
\Gamma^{\rm BAS}_{n}(\cdots,b_{\ell},a_{\ell},\cdots|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{n})
(or,
\Gamma^{\rm BAS}_{n}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{n}|\cdots,a_{\ell},b_{\ell},\cdots)
and
\Gamma^{\rm BAS}_{n}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{n}|\cdots,b_{\ell},a_{\ell},\cdots)

5

5

\Gamma^{\rm BAS}_{n}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\prime}_{n}|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{n})
encodes the contribution from
\Gamma
to the BAS amplitude
{\cal A}^{\rm BAS}_{n}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\prime}_{n}|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{n})

{\cal A}^{\rm BAS}_{n}(\cdots,a_{\ell},b_{\ell},\cdots|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{n})
and
{\cal A}^{\rm BAS}_{n}(\cdots,b_{\ell},a_{\ell},\cdots|\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{n})
in the expansion (
27

By employing the observation (

\displaystyle C^{\rm eff}(\epsilon,\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}_{n-2})\,C^{\rm eff}(\widetilde{\epsilon},\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}^{\prime}_{n-2})\,\Gamma^{\rm BAS}_{n}(\hat{i},\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}_{n-2},\hat{j}|\hat{i},\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}^{\prime}_{n-2},\hat{j})\,\sim\,{\cal O}(\tau^{c+c^{\prime}-t})\,,

5
Summary and discussion

In this paper, we reveal the presence of hidden zeros for higher-derivative YM and GR amplitudes at the tree-level, including the gluon amplitudes with a single insertion of the local
F^{3}
operator, as well as
R^{2}
and
R^{3}
amplitudes on the GR side. By exploiting the universal expansions and the KK relation, these hidden zeros are shown to originate from zeros in BAS amplitudes. The kinematic condition for hidden zeros leads to singular propagators which are unavoidable in unordered graviton amplitudes. We also systematically analyze the cancellations of divergences arising from these singular propagators. These cancellations resolve a key ambiguity in establishing the existence of hidden zeros.

Prior to this work, all amplitudes found to contain hidden zeros consistently exhibited novel factorization behavior called
2
-split near these zeros
Cao:2024qpp
. This leads to an intriguing question: is the presence of hidden zeros always accompanied by—or does it necessarily imply—the
2
-split behavior? The new hidden zeros uncovered in this work offer a perspective for investigating this issue. That is, we can study whether the
F^{3}
,
R^{2}
and
R^{3}
amplitudes exhibit
2
-split. This will be the primary objective of our subsequent research.

Another natural and valuable direction for future work is to investigate whether the combination of hidden zeros and factorization on physical poles is sufficient to uniquely determine the
F^{3}
,
R^{2}
and
R^{3}
amplitudes. An affirmative answer would open the door to systematically constructing a new effective approach for their calculation, using these zeros as a foundational blueprint—in direct analogy to the novel on-shell recursion relation developed for NLSM amplitudes in
Li:2025suo
.

Isocitrate dehydrogenase 2 mutation promotes cytarabine resistance in acute myeloid leukemia by Warburg effect
Authors: Jinrong Yang, Zixu Wang, Kun Wu, Bo Nie, Liyin Li, Jingyan Ruan, Qiang Zhou, Yun Zeng, Mingxia Shi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractMutation of isocitrate dehydrogenase 2 (IDH2) is a key factor in promoting cytarabine (Ara‐C) resistance in acute myeloid leukemia (AML), however the underly mechanism remains unclear. Acute myeloid leukemia cells, were cultured with either IDH2 knockdown (KD‐IDH2) or overexpression (OE‐IDH2) to elucidate the role of IDH2 in these leukemic cell lines. Additionally, mutant cell lines were engineered to replicate clinically relevant IDH2 mutations. To investigate cellular responses, the glycolytic inhibitor 2‐deoxy‐D‐glucose (2‐DG) was administered to the cells. Cell proliferation was quantified using a Cell Counting Kit‐8 (CCK‐8), while apoptosis was evaluated through propidium iodide staining followed by flow cytometry. Glycolytic metabolism levels were measured using a specific reagent kit, and Western blotting was employed to determine the expression levels of glycolysis‐related proteins. Transcriptome sequencing was conducted to elucidate the mechanisms by which IDH2 mutations influence glycolysis. Furthermore, both in vitro cell experiments and in vivo subcutaneous transplantation tumor models in nude mice were utilized to validate these mechanisms. OE‐IDH2 in AML cells, enhances resistance to the Ara‐C, promotes cell proliferation and glycolysis, and inhibits apoptosis. KD‐IDH2 exhibits opposite effects. Both IDH2 mutations and OE‐IDH2 produce similar effects on these cellular processes. The increase in glycolysis levels following IDH2 mutation may contribute to the reduced efficacy of Enasidenib in inhibiting the proliferation of IDH‐mutant AML cells. Transcriptome sequencing results indicate an enrichment of the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway in IDH2‐mutant AML cells. BEZ235 significantly inhibits the expression of phosphorylated PI3K (p‐PI3K), phosphorylated Akt (p‐Akt), mTOR, glycolytic metabolism, and Ara‐C resistance both in vitro and in vivo. Overexpression and mutation of IDH2 coordinate with the Warburg effect through the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway to promote Ara‐C resistance in AML.Keywords: acute myeloid leukemia, AML cell, Ara‐C resistant, IDH2 mutation, Warburg effect

INTRODUCTION

Acute myeloid leukemia (AML) is a malignancy that originates from hematopoietic stem cells, characterized by a complex pathogenesis that is yet to be fully elucidated. 1 , 2 Among the many molecular mechanisms of AML, isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH) indeed plays an important role as a key player in the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle. 3 IDH mutations have been shown to lead to hypermethylation of the genome and proteins in AML, ultimately resulting in the downregulation of differentiation‐associated genes and contributing to the development of the disease. 4 Currently, the IDH mutase family has emerged as an enticing target for precision therapeutic interventions in AML. Inhibitors of IDH, such as Ivosidenib and Enasidenib, have garnered approval for AML‐targeted treatment, demonstrating commendable tolerability. 5 However, resistance persists. As novel mutations emerge, the efficacy of IDH inhibitors diminishes, thereby amplifying the enigma of AML resistance and relapse. 6 , 7 Therefore, a deeper understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying this resistance, as well as the development of novel therapeutic strategies, is imperative to optimize the clinical outcomes of AML patients harboring IDH mutations.

The Warburg effect refers to the metabolic abnormalities in which cancer cells predominantly rely on glycolysis rather than the aerobic cycle. 8 Recent studies have consistently highlighted a correlation between metabolic aberrations triggered by oncogenes in solid tumors and the manifestation of aggressive tumor phenotypes as well as drug sensitivity. 9 Notably, studies have identified a critical role of IDH mutations are associated with increased mitochondrial oxidative metabolic capacity and heightened activity of electron transport chain complexes. 10 Despite the profound impact of IDH mutations on cellular metabolic landscapes, a comprehensive understanding of the intricate interplay between IDH2‐regulated glycolysis and its ramifications in AML drug resistance and relapse has yet to be fully elucidated.

Therefore, this study was designed to illuminate uncharted trajectories and targets for IDH2‐targeted therapeutic strategies in the context of drug‐resistant and relapsed AML. This multifaceted approach aims to provide novel insights and potential targets for targeted isocitrate dehydrogenase 2 (IDH2) therapy in Ara‐C‐resistant and relapsed AML.

MATERIAL AND METHODS

Materials

The culture and treatment of AML cells

The phenol‐red‐free RPMI medium 1640 (Gibco) was used to culture the HL‐60, KG‐1 cell lines (Procell). At the same time, supplemented with 10% fetal calf serum, 60 μg/mL penicillin, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin. The 1 × 106 cells and 20 mL with fresh medium were placed in flasks. The incubator was set at 37°C with 5% CO2 and passaged once every 2–3 days. The 2‐deoxy‐D‐glucose (2‐DG, MCE) as a glycolytic inhibitor was added when the cells were in log phase growth. The 50 nmol/L concentration of BEZ235 (Selleckchem) for 24 h.

Animal source

SPF‐grade (BALB/C) nude mice 4 of 6 weeks of age, male, weighing 16–20 g were purchased from Beijing Viton Lihua Laboratory Animal Co., Ltd (SCXK (Jing) 2021‐0011). Free consumption of food and water, with alternating light/dark cycles for 12 h each day. The operation of this study conforms to the 3R principle of experimental animals. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of Kunming Medical University (Kmmu20220996).

Methods

Cell counting kit‐8 (CCK‐8)

The cell suspension was prepared as 2 × 105 cells/mL then added 100 μL into 96‐well plates with 4 replicate wells in each group, and the cells were cultured at 37°C in a 5% CO2 incubator. Cell proliferation capacity was measured every 24 h according to the CCK‐8 instructions.

Drug sensitivity testing for Ara‐C

First, the cells were resuspended and diluted to the desired concentration. The diluted cells were added to cell plates with triple diluted chemotherapeutic agents and incubated for 72 h at 37°C with 5% CO2. Then cells were mixed 1:1 with ddH2O, added to the cell plate, and left to stand for 10 min before placing in the enzyme marker. The data were analyzed to plot the IC50 curve.

Silencing and overexpression of IDH2 of AML cells

IDH2 of AML cells were silenced by shRNA lentiviral vectors (Responsible by Tsingke). Overexpression vector is pLV4ltr‐Puro‐CMV, shRNA silencing vector is pLKO.1‐CMV‐copGFP‐PURO (cleavage site AgeI and EcoRI). Intervention with IDH2 inhibitor enasidinib (AG‐221, 5 μmol/L, Selleck) on IDH2 mutated AML cells.

Quantitative real‐time PCR (RT‐qPCR)

RT‐qPCR was used to detect silencing and overexpression effects, and the sequence of primers was synthesized by Tsingke (Table 1). Specific operations were performed strictly according to the instructions of the BeyoFast™ SYBR Green One‐Step qRT‐PCR Kit (Beyotime).

Glycolysis metabolic enzyme assay

The glucose, lactic acid, and pyruvate during glycolysis were detected by glycolysis‐related kits (Boxbio, AKSU001M), the ADP/ATP value was measured by ADP/ATP ratio assay kit (Abcam, ab65313), glucokinase activity was detected using glucokinase activity assay kit (Boxbio, AKSUO61M), phosphofructose activation relied on the phosphofructose activation kit to be observed (Boxbio, AKSU062M), and pyruvate kinase activity was tested by pyruvate kinase activity kit (Boxbio, AKSU066M). All experiments were conducted according to the instructions of the reagent kit.

Flow cytometry analysis for apoptosis

Cells were seeded in 12‐well plates at a density of 1 × 105/mL. The experimental group received the corresponding drugs, while the control group was treated with an equal amount of DMSO. After 48 h of incubation, the cells were harvested. Apoptosis was detected following the instructions provided in the experimental protocol.

Western blot

Separate and extract total protein from cells in culture, and measure the concentration of total protein. Western blotting was performed to measure the protein expression of HK2 (Abcam, ab273721), LDHA (Abcam, ab112996), PGK1 (Abcam, ab199438), PKM (Abcam, ab85555)2, p‐ P85α, p‐AKT, mTOR (Abcam, ab283852).

Transcriptome sequencing

A total of 12 samples were subjected to transcriptome sequencing (Tsingke Biotechnology Co., Ltd.). The sequencing results were performed by bioinformatic analysis. The differential expression gene was screened by Edge R package (Fold Change ≥ 1.5 and p‐value <0.05). ClusterProfiler and package loaded, the enrich org.Hs.for example,.dbGO and enrichKEGG were used for Gene Ontology function annotation analysis and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) signal pathway enrichment analysis, respectively.

The subcutaneous transplanted tumor model construction

Randomly divide nude mice into a control group, IDH2 mutation group, and IDH2 mutation and inhibitor treatment group. Logarithmic growth phase cells HL‐60, KG‐1, and their mutant cell lines were used to construct a subcutaneous transplanted tumor model. Nude mice with tumors were divided into 3 groups on average, with 6 mice in each group, Intervened with a pathway inhibitor after 14 days. All model animals were treated with Ara‐C 2 mg/kg/2 days by intravenous injection, control group was injected with an equal amount of physiological saline. IDH2 mutation and inhibitor treatment group with PI3K/Akt signaling pathway inhibitor (BEZ235, 60 mg/kg/day by gavage). The body weight and tumor size of nude mice were detected every 3 days after the use of inhibitors. The experiment will end in 35 days. Vernier calipers were used to measure the longest diameter of the transplanted tumor (a) and the largest transverse diameter in the vertical direction (b), and the volume of the transplanted tumor was calculated according to the formula V (mm3) = ab2/2, finally, the growth curve of the transplanted tumor was plotted.

Statistical analysis

All data were presented in the form of mean ± standard deviation (mean ± SD), and differences between different groups were one‐way or two‐way ANOVA by GraphPad Prism 9.0 software. *p < 0.05 represents statistically significant.

RESULTS

IDH2 participates in the development of AML

To investigate the effect of IDH2 on AML, we overexpressed or knocked down the expression of IDH2 in the AML cell lines. RT‐qPCR and Western Blot assay indicated the successful construction of knockdown and overexpressed AML cell lines (Figure 1A–C). We examined the impact of IDH2 expression levels on AML cell proliferation, drug resistance and apoptosis. Our results demonstrated that OE‐IDH2 significantly increased the proliferative capacity of HL‐60 and KG‐1 cell lines (Figure 1D,E), elevated the IC50 value of Ara‐C in AML cells (Figure 1F,G) and reduced the level of apoptosis (Figure 1H–J). Conversely, KD‐IDH2 reversed these trends.

Furthermore, OE‐IDH2 decreased glucose content and ADP/ATP ratio, while promoting cellular lactic acid and pyruvate levels (Figure 1K–P). Additionally, OE‐IDH2 enhanced cellular extracellular acidification rate (ECAR) (Figure 1L,M) and increased the activities of key kinases involved in glycolytic metabolism (PK, PFK, KH) (Figure 1Q–S). Conversely, KD‐IDH2 reversed these trends. Western blot analysis revealed that the expression levels of HK2, LDHA, PGK1, and PKM2 were elevated in the OE‐IDH2 group but decreased in the KD‐IDH2 group (Figure 1T). These findings suggest that IDH2 play a crucial role in the development of AML.

IDH2 mutations reduce AML sensitivity to Enasidinib

Given that IDH2 mutations enhance AML resistance, we utilized IDH2 mutant AML cells to assess the effect of Enasidenib on their proliferation and apoptosis. Enasidenib inhibited proliferation and promoted apoptosis in AML wild‐type cells. However, in IDH2 mutant AML cells, the inhibitory effect of Enasidenib on proliferation was not significant, nor did it significantly promote apoptosis (Figure 2A–C). Additionally, Enasidenib inhibited glucose, lactate, and pyruvate levels in AML wild‐type cells, but this inhibitory effect was significantly reduced in IDH2 mutant AML cells (Figure 2D–F). These fingdings suggest that IDH2 mutations reduce AML sensitivity to Enasidinib.

IDH2 mutations regulate the development of AML by regulating the glycolytic pathway

A series of key enzymes in glycolysis play crucial roles in AML proliferation, invasion, metastasis, and the progression of drug resistance. To investigate the effect of IDH2 mutations on glycolysis, we used glycolytic inhibitors (2‐DG) to test whether the IDH2 mutation affects the sensitivity of AML to Ara‐C through the glycolytic pathway. The experimental results show that IDH2 mutation made the enlarged 2‐DG IC50 of AML cells (Figure 3 A). IDH2 mutants increased the cell proliferative capacity and decreased apoptosis in AML cells (Figure 3B,C). Moreover, IDH2 mutation decreased the ADP/ATP ratio and promoted the extracellular acidification rate (ECAR) of AML (Figure 3D,E). In contrast, 2‐DG reversed the trends caused by IDH2 mutation. IDH2 mutation induced cellular glucose consumption, lactic acid, and pyruvate production (Figure 3F–H), and promoted the glycolysis‐related key enzymes HK, PFK, and PK (Figure 3I–K). In summary, 2‐DG significantly inhibited cell proliferation and glycolysis in IDH2 mutation cell lines. These findings suggest that IDH2 mutations may affect the sensitivity of AML to Ara‐C through the glycolytic pathway.

Transcriptome sequencing analysis of the regulatory mechanism of IDH2 mutation

Based on the above studies, we have preliminarily demonstrated that IDH2 mutation can affect glycolysis, proliferation, apoptosis in AML, thereby increasing drug resistance. To further investigate the mechanisms by which IDH2 mutations regulate AML development, we performed RNA‐seq analysis. Sequencing results from 12 samples identified 2034 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in HL‐60 cell line control group versus IDH2‐mutant HL‐60 cells group, including 535 up‐regulated genes and 1499 down‐regulated genes after mutation (Figure 4A). A total of 253 DEGs were found in the control KG‐1 cell line versus IDH2‐mutant KG‐1 cells, of which 54 up‐regulated genes and 199 down‐regulated genes were found after mutation (Figure 4B). KEGG pathway analysis revealed significant enrichment of cancer‐related pathways following IDH2 mutation in HL‐60 cells. Pathways associated with environmental information processing such as the PI3K‐Akt signaling pathway, cytokine‐cytokine receptor interaction, and Ras signaling pathway, were notably enriched (Figure 4C). In KG‐1 cells, metabolism‐related pathways, including the biosynthesis of amino acids and the MAPK and PI3K‐Akt signaling pathways, were enriched following IDH2 mutation (Figure 4D). The PI3K‐Akt signaling pathway was significantly enriched in the analysis of both cell lines, suggesting that this pathway may be an important target for IDH2 mutation‐regulated regulation of AML development.

Inhibition of the PI3K‐Akt pathway reduced Ara‐C resistance and glycolysis levels in IDH 2 mutant cells

RNA‐seq combined with bioinformatics analysis revealed significant enrichment of the PI3K‐AKT‐mTOR pathway in IDH2 mutant cells. To validate the involvement of this pathway, we conducted cell experiments. Western blot results in HL‐60 and KG‐1 cells showed a significant increase in the activities of p‐PI3K, p‐AKT, and p‐mTOR after IDH2 mutation (Figure 5A). Additionally, IDH2 mutations led to a significant increase in the activities of glycolytic enzymes HK2, LDHA, PGK2, and PKM2 (Figure 5B). To explore the role of this pathway in AML development, we used the PI3K‐Akt pathway inhibitor BEZ235. The results indicated that BEZ235 reversed the effects of IDH2 mutations on cell proliferation, ADP/ATP ratio, pyruvate, and lactic acid content (Figure 5C–F). The use of BEZ235 also resulted in a decrease in cell IC50 (Figure 5G), Furthermore, the levels of p‐PI3K, p‐AKT, and p‐mTOR were reduced (Figure 5H). These findings suggest that inhibition of the PI3K‐Akt pathway by BEZ235 reduces Ara‐C resistance and glycolysis levels in IDH2 mutant cells.

Inhibition of the PI3K‐AKT‐mTOR pathway inhibits glycolysis and tumor growth in AML

We further explored the role of the PI3K‐Akt pathway in AML development in vivo. The fastest tumor growth was observed in AML cell‐transplanted tumor nude mice with IDH2 mutations, resulting in the maximum tumor weight (Figure 6A–C). Tissue homogenate assay of transplanted tumors in each group indicated the highest ECAR in the IDH2 mutation group (Figure 6D), the glucose levels in the IDH2 mutation group were significantly lower than those in the wild‐type group, while lactic acid and pyruvate levels were significantly higher (Figure 6E–G). The activities of glycolytic enzymes HK2, LDHA, PGK1, and PKM2 followed the same trends (Figure 6H). However, these trends were reversed by using the PI3K/Akt pathway inhibitor BEZ235. These findings further clarify that IDH2 mutations affect the glycolytic process in AML through the PI3K‐Akt pathway, thereby promoting the development of AML.

DISCUSSION

Clinical investigations have unveiled a noteworthy correlation between glycolytic activity in AML patients and extended overall survival. 11 Encouragingly, these studies have illuminated the use of glycolytic disruptors that can induce apoptosis of AML cells, holding promise for targeting the glycolytic pathway in AML treatment. The primary objective of our study was to meticulously dissect the intricate regulatory mechanisms governing glycolytic metabolic reprogramming, which undergo alteration due to mutations in the IDH2 gene, providing novel trajectories and ideas for AML‐targeted IDH2 therapy. Our outcomes revealed an increased cellular Ara‐C resistance following IDH2 mutation and overexpression. Simultaneously, the introduction of glycolysis inhibitor enzymes into IDH2 gene mutant cells led to the inhibition of cell proliferation. Conversely, a low level of IDH2 was favorable for enhancing AML sensitivity to Ara‐C treatment. 12 Collectively, these findings underscore the implication of IDH2 mutation in the orchestration of AML progression and Ara‐C resistance, with the glycolytic pathway emerging as a significant regulatory avenue. 13

The transcriptome of IDH2 mutant cells has indicated key pathways in glycolysis regulation, such as the PI3K‐Akt signaling pathway, MAPK signaling pathway, and cytokine‐cytokine receptor interaction, among others, these are consistent with previous research. 14 Notably, the PI3K/Akt pathway is a pathway commonly obtained in two cell lines, the hyperactivation of the PI3K/Akt pathway has been implicated in tumor survival, growth, and metabolism. 15 The PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway serves as a linchpin in hematopoietic cells, orchestrating crucial functions such as cell proliferation, differentiation, and survival. 16 In the context of AML, the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway is highly expressed and significantly contributes to cellular metabolic reprogramming through a multitude of mechanisms, aligning with AML development and resistance to chemotherapy drugs. 17 Enhanced PI3K activation characterizes around 50% of AML patients, and Akt phosphorylation at various PIK sites has been linked to unfavorable patient survival. 18 The activation of Akt sustains refractory AML status by fueling glycolysis and mitochondrial respiration. 19 Furthermore, Akt overexpression has been shown to promote aerobic glycolysis, influencing both pyruvate entry into mitochondrial metabolism and oxidative phosphorylation. Inhibition of oxidative phosphorylation heightens Ara‐C sensitivity in leukemia cell lines. 20 This pathway's regulation can be attributed to membrane‐bound proteins in certain instances, while molecular isoforms and mutations play a role in others. Notably, FLT3 mutations foster aberrant PI3K/Akt/mTOR activation, a common occurrence in AML. 21 Cumulative evidence has clarified that disrupted PI3K/Akt expression directly shapes AML's glycolytic metabolism.

Our investigations validated that IDH2 mutations drive aberrant PI3K/Akt expression, thereby influencing metabolic reprogramming in tumor glycolysis. This reprogramming potentially underpins tumor progression and contributes to the correlation between IDH2 mutations and unfavorable patient survival. Given the clinical utilization of numerous pathway inhibitors targeting PI3K/Akt/mTOR for AML treatment, along with the promising potential of combining these with MEK, SYK inhibitors, and other agents, there is anticipation that future strategies may attempt to synergize IDH2 inhibitors with PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway inhibitors to augment AML treatment outcomes. 22

CONCLUSION

The IDH2 mutations are involved in Ara‐C resistance by affecting the process of glycolysis in AML, and the PI3K‐Akt signaling pathway plays an important role in this process. These pathways are expected to be important targets for targeted therapeutic intervention in the AML setting.

Abstract
. Given k k similarity classes of invertible matrices, the Deligne-Simpson problem asks to determine whether or not one can find matrices in these classes whose product is the identity and with no common invariant subspace. The first author conjectured an answer in terms of an associated root system, and proved one implication in joint work with Shaw. In this paper we prove the other implication, thus confirming the conjecture.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 15A24; Secondary 14H60, 16G20, 34M50

1.
Introduction

Given conjugacy classes
C_{1},\dots,C_{k}
in
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, the Deligne-Simpson problem asks to determine whether or not there is a solution to the equation

A_{1}A_{2}\dots A_{k}=1\qquad(A_{i}\in C_{i})

which is irreducible in the sense that the
A_{i}
have no non-trivial common invariant subspace. This is motivated by the problem of classifying systems of linear ordinary differential equations in the complex domain in terms of their local monodromies; see
[
13
]
for more background and motivation. In
[
2
]
the first author has given a conjectural solution to this problem, and one implication has been proven by Crawley-Boevey and Shaw
[
7
]
. Here we prove the other implication, thus confirming the conjecture.

In order to fix the conjugacy classes, we choose a
weight sequence

\mathbf{w}=(w_{1},\dots,w_{k})
with
w_{i}\geq 1
and a collection of non-zero complex numbers
\xi=(\xi_{ij})
(
1\leq i\leq k
,
1\leq j\leq w_{i}
) with

(A_{i}-\xi_{i1}1)(A_{i}-\xi_{i2}1)\dots(A_{i}-\xi_{i,w_{i}}1)=0

for
A_{i}\in C_{i}
. For example
w_{i}
can be the degree of the minimal polynomial of
A_{i}
, and
\xi_{i1},\dots,\xi_{i,w_{i}}
its roots, in some order. The conjugacy classes are then determined by
n
,
\mathbf{w}
,
\xi
and the ranks of the products

(A_{i}-\xi_{i1}1)(A_{i}-\xi_{i2}1)\dots(A_{i}-\xi_{ij}1)

where
A_{i}\in C_{i}
, for
1\leq i\leq k
and
1\leq j<w_{i}
.

This data can best be understood in terms of a suitable root system. Associated to a quiver
Q
with vertex set
I
there is root system (consisting of real and imaginary roots,
positive or negative) contained in the root lattice

\Gamma_{Q}=\bigoplus_{v\in I}\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{v}=\{\sum_{v\in I}n_{v}\alpha_{v}:n_{v}\in\mathbb{Z}\}

where the
\alpha_{v}
may be regarded as symbols, see for example
[
12
]
. The Euler form
\langle-,-\rangle_{Q}
on
\Gamma_{Q}
is given by

\langle\sum_{v\in I}n_{v}\alpha_{v},\sum_{v\in I}n^{\prime}_{v}\alpha_{v}\rangle_{Q}=\sum_{v\in I}n_{v}n^{\prime}_{v}-\sum_{a\in Q}n_{t(a)}n^{\prime}_{h(a)},

where
h(a)
and
t(a)
are the head and tail vertices of an arrow
a
in
Q
. Its symmetrization is
(\alpha,\beta)_{Q}=\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle_{Q}+\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle_{Q}
, and the corresponding quadratic form is
q_{Q}(\alpha)=\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle_{Q}
. We define
p_{Q}(\alpha)=1-q_{Q}(\alpha)
.

Given a weight sequence
\mathbf{w}
, let
Q_{\mathbf{w}}
be the star-shaped quiver

with vertex set
I_{\mathbf{w}}=\{*\}\cup\{[i,j]:1\leq i\leq k,1\leq j<w_{i}\}
.
To simplify notation we write a subscript
[i,j]
as
ij
or
i,j
. We write
\Gamma_{\mathbf{w}}
for the root lattice
\Gamma_{Q_{\mathbf{w}}}
, and
p_{\mathbf{w}}
for the function
p_{Q_{\mathbf{w}}}
, so elements of
\Gamma_{w}
are of the form

\alpha=n_{*}\alpha_{*}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{w_{i}-1}n_{ij}\alpha_{ij}\quad(n_{*},n_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}),

and

p_{\mathbf{w}}(\alpha)=1-n_{*}^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{w_{i}-1}n_{ij}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}n_{*}n_{i1}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{w_{i}-2}n_{ij}n_{i,j+1}.

Given
\xi=(\xi_{ij})
as above, for
\alpha\in\Gamma_{\mathbf{w}}
we define

\xi^{[\alpha]}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\prod_{j=1}^{w_{i}}\xi_{ij}^{n_{i,j-1}-n_{ij}}

with the convention that
n_{i0}=n_{*}
and
n_{i,w_{i}}=0
.

The conjugacy classes
C_{i}
determine an element
\alpha_{C}\in\Gamma_{\mathbf{w}}
, with
n_{*}=n
and

n_{ij}=\rank(A_{i}-\xi_{i1}1)(A_{i}-\xi_{i2}1)\dots(A_{i}-\xi_{ij}1)

where
A_{i}\in C_{i}
, for
1\leq i\leq k
and
1\leq j<w_{i}
. Our main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

For there to be an irreducible solution to

A_{1}\dots A_{k}=1
with matrices
A_{i}\in C_{i}

it is necessary and sufficient that
\alpha=\alpha_{C}
be a positive root,

\xi^{[\alpha]}=1
, and
p_{\mathbf{w}}(\alpha)>p_{\mathbf{w}}(\beta)+p_{\mathbf{w}}(\gamma)+\dots
for
any nontrivial decomposition of
\alpha
as a sum of positive
roots
\alpha=\beta+\gamma+\dots
with
\xi^{[\beta]}=\xi^{[\gamma]}=\dots=1
.

The sufficiency is proved in
[
7
]
. In section
7
we prove necessity (the combination of Lemma
7.1
and Theorem
7.5
).

This result was already announced in
[
3
]
. The original plan was to prove the results of section
5
along the lines of
[
1
, Theorem 9.1]
, with parabolic sheaves and their connections replacing representations of quivers and deformed preprojective algebras. However, the general position argument used in
[
1
]
turned out to be difficult to write up, so this paper is based on the argument of
[
6
]
instead.

A draft of this paper, including a complete proof, was written in May 2018. Our plan was to combine it with results of the second author on coherent sheaves on weighted projective lines and connections on such sheaves, clarifying and improving the results in
[
5
]
. Partial results along these lines were included in his 2023 Habilitation thesis
[
10
]
, and he announced a solution of the DSP along these lines in his lectures at the 21st International Conference on Representations of Algebras in Shanghai in August 2024. But this work is still not written up. With the announcement of another proof, obtained independently by Cheng Shu
[
18
]
, this slow approach is no longer tenable, and so the present paper is based on the draft from 2018.

2.
Weighted projective lines and parabolic bundles

Let
\mathbb{X}
be the weighted projective line over
\mathbb{C}
consisting of the projective line
X=\mathbb{P}^{1}
,
a collection
D=(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k})
of distinct marked points in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
,
and a weight sequence
\mathbf{w}=(w_{1},\dots,w_{k})
with
w_{i}\geq 1

(where
w_{i}=1
is equivalent to the point
a_{i}
being unmarked).

Let
\Coh\mathbb{X}
be the category of coherent sheaves on
\mathbb{X}
defined by Geigle and Lenzing
[
8
]
.
We call its objects
parabolic sheaves
.
This is an abelian category, and we use the name
subsheaf
for a sub-object in this category.
Every parabolic sheaf is the direct sum of a torsion parabolic sheaf and a torsion-free parabolic sheaf.
There is one simple torsion parabolic sheaf
S_{a}
supported at each point
a\notin D
, and at the point
a_{i}
there are simple
torsion parabolic sheaves
S_{ij}
(
0\leq j<w_{i}
) with exact sequences

0\to\mathcal{O}(j\vec{x}_{i})\to\mathcal{O}((j+1)\vec{x}_{i})\to S_{ij}\to 0

see
[
8
, (2.5.2)]
.
As in
[
17
, §5.1]
,
we identify the Grothendieck group
K_{0}(\Coh\mathbb{X})
with
\hat{\Gamma}=\Gamma\oplus\mathbb{Z}\partial
,
where
\Gamma=\Gamma_{\mathbf{w}}
is the root lattice for the quiver
Q_{\mathbf{w}}
,
with

[\mathcal{O}(k\vec{c})]=\alpha_{*}+k\partial,\quad[S_{a}]=\partial,\quad[S_{ij}]=\begin{cases}\alpha_{ij}&(j\neq 0)\\
\partial-\sum_{\ell=1}^{w_{i}-1}\alpha_{i\ell}&(j=0).\end{cases}

The
dimension vector

\dimv\mathcal{E}\in\Gamma
and degree
\deg_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{Z}
are defined by

[\mathcal{E}]=\dimv\mathcal{E}+(\deg_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\mathcal{E})\partial.

The Euler form
\langle-,-\rangle_{\mathbb{X}}:\Gamma\times\Gamma\to\mathbb{Z}
with

\langle[\mathcal{E}],[\mathcal{F}]\rangle_{\mathbb{X}}=\dim\Hom(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})-\dim\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})

is given as follows.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
\alpha=\sum_{v\in I}n_{v}\alpha_{v}
,
\alpha^{\prime}=\sum_{v\in I}n^{\prime}_{v}\alpha_{v}\in\Gamma
and
k,k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\langle\alpha+k\partial,\alpha^{\prime}+k^{\prime}\partial\rangle_{\mathbb{X}}=\langle\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\rangle_{Q_{\mathbf{w}}}+k^{\prime}n_{*}-kn^{\prime}_{*}.

Proof.

Straightforward.
∎

Torsion-free parabolic sheaves on
\mathbb{X}
are also called
parabolic bundles
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and following Lenzing
[
14
]
,
they may be identified with collections
\mathcal{E}=(E,E_{ij})
consisting of an (algebraic or holomorphic) vector bundle
E
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and flags of vector subspaces

E_{a_{i}}=E_{i0}\supseteq E_{i1}\supseteq\dots\supseteq E_{i,w_{i}-1}\supseteq E_{i,w_{i}}=0

of the fibres
E_{a_{i}}
at each of the marked points
a_{i}
.
As in
[
4
]
, we make the identification in such a way that

\dimv\mathcal{E}=n_{*}\alpha_{*}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{w_{i}-1}n_{ij}\alpha_{ij}\in\Gamma,

with
n_{*}=\rank E
and
n_{ij}=\dim E_{ij}
, and
\deg_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\mathcal{E}
is the usual degree of the vector bundle
E
. Observe that the dimension vector of a parabolic bundle is necessarily
strict
, meaning that
n_{*}\geq n_{i1}\geq n_{i2}\geq\dots\geq n_{i,w_{i}-1}\geq 0
.
We denote by
\Bun\mathbb{X}
the category of parabolic bundles on
\mathbb{X}
.
Morphisms between parabolic bundles correspond to vector bundle homomorphisms which respect the flags.

We write
\Omega=\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(\log D)
for the sheaf of differential 1-forms on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with logarithmic poles on
D
. It is a line bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. If
\theta\in\Hom(F,E\otimes\Omega)
, then there are residue maps
\Res_{a_{i}}\theta:F_{a_{i}}\to E_{a_{i}}
.

Geigle and Lenzing introduce a twist
\mathcal{E}(\vec{\omega})
, giving Serre duality on
\Coh\mathbb{X}
in the form

\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})\cong\Hom(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E}(\vec{\omega}))^{*}.

Lemma 2.2
.

Given parabolic bundles
\mathcal{E}=(E,E_{ij})
and
\mathcal{F}=(F,F_{ij})
,
we can identify
\Hom(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E}(\vec{\omega}))
with the set

\begin{split}H_{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E}}=\{\theta\in\Hom(F,E\otimes\Omega):(\Res_{a_{i}}\theta)(F_{i,j})\subseteq E_{i,j+1}\\
(1\leq i\leq k,0\leq j<w_{i})\}.\end{split}

Proof.

In the notation of
[
8
, §2.2]
, we have
\vec{\omega}=(k-2)\vec{c}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\vec{x}_{i}
, so

\mathcal{E}(\vec{\omega})=(E\otimes\Omega)(-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\vec{x}_{i})
.

If
\mathcal{E}
is torsion-free, then so is
\mathcal{E}(\vec{\omega})
.
Now the twist operation
\mathcal{E}(\vec{x}_{i})
of
[
8
, §1.7]
can be understood as in
[
14
, §4.1]
and
[
5
, §2]

as the operation of rotating a cycle.

The fibre
E_{a}
at a point
a\in X
is
E\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X,a}}(\mathcal{O}_{X,a}/\mathfrak{m}_{X,a})
.
We write
i_{a}(V)
for the skyscraper sheaf given by a finite-dimensional vector space
V
at the point
a
. It is a coherent sheaf
isomorphic to the direct sum of
\dim V
copies of the simple torsion sheaf at
a
.
There is a natural map
E\to i_{a}(E_{a})
. Given any subspace
V
of
E_{a}
we get an exact sequence

0\to E^{a,V}\to E\to i_{a}(E_{a}/V)\to 0.

and
E^{a,0}\cong E(-a)
.

The parabolic structure of
\mathcal{F}
at
a_{i}
is given by subspaces
F_{ij}
, and it corresponds to a cycle of vector bundles

F^{a_{i},0}\to F^{a_{i},F_{i,w_{i}-1}}\to\dots F^{a_{i},F_{i2}}\to F^{a_{i},F_{i1}}\to F\cong F^{a_{i},0}(a_{i})

Now
\mathcal{E}(\vec{\omega})
is given by tensoring
E
with
\Omega
and rotating the cycle at each marked point, so at
a_{i}
it is given by a cycle of vector bundles

E^{a_{i},E_{i1}}(-a_{i})\otimes\Omega\to E^{a_{i},0}\otimes\Omega\to E^{a_{i},E_{i,w_{i}-1}}\otimes\Omega\to\dots\to E^{a_{i},E_{i1}}\otimes\Omega

Now a morphism from
\mathcal{F}
to
\mathcal{E}(\vec{\omega})
is given by a morphism between these cycles. Since the maps in the cycles are monomorphisms, such a morphism is determined by a morphism
F\to E\otimes\Omega
, subject to the condition that it sends

F^{a_{i},F_{ij}}
into
E^{a_{i},E_{i,j+1}}\otimes\Omega
for all
j
.
This is the condition that
(\Res_{a_{i}}\theta)(F_{i,j})\subseteq E_{i,j+1}
.
∎

3.
Connections on parabolic bundles

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a weighted projective line of weight type
\mathbf{w}=(w_{1},\dots,w_{k})
.
Let
\zeta=(\zeta_{ij})
(
1\leq i\leq k
,
1\leq j\leq w_{i}
) be a collection of complex numbers.

We say that
\zeta
is
non-resonant
if the numbers
\zeta_{ij}
for fixed
i
never differ by a non-zero integer,
that is
\zeta_{ij}-\zeta_{i,j^{\prime}}\notin\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
for
1\leq i\leq k
and
1\leq j,j^{\prime}\leq w_{i}
.

In
[
5
]
we have constructed functorial exact sequences

0\to\mathcal{E}(\vec{\omega})\to D_{\zeta}(\mathcal{E})\to\mathcal{E}\to 0

for
\mathcal{E}
a parabolic sheaf on
\mathbb{X}
, and we define a
\zeta
-connection
on
\mathcal{E}
to be a section
s:\mathcal{E}\to D_{\zeta}(\mathcal{E})
.

Recall that a parabolic bundle on
\mathbb{X}
can be identified with a tuple
(E,E_{ij})
consisting of a vector bundle
E
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}

and flags of subspaces
E_{ij}
of the fibres
E_{a_{i}}
.
In this setting we can make a different definition.

Recall that
\Omega
denotes the sheaf of differential 1-forms on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with logarithmic poles on
D
.
Given a vector bundle
E
on
X
, recall that a
logarithmic connection on
E

is a homomorphism of sheaves of abelian groups

\nabla:E\to E\otimes\Omega

satisfying Leibnitz’s rule. It has residues
\Res_{a_{i}}\nabla\in\End(E_{a_{i}})
.
The following is a special case of
[
16
, Corollaire 3]
, where, as mentioned in
[
2
, §7]
,
we use the opposite sign convention to Mihai for residues.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
E
is a vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
\nabla:E\to E\otimes\Omega
is a logarithmic connection,
then

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\tr(\Res_{a_{i}}\nabla)=-\deg E.

If
\mathcal{E}=(E_{ij},E)
is a parabolic bundle, an alternative notion of a
\zeta
-connection on
\mathcal{E}
is a logarithmic connection
\nabla
on
E
satisfying

(\Res_{a_{i}}\nabla-\zeta_{ij}1)(E_{i,j-1})\subseteq E_{ij}

for all
1\leq i\leq k
and
1\leq j\leq w_{i}
.
In
[
5
]
we have proved that these are equivalent concepts.

Lemma 3.2
.

For a parabolic bundle
\mathcal{E}=(E,E_{ij})
on
\mathbb{X}
, the
\zeta
-connections
s
on
\mathcal{E}
are in 1:1 correspondence with
\zeta
-connections
\nabla
on
E
.

We denote by
\CohConn_{\zeta}\mathbb{X}
the category of pairs
(\mathcal{E},s)

where
\mathcal{E}
is a parabolic sheaf on
\mathbb{X}
and
s:\mathcal{E}\to D_{\zeta}(\mathcal{E})
is a
\zeta
-connection
on
\mathcal{E}
.
Given
(\mathcal{E},s)
in
\CohConn_{\zeta}\mathbb{X}
, we say that a subsheaf
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathcal{E}

is
invariant
provided that the composition

\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{E}\xrightarrow{s}D_{\zeta}(\mathcal{E})\to D_{\zeta}(\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{F})

is zero, so that the restriction of
s
to
\mathcal{F}
factors through
the morphism
D_{\zeta}(\mathcal{F})\to D_{\zeta}(\mathcal{E})
, where
\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{E}
is the inclusion morphism.
In this case the induced morphism
s^{\prime}:\mathcal{F}\to D_{\zeta}(\mathcal{F})
is a
\zeta
-connection on
\mathcal{F}
, and there is an induced morphism

\overline{s}:\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{F}\to D_{\zeta}(\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{F})
which is a
\zeta
-connection
on
\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{F}
.

We say that a pair
(\mathcal{E},s)
in
\CohConn_{\zeta}\mathbb{X}
is
irreducible
if
\mathcal{E}
is non-zero and
it has has no non-zero proper invariant subsheaves.
We say that a pair
(\mathcal{E},s)
with
\mathcal{E}
non-zero a parabolic bundle is
weakly irreducible

if it has no non-zero proper invariant subsheaves such that the quotient is a parabolic bundle.

We denote by
\BunConn_{\zeta}\mathbb{X}
the category of pairs
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)

consisting of a parabolic bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
\mathbb{X}
and a
\zeta
-connection
\nabla
.
By the discussion above, this can be identified with the full subcategory of
\CohConn_{\zeta}\mathbb{X}

consisting of the pairs
(\mathcal{E},s)
where
\mathcal{E}
is a parabolic bundle.

For
\alpha=n_{*}\alpha_{*}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{w_{i}-1}n_{ij}\alpha_{ij}\in\Gamma_{\mathbf{w}}
we define

\zeta*{[\alpha]}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{w_{i}}\zeta_{ij}{(n_{i,j-1}-n_{ij})},

with the convention that
n_{i0}=n_{*}
and
n_{i,w_{i}}=0
.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
\mathcal{E}
is a parabolic bundle on
\mathbb{X}
, then there exists
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)\in\BunConn_{\zeta}\mathbb{X}

if and only if
\deg_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\mathcal{E}^{\prime}+\zeta*[\dimv\mathcal{E}^{\prime}]=0
for all indecomposable direct summands
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{E}
.
In particular, if there exists
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
, then

\deg_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\mathcal{E}+\zeta*[\dimv\mathcal{E}]=0
.

Proof.

This is
[
2
, Theorem 7.1]
. Alternatively, the last part follows directly
from Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

The invariant subsheaves
\mathcal{F}=(F,F_{ij})
of an object
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)\in\BunConn_{\zeta}\mathbb{X}
are given by subsheaves

F
of
E
with
\nabla(F)\subseteq F\otimes\Omega
,
and subspaces
F_{ij}
satisfying

(\Res_{a_{i}}\nabla-\zeta_{ij})(F_{i,j-1})\subseteq F_{ij}

for all
i,j
. In particular, if
F
is a subsheaf of
E
with
\nabla(F)\subseteq F\otimes\Omega
, one can take
F_{ij}=F_{a_{i}}\cap E_{ij}
.

Proof.

Clear.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

If
\zeta
is non-resonant, then
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)\in\BunConn_{\zeta}\mathbb{X}
is irreducible if and only if it is weakly irreducible.

Proof.

If it is irreducible, it is certainly weakly irreducible.
Conversely, suppose it is weakly irreducible, but not irreducible.
Then there is a non-zero proper invariant subsheaf
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
of
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
.
Since
\mathcal{E}
is torsion-free, so is
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
.
Let
(\mathcal{E}^{\prime},\nabla^{\prime})
be the corresponding element of
\BunConn_{\zeta}\mathbb{X}
.
Since
\zeta
is non-resonant, the morphism
(\mathcal{E}^{\prime},\nabla^{\prime})\to(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
has constant rank,
see
[
2
, Theorem 6.1]
. It follows that the quotient
\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
is torsion-free.
But this contradicts weak irreducibility.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

If
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
is in
\BunConn_{\zeta}\mathbb{X}
,
and there is a decomposition
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}^{1}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{2}
with

\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{E}^{2},\mathcal{E}^{1})=0
, then
\mathcal{E}^{1}
is
an invariant subsheaf for
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
.

Proof.

It is rather easy to see this for
(\mathcal{E},s)\in\CohConn_{\zeta}\mathbb{X}
. The composition

\mathcal{E}^{1}\to\mathcal{E}\xrightarrow{s}D_{\zeta}(\mathcal{E})\to D_{\zeta}(\mathcal{E}^{2})\to\mathcal{E}^{2}

is the same as the inclusion of
\mathcal{E}^{1}
in
\mathcal{E}
followed by the projection onto
\mathcal{E}^{2}
,
so it is zero. Thus the map
\mathcal{E}^{1}\to D_{\zeta}(\mathcal{E}^{2})
factors through
\mathcal{E}^{2}(\vec{\omega})
.
But then it is zero, as
\Hom(\mathcal{E}^{1},\mathcal{E}^{2}(\vec{\omega}))\cong D\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{E}^{2},\mathcal{E}^{1})=0
by
Serre duality.

For later comparison, however, we write it out in terms of parabolic bundles.
The underlying bundle of
\mathcal{E}
is
E^{1}\oplus E^{2}

and the parabolic structure is
E_{ij}=E^{1}_{ij}\oplus E^{2}_{ij}
.
The connection takes block form

\begin{pmatrix}\nabla_{11}&\nabla_{12}\\
\nabla_{21}&\nabla_{22}\end{pmatrix}

where
\nabla_{pp}:E^{p}\to E^{p}\otimes\Omega
is a logarithmic connection on
E^{p}

and
\nabla_{pq}:E^{q}\to E^{p}\otimes\Omega
is a homomorphism of bundles for
p\neq q
.
Moreover the residues

\Res_{a_{i}}\nabla:E^{1}_{a_{i}}\oplus E^{2}_{a_{i}}\to E^{1}_{a_{i}}\oplus E^{2}_{a_{i}}

take block form

R=\begin{pmatrix}R_{11}&R_{12}\\
R_{21}&R_{22}\end{pmatrix}

where
R_{pq}=\Res_{a_{i}}\nabla_{pq}:E^{q}_{a_{i}}\to E^{p}_{a_{i}}
.
By assumption

(R-\zeta_{ij}1)(E_{i,j-1})\subseteq E_{ij}

which implies that

(R_{pp}-\zeta_{ij}1)(E^{p}_{i,j-1})\subseteq E^{p}_{ij}

and

R_{pq}(E^{q}_{i,j-1})\subseteq E^{p}_{ij}

for
p\neq q
.
Thus by Lemma
2.2
,

\nabla_{21}
defines a homomorphism of parabolic bundles from
\mathcal{E}^{1}
to
\mathcal{E}^{2}(\vec{\omega})
, so it must be zero.
Thus
\mathcal{E}^{1}
is an invariant subsheaf for
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
.
∎

4.
The tubular case

We begin with some standard definitions which are valid for all
\mathbb{X}
, but particularly useful in the tubular case.
Let
\mathbb{X}
be a weighted projective line of weight type
\mathbf{w}=(w_{1},\dots,w_{k})
.
Let
w
be the least common multiple of the components of
\mathbf{w}
.
We write
\deg_{\mathbb{X}}\mathcal{E}
for the degree of a parabolic sheaf in the sense of Geigle and Lenzing
[
8
, Proposition 2.8]
,

\deg_{\mathbb{X}}\mathcal{E}=w\deg_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\mathcal{E}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{w_{i}-1}n_{ij}w/w_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}

where the
n_{ij}
are given by

\dimv\mathcal{E}=n_{*}\alpha_{*}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{w_{i}-1}n_{ij}\alpha_{ij}.

The
slope
of a parabolic sheaf
\mathcal{E}
is
\mu(\mathcal{E})=\deg_{\mathbb{X}}\mathcal{E}/\rank\mathcal{E}
, see
[
15
, §2.5]
,
so parabolic bundles have slope in
\mathbb{Q}
, and the torsion parabolic sheaves have slope
\infty
.
A parabolic sheaf
\mathcal{E}
is
semistable
(respectively
stable
) if for every non-zero proper subsheaf

\mathcal{F}
of
\mathcal{E}
we have
\mu(\mathcal{F})\leq\mu(\mathcal{E})
(respectively
\mu(\mathcal{F})<\mu(\mathcal{E}))
.

In the rest of this section we assume that
\mathbb{X}
is of
tubular
type, or
virtual genus
1,
meaning that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}1/w_{i}=k-2
.
Assuming that the
w_{i}
are non-increasing, the possible weight types are
(2,2,2,2)
,
(3,3,3)
,
(4,4,2)
and
(6,3,2)
.
Observe that the quiver
Q_{\mathbf{w}}
is extended Dynkin, and let
0
be an extending vertex.
With the
w_{i}
non-increasing, we have
w=w_{1}
and can take
0=[1,w_{1}-1]
.
Let
\delta
be the minimal positive imaginary root for
Q_{\mathbf{w}}
. Thus the coefficient of
\alpha_{*}
in
\delta
is
w
.
The representation theory of tubular weighted projective lines
has been worked out by Geigle and Lenzing
[
8
]
and Lenzing and Meltzer
[
15
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

(i) For a parabolic sheaf
\mathcal{E}
we have

\deg_{\mathbb{X}}\mathcal{E}=\langle\delta-w\partial,[\mathcal{E}]\rangle_{\mathbb{X}}=w\deg_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\mathcal{E}+w\rank\mathcal{E}+\langle\delta,\dimv\mathcal{E}\rangle_{Q_{\mathbf{w}}}.

In particular, if
\dimv\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{Z}\delta
, then
\deg_{\mathbb{X}}\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{Z}w
.

(ii) For parabolic bundles
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
of ranks
r
and
r^{\prime}
we have

w^{2}\langle[\mathcal{E}],[\mathcal{E}^{\prime}]\rangle_{\mathbb{X}}=wrr^{\prime}(\mu(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})-\mu(\mathcal{E}))+\langle w\dimv\mathcal{E}-r\delta,w\dimv\mathcal{E}^{\prime}-r^{\prime}\delta\rangle_{Q_{\mathbf{w}}}.

Proof.

Straightforward.
∎

The indecomposables of a given slope
q\in\mathbb{Q}
are semistable, and form a uniserial abelian category
whose Auslander-Reiten components are tubes
[
8
, Theorem 5.6]
.
It follows that
\Hom(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})=0
if
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
are indecomposable with
\mu(\mathcal{E})>\mu(\mathcal{F})
.
The simple objects in this category are the stable parabolic sheaves of slope
q
.
Moreover this category is equivalent to the category of torsion parabolic sheaves
[
15
, Theorem 4.4]
,
so the ranks of the inhomogeneous tubes are equal to the components of
\mathbf{w}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose
T
is an inhomogeneous tube of slope
q\in\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathcal{E}
is the direct sum of one copy of each stable parabolic bundle in
T
.

(i)
\dimv\mathcal{E}=k\delta
, where
k
is a positive integer.

(ii) The dimension vectors of the stable parabolic bundles in
T
are linearly independent.

(iii) If
q\in w\mathbb{Z}
then
k=1
.

(iv) Any indecomposable parabolic sheaf of slope
<q
has a non-zero map to
\mathcal{E}

and any indecomposable parabolic sheaf of slope
>q
has a non-zero map from
\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

(i) We have
\mathcal{E}\cong\mathcal{E}(\vec{\omega})
. Then

\langle[\mathcal{F}],[\mathcal{E}]\rangle_{\mathbb{X}}=\langle[\mathcal{F}],[\mathcal{E}(\vec{\omega})]\rangle_{\mathbb{X}}=-\langle[\mathcal{E}],[\mathcal{F}]\rangle_{\mathbb{X}}.

Thus
([\mathcal{F}],[\mathcal{E}])=0
for all
\mathcal{F}
. If
[\mathcal{E}]=\alpha+s\partial
and
[\mathcal{F}]=\alpha^{\prime}+s^{\prime}\partial
this says that
(\alpha^{\prime},\alpha)=0

for all
\alpha^{\prime}
. Thus
\alpha=k\delta
, some
k
.
Of course
k
is a positive integer since
k\delta_{*}=\alpha_{*}=\rank\mathcal{E}>0
.

(ii) Suppose some linear combination is zero, say
\sum_{i}m_{i}\dimv\mathcal{S}_{i}=0
, with
i
running through
\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z}
,
where
r
is the rank of the tube, and
\mathcal{S}_{i+1}=\mathcal{S}_{i}(\vec{\omega})
.
Since the rank of a parabolic sheaf is the
*
-component of its dimension vector, we have

\sum_{i}m_{i}\left(w\dimv\mathcal{S}_{i}-(\rank\mathcal{S}_{i})\delta\right)=0.

Thus by the lemma, we have
\sum_{i}m_{i}\langle[\mathcal{S}_{i}],[\mathcal{S}_{j}]\rangle_{\mathbb{X}}=0
for all
j
. Now

\langle[\mathcal{S}_{i}],[\mathcal{S}_{j}]\rangle_{\mathbb{X}}=\begin{cases}1&(i=j)\\
-1&(i=j+1)\\
0&(\text{otherwise}).\end{cases}

so
m_{j}-m_{j+1}=0
, so the
m_{i}
are all equal. But now the
m_{i}
are zero by (i).

(iii) Let
\mathcal{F}
be a stable parabolic bundle in
T
and let
\alpha=\dimv\mathcal{F}
. Then
\langle[\mathcal{F}],[\mathcal{F}]\rangle_{\mathbb{X}}=1
so
\alpha
is a positive real root in
\Gamma
.
We have
q\rank\mathcal{F}=\langle\delta,\alpha\rangle_{Q_{\mathbf{W}}}+w\rank\mathcal{F}+w\deg_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\mathcal{F}
.

Now
\alpha-\delta
is not zero, since
\alpha
is a real root, so it a root, hence positive or negative. If it is positive,
then by
[
15
, Theorem 4.6]
or
[
4
]
there is an indecomposable parabolic sheaf
\mathcal{G}
with
[\mathcal{G}]=\alpha-\delta+s\partial

where
s=\deg\mathcal{F}+w-q
.
Then
\mathcal{G}
has slope

\mu(\mathcal{G})=(\langle\delta,\alpha-\delta\rangle_{\mathbb{X}}+w(\rank\mathcal{F}-w)+ws)/(\rank\mathcal{F}-w)=q.

Moreover
\langle\mathcal{G},\mathcal{F}\rangle_{\mathbb{X}}=\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle_{\mathbb{X}}=1
, so there is a non-zero map
\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{F}
.
This is impossible.

Thus
\alpha-\delta
is a negative root. Thus (as above) there is an indecomposable parabolic sheaf
\mathcal{G}
with
[\mathcal{G}]=\delta-\alpha+t\partial

where
t=\deg\mathcal{F}+q-w
.
This
\mathcal{G}
has slope

\mu(\mathcal{G})=(\langle\delta,\delta-\alpha\rangle_{\mathbb{X}}+w(w-\rank\mathcal{F})+wt)/(w-\rank\mathcal{F})=q.

Moreover
\langle[\mathcal{G}],[\mathcal{F}]\rangle_{\mathbb{X}}=-1=\langle[\mathcal{F}],[\mathcal{G}]\rangle_{\mathbb{X}}
.
Thus there are non-split extensions of
\mathcal{F}
on top of the top of
\mathcal{G}
, and of the socle of
\mathcal{G}
on top of
\mathcal{F}
. It follows from the uniserial structure of the parabolic sheaves of slope
q
that
\mathcal{F}\oplus\mathcal{G}
must involve all of the stable parabolic sheaves in the tube
T
.
By construction
\dimv\mathcal{F}\oplus\mathcal{G}=\delta
. Thus the dimension vector of
\mathcal{E}
is at most
\delta
, and hence equal to
\delta
.

(iv) We use that if
\dimv\mathcal{E}=k\delta
and
\mu(\mathcal{E})=q
then

\langle[\mathcal{E}],[\mathcal{F}]\rangle_{\mathbb{X}}=\langle k\delta+(\deg_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\mathcal{E})\partial,\dimv\mathcal{F}+(\deg_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\mathcal{F})\partial\rangle_{\mathbb{X}}

=k(\mu(\mathcal{F})-q)\rank\mathcal{F}

and similarly for
\langle[\mathcal{F}],[\mathcal{E}]\rangle_{\mathbb{X}}
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

If
\mathcal{F}
is an exceptional parabolic bundle on the mouth of a tube in the Auslander-Reiten quiver of
\Coh\mathbb{X}
,
then
\mathcal{F}^{\perp}
is equivalent to the category of finite dimensional representations of an extended Dynkin quiver.

Proof.

By Hübner and Lenzing
[
11
]
,
[
5
, Theorem 4.2]
, the perpendicular category is equivalent to
the category of finite dimensional
A
-modules for finite dimensional hereditary algebra
A
;
we just need to see that
A
is tame hereditary.
Let
\mathcal{F}
have slope
q
.
The coherent sheaves of slope
q
form a tubular family, the tubes not containing
\mathcal{F}

all belong to
\mathcal{F}^{\perp}
, and the intersection of the tube
T
containing
\mathcal{F}
with
\mathcal{F}^{\perp}

is a tube of rank 1 less than the rank of
T
.
Thus the Auslander-Reiten quiver of
A
contains tubes, so
A
must have a tame hereditary factor.
Now since any indecomposable
A
-module is either in a tube, or has a non-zero map to or from a fixed tube,
it follows that
A
is connected.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)\in\BunConn_{\zeta}\mathbb{X}

and
\dimv\mathcal{E}=hm\delta

with
m\geq 1
and
h\geq 2
.
Suppose that
s=\zeta*[m\delta]\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
is not irreducible.

Proof.

First suppose that

\mathcal{E}
has indecomposable summands of different slopes, or, all indecomposable summands of
\mathcal{E}
have the same slope,
but they belong to at least two different tubes.
In this case there is a non-trivial decomposition
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}^{1}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{2}
with
\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{E}^{2},\mathcal{E}^{1})=0
, so
Lemma
3.6
applies.

Thus we may suppose that all indecomposable summands of
\mathcal{E}
have the same slope
q

and belong the same tube
T
. Choose a different inhomogeneous tube of slope
q
, and an exceptional
parabolic bundle
\mathcal{F}
on the mouth of the tube.
Then
\mathcal{F}^{\perp}
is equivalent to the category of representations of an extended Dynkin quiver
Q^{\prime}
.
By
[
5
]
, the category of objects
(\mathcal{G},s)\in\CohConn_{\zeta}\mathbb{X}
with
\mathcal{G}\in\mathcal{F}^{\perp}
is equivalent to
the category of representations of the deformed preprojective algebra
\Pi^{\lambda}(Q^{\prime})
for some
\lambda
.
In particular
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
corresponds to such a representation, call it
M
.

Consider an indecomposable uniserial object
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
in
T
having as composition factors one copy of each of the simples
on the mouth of
T
. We must have
\dimv\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=a\delta
for some
a
.
Since it has slope
q
, it has degree
a(q-w)
, so
aq\in\mathbb{Z}
.

By Lemma
3.3
, we have
\deg_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\mathcal{E}=-hs\in h\mathbb{Z}
.
Thus there is a class
\phi=m\delta-s\partial\in K_{0}(\Coh\mathbb{X})
with
h\phi=[\mathcal{E}]
.
By
[
15
, Theorem 4.6(iv)]
there is an indecomposable object
\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}
in
T
with
[\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}]=\phi
.
Now clearly we must have
m\delta
a multiple of
a\delta
. Thus
a
divides
m
.

Since the dimension vector of
\mathcal{E}
is a multiple of
\delta
, it must involve each simple in
T
with the same multiplicity,
so
\dimv\mathcal{E}=b\dimv\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=ba\delta
for some
b\geq 1
. Thus
ba=hm
. It follows that
b\geq 2
.

Now
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
corresponds to a representation
M^{\prime}
of
Q^{\prime}
of dimension vector
\delta^{\prime}
,
the minimal imaginary root
\delta^{\prime}
for
Q^{\prime}
. Then
M
corresponds to a representation of dimension vector
b\delta^{\prime}
.
Since
M
exists as a
\Pi^{\lambda}(Q)
-module, we must have
\lambda\cdot b\delta^{\prime}=0
.
Thus also
\lambda\cdot\delta^{\prime}=0
.

Then by
[
1
, Theorem 1.2]
there can be no simple module
M
.
(All that is needed is the argument of Case (I) in
[
1
, §10]
.
Alternatively one could use Crawley-Boevey and Hubery
[
6
]
.)
∎

For later use we need the following.

Lemma 4.5
.

If
\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
has dimension vector a multiple of
\delta
, where
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
are direct sums of indecomposables
in disjoint sets of tubes of the same slope, then the dimension vectors of
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
are also multiples of
\delta
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.1
we have
\langle[\mathcal{E}],[\mathcal{E}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\prime}]\rangle_{\mathbb{X}}=0
.
Since
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
are in different tubes,
\langle[\mathcal{E}],[\mathcal{E}^{\prime}]\rangle_{\mathbb{X}}=0
. Thus
\langle[\mathcal{E}],[\mathcal{E}]\rangle_{\mathbb{X}}=0
,
so
w\dimv\mathcal{E}-(\rank\mathcal{E})\delta
is a multiple of
\delta
, hence so is
\dimv\mathcal{E}
.
∎

5.
The extended tubular case

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a weighted projective line of weight type
\mathbf{w}=(w_{1},\dots,w_{k})
.
In this section we assume that
\mathbb{X}
is of
extended tubular
type,
meaning that
\mathbf{w}
is one of
(3,2,2,2)
,
(4,3,3)
,
(5,4,2)
and
(7,3,2)
.
Thus
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}=(w_{1}-1,w_{2},\dots,w_{k})
is of tubular type.
Let
\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
be the weighted projective line of weight type
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}

with the same marked points as
\mathbb{X}
.
We denote by
\delta
the minimal positive imaginary root for
Q_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}
, considered as
a dimension vector for
Q_{\mathbf{w}}
.
We write
w^{\prime}
for the least common multiple of the components of
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}
.
We write
\mu
for the slope function for
\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
.

To shorten notation, we write
\infty
and
0
for the vertices
[1,w_{1}-1]

and
[1,w_{1}-2]
of
Q_{\mathbf{w}}
. Note that
0
is an extending vertex for the
extended Dynkin quiver
Q_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}
.
Thus we write
S_{\infty}
and
S_{0}
for the simple torsion parabolic sheaves
S_{1,w_{1}-1}
and
S_{1,w_{1}-2}
in
\Coh\mathbb{X}
.
Thus by
[
8
, (2.5.4)]
we have
S_{\infty}(\vec{\omega})\cong S_{0}
and
S_{1,0}(\vec{\omega})\cong S_{\infty}
.

There is a notion of
reduction of weight
considered by Geigle and Lenzing
[
9
, §9]
.
It gives the following.

Lemma 5.1
.

There are functors

\Coh\mathbb{X}\rightoverleft^{f}_{i}\Coh\mathbb{X}^{\prime}

satisfying

(i)
i
is left adjoint to
f
and
fi\cong 1
and
f
is exact.

(ii)
i
is fully faithful and exact and gives an equivalence between
\Coh\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
and
C=S_{1,0}^{\perp}={}^{\perp}S_{\infty}
.

(iii)
S_{0}\in C
so it is isomorphic to
i(S_{0}^{\prime})
for some simple torsion parabolic sheaf
S_{0}^{\prime}
fo
\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
.

(iv)
i
preserves rank, sends torsion parabolic sheaves to torsion parabolic sheaves and parabolic bundles to parabolic bundles.

(v) Considering parabolic bundles as vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
equipped with flags of subspaces of the fibres,

i
extends the parabolic structure by zero at the vector space
E_{\infty}
and
f
forgets the vector space
E_{\infty}
.

(vi) If
\mathcal{E}
is a parabolic sheaf on
\mathbb{X}
, then
f(\mathcal{E})=0
if and only if
\mathcal{E}
is isomorphic to a direct sum of copies of
S_{\infty}
.
Thus, for any parabolic sheaf
\mathcal{E}
on
\mathbb{X}
, the cokernel of the natural map
if(\mathcal{E})\to\mathcal{E}
is isomorphic to a direct sum of copies of
S_{\infty}
.

Proof.

The functor
f
is
\varphi_{*}
of
[
9
, Theorem 9.5]
. As remarked there, it has a left adjoint, which we denote by
i
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

We have
\Ext^{1}(i(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}),\mathcal{E})\cong\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime},f(\mathcal{E}))
for
\mathcal{E}\in\Coh\mathbb{X}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\in\Coh\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
.
In particular,
i
induces an isomorphism on
\Ext^{1}
.

Proof.

Given an exact sequence
0\to\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{G}\to i(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})\to 0
, applying
f
gives an exact sequence
0\to f(\mathcal{E})\to f(\mathcal{G})\to\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\to 0
. This gives a map
\Ext^{1}(i(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}),\mathcal{E})\to\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime},f(\mathcal{E}))
. Conversely, given an exact sequence
0\to f(\mathcal{E})\to\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\to 0
, applying
i
and taking the pushout along the morphism
if(\mathcal{E})\to\mathcal{E}
gives an exact sequence
0\to\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{G}\to i(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})\to 0
. This gives a map
\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime},f(\mathcal{E}))\to\Ext^{1}(i(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}),\mathcal{E})
.
It is easy to see that these are inverses.

The last part is actually obvious, since the functor
i
identifies
\Coh\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
with a full extension-closed subcategory of
\Coh\mathbb{X}
, so
i
induces an isomorphism on
\Ext^{1}
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Say
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)\in\BunConn_{\zeta}\mathbb{X}
and
\dimv\mathcal{E}=\alpha_{\infty}+h\delta
with
h\geq 2
.
We suppose that
s=\zeta*[\delta]\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\zeta*[\alpha_{\infty}]=0
.
Then
q=\mu(f(\mathcal{E}))
is in
\mathbb{Z}
.
We decompose

f(\mathcal{E})=\mathcal{E}^{<q}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{=q}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{>q}

where each term is a direct sum of
indecomposables with slopes in the indicated ranges.
If

0\to\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{E}^{>q}\to S_{0}^{\prime}\to 0

is an exact sequence in
\Coh\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
,
then
\mu(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})\geq q
for any non-zero direct summand
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

Because of the existence of
\nabla
, we have

\deg_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\mathcal{E}=-\zeta*[\dimv\mathcal{E}]=-hs.

Now this is just the usual degree of the vector bundle
E
. so it is also
\deg_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
.
Thus by Lemma
4.1
we have

q=\mu(f(\mathcal{E}))=[w^{\prime}(\deg_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\mathcal{E})+w^{\prime}\rank\mathcal{E}]/(\rank\mathcal{E})=w^{\prime}-s.

Thus
q\in\mathbb{Z}
. Suppose
\mu(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})<q
.
Take the pushout of the exact sequence defining
\mathcal{F}
along the projection
\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
to get

0\to\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{G}\to S_{0}^{\prime}\to 0.

There is an epimorphism from
\mathcal{E}^{>q}\to\mathcal{G}
, so by the Hom properties of slopes in the tubular case,
we must have
\mu(\mathcal{G})>q
.
Let
m=\rank\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\rank\mathcal{G}
. Then

\deg_{\mathbb{X}^{\prime}}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}<mq
,
\deg_{\mathbb{X}^{\prime}}\mathcal{G}>mq
and
\deg_{\mathbb{X}^{\prime}}\mathcal{G}-\deg_{\mathbb{X}^{\prime}}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\deg_{\mathbb{X}^{\prime}}S_{0}^{\prime}=1
.
This is impossible since
mq
,
\deg_{\mathbb{X}^{\prime}}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
and
\deg_{\mathbb{X}^{\prime}}\mathcal{G}
are integers.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Suppose that
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)\in\BunConn_{\zeta}\mathbb{X}
and

\dimv\mathcal{E}=\alpha_{\infty}+h\delta
with
h\geq 2
,

\zeta*[\alpha_{\infty}]=0
and
\zeta*[\delta]\in\mathbb{Z}
.
If
f(\mathcal{E})
has indecomposable summands of different slopes, then
(\mathcal{E},\nabla)
is not irreducible.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=f(\mathcal{E})\in\Coh\mathbb{X}^{\prime}
. Let
q=\mu(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
.
This gives
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=\mathcal{E}^{1}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{2}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{3}
where
\mathcal{E}^{1}=(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})^{>q}
,

\mathcal{E}^{2}=(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})^{=q}
and
\mathcal{E}^{3}=(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})^{<q}
.
By assumption
\mathcal{E}^{1}
and
\mathcal{E}^{3}
are non-zero.
This gives a decomposition of the vector bundle
E=E^{1}\oplus E^{2}\oplus E^{3}
.
Let
E^{r}_{ij}=E^{r}_{a_{i}}\cap E_{ij}
.
Then
E_{ij}=E^{1}_{ij}\oplus E^{2}_{ij}\oplus E^{3}_{ij}
except at the
\infty
vertex.

Now
(\Res_{a_{1}}\nabla-\zeta_{\infty})(E_{0})\subseteq E_{\infty}
, so

\phi=(\Res_{a_{1}}\nabla-\zeta_{\infty})|_{E_{0}}
considered as a map
E_{0}\to E_{0}
has rank
\leq 1
.
Using the decomposition of
E_{0}
it becomes a 3 by 3 block matrix.

\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{11}&\phi_{12}&\phi_{13}\\
\phi_{21}&\phi_{22}&\phi_{23}\\
\phi_{31}&\phi_{32}&\phi_{33}\end{pmatrix}.

Thus
\phi_{31}
has rank
\leq 1
.
Let
\mathcal{F}
be the parabolic bundle obtained from
\mathcal{E}^{1}
by replacing the subspace
E^{1}_{0}
by
\Ker\phi_{31}
.
By Lemma
5.3
, for any summand
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{F}
we have
\mu(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})\geq q
.
Thus
\Hom(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{E}^{3}(\vec{\omega}))=0
.

But now
\nabla_{31}
defines a map of parabolic bundles from
\mathcal{F}
to
\mathcal{E}^{3}(\vec{\omega})
, so

\nabla_{31}=0
.
In particular

are equal, except that the parabolic structures

morphism
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{E}
which is the identity map on the underlying vector bundle.
Thus the spaces
E^{\prime}_{ij}
are subspaces of the
E_{ij}
.
But then the fact that
\nabla
is normal for
\mathcal{E}
ensures that

E_{ij}=(\Res_{a_{i}}\nabla-\zeta_{ij}1)\dots(\Res_{a_{i}}\nabla-\zeta_{i1}1)(E_{a_{i}}),

while

E^{\prime}_{ij}\supseteq(\Res_{a_{i}}\nabla-\zeta_{ij}1)\dots(\Res_{a_{i}}\nabla-\zeta_{i1}1)(E_{a_{i}})

and hence
E^{\prime}_{ij}=E_{ij}
for all
i,j
. Thus
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=\mathcal{E}
.

Now in case (a), Lemma
4.4
gives a contradiction. In case (b),
the condition
\xi^{[\alpha_{\infty}]}=1
implies that
\xi_{1,w_{1}}=\xi_{1,w_{1}-1}
, so
\zeta_{1,w_{1}}=\zeta_{1,w_{1}-1}
, and hence
\zeta*[\alpha_{\infty}]=0
.
Then Lemmas
5.4
and
5.5
give a contradiction.
∎

7.
Multiplicative preprojective algebras

Let
Q
be a quiver with vertex set
I
and let
q\in(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{I}
. Let
\Lambda^{q}(Q)
be the corresponding multiplicative preprojective algebra, see
[
7
]
. For
\alpha\in\Gamma_{Q}
, say
\alpha=\sum_{i\in I}n_{i}\alpha_{i}
, we define
q^{\beta}=\prod_{i\in I}q_{i}^{n_{i}}
.

Given conjugacy classes
C_{1},\dots,C_{k}
in
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
as in the introduction, we choose
k,\mathbf{w},\xi
as there, and obtain a quiver
Q_{\mathbf{w}}
, root lattice
\Gamma_{\mathbf{w}}
and an element
\alpha_{C}\in\Gamma_{\mathbf{w}}
. We define
q_{C}\in(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{I_{\mathbf{w}}}
by
q_{*}=1/\prod_{i=1}^{k}\xi_{i1}
and
q_{ij}=\xi_{ij}/\xi_{i,j+1}
, so that
\xi^{[\alpha]}=1/q^{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in\Gamma_{\mathbf{w}}
. By
[
7
, Lemma 8.3]
we have the following.

Lemma 7.1
.

There is an irreducible solution to
A_{1}\dots A_{k}=1
with matrices
A_{i}\in C_{i}
if and only if there is a simple
\Lambda^{q_{C}}(Q_{\mathbf{w}})
-module of dimension vector
\alpha_{C}
.

For compatibility with earlier work, we now change notation: the root lattice is
\mathbb{Z}^{I}
, the simple roots are denoted
\epsilon_{i}
, and
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
are the components of
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{I}
.
By
[
7
]
there are reflections
u_{v}(q)
and reflection functors.
Consider pairs
[q,\alpha]
with
q\in(K^{*})^{I}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{I}
.
The reflection at
i
is
admissible
for the pair
[q,\alpha]
if
q_{i}\neq 1
.
The equivalence relation
\sim

[q,\alpha]\sim[u_{v}(q),s_{v}(\alpha)]
whenever the reflection at the vertex

is admissible for
[q,\alpha]
, where

is the corresponding reflection on
\mathbb{Z}^{I}
.
Let
\bar{R}^{+}_{q}
be the set of positive roots
\alpha
with

.
Let
\mathbb{N}\bar{R}^{+}_{q}
be the set of sums of elements of
\bar{R}^{+}_{q}
.
Let
\bar{\Sigma}_{q}
be the set of elements
\alpha\in\bar{R}^{+}_{q}

such that there is no decomposition
\alpha=\beta+\gamma+\dots
with

\beta,\gamma,\dots\in\bar{R}^{+}_{q}

Theorem 7.2

If
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{I}

\alpha\in\bar{\Sigma}_{q}
if and only if

and
(\beta,\alpha-\beta)\leq-2

are non-zero and in

Proof.

This is analogous to
[

, Theorem 5.6]

[
1

.

Let
\bar{F}_{q}
be the set of
\alpha\in\bar{R}^{+}_{q}
with
(\alpha^{\prime},\epsilon_{i})\leq 0
for any
[q^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime}]\sim[q,\alpha]
and any vertex

.

Lemma 7.3
.

If there is a simple
\Lambda^{q}
-module of dimension

is equivalent to a pair
[q^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime}]
with

Theorem 7.4
.

If
[q,\alpha]
is a pair with
\alpha\in\bar{F}_{q}\setminus\bar{\Sigma}_{q}
, then after first passing to an
equivalent pair, and then passing to the support quiver of
\alpha
and the corresponding restrictions of
q
and
\alpha
,
one of the following cases holds:

(I)
Q
is extended Dynkin with minimal positive imaginary root
\delta
,
the dimension vector is
\alpha=hm\delta
with
m\geq 1
and
h\geq 2

and
q^{\delta}
a primitive
m
th root of unity.

(II).
Q
is a disjoint union of two parts connected by one arrow between vertices
i
,
j
, with
\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{j}=1
,
and if
\alpha=\beta+\gamma
where
\beta
and
\gamma
are the restriction of
\alpha
to each
side of the edge joining
i
and
j
, then
q^{\beta}=q^{\gamma}=1
.

(III)
Q
is a disjoint union of two parts connected by one arrow between vertices
j
,
k
with
\alpha_{j}=1
,
the part containing
k
is extended Dynkin with
k
as an extending vertex and minimal imaginary root
\delta
,
the restriction of
\alpha
to this part is
h\delta
with
h\geq 2
and
q^{\delta}=1
.

Proof.

This is an analogue of
[
1
, Theorem 8.1]
, and is obtained by modifying the proof of that result, including straightforward analogues of
[
1
, Lemmas 8.2-8.15]
(8.8 and 8.13 need no change).

For
\alpha\in\bar{F}_{q}
, we say that
\beta
is a
(-1)
-vector
for the pair
[q,\alpha]
if
\beta,\alpha-\beta\in\mathbb{N}\bar{R}^{+}_{q}
and
(\beta,\alpha-\beta)=-1
.
We say that
\beta
is a
divisor
for
[q,\alpha]
if it is a
(-1)
-vector,
(\beta,\epsilon_{i})\leq 0
for all
i
, and
(\alpha-\beta,\epsilon_{i})\leq 0

whenever
(\beta,\epsilon_{i})=0
. If so, then

-1=(\beta,\alpha-\beta)=\sum_{i}(\alpha-\beta)_{i}(\beta,\epsilon_{i}),

and the terms in the sum are
\leq 0
, so there is a unique vertex
j
, called the
critical vertex
for
\beta
, with
(\beta,\epsilon_{j})=-1
and
(\alpha-\beta)_{j}=1
, and for every other vertex
i
one has
(\beta,\epsilon_{i})=0
or
(\alpha-\beta)_{i}=0
.

Suppose that
\alpha\in\bar{F}_{q}\setminus\bar{\Sigma}_{q}
. Since
\alpha
is an imaginary root,
q(\alpha)\leq 0
.

Suppose first that
q(\alpha)=0
. By passing to an equivalent pair we may assume by the analogue of
[
1
, Lemma 7.1]
, that
\alpha
is in the fundamental region. Since
q(\alpha)=0
this implies that the support of
\alpha
is extended Dynkin and
\alpha
is a multiple of the minimal imaginary root
\delta
.
Let
m
be the smallest positive integer with
(q^{\delta})^{m}=1
. Since
q^{\alpha}=1
, it follows that
\alpha=hm\delta
for some
h\geq 1
.
If
h=1
then in any proper decomposition of
\alpha
as a sum of roots
\alpha=\beta+\gamma+\dots
in
\bar{R}^{+}_{q}
,
the terms must be real roots, so
p(\alpha)>p(\beta)+p(\gamma)+\dots
. Thus
\alpha\in\bar{\Sigma}_{q}
, a contradiction. Thus

, so we are in case (I).

Thus we may suppose that
q(\alpha)<0
. We replace
[q,\alpha]
by an equivalent pair to ensure that
\alpha
has support as small as possible. Then we pass to the support quiver
Q^{\prime}
of
\alpha
and the restrictions
[q^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime}]
of
q
and
\alpha
.
Clearly
\alpha^{\prime}\in\bar{F}_{q^{\prime}}\setminus\bar{\Sigma}_{q^{\prime}}
. Observe that by minimality, if
[q^{\prime\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime}]
is a pair equivalent to
[q^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime}]
,
then
\alpha^{\prime\prime}
has support
Q^{\prime}
, and the pair
[q^{\prime\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime}]
could be obtained from
[q,\alpha]
by first applying admissible reflections, and then passing to the support quiver of
\alpha^{\prime\prime}
.

By the analogue of
[
1
, Lemma 8.3]
there is a
(-1)
-vector
\beta
for
[q^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime}]
, and hence a divisor

for some equivalent pair

[

. Now
\beta^{\prime}
and

cannot both be sincere by the analogue of
[
1

. Thus either
\beta^{\prime}
is a non-sincere divisor, or we obtain a non-sincere divisor for some pair equivalent to
[q^{\prime\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime}]

to the
(-1)
-vector

Theorem 7.5
.

If there is a simple
\Lambda^{q}(Q)
-module of dimension
\alpha
, then
\alpha\in\bar{\Sigma}_{q}
.

Proof.

If there is a simple module, then by the reflection functors and Lemma
7.3
, we may assume that
\alpha
is the coordinate vector of a loopfree vertex or
\alpha\in\bar{F}_{q}
. In the first case we have
\alpha\in\bar{\Sigma}_{q}
, so we may assume that
\alpha\in\bar{F}_{q}
. Again, because of the reflection functors of
[
7
]
, it suffices to show that there is no simple module in the three cases (I), (II), (III) of Theorem
7.4
.

In case (I) there is no simple by Theorem
6.2
(a).

In case (II), the conditions on
q
ensure that the arrows from
i
to
j
and from
j
to
i

are represented in the simple module by
1\times 1
matrices with product zero. Thus one of the matrices is zero, and hence the restriction of the representation to one part or other of the quiver will be a non-trivial subrepresentation, contradicting simplicity.

For case (III), first suppose that the extended Dynkin quiver is star-shaped. Then the conditions ensure that by restriction one obtains a representation of a multiplicative preprojective algebra for the full subquiver of
Q
given by the extended Dynkin subquiver together with vertex
j
. This representation must also be simple, but this is impossible by Theorem
6.2
(b).

On the other hand, suppose the extended Dynkin quiver is of type
\tilde{A}_{n}
. We may suppose that the arrow connecting
j
and
k
is
a:j\to k
and the extended Dynkin quiver has vertices and arrows

k\xleftarrow{b_{0}}1\xleftarrow{b_{1}}2\leftarrow\dots\leftarrow r\xleftarrow{b_{r}}k

with
r\geq 0
. The relations for the multiplicative preprojective algebra can be written as

(1+aa^{*})(1+b_{0}b_{0}^{*})=q_{k}(1+b_{r}^{*}b_{r}),\quad\text{and}

(1+b_{i}b_{i}^{*})=q_{i}(1+b_{i-1}^{*}b_{i-1})\quad(i=1,\dots,r).

In the representation, the arrows
b_{i}
and
b_{i}^{*}
are all represented by
h\times h
matrices, while
a
is an
h\times 1
matrix and
a^{*}
is a
1\times h
matrix. Now the condition
q^{\delta}=1
gives
q_{k}q_{1}\dots q_{r}=1
.

Consider the full subquiver
Q^{\prime\prime}
of
Q
given by the extended Dynkin quiver together with vertex
j
, and the (additive) deformed preprojective algebra
\Pi^{\lambda}(Q^{\prime\prime})
, where
\lambda
is given by

\lambda_{j}=0,\quad\lambda_{k}=q_{k}-1,\quad\lambda_{i}=\frac{q_{i}-1}{q_{1}\dots q_{i}}\quad(i=1,\dots,r).

Clearly
\lambda\cdot\delta=0
. Let

a^{\circ}=a^{*}(1+b_{0}b_{0}^{*}),\quad b_{i}^{\circ}=b_{0}^{*},\quad b_{i}^{\circ}=\frac{1}{q_{1}q_{2}\dots q_{i}}b_{i}^{*}\quad(i=1,\dots,r).

We obtain

b_{i}b_{i}^{\circ}-b_{i}^{\circ}b_{i}=\lambda_{i}1\quad(i=1,\dots,r)

and

b_{0}b_{0}^{\circ}-b_{r}^{\circ}b_{r}+aa^{\circ}=\lambda_{k}1.

Summing the traces of all these, and using that
\lambda\cdot\delta=0
, we see that
aa^{\circ}
has trace zero. Thus
a^{\circ}a
has trace zero, and since it is a
1\times 1
matrix, it is zero. Thus the matrices
a,b_{1},\dots,b_{r}
, together with
a^{\circ},b_{1}^{\circ},\dots,b_{r}^{\circ}
give a representation of
\Pi^{\lambda}(Q^{\prime\prime})
. Moreover it is clearly simple, since any subrepresentation, by the reverse process, gives rise to a submodule of the
\Lambda^{q}(Q)
-module. But this is impossible by
[
1
, Theorem 9.1]
or
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
.
∎

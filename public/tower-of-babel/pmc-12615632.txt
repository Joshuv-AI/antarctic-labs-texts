Edge-condensation to access a crystalline planar icosagen cation with σ-aromaticity
Authors: Zhongtao Feng, Rei Kinjo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractParallel to the hexagonal C6 motif in carbon materials (such as graphene and carbon nanotubes) and polyaromatic hydrocarbons, three-membered boron and aluminum rings are the smallest key units seen in their clusters and materials (such as borophene). Herein, we report an edge-condensation protocol of a B3 ring to render a cationic icosagen species. A single-crystal X-ray diffraction analysis and computational studies reveal that the planar B3Al2 core consists of three consecutive three-center two-electron bonds. Remarkably, the icosagen ring exhibits global σ-aromaticity. The reactions with a cationic gallium and a copper complex afford a dicationic B3Al2 species assimilating a Ga atom by coordination with two borafluorene ligands and a dinuclear copper complex bearing a η2-dianionic diborane ligand, respectively, demonstrating the π-donor property as well as the capability of transferring the dianionic diborane fragment.Subject terms: Chemical bonding, Electronic properties and materials, Synthetic chemistry methodology

Introduction

Hexagonal carbon motif C6 is a key unit ubiquitously seen in carbon allotropes such as graphene, carbon nanotubes, and fullerene, as well as polyaromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs). One of the representative processes to expand the system to be larger is the sequential addition of the C6 unit to the edge of existing rings—edge-condensation (Fig. 1a), which is not only computationally verified in the growth process of carbon materials but indeed employed in the organic synthesis of PAHs1–6.

The three-membered boron ring B3 represents the smallest cyclic basic unit of boron allotropes and clusters7,8. Parallel to the carbon system of C6, it is reasonable that B3 undergoes a formal edge-condensation via planar rhomboid tetraborane rB4 and distorted pentagonal pentaborane pB5 to enlarge the scaffold (Fig. 1a). This transformation establishes their analogy in terms of geometric structure, albeit not in electronic configuration. However, such clusters are only detected by photoelectron spectroscopy and studied mainly by computational means9–14. As isolable molecules, B3 ring-containing compounds of the electron-deficient15–19, electron-precise20 and 2π aromatic systems21 have been reported. Compounds based on the rhombic B4 skeleton were first isolated by Siebert et al. in 200222, and later, the groups of Berndt and Himmel independently developed a series of derivatives in different oxidation states23–30. Contrarily, no literature reports isolable species with a flat pentaborane core, while only the relevant B3C2 anion was structurally characterized in 199731.

Three-membered ring systems consisting of aluminum, the heavier congener of boron, are significantly limited32. Reflecting the intrinsic electron-deficient nature, icosagen such as boron and aluminum tend to form polyhedral structures by sharing skeletal electrons three-dimensionally33,34. Accordingly, the construction of planar multinuclear icosagen cores is synthetically highly challenging35. To the best of our knowledge, chemical synthesis to extend the flat icosagen core by the well-defined edge-condensation process (such as B3 → B4 → B5 ···) remains elusive.

Recently, we reported the synthesis of a trianionic triangular triboron species [(BR2)3]3− 1 through the reductive dealumination from a Cp*Al(BR2)3 anion (BR2 = 9-borafluorenyl. Cp* = 1,2,3,4,5-pentamethylcyclopentadienyl)20. This molecule not only undergoes electron transfer but also acts as a surrogate for the elusive [R2B]− and [R2BBR2]2− species, proving both reducing ability and nucleophilicity. Given the demonstrated peculiar reactivity, we pondered whether 1 could be used for the edge-condensation reaction. Our initial attempts, however, to enlarge the planar boron core by the reactions of 1 with various boron substrates failed, presumably due to the steric congestion along with the small atomic radius (0.90 Å) of boron. We reasoned that the employment of aluminum with a greater atomic radius (1.43 Å) may circumvent this problem, allowing for icosagen expansion via the edge-condensation reaction. Herein, we report the synthesis, single-crystal X-ray diffraction, and computational studies of the flat icosagen species — B/Al-mixed analog of pB5 (Fig. 1b). The aromatic property as well as reactivity, was further assessed.

Results and discussion

Synthesis and characterization of 3

In the presence of an excess amount of [Cp*Al]4, reaction of 1 with one equivalent of Cp*AlCl2 in tetrahydrofuran (THF) at ambient temperature resulted in the complete consumption of 1 and the appearance of a set of resonances consistent with the formation of 2 in the resultant 1H nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectrum (Fig. S1). Treatment of 2 with two equivalents of Ph3C[B(C6F5)4] in fluorobenzene at room temperature led to a yellow solution. After workup, yellow single crystals of product 3 were gained in 29% isolated yield (Fig. 2a). The single crystal X-ray diffraction (SC-XRD) study revealed that 3 is an ionic compound with [B(C6F5)4]− as the counterion, and three boron atoms and two aluminum atoms in the cationic fragment are coplanar, forming a B3Al2 distorted pentagon skeleton (Fig. 2b). The B1 atom is hexacoordinate with the Al1-B1-Al2 bond angle of 170.6(2)° while B2 and B3 atoms are pentacoordinate. The B2-B1-B3, B1-B2-B3 and B1-B3-B2 bond angles are 57.9(3)°, 62.6(3)° and 59.5(3)°, respectively. The Al1-B2-B3 (139.1(3)°) and Al2-B3-B2 (131.3(3)°) bond angles are wide whereas the B1-Al1-B2 (47.69(19)°) and B1-Al2-B3 (50.02(18)°) bond angels are acute. Three borafluorene planes are nearly perpendicular to the Al2B3 plane with the dihedral angles of 87.91° (B1), 88.32° (B2) and 89.06° (B3). The B1-B2, B1-B3, and B2-B3 bonds are 1.830(7) Å, 1.886(7) Å and 1.799(7) Å, respectively, which are comparable to those edge B–B bonds (1.786(3) Å and 1.779(3) Å) and significantly shorter than the wingtip B–B bond (1.906(3) Å) in 220. The Al1-B2, Al2-B3 bonds (2.041(5) Å and 2.090(6) Å) are shorter than those in 2 (2.131(2) Å and 2.178(2) Å) and the reported anionic AlB2 ring (2.139(5) Å and 2.169(5) Å)36, whereas the Al1-B1 (2.409(5) Å) and Al2-B1 (2.338(5) Å) bonds are significantly elongated. In line with the solid-state structure, the 1H NMR spectrum of 3 in C6D5Cl shows one singlet at 0.68 ppm for 1,2,3,4,5-pentamethylcyclopentadienyl groups (Cp*) and two sets of signals for borafluorene in 1:2 ratio, indicating its symmetric property in the solution in the NMR time scale. In 11B NMR, three peaks are observed at −8.2 ppm for B1, −20.6 ppm for B2/B3 and −14.8 ppm for [B(C6F5)4]−, respectively. UV-vis spectrum of 3 in fluorobenzene (Fig. S18) exhibits three absorption bands at 285 nm, 326 nm, and 388 nm. The peak at 388 nm is assigned to the HOMO−1 → LUMO electron transition based on time-dependent DFT calculation (cal: 383 nm, f = 0.26). It is salient to highlight that apart from three-dimensional boron clusters, molecules involving a hexacoordinate boron center are extremely rare37. Interestingly, according to the polyhedral skeletal electron pair theory (PSEPT), also known as Wade–Mingos’ rules, the favored three-dimensional structure of a B3Al2R8+ cluster is predicted to be closo trigonal bipyramidal. Thus, 3 defies the PSEPT-based structural prediction.

To verify the electronic structure of the B3Al2 pentagon framework of 3, density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed at the B3LYP/6-31 G** level of theory. The bonding molecular orbitals for the σ skeleton of the B3Al2 core are found in the HOMO−1, HOMO−4 and HOMO−18 (Fig. 2c). Similar bonding orbitals are confirmed in the frontier orbitals (HOMO, HOMO−1, and HOMO−4) of the parent compound [B3Al2H8]+ (Fig. S22), that corresponds to the HOMO−18, HOMO−4, and HOMO−1 of 3, respectively. The HOMO of [B3Al2H8]+ fully involves the σ bonding inside the B3Al2 core with an in-plan lobe outside the peripheral B–B moiety. The HOMO−1 indicates two B2Al σ bonding orbitals with a node between them, whereas the HOMO−4 shows σ bonding interaction at the central B3 part. Six σ electrons occupying these MOs follow the [4n + 2] rule for σ aromaticity, implying that 3 exhibits a global σ aromatic feature. The boron and aluminum atoms of the B3Al2 core share only six σ electrons, indicative of the involvement of the delocalized bonding. Indeed, natural localized molecular orbital (NLMO) analysis confirms that the B3Al2 framework consists of three 3-center 2-electron (3c2e) bonds—two for the edge BAl2 rings and one for the central B3 ring (Fig. 2d). This bonding situation is further supported by adaptive natural density partitioning (AdNDP) analysis (Fig. S21). The electron localization function (ELF) plot shows a high electron accumulation inside the Al2B and B3 rings (Fig. 2e). The quantum theory of atoms in molecules (QTAIMs) calculation displays bond paths and bond critical points (BCPs) amongst the central B3 moiety as well as Al1-B2 and B3-Al2 parts (Fig. 2f). The absence of the bond paths and BCPs for the Al1-B1 and B1-Al2 moieties is reminiscent of the observed phenomena for Wagner’s B2C three-member ring system, indicating that the topological instabilities are provoked by the triangular nuclear arrangements38. Consistent with the delocalized multicenter bonding, the Wiberg bond indices (WBIs) indicate values lower than 1—B1-B2/B1-B3 (0.73), B2-B3 (0.54), B1-Al1/B1-Al2 (0.14), B2-Al1/B3-Al2 (0.34). Natural population analysis (NPA) reveals that the Al atoms bear positive charges (+2.08 a.u.) while the B atoms bear negative charges (B1: −0.06 a.u., B2/B3: −0.29 a.u.).

The presence of the delocalized 3c2e bonds within the B3Al2 core of 3 implies the σ-aromatic property. To assess the aromatic nature, the nucleus-independent chemical shift (NICS), gauge-including magnetically induced currents (GIMIC) calculations were performed. Compared with NICS(1) values (−4.77 for AlB2 rings, −4.99 for B3 ring), significantly negative NICS(0) values (−24.49 for AlB2 rings, −31.83 for B3 ring) are gained, indicating that those negative NICS values arise from the delocalization of σ-electrons. The induced currents streamline map (GIMIC) reveals the induced ring current flows around the Al2B3 core, further supporting the σ-aromatic nature (Fig. 2g). Continuous transformation of origin of current density (CTOCD) analysis with a CTOCD-DZ2 method confirms the induced current arises from σ bonding orbital of B3Al2 core (HOMO−18, HOMO−4 and HOMO−1) (Fig. 2h). The delocalization index (DI)39—a robust electronic criterion for aromaticity, further supports the delocalized σ-bonding character of the B₃Al₂ core. The DI value of both Al1-B3 and Al2-B2 is found to be 0.061, comparable to that of para-positioned carbons of benzene (0.093)39. Of note, compound 3 is the first isolated instance of the global aromatic system with three consecutive three-membered rings.

Both compounds 2 and 3 feature the planar and six σ-electrons B₃Aln cores (n = 1, 2). However, it should be noted that the sequential addition of an icosagen atom may not undergo the edge-condensation with expanding the flat core (Fig. 3a). Insertion of a BH unit into [B₃H₆]3− I induces a structural transition to [B₄H₇]3− II with a puckered B₄ framework. Further BH insertion into [B₄H₇]3− II yields [B₅H₈]3− III with a planar bicyclic B₅ framework. Thus, a simple addition of a BH unit neither keeps the planar structure nor increases the B3 triangle unit one by one. Upon removal of two electrons from [B₄H₇]3− II, a planar compound [B₄H₇]− IV of a 6σ-electron system with two bridged B–H–B bonds is formed. From [B₅H₈]3− III, removal of four electrons affords [B₅H₈]⁺ V with a planar B5 core. Accordingly, oxidation is the key to retaining the planar icosagen cores with the expanded triangle motifs. These data are consistent with experimental results, as flat compounds 2—analog of [B₄H₇]− IV and 3—analog of [B₅H₈]⁺ V are monoanionic and monocationic, respectively. Similarly, simple addition of one and two AlH units to I furnishes [B3AlH7]3− VI and [B3Al2H8]3− VII with the distorted structures, whereas removal of two and four electrons from VI and VII produces [B3AlH7]− VIII and [B3Al2H8]+ IX with the planar core, respectively (Fig. 3b).

Reactivity of 3

To disclose the chemical property of 3, we next turned our attention to the stability and reactivity. In the solid state, compound 3 exhibits remarkable thermal robustness under an argon atmosphere for several months, decomposing at T > 164 °C, whereas it appears to be highly sensitive toward moisture and oxygen. While 3 is stable in benzene, fluorobenzene, chlorobenzene, and ethyl ether, it decomposes immediately in THF, CH3CN, CH2Cl2 and CHCl3. No decomposition was observed under photoreactor or heating to 100 °C in fluorobenzene.

Interestingly, chemical reduction of 3 with potassium graphite (KC8), potassium hexamethyldisilazide (KHMDS) or potassium tert-butoxide (KOtBu) reproduces 2, establishing the reversible 2 ↔ 3 formation (Fig. 2a). While this result indicates the oxidizing ability of 3, we postulated that despite the cationic nature, 3 equips the electron donor property because the HOMO is not the electron-deficient B3Al2 core but predominantly the delocalized π orbitals of the borafluorene rings, thus suggesting the π-donor ability (Fig. S20). To bear out the hypothesis, 3 was reacted with two equivalents of Ga(PhF)2[Al(OC(CF3)3)4] at room temperature. Recrystallization from fluorobenzene affords yellow single crystals of 4 in 65% isolated yield (Fig. 4a). The SC-XRD study (Fig. 4b left) reveals the formation of a dicationic species with the B3Al2 core being retained, but with Ga-atom assimilation being coordinated by two borafluorene ligands, confirming the π-donor property of 3. While the atomic connectivity is definitively established, the twinning and serious disorder result in poor X-ray diffraction data despite many attempts, impeding the discussion of the structural parameters in detail. Reacting 2 with IPrCuOtBu (IPr = 1,3-bis(2,6-diisopropylphenyl)imidazol-2-ylidene) in Et2O at room temperature results in the formation of a dinuclear copper complex supported by the dianionic diborane 5 (Fig. 4a, b right). The B–B (1.701(7) Å) and B–Cu (2.106(3) Å − 2.142(3) Å) bond lengths in 5 are comparable to those (B–B: 1.719(4) Å; B–Cu: 2.178(3) Å − 2.189(3) Å) in (LCu)2[(o-tolyl)2B = B(o-toryl)2] (L = 1,3-bis(2,4,6-trimethylphenyl)imidazol-2-ylidene)40. While the reaction mechanism is unclear, the formation of 4 is remarkable as it indicates a formal transfer of the dianionic diborane fragment from 3, albeit its cationic nature.

In conclusion, the icosagen cation 3 was synthesized through sequential edge-condensation of B–B bonds of the triangular B3 trianion. The SC-XRD analysis revealed that this species features a flat B3Al2 core involving hexa- and penta-coordinate boron centers. Theoretical studies indicate the B3Al2 core is constituted of three consecutive 3c2e σ bonds and exhibits a global σ aromaticity. The reactivity studies reveal that the formation of 3 from 2 is chemically reversible, and 3 has the π-donor property as well as the ability to transfer the dianionic diborane fragment despite the electron-deficient monocationic nature. The isolation of this molecule 3 paves the way for the discovery of other expanded icosagen ring systems featuring a unique aromatic nature.

Methods

General considerations

All experiments and manipulations were carried out under an argon atmosphere using standard Schlenk techniques or in an argon-filled glove box. Solvents were dried by the proper drying agents: NaK/benzophenone (tetrahydrofuran, benzene, diethyl ether and n-hexane), CaH2 (fluorobenzene, chlorobenzene), and degassed before use. Unless otherwise stated, all commercially available reagents were used without further purification. The 1H, 13C{1H},11B{1H}, 18F{1H},27Al{1H} NMR spectra were recorded on Bruker 400 MHz BBFO and ECA400 spectrometers at 298 K referenced to residual solvent signals as internal standards (1H NMR: C6D6, 7.16 ppm; C6D5F, 7.09, 6.92, 6.89 ppm; C6D5Cl, 7.09, 6.95, 6.92 ppm, THF-d8, 3.58, 1.72 ppm; 13C{1H} NMR: C6D6, 128.1 ppm; C6D5F, 163.1, 129.5, 123.5, 115.0 ppm; C6D5Cl, 134.2, 129.2, 128.3, 126.0 ppm). Electrospray ionization (ESI) mass spectra were obtained at the Mass Spectrometry Laboratory at the School of Chemistry, Chemical Engineering and Biotechnology (CCEB), Nanyang Technological University (NTU). Melting points were measured with OptiMelt (Stanford Research Systems). Elemental analysis was carried out at the Elemental Analysis Laboratory at CCEB-NTU.

Syntheses of compounds

1,3-bis(2,6-diisopropylphenyl)imidazol-2-ylidene was prepared by the following procedure41: Anhydrous THF (150 mL) was added to the mixture of 1,3-diisopropyl-4,5-dimethyl-1,3-dihydro-2H-imidazole-2-thione (5.0 g, 23.5 mmol) and potassium (2.0 g, 51.3 mmol). The reaction mixture was refluxed for 24 h, and then the insoluble residue was filtered off. The filtrate was removed under vaccum to afford 1,3-bis(2,6-diisopropylphenyl)imidazol-2-ylidene as a pale-yellow solid in 70% yield (3.1 g, 16.5 mmol).

CuOtBu was prepared by the following procedure42: Anhydrous THF (150 mL) was added to the mixture of CuI (3.56 g, 18.7 mmol) and KOtBu (2.00 g, 17.8 mmol) pre-cooled at liquid N2 temperature. The reaction mixture was warmed up to room temperature and stirred overnight. After removal of the solvent in vacuo, the solid was purified by sublimation at 150 °C/0.01 mbar to give CuOtBu in 33% yield (0.8 g, 5.9 mmol).

Ga(PhF)2[Al(OC(CF3)3)4] was prepared by the following procedure43: Ag+[Al(OC(CF3)3)4]− (2.0 g, 1.85 mmol) and gallium (0.5 g, 6.9 mmol) were reacted in C6H5F (10 mL) under ultrasonication at 50 °C for 24 h. The solid residue was removed by filtration, and all volatiles were removed under vaccum to afford Ga(PhF)2[Al(OC(CF3)3)4] as a white solid in 80% yield (1.8 g, 1.5 mmol).

1 was prepared by the following procedure20: THF (5 mL) was added to the mixture of 2 (0.35 g, 0.5 mmol) and KC8 (0.34 g. 2.5 mmol) at room temperature, and the reaction mixture was stirred overnight. The solid part was filtered off, and all volatiles were removed under vacuum. The black powder was washed with benzene and dried under vacuum to afford 1 in 90% yield (0.64 g, 0.44 mmol).

2 was prepared by the following procedure20: Benzene (10 mL) was added to the mixture of [Cp*Al]4 (324 mg, 0.5 mmol) and 9-chloro-9-borafluorene (198 mg, 1 mmol). The reaction mixture was kept at 75 °C with sonication overnight. Then, 9-chloro-9-borafluorene (198 mg, 1 mmol) was added to the mixture, and the solution was stirred for 5 h to form a yellow powder. The yellow powder was washed with benzene and dried under vacuum. THF (10 mL) and KCl (80 mg, 1 mmol) were added to the reaction mixture and kept for 3 h. After filtration, pentane was layered over the THF solution (pentane: THF = 1:1) to afford an orange-red solid, which was collected and dried under vacuum to afford 2 in 30% yield (0.24 g, 0.3 mmol).

Et2O (1 mL) was added to the mixture of 3 (20.0 mg, 0.013 mmol) and KOtBu (3.0 mg, 0.026 mmol)/ KHMDS (5.3 mg, 0.026 mg)/ KC8 (3.6 mg, 0.026 mmol) at room temperature. After the reaction mixture was stirred overnight, the solvent was removed under vacuum. The remaining solid was extracted with THF (1 mL). Red crystals of 2 were grown by laying pentane (1 mL) over the THF solution. The crystals were collected and dried under vacuum to afford 2 in 76% (KOtBu)/ 70% (KHMDS)/ 85% (KC8) yield, respectively. The spectroscopic data are the same as reported in the reference20.

X-ray crystallographic studies

X-ray data collection and structural refinement. Intensity data for compounds 3–5 were collected using a Bruker APEX II diffractometer. The crystals were measured at 100(2) K for 3 and 5, and 170(2) K for 4. The structure was solved by direct phase determination (SHELX-2014) and refined for all data by full-matrix least-squares methods on F244. All non-hydrogen atoms were subjected to anisotropic refinement. The hydrogen atoms were generated geometrically and allowed to ride in their respective parent atoms; they were assigned appropriate isotropic thermal parameters and included in the structure-factor calculations.

*Note: The crystal of 4 is twinned, and the main molecule including the cations and anions, is disordered, resulting in very weak reflections in high angles and leading to high R1 value, wR2 value, poor data / parameter ratio and low bond precision on C–C Bonds. As for “Isolated metal atom found in structure (Unusual)”, the Ga atom coordinates to the π electrons of two phenyl rings and F46.

Computational details

Gaussian 16 was used for all density functional theory (DFT) calculations45. Geometry optimization, frequency calculations were performed at the B3LYP/6-31 G(d,p) level. All local minimum points were confirmed by the frequency calculations to have zero imaginary frequencies. Natural bond orbital (NBO) analysis were performed using the NBO 7.0 program46 with the Gaussian 16 package at the B3LYP/6-31 G(d,p) level of theory. Multiwfn 3.8(dev) program47 was used for adaptive natural density partitioning (AdNDP) and the topological analysis, including the quantum theory of atoms in molecules (QTAIM) and the electron localization function (ELF). The wavefunction files (.wfn) for the topological analysis was obtained from Gaussian 16 at the (U)B3LYP/6-31 G(d,p) level of theory using the optimized geometry. Nucleus independent chemical shifts (NICS) and gauge including magnetically induced currents (GIMIC) analysis48,49 were obtained with Gaussian 16 at the B3LYP/6-31 G(d,p) level of theory using the optimized geometry. Continuous transformation of the origin of the current density (CTOCD) analyses were performed using the SYSMOIC package50 integrated with Gaussian 16 at the B3LYP/6-31 G(d,p) level of theory.

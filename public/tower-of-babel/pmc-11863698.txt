Liquid biopsy for assessment of RFS (recurrence-free survival) in NSCLC (non-small cell lung cancer) patients post-treatment through circulating tumour DNA detection: A meta-analysis
Authors: Urvi Zala, Rushi Patel, Viraj Panchal, Joy Chaudhari, Vedant Shah, Abhi Shah
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOne of the main techniques for diagnosing lung cancer is still tissue biopsy. The more recent liquid biopsy technique includes the assessment of fragments of cfDNA, ctDNA, and CTCs, which are released by tumour cells and act as biomarkers. After compiling information from the existing literature, we conducted a statistical analysis to evaluate the prognosis and recurrence of NSCLC post-treatment using ctDNA levels.MethodBased on predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria, published studies between 2000 and 2022 from electronic databases with statistical data on ctDNA levels for NSCLC were included. Then, using PRISMA guidelines, we carried out a systematic review of those studies.ResultA total of 7 studies were included in the analysis, and we found a correlation of ctDNA levels for determination of prognosis and recurrence after treatment in NSCLC. Meta-analysis was performed using RevMan version 5.4. A P-value of less than 0.05 was considered significant. Studies were divided into three groups: <1 week post-surgery, one week to 3 months post-surgery, and > three months post-surgery. Analysis showed that in <1-week post-surgery group, ctDNA-negative post-treatment detection had an HR of 4.85 (95 % CI: 2.41 to 9.77; p=<0.0001, I2 = 0 %) for recurrence-free survival compared to ctDNA-positive post-treatment detection. Similar findings were obtained for one week to 3 months and > three months group, with HR of 5.27 (95 % CI: 3.62 to 7.67; p=<0.00001, I2 = 11 %) and HR of 6.62 (95 % CI: 2.85 to 15.35; p=<0.0001, I2 = 64 %) respectively.ConclusionAccording to our meta-analysis, patients with NSCLC who were ctDNA negative after surgery had a significantly longer recurrence-free survival than those who were ctDNA positive. As a result, liquid biopsy used to measure ctDNA levels is a useful non-invasive technique to assess the prognosis in such patients. The early detection of recurrence may also allow timely therapeutic intervention, leading to better outcomes.Keywords: ctDNA, Liquid biopsy, Non-small cell lung cancer prognosis after treatment, Minimal residual disease (MRD), Recurrence-free survival in non-small cell lung cancer patients, ctDNA detection

Introduction

As the primary cause of cancer-related deaths, lung cancer is expected to claim 1.8 million lives and diagnose 2.2 million people worldwide in 2020 [1]. Small-cell lung cancer (SCLC), the most aggressive type of lung cancer, and non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC), which advances more slowly, can be distinguished based on tissue morphology [2]. Surgery is still the first line of treatment for people with early-stage NSCLC. While the tissue biopsy technique is indispensable, it is frequently used despite its drawbacks. It requires tissue sampling from a specific location, making it impossible to perform regularly, and it does not capture the full scope of the malignancy. An approach that partially gets around the aforementioned restrictions is liquid biopsy, which is based on examining peripheral blood. Liquid biopsies are becoming increasingly popular because they are less invasive and more cost-effective than traditional solid tumour tissue diagnostics [3,4]. The main benefit of liquid biopsy is that it is an easy-to-obtain, non-invasive method for finding a variety of cancer-associated biomarkers that can reflect the characteristics of the entire tumour malignancy [5].

Liquid biopsy refers to the analysis of bodily fluid for cancer detection [6,7]. The concept was developed for the analysis of circulating tumour cells (CTCs) and quickly expanded to include circulating tumour DNA (ctDNA) and other tumour-derived markers such as circulating cell-free RNA (noncoding and messenger) [[8], [9], [10], [11]]. Cell-free DNA (cfDNA) are fragments of DNA released into the bloodstream that originate mainly from the apoptosis of hematopoietic cells [12,13]. The proportion of ctDNA within the cfDNA varies, ranging from 0.003 to 95 % [14]. According to recent research, cancer patients have higher levels of cfDNA than healthy people. The half-life of cfDNA is 15 min; however, due to constant shedding, overall concentrations may remain stable and even increase with higher tumour grade [15]. Current research suggests that plasma, rather than serum, is a suitable biological sample for analysing cfDNA or ctDNA [16]. ctDNA allows for serial tumour monitoring over time, including identifying nucleotide variants and epigenetic changes and capturing intratumoral heterogeneity [16,17].

Minimal residual disease (MRD) refers to the small number of cancer cells that remain in the body after treatment. MRD detection may indicate the treatment was ineffective or incomplete [18]. We have analysed and discussed the prognostic value of pre-treatment and post-treatment ctDNA levels in non-small cell lung carcinoma (NSCLC) by comparing the recurrence-free survival (RFS) from different studies and mentioning the limitations and potential uses of ctDNA MRD in NSCLC patients.

Methods

Search strategy

We performed a meta-analysis according to the Preferred Reporting for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. A systematic search was carried out in PubMed and Google Scholar for published articles between 2000 and 2022 with the following keywords: ("non-small-cell lung cancer" OR "NSCLC" OR "adenocarcinoma lung" OR "lung squamous carcinoma" OR “LUSC” AND "liquid biopsy" OR "ctDNA" OR "circulating tumour DNA" AND "hazard ratio" OR "recurrence" OR "relapse"). After the removal of duplicated and irrelevant studies, each potential article was examined by two investigators independently to know if the selected studies fit the predetermined inclusion criteria. Any disagreement between the investigators was settled through discussion.

Study Selection

All the published articles were selected, which showed quantitative data on the relationship between detectable ctDNA via liquid biopsy technique and recurrence-free survival (RFS) in NSCLC patients. We further refined the included studies where patients were pathologically diagnosed with resectable NSCLC and were treated with either/combination of the following methods: surgical resection, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and immunotherapy. Any studies where patients did not receive treatment, had a sample size of less than 20, or qualitative data provided regarding graphs or diagrams without proper information, review articles, case reports, and unpublished studies were excluded. Studies that mentioned the use of Circulating tumour cells (CTC) alone or combined with ctDNA to assess prognosis in NSCLC were also disregarded.

The data was collected from the studies regarding the patient's characteristics, the outcome of interest, the reference test used, and the new comparison test. Data on various study characteristics, including study design, publication year, country of conduct, sample size, mean/median age, gender distribution, and diagnostic parameter information were extracted.

Primary endpoint

Correlation to associate the utilisation of ctDNA liquid biopsy aimed for recurrence-free survival in patients treated for NSCLC with the available hazard ratio of ctDNA negative patients compared to ctDNA positive patients.

Quality assessment

To assess the methodological quality of the included studies, the risk of bias, and applicability concerns, the Newcastle-Ottawa quality assessment scale (NOS) was used. Two investigators assessed the quality of included studies, scoring each study from 0 to 9, where studies with a score of at least six were considered high-quality.

Statistical analysis

The Generic Inverse Variance method was used to determine the Pooled Hazard Ratio (HR) value of RFS in ctDNA negative compared with RFS in ctDNA positive at a 95 % confidence interval (CI), and forest plots were used. Log HR was used as point estimates, as the data entered was assessed on a log scale and standard errors (SE) in the Generic Inverse Variance method. Review Manager 5.4 software version was used for analysis. We preferred the random effect model over the fixed effect model to avoid the likelihood of interstudy variability. Forest plots were used to assess the publication bias. Statistical Heterogeneity (I2), which shows the variation across the selected studies due to heterogeneity rather than chance, was reported using the I2 statistics, where I2 if >75 % were considered high heterogeneity. Those analyses that reported high heterogeneity were subjected to sensitive analysis using a “leave-out” analysis technique in random effect. P value < 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant.

Relationship between ctDNA status and risk of recurrence

<1 week after surgery

Two studies were included to know the relationship for recurrence-free survival in terms of detection of ctDNA at less than one week after surgery. Results obtained on random-effect pattern showed that compared to ctDNA-positive post-treatment detection, ctDNA-negative post-treatment detection had a higher HR of 5.91 at a 95 % confidence interval of 2.80–12.51 (p < 0.00001) for recurrence-free survival, as shown in Fig. 2. The heterogeneity obtained was 59 %, which was also not significant (p = 0.12).

Random-effect Forest plot showing the relationship of recurrence-free survival in ctDNA-negative compared to ctDNA-positive at less than one-week post-treatment.

1 week to 3 months after surgery

Six studies were included with ctDNA-positive and ctDNA-negative cases at one week to 3 months after surgery, which had the data for recurrence-free survival in terms of detection of ctDNA. Initially, the results obtained on random-effect patterns were used for assessment, which showed that compared to ctDNA-positive post-treatment detection, ctDNA-negative post-treatment detection had a higher HR of 5.27 at 95 % confidence interval of 3.62–7.67 (p < 0.00001) for recurrence-free survival, as shown in Fig. 3. The heterogeneity obtained was 11 %, which was not significant (p = 0.35).

Three studies were included to know the relationship for recurrence-free survival in terms of detection of ctDNA more than three months after surgery. Discussion

The National Cancer Institute states that a liquid biopsy is; “A test done on a sample of blood to look for cancer cells from a tumour that are circulating in the blood or for pieces of DNA from tumour cells that are in the blood” [26]. ctDNA is a component of cfDNA released from the malignant tumour cells into peripheral blood and other body fluids depending on the tumour's location [27]. Detection of ctDNA after curative surgery or treatment may indicate the presence of MRD and help identify patients at higher risk of relapse [12]. Multiple studies have found that in patients with different types of solid tumours, the identification of minimal residual disease (MRD) through ctDNA profiling is associated with a more unfavourable prognosis [[28], [29], [30], [31]]. In a study by Chaudhuri et al. in localised lung cancer patients, post-treatment ctDNA-positive patients had poor overall survival compared to ctDNA-negative patients with an HR of 14.3 and p value < 0.001. They also noted that post-treatment ctDNA detection preceded visible radiological progression by around 5.2 months in 72 % of recurring patients [32]. Another study by Peng et al. also had similar findings and further suggested that along with early relapse detection, ctDNA identification can also play a critical role in categorising patients for treatment [33].

In our study, we divided the data into three time periods: Less than one week, one week to 3 months, and more than three months. Based on the analysis conducted, it was found that data from studies of all three time periods coherently pointed towards a single conclusion. The derived conclusion indicated that ctDNA-negative patients post-treatment experienced longer recurrence-free survival than ctDNA-positive patients. The less than-week period included two studies (Fig. 2), which, upon analysis, led to the HR value of 4.85 at a 95 % confidence interval of 2.41–9.77 (p < 0.0001) for recurrence-free survival in ctDNA-negative patients compared to ctDNA positive patients post-treatment. Similar predictive findings were obtained during the analysis of six studies included in the 1-week to 3-month period (Fig. 3). A higher HR value of 5.31 at a 95 % confidence interval of 3.76–7.49 (p < 0.00001) was for recurrence-free survival in the ctDNA negative patients post-treatment compared to the corresponding ctDNA positive patients. Three studies were used to determine the correlation between ctDNA detection more than three months after surgery and recurrence-free survival. According to random-effect pattern data, ctDNA-negative post-treatment detection had a higher HR of 6.62 at a 95 % confidence interval of 2.85–15.35 (p < 0.0001) for recurrence-free survival, as shown in (Fig. 4). Since a p-value of high significance was found in each time period, we could strongly argue regarding the utility of ctDNA liquid biopsy in predicting the prognosis of NSCLC patients post-treatment. Additionally, the studies we found involved individuals who had received various treatments, including curative intent resection, adjuvant chemotherapy such as EGFR (epidermal growth factor receptor) growth inhibitor, neoadjuvant immunotherapy, and radiation therapy. Previous studies have successfully indicated the prognostic role of liquid biopsy in post-surgical NSCLC patients [19]. Still, our analysis throws light on the clinical utility of ctDNA detection in finding RFS of NSCLC patients undergoing multiple treatment combinations, including surgical and non-surgical modalities.

This meta-analysis aimed to provide evidence-based literature supporting the use of the MRD-ctDNA technique in NSCLC patients by assessing its prognostic value. While there have been reports on the use of ctDNA analysis to detect patients at a high risk of recurrence in various types of cancer, there is currently a limited number of published studies on postoperative ctDNA measurements, specifically in patients with localised non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) [29,34,35]. Li, N, Wang et al. state that patients with operable non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) who exhibit detectable circulating tumour DNA (ctDNA) following surgery may benefit from receiving early intensified treatment [14,23]. The ADAURA trial demonstrated that in cases of localised non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) with EGFR (epidermal growth factor receptor) mutations, the administration of adjuvant Osimertinib over three years resulted in a significant extension of cancer-free survival [15,36]. A ctDNA approach in postoperative surveillance may also offer a preliminary understanding of tumour features, including the molecular environment [16,37].

Adding to the coherence, ctDNA-based techniques for MRD detection strongly correlated with clinicopathological factors such as TNM staging and LNM status in predicting the RFS of NSCLC patients [19,20]. The current clinical guidelines suggest using radiological imaging to monitor disease recurrence in NSCLC patients [38]. However, the novel ctDNA-based MRD detection technique should be seriously considered as it is a more sensitive predictive marker for prognosis. Based on prior investigations, the longitudinal ctDNA monitoring of at least two post-treatment points preceded the radiological evidence for relapse detection of NSCLC patients by a median of 145 days [19]. Additionally, compared to CTC analysis, the ctDNA analysis technique seems more tempting because it is simpler to isolate the former from the plasma samples of the patients [39].

One of the limitations of our study is the inherent heterogeneity bias of the included studies it brings in any meta-analysis. Furthermore, we addressed the recurrence-free survival as the only primary endpoint without having any secondary clinical endpoints, which may have shown a consistent overlap with the estimated effect. However, the more accurate estimate of effect size obtained by including a patient population, which increases the power, and carrying out a subgroup analysis, which decreases the variation, helps to quantify the estimated effect size objectively and provides evidence in making clinical decisions. More randomised observational multicenter studies are needed to support the results obtained through this analysis.

Conclusion

Patients with malignancy are managed using different imaging tools, histopathology reports, and tissue biopsies. These approaches are considered undeniably relevant and can never be replaced. However, liquid biopsy can be the next diagnostic modality in routine life if the primary tests fail or fall short of diagnosing the tumour or its recurrence. The liquid biopsy method can be used to examine and monitor the malignancy in the patients without adding further burden on them. This meta-analysis thoroughly describes the prognostic value of ctDNA in detecting MRD in patients with different stages of NSCLC in terms of RFS by comparing the post-treatment ctDNA levels. All the included studies, despite being heterogeneous, suggested that ctDNA can be used as a reliable investigation for calculating the likelihood of disease relapse and thus can be used to examine the status of the tumour. It can be used at different time points post-treatment to measure the residual tumour burden and, hence, aid the therapy protocol. The oncologists can utilise the high sensitivity and specificity of the liquid biopsy in tailoring the regimen.

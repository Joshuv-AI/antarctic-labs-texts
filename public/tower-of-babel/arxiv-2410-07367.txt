Abstract
Let L s , p ​ ( ℝ n ) L^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) denote the homogeneous Sobolev-Slobodeckij space. In this paper, we demonstrate the existence of a bounded linear extension operator from the jet space J E ⌊ s ⌋ ​ L s , p ​ ( ℝ n ) J^{\lfloor s\rfloor}_{E}L^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) to L s , p ​ ( ℝ n ) L^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) for any E ⊆ ℝ n E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n} , p ∈ [ 1 , ∞ ) p\in[1,\infty) , and s ∈ ( 0 , ∞ ) s\in(0,\infty) satisfying n p < { s } \frac{n}{p}<\{s\} , where { s } \{s\} represents the fractional part of s s . Our approach builds upon the classical Whitney extension operator and uses the method of exponentially decreasing paths.

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a seminormed vector space consisting of functions that are
m
-times differentiable, where
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and these functions are defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For any subset
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, define the
jet space

J^{m}_{E}\mathbb{X}=\{(J^{m}_{x}F)_{x\in E}:F\in\mathbb{X}\}
, where
J^{m}_{x}F
represents the
m
-th Taylor polynomial of
F\in\mathbb{X}
at the point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We refer to
(J^{m}_{x}F)_{x\in E}
as the
jet
of
F
on the set
E
. For any given jet
f
in
J^{m}_{E}\mathbb{X}
, the seminorm of
f
on
J^{m}_{E}\mathbb{X}
is defined by

\|f\|_{J^{m}_{E}\mathbb{X}}:=\inf\{\|F\|_{\mathbb{X}}:F\in\mathbb{X}\text{ and }(J^{m}_{x}F)_{x\in E}=f\}.

Providing the
m
-th Taylor polynomials is equivalent to specifying the function values and derivatives up to
m
-th order.

For any operator
T:J^{m}_{E}\mathbb{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{X}
, we say
T
is an extension operator if for any
f\in J^{m}_{E}\mathbb{X}
,
(J^{m}_{x}Tf)_{x\in E}=f
. This gives rise to the extension problem:
Can we find a bounded linear extension operator from
J^{m}_{E}\mathbb{X}
to
\mathbb{X}
?

The extension problem was first introduced and studied by H. Whitney in 1934
[
15
,
16
,
17
]
. In particular, he developed the Whitney extension operator and provided a solution to the extension problem for
C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in
[
15
]
. Later in 1958, G. Glaeser showed that the Whitney extension operator is a bounded linear operator from the jet space
J_{E}^{m}C^{m,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to the Hölder space
C^{m,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for any natural number
m
and
0<r\leq 1

[
8
]
; (see also Chapter VI in
[
14
]
.) For Sobolev spaces, P. Shvartsman, in 2009, proved the existence of a bounded linear extension operator from
J^{0}_{E}L^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and from
J^{0}_{E}W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
p>n
by adapting the classical Whitney extension operator
[
12
]
. Later in 2017, he extended the above results to
J^{m-1}_{E}L^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
J^{m-1}_{E}W^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
where
m
is an arbitrary positive integer and
p>n

[
13
]
. For other works relevant to the classical Whitney extension operator, see, e.g., Chapter VI in
[
14
]
and
[
7
,
1
]
.

For the extension of function values on a set
E
, without known derivatives, H. Whitney first considered the space of
m
-th continuously differentiable functions
\mathbb{X}=C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and solved the problem for
n=1

[
16
]
. Then C. Fefferman solved the case for all dimensions
n
in 2006
[
2
]
. For the work on homogeneous Sobolev space, in 2013, A. Israel proved the existence of a bounded linear extension operator from the trace space to the homogeneous Sobolev space
L^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
p>n
, where they introduced the exponential decreasing path
[
9
]
. Later, in 2014, C. Fefferman, A. Israel and G. Luli
[
5
]
extended the result and proved the existence of a bounded linear extension operator for
L^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
p\in(n,\infty)
and
m
is any positive integer. There are also related works for the range restriction extension problem. See, e.g.,
[
3
,
4
,
6
]
and the references therein.

All of the above results,
however
, deal with Sobolev spaces with integer regularity parameters. In this paper, we address the Whitney extension problem for the homogeneous Sobolev-Slobodeckij space
\mathbb{X}=L^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
p>n
and the regularity parameter
s>0
is
non-integer
. We will require the regularity parameter to satisfy
\frac{n}{p}<\{s\}
in order to guarantee that the functions in
L^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
belong to
C^{\lfloor s\rfloor}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, by the Sobolev embedding theorem. Here,
\lfloor s\rfloor\in\mathbb{N}
denotes the integer part of
s
, and

\{s\}\in[0,1)
represents the fractional part.

We note that a
key difference
between the current work and the related work of
[
12
,
13
]
is the consideration of
non-integer regularity parameter

s
. While
[
12
,
13
]
rely on the estimation of sharp maximal functions, this work adopts an alternative approach, using the technique of integrating along exponentially decreasing paths in the Whitney decomposition.

Our main result can be stated as follows:

Theorem 1

(Main Result)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
s\in(0,\infty)
, and
p\in[1,\infty)
. If
\frac{n}{p}<\{s\}
, where
\{s\}
is the fractional part of
s
, and
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then there exists a bounded linear extension operator
T:J^{\lfloor s\rfloor}_{E}L^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

\|Tf\|_{L^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{n,s,p}\|f\|_{J^{\lfloor s\rfloor}_{E}L^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where
C_{n,s,p}
is a positive constant depending only on
n
,
s
, and
p
.

Technique of the proof:
To establish the result, we construct our operator by adapting techniques from the classical Whitney extension operator (see, for example, Chapter
VI
in
[
14
]
), and utilize the exponentially decreasing paths (see Lemma
7
) for norm estimation. Specifically, we prove the theorem through the following steps.
First
, we decompose
\overline{E}^{c}
, which is the complement of the closure of set
E
, into a family of cubes
\mathscr{F}=\{Q_{k}\}
by adapting the classical Whitney decomposition, and for each cube
P
in
\mathscr{F}
we choose a point
x_{P}\in\overline{E}
which is also one of the closest points in
\overline{E}
to the cube
P
(and it might not be unique).
Then
we construct the extension operator
T
by the classical method and show it is indeed bounded.

To demonstrate that
T
is bounded,
we need to show that

\|Tf\|_{L^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

for any
f
and
for any
F\in L^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying
(J_{x}^{\lfloor s\rfloor}F)_{x\in E}=f
.

For this purpose, we decompose
\|Tf\|_{L^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
into several pieces and show that each piece is bounded by the sum of
\|F\|_{L^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
and another term whose main part is about the difference between
\partial^{i}F
and its Taylor polynomial at some point
a_{P}
, i.e.,

J^{\lfloor s\rfloor-|i|}_{x_{P}}\partial^{i}F(a_{P})-\partial^{i}F(a_{P}).

(1)

By the geometric properties of our Whitney decomposition (as detailed in Lemma
1
below), the line connecting
a_{P}
and
x_{P}
can be covered by countably many cubes
\{P_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
from the decomposition
\mathscr{F}
,
which satisfies the exponentially decreasing property described in Lemma
7
.
Using this approach, we bound the term (
1
) above by a series involving
\|F\|_{L^{s,p}(P_{j})}
. Consequently, this provides an upper bound for
\|Tf\|_{L^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
in terms of
\|F\|_{L^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
. This completes the proof of the main theorem.

The organization of the paper is as follows: After introducing the necessary notations and preliminary results in Section
2
, we prove the main lemmas in Section
3
, which are essential for establishing the main result of this paper. In Section
4
, we present the proof of Theorem
1
.

\|f\|_{L^{s,p}(S)}:=\left(\int_{S}\int_{S}\frac{\big|\sum_{|k|=\lfloor s\rfloor}|\partial^{k}f(x)-\partial^{k}f(y)|\big|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+\{s\}p}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(2)

(3)

\|f\|_{J^{\lfloor s\rfloor}_{E}L^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\inf\{\|F\|_{L^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:F\in L^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ and }(J^{\lfloor s\rfloor}_{x}F)_{x\in E}=f\}.

We recall the definitions of the Hölder spaces and the Sobolev embedding theorem.

Definition 2

(Homogeneous Hölder spaces)
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
0<r<1
, we define
\dot{C}^{m,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as the space of functions
f\in C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for which the following seminorm is finite:

\|f\|_{\dot{C}^{m,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,x\neq y}\frac{|\nabla^{m}f(x)-\nabla^{m}f(y)|}{|x-y|^{r}}.

Proposition 1

(Sobolev Embedding Theorem
[
10
]
)
.

Let
\frac{n}{p}<\{s\}
and let
Q
be a cube in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then every
F\in L^{s,p}(Q)
has a unique representative
\overline{F}\in\dot{C}^{\lfloor s\rfloor,\{s\}-\frac{n}{p}}(Q)
such that

\|\overline{F}\|_{\dot{C}^{\lfloor s\rfloor,\{s\}-\frac{n}{p}}(Q)}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{s,p}(Q)}.

In particular, for any
k
satisfying
|k|=\lfloor s\rfloor
,
x,y\in Q
, we have

|\partial^{k}\overline{F}(x)-\partial^{k}\overline{F}(y)|\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{s,p}(Q)}|x-y|^{\{s\}-\frac{n}{p}}.

Proposition 2
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
F\in C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. For any
x,x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there exists
t\in(0,1)
such that

F(x)-J_{x_{0}}^{m}F(x)=\sum_{|k|=m}\frac{1}{k!}\big(\partial^{k}F(tx+(1-t)x_{0})-\partial^{k}F(x_{0})\big)(x-x_{0})^{k}.

Proof.

We first prove the result in the one-dimensional case, i.e.,
n=1
. Let
x,x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, by the Lagrange remainder formula (see
[
11
]
), there exists
t\in(0,1)
such that

\begin{split}F(x)-J^{m}_{x_{0}}F(x)=&F(x)-J^{m-1}_{x_{0}}F(x)-\frac{1}{m!}\partial^{m}F(x)(x-x_{0})^{m}\\
=&\frac{1}{m!}\big(\partial^{m}F(tx+(1-t)x_{0})-\partial^{m}F(x_{0})\big)(x-x_{0})^{m}.\end{split}

In the higher-dimensional case, let
\varphi(t)=F(tx+(1-t)x_{0})
. Then
\varphi\in C^{m}(\mathbb{R})
, and by the one-dimensional result, there exists
t\in(0,1)
such that

\varphi(1)-J^{m}_{0}\varphi(1)=\frac{1}{m!}(\partial^{m}\varphi(t)-\partial^{m}\varphi(0)).

(4)

For any
j\in\{0,1,\dots,m\}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, by chain rule, we have

\partial^{j}\varphi(t)=\left(((x-x_{0})\cdot\nabla)^{j}F\right)(tx+(1-t)x_{0})=\sum_{|k|=j}\frac{j!}{k!}\partial^{k}F(tx+(1-t)x_{0})(x-x_{0})^{k}.

Using the above equation, we get

\begin{split}J^{m}_{0}\varphi(1)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{1}{j!}\partial^{j}\varphi(0)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{|k|=j}\frac{1}{k!}\partial^{k}F(x_{0})(x-x_{0})^{k}=\sum_{|k|\leq m}\frac{1}{k!}\partial^{k}F(x_{0})(x-x_{0})^{k}=J^{m}_{x_{0}}F(x),\end{split}

and

\partial^{m}\varphi(t)-\partial^{m}\varphi(0)=\sum_{|k|=m}\frac{m!}{k!}(\partial^{k}F(tx+(1-t)x_{0})-\partial^{k}F(x_{0}))(x-x_{0})^{k}.

We complete the proof of the proposition by substituting the last two equations into equation (
4
) and using the fact that
\varphi(1)=F(x)
.
∎

\delta_{Q}\leq\frac{1}{10}\text{dist}(Q,D)\leq\frac{1}{10}|x-x_{0}|.

(32)

Combining the above two inequalities together, we get

|x-x_{P}|\leq\frac{47}{10}|x-x_{0}|.

(33)

From inequality (
32
) and Lemma
2
(
1
), we get

\delta_{P}\leq 2\delta_{Q}\leq\frac{1}{5}|x-x_{0}|.

(34)

Next, we will derive an upper bound for
\partial^{i}H(x)
:

\displaystyle|\partial^{i}H(x)|=

\displaystyle\Big|\partial^{i}\sum_{P}\theta_{P}(x)\big(J_{x_{P}}^{m}F(x)-F(x)\big)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{j\leq i}\sum_{P}\big|\partial^{i-j}\theta_{P}(x)\big|\big|\partial^{j}J_{x_{P}}^{m}F(x)-\partial^{j}F(x)\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{j\leq i}\sum_{P\leftrightarrow Q}\delta_{P}^{|j|-|i|}\big|J_{x_{P}}^{m-|j|}\partial^{j}F(x)-\partial^{j}F(x)\big|

\displaystyle(\text{by Remark \ref{11})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{j\leq i}\sum_{P\leftrightarrow Q}\delta_{P}^{|j|-|i|}\sum_{|h|=m}\sup_{|t-x|\leq|x-x_{P}|}|\partial^{h}F(t)-\partial^{h}F(x_{P})||x-x_{P}|^{m-|j|}

\displaystyle(\text{by Proposition \ref{p1}})

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{P\leftrightarrow Q}\sum_{|h|=m}\sup_{|t-x|\leq 5|x-x_{0}|}|\partial^{h}F(t)-\partial^{h}F(x_{P})||x-x_{0}|^{m-|i|},

(35)

where the latter inequality is derived from inequality (
33
) and inequality (
34
), all constants are only depending on
n
and
m
. By triangle inequality and inequality (
33
), we shall continue as follows:

\displaystyle\eqref{by(6)}~\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{P\leftrightarrow Q}\sum_{|h|=m}\sup_{|t-x|\leq 5|x-x_{0}|}|\partial^{h}F(t)-\partial^{h}F(x_{0})||x-x_{0}|^{m-|i|}

\displaystyle+\sum_{P\leftrightarrow Q}\sum_{|h|=m}\sup_{|t-x|\leq 5|x-x_{0}|}|\partial^{h}F(x_{0})-\partial^{h}F(x_{P})||x-x_{0}|^{m-|i|}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|x-x_{0}|^{m-|i|}\sum_{|h|=m}\sup_{|t-x|\leq 5|x-x_{0}|}|\partial^{h}F(t)-\partial^{h}F(x_{0})|.

(36)

For any
0\leq k<m
, assume that the statement
S(k)
is true. Then, for any
i
with
|i|=k
, and for any
x_{0}\in D
and
x\in D^{c}
, we have
\partial^{i}H(x_{0})=0
because
S(k)
is true. Therefore, by inequality (
36
), we can write:

\begin{split}|\partial^{i}H(x)-\partial^{i}H(x_{0})|=&|\partial^{i}H(x)|\\
\lesssim&|x-x_{0}|^{m-|i|}\sum_{|h|=m}\sup_{|t-x|\leq 5|x-x_{0}|}|\partial^{h}F(t)-\partial^{h}F(x_{0})|.\end{split}

Notice that

|x-x_{0}|^{m-1-|i|}\sum_{|h|=m}\sup_{|t-x|\leq 5|x-x_{0}|}|\partial^{h}F(t)-\partial^{h}F(x_{0})|\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\quad x\rightarrow x_{0},\ x\in D^{c},

because
|i|=k<m
.
Therefore

\frac{|\partial^{i}H(x)-\partial^{i}H(x_{0})|}{|x-x_{0}|}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\quad x\rightarrow x_{0},\ x\in D^{c}.

Also, for any
x\in D
,
\partial^{i}H(x)=\partial^{i}H(x_{0})=0
. In conclusion, we have

\frac{|\partial^{i}H(x)-\partial^{i}H(x_{0})|}{|x-x_{0}|}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\quad x\rightarrow x_{0}.

(37)

This implies that
\partial^{i}H
is differentiable at
x_{0}\in D
. Recall
H
is
C^{m}
on
D^{c}
. Therefore,
\partial^{i}H
is differentiable on the entire
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Furthermore, by the formula (
37
) and the definition of derivative,
for any
j
such that
|j|=|i|+1=k+1
, we have
\partial^{j}H(x)=0
.

By induction, we conclude that
S(k)
is true for all
0\leq k\leq m
, and therefore also for
k=m
. This means that
H
is
m
-times differentiable and that for any
|i|\leq m
and
x\in D
,

\partial^{i}H(x)=0.

(38)

The final step is to prove that
\partial^{i}G
is continuous for
|i|=m
. We only need to show that it is continuous at every point in
D
.

For any
x_{0}\in D
and any
x\in D^{c}
, using inequality (
36
), we have

|\partial^{i}H(x)-\partial^{i}H(x_{0})|=|\partial^{i}H(x)|\lesssim\sum_{|h|=m}\sup_{|t-x|\leq 5|x-x_{0}|}|\partial^{h}F(t)-\partial^{h}F(x_{0})|\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\quad x\rightarrow x_{0},\,x\in D^{c}.

For any
x\in D
, by equation (
38
),

|\partial^{i}H(x)-\partial^{i}H(x_{0})|=0.

Therefore

\lim_{x\rightarrow x_{0}}|\partial^{i}H(x)-\partial^{i}H(x_{0})|=0.

This implies that
\partial^{i}H
is continuous at every point in
D
, which in turn means that
\partial^{i}H
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Since
H\in C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, it follows that
G=H+F\in C^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. For any
x\in D
and for all multi-indices
i
with
|i|\leq m
, we have

\partial^{i}G(x)=\partial^{i}H(x)+\partial^{i}F(x)=\partial^{i}F(x).

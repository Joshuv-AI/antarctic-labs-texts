Effectiveness of integrated interdisciplinary postgraduate workshops with competency-based assessment: A mixed method approach
Authors: Aradhana Sanga, Anjali Borle, Khushbo Rani, Zenith H. Kerketta, Deepali Tirkey, D. K. Sinha, Sunil K. Mahto, K. Sandhya
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:An interdisciplinary curriculum refers to curriculum organisation that cuts across subject matter lines to focus upon comprehensive life problems or broad-based areas of study that brings together the various segments of the curriculum into meaningful association. The current study is intended to improve the knowledge and application of subject across the departments to build a strong foundation for future clinicians and enhance interdepartmental coordination.Objective:The study was done to compare the Integrated post-session (OSCE) Objective structured clinical examination/(OSPE) Objective structured Practical examination scores with the traditional scores of two groups followed by a Focus Group Discussion.Method:Forty two first year postgraduate residents from Surgery, pathology and anatomy departments in the year 2025 included a pre-test on knowledge and skill of the participants followed by an integrated session on the topic decided based on Postgraduate competency, randomization of the inclusion group into an intervention group having demonstrate, observe, assist and perform session followed by post-test and Focus group Discussion whose participants were recruited by purposive sampling.Conclusion:Beneficial impact of the intervention on knowledge and skill of the participants post intervention was noted with statistically significant mean score difference of 9.79 ± 1.87 and 12.38 ± 3.03. Focus Group Discussion analysis yielded 13 deductive codes 7 sub-codes and 3 themes being i) increase competency, ii) content integration, and iii) positive personal and social impact. Integrated interdisciplinary post graduate workshop with competency-based assessment will not only enhance Participant satisfaction with skill enhancement, but also promote Interdisciplinary collaboration.

Introduction

The interdisciplinary education is structured to foster team work by health care providers and foster collaborative relationships among learners, teachers, and the content itself.[123] The non-integrated approach imparts knowledge in a fragmented manner leading to overburdening of the subject and resulting in rote memorizing instead of understanding.[4] Petrie’s “cognitive map” theory tells us that a non-integrated approach gives a fragmented cognitive and perceptual approach to each discipline such that two disciplinarian look at the same thing but do not see it.[5] An interdisciplinary curriculum refers to a curriculum organization, which cuts across subject matter lines to focus upon comprehensive life problems or broad based areas of study that brings together the various segments of the curriculum into meaningful association.[2678]

Collaboration helps in incorporating knowledge from other department to enhance one’s own skill and knowledge, the automated machine like performance along with redundancy of collaborating departments can be minimized.[9] Appreciation and understanding each other’s discipline early in clinical fields will make the students aware of the inherent limitations of one’s own department thus helping to focus and plan treatment in best interest of patient.[10] Bridging the traditional barriers between basic and clinical departments will make the departments familiar with the intricacies and complexities of each other and remove personal biases which have long been prevalent.[1112]

The Post graduate residents’ foundational knowledge of core subject needs to be sound to prepare them for better application and summation. This is only made possible when they are aware of location, structure, function, derangements, diagnostic tests, and plans required for treatment of the disease.[2313] Preparation of the post graduate summative exam paper 1 which comprises of core Anatomy of the subject can also be emphasized.

The current study is intended to improve the knowledge and application of subject across the departments to build a strong foundation for future clinicians helping to analyses clinical problems in a critical way and facilitate deep learning for development of habit of life long learner.[14] and move them ahead from passive to interactive learner according to National Medical Commission (NMC) guidelines.[15] On doing a literature search many articles on cognitive aspects of the method and perception based on Likert scale is available but skill assessment of integrated learning with qualitative perception have not been reported.[161718]

This method will help prepare the Post Graduate Students to answer the summative university exam paper one more effectively, which comprises of gross anatomy related to the subject. The interdepartmental coordination will help the medical education unit and curriculum committee of the institute to further work in an integrated manner. Furthermore, the six core competencies for the residency programmers, which includes interpersonal skills, need to be assessed as directed by the Accreditation Council for Graduate Medical Education.

After going through the various literatures Methodological gap, Practical-knowledge conflict gap and Empirical gap was found and thus addressed by Integrated workshops with competency-based assessment for post graduate students, using a structured skill assessment approach and a validated questionnaire. Mixed method research was thus applied in which workshop was conducted comprising of seminar, with skill assessment along with multiple sessions of focus group discussion.

The approach will enhance the clinical reasoning, collaborative working and diagnostic accuracy of the resident doctors have a direct impact on patient care giving it a more patient-centric, outcome-oriented approach along with strengthening the necessity of the subject. This study aims to assess the effectiveness of interdisciplinary collaborative postgraduate seminars on admitted patients using a mixed method approach.

Results and Statistical Analysis

A total of 42 residents participated in the study out of which 27 fulfilled the inclusion criteria (Response rate-64.2%) (Males = 16, Females = 11) Analysis was conducted using SPSS 22 for: a) Content Validation of Questionnaire, b) Pre and post assessment score of both intervention and control group: independent sample t test, and c) Thematic analysis for focus group discussion.

Validation of QuestionnaireThe content validity of the Questionnaire was established through expert evaluation. Those included as review experts belonged to the three collaborating departments with more than 5 years of research experience while 6 other experts who rated the questionnaire had more than 7 years of research experience. The rating expert rated each item on a four point relevance scale. The I-CVI for individual items ranged from 0.83 to 1.00. The scale- level indices, S-CVI/Ave = 0.96 and S-CVI/UA = 0.80, indicated excellent content validity. The instrument’s internal consistency was confirmed by Cronbach’s alpha of 0.99 demonstrating good reliability.Pre and post assessment score:On doing a normality test for difference between the scores of both the sessions (diff_1, diff_2) both were found to be not normally distributed [Table 1]Since P value < 0.05 hence the data do not follow a normal distribution due to which non-parametric test was applied.The Wilcoxon sign rank test indicated that mean post-test ranks for assessment were statistically significantly higher than pre-test as shown in Table 2.For comparison of post score between the intervention and non-intervention group in session 1, the descriptive data is as follows [Tables 3 and 4].Difference in mean score of the intervention group of the two sessions was noted as tabulated above. Discussion

Various researches were conducted on integrated innovative sessions with the residents have highlighted its beneficial effects along with enhancement of post intervention scores as under [Table 6]. Our findings are more in line with He et al.[19] who saw the Effect of case based collaborative learning post-test score mean to be more than pre-test score mean by 7, while we had a mean score difference of 10, 12, respectively, in the two sessions. A variety of authors have tried to see the benefits of integrated sessions in various aspects. Miller ME et al.[1] evaluated 25% improvement of quality and patient care to be increased with a statistical significance, and all found, integrated multidisciplinary session were beneficial for D’Souza et al.[2] with 6 score increase.Literature has also shown the beneficial effects of Focus group discussion after the implementation of the intervention. As mentioned by Sulena et al.[13] with respect to the focus group discussion on perception towards integrated case-based sessions, we found three aspects similar in our research, i) aligning learning objective, ii) promote problem solving and iii) promote teamwork.The limitations of the study were that the study was conducted with the collaboration of only three departments, an institutional level generalizability needs to be assessed. Finally, assessment needs to be followed up after 6 months to bring out the actual effect of change in critical skill of the PG’s involved.The study is meant to introduce quality teaching in traditional curriculum with improvement in diagnostic skills, critical thinking, self-learning ability, deep learning, and clinical problem-solving skills. Student’s will be able to relate their prior knowledge to the new knowledge and application of acquired theoretical concepts to everyday experience. Doctors with a deep approach are more likely to develop a habit of life-long learning. The importance of Interdisciplinary collaboration for improvement of knowledge and application for expanding curricula across the departments and allied medical branched will thus be applied with a more practical approach. This study has great potential in several areas, providing a thorough framework for institutional growth, clinical practice enhancement, holistic learning, and more extensive educational reforms.

Educational Scope: This study has the potential to improve postgraduate medical education by fusing experiential learning with group discussions. Clinical thinking and decision-making abilities would be improved by combining multidisciplinary discussion with real-world case-based learning.

Clinical Scope: By enhancing treatment approaches and diagnostic accuracy, the study may have a direct impact on patient care, by promoting clinical reasoning process by collaborative learning.

Research Scope: This intervention may also produce scalable metrics that may be used as standards to evaluate decision-making and diagnostic precision.

Institutional Scope: By encouraging cooperation amongst department by promoting reciprocal learning, creating uniform teaching and assessment procedures at the institutional level it might help in quality assurance.

Wider Impact: The findings of this study may have an impact on the creation of postgraduate curricula outside of academic institutions. Legislators might think about introducing multidisciplinary collaborative learning models into medical school curricula. The study may contribute to the development of a workforce prepared for the challenges of contemporary healthcare.

Conclusion

The impact of the integrated interdisciplinary post graduate workshop with competency-based assessment on knowledge and skill of the participants post intervention was noted with statistically significant mean score difference of 9.79 ± 1.87 and 12.38 ± 3.03. thus highlighted the beneficial effects. The Focus group discussion also highlighted the benefits as: i) increase competency, ii) content integration, and iii) positive personal and social impact.

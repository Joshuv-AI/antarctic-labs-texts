Thermoelastic Properties of the Ti 2 AlC MAX Phase: An ab initio study.
Bill C. Oyomo, Leah W. Mungai, Geoffrey Arusei, Michael Atambo, Mirriam Chepkoech, Nicholas Makau, G O Amolo

Abstract
The MAX phases are in use at an industrial scale in the transportation, armour and furnace development sectors, among others. However, data on the finite temperature dynamical properties of these materials under varying conditions of temperature and pressure are rare or unavailable. This study reports on the dynamical properties of the elastic constants and moduli under these conditions, obtained from first principle calculations. Both static and dynamical results are presented and discussed. It is observed, from the dynamical results, that the elastic moduli are degraded, specifically, the bulk and shear moduli show reduction ranging from 15 - 29% and 13 - 31%, respectively, between pressures of 10-30 GPa and in the temperature range of 300 - 1200 K. Such data is useful as part of decision support tools that can inform applications as well as the limitations of use.



I
Introduction

MAX phase materials have found various applications in the industry involving varying pressure and temperature conditions. They are largely used as high-temperature materials owing to their resistance to oxidation under these conditions
Guo et al. 2024
. Hence, simulations of their elastic properties under these conditions are useful to make decisions on possible applications as well as the limits of use. Reports on the dynamic properties of the MAX phases in the literature are rare or unavailable. This work aims to provide indications of how the elastic constants and the moduli will be affected under dynamic pressure and temperature conditions. The Ti
2
AlC MAX phase is considered to be representative of these materials as it has been extensively used in various applications. First principle approaches are employed using the Quantum Espresso (QE)
Giannozzi et al. 2009
code as well as efficient processing tools such as the cij code
Luo et al. 2021
for evaluating the thermoelastic properties and snakemake
Köster and Rahmann 2012
for workflow management and efficient processing of the data.

II
Methodology

This study employed all first-principle calculations within the framework of Density Functional Theory (DFT)
Sholl and Steckel 2011
;
Kohn and Sham 1965
;
Hohenberg and Kohn 1964
as implemented in the Quantum Espresso code
Giannozzi et al. 2009
;
Scandolo et al. 2005
, with 60 Ry used as the energy cutoff (ecutwfc) for the plane wave basis set. Electron-ion interactions were treated using ultrasoft pseudopotentials. The exchange and correlation functional potentials were estimated using the generalized gradient approximation in the form of Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) functionals
Perdew et al. 1996
. A 12 x 12 x 2 Monkhorst-Pack k-point mesh was used to sample the first Brillouin zone. To obtain the structures at higher pressures, they were generated by uniformly scaling the ambient pressure structure, followed by relaxation at fixed volumes. The static elastic tensor was then derived from the stress-strain relationships denoted by equation (
1
), applying small strains of ±0.5%.

\sigma_{ij}=C_{ijkl}\epsilon_{jk},

(1)

where
C_{ijkl}
is the
4^{th}
order elastic tensor while
\sigma_{ij}
and
\epsilon_{kl}
represent the
2^{nd}
order stress and strain tensor, respectively
Da Silveira et al. 2013
.

A 2 x 2 x 2 q-point was used in the QE code to obtain the static energies and phonon frequencies for the phonon calculations at different volumes or pressures.
The Snakemake workflow management system
Köster and Rahmann 2012
was used to automate the whole process of generating input files, submitting jobs, and collecting results for both static elasticity and phonon calculations at different pressures. The post-processed outputs for static elasticity and phonon calculations from QE were then collected and used as inputs in the cij code
Luo et al. 2021
to calculate the elastic tensor at elevated temperatures. The elastic coefficients or elements of the isothermal elastic tensor are expressed as strain derivatives of the Helmholtz free energy
Luo et al. 2021
;
Wu and Wentzcovitch 2011
;
Wang et al. 2024
:

c_{ijkl}^{T}=\frac{1}{V}\left(\frac{\delta^{2}F}{\delta e_{ij}\delta e_{kl}}\right)+\frac{1}{2}P(2\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}-\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}-\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl})

(2)

where,
e_{ij}

;
Wang et al. 2024
.
The Helmholtz free energy was computed based on the quasiharmonic approximation (QHA)

F(e,V,T)=U^{st}(e,V)+\sum_{q}m\frac{1}{2}\hbar\omega_{qm}(e,V)+k_{b}T\sum_{qm}ln\left[1-exp\left(\frac{-\hbar\omega_{qm}(e,V)}{k_{B}T}\right)\right]

(3)

where,
U^{st}(e,V)
, q, V,
k_{B}
, T, and
\omega_{qm}
represent the static total energy, phonon wave vector, equilibrium volume, Boltzmann constant, temperature, and the phonon frequencies corresponding to the

III.2
Elastic properties under dynamic conditions

In Figure 3, the behaviour of the elastic constants under varying conditions of temperature and pressure are shown.
It is observed that all the 5 elastic constants are showing softening with the increase in pressure across the temperature profiles studied and in the 0 – 1100 K range. Only selected profiles are shown in the current graphs. The upper-temperature limit of 1200 K is close to the predicted melting point of the material. These calculations assume that the materials under investigation are pristine. Variations may be expected if the calculations include simple or extended defects as would occur in nature. It is observed that
C_{11}
,
C_{12}
and
C_{44}
show similar trends under varying conditions of rising temperature and pressure leading to a reduction in the values of the elastic constants.
C_{13}
and
C_{33}

C_{ijs}

The combined effect of the application of increasing temperature and pressure is exhibited in a complex way. For

The data shows that for applications at other fixed pressures and varying temperatures, there are notable changes in both the bulk and shear moduli. IV
Conclusion

Elastic constants, bulk, and shear moduli of Ti
2
AlC have been studied under dynamic pressure and temperature conditions in the 0–1200 K range with pressures ranging from 0–35 GPa. These investigations have employed
ab initio
approaches and postprocessing techniques. All the 5 elastic constants have shown an isotropic reduction in the values under dynamic conditions compared to those obtained purely under the static case: The bulk and shear moduli show reductions of up to 30% at temperatures close to 1200 K relative to the room temperature of 300 K, suggesting limits on where these materials are likely to find applications. This decrease in moduli at elevated temperatures can be attributed to the increased atomic vibrations and thermal expansion, which reduce the material’s resistance to deformation under dynamic loading. The temperature-induced softening highlights the effect of thermal conditions on the mechanical properties of Ti
2
AlC, particularly in high-temperature applications.

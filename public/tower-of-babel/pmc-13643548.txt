Shocked Beyond the Field
Authors: Kyoung-Ryul Julian Chun, Melanie Gunawardene, Boris Schmidt
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Corresponding Author

The report by Saida et al1 in this issue of JACC: Case Reports further expands the evolving spectrum of coronary vasospasm associated with pulsed field ablation (PFA). Importantly, this case challenges the prevailing concept that PFA-related coronary spasm is primarily a proximity-dependent phenomenon caused by direct energy delivery adjacent to epicardial coronary arteries.2 In the present report, severe multivessel coronary vasospasm with cardiogenic shock occurred despite fluoroscopic and computed tomography/FARAVIEW Software Module (Boston Scientific) evidence suggesting anatomical remoteness between the PFA sites and the coronary vasculature. Similar observations of remote or diffuse vasospastic reactions following PFA suggest that this phenomenon may extend beyond simple catheter-coronary proximity mechanisms.3 The investigators should be congratulated for presenting a highly illustrative and clinically important case that further expands our understanding of uncommon but potentially severe vascular reactions associated with contemporary PFA technologies.

Several clinically and mechanistically important considerations emerge from this observation.

First, improved identification of patients at increased risk for vasospastic complications may become increasingly relevant as PFA adoption expands globally.4,5 Baseline endothelial dysfunction, autonomic imbalance, impaired ventricular function, vasoreactive predisposition, and ethnicity-related susceptibility to vasospastic phenomena may all contribute to individual vulnerability. Future investigations should therefore focus not only on procedural characteristics but also on patient-specific biological substrates predisposing to exaggerated vasomotor responses.

Second, temporal aspects of vasospasm warrant careful consideration. Coronary spasm may not necessarily occur exclusively during energy delivery itself. Delayed ischemic electrocardiographic changes and postprocedural hemodynamic deterioration may have important implications in the era of increasingly simplified workflows and same-day discharge strategies following atrial fibrillation ablation.6,7 Although such events appear to remain rare, even infrequent complications assume increasing relevance as procedural pathways become progressively accelerated and resource optimized.

Third, the role of the applied energy source deserves further attention.8 Coronary vasospasm has been reported with both thermal and nonthermal ablation technologies.9 However, the increasing recognition of diffuse, nitrate-responsive, anatomically remote vasospasm following electroporation-based ablation raises the possibility that PFA may interact with vascular or autonomic pathways beyond direct local tissue effects. Potential mechanisms include ganglionated plexus modulation, endothelial perturbation, electroporation-associated vascular reactivity, procedure-related hemolysis with nitric oxide scavenging, and pharmacologic modulation of vasomotor tone.

Perhaps most importantly, this case underscores the importance of interdisciplinary exchange and immediate coronary access within contemporary electrophysiology programs. Centers performing complex PFA procedures should not only possess expertise in advanced left atrial ablation but also maintain immediate availability of invasive coronary angiography and interventional cardiology support.

Finally, despite the overall favorable safety profile of contemporary PFA technologies, improved mechanistic understanding and systematic characterization of rare vasospastic complications remain essential. Continued reporting of similar cases will be critical to better define their underlying mechanisms and clinical relevance. The present case suggests that electroporation-mediated effects may induce broader vascular, autonomic, and potentially other nonablation biological responses. In rare instances, patients may therefore become not only electrically treated but also biologically “shocked beyond the field.”

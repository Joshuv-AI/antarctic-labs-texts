CT-based morphometry and Mimics-guided virtual implantation of a facet-fusion-integrated posterior cervical semi-open-door system: an anatomical feasibility study
Authors: Wei-xin Dong, Weihu Ma, Yong Hu, Nanjian Xu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe management of multilevel cervical spondylosis with concomitant foraminal stenosis and instability remains challenging. Anterior-only, posterior-only, and circumferential procedures each present disadvantages regarding decompression adequacy, implant burden, and complication risk. We developed an integrated posterior system combining facet joint fusion and semi-open-door laminoplasty to enable decompression and stabilization through a single approach.MethodsThirty cervical CT scans from healthy adults (C2-C7) were reconstructed in Mimics. Key morphometric parameters relevant to device design were assessed, including lateral mass height and facet dimensions, minimum lamina height, lamina safety length, interfacet gap height, facet inclination, and spinous process screw height. A concept construct comprising a lateral mass plate, interfacet fusion cage, and laminoplasty plate was dimensioned from these data and virtually implanted to evaluate anatomical compatibility. Bilateral and sex-related differences were analyzed (two-tailed α = 0.05) with prespecified assumptions for parametric testing and multiple-comparison control.ResultsKey morphometric parameters (e.g., lateral mass height: 11.5–13.3 mm; lamina safety length: 25.3–28.6 mm) supported the dimensional design of the system components. Statistical analysis showed no significant differences related to sex or side (all p > 0.05). Crucially, Mimics-guided virtual implantation demonstrated successful positioning of all components without cortical breach, validating the anatomical compatibility of the construct.ConclusionCT-based morphometry and Mimics-guided virtual implantation demonstrate that the proposed facet-fusion-integrated posterior cervical semi–open-door system is anatomically feasible and dimensionally compatible with subaxial cervical anatomy. However, as this study is limited to imaging data and virtual simulation without biomechanical or clinical validation, the system should currently be regarded as a proof-of-concept design rather than a clinically established technique.

Introduction

Degenerative cervical disease involving multiple levels and combining canal and foraminal stenosis (long-segment mixed-type cervical spondylosis) carries a high risk of neurological deterioration, even after minor trauma. Surgical strategies must provide reliable decompression and stability, yet anterior-only, posterior-only, and circumferential approaches each have limitations [1–4]. Posterior laminectomy with lateral mass screw-rod fixation enlarges the canal but may alter posterior tension-band integrity and introduce construct-related concerns in multilevel settings [5]. Anterior procedures offer direct ventral decompression but, in long-segment disease, are limited by construct complexity and complications [6]. Circumferential surgery improves alignment and stability but requires repositioning, longer operative times, and greater resource use [7]. Degenerative cervical myelopathy is often under-recognized, with delayed presentation common, highlighting the need for dependable multilevel options [8]. Perioperative studies also show distinct care pathways for myelopathy versus radiculopathy [9]. Comparative evidence confirms trade-offs across anterior, posterior, and combined procedures, with no universally superior strategy [5, 10]. Within posterior paradigms, unilateral open-door laminoplasty with lateral-mass fixation often employs existing plates and rods, with inconsistent graft containment and foraminal enlargement. Anatomical variability further complicates construct design. Variants such as os odontoideum and posterior accessory ossicles may alter screw corridors and fusion surfaces, requiring meticulous preoperative planning [11–13]. To address these gaps, we developed a posterior system integrating facet-joint fusion for foraminal distraction with semi-open-door laminoplasty for canal expansion, coordinated through screw/plate trajectories. Objective. This study assessed its anatomical feasibility from C2-C7 using CT-based morphometry and Mimics-guided virtual implantation, hypothesizing that morphometric parameters would confirm dimensional compatibility and safe virtual placement without cortical breach, establishing a foundation for biomechanical and clinical validation.

Materials and methods

Data collection and measurement

Thirty cervical CT scans with three-dimensional reconstruction data were randomly selected from our hospital’s imaging system. All scans, including that of the oldest participant (70 years), were independently reviewed by two senior spine surgeons to confirm the absence of cervical degeneration. Exclusion criteria were disc space narrowing, facet joint arthropathy, osteophyte formation, developmental anomalies, metabolic bone disease, ankylosing spondylitis, prior surgery, or other pathological conditions. Only anatomically normal spines were included. The final sample comprised 15 males and 15 females aged 19–70 years (mean 37.4 ± 12.1 years). Although the sample size was modest, it aligns with previous anatomical morphometry studies. However, the study was not powered for inferential subgroup analysis, and sex- or side-related comparisons were interpreted descriptively. DICOM data were imported into Mimics software (Materialise, Leuven, Belgium) for three-dimensional reconstruction, and key anatomical parameters were measured on the reconstructed models. Statistical analysis was subsequently performed on the collected data.

Anatomical measurement indicators for C2-C7

Guided by the design requirements of the integrated system, eight prespecified morphometric parameters were obtained bilaterally at each level from C2 to C7 on 3D reconstructions (Fig. 1). To ensure consistency and surgical relevance, bony landmarks and reference planes were defined a priori on standardized axial, sagittal, and coronal views. Measurements are reported in millimetres (linear) and degrees (angular). The following eight parameters were measured bilaterally from C2 to C7: 1.Lateral mass height: the perpendicular distance between the midpoints of the superior and inferior articular facet surfaces of the same lateral mass. 2.Facet transverse diameter (mediolateral): the maximum mediolateral width of the lateral-mass articular surface. 3.Facet anteroposterior diameter: the maximum anteroposterior length of the lateral-mass articular surface measured in the plane of the facet. 4.Minimum lamina height: the shortest perpendicular distance between the superior and inferior borders of the lamina at each level. 5.Lamina safety length: the linear distance along the laminar midline from the lateral edge of the lateral mass to the base of the spinous process (intended corridor for plate/screw passage). 6.Interfacet gap height: the perpendicular distance between the facing cortices of adjacent facet surfaces (cephalad vs. caudal level) across the motion segment. 7.Interfacet gap–facet angle: the angle between the line representing the interfacet gap axis and the plane of the adjacent facet surface, reflecting facet-space inclination relative to the posterior elements. 8.Spinous-process screw height (available thickness): the vertical thickness of the spinous process at its base measured along its central axis, representing the safe height for screw insertion. For each metric, the measurement plane and landmarks were visually confirmed on multiplanar reconstructions; when needed, the laminar midline and facet best-fit planes were defined to maintain orthogonality and reduce parallax.

Design of the facet joint fusion-integrated posterior cervical semi-open door laminoplasty system

The investigational system is manufactured from medical-grade titanium alloy and comprises four components: a lateral-mass screw fixation plate, an interfacet expansion-and-fusion device, a semi–open-door fixation plate, and associated screws (Fig. 2). Dimensions are provided to guide feasibility assessment and virtual implantation rather than to prescribe definitive implant sizes. 1.Lateral-mass screw fixation plate: A low-profile plate (thickness 2 mm, width 8 mm) supplied in multiple lengths to accommodate interpatient variation. Screw holes accept Ø3.5 mm lateral-mass screws. The plate provides the primary anchor for coupling with the interfacet device. 2.Interfacet expansion-and-fusion device: A hollow, horseshoe-shaped spacer designed to permit in-device bone graft packing. The superior and inferior bearing faces are polyhedral to increase contact area with the facet surfaces. Size options include thickness 2/3/4 mm, anteroposterior diameter 8/10/12 mm, and transverse diameter 8/10/12 mm. The device is integrated with the lateral-mass plate at a nominal 40° interface; intraoperative adjustment of this angle is feasible using dedicated instruments to align with individual facet inclination. Integration with the plate is intended to simplify construct assembly and maintain graft containment. 3.Semi–open-door fixation plate: A rectangular plate (lengths 25/28/31/34/37/40 mm, width 6 mm) for laminoplasty stabilization. The outer holes accept Ø3.5 mm screws; the inner side includes two Ø2.0 mm holes configured for spinous-process or laminar purchase according to surgeon preference and local bone stock. 4.Associated screws: Lateral-mass screws: Ø3.5 mm with lengths 12/14/16 mm; Spinous-process screws: Ø2.0 mm with lengths 10/12/14 mm; Screw trajectories and lengths were selected based on the morphometric corridors identified in Sect. 1.1–1.2 and validated by virtual implantation.

Surgical design and simulation of the facet joint Fusion-Integrated posterior cervical Semi-Open door laminoplasty system

Surgical approach (conceptual workflow)

A standard posterior cervical approach was used. With the patient prone and the head secured in a radiolucent head holder, a posterior midline incision was made and subperiosteal exposure performed to the lateral masses and laminae. After confirming the target levels, the facet surfaces were lightly decorticated with a burr to promote arthrodesis. The interfacet expansion-and-fusion device was inserted obliquely (30°−40° to the posterior lateral-mass plane) under direct visualization and manual distraction to align with the individual facet inclination. Following confirmation of seating and cortical contact, the lateral-mass fixation plate was secured with self-tapping Ø3.5 mm screws along preplanned trajectories. When additional canal enlargement was required (e.g., severe stenosis), a semi-open-door laminoplasty was performed on the contralateral side, and a semi–open-door plate (outer Ø3.5 mm and inner Ø2.0 mm screw options) was applied to maintain the opened lamina. Component integration relied on coordinated plate geometry and screw trajectories rather than cross-linking. Throughout the workflow, soft-tissue preservation and protection of the C5 root corridor and vertebral artery groove were emphasized. This description reflects a conceptual technique used to inform feasibility assessment and does not constitute a prescriptive surgical protocol.

Virtual simulation

Implantation was simulated in Mimics on the reconstructed C2-C7 models to assess anatomical compatibility of each component (fit on bony footprints, screw corridor containment, and avoidance of simulated cortical breach). Device sizes (plate lengths; interfacet device thickness 2/3/4 mm and AP/ML options) and the nominal 30°−40° insertion angle were iteratively adjusted to match level-specific morphometry obtained in Sect. 1.1–1.2. The simulation was used solely to evaluate geometric feasibility and to refine size ranges and trajectories; no biomechanical or clinical performance was inferred from this step.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (version 21.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Normality was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Paired-sample t tests were used to compare left-right side differences, and independent-sample t tests were applied for sex-related differences. Inter-observer reliability between the two spine surgeons was assessed using the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) with a two-way mixed-effects model for absolute agreement. All tests were two-tailed, with a significance threshold of α = 0.05. Given the modest sample size, subgroup comparisons were considered exploratory and interpreted with caution. ICCs were interpreted as poor (< 0.50), moderate (0.50–0.75), good (0.75–0.90), and excellent (> 0.90). Inter-observer reliability between the two spine surgeons for all anatomical measurements was assessed using the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) with a two-way mixed-effects model for absolute agreement.

Results

Measurement results of C2-C7 lateral mass anatomical parameters

Summary statistics for C2-C7 are presented in Tables 1 and 2 (mean ± SD). Data distributions met the assumptions of normality (Shapiro-Wilk test) and homogeneity of variance (Levene’s test). Independent-samples t tests showed no sex-related differences across levels (all p > 0.05); data from males and females were therefore pooled. Paired-samples t tests demonstrated no left–right side differences (all p > 0.05); measurements were consequently combined across sides for subsequent analyses. Thus, the descriptive statistics presented in Tables 1 and 2 are derived from the analysis of 30 individuals, with the absence of significant side or sex differences justifying the reporting of pooled data. The inter-observer reliability for the key anatomical measurements ranged from moderate to excellent, as detailed in Table 3.

Simulation of implantation in mimics software

Implantation of the integrated system was simulated on the reconstructed C2-C7 models in Mimics (Fig. 3). Component positioning-lateral-mass screws, spinous-process screws, interfacet expansion-and-fusion device, lateral-mass plate, and semi-open-door plate-was achieved along preplanned corridors without simulated cortical breach. Device dimensions and orientations were derived directly from the morphometry: for example, lateral-mass height (11.54–13.28 mm) informed plate profile and screw trajectory; interfacet gap height (1.35–1.43 mm) guided interfacet device thickness options (2/3/4 mm); and lamina safety length (25.26–28.64 mm) together with minimum lamina height (10.52–14.75 mm) supported selection and angulation of the laminoplasty plate and spinous-process screws. These geometry-based simulations demonstrated the anatomical compatibility of the proposed construct within the virtual environment, without inference regarding biomechanical performance or clinical outcomes.

Discussion

To address these challenges, we developed an integrated posterior construct, designed to enable single-stage posterior decompression of both the spinal canal and intervertebral foramina while simultaneously providing construct stability. In this study, CT-based morphometry and Mimics-guided simulation demonstrated the system’s dimensional compatibility and anatomical feasibility, establishing a structural basis for further biomechanical and translational validation. From a fixation perspective, the choice of anchor points remains central to posterior cervical instrumentation. Comparative studies have shown that pedicle screws provide superior pull-out strength but carry higher risks of neurovascular injury compared with lateral-mass screws [14]. Spinous-process screw fixation has been described as an alternative salvage technique in subaxial cervical instrumentation, offering additional stability in selected cases [15]. Likewise, laminoplasty has been proposed as a motion-preserving alternative to laminectomy with fusion, with meta-analyses suggesting comparable neurological improvement but differing profiles in cervical alignment and axial symptoms [16, 17]. These findings contextualize our system, which seeks to integrate facet-based fusion with semi-open-door laminoplasty into a single posterior construct.

Design concept, innovations, and limitations

Design concept

Unlike conventional laminoplasty plates or standalone lateral mass fixation systems, our integrated construct is designed to simultaneously address canal stenosis and foraminal narrowing through a coordinated facet fusion and laminoplasty mechanism within a single-stage posterior approach. The principal innovation of this study lies in the integration of facet joint fusion with semi-open-door laminoplasty within a single, coordinated posterior construct, which, to our knowledge, has not been previously described in the literature. The concept is to couple controlled interfacet distraction (for foraminal enlargement) with semi-open-door laminoplasty (for canal expansion) using coordinated plates/screws and level-specific sizing derived from morphometry [18–20].

These features are intended to facilitate geometric compatibility and reproducible placement; no claims about biomechanical superiority or clinical efficacy are made in the present work.

Limitations and future work

This study is limited to 30 healthy adult CT datasets and software-based virtual implantation; soft-tissue interactions, surgical exposure constraints, intraoperative variability, and dynamic loading were not modeled. The cohort comprised healthy Chinese adults, which may limit generalizability across ethnicities, age ranges, and pathological conditions. Consequently, the findings demonstrate anatomical feasibility only. Feasibility of surgical approach and practical implementation of the facet joint fusion-integrated posterior cervical semi-open door laminoplasty system

The investigational system was conceived to align decompression, facet preparation, and implant fixation within a coordinated posterior workflow. “Integration” refers to device design and sequencing; intraoperative steps remain sequential. The construct couples a semi-open-door laminoplasty plate with a lateral-mass-anchored interfacet spacer that permits in-device graft packing and aims to maintain canal expansion while facilitating facet arthrodesis. This configuration is designed to streamline instrumentation compared with separate posterior fixation and laminoplasty systems, while preserving familiar posterior midline exposure. A conventional posterior midline approach is used, comprising three elements already established in clinical practice: (1) interfacet distraction with grafting at the lower cervical levels, (2) semi-open-door laminoplasty to enlarge the canal, and (3) fixation using lateral-mass and, where appropriate, spinous-process purchase. Seminal and contemporary reports support the safety and practicality of open-door laminoplasty for multilevel cervical myelopathy [23, 24], while comparative analyses indicate that laminoplasty can provide neurological improvement comparable to laminectomy with fusion but with different profiles for alignment and axial symptoms [16, 17, 24]. With respect to anchors, the trade-off between pedicle screws and lateral-mass screws is well documented-pedicle screws offer greater pull-out strength at the cost of narrower safety corridors-supporting the rationale for lateral-mass-based constructs in many subaxial applications [18]. Spinous-process screws have been described as a salvage or adjunct option that can augment stability in selected settings [19]. In aggregate, these data provide clinical context for a posterior, single-stage workflow that integrates foraminal and canal enlargement within a unified construct. In our Mimics simulations, planned screw trajectories and device footprints were contained within cortical boundaries across C2-C7, and component sizing followed level-specific morphometry (Sect. 1.1–1.2), indicating anatomical compatibility in silico. A posterior approach also avoids anterior-neck structures (esophagus, great vessels, recurrent laryngeal nerve), which are recognized sources of approach-related complications in anterior cervical surgery [25]. Nevertheless, clinical implementation will require attention to accuracy of plate/screw placement, prevention of graft migration, and mitigation of fixation failure. Future work will emphasize finite-element analysis, bench-top testing, and cadaveric implantation to optimize geometry, verify handling, and inform indications before prospective evaluation (see 3.1 Limitations and future work).

Indications and contraindications

Indications

The facet-fusion-integrated posterior semi–open-door laminoplasty system is conceptually intended for scenarios in which posterior canal enlargement and/or foraminal decompression can be achieved concomitantly with posterior stabilization. Potential indications include: (1) lower cervical instability due to degeneration or trauma when posterior anchors are appropriate; (2) multilevel cervical myelopathy with preserved or correctable sagittal alignment where a motion-preserving posterior decompression is favored; and (3) radiculopathy from foraminal stenosis where facet-based distraction and posterior foraminotomy are considered suitable [26–28]. Selection should account for established criteria for laminoplasty (multilevel stenosis, neutral/lordotic alignment, limited axial pain) and for posterior cervical foraminotomy (unilateral radiculopathy without marked segmental kyphosis or gross instability) [26–28].

Contraindications

Relative or absolute contraindications mirror those reported for posterior cervical procedures: fixed kyphosis or substantial segmental instability, severe axial neck pain predominating over myelopathic symptoms, and advanced autofusion/ankylosis that precludes safe facet distraction or adequate decompression [26, 28]. Congenital deformity or destructive pathology at the target level similarly warrants alternative strategies or staged correction.

Rationale and practical implications

This integrated construct is designed to couple interfacet distraction with in-device grafting (for foraminal enlargement and potential facet arthrodesis) and semi–open-door laminoplasty (for canal expansion) within a familiar posterior workflow, potentially simplifying instrumentation compared with separate systems. Final candidacy should be individualized using preoperative imaging to verify alignment, stability, and facet geometry, applying guideline-based decision frameworks for degenerative cervical myelopathy and radiculopathy [26–29]. Anterior-approach risks (e.g., dysphagia, recurrent laryngeal nerve injury) remain a consideration in approach selection but do not, by themselves, determine indication for this device [25].

Limitations

This study is limited to CT morphometry and software-based virtual implantation in 30 healthy Chinese adults. The modest sample size (n = 30) of healthy Chinese adults, while consistent with prior anatomical morphometry studies, limits the generalizability of our findings to other ethnic groups, age ranges, and patients with degenerative pathology. Future studies with larger, multi-ethnic cohorts are warranted. No biomechanical testing, cadaveric validation, or clinical outcomes were obtained; therefore, the translational value remains provisional. Anatomical variability-including degenerative changes, elderly bone quality, and ethnic morphometric differences-may alter screw corridors, interfacet contact, and fusion surfaces. In line with these uncertainties, broader anatomical sampling and a stepwise validation program (finite-element analysis under physiological loads, bench testing of component interfaces, cadaveric implantation to verify corridors/handling/graft containment, and prospective pilot studies) are required before clinical adoption. Recognition of variants such as os odontoideum and posterior accessory ossicles is particularly important for preoperative planning and risk mitigation.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that the facet-fusion-integrated posterior system is anatomically feasible and dimensionally compatible within a virtual environment, based on morphometric data from a healthy cohort. However, these geometric findings are provisional; they establish a structural basis but do not constitute evidence of biomechanical performance or therapeutic benefit. The translational value of this conceptual design remains to be proven through a structured validation pathway encompassing finite-element modeling, bench-top mechanical tests, cadaveric verification, and ultimately, prospective clinical studies. Until these steps are completed, the system should be considered a proof-of-concept rather than a validated clinical solution.

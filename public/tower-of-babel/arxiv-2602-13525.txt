Regularity and stability of two coupled Euler-Bernoulli equations with a localized singular structural damping
Kaïs AMMARI, Fathi HASSINE, Louis Tebou

Abstract
. This paper is concerned with the study of regularity and stability properties of two Euler–Bernoulli beam equations with localized singular damping. Under suitable regularity assumptions on the damping coefficient, we establish Gevrey regularity for the semigroup generated by the associated operator. Furthermore, for a broader class of damping mechanisms, including less regular damping, we derive uniform stability result. These findings provide a detailed description of the long-term behavior of the corresponding dynamical systems.

In 1982, G. Chen and D.L. Russell

In the same paper, they also proposed the following partial answers:

The problem to be tackled in this paper falls within the class of simultaneous control. Simultaneous control of a system of two or more equations involves controlling the system using the same control device for all the components of this system. This notion in the framework of the controllability of distributed systems was introduced by Russell in his study of the boundary controllability of Maxwell equations in rectangular domains
[
25
]

To solve that problem, he transformed it into a controllability problem for a system of two uncoupled wave equations, one having the Dirichlet boundary conditions while the other one having the Neumann boundary conditions. That simultaneous controllability result was later generalized by Lions in the first volume of his monograph on controllability to a larger class of domains, and to uncoupled plate equations,
[
19
]
. As for the simultaneous internal controllability of uncoupled wave equations (two or more equations) with different speeds of propagation, Haraux initiated that work and he established some unique continuation results leading to approximate controllability in all space dimensions,
[
8
]
. He also proved an exact controllability result in one space dimension where the control region was an arbitrary open subinterval of the interval under consideration, and he proved another one in higher space dimensions where the control region was the whole domain under consideration. The one-dimensional exact controllability result of haraux was generalized to all space dimensions in
[
27

\omega

The type of system that we shall consider in this work is similar to (
1.1

This makes the investigation of stability and regularity more challenging than in the case of global damping mechanisms. This justifies our interest in this problem.

In the sequel, we shall use the following notation: Let
\Omega\subset\mbox{\Blackboard R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2,
be a bounded domain with a sufficiently smooth boundary
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
.

Consider the damped plate system

(1.2)

\partial_{t}^{2}u+d\,\Delta^{2}u-\,\mathrm{div}(a(x)\,\nabla(\partial_{t}u+\partial_{t}v))=0\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,+\infty),

(1.3)

\partial_{t}^{2}v+c\,\Delta^{2}v-\,\mathrm{div}(a(x)\,\nabla(\partial_{t}u+\partial_{t}v))=0\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,+\infty),

(1.4)

u=\partial_{\nu}u=v=\partial_{\nu}v=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\times(0,+\infty),

(1.5)

u(x,0)=u^{0}(x),\,v(x,0)=v^{0}(x),\partial_{t}u(x,0)=u^{1}(x),\partial_{t}v(x,0)=v^{1}(x)\text{ in }\Omega,

where
c\neq d>0
are constants and
a\in L^{\infty}(\Omega),
is nonnegative in
\Omega
and positive in
\omega
, where
\omega
is an arbitrary nonempty open subset of
\Omega
.

The natural energy of the solution of
(
1.2
)
-
(
1.5
)
at time
t
is given by

E(u,v,t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(\left|\partial_{t}u(x,t)\right|^{2}+\left|\partial_{t}v(x,t)\right|^{2}+d\,\left|\Delta u(x,t)\right|^{2}+c\,\left|\Delta v(x,t)\right|^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x,\,\forall\,t\geq 0.

Simple formal calculations yield

E(u,v,t)-E(u,v,s)=-\,\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\Omega}a(x)\,\left|\nabla(\partial_{t}u(x,s)+\partial_{t}v(x,s))\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s,\forall t>s\geq 0,

and therefore, the energy is a nonincreasing function of the time variable
t
.

The questions that we would like to address in this note are the following:

•

Does the underlying semigroup decay to zero as the time variable
t
goes to infinity? If so, what is its decay rate?

•

What is the regularity of the semigroup? Given the abstract result of
[
14
]
, we know that this semigroup is analytic when the support
\omega
of the feedback control is the whole domain
\Omega
. Thanks to a recent semigroup regularity result of
[
15
]
, we know that for a single Euler-Bernoulli plate with localized structural damping, the semigroup is of Gevrey class
s
for every
s>5/2
provided the proper subset
\omega
is big enough, (a simple example is the case where
\omega
is a collar around the whole boundary of
\Omega
. It then makes sense to reformulate the regularity question as: Is the semigroup for (
1.3
)-(
1.5
) of a certain Gevrey class? If so, which one?

Before attempting to answer those questions, we want to draw the reader’s attention to the importance of different speeds of propagation. Indeed, if
c=d
, then the system is unstable; to see this, assume
c=d
and set
p=u+v
and
q=u-v
, then the functions
p
and
q
satisfy

(1.6)

\partial_{t}^{2}p+c\,\Delta^{2}p-\,2\mathrm{div}(a(x)\,\nabla(\partial_{t}p))=0\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,+\infty),

(1.7)

\partial_{t}^{2}q+c\,\Delta^{2}q=0\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,+\infty),

(1.8)

p=\partial_{\nu}p=q=\partial_{\nu}q=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\times(0,+\infty),

\displaystyle p(x,0)=u^{0}(x)+v^{0}(x),\,q(x,0)=u^{0}(x)-v^{0}(x)\text{ in }\Omega,

(1.9)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}p(x,0)=u^{1}(x)+v^{1}(x),\partial_{t}q(x,0)=u^{1}(x)-v^{1}(x)\text{ in }\Omega.

Notice that the
(p,q)-
system is uncoupled and the energy of the

system is conserved, while one can show that the energy of the
p-
system decays to zero. Thus, the
(u,v)-
system is unstable since it will vibrate continuously,
[
1
]
.

Before stating our results, we start by introducing
the energy space by
\mathcal{H}=(H_{0}^{2}(\Omega))^{2}\times(L^{2}(\Omega))^{2}
which is endowed with the usual inner product

\left\langle(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4});(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4})\right\rangle=

d\,\int_{\Omega}\Delta u_{1}(x)\Delta\overline{v}_{1}(x)\,{\rm d}x+c\,\int_{\Omega}\Delta u_{2}(x)\Delta\overline{v}_{2}(x)\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}u_{3}(x)\overline{v}_{3}(x)\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}u_{4}(x)\overline{v}_{4}(x)\,{\rm d}x.

Next, we shall define the linear unbounded operator
\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})\subset\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})=\{(u_{1},u_{2},v_{1},v_{2})\in\mathcal{H}:v_{1},v_{2}\in H_{0}^{2}(\Omega),\;d\Delta^{2}u_{1}-\mathrm{div}(a\nabla(v_{1}+v_{2}))\in L^{2}(\Omega),

\displaystyle c\Delta^{2}u_{2}-\mathrm{div}(a\nabla(v_{1}+v_{2}))\in L^{2}(\Omega)\}

and

\mathcal{A}(u_{1},u_{2},v_{1},v_{2})^{t}=(v_{1},v_{2},-d\,\Delta^{2}u_{1}+\mathrm{div}(a\nabla(v_{1}+v_{2})),-c\,\Delta^{2}u_{2}+\mathrm{div}(a\nabla(v_{1}+v_{2})))^{t}.

We also recall the following geometric condition that will be useful in the proofs of exponential stability and regularity results.

Introduce a geometric constraint (GC) on the subset

\omega
where the dissipation is effective; we proceed as in

[
20
]
, (see also
[

19
]
for a special case).

(GC).
There exist open sets
\Omega_{j}\subset\Omega

, and points

,

j=1,2,\dots,J

\Omega_{i}\cap\Omega_{j}=\emptyset

\Omega\cap{\mathcal{N}}_{\delta}\left[\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^{J}\Gamma_{j}\right)\bigcup\left(\Omega\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{J}\Omega_{j}\right)\right]\subset\omega,

for some
\delta>0

{\mathcal{N}}_{\delta}(S)=\displaystyle\bigcup_{x\in S}\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n};|x-y|<\delta\},\hbox{

\Gamma_{j}=\left\{x\in\partial\Omega_{j};(x-x_{0}^{j})\cdot\nu^{j}(x)>0\right\},

Let

Q_{1}={\mathcal{N}}_{\delta_{1}}(S)

Our main result is a regularity result for the corresponding semigroup, and it reads:

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose now that
\omega
satisfies the geometric constraint
(GC)
. Further, assume that the function

lies in

, vanishes in
\Omega\setminus\omega

(1.10)

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\exists\,M>0:|\nabla a(x)|^{4}\leq M(a(x))^{3}\text{ and }|D^{2}a(x)|^{2}\leq Ma(x),\quad\forall\,x\in\Omega,\\
\cr&\text{there exists an open set }\omega_{1}\Subset\omega,~\exists\,a_{0}>0:a(x)\geq a_{0},\quad\forall\,x\in\omega_{1}.\end{array}

where
D^{2}a(x)
denotes the Hessian matrix of

Then, the semigroup
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
is of Gevrey class

, as its resolvent satisfies the following estimate

(1.11)

\displaystyle\exists\,C_{0}>0:\,|\lambda|^{\frac{2}{5}}\left\|(i\lambda I-{\mathcal{A}})^{-1}\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{H}})}\leq C_{0},\quad\forall\,\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}\text{ with }|\lambda|\text{ large enough}.

Therefore, the semigroup
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
is infinitely differentiable at

t>0
.

Remark 1.1
.

This regularity result shows that the Gevrey class established in
[
15
]
for a single Euler-Bernoulli equation is the same for the singular system that we are dealing with. The proof of Theorem

is delicate; given that the matrix defining the simultaneous damping mechanism is singular, to guarantee the claimed regularity, one should be careful when estimating the localized energy, and also when propagating the damping effect from the damping region
\omega

\Omega

An example of a function

satisfying the constraints (

[

Now, we are going to state an exponential stability result for the semigroup. Notice that the resolvent estimate in Theorem

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose now that
\omega
satisfies the geometric constraint
(GC)
. Further, assume that the nonnegative function
a
lies in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, and satisfies:

(1.12)

\exists\,a_{0}>0:a(x)\geq a_{0},\quad\text{ for a.e. }x\in\omega.

Then, the energy of System (

(u^{0},u^{1},v^{0},v^{1})

{\mathcal{H}}

(1.13)

\displaystyle E(t)\leq C_{0}e^{-\mu t}E(0),\quad\forall\,t\geq 0.

Remark 1.2
.

The exponential decay of the energy in Theorem

should be contrasted with the polynomial decay of the energy established in the case two wave equations similarly damped, even with a smooth damping coefficient,
[
1

. Indeed, in the case of the wave equations, the localized simultaneous damping mechanism is Kelvin-Voigt type, and the corresponding semigroup is not differentiable even when this damping mechanism is globally distributed in

In the case at hand, the damping mechanism is structural or square root type, and it is known that the semigroup is analytic when the damping is globally distributed. It would be interesting to examine what happens for a system of two Euler-Bernoulli plates when the localized simultaneous structural damping is replaced by a localized simultaneous Kelvin-Voigt damping; in this regard, we refer the interested reader to the recent work
[

, where the authors proved some regularity as well as stability results for the semigroup corresponding to a single Euler-Bernoulli plate with localized Kelvin-Voigt damping.

2.
Well-posedness and strong stability

We can write (
1.2
)-(
1.5
) as the following Cauchy problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}(u(t),v(t),\partial_{t}u(t),\partial_{t}v(t))^{t}=\mathcal{A}(u(t),v(t),\partial_{t}u(t),\partial_{t}v(t))^{t},\\
(u(0),v(0),\partial_{t}u(0),\partial_{t}v(0))=(u^{0},u^{1},v^{0},v^{1}).\end{array}\right.

Theorem 2.1
.

The operator
\mathcal{A}
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup of contractions on the energy space
\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

Firstly, it is easy to see that for all
(u_{1},u_{2},v_{1},v_{2})\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
, we have

\mathrm{Re}\left\langle\mathcal{A}(u_{1},u_{2},v_{1},v_{2});(u_{1},u_{2},v_{1},v_{2})\right\rangle=-\int_{\Omega}a(x)\,|\nabla(v_{1}(x)+v_{2}(x))|^{2}\,{\rm d}x,

which show that the operator
\mathcal{A}
is dissipative.

Next, for any given
(f_{1},f_{2},g_{1},g_{2})\in\mathcal{H}
, we solve the equation
\mathcal{A}(u_{1},u_{2},v_{1},v_{2})=(f_{1},f_{2},g_{1},g_{2})
, which is recast on the following way

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}v_{1}=f_{1},\\
v_{2}=f_{2},\\
-d\Delta^{2}u_{1}+\mathrm{div}(a\nabla(f_{1}+f_{2}))=g_{1},\\
-c\Delta^{2}u_{2}+\mathrm{div}(a\nabla(f_{1}+f_{2}))=g_{2}.\end{array}\right.

It is well known that by Lax-Milgram’s theorem the system (
2.1
) admits a unique solution
u\in H_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
. Moreover by multiplying the second line of (
2.1
) by
\overline{u}
and integrating over
\Omega
and using Poincaré inequality and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we find that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

d\,\int_{\Omega}|\Delta u_{1}(x)|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+c\,\int_{\Omega}|\Delta u_{2}(x)|^{2}\,{\rm d}x

\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla f_{1}(x)|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla f_{2}(x)|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla g_{1}(x)|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}|g_{2}(x)|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\right).

It follows that for all
(u,v)\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
we have

\|(u_{1},u_{2},v_{1},v_{2})\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq C\|(f_{1},f_{2},g_{1},g_{2})\|_{\mathcal{H}}.

This implies that
0\in\rho(\mathcal{A})
and by contraction principle, we easily get
R(\lambda\mathrm{I}-\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{H}
for sufficient small
\lambda>0
. The density of the domain of
\mathcal{A}
follows from
[
23
, Theorem 1.4.6]
. Then thanks to Lumer-Phillips Theorem (see
[
23
, Theorem 1.4.3]
), the operator
\mathcal{A}
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup of contractions on the Hilbert
\mathcal{H}
.
∎

Theorem 2.2
.

The semigroup
e^{t\mathcal{A}}
is strongly stable in the energy space
\mathcal{H}
, i.e,

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\|e^{t\mathcal{A}}(u^{0},v^{0},u^{1},v^{1})^{t}\|_{\mathcal{H}}=0,\;\forall\,(u^{0},v^{0},u^{1},v^{1})\in\mathcal{H}.

Proof.

Since the resolvent of the operator
\mathcal{A}
is compact, it follows from
[
3
,
7
]
that, in order to establish the strong stability of the semigroup
(e^{t\mathcal{A}})_{t\geq 0}
, it suffices to show that

\sigma(\mathcal{A})\cap i\mathbb{R}=\varnothing.

Moreover, since
0\in\rho(\mathcal{A})
, it remains to prove that the operator
(i\mu I-\mathcal{A})
is injective for all
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
.

Let
(u_{1},u_{2},v_{1},v_{2})\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
such that

(2.2)

\mathcal{A}(u_{1},u_{2},v_{1},v_{2})^{t}=i\mu(u_{1},u_{2},v_{1},v_{2})^{t}.

Then taking the real part of the scalar product of (
2.2
) with
(u,v)
we get

\mathrm{Re}(i\mu\|(u_{1},u_{2},v_{1},v_{2})\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2})=\mathrm{Re}\left\langle\mathcal{A}(u_{1},u_{2},v_{1},v_{2}),(u_{1},u_{2},v_{1},v_{2})\right\rangle=-\int_{\Omega}a|\nabla(v_{1}+v_{2})|^{2}{\rm d}x=0.

which implies that

(2.3)

a\,\nabla(v_{1}+v_{2})=0\qquad\text{ in }\,\Omega.

Inserting (
2.3
) into (
2.2
), we obtain

(2.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mu^{2}u_{1}-d\Delta^{2}u_{1}=0&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\mu^{2}u_{2}-c\Delta^{2}u_{2}=0&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\nabla(u_{1}+u_{2})=0&\text{in }\omega\\
u_{1}=u_{2}=0,\partial_{\nu}u_{1}=\partial_{\nu}u_{2}=0&\text{on }\Gamma.\end{array}\right.

We denote by
w^{i}_{j}=\partial_{x_{j}}u_{i},i=1,2
and we derive the equations of (
2.4
), one gets

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mu^{2}w^{1}_{j}-d\Delta^{2}w^{1}_{j}=0&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\mu^{2}w^{2}_{j}-c\Delta^{2}w^{2}_{j}=0&\text{in }\Omega,\\
w^{1}_{j}+w^{2}_{j}=0&\text{in }\omega.\end{array}\right.

Which implies that

d\Delta^{2}w^{1}_{j}+c\Delta^{2}w^{2}_{j}=0\,\text{in }\omega\Longrightarrow(d-c)\Delta^{2}w^{i}_{j}=0,\,\text{in }\omega,\,i=1,2\Longrightarrow\Delta^{2}w^{i}_{j}=0\,\text{in }\omega,i=1,2.

According to the above system we have that
w^{i}_{j}=0\,\text{in }\omega,i=1,2
and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mu^{2}w^{1}_{j}-d\Delta^{2}w^{1}_{j}=0&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\mu^{2}w^{2}_{j}-c\Delta^{2}w^{2}_{j}=0&\text{in }\Omega,\\
w^{1}_{j}=w^{2}_{j}=0&\text{in }\omega.\end{array}\right.

Hence, from the unique continuation theorem we deduce that
w^{i}_{j}=0
in
\Omega,i=1,2
and therefore
u_{1},u_{2}
are constants in
\Omega
and since
{u_{i}}_{|\Gamma}={\partial_{\nu}u_{i}}_{|\Gamma}=0,i=1,2
we follow that
u_{i}\equiv 0,i=1,2
. We have thus proved that
\mathrm{Ker}(i\mu I-\mathcal{A})=\left\{0\right\}
. The proof is thus complete.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we shall prove Theorem
1.1
. The main idea of the proof is to treat each equation separately and then suitably combine the estimates to derive the claimed Gevrey regularity.
For this proof, we will rely on Taylor’s sufficient condition for Gevrey regularity,
[
26
, Theorem 4, p.155]
.
This proof will be completed as soon as we establish the following resolvent
estimate

(3.1)

\|(i\lambda{\mathcal{I}}-{\mathcal{A}})^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{H}})}=O(|\lambda|^{-\frac{2}{5}})\text{ as }|\lambda|\nearrow+\infty.

To this end, let
U\in{\mathcal{H}}
, and let
\lambda
be a real number with
|\lambda|>1
.
Since the range of
i\lambda{\mathcal{I}}-{\mathcal{A}}
is
{\mathcal{H}}
, there
exists
Z\in{D}({\mathcal{A}})
such that

(3.2)

i\lambda Z-{\mathcal{A}}Z=U.

The desired resolvent estimate will be established once we prove

(3.3)

\|Z\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq K_{0}|\lambda|^{-\frac{2}{5}}\,\|U\|_{\mathcal{H}},

where here and in the sequel,
K_{0}
is a generic positive constant that may eventually
depend on

\Omega
,
\omega
, and
a
, but never on
\lambda
.

To establish (
3.3
), first, we note that
if
Z=(u,w,v,z)
and
U=(f,h,g,\ell)
, then (
3.2
) may be recast as

\displaystyle i\lambda u-v=f

(3.4)

\displaystyle i\lambda v+d\Delta^{2}u-\text{div}(a\nabla(v+z))=g

\displaystyle i\lambda w-z=h

\displaystyle i\lambda z+c\Delta^{2}w-\text{div}(a\nabla(v+z))=\ell.

First taking the inner product with
Z

on both sides of (
3.2
), then taking the real parts, we immediately
get

(3.5)

|\sqrt{a}(\nabla v+\nabla z)|_{2}^{2}\leq\|U\|_{\mathcal{H}}\|Z\|_{\mathcal{H}}.

It follows from (
3.5
), the
first and third equations in (
3
), as well as Poincaré inequality:

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}|\sqrt{a}(\nabla u+\nabla w)|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2|\sqrt{a}(\nabla v+\nabla z)|_{2}^{2}+2|\sqrt{a}(\nabla f+\nabla h)|_{2}^{2}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq 2\|U\|_{\mathcal{H}}\|Z\|_{\mathcal{H}}+K_{0}\left(\|f\|_{2}^{2}+\|h\|_{2}^{2}\right)\leq 2\|U\|_{\mathcal{H}}\|Z\|_{\mathcal{H}}+K_{0}\|U\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

Henceforth,
|.|_{2}
stands for
\|.\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
or
\|.\|_{[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{n}}
, while
\|.\|_{s}
stands for
\|.\|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}
for every nonzero
s
in
{\mathbb{R}}
.

Methodology of the proof.
Due to the fact that the damping is localized, we will need first order or flow multipliers to propagate the effect of the damping mechanism from the damped region
\omega
to the whole domain
\Omega
. We note that this damping propagation process is quite challenging for the following reasons:

•

the damping is unbounded and localized,

•

the matrix defining the damping is singular, making it quite tricky to combine the estimates established for
(u,v)
and
(w,z)
-systems; this is why we need the flexural rigidity constants of the two plates to be distinct;

•

our desire is to get the same Gevrey class for this singular system as for a single Euler-Bernoulli plate with localized structural damping established in
[
15
]
.

The rest of the proof will go along the following steps:

Step 1: Preliminary estimates. Here, we will focus on velocities with the ultimate goal of estimating the localized kinetic energy. The main idea is to express the localized kinetic energy using the sum of velocities, so that we can then invoke (
3.5
);

Step 2: Estimating the localized energy. Once the localized kinetic energy is estimated, we just rely on the local equipartition of energy identity and on grouping the terms involving the damping to estimate the localized potential energy in a suitable way, (the grouping enables the canceling of undesirable terms) then derive an estimate for the localized energy;

Step 3: Geometric propagation of "regularity". In this step, we shall use appropriate flow multipliers to propagate the localized "regularity" from
\omega
to the whole domain
\Omega
;

Step 4: Final estimate and completion of the proof.

STEP 1: Estimating the localized kinetic energy.
First, we shall derive several estimates for the velocities
v
and
z
. Then, we shall use those estimates to derive an estimate of the term
|\sqrt{a}(v+z)|_{2}
. Afterward, we shall estimate the localized crossed product
\displaystyle\Re\int_{\Omega}av\bar{z}\,dx
. Finally, combining those two estimates we will get the sought after estimate of the localized kinetic energy of the coupled system.

The first step in this process is to decompose each velocity into two component: a very smooth one and a less smooth one; this follows an idea introduced in the literature by Liu and Renardy
[
22
]
when investigating the analyticity of the semigroup associated with an Euler-Bernoulli thermoelastic plate. This idea has been very useful in proving the regularity of the semigroup in the following works, e.g.
[
2
,
11
,
15
,
28
]
). Set

v=v_{1}+v_{2},\text{ with }i\lambda v_{1}-\Delta v_{1}=g,\text{ and }i\lambda v_{2}=-d\Delta^{2}u+\text{div}(a\nabla(v+z))-\Delta v_{1}.

Similarly, set

z=v_{1}+z_{2},\text{ with }i\lambda z_{1}-\Delta z_{1}=\ell,\text{ and }i\lambda z_{2}=-c\Delta^{2}w+\text{div}(a\nabla(v+z))-\Delta z_{1}.

On the one hand, the energy method readily shows

\displaystyle|\lambda||v_{1}|_{2}+|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla v_{1}|_{2}\leq K_{0}||U||_{\mathcal{H}}

(3.7)

\displaystyle|\lambda||z_{1}|_{2}+|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla z_{1}|_{2}\leq K_{0}||U||_{\mathcal{H}}.

On the other hand, we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle|\lambda|||v_{2}||_{-2}\leq K_{0}|\Delta u|_{2}+||\hbox{div}(a\nabla(v+z))||_{-2}+K_{0}|v_{1}|_{2}.

Given that the function
a
lies in
C^{2}(\bar{\Omega})
, the application of Hahn-Banach theorem shows that there exists some function
\varphi
in
H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
with
||\hbox{div}(a\nabla v)||_{-2}=||\varphi||_{2}
such that

||\hbox{div}(a\nabla(v+z))||_{-2}^{2}=-\int_{\Omega}a\nabla(v+z)\cdot\nabla\bar{\varphi}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}(v+z)\nabla a\cdot\nabla\bar{\varphi}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}(v+z)a\Delta\bar{\varphi}\,dx.

It then readily follows

||\hbox{div}(a\nabla(v+z))||_{-2}\leq K_{0}(|v|_{2}+|z|_{2}).

Therefore, combining the last inequality with (
3.8
) and using (
3
), we derive

(3.9)

\displaystyle|\lambda|||v_{2}||_{-2}\leq K_{0}(||Z||_{\mathcal{H}}+|\lambda|^{-1}||U||_{\mathcal{H}}).

In a similar way, it follows

(3.10)

\displaystyle|\lambda|||z_{2}||_{-2}\leq K_{0}(||Z||_{\mathcal{H}}+|\lambda|^{-1}||U||_{\mathcal{H}}).

By interpolation, we also derive, thanks to (
3.9
) and (
3.10
):

\displaystyle||v_{2}||_{-1}\leq K_{0}||v_{2}||_{-2}^{\frac{1}{2}}(|v|_{2}+|v_{1}|_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq K_{0}|\lambda|^{-{\frac{1}{2}}}(||Z||_{\mathcal{H}}+|\lambda|^{-1}||U||_{\mathcal{H}})

(3.11)

\displaystyle||z_{2}||_{-1}\leq K_{0}||z_{2}||_{-2}^{\frac{1}{2}}(|z|_{2}+|z_{1}|_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq K_{0}|\lambda|^{-{\frac{1}{2}}}(||Z||_{\mathcal{H}}+|\lambda|^{-1}||U||_{\mathcal{H}}).

Since the damping term involves the sum of the velocities, to estimate the localized kinetic energy, we start by estimating
|\sqrt{a}(v+z)|_{2}
. Using our decomposition, we find

\displaystyle|\sqrt{a}(v+z)|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq|\sqrt{a}(v_{1}+z_{1})|_{2}+|\sqrt{a}(v_{2}+z_{2})|_{2}

(3.12)

\displaystyle\leq K_{0}|\lambda|^{-1}||U||_{\mathcal{H}}+K_{0}||\sqrt{a}(v_{2}+z_{2})||_{-2}^{1\over 3}(||\sqrt{a}(v+z)||_{1}+||\sqrt{a}(v_{1}+z_{1})||_{1})^{\frac{2}{3}}.

Thanks to Poincaré inequality and the structural constraint on the function
a
, we have

\displaystyle||\sqrt{a}(v+z)||_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\nabla a}{2\sqrt{a}}(v+z)+\sqrt{a}\nabla(v+z)\right|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq K_{0}\left(|v|_{2}^{2}+|z|_{2}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}a|\nabla(v+z)|^{2}\,dx\right)

(3.13)

\displaystyle\leq K_{0}\left(||Z||_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+||Z||_{\mathcal{H}}||U||_{\mathcal{H}}\right),\text{ by invoking \eqref{dislaw}}.

Similarly, one readily checks

(3.14)

\displaystyle||\sqrt{a}v_{1}||_{1}^{2}+||\sqrt{a}z_{1}||_{1}^{2}\leq K_{0}(|v_{1}|_{2}^{2}+|z_{1}|_{2}^{2})+K_{0}\int_{\Omega}(|\nabla v_{1}|^{2}+|\nabla z_{1}|^{2})\,dx\leq K_{0}|\lambda|^{-1}||U||_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

The application of Hahn-Banach Theorem shows that there exists a function
\psi
in
H_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
such that
||\psi||_{2}=||\sqrt{a}v_{2}||_{-2}
and

(3.15)

\displaystyle||\sqrt{a}v_{2}||_{-2}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}\sqrt{a}v_{2}\bar{\psi}\,dx\leq||v_{2}||_{-2}||\sqrt{a}\psi||_{2}\leq K_{0}|\lambda|^{-1}\left(||Z||_{\mathcal{H}}+||\lambda|^{-1}||U||_{\mathcal{H}}\right)||\sqrt{a}\psi||_{2}.

Notice that with
a
in
C^{2}(\bar{\Omega})
and the structural constraint (
1.10
) being enforced, the function
\sqrt{a}\psi
is in
H_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
. The proof of this claim is elementary and may be found in the Appendix of
[
15
, see (45) in Appendix A]
.

By Rellich inequality, we have

\displaystyle||\sqrt{a}\psi||_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\Delta(\sqrt{a}\psi)|^{2}\,dx

(3.16)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\psi(2^{-1}a^{-{1\over 2}}\Delta a-4^{-1}a^{-{3\over 2}}|\nabla a|^{2})+a^{-{1\over 2}}(\nabla a\cdot\nabla\psi)+\sqrt{a}\Delta\psi|^{2}\,dx

The application of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, Rellich inequality and (
1.10
) lead to

(3.17)

\displaystyle||\sqrt{a}\psi||_{2}\leq K_{0}|\Delta\psi|_{2}

so that (
3.15
) becomes

(3.18)

\displaystyle||\sqrt{a}v_{2}||_{-2}\leq K_{0}|\lambda|^{-1}\left(||Z||_{\mathcal{H}}+||\lambda|^{-1}||U||_{\mathcal{H}}\right).

In a similar way, one shows

(3.19)

\displaystyle||\sqrt{a}z_{2}||_{-2}\leq K_{0}|\lambda|^{-1}\left(||Z||_{\mathcal{H}}+||\lambda|^{-1}||U||_{\mathcal{H}}\right).

Gathering (
3
), (
3
), (
3.14
), (
3.18
) and (
3.19
), we derive

\displaystyle|\sqrt{a}(v+z)|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq K_{0}|\lambda|^{-1}||U||_{\mathcal{H}}

(3.20)

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38092pt+K_{0}|\lambda|^{-{1\over 3}}\left(||Z||_{\mathcal{H}}+||\lambda|^{-1}||U||_{\mathcal{H}}\right)^{1\over 3}\left(||Z||_{\mathcal{H}}+||Z||_{\mathcal{H}}^{1\over 2}||U||_{\mathcal{H}}^{1\over 2}+|\lambda|^{-{1\over 2}}||U||_{\mathcal{H}}\right)^{2\over 3}.

The last inequality simplifies to

(3.21)

\displaystyle|\sqrt{a}(v+z)|_{2}\leq K_{0}|\lambda|^{-{1\over 3}}\left(||Z||_{\mathcal{H}}+||Z||_{\mathcal{H}}^{2\over 3}||U||_{\mathcal{H}}^{1\over 3}+|\lambda|^{-{1\over 3}}||Z||_{\mathcal{H}}^{1\over 3}||U||_{\mathcal{H}}^{2\over 3}+|\lambda|^{-{2\over 3}}||U||_{\mathcal{H}}\right).

At this stage, we draw the reader’s attention to the identity

(3.22)

|\sqrt{a}v|_{2}^{2}+|\sqrt{a}z|_{2}^{2}=|\sqrt{a}(v+z)|_{2}^{2}-2\Re\int_{\Omega}{a}v\bar{z}\,dx.

To complete this step, it remains to estimate the last integral term. To do so, we shall use Green’s formula. Multiply the second equation in (
3
) by
c\bar{z})
and the conjugate of its last equation by
dv
, then apply Green’s formula over
\Omega
to get the two identities

\displaystyle c\int_{\Omega}av\bar{z}\,dx

\displaystyle=-\frac{cd}{i\lambda}\int_{\Omega}\Delta u(a\Delta\bar{z}+2\nabla a\cdot\nabla\bar{z}+\bar{z}\Delta a)\,dx

(3.23)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.45377pt+\frac{1}{i\lambda}\int_{\Omega}cag\bar{z}\,dx-\frac{c}{i\lambda}\int_{\Omega}a\nabla(v+z)\cdot(a\nabla\bar{z}+\bar{z}\nabla a)\,dx

\displaystyle-d\int_{\Omega}a\bar{z}v\,dx

\displaystyle=-\frac{cd}{i\lambda}\int_{\Omega}\Delta\bar{w}(a\Delta v+2\nabla a\cdot\nabla v+v\Delta a)\,dx

(3.24)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.45377pt+\frac{1}{i\lambda}\int_{\Omega}da\bar{\ell}u\,dx-\frac{d}{i\lambda}\int_{\Omega}a\nabla(\bar{v}+\bar{z})\cdot(a\nabla v+v\nabla a)\,dx

Adding (
3.24
) to (
3.23
), using the first and third equations in (
3
), and taking real parts, one derives

\displaystyle\Re\int_{\Omega}av\bar{z}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c-d}\Bigg[\Re\frac{1}{i\lambda}\int_{\Omega}a\Big[cd(\Delta u\Delta\bar{h}-\Delta\bar{w}\Delta f)+cg\bar{z}+d\ell v\Big]\,dx

(3.25)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.45377pt-\Re\frac{cd}{i\lambda}\int_{\Omega}\Delta\bar{w}(2\nabla a\cdot\nabla v+v\Delta a)+\Delta u(2\nabla a\cdot\nabla\bar{z}+\bar{z}\Delta a)\,dx

\displaystyle\hskip 14.45377pt-\Re\frac{1}{i\lambda}\int_{\Omega}a\Big[c\nabla(v+z)\cdot(a\nabla\bar{z}+\bar{z}\nabla a)+d\nabla(\bar{v}+\bar{z})\cdot(a\nabla v+v\nabla a)\Big]\,dx.\Bigg]

The application of Cauchy-Schwarz inequality leads to the following estimates

\displaystyle\left|\Re\frac{1}{i\lambda}\int_{\Omega}a\Big[cd(\Delta u\Delta\bar{h}-\Delta\bar{w}\Delta f)+cg\bar{z}+d\ell v\Big]\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq K_{0}|\lambda|^{-1}(|\Delta u|_{2}|\Delta h|_{2}+|\Delta w|_{2}|\Delta f|_{2})

\displaystyle\hskip 14.45377pt+K_{0}|\lambda|^{-1}(|g|_{2}|z|_{2}+|v|_{2}|\ell|_{2})

(3.26)

\displaystyle\leq K_{0}|\lambda|^{-1}||Z||_{{\mathcal{H}}}||U||_{{\mathcal{H}}}.

and

\displaystyle\left|\Re\frac{cd}{i\lambda}\int_{\Omega}\Delta\bar{w}(2\nabla a\cdot\nabla v+v\Delta a)+\Delta u(2\nabla a\cdot\nabla\bar{z}+\bar{z}\Delta a)\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq K_{0}|\lambda|^{-1}(|\Delta w|_{2}(|\nabla v|_{2}+|v|_{2})+|\Delta u|_{2}(|\nabla z|_{2}+|z|_{2}))

(3.27)

\displaystyle\leq K_{0}|\lambda|^{-1}||Z||_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}+K_{0}|\lambda|^{-1}||Z||_{{\mathcal{H}}}(|v|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\Delta v|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}+|z|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\Delta z|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}),

where in the last inequality, we have invoked the elementary interpolation inequality:
|\nabla q|_{2}\leq C|q|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\Delta q|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}
for every
q
in
H_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
.

Using the first and third equations in (
3
), one readily derives

(3.28)

|\Delta v|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}+|\Delta z|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq K_{0}(|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}||Z||_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+||U||_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}).

The combination of (
3
) and (
3.28
) yields

\displaystyle\left|\Re\frac{cd}{i\lambda}\int_{\Omega}\Delta\bar{w}(2\nabla a\cdot\nabla v+v\Delta a)+\Delta u(2\nabla a\cdot\nabla\bar{z}+\bar{z}\Delta a)\,dx\right|

(3.29)

\displaystyle\leq K_{0}\left(|\lambda|^{-\frac{1}{2}}||Z||_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}+|\lambda|^{-1}||Z||_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{\frac{3}{2}}||U||^{\frac{1}{2}}\right).

Similarly, one derives

\displaystyle\left|\Re\frac{1}{i\lambda}\int_{\Omega}a\Big[c\nabla(v+z)\cdot(a\nabla\bar{z}+\bar{z}\nabla a)+d\nabla(\bar{v}+\bar{z})\cdot(a\nabla v+v\nabla a)\Big]\,dx\right|

(3.30)

\displaystyle\leq K_{0}|\lambda|^{-1}||Z||_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}||U||_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(||Z||_{{\mathcal{H}}}+|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}||Z||_{{\mathcal{H}}}+||Z||_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}||U||_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq K_{0}\left(|\lambda|^{-\frac{1}{2}}||U||_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}||Z||_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{\frac{3}{2}}+|\lambda|^{-1}||Z||_{{\mathcal{H}}}||U||_{{\mathcal{H}}}\right).

Gathering (
3
), (
3
), (
3
) and (
3
), we find

(3.31)

\displaystyle\left|\Re\int_{\Omega}av\bar{z}\,dx\right|\leq K_{0}\left(|\lambda|^{-\frac{1}{2}}||Z||_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}+|\lambda|^{-\frac{1}{2}}||U||_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}||Z||_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{\frac{3}{2}}+|\lambda|^{-1}||Z||_{{\mathcal{H}}}||U||_{{\mathcal{H}}}\right).

Finally, the combination of (
3.21
), (
3.22
) and (
3.31
) leads to the following estimate for the localized kinetic energy

\displaystyle|\sqrt{a}v|_{2}^{2}+|\sqrt{a}z|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq K_{0}|\lambda|^{-{2\over 3}}\left(||Z||_{\mathcal{H}}^{4\over 3}||U||_{\mathcal{H}}^{2\over 3}+|\lambda|^{-{2\over 3}}||Z||_{\mathcal{H}}^{2\over 3}||U||_{\mathcal{H}}^{4\over 3}+|\lambda|^{-{4\over 3}}||U||_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right)

(3.32)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.45377pt+K_{0}\left(|\lambda|^{-\frac{1}{2}}||Z||_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}+|\lambda|^{-\frac{1}{2}}||U||_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}||Z||_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{\frac{3}{2}}+|\lambda|^{-1}||Z||_{{\mathcal{H}}}||U||_{{\mathcal{H}}}\right).

The above estimate is "optimal" in the sense that there is no "loss" of analyticity; the latter is totally transferred from the "parabolic" components
v_{1}
and
z_{1}
.
It gives perfect scaling,
[
15
]
:

\displaystyle|a^{1/2}v|_{2}+|a^{1/2}z|_{2}

\displaystyle\sim||\lambda^{-1}U||_{{\mathcal{H}}}+|||\lambda|^{-1/4}Z||_{{\mathcal{H}}}+||Z||^{2/3}_{{\mathcal{H}}}||\lambda^{-1}U||^{1/3}_{{\mathcal{H}}}+||\lambda^{-1}U||^{2/3}_{{\mathcal{H}}}||Z||^{1/3}_{{\mathcal{H}}}

(3.33)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.45377pt+||Z||^{3/4}_{{\mathcal{H}}}||\lambda^{-1}U||^{1/4}_{{\mathcal{H}}}+||\lambda^{-1}U||^{1/2}_{{\mathcal{H}}}||Z||^{1/2}_{{\mathcal{H}}}.

STEP 2: Estimation of localized potential energy.
First, we are going to use the equipartition of localized energy to estimate the localized potential energy of the
(u,v)-
system. The estimate of the localized potential energy of the
(w,z)-
system is established similarly; so the details will be omitted. Then, we will combine the two estimates to derive an estimate of the localized potential energy of the coupled system. Finally, we shall gather the estimates of localized kinetic and potential energies to derive an estimate for the localized energy of the coupled system.

Multiply the second equation in (
3
) by
a\bar{v}
, use Green’s formula and take real parts to derive

\displaystyle d\int_{\Omega}a|\Delta u|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}a|v|^{2}\,dx+\Re\frac{d}{i\lambda}\int_{\Omega}\Delta u(2\nabla a\cdot\nabla\bar{v}+\bar{v}\Delta a-a\Delta\bar{f})\,dx

(3.34)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.45377pt\Re\frac{1}{i\lambda}\int_{\Omega}a\nabla(v+z)\cdot(a\nabla\bar{v}+\bar{v}\nabla a)\,dx-\Re\frac{1}{i\lambda}\int_{\Omega}ag\bar{v}\,dx.

Proceeding similarly, we find

\displaystyle c\int_{\Omega}a|\Delta w|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}a|z|^{2}\,dx+\Re\frac{c}{i\lambda}\int_{\Omega}\Delta w(2\nabla a\cdot\nabla\bar{z}+\bar{z}\Delta a-a\Delta\bar{h})\,dx

(3.35)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.45377pt\Re\frac{1}{i\lambda}\int_{\Omega}a\nabla(v+z)\cdot(a\nabla\bar{z}+\bar{z}\nabla a)\,dx-\Re\frac{1}{i\lambda}\int_{\Omega}a\ell\bar{z}\,dx.

Adding (
3
) to (
3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a(d|\Delta u|^{2}+c|\Delta w|^{2})\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}a(|v|^{2}+|z|^{2})\,dx+\Re\frac{d}{i\lambda}\int_{\Omega}\Delta u(2\nabla a\cdot\nabla\bar{v}+\bar{v}\Delta a-a\Delta\bar{f})\,dx

(3.36)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.45377pt\Re\frac{1}{i\lambda}\int_{\Omega}a\nabla(v+z)\cdot(\bar{v}\nabla a)\,dx-\Re\frac{1}{i\lambda}\int_{\Omega}ag\bar{v}\,dx

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38092pt+\Re\frac{c}{i\lambda}\int_{\Omega}\Delta w(2\nabla a\cdot\nabla\bar{z}+\bar{z}\Delta a-a\Delta\bar{h})\,dx

\displaystyle\hskip 14.45377pt\Re\frac{1}{i\lambda}\int_{\Omega}a\nabla(v+z)\cdot(\bar{z}\nabla a)\,dx-\Re\frac{1}{i\lambda}\int_{\Omega}a\ell\bar{z}\,dx,

where the imaginary term involving
|\nabla(v+z)|^{2}
disappears.

Now, we are going to estimate the crossed products coming from the
(u,v)
-system; similar estimates will hold for the corresponding terms coming from the
(w,z)
-system.

Thanks to the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the dissipation law, one readily gets the following estimates

\displaystyle\left|\Re\frac{1}{i\lambda}\int_{\Omega}d\Delta u(\bar{v}\Delta a-a\Delta\bar{f})-g\bar{v}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq K_{0}|\lambda|^{-1}(|\Delta u|_{2}(|v|_{2}+|\Delta f|_{2})+|g|_{2}|v|_{2})

(3.37)

\displaystyle\leq K_{0}|\lambda|^{-1}(||Z||_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}+||Z||_{{\mathcal{H}}}||U||_{{\mathcal{H}}})

\displaystyle\left|\Re\frac{1}{i\lambda}\int_{\Omega}a\nabla(v+z)\cdot(\bar{v}\nabla a)\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq K_{0}|\lambda|^{-1}||U||_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}||Z||_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{\frac{3}{2}}.

Similarly, one derives, (by using the interpolation inequality:
|\nabla v|_{2}\leq K_{0}|v|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\Delta v|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}
and the first equation in (
3
)):

\displaystyle\left|\Re\frac{d}{i\lambda}\int_{\Omega}\Delta u(2\nabla a\cdot\nabla\bar{v})\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq K_{0}|\lambda|^{-1}|\Delta u|_{2}|v|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}(|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}|\Delta u|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}+|\Delta f|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}})

(3.38)

\displaystyle\leq K_{0}(|\lambda|^{-\frac{1}{2}}||Z||_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}+|\lambda|^{-1}||U||_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}||Z||_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{\frac{3}{2}}),

as well as

\displaystyle\left|\Re\frac{1}{i\lambda}\int_{\Omega}c\Delta w(\bar{z}\Delta a-a\Delta\bar{h})-\ell\bar{z}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq K_{0}|\lambda|^{-1}(|\Delta w|_{2}(|z|_{2}+|\Delta h|_{2})+|\ell|_{2}|z|_{2})

(3.39)

\displaystyle\leq K_{0}|\lambda|^{-1}(||Z||_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}+||Z||_{{\mathcal{H}}}||U||_{{\mathcal{H}}})

\displaystyle\left|\Re\frac{1}{i\lambda}\int_{\Omega}a\nabla(v+z)\cdot(\bar{z}\nabla a)\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq K_{0}|\lambda|^{-1}||U||_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}||Z||_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{\frac{3}{2}}.

and

\displaystyle\left|\Re\frac{c}{i\lambda}\int_{\Omega}\Delta w(2\nabla a\cdot\nabla\bar{z})\,dx\right|

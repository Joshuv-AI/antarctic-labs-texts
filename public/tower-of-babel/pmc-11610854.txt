A retrospective observational study of orbital cellulitis in children - Recent trends
Authors: Santosh Kumar Mishra, Sunil Kumar, Arti Kumari, Nageshwar Sharma
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTPurpose:Orbital cellulitis is an acute post-septal orbital infection caused mostly by bacteria. This may result in serious complications including blindness, meningitis, cavernous sinus thrombosis, brain abscess, and even death. The delay in treatment increases morbidity and mortality. The purpose of present study was to review cases of orbital cellulitis in recent years regarding its clinical presentation, diagnostic evaluation, treatment policy, complications, and disabilities. The outcome of study will help in early diagnosis and better management of orbital cellulitis cases.Materials and Method:Total 20 cases of orbital cellulitis in children from March 2021 to February 2024 were evaluated and analyzed in department of ophthalmology, Patna Medical College and Hospital, Patna.Result:Total 20 children with ages <18 years were evaluated. The average age of presentation was 7 years (range between 1 year and 17 years). 70% of cases were seen in male children. All patients presented with chemosis, congestion, eyelid swelling, proptosis, and restriction of eye movement. Orbital CT scan diagnosed 100% cases of orbital cellulitis (P < 0.01). In 45% of children, sinusitis was initial site of infection (P < 0.01). Swab culture was positive in 40% of the cases, organism being gram-positive cocci. Complete recovery was seen in 75% with 25% having some deficit in visual acuity or eye movement.Conclusion:Clinical symptoms and signs aided with orbital CT scan help in early diagnosis early detection and aggressive treatment with multidisciplinary approach may limit the complications of orbital cellulitis and also aid in complete recovery without any visual deficit.

Introduction

Orbital cellulitis is an acute post-septal orbital infection usually caused by bacteria. It may result in serious complications including blindness, meningitis, cavernous sinus thrombosis, subdural empyema, and even brain abscess. This condition can be diagnosed by its clinical presentation as conjunctival chemosis, ophthalmoplegia, pain with ocular movements, and proptosis. Orbital CT scan and MRI aid in diagnosis. It affects all age groups but is seen more commonly in children. Orbital cellulitis has an acute onset with rapid progression. Early diagnosis and timely institution of treatment will prevent complications and disabilities.

The aim of present study was to review cases of orbital cellulitis, its clinical presentation, diagnostic evaluation, treatment policy, complications, and disabilities in recent years. The study on recent trends of orbital cellulitis will help in early treatment with faster recovery without any complications.

Materials and Method

This was a retrospective observational study conducted in department of ophthalmology Patna Medical College and Hospital Patna. All cases of orbital cellulitis were selected, presenting between March 2021 and February 2024. Case selection was done considering inclusion and exclusion criteria. Patient consent was taken, and institutional research review committee permission was taken. Approved from college ethical committee in January 2021.

Inclusion criteria

Orbital cellulitis in children <18 years of age confirmed clinically and/or radiologically.

Exclusion criteria

Orbital cellulitis in adult.Orbital cellulitis secondary to malignancy or fungal infection.Orbital cellulitis in immunocompromised.

Total number of cases of orbital cellulitis from March 2021 to February 2024 was 28.

20 cases of orbital cellulitis were seen in children, 6 cases in adults, 1 in immunocompromised, and 1 case in malignancy.

All 20 cases were evaluated with respect to age, gender, clinical symptoms, visual acuity at time of admission, eye movement, hospital stay duration, mean duration of antibiotic treatment, positivity of swab culture, initial infection site, final recovery, and the interval between the onset of symptoms, and availing healthcare service. Results were interpreted and statistically analyzed.

Discussion

Orbital cellulitis is an ophthalmic emergency. 70% cases of orbital cellulitis were present in children which is in accordance with study done by Mukund et al.[1] in 2019. Male child was affected in 70% of cases. This gender preponderance was supported by studies by Nageswaran et al.[2] and Sobol et al.[3] Chemosis, congestion, eyelid swelling, proptosis, and restriction of eye movement were present in all cases, which is significant (P < 0.01). Similar findings were present in study by Mukund et al.,[1] Chaudhary et al.,[4] Murphy et al.,[5] Alam et al.[6]

Fever was found in only 25%. Treatment must not be delayed owing to absence of fever or another systemic sign. Orbital CT scan diagnosed cellulitis in all 16 cases out of total 20 cases presented for scanning which is significant (P < 0.01) supported by Basraoui et al.[7] It also diagnoses orbital abscess and subperiosteal abscess when present. Gram-positive cocci (staphylococcus and streptococcus) were the most commonly isolated organisms in swab culture.[48] The most common initial site of infection was sinuses (ethmoid and maxillary) as seen in study by Tzelnick et al.[9] in 2019. The other source of infection was trauma and dental infection.

The commonest antibiotic regime in our study was injection ceftriaxone, infusion metronidazole, and injection amikacin. The dose was adjusted according to the body weight of the child. In some patients, regime was modified according to swab culture report, which warranted the use of vancomycin for MRSA (Methicillin resistant Staphylococcus aureus) organism. Abscess was drained in 4 patients.[45] Multidisciplinary approach by ophthalmologist and otolaryngologist was required for early diagnosis and treatment especially cases caused by sinusitis.

Conclusion

Clinical symptoms and signs aided with orbital CT scan help in early diagnosis. A multidisciplinary approach enhances the recovery of patients with orbital cellulitis, limiting its complications.

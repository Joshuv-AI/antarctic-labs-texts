Abstract
. 1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a metric space, and let
r>0
.
The
Vietoris–Rips simplicial complex
\mathrm{VR}(X;r)
has
X
as its vertex set, and a finite subset
\sigma\subseteq X
as a simplex when its diameter is less than
r
.
Vietoris–Rips complexes were invented by Vietoris in a cohomology theory for metric spaces
[
22
,
19
]
.
Independently, they were introduced by Rips in geometric group theory as a natural way to thicken a space.
Indeed, Rips used these complexes to show that torsion-free hyperbolic groups have Eilenberg–MacLane spaces that are finite simplicial complexes
[
8
]
.

More recently, Vietoris–Rips complexes and persistent homology have become commonly used tools in applied and computational topology, motivated by applications to
data analysis
[
9
,
12
]
.
For
M
a manifold and scale
r
sufficiently small depending on the curvature of
M
, Hausmann
[
15
]
proves there is a homotopy equivalence
\mathrm{VR}(M;r)\simeq M
.
Furthermore, for dataset
X
sufficiently close to
M
in the Gromov–Hausdorff distance, Latschev
[
18
]
proves there is a homotopy equivalence
\mathrm{VR}(X;r)\simeq M
.

If
X
is not a discrete metric space, then the inclusion
X\hookrightarrow\mathrm{VR}(X;r)
is not continuous, since the vertex set of a simplicial complex is equipped with the discrete topology.
For example, if
M
is a manifold of dimension at least one, then
M\hookrightarrow\mathrm{VR}(M;r)
is not continuous.
This observation helped motivate the definition of Vietoris–Rips metric thickenings in
[
1
]
.
For
X
a metric space and
r>0
, the
Vietoris–Rips metric thickening
\mathrm{VR}^{\mathrm{m}}(X;r)
is the space of finitely-supported probability measures on
X
whose support has diameter less than
r
, equipped with an optimal transport metric.
The inclusion
X\hookrightarrow\mathrm{VR}^{\mathrm{m}}(X;r)
, obtained by mapping a point
x\in X
to the Dirac delta measure
\delta_{x}
, is now an isometric embedding.

What is the relationship between Vietoris–Rips simplicial complexes and metric thickenings?
For
X
a finite metric space,
\mathrm{VR}(X;r)
and
\mathrm{VR}^{\mathrm{m}}(X;r)
are homeomorphic
[
1
]
.
For
X
an arbitrary metric space, Gillespie showed that the natural bijection
\mathrm{VR}(X;r)\to\mathrm{VR}^{\mathrm{m}}(X;r)
is a weak homotopy equivalence, i.e., induces isomorphisms on all homotopy groups
[
13
]
, improving upon
[
3
]
.
The question remains if
\mathrm{VR}(X;r)
and
\mathrm{VR}^{\mathrm{m}}(X;r)
are homotopy equivalent.
Since
\mathrm{VR}(X;r)
is a simplicial complex (and hence a CW complex), this result would be implied by Whitehead’s theorem if
\mathrm{VR}^{\mathrm{m}}(X;r)
were known to be an absolute neighborhood retract, but to the best of our knowledge this question remains unknown for general metric spaces
X
.

We prove that if
X
is a finite-dimensional Polish metric space and
r>0
, then

•

\mathrm{VR}^{\mathrm{m}}(X;r)
is an absolute neighborhood retract, and hence

•

the natural bijection
\mathrm{VR}(X;r)\to\mathrm{VR}^{\mathrm{m}}(X;r)
from the (open) Vietoris–Rips complex to the Vietoris–Rips metric thickening is a homotopy equivalence.

This occurs, for example, if
X=M
is a Riemannian manifold.

As a result, we get a version of Latschev’s theorem for metric thickenings, resolving
[
2
, Conjecture 6.9]
in the affirmative; see Corollary
3.5
.

More generally, for
\mathcal{W}
a uniformly bounded cover of a Polish metric space, we show that the natural bijection
\mathrm{V}(\mathcal{W})\to\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})
from the Vietoris complex to the Vietoris metric thickening is a homotopy equivalence; see Theorem
3.1
.
This also implies that the map
\mathrm{\check{C}}(X;r)\to\mathrm{\check{C}}^{\mathrm{m}}(X;r)
from the (open) Čech complex to the Čech metric thickening is a homotopy equivalence.

Furthermore, if
X
is a compact metrizable space, then we show that
\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})
is strongly locally contractible, using Michael’s selection theorem
[
20
]
.

We begin in Section
2
with background and definitions.
In Section
3
we prove our main result, Theorem
3.1
.
We prove that
\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})
is strongly locally contractible for
X
a compact metrizable space in Section
4
.
In Section
5
we conclude with a list of open questions.

2.
Preliminaries

We provide some background on metric spaces, Vietoris–Rips and Čech simplicial complexes, Vietoris simplicial complexes, spaces of measures, metric thickenings, absolute neighborhood retracts, Whitehead’s theorem, dimension, and local contractibility.

2.1.
Metric spaces

Let
X=(X,d)
be a metric space.
The
diameter
of a subset
A\subseteq X
is defined as
\mathrm{diam}(A)=\sup\{d(a,a^{\prime})~|~a,a^{\prime}\in A\}
.
The
open ball
of radius
r>0
centered at
x\in X
is
B(x;r)=\{y\in X~|~d(y,x)<r\}
, which we sometimes denote as
B_{X}(x;r)
.

2.2.
Vietoris–Rips and Čech simplicial complexes

Let
X
be a metric space and let
r>0
.

The
Vietoris–Rips simplicial complex
\mathrm{VR}(X;r)
has
X
as its vertex set, and a finite subset
\sigma\subseteq X
as a simplex when
\mathrm{diam}(\sigma)<r
.

The
intrinsic Čech simplicial complex

\mathrm{\check{C}}(X;r)
has
X
as its vertex set, and a finite subset
\sigma\subseteq X
as a simplex when
\cap_{x\in\sigma}B(x;r)\neq\emptyset
.

If
Z\supseteq X
is a metric space extending the metric on
X
, then the
ambient Čech simplicial complex

\mathrm{\check{C}}(X,Z;r)
has
X
as its vertex set, and a finite subset
\sigma\subseteq X
as a simplex when
\cap_{x\in\sigma}B_{Z}(x;r)\neq\emptyset
.

A common example of an ambient Čech complex is when
X\subseteq Z=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
When
Z=X
, we note that the ambient Čech complex recovers the intrinsic Čech complex, namely
\mathrm{\check{C}}(X,X;r)=\mathrm{\check{C}}(X;r)
.
For this reason, we can state our results with ambient Čech complexes while still handling the case of intrinsic Čech complexes.

We identify simplicial complexes with their geometric realizations.

2.3.
Vietoris simplicial complexes

Given a cover
\mathcal{W}
of a metric space, the
Vietoris complex

\mathrm{V}(\mathcal{W})
of
\mathcal{W}
has
X
as its vertex set, and a finite subset
\sigma\subseteq X
as a simplex when
\sigma
is contained in an element of
\mathcal{W}
.
By Dowker duality
[
10
]
,
\mathrm{V}(\mathcal{W})
is homotopy equivalent to the nerve of
\mathcal{W}
.

Let
r>0
.
When
\mathcal{W}
consists of all open subsets of diameter less than
r
, we have
\mathrm{V}(\mathcal{W})=\mathrm{VR}(X;r)
.
If instead
\mathcal{W}=\{B_{X}(x;r)\}_{x\in X}
consists of all open
r
-balls in
X
, then
\mathrm{V}(\mathcal{W})=\mathrm{\check{C}}(X;r)
.
For
Z\supseteq X
a metric space extending the metric on
X
, if
\mathcal{W}=\{X\cap B_{Z}(z;r)\}_{z\in Z}
, then
\mathrm{V}(\mathcal{W})=\mathrm{\check{C}}(X,Z;r)
.

2.4.
Spaces of measures

We follow
[
23
,
11
]
.

For
X
a topological space, we let
P(X)
be the space of probability measures on the Borel sigma algebra of
X
, equipped with the weak topology.
The weak topology on
P(X)
is induced by convergence against
C_{b}(X)
, the bounded continuous test functions.
In more detail, a sequence
\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3},\ldots
in
P(X)
is said to converge weakly to
\mu\in P(X)
if for all bounded continuous functions
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
, we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{X}f(x)\,d\mu_{n}=\int_{X}f(x)\,d\mu
.

For
k\geq 0
, let
P_{k}(X)
be the set of all measures in
P(X)
whose support have cardinality at most
k
.
So, a measure
\mu\in P_{k}(X)
can be written as
\mu=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\lambda_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}
for some
x_{i}\in X
and
\lambda_{i}\geq 0
with
\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\lambda_{i}=1
.
The
support
of a measure
\mu=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\lambda_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}\in P_{k}(X)
is
\mathrm{supp}(\mu)=\{x_{i}~|~\lambda_{i}>0\}
.

If
(X,d)
is a metric space, then we may also define optimal transport metrics on
\cup_{k\geq\infty}P_{k}(X)
, the space of all finitely supported measures.
Given finitely supported measures
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
, a
coupling
between them is a probability measure
\mu
on
X\times X
with marginals
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
.
This means
(\pi_{1})_{\sharp}\mu=\mu_{1}
and
(\pi_{2})_{\sharp}\mu=\mu_{2}
, where
\pi_{i}\colon X\times X\to X
is the projection map defined by
\pi_{i}(x_{1},x_{2})=x_{i}
for
i=1,2
, and where
\sharp
denotes the pushforward.
For
q\in[1,\infty)
we may equip
\cup_{k\geq\infty}P_{k}(X)
with the
q
-
Wasserstein-Kantorovich-Rubinstein distance
, given by

(1)

d_{W^{q}}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})=\inf_{\mu}\left(\int_{X\times X}(d(x,x^{\prime}))^{q}\,d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

where
\mu
varies over all couplings between
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
.

A
Polish space

X
is a separable completely metrizable topological space, that is, a space homeomorphic to a complete metric space that has a countable dense subset.

Let
k\geq 1
, and let
q\in[1,\infty)
.
If
X
is a bounded Polish metric space, then the
q
-Wasserstein-Kantarovoich-Rubenstein metric on
P_{k}(X)
agrees with the topology induced by weak convergence on
P_{k}(X)
by
[
23
, Corollary 6.13]
.
In turn, the topology induced by weak convergence coincides with the weak topology on
P(X)
(see, e.g.,
[
7
, Definitions 8.1.1 and 8.1.2]
and
[
16
]
).
As a consequence, different choices of
q\in[1,\infty)
all induce the same topology on
P_{k}(X)
, and we have the following observation.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a bounded Polish space.
Then the weak topology on
P_{k}(X)
coincides with the topology induced by the
q
-Wasserstein-Kantarovoich-Rubenstein metric for any
q\in[1,\infty)
.

2.5.
Metric thickenings

For
X
a metric space and
r>0
, the
Vietoris–Rips metric thickening
is the space

\mathrm{VR}^{\mathrm{m}}(X;r)=\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\lambda_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}\in P(X)~\mid~k\geq 1,\ \lambda_{i}\geq 0,\ \sum_{i}\lambda_{i}=1,\ \mathrm{diam}(\{x_{0},\ldots,x_{k-1}\})<r\right\},

equipped with the optimal transport (or
1
-Wasserstein-Kantarovoich-Rubenstein) metric.

More generally, for an open cover
\mathcal{W}
of
X
, the
metric thickening
of
\mathrm{V}(\mathcal{W})
is the space

\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})=\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\lambda_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}\in P(X)~\mid~k\geq 1,\ \lambda_{i}\geq 0,\ \sum_{i}\lambda_{i}=1,\ \exists W\in\mathcal{W}:\{x_{0},\ldots,x_{k-1}\}\subseteq W\right\},

equipped with the optimal transport metric.
Choosing
\mathcal{W}
as in Section
2.3
gives the
Čech metric thickenings

\mathrm{\check{C}}^{\mathrm{m}}(X;r)
and
\mathrm{\check{C}}^{\mathrm{m}}(X,Z;r)
.

The natural bijection
\mathrm{VR}(X;r)\to\mathrm{VR}^{\mathrm{m}}(X;r)
is the continuous map defined by sending a point
\sum\lambda_{i}x_{i}
in the geometric realization of
\mathrm{VR}(X;r)
to the probability measure
\sum\lambda_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}
in
\mathrm{VR}^{\mathrm{m}}(X;r)
.
The same is true for the natural bijection
\mathrm{V}(\mathcal{W})\to\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})
.

In Corollary
3.2
we show that for
X
a finite-dimensional Polish metric space, the natural bijection
\mathrm{VR}(X;r)\to\mathrm{VR}^{\mathrm{m}}(X;r)
is a homotopy equivalence.

\mathrm{VR}(X;r)

\leq r
instead of

.
Indeed,
\mathrm{VR}_{\leq}(X;0)
is
X
with the discrete topology, whereas

2.6.
Topological spaces

For
Y
a topological space and
y\in Y
, we let
\pi_{n}(Y,y)
denote the
n
-th homotopy group of
Y
, with basepoint
y
.

A subset
A
of a space
Y
is a
retract of
Y
if there is a map
r\colon Y\to A
with
r|_{A}=\mathrm{id}_{A}
.
This implies that
A
is closed in
Y
.
A
neighborhood retract of
Y
is a closed set in
Y

that is a retract of some neighborhood in
Y
.
A metrizable space
Y
is called an

absolute neighborhood retract (ANR)
if
Y
is a
neighborhood retract of any metrizable space that contains
Y
as a closed subspace.
See
[
21
]
for more information on ANRs.

By
[
21
, Corollary 6.6.5]
a space has the homotopy type of a simplicial complex if and only if it has the homotopy type of an ANR.

2.7.
Whitehead’s theorem

Let
Y
and
Y^{\prime}
be topological spaces.
A map
f\colon Y\to Y^{\prime}
is a
weak homotopy equivalence
if it induces

•

a bijection from the path components of
Y
to the path components of
Y^{\prime}
, and

•

an isomorphism
\pi_{n}(Y,y)\to\pi_{n}(Y^{\prime},y)
for all
n\geq 1
and
y\in Y
.

The classical version of Whitehead’s theorem states that if
Y
and
Y^{\prime}
are CW complexes, then every weak homotopy equivalence
f\colon Y\to Y^{\prime}
is a homotopy equivalence
[
14
]
.

The CW complex assumption can be relaxed, as follows.
By
[
21
, Corollary 6.6.6]
, if
Y
and
Y^{\prime}
are ANRs, then every weak homotopy equivalence
f\colon Y\to Y^{\prime}
is a homotopy equivalence.

2.8.
Dimension

We refer the reader to Chapter 5 of Sakai’s book
[
21
]
for background on dimension.

Let
Y
be a topological space, and let
\mathcal{U}
be an open cover of
Y
.
For any point
y\in Y
, let
\mathcal{U}[y]=\{U\in\mathcal{U}~|~y\in U\}
be the subcollection of sets in
\mathcal{U}
containing
y
, and let
\mathrm{card}(\mathcal{U}[y])
be the cardinality of this set.
The
order
of
\mathcal{U}
is
\mathrm{ord}(\mathcal{U})=\sup\{\mathrm{card}(\mathcal{U}[y])~|~y\in Y\}
.
We define the
(covering) dimension
\dim Y
of
Y
as follows.
Let
n
be a nonnegative integer.
If every finite open cover of
Y
has a finite open refinement
\mathcal{U}
with
\mathrm{ord}(\mathcal{U})\leq n+1
, then
\dim Y\leq n
.
(When
Y
is paracompact, the word “finite” can be removed in both places in the prior sentence, still yielding an equivalent definition.)
We have
\dim Y=n
if
\dim Y\leq n
and
\dim Y\nleq n-1
, in which case we say that the space
Y
is
n
-dimensional
.
The space
Y
is
finite-dimensional
if
\dim Y\leq n
for some finite
n
, and otherwise
Y
is
infinite-dimensional
.

The space
Y
is
countable-dimensional
if it is a countable union of finite-dimensional normal subspaces.
Though a subspace of a normal space need not be normal, a subspace of a metric space is a metric space and hence is normal.
Therefore, a metric space
X
is countable-dimensional if it is a countable union of finite-dimensional subspaces.

The countable sum theorem
[
21
, Theorem 5.4.1]
says that if the countable union
Y=\cup_{i\geq 1}F_{i}
is normal, if each
F_{i}
is closed in
Y
, and if
\dim F_{i}\leq n
for every
i\geq 1
, then
\dim Y\leq n
.

Let
X
be a metric space.
The subset theorem
[
21
, Theorem 5.3.3]
says that for every subset
A
of a metrizable space
X
, we have
\dim A\leq\dim X
.

The decomposition theorem
[
21
, Theorem 5.4.5]
states that a metric space
X
satisfies
\dim X\leq n
if and only if
X
is covered by
n+1
many
0
-dimensional subsets
X_{1}
, …,
X_{n+1}
.
It follows that a metric space
X
is countable-dimensional if and only if it is a countable union of 0-dimensional subspaces.

By
[
21
, Theorem 6.10.1]
, every countable-dimensional locally contractible metrizable space is an ANR.

2.9.
Local contractibility

A topological space
Y
is
locally contractible at
y\in Y
if for every neighborhood
U
of
y
, there is a neighborhood
y\in V\subseteq U
such that the inclusion
V\hookrightarrow U
is nullhomotopic in
U
.
The space
Y
is
locally contractible
if it is locally contractible at each
y\in Y
.

A topological space
Y
is
strongly locally contractible at
y\in Y
if for every neighborhood
U
of
y
, there is a contractible neighborhood
y\in V\subseteq U
.
In other words, if every point
y\in Y
has a local base of contractible neighborhoods, then we say that
Y
is
strongly locally contractible
.

A neighborhood
U

strongly
deformation retracts onto
y
if the deformation retract fixes the point
y
throughout the homotopy.
If every point
y\in Y
has a local base of neighborhoods which strongly deformation retract onto
y
, then we say that
Y
is
strictly strongly locally contractible
.

3.
\mathrm{V}(\mathcal{W})\to\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})
is a homotopy equivalence for
X
finite-dimensional

We prove that if
\mathcal{W}
is a uniformly bounded open cover of a finite-dimensional metric space
X
, then the Vietoris complex of
\mathcal{W}
and its metric thickening are homotopy equivalent.
A cover
\mathcal{W}
of
X
is
uniformly bounded
if there exists some
D>0
such that
\mathrm{diam}(W)<D
for all
W\in\mathcal{W}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{W}
be a uniformly bounded open cover of a finite-dimensional Polish metric space
X
.
The natural bijection
\mathrm{V}(\mathcal{W})\to\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})
from the Vietoris complex to the Vietoris metric thickening is a homotopy equivalence.

The following corollary is immediate by choosing
\mathcal{W}
to be as in Section
2.3
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
X
be a finite-dimensional Polish metric space, and let
r>0
.
The natural bijection
\mathrm{VR}(X;r)\to\mathrm{VR}^{\mathrm{m}}(X;r)
is a homotopy equivalence.
For
Z\supseteq X
a metric space extending the metric on
X
, the natural bijection
\mathrm{\check{C}}(X,Z;r)\to\mathrm{\check{C}}^{\mathrm{m}}(X,Z;r)
is a homotopy equivalence.

The proof of Theorem
3.1
will rely on showing that in this setting,
\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})
is countable-dimensional and an ANR.
The proof that
\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})
is countable-dimensional is closely related to the fact that if
X
is a finite-dimensional metric space, then for any integer
k
, the space of all probability measures with support of size at most
k
is finite-dimensional.
See for example Equation (3.1) on Page 57 of
[
11
]
,
which follows from a more general result of Basmanov
[
5
]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
\mathcal{W}
is a uniformly bounded open cover of a finite-dimensional Polish metric space
X
, then
\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})
is countable-dimensional.

Proof.

Recall

\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})=\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\lambda_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}\in P(X)~\mid~k\geq 1,\ \lambda_{i}\geq 0,\ \sum_{i}\lambda_{i}=1,\ \exists W\in\mathcal{W}:\{x_{0},\ldots,x_{k-1}\}\subseteq W\right\}.

For any integer
k\geq 0
, let

\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})_{k}=\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\lambda_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}\in P(X)~\mid~\lambda_{i}\geq 0,\ \sum_{i}\lambda_{i}=1,\ \exists W\in\mathcal{W}:\{x_{0},\ldots,x_{k-1}\}\subseteq W\right\}

be the set of all measures in
\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})
with support of size at most
k
.
Note that we can write the Vietoris metric thickening as the countable union
\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})=\cup_{k\geq 1}\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})_{k}
.
So, in order to show that
\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})
is countable-dimensional, it suffices to show that each
\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})_{k}
is finite-dimensional.

Let
Y
be a topological space.
For
k
an integer, recall that
P_{k}(Y)
is the set of measures with support of size at most
k
,

P_{k}(Y)=\{\mu\in P(Y)~|~\mathrm{card}(\mathrm{supp}(\mu))\leq k\},

equipped with the weak topology.
For
Y
a completely regular space, Equation (5.1) of
[
11
]
states
\dim(P_{k}(Y))\leq k\dim(Y)+(k-1)
; see also Basmanov
[
5
]
.

Since
X
is a metric space, it is completely regular, giving
\dim(P_{k}(X))\leq k\dim(X)+(k-1)\eqqcolon D_{k}
.
Fix a point
a\in X
and for any positive integer
i\geq 1
, let
X_{n}=\{x\in X\mid d(a,x)\leq i\}
.
Then the Wasserstein-Kantarovoich-Rubenstein metric on
P_{k}(X_{i})
agrees with the weak topology on
P_{k}(X_{i})
by Proposition
2.1
.
By the subset theorem
[
21
, Theorem 5.3.3]
, the inclusion
P_{k}(X_{i})\subseteq P(X)
gives
\dim(P_{k}(X_{i}))\leq D_{k}
for all integers
i\geq 1
.
Since
P_{k}(X)=\cup_{i\geq 1}P_{k}(X_{i})
, the countable cum theorem
[
21
, Theorem 5.4.1]
implies

4.
Strong local contractibility of
\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})

In this section we prove that for any uniformly bounded open cover
\mathcal{W}
of a compact metric space
X
, the space
\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})
is strongly locally contractible — in fact strictly strongly locally contractible.
This means, as defined in Subsection
2.9
, that each
\mu\in\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})
has a local base of neighborhoods which strongly deformation retract onto
\mu
.
As before, the cases when
\mathcal{W}
is chosen as in Section
2.3
to recover
\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})=\mathrm{VR}^{\mathrm{m}}(X;r)
or
\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})=\mathrm{\check{C}}^{\mathrm{m}}(X,Z;r)
are especially pertinent.

Let
\mathcal{W}
be a uniformly bounded open cover of a compact metrizable space
X
.
By
P(X)
we denote the set of all probability measures on
X
, and by
M(X)
we denote the space of all measures of
X
.
By the Riesz theorem,
M(X)
is isomorphic to the dual of
C(X)
, and so
M(X)
is a Banach space.
In particular, it makes sense to discuss convex subsets of
P(X)
.

4.1.
Alternative basis

Since

is compact, we have already stated (see Proposition
2.1
) that the weak topology on
\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})
coincides with the one induced by the Wasserstein metric.
In this subsection we will construct a convenient basis of this topology, consisting of sets
N(\eta,\mathcal{U},\varepsilon)
defined below.

For a measure
\eta=\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}\delta_{y_{i}}
on
X
, a continuous functions
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
, and a subset
Y
of
X
, we let

\eta(Y)=\sum_{y_{i}\in Y}p_{i}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\eta(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}f(y_{i}).

Recall that the basis of the weak topology on
P(X)
is given by the sets of the form

\widetilde{O}(\eta,h_{1},\dots,h_{k},\varepsilon)=\{\nu\in P(X)~:~|\eta(h_{i})-\nu(h_{i})|<\varepsilon\text{ for }i=1,2,\dots,k\},

where
h_{1},h_{2},\dots,h_{k}
are continuous real-valued functions on

and
\varepsilon>0
.
We define

O(\eta,h_{1},\dots,h_{k},\varepsilon)=\widetilde{O}(\eta,h_{1},\dots,h_{k},\varepsilon)\cap\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W}).

Let
\eta=\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}\delta_{y_{i}}
be a measure in
\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})

Let
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{1},U_{2},\dots,U_{n}\}

at

Lemma 4.1
.

For each

\nu=\sum_{j=1}^{m}w_{j}\delta_{a_{j}}\in N(\eta,\mathcal{U},\varepsilon)

there exists a collection of disjoint open sets
\mathcal{V}=\{V_{1},\dots,V_{m}\}

and
\delta>0

\cup\mathcal{V}
is contained in an element of
\mathcal{W}

a_{j}\in V_{j}
for all

Proof.

Let
\delta=\frac{1}{2m}\min\{\varepsilon-|\eta(U_{i})-\nu(U_{i})|:i=1,2,\dots,n\}
.
Since
\nu\in\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})
, we have that
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\}
is contained in an element of
\mathcal{W}
.
Consider disjoint open neighborhoods
V_{1},\dots,V_{m}
of

such that

whenever

\mathcal{W}

Let

Then for all

\displaystyle|\nu(U_{i})-\zeta(U_{i})|

\displaystyle\leq|\nu(U_{i})-\nu(U_{i})|+|\nu(U_{i})-\zeta(U_{i})|

Lemma 4.2
.

For each set of the form

Let

Lemma 4.3
.

The sets of the form
N(\eta,\mathcal{U},\varepsilon)
form a basis for
\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})
at
\eta
.

Proof.

Let
\eta=\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}\delta_{y_{i}}
.
Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
imply that sets of the form

are open in the weak topology.
Consider an open set
O(\nu,h_{1},\dots,h_{k},\varepsilon)

Let

M=1+\max\{|h_{t}(x)|~:~x\in X,\ t=1,2,\dots,k\},

\delta=\frac{\varepsilon}{M(2n+1)}

There exists a collection
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{1},\dots,U_{n}\}
of disjoint open neighborhoods of

such that

is contained in an element of
\mathcal{W}
, and such that for each

we have

for all

and

Pick

Then

\displaystyle|\eta(h_{t})-\nu(h_{t})|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}h_{t}(y_{i})-\sum_{j=1}^{m}w_{j}h_{t}(a_{j})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(p_{i}h_{t}(y_{i})-\sum_{a_{j}\in U_{i}}w_{j}h_{t}(a_{j})\right)\right|+\left|\sum_{a_{j}\notin\cup\mathcal{U}}w_{j}h_{t}(a_{j})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(p_{i}h_{t}(y_{i})-\sum_{a_{j}\in U_{i}}w_{j}h_{t}(y_{i})\right)\right|+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{a_{j}\in U_{i}}w_{j}|h_{t}(y_{i})-h_{t}(a_{j})|\right)+M\sum_{a_{j}\notin\cup\mathcal{U}}w_{j}

\displaystyle<M\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|p_{i}-\sum_{a_{j}\in U_{i}}w_{j}\right|+\delta+Mn\delta

\displaystyle\leq M\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\eta(U_{i})-\nu(U_{i})|+M(n+1)\delta

\displaystyle\leq Mn\delta+M(n+1)\delta=\varepsilon.

Therefore
\nu\in O(\eta,h_{1},\dots,h_{k},\varepsilon)
.
∎

4.2.
Strong local contractibility via Michael’s selection theorem for multivalued maps

Our proof that
\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})
is strictly strongly locally contractible in Theorem
4.10
will use Michael’s selection theorem
[
20
]
, which we recall now.

A multivalued map

assigns to each point

a subset
F(x)\subseteq Y
.
A
selection
of
F
is a function
f\colon X\to Y
with
f(x)\in F(x)
for all
x\in X
.

Theorem 4.4

(Michael’s selection theorem
[
20
]
)
.

Let
X
be a paracompact space, let

be a Banach space, and let

be a lower semi-continuous multivalued map with non-empty, convex, and closed values.
Then
F
has a continuous selection
f\colon X\to Y
.
Moreover, if
A
is a closed subset of
X
and
g\colon A\to Y
is a continuous selection of
F|_{A}
then
f
can be assumed to extend
g
.

Recall that a multivalued map
F\colon X\to Y
is
lower semi-continuous
if for any open subset
S
of
Y
, the set
\{x\in X\mid F(x)\cap S\neq\varnothing\}
is open in
X
.

We now build up notation necessary for the proof of Theorem
4.10
.
For any subset

of
X
, we define

\displaystyle P_{U}

\displaystyle=\{\mu\in\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})\mid\mathrm{supp}\,\mu\cap U\neq\varnothing\}

\displaystyle P^{U}

\displaystyle=\{\mu\in\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})\mid\mathrm{supp}\,\mu\subseteq U\}.

Furthermore, for a finite collection
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{1},U_{2},\dots,U_{n}\}
of subsets

, let

Propositions

,

is a compact metrizable space then
\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})
is strictly strongly locally contractible.

Proof.

As everywhere in this section, let
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}
be a measure in
\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})
.
We must show that
\mu
has a local base of neighborhoods which strongly deformation retract onto
\mu
.

Consider an open ball
B(\mu)
.
Because of Lemma
4.3
, we can find
\varepsilon>0
and a collection
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{1},U_{2},\dots,U_{n}\}
of disjoint open neighborhoods of
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}
, such that
\cup\mathcal{U}
is contained in an element of
\mathcal{W}
, and
P(\mu,\mathcal{U},\varepsilon)\subseteq B(\mu)
.
By choosing sufficiently small
\varepsilon
we can also require that
P(\mu,\mathcal{U},\varepsilon)\subseteq P_{\mathcal{U}}
.

Let
F\colon P_{\mathcal{U}}\cap B(\mu)\to P(X)
be the multivalued map, defined, as before, by

F(\zeta)=\{\nu\in P(\mu,\mathcal{U},\varepsilon)~|~\mathrm{supp}(\nu)\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\zeta)\}.

By Propositions
4.8
and
4.9
,
F
is a lower semi-continuous map with nonempty closed and convex values.
Therefore, we can apply Michael’s selection theorem
[
20
]
(see Theorem
4.4
) to obtain a continuous selection
f\colon P_{\mathcal{U}}\cap B(\mu)\to P(\mu,\mathcal{U},\varepsilon)
.
Moreover, by the relative version of the selection theorem, we may assume that
f(\mu)=\mu
.

To construct a deformation retraction of
P_{\mathcal{U}}\cap B(\mu)
to
\mu
that fixes
\mu
, we first join
\zeta
to
f(\zeta)
by a straight-line homotopy.
Indeed, note that for all
0\leq t\leq 1
we have
(1-t)\zeta+t\cdot f(\zeta)\in\mathrm{V}^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathcal{W})\cap\ \tilde{B}(\mu)=B(\mu)
since
\mathrm{supp}f(\zeta)\subseteq\mathrm{supp}\zeta
, while the continuity of this straight-line homotopy follows from
[
4
, Proposition 2.4]
or
[
1
, Lemma 3.9]
.
The image of
P_{\mathcal{U}}\cap B(\mu)
under this retraction is contained in
P(\mu,\mathcal{U},\varepsilon)
, which is convex by Proposition
4.7
.
And any convex set containing
\mu
can be strongly deformation retracted onto
\mu
, finishing the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.11
.

Let
X
be a compact metric space and let
r>0
.
Then
\mathrm{VR}^{\mathrm{m}}(X;r)
is strictly strongly locally contractible.
If
Z\supseteq X
is a metric space extending the metric on
X
, then
\mathrm{\check{C}}^{\mathrm{m}}(X,Z;r)
is strictly strongly locally contractible.

5.
Conclusion

We end with some open questions.

(1)

For
X
compact but not necessarily finite-dimensional, is
\mathrm{VR}^{\mathrm{m}}(X;r)
an ANR or an ANE?

(2)

Is it true that for
X
compact, any two sufficiently close maps into
\mathrm{VR}^{\mathrm{m}}(X;r)
are homotopic?
(This is known to be true for ANRs, and hence is a relaxation of Question (1).)

(3)

For
X
an arbitrary metric space, is
\mathrm{VR}^{\mathrm{m}}(X;r)
strongly locally contractible?

(4)

When can a map
S^{n}\to\mathrm{VR}^{\mathrm{m}}(X;r)
from the
n
-sphere be deformed (up to homotopy) to have its image contained in
\mathrm{VR}^{\mathrm{m}}(X;r)\cap P_{n+1}(X)
?

(5)

Let
X
be an ANR.
When is
\mathrm{VR}^{\mathrm{m}}(X;r)\cap P_{n+1}(X)
an ANR?

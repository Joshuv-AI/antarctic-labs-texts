Diagnostic value of unilateral adrenal vein sampling with simple and biochemical/imaging correction: a study on early screening and typing for primary aldosteronism
Authors: Yi Yan, Caie Li, Jianshu Chen, Wei Liang, Qiongying Wang, Jiazheng Li, Fang Du, Yuzhe Song, Lijuan Huang, Zhaofen Wang, Jing Yu, Peng Chang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveAdrenal vein sampling (AVS) is the gold standard for distinguishing subtypes for primary aldosteronism. However, its technical challenges and frequent intubation failures pose difficulties. Our study aimed to establish diagnostic models for unilateral primary aldosteronism (UPA) to guide appropriate treatment in early patients.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed 274 consecutive patients undergoing AVS without adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) stimulated. We established a diagnostic model corrected by biochemical/imaging, and evaluated the diagnostic value of adrenal vein aldosterone/cortisol concentration (A/C) and its ratio to inferior vena cava (AV/IVC) before and after adjusted.ResultsIn a population with a median hypertension history of 3.5 years, the area under the curve (AUC) of adrenal vein A/C index for diagnosing ipsilateral and contralateral disease was 0.829 and 0.609, respectively. AUC of AV/IVC was 0.809 and 0.754, respectively. Factors like aldosterone concentration after saline infusion test (SIT), lowest blood potassium, and the presence of consistent adrenal gland nodules on CT were associated with UPA diagnosis, which were therefore included in the diagnostic models. The biochemical/imaging-corrected model correctly classified majority of patients (AUC: 0.828 - 0.893). For diagnosing ipsilateral aldosteronism, when 95% specificity achieved, sensitivities were 60% and 64% in A/C-adjusted and AV/IVC-adjusted models, respectively. As for diagnosing contralateral disease, when 95% specificity achieved, sensitivities were 50% and 53% in A/C-adjusted and AV/IVC-adjusted models, respectively.ConclusionsFor early primary aldosteronism patients, the integration of unilaterally adjusted AVS guided by biochemical and imaging indicators into clinical practice is feasible. While it does not play a definitive role, it provides valuable insights to support treatment decisions.Supplementary informationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12902-026-02303-0.Keywords: Adrenal vein sampling, Computed tomography angiography, Endocrine, Diagnose model, Primary aldosteronism

Introduction

The development of primary aldosteronism has a prolonged process, initially manifested as normal blood pressure and renin inhibition [1], subsequently evolving into increased urine aldosterone concentration, finally stage II to III hypertension or refractory hypertension [2]. With the progression of hypertension severity, the detection rate of primary aldosteronism increases [3–5]. In refractory hypertension, defined as uncontrolled blood pressure despite using at least five antihypertensive drugs, the prevalence of primary aldosteronism exceeds 29% [6].

In addition to damaging the cardiovascular system by causing hypertension, aldosteronism directly impacts the cardiovascular system, leading to tissue inflammation and promoting cardiovascular remodeling and fibrosis [7, 8]. Compared with essential hypertension with matched blood pressure, patients with primary aldosteronism exhibit increased left ventricular hypertrophy, integrated backscatter (a marker of myocardial fibrosis), carotid intima-media thickness, and cervical-femoral pulse wave conduction velocity, resulting in arrhythmia, myocardial infarction, stroke, dementia, and increased mortality [9–11]. Therefore, although screening milder patients will increase medical costs, early diagnosis and treatment of primary aldosteronism is highly valuable [12].

Therapeutic schedule depends on its subtypes. The most meaningful classification of primary aldosteronism divides it into two main categories: surgically correctable and non-correctable subtypes [13]. Unilateral primary aldosteronism (UPA) is mainly due to aldosterone-producing adenomas (APAs), which can be cured by adrenalectomy. Identifying UPA among primary aldosteronism patients is crucial. Adrenal vein sampling (AVS) is the gold standard for distinguishing UPA from bilateral forms. However, technical challenges and intubation failures hinder its widespread use.

In this study, our main objective was to establish diagnostic models of subtypes based on the results of unilateral AVS to address the diagnostic dilemma faced when blood sampling fails on one side in patients with early primary aldosteronism. Common predictors of aldosterone hypersecretion on one side include adrenal vein aldosterone/cortisol concentration (A/C) and its ratio to the inferior vena cava (AV/IVC), to which we added biochemical and imaging indicators for adjustment, to obtain better sensitivity and specificity of the model. It is worth noting that the laterality index for the diagnosis of UPA strict follows guidelines in our study, although previous studies suggest that increasing cutoff value of laterality index will increase the area under ROC curve of the prediction models [14, 15]. Our innovation is that the modeling is based on early primary aldosteronism and is adjusted by relevant indicators. We provide explicit formulas as well as detailed sensitivity and specificity at different cutoff values for future clinical applications. Our center proactively screened primary aldosteronism, so the mean age of the patients studied, duration of hypertension and mean number of antihypertensive medications needed was substantially lower than the majority of recent papers published on AVS in primary aldosteronism. Interestingly, our model validates what was suspected at the beginning of the study design, that there are large differences in patient characteristics between early and late primary aldosteronism that make their diagnostic model characteristics also differ. As a result, the model we developed is of unique value in the historical trend of promoting health.

Methods

Patient cohorts and data collection

This retrospective study received approval from the Ethical Review Board of Lanzhou University Second Hospital. We meticulously examined the medical records of patients with primary aldosteronism who underwent AVS in the Department of Cardiology at Lanzhou University Second Hospital between January 28, 2019 and December 7, 2023. All participants were 18 years old or older. A positive screening test was defined as an aldosterone-to-renin ratio (ARR) beyond 30 (ng/dl)/(ng/ml/h) or aldosterone concentration exceeding 600 pg/ml (1 ng/dl = 10 pg/ml). The diagnosis of primary aldosteronism adhered to the guidelines of the American Endocrine Society, relying on a confirmatory saline infusion test (SIT), which was performed on almost all of patients included in this study [16]. To enhance precision, interfering drugs were withdrawn for a minimum of 4 weeks before examination (6 weeks for mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists and diuretics). Exclusions comprised patients with unsuccessful unilateral intubation, hypercortisolism, imprecise aldosterone or cortisol concentration values, and those who had undergone removal of a unilateral local adrenal.

Adrenal venous sampling and subtype identification

In patients with confirmed primary aldosteronism, adrenal glands were assessed via computed tomography angiography (CTA) to identify lesions and localize adrenal veins. AVS without adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) stimulation was performed by two experienced cardiologists. The procedure involved sequential cannulation, with no more than 15 minutes between sampling of the left and right adrenal veins. A successful catheterization defined by a selectivity index (SI) greater than 2 (cortisol concentrations from adrenal veins divided by inferior vena cava) [16–18]. Clinicians utilized the cortisol-corrected aldosterone laterality index (high to low side) of more than 2:1 to confirm unilateral disease [16, 17, 19, 20]. In inconclusive cases, a repeat AVS was offered.

For each adrenal gland, hypersecretion was categorized by bilateral successful AVS as ipsilateral, contralateral, or bilateral. Diagnostic accuracy was determined using the aldosterone-to-cortisol ratio (A/C) of the single adrenal vein (AV) and the AV/IVC index, defined as (aldosterone/cortisol)AV/(aldosterone/cortisol)IVC.

Biochemical measurements and calculations

Plasma aldosterone concentration (PAC) (pg/ml) was measured using automatic chemiluminescence immunoassay, and plasma renin activity (PRA) was determined using radioimmunoassay. The aldosterone-to-renin ratio (ARR) was calculated as PAC/PRA, with a lower detection limit for PRA set at 0.01 ng/ml/h.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS 23.0 and MedCalc 22.016. Categorical variables were presented as number (percentage), and continuous variables as mean ± SD or median (interquartile range). Pearson’s chi-square test was employed for categorical variables, while the independent sample t-test and Mann-Whitney-Wilcoxon test were used for normally distributed or non-normally distributed continuous variables. A significance level of p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Under the missing at random assumption, we performed multiple imputation by chained equations to impute missing data for BMI (13.76% missing), duration of hypertension (0.01% missing), maximum systolic blood pressure (0.01% missing), maximum diastolic blood pressure (0.01% missing), number of antihypertensive medications (0.01% missing), left ventricular hypertrophy (2.12% missing), cerebral infarction (2.65% missing) and plasma aldosterone concentration after saline infusion test in prone (1.59% missing). There are no missing values for other variables. In each model, three cases affected by missing data.

Pairwise analysis distinguished ipsilateral and bilateral aldosterone hypersecretion, and contralateral and bilateral aldosterone hypersecretion. These three categories were determined by bilateral successful AVS. Receiver-operating characteristic (ROC) curves were utilized to assess the performance of each adrenal vein A/C and AV/IVC index.

Multivariate models, incorporating factors such as a localized adrenal lesion, historical minimum potassium, and PAC after SIT, were created using logistic regression analyses. This was done by introducing indicators that were statistically different in the univariate logistic regression into the multivariate logistic regression model and eliminating indicators that were no longer correlated with the UPA in the multivariate model. An odds ratio (OR) greater than 1 indicated an increased likelihood of a diagnosis of idiopathic hyperaldosteronism (IHA) in the univariate model and an increased likelihood of a diagnosis of UPA in the multivariate model. Subsequently, ROC curves were generated to evaluate the model’s performance based on predictor combinations with coefficients obtained through logistic regression, using the formula loge p/(1-p) = β0 + β1x1 + β2x2 + β3x3 + β4x4. In all ROC curve analyses, cut-offs corresponding to a specificity of 95% and 90%, sensitivity of 95% and 90%, and the optimal cut-offs according to the Youden’s index criterion were identified, providing corresponding sensitivity and specificity values.

Role of the funding source

The study’s funder played no role in study design, data collection, data analysis, data interpretation, or report writing. Corresponding author P.C. had complete access to all study data and took final responsibility for the decision to submit for publication.

Results

Patient characteristics

Between January 2019 and December 2023, 274 patients with primary aldosteronism underwent AVS at our center. Excluding 85 patients according to exclusion criteria, the analysis included 189 patients with a mean age of 47.7 ± 10.7 years and a hypertension duration of 3.5 (1.0, 10.0) years. Subtype diagnosis revealed 93 cases (49.2%) of lateralized aldosterone secretion (47.3% left, 52.7% right) and 96 individuals (50.8%) with IHA.

The baseline characteristics in Table 1 showed no significant differences between UPA and IHA groups in sex, age at diagnosis, BMI, duration of hypertension, maximum systolic blood pressure, maximum diastolic blood pressure, number of antihypertensive drugs, left ventricular hypertrophy at echocardiography, and ARR at screening. As expected, the UPA patients group had higher plasma aldosterone concentration at screening in standing [211 (152, 302) vs. 180 (140, 248) pg/ml; p = 0.007], before saline infusion test in prone [191 (99, 375) vs. 120 (57, 238) pg/ml; p = 0.002], after saline infusion test in prone [134 (89, 174) vs. 84 (64, 118) pg/ml; p < 0.001], and had lower serum potassium level [3.23 (3.02, 3.36) vs. 3.37 (3.23, 3.57) mmol/L; p < 0.001]. The performance on CT scans was also different between the two groups. In UPA group, approximating 25.8% (24/93) patients absent lesion adrenal or only had diffuse adrenal hyperplasia, 39.8% (37/93) patients had nodule on the left adrenal, 21.5% (20/93) patients had nodule on the right side, and 12.9% (12/93) patients had both sides nodules. The corresponding situation of the IHA group is 52.1% (50/96), 31.3% (30/96), 4.2% (4/96), and 12.5% (12/96). Sum of these, UPA group had a higher percentage of unilateral nodule (61.3 vs. 35.4%; p < 0.001). Meanwhile, UPA also showed a higher rate of ≥1 cm adenoma (41.9 vs. 25.0%; p = 0.014).

Prediction models

Multivariate logistic regression models, incorporating nodular consistency at imaging (NCI), lowest serum potassium (LK), and plasma aldosterone concentration after saline infusion test (PACSIT), were employed to create biochemical/imaging-corrected adrenal vein A/C index and AV/IVC index. After exploring and trying, we found that the most effective imaging index for identifying the hypersecretion side in the model was that there were nodules on one side and no nodules on the opposite side. Therefore, nodular consistency at imaging in the model that distinguishes between the ipsilateral and bilateral was referred to nodules are present on the side where blood is obtained, and the opposite side showed normal or diffuse hyperplasia. However, adrenal showed normal or diffuse hyperplasia where blood is obtained while nodules are present on the opposite side, was defined as nodular consistency at imaging in differentiating contralateral and bilateral. Complete data for the final logistic regression models were presented in Tables S3–S6. Regarding to model equation, adrenal vein A/C index in diagnosing ipsilateral disease was 0.027A/C + 0.006PACSIT − 2.112LK + 1.812NCI + 3.830 (Table S3). AV/IVC index in diagnosing ipsilateral disease was 0.692AC/IVC + 0.009PACSIT − 2.675LK+ 1.847NCI + 4.892 (Table S4). Adrenal vein A/C index in diagnosing contralateral disease was −0.017AC + 0.012PACSIT − 2.793LK + 1.578NCI +6.894 (Table S5). AV/IVC index in diagnosing ipsilateral disease was −0.832AC/IVC + 0.011PACSIT − 2.449LK +1.530NCI + 6.473 (Table S6).

Diagnostic accuracy before adjustment

We evaluated the diagnostic performance of adrenal vein A/C index and the AV/IVC index in distinguishing between contralateral and ipsilateral hypersecretion and bilateral disease. Before any adjustments for biochemical and imaging factors, the application of adrenal vein A/C index to diagnose ipsilateral disease yielded the highest AUC at 0.829 (95% CI 0.779 - 0.879), while its use in diagnosing contralateral disease resulted in the lowest AUC of 0.609 (95% CI 0.535 - 0.682). The AV/IVC index exhibited predictive capabilities for both ipsilateral (AUC = 0.809, 95% CI 0.756 - 0.861) and contralateral (AUC = 0.754, 95% CI 0.692 - 0.817) disease.

Table 3 details the sensitivity and specificity of various cutoff values for the indices. In the detection of ipsilateral disease, an adrenal vein A/C index > 46.76 (pg/ml)/(ug/dl) showed a sensitivity of 54%, specificity of 90%, and a positive likelihood ratio of 5.43. Conversely, an AV/IVC index > 3.45 demonstrated a sensitivity of 45%, specificity of 96%, and a positive likelihood ratio of 12.4. The use of adrenal vein A/C index and AV/IVC index to identify contralateral disease achieved 95% specificity at low cutoffs [3.64 (pg/ml)/(ug/dl) and 0.25, respectively], but sensitivity was modest (16 and 19%, respectively). Improving the cutoff for the AV/IVC index to 0.63 increased sensitivity to 68%, though specificity decreased to 79%, indicating that 21% of patients might undergo inappropriate adrenalectomy.

Diagnostic accuracy after adjustment

The ROC curves, assessing the performance of multivariate regression models, demonstrated a notable enhancement in the accuracy of predicting ipsilateral and contralateral aldosterone hypersecretion following adjustments for biochemical and imaging variables. Specifically, for predicting contralateral disease, both the biochemical/imaging-corrected adrenal vein A/C index (AUC 0.828, 95% CI 0.775 - 0.880; Fig. 2c) and AV/IVC index (AUC 0.845, 95% CI 0.795 - 0.895; Fig. 2d) exhibited improved accuracy. Achieving 95% specificity (−0.065 and 0.41, respectively), they demonstrated moderate sensitivity (50% and 53%, respectively) and high positive likelihood ratios (10.0 and 10.6, respectively). With a decrease in the adjusted AV/IVC index to −0.45, sensitivity increased to 69%, but specificity decreased to 86%. Similarly, in diagnosing ipsilateral disease, both the biochemical/imaging-corrected adrenal vein A/C index (AUC 0.881, 95% CI 0.838 - 0.924) and AV/IVC index (AUC 0.893, 95% CI 0.853–0.934) achieved heightened accuracy. At 95% specificity, the adjusted-A/C and adjusted-AV/IVC exhibited a sensitivity of 60% and 64%, respectively, with positive likelihood ratios of 12.0 and 12.8, respectively.

Predictive value of typical unilateral primary aldosteronism

Five patients met the criteria of being young (age < 35), experiencing spontaneous hypokalemia, having aldosterone concentration > 200 pg/ml, and possessing an isolated unilateral adrenal cortical adenoma > 10 mm on adrenal CT scan. Their patient characteristics and AVS results are detailed in Table S7. Our models also have a good predictive value for them.

Discussion

In this study, based on the primary aldosteronism patients with a median history of hypertension of 3.5 years, we investigated the diagnostic efficacy of adrenal vein A/C index and AV/IVC index in identifying the side of aldosterone excess secretion in cases of failed lateral adrenal vein intubation. Additionally, we constructed a logistic regression model based on aldosterone concentration after saline infusion test, lowest blood potassium, and the presence or absence of consistent adrenal gland nodules on CT scans. We evaluated the diagnostic utility of this model.

Current guidelines recommend complete AVS for all patients, establishing the dominant secretory side by comparing A/C values on each side, except for typical UPA patients under 35 years, where this is considered the gold standard for primary aldosteronism typing [16]. However, technical challenges in intubation often lead to failed blood extraction on one side, with a success rate ranging from 8.0% to 30.5% in centers performing a small number of AVS procedures per year [21]. Therefore, numerous studies have sought indicators to predict subtype diagnosis from incomplete AVS data, not only avoiding the risks and costs associated with repeated AVS but also encouraging broader AVS adoption. Despite the majority of research focusing on ACTH-stimulated studies [15, 22], the number of AVS centers without ACTH stimulation was twice that with ACTH stimulation [23]. Non-ACTH-stimulated AVS, while reducing intubation success rates, demonstrated better repeatability and lower misdiagnosis rates [24–26]. In addition, recent studies have found that under the stimulation of ACTH, blood extraction from the junction of the right adrenal vein and inferior vena cava can better replace the right adrenal vein [27]. Thus, research under non-ACTH stimulation conditions holds considerable significance.

Endocrine Society Clinical Practice Guideline recommended a cutoff value for diagnosing UPA with a laterality index greater than 2, based on non-ACTH-stimulated AVS [16]. In our center, AVS were performed without ACTH stimulation, and a laterality index greater than 2 was used as the diagnostic criterion for UPA. In our study, when AV/IVC was greater than 3.45, the specificity for the diagnosis of ipsilateral disease was 96% and the sensitivity was 45%; when AV/IVC was less than 0.35, the specificity for the diagnosis of contralateral lesions was 90% and the sensitivity was 36%. Before adjustment, the diagnostic accuracy was in the middle of similar studies.

To the best of our knowledge, only one team has developed a clinically-adjusted predictive model, which is for individuals with a median hypertensive history of 10 years [28]. Parasiliti-Caprino et al. [28] defined UPA as a laterality index greater than 3. The optimal prediction model developed by their team shows that when the specificity is 95%, the sensitivity to diagnose ipsilateral disease is 70–80%, and the sensitivity to diagnose contralateral lesions is 52–72% [28]. Our biochemical/imaging-corrected AV/IVC prediction model achieved 95% specificity with 64% sensitivity for diagnosing ipsilateral diseases and 53% sensitivity for contralateral diseases, which is slightly lower than that study. The relatively younger age of our patients, along with shorter history of hypertension and less severe disease (e.g., lower aldosterone levels and smaller nodules on CT), likely contributes to the diagnostic challenges. In their study, the mean age at diagnosis of primary aldosteronism was 49 years, the duration of hypertension was 6 years, and 75% of patients had lesion sizes ≥ 1 cm. In contrast, our study showed a mean age of 47.7 years, a hypertension duration of 3.5 years, and only 33% of patients had lesions ≥ 1 cm. In addition, a higher laterality index cutoff value can often build a better prediction model. It’s similar to that, in the non-adjusted state, Malley et al.‘s [15] cutoff value of laterality index is 4 and Wang et al. [14] is 3, so Malley et al. has the larger area under the ROC curve.

The limitations of our study must be acknowledged. Firstly, it was retrospective, introducing inherent biases. Secondly, the model’s generalizability remains uncertain due to no external validation is performed. Thirdly, we did not use the patient population, collection method and cutoff value that were most conducive to building a predictive model, but instead used these recommended by the guidelines, which reduced the accuracy of our predictive model. Finally, the relatively scattered nature of our patient data and the different treatment approaches, including total adrenalectomy, partial adrenalectomy and selective adrenal artery embolization, contribute to the challenge of confirming UPA diagnoses through postoperative follow-up.

In conclusion, in the case of failure of unilateral blood extraction, it is valuable to use a reasonable model to distinguish the subtypes of primary aldosteronism. While partial AVS data does not play a definitive role, it can help early clinical decision.

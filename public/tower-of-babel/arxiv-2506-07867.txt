Equivariant K K -theory of cellular toroidal embeddings

Abstract
. Introduction

Let
G
denote a complex connected reductive algebraic group. Let
C
denote the center of
G
and let
G_{ad}=G/C
be the corresponding
semisimple adjoint group.

An
equivariant embedding

\mathbb{X}
of
G
is a normal

G\times G
-variety containing a point
x
such that the orbit

(G\times G)\cdot x
is open and isomorphic to
G\cong G\times 1
. When
G=T
is a torus, the
T
-embeddings are nothing but the
T
-toric
varieties. We say that the equivariant embedding
\mathbb{X}
of
G

is
toroidal
if the quotient map
p:G\rightarrow G_{ad}
extends to a
unique
G\times G
-equivariant morphism

p:\mathbb{X}\rightarrow\overline{G_{ad}}
, where
\overline{G_{ad}}
denotes the
wonderful compactification of
G_{ad}
defined by De Concini and
Procesi (see
[
12
]
). Here the
G\times G
-action on
\overline{G_{ad}}
is
via its projection to
G_{ad}\times G_{ad}
.

The smooth toroidal embeddings are exactly the regular embeddings (see

[
30
, Chapter 5, Theorem 29.2]
,
[
4
]
,
[
7
, Section
1.4]
).

Let
\mathbb{X}
denote a toroidal embedding of
G
. Let
T\subseteq G
a maximal torus
of
G
. Let
W
denote the Weyl group of
(G,T)
. Let

X=\overline{(T\times T)\cdot x}
denote the closure of

T\cong T\times 1
in
\mathbb{X}
. The fan
{\mathcal{F}}
associated to
X

is a subdivision of the Weyl chambers in
X_{*}(T)_{\mathbb{R}}
. Let

T\subseteq B\subseteq G
be a Borel subgroup
of
G
. Let

X^{+}
denote the toric variety whose fan
{\mathcal{F}}^{+}
is the subfan of

{\mathcal{F}}
consisting of the cones in the positive Weyl chamber. In
particular,
{\mathcal{F}}^{+}
is a subdivision of the positive Weyl chamber

\mathcal{C}^{+}
associated to
(G\supseteq B\supseteq T)
(see
Section
6
,
[
8
, Proposition 6.2.4]
).

We recall here that a
T
-cellular variety
is a
T
-variety
X

equipped with a
T
-stable algebraic cell decomposition. In other
words there is a filtration

(1.1)

X=Z_{1}\supseteq Z_{2}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq Z_{m}\supseteq Z_{m+1}=\emptyset

where
each
Z_{i}
is a closed
T
-stable subvariety of
X
and

Z_{i}\setminus Z_{i+1}=Y_{i}
is
T
-equivariantly isomorphic to the
affine space
{\mathbb{C}}^{k_{i}}
equipped with a linear action of
T

for
1\leq i\leq m
. Furthermore,
Y_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
, are the
Bialynicki-Birula cells associated to a generic one-parameter subgroup
of
T
(see Definition
3.2
and
[
34
]
). In other words a

T
-variety is said to be
T
-cellular
if it admits a filtrable
Bialynicki-Birula cellular decomposition with respect to a generic
one-parameter subgroup such that the Bialyncki-Birula cells are
smooth. Any smooth projective complex variety

with
T
-action
having only finitely many
T
-fixed points is
T
-cellular. Indeed for
any projective variety the Bialynicki-Birula cell decomposition is
filtrable (see
[
3
]
) and since

is smooth the
Bialynicki-Birula cells are smooth (see
[
6
, Section 3.1]
).

We let

Let

We state below our main results:

Theorem 1.1
.

The ring

has the following

This direct sum is a free

K^{0}_{{\widetilde{T}}_{comp}}(X^{+})\otimes R({\widetilde{G}}_{comp})
-module of rank

|W|
with basis

\displaystyle\Big\{\prod_{\alpha\in I}(1-e^{\alpha})\otimes f_{v}:v\in C^{I}\penalty\ and\penalty\ I\subseteq\Delta\Big\}

where

generated by the
classes of
{{\widetilde{G}}_{comp}\times{\widetilde{G}}_{comp}}
-equivariant line bundles on

\mathbb{X}

Theorem 1.2
.

We have the following isomorphism of

-algebras

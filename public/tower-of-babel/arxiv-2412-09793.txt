Semisupervised Community Detection via Quasi-Stationary Distributions
Nicolas Fraiman, Michael Nisenzon

Abstract
. Spectral clustering is a widely used method for community detection in networks. We focus on a semi-supervised community detection scenario in the Partially Labeled Stochastic Block Model (PL-SBM) with two balanced communities, where a fixed portion of labels is known. Our approach leverages random walks in which the revealed nodes in each community act as absorbing states. By analyzing the quasi-stationary distributions associated with these random walks, we construct a classifier that distinguishes the two communities by examining differences in the associated eigenvectors. We establish upper and lower bounds on the error rate for a broad class of quasi-stationary algorithms, encompassing both spectral and voting-based approaches. In particular, we prove that this class of algorithms can achieve the optimal error rate in the connected regime. We further demonstrate empirically that our quasi-stationary approach improves performance on both real-world and simulated datasets.



1.
Introduction

Community detection is the problem of identifying subsets of nodes (communities) in a network that are more densely connected internally than to the rest of the network. Such communities often reveal key structural properties in diverse settings, including social networks, biological systems, and information networks. Mathematically, one often models this structure via an unknown partition
\sigma
of the node set
[n]
. Given the network’s adjacency matrix
A
, the task is to infer this partition
\sigma
, providing insight into the network’s organization and underlying processes.

A common generative model for networks with latent community structure is the stochastic block model (SBM) introduced by Holland, Laskey, and Leinhardt
[
11
]
. This model has been studied extensively as a theoretical benchmark for evaluating community detection algorithms, for a survey of results see
[
1
]
. The balanced stochastic block model (SBM) with two communities generates a random graph on
n
nodes partitioned into two equally sized groups,
\mathcal{C}_{+}
and
\mathcal{C}_{-}
, where edges appear with probability
p
within each community and
q
across communities.

Previous work
[
2
,
10
]
established that in the connected regime, exact recovery of the two communities is possible for a sufficiently large gap between
p
and
q
. Spectral algorithms are a popular and easily implemented class of methods for community recovery that leverage the eigenstructure of matrices associated with the graph, such as the adjacency matrix or the Laplacian matrix, to reveal these communities.

In many scenarios, some node labels are partially revealed, making the problem semi-supervised. Incorporating these labels can improve the rate of recovery. In the bounded average degree regime, even an arbitrary small fraction of revealed labels strictly improve the achievable recovery rate, and message passing algorithms that incorporate side information asymptotically achieve the optimal recovery rate
[
14
]
. Moreover, partially revealed labels allow recovery for parameters outside the regular recovery region
[
17
]
.

Quasi-stationary distributions (QSDs) provide a natural way to integrate partial label information. Given a set of revealed nodes in one community, we define a random walk on the unrevealed nodes while treating the revealed nodes as absorbing states. The limiting distribution of this random walks conditional on not being absorbed is a QSD. See the book by Collet, Martinez and San Martin
[
5
]
for a general reference on the subject. Yaglom
[
20
]
introduced the concept and showed that such limiting distributions exist for branching processes conditional on avoiding extinction. Darroch and Seneta
[
6
]
characterized QSDs for discrete–time Markov Chains as left principal eigenvectors of a suitably defined submatrix of the transition matrix. Thus, QSDs methods allow eigenvector analysis similar to the classical spectral methods for community detection to bound the error rate.

Due to the heterogeneous structure of the SBM, we expect an unabsorbed random walk to spend less time in the nodes of the same community as the absorbing set. Darroch and Seneta
[
6
]
also interpreted the right eigenvector of the transition matrix as a vector of nonnegative weights on the quasi-stationary distribution such that the entrywise product is the occupation measure (the proportion of time spent at each node) conditional on non-absorption.

In this paper, we apply quasi-stationary distributions to semi-supervised community detection in the partially labeled SBM. By treating revealed nodes as absorbing states and examining the corresponding QSDs, we construct classifiers that leverage both the graph structure and partial label information. We quantify the recovery rate of our methods, and show that our methods extend the range of exact recovery (i.e., identifying all community labels correctly with high probability) in the presence of partially revealed labels all the way to the impossibility threshold shown by Saad and Nosratinia
[
16
]
.

1.1.
Related results

For the connected regime, Abbé, Bandeira, and Hall
[
2
]
and Hajek, Wu, and Xu
[
10
]
established the exact recovery threshold for community detection in the SBM in the connected regime where here
p=a\log n/n
and
q=b\log n/n
. They proved that below a certain value, exact recovery is information-theoretically impossible and showed that above this value communities can be recovered exactly with spectral methods with refinement, and semidefinite programming respectively.

Zhang and Zhou
[
22
]
employed a minimax formulation to determine the optimal error rate for for growing degree networks. Later, Abbé, Fan, Wang, and Zhong
[
3
]
showed that a simple spectral algorithm using the second eigenvector of the adjacency matrix has a recovery rate that achieves the information-theoretic upper bound for community detection in the connected regime. In particular, the second eigenvector
\bar{\nu}_{2}
of the expected adjacency matrix
\mathbb{E}\left[A\right]
exactly classifies the communities since
\bar{\nu}_{2}\propto\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{C}_{+}}-\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{C}_{-}}
. Unfortunately, the second eigenvector
\nu_{2}
of
A
is not concentrated tightly enough around
\bar{\nu}_{2}
for exact recovery. Instead, they used the image
A\bar{\nu}_{2}/\lambda_{2}(\bar{A})
and showed that this product still achieves the optimal recovery rate of
n^{-(\sqrt{a}-\sqrt{b})^{2}/2}
with additional lower order terms. Methodologically, they applied the leave–one–out technique to bound the entrywise fluctuations of the
\nu_{2}
eigenvector around the image
A\bar{\nu}_{2}/\lambda(\bar{A})
to a lower order. As a result, under appropriate choices of the connectivity parameters
p,q
, the second eigenvector
\nu_{2}
of
A
would correctly classify all nodes with high probability, and therefore spectral methods achieve the optimal recovery rate in the connectivity regime.

Subsequently, Deng, Ling, and Strohmer
[
8
]
extended the results in
[
3
]
to normalizing under the degree matrix
D
. In particular, for the symmetric degree-normalized graph Laplacian
I-D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2}
, they used a generalized Davis-Kahan theorem to bound the perturbation in the eigenspace. They found that the Laplacian still gives the same rates for exact recovery in the connected case and showed more stable empirical results.

For the sparse regime, Mossel, Neeman, and Sly
[
15
]
and separately Massoulié
[
13
]
proved the conjecture of Decelle, Krzakala, Moore, and Zdeborová
[
7
]
identifying the so-called “detectability threshold”. Below this threshold, it becomes information-theoretically impossible to reliably detect communities, regardless of the algorithm used. The detectability threshold is determined by a signal–to–noise ratio (SNR) given by
(a-b)^{2}/2(a+b)
, where partial recovery is possible when
\text{SNR}>1
. Moreover, Chin, Rao, and Vu
[
4
]
found that spectral methods for the adjacency matrix achieve near optimal rates of recovery in the sparse case, with bounds on rates of recovery based on the signal–to–noise ratio.

Community detection with side information has been examined as a potential way to refine and improve the sharp threshold for exact recovery in the connected regime. Saad and Nosratinia
[
16
]
considered the connected SBM with partially revealed labels subject to noise and found necessary and sufficient conditions for exact recovery, as well as a two-step algorithm to achieve recovery. In this case, the second step is a corrective voting phase.

More recently, Gaudio and Joshi
[
9
]
found an information-theoretic threshold for recovery under partial and noisy side information by comparing to genie-aided estimators where all but one node are revealed. They also found single-step algorithms that achieve exact recovery. In both cases, the parameters for exact recovery do not change substantively. In particular, we cannot improve the range of parameters where exact recovery is possible from the bound in
[
2
]
unless
1-o(1)
of the labels are revealed.

In the bounded degree case, when
p=a/n
and
q=b/n
, exact recovery is impossible as there are many isolated nodes. However, partial recovery is still possible when
\text{SNR}>1
. In the presence of side information, Mossel and Xu
[
14
]
show that there exist algorithms that achieve optimal performance as well. More recently, Strohmer and Sheng
[
17
]
show that in the presence of side information, partial recovery is possible below the
\text{SNR}>1
threshold. In particular, any constant fraction
\delta
of revealed nodes allows for weak recovery for any
\text{SNR}>0
.

1.2.
Our contributions

In this paper, we consider the case of a SBM with two balanced communities and partially revealed, noise-free labels. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first paper to consider and apply quasi-stationary distributions to community detection. By treating revealed nodes as absorbing states, we define transition submatrices
P_{i}
for each community. Under appropriate connectivity conditions, each quasi-stationary distribution
\mu_{i}
exists and assigns lower probabilities to nodes within the same community, facilitating community detection.

We formulate a class of single step estimators for community detection based on the eigenvectors of
P_{i}
that includes a simple voting component based on the revealed nodes. The class of estimators is parametrized by a weight to the quasi-stationary component. Using the leave–one–out technique in
[
3
]
and the generalized Davis-Kahan theorem in
[
8
]
allows us to extend the entrywise eigenvector analysis from the adjacency to the transition matrix. We establish an upper bound on error rates and empirically demonstrate improvements under various parameter settings, particularly in the bounded degree regime.

In addition to an upper bound, we show a minimax lower bound on the error rate with side information over all balanced partitions and partial labellings
(\sigma,\ell)
, analogous to Zhang and Zhou
[
22
]
. In particular, the error rate does not change asymptotically with revealed labels in the connected regime and the QSD class achieves the optimal error rate in the connected regime. This aligns with previous work
[
9
,
16
]
, which confirms the known range for exact recovery under partial information. We present a concise proof via an extension of equivariance as defined in Xu, Jog, and Loh
[
19
]
under community–preserving permutations of the clustering and revealed labels.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
1.1
, we review related work. In Section
2
we define our quasi-stationary algorithm and present upper and lower bounds on its error rate. In Section
3
, we show the equivariance lemma needed for the lower bound and develop the matrix and eigenvector concentration results needed for the upper bound. Finally, in Section
4
, we empirically compare the QSD method with the classic spectral algorithm over a collection of real and simulated datasets.

Setup and Notation

We first define the model we study, the Partially Labeled Balanced SBM (PL–SBM).

Definition 1

(Balanced partitions and partial labels)
.

Let
\mathcal{V}=[n]

. We say that

is in the
\delta
-fraction revealed class of balanced partitions and partial labels if

|\mathcal{C}_{+}|=|\mathcal{C}_{-}|=n/2\text{ and }|\mathcal{R}_{+}|=|\mathcal{R}_{-}|=\delta n/2.

Definition 2

(Partially Labeled Balanced SBM)
.

Given a pair

The above submatrix definitions extend naturally to the expected adjacency matrix
\bar{A}=\mathbb{E}\left[A\right]
, expected degree matrix
\bar{D}=\mathbb{E}\left[D\right]
and expected transition matrix
\bar{P}=\bar{D}^{-1}\bar{A}
. All matrices are interpreted as functions, with

Throughout the paper, for concision, we use

We proceed to describe the eigenvector of the expected transition matrix
\bar{P}_{i}
and establish constants used throughout the paper. We note that the expected adjacency matrix
\bar{A}
follows the form

\bar{A}=\frac{\log n}{n}\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
b&a\end{bmatrix}\otimes\mathbf{J}_{n/2\times n/2}

where
\mathbf{J}
is the all-ones matrix.

Then
\bar{P}_{i}
is also a rank-two block matrix with two distinct values and the principal eigenvector
\bar{\pi}_{i}
is proportional to
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}+\rho\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{C}_{-i}}
for some
\rho>0
. Then we see that

(1-\delta)\frac{na}{2}+\rho\frac{nb}{2}=\frac{1}{\rho}\left((1-\delta)\frac{nb}{2}+\delta\frac{na}{2}\right).

Rearranging terms gives us the quadratic equation

\rho^{2}-\frac{a\delta\rho}{b}-(1-\delta)=0

which has a positive solution of

\rho=\frac{1}{2b}(a\delta+\sqrt{a^{2}\delta^{2}+4(1-\delta)b^{2}}).

We use
\rho
to normalize the mean–field eigenvector
\bar{\pi}_{i}
such that
\left\|\bar{\pi}_{i}\right\|_{2}=1
.

Definition 4

(Mean–field eigenvector)
.

For
i\in\{+,-\}
, we normalize the eigenvector
\bar{\pi}_{i}
as

\bar{\pi}_{i}=\frac{1}{\gamma\sqrt{n}}(\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}+\rho\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{C}_{-i}})

where
\rho=\dfrac{1}{2b}(a\delta+\sqrt{a^{2}\delta^{2}+4(1-\delta)b^{2}})
and
\gamma=\sqrt{\dfrac{1-\delta+\rho^{2}}{2}}
.

Finally, we define the error rate for a recovery algorithm. Any recovery algorithm
\hat{\sigma}
takes in an adjacency matrix
A
and partial labelling
\ell
and returns an estimator
\hat{\sigma}[A,\ell]:\mathcal{U}\to\{1,-1\}
that can be compared to the true assignment
\sigma
using the Hamming distance to find the error rate. In the unlabeled case, this holds up to a global flip between the communities
[
3
]
, but partial labellings allow us to identify the respective communities so such a realignment is no longer necessary. Then for a fixed clustering
\sigma
and a fixed partial labelling
\ell
, the error rate for
\hat{\sigma}
is the normalized Hamming distance on the unrevealed nodes
\mathcal{U}
.

Definition 5

(Error rate)
.

For an estimator
\hat{\sigma}
, the error rate
r(\hat{\sigma})
is defined as

r(\hat{\sigma})=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{U}|}\sum_{v\in\mathcal{U}}\mathbbm{1}_{\hat{\sigma}(v)\neq\sigma(v)}.

2.2.
Minimax bound for semi-supervised clustering

Following the approach of Zhang and Zhou
[
22
]
, we derive a minimax bound that suggests that the quasi-stationary method in the connected regime has asymptotic performance matching the optimal unsupervised case. This requires showing a partially revealed equivariance lemma analogous to their global-to-local lemma.

Throughout this paper, we only consider permutation equivariant and community equivariant estimators
\hat{\sigma}
extending the definitions of Xu, Jog, and Loh
[
19
]
.

Let
\pi\in\Pi_{n}
be a permutation on all
n
nodes and
A^{\pi},\sigma^{\pi},\ell^{\pi}
be the permuted adjacency matrix, labels, and revealed labels, where
A^{\pi}(\pi(u),\pi(v))=A(u,v)
.

Definition 6

(Equivariance)
.

\hat{\sigma}
is permutation equivariant if permuting the labels does not affect the estimate of the permuted nodes

\hat{\sigma}[A,\ell]=\hat{\sigma}[A^{\pi},\ell^{\pi}]\circ\pi

In addition,
\hat{\sigma}
is community equivariant if permuting the communities of the revealed nodes commutes with the estimator

\hat{\sigma}[A,-\ell]=-\hat{\sigma}[A,\ell].

We note that permutation and community equivariance both hold for a wide variety of estimators, including the quasi-stationary method used in this paper.

In our setting, the minimax theorem corresponds to the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

For equivariant estimators
\hat{\sigma}
we have

\inf_{\hat{\sigma}}\mathbb{E}\left[r(\hat{\sigma})\right]\geq n^{-(1+o(1))(\sqrt{a}-\sqrt{b})^{2}/2}.

Proof.

Let
\hat{\sigma}
be a permutation and community equivariant estimator that satisfies

\mathbb{E}\left[r(\hat{\sigma})\right]=\inf_{\hat{\sigma}}\mathbb{E}\left[r(\hat{\sigma})\right].

By Lemma
1
, for any nodes
u,v\in\mathcal{U}

\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{\sigma}(u)\neq\sigma(u)\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{\sigma}(v)\neq\sigma(v)\right).

We then apply linearity to see that

\mathbb{E}\left[r(\hat{\sigma})\right]=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{U}|}\sum_{v\in\mathcal{U}}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\hat{\sigma}(v)\neq\sigma(v)}\right]=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{U}|}\sum_{v\in\mathcal{U}}\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{\sigma}(v)\neq\sigma(v)\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{\sigma}(v)\neq\sigma(v)\right)

for any unrevealed node
v\in\mathcal{U}
.

Lemma 5.1 in Zhang and Zhou
[
22
]
provides a lower bound on the entrywise error rate by considering the case where all other labels
[n]\setminus v
are revealed and then taking a likelihood test between
v\in\mathcal{C}_{+}
and
v\in\mathcal{C}_{-}
. In particular, the entrywise error rate is minimized by taking a majority vote on all
[n]\setminus v
. Since full information includes any partial information, this must also be a lower bound for the entrywise error rate of the PL–SBM. Therefore,

\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{\sigma}(v)\neq\sigma(v)\right)\geq\varepsilon\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n/2}(W_{i}-Z_{i})\leq 0\right)

for some
\varepsilon>0
, where the
W_{i}
and
Z_{i}
are i.i.d. Bernoulli
(a\log n/n)
and

Bernoulli
(b\log n/n)
respectively. By Lemma 5.2 in
[
22
]

\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n/2}(W_{i}-Z_{i})\leq 0\right)\geq n^{-(1+o(1))(\sqrt{a}-\sqrt{b})^{2}/2}.

We conclude that

\inf_{\hat{\sigma}}\mathbb{E}\left[r(\hat{\sigma})\right]\geq n^{-(1+o(1))(\sqrt{a}-\sqrt{b})^{2}/2}.\qed

2.3.
Performance of QSD method

The existence of revealed nodes
\mathcal{R}_{i}
allows us to define voting methods, in particular quasi-stationary distribution (QSD) methods and simple voting. We define the quasi-stationary method over two communities by taking the difference between the corresponding principal eigenvectors.

Definition 7
.

(QSD score)
For each node
u\in\mathcal{U}
, the quasi-stationary score
Q(u)
is defined as the difference between the left eigenvectors

Q(u)=\mu_{-}(u)-\mu_{+}(u).

with a corresponding estimator

\hat{\sigma}_{Q}(u)=\sgn(Q(u))

We define simple voting by subtracting the revealed group connections.

Definition 8
.

(Simple voting score)
For each
u\in\mathcal{U}
, simple voting
S(u)
is given by

S(u)=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{R}_{+}}A(u,v)-\sum_{v\in\mathcal{R}_{-}}A(u,v)

with a corresponding estimator

\hat{\sigma}_{S}(u)=\sgn(S(u))

For a fixed choice of the connectivity parameters
a,b
and an estimator
\hat{\sigma}
, we would like to bound the expected error rate
\mathbb{E}\left[r(\hat{\sigma})\right]
over all networks generated from
\sigma,\ell
. Through equivariance as applied in Theorem
1
, we relate the global error rate to an entrywise error rate
\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{\sigma}(u)\neq\sigma(u)\right)
for any unseen node
u\in\mathcal{U}
. The entrywise error is in turn bounded using Lemma
7
in terms of an exponential rate
I
analogous to the information-theoretic optimum in the unsupervised case
[
22
]
. A natural parametrization of the rate
I
is by weights
(\alpha,\beta)
on some functions of the unrevealed and revealed nodes, respectively. As a result, for some constants
(\alpha,\beta)
, the desired bounds on the error rate follow the form

\mathbb{E}\left[r(\hat{\sigma})\right]\leq n^{-I(\alpha,\beta)}.

We state the main results of our paper. We first show a bound on the quasi-stationary estimator
\hat{\sigma}_{Q}
.

Theorem 2
.

Given
(\sigma,\ell)\in\mathfrak{C}(\delta)
consider a
\pSBM(p,q,\sigma,\ell)
with
p=a\log n/n
,
q=b\log n/n
and
a+b>4
. Then the quasi-stationary estimator
\hat{\sigma}_{Q}
has an error rate of at most

\mathbb{E}\left[r(\hat{\sigma}_{Q})\right]\leq n^{-(1+o(1))I(\rho-1,\rho)}+o(n^{-1}).

for

I(\alpha,\beta)=\frac{1}{2}\sup_{\theta>0}a+b-(1-\delta)ae^{-\theta\alpha}-(1-\delta)be^{\theta\alpha}-\delta ae^{-\theta\beta}-\delta be^{\theta\beta}.

where
\rho
is defined in Definition
4
.

Proof.

By Lemma
1
, we see that the expected error rate is equal to the probability of misclassifying any node
u

\mathbb{E}\left[r(\hat{\sigma}_{Q})\right]=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{U}|}\sum_{v\in\mathcal{U}}\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{\sigma}_{Q}(v)\neq\sigma(v)\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{\sigma}_{Q}(u)\neq\sigma(u)\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma(u)Q(u)<0\right).

By Lemma
15
, we can approximate
\mu_{-}-\mu_{+}
by a difference of right eigenvectors
D_{-}\pi_{-}-D_{+}\pi_{+}
with a scaling factor of
\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\left\|\bar{A}_{i}\bar{\pi}_{i}\right\|_{1}}=\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\bar{c}\sqrt{n}\log n}
for some constant
\bar{c}
with value explicitly stated in Definition
9
. In particular, with probability
1-o(n^{-1})

(1)

\displaystyle\max_{u\in\mathcal{U}}\left|(\mu_{-}-\mu_{+})(u)-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\bar{c}\sqrt{n}\log n}d(u)(\pi_{-}-\pi_{+})(u)\right|=o(n^{-1}).

As a result, the error from approximating
Q(u)
by right eigenvectors
\pi_{i}
is controlled by upper-bounding with an expression of order
O(n^{-1})
. It is then sufficient to show that there is a
\varepsilon(a,b)>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma(u)Q(u)<0\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma(u)(\mu_{-}-\mu_{+})(u)<0\right)

(2)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma(u)\left((\mu_{-}-\mu_{+})(u)-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\bar{c}\sqrt{n}\log n}d(u)(\pi_{-}-\pi_{+})(u)\right)\leq-\frac{\varepsilon}{n}\right)

(3)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\bar{c}\sqrt{n}\log n}\sigma(u)d(u)(\pi_{-}-\pi_{+})(u)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{n}\right)

We bound both terms separately. First, by our work above in (
1
), (
2
) is bounded by

\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma(u)\left((\mu_{-}-\mu_{+})(u)-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\bar{c}\sqrt{n}\log n}d(u)(\pi_{-}-\pi_{+})(u)\right)\leq-\frac{\varepsilon}{n}\right)=o(n^{-1})

for any constant
\varepsilon>0
.

We bound
(
3
)
by applying the right eigenvector bound in Lemma
16
. We scale by the constant factor
\gamma=\sqrt{(1-\delta+\rho^{2})/2}
defined in Definition
4
. We then note that
\bar{c}
is also a constant and substitute
\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon\bar{c}\gamma
to get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\bar{c}\sqrt{n}\log n}\sigma(u)d(u)(\pi_{-}-\pi_{+})(u)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{n}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma(u)\gamma\lambda_{1}\sqrt{n}d(u)(\pi_{-}-\pi_{+})(u)\leq\varepsilon\bar{c}\gamma\log n\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma(u)\gamma\lambda_{1}\sqrt{n}d(u)(\pi_{-}-\pi_{+})(u)\leq\varepsilon_{1}\log n\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq n^{-I(\rho-1,\rho)+O(\varepsilon_{1})}+o(n^{-1})

where we use the definition of
I
from Lemma
16
to get

I(\rho-1,\rho)=\frac{1}{2}\sup_{\theta>0}(a+b)-(1-\delta)ae^{-\theta(\rho-1)}-(1-\delta)be^{\theta(\rho-1)}-\delta ae^{-\theta\rho}-\delta be^{\theta\rho}.

We note that
I(\rho-1,\rho)
does not have a closed–form solution.

Then we combine our bounds on (
2
) and (
3
) to conclude that

\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma(u)(\mu_{-}-\mu_{+})(u)<0\right)\leq n^{-I(\rho-1,\rho)+O(\varepsilon_{1})}+o(n^{-1}).

The result follows by taking
\varepsilon_{1}\rightarrow 0
.
∎

In addition to the quasi-stationary estimator, we also analyze the simple voting estimator.

Theorem 3
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
2
, the simple voting estimator
\hat{\sigma}_{S}
achieves the following error rate

\mathbb{E}\left[r(\hat{\sigma}_{S})\right]\leq n^{-(1+o(1))\delta(\sqrt{a}-\sqrt{b})^{2}/2}.

Proof.

We first note that the simple voting estimator satisfies

\hat{\sigma}_{S}(u)=\sgn(S(u)).

As in Theorem
2
, we apply equivariance via Lemma
1
to show that the expected error rate is equal to the probability of misclassifying any node
u

\mathbb{E}\left[r(\hat{\sigma}_{S})\right]=\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma(u)\hat{\sigma}_{S}(u)<0\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma(u)S(u)<0\right).

Note that
\sigma(u)S(u)=\sigma(u)\left(\sum_{v\in\mathcal{R}_{+}}A(u,v)-\sum_{v\in\mathcal{R}_{-}}A(u,v)\right)
is equal in distribution to
\sum_{i=1}^{\delta n/2}(W_{i}-Z_{i})
where
W_{i}
and
Z_{i}
are i.i.d. Bernoulli
(a\log n/n)
and Bernoulli
(b\log n/n)
respectively.
Then we apply the Chernoff bound in Lemma
8
with
\alpha=0,\beta=1
to conclude that there exists a
\varepsilon>0
such that

\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma(u)S(u)<0\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\delta n/2}W_{i}-Z_{i}\leq\varepsilon\log n\right)\leq n^{-I(0,1)+O(\varepsilon)}

where

I(0,1)=\frac{\delta}{2}\sup_{\theta>0}(a+b)-ae^{-\theta}-be^{\theta}

We set
\theta=\log(a/b)/2
to get that
I(0,1)\geq\delta(\sqrt{a}-\sqrt{b})^{2}/2
, which yields the desired bound

\mathbb{E}\left[r(\hat{\sigma}_{S})\right]=n^{-\delta(\sqrt{a}-\sqrt{b})^{2}/2+O(\varepsilon)}.

The result follows by taking
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.
∎

Remark 1
.

We note that simple voting achieves exact recovery for sufficiently large values of
\delta
. Saad and Nosratinia
[
16
]
previously showed that when
\log(1-\delta)=-c\log n
for
c\in(0,1)
and
\sqrt{a}-\sqrt{b}>\sqrt{2-2c}
, applying a union bound over
(1-\delta)n
nodes we get an error rate of

\mathbb{E}\left[r(\hat{\sigma}_{s})\right]\leq n^{1-c}\cdot\left(n^{-(1+o(1))(\sqrt{a}-\sqrt{b})^{2}/2}\right)=o(1).

In this case, spectral methods are unnecessary as simple voting achieves exact recovery and voting has an
O(n)
runtime advantage over spectral methods. This is also shown in more generality in Gaudio and Joshi
[
9
]
, with the threshold being extended to the case of unbalanced communities with distinct connectivity parameters under noisy labels.

As neither
\hat{\sigma}_{Q}
nor
\hat{\sigma}_{S}
achieves the information-theoretically optimal recovery rate of
n^{-(1+o(1))(\sqrt{a}-\sqrt{b})^{2}/2}
that some unsupervised methods do
[
3
]
, we also consider a weighted sum of the previous estimators that does achieve the optimal rate.

Definition 9
.

(Mixed QSD method) For each node
u\in\mathcal{U}
, the mixed QSD method is defined as

M(u)=\bar{c}\gamma n\log nQ(u)-S(u)

with a corresponding estimator

\hat{\sigma}_{M}(u)=\sgn(M(u))

where

\displaystyle\rho=\frac{1}{2b}(a\delta+\sqrt{a^{2}\delta^{2}+4(1-\delta)b^{2}})

\displaystyle\gamma=\sqrt{\frac{(1-\delta+\rho^{2})}{2}}

\displaystyle\bar{c}=\frac{\left\|\bar{A}_{i}\bar{\pi}_{i}\right\|_{1}}{\sqrt{n}\log n}=\frac{1}{4}\left(a(\rho+(1-\delta)^{2})+b(\rho+1)(1-\delta)\right)

Theorem 4
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
2
, the mixed estimator
\hat{\sigma}_{M}
achieves an error rate of

\mathbb{E}\left[r\left(\hat{\sigma}_{M}\right)\right]\leq n^{-(1+o(1))(\sqrt{a}-\sqrt{b})^{2}/2}+o(n^{-1}).

In particular, the mixed QSD method achieves the optimal asymptotic error rate.

Proof.

The proof proceeds like in Theorem
2
. By equivariance, we reduce the expected error rate to the entrywise error rate for any unseen
u\in\mathcal{U}

\mathbb{E}\left[r\left(\hat{\sigma}_{M}\right)\right]=\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma(u)M(u)<0\right)

where

M(u)=\gamma\bar{c}n\log n(\mu_{-}-\mu_{+})(u)-S(u).

Then we expand out the QSD component
(\mu_{-}-\mu_{+})(u)
and approximate by right eigenvectors to get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma(u)M(u)<0\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma(u)\left(\gamma\bar{c}n\log n(\mu_{-}-\mu_{+})(u)-S(u)\right)<0\right)

(4)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma(u)\gamma\bar{c}n\log n\left((\mu_{-}-\mu_{+})(u)-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\bar{c}\sqrt{n}\log n}d(u)(\pi_{-}-\pi_{+})(u)\right)\leq-\varepsilon\log n\right)

(5)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma(u)\left(\gamma\lambda_{1}\sqrt{n}d(u)(\pi_{-}-\pi_{+})(u)-S(u)\right)\leq\varepsilon\log n\right)

We first bound the error of the right eigenvector approximation (
4
) by
o(n^{-1})
exactly as in Theorem
2
. Next, we bound (
5
) with the right eigenvector bound in Lemma
16
to see that for some
\varepsilon>0

\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma(u)\left(\gamma\lambda_{1}\sqrt{n}d(u)(\pi_{-}-\pi_{+})(u)-S(u)\right)\leq\varepsilon\log n\right)\leq n^{-I(\rho-1,\rho-1)+O(\varepsilon)}+o(n^{-1})

where

I(\rho-1,\rho-1)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sup_{\theta>0}a+b-ae^{-\theta(\rho-1)}-be^{\theta(\rho-1)}\right)

This optimization problem is solved in Theorem

, leading to a bound of

\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma(u)\left(\gamma\lambda_{1}\sqrt{n}d(u)(\pi_{-}-\pi_{+})(u)-S(u)\right)\leq\varepsilon\log n\right)\leq n^{-(\sqrt{a}-\sqrt{b})^{2}/2+O(\varepsilon)}+o(n^{-1})

and an expected error rate of

\mathbb{E}\left[r\left(\hat{\sigma}_{M}\right)\right]\leq n^{-(\sqrt{a}-\sqrt{b})^{2}/2+O(\varepsilon)}+o(n^{-1}).

The result follows by taking
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.
∎

3.1.
Equivariance

In this section, we prove the equivariance lemma, showing that the probability of misclassifying any label is equal over all permutation and community equivariant estimators
\hat{\sigma}
.

Lemma 1
.

For any unrevealed nodes
u,v\in\mathcal{U}
and an equivariant estimator
\hat{\sigma}

\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{\sigma}(u)\neq\sigma(u)\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{\sigma}(v)\neq\sigma(v)\right).

Proof.

Let
\hat{\sigma}
be a permutation and community equivariant estimator. We show that

\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{\sigma}(u)\neq\sigma(u)\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{\sigma}(v)\neq\sigma(v)\right).

for
u,v\in\mathcal{U}
. We have two cases: either
u,v
are in the same community and
\sigma(u)=\sigma(v)
or
u,v
are in different communities and
\sigma(u)\neq\sigma(v)
. For both cases, we show equality via a community-preserving permutation
\pi
on
\mathcal{U}
such that if
u,v
are in the same community, then
\pi(u),\pi(v)
remain in the same community, and
\pi(u)=v
,
\pi(v)=u
.

In the first case, where
\sigma(u)=\sigma(v)
, let
\pi\in\Pi_{|\mathcal{U}|}
be the transposition
(u,v)
that leaves all other nodes unchanged. In the case where
\sigma(u)\neq\sigma(v)
, let
\pi
be the permutation that maps nodes in
\mathcal{U}_{+}
to
\mathcal{U}_{-}
and transposes
u,v
. Clearly both choices preserve communities and transpose
u,v
. In addition, since the underlying SBM is balanced with balanced label cardinalities,
A^{\pi}
is identically distributed to
A
in both cases. We also note that there exists
\tau\in\{-1,1\}
such that
\ell^{\pi}=\tau\cdot\ell
for both cases.

Applying permutation equivariance, community equivariance, and exchangeability we show the desired equality for both cases as follows

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{\sigma}[A,\ell](u)\neq\sigma(u)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{\sigma}[A^{\pi},\ell^{\pi}](\pi(u))\neq\sigma(u)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{\sigma}[A^{\pi},\tau\cdot\ell](v)\neq\tau\cdot\sigma(v)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{\sigma}[A,\tau\cdot\ell](v)\neq\tau\cdot\sigma(v)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\mathbb{P}\left(\tau\cdot\hat{\sigma}[A,\ell](v)\neq\tau\cdot\sigma(v)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{\sigma}[A,\ell](v)\neq\sigma(v)\right)

where the first equality follows by permutation equivariance, the second equality follows by choice of
\tau\in\{-1,1\}
, the third equality follows by exchangeability under community preserving transformations, and the fourth equality by community equivariance.

Therefore, for any
u,v\in\mathcal{U}

\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{\sigma}(u)\neq\sigma(u)\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\hat{\sigma}(v)\neq\sigma(v)\right).\qed

3.2.
Normalized matrix bounds

We start by noting the following concentration result of Lei and Rinaldo
[
12
]
on the adjacency matrix.

Theorem 5

(Lei and Rinaldo
[
12
]
Theorem 5.2)
.

Let
\max_{u,v}\bar{A}(u,v)\geq k\log n/n
. For any
r>0
, there exist
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that
\left\|A-\bar{A}\right\|_{2}<\max_{u,v}c_{1}(r,k)\sqrt{n\bar{A}(u,v)}
with probability
1-c_{2}n^{-r}
.

In particular, it is well-known
[
3
]
that the result holds for
r=3
. For the expected adjacency matrix
\bar{A}
, the nodes have degrees of order
O(\log n)
. To extend Lei and Rinaldo to the normalized matrix
P
, we must show that the minimum degree is also of order
O(\log n)
with high probability.

Lemma 2
.

Let the adjacency matrix
A
have connectivity parameters
a,b
such that
a+b>2
. Then there exist
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}>0
such that
d_{\min}\geq c_{1}\log n
and
d_{\max}\geq c_{2}\log n
with probability at least
1-c_{3}(n^{1-(a+b)/2})
.

Proof.

Since
a+b\geq 2
,
A
is connected with high probability. By Bennett’s inequality, for any
u\in V
,
t<(a+b)/2
, and
h(x)=(1+x)\log(1+x)-x
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(d(u)\leq\frac{a+b}{2}\log n-t\log n\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{v\in V}A(u,v)\leq\frac{a+b}{2}\log n-t\log n\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\exp\left(-\left(\sum_{v\in V}\mathbb{V}\left[A(u,v)\right]\right)h\left(\frac{-t\log n}{\sum_{v\in V}\mathbb{V}\left[A(u,v)\right]}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\exp\left(-\left(\sum_{v\in V}\mathbb{E}\left[A(u,v)^{2}\right]\right)h\left(\frac{-t\log n}{\sum_{v\in V}\mathbb{E}\left[A(u,v)^{2}\right]}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\exp\left(-\log n\left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right)h\left(\frac{-2t}{a+b}\right)\right)

In addition, we know that
\left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right)h\left(\frac{-2t}{a+b}\right)
is increasing with
t
and

\lim_{t\rightarrow(a+b)/2}\left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right)h\left(\frac{-2t}{a+b}\right)=\frac{a+b}{2}

Then for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
t<(a+b)/2
such that for some
c_{3}>0
,

\mathbb{P}\left(d(v)\leq\frac{a+b}{2}\log n-t\log n\right)\leq c_{3}n^{-(a+b)/2+\varepsilon}.

Finally, taking a union bound over
O(n)
unrevealed entries gives us

\mathbb{P}\left(d_{\min}\geq\frac{a+b}{2}\log n-t\log n\right)\geq 1-c_{3}n^{-(a+b)/2+1+\varepsilon}.

We conclude by defining
c_{1}=\frac{a+b}{2}-t
.

Applying the same chain of reasoning for the maximum, we note that

\mathbb{P}\left(d(u)\geq\frac{a+b}{2}\log n+t_{1}\log n\right)\leq c_{3}n^{-(a+b)/2+\varepsilon}

for some choice of
t_{1}>0
, which gives us the same union bound

\mathbb{P}\left(d_{\max}\leq\frac{a+b}{2}\log n+t_{1}\log n\right)\geq 1-c_{3}n^{-(a+b)/2+1+\varepsilon}.

where
c_{2}:=(a+b)/2+t_{1}
. ∎

Note that the probability
1-c_{3}(n^{-(a+b)/2+1})
is
1-o(n^{-1})
for
a+b>4
. Denote by
P_{i}^{m}
the leave–one–out matrix given by

P_{i}^{m}(u,v)=\begin{cases}P_{i}(u,v),&\text{if}\ u,v\neq m\\
\bar{P}_{i}(u,v),&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Lemma 3
.

Assume that
n\max_{u,v}A(u,v)>c_{0}\log n
for some
c_{0}>1
. Then with probability
1-o(n^{-1})
, for any
r>0
, there exist constants
C,c_{2}>0
such that

\left\|P_{i}-\bar{P}_{i}\right\|_{2}<\frac{c_{2}}{\sqrt{\log n}}\quad\text{and}\quad\max_{m\in\mathcal{V}_{i}}\left\|P_{i}^{m}-\bar{P}_{i}\right\|_{2}<\frac{c_{2}}{\sqrt{\log n}}.

Proof.

We begin by noting that the first statement implies the second since for any
m\in\mathcal{U}
,
(P_{i}^{m}-\bar{P}_{i})
is a principal submatrix of
(P_{i}-\bar{P}_{i})
and the
p,q
-norms for a principal submatrix
G\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
is bounded by that of the matrix
H\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, that is,

\left\|G\right\|_{q\rightarrow p}=\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{k}}\frac{\left\|Gx\right\|_{p}}{\left\|x\right\|_{q}}=\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{k}}\frac{\left\|H(x,\mathbf{0}_{n-k})\right\|_{p}}{\left\|x\right\|_{q}}\leq\max_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\left\|Hy\right\|_{p}}{\left\|y\right\|_{q}}\leq\left\|H\right\|_{q\rightarrow p}.

Next, we see that

\left\|P_{i}-\bar{P}_{i}\right\|_{2}=\left\|D_{i}^{-1}A_{i}-\bar{D}_{i}^{-1}\bar{A}_{i}\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|D_{i}^{-1}(A_{i}-\bar{A}_{i})\right\|_{2}+\left\|(\bar{D}_{i}^{-1}-D_{i}^{-1})\bar{A}_{i}\right\|_{2}\\

The first term is bounded by Theorem
5
and the general fact that norms of principal submatrices are strictly smaller than norms of the original matrix.

\left\|D_{i}^{-1}(A_{i}-\bar{A}_{i})\right\|_{2}\leq\frac{\left\|(A_{i}-\bar{A}_{i})\right\|_{2}}{d_{\min}}\leq\frac{\left\|(A-\bar{A})\right\|_{2}}{d_{\min}}=\frac{c_{2}\sqrt{\log n}}{\log n}\leq\frac{c_{2}}{\sqrt{\log n}}

with probability
1-o(n^{-1})
given an appropriate choice of
c_{2}
from Lemma
2
.

We note that the degree
d_{i}(u)=d(u)
when defined. For the second term, applying the same results tells us that with probability
1-o(n^{-1})
for some
c_{3}
we have

\displaystyle\left\|(\bar{D}_{i}^{-1}-D_{i}^{-1})\bar{A}_{i}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|(\bar{D}_{i}^{-1}-D_{i}^{-1})\bar{A}_{i}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\sum_{u,v\in\mathcal{V}_{i}}\bar{A}_{i}(u,v)^{2}\left(\frac{1}{d(u)}-\frac{1}{\bar{d}(v)}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{u,v}\bar{A}(u,v)\sqrt{\sum_{u,v\in\mathcal{V}_{i}}\left(\frac{d(u)-\bar{d}(u)}{d(u)\bar{d}(u)}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\max_{u,v}\bar{A}(u,v)}{d_{\min}\bar{d}_{\min}}\sqrt{\sum_{u,v\in\mathcal{V}_{i}}(d(u)-\bar{d}(u))^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{n}\max_{u,v}\bar{A}(u,v)}{d_{\min}\bar{d}_{\min}}\sqrt{\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}_{i}}(d(v)-\bar{d}(v))^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{n}\max_{u,v}\bar{A}(u,v)}{d_{\min}\bar{d}_{\min}}\left\|(A_{i}-\bar{A}_{i})\mathbbm{1}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{n}\max_{u,v}\bar{A}(u,v)}{d_{\min}\bar{d}_{\min}}\left\|(A_{i}-\bar{A}_{i})\right\|_{2}\cdot\left\|\mathbbm{1}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(n\max_{u,v}\bar{A}(u,v))^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\min(d_{\min},\bar{d}_{\min})^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{3}}{\sqrt{\log n}}.\qed

3.3.
Eigenvalue bounds

The approximation
\pi_{i}\approx P_{i}\bar{\pi}_{i}/\lambda_{1}
requires bounding the norm of
P_{i}
and the corresponding eigengap. Bounding the eigengap of the normalized submatrix first requires showing that the principal eigenvalue is bounded above and below by positive constants.

Lemma 4
.

When a
\delta
–fraction of nodes have been revealed, the expected substochastic matrix
\bar{P}_{i}
has principal eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
such that
2/(2+\delta)\leq\lambda_{1}\leq 1
.

Proof.

The eigenvector
\pi_{i}
is proportional to
x\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}+y\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{C}_{-i}}
, where
x,y
are normalized such that
(1-\delta)x+y=1
. Then solving for the eigenvalue explicitly,

\displaystyle(1-\delta)x\frac{a}{a+b}+y\frac{b}{a+b}=\lambda_{1}x

\displaystyle(1-\delta)x\frac{b}{a+b}+y\frac{a}{a+b}=\lambda_{1}y

Summing the two equations and using the relationship of
x,y
and
\delta
gives us

1=(1-\delta)x+y=\lambda_{1}(x+y)=\lambda_{1}(1+\delta x).

Therefore, since
0<x<y
and
0<\delta<1
we get

1\geq\lambda_{1}=\frac{1}{(1+\delta x)}>\frac{2}{(2+\delta)}.\qed

As the normalized expectation is a rank-2 matrix, a bound on the eigengap follows naturally.

Lemma 5
.

For the matrix
\bar{P}_{i}
, the eigengap
\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\geq\delta^{2}/(2+\delta)
.

Proof.

The trace of the expected transition matrix
\bar{P}
is
2a/(a+b)
. In addition,
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=\tr(\bar{P}_{i})=(a+(1-\delta)a)/(a+b)
. From Lemma
4
,
\lambda_{1}>2/(2+\delta)
. Since
b\geq 0
, the eigengap is bounded by

\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}=2\lambda_{1}-\tr(\bar{P}_{i})\geq\frac{4}{2+\delta}-\frac{a+(1-\delta)a}{a+b}\geq\frac{4}{2+\delta}-(2-\delta)=\frac{\delta^{2}}{2+\delta}.\qed

We also consider a bound on the principal eigenvalue of
P_{i}
.

Lemma 6
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that
|\lambda_{1}(P_{i})-\lambda_{1}|\geq C/\sqrt{\log n}
with probability
1-o(n^{-1})
.

Proof.

By Weyl’s inequality between eigenvalues and Lemmas
3
and
4
, for some
c_{1}>0
with probability
1-o(n^{-1})
we have

\lambda_{1}(P_{i})\geq\lambda_{1}(\bar{P}_{i})-\lambda_{1}(P_{i}-\bar{P}_{i})\geq\lambda_{1}(\bar{P}_{i})-\left\|P_{i}-\bar{P}_{i}\right\|_{2}\geq\lambda_{1}-C/\sqrt{\log n}

for sufficiently large
n
. The other direction follows analogously. ∎

3.4.
Bounding binomial differences

In this section, we bound the error rate of the mean-field approximation for the QSD and mixed methods by reducing bounds on the transition submatrices
P_{i}
to bounds on the adjacency submatrices
A_{i}
. We note that using the approximations
A_{i}\bar{\pi}_{i}
instead of
\mu_{i}
and scaling by
\sqrt{n}
gives the following expression an unrevealed node
u\in\mathcal{U}

(6)

\displaystyle\sigma(u)(\sqrt{n}\gamma(A_{-}\bar{\pi}_{-}-A_{+}\bar{\pi}_{+})(u)+\gamma_{s}S(u))

where
\gamma_{s}
is a weight on the simple voting component. By Definitions
7
and
9
, we note that
\gamma_{s}=0
for the QSD method and that
\gamma_{s}=-1
for the mixed method.

The following lemma bounds the error rate for each unrevealed node.

Lemma 7
.

For each unrevealed
u\in\mathcal{U}
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that for any constant
\gamma_{s}

\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma(u)\left(\gamma\sqrt{n}(A_{-}\bar{\pi}_{-}-A_{+}\bar{\pi}_{+})(u)+\gamma_{s}S(u)\right)\leq\varepsilon\log n\right)\leq n^{-I(\rho-1,\rho+\gamma_{s})+O(\varepsilon)}

where

I(\alpha,\beta)=\frac{1}{2}\sup_{\theta>0}a+b-(1-\delta)ae^{-\theta\alpha}-(1-\delta)be^{\theta\alpha}-\delta ae^{-\theta\beta}-\delta be^{\theta\beta}.

Proof.

We note by Definition
1
that
S(u)=\left(\sum_{v\in\mathcal{R}_{+}}A(u,v)-\sum_{v\in\mathcal{R}_{-}}A(u,v)\right)
where
|\mathcal{R}_{+}|=|\mathcal{R}_{-}|=\delta n/2
. In addition, following Definition
4
,
\gamma\sqrt{n}\bar{\pi}_{i}=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}+\rho\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{C}_{-i}}
. We show that for each
u\in\mathcal{U}
, the expression in (
6
) is equal in distribution to a difference of binomials. Rearranging the eigenvector difference

\sigma(u)\left(\sqrt{n}\gamma(A_{-}\bar{\pi}_{-}-A_{+}\bar{\pi}_{+})(u)\right)
yields

\displaystyle\sigma(u)\left(\sqrt{n}\gamma(A_{-}\bar{\pi}_{-}-A_{+}\bar{\pi}_{+})(u)\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\sigma(u)\left(\sum_{v\in\mathcal{C}_{+}}\rho A(u,v)+\sum_{v\in\mathcal{U}_{-}}A(u,v)-\sum_{v\in\mathcal{C}_{-}}\rho A(u,v)-\sum_{v\in\mathcal{U}_{+}}A(u,v)\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\sigma(u)\left((\rho-1)\left(\sum_{v\in\mathcal{U}_{+}}A(u,v)-\sum_{v\in\mathcal{U}_{-}}A(u,v)\right)+\rho\left(\sum_{v\in\mathcal{R}_{+}}A(u,v)-\sum_{v\in\mathcal{R}_{-}}A(u,v)\right)\right)

Then we add in the
\sigma(u)\gamma_{s}S(u)
term and show that the sum is equal in distribution to

\displaystyle\sigma(u)\left((\rho-1)\left(\sum_{v\in\mathcal{U}_{+}}A(u,v)-\sum_{v\in\mathcal{U}_{-}}A(u,v)\right)+(\rho+\gamma_{s})\left(\sum_{v\in\mathcal{R}_{+}}A(u,v)-\sum_{v\in\mathcal{R}_{-}}A(u,v)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad\overset{d}{=}(\rho-1)\sum_{i=1}^{(1-\delta)n/2}(W_{n/2+1-i}-Z_{n/2+1-i})+(\rho+\gamma_{s})\sum_{i=1}^{\delta n/2}(W_{i}-Z_{i})

where the
W_{i}
,
Z_{i}
are i.i.d. Bernoulli
(a\log n/n)
and Bernoulli
(b\log n/n)
respectively. We apply Lemma
8
below with
\alpha=\rho-1
,
\beta=\rho+\gamma_{s}
and choose
\varepsilon(a,b)>0
such that

\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma(u)(\gamma\sqrt{n}(A_{-}\bar{\pi}_{-}-A_{+}\bar{\pi}_{+})+\gamma_{s}S)(u)\leq\varepsilon\log n\right)\leq n^{-I(\rho-1,\rho+\gamma_{s})+O(\varepsilon)}.

for each
u\in\mathcal{U}
, where

I(\alpha,\beta)=\frac{1}{2}\sup_{\theta>0}a+b-(1-\delta)ae^{-\theta\alpha}-(1-\delta)be^{\theta\alpha}-\delta ae^{-\theta\beta}-\delta be^{\theta\beta}.\qed

We show the Chernoff bound for the difference of binomials in the following lemma.

Lemma 8
.

Suppose
a>b
, the variables
W_{i}
and
Z_{i}
are i.i.d. Bernoulli
(a\log n/n)
and Bernoulli
(b\log n/n)
and let
\delta\in(0,1)
. Then for any choice of
\gamma_{s}
, there exists an
\varepsilon>0
independent of
n
such that

\mathbb{P}\left(\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{(1-\delta)n/2}(W_{n/2+1-i}-Z_{n/2+1-i})+\beta\sum_{i=1}^{\delta n/2}(W_{i}-Z_{i})\leq\varepsilon\log n\right)\leq n^{-I(\alpha,\beta)+O(\varepsilon)}

where
\rho
is defined in Definition
4
and

I(\alpha,\beta)=\frac{1}{2}\sup_{\theta>0}a+b-(1-\delta)ae^{-\theta\alpha}-(1-\delta)be^{\theta\alpha}-\delta ae^{-\theta\beta}-\delta be^{\theta\beta}.

Proof.

We apply a Chernoff bound to see that for
\theta>0

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{(1-\delta)n/2}(W_{n/2+1-i}-Z_{n/2+1-i})+\beta\sum_{i=1}^{\delta n/2}(W_{i}-Z_{i})\leq\varepsilon\log n\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathbb{P}\left(\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{(1-\delta)n/2}(Z_{n/2+1-i}-W_{n/2+1-i})+\beta\sum_{i=1}^{\delta n/2}(Z_{i}-W_{i})\geq-\varepsilon\log n\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\theta(\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{(1-\delta)n/2}(Z_{i}-W_{i})+\beta\sum_{i=1}^{\delta n/2}(Z_{i}-W_{i}))}\right]e^{\theta\varepsilon\log n}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\theta\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{(1-\delta)n/2}(Z_{n/2+1-i}-W_{n/2+1-i})}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\theta\beta\sum_{i=1}^{\delta n/2}(Z_{i}-W_{i})}\right]e^{\theta\varepsilon\log n}

\displaystyle\qquad=e^{\theta\varepsilon\log n}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{(1-\delta)n/2}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\theta\alpha(Z_{i}-W_{i})}\right]\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{\delta n/2}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\theta\beta(Z_{i}-W_{i})}\right]

We note that

\log(\mathbb{E}\left[e^{kZ_{i}}\right])=\log(e^{k}\cdot b\frac{\log n}{n}+1-b\frac{\log n}{n})\leq(e^{k}-1)\cdot b\frac{\log n}{n}

and that

\log(\mathbb{E}\left[e^{kW_{i}}\right])=\log(e^{k}\cdot a\frac{\log n}{n}+1-a\frac{\log n}{n})\leq(e^{k}-1)\cdot a\frac{\log n}{n}.

Then taking a log of the bound tells us that

\displaystyle\log\mathbb{P}\left(\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{(1-\delta)n/2}(Z_{n/2+1-i}-W_{n/2+1-i})+\beta\sum_{i=1}^{\delta n/2}(Z_{i}-W_{i})\geq-\varepsilon\log n\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{\log n}{2}\left((1-\delta)a(e^{-\theta\alpha}-1)+(1-\delta)b(e^{\theta\alpha}-1)+\delta a(e^{-\theta\beta}-1)+\delta b(e^{\theta\beta}-1)+2\theta\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{\log n}{2}\left((1-\delta)ae^{-\theta\alpha}+(1-\delta)be^{\theta\alpha}+\delta ae^{-\theta\beta}+\delta be^{\theta\beta}-(a+b)+2\theta\varepsilon\right)

Then substituting

I(\alpha,\beta)=\frac{1}{2}\sup_{\theta>0}a+b-(1-\delta)ae^{-\theta\alpha}-(1-\delta)be^{\theta\alpha}-\delta ae^{-\theta\beta}-\delta be^{\theta\beta}

gives us the desired upper bound of

\mathbb{P}\left(\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{(1-\delta)n/2}(W_{n/2+1-i}-Z_{n/2+1-i})+\beta\sum_{i=1}^{\delta n/2}(W_{i}-Z_{i})\leq\varepsilon\log n\right)\leq n^{-I(\alpha,\beta)+O(\varepsilon)}.\qed

3.5.
Entrywise bounds

In this section, we develop the entrywise bound that states
\left\|\pi_{i}-P_{i}\bar{\pi}_{i}/\lambda_{1}\right\|_{\infty}=o(n^{-1})
with high probability. We build off the techniques in Deng et al.
[
8
]
to prove this formally.

Following the leave–one–out technique from
[
3
]
, we first bound the difference between the eigenvectors of
P_{i}
and
P_{i}^{m}
. Let
\pi_{i}^{m}
be normalized so that
\left\|\pi_{i}^{m}\right\|_{2}=1
is the eigenvector of
P_{i}^{m}
from the leave–one–out technique, where
m\in\mathcal{U}
and

P_{i}^{m}(u,v)=\begin{cases}P_{i}(u,v),&\text{if}\ u,v\neq m\\
\bar{P}_{i}(u,v),&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

As in Abbé et al.
[
3
]
, we will use the Davis–Kahan theorem to bound the eigenvector perturbation by matrix perturbations for symmetric matrices. However, because the transition matrices
P_{i}=D^{-1}A
are not symmetric, a generalization of Davis–Kahan is necessary. We use a simplified version of Theorem 3 in Deng et al.
[
8
]
for subspaces of dimension 1.

Theorem 6

(Generalized Davis-Kahan)
.

Let
M,N
be symmetric matrices with
N
positive definite, let
u
be the principal eigenvector of
N^{-1}M
, let
\hat{u}
be any vector, let
\hat{\lambda}
be a constant, and let
\kappa(N)
be the condition number of
N
. Then the angle
\theta=\arccos(\langle u,\hat{u}\rangle)
between the two vectors satisfies

\sin(\theta)\leq\frac{\kappa(N)||(N^{-1}M-\hat{\lambda}I)\hat{u}||_{2}}{|\hat{\lambda}-\lambda_{2}(N^{-1}M)|}.

Lemma 9
.

For some
C>0
, with probability
1-o(n^{-1})
we have

\max_{m\in\mathcal{V}_{i}}\left\|\pi_{i}-\pi_{i}^{m}\right\|_{2}\leq C\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

Let
\theta
be the angle between
\pi_{i}
and
\pi_{i}^{m}
. By the law of cosines and the normalization
\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{2}=\left\|\pi_{i}^{m}\right\|_{2}=1
, we get

\left\|\pi_{i}-\pi_{i}^{m}\right\|_{2}=\sqrt{2-2\cos(\theta)}\leq\sqrt{2-2\cos^{2}(\theta)}=\sqrt{2}\sin(\theta).

We use Theorem
6
to bound
\sin(\theta)
. Setting
M=A_{i}^{m},N=D_{i}^{m},\hat{\lambda}=\lambda_{1}(P_{i})
, and
\hat{u}=\pi_{i}
we get

\sin\theta\leq\frac{\kappa(D_{i}^{m})\left\|(P_{i}^{m}-P_{i})\pi_{i}\right\|_{2}}{|\lambda_{1}(P_{i})-\lambda_{2}(P_{i}^{m})|}.

We bound the denominator and numerator separately. First, by Lemma
2
,
\kappa(D_{i}^{m})\leq\frac{d_{\max}}{d_{\min}}\leq c_{1}
for some
c_{1}>0
for all
m\in\mathcal{U}
with probability
1-o(n^{-1})
. In addition, by Weyl’s inequality and Lemma
3
with probability
1-o(n^{-1})
for some
c_{2}>0

\lambda_{1}(P_{i})-\lambda_{2}(P_{i}^{m})>\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}-\left\|\bar{P}_{i}-P_{i}\right\|_{2}-\max_{m\in\mathcal{V}_{i}}\left\|\bar{P}_{i}-P_{i}^{m}\right\|_{2}\geq c_{2}.

It is sufficient to show that the numerator
(P_{i}^{m}-P_{i})\pi_{i}=O\left(\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}\right)
. To do so, we bound the
m
th entry of
(P_{i}^{m}-P_{i})\pi_{i}
and the other entries separately. For all entries
u\neq m
, we have

\left|(P_{i}^{m}-P_{i})\pi_{i}(u)\right|=\left|\left(\bar{P}_{i}(m,u)-P_{i}(m,u)\right)\pi_{i}(u)\right|\leq\left|\bar{P}_{i}(m,u)-P_{i}(m,u)\right|\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}

Otherwise, when
u=m

\displaystyle\left|(P_{i}^{m}-P_{i})\pi_{i}(m)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}_{i}}\left(\bar{P}_{i}(v,m)-P_{i}(v,m)\right)\pi_{i}(v)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}_{i}}\left|\left(\bar{P}_{i}(v,m)-P_{i}(v,m)\right)\right|\cdot\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}

Then we apply Lemma
3
to see that with probability
1-o(n^{-1})
for some
c_{3}>0

\displaystyle\left\|(P_{i}^{m}-P_{i})\pi_{i}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(2\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty})^{2}+\sum_{u\in\mathcal{V}_{i},u\neq m}\left(\bar{P}_{i}(m,u)-P_{i}(m,u)\right)^{2}\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}+\left\|\bar{P}_{i}-P_{i}\right\|_{2,\infty}^{2}\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}+\left\|\bar{P}_{i}-P_{i}\right\|_{2}^{2}\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c_{3}\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}

We conclude that for some constant
C>0
, with probability
1-o(n^{-1})

\max_{m\in\mathcal{V}_{i}}\left\|\pi_{i}-\pi_{i}^{m}\right\|_{2}\leq C\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}.\qed

Lemma 10
.

There exists a
C>0
such that with probability
1-o(n^{-1})

\left\|\pi_{i}-\bar{\pi}_{i}\right\|_{2}\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{\log n}}.

Proof.

Let
\theta
be the angle between
\pi_{i}
and
\bar{\pi}_{i}
. By the law of cosines, the normalization
\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{2}=\left\|\bar{\pi}_{i}\right\|_{2}=1
and Theorem
6
with
N=D_{i}
,
M=A_{i}
,
\hat{u}=\bar{P}_{i},
and
\hat{\lambda}=\lambda_{1}(\bar{P}_{i})
,

\left\|\pi_{i}-\bar{\pi}_{i}\right\|_{2}=\sqrt{2-2\cos(\theta)}\leq\sqrt{2-2\cos^{2}(\theta)}=\sqrt{2}\sin(\theta)\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}\kappa(D_{i})\left\|(P_{i}-\bar{P}_{i})\bar{\pi}_{i}\right\|_{2}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}(P_{i})}.

We note that

\left\|(P_{i}-\bar{P}_{i})\bar{\pi}_{i}\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|P_{i}-\bar{P}_{i}\right\|_{2}\left\|\bar{\pi}_{i}\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|P_{i}-\bar{P}_{i}\right\|_{2}

and that

\kappa(D_{i})\leq\frac{d_{\max}}{d_{\min}}\leq c_{1}
for some
c_{1}>0
with probability
1-o(n^{-1})
.

By Theorem
3
, with probability
1-o(n^{-1})

\left\|P_{i}-\bar{P}_{i}\right\|_{2}=\frac{c_{2}}{\sqrt{\log n}}.

For the denominator, applying Weyl’s inequality and Lemma
3
tells us that for some
c_{3}>0
with probability
1-o(n^{-1})

\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}(P_{i})\geq\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}-\left\|P_{i}-\bar{P}_{i}\right\|_{2}\geq c_{3}.\qed

We put together Lemmas
9
and
10
to obtain an entrywise bound on the product
A_{i}(\pi_{i}-\bar{\pi}_{i})
below.

Lemma 11
.

There exists a
C>0
such that with probability
1-o(n^{-1})

\displaystyle\left\|A_{i}(\pi_{i}-\bar{\pi}_{i})\right\|_{\infty}\leq C\log n\left(\frac{\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}}{\log\log n}+\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{n}\log\log n}\right).

Proof.

The proof closely follows the argument in Lemma 18 in
[
22
]
but we write it out here for completeness. We first bound
\left\|A_{i}(\pi_{i}-\bar{\pi}_{i})\right\|_{\infty}
.
We note that

\displaystyle\left\|A_{i}(\pi_{i}-\bar{\pi}_{i})\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\max_{m\in\mathcal{V}_{i}}|\langle A_{i}(m,\,\cdot\,),\pi_{i}-\bar{\pi}_{i}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{m\in\mathcal{V}_{i}}|\langle A_{i}(m,\,\cdot\,),\pi_{i}-\pi_{i}^{m}\rangle|+|\langle A_{i}(m,\,\cdot\,),\pi_{i}^{m}-\bar{\pi}_{i}\rangle|

(7)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{m\in\mathcal{V}_{i}}\left\|A_{i}\right\|_{2,\infty}\left\|\pi_{i}-\pi_{i}^{m}\right\|_{2}

(8)

\displaystyle\quad+\max_{m\in\mathcal{V}_{i}}|\langle\bar{A}_{i}(m,\,\cdot\,),\pi_{i}^{m}-\bar{\pi}_{i}\rangle|

(9)

\displaystyle\quad+\max_{m\in\mathcal{V}_{i}}|\langle\bar{A}_{i}(m,\,\cdot\,)-A_{i}(m,\,\cdot\,),\pi_{i}^{m}-\bar{\pi}_{i}\rangle|

For the first part of the sum (
7
), by Theorem
5
, for some
c_{1}>0

\left\|A_{i}\right\|_{2,\infty}\leq\left\|\bar{A}_{i}\right\|_{2,\infty}+\left\|A_{i}-\bar{A}_{i}\right\|_{2}\leq c_{1}\sqrt{\log n}.

and by the leave–one–out construction in Lemma
9

\max_{m\in\mathcal{V}_{i}}\left\|\pi_{i}-\pi_{i}^{m}\right\|_{2}\leq c_{2}\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}

both with probability
1-o(n^{-1})
.
Therefore,

\max_{m\in\mathcal{V}_{i}}\left\|A_{i}\right\|_{2,\infty}\left\|\pi_{i}-\pi_{i}^{m}\right\|_{2}\leq c_{1}c_{2}\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}\sqrt{\log n}.

For the second term (
8
), for some
c_{3}>0
, with probability
1-o(n^{-1})
, we apply Lemma
10
to get

\displaystyle\max_{m\in\mathcal{V}_{i}}|\langle\bar{A}_{i}(m,\,\cdot\,),\pi_{i}^{m}-\bar{\pi}_{i}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{m\in\mathcal{V}_{i}}\left\|\bar{A}_{i}\right\|_{2,\infty}\left\|\pi_{i}^{m}-\bar{\pi}_{i}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\bar{A}_{i}\right\|_{2,\infty}\left(\max_{m\in\mathcal{V}_{i}}\left\|\pi_{i}-\pi_{i}^{m}\right\|_{2}+\left\|\pi_{i}-\bar{\pi}_{i}\right\|_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{3}\log n}{\sqrt{n}}\left(\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log n}}\right)

Let
k=|\mathcal{V}_{i}|=\left(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\right)n
. The third term (
9
) uses the row-concentration property from
[
8
]
, that with probability at least
1-o(n^{-1})

\max_{m\in\mathcal{V}_{i}}|\langle(A_{i}(m,\,\cdot\,)-\bar{A}_{i}(m,\,\cdot\,)),(\pi_{i}^{m}-\bar{\pi}_{i})\rangle|\leq c_{4}\left(\max_{m\in\mathcal{V}_{i}}\left\|w\right\|_{\infty}\phi\left(\frac{\left\|w\right\|_{2}}{\sqrt{k}\left\|w\right\|_{\infty}}\right)\log n\right)

where
w=\pi_{i}^{m}-\bar{\pi}_{i}
and
\phi(t)=(1\lor\log(1/t))^{-1}
for
t>0
.

Let
x=\sqrt{k}\left\|w\right\|_{\infty}
,
y=\left\|w\right\|_{2}
, then we have by Lemma
9
with probability
1-o(n^{-1})
that

\displaystyle\max_{m\in\mathcal{V}_{i}}x

\displaystyle=\sqrt{k}\max_{m\in\mathcal{V}_{i}}\left\|\pi_{i}^{m}-\bar{\pi}_{i}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{k}\left(\max_{m\in\mathcal{V}_{i}}\left(\left\|\pi_{i}^{m}-\pi_{i}\right\|_{2}\right)+\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\bar{\pi}_{i}\right\|_{\infty}\right)

\displaystyle\leq c_{5}\sqrt{n}\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}.

In addition, by Lemmas
9
and
10
, with probability
1-o(n^{-1})

\max_{m\in\mathcal{V}_{i}}y=\max_{m\in\mathcal{V}_{i}}\left\|\pi_{i}^{m}-\bar{\pi}_{i}\right\|_{2}\leq\max_{m\in\mathcal{V}_{i}}\left\|\pi_{i}^{m}-\pi_{i}\right\|_{2}+\left\|\pi_{i}-\bar{\pi}_{i}\right\|_{2}\leq c_{6}\left(\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log n}}\right).

We see that for all possible values of
x,y
we have

\displaystyle\left\|w\right\|_{\infty}\phi\left(\frac{\left\|w\right\|_{2}}{\sqrt{k}\left\|w\right\|_{\infty}}\right)\log n

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{n}}x\phi\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{n}}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{\frac{x}{y}\geq\sqrt{\log n}}x\phi\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)+\mathbbm{1}_{\frac{x}{y}<\sqrt{\log n}}y\frac{x}{y}\phi\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{n}}\left(x\phi\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log n}}\right)+y\sqrt{\log n}\phi\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log n}}\right)\right).

as
\phi
is monotone increasing while
\phi(t)/t
is monotone decreasing.

As a result, for some constants
c_{7},C>0
, with probability
1-o(n^{-1})
we have

\displaystyle\max_{m\in\mathcal{V}_{i}}|\langle(A_{i}-\bar{A}_{i})(m,\cdot),\pi_{i}^{m}-\bar{\pi}_{i}\rangle|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq c_{4}\max_{m\in\mathcal{V}_{i}}\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{n}}\left(x\phi\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log n}}\right)+y\sqrt{\log n}\phi\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log n}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{c_{4}\log n\phi\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log n}}\right)}{\sqrt{n}}\left(c_{5}\sqrt{n}\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}+c_{6}\sqrt{\log n}\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}+c_{6}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{c_{7}\log n}{\sqrt{n}\log\log n}\left(\sqrt{n}\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}+1\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq C\left(\frac{\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}\log n}{\log\log n}+\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{n}\log\log n}\right).

as desired. We conclude that
\left\|A_{i}(\pi_{i}-\bar{\pi}_{i})\right\|_{\infty}\leq C\log n\left(\dfrac{\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}}{\log\log n}+\dfrac{\log n}{\sqrt{n}\log\log n}\right)
.
∎

We use the previous lemma to prove an explicit entrywise bound for
P_{i}(\pi_{i}-\bar{\pi}_{i})
.

Lemma 12
.

There exists a
C>0
such that with probability
1-o(n^{-1})

\left\|P_{i}(\pi_{i}-\bar{\pi}_{i})\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{n}\log\log n}.

Proof.

The result follows from Lemma
11
if we show that for some
c_{1}>0
, with probability
1-o(n^{-1})

\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{n}}.

We begin by expanding

\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}(P_{i})}P_{i}\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}(P_{i})}P_{i}\bar{\pi}_{i}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}(P_{i})}P_{i}(\pi_{i}-\bar{\pi}_{i})\right\|_{\infty}.

We bound the second term with Lemma
11
by pulling out the degree matrix
D^{-1}
and we account for dividing by the eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(P_{i})
by applying our bound in Lemma
6
. We then see that with probability
1-o(n^{-1})
, for some
c_{2}>0

\lambda_{1}(P_{i})\geq c_{2},

giving upper bounds with high probability of

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}(P_{i})}P_{i}\bar{\pi}_{i}\right\|_{\infty}\leq c_{2}\left\|P_{i}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\bar{\pi}_{1}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{c_{3}}{\sqrt{n}}.

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}(P_{i})}P_{i}(\pi_{i}-\bar{\pi}_{i})\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|D^{-1}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}(P_{i})}A_{i}(\pi_{i}-\bar{\pi}_{i})\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{c_{4}\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}}{\log\log n}+\frac{c_{4}}{\sqrt{n}\log\log n}.

We conclude that
\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}\leq c_{1}/\sqrt{n}
for some
c_{1}>0
with probability
1-o(n^{-1})
. ∎

We conclude our entrywise analysis by giving an explicit order to the following expression.

Lemma 13
.

There exists a
C>0
such that with probability
1-o(n^{-1})

\left\|\pi-\frac{P_{i}\bar{\pi}_{i}}{\lambda_{1}}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{n}\log\log n}.

Proof.

The result follows from expanding
\pi_{i}
as an eigenvector and applying Lemma
12
. In addition, we also use the bound on
\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}=O(n^{-1/2})
in Lemma
12
, as well as Lemma
6
to show that there exist
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that with probability
1-o(n^{-1})

\displaystyle\left\|\pi-\frac{P_{i}\bar{\pi}_{i}}{\lambda_{1}}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}}\left\|P_{i}(\pi_{i}-\bar{\pi}_{i})\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|P_{i}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}(P_{i})}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{n}\log\log n}+\frac{c_{2}}{\sqrt{n\log n}}.

The result follows.
∎

3.6.
Equivalence of left and right transition eigenvectors

Lemma 14
.

For connected graphs, the left eigenvector
\mu_{i}
of the transition matrix
P_{i}
satisfies
\mu_{i}\propto D_{i}\pi_{i}
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to show that
D_{i}\pi_{i}
is a left eigenvector of
P_{i}
with the eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(P_{i})
. Using that
\pi_{i}
is a principal right eigenvector of
P_{i}
we have

P_{i}^{T}D_{i}\pi_{i}=A_{i}D_{i}^{-1}D_{i}\pi_{i}=D_{i}D_{i}^{-1}A_{i}\pi_{i}=D_{i}P_{i}\pi_{i}=\lambda(P_{i})D_{i}\pi_{i}\qed

We use Lemma
14
to bound the difference between using the left and right eigenvectors for classification.

Lemma 15
.

Let
\bar{c}=\left\|\bar{A}_{i}\bar{\pi}_{i}\right\|_{1}/\sqrt{n}\log n
. Then there exists a
C>0
such that with probability
1-o(n^{-1})
we have

\left\|(\mu_{-}-\mu_{+})-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\bar{c}\sqrt{n}\log n}(D_{-}\pi_{-}-D_{+}\pi_{+})\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{C}{n\log\log n}

where the differences are defined by restricting to unseen nodes
u\in\mathcal{U}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
14
we have
\mu_{i}=D_{i}\pi_{i}/\left\|D_{i}\pi_{i}\right\|_{1}
. Let us first denote the normalizing factor for the right eigenvectors by
\tilde{c}=\bar{c}\sqrt{n}\log n/\lambda_{1}
.
By applying Lemma
14
, we see that

\displaystyle\left\|(\mu_{-}-\mu_{+})-\frac{1}{\tilde{c}}(D_{-}\pi_{-}-D_{+}\pi_{+})\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\left\|\frac{1}{\left\|D_{-}\pi_{-}\right\|_{1}}D_{-}\pi_{-}-\frac{1}{\left\|D_{-}\pi_{-}\right\|_{1}}D_{+}\pi_{+}-\frac{1}{\tilde{c}}(D_{-}\pi_{-}-D_{+}\pi_{+})\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\left\|\left(\frac{1}{\tilde{c}}-\frac{1}{\left\|D_{-}\pi_{-}\right\|_{1}}\right)D_{-}\pi_{-}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\left(\frac{1}{\tilde{c}}-\frac{1}{\left\|D_{+}\pi_{+}\right\|_{1}}\right)D_{+}\pi_{+}\right\|_{\infty}

Both terms are identically distributed by exchangeability, so it is sufficient to bound

\left\|\left(\frac{1}{\tilde{c}}-\frac{1}{\left\|D_{i}\pi_{i}\right\|_{1}}\right)D_{i}\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{\left\|D_{i}\pi_{i}\right\|_{1}\tilde{c}}\left|\left\|D_{i}\pi_{i}\right\|_{1}-\tilde{c}\right|\left\|D_{i}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}

for either community
i\in\{+,-\}
. We proceed term by term. First, we note by Lemma
12
and Lemma
2
that there exist
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that with probability
1-o(n^{-1})
,
\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}\leq c_{1}/\sqrt{n}
and
\left\|D_{i}\right\|_{\infty}=d_{\max}\leq c_{2}\log n
. Next, we have that
\tilde{c}=\left\|\tilde{A}_{i}\tilde{\pi}_{i}\right\|_{1}/\lambda_{1}=\Theta\left(1/\sqrt{n}\log n\right)
. In addition, since
\left\|\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}=O\left(1/\sqrt{n}\right)
, it holds for some
c_{3}>0
with probability
1-o(n^{-1})
,
(\left\|D_{i}\pi_{i}\right\|_{1})^{-1}\leq c_{3}/(\sqrt{n}\log n)
.
Finally, we bound
\left|\left\|D_{i}\pi_{i}\right\|_{1}-\tilde{c}\right|
by the reverse triangle inequality, Lemma
13
, and Theorem
5
. As a result, there exist
c_{4},c_{5},c_{6}>0
such that with probability
1-o(n^{-1})
we have

\displaystyle\left|\left\|D_{i}\pi_{i}\right\|_{1}-\tilde{c}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|D_{i}\pi_{i}-\frac{\bar{A}_{i}\bar{\pi}_{i}}{\lambda_{1}}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|D_{i}\pi_{i}-\frac{D_{i}P_{i}\bar{\pi}_{i}}{\lambda_{1}}\right\|_{1}+\left\|\frac{D_{i}P_{i}\bar{\pi}_{i}}{\lambda_{1}}-\frac{\bar{A}_{i}\bar{\pi}_{i}}{\lambda_{1}}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|D_{i}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\pi_{i}-\frac{P_{i}\bar{\pi}_{i}}{\lambda_{1}}\right\|_{1}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}}\left\|A_{i}\bar{\pi}_{i}-\bar{A}_{i}\bar{\pi}_{i}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq n\left\|D_{i}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\pi_{i}-\frac{P_{i}\bar{\pi}_{i}}{\lambda_{1}}\right\|_{\infty}+\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\lambda_{1}}\left\|A_{i}\bar{\pi}_{i}-\bar{A}_{i}\bar{\pi}_{i}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq c_{4}\left(n\log n\left\|\pi_{i}-\frac{P_{i}\bar{\pi}_{i}}{\lambda_{1}}\right\|_{\infty}+\sqrt{n}\left\|A_{i}-\bar{A}_{i}\right\|_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq c_{5}\left(\frac{n\log n}{\sqrt{n}\log\log n}+\sqrt{n\log n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq c_{6}\frac{\sqrt{n}\log n}{\log\log n}

Combining the bounds, there exists
C>0
such that with probability
1-o(n^{-1})
we get

\left\|\left(\frac{1}{\tilde{c}}-\frac{1}{\left\|D_{i}\pi_{i}\right\|_{1}}\right)D_{i}\pi_{i}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{C}{2}\left(\frac{1}{n\log^{2}n}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{n}\log n}{\log\log n}\cdot\log n\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right)=\frac{C}{2n\log\log n}

The result follows.
∎

3.7.
Entrywise error rate for right eigenvectors

The entrywise error rate of
Q(u)
is
\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma(u)Q(u)<0\right)
, which is bounded above by
\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma(u)Q(u)\leq\varepsilon\log n\right)
, where we include a noise term of
\varepsilon\log n
for some
\varepsilon>0
. From Lemma
15
, we note that we can approximate
Q(u)=(\mu_{-}-\mu_{+})(u)
by
d(u)(\pi_{-}-\pi_{+})(u)
up to a constant factor. To analyze both the QSD and mixed methods, we include a weight
\gamma_{s}
for a simple voting component
S(u)
and rescale the quasi-stationary component appropriately. By Definition
4
, the mean-field eigenvector
\bar{\pi}_{i}
satisfies
\gamma\sqrt{n}\bar{\pi}_{i}=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}+\rho\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{C}_{-i}}
. An additional factor of
\lambda_{1}
follows from the approximation
\pi_{i}\sim P_{i}\bar{\pi}_{i}/\lambda_{1}
inspired by the approach in
[
3
]
. Scaling by these constants gives the following lemma.

Lemma 16
.

For any unseen node
u\in\mathcal{U}
and any constant
\gamma_{s}
, we have that

\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma(u)\left(\gamma\lambda_{1}\sqrt{n}d(u)(\pi_{-}-\pi_{+})(u)+\gamma_{s}S(u)\right)\leq\varepsilon\log n\right)\leq n^{-I(\rho-1,\rho+\gamma_{s})+O(\varepsilon)}+o(n^{-1})

where

(11)

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma(u)\gamma\lambda_{1}\sqrt{n}d(u)\left(\left(\pi_{-}-\frac{P_{-}\bar{\pi}_{-}}{\lambda_{1}}\right)-\left(\pi_{+}-\frac{P_{+}\bar{\pi}_{+}}{\lambda_{1}}\right)\right)(u)\leq-\varepsilon\log n\right)

In this work, we examined quasi-stationary algorithms for community detection on Stochastic Block Model (SBM) with two balanced communities and partially revealed labels under noiseless conditions. We introduced a class of single-step estimators based on quasi-stationary distributions, which combine a spectral component and a simple voting scheme utilizing revealed nodes. By aligning a random walk intuition with a spectral algorithm, we achieved asymptotically optimal recovery rates and a useful way to combine side information from partially revealed communities. This was accomplished by extending entrywise eigenvector analysis from the adjacency and Laplacian matrices to the transition submatrices corresponding to revealed nodes within each community.

In the connected regime, tight information-theoretic limits imply that nearly all labels must be revealed to change the exact recovery threshold. Our bound in Theorem
4
for the mixed method gives the optimal asymptotic rate by a careful tuning of the weight to the quasi-stationary component. Future parameter tuning may yield non-asymptotic improvements in the error rate. In the bounded degree regime, this framework may yield additional insight for the statistical physics conjectures of Zhang, Moore, and Zdeborová.
[
23
]
on partial recovery with side information, and our experimental findings suggest significant potential gains from side information.

In future extensions of the PL–SBM model and analysis, we will consider random graphs with weighted edges, extend the approach to networks with multiple communities, and derive error bounds in the bounded degree regime.

Positive m m -homogeneous polynomial ideals
Adel Bounabab and Khalil Saadi

Abstract
. We introduce and study the concept of positive polynomial ideals between Banach lattices. The paper develops the basic principles of these classes and presents methods for constructing positive polynomial ideals from given positive operator ideals. In addition, we provide concrete examples of positive polynomial ideals that illustrate the relevance and significance of these classes.

Several attempts have been made to extend the theory of operator ideals to
nonlinear contexts. After the development of multi-ideal theory for

2.
Positive polynomial ideals

In this section, we introduce positive polynomial ideals and develop
abstract methods for their construction in the setting of
m
-homogeneous
polynomials. The positive ideal property is examined through the behavior of
associated linear operators, establishing a natural connection between the
linear and polynomial frameworks. These classes extend and complement the
theory of positive multilinear ideals studied in detail in
[
15
]

Definition 2.1
.

A positive left polynomial ideal (or positive left ideal of
m
-homogeneous
polynomials), denoted by
\mathcal{P}_{L}^{+},
is a subclass of all
continuous
m
-homogeneous polynomials from a Banach space into a Banach
lattice such that for all Banach space

and Banach lattice

the
components

\mathcal{P}_{L}^{+}\left({}^{m}X;E\right):=\mathcal{P}\left({}^{m}X;E\right)\cap\mathcal{P}_{L}^{+},

satisfy:

(i)

\mathcal{P}_{L}^{+}\left({}^{m}X;E\right)
is a linear subspace of
\mathcal{P}\left({}^{m}X;E\right)
containing the polynomials of finite rank.

(ii)
The positive ideal property: If
P\in\mathcal{P}_{L}^{+}\left({}^{m}X;E\right),u\in\mathcal{L}\left(Y;X\right)
and
v\in\mathcal{L}^{+}\left(E;F\right)
, then
v\circ P\circ u
is in
\mathcal{P}_{L}^{+}\left({}^{m}Y;F\right)
.

If
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{P}_{L}^{+}}:\mathcal{P}_{L}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
satisfies:

a)
\left(\mathcal{P}_{L}^{+}\left({}^{m}X;E\right),\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{P}_{L}^{+}}\right)
is a Banach (quasi-Banach) space for all
Banach space
X
and Banach lattice
E
,

b) The polynomial form
P^{m}:\mathbb{K}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
given by
P^{m}\left(\lambda\right)=\lambda^{m}
satisfies
\left\|P^{m}\right\|_{\mathcal{P}_{L}^{+}}=1
,

c) If
P\in\mathcal{P}_{L}^{+}\left({}^{m}X;E\right),u\in\mathcal{L}\left(Y;X\right)
and
v\in\mathcal{L}^{+}\left(E;F\right),
then

\left\|v\circ T\circ u\right\|_{\mathcal{P}_{L}^{+}}\leq\|v\|\|P\|_{\mathcal{P}_{L}^{+}}\left\|u\right\|^{m}.

The class
\left(\mathcal{P}_{L}^{+},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{P}_{L}^{+}}\right)
is a positive left Banach (quasi-Banach) polynomial ideal.

\displaystyle Q_{1}\circ\pi_{1}\left(x,y\right)+Q_{2}\circ\pi_{2}\left(x,y\right)=B\left(x,y\right).

Similarly, since

and
Q_{2}\in\mathcal{P}_{L}^{+}\left(Y;F\right),
we have

. Consequently,

B=Q_{1}\circ\pi_{1}+Q_{2}\circ\pi_{2}\in\mathcal{P}_{L}^{+}\left({}^{m}X\times Y;F\right).

A simple calculation shows that

P_{1}+P_{2}=B\circ A.

Let
P_{f}\in\mathcal{P}(^{m}E;F)
be a finite-rank operator. It can be

\varphi\in E^{\ast}

Let

By the vector space structure of

P_{f}\in\mathcal{P}_{L}^{+}\circ\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}\left({}^{m}E;F\right).
Finally, we verify

Let
\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}
be a positive right Banach ideal and
\mathcal{P}_{L}^{+}

Proposition 2.5
.

1) For every

(2.2)

Proof.

1) Let
P\in\mathcal{P}_{L}^{+}\circ\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}(^{m}E;F),u\in\mathcal{L}^{+}\left(G;E\right)

\displaystyle\|v\circ Q_{0}\|_{\mathcal{P}_{L}^{+}}\left\|u_{0}\circ u\right\|_{\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}^{m}

Taking the infimum over all representations of
P

2) Note that

\displaystyle\left\|\varphi\circ P\circ u_{0}\right\|_{\mathcal{P}_{L}^{+}\circ\mathcal{B}_{R}^{+}}

Lemma 2.6
.

The quantity

3.1.
Cohen positive strongly
p
-summing polynomials

Hamdi et al.
[
17

p

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\left\langle P\left(x_{i}\right),y_{i}^{\ast}\right\rangle\right|\leq C(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left\|x_{i}\right\|^{mp})^{\frac{1}{p}}\left\|\left(y_{i}^{\ast}\right)_{i=1}^{n}\right\|_{p^{\ast},w}.

(3.1)

The space consisting of all such mappings is denoted by
\mathcal{P}_{Coh,p}^{+}\left({}^{m}X;E\right)

d_{p}^{m+}\left(P\right)=\inf\{C>0:C\text{ {satisfies }}(\ref{3.1})\}.

Proposition 3.1
.

Directly by Proposition

Positive Cohen

Achour and Alouani
[

]
introduced the notion of Cohen
p
-nuclear
multilinear operators as a natural extension of the linear concept
originally proposed by Cohen
[
12
]
. The polynomial counterpart was
later introduced and studied in
[
6
]
. Hammou et al.
[
18
]

subsequently developed the positive version of this notion.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Let
E
and
F
be Banach
lattices. A polynomial
P\in\mathcal{P}\left({}^{m}E;F\right)

any
(x_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\subset E^{+}
and
\left(y_{i}^{\ast}\right)_{i=1}^{n}\subset F^{\ast+}

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\left\langle P\left(x_{i}\right),y_{i}^{\ast}\right\rangle\right|\leq C\sup_{x^{\ast}\in B_{E^{\ast}}^{+}}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle x^{\ast},x_{i}\right\rangle^{mp}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left\|\left(y_{i}^{\ast}\right)_{i=1}^{n}\right\|_{p^{\ast},w}.

(3.2)

The space consisting of all such mappings is denoted by
\mathcal{P}_{N\text{-}p}^{c+}\left({}^{m}E;F\right)
. In this case, we define

n_{p}^{m+}\left(P\right)=\inf\{C>0:C\text{ {satisfies }}(\ref{3.2})\}.

It is easy to verify that every Cohen
p

Proposition 3.3
.

The class
\mathcal{P}_{N\text{-}p}^{c+}
is a positive polynomial ideal
defined by,
\mathcal{P}_{N\text{-}p}^{c+}=\mathcal{P}_{Coh,p}^{+}\circ\Pi_{p}^{+}.
That is, for every pair of Banach lattices

and

\mathcal{P}_{N\text{-}p}^{c+}\left({}^{m}E;F\right)=\mathcal{P}_{Coh,p}^{+}\circ\Pi_{p}^{+}\left({}^{m}E;F\right).

Proof.

Directly by
[
18
, Theorem 9]
.
∎

3.3.
Positive
p
-dominated polynomials

The concept of positive
p
-dominated polynomials has been introduced by
Hamdi et al.
[
17
]
. A polynomial
P\in\mathcal{P}\left({}^{m}E;Y\right)
is said to be positive
p
-dominated if there exists a
constant
C>0
such that for any
(x_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\subset E^{+}
, the
following inequality holds:

(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|P\left(x_{i}\right)\right\|^{\frac{p}{m}})^{\frac{m}{p}}\leq C\sup_{x^{\ast}\in B_{E^{\ast}}^{+}}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left|x^{\ast}\left(x_{i}\right)\right|^{p})^{\frac{m}{p}}.

(3.3)

The space consisting of all such mappings is denoted by
\mathcal{P}_{d,p}^{+}\left({}^{m}E;Y\right)
. In this case, we define

\delta_{p}^{+}\left(P\right)=\inf\{C>0:C\text{ {satisfies }}(\ref{3.3})\}.

We note that
p\geq m
,
\delta_{p}^{+}\left(\cdot\right)
is a norm,
but for
p<m
, it is only a quasi-norm.

Theorem 3.4
.

[
17
, Theorem 6]
Let
1\leq p<\infty
; an
m
-homogeneous polynomial
P:E\rightarrow Y
is positive
p
-dominated if
there are
C>0
and a probability measure
\mu
on
B_{E^{\ast}}^{+}
such
that for every
x\in E^{+}

\left\|P\left(x\right)\right\|\leq C\left(\int_{B_{E^{\ast}}^{+}}\left\langle x^{\ast},x\right\rangle^{p}d\mu\right)^{\frac{m}{p}}.

Moreover, the smallest
C
is
\delta_{p}^{+}\left(P\right).

The authors in
[
17
]
did not provide a factorization result for this
class. In what follows, we present a version of Kwapień’s theorem
concerning the class of positive
p
-dominated polynomials. This allows us
to establish that this class can be interpreted through the factorization
method from the positive class
\Pi_{p}^{+}.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq p<\infty
. An
m
-homogeneous polynomial
P:E\rightarrow Y
is positive
p
-dominated if and only if, there exist a
Banach space
X
, a positive
p
-summing operator
u:E\rightarrow X,
and a
polynomial
Q\in\mathcal{P}\left({}^{m}X;Y\right)
such that

P=Q\circ u.

Moreover,

\delta_{p}^{+}(P)=\inf\left\{\left\|Q\right\|\pi_{p}^{+}\left(u\right)^{m}:P=Q\circ u\right\}.

Proof.

Let
P:E\rightarrow Y
be an
m
-homogeneous polynomial such that
P=Q\circ u
where
u\in\Pi_{p}^{+}\left(E;X\right)
and
Q\in\mathcal{P}\left({}^{m}X;Y\right).
Let
x\in E^{+}.
We have

\displaystyle\left\|P\left(x\right)\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|Q\circ u\left(x\right)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|Q\right\|\left\|u\left(x\right)\right\|^{m}.

Since
u
is positive
p
-summing, by
(\ref{DomiSumming})
we obtain

\left\|P\left(x\right)\right\|\leq\left\|Q\right\|\pi_{p}^{+}\left(u\right)^{m}\left(\int_{B_{E^{\ast}}^{+}}\left\langle x^{\ast},x\right\rangle^{p}d\mu\right)^{\frac{m}{p}}.

Then,
P
is positive
p
-dominated and

\delta_{p}^{+}(P)\leq\left\|Q\right\|\pi_{p}^{+}\left(u\right)^{m}.

Taking the infimum over all representation of
P
, we get

\delta_{p}^{+}(P)\leq\inf\left\{\left\|Q\right\|\pi_{p}^{+}\left(u\right)^{m}:P=Q\circ u\right\}.

To prove the first implication. Let
P\in\mathcal{P}_{d,p}^{+}\left({}^{m}E;Y\right).
By Theorem
3.4
, there is a probability measure
\mu
on
B_{E^{\ast}}^{+}
such that for all
x\in E^{+}
we have

\left\|P\left(x\right)\right\|\leq\delta_{p}^{+}\left(P\right)\left(\int_{B_{E^{\ast}}^{+}}\left\langle x^{\ast},x\right\rangle^{p}d\mu\right)^{\frac{m}{p}}.

We now consider the operator
u_{0}:E\rightarrow L_{p}\left(B_{E^{\ast}}^{+},\mu\right)
which is given by
u_{0}\left(x\right)(x^{\ast})=x^{\ast}(x)
. Notice that for all
x\in E^{+},
we have

\displaystyle\left\|u_{0}\left(x\right)\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{E^{\ast}}^{+}}\langle x,x^{\ast}\rangle^{p}d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|x\right\|.

Let
X=\overline{u_{0}(E)}^{L_{p}\left(B_{E^{\ast}}^{+},\mu\right)}
be
the closure in
L_{p}\left(B_{E^{\ast}}^{+},\mu\right)
of the range of
u_{0}
, and let
u:E\rightarrow X
be the induced operator. Note that
u
is
positive
p
-summing with
\pi_{p}^{+}\left(u\right)\leq 1
. Let
Q_{0}:u_{0}\left(E\right)\rightarrow Y
be the polynomial operator defined
on
u_{0}\left(E\right)
by

Q_{0}\left(u_{0}\left(x\right)\right)=P\left(x\right)

this definition makes sense because

\left\|Q_{0}\left(u_{0}\left(x\right)\right)\right\|\leq C\left\|u_{0}\left(x\right)\right\|^{m}.

It follows that
Q_{0}
is continuous on
u_{0}\left(E\right)
and has a
unique bounded polynomial extension
Q
to
X
. Finally,
P=Q\circ u
where
u\in\Pi_{p}^{+}\left(E;X\right)
and
Q\in\mathcal{P}(^{m}X;Y)
and
this ends the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
E
be a Banach lattice and
Y
a Banach space. The class
\mathcal{P}_{d,p}^{+}
is a positive right polynomial ideal obtained through the

\Pi_{p}^{+}.

Specifically, for every Banach lattice

and Banach space

The concept of absolutely

Definition 3.7
.

Let

Proposition 3.8

Let

Let
(x_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\subset E^{+}
and
\left(y_{i}^{\ast}\right)_{i=1}^{n}\subset F^{\ast+}.
Then

\displaystyle(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left|\left\langle v\circ P\circ u\left(x_{i}\right),y_{i}^{\ast}\right\rangle\right|^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left|\left\langle P\circ u\left(x_{i}\right),v^{\ast}\circ y_{i}^{\ast}\right\rangle\right|^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{d\left(q;r\right)}^{+}(P)\left\|\left(u\left(x_{i}\right)\right)_{i=1}^{n}\right\|_{q,w}^{m}\left\|\left(v^{\ast}\circ y_{i}^{\ast}\right)_{i=1}^{n}\right\|_{r,w}

\displaystyle d_{d\left(q;r\right)}^{+}(P)\left\|u\right\|^{m}\left\|\left(x_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{n}\right\|_{q,w}^{m}\left\|v^{\ast}\right\|\left\|\left(y_{i}^{\ast}\right)_{i=1}^{n}\right\|_{r,w}

\displaystyle\left\|v\right\|d_{d\left(q;r\right)}^{+}(P)\left\|u\right\|^{m}\left\|\left(x_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{n}\right\|_{q,w}^{m}\left\|\left(y_{i}^{\ast}\right)_{i=1}^{n}\right\|_{r,w}

(q;r)

∎

By Theorem

\mu
on

\eta
on

Define the operator
u_{0}:E\rightarrow L_{q}\left(B_{E^{\ast}}^{+},\mu\right)

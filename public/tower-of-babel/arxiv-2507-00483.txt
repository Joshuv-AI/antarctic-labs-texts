Explicit conserved operators for a class of integrable bosonic networks from the classical Yang-Baxter equation
Phillip S. Isaac, Jon Links, Inna Lukyanenko, Jason L. Werry

Abstract
Let B B denote the weighted adjacency matrix of a balanced, symmetric, bipartite graph. We define a class of bosonic networks given by Hamiltonians whose hopping terms are determined by B B . We show that each quantum Hamiltonian is Yang-Baxter integrable, admitting a set of mutually commuting operators derived through a solution of the classical Yang-Baxter equation. We discuss some applications and consequences of this result.

To define the class of models, let

\displaystyle B=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&|&{\mathcal{B}}\\
-&&-\\
{\mathcal{B}}&|&0\end{array}\right)

is a real, symmetric matrix. There are no other constraints placed on

Let

denote mutually commuting sets of canonical boson operators satisfying

\displaystyle[a_{j},\,a_{k}^{\dagger}]=[b_{j},\,b_{k}^{\dagger}]=\delta_{jk}I,

(4)

\displaystyle[a_{j},\,a_{k}]=[a_{j}^{\dagger},\,a_{k}^{\dagger}]=[b_{j},\,b_{k}]=[b^{\dagger}_{j},\,b_{k}^{\dagger}]=0,

Define the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{U}(N^{2}_{a}+N^{2}_{b}-I)+\sum_{j,k=1}^{m}{\mathcal{B}}^{k}_{j}(a_{j}^{\dagger}b_{k}+b_{j}^{\dagger}a_{k})

(5)

\displaystyle N_{a}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}^{\dagger}a_{j},\,N_{b}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{j}^{\dagger}b_{j}

A remarkable feature of the model was observed in
[

Since (

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{{\mathcal{N}}=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}({\mathcal{N}})

Now,

\mathcal{H}(1)=B\cong\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}{\mathcal{B}}&|&0\\
-&&-\\

is embedded into the action of (

[

5

In Sect. 2 we provide preliminary background to formulate the results on the framework of Lie algebras and dual Poisson structures. Using the classical Yang-Baxter equation allows for the generation of a commutative Poisson subalgebra, representing conservation laws in an abstract dynamical system. This formalism is shown to be realised through the Lie algebra

In Sect. 3 these procedures are implemented for a specific solution of the classical Yang-Baxter equation. It is described how this solution is amenable for the construction of (

2.1
Lie algebras and their dual Poisson algebras

Here we follow the frameworks set out in
[
1
,
57
,
67
]
.
Let
\mathfrak{g}
denote a Lie algebra with basis
\{X_{j}:j=1,\dots,d\}
and structure constants

\{c_{jk}^{l}:j,k,l=1,\dots,d\}
such that the Lie bracket

\displaystyle[X_{j},\,X_{k}]=\sum_{l=1}^{d}c_{jk}^{l}X_{l}

holds. Let
U(\mathfrak{g})
denote the universal enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
with identity element
I
.
We remark that
U(\mathfrak{g})
naturally acquires a Lie algebra structure with commutation relations iteratively evaluated via the Leibniz property; i.e. for
F,G,H\in\,U(\mathfrak{g})

\displaystyle[F,\,GH]=[F,\,G]H+G[F,\,H].

(6)

We say that
F,G\in\,U(\mathfrak{g})

commute
if

\displaystyle[F,\,G]=0.

Let
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
denote the dual algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, with basis
\{\mathcal{X}_{j}:j=1,\dots,d\}
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{j}(X_{k})=\delta_{jk}.

Let
P(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
denote the associated polynomial algebra generated by
\{\mathcal{X}_{j}:j=1,\dots,d\}
, equipped with a Poisson algebra structure through the bracket

\displaystyle\{f,\,g\}=\sum_{j,k,l=1}^{d}c_{jk}^{l}\mathcal{X}_{l}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\mathcal{X}_{j}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial\mathcal{X}_{k}}.

(7)

Note

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{X}_{j},\,\mathcal{X}_{k}\}=\sum_{l=1}^{d}c_{jk}^{l}\mathcal{X}_{l}

(8)

and that the Leibniz property

\displaystyle\{f,\,gh\}=\{f,\,g\}h+g\{f,\,h\}

(9)

holds. We say that
f,g\in\,P(\mathfrak{g}^{*})

Poisson-commute
if

\displaystyle\{f,\,g\}=0

(10)

and a set of elements that pair-wise Poisson-commute is a
commutative Poisson subalgebra
.

Let
P_{k}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
denote the space of homogeneous polynomials of degree
k
, satisfying

\displaystyle\{P_{j}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}),\,P_{k}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})\}\subseteq P_{j+k-1}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})

as a result of (
7
). In particular we have

\displaystyle\{P_{1}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}),\,P_{k}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})\}\subseteq P_{k}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}).

(11)

Eq. (
8
) shows that the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is canonically embedded in
P(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
as

P_{1}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
, in that the mapping
\mathcal{X}_{j}\mapsto X_{j}
between basis elements provides a Lie algebra isomorphism. For
\mathcal{Y}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{Y}_{k}\in\,\mathfrak{g}^{*}
let the corresponding images under this isomorphism be denoted
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{k}\in\,\mathfrak{g}
. Let
S_{k}
denote the symmetric group on
k
objects. Define the vector space isomorphism
\iota:P(\mathfrak{g}^{*})\rightarrow U(\mathfrak{g})
via the following action on products of elements in

\mathfrak{g}^{*}

\displaystyle\iota(\mathcal{Y}_{1}\dots\mathcal{Y}_{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}Y_{\sigma(1)}\dots Y_{\sigma(k)},

(12)

\displaystyle\iota(1)

\displaystyle=I,

and extended linearly to all of
P(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
. Above,
S_{k}
denote the group of permutations on
k
objects. Set
U_{k}(\mathfrak{g})=\iota(P_{k}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}))
. It then follows
[
67
]

\displaystyle U(\mathfrak{g})=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}U_{k}(\mathfrak{g})

provides a
\mathfrak{g}
-module decomposition of the universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})
in view of (
11
).

The action

\displaystyle\tilde{\sigma}(a)=-a.

on
\mathfrak{g}
is an anti-involution, i.e.
\tilde{\sigma}^{2}={\rm id}
and

\displaystyle\tilde{\sigma}([a,\,b])=[\tilde{\sigma}(b),\,\tilde{\sigma}(a)],\qquad a,b\in\,\mathfrak{g}.

This action extends to
U(\mathfrak{g})
such that

\displaystyle\tilde{\sigma}(U_{k}(\mathfrak{g}))=(-1)^{k}U_{k}(\mathfrak{g}).

Now take
F\in U_{j}(\mathfrak{g})
and
G\in U_{k}(\mathfrak{g})
. With respect to commutation relations within
U(\mathfrak{g})
we have the anti-involution property

\displaystyle\tilde{\sigma}([F,\,G])=[\tilde{\sigma}(G),\,\tilde{\sigma}(F)].

Define
\sigma=-\tilde{\sigma}
. Then
\sigma
is an involution, viz.

\displaystyle{\sigma}([F,\,G])=[{\sigma}(F),\,{\sigma}(G)].

In summary, the action of
\sigma
given by

\displaystyle{\sigma}(U_{k}(gl(n))=(-1)^{k+1}U_{k}(gl(n))

is a automorphism on
U(\mathfrak{g})
that provides a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading with respect to the
commutator
action. Note that it is not an automorphism with respect to
multiplication
within
U(\mathfrak{g})
, since for example

\sigma(I)=-I
.

2.2
Classical Yang-Baxter equation for generating commutative Poisson subalgebras

Here we follow the framework set out in
[
25
,
50
,
51
]
. Let
V
denote a complex vector space of dimension
n
, and let
r(u,v)\in\,{\rm End}(V\otimes V)
with
u,v\in\,{\mathbb{C}}
. Then
r(u,v)
is termed an
r
-
matrix
if it is a solution of the classical Yang-Baxter equation

\displaystyle[r_{12}(u,v),\,r_{23}(v,w)]+[r_{13}(u,w),\,r_{23}(v,w)]-[r_{21}(v,u),\,r_{13}(u,w)]=0

(13)

acting on
V\otimes V\otimes V
. Above, the subscripts on
r(u,v)
denote the embedding of the action within the three-fold tensor product space.

Introduce the set of generating elements
\{\mathcal{T}^{j}_{k}(u):j,k=1,\dots n\}
for an abstract Poisson algebra
{\mathcal{P}}
and set

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(u)=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}e^{k}_{j}\otimes\mathcal{T}^{j}_{k}(u).

A Poisson bracket is imposed according to

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{T}_{1}(u),\,\mathcal{T}_{2}(v)\}=[\mathcal{T}_{2}(v),\,r_{12}(u,v)]-[\mathcal{T}_{1}(u),\,r_{21}(v,u)].

(14)

In component form this reads

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{T}^{j}_{k}(u),\,\mathcal{T}^{p}_{q}(v)\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}\left(r^{jp}_{k\alpha}(u,v)\mathcal{T}^{\alpha}_{q}(v)-r^{j\alpha}_{kq}(u,v)\mathcal{T}^{p}_{\alpha}(v)+r^{p\alpha}_{qk}(v,u)\mathcal{T}^{j}_{\alpha}(u)-r^{pj}_{q\alpha}(v,u)\mathcal{T}^{\alpha}_{k}(u)\right).

The Jacobi identity

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{T}^{j}_{k}(u),\,\{\mathcal{T}^{p}_{q}(v),\,\mathcal{T}^{a}_{b}(w)\}\}+\{\mathcal{T}^{p}_{q}(v),\,\{\mathcal{T}^{a}_{b}(w),\,\mathcal{T}^{j}_{k}(u)\}\}+\{\mathcal{T}^{a}_{b}(w),\,\{\mathcal{T}^{j}_{k}(u),\,\mathcal{T}^{p}_{q}(v)\}\}=0

for the Poisson bracket holds as a result of (
13
).

Define

\displaystyle(\mathcal{T}^{(1)})^{j}_{k}(u)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}^{j}_{k}(u),

\displaystyle(\mathcal{T}^{(s+1)})^{j}_{k}(u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{n}(\mathcal{T}^{(s)})^{j}_{l}(u)\mathcal{T}^{l}_{k}(u)=\sum_{l=1}^{n}\mathcal{T}^{j}_{l}(u)(\mathcal{T}^{(s)})^{l}_{k}(u),

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{(s)}(u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\mathcal{T}^{(s)})^{j}_{j}(u).

Then through use of (
9
) and (
14
) it is found that for
s>1

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{T}^{j}_{k}(u),\,\mathcal{I}^{(s)}(v)\}

\displaystyle=s\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}\{\mathcal{T}^{j}_{k}(u),\,\mathcal{T}^{p}_{q}(v)\}(\mathcal{T}^{(s-1)})^{q}_{p}(v)

\displaystyle=s\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}\left(r^{jp}_{k\alpha}(u,v)(\mathcal{T}^{(s)})^{\alpha}_{p}(v)-r^{j\alpha}_{kq}(u,v)(\mathcal{T}^{(s)})^{q}_{\alpha}(v)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+s\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}\left(r^{p\alpha}_{qk}(v,u)\mathcal{T}^{j}_{\alpha}(u)(\mathcal{T}^{(s-1)})^{q}_{p}(v)-r^{pj}_{q\alpha}(v,u)\mathcal{T}^{\alpha}_{k}(u)(\mathcal{T}^{(s-1)})^{q}_{p}(v)\right)

leading to

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{I}^{(r)}(u),\,\mathcal{I}^{(s)}(v)\}

\displaystyle=r\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\{\mathcal{T}^{j}_{k}(u),\,\mathcal{I}^{(s)}(v)\}(\mathcal{T}^{(r-1)})^{k}_{j}(u)

\displaystyle=0.

(15)

Formally, taking the Laurent series

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{(s)}(u)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathcal{I}_{j}^{(s)}u^{j}

it follows that

\{\mathcal{I}_{j}^{(r)},\,\mathcal{I}_{k}^{(s)}\}=0
; i.e. these elements form a commutative Poisson subalgebra.

2.3
Shift elements and realisation of the commutative Poisson subalgebra

We take the standard

basis elements

\{E^{j}_{k}:j,k=1,\dots,n\}
with commutation relations

\displaystyle[E^{j}_{k},\,E^{p}_{q}]=\delta^{p}_{k}E^{j}_{q}-\delta^{j}_{q}E^{p}_{k},

and let
\{\mathcal{E}^{j}_{k}:j,k=1,\dots,n\}
denote the dual-space basis with Poisson bracket

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{E}^{j}_{k},\,\mathcal{E}^{p}_{q}\}=\delta^{p}_{k}\mathcal{E}^{j}_{q}-\delta^{j}_{q}\mathcal{E}^{p}_{k}.

(16)

For later use we introduce the algebra involution

\displaystyle\theta(E^{j}_{k})=-E^{k}_{j},

(17)

extended to all of
U(gl(n))
such that

\displaystyle{\theta}([a,\,b])=[{\theta}(a),\,{\theta}(b)]

holds for all
a,\,b\in U(gl(n))
.

Let
B(u)\in{\rm End}(V)
satisfy

\displaystyle[B_{2}(v),\,r_{12}(u,v)]=[B_{1}(u),\,r_{21}(v,u)],

(18)

which we refer to as a
shift
element following
[
52
]
.
It is straightforward to check, as a result of (

,

) that the mapping

\displaystyle\varrho(\mathcal{T}^{j}_{k}(u))=B^{j}_{k}(u)+\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}r^{jp}_{kq}(u,v)\mathcal{E}^{q}_{p}

(19)

preserves the Poisson bracket relation (

). This realisation of the
\mathcal{T}^{j}_{k}(u)
in turn affords a realisation to generate a commutative Poisson subalgebra in
{\mathcal{P}}(gl(n)^{*})
.

2.4
Quantisation

An attempt to quantise the above considerations, by replacing Poisson brackets with commutators, faces a roadblock. Introduce the set of generating elements
\{T^{j}_{k}(u):j,k=1,\dots n\}
for an abstract classical Yang-Baxter algebra
Y
and set

\displaystyle T(u)=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}e^{k}_{j}\otimes T^{j}_{k}(u).

The Lie bracket is imposed according to

\displaystyle[T_{1}(u),\,T_{2}(v)]=[T_{2}(v),\,r_{12}(u,v)]-[T_{1}(u),\,r_{21}(v,u)].

(20)

In component form this reads

\displaystyle[T^{j}_{k}(u),\,T^{p}_{q}(v)]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}\left(r^{jp}_{k\alpha}(u,v)T^{\alpha}_{q}(v)-r^{j\alpha}_{kq}(u,v)T^{p}_{\alpha}(v)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}\left(r^{p\alpha}_{qk}(v,u)T^{j}_{\alpha}(u)-r^{pj}_{q\alpha}(v,u)T^{\alpha}_{k}(u)\right).

(21)

The Jacobi identity

\displaystyle[T^{j}_{k}(u),\,[T^{p}_{q}(v),\,T^{a}_{b}(w)]]+[T^{p}_{q}(v),\,[T^{a}_{b}(w),\,T^{j}_{k}(u)]]+[T^{a}_{b}(w),\,[T^{j}_{k}(u),\,T^{p}_{q}(v)]]=0

(22)

for the Lie bracket holds due to (
13
).

As a result of (
13
) and (
20
), it is seen that the
r
-matrix affords the
defining realisation

\pi_{v}
of
Y
through the mapping

\displaystyle{\pi}_{v}(T^{j}_{k}(u))=\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}r^{jp}_{kq}(u,v)E^{q}_{p},

The shift element equation (
18
) has the following interpretation in
Y

[
33
]
.
For
Z(u)\in{\rm End}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
define an action on
r(u,v)
through

\displaystyle(Z\circ r)_{12}(u,v)=[Z_{2}(v),\,r_{12}(u,v)]-[Z_{1}(u),\,r_{21}(v,u)],

which has the following skew-symmetry

\displaystyle(Z\circ r)_{12}(u,v)=-(Z\circ r)_{21}(v,u).

Symmetries of
r(u,v)
are identified as those
Z(u)
for which the skew-symmetric action is identically zero,
coinciding with (
18
).
For any one-dimensional realisation
\eta:Y\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
we have

\displaystyle\eta\left([T^{j}_{k}(u),\,T^{p}_{q}(v)]\right)=0.

It follows that if
B(u)
satisfies (
18
), the mapping given by

\displaystyle\eta(T^{j}_{k}(u))=B^{j}_{k}(u)

preserves the commutation relations (
21
), thus providing a one-dimensional realisation.
Such one-dimensional realisations encode the symmetries of
r(u,v)
in a generalised sense, and will play a significant role in later calculations. Finally, taking the tensor product of a one-dimensional realisation with the defining realisation yields a realisation expressible as

\displaystyle\rho_{v}(T^{j}_{k}(u))

\displaystyle=\eta(T^{j}_{k}(u))I+\pi_{v}(T^{j}_{k}(u))

\displaystyle=B^{j}_{k}(u)I+\sum_{p,q=1}^{n}r^{jp}_{kq}(u,v)E^{q}_{p}.

(23)

Now, following the procedures of Sect.
2.3
further by defining

\displaystyle(T^{(1)})^{j}_{k}(u)

\displaystyle=T^{j}_{k}(u),

\displaystyle(T^{(s+1)})^{j}_{k}(u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{n}(T^{(s)})^{j}_{l}(u)T^{l}_{k}(u)=\sum_{l=1}^{n}T^{j}_{l}(u)(T^{(s)})^{l}_{k}(u),

\displaystyle t^{(s)}(u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\mathcal{T}^{(s)})^{j}_{j}(u)

(24)

it is generally the case that

\displaystyle\neq 0.

This has been explicitly demonstrated in the study

3.1
Solution of the classical Yang-Baxter equation, shift element, and the Hamiltonian

In order to apply the above considerations to investigate the conserved operators for the Hamiltonian (

Let

\displaystyle P({\mathbf{x}}\otimes{\mathbf{y}})={\mathbf{y}}\otimes{\mathbf{x}},\qquad{\mathbf{x}},\,{\mathbf{y}}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}.

Set

\displaystyle r(u,v)=\frac{u}{2}\left(\frac{1}{u-v}I\otimes I+\frac{1}{u+v}A\otimes A\right)P.

It may be checked directly that (

.
Next, we turn towards the shift element.
Setting

)
holds provided

. These conditions are satisfied by choosing

and

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=-I\otimes\sigma^{z},

(26)

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle={\mathcal{B}}\otimes\sigma^{x}

(27)

for

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\frac{u^{2}(vu^{-1})^{[j+k]}}{u^{2}-v^{2}}e^{j}_{k}\otimes e^{k}_{j},

\displaystyle[T^{j}_{k}(u),\,T^{p}_{q}(v)]

\displaystyle=\delta^{p}_{k}\frac{u^{2}(vu^{-1})^{[j+k]}}{u^{2}-v^{2}}T^{j}_{q}(v)-\delta^{j}_{q}\frac{u^{2}(vu^{-1})^{[j+k]}}{u^{2}-v^{2}}T^{p}_{k}(v)

\displaystyle\qquad+\delta^{p}_{k}\frac{v^{2}(uv^{-1})^{[p+q]}}{v^{2}-u^{2}}T^{j}_{q}(u)-\delta^{j}_{q}\frac{v^{2}(uv^{-1})^{[p+q]}}{v^{2}-u^{2}}T^{p}_{k}(u)

\displaystyle B^{2p}_{2q-1}=B^{2p-1}_{2q}={\mathcal{B}}^{p}_{q}

5
Discussion

The main objective of this work was to provide explicit forms for the conserved operators of the Hamiltonian
(
5
). These conserved operators are expressed through (
42
) and (
43
). They were obtained by quantisation of classical counterparts obtained through a Poisson algebra approach.
These results confirm that the models studied in
[
73
]
associated with complete bipartite graphs generalise to models associated with arbitrary bipartite graphs.

The elements of the commutative Poisson subalgebra were obtained as the leading-order terms in the expansions
(
34
). Even though only leading order terms were taken, these are sufficient to provide a set of algebraically independent conserved operators with cardinality equal to
2m
, the number of degrees of freedom for the system. When
{\mathcal{B}}
is generic as defined in Sect.
3.3
, the method of proof follows the lines proposed in
[
34
]
, with detailed analysis to be provided in a future work.

This finding opens avenues for the investigation of models defined on well-recognised lattice structures. For instance, the case of the square lattice is presented in
[
35
]
. The explicit form for the Bethe Ansatz solution is described for open, cylindrical, and toroidal boundary conditions. The problem of incorporating an integrable defect into the model is also discussed.

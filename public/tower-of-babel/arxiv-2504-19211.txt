A class of thin-film equations with space-time dependent gradient nonlinearity and its application to image sharpening
Yuhang Li a {}^{\ \textrm{a}}, Zhichang Guo \cormark a , ​ [ 1 ] {}^{\ \textrm{a},}\cormark[1], Fanghui Song a {}^{\ \textrm{a}}, Boying Wu a {}^{\ \textrm{a}}, Bin Guo b {}^{\ \textrm{b}}

Abstract
We introduce a class of thin-film equations with space-time dependent gradient nonlinearity and apply them to image sharpening. By modifying the potential well method, we overcome the challenges arising from variable exponents and the “moving" Nehari manifold, thereby providing a classification of global existence and finite time blow-up of solutions under different initial conditions. Decay rates for global solutions and lifespan estimates for blow-up solutions are also established. Numerical experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of this class of equations as image sharpening models.

Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
(
N\geq 2
) be a bounded domain.
For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we consider the following
initial-boundary value problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}u=\operatorname{div}\left(F(t,x,\nabla u)\right),&x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\\
u=0,&x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

Here,
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}=(-\Delta)^{2}+\alpha(-\Delta)^{2s}
is a
mixed local-nonlocal biharmonic operator,
s\in(0,1)
,

F(t,x,\xi)=\xi-k(t)|\xi|^{p(x)-2}\xi
,
p:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to(2,+\infty)
.
This type of problem appears in the modeling of epitaxial
thin film growth. On a square domain, letting
u(t,x)
denote the height of the thin film,
the continuum model for epitaxial thin film growth is given by
[
81
]
:

u_{t}=-\operatorname{div}(\mathbf{j})+g+\eta,

(1.2)

where
\mathbf{j}=\mathbf{j}(t,x)
,
g=g(t,x)
,
\eta=\eta(t,x)
denotes
all process of moving atoms along the surface,
the deposition flux and Gaussian noise, respectively.
One of the most widespread treatments is to neglect the noise term and assume

[
81
,
52
]

\mathbf{j}=A_{1}\nabla u+A_{2}\nabla(\Delta u)+A_{3}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u,

so that (
1.2
) reduces to

u_{t}+A_{1}\Delta u+A_{2}\Delta^{2}u+A_{3}\operatorname{div}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)=g.

(1.3)

In this case, the term
A_{1}\Delta u
corresponds to diffusion driven
by evaporation-condensation,
A_{2}\Delta^{2}u
represents capillarity-driven
surface diffusion, and
A_{3}\operatorname{div}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)

accounts for the upward hopping of atoms
[
51
,
15
]
.

The intriguing mathematical properties of (
1.3
) have attracted extensive
research interest over the past few decades. King et al.
[
37
]

studied the global existence and asymptotic behavior of solutions to (
1.3
)
in the case where
A_{1}>0,A_{2}>0,A_{3}<0
and
g=0
. Later, Kohn et al.
[
38
]

analyzed the coarsening behavior of solutions under the same setting.
Sandjo et al.
[
60
]
investigated the local well-posedness in
L^{p}

space of (
1.3
) when
A_{1}=0,A_{2}>0,A_{3}\neq 0
; afterward, Li et al.
[
44
]

established well-posedness results in local BMO space for
p=4
and further studied
the stability for global solutions for small initial data in VMO space. Related problems
include variants of (
1.3
) involving nonlinear source terms

[
43
,
34
,
41
]
, time-dependent
coefficients
[
56
,
57
,
84
]
,
or Hessians
[
23
,
47
]
.

In recent years, particular attention
has been devoted to the case where
A_{2}>0
and
A_{3}>0
. Ishige
[
35
]

et al. studied the local existence and sufficient conditions for blow-up of solutions
to the Cauchy problem for

u_{t}+(-\Delta)^{2}u=-\operatorname{div}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\right),

(1.4)

on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Feng et al.
[
24
]
investigated the global existence and
blow-up for (
1.4
) with and without advection on the
torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. Miyake et al.
[
50
]
first established the asymptotic
behavior of global solutions with subcritical initial energy under Neumann boundary
conditions, and Zhao et al.
[
83
]
subsequently completed the classification of
global existence and blow-up for this problem. The problem (
1.1
) studied in this
paper corresponds to the case
A_{1}<0
,
A_{2}>0
and
A_{3}>0
. Specifically, by
setting
\alpha=0
and taking
F(t,x,\xi)=-|\xi|^{p-2}\xi
in (
1.1
), we
recover the form of (
1.4
).

We claim that (
1.1
) can serve as an image sharpening model.
Image sharpening refers to the enhancement of edges in an image to make it
clearer and more visually appealing. Unlike image restoration, which
emphasizes fidelity to a clean reference image, sharpening focuses more
on improving the overall visual clarity
[
30
]
.
It is commonly used in scenarios where images are intended for public
display and aesthetic presentation. The simplest sharpening PDE
is the linear backward diffusion equation, which is ill-posed and
extremely sensitive to noise in the image, and thus requiring
some form of stabilization or regularization techniques in practical computations

[
54
,
69
,
73
]
.
Aside from linear backward diffusion, other well-known sharpening PDEs include
nonlinear backward diffusion equations
[
58
,
3
,
62
]
,
anisotropic forward-backward diffusion equations

[
28
,
29
,
7
]
, and a class of nonlinear hyperbolic
equations known as shock filters
[
53
]
.

Problem (
1.1
) involves a mixed local-nonlocal biharmonic operator and a
space-time dependent nonlinear gradient term. Early works

[
79
,
46
,
4
,
33
,
42
]

employed fourth-order PDEs for image restoration,
demonstrating their effectiveness in avoiding staircase effects and preserving
ramps (affine regions). A recent study
[
66
]
applied a thin film-type
equation to image segmentation and
reported favorable results. As nonlocal operators have been found to be effective
in texture preservation
[
27
,
9
,
68
,
78
,
71
]
,
mixed local and nonlocal operators have recently attracted
increasing attention in image processing

[
11
,
16
,
64
,
65
]
. Moreover, spatially or
space-time dependent differential operators are commonly used in image processing models as
they can more effectively exploit the intrinsic features of the image and
improve the self-adaptability of models

[
13
,
32
,
70
,
63
,
76
]
.

Inspired by the above works and ideas, we propose (
1.1
) as an image sharpening model,
which can be analyzed as follows:
if we disregard the term
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}u
on the left-hand side
and focus on the right-hand side term

\operatorname{div}\left((1-k(t)|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2})\nabla u\right)
, one can
identify a space-time dependent threshold function

\mathfrak{T}(t,x)=(k(t))^{\frac{1}{2-p(x)}}
, such that:
when
|\nabla u|<\mathfrak{T}(t,x)
, the diffusion coefficient
1-k(t)|\nabla u|^{p(x)-2}

is positive, exhibiting forward diffusion behavior;
when
|\nabla u|>\mathfrak{T}(t,x)
, the coefficient becomes negative,
leading to backward diffusion.
By appropriately designing the functions
k(t)
and
p(x)
, one can control
the evolution process and ensure that backward diffusion occurs primarily near image edges,
thereby achieving edge enhancement.
The term
\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}u
, on the other hand, acts as a viscosity-type term
that helps prevent the model from becoming ill-posed and reduces the influence of noise during
the sharpening process. By tuning the parameter
\alpha
, one can achieve edge enhancement
without amplifying noise in smooth regions, and maintaining fine details such as ramps and
textures.

The main contribution of this paper is the theoretical analysis of problem (
1.1
),
especially the classification of global existence and finite time blow-up, which can be
regarded as an extension and continuation of previous works
[
50
,
83
]

on (
1.4
). The analyses in
[
50
]
and
[
83
]
are
mainly based on the potential well method, originally introduced by Sattinger and Payne

[
61
,
55
]
and modified by Liu et al.
[
45
]
,
Gazzola et al.
[
26
]
and Xu et al.
[
74
]
. Besides its
application to thin-film equations
[
23
,
43
,
22
,
34
,
50
,
83
]
, the potential well method has also been
utilized in the study of other types of equations, such as reaction-diffusion equations

[
36
,
72
,
19
,
31
]
,
pseudo-parabolic equations
[
10
,
67
,
12
,
80
]
, and wave
equations
[
25
,
75
,
82
,
20
]
.
For problem (
1.1
), the application of the potential well method faces two main
difficulties. First, the time-dependent coefficient
k(t)
result in a “moving" Nehari
manifold, which brings challenges to the verification of invariant sets and the analysis
for high initial energy cases. Second, the presence of variable exponents makes an
explicit expression for the potential well depth
d(t)
cannot generally be obtained,
and additional technical challenges are introduced in the derivation of differential
inequalities. In this paper, we incorporate techniques from

[
36
,
67
,
31
]
, further modify the classical potential well
method to overcome these difficulties and combine it with the Galerkin method, the differential
inequality technique, and Levine’s concavity argument
[
40
]
to classify
the global existence and finite time blow-up of solutions. Moreover,
we investigate decay rates for global solutions as well as
lifespan estimates for blow-up solutions.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Sect.
2
, we introduce necessary
notations, definitions, assumptions and establish a series of preliminary results for potential
wells that will be used later. Section
3
establishes the local existence of
solutions to (
1.1
). In Sect.
4
, we provide threshold results for the
solutions to exist globally or to blow up in finite time when
J(u_{0};0)\leq\underline{d}
,
and get a decay rate of the
H^{2}
-norm for global solutions.
Section
5
gives sufficient conditions for global existence
when
J(u_{0};0)\geq\overline{d}
and for finite time blow-up when
J(u_{0};0)>0

(the notations
J(u_{0};0)
,
\underline{d}
and
\overline{d}
will be defined in Sect.
2
).
A lower bound estimate for the blow-up time is established in Sect.
6
.
Finally, in Sect.
7
, we propose a numerical scheme for solving (
1.1
) and
present several numerical examples, where experiments on images are conducted to verify
the effectiveness of (
1.1
) as an image sharpening model.

2.2
The potential well framework

Let us start with making the following assumptions on
p,\alpha
and the function
k(t)
:

(H1)

p\in\mathcal{P}^{\log}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is bounded and

N\geq 3
.

(H2)

Either
\alpha>-\lambda_{1}^{1-s}(1-s)^{1-s}s^{-s}
, or

\alpha>-(2-2s)^{2s-2}(2s-1)^{1-2s}
with
s\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)
.
Here
\lambda_{1}
denotes the principle eigenvalue of the Dirichlet problem

-\Delta v=\lambda v~~\textrm{in}~~\Omega,\quad v=0~~\textrm{on}~~\partial\Omega.

(H3)

k\in C^{1}([0,+\infty))
,
k(0)>0
and for every
t\geq 0
,
k^{\prime}(t)\geq 0
.

For
v\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
and
any
t\geq 0
, the time-dependent energy functional and Nehari functional associated with
problem (
1.1
) are given by

J(v;t)=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta v\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|(-\Delta)^{s}v\|_{2}^{2}-k(t)\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{p(x)}|\nabla v|^{p(x)}dx,

and

I(v;t)=\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}+\|\Delta v\|_{2}^{2}+\alpha\|(-\Delta)^{s}v\|_{2}^{2}-k(t)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p(x)}dx.

(H1) ensures that both
J(v;t)
and
I(v;t)
are well-defined. It is not difficult to verify
that for each
t\geq 0
, both
J(\cdot;t)
and
I(\cdot;t)
are continuous
on
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
. (H2) enables us to obtain the following result concerning the equivalence
of norms on
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let (H2) be fulfilled,
v\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
. Then

\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}+\|\Delta v\|_{2}^{2}+\alpha\|(-\Delta)^{s}v\|_{2}^{2}\sim\|v\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}.

(2.3)

Proof.

To see this we use interpolation arguments
[
14
,
8
]
.
For all
\varepsilon>0
, using
Plancherel’s theorem in combination with Hölder’s and Young’s inequality we have

\displaystyle\|(-\Delta)^{s}v\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\xi|^{4s}|\widehat{v}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\widehat{v}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right)^{1-s}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\xi|^{4}|\widehat{v}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right)^{s}

\displaystyle=\|v\|_{2}^{2-2s}\|\Delta v\|_{2}^{2s}\leq(1-s)\varepsilon^{-\frac{s}{1-s}}\|v\|_{2}^{2}+s\varepsilon\|\Delta v\|_{2}^{2}.

(2.4)

Denote
\alpha^{+}=\max\{\alpha,0\}
and
\alpha^{-}=\max\{-\alpha,0\}
. Then

\displaystyle\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}+\|\Delta v\|_{2}^{2}+\alpha\|(-\Delta)^{s}v\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha^{+}(1-s)\varepsilon^{-\frac{s}{1-s}}\|v\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}+\alpha^{+}s\varepsilon\|\Delta v\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}.

(2.5)

When
\alpha>-\lambda_{1}^{1-s}(1-s)^{1-s}s^{-s}
, one can choose
\varepsilon>0

such that

\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1-s}{s}}(1-s)^{\frac{1-s}{s}}(\alpha^{-})^{\frac{1-s}{s}}<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{\alpha^{-}s},

in which case
1-\alpha^{-}s\varepsilon>0
and

\lambda_{1}-\alpha^{-}(1-s)\varepsilon^{-\frac{1-s}{s}}>0
hold.
It follows from Poincaré’s inequality that

\displaystyle\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}+\|\Delta v\|_{2}^{2}+\alpha\|(-\Delta)^{s}v\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\lambda_{1}-\alpha^{-}(1-s)\varepsilon^{-\frac{1-s}{s}}\right)\|v\|_{2}^{2}+\left(1-\alpha^{-}s\varepsilon\right)\|\Delta v\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\|v\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}.

(2.6)

Combining (
2.5
) with (
2.6
), we get
(
2.3
).

As for the case
\alpha>-(2-2s)^{2s-2}(2s-1)^{1-2s}
along
with
s\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)
, a similar process yields that

\|(-\Delta)^{s}v\|_{2}^{2}\leq(2-2s)\varepsilon^{-\frac{1-2s}{2-2s}}\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}+(2s-1)\varepsilon\|\Delta v\|_{2}^{2}.

In this case we can choose
\varepsilon>0
such that

(2-2s)^{\frac{2-2s}{2s-1}}(\alpha^{-})^{\frac{2-2s}{2s-1}}<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{\alpha^{-}(2s-1)},

then
1-\alpha^{-}(2s-1)\varepsilon>0
and

1-\alpha^{-}(2-2s)\varepsilon^{\frac{1-2s}{2-2s}}>0
hold true.
Hence,

\displaystyle\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}+\|\Delta v\|_{2}^{2}+\alpha\|(-\Delta)^{s}v\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\alpha^{-}(2-2s)\varepsilon^{\frac{1-2s}{2-2s}}\right)\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}+\left(1-\alpha^{-}(2s-1)\varepsilon\right)\|\Delta v\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\|v\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2},

(2.7)

then (
2.5
) and (
2.7
) conclude the proof.
∎

Setting

\|v\|_{(\alpha)}\coloneqq\left(\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2}+\|\Delta v\|_{2}^{2}+\alpha\|(-\Delta)^{s}v\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

the above lemma implies that as long as (H2) is satisfied,

\|\cdot\|_{(\alpha)}
and the
usual
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
-norm are actually equivalent on the space

\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
. For
p
satisfying (H1),
we denote by
S_{p(\cdot)}
a positive constant that
the optimal embedding constant of
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{W}_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, i.e.

\frac{1}{S_{p(\cdot)}}=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\|v\|_{(\alpha)}}{\|\nabla v\|_{p(\cdot)}}.

The Nehari manifold associated with
J(v;t)
is given by

\mathcal{N}(t)=\{v\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\}:I(v;t)=0\}.

The following lemma reveals that for any
t\geq 0
,
\mathcal{N}(t)
is nonempty.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let (H1)-(H3) be fulfilled. For a given
v\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)

with
\|v\|_{(\alpha)}\neq 0
and each
t\geq 0
, there exists a constant

\mu^{*}=\mu^{*}(v,t)>0
such that
I(\mu^{*}v;t)=0
.

Proof.

For any
\mu>0
and
fixed
t
, we set
h(\mu)\coloneqq I(\mu v;t)
. It is easy to see that
h(\mu)
is
continuous with respect to
\mu
. Moreover, it holds that

\displaystyle h(\mu)

\displaystyle\geq\mu^{2}\|v\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}-k(t)\max\left\{\mu^{p^{-}}\|\nabla v\|_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{-}},\mu^{p^{+}}\|\nabla v\|_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{+}}\right\}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\mu^{2}\|v\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}-\mu^{p^{-}}k(t)\|\nabla v\|_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{-}},\mu^{2}\|v\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}-\mu^{p^{+}}k(t)\|\nabla v\|_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{+}}\right\},

A simple calculation yields that there exists a positive constant

\widehat{\mu}_{1}=\widehat{\mu}_{1}(v,t)
given by

\widehat{\mu}_{1}\coloneqq\min\left\{\left(\frac{\|v\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}}{k(t)\|\nabla v\|_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{-}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{-}-2}},\left(\frac{\|v\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}}{k(t)\|\nabla v\|_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{+}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{+}-2}}\right\}

such that
h(\mu)\geq 0
for
\mu\in(0,\widehat{\mu}_{1}]
. Similarly, it follows from

h(\mu)\leq\max\left\{\mu^{2}\|v\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}-\mu^{p^{-}}k(t)\|\nabla v\|_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{-}},\mu^{2}\|v\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}-\mu^{p^{+}}k(t)\|\nabla v\|_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{+}}\right\}

that there exists a positive constant

\widehat{\mu}_{2}=\widehat{\mu}_{2}(v,t)\geq\widehat{\mu}_{1}
given by

\widehat{\mu}_{2}\coloneqq\max\left\{\left(\frac{\|v\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}}{k(t)\|\nabla v\|_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{-}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{-}-2}},\left(\frac{\|v\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}}{k(t)\|\nabla v\|_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{+}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{+}-2}}\right\}

such that
h(\mu)\leq 0
for
\mu\in[\widehat{\mu}_{2},+\infty)
. Therefore, there exists a positive
constant
\mu^{*}=\mu^{*}(v,t)
such that
\widehat{\mu}_{1}\leq\mu^{*}\leq\widehat{\mu}_{2}
and

I(\mu^{*}v;t)=0
.
∎

For each
t\geq 0
,
\mathcal{N}(t)
separates the two unbounded sets

\begin{array}[]{c}\vskip 2.5pt\mathcal{N}_{+}(t)=\{v\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega):I(v;t)>0\}\cup\{0\},\\
\mathcal{N}_{-}(t)=\{v\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega):I(v;t)<0\}.\end{array}

We proceed to define the potential well for problem
(
1.1
) by

\begin{array}[]{c}\vskip 2.5ptW(t)=\{v\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega):J(v;t)<d(t),I(v;t)>0\}\cup\{0\},\\
\end{array}

where

d(t)=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{N}(t)}J(v;t)

denotes the depth of the potential well
W(t)
. The nonemptiness of
\mathcal{N}(t)
ensures that the potential well depth
d(t)
is
well-defined. We denote

\underline{d}\coloneqq\inf_{t\geq 0}d(t),\quad\overline{d}\coloneqq\sup_{t\geq 0}d(t).

The following lemma provides several important properties of
d(t)
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let (H1)-(H3) be fulfilled. Then for any
t\geq 0
, the following statements hold true:

(i)

It holds that

d(t)\leq\frac{p^{+}-2}{2p^{+}}\max\left\{\left(k(t)S_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{-}}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-p^{-}}},\left(k(t)S_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{+}}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-p^{+}}}\right\},

and

d(t)\geq\frac{p^{-}-2}{2p^{-}}\min\left\{\left(k(t)S_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{-}}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-p^{-}}},\left(k(t)S_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{+}}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-p^{+}}}\right\}.

(ii)

0\leq\underline{d}\leq d(t)\leq\overline{d}<+\infty
. Additionally,
if
k(t)
is bounded, then
\underline{d}>0
.

(iii)

For any
v\in\mathcal{N}_{-}(t)
,

\displaystyle d(t)\leq\min\left\{\frac{p^{+}-2}{2p^{+}}\|v\|_{(\alpha)}^{2},k(t)\int_{\Omega}\frac{p(x)-2}{2p(x)}|\nabla v|^{p(x)}dx\right\}.

(2.8)

Proof.

We first prove (i). Fix
v\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\}
.
For each
t\geq 0
, Lemma
2.8
implies that
there exists a positive constant
\mu^{*}=\mu^{*}(v,t)
such that

\mu^{*}v\in\mathcal{N}(t)
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle d(t)\leq J(v;t)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\mu^{*}v\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}-\frac{k(t)}{p^{+}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(\mu^{*}v)|^{p(x)}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p^{+}}I(\mu^{*}v;t)+\frac{p^{+}-2}{2p^{+}}(\mu^{*})^{2}\|v\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{+}-2}{2p^{+}}(\mu^{*})^{2}\|v\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}.

(2.9)

It follows from the proof of Lemma
2.9
that

\displaystyle d(t)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{p^{+}-2}{2p^{+}}\|v\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}\widehat{\mu}_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{+}-2}{2p^{+}}\max\left\{\left(\frac{\|v\|_{(\alpha)}^{p^{-}}}{k(t)\|\nabla v\|_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{-}}}\right)^{\frac{2}{p^{-}-2}},\left(\frac{\|v\|_{(\alpha)}^{p^{+}}}{k(t)\|\nabla v\|_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{+}}}\right)^{\frac{2}{p^{+}-2}}\right\}.

By the arbitrariness of
v\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\}
and
the definition of
S_{p(\cdot)}
, it follows that for any
t\geq 0
,

d(t)\leq\frac{p^{+}-2}{2p^{+}}\max\left\{\left(k(t)S_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{-}}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-p^{-}}},\left(k(t)S_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{+}}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-p^{+}}}\right\}.

Now we choose
v\in\mathcal{N}(t)
. Then

\displaystyle\|v\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}

\displaystyle=k(t)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p(x)}dx\leq k(t)\max\left\{\|\nabla v\|_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{-}},\|\nabla v\|_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{+}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq k(t)\max\left\{S_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{-}}\|v\|_{(\alpha)}^{p^{-}},S_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{+}}\|v\|_{(\alpha)}^{p^{+}}\right\},

(2.10)

from which it follows that

\displaystyle\|v\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}\geq\min\left\{\left(k(t)S_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{-}}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-p^{-}}},\left(k(t)S_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{+}}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-p^{+}}}\right\}.

(2.11)

Since
v\in\mathcal{N}(t)
, we get

\displaystyle J(v;t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|v\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}-k(t)\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{p(x)}|\nabla v|^{p(x)}dx

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|v\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}-\frac{k(t)}{p^{-}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p(x)}dx=\frac{p^{-}-2}{2p^{-}}\|v\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{p^{-}-2}{2p^{-}}\min\left\{\left(k(t)S_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{-}}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-p^{-}}},\left(k(t)S_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{+}}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-p^{+}}}\right\}.

The definition of
d(t)
concludes the proof of (i).

As for (ii), since
k(t)
is nondecreasing, we obtain that

0<d(t)\leq\frac{p^{+}-2}{2p^{+}}\max\left\{\left(k(0)S_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{-}}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-p^{-}}},\left(k(0)S_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{+}}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-p^{+}}}\right\},

hence,
0\leq\underline{d}\leq\overline{d}<+\infty
. If
k(t)
is bounded, then

\underline{d}\geq\frac{p^{-}-2}{2p^{-}}\min\left\{\left(k(+\infty)S_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{-}}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-p^{-}}},\left(k(+\infty)S_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{+}}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-p^{+}}}\right\}>0.

Finally, let us prove (iii). Recall the function
h(\mu)
defined in the proof of
Lemma
2.8
. For any given
v\in\mathcal{N}_{-}(t)
,
we know that
h(1)<0
. A direct computation shows that

h(\mu)\geq\mu^{2}\|v\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}-\mu^{p^{-}}k(t)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p(x)}dx

holds for
\mu\in(0,1)
, which implies that there exists a positive constant

\mu_{0}=\mu_{0}(v,t)<1
such that
h(\mu_{0})>0
. Therefore,
there exists is a positive constant
\mu^{*}=\mu^{*}(v,t)\in(0,1)

such that
\mu^{*}v\in\mathcal{N}(t)
. It follows from
(
2.9
) that

d(t)\leq J(v;t)\leq\frac{p^{+}-2}{2p^{+}}(\mu^{*})^{2}\|v\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}\leq\frac{p^{+}-2}{2p^{+}}\|v\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle d(t)\leq J(\mu^{*}v;t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}I(\mu^{*}v;t)+k(t)\int_{\Omega}\frac{p(x)-2}{2p(x)}(\mu^{*})^{p(x)}|\nabla v|^{p(x)}dx

\displaystyle\leq k(t)\int_{\Omega}\frac{p(x)-2}{2p(x)}|\nabla v|^{p(x)}dx,

which concludes the proof of (iii).
∎

.

Lemma 2.10

\mathcal{N}_{+}(t)

Lemma 2.11
.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let (H1)-(H3) be fulfilled. Then:

(i)

For each

and any
\varsigma>d(t)

\lambdabar_{\varsigma}(t)>0
. Additionally, if

is bounded
and
\varsigma>\overline{d}

\underline{\lambdabar}_{\varsigma}>0
.

(ii)

If
p^{+}\leq\frac{2(N+4)}{N+2}

\lambdabar_{\infty}(t)>0

t\geq 0
. Furthermore, if
k(t)
is bounded,

\underline{\lambdabar}_{\infty}>0
.

Proof.

Fix
t\geq 0

.
It follows from the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (see
[
1
]
),

(2.13)

2.12

\|v\|_{2}\geq\min\left\{\left[\frac{1}{\widetilde{C}_{1}}\left(k(t)\right)^{-\frac{1}{p^{-}}}\|v\|_{(\alpha)}^{\frac{2}{p^{-}}-\theta}\right]^{\frac{1}{1-\theta}},\left[\frac{1}{\widetilde{C}_{1}}\left(k(t)\right)^{-\frac{1}{p^{+}}}\|v\|_{(\alpha)}^{\frac{2}{p^{+}}-\theta}\right]^{\frac{1}{1-\theta}}\right\}.

In this case, Lemma

that the right-hand side of (

Therefore,
\lambdabar_{\infty}(t)>0
for any

.
When

is bounded, the right-hand side of (

a uniform positive lower bound, hence
\underline{\lambdabar}_{\infty}>0
.
∎

In the study of blow-up properties, we consider solutions that satisfy a conservation law,
as defined in the following.

Definition 2.13

(Strong solution)
.

A function

u\in C([0,T];\mathcal{W}_{0}^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega))
is said to be a
(strong) solution to problem (
1.1
) on
[0,T]
if it is a
weak solution to problem (
1.1
) and the following conservation law

J(u(t);t)+\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{t}u(\tau)\|_{2}^{2}d\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\frac{k^{\prime}(\tau)}{p(x)}|\nabla u(\tau)|^{p(x)}dxd\tau=J(u_{0};0)

(2.15)

holds for
t\in[0,T]
.

Remark 2.14
.

(i) It is clear that any solution
u(t)
to problem (
1.1
) on
[0,T]
satisfies

J(u(t);t)\in C([0,T])
. Combined with Lemma
2.5
, this yields

\|u(t)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}\in C([0,T])
and
I(u(t);t)\in C([0,T])
.

(ii) One may obtain (
2.15
) by taking
\varphi=u_{t}
in
(

). Unfortunately, this argument is only formal as it requires

u
to be sufficiently regular so that
u_{t}\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega))
.
Standard approximation arguments yield only an energy inequality,
namely (
3.1
) presented in the next section. The conservation
law (
2.15
) is so essential that the analysis of blow-up properties,
both in this paper and in many previous works

[
43
,
22
,
34
,
19
,
31
,
83
]
,
is actually carried out for stronger solutions like those defined in
Definition
2.13
rather than for weak solutions directly. We emphasize this
distinction here in the interest of mathematical rigor; however, this does not diminish
the value of our work or those of prior works on blow-up behavior.

To close this section, we provide the definitions of the maximal existence time and
finite time blow-up for solutions to problem (
1.1
).

Definition 2.15

(Maximal existence time)
.

Suppose that
u(t)
is a weak (or strong) solution to problem (
1.1
). We
say that a positive
T_{*}
is the maximal existence time of

as follows:

(i)

If for all

(ii)

If there exists a

such that

exists
on

for all

, but does not exist on
[0,T^{\prime}]

.

Definition 2.16

(Finite time blow-up)
.

Suppose that
u(t)
is a weak (or strong) solution to problem (

). We say

Set

By using

again

Remark 3.2
.

Definition
2.6
(

) can be understood as

u(t,\cdot)\to u_{0}
strongly in
\mathcal{H}_{0}(\Omega)
.
We can also interpret it as

u(t,\cdot)\rightharpoonup u_{0}
weakly in
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)

since
u\in C_{w}([0,T];\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega))
.
Moreover,
u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega))
implies that there exists

N\subset[0,T]
of measure zero such that for all
t\in[0,T]\backslash N
,

\|u(t)\|_{(\alpha)}<+\infty
. For
t\in N
, we can approximate
t
by
a sequence
\{t_{j}\}\subset[0,T]\backslash N
. Since

u\in C_{w}([0,T];\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega))
, we have

u(t_{j})\rightharpoonup u(t)
weakly in
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
,
which implies that
t\in[0,T]\backslash N
for each there exists

\varXi(t)>0
independent of
j
such that
\|u(t_{j})\|_{(\alpha)}\leq\varXi(t)

for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Consequently,
\|u(t)\|_{(\alpha)}\leq\varliminf_{j\to\infty}\|u(t_{j})\|_{(\alpha)}\leq\varXi(t)<+\infty
.
Therefore, for all
t\in[0,T]
, we conclude that

\|\Delta u(t)\|_{2}\sim\|u(t)\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\sim\|u(t)\|_{(\alpha)}<+\infty
.
From the above local existence theorem and the standard continuation procedure,
it follows that as long as
\|\Delta u\|_{2}
remains bounded,
the weak solution can be extended. Using this procedure, we can also derive the
following alternative property.

Corollary 3.3
.

The weak solution to problem (
1.1
) either exists globally or
blows up in finite time.

Proof.

To see this we argue by contradiction. Let
u(t)
be a weak solution to problem
(
1.1
). Suppose that its maximal existence time

T_{*}
is finite but it does not blow up. That is, there exist a
\varTheta>0
and
a sequence
\{t_{j}\}
such that
t_{j}\nearrow T_{*}
as
t_{j}\to\infty

and
\|u(t_{j})\|_{(\alpha)}\leq\varTheta
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. As shown in
the proof of the local existence theorem above, there exists

\widehat{T}=\widehat{T}(\varTheta)>0

such that for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
u(t)
exists on
[t_{j},t_{j}+\widehat{T}]

When

T_{*}<t_{j}+\widehat{T}
, which contradicts
the assumption that
T_{*}
is the maximal existence interval. This completes the proof.
∎

4
Global existence and finite time blow-up of solutions with
J(u_{0};0)\leq\underline{d}

4.1
The case
J(u_{0};0)<\underline{d}

We begin by proving the continuity of
I(u^{m}(t);t)
with respect to
t
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For each
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
I(u^{m}(t);t)\in C([0,T_{m}))
, where
u^{m}(t)

denotes the approximate solutions constructed in the proof of Theorem

3.1
and
T_{m}
denotes their maximal existence time.
Moreover, for
t\in[0,T_{m})
, it holds that

\frac{1}{2}\|u^{m}(t)\|_{2}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}I(u^{m}(\tau);\tau)d\tau=\frac{1}{2}\|u^{m}(0)\|_{2}^{2}.

(4.1)

Proof.

Multiplying (
3.3
) by

g_{i}^{m}(t)
and summing over
i=1,2,\cdots,m
, we obtain that

I(u^{m}(t);t)=-(u^{m}(t),\partial_{t}u^{m}(t))=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u^{m}(t)\|_{2}^{2}

(4.2)

for
t\in[0,T_{m})
. Fix
\widehat{T}\in(0,T_{m})
. Since

u^{m}\in C^{1}([0,T_{m});\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega))
, it follows that

u^{m}\in C([0,\widehat{T}];\mathcal{H}_{0}(\Omega))
and

\partial_{t}u^{m}\in C_{w}([0,\widehat{T}];\mathcal{H}_{0}(\Omega))
.
For any
t\in[0,\widehat{T}]
, consider a sequence
\{t_{j}\}

such that
t_{j}\to t
as
j\to\infty
, then

u(t_{j})\rightharpoonup u(t)
weakly in
\mathcal{H}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Therefore, there exists a constant
\varXi>0
independent of
j

such that
\|u(t_{j})\|_{2}\leq\varXi
for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then

\displaystyle\left|I(u^{m}(t);t)-I(u^{m}(t_{j});t_{j})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|(u^{m}(t),\partial_{t}u^{m}(t))-(u^{m}(t_{j}),\partial_{t}u^{m}(t_{j}))\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|(u^{m}(t)-u^{m}(t_{j}),\partial_{t}u^{m}(t_{j}))\right|+\left|(u^{m}(t)-u^{m}(t_{j}),\partial_{t}u^{m}(t))\right|

\displaystyle\leq\varXi\left\|u^{m}(t)-u^{m}(t_{j})\right\|_{2}+\left|(u^{m}(t)-u^{m}(t_{j}),\partial_{t}u^{m}(t))\right|\to 0

as
j\to\infty
, which implies that
I(u^{m}(t);t)
is continuous at
any
t\in[0,\widehat{T}]
. It follows from the arbitrariness of

\widehat{T}
that
I(u^{m}(t);t)\in C([0,T_{m}))
for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
To derive (
4.1
) one only need to
integrate (
4.2
) over
(0,t)
so that
t\in[0,T_{m})
.
∎

Now we present a global well-posedness result for problem (
1.1
) under the
condition that
J(u_{0};0)<\underline{d}
.
Note that this case is only possible when
\underline{d}>0
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
. If
J(u_{0};0)<\underline{d}
and

I(u_{0};0)>0
, then problem (
1.1
) possesses a unique
global weak solution
u(t)
. That is, for any
T^{\prime}\in(0,+\infty)
,
the unique weak solution
u(t)
exists on
[0,T^{\prime}]
and there exists a
positive constant
M>0
independent of
u_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T^{\prime}]}\|u(t)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T^{\prime}}\|\partial_{t}u(\tau)\|_{2}^{2}d\tau\leq M.

(4.3)

Moreover, there exists a
constant
\delta_{1}>0
such that
\|u(t)\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=O(e^{-\delta_{1}t})
.

Proof.

The proof will be completed in two steps.

Step 1. Global well-posedness.

Since
J(\cdot;0)
and
I(\cdot;0)
are continuous on
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
,
we find
m_{*}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

J(u^{m}(0);0)<\underline{d}
and
I(u^{m}(0);0)>0
for
m\geq m_{*}
. From now
until the end of this proof, we let
m\geq m_{*}
. The fact that
J(u^{m}(t);t)

is nonincreasing with respect to
t\in[0,T_{m})
implies
that
J(u^{m}(t);t)<\underline{d}
for
t\in(0,T_{m})
.

Now we prove that for

t\in(0,T_{m})
,
u^{m}(t)\in\mathcal{N}_{+}(t)
. If not, then

S\coloneqq\left\{t\in(0,T_{m}):I(u^{m}(t);t)\leq 0\ \textrm{and}\ u^{m}(t)\not\equiv 0\right\}\neq\varnothing.

Set
t_{0}=\inf S
. It follows from
I(u^{m}(0);0)>0
and
the continuity of
t\mapsto I(u^{m}(t);t)
that
t_{0}\in(0,T_{m})
.
A standard proof by contradiction yields that
I(u^{m}(t_{0});t_{0})=0
.
We assert that
u^{m}(t_{0})\not\equiv 0
. Otherwise, by Lemma
2.10
and
the continuity of
u^{m}(t)
in
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
we know that there exists

\delta>0
depending only on
T^{\prime}
such that for every

t\in(t_{0},t_{0}+\delta]
, either
u^{m}(t)\equiv 0
or
I(u^{m}(t);t)>0
.
If the latter is true, then it follows from

\displaystyle I(u^{m}(t);t)

\displaystyle=\|u^{m}(t)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}-k(t)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{m}(t)|^{p(x)}dx

\displaystyle=2J(u^{m}(t);t)-k(t)\int_{\Omega}\frac{p(x)-2}{p(x)}|\nabla u^{m}(t)|^{p(x)}dx>0

that
J(u^{m}(t);t)>0
for
t\in(t_{0},t_{0}+\delta]
. However, since

J(u^{m}(t_{0});t_{0})=0
and
J(u^{m}(t);t)
is nonincreasing with respect to
t
,
this leads to a contradiction. Thus, for
t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\delta]
, it must hold that

u^{m}(t)\equiv 0
. Since
\delta
depends only on
T^{\prime}
, this process can be repeated to
conclude that
u^{m}(t)\equiv 0
for all
t\in[t_{0},T_{m})
. However, this
contradicts
S\neq\varnothing
. Therefore,
u^{m}(t_{0})\equiv 0
cannot hold, leaving

u^{m}(t_{0})\in\mathcal{N}(t_{0})
as the only possibility. By the definition
of
\mathcal{N}(t_{0})
, we have

J(u^{m}(t_{0});t_{0})\geq d(t_{0})\geq\underline{d},

which contradicts
J(u^{m}(t_{0});t_{0})<\underline{d}
.

The argument above shows that our initial assumption is false. Thus, we have proved
that for
t\in(0,T_{m})
,
u^{m}(t)\in\mathcal{N}_{+}(t)
. It follows from
(
4.1
) that

\sum_{j=1}^{m}(g_{j}^{m}(t))^{2}=\|u^{m}(t)\|_{2}^{2}=\|u^{m}(0)\|_{2}^{2}-2\int_{0}^{t}I(u^{m}(\tau);\tau)d\tau\leq\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}

(4.4)

for
t\in(0,T_{m})
. Thus,
T_{m}=T^{\prime}
. Then, using the following inequality

J(u^{m}(t);t)\geq\frac{p^{-}-2}{2p^{-}}\|u^{m}(t)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}+\frac{1}{p^{-}}I(u^{m}(t);t)\geq\frac{p^{-}-2}{2p^{-}}\|u^{m}(t)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}

(4.5)

and (
3.4
), we have

\frac{p^{-}-2}{2p^{-}}\|u^{m}(t)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{t}u^{m}(\tau)\|_{2}^{2}d\tau\leq J(u^{m}(0);0)<\underline{d}

for
t\in[0,T^{\prime}]
, which implies that

\sup_{t\in[0,T^{\prime}]}\|u^{m}(t)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T^{\prime}}\|\partial_{t}u^{m}(\tau)\|_{2}^{2}d\tau\leq\frac{2p^{-}\underline{d}}{p^{-}-2}+\underline{d}=\frac{3p^{-}-2}{p^{-}-2}\underline{d}.

Once again, following the lines of Step 3 in the proof of
Theorem
3.1
,
we deduce that there exists
u\in L^{\infty}(0,T^{\prime};\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega))\cap H^{1}(0,T^{\prime};\mathcal{H}_{0}(\Omega))
and a subsequence of
\{u^{m}\}
(still denoted by

\{u^{m}\}
) such that

\displaystyle u^{m}\rightharpoonup u\quad\textrm{weakly-*}

in

\displaystyle L^{\infty}(0,T^{\prime};\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)),

(4.6a)

\displaystyle u^{m}\rightharpoonup u\quad\textrm{weakly}

in

\displaystyle H^{1}(0,T^{\prime};\mathcal{H}_{0}(\Omega)),

(4.6b)

\displaystyle u^{m}\to u\quad\textrm{strongly}

in

\displaystyle C([0,T^{\prime}];\mathcal{W}_{0}^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega))

(4.6c)

as
m\to\infty
and
u
is a weak solution to problem (
1.1
).
Moreover,
u\in C_{w}([0,T^{\prime}];\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega))
and there exists a positive
constant
M>0
independent of
m
and
u_{0}
such that
(
4.3
) holds.
Step 4 in the proof of Theorem
3.1
shows that
the weak solution is always unique within its existence interval. The arbitrariness of

T^{\prime}
thereby completes the proof of global well-posedness.

Step 2. Decay rate.

For a given
t\geq 0
, we first assume that
u^{m}(t)\not\equiv 0
.
By a following a process similar to that in the proof of Lemma
2.9
(iii),
we find that there exists
\mu^{*}(t)\in(1,+\infty)
such
that
\mu^{*}(t)u^{m}(t)\in\mathcal{N}(t)
. Thus,

\displaystyle 0=I(\mu^{*}(t)u^{m}(t);t)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mu^{*}(t)\right)^{2}\|u^{m}(t)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}-k(t)\left(\mu^{*}(t)\right)^{p^{-}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{m}(t)|^{p(x)}dx

\displaystyle=\left(\left(\mu^{*}(t)\right)^{2}-\left(\mu^{*}(t)\right)^{p^{-}}\right)\|u^{m}(t)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}+\left(\mu^{*}(t)\right)^{p^{-}}I(u^{m}(t);t),

that is

I(u^{m}(t);t)\geq\left(1-\left(\mu^{*}(t)\right)^{2-p^{-}}\right)\|u^{m}(t)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\underline{d}\leq d(t)

\displaystyle\leq J(\mu^{*}(t)u^{m}(t);t)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\mu^{*}(t)\right)^{2}\|u^{m}(t)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}-k(t)\left(\mu^{*}(t)\right)^{p^{-}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{p(x)}|\nabla u^{m}(t)|^{p(x)}dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\mu^{*}(t)\right)^{2}\|u^{m}(t)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}-k(t)\left(\mu^{*}(t)\right)^{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{p(x)}|\nabla u^{m}(t)|^{p(x)}dx

\displaystyle=\left(\mu^{*}(t)\right)^{2}J(u^{m}(t);t)\leq\left(\mu^{*}(t)\right)^{2}J(u^{m}(0);0),

which implies that there exist a constant

\delta_{0}\coloneqq\sup_{m\geq m^{*}}\left(\frac{J(u^{m}(0);0)}{\underline{d}}\right)^{\frac{p^{-}-2}{2}}\in(0,1)

such that

\left(\mu^{*}(t)\right)^{2-p^{-}}\leq\left(\frac{J(u^{m}(0);0)}{\underline{d}}\right)^{\frac{p^{-}-2}{2}}\leq\delta_{0},\quad\forall m\geq m_{*}.

Therefore, we have

I(u^{m}(t);t)\geq(1-\delta_{0})\|u^{m}(t)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}

(4.7)

for
t\geq 0
, and it also holds for the case where
u^{m}(t)\equiv 0
.

Using the following inequality

J(u^{m}(t);t)\leq\frac{p^{+}-2}{2p^{+}}\|u^{m}(t)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}+\frac{1}{p^{+}}I(u^{m}(t);t)

and (
4.7
), we obtain that

J(u^{m}(t);t)\leq\frac{p^{+}-2\delta_{0}}{2p^{+}(1-\delta_{0})}I(u^{m}(t);t).

(4.8)

Integrating both sides of the above inequality over
(t,t^{\prime})
, where

t^{\prime}
can be chosen sufficiently large, we get

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}J(u^{m}(\tau);\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\frac{p^{+}-2\delta_{0}}{2p^{+}(1-\delta_{0})}\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}I(u^{m}(\tau);\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\frac{p^{+}-2\delta_{0}}{2p^{+}(1-\delta_{0})}\|u^{m}(t)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{p^{+}-2\delta_{0}}{2B_{2}^{2}p^{+}(1-\delta_{0})}\|u^{m}(t)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{p^{-}(p^{+}-2\delta_{0})}{B_{2}^{2}p^{+}(p^{-}-2)(1-\delta_{0})}J(u^{m}(t);t)

by using (
4.1
), (
4.5
)
and (
4.8
). Here

B_{2}\coloneqq\inf_{v\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\|v\|_{(\alpha)}}{\|v\|_{2}}.

Furthermore, it follows from
J(u^{m}(t);t)\leq\underline{d}
that for any
t^{\prime}>t
,

\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}J(u^{m}(\tau);\tau)d\tau
has a uniform positive upper
bound. Since
J(u^{m}(t);t)
is nonnegative,

\int_{t}^{+\infty}J(u^{m}(\tau);\tau)d\tau
converges and it holds that

\int_{t}^{+\infty}J(u^{m}(\tau);\tau)d\tau\leq\frac{1}{2\delta_{1}}J(u^{m}(t);t),

(4.9)

where
\delta_{1}\coloneqq\frac{B_{2}^{2}p^{+}(p^{-}-2)(1-\delta_{0})}{2p^{-}(p^{+}-2\delta_{0})}
.
From
[
48
, Lemma 1]
, it follows that

J(u^{m}(\tau);\tau)\leq J(u^{m}(0);0)e^{1-2\delta_{1}t}<\underline{d}e^{1-2\delta_{1}t},

combining with (
4.5
), we deduce that for any
t\geq 0
,

\|u^{m}(t)\|_{(\alpha)}\leq\left(\frac{2ep^{-}\underline{d}}{p^{-}-2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\delta_{1}t}.

Then, (
4.6a
) implies that for any
t>0
,

\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{\tau\in(t,t+1)}\|u(\tau)\|_{{}_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}}\leq\varliminf_{m\to\infty}\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{\tau\in(t,t+1)}\|u^{m}(\tau)\|_{{}_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}}\lesssim e^{-\delta_{1}t}.

Therefore, there exists
N_{t}\subset(t,t+1)
of measure zero such that

\|u(\tau)\|_{{}_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}}\lesssim e^{-\delta_{1}t}

for
\tau\in(t,t+1)\backslash N_{t}
. Consider a sequence
\{t_{j}\}\subset(t,t+1)\backslash N_{t}

such that
t_{j}\to t
as
j\to\infty
. By weak continuity, we have

\|u(t)\|_{{}_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}}\leq\varliminf_{j\to\infty}\|u(t_{j})\|_{{}_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}}\lesssim e^{-\delta_{1}t}.

The arbitrariness of
t>0
concludes the proof.
∎

We then give a result concerning the finite time blow-up properties of solutions
to problem (
1.1
) when
J(u_{0};0)<\underline{d}
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Assume that
u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
. If
J(u_{0};0)<\underline{d}
and

I(u_{0};0)<0
, then any solution to problem (
1.1
) blows up in finite time.

Proof.

Some ideas of this proof comes from Theorem 3.2 in
[
36
]
.
Assume that
u(t)
is a global solution to problem (
1.1
).
Firstly we prove that for

t\geq 0
,
u(t)\in\mathcal{N}_{-}(t)
. If not, the continuity of

t\mapsto I(u(t);t)
implies that there exists
t_{1}>0
such that

I(u(t_{1});t_{1})=0
and
I(u(t);t)<0
for
t\in[0,t_{1})
.
From the continuity of
t\mapsto\|u(t)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}
and the proof of
Lemma
2.11
, it follows that

\|u(t_{1})\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}=\lim_{t\nearrow t_{1}}\|u(t_{1})\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}\geq\min\left\{\left(k(t_{1})S_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{-}}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-p^{-}}},\left(k(t_{1})S_{p(\cdot)}^{p^{+}}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-p^{+}}}\right\}>0,

which implies that
u(t_{1})\in\mathcal{N}(t_{1})
. By the definition of
d(t_{1})

we have
J(u(t_{1});t_{1})\geq d(t_{1})\geq\underline{d}>J(u_{0};0)
.
Since
J(u(t);t)
is nonincreasing with respect to
t
,
this leads to a contradiction. Thus,
u(t)\in\mathcal{N}_{-}(t)
for
t\geq 0
.

Let

F_{1}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2},

then
F_{1}(t)
is absolutely continuous and

F_{1}^{\prime}(t)=(u,u_{t})=-I(u(t);t)=k(t)\int_{\Omega}\frac{p(x)-2}{p(x)}|\nabla u(t)|^{p(x)}dx-2J(u(t);t)

for almost all
t\geq 0
. In the case where
\underline{d}=0
, since

J(u(t);t)<\underline{d}=0
, we can choose
C_{0}=k(0)\frac{p^{-}-2}{p^{-}}

such that for almost all
t\geq 0
,

F_{1}^{\prime}(t)\geq C_{0}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(t)|^{p(x)}dx.

(4.10)

If
\underline{d}>0
, from (
2.8
), we have

\displaystyle F_{1}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\geq k(t)\left(1-\frac{J(u(t);t)}{d(t)}\right)\int_{\Omega}\frac{p(x)-2}{p(x)}|\nabla u(t)|^{p(x)}dx

\displaystyle\geq k(0)\frac{p^{-}-2}{p^{-}}\left(1-\frac{J(u_{0};0)}{\underline{d}}\right)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(t)|^{p(x)}dx

for a.e.
t\geq 0
. Thus, we can choose

C_{0}\coloneqq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}k(0)\frac{p^{-}-2}{p^{-}}\left(1-\frac{J(u_{0};0)}{\underline{d}}\right),&\textrm{if}\quad\underline{d}>0,\\
k(0)\frac{p^{-}-2}{p^{-}},&\textrm{if}\quad\underline{d}=0,\end{array}\right.

such that (
4.10
) holds for a.e.
t\geq 0
.
Define the sets
\Omega_{+}=\{x\in\Omega:|\nabla u|\geq 1\}
and

\Omega_{-}=\{x\in\Omega:|\nabla u|<1\}
. Then for almost all
t\geq 0
,

F_{1}^{\prime}(t)\geq\int_{\Omega_{+}}|\nabla u(t)|^{p^{-}}dx\geq C_{0}|\Omega|^{\frac{2-p^{-}}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{+}}|\nabla u(t)|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p^{-}}{2}}

and

F_{1}^{\prime}(t)\geq\int_{\Omega_{-}}|\nabla u(t)|^{p^{+}}dx\geq C_{0}|\Omega|^{\frac{2-p^{+}}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{-}}|\nabla u(t)|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p^{+}}{2}},

which implies that there exists a positive constant

C_{1}\coloneqq\lambda_{1}\min\left\{C_{0}^{\frac{2}{p^{+}}}|\Omega|^{\frac{2-p^{+}}{p^{+}}},C_{0}^{\frac{2}{p^{-}}}|\Omega|^{\frac{2-p^{-}}{p^{-}}}\right\}

such that

(F_{1}^{\prime}(t))^{\frac{2}{p^{-}}}\left(1+(F_{1}^{\prime}(t))^{\frac{2}{p^{+}}-\frac{2}{p^{-}}}\right)=(F_{1}^{\prime}(t))^{\frac{2}{p^{-}}}+(F_{1}^{\prime}(t))^{\frac{2}{p^{+}}}\geq C_{1}F_{1}(t)

for a.e.
t\geq 0
. For almost all
t\geq 0
,
F_{1}^{\prime}(t)=-I(u(t);t)>0
holds,
which implies that

F_{1}(t)\geq F_{1}(0)+\int_{0}^{t}F_{1}^{\prime}(\tau)d\tau\geq F_{1}(0)>0

for all
t\geq 0
.
Therefore, for a.e.
t\geq 0
,
F_{1}^{\prime}(t)\geq C_{2}\coloneqq\min\left\{\left(\frac{C_{1}}{2}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{\frac{p^{+}}{2}},\left(\frac{C_{1}}{2}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{\frac{p^{-}}{2}}\right\}.
Set

C_{3}\coloneqq\left(\frac{C_{1}}{1+C_{2}^{\frac{2}{p^{+}}-\frac{2}{p^{-}}}}\right)^{\frac{2}{p^{-}}},

then for a.e.
t\geq 0
,

F_{1}^{\prime}(t)\geq C_{3}(F_{1}(t))^{\frac{p^{-}}{2}}.

(4.11)

Integrating (
4.11
) over
(0,t)
, we deduce that for
all
t\geq 0
,

\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{2}{\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{1-\frac{p^{-}}{2}}-\frac{p^{-}-2}{2}C_{3}t\right]^{\frac{2}{p^{-}-2}}}.

Hence,

\lim_{t\to\frac{2}{C_{3}(p^{-}-2)}\left(\frac{1}{2}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{1-\frac{p^{-}}{2}}}\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}=+\infty,

which contradicts the assumption that
u(t)
is a global solution.
Therefore,
u(t)
blows up in finite time and its maximal existence interval

T_{*}
satisfies

T_{*}\leq\frac{2}{C_{3}(p^{-}-2)}\left(\frac{1}{2}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{1-\frac{p^{-}}{2}}.

This fact concludes the proof.
∎

4.2
The case
J(u_{0};0)=\underline{d}

By combining Theorem
4.2
with an approximation argument,
we obtain a global existence result for the case
J(u_{0};0)=\underline{d}
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
. If
J(u_{0};0)=\underline{d}
and

I(u_{0};0)>0
, then problem (
1.1
) possesses a unique
global weak solution
u(t)
.

Proof.

Set
\mu_{n}=\frac{n}{n+1}
,
u_{0n}(x)=\mu_{n}u_{0}(x)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Consider the following
problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{\alpha}u=\operatorname{div}\left(F(t,x,\nabla u)\right),&x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0n}(x),&x\in\Omega,\\
u=0,&x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(4.12)

When
\mu\in(0,1)
, it follows from
I(u_{0};0)>0
that

\displaystyle I(\mu u_{0};0)

\displaystyle\geq\mu^{2}\|u_{0}\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}-k(0)\mu^{p^{-}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{0}|^{p(x)}dx

\displaystyle=\mu^{p^{-}}I(u_{0};0)+\left(\mu^{2}-\mu^{p^{-}}\right)\|u_{0}\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}>0.

Set
j_{0}(\mu)=J(\mu u_{0};0)
. Then for
\mu\in(0,1)
, it holds that

\frac{d}{d\mu}j_{0}(\mu)=\mu\|u_{0}\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}-k(0)\int_{\Omega}\mu^{p(x)-1}|\nabla u_{0}|^{p(x)}dx=\frac{1}{\mu}I(\mu u_{0};0)>0,

that is,
\mu\mapsto J(\mu u_{0};0)
is strictly increasing on
(0,1)
. Then from

\mu_{n}\in(0,1)
we obtain that
I(u_{0n};0)>0
and

J(u_{0n};0)<J(u_{0};0)=\underline{d}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By Theorem

4.2
we know that for each
n
and
T^{\prime}>0
, problem
(
4.12
) possesses a unique weak solution
u_{n}
on
[0,T^{\prime}]

satisfying

(\partial_{t}u_{n},\varphi)+(\Delta u_{n},\Delta\varphi)+\alpha((-\Delta)^{s}u_{n},(-\Delta)^{s}\varphi)+\int_{\Omega}F(t,x,\nabla u_{n})\cdot\nabla\varphi dx=0

(4.13)

for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
and almost all
t\in(0,T^{\prime})
.
Besides, there exists a positive constant
M>0
independent of
n
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T^{\prime}]}\|u_{n}(t)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T^{\prime}}\|\partial_{t}u_{n}(\tau)\|_{2}^{2}d\tau\leq M.

(4.14)

Therefore, there exists
u\in L^{\infty}(0,T^{\prime};\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega))\cap H^{1}(0,T^{\prime};\mathcal{H}_{0}(\Omega))
and a subsequence of
\{u_{n}\}
(still denoted by

\{u_{n}\}
) such that

\displaystyle u_{n}\rightharpoonup u\quad\textrm{weakly-*}

in

\displaystyle L^{\infty}(0,T^{\prime};\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle u_{n}\rightharpoonup u\quad\textrm{weakly}

in

\displaystyle H^{1}(0,T^{\prime};\mathcal{H}_{0}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle u_{n}\to u\quad\textrm{strongly}

in

\displaystyle C([0,T^{\prime}];\mathcal{W}_{0}^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega))

as
n\to\infty
. Letting
n\to\infty
in (
4.13
) we
get

(u_{t},\varphi)+(\Delta u,\Delta\varphi)+\alpha((-\Delta)^{s}u,(-\Delta)^{s}\varphi)+\int_{\Omega}F(t,x,\nabla u)\cdot\nabla\varphi dx=0

for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
and almost all
t\in(0,T^{\prime})
,
which implies that
u(t)
is the weak solution to problem (
2.2
)
on
[0,T^{\prime}]
. The arbitrariness of
T^{\prime}
thereby completes the proof.
∎

We deduce the following finite time blow-up result for
J(u_{0};0)=\underline{d}
from
Theorem
4.3
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Assume that
u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
. If
J(u_{0};0)=\underline{d}
and

I(u_{0};0)<0
, then any solution to problem (
1.1
) blows up in finite time.

Proof.

Assume that
u(t)
is a global solution to problem (
1.1
). Since

J(u_{0};0)=\underline{d}>0
and
I(u_{0};0)<0
,
the continuity of
J(u(t);t)
and
I(u(t);t)
with respect to
t
implies that
there exists
t_{1}>0
such that
J(u(t);t)>0
and
I(u(t);t)<0
for

t\in[0,t_{1})
.
From
(u,u_{t})=-I(u(t);t)>0
for a.e.
t\in[0,t_{1}]
, we have that

u_{t}
is not identically zero on
[0,t_{1}]
. Hence,

J(u(t_{1});t_{1})\leq J(u_{0};0)-\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\|\partial_{t}u(\tau)\|_{2}^{2}d\tau<\underline{d}.

Working as in the proof of Theorem
4.3
we get

u(t)\in\mathcal{N}_{-}(t)
for
t\geq t_{1}
. Furthermore, we can choose

C_{0}=k(0)\frac{p^{-}-2}{p^{-}}\left(1-\frac{J(u(t_{1});t_{1})}{\underline{d}}\right)

such that for almost all
t\geq t_{1}
,

\displaystyle F_{1}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\geq k(t)\left(1-\frac{J(u(t);t)}{d(t)}\right)\int_{\Omega}\frac{p(x)-2}{p(x)}|\nabla u(t)|^{p(x)}dx

\displaystyle\geq k(t)\left(1-\frac{J(u(t_{1});t_{1})}{\underline{d}}\right)\int_{\Omega}\frac{p(x)-2}{p(x)}|\nabla u(t)|^{p(x)}dx

\displaystyle=C_{0}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(t)|^{p(x)}dx.

The remaining part of the proof follows the same arguments as that of
Theorem
4.3
, and thus we omit the details here.
∎

5
High initial energy case
J(u_{0};0)>0

Let us recall the properties of
\lambdabar_{\varsigma}(t)
presented in
Section
2
. Following the ideas of Gazzola et al.
[
26
]
,
we first establish a sufficient condition for the global existence of solutions
when
J(u_{0};0)>\overline{d}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
k(t)
be bounded and
u_{0}\in\mathcal{N}_{+}(0)
. Assume that

\overline{d}<J(u_{0};0)\leq\widetilde{d}
with

\widetilde{d}\in(\overline{d},+\infty)
. Then, there exists

\mathfrak{L}:\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}

such that if
\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\mathfrak{L}(u_{0})
holds,
problem (
1.1
) possesses a unique
global weak solution
u(t)
.

Proof.

Recall the approximate solution
u^{m}
defined in the proof of
Theorem
3.1
and its maximal existence interval
T_{m}
.
We find
m_{**}=m_{**}(u_{0})\in\mathbb{N}

such that
J(u^{m}(0);0)>\overline{d}
,
I(u^{m}(0);0)>0
for
m\geq m_{**}
.
From now until the end of this proof, we let
m\geq m_{**}
. Setting

K_{3}=K_{3}(u_{0})\coloneqq\sup_{m\geq m_{**}}J(u^{m}(0);0),

we define

\mathfrak{L}(u_{0})\coloneqq\underline{\lambdabar}_{K_{3}(u_{0})}
.
The boundedness of
k(t)
ensures that
\mathfrak{L}(u_{0})>0
. Assume that

\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\mathfrak{L}(u_{0})
holds, then

\|u^{m}(0)\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\underline{\lambdabar}_{K_{3}}.

We claim that for

t\in(0,T_{m})
,
u^{m}(t)\in\mathcal{N}_{+}(t)
. Otherwise, by a similar argument as in
the proof of Theorem
4.2
, we know that
there exists
t_{0}\in(0,T_{m})
such that
u^{m}(t)\in\mathcal{N}_{+}(t)

for
t\in[0,t_{0})
and
u^{m}(t_{0})\in\mathcal{N}(t)
.
Then (
4.2
) implies
that
\partial_{t}u^{m}\not\equiv 0
for
(t,x)\in(0,t_{0})\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
and

\|u^{m}(t_{0})\|_{2}^{2}<\|u^{m}(0)\|_{2}^{2}\leq\underline{\lambdabar}_{K_{3}}.

(5.1)

However, from (
3.4
), it follows that

J(u^{m}(t_{0});t_{0})<J(u^{m}(0);0)\leq K_{3}
, which implies
that
u^{m}(t_{0})\leq\mathcal{N}_{K_{3}}(t_{0})
. This yields that

\|u^{m}(t_{0})\|_{2}^{2}\geq\lambdabar_{K_{3}}(t_{0})\geq\underline{\lambdabar}_{K_{3}},

which contradicts (
5.1
). Therefore,
for
t\in[0,T_{m})
,
u^{m}(t)\in\mathcal{N}_{+}(t)
. This
implies that (
4.4
) holds for
t\in(0,T_{m})

so that
T_{m}=T^{\prime}
. Similar to the proof of
Theorem
4.2
, we can obtain the following estimate:

\sup_{t\in[0,T^{\prime}]}\|u^{m}(t)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T^{\prime}}\|\partial_{t}u^{m}(\tau)\|_{2}^{2}d\tau\leq\frac{3p^{-}-2}{p^{-}-2}\widetilde{d},

and once again derive (
4.6a
)-(
4.6c
).
The remaining proof is the same as that of
Theorem
4.2
and hence we omit it.
∎

Further assumptions on
p
lead to a more explicit sufficient condition,
as shown in the following corollary.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
p^{+}\leq\frac{2(N+4)}{N+2}
and
k(t)
be bounded.
Let
u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
. Assume that

\overline{d}<J(u_{0};0)\leq\widetilde{d}
with

\widetilde{d}\in(\overline{d},+\infty)
. If

\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}<\underline{\lambdabar}_{\infty}
,
then problem (
1.1
) possesses a unique
global weak solution
u(t)
.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
2.12
(ii) that

\underline{\lambdabar}_{\infty}>0
.
We claim that
u_{0}\in\mathcal{N}_{+}(0)
. By the definition
of
\underline{\lambdabar}_{\infty}(0)
, we know that

\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}<\underline{\lambdabar}_{\infty}\leq\lambdabar_{\infty}(0)=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{N}(0)}\|v\|_{2}^{2},

(5.2)

from which it follows that
u_{0}\notin\mathcal{N}(0)
.
If
u_{0}\in\mathcal{N}_{-}(0)
, a similar argument as in the proof of
Lemma
2.9
(iii) shows that there exists a constant
\mu^{*}\in(0,1)

such that
\mu^{*}u_{0}\in\mathcal{N}(0)
. Review the definition
of
\lambdabar_{\infty}(0)
, we obtain that

\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\geq(\mu^{*})^{-2}\lambdabar_{\infty}(0)>\lambdabar_{\infty}(0),

which contradicts (
5.2
). Thus,

u_{0}\in\mathcal{N}_{+}(0)
and it holds that

\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}<\underline{\lambdabar}_{\infty}\leq\underline{\lambdabar}_{K_{3}}(u_{0})=\mathfrak{L}(u_{0}).

The conclusion then follows
directly from Theorem
5.1
.
∎

Due to the time-dependence of
\mathcal{N}(t)
, the method used in
[
26
]
is
not readily applicable for analyzing the
\omega
-limit set of
u_{0}
, which makes it difficult
to establish a blow-up result for the case
J(u_{0};0)>\overline{d}
by contradiction.
Thanks to Levine’s concavity argument
[
40
]
, we are able to obtain a
sufficient condition for finite time blow-up with arbitrarily high initial
energy
J(u_{0};0)>0
.

Lemma 5.3

(
[
40
]
)
.

Assume that
F:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is differentiable on
[0,T]
,
and
F^{\prime}
is absolutely continuous on
[0,T]
with
F^{\prime}(0)>0
. If there exists
positive constant
\alpha>0
such that
FF^{\prime\prime}-(1+\alpha)F^{\prime}\geq 0
a.e. on
[0,T]
,
then
T\leq t^{*}\coloneqq\frac{F(0)}{\alpha F^{\prime}(0)}
, and there exists

t_{*}\in(0,t^{*}]
such that
F(t)\to+\infty
as
t\nearrow t_{*}
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Assume that
u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
. If

0<J(u_{0};0)<\frac{p^{-}-2}{2p^{-}}B_{2}^{2}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}
, then any solution to
problem (
1.1
) blows up in finite time.

Proof.

Assume that
u(t)
is a global solution to problem (
1.1
).
It follows from

\displaystyle I(u(t);t)

\displaystyle\leq p^{-}J(u(t);t)-\frac{p^{-}-2}{2}\|u(t)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq p^{-}J(u_{0};0)-\frac{p^{-}-2}{2}B_{2}^{2}\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle<\frac{p^{-}-2}{2}B_{2}^{2}\left(\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}-\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}\right)

(5.3)

that
I(u_{0};0)<0
. By employing an approach similar to the one
in the proof of Theorem
4.3
, we prove that
for
t\geq 0
,
u(t)\in\mathcal{N}_{-}(t)
.
If not, then there exists
t_{1}>0
such that

I(u(t_{1});t_{1})=0
and
I(u(t);t)<0
for
t\in[0,t_{1})
. Recall the function

F_{1}(t)
as defined in the proof of Theorem

4.3
. From

F_{1}(t_{1})\geq F_{1}(0)+\int_{0}^{t_{1}}F_{1}^{\prime}(\tau)d\tau\geq F_{1}(0)>0,

it follows that for
t\in[0,t_{1})
,

\displaystyle 0<J(u_{0};0)<\frac{p^{-}-2}{2p^{-}}B_{2}^{2}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{p^{-}-2}{2p^{-}}B_{2}^{2}\|u(t_{1})\|_{2}^{2}.

(5.4)

On the other hand, from (
5.3
) we have

J(u_{0};0)\geq J(u(t_{1});t_{1})\geq\frac{1}{p^{-}}I(u(t_{1});t_{1})+\frac{p^{-}-2}{2p^{-}}\|u(t_{1})\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}\geq\frac{p^{-}-2}{2p^{-}}B_{2}^{2}\|u(t_{1})\|_{(\alpha)}^{2},

which contradicts (
5.4
).
Therefore, for
t\geq 0
,
u(t)\in\mathcal{N}_{-}(t)
and
F_{1}(t)
is strictly
increasing.

Set
\eta\coloneqq\frac{p^{-}}{p^{-}-1}\left(\frac{p^{-}-2}{2p^{-}}B_{2}^{2}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}-J(u_{0};0)\right)
and

\sigma\coloneqq\frac{2\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}}{(p^{-}-2)\eta}
. For any
T^{\prime}>0
, we define

F_{2}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(\tau)\|_{2}^{2}d\tau+\frac{T^{\prime}-t}{2}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\eta}{2}(t+\sigma)^{2}

for
t\in[0,T^{\prime}]
. Then
F_{2}(t)
is differentiable on
[0,T^{\prime}]
, and for
all
t\in[0,T^{\prime}]
,

F_{2}^{\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+\eta(t+\sigma)=F_{1}(t)-F_{1}(0)+\eta(t+\sigma)>0,

which implies that
F_{2}(t)>0
for all
t\in[0,T^{\prime}]
.
Moreover,
F_{2}^{\prime}(t)
is absolutely continuous on
[0,T^{\prime}]
and

F_{2}^{\prime\prime}(t)=(u,u_{t})+\eta=-I(u(t);t)+\eta=F_{1}^{\prime}(t)+\eta

for almost all
t\in[0,T^{\prime}]
. From (
5.3
), it follows that
for a.e.
t\in[0,T^{\prime}]
,

\displaystyle F_{2}^{\prime\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\geq-p^{-}J(u(t);t)+\frac{p^{-}-2}{2}B_{2}^{2}\|u(t)\|_{2}^{2}+\eta

\displaystyle\geq-p^{-}J(u_{0};0)+p^{-}\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{t}u(\tau)\|_{2}^{2}d\tau+\frac{p^{-}-2}{2}B_{2}^{2}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+\eta.

(5.5)

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Hölder’s inequality
we know that for a.e.
t\in[0,T^{\prime}]
,

\varPhi(t)\coloneqq\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|u(\tau)\|_{2}^{2}d\tau+\eta(t+\sigma)^{2}\right)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{t}u(\tau)\|_{2}^{2}d\tau+\eta\right)-\left(\int_{0}^{t}(u,\partial_{t}u(\tau))d\tau+\eta(t+\sigma)\right)^{2}\geq 0.

Then for a.e.
t\in[0,T^{\prime}]
, it holds that

\displaystyle(F_{2}^{\prime}(t))^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{t}(u,\partial_{t}u(\tau))d\tau+\eta(t+\sigma)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(2F_{2}(t)-(T^{\prime}-t)\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\right)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{t}u(\tau)\|_{2}^{2}d\tau+\eta\right)-\varPhi(t)

\displaystyle\leq 2F_{2}(t)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{t}u(\tau)\|_{2}^{2}d\tau+\eta\right).

(5.6)

In view of (
5.5
) and (
5.6
), we deduce that

F_{2}(t)F_{2}^{\prime\prime}(t)-\frac{p^{-}}{2}(F_{2}^{\prime}(t))^{2}\geq F_{2}(t)\left(\frac{p^{-}-2}{2}B_{2}^{2}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}-p^{-}J(u_{0};0)-(p^{-}-1)\eta\right)=0

for a.e.
t\in[0,T^{\prime}]
. It follows from Lemma
5.3
that

T^{\prime}\leq\frac{2F_{2}(0)}{(p^{-}-2)F_{2}^{\prime}(0)}=\frac{T^{\prime}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+\eta\sigma^{2}}{(p^{-}-2)\eta\sigma}=\frac{T^{\prime}}{2}+\frac{2\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}}{(p^{-}-2)^{2}\eta},

namely,

T^{\prime}\leq\frac{4\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}}{(p^{-}-2)^{2}\eta}=\frac{8(p^{-}-1)\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}}{(p^{-}-2)^{2}\left((p^{-}-2)B_{2}^{2}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}-2p^{-}J(u_{0};0)\right)},

and there exists
t_{*}
such that

\lim_{t\nearrow t_{*}}\int_{0}^{t}\|u(\tau)\|_{2}^{2}d\tau=+\infty,

which contradicts the assumption that
u(t)
is a global solution.
Thus,
u(t)
blows up in finite time and its maximal existence interval

T_{*}
satisfies

T_{*}\leq\frac{8(p^{-}-1)\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}}{(p^{-}-2)^{2}\left((p^{-}-2)B_{2}^{2}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}-2p^{-}J(u_{0};0)\right)},

which concludes the proof.
∎

The section ends with a corollary of Theorem
5.4
.

Corollary 5.5
.

For any
R>0
, there exists a
u_{R}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
such that

J(u_{R};0)=R
and the solution of problem (
1.1
)
blows up in finite time with initial data
u_{R}
.

Proof.

Let
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
be two disjoint open subdomains of
\Omega
.
Consider an arbitrary
v_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega_{1})\backslash\{0\}
,
one can choose
\overline{\mu}>0
large enough such that

J(\overline{\mu}v_{0};0)\leq 0
and

\|\overline{\mu}v_{0}\|_{2}>B_{2}^{-2}\frac{2p^{-}}{p^{-}-2}R
.
On the other hand, for fixed
\overline{\mu}>0
, we choose

w_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega_{2})
such
that
J(w_{0};0)+J(\overline{\mu}v_{0};0)=R
. Then

u_{R}\coloneqq\overline{\mu}v_{0}+w_{0}
satisfies
J(u_{R};0)=R
, and
it follows that

\frac{p^{-}-2}{2p^{-}}B_{2}^{2}\|u_{R}\|_{2}\geq\frac{p^{-}-2}{2p^{-}}B_{2}^{2}\|\overline{\mu}v_{0}\|_{2}>R=J(u_{R};0)>0.

Thus the proof is completed by Theorem
5.4
.
∎

6
Lower bound for the lifespan

In this section, we shall establish a lower bound for the blow-up time.
The following lemma shows that when
p^{+}<\frac{2(N+4)}{N+2}
, blow-up in the

H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
-norm is equivalent to blow-up in the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
-norm.

Lemma 6.1
.

Assume that
p^{+}<\frac{2(N+4)}{N+2}
. Let
u(t)
be a solution to
problem (
1.1
) with maximal existence time
T_{*}<+\infty
. Then
the blow up also occurs in the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
-norm, that is,

\|u(t)\|_{2}\to+\infty
as
t\nearrow T_{*}
.

Proof.

For
t\in[0,T_{*})
, it follows from
J(u(t);t)\leq J(u_{0};0)
that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|u(t)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq J(u_{0};0)+\frac{k(t)}{p^{-}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p(x)}dx

\displaystyle\leq J(u_{0};0)+\frac{k(T_{*})}{p^{-}}|\Omega|+\frac{k(T_{*})}{p^{-}}\|\nabla u\|_{p^{+}}^{p^{+}}.

(6.1)

Using the Gagliardo-Nirenberg
inequality and Young’s inequality, we know that
there exists a positive constant
\widetilde{C}_{3}
such that

\|\nabla u\|_{p^{+}}^{p^{+}}\leq\widetilde{C}_{3}\|u\|_{(\alpha)}^{\theta p^{+}}\|u\|_{2}^{(1-\theta)p^{+}}\leq\widetilde{C}_{3}\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{\theta p^{+}}}\|u\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}+\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{2-\theta p^{+}}}\|u\|_{2}^{\frac{2(1-\theta)p^{+}}{2-\theta p^{+}}}\right)

(6.2)

holds for all
\varepsilon>0
,
where
\theta p^{+}=\frac{(N+2)p^{+}-2N}{4}<2
.
Choosing
\varepsilon=\left(\frac{4\widetilde{C}_{3}k(T_{*})}{p^{-}}\right)^{-\frac{\theta p^{+}}{2}}
in (
6.2
) and combining
it with (
6.1
), we obtain that

\|u(t)\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\sim\|u(t)\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}\leq 4J(u_{0};0)+\frac{4k(T_{*})}{p^{-}}|\Omega|+\left(\frac{4\widetilde{C}_{3}k(T_{*})}{p^{-}}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-\theta p^{+}}}\left(\|u\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{1+\frac{p^{+}-2}{2-\theta p^{+}}}.

Letting
t\nearrow T_{*}
concludes the proof.
∎

We now proceed to estimate a lower bound for the lifespan.

Theorem 6.2
.

Suppose that
u(t)
is a solution to
problem (
1.1
) with maximal existence time
T_{*}<+\infty
.
If
p^{+}<\frac{2(N+4)}{N+2}
, then there holds

T_{*}\geq\int_{F_{1}(0)}^{+\infty}\frac{dy}{C_{4}y^{r^{+}}+C_{5}y^{r^{-}}},

where
F_{1}(0)=\frac{1}{2}\|u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}
, and
r^{-}
,
r^{+}
,
C_{4}
,
C_{5}

are positive constants independent of
u(t)
that will be defined later.

Proof.

Recall the function
F_{1}(t)
defined in the proof of Theorem
4.3
.
A simple computation shows that for a.e.
t\in(0,T_{*})
,

\displaystyle F_{1}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=-I(u(t))=-\|u\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}+k(t)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p(x)}dx

\displaystyle\leq-\|u\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}+\kappa^{*}\|\nabla u\|_{p^{+}}^{p^{+}}+\kappa^{*}\|\nabla u\|_{p^{-}}^{p^{-}},

(6.3)

where
\kappa^{*}
denotes an arbitrary upper bound for
k(T_{*})
.
Choosing
\varepsilon=\left(2\kappa^{*}\widetilde{C}_{3}\right)^{-\frac{\theta p^{+}}{2}}
in (
6.2
), we deduce that for
all
t\in[0,T_{*})
,

\kappa^{*}\|\nabla u\|_{p^{+}}^{p^{+}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}+\left(2^{\frac{p^{+}}{2}}\kappa^{*}\widetilde{C}_{3}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-\theta p^{+}}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{\frac{(1-\theta)p^{+}}{2-\theta p^{+}}}=\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}+C_{4}(F_{1}(t))^{r^{+}},

(6.4)

where

C_{4}\coloneqq\left(2^{\frac{p^{+}}{2}}\kappa^{*}\widetilde{C}_{3}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-\theta p^{+}}}=\left(2^{\frac{p^{+}}{2}}\kappa^{*}\widetilde{C}_{3}\right)^{\frac{4}{2N+8-(N+2)p^{+}}}
,

r^{+}\coloneqq\frac{(1-\theta)p^{+}}{2-\theta p^{+}}=\frac{2N-(N-2)p^{+}}{2N+8-(N+2)p^{+}}
.
Similarly, utilizing the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality and Young’s inequality again,
we obtain that there exists a constant
\widetilde{C}_{4}>0
such that

\|\nabla u\|_{p^{-}}^{p^{-}}\leq\widetilde{C}_{4}\|u\|_{(\alpha)}^{\gamma p^{-}}\|u\|_{2}^{(1-\gamma)p^{-}}\leq\widetilde{C}_{4}\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{\gamma p^{-}}}\|u\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}+\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{2-\gamma p^{-}}}\|u\|_{2}^{\frac{2(1-\gamma)p^{-}}{2-\gamma p^{-}}}\right)

(6.5)

for all
\varepsilon>0
and
t\in[0,T_{*})
,
where
\gamma p^{-}=\frac{(N+2)p^{-}-2N}{4}<2
.
Choosing
\varepsilon=\left(2\kappa^{*}\widetilde{C}_{4}\right)^{-\frac{\gamma p^{-}}{2}}
in (
6.5
), we get

\kappa^{*}\|\nabla u\|_{p^{-}}^{p^{-}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{(\alpha)}^{2}+C_{5}(F_{1}(t))^{r^{-}},

(6.6)

where

C_{5}\coloneqq\left(2^{\frac{p^{-}}{2}}\kappa^{*}\widetilde{C}_{4}\right)^{\frac{4}{2N+8-(N+2)p^{-}}}
,

r^{-}\coloneqq\frac{2N-(N-2)p^{-}}{2N+8-(N+2)p^{-}}
.
Combining (
6.3
), (
6.4
), and (
6.6
),
we know that for a.e.
t\in(0,T_{*})
,

F_{1}^{\prime}(t)\leq C_{4}(F_{1}(t))^{r^{+}}+C_{5}(F_{1}(t))^{r^{-}}.

(6.7)

Integrating (
6.7
) from
0
to
t
, it follows that

\int_{F_{1}(0)}^{F_{1}(t)}\frac{dy}{C_{4}y^{r^{+}}+C_{5}y^{r^{-}}}\leq t

holds for all
t\in[0,T_{*})
.
Letting
t\nearrow T_{*}
, Lemma
6.1
implies that

F_{1}(t)\to+\infty
, which leads to

T_{*}\geq\int_{F_{1}(0)}^{+\infty}\frac{dy}{C_{4}y^{r^{+}}+C_{5}y^{r^{-}}}.

It is easy to check that the right-hand side of the above inequality is convergent,
as
2<p^{-}\leq p^{+}<\frac{2(N+4)}{N+2}
implies
r^{+}\geq r^{-}>1
.
This fact concludes the proof.
∎

7
Numerical experiments

We begin this section by performing a numerical discretization of (
1.1
)
on the two-dimensional rectangular domain

\Omega=(0,L_{x})\times(0,L_{y})
. This discretization is based on finite difference method and
Fourier spectral method, partially inspired by the work of A. Bueno-Orovio et
al.
[
6
]
. In the
x
and
y
directions, we select
N_{x}
and
N_{y}
nodes
respectively, which results in spatial grid sizes
of
\Delta x=L_{x}/(N_{x}+1)
,
\Delta y=L_{y}/(N_{y}+1)
.
Denote by
\Delta t
the time step size, then the spatio-temporal grid is given by

\begin{array}[]{ll}t_{n}=n\Delta t,&n=0,1,2,\cdots\\
x_{i}=i\Delta x,&y_{j}=j\Delta y,\quad i=0,\cdots,N_{x}+1,\quad j=0,\cdots,N_{y}+1.\end{array}

For any function
v(t,x,y)
, we denote by
v^{n}=\{v_{i,j}^{n}\}_{\forall i,j}
the
discrete function defined on the above grid, which approximates the value of

v(t_{n},x_{i},y_{j})
. The following notations and assumptions are introduced for
the formulation of the numerical scheme:

\displaystyle x_{-1}=-\Delta x,\ y_{-1}=-\Delta y,\ x_{N_{x}+2}=L_{x}+\Delta x,\ y_{N_{y}+2}=L_{y}+\Delta y,

\displaystyle u^{n}_{i,-1}=u^{n}_{i,0}=u^{n}_{i,N_{y}+1}=u^{n}_{i,N_{y}+2}=0,

\displaystyle u^{n}_{-1,j}=u^{n}_{0,j}=u^{n}_{N_{x}+1,j}=u^{n}_{N_{x}+2,j}=0,

\displaystyle u^{0}_{i,j}=u_{0}(x_{i},y_{j}),\ k^{n}=k(t_{n}),\ p_{i,j}=p(x_{i},y_{j}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}u=\left((-\Delta)^{2}+\alpha(-\Delta)^{2s}-\Delta\right)u,\ \mathcal{B}u=\operatorname*{div}\left(|\nabla u|^{p(x,y)-2}\nabla u\right).

We intend to use a semi-implicit scheme formally expressed as

\frac{u^{n+1}-u^{n}}{\Delta t}+(\mathcal{A}u)^{n+1}=-k^{n}(\mathcal{B}u)^{n}.

(7.1)

To this end, we begin by discretizing
(\mathcal{B}u)^{n}
.
For
i=0,\cdots,N_{x}+1,j=0,\cdots,N_{y}+1
, we define a grid function
c^{n}
given by

c^{n}_{i,j}=\left(\left(\frac{u^{n}_{i+1,j}-u^{n}_{i-1,j}}{2\Delta x}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{u^{n}_{i,j+1}-u^{n}_{i,j-1}}{2\Delta y}\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{p_{i,j}}{2}-1}.

For
i=1,\cdots,N_{x},j=1,\cdots,N_{y}
, set

c^{n}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}=\frac{c^{n}_{i+1,j}+c^{n}_{i,j}}{2},\ c^{n}_{i-\frac{1}{2},j}=\frac{c^{n}_{i,j}+c^{n}_{i-1,j}}{2},\ c^{n}_{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{c^{n}_{i,j+1}+c^{n}_{i,j}}{2},\ c^{n}_{i,j-\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{c^{n}_{i,j}+c^{n}_{i,j-1}}{2},

then
(\mathcal{B}u)^{n}
can be discretized using a standard central difference approach:

\displaystyle(\mathcal{B}u)_{i,j}^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{c^{n}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}\frac{u^{n}_{i+1,j}-u^{n}_{i,j}}{\Delta x}-c^{n}_{i-\frac{1}{2},j}\frac{u^{n}_{i,j}-u^{n}_{i-1,j}}{\Delta x}}{\Delta x}+\frac{c^{n}_{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}\frac{u^{n}_{i,j+1}-u^{n}_{i,j}}{\Delta y}-c^{n}_{i,j-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{u^{n}_{i,j}-u^{n}_{i,j-1}}{\Delta y}}{\Delta y}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2(\Delta x)^{2}}\left[(c^{n}_{i+1,j}+c^{n}_{i,j})u^{n}_{i+1,j}-(c^{n}_{i+1,j}+2c^{n}_{i,j}+c^{n}_{i-1,j})u^{n}_{i,j}+(c^{n}_{i,j}+c^{n}_{i-1,j})u^{n}_{i-1,j}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2(\Delta y)^{2}}\left[(c^{n}_{i,j+1}+c^{n}_{i,j})u^{n}_{i,j+1}-(c^{n}_{i,j+1}+2c^{n}_{i,j}+c^{n}_{i,j-1})u^{n}_{i,j}+(c^{n}_{i,j}+c^{n}_{i,j-1})u^{n}_{i,j-1}\right].

(7.2)

To handle the second term on the left-hand side of (
7.1
),
we use the two-dimensional discrete sine transform (DST). For a function

v=\{v_{i,j}\}_{\forall i,j}
defined on the aforementioned spatial grid,
its DST is given by

\widehat{v}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{x}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{y}}v_{i,j}\sin(\omega_{1}x_{i})\sin(\omega_{2}y_{j}),

and it can be reconstructed by the two-dimensional inverse discrete sine transform (IDST):

v_{i,j}=\frac{4}{(N_{x}+1)(N_{y}+1)}\sum_{m=1}^{N_{x}}\sum_{l=1}^{N_{y}}\widehat{v}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\sin(\omega_{1}x_{i})\sin(\omega_{2}y_{j}),

where

\omega_{1}=\frac{\pi m}{L_{x}},\ \omega_{2}=\frac{\pi l}{L_{y}},\ m=1,\cdots,N_{x},\ n=1,\cdots,N_{y}.

Let
\lambda(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=\omega_{1}^{2}+\omega_{2}^{2}
. An approximation
of the DST to
(\mathcal{A}u)^{n+1}
can then be obtained by

\widehat{(\mathcal{A}u)^{n+1}}=\left(\lambda^{2}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})+\lambda^{2s}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})+\lambda(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\right)\widehat{u}^{n+1}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2}).

Consequently, equation (
7.1
) can be computed by

\widehat{u}^{n+1}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=\frac{\widehat{u}^{n}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})-\Delta t\cdot k^{n}\widehat{(\mathcal{B}u)^{n}}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})}{1+\Delta t\left[\left(\omega_{1}^{2}+\omega_{2}^{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\omega_{1}^{2}+\omega_{2}^{2}\right)^{2s}+\omega_{1}^{2}+\omega_{2}^{2}\right]}.

Taking the IDST on both sides of the above equality yields
u^{n+1}
.

We now proceed to show how (
1.1
) can be applied to image sharpening.
In the subsequent experiments, we consistently set
\Delta t=5\times 10^{-4}
,

\alpha=-0.75
,
s=0.9
,
k(t)=0.1\times 5^{9t}
and

p(x,y)=\min\left\{3.5,3+0.1|\nabla u_{0}(x,y)|^{0.25}\right\}.

Let us revisit the analysis of the model presented in the introduction.
Under the above setting, the function
p(x,y)
is designed to take larger
values at locations where the initial gradient
|\nabla u_{0}|
is large.
When
t
is not too large so that
k(t)<1
, locations with larger

|\nabla u_{0}|
correspond to smaller thresholds
\mathfrak{T}
, favoring
the occurrence of backward diffusion; in contrast, locations with smaller

|\nabla u_{0}|
yield larger thresholds
\mathfrak{T}
, making forward
diffusion more likely. Moreover, since
k(t)
is designed to be increasing
in time, the threshold
\mathfrak{T}
decreases with time at each spatial
location, which encourages noise suppression in the very early stages
of the evolution and promotes rapid edge enhancement thereafter, ultimately
leading to better visual quality.

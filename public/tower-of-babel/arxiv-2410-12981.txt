Regular bipartite decompositions of pseudorandom graphs

Abstract
In 1972, Kotzig proved that for every even n n , the complete graph K n K_{n} can be decomposed into ⌈ log 2 ⁡ n ⌉ \lceil\log_{2}n\rceil edge-disjoint regular bipartite spanning subgraphs, which is best possible. In this paper, we study regular bipartite decompositions of ( n , d , λ ) (n,d,\lambda) -graphs, where n n is an even integer and d 0 ≤ d ≤ n − 1 d_{0}\leq d\leq n-1 for some absolute constant d 0 d_{0} . This is best possible up to the additive constant term. As a consequence, we also improve the best known bounds on λ = λ ⁡ ( d ) \lambda=\lambda(d) by Ferber and Jain (2020) to guarantee that an ( n , d , λ ) (n,d,\lambda) -graph on an even number of vertices admits a 1 1 -factorization, showing that λ ≤ c ​ d \lambda\leq cd is sufficient for some absolute constant c > 0 c>0 .

Introduction

Packing and covering problems have a long history in graph theory. A
covering
of a graph
G
is a collection
\mathcal{C}
of subgraphs of
G
such that
E(G)=\bigcup_{H\in\mathcal{C}}E(H)
. If the graphs in
H
are edge-disjoint, then
\mathcal{C}
is called a
decomposition
, or
packing
, of
G
. Typical problems in the area ask for the minimum size of a decomposition
\mathcal{C}
(or covering) of a fixed graph
G
, subject to each subgraph in
\mathcal{C}
having a certain form. For example, one might look for a decomposition (or covering) into matchings
[
25
]
, paths
[
21
,
8
]
, cycles
[
4
,
13
,
20
,
8
]
, triangles
[
15
]
, cliques
[
7
]
, or bicliques
[
12
]
.

In this paper, we consider decompositions of a graph
G
into regular bipartite spanning subgraphs.
This type of decomposition problem was first studied by Kotzig
[
16
]
, who proved the following exact result for the complete graph
K_{n}
on an even number of vertices.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
be an even integer. Then the edge set of the complete graph
K_{n}
can be decomposed into
\lceil\log_{2}n\rceil
regular bipartite spanning subgraphs, which is best possible.

We aim to extend
Theorem

1.1
to a larger class of graphs. For a graph
G
to admit a decomposition into regular bipartite spanning subgraphs,
G
must be regular (
1
-factorizable, in fact) on an even number of vertices. Our main result concerns regular bipartite decompositions of regular
pseudorandom
graphs. Specifically, we are interested in the class of
(n,d,\lambda)
-graphs
. These graphs play an important role in combinatorics and theoretical computer science, including sampling, complexity theory, and
the design of error-correcting codes
[
2
,
14
,
22
]
. These are defined in the following way. Given a graph
G
on vertex set
V=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
, its
adjacency matrix

A:=A(G)
is an
n\times n
,
0/1
matrix, defined by
A_{ij}=1
if and only if
v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)
. Let
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\ldots\geq\lambda_{n}
be the eigenvalues of
A
. It is a well-known fact that for a
d
-regular graph
G
, we always have
\lambda_{1}=d
and
\lambda_{n}\geq-d
. We say that
G
is an
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph
if it is a
d
-regular graph on
n
vertices with
\lambda(G)\coloneqq\max\{|\lambda_{2}|,|\lambda_{n}|\}\leq\lambda
.

Decomposition problems in pseudorandom graphs have been intensively studied in recent years. For example, the
perfect matching
, or
1
-factor
, which is a collection of vertex-disjoint edges covering all the vertices, is the most fundamental type of regular bipartite spanning subgraph. A decomposition of a graph into perfect matchings is called a
1
-factorization
. For finding a single perfect matching, Krivelevich and Sudakov
[
18
]
showed that an
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph with an even number of vertices contains a perfect matching whenever
\lambda\leq d-2
. More precise spectral conditions to guarantee a perfect matching were later given by Cioabǎ, Gregory and Haemers
[
5
]
. As for
1
-factorizations of
(n,d,\lambda)
-graphs, Ferber and Jain
[
10
]
proved the following result.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem 1.1 in
[
10
]
)
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
d_{0},n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all even integers
n\geq n_{0}
and for all
d\geq d_{0}
the following holds. Suppose that
G
is an
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph with
\lambda\leq d^{1-\varepsilon}
. Then the edge set of
G
can be decomposed into
d
edge-disjoint perfect matchings.

Another important decomposition problem which has received much attention is that of decomposing a graph into edge-disjoint
Hamilton cycles
, which are cycles passing through all the vertices. Hamilton cycles are one of the most studied objects in graph theory, and determining if a graph contains a single Hamilton cycle is an NP-complete problem. The existence of Hamilton cycles in
(n,d,\lambda)
-graphs was first studied by Krivelevich and Sudakov
[
17
]
, who proved that for sufficiently large
n
, any
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph with

\lambda/d\leq\frac{(\log\log n)^{2}}{1000\log n(\log\log\log n)}

has a Hamilton cycle. They also conjectured in the same paper that the condition can be reduced to
\lambda/d\leq c
for some absolute constant

. After some intermediate results by Glock, Correia and Sudakov
[
11
]
, and Ferber, Han, Mao and Vershynin
[
9
]
, the conjecture was finally proved very recently by Draganić, Montgomery, Correia, Pokrovskiy and Sudakov
[
6
]
. Regarding the corresponding decomposition problem, the best known result was given by Kühn and Osthus
[
19
]
(see also Theorem 1.11 in
[
20

), who proved that for any

and sufficiently large even integer

, any
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph with
d\geq\alpha n
and
\lambda\leq\varepsilon n
for some constant
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\alpha)
can be decomposed into edge-disjoint Hamilton cycles.

We now state our main result, which says that for sufficiently large

, any
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph on an even number of vertices with

can be decomposed into

Theorem 1.3
.

There exists a positive integer
d_{0}

admits a decomposition into at most

Our proof of Theorem

Since any regular bipartite graph can be decomposed into perfect matchings,

[

Corollary 1.4

There exists an integer
d_{0}
such that the following holds for every integer

and every even integer

G
be an
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph with
\lambda\leq\frac{d}{12}

-factorization.

1.2
Proof outline

We use a randomized algorithm to decompose the
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph
G
into
\log_{2}d+O(1)
regular bipartite spanning subgraphs. Roughly speaking, we first set aside a specially chosen “absorber”, then we decompose the remaining graph into
almost
regular bipartite spanning subgraphs. Our absorber will simply be an induced balanced bipartite subgraph
G[X,Y]
, where the bipartition
\{X,Y\}
is chosen randomly so that
G[X,Y]
is almost
\frac{d}{2}
-regular. The success of our procedure relies on the following remarkable property of
G
: for this choice of
\{X,Y\}
, the graph
G[X,Y]
can “regularize” just about any pair
H_{X}
,
H_{Y}
of almost regular bipartite spanning subgraphs of
G[X]
and
G[Y]
, respectively (see Lemmas
4.5
and
4.6
), in the sense that we can robustly find a sparse subgraph
R
of
G[X,Y]
such that
H_{X}\cup H_{Y}\cup R
is regular and bipartite. Therefore, after obtaining suitable decompositions
\{H_{X,j}\}_{j}
and
\{H_{Y,j}\}_{j}
of
G[X]
and
G[Y]
, respectively, we can find edge-disjoint subgraphs
\{R_{j}\}_{j}
of
G[X,Y]
so that each
H_{X,j}\cup H_{Y,j}\cup R_{j}
is a regular bipartite spanning subgraph of
G
. Since
G
was regular to begin with, the remaining subgraph
H\subseteq G[X,Y]
will be regular as well, so
\{H_{X,j}\cup H_{Y,j}\cup R_{j}\}_{j}\cup\{H\}
will be a decomposition of
G
into regular bipartite spanning subgraphs.

To illustrate the regularization step in more detail, suppose that
H_{X}
and
H_{Y}
have respective bipartitions
\{X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime}\}
and
\{Y^{\prime},Y^{\prime\prime}\}
such that
|X^{\prime}|=|Y^{\prime}|
,
|X^{\prime\prime}|=|Y^{\prime\prime}|
, and both
G[X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}]
and

have minimum degree at least
\frac{d}{5}
. Further suppose that
H_{X}
and
H_{Y}
have the same number of edges. A theorem of Ore (

and

R^{\prime}
and
R^{\prime\prime}
with prescribed degrees so that
H_{X}\cup H_{Y}\cup R^{\prime}\cup R^{\prime\prime}
is regular. This condition is easily verified, with room to spare, by the expander mixing lemma (

\lambda\leq\frac{d}{12}
. Note that

Moreover, the graphs

With the help of the Lovász Local Lemma (Lemmas

At last, we can use our absorber

Preliminaries

2.1
Probabilistic tools

Throughout the paper, we will extensively use the following well-known Chernoff’s bound (see, e.g., in
[
1
]
).

Theorem 2.1

(Chernoff’s bounds)
.

Let
X\sim\mathrm{Bin}\left(n,\frac{1}{2}\right)
. Then for any
t\geq 0
, we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|X-\frac{n}{2}\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{2t^{2}}{n}\right).

We will also make use of the following, known as the Lovász Local Lemma (see, e.g., in
[
1
]
). Before stating the lemma we need to introduce some notation: We say that a digraph
D
(possibly with loops) is a
dependency digraph
for a set
\mathcal{A}
of events if
V(D)=\mathcal{A}
, and every event
A\in\mathcal{A}
is mutually independent of the set
\mathcal{A}\setminus(\{A\}\cup N_{D}^{+}(A))
.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a set of events determined by a set
\mathcal{Z}=\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{k}\}
of independent random variables. Specifically, suppose that each event
A\in\mathcal{A}
is determined by some subset
{\mathcal{Z}_{A}\subseteq\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{k}\}}
. Then any digraph
D
on
\mathcal{A}
satisfying

\{A^{\prime}:A^{\prime}\neq A,\,\mathcal{Z}_{A^{\prime}}\cap\mathcal{Z}_{A}\neq\emptyset\}\subseteq N_{D}^{+}(A)

for each event
A\in\mathcal{A}
is a dependency digraph for
\mathcal{A}
.

We are now ready to state the lemma.

Lemma 2.3

(Lovász Local Lemma (asymmetric version))
.

Let
(A_{i})^{n}_{i=1}
be a sequence of events in some probability space. Suppose that
D
is a dependency graph for
(A_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
, and suppose that there exist real numbers
(\alpha_{i})^{n}_{i=1}
, such that
0\leq\alpha_{i}<1
, and

\mathbb{P}\left(A_{i}\right)\leq\alpha_{i}\prod_{(A_{i},A_{j})\in E(D)}(1-\alpha_{j}).

for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Then
\mathbb{P}\left(\bar{A}_{i}\right)\geq\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-\alpha_{i})
.

For certain applications, the following symmetric version of the Lovász Local Lemma will be enough.

Lemma 2.4

(Lovász Local Lemma (symmetric version))
.

Let
(A_{i})^{n}_{i=1}
be a sequence of events in some probability space. Suppose that
D
is a dependency digraph for
(A_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
with maximum out-degree at most
\Delta
. If
{\mathbb{P}\left(A_{i}\right)\leq\frac{1}{e(\Delta+1)}}

for all
1\leq i\leq n
, then
\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\bar{A}_{i}\right)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{\Delta+1}\right)^{n}
.

\left|e_{G}(S,T)-\frac{d}{n}|S||T|\right|\leq\lambda\sqrt{|S||T|}.

Theorem 2.6

(Vizing’s theorem)
.

Every graph with maximum degree

(

.

Let

for

3
Almost regular decomposition via random bisections

The results of this section involve nothing more than routine applications of Chernoff’s bounds (
Theorem

2.1
) and the Lovász Local Lemma (Lemmas
2.3
and
2.4
). The basic underlying principle for each of these results is the following: in a random (nearly) balanced bipartition of the vertex set of an almost regular graph, one can ensure that the neighborhood of each vertex is nearly bisected as well. This principle is most simply illustrated by our first lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists a positive integer
d_{0}
such that the following holds for all integers
d\geq d_{0}
. Let
n\geq d+1
be an even integer, and let
G
be a
d
-regular graph on
[n]
. Then there exists a balanced bipartition
\{X,Y\}
of
[n]
such that for each graph
H\in\{G[X,Y],G[X],G[Y]\}
, we have
{\deg_{H}(v)=\frac{d}{2}\pm d^{2/3}}
for every
v\in V(H)
.

Proof.

We choose
\{X,Y\}
randomly according to some distribution on the balanced bipartitions of
[n]
. Rather than use a uniform distribution, we instead determine
\{X,Y\}
by the outcome of
\frac{n}{2}
independent random choices, which will give us enough independence to use the symmetric version of the Lovász Local Lemma (
Lemma

2.4
). Let
M=\{u_{1}v_{1},\ldots,u_{n/2}v_{n/2}\}
be any fixed perfect matching in the underlying complete graph
K_{n}
. For each
i=1,\ldots,\frac{n}{2}
, independently select exactly one of
u_{i}
or
v_{i}
to be in
X
and the other to be in
Y
, with equal probability
\frac{1}{2}
. We will refer to this random decision as
s_{i}
.

For each vertex
v\in[n]
, let
A_{v}
be the event that
|N_{G}(v)\cap X|\notin\frac{d}{2}\pm d^{2/3}
. It suffices to show that for sufficiently large
d
, we have with positive probability that no
A_{v}
occurs.

Fix a vertex
v
. Let
M_{v}\subseteq M
be the set of pairs in
M
with both endpoints in
N_{G}(v)
. Let
{M^{\prime}_{v}\subseteq M}
be the set of pairs in
M
with exactly one endpoint in
N_{G}(v)
. Each pair from
M_{v}
will contribute
1
to
|N_{G}(v)\cap X|
with probability
1
, and each pair from
M^{\prime}_{v}
will contribute
1
or
0
independently with probability
\frac{1}{2}
. Thus
|N_{G}(v)\cap X|-|M_{v}|\sim\mathrm{Bin}\left(|M^{\prime}_{v}|,\frac{1}{2}\right)
. Since
d=2|M_{v}|+|M^{\prime}_{v}|
, we have by Chernoff’s bounds (
Theorem

2.1
) that

\mathbb{P}(A_{v})=\mathbb{P}\left(\left||N_{G}(v)\cap X|-|M_{v}|-\frac{|M^{\prime}_{v}|}{2}\right|>d^{2/3}\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{2d^{4/3}}{|M^{\prime}_{v}|}\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-2d^{1/3}\right).

We now construct a dependency digraph
D
for the events
\{A_{v}\}
. An event
A_{v}
is determined by the those decisions
s_{i}
for which
\{u_{i},v_{i}\}\in M^{\prime}_{v}
. By Remark
2.2
, we can thus construct
D
by including an edge from
A_{v}
to
A_{v^{\prime}}
whenever there is a sequence
(v,u,u^{\prime},v^{\prime})
of vertices in
[n]
such that
vu,u^{\prime}v^{\prime}\in E(G)
, and either
u=u^{\prime}
or
\{u,u^{\prime}\}\in M
. Note that
\Delta:=\Delta^{+}(D)\leq 2d^{2}
, so for sufficiently large
d
, we have

\mathbb{P}(A_{v})\leq 2\exp\left(-2d^{1/3}\right)\leq\frac{1}{e(\Delta+1)}

for every event
A_{v}
. By the symmetric version of the Lovász Local Lemma (
Lemma

2.4
), we have with positive probability that no event
A_{v}
occurs. This completes the proof.
∎

Our next lemma follows the same principle, only now we have two graphs: one bipartite graph
H
with bipartition
\{X,Y\}
, and another graph
G_{X}
contained entirely within the part
X
. We wish to bisect
X
in such a way that every
G_{X}
-neighborhood is nearly bisected, and also so that the
H
-neighborhood of every vertex in
Y
is nearly bisected. The latter condition will come up frequently, so we make the following definition.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
H=(X\cup Y,E)
be a balanced bipartite graph on
[n]=X\cup Y
, and let
d>0
. We say that a bipartition
\{X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime}\}
of
X
is
d
-good
with respect to
H
if the following two conditions hold:

(G1)

\left||X^{\prime}|-|X^{\prime\prime}|\right|\leq 1
;

(G2)

|N_{H}(y)\cap X^{\prime}|,|N_{H}(y)\cap X^{\prime\prime}|\geq d
for every
y\in Y
.

We define a
d
-good
bipartition of
Y
with respect to
H
analogously.

Lemma 3.3
.

There exists a positive integer
d_{0}
such that the following holds for all
d\geq d_{0}
. Let
0<\varepsilon\leq\frac{5}{8}
, and let
2^{18}\leq d^{\prime}\leq d
. Let
n
be a positive even integer, and let
H=(X\cup Y,E)
be a balanced bipartite graph on
[n]
satisfying
\deg_{H}(v)=\frac{d}{2}\pm d^{2/3}
for every
v\in[n]
. Let
G_{X}
be a graph on
X
satisfying
\deg_{G_{X}}(x)=(1\pm\varepsilon)d^{\prime}
for every
x\in X
. Then there exists a bipartition
\{X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime}\}
of
X
such that the following two properties hold:

(L1)

the graph
H_{X}:=G_{X}[X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime}]
satisfies

\deg_{H_{X}}(x)=\left(1\pm\frac{2}{(d^{\prime})^{1/3}}\right)\frac{\deg_{G_{X}}(x)}{2}

for every
x\in X
.

(L2)

\{X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime}\}
is
\frac{d}{5}
-good with respect to
H
.

Proof.

We choose
\{X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime}\}
randomly according to the following distribution, similar to the proof of
Lemma

3.1
. Let

\tilde{X}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l l}X&\text{if $|X|$ is even},\\
X\cup\{\tilde{v}\}&\text{if $|X|$ is odd},\end{array}\right.

where
\tilde{v}\notin[n]
is a dummy vertex. Let
M=\{u_{1}v_{1},\ldots,u_{\lceil n/4\rceil}v_{\lceil n/4\rceil}\}
be a matching in the underlying complete graph on
\tilde{X}
. For each
i=1,\ldots,\left\lceil\frac{n}{4}\right\rceil
, we independently select exactly one of
u_{i}
or
v_{i}
to be in
X^{\prime}
and the other to be in
X^{\prime\prime}
, with equal probability
\frac{1}{2}
. As an exception to this rule, the dummy vertex
\tilde{v}
is never added to
X^{\prime}
or
X^{\prime\prime}
, so we have that
\{X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime}\}
is a bipartition of
X
.
We denote this random decision by
s_{i}
.

For
x\in X
, let
A_{x}
denote the event that
|N_{G_{X}}(x)\cap X^{\prime}|\notin\left(1\pm\frac{2}{(d^{\prime})^{1/3}}\right)\frac{\deg_{G_{X}}(x)}{2}
, and for
y\in Y
, let
B_{y}
denote the event that
|N_{H}(y)\cap X^{\prime}|\notin\frac{\deg_{H}(y)}{2}\pm\left(\frac{d}{20}-\frac{d^{2/3}}{2}\right)
. We will show using the asymmetric version of the Lovász Local Lemma that with positive probability, none of these events occur.

For
x\in X
, let
M_{x}\subseteq M
denote the set of pairs in
M
with both endpoints in
N_{G_{X}}(x)
, and let
M_{x}^{\prime}\subseteq M
denote the set of pairs in
M
with exactly one endpoint in
N_{G_{X}}(x)
. Define
M_{y}
and
M_{y}^{\prime}
analogously for
y\in Y
.

Fix a vertex
x\in X
. Each pair from
M_{x}
will contribute
1
to
|N_{G_{X}}(x)\cap X^{\prime}|
with probability
1
, and each pair from
M^{\prime}_{x}
will contribute
1
or
0
independently with probability
\frac{1}{2}
. Thus

{|N_{G_{X}}(x)\cap X^{\prime}|-|M_{x}|\sim\mathrm{Bin}\left(|M^{\prime}_{x}|,\frac{1}{2}\right)}.

Since
\deg_{G_{X}}(x)=2|M_{x}|+|M^{\prime}_{x}|
and
\varepsilon\leq\frac{5}{8}
, we have by Chernoff’s bounds (
Theorem

2.1
) that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(A_{x}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\left||N_{G_{X}}(x)\cap X^{\prime}|-|M_{x}|-\frac{|M^{\prime}_{x}|}{2}\right|>\frac{\deg_{G_{X}}(x)}{(d^{\prime})^{1/3}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{2(\deg_{G_{X}}(x))^{2}}{(d^{\prime})^{2/3}|M^{\prime}_{x}|}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{2\deg_{G_{X}}(x)}{(d^{\prime})^{2/3}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{3(d^{\prime})^{1/3}}{4}\right).

We similarly have for each
y\in Y
that

|N_{H}(y)\cap X^{\prime}|-|M_{y}|\sim\mathrm{Bin}\left(|M^{\prime}_{y}|,\frac{1}{2}\right),

and
{\deg_{H}(y)=2|M_{y}|+|M^{\prime}_{y}|}
, so

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(B_{y})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\left||N_{H}(y)\cap X^{\prime}|-|M_{y}|-\frac{|M_{y}^{\prime}|}{2}\right|>\frac{d}{20}-\frac{d^{2/3}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{2\left(\frac{d}{20}-\frac{d^{2/3}}{2}\right)^{2}}{\frac{d}{2}+d^{2/3}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\frac{d}{120}\right)

for sufficiently large
d
.

Note that each event
A_{x}
is determined by those decisions
s_{i}
for which
\{u_{i},v_{i}\}\cap N_{G_{X}}(x)\neq\emptyset
, and each event
B_{y}
is determined by those decisions
s_{i}
for which
\{u_{i},v_{i}\}\cap N_{H}(y)\neq\emptyset
. Therefore, since
G_{X}
has maximum degree at most
(1+\varepsilon)d^{\prime}
, and
H
has maximum degree at most
\frac{d}{2}+d^{2/3}
, it follows from
Remark

2.2
that there exists a dependency digraph
D
for
\{A_{x}:x\in X\}\cup\{B_{y}:y\in Y\}
with

\displaystyle\#\{x^{\prime}:(A_{x},A_{x^{\prime}})\in E(D)\}

\displaystyle\leq 2(1+\varepsilon)^{2}(d^{\prime})^{2}\leq 6(d^{\prime})^{2}

\displaystyle\text{for each $x\in X$};

\displaystyle\#\{y:(A_{x},B_{y})\in E(D)\}

\displaystyle\leq 2(1+\varepsilon)d^{\prime}\left(\frac{d}{2}+d^{2/3}\right)\leq 2d^{\prime}d

\displaystyle\text{for each $x\in X$};

\displaystyle\#\{x:(B_{y},A_{x})\in E(D)\}

\displaystyle\leq 2(1+\varepsilon)d^{\prime}\left(\frac{d}{2}+d^{2/3}\right)\leq 2d^{\prime}d

\displaystyle\text{for each $y\in Y$};

\displaystyle\#\{y^{\prime}:(B_{y},B_{y^{\prime}})\in E(D)\}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\frac{d}{2}+d^{2/3}\right)^{2}\leq d^{2}

\displaystyle\text{for each $y\in Y$}.

Let
\alpha:=\frac{1}{12(d^{\prime})^{2}}
, and let
\beta:=\frac{1}{d^{3}}
. For every
x\in X
, we have for sufficiently large
d
that

\displaystyle\alpha(1-\alpha)^{6(d^{\prime})^{2}}(1-\beta)^{2d^{\prime}d}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha\left(1-6\alpha(d^{\prime})^{2}\right)\left(1-2\beta d^{\prime}d\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{12(d^{\prime})^{2}}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(1-\frac{2}{d}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 2\exp\left(-\frac{3(d^{\prime})^{1/3}}{4}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}(A_{x}),

since
2^{18}\leq d^{\prime}\leq d
. We also have for every
y\in Y
and sufficiently large
d
that

\displaystyle\beta(1-\alpha)^{2d^{\prime}d}(1-\beta)^{d^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\beta\left(1-2\alpha d^{\prime}\right)^{d}\left(1-\beta d^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{d^{3}}\left(1-\frac{1}{6\cdot 2^{18}}\right)^{d}\left(1-\frac{1}{d}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left(-\frac{d}{120}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}(B_{y}).

Therefore, by the asymmetric version of the Lovász Local Lemma (
Lemma

2.3
), we have with positive probability that no event
A_{x}
nor
B_{y}
occurs. The fact that no
A_{x}
occurs ensures
(L1)
, and the fact that no
B_{y}
occurs ensures
(G2)
with
\frac{d}{5}
in place of
d
. Note that
(G1)
is satisfied automatically by design, so
(L2)
is satisfied. This completes the proof.
∎

After applying
Lemma

3.1
to bisect the vertex set of our
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph
G
into
\{X,Y\}
,
Lemma

3.3
can be iterated to efficiently pull out edge-disjoint almost regular bipartite subgraphs from
G[X]
until the remaining subgraph
G_{X}
of
G[X]
has constant maximum degree. The following lemma allows us to decompose this remainder
G_{X}
into a constant number of bipartite subgraphs, each with a bipartition which is
\frac{d}{5}
-good with respect to
G[X,Y]
. To accomplish this, we take constantly many independent random bisections
\left\{\{X^{\prime}_{j},X^{\prime\prime}_{j}\}\right\}_{j}
of
X
so that each vertex in
Y
has large
G
-degree in each
X^{\prime}_{j}
and
X^{\prime\prime}_{j}
, and also so that each
G_{X}
-edge crosses at least one of the bisections
\{X^{\prime}_{j},X^{\prime\prime}_{j}\}
. Thus there exists a decomposition of
G_{X}
into bipartite subgraphs with these bipartitions. Since each bipartite subgraph obtained in this way will have bounded maximum degree, we do not need to concentrate the
G_{X}
-degrees in these bisections in order to be able to apply our regularization step in
Section

4
(specifically,
(A1)
in
Lemma

4.6
will be satisfied automatically).

Lemma 3.4
.

There exists a positive integer
d_{0}
such that the following holds for all
d\geq d_{0}
. Let
n
be a positive even integer, and let
H=(X\cup Y,E)
be a balanced bipartite graph on
[n]
satisfying
\deg_{H}(v)=\frac{d}{2}\pm d^{2/3}
for every
v\in[n]
. Let
G_{X}
be a graph on
X
with maximum degree at most
\Delta
, where
1\leq\Delta\leq d
. Then there exist
k:=\lceil\log_{2}\Delta\rceil+8
bipartitions
\{X^{\prime}_{1},X^{\prime\prime}_{1}\},\ldots,\{X^{\prime}_{k},X^{\prime\prime}_{k}\}
of
X
such that the following two properties hold:

(M1)

for every edge
e\in E(G_{X})
, there is at least one index
j\in[k]
such that
e
crosses
\{X^{\prime}_{j},X^{\prime\prime}_{j}\}
;

(M2)

for each
1\leq j\leq k
,
\{X^{\prime}_{j},X^{\prime\prime}_{j}\}
is
\frac{d}{5}
-good with respect to
H
.

Proof.

We choose the bipartitions
\{X^{\prime}_{1},X^{\prime\prime}_{1}\},\ldots,\{X^{\prime}_{k},X^{\prime\prime}_{k}\}
independently at random according to the distribution described in the proof of
Lemma

3.3
. Specifically, we introduce a dummy vertex
{\tilde{v}\notin[n]}
, and we define

\tilde{X}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l l}X&\text{if $|X|$ is even},\\
X\cup\{\tilde{v}\}&\text{if $|X|$ is odd}.\end{array}\right.

We fix a perfect matching
M=\{u_{1}v_{1},\ldots,u_{\lceil n/4\rceil}v_{\lceil n/4\rceil}\}
in the underlying complete graph on
\tilde{X}
, and for each
i=1,\ldots,\left\lceil\frac{n}{4}\right\rceil
and each
j=1,\ldots,k
, we independently select exactly one of
u_{i}
or
v_{i}
to be in
X^{\prime}_{j}
and the other to be in
X^{\prime\prime}_{j}
(excepting
\tilde{v}
), with equal probability
\frac{1}{2}
.
We denote this random decision by
s_{i,j}
.

For
e\in E(G_{X})
, let
A_{e}
be the event that
(M1)
fails for
e
. For
y\in Y
and
j\in[k]
, let
B_{y,j}
be the event that
|N_{H}(y)\cap X_{j}^{\prime}|\notin\frac{\deg_{H}(y)}{2}\pm\left(\frac{d}{20}-\frac{d^{2/3}}{2}\right)
. For every
j\in[k]
and
e\in E(G_{X})
, the probability that
e
crosses
\{X^{\prime}_{j},X^{\prime\prime}_{j}\}
is either
1
or
\frac{1}{2}
, depending on whether
e\in M
or not. Thus we have

\mathbb{P}(A_{e})\leq 2^{-k}\leq\frac{1}{256\Delta}

for every
e\in E(G_{X})
, and

\mathbb{P}(B_{y,j})\leq\exp\left(-\frac{d}{120}\right)

for every
y\in Y
and
j\in[k]
(see the proof of
Lemma

3.3
).

Note that each event
A_{e}
is determined by those decisions
s_{i,j}
for which
\{u_{i},v_{i}\}\cap e\neq\emptyset
, and each
B_{y,j}
is determined by those decisions
s_{i,j}
for which
\{u_{i},v_{i}\}\cap N_{H}(y)\neq\emptyset
. Therefore, since
G_{X}
has maximum degree at most
\Delta
, and
H
has maximum degree at most
\frac{d}{2}+d^{2/3}
, it follows from
Remark

2.2
that there exists a dependency digraph
D
for
\{A_{e}:e\in E(G_{X})\}\cup\{B_{y,j}:y\in Y,\,j\in[k]\}
with

\displaystyle\#\{e^{\prime}:(A_{e},A_{e^{\prime}})\in E(D)\}

\displaystyle\leq 4\Delta

\displaystyle\text{for each $e\in E(G_{X})$};

\displaystyle\#\{(y,j):(A_{e},B_{y,j})\in E(D)\}

\displaystyle\leq 4\left(\frac{d}{2}+d^{2/3}\right)k\leq 4dk

\displaystyle\text{for each $e\in E(G_{X})$};

\displaystyle\#\{e:(B_{y,j},A_{e})\in E(D)\}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\frac{d}{2}+d^{2/3}\right)\Delta\leq 2\Delta d

\displaystyle\text{for each $y\in Y$, $j\in[k]$};

\displaystyle\#\{(y^{\prime},j^{\prime}):(B_{y,j},B_{y^{\prime},j^{\prime}})\in E(D)\}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\frac{d}{2}+d^{2/3}\right)^{2}\leq d^{2}

\displaystyle\text{for each $y\in Y$, $j\in[k]$}.

Set
\alpha:=\frac{1}{250\Delta}
and
\beta:=\frac{1}{d^{3}}
. For this choice of parameters, we have for sufficiently large
d
that

\displaystyle\alpha(1-\alpha)^{4\Delta}(1-\beta)^{4dk}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha(1-4\alpha\Delta)(1-4\beta dk)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{250\Delta}\cdot\frac{123}{125}\left(1-\frac{4k}{d^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}(A_{e})

for each
e\in E(G_{X})
, and

\displaystyle\beta(1-\alpha)^{2\Delta d}(1-\beta)^{d^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\beta(1-2\alpha\Delta)^{d}(1-\beta d^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d^{3}}\left(\frac{124}{125}\right)^{d}\left(1-\frac{1}{d}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2d^{3}}\exp\left(-\frac{d}{124}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}(B_{y,j})

for each
y\in Y
,
j\in[k]
. Here we used that
1-x=\left(1+\frac{x}{1-x}\right)^{-1}\geq\exp\left(-\frac{x}{1-x}\right)
for every
x<1
. Therefore, by the asymmetric version of the Lovász Local Lemma (
Lemma

2.3
), we have with positive probability that none of the events
A_{e}
nor
B_{y,j}
occur, so both
(M1)
and
(M2)
hold.
∎

4
Regularization

4.1
Pairing subgraphs with the same number of edges

In this subsection, we prove two simple lemmas which allow us to fine-tune the number of edges each bipartite subgraph has in our decomposition at different stages. Recall that we first take a bisection
\{X,Y\}
of the vertex set of our
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph
G
. By iteratively applying
Lemma

3.3
, then finally applying
Lemma

3.4
to leftover graphs
G_{X}
and
G_{Y}
of constant maximum degree, we obtain decompositions
\{H_{X,j}\}_{j}
and
\{H_{Y,j}\}_{j}
of
G[X]
and
G[Y]
into almost regular bipartite spanning subgraphs of
G
. Our next two lemmas will be used to ensure that
e(H_{X,j})=e(H_{Y,j})
for each
j
. The first one will be applied repeatedly, after each iterative application of
Lemma

3.3
, while the second one will be applied once at the very end to the decompositions of
G_{X}
and
G_{Y}
obtained via
Lemma

3.4
. These applications can be viewed as “correcting” steps that will make sure that the decompositions we obtain satisfy the necessary properties (specifically
(A2)
) to apply our main regularization lemma (
Lemma

4.6
) and obtain the final desired decomposition.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
H
be a nonempty graph, and let
m\leq e(H)
be a positive integer. Then there exists an
m
-edge spanning subgraph
H^{\prime}\subseteq H
satisfying the following properties:

(S1)

\Delta(H^{\prime})-\delta(H^{\prime})\leq\Delta(H)-\delta(H)+2
;

(S2)

\Delta(H\setminus H^{\prime})\leq(\Delta(H)+1)\frac{e(H)-m}{e(H)}+1.

Proof.

If
e(H)=m
already, then we simply take
H^{\prime}:=H
. Otherwise, by Vizing’s theorem (
Theorem

2.6
), there exists a partition
E(H)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\Delta+1}M_{i}
of
E(H)
into
\Delta+1
matchings
M_{1},\ldots,M_{\Delta+1}
, where
\Delta:=\Delta(H)
. We order the matchings so that
|M_{1}|\geq\cdots\geq|M_{\Delta+1}|
. Let
t
be the minimum positive integer such that
{\sum_{i=1}^{t}|M_{i}|\geq e(H)-m}
. Let
M_{t}^{\prime}
be a submatching of
M_{t}
such that
{\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}|M_{i}|+|M^{\prime}_{t}|=e(H)-m}
. Let
H^{\prime}
be the spanning subgraph of
H
with edge set
{E(H^{\prime}):=(M_{t}\setminus M_{t}^{\prime})\cup\bigcup_{i=t+1}^{\Delta+1}M_{i}}
. Then
e(H^{\prime})=m
, as desired.

Note that for every
v\in V(H)
, we have

\deg_{H^{\prime}}(v)\geq\deg_{H}(v)-t\geq\delta(H)-t

since
E(H\setminus H^{\prime})
is a union of
t
matchings. On the other hand, we have

\deg_{H^{\prime}}(v)\leq\Delta(H)+2-t

since
E(H^{\prime})
is itself a union of
\Delta+2-t
matchings. This verifies
(S1)
.

To prove
(S2)
, note that if
t>1
, then
\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}|M_{i}|<e(H)-m
implies that
|M_{i}|\leq\frac{e(H)-m}{t-1}
for every
i\geq t-1
. Thus

e(H)=\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}|M_{i}|+\sum_{i=t}^{\Delta+1}|M_{i}|\leq(\Delta+1)\frac{e(H)-m}{t-1}.

Rearranging gives

\Delta(H\setminus H^{\prime})\leq t\leq(\Delta+1)\frac{e(H)-m}{e(H)}+1,

(2)

as required. Note that (
2
) holds if
t=1
as well. This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
A
and
B
be nonempty finite sets of the same cardinality, and let
{\ell\geq 2}
. Let
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
be partitions of
A
and
B
, respectively, with
|\mathcal{P}|+|\mathcal{Q}|=\ell
. Then there exist refinements
{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{t}\}}
of
\mathcal{P}
and
{\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}=\{T_{1},\ldots,T_{t}\}}
of
\mathcal{Q}
such that
t\leq\ell-1
, and
|S_{i}|=|T_{i}|
for every
i=1,\ldots,t
.

Proof.

We prove by induction on
\ell
. If
\ell=2
, then we can take
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\mathcal{P}=\{A\}
and
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}=\mathcal{Q}=\{B\}
.

Now assume that
\ell>2
. Let
S
be a set in
\mathcal{P}\cup\mathcal{Q}
with minimum cardinality, say
S\in\mathcal{P}
without loss of generality. Choose any set
T\in\mathcal{Q}
, and find
T^{\prime}\subseteq T
with
|T^{\prime}|=|S|
. Apply the inductive hypothesis to

\mathcal{P}_{1}:=\mathcal{P}\setminus\{S\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{Q}_{1}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l l}(\mathcal{Q}\setminus\{T\})\cup\{T\setminus T^{\prime}\}&\text{if }T^{\prime}\neq T\\
\mathcal{Q}\setminus\{T\}&\text{if }T^{\prime}=T\end{array}\right.

to obtain refinements
\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{Q}_{1}
with parts of equal size and
|\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime}|=|\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{\prime}|\leq\ell-2
. Now
\mathcal{P}_{1}^{\prime}\cup\{S\}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{\prime}\cup\{T^{\prime}\}
are the desired refinements of
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
. The proof is complete.
∎

4.2
Obtaining a regular decomposition from almost regular decompositions

After taking a typical balanced bipartition
\{X,Y\}
of a
d
-regular graph
G
, the tools developed up to this point allow us to obtain decompositions
\{H_{X,j}\}_{j=1}^{K}
of
G[X]
and
\{H_{Y,j}\}_{j=1}^{K}
of
G[Y]
into
{K=\log_{2}d+O(1)}
almost regular bipartite spanning subgraphs, with
e(H_{X,j})=e(H_{Y,j})
for each
j\in[K]
. We can also ensure that each of these subgraphs admits a bipartition which is
\frac{d}{5}
-good with respect to
G[X,Y]
.

In the remainder of this section, we develop some additional tools to use edges from
G[X,Y]
to regularize these decompositions of
G[X]
and
G[Y]
and produce our desired decomposition of
G
. The main two lemmas of this section (
Lemma

4.5
and
Lemma

4.6
) will be used to show that if
G
is an
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph with
\lambda\leq\frac{d}{12}
, then the induced bipartite subgraph
G[X,Y]
contains edge-disjoint subgraphs
R_{1},\ldots,R_{K}
such that each
H_{X,j}\cup H_{Y,j}\cup R_{j}
is a regular bipartite spanning subgraph of
G
. Thus
\left\{H_{X,j}\cup H_{Y,j}\cup R_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{K}\cup\left\{G[X,Y]\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{K}R_{j}\right\}
will be our desired decomposition, completing the proof of
Theorem

1.3
.

For this, we make use of the following definitions.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
H=(X\cup Y,E)
be a balanced bipartite graph, and let
d,\rho,\alpha,\gamma\geq 0
. We say that
H
is
(d,\rho,\alpha,\gamma)
-robustly matchable
if for every subgraph
F\subseteq H
of maximum degree at most
\rho d
, and for every positive
\alpha^{\prime}\leq\alpha
, the bipartite graph
H\setminus F
has an
f
-factor for every integer-valued function
f:X\cup Y\to[(1-\gamma)\alpha^{\prime}d,\alpha^{\prime}d]
satisfying
f(X)=f(Y)
.

Note that in particular, any balanced bipartite graph
H
which is
(d,\rho,\alpha,\gamma)
-robustly matchable with
\alpha d\geq 1
contains a perfect matching, but clearly this property is much stronger than that. We now add yet another layer of flexibility.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
H=(X\cup Y,E)
be a balanced bipartite graph, and let
d,\rho,\alpha,\gamma\geq 0
. We say that
H
is
(d,\rho,\alpha,\gamma)
-good
if the following property holds: for any
X^{\prime}\subseteq X
and
Y^{\prime}\subseteq Y
satisfying
|X^{\prime}|=|Y^{\prime}|
and
\delta(H[X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}])\geq\frac{d}{5}
, we have that
H[X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}]
is
(d,\rho,\alpha,\gamma)
-robustly matchable.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\rho,\alpha,\gamma>0
with
\rho+\alpha\leq\frac{1}{100}
and
\gamma\leq\frac{1}{30}
.
Let
G
be an
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph with
n
even and
\lambda\leq\frac{d}{12}
. Then for any balanced bipartition
\{X,Y\}
of
[n]
, the induced bipartite subgraph
G[X,Y]
is
(d,\rho,\alpha,\gamma)
-good.

Proof.

Let
F\subseteq G[X,Y]
be a subgraph with maximum degree at most
\rho d
. Let
X^{\prime}\subseteq X
and
Y^{\prime}\subseteq Y
satisfying
|X^{\prime}|=|Y^{\prime}|
and
\delta(G[X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}])\geq\frac{d}{5}
. Let
\alpha^{\prime}\leq\alpha
, and let
f:X^{\prime}\cup Y^{\prime}\to[(1-\gamma)\alpha^{\prime}d,\alpha^{\prime}d]
be an integer-valued function satisfying
f(X^{\prime})=f(Y^{\prime})
.

Suppose that
G[X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}]\setminus F
does not have an
f
-factor. Then by Ore’s theorem (
Theorem

2.8
), there exist subsets
S\subseteq X^{\prime}
and
T\subseteq Y^{\prime}
such that

e_{G\setminus F}(S,T)<f(S)+f(T)-f(X^{\prime})=f(T)-f(X^{\prime}\setminus S)\leq\alpha^{\prime}d(|T|-(1-\gamma)|X^{\prime}\setminus S|)\leq\alpha^{\prime}d|T|.

(3)

We assume without loss of generality that
|S|\geq|T|
.

Suppose for a moment that
|T|\geq\frac{n}{10}
. Then
|S|\geq\frac{n}{10}
as well, so by the expander mixing lemma (
Lemma

2.5
), we have

e_{G}(S,T)\geq\frac{d}{n}|S||T|-\lambda\sqrt{|S||T|}\geq\left(\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{12\sqrt{|S||T|}}\right)d|S||T|\geq\left(\frac{1}{n}-\frac{5}{6n}\right)\frac{dn}{10}|T|=\frac{d}{60}|T|.

Therefore, we have

e_{G\setminus F}(S,T)=e_{G}(S,T)-e_{F}(S,T)\geq\frac{d}{60}|T|-\rho d|T|\geq\alpha d|T|

since
\Delta(F)\leq\rho d
and
\rho+\alpha\leq\frac{1}{100}
, and this contradicts (
3
). We conclude that
|T|\leq\frac{n}{10}
.

Recall that
(1-\gamma)|X^{\prime}\setminus S|<|T|
by (
3
). By the expander mixing lemma (
Lemma

2.5
), we have

\displaystyle e_{G\setminus F}(X^{\prime}\setminus S,T)

\displaystyle\leq e_{G}(X^{\prime}\setminus S,T)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{d}{n}|X^{\prime}\setminus S||T|+\lambda\sqrt{|X^{\prime}\setminus S||T|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\left(\frac{|T|}{n}+\frac{1}{12}\right)d|T|

\displaystyle<\frac{19d}{100}|T|

since
\gamma\leq\frac{1}{30}
.
On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle e_{G\setminus F}(X^{\prime},T)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{v\in T}\left(\deg_{G[X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}]}(v)-\deg_{F}(v)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{d}{5}|T|-\rho d|T|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{19d}{100}|T|+\alpha d|T|

\displaystyle>e_{G\setminus F}(X^{\prime}\setminus S,T)+e_{G\setminus F}(S,T)

\displaystyle=e_{G\setminus F}(X^{\prime},T)

since
\rho+\alpha\leq\frac{1}{100}
. This is clearly impossible, and the proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
n
be an even integer, and let
d,\rho,\alpha,\gamma>0
. Let
G
be a regular graph on
[n]
. Let
\{X,Y\}
be a balanced bipartition of
[n]
, and suppose that
G[X,Y]
is
(d,\rho,\alpha,\gamma)
-good. Further suppose that for some integer
K
with
\frac{\rho}{\alpha}\leq K\leq\frac{\gamma\rho d}{2}
, there exist bipartite decompositions
\{H_{X,j}\}_{j\in[K]}
and
\{H_{Y,j}\}_{j\in[K]}
of
G[X]
and
G[Y]
, respectively, such that the following conditions hold for each
j\in[K]
:

(A1)

for each
U\in\{X,Y\}
, we have
\Delta(H_{U,j})-\delta(H_{U,j})\leq\frac{\gamma\rho d}{4K};

(A2)

e(H_{X,j})=e(H_{Y,j})
;

(A3)

H_{X,j}
and
H_{Y,j}
have respective bipartitions
\{X_{j}^{\prime},X_{j}^{\prime\prime}\}
and
\{Y_{j}^{\prime},Y_{j}^{\prime\prime}\}
which are
\frac{d}{5}
-good with respect to
G[X,Y]
.

Then there exists a decomposition of
G
into
K+1
regular bipartite spanning subgraphs.

Proof.

For each
j=1,\ldots,K
, in turn, we obtain a pair of spanning subgraphs
{R^{\prime}_{j}\subseteq G[X^{\prime}_{j},Y^{\prime}_{j}]}
and
{R^{\prime\prime}_{j}\subseteq G[X^{\prime\prime}_{j},Y^{\prime\prime}_{j}]}
such that
H_{X,j}\cup H_{Y,j}\cup R^{\prime}_{j}\cup R^{\prime\prime}_{j}
is a regular bipartite spanning subgraph of
G
, as follows. All the while, we maintain that the graphs
R^{\prime}_{j}\cup R^{\prime\prime}_{j}
are edge-disjoint, and that
\Delta(R^{\prime}_{j}\cup R^{\prime\prime}_{j})\leq\frac{\rho d}{K}
.

By relabeling if necessary, we may assume by
(G1)
that
|X^{\prime}_{j}|=|Y^{\prime}_{j}|=\left\lceil\frac{n}{4}\right\rceil
and
|X^{\prime\prime}_{j}|=|Y^{\prime\prime}_{j}|=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\right\rfloor
. Set
C:=\left\lceil(1-\gamma)\frac{\rho d}{K}\right\rceil
. Let
f_{j}^{\prime}:X^{\prime}_{j}\cup Y^{\prime}_{j}\to\mathbb{N}
be given by

f^{\prime}_{j}(v)=C+\Delta(H_{X,j}\cup H_{Y,j})-\deg_{H_{X,j}\cup H_{Y,j}}(v)

for every
v\in X^{\prime}_{j}\cup Y^{\prime}_{j}
, and let
f_{j}^{\prime\prime}:X^{\prime\prime}_{j}\cup Y^{\prime\prime}_{j}\to\mathbb{N}
be given by

f^{\prime\prime}_{j}(v)=C+\Delta(H_{X,j}\cup H_{Y,j})-\deg_{H_{X,j}\cup H_{Y,j}}(v)

for every
v\in X^{\prime\prime}_{j}\cup Y^{\prime\prime}_{j}
. We wish to find an
f^{\prime}_{j}
-factor
R^{\prime}_{j}
of
(G\setminus F_{j})[X^{\prime}_{j},Y^{\prime}_{j}]
and an
f^{\prime\prime}_{j}
-factor
R^{\prime\prime}_{j}
of
(G\setminus F_{j})[X^{\prime\prime}_{j},Y^{\prime\prime}_{j}]
, where
F_{j}:=\bigcup_{i<j}(R^{\prime}_{i}\cup R^{\prime\prime}_{i})
.

It follows from
(A2)
and the fact that

V(H_{X,j})=|X|=|Y|=V(H_{Y,j})

that
H_{X,j}
and
H_{Y,j}
have the same average degree, so we have

\displaystyle\Delta(H_{X,j}\cup H_{Y,j})-\delta(H_{X,j}\cup H_{Y,j})

\displaystyle\leq\left(\Delta(H_{X,j})-\delta(H_{X,j})\right)+\left(\Delta(H_{Y,j})-\delta(H_{Y,j})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma\rho d}{2K}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\rho d}{K}-C

by
(A1)
and the assumption that
K\leq\frac{\gamma\rho d}{2}
. This means that
f_{j}^{\prime}
and
f^{\prime\prime}_{j}
both take values in
\left[(1-\gamma)\frac{\rho d}{K},\frac{\rho d}{K}\right]
. Furthermore, we have by
(A2)
that

\displaystyle f^{\prime}_{j}(X^{\prime}_{j})

\displaystyle=\left(C+\Delta(H_{X,j}\cup H_{Y,j})\right)|X^{\prime}_{j}|-e(H_{X,j})

\displaystyle=\left(C+\Delta(H_{X,j}\cup H_{Y,j})\right)|Y^{\prime}_{j}|-e(H_{Y,j})

\displaystyle=f^{\prime}_{j}(Y^{\prime}_{j}),

and a similar calculation shows
f^{\prime\prime}_{j}(X^{\prime\prime}_{j})=f^{\prime\prime}_{j}(Y^{\prime\prime}_{j})
. Also recall that
\frac{\rho}{K}\leq\alpha
by assumption, and that
\Delta\left(F_{j}\right)\leq\rho d
by the inductive hypothesis. Therefore, by
(A3)
and the fact that
G[X,Y]
is
(d,\rho,\alpha,\gamma)
-good, we see that
\left(G\setminus F_{j}\right)[X^{\prime}_{j},Y^{\prime}_{j}]
has an
f^{\prime}_{j}
-factor
R^{\prime}_{j}
, and
\left(G\setminus F_{j}\right)[X^{\prime\prime}_{j},Y^{\prime\prime}_{j}]
has an
f^{\prime\prime}_{j}
-factor
R^{\prime\prime}_{j}
. Now by design, we have that
R^{\prime}_{j}\cup R^{\prime\prime}_{j}
is edge-disjoint from
{F_{j}=\bigcup_{i<j}(R^{\prime}_{i}\cup R^{\prime\prime}_{i})}
, and that
\Delta(R^{\prime}_{j}\cup R^{\prime\prime}_{j})\leq\frac{\rho d}{K}
. We also have that
H_{X,j}\cup H_{Y,j}\cup R^{\prime}_{j}\cup R^{\prime\prime}_{j}
is a bipartite
\left(C+\Delta(H_{X,j}\cup H_{Y,j})\right)
-regular spanning subgraph of
G
, with bipartition
\{X^{\prime}_{j}\cup Y^{\prime\prime}_{j},X^{\prime\prime}_{j}\cup Y^{\prime}_{j}\}
. This completes the inductive step.

At last, we define
H:=G\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{K}(H_{X,j}\cup H_{Y,j}\cup R^{\prime}_{j}\cup R^{\prime\prime}_{j})
. Since
G
is regular, and the edge-disjoint regular graphs
H_{X,j}\cup H_{Y,j}\cup R^{\prime}_{j}\cup R^{\prime\prime}_{j}
cover all of the edges in
G[X]\cup G[Y]
, we see that
H
is a regular spanning subgraph of
G[X,Y]
. Thus
\{H_{X,j}\cup H_{Y,j}\cup R^{\prime}_{j}\cup R^{\prime\prime}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{K}\cup\{H\}
is a decomposition of
G
into
K+1
regular bipartite spanning subgraphs, as desired.
∎

5
Proof of our main result

We are now ready to prove our main theorem.

Proof of
Theorem

1.3
.

Let
d
be a sufficiently large positive integer, and let
n\geq d+1
be an even integer. Let
G
be an
(n,d,\lambda)
-graph on
[n]
with
\lambda\leq\frac{d}{12}
. First, we take a balanced bipartition
\{X,Y\}
of
[n]
as in
Lemma

3.1
. Note that
e(G[X])=e(G[Y])
. Indeed, since
G
is regular and
|X|=|Y|=\frac{n}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle 2e(G[X])+e(G[X,Y])

\displaystyle=d|X|=d|Y|=2e(G[Y])+e(G[X,Y]).

Let
M:=\lfloor\log_{2}d\rfloor-18
. For
1\leq j\leq M
, we iteratively obtain a sequence of pairs
(H_{X,j},H_{Y,j})
of edge-disjoint bipartite spanning subgraphs
H_{X,j}\subseteq G[X]
and
H_{Y,j}\subseteq G[Y]
. For each
U\in\{X,Y\}
, we set
G_{U,0}:=G[U]
, and for
1\leq j\leq M
, we set
G_{U,j}:=G_{U,j-1}\setminus H_{U,j}
. For
0\leq j\leq M
, we also define
d_{j}:=\frac{d}{2^{j+1}}
and
\varepsilon_{j}:=40d_{j}^{-1/3}
.

Claim 1
.

We can ensure the following properties for
0\leq j\leq M
:

(I1)

if
j\geq 1
, then for each
U\in\{X,Y\}
, we have
\Delta(H_{U,j})-\delta(H_{U,j})\leq 70d_{j}^{2/3}
;

(I2)

if
j\geq 1
, then
e(H_{X,j})=e(H_{Y,j})
;

(I3)

if
j\geq 1
, then for each
U\in\{X,Y\}
, the bipartite graph
H_{U,j}
has a bipartition which is
\frac{d}{5}
-good with respect to
G[X,Y]
;

(I4)

for each
U\in\{X,Y\}
, we have
\deg_{G_{U,j}}(v)=(1\pm\varepsilon_{j})d_{j}
for every
v\in U
.

Proof of
Claim

1
.

We observe that
(I1)
–
(I4)
are satisfied for
j=0
already. Indeed,
(I1)
–
(I3)
hold trivially, and by
Lemma

3.1
, for each
U\in\{X,Y\}
, we have

\deg_{G_{U,0}}(v)=\frac{d}{2}\pm d^{2/3}=(1\pm\varepsilon_{0})d_{0}

for every
v\in U
.
Now suppose that, for some
j\geq 0
, we have the graphs
H_{X,1},\ldots,H_{X,j}
and
H_{Y,1},\ldots,H_{Y,j}
as claimed. If
j<M
, then we now wish to obtain
H_{X,j+1}
and
H_{Y,j+1}
.

We first obtain, for each
U\in\{X,Y\}
, an induced bipartite spanning subgraph
H_{U,j+1}^{\prime}
of
G_{U,j}
as in
Lemma

3.3
, with
\varepsilon_{j}
,
d_{j}
,
G[X,Y]
,
U
,
G_{U,j}
,
H^{\prime}_{U,j+1}
in place of
\varepsilon
,
d^{\prime}
,
H
,
X
,
G_{X}
,
H_{X}
, respectively.
We note that
d_{j}\geq\frac{d}{2^{M}}\geq 2^{18}
, so then
\varepsilon_{j}\leq 40\cdot 2^{-18/3}=\frac{5}{8}
, and our application of
Lemma

3.3
is justified. Specifically, by
(L1)
for
H^{\prime}_{U,j+1}
and
(I4)
for
G_{U,j}
, we have that

\deg_{H_{U,j+1}^{\prime}}(v)=\left(1\pm 2d_{j}^{-1/3}\right)\frac{\deg_{G_{U,j}}(v)}{2}=\left(1\pm 2d_{j}^{-{1/3}}\right)\frac{(1\pm\varepsilon_{j})d_{j}}{2}

(4)

for every
v\in U
.

Next, let
m:=\min\{e(H^{\prime}_{X,j+1}),e(H^{\prime}_{Y,j+1})\}
. For each
U\in\{X,Y\}
, let
H_{U,j+1}
be an
m
-edge spanning subgraph of
H^{\prime}_{U,j+1}
as in
Lemma

4.1
, with
H^{\prime}_{U,j+1}
,
H_{U,j+1}
in place of
H
,
H^{\prime}
, respectively. By
(S1)
for
H_{U,j+1}
and (
4
), we have

\displaystyle\Delta(H_{U,j+1})-\delta(H_{U,j+1})

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+2d_{j}^{-1/3}\right)\frac{(1+\varepsilon_{j})d_{j}}{2}-\left(1-2d_{j}^{-1/3}\right)\frac{(1-\varepsilon_{j})d_{j}}{2}+2

\displaystyle=42d_{j}^{2/3}+2

\displaystyle<70d_{j+1}^{2/3}.

Thus each
H_{U,j+1}
satisfies
(I1)
. Now
e(H_{X,j+1})=m=e(H_{Y,j+1})
, so
(I2)
is satisfied as well. Also, since each
H_{U,j+1}
is a spanning subgraph
of
H^{\prime}_{U,j+1}
, we see that
(I3)
follows from
(L2)
for
H^{\prime}_{U,j+1}
.

It remains to verify that each
G_{U,j+1}=G_{U,j}\setminus H_{U,j+1}
satisfies
(I4)
. We first observe that

\deg_{G_{U,j}\setminus H^{\prime}_{U,j+1}}(v)\leq\deg_{G_{U,j+1}}(v)\leq\deg_{G_{U,j}\setminus H^{\prime}_{U,j+1}}(v)+\Delta(H^{\prime}_{U,j+1}\setminus H_{U,j+1})

(5)

for each

and every

. Next, note that that

e(H^{\prime}_{U,j+1})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in U}\deg_{H^{\prime}_{U,j+1}}(v)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in U}\left(1\pm 2d_{j}^{-1/3}\right)\frac{\deg_{G_{U,j}}(v)}{2}=\left(1\pm 2d_{j}^{-1/3}\right)\frac{e(G_{U,j})}{2}

for each

by (

). Also, recall that

, so by
(I2)
and induction, we have that

. It follows that for each

, we have by our choice of

Now by
(S2)
and (

), we have

\displaystyle\Delta(H^{\prime}_{U,j+1}\setminus H_{U,j+1})

\displaystyle\leq\left(\Delta(H^{\prime}_{U,j+1})+1\right)\frac{e(H^{\prime}_{U,j+1})-m}{e(H^{\prime}_{U,j+1})}+1

\displaystyle\leq\left(\Delta(H^{\prime}_{U,j+1})+1\right)\frac{4d_{j}^{-1/3}}{1+2d_{j}^{-1/3}}+1

\displaystyle\leq 2(1+\varepsilon_{j})d_{j}^{2/3}+3.

Now using the upper and lower bounds (
5
) on
\deg_{G_{U,j+1}}(v)
observed above, we have by (

) and
(I4)
for

that

\displaystyle\deg_{G_{U,j+1}}(v)

\displaystyle\leq(1+2d_{j}^{-1/3})\frac{(1+\varepsilon_{j})d_{j}}{2}+2(1+\varepsilon_{j})d_{j}^{2/3}+3

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{j}}{2}+23d_{j}^{2/3}+120d_{j}^{1/3}+3

\displaystyle<\frac{d_{j}}{2}+25d_{j}^{2/3}

\displaystyle<d_{j+1}+40d_{j+1}^{2/3}

\displaystyle=(1+\varepsilon_{j+1})d_{j+1}

since
d_{j}\geq 2^{18}
, and also

\deg_{G_{U,j+1}}(v)\geq\left(1-2d_{j}^{-1/3}\right)\frac{(1-\varepsilon_{j})d_{j}}{2}\geq\frac{d_{j}}{2}-21d_{j}^{2/3}\geq(1-\varepsilon_{j+1})d_{j+1}.

Thus
G_{U,j+1}
satisfies
(I4)
, and the proof of
Claim

1
is complete.
∎

We are now left with graphs
G_{X,M}
and
G_{Y,M}
of maximum degree at most
(1+\varepsilon_{M})\frac{d}{2^{M+1}}\leq 2^{19}
. By
Lemma

3.4
, there exist spanning bipartite decompositions
\{H^{\prime}_{X,M+j}\}_{j=1}^{27}
and
\{H^{\prime}_{Y,M+j}\}_{j=1}^{27}
of
G_{X,M}
and
G_{Y,M}
, respectively, such that each

and each

has a bipartition which is
\frac{d}{5}
-good with respect to

. By

and
\{E(H_{Y,M+j})\}_{j=1}^{27}
, there exist

and respective spanning bipartite decompositions

and
\{H_{X,M+j}\}_{j=1}^{t}
of

and

such that for each

,

is a spanning subgraph of

for some

, and

.

Finally, we claim that
\{H_{X,j}\}_{j=1}^{M+t}
and

satisfy the hypotheses of

4.6
with

{\rho:=\frac{1}{200}},\quad\alpha:=\frac{1}{200},\quad\gamma:=\frac{1}{30},\quad\text{and}\quad K:=M+t

if

is

-good by

. We also have
\frac{\rho}{\alpha}\leq K\leq\frac{\gamma\rho d}{2}
. For

and

, we have
\Delta(H_{U,j})-\delta(H_{U,j})\leq 70d^{2/3}\leq\frac{\gamma\rho d}{2K}
by

. This, together with

–

, verifies

–

. For

, so

is satisfied. We have already observed that

follows from the fact that each

. This concludes the proof of

.
∎

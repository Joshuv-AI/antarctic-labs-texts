Parity and symmetry of polarized endomorphisms on cohomology
Fei Hu

Abstract
. We show that the eigenvalues of any polarized endomorphism acting on the ℓ \ell -adic étale cohomology of a smooth projective variety satisfy certain parity and symmetry properties, as predicted by the standard conjectures. These properties were previously known for Frobenius endomorphisms. Besides the hard Lefschetz theorem, a key new ingredient is a recent Weil’s Riemann hypothesis-type result due to J. Xie. We also prove a "Newton over Hodge" type property for abelian varieties and Grassmannians.

14A10, 14G17, 14F20, 14F40, 15A63.

1.
Introduction

We work over an algebraically closed field
\mathbbm{k}
of characteristic
p\geq 0
.
Let
\ell
be a prime number distinct from
p
.
Let
q\in\mathbf{Z}_{>1}
be an integer greater than
1
, which is not necessarily a power of
p
and may have distinct prime factors.
An algebraic integer
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbf{Z}}
is called a
q
-Weil integer of weight
w
, if for every field embedding
\iota\colon\mathbf{Q}(\lambda)\to\mathbf{C}
, we have

|\iota(\lambda)|^{2}=\iota(\lambda)\cdot\overline{\iota(\lambda)}=q^{w}.

Motivated, presumably, by the Kähler analog of Weil’s Riemann hypothesis established by Serre
[
Ser60
]
, Tate proposed the following lesser-known conjecture in his 1963 Purdue talk (see also his 1964 Woods Hole talk
[
Tat64
]
), which has been somewhat obscured by the prominence of the Tate conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1

(cf.
[
Tat65
, §3, Conjecture (d)]
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
d
over
\mathbbm{k}
, and let
f
be a (
q
-)polarized endomorphism of
X
, i.e., there is an ample divisor
H_{X}
on
X
such that
f^{*}H_{X}\sim qH_{X}
for some integer
q>1
.
Then, for each
0\leq i\leq 2d
, the following assertions hold:

(1)

All eigenvalues of
f^{*}|_{H^{i}_{\emph{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})}
are
q
-Weil integers of weight
i
.

(2)

The linear transformation
f^{*}|_{H^{i}_{\emph{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})}
is semisimple.

Remark 1.2
.

(1)

As Tate observed, in characteristic zero,
1.1
follows from Hodge theory by Serre’s pioneering work
[
Ser60
]
.
When
\mathbbm{k}=\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{q}
and
f
is the geometric Frobenius endomorphism
\mathsf{Frob}_{q}
of
X
, part
(1)
of
1.1
corresponds to Weil’s Riemann hypothesis, which was proven by Deligne
[
Del74
]
.
However, its part
(2)
, known as the semisimplicity conjecture, remains open in general.
It is well known that
1.1
follows from the standard conjectures (see, e.g.,
[
Kle68
, §4]
).

(2)

Building on Deligne’s groundbreaking work
[
Del74
]
, Katz and Messing
[
KM74
]
proved that the standard conjecture
C
holds for all smooth projective varieties
Y
over finite fields.
As a consequence, the characteristic polynomial of any endomorphism
g
of
Y
acting on any Weil cohomology group
H^{*}(Y)
has integer coefficients and is independent of the choice of Weil cohomology.
Then, by applying a standard spreading-out and specialization argument, it follows that all eigenvalues appearing in
1.1
(1)
are algebraic integers.

In a recent preprint
[
Xie24
]
, Xie has made substantial progress on
1.1
, fully proving its longstanding open part
(1)
, which had remained unresolved for 60 years.
His proof relies on Deligne’s resolution of Weil’s Riemann hypothesis.
In particular, his result below simultaneously extends
[
Del74
, Theorem 1.6]
on the eigenvalues of the Frobenius endomorphisms to arbitrary polarized endomorphisms and
[
Ser60
, Théorème 1]
to arbitrary characteristic, as anticipated by the standard conjectures.

Theorem 1.3

(cf.
[
Xie24
, Corollary 1.5]
)
.

1.1
(1)
is true.

From a motivic perspective, polarized endomorphisms exhibit behavior analogous to that of Frobenius endomorphisms.
For instance, the standard conjectures imply both Weil’s Riemann hypothesis and the semisimplicity of not only Frobenius endomorphisms but also all polarized endomorphisms (see
[
Kle68
, §4]
).
In this short note, we further supplement that certain parity and symmetry properties of Frobenius endomorphisms also extend to polarized endomorphisms.
For details on these properties, see
[
Suh12
,
SZ16
]
.

Before stating the result, we introduce some notation.
Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
d
over
\mathbbm{k}
.
Assume that
f
is an arbitrary endomorphism of
X
.
For any
0\leq i\leq 2d
, denote the algebraic multiplicity of an eigenvalue
\lambda
of
f^{*}|_{H^{i}_{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})}
by
\mu_{\lambda}\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
For each
e\geq 1
, let
\mu_{\lambda,e}\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 0}
denote the number of
e\times e
Jordan blocks associated with the eigenvalue
\lambda
in the Jordan canonical form of
f^{*}|_{H^{i}_{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})}
.
Denote the characteristic polynomial of
f^{*}|_{H^{i}_{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})}
by

P_{i}(t)\coloneqq\det(t\operatorname{id}-f^{*}|_{H^{i}_{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})})=\prod_{\lambda}\,(t-\lambda)^{\mu_{\lambda}}\in\mathbf{Z}[t].

Note that
\mu_{\lambda}=\sum_{e\geq 1}e\mu_{\lambda,e}
, and the geometric multiplicity of
\lambda
equals
\sum_{e\geq 1}\mu_{\lambda,e}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
d
over
\mathbbm{k}
, and
f
a
q
-polarized endomorphism of
X
, i.e., there is an ample divisor
H_{X}
on
X
such that
f^{*}H_{X}\sim qH_{X}
for some
q\in\mathbf{Z}_{>1}
.
Let
\ell
be a prime distinct from
p
.
Then the following assertions hold:

(1)

For any
0\leq i\leq 2d
, any eigenvalue
\lambda
of
f^{*}|_{H_{\emph{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}}^{i}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})}
, and any
e\geq 1
, we have
\mu_{\lambda,e}=\mu_{q^{i}/\lambda,e}
;
in particular,
\mu_{\lambda}=\mu_{q^{i}/\lambda}
.

(2)

For any
0\leq i\leq 2d
, the characteristic polynomial
P_{i}(t)
satisfies the functional equation

t^{b_{i}}P_{i}(q^{i}/t)=(-1)^{\epsilon_{i}}q^{\frac{ib_{i}}{2}}P_{i}(t),

where
b_{i}\coloneqq b_{i}(X)\coloneqq\dim_{\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}}\!H^{i}_{\emph{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})
is the
i
th Betti number of
X
and
\epsilon_{i}\in\{0,1\}
.

(3)

For any odd
0<i<2d
, the real roots
\pm q^{i/2}
of
P_{i}(t)
have even multiplicity, i.e.,
\mu_{\pm q^{i/2}}
are even (possibly zero).
In particular, for odd
i
, the
\epsilon_{i}
in the functional equation is zero.

Remark 1.5
.

(1)

The above assertion
(3)
provides a partial answer to a question posed by the author in 2019, which, as Deligne kindly pointed out, also follows from the standard conjectures (see
[
Hu24
, Remark 1.3]
).
When
\mathbbm{k}=\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{q}
and
f
is the geometric Frobenius endomorphism
\mathsf{Frob}_{q}
of
X
,
theorem

1.4
is a well-known consequence of Deligne’s Weil II
[
Del80
]
.
Moreover, these parity and symmetry properties hold even for the Frobenius endomorphism of
proper
varieties.
For further details, see
[
Suh12
,
SZ16
]
.

(2)

It is worth mentioning that by Poincaré duality one can easily derive another functional equation for characteristic polynomials
P_{i}
and
P_{2d-i}
as follows:

t^{b_{i}}P_{i}(q^{d}/t)=(-1)^{\epsilon_{i}}q^{\frac{ib_{i}}{2}}P_{2d-i}(t),

where the sign is exactly the same as in the assertion
(2)
.

As a direct consequence of
theorem

1.4
, we describe the nonarchimedean valuations of the eigenvalues of polarized endomorphisms acting on cohomology, or equivalently, the Newton polygons of the corresponding characteristic polynomials.

Recall that for a polynomial

P(t)=a_{0}t^{n}+a_{1}t^{n-1}+\dots+a_{n}\in K[t]

with coefficients
a_{i}
in a nonarchimedean field
(K,\nu)
, the
Newton polygon
\mathsf{NP}_{\nu}(P)
of
P(t)
is defined as the lower boundary of the convex hull of the set of points
(i,\nu(a_{i}))
with
a_{i}\neq 0
in
\mathbf{R}^{2}
.
This polygon fully encodes the
\nu
-valuations of the roots of
P(t)
.
Indeed, if the Newton polygon
\mathsf{NP}_{\nu}(P)
has a segment of slope
\lambda
and (horizontal) length
l
, then
P(t)
has precisely
l
roots (counted with multiplicity) with
\nu
-valuation
\lambda
.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
d
over
\mathbbm{k}
, and
f
a
q
-polarized endomorphism of
X
with
\operatorname{char}\mathbbm{k}=p\geq 0
and
q\in\mathbf{Z}_{>1}
.
Let
\ell
be a prime distinct from
p
.
Denote by
\nu_{p}
(resp.
\nu_{\ell}
) a
p
-adic (resp.
\ell
-adic) valuation on
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}
.
For any
0\leq i\leq 2d
, denote the characteristic polynomial of
f^{*}|_{H^{i}_{\emph{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})}
by
P_{i}(t)\in\mathbf{Z}[t]
.
Then the following assertions hold:

(1)

If
\ell\nmid q
, then the Newton polygon
\mathsf{NP}_{\nu_{\ell}}(P_{i})
has a single segment of slope
0
and length
b_{i}
, i.e., all eigenvalues of
f^{*}|_{H_{\emph{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}}^{i}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})}
are
\ell
-adic units.

(2)

If
p>0
and
p\nmid q
, then
\mathsf{NP}_{\nu_{p}}(P_{i})
has a single segment of slope
0
and length
b_{i}
.

(3)

If
\ell\!\mid\!q
and
\nu_{\ell}
is normalized so that
\nu_{\ell}(q)=1
, then
\mathsf{NP}_{\nu_{\ell}}(P_{i})
is symmetric, i.e., its slopes are rational numbers in
[0,i]
, and each slope
\alpha
appears with the same length as
i-\alpha
.

(4)

If
p>0
,
p\!\mid\!q
, and
\nu_{p}
is normalized so that
\nu_{p}(q)=1
, then
\mathsf{NP}_{\nu_{p}}(P_{i})
is symmetric.

A sophisticated reader may wonder what lies beyond the symmetry in
corollary

1.6
(3)
and
(4)
.
Indeed, Katz conjectured, first proven by Mazur
[
Maz72
,
Maz73
]
and later extended by Ogus
[
BO78
, §8]
, that the Newton polygon of the characteristic polynomial of the geometric Frobenius
\mathsf{Frob}_{q}
on
H^{i}_{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})
lies on or above the Hodge polygon
\mathsf{HP}_{i}(X)
of
X
of weight
i
.

Recall that for a smooth projective variety
X
of dimension
d
over
\mathbbm{k}
, and an integer
0\leq i\leq 2d
, the
Hodge polygon

\mathsf{HP}_{i}(X)
of
X
of weight

is a piecewise linear (convex) function passing through the origin
(0,0)
and the points

\Bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{k}h^{j,i-j},\sum_{j=0}^{k}jh^{j,i-j}\Bigg),\quad k=0,1,\dots,i,

where
h^{j,i-j}
are the Hodge numbers, defined as

h^{j,i-j}\coloneqq h^{j,i-j}(X)\coloneqq\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}H^{i-j}(X,\Omega_{X/\mathbbm{k}}^{j}).

Equivalently,
\mathsf{HP}_{i}(X)
has slope zero over the interval
(0,h^{0,i})
and slope
k=1,2,\dots,i
over the interval

(h^{0,i}+\dots+h^{k-1,i-k+1},h^{0,i}+\dots+h^{k,i-k}).

As an analog of Katz’s conjecture or the Mazur–Ogus theorem (see
[
BO78
, Theorem 8.39]
), it is natural to ask the following question.

Question 1.7
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
d
over
\mathbbm{k}
, and
f
a
q
-polarized endomorphism of
X
with
\operatorname{char}\mathbbm{k}=p\geq 0
and
q\in\mathbf{Z}_{>1}
.
Let
\ell
be a prime distinct from
p
.
Assume that
\ell\!\mid\!q
(resp.
p>0
,

), and let
\nu_{\ell}
(resp.
\nu_{p}
) denote the normalized
\ell
-adic (resp.

-adic) valuation on
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}
so that
\nu_{\ell}(q)=1
(resp.
\nu_{p}(q)=1

Then, for any

, is it true that

\mathsf{NP}_{\nu_{\ell}}(P_{i})\geq\mathsf{HP}_{i}(X)\ \ \text{(resp. }\mathsf{NP}_{\nu_{p}}(P_{i})\geq\mathsf{HP}_{i}(X)\text{)}?

Remark 1.8
.

(1)

If

and

is the geometric Frobenius endomorphism

of

(in particular,

is precisely the Mazur–Ogus theorem, whose proof relies on crystalline cohomology.
Hence, for a general polarized endomorphism

is independent of the chosen Weil cohomology theory, it may still be useful to study the induced action on crystalline cohomology.

(2)

The above

remains compelling even in characteristic zero, as it imposes conjectural numerical constraints on polarized endomorphisms, whose classification in characteristic zero is still largely open (see, e.g.,
[
MZ18
]
).

(3)

By the Lefschetz trace formula, an affirmative answer to

would also yield an analogous divisibility statement for the number of periodic points of

, counted with multiplicity (cf.
[
Maz72
, Divisibility Corollary]
).

Proposition 1.9
.

1.7
admits an affirmative answer in the cases of abelian varieties and Grassmannians.

Remark 1.10
.

For any
q
-polarized endomorphism

of an irregular smooth projective variety

over
\mathbbm{k}
, using the fact that the Albanese morphism
\operatorname{alb}_{X}\colon X\to\operatorname{Alb}(X)
is

(see also

).

2.
Proof of main results

2.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.4
and Corollary
1.6

Keep in mind the assumption and the notation of
theorem

1.4
.
For any
0\leq i\leq d
, consider the following bilinear form

(2.1)

\begin{array}[]{ccc}B\colon H^{i}_{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})\times H^{i}_{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})&\to&H^{2d}_{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})\simeq\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}\\
\qquad(\alpha,y)&\mapsto&\alpha\cup y\cup\operatorname{cl}_{X}(H_{X})^{d-i},\end{array}

where
\operatorname{cl}_{X}\colon\mathsf{CH}^{i}(X)\to H^{2i}_{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})
is the cycle class map.
By Poincaré duality and the hard Lefschetz theorem
[
Del80
, Théorème 4.1.1]
,
B
is nondegenerate;
moreover,
B
is alternating (resp. symmetric), if
i
is odd (resp. even).

Lemma 2.1
.

For each
0\leq i\leq d
, let
\varphi_{i}
denote the following normalized linear transformation

q^{-i/2}f^{*}|_{H^{i}_{\emph{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})}

on
H^{i}_{\emph{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})
.
Then the bilinear form
B
defined in (
2.1
) is preserved by
\varphi_{i}
, i.e.,
for any
\alpha,\beta\in H^{i}_{\emph{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})
, we have

B(\varphi_{i}(\alpha),\varphi_{i}(\beta))=B(\alpha,\beta).

In particular,
\varphi_{i}
is symplectic (resp. orthogonal), if
i
is odd (resp. even).

Proof.

Since
f^{*}H_{X}\sim qH_{X}
, it is well known that such an
f
is a finite morphism of degree
\deg(f)=q^{d}
.
By the projection formula, we have for any
\alpha,\beta\in H^{i}_{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})
,

\displaystyle B(f^{*}\alpha,f^{*}\beta)

\displaystyle=f^{*}\alpha\cup f^{*}\beta\cup\operatorname{cl}_{X}(H_{X})^{d-i}

\displaystyle=f^{*}\alpha\cup f^{*}\beta\cup\frac{1}{q^{d-i}}\operatorname{cl}_{X}(f^{*}H_{X})^{d-i}

\displaystyle=\frac{\deg(f)}{q^{d-i}}\,\alpha\cup\beta\cup\operatorname{cl}_{X}(H_{X})^{d-i}

\displaystyle=q^{i}B(\alpha,\beta).

Thus, it follows that
B(\varphi_{i}(\alpha),\varphi_{i}(\beta))=q^{-i}B(f^{*}\alpha,f^{*}\beta)=B(\alpha,\beta)
.
∎

Proof of
theorem

1.4
.

According to a fundamental result of Katz and Messing
[
KM74
]
, we now know that the characteristic polynomial
P_{i}(t)
of
f^{*}|_{H^{i}_{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})}
has integer coefficients, whose roots are algebraic integers.
Note, by Poincaré duality, that the transpose of
f^{*}|_{H^{2d-i}_{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})}
is equal to
q^{d}(f^{*}|_{H^{i}_{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})})^{-1}
.
Thus, it follows that

P_{2d-i}(t)=\det(t\operatorname{id}-f^{*}|_{H^{2d-i}_{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})})=\frac{(-t)^{b_{i}}}{\det(f^{*}|_{H^{i}_{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})})}P_{i}(q^{d}/t).

In particular, the eigenvalues of
f^{*}|_{H^{2d-i}_{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})}
are precisely given by
q^{d}/\lambda
, where the
\lambda
are the eigenvalues of
f^{*}|_{H^{i}_{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})}
.
Replacing
t
by
q^{2d-i}/t
, one obtains that

t^{b_{2d-i}}P_{2d-i}(q^{2d-i}/t)=\frac{(-1)^{b_{i}}q^{(2d-i)b_{i}}}{\det(f^{*}|_{H^{i}_{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})})}P_{i}(t/q^{d-i}).

Therefore, it suffices to prove
theorem

1.4
for
0\leq i\leq d
.

Fix any
0\leq i\leq d
and consider the nondegenerate bilinear form
B
defined in (
2.1
).
Thanks to
lemma

2.1
, the normalized linear transformation
\varphi_{i}
of
H^{i}_{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})
preserves
B
.
It follows that
\varphi_{i}\in\mathrm{Sp}(H^{i}_{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}),B)
(resp.
\mathrm{O}(H^{i}_{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}),B)
), the symplectic (resp. orthogonal) group of
(H^{i}_{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}),B)
, if
i
is odd (resp. even).
In particular, the determinant of
\varphi_{i}
is
\pm 1
, and the inverse
\varphi_{i}^{-1}
of
\varphi_{i}
is similar to the transpose
\varphi_{i}^{\mathsf{T}}
of
\varphi_{i}
.
Equivalently, the transpose of
f^{*}|_{H^{i}_{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})}
is similar to
q^{i}\,(f^{*}|_{H^{i}_{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})})^{-1}
.
The assertion
(1)
thus follows.

Let
P_{\varphi_{i}}(t)\in\mathbf{Q}(\sqrt{q})[t]
denote the characteristic polynomial of the linear map
\varphi_{i}
.
Then

\displaystyle P_{\varphi_{i}}(t)

\displaystyle=P_{\varphi_{i}^{-1}}(t)=\det(t\operatorname{id}-\varphi_{i}^{-1})=\det(\varphi_{i})^{-1}\det(t\,\varphi_{i}-\operatorname{id})

\displaystyle=\det(\varphi_{i})(-t)^{b_{i}}\det(t^{-1}\operatorname{id}-\varphi_{i})=\det(\varphi_{i})(-t)^{b_{i}}P_{\varphi_{i}}(t^{-1}).

On the other hand, by the definition of
\varphi_{i}
, one has

P_{\varphi_{i}}(t)=q^{-\frac{ib_{i}}{2}}P_{i}(q^{i/2}\,t)
.
The assertion
(2)
thus follows.

Suppose now that
0<i\leq d
is odd and hence
\varphi_{i}\in\mathrm{Sp}(H^{i}_{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}),B)
.
Then the determinant of
\varphi_{i}
is one and the
i
-th Betti number
b_{i}
is even.
In particular, the characteristic polynomial
P_{\varphi_{i}}(t)
of
\varphi_{i}
is self-reciprocal, i.e.,
P_{\varphi_{i}}(t)=t^{b_{i}}P_{\varphi_{i}}(t^{-1})
.
Recall that the characteristic polynomial of
f^{*}|_{H^{i}_{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})}
has integer coefficients, whose roots are algebraic integers denoted by
\lambda_{i,j}
with
1\leq j\leq b_{i}
(see
remark

1.2
).
Xie’s recent brilliant result shows that

\lambda_{i,j}=q^{i/2}\,e^{\sqrt{-1}\,\theta_{i,j}}

with
\theta_{i,j}\in[0,2\pi)
for all
j
(see
[
Xie24
, Corollary 1.5]
or
theorem

1.3
which extends
[
Ser60
, Théorème 1]
to arbitrary characteristic).
Hence the eigenvalues of the normalized
\varphi_{i}
are exactly these
e^{\sqrt{-1}\,\theta_{i,j}}
.
Note that the sum of the multiplicities of the two real eigenvalues
\pm 1
is even, because
P_{\varphi_{i}}(t)\in\mathbf{Q}(\sqrt{q})[t]
and
\deg P_{\varphi_{i}}(t)=b_{i}
is even.
The multiplicity of the eigenvalue
-1
is also even, since the determinant of
\varphi_{i}
is one.
It follows that the two real roots
\pm 1
of
P_{\varphi_{i}}(t)
have even multiplicity (possibly zero), so are the real eigenvalues
\pm q^{i/2}
of
f^{*}|_{H^{i}_{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})}
.
This completes the proof of
theorem

1.4
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

It follows from the above proof that

, where

where

is the Euler characteristic of

and as before
\mu_{-q^{d/2}}
denotes the multiplicity of

as an eigenvalue of

.

Proof of
corollary

1.6
.

Recall again by Katz–Messing
[
KM74
]
, the eigenvalues of
f^{*}|_{H^{i}_{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})}
are algebraic integers.
When
\ell\nmid q
, the
\ell
-adic valuations of the eigenvalues
\lambda
of
f^{*}|_{H^{i}_{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})}
are nonnegative.
On the other hand, Poincaré duality implies that
q^{d}/\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
f^{*}|_{H^{2d-i}_{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})}
, and hence also has nonnegative
\ell
-adic valuations.
It follows that
\lambda
is an
\ell
-adic unit in a suitable field extension over
\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}
.

When
\ell\!\mid\!q
, thanks to Xie’s
theorem

1.3
, if
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
f^{*}|_{H^{i}_{{\textrm{\'{e}t}}}(X,\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})}
, then so is its complex conjugate
\overline{\lambda}
, and moreover,
\lambda\cdot\overline{\lambda}=q^{i}
.
This implies that
\nu_{\ell}(\lambda)+\nu_{\ell}(\overline{\lambda})=i
, and hence the Newton polygon
\mathsf{NP}_{\nu_{\ell}}(P_{i})
of the characteristic polynomial
P_{i}(t)
is symmetric.

The remaining assertions for the Newton polygon
\mathsf{NP}_{\nu_{p}}(P_{i})
follow by similar reasoning.
∎

2.2.
Proof of Proposition
1.9

Recall that for a monic polynomial
P(t)=t^{n}+a_{1}t^{n-1}+\dots+a_{n}
over a nonarchimedean valued field
(K,\nu)
, its Newton polygon
\mathsf{NP}_{\nu}(P)
is completely characterized by a finite sequence of numbers

s_{1}(P)\leq s_{2}(P)\leq\dots\leq s_{n}(P),

where the
s_{i}(P)
are called the
slopes
of the polygon
\mathsf{NP}_{\nu}(P)
, corresponding to the
\nu
-valuations of the roots of
P(t)
.
In particular, the Newton polygon
\mathsf{NP}_{\nu}(P)
is represented by the piecewise linear function
\varphi_{P}\colon[0,n]\to\mathbf{R}
such that
\varphi_{P}(0)=0
and for
k=1,2,\ldots,n
,

\varphi_{P}(k)=s_{1}(P)+s_{2}(P)+\dots+s_{k}(P).

Hence for any two monic polynomials
P(t),Q(t)\in K[t]
of the same degree
n
, the associated Newton polygons satisfy
\mathsf{NP}_{\nu}(P)\geq\mathsf{NP}_{\nu}(Q)
if and only if for all
k=1,2,\ldots,n
,

\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}(P)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}(Q).

This is reminiscent of the theory of majorization.

For any
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
, let

x_{(1)}\leq x_{(2)}\leq\dots\leq x_{(n)}\quad\text{(resp. }x_{[1]}\geq x_{[2]}\geq\dots\geq x_{[n]}\text{)}

denote the components of
x
in nondecreasing (resp. nonincreasing) order.

Definition 2.3

(cf.
[
MOA11
, Definition A.1]
)
.

For any
x,y\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
, we say that
x
is
majorized
by
y
(or
y

majorizes

x
) and write
x\prec y
, if for all
k=1,2,\dots,n-1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{[i]}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}y_{[i]},\ \text{ and }\ \sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{[i]}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{[i]}.

Equivalently,
x\prec y
if for all
k=1,2,\dots,n-1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{(i)}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}y_{(i)},\ \text{ and }\ \sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{(i)}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{(i)}.

To show
proposition

1.9
in the case of abelian varieties, we need the following classic result essentially due to Schur.

Theorem 2.4

(cf.
[
MOA11
, Proposition C.2]
)
.

If
\varphi\colon\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}
is a symmetric and convex function, then
\varphi
is Schur-convex, i.e.,
x\prec y
implies
\varphi(x)\leq\varphi(y)
.

For any
x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
and
k=1,2,\dots,n
, denote

(2.2)

x^{(k)}=(x_{i_{1}}+x_{i_{2}}+\dots+x_{i_{k}})_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots<i_{k}\leq n}\in\mathbf{R}^{\binom{n}{k}},

where the indices
(i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{k})\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}^{k}
are sorted in the following natural way:
(i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{k})<(i^{\prime}_{1},i^{\prime}_{2},\dots,i^{\prime}_{k})
if the left-most nonzero component of
(i_{1}-i^{\prime}_{1},i_{2}-i^{\prime}_{2},\dots,i_{k}-i^{\prime}_{k})
is negative.
In particular,
x^{(1)}=x
,
x^{(n)}=x_{1}+x_{2}+\dots+x_{n}
, and

x^{(2)}=(x_{1}+x_{2},x_{1}+x_{3},\dots,x_{1}+x_{n},x_{2}+x_{3},\dots,x_{n-1}+x_{n})\in\mathbf{R}^{\binom{n}{2}}.

This gives rise to an
\mathbf{R}
-linear map

\displaystyle c_{k}\colon\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}^{\binom{n}{k}},\quad x\mapsto x^{(k)}.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
x,y\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
such that
x\prec y
.
Then for any
k=2,3,\dots,n
, we have
x^{(k)}\prec y^{(k)}
.

Proof.

Let
k
be fixed.
Let
l
be an arbitrary positive integer
\leq\binom{n}{k}
.
Define a function
s_{l}\colon\mathbf{R}^{\binom{n}{k}}\to\mathbf{R}
as follows: for
z\in\mathbf{R}^{\binom{n}{k}}
,
s_{l}(z)
is the sum of the
l
largest components of
z
, i.e.,

s_{l}(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{l}z_{[j]}.

Since
s_{l}
is the maximum of all possible sums of
l
different components of
z
, it is convex.
As
c_{k}
is
\mathbf{R}
-linear, the composite function
s_{l}\circ c_{k}\colon\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}
is also convex.
On the other hand, by definition,
s_{l}\circ c_{k}
is symmetric.
Therefore, thanks to
theorem

2.4
,
s_{l}\circ c_{k}
is Schur-convex; in particular, we prove that
s_{l}(x^{(k)})\leq s_{l}(y^{(k)})
.

When
l=\binom{n}{k}
, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle s_{l}(x^{(k)})

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots<i_{k}\leq n}(x_{i_{1}}+x_{i_{2}}+\dots+x_{i_{k}})

\displaystyle=\binom{n-1}{k-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}=\binom{n-1}{k-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}=s_{l}(y^{(k)}).

Hence by
definition

2.3
, we have shown that
x^{(k)}\prec y^{(k)}
.
∎

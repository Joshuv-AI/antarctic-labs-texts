Life devoted to healing: remembering a pioneer who never stopped serving
Authors: Hyung Bin Park, Kyu-Hak Jung
Published: 2025

The Korean orthopedic community has lost a distinguished leader, educator, and pioneer in shoulder and elbow surgery. Professor Young-Kyu Kim, who dedicated his life to advancing orthopedic medicine and training the next generation of surgeons, passed away on August 29, 2025 in South Korea. He was 66 years old (Fig. 1).

Born on May 27, 1959, Professor Kim graduated from Kyung Hee University School of Medicine in 1986, where he also completed his master's (1990) and doctoral degrees (1997). His dedication to orthopedic surgery began during his residency training, and he subsequently pursued extensive fellowship training both domestically and internationally to master shoulder and elbow surgery. Professor Kim's illustrious career spanned over three decades at Gachon University Gil Medical Center, where he served from 1997 until his retirement in July 2024. He began his association with the Gil Hospital system even earlier, serving as Chief of Orthopaedic Surgery at Dong-Incheon Gil Hospital from 1994 to 1997. Following his retirement ceremony in July 2024, Professor Kim continued his dedication to patient care by joining S Seoul Hospital in Suwon as Medical Director, where he practiced until his final illness.

Professor Kim was instrumental in introducing reverse total shoulder arthroplasty to Korea, being among the first to perform this procedure in a university hospital setting. His expertise in shoulder and elbow surgery earned him international recognition, and he became a leading authority in sports medicine and shoulder pathology. Through his extensive overseas training—including fellowships at prestigious institutions such as Columbia University, Steadman-Hawkins Sports Medicine Hospital, and various European centers—he brought cutting-edge techniques and knowledge back to Korea.

Professor Kim's commitment to advancing orthopedic knowledge was evidenced by his prolific academic output, which includes over 60 peer-reviewed publications in prestigious journals such as the American Journal of Sports Medicine, Journal of Shoulder and Elbow Surgery, and Clinical Orthopaedics and Related Research. He served as editor-in-chief of Clinics in Shoulder and Elbow (CiSE) from 2014 to 2022, significantly elevating the journal's international standing. His leadership extended to numerous professional organizations. He served as president of the Korean Shoulder and Elbow Society (2009), Korean Sports Medicine Society (2015–2017), Korean Orthopaedic Sports Medicine Society (2020–2021), and Korean Orthopaedic Pain Society (2020–2021). He also held important positions including vice-chairman of the Korean Olympic Committee Medical Commission (2017 Korean Orthopaedic Sports Medicine Society 2022). Professor Kim trained 11 shoulder and elbow fellows who now serve in prominent positions across Korea and around the world. His teaching philosophy emphasized not only technical excellence but also the fundamental principle that "patients must never suffer because of our inadequacy." This guiding principle shaped generations of orthopedic surgeons who continue to honor his legacy.

One of Professor Kim's most enduring contributions was the establishment of the Gachon Shoulder Meeting in 2010, which became one of Korea's premier shoulder surgery symposiums. For over a decade, this annual event featured live surgery demonstrations and brought together leading international experts, significantly advancing shoulder surgery education in Korea and Asia. Professor Kim's expertise in sports medicine was recognized through his long-term service as team physician for professional sports teams, including the SK Wyverns baseball team (2002–2006) and Incheon United FC (2005–2025). His contributions to Korean sports medicine helped elevate the standard of care for professional athletes.

He is survived by his loving wife and two sons, whose steadfast support enabled him to dedicate his life to medicine. He valued his family deeply and maintained a close bond with them throughout his distinguished career, balancing his professional commitments with devotion at home. In February 2025, he was diagnosed with advanced small cell lung cancer with systemic metastases and was given an initial prognosis of three months. Despite this, he continued his clinical and academic duties with characteristic resolve and professionalism. Responding well to treatment, he remained committed to his work and his family, demonstrating unwavering strength throughout his illness. His health declined in July 2025, and he passed away peacefully on August 29, 2025, surrounded by his loved ones. Professor Young-Kyu Kim’s legacy extends far beyond his publications and surgical innovations. He embodied the ideals of academic medicine through unwavering dedication to patient care, the advancement of medical knowledge, and the education of future surgeons. His contributions to Korean orthopedic surgery—particularly in shoulder and elbow surgery—remain deeply influential.

Even during his final years, he continued to work and teach despite illness, demonstrating that the essence of medicine lies in healing and service. His life stands as a testament to how one surgeon’s dedication can transform a field, and his influence will endure through the many physicians he trained and inspired.

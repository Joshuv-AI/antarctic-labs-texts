A simple proof of local universality for roots of Kac polynomials
Marcus Michelen, Oren Yakir

Abstract
. Let f n f_{n} be a random polynomial of degree n n with i.i.d. mean-zero and finite variance random coefficients. It is well known that the roots of f n f_{n} cluster uniformly around the unit circle as n n grows large. We give a simple and self-contained proof of local universality for the correlation functions of the roots at the microscopic scale 1 / n 1/n around a fixed point on the circle. While previous proofs of local universality were focused on studying the logarithmic potential of f n f_{n} , we instead directly compare the scaled random polynomial to a limiting Gaussian analytic function, and establish convergence of correlations via a soft argument, using only basic complex analysis and an anti-concentration bound of Esseen.



1.
Introduction

We consider the random Kac polynomial

(1)

f_{n}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\xi_{k}\,z^{k}\,,

where
\xi_{0},\ldots,\xi_{n}
are i.i.d. (possibly complex) random variables satisfying

(2)

\mathbb{E}[\xi_{0}]=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbb{E}[|\xi_{0}|^{2}]=1\,.

Classical works
[
6
,
17
]
show that with high probability, most roots of
f_{n}
tend to cluster around the unit circle as
n\to\infty
. Further, most roots are typically at distance
O(n^{-1})
from the unit circle, and
[
9
]
computed the asymptotics for the (properly normalized) first intensity function. In short, this allows one to compute the expected number of roots in different sets at this local scale around the unit circle.
To understand more than just the expected number of roots, it is natural to study the random roots at a
microscopic
scale: take a point on the unit circle, rescale by a factor of
n^{-1}
so roots become macroscopically spaced, and understand the random set of roots from the perspective of this point. More formally, let
\zeta_{0}=e^{i\theta}
for some fixed
\theta\in(0,\pi)
1
1

1

In this paper, for brevity, we only consider local universality of complex roots, though our methods are also applicable to studying real roots; see Section
1.3
for a further discussion.
, and set

(3)

F_{n}(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}f_{n}\Big(\zeta_{0}+\zeta_{0}\frac{z}{n}\Big)\,,

with
f_{n}
given by (
1
). Since
F_{n}
is a normalized sum of independent random variables, we expect the distribution of it (and hence, also of its roots) to be
universal
, in the sense that it should not really depend on the specific law of the random coefficients. Indeed, results of this flavor were established in
[
19
,
3
,
15
]
under different assumptions on the random coefficients and for many other models of random functions with independent coefficients. In short, these papers show that the
k
-point correlation measures (see Section
1.2
below for the definition of these measures) for the roots of
F_{n}
all become asymptotically close as
n\to\infty
. This is quite useful, since when the coefficients of
f_{n}
are independent Gaussian random variables, these correlations have exact integral expressions from the Kac-Rice formula, so many results proved for Gaussian random polynomials can be transferred to random polynomials with more general coefficients.

1.1.
Local universality result

The goal of this paper is to give a short and direct proof for the local universality result for the
k
-point measures, assuming only (
2
). A key step is to identify the scaling limit for the roots of
F_{n}
as
n\to\infty
.
The limiting point process will be given by the random zero set of the Gaussian analytic function

(4)

G(z)=\int_{0}^{1}e^{zt}\,{\rm d}B_{\mathbb{C}}(t)\,,

where
B_{\mathbb{C}}
is a complex-valued Brownian motion. Namely,
B_{\mathbb{C}}(t)=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\big(B_{1}(t)+iB_{2}(t)\big)
where
B_{1},B_{2}
are independent (real) standard Brownian motions.
For readers who are not familiar with this notation of stochastic integrals,
G:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
is simply a mean-zero Gaussian process with
\mathbb{E}\big[G(z)G(w)\big]=0
for all
z,w\in\mathbb{C}
, and (complex) covariance kernel

(5)

K(z,w)=\mathbb{E}\big[G(z)\overline{G(w)}\big]=\int_{0}^{1}e^{(z+\overline{w})t}\,{\rm d}t\,.

For a multiset of complex numbers
\Lambda
and
k\geq 1
we denote by

(6)

\Lambda_{\not=}^{k}=\Big\{(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})\,:\,\lambda_{j}\in\Lambda\,,\ \lambda_{j}\not=\lambda_{j^{\prime}}\ \text{ for }\ j\not=j^{\prime}\Big\}\,.

For an entire function
g
we denote by
\mathcal{Z}_{g}
the multiset of its zeros.

Theorem 1
.

For all
k\geq 1
and
\Phi:\mathbb{C}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
continuous with compact support we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})\in(\mathcal{Z}_{F_{n}})_{\not=}^{k}}\Phi(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})\bigg]=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})\in(\mathcal{Z}_{G})_{\not=}^{k}}\Phi(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})\bigg]\,.

Since the expectation on the right-hand side is always finite, the following corollary is immediate.

Corollary 2
.

Let
f_{n}
and
\widetilde{f}_{n}
be random polynomials of the form (
1
), and let
F_{n}
and
\widetilde{F}_{n}
denote their corresponding local scalings (
3
). We have that

\Bigg|\,\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})\in(\mathcal{Z}_{F_{n}})_{\not=}^{k}}\Phi(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})\bigg]-\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{(\widetilde{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\alpha}_{k})\in(\mathcal{Z}_{\widetilde{F}_{n}})_{\not=}^{k}}\Phi(\widetilde{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\alpha}_{k})\bigg]\Bigg|\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0\,,

for all
k\geq 1
and for all
\Phi:\mathbb{C}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
continuous with compact support.

1.2.
k
-point measures

Recall that a point process
\Lambda
in
\mathbb{C}
is a random locally finite multiset of complex numbers. For every point process
\Lambda
and
k\geq 1
, the
k
-point measure of
\Lambda
is a measure
\alpha_{\Lambda}^{k}
on
\mathbb{C}^{k}
, which is defined via the relation

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})\in\Lambda_{\not=}^{k}}\Phi(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})\bigg]=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{k}}\Phi\,{\rm d}\alpha_{\Lambda}^{k}\,.

If the point process
\Lambda
is simple (that is, if all multiplicities are equal to 1 almost surely) and the measure
\alpha_{\Lambda}^{k}
is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{C}^{k}
, then the associated density is called the
k
-point function of the process. With this terminology, Theorem
1
is saying that for each
k\geq 1
,

(7)

\qquad\alpha_{\mathcal{Z}_{F_{n}}}^{k}\xrightarrow{\ w^{\ast}\ }\alpha_{\mathcal{Z}_{G}}^{k}\qquad\qquad\text{as $n\to\infty$},

in the vague topology of measures on
\mathbb{C}^{k}
. In fact, the limiting zero set
\mathcal{Z}_{G}
is a simple point process (see Fact
8
below), and the corresponding
k
-point functions can be computed explicitly via the celebrated Kac-Rice formulas, see
[
8
, Corollary 3.4.2]
.

1.3.
Real roots and other ensembles

When the random coefficients of
f_{n}
are real, the roots of
f_{n}
come in conjugate pairs and
f_{n}
has
\Theta(\log n)
many real roots with high probability
[
12
,
5
,
10
]
.
Furthermore, in
[
19
,
3
,
15
,
13
]
various local universality results for real roots of
f_{n}
were derived. To keep this note brief, we focus only on complex roots, but our methods translate directly to deal with real roots as well. The key difference to point out is that when scaling near the real axis, the limiting Gaussian function is slightly different. In short, one needs to replace the complex Brownian motion
B_{\mathbb{C}}
in (
4
) with a standard
real
Brownian motion. Besides that, all other details of the proof are essentially the same.

While we do not pursue it here, it seems plausible that the general strategy we follow may be useful for proving local universality results for other models of random functions which are sums of many independent contributions.

1.4.
Previous proofs and other related works

As we already mentioned, different versions of Corollary
2
already exist in the literature. The starting point for all existing proofs is the fact that
\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\Delta\log|F_{n}|
is equal to the root count in a distributional sense. Using this observation, Tao-Vu
[
19
, Theorem 5.7]
applied a Lindeberg’s swapping argument for the random coefficients to show that the distribution of
\log|F_{n}|
is asymptotically universal, and with that conclude local universality of the roots. The proof in
[
19
]
is quite hard and by no means elementary (e.g. it uses a quantitative version of Gromov’s polynomial growth theorem
[
16
]
). Subsequently, Do-Nguyen-Vu
[
3
, Theorem 2.3]
established a local universality result which also allows the random coefficients to have a variance profile. The argument in
[
3
]
is different from
[
19
]
, but relies on a highly non-trivial result of Nazarov-Nishry-Sodin
[
14
]
on log-integrability of Rademacher Taylor series.
Finally, in the recent paper
[
15
]
, Nguyen-Vu came up with a unified treatment for different local universality theorems. While the approach in
[
15
]
is more elementary compared to
[
19
,
3
]
, it still applies a sophisticated argument to prove anti-concentration bounds to deal with the logarithmic singularity in
\log|F_{n}|
.

On the other hand, our proof of Theorem
1
is quite simple and direct.
We first identify the limiting function
G
and prove convergence of the random zeros in law. To upgrade this convergence to the convergence (
7
), we establish a uniform control on the tails for the local root count (thus showing uniform integrability, in an appropriate sense). The latter step is achieved by a simple argument, which involves only Jensen’s formula and a classical small-ball probability bound for non-singular random vectors due to Esseen
[
7
, Theorem 6.2]
(see Claim
12
).

We remark that in all of the previous proofs
[
19
,
3
,
15
]
mentioned above, it is assumed that the random coefficients have finite
(2+\varepsilon)
-moment for some fixed
\varepsilon>0
, while in our result we require only finite second moment via (
2
).

Remark
.

We note that the first step of proving convergence of the random zeros in law was also established in the very recent preprint
[
11
, Theorem 3.1]
by a similar method while the present work was being completed. The work
[
11
]
also allows the random coefficients to have a regularly-varying variance profile and demonstrates that the local limit changes from liquid-like to crystalline-like as the growth of the variance profile varies;
[
11
]
does not address convergence of
k
-point measures.

2.1.
Convergence in distribution

Recall that
F_{n}
is given by (
3

G
is given by (
4

\{F_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
and

\mathcal{E}

\{\mathcal{Z}_{F_{n}}\}_{n\geq 1}

Lemma 3
.

We have
F_{n}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\ d\ }G
, where convergence is in distribution as random elements of
\mathcal{E}
.

Before we turn to prove the lemma, we first show tightness of this family in
\mathcal{E}
.

Claim 4
.

For all
R\geq 1
and for all
n\geq 1
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\max_{|z|\leq R}|F_{n}(z)|\,\big]\leq\exp(R)\,.

Proof.

We first Taylor expand and then apply Cauchy-Schwarz to see

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\max_{|z|\leq R}|F_{n}(z)|\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\max_{|z|\leq R}\Big|\sum_{j\geq 0}F_{n}^{(j)}(0)\frac{z^{j}}{j!}\Big|\,\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{R^{j}}{j!}\big(\mathbb{E}[|F_{n}^{(j)}(0)|^{2}]\big)^{1/2}=\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{R^{j}}{j!}\Big(\frac{1}{n^{2j+1}}\sum_{k=j}^{n}\Big(\frac{k!}{(k-j)!}\Big)^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\leq\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{R^{j}}{j!}\,.\qed

Claim 5
.

For all
z,w\in\mathbb{C}
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\big[F_{n}(z)\overline{F_{n}(w)}\,\big]=K(z,w)\,,

where
K
is given by (
5
). Furthermore, we have that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\big[F_{n}(z)F_{n}(w)\big]=0\,.

Proof.

Set
\psi_{n}(z,w)=\big(1+\frac{z}{n}\big)\big(1+\frac{w}{n}\big)\,
and use (
3
) to compute

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[F_{n}(z)\overline{F_{n}(w)}\,\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\big(\psi_{n}(z,\overline{w})\big)^{k}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\exp\Big(\frac{k}{n}\big(z+\overline{w}\big)\Big)+O(n^{-1})\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}e^{t(z+\overline{w})}\,{\rm d}t\,,

where the third equality is just convergence of Riemann sums to the limiting integral. This proves the first assertion of the claim. With the above notation, we also have that

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\big[F_{n}(z)F_{n}(w)\,\big]\right|=\frac{|\mathbb{E}[\xi_{0}^{2}]|}{n}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n}\zeta_{0}^{2k}\big(\psi_{n}(z,w)\big)^{k}\right|\leq\frac{1}{n}\left|\left(\frac{1-\zeta_{0}^{2}}{1-\zeta_{0}^{2}\psi_{n}(z,w)}\right)\right|\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0

where
\zeta_{0}=e^{i\theta}
is the point on the unit circle around which we rescale the random polynomial
f_{n}
, and for the final bound we used
\theta\in(0,\pi)
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3
.

For
\ell\geq 1
we set

\mathcal{K}_{\ell}=\bigcap_{j\geq\ell}\Big\{f\in\mathcal{E}\,:\,\max_{|z|\leq j}|f(z)|\leq j^{2}\exp(j)\Big\}\,.

By Montel’s theorem
[
1
, Chap. 5, § 5.2]
,
\mathcal{K}_{\ell}\subset\mathcal{E}
is compact for each
\ell\geq 1
. Furthermore, Claim

\mathbb{P}\big(F_{n}\not\in\mathcal{K}_{\ell}\big)\leq\sum_{j\geq\ell}\mathbb{P}\Big(\max_{|z|\leq j}|F_{n}(z)|>j^{2}\exp(j)\Big)\leq\sum_{j\geq\ell}\frac{1}{j^{2}}\,,

uniformly in
n\geq 1
. This shows that the sequence of probability measures on
\mathcal{E}
induced by
\{F_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
is tight. Hence, to establish the desired convergence in distribution, it is now enough to check the convergence of the finite dimensional laws, i.e. to show that for all
z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\in\mathbb{C}
fixed we have

(8)

\big(F_{n}(z_{1}),\ldots,F_{n}(z_{m})\big)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\ d\ }\big(G(z_{1}),\ldots,G(z_{m})\big)\,,

where the convergence is in distribution as random vectors in
\mathbb{C}^{m}
. Claim
5
shows the corresponding correlation matrices in (
8

By the Cramér-Wold device, the stated convergence in (
8

\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}\in\mathbb{C}
we have

(9)

\sum_{j=1}^{m}\operatorname{Re}\big(\gamma_{j}\,F_{n}(z_{j})\big)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\ d\ }\sum_{j=1}^{m}\operatorname{Re}\big(\gamma_{j}\,G(z_{j})\big)\,.

Indeed, note that the left-hand side of (

\sum_{j=1}^{m}\operatorname{Re}\big(\gamma_{j}\,F_{n}(z_{j})\big)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\operatorname{Re}\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\xi_{k}\,v_{k}\Big)\qquad\text{ where }\qquad v_{k}=\zeta_{0}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\gamma_{j}\Big(1+\frac{z_{j}}{n}\Big)^{k}\,.

That is, the left-hand side of (

[
4

for triangular arrays (noting that
\{v_{k}\}

\{\gamma_{j}\}

8
) and we are done.

\mathbb{P}\Big(\big\|\sum_{j=0}^{n}\xi_{j}V_{j}\big\|\leq 1\Big)\leq C\,n^{-d/2}\,,

\xi
.

Proof.

Since
\xi
is non-degenerate, either the real or imaginary part of
\xi
is non-degenerate so assume without loss of generality that the real part of
\xi
is non-degenerate; then it is sufficient to assume
\xi_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let
\varphi_{\xi}(t)=\mathbb{E}[\exp(it\xi)]
denote the characteristic function of
\xi
. Esseen’s inequality
[
7
]
(see also
[
18
, Lemma 7.17]
for a textbook treatment) shows that for each
s\geq 0

(11)

\max_{w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{P}\Big(\big\|\sum_{j=0}^{n}\xi_{j}V_{j}-w\big\|\leq s\Big)\leq C_{d}\int_{\{\|\eta\|\leq\frac{s}{4}\}}\Big|\prod_{j=0}^{n}\varphi_{\xi}\big(\langle V_{j},\eta\rangle\big)\Big|\,{\rm d}m(\eta)\,,

where
m
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. By covering the unit ball with
O_{d,s}(1)
many balls of radius
s>0
, it is sufficient to bound the right-hand-side of (
11
) by
O(n^{-d/2})
for some fixed
s=s(M,d,\xi)>0
. Let
\xi^{\prime}
be an independent copy of the random variable
\xi
. Since
\xi
has a non-degenerate distribution, there exists
a>0
so that
\mathbb{P}(a^{-1}\leq|\xi-\xi^{\prime}|\leq a)>0
, and we get that

\displaystyle\big|\varphi_{\xi}(t)\big|^{2}=\big|\mathbb{E}\cos((\xi-\xi^{\prime})t)\big|\leq\exp(-c_{0}t^{2})

for some
c_{0}>0
depending only on the distribution of
\xi
and for all
|t|\leq\tfrac{1}{4aM}
. Setting
s=1/(aM)
we may bound

\displaystyle\int_{\{\|\eta\|\leq\frac{s}{4}\}}\Big|\prod_{j=0}^{n}\varphi_{\xi}\big(\langle V_{j},\eta\rangle\big)\Big|\,{\rm d}m(\eta)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{\|\eta\|\leq\frac{s}{4}\}}e^{-c\sum_{j=0}^{n}\langle V_{j},\eta\rangle^{2}}\,{\rm d}m(\eta)=\int_{\{\|\eta\|\leq\frac{s}{4}\}}e^{-cn\,\langle\eta,\Sigma_{n}\eta\rangle}\,{\rm d}m(\eta)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{\|\eta\|\leq\frac{s}{4}\}}e^{-c\kappa n\,\|\eta\|^{2}}\,{\rm d}m(\eta)\leq C^{\prime}n^{-d/2}\,.\qed

Next, we show how to get Lemma
11
from Esseen’s bound.

Proof of Lemma
11
.

By possibly increasing
R
by a factor which depends only on
k\geq 1
, we may assume that there exists
0\leq\varphi_{1}<\varphi_{2}<\cdots<\varphi_{4k}\leq R
so that

\varphi_{j+1}-\varphi_{j}\geq 8k\,,\qquad\text{for all }\ j=1,\ldots,4k.

Since the map
R\mapsto\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{D}_{R})
is non-decreasing, proving the conclusion of the lemma with this (possibly larger)
R=R_{k}
suffices. We use Claim
12
to show that
F_{n}
is typically not too small on some
\{i\varphi_{j}\}
. Define

V_{j}=\operatorname{Re}\left(\zeta_{0}^{j}\cdot\Big(\Big(1+\frac{i\varphi_{1}}{n}\Big)^{j},\ldots,\Big(1+\frac{i\varphi_{4k}}{n}\Big)^{j}\Big)\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{4k}\,.

Note first that
\|V_{j}\|=O_{k}(1)
. To verify the non-singularity condition of Claim
12
, we see that
\Sigma_{n}
is the covariance matrix for the vector

\Big(\operatorname{Re}F_{n}(i\varphi_{1}),\ldots,\operatorname{Re}F_{n}(i\varphi_{4k})\Big)\,.

Claim
5
implies that it suffices to show uniform non-singularity when
F_{n}
is replaced by
G
. Let us write this matrix
\Sigma(j,j^{\prime})=\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{Re}G(i\varphi_{j})\operatorname{Re}G(i\varphi_{j^{\prime}})]
. Note that
\Sigma(j,j)=1/2
and for
j\neq j^{\prime}
we have

|\Sigma(j,j^{\prime})|\leq|K(i\varphi_{j},i\varphi_{j^{\prime}})|=\Big|\int_{0}^{1}e^{it(\varphi_{j}-\varphi_{j^{\prime}})}{\rm d}t\Big|\leq\frac{2}{|\varphi_{j}-\varphi_{j^{\prime}}|}\leq\frac{1}{4k}\,.

For every
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\|\eta\|=1
we then have

1/2-\langle\eta,\Sigma\eta\rangle=\sum_{j^{\prime}\neq j}v_{j}\Sigma_{j,j^{\prime}}v_{j^{\prime}}\leq\frac{1}{4k}\sum_{j^{\prime}\neq j}|v_{j}||v_{j^{\prime}}|\leq\frac{1}{4}

where the last bound is by Cauchy-Schwarz. By scaling, this shows
\langle\eta,\Sigma\eta\rangle\geq(1/4)\|\eta\|^{2}
for all
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which gives the desired non-singularity. If we set
\mathcal{B}=\{|F_{n}(i\varphi_{j})|\leq 1\text{ for all }j=1,\ldots,4k\}
, then Claim
12
shows
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{B})\leq C_{k}n^{-2k}
.
On the other hand, Jensen’s formula
[
1
, Chap. 5, § 3.1]
states that for all
r>0

\log|F_{n}(i\varphi_{j})|=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log|F_{n}\big(i\varphi_{j}+re^{i\theta}\big)|\,{\rm d}\theta-\sum_{\alpha\,:F_{n}(\alpha)=0}\log_{+}\Big(\frac{r}{|\alpha-i\varphi_{j}|}\Big)\,.

On the event
\mathcal{B}^{c}
, we apply this formula with
r=3R
and the index on which
|F_{n}|\geq 1
to get that

\mathcal{Z}_{n}\big(\mathbb{D}_{R}\big)\leq\frac{1}{\log(2)}\max_{|z|\leq 4R}\log|F_{n}(z)|\,.

On the other hand, since
F_{n}
is a polynomial, we always have the trivial bound
\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{D}_{R})\leq n
. Combining the above with the inequality
\log^{k}(x)\leq C_{k}x
, we finally arrive at

\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathcal{Z}_{n}\big(\mathbb{D}_{R}\big)^{k}\Big]\leq n^{k}\,\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{B})+C_{k}\mathbb{E}\big[\max_{|z|\leq 4R}\log^{k}|F_{n}(z)|\big]\leq C_{k}\Big(n^{-k}+\mathbb{E}\big[\max_{|z|\leq 4R}|F_{n}(z)|\big]\Big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Claim}~\ref{claim:F_n_and_derivatives_are_controlled}}}{{\leq}}C(k,R)\,.\qed

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Without loss of generality we may assume that
\Phi
is non-negative and has
\Phi\leq 1
. For
M\geq 1
we set
g_{M}(x)=\min\{x,M\}
, and note that Claim
10
implies that

(12)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\big[g_{M}\big(T_{\Phi}(\mathcal{Z}_{F_{n}})\big)\big]=\mathbb{E}\big[g_{M}\big(T_{\Phi}(\mathcal{Z}_{G})\big)\big]\,.

Note also that by the monotone convergence theorem we have
\mathbb{E}\big[g_{M}\big(T_{\Phi}(\mathcal{Z}_{G})\big)\big]\xrightarrow{M\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\big[T_{\Phi}(\mathcal{Z}_{G})\big]\,.
Thus, to show
\mathbb{E}\big[T_{\Phi}(\mathcal{Z}_{F_{n}})\big]\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\big[T_{\Phi}(\mathcal{Z}_{G})\big]
, it is enough to prove

(13)

\lim_{M\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\Big|\mathbb{E}\big[T_{\Phi}(\mathcal{Z}_{F_{n}})\big]-\mathbb{E}\big[g_{M}\big(T_{\Phi}(\mathcal{Z}_{F_{n}})\big)\big]\Big|=0\,.

To prove (
13
), take
R\geq 1
large enough so that
\text{supp}(\Phi)\subset\mathbb{D}_{R}^{k}
, and note
|T_{\Phi}(\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{D}_{R}))|\leq\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{D}_{R})^{k}
since
\Phi\leq 1
. By Cauchy-Schwarz we may bound

\displaystyle\Big|\mathbb{E}\big[T_{\Phi}(\mathcal{Z}_{F_{n}})-g_{M}\big(T_{\Phi}(\mathcal{Z}_{F_{n}})\big)\big]\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathbf{1}_{\{\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{D}_{R})^{k}\geq M\}}\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{D}_{R})^{k}\Big]\leq\left(\mathbb{P}\big(\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{D}_{R})^{k}\geq M\big)\cdot\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{D}_{R})^{2k}]\right)^{1/2}\leq\frac{\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{D}_{R})^{2k}]}{M}\,,

where in the last step we used Markov’s inequality. In view of Lemma
11
, the above display confirms (
13
) and completes the proof.

A Central Limit Theorem for the Ewens–Pitman random partition in the large- θ \theta regime via a martingale approach
B. Bercu, C. Contardi, E. Dolera, S. Favaro

Abstract
The Ewens-Pitman model defines a distribution on random partitions of { 1 , … , n } \{1,\ldots,n\} , with parameters α ∈ [ 0 , 1 ) \alpha\in[0,1) and θ > − α \theta>-\alpha ; the case α = 0 \alpha=0 reduces to the classical Ewens model from population genetics. We investigate the large- n n asymptotic behaviour of the Ewens-Pitman random partition in the nonstandard regime θ = λ ​ n \theta=\lambda n with λ > 0 \lambda>0 , establishing joint fluctuation results for the total number of blocks K n { n } K_{n}^{\{n\}} and the counts K r , n { n } K_{r,n}^{\{n\}} of blocks of sizes r = 1 , … , d r=1,\dots,d , for fixed d ∈ ℕ d\in\mathbb{N} . In particular, for α ∈ [ 0 , 1 ) \alpha\in[0,1) and θ = λ ​ n \theta=\lambda n , our main result provides a strong law of large numbers and a central limit theorem for the ( d + 1 ) (d+1) -dimensional vector 𝐊 d , n { n } = ( K n { n } , K 1 , n { n } , … , K d , n { n } ) T \mathbf{K}_{d,n}^{\{n\}}=\bigl(K_{n}^{\{n\}},K_{1,n}^{\{n\}},\dots,K_{d,n}^{\{n\}}\bigr)^{T} as n → ∞ n\to\infty . The proof exploits the Chinese restaurant sequential construction under θ = λ ​ n \theta=\lambda n and a central limit theorem for triangular arrays of martingales, extending techniques previously developed for the classical regime with fixed θ \theta . As corollaries of our results, we recover known asymptotics for K n { n } K_{n}^{\{n\}} and derive new strong laws and central limit theorems for each fixed K r , n { n } K_{r,n}^{\{n\}} , thereby completing earlier weak-law results and providing a comprehensive asymptotic description of the Ewens-Pitman partition structure in the large- θ \theta setting.



1
Introduction

The Ewens-Pitman model for random partitions first appeared in
[
32
]
as a two-parameter generalization of the celebrated Ewens model in population genetics
[
14
]
; see
[
10
]
and references therein. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, denote by
\Pi_{n}
a random partition of the set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
into
K_{n}\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
blocks of sizes (or empirical frequencies)
\mathbf{N}_{n}=(N_{1,n},\ldots,N_{K_{n},n})\in\mathbb{N}^{K_{n}}
such that
n=\sum_{1\leq i\leq K_{n}}N_{i,n}
. For
\alpha\in[0,1)
and
\theta>-\alpha
, the Ewens-Pitman model assigns to
\Pi_{n}
the probability

P[K_{n}=k,\mathbf{N}_{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})]=\frac{1}{k!}{n\choose n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}}\frac{(\theta)_{(k,\alpha)\uparrow}}{(\theta)_{n\uparrow}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-\alpha)_{(n_{i}-1)\uparrow},

(1)

where
(x)_{(n,a)\uparrow}
denotes the rising factorial of
x
of order
n
and increment
a
, i.e.
(x)_{(n,a)\uparrow}:=\prod_{0\leq i\leq n-1}(x+ia)
and
(x)_{n\uparrow}:=(x)_{(n,1)\uparrow}
. The distribution (
1
) admits a sequential construction in terms of the Chinese restaurant process
[
32
,
39
]
and a Poisson process construction by random sampling the two-parameter Poisson-Dirichlet distribution
[
31
,
34
]
; see also
[
12
,
13
]
for a construction through the negative-Binomial compound Poisson model for random partitions
[
8
]
. For
\alpha=0
the Ewens-Pitman model reduces to the Ewens model, arising by random sampling the Poisson-Dirichlet distribution
[
27
]
. The Ewens-Pitman model plays a critical role in a variety of research areas, e.g., population genetics, Bayesian statistics, combinatorics, machine learning and statistical physics. See
[
33
, Chapter 3]
for an overview of the Ewens-Pitman model.

Under the Ewens-Pitman model (
1
), there have been several works on the large
n
asymptotic behaviour of
K_{n}
, showing different behaviours depending on whether
\alpha=0
or
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Throughout, denote the almost sure and weak convergence by
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\longrightarrow}}
and
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{\longrightarrow}}
, respectively. For
\alpha=0
and
\theta>0
,
[
28
, Theorem 2.3]
and Lindeberg-Feller central limit theorem show that as
n\rightarrow+\infty

\frac{K_{n}}{\log n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\longrightarrow}}\theta

(2)

and

\sqrt{\log n}\left(\frac{K_{n}}{\log n}-\theta\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sqrt{\theta}\,\mathcal{N}(0,1),

(3)

where
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
denotes the standard Gaussian random variable. Instead, for
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\theta>-\alpha
,
[
33
, Theorem 3.8]
and
[
5
, Theorem 2.1 and Theorem 2.3]
show that as
n\rightarrow+\infty

\frac{K_{n}}{n^{\alpha}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\longrightarrow}}S_{\alpha,\theta}

(4)

and

\sqrt{n^{\alpha}}\left(\frac{K_{n}}{n^{\alpha}}-S_{\alpha,\theta}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sqrt{\tilde{S}_{\alpha,\theta}}\,\mathcal{N}(0,1),

(5)

where
S_{\alpha,\theta}
and
\tilde{S}_{\alpha,\theta}
are scaled Mittag-Leffler random variables
[
40
]
, sharing the same distribution, and
\tilde{S}_{\alpha,\theta}
is independent of
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
. See
[
33
, Chapter 3]
for details on (
2
) and (
4
).

An equivalent description of the partition
\Pi_{n}
is given, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, by the sequence
\mathbf{K}_{n}:=(K_{n},K_{1,n},...,K_{n,n},K_{n+1,n},...)
, where for
r\in\mathbb{N}
,
K_{r,n}
denotes the number of blocks of
\Pi_{n}
of size
r
, that is

K_{r,n}=\sum_{k=1}^{K_{n}}\mathds{1}_{\{N_{k,n}=r\}}

with
\mathds{1}
denoting the indicator function. Clearly, for
r>n
,
K_{r,n}=0
a.s., and furthermore it is easy to verify that

\sum_{r=1}^{n}K_{r,n}=K_{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{r=1}^{n}r\,K_{r,n}=n.

These quantities arise naturally from the sequential construction of the Ewens–Pitman distribution; see appendix
A.5
for details. There are several known results regarding the asymptotic behaviour of
\mathbf{K}_{n}
under the Ewens–Pitman model. In particular,
[
2
, Theorem 1]
entails that for
\alpha=0

K_{r,n}\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}}Z_{r}

(6)

for every
r\in\mathbb{N}
,
and

(K_{1,n},K_{2,n},\ldots)\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{\longrightarrow}}}(Z_{1},Z_{2},\ldots),

(7)

where the
Z_{r}
’s are independent Poisson random variables with
\mathbb{E}[Z_{r}]=\theta/r
, for all
r\geq 1
. See
[
3
]
for generalizations and refinements of these asymptotic results. For
\alpha\in(0,1)
, it follows from
[
33
, Lemma 3.11]
that

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{K_{r,n}}{n^{\alpha}}=p_{\alpha}(r)S_{\alpha,\theta}\qquad\text{a.s.},

(8)

where

p_{\alpha}(r)=\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)_{(r-1)\uparrow}}{r!}.

We refer to
[
5
, Theorem 3.3. and Theorem 3.4]
for Gaussian fluctuations and laws of iterated logarithm for
K_{r,n}
.

Beyond the almost-sure convergence and Gaussian fluctuations,

1.1
Main result

Under the Ewens-Pitman model (
1
), we study the large
n
asymptotic behaviour of the random
(d+1)-
dimensional vector

\mathbf{K}_{d,n}^{\{n\}}=\left(K_{n}^{\{n\}},K_{1,n}^{\{n\}},...,K_{d,n}^{\{n\}}\right)^{T}

for fixed
d\in\mathbb{N}
, where the superscript
\{n\}
denotes the fact that the parameter
\theta
is allowed to depend linearly on
n\in\mathbb{N}
, namely
\theta=\lambda n
with
\lambda>0
. This non-standard asymptotic regime first appeared in
[
18
]
for the the Ewens model, i.e.
\alpha=0
. In particular,
[
37
, Proposition 2 and Theorem 2]
established a law of large numbers (LLN) and a central limit theorem (CLT) for
K^{\{n\}}_{n}
for
\alpha=0
and
\theta=\lambda n
, later extended to
\alpha\in[0,1)
by
[
9
, Theorem 1.2]
and
[
35
, Theorems 1 and 2]
. Further,
[
4
, Proposition 1]
established a weak law of large number for the
K_{r,n}^{\{n\}}
.

Denote by
\mathcal{S}_{+}^{d+1}(\mathbb{R})
the set of symmetric and positive-semidefinite
(d+1)\times(d+1)
real matrices. The next theorem states the main result of the paper, namely a LLN and a CLT for
\mathbf{K}_{d,n}^{\{n\}}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
d\geq 1
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathbf{K}_{d,n}^{\{n\}}

be the random vector containing the number of partition blocks and the number of partition blocks of sizes
1,...,d
under the Ewens-Pitman model with parameters
\alpha\in[0,1)
and
\theta=\lambda n
, with
\lambda>0
. Then, as
n\rightarrow+\infty
there hold:

i)

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{K}_{d,n}^{\{n\}}\right]=n\,\mathfrak{M}_{d,\alpha,\lambda}+O(1)

(9)

ii)

(SLLN)

\frac{\mathbf{K}_{d,n}^{\{n\}}}{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{M}_{d,\alpha,\lambda};

(10)

iii)

(CLT)

\frac{\mathbf{K}_{d,n}^{\{n\}}-n\,\mathfrak{M}_{d,\alpha,\lambda}}{\sqrt{n}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{w}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\Sigma_{d,\alpha,\lambda}).

(11)

where
\mathbf{\mathfrak{M}}_{d,\alpha,\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
and
\Sigma_{d,\alpha,\lambda}\in\mathcal{S}_{+}^{d+1}(\mathbb{R})
are explicit (see equations (
13
) and (
14
) below) and only depend on
\alpha
and
\lambda
.

Theorem
1.1
provides a novel, comprehensive description of the behaviour of the Ewens-Pitman random partition under the non-standard asymptotic regime
\theta=\lambda n
, with
\lambda>0
. By comparing the LLN (
10
) with the almost-sure fluctuation results (
2
), (
4
), (
7
) and (
8
), we can assess how the linear dependence of
\theta
on
n
, i.e.
\theta=\lambda n
, affects the large
n
asymptotic behaviour of
K_{n}
in terms of both scaling and limiting behaviour. Specifically, while the almost-sure fluctuations exhibit different scalings depending on the value of
\alpha
, and for
K_{n}
as compared to
K_{r,n}
, the LLN (
10
) exhibits the “usual” linear scaling in
n
for all
\alpha\in[0,1)
and for
K_{n}^{\{n\}}
as well as all the
K_{r,n}^{\{n\}}
. Moreover, in the almost-sure fluctuation results (
2
), (
4
) and (
8
) the limiting behaviour is non-random for
\alpha=0
and random for
\alpha\in(0,1)
, which in turn determines the non-random and random centerings in (
3
) and (
5
) , respectively. In contrast, the LLN (
10
) yields a non-random limit for all
\alpha\in[0,1)
, leading to a non-random centering in the CLT (
11
).

The proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on the sequential construction of the Ewens–Pitman model in the regime
\theta=\lambda n
. The proof of the CLT (
11
) follows by adapting a martingale argument used in
[
5
]
to the setting of such construction. However, in our non-standard large-

with

Finally,

. The genetic interpretation of

To prove Theorem

2.1
Sequential construction of the partition in the large-
\theta
regime

It is well-known that the (standard) Ewens–Pitman model admits a sequential construction in terms of the Chinese Restaurant Process (CRP) – see Appendix
A.5
for details. The model in the “large-
\theta
” setting also admits a sequential construction in terms of the CRP. However, this requires a careful specification of the objects at hand, to avoid confusion with the model in the classical setting. In particular, the sequential construction in this case is used to build finite arrays of partitions, defined as follows.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
h\in\{1,...,n\}
, let

\mathbf{K}_{h}^{\{n\}}=\left(K_{0,h}^{\{n\}},K_{1,h}^{\{n\}},...,K_{h,h}^{\{n\}}\right)

be a variable encoding a random partition of
[h]=\{1,...,h\}
, with
K_{0,h}^{\{n\}}:=K_{h}^{\{n\}}
the number of blocks and for
r\geq 1
,
K_{r,h}^{\{n\}}
the number of blocks of numerosity
r
. Defining, for
r\in\{0,...,n\}
,

\xi_{r,h}^{\{n\}}=K_{r,h}^{\{n\}}-K_{r,h-1}^{\{n\}}

(22)

and letting

\mathcal{F}_{h-1}^{\{n\}}=\sigma\left(\mathbf{K}_{1}^{\{n\}},...,\mathbf{K}_{h-1}^{\{n\}}\right),

for
h\in\{2,...,n\}
, the following sequential relation holds:

P\left[\xi_{r,h}^{\{n\}}=k\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{h-1}^{\{n\}}\right]=\begin{cases}p_{h-1}^{\{n\}}&\text{ if }k=1\\
q_{h-1}^{\{n\}}&\text{ if }k=-1\\
1-p_{h-1}^{\{n\}}-q_{h-1}^{\{n\}}&\text{ if }k=0\end{cases}

(23)

with

p_{r,h-1}^{\{n\}}=\begin{cases}\frac{\alpha K_{h-1}^{\{n\}}+\lambda n}{\lambda n+h-1}&\text{ if }r=0,1\\
\frac{(r-1-\alpha)K_{r-1,h-1}^{\{n\}}}{\lambda n+h-1}&\text{ if }r\geq 2\end{cases}

(24)

and

q_{r,h-1}^{\{n\}}=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }r=0\\
\frac{(r-\alpha)K_{r,h-1}^{\{n\}}}{\lambda n+h-1}&\text{ if }r\geq 1\end{cases}

(25)

Clearly, (
23
) is recovered by substituting
\theta=\lambda n
in the equivalent relation (
A.39
) of the standard CRP construction. It should be noted, however, that in this regime the recursive relation only holds for
h\leq n
, and the array
\left(\mathbf{K}_{1}^{\{n\}},...,\mathbf{K}_{n}^{\{n\}}\right)
is not further extendable to an exchangeable random partition of
[n+1]
. Furthermore, two partitions
\mathbf{K}_{i}^{\{n\}}
and
\mathbf{K}_{j}^{\{m\}}
with
m\neq n
do not satisfy compatibility relations of any kind. In other words, we are considering triangular arrays of the form

\displaystyle(\theta=\lambda)\quad

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{K}_{1}^{{\{1\}}}}

\displaystyle(\theta=2\lambda)\quad

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}_{1}^{\{2\}}\,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{K}_{2}^{\{2\}}}

\displaystyle\vdots\quad\quad\quad

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\ddots

\displaystyle(\theta=n\lambda)\quad

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}_{1}^{\{n\}}\,\mathbf{K}_{2}^{\{n\}}\,...\,\mathbf{K}_{n-1}^{\{n\}}\,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{K}_{n}^{\{n\}}}\quad\quad

\displaystyle\vdots\quad\quad\quad

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ddots

and Theorem
1.1
is concerned with the “diagonal” sequence
\left(\mathbf{K}_{d,n}^{\{n\}}\right)_{n}
where for each
n
,
\mathbf{K}_{d,n}^{\{n\}}
is the projection of
\mathbf{K}_{n}^{\{n\}}
on the first
d+1
coordinates.

2.2
An important lemma

This result is an essential preliminary to the proof of theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
x\in[0,1]
, define

m_{0;\alpha,\lambda}(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}\left[\left(\frac{\lambda+x}{\lambda}\right)^{\alpha}-1\right]&\text{if }\alpha\in(0,1)\\
\lambda\log\left(\frac{\lambda+x}{\lambda}\right)&\text{if }\alpha=0\end{cases}

and for
r\geq 1
,

m_{r;\alpha,\lambda}(x)=\frac{(1-\alpha)_{\uparrow r-1}}{r!}\,x^{r}\lambda^{1-\alpha}(x+\lambda)^{\alpha-r}

Then, as
n\to+\infty
, for all
r\in\{0,...,n\}
,

1.

\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[K_{r,\lfloor xn\rfloor}^{\{n\}}\right]}{n}\rightarrow m_{r;\alpha,\lambda}(x)

uniformly on
[0,1]
.

2.

the following SLLN holds uniformly for
x\in[0,1]
:

\frac{K_{r,\lfloor xn\rfloor}^{\{n\}}}{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\longrightarrow}}m_{r;\alpha,\lambda}(x)

(26)

Proof.

The structure of the proof is the same for the three cases
r\geq 1
;
r=0,\,\alpha=0
and
r=0,\,\alpha\in(0,1)
, but it involves different calculations. For this reason, we present here only the general idea; the complete calculations, differentiated for the three cases, are deferred to appendix
A.6
.

For all
r\in\{0,...,n\},\alpha\in[0,1)
and
\lambda>0
, we exploit the explicit expressions for

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(K_{r,h}^{\{n\}}\right)_{\downarrow s}\right].

They are available for all
s\in\mathbb{N}
in
[
15
, Proposition 1]
for
r\geq 1
and can be derived from
[
33
, Equation 3.11]
for
r=0
. Upon substituting
h
with
\lfloor xn\rfloor
, standard asymptotical results allow to prove by direct calculation that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\frac{1}{n^{s-2}}\cdot\left|\mathbb{E}\left[(K_{r,\lfloor xn\rfloor}^{\{n\}})_{\downarrow s}\right]-\left(n^{s}\cdot\left(m_{r;\alpha,\lambda}(x)\right)^{s}+n^{s-1}\cdot\mathcal{S}_{s}(x)\right)\right|=c

(27)

for some constant
c
and some explicit function
\mathcal{S}_{s}(x)
not depending on
n
. In particular, for
s=1
, this rewrites as

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\left|\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[K_{r,\lfloor xn\rfloor}^{\{n\}}\right]}{n}-m_{r;\alpha,\lambda}(x)\right|=0

Proving point (i).

To prove the strong law of large numbers (
26
), we show complete convergence of the sequence
K_{r,\lfloor xn\rfloor}^{\{n\}}/n
by showing that
\mathbb{E}\left[\left(K_{r,\lfloor xn\rfloor}^{\{n\}}-\mathbb{E}\left[K_{r,\lfloor xn\rfloor}^{\{n\}}\right]\right)^{4}\right]=O(n^{2})
uniformly for
x\in[0,1]
. Write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(K_{r,\lfloor xn\rfloor}^{\{n\}}-\mathbb{E}\left[K_{r,\lfloor xn\rfloor}^{\{n\}}\right]\right)^{4}\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{k=0}^{4}(-1)^{4-k}\binom{4}{k}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[K_{r,\lfloor xn\rfloor}^{\{n\}}\right]\right)^{4-k}\sum_{s=0}^{k}S(k,s)\,\mathbb{E}\left[\left(K_{r,\lfloor xn\rfloor}^{\{n\}}\right)_{\downarrow s}\right],

where
S(k,s)
denote the Stirling number of the second kind. Expanding such expression and computing the expansion of powers of
\mathbb{E}\left[K_{r,\lfloor xn\rfloor}^{\{n\}}\right]
yields, after tedious but straightforward calculations,

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\,\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\left(K_{r,\lfloor xn\rfloor}^{\{n\}}-\mathbb{E}\left[K_{r,\lfloor xn\rfloor}^{\{n\}}\right]\right)^{4}\right]-\left(n^{4}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{r;\alpha,\lambda}(x)+n^{3}\cdot\mathcal{B}_{r;\alpha,\lambda}(x)\right)\right|=c

for some constant
c
, where

\mathcal{A}_{r;\alpha,\lambda}(x)=\left(m_{r;\alpha,\lambda}(x)\right)^{4}\cdot\left(1-4+6-3\right)=0

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{r;\alpha,\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=m^{3}_{r;\alpha,\lambda}(x)\left[(-12+12-4)\mathcal{S}_{1}(x)+(6-12+6)\right]+6\,m^{2}_{r;\alpha,\lambda}(x)\,\mathcal{S}_{2}(x)

\displaystyle\quad+m_{r;\alpha,\lambda}(x)\,\mathcal{S}_{3}(x)+\mathcal{S}_{4}(x)

\displaystyle=-4\,m^{3}_{r;\alpha,\lambda}(x)\,\mathcal{S}_{1}(x)+6\,m^{2}_{r;\alpha,\lambda}(x)\,\mathcal{S}_{2}(x)-4m_{r;\alpha,\lambda}(x)\,\mathcal{S}_{3}(x)+\mathcal{S}_{4}(x).

Further, it can be proven by substituting the explicit expressions for
S_{s}(x)
that

\mathcal{B}_{r;\alpha,\lambda}(x)=0

(28)

for all
r\in\{0,...,n\}
and
\alpha\in[0,1)
; see appendix
A.6
for details. Therefore,

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\sup_{x\in[0,1]}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\left(K_{r,\lfloor xn\rfloor}^{\{n\}}-\mathbb{E}\left[K_{r,\lfloor xn\rfloor}^{\{n\}}\right]\right)^{4}\right]=c

and the proof is concluded.
∎

2.4
Proof of the CLT (
11
)

The proof of (
11
) relies on the construction of a martingale and on the use of the Lindeberg–Levy central limit theorem for arrays of martingales given in
[
25

.

2.4.1
Construction of the martingale

The sequential construction of the partition under the large-
\theta
regime of Section
2.1
entails that for all
h\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and for all
r\geq 0

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[K_{r,h}^{\{n\}}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\{n\}}\right]

\displaystyle=K_{r,h-1}^{\{n\}}+p_{r,h-1}^{\{n\}}-q_{r,h-1}^{\{n\}}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{r,h-1}^{\{n\}}K_{r,h-1}^{\{n\}}+\beta_{r,h-1}^{\{n\}}

(29)

where

\gamma_{r,h-1}^{\{n\}}=\frac{\lambda n+h-1-r+\alpha}{\lambda n+h-1}

and

\beta_{r,h-1}^{\{n\}}=\begin{cases}\frac{\lambda n}{\lambda n+h-1}&\text{ if }r=0\\
p_{r,h-1}^{\{n\}}&\text{ if }r\geq 1\end{cases}.

For
r\in\{0,...,n\}
and for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
h\in\{1,...,n\}
, define

a_{r,h}^{\{n\}}=\prod_{k=1}^{h-1}\left(\gamma_{r,k}^{\{n\}}\right)^{-1}

(30)

and

A_{r,h}^{\{n\}}=\sum_{k=1}^{h-1}a_{r,k+1}^{\{n\}}\beta_{r,k}^{\{n\}}.

(31)

Further, for fixed
d\in\mathbf{N}
, let

\displaystyle\mathbf{\Xi}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}

\displaystyle=\left(\xi_{0,h}^{\{n\}},\xi_{1,h}^{\{n\}},...,\xi_{d,h}^{\{n\}}\right)^{T}

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}

\displaystyle=\left(A_{0,h}^{\{n\}},A_{1,h}^{\{n\}},...,A_{d,h}^{\{n\}}\right)^{T}

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}

\displaystyle=\left(a_{0,h}^{\{n\}},a_{1,h}^{\{n\}},...,a_{d,h}^{\{n\}}\right)^{T}.

We introduce the following notation: given a vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
, we denote by
\operatorname{Diag}(v)
the
(d+1)\times(d+1)
diagonal matrix defined by

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{Diag}(v)\right)_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }i\neq j\\
v_{i}&\text{ if }i=j\end{cases}

With this convention, let

\mathcal{A}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}=\operatorname{Diag}\left(\mathbf{a}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}\right)

and finally define

\mathbf{M}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}=\mathcal{A}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}\cdot\mathbf{K}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}-\mathbf{A}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}

(32)

and

\mathbf{\Delta}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}=\mathbf{M}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}-\mathbf{M}_{d,h-1}^{\{n\}}

(33)

Lemma 2.2
.

For every
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\left(\mathbf{M}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}\right)_{1\leq h\leq n}
is a martingale with respect to the filtration
\left(\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\{n\}}\right)_{1\leq h\leq n}
.

The proof of this lemma is immediate and relies on the identity

\mathbf{\Delta}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}=\mathcal{A}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}\cdot\left[\mathbf{\Xi}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}-\left(p_{r,h-1}^{\{n\}}-q_{r,h-1}^{\{n\}}\right)_{r\in\{0,...,d\}}\right].

(34)

See appendix
A.7
for details.

2.4.2
Completing the proof

Let
n
be a sequence of integers such that
h_{n}\to+\infty
as
n\to+\infty
. Denote by

\langle\mathbf{M}_{d}^{\{n\}}\rangle=\left(\langle\mathbf{M}_{d}^{\{n\}}\rangle_{1},...,\langle\mathbf{M}_{d}^{\{n\}}\rangle_{n}\right)^{T}

the increasing process of
\mathbf{M}_{d}^{\{n\}}
, i.e.

\langle\mathbf{M}_{d}^{\{n\}}\rangle_{h_{n}}=\sum_{h=1}^{h_{n}}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{\Delta}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}\cdot\left(\mathbf{\Delta}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}\right)^{T}\,\bigg|\,\mathcal{F}_{h-1}^{\{n\}}\right].

(35)

The following lemma is an immediate consequence of
[
25
, Corollary 3.1]
:

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume that the following two conditions are satisfied,

1.

\frac{\langle\mathbf{M}_{d}^{\{n\}}\rangle_{h_{n}}}{h_{n}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\longrightarrow}}G_{d,\alpha,\lambda}

for some positive semi-definite
(d+1)\times(d+1)
matrix
G_{d,\alpha,\lambda}

2.

For every
\varepsilon>0
,

\frac{1}{h_{n}}\,\sum_{h=1}^{h_{n}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert\mathbf{\Delta}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}\right\rVert^{2}\cdot\mathds{1}_{\left\{\left\lVert\mathbf{\Delta}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}\right\rVert^{2}>h_{n}\,\varepsilon\right\}}\,\bigg|\,\mathcal{F}_{h-1}^{\{n\}}\right]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\longrightarrow}}0

Then, we have

\frac{\mathbf{M}_{d,h_{n}}^{\{n\}}}{\sqrt{h_{n}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{\longrightarrow}}N(\mathbf{0},G_{d,\alpha,\lambda})

(36)

We now show that, for the sequence
h_{n}=n
, conditions (a) and (b) of lemma
2.3
are satisfied with
G_{d,\alpha,\lambda}=\Gamma_{d,\alpha,\lambda}
where
\Gamma_{d,\alpha,\lambda}
is as in (
15
). To prove (a), we work component by component. It follows from (
34
) that

\displaystyle\left(\mathbf{\Delta}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}\cdot\left(\mathbf{\Delta}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}\right)^{T}\right)_{i,j}

\displaystyle\quad=a_{i-1,h}^{\{n\}}a_{j-1,h}^{\{n\}}\cdot\left(\xi_{i-1,h}^{\{n\}}-p_{i-1,h-1}^{\{n\}}+q_{i-1,h-1}^{\{n\}}\right)\left(\xi_{j-1,h}^{\{n\}}-p_{j-1,h-1}^{\{n\}}+q_{j-1,h-1}^{\{n\}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=a_{i-1,h}^{\{n\}}a_{j-1,h}^{\{n\}}\cdot\left[\xi_{i-1,h}^{\{n\}}\xi_{j-1,h}^{\{n\}}-\left(p_{i-1,h-1}^{\{n\}}-q_{i-1,h-1}^{\{n\}}\right)\xi_{j-1,h}^{\{n\}}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\left.-\left(p_{j-1,h-1}^{\{n\}}-q_{j-1,h-1}^{\{n\}}\right)\xi_{i-1,h}^{\{n\}}+\left(p_{i-1,h-1}^{\{n\}}-q_{i-1,h-1}^{\{n\}}\right)\left(p_{j-1,h-1}^{\{n\}}-q_{j-1,h-1}^{\{n\}}\right)\right]

so that

\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{\Delta}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}\cdot\left(\mathbf{\Delta}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}\right)^{T}\,\bigg|\,\mathcal{F}_{h-1}^{\{n\}}\right]\right)_{i,j}=a_{i-1,h}^{\{n\}}a_{j-1,h}^{\{n\}}\cdot\left[\left(P_{h-1}^{\{n\}}\right)_{i,j}-\left(R_{h-1}^{\{n\}}\right)_{i,j}\right]

where, for all
1\leq i\leq j
,

\left(P_{h-1}^{\{n\}}\right)_{i,j}=\mathbb{E}\left[\xi_{i-1,h}^{\{n\}}\xi_{j-1,h}^{\{n\}}\,\bigg|\,\mathcal{F}_{h-1}^{\{n\}}\right]=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }j\geq i+2\\
-q_{i-1,h-1}^{\{n\}}&\text{ if }j=i+1\text{ and }i\geq 2\\
p_{i-1,h-1}^{\{n\}}+q_{i-1,h-1}^{\{n\}}&\text{ if }j=i\text{ or }i=1,j=2\end{cases}

and

\left(R_{h-1}^{\{n\}}\right)_{i,j}=\left(p_{i-1,h-1}^{\{n\}}-q_{i-1,h-1}^{\{n\}}\right)\left(p_{j-1,h-1}^{\{n\}}-q_{j-1,h-1}^{\{n\}}\right).

This implies

\displaystyle\left(\langle\mathbf{M}_{d}^{\{n\}}\rangle_{n}\right)_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{h=1}^{n}a_{i-1,h}^{\{n\}}a_{j-1,h}^{\{n\}}\cdot\left[\left(P_{h-1}^{\{n\}}\right)_{i,j}-\left(R_{h-1}^{\{n\}}\right)_{i,j}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{h=1}^{n}a_{i-1,n\cdot\frac{h-1}{n}+1}^{\{n\}}a_{j-1,n\cdot\frac{h-1}{n}+1}^{\{n\}}\cdot\left[\left(P_{n\cdot\frac{h-1}{n}}^{\{n\}}\right)_{i,j}-\left(R_{n\cdot\frac{h-1}{n}}^{\{n\}}\right)_{i,j}\right]

\displaystyle=n\cdot\sum_{h=1}^{n}a_{i-1,n\cdot\frac{h-1}{n}+1}^{\{n\}}a_{j-1,n\cdot\frac{h-1}{n}+1}^{\{n\}}\cdot\left[\left(P_{n\cdot\frac{h-1}{n}}^{\{n\}}\right)_{i,j}-\left(R_{n\cdot\frac{h-1}{n}}^{\{n\}}\right)_{i,j}\right]\cdot\frac{1}{n}

so that
\left(\langle\mathbf{M}_{d}^{\{n\}}\rangle_{n}\right)_{i,j}/n
can be viewed as a Riemann sum for the function
F^{\{n\}}_{i,j}
on
[0,1]
,
where

F^{\{n\}}_{i,j}(x)=a_{i-1,\lfloor nx\rfloor+1}^{\{n\}}a_{j-1,\lfloor nx\rfloor+1}^{\{n\}}\cdot\left[\left(P_{\lfloor nx\rfloor}^{\{n\}}\right)_{i,j}-\left(R_{\lfloor nx\rfloor}^{\{n\}}\right)_{i,j}\right]

(37)

Making use of lemma
2.1
we are able to show that

F^{\{n\}}_{i,j}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\longrightarrow}}f_{i,j}(x)

uniformly for
x\in[0,1]
, where
f_{i,j}(x)
is a deterministic, explicit function only depending on
\alpha,\lambda,i
and
j
, and it is Riemann integrable on [0,1]. By definition and elementary properties of the Riemann integral,

\frac{\left(\langle\mathbf{M}_{d}^{\{n\}}\rangle_{n}\right)_{i,j}}{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\longrightarrow}}\int_{0}^{1}f_{i,j}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

and since we are able to prove

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}f_{i,j}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\left(\Gamma_{d,\alpha,\lambda}\right)_{i,j},

the proof is concluded. See appendix
A.8
for the proof of the convergence of
F_{i,j}^{\{n\}}
, the definition of
f_{i,j}
and the computation of the integral.

For what concerns condition (b), we can write

\displaystyle S_{n}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{n}\,\sum_{h=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert\mathbf{\Delta}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}\right\rVert^{2}\cdot\mathds{1}_{\left\{\left\lVert\mathbf{\Delta}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}\right\rVert^{2}>n\varepsilon\right\}}\,\bigg|\,\mathcal{F}_{h-1}^{\{n\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{h=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\left\lVert\mathbf{\Delta}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}\right\rVert^{4}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{\Delta}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}\right\rVert^{2}}\cdot\mathds{1}_{\left\{\left\lVert\mathbf{\Delta}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}\right\rVert^{2}>n\varepsilon\right\}}\,\bigg|\,\mathcal{F}_{h-1}^{\{n\}}\right]

and since

\frac{\mathds{1}_{\left\{\left\lVert\mathbf{\Delta}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}\right\rVert^{2}>n\varepsilon\right\}}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{\Delta}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}\right\rVert^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{n\varepsilon}

almost surely, then

\displaystyle S_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}\,\varepsilon}\,\sum_{h=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert\mathbf{\Delta}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}\right\rVert^{4}\,\bigg|\,\mathcal{F}_{h-1}^{\{n\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}\,\varepsilon}\,\sum_{h=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert\mathcal{A}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}\cdot\left[\mathbf{\Xi}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{\Xi}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{h-1}^{\{n\}}\right]\right]\right\rVert^{4}\,\bigg|\,\mathcal{F}_{h-1}^{\{n\}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathcal{C}}{n^{2}\,\varepsilon}\,\sum_{h=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert\mathcal{A}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}\right\rVert^{4}\cdot\left\lVert\mathbf{\Xi}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{\Xi}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{h-1}^{\{n\}}\right]\right\rVert^{4}\,\bigg|\,\mathcal{F}_{h-1}^{\{n\}}\right]

for some positive constant
\mathcal{C}
. Finally, observe that, since the
\xi_{r,h}^{\{n\}}
take values in
\{-1,0,1\}
,

\left\lVert\ \mathbf{\Xi}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{\Xi}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{h-1}^{\{n\}}\right]\right\rVert\leq 2

almost surely; furthermore, by definition of
\mathcal{A}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}
,

\left\lVert\mathcal{A}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}\right\rVert=\left\lVert\mathbf{a}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}\right\rVert.

Then, we can bound
S_{n}
by

\displaystyle S_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{16\,\mathcal{C}}{n^{2}\varepsilon}\,\sum_{h=1}^{n}\left\lVert\mathbf{a}_{d,h}^{\{n\}}\right\rVert^{4}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{16\,\mathcal{C}}{n\varepsilon}\cdot\max_{r\in\{0,...,d\},\,x\in[0,1]}\left(a_{r,\lfloor nx\rfloor}^{\{n\}}\right)^{4}

\displaystyle=:\mathfrak{S}_{n}

We know from (i) of lemma
A.1
that for every
r\in\{1,...,d\}

a_{r,\lfloor nx\rfloor+1}^{\{n\}}=\left(\frac{\lambda+x}{\lambda}\right)^{r-\alpha}+O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\leq\left(\frac{\lambda+1}{\lambda}\right)^{d-\alpha}+O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)

and for
r=0
,

a_{r,\lfloor nx\rfloor+1}^{\{n\}}=\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+x}\right)^{1+\alpha}+O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\leq 1+O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)

uniformly for
x\in[0,1]
. In conclusion

\mathfrak{S}_{n}=\frac{16\,\mathcal{C}}{n\varepsilon}\cdot\left(\frac{\lambda+1}{\lambda}\right)^{4(d-\alpha)}+O\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)=O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)

and this proves point (b).

Therefore, it follows from Lemma
2.3
that as
n\to+\infty
,

\frac{\mathbf{M}_{d,n}^{\{n\}}}{\sqrt{n}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{\longrightarrow}}N(\mathbf{0},\Gamma_{d,\alpha,\lambda})

(38)

By definition of
\mathbf{M}_{d,n}^{\{n\}}
(
32
),

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}_{d,n}^{\{n\}}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{A}_{d,n}^{\{n\}}\right)^{-1}\cdot\left[\mathbf{M}_{d,n}^{\{n\}}+\mathbf{A}_{d,n}^{\{n\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{A}_{d,n}^{\{n\}}\right)^{-1}\cdot\mathbf{M}_{d,n}^{\{n\}}+\mathfrak{M}_{d,n}^{\{n\}}+\left(\mathcal{A}_{d,n}^{\{n\}}\right)^{-1}\cdot\mathbf{A}_{d,n}^{\{n\}}-\mathfrak{M}_{d,n}^{\{n\}}

It follows from lemma
2.1
that

\left(\mathcal{A}_{d,n}^{\{n\}}\right)^{-1}\cdot\mathbf{A}_{d,n}^{\{n\}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{M}_{d,n}^{\{n\}},

and by (i) of lemma
A.1

\left(\mathcal{A}_{d,n}^{\{n\}}\right)^{-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{A}_{d,\alpha,\lambda}

where

\mathfrak{A}_{d,\alpha,\lambda}=\operatorname{Diag}\left(\left(\left[\frac{\lambda+1}{\lambda}\right]^{r-\alpha}\right)_{r\in\{0,...,d\}}\right).

In conclusion, apply Slutsky’s lemma to show that

\frac{\mathbf{K}_{d,n}^{\{n\}}-\mathfrak{M}_{d,m}^{\{n\}}}{\sqrt{n}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{\longrightarrow}}N(\mathbf{0},\mathfrak{A}_{d,\alpha,\lambda}^{T}\cdot\Gamma_{d,\alpha,\lambda}\cdot\mathfrak{A}_{d,\alpha,\lambda})

and since by definition (
14
)

\mathfrak{A}_{d,\alpha,\lambda}^{T}\cdot\Gamma_{d,\alpha,\lambda}\cdot\mathfrak{A}_{d,\alpha,\lambda}=\Sigma_{d,\alpha,\lambda}

the proof is concluded.

A.5
The Chinese Restaurant Process

The sequential construction of the Ewens–Pitman model was introduced in the seminal work of
[
32
, Proposition 9]
.
For
\alpha\in[0,1)
and
\theta>-\alpha
, it is possible to show that the following recursive construction yields an exchangeable random partition of the set
[n]
.

Conditionally on the number of blocks
K_{n}=k
and on the partition subsets (the “tables”, in the restaurant metaphor)
\{T_{1},\ldots,T_{k}\}
with corresponding sizes
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
, the partition of
[n+1]

is obtained by extending that of
[n]
in such a way that the element
n+1
is assigned
to an existing subset
T_{i}
, for
1\leq i\leq k
, with probability

\frac{n_{i}-\alpha}{n+\theta},

or initiates a new subset with probability

\frac{\alpha k+\theta}{n+\theta}.

Since
n=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}
, we clearly have

\frac{1}{n+\theta}\sum_{i=1}^{k}(n_{i}-\alpha)+\frac{\alpha k+\theta}{n+\theta}=\frac{n-\alpha k+\alpha k+\theta}{n+\theta}=1.

From now on, for
K_{n}\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, let

\mathbf{N}_{n}=(N_{1,n},\ldots,N_{K_{n},n})

denote the sizes of the partition subsets
\{T_{1},\ldots,T_{K_{n}}\}
. As shown in

[
32
, Proposition 9]
, the above construction yields the joint distribution
(
1
) of the random vector
(K_{n},\mathbf{N}_{n})
.

For
r=1,...,n
, we introduce the notation
K_{r,n}
to denote the number of tables with given numerosity
r
. Note that the above construction immediately yields a recursive structure not only for
K_{n}
, but also for the
K_{r,n}
. In fact, letting

\mathbf{K}_{n}=\left(K_{n},K_{1,n},...,K_{n,n}\right)

and

\mathcal{F}_{n}=\sigma\left(\mathbf{K}_{1},...,\mathbf{K}_{n}\right)

there hold

P(K_{n+1}=K_{n}+1\mid\mathcal{F}_{n})=\frac{\alpha K_{n}+\theta}{n+\theta}.

and for
r\geq 1
,

P\left[K_{r,n+1}=K_{r,n}+k\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{n}\right]=\begin{cases}p_{r,n}&\text{ if }k=1\\
q_{r,n}&\text{ if }k=-1\\
1-p_{r,n}-q_{r,n}&\text{ if }k=0\end{cases}

(A.39)

Search for spatial coincidences between galaxy mergers and Fermi-LAT 4FGL-DR4 sources
Siddhant Manna, Shantanu Desai

Abstract
We present a systematic search for spatial association between a high-confidence sample of 3,166 morphologically selected galaxy mergers detected through SDSS imaging and the 4FGL-DR4 catalog of gamma-ray sources detected by the Fermi Large Area Telescope (LAT). Using a conservative 4 σ \sigma positional uncertainty threshold and a Poisson-based statistical framework, we identify 21 statistically significant associations with match probabilities p < 0.05 p<0.05 . Among these are known classes of gamma-ray emitters such as flat-spectrum radio quasars (FSRQs), BL Lacertae objects (BLL), and radio galaxies bolstering the hypothesis that merger-driven processes can fuel high-energy activity. Intriguingly, five of the associated sources remain unclassified in 4FGL-DR4, hinting at a possible link between galaxy mergers and a hitherto unrecognized population of gamma-ray sources. The dominance of AGN-like matches supports the scenario in which mergers trigger accretion onto central supermassive black holes, initiating AGN activity observable at gamma-ray energies. Moreover, the recurrent presence of unassociated sources among secure matches underscores the potential of merger catalogs as physically motivated priors in gamma-ray source identification efforts. This work constitutes the first dedicated effort to explore associations between gamma-ray sources and a large, morphologically selected sample of galaxy mergers, opening new avenues for understanding the role of interactions in high-energy astrophysics. We additionally examined the sample of 70 galaxy pairs from the Canadian Network for Observational Cosmology Field Galaxy Redshift Survey and found no statistically significant matches, with p < 0.05 p<0.05 within the 4 σ \sigma positional uncertainty threshold.



I
Introduction

The Fermi Large Area Telescope (LAT) has been a cornerstone in the exploration of the gamma-ray universe at GeV energies since its launch in 2008
Atwood et al. 2009

1
1

1

https://fermi.gsfc.nasa.gov/science/instruments/lat.html
. It has provided an unprecedented view of the high-energy sky, enabling the detection and characterization of thousands of gamma-ray sources. The most recent data release, the Fourth Fermi-LAT Source Catalog Data Release 4 (4FGL-DR4) provides a comprehensive catalog of 7,195 gamma-ray sources observed over 14 years from 50 MeV to 1 TeV
Ballet et al. 2023
(see also the related 4FGL-DR3 catalog
Abdollahi et al. 2022
). These sources include a variety of astrophysical objects, such as blazars, radio galaxies, pulsars. However, a large number of Fermi-LAT sources (
\sim 2600
) remain unidentified and no counterparts at lower energies have been identified.
Numerous studies have therefore searched for spatial associations of Fermi 4FGL-DR4 sources with multi-wavelength source catalogs, including X-ray catalogs from SRG/eROSITA
Mayer and Becker 2024
and SWIFT
Ulgiati et al. 2025
, radio source catalogs
Bruzewski et al. 2023
, SPT point sources at millimeter wavelengths
Zhang et al. 2022
, magnetars
Ramakrishnan and Desai 2024
, IceCube neutrino events
Negro et al. 2023
, MeerKAT sources
Himes et al. 2025
etc.

In this work, we search for statistically significant association between Fermi 4FGL-DR4 catalog and galaxy mergers.
Galaxy mergers are pivotal events in the evolution of galaxies, driving significant morphological and dynamical changes that influence star formation, black hole growth, and active galactic nuclei (AGN) activity
Hopkins et al. 2006
;
Kaviraj 2025
. These processes can produce energetic phenomena at gamma-ray energies, which can be detected by LAT. Galaxy mergers are thought to trigger intense star formation and fuel supermassive black holes, potentially leading to relativistic jets or other mechanisms responsible for gamma-ray production. Understanding the potential connection between galaxy mergers and gamma-ray sources is crucial for unraveling the physical processes governing these extreme astrophysical environments. A systematic search for associations between a comprehensive, high-confidence catalog of galaxy mergers and the latest Fermi-LAT data release has not yet been done, to the best of our knowledge.

This work is heavily inspired by
Bouri et al. 2025
, which searched for a spatial association between IceCube-detected neutrinos and sources from six different galaxy merger catalogs, since neutrinos could be produced through shock acceleration from galaxy mergers, as discussed in various works
Lisenfeld and Völk 2010
;
Kashiyama and Mészáros 2014
;
Yuan et al. 2018
.
Along with the associated neutrinos, one would also expect high-energy gamma-ray radiation, which could be detected by Fermi-LAT. We briefly review some of the gamma-ray production mechanisms discussed in literature. The first estimate of gamma-ray flux from galaxy merger candidates was done in
Lisenfeld and Völk 2010
. This work considered two specific merger systems, viz. UGC 12914/5 and UGC 813/6
Lisenfeld and Völk 2010
. These candidates are located at distances of 60 Mpc and 69 Mpc, respectively. The hadronic gamma-ray flux from
\pi^{0}
decays resulting from energetic proton collisions with gas nuclei in the shocked region has been estimated in this fork to be
\mathcal{O}(10^{-15})~\rm{ph~cm^{-2}~s^{-1}}
, with a spectral index of 1.1 normalized at 1 TeV
Lisenfeld and Völk 2010
. Although this flux is too small to be detected by Fermi-LAT, for a galaxy merger happening at distances between 2.6 and 3.9 Mpc, this flux could be up to 700 times higher, thereby enhancing the detection probability of mergers in current generation gamma-ray telescopes such as Fermi-LAT. Then,
Kashiyama and Mészáros 2014
investigated the shock acceleration of particles in galaxy mergers with stellar masses of
\sim 10^{11}M_{\odot}
and showed that cosmic rays could be accelerated upto 0.1-1 EeV with a spectral index of 2.0. It was shown that for a cosmic ray acceleration efficiency of 10%, one could detect gamma-ray radiation from about 10 nearby merging galaxies with Cherenkov Telescope Array
Kashiyama and Mészáros 2014
. In addition to UGC 12914/5 and UGC 813/6, another galaxy merger candidate, viz. VV 114 located at a distance of
\sim 77
Mpc
Vorontsov-Velyaminov et al. 2001
was identified as a potential gamma ray source. Subsequently,
Yuan et al. 2018
extended this analysis and calculated the cumulative diffuse gamma-ray background by incorporating mergers of galaxy cluster and group candidates, and also incorporated the redshift evolution of average galactic radius, shock velocity and gas content inside haloes and galactic magnetic fields. This model is also consistent with the non-blazar contribution to extragalactic gamma-ray background
Yuan et al. 2018
.

The empirical study of galaxy mergers and correctly identifying galaxies that have been merging has traditionally been quite challenging (See
Kaviraj 2025
for a recent review).
Recent advances in catalogs from large-scale optical surveys, such as the Sloan Digital Sky Survey Data Release 16 (SDSS DR16)
Ahumada et al. 2020
, have however enabled the identification of large samples of galaxy mergers through morphological and spectroscopic signatures. Using machine learning techniques and high-confidence classification thresholds,
Ackermann et al. 2018
constructed a galaxy merger catalog. This catalog contains a high-confidence sample of 3,166 galaxy mergers derived from a catalog of 328,151 candidates in the redshift range
0.01\lesssim z\lesssim 1.3469

Ackermann et al. 2018
.
The second catalog we considered for our analysis is the
J/AJ/130/2043
catalog, from the Canadian Network for Observational Cosmology field galaxy redshift survey, contains 70 close galaxy pairs across four sky regions covering 1.5 square degrees, with redshifts in the range
0.1\lesssim z\lesssim 0.6

Patton et al. 2005
. We use both these aformentioned catalogs for spatial coincidence with Fermi-LAT point sources.
These catalogs were also used for spatial coincidence analysis with IceCube neutrinos in
Bouri et al. 2025
.
We note that other major merger catalogs
Hwang and Chang 2009
;
Gimeno et al. 2004
used in
Bouri et al. 2025
lack essential photometric or distance information required for flux-based probability estimates. Furthermore, several prominent merger systems discussed in the literature as promising gamma-ray sources, including UGC 12914/5, UGC 813/6, VV 114, NGC 660, and NGC 3256, are not present in the galaxy merger catalogs used in this study
Ackermann et al. 2018
. We also searched for spatial associations of these systems with sources in the 4FGL-DR4 catalog within a
1^{\prime}
radius but found no matches.
A targeted analysis of such individual systems will be conducted in a follow-up study.

This paper is organized as follows. Section
II
describes the data selection and cross-matching methodology, Section
III
presents the findings, and Section
IV
discusses the implications and future directions.

II
Data Analysis

We analyze the aformentioned galaxy merger catalog that contains 328,151 candidate mergers
Ackermann et al. 2018
. Each galaxy image in the catalog was processed by a convolutional neural network (CNN) classifier, which outputs a merger probability score
p_{m}\in[0,1]
, representing the likelihood that the system is undergoing a merger (with
p_{m}=0
indicating a non-interacting galaxy and
p_{m}=1
indicating a merger). The classifier was trained using 4-fold cross-validation, producing four independent estimates of
p_{m}
for each galaxy
Ackermann et al. 2018
. To isolate high-confidence systems, we applied a strict threshold of
p_{m}>0.95
across all four classifier outputs, retaining only those galaxies that satisfied this criterion in every fold. This conservative approach yielded a subset of 3,711 robust merger candidates. This stricter criterion minimizes the inclusion of ambiguous cases and emphasizes classifier agreement, albeit at the cost of reducing the final sample size.
These selected objects were then cross-matched with the SDSS DR16 catalog
Ahumada et al. 2020
using
TOPCAT
version 4.10-3
Taylor 2005
, employing a matching radius of 0.1 arcseconds. The resulting cross-match identified 3,185 mergers with SDSS counterparts. Among these matched sources, 1,147 have measured spectroscopic redshifts and 3,166 sources possess SDSS
r
-band apparent magnitudes.
To characterize the spatial extent of the merger sample, we estimated the total angular sky area over which the 3,166 galaxy mergers are distributed. This effective footprint was computed by dividing the sky into finer bins in right ascension and declination and summing up the area of all the spatial bins that contained at least one merger. The contribution of each bin was corrected for spherical projection by weighting with cos(
\delta
), where
\delta
is the central declination of the bin. This produced an estimated sky coverage of approximately
6190~\mathrm{deg}^{2}
, which was used for surface density estimates and association probability calculations. Figure
1
shows the sky distribution in equatorial coordinates using an Aitoff projection.

For the gamma-ray dataset, we utilized 4FGL-DR4 catalog, which contains 7,195 gamma-ray sources detected between 50 MeV and 1 TeV over 14 years of observations
Ballet et al. 2023
.

To identify possible associations, we searched for candidate galaxy mergers located within the 4
\sigma
positional uncertainty ellipse of each 4FGL-DR4 source. This conservative threshold, similar to that used in
Zhang et al. 2022
, accounts for both Fermi-LAT’s positional uncertainties and uncertainties in the external catalogs. When multiple optical sources were found within a single gamma-ray error ellipse, we assessed the probability of random association using a Poisson-based framework as described below
Browne and Cohen 1978
;
Downes et al. 1986
;
Biggs et al. 2011
;
Zhang et al. 2022
.

The expected number of chance associations
(\mu
) is given by
Browne and Cohen 1978
;
Downes et al. 1986
;
Biggs et al. 2011
;
Zhang et al. 2022
:

\mu=\pi ab\,n_{s}(>S),

(1)

where
a
and
b
are the semi-major and semi-minor axes of the Fermi positional ellipse, and
n_{s}(>S)
is the surface density of mergers with flux greater than that of the SDSS counterpart having flux equal to
S
. To evaluate
n_{s}(>S)
, we used the SDSS-derived fluxes computed from the
r
-band magnitudes of the merger candidates. For each candidate to be tested, we determined the number of mergers in the full sample with
r
-band fluxes exceeding the corresponding candidate flux value. The details of the calculation of
r
-band flux from the estimated magnitude can be found in Appendix
A
.
Dividing this count by the total sky area covered by the catalog yielded a flux-dependent surface density. This value of
n_{s}(>S)
was then used in Eq.
1
to calculate the expected number of chance associations (
\mu
), allowing us to assess the probability of random coincidence.

The probability of random coincidence (i.e., the
p
-value) is then given by:

p=1-e^{-\mu}.

(2)

A small value
p
indicates a low probability of random association, which implies a more reliable counterpart match. We adopt a threshold of
p<0.05
to define a “secure” association, with a secondary “relaxed” threshold of
p<0.1
also examined, following the criteria in
Zhang et al. 2022
. Given the rarity and low surface density of galaxy mergers, we expect the false match rate to remain low even with a relaxed threshold
Downes et al. 1986
;
Biggs et al. 2011
.

III
Results

We began our analysis by examining a supplementary sample of 70 galaxy pairs from the Canadian Network for Observational Cosmology Field Galaxy Redshift Survey, as defined in
Patton et al. 2005
. Applying the same matching criterion and statistical methodology, we found no statistically significant associations with Fermi 4FGL-DR4 sources within the 4
\sigma
positional uncertainty threshold
p<0.05
. Therefore, we do not find any Fermi 4GL-DR4 counterparts in the J/AJ/130/2043 merger catalog.

Next, we applied the matching criterion defined in Equation
2
to our primary sample of 3,166 morphologically selected galaxy mergers identified from SDSS imaging. This resulted in identification of 38 spatial coincidences between Fermi 4FGL-DR4 sources and galaxy mergers from our sample.
To assess the likelihood of true physical associations versus chance alignments, we computed Poisson-based match probabilities for each pair using Eq.
2
. Among these 38 matches, 21 exhibit
p
-values below 0.05, indicating a low probability of random association. We classify these as statistically secure matches.
According to source classifications in the 4FGL-DR4 catalog, the 21 matches within the 4
\sigma
ellipses include four Flat Spectrum Radio Quasars (FSRQs), six BL Lacertae objects (BLL), three Blazar Candidates of Uncertain type (BCUs), and three Radio Galaxies (RDGs). The remaining five are unassociated sources (UIDs) lacking confirmed multiwavelength counterparts. Notably, none of the secure matches is classified as a pulsar. The five UIDs span gamma-ray flux densities in the range
(0.55\text{--}1.90)\times 10^{-35}\,\mathrm{erg\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}\,Hz^{-1}}
, suggesting the possible existence of a previously unrecognized population of gamma-ray sources associated with galaxy mergers.

Overall, our analysis reveals that approximately 0.67% (21 out of 3,166) of galaxy mergers exhibit statistically robust associations with gamma-ray sources. The predominance of blazar-type objects among these secure matches accounting for 62% is consistent with their known dominance in the extragalactic gamma-ray sky. However, the presence of radio galaxies and a significant fraction of unassociated sources (24%) suggests that merger-driven gamma-ray activity may originate from a broader range of physical mechanisms than previously recognized. (BCU),

IV
Conclusions

We have presented the first dedicated cross-matching analysis between two galaxy merger catalogs and the Fermi-LAT 4FGL-DR4 gamma-ray source catalog
Ballet et al. 2023
. The first catalog consists of a sample of 70 galaxy pairs from the Canadian Network for Observational Cosmology Field Galaxy Redshift Survey and found no statistically significant associations within the 4
\sigma
positional uncertainty threshold (
p<0.05
).
The second catalog comprises of a high-confidence sample of 3,166 morphologically selected galaxy mergers
Ackermann et al. 2018
and the Fermi-LAT 4FGL-DR4 gamma-ray source catalog
Ballet et al. 2023
. Using a conservative 4
\sigma
positional uncertainty criterion and a Poisson-based statistical framework, we identified 38 spatial coincidences, of which 21 exhibit
p
-values below 0.05. These matches predominantly include well-known gamma-ray source classes, FSRQs, BL Lacs, and radio galaxies, supporting the hypothesis that merger-driven processes can fuel AGN activity and high-energy emission. Notably, five secure associations are with unclassified (unassociated) Fermi sources from the fourth FGL-DR4 catalog, suggesting that merging systems may host previously unrecognized gamma-ray populations.

While blazar-like AGN dominate the matched sample, the presence of radio galaxies, UIDs and the broad range of observed gamma-ray flux densities point to a diversity of emission mechanisms. These may include merger-induced starbursts, large-scale shocks, or obscured AGN activity, highlighting the need for deeper multi-wavelength follow-up
Torres 2004
;
Ackermann et al. 2012
.
The consistent appearance of unassociated sources within the secure sample underscores the value of galaxy merger catalogs as informative priors for gamma-ray source identification. As gamma-ray localization continues to improve and future optical surveys such as Vera Rubin LSST, Euclid, and DESI expand the census of galaxy mergers, the ability to resolve such associations will significantly increase. We shall also conduct a targeted search for some specific merger candidates, such as UGC 12914/5, UGC 813/6, and VV 114 in a forthcoming work.

Appendix A
SDSS Flux Conversion Methodology

SDSS magnitudes are reported in the
asinh
magnitude system
Lupton et al. 1999
, which handle low S/N and negative fluxes more robustly than traditional Pogson magnitudes. The inverse asinh formula for converting
r
-band apparent magnitudes (
m_{r}
) to fluxes is:

\frac{f_{r}}{f_{0}}=2b\,\sinh\left(\frac{-2.5\,m_{r}}{\ln(10)}-\ln(b)\right),

(3)

where
f_{0}
is the reference flux (1 maggy by definition), and
b=1.2\times 10^{-10}
is the softening parameter for the
r
-band (see Table 21 in
Stoughton et al. 2002
).

For completeness, the forward
asinh
magnitude definition is:

m=-\frac{2.5}{\ln(10)}\left[\text{asinh}\left(\frac{f/f_{0}}{2b}\right)+\ln(b)\right].

(4)

The resulting fluxes, in units of nanomaggies, were converted to Janskys using the SDSS relation:

1\,\mathrm{nMgy}=3.631\times 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{Jy},

and subsequently to cgs units via:

1\,\mathrm{Jy}=10^{-23}\,\mathrm{erg\,s^{-1}\,cm^{-2}\,Hz^{-1}}.

Early Outcomes of Zone 2 Thoracic Endovascular Aortic Repair with an Integrated Branch Stent Graft for the Left Subclavian Artery: Evidence from a Western Centre
Authors: Antonio Marzano, Claudia Bittoni, Francesca Miceli, Marta Ascione, Luca di Marzo, Wassim Mansour
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThoracic endovascular aortic repair (TEVAR) is widely used for thoracic aortic diseases, most commonly with a proximal landing in Ishimaru zone 3. When an adequate proximal sealing zone is lacking, extension into zone 2 is required, often necessitating left subclavian artery (LSA) revascularisation. The Castor single branched stent graft provides a fully endovascular solution for zone 2 TEVAR by incorporating an antegrade branch for the LSA. This study reports a single centre Western experience with this device.MethodA retrospective analysis was conducted on 15 consecutive patients undergoing elective zone 2 TEVAR with the Castor single branched stent graft between July 2023 and December 2025. Indications included thoracic aortic aneurysm, subacute or chronic Stanford type B dissection, and penetrating aortic ulcer. The primary endpoint was technical success, defined as accurate deployment without type I or III endoleak and with preserved LSA patency. Secondary endpoints included clinical success, access related complications, intensive care unit (ICU) and hospital stays, re-intervention, and death. Follow up consisted of serial computed tomography angiography.ResultsThe mean age was 63.9 ± 11 years, and 87% of patients were men. Technical and clinical success were achieved in 14 patients (93%). One patient required adjunctive covered stenting of the LSA for incomplete branch expansion. There were no access related complications or 30 day deaths. Mean ICU and hospital stays were 1.2 ± 0.6 days and 15.3 ± 9.6 days, respectively. Over a mean follow up of 13.5 ± 8.4 months, all LSA branches remained patent, with no endoleak, device migration, or re-intervention.ConclusionZone 2 TEVAR using the Castor single branched stent graft appears feasible, safe, and effective in a Western population. In this small elective series, LSA branch patency was maintained and no device related re-intervention occurred during available follow up. Larger studies with longer follow up are needed to assess durability and longer term outcomes.

INTRODUCTION

Thoracic endovascular aortic repair (TEVAR) has become the treatment of choice for many thoracic aortic pathologies involving Ishimaru zone 3.1, 2, 3 Nevertheless, a frequent anatomic challenge is that the segment in zone 3 does not provide an adequate healthy proximal landing zone, thus necessitating extension of the seal into zone 2 of the aortic arch. To enable a safe and durable fixation in this setting, operators commonly resort to adjunctive strategies such as surgical bypass (or transposition) of the left subclavian artery (LSA) (which adds operating time, prolongs hospital stay, and increases surgical burden), intentional coverage of the LSA (especially in emergency settings) with attendant risks of cerebrovascular or spinal cord ischaemia, and off label endovascular approaches such as chimney grafts or in situ fenestration.4, 5, 6 Alternative strategies require waiting for fully customised patient specific endografts, which are often unsuitable for urgent or emergency use.

The introduction of a semi-custom, or readily available, branched stent graft with a dedicated branch for the LSA offers a promising solution. The Castor single branched stent graft (MicroPort Medical, Shanghai, China) features a unibody main body plus LSA branch, allowing proximal landing in zone 2 while preserving LSA perfusion. Early experience in Asian cohorts has shown high technical success (96–97%) and excellent branch patency (≈96%) with low branch occlusion and favourable aortic remodelling data.7, 8, 9 Despite these encouraging data, there is still limited published experience in Western populations, where anatomic differences, comorbidity profiles, and procedural logistics may vary.10, 11, 12

This paper presents initial experience with the Castor single branched stent graft for TEVAR requiring proximal landing in zone 2 to treat aortic pathologies involving zone 3. It describes patient selection, procedure approach, and early outcomes to evaluate the device’s feasibility, safety, and technical performance in a Western population, and to contribute to the growing evidence supporting its use beyond Asian cohorts.

STUDY DESIGN

A single centre, retrospective study was conducted including 15 consecutive patients who underwent TEVAR with implantation of the Castor single branched stent graft at the Vascular Surgery Unit of Policlinico Umberto I, Rome, between July 2023 and December 2025.

METHODS

The study was conducted in a high volume tertiary university vascular centre and regional referral hub for complex aortic disease. The unit includes eight vascular surgeons, including four senior surgeons, with dedicated expertise in open and endovascular aortic procedures. More than 40 thoracic aortic cases are treated annually, including thoracic aortic aneurysms (TAA), Stanford type B aortic dissections (TBAD), blunt thoracic aortic injuries, penetrating aortic ulcers (PAU), and intramural haematomas. Eligible patients were adults (aged ≥ 18 years) who were electively treated for thoracic aortic disease requiring proximal landing in zone 2 and involving zone 3 of the aortic arch. Indications included TBAD, TAA, intramural haematomas, or PAU located ≤1 cm distal to the LSA origin.

Eligibility and selection framework

Inclusion criteria comprised: elective procedures; distance ≥5 mm between the distal edge of the left common carotid artery and the proximal edge of the LSA; ≥25 mm between the LSA origin and the vertebral artery; LSA diameter 5–13 mm; zone 2 diameter 22–40 mm; sealing within a previous endograft or native aorta with diameter ≥18 mm.

Exclusion criteria included emergency or urgent repair, ongoing infection, known nickel allergy, and connective tissue disorders (e.g., Marfan syndrome).

Acute TBAD cases were not represented in this series because the study was intentionally restricted to elective procedures. In the institutional pathway, acute uncomplicated TBAD is managed with optimal medical therapy in a monitored setting and close clinical and imaging surveillance, in line with current guideline recommendations.1,2 Acute complicated TBAD follows a separate urgent pathway, and when intervention is required, treatment is individualised with TEVAR based strategies, including TEVAR alone, provisional extension to induce complete attachment (PETTICOAT), or stent assisted balloon induced intimal disruption and relamination in aortic dissection repair (STABILISE), according to malperfusion status, true lumen collapse, distal extension, and overall aortic anatomy.

During the study period, all elective zone 2 TEVAR procedures performed at this institution were reviewed to contextualise device selection. The Castor single branched stent graft was considered the preferred endovascular option when pre-operative computed tomography angiography (CTA) demonstrated fulfilment of the pre-specified anatomic criteria for implantation, particularly a ≥5 mm distance between the distal edge of the left common carotid artery and the proximal edge of the LSA, and an LSA diameter between 5–13 mm. Alternative zone 2 strategies were adopted when these anatomic criteria were not met. No anatomically eligible patient was treated with an alternative strategy because of operator preference or device non-availability.

During the same study period, urgent or emergency zone 2 TEVAR procedures were not eligible for inclusion in the present cohort by study design. In these cases, treatment strategy was driven by clinical urgency and anatomy rather than by planned Castor eligibility assessment.

Data collection

Clinical, anatomic, technical, and follow up data were retrieved from a prospectively maintained institutional database and analysed retrospectively. Recorded variables included demographics, comorbidities, anatomic characteristics, intra-operative details, procedure related complications, need for re-intervention, intensive care unit (ICU) and in hospital stays, as well as aortic related and all cause death.

Pre-operative planning

All patients underwent detailed pre-operative CTA with multiplanar reconstruction and centreline analysis using OsiriX software (Pixmeo, Geneva, Switzerland). Device sizing was based on approximately 10% oversizing of the aortic diameters, the LSA branch was oversized by 10% relative to the native arterial diameter; for TBAD, proximal oversizing was limited to 5% to reduce the risk of retrograde type A dissection.

The Castor endograft is available in various configurations, with proximal diameters ranging 26–44 mm, distal diameters 20–44 mm, branch diameters 6–14 mm, main graft length 100–210 mm, and branch length 25 or 30 mm. The distance from the proximal edge of the main body to the branch origin varies between 5 and 30 mm. Device selection was tailored to maximise proximal and distal sealing while maintaining appropriate spatial relationships between the left common carotid, subclavian, and vertebral arteries.

Procedure

All procedures were performed by a dedicated aortic team including senior vascular surgeons with established experience in standard TEVAR and complex aortic endovascular procedures. All interventions were performed under general anaesthesia. Bilateral femoral access was obtained, and the left brachial artery was exposed surgically to ensure safe manipulation and minimise the risk of vascular injury in small calibre vessels.13 Intravascular ultrasound guidance was used in cases of aortic dissection.

After establishment of a brachiofemoral through and through wire, the Castor device was advanced from the femoral access under fluoroscopic guidance and oriented toward the LSA using the radiopaque markers. Deployment occurred under controlled pharmacological hypotension and bradycardia to optimise precision. When distal disease extension was present (e.g., in complicated dissections or thoraco-abdominal aneurysms), distal sealing was achieved within another endograft module.

Endpoints and follow up

The primary endpoint was technical success, defined as correct device deployment without type Ia, Ib, Ic, or III endoleak related to the Castor system, maintained patency of the LSA branch, and absence of unplanned adjunctive procedures on completion angiography.

The secondary endpoint was clinical success, defined as the absence of major adverse events within 30 days, including myocardial infarction, acute kidney injury, respiratory failure requiring mechanical or non-invasive ventilation, mesenteric ischaemia, stroke, or spinal cord ischaemia. Access site complications, ICU and hospital stays, and 30 day death were also recorded.

Post-operative surveillance included CTA at one and six months and annually thereafter to assess endoleak presence, LSA patency, and graft migration. Castor related death (death attributable to the aortic pathology treated with the Castor endograft or to procedure related complications) and re-interventions (secondary procedures performed to correct device or aorta associated complications within the treated segment) were also documented.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were performed using Microsoft Excel (Microsoft Corp., Redmond, WA, USA). Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation and range, and categorical variables as counts and percentages.

Ethical statement

This retrospective study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Ethical approval was waived by the Institutional Review Board of Policlinico Umberto I, as all data were anonymised and derived from routine clinical practice. Written informed consent for the procedure and use of anonymised data for research purposes was obtained from all patients.

During the study period, 30 zone 2 TEVAR procedures were performed at the institution. Of these, 27 were elective and three were urgent or emergency. Among the 27 elective procedures, 15 patients (56%) underwent treatment with the Castor single branched stent graft and constituted the present study cohort. In the remaining 12 patients, alternative strategies were selected because the anatomy did not meet the pre-specified criteria for Castor implantation. Specifically, nine patients underwent zone 2 TEVAR with carotid subclavian bypass because the distance between the distal edge of the left common carotid artery and the proximal edge of the LSA was <5 mm, while three patients were treated with the Gore TAG Thoracic Branch Endoprosthesis (TBE) (W.L. Gore and Associates, Flagstaff, AZ, USA), because the native LSA diameter was >13 mm. The three urgent or emergency zone 2 TEVAR cases were not included in the study cohort because urgent repair was an exclusion criterion; all were managed with intentional LSA coverage, and secondary carotid subclavian bypass was required in one patient because of symptoms related to subclavian coverage. No additional patients were excluded because of LSA to vertebral distance <25 mm, zone 2 diameter outside the device range, ongoing infection, known nickel allergy, or connective tissue disorder.

Procedural details

Technical success was achieved in 14 patients (93%). In one case, a covered stent was required within the LSA due to incomplete opening of the Castor branch, which resulted in a type Ic endoleak.

Clinical success was achieved in 14 patients (93%); one patient developed acute kidney injury not requiring dialysis, with complete recovery of baseline creatinine within five days post-procedure. No access related complications occurred at the femoral or brachial sites.

The mean ICU stay was 1.2 ± 0.6 days (range 1–3 days), and the mean overall hospital stay was 15.3 ± 9.6 days (range 4–31 days). There was no 30 day death. The mean post-operative hospital stay was 4.9 ± 1.8 days (range 2–9).

The mean follow up was 13.5 ± 8.4 months (range 2–31 months). No cases of endoleak, bird beak, or device migration were detected on surveillance CTA. The Castor LSA branch remained patent in all patients. At the latest follow up, 100% of patients were alive and free from Castor related re-intervention or death.

DISCUSSION

Designed for zone 2 interventions, the Castor single branched stent graft enables anatomic preservation of the LSA through a fully endovascular approach, thereby avoiding the need for hybrid or surgical revascularisation. Establishing an adequate proximal sealing zone is essential in these anatomies, as insufficient fixation may result in procedural failure or late complications. In this setting, the Castor device provides a reliable alternative to surgical debranching or chimney techniques, offering both zone2 sealing and LSA patency. Its integrated branch also contributes to improved proximal fixation, potentially reducing the risk of distal migration.14

The most technically demanding step during implantation was achieving the correct orientation and deployment of the device. Rotational adjustments within the introducer system can be challenging; if the radiopaque markers appear misaligned or the coaxial wire becomes entangled around the main body upon reaching the aortic arch, the graft must be withdrawn into the introducer for repositioning. Repeating this manoeuvre can significantly increase procedure time and radiation exposure. In this series, the only case requiring a covered stent in the LSA was probably due to suboptimal marker alignment and branch orientation, highlighting the critical importance of precise positioning.

Many of the handling limitations of the original Castor system have been addressed with the Cratos (MicroPort Medical, Shanghai, China) iteration. Importantly, Cratos preserves the same fundamental unibody integrated branch stent graft architecture as Castor; the upgrade primarily concerns the delivery and release platform, with improved control of orientation, proximal apposition, and bird beak management.15, 16, 17, 18 Accordingly, the present Castor series remains clinically relevant for evaluating the feasibility, safety, branch patency, and early branch performance of this integrated branch concept in Western patients. However, potential technical advantages specifically related to deployment control and manoeuvrability should be assessed in dedicated Cratos specific series.

From a clinical perspective, the Castor stent graft enabled complete arch repair without the need for extra-anatomic bypass, which is especially beneficial in frail or high risk patients. As previously emphasised in the literature, frailty plays a key role in the management of complex aortic procedures, making single session, minimally invasive strategies particularly valuable in elderly or comorbid patients.19 From current experience, the fully endovascular approach translated into short ICU stays (mean 1.2 days), reflecting the reduced physiological impact of the procedure. Although the overall hospital stay was longer (mean 15.3 days), this largely reflected the inclusion of patients with subacute TBAD who underwent initial monitored medical stabilisation before intervention. By contrast, the mean post-operative hospital stay was 4.9 ± 1.8 days, supporting the reduced peri-operative burden of a totally endovascular strategy without surgical supra-aortic revascularisation. This interpretation is also consistent with contemporary comparative data suggesting that fully endovascular zone 2 strategies may reduce length of stay compared with TEVAR combined with carotid subclavian bypass.15,20

Promising results have also been reported with the Gore TBE device.20, 21, 22, 23, 24 Several potential advantages of TBE over Castor should be acknowledged. Firstly, TBE provides broader branch diameter flexibility and can accommodate larger native LSAs, which is particularly relevant in degenerative arch pathology and in Western anatomy; notably, in current institutional experience, the three patients treated with TBE during the study period were selected because the native LSA diameter exceeded the Castor range (> 13 mm). Secondly, TBE can also accommodate smaller proximal aortic diameters than Castor, potentially expanding applicability in small native thoracic aortas, including selected traumatic lesions. Thirdly, TBE is a true off the shelf platform with Food and Drug Administration approval and European Conformity marking, whereas Castor availability outside selected markets remains more limited. Finally, TBE benefits from a strong Western evidence base in distal arch aneurysm and mixed arch pathology.15

However, preference for the Castor single branched stent graft over the Gore TBE was guided by both technical and anatomic considerations, as well as operator familiarity. Firstly, the team has extensive previous experience with the Castor system, which may have facilitated procedural efficiency. More importantly, the authors believe that its design may be better suited to the specific anatomic demands of subacute complicated TBAD.

The Gore TBE features a retrograde branch configuration that may be considered less anatomic and may not contribute to additional fixation or sealing at the branch level. Furthermore, it requires placement of a separate bridging stent into the LSA, which may increase procedural complexity and the potential for endoleak or misalignment, issues well recognised with bridging stents in thoraco-abdominal repairs or multicomponent endovascular aortic repair systems.25, 26, 27, 28, 29 In contrast, the Castor device integrates an antegrade branch that allows direct anatomic engagement of the LSA, while offering greater configurational flexibility, particularly in the distance between the proximal edge of the graft and the branch origin. This distance can be as short as five mm in the Castor system, compared with a minimum of 20 mm in the Gore TBE, which may be particularly advantageous in patients with short landing zones between the left common carotid and subclavian arteries.10

Overall, initial experience, aligned with the three previously reported European series,12,30,31 supports the feasibility, safety, and technical effectiveness of the Castor endograft for zone 2 TEVAR in Western patients. While the results are consistent with those reported in large Asian cohorts, further studies are needed to confirm long term durability and to directly compare outcomes between the Castor and other branched devices such as the Gore TBE. Comparative multicentre data, particularly in anatomically complex or emergency settings, will be essential to define device selection criteria and optimise treatment algorithms for aortic arch pathologies.

The study had several limitations, including its retrospective design, small sample size, and single centre nature. Follow up duration was limited, and longer term outcomes cannot yet be assessed. No formal time to event analysis was performed, given the small cohort size, limited follow up duration, and absence of branch occlusion or device related re-intervention events.

This early institutional experience with the Castor single branched stent graft suggests that the device is a feasible, safe, and technically effective option for elective zone 2 TEVAR in Western patients. Despite the small sample size and limited follow up, technical and clinical success were high, all LSA branches remained patent, and no device related re-intervention, migration, or death occurred during available follow up. These early findings are broadly consistent with those reported in larger Asian cohorts and with other preliminary European experiences.

Nevertheless, larger multicentre and international studies are warranted to validate these encouraging results, define long term durability, and refine patient selection criteria. Continuous clinical and imaging surveillance will also be essential to assess aortic remodelling, late endoleak occurrence, and overall device performance over extended follow up periods.

Finally, further investigation is needed to evaluate the actual advantages of the next generation Cratos system, which maintains the Castor stent graft architecture but introduces a redesigned delivery system that may enhance accuracy, manoeuvrability, and procedural efficiency.

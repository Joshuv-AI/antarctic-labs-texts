Toeplitz Operators on Fock Space F α ∞ F_{\alpha}^{\infty}
Sui Huang

Abstract
. In this paper, we study necessary and sufficient conditions for a positive Borel measure μ \mu on the complex space ℂ \mathbb{C} to be a ( ∞ , q ) (\infty,q) or ( p , ∞ ) (p,\infty) (vanishing) Fock-Carleson measure through its Berezin transform. Then we discuss boundedness and compactness of the Toeplitz operator T μ T_{\mu} with a positive Borel measure μ \mu as symbol on Fock space F α ∞ F_{\alpha}^{\infty} . Furthermore, we charaterize these properties of the Toeplitz operator T φ T_{\varphi} with a symbol φ \varphi which is in B ​ M ​ O BMO .

Fock spaces are also called Segal-Bargmann spaces. The study of Toeplitz operators on the classical Fock spaces started in the 1980s
and has been fruitful so far, see [1-5].
The properties of Toeplitz operators on the generalized Fock spaces and Fock-Sobolev spaces have been the subjects of comprehensive research over many years, see [6-11].
The Berezin transform of measures, functions, and operators is one of important tools
in the studies of properties of the Toeplitz operators.
Isralowitz and Zhu introduced the Fock-Carleson measures on
\mathbb{C}
in [12].
With the Berezin transform of measures, they discussed the properties of the Fock-Carleson measure,
and studied boundedness, compactness and Schatten classes of the Toeplitz operator
T_{\mu}
on
F_{\alpha}^{2}
.
Hu and Lv defined
(p,q)
Fock-Carleson measures in [13], characterizing Berezin transform of those measures,
boundedness and compactness of
T_{\mu}
from
F_{\alpha}^{p}
to
F_{\alpha}^{q}
.
Furthermore, they extended their results to the generalized Fock spaces in [8].
Mengestle discussed boundedness and compactness of
T_{\mu}
between
F_{\alpha}^{p}
and
F_{\alpha}^{1}
,
F_{\alpha}^{\infty}
in [14] on the basis of the works of Hu and Lv.

Based on the studies of Hu and Lv,
our work in this paper is to characterize necessary and sufficient conditions for a positive Borel measures

to be a

,

(vanishing) Fock-Carleson measure
by its

\lim_{n}\int_{\mathbb{C}}|f_{n}(z)e^{-\frac{\alpha|z|^{2}}{2}}|^{q}\text{d}\mu(z)=0,

we can get that
\mu
is a
(\infty,q)
vanishing Fock-Carleson measure.
∎

∎

3.
(p,\infty)
Fock-Carleson Measure

In this section we will discuss necessary and sufficient conditions for a positive Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{C}
to be
a
(p,\infty)
(vanishing) Fock-Carleson measure.

\mu
needs to satisfy the little stronger condition that
\mu(\mathbb{C})
is finite.
We prove the following theorem firstly.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure, then
\lim\limits_{p\to+\infty}||f||_{p,\mu}=||f||_{\infty,\mu}

for any
f\in\displaystyle{\bigcup_{p\geqslant 1}}L_{\alpha}^{p}(\text{d}\mu)
.

Proof.

Let
f\in L_{\alpha}^{\infty}(\text{d}\mu)
,
then
|f(z)e^{-\frac{\alpha|z|^{2}}{2}}|\leqslant||f|||_{\infty,\mu}
.
It can be obtained

\displaystyle||f||_{p,\mu}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{C}}|f(z)e^{-\frac{\alpha|z|^{2}}{2}}|^{p}\text{d}\mu(z)

(3.1)

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{C}}||f||_{\infty,\mu}^{p}\text{d}\mu(z)

(3.2)

\displaystyle\leqslant||f||_{\infty,\mu}^{p}\mu(\mathbb{C}),

thus

\varliminf_{p\to+\infty}||f||_{p,\mu}\leqslant||f||_{\infty,\mu}.

On the other hand, we denote
E_{\varepsilon}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}||f(z)|\geqslant||f||_{\infty,\mu}-\varepsilon\}
for any
\varepsilon>0
,
then
\mu(E_{\varepsilon})>0
. If
\mu(E_{\varepsilon})=0
,
then
|f(z)|\leqslant||f||_{\infty,\mu}-\varepsilon
for any
z\in E-E_{\varepsilon}
.
This contradicts the definition of
||f||_{\infty,\mu}
. At this moment

\displaystyle||f||_{p,\mu}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{C}}|f(z)e^{-\frac{\alpha|z|^{2}}{2}}|^{p}\text{d}\mu

(3.3)

\displaystyle\geqslant\int_{E_{\varepsilon}}|f(z)e^{-\frac{\alpha|z|^{2}}{2}}|^{p}\text{d}\mu

(3.4)

\displaystyle\geqslant(||f||_{\infty}-\varepsilon)^{p}\mu(E_{\varepsilon}),

so

(||f||_{\infty}-\varepsilon)(\mu(E_{\varepsilon}))^{\frac{1}{p}}\leqslant||f||_{p,\mu},

then

||f||_{\infty}\leqslant\varliminf_{p\to+\infty}||f||_{p,\mu}.

This means
||f||_{\infty,\mu}=\lim\limits_{p\to+\infty}||f||_{p,\mu}.

∎

∎

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on
\mathbb{C}
.

\mu
is a
(p,\infty)
Fock-Carleson measure if and only if
\mu
is a
(p,q)
Fock-Carleson measure.

Proof.

Let
\mu
be a
(p,\infty)
Fock-Carleson measure firstly. For any
f\in F_{\alpha}^{p}
, there is a constant
C>0
such that

||f||_{\infty,\mu}=\text{esssup}_{z\in\mathbb{C}}|f(z)e^{-\frac{\alpha|z|^{2}}{2}}|\leqslant C||f||_{p,\alpha}.

Because

\lim\limits_{q\to+\infty}||f||_{q,\mu}=||f||_{\infty,\mu},

we can get

\int_{\mathbb{C}}|f(z)e^{-\frac{\alpha|z|^{2}}{2}}|^{q}\text{d}\mu\leqslant||f||_{p,\alpha}^{q}.

So
\mu
is
(p,q)
Fock-Carleson measure.

We now prove the necessity. Let
\mu
be a
(p,q)
Fock-Carleson measure, then
there exists constant
C>0
for any
f\in F_{\alpha}^{p}
such that

||f||_{q,\mu}\leqslant C||f||_{p,\alpha}.

Because

\lim\limits_{q\to+\infty}||f||_{q,\mu}=||f||_{\infty,\mu},

so

||f||_{\infty,\mu}\leqslant C||f||_{p,\alpha},

we can obtain that
\mu
is a
(p,\infty)
Fock-Carleson measure.
∎

∎

Theorem 3.2

Let

be a positive finite measure Borel measure on

\mu

(p,\infty)
vanishing Fock-Carleson measure

\mu

vanishing Fock-Carleson measure for any

Proof.

4.

In this section, we will focus on boundedness and compactness of the Toeplitz operator
T_{\mu}
on
F_{\alpha}^{\infty}
with the symbols
\mu
which satisfying the condition (M).
Let
\mu
be a positive finite Borel measure on
\mathbb{C}
, and define Toeplitz operator
T_{\mu}
with
\mu
on
F_{\alpha}^{\infty}
as following

T_{\mu}f(z)=\int_{\mathbb{C}}f(\omega)\overline{K_{z}(\omega)}e^{-\alpha|\omega|^{2}}\text{d}\mu(\omega),

where

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mu
be a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{C}
.

T_{\mu}
is bounded on

if and only if
\tilde{\mu}_{1}(t)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{C},\text{d}A)

Proof.

For any

,

\displaystyle|T_{\mu}f(z)e^{-\frac{\alpha|z|^{2}}{2}}|

\displaystyle=|\int_{\mathbb{C}}f(\omega)\overline{K_{z}(\omega)}e^{-\alpha|\omega|^{2}}e^{-\frac{\alpha|z|^{2}}{2}}\text{d}\mu(\omega)|

(4.1)

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{C}}|f(\omega)e^{-\frac{\alpha|\omega|^{2}}{2}}||k_{z}(\omega)e^{-\frac{\alpha|\omega|^{2}}{2}}|\text{d}\mu(\omega)

(4.2)

\displaystyle\leqslant||f||_{\infty,\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{C}}|k_{z}(\omega)e^{-\frac{\alpha|\omega|^{2}}{2}}|\text{d}\mu(\omega)

(4.3)

\displaystyle=||f||_{\infty,\alpha}\tilde{\mu}_{1}(z).

If
\tilde{\mu}_{1}(z)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{C},\text{d}A)
, then
T_{\mu}f\in F_{\alpha}^{\infty}
.

On the contrary, let
T_{\mu}
be bounded on
F_{\alpha}^{\infty}
, thus

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}_{2}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{C}}|k_{z}(\omega)e^{-\frac{\alpha|\omega|^{2}}{2}}|^{2}\text{d}\mu(\omega)

(4.4)

\displaystyle=|<T_{\mu}k_{z},k_{z}>|

(4.5)

\displaystyle\leqslant||T_{\mu}k_{z}||_{\infty,\alpha}||k_{z}||_{1,\alpha}

(4.6)

\displaystyle\leqslant||T_{\mu}||,

then
\tilde{\mu}_{2}(z)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{C},\text{d}A)
.
By Lemma 1.3, we have
\tilde{\mu}_{1}(z)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{C},\text{d}A)
.
∎

∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\mu
be a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{C}

,

On the contrary, let

|\omega|\leqslant R

\infty

And

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}_{2}(z)=|<T_{\mu}k_{z},k_{z}>|

\displaystyle\leqslant||T_{\mu}k_{z}||_{\infty,\alpha}||k_{z}||_{1,\alpha}

(4.7)

\displaystyle\leqslant||T_{\mu}k_{z}||_{\infty,\alpha}.

Since

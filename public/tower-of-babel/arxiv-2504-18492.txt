Rashad Hamidi, Ben Hoare

Abstract




Rashad Hamidi and Ben Hoare

Department of Mathematical Sciences

Durham University

Durham DH1 3LE, UK

Abstract

We introduce the
\mathds{Z}_{N}
-twisted trigonometric sigma models, a new class of integrable deformations of the principal chiral model.
Starting from 4d Chern-Simons theory on a cylinder, the models are constructed by introducing a
\mathds{Z}_{N}
branch cut running along the non-compact direction.
As we pass through the branch cut we apply a
\mathds{Z}_{N}
automorphism to the algebra-valued and group-valued fields of the theory.
Two instances of models in this class have appeared in the literature and we explain how these fit into our general construction.
We generalise both to the case of
\mathds{Z}_{N}
-twistings, and for each we construct two further deformations, leading to four doubly-deformed models.

Mapping the cylinder to a sphere, a novel feature of the construction is that the branch points are at the simple poles corresponding to the ends of the cylinder.
As a result, the untwisted and twisted models have the same number of degrees of freedom.
While twisting does not always lead to inequivalent models, we show that if we use an outer automorphism to twist then the untwisted and twisted models have different symmetries, hence the latter are new integrable sigma models.

4d Chern-Simons theory is defined on a product of the 2d space-time and a Riemann surface.
Depending on whether the Riemann surface is a sphere, cylinder or torus, the resulting 2d integrable sigma models can be understood as rational, trigonometric or elliptic respectively.
There are close relationships between these surfaces, e.g., the cylinder can be mapped to a sphere with two simple poles, hence the same integrable sigma model can have different descriptions.

In this paper we investigate trigonometric models, but we often exploit the closely related rational description.

To construct 2d integrable sigma models from 4d Chern-Simons we introduce defects.
There are different types of defect and our focus is on the so-called disorder defects.
To write down the action of 4d Chern-Simons, the Chern-Simons 3-form needs to be paired with a 1-form
\omega

Disorder defects are the zeroes of this meromorphic 1-form, and have been extensively studied in the case that the Riemann surface is a sphere, leading to the construction of new 2d integrable sigma models and a better understanding of the relations between them

In general, when the Riemann surface is a cylinder, disorder defects are less well-studied.

The zeroes in

Once the meromorphic 1-form is given, along with regularity conditions for

We will not give a detailed account of how to extract 2d integrable sigma models from 4d Chern-Simons here, or review the different methods that are described in the literature.

The presence of poles in

Varying the action (

\omega

As already discussed, in this paper we consider twist functions that have real double poles with vanishing residue, pairs of real simple poles with equal and opposite residues, and pairs of complex conjugate simple poles with equal and opposite residues.

Double poles with vanishing residues.

Let us consider a double pole with vanishing residue at

The contribution to the boundary term (

and bilinear form

\langle\!\langle(X,X^{\prime}),(Y,Y^{\prime})\rangle\!\rangle=\ell_{r,1}(\tr(XY^{\prime})+\tr(X^{\prime}Y))\penalty\ ,

(3.11)

where, with a minor abuse of notation,

This corresponds to taking

to be valued in the Lagrangian subalgebra

Now considering how the surviving external gauge transformations act on the edge modes

3.3
Internal gauge symmetry

Finally turning to the internal gauge symmetries, we recall from eq. (

\mathrm{G}_{0}
at the fixed points of

If we impose

\tilde{g}\to\tilde{g}v\penalty\ ,\qquad\tilde{g},v\in\mathrm{G}_{0}\penalty\ .

(3.32)

On the other hand, if we impose

(3.36)

3.4
Actions

We conclude this section by giving the general form of the actions of the twisted trigonometric 2d integrable sigma models that follow from the construction outlined above.
The boundary conditions that we consider allow us to use the unifying action constructed from 4d Chern-Simons in

]

Defining the Wess-Zumino term

\begin{split}\mathcal{S}_{\text{WZ}}(g)&=\mathinner{\frac{1}{3}}\int_{[0,1]\times\Sigma}d^{3}\sigma\,\epsilon^{ijk}\tr\big(g^{-1}\partial_{i}g[g^{-1}\partial_{j}g,g^{-1}\partial_{k}g]\big)\penalty\ ,\end{split}

(3.38)

Recall that the set of poles

does not include the poles at
z_{\mathrm{f.p.}}=0,\infty
.

\eta

3.32

On the other hand, the general form of the
\lambda

\begin{split}\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}^{(N)}(\hat{g})&=\sum_{r\in\mathfrak{p}}\bigg(\int_{\Sigma}d^{2}\sigma\sum_{p_{r}=0}^{m_{r}-1}\ell_{r,p_{r}}\tr\big((\partial_{z}^{p_{r}}\mathcal{L}_{+})\hat{g}^{-1}\partial_{-}\hat{g}-\hat{g}^{-1}\partial_{+}\hat{g}(\partial_{z}^{p_{r}}\mathcal{L}_{-})\big)|_{z=r}+\ell_{r,0}\,\mathcal{S}_{\text{WZ}}(\hat{g}|_{z=r})\bigg)\\
&\quad+\int_{\Sigma}d^{2}\sigma\,\ell_{0}\tr\big(\mathcal{L}_{+}\tilde{g}^{-1}\partial_{-}\tilde{g}-\tilde{g}^{-1}\partial_{+}\tilde{g}\mathcal{L}_{-}\big)|_{z=0}+\ell_{0}\,\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{WZ}}(\tilde{g})\penalty\ .\end{split}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} B_{\theta_{1}\phi_{1}}&=-8\eta\sin 2\theta_{1}\cos 2\theta_{2}\cos^{2}2\psi_{2}\penalty\ ,\\
B_{\theta_{2}\phi_{2}}&=-8\eta\sin 2\theta_{1}\cos 2\theta_{2}\sin^{2}2\psi_{2}\penalty\ ,\end{aligned}

\displaystyle B_{\theta_{1}\phi_{2}}=B_{\theta_{2}\phi_{1}}=-8\eta\cos 2\theta_{1}\sin 2\theta_{2}\penalty\ ,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} B_{\theta_{1}\phi_{2}}&=-8\eta\cos 2\theta_{1}\sin 2\theta_{2}\cos^{2}2\psi_{2}\penalty\ ,\\
B_{\theta_{2}\phi_{1}}&=-8\eta\cos 2\theta_{1}\sin 2\theta_{2}\sin^{2}2\psi_{2}\penalty\ ,\end{aligned}

\displaystyle B_{\theta_{1}\theta_{2}}=4\eta\sin 4\psi_{2}\penalty\ ,

\displaystyle B_{\phi_{1}\phi_{2}}=-2\eta(\cos 4\theta_{1}-\cos 4\theta_{2})\sin 4\psi_{2}\penalty\ .

The first thing we note is that the untwisted model has a manifest
\mathrm{U}(1)^{4}
global symmetry.
Two of these, shifts in
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
, come from the unbroken left-acting symmetries, while the other two, shifts in
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
correspond to the right-acting Cartan symmetry.
The twisted model only has a manifest
\mathrm{U}(1)^{3}
global symmetry, and we see explicitly that the right-acting Cartan symmetry not generated by an element of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
, i.e. shifts in
\psi_{2}
, is no longer a symmetry.
A further indication that these two backgrounds are not equivalent is that the B-field for the untwisted model is closed, while for the
\mathds{Z}_{2}
-twisted model it is not.

7
Conclusions

In this paper we have introduced a new class of integrable sigma models, the
\mathds{Z}_{N}
-twisted trigonometric sigma models.
Starting from 4d Chern-Simons on the product of 2d space-time and a cylinder, these models are constructed by introducing a
\mathds{Z}_{N}
branch cut along the non-compact direction of the cylinder.
We have described the general procedure for constructing real Lorentz-invariant
\mathds{Z}_{N}
-twisted trigonometric sigma models and implemented this for a number of explicit examples.
Two of these are the
\mathds{Z}_{N}
-twisted
\eta
-model and
\lambda
-model whose actions are given in eqs. (
4.29
) and (
4.31
), while their Lax connections can be found in (
4.25
) and (
4.28
).
These models are expected to be related by Poisson-Lie duality with respect to the fixed-point subgroup of the
\mathds{Z}_{N}
automorphism
\sigma
,
\mathrm{G}_{0}
.
The remaining four, constructed in
sec.
4.2
are deformations of these models, or their non-abelian duals with respect to the global
\mathrm{G}
symmetry, and, in the nomenclature we have introduced, are the:

•

YB-deformed
\mathds{Z}_{2}
-twisted
\eta
-model,

•

CC-deformed
\mathds{Z}_{2}
-twisted
\eta
-model,

•

YB-deformed
\mathds{Z}_{2}
-twisted
\lambda
-model,

•

CC-deformed
\mathds{Z}_{2}
-twisted
\lambda
-model.

Here the YB-deformed models have the
\mathds{Z}_{N}
-twisted
\eta
-model and
\lambda
-model as their undeformed limits, while the CC-deformed models have their non-abelian duals.
The specialisation to
N=2
for these doubly-deformed models is for practical reasons and there is, in principle, no obstruction to generalising to any
N
.

Having introduced this class of models, there are a number of interesting directions to explore, including whether the twisted sigma models are equivalent to untwisted sigma models, their underlying integrable structure, and whether we can generalise the construction to other symmetric sigma models, including those that describe the worldsheet theories of strings.

Equivalence to untwisted models.

We have started to answer the first of these questions.
In particular, we argued that the
\mathds{Z}_{2}
-twisted
\mathrm{SU}(2)
and
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathds{R})
models, with
\sigma
an inner automorphism (either in the real form or complexified group), are all related to the untwisted models by parameter-dependent field redefinitions, dropping a closed B-field and 2d space-time dualities.
We then showed that the untwisted and
\mathds{Z}_{2}
-twisted
\mathrm{SU}(n)

\eta
-models, with
\sigma
given by an outer automorphism, have different global symmetries.
Moreover, in the closely related case of
\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)
with the automorphism that swaps the two copies, the untwisted model has a closed B-field, while the
\mathds{Z}_{2}
-twisted one does not.
Therefore, in these cases the two models cannot be equivalent.
While we leave a full analysis for future work, it is natural to speculate that the inequivalent
\eta
-models might be enumerated by the outer automorphisms of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.

It is also interesting to note that for the
\eta
-models the R-matrix
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}
is required to commute with
\sigma
, while for the
\lambda
-models there is no analogous restriction.
Therefore, if there are
\mathds{Z}_{N}
automorphisms with the property that there exists no R-matrix, either split or non-split, that commutes with
\sigma
, the
\mathds{Z}_{N}
-twisted
\eta
-model would not exist, while the
\mathds{Z}_{N}
-twisted
\lambda
-model would.

Underlying integrable structure.

Much remains to be understood with regards to the integrable structure of these models.
A key next step would be to analyse them in the Hamiltonian formalism, computing the Poisson bracket of the Lax matrix and extracting the conserved charges
[
17
,
18
]
, and investigating their underlying affine Gaudin models
[
15
,
16
,
14
]
.
It is also interesting to note that the Yang-Baxter deformation of the principal chiral model has
q
-deformed symmetries
[
53
,
54
]
.
For the same finite Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, we can consider untwisted or twisted affinisations, each of which can be
q
-deformed.
Inequivalent twistings are again classified by outer automorphisms of
\mathfrak{g}
, hence we may ask if the symmetries of the
\mathds{Z}_{N}
-twisted
\eta
-models are given by the
q
-deformed twisted affine Lie algebras.
Derivations of conserved charges from the monodromy matrix for the untwisted and
\mathds{Z}_{2}
-twisted
\mathrm{SU}(2)

\eta
-models supporting this idea appear in
[
55
]
.

It would also be interesting to understand the
\mathcal{E}
-model formulation
[
56
,
57
]
of the twisted models, which unifies models with the same twist function in a single first-order 2d model on a doubled space.
Different boundary conditions then correspond to integrating out different sets degrees of freedom.
For example, both the Yang-Baxter deformation of the principal chiral model, the untwisted
\eta
-model, and the current-current deformation of the WZW model, the untwisted
\lambda
-model, can be found from the same
\mathcal{E}
-model
[
58
]
.

\mathcal{E}
-models can be found directly from 4d Chern-Simons
[
14
]
, and given the importance of gauge symmetries in our analysis, degenerate
\mathcal{E}
-models may play a central role
[
59
,
60
]
.

Let us also briefly comment on quantum integrability and S-matrices.
For compact Lie groups
\mathrm{G}
, the principal chiral model is asymptotically free and strongly-coupled in the IR.
Their massive S-matrices are tensor products of two
\mathrm{G}
-invariant R-matrices, corresponding to the left-acting and right-acting symmetries, each with an infinite-dimensional Yangian symmetry
[
61
,
62
,
63
]
.
The
q
-deformations of these R-matrices are known and are in correspondence with the
q
-deformed untwisted and twisted affine Lie algebras
[
64
]
.
Again, since these are classified by outer automorphisms, we may ask if the quantum S-matrices of the untwisted models are tensor products of a Yangian and a
q
-deformed untwisted R-matrix, while those of the twisted models are tensor products of a Yangian and a
q
-deformed twisted R-matrix.
Both the untwisted and twisted R-matrices for the same Lie group
\mathrm{G}
have the same Yangian R-matrix as their undeformed limit, mirroring the fact that the untwisted and twisted sigma models both limit to the principal chiral model.
As a starting point it would be important to compute the renormalisation group flow of the twisted models either directly in 2d
[
65
,
66
]
or using general results from 4d Chern-Simons
[
67
,
68
]
.

Generalisations to other symmetric sigma models.

There are a number of generalisations to other symmetric sigma models that it would be possible to pursue.
In this paper we have considered disorder defects, however, there is another important class known as order defects.
While disorder defects lead to a non-ultralocal Poisson bracket of the Lax matrix, order defects lead to an ultralocal bracket making their integrability analysis more tractable.
Therefore, it would be interesting to consider
\mathds{Z}_{N}
-twistings of trigonometric integrable models constructed from order defects such as those in
[
69
,
5
]
, or mixed order and disorder defects.

Returning to disorder defects, the simplest extension is to ask how the construction is modified if we allow the residues at the fixed points
z_{\mathrm{f.p.}}=0,\infty
to sum to a non-zero value.
In the untwisted case with
\eta
-type boundary conditions this gives the Yang-Baxter deformation of the principal chiral model plus WZ term
[
70
]
.
Another direction would be to explore coupled models along the lines of
[
71
,
12
]
.
This would generalise our explicit examples to models with more fields, where on the original sheet we have more than a double pole or pair of simple poles.
If we have
N_{\mathrm{f}}
double poles and pairs of simple poles, then we expect to find a twisted model with
N_{\mathrm{f}}\dim\mathrm{G}
degrees of freedom.
An example of such a twist function on the
N
-fold cover is given in
fig.

.

Finally, let us comment on the generalisation to coset models, including the symmetric space sigma model and the
\mathds{Z}_{4}
-coset models that describe the worldsheet theories of strings.
Such models have been studied from the perspective of 4d Chern-Simons in, e.g.,
[
5
,
27
,
29
,
30
,
31
,
32
]
.
Coset models are constructed from 4d Chern-Simons by introducing a branch cut between two marked points that are not poles or zeroes.
As an example, if we start from a twist function with
N_{\mathrm{f}}+1
double poles and pairs of simple poles on
\mathds{C}\mathbf{P}^{1}
, following the standard construction, the resulting 2d integrable sigma model will have target space
\mathrm{G}^{N_{\mathrm{f}}+1}/\mathrm{G}

[
12
]
.
Introducing a
\mathds{Z}_{N}
branch cut between two marked points and imposing
\mathds{Z}_{N}
-equivariance then yields an integrable sigma model on
\mathrm{G}^{N_{\mathrm{f}}+1}/\mathrm{G}_{0}
.
This class of sigma models have been constructed from affine Gaudin models in
[
72
]
.
Note that for a
\mathds{Z}_{N}
branch cut between marked points, the branch points become zeroes of order
N-1
on the
N
-fold cover, in contrast with the construction described in this paper where the branch points remain simple poles.

In principle, to construct a
\mathds{Z}_{N}
-twisted
\mathds{Z}_{M}
-coset model we would need to introduce two branch cuts on the original sheet, a
\mathds{Z}_{N}
branch cut between two simple poles and a
\mathds{Z}_{M}
branch cut between two marked points.
The simplest example would be the
\mathds{Z}_{2}
-twisted
\mathds{Z}_{2}
-coset
\eta
-model, for which the untwisted model is the Yang-Baxter deformation of the symmetric space sigma model
[
73
]
.
Since this involves introducing more than one branch cut it appears that the Riemann surface is higher genus and the resulting model, if it exists, would be elliptic.
Disorder defects in elliptic models have been investigated, e.g. in
[
74
]
, and it would be interesting to see if it is possible to construct such a
\mathds{Z}_{N}
-twisted
\mathds{Z}_{M}
-coset model.

The elliptic sigma models of
[
74
]
are related to the trigonometric sigma models in this paper.
Working with
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathds{R})
, the Riemann surface of 4d Chern-Simons is taken to be a torus and
\mathds{Z}_{n}\times\mathds{Z}_{n}
-equivariance is imposed.
The
\mathds{Z}_{n}\times\mathds{Z}_{n}
action on the torus is along its two cycles, similar to the
\mathds{Z}_{N}
action we have considered, which acts along the cycle of the cylinder, while the
\mathds{Z}_{n}
automorphisms of the Lie algebra are particular inner automorphisms.
The trigonometric limit corresponds to decompactifying one of the cycles and yields a special case of the
\mathds{Z}_{N}
-twisted
\eta
-model.
For
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathds{R})
this is a
\mathds{Z}_{n}
-twisted
\eta
-model, where the R-matrix
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}
is constructed from the
\mathds{Z}_{n}

\mathds{Z}_{n}
-equivariance along the now decompactified cycle and coincides with a particular Reshetikhin twist of the split Drinfel’d-Jimbo R-matrix.
For
n=2
there is no Reshetikhin twist and the whole construction can be analytically-continued to the compact real form
\mathrm{SU}(2)
while preserving reality.
In this case the resulting sigma model is the elliptic deformation of the
\mathrm{SU}(2)
principal chiral model
[
10
]
whose trigonometric limit is the
\mathds{Z}_{2}
-twisted
\mathrm{SU}(2)

\eta
-model.

If it is possible to construct a
\mathds{Z}_{N}
-twisted
\mathds{Z}_{M}
-coset model, this may be a way to bypass the presence of a curvature singularity in the supergravity backgrounds for the

Weighted Low-rank Approximation via Stochastic Gradient Descent on Manifolds Thanks: Correspondence to: Hao Wu ( haowu@gwu.edu ) Thanks: The authors would like to thank Yingfeng Hu for interesting and helpful conversations.
Conglong Xu, Peiqi Yang, Hao Wu

Abstract
. We solve a regularized weighted low-rank approximation problem by a stochastic gradient descent on a manifold. To guarantee the convergence of our stochastic gradient descent, we establish a convergence theorem on manifolds for retraction-based stochastic gradient descents admitting confinements. On sample data from the Netflix Prize training dataset, our algorithm outperforms the existing stochastic gradient descent on Euclidean spaces. We also compare the accelerated line search on this manifold to the existing accelerated line search on Euclidean spaces.

(The Weighted Low-Rank Approximation Problem)

\displaystyle O_{n}

Overview and main contributions.

In Section
2
, we establish convergence Theorem
2.6
for stochastic gradient descents on manifolds admitting confinements. Theorem
2.6
is the main theoretical contribution of this paper. This theorem is inspired by Section
5
of Bottou (1999). Intuitively speaking, there are two obvious ways for a gradient descent to diverge. First, the gradient flow-line could be unbounded in the descending direction. Bottou (1999) avoids this by assuming the existence of a confinement (
[
6
, (5.1)]
). We do the same by adapting Bottou’s ideas to manifolds. Second, the gradient vector of the cost function could grow so rapidly that the gradient descent fails to be a good approximation of the gradient flow-line. To avoid this, Bottou elected to impose constraints to limit the growth of the gradient vector (
[
6
, (5.2)]
). These constraints somewhat limit the applications of his version of the convergence theorem though. We choose to resolve this issue by a “semi-adaptive” approach. That is, we start with a preferred sequence of step sizes and then, depending on the outcome of each iteration of the algorithm, adjust the step size for the following iteration to guarantee convergence.

In Section
3
, we interpret Problem
1.4
as an expectation cost problem and apply Theorem
2.6
to design a convergent stochastic gradient descent (Algorithm
3.13
) for Problem
1.4
.

In Section
4
, we explore the numerical performances of Algorithms
3.13
and
C.8
. In particular, we apply these algorithms to the Matrix Completion Problem, which is a special case of Problem
1.1
with binary weights. We first compare Algorithm
3.13
to the benchmark given by the stochastic gradient descent Algorithm
D.7
on the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times k}
over the same number of iterations. Algorithm
D.7
was introduced in the previous literatures (for instance,
[
2
]
). Our numerical results indicate that, on sample data from the Netflix Prize training dataset, Algorithm
3.13
outperforms Algorithm
D.7
. We also compare Algorithms
3.13
and
C.8
to the benchmark given by the accelerated line search Algorithm
D.17
on the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times k}
over the same runtime. Our numerical results indicate that, on the same sample data,

•

Algorithm
C.8
converges faster than Algorithm
D.17
,

•

Algorithm
D.17
converges to a local minimum with lower value for the unregularized cost function when
\lambda
is sufficiently small,

•

Algorithm

outperforms Algorithm
3.13
,

•

Algorithm

Definition 2.1
.

Let
M

\Omega

|f(x,\omega)|\leq C_{K}
for every

\omega\in\Omega
.

In Definition
2.1
,
M
is the manifold on which we search for the minimum of an expectation cost function, and
\Omega
is the space of samples used to guide our search. While designing gradient descent algorithms on manifolds, retractions are often used to replace the often computationally expensive exponential maps of Riemannian manifolds (
[

]
,
[

]

). We recall the definition of retractions on differentiable manifolds below.

Definition 2.2
.

(
[
1
, Definition 4.1.1]
)
Let
M
be a differentiable manifold. A retraction on
M
is a
C^{1}
map
R:TM\rightarrow M
such that, for every
x\in M
, the restriction
R_{x}=R|_{T_{x}M}
satisfies

•

R_{x}(\mathbf{0}_{x})=x
, where
\mathbf{0}_{x}
is the zero vector in
T_{x}M
,

•

dR_{x}(\mathbf{0}_{x})=\mathrm{id}_{T_{x}M}
under the canonical identification
T_{\mathbf{0}_{x}}T_{x}M\cong T_{x}M
, where
dR_{x}
is the differential of
R_{x}
and
\mathrm{id}_{T_{x}M}
is the identity map of
T_{x}M
.

From a theoretical perspective, retractions pull back the differential calculations in a manifold onto its tangent spaces. Our work shows that, if one is careful, most such computations can be done in single tangent spaces of the manifold. And computations on such spaces are simply differential calculus in inner product spaces. This allows us to mostly avoid the more delicate aspects of Riemannian geometry such as connections and geodesics. To do this, it is convenient to use the concept of retraction-dependent Lipschitz gradients to replace the common notion of Lipschitz gradients.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold,
R
a given retraction on
M
, and
\Omega
a probability space. Denote by
\left\langle\ast,\ast\right\rangle_{x}
the Riemannian inner product on

\|\ast\|_{x}

\nabla f_{x,\omega}
is the vector in

via the inner product
\left\langle\ast,\ast\right\rangle_{x}

•

\|\nabla f_{x,\omega}(\mathbf{v})-\nabla f_{x,\omega}(\mathbf{0}_{x})\|_{x}\leq C\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}

In the case

Remark 2.4
.

In Definition

\nabla_{M}f

\nabla_{M}f(x,\omega)=\nabla f_{w}(x)

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla f_{x,\omega}(\mathbf{0}_{x}),\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{x}

This shows that

(2.1)

\nabla f_{x,\omega}(\mathbf{0}_{x})=\nabla_{M}f(x,\omega).

Finally we introduce the concept of confinements of random functions on Remannian manifolds, which plays a central role in this manuscript.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold,
\Omega
a probability space, and

•

Existence of confinement of a random function guarantees that the stochastic gradient descent happens in a compact subset of the manifold and therefore has convergent subsequences.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
h:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be any
C^{2}
function. Then, for any
x\in M
and
\mathbf{v}\in T_{x}M
, there exists an
s^{\star}\in[0,1]
such that
h(R_{x}(\mathbf{v}))=h(x)+\left\langle\nabla h(x),\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{x}+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Hess}(h\circ R_{x})|_{s^{\star}\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v})
.

Proof.

Define
p(s)=h(R_{x}(s\mathbf{v}))
. Then, by Taylor’s Theorem, there exists an
s^{\star}\in[0,1]
such that
p(1)=p(0)+p^{\prime}(0)+\frac{1}{2}p^{\prime\prime}(s^{\star})
. Note that:

•

p(0)=h(R_{x}(\mathbf{0}_{x}))=h(x)
and
p(1)=h(R_{x}(\mathbf{v}))
;

•

p^{\prime}(s)=\left\langle\nabla(h\circ R_{x})(s\mathbf{v}),\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{x}
and therefore
p^{\prime}(0)=\left\langle\nabla(h\circ R_{x})(\mathbf{0}_{x}),\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{x}=\left\langle\nabla h(x),\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{x}
, where the last equation follows from Equation (
2.1
);

•

p^{\prime\prime}(s)=\frac{d}{ds}\left\langle\nabla(h\circ R_{x})(s\mathbf{v}),\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{x}=\left\langle\frac{d}{ds}\nabla(h\circ R_{x})(s\mathbf{v}),\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{x}=\mathrm{Hess}(h\circ R_{x})|_{s\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v})
.

Combining the above, we get that
h(R_{x}(\mathbf{v}))=h(x)+\left\langle\nabla h(x),\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{x}+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Hess}(h\circ R_{x})|_{s^{\star}\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v})
.
∎

Lemma A.4
.

F
is first-order differentiable and has locally
R
-Lipschitz gradient. Moreover, there exists a
C_{1}>0
such that
\|\nabla(F\circ R_{x})(\mathbf{v})-\nabla F(x)\|_{x}\leq C_{1}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}
and
F(R_{x}(\mathbf{v}))\leq F(x)+\left\langle\nabla F(x),\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{x}+\frac{C_{1}}{2}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}^{2}

satisfying
\rho(x)\leq\rho_{1}
and every
\mathbf{v}\in T_{x}M
satisfying
\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}\leq G_{1}
, where

(A.2)

G_{1}:=\sup\{\|\nabla_{M}f(x,\omega)\|_{x}~\big|~x\in M\text{ satisfying }\rho(x)\leq\rho_{1},~\omega\in\Omega\}.

Proof.

Since
\|\nabla_{M}f(x,\omega)\|
is locally bounded, it follows from the Donimated Convergence Theorem that
F
is first-order differentiable and
\nabla F(x)=\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{M}f(x,\omega)d\mu
. Since
R
is
C^{2}
,
\|\nabla_{T_{x}M}f(R_{x}(\mathbf{v}),\omega)\|
is also locally bounded. So, again by the Donimated Convergence Theorem, we have

\nabla(F\circ R_{x})(\mathbf{v})=\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{T_{x}M}f(R_{x}(\mathbf{v}),\omega)d\mu=\int_{\Omega}\nabla f_{x,\omega}(\mathbf{v})d\mu,

where
f_{x,\omega}:=f(R_{x}(\ast),\omega):T_{x}M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is given in Definition
2.3
.

Since
f
has locally
R
-Lipschitz gradient, we know that, for every compact subset
K
of
M
and every
r>0
, there is a constant
C_{K,r}>0
such that
\|\nabla f_{x,\omega}(\mathbf{v})-\nabla f_{x,\omega}(\mathbf{0}_{x})\|_{x}\leq C_{K,r}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}
for every
x\in K
, every
\mathbf{v}\in T_{x}M
satisfying
\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}\leq r
and every
\omega\in\Omega
. Thus,

\displaystyle\|\nabla(F\circ R_{x})(\mathbf{v})-\nabla(F\circ R_{x})(\mathbf{0}_{x})\|_{x}=\|\int_{\Omega}\nabla f_{x,\omega}(\mathbf{v})d\mu-\int_{\Omega}\nabla f_{x,\omega}(\mathbf{0}_{x})d\mu\|_{x}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla f_{x,\omega}(\mathbf{v})-\nabla f_{x,\omega}(\mathbf{0}_{x})\|_{x}d\mu\leq\int_{\Omega}C_{K,r}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}d\mu=C_{K,r}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}.

F
has locally
R
-Lipschitz gradient.

Now consider the special case
K=\{x\in M~\big|~\rho(x)\leq\rho_{1}\}
and

. Let
C_{1}=C_{K,G_{1}}
. Then, by Equation (
2.1
) and the above inequality,
\|\nabla(F\circ R_{x})(\mathbf{v})-\nabla F(x)\|_{x}\leq C_{1}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}
for every

satisfying
\rho(x)\leq\rho_{1}
and every
\mathbf{v}\in T_{x}M
satisfying
\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}\leq G_{1}
. Let

for

\displaystyle F(R_{x}(\mathbf{v}))-F(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p(1)-p(0)=\int_{0}^{1}p^{\prime}(s)ds=\int_{0}^{1}\left\langle\nabla(F\circ R_{x})(s\mathbf{v}),\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{x}ds

Lemma A.5
.

\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}c_{t}E(\|\nabla F(x_{t})\|_{x_{t}}^{2})

By Lemma

,
\{x_{t}\}

{0\leq\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}\|\nabla F(x_{t})\|_{x_{t}}^{2}\leq\frac{c_{t}}{\Phi_{\min}}\|\nabla F(x_{t})\|_{x_{t}}^{2}}

Lemma A.6

\{\omega_{t}\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
. For any
t\geq 0
, we have that

E(z_{t})=E(z_{t-1})+E(\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}u_{t})=E(z_{t-1})+E(E(\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}u_{t}~\big|~\omega_{0},\dots,\omega_{t-1}))=E(z_{t-1})=\cdots=E(z_{0})=0.

Note that
z_{t-1}
is also determined by
\omega_{0},\dots,\omega_{t-1}
, So the variance of
z_{t}
satisfies

\displaystyle Var(z_{t})=E(z_{t}^{2})=E((\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}u_{t}+z_{t-1})^{2})=E(\frac{c_{t}^{2}}{\varphi_{t}^{2}}u_{t}^{2}+z_{t-1}^{2}+2\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}u_{t}z_{t-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Var(z_{t-1})+E(\frac{c_{t}^{2}}{\varphi_{t}^{2}}u_{t}^{2})+2E(\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}u_{t}z_{t-1})\leq Var(z_{t-1})+\frac{c_{t}^{2}U^{2}}{\Phi_{\min}^{2}}+2E(\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}u_{t}z_{t-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Var(z_{t-1})+\frac{c_{t}^{2}U^{2}}{\Phi_{\min}^{2}}+2E(z_{t-1}E(\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}u_{t}~\big|~\omega_{0},\dots,\omega_{t-1}))=Var(z_{t-1})+\frac{c_{t}^{2}U^{2}}{\Phi_{\min}^{2}}.

Summing the above inequality from
1
to
t
, we get that

Var(z_{t})\leq Var(z_{0})+\frac{U^{2}}{\Phi_{\min}^{2}}\sum_{\tau=1}^{t}c_{\tau}^{2}\leq Var(z_{0})+\frac{U^{2}}{\Phi_{\min}^{2}}\sigma.

This shows that
\{Var(z_{t})\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
is bounded. Thus, by the Martingale Convergence Theorem,
{\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}z_{t}=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}u_{t}}
converges almost surely. By Lemma
A.5
,
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}\|\nabla F(x_{t})\|_{x_{t}}^{2}
also converges almost surely. Thus,

\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}\left\langle\nabla F(x_{t}),\nabla_{M}f(x_{t},\omega_{t})\right\rangle_{x_{t}}=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}u_{t}+\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}\|\nabla F(x_{t})\|_{x_{t}}^{2}

converges almost surely.

Next consider
\{F(x_{t})\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
. Assume that
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}\left\langle\nabla F(x_{t}),\nabla_{M}f(x_{t},\omega_{t})\right\rangle_{x_{t}}
converges. Define

v_{t}=F(x_{t})-\sum_{\tau=t}^{\infty}\frac{c_{\tau}}{\varphi_{\tau}}\left\langle\nabla F(x_{\tau}),\nabla_{M}f(x_{\tau},\omega_{\tau})\right\rangle_{x_{\tau}}+\frac{C_{1}G_{1}^{2}}{2\Phi_{\min}^{2}}\sum_{\tau=t}^{\infty}c_{\tau}^{2},

where
G_{1}
is defined in Equation (
A.2
). By Inequality (
A.3
),
\{v_{t}\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
is a decreasing sequence. By Lemmas
A.2
and
A.4
,
\{F(x_{t})\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
is a bounded sequence. Since
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}c_{t}^{2}
and
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}\left\langle\nabla F(x_{t}),\nabla_{M}f(x_{t},\omega_{t})\right\rangle_{x_{t}}
both converge, the sequences
\{\sum_{\tau=t}^{\infty}c_{\tau}^{2}\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
and
\{\sum_{\tau=t}^{\infty}\frac{c_{\tau}}{\varphi_{\tau}}\left\langle\nabla F(x_{\tau}),\nabla_{M}f(x_{\tau},\omega_{\tau})\right\rangle_{x_{\tau}}\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
are also bounded. So
\{v_{t}\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
is bounded too. Thus,
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}v_{t}
converges. Note that

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{\tau=t}^{\infty}c_{\tau}^{2}=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{\tau=t}^{\infty}\frac{c_{\tau}}{\varphi_{\tau}}\left\langle\nabla F(x_{\tau}),\nabla_{M}f(x_{\tau},\omega_{\tau})\right\rangle_{x_{\tau}}=0.

This shows that
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}F(x_{t})=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}v_{t}
converges when
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}\left\langle\nabla F(x_{t}),\nabla_{M}f(x_{t},\omega_{t})\right\rangle_{x_{t}}
converges. But we have shown that the latter sum converges almost surely. Hence,
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}F(x_{t})
also converges almost surely.
∎

Lemma A.7
.

There are positive constants
C_{2}
and
r_{2}
such that

\left|\|\nabla F(R_{x}(\mathbf{v}))\|_{R_{x}(\mathbf{v})}-\|\nabla(F\circ R_{x})(\mathbf{v})\|_{x}\right|\leq C_{2}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}

for every
x\in M
satisfying
\rho(x)\leq\rho_{1}
and every
\mathbf{v}\in T_{x}M
satisfying
\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}\leq r_{2}
.

Proof.

For every
x\in M
, there is a
C^{\infty}
coordinate chart
(\mathcal{W}_{x},(y_{1},\dots,y_{m}))
such that
x\in\mathcal{W}_{x}
and the closure
\overline{\mathcal{W}_{x}}
is compact. Choose an open subset
\mathcal{U}_{x}
of
\mathcal{W}_{x}
such that
x\in\mathcal{U}_{x}\subset\overline{\mathcal{U}_{x}}\subset\mathcal{W}_{x}
. Note that the closure
\overline{\mathcal{U}_{x}}
of
\mathcal{U}_{x}
is also compact.

For
y\in\mathcal{W}_{x}
, apply the Gram-Schmidt Process to the basis
[\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}},\dots,\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{m}}]
of
T_{x}M
and then normalize the length. This gives an orthonormal basis
[\mathbf{v}_{1}(y),\dots,\mathbf{v}_{m}(y)]
of
T_{y}M
and a
C^{\infty}

m\times m
invertible upper-triangular matrix
P(y)
such that
[\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}},\dots,\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{m}}]=[\mathbf{v}_{1}(y),\dots,\mathbf{v}_{m}(y)]P(y)
. For the tangent bundle
T\mathcal{W}_{x}
of
\mathcal{W}_{x}
, we use the coordinates
(y_{1},\dots,y_{m},z_{1},\dots,z_{m})
where, for any
y\in\mathcal{W}_{x}
and
\mathbf{v}\in T_{y}\mathcal{W}_{x}
, we have
\mathbf{v}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}z_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}
. Since the frame
[\mathbf{v}_{1}(y),\dots,\mathbf{v}_{m}(y)]
is
C^{\infty}
on
\mathcal{W}_{x}
, the coordinates
(y_{1},\dots,y_{m},z_{1},\dots,z_{m})
are
C^{\infty}
on
T\mathcal{W}_{x}
. Let
[dR_{i,j}]_{m\times m}=[dR_{i,j}(y_{1},\dots,y_{m},z_{1},\dots,z_{m})]_{m\times m}
be the
m\times m
matrix representing
dR_{y}|_{\mathbf{v}}
on
T\mathcal{W}_{x}
in the coordinates
(y_{1},\dots,y_{m},z_{1},\dots,z_{m})
. Since the retraction
R
is
C^{2}
, we know that
[dR_{i,j}]
is
C^{1}
.

By the compactness of
\overline{\mathcal{U}_{x}}
, there is an
r_{x}>0
such that
R_{y}(\mathbf{v})\in\mathcal{W}_{x}
whenever
y\in\overline{\mathcal{U}_{x}}
and
\mathbf{v}\in T_{y}M
satisfying
\|\mathbf{v}\|_{y}\leq r_{x}
. For any
y\in\overline{\mathcal{U}_{x}}
,
\mathbf{v}\in T_{y}M
satisfying
\|\mathbf{v}\|_{y}\leq r_{x}
and
\mathbf{u}\in T_{y}M
, we have that

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla(F\circ R_{y})(\mathbf{v}),\mathbf{u}\right\rangle_{y}=d(F\circ R_{y})|_{\mathbf{v}}(\mathbf{u})=((dF|_{R_{y}(\mathbf{v})})\circ(dR_{y}|_{\mathbf{v}}))(\mathbf{u})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle(\nabla F)(R_{y}(\mathbf{v})),(dR_{y}|_{\mathbf{v}})(\mathbf{u})\right\rangle_{R_{y}(\mathbf{v})}=\left\langle\mathrm{adj}(dR_{y}|_{\mathbf{v}})((\nabla F)(R_{y}(\mathbf{v}))),\mathbf{u}\right\rangle_{y},

where
\mathrm{adj}(dR_{y}|_{\mathbf{v}})
is the adjoint of
dR_{y}|_{\mathbf{v}}
with respect to the inner products
\left\langle\ast,\ast\right\rangle_{y}
and
\left\langle\ast,\ast\right\rangle_{R_{y}(\mathbf{v})}
. This shows that

(A.5)

\nabla(F\circ R_{y})(\mathbf{v})=\mathrm{adj}(dR_{y}|_{\mathbf{v}})((\nabla F)(R_{y}(\mathbf{v}))).

Recall that
[\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{m}]
is orthonormal in
T\mathcal{W}_{x}
. So
\mathrm{adj}(dR_{y}|_{\mathbf{v}})
is represented by the matrix
[dR_{i,j}]^{T}
under the coordinates
(y_{1},\dots,y_{m},z_{1},\dots,z_{m})
.

Recall that each entry of the matrix
[dR_{i,j}]
is a
C^{1}
function on
T\mathcal{W}_{x}
. In particular, the gradient
\nabla_{z}dR_{i,j}=\sum_{p=1}^{m}\frac{\partial dR_{i,j}}{\partial z_{p}}\mathbf{v}_{p}\in T_{y}M
is bounded on the compact set
\{\mathbf{v}\in T_{y}M~\big|~y\in\mathcal{U}_{x},~\|\mathbf{v}\|_{y}\leq R_{x}\}
. Define
B_{x}=\max\{\|\nabla_{z}dR_{i,j}(y,\mathbf{v})\|_{y}~\big|~1\leq i,j\leq m,~y\in\mathcal{U}_{x},~\mathbf{v}\in T_{y}M,~\|\mathbf{v}\|_{y}\leq r_{x}\}
. Let
y\in\mathcal{U}_{x}
be the point given by the coordinates
(y_{1},\dots,y_{m})
and
\mathbf{v}=\sum_{p=1}^{m}z_{p}\mathbf{v}_{p}(y)
satisfying
\|\mathbf{v}\|_{y}\leq r_{x}
. By the Mean Value Theorem, there is an
s^{\star}\in[0,1]
such that

\displaystyle dR_{i,j}(y_{1},\dots,y_{m},z_{1},\dots,z_{m})-dR_{i,j}(y_{1},\dots,y_{m},0,\dots,0)=\left\langle\nabla_{z}dR_{i,j}(y,s^{\star}\mathbf{v}),\mathbf{v}\right\rangle_{y}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{z}dR_{i,j}(y,s^{\star}\mathbf{v})\|_{y}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{y}\leq B_{x}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{y}.

Recall that
dR_{y}|_{\mathbf{0}_{y}}=\mathrm{id}_{T_{y}M}
. So
[dR_{i,j}(y_{1},\dots,y_{m},0,\dots,0)]=I_{m}
, that is,

dR_{i,j}(y_{1},\dots,y_{m},0,\dots,0)=\delta_{i,j}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }i=j,\\
0&\text{if }i\neq j.\end{cases}

Combining the above, we get that

(A.6)

dR_{i,j}(y_{1},\dots,y_{m},z_{1},\dots,z_{m})-\delta_{i,j}\leq B_{x}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{y}.

Since
K:=\{x\in M~\big|~\rho(x)\leq\rho_{1}\}
is compact, there is a finite subset
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}\subset K
such that
K\subset\bigcup_{q=1}^{n}\mathcal{U}_{x_{q}}
. Define
r_{2}=\min\{r_{x_{1}},\dots,r_{x_{n}}\}
,
B=\max\{B_{x_{1}},\dots,B_{x_{n}}\}
and
{G_{2}=\sup\{\|\nabla F(x)\|_{x}~\big|~x\in\bigcup_{q=1}^{n}\mathcal{W}_{x_{q}}\}}
. Given
x\in K
, pick an
x_{q}
such that
x\in\mathcal{U}_{x_{q}}
. Denote

•

by
(y_{1},\dots,y_{m},z_{1},\dots,z_{m})
the coordinate on
T\mathcal{W}_{x_{p}}
given in the first half of the proof,

•

by
[\mathbf{v}_{1}(y),\dots,\mathbf{v}_{m}(y)]
the orthonormal frame of
T\mathcal{W}_{x_{p}}
given in the first half of the proof,

•

for
y\in\mathcal{W}_{x_{q}}
, by
\mathbf{w}(y)=[w_{1}(y),\dots,w_{m}(y)]^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
the vector satisfying that
\nabla F(y)=\sum_{p=1}^{m}w_{p}(y)\mathbf{v}_{p}(y)
.

Furthermore, denote by
\|\ast\|
the standard norm on
R^{m}
and by
\|\ast\|_{F}
the Frobenius norm on
\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
. Then, for
x\in\mathcal{U}_{x_{q}}
and
\mathbf{v}\in T_{x}M
satisfying
\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}\leq r_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\|\nabla F(R_{x}(\mathbf{v}))\|_{R_{x}(\mathbf{v})}-\|\nabla(F\circ R_{x})(\mathbf{v})\|_{x}\right|=\left|\|\nabla F(R_{x}(\mathbf{v}))\|_{R_{x}(\mathbf{v})}-\|\mathrm{adj}(dR_{x}|_{\mathbf{v}})((\nabla F)(R_{x}(\mathbf{v})))\|_{x}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\|[\mathbf{v}_{1}(R_{x}(\mathbf{v})),\dots,\mathbf{v}_{m}(R_{x}(\mathbf{v}))]\mathbf{w}(R_{x}(\mathbf{v}))\|_{R_{x}(\mathbf{v})}-\|[\mathbf{v}_{1}(x),\dots,\mathbf{v}_{m}(x)][dR_{i,j}(x,\mathbf{v})]^{T}\mathbf{w}(R_{x}(\mathbf{v}))\|_{x}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\|\mathbf{w}(R_{x}(\mathbf{v}))\|-\|[dR_{i,j}(x,\mathbf{v})]^{T}\mathbf{w}(R_{x}(\mathbf{v}))\|\right|\leq\|[dR_{i,j}(x,\mathbf{v})]^{T}\mathbf{w}(R_{x}(\mathbf{v}))-\mathbf{w}(R_{x}(\mathbf{v}))\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|([dR_{i,j}(x,\mathbf{v})]^{T}-I_{m})\mathbf{w}(R_{x}(\mathbf{v}))\|=\|([dR_{i,j}(x,\mathbf{v})-\delta_{i,j}]^{T})\mathbf{w}(R_{x}(\mathbf{v}))\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{m}(\sum_{i=1}^{m}(dR_{i,j}(x,\mathbf{v})-\delta_{i,j})w_{i}(R_{x}(\mathbf{v})))^{2}}\leq\sqrt{m\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}(dR_{i,j}(x,\mathbf{v})-\delta_{i,j})^{2}(w_{i}(R_{x}(\mathbf{v})))^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{m\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}B^{2}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}^{2}(w_{i}(R_{x}(\mathbf{v})))^{2}}=\sqrt{m^{2}B^{2}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}(w_{i}(R_{x}(\mathbf{v})))^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle mB\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}\|\mathbf{w}(R_{x}(\mathbf{v}))\|=mB\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}\|\nabla F(R_{x}(\mathbf{v}))\|_{R_{x}(\mathbf{v})}\leq mB\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}G_{2}.

Thus, if we choose
C_{2}=mBG_{2}
, then
\left|\|\nabla F(R_{x}(\mathbf{v}))\|_{R_{x}(\mathbf{v})}-\|\nabla(F\circ R_{x})(\mathbf{v})\|_{x}\right|\leq C_{2}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}

for every
x\in K
and every
\mathbf{v}\in T_{x}M
satisfying
\|\mathbf{v}\|_{x}\leq r_{2}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma A.8
.

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|\nabla F(x_{t})\|_{x_{t}}=0
almost surely.

Proof.

By Lemmas
A.5
and
A.6
,
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}\|\nabla F(x_{t})\|_{x_{t}}^{2}
,
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}\left\langle\nabla F(x_{t}),\nabla_{M}f(x_{t},\omega_{t})\right\rangle_{x_{t}}
and
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}F(x_{t})
all converge almost surely. So, to prove the current lemma, we only need to show that
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|\nabla F(x_{t})\|_{x_{t}}=0
if all these three are convergent, which we will assume throughout this proof.

First, we claim that
\liminf_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|\nabla F(x_{t})\|_{x_{t}}=0
. Otherwise, there would be an
\epsilon>0
and a
\tau>0
such that
\|\nabla F(x_{t})\|_{x_{t}}>\epsilon
if
t\geq\tau
. Then
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}\|\nabla F(x_{t})\|_{x_{t}}^{2}\geq\sum_{t=\tau}^{\infty}\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}\|\nabla F(x_{t})\|_{x_{t}}^{2}\geq\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{\Phi_{\max}}\sum_{t=\tau}^{\infty}c_{t}=\infty
, which contradicts our assumption that
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}\|\nabla F(x_{t})\|_{x_{t}}^{2}
converges. This shows that
\liminf_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|\nabla F(x_{t})\|_{x_{t}}=0
.

It remains to show that, under our assumptions,
\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|\nabla F(x_{t})\|_{x_{t}}=0
, too. Let us prove this by contradiction again. Assume
\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|\nabla F(x_{t})\|_{x_{t}}=s>0
. Let
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
,
G_{1}
and
r_{2}
be the positive constants given in Lemmas
A.4
and
A.7
. Since
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}c_{t}=0
, there is a
T>0
such that
\frac{c_{t}(C_{1}+C_{2})G_{1}}{\Phi_{\min}}\leq\frac{s}{8}
and
\frac{c_{t}G_{1}}{\Phi_{\min}}\leq r_{2}
if
t>T
. Since
\liminf_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|\nabla F(x_{t})\|_{x_{t}}=0
and
\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|\nabla F(x_{t})\|_{x_{t}}=s>0
, there are two infinite sequences
\{p_{i}\}
and
\{q_{i}\}
of positive integers such that

•

T<p_{1}<q_{1}<p_{2}<q_{2}<\cdots<p_{i}<q_{i}<p_{i+1}<\cdots
,

•

\|\nabla F(x_{p_{i}})\|_{x_{p_{i}}}<\frac{s}{4}
,
\|\nabla F(x_{q_{i}})\|_{x_{q_{i}}}>\frac{s}{2}
and
\frac{s}{4}\leq\|\nabla F(x_{t})\|_{x_{t}}\leq\frac{s}{2}
if
p_{i}<t<q_{i}
for
i=1,2\dots
.

Then, by Lemmas
A.4
and
A.7
, we have that, for
i=1,2\dots
,

\displaystyle\frac{s}{4}<\|\nabla F(x_{q_{i}})\|_{x_{q_{i}}}-\|\nabla F(x_{p_{i}})\|_{x_{p_{i}}}\leq\sum_{t=p_{i}}^{q_{i}-1}\left|\|\nabla F(x_{t+1})\|_{x_{t+1}}-\|\nabla F(x_{t})\|_{x_{t}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t=p_{i}}^{q_{i}-1}\left|\|\nabla F(R_{x_{t}}(-\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}\nabla_{M}f(x_{t},\omega_{t})))\|_{R_{x_{t}}(-\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}\nabla_{M}f(x_{t},\omega_{t}))}-\|\nabla F(x_{t})\|_{x_{t}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{t=p_{i}}^{q_{i}-1}\left|\|\nabla F(R_{x_{t}}(-\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}\nabla_{M}f(x_{t},\omega_{t})))\|_{R_{x_{t}}(-\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}\nabla_{M}f(x_{t},\omega_{t}))}-\|\nabla(F\circ R_{x_{t}})(-\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}\nabla_{M}f(x_{t},\omega_{t}))\|_{x_{t}}\right|

\displaystyle+\sum_{t=p_{i}}^{q_{i}-1}\left|\|\nabla(F\circ R_{x_{t}})(-\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}\nabla_{M}f(x_{t},\omega_{t}))\|_{x_{t}}-\|\nabla F(x_{t})\|_{x_{t}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{t=p_{i}}^{q_{i}-1}C_{2}\|-\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}\nabla_{M}f(x_{t},\omega_{t})\|_{x_{t}}+\sum_{t=p_{i}}^{q_{i}-1}C_{1}\|-\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}\nabla_{M}f(x_{t},\omega_{t})\|_{x_{t}}=(C_{1}+C_{2})\sum_{t=p_{i}}^{q_{i}-1}\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}\|\nabla_{M}f(x_{t},\omega_{t})\|_{x_{t}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(C_{1}+C_{2})G_{1}}{\Phi_{\min}}\sum_{t=p_{i}}^{q_{i}-1}c_{t}.

Thus,

(A.7)

\sum_{t=p_{i}}^{q_{i}-1}c_{t}>\frac{s\Phi_{\min}}{4(C_{1}+C_{2})G_{1}}>0\text{ for }i=1,2\dots.

Using Lemmas
A.4
and
A.7
again, we get

\displaystyle\frac{s}{4}-\|\nabla F(x_{p_{i}})\|_{x_{p_{i}}}\leq\|\nabla F(x_{{p_{i}}+1})\|_{x_{{p_{i}}+1}}-\|\nabla F(x_{p_{i}})\|_{x_{p_{i}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\nabla F(R_{x_{p_{i}}}(-\frac{c_{p_{i}}}{\varphi_{p_{i}}}\nabla_{M}f(x_{p_{i}},\omega_{p_{i}})))\|_{R_{x_{p_{i}}}(-\frac{c_{p_{i}}}{\varphi_{p_{i}}}\nabla_{M}f(x_{p_{i}},\omega_{p_{i}}))}-\|\nabla F(x_{p_{i}})\|_{x_{p_{i}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\|\nabla F(R_{x_{p_{i}}}(-\frac{c_{p_{i}}}{\varphi_{p_{i}}}\nabla_{M}f(x_{p_{i}},\omega_{p_{i}})))\|_{R_{x_{p_{i}}}(-\frac{c_{p_{i}}}{\varphi_{p_{i}}}\nabla_{M}f(x_{p_{i}},\omega_{p_{i}}))}-\|\nabla(F\circ R_{x_{p_{i}}})(-\frac{c_{p_{i}}}{\varphi_{p_{i}}}\nabla_{M}f(x_{p_{i}},\omega_{p_{i}}))\|_{x_{p_{i}}}\right|

\displaystyle+\|\nabla(F\circ R_{x_{p_{i}}})(-\frac{c_{p_{i}}}{\varphi_{p_{i}}}\nabla_{M}f(x_{p_{i}},\omega_{p_{i}}))-\nabla F(x_{p_{i}})\|_{x_{p_{i}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}\|-\frac{c_{p_{i}}}{\varphi_{p_{i}}}\nabla_{M}f(x_{p_{i}},\omega_{p_{i}})\|_{x_{p_{i}}}+C_{1}\|-\frac{c_{p_{i}}}{\varphi_{p_{i}}}\nabla_{M}f(x_{p_{i}},\omega_{p_{i}})\|_{x_{p_{i}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c_{p_{i}}(C_{1}+C_{2})}{\Phi_{\min}}\|\nabla_{M}f(x_{p_{i}},\omega_{p_{i}})\|_{x_{p_{i}}}\leq\frac{c_{p_{i}}(C_{1}+C_{2})G_{1}}{\Phi_{\min}}\leq\frac{s}{8}.

This shows that

(A.8)

\|\nabla F(x_{p_{i}})\|_{x_{p_{i}}}\geq\frac{s}{8}.

By Inequalities (
A.3
), (
A.8
) and the definitions of
p_{i}
,
q_{i}
, we have

\displaystyle F(x_{q_{i}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle F(x_{p_{i}})-\sum_{t=p_{i}}^{q_{i}-1}\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}\left\langle\nabla F(x_{t}),\nabla_{M}f(x_{t},\omega_{t})\right\rangle_{x_{t}}+\frac{C_{1}G_{1}^{2}}{2\Phi_{\min}^{2}}\sum_{t=p_{i}}^{q_{i}-1}c_{t}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F(x_{p_{i}})-\sum_{t=p_{i}}^{q_{i}-1}\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}\left\langle\nabla F(x_{t}),\nabla_{M}f(x_{t},\omega_{t})-\nabla F(x_{t})\right\rangle_{x_{t}}-\sum_{t=p_{i}}^{q_{i}-1}\frac{c_{t}}{\varphi_{t}}\|\nabla F(x_{t})\|_{x_{t}}^{2}+\frac{C_{1}G_{1}^{2}}{2\Phi_{\min}^{2}}\sum_{t=p_{i}}^{q_{i}-1}c_{t}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

Appendix F
A New Proof of the Eckart-Young Theorem

The Eckart-Young Theorem plays a key role in the theory of low-rank approximation. While studying Problem
1.1
, we found a new proof of this theorem using a direct critical point analysis, instead of the tricky interplay between the spectral and Frobenius norms employed by the traditional proof (for instance,
[
16
]
).

F.1.
The Eckart-Young Theorem

Theorem F.1

(Eckart-Young)
.

For a matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, counting multiplicity, denote by
s_{1}\geq\cdots\geq s_{r}
the positive singular values of
A
. For any matrix
P\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
of satisfying
rankP\leq k
, we have that
\|A-P\|_{F}^{2}\geq\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}s_{j}^{2}
, where

•

\|X\|_{F}:=\sqrt{\mathrm{Tr}(X^{T}X)}=\sqrt{\mathrm{Tr}(XX^{T})}
is the Frobenius norm of a real matrix
X
,

•

s_{j}=0
if
j>r
.

In particular,
\|A-P\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}s_{j}^{2}
if and only if there are a matrix
V\in O_{m}
and a matrix
U\in O_{n}
such that

(F.1)

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V\mathrm{diag}_{m\times n}\{s_{1},\dots,s_{r},0,\dots,0\}U^{T},

(F.2)

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V\mathrm{diag}_{m\times n}\{s_{1},\dots,s_{k},0,\dots,0\}U^{T},

where
\mathrm{diag}_{m\times n}\{t_{1},\dots,t_{\min\{m,n\}}\}
is the
m\times n
diagonal matrix
[x_{i,j}]_{m\times n}
satisfying
x_{i,j}=\begin{cases}t_{i}&\text{if }i=j,\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
.

F.2.
A New Proof

As mentioned above, we prove the Eckart-Young Theorem via a direct critical point analysis. The linear algebra arguments involved in our proof come largely from rehashing the proof of the Singular Value Decomposition. We will be using the following notations in our proof.

•

Denote by
\left\langle A,B\right\rangle:=\mathrm{Tr}(A^{T}B)=\mathrm{Tr}(BA^{T})
the standard bi-invariant inner product of
\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
. Note that
\|A\|_{F}^{2}=\left\langle A,A\right\rangle
for
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
.

•

\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}_{k}
is the set of
m\times n
real matrices of rank at most
k
.

Lemma F.2
.

Define
f:\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}_{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
to be the function
f(P)=\|A-P\|_{F}^{2}
for all
P\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}_{k}
. Then
f
attains its minimal value.

Proof.

Note that
\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}_{k}=\{P\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}~|~\text{the determinants of all }(k+1)\times(k+1)\text{ minors of }P\text{ are }0\}
. Since determinants of minors are continuous functions of
P
, this implies that
\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}_{k}
is a closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
. Furthermore,
f(P)\geq(\|A\|_{F}-\|P\|_{F})^{2}
. So
f(P)>\|A\|_{F}^{2}=f(0)
if
\|P\|_{F}>2\|A\|_{F}
. Therefore,

\inf\{f(P)~|~P\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}_{k}\}=\inf\{f(P)~|~P\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}_{k}\text{ and }\|P\|_{F}\leq 2\|A\|_{F}\}.

But
\{P\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}_{k}~|~\|P\|_{F}\leq 2\|A\|_{F}\}
is a compact set since it is the intersection of the closed set
\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}_{k}
and the compact set
\{P\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}~|~\|P\|_{F}\leq 2\|A\|_{F}\}
. Thus,
f
attains its minimal value by the Extreme Value Theorem.
∎

Lemma F.3
.

Assume that
P_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}_{k}
is a minimizer of the function
f:\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}_{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by
f(P)=\|A-P\|_{F}^{2}
. Then
A^{T}P_{0}=P_{0}^{T}P_{0}
and
P_{0}A^{T}=P_{0}P_{0}^{T}
.

Proof.

For any matrix
R\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
and matrix
S\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, define
g:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

g(x,y)=\|A-(I_{m}+xR)P_{0}(I_{n}+yS)\|_{F}^{2},

where
I_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
and
I_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
are the identity matrices. Note that
(I_{m}+xR)P_{0}(I_{n}+yS)\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}_{k}
for all
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
since
P_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}_{k}
. One can see that
g(x,y)=f((I_{m}+xR)P_{0}(I_{n}+yS))\geq f(P_{0})=g(0,0)
for all
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
since
P_{0}
is a minimizer of
f
. So
\frac{\partial g}{\partial x}(0,0)=\frac{\partial g}{\partial y}(0,0)=0
. Then

0=\frac{\partial g}{\partial x}(0,0)=2\left\langle-RP_{0},A-P_{0}\right\rangle=-2\mathrm{Tr}((A-P_{0})P_{0}^{T}R^{T})=-2\left\langle R,(A-P_{0})P_{0}^{T}\right\rangle.

But the above equation is true for every matrix
R\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
. This implies that
(A-P_{0})P_{0}^{T}=0
, that is
AP_{0}^{T}=P_{0}P_{0}^{T}
. Taking transpositions of both sides of the latter equation, one gets that
P_{0}A^{T}=P_{0}P_{0}^{T}
. Similarly, using
\frac{\partial g}{\partial y}(0,0)=0
, one gets that
A^{T}P_{0}=P_{0}^{T}P_{0}
.
∎

Lemma F.4
.

Assume that
A,P\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
satisfy
A^{T}P=P^{T}P
and
PA^{T}=PP^{T}
. Then there are

•

a matrix

,

•

a matrix

,

•

a non-negative integer
r

\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r}>0

•

a non-negative integer

such that

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V\mathrm{diag}_{m\times n}\{\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r},0,\dots,0\}U^{T},

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=

Proof.

Set
r=\mathrm{rank}A
and

0

Multiplying

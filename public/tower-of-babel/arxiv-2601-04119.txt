Quantitative Constraints for Stable Sampling on the Sphere
Martin Ehler, Karlheinz Gröchenig

Abstract
. We derive quantitative volume constraints for sampling measures μ t \mu_{t} on the unit sphere 𝕊 d \mathbb{S}^{d} that satisfy Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities of order t t . Using precise localization estimates for Jacobi polynomials, we obtain explicit upper and lower bounds on the μ t \mu_{t} -mass of geodesic balls at the natural scale t − 1 t^{-1} . Whereas constants are typically left implicit in the literature, we place special emphasis on fully explicit constants, and the results are genuinely quantitative. Moreover, these bounds yield quantitative constraints for the s s -dimensional Hausdorff volume of Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund sampling sets and, in particular, optimal lower bounds for the length of Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund curves.

Introduction

Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities are often referred to as “sampling inequalities” and form the foundation of
stable sampling, see

Necessary conditions for Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities have been known for some time on

[

,

,

Riemannian manifolds
[

,
26

and on metric measure spaces

[

,
26

or do not appear in density formulations
[

Several quantitative

[
8
,
10
,
20
,
23
]
.
In contrast, the situation for necessary conditions is different, and the quantitative picture has remained incomplete.

In the present work we complete this picture by providing fully quantitative

[

22

To state our results precisely, we now introduce the necessary technical framework.

Sampling inequalities

Proofs via quantitative localization

We remark that the
original proof of (
2
) in
[
5
]
relies on the exactness of the
quadrature rule and does not extend to Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund measures. In this work we
develop a new approach whose key ingredient is a quantitative
localization principle
of reproducing kernels for spherical harmonics at the natural scale

t^{-1}
, which is obtained from explicit estimates of
Jacobi polynomials. Using these well-localized kernels as test
functions in
\Pi_{t}
, we derive the upper volume bound (A.1). The
proofs of (A.2a) and (A.2b) then rely on (A.1).

In the last part, we establish lower volume bounds complementary to
(A.1), and
show that every Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund measure
\mu_{t}
must assign sufficient mass to each
spherical cap,

(3)

\mu_{t}(B_{R})\geq C_{3}\frac{a}{\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}\,,\qquad\text{for }R\geq C_{4}t^{-1}\,,

with constants
C_{3}=C_{3}(d),\,C_{4}=C_{4}(d)
made explicit in Theorem
7.2
.
In particular, this recovers a portion of
[
9
, Theorem 5.5 (b)]
, but now with explicit constants for the sphere.

The bounds (
3
) and (A.1) control the local mass distribution of Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund measures:
(A.1) prevents concentration, and (
3
) excludes large holes
in the sampling manifold. Taken in combination,
they imply that Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund measures quantitatively resemble the normalized surface
measure at scale
t^{-1}
. However, it should be noted that the
constant
C_{4}
grows rapidly with the dimension
d
, cf. (
44
)
in Theorem

emerges only by the explicit tracking of constants.

The analysis of (

[
7

for Jacobi polynomials. These estimates are essential for controlling the off-diagonal decay of the associated kernels with the required precision, which is yet another instance of kernel localization.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we fix notation and
summarize the basic facts about polynomial subspaces on the
sphere.

2.1.
Notation and spherical geometry

The standard inner product in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
is denoted by
x\cdot y
. It induces the norm
\|x\|^{2}=x\cdot x
, so that the unit sphere is
\mathbb{S}^{d}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:\|x\|=1\}
. The standard metric on the sphere measures distance between
x
and
y
in
\mathbb{S}^{d}
by the angle
\phi\in[0,\pi]
satisfying
\cos\phi=x\cdot y
. A closed ball on the sphere of radius
r\in[0,\pi]
centered at
x
is then the spherical cap

B_{r}(x)=\{y\in\mathbb{S}^{d}:x\cdot y\geq\cos r\}\,.

Thus
y\in B_{r}(x)
if and only if the angle
\phi
between
x
and
y
satisfies
0\leq\phi\leq r
.

We endow
\mathbb{S}^{d}
with the normalized surface measure, so that the volume of a subset
S\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{d}
may be denoted by
|S|=\int_{S}1dx
with
|\mathbb{S}^{d}|=1
. The inner product between
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
is written as
\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}f(x)\overline{g(x)}dx
and induces the norm
\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}
.

All functions
f
we consider are spherical polynomials, and we next summarize basic algebraic properties of the space of polynomials on the sphere of degree
t
.

2.2.
Polynomials on the sphere and reproducing kernels

The subspace
\Pi_{t}\subseteq L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})
consists of all algebraic polynomials in
d+1
variables of degree at most
t\in\mathbb{N}
restricted to the sphere. Its dimension is

(4)

\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}=\binom{d+t}{t}+\binom{d+t-1}{t-1}=\binom{d+t}{t}\,\frac{2t+d}{t+d}\,.

Let
k^{t}:\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
denote the reproducing kernel of
\Pi_{t}
. According to the convention
k^{t}_{x}(y):=k^{t}(x,y)
, the kernel reproduces every
f\in\Pi_{t}
by

f(x)=\langle f,k^{t}_{x}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\,.

Since the space
\Pi_{t}
is orthogonally invariant, so is its reproducing kernel
k^{t}
. It can be expressed by Jacobi polynomials
P^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{t}
that are orthogonal
on
[-1,1]
with respect to the weight function
(1-u)^{\alpha}(1+u)^{\beta}
and
normalized by
†
†

†

We use the Gamma-function extension of the binomial coefficient and set
\binom{x}{y}=\frac{\Gamma(x+1)}{\Gamma(y+1)\Gamma(x-y+1)}

for real
x,y
whenever the right-hand side is finite.

P^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{t}(1)=\binom{t+\alpha}{t}\,.

Using the (re-)normalized Jacobi polynomials

\widetilde{P}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{t}:=P^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{t}/P^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{t}(1)

for the parameters
(\alpha,\beta)=(\frac{d}{2},\frac{d}{2}-1)
, the reproducing kernel has the clean representation

(5)

k^{t}(x,y)=\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}\,\widetilde{P}_{t}^{(\frac{d}{2},\frac{d}{2}-1)}(x\cdot y)\,,\qquad x,y\in\mathbb{S}^{d}\,,

see
[
30
, (4.31)]
. The
L^{2}
-norm of the polynomial
k^{t}_{x}\in\Pi_{t}
is

(6)

\|k^{t}_{x}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}=\langle k^{t}_{x},k^{t}_{x}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}=k^{t}(x,x)=\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}\,,\qquad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{S}^{d}\,.

Instead of
k^{t}
, it is sometimes more convenient to directly use the normalized Jacobi polynomial

f^{t}(x,y):=\widetilde{P}_{t}^{(\frac{d}{2},\frac{d}{2}-1)}(x\cdot y)=\frac{k^{t}(x,y)}{\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}

that satisfies
f^{t}(x,x)=1
and

(7)

\|f^{t}_{x}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}\,.

Having established these algebraic features, we now shift towards analytic properties of Jacobi polynomials.

3.
Localization of the Jacobi polynomials

In this section we quantify the localization properties of the Jacobi polynomials that generate the reproducing kernel
k^{t}
of
\Pi_{t}
, cf. (
5
). These estimates capture how strongly the kernel concentrates near the diagonal and provide the key analytic input for the volume bounds established in the following sections. Although not strictly needed for our purposes, we present the arguments for the full range of parameters
\alpha,\beta
.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
\alpha\geq\beta>-1
and
t\in\mathbb{N}
, then we have

(8)

\Big|\widetilde{P}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{t}(\cos\phi)-1\Big|\leq\frac{t\,(t+\alpha+\beta+1)}{2(\alpha+1)}\,\phi^{2}\,.

Proof.

For
t=0
, the inequality is trivially satisfied due to
\widetilde{P}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{0}\equiv 1
. We now suppose that
t\geq 1
and define the function
p(\phi)=\widetilde{P}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{t}(\cos\phi)
. The mean value theorem of differential calculus yields

\displaystyle|p(\phi)-p(0)|

\displaystyle\leq|\phi|\sup_{|\theta|\leq|\phi|}|p^{\prime}(\theta)|

\displaystyle=|\phi|\sup_{|\theta|\leq|\phi|}\Big|\big(\widetilde{P}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{t}\big)^{\prime}(\cos\theta)\,\,\sin\theta\Big|\,.

Since
p(0)=\widetilde{P}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{t}(1)=1
and
|\sin\theta|\leq|\theta|
, we have

(9)

\Big|\widetilde{P}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{t}(\cos\phi)-1\Big|\leq|\phi|^{2}\sup_{|\theta|\leq|\phi|}\Big|\big(\widetilde{P}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{t}\big)^{\prime}(\cos\theta)\Big|\,.

The derivative of
P^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{t}
is the scaled Jacobi polynomial

\big(P^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{t}\big)^{\prime}=\frac{t+\alpha+\beta+1}{2}P_{t-1}^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}\,,

see
[
32
, 18.9.15]
. If
\alpha+1\geq\beta+1>-1
and

\max(\alpha+1,\beta+1)\geq-\frac{1}{2}
, then, according to
[
31
, Theorem 7.32.1]
, the maximum of
\big|P_{t-1}^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}\big|
on
[-1,1]
is attained in
1
, i.e.,

\max_{u\in[-1,1]}\big|P_{t-1}^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}(u)\big|=P_{t-1}^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}(1)=\binom{t+\alpha}{t-1}\,.

Therefore, the derivative of the normalized Jacobi polynomial satisfies

\displaystyle\Big|\big(\widetilde{P}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{t}\big)^{\prime}(\cos\theta)\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\frac{1}{\binom{t+\alpha}{t}}\,\frac{t+\alpha+\beta+1}{2}P_{t-1}^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}(\cos\theta)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\frac{(t+\alpha+\beta+1)\binom{t+\alpha}{t-1}}{2\binom{t+\alpha}{t}}\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\frac{t\,(t+\alpha+\beta+1)}{2(\alpha+1)}\Big|\,.

We substitute this estimate into (
9
) and obtain the claim (
8
).

In the case
(\alpha,\beta)=(\frac{d}{2},\frac{d}{2}-1)

the Jacobi polynomial induces the reproducing kernel

k^{t}(x,y)=\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}\,\widetilde{P}^{(\frac{d}{2},\frac{d}{2}-1)}_{t}(x\cdot y)
, see (
5
). We now introduce a
radius
r
, so that the normalized Jacobi polynomial stays above a
target level
\nu
for all angles
\phi\leq r
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\nu\in(0,1)
. If the radius
r=r(d,t,\nu)>0
is chosen by

(10)

r^{2}=(1-\nu)\frac{(d+2)}{t(t+d)}\,,

then the normalized Jacobi polynomial obeys the lower bound

\widetilde{P}^{(\frac{d}{2},\frac{d}{2}-1)}_{t}(\cos\phi)\geq\nu\,\qquad\forall\,0\leq\phi\leq r\,.

This proposition provides a uniform lower bound on the
reproducing kernel
k^{t}
near the diagonal and shows that the natural
scale is of the order
t^{-1}
.

Proof.

According to Lemma
3.1
, we obtain
\widetilde{P}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{t}(\cos\phi)\geq\nu
for
0\leq\phi\leq r
by choosing
r>0
as the solution of the equation

1-\frac{t\,(t+\alpha+\beta+1)}{2(\alpha+1)}\,r^{2}=\nu\,.

This leads to

r^{2}=(1-\nu)\frac{2(\alpha+1)}{t(t+\alpha+\beta+1)}\,,

and
(\alpha,\beta)=(\frac{d}{2},\frac{d}{2}-1)
yields

as in (

4.
Consequences of Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities

In this section we extract quantitative consequences of the Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities. The resulting mass and support constraints form the basis for the length and volume estimates developed in the subsequent sections.

Recall that a Borel measure
\mu_{t}
on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}
is called a Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund measure if there exist constants
a,A>0
such that

a\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{lower}}}{{\leq}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}|f(x)|^{2}\,d\mu_{t}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{upper}}}{{\leq}}A\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}\qquad\text{for all }f\in\Pi_{t}\,.

We refer to these estimates as the lower and upper Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities. Measures satisfying the upper inequality are also called Carleson measures in complex analysis.

4.1.
Consequences of the upper Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality

For a measure
\mu_{t}
satisfying the upper Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality, we will derive an upper bound on
\mu_{t}\big(B_{r}(x)\big)
.

Such an upper bound can be derived by a simple idea: for
every closed, nonempty subset
S\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{d}
and every

f\in\Pi_{t}
, the following chain of inequalities holds,

\displaystyle\min_{y\in S}\big(|f(y)|^{2}\big)\,\mu_{t}(S)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{S}|f(y)|^{2}\,d\mu_{t}(y)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}|f(y)|^{2}\,d\mu_{t}(y)

\displaystyle\leq A\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}\,,

where the last estimate is due to the upper Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality. Therefore, the
volume
\mu_{t}(S)
must obey

(11)

\mu_{t}(S)\leq\frac{A\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}}{\min_{y\in S}|f(y)|^{2}}\qquad\forall f\in\Pi_{t}\,.

The right-hand side does not depend on
\mu_{t}
and may constitute a
reasonable bound if we feed in a polynomial
f\in\Pi_{t}
that
is well-localized in
S
. The natural choice of
S
is a spherical cap
B_{r}(x)
.

Theorem 4.1

(upper volume bounds)
.

Let
\nu\in(0,1)
and assume that
\mu_{t}
satisfies the upper Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality of order
t
. If the radius
r>0
is chosen by

(12)

r^{2}=(1-\nu)\frac{(d+2)}{t(t+d)}\,,

then the upper volume bound

(13)

\mu_{t}\big(B_{r}(x)\big)\leq\frac{A}{\nu^{2}\,\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}

holds for all
x\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
.

Theorem (
4.1
) can be read on different levels. Extracting

\nu
from (
12
) yields

\nu=1-r^{2}\frac{t(t+d)}{d+2}\,,

so that the upper bound is just

\mu_{t}\big(B_{r}(x)\big)\leq\frac{A}{\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}\,\frac{1}{(1-r^{2}\frac{t(t+d)}{d+2})^{2}}\,.

This inequality holds for all
r
for which

\nu\in(0,1)
, but it is meaningful only for small
r
or
\nu

close to
1
.
By choosing
r=\rho\sqrt{d}/(2t)
, the right-hand side is bounded
by
\frac{A}{\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}(1-2\rho^{2})^{-2}
. Thus,
at the scale
r\asymp t^{-1}
, the resulting upper volume bound is of
order
A/t^{d}
. This substantially sharpens the necessary quadrature
bounds in
[
30
, Section 6.5]
, which treat only the special
case
a=A=1
for finitely supported measures and without explicit
constants. Our result applies to a far more general measure-theoretic
setting, only the one-sided upper Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality is needed, and it
provides fully quantitative bounds.

The proof is based on choosing the kernel
k^{t}_{x}=\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}\widetilde{P}^{(\frac{d}{2},\frac{d}{2}-1)}_{t}(x\cdot y)
or
equivalently the Jacobi polynomial

f^{t}_{x}(y)=\widetilde{P}^{(\frac{d}{2},\frac{d}{2}-1)}_{t}(x\cdot y)
for
the right-hand side of (
11
).

Proof.

Let
f^{t}(x,y)=\widetilde{P}^{(\frac{d}{2},\frac{d}{2}-1)}_{t}(x\cdot y)
. By (
7
), the
L^{2}
-norm of
f^{t}_{x}
is

\|f^{t}_{x}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}\,.

For
\cos\phi=x\cdot y
, the condition
y\in B_{r}(x)
translates to
0\leq\phi\leq r
.
By Proposition
3.2
we have the lower bound

(14)

|f^{t}_{x}(y)|=|\widetilde{P}^{(\frac{d}{2},\frac{d}{2}-1)}_{t}(\cos\phi)|\geq\nu\,,\qquad\forall\,y\in B_{r}(x)\,.

Thus, (
11
) leads to
\mu_{t}\big(B_{r}(x)\big)\leq\frac{A}{\nu^{2}\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}
.

Near-optimal choice

The reproducing kernel is a nearly optimal choice in (
11
) when compared to the best value

\min_{f\in\Pi_{t}}\,\frac{A\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}}{\min_{y\in S}|f(y)|^{2}}\geq\frac{A}{\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}\,.

To see this, fix
S\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{d}
and let
f\in\Pi_{t}
be arbitrary. Since
\min_{y\in S}|f(y)|\leq|f(x)|
for all
x\in S
and

|f(x)|^{2}=|\langle f,k_{x}^{t}\rangle|^{2}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}
, we always have

\displaystyle\frac{A\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}}{\min_{y\in S}|f(y)|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{A\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}}{|f(x)|^{2}}\geq\frac{A}{\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}\,.

By comparison, for small spherical caps
B_{r}(x)
such that
r
is correlated to
\nu\in(0,1)
, Theorem
4.1

yields the bound
\frac{A}{\nu^{2}\,\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}
. In the context of the
estimate (
11
), the kernel
k^{t}_{x}
(or
equivalently
f^{t}_{x}
) is therefore a nearly optimal choice for achieving the upper bound with respect to the spherical cap
B_{r}(x)
.

Sub-optimal choice

Another natural choice for
f
in (
11
) is

(15)

K^{t}(x,y)=K^{t}_{x}(y)=(x\cdot y)^{t}\,.

It peaks at
y=x
and is orthogonally invariant, just as
k^{t}
is. Since the surface measure on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
is rotation-invariant, the
L^{2}
-norm of
K^{t}_{x}
does not depend on the specific choice of
x\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
and we may define

(16)

\Lambda_{d,t}:=\|K^{t}_{x}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}(x\cdot y)^{2t}\,dy\,.

For all
y\in B_{r}(x)
, we have
x\cdot y\geq\cos r
and therefore

|K^{t}_{x}(y)|^{2}\geq(\cos r)^{2t}
. Hence, substituting

f=K^{t}_{x}
in (
11
), leads to

(17)

\mu_{t}\big(B_{r}(x)\big)\leq\frac{A\,\Lambda_{d,t}}{(\cos r)^{2t}}\,.

We still need to compare
\Lambda_{d,t}
with the factor
1/\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}
in Theorem
4.1
. Repeated application of the Laplace operator in spherical coordinates, as executed in
[
1
]
for instance, leads to

\Lambda_{d,t}=\frac{1\cdot 3\cdots(2t-1)}{(d+1)(d+3)\cdots(d+2t-1)}=\frac{(\tfrac{1}{2})_{t}}{(\frac{d+1}{2})_{t}}\,,

where
(u)_{t}
is the Pochhammer symbol
(u)_{t}=\frac{\Gamma(t+u)}{\Gamma(u)}=u(u+1)\cdots(u+t-1)
. Let us fix the dimension
d
, consider large
t
, and choose
r\asymp t^{-1}
, which matches the choice in (
12
). Since

\Lambda_{d,t}=\frac{\Gamma(t+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{d+1}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(t+\frac{d+1}{2})}\sim\frac{\Gamma(\frac{d+1}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})}t^{-\frac{d}{2}}\,,

whereas
1/\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}\sim\frac{2}{d!}t^{-d}
, (
13
) in Theorem
4.1
is orders of magnitude sharper than (
17
). This shows that the kernel
K^{t}
is not sufficiently localized for the approach (
11
) at scale
t^{-1}
. Nonetheless, its simplicity also has benefits that we will use next.

4.2.
Elementary consequences of the lower Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality

We now verify necessary support conditions for the lower Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality to hold. Here, the simplicity of
K^{t}
in (
15
) is advantageous and it is therefore used to generate test functions in
\Pi_{t}
.

Recall the moments
\Lambda_{d,t}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}(x\cdot y)^{2t}\,dy
of the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
from (
16
). For
t=1
, we obtain
\Lambda_{d,1}=\frac{1}{d+1}
, and
g(y)=(x\cdot y)^{2}
leads to
\Lambda_{d,t}^{1/t}=\|g\|_{L^{t}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}
. To estimate
\Lambda_{d,t}^{1/t}
, for
1\leq t\leq\infty
, the Hölder inequality yields

\frac{1}{d+1}=\Lambda_{d,1}=\|g\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\leq\|g\|_{L^{t}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\leq\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}=1\,.

Consequently,
\Lambda_{d,t}^{1/t}
is sandwiched between

(18)

\frac{1}{d+1}\leq\Lambda_{d,t}^{1/t}\leq 1\,.

and satisfies
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\Lambda_{d,t}^{1/t}=1
.

We derive the following elementary covering property of Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund measures. It implies that the support of Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund measures cannot be contained in a spherical cap that is too small.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that
\mu_{t}
satisfies the lower Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality of order
t
and let
r\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
. If
\supp\mu_{t}\subseteq B_{r}(x)
, then

(19)

(\sin r)^{2t}\geq\frac{a}{\mu_{t}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\,\Lambda_{d,t}\,.

In other words, if (
19
) is not satisfied, then the support of
\mu_{t}
cannot be contained in any ball of radius
r
. Also
notice the requirement
0<r\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
, so that Lemma
4.2
does not apply to the complementary cap
\overline{\mathbb{S}^{d}\setminus B_{r}(x)}=B_{\pi-r}(-x)
.

Proof.

We first assume that
z=e_{d+1}
is the north pole and
\supp\mu_{t}\subseteq B_{r}(e_{d+1})
.
Consider
f(y)=y_{1}^{t}
, so that the lower Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund bound yields

(20)

a\,\Lambda_{d,t}\leq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}|y_{1}|^{2t}\,d\mu_{t}(y)\,,\qquad\forall\,t\in\mathbb{N}\,.

Since
y\in B_{r}(e_{d+1})
implies
y_{d+1}\geq\cos r
and
\cos r\geq 0
for
0<r\leq\pi/2
, we deduce
y_{d+1}^{2}\geq(\cos r)^{2}
and, hence,

|y_{1}|^{2}\leq 1-|y_{d+1}|^{2}\leq 1-(\cos r)^{2}=(\sin r)^{2}\,.

Therefore, on
\supp\mu_{t}
the function
|y_{1}|^{2t}
is bounded by
(\sin r)^{2t}
, and we obtain

\displaystyle a\Lambda_{d,t}\leq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}|y_{1}|^{2t}\,d\mu_{t}(y)\leq(\sin r)^{2t}\mu_{t}(\mathbb{S}^{d})\,.

This directly translates into
(\sin r)^{2t}\geq\frac{a}{\mu_{t}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\Lambda_{d,t}
.

For the general case
x\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
, we apply a rotation
O
such that
Oe_{d+1}=x
. The push-forward measure
\tilde{\mu}_{t}:=O_{*}\mu_{t}
is defined by
\tilde{\mu}_{t}(S)=\mu_{t}(O^{*}S)
for every measurable subset
S\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{d}
. The condition
\supp\mu_{t}\subseteq B_{r}(x)
directly implies
\supp\tilde{\mu}_{t}\subseteq B_{r}(e_{d+1})
. Since the space
\Pi_{t}
is orthogonally invariant,
\tilde{\mu}_{t}
also satisfies the lower Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality with the same constant
a
. Our above analysis now implies the condition on
r
.

5.
Bounds on the length of Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund curves

We specialize the upper volume bounds in Theorem
4.1
to measures induced by curves and derive quantitative lower bounds on the length of Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund curves.

A curve is a continuous, piecewise smooth function
\gamma:[0,L]\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{d}
that we may consider as a space curve in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
. We always assume that
\gamma
is a closed curve, i.e.,
\gamma(0)=\gamma(L)
, and parametrized by arc-length, so that the speed
\|\dot{\gamma}\|
equals
1
almost everywhere, the length satisfies
\ell(\gamma)=L
, and the line integral is

\int_{\gamma}f:=\int_{0}^{L}f(\gamma(u))\,du\,.

In the following we also consider a weighted version. For a positive integer
t
, let
w_{t}
be a nonnegative weight function that is defined and integrable on the trajectory of the curve
\gamma_{t}
. Every weighted curve
(\gamma_{t},w_{t})
induces a measure
\mu_{t}
satisfying
\mu_{t}(B_{r}(x))=\frac{1}{\ell(\gamma_{t})}\int_{\gamma_{t}}1_{B_{r}(x)}w_{t}
and

(21)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}|f(x)|^{2}\,d\mu_{t}=\frac{1}{\ell(\gamma_{t})}\int_{\gamma_{t}}|f|^{2}w_{t}\,.

This enables us to define Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund curves.

Definition 5.1
.

We say that
(\gamma_{t},w_{t})
is a (weighted) Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund curve (of order
t
or for
\Pi_{t}
) if

a\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{lower}}}{{\leq}}\frac{1}{\ell(\gamma_{t})}\int_{\gamma_{t}}|f|^{2}w_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{upper}}}{{\leq}}A\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}

holds for all
f\in\Pi_{t}
.

For related concepts, see
[
5
,
6
,
18
]
.
The upper volume bound in Theorem
4.1
and the support
estimates in Lemma
4.2
lead to the following lower bound
on the length of a Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund curve.

Theorem 5.1

(weighted curves)
.

Assume
t\geq d\geq 2
. If
(\gamma_{t},w_{t})
is a weighted Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund curve of order
t
on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
, then its length must satisfy

(22)

\ell(\gamma_{t})\geq\frac{\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}{t}\,\frac{\sqrt{d+2}}{4}\,\Big(\frac{a}{A}\Big)^{1+\frac{1}{2t}}\,.

Since the dimension
\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}=\binom{d+t}{t}+\binom{d+t-1}{t-1}
is bounded by

(23)

\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}=\frac{(t+1)\cdots(t+d)}{d!}+\frac{t\cdots(t+d-1)}{d!}\geq\frac{2}{d!}t^{d}\,,

the main estimate (
22
) leads to
\ell(\gamma_{t})\gtrsim t^{d-1}
with the quantitative version

(24)

\ell(\gamma_{t})\geq\Big(\frac{a}{A}\Big)^{1+\frac{1}{2t}}\,\frac{\sqrt{d+2}}{2\,d!}\,t^{d-1}\,.

As an important special case, we formulate the following concequence.

Corollary 5.2
.

Assume
t\geq d\geq 2
. If
\gamma_{t}
is a
t
-design curve, then

\ell(\gamma_{t})\geq\frac{\sqrt{d+2}}{2\,d!}\,t^{d-1}\,.

Proof of Corollary
5.2
.

Any
t
-design curve has Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund constants
a=A=1
, so that (
24
) provides the estimate.

This was proved in
[
5
]
for a generic constant with a
different method that requires exact quadrature. The novelty here is
the explicit constant that shows
the dependence of the dimension.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

We will apply Theorem
4.1
and use the relation

r^{2}=(1-\nu)\frac{(d+2)}{t(t+d)}
from (
12
), with a value of
r=r(d,t,\nu)\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
to be determined by
\nu\in(0,1)
.

Consequences of the upper volume bound:

The upper volume bound in Theorem
4.1
leads to

\mu_{t}(B_{r}(x))=\frac{1}{\ell(\gamma_{t})}\int_{\gamma_{t}}1_{B_{r}(x)}w_{t}\leq\frac{A}{\nu^{2}\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}\,,

which yields the inequality

(25)

\ell(\gamma_{t})\geq\frac{\nu^{2}\,\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}{A}\int_{\gamma_{t}}1_{B_{r}(x)}w_{t}\,.

It remains to find an estimate on
\int_{\gamma_{t}}1_{B_{r}(x)}w_{t}
for some
x\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
.
Next, we determine a radius
r
by choosing
\nu
such that
\gamma_{t}
is not contained in
B_{r}(x)
.

Determine
r
by choosing
\nu
such that
\gamma_{t}\not\subseteq B_{r}(x)
:

To ensure that
\gamma_{t}
is not contained in any spherical cap
B_{r}(x)
, we must make sure that the radius
r=r(d,t,\nu)
violates (
19
) in Lemma
4.2
, i.e.,
we need
(\sin r)^{2t}<\frac{a}{\mu_{t}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\,\Lambda_{d,t}
,
where
\Lambda_{d,t}
is as in (
16
). Since
(\sin r)^{2t}\leq r^{2t}
,
\Lambda_{d,t}\geq\big(\frac{1}{d+1}\big)^{t}
by (
18
), and the upper Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality yields

\mu_{t}(\mathbb{S}^{d})\leq A
, it suffices to satisfy
the slightly stronger inequality

r^{2t}<\frac{a}{A}\,\Big(\frac{1}{d+1}\Big)^{t}\,.

This leads to the requirement

\displaystyle r^{2}=(1-\nu)\frac{(d+2)}{\big(t(t+d)\big)}<\Big(\frac{a}{A}\Big)^{1/t}\,\frac{1}{d+1}\,,

which is equivalent to

(26)

1-\nu<\Big(\frac{a}{A}\Big)^{1/t}\,\,\frac{t(t+d)}{(d+2)(d+1)}\,.

For

t\geq d\geq 2
, we obtain

(27)

\frac{t(t+d)}{(d+2)(d+1)}\geq\frac{2}{3}\,,

and we may now choose
\nu
as

1-\nu=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{a}{A}\Big)^{1/t}\,.

For this value of
\nu
we know that
(\sin r)^{2t}<\frac{a}{\mu_{t}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\,\Lambda_{d,t}
. Hence,
\gamma_{t}
cannot be contained in any ball of radius
r
.

Find
x_{t}
such that
\int_{\gamma_{t}}1_{B_{r}(x_{t})}w_{t}\geq 2ra
:

Let
x
lie on the trajectory of
\gamma_{t}
. Since
\gamma_{t}
is a closed curve not contained in
B_{r}(x)
, it must enter and leave the ball, hence, at least a piece of length
2r
must be contained in
B_{r}(x)
. If
w_{t}\equiv 1
, then we are done, because
\int_{\gamma_{t}}1_{B_{r}(x_{t})}w_{t}\geq 2r
. If
w_{t}
is not constant, however, it could be rather small on that particular segment and we cannot immediately deduce
\int_{\gamma}1_{B_{r}(x)}w_{t}\geq 2r
.
In the following we will argue that there must exist at least one
x_{t}
on the trajectory of
\gamma_{t}
such that
\int_{\gamma_{t}}1_{B_{r}(x_{t})}w_{t}
is sufficiently big.

Recall that
L=\ell(\gamma_{t})
and let us extend the arc-length parametrization of the closed curve
\gamma_{t}
periodically beyond the interval
[0,L]
. We also regard the characteristic function
1_{[-r,r]}
as an
L
-periodic functions and now define
v:[0,L]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by the cyclic convolution
v=(w_{t}\circ\gamma_{t})*1_{[-r,r]}
given by

v(u):=\int_{0}^{L}w_{t}(\gamma_{t}(p))\,1_{[-r,r]}(p-u)\,dp\,.

Integration over
[0,L]
and
2r\leq L
lead to

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}v(u)\,du

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}w_{t}(\gamma_{t}(p))\,1_{[-r,r]}(p-u)\,dp\,du

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{L}w_{t}(\gamma_{t}(p))\int_{0}^{L}\,1_{[-r,r]}(p-u)\,du\,dp

\displaystyle=2r\,\int_{0}^{L}w_{t}(\gamma_{t}(p))\,dp

\displaystyle=2r\int_{\gamma_{t}}w_{t}\,.

The lower Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality implies
\int_{\gamma_{t}}w_{t}\geq a\,L
, so
that we arrive at
\int_{0}^{L}v(u)\,du\geq 2raL
. Thus, there is

u_{t}\in[0,L]
such that
v(u_{t})\geq 2ra
. We now select

x_{t}:=\gamma_{t}(u_{t})
. Since
\gamma_{t}(p)\in B_{r}(x_{t})
for

p\in[u_{t}-r,u_{t}+r]
and
w_{t}\geq 0
, we obtain

(28)

\int_{\gamma_{t}}1_{B_{r}(x_{t})}w_{t}\geq\int_{u_{t}-r}^{u_{t}+r}w_{t}(\gamma_{t}(p))\,dp=v(u_{t})\geq 2ra\,.

Final estimates:

With the specified choice of
r
and
\nu
the lower bound
(
25
) turns into

(29)

\ell(\gamma_{t})\geq\nu^{2}\,\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}\,\frac{a}{A}\,\,2r.

Since
\frac{a}{A}\leq 1
, we have

\nu^{2}=\big(1-\frac{1}{2}\big(\frac{a}{A}\big)^{1/t}\big)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{4}
and therefore

\displaystyle r^{2}

\displaystyle=(1-\nu)\,\frac{d+2}{t(t+d)}=\frac{1}{t^{2}}(1-\nu)\,\frac{d+2}{1+d/t}\geq\frac{1}{t^{2}}\frac{(1-\nu)}{2}\,(d+2)\,.

We now insert
r
into the bound (
29
) and
substitute
1-\nu=\frac{1}{2}\big(\frac{a}{A}\big)^{1/t}
to get

\displaystyle\ell(\gamma_{t})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\frac{\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}{t}\,\frac{a}{A}\,2\sqrt{\frac{(1-\nu)}{2}(d+2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\frac{\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}{t}\,\frac{a}{A}\,\sqrt{d+2}\,\Big(\frac{a}{A}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2t}}\,,

which concludes the proof.

For the asymptotics we view the dimension
d
as fixed and let the
degree
t

grow. However, large
t

is not essential for the bounds; the proof goes through with slightly
different constants, as is indicated by the extreme case
t=1
of linear functions.

Remark 5.1
.

If
(\gamma_{1},w_{1})
is a weighted Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund curve of order
1
on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
, then its length must satisfy

(30)

\ell(\gamma_{1})\geq 2\,\sqrt{d+1}\,\frac{d+1}{d+2}\,\Big(\frac{a}{A}\Big)^{3/2}\,.

To see this, we may follow the proof of Theorem
5.1
with minor adjustments according to
t=1
. First, we have
\dim\Pi_{1}=d+2
and define the radius
r
as usual by
r^{2}=(1-\nu)\frac{(d+2)}{t(t+d)}=(1-\nu)\frac{d+2}{d+1}
. For an arbitrary parameter
\delta\in(0,1)
, we choose
1-\nu=\delta\,\frac{a}{A}\,\frac{1}{d+2}
. Then (
26
) is satisfied for
t=1
. Consequently,
\gamma_{1}
is not contained in any ball of radius
r
, where
r^{2}=\delta\,\frac{a}{A}\,\frac{1}{d+1}
.
Thus, the inequality (
29
) holds, i.e.,

\ell(\gamma_{1})\geq\nu^{2}\,(d+2)\,\frac{a}{A}\,\,2r\,.

Since
\nu^{2}=(1-\delta\,\frac{a}{A}\,\frac{1}{d+2})^{2}\geq\big(\frac{d+1}{d+2}\big)^{2}
and
r=\sqrt{\delta\,\frac{a}{A}\,\frac{1}{d+1}}
, we eventually derive

\displaystyle\ell(\gamma_{1})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(d+1)^{3/2}}{d+2}\,\Big(\frac{a}{A}\Big)^{3/2}\,\,2\sqrt{\delta}\,.

Since this inequality holds for all
\delta\in(0,1)
, we obtain (
30
).

6.
Sets of arbitrary Hausdorff dimension

While piecewise smooth curves
have Hausdorff dimension
1
, one may also consider measures that are
supported on

s
-dimensional subsets of the sphere. In this section we establish
lower bounds on their Hausdorff measure for arbitrary dimension
s<d
.

6.1.
s
-dimensional Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund sets

For
0\leq s\leq d
, we denote the
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure by
h_{s}
.
Every
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measurable subset
X_{t}\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{d}

with positive, finite Hausdorff measure
0<h_{s}(X_{t})<\infty
and an
integrable weight function
w_{t}:X_{t}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
induces a
measure
\mu_{t}
on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
defined by
\mu_{t}(B_{r}(z))=\frac{1}{h_{s}(X_{t})}\int_{X_{t}\cap B_{r}(z)}w_{t}(x)\,dh_{s}(x)
and

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}|f(x)|^{2}\,d\mu_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{h_{s}(X_{t})}\int_{X_{t}}|f(x)|^{2}w_{t}(x)\,dh_{s}(x)\,.

This enables us to define Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund sets
X_{t}
.

Definition 6.1
.

We say that an
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measurable subset
X_{t}\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{d}
with weight function
w_{t}:X_{t}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
satisfies the Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities (of order
t
or for
\Pi_{t}
) if

a\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{lower}}}{{\leq}}\frac{1}{h_{s}(X_{t})}\int_{X_{t}}|f(x)|^{2}w_{t}(x)\,dh_{s}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{upper}}}{{\leq}}A\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d})}^{2}

holds for all
f\in\Pi_{t}
.

For such Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund sets we can prove an extension of
Theorem
5.1
to the whole range of Hausdorff
dimensions
0\leq s\leq d
.

Theorem 6.1

(
s
-dimensional sets)
.

Assume that
X_{t}\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{d}
is an
s
-dimensional Hausdorff
measurable subset with weight function
w_{t}\geq 0
satisfying the
upper Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality of order
t
. If there exists a point
x_{t}\in X_{t}
, parameters
\nu\in(0,1)
and
q>0
, such that

(31)

\int_{X_{t}\cap B_{r}(x_{t})}w_{t}(x)\,dh_{s}(x)\geq q\,r^{s}\,,\qquad r^{2}=(1-\nu)\frac{(d+2)}{t(t+d)}\,,

then the Hausdorff volume of
X_{t}
satisfies

h_{s}(X_{t})\geq c\,\frac{\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}{t^{s}}\,,

with the “constant”
c=c(d,t,q,\nu,A,s)=\frac{q\,\nu^{2}}{A}\big(\frac{1-\nu}{1+d/t}\,(d+2)\big)^{s/2}
.

In case
t=1
, we have
c=\frac{q\,\nu^{2}}{A}\big((1-\nu)\frac{d+2}{d+1}\big)^{s/2}
.
For the standard case
t\geq d
, we can replace
c
by its lower
estimate
c\geq\frac{q\,\nu^{2}}{A}\big(\frac{1-\nu}{2}\,(d+2)\big)^{s/2}
.

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

The radius
r
is as needed in Theorem
4.1
when applied to
\mu_{t}
, and we obtain

\mu_{t}(B_{r}(x_{t}))=\frac{1}{h_{s}(X_{t})}\int_{X_{t}\cap B_{r}(x_{t})}w_{t}(x)\,dh_{s}(x)\leq\frac{A}{\nu^{2}\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}\,.

The assumption (
31
) leads to

\displaystyle h_{s}(X_{t})

\displaystyle\geq\int_{X_{t}\cap B_{r}(x_{t})}w_{t}(x)\,dh_{s}(x)\,\frac{\nu^{2}\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}{A}

\displaystyle\geq q\,r^{s}\,\frac{\nu^{2}\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}{A}\,.

Since
r=\frac{1}{t}\sqrt{(1-\nu)\frac{d+2}{1+d/t}}
,
we may define the constant
c:=\frac{q\,\nu^{2}}{A}\big(\frac{1-\nu}{1+d/t}\,(d+2)\big)^{s/2}
and deduce

\displaystyle h_{s}(X_{t})

\displaystyle\geq c\,\frac{\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}{t^{s}}\,,

which concludes the proof.

Remark 6.1
.

The technical condition (
31
) is related to the notion
of Ahlfors regularity from geometric measure theory.
A Hausdorff measurable subset
X\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{d}
with weight function
w:X\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is called lower Ahlfors
s
-regular if there is a constant
q>0
such that

(32)

\int_{X\cap B_{r}(x)}w(y)\,dh_{s}(y)\geq q\,r^{s}\,,\qquad\forall\,x\in X\,,\quad 0<r<\diam(X)\,.

Note, however, that we need this condition only at a single point

x_{t}\in X_{t}
and for a single radius
r
. Since
q,\nu,r
are not independent, one could further optimize the
constant
c
subject to
qr^{s}=\mathrm{const}
and to the relation
between
r
and
\nu
.

The regularity is automatically satisfied for point sets
and curves.

Remark 6.2

(points)
.

For a finite point set
X_{t}
, the maximum
w_{\max}=\max_{x\in X_{t}}w_{t}(x)
of the weights is attained at
x_{\max}\in X_{t}
such that
w_{\max}=w_{t}(x_{\max})
. Then
X_{t}
with weight function
w_{t}
is lower Ahlfors
0
-regular at
x_{\max}
for
q=w_{\max}
and all
r\in(0,\pi]
.

Remark 6.3

(curves)
.

For
s=1
, the
Hausdorff measure
h_{1}
corresponds to the arc-length and

\frac{\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}{t}\asymp t^{d-1}
. This is consistent with the results
for Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund curves in Theorem
5.1
.

In light of Section
5
, the regularity assumption follows for curves directly from the lower Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality. Indeed, the proof of Theorem
5.1
shows that the lower Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality forces the weighted curve to be lower Ahlfors
1
-regular at least at some point
x_{t}
for
q=2a
, cf. (
28
), with the radius
r
specified in (
12
) of Theorem
4.1
.

6.2.
Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund points

Let us now consider the case
s=0
more closely. If a finite subset
X_{t}\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{d}
with weight function
w_{t}
satisfies the
lower
Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality of order
t
, then an elementary and well-known
dimension count yields that the cardinality of
X_{t}
must obey

(33)

\#X_{t}\geq\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}\,.

The quotient
\frac{\#X_{t}}{\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}
is usually referred to as the oversampling factor.

Typically, the
lower
Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities are widely viewed as the
more substantial part, so that the
upper
Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund bound often
receives comparatively little attention in the literature. In contrast
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
or other non-compact manifolds, the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}
is compact and hence a confined space, in which the local
density of
upper
Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund points cannot exceed the (scaled) oversampling
factor
\frac{\#X_{t}}{\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}\,A
. This also provides a lower bound on the cardinality.

Corollary 6.2
.

If a finite subset
X_{t}\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{d}
with weight function
w_{t}:X_{t}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
satisfies the upper Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality of order
t
, then

w_{\max}=\max_{x\in X_{t}}w_{t}(x)\leq\frac{\#X_{t}}{\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}\,A\,.

Equivalently, it must hold

(34)

\#X_{t}\geq\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}\,\frac{w_{\max}}{A}\,.

Thus, we have a lower bound on the cardinality comparable to
(
33
). It is remarkable that we need not assume the lower Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality.

Proof.

As mentioned in Remark
6.2
, the Ahlfors regularity assumption in the point
x_{\max}
with
w_{\max}=w_{t}(x_{\max})
is always satisfied for
q=w_{\max}
and all
r\in(0,\pi]
. Thus, Theorem
6.1
leads to

\#X_{t}\geq\frac{w_{\max}\,\nu^{2}}{A}\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}\,,

for all
\nu\in(0,1)
. The inequality must then also be valid for
\nu=1
, so that

w_{\max}\leq\frac{\#X_{t}}{\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}\,A

must hold.

7.
Lower volume bounds

This section complements the upper volume bounds of Theorem

4.1
by (almost) matching lower bounds. The arguments are
more involved and we will use precise
off-diagonal decay estimates of the kernel.

As a preparation, we notice that the integral over a
rotation-invariant function on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
can be reduced to a
one-dimensional integral via the formula
[
30
]

(35)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}f(x\cdot y)\,dy=\frac{\vol(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}{\vol(\mathbb{S}^{d})}\int_{0}^{\pi}f(\cos\theta)\,(\sin\theta)^{d-1}\,d\theta\,,

where
\vol(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}):=\frac{2\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2)}

denotes the Euclidean non-normalized surface measure of the full

d-1
-sphere. To keep track of the many constants, we abbreviate the
coefficient in front of the integral by

(36)

\Theta_{d}:=\frac{\vol(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}{\vol(\mathbb{S}^{d})}=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{d+1}{2})}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\frac{d}{2})}\,.

The integral over a spherical cap can then be written as

(37)

\int_{B_{r}(x)}f(x\cdot y)\,dy=\Theta_{d}\int_{0}^{r}f(\cos\theta)\,(\sin\theta)^{d-1}\,d\theta\,.

In particular, the volume of a spherical cap is

|B_{r}(x)|=\Theta_{d}\int_{0}^{r}(\sin\theta)^{d-1}\,d\theta\,.

Since
\sin\theta\geq\theta-\theta^{3}/6\geq\theta(1-\frac{r^{2}}{6})
, for
0\leq\theta\leq r
, this leads to the elementary lower estimate

(38)

|B_{r}(x)|\geq\Theta_{d}\frac{\big(1-\tfrac{r^{2}}{6}\big)^{d-1}}{d}\,r^{d}

that we use later.

7.1.
Off-diagonal decay of the reproducing kernel

We next complement Proposition
3.2
on the local
concentration of the reproducing kernel by a result about its off-diagonal decay.

In the proofs we will use very strong, uniform estimates for Jacobi polynomials
from
[
7
]
, see also
[
16
]
. These estimates are
formulated for
orthonormal
Jacobi
polynomials and contain the normalization factor

\kappa^{(\alpha,\beta)}(t)=\frac{2^{\alpha+\beta+1}}{2t+\alpha+\beta+1}\,\frac{\binom{t+\alpha}{t}}{\binom{t+\alpha+\beta}{t+\beta}}\,,

so that the polynomials

\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa^{(\alpha,\beta)}(t)}}P_{t}^{(\alpha,\beta)}

are orthonormal on
[-1,1]
with respect to the weight function

(1-x)^{\alpha}(1+x)^{\beta}
.
The Erdélyi-Magnus-Nevai bound
[
7
]
for
P^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{t}
with
\alpha,\beta>-1/2
is then
given by

(39)

(1-u^{2})^{1/2}(1-u)^{\alpha}(1+u)^{\beta}\frac{1}{\kappa^{(\alpha,\beta)}(t)}\,\big|P_{t}^{\alpha,\beta}(u)\big|^{2}\leq\frac{2e}{\pi}(2+\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}})\,.

For
(\alpha,\beta)=(\frac{d}{2},\frac{d}{2}-1)
we abbreviate the
constant on the right-hand side as

(40)

M_{d}=\frac{2e}{\pi}\Big(2+\sqrt{\frac{d^{2}}{2}-d+1}\Big)

and note that
M_{d}\leq\frac{e}{\pi}(4+\sqrt{2}\,d)
and
M_{d}\asymp d
.

We additionally introduce the constant

(41)

\Omega_{d,t}:=\frac{2\,\kappa^{(\frac{d}{2},\frac{d}{2}-1)}(t)}{\binom{t+\frac{d}{2}}{t}^{2}}

and recall
\Theta_{d}=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{d+1}{2})}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\frac{d}{2})}\asymp\sqrt{d}
from (
36
). With all these constants the
off-diagonal decay of the reproducing kernel can be described as
follows.

Lemma 7.1
.

For all
0<r\leq\pi
, we have

(42)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}\setminus B_{r}(x)}\big|\widetilde{P}_{t}^{(\frac{d}{2},\frac{d}{2}-1)}(x\cdot y)\big|^{2}\,dy\leq M_{d}\,\Theta_{d}\,\frac{\Omega_{d,t}}{r}\,.

In the proof we will find

(43)

\Omega_{d,t}=\frac{2^{{d+1}}}{\binom{d}{\frac{d}{2}}}\,\frac{1}{(t+\frac{d}{2})\,\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}\,,

so that

\Omega_{d,t}=\mathcal{O}(t^{-(d+1)})
. Thus, the mass of the
normalized Jacobi kernel outside a spherical cap of radius
r
is of
order
t^{-(d+1)}
.

Proof.

The Erdélyi-Magnus-Nevai bound (
39
) implies for the standard Jacobi
polynomial
P_{t}^{(\alpha,\beta)}
that

\displaystyle\left|P_{t}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(u)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa^{(\alpha,\beta)}(t)\,M_{d}(1-u)^{-\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}(1+u)^{-\beta-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\kappa^{(\alpha,\beta)}(t)\,M_{d}(1-u)^{\beta-\alpha}(1-u^{2})^{-\beta-\frac{1}{2}}\,.

For the normalized Jacobi polynomial
\widetilde{P}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{t}
and
x\cdot y=\cos\phi
, this leads to

\displaystyle\big|\widetilde{P}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{t}(x\cdot y)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\binom{t+\alpha}{t}^{2}}\big|P_{t}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(\cos\phi)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq M_{d}\,\frac{\kappa^{(\alpha,\beta)}(t)}{\binom{t+\alpha}{t}^{2}}\,(1-\cos\phi)^{\beta-\alpha}(\sin\phi)^{-2\beta-1}\,.

Note that
y\in\mathbb{S}^{d}\setminus B_{r}(x)
translates
into
r<\phi<\pi
. By
switching the integration from
y
to
\phi
and using
(
37
),
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}\setminus B_{r}(x)}\big|\widetilde{P}_{t}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x\cdot y)\big|^{2}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq M_{d}\,\Theta_{d}\,\frac{\kappa^{(\alpha,\beta)}(t)}{\binom{t+\alpha}{t}^{2}}\int_{r}^{\pi}(1-\cos\phi)^{\beta-\alpha}(\sin\phi)^{-2\beta-1}(\sin\phi)^{d-1}\,d\phi\,.

For
(\alpha,\beta)=(\frac{d}{2},\frac{d}{2}-1)
, the sines cancel each other, so that the integral reduces to

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{\pi}(1-\cos\phi)^{-1}\,d\phi=\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{r}^{\pi}(\sin\tfrac{\phi}{2})^{-2}\,d\phi

\displaystyle=\left(-\cot(\tfrac{\pi}{2})+(\cot\tfrac{r}{2})\right)=\cot\tfrac{r}{2}\leq 2/r\,.

The factor of interest is

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa^{(\alpha,\beta}(t)}{\binom{t+\alpha}{t}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\alpha+\beta+1}}{2t+\alpha+\beta+1}\,\,\frac{1}{\binom{t+\alpha}{t}\,\binom{t+\alpha+\beta}{t+\beta}}\,,

and, for
(\alpha,\beta)=(\frac{d}{2},\frac{d}{2}-1)
and after some
manipulations of binomial coefficients, it reduces to

\displaystyle\tilde{\Omega}_{d,t}:=\frac{\kappa^{(\frac{d}{2},\frac{d}{2}-1)}(t)}{\binom{t+\frac{d}{2}}{t}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{d}}{(2t+d)\,\binom{t+\frac{d}{2}}{t}\,\binom{t+d-1}{t+\frac{d}{2}-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{d}}{(2t+d)}\,\frac{(t+d)}{(t+\frac{d}{2})}\,\frac{1}{\binom{t+d}{t}\,\binom{d}{\frac{d}{2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{{d}}}{\binom{d}{\frac{d}{2}}}\,\frac{1}{(t+\frac{d}{2})\,\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}\,,

where the latter equality is due to
\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}=\binom{t+d}{t}\,\frac{2t+d}{t+d}
as stated in (
4
).

Using the abbreviations for the three constants, we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}\setminus B_{r}(x)}\big|\widetilde{P}_{t}^{(\frac{d}{2},\frac{d}{2}-1)}(x\cdot y)\big|^{2}\,dy\leq M_{d}\,\Theta_{d}\,\tilde{\Omega}_{d,t}\,\frac{2}{r}\,.

Finally, set
\Omega_{d,t}=2\tilde{\Omega}_{d,t}
.

7.2.
Quantitative lower volume bounds

We now verify that Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund measures must have enough mass in any spherical cap of sufficient size. The proof relies on the off-diagonal decay estimates in Lemma
7.1
and also makes use of the upper volume bounds in Theorem
4.1
.

Theorem 7.2

(lower volume bounds)
.

For
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, we define the constant

(44)

V_{d}=\frac{A/a}{(1-\epsilon)}\,\pi\,M_{d}\,d^{2}\,2^{\frac{d}{2}+6}(\sqrt{d}/e)^{d}\,,

where
M_{d}
is the Erdélyi-Magnus-Nevai bound as in (
40
).
If
\mu_{t}
is a Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund measure of order
t
on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
and

R\geq\frac{V_{d}}{t}\,,

then
\mu_{t}
satisfies the lower volume bound

\mu_{t}\big(B_{R}(z))\geq\frac{a\,\epsilon}{\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}\,,\qquad\forall\,z\in\mathbb{S}^{d}\,.

Theorem
7.2
complements the upper volume bounds in Theorem
4.1
. In particular for the radius
R\asymp t^{-1}
, the lower volume bound scales like
t^{-d}
, in agreement with the complementary upper bound in Theorem
4.1
.

Remark 7.1

(Requires
t\gg d
)
.

To ensure
R<\pi
, the term
t
must counterbalance
V_{d}
. Yet,

V_{d}
grows rapidly with
d
, in fact, super-exponentially in
d
.
Consequently,
t
has to be large relative to
d
, otherwise the lower bound becomes trivial since every cap of radius at least
\pi
contains the whole sphere.

The proof of Theorem
7.2
requires some preparations. We begin by applying the
Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities to the normalized Jacobi polynomial
f^{t}_{y}\in\Pi_{t}
,

(45)

f^{t}_{y}(x)=\widetilde{P}^{(\frac{d}{2},\,\frac{d}{2}-1)}_{t}(x\cdot y)\,.

The Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality then yields

(46)

\frac{a}{\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}|f^{t}_{y}(x)|^{2}\,d\mu_{t}(x)\leq\frac{A}{\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}\,.

We now integrate the parameter
y
over a small ball
B_{r}(z)
for
some
r>0
to be determined and obtain

(47)

\frac{a|B_{r}|}{\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}\leq\int_{B_{r}(z)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}|f^{t}_{y}(x)|^{2}\,d\mu_{t}(x)\,dy\leq\frac{A|B_{r}|}{\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}\,.

For
r\leq R\leq\pi
, we decompose the integration in the center variable
x
into

\mathbb{S}^{d}=B_{R}(z)\cup\big(\mathbb{S}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(z)\big)
and define

(48)

\displaystyle I_{r,R}(z)

\displaystyle:=\int_{B_{r}(z)}\int_{B_{R}(z)}|f^{t}_{y}(x)|^{2}\,d\mu_{t}(x)\,dy,\,

(49)

\displaystyle I\!I_{r,R}(z)

\displaystyle:=\int_{B_{r}(z)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(z)}|f^{t}_{y}(x)|^{2}\,d\mu_{t}(x)\,dy\,.

The above decomposition into near and far regions is a first step
towards a quantitative lower estimate of
\mu_{t}(B_{R}(z))
by suitably estimating
I_{r,R}
and rearranging terms.

Lemma 7.3
.

If
\mu_{t}
satisfies the
lower
Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality of order
t
on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
, then for every
r\leq R\leq\pi
,

\mu_{t}\big(B_{R}(z)\big)\;\geq\;\frac{a}{\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}\;-\;\frac{I\!I_{r,R}(z)}{|B_{r}|}\,.

Proof.

Since
|f^{t}_{y}(x)|^{2}\leq 1
, we obtain

I_{r,R}(z)\leq\mu_{t}(B_{R}(z))\,|B_{r}(z)|\,.

The lower bound in (
47
) leads to

\displaystyle\frac{a|B_{r}|}{\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}-I\!I_{r,R}(z)

\displaystyle\leq I_{r,R}(z)\leq\mu_{t}(B_{R}(z))\,|B_{r}|\,,

so that division by
|B_{r}|
yields the claim.

According to Lemma
7.3
, we now need
a suitable upper bound on
I\!I_{r,R}
.

Lemma 7.4
.

Assume that
\nu\in(0,1)
and
r>0
are related by
r^{2}=(1-\nu)\frac{(d+2)}{t(t+d)}
as in (
12
) and let
r\leq R\leq\pi
.
If
\mu_{t}
satisfies the
upper
Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality of order
t
on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
, then

I\!I_{r,R}(z)\leq\frac{A}{\nu^{2}\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}\setminus B_{R-r}(z)}|f^{t}_{z}(x)|^{2}\,dx\,.

Proof.

We first rewrite
I\!I_{r,R}(z)
in (
49
). The integral over
y
is treated first and the rewrite relies on applying a suitable reflection.

For
v\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
, the Householder transform

U=I-2vv^{\top}\in\mathcal{O}(d+1)
is the reflection with respect to
the hyperplane orthogonal to
v
. Therefore, given
x,z\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
and choosing
v=(z-x)/\|z-x\|
, the reflection satisfies
Uz=U^{\top}z=x
. The orthogonal invariance of

f^{t}(y,x)=\widetilde{P}^{(\frac{d}{2},\,\frac{d}{2}-1)}_{t}(x\cdot y)

then leads to

f^{t}(Uy,x)=f^{t}(y,U^{\top}x)=f^{t}(y,z)\,,

and
U^{\top}B_{r}(z)=B_{r}(U^{\top}z)=B_{r}(x)

implies

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(z)}|f^{t}(y,x)|^{2}\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{U^{\top}B_{r}(z)}|f^{t}(Uy,x)|^{2}\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r}(U^{\top}z)}|f^{t}(y,z)|^{2}\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r}(x)}|f^{t}(y,z)|^{2}\,dy\,.

The identity

1_{B_{r}(x)}(y)=1_{B_{r}(y)}(x)
yields the full expression for
I\!I_{r,R}
as

\displaystyle I\!I_{r,R}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(z)}\int_{B_{r}(x)}|f^{t}(y,z)|^{2}\,dy\,d\mu_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(z)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}|f^{t}(y,z)|^{2}1_{B_{r}(x)}(y)\,dy\,d\mu_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(z)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}|f^{t}(y,z)|^{2}1_{B_{r}(y)}(x)\,dy\,d\mu_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}|f^{t}(y,z)|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(z)}1_{B_{r}(y)}(x)\,d\mu_{t}(x)\,dy\,.

If
B_{r}(y)\cap\big(\mathbb{S}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(z)\big)\neq\emptyset
,
then
y\in\mathbb{S}^{d}\setminus B_{R-r}(z)
.
Therefore, the integral over
x
is majorized by

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}\setminus B_{R}(z)}\,1_{B_{r}(y)}(x)\,d\mu_{t}(x)

\displaystyle\leq 1_{\mathbb{S}^{d}\setminus B_{R-r}(z)}(y)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\,1_{B_{r}(y)}(x)\,d\mu_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=1_{\mathbb{S}^{d}\setminus B_{R-r}(z)}(y)\,\mu_{t}(B_{r}(y))\,.

Inserting in
I\!I_{r,R}
and applying Theorem
4.1
to estimate

\mu_{t}(B_{r}(y))
, we have now for any given
\nu\in(0,1)
, such that
r\leq R\leq\pi
,

\displaystyle I\!I_{r,R}(z)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}|f^{t}(y,z)|^{2}1_{\mathbb{S}^{d}\setminus B_{R-r}(z)}(y)\,\mu_{t}(B_{r}(y))\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{A}{\nu^{2}\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}\setminus B_{R-r}(z)}|f^{t}(y,z)|^{2}\,dy\,.

This concludes the proof.

To verify Theorem
7.2
, we now suitably estimate the integral

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}\setminus B_{R-r}(z)}|f^{t}_{z}(x)|^{2}\,dx
by the off-diagonal decay estimates in Lemma
7.1
.

Proof of Theorem
7.2
.

The proof comes down to determining
r
and
R
such that the requirement

(50)

\frac{I\!I_{r,R}}{|B_{r}|}\leq\frac{a}{\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}(1-\epsilon),

holds, because Lemma
7.3
then implies

\mu_{t}\big(B_{R}(z)\big)\,\geq\,\frac{a}{\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}-\frac{I\!I_{r,R}(z)}{|B_{r}|}\geq\epsilon\frac{a}{\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}\,.

To verify (
50
), we use Lemma
7.4
and
the off-diagonal decay (
42
) of the kernel.
For
0<R-r<\pi
and
r^{2}=(1-\nu)\frac{(d+2)}{t(t+d)}
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}\setminus B_{R-r}(z)}\big|f^{t}_{z}(x)\big|^{2}\,dx\leq\,\frac{M_{d}\,\Theta_{d}\,\Omega_{d,t}}{R-r}\,,

and Lemma
7.4
leads to

I\!I_{r,R}\leq\frac{A}{\nu^{2}\,\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}\,\frac{M_{d}\,\Theta_{d}\,\Omega_{d,t}}{R-r}\,.

By taking into account
|B_{r}(x)|\geq\Theta_{d}\frac{\big(1-\tfrac{r^{2}}{6}\big)^{d-1}}{d}\,r^{d}
as derived in (
38
), choose
R
according to the stronger requirement

\frac{I\!I_{r,R}}{|B_{r}|}\leq\frac{A}{\nu^{2}\,\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}\,\frac{M_{d}\,\Theta_{d}\,\Omega_{d,t}}{R-r}\,\frac{d}{\Theta_{d}\big(1-\tfrac{r^{2}}{6}\big)^{d-1}\,r^{d}}\,\leq\frac{a(1-\epsilon)}{\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}\,.

This simplifies to

\frac{A\,M_{d}\,\Omega_{d,t}\,d}{\nu^{2}\,(R-r)\,\big(1-\tfrac{r^{2}}{6}\big)^{d-1}\,r^{d}}\,\leq a(1-\epsilon)\,,

and solving for
R-r
yields

(51)

\frac{A\,d\,M_{d}}{\nu^{2}\,a(1-\epsilon)\,\big(1-\tfrac{r^{2}}{6}\big)^{d-1}}\,\frac{\Omega_{d,t}}{r^{d}}\leq R-r\,.

As already argued above, (
51
) implies
\mu_{t}\big(B_{R}(z))\geq\frac{a\,\epsilon}{\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}
.

It remains to simplify the left-hand side of (
51
), to remove
the dependence on
t
, and to find an explicit
constant by a suitable choice of
\nu
and
r
.

Determine
\nu
,
r
,
(t\,r)^{d}
, and estimate
\big(1-\tfrac{r^{2}}{6}\big)^{d-1}
:

For
t\geq d+1
, we choose
\nu
according to
(1-\nu)=\frac{1}{2}(1+d/t)
. The constraint
t\geq 2d
then leads to

\nu^{2}=\Big(1-\tfrac{1}{2}(1+d/t)\Big)^{2}\geq\Big(\frac{1}{4}\Big)^{2}=2^{-4}\,.

For this choice of
\nu
, the radius
r
is

r=\frac{1}{t}\sqrt{(1-\nu)\frac{d+2}{1+d/t}}=\frac{1}{t}\sqrt{\frac{d+2}{2}}\,.

Hence, we observe
(t\,r)^{d}=\big(\frac{d}{2}+1\big)^{d/2}
.

To get a lower bound on
\big(1-\tfrac{r^{2}}{6}\big)^{d-1}
, we use
t\geq d+2
and majorize
r^{2}
by

r^{2}=\frac{1}{t^{2}}\,\frac{d+2}{2}\leq\frac{1}{2(d+2)}\leq\frac{1}{2d}\,.

Hence, we have

\big(1-\tfrac{r^{2}}{6}\big)^{d-1}\geq\big(1-\tfrac{1}{12d}\big)^{d-1}\geq e^{-\frac{1}{12}}\,.

Estimate
\frac{\Omega_{d,t}}{r^{d}}
:

We now consider
\frac{\Omega_{d,t}}{r^{d}}=\frac{\Omega_{d,t}t^{d}}{(t\,r)^{d}}
, where
\Omega_{d,t}
is given in (
43
).
To estimate the numerator
\Omega_{d,t}t^{d}
, we use
\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}\geq\frac{2}{d!}t^{d}
from (
23
) and obtain

\Omega_{d,t}\,t^{d}=\frac{2^{{d+1}}}{\binom{d}{\frac{d}{2}}}\,\frac{1}{(t+\frac{d}{2})}\,\frac{t^{d}}{\mathrm{dim}\,\Pi_{t}}\leq\frac{2^{{d}}\,\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}+1)^{2}}{(t+\frac{d}{2})}\,.

By combining the above with
(t\,r)^{d}=\big(\frac{d}{2}+1\big)^{d/2}
, we have verified that

\frac{\Omega_{d,t}}{r^{d}}=\frac{\Omega_{d,t}t^{d}}{(t\,r)^{d}}\leq\frac{2^{d}\,\Gamma(\tfrac{d}{2}+1)^{2}}{\big(\frac{d}{2}+1\big)^{d/2}}\,\frac{1}{t+\frac{d}{2}}\,.

Collect all estimates:

We now collect all the estimates for the terms in (
51
), use

\frac{1}{t+\frac{d}{2}}\leq\frac{1}{t}
, and choose
R
so that

\left(\frac{A\,e^{\frac{1}{12}}\,d\,M_{d}}{a(1-\epsilon)}\,\frac{2^{d+4}\,\Gamma(\tfrac{d}{2}+1)^{2}}{\big(\frac{d}{2}+1\big)^{d/2}}\,+\,\sqrt{\frac{d+2}{2}}\right)\frac{1}{t}\leq R\,.

Since the summand
\sqrt{\frac{d+2}{2}}
is dominated by the term on its left, we simply absorb it by replacing the factor
2^{d+4}
with
2^{d+5}
.
We now could choose
R\geq\frac{\widetilde{V}_{d}}{t}
, where

\widetilde{V}_{d}=\frac{A\,e^{\frac{1}{12}}\,d\,M_{d}}{a(1-\epsilon)}\,\frac{2^{d+5}\,\Gamma(\tfrac{d}{2}+1)^{2}}{\big(\frac{d}{2}+1\big)^{d/2}}\,.

For the further elaboration of the constants, we use Stirling’s
formula for the Gamma function.

Stirling’s estimate for
\frac{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}+1)^{2}}{(\frac{d}{2}+1)^{d/2}}
:

The constant
V_{d}
in the theorem results from majorizing
\frac{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}+1)^{2}}{(\frac{d}{2}+1)^{d/2}}
.
Evaluating Stirling with remainder,

\Gamma(x+1)\leq\sqrt{2\pi x}\Big(\frac{x}{e}\Big)^{x}e^{\frac{1}{12x}}\,,\quad x>0\,,

at
x=d/2
leads to

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(\tfrac{d}{2}+1)^{2}}{\big(\frac{d}{2}+1\big)^{d/2}}

\displaystyle\leq\pi d\,\frac{\big(\frac{d}{2}\big)^{\frac{d}{2}}\,\big(\frac{d}{2}\big)^{\frac{d}{2}}e^{-d}e^{\frac{1}{3d}}}{\big(\frac{d}{2}+1\big)^{d/2}}

\displaystyle\leq\pi d\,2^{-\frac{d}{2}}(\sqrt{d}/e)^{d}\,e^{\frac{1}{3d}}\,.

We substitute this expression into
\widetilde{V}_{d}
and obtain

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{d}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{A/a}{(1-\epsilon)}\,\pi\,M_{d}\,d^{2}\,2^{\frac{d}{2}+5}\,e^{\frac{1}{12}+\frac{1}{3d}}\,(\sqrt{d}/e)^{d}\,.

Since
e^{\frac{1}{12}+\frac{1}{3d}}\leq 2
, the right-hand side is bounded by
V_{d}
in (
44
).

The auxiliary assumptions
t\geq d+2
and
t\geq 2d
made through the above derivations can be discarded since the lower volume estimate is only nontrivial for
R<\pi
. In this case we have
\pi t\geq V_{d}
, and this condition is already much stronger than the auxiliary ones. This completes the proof.

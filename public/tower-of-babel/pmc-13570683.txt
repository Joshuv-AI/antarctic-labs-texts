Beyond the Test: The Quiet Promise of Preventive Health and it is Unknowns
Authors: Shweta Chauhan, Advait Vaibhav Puri
Published: 2026

The advent of the 21st Century brought a new dawn to the healthcare revolution—marked with technological innovation, financial and legal equity in access, system-wide upgrades, and expedited privatization. The latter most unequivocally effectuates a cycle open to dubious and ambivalent elucidations.

From the International Sanitary Conference (1851–1938), Alma-Ata Conference (1978), First National Conference on Chronic Disease Prevention and Control (1986), to the Preventive Medical World Conference (2025), there has been a restrained yet steady drift in the pivot of the execution strategies employed—from curative to preventive care.

The cornerstone of preventive care is indisputably preventive health check-ups. Their execution, operations, administration, and hence their accomplishment in purpose are almost entirely the result of grassroots mobilization and participation.

But there are two sides to every story—and this is no exception. Nobility, honor, and integrity in purpose may occasionally be shrouded by egocentric motives, mercenary dispositions, and acquisitive obsessions.

Primary Health Check-ups have played a crucial role in significantly reducing morbidity and mortality rates, particularly those of non-communicable diseases, and thereby alleviating the burden on the healthcare system, society, and the global financial system. Considering the cost and the benefits derived from resultant increases in annual income, health check-ups can be regarded as cost-effective.[1]

On the downside, the ramifications of over-commercialization of the establishment rear its ugly head as often as not. Monopolization and the unrestricted jurisdiction of profit-oriented enterprises, particularly in urban areas, have at least partially stripped health providers of their ability to adopt a patient-centric approach. Factors driving overdiagnosis include advances in technology with increasingly sensitive imaging and tests, widening of disease definitions or treatment thresholds that yield much larger populations with a disease, and “incidentalomas” or incidental findings in individuals being investigated for other reasons.[2] Inspired by the requirement to establish a balanced approach, the American Board of Internal Medicine Foundation started the Choosing Wisely Campaign in 2012 with nine national specialty societies (representing 375,000 clinicians) offering 45 examples of tests or treatments that were commonly used in their fields, but lacked strong supporting evidence.[3]

From unnecessary, non-specific investigations to reports of questionable authenticity, these occurrences not only highlight the incompetence of medical practitioners, but also the attempt to actualize a calculated and premeditated inward-looking agenda, resulting in a direct assault on the helpless by depriving them of the benefits of quaternary prevention. By instilling a sense of heightened fear among the masses, they indirectly coerce the financially unprivileged to opt for the extravagant executive packages provided by them, a bouquet of non-essential, unwarranted tests. Evidence suggests that health checkups should be targeted at specific groups based on age, gender, and occupation. It should not be dictated by availability, desires, external pressures, or affordability. Health checkups should fulfill minimum quality assurance standards at a reasonable cost.[4]

Given the potentially limited and heterogeneous benefits of general health check-ups, it seems rational to target populations with many preventive care needs or those at the highest risk.[5] A significant shift in attitudes among healthcare workers, including doctors, nurses, and medical laboratory scientists, is necessary to foster a more proactive approach to routine checkups.[6] Emergency safeguard measures need to be enacted on a war footing by the government regulatory authorities to keep a check on the merciless greed of the corporates and shield the powerless from the devastation so caused.

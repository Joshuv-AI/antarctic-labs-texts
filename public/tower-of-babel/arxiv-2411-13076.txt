Hints of Prompt: Enhancing Visual Representation for Multimodal LLMs in Autonomous Driving
Hao Zhou, Zhanning Gao, Maosheng Ye, Zhili Chen, Qifeng Chen, Tongyi Cao, Honggang Qi, [3pt] UCAS

Abstract
In light of the dynamic nature of autonomous driving environments and stringent safety requirements, general MLLMs combined with CLIP alone often struggle to represent driving-specific scenarios accurately, particularly in complex interactions and long-tail cases. To address this, we propose the Hints of Prompt (HoP) framework, which introduces three key enhancements: Affinity hint to emphasize instance-level structure by strengthening token-wise connections, Semantic hint to incorporate high-level information relevant to driving-specific cases, such as complex interactions among vehicles and traffic signs, and Question hint to align visual features with the query context, focusing on question-relevant regions. These hints are fused through a Hint Fusion module, enriching visual representations and enhancing multimodal reasoning for autonomous driving VQA tasks. Extensive experiments confirm the effectiveness of the HoP framework, showing it significantly outperforms previous state-of-the-art methods across all key metrics.

Introduction

In recent years, there have been growing trends towards formalizing autonomous driving within end-to-end learning-based frameworks
Grigorescu et al. 2020
. Leveraging extensive human driving data, these approaches demonstrate human-like behavior and generalization capabilities, enabling them to handle intricate scenarios more effectively. However, the end-to-end pipeline often functions as a
black box
, making it challenging to interpret or explain the neural network-driven planning processes
Chen et al. 2024a
. This opacity raises significant ethical and legal concerns regarding accountability and trustworthiness in safety-critical applications.

Figure 2
:
Visualization of token affinity from CLIP
Radford et al. 2021
and DINOv2
Oquab et al. 2023
. Similar colors indicate higher affinity scores, with color values derived from a PCA-reduced token vector space. DINOv2 token-wise similarity denotes tokens embedded only from the similarity matrix.

Interpretability has emerged as a crucial research focus in autonomous driving to address these concerns. Visual Question Answering (VQA) tasks have gained considerable attention in this context
Sharma and Jalal 2021
, as they allow systems to articulate their observations and actions through natural language interactions. By bridging the gap between complex decision-making processes and user comprehension, VQA enhances system transparency and fosters user trust.

The application of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)
Bai et al. 2023
;
Wang et al. 2023a
;
Tong et al. 2024
;
Chen et al. 2024b
;
Liu et al. 2024a
;
Dai et al. 2023
has significantly advanced VQA tasks by integrating visual encoders with Large Language Models (LLMs)
Chowdhery et al. 2023
;
Touvron et al. 2023
;
Chiang et al. 2023
for deeper multimodal understanding. Typically, MLLMs comprise a visual encoder, an adapter, and an LLM. For VQA, the visual encoder extracts image features, aligned by the adapter with text tokens. The LLM tokenizes the question and integrates the aligned visual tokens to generate an answer.

For VQA in autonomous driving, the dynamic nature of driving environments and stringent safety requirements pose challenges for general MLLMs, particularly in representing hierarchical visual features crucial for complex interactions and long-tail cases. Although CLIP-based models
Radford et al. 2021
;
Zhai et al. 2023
have dominated visual encoders, as shown in
Fig.

2
, CLIP visual tokens overlook token-wise affinity, which is essential for preserving instance-level structure and establishing basic objectness understanding in driving scenarios. In contrast, DINOv2
Oquab et al. 2023
better maintains this structure. Additionally, general visual encoders underrepresent domain-specific semantics, making them prone to missing small yet crucial elements for driving, such as distant vehicles, pedestrians, and traffic signs—features vital for reasoning in complex interactions, like pedestrians unexpectedly crossing the street. Lastly, current MLLMs struggle to adjust visual representations based on specific questions, as visual and text tokens are processed separately. This separation limits the model’s ability to focus on query-relevant image regions, reducing the effectiveness of context-aware responses in autonomous driving.

Based on these observations, we introduce stratified tokens as hints to enhance visual representation. Specifically, we propose three key enhancements:
Affinity hint
to capture instance-level structure and strengthen token-wise connections,
Semantic hint
to incorporate high-level semantic information for domain-specific elements, and
Question hint
to correlate visual features according to the query context, ensuring focus on question-relevant regions. We conduct comprehensive studies on designing effective and efficient hints fusion strategies for combining question-specific and hierarchical semantic cues tailored to autonomous driving, thereby enhancing multimodal reasoning in these scenarios. Moreover, we propose an efficient version of HoP that maintains competitive performance, achieved by extracting Affinity and Semantic hints directly from two lightweight heads distilled and fine-tuned from the CLIP backbone.
Fig.

1
illustrates our proposed method, referred to as Hints of Prompt (HoP) framework throughout the paper.

Extensive experiments demonstrate significant improvements for VQA. On the LingoQA
Marcu et al. 2023
benchmark, as shown in
Table

1
, HoP achieves a Lingo-Judge score of
67.8
, establishing a new state-of-the-art. Ablation studies confirm that the three types of hints are complementary, collectively enhancing representation capability. Additionally, we explored various forms of Semantic and Affinity hints from different sources. Results indicate that as long as hint tokens represent token-wise affinity, performance improves, even when using a similarity matrix as Affinity hint tokens.

To summarize, we would like to highlight our contributions as follows:

•

We identify key limitations in current MLLMs combined with CLIP alone for VQA tasks in autonomous driving.

•

We introduce the Hints of Prompt (HoP) framework, which incorporates Affinity, Semantic, and Question hint tokens through a fusion module, significantly enhancing visual representation, enriching the LLM’s understanding of complex driving scenes, and boosting overall performance.

•

Extensive experiments demonstrate significant improvements of our proposed methods for VQA, establishing new state-of-the-art results on LingoQA
Marcu et al. 2023
, DRAMA
Malla et al. 2023
, and BDD-X
Kim et al. 2018

3
Method

3.1
Overview

As shown in
Fig.

1
, the Hints of Prompt (HoP) framework leverages three distinct token types—Affinity, Semantic, and Question hints—each representing a different level of information. These hints progress from instance-level, token-wise connections in the Affinity hint, to high-level semantic information in the Semantic hint, and finally to question-specific context in the Question hint, gradually refining and aligning the visual representation with the LLM. A simple Hint Fusion module integrates these hints into a cohesive visual representation, which is then processed by an adapter and passed to the LLM for answer generation. By enabling the visual encoder to dynamically incorporate hints across different information levels, our approach enhances context-aware responses in VQA for autonomous driving scenarios.

3.2
Hint Tokens

Affinity hint
The default practice of MLLMs
Liu et al. 2024b
;
Lu et al. 2024
;
Liu et al. 2024a
applies the CLIP
Radford et al. 2021
encoder for visual feature extraction, where the features are well-aligned with the text embedding space and contain rich semantic knowledge. However, the CLIP visual tokens struggle to understand driving scenes that require spatial reasoning among instances on the road. As shown in
Fig.

2
, we observe that the CLIP visual tokens drop the affinity information among instances, which might hinder the following LLM from reasoning upon these limited visual tokens. We find that DINOv2
Oquab et al. 2023
maintains significant affinity among tokens within the same instances, offering a more coherent spatial representation. Therefore, we use DINOv2 tokens as Affinity hint tokens to reinforce the instance-level structure in complex driving scenes.

To verify that the enhancement is due to token-wise affinity rather than specific DINOv2 features, we experiment with similarity information among the DINOv2 visual tokens and observe a notable performance gain. A detailed discussion is provided in
Section

4.4
.

Semantic hint
After implementing instance-level enhancement, providing a semantic category specific to the driving domain can further focus visual representations on key instances, such as the cyclist shown in
Fig.

3
. The Semantic hint aims to incorporate high-level, domain-specific semantic information, such as vehicles, traffic signs, and pedestrians, which is crucial for understanding complex interactions. To achieve this, we employ sparse queries from DETR-like
Carion et al. 2020
detection or segmentation pipelines as Semantic hints. Specifically, GroundDINO
Liu et al. 2023
or Mask2Former
Cheng et al. 2022
will be utilized for Semantic hint extraction. Experimental results (
Table

5
) show that, benefiting from Mask2Former’s adaptation to driving scenarios, it aligns more effectively with the task-specific requirements of VQA in autonomous driving.

Question hint
In VQA tasks, different questions often shift attention to specific regions of an image. For example, a question about the weather may focus on broader aspects, such as the sky or overall lighting. In contrast, a question about pedestrians in the path directs attention to finer details and specific areas. Incorporating question-specific information can further refine this focus by enhancing visual representation to capture key instances in the driving environment. Aligning the visual representation with each question’s particular demands enables a more targeted, context-aware interpretation, thus enhancing relevance and effectiveness in complex driving scenarios. Similar to QA-ViT
Ganz et al. 2024
, we found that using LLM’s text tokens as the Question hint effectively achieves context-aware representation. We also explored an alternative approach that utilizes CLIP’s text encoder to generate the Question hint. A comparison of these methods is shown in
Table

6
.

As shown in
Fig.

3
, incorporating the three proposed hints significantly improves the model’s response over the baseline. The baseline incorrectly suggests overtaking is possible, overlooking oncoming traffic. With the added hints, the model gains instance-level structure and focuses more accurately on key elements, such as the cyclist and oncoming vehicles. This added context enhances the model’s spatial reasoning, resulting in a more precise and relevant response in complex driving scenarios. Detailed quantitative analysis is available in
Section

4
.

3.3
Hints Fusion

We explore several fusion strategies to merge the above hints into the original CLIP
Radford et al. 2021
visual tokens. Formally, we denote Affinity, Semantic, and Question hints as
\mathbf{H}_{\text{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}
,
\mathbf{H}_{\text{S}}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times d}
, and
\mathbf{H}_{\text{Q}}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times d}
, respectively, where
N
,
M
, and
K
are the number of tokens for each hint, and
d
is the embedding dimension. The original CLIP visual tokens are represented as
\mathbf{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times d}
, and the fused visual tokens are denoted as
\mathbf{P}_{\text{f}}\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times d}
. The notation
[·]
indicates the concatenation operation,
\texttt{CA}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{H})
denotes a Cross-Attention layer with
\mathbf{P}
as query and
\mathbf{H}
as key-value, and
SA(·)
denotes a Self-Attention layer. As shown in
Fig.

4
, we present the different fusion strategies as follows.

Concatenation
As a baseline strategy, hint tokens are directly concatenated with CLIP tokens, as illustrated in
Fig.

4
(a):

\mathbf{P}_{\text{f}}=\texttt{[}\mathbf{P},\mathbf{H}_{\text{A}},\mathbf{H}_{\text{S}},\mathbf{H}_{\text{Q}}\texttt{]}.

(1)

Interestingly, we find that simple concatenation can harm visual representation and degrade performance (see
Table

7
), suggesting that directly injecting multi-level information may confuse the adapter and LLM, hindering relevant information extraction.

Self-Cross-Attention
To enable deeper interaction between visual tokens and hint tokens, we propose a Self-Cross-Attention module (
Fig.

4
(b)) that mimics the standard Transformer
Vaswani 2017
layer but omits the feed-forward network (FFN) to accommodate the subsequent adapter module:

\mathbf{P}_{\text{f}}=\mathbf{P}+\texttt{CA}(\texttt{SA}(\mathbf{P}),\texttt{[}\mathbf{H}_{\text{A}},\mathbf{H}_{\text{S}},\mathbf{H}_{\text{Q}}\texttt{]}).

(2)

This allows refinement of the visual tokens before incorporating the hint tokens.

Joint Cross-Attention
As shown in
Fig.

4
(c), inspired by Flamingo
Alayrac et al. 2022
, Joint Cross-Attention omits the self-attention stage and uses the visual representation
\mathbf{P}
as both query and key-value in a single cross-attention layer:

\mathbf{P}_{\text{f}}=\mathbf{P}+\texttt{CA}(\mathbf{P},\texttt{[}\mathbf{P},\mathbf{H}_{\text{A}},\mathbf{H}_{\text{S}},\mathbf{H}_{\text{Q}}\texttt{]}).

(3)

The design simplifies the fusion process without weakening the interaction between visual tokens and hint tokens.

To compare the effects of different query-hint interaction strategies, we further explore two additional approaches:
Parallel Cross-Attention
and
Sequential Cross-Attention
, as shown in
Fig.

4
(d, e). In these strategies, hint tokens first interact individually with the query and are then fused through either a parallel or cascading way.

Sequential Cross-Attention
sequentially applies cross-attention between the visual representation and each hint, progressively enriching the representation:

\mathbf{P}_{\text{f}}=\mathbf{P}+\texttt{CA}(\texttt{CA}(\texttt{CA}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{H}_{\text{A}}),\mathbf{H}_{\text{S}}),\mathbf{H}_{\text{Q}}).

(4)

Parallel Cross-Attention
applies cross-attention simultaneously between the visual representation and each hint token, which allows all hint tokens to influence the visual representation in parallel:

\mathbf{P}_{\text{f}}=\mathbf{P}+\texttt{CA}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{H}_{\text{A}})+\texttt{CA}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{H}_{\text{S}})+\texttt{CA}(\mathbf{P},\mathbf{H}_{\text{Q}}).

(5)

We empirically find that the proposed Joint Cross-Attention achieves the best performance with high efficiency across diverse VQA tasks in autonomous driving, outperforming the other fusion strategies mentioned above. More details are provided in
Section

4.4
.

3.4
Efficient Hints of Prompt

Although introducing extra hint tokens significantly improves VQA performance, extracting these hints adds a computational burden. To reduce resource overhead, we propose an efficient version of HoP. The main idea is to distill or fine-tune lightweight heads to mimic the behavior of DINOv2
Oquab et al. 2023
or Mask2Former
Cheng et al. 2022
, providing additional instance-level and semantic-level hints to enhance the visual representation while maintaining low latency. More details are provided in
Section

4.1
and
Section

4.4
.

4.1
Implementation Details

We implement the Hints of Prompt (HoP) method based on LLaVA-v1.5
Liu et al. 2024a
, freezing the visual encoder and fully fine-tuning the remaining modules.

For the Affinity hint, we use DINOv2-large
Oquab et al. 2023
to extract 576 hint tokens by downsampling the feature map to align with the CLIP tokens (
24\times 24
). To verify that the improvement is due to token-wise affinity rather than specific DINOv2 features, we compute a similarity matrix from the 576 DINOv2 tokens and use it as hint tokens, processed through a linear layer. Results are shown in
Table

4
.

For the Semantic hint, we select the top-K queries from Mask2Former
Cheng et al. 2022
or GroundingDINO
Liu et al. 2023
as hint tokens, based on confidence scores, with each token adding an embedding feature that corresponds to the label index. The Question hint uses the LLM’s textual embeddings associated with question tokens.

To create Efficient HoP, we distill and fine-tune two lightweight heads from the CLIP backbone: a 4-layer, 8-heads, 512-dimensional ViT-like
Dosovitskiy 2020
decoder for the Affinity hint, distilled via cosine similarity to DINOv2 features on the LingoQA training dataset; and a ViTDet-like
Li et al. 2022
neck with a Mask2Former head for the Semantic hint, trained on the Cityscapes dataset
Cordts et al. 2016
. The training setup follows that of the original Mask2Former.

The whole HoP training is conducted for one epoch using the AdamW optimizer with a cosine learning rate schedule, a warm-up ratio of 0.03, and a base learning rate of 2e-5. All models are trained on 32 NVIDIA A100 GPUs with a batch size of 4 per GPU.

4.2
Datasets and Benchmarks

LingoQA

Marcu et al. 2023
is a benchmark dataset for video question answering in autonomous driving, with 28K unique driving scenarios and 419K question-answer (QA) pairs across tasks like object recognition, scene description, driving reasoning, and action justification. The dataset consists of two subsets:
Action
and
Scenery
, plus a high-quality
Evaluation
subset for benchmarking. The main metric, Lingo-Judge, is an evaluation model aligned with human preferences, while BLEU
Papineni et al. 2002
, METEOR
Banerjee and Lavie 2005
, and CIDEr
Vedantam et al. 2015
provide lexical similarity assessments.

DRAMA

Malla et al. 2023
dataset comprises 17K video clips of interactive urban traffic scenes in Tokyo, and provides extensive annotations for autonomous driving risk assessment. It includes lots of VQA pairs about 11.8K training samples and 2.5K testing samples. Evaluation metrics include BLEU, METEOR, CIDEr, ROUGE
Lin 2004
, and SPICE
Anderson et al. 2016
.

BDD-X

Kim et al. 2018
is a dataset developed for autonomous driving tasks, focusing on video captioning and rationales, but it lacks native VQA pairs. To address this, we adopt the VQA pairs and evaluation protocol provided by DriveGPT4
Xu et al. 2024
, which include 26K training samples and 1.8K testing samples. Metrics such as BLEU, CIDEr, and ROUGE are used to ensure consistency and comparability of results.

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we introduce the Hints of Prompt (HoP) framework to address key limitations of current MLLMs in VQA tasks for autonomous driving. By incorporating Affinity, Semantic, and Question hints, HoP effectively captures instance-level structure, domain-specific elements, and query-specific context, leading to enhanced interpretability and reasoning capabilities. Extensive experiments on the LingoQA, DRAMA, and BDD-X datasets demonstrate that HoP consistently achieves state-of-the-art performance, validating the effectiveness of our proposed method. Additionally, we propose an Efficient HoP variant that reduces computational overhead while maintaining high performance. We believe that HoP’s structured integration of multi-level hints offers a promising direction for advancing VQA in complex, safety-critical scenarios.

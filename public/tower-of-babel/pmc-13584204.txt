Real-world experience with ipilimumab in combination with nivolumab in advanced sarcoma: a retrospective analysis including ultra-rare sarcomas
Authors: Benjamin Snyder, Casey Wendorff, Korbin Davis, Lauren Blackwell, Jason Childress, Taylor Stethen, Ani Toklu, Heather Burney, Sarah Spargo, Bharathi Muthusamy, Daniel A Rushing, Samantha A Armstrong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPatients with locally advanced or metastatic sarcoma have a poor prognosis and limited efficacious treatment options. Phase 2 trials have demonstrated efficacy for the combination of ipilimumab and nivolumab in select sarcoma histologies, but real-world data is limited.MethodsIn this single-institution retrospective analysis, patients with metastatic or unresectable sarcoma treated with the combination of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) ipilimumab and nivolumab were assessed for response by iRECIST criteria and were correlated with clinical outcomes. The Kaplan–Meier method was used for survival analysis.ResultsFrom 2019 to 2024, 90 patients with advanced sarcoma were treated with combination ICI and were evaluable for outcomes. The observed objective response rate (ORR) was 16.7% with clinical benefit rate (CBR) of 30%. The best response was partial response (PR) in 14.4%, stable disease (SD) in 23.3%, and progressive disease (PD) in 62.2%. Median overall survival (OS) (95% confidence interval [CI] 14.4-NE months) and median progression-free survival (mPFS) (95% CI, 9.6-NE months) were not reached for patients achieving an objective response. OS was superior in patients who experienced immune-related adverse events (irAEs). Both mPFS and median overall survival were superior in patients with thyroid irAEs. Highest ORR were seen in angiosarcoma, malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumor, leiomyosarcoma, undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma, and chondrosarcoma.ConclusionsReal-world analysis of combination ICI in a large cohort of heavily pretreated patients with advanced sarcomas demonstrates clinical benefit in a meaningful number of patients, including historically unresponsive histologies, though predictive factors remain elusive. The development of irAEs, specifically thyroid dysfunction, was associated with improved survival. Sustained responses were observed in patients who achieved an objective response.

Combination immunotherapy is an available treatment strategy for advanced, unresectable, and metastatic sarcomas providing prolonged disease control in a small subset of patients while most patients experience rapid progression. This real-world study observes durable disease control in select patients with sarcoma, including ultra-rare sarcoma histologies. Immune related adverse events may be associated with improved survival with immune checkpoint inhibitor treatment in sarcoma.

Introduction

Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have revolutionized the care of advanced solid organ malignancies, but there is significant variedness in response to ICI between malignancies. The management of locally advanced and metastatic soft tissue sarcoma (STS) and bone sarcomas remains heavily dependent on cytotoxic chemotherapy (CT). In current literature, there is substantial heterogeneity of response to ICI across sarcoma histologies. The broad application of ICI to sarcoma has remained elusive due to an immunosuppressed tumor microenvironment (TME).1 Additionally, traditional biomarkers of response to ICI such as tumor mutational burden (TMB) and PD-L1 status have proven unreliable across the advanced sarcoma spectrum.2,3 Recent evidence from the PEMBROSARC trial4 and a trial of PD-1 blockade in undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma (UPS)5 have suggested the potential of tertiary lymphoid structures (TLS) as a predictive biomarker of ICI response in STS, though widespread clinical applicability of TLS has not yet been proven.

Sarcomas represent a widely diverse group of more than 80 histologies6 with widely differing biology. Ongoing work in the field of sarcoma continues to identify novel oncogenic drivers and fusions leading to further subclassification of tumors. However, the presence or absence of oncogenic mutations or driver fusions is not significantly predictive of clinical response. Additionally, sarcomas often have low TMB with substantial heterogeneity in neoantigen expression and immune infiltration across histologies.7 Locally advanced and metastatic sarcoma have a poor prognosis, and the field remains in need of novel therapeutic approaches with greater efficacy.

Despite a generally immune-suppressed TME, immunotherapy has been increasingly studied in sarcoma with trials leading to FDA approval of atezolizumab for advanced alveolar soft part sarcoma (ASPS) and afamitresgene autoleucel for synovial sarcoma,8 while clinical studies have provided rationale for treatment of UPS, dedifferentiated liposarcoma (DDLPS),8 myxofibrosarcoma,9 and angiosarcoma in certain circumstances.10–12 However, the clinical application of ICI in sarcoma remains challenging given the previously discussed lack of reliable biomarkers and logistical challenges of running adequately powered clinical trials to assess the promise of ICI in specific histologies. Novel ICI combinations such as botensilimab and balstilimab with enhanced immunogenicity show promise12 and additional combinations with anti-angiogenesis agents such as cabozantinib or CT continue to be investigated.10,13 Additional immunotherapy strategies such as adoptive cell therapy have found applications in synovial sarcoma with approval from SPEARHEAD-1 trial14 and osteosarcoma with emerging CAR-T and novel immunotherapeutics targeting HER-2 or GD2.4,15 Given the challenges in scaling adoptive cell therapy and accessing exploratory immunotherapies, there remains a significant need for widely accessible and effective immunotherapy strategies. With substantial real world data establishing efficacy and safety across other solid tumors, the alure of ICI remains strong. However, there is limited published data regarding the real-world application of ICI in sarcoma. This study represents a retrospective analysis of treatment response from the broad application of combination ICI with ipilimumab and nivolumab across a variety of sarcoma histologies and in heavily pretreated patients at a single large sarcoma center with additional clinical correlates to assess for markers or predictors of response.

Methods

Patients with unresectable or metastatic sarcomas treated at Simon Comprehensive Cancer Center treated off label with combination ICI between January 2019 and December 2024 were reviewed through the electronic medical record with IRB permission and informed consent was waived. Patients were included in statistical analysis for progression free survival (PFS) if baseline and follow-up imaging were available to assess response based on iRECIST criteria. All patients received standard treatment with 1 mg/kg of ipilimumab and 3 mg/kg of nivolumab every 3 weeks for up to 4 cycles followed by administration of 480 mg of nivolumab every 4 weeks as maintenance therapy until unacceptable toxicity, clinical/radiographic progression, death, or per treating physician discretion. For the study population, ultra-rare sarcomas were defined by sarcoma community consensus critieria.16

Routine imaging with CT or MRI was retrospectively reviewed for disease response and utilized for the assessment of outcomes. Response was determined using iRECIST criteria for complete response (iCR), partial response (iPR), stable disease (iSD), unconfirmed progressive disease (iUPD), and confirmed progressive disease (iCPD). The most immediate prior studies before the start of immunotherapy were used as the baseline comparison for iRECIST assessment. Summed measurements of various target lesions at each time point were compared to the baseline measurements during the immunotherapy treatment window in accordance with iRECIST guidelines. Non-target lesions were also assessed in terms of being present or absent. If immunotherapy was stopped before there could be confirmation of progression, the next available study outside the treatment window was used to assess for confirmation of PD. For patients that only received 1 dose of ipilimumab/nivolumab, the next available study was used to make an assessment, and they were included in the survival analysis. All data for analysis was obtained from review of imaging, laboratory data, and clinical notes.

Histologic grade was assigned according to Fédération Nationale des Centres de Lutte Contre le Cancer (FNCLCC) grading criteria when applicable and was often based on biopsy rather than resection specimens. TMB and mutation status was assessed via commercially available genomic assays including Ashion, Caris, FoundationOne CDx, MD Anderson MAPP, and Tempus and reported in mutations per megabase (mut/Mb). TMB high status was defined as greater than 10 mut/Mb according to FDA tissue-agnostic approval for ICI.17,18 PD-L1 tumor proportion score (TPS) was performed by commercially available PD-L1 22C3 pharmDx through Foundation Medicine for 1 patient and combined positive score (CPS) was performed by other commercially available immunohistochemical staining methodologies.

Categorial data were summarized with frequency and percentage. Continuous data were summarized using median and range. Comparisons between best response groups were made using chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Efficacy evaluations included best response, objective response rate (ORR), clinical benefit rate (CBR), progression free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and duration of response (DOR). ORR was calculated as the percentage of patients who achieved CR or PR using iRECIST criteria. CBR was calculated as the percentage of patients who achieved iCR or iPR or maintained iSD for 24 weeks or longer. ORR and CBR were reported for the total sample as well as by sarcoma histology subtype. The Kaplan–Meier method was used to estimate median PFS and OS. PFS was calculated from the start of immunotherapy to the date of progression for patients who progressed or date of death for patients who died without progressing. Patients remaining alive and progression free were censored at their date of last disease evaluation. OS was calculated from the start of immunotherapy to date of death for patients who died. Patients remaining alive were censored at their last known alive date or the study cutoff date of 12/31/2024 whichever came first. Median PFS and OS with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were provided along with Kaplan–Meier plots with the number of patients at risk. Log-rank tests were conducted to evaluate potential differences in PFS and OS. All analyses were performed using SAS Version 9.4 (Cary, NC).

Results

Between January 2019 and December 2024, 98 patients received at least 1 dose of ipilimumab with nivolumab at Simon Comprehensive Cancer Center. Of the 97 patients treated with combination ICI during the study period, 90 had relevant follow-up and adequate clinical data for evaluation for response, survival, and toxicity (Table 1). Demographics included 56.7% male, 86.7% white race, median age at initiation of treatment 56 (25-82), baseline Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status at start of treatment was 0-3 with 69.6% of patients having and ECOG PS of 1-2. The most common histologies were liposarcoma (LPS) (13.3%), UPS (12.2%), leiomyosarcoma (LMS) (11.1%), angiosarcoma (10.0%), and chondrosarcoma (CS) (7.8%). Unspecified sarcomas histologies accounted for 11.1% and included insufficient tissue for definitive histological classification or pathology evaluated at an outside institution with confirmation of sarcoma diagnosis but incomplete histologic classification. Ultra-rare sarcoma histologies included clear cell sarcoma, high grade endometrial stromal sarcoma, epithelioid sarcoma, gastrointestinal primitive neuroectodermal tumor (Gastrointestinal NET), Ewing’s primitive neuroectodermal tumor (Ewing’s PNET), intimal sarcoma, and myofibroblastic sarcoma. Complete description of histologies is included in Table 1. Most tumors (65.6%) had Grade 3 histology while grade 2 histology was observed in an additional 15.6%. Data were insufficient to determine grade in 3 cases. Just under half of patients completed 4 cycles of induction combination ICI and went on to receive ongoing treatment with single agent nivolumab. There were 44 patients who underwent genomic testing and were assessed for TMB with 86.4% having TMB < 10 mut/Mb. PD-L1 CPS or TPS was evaluated in 7 patients, primarily earlier in the study period, and was less than 10% for all specimens except for 1 case of pleomorphic rhabdomyosarcoma with PD-L1 CPS of 50%.

The ORR for the study population was 16.7% with median DOR not reached, while SD was observed as best response in 23.3% of patients. PD or death occurred in 62.2% of patients during the induction treatment period. Histologies with the best ORR included angiosarcoma (22.2%) and malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumor (MPNST) (25%). CBR was 30.0% for the entire cohort and greater than 20% in angiosarcoma, CS, LMS, MPNST, myxofibrosarcoma, osteosarcoma, UPS, and the group with unspecified histology (Table 2). For the entire cohort median PFS was 2.4 months (0.2-0.4, 95% CI) and median OS 12.0 months (0.6-1.5 years, 95% CI) with median PFS and OS not reached for patients achieving an objective response (Figure 1). Cases with responses reported in the following narrative examples have additional molecular data provided in Table S1. One male patient with LMS originating from the stomach with TMB of 5 mut/Mb by next generation sequencing (NGS) experienced prolonged clinical benefit with PFS 31.2 months with decrease in size of target lesions not meeting criteria for response but enabling subsequent surgery rendering the patient disease free. A second case of LMS originating from the retroperitoneum lacking genomic data with an objective response observed by RECIST had a PFS of 24.6 months. One patient with myofibroblastic sarcoma of the arm with TMB 1 mut/Mb by NGS had PFS 9.7 months. One patient with myxofibrosarcoma of right thigh without genomic data had PFS 8.9 months. A patient with clear cell sarcoma of the lower extremity had PFS of 8.3 months and OS 10.9 months when receiving combination ICI in the second line. One patient with epithelioid sarcoma of the lower extremity (TMB 3 mut/Mb) had PFS 13.7 months when receiving combination IO in the second line. One patient with intimal sarcoma of the left atrium (TMB 6 mut/Mb) developed severe grade 3 gastritis after 3 cycles of combination therapy but had prolonged disease control with PFS 27.0 months. One patient with neurofibromatosis type 1 and MPNST of the pelvis received combination IO in the second line and completed 2 years of immunotherapy with best response of PR and PFS of 55.8 months without progression of disease on observation at time of data cutoff. One patient with dedifferentiated CS (PD-L1 CPS 8%, TMB 4 mut/Mb) suffered grade 3 colitis during induction and after recovering resumed nivolumab with best response of SD with PD only after nivolumab discontinuation after 2 years of intermittent treatment. Upon rechallenge with ICI, disease control was again durable and ongoing at the data cutoff for the study. In the osteosarcoma group, 3 patients experienced clinical benefit with prolonged PFS. Two patients with clinical benefit had radiation-associated osteosarcoma with anatomic sites of mandible (PFS 6.6 months) and clavicle (PFS 21.3 months). An objective response observed in one patient with osteosarcoma (PFS 39 months at censoring) who had Lynch Syndrome and a TMB of 16 mut/Mb with immunotherapy stopped after 3 years of maintenance and disease remaining radiographically stable at data cutoff.

Toxicities were observed at rates comparable to the reported literature19 with 62.2% of patients experiencing immune-related adverse events (irAE) of any grade. Grade 3/4 AEs were experienced by 12.2% of patients. The most frequent irAE was hypothyroidism, occurring in 28.9% of patients. There were no statistically significant differences in irAEs between patients who had an objective response and those who did not (Table 3). The mPFS was 2.4 and 3.6 months for those without and with irAEs respectively (P = .0864). There was a significant difference in median OS for patients not experiencing an irAE versus experiencing an irAE at 4.8 and 15.6 months respectively (P = .0029). In patients experiencing a thyroid disorder requiring replacement with levothyroxine, median OS was also improved compared to those who did not experience hypothyroidism (18 vs. 10.8 months, P = .0405) (Figure 4). Another consideration was given to whether irAE severity, as determined by administration of steroids for irAE, may be associated with improved PFS or OS. For patients receiving steroids for an irAE, there was not a statistically significant difference in OS (Figure S2).

Discussion

In this retrospective, single-center study, including a comparatively large population of patients with sarcomas treated with combination ICI, signals for efficacy and safety were observed. This study represents a large real-world retrospective analysis of combination ICI in sarcoma including ultra-rare histologies and builds on a smaller previous retrospective analysis,20 clinical trial data,21 and a recent larger report from another academic center.22 Over the course of the study period, treatment patterns shifted as additional data regarding optimal application of ICI in patients with sarcoma emerged. Though the ORR (16.7%) was relatively low, it was comparable with the initial Alliance A091401 trial.9 A significant percentage of patients had clinical benefit (30%) with multiple prolonged responses observed including in ultra-rare histologies. However, most patients rapidly progressed on combination ICI making optimal patient selection imperative.

In this study, ICI was administered without reference to biomarker status based on available data at the time of treatment. While immunotherapy holds promise in advanced sarcoma, the lack of reliable biomarkers and heterogeneity of disease biology remain barriers to broad application of available ICI treatment modalities. While there have been multiple negative trials for single agent ICI with pembrolizumab23,24 and nivolumab,25 combination immunotherapeutic approaches continue to hold promise for more robust immunologic recruitment in sarcoma.10,21,26–28 Nearly two-thirds of patients (62.2%) experienced rapid progression of disease and did not derive clinical benefit demonstrating the inadequacy of current combination ICI for the majority of patients.

Real-world efficacy is observed in this study in histologies, with previously established response profiles, such as angiosarcoma, sarcomas associated with radiation, UPS, and DD LPS. However, cases of response and disease control were demonstrated in LMS, CS, clear cell sarcoma, myofibroblastic sarcoma, intimal sarcoma, epithelioid sarcoma, and MPNST, histologies that have limited response to systemic therapy in clinical trials of standard therapies and limited options.9,23 These data suggest that ongoing evaluation of immunotherapeutic approaches including ICI are warranted even in histologies that have historically been considered resistant to immunotherapy.

Novel strategies remain highly relevant as response rates even for combination approaches in bone sarcoma are poor, and our data is consistent with what was observed in historical trials.24,28 However, we did observe 1 robust response in osteosarcoma in the setting of Lynch Syndrome. Additional combination approaches such as radiation therapy (RT) with ICI8,27 and C) with ICI26,29,30 have also shown promise in specific settings, such as neoadjuvant or adjuvant therapy, and with specific histologies. Specifically, the adaptation of the TME by anti-angiogenesis agents continues to hold promise with many combinations under study.31 Novel targets are emerging within the TME that may provide future strategies to combine with immune checkpoint inhibition, including targeting B-cells32 and macrophages.3

Limitations of this study include the real-world and retrospective nature of the data, including missing and incomplete variables limiting complete correlative analysis and potentially biasing findings to either positive or negative results. Many patients who failed to respond received only 1 or 2 doses of combination ICI in the setting of rapidly progressive and severely refractory disease potentially biasing this data to negative findings and highlighting the importance of careful patient selection. Additionally, given a lack of protocol for following routine imaging and clinical evaluation, additional variables may have been introduced in review of responses limiting the broad applicability of findings. The narrow racial/ethnic demographics of the study center may also have implications for extrapolation to other less homogenous populations. However, the large number of patients highlights that individual patients with rare malignancies may experience benefit from therapies when clinical trials may otherwise be infeasible making real-world data important. As in other solid tumors such as colorectal,33 renal cell carcinoma,34,35 and non-small cell lung cancer,36,37 irAEs have been associated with improved survival,38 as was observed in this study, and provide an additional clinical factor for prognostication.

Conclusion

Despite the limitations of this retrospective cohort study presentation of cases of response and a response rate comparable to cytotoxic therapy in many sarcoma histologies even the setting of advanced disease warrants strong consideration for exploration of novel ICI-strategies in both ultra-rare and previously ICI-unresponsive histologies. The response rate observed in this study affirms the legitimacy of combination ICI as a reasonable later line therapy across the sarcoma spectrum while additional biomarkers for response remain elusive. As seen in non-sarcoma tumor types, irAEs may be predictors of more durable response, and this deserves further prospective evaluation. Ongoing clinical trials will further elucidate coherent application of ICI in sarcoma with more tailored approaches and novel combination regimens.

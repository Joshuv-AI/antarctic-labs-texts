Functional analysis of StMAPK19 gene in response to salt stress in potato (Solanum tuberosum L.)
Authors: Xi Zhu, Yasir Majeed, Xiaoqin Duan, Kaitong Wang, Huimin Duan, Junfu Luo, Nengkang Guan, Haifei Zheng, Hui Jin, Zhuo Chen, Wei Li, Shu Chen, Jinghua Tang, Yu Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Soil salinization critically impacts potato cultivation by restricting growth, yield, and tuber quality. The MAPK pathway, evolutionarily conserved in plants, modulates hormonal balance, development, and abiotic stress responses. This study investigates the functional role of StMAPK19, a TDY-type mitogen-activated protein kinase gene, in potato salt tolerance. Phylogenetic analysis classified StMAPK19 into a TDY-motif subgroup, and subcellular localization revealed its presence in the plasma membrane, cytoplasm, and nucleus. Under NaCl stress (50, 100, and 150 mM), StMAPK19 expression was significantly upregulated in roots, stems, and leaves. Phenotypic analyses demonstrated that StMAPK19-overexpressing (OE) plants exhibited enhanced salt tolerance, marked by improved plant height, tuber yield, and biomass (fresh/dry weights), whereas RNA interference (RNAi) knockdown lines showed reduced growth. OE plants displayed elevated antioxidant enzyme activities, such as catalase (CAT), peroxidase (POD), superoxide dismutase (SOD), higher proline accumulation, and reduced oxidative stress markers, including hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂) and malondialdehyde (MDA), while RNAi lines exhibited compromised antioxidant capacity and exacerbated oxidative damage. Expression profiling revealed upregulation of stress-responsive genes (StCAT, StSOD, StPOD, StP5CS) in OE plants and downregulation in RNAi lines. Gas exchange measurements further indicated that OE plants maintained higher photosynthetic rates, transpiration, and stomatal conductance under salt stress compared to non-transgenic (NT) controls, whereas RNAi plants experienced significant declines. These findings demonstrate that StMAPK19 enhances salt tolerance by modulating ROS scavenging, osmoregulatory substance biosynthesis, photosynthetic efficiency, and stress-related gene expression. This study provides critical insights into the molecular mechanisms of salt tolerance in potatoes, offering potential targets for breeding resilient varieties to mitigate salinity-induced agricultural losses.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-025-07434-5.

Introduction

Potato (Solanum tuberosum L.), the fourth most essential global food crop after rice (Oryza sativa), wheat (Triticum aestivum), and maize (Zea mays), plays a pivotal role in food security, particularly in developing countries with rapidly expanding populations [1]. Tuber yield, the primary agronomic trait of potato cultivation, is intrinsically linked to tuber initiation and development [2], and is severely impaired by abiotic stressors, including salinity, drought, and temperature extremes [3]. Soil salinization, affecting over 800 million hectares globally [4], disrupts critical physiological processes in potatoes, such as photosynthetic efficiency, ion homeostasis, and ROS metabolism [5]. Salt stress also impairs stomatal conductance and transpiration rates [6], while simultaneously altering the activities of antioxidant enzymes (SOD, POD, CAT) and osmolyte accumulation [5]. By 2050, the number of salt-affected regions in South Asia is expected to nearly triple [7], intensifying challenges in major potato-growing regions, particularly arid zones where soil salinization intersects with agricultural productivity.

Plants employ evolutionarily conserved signaling cascades to mitigate abiotic stresses, with the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathway emerging as a central regulatory module [8]. This three-tiered phosphorylation cascade—MAPKKK → MAPKK → MAPK—transduces extracellular stimuli into cellular responses through sequential activation: MAPKKK phosphorylates the S/T-X-S/T motif in MAPKK, which subsequently activates MAPK via T-X-Y motif phosphorylation [9, 10]. Activated MAPK systematizes stress adaptation by modulating transcription factors (TFs) and kinase proteins governing cell proliferation, differentiation, and stress-responsive gene expression [11, 12]. Additionally, MAPK in plants is classified into two subtypes depending on the amino acid that appears in the center of the TXY motif: a plant-specific TDY subtype and a TEY subtype that is connected to the ERK subfamily in yeast and other animals [13]. Genome-wide analyses have identified MAPK orthologs across different plants with their specific functional corroboration under salt stress, including Arabidopsis (Arabidopsis thaliana) AtMAPK3/4/6 regulating salinity tolerance [14], rice (Oryza sativa) OsMAPK4/5/33 [15] and OsMPK3/5/6/44 [16], enhancing salt resistance, cassava (Manihot esculenta) MeMAPK16/17/19 repressing after salt treatments [17], and kiwifruit (Actinidia chinensis) AcMAPK4/5/9/12 heightening tolerance to salt stress [18]. These findings underscore the functional diversification of MAPK signaling in salt stress adaptation, providing a framework for investigating uncharacterized MAPK homologs in plants.

In potatoes, genome-wide studies have identified 20 MAPK genes (from StMAPK1 to StMAPK20) [19], yet functional characterization remains limited to StMAPK1, StMAPK3, StMAPK10, and StMAPK11 in abiotic stress responses [20–23]. Additionally, multiple sequence alignment research revealed that the potato MAPKs protein sequence had the conserved catalytic domain T(E/D) YVxTRWYRAPE(L/V), where T: threonine, E: glutamic acid, Y: tyrosine, and D: aspartic acid, and the StMAPKs members from group D (StMAPK1/2/3, StMAPK8/9/10/11/12/13, StMAPK16/17, and StMAPK19) all had TDY activation domain, while those from groups A (StMAPK14 and StMAPK20), B (StMAPK4, StMAPK15, and StMAPK18), and C (StMAPK5/6/7) all had TEY activation domain [19]. Although 20 MAPK genes have been identified in potatoes, the characterization of the TDY subtype of the MAPK gene family in potatoes under salt stress is lacking. The evolutionary conservation of StMAPK19 and its functional similarity to TDY-type MAPKs in other plant species have not been well studied. Additionally, there is limited comparative data on the functional differences between StMAPK19 and other MAPK genes in potatoes under salt stress. The study of StMAPK19 under salt stress in potatoes addresses several critical research gaps, including its functional characterization, role in stress signaling, and potential for crop improvement. By investigating StMAPK19, researchers can contribute to a deeper understanding of MAPK-mediated stress responses in potatoes and develop strategies to enhance salt stress tolerance in this important crop. In this study, the TDY-type MAPK, StMAPK19 (XP_006360027.1) with salt-responsive tissue expression, was localized to the plasma membrane, cytoplasm, and nucleus. Its functional modulation enhanced growth/biomass under salinity by scavenging reactive oxygen species (ROS) via antioxidants, synthesizing osmolytes, alleviating oxidative damage, improving photosynthesis, and regulating stress-related genes. These insights advance the mechanistic understanding of salt tolerance and inform precision breeding of resilient potato cultivars.

Results

Phylogenetic and multiple-sequence alignment analysis

Sequence alignment of MAPK19 proteins from 11 plant species, including tomato (Solanum lycopersicum) SlMAPK19, pepper (Capsicum annuum) CaMAPK19, African boxthorn (Lycium ferocissimum) LfMAPK19, Jimsonweed (Datura stramonium) DsMAPK19, potato (Solanum tuberosum) StMAPK19, Korean forsythia (Forsythia ovata) FoMAPK19, sesame (Sesamum indicum) SiMAPK19, flowering dogwood (Cornus florida) CfMAPK19, fuzzy kiwi (Actinidia eriantha) AeMAPK19, castor bean (Ricinus communis) RcMAPK19, and thale cress (Arabidopsis thaliana) AtMAPK19 revealed 11 conserved catalytic domains (I–XI), including the T-X-Y phosphorylation motif in domain VIII critical for activation across species (Fig. 1A). Protein IDs of mitogen-activated protein kinase 19 in different plant species are listed in Supplementary Table S1. Phylogenetic analysis classified a distinct clustering pattern among MAPK19 orthologs (AtMAPK19, RcMAPK19, AeMAPK19, CfMAPK19, SiMAPK19, FoMAPK19, StMAPK19, DsMAPK19, LfMAPK19, CaMAPK19, and SlMAPK19), with members within each cluster displaying high sequence homology and shared evolutionary trajectories (Fig. 1B). Furthermore, phylogenetic analysis of D-group MAPKs across species revealed that StMAPK19 clusters within the D-group clade containing the TDY motif, distinct from Groups A–C harboring the TEY motif (Fig. S1). Comparative analysis with Arabidopsis MAPKs and tomato SlMAPKs revealed significant evolutionary divergence among the four MAPK groups. The branching topology, supported by high bootstrap values, positioned StMAPK19 in close association with AtMAPK19 and SlMAPK16, indicating a high degree of evolutionary conservation, as shown in Fig. S1. The gene IDs of MAPK family genes in Arabidopsis thaliana and Solanum lycopersicum are recorded in Supplementary Table S2. These findings highlight the evolutionary stability of D-group MAPKs and suggest that StMAPK19 may retain conserved functional roles across plant species.

All MAPK19 proteins retained a conserved TDY activation domain, a plant-specific structural signature associated with stress signaling, suggesting conserved activation mechanisms and potential functional redundancy in stress adaptation or developmental regulation. Protein sequences were identified via NCBI BLAST (Table S1), corroborating structural conservation and evolutionary insights derived from multi-species alignment. Studying the TDY-type motif in StMAPK19 through sequence alignment and phylogenetic tree analysis provides a foundation for understanding its evolutionary relationships, functional conservation, and potential role in stress responses. This analysis not only confirms the classification of StMAPK19 as a TDY-type MAPK but also highlights its unique features and evolutionary significance. The findings can guide further functional studies and applications in improving stress tolerance in potatoes.

StMAPK19 expression profile under salt stress in potatoes

To conduct a more comprehensive and systematic analysis of the relative mRNA expression levels of StMAPK19, we exposed different potato plant tissues (roots, stems, and leaves) to varying concentrations of salt stress and performed qRT-PCR analysis. When potato plants were exposed to 50, 100, and 150 mM NaCl stress at intervals of 0, 1, 3, 6, 12, 18, 24, and 48 h, the expression trend of StMAPK19 in their leaves, stems, and roots dynamically changed (Fig. 2A-I). When salt stress treatment was increased to 100 and 150 mM NaCl, notable fluctuations were observed in roots (Fig. 2A-B-C) at certain periods, indicating that high salt concentrations exert complex effects on the relative expression of StMAPK19 in roots. Similarly, the expression trends in stems (Fig. 2D-E-F) and leaves (Fig. 2G-H-I) also differed. Under varying salt concentrations, the relative expression levels of StMAPK19 in stems and leaves show significant divergence. For instance, at 150 mM NaCl treatment, the amplitude of change in StMAPK19 expression in leaves is likely greater than in stems under the same treatment, suggesting that leaves may exhibit a more sensitive response to high salt stressors. This differential expression pattern highlights the tissue-specific regulatory mechanisms of StMAPK19 in response to salt stress, providing insights into the molecular adaptation strategies of plants under abiotic stress conditions.

Subcellular localization analysis

To elucidate the subcellular localization of the StMAPK19 protein, this study employed an Agrobacterium tumefaciens GV3101-mediated transient expression system. Recombinant Agrobacterium tumefaciens GV3101 strains harboring either the StMAPK19-EGFP fusion expression vector (pCAM35S-EGFP-StMAPK19) or the empty vector control (pCAM35S-EGFP) were separately infiltrated into tobacco leaves via syringe injection. Subsequent subcellular localization analysis of transformed cells was performed using DAPI staining (nuclear marker) (Fig. 3). Results demonstrated that the StMAPK19-EGFP fusion protein displayed distinct green fluorescent protein (GFP) signals in the cell membrane, cytoplasm, and nucleus, with the signal distribution pattern indicating localization of StMAPK19-EGFP to these compartments. Notably, GFP signals from the empty vector control (pCAM35S-EGFP) were also diffusely distributed across the cell membrane, cytoplasm, and nucleus. Given the marked reproducibility of the StMAPK19-EGFP signal distribution (observed consistently in membrane, cytoplasm, and nucleus) and its high degree of concordance with the diffuse pattern of the empty vector control, the present observations demonstrate that the StMAPK19 protein specifically localizes to the cell membrane, cytoplasm, and nucleus.

Construction of StMAPK19 transgenic (overexpression and RNA interference) potato plants

To elucidate the functional role of StMAPK19 in salt stress responses, stable transgenic potato lines were developed through Agrobacterium-mediated transformation, comprising both StMAPK19 overexpression (OE) and RNA interference-mediated knockdown constructs. The overall transformation strategy is schematically presented in Fig. S2. The qRT-PCR analysis confirmed stable genetic transformation, with StMAPK19 overexpression lines (OE-1 to OE-8) showing significantly elevated transcript levels (***P < 0.001; Fig. S3-A), while RNAi lines (RNAi-1 to RNAi-8) exhibited marked downregulation (***P < 0.001; Fig. S3-B). Based on expression profiling, overexpression lines exhibiting the highest StMAPK19 transcript levels (OE-1, OE-3, OE-4) and RNAi lines with the lowest expression (RNAi-2, RNAi-3, RNAi-6) were selected. Transcript levels were validated by StMAPK19-specific RT-PCR amplification (Fig. S3-C). Western blot analysis further confirmed significantly elevated StMAPK19 protein expression in overexpression lines compared to untransformed controls, with antibodies specifically detecting the target at ~ 67 kDa. Conversely, RNAi lines showed markedly reduced target protein expression (Fig. S3-D). All immunoblot data were normalized against β-Actin as the internal control.

Growth parameters of StMAPK19 transgenic (OE, RNAi) and NT plants under control and salt stress

Under control conditions (0 mM NaCl), StMAPK19 overexpression (OE) lines (OE-1, OE-3, OE-4) exhibited significantly increased plant heights (79.13 ± 11.56 cm​ for OE-4) and higher tuber yields (50.08 ± 7.95 g​ for OE-4) compared to RNAi lines (RNAi-2, RNAi-3, RNAi-6) and NT plants. However, under salt stress (50, 100, and 150 mM NaCl), all genotypes showed reduced plant height and tuber yield, with OE lines consistently outperforming RNAi and NT lines. For instance, at 150 mM NaCl, OE-1 maintained a plant height of ​39.05 ± 6.35 cm​ and a tuber yield of ​26.53 ± 4.41 g, while RNAi-3 exhibited significantly lower values (20.68 ± 3.64 cm​ and ​14.60 ± 1.88 g, respectively) (Table 1). These results suggest that the overexpressed gene enhances salt tolerance by mitigating growth inhibition under salt stress. The fresh and dry weights of the OE lines were significantly higher than those of other lines under both control and salt stress conditions. For example, when treated with 100 mM NaCl, the fresh weight of OE-1 reached 123.64 ± 19.50 g, and the dry weight was 19.77 ± 3.49 g, while the corresponding values of RNAi-2 were lower (88.71 ± 11.84 g and 14.32 ± 2.42 g, respectively). Under severe stress (150 mM NaCl), this trend persisted. OE-1 maintained a relatively high biomass (fresh weight of 69.69 ± 11.87 g and dry weight of 9.90 ± 1.56 g), while the biomass of RNAi-3 was lower (fresh weight of 42.16 ± 5.98 g and dry weight of 5.97 ± 1.04 g) (Table 1).

StMAPK19 regulates physiological parameters under salt stress in potatoes

Under control conditions, no significant differences in physiological parameters were observed between transgenic and NT potato plants (P > 0.05; Fig. 4A–R). At 50 mM NaCl stress induction after 48 h, all plants exhibited moderate stress symptoms. Compared to NT plants, StMAPK19-overexpressing lines (OE-1, OE-3, and OE-4) showed increased proline content (Fig. 4C), catalase (CAT; Fig. 4D), superoxide dismutase (SOD; Fig. 4E), and peroxidase (POD; Fig. 4F) activities, alongside reduced hydrogen peroxide (H2O2; Fig. 4A) and malondialdehyde (MDA; Fig. 4B) levels. In contrast, RNAi lines (RNAi-2, RNAi-3, and RNAi-6) displayed elevated H2O2 and MDA levels, reduced proline content, and diminished antioxidant enzyme activities.

Discussion

The abiotic stress-regulatory role of MAPK signaling pathways in plant growth and regulation has been explored in several reports [24–28]. In our prior research, we identified StMAPK3, a gene associated with salt tolerance in potato, which belongs to the TEY-motif subtype of the MAPK family [20]. To further elucidate the functional roles of MAPK genes in potato under salt stress, we conducted an in-depth investigation into the salt-tolerance mechanism of StMAPK19, a member of the TDY-motif MAPK subtype. We subjected the roots, stems, and leaves of potato plants to three distinct NaCl concentrations (50, 100, and 150 mM) to systematically evaluate their responses. This study represents a more comprehensive and systematic analysis of salt stress responses mediated by StMAPK genes in potato. Notably, this is the first report investigating the salt tolerance function of a TDY-motif MAPK in potato, providing novel insights into the molecular mechanisms of stress adaptation in this crop. Comprehensive phylogenetic reconstruction and multi-sequence alignment analyses of predicted amino acid sequences revealed distinct evolutionary relationships among MAPK19 orthologs from potato (Solanum tuberosum StMAPK19) and other plant species, including Ricinus communis RcMAPK19, Arabidopsis thaliana AtMAPK19, Actinidia eriantha AeMAPK19, Cornus florida CfMAPK19, Sesamum indicum SiMAPK19, Forsythia koreana FoMAPK19, Datura stramonium DsMAPK19, Lycium ferocissimum LfMAPK19, Capsicum annuum CaMAPK19, and Solanum lycopersicum SlMAPK19. All orthologs universally retain the threonine-glutamate-tyrosine (TDY) activation domain, a signature feature of MAPK signaling modules. The catalytic domains of these kinases are characterized by conserved structural motifs, notably the S/TXXXXX-S/T dual phosphorylation motif and the T-X-Y tripeptide motif, which are critical for maintaining enzymatic activity, substrate recognition, and conformational stability. These motifs exhibit high evolutionary conservation across MAPK families, including potato MAPKs, underscoring their indispensable role in coordinating phosphorylation cascades during stress adaptation and developmental regulation [29]. Similarly, the MAPK of group D, including Arabidopsis, potato, and alfalfa, contained the TDY catalytic domain (T: threonine, Y: tyrosine, D: aspartic acid) [19]. According to our research, potatoes and other plant species exhibit significant sequence similarity beyond the catalytic domains (I-XI) and TDY phosphorylation regions in all MAPK19 proteins (Fig. 1A). Among StMAPK19, SiMAPK19, FoMAPK19, DsMAPK19, LfMAPK19, CaMAPK19, and SlMAPK19 in group C, the evolutionary relationships of MAPK19 were very similar (Fig. 1B), suggesting that the MAPK19 gene may have undergone comparable changes over time and perform similar functions in the seven plant species, including potatoes mentioned above. The evaluated expression profile of StMAPK19 under varying degrees of NaCl treatment (50, 100, and 150 mM) was observed in potato plants’ roots, stems, and leaves (Fig. 2A-I). Genome-wide identification of the MAPK gene family in Limonium bicolor revealed that the LbMAPK10/18/19 showed an up-regulated expression pattern in response to drought stress, while LbMAPK10/12/18 depicted highly differential expression when 200 mM NaCl stress treatment was applied after 4 h [30]. Correspondingly, Genome-wide identification of the MAPK gene family in Gossypium raimondii validated the significant expression level of GrMPK19 under various abiotic stress treatments such as heat (37 °C), cold (4 °C), NaCl (200 mM), H2O2 (10 mM), and ABA (100 mM) [31]. Moreover, differential expression profiling revealed distinct stress-specific regulatory patterns among MAPK family members. Integration of the present findings with previous reports demonstrated that StMAPK19 is strongly induced by salt stress, displaying a 2.6–16.1-fold transcriptional upregulation within 24–48 h under 50–150 mM NaCl treatments (Fig. 2). The magnitude of induction increased proportionally with salt concentration, indicating a dose-dependent response. In contrast, StMAPK1 [21] was predominantly activated under heat stress, with significant upregulation at 35–40 °C, whereas StMAPK10 [23] and StMAPK11 [22] exhibited preferential induction under drought conditions (PEG-mediated or water deficit treatments). These findings highlight a stress-type-dependent divergence in MAPK family regulation and suggest that StMAPK19 functions as a central component in salt stress signal transduction. Functional redundancy and specificity were observed within the potato MAPK family concerning stress adaptation. StMAPK19, along with StMAPK1/10/11, contributes to mitigating oxidative damage by modulating antioxidant enzyme activities (SOD, CAT, POD) and by maintaining photosynthetic performance under respective stress conditions. However, StMAPK19 displays distinct functional specialization in salt stress responses. Overexpression of StMAPK19 not only enhanced antioxidant enzyme activities and proline accumulation but also significantly upregulated the expression of the key proline biosynthetic gene (StP5CS), ultimately leading to an increase in tuber yield per plant. This functional role contrasts with that of StMAPK1, which is primarily associated with heat stress, and StMAPK10/11, which are more responsive to drought stress. Collectively, these findings suggest that StMAPK19 exerts a unique and central regulatory function in potato growth and productivity under salt stress, highlighting its exclusive role within the Group D MAPK signaling pathway.

The subcellular localization of the MAPK gene families varies [32]. Previously, we investigated that StMAPK11 [21] is localized in the cytoplasm and nucleus. The prediction of the above genes was confirmed by constructing the sequences of StMAPK11 into the pCAM35S-GFP vector and transferring the recombinant plasmids (pCAM35-GFP-StMAPK11) to Agrobacterium tumefaciens strain Gv3101. A laser confocal microscope was used to detect the GFP signals in the cytoplasm and nucleus for the StMAPK11 protein. These outcomes showed a similar trend to our current study, which revealed that the StMAPK19 is localized in the nucleus by the GFP signals (Fig. 3), and the control vector (pCAM35-GFP) exhibited fluorescence in the membrane, cytoplasm, and nucleus. These results suggested that StMAPK19 may play a crucial role as a regulatory network in the signaling pathway because, after MAPK cascade activation under stress stimuli through surface receptors, it enters the nucleus and initiates the activation of TFs and other stress-related genes, enabling plants to induce prompt adaptive responses [32]. Phenotypic parameters, such as plant height, crop yield, root architecture, and biomass, are observed in physical traits under salt stress conditions. These traits assess a crop’s tolerance to salt and its increased productivity, with favorable phenotypic parameters indicating greater salt tolerance and potentially higher crop yields [33]. In the current study, phenotypic analysis was carried out to compare the transgenic and NT potato plants for various growth indexes, such as plant height, tuber yield per plant, and plant and root fresh and dry weights at various levels of induced NaCl stress application. Previously, compared with NT plants, overexpression of StMAPK3 increased the plant height, stem diameter, and number of lateral roots after being treated with 80 mM NaCl stress relative to NT plants, and the opposite was true for knockdown expression plants [20]. In another report, maximum fresh weights were sustained by MAPKK4 under saline conditions relative to the control group [34]. Our findings were supported by the above research works, in which various growth traits (Table 1) were improved by overexpression of the StMAPK19 after 50 days of stress treatment with 50, 100, and 150 mM NaCl compared to NT potato lines, while knockdown plants showed opposite trends.

Plants utilize their defense system, which comprises enzymatic antioxidants such as POD, SOD, and CAT, as well as certain non-enzymatic compounds like proline, that react to ROS under harsh conditions [35]. To scavenge ROS and preserve the equilibrium between ROS and antioxidant defense, these antioxidants’ enzymatic systems are essential for cell development and survival under stress conditions [36]. Similarly, plant growth and stability are sustained by upholding the H2O2 and MDA contents under high saline conditions. These physiological markers increase under salt stress due to the production of excessive ROS levels, causing an oxidative burst. Both enzymatic and non-enzymatic compounds scavenge excess ROS, preventing oxidative damage [37]. In the case of our study, an elevated proline (Fig. 45-6C) content, and boosted antioxidant enzymatic activities, such as CAT (Figs. 4 and 5-6D), SOD (Figs. 4 and 5-6E), and POD (Figs. 4 and 5-6F), and reduced levels of H2O2 (Figs. 4 and 5-6A) and MDA (Figs. 4 and 5-6B) were examined under various NaCl stress (50, 100, 150 mM) treatments after 48 h due to overexpression of the StMAPK19 gene, compared to NT plants, while down-regulated plants showed contrary aftermaths. These results are confirmed by a previous investigation, which validated that VvMAPK9, a grape MAP kinase, enhances salt tolerance in Arabidopsis and grape callus by strengthening the antioxidant defense system. Under salt stress, VvMAPK9 overexpression increases the activity of key antioxidant enzymes (SOD, CAT, POD, APX), reducing harmful ROS (H₂O₂, O₂⁻) accumulation and preventing oxidative damage (measured by lower MDA levels [38]. These findings collectively suggest that the VvMAPK9 gene plays an important role in response to salt stress by boosting antioxidant enzyme activities and ROS scavenging capacity, which showed consistency with our findings of the StMAPK19 gene salt stress regulation in potato plants by overexpression and knockdown expression as mentioned above. SOD (superoxide dismutase), POD (peroxidase), CAT (catalase), and P5CS (Pyrroline-5-carboxylate synthase) are examples of stress-related genes that, through their antioxidant qualities, primarily work to mitigate oxidative damage caused by the accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) [39]. P5CS (pyrroline-5-carboxylate synthetase) is a core enzyme in the proline metabolic pathway. It is involved in the biosynthesis of proline, Bi-functional Catalytic Activity, osmotic regulation, and plant stress resistance under harsh environmental conditions [40]. The MAPK signaling cascade is initiated when stress sensors detect ROS or other stress signals, activating MAPKKKs. This triggers a phosphorylation cascade (MAPKKK→MAPKK→MAPK), leading to the activation of downstream MAPKs [41]. Activated MAPKs, particularly MPK3/6-like kinases, phosphorylate TFs. These phosphorylated TFs then upregulate antioxidant genes (SOD, CAT, APX) and modulate NADPH oxidases (RBOHs) to fine-tune ROS levels [42]. MAPK activity is dynamically regulated by ROS levels, phosphatases (MKPs), and negative regulators (PP2Cs). This feedback ensures balanced ROS signaling, preventing oxidative damage while maintaining stress-responsive gene expression [43]. Our findings of StMAPK19 induction correlate with observed ROS accumulation patterns, suggesting its potential role in regulating the oxidative stress response. Like other stress-activated MAPKs, StMAPK19 may phosphorylate ROS-related stress-responsive genes (StSOD, StCAT, StPOD, and StP5CS), creating a balanced oxidative signaling environment crucial for stress adaptation. Previously, it was investigated that in wheat (Triticum aestivum) under heat stress (HS), MAPK exhibited a significant positive correlation with the expression levels of TFs, heat shock proteins (HSPs), and genes encoding antioxidant enzymes (e.g., superoxide dismutase SOD, catalase CAT, and glutathione peroxidase GPX). These correlations were particularly evident in pathways associated with photosynthesis and starch biosynthesis [44]. Similarly, in poplar (Populus trichocarpa) under drought stress, the PtMKK4 transgenic lines showed increased expression levels of many drought marker genes, including PtP5CS, PtSUS3, PtLTP3, and PtDREB8, compared to the wild-type plants [45]. Previous studies have focused on various TFs, antioxidant stress marker genes, proline catabolism-related genes, and heat shock proteins under abiotic stress (heat and drought) conditions. Our investigation showed similarity in proline bio-synthesis (StP5CS) and antioxidant related stress-marker genes (StSOD, StCAT, and StPOD) under abiotic stress (salt) conditions modulated by the StMAPK19 gene, demonstrating that, under various concentrations of NaCl stress (50, 100, 150 mM) application after 0 h, 6 h, 12 h, 18 h, 24 h, and 48 h, the stress-responsive genes such as StSOD (Fig. 7A, B, C), StCAT (Fig. 7D, E, F), StPOD (Fig. 7G, H, I), and StP5CS (Fig. 7J, K, L), showed an up-regulation in StMAPK19-overexpression plants, which causes a series of transcriptional changes which indirectly related to these stress-related genes, compared to NT plants, whereas RNA interference plants showed a down-regulation due to knockdown expression of the StMAPK19 gene which interrupted the signal transduction chain, and stress signals cannot be effectively transmitted to downstream genes. As a result, stress-related genes lack the necessary activation signals, making it difficult to initiate the transcription program.

Salinity affects plant photosynthesis because an excessive salinity level upsets the nutritional balance, which alters cellular structure and physiological processes [46]. An investigation revealed that the SlMAPK3 overexpressed tomato plants exhibit higher levels of photosynthesis and chlorophyll content, whereas knockout tomato plants exhibit the reverse effects compared to wild-type plants [47]. An earlier investigation determined that the RNAi-mediated knockdown of NaMPK4 in Nicotiana attenuata impairs drought tolerance and alters developmental traits. Knockdown of NaMPK4 (N. attenuata MPK4 ortholog) via RNA interference reduced plant stature, delayed senescence, and enhanced stomatal conductance and photosynthetic rates during late vegetative stages. These phenotypic changes correlated with a significant increase in seed yield. Biochemical analysis of guard cell function revealed impaired stomatal closure responses to abscisic acid (ABA) and H2O2 in irNaMPK4 plants, accompanied by elevated transpiration rates and accelerated wilt under water-deficit conditions, and as a result, a decrease in photosynthesis. These findings suggest that NaMPK4 plays a critical role in integrating hormonal signaling, stomatal regulation, and stress responses to modulate plant growth, reproduction, and drought adaptation in N. attenuata [48]. Our study investigated the abiotic response, which showed similar trend with above research and demonstrated that when StMAPK19-overexpressing plants were exposed to varying degrees of NaCl stress treatments after 48 h, an increased photosynthesis rate (Fig. 8A-B-C), transpiration rate (Fig. 8D-E-F), and stomatal conductance (Fig. 8G-H-I) were detected, compared to NT plants, and knockdown plants showed lowered photosynthesis activities due to down-regulation of the StMAPK19 gene. Overall, StMAPK19-OE enhances salt tolerance in potato plants by activating multiple protective mechanisms. It supports plant growth and biomass accumulation, safeguards photosynthetic activity, regulates oxidative stress through antioxidant enzymes (SOD, POD, CAT), and promotes the accumulation of osmoprotectants such as proline. In addition, it triggers the expression of stress-responsive genes (StSOD, StPOD, StCAT, StP5CS). Through the coordinated regulation of these pathways, StMAPK19 enables potato plants to better withstand salt stress and minimize cellular damage. Although the role of StMAPK19 in potato salt tolerance has been validated, several unresolved issues remain in this study. Firstly, the functional verification of StMAPK19 is currently limited to a single potato cultivar, “Atlantic”, which lacks comprehensive validation across diverse potato varieties and thus fails to fully reflect its role within the broader potato population. Secondly, field trials under natural saline-alkaline conditions are urgently needed to confirm its practical efficacy in environments closer to real-world scenarios. Additionally, the regulatory network of StMAPK19 in potato remains poorly understood, which limits the in-depth exploration of its mechanisms. In this study, certain findings were observed under non-salt stress conditions. The overexpression (OE) lines exhibited superior performance in growth parameters such as plant height, tuber yield, and biomass of both aerial and root parts, while the RNAi lines performed poorly. The NT group showed intermediate performance. Notably, the role of StMAPK19 in promoting potato growth and development shares similarities with the MAPK cascade pathways reported in Arabidopsis [14], cotton [28], tomato [49], maize [50], and rice [51]. However, despite these parallel investigations, the regulatory network of StMAPK19 in potato remains elusive. In light of these gaps, future omics studies and yeast two-hybrid library screening will focus on identifying specific regulatory factors involved in potato growth, development, and stress resistance. These efforts are expected to provide critical insights into resolving the current limitations and deepening the understanding of the mechanisms underlying StMAPK19 function under harsh environmental conditions.

Materials and methods

Plant material and growth conditions

The plant materials of the potato cultivar ‘Atlantic’ were obtained from Gansu Agricultural University, Lanzhou, China. The pot planting of transgenic and NT plant materials was completed at the South Subtropical Crops Research Institute of the Chinese Academy of Tropical Agricultural Sciences, Zhanjiang, Guangdong, China. Explants of the potato (Solanum tuberosum L.) cultivar ‘Atlantic’ were cultured on a Murashige and Skoog (MS) medium (pH 5.8–6.0) supplemented with 3% sucrose and 0.7% agar. Plants were grown in an MGC-450HP LED growth chamber (Yiheng Scientific Instruments Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) under a 16-hour light (2800 lx)/8-hour dark photoperiod at 22 ± 1 °C.

Salt stress treatment and sample collection

To determine the expression profile of StMAPK19 under salt stress, four-week-old healthy potato seedlings were transferred to 30 mL of liquid MS medium supplemented with 0 (control), 50, 100, or 150 mM NaCl. Plant tissue specimens (roots, stems, leaves) were harvested at eight chronologically spaced intervals (0, 1, 3, 6, 12, 18, 24 and 48 h post-treatment), flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen within 30 s of collection to prevent RNA degradation, and cryopreserved at − 80 °C in triplicate biological replicates until subsequent RNA isolation procedures. The relative expression levels of StMAPK19 were quantified using quantitative reverse transcription PCR (qRT-PCR). This part of the experimental design has been presented in the research design flowchart of Fig. S4.

Preparation of stem segments and tuber induction

Stem segments (2 cm long) containing axillary buds were aseptically excised from apical-and basal-removed virus-free potato seedlings. These explants were transferred into 150 mL sterile Erlenmeyer flasks containing 30 mL of liquid MS basal medium (pH 5.8 ± 0.1) supplemented with 30 g·L⁻¹ sucrose, followed by culture under controlled conditions: 22 ± 1 °C with a 16-hour photoperiod (2800 lx illumination) alternating with 8-hour darkness for four weeks. To initiate tuberization, both transgenic and NT potato plantlets were subsequently transferred to solid MS medium containing 80 g·L⁻¹ sucrose and incubated under continuous darkness at 22 ± 1 °C for 56 days.

Pot experiment and stress treatment

Following dormancy termination, tubers were aseptically transplanted into 20 × 40 cm (diameter × height) pots containing a homogenized substrate blend (1:1 v/v ratio of organic-enriched soil to horticultural perlite). Soil moisture was maintained at 70–75% for 14 days. Uniformly growing transgenic and NT potato plants were selected for further experiments. One group of plants was subjected to different NaCl stress treatments (0, 50, 100, and 150 mM) in pots for 50 days, after which growth parameters were statistically evaluated. Another group of plants was exposed to the same NaCl stress treatments for 48 h, followed by the assessment of physiological and photosynthetic parameters. All the above experimental designs are illustrated in the research design flow chart of Fig. S4, including two independent treatment groups: short-term salt stress treatment (0–48 h) and long-term salt stress treatment (50 d).

Multiple-sequence alignments and phylogenetic analysis

For multiple sequence alignment (MSA), the StMAPK19 protein sequence was aligned against orthologs from 10 plant species using Jalview 2.11.4.1 software [52]. Additionally, MSA between StMAPK19 and MAPK sequences from Arabidopsis thaliana and Solanum lycopersicumwas performed with CLUSTALW 1.83 [53]. Conserved functional motifs across these sequences were subsequently identified based on the resulting alignment profiles. Phylogenetic reconstruction was conducted using MEGAX 4.1 software, implementing the neighbor-joining (NJ) method with bootstrap validation of 1,000 replicates [54].

StMAPK19 gene expression patterns by qRT-PCR analysis

Total RNA isolation from experimental samples was performed utilizing the TRIzol RNA extraction kit (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA), followed by first-strand cDNA synthesis using the TransGen Biotech reverse transcription system (Beijing, China). Quantitative PCR amplifications were conducted on a Roche Light Cycler 480-II platform (Rotkreuz, Switzerland) with reaction mixtures containing 100 ng cDNA template, 0.8 µL sequence-specific primers, and 10 µL SYBR Premix Ex Taq (2× concentration; Takara, Tokyo, Japan). Thermal cycling parameters included an initial denaturation at 94 °C for 120 s, followed by 34 cycles of denaturation (94 °C, 30 s), primer annealing (60 °C, 34 s), and extension (72 °C, 30 s). The Stef1a housekeeping gene provided internal normalization control, with relative mRNA quantification calculated via the 2−ΔΔCt method [55]. The experimental design incorporated triplicate biological replicates with triplicate technical replicates per sample. Primer sequences are detailed in Supplementary Table S3.

Construction of expression vectors and genetic transformation

The coding sequence of the StMAPK19 (GeneBank ID: XM_006359965.2) was amplified and then cloned into the pBI121-EGFP plasmid to create StMAPK19-overexpressing transgenic potato plants. This was achieved by employing specific primers: forward primer 5’- ATGGAGGCAAGTTTAGGTGATC-3’ and reverse primer 5’- GTGAGTTGGATCTGGATTAAAGTTC-3’, which were designed with the assistance of Bioeditas (Shaanxi, China), using an earlier method [56]. Similarly, a previously developed technique [57] was used to create the RNAi expression plants. The forward primer, including an EcoR I restriction site and a reverse primer, consisting of a Kpn I restriction site, was utilized to expand the sense cDNA sequence and was then injected into the pHANNIBAL vector (designated as pHAN-StMAPK19-R) as an EcoR I-Kpn I fragment. Similarly, the antisense cDNA sequence was amplified using a forward primer with a Hind III restriction site and a reverse primer with an Xba I restriction site and was inserted into the same pHANNIBAL vector (designated as pHAN-StMAPK19-RF) as a Hind III-Xba I fragment. The pART-StMAPK19-RNAi vector was constructed by sub-cloning pHAN-StMAPK19 into the pART-vector using the Xho I and Xba I restriction sites. Then, this recombinant plasmid (pART-StMAPK19-RNAi) was transferred into Agrobacterium tumefaciens strain LBA4404 and incubated at 28 °C for 48 h. PCR validation was achieved using particular primers for the RNAi construct (forward: 5’-AATTCGTTCCAACCAACAATTGAC-3’; reverse: 5’- AGTATGCAACCAACTGACCAGAT-3’) and the NPT II gene (forward: 5’-ATGACTGGGCACAACAGACAATCG-3’; reverse: 5’-TCAGAAGAACTCGTCAAGAAGGCG-3’). The plasmid-harboring Agrobacterium was cultured in an LB medium supplemented with 50 mg/L spectinomycin and 50 mg/L gentamicin at 28 °C for 48 h. Subsequently, the cultured medium was centrifuged at 5000 rpm for 10 min, and the pellet was resuspended in MS medium to achieve an optical density of 0.3 (OD600). Sterile potato seedlings measuring 2 cm in length were then cultivated in MS medium containing 7.4 g/L agar, 30 g/L sucrose, 0.5 mg/L 6-BA, 2 mg/L ZT, 0.2 mg/L GA3, and 1 mg/L IAA, adjusted to pH 5.8. The seedlings were incubated with the Agrobacterium suspension for 10 min and maintained in darkness for 3 days. For differentiation, the seedlings were transferred to MS medium enriched with 7.4 g/L agar, 30 g/L sucrose, 300 mg/L Timentin, 100 mg/L kanamycin, 0.5 mg/L 6-BA, 2 mg/L ZT, 0.2 mg/L GA3, and 1 mg/L IAA (pH 5.8), with the medium replaced every two weeks. Finally, the resistant buds were cultivated in rooting MS medium supplemented with 7.4 g/L agar, 30 g/L sucrose, 300 mg/L Timentin, and 100 mg/L kanamycin (pH 5.8) to induce adventitious root formation.

Antibody production for StMAPK19 protein and western blotting assays

The preparation of the antibody specific to the StMAPK19 protein, as well as the subsequent western blot analyses, were performed in accordance with previously established protocols [21]. All experimental procedures, including antibody production, sample preparation, protein extraction, and immunoblotting, were conducted under standardized laboratory conditions to ensure reproducibility and accuracy. A comprehensive description of the methodology, along with step-by-step details of the experimental design and conditions, has been provided in Supplementary Data S1.

The subcellular localization analysis of the StMAPK19 protein

The complete coding sequence (CDS) region of StMAPK19 was augmented using the particular primers (forward, 5’-CTCGAGATGGAGGCAAGTTTAGGTGATC-3’ and reverse 5’- GTCGACGTGAGTTGGATCTGGATTAAAGTTC-3’). The StMAPK19 was ligated to the pCAM35s-GFP vector after being digested with Xho I and Sal I enzymes. For the experiment, we selected the leaves of tobacco plants that were in optimal condition (Healthy leaves free from pest infestation, mechanical damage, or other visible injuries were selected for the study. The selected leaves exhibited optimal morphological characteristics, including a smooth surface without noticeable wrinkles or curling) and had consistent growth, namely the leaves positioned second to third from the top. The recombinant plasmid (pCAM35s-GFP-StMAPK19) was transformed into Agrobacterium tumefaciens strain Gv3101, which was subsequently infiltrated into tobacco (Nicotiana benthamiana) epidermal cells using a syringe-based method as previously described [58]. The infiltrated leaf sections were excised after 12 h of incubation at 24 °C in the greenhouse. Following a 48-hour incubation period, the epidermal layer was meticulously dissected, and the GFP signals were subsequently imaged utilizing a Leica TCS SP8 confocal laser scanning microscope (Leica Microsystems, Wetzlar, Germany).

Evaluation of growth indices between transgenic and non-transgenic potato plants

Plant height, tuber yield per plant, plant fresh and dry weights, as well as root fresh and dry weights, were assessed and analyzed statistically after the application of various NaCl stress treatments as described earlier. Plant height was measured from the surface of the ground junction to the top of the shoot tip. Biomass was calculated by measuring the fresh weights, and then the whole plant, as well as its roots, were kept in an oven at 70 °C to attain dry weights.

Analysis of proline, MDA, H2O2 content, and antioxidant enzymes (SOD, POD, and CAT) activities

After various degrees of NaCl stress induction as mentioned above, different physiological indices, such as proline [59], MDA [60], and H2O2 [61], contents, as well as CAT, POD, and SOD [62], activities were estimated as previously determined (See Supplementary Data 1). For each replicate treatment, five pots containing one plant per pot were used (3 replications × 3 treatments × 5 pots× 7 lines). In a completely randomized design, for each treatment, three replications were set.

Evaluation of photosynthetic parameters

From the top of each plant, the third leaf was collected during the day between 9:30 and 11:30. By using a transportable photosynthetic LI-6400XT system (Li-COR, Lincoln, NE, USA), net photosynthetic rate, transpiration rate, and stomatal conductance were measured. The leaf chamber’s relative moisture content was kept between 60 and 70%, and the photon flux density was consistently recorded at 1500 µmol·m−2·s−1. The measured CO2 concentration was 400 µmol·mol−1. Each experiment was carried out in three replications utilizing a completely randomized design.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism (GraphPad, San Diego, CA, USA) and IBM SPSS 19.0 (IBM, Chicago, IL, USA). The normality of the data distribution was examined through the Shapiro-Wilk and Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests, and the assumption of homogeneity of variances was verified using Levene’s test. It was determined that all of the data had homoscedasticity and conformed to a normal distribution. The mean ± standard deviation is used to show the results. The statistical significance of mean differences was assessed using a one-way ANOVA, and Dunnett’s technique was employed for correction.

Conclusions

In summary, following different NaCl stress treatments, StMAPK19 with TDY subtypes MAPK motif showed notable and increased variable expression in the potato plants’ roots, stems, and leaves. The StMAPK19 was localized in the plasma membrane, cytoplasm, and nucleus by subcellular localization analysis. In particular, it significantly amplified the potato phenotypes under salinity by overexpression. Moreover, by raising the levels of proline, CAT, POD, and SOD activities and decreasing the amounts of MDA and H2O₂ in potato plants, StMAPK19-overexpression improved salt resistance. Additionally, its overexpression promoted stomatal conductance, transpiration rates, and photosynthesis following the application of NaCl stress treatments. Finally, the up-regulated expression of StSOD, StPOD, StCAT, and StP5CS in StMAPK19 overexpression plants corroborates the functional study of this gene in response to salinity. Altogether, this study investigates StMAPK19’s role in salt stress response and potential mechanisms for controlling salt tolerance in potato plants, providing insights for future research and practical applications.

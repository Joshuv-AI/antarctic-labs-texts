Entanglement suppression and quantum scars in a three-oscillator gravitational analogue
P. George Christopher, S. Shankaranarayanan

Abstract
We study quantum correlations in a tripartite system of coupled harmonic oscillators, modeling mediator-induced interactions akin to those in quantum gravity proposals. Surprisingly, despite the system’s integrability, we identify regimes where bipartite entanglement between distant oscillators vanishes even with non-zero quantum couplings. In the Heavy Mediator Regime , entanglement is restricted to sharply localized islands in parameter space, surrounded by extended regions of suppression, analogous to quantum scars in non-integrable systems. Fidelity analysis reveal that these low-entanglement states exhibit dynamical stability and spectral signatures reminiscent of quantum scars in non-integrable systems, suggesting a continuous-variable analogue of scarring tied to hidden phase-space symmetries. The Light Mediator Regime , by contrast, displays smooth entanglement generation. These results challenge entanglement-based tests of quantum gravity: the absence of entanglement need not imply classical mediation but may instead reflect a mediator dynamically constrained to a quantum subspace—akin to gravitational memory or decoherence-free subspaces. Our findings emphasize the necessity of probing the mediator’s dynamical regime to unambiguously diagnose quantum gravity.

1.

Here,

\tilde{m}_{B}=\frac{m_{B}}{1-\lambda_{B}/\omega_{B}},~\tilde{\omega}_{B}^{2}=\omega_{B}^{2}-\lambda_{B}^{2},~\tilde{k}_{B}=\tilde{m}_{B}\tilde{\omega}_{B}^{2},

(11)

\tilde{m}_{B}

\tilde{k}_{B}=\tilde{m}_{B}\tilde{\omega}_{B}^{2}

\hat{H}_{0B}\xrightarrow[\lambda_{B}\to\omega_{B}]{}\frac{1}{2}\tilde{k}_{B}\,\hat{x}_{B}^{2}.

Thus, in HMR, the mediator loses its ability to coherently store or transmit quantum information. Its Hilbert space remains intact, but its dynamical role is effectively frozen: the canonical momentum

V
Results and Implications

Our numerical analysis, presented in Fig.
2
reveal that both (HMR and LMR) regimes can support generation of quantum correlations, quantified by
\langle\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{N}}\rangle
and
\langle\mathcal{D}\rangle
, between subsystems
A
and
C
. However, these regimes exhibit
markedly different structural and spectral features
in their entanglement.

The LMR consistently exhibits a smoother, more continuous entanglement landscape. Here, non-zero quantum correlations are broadly distributed across wider parameter ranges, indicating a robust ability of the “light” (dynamically active) mediator
B
to facilitate entanglement between
A
and
C
. This suggests that when the mediator can fluctuate significantly, it readily mediates quantum correlations.

In stark contrast, HMR presents a highly fragmented entanglement landscape. Entanglement emerges only within
sharply localized islands
in parameter space, surrounded by expansive regions where quantum correlations are strongly suppressed, often vanishing entirely. Despite the system’s integrability, the emergence of such sharply structured regions in the HMR is strikingly reminiscent of
quantum many-body scars
. In systems with scars, specific non-generic eigenstates exhibit unexpectedly low entanglement and resist thermalization, maintaining enhanced localization in Hilbert space. Our findings suggest an
analogous form of scars
, not necessarily in the many-body Hilbert space, but within the space of Gaussian states of this continuous-variable system. Further fidelity analysis support this: in the low-entanglement regions, the fidelity of the evolved state with the initial state remains remarkably close to unity over long times. This indicates
dynamical stability
and a resistance to spreading in the Hilbert space, akin to localized scarred states. Conversely, within the high-entanglement islands, fidelity oscillates significantly away from
1
, reflecting more complex, delocalized, and dynamically richer behavior. This coexistence of dynamically localized and delocalized states within an integrable system points towards selective resonance effects or subtle
hidden symmetries
that prevent full ergodic mixing in the continuous-variable phase space.

These differences are greatly reflected in the underlying
eigenmode structure
of the system, characterized by the normal mode frequencies
k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}
. In the HMR (
\lambda_{B}\to\omega_{B}
), one eigenmode approaches zero (
k_{2}\to 0
), signifying the “freezing” of the mediator’s kinetic degree of freedom, while the other two become degenerate (
k_{1}\to k_{3}
), implying a symmetric, coupled motion of
A
and
C
dominated by the static mediator. In the LMR (
\lambda_{A}\to\omega_{A}
), a different spectral signature emerges: two eigenmodes vanish simultaneously (
k_{1},k_{2}\to 0
), with the third remaining finite. This indicates a different type of effective decoupling or simplification of dynamics, possibly leaving the mediator’s own quantum fluctuations more dominant in mediating correlations. These contrasting signatures highlight a deeper
asymmetry in the dynamical role of the mediator
across the two regimes and may have implications for relativistic systems
Fragkos et al. 2022
,
Martín-Martínez and Perche 2023
.

Crucially, these results have significant implications for experimental tests of quantum gravity, such as
BMV-type scenarios
where two massive systems interact via a presumed quantum mediator. Our findings suggest that bipartite entanglement alone may offer
only
a partial diagnostic of the mediator’s quantum nature. Specifically, the absence of observed entanglement does not necessarily imply classical mediation. Instead, it may indicate that the mediator (analogous to oscillator
B
) is operating within a dynamically constrained subspace of its Hilbert space. This corresponds to the singular limit
\lambda_{B}\to\omega_{B}
(HMR), wherein its ability to generate and transmit quantum correlations is effectively frozen, despite being fundamentally a quantum system. The observed vanishing entanglement in these specific parameter regions, even for non-zero quantum interactions, presents a critical caveat for entanglement-based protocols.

This singular limit, where the mediator’s kinetic energy is suppressed and its position becomes effectively static, may have a deeper physical interpretation within the context of quantum gravity. Given that gravity is inherently self-interacting and highly non-linear, such constrained dynamical behavior could be reminiscent of
gravitational memory effects
. In the context of BMV-type proposals, this suggests that even within a fully quantum framework of gravity, certain quasi-classical features (or strong self-interactions analogous to the self-squeezing term) could drive the mediating gravitational field into a subspace where it is dynamically inert and unable to coherently transmit quantum correlations. This implies that the quantum signature of gravity might be masked under specific, physically plausible conditions.

Therefore, for experimental tests aiming to definitively establish the quantum nature of gravity, it is paramount to
account for such subtle dynamical constraints
. Protocols must ensure that the hypothesized quantum mediator (e.g., gravitons or their effective field) operates in a regime where its quantum dynamical role is active and observable, rather than being effectively “frozen” into a quasi-classical configuration by strong self-interactions or specific environmental couplings. Our work provides a concrete, integrable model demonstrating how entanglement can vanish in a quantum system with non-zero interactions, serving as a critical benchmark for design and interpretation of future quantum gravity experiments.

We begin by defining a linear vector space (LVS) by collecting the annihilation and creation operators into a column vector of the form:

\displaystyle\left[\hat{\mathbb{X}}_{i},\hat{\mathbb{X}}^{{\dagger}}_{j}\right]=i\Omega_{ij}

(13)

where,
\Omega

\displaystyle\Omega=-i\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{n}&\mathbb{O}_{n}\\

In the traditional density matrix formalism the time evolution of the system is given by a unitary operator
\hat{U}(t)
such that
\rho_{0}\rightarrow\rho(t)=\hat{U}(t)\rho_{0}\hat{U}^{{\dagger}}(t)

\sigma_{0}\rightarrow\sigma(t)=S_{H}(t)\sigma_{0}S_{H}^{T}(t)\,,

where the covariance matrix is defined as,

\displaystyle\sigma_{ij}=\langle\{\hat{\mathbb{X}}_{i},\hat{\mathbb{X}}_{j}^{{\dagger}}\}\rangle-2\langle\hat{\mathbb{X}}_{i}\rangle\langle\hat{\mathbb{X}}^{{\dagger}}_{j}\rangle

For time independent Hammitonian’s the symplectic evolution matrix is given by
S_{H}(t)=\exp[\Omega Ht]
(
\hbar=1

\bm{V}^{*}&\bm{U}^{*}\end{pmatrix}

and

\hat{\mathbb{X}}=\mathbb{U}\;\hat{\mathbb{X}_{r}}

\mathbb{U}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{n}&i\mathbb{I}_{n}\\
\mathbb{I}_{n}&-i\mathbb{I}_{n}\end{pmatrix}

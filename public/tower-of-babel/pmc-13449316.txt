Identification of candidate genes regulating seed oil and protein content in Brassica napus via integrated genome-wide association and transcriptome analysis
Authors: Zhongchun Xiao, Zhengting Huang, Chao Zhang, Jihua Guo, Guidong Miao, Yingjuan Wang, Pei Zhang, Lijuan Wei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSeed oil and protein content (SOP) are economically important traits in Brassica napus. Simultaneously improving these traits to cultivate high-oil and high-protein varieties is vital for maximizing production efficiency per unit area. This study investigated the total SOP in Brassica napus to identify regulatory genes, facilitating molecular breeding and enhancing the crop’s commercial value. High-throughput genome resequencing yielded 385,692 high-quality single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) with a minor allele frequency (MAF) > 0.05. A genome-wide association study (GWAS) identified nine SNPs significantly associated with SOP: five on chromosomes C01 (1) and C03 (4), and four on chromosome A10. Individual SNPs explained 5.32%-7.02% of the phenotypic variance. GWAS screening revealed 428 candidate genes within the confidence intervals of significant SNPs. Comparative transcriptome analysis of seeds from lines with extremely high- and low- SOP across developmental stages in two environments (Guiyang and Xingyi) identified 117 common differentially expressed genes (DEGs). Integrating GWAS and transcriptomic data narrowed the candidates down to two key genes regulating SOP. By integrating transcriptome analysis and GWAS, we identified two priority candidate genes within significant SNP intervals that regulate SOP. This work establishes a foundation for breeding high-SOP Brassica napus varieties via molecular marker-assisted selection.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-026-09134-0.Keywords: Brassica napus, Genome-wide association study, Seed oil and protein content, Single nucleotide polymorphisms, Candidate genes

Introduction

Brassica napus is a globally important crop for both human consumption and animal feed. Its mature seeds are rich in oil and protein, offering high nutritional value due to their substantial caloric and protein density [1]. Rapeseed oil accounts for 22.9% of phytosterol intake in the Chinese diet, making it the primary dietary source of these compounds [2]. Compared to other major oilseed crops, rapeseed meal-a key by-product of oil extraction-possesses a high crude protein content, second only to soybean. However, antinutritional factors such as fiber and polyphenols reduce its protein digestibility [3]. Rapeseed Biodiesel (R-BD) serves as a sustainable feedstock that offers significant environmental benefits, reducing greenhouse gas emissions-specifically CO2-by up to 48% compared to conventional diesel [4]. Furthermore, research in biomaterials indicates that rapeseed meal can be transformed into high-value bioplastics after oil extraction, significantly enhancing the utility of processing by-products [5]. Therefore, identifying key genes regulating total seed oil and protein content (SOP) is crucial for breeding new rapeseed varieties with high production efficiency.

The advancement of omics technologies has established multi-omics analysis as a powerful approach for elucidating the regulatory mechanisms underlying complex traits [6–9]. Genome-Wide Association Studies (GWAS) systematically identify associations between phenotypes and molecular markers (e.g., SNPs) by exploiting linkage disequilibrium within natural populations. This approach offers an efficient strategy for identifying quantitative trait loci (QTLs). A core advantage of GWAS is its ability to parallelly detect numerous allelic variants, combining high mapping resolution with practical utility and cost-effectiveness [10, 11]. Genetic loci and candidate genes controlling key quality traits have been successfully identified via GWAS in crops such as cotton [6], soybean [7], and Brassica napus [8, 9]. For instance, a GWAS of 520 rapeseed accessions identified FAX1-1, a key gene that enhances oil content, quality, and biological yield [12]. Furthermore, a study using a 60 K SNP array across 370 rapeseed accessions identified seven SNPs on chromosomes A04, A07, C01, C02, and C03 associated with SOP, along with five candidate genes [13]. To date, while GWAS has been extensively applied to seed oil content (SOC), research specifically addressing seed protein content or the combined traits remains limited. In our previous study, we performed a GWAS on the seed oil content of 516 rapeseed genotypes using high-throughput resequencing and identified 17 significant SNPs, subsequently leveraging transcriptomic data to pinpoint seven potential genes associated with SOC [14]. Collectively, these studies demonstrate that while numerous SNPs associated with SOC have been identified in Brassica napus, research focusing on the genetic regulation of SOP remains scarce.

Although the specific roles of genes regulating total SOP remain understudied, genetic engineering has significantly advanced the enhancement of seed oil content (SOC) in Brassica napus. For instance, CRISPR/Cas9-mediated dual-site editing of the BnTT8 gene generated a novel yellow-seeded germplasm. These mutants exhibited substantially increased seed oil and protein accumulation and optimized fatty acid composition while maintaining key agronomic traits, positioning them as vital resources for quality breeding [15]. Similarly, CRISPR/Cas9-mediated knockdown of BnaMYB52 significantly increased SOC per unit seed weight by reducing seed coat thickness and increasing kernel weight [16]. Targeting the fatty acid desaturase gene BnFAD2 markedly elevated the proportion of monounsaturated oleic acid, thereby improving oil quality. Additionally, modifying the fatty acid elongase gene BnFAE1-a traditional target for quality improvement-effectively reduces erucic acid, a compound detrimental to human health [17]. Furthermore, studies have shown that BnaC04.bZIP16, a negative regulator of fatty acid accumulation, is phosphorylated and inhibited by BnaA06.SnRK2 [18]. Therefore, identifying candidate genes capable of simultaneously increasing SOP is critical for the genetic improvement of Brassica napus.

In this study, we utilized whole-genome resequencing of 516 Brassica napus accessions to investigate the genetic regulation of SOP. The genotyping data were then used to perform genome-wide association analysis using a mixed linear model (MLM, PCA+Kinship) to systematically identify SNPs that were significantly associated with SOP. Subsequently, we integrated GWAS and transcriptomic data to screen for candidate genes governing the synergistic accumulation of seed oil and protein. To provide a theoretical foundation for the breeding of new rapeseed varieties with both high SOP, the study set out to identify the major candidate genes that control the synergistic accumulation of SOP during the development of Brassica napus seeds.

Results

Phenotypic variation of SOP

A panel of 516 Brassica napus accessions was cultivated in the same environment for four consecutive years (2016–2019), with three biological replicates performed each year. The complete phenotyping data are provided in Table S1, while the phenotypic variation of SOP across the four environments is summarized in Table 1. SOP values ranged from 54.15% to 70.23% (mean: 61.29%) in 2016CQ, 54.71% to 69.57% (mean: 62.44%) in 2017CQ, 59.26% to 73.71% (mean: 67.25%) in 2018CQ, and 59.36% to 72.82% (mean: 67.15%) in 2019CQ. We performed BLUP analysis on four-year phenotypic data. The SOP content ranged from 59.8% to 69.85%, with an average value of 64.46% and a coefficient of variation of 2.9%. In all four environments and BLUP, the coefficient of variation (CV) for SOP remained below 5%, indicating limited phenotypic variation across the GWAS panel. The broad-sense heritability of SOP is 83.62%, which was calculated following the method described by Lu et al. [19]. Consistent with quantitative traits, SOP displayed continuous variation and followed an approximate normal distribution across all four years (Fig. 1). This distribution suggests that SOP is a polygenic trait regulated by multiple genes rather than a single locus. These results indicate that while environmental factors significantly influenced SOP, genetic factors accounted for the majority of the phenotypic variance.

Genome-wide association analysis

This work employed the PCA + K model for GWAS analysis of SOP, which may be used to the discovery of related single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs), as we discovered from the Quantile-quantile plot findings that the PCA + K model was closest to the predicted model (Fig. 2a). In all, the study found nine SOP-associated single nucleotide polymorphism loci that were significant (P<2.59 × 10− 6). These SNPs were unevenly distributed on chromosomes A10, C1 and C3 (Fig. 2b; Table 2). Of these, only one SNP was scattered on chromosome C1, whereas four significant SNPs were on chromosome A10 with a significant scattered area spanning 1.52–17.33 Mb. Additionally, chromosome C3 had the remaining four SNPs. A single SNP can account for a range of 5.32–7.02% of the phenotypic variation of SOP (R2). The locations and specifics of each SNP were listed Table 2. Meanwhile, only S10_17029919 showed no overlaps with previously published SNPs, whereas all other detected SNPs have been reported in earlier studies, further verifying the reliability of our findings [20–22].

The phenotype analysis of extremely high- and low- SOP material across Xingyi and Guiyang regions

Near-infrared spectrometry (NIRS DS2500) was used to determine the SOP phenotypes of the mature and dried seeds of five very low SOP and five very high SOP extreme Brassica napus materials. At the meantime, the five accessions with the highest and lowest BLUP values for SOP, representing the top and bottom 1% of the population. The results showed that the mean values of the seed SOPs between the high SOP materials and the low SOP materials under both Guiyang and Xingyi conditions differed by approximately 8% (Fig. 3, Table S2), which reached a highly significant level and could thus be used for further transcriptome analysis. The identical material’s SOP in Guiyang was much greater than in Xingyi, suggesting that the environment there was more conducive to SOP accumulation.

Transcriptome analyses for differentially expressed genes

Using seeds from various developmental stages of extreme high- and low- SOP materials of Brassica napus in Guiyang and Xingyi, an RNA-Seq library comprising 12 independent samples and 36 libraries (three biological replicates per sample) was constructed for this study. 248.18 Gb clean reads were obtained by quality control, with the percentage of Q30 bases at 93.78% and above (Table S3). The data’s high reliability was confirmed by a separate sequence comparison of Clean Reads from each sample with the reference genome (http://cbi.hzau.edu.cn/rape/download_ext/zs11.genome.fa). The comparison efficiency varied between 90.21% and 94.84% (Table S4), and all biological inter-repeat correlation coefficients were greater than 0.9. DEGs in high- and low- SOP seeds of Brassica napus in the Guiyang and Xingyi were examined using transcriptome analyses at 20, 30, and 40 days post-flowering. A total of common 753 DEGs were found in the three periods in Guiyang, with 3068 (Up:1425, Down: 1643), 3757(Up: 1819, Down: 1938), and 2692 (Up: 1432, Down: 1260) DEGs identified in high- and low- SOP accessions at 20d, 30d, and 40d, respectively (Fig. 4a, b). In Xingyi, a total of 576 common DEGs were found in the three periods, with 5382 (Up: 2251, Down: 3131), 3814 (Up: 1985, Down: 1829), and 4662 (Up: 2308, Down: 2354) DEGs found in high- and low- SOP accessions at 20d, 30d, and 40d, respectively (Fig. 4a, c). We identified 753 DEGs in seeds at different developmental stages of high- and low-SOP varieties in Guiyang. Besides, we identified 576 common DEGs in seeds at different developmental stages of high- and low-SOP varieties in Xingyi, respectively (Table S5). Transcriptome analysis revealed regional variation in the environmental plasticity of SOP content in Brassica napus seeds, with gene expression variability significantly higher in the Guiyang trial site than in Xingyi (P < 0.05). Ultimately, 117 key DEGs stably regulating SOP were screened across both locations (Fig. 4d, Table S5).

Functional classification of 117 common DEGs at different stages seeds of Brassica napus between Guiyang and Xingyi regions

To comprehend the functions of 117 common DEGs related to SOP in rapeseed seeds at different developmental stages, the 117 common DEGs were subjected to GO enrichment and KEGG metabolic pathway analyses. GO enrichment was examined based on the three main categories of biological processes, cellular components, and molecular functions. In biological processes, these DEGs were mainly enriched in metabolic processes, cellular processes, and single-organism processes (Fig. 5a); In cellular components, these DEGs were primarily enriched in membranes and membrane part, cell and cell part (Fig. 5a); In terms of molecular function, these genes are mainly enriched in binding, catalytic and transporter activity Fig. 5(a). Through KEGG enrichment analysis, the most significantly enriched pathways were those linked to metabolism, particularly involved in cutin, suberin and wax biosynthesis, while other pathways are mainly concentrated in ABC transporters, peroxisome, ribosome, and spliceosome (Fig. 5b, Table S6). Subsequently, we analyzed the expression patterns of the 45 DEGs involved in the KEGG pathways in high- and low-SOP materials. The results showed that 24 DEGs (53.3%) exhibited significantly higher expression levels in high-SOP materials than in low-SOP materials across different developmental stages in both Guiyang and Xingyi regions, while the remaining 21 DEGs (46.7%) displayed the opposite expression trend (Fig. 5c). These results indicate that these genes are closely associated with SOP in Brassica napus and are not affected by environmental factors. Among them, the 24 stably up-regulated genes can serve as molecular markers for high SOP, which can be applied to early marker-assisted selection in rapeseed breeding programs.

Identification candidate genes by combining GWAS and transcriptome sequencing analysis

As previously mentioned, 428 potential genes were found in this investigation by extending the nine SNPs that were strongly linked with SOP from GWAS analysis by 300 kb on each side (Table S7). Following transcriptome sequencing analysis, We analyzed the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and their overlapping sets in Guiyang and Xingyi at 20, 30, and 40 days; the results indicated that by integrating genome-wide association study (GWAS) with transcriptome data of DEGs from Guiyang at these time points, four candidate key genes associated with SOP were identified. These genes are BnaC01G0190300ZS, BnaC01G0188500ZS, BnaA10G0124700ZS, and BnaA10G0123700ZS. Furthermore, by combining GWAS with the transcriptome data of DEGs from Xingyi at 20, 30, and 40 days, two candidate key genes associated with SOP were obtained. Specifically, these are BnaC01G0188500ZS and BnaC01G0190300ZS (Table S5). Additionally, 117 common DEGs were identified in seeds at different developmental stages of Brassica napus lines with high- and low- SOP in Guiyang and Xingyi. By combining GWAS and transcriptome data, two key candidate genes (BnaC01G0188500ZS and BnaC01G0190300ZS ) associated with SOP were ultimately obtained (Fig. 6a; Table 3). The expression levels of these two candidate genes in seeds of high- SOP lines at different developmental periods in Guiyang and Xingyi were significantly higher than those of low- SOP lines, which suggests that these two candidate genes may play a significant role in the formation of high SOP (Fig. 6b).

Discussion

Enhancing total seed oil and protein (SOP) content is a pivotal strategy for maximizing the caloric and nutritional yield per unit area in Brassica napus. The SOP are intricate quantitative characteristic that are frequently influenced by external circumstances. Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) have been successfully applied to dissect important traits in Brassica napus, including seed oil content [23], fatty acid composition [24], Sclerotinia resistance [25], flowering time [26], and seed vigor [27]. While previous research utilizing GWAS and QTL mapping has identified SNPs associated with oil or protein content individually, studies specifically targeting the combined SOP trait remain limited. To address this gap, we integrated GWAS with transcriptome sequencing to identify candidate genes regulating SOP. We identified nine significant SNPs associated with SOP distributed across chromosomes A10, C1, and C3, explaining up to 7.02% of the phenotypic variance. Furthermore, we pinpointed two high-confidence candidate genes-BnaC01G0188500ZS and BnaC01G0190300ZS, whose expression profiles strongly correlated with high SOP accumulation. Notably, comparison with previous studies revealed that eight out of the nine identified SNPs were matched with overlapping SNPs, while only S10_17029919 represent novel SNP associated with SOP in Brassica napus (Table 2) [20–22].

A finding of our study is the significant influence of environmental conditions on SOP accumulation, with the Guiyang environment promoting significantly higher SOP levels compared to Xingyi. Research indicates that light intensity and temperature are critical environmental factors regulating oil and protein content. Elevated temperatures typically result in decreased oil content and increased protein content [28, 29]. Environment-induced transcriptional changes mediate the seed development process, and a total of 342 differentially accumulated proteins have been identified between lines with high and low oil content. Further research revealed that light-responsive genes, such as BnaLSH5, are activated under specific environmental conditions and contribute to the regulation of lipid synthesis [30]. The present study demonstrates that environmental conditions exert a substantial impact on Seed Protein Content (SOP) accumulation, with SOP levels in Guiyang being significantly higher than those in Xingyi. To explore the main reason why Guiyang’s SOP level is significantly higher than Xingyi’s, we further analyzed the temperature and precipitation during the milking period in Guiyang and Xingyi. The results showed that the average temperature during the milking period in Guiyang (15.83 °C) was significantly lower than that in Xingyi (18.83 °C). Studies have indicated that lower daytime temperatures and a cool nighttime environment can extend the grain milking time of rapeseed, reduce respiratory consumption, and thereby increase the final accumulation of oil and protein in the seeds. This explains why the SOP of Guiyang materials is significantly higher than that of Xingyi (Table S9). This phenotypic plasticity was mirrored at the transcriptional level, characterized by markedly higher gene expression variability in Guiyang. The reduced number of DEGs in Xingyi suggests that environmental stress or suboptimal conditions may suppress regulatory networks, thereby diminishing the phenotypic divergence between lines with high and low SOP levels. Brassica napus breeding efficiency has been significantly enhanced by integrating advanced technologies, such as gene editing, with the functional characterization of key genes controlling agronomic traits [31, 32]. For instance, CRISPR/Cas9-mediated suppression of BnLPAT2 and its homolog BnLPAT5 in Brassica napus led to a notable reduction in seed oil content. Specifically, the knockdown of these genes resulted in aberrant oil body morphology and significantly reduced oil accumulation. Conversely, increasing the expression of LPAT2 homologs in plants such as Arabidopsis thaliana markedly elevated specific unsaturated fatty acid levels and seed oil content [33]. Similarly, overexpression of GmWRI1c, an Arabidopsis WRI1 homolog, significantly elevated palmitic acid content and biomass in transgenic soybean hairy roots by activating the lipid synthesis pathway [34]. In contrast, overexpressing GmWRI1a in seeds reduced palmitic acid content from 11.08% (wild type) to 10.40-10.95% (transgenic lines) while significantly increasing total seed oil content [35]. Therefore, identifying key candidate genes controlling SOP is essential for molecular breeding aimed at developing superior Brassica napus genotypes. Given the limited research on candidate genes specifically regulating SOP, this study integrated transcriptome sequencing with GWAS to precisely identify relevant regulatory genes. A total of 428 candidate genes were identified within a 300-kb genomic window flanking the nine SNPs significantly associated with SOP (Table S7).

By intersecting the candidate genes derived from GWAS with the 117 common DEGs identified via transcriptome sequencing across the Xingyi and Guiyang environments, we identified two key candidate genes regulating SOP in Brassica napus seeds. BnaC01G0190300ZS encodes a protein of unknown function, and its expression levels were significantly higher in high-SOP lines (GH and XH) compared to low-SOP lines (GL and XL) (Fig. 6b). This suggests that upregulated expression of BnaC01G0190300ZS contributes to increased SOP accumulation. The second candidate, BnaC01G0188500ZS, is a homolog of Arabidopsis thaliana AT4G25310; it encodes a putative 2-oxoglutarate (2OG) and Fe(II)-dependent oxygenase and was enriched in the ABC transporter pathway according to KEGG analysis. 2OG family members (such as F3’H) enhance seed storability by reinforcing the physical integrity of the seed coat and hilum, indirectly maintaining seed physiologictions during late development [36]. Future functional validation via knockout and overexpression is required to elucidate the specific roles of these genes in regulating SOP accumulation in Brassica napus.

Materials and methods

Plant materials and phenotyping

In this study, 516 Brassica napus lines, including spring, winter, and semi-winter varieties, were gathered and planted at Southwest University’s experimental base in Beibei, Chongqing, China (29°45’N, 106°22’E; 238.57 m above sea level) over the course of four consecutive growing seasons from 2016 to 2019. All 516 Brassica napus lines were provided by the Integrative Science Center of Germplasm Creation in Western China (CHONGQING) Science City and Southwest University, College of Agronomy and Biotechnology, Southwest University. Each germplasm resource’s plot comprised two rows, with 10–12 plants planted in each row, spaced 20 cm and 30 cm apart, and all field experiments were carried out using a randomised complete block design with three biological replicates. Standard cultivation management was followed throughout the experiment. Five typical plants were chosen from the middle of each plot at the maturity stage, and their open-pollinated seeds were harvested one at a time. After the seeds were dried, the SOP were measured using near infrared reflectance spectroscopy (NIRS DS2500), and the sum of the seed oil content and protein content values is taken as the phenotypic value of SOP.

Genome-wide association analysis

The best linear unbiased prediction (BLUP) approach was utilized to evaluate the best linear unbiased prediction of SOP for each material using R language, based on field trial data from four consecutive growing seasons (2016–2019). To find genetic SNPs that are substantially linked to the target attributes, genome-wide association analysis (GWAS) was carried out in TASSEL 5.2.1 software using principal component analysis in conjunction with the K matrix model (PCA + K) [37]. Previous work has detailed the SNP genotyping information, kinship (K matrix), and population structure (Q matrix) of the association populations used in this investigation [38]. A criterion of P < 2.59 × 10− 6(1/valid SNPs used,-log10(1/385,692) = 5.59) was established for GWAS significant unity. Manhattan plots were created using the R package qqman to visualize genome-wide association data, and quantile-quantile plots (Q-Q plots) were created by comparing the observed SNP -log10(P) values (y-axis) with the anticipated P values (x-axis).

Transcriptome sequencing and identification of differentially expressed genes

Seed samples were systematically collected at 20, 30, and 40 days after anthesis, and the seeds were harvested in May 2022 for SOP determination. Five high SOP materials and five low SOP materials were planted in Guiyang City and Xingyi City, respectively, in 2021. For every combination of location and time point, three biological duplicates were established; each replication included a combination of seeds from three separate plants. And all samples were liquid nitrogen snap frozen and kept at -80 °C for transcriptome sequencing (RNA-Seq). GH20, GL20, GH30, GL30, GH40, and GL40 represent the seeds of high- and low- SOP materials in Guiyang at 20, 30, and 40 days after flowering, respectively. And the seeds of high and low SOP materials in Xingyi at 20, 30, and 40 days after blooming are denoted by the numbers XH20, XL20, XH30, XL30, XH40, and XL40, respectively. The 36 RNA-seq libraries were created by mixing seeds from the same location and the same developmental period of five high- and five low- SOP materials, which to balance the heterogeneity among the materials. Beijing Baimike Biotechnology Co. constructed and sequenced the RNA-seq libraries. FPKM (fragments per kilobase of exon per million mapped fragments) was used to measure the amount of gene expression. Cuffdiff software was used to identify DEGs, and the screening criteria were |log2 fold change| > 1.0 and false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05.

GO and KEGG enrichment analysis of DEGs

DEGs were subjected to KEGG pathway analysis and GO enrichment using the BMK online cloud platform BMKCloud (www.biocloud.net) [39]. For highly enriched GO entries, a false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05 served as the screening criteria [19]. Based on 117 common DEGs identified in extreme high- and low-SOP Brassica napus materials from Guiyang and Xingyi regions, combined with transcriptome data and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis, the transcriptome data of 45 KEGG pathway DEGs across three biological replicates at three seed developmental stages (20, 30, and 40 days after pollination, DAP) were subjected to mean value calculation. The expression levels were normalized using Log₂(FPKM + 1), and heatmap was plotted via TBtools software to visually display the up- or down-regulation patterns of genes at different stages [40].

Identification of potential candidate genes

A 300 kb upstream and downstream genomic area was identified as a potential interval for SNPs that were strongly linked with the aforementioned attributes in genome-wide association study (GWAS) in order to test candidate genes for controlling SOP in seeds [41]. The important candidate genes related to SOP were obtained by combining the candidate genes within the confidence intervals of the SNPs that were significantly associated with SOP with the common DEGs in seeds from Guiyang and Xingyi at different developmental periods.

Real time-quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR)

Total RNA was extracted from various tissues including roots, stems, leaves, flowers, flower buds, pistils, stamens, seeds, and silique walls of Brassica napus. A universal total RNA extraction kit was employed, with tissue samples processed according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The extracted RNA was subsequently stored at -80 °C. cDNA synthesis was performed using the Evo M-MLV Reverse Transcription Pre-mix Kit (containing gDNA removal reagent for qPCR) Ver.2 according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The reaction system was prepared as specified in the kit instructions. The resulting cDNA was aliquoted and stored at -20 °C. Using BnActin as the internal reference gene, real-time quantitative PCR was performed with the SYBR Green Pro Taq HS Premix Reagent Kit. The reaction volume was 10 µL, comprising 1 µL of 1:10 diluted cDNA template, 0.2 µL of each primer (10 µM), 5 µL of 2× SYBR Green Pro Taq HS Premix, and 3.6 µL of ddH2O. The reaction programme was set as follows: 30 s pre-denaturation at 95℃; 40 cycles (5 s at 95℃, 30 s at 60℃); 10 s at 72℃; followed by a melting curve analysis. The experiment was conducted using Bio-Rad CFX Manager 3.1 (admin), with data analysed via the 2^(-ΔΔCt) method to quantify results from three biological replicates. Primer sequences employed in the experiment are detailed in Table S8, with all primers validated for specificity via BLAST.

Conclusions

In this study, we identified nine SNPs significantly associated with total SOP in Brassica napus via a genome-wide association study (GWAS). By integrating these findings with 117 DEGs screened from seeds across varying developmental stages and environments, we pinpointed two priority candidate genes-BnaC01G0190300ZS and BnaC01G0188500ZS-located within the confidence intervals of significant SOP-associated SNPs. These findings provide a theoretical foundation and potential molecular markers for breeding high-SOP Brassica napus varieties.

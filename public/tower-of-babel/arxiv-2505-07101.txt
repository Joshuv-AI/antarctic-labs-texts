Eluder-Dimension-Free Regret for Stage-Wise Constrained Contextual Bandits via Offline Regression
Haichen Hu, David Simchi-Levi, Navid Azizan

Abstract
Contextual bandits with stage-wise constraints model decisions that must control risk while learning from their outcomes. We study this problem when only the selected action’s reward and cost are observed, with a general reward class and a convex class of nonlinear cost functions. We propose CS-UCCB, which combines offline regression, cost confidence sets, and upper counterfactual confidence bounds. Each regression fit defines a valid cost confidence set, while a geometry derived from covering entropy controls cumulative cost uncertainty along the sampled decisions. Under polylogarithmic covering entropy for both classes and polylogarithmic offline estimation error, the finite-action algorithm guarantees, with high probability, feasibility at every round and 𝒪 ~ ​ ( T ) \widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{T}) regret without an eluder-dimension term. We also extend the analysis to infinite actions under an explicit reward-design condition and compare our bound with existing eluder-dimension bounds.

Introduction

Contextual sequential decision-making has applications in online
platforms
(
Ban et al., 2024
)
and healthcare
(
Zhou et al., 2023
)
,
and is commonly modeled through contextual bandits

(
Tewari and Murphy, 2017
;
Tang et al., 2014
)
. Many applications also impose
safety, fairness, or risk constraints

(
Min and Azizan, 2024
;
Ceusters et al., 2023
;
Verma et al., 2024
;
Galichet et al., 2013
)
.
These requirements complicate exploration: a decision can be informative
yet inadmissible because its cost is uncertain. Existing
frameworks include knapsack bandits with cumulative resource budgets

(
Badanidiyuru et al., 2018
;
Immorlica et al., 2022
)
, survival bandits

(
Riou et al., 2024
;
Veroutis and Godin, 2024
)
, and conservative bandits that
compare cumulative performance with a baseline

(
Wu et al., 2016
;
Kazerouni et al., 2017
)
.

We consider stage-wise constraints on the expected cost of each
context-dependent randomized decision. The learner observes the reward
and cost of its selected action after acting, and receives no cost
labels for other actions. It must learn both responses while keeping
each chosen distribution feasible. Linear models have been studied
extensively
(
Amani et al., 2019
;
Moradipari et al., 2020
;
Pacchiano et al., 2021
;
Liu et al., 2021
)
.
For nonlinear models,
Pacchiano et al. (2025)
analyze regression
confidence sets using the eluder dimension, which describes how model
uncertainty can persist along sequentially chosen inputs

(
Russo and Van Roy, 2013
)
. This quantity can be much larger than the
logarithm of a covering number. Offline regression, by contrast,
provides a direct way to estimate these functions from a batch of data.
These observations motivate the question:

Can we use offline regression oracles to guarantee stage-wise
feasibility and optimal regret for nonlinear contextual bandits
without dependence on the eluder dimension?

Our contribution.

We propose CS-UCCB, an offline-oracle-based algorithm that combines
cost confidence sets with constrained counterfactual policy
simulation. At each round, we test the cost class against the
current offline fit, certifying feasible policies and controlling
cost uncertainty in optimistic exploration. Counterfactual simulation
evaluates historical policies at the current context to construct
reward confidence bounds from offline regression estimates.

Our main technical step connects cost entropy to sequential uncertainty.
Using convexity and the entropy of cost differences, we construct a
finite-dimensional uniform approximation and a normalized geometric
representation for analysis. The current confidence-set constraint supplies a common
empirical error budget for models in the current set. Combining this budget
with the derived geometry controls cumulative cost width under the
policies actually sampled. A cost-width bonus then compensates
for the reward loss of maintaining a pessimistic feasible region.
Together with UCCB reward confidence, this gives feasibility from the
first round and an explicit regret bound in terms of covering entropy
and offline estimation error. Polylogarithmic budgets yield

\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{T})
regret for finite actions.

We also extend the decision and regret analysis to infinite actions
under a cumulative reward-design condition. The geometric
representation is a proof tool; the algorithm does not require a
supplied kernel or feature map. Each cost confidence set uses one
convex regression constraint. With a tractable class representation,
this permits convex support queries for upper costs and
widths, without enumerating a covering net. We specify the required
regression and optimization access separately.

We write

,

.
The covering number

uses centers in

and balls of radius at most

. For a distribution
p
on

h(x,p)=\int h(x,a)\,p(da)
; for a policy
\pi
, we abbreviate

h(x,\pi)=h(x,\pi(\cdot\mid x))

2.1
Contextual Bandits with Stage-wise Constraints

We study a
T
-round sequential decision-making problem with context
space
\mathcal{X}
and finite action set
\mathcal{A}=\{a^{1},\ldots,a^{K}\}
.
The extension to infinite action spaces is presented in
Section
5
. At round
t
, the learner observes

X_{t}\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}Q_{x}
, selects a distribution

\pi_{t}(\cdot\mid X_{t})
, and samples
A_{t}
from it. After taking the
action, the learner observes its reward
R_{t}\in[0,1]
and cost

Y_{t}\in[0,1]
. We assume realizability in known classes

\mathcal{F},\mathcal{C}\subseteq[0,1]^{\mathcal{Z}}
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[R_{t}\mid\mathcal{H}_{t-1},X_{t},A_{t}]

\displaystyle=f^{*}(X_{t},A_{t}),\quad f^{*}\in\mathcal{F},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y_{t}\mid\mathcal{H}_{t-1},X_{t},A_{t}]

\displaystyle=c^{*}(X_{t},A_{t}),\quad c^{*}\in\mathcal{C}.

The history
\mathcal{H}_{t-1}
records past observations and learner
randomization used before the current context. Reward and cost noises need not be
independent. Both feedback signals concern only the selected action;
the learner receives no observations of unselected actions.

A policy is a measurable kernel
\pi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{A})
.
Given a known threshold
\tau\in(0,1)
, its stage-wise constraint is

c^{*}(x,\pi)\leq\tau\qquad\text{for every }x\in\mathcal{X}.

(1)

This is the conditional expected cost of the randomized decision at
that context. An individual sampled action or realized cost can exceed

\tau
; the guarantee concerns the distribution chosen before sampling.
As in the stage-wise model of
Pacchiano et al. (2025)
, replacing
(
1
) by an average over contexts would impose
a different constraint.
Concretely,
c^{*}(x,\pi)
averages only over the policy’s action
randomization while keeping the current context fixed; it does not
average over
Q_{x}
. A population constraint could offset a violation at
one context with slack at another, even though the former decision is
unsafe. Our requirement rules out this compensation: after observing

x
, the learner must choose a distribution whose conditional mean cost
is at most
\tau
.

Assumption 1
.

There are a known action
a_{0}
and a known constant
0\leq c_{0}<\tau

such that
c^{*}(x,a_{0})=c_{0}
for every
x\in\mathcal{X}
.
Write
\Delta=\tau-c_{0}
and let
\pi_{0}
always select
a_{0}
.

Since
a_{0}
and
c_{0}
are known, we discard all candidate cost functions
that violate this identity, retaining
c^{*}
in the class. Thus, without
loss of generality, we assume
c(x,a_{0})=c_{0}
for every
c\in\mathcal{C}
and

x\in\mathcal{X}
throughout;
\mathcal{C}
denotes this restricted class below.
The reward of
a_{0}
need not be known.
The baseline makes the feasible set nonempty and allows exploration
through safe mixtures, a standard device in stage-wise constrained
bandits
(
Pacchiano et al., 2021
;
Pacchiano et al., 2025
)
.

The benchmark is a pointwise optimal feasible policy,

\displaystyle\pi^{*}(\cdot\mid x)\in\mathop{\mathrm{argmax}}_{p\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{A})}

\displaystyle f^{*}(x,p)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle c^{*}(x,p)\leq\tau.

If
f^{*}
and
c^{*}
were known, this is the policy a clairvoyant decision
maker would use at each context. Regret measures the reward lost
relative to this benchmark, which obeys the same pointwise safety
requirement as the learner.
We seek simultaneous feasibility at every round and context, with high
probability, together with a bound on

\operatorname{Reg}(T)=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{X_{t}\sim Q_{x}}[f^{*}(X_{t},\pi^{*})-f^{*}(X_{t},\pi_{t})].

(2)

Each expectation is conditional on the pre-round history
\mathcal{H}_{t-1}

and averages over the current context
X_{t}\sim Q_{x}
; the action
expectation is included in
f^{*}(X_{t},\pi)
.
Regret therefore remains random through the learned policies. We assume
measurability of the suprema and policy rules below, and measurable
maximizers with a fixed tie-breaking rule.

Illustrative example.

In an adaptive clinical trial,
X_{t}
records a patient’s characteristics,

A_{t}
is the assigned dosage,
R_{t}
measures treatment benefit, and

Y_{t}
indicates an adverse event observed after treatment.
The unknown function
c^{*}(x,a)
is the probability of that event.
Before assigning a dosage, the trial requires the patient’s risk under
its randomized treatment rule to be at most
\tau
. Outcomes then
inform future decisions. A conservative treatment with known risk
provides the safe action. Thus both therapeutic benefit and risk are
learned from outcomes of the administered treatments.

2.2
Function Classes and Offline Regression

The analysis uses separate complexity assumptions for the reward and
cost classes, together with an offline regression guarantee.

Assumption 2
.

The reward class
\mathcal{F}
contains
f^{*}
and all possible reward-oracle
outputs. There are known constants
D_{\mathcal{F}}\geq 1
,
A_{\mathcal{F}}\geq e
,
and
p_{\mathcal{F}}\geq 1
, independent of
T
and
\delta
, such that
for
0<u\leq 1
,

\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{F}}(u)=\log\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{F},u,\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\leq D_{\mathcal{F}}[\log(A_{\mathcal{F}}/u)]^{p_{\mathcal{F}}}.

(3)

The reward class need not be convex or admit a linear parameterization.

Assumption 3
.

The class
\mathcal{C}
is closed under convex combinations of functions. There
are known constants
D_{\mathcal{C}}\geq 1
,
A_{\mathcal{C}}\geq e
, and
p_{\mathcal{C}}\geq 1
,
independent of
T
and
\delta
, such that, for
0<u\leq 1
,

\log\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{C},u,\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\leq D_{\mathcal{C}}[\log(A_{\mathcal{C}}/u)]^{p_{\mathcal{C}}}.

(4)

Convexity here means closure under mixtures of cost functions, not
convexity of each function in its inputs. Nonlinear basis expansions
and suitably bounded infinite series provide examples. The exponent

p_{\mathcal{C}}>1
permits infinite-dimensional convex classes; when

p_{\mathcal{C}}=1
, the all-scale bound forces finite affine dimension.
We give examples in Appendix
G
.
In Appendix
H.5
, we prove that this cost
condition also bounds the eluder dimension polylogarithmically.
Appendix
H.4
explains why small
covering entropy alone is insufficient for general nonconvex costs.

Definition 1
.

For observations
L_{i}=h^{*}(Z_{i})+\xi_{i}
with
h^{*}\in\mathcal{K}
and conditionally mean-zero
noise, an offline regression oracle returns a predictor
\widehat{h}^{t}

from the first
t-1
observations. For every admissible data-collection
rule and failure probability
\rho\in(0,1)
, its guarantee is

\mathbb{P}\left\{\sum_{i<t}(\widehat{h}^{t}(Z_{i})-h^{*}(Z_{i}))^{2}\leq\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{off}}(\mathcal{K},t,\rho)\right\}\geq 1-\rho.

(5)

The inputs may depend on past observations, but are chosen before their
current labels are observed. The same fitted predictor is evaluated on
all
t-1
samples in this guarantee.

This distinction matters: the offline bound refits once on the full
historical batch and evaluates
\widehat{h}^{t}
retrospectively at every

Z_{i}
. By contrast, an online guarantee controls
\widetilde{h}^{i}(Z_{i})
,
where the predictor may change with
i
and is formed before seeing

L_{i}
. The confidence-set construction uses the batch guarantee to test candidate cost models
against each newly fitted predictor on the available observations.

Assumption 4
.

We have offline oracles for
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{C}
, with reward output

\widehat{f}^{t}\in\mathcal{F}
and known budgets in (
5
).
The budgets are nondecreasing in sample size and nonincreasing in
the failure probability
\rho
. All oracle outputs can be evaluated at queried
context–action pairs.

Clipping an output to
[0,1]
preserves its error guarantee. The cost
output need not belong to
\mathcal{C}
because it serves only as a reference
in the confidence-set constraints. The statistical results keep the entropy
parameters and both oracle budgets explicit. Polylogarithmic offline
budgets give the
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{T})
specialization.

3.1
Cost Confidence Sets

Let
B_{1}^{\mathcal{C}}=B_{1}^{\mathcal{F}}=0
. For
t\geq 2
, we use the
known offline estimation budgets

B_{t}^{\mathcal{K}}=\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{off}}(\mathcal{K},t,\delta/(4T)),\qquad\mathcal{K}\in\{\mathcal{F},\mathcal{C}\}.

(6)

We test the full cost class against the current fit on all past
observations, using one convex squared-error constraint:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{C},

(7)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{t}

\displaystyle=\left\{c\in\mathcal{C}:\sum_{i<t}(c(Z_{i})-\widehat{c}^{t}(Z_{i}))^{2}\leq B_{t}^{\mathcal{C}}\right\}.

Thus
{\mathcal{S}}_{t}
contains the candidates consistent with the current
regression estimate. The test is convex in
c
and uses the full data
prefix. Each round constructs its own confidence set. For replay,
we archive its defining fit, data prefix, and estimation budget.

The oracle guarantee ensures that the true cost function passes all
these tests simultaneously, with high probability. The current test
also gives a uniform empirical pair bound:

\sum_{i<t}(c(Z_{i})-c^{\prime}(Z_{i}))^{2}\leq 4B_{t}^{\mathcal{C}},\qquad c,c^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t}.

(8)

We establish these statements in Lemma
1
. They turn
the offline estimation guarantee into a common error budget for all
cost models considered by the decision rule.

Choosing the safe action.

If a required confidence set is empty at round
t
, we choose the safe
action
a_{0}
in this and all remaining rounds, setting

\pi_{s}(\cdot\mid x)=\pi_{0}=\delta_{a_{0}}
for every
s\geq t
and context

x
. This instruction is recorded for replay. The rule keeps the policy well defined
on histories where the regression confidence guarantees fail.
Lemma
1
shows that the instruction is never needed on the
cost-oracle confidence event. The algorithm starts with robust policy
optimization at round one; it has no initial exploration phase.

For a nonempty confidence set, define

\displaystyle V_{t}^{\delta}(x,p)

\displaystyle=\sup_{c\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t}}c(x,p),

(9)

\displaystyle w_{t}(x,a)

\displaystyle=\sup_{c,c^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t}}|c(x,a)-c^{\prime}(x,a)|,

\displaystyle\Pi_{t}^{\delta}(x)

\displaystyle=\{p\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{A}):V_{t}^{\delta}(x,p)\leq\tau\}.

We write
w_{t}(x,p)=\int w_{t}(x,a)\,p(da)
. The safe action has upper
cost
c_{0}
and width zero, so it is always robustly feasible.

Role of the cost confidence sets.

The convex confidence sets serve both estimation and decision-making.
Truth retention certifies the robust feasible region, while the
empirical pair bound supplies the input to our cumulative-width
analysis. Convexity and covering entropy then provide a geometric
representation in which this budget controls uncertainty along the
sampled decisions. The width bonus uses the resulting bound to
compensate for the reward loss of conservative policies in the
optimism and regret proofs.
The cost support queries maximize linear functionals over
{\mathcal{S}}_{t}
,
and the finite-action policy problem has a linear objective and a
convex robust-cost constraint. Both can therefore be formulated as
convex programs. With a tractable finite-dimensional affine class
representation, each support problem has a single empirical
second-order cone constraint. We give these formulations and their
computational requirements in Appendix
I
.

3.2
Constrained Counterfactual Simulation

For finite actions, we adapt UCCB
(
Xu and Zeevi, 2020
)
with the
cumulative policy weights and the deterministic reward bonus

\omega_{t}(x,a)=\sum_{i<t}\pi_{i}(a\mid x),\qquad q_{t}(x,a)=\frac{1}{1+\omega_{t}(x,a)}.

(10)

For a distribution
p
, let
q_{t}(x,p)=\sum_{a}p(a)q_{t}(x,a)
.
The unit pseudocount regularizes the denominator and does not represent
an executed action or a regression observation. We set

\beta_{t}=\sqrt{tH_{t}/K}
, with the known reward budget
H_{t}
specified
in (
12
), and choose

\pi_{t}(\cdot\mid x)\in\mathop{\mathrm{argmax}}_{p\in\Pi_{t}^{\delta}(x)}\{\widehat{f}^{t}(x,p)+\beta_{t}q_{t}(x,p)+\alpha_{r}w_{t}(x,p)\}.

(11)

The reward bonus favors actions with little historical policy mass
at the current context. The cost-width bonus compensates for
conservative reward loss
(
Pacchiano et al., 2025
)
.
The robust constraint controls feasibility throughout this tradeoff.
The rule applies from the first round and can explore an uncertain
action by mixing it with
a_{0}
while maintaining the policy-level
constraint.

To evaluate (
10
) at a newly observed context, we
replay the archived policy rules at that context. The fits, confidence
sets, coefficients, and tie-breaking choices at historical stages are
kept fixed. Replay therefore reconstructs the actual historical

We first state the finite-action guarantee and then explain how

Fix

Remark 1

Lemma 1

The cost confidence sets are convex subsets of
\mathcal{C}

With probability at least

We prove these statements in Appendix

{\mathcal{S}}_{t}

Property 1

With probability at least
1-\delta/2
, the cost confidence sets satisfy

(16)

for the actual history-dependent policy sequence. The result holds
for any such sampling rule using these confidence sets.

The width argument uses only the class geometry and the empirical
pair certificate. It applies to any history-measurable confidence
sets satisfying that certificate. The expectations track the actual
sampling policies, linking cost learning to exploration under
selected-action feedback.

Proof sketch.

We first use the offline cost guarantee to retain
c^{*}
in every
convex confidence set. For any
c,c^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t}
, comparison with the
same current fit gives

\sum_{i<t}(c(Z_{i})-c^{\prime}(Z_{i}))^{2}\leq 4B_{t}^{\mathcal{C}}\leq B_{T}^{\rm pair}.

The convex entropy bound then lets us approximate all cost differences
by a common finite-dimensional space. Convexity keeps the symmetric
convex hull of cost differences inside the difference class. If too
many differences remain separated from the span of those already
chosen at the target resolution, that hull would require more covering
centers than the entropy bound permits.
John’s ellipsoid theorem supplies features of norm at most one and
coefficients of norm at most

2\sqrt{m_{T}}
. The empirical pair budget bounds their quadratic norm
under the matrix of actual sampled features. Because this matrix
accumulates the selected pairs, its next increment is generated by
the policy whose width enters the regret bound. The elliptical-potential
inequality and a martingale comparison inequality therefore yield
Property
1
.

For rewards, offline estimation and a uniform covering argument give,
simultaneously for all policies,

\left|\mathbb{E}_{X}[\widehat{f}^{t}(X,\pi)-f^{*}(X,\pi)]\right|\leq\beta_{t}\mathbb{E}_{X}q_{t}(X,\pi)+\frac{K\beta_{t}}{4t}.

In the analysis, we mix the true optimal policy with the safe action
until it is robustly feasible. Its reward loss is bounded by
\alpha_{r}

times its own cost width. The policy optimization then gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{X}f^{*}(X,\pi^{*})\leq{}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{X}[\widehat{f}^{t}(X,\pi_{t})+\beta_{t}q_{t}(X,\pi_{t})

(17)

\displaystyle+\alpha_{r}w_{t}(X,\pi_{t})]+\frac{K\beta_{t}}{4t}.

with the optimism argument; the remaining uncertainty is charged to

the deterministic potential bound in Lemma

5
Extension to Infinite Actions

To extend CS-UCCB to infinite action spaces, we replace the finite-action
reward bonus with a regularized nonlinear design bonus. The cost update
continues to use the selected action’s observed cost. The proofs are
in Appendix

, and a nonlinear continuous-action
example appears in Appendix
G.4
.

Definition 2
.

For a context

\mathcal{A}

\mathcal{V}_{x}(a\|\rho):=\sup_{f,f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}}\frac{(f(x,a)-f^{\prime}(x,a))^{2}}{\int_{\mathcal{A}}(f(x,a^{\prime})-f^{\prime}(x,a^{\prime}))^{2}\,d\rho(a^{\prime})}.

We adopt the conventions
0/0=0

b/0=+\infty

Write

We choose this design using the reward class
(
Agarwal et al., 2024
)

For a known reference mass

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}_{t-1}(\cdot|x)

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{x}\rho_{x}^{*}+\sum_{i<t}\pi_{i}(\cdot|x)}{d_{x}+t-1},

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{V}_{x}(a\|\bar{\rho}_{t-1})}{d_{x}+t-1}.

6
Discussion

CS-UCCB addresses contextual bandits with stage-wise constraints and
selected-action reward and cost feedback. Offline cost estimates
define confidence sets that certify
feasibility and bound the cost uncertainty in the regret analysis.
For convex cost classes with polylogarithmic covering entropy, this
analysis yields
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{T})
regret under the stated
reward and oracle conditions. The geometry needed for this bound is
derived directly from the cost class, and each confidence-set query
uses one convex regression constraint. Our bound has no explicit
eluder-dimension term; the cost assumptions themselves imply a
polylogarithmic eluder dimension. The example in Appendix
H

gives a logarithmic cost improvement over the published bound at its
stated resolution, together with a polynomial reward improvement.
An open question is which weaker conditions on nonconvex cost classes
permit comparable guarantees without paying for a much larger convex
hull. The counterexample in Appendix
H.4

shows that small covering entropy alone is insufficient.

Appendix A
Detailed Related Work

Constrained contextual bandits.

Stage-wise constraints have been studied for linear payoffs

(
Amani et al., 2019
;
Moradipari et al., 2020
;
Pacchiano et al., 2021
;
Liu et al., 2021
)
.
For nonlinear models,
Pacchiano et al. (2025)
use regression
confidence sets and eluder dimension to control uncertainty in rewards
and costs. We use the same selected-action reward and cost feedback,
with the additional structure of a convex cost class and its entropy
profile. Our reward class need not be convex. These model assumptions,
rather than a change in the observation protocol, distinguish the
results. We give a quantitative comparison with the published finite-class
theorem in Appendix
H
.

Regression-oracle methods also address packing and covering constraints

(
Slivkins et al., 2023
)
and contextual knapsack budgets

(
Han et al., 2023
)
. Bandits with non-monotonic resource use and
online optimization with stochastic or long-term constraints provide
related cumulative formulations

(
Kumar and Kleinberg, 2022
;
Yu et al., 2017
;
Liakopoulos et al., 2019
)
.
These formulations impose constraints over a horizon, whereas our
feasibility requirement applies to each
context-dependent randomized decision at every round. Conservative
bandits compare cumulative performance with a baseline

(
Wu et al., 2016
;
Kazerouni et al., 2017
;
Deb et al., 2025
)
;
this differs from learning a separate cost response subject to a
per-round threshold.

Kernel bandits and safe Gaussian-process optimization.

GP-UCB relates regret to maximal information gain

(
Srinivas et al., 2010
)
, and
Chowdhury and Gopalan (2017)

develop confidence bounds and algorithms for kernelized bandits with
rewards in a reproducing kernel Hilbert space.
SafeOpt
(
Sui et al., 2015
)
and StageOpt
(
Sui et al., 2018
)

use Gaussian-process confidence bounds to expand a safe region and
certify sampled decisions;
Amani et al. (2020)
give regret bounds
for safe Gaussian-process bandit optimization. These approaches use a
specified kernel or Gaussian-process model. Our assumptions concern
the convex cost class and its uniform entropy, from which we derive
an approximation geometry for analysis. Our safety requirement applies
to the conditional expected cost of each randomized decision.

Regression-oracle contextual bandits.

RegCB
(
Foster et al., 2018
)
uses least-squares confidence bounds,
while SquareCB
(
Foster and Rakhlin, 2020
)
reduces realizable contextual
bandits to online regression. Offline-oracle approaches include
inverse-gap weighting
(
Simchi-Levi and Xu, 2021
)
and UCB-style methods

(
Xu and Zeevi, 2020
;
Levy et al., 2024
)
. Other developments study tabular
MDPs with low oracle complexity
(
Qian et al., 2024
)
, functional
regression, finite hypothesis classes, and related sequential settings

(
Hu et al., 2025
;
Foster et al., 2024
;
Krishnamurthy et al., 2021
;
Wei and Luo, 2021
;
Sen et al., 2021
)
.

Qin and Zhang (2026)
develop an offline-oracle framework using
exploitative
F
-design and a decision–offline-estimation complexity
measure. We add an unknown cost model and certify stage-wise feasible
policies from the selected actions’ outcomes. For infinite actions, we
state explicitly the additional cumulative reward-design condition.
Small-ball methods also yield complexity-dependent error rates
for regularized regression
(
Lecué and Mendelson, 2017
)
.

Our use of offline regression concerns statistical estimation.
Evaluating convex confidence-set supports and solving the constrained
policy problem are separate computational operations. The version-space
formulation avoids enumerating a covering net. We give its optimization
formulations and access requirements in
Appendix
I
.

Eluder dimension and disagreement complexity.

Russo and Van Roy (2013)
introduce eluder dimension for optimistic
exploration. For reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces,

Huang et al. (2021)
establish quantitative relations between
eluder dimension and information gain.

Foster et al. (2021)
develop a disagreement-based
view of instance-dependent contextual-bandit complexity and implement
AdaCB with offline regression. Broader treatments cover neural-network
learning complexity
(
Anthony and Bartlett, 2009
)
and structural relations
among eluder and disagreement-type dimensions

(
Li et al., 2022
;
Hanneke, 2024
)
. The difference between a
sequential complexity and a covering entropy motivates our analysis.
Convexity alone does not make these quantities equal: we give an explicit
convex-class separation in Appendix
H
.
Our proof bounds a cumulative prediction width from the full entropy
profile and the empirical certificates supplied by the cost confidence sets.

Entropy, approximation widths, and misspecification.

Relations between entropy and finite-dimensional approximation are
classical in approximation theory:
Carl (1981)
compares
entropy and approximation numbers for Banach-space operators, and

Pinkus (1985)
develops the theory of approximation widths.
In bandits,
Lattimore et al. (2020)
study the interaction
between linear approximation error, feature geometry, and regret.
Our argument constructs an approximating space from the function
class and accounts explicitly for its uniform approximation error.

Appendix B
Useful Math Tools

We collect the tools used in the cost-width analysis and the regret
comparison. The first lemma controls bounded martingale differences
in Appendix
H
. The remaining two compare
conditional means with observed sums and bound the elliptical
potential in Appendix
C
. Let
(\mathcal{H}_{t})
be a
filtration, and write
\mathbb{E}_{t-1}
for conditional expectation given

\mathcal{H}_{t-1}
.

Lemma 2
.

If
X_{t}
is adapted and
|X_{t}|\leq R
, then for any fixed
n
and
\delta\in(0,1)
, with probability at least
1-\delta
,

\left|\sum_{t=1}^{n}(X_{t}-\mathbb{E}_{t-1}X_{t})\right|\leq R\sqrt{2n\log(2/\delta)}.

This is the bounded-increment martingale inequality; see, for example,
Sason (2011)
. We include the argument with the constants used here.

Proof of Lemma
2
.

The case
R=0
is immediate. Otherwise, put

\mu_{t}=\mathbb{E}_{t-1}X_{t}
and
D_{t}=X_{t}-\mu_{t}
.
For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, define the conditional log-moment function

\psi_{t}(\lambda)=\log\mathbb{E}_{t-1}e^{\lambda X_{t}}-\lambda\mu_{t}.

Boundedness permits differentiation under the conditional expectation.
We have
\psi_{t}(0)=\psi_{t}^{\prime}(0)=0
, and

\psi_{t}^{\prime\prime}(\lambda)=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{t-1}[X_{t}^{2}e^{\lambda X_{t}}]}{\mathbb{E}_{t-1}e^{\lambda X_{t}}}-\left(\frac{\mathbb{E}_{t-1}[X_{t}e^{\lambda X_{t}}]}{\mathbb{E}_{t-1}e^{\lambda X_{t}}}\right)^{2}\leq R^{2}.

Integrating twice gives
\psi_{t}(\lambda)\leq\lambda^{2}R^{2}/2
for
both signs of
\lambda
. Consequently,

\mathbb{E}_{t-1}e^{\lambda D_{t}}\leq e^{\lambda^{2}R^{2}/2}.

Iterating conditional expectations, we obtain

\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda\sum_{t=1}^{n}D_{t}}\leq e^{n\lambda^{2}R^{2}/2}
.
For
u>0
, Markov’s inequality with
\lambda=u/(nR^{2})
yields

\mathbb{P}\!\left(\sum_{t=1}^{n}D_{t}\geq u\right)\leq e^{-\lambda u+n\lambda^{2}R^{2}/2}=e^{-u^{2}/(2nR^{2})}.

Applying the same argument to
-D_{t}
and adding the two tail
probabilities gives
2e^{-u^{2}/(2nR^{2})}
. Substituting

u=R\sqrt{2n\log(2/\delta)}
proves the claim.
∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
0\leq L_{t}\leq 1
be adapted and put

\mu_{t}=\mathbb{E}[L_{t}\mid\mathcal{H}_{t-1}]
. For every
\zeta\in(0,1)
, with
probability at least
1-\zeta
, simultaneously for all
n\leq T
,

\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mu_{t}\leq 2\sum_{t=1}^{n}L_{t}+2\log(1/\zeta).

(21)

The conditional means may depend on the entire past.

Proof of Lemma
3
.

Set
a=1-e^{-1}>1/2
. For
u\in[0,1]
, the chord bound for the convex
function
e^{-u}
gives

e^{-u}\leq 1-au.

Consequently,

\mathbb{E}[e^{-L_{t}}\mid\mathcal{H}_{t-1}]\leq 1-a\mu_{t}\leq e^{-a\mu_{t}}.

We define the nonnegative process

M_{n}=\exp\left\{a\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mu_{t}-\sum_{t=1}^{n}L_{t}\right\},\qquad M_{0}=1.

The preceding conditional inequality implies

\mathbb{E}[M_{n}\mid\mathcal{H}_{n-1}]\leq M_{n-1}
.
Let
\nu
be the first
n\leq T
with
M_{n}\geq 1/\zeta
, and set

\nu=T
if there is no such index. We obtain

\mathbb{E}M_{\nu}\leq 1
by the optional stopping theorem for bounded stopping times. Since
M_{\nu}
is
nonnegative,

\mathbb{P}\{\max_{n\leq T}M_{n}\geq 1/\zeta\}\leq\zeta\mathbb{E}M_{\nu}\leq\zeta.

Outside this event, taking logarithms yields

a\sum_{t\leq n}\mu_{t}\leq\sum_{t\leq n}L_{t}+\log(1/\zeta)
for every

n\leq T
. Both terms on the right are nonnegative, so
a^{-1}<2

implies (
21
). This argument uses no
independence assumption on the observations.
∎

Lemma 4
.

Let
m\geq 1
, let
\phi_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
satisfy
\|\phi_{t}\|_{2}\leq 1
, and
define

\Sigma_{t}=I_{m}+\sum_{i<t}\phi_{i}\phi_{i}^{\top},\qquad L_{t}=\phi_{t}^{\top}\Sigma_{t}^{-1}\phi_{t}.

For every sequence, including an adaptively selected sequence,

0\leq L_{t}\leq 1,\qquad\sum_{t=1}^{n}L_{t}\leq 2m\log(1+n/m)\quad(n\geq 1).

(22)

Proof of Lemma
4
.

The matrix inequality
\Sigma_{t}\succeq I_{m}
gives

0\leq L_{t}\leq\|\phi_{t}\|_{2}^{2}\leq 1
.
By the matrix determinant lemma for a rank-one update,

\det\Sigma_{t+1}=\det\Sigma_{t}(1+L_{t}).

For
u\in[0,1]
, the derivative of

\log(1+u)-u/2
is nonnegative. It follows that

\sum_{t=1}^{n}L_{t}\leq 2\sum_{t=1}^{n}\log(1+L_{t})=2\log\det\Sigma_{n+1}.

We apply the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality to the
m

positive eigenvalues of
\Sigma_{n+1}
. Since

\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_{n+1})=m+\sum_{t\leq n}\|\phi_{t}\|_{2}^{2}\leq m+n
,

\log\det\Sigma_{n+1}\leq m\log\left(\frac{\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_{n+1})}{m}\right)\leq m\log(1+n/m).

Combining the two bounds proves (
22
).
All steps are deterministic.
∎

Appendix C
Proofs of the Cost Confidence Guarantees

We first prove Lemma
1
: the convex regression confidence
sets retain the true cost function, and their defining regression
constraints control every pair of candidate models. We then prove
Property
1
by converting this empirical
bound into cumulative-width control. Convexity and covering entropy
provide a fixed finite-dimensional approximation; an elliptical
potential argument controls its uncertainty along the selected pairs.
We state auxiliary results where they enter these arguments and give
their detailed proofs in Appendix
J

Appendix D
Proof of Regret Guarantee with Finite Actions

We prove Theorem
1
in three steps. First,
a uniform covering argument turns offline reward error into
confidence bounds for every policy. Next, we verify that replay
computes the required historical policy masses and bound the
cumulative reward bonus. Finally, the cost confidence guarantees allow
us to construct a safe comparator and establish constrained
optimism. The resulting one-round regret bound separates into
reward-bonus terms and a cost-width term, which we sum using the
reward potential below and Property
1
.

We work with the pre-context history
\mathcal{H}_{t-1}
from
Section
2
. It contains both observed signals from
previous rounds and any random seed used to define the current
policy rule. Given this history,
\pi_{t}(\cdot\mid x)
is a fixed
measurable kernel for every
x
. Both current signals arrive after
the current action, so neither enters this kernel. Throughout this
appendix, an expectation over
X\sim Q_{x}
integrates a fresh context
with the realized history fixed. We never condition that fresh
context on a future confidence event.

D.1
A uniform reward transfer bound

The offline oracle controls error on the observed pairs. To obtain
policy confidence, we first relate this empirical error to error
averaged over the historical sampling policies. We establish the
relation for a fixed predictor and then extend it uniformly to the
reward class so that it applies to the fitted predictor.

Fix
T\geq 2
,
\delta\in(0,1)
, and

\eta_{\mathcal{F}}=1/(6T)
. We choose once and for all a deterministic proper

\eta_{\mathcal{F}}
-net
\mathcal{F}_{\eta}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
of size
N_{\mathcal{F}}
.
This net is used only in the proof. Its centers need not include
f^{*}
,
and the algorithm does not enumerate them.

For each fixed
f\in\mathcal{F}
, define

L_{f,i}=(f(Z_{i})-f^{*}(Z_{i}))^{2},\qquad\mu_{f,i}=\int_{\mathcal{X}}\int_{\mathcal{A}}(f(x,a)-f^{*}(x,a))^{2}\,\pi_{i}(da\mid x)\,Q_{x}(dx).

The second expression holds the pre-round history fixed. Because

X_{i}
is independent of that history and

A_{i}\sim\pi_{i}(\cdot\mid X_{i})
, it is a version of

\mathbb{E}[L_{f,i}\mid\mathcal{H}_{i-1}]
for every deterministic
f
.
The integral formula defines
\mu_{f,i}
jointly for all functions,
without choosing different exceptional sets for different
f
.
Boundedness gives
0\leq L_{f,i}\leq 1
.

Lemma 10
.

For each deterministic
f\in\mathcal{F}
,
1\leq t\leq T
, and

\rho\in(0,1)
, with probability at least
1-\rho
,

\sum_{i<t}\mu_{f,i}\leq 2\sum_{i<t}L_{f,i}+2\log(1/\rho).

Proof of Lemma
10
.

Let
b=1-e^{-1}>1/2
. Convexity of the exponential function gives

e^{-u}\leq 1-bu
for
u\in[0,1]
. Hence

\mathbb{E}[e^{-L_{f,i}}\mid\mathcal{H}_{i-1}]\leq 1-b\mu_{f,i}\leq e^{-b\mu_{f,i}}.

We obtain the nonnegative supermartingale

M_{f,n}=\exp\left\{b\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{f,i}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}L_{f,i}\right\},\qquad M_{f,0}=1.

In particular,
\mathbb{E}M_{f,t-1}\leq 1
. Markov’s inequality shows that

M_{f,t-1}\leq 1/\rho
except on an event of probability at most
\rho
.
Taking logarithms and dividing by
b
gives the claim, because

b^{-1}<2
and both terms on the right are nonnegative.
For
t=1
, both sums are empty and the same conclusion holds
deterministically.
∎

The latest fit depends on the same observations used to measure
its error. We therefore make the preceding inequality simultaneous
over all predictors and rounds before substituting that fit. A
finite covering net supplies the common event in the next lemma.

Lemma 11
.

There is an event
\mathcal{E}_{R,\mathrm{cov}}
of probability at least

1-\delta/4
on which, simultaneously for every
1\leq t\leq T

and every
f\in\mathcal{F}
,

\sum_{i<t}\mu_{f,i}\leq 2\sum_{i<t}(f(Z_{i})-f^{*}(Z_{i}))^{2}+2\log\frac{4TN_{\mathcal{F}}}{\delta}+6(t-1)\eta_{\mathcal{F}}.

(36)

Proof of Lemma
11
.

We apply Lemma
10
to each of the

TN_{\mathcal{F}}
pairs
(t,v)
with
v\in\mathcal{F}_{\eta}
, with failure probability
at most
\rho=\delta/(4TN_{\mathcal{F}})
. A union bound gives a single event
whose complement has probability at most
\delta/4
and on which

\sum_{i<t}\mu_{v,i}\leq 2\sum_{i<t}L_{v,i}+2\log\frac{4TN_{\mathcal{F}}}{\delta}\quad\text{for every }t\leq T,\ v\in\mathcal{F}_{\eta}.

Now fix any
f\in\mathcal{F}
and choose
v\in\mathcal{F}_{\eta}
with

\|f-v\|_{\infty}\leq\eta_{\mathcal{F}}
. Since all three functions

f,v,f^{*}
take values in
[0,1]
, we have pointwise

|(f-f^{*})^{2}-(v-f^{*})^{2}|=|f-v|\,|f+v-2f^{*}|\leq 2\eta_{\mathcal{F}}.

The same bound after integration gives

|\mu_{f,i}-\mu_{v,i}|\leq 2\eta_{\mathcal{F}}
. Substituting both
approximations into the preceding finite-net inequality yields

\sum_{i<t}\mu_{f,i}\leq 2\sum_{i<t}L_{f,i}+2\log\frac{4TN_{\mathcal{F}}}{\delta}+(4+2)(t-1)\eta_{\mathcal{F}},

which is (
36
). The extension from the finite
net is deterministic and holds for every
f
on the same event.
We may therefore evaluate this inequality at an adaptively fitted
function after the event has been formed.
∎

In particular, substituting
f=\widehat{f}^{t}
means evaluating the random
map
f\mapsto\mu_{f,i}
at the latest fitted function. It does not
assert that
\widehat{f}^{t}
was measurable before observation
i
, and
does not move a future fit inside an earlier conditional expectation.
This distinction is the reason for taking the fixed-net event first.

D.2
From offline reward fits to policy confidence

We now apply Lemma
11
to the
latest offline fit and combine it with the oracle error budget.
The goal is a reward-confidence bound for any policy, with
uncertainty measured by the bonus used in the algorithm.
For finite actions, write

\omega_{t}(x,a)=\sum_{i<t}\pi_{i}(a\mid x),\qquad q_{t}(x,a)=\frac{1}{1+\omega_{t}(x,a)},\qquad Q_{t}(\pi)=\mathbb{E}_{X}\sum_{a}\pi(a\mid X)q_{t}(X,a).

These are the bonuses in (
10
). The unit
pseudocount makes every denominator positive, including at
t=1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{R,\mathrm{or}}
be the event that

\sum_{i<t}(\widehat{f}^{t}(Z_{i})-f^{*}(Z_{i}))^{2}\leq B_{t}^{\mathcal{F}}\quad\text{for every }2\leq t\leq T.

Each fit uses confidence
\delta/(4T)
as specified in
(
6

4
and a union
bound therefore imply

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{R,\mathrm{or}}^{c})\leq(T-1)\frac{\delta}{4T}\leq\frac{\delta}{4}.

We set

\mathcal{E}_{R}=\mathcal{E}_{R,\mathrm{or}}\cap\mathcal{E}_{R,\mathrm{cov}}
, so that

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{R})\geq 1-\delta/2
.

On this event, the uniform inequality (
36
)
can be evaluated at
\widehat{f}^{t}\in\mathcal{F}
. By the conditional-mean
identity

\mu_{f,i}=\mathbb{E}_{X,A\sim\pi_{i}}[(f(X,A)-f^{*}(X,A))^{2}],

(37)

we obtain, for all
t\leq T
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i<t}\mathbb{E}_{X,A\sim\pi_{i}}[(\widehat{f}^{t}(X,A)-f^{*}(X,A))^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq 2B_{t}^{\mathcal{F}}+2\log\frac{4TN_{\mathcal{F}}}{\delta}+6(t-1)\eta_{\mathcal{F}}

(38)

\displaystyle=H_{t}-1.

For
t=1
, the left side is zero and the inequality holds without
an oracle guarantee. Notice that every historical expectation in
(
38
) evaluates the same latest fit

\widehat{f}^{t}
, with the corresponding historical policy held fixed.

Equation (
38
) controls squared error
under the historical policies. The next lemma converts this bound
into expected reward error under any candidate policy; the factor

Q_{t}(\pi)
accounts for how little mass its actions received from
those historical policies.

Lemma 12
.

On
\mathcal{E}_{R}
, simultaneously for every
1\leq t\leq T
and every
measurable policy
\pi
,

\left|\mathbb{E}_{X}[\widehat{f}^{t}(X,\pi)-f^{*}(X,\pi)]\right|\leq\sqrt{H_{t}Q_{t}(\pi)}.

Proof of Lemma
12
.

Fix a realized history in
\mathcal{E}_{R}
, a round
t
, and a policy
\pi
.
Set
e_{t}(x,a)=\widehat{f}^{t}(x,a)-f^{*}(x,a)
, which belongs to
[-1,1]
.
We apply the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality with respect to the measure

Q_{x}(dx)\pi(a\mid x)
:

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{X}\sum_{a}\pi(a\mid X)e_{t}(X,a)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathbb{E}_{X}\sum_{a}\frac{\pi(a\mid X)}{1+\omega_{t}(X,a)}\right)\left(\mathbb{E}_{X}\sum_{a}\pi(a\mid X)(1+\omega_{t}(X,a))e_{t}(X,a)^{2}\right).

The first factor equals
Q_{t}(\pi)
. In the second factor, boundedness
and
\pi(a\mid x)\leq 1
imply

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{X}\sum_{a}\pi(a\mid X)(1+\omega_{t}(X,a))e_{t}(X,a)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\mathbb{E}_{X}\sum_{a}\omega_{t}(X,a)e_{t}(X,a)^{2}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{i<t}\mathbb{E}_{X,A\sim\pi_{i}}e_{t}(X,A)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq H_{t},

where the last step is (
38
).
Taking square roots proves the claim. The argument applies to
every policy on the same fixed history, so it also covers policies
chosen using all information currently available, including the
safe mixture constructed below. No union bound over policies is
required.
∎

To match the linear bonus in the policy objective, we next separate
this square-root confidence bound into a multiple of
Q_{t}(\pi)

and a policy-independent remainder. We will use this form both for
the safe comparator and for the policy selected by the algorithm.

Lemma 13
.

On
\mathcal{E}_{R}
, with
\beta_{t}=\sqrt{tH_{t}/K}
, every
t\leq T
and
policy
\pi
satisfy

\left|\mathbb{E}_{X}[\widehat{f}^{t}(X,\pi)-f^{*}(X,\pi)]\right|\leq\beta_{t}Q_{t}(\pi)+\frac{K\beta_{t}}{4t}.

(39)

Proof of Lemma
13
.

For
H,Q\geq 0
and
\beta>0
,

0\leq\left(\sqrt{\beta Q}-\sqrt{H/(4\beta)}\right)^{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\sqrt{HQ}\leq\beta Q+\frac{H}{4\beta}.

We apply this to Lemma
12
.
Since
H_{t}>0
and
\beta_{t}^{2}=tH_{t}/K
,

H_{t}/(4\beta_{t})=K\beta_{t}/(4t)
. This proves the result at every
round, including
t=1
with its arbitrary initial fit in
\mathcal{F}
.
∎

The confidence bounds above use the historical kernels evaluated

Lemma 14

Algorithm

for
1\leq i\leq t
. In particular, its
output equals the rule in (
11
) unless an
empty-set check has instructed the algorithm to choose
a_{0}
in all
remaining rounds, in which case it equals
\pi_{0}
.

Proof of Lemma
14
.

Fix the actual history through the round-
t
model update and a
queried context
x
. All archived predictors, cost confidence
objects, coefficients, recorded instructions, and tie-breaking rules are now fixed.
At
i=1
, both replay and the historical rule have zero cumulative
mass and bonus
q_{1}(x,a)=1
. If the archive instructs choosing
a_{0}
, both
return
\pi_{0}
; otherwise they maximize the same objective over

\Pi_{1}^{\delta}(x)
with the same tie-breaking rule.

Suppose the kernels agree for all indices
j<i
. Replay has updated
its stored mass after each preceding stage, including a stage at which
the recorded instruction selects
a_{0}
. Consequently its current value satisfies

\omega_{i}(x,a)=\sum_{j<i}\widetilde{\pi}_{t,j}(a)=\sum_{j<i}\pi_{j}(a\mid x).

The computed reciprocal is exactly
q_{i}(x,a)
. If the round-
i

archive instructs choosing
a_{0}
, the two outputs again both equal
\pi_{0}
.
Otherwise the archived
\widehat{f}^{i}
,
{\mathcal{S}}_{i}
,
w_{i}
,
\beta_{i}
, and

\alpha_{r}
define the same feasible set and the same objective

p\longmapsto\widehat{f}^{i}(x,p)+\beta_{i}q_{i}(x,p)+\alpha_{r}w_{i}(x,p).

Fixed tie-breaking gives identical maximizers. Induction proves
the assertion for every
i\leq t
.

On a history where the empty-set check has not been triggered, pointwise maximality also implies
maximality after integrating over a fresh context: for every
measurable feasible comparator
\pi
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{X}[\widehat{f}^{i}(X,\pi_{i})+\beta_{i}q_{i}(X,\pi_{i})+\alpha_{r}w_{i}(X,\pi_{i})]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{X}[\widehat{f}^{i}(X,\pi)+\beta_{i}q_{i}(X,\pi)+\alpha_{r}w_{i}(X,\pi)].

Measurability is part of the setup. Throughout replay, the real
dataset remains fixed; no oracle is refitted, and no new observation
is created.
∎

With replay connected to the policy rule, it remains to control
the bonuses accumulated by that rule. The next bound holds for
every sequence of policy kernels, so it applies directly to the
algorithm’s history-dependent choices.

Lemma 15
.

For every context
x
, every sequence of policy kernels, and every

T\geq 1
,

\sum_{t=1}^{T}q_{t}(x,\pi_{t})\leq\frac{K}{\log 2}\log(1+T/K).

Consequently, for every realized adaptive history,

\sum_{t=1}^{T}Q_{t}(\pi_{t})\leq\frac{K}{\log 2}\log(1+T/K).

(40)

Proof of Lemma
15
.

Fix
x
and put
p_{t,a}=\pi_{t}(a\mid x)
and

S_{t,a}=\sum_{i\leq t}p_{i,a}
, with
S_{0,a}=0
.
Then
u_{t,a}=p_{t,a}/(1+S_{t-1,a})
lies in
[0,1]
.
Concavity of the logarithm gives

\log(1+u)\geq u\log 2
on that interval. We obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}q_{t}(x,\pi_{t})

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{p_{t,a}}{1+S_{t-1,a}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\log 2}\sum_{a}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\log\left(1+\frac{p_{t,a}}{1+S_{t-1,a}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\log 2}\sum_{a}\log(1+S_{T,a})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{K}{\log 2}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{K}\sum_{a}S_{T,a}\right)=\frac{K}{\log 2}\log(1+T/K).

The penultimate step is Jensen’s inequality, and the last uses

\sum_{a}S_{T,a}=T
. This bound is deterministic for each sequence
of kernels. After fixing a realized history, we apply it at every
context and integrate. The result is (
40
);
adaptivity introduces no additional event or independence
requirement.
∎

D.4
Safe mixtures and optimism

Following the safe-mixture argument of

Pacchiano et al. (2021)
;
Pacchiano et al. (2025)
, we next
connect reward confidence to the cost constraint. The true
optimal policy may lie outside the robust feasible set. Mixing it
with the known safe action gives a feasible comparator whose
reward loss is covered by its own cost-width bonus. Together with
Lemmas
13
and
14
,
this comparison yields the following optimism bound.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{C,\mathrm{or}}
denote the simultaneous cost-oracle event.
Its failure probability is at most
\delta/4
, by exactly the union
bound used for the reward fits. Lemma
1
, proved in
Appendix
C
, implies that on this event

c^{*}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t}
at every round and the empty-set check is not triggered.
The same event suffices for truth retention, nonemptiness, and the
resulting policy-cost confidence bounds.

Lemma 16
.

On
\mathcal{E}_{R}\cap\mathcal{E}_{C,\mathrm{or}}
, every
1\leq t\leq T
satisfies

\mathbb{E}_{X}f^{*}(X,\pi^{*})\leq\mathbb{E}_{X}[\widehat{f}^{t}(X,\pi_{t})+\beta_{t}q_{t}(X,\pi_{t})+\alpha_{r}w_{t}(X,\pi_{t})]+\frac{K\beta_{t}}{4t},\qquad\alpha_{r}=\frac{1}{\Delta}.

Proof of Lemma
16
.

Fix a realized history in this intersection and a round
t
.
For each context and distribution
p
, true-cost retention gives

0\leq V_{t}^{\delta}(x,p)-c^{*}(x,p)\leq w_{t}(x,p).

Indeed,
c^{*}
is one of the functions in the supremum defining

V_{t}^{\delta}
, and every other
c\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t}
satisfies

c(x,p)-c^{*}(x,p)\leq\sum_{a}p(a)|c(x,a)-c^{*}(x,a)|\leq\sum_{a}p(a)w_{t}(x,a).

Taking the supremum proves the stated upper bound.

All candidates have the known safe-action cost
c_{0}
. Therefore

V_{t}^{\delta}(x,\pi_{0})=c_{0}
and
w_{t}(x,\pi_{0})=0
. For any

\gamma\in[0,1]
we have the exact mixture identity

V_{t}^{\delta}(x,\gamma p+(1-\gamma)\pi_{0})=\gamma V_{t}^{\delta}(x,p)+(1-\gamma)c_{0}.

We obtain it by taking the supremum of

\gamma c(x,p)+(1-\gamma)c_{0}
over
c\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t}
.
No attainment of the supremum is needed.

Define, pointwise in
x
,

h_{t}(x)=[V_{t}^{\delta}(x,\pi^{*})-\tau]_{+},\qquad\gamma_{t}(x)=\frac{\Delta}{\Delta+h_{t}(x)},\qquad\widetilde{\pi}_{t}=\gamma_{t}\pi^{*}+(1-\gamma_{t})\pi_{0}.

These functions are measurable by the setup assumptions, and

\gamma_{t}(x)\in(0,1]
. The mixture identity yields

\displaystyle V_{t}^{\delta}(x,\widetilde{\pi}_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\gamma_{t}(x)(\tau+h_{t}(x))+(1-\gamma_{t}(x))c_{0}

\displaystyle=c_{0}+\gamma_{t}(x)(\Delta+h_{t}(x))=\tau.

Thus
\widetilde{\pi}_{t}
is robustly feasible at every context.
Since
c^{*}(x,\pi^{*})\leq\tau
, the upper-cost error bound also gives

h_{t}(x)\leq w_{t}(x,\pi^{*})
.
Width is linear in the action distribution, and its safe-action
value vanishes, so

w_{t}(x,\widetilde{\pi}_{t})=\gamma_{t}(x)w_{t}(x,\pi^{*})
.

We now bound the reward loss using only its range
[0,1]
:

\displaystyle f^{*}(x,\pi^{*})-f^{*}(x,\widetilde{\pi}_{t})

\displaystyle=(1-\gamma_{t}(x))[f^{*}(x,\pi^{*})-f^{*}(x,a_{0})]

(41)

\displaystyle\leq 1-\gamma_{t}(x)=\frac{\gamma_{t}(x)h_{t}(x)}{\Delta}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma_{t}(x)w_{t}(x,\pi^{*})}{\Delta}=\alpha_{r}w_{t}(x,\widetilde{\pi}_{t}).

This calculation requires no known reward for the safe action.

For the fixed history, set

J_{t}(\pi)=\mathbb{E}_{X}[\widehat{f}^{t}(X,\pi)+\beta_{t}q_{t}(X,\pi)+\alpha_{r}w_{t}(X,\pi)]
. We integrate
(
41
) and apply
Lemma
13
to the entire policy

\widetilde{\pi}_{t}
, obtaining

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{X}f^{*}(X,\pi^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{X}f^{*}(X,\widetilde{\pi}_{t})+\alpha_{r}\mathbb{E}_{X}w_{t}(X,\widetilde{\pi}_{t})

\displaystyle\leq J_{t}(\widetilde{\pi}_{t})+\frac{K\beta_{t}}{4t}

\displaystyle\leq J_{t}(\pi_{t})+\frac{K\beta_{t}}{4t}.

The last inequality uses robust feasibility of the mixture,
nonemptiness of the cost sets, and the policy optimality identified
by Lemma
14
. It proves the claim.
All context-dependent mixture coefficients stay inside their
corresponding integrals.
∎

D.5
Completion of the finite-action theorem

We now combine these results. Constrained optimism and reward
confidence give the one-round regret bound, while
Lemma
15
and
Property
1
control its cumulative reward
and cost terms, respectively. We first place both arguments on a

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{C,\mathrm{pot}}
be the cost-geometry martingale event

\delta/4
. On

\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{X,A\sim\pi_{t}}w_{t}(X,A)\leq\mathcal{W}_{T}.

The bound applies to the actual sampled policy sequence, whose
observations generated both regression datasets. We work on

The four failure probabilities are each at most
\delta/4
.
A union bound therefore gives

independence assumption between reward and cost noise.

On

at every context. Consequently

c^{*}(x,\pi_{t})\leq V_{t}^{\delta}(x,\pi_{t})\leq\tau\quad\text{for all }x,\ 1\leq t\leq T.

This includes the first round:
{\mathcal{S}}_{1}=\mathcal{C}
before any
observations, and the safe policy
already makes the robust feasible set nonempty.

For regret, subtract

from the optimism bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{X}[f^{*}(X,\pi^{*})-f^{*}(X,\pi_{t})]

(42)

Summing and using

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{\frac{T\bar{H}_{T}}{K}}\,\frac{K}{\log 2}\log(1+T/K)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\log 2}\sqrt{KT\bar{H}_{T}}\log(1+T/K).

For the remaining reward term, the round-dependent bound on

\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{K\beta_{t}}{2t}\leq\frac{\sqrt{K\bar{H}_{T}}}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}t^{-1/2}\leq\sqrt{KT\bar{H}_{T}}.

The final inequality follows from

\int_{0}^{T}u^{-1/2}\,du=2\sqrt{T}
.

\operatorname{Reg}(T)\leq\sqrt{KT\bar{H}_{T}}\left[1+\frac{2\log(1+T/K)}{\log 2}\right]+\frac{\mathcal{W}_{T}}{\Delta}.

(43)

This is exactly (
15

∎

Appendix E
Proof of Regret Guarantee with Infinite Actions

We prove Theorem
2
by extending the
reward-confidence and replay arguments to the regularized design bonus.
We first derive policy confidence from the offline reward error and
verify that replay reconstructs the kernels used in this bound.
Assumption
5
then controls their cumulative bonus;
we also give a sufficient condition used in the continuous-action
example of Appendix
G.4
.
Finally, a safe comparator yields constrained optimism. Combining it
with reward confidence and the cost-width bound in
Property

completes the joint feasibility
and regret guarantee.

We use the same observation history
\mathcal{H}_{t-1}
as in the finite-action
analysis. A policy kernel
\pi_{t}(\cdot|x)
is fixed by this history before
the new context is drawn. Throughout the proof,
\mathbb{E}_{X}
integrates an
independent
X\sim Q_{x}
while holding that history fixed. Rewards and

E.1
Reward Confidence with a Reference Design

We first verify that the reward population-error argument does not
require a finite action set. We then use the reference design to
convert that error budget into confidence for any candidate policy.
The design regularizes the bonus from round one without contributing
observations to the regression data. Put
n=t-1
and fix a reward model

f\in\mathcal{F}
. For the actual observations
Z_{i}=(X_{i},A_{i})
, write

L_{f,i}=(f(Z_{i})-f^{*}(Z_{i}))^{2},\qquad\mu_{f,i}=\mathbb{E}[L_{f,i}\mid\mathcal{H}_{i-1}]=\mathbb{E}_{X}\int_{\mathcal{A}}(f(X,a)-f^{*}(X,a))^{2}\,d\pi_{i}(a|X).

Both quantities lie in
[0,1]
. Convexity of the exponential function on this
interval gives

\mathbb{E}[e^{-L_{f,i}}\mid\mathcal{H}_{i-1}]\leq 1-(1-e^{-1})\mu_{f,i}\leq e^{-\mu_{f,i}/2}
.
Iterating this conditional-expectation bound and applying Markov’s
inequality show that, for any fixed

f
and prefix length
n
, with failure probability at most
\zeta
,

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{f,i}\leq 2\sum_{i=1}^{n}L_{f,i}+2\log(1/\zeta).

We apply this statement to a fixed proper uniform

\eta_{\mathcal{F}}=1/(6T)
-net of
\mathcal{F}
and all
T
prefixes. The union bound,
with
\zeta=\delta/(4TN_{\mathcal{F}})
, costs at most
\delta/4
.

For functions within uniform distance
\eta_{\mathcal{F}}
, their squared errors
relative to
f^{*}
differ by at most
2\eta_{\mathcal{F}}
, both pointwise and after
integration. Approximating an arbitrary
f\in\mathcal{F}
by its net point gives,
simultaneously for every
f
and
t
,

\sum_{i<t}\mu_{f,i}\leq 2\sum_{i<t}(f(Z_{i})-f^{*}(Z_{i}))^{2}+2\log(4TN_{\mathcal{F}}/\delta)+6(t-1)\eta_{\mathcal{F}}.

We form this common event before substituting the random fit

f=\widehat{f}^{t}
. The reward oracle inequalities, each with failure probability
at most
\delta/(4T)
, hold jointly with failure probability at most
\delta/4
.
Consequently their intersection, denoted
\mathcal{E}_{R}
, has probability at
least
1-\delta/2
, and it gives

\mathbb{E}_{X}\sum_{i<t}\int_{\mathcal{A}}(\widehat{f}^{t}(X,a)-f^{*}(X,a))^{2}\,d\pi_{i}(a|X)\leq H_{t}-1,\qquad 1\leq t\leq T.

(48)

At
t=1
the sum is zero, so the statement also covers the prescribed
initial fit
\widehat{f}^{1}\in\mathcal{F}
. Evaluating the uniform inequality at

\widehat{f}^{t}
does not assert that this fit was known at time
i<t
;
the historical population integrals retain the archived kernels.

The next lemma turns this historical error budget into expected reward
confidence under an arbitrary policy. The reference part of the
denominator contributes the additional
D_{0}
term, and the final
inequality puts the bound in the linear bonus form used by the policy
objective.

Lemma 17
.

On
\mathcal{E}_{R}
, simultaneously for every
1\leq t\leq T
and every measurable
policy
\pi
, let
Q_{t}^{\infty}(\pi)=\mathbb{E}_{X}q_{t}^{\infty}(X,\pi)
. Then

\left|\mathbb{E}_{X}[\widehat{f}^{t}(X,\pi)-f^{*}(X,\pi)]\right|\leq\sqrt{(H_{t}+D_{0})Q_{t}^{\infty}(\pi)}\leq\beta_{t}Q_{t}^{\infty}(\pi)+\frac{\bar{\mathcal{M}}\beta_{t}}{4t}.

Proof of Lemma
17
.

For
h=f-f^{\prime}
with
f,f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
, define

D_{t,h}(x)=d_{x}\int h(x,a)^{2}\,d\rho_{x}^{*}(a)+\sum_{i<t}\int h(x,a)^{2}\,d\pi_{i}(a|x).

The normalization in (
18
) cancels, giving

q_{t}^{\infty}(x,a)=\sup_{f,f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}}\frac{(f(x,a)-f^{\prime}(x,a))^{2}}{D_{t,f-f^{\prime}}(x)}.

(49)

If the design integral of a difference vanishes, finiteness of

\mathcal{V}_{x}(\rho_{x}^{*})
implies that the difference vanishes at every action.
Otherwise the denominator is at least its positive design contribution.
This establishes

0\leq q_{t}^{\infty}(x,a)\leq\frac{\mathcal{V}_{x}(a\|\rho_{x}^{*})}{d_{x}}\leq\mathcal{M}_{x}.

(50)

In particular, the bonus is integrable over contexts under every policy.

Now put
e_{t}=\widehat{f}^{t}-f^{*}
and

\widetilde{S}_{t}(x)=d_{x}\int e_{t}(x,a)^{2}\,d\rho_{x}^{*}(a)+\sum_{i<t}\int e_{t}(x,a)^{2}\,d\pi_{i}(a|x).

Because both reward functions lie in
[0,1]
, the design contribution
is at most
d_{x}
. Equation (
48
) gives

\mathbb{E}_{X}\widetilde{S}_{t}(X)\leq H_{t}-1+\mathbb{E}_{X}d_{X}\leq H_{t}+D_{0}.

We have
e_{t}(x,a)^{2}\leq q_{t}^{\infty}(x,a)\widetilde{S}_{t}(x)
whenever

\widetilde{S}_{t}(x)>0
. If this quantity is zero, the preceding
finite-design argument gives
e_{t}(x,a)=0
for every action, so the
same inequality remains valid. We integrate and apply the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality
on the joint distribution of
X
and
A\sim\pi(\cdot|X)
:

\left|\mathbb{E}_{X,A}e_{t}(X,A)\right|\leq\mathbb{E}_{X,A}\sqrt{q_{t}^{\infty}(X,A)\widetilde{S}_{t}(X)}\leq\sqrt{Q_{t}^{\infty}(\pi)\mathbb{E}_{X}\widetilde{S}_{t}(X)}.

Finally,
\sqrt{uv}\leq\beta u+v/(4\beta)
and

\beta_{t}^{2}=t(H_{t}+D_{0})/\bar{\mathcal{M}}
give the second assertion. All statements hold
pointwise on the common event for every policy kernel, so data-dependent
policy selection requires no additional confidence allocation.
∎

E.2
Counterfactual Replay

The confidence bound depends on the actual historical kernels
evaluated at a fresh context. We next verify that the implemented
replay reconstructs these kernels exactly. This also justifies the
policy-optimality comparison used in the optimism argument.

Lemma 18
.

Archive each round’s reward fit, cost confidence set, any instruction to
choose
a_{0}
in all remaining rounds, and
tie-breaking rule. At a queried context
x
, reconstruct rounds

i=1,\ldots,t
in order, forming the bonus from
d_{x}\rho_{x}^{*}
and the
already reconstructed kernels and applying (
19
)
with that round’s archived objects. This replay returns

\pi_{t}(\cdot|x)
exactly.

Proof of Lemma
18
.

Fix the observed history through round
t-1
and a context
x
. Write

\widetilde{\pi}_{t,i}(\cdot|x)
for the kernel obtained at replay step
i
.
At
i=1
, the bonus is
\mathcal{V}_{x}(a\|\rho_{x}^{*})/d_{x}
in both constructions.
The initial fit, confidence set, coefficient
\beta_{1}
, and tie-breaking
rule are also identical. Thus
\widetilde{\pi}_{t,1}=\pi_{1}
.

Suppose the equality holds through step
i-1
. If round
i
’s archive
instructs choosing
a_{0}
, both constructions return the safe point mass.
Otherwise,

\frac{d_{x}\rho_{x}^{*}+\sum_{j<i}\widetilde{\pi}_{t,j}(\cdot|x)}{d_{x}+i-1}=\frac{d_{x}\rho_{x}^{*}+\sum_{j<i}\pi_{j}(\cdot|x)}{d_{x}+i-1}.

The bonuses agree, and the remaining optimization data are the same
archived objects. Identical tie-breaking therefore gives

\widetilde{\pi}_{t,i}=\pi_{i}
. Induction proves the claim.

The replay uses distributions rather than hypothetical sampled actions.
Neither oracle is refitted, and no past confidence set is recomputed
using later labels. Hence every reconstructed kernel is the evaluation
of its actual historical rule at the queried context. Pointwise
maximization implies the corresponding integrated objective comparison
after integration against
Q_{x}
; the algorithm does not need to know
this distribution.
∎

E.3
Cumulative Reward Bonus and a Sufficient Design Condition

After identifying the replayed kernels, we need to bound the bonuses
they accumulate. The theorem uses
Assumption
5

verifies its cumulative-potential inequality at a fixed context when
reward differences admit a finite feature representation. It supplies the bound used in
Appendix

the stated design assumption. The features may be nonlinear in the

Fix a context and suppose every reward difference has the form

h(a)=\theta_{h}^{\top}\phi(a)
with
\phi(a)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\rm feat}}
. Let

G_{0}=\int\phi(a)\phi(a)^{\top}\,d\rho_{x}^{*}(a)\succ 0
, and choose
d_{x}

so that
\sup_{a}\phi(a)^{\top}(d_{x}G_{0})^{-1}\phi(a)\leq 1
.
For arbitrary policy kernels set

J_{t}=\int\phi(a)\phi(a)^{\top}\,d\pi_{t}(a|x),\qquad S_{t-1}=d_{x}G_{0}+\sum_{i<t}J_{i}.

The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality in the
S_{t-1}
norm gives

q_{t}^{\infty}(x,a)\leq\phi(a)^{\top}S_{t-1}^{-1}\phi(a)
.
We therefore have

q_{t}^{\infty}(x,\pi_{t})\leq\operatorname{tr}(S_{t-1}^{-1}J_{t})
.
For
\Xi_{t}=S_{t-1}^{-1/2}J_{t}S_{t-1}^{-1/2}
, every eigenvalue is
nonnegative and

\operatorname{tr}(\Xi_{t})\leq\operatorname{tr}((d_{x}G_{0})^{-1}J_{t})\leq 1
.
The scalar inequality
u\leq 2\log(1+u)
for
0\leq u\leq 1
then gives

\operatorname{tr}(\Xi_{t})\leq 2\log\det(I+\Xi_{t})=2\log\frac{\det S_{t}}{\det S_{t-1}}.

After summing, the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality for eigenvalues
and
\sum_{t}\operatorname{tr}((d_{x}G_{0})^{-1}J_{t})\leq T
give

\sum_{t=1}^{T}q_{t}^{\infty}(x,\pi_{t})\leq 2\log\frac{\det S_{T}}{\det S_{0}}\leq 2d_{\rm feat}\log(1+T/d_{\rm feat}).

(51)

This proof applies to every policy sequence, including sequences that
never select an informative action. Their unselected-action bonuses may
remain large, but the cumulative on-policy quantity still satisfies
the displayed bound.

E.4
Safe Mixtures and Constrained Optimism

We now combine reward confidence with the cost confidence sets.
As in the finite-action argument, mixing the optimal feasible policy
with the safe action produces a robustly feasible comparator. Its
reward loss is covered by its own cost-width bonus. Applying
Lemma
17
to this mixture and using the
policy optimality established in
Lemma
18
yields constrained optimism.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{C,\mathrm{all}}
be the intersection of the cost-oracle event and the
cost-geometry event in Property
1
.
When
m_{T}=0
, take the latter event to be the whole sample space.
Each constituent event has failure probability at most
\delta/4
, so

\Pr(\mathcal{E}_{C,\mathrm{all}}\cap\mathcal{E}_{R})\geq 1-\delta
. On this intersection the true cost
belongs to every
\mathcal{S}_{t}
, and the cumulative width bound applies
to the actual policy sequence.

Lemma 19
.

On
\mathcal{E}_{C,\mathrm{all}}\cap\mathcal{E}_{R}
, for every
1\leq t\leq T
,

\mathbb{E}_{X}f^{*}(X,\pi^{*})\leq\mathbb{E}_{X}[\widehat{f}^{t}(X,\pi_{t})+\beta_{t}q_{t}^{\infty}(X,\pi_{t})+\alpha_{r}w_{t}(X,\pi_{t})]+\frac{\bar{\mathcal{M}}\beta_{t}}{4t}.

Proof of Lemma
19
.

Fix a history on this event. Truth inclusion and the definition of the
action-wise width imply, for every context and policy,

0\leq V_{t}^{\delta}(x,\pi)-c^{*}(x,\pi)\leq w_{t}(x,\pi).

(52)

Every candidate has cost
c_{0}
at
a_{0}
. Consequently,

w_{t}(x,a_{0})=0
and, for
\lambda\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle V_{t}^{\delta}(x,\lambda\pi+(1-\lambda)\delta_{a_{0}})

\displaystyle=\lambda V_{t}^{\delta}(x,\pi)+(1-\lambda)c_{0},

\displaystyle w_{t}(x,\lambda\pi+(1-\lambda)\delta_{a_{0}})

\displaystyle=\lambda w_{t}(x,\pi).

The first identity follows by taking the supremum after integrating
each candidate cost. It does not require a maximizing candidate.

Put
\Delta=\tau-c_{0}
,
h_{t}(x)=[V_{t}^{\delta}(x,\pi^{*})-\tau]_{+}
, and

\lambda_{t}(x)=\frac{\Delta}{\Delta+h_{t}(x)},\qquad\widetilde{\pi}_{t}(\cdot|x)=\lambda_{t}(x)\pi^{*}(\cdot|x)+(1-\lambda_{t}(x))\delta_{a_{0}}.

Since
c^{*}(x,\pi^{*})\leq\tau
, equation
(
52
) gives

h_{t}(x)\leq w_{t}(x,\pi^{*})
. If
h_{t}(x)=0
, the optimal policy itself
is robustly feasible. If
h_{t}(x)>0
, the mixture’s upper cost is

c_{0}+\lambda_{t}(x)(\Delta+h_{t}(x))=\tau
. Thus the mixture belongs to

\Pi_{t}^{\delta}(x)
in both cases.

Rewards lie in
[0,1]
, without requiring knowledge of the safe action’s
reward. We therefore obtain

\displaystyle f^{*}(x,\pi^{*})-f^{*}(x,\widetilde{\pi}_{t})

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda_{t}(x))[f^{*}(x,\pi^{*})-f^{*}(x,a_{0})]

(53)

\displaystyle\leq 1-\lambda_{t}(x)=\frac{\lambda_{t}(x)h_{t}(x)}{\Delta}\leq\alpha_{r}w_{t}(x,\widetilde{\pi}_{t}).

We integrate this inequality and apply
Lemma
17
to
\widetilde{\pi}_{t}
. Its robust
feasibility lets us compare its score with the maximizing score of

\pi_{t}
by Lemma
18
.
The resulting two inequalities give

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{X}f^{*}(X,\pi^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{X}[f^{*}(X,\widetilde{\pi}_{t})+\alpha_{r}w_{t}(X,\widetilde{\pi}_{t})]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{X}[\widehat{f}^{t}(X,\widetilde{\pi}_{t})+\beta_{t}q_{t}^{\infty}(X,\widetilde{\pi}_{t})+\alpha_{r}w_{t}(X,\widetilde{\pi}_{t})]+\frac{\bar{\mathcal{M}}\beta_{t}}{4t}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{X}[\widehat{f}^{t}(X,\pi_{t})+\beta_{t}q_{t}^{\infty}(X,\pi_{t})+\alpha_{r}w_{t}(X,\pi_{t})]+\frac{\bar{\mathcal{M}}\beta_{t}}{4t}.

The context-dependent mixing coefficient stays inside each expectation.
∎

E.5
Completion of the Infinite-Action Theorem

The remaining step is to sum the one-round regret bound.
Lemma
19
compares the optimal
feasible reward with the algorithm’s score, and
Lemma
17
controls the reward estimation
error of its selected policy. Their combination leaves a cumulative
reward bonus, bounded by Assumption
5
, and a
cumulative cost width, bounded by
Property
1
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Work on
\mathcal{E}_{C,\mathrm{all}}\cap\mathcal{E}_{R}
. The safe point mass belongs to every robust
feasible set, and truth inclusion ensures

c^{*}(x,\pi_{t})\leq V_{t}^{\delta}(x,\pi_{t})\leq\tau
at every round.
In particular, no unsafe initialization is required.

For each fixed context and realized history, Assumption
5

applies to the entire sequence of archived kernels.
Lemma
18
identifies these kernels with
the policies actually used by the algorithm.
We integrate the nonnegative finite sum to obtain

\sum_{t=1}^{T}Q_{t}^{\infty}(\pi_{t})\leq C_{V}\mathbb{E}_{X}\mathcal{M}_{X}(1+\log T)^{p_{V}+1}=P_{T}.

(54)

This is a deterministic application of the assumed potential bound;
there is no new probability event.

Subtract the true reward of
\pi_{t}
from the optimism inequality in
Lemma
19
and apply
Lemma
17
once more to
\pi_{t}
. We get

\mathbb{E}_{X}[f^{*}(X,\pi^{*})-f^{*}(X,\pi_{t})]\leq 2\beta_{t}Q_{t}^{\infty}(\pi_{t})+\frac{\bar{\mathcal{M}}\beta_{t}}{2t}+\alpha_{r}\mathbb{E}_{X}w_{t}(X,\pi_{t}).

The inequality includes
t=1
. Since

\beta_{t}\leq\sqrt{t(\bar{H}_{T}+D_{0})/\bar{\mathcal{M}}}
and

\sum_{t=1}^{T}t^{-1/2}\leq 2\sqrt{T}
, summation yields

\displaystyle\operatorname{Reg}(T)

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{\frac{T(\bar{H}_{T}+D_{0})}{\bar{\mathcal{M}}}}P_{T}+\frac{\sqrt{\bar{\mathcal{M}}(\bar{H}_{T}+D_{0})}}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}t^{-1/2}+\alpha_{r}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{X}w_{t}(X,\pi_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{2P_{T}}{\bar{\mathcal{M}}}\right)\sqrt{\bar{\mathcal{M}}T(\bar{H}_{T}+D_{0})}+\alpha_{r}\mathcal{W}_{T}.

For the last step we use Property
1
.
Its design matrix uses the actual selected-action covariates
Z_{i}
,
so the same cost-width proof applies on an infinite action space.
All four allocated failure probabilities sum to at most
\delta
.
Thus feasibility and the stated regret bound hold on the same event
with probability at least
1-\delta
, as claimed in
Theorem
2
.
∎

Under Assumptions
2
and
3

and the concrete oracle-error conditions in
(
44
), we now specify the corresponding
constants and logarithmic exponents for
Theorem
2
. Let

\ell_{T}=\log(eT/\delta)
and use
C_{H},C_{R},C_{L},h_{F},\gamma_{C}
from
the calculation following the proof of Theorem
1

in Appendix

. The same reward and cost estimates give

\bar{H}_{T}+D_{0}\leq(C_{H}+D_{0})\ell_{T}^{h_{F}},\qquad\mathcal{W}_{T}\leq 1+\sqrt{C_{R}C_{L}T}\,\ell_{T}^{\gamma_{C}}.

The additional contribution comes from the reward-design potential.
Since
1+\log T\leq\ell_{T}
, Assumption
5
gives

\frac{P_{T}}{\bar{\mathcal{M}}}=C_{V}\frac{\mathbb{E}_{X}\mathcal{M}_{X}}{\bar{\mathcal{M}}}(1+\log T)^{p_{V}+1}\leq C_{V}\ell_{T}^{p_{V}+1}.

Substituting these bounds into Theorem
2
,
and using
\alpha_{r}=1/\Delta
, we obtain on the same confidence event

\operatorname{Reg}(T)\leq(2C_{V}+1)\sqrt{\bar{\mathcal{M}}(C_{H}+D_{0})T}\,\ell_{T}^{p_{V}+1+h_{F}/2}+\frac{1+\sqrt{C_{R}C_{L}T}\,\ell_{T}^{\gamma_{C}}}{\Delta}.

(55)

This makes the dependence on the design parameters and safety gap
explicit. For fixed
D_{0},C_{V},\bar{\mathcal{M}},p_{V}
, positive safety gap,
and class and oracle-error parameters, the rate is

\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{T})
. The continuous-action example in
Appendix
G.4
verifies the design condition
and evaluates this guarantee for a specific nonlinear model.

Recovery of the finite-action guarantee.

For a tabular reward class, choose the uniform reference design,

d_{x}=\mathcal{M}_{x}=K
, and
D_{0}=\bar{\mathcal{M}}=K
. Then

q_{t}^{\infty}(x,a)=1/(1+\sum_{i<t}\pi_{i}(a\mid x))
.
In this case, Lemma
12
gives the sharper
confidence bound
\sqrt{H_{t}Q_{t}^{\infty}(\pi)}

\pi(a\mid x)\leq 1
to bound the reference contribution by one,
rather than summing it over all
K
actions. We may therefore
replace
H_{t}+D_{0}
by
H_{t}
in the confidence argument and choose

\beta_{t}=\sqrt{tH_{t}/K}
. The one-round regret inequality above
then becomes

\mathbb{E}_{X}[f^{*}(X,\pi^{*})-f^{*}(X,\pi_{t})]\leq 2\beta_{t}Q_{t}^{\infty}(\pi_{t})+\frac{K\beta_{t}}{2t}+\alpha_{r}\mathbb{E}_{X}w_{t}(X,\pi_{t}).

Using the exact finite-action potential from
Lemma
15
, we obtain

\operatorname{Reg}(T)\leq\sqrt{KT\bar{H}_{T}}\left[1+\frac{2\log(1+T/K)}{\log 2}\right]+\alpha_{r}\mathcal{W}_{T},

which is Theorem

into the general

inside the square root.

Appendix G
Nonlinear Examples and Their Regret Bounds

In this section, we give nonlinear examples to illustrate our model
and show how its assumptions can be verified. We construct a convex
cost class with nonlinear context dependence and use it in both
finite-action and continuous-action problems. For these examples,
we verify the covering-number and offline regression conditions and
derive the resulting regret bounds. The continuous-action example
also verifies the reward-design condition in
Assumption
5
. Throughout this section, write

\ell_{T}=\log(eT/\delta)
.

G.1
A Convex Cost Class with Nonlinear Context Dependence

Let
x\in[-1,1]
,
a\in[0,1]
, and

\psi_{j}(x)=\cos(j\pi x)
. We define the cost class directly on
context–action pairs:

\mathcal{C}=\left\{c_{\theta}(x,a)=\frac{a}{2}+\frac{a}{16}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{-j}\theta_{j}\psi_{j}(x):\theta\in[-1,1]^{\mathbb{N}}\right\}.

(56)

The series converges uniformly. Since its coefficient set is convex,

\lambda c_{\theta}+(1-\lambda)c_{\theta^{\prime}}=c_{\lambda\theta+(1-\lambda)\theta^{\prime}}
belongs to
\mathcal{C}
.
Moreover,

\frac{7a}{16}\leq c_{\theta}(x,a)\leq\frac{9a}{16},\qquad c_{\theta}(x,0)=0.

(57)

All costs are bounded and share the known safe action
a_{0}=0
.
The linear independence of the Fourier functions shows that the affine
span is infinite-dimensional.

The parameter cube is compact in its countable product topology.
The uniformly summable coefficients make the map from this cube to

\mathcal{C}
continuous in the uniform norm: convergence of a finite
prefix controls the corresponding finite sum, and the remaining tail
is uniformly small. Thus
\mathcal{C}
is compact in the uniform norm.

We next verify the entropy condition. For
0<u\leq 1
, let

J=\lceil\log_{2}(1/u)\rceil
and set all coordinates beyond
J
to zero.
The discarded tail changes the function by at most

2^{-J}/16\leq u/16
. In each retained coordinate use a grid on
[-1,1]

whose mesh is at most
u
and which has at most
4/u
points.
Rounding the first
J
coordinates changes the function by at most

\frac{1}{16}\sum_{j=1}^{J}2^{-j}u\leq u/16.

Every grid function remains in
\mathcal{C}
. This proper net has radius
at most
u/8
, and hence is also a proper
u
-net. Its cardinality gives

\log\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{C},u,\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\leq J\log(4/u)\leq 2[\log(e/u)]^{2}.

(58)

At
u=1
, the truncation has no coordinates and the inequality holds
directly. This verifies the cost entropy assumption with

D_{\mathcal{C}}=2
,
A_{\mathcal{C}}=e
, and
p_{\mathcal{C}}=2
.

There is also an explicit approximation space for cost differences.
The tail of
c_{\theta}-c_{\theta^{\prime}}
after
J
coordinates has norm at most

2^{-J}/8
. The span of

a\psi_{1}(x),\ldots,a\psi_{J}(x)
therefore approximates every difference
to this precision. We obtain
m_{T}=\mathcal{O}(\log(eT))
without imposing a
finite-dimensional representation on the full cost class.

For a finite-dimensional variant, fix
J_{0}\geq 2
and require

\theta_{j}=0
for
j>J_{0}
. The resulting convex class

\mathcal{C}^{(J_{0})}
has affine dimension
J_{0}
and satisfies

\log\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{C}^{(J_{0})},u,\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\leq J_{0}\log(4/u)
.
The same range, safe action, and decision calculations below apply.
Its cost oracle is a box-constrained quadratic program with
J_{0}

variables. Thus the construction covers both fixed finite dimension
and an infinite-dimensional limit.

G.2
A Finite-Action Problem with an Active Safety Constraint

Let
X\sim\operatorname{Unif}([-1,1])
,
\mathcal{A}=\{0,1\}
,

\tau=1/4
, and
c_{0}=0
. Restrict
(
56
) to these two actions.
For rewards, put
\ell(z)=(1+e^{-z})^{-1}
and use

\mathcal{F}=\left\{f_{b}(x,a)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{a}{4}[2\ell(x+b)-1]:b\in[-1,1]\right\}.

These rewards lie in
[1/4,3/4]
. After selecting
A_{t}
, we observe
reward and cost labels that, conditional on the selected pair and the
history, are independent Bernoulli variables with respective means

f_{b^{*}}(X_{t},A_{t})
and
c_{\theta^{*}}(X_{t},A_{t})
.
The algorithm receives no labels for other actions.

Writing
p
for the probability of action one, feasibility at context

x
is exactly

p\,c_{\theta^{*}}(x,1)\leq 1/4
.
Equation (
57
) ensures that its upper
feasible probability lies strictly between zero and one. Except at the
single context
x=-b^{*}
, the unique optimal probability is

p^{*}(x)=\begin{cases}0,&x+b^{*}<0,\\
1/[4c_{\theta^{*}}(x,1)],&x+b^{*}>0.\end{cases}

(59)

For
b^{*}=0
, both regions have positive context probability.
The reward parameter determines which contexts receive the costly
action, and the unknown cost function determines its optimal positive
probability. Thus neither learning problem is vacuous.

Since
\ell^{\prime}\leq 1/4
,

\|f_{b}-f_{b^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}\leq|b-b^{\prime}|/8
.
A parameter grid of mesh at most
u
gives

\log\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{F},u,\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\leq\log(4/u)
for
0<u\leq 1
.
Thus Assumption
2
holds with

D_{\mathcal{F}}=1
,
A_{\mathcal{F}}=4
, and
p_{\mathcal{F}}=1
.
The regression construction below supplies the required oracle budgets
for both classes. It follows that all assumptions of
Theorem
1
hold, with

B_{T}^{\mathcal{F}}=\mathcal{O}(\ell_{T})
,
B_{T}^{\mathcal{C}}=\mathcal{O}(\ell_{T}^{2})
, and

\alpha_{r}=4
.

We also verify policy optimization in this example. For a nonempty
convex confidence set, write

\bar{c}_{t}(x)=\sup_{c\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}c(x,1)
and

\underline{c}_{t}(x)=\inf_{c\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}c(x,1)
.
The feasible probabilities form the closed interval

[0,1/(4\bar{c}_{t}(x))]
, and

w_{t}(x,1)=\bar{c}_{t}(x)-\underline{c}_{t}(x)
.
The score is affine in
p
, so choosing its maximizing endpoint with
a fixed tie rule gives a policy. We verify that its coefficients are
measurable. The current squared-error constraint is continuous in

c
under the uniform norm. Thus the nonempty set
\mathcal{S}_{t}
is
a closed subset of the compact class
\mathcal{C}
, and both extrema
are attained.

For completeness, fix a countable dense subset
D\subset\mathcal{C}
and put

L_{t}(c)=\sum_{i<t}(c(Z_{i})-\widehat{c}^{t}(Z_{i}))^{2}-B_{t}^{\mathcal{C}}\qquad(t\geq 2),

with
L_{1}(c)=-1
. Each
L_{t}(c)
is measurable in the history and
continuous in
c
. Compactness and density imply

\mathcal{S}_{t}\neq\varnothing
if and only if

\inf_{c\in D}L_{t}(c)\leq 0
, so the empty-set check is measurable.
On nonempty histories, compactness and density give

\bar{c}_{t}(x)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\sup\{c(x,1):c\in D,\ L_{t}(c)<1/k\}.

Indeed, density approximates every feasible function within each strict
relaxation, while compactness gives a feasible limit of approximate
maximizers as
k\to\infty
. Each supremum is countable, hence measurable
in the history and queried context. Applying the same argument to

-c(x,1)
proves measurability of the lower endpoint. Consequently the
endpoint policy with its fixed tie rule is measurable. The theorem gives feasible policies from round one and
regret
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{T}\,\ell_{T}^{2})
for this example.

G.3
Least-Squares Oracles for Selected-Action Data

We give a common oracle guarantee for the examples rather than assuming
an additional independence between past actions and observations.
Let
\mathcal{K}\subset[0,1]^{\mathcal{Z}}
be either working class, with
true function
h^{*}\in\mathcal{K}
. Suppose each label
Y_{i}\in[0,1]
has
conditional mean
h^{*}(Z_{i})
given the information available after
selecting
Z_{i}
and before observing
Y_{i}
. The covariates may have been
chosen using all earlier observations.

Let
n=N-1\geq 1
and let
\widehat{h}\in\mathcal{K}
satisfy

\sum_{i=1}^{n}(Y_{i}-\widehat{h}(Z_{i}))^{2}\leq\inf_{h\in\mathcal{K}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(Y_{i}-h(Z_{i}))^{2}+\frac{1}{2}.

If
\mathsf{H}_{\mathcal{K}}(u)
is a known upper bound on

\log\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{K},u,\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
, then a valid error budget is

\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{off}}(\mathcal{K},N,\rho)=\begin{cases}0,&N=1,\\
2\mathsf{H}_{\mathcal{K}}(1/(N-1))+2\log(1/\rho)+7,&N\geq 2.\end{cases}

(60)

We may take a nonincreasing entropy envelope in its radius, which makes
this budget nondecreasing in sample size and nonincreasing in the
failure probability
\rho
.

To prove the bound, write
w_{i}=Y_{i}-h^{*}(Z_{i})
.
For a fixed function
h
in a proper
1/n
-net
V
, set

e_{h}(i)=h(Z_{i})-h^{*}(Z_{i}),\quad E_{h}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{h}(i)^{2},\quad Z_{h}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}e_{h}(i).

Conditional on the information before the label,
w_{i}
has mean zero
and lies in an interval of length one. Hoeffding’s lemma gives

\mathbb{E}[\exp(\lambda w_{i}e_{h}(i))\mid\text{information before }Y_{i}]\leq\exp(\lambda^{2}e_{h}(i)^{2}/8).

Using
\lambda=2
and iterating yields

\mathbb{E}\exp(2Z_{h}-E_{h}/2)\leq 1
.
Markov’s inequality followed by the finite union bound therefore gives,
with probability at least
1-\rho
,

2Z_{h}\leq E_{h}/2+\log(|V|/\rho)\qquad(h\in V).

(61)

Using
h^{*}
as a comparator in the approximate least-squares guarantee gives

E_{\widehat{h}}\leq 2Z_{\widehat{h}}+1/2
.
On the common net event choose
h\in V
within
1/n
of
\widehat{h}
.
Since both functions and
h^{*}
lie in
[0,1]
, and
|w_{i}|\leq 1
,

|E_{h}-E_{\widehat{h}}|\leq 2,\qquad|Z_{h}-Z_{\widehat{h}}|\leq 1.

We substitute these two estimates into (
61
):

E_{\widehat{h}}\leq 2Z_{h}+\frac{5}{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}E_{h}+\log(|V|/\rho)+\frac{5}{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}E_{\widehat{h}}+\log(|V|/\rho)+\frac{7}{2}.

Rearranging proves (
60
).
The event is uniform over the net before selecting the fitted function;
no claim that the fit was fixed during data collection is needed.

For the finite-dimensional reward families, exact least squares over
the compact parameter interval or square attains its minimum.
The objective is continuous in the parameters and measurable in the
data, so we fix a measurable minimizing selection. This is a proper
regression oracle, even though the reward family need not be convex.

For the cost class (
56
), the oracle can
use only finitely many coefficients. Take

J_{n}=\lceil\log_{2}(n\vee 2)\rceil
, fix
\theta_{j}=0
for
j>J_{n}
, and
solve the box-constrained quadratic program

\min_{\theta\in[-1,1]^{J_{n}}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[Y_{i}-\frac{A_{i}}{2}-\frac{A_{i}}{16}\sum_{j=1}^{J_{n}}2^{-j}\theta_{j}\psi_{j}(X_{i})\right]^{2}.

(62)

A fixed minimum-norm selection among minimizers makes the output
measurable. Truncating any full-class function changes each prediction
by at most
2^{-J_{n}}/16\leq 1/(16n)
. Squared loss on
[0,1]
is

2
-Lipschitz in its prediction, so the total loss increases by at most

1/8
. Hence the truncated minimizer is a
1/2
-approximate minimizer
over the full class and satisfies the preceding oracle guarantee.
This procedure uses
\mathcal{O}(\log(en))
variables and returns a member of
the original convex class.

Applying (
58
) and the displayed reward
covering bounds to (
60
), we obtain
polylogarithmic budgets with nonzero observation noise. The same proof
applies to the continuous-action example below, because its labels
are also observed only at the selected context–action pair.

G.4
Continuous Actions with Two Independent Reward Features

We now give a continuous-action example in which the optimal decisions
range over an interval and the reward differences span two independent
action features. The cost class remains the infinite-dimensional convex
class in (
56
).

Model and optimal decisions.

Let
X\sim\operatorname{Unif}([-1,1])
,
\mathcal{A}=[0,1]
,

a_{0}=0
,
c_{0}=0
, and
\tau=1/4
. With

\ell(z)=(1+e^{-z})^{-1}
, take

\mathcal{F}^{(2)}=\left\{f_{b_{1},b_{2}}(x,a)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{a}{4}[2\ell(x+b_{1})-1]-\frac{a^{2}}{8}\ell(x+b_{2}):(b_{1},b_{2})\in[-1,1]^{2}\right\}.

(63)

These functions lie in
[1/8,3/4]
. After choosing
A_{t}
, we observe
reward and cost labels that, conditional on the selected pair and the
history, are independent Bernoulli variables with respective means

f_{b_{1}^{*},b_{2}^{*}}(X_{t},A_{t})
and
c_{\theta^{*}}(X_{t},A_{t})
.

Let
s_{j}(x)=\ell(x+b_{j}^{*})
and

\mu_{\max}^{*}(x)=1/[4c_{\theta^{*}}(x,1)]\in[4/9,4/7]
.
A policy is feasible exactly when its mean action

\mu(x)=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}a
is at most
\mu_{\max}^{*}(x)
. Since
s_{2}(x)>0
, Jensen’s inequality gives

f^{*}(x,\pi)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2s_{1}(x)-1}{4}\mu(x)-\frac{s_{2}(x)}{8}\mathbb{E}_{\pi}a^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2s_{1}(x)-1}{4}\mu(x)-\frac{s_{2}(x)}{8}\mu(x)^{2}.

Equality holds only when the action is constant
\pi
-almost surely. Maximizing this strictly
concave quadratic on
[0,\mu_{\max}^{*}(x)]
yields the unique optimal policy

\pi^{*}(\cdot|x)=\delta_{a^{*}(x)},\qquad a^{*}(x)=\min\left\{\mu_{\max}^{*}(x),\max\left\{0,\frac{2s_{1}(x)-1}{s_{2}(x)}\right\}\right\}.

(64)

Both reward parameters can affect interior optimal actions, while the
unknown cost function determines the binding safety limit.

For a concrete true instance, set
b_{1}^{*}=b_{2}^{*}=0
and

\theta_{j}^{*}=0
for every
j
. Then
c^{*}(x,a)=a/2
and

a^{*}(x)=\begin{cases}0,&-1\leq x\leq 0,\\
1-e^{-x},&0<x<\log 2,\\
1/2,&\log 2\leq x\leq 1.\end{cases}

The safe, interior, and constraint-binding regions all have positive
context probability. This example cannot be reduced to randomizing
between actions zero and one. On
0<x<\log 2
, an endpoint mixture with
mass
p
at action one has reward

1/2+p[3\ell(x)-2]/8\leq 1/2
, whereas the optimal interior action gives

\frac{1}{2}+\frac{(2\ell(x)-1)^{2}}{8\ell(x)}>\frac{1}{2}.

In addition, varying any of the cost coefficients on a region where its
Fourier feature is nonzero changes the safety boundary. Thus the cost
learning problem is not removed by this choice of a simple true
parameter.

Covering numbers and regression oracles.

The derivative bound
\ell^{\prime}\leq 1/4
gives

\|f_{b_{1},b_{2}}-f_{b_{1}^{\prime},b_{2}^{\prime}}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{8}|b_{1}-b_{1}^{\prime}|+\frac{1}{32}|b_{2}-b_{2}^{\prime}|.

Grids of mesh at most
u
in each coordinate therefore form a proper

u
-net with at most
(4/u)^{2}
elements for
0<u\leq 1
. Hence

\log\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{F}^{(2)},u,\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\leq 2\log(4/u).

This verifies Assumption
2
with

D_{\mathcal{F}}=2
,
A_{\mathcal{F}}=4
, and
p_{\mathcal{F}}=1
.
The cost entropy is bounded by (
58
).
We use exact least squares over the reward parameter square and the
finite box-constrained program (
62
) for
costs. The proof in Appendix
G.3
gives

B_{T}^{\mathcal{F}}=\mathcal{O}(\ell_{T})
and
B_{T}^{\mathcal{C}}=\mathcal{O}(\ell_{T}^{2})
for the actual
selected-action datasets. The safe action and cost range are already
verified in (
57
).

An optimal reward design.

Put
\phi(a)=(a,a^{2})^{\top}
. At any fixed context, the coefficient vector
of these two action features ranges over a rectangle with nonempty
interior as
(b_{1},b_{2})
varies. Its difference set contains a
neighborhood of zero. Every direction in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
can therefore be
scaled into a realizable reward difference.

For
G_{\rho}=\int\phi(a)\phi(a)^{\top}\,d\rho(a)\succ 0
, maximizing the
generalized Rayleigh quotient over reward-difference directions gives

\mathcal{V}_{x}(a\|\rho)=\phi(a)^{\top}G_{\rho}^{-1}\phi(a).

If
G_{\rho}
is singular, choose a nonzero vector in its null space.
The corresponding polynomial in
a
is not identically zero, and a
scaled version is a reward difference. The integral of its squared value
under
\rho
is zero, while its value is nonzero somewhere, so the design condition
number is infinite. For nonsingular designs,

\int\mathcal{V}_{x}(a\|\rho)\,d\rho(a)=\operatorname{tr}(G_{\rho}^{-1}G_{\rho})=2.

Thus every finite design condition number is at least two.

Consider the context-independent design

\rho_{x}^{*}=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{1/2}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{1},\qquad G_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}5/8&9/16\\
9/16&17/32\end{pmatrix},\qquad G_{0}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}34&-36\\
-36&40\end{pmatrix}.

(65)

Its leverage is
34a^{2}-72a^{3}+40a^{4}
. We compute

2-\phi(a)^{\top}G_{0}^{-1}\phi(a)=2(1-a)(2a-1)^{2}(5a+1)\geq 0,\qquad 0\leq a\leq 1.

Equality holds at
a=1/2
and
a=1
. The design is therefore optimal,
with
\mathcal{V}_{x}(\rho_{x}^{*})=2
, and is measurable.

Potential for every policy sequence.

Take
d_{x}=\mathcal{M}_{x}=2
and
D_{0}=\bar{\mathcal{M}}=2
.
For any sequence of policies, write

J_{t}(x)=\int\phi(a)\phi(a)^{\top}\,d\pi_{t}(a|x),\qquad S_{t-1}(x)=2G_{0}+\sum_{i<t}J_{i}(x).

The same Rayleigh-quotient calculation gives

q_{t}^{\infty}(x,a)=\phi(a)^{\top}S_{t-1}(x)^{-1}\phi(a)
exactly.
Moreover,

\sup_{a}\phi(a)^{\top}(2G_{0})^{-1}\phi(a)=1
.
We may therefore apply (
51
)
with
d_{\rm feat}=2
to obtain

\sum_{t=1}^{T}q_{t}^{\infty}(x,\pi_{t})\leq 4\log(1+T/2)\leq 4(1+\log T).

(66)

This proves Assumption
5
with
C_{V}=2
and
p_{V}=0
,
uniformly over contexts and every policy sequence. The reference design
is used only in the bonus and is never an executed initialization policy.

Continuous policy optimization.

For a nonempty convex confidence set define

\bar{c}_{t}(x)=\max_{c\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}c(x,1),\qquad\underline{c}_{t}(x)=\min_{c\in\mathcal{S}_{t}}c(x,1),\qquad d_{t}^{c}(x)=\bar{c}_{t}(x)-\underline{c}_{t}(x).

The set is compact: it is the full compact cost class at
t=1
,
and its intersection with one closed squared-error sublevel set
at
t\geq 2
. Thus both extrema exist.
Every candidate is linear in
a
with its context-dependent
coefficient, so

V_{t}^{\delta}(x,\pi)=\bar{c}_{t}(x)\mathbb{E}_{\pi}a,\qquad w_{t}(x,\pi)=d_{t}^{c}(x)\mathbb{E}_{\pi}a.

The feasible mean is consequently bounded by

\mu_{t,\max}(x)=1/(4\bar{c}_{t}(x))\in[4/9,4/7]
.

Choose
\widehat{f}^{1}=f_{0,0}
and let

(\widehat{b}_{1,t},\widehat{b}_{2,t})
denote the fitted parameters
thereafter. The objective in (
19
) integrates the
continuous polynomial

\displaystyle P_{t}(x,a)={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}+\frac{a}{4}[2\ell(x+\widehat{b}_{1,t})-1]-\frac{a^{2}}{8}\ell(x+\widehat{b}_{2,t})

\displaystyle+\beta_{t}\phi(a)^{\top}S_{t-1}(x)^{-1}\phi(a)+\alpha_{r}d_{t}^{c}(x)a.

We show that a maximizing distribution needs at most two support
points. The attainable pairs

(\mathbb{E}_{\pi}a,\mathbb{E}_{\pi}P_{t}(x,a))
form the compact convex hull of the
graph
(a,P_{t}(x,a))
. At a maximizing mean, a maximizing pair lies
on the upper boundary of this hull. At mean zero it is represented
by
\delta_{0}
. At an interior mean, a supporting line meets the graph
at contact points whose convex hull contains the maximizing pair.
The smallest and largest contact abscissas suffice to represent its
mean and value. Since
\mu_{t,\max}(x)<1
, these cases cover every optimizer.

It thus suffices to solve the finite-dimensional problem

\max_{(p,a_{1},a_{2})\in[0,1]^{3}}\{pP_{t}(x,a_{1})+(1-p)P_{t}(x,a_{2})\}\quad\text{subject to}\quad pa_{1}+(1-p)a_{2}\leq\mu_{t,\max}(x).

Its feasible set is nonempty and compact. As
\mu
varies in
[4/9,4/7]
,
the feasible correspondence is continuous: if the bound decreases to

\mu^{\prime}
, scaling both actions by
\mu^{\prime}/\mu
gives a feasible triple converging
to the original one; if it increases, the original triple remains
feasible. The coefficients of the objective and
\mu_{t,\max}(x)
are measurable.
The countable dense-set argument in Appendix
G.2

shows that the cost extrema are measurable in the history and queried
context. For each fixed history, the uniformly convergent Fourier series
also makes them continuous in
x
. The archived policy integrals and
the matrix inverse are measurable as well. The measurable maximum theorem therefore gives
a measurable maximizing triple, which we fix as the tie-breaking rule.

This verifies all optimization and statistical requirements of
Theorem
2
. Its reward term is

\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{T}\,\ell_{T}^{3/2})
and its cost term is

\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{T}\,\ell_{T}^{2})
. We obtain

\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{T}\,\ell_{T}^{2})
regret and feasible policies in every round
for a noisy continuous-action problem with an infinite-dimensional
convex cost class and two independent nonlinear reward features.

Appendix H
Comparison with the Published Regret Bound of Pacchiano et al.

We construct unknown reward and cost classes satisfying our setup and
compare our guarantee with the published bound of

Pacchiano et al. (2025)
. Both learners observe only the selected
action’s reward and cost. The reward contributions exhibit a polynomial
gap, while the cost contributions exhibit a logarithmic gap in the
published upper-bound expressions. We state the scope of the cost
comparison explicitly below.
We then explain the role of the cost-class assumptions through a
nonconvex lower bound and a general entropy-to-eluder implication.

H.1
Model Classes and Verification of the Assumptions

Fix an integer
M\geq 3
and set

\varepsilon_{M}=e^{-M},\qquad T=\lceil e^{2M}\rceil,\qquad N_{r}=\lfloor T^{1/4}\rfloor,\qquad N_{c}=M^{4}.

and

Define

\displaystyle f_{h}((i,j),a_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4},

\displaystyle f_{h}((i,j),a_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}+\frac{1}{4}\mathbf{1}\{i\leq h\},

(67)

\displaystyle c_{\theta,v}((i,j),a_{1})

Write

After choosing

All functions take values in

Both unknown functions affect the optimal decision. Its probability

(68)

Indeed, the reward advantage of

-1/8
or

At the concrete truth

. Thus neither

The cost-class diameter is
1/4+\varepsilon_{M}

width bound would be linear in

Offline regression oracles.

Reward ERM over the
N_{r}+1
thresholds has the valid budget

\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{off}}(\mathcal{F}_{N_{r}},n,\rho)=8\log((N_{r}+1)/\rho)
for

by the finite-class argument in Appendix
G.3
.
For costs, a scalar fit exploits the size of the coordinate

s=n-1\geq 1

K_{s}
count the observations
with
A_{i}=a_{1}

[3/8,5/8]
to

\widehat{\theta}

\widehat{\theta}

Conditional Hoeffding’s lemma gives

\mathbb{E}\exp(\lambda S_{s}-\lambda^{2}K_{s}/8)\leq 1
.
For each possible

Every selected risky mean lies in
[\theta^{*},\theta^{*}+\varepsilon_{M}]

Projection onto
[3/8,5/8]

(69)

For

For a polylogarithmic budget at every horizon for each fixed class,

\min_{\theta\in[3/8,5/8],\ v\in\Lambda_{N_{c}}}\sum_{i<n:A_{i}=a_{1}}(\theta+\varepsilon_{M}v_{J_{i}}-Y_{i})^{2}.

(70)

Proposition
2

(71)

Proposition 2

The same event, of probability at least
1-\delta

at every context and round.

Published OPNLB bound.

Apply
Pacchiano et al. (2025, Theorem 38)

\mathcal{F}_{N_{r}}

\mathcal{C}_{M}^{\rm fin}

(f_{h^{*}},c_{\theta_{-},0})

\begin{gathered}\operatorname{Reg}_{\rm OPNLB}^{\rm ctx}(T)\leq C\left[\underbrace{\sqrt{TN_{r}\gamma_{r}}+N_{r}}_{\text{reward contribution}}+\underbrace{\frac{3}{2}\bigl(\sqrt{TN_{c}\gamma_{c}}+N_{c}\bigr)}_{\text{cost contribution}}\right],\\

Here

A common regret criterion.

The superscript

\max_{A\in\{\mathrm{CS\text{-}UCCB},\mathrm{OPNLB}\}}|\operatorname{Reg}_{A}^{\rm ctx}(T)-\operatorname{Reg}_{A}(T)|\leq\sqrt{2T\log(4/\eta)}

(82)

with probability at least

For total failure probability

Scope of the cost comparison.

The logarithmic cost gap concerns the published theorem’s
1/T

resolution. It is not a separation from an eluder analysis with an
optimized cutoff. In fact,
d_{E}(\mathcal{C}_{M}^{\rm fin},2\varepsilon_{M})\leq 2
.
To see this, group the finite prior into the low-cost models

c_{3/8,0},c_{3/8,e_{j}}
and the two high-cost constant models.
Within-group differences are below
2\varepsilon_{M}
, so every witness must
cross groups. Such a difference equals
\theta_{\pm}-3/8
at every risky
coordinate except possibly one, where it is smaller by
\varepsilon_{M}
.
After two distinct risky coordinates, some historical difference
therefore equals the largest possible current difference. A third
point cannot be independent; repeated coordinates and the safe
action cannot extend an independent sequence either.
Retaining a cutoff
\alpha
in the usual eluder-width summation gives

\mathcal{O}(\alpha T+d_{E}(\alpha)+\sqrt{Td_{E}(\alpha)\gamma_{c}})
.
Choosing
\alpha=2\varepsilon_{M}
reduces the cost expression to

\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{T\log\log T})
at fixed confidence.
Thus optimizing that analysis removes the displayed cost gap.

Cost learning is necessary.

The scalar component requires nontrivial learning in this same
family. Consider any algorithm that is feasible at all contexts
and rounds with probability at least
1-\delta
for both truths

(f_{h^{*}},c_{\theta_{-},0})
and
(f_{h^{*}},c_{\theta_{+},0})
.
Let
P_{-}
and
P_{+}
be its full transcript laws, including its
randomness. Only the selected risky cost labels change. The chain
rule for relative entropy gives

\mathrm{KL}(P_{-}\|P_{+})\leq T\,\mathrm{kl}(\mathrm{Ber}(\theta_{-}),\mathrm{Ber}(\theta_{+}))\leq\frac{T(\theta_{-}-\theta_{+})^{2}}{\theta_{+}(1-\theta_{+})}\leq\frac{1}{192},

since
\theta_{+}-\theta_{-}=1/(32\sqrt{T})
and

\theta_{+}(1-\theta_{+})\geq 3/16
. Pinsker’s inequality bounds total variation
by
1/\sqrt{384}
. Write
p_{t}(i,j):=\pi_{t}(a_{1}\mid(i,j))
.
The event
p_{t}(i,j)\leq(1/2)/\theta_{+}
for all

t,i,j
has
P_{+}
-probability at least
1-\delta
, and hence

P_{-}
-probability at least
1-\delta-1/\sqrt{384}
.
On this event, favorable contexts have reward advantage
1/8

and probability
\mu=h^{*}/N_{r}\geq 1/3
, so

\operatorname{Reg}_{-}(T)\geq\frac{\mu T}{8}\left(\frac{1/2}{\theta_{-}}-\frac{1/2}{\theta_{+}}\right)=\frac{\mu\sqrt{T}}{512\theta_{-}\theta_{+}}\geq\frac{\sqrt{T}}{486}.

Unfavorable contexts contribute nonnegative regret. This argument
still holds if the reward function is revealed to the learner;
therefore the square-root cost term reflects necessary cost learning,
not a vanishing diameter or an already known optimal policy.

H.4
Why Small Covering Entropy Alone Is Insufficient

We now give a separate construction that explains the need for
structure beyond a covering-number bound. Let
M\geq 2
and
T\geq 1

be arbitrary, take
X_{t}\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\operatorname{Unif}([M])
, and use two actions
a_{0},a_{1}
.
The reward is known and deterministic:

f^{*}(x,a_{0})=0
and
f^{*}(x,a_{1})=1
.
Set
\tau=1/2
and consider the nonconvex cost class

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{M}^{\rm sp}

\displaystyle=\{c^{(0)},c^{(1)},\ldots,c^{(M)}\},

(84)

\displaystyle c^{(0)}(x,b)

\displaystyle=0,\qquad c^{(j)}(x,b)=\mathbf{1}\{x=j,\ b=a_{1}\},\quad j\in[M].

The learner observes only the selected action’s deterministic reward
and cost, after acting. The safe action has known cost zero under
every model, with fixed safety gap
\Delta=1/2
.
Under
c^{(0)}
, always choosing
a_{1}
is optimal. Under
c^{(j)}
,
feasibility at context
j
requires

\pi_{t}(a_{1}\mid j)\leq 1/2
.

Distinct models in (
84
) have uniform distance
one. With our convention that covering centers lie in the class,

\log\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{C}_{M}^{\rm sp},u,\|\cdot\|_{\infty})=\log(M+1)\qquad(0<u<1).

(85)

In this noiseless instance, consistent least squares has zero
empirical prediction error under every admissible sampling rule:
every returned model agrees with the true cost on all queried
pairs. The known reward also has a zero-error oracle. Nevertheless,
the following lower bound holds for every learning algorithm,
regardless of its computation or access to these regression oracles.

Proposition 4
.

Fix
\delta\in(0,1/4)
. Suppose an algorithm satisfies, for every

j\in\{0,\ldots,M\}
,

\mathbb{P}_{j}\!\left\{c^{(j)}(x,\pi_{t})\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\text{ for every }x\in[M],\ t\leq T\right\}\geq 1-\delta,

where
\mathbb{P}_{j}
denotes its interaction law when the true cost is

c^{(j)}
. Then under
c^{(0)}
,

\mathbb{P}_{0}\!\left\{\operatorname{Reg}(T)\geq\tfrac{1}{8}\min\{T,M\}\right\}\geq 1-4\delta.

(86)

Moreover,

\mathbb{E}_{0}\operatorname{Reg}(T)\geq(1/4-\delta/2)\min\{T,M\}
.

Proof of Proposition
4
.

Write
p_{t}(j)=\pi_{t}(a_{1}\mid j)
and let

U_{j}=\inf\{s\leq T:(X_{s},A_{s})=(j,a_{1})\}
, with
U_{j}=\infty

if this pair is never selected. Define the stopped event

E_{j}=\{\text{there exists }t\leq T\text{ with }t\leq U_{j}\text{ and }p_{t}(j)>1/2\}.

The round
t=U_{j}
is included because its policy is chosen before
the cost feedback arrives. Couple the null model
c^{(0)}
and
the spike model
c^{(j)}
using the same contexts and learner
randomness. All observed labels, histories, and policy choices
coincide until the feedback from the first selected pair
(j,a_{1})
.
The event
E_{j}
therefore occurs identically under this coupling.
Since it is a safety violation under
c^{(j)}
, we obtain

\mathbb{P}_{0}(E_{j})=\mathbb{P}_{j}(E_{j})\leq\delta.

Let
B=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\mathbf{1}_{E_{j}}
under
\mathbb{P}_{0}
.
Linearity of expectation gives
\mathbb{E}_{0}B\leq M\delta
; independence
between these events is not required. Markov’s inequality then gives

\mathbb{P}_{0}(B\leq M/4)\geq 1-4\delta.

(87)

On each history, by round
t
at most
t-1
distinct pairs of the
form
(j,a_{1})
have been observed. Apart from at most
B
contexts,
every remaining unqueried context has
p_{t}(j)\leq 1/2
.
Under
c^{(0)}
, the regret contribution at round
t
is exactly

M^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{M}(1-p_{t}(j))
. Hence, with

n=\min\{T,M\}
and using nonnegativity of all later contributions,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Reg}(T)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\left(1-\frac{t-1}{M}-\frac{B}{M}\right)

(88)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[n-\frac{n(n-1)}{2M}-\frac{nB}{M}\right].

On
B\leq M/4
, this is at least
n/8
, proving
(
86
). Taking expectations in
(
88
) gives the stated expectation bound.
The calculation uses exactly our regret definition, with the current
context averaged conditional on the pre-round history; no conversion
between regret criteria is needed.
∎

Taking
M=T
, we obtain a family with logarithmic covering entropy
and zero oracle error, yet linear regret with probability at least

1-4\delta
. Thus polylogarithmic original-class covering entropy
and offline error alone cannot imply a uniform

\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{T})
regret guarantee. This shows
that additional structure is needed; convexity together with the
entropy condition is the sufficient structure used in our analysis.
The example does not assert that convexity is the only such structure.

Convexification accounts for the difficulty rather than removing it
at the same complexity. In fact,

\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal{C}_{M}^{\rm sp})=\{c_{v}(x,b)=v_{x}\mathbf{1}\{b=a_{1}\}:v\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{M},\ \textstyle\sum_{j}v_{j}\leq 1\}.

For every subset
S\subseteq[M]
, this hull contains

v^{S}=M^{-1}\mathbf{1}_{S}
. The
2^{M}
resulting functions are pairwise

1/M
apart, so a uniform ball of radius
u=1/(8M)
can contain
at most one of them. Conversely, rounding every coordinate of
v

down to a multiple of
u
gives a proper
u
-net with at most

\binom{9M}{M}\leq(9e)^{M}
centers. We therefore obtain

M\log 2\leq\log\mathcal{N}(\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal{C}_{M}^{\rm sp}),1/(8M),\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\leq M\log(9e).

(89)

Thus the convex hull has entropy of order
M
at this resolution,
consistent with the convex-hull guarantee in
Remark
1
.

H.5
Cost Entropy and Eluder Dimension

Assumption
3
also bounds the pairwise eluder
dimension used in this appendix. We use the convention of

Pacchiano et al. (2025, Definition 36)
:
d_{E}(\mathcal{C},\varepsilon)

is the supremum of the lengths of sequences that are independent
at some common threshold
\varepsilon^{\prime}\geq\varepsilon
.

Proposition 5
.

Under Assumption
3
, there is a finite constant

C_{p_{\mathcal{C}}}
, depending only on
p_{\mathcal{C}}
, such that, for every

0<\varepsilon\leq 1
,

d_{E}(\mathcal{C},\varepsilon)\leq C_{p_{\mathcal{C}}}D_{\mathcal{C}}\left[\log\frac{eA_{\mathcal{C}}D_{\mathcal{C}}}{\varepsilon}\right]^{p_{\mathcal{C}}}.

(90)

In particular, fixed entropy parameters imply

d_{E}(\mathcal{C},1/T)=\mathcal{O}_{p_{\mathcal{C}}}\bigl(D_{\mathcal{C}}[\log(eA_{\mathcal{C}}D_{\mathcal{C}}T)]^{p_{\mathcal{C}}}\bigr)
.

Proof of Proposition
5
.

Write
D=D_{\mathcal{C}}
,
A=A_{\mathcal{C}}
, and
p=p_{\mathcal{C}}
. Consider any
nonempty independent sequence
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}
at a common threshold

\varepsilon^{\prime}\geq\varepsilon
. For each
k
, independence supplies

h_{k}=c_{k}-c_{k}^{\prime}
with
c_{k},c_{k}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
such that

\sum_{i<k}h_{k}(z_{i})^{2}\leq(\varepsilon^{\prime})^{2},\qquad|h_{k}(z_{k})|>\varepsilon^{\prime}.

(91)

Since costs take values in
[0,1]
, such a sequence requires

\varepsilon^{\prime}<1
. In particular,
d_{E}(\mathcal{C},1)=0
.

Set
r=\varepsilon/(2\sqrt{n})
and
m=m(r)
, with
m(r)
as defined
after Lemma
7
. Then
0<r\leq 1/2
.
If
m=0
, Lemma
8
gives

\|h_{k}\|_{\infty}\leq r<\varepsilon^{\prime}
, contradicting
(
91
). Hence
m\geq 1
. The same lemma
provides a fixed map
\phi:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
and vectors
\theta_{k}
with

\|\phi(z)\|_{2}\leq 1,\qquad\|\theta_{k}\|_{2}\leq 2\sqrt{m},\qquad\|h_{k}-\theta_{k}^{\top}\phi\|_{\infty}\leq r.

Define

\lambda=\frac{(\varepsilon^{\prime})^{2}}{4m},\qquad\Sigma_{k}=\lambda I+\sum_{i<k}\phi(z_{i})\phi(z_{i})^{\top}.

The approximation and (
91
) imply

|\theta_{k}^{\top}\phi(z_{k})|>\varepsilon^{\prime}-r\geq\varepsilon^{\prime}/2

and

\displaystyle\theta_{k}^{\top}\Sigma_{k}\theta_{k}

\displaystyle=\lambda\|\theta_{k}\|_{2}^{2}+\sum_{i<k}(\theta_{k}^{\top}\phi(z_{i}))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(\varepsilon^{\prime})^{2}+2\sum_{i<k}h_{k}(z_{i})^{2}+2(k-1)r^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 3(\varepsilon^{\prime})^{2}+2nr^{2}\leq\frac{7}{2}(\varepsilon^{\prime})^{2}.

The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality in the
\Sigma_{k}
norm therefore yields

\ell_{k}:=\phi(z_{k})^{\top}\Sigma_{k}^{-1}\phi(z_{k})\geq\frac{(\theta_{k}^{\top}\phi(z_{k}))^{2}}{\theta_{k}^{\top}\Sigma_{k}\theta_{k}}>\frac{1}{14}.

The determinant identity and the arithmetic–geometric mean
inequality, together with
\|\phi(z_{k})\|_{2}\leq 1
, give

\displaystyle n\log(15/14)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\log(1+\ell_{k})=\log\frac{\det\Sigma_{n+1}}{\det(\lambda I)}

\displaystyle\leq m\log\left(1+\frac{n}{m\lambda}\right)=m\log\left(1+\frac{4n}{(\varepsilon^{\prime})^{2}}\right)\leq m\log\left(1+\frac{4n}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right).

As
1/\log(15/14)<15
, this proves

n\leq 15\,m\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2\sqrt{n}}\right)\log\left(1+\frac{4n}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right).

(92)

Let
K_{p}\geq 1
be a valid constant in
Lemma
7
, and put

L=\log\frac{2eAD}{\varepsilon},\qquad a=30K_{p}D.

That lemma bounds the approximation dimension by

K_{p}D[L+\tfrac{1}{2}\log n]^{p-1}
. Since
A\geq e
,
D\geq 1
,

n\geq 1
, and
\varepsilon\leq 1
, we also have

\log(1+4n/\varepsilon^{2})\leq 2\log(2eAD\sqrt{n}/\varepsilon)=2[L+\tfrac{1}{2}\log n].

Substitution into (
92
) gives

n\leq a[L+\tfrac{1}{2}\log n]^{p}.

To remove the remaining dependence on
n
from the right-hand side,
first suppose
n\geq a
and set
y=(n/a)^{1/p}\geq 1
. Then

y\leq L+\tfrac{1}{2}\log a+\tfrac{p}{2}\log y.

Using
\log(y/p)\leq y/p-1
and rearranging gives

y\leq 2L+\log a-p+p\log p\leq 2L+\log a+p\log p.

The last upper bound exceeds one, so the case
n<a
is covered
as well. Consequently,

n\leq 30K_{p}D\left[2\log\frac{2eAD}{\varepsilon}+\log(30K_{p}D)+p\log p\right]^{p}.

To absorb constants, write
B=\log(eAD/\varepsilon)\geq 1
.
The bracket is at most

[3+2\log 2+\log(30K_{p})+p\log p]B
, because
\log D\leq B
.
Thus (
90
) holds with

C_{p}=30K_{p}[3+2\log 2+\log(30K_{p})+p\log p]^{p}.

This bound applies to every finite independent sequence and every
common threshold
\varepsilon^{\prime}\geq\varepsilon
. Taking the supremum
of their lengths proves the claim, including finiteness of the
eluder dimension. If there is no nonempty independent sequence,
the bound holds trivially.
∎

This general bound need not be sharp for a particular class.
For the comparison family,
(D_{\mathcal{C}},A_{\mathcal{C}},p_{\mathcal{C}})=(2,e,5)
,
so the proposition gives

d_{E}(\mathcal{C}_{M},1/T)\leq 2C_{5}[\log(2e^{2}T)]^{5}=\mathcal{O}((\log T)^{5}).

In contrast, Proposition
3
gives the exact
value
d_{E}(\mathcal{C}_{M},1/T)=d_{E}(\mathcal{C}_{M}^{\rm fin},1/T)=M^{4}=\Theta((\log T)^{4})
when
T=\lceil e^{2M}\rceil
.
We use that exact value in (
81
),
so the general upper bound is consistent with the logarithmic cost
comparison in (
83
). That comparison
concerns the published
1/T
formula, with the cutoff qualification
explained above. The cost assumptions thus control the eluder
dimension without making these particular upper-bound expressions
identical. This consequence of convexity does not apply to the
general nonconvex reward class.

Our results leave the characterization of sufficient structure for
nonconvex cost classes unresolved. The lower bound in
Appendix
H.4
shows that covering
entropy alone cannot provide the uniform regret guarantee, while
convexity together with the entropy condition provides the structure
used here.

Appendix I
Computation

We describe how to compute the quantities used by CS-UCCB. Its cost
confidence set has one convex squared-error constraint from the
current fit, so support queries are convex optimization problems over
the cost class. We give their explicit formulation and a finite-dimensional
implementation below. Efficient solution requires a tractable class
representation; convexity and covering entropy alone do not provide
one. We count arithmetic operations and oracle calls, and require
numerical implementations to certify feasibility.

I.1
Computing the Cost Confidence Set and Its Supports

At
t=1
, we set
{\mathcal{S}}_{1}=\mathcal{C}
. At each subsequent stage, the current
cost fit defines one convex squared-error constraint:

{\mathcal{S}}_{t}=\left\{c\in\mathcal{C}:\sum_{i<t}(c(Z_{i})-\widehat{c}^{t}(Z_{i}))^{2}\leq B_{t}^{\mathcal{C}}\right\}.

We archive each stage’s fit, data prefix, and budget to represent its
confidence set during replay. Evaluating the current fit on its full
prefix takes
t-1
function evaluations; these values can be cached.
Earlier fits specify earlier confidence sets, and impose no additional
constraints on
{\mathcal{S}}_{t}
. Thus each set can be represented implicitly
without enumerating functions. Set emptiness is a convex
feasibility problem; an empty set triggers the safe-action instruction
in Algorithm
2
.

For
b\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
, define the support query

M_{t}(x,b)=\sup_{c\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t}}\sum_{a}b_{a}c(x,a).

(93)

This maximizes a linear functional over a convex set. To make the
computational requirements concrete, suppose the class has a known
affine representation

c_{\theta}(z)=c_{\rm ref}(z)+\phi(z)^{\top}\theta,\qquad\theta\in\Theta\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
\Theta
is a tractable convex domain. Let
\Phi_{t}
have rows

\phi(Z_{i})^{\top}
for
i<t
, and put

h_{t}=(\widehat{c}^{t}(Z_{i})-c_{\rm ref}(Z_{i}))_{i<t}
.
Then (
93
) is

\displaystyle\max_{\theta\in\Theta}

\displaystyle\sum_{a}b_{a}c_{\rm ref}(x,a)+\left(\sum_{a}b_{a}\phi(x,a)\right)^{\top}\theta

(94)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{t}\theta-h_{t}\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{B_{t}^{\mathcal{C}}}.

At
t=1
, there are no empirical constraints. For a polyhedral

\Theta
, this is a second-order cone program with one empirical
norm constraint containing
t-1
residuals; other convex representations
give the corresponding constrained quadratic or conic formulation.
Efficient convex solvers
therefore apply when the domain and its numerical encoding permit
them. Least-squares regression over this affine class is also a
convex optimization problem.

The empirical constraint can instead be stored in the quadratic form

\theta^{\top}G_{t}\theta-2g_{t}^{\top}\theta+\kappa_{t}\leq B_{t}^{\mathcal{C}},

where
G_{t}=\Phi_{t}^{\top}\Phi_{t}
,
g_{t}=\Phi_{t}^{\top}h_{t}
, and

\kappa_{t}=\|h_{t}\|_{2}^{2}
.
The feature Gram matrix
G_{t}
admits a rank-one update. The vector

h_{t}
uses the current predictor on every past sample, so
g_{t}
and

\kappa_{t}
are recomputed after each refit. Archiving these coefficients
is an alternative to retaining every residual vector.

A support routine used for safe policy optimization must return a
certified upper value, and a violating model when separation is
required. These requirements also apply to implicit or
infinite-dimensional classes: their support queries need a suitable
optimization procedure. A finite-dimensional approximation is usable
only if its error is included in the safety certificate.

I.2
Computing Cost Widths and Robustly Feasible Policies

The support values give the quantities in the policy objective:

V_{t}^{\delta}(x,p)=M_{t}(x,p),\qquad w_{t}(x,a)=M_{t}(x,e_{a})+M_{t}(x,-e_{a}).

We therefore use
2K
support queries to form the width vector.
During replay, these queries use the archived stage’s own current-fit
confidence set.
Given the reward bonus vector
q_{t}(x,\cdot)
, set

s_{a}=\widehat{f}^{t}(x,a)+\beta_{t}q_{t}(x,a)+\alpha_{r}w_{t}(x,a)
.
The exact policy problem is the robust linear program

\displaystyle\max_{p\in\mathbb{R}^{K}}

\displaystyle\sum_{a}p_{a}s_{a}

(95)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle p_{a}\geq 0,\qquad\sum_{a}p_{a}=1,

\displaystyle\sum_{a}p_{a}c(x,a)\leq\tau\qquad\text{for all }c\in{\mathcal{S}}_{t}.

Equivalently, the last constraint is
M_{t}(x,p)\leq\tau
. The support
function is convex in
p
, so this is a convex policy optimization
problem with
K
decision variables. A support query tests feasibility;
if a returned model violates the constraint, its cost vector supplies
a separating row. This gives a separation-based implementation of the
semi-infinite LP. A finite-row LP requires additional structure.

When the convex hull of the attainable cost vectors at
x
is supplied
as a polytope with vertices
b^{1},\ldots,b^{J}\in[0,1]^{K}
, the formulation
reduces exactly to

\displaystyle\max_{p\geq 0}

\displaystyle s^{\top}p

(96)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}^{\top}p=1,\qquad(b^{j})^{\top}p\leq\tau\quad(j\in[J]).

Ordinary LP algorithms apply. Both formulations include the known safe
action, so their feasible regions are nonempty. If a solver returns a
certified feasible policy whose exact score is within
\zeta_{t}
of
the optimum at every context,
we obtain the same optimism inequality with an additional
\zeta_{t}
.
Summing the regret proof adds at most
\sum_{t}\zeta_{t}
. The solver’s
precision and tie-breaking rule are archived and reused during replay.

With approximate support values, an upper bound certifies safety,
whereas a violating model supplies a valid separating row. Support and
policy tolerances must also certify the stated objective error

\zeta_{t}
; feasibility alone does not control the regret loss.

I.3
Computing the Counterfactual Bonus by Cumulative Policy Mass

At a queried context
x
, initialize
\omega_{1}(x,a)=0
. Replay each archived
stage and update

q_{i}(x,a)=\frac{1}{1+\omega_{i}(x,a)},\qquad\omega_{i+1}(x,a)=\omega_{i}(x,a)+\widetilde{\pi}_{t,i}(a\mid x).

We compute a stage’s policy using (
95
), or set it to

\pi_{0}
if the archive instructs choosing the safe action
a_{0}
.
The updated vector
\omega_{i+1}(x,\cdot)
is
exactly
\sum_{j\leq i}\pi_{j}(\cdot\mid x)
by
Lemma
14
. Each stage needs
O(K)
bonus
arithmetic and
O(K)
working storage.
Through stage
t
, the bonus computation requires
O(Kt)

arithmetic operations. If the same context appears again,
we reuse its stored cumulative policy masses and update them
through the newly archived stages.

Total computational accounting.

There are at most
T-1
calls to each regression oracle and at most

T-1
convex feasibility checks for empty confidence sets; replay never
refits either model. Across
T
distinct queried contexts, replay uses
at most
T(T+1)/2
policy solves and
O(KT^{2})
bonus arithmetic.
Caching each current fit’s values on its data prefix uses at most

T(T-1)/2
cost-fit evaluations in total. These values are reused
during replay. For the affine representation, reference values and
feature vectors on the observed pairs can also be cached. The
quadratic coefficients above can then be assembled in

O(Td^{2}+T^{2}d)
arithmetic operations:
G_{t}
is updated incrementally,
while
g_{t}
and
\kappa_{t}
are recomputed from each new fit’s full
prefix. These counts exclude the regression and optimization solves.
If
L_{t,i}
support-based separation tests are used at replay stage
i

of round
t
, the cost-support queries are bounded by

\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{t}(2K+L_{t,i}),

with zero contribution at stages where the recorded instruction selects

a_{0}
. These counts separate
regression, bonus calculation, and constrained optimization.

Each archived confidence set after stage one uses that stage’s single
current-fit constraint; replay does not replace it with a later set
or refit a predictor.
There is no covering-number factor in these computational counts.
The cost of each support or policy solve depends on the representation,
the prefix length (or the compressed quadratic representation), and
the requested accuracy.
A polynomial running-time guarantee therefore requires efficient
regression and optimization routines with suitable numerical encodings;
it does not follow from an abstract convex class alone.

I.4
Computing the Bonus and Policy for Infinite Actions

The cost update is unchanged. For the reward bonus, an implementation
needs the reference design and the function-pair optimization

q_{t}^{\infty}(x,a)=\sup_{f,f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}}\frac{(f(x,a)-f^{\prime}(x,a))^{2}}{d_{x}\int(f(x,b)-f^{\prime}(x,b))^{2}\,\rho_{x}^{*}(db)+\sum_{i<t}\int(f(x,b)-f^{\prime}(x,b))^{2}\,\pi_{i}(db\mid x)}.

We use the zero-denominator convention from Definition
2
.
At
t=1
only the reference term appears; it creates no regression
samples. Policy optimization is (
95
) with action
integrals replacing finite sums. Its solution requires an optimization
procedure for the supplied class and action space.

In the two-feature example of Appendix
G.4
,
the bonus is the explicit quadratic form

\phi(a)^{\top}S_{t-1}(x)^{-1}\phi(a)
, and we reduce policy optimization
to three variables
(p,a_{1},a_{2})\in[0,1]^{3}
. These features are part of
that example’s representation. The cost geometry derived for the
general regret proof is not an additional computational input.

Proof of Lemma
7
.

We first give the volume argument for a symmetric convex set
D

contained in the unit ball of a Banach space. Suppose

f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\in D
satisfy

\operatorname{dist}\bigl(f_{i},\operatorname{span}\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{i-1}\}\bigr)>r.

For each
i
, we apply the Hahn–Banach theorem in the quotient by the
preceding span to obtain a bounded linear functional
\ell_{i}
with norm one,
vanishing on that span, and
\ell_{i}(f_{i})>r
after a possible sign
change. Let
L(f)=(\ell_{1}(f),\ldots,\ell_{m}(f))
.
The matrix with columns
L(f_{j})
is triangular with diagonal entries
larger than
r
. Its determinant consequently has magnitude greater
than
r^{m}
.

Symmetry and convexity give

\operatorname{absconv}\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\}\subseteq D
. Its image under

L
is an invertible linear image of the
\ell_{1}
unit ball in

\mathbb{R}^{m}
. The latter has volume
2^{m}/m!
, and hence that image has
volume greater than
2^{m}r^{m}/m!
.
Every uniform ball of radius
u
has its image inside an

m
-dimensional cube of side
2u
, because
\|\ell_{i}\|=1
.
Comparing volumes for any finite cover of
D
gives

\mathcal{N}(D,u,\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\geq\frac{(r/u)^{m}}{m!}.

(97)

Only the compact cross-polytope just constructed enters this volume
comparison; no compactness of
D
is needed.

Now suppose
\log\mathcal{N}(D,u)\leq a[\log(b/u)]^{p}
, with

a\geq 1
,
b\geq e
, and
p\geq 1
. For
s>0
, choose

u=re^{-s}/m
, which lies in
(0,1]
. Since
m!\leq m^{m}
,
(
97
) gives

ms\leq a[\log(bm/r)+s]^{p}.

(98)

For
p=1
, division by
s
followed by
s\to\infty
yields
m\leq a
.
For
p>1
, put
q=p-1
and

k_{p}=p^{p}/(p-1)^{p-1}
. We choose

s=\log(bm/r)/(p-1)
in (
98
) and obtain

m\leq k_{p}a[\log(bm/r)]^{q}.

Here is an explicit resolution of this implicit inequality.
Set
a^{\prime}=k_{p}a
and
L_{0}=\log(ba^{\prime}/r)\geq 1
. If
m<a^{\prime}
, then

m\leq a^{\prime}L_{0}^{q}
. Otherwise let
x=m/a^{\prime}\geq 1
and
y=x^{1/q}
.
The last inequality implies
y\leq L_{0}+q\log y
. Using

\log u\leq u-1
with
u=y/(2q)
, we bound

q\log y\leq y/2+q\log(2q)-q\leq y/2+q\max\{0,\log(2q)\}.

It follows that

y\leq[2+2q\max\{0,\log(2q)\}]L_{0}
.
This establishes

m\leq C_{p}^{\prime}a[\log(eba/r)]^{p-1}
for a constant depending only on
p
.

We apply this result to
D=\mathcal{D}
,
a=2D_{\mathcal{C}}
, and
b=2A_{\mathcal{C}}
using
(
26
). Starting from the zero space, choose
any
f_{i}\in\mathcal{D}
whose distance from the current span exceeds
r
,
whenever such an element exists. A supremum need not be attained:
the strict inequality provides a permissible choice. The dimension
bound just proved prevents this construction from continuing
arbitrarily long. At termination its span satisfies
(
27
). Absorbing the fixed factors in
a,b

into
C_{p_{\mathcal{C}}}
gives the stated constants.

For completeness, when
p_{\mathcal{C}}=1
we obtain the sharper affine-dimension
claim directly from
\mathcal{C}
. If
\bar{c}_{0},\ldots,\bar{c}_{d}\in\mathcal{C}

\mathcal{C}
. On the

d

\bar{c}_{i}-\bar{c}_{0}

functionals and extend them to
\mathbb{B}
by the Hahn–Banach theorem. Their
joint image of this simplex has positive
d
-dimensional volume.
The image of a uniform
u

\log\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{C},u,\|\cdot\|_{\infty})\geq d\log(1/u)+O(1)\quad(u\downarrow 0).

Comparison with

Proof of Lemma

Introduction

This paper is a continuation of the paper
[

In the previous paper, we introduced the notion of entropy for harmonic metrics on cyclic Higgs bundles, where the Higgs field may have multivalency of cyclic type, and for Hermitian metrics that solve the Hitchin equation with a “collapsed” coefficient obtained as the limit of infinitely increasing covering order. In this paper, we continue to investigate the properties of entropy and introduce a quantity called
free energy
, in analogy with the theory of canonical ensemble in statistical physics. Unlike entropy, free energy is related to existing quantities that have been well studied so far: the energy density of harmonic maps, or the norm of the Higgs field. One of the aims of this paper is to establish both an extension of existing results on the energy density of harmonic maps and a new interpretation of them.

A cyclic Higgs bundle is a Higgs bundle associated with a holomorphic section of a power of the canonical bundle on a Riemann surface (cf.
[
2
,
28
,
29

Cyclic Higgs bundles and harmonic metrics thereon are special cases of more general Higgs bundles and harmonic metrics (cf.

For each

2.6
Canonical ensemble

We provide a very short review of the notions that appear in relation with the canonical ensemble in the theory of equilibrium statistical mechanics (cf.
[
18
,
24
,
33
]
). Throughout this section, we do not care the so called Boltzmann constant. Let
\Omega
be a set of microstates. We suppose that
\Omega
is a finite set for simplicity. Let
E:\Omega\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be a map that associates each microstate
i\in\Omega
with the energy
E_{i}
of that state
i
. Let
\beta
be a positive real constant, called
inverse temperature
. Then we call the following probability distribution
(p_{i}(\beta))_{i\in\Omega}
on
\Omega

canonical ensemble
:

\displaystyle p_{i}(\beta)\coloneqq\frac{e^{-\beta E_{i}}}{\sum_{j\in\Omega}e^{-\beta E_{j}}}\ \text{for $i\in\Omega$}.

(10)

We denote by
Z(\beta)
the normalization constant of the above probability distribution

\displaystyle Z(\beta)\coloneqq\sum_{j\in\Omega}e^{-\beta E_{j}}

and call it the
partition function
. Then logarithm of the partition function multiplied by
-\beta^{-1}
is called the
free energy
:

\displaystyle F(\beta)\coloneqq-\frac{1}{\beta}\log Z(\beta).

We set a new variable
T
as
T\coloneqq\beta^{-1}
. Although it is a bit of an abuse of notation, let
F(T)
be the free energy considered as a function of
T
. Then we can check that
F(T)
is a convex function. At the end of this section, we define the following function, which we call entropy:

\displaystyle S(\beta)\coloneqq\beta(\langle E\rangle^{{\rm can}}-F(\beta)),

where
\langle E\rangle^{{\rm can}}=\sum_{j\in\Omega}E_{j}p_{j}(\beta)
is the average of
E
with respect to the canonical ensemble. One can check that the above entropy
S(\beta)
coincides with the following
Shannon entropy
:

\displaystyle S(\beta)=-\sum_{j\in\Omega}p_{j}(\beta)\log p_{j}(\beta).

It can be checked that we have the following estimate:

\displaystyle 0\leq S(\beta)\leq\log|\Omega|,

where we denote by
|\Omega|
the cardinality of
\Omega
.
The minimum is attained if and only if
p_{j}(\beta)=1
for some
j\in\Omega
and the others are 0, and the maximum is attained if and only if
p_{j}(\beta)=1/|\Omega|
for all
j\in\Omega
, i.e.,
E:\Omega\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is a constant function.

4
Free energy

In this section we introduce the notion of
free energy
, in analogy with the theory of canonical ensemble in statistical physics. Let
H_{1},\dots,H_{r}
be Hermitian metrics obtained from a solution to equation (

Definition 21

We call the following function free energy:

\displaystyle F(r,\beta,\varphi,H_{\ast})\coloneqq-\frac{1}{\beta}\log(\sum_{j=1}^{r}({\rm vol}(H_{j})/{\rm vol}(H_{\ast}))^{\beta}),

where

is a reference metric.

Note that the difference between free energy functions with the same reference metric does not depend on the choice of the reference metric.

Remark 22

The analogy of the canonical ensemble can be further explored to introduce concepts such as energy and heat capacity, but since there are no meaningful results at this stage, we will not introduce them in this paper yet.

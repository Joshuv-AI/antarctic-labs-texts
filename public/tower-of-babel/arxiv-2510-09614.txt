1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
be the open unit disc and
\mathbb{T}
be the unit circle in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
. Let
dA
denote the normalized Lebesgue area measure on
\mathbb{D}
. The space
L^{2}(\mathbb{D},dA)
stands for the complex Hilbert space of square integrable functions on
\mathbb{D}
with the inner product defined by

\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}f(z)\overline{g(z)}\,dA(z)\quad(f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{D},dA)).

The
Bergman space
, denoted by
L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{D})
, is the closed subspace of all analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{D},dA)
. Moreover, the space
L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{D})
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space with respect to the kernel function
K_{z}
given by

K_{z}(w)=K(w,z)=\frac{1}{(1-\bar{z}w)^{2}}\quad(z,w\in\mathbb{D}).

Let
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
be the Hardy space of analytic functions
f
on
\mathbb{D}
such that

\|f\|=\sup_{0\leq r<1}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{it})|^{2}\,dt\right)^{1\over 2}<\infty.

We denote by
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
(or
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
) the Banach space of essentially bounded functions on
\mathbb{D}
(or
\mathbb{T}
) equipped with the sup-norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
. For
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
, the Toeplitz operator
T_{\varphi}
on the
Bergman space
is defined by

T_{\varphi}f=P(\varphi f),\quad(f\in L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{D})),

where
P
is the orthogonal projection of
L^{2}(\mathbb{D},dA)
onto
L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{D})
. For
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
, the Berezin transform of
\varphi
on
\mathbb{D}
, denoted by
\widetilde{\varphi}
, is defined as follows

\widetilde{\varphi}(z)=\langle T_{\varphi}k_{z},k_{z}\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\varphi(w)\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}{\left|1-\bar{z}w\right|^{4}}\,dA(w)\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}),

where
k_{z}
is the normalized reproducing kernel, i.e.,
k_{z}=\dfrac{K_{z}}{\|K_{z}\|}
. On the other hand, for
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, the harmonic extension of the function
\varphi
in
\mathbb{D}
, denoted by
\breve{\varphi}
, is given by the Poisson integral formula:

\breve{\varphi}(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\varphi(e^{it})\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{\left|1-ze^{-it}\right|^{2}}\,dt\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

It is easy to see that the harmonic extension of a function
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
in
\mathbb{D}
coincides with the Berezin transform of the Toeplitz operator
T_{\varphi}
with symbol
\varphi
on the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
over the unit disc. The main purpose of this note is to study the invertibility criteria for the certain Toeplitz operators on the Bergman space.

One of the key problems in operator theory and function theory is to determine the invertibilty of Toeplitz operators. In this direction, the complete characterization of invertible Toeplitz operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
was studied by Devinatz
[
1
]
. Douglas
[
2
]
characterized the invertibility of Toeplitz operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
with continuous symbols by showing that for
\varphi\in C(\mathbb{T})
, the space of continuous functions on
\mathbb{T}
, the Toeplitz operator
T_{\varphi}
is invertible on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
if there exists
\delta>0
such that
|\breve{\varphi}(z)|\geq\delta
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Thereby, Douglas raised the following problem:

Let
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
. Suppose there exists a
\delta>0
such that
|\breve{\varphi}(z)|\geq\delta
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Is the Toeplitz operator
T_{\varphi}
invertible on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
?

Many researchers including Chang and Tolokonnikov
[
9
]
investigated the above problem and obtained a sufficient condition that there exists a positive
\delta_{0}~(<1)
such that if
\delta_{0}<|\breve{\varphi}(z)|<1
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, then
T_{\varphi}
is invertible on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
(see also
[
7
]
). On the other hand, Wolff
[
10
]
constructed a function in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
that serves as an elegant counterexample for a negative answer to Douglas’s question. Thus, in the Bergman space setting the question reads:

Question 1
.

Is the invertibility of Bergman Toeplitz operator
T_{\varphi}
for
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
equivalent to the invertibility of the Berezin transform
\widetilde{\varphi}
in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
?

The above question is incredibly challenging and even for a bounded harmonic function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{D}
, the problem is still unsolved. However, the affirmative answers are available in the literature in the case when
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
is either ‘real harmonic’, or ‘non-negative’, or ‘analytic’, or ‘coanalytic’. For a detailed survey, see
[
5
,
6
,
12
]
. The problem of invertibility for Bergman Toeplitz operators with harmonic symbols had also been explored by Yoneda
[
11
]
. He obtained the invertibility of Berezin transform of the harmonic symbol of the form
\varphi=cg+d\overline{g}
, where
c,d\in\mathbb{C}
and
g\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
as a sufficient condition for the invertibility of the Bergman Toeplitz operator
T_{\varphi}
. Moreover, for such a
\varphi
if
g\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})\cap C(\overline{\mathbb{D}})
, then he obtained an affirmative answer to the Question
1
(see
[
11
, Theorem 2.6, Theorem 2.8]
). For a thorough discussion on Bergman Toeplitz operators, one can see
[
3
,
4
,
13
]
.

In this paper we have obtained an affirmative answer to Question
1
for harmonic symbols of the form
cg+d\overline{g}
, where
g\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
which relaxes the condition that
g
be continuous on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
unlike Yoneda’s result. Along the way, we prove some characterization results in the general setting of Hilbert space. In turn, it precisely extends the results concerning the invertibility of
T_{\varphi}
in terms of the invertibility of the Berezin transform of its symbol when
\varphi
is either real harmonic, analytic, coanalytic, or
T_{\varphi}
is normal provided
\varphi
is bounded harmonic.

The rest of the paper is structured as follows. Section
2
focuses on establishing the necessary background, and along the way, we fix some notations and definitions. In Section 3, we prove the invertibility of the Berezin transform of certain harmonic symbols determines the invertibility of the Bergman Toeplitz operator which extends Yoneda’s result.

2.
Preliminaries

This section compiles notations, definitions, and some basic results that are used in this paper. We denote
\mathcal{H}
as a Hilbert space over the field of complex numbers

\mathbb{C}
and
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
as the
C^{*}
-algebra of all bounded linear operators on

\mathcal{H}
. A linear operator
T
is said to be bounded below if there exists
c>0
such that
\|Th\|\geq c\|h\|
for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
. An operator
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is said to be hyponormal if
T^{*}T-TT^{*}
is a positive operator, equivalently,
\|Th\|\geq\|T^{*}h\|
for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
.

Let
H(\mathbb{D})
be a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (for e.g.
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
,
L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{D})
) on

\mathbb{D}
and the map
K:\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

defined by

K(w,z)=K_{z}(w)=\langle K_{z},K_{w}\rangle

be the reproducing kernel function of
H(\mathbb{D})
. We denote
k_{z}=\dfrac{K_{z}}{\|K_{z}\|}
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
as the normalized reproducing kernel of
H(\mathbb{D})
and the set
\{{k}_{z}:z\in\mathbb{D}\}
is a total set in
H(\mathbb{D})
. For more details and references on RKHS, see
[
8
]
. For a bounded operator
T
on
H(\mathbb{D})
, the
Berezin transform
of
T
, denoted by
\widetilde{T}
, is a complex-valued function on
\mathbb{D}
defined as

\widetilde{T}(z)=\langle Tk_{z},k_{z}\rangle\quad\text{for all }z\in\mathbb{D}.

Thus, every bounded linear operator
T
on
H(\mathbb{D})
induces a bounded function

\widetilde{T}
on
H(\mathbb{D})
. Indeed,
\|\widetilde{T}\|_{\infty}\leq\|T\|
. Therefore, the
Berezin transform
of an operator yields crucial information about the operator. In this note we mainly focus on the
Berezin transform
of Toeplitz operators on the Bergman space to determine the invertibility criterion of the Toeplitz operators. Throughout this article,
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
stands for the algebra of bounded analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
and it is also a multiplier algebra for the Hardy space as well as the Bergman space, and
C(\mathbb{T})
is the space of all continuous functions on
\mathbb{T}
.

We will be using the following well-known results very often.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\varphi,\psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
and
g\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}).
For
c,d\in\mathbb{C},
the following results hold for the Bergman Toeplitz operators

(1)

T_{c\varphi+d\psi}=cT_{\varphi}+dT_{\psi}
.

(2)

T_{\varphi}^{*}=T_{\overline{\varphi}}
.

(3)

T_{\varphi}T_{g}=T_{\varphi g}
.

(4)

T_{\overline{g}}T_{\varphi}=T_{\overline{g}\varphi}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose
T
is a bounded operator on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Then

(1)

T
is invertible if and only if both
T
and
T^{*}
are bounded below.

(2)

T
is bounded below if and only if
T^{*}
is surjective.

Theorem 2.3

(cf.
[
6
]
)
.

For
\varphi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
, let
T_{\varphi}:L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{D})\to L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{D})
be the Toeplitz operator. Then the following are equivalent

(1)

T_{\varphi}
is invertible.

(2)

T_{\overline{\varphi}}
is invertible.

(3)

\inf\limits_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|\varphi(z)|>0
.

Theorem 2.4

(cf.
[
12
]
)
.

Let
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
be a bounded harmonic function on
\mathbb{D}
such that
T_{\varphi}
is a normal operator on
L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{D})
. Then
T_{\varphi}
is invertible if and only if

\inf\limits_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|\widetilde{\,{\varphi}}(z)|=\inf\limits_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|\varphi(z)|>0
.

3.
Toeplitz operator on the Bergman space

In this section we discuss the invertibility of Bergman Toeplitz operators with the help of the Berezin transform of the symbol. More precisely, Theorem
3.1
gives an affirmative answer to the Problem 2.3 stated in
[
11
]
in a more general setting. Consequently, we obtain a refined characterization of invertible Bergman Toeplitz operators for some certain bounded harmonic functions.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a hyponormal operator and
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
|s|<1
. Then the following two are equivalent

(1)

T^{*}
is bounded below.

(2)

sT+T^{*}
is bounded below.

Proof.

Suppose
T^{*}
is bounded below on
\mathcal{H}
. Since
T
is hyponormal on
\mathcal{H}
,
\|Th\|\geq\|T^{*}h\|
for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
, and hence
T
is also bounded below.
Now Lemma
2.2
infers that
T
is invertible. If
f\in\mathcal{H}
and
f\neq 0
, then we choose
0\neq h\in\mathcal{H}
such that
f=Th
. For

s\in\mathbb{C}
with
|s|<1
, consider

\displaystyle\|(sT+T^{*})f\|

\displaystyle=\|(sT+T^{*})Th\|

\displaystyle=\sup\{|\langle(sT+T^{*})Th,h^{\prime}\rangle|:h^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H},\,\|h^{\prime}\|=1\}

\displaystyle\geq\left|\left\langle(sT+T^{*})Th,\frac{h}{\|h\|}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\|h\|}|\langle sT^{2}h+T^{*}Th,h\rangle|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\|h\|}|s\langle Th,T^{*}h\rangle+\|Th\|^{2}|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\|h\|}\left(\|Th\|^{2}-|s||\langle Th,T^{*}h\rangle|\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\|h\|}(\|Th\|^{2}-|s|\|Th\|\|T^{*}h\|)

(By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\|h\|}(\|Th\|^{2}-|s|\|Th\|^{2})

(By hyponormality of
T
)

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-|s|)}{\|h\|}\|Th\|^{2}.

Now
h=T^{-1}f
. Thus,
0<\|h\|\leq\|T^{-1}\|\|f\|
. Therefore

\displaystyle\|(sT+T^{*})f\|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(1-|s|)}{\|T^{-1}\|\|f\|}\|f\|^{2}=\frac{(1-|s|)}{\|T^{-1}\|}\|f\|\qquad(f\in\mathcal{H}).

Hence
sT+T^{*}
is bounded below.

Conversely, assume that
sT+T^{*}
is a bounded below operator on
\mathcal{H}
. Suppose, if possible, that
T^{*}
is not bounded below. Then there exists a sequence
(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
in
\mathcal{H}
with
\|f_{n}\|=1
for every
n\geq 1
and
\|T^{*}f_{n}\|\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Recall that an operator
A
on
\mathcal{H}
is bounded below if and only if
A^{*}
is surjective. Therefore, for every
n\geq 1
, there exists
h_{n}\in\mathcal{H}
such that

(\bar{s}T^{*}+T)h_{n}=f_{n}.

Also,

\displaystyle\|T^{*}f_{n}\|

\displaystyle=\|T^{*}(\bar{s}T^{*}+T)h_{n}\|

\displaystyle=\|\bar{s}T^{*2}h_{n}+T^{*}Th_{n}\|

\displaystyle\geq\left|\left\langle\bar{s}T^{*2}h_{n}+T^{*}Th_{n},\frac{h_{n}}{\|h_{n}\|}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\|h_{n}\|}\left|\bar{s}\langle T^{*}h_{n},Th_{n}\rangle+\|Th_{n}\|^{2}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\|h_{n}\|}(\|Th_{n}\|^{2}-|s|\|Th_{n}\|\|T^{*}h_{n}\|)

(By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\|h_{n}\|}(\|Th_{n}\|^{2}-|s|\|Th_{n}\|^{2})

(By hyponormality of
T
)

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-|s|)}{\|h_{n}\|}\|Th_{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=(1-|s|)\|h_{n}\|\left\|T\left(\frac{h_{n}}{\|h_{n}\|}\right)\right\|^{2}.

Now

1=\|f_{n}\|=\|(\bar{s}T^{*}+T)h_{n}\|\leq\|\bar{s}T^{*}+T\|\|h_{n}\|~~\text{for every}~~n\geq 1.

Thus,

\displaystyle\|T^{*}f_{n}\|\geq\frac{(1-|s|)}{\|\bar{s}T^{*}+T\|}\left\|T\left(\frac{h_{n}}{\|h_{n}\|}\right)\right\|^{2}\geq\frac{(1-|s|)}{\|\bar{s}T^{*}+T\|}\left\|T^{*}\left(\frac{h_{n}}{\|h_{n}\|}\right)\right\|^{2}\qquad(n\geq 1).

Thus it follows that

\left\|T\left(\frac{h_{n}}{\|h_{n}\|}\right)\right\|\to 0\text{ and }\left\|T^{*}\left(\frac{h_{n}}{\|h_{n}\|}\right)\right\|\to 0\text{ as }n\to\infty.

Since
sT+T^{*}
is bounded below, we can choose
M>0
such that
M\|h_{n}\|\leq\|(sT+T^{*})h_{n}\|
for every
n\geq 1
. Therefore

0<M\leq\left\|sT\left(\frac{h_{n}}{\|h_{n}\|}\right)+T^{*}\left(\frac{h_{n}}{\|h_{n}\|}\right)\right\|\to 0\text{ as }n\to\infty,

which leads to a contradiction that
sT+T^{*}
is bounded below. This establishes
(2)\implies(1)
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a hyponormal operator and
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
|s|>1
. The following two are equivalent

(1)

T
is bounded below.

(2)

sT+T^{*}
is bounded below.

Proof.

For
h\in\mathcal{H}
,

\displaystyle(|s|+1)\|Th\|

\displaystyle=|s|\|Th\|+\|Th\|

\displaystyle\geq|s|\|Th\|+\|T^{*}h\|

(By hyponormality of
T
)

\displaystyle\geq\|sTh+T^{*}h\|=\|(sT+T^{*})h\|

\displaystyle\geq|s|\|Th\|-\|T^{*}h\|

\displaystyle\geq(|s|-1)\|Th\|.

Now
|s|-1>0
as
|s|>1
. Therefore, the above chain of inequalities establishes that

(1)\iff(2)
.

Example 3.3
.

Consider the shift operator
T_{z}
on the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. It is an analytic Toeplitz operator so is hyponormal. Also,
T_{z}
is bounded below, in fact,
\|T_{z}h\|=\|h\|
for all
h\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. If
|s|>1
then it follows from Theorem
3.2
that
sT_{z}+T_{z}^{*}
is also bounded below. Note that
T_{z}
is not
surjective and hence by Lemma
2.2
,

T_{z}^{*}
is not bounded below. This shows that
T^{*}
may not be bounded below while
sT+T^{*}
is bounded below for
|s|>1
.

From the above theorems, we have the following result.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be hyponormal on
\mathcal{H}
and
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
|s|\neq 1
. Then the following are equivalent

(1)

T
is invertible on
\mathcal{H}
.

(2)

sT+T^{*}
is invertible on
\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

By virtue of Lemma
2.2
, it suffices to show that both
T
and
T^{*}
are bounded below if and only if both
sT+T^{*}
and
\bar{s}T^{*}+T
are bounded below. First we note that if
s=0
, then it is trivial as
T
is invertible if and only if
T^{*}
is invertible. Now assume that
s\neq 0
.

Case (i):
Suppose
|s|>1
.

Let both
T
and
T^{*}
be bounded below. It follows from Theorem
3.2
that
sT+T^{*}
is bounded below. Moreover,

(sT+T^{*})^{*}=\bar{s}T^{*}+T=\bar{s}\left(\frac{1}{\bar{s}}T+T^{*}\right)\text{ with }\left|\frac{1}{\bar{s}}\right|=\frac{1}{|s|}<1.

Thus, Theorem
3.1
implies that
\bar{s}T^{*}+T
is bounded below as well.

On the other hand, if both
sT+T^{*}
and
\bar{s}T^{*}+T
are bounded below, then
T
is bounded below follows from Theorem
3.2
. However, the relation
\frac{1}{\bar{s}}T+T^{*}=\frac{1}{\bar{s}}(\bar{s}T^{*}+T)
along with Theorem
3.1
yields
T^{*}
to be bounded below too.

Case (ii):
Suppose
0<|s|<1
.

Using Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
and the relation

\bar{s}T^{*}+T=\bar{s}\left(\frac{1}{\bar{s}}T+T^{*}\right)\text{ with }\left|\frac{1}{\bar{s}}\right|=\frac{1}{|s|}>1,

yields that both
T
and
T^{*}
are bounded below if and only if both
sT+T^{*}
and

\bar{s}T^{*}+T
are bounded below.

We are now in a position to present the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\varphi=cg+d\overline{g}
, where
g\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
and the constants
c,d\in\mathbb{C}
, then the following are equivalent

(1)

T_{\varphi}
is invertible on
L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{D})
.

(2)

There exists
\delta>0
such that
|\varphi(z)|\geq\delta
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, i.e.,
\inf\limits_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|\widetilde{\,{\varphi}}(z)|=\inf\limits_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|\varphi(z)|>0
.

Proof.

If either
c=0
or
d=0
, then the equivalence of (1) and (2) follows from Theorem
2.3
. Assume now that
c\neq 0
and
d\neq 0
and let
s=c/d
, then
T_{\varphi}=dT_{sg+\overline{g}}
.

Case (i):
For
|s|=1
.

Firstly,
T_{\varphi}
is normal on
L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{D})
. Indeed,

\displaystyle T_{\varphi}^{*}T_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=|d|^{2}(\bar{s}T_{\overline{g}}+T_{g})(sT_{g}+T_{\overline{g}})

\displaystyle=|d|^{2}(T_{\overline{g}}T_{g}+sT_{g}T_{g}+\bar{s}T_{\overline{g}}T_{\overline{g}}+T_{g}T_{\overline{g}})

\displaystyle=|d|^{2}(sT_{g}+T_{\overline{g}})(\bar{s}T_{\overline{g}}+T_{g})

\displaystyle=T_{\varphi}T_{\varphi}^{*}.

Hence, Theorem
2.4
yields the equivalence of
(1)
and
(2)
.

Case (ii):
When
|s|\neq 1
.

Note that for
g\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
,
T_{g}
is hyponormal because
for
f\in L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{D})
,

\|T^{*}_{g}f\|=\|T_{\overline{g}}f\|=\|P(\overline{g}f)\|\leq\|\overline{g}f\|=\|gf\|=\|T_{g}f\|.

Also, for
|s|\neq 1
we have

||s|-1||g(z)|=\left||sg(z)|-\left|\overline{g(z)}\right|\right|\leq\left|sg(z)+\overline{g(z)}\right|\leq(|s|+1)|g(z)|,\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

Thus

||s|-1|\left(\inf_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|g(z)|\right)\leq\inf_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left|sg(z)+\overline{g(z)}\right|\leq(|s|+1)\left(\inf_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|g(z)|\right).

(3.1)

Let
T_{\varphi}
be invertible on
L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{D})
. Thus,
T_{sg+\overline{g}}=sT_{g}+T^{*}_{g}
is invertible. Employing Proposition
3.4
, we have
T_{g}
is invertible on
L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{D})
. It now follows from Theorem
2.3
that
\inf\limits_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|g(z)|>0.
Hence the inequality (
3.1
) implies that

\inf_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|\varphi(z)|=|d|\inf_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left|sg(z)+\overline{g(z)}\right|>0.

Conversely, assume that
\inf\limits_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|\varphi(z)|>0
. Again, the inequality (
3.1
) and Theorem
2.3
infers that
T_{g}
is invertible on
L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{D})
. Finally, Proposition
3.4
yields that
T_{\varphi}
is invertible on
L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{D})
. This completes the proof.

We now give a concrete example to supplement our results.

Example 3.6
.

For fix
t\in\mathbb{R}
and consider the function

\varphi(z)=\left(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right)^{it}\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

Clearly,
\varphi\notin C(\overline{\mathbb{D}})
and it is known that
\varphi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
. Note that if
1+z=re^{i\theta}
and
1-z=\rho e^{i\eta}
, where
0<r,\rho<2
and

-\pi/2<\theta,\rho<\pi/2
then

\left|(1+z)^{it}\right|=\left|e^{it\log(1+z)}\right|=\left|e^{it(\log r+i\theta)}\right|=e^{-t\theta}\geq e^{-|t\theta|}\geq e^{-|t|\pi/2}>0.

Similarly,

\left|(1-z)^{it}\right|=\left|e^{it\log(1-z)}\right|=\left|e^{it(\log\rho+i\eta)}\right|=e^{-t\eta}\geq e^{-|t\eta|}\geq e^{-|t|\pi/2}>0.

Consequently, the Toeplitz operators
T_{(1+z)^{it}}
and
T_{(1-z)^{it}}
are invertible on
L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{D})
. Now we write

(1-z)^{it}\varphi(z)=(1+z)^{it}\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

Therefore,

T_{(1+z)^{it}}T_{\varphi}=T_{(1-z)^{it}}

from which it follows that
T_{\varphi}
is invertible on
L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{D})
. In this case,

\inf\limits_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|\varphi(z)|>0
.

Generalization and Optimization of SGD with LookAhead
Kangcheng Li, Yunwen Lei*

Abstract
The Lookahead optimizer [ 41 ] enhances deep learning models by employing a dual-weight update mechanism, which has been shown to improve the performance of underlying optimizers such as SGD. However, most theoretical studies focus on its convergence on training data, leaving its generalization capabilities less understood. Existing generalization analyses are often limited by restrictive assumptions, such as requiring the loss function to be globally Lipschitz continuous, and their bounds do not fully capture the relationship between optimization and generalization. In this paper, we address these issues by conducting a rigorous stability and generalization analysis of the Lookahead optimizer with minibatch SGD. We leverage on-average model stability to derive generalization bounds for both convex and strongly convex problems without the restrictive Lipschitzness assumption. Our analysis demonstrates a linear speedup with respect to the batch size in the convex setting.

Introduction

Stochastic optimization has become the method of choice to train modern machine learning models due to its efficiency and scalability
[
19
]
. A simple stochastic optimization method is the minibatch stochastic gradient descent (minibatch SGD)
[
13
,
14
,
23
,
31
]
, where a minibatch of training examples are randomly sampled to build gradient estimates with a reduced variance. Due to its simplicity, computational efficiency and strong generalization in practice
[
43
,
4
]
, minibatch SGD remains one of the most preferable algorithms. Another representative stochastic optimization method is Adam
[
19
]
, which augments SGD with coordinate-wise adaptive learning rates and momentum, often accelerating convergence and improving robustness to ill-conditioning.

To further enhance generalization performance, the Lookahead optimizer
[
41
]
was introduced as an orthogonal method. It introduces a two-timescale updating framework of two parameters: the fast weights
\mathbf{v}
and the slow weights
\mathbf{w}
. In the inner loop, starting from the slow weights
\mathbf{w}
, the fast weights are updated by applying a standard optimizer
\mathcal{A}
for
k
times and output
\mathbf{v}_{k}
; for the outer loop, the slow weights are updated towards the fast weights by
\mathbf{w}_{+}=\alpha\mathbf{v}_{k}+(1-\alpha)\mathbf{w}
, where
\alpha\in(0,1]
is an interpolation parameter. This mechanism dampens oscillations, reduces sensitivity to learning-rate schedules and synchronization periods, and improves robustness across tasks with negligible overhead, often matching or improving the accuracy of the underlying base optimizer
[
41
]
.

The empirical efficiency of the Lookahead optimizer motivates a lot of theoretical studies to understand its behavior.
However, most of existing studies focus on their convergence to minimize the training errors
[
39
,
9
,
41
]
.
As a comparison, there are far less studies on how the training behavior generalizes to testing examples, which is a concept of central interest in machine learning. To our best knowledge, the only work on the generalization analysis is
Zhou et al. 2021
, which conducted a stability analysis to argue that the Lookahead optimizer can generalize better than SGD and Adam. While these results provide a sound foundation on the use of the Lookahead mechanism, there are still some issues to be addressed. For example, their analysis hinges on the Lipschitzness condition on the loss, which is often restrictive in high-dimensional problems where gradients can be unbounded and the loss landscapes are non-Lipschitz globally. Furthermore, their stability bounds are not optimistic and cannot fully capture the connection between generalization and optimization.

This paper aims to address the above issues by improving the existing stability and generalization analysis of the Lookahead optimizer. Our main contributions can be summarized as follows.

1.

We leverage the on-average model stability to analyze the generalization behavior of the Lookahead methods for both convex and strongly convex problems. Our analysis removes the restrictive Lipschitzness assumptions of the loss functions, which can imply effective generalization bounds in the case with unbounded gradients. Furthermore, our analysis clearly shows how the interpolation parameter
\alpha
strengthens the stability, which shows a clear benefit of the Lookahead mechanism.

2.

Our stability bounds are optimistic, meaning that they depend on the empirical risk of the iterates produced by the algorithm. As the optimizer minimizes the empirical risk during the optimization process, our bounds become progressively tighter, offering a more refined and practical characterization of stability compared to existing bounds that rely on worst-case global constants.

3.

By carefully combining our stability bounds with the convergence rates, we establish optimal excess risk rates for SGD with Lookahead. We show that it achieves a rate of
\mathcal{O}(1/n)
for convex problems and a rate of
\mathcal{O}(1/(n\mu))
for
\mu
-strongly convex problems, where
n
is the sample size. Furthermore, our analysis shows a linear speedup with respect to the batch size
b
, meaning that the number of required iterations is decreased by a factor of
b
to achieve the optimal excess risk bounds.

The paper is organized as follows. We review the related work in Section
2
and introduce the problem formulation in Section
3
. We present our main theoretical results in Section
5
. The detailed proofs are provided in Section
6
. We conclude the paper in Section
7
.

Notations and Preliminaries

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a probability measure defined on a sample space
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
, where
\mathcal{X}
is an input space and
\mathcal{Y}
is an output space. Let
S=\{z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n}\}
be a sample drawn independently and identically (i.i.d.) from
\mathcal{D}
, based on which we aim to learn a model
h:\mathcal{X}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
for prediction. We assume the model is characterized by a parameter
\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
\mathcal{W}
is a parameter space. The performance of a model
\mathbf{w}
on a single data point
z
is measured by a non-negative loss function
f(\mathbf{w};z)
, from which we can define empirical risks
F_{S}(\mathbf{w})
and population risks
F(\mathbf{w})
to measure the behavior of
\mathbf{w}
on training and testing datasets, respectively

F_{S}(\mathbf{w}):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(\mathbf{w};z_{i})\quad\text{and}\quad F(\mathbf{w}):=\mathbb{E}_{z\sim\mathcal{D}}[f(\mathbf{w};z)],

where
\mathbb{E}_{z}[\cdot]
means the expectation w.r.t.
z
.

We often apply a randomized optimizer
A
to approximately minimize
F_{S}
to train a model. We use
A(S)
to denote the model produced by applying
A
to
S
, and are interested in its relative performance w.r.t. the best model
\mathbf{w}^{*}=\argmin_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}}F(\mathbf{w})
, which is quantified by the excess risk defined by
\mathbb{E}[F(A(S))-F(\mathbf{w}^{*})]
. A powerful method to study the excess risk is to decompose it into two components
[
5
]
:

\mathbb{E}[F(A(S))-F(\mathbf{w}^{*})]=\underbrace{\mathbb{E}[F(A(S))-F_{S}(A(S))]}_{\text{Generalization Error}}+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}[F_{S}(A(S))-F_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{*})]}_{\text{Optimization Error}},

(3.1)

where the expectation is taken over the randomness of the training set
S
and any randomness within the algorithm itself. Here we use the identity
\mathbb{E}[F_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{*})]=F(\mathbf{w}^{*})
.
We refer to
\mathbb{E}[F(A(S))-F_{S}(A(S))]
as the generalization gap, which shows the cost we suffer when we generalize the behavior from training to testing. A small generalization gap indicates that the model does not overfit the training data and its performance is likely to be representative of its true performance. We refer to
\mathbb{E}[F_{S}(A(S))-F_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{*})]
as the optimization error, which measures the gap between the estimated model and the true optimal model on empirical risk.

We introduce the following necessary definitions for our analysis. Let
\|\cdot\|_{2}
denote the Euclidean norm.

Definition 1
.

Let
g:\mathcal{W}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
,
G,L>0
and
\mu\geq 0
. We denote the gradient of
g
by
\nabla g
.

1.

A function
g(\mathbf{w})
is
\mu
-strongly convex for some
\mu>0
if it satisfies:

g(\mathbf{w}_{1})\geq g(\mathbf{w}_{2})+\langle\nabla g(\mathbf{w}_{2}),\mathbf{w}_{1}-\mathbf{w}_{2}\rangle+\frac{\mu}{2}\|\mathbf{w}_{1}-\mathbf{w}_{2}\|_{2}^{2},\quad\forall\mathbf{w}_{1},\mathbf{w}_{2}\in\mathcal{W}.

A function
g(\mathbf{w})
is convex if it is
\mu
-strongly convex with
\mu=0
.

2.

A function
g(\mathbf{w})
is
G
-Lipschitz continuous if the function value is bounded in its change:

|g(\mathbf{w}_{1})-g(\mathbf{w}_{2})|\leq G\|\mathbf{w}_{1}-\mathbf{w}_{2}\|_{2},\quad\forall\mathbf{w}_{1},\mathbf{w}_{2}\in\mathcal{W}.

3.

A differentiable function
g(\mathbf{w})
is
L
-smooth if its gradient is Lipschitz continuous with the constant
L
:

\|\nabla g(\mathbf{w}_{1})-\nabla g(\mathbf{w}_{2})\|_{2}\leq L\|\mathbf{w}_{1}-\mathbf{w}_{2}\|_{2},\quad\forall\mathbf{w}_{1},\mathbf{w}_{2}\in\mathcal{W}.

4
Algorithmic Stability

To control the generalization gap, we analyze the stability of our learning algorithm. We say an algorithm is on-average stable if its output model does not change significantly when a single data point in the training set is modified. Let
A
be a learning algorithm that takes a dataset
S
and outputs a model
A(S)
. We denote
S\sim S^{\prime}
if
S
and
S^{\prime}
differ by at most one data point. Specifically, we let
S^{(i)}
be a dataset identical to
S
except that the
i
-th data
z_{i}
is replaced with a new point
z_{i}^{\prime}
, drawn from the same distribution
\mathcal{D}
. That is,
S^{(i)}=\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{i-1},z_{i}^{\prime},z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{n}\}
.

Definition 2

(Uniform Stability)
.

An algorithm
A
has uniform stability
\epsilon
if

\sup_{z\in\mathcal{Z}}\sup_{S\sim S^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}\left[|f(A(S);z)-f(A(S^{\prime});z)|\right]\leq\epsilon.

Definition 3

(On-Average Model Stability
[
21
]
)
.

We say a randomized optimizer
A
is
\ell_{1}
on-average model
\epsilon
-stable if

\mathbb{E}_{S,S^{\prime},A}\Big[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|A(S)-A(S^{(i)})\|_{2}\Big]\leq\epsilon.

We say
A
is
\ell_{2}
on-average model
\epsilon
-stable if

\mathbb{E}_{S,S^{\prime},A}\Big[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|A(S)-A(S^{(i)})\|_{2}^{2}\Big]\leq\epsilon^{2}.

The following lemma provides a connection between the generalization gap and on-average model stability.

Lemma 1

(
[
21
]
)
.

Let
S,S^{\prime}
and
S^{(i)}
be constructed as in Definition 2, and let
\gamma>0
.

(a)

Suppose for any
z
, the function
\mathbf{w}\mapsto f(\mathbf{w};z)
is convex. If
A
is
\ell_{1}
on-average model
\epsilon
-stable and
\sup_{z}\|\nabla f(A(S);z)\|_{2}\leq G
for any
S
, then
\lvert\mathbb{E}_{S,A}[F_{S}(A(S))-F(A(S))]\rvert\leq G\epsilon
.

(b)

Suppose for any
z
, the function
\mathbf{w}\mapsto f(\mathbf{w};z)
is nonnegative and
L
-smooth. If
A
is
\ell_{2}
on-average model
\epsilon
-stable, then the following inequality holds

\mathbb{E}_{S,A}[F(A(S))-F_{S}(A(S))]\leq\frac{L}{\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{S,A}[F_{S}(A(S))]+\frac{L+\gamma}{2n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{S,S^{\prime},A}[\|A(S^{(i)})-A(S)\|_{2}^{2}].

5
Generalization Analysis of Lookahead Algorithm

In this section, we discuss the stability performance of Lookahead on convex and strongly convex problems.
While previous work has shown that Lookahead achieves lower excess risk error compared to its vanilla inner optimizer when choosing
\mathcal{A}
as SGD
[
44
]
, existing analysis of its generalization and optimization error suffer from two key limitations. First, they hinge on a restrictive Lipschitzness condition on the loss function. Second, they cannot imply optimistic rates to show the benefit of low-noise condition to get fast rates. In the following sections, we will analyze the stability bound of Lookahead via the
\ell_{2}
on-average model stability. This approach notably allows us to derive generalization bounds for Lookahead without requiring the Lipschitzness condition
[
22
]
. Furthermore, by carefully selecting the algorithm’s hyperparameters, we establish optimal excess risk bounds.

5.1
Convex Case

We first investigate stability bounds of Lookahead under convex condition, where Eq. (
5.1
) considers the
\ell_{1}
on-average stability and Eq. (
5.2
) considers the
\ell_{2}
on-average stability. The proof will be given in Section
6.1
.

Theorem 2

(Stability Bound of Lookahead: Convex Case)
.

Suppose the map
\mathbf{w}\mapsto f(\mathbf{w};z)
is convex, nonnegative and
L
-smooth for all
z\in\mathcal{Z}
. Let
\left\{\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}\right\}
and
\{\mathbf{w}_{t}\}
,
\{\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}^{(i)}\}
and
\{\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i)}\}
be produced based on
S
and
S^{(i)}
respectively with
\eta_{\tau,t}\leq\frac{1}{L}
. We have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum^{n}_{i=1}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}^{(i)}_{t+1}\|_{2}\big]\leq\alpha\sum_{h=1}^{t+1}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{2\eta_{j,h}\sqrt{2L\mathbb{E}\left[F_{S}\left(\mathbf{v}_{j,h}\right)\right]}}{n}

(5.1)

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}^{(i)}_{t+1}\|^{2}_{2}\big]\leq\left(\frac{16\alpha^{2}L}{nb}+\frac{16\alpha^{2}L(t+1)k}{n^{2}}\right)\sum_{h=1}^{t+1}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\eta_{j,h}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[F_{S}\left(\mathbf{v}_{j,h}\right)\right].

(5.2)

Remark 1

(Comparison with existing stability bounds for Lookahead)
.

For
L
-smooth,
G
-Lipschitz and convex problems, a similar
\ell_{1}
-stability bound was derived in
[
44
]
as shown below

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}^{(i)}_{t+1}\|_{2}\big]\leq\frac{2\alpha\eta GkT}{n}.

This bound grows linearly with
kT
, is independent of the mini-batch size
b
, and involves the global Lipschitz constant
G
.
Our analysis removes the global
G
-Lipschitz requirement and thus avoids the
G
factor. A notable feature of our bound is its dependence on the empirical risk,
\mathbb{E}[F_{S}(\mathbf{v}_{j,h})]
, rather than the global Lipschitz constant
G
in
[
44
]
. Since the objective of the inner-loop optimizer is precisely to minimize
F_{S}
, we expect this term to decrease as training progresses. Consequently, our stability bounds become progressively tighter throughout the optimization process
[
20
,
21
]
. Furthermore, the bound in Eq. (
5.2
) provides clear intuition about the role of Lookahead’s hyperparameters:

•

Batch Size (
b
):
The term
1/nb
shows that increasing the minibatch size improves stability. As a comparison, the stability analysis in
[
44
]
does not show the effect of the batch size since their stability bound is independent of
b
.

•

Inner Loop Iteration Number (
k
):
The bound increases with
k
, suggesting that running the inner loop for too many steps can degrade stability, likely due to the fast weights overfitting to the training set
S
.

•

Outer Loop Step Size (
\alpha
):
Stability is proportional to
\alpha
. A smaller
\alpha
dampens the influence of the potentially unstable fast weights, leading to a more stable trajectory for the slow weights. This shows a clear advantage of the Lookahead mechanism in improving the stability and generalization.

Theorem 3

(Excess Risk Bound of Lookahead: Convex Case)
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
2
hold and
R=Tk
. Then for
\bar{\mathbf{v}}_{R}=\frac{1}{Tk}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{\tau=0}^{k-1}\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}
and
\gamma>0
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[F(\overline{\mathbf{v}}_{R})\right]-F(\mathbf{w}^{*})\lesssim\frac{L\eta F(\mathbf{w}^{*})}{b}+\frac{1}{\alpha\eta R}+\frac{F(\mathbf{w}^{*})+L\eta/b+1/(\alpha\eta R)}{\gamma}\\
+L(L+\gamma)\alpha^{2}\eta^{2}\left(\frac{1}{nb}+\frac{R}{n^{2}}\right)\left(RF(\mathbf{w}^{*})+\frac{RL\eta}{b}+\frac{1}{\alpha\eta}\right).

(5.3)

Since there are terms directly proportional to
F(\mathbf{w}^{*})
, the excess risk bound will be tighter when the optimal risk
F(\mathbf{w}^{*})
is small, which is common in many machine learning problems where a model can fit the data well. Excess risk bounds with this feature are called optimistic bounds
[

]
. The terms involving
F(\mathbf{w}^{*})
are directly related to gradient noise, as the variance of stochastic gradients can often be bounded by the function’s value at the optimum.

Remark 2

(Comparison with Minibatch SGD)
.

The excess risk bound for Lookahead in Theorem
3
shares a fundamental structure with the bound for Minibatch SGD as in
[
22
]
. Both are optimistic bounds that explicitly depend on the optimal risk. This similarity is expected, as both analyses aim to control generalization gap by plugging stability bounds into Lemma 3.1, then adding optimization error terms. Although the structure is similar, the specific coefficients and dependencies on parameters such as
\alpha
and the structure of the variance term differ due to the unique dynamics of the Lookahead optimizer compared to standard SGD.

We now develop an explicit excess risk bound for Lookahead by choosing step sizes and number of iterations. The proof is given in Sec
6.2
.

Corollary 4
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
3
hold.

1.

If
F(\mathbf{w}^{*})\geq 1/n
, we can take
\eta=\frac{b}{\sqrt{nF(\mathbf{w}^{*})}}
,
R\asymp\frac{n}{b}
,
\gamma=\sqrt{nF(\mathbf{w}^{*})}\geq 1
, and
b\leq\sqrt{nF(\mathbf{w}^{*})}/(2L)
to derive
\mathbb{E}[F(\bar{\mathbf{v}}_{R})]-F(\mathbf{w}^{*})\lesssim\frac{LF(\mathbf{w}^{*})^{1/2}}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{L^{2}}{n}
.

2.

If
F(\mathbf{w}^{*})<1/n
, we can take
\eta=\frac{1}{2L}
,
R\asymp n
, and
\gamma=1
to derive
\mathbb{E}[F(\bar{\mathbf{v}}_{R})]-F(\mathbf{w}^{*})\lesssim\frac{L}{n}+F(\mathbf{w}^{*})
.

Remark 3
.

Corollary
4
distinguishes between two key regimes based on the magnitude of the optimal risk
F(\mathbf{w}^{*})
relative to the sample size
n
.

1.

F(\mathbf{w}^{*})\geq 1/n
: Our analysis shows that the algorithm achieves an excess risk bound of
O(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}})
. Crucially, the number of required iterations
R
is on the order of
n/b
, demonstrating a linear speedup
[
12
]
. This means that by increasing the minibatch size b, one can use a proportionally larger learning rate
\eta
and achieve the same error bound with fewer iterations. This acceleration is a direct benefit of variance reduction from larger batch sizes.

2.

F(\mathbf{w}^{*})<1/n
: Now the required number of iterations
R
scales with
n
, irrespective of the batch size
b
. In this case, the linear speedup vanishes. The optimal learning rate becomes constant, and increasing the batch size does not reduce the number of iterations needed to reach the desired error threshold. This suggests a small stochastic gradient noise, which means variance is no longer the main limitation of the learning process.

Remark 4

(Comparison with Existing Excess Risk Bounds with Lookahead)
.

The work
[
44
]
gave the following excess risk bound for Lookahead under convexity and
G
-Lipschitz continuity assumption

\epsilon_{\text{opt}}+\epsilon_{\text{gen}}\leq\frac{1}{2\alpha\eta kT}\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{w}_{0}-\mathbf{w}^{*}\|^{2}]+\frac{\eta G^{2}}{2}+\frac{\alpha\eta G^{2}kT}{n}.

By setting
\eta\asymp 1/\sqrt{n}
and choosing
\alpha Tk\asymp n
, all three terms can be made to be of the order
O(1/\sqrt{n})
. This leads to an optimized excess risk bound of order
G^{2}/\sqrt{n}
, which is standard for stochastic convex optimization under a Lipschitz assumption. However, it is not adaptive and can be suboptimal in many practical scenarios. In the case of
F(\mathbf{w}^{*})\geq 1/n
, our bound is of order
\frac{L\sqrt{F(\mathbf{w}^{*})}}{\sqrt{n}}
. As the optimal risk
F(\mathbf{w}^{*})
decreases, our bound becomes tighter. For problems where
L\sqrt{F(\mathbf{w}^{*})}\ll G^{2}
, our bound is substantially sharper than the generic
O(G^{2}/\sqrt{n})
rate. In the case of
F(\mathbf{w}^{*})<1/n
, our analysis reveals a much faster convergence rate of
\lesssim\frac{L}{n}
. This is a linear convergence rate with respect to the sample size
n
. Achieving an
O(1/n)
rate is a major acceleration compared to the standard
O(1/\sqrt{n})
rate. It shows that Lookahead can effectively leverage low-noise conditions to converge significantly faster, a behavior that the existing bound fails to capture. Furthermore, our analysis shows a linear speedup on the batch size, while the discussions in
[
44
]
do not show the benefit of considering minibatch in both generalization and optimization.

5.2
Strongly Convex Case

We now consider strongly convex problems. The following theorem provides stability bounds for Lookahead. The proof is given in Sec
6.3
.

Theorem 5

(Stability Bound of Lookahead: Strongly Convex Case)
.

Suppose the map
\mathbf{w}\mapsto f(\mathbf{w};z)
is
\mu
-strongly convex, nonnegative and
L
-smooth for all
z\in\mathcal{Z}
. Let
\left\{\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}\right\}
and
\{\mathbf{w}_{t}\}
,
\{\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}^{(i)}\}
and
\{\mathbf{w}_{t}^{(i)}\}
be produced based on
S
and
S^{(i)}
respectively with
\frac{2\ln 2}{k\mu}\leq\eta_{\tau,t}\leq\frac{1}{L}
. We have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum^{n}_{i=1}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}^{(i)}_{t+1}\|_{2}\big]\leq\frac{2\alpha\sqrt{2L}}{n}\sum_{t^{\prime}=1}^{t+1}(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})^{t+1-t^{\prime}}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\eta_{j,t^{\prime}}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[F_{S}\left(\mathbf{v}_{j,t^{\prime}}\right)\right]}\prod_{j^{\prime}=j+1}^{k-1}\left(1-\frac{\mu\eta_{j^{\prime},t^{\prime}}}{2}\right)

(5.4)

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}^{(i)}_{t+1}\|_{2}^{2}\big]\leq\sum_{t^{\prime}=1}^{t+1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\Big(\frac{16\alpha^{2}\eta_{j,t^{\prime}}^{2}}{nb}+\frac{32\left(t+1\right)\alpha^{2}\eta_{j,t^{\prime}}}{n^{2}\mu}\Big)\mathbb{E}\left[F_{S}\left(\mathbf{v}_{j,t^{\prime}}\right)\right]\prod_{j^{\prime}=j+1}^{k-1}\Big(1-\frac{\mu\eta_{j^{\prime},t^{\prime}}}{2}\Big)^{2}.

(5.5)

Eq. (
5.4
) provides an
\ell_{1}
-on-average stability bound. A key feature of this bound is its dependence on the empirical risk,
\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[F_{S}(\mathbf{v}_{j,t^{\prime}})]}
. This indicates that the stability of the Lookahead algorithm improves as it finds iterates with smaller empirical risks. Eq. (
5.5
) provides an
\ell_{2}
-on-average stability bound. This bound explicitly shows the benefit of minibatching. The term
\frac{16\alpha^{2}\eta_{j,t^{\prime}}}{nb}
demonstrates that increasing the batch size
b
directly improves the stability bound by reducing the variance introduced by the stochastic gradients. This is a crucial property for large-scale learning, confirming that larger batches contribute to a more stable training process for the Lookahead algorithm.

Theorem 6

(Excess Risk Bound of Lookahead: Strongly Convex Case)
.

Let assumptions in Theorem
5
hold and let
\eta=\frac{b\mu}{2L^{2}(b+1)}
,

k=\frac{2L}{\alpha\mu}
and
T\asymp\log(\mu n)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[F(\mathbf{w}_{T})]-F(\mathbf{w}^{*})\lesssim\frac{1}{n\mu}+\big(\frac{1}{nL}+1\big)\mathbb{E}[F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})]+\big(\frac{1}{n^{2}}+\frac{L}{n}\big)\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{w}_{0}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}].

(5.6)

Remark 5

(Comparison with Existing Excess Risk Bound with Lookahead)
.

Compared with the existing Lookahead bound in the work
[
44
]
, which yields a sum of terms of order
O(1/(\lambda^{2}((t+1)k)^{2\alpha}))+O(G/(n\lambda))
and therefore requires
tk
to scale polynomially with
n
to reach the
O(1/n)
regime, our Theorem 6 delivers a fast-rate excess risk of order
1/(n\mu)
with only
T\asymp\log(\mu n)
iterations. Moreover, our bound is adaptive: it tightens with the data through
(1/(nL)+1)\mathbb{E}[F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})]
and through
(1/n^{2}+L/n)\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{w}_{0}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}]
, becoming much smaller under interpolation, which is not captured by the existing result. Finally, the stepsize
\eta
scales with the minibatch
b
, implying linear speedup in
b
, while prior analyses do not show such minibatch gains.

6.1
Proof of Theorem

Our proof of Theorem

shows the self-bounding property for nonnegative and smooth functions, meaning that the norm of gradients can be bounded by function values. Lemma

establishes the co-coercivity of smooth and convex functions, as well as the non-expansiveness of the gradient operator

Lemma 7

(Self-Bounding Property
[
32
]
)
.

Assume for all
z
, the function
\mathbf{w}\mapsto f(\mathbf{w};z)
is nonnegative and
L
-smooth. Then

\|\nabla f\left(\mathbf{w};z\right)\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2Lf\left(\mathbf{w};z\right).

Lemma 8

(
[
18
]
)
.

Assume for all
z\in Z
, the function
\mathbf{w}\mapsto f(\mathbf{w};z)
is convex and
L
-smooth. Then for
\eta\leq 2/L
we have

\|\left(\mathbf{w}-\eta\nabla f\left(\mathbf{w};z\right)\right)-\left(\mathbf{w}^{\prime}-\eta\nabla f\left(\mathbf{w}^{\prime};z\right)\right)\|_{2}\leq\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\|_{2}.

Furthermore, if
\mathbf{w}\mapsto f(\mathbf{w};z)
is
\mu
-strongly convex and

then

\displaystyle\|\left(\mathbf{w}-\eta\nabla f\left(\mathbf{w};z\right)\right)-\left(\mathbf{w}^{\prime}-\eta\nabla f\left(\mathbf{w}^{\prime};z\right)\right)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\eta\mu/2\right)\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\|_{2},

\displaystyle\|\left(\mathbf{w}-\eta\nabla f\left(\mathbf{w};z\right)\right)-\left(\mathbf{w}^{\prime}-\eta\nabla f\left(\mathbf{w}^{\prime};z\right)\right)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\eta\mu\right)\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\|_{2}^{2}.

We can now prove Theorem
2
. For simplicity, we define
J_{\tau,t}=\{i_{\tau,t}^{(1)},\ldots,i_{\tau,t}^{(b)}\}

j

\mathcal{B}_{\tau,t}=\{z_{i_{\tau,t}^{(1)}},\ldots,z_{i_{\tau,t}^{(b)}}\}
.

Proof.

To begin with, define

A^{(m)}_{\tau,t}=\lvert\{j:i^{(j)}_{\tau,t}=m\}\rvert,

A^{(m)}_{\tau,t}
represents the number of indices equal to
m

t

\tau

\begin{split}\mathbf{w}_{t+1}&=\left(1-\alpha\right)\mathbf{w}_{t}+\alpha\mathbf{v}_{k,t+1}\\

&-\frac{A^{(i)}_{k,t+1}\eta_{k-1,t+1}}{b}\nabla f(\mathbf{v}^{(i)}_{k-1,t+1};z^{\prime}_{i})\Big),\end{split}

(6.1)

from which we know

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}^{(i)}_{t+1}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\big(1-\alpha\big)\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}^{(i)}_{t}\|_{2}+\alpha\|\mathbf{v}_{k,t+1}-\mathbf{v}^{(i)}_{k,t+1}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\alpha\right)\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}^{(i)}_{t}\|_{2}+\alpha\big\|\mathbf{v}_{k-1,t+1}-\frac{\eta_{k-1,t+1}}{b}\sum_{m:m\neq i}^{n}A^{(m)}_{k-1,t+1}\nabla f(\mathbf{v}_{k-1,t+1};z_{m})

\displaystyle-\frac{A^{(i)}_{k-1,t+1}\eta_{k-1,t+1}}{b}\nabla f\left(\mathbf{v}_{k-1,t+1};z_{i}\right)-\mathbf{v}^{(i)}_{k-1,t+1}+\frac{\eta_{k-1,t+1}}{b}\sum_{m:m\neq i}^{n}A^{(m)}_{k-1,t+1}\nabla f(\mathbf{v}^{(i)}_{k-1,t+1};z_{m})

\displaystyle+\frac{A^{(i)}_{k-1,t+1}\eta_{k-1,t+1}}{b}\nabla f(\mathbf{v}^{(i)}_{k-1,t+1};z^{\prime}_{i})\big\|_{2}.

Define

f

L
-smooth and
\sum_{m:m\neq i}^{n}A^{(m)}_{k-1,t+1}\leq b

\mathbf{v}\mapsto\frac{1}{b}\sum_{m:m\neq i}^{n}A^{(m)}_{k-1,t+1}f\left(\mathbf{v};z_{m}\right)

8

\begin{split}&\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}^{(i)}_{t+1}\|_{2}\\
&\leq(1-\alpha)\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}^{(i)}_{t}\|_{2}+\frac{\alpha A^{(i)}_{k-1,t+1}\eta_{k-1,t+1}}{b}\|\nabla f\left(\mathbf{v}_{k-1,t+1};z_{i}\right)-\nabla f(\mathbf{v}^{(i)}_{k-1,t+1};z^{\prime}_{i})\big\|_{2}\\
&+\alpha\big\|\mathbf{v}_{k-1,t+1}-\frac{\eta_{k-1,t+1}}{b}\sum_{m:m\neq i}^{n}A^{(m)}_{k-1,t+1}\nabla f(\mathbf{v}_{k-1,t+1};z_{m})-\big(\mathbf{v}^{(i)}_{k-1,t+1}-\frac{\eta_{k-1,t+1}}{b}\sum_{m:m\neq i}^{n}A^{(m)}_{k-1,t+1}\nabla f(\mathbf{v}^{(i)}_{k-1,t+1};z_{m})\big)\big\|_{2}\\

Note the above inequality actually shows a recurrent relationship on

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}^{(i)}_{t+1}\|_{2}

\mathbf{v}_{0,t+1}=\mathbf{w}_{t}

\begin{split}\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}^{(i)}_{t+1}\|_{2}&\leq\frac{\alpha}{b}\sum_{h=1}^{t+1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\eta_{j,h}A^{(i)}_{j,h}\mathfrak{C}^{(i)}_{j,h}.\end{split}

(6.3)

By definition of

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}^{(i)}_{t+1}\|_{2}\big]

(6.7)

where we used (

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\sum_{h=1}^{t+1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{2\eta_{j,h}}{n}\sqrt{\frac{2L}{n}\sum^{n}_{i=1}\mathbb{E}\left[f(\mathbf{v}_{j,h};z_{i})\right]}

(6.8)

This established the stated

(6.14)

where the second inequality holds by applying Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and the third inequality follows from self-bounding property.

We first introduce the optimization error bound for Lookahead in the convex case.

Lemma 9

(Optimization Errors of Lookahead: Convex Case)

Suppose the assumptions in Theorem

hold, and further assume that
\eta<\frac{b}{L(b+1)}
, then the following inequality holds

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[F_{S}\left(\overline{\mathbf{v}}_{R}\right)-F_{S}\left(\mathbf{w}^{*}\right)\right]\leq\frac{b\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathbf{w}_{0}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\right]}{2\alpha\eta kT\big(b-L\eta(b+1)\big)}+\frac{L\eta\mathbb{E}\big[F_{S}\big(\mathbf{w}_{S}\big)\big]}{b-L\eta(b+1)},

(6.15)

where
\bar{\mathbf{v}}_{R}=\frac{1}{Tk}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{\tau=0}^{k-1}\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}
.

We need the following property for the
L
-smooth and convex functions for the proof.

Lemma 10

(

)
.

For any
L

, and any

\displaystyle\|\nabla F\left(x\right)-\nabla F\left(y\right)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq L\langle\nabla F\left(x\right)-\nabla F\left(y\right),x-y\rangle,

\displaystyle\|\nabla F\left(x\right)-\nabla F\left(y\right)\|^{2}

Proof of Lemma
9
.

Since
F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})\leq F_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{*})
, an upper bound for
F_{S}(\overline{\mathbf{v}}_{R})-F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})
is also an upper bound for
F_{S}(\overline{\mathbf{v}}_{R})-F_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{*})
. For the proof below, we assume that the learning rate is constant, that is,
\eta_{\tau,t}=\eta
. We denote
B_{k,t}=\{z_{i_{k,t}^{(1)}},\ldots,z_{i_{k,t}^{(b)}}\}
and
f\big(\mathbf{v};B_{k,t}\big)=\frac{1}{b}\sum_{j=1}^{b}f(\mathbf{v};z_{i_{k,t}^{(j)}})
. We can hence reformulate the minibatch SGD update as

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{\tau+1,t}=\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}-\eta\nabla f\big(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t};B_{\tau,t}\big).

We first notice that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla f\big(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t};B_{\tau,t}\big)\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla f\big(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t};B_{\tau,t}\big)-\nabla F_{S}\left(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}\right)\|^{2}\big]+\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla F_{S}\left(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}\right)\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{b}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla f\big(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t};z_{i_{\tau,t}^{(1)}}\big)-\nabla F_{S}\left(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}\right)\|^{2}\big]+\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla F_{S}\left(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}\right)\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla f\big(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t};z_{i_{\tau,t}^{(1)}}\big)\|^{2}\big]}{b}-\frac{\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla F_{S}\left(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}\right)\|^{2}\big]}{b}+\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla F_{S}\left(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}\right)\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2L\mathbb{E}\big[F_{S}(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t})\big]}{b}+\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla F_{S}\left(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}\right)\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2L\mathbb{E}\big[F_{S}(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t})\big]}{b}+2L\mathbb{E}[F_{S}(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t})-F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})],

(6.16)

where the last inequality follows from Lemma
10
, where we set
y=\mathbf{w}_{S}
. We then analyze the single step in the inner-loop,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau+1,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}-\eta\nabla f\big(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t};B_{\tau,t}\big)-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}-2\eta\langle\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S},{\nabla f\big(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t};B_{\tau,t}\big)}\rangle+\eta^{2}\|\nabla f\big(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t};B_{\tau,t}\big)\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\right]-2\eta\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S},\nabla F_{S}\left(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}\right)\right\rangle\right]+\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla f\big(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t};B_{\tau,t}\big)\|^{2}\right].

(6.17)

By convexity, we have
\left\langle\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S},\nabla F_{S}\left(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}\right)\right\rangle\geq F_{S}\left(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}\right)-F_{S}\left(\mathbf{w}_{S}\right)
. Substituting this and the above result, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau+1,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\right]-2\eta\mathbb{E}\left[F_{S}\left(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}\right)-F_{S}\left(\mathbf{w}_{S}\right)\right]+\eta^{2}\Big(\frac{2L\mathbb{E}\big[F_{S}(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t})\big]}{b}+2L\mathbb{E}\big[F_{S}(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t})-F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})\big]\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\right]-\big(2\eta-\frac{2L\eta^{2}(b+1)}{b}\big)\mathbb{E}\left[F_{S}\left(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}\right)-F_{S}\left(\mathbf{w}_{S}\right)\right]+\frac{2L\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}\big[F_{S}\left(\mathbf{w}_{S}\right)\big]}{b}.

It then follows that

2\eta\Big(1-\frac{L\eta(b+1)}{b}\Big)\mathbb{E}\left[F_{S}\left(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}\right)-F_{S}\left(\mathbf{w}_{S}\right)\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}-\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau+1,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\right]+\frac{2L\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}\big[F_{S}\left(\mathbf{w}_{S}\right)\big]}{b}.

Recall the assumption of
\eta\leq\frac{b}{L(b+1)}
, we can divide by
2\eta(1-\frac{L\eta(b+1)}{b})
and get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[F_{S}\left(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}\right)-F_{S}\left(\mathbf{w}_{S}\right)\right]\leq\frac{b}{2\eta\big(b-L\eta(b+1)\big)}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}-\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau+1,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\right]+\frac{L\eta\mathbb{E}\big[F_{S}\big(\mathbf{w}_{S}\big)\big]}{b-L\eta(b+1)}.

We take an average of the above inequality from
\tau=0
to
k-1
, and get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\sum_{\tau=0}^{k-1}\mathbb{E}\left[F_{S}\left(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}\right)-F_{S}\left(\mathbf{w}_{S}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{b}{2\eta k\big(b-L\eta(b+1)\big)}\sum_{\tau=0}^{k-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}-\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau+1,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\right]+\frac{L\eta\mathbb{E}\big[F_{S}\big(\mathbf{w}_{S}\big)\big]}{b-L\eta(b+1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{b}{2\eta k\big(b-L\eta(b+1)\big)}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathbf{v}_{0,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}-\|\mathbf{v}_{k,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\right]+\frac{L\eta\mathbb{E}\big[F_{S}\big(\mathbf{w}_{S}\big)\big]}{b-L\eta(b+1)}.

(6.18)

By the slow updating rule of Lookahead, we know
(1-\alpha)(\mathbf{w}_{t-1}-\mathbf{w}^{*})=(\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}^{*})-\alpha(\mathbf{v}_{k,t}-\mathbf{w}^{*})
and get

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{v}_{0,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}-\|\mathbf{v}_{k,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}=\|\mathbf{w}_{t-1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}-\|\mathbf{v}_{k,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\|\mathbf{w}_{t-1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}-\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\right).

Substituting this into (
6.18
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\sum_{\tau=0}^{k-1}\mathbb{E}\left[F_{S}\left(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}\right)-F_{S}\left(\mathbf{w}_{S}\right)\right]\leq\frac{b}{2\alpha\eta k\big(b-L\eta(b+1)\big)}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathbf{w}_{t-1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}-\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\right]+\frac{L\eta\mathbb{E}\big[F_{S}\big(\mathbf{w}_{S}\big)\big]}{b-L\eta(b+1)}.

We take an average of the above inequality and get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{kT}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{\tau=0}^{k-1}\mathbb{E}\left[F_{S}\left(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}\right)-F_{S}\left(\mathbf{w}_{S}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{b}{2\alpha\eta kT\big(b-L\eta(b+1)\big)}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathbf{w}_{t-1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}-\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\right]+\frac{L\eta\mathbb{E}\big[F_{S}\big(\mathbf{w}_{S}\big)\big]}{b-L\eta(b+1)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{b\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathbf{w}_{0}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\right]}{2\alpha\eta kT\big(b-L\eta(b+1)\big)}+\frac{L\eta\mathbb{E}\big[F_{S}\big(\mathbf{w}_{S}\big)\big]}{b-L\eta(b+1)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{b\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathbf{w}_{0}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\right]}{2\alpha\eta kT\big(b-L\eta(b+1)\big)}+\frac{L\eta\mathbb{E}\big[F_{S}\big(\mathbf{w}^{*}\big)\big]}{b-L\eta(b+1)}.

(6.19)

We complete the proof by applying the Jensen’s inequality.
∎

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\alpha^{2}}{nb}\sum_{t^{\prime}=1}^{t+1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\eta_{j,t^{\prime}}^{2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\big(\mathfrak{C}^{(i)}_{j,t^{\prime}}\big)^{2}\Big]\prod_{j^{\prime}=j+1}^{k-1}\Big(1-\frac{\mu\eta_{j^{\prime},t^{\prime}}}{2}\Big)^{2}+\frac{2\alpha^{2}}{n^{2}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\sum_{t^{\prime}=1}^{t+1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\eta_{j,t^{\prime}}\mathfrak{C}^{(i)}_{j,t^{\prime}}\prod_{j^{\prime}=j+1}^{k-1}\Big(1-\frac{\mu\eta_{j^{\prime},t^{\prime}}}{2}\Big)\Big)^{2}\Big]

(6.26)

where we used (
6.1
) and
\mathbb{E}_{B_{j,t^{\prime}}}\big[\big(A^{(i)}_{j,t^{\prime}}-\frac{b}{n}\big)^{2}\big]\leq\frac{b}{n}
.
For the second term, we apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{t^{\prime}=1}^{t+1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\eta_{j,t^{\prime}}\mathfrak{C}^{(i)}_{j,t^{\prime}}\prod_{j^{\prime}=j+1}^{k-1}\Big(1-\frac{\mu\eta_{j^{\prime},t^{\prime}}}{2}\Big)\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{t^{\prime}=1}^{t+1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\eta_{j,t^{\prime}}\big(\mathfrak{C}^{(i)}_{j,t^{\prime}}\big)^{2}\prod_{j^{\prime}=j+1}^{k-1}\Big(1-\frac{\mu\eta_{j^{\prime},t^{\prime}}}{2}\Big)\Big)\Big(\sum_{t^{\prime}=1}^{t+1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\eta_{j,t^{\prime}}\prod_{j^{\prime}=j+1}^{k-1}\Big(1-\frac{\mu\eta_{j^{\prime},t^{\prime}}}{2}\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2(t+1)}{\mu}\Big(\sum_{t^{\prime}=1}^{t+1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\eta_{j,t^{\prime}}\big(\mathfrak{C}^{(i)}_{j,t^{\prime}}\big)^{2}\prod_{j^{\prime}=j+1}^{k-1}\Big(1-\frac{\mu\eta_{j^{\prime},t^{\prime}}}{2}\Big)\Big),

(6.27)

where the following result is used in the last inequality

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\eta_{j,t^{\prime}}\prod_{j^{\prime}=j+1}^{k-1}\left(1-\frac{\mu\eta_{j^{\prime},t^{\prime}}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\mu}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\left(1-\left(1-\frac{\mu\eta_{j,t^{\prime}}}{2}\right)\right)\prod_{j^{\prime}=j+1}^{k-1}\left(1-\frac{\mu\eta_{j^{\prime},t^{\prime}}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\mu}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\Big(\prod_{j^{\prime}=j+1}^{k-1}\left(1-\frac{\mu\eta_{j^{\prime},t^{\prime}}}{2}\right)-\prod_{j^{\prime}=j}^{k-1}\left(1-\frac{\mu\eta_{j^{\prime},t^{\prime}}}{2}\right)\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\mu}\Big(1-\prod_{j^{\prime}=0}^{k-1}\left(1-\frac{\mu\eta_{j^{\prime},t^{\prime}}}{2}\right)\Big)\leq\frac{2}{\mu}.

(6.28)

Combining the above discussions together, we further get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}^{(i)}_{t+1}\|_{2}^{2}\big]\leq\sum_{t^{\prime}=1}^{t+1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\Big(\frac{2\alpha^{2}\eta_{j,t^{\prime}}^{2}}{nb}+\frac{4\left(t+1\right)\alpha^{2}\eta_{j,t^{\prime}}}{n^{2}\mu}\Big)\mathbb{E}\big[\big(\mathfrak{C}^{(i)}_{j,t^{\prime}}\big)^{2}\big]\prod_{j^{\prime}=j+1}^{k-1}\left(1-\frac{\mu\eta_{j^{\prime},t^{\prime}}}{2}\right).

Recalling result in (
6.1
),
\mathbb{E}\big[\big(\mathfrak{C}^{(i)}_{j,t^{\prime}}\big)^{2}\big]\leq 8L\mathbb{E}\left[f\left(\mathbf{v}_{j,h};z_{i}\right)\right]
, we further derive

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}^{(i)}_{t+1}\|_{2}^{2}\big]\leq\sum_{t^{\prime}=1}^{t+1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\Big(\frac{16\alpha^{2}\eta_{j,t^{\prime}}^{2}}{nb}+\frac{32\left(t+1\right)\alpha^{2}\eta_{j,t^{\prime}}}{n^{2}\mu}\Big)\mathbb{E}\left[f\left(\mathbf{v}_{j,t^{\prime}};z_{i}\right)\right]\prod_{j^{\prime}=j+1}^{k-1}\left(1-\frac{\mu\eta_{j^{\prime},t^{\prime}}}{2}\right).

Taking an average over
i\in[n]
, we get the stated bound

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathbf{w}_{t+1}-\mathbf{w}^{(i)}_{t+1}\|_{2}^{2}\big]\leq\sum_{t^{\prime}=1}^{t+1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\Big(\frac{16\alpha^{2}\eta_{j,t^{\prime}}^{2}}{nb}+\frac{32\left(t+1\right)\alpha^{2}\eta_{j,t^{\prime}}}{n^{2}\mu}\Big)\mathbb{E}\left[F_{S}\left(\mathbf{v}_{j,t^{\prime}}\right)\right]\prod_{j^{\prime}=j+1}^{k-1}\left(1-\frac{\mu\eta_{j^{\prime},t^{\prime}}}{2}\right).

The proof is completed.
∎

Proof of Theorem

We first state and prove the optimization error bound.

Lemma 11

(Optimization Error of Lookahead: Strongly Convex Case)
.

Suppose the assumptions in Theorem
5
hold, by setting the learning rate
\eta=\frac{b\mu}{2L^{2}(b+1)}
, the optimization error of the output
\mathbf{w}_{T}
of Lookahead satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{T})-F_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{*})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L}{2}\,e^{-\frac{3}{4}\alpha k\mu\eta T}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\|\mathbf{w}_{0}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{L\alpha}{2}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}e^{-\frac{3}{4}\alpha k\mu\eta t}\;\sum_{k^{\prime}=0}^{k-1}e^{-\frac{3}{4}\mu\eta k^{\prime}}\;\frac{2\eta^{2}L}{b}\;\mathbb{E}\!\left[F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})\right].

(6.29)

Furthermore, by choosing
b\lesssim n
,

k=\frac{2L}{\alpha\mu}
, and
T\asymp\log(\mu n)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{T})-F_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{*})\right]\lesssim\frac{L}{n}\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{w}_{0}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}]+\mathbb{E}[F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})].

(6.30)

Proof.

Since
F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})\leq F_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{*})
, an upper bound for
F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{T})-F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})
is also an upper bound for
F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{T})-F_{S}(\mathbf{w}^{*})
. Since the function
F_{S}(\mathbf{w})
is
\mu
-strongly convex and
\mathbf{w}_{S}
is the optimum of
F_{S}(\mathbf{w})
, we have

\displaystyle F_{S}\!\left(\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t}\right)

\displaystyle\geq F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})+\left\langle\nabla F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S}),\,\mathbf{w}_{S}-\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t}\right\rangle+\frac{\mu}{2}\left\|\mathbf{w}_{S}-\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})+\frac{\mu}{2}\left\|\mathbf{w}_{S}-\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t}\right\|_{2}^{2}.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})

\displaystyle\geq F_{S}\!\left(\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t}\right)+\left\langle\nabla F_{S}\!\left(\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t}\right),\,\mathbf{w}_{S}-\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t}\right\rangle+\frac{\mu}{2}\left\|\mathbf{w}_{S}-\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t}\right\|_{2}^{2}.

It then follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\big\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\big\|^{2}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\big[\big\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t}-\eta\,\nabla f\!\big(\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t};B_{\tau-1,t}\big)-\mathbf{w}_{S}\big\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\big[\big\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\big\|^{2}-2\eta\big\langle\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S},\,\nabla f\!\big(\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t};B_{\tau-1,t}\big)\big\rangle+\eta^{2}\big\|\nabla f\!\big(\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t};B_{\tau-1,t}\big)\big\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\big[\big\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\big\|^{2}-2\eta\big\langle\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S},\,\nabla F_{S}\!\left(\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t}\right)\big\rangle+\eta^{2}\big\|\nabla f\!\big(\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t};B_{\tau-1,t}\big)\big\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\big[\big\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\big\|^{2}+2\eta\Big(F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})-F_{S}\!\left(\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t}\right)-\frac{\mu}{2}\big\|\mathbf{w}_{S}-\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t}\big\|_{2}^{2}\Big)+\eta^{2}\big\|\nabla f\!\big(\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t};B_{\tau-1,t}\big)\big\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\big[\big\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\big\|^{2}+2\eta\big(-\frac{\mu}{2}\big\|\mathbf{w}_{S}-\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t}\big\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{\mu}{2}\big\|\mathbf{w}_{S}-\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t}\big\|_{2}^{2}\big)+\eta^{2}\big\|\nabla f\!\big(\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t};B_{\tau-1,t}\big)\big\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\leq(1-2\mu\eta)\mathbb{E}[\big\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\big\|^{2}]+\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}\big[\big\|\nabla f\!\big(\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t};B_{\tau-1,t}\big)\big\|^{2}\big].

For the second term, we use the result of (
6.16
) and have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\big\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\big\|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\leq(1-2\mu\eta)\mathbb{E}\big[\big\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\big\|^{2}\big]+\eta^{2}\frac{2L\mathbb{E}\big[F_{S}(\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t})\big]}{b}+2L\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}[F_{S}(\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t})-F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})]

\displaystyle\leq(1-2\mu\eta+\eta^{2}L^{2})\mathbb{E}\big[\big\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\big\|^{2}\big]+\eta^{2}\frac{2L\mathbb{E}\big[F_{S}(\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t})-F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})\big]+2L\mathbb{E}[F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})]}{b}

\displaystyle\leq(1-2\mu\eta+\eta^{2}\frac{L^{2}(b+1)}{b})\mathbb{E}\big[\big\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\big\|^{2}\big]+\eta^{2}\frac{2L\mathbb{E}[F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})]}{b},

where we have used
F_{S}(\mathbf{w})-F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})\leq\frac{L}{2}\|\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}_{2}
.
For simplicity, we define
C
as

C=\frac{L^{2}(b+1)}{b}.

The recurrence relation simplifies as

\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}]\leq\left(1-2\mu\eta+C\eta^{2}\right)\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}]+\eta^{2}\frac{2L\mathbb{E}[F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})]}{b}.

(6.31)

We now choose

\eta=\frac{\mu}{2C}=\frac{\mu b}{2L^{2}(b+1)}.

Substituting this value back into the multiplicative factor gives

1-2\mu\left(\frac{\mu}{2C}\right)+C\left(\frac{\mu}{2C}\right)^{2}=1-\frac{\mu^{2}}{C}+\frac{\mu^{2}}{4C}=1-\frac{3\mu^{2}}{4C}=1-\frac{3}{2}\mu\eta.

With this choice, the one-step recurrence (
6.31
) becomes

\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}]\leq\left(1-\frac{3}{2}\mu\eta\right)\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau-1,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}]+\eta^{2}\frac{2L\mathbb{E}[F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})]}{b}.

By applying the previous inequality recursively for the inner loop, we have

\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{v}_{k,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}]\leq\left(1-\frac{3}{2}\mu\eta\right)^{k}\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{w}_{t-1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}]+\sum_{k^{\prime}=0}^{k-1}\left(1-\frac{3}{2}\mu\eta\right)^{k^{\prime}}\eta^{2}\frac{2L\mathbb{E}[F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})]}{b}.

We now substitute this result back to the outer-loop. Recall the slow weights recurrence
\mathbf{w}_{t}=(1-\alpha)\mathbf{w}_{t-1}+\alpha\mathbf{v}_{k,t}
,

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|(1-\alpha)(\mathbf{w}_{t-1}-\mathbf{w}_{S})+\alpha(\mathbf{v}_{k,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha)\|\mathbf{w}_{t-1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}+\alpha\|\mathbf{v}_{k,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}.

Taking the expectation gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha)\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{w}_{t-1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}]+\alpha\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{v}_{k,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\alpha)\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{w}_{t-1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}]+\alpha\left(1-\frac{3}{2}\mu\eta\right)^{k}\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{w}_{t-1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}]+\alpha\sum_{k^{\prime}=0}^{k-1}\left(1-\frac{3}{2}\mu\eta\right)^{k^{\prime}}\frac{2L\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}[F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})]}{b}

\displaystyle=\left[1-\alpha+\alpha\left(1-\frac{3}{2}\mu\eta\right)^{k}\right]\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{w}_{t-1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}]+\alpha\sum_{k^{\prime}=0}^{k-1}\left(1-\frac{3}{2}\mu\eta\right)^{k^{\prime}}\frac{2L\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}[F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})]}{b}

\displaystyle=\left[1-\alpha\left(1-\left(1-\frac{3}{2}\mu\eta\right)^{k}\right)\right]\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{w}_{t-1}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}]+\alpha\sum_{k^{\prime}=0}^{k-1}\left(1-\frac{3}{2}\mu\eta\right)^{k^{\prime}}\frac{2L\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}[F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})]}{b}.

Let
\rho
be the contraction factor for the outer loop:

\rho=1-\alpha\left(1-\left(1-\frac{3}{2}\mu\eta\right)^{k}\right).

Since
0<(1-\frac{3}{2}\mu^{2}/C)<1
and
\alpha>0
, we have
0<\rho<1
. Unwinding this recurrence from
t=1
to
T
:

\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}]\leq\rho^{t}\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{w}_{0}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}]+\alpha\sum_{t^{\prime}=0}^{t-1}\rho^{t^{\prime}}\sum_{k^{\prime}=0}^{k-1}\left(1-\frac{3}{2}\mu\eta\right)^{k^{\prime}}\frac{2L\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}[F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})]}{b}.

(6.32)

Finally, using the
L
-smoothness property,
\mathbb{E}[F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{t})-F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})]\leq\frac{L}{2}\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{w}_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}]
, we arrive at the final optimization error bound.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{T})-F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L}{2}\left[1-\alpha\left(1-\left(1-\frac{3}{2}\mu\eta\right)^{k}\right)\right]^{T}\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{w}_{0}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{L\alpha}{2}\sum_{t^{\prime}=0}^{T-1}\left[1-\alpha\left(1-\left(1-\frac{3}{2}\mu\eta\right)^{k}\right)\right]^{t^{\prime}}\sum_{k^{\prime}=0}^{k-1}\left(1-\frac{3}{2}\mu\eta\right)^{k^{\prime}}\frac{2L\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}[F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})]}{b}.

(6.33)

We use the inequalities
1+x\leq e^{x}
for all real
x
and
1-e^{-x}\geq\frac{x}{1+x}
for all
x\geq 0
to get the following.

\displaystyle\Big[1-\alpha\bigl(1-(1-\frac{3}{2}\mu\eta)^{k}\bigr)\Big]^{T}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\Big\{-\alpha\bigl[1-(1-\frac{3}{2}\mu\eta)^{k}\bigr]T\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\Big\{-\alpha\bigl(1-\exp\big\{-\frac{3}{2}k\mu\eta\big\}\bigr)T\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\Big\{-\alpha\frac{3k\mu\eta}{3k\mu\eta+2}T\Bigr\}.

Then the optimization error bound becomes

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{T})-F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L}{2}\exp\big\{-\alpha\frac{3k\mu\eta}{3k\mu\eta+2}T\big\}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathbf{w}_{0}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{L\alpha}{2}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\exp\Big\{-\alpha\frac{3k\mu\eta}{3k\mu\eta+2}t\Big\}\sum_{k^{\prime}=0}^{k-1}\exp\{-\frac{3}{2}\mu\eta k^{\prime}\}\frac{2\eta^{2}L}{b}\;\mathbb{E}\!\left[F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})\right].

(6.34)

We now choose the parameters to be

k=\frac{2L}{\mu\alpha}
,
T\asymp\log(n)
, and we fix
\alpha
. Since
b\geq 1
, we have
L\eta=\frac{b\mu}{2L(b+1)}\in[1/4,1/2)
. Then with the above
k
, we know

\displaystyle k\mu\eta\geq\frac{2L}{\mu\alpha}\mu\eta=\frac{2}{\alpha}L\eta\geq\frac{1}{2\alpha}.

Hence

\displaystyle\frac{3k\mu\eta}{3k\mu\eta+2}\geq\frac{3}{3+4\alpha}.

(6.35)

It then follows that

\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\exp\Big\{-\alpha\frac{3k\mu\eta}{3k\mu\eta+2}t\Bigr\}\leq\frac{1}{1-e^{-3\alpha/(3+4\alpha)}}\asymp 1.

Also, since
\mu\eta\leq\mu/2L\leq 1
, we can use
1-e^{-x}\geq x/2
for
x\in(0,1]
and get

\sum_{k^{\prime}=0}^{k-1}e^{-\frac{3}{2}\mu\eta k^{\prime}}\;=\;\frac{1-e^{-\frac{3}{2}\mu\eta k}}{1-e^{-\frac{3}{2}\mu\eta}}\;\leq\;\frac{1}{1-e^{-\mu\eta}}\;\leq\;\frac{2}{\mu\eta}.

Plugging these into (
6.34
) yields the bound for the second term

\displaystyle\frac{L\alpha}{2}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\exp\Big\{-\alpha\frac{3k\mu\eta}{3k\mu\eta+2}T\Bigr\}\sum_{k^{\prime}=0}^{k-1}\exp\{-\frac{3}{2}\mu\eta k^{\prime}\}\frac{2\eta^{2}L}{b}\mathbb{E}\left[F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{L\alpha}{2}\frac{2}{\mu\eta}\frac{2\eta^{2}L}{b}\,\mathbb{E}\left[F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{L^{2}\eta}{\mu b}\mathbb{E}\left[F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})\right].

Since
\eta=\frac{\mu b}{2L^{2}(b+1)}
, this simplifies to

\displaystyle\frac{L\alpha}{2}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\exp\Big\{-\alpha\frac{3k\mu\eta}{3k\mu\eta+2}t\Bigr\}\sum_{k^{\prime}=0}^{k-1}\exp\{-\frac{3}{2}\mu\eta k^{\prime}\}\frac{2\eta^{2}L}{b}\mathbb{E}\left[F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})\right]\lesssim\frac{1}{2(b+1)}\mathbb{E}\left[F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})\right]\lesssim\mathbb{E}\left[F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})\right].

(6.36)

For the first term, together with (
6.35
), our choice of
T
ensures

\displaystyle\frac{L}{2}\exp\big\{-\alpha\frac{3k\mu\eta}{3k\mu\eta+2}T\big\}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathbf{w}_{0}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\right]\lesssim\frac{L}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathbf{w}_{0}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}\right].

(6.37)

Combining (
6.36
) and (
6.37
) gives the final result.
∎

We now state and prove the generalization bound.

Lemma 12

(Generalization Gap of Lookahead: Strongly Convex Case)
.

Suppose the assumptions in Theorem
5
hold. Let
\mathbf{w}_{T}
be the final output of Lookahead optimizer. By setting the learning rate
\eta=\frac{b\mu}{2L^{2}(b+1)}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[F(\mathbf{w}_{T})-F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{T})]\lesssim\frac{1}{n\mu}+\frac{1}{n^{2}}\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{w}_{0}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}]+\frac{1}{nL}\mathbb{E}[F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})].

Proof of Lemma
12
.

We now assume the constant step size
\eta_{\tau,t}=\eta
. Let
\mathbf{w}_{S}=\argmin_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}}F_{S}(\mathbf{w})
.
We denote
B_{k,t}=\{z_{i_{k,t}^{(1)}},\ldots,z_{i_{k,t}^{(b)}}\}
and
f\big(\mathbf{v};B_{k,t}\big)=\frac{1}{b}\sum_{j=1}^{b}f(\mathbf{v};z_{i_{k,t}^{(j)}})
. We can hence reformulate the minibatch SGD update as

\begin{split}\mathbf{v}_{\tau+1,t}=\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}-\eta\nabla f(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t};B_{\tau,t}).\end{split}

(6.38)

By the strong convexity of
f
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau+1,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}_{2}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}-\eta\nabla f(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t};B_{\tau,t})-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|_{2}^{2}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|^{2}_{2}]-2\eta\mathbb{E}[\langle\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S},\nabla F_{S}(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t})\rangle]+\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t};B_{\tau,t})\|_{2}^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\mu\eta_{\tau,t})\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t}-\mathbf{w}_{S}\|_{2}^{2}]-2\eta\mathbb{E}[F_{S}(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t})-F_{S}(\mathbf{w}_{S})]+\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}[\|\nabla f(\mathbf{v}_{\tau,t};B_{\tau,t})\|^{2}_{2}].

(6.39)

For the last term, we bound it using (
6.16

7
Conclusion

In this work, we investigate the stability and generalization properties of the Lookahead optimizer, a widely used algorithm for large-scale machine learning problems. While many discussions focus on its optimization benefits, we provide a rigorous analysis from the perspective of statistical learning theory. We develop on-average stability bounds for both convex and strongly convex problems, and we show how stability can be improved by small training errors, leading to optimistic bounds that depend on the empirical risk rather than a restrictive, global Lipschitz constant.

Our stability analysis implies optimal excess population risk bounds for both settings. Specifically, we demonstrate that Lookahead achieves the standard
O(1/\sqrt{n})
rate for convex problems and the optimal
O(1/(n\mu))
rate for strongly convex problems. A key finding is the adaptivity of Lookahead in the convex case, which achieves its rate without prior knowledge of the optimal risk
F(\mathbf{w}^{*})
, a practical advantage over standard Minibatch SGD.

There are several limitations to our current work which open avenues for future research. A primary limitation is that our analysis is confined to convex and strongly convex loss functions. Given the prevalence of non-convex optimization in modern deep learning, extending our stability analysis to the non-convex setting is a crucial next step. Furthermore, while we establish the optimal statistical rate for the strongly convex case, our analysis does not demonstrate a linear speedup with respect to the batch size, a property observed in Minibatch SGD. Investigating whether different hyperparameter schedules could unlock such a speedup for Lookahead would be of significant interest. We plan to address these limitations in our future research.

Quantitative native T1 mapping identifies papillary muscle and intracavitary cardiac lipomas: A multiparametric CMR case series
Authors: Manuela Montatore, Marco Rella, Maria Milo, Federica Masino, Ruggiero Tupputi, Gabriele Fanigliulo, Gianmichele Muscatella, Valentina Testini, Eluisa Muscogiuri, Giuseppe Guglielmi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Cardiac lipomas are rare benign tumors composed of mature adipose tissue. Although typically identifiable using conventional cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) sequences, diagnostic uncertainty may arise when lesions are small, located in anatomically complex regions, or mimic other intracardiac masses. We report 2 cases of cardiac lipoma with different anatomical presentations: a papillary muscle lipoma in a 47-year-old woman and an intracavitary left ventricular lipoma in a 31-year-old Asian man. In both cases, conventional CMR demonstrated characteristic fat signal features, while native T1 mapping revealed markedly reduced T1 values (250 ms and 405 ms at 1.5 T, respectively), enabling confident tissue characterization. These cases highlight the incremental value of parametric mapping as a quantitative, reproducible tool for diagnosing cardiac lipomas across diverse anatomical locations.

Introduction

Primary cardiac tumors are rare, with a reported prevalence below 0.1%, and the majority are benign. Cardiac lipomas, composed of mature adipocytes, represent an uncommon subset and are often detected incidentally during imaging performed for unrelated indications [1,2].

Cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) is the reference standard for non-invasive evaluation of cardiac masses due to its superior soft tissue contrast and multiparametric capabilities. In most cases, lipomas exhibit characteristic imaging features on conventional sequences, allowing straightforward diagnosis. However, diagnostic confidence may be reduced when lesions arise in anatomically complex or atypical locations, such as papillary muscles, or when they present as small intracavitary masses adherent to the endocardium. Partial-volume effects, motion, and proximity to normal myocardium may obscure the typical signal characteristics.

Parametric mapping techniques, particularly native T1 mapping, allow quantitative tissue characterization and may provide decisive incremental value in these challenging scenarios. The present report describes 2 cases of cardiac lipoma with different anatomical locations, emphasizing the role of native T1 mapping in achieving a confident diagnosis [[3], [4], [5]].

Case presentation

A 47-year-old woman was referred for CMR for evaluation of a suspected cardiac mass previously identified at another institution. She was asymptomatic and had no history of cardiovascular disease. Prior CMR and PET/CT examinations were available for comparison CMR was performed on a 1.5-T scanner, including cine SSFP imaging, T1-weighted turbo spin-echo (TSE) sequences, T2-weighted STIR sequences, native T1 and T2 mapping, first-pass perfusion imaging, and late gadolinium enhancement (LGE) using phase-sensitive inversion recovery (PSIR).

Case 2

A 31-year-old Asian man was referred for CMR for further evaluation of a suspected intracardiac mass detected on prior echocardiography. The patient was asymptomatic, with no history of cardiovascular disease.

CMR was performed on a 1.5-T scanner using SSFP cine imaging, T1-weighted turbo spin-echo (TSE) sequences, T2-weighted STIR sequences, and native T1 mapping. No intravenous contrast agent was administered, as per the referring physician’s request.

Imaging findings

•Cine SSFP short-axis images acquired in different phases of the cardiac cycle demonstrated a well-defined, oval mass adherent to the left ventricular endocardium at the mid-anterior wall, in close continuity with the anterior interventricular septum. The lesion moved synchronously with the myocardium throughout the cardiac cycle, without evidence of obstruction or impairment of ventricular function (Fig. 1).•An adapted 3-chamber view allowed improved visualization of the intracavitary mass.•T1-weighted images showed marked hyperintensity of the lesion, while T2-weighted STIR images demonstrated signal hypointensity, consistent with fat signal characteristics. No associated myocardial edema was identified.•Native T1 mapping revealed a markedly reduced T1 value of approximately 405 ms at 1.5 T, clearly lower than surrounding myocardium, confirming adipose tissue composition (Fig. 1E).•The mass measured approximately 14 × 8 × 18 mm. Left ventricular size, wall thickness, and global systolic function were normal (LVEF 69%). Right ventricular size and function were also within normal limits. No significant valvular abnormalities or pericardial effusion were observed.

Discussion

Cardiac lipomas are benign tumors composed of mature adipocytes and may arise from the endocardium, myocardium, or epicardium. Although their imaging appearance on conventional CMR sequences is often characteristic, certain anatomical locations may pose diagnostic challenges. Papillary muscle involvement is uncommon and may mimic hypertrophy or other focal abnormalities, while intracavitary endocardial lipomas may raise concern for thrombus or neoplastic lesions [[6], [7], [8]]. The 2 cases presented illustrate how native T1 mapping provides consistent and reproducible diagnostic information across different anatomical presentations. In the first case, a papillary muscle lipoma demonstrated an extremely low native T1 value (250 ms at 1.5 T), producing a characteristic hypointense “black hole” appearance (Fig. 3). In the second case, despite a different anatomical location, the intracavitary lipoma showed similarly reduced native T1 values (405 ms), confirming adipose tissue composition (Fig. 5E).

Differential diagnosis

The principal differential diagnoses for papillary muscle or intracavitary lesions include:•Papillary muscle hypertrophy, which demonstrates normal native T1 and T2 values and lacks fat signal characteristics.•Thrombus, which may show variable T1 depending on age but generally demonstrates higher T1 values than adipose tissue and lacks fat suppression.•Fibroma, typically characterized by higher native T1 values, low T2 signal, and intense late gadolinium enhancement.

Role of parametric mapping

Native T1 mapping enables quantitative, pixel-wise assessment of myocardial tissue properties. At 1.5 T, normal myocardium typically demonstrates native T1 values of approximately 950–1050 ms, whereas adipose tissue is characterized by markedly reduced T1 values [[3], [4], [5], [6]]. The concordant findings in both cases reinforce the robustness of native T1 mapping for identifying fat, regardless of lesion size or anatomical location.

Native T2 mapping further complements tissue characterization by excluding edema or inflammation, supporting the benign, non-inflammatory nature of lipomatous lesions.

The integration of parametric mapping with conventional CMR sequences enhances diagnostic confidence, reduces ambiguity related to partial volume effects, and may obviate the need for invasive diagnostic procedures or additional imaging [[8], [9], [10], [11]].

Conclusion

Native T1 mapping represents a robust and reproducible quantitative tool for tissue characterization of cardiac masses and provides substantial incremental diagnostic value over conventional CMR sequences. The 2 cases presented—1 involving a papillary muscle lipoma and the other an intracavitary left ventricular lipoma—demonstrate that markedly reduced native T1 values reliably identify adipose tissue across different anatomical locations and lesion morphologies.

In anatomically complex or atypical settings, where partial volume effects, motion, or proximity to normal myocardium may limit the diagnostic performance of conventional imaging, native T1 mapping enables confident differentiation of cardiac lipomas from thrombus, fibroma, and hypertrophic myocardial variants. The characteristic hypointense appearance on color-coded T1 maps, coupled with absolute low T1 values, provides an intuitive and objective imaging signature of fatty tissue.

Incorporation of parametric mapping into routine CMR protocols can improve diagnostic confidence, support conservative clinical management in asymptomatic patients, and reduce the need for invasive procedures or additional imaging. As CMR continues to evolve toward quantitative imaging, native T1 mapping should be considered an essential component in the multiparametric assessment of cardiac masses, particularly when lesions are small or located in anatomically challenging regions.

Correlated Gromov-Witten invariants
Thomas Blomme, Francesca Carocci

Abstract
. We introduce a geometric refinement of Gromov-Witten invariants for ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} -bundles relative to the natural fiberwise boundary structure. We call these refined invariant correlated Gromov-Witten invariants. Furthermore we prove a refinement of the degeneration formula [ Li02 , Ran22 , KLR18 ] keeping track of the correlation. Finally, combining certain invariance properties of the correlated invariant, a local computation and the refined degeneration formula we follow [ Blo23 ] floor diagrams technique to prove regularity results for the generating series of the invariants in the case of ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} -bundles over elliptic curves. Such invariants are expected to play a role in the degeneration formula for reduced Gromov-Witten invariants for abelian and K3 surfaces.

Introduction

1.1.
Setting

In this paper we introduce and study a refinement of the relative (or logarithmic) Gromov-Witten invariants
[
GS13
,
Li01
,
Kim10
,
AC14
]
of the pair
(Y=\mathbb{P}_{X}(\mathcal{O}\oplus L),D)
, where
D=D^{+}+D^{-}=\mathbb{P}_{X}(L)\oplus\mathbb{P}_{X}(\mathcal{O})
and
X
is a smooth projective variety over
\mathbb{C}
. We fix the following discrete data:

\triangleright

g,n,m
are positive integers,

\triangleright

{\boldsymbol{w}}=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
is a
n
-tuple of non-zero integers with
\sum w_{i}=0;
we moreover set
b({\boldsymbol{w}})=\sum_{w_{i}>0}w_{i}=-\sum_{w_{i}<0}w_{i}

\triangleright

\beta\in H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
is an effective homology class.

Let
\mathcal{M}:=\mathcal{M}_{g,m}(Y|D^{\pm},\beta,{\boldsymbol{w}})
be the moduli space of logarithmic stable maps parametrizing

f:(C,p_{1},\dots,p_{m},q_{1},\dots,q_{n})\to Y,

where
C
is a stable curve of genus
g
with image in class
\beta+b({\boldsymbol{w}})F\in H_{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z}),
the marked point
q_{i}
is mapped to
D^{\mathrm{sgn}(w_{i})}
with tangency order
|w_{i}|
, the marked points
p_{i}
are mapped to the interior. The vector
{\boldsymbol{w}}
is thus called
tangency profile
.
The logarithmic (or equivalently relative
[
AMW14
]
) Gromov-Witten invariants are defined integrating constraints pulled-back along evaluation maps

\mathrm{ev}:\mathcal{M}_{g,m}(Y|D^{\pm},\beta,{\boldsymbol{w}})\longrightarrow X^{n}\times Y^{m},

against the virtual fundamental class
[\mathcal{M}]^{\mathrm{vir}}

[
GS13
]
.

We propose a refinement for these GW invariants by remembering a discrete quantity depending on the position of the points mapped to the boundary divisor. This quantity is called a
correlator
.

1.2.
The Albanese evaluation

The idea of the refinement comes from reinterpreting the map to
(Y,D^{\pm})=(\mathbb{P}_{X}(\mathcal{O}\oplus L),D^{\pm})
as the data of a map
f:C\to X
together with the choice of an isomorphism
f^{*}L\cong\mathcal{O}_{C}(\alpha)
where
\alpha\in H^{0}(C,\overline{M}_{C}^{gp})
is defined using the logarithmic structure on
C.
When
C
is smooth, this condition takes the familiar form
f^{*}L\simeq\mathcal{O}_{C}(\sum w_{i}q_{i}).

1.2.1.
Case of
X\times\mathbb{P}^{1}

To simplify, let us momentarily assume that
L
is the trivial bundle and that the source curve
C
is smooth.
Then, reinterpreting
f\in\mathcal{M}
as explained above, part of the data of a log stable map is the isomorphism

\mathcal{O}_{C}\left(\sum w_{i}q_{i}\right)\cong\mathcal{O}\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(C).

For a smooth curve, the Abel-Jacobi theorem ensures that
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(C)\simeq\operatorname{Alb}(C)
, where
\operatorname{Alb}(C)
is the Albanese variety of
C
.
We denote by
a_{X}:X\to\operatorname{Alb}(X)
the map from
X
to its Albanese variety, unique up to translation. By the functoriality of the Albanese variety,
f
induces a morphism of Abelian varieties

f_{*}\colon\operatorname{Alb}(C)\to\operatorname{Alb}(X)

compatible with the Albanese maps.

We denote by
a_{\boldsymbol{w}}
the morphism

a_{\boldsymbol{w}}:(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in X^{n}\longmapsto\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}a_{X}(x_{i})\in\operatorname{Alb}(X).

As
\sum w_{i}=0
, the map
a_{\boldsymbol{w}}
does not change if we compose
a_{X}
with a translation. From
\mathcal{O}_{C}(\sum w_{i}q_{i})\simeq\mathcal{O}\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(C)
we the get that:

(1)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}a_{X}(f(q_{i}))=0\in\operatorname{Alb}(X).

Equation (
1
) is a constraint on the relative position of the images
f(q_{i})
. In other words, the
X^{n}
part of the evaluation map is not surjective, and truly has values in the subset
a_{\boldsymbol{w}}^{-1}(0)
.

1.2.2.
Case of a non-trivial bundle

If
L
is not assumed to be trivial anymore, we prove in Lemma
3.2
that there is a natural map
\varphi_{\beta}:\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X)\to\operatorname{Alb}(X)
defined through Hodge theory, which only depend on the curve class. Then
Proposition
3.4
ensures that equation (
1
) becomes the following:

(2)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}a_{X}(f(q_{i}))=\varphi_{\beta}(L)\in\operatorname{Alb}(X).

In other words, the image depends only on the degree
\beta
and the line bundle
L
and not on
f.

1.3.
Correlated classes and correlated GW invariants

We now assume that the tangency orders
w_{i}
have a non-trivial common divisor
\delta
. We then consider the map

a_{{\boldsymbol{w}}/\delta}:(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in X^{n}\longmapsto\sum_{1}^{n}\frac{w_{i}}{\delta}a_{X}(x_{i})\in\operatorname{Alb}(X).

The above morphism induces a morphism from the space of log stable maps
\mathcal{M}

\kappa^{\delta}(f)=\sum\frac{w_{i}}{\delta}a_{X}(f(q_{i}))\in\operatorname{Alb}(X).

It follows from Equation (
2
) that

\delta\cdot\kappa^{\delta}(f)=\varphi_{\beta}(L).

Therefore, the map
\kappa^{\delta}
takes values in the torsor of
\delta
-roots of
\varphi_{\beta}(L)
, denoted by
T^{\delta}(L,\beta)
. The value of
\kappa^{\delta}
is called a
correlator
and is denoted by
\theta
.
We want to refine the Gromov-Witten of
(Y,D^{\pm})
keeping track of the value of the correlator.

The discussion above assumes the source curve
C
to be smooth, but the morphism
\kappa^{\delta}
is also defined on the boundary of the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{g,m}(Y|D^{\pm},\beta,{\boldsymbol{w}})
, still with values in
T_{\delta}(L,\beta)
(see Section
3.2.2
).
This shows that

\mathcal{M}
splits into distinct (possibly still disconnected) components
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}
indexed by the values of the correlator
\theta\in T^{\delta}(L,\beta);
in particular the virtual class splits as a sum of the so-called
correlated classes

[\mathcal{M}^{\theta}]^{\mathrm{vir}}.
We define the
full correlated class

[\![\mathcal{M}]\!]^{\delta}
:

[\![\mathcal{M}]\!]^{\delta}=\sum_{\theta\in T^{\delta}(L,\beta)}[\mathcal{M}^{\theta}]^{\mathrm{vir}}\cdot(\theta)\in\mathbb{Q}[\operatorname{Alb}(X)]\otimes H_{\bullet}(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{Q}),

which is an element of the group algebra with cycle coefficients with support on
T_{\delta}(L,\beta)
.

Correlated GW invariants
are obtained by capping these classes with some pull-back by the evaluation map: if
\gamma\in H^{\bullet}(X^{n}\times Y^{m},\mathbb{Q})
, we set

\langle\langle\gamma\rangle\rangle^{\delta}_{g,\beta,{\boldsymbol{w}}}=\int_{[\![\mathcal{M}]\!]^{\delta}}\mathrm{ev}^{*}\gamma\in\mathbb{Q}[\operatorname{Alb}(X)],

which is an element of the group algebra
\mathbb{Q}[\operatorname{Alb}(X)]
with support on the torsor
T^{\delta}(L,\beta)
. Distinct correlators may yield different Gromov-Witten invariants, as illustrated by the computations in the elliptic case.

It follows from the discussion above that the evaluation map truly has values in
a_{\boldsymbol{w}}^{-1}(\varphi_{\beta}(L))=\bigsqcup a_{{\boldsymbol{w}}/\delta}^{-1}(\theta)
. A broader generalization of GW invariants would be obtained by allowing the pull-back of cohomology classes from
a_{\boldsymbol{w}}^{-1}(\varphi_{\beta}(L))
rather than
X^{n}
. Correlated invariants are a particular case of these invariants where we pull-back the classes
1_{\theta}\in H^{0}(a_{\boldsymbol{w}}^{-1}(\varphi_{\beta}(L)),\mathbb{Q})
, corresponding to the components
a_{{\boldsymbol{w}}/\delta}^{-1}(\theta)
of
a_{{\boldsymbol{w}}}^{-1}(\varphi_{\beta}(L))
.

1.4.
Properties of the correlated classes

1.4.1.
Deformation invariance

We prove that the correlated class is deformation invariant, so that we are right to speak about a refinement of the GW invariants. Here we are allowed to deform both the variety
X
and the line bundle
L
, considering a family
\mathcal{X}\to S
with a line bundle
\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{X}
. Notice the correlators take values in the torsor inside the Albanese variety
\operatorname{Alb}_{\mathcal{X}/S}
which is also deformed. Using the latter for some suitable choice of family, we furthermore prove the following:

Theorem
.

3.11

The correlated invariants
\langle\langle\gamma\rangle\rangle^{\delta}_{g,\beta,{\boldsymbol{w}}}
are invariant under the action of the subgroup
\varphi_{\beta}(\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X)))
.

In the case where
X=E
is an elliptic curve, we have
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(E)\simeq\operatorname{Alb}(E)\simeq E
. If the chosen homology class is
\beta=a[E]
, the map
\varphi_{\beta}:E\to E
is actually the multiplication by
a
, so that we have

\varphi_{a[E]}(\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(E))=\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta/\gcd(a,\delta)}(E).

In the two extremal case, it means the following:

\triangleright

If
a
and
\delta
are coprime, the subgroup is
\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(E)
, so that the classes
[\mathcal{M}^{\theta}]^{\mathrm{vir}}
all provide the same invariants.

\triangleright

If
\delta|a
, the subgroup is trivial and we have no further information on the correlated invariants.

Specific to the elliptic case, choosing
L=\mathcal{O}
so that
T^{\delta}(L,\beta)=\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(E)
, we furthermore prove that the invariant for the class
[\mathcal{M}^{\theta}]^{\mathrm{vir}}
only depends on
\theta
through its order in
\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(E)
.

1.4.2.
Degeneration formula

One of the most important tools in the computation of Gromov-Witten invariants is the degeneration formula
[
Li02
,
MR23
,
KLR18
]
. Our second contribution is a degeneration formula suited for the correlated classes.

We state two degeneration formulas. The first one is stated in Theorem
4.7
and valid for any variety
X
. The drawback of the latter is that to be useful, one needs an expression for a refinement of a diagonal class. In the usual degeneration formula, such expression is provided by the to Künneth decomposition formula. In our situation, such a decomposition is not obvious to get.
The second formula deals with the particular case where the maps
H^{\bullet}(X^{n},\mathbb{Q})\to H^{\bullet}(a_{{\boldsymbol{w}}/\delta}^{-1}(\theta),\mathbb{Q})
are surjective. Under this assumption, we recover an expression using the usual diagonal with its Künneth decomposition. To state the formula, we use the operator
\mathfrak{d}\left[\frac{1}{\delta}\right]
on
\mathbb{Q}[\operatorname{Alb}(X)]
, which maps
(\theta)
to the average of its
\delta
-roots.

Theorem
.

(Corollary
4.12
)
For any class
\gamma\in H^{\bullet}(X^{n}\times Y^{m},\mathbb{Q})
, we have the following equality between intersection numbers:

\mathrm{ev}^{*}\gamma\cap[\![\mathcal{M}_{0}]\!]^{\delta}=\sum_{\Gamma}\frac{\prod w_{e}}{|\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)|}(\mathrm{ev}^{*}\gamma\cup\mathrm{ev}^{*}{\boldsymbol{D}}^{\vee})\cap\mathfrak{d}\left[\frac{1}{\delta/\delta_{\Gamma}}\right]\prod_{V}[\![\mathcal{M}_{V}]\!]^{\delta_{\Gamma}},

where the sum is over degeneration graphs (Definition
4.1
),
{\boldsymbol{D}}^{\vee}
is the Poincaré dual of the class of the diagonal, and
\mathfrak{d}
is the division operator in the group algebra.

This technical surjectivity assumption is satisfied in the case where
X
is an elliptic curve
E
since in that case the map
a_{{\boldsymbol{w}}/\delta}^{-1}(\theta)\hookrightarrow X^{n}
is just the inclusion of a subtorus.

1.5.
The elliptic case

The remainder of the paper focuses on the easiest possible case, where
X
is an elliptic curve. For simplicity in the introduction we state the theorems for the case
L\cong\mathcal{O}.

1.5.1.
Local invariants

In Section
5
, we are able to provide an explicit computation of the following correlated Gromov-Witten invariants, called
local GW invariants
:

\langle\langle\mathrm{pt}_{0},1_{w_{1}},\mathrm{pt}_{w_{2}},\dots,\mathrm{pt}_{w_{n}}\rangle\rangle^{\delta}_{1,a[E],{\boldsymbol{w}}}.

For this specific choice of insertions, the invariant is a concrete count of curves which are in this situation covers of
E
. The enumeration is thus possible through a careful study of these covers.

Theorem
.

5.3

The full local correlated invariant has the following expression:

\langle\langle\mathrm{pt}_{0},1_{w_{1}},\mathrm{pt}_{w_{2}},\dots,\mathrm{pt}_{w_{n}}\rangle\rangle^{\delta}_{1,a[E],{\boldsymbol{w}}}=a^{n-1}w_{1}^{2}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\delta}(a),

where
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\delta}(a)
is a refinement of the sum of divisors function
\sigma(a)=\sum_{d|a}d
(see Section
5
).

We provide an explicit expression for
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\delta}(a)
. These invariants are in a sense the most simple ones because they carry a unique interior point constraint. They enable the computation of invariants with more point constraints through the degeneration formula as seen in the next section.

1.5.2.
Floor diagrams and regularity

Using the degeneration formula, the above local invariants are in fact the ground blocks enabling the computations of the more general correlated invariants
\langle\langle\mathrm{pt}^{n+g-1},1_{w_{1}},\dots,1_{w_{n}}\rangle\rangle^{\delta}_{g,a[E],{\boldsymbol{w}}}
. The degeneration formula reduces the computation to the enumeration of
floor diagrams
with some new multiplicities.

Floor diagrams are a combinatorial tool introduced by E. Brugallé and G. Mikhalkin to enumerate some planar curves subject to point conditions chosen in a stretched configuration
[
BM07
,
BM08
]
. Many variations have since been introduced to deal with various situations, see
[
Blo23
]
for the version relevant for this paper.

Using the floor diagram algorithm, we are able to provide two regularity statements refining Theorems 6.6 and 6.9 from
[
Blo23
]
. The first statement deals with the generating series in the class direction.

Theorem
.

6.12

Let
{\boldsymbol{w}}
be a tangency profile of length
n
. The generating series

\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}\langle\langle\mathrm{pt}^{n+g-1},1_{w_{1}},\dots,1_{w_{n}}\rangle\rangle_{g,a[E],{\boldsymbol{w}}}^{\delta}\mathsf{q}^{a},

is quasi-modular for the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(\delta)
with values in the group algebra
\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(E)]
.

Compared to
[
Blo23
, Theorem 6.9]
, the refinement loses regularity since the quasi-modularity is only satisfied for the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(\delta)
and not
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. The next regularity statement concerns the dependance in the tangency orders
{\boldsymbol{w}}
. Namely, we consider the following functions:

N^{\delta}_{g,a}:{\boldsymbol{w}}\longmapsto\langle\langle\mathrm{pt}^{n+g-1},1_{w_{1}},\dots,1_{w_{n}}\rangle\rangle^{\delta}_{g,a[E],{\boldsymbol{w}}}\in\mathbb{Q}[E],

defined on the set of tangency profile
{\boldsymbol{w}}
divisible by
\delta
.

Theorem
.

6.17

For fixed
a,g,n,\delta
, the function
{\boldsymbol{w}}\mapsto N^{\delta}_{g,a}({\boldsymbol{w}})
is piecewise polynomial.

Theorem
6.17
states that the piecewise polynomiality from
[
Blo23
, Theorem 6.6]
survives the refinement. Such behavior has also been observed in a number of similar situations, such as double Hurwitz numbers and relative GW invariants of Hirzebruch surfaces
[
AB17
]
.

1.6.
Future directions

Correlated GW invariants from the elliptic case are interesting due to their relation to the GW invariants of bielliptic surfaces, when caring about the torsion part in the homology group of a bielliptic surface. The GW invariants up to torsion have already be computed by the first author in
[
Blo24
]
, and the use of correlated invariants is necessary for the torsion part.

Importantly, these invariants seem also to be necessary for the study of reduced GW invariants of abelian surfaces, and the mysterious multiple cover formula they should satisfy
[
Obe22
,
Blo22
]
. We plan to investigate this further in subsequent works.

Many problems concerning these correlated invariants and their computation remain open, such as their computation in the presence of
\psi
-classes or
\lambda
-classes. It would also be interesting to see if we can have a correlated double-ramification cycle formula refining the formula from
[
JPPZ20
]
.

3.1.1.

For

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle\longmapsto\left(\omega\mapsto\int_{\gamma}\omega\right)

(1)

\mathcal{X}\times\operatorname{Pic}^{0}_{\mathcal{X}/\penalty S}
rigidified along the section
\sigma_{0}.

3.2.
Refinement of the moduli spaces

Let
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}_{g,m}(Y|D^{\pm},\beta,{\boldsymbol{w}})
be the moduli space of logarithmic stable maps to
Y=\mathbb{P}_{X}^{1}(\mathcal{O}\oplus L)
considered in the previous section and let us now assume that
L\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X)
: we have
c_{1}(L)=0
.

3.2.1.
Morphism to
\operatorname{Alb}(X)

Using the tangency orders
{\boldsymbol{w}}
, we consider the map

a_{\boldsymbol{w}}:(x_{i})\in X^{n}\longmapsto\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}a_{X}(x_{i})\in\operatorname{Alb}(X).

As
\sum w_{i}=0
, this map does not depend on the choice of a base point in
X
and is uniquely defined. Indeed, for
z_{0},z_{0}^{\prime}
two choices of base point we have that
a^{\prime}_{X}=t_{-a^{\prime}_{X}(z_{0})}\circ a_{X}
and thus
\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}a^{\prime}_{X}(x_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}(a_{X}(x_{i})-a^{\prime}_{X}(z_{0}))=\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}a_{X}(x_{i})
. Now,consider the morphism from the moduli space to the Albanese variety of
X
by composing with the evaluation map:

\kappa\colon\mathcal{M}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{ev}}X^{n}\xrightarrow{a_{\boldsymbol{w}}}\operatorname{Alb}(X)

defined by

(4)

(\mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{f}X,(p_{j})_{j=1}^{m},(q_{i})_{i=1}^{n},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}(\alpha)\cong^{\lambda}f^{*}L)\mapsto(x_{i}:=f\circ q_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}a_{X}(x_{i}).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\beta\in H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
be the homology class of a complex curve. The bilinear map

\phi:\alpha\otimes\omega\in H^{0,1}(X)\otimes H^{1,0}(X)\longmapsto\int_{\beta}\alpha\wedge\omega\in\mathbb{C},

induces a morphism

\varphi_{\beta}:\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Alb}(X).

Proof.

We use the isomorphisms
H^{0,1}(X)=H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
and
H^{1,0}(X)=H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})
as well as the Hodge decomposition
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})=H^{0,1}(X)\oplus H^{1,0}(X)
. Through the above isomorphisms, the map
H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})\to H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
yielding
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X)
is actually the composition of
H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})\to H^{1}(X,\mathbb{C})
followed by the projection onto
H^{0,1}(X)
.
The bilinear map
\phi
induces a linear map
\phi^{*}:H^{0,1}(X)\to H^{1,0}(X)^{*}
. To prove that it descends to a map
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X)\to\operatorname{Alb}(X)
, we need to show that

\phi^{*}(H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Z}))\subset H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})\subset H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})^{*}.

Let
\omega\in H^{1,0}(X)
and
\gamma\in H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})
. We write
\gamma\otimes\mathbb{C}=\gamma^{0,1}+\gamma^{1,0}
for its Hodge decomposition in
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})
. We have

\displaystyle\phi^{*}(\gamma)(\omega)=

\displaystyle\int_{\beta}\gamma^{0,1}\wedge\omega\text{ by definition,}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\beta}(\gamma^{0,1}+\gamma^{1,0})\wedge\omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\cap(\gamma\cup\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\beta\cap\gamma)\cap\omega,

where the second equality follows from the fact that
\int_{\beta}\gamma^{1,0}\wedge\omega=0
, since
\beta
is the class of a complex curve. The last line ensures that
\phi^{*}(\gamma)(\omega)
is a period of
\omega
since
\beta\cap\gamma\in H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})
. Therefore, as desired,
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\to H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})^{*}
descends to a map

\varphi_{\beta}:H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})/H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})^{*}/H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z}).

∎

Remark 3.3
.

Giving a non-degenerate positive bilinear map
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\otimes H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})\to\mathbb{C}
amounts to provide a polarization, i.e. an ample line bundle
\mathcal{L}
, on the Abelian variety
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X)
, see for example
[
MRM74
]
. In an Abelian variety, a polarization then induces a map

A_{\mathcal{L}}:L\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X)\longmapsto t_{L}^{*}\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{-1}\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X))=\operatorname{Alb}(X).

Assume
X
is projective with hyperplane class
h\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
giving a Kähler form on
X
. Then we have a polarization given by the non-degenerate positive bilinear form

\phi:\alpha\otimes\omega\longmapsto\int_{X}h^{n-1}\wedge\alpha\wedge\omega.

Assume the class
\beta
is obtained by intersecting sufficiently many hyperplane sections:
\beta
is Poincaré dual to
h^{n-1}
. Then, the previously defined morphism
\varphi_{\beta}
is actually the morphism
A_{\mathcal{L}}
provided by the above polarization on
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X)
.

\blacklozenge

We claim that the morphism
\kappa
is in fact constant to
\varphi_{\beta}(L)
. To prove the latter, we start with the case of smooth curve, and then use it to deal with the case of nodal curves.

Proposition 3.4
.

The morphism
\kappa\colon\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\operatorname{Alb}^{0}(X)
defined above is constant equal to
\varphi_{\beta}(L)
.

Proof.

\bullet
Let us first look at a geometric point
s=\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathcal{M}
of the moduli space such that the source curve
C
is smooth. We first claim that in such case

\kappa(s)=f_{*}f^{*}L

where

\mathcal{O}_{C}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}q_{i})\cong f^{*}L
(by definition of the moduli functor) and

f_{*}\colon\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(C)\to\operatorname{Alb}^{0}(X)
is the morphism obtained dualizing
f^{*}\colon\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X)\to\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(C)
via the self-duality isomorphism of
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(C).

To see that, choose
p_{0}
a point in
C
and
z_{0}=f(p_{0});
then the functoriality of the Albanese morphism for compatible choices of base point gives

f_{*}(f^{*}L)=f_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}q_{i}))=f_{*}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}A_{p_{0}}(q_{i}))=\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}a_{X}(x_{i})

with the last equality following from the commutativity.

Since we are working over
\mathbb{C}
, we may use that for
X
(resp.
C
), we have

\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X)=H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})/H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})\text{ and }\operatorname{Alb}(X)=H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})^{*}/H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z}).

Through Hodge theory,
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
is isomorphic to the Dolbeault cohomology group
H^{0,1}(X)
. In particular, by functoriality, the pull-back map
f^{*}:\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X)\to\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(C)
is in fact induced by
f^{*}:H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\to H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})
, which is the pull-back at the level of
(0,1)
-forms. Using that
H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})=H^{1,0}(X)
, the composition
f_{*}f^{*}
is induced by the
\mathbb{C}
-linear map

\varphi_{C}:H^{0,1}(X)\xrightarrow{f^{*}}H^{0,1}(C)\simeq H^{1,0}(C)^{*}\xrightarrow{f_{*}}H^{1,0}(X)^{*},

where the second arrow is the dual to the pull-back map for holomorphic forms. The middle isomorphism is provided by the Poincaré duality. Finally, if
\alpha\in H^{0,1}(X)
is a
(0,1)
-form on
X
and
\omega\in H^{1,0}(X)
an holomorphic
1
-form, we have

\varphi_{C}(\alpha)(\omega)=\int_{C}f^{*}\alpha\wedge f^{*}\omega=\int_{C}f^{*}(\alpha\wedge\omega)=\int_{f(C)}\alpha\wedge\omega=\int_{\beta}\alpha\wedge\omega,

where the penultimate equality is push-pull formula.

\bullet
Now, assume that
C
is nodal; we write
\nu\colon\bigsqcup C_{V}\to C
for the normalization map and
f_{V}\colon C_{V}\to X
for the restriction of
f\circ\nu
to the component
C_{V}
of the normalization. Since each
C_{V}
is now smooth, the discussion above tells us that the morphism

f_{V\ast}f_{V}^{*}\colon\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X)\to\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(C_{V})\to\operatorname{Alb}^{0}(X)

is equal to
\varphi_{\beta_{V}}
, where
\beta_{V}=f_{V\ast}[C_{V}]
. Furthermore, since
\sum\beta_{V}=\beta
we have

\displaystyle\varphi_{\beta}=\sum\varphi_{\beta_{V}}\colon\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X)

\displaystyle\to\prod_{V}\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(C_{V})\to\operatorname{Alb}^{0}(X)

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle\to\nu^{*}f^{*}L\to\sum_{V}f_{V\ast}(f_{V}^{*}L).

To conclude the proof we only need to argue that

\sum_{V}f_{V\ast}(f_{V}^{*}L)=\kappa(s).

When
C
is singular,
f^{*}L\cong^{\lambda}\mathcal{O}_{C}(\alpha)
for
\alpha\in H^{0}(C,\overline{M}_{C}^{gp})
such that

\mathcal{O}_{C}(\alpha)\rvert_{C_{V}}=\mathcal{O}_{C_{V}}(\sum_{e\vdash V}s_{e,V}(\alpha)q_{e}),

satisfying the balancing condition
\sum_{e\vdash V}s_{e,V}(\alpha)=0
. Here
s_{e,V}(\alpha)
is the outgoing slope of
\alpha
at
V
. Notice that
\alpha
induces an orientation on the edges of
\Gamma
. We denote with
w_{e}
the slope along an edge with this induced orientation. Then if
V,W
are the two vertices adjacent to a bounded edge
e
, we will have
s_{e,V}(\alpha)=-s_{e,W}(\alpha)
with one of the two being
w_{e}
.

We know from the analysis for the smooth case that

f_{V\ast}(f_{V}^{*}L)=\sum_{e\vdash V}s_{e,V}(\alpha)a_{X}(f_{V}\circ q_{i}).

From the observation above and the fact that
f_{V}(q_{e})=f_{W}(q_{e})
since the maps are induced by the maps on the nodal curves, it follows that when we consider
\sum_{V}f_{V\ast}(f_{V}^{*}L)
the contributions coming from the nodes cancel out and we obtain the required identity.
∎

Example 3.5
.

Assume
X=E
is an elliptic curve. We have
\operatorname{Alb}(E)\cong\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(E)\cong E

Remark 3.6
.

For a family
C/\penalty S\xrightarrow{F}X\times S
with non-smooth family of source curves, it is not obvious how to define

F_{*}\colon\operatorname{Pic}^{[0]}_{C/\penalty S}\to\operatorname{Alb}^{0}(X)\times S.

Indeed
\operatorname{Pic}^{[0]}_{C/\penalty S}\to S
is not an abelian scheme, as it is no longer proper, and we do not have an Abel-Jacobi map. There are two possible ways to fix the situations.

\triangleright

We can consider the compactified Jacobian
\operatorname{Pic}^{[0]}_{C/\penalty S}\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{J}}_{C/S}^{\mathcal{E}_{\pi},\sigma}
parametrizing rank one, torsion free simple
(\mathcal{E}_{\pi},\sigma)
-quasi-stable sheaves on the fibers of
C\to S
for
\mathcal{E}_{\pi}
the canonical degree
0
polarization on
C/\penalty S
in the sense of
[
Mel15
, Section 2.1]
. We refer the reader to
[
Mel15
]
and to
[
MRV19
]
for existence and properties of the compactifications.
Then a family version of the argument in
[
MRV19
, Section 5]
shows that there is an isomorphism of
S
-group:

\operatorname{Pic}^{[0]}_{C/\penalty S}\to\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(\overline{\mathbb{J}}_{C/S}^{\mathcal{E}_{\pi},\sigma})

and we can then define
F_{*}
as the dual of
F^{*}
composed with the natural inclusion of
\operatorname{Pic}^{[0]}_{C/\penalty S}
with the compactified Jacobian.

\triangleright

Alternatively, one should in future be able to use the theory of logarithmic abelian varieties and of Logarithmic Picard group developed by Molcho-Wise
[
MW22
]
. They show that
\operatorname{LogPic}_{C^{v}/\penalty S}\to S
(where the superscript
C^{v}
means we are considering
C\to S
endowed with the vertical log structure)
is a proper group stack over
\operatorname{LogSch}

whose fibers are logarithmic abelian varieties in the sense of Kajiwara, Kato and Nakayama
[
KKN08
]
.
It is expected that many classical results about abelian varieties admit generalization for logarithmic abelian varieties. In this spirit, in a forthcoming paper
[
MUW
]
Molcho-Ulirsch -Wise will show that
\operatorname{LogPic}_{C^{v}/\penalty S}
is self dual in the category of logarithmic Abelian variety, admits a Abel Jacobi map and is universal for morphism to abelian groups over log schemes.
Once such a theory will be fully developed, considering the composition:

\operatorname{Pic}^{[0]}_{C/\penalty S}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{LogPic}_{C^{v}/\penalty S}\xrightarrow{F_{*}}Alb(X)\times S

we would obtain the desired extension.

Once the extension
F_{*}
is defined, one can look at the closed points to give an explicit description of
F_{*}F^{*}L
and verify that this coincide with
\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}a_{X}(f\circ q_{i}).

Since we do not need the extension at the level of universal Jacobian but only the map from
\mathcal{M}
, we do not fill in the technical details left out in this remark.

\blacklozenge

3.2.2.
Refinement by correlation

Let us go back to the setting of
\mathcal{M}(X,L)
where we fix the ramification profile. To simplify the notation, for a given point in
\mathcal{M}
, we set
x_{i}=f\circ q_{i}
. We are interested in the case where the contact orders have a non-trivial common divisor, i.e. there is some
1\neq\delta|\gcd(w_{i});
the we can consider the morphism

a_{{\boldsymbol{w}}/\delta}:(x_{i})\in X^{n}\longmapsto\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{w_{i}}{\delta}a_{X}(x_{i}).

Composing with the evaluation map, we define

(5)

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\kappa^{\delta}:&\mathcal{M}&\longrightarrow&T^{\delta}(L,\beta)\subset\operatorname{Alb}(X)\\
&(f:C\to X,(p_{j})_{j=1}^{m},(q_{i})_{i=1}^{n},\mathcal{O}_{C}(\alpha)\rvert_{[f]}\cong^{\lambda}f^{*}L)&\longmapsto&\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{w_{i}}{\delta}a_{X}(x_{i})\end{array}.

In fact,
\kappa^{\delta}
composed with the multiplication by
\delta
is the previously defined
\kappa
. As
\kappa
is constant equal to
\varphi_{\beta}(L)
by Proposition
3.4
, this ensures that
\kappa^{\delta}
has values in
T^{\delta}(L,\beta)
, the set of
\delta
-roots of
\varphi_{\beta}(L)
. The latter is a torsor under the finite group
\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(\operatorname{Alb}(X))
of
\delta
-torsion elements in
\operatorname{Alb}(X)
. In the particular case where
L=\mathcal{O}_{X}
,
T^{\delta}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
is exactly the set of
\delta
-torsion elements in the Albanese variety
\operatorname{Alb}(X)
.

As
L
does not possess any canonical
\delta
-root, Proposition
3.4
no longer applies for
\kappa^{\delta}
, which has no reason to be constant anymore. The morphism
\kappa^{\delta}
defined in (
5
) thus determines a decomposition of the moduli space of logarithmic maps to
\mathbb{P}_{X}(\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus L)
into components, according to the value taken by
\kappa^{\delta}
. This refinement is called

, as it stems from relations between the images of the points inside the Albanese variety. The image
\kappa^{\delta}(f)
is called a

and is usually denoted by
\theta
.

Remark 3.7
.

The preimages
(\kappa^{\delta})^{-1}(\theta)
of elements
\theta\in T^{\delta}(L,\beta)
are called components of the moduli space but they are not necessarily connected.

\blacklozenge

In particular it follows from the discussion above that we have the following:

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be the moduli space of logarithmic stable maps to

with the divisorial log structure defined by

Assume that

and that
\delta|\gcd(w_{i}).

Then we have a splitting of the virtual class:

[\mathcal{M}]^{\mathrm{vir}}=\sum_{\theta\in T^{\delta}(L,\beta)}[\mathcal{M}^{\theta}]^{\mathrm{vir}}

where
T^{\delta}(L,\beta)
is the
\operatorname{Tor}_{\delta}(\operatorname{Alb}(X))
-torsor of
\delta
-roots of
\varphi_{\beta}(L).

Definition 3.9
.

We encompass the information of the correlated virtual classes in the
full correlated virtual class
:

[\![\mathcal{M}]\!]^{\delta}=\sum_{\theta\in T^{\delta}(L,\beta)}[\mathcal{M}^{\theta}]^{\mathrm{vir}}\cdot(\theta),

which is an element in the group algebra
\mathbb{Q}[\operatorname{Alb}(X)]
with cycle coefficients and support over the torsor

.

3.2.3.

The projection

and

with

\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X)
to itself. We thus have

\mu_{\delta}^{*}T^{\delta}_{B}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})=\{(L,\theta)\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X)\times\operatorname{Alb}(X)\text{ s.t. }\;\delta\theta=\varphi_{\beta}(L^{\otimes\delta})\in\operatorname{Alb}(X)\}.

This torsor now has a section provided by
L\mapsto\theta_{0}(L)=\varphi_{\beta}(L)
.
Deformation invariance tells us that for any choice of
R
, the correlators
\theta_{0}(L)
and
\theta_{0}(L\otimes R)
provide the same invariants. However, the latter are correlators for the a priori distinct
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundles associated to
L^{\otimes\delta}
and
L^{\otimes\delta}\otimes R^{\otimes\delta}
. Assuming
R
is of
\delta
-torsion, these
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundles are the same. Furthermore, the correlators differ by

\theta_{0}(L\otimes R)-\theta_{0}(L)=\varphi_{\beta}(L\otimes R)-\varphi(L)=\varphi_{\beta}(R),

yielding the result.
∎

Finally, we have the following relations between the classes corresponding to different levels of refinement.

Lemma 3.12
.

The multiplication by
d
in
\operatorname{Alb}(X)
induces a morphism of
\mathbb{Q}[\operatorname{Alb}(X)]
denoted by
\mathfrak{m}\left[d\right]
. The different full refined virtual classes are related as follows:

\mathfrak{m}\left[\frac{\delta}{\delta^{\prime}}\right]\left([\![\mathcal{M}]\!]^{\delta}\right)=[\![\mathcal{M}]\!]^{\delta^{\prime}}.

4.2.
Toward the refined degeneration formula

For
\mathcal{Y}_{t}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
a degeneration coming from a divided tropical line, the interpretation of the moduli space given in Corollary
2.15
allows us to define the refinement morphism:

\kappa_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}\colon\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{Y}_{t}/\penalty\mathbb{A}^{1})\to\operatorname{Alb}(\mathcal{X}/\penalty\mathbb{A}^{1})

as the Albanese evaluation, i.e.

(\mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{f}\mathcal{X},(p_{j})_{j=1}^{m},(q_{i})_{i=1}^{n},f^{*}\mathcal{L}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{C}}(\alpha))\rightarrow\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}a_{\mathcal{X}/\penalty\mathbb{A}^{1}}(f\circ q_{i}).

The same argument given in the second part of Proposition
3.4
to compute the
\kappa(s)
in the case of a semi-stable curve
allows us to show that :

\kappa_{0}\colon\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{Y}_{0}/\penalty\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}))\to\operatorname{Alb}(\mathcal{X}_{0})

is the constant morphism
\varphi_{\beta_{0}}(\mathcal{L}_{0})
. Therefore, as in the case of smooth one parameter families we have that if
\delta
divides
\gcd(w_{i})
there is a morphism:

\kappa_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}^{\delta}\colon\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{Y}_{t}/\penalty\mathbb{A}^{1})\to T^{\delta}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L}).

Up to passing to an étale neighbourhoud of
0\in\mathbb{A}^{1}
we can assume that
T^{\delta}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
trivializes.
In particular we obtain a decomposition of
\mathcal{M}_{0}=\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{Y}_{0}/\penalty\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}))
into connected components indexed by
\theta\in T^{\delta}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})\rvert_{0}=T^{\delta}(\mathcal{X}_{0},\mathcal{L}_{0})
:

[\mathcal{M}_{0}]^{\mathrm{vir}}=\sum_{\theta\in T^{\delta}(\mathcal{X}_{0},\mathcal{L}_{0})}[\mathcal{M}_{0}^{\theta}]^{\mathrm{vir}}.

Our goal is now to prove a decomposition formula for the components
[\mathcal{M}_{0}^{\theta}]^{vir}.

Let
\Gamma
be a degeneration graph; for each vertex
V
let
\delta_{V}
is the g.c.d of the weights of the edges adjacent to
V
. Then, as discussed in Section

[\mathcal{M}_{V}]^{\mathrm{vir}}=\sum_{\theta_{V}\in T^{\delta_{V}}}[\mathcal{M}_{V}^{\theta_{V}}]^{\mathrm{vir}},

where

is the torsor providing the refinement for
Y_{\phi(V)}
. We aim to describe the components
[\mathcal{M}_{0}^{\theta}]^{\mathrm{vir}}
in terms of the refined components
\mathcal{M}_{V}^{\theta_{V}}
.

Remark 4.4
.

Clearly several choices of
{\underline{\theta}}=(\theta_{V})
may contribute to a common

on the smooth fiber. On the other hand, as we will see shortly, a fixed collection
{\underline{\theta}}=(\theta_{V})
may contribute to several distinct

and should thus not be considered as a further refinement of the
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{\theta}.

\blacklozenge

For this reason, the best way to express the refined degeneration formula is in term of
full correlated virtual class
(see Definition

[\![\mathcal{M}]\!]^{\delta}=\sum_{\theta\in T^{\delta}(L,\beta)}[\mathcal{M}^{\theta}]^{\mathrm{vir}}\cdot(\theta)\in H_{\bullet}(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{Q})\otimes\mathbb{Q}[\operatorname{Alb}(X)]

In the group algebra
\mathbb{Q}[\operatorname{Alb}(X)]
, we also have the division operators defined as follows on generators:

\mathfrak{d}\left[\frac{1}{d}\right](\theta)=\frac{1}{d^{2r}}\sum_{d\theta^{\prime}\equiv\theta}(\theta^{\prime}),

where

\mathfrak{d}\left[\frac{1}{d}\right]
maps an element to the average of its

Lemma 4.5

Let

\frac{\delta}{\delta_{\Gamma}}a_{{\boldsymbol{w}}/\delta}(x)=\sum_{V}\frac{\delta_{V}}{\delta_{\Gamma}}\theta_{V}.

Proof.

\displaystyle\frac{\delta}{\delta_{\Gamma}}a_{{\boldsymbol{w}}/\delta}(x)=\frac{\delta}{\delta_{\Gamma}}\sum_{e\;\text{leg}}\frac{w_{e}}{\delta}x_{e}=\sum_{e\;\text{leg}}\frac{w_{e}}{\delta_{\Gamma}}x_{e}=

\displaystyle\sum_{V}\sum_{e\vdash V}\frac{w_{e}}{\delta_{\Gamma}}x_{V,e}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{V}\frac{\delta_{V}}{\delta_{\Gamma}}a_{V}(x_{V})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{V}\frac{\delta_{V}}{\delta_{\Gamma}}\theta_{V}.

∎

Theorem 4.7

In the notation of this section, we have a refined decomposition

[\mathcal{M}_{0}^{\theta}]^{\mathrm{vir}}=\sum_{\widetilde{\Gamma}}\sum_{{\underline{\theta}}}\dfrac{l_{\Gamma}}{|E(\Gamma)|!}F_{*}\phi_{\Gamma}^{*}\Delta^{{{\underline{\theta}},\theta},!}\left(\prod_{V}[\mathcal{M}_{V}^{\theta_{V}}]^{vir}\right),

The proof follows from the usual decomposition formula and Lemma

for

4.4.
Degeneration in the elliptic case

We first prove the following lemma.

Lemma 4.8
.

The diagonal components
{\boldsymbol{D}}^{{\underline{\theta}},\theta}
indexed by
\theta\in a_{{\boldsymbol{w}}/\delta}({\boldsymbol{D}}^{\underline{\theta}})
are cobordant in
{\boldsymbol{D}}
(and thus in
{\boldsymbol{X}}
). In other words,
\iota_{*}[{\boldsymbol{D}}^{{\underline{\theta}},\theta}]
does not depend on the choice of
\theta\in a_{{\boldsymbol{w}}/\delta}({\boldsymbol{D}}^{\underline{\theta}})
.

Proof.

Let
\theta\in a_{{\boldsymbol{w}}/\delta}({\boldsymbol{D}}^{\underline{\theta}})
and
\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t)\in\operatorname{Alb}(X)^{|\mathcal{V}(\Gamma)|}
be a loop based at
{\underline{\theta}}
. The idea is to use the loop
\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t)
to construct a cobordism between the different components
{\boldsymbol{D}}^{{\underline{\theta}},\theta}
. We claim that there is a unique path
\gamma(t)\in\operatorname{Alb}(X)
such that
\gamma(0)=\theta
and in
\operatorname{Alb}(X)
we have

\frac{\delta}{\delta_{\Gamma}}\gamma(t)=\sum\frac{\delta_{V}}{\delta_{\Gamma}}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{V}(t).

To construct it, let
\widetilde{\theta}
be a lift of
\theta
in the universal cover
H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})^{*}
of
\operatorname{Alb}(X)
, and consider the loop
\sum_{V}\frac{\delta_{V}}{\delta_{\Gamma}}\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{V}(t)
in
\operatorname{Alb}(X)
. We can lift the loop to a path in
H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})^{*}
starting at
\frac{\delta}{\delta_{\Gamma}}\widetilde{\theta}
. This path can be written as follows:

t\mapsto\frac{\delta}{\delta_{\Gamma}}\widetilde{\theta}+\rho(t),

where
\rho(0)=0
and
\rho(1)\in H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})\subset H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})^{*}
. The path
\rho
is determined by
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
. It corresponds to a loop in
\operatorname{Alb}(X)
, and its class
[\rho]=\rho(1)\in H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})\subset H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})^{*}
satisfies
[\rho]=\Sigma_{*}(\boldsymbol{\gamma})
, where
\Sigma_{*}:H_{1}(\operatorname{Alb}(X)^{|\mathcal{V}(\Gamma)|},\mathbb{Z})\to H_{1}(\operatorname{Alb}(X),\mathbb{Z})
is induced by
\Sigma:(\theta^{\prime}_{V})\mapsto\sum\frac{\delta_{V}}{\delta_{\Gamma}}\theta^{\prime}_{V}
.
Finally, we divide by
\delta/\delta_{\Gamma}
: the path we care about is the image in
\operatorname{Alb}(X)
of
t\mapsto\widetilde{\theta}+\frac{1}{\delta/\delta_{\Gamma}}\rho(t)
.
In particular,
\gamma(t)\in\operatorname{Alb}(X)
may not be a loop.

Starting with a generic path
\boldsymbol{\gamma}(t)
, the path
\gamma(t)\in\operatorname{Alb}(X)
is also generic. Therefore, its preimage by
a_{{\boldsymbol{w}}/\delta}|_{{\boldsymbol{D}}}:{\boldsymbol{D}}\to\operatorname{Alb}(X)
provides a cobordism in
{\boldsymbol{D}}
between
{\boldsymbol{D}}^{{\underline{\theta}},\gamma(0)}
and
{\boldsymbol{D}}^{{\underline{\theta}},\gamma(1)}
.

To finish the proof, we need to prove that
\gamma(1)
may take any value in
a_{{\boldsymbol{w}}/\delta}({\boldsymbol{D}}^{\underline{\theta}})
. Equivalently, we need to show that any element of
\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta/\delta_{\Gamma}}(\operatorname{Alb}(X))
has a lift in
H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X})^{*}
of the form
\frac{1}{\delta/\delta_{\Gamma}}\rho(1)
, with
\rho
as above. This amounts to the surjectivity of
\Sigma_{*}
. The latter is ensured by the fact that the g.c.d. of
\delta_{V}/\delta_{\Gamma}
is
1
, finishing the proof.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

The key point in the proof is the surjectivity. Otherwise, the elements in the torsor would split in different classes modulo the image of the morphism.

\blacklozenge

Remark 4.10
.

This Lemma is easier to prove in the elliptic case since all maps are actually group morphisms, so that the various components are in fact parallel subtori.

\blacklozenge

Now, let us work under the additional assumption that the
\iota^{*}:H^{\bullet}(X^{n_{V}},\mathbb{Q})\to H^{\bullet}(\mathcal{H}_{V}^{\theta_{V}},\mathbb{Q})
are surjective. This hypothesis is satisfied when
X
is an elliptic curve, since each
\mathcal{H}_{V}^{\theta_{V}}
is now a subtorus of
X^{n_{V}}
.
Using Lemma
4.8
, we can now prove the following:

Lemma 4.11
.

Given
\gamma\in H^{\bullet}({\boldsymbol{X}},\mathbb{Q})
and
[\mathcal{N}]
a cycle class in
H_{\bullet}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}^{\underline{\theta}},\mathbb{Q})
, the intersection numbers

\widetilde{\mathrm{ev}}_{*}[\mathcal{N}]\cap(({\boldsymbol{D}}^{{\underline{\theta}},\theta})^{\vee}\cup\iota^{*}\gamma)

do not depend on
\theta\in a_{{\boldsymbol{w}}/\delta}({\boldsymbol{D}}^{\underline{\theta}})
:

Proof.

If
L
is chosen generically, every choice of
{\underline{\theta}}
is also generic and
\mathcal{H}_{V}^{\theta_{V}}
is thus a submanifold of
X^{n_{V}}
. In particular, we have Poincaré duality. Let
\mu
be the class Poincaré dual to
\widetilde{\mathrm{ev}}_{*}[\mathcal{N}]
inside
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}^{\underline{\theta}}
, so that we have

\widetilde{\mathrm{ev}}_{*}[\mathcal{N}]\cap(({\boldsymbol{D}}^{{\underline{\theta}},\theta})^{\vee}\cup\iota^{*}\gamma)=[\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}^{\underline{\theta}}]\cap(({\boldsymbol{D}}^{{\underline{\theta}},\theta})^{\vee}\cup\mu\cup\iota^{*}\gamma)=[{\boldsymbol{D}}^{{\underline{\theta}},\theta}]\cap(\mu\cup\iota^{*}\gamma),

since
({\boldsymbol{D}}^{{\underline{\theta}},\theta})^{\vee}
is Poincaré dual to
{\boldsymbol{D}}^{{\underline{\theta}},\theta}
inside
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}^{\underline{\theta}}
. By surjectivity, we can write
\mu=\iota^{*}\widetilde{\mu}
. Moreover, as we compute an intersection number, we may as well compute its push-forward inside
H_{0}({\boldsymbol{X}},\mathbb{Q})
and use push-pull formula, so that the number we care about is

\iota_{*}\left([{\boldsymbol{D}}^{{\underline{\theta}},\theta}]\cap\iota^{*}(\widetilde{\mu}\cup\gamma)\right)=\iota_{*}[{\boldsymbol{D}}^{{\underline{\theta}},\theta}]\cap(\widetilde{\mu}\cup\gamma).

As by Lemma
4.8

\iota_{*}[{\boldsymbol{D}}^{{\underline{\theta}},\theta}]
does not depend on
\theta
, we get that the intersection numbers do not depend on the particular choice of
\theta\in a_{{\boldsymbol{w}}/\delta}({\boldsymbol{D}}^{\underline{\theta}})
.
∎

We may now state the degeneration formula under the surjectivity assumption.

Theorem 4.12
.

Consider a degeneration
\mathcal{Y}_{t}
of ruled surfaces over
X
whose central fiber is a union of ruled surfaces glued over their boundary divisors. Under the surjectivity assumption, we have the following decomposition of the virtual classes:

[\![\mathcal{M}_{0}]\!]^{\delta}\equiv\sum_{\Gamma}\sum_{\underline{\theta}}\frac{\prod w_{e}}{|\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)|}\left(\Delta^{!}\prod_{V}[\mathcal{M}_{V}^{\theta_{V}}]^{\mathrm{vir}}\right)\mathfrak{d}\left[\frac{1}{\delta/\delta_{\Gamma}}\right]\left(\sum\frac{\delta_{V}}{\delta_{\Gamma}}\theta_{V}\right),

where
\Delta\colon{\boldsymbol{D}}\to{\boldsymbol{X}}
is the usual diagonal inclusion.

The “
\equiv
" means that the equality is true when we compute the intersection with the pull-back of a cohomology class by the evaluation map.

Proof.

We just rebrand Theorem
4.7
using Lemma
4.11
to show that both classes have the same intersection numbers with classes of the form
\iota^{*}\gamma
. Let
[\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}^{\underline{\theta}}]^{\mathrm{vir}}=\prod[\mathcal{M}_{V}^{\theta_{V}}]^{\mathrm{vir}}
and
r
be the dimension of
\operatorname{Alb}(X)
, so that the cardinal of
\delta/\delta_{\Gamma}
-torsion elements is
(\delta/\delta_{\Gamma})^{2r}
. Intersection numbers in the (co)homology of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}^{\underline{\theta}}
between a class in
H_{\bullet}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}^{\underline{\theta}},\mathbb{Q})
, some
({\boldsymbol{D}}^{{\underline{\theta}},\theta})^{\vee}
and some
\iota^{*}\gamma
can be computed in the (co)homology of
{\boldsymbol{X}}
by push-pull-formula. By Lemma
4.11
, the results do not depend on the specific choice of
\theta
. Therefore, they are equal to their average:

\displaystyle\iota_{*}\left(\widetilde{\mathrm{ev}}_{*}[\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}^{\underline{\theta}}]^{\mathrm{vir}}\cap(({\boldsymbol{D}}^{{\underline{\theta}},\theta})^{\vee}\cup\iota^{*}\gamma)\right)=

\displaystyle\iota_{*}\left(\widetilde{\mathrm{ev}}_{*}[\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}^{\underline{\theta}}]^{\mathrm{vir}}\cap\left(\frac{1}{(\delta/\delta_{\Gamma})^{2r}}\sum_{\theta}({\boldsymbol{D}}^{{\underline{\theta}},\theta})^{\vee}\cup\iota^{*}\gamma\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\delta/\delta_{\Gamma})^{2r}}\iota_{*}\left(\widetilde{\mathrm{ev}}_{*}[\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}^{\underline{\theta}}]^{\mathrm{vir}}\cap(\iota^{*}{\boldsymbol{D}}^{\vee}\cup\iota^{*}\gamma\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\delta/\delta_{\Gamma})^{2r}}\mathrm{ev}_{*}[\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}^{\underline{\theta}}]^{\mathrm{vir}}\cap\left({\boldsymbol{D}}^{\vee}\cup\gamma\right).

As the
\frac{1}{(\delta/\delta_{\Gamma})^{2r}}
are precisely the coefficients appearing through the use of
\mathfrak{d}\left[\frac{1}{\delta/\delta_{\Gamma}}\right]
and its support the
\theta
we care about, we get the result.
∎

Corollary 4.13
.

Under the surjectivity assumption, the full correlated virtual classes satisfy the following decomposition formula:

[\![\mathcal{M}_{0}]\!]^{\delta}\equiv\sum_{\Gamma}\frac{\prod w_{e}}{|\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)|}\mathfrak{d}\left[\frac{1}{\delta/\delta_{\Gamma}}\right]\Delta^{!}\prod_{V}[\![\mathcal{M}_{V}]\!]^{\delta_{\Gamma}}.

Proof.

We rewrite Theorem
4.12
to make appear the full refined classes at the vertex level:

\displaystyle\prod_{V}[\![\mathcal{M}_{V}]\!]^{\delta_{\Gamma}}=

\displaystyle\prod_{V}\mathfrak{m}\left[\frac{\delta_{V}}{\delta_{\Gamma}}\right]\left(\sum_{\theta_{V}}[\mathcal{M}_{V}^{\theta_{V}}]^{\mathrm{vir}}\cdot(\theta_{V})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{V}\sum_{\theta_{V}}[\mathcal{M}_{V}^{\theta_{V}}]^{\mathrm{vir}}\cdot\left(\frac{\delta_{V}}{\delta_{\Gamma}}\theta_{V}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{{\underline{\theta}}}\prod_{V}[\mathcal{M}_{V}^{\theta_{V}}]^{\mathrm{vir}}\cdot\left(\sum\frac{\delta_{V}}{\delta_{\Gamma}}\theta_{V}\right),

yielding the result.
∎

Remark 4.14
.

The formula tells us that even if each vertex is entitled to a refinement at the level
\delta_{V}
, to recover the refinement for the global degeneration graph at the level
\delta
, we only need to know the refinements at the level
\delta_{\Gamma}
. Moreover, due to the presence of
\mathfrak{d}\left[\frac{1}{\delta/\delta_{\Gamma}}\right]
, the class associated to the graph
\Gamma
is invariant under
\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta/\delta_{\Gamma}}(\operatorname{Alb}(X))
. In particular, if
\delta_{\Gamma}=1
, all the correlated classes coming from
\Gamma
yield the same invariants.

5.
Computation of local invariants

5.1.
General considerations and statement

Our goal in this section is to compute the correlated GW invariants in the case where
X=E
is an elliptic curve, the genus of the source curve is
g=1
, and with a unique interior point constraint. These
local invariants
will be used in combination with Theorem
4.12
to obtain an explicit computation algorithm and derive regularity results for general correlated invariants in Section
6
.

We consider
Y=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}\oplus L)
for
L\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(E);
fix an homology class
\beta=a[E]\in H_{2}(E,\mathbb{Z})
and a vector
{\boldsymbol{w}}=(w_{1},\cdots,w_{n})
of tangency orders. The moduli space

\mathcal{M}(a,{\boldsymbol{w}})=\mathcal{M}_{1,1}(Y|D^{\pm},a[E],{\boldsymbol{w}}),

is the moduli space of log stable maps as in the previous section.
For
\delta|\mathrm{gcd}(w_{i})
we saw that it decomposes into components
\mathcal{M}^{\theta}(a,{\boldsymbol{w}}).

The correlators
\theta
satisfy

\delta\theta\equiv\varphi_{a[E]}(L)=a\lambda,

where
\lambda=\varphi_{[E]}(L)
is the image of the line bundle
L\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(E)\simeq E
through the isomorphism
\varphi_{[E]}
. We denote the torsor by
T^{\delta}(L,a)
.

5.1.1.
Uncorrelated case

We recall the computation of the non-refined invariant:

\langle\mathrm{pt}_{0},1_{w_{1}},\mathrm{pt}_{w_{2}},\cdots,\mathrm{pt}_{w_{n}}\rangle_{1,a[E],{\boldsymbol{w}}}=\int_{[\mathcal{M}(a,{\boldsymbol{w}})]^{\mathrm{vir}}}\mathrm{ev}_{0}^{*}(\mathrm{pt})\prod_{2}^{n}\mathrm{ev}_{i}^{*}(\mathrm{pt}).

Lemma 5.1
.

The uncorrelated relative GW invariant has the following value:

\langle\mathrm{pt}_{0},1_{w_{1}},\mathrm{pt}_{w_{2}},\cdots,\mathrm{pt}_{w_{n}}\rangle_{1,a[E],{\boldsymbol{w}}}=a^{n-1}\sigma(a)\cdot w_{1}^{2},

where
\sigma(a)=\sum_{d|a}d
is the sum of divisors function.

Proof.

This elementary formula is computed for instance in
[
Blo23
]
. Briefly, it may be obtained as follows. Genus
1
parametrized curves in
Y
realizing the class
a[E]+b({\boldsymbol{w}})[\mathbb{P}^{1}]
come from degree
a
covering maps
f:C\to E
. Up to translation, these are group homomorphisms. We may fix the translation parameter using the marked point
p_{0}
, mapped to a fixed point in
E
.
Then we see that these are in bijection with the index
a
sublattices of
\pi_{1}(E)\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, of which there are
\sigma(a)
.

Given one of the
\sigma(a)
covers
f:C\to E
, enhancing to a map to
Y=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}\oplus L)
amounts to find a section of
f^{*}L
, with poles and zeros of prescribed order
w_{i}
and fixed image in
E
.

We denote by
y_{i}
the marked points in
C
(with not trivial contact order) and let
x_{i}=f(y_{i})
their image in
E
. For
2\leqslant i\leqslant n
, there are
a
possible choices for each
y_{i}
given that
x_{i}
is fixed. The position of
y_{1}
is determined by the relation
\sum w_{i}y_{i}\equiv f^{*}(\lambda)
in
C
, i.e.
y_{1}
is a
w_{1}
-root of
f^{*}(\lambda)-\sum_{i=2}^{n}w_{i}y_{i}.

There are
w_{1}^{2}
possible choices for
y_{1}
, yielding the result.
∎

5.1.2.
Statement

We now consider the correlated invariants

\langle\langle\mathrm{pt}_{0},1_{w_{1}},\mathrm{pt}_{w_{2}},\cdots,\mathrm{pt}_{w_{n}}\rangle\rangle^{\delta}_{1,\beta,{\boldsymbol{w}}}=\int_{[\![\mathcal{M}(a,{\boldsymbol{w}})]\!]^{\delta}}\mathrm{ev}_{0}^{*}(\mathrm{pt})\prod_{2}^{n}\mathrm{ev}_{i}^{*}(\mathrm{pt}),

which is an element of the group algebra
\mathbb{Q}[E]
with support on
T^{\delta}(L,a)
.

Before giving a closed formula for the full local correlated invariant we need to introduce certain functions with values in
\mathbb{Q}[E].

First, we consider the average of torsion elements:

\vartheta_{d}:=\frac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{d\theta\equiv 0}(\theta)\in\mathbb{Q}[E]

They satisfy

\vartheta_{d_{1}}\vartheta_{d_{2}}=\vartheta_{\mathrm{lcm}(d_{1},d_{2})}

We then define the following functions: for
d|\delta
,

\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{\delta}(d)=\prod_{p}\left(\vartheta_{p^{\nu_{p}(d)}}-\mathds{1}_{\nu_{p}(d)<\nu_{p}(\delta)}\vartheta_{p^{\nu_{p}(d)+1}}\right),

where the products is over primes and
\nu_{p}
is the
p
-adic valuation. The
\mathds{1}
means the second term only appears if
\nu_{p}(d)<\nu_{p}(\delta)
. So
\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{\delta}(d)
essentially depends on
d
, but we only care for the values when
d
divides
\delta
, with a slight modification of the expression if
d
maximizes some
p
-adic valuation.

Example 5.2
.

If
d=\delta
, so that
\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{\delta}(\delta)=\vartheta_{\delta}
. If
\delta=p^{v}
is the power of a prime number
p
, the values of
\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{p^{v}}
for its divisors
1,p,\dots,p^{v-1},p^{v}
are

\vartheta_{1}-\vartheta_{p},\vartheta_{p}-\vartheta_{p^{2}},\dots,\vartheta_{p^{v-1}}-\vartheta_{p^{v}}\text{ and }\vartheta_{p^{v}}.

\lozenge

Next, we consider the variants of the arithmetic function
\sigma(a)=\sum_{d|a}d
defined as follows:
\overline{\sigma}^{d}(a)=\sigma(a/d)
if
d|a
and
0
else. Combining both, we define the following function, with values in
\mathbb{Q}[\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(E)]
:

\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\delta}(a)=\sum_{d|\delta}\overline{\sigma}^{\delta/d}(a)\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{\delta}(d).

For concrete computations, it may be convinient to express
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\delta}(a)
as a linear combination of the
\vartheta_{d}
for
d|\delta
but with different coefficients. To do so, consider the following function on
\mathbb{N}
.

\Upsilon^{\delta}_{d}(a)=\prod_{p}\left(\overline{\sigma}^{p^{\nu_{p}(d)}}-\mathds{1}_{\nu_{p}(d)<\nu_{p}(\delta)}\overline{\sigma}^{p^{\nu_{p}(d)+1}}\right)(p^{\nu_{p}(a)}).

In the above product of function, the argument is factorized over prime numbers, so that
\overline{\sigma}^{d_{1}d_{2}}=\overline{\sigma}^{d_{1}}\overline{\sigma}^{d_{2}}
for
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
coprime. The following identity is proven in the proof of Theorem
5.3
. Thanks to multiplicativity, it suffices to check it for powers of primes, where it stems from a summation by part:

\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\delta}(a)=\sum_{d|\delta}\Upsilon_{d}^{\delta}(a)\vartheta_{\delta/d}.

Doing a summation by parts for each prime numbers is called a
multiplicative summation by parts
.

The torsor consists of
\delta
-roots of
a\lambda
. However,
a\lambda
has no canonical
\delta
-root. The furthest we can naturally define is the
\gcd(a,\delta)
-root
\frac{a\lambda}{\gcd(a,\delta)}
. Then, pick
\theta_{0}
to be any choice of root such that
\frac{\delta}{\gcd(a,\delta)}\theta_{0}=\frac{a}{\gcd(a,\delta)}\lambda
. Theorem
3.11
ensures that the result does not depend on the choice of the latter. This correlators are called
special correlators
.

One way to construct special correlators is as follows: if
\lambda_{0}
satisfies
\delta\lambda_{0}=\lambda
, one may take
\theta_{0}=a\lambda_{0}
. Indeed, one has

\frac{\delta}{\gcd(a,\delta)}(a\lambda_{0})=\frac{a}{\gcd(a,\delta)}\delta\lambda_{0}=\frac{a}{\gcd(a,\delta)}\lambda.

Theorem 5.3
.

The full local correlated invariant has the following expression:

(8)

\langle\langle\mathrm{pt}_{0},1_{w_{1}},\mathrm{pt}_{w_{2}},\cdots,\mathrm{pt}_{w_{n}}\rangle\rangle^{\delta}_{1,a[E],{\boldsymbol{w}}}=a^{n-1}w_{1}^{2}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\delta}(a)\cdot(\theta_{0}),

where
\theta_{0}
is any special correlator , i.e. satisfies
\frac{\delta}{\gcd(a,\delta)}\theta_{0}=\frac{a}{\gcd(a,\delta)}\lambda
.

Without assuming Theorem
5.3
, it can be checked from the definition of
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
that

(9)

\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\delta}(a)=\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\delta}(a)\vartheta_{\delta/\gcd(a,\delta)},

so that the right-hand side of (
8
) does not depend on which
\theta_{0}
we choose. It may thus be replaced by
\vartheta_{\delta/\gcd(a,\delta)}\cdot(\theta_{0})=\mathfrak{d}\left[\frac{1}{\delta/\gcd(a,\delta)}\right]\left(\frac{a}{\gcd(a,\delta)}\lambda\right)
.

Assuming instead Theorem
5.3
, Equation (
9
) is in fact a consequence from Theorem
3.11
, which tells us that the correlated invariant is invariant when multiplying by an element of

\varphi_{a[E]}(\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(E))=a\cdot\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(E)=\mathrm{Tor}_{a/\gcd(a,\delta)}(E).

See the second part of the proof of Lemma
5.7
for a proof of the second equality. In particular, it is also invariant by multiplication by
\vartheta_{\delta/\gcd(a,\delta)}
.

With the above formulation, the correlated invariant appears as a refined version of the uncorrelated invariant, and the refinement takes the form of
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\delta}
replacing
\sigma
.

The rest of the section is dedicated to the proof of Theorem
5.3
, by refining the proof of Lemma
5.1
. We proceed in several steps. The first is to study the curves coming from a common covering map
f:C\to E
. Summing over covering maps in Section
5.3
, we are then able to find a closed expression for the
(\theta_{0})
-coefficient. Multiplicativity properties and an induction relation are thus sufficient to prove the formula from Theorem
5.3
.

5.2.
Contribution of a fixed cover

Let us consider a fixed cover
f:C\to E
, which is a group homomorphism choosing the marked point
p_{0}
and its image as neutral element. Its kernel
\ker f
has cardinal
a
, the degree of the covering. Let
f^{*}:E\to C
be the dual map between the curves seen as their Picard groups. The lifting condition writes itself
\sum_{1}^{n}w_{j}y_{j}=f^{*}(\lambda)
, where
\lambda\in E
corresponds to the line bundle
L
. We have the following group morphism:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}C^{n}&\longrightarrow&E^{n-1}\times C,\\
(k_{j})&\longmapsto&\left(f(k_{2}),\dots,f(k_{n}),\sum_{1}^{n}w_{j}k_{j}\right)\end{array}

with its kernel
K(f)=\left\{(k_{j})\in C\times(\ker f)^{n-1}\text{ s.t. }\sum_{1}^{n}w_{j}k_{j}=0\right\}
. In particular, it sits in the following exact sequence, from which we see it has cardinal
w_{1}^{2}a^{n-1}
:

0\to\mathrm{Tor}_{w_{1}}(C)\to K(f)\to(\ker f)^{n-1}\to 0.

For the cover
f
, the set of curves matching the constraints
(x_{j})
is in bijection with the following
K(f)
-torsor:

S_{\lambda,x_{2},\dots,x_{n}}=\left\{(y_{j})\in C^{n}\text{ s.t. }\forall\ 2\leqslant j\leqslant n\ f(y_{j})=x_{j}\text{ and }\sum_{1}^{n}w_{j}y_{j}=f^{*}(\lambda)\in C\right\},

also having cardinal
w_{1}^{2}a^{n-1}
. We now wish to refine the above description. To do so, we use the correlator function

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\kappa^{\delta}:&C^{n}&\longrightarrow&E.\\
&(y_{j})&\longmapsto&\sum_{1}^{n}\frac{w_{j}}{\delta}f(y_{j})\end{array}

The correlators are the elements
\theta\in E
satisfying
\delta\theta=f_{*}f^{*}(\lambda)=a\lambda
. Among them, recall we have the
special correlators
satisfying
\frac{\delta}{\gcd(a,\delta)}\theta_{0}=\frac{a}{\gcd(a,\delta)}\lambda
and that
a\lambda_{0}
, where
\delta\lambda_{0}=\lambda
is a special correlator.

Lemma 5.7
.

The image of
K(f)
via the correlator function is
\kappa^{\delta}(K(f))=f(\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(C))
. Furthermore, it contains
\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta/\gcd(a,\delta)}(E)
.

Proof.

The equality follows from the definitions. Assume that
(k_{j})\in K(f)
. Then
\sum_{1}^{n}w_{j}k_{j}=0
and it follows that
\sum_{1}^{n}\frac{w_{j}}{\delta}k_{j}\in\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(C)
and consequently
\kappa^{\delta}((k_{j}))=f\left(\sum_{1}^{n}\frac{w_{j}}{\delta}k_{j}\right)\in f(\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(C))
. Conversely, let
f(s)\in f(\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(C))
with
s\in\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(C)
. Let
k_{1}
be such that
s=\frac{w_{1}}{\delta}k_{1}
, which exists because
C
is divisible, and
k_{j}=0\in\ker f
if
j\geqslant 2
. We have

f(s)=f\left(\frac{w_{1}}{\delta}k_{1}\right)=\kappa^{\delta}(k_{1},0,\dots,0)\text{ and }\sum_{1}^{n}w_{j}k_{j}=\delta s=0.

Thus, we have the reverse inclusion
f(\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(C)\subset\kappa^{\delta}(K(f))
.

We now prove that
\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta/\gcd(a,\delta)}(E)\subset f(\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(C))
. Using the dual morphism
f^{*}
we start from
f^{*}(\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(E))\subset\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(C)
.
We now apply the morphism
f
and use that the composition
f\circ f^{*}:E\to E
is the multiplication by
a
, so that
a\cdot\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(E)\subset f(\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(C))
.
We now claim that
a\cdot\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(E)=\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta/\gcd(a,\delta)}(E)
, which concludes the proof:

\triangleright

If
x
is
\delta
-torsion, we have
\frac{\delta}{\gcd(a,\delta)}ax=\frac{a}{\gcd(a,\delta)}\delta x=0
, so that we have the inclusion
a\cdot\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(E)\subset\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta/\gcd(a,\delta)}(E)
.

\triangleright

As
|\mathrm{Tor}_{u}(E)|=u^{2}
, the short exact sequence

0\to\mathrm{Tor}_{a}(E)\cap\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(E)=\mathrm{Tor}_{\gcd(a,\delta)}(E)\to\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(E)\xrightarrow{a\cdot}a\cdot\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(E)\to 0,

ensures that they have the same cardinal
\left(\frac{\delta}{\gcd(a,\delta)}\right)^{2}
.

∎

The following proposition gives a description of the correlators in the image of the torsor
S_{\lambda,x_{2},\dots,x_{n}}
.

Proposition 5.8
.

The correlators achieved by the solutions, i.e. the set
\kappa^{\delta}(S_{\lambda,x_{2},\dots,x_{n}}),
form a
f(\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(C))
-torsor which contains the special correlators
\theta_{0}
. Solutions are uniformely spread among the correlators.

Proof.

The solutions form the
K(f)
-torsor
S_{\lambda,x_{2},\dots,x_{n}}
. Applying the correlator function, we immediately get a torsor under
\kappa^{\delta}(K(f))
, equal to
f(\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(C))
by Lemma
5.7
. We also get that the solutions split evenly among the elements.

We now need to prove that it contains the special correlators. Special correlators form a torsor under
\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta/\gcd(a,\delta)}(E)
, which lies in
f(\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(C))
by Lemma
5.7
. Thus, it suffices to show it contains one of them.
Pick
y_{2},\dots,y_{n}
such that
f(y_{j})=x_{j}
and choose
y_{1}
such that
\sum_{1}^{n}\frac{w_{j}}{\delta}y_{j}=f^{*}(\lambda_{0})
, with
\delta\lambda_{0}=\lambda
; notice that this always exists as
C
as well is a divisible group. In particular, as
\delta\lambda_{0}=\lambda
, we have that
\sum_{1}^{n}w_{j}y_{j}=f^{*}(\lambda)
and
(y_{j})
is indeed an element of
S_{\lambda,x_{2},\dots,x_{n}}
. Moreover, we have that

\kappa^{\delta}((y_{j}))=f\circ f^{*}(\lambda_{0})=a\lambda_{0},

which is one of the special correlators. Therefore, all special correlators belong to the image torsor.
∎

To finish this section, we provide a first expression of the local correlated invariant as a sum over the covers
f:C\to E
. The cover
f
corresponds to a sublattice
\Lambda\subset\pi_{1}(E)\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. We can find a basis
(e_{1},e_{2})
such that
\Lambda=\langle ke_{1},(a/k)e_{2}\rangle
for some unique
k
such that
k^{2}|a
. We say that
\Lambda
, and by extension the associated cover, is of type
(k,a/k)
.

Let
\vartheta(f)\in\mathbb{Z}[E]
be the element with coefficient
1
for every element in
f(\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(C))
.

Proposition 5.9
.

The correlated invariant admits the following expression

\langle\langle\mathrm{pt}_{0},1_{w_{1}},\mathrm{pt}_{w_{2}},\dots,\mathrm{pt}_{w_{n}}\rangle\rangle^{\delta}_{1,a[E],{\boldsymbol{w}}}=a^{n-1}\left(\frac{w_{1}}{\delta}\right)^{2}\sum_{f:C\to E}\gcd(k,\delta)\gcd(a/k,\delta)\vartheta(f)\cdot(\theta_{0}).

Proof.

We know by Proposition
5.8
that the
w_{1}^{2}a^{n-1}
solutions for a fixed cover
f:C\to E
uniformely spread among the possible correlators, so that we get some integer multiple of the torsor
\vartheta(f)\cdot(\theta_{0})
indexing the possible correlators. To conclude, we merely need to compute the cardinal of
f(\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(C))
.

The basis
(e_{1},e_{2})
diagonalizing the lattice inclusion provides real coordinates on
E
and
C
such that
f
has the following expression:

\begin{array}[]{rccl}f:&C\simeq(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})^{2}&\longrightarrow&E\simeq(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})^{2},\\
&(u,v)&\longmapsto&\left(ku,(a/k)v\right)\end{array}

from which we see that
|f(\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(C))|=\frac{\delta^{2}}{\gcd(k,\delta)\gcd(a/k,\delta)}
. Therefore, each correlator carries
a^{n-1}\left(\frac{w_{1}}{\delta}\right)^{2}\gcd(k,\delta)\gcd(a/k,\delta)
solutions. Summing over covers yields the result.
∎

Our next goal is to find an expression avoiding the summation over the morphisms
f:C\to E
, i.e. the index
a
sublattices of
\pi_{1}(E)\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

5.3.
Computation of the
(\theta_{0})
-coefficient

The next step toward the proof of Theorem

is to use Proposition

to compute the
(\theta_{0})
-coefficient, where

To do so, we need to know the number of sublattices of
\pi_{1}(E)\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{2}
of a given type, which is precisely the definition of the Dedekind
\psi
-function: it the unique multiplicative function such that for any prime number

\psi(p^{\alpha})=p^{\alpha-1}(p+1)
. There are
\psi(n)
index

\mathbb{Z}^{2}
having type
(1,n)
, which are also known as

sublattices. Merely dividing by
k
, we get that there are
\psi(\frac{a}{k^{2}})
sublattices of type
\left(k,a/k\right)
.

Example 5.10
.

We have
\psi(2)=3
as there are
3
sublattices of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
of index

. If
(e_{1},e_{2})
is a basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, these lattices are
\langle 2e_{1},e_{2}\rangle

\langle e_{1}+e_{2},e_{1}-e_{2}\rangle

\lozenge

Remark 5.11
.

In particular, as there are

This may also be checked using the multiplicativity of

\blacklozenge

Using Proposition
5.9
, we get the following expression.

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
s_{\delta}(a)=\sum_{k^{2}|a}\gcd(k,\delta)\gcd(a/k,\delta)\psi\left(\frac{a}{k^{2}}\right)
. For the level
\delta

a^{n-1}\left(\frac{w}{\delta}\right)^{2}s_{\delta}(a)
.

Proof.

We need to compute the
(0)
-coefficient of
\sum_{f:C\to E}\gcd(k,\delta)\gcd(a/k,\delta)\vartheta(f)
. As each

has by definition coefficient

lattices of type

Before getting to the computation of the full local correlated invariant, we transform the above expression to better suit for our purposes, transforming the sum over
k^{2}|a
into a sum over
d|\delta
using arithmetic functions more convenient than
\psi
.

\triangleright

We already introduced the sum of divisors function
\sigma(a)=\sum_{d|a}d
, whose Dirichlet generating series is
\zeta(s)\zeta(s-1)
.

\triangleright

Given
d\in\mathbb{N}
, the Dirichlet generating series of
\overline{\sigma}^{d}(a)=\mathds{1}_{d|a}\sigma(a/d)
is

\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}\frac{\overline{\sigma}^{d}(a)}{a^{s}}=\sum_{a^{\prime}=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sigma(a^{\prime})}{(da^{\prime})^{s}}=\frac{1}{d^{s}}\zeta(s)\zeta(s-1).

\triangleright

The Dirichlet generating series of the second Jordan function
J_{2}
is

\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\frac{J_{2}(d)}{d^{s}}=\frac{\zeta(s-2)}{\zeta(s)}.

Lemma 5.13
.

The function
s_{\delta}(a)
is fully multiplicative in the following sense:

s_{\delta}(a)=\prod s_{p^{\nu_{p}(\delta)}}(p^{\nu_{p}(a)}),

where products are over the set of prime numbers. Furthermore, we have the following expression:

s_{\delta}(a)=\sum_{d|\delta}J_{2}(d)\overline{\sigma}^{d}(a).

Proof.

We first prove the multiplicativity: assume that we have the decomposition in products of primes
a=\prod p^{\alpha_{p}}
and
\delta=\prod p^{\delta_{p}}
. The sum over
k^{2}|a
writes itself as sum over the families
(i_{p})
with
2i_{p}\leqslant\alpha_{p}
indexed by the set of prime numbers
\mathcal{P}
:

\sum_{k^{2}|a}\gcd(k,\delta)\gcd(a/k,\delta)\psi(a/k^{2})=\sum_{2i_{p}\leqslant\alpha_{p}}\prod\psi(p^{\alpha_{p}-2i_{p}})p^{\min(i_{p},\delta_{p})+\min(\alpha_{p}-i_{p},\delta_{p})},

using the multiplicativity of the Dedekind function
\psi
. We can factor the sum as a product and get the sought multiplicativity.

To prove the desired identity, we compute both Dirichlet generating series. The multiplicativity allows to factor the generating series as a product over prime numbers so that

\sum_{a,\delta=1}^{\infty}\frac{s_{\delta}(a)}{\delta^{s}a^{t}}=\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\left(\sum_{d,2i\leqslant\alpha}\psi(p^{\alpha-2i})p^{\min(i,d)+\min(\alpha-i,d)}p^{-sd-t\alpha}\right).

To finish the computation of the Dirichlet series for
s_{\delta}(a)
, the inner sum may be rewritten

\sum_{i,j,k}\psi(p^{i})p^{\min(j,k)+\min(i+j,k)}p^{-ks-2jt-it},

where
p
is a prime number and
i,j,k\geqslant 0
. Setting aside
i=0
,
\psi(p^{i})=p^{i-1}(p+1)
and we can split according to the value of
k
and compute the geometric sums, yielding some rational function in
p^{-t}
and
p^{-s}
.

For the second expression, the Dirichlet series is:

\displaystyle\sum_{\delta,a}\frac{\sum_{d|\delta}J_{2}(d)\overline{\sigma}^{d}(a)}{\delta^{s}a^{t}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{d,k,a}\frac{J_{2}(d)\overline{\sigma}^{d}(a)}{d^{s}k^{s}a^{t}}\text{ where }\delta=dk

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{d,k}\frac{J_{2}(d)}{d^{s}}\frac{1}{k^{s}}\frac{1}{d^{t}}\zeta(t)\zeta(t-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta(s+t-2)}{\zeta(s+t)}\zeta(s)\zeta(t)\zeta(t-1).

Using that
\zeta(s)=\prod\frac{1}{1-p^{-s}}
, it can also be expressed as the product of values of a rational function at prime numbers. By a technical but elementary computation, we check that both rational functions are actually the same, so that generating functions have equal coefficients, finishing the proof of the identity.
∎

Notice that if
\gcd(a,\delta)=1
, we have
\overline{\sigma}^{d}(a)=0
for every divisor
d
of
\delta
. Then, we have
s_{\delta}(a)=\sigma(a)
. This way, the remaining terms may appear as a correction term when
\gcd(a,\delta)\neq 1
.

are distinct prime numbers, we have the following values:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}s_{p}&=&\sigma+(p^{2}-1)\overline{\sigma}^{p},\\

5.4.
Case of other correlators

To finish the proof of Theorem
5.3
, we now study the case where
\theta\neq\theta_{0}
is another correlator. We proceed in several steps:

\circ

First prove that the coefficients do not depend on the precise correlator
\theta
but only on the order of
\theta-\theta_{0}
inside
\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(E)
. To do so, we use the deformation invariance for a suitable family where we deform the base curve
E
.

\circ

Then, through Lemma
3.12
, we prove an induction relation that enables a formal computation of the correlated invariant for
\theta\neq\theta_{0}
.

\circ

Using the induction relation, we show the multiplicativity of the coefficient functions, so that we may restrict to the case of powers of primes, which we compute also using the induction. Combination of both leads to Theorem
5.3
.

5.4.1.
Dependence on the order.

Assumed that
L=\mathcal{O}
and choose
\theta_{0}=0
, so that the torsor of correlators is actually
\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(E)
. For
\theta\in\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(E)
, its order
\omega(\theta)
is the smallest positive integer
n
such that
n\theta\equiv 0
.

Lemma 5.15
.

Assuming
L=\mathcal{O}
, the correlated invariant
\langle\mathrm{pt}_{0},1_{w_{1}},\mathrm{pt}_{w_{2}},\cdots,\mathrm{pt}_{w_{n}}\rangle_{1,a[E],{\boldsymbol{w}}}^{\theta}
only depends on the order
\omega(\theta)
.

Proof.

Write
E
as some
E_{\tau}=\mathbb{C}/\langle 1;\tau\rangle
for some complex number
\tau
in the Poincaré half-plane. For any
\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, we have an isomorphism between
E_{\tau}
and
E_{\gamma\cdot\tau}
where
\gamma\cdot\tau=\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}
. The isomorphism is actually given by

\varphi:\left(z\ \mathrm{mod}\ 1,\tau\right)\longmapsto\frac{z}{c\tau+d}\ \mathrm{mod}\ 1,\gamma\cdot\tau.

Choose a path
\tau(t)
in the Poincaré half-plane going from
\tau(0)=\tau
to
\tau(1)=\gamma\cdot\tau
. The
\delta
-torsion elements in
E_{\tau(t)}
are deformed continuously along with
\tau(t)
as they can be written as
u\tau(t)+v
, where
u,v\in\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})
. The identification
\varphi:E_{\tau}\to E_{\gamma\cdot\tau}
then induces some monodromy among torsion elements. More precisely, if
u,v\in\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})
, we have

\varphi^{-1}\left(u\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}+v\right)=u(a\tau+b)+v(c\tau+d)=(au+cv)\tau+(bu+dv).

Therefore, we deduce that the values of the correlated invariants for the torsion elements
\theta=u\tau+v
and
\gamma\cdot\theta=(au+cv)\tau+(bu+dv)
are the same. As the action of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on the set of torsion elements of the same order in
\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}\left((\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})^{2}\right)
is transitive, we conclude.
∎

5.4.2.
Coefficients, induction relation and multiplicativity.

For
r|\delta
, let
s_{\delta}[r](a)
be such that for any
\theta\in\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(E)
,

\langle\mathrm{pt}_{0},1_{w},\mathrm{pt}_{w_{2}},\dots,\mathrm{pt}_{w_{n}}\rangle_{1,a[E],{\boldsymbol{w}}}^{\theta}=a^{n-1}\left(\frac{w}{\delta}\right)^{2}s_{\delta}[\omega(\theta)](a).

In particular, we have
s_{\delta}[1]=s_{\delta}
, corresponding to
\theta=0
. We also set the global function with values in the group algebra
S_{\delta}(a)=\sum_{\delta\theta\equiv 0}s_{\delta}[\omega(\theta)](a)\cdot(\theta)
, so that

\langle\langle\mathrm{pt}_{0},1_{w},\mathrm{pt}_{w_{2}},\dots,\mathrm{pt}_{w_{n}}\rangle\rangle^{\delta}_{1,a[E],{\boldsymbol{w}}}=a^{n-1}\left(\frac{w}{\delta}\right)^{2}S_{\delta}(a).

The definition of
S_{\delta}
directly comes from the invariants. Theorem
5.3
consists in proving that
\frac{S_{\delta}}{\delta^{2}}=\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\delta}
, as defined before Theorem
5.3
.

Lemma 5.16
.

For every pair
\delta^{\prime}|\delta
, we have the following relation:

\sum_{r|\delta^{\prime}}J_{2}(r)s_{\delta}[r](a)=(\delta^{\prime})^{2}s_{\delta/\delta^{\prime}}(a).

Proof.

We already have
\mathfrak{m}\left[\delta^{\prime}\right]\left([\![\mathcal{M}(a,{\boldsymbol{w}})]\!]^{\delta}\right)=[\![\mathcal{M}(a,{\boldsymbol{w}})]\!]^{\delta/\delta^{\prime}}
. Integrating the point constraints and looking at the
(0)
-coefficient yields

a^{n-1}\left(\frac{w}{\delta/\delta^{\prime}}\right)^{2}s_{\delta/\delta^{\prime}}(a)=\sum_{\delta^{\prime}\theta\equiv 0}a^{n-1}\left(\frac{w}{\delta}\right)^{2}s_{\delta}[\omega(\theta)](a).

The sum is over
\theta
such that
\delta^{\prime}\theta\equiv 0
. For any
r|\delta^{\prime}
, there are
J_{2}(r)
elements of order excactly
r
, yielding the desired relation.
∎

These relations form a system which is triangular for the order given by the divisibility. They are thus sufficient to compute all the functions
s_{\delta}[r]
. We carry out some examples below.

Example 5.17
.

If
\delta=p
is a prime number, we have

s_{p}[1]+(p^{2}-1)s_{p}[p]=p^{2}\sigma.

As we already know that
s_{p}[1]=s_{p}=\sigma+(p^{2}-1)\overline{\sigma}^{p}
, we deduce that

(p^{2}-1)\overline{\sigma}^{p}+(p^{2}-1)s_{p}[p]=(p^{2}-1)\sigma,

and thus

s_{p}[p]=\sigma-\overline{\sigma}^{p}.

\lozenge

Example 5.18
.

For
\delta=p^{2}
, we have the following equations:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}s_{p^{2}}+(p^{2}-1)s_{p^{2}}[p]+(p^{4}-p^{2})s_{p^{2}}[p^{2}]&=p^{4}s_{1}&=p^{4}\sigma,\\
s_{p^{2}}+(p^{2}-1)s_{p^{2}}[p]&=p^{2}s_{p}&=p^{2}\sigma+(p^{4}-p^{2})\overline{\sigma}^{p},\\
s_{p^{2}}&=s_{p^{2}}&=\sigma+(p^{2}-1)\overline{\sigma}^{p}+(p^{4}-p^{2})\overline{\sigma}^{p^{2}},\\
\end{array}

which solves for

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}s_{p^{2}}&=\sigma+(p^{2}-1)\overline{\sigma}^{p}+(p^{4}-p^{2})\overline{\sigma}^{p^{2}},\\
s_{p^{2}}[p]&=\sigma+(p^{2}-1)\overline{\sigma}^{p}-p^{2}\overline{\sigma}^{p^{2}},\\
s_{p^{2}}[p^{2}]&=\sigma-\overline{\sigma}^{p}.\\
\end{array}\right.

\lozenge

To reduce the computation down to the powers of primes, we use the induction relation to prove the multiplicativity of the functions
s_{\delta}[r](a)
and
S_{\delta}(a)
.

Proposition 5.19
.

The function
s_{\delta}[r](a)
and
S_{\delta}(a)
are fully multiplicative: for pairs of coprime elements
(r_{1},r_{2})
,
(a_{1},a_{2})
and
(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})
, we have

s_{\delta_{1}\delta_{2}}[r_{1}r_{2}](a_{1}a_{2})=s_{\delta_{1}}[r_{1}](a_{1})s_{\delta_{2}}[r_{2}](a_{2})\text{ and }S_{\delta_{1}\delta_{2}}(a_{1}a_{2})=S_{\delta_{1}}(a_{1})S_{\delta_{2}}(a_{2}).

Proof.

We show multiplicativity by induction. It is true for
r=1
by multiplicativity of
s_{\delta}
. For the induction step, we use the identity from Lemma
5.16
and the multiplicativity of
s_{\delta}
:

\begin{array}[]{>{\displaystyle}rc>{\displaystyle}l}(\delta^{\prime}_{1})^{2}(\delta^{\prime}_{2})^{2}s_{\delta_{1}/\delta^{\prime}_{1}}(a_{1})s_{\delta_{2}/\delta^{\prime}_{2}}(a_{2})&=&(\delta^{\prime}_{1}\delta^{\prime}_{2})^{2}s_{\delta_{1}\delta_{2}/\delta^{\prime}_{1}\delta^{\prime}_{2}}(a_{1}a_{2})\\
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r_{1}|\delta^{\prime}_{1}\\
r_{2}|\delta^{\prime}_{2}\end{subarray}}J_{2}(r_{1})J_{2}(r_{2})s_{\delta_{1}}[r_{1}](a_{1})s_{\delta_{2}}[r_{2}](a_{2})&=&\sum_{r|\delta^{\prime}_{1}\delta^{\prime}_{2}}J_{2}(r)s_{\delta_{1}\delta_{2}}[r](a_{1}a_{2})\\
&=&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r_{1}|\delta^{\prime}_{1}\\
r_{2}|\delta^{\prime}_{2}\end{subarray}}J_{2}(r_{1})J_{2}(r_{2})s_{\delta_{1}\delta_{2}}[r_{1}r_{2}](a_{1}a_{2}).\\
\end{array}

To get the last sum, we use that each divisor of
\delta^{\prime}_{1}\delta^{\prime}_{2}
can uniquely been written as a product of a divisor of
\delta^{\prime}_{1}
and a divisor of
\delta^{\prime}_{2}
. If we assume multiplicativity for
r
a strict divisor of
\delta^{\prime}_{1}\delta^{\prime}_{2}
, all the terms in the sum except the last one are equal, and we are left with the multiplicativity for
r=\delta^{\prime}_{1}\delta^{\prime}_{2}
, finishing the induction. The multiplicativity of
S_{\delta}
follows from the multiplicativity of
s_{\delta}[r]
:

\left(\sum_{\delta_{1}\theta_{1}\equiv 0}s_{\delta_{1}}[\omega(\theta_{1})](a_{1})\cdot(\theta_{1})\right)\left(\sum_{\delta_{2}\theta_{2}\equiv 0}s_{\delta_{2}}[\omega(\theta_{2})](a_{2})\cdot(\theta_{2})\right)

=\sum_{\delta_{1}\theta_{1}\equiv\delta_{2}\theta_{2}\equiv 0}s_{\delta_{1}}[\omega(\theta_{1})](a_{1})s_{\delta_{2}}[\omega(\theta_{2})](a_{2})\cdot(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}).

As each
\delta_{1}\delta_{2}
torsion element can be written uniquely as the sum of a
\delta_{1}
-torsion and a
\delta_{2}
-torsion element, we conclude.
∎

5.4.3.
Computation for powers of primes.

Because of multiplicativity, we only need to compute the function when
a,r,\delta
are the power of a common prime
p
. Recall that we defined the elements of
\mathbb{Q}[E]
:
\vartheta_{d}=\frac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{d\theta\equiv 0}(\theta)
. We also momentarily set
\vartheta_{d}^{\mathrm{prim}}=\sum_{\omega(\theta)=d}(\theta)
.

Proposition 5.20
.

The function
(\delta,r,a)\mapsto s_{\delta}[r](a)
has the following values over powers of primes, for
r>0
:

s_{p^{d}}[p^{r}]=s_{p^{d-r}}-p^{2d-2r}\overline{\sigma}^{p^{d-r+1}}=\sum_{0}^{d-r}J_{2}(p^{j})\overline{\sigma}^{p^{j}}-p^{2d-2r}\overline{\sigma}^{p^{d-r+1}}.

Consequently, we find the following expressions for
S_{p^{d}}
:

\frac{S_{p^{d}}}{p^{2d}}=\sum_{r=0}^{d-1}\left(\overline{\sigma}^{p^{r}}-\overline{\sigma}^{p^{r+1}}\right)\vartheta_{p^{d-r}}+\overline{\sigma}^{p^{d}}\vartheta_{1}=\sigma\vartheta_{p^{d}}+\sum_{1}^{d}\overline{\sigma}^{p^{r}}\left(\vartheta_{p^{d-r}}-\vartheta_{p^{d-r+1}}\right).

Proof.

By Lemma
5.16
, we have the following relations:

p^{2r}s_{p^{d-r}}=\sum_{j=0}^{r}J_{2}(p^{j})s_{p^{d}}[p^{j}].

For
r>0
, making the difference between relations for
r
and
r-1
, we get

p^{2r}s_{p^{d-r}}-p^{2r-2}s_{p^{d-r+1}}=J_{2}(p^{r})s_{p^{d}}[p^{r}]=p^{2r-2}(p^{2}-1)s_{p^{d}}[p^{r}].

Therefore, after dividing by
p^{2r-2}
, using that
s_{p^{d-r+1}}=s_{p^{d-r}}+J_{2}(p^{d-r+1})\overline{\sigma}^{p^{d-r+1}}
, we finally get

(p^{2}-1)s_{p^{d-r}}-J_{2}(p^{d-r+1})\overline{\sigma}^{p^{d-r+1}}=(p^{2}-1)s_{p^{d}}[p^{r}].

Dividing by
p^{2}-1
yields the desired expression. Then, we deduce that
S_{p^{d}}
has the following expression:

\displaystyle S_{p^{d}}=

\displaystyle s_{p^{d}}\vartheta_{1}+\sum_{r=1}^{d}(s_{p^{d-r}}-p^{2(d-r)}\overline{\sigma}^{p^{d-r+1}})\cdot\vartheta_{p^{r}}^{\mathrm{prim}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s_{p^{d}}\vartheta_{1}+\sum_{r=1}^{d}(s_{p^{d-r}}-p^{2(d-r)}\overline{\sigma}^{p^{d-r+1}})\cdot(p^{2r}\vartheta_{p^{r}}-p^{2r-2}\vartheta_{p^{r-1}}).

We can now split the sum performing a summation by part to get

\displaystyle S_{p^{d}}=

\displaystyle(s_{p^{d}}-s_{p^{d-1}}+p^{2(d-1)}\overline{\sigma}^{p^{d}})\vartheta_{1}+(\sigma-\overline{\sigma}^{p})p^{2d}\vartheta_{p^{d}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{1}^{d-1}p^{2r}\vartheta_{p^{r}}\left(s_{p^{d-r}}-p^{2(d-r)}\overline{\sigma}^{p^{d-r+1}}-s_{p^{d-r-1}}+p^{2(d-r-1)}\overline{\sigma}^{p^{d-r}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{2d}\overline{\sigma}^{p^{d}}\vartheta_{1}+p^{2d}\sum_{1}^{d}(\overline{\sigma}^{p^{d-r}}-\overline{\sigma}^{p^{d-r+1}})\vartheta_{p^{r}}.

This yields the first expression. Performing a second summation by part yields the second expression.
∎

We have two expressions of
S_{p^{d}}
, depending on which feature we wish to emphasize: the
\vartheta_{d}
or the
\overline{\sigma}^{d}
. The first ones are useful to compute in the group algebra since they are projectors, while the second are multiplicative functions that provide quasi-modularity properties.

Proof of Theorem
5.3
.

Combining the multiplicativity from Proposition
5.19
and the expression for powers of primes from Proposition
5.20
yields and explicit expression for
S_{\delta}
and we finally get that
\frac{S_{\delta}}{\delta^{2}}=\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\delta}
.
∎

6.
Floor diagrams and regularity

Our goal in this section is to obtain regularity results in the flavor of
[
Blo23
]
in the refined setting provided by the correlation. To do so, we use the correlated degeneration formula from Theorem

along with the local computation from Theorem
5.3

with central fiber a chain of
E\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
glued along their boundary divisors leads to combinatorial objects called

. The latter appear in
[
Blo23
]
although they are obtained through tropical techniques. Using these floor diagrams, we are able to prove the quasi-modularity in the base direction, and the piecewise polynomiality in the tangency orders. We explain how to adapt the floor diagram to the correlated setting, using coefficients in the group algebra
\mathbb{Q}[E]

6.1.
Floor diagrams and their multiplicities

We first recall the notion of

in the setting of curves in

Definition 6.1
.

A floor diagram

is the data of a weighted oriented graph with the following properties:

(1)

The graph has three kind of vertices:

\triangleright

sinks and sources which are univalent vertices, referred as

\triangleright

\triangleright

floors

(2)

The set of flat vertices and floors carry a total order compatible with the orientation.

(3)

The edges have a positive weight
w_{e}
, such that the weighting makes floors and flat vertices balanced.

(4)

The complement of flat vertices is without cycle and each connected component of the complement contains a unique sink or source.

For floor diagram, as for curves, we have a notion of genus and class, recalled in the next definition.

\triangleright

for edges adjacent to infinite vertices (with a

\triangleright

6.2.
Correspondence statement

We now use the degeneration formula to prove that the count of floor diagrams with the ad-hoc multiplicity yields the correlated GW-invariant. We consider a degeneration
\mathcal{Y}_{t}

n+g-1

\mathcal{Y}_{0}

Theorem 6.5
.

We have the following equality:

\langle\langle\mathrm{pt}^{n+g-1},1_{w_{1}},\dots,1_{w_{n}}\rangle\rangle^{\delta}_{g,a[E],{\boldsymbol{w}}}=\sum_{\mathfrak{D}}{\boldsymbol{m}}_{\delta}(\mathfrak{D}),

where we sum over floor diagrams
\mathfrak{D}
of genus
g
, in the class
\beta
, having tangency profile
{\boldsymbol{w}}
.

The proof is standard application of the decomposition formula which we recall for sake of completeness.

Up to the factor
\frac{\prod w_{e}}{|\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)|}
and the operator
\mathfrak{d}\left[\frac{1}{\delta/\delta_{\Gamma}}\right]
, the multiplicity of a degeneration graph
\Gamma
given by the degeneration formula is as follows:

\int_{\prod_{V}[\![\mathcal{M}_{V}]\!]^{\delta_{\Gamma}}}\mathrm{ev}^{*}\Delta\cup\prod_{1}^{n+g-1}\mathrm{ev}_{i}^{*}(\mathrm{pt}).

This integral splits as a product over the vertices of the graph once we use the Künneth decomposition of the diagonal. If
(b_{i})
is a basis and
(b_{i}^{\#})
the Poincaré dual basis, we have
\Delta=\sum_{i}(-1)^{\mathrm{rk}b_{i}}b_{i}\otimes b_{i}^{\#}
. In our case, taking the basis
(1,\alpha,\beta,\mathrm{pt})
of
H^{\bullet}(E,\mathbb{Q})
, we have

\Delta=1\otimes\mathrm{pt}+\mathrm{pt}\otimes 1+\alpha\otimes\beta-\beta\otimes\alpha.

Expanding, it amounts to sum over all the possible insertions of Poincaré dual classes of
E
at the extremities of bounded edges of
\Gamma
, which we call a
Poincaré insertion
. We now consider a degeneration graph
\Gamma
and aim at transforming it into a floor diagram. This is the content of Lemmas
6.6
and
6.7
.

Lemma 6.6
.

If
\Gamma
is a degeneration graph with non-zero multiplicity, we have the following:

(1)

Inner vertices have genus
0
or
1
, and a genus
1
vertex is adjacent to a marked point.

(2)

A genus
0
vertices is bivalent and its class is of the form
w[\mathbb{P}^{1}]
.

(3)

For a marked genus
0
vertex, adjacent Poincaré insertions are
1\in H^{0}(E,\mathbb{Q})
.

Proof.

(1) Let
V
be a vertex with genus
g_{V}
, valency
n_{V}
, and
m_{V}=0,1
adjacent marked points. The dimension of
[\![\mathcal{M}_{V}]\!]^{\delta_{\Gamma}}
is
n_{V}+m_{V}+g_{V}-1
. For the multiplicity associated to a Poincaré insertion to be non-zero, the sum of ranks of the insertions (Poincaré insertions coming from
\Delta
and the
\mathrm{pt}
if
m_{V}=1
) needs to match this dimension. The sum of ranks of these insertions is at most
(n_{V}-1)+2m_{V}
. The maximal rank of Poincaré insertions is
n_{V}-1
and not
n_{V}
due to the existence of a relation between the position of points at infinity: choosing only
\mathrm{pt}
insertions at every flag yields
0
invariant. Therefore, we have the inequality

n_{V}+m_{V}+g_{V}-1\leqslant n_{V}-1+2m_{V}.

In other words,
g_{V}\leqslant m_{V}
, which implies (1):
g_{V}
may only take the values
0,1
, and a genus
1
vertex is marked.

(2) Assume that
g_{V}=0
. As there all maps from a rational curve to an elliptic curve are constant, the class associated to
V
is necessarily of the form
w[\mathbb{P}^{1}]
. Fibers varying in a
1
-dimensional family, we may only impose a unique point constraint:

\triangleright

If
m_{V}=1
, we already have an interior point constraint, and the dimension is
(n_{V}+1)-1
. Thus, we have

.

\triangleright

If

, the dimension is

, and we the sum of rank of the Poincaré insertions is at most

, so that we also have

.

In either case, as

due to the class intersecting both divisors of the singular locus, we get the bivalency statement from (2).

(3) Assume that

has genus

is

, and is already matched by the rank of the point insertion at the marked point, finishing the proof.

Lemma
6.6
is a big step toward the transfiguration of a degeneration graph into a floor diagram: edges are already weighted, vertices are split into flat bivalent vertices and floors as advertised after Example
6.3
. The total order comes from the total order on the components of
\mathcal{Y}_{0}
. However, we still need three features:

\triangleright

delete unmarked genus
0
vertices, which is achieved through Lemma
6.8
,

\triangleright

prove that the complement of genus
0
marked vertices is a forest with each component containing a unique infinite vertex,

\triangleright

the multiplicity matches
{\boldsymbol{m}}_{\delta}
.

Consider
\Gamma
our degeneration graph. We can cut
\Gamma
at each genus
0
marked vertices, replacing esch bivalent vertex by two univalent vertices. The new graph (which may be disconnected) is denoted by
\widehat{\Gamma}
. We then remove the infinite vertices and get a non-compact graph
\widehat{\Gamma}^{\circ}
called

.

Lemma 6.7
.

If
\Gamma
is a degeneration graph with non-zero multiplicity, and
\widehat{\Gamma}^{\circ}
the associated open cut graph, we have the following:

(1)

Every connected component of
\widehat{\Gamma}^{\circ}
has Euler characteristic
0
.

(2)

There are no compact connected component of
\widehat{\Gamma}^{\circ}
: every component of
\widehat{\Gamma}
contains an infinite vertex.

(3)

\widehat{\Gamma}
has no cycle, and each connected component contains a unique infinite vertex.

(4)

The Poincaré insertions are uniquely determined:

\triangleright

for a marked genus
0
vertex,
1,1\in H^{\bullet}(E,\mathbb{Q})
,

\triangleright

for an unmarked genus
0
vertex,
1,\mathrm{pt}\in H^{\bullet}(E,\mathbb{Q})
,

\triangleright

for a (marked) genus
1
vertex

.

Proof.

Let
C
be a connected component of the compact graph
\widehat{\Gamma}
. We denote by:

-

\mathcal{V}_{1}
the set of genus
1
(inner) vertices,

-

\mathcal{V}_{0}
the set of genus
0
unmarked vertices,

-

\partial
the set of univalent vertices resulting from the cut,

-

\partial_{\infty}
the set of infinite vertices,

-

E_{b}
the set of edges not adjacent to a vertex of
\partial\cup\partial_{\infty}
.

Notice that we counting the flags adjacent to the vertices of
\mathcal{V}_{0}\cup\mathcal{V}_{1}
in two ways, we have that

\sum_{\mathcal{V}_{1}}n_{V}+2|\mathcal{V}_{0}|=2|E_{b}|+|\partial|+|\partial_{\infty}|.

The dimension of
\prod_{V\in\mathcal{V}_{0}(C)\cup\mathcal{V}_{1}(C)}[\![\mathcal{M}_{V}]\!]^{\delta_{\Gamma}}
is

\displaystyle\prod_{V\in\mathcal{V}_{0}(C)\cup\mathcal{V}_{1}(C)}[\![\mathcal{M}_{V}]\!]^{\delta_{\Gamma}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal{V}_{1}(C)}(n_{V}+1)+\sum_{\mathcal{V}_{0}(C)}1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2|E_{b}|+|\partial|+|\partial_{\infty}|+|\mathcal{V}_{1}|-|\mathcal{V}_{0}|.

For the invariant to be non-zero, it needs to match the sum of ranks of the insertions:

\triangleright

the points for each vertex of
\partial
through Lemma

,

\triangleright

the point insertions for every vertex of
\mathcal{V}_{1}

\triangleright

the diagonal insertions for every edge of

Therefore, we have

This quantity is precisely the Euler characteristic of

where we deleted the vertices of

As the Euler characteristic is

\triangleright

the connected component is compact with a unique cycle,

\triangleright

the connected component has no cycle and contains a unique end, meaning

The point (2) forbids the first possibility so that we have (3). To prove (2), we may use

The statement about insertions follows from an induction on the trees of the forest

, pruning the branches until we are left with the edges adjacent to

∎

Proof of Theorem

.

The number of marked points is equal to

. We consider a degeneration

of

We need to show that the degeneration graphs with non-zero multiplicity are actually the floor diagrams involved in the sum, and that they share the same multiplicity.

6.3.
Quasi-modularity

We now use the floor diagrams to prove some regularity statements for the invariants, starting with the generating series in the base direction: varying
a
in the curve class
a[E]+b[\mathbb{P}^{1}]
. For that purpose, we assume that
L=\mathcal{O}
, so that we may choose
\lambda_{0}=0
and the term
(a\lambda_{0})
in the diagram multiplicities disappears.

We consider quasi-modular forms with values in the vector space
\mathbb{Q}[\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(E)]
. Usual quasi-modular definitions extend naturally to the setting of functions in a vector space.

Definition 6.10
.

Let
V
be a complex vector space and
f:\mathbb{H}\to V
a meromorphic function on the Poincaré half-plane and
\Gamma\subset SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
a subgroup.

(1)

We say that
f
is modular of weight
k
if for any
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma
we have

(c\tau+d)^{k}f\left(\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right)=f(\tau).

(2)

We say that
f
is quasi-modular of weight
k
and depth
s
if there exists functions
f_{j}:\mathbb{H}\to V
such that for any
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma
we have

(c\tau+d)^{k}f\left(\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right)=\sum_{0}^{s}f_{j}(\tau)\left(\frac{c}{c\tau+d}\right)^{j}.

A function
f:\mathbb{H}\to V
is (quasi-)modular if and only if all its coordinate functions are (quasi-)modular. Furthermore, in the quasi-modularity case, all
f_{j}
are also quasi-modular forms and
f_{0}=f
. Furthermore, if the vector space
V
is endowed with an
\mathbb{C}
-algebra structure, the product of quasi-modular forms is also quasi-modular. If
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma
, we can set
\mathsf{q}=e^{2i\pi\tau}
and develop the quasi-modular forms in Fourier series, or conversely consider generating series in
\mathsf{q}
as meromorphic functions potentially quasi-modular.

Example 6.11
.

We already saw that the generating series
\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\delta}(a)\mathsf{q}^{a}
are quasi-modular forms for the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(\delta)
. Its derivatives
\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}a^{n}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\delta}(a)\mathsf{q}^{a}
are as well.

Theorem 6.12
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{w}}
be a tangency profile of length
n
. The generating series

\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}\langle\langle\mathrm{pt}^{n+g-1}\rangle\rangle_{g,a[E],{\boldsymbol{w}}}^{\delta}\mathsf{q}^{a},

is a quasi-modular form for the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(\delta)
with values in the group algebra
\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(E)]
.

Proof.

Up to the labeling of floors by
(a_{V})
, there is a finite number of floor diagrams. It thus suffices to prove the statement for each unlabelled floor diagram.

Let

a floor diagram without floor labels and let
W=\prod_{E_{b}(\mathfrak{D})}w_{e}\prod_{E_{\circ}(\mathfrak{D})}w_{e}^{2}

be the floor diagram obtained by labeling the vertices with

\mathfrak{d}\left[\frac{1}{\delta/\delta_{\Gamma}}\right]
is compatible with the product, we have the following factorization:

\displaystyle\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{\Sigma a_{V}=a}{\boldsymbol{m}}_{\delta}\left(\mathfrak{D}\big((a_{V})\big)\right)\right)\mathsf{q}^{a}=

\displaystyle\sum_{(a_{V})}\boldsymbol{m}_{\delta}\left(\mathfrak{D}\big((a_{V})\big)\right)\mathsf{q}^{\sum a_{V}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W\cdot\mathfrak{d}\left[\frac{1}{\delta/\delta_{\mathfrak{D}}}\right]\prod_{V}\left(\sum_{a_{V}=1}^{\infty}a_{V}^{n_{V}-1}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\delta_{\mathfrak{D}}}(a_{V})\mathsf{q}^{a_{V}}\right)

As each series is a quasi-modular form, so is their product.

6.4.
Piecewise polynomiality

We now vary the tangency orders, considering them as variables. We study the function

N^{\delta}_{a,g}:{\boldsymbol{w}}=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})\longmapsto\langle\langle\mathrm{pt}^{n+g-1},1_{w_{1}},\dots,1_{w_{n}}\rangle\rangle^{\delta}_{g,a[E],{\boldsymbol{w}}}\in\mathbb{Q}[\mathrm{Tor}_{\delta}(E)].

For this definition to make sense, we need to restrict to the sublattice where all the
w_{i}
are divisible by
\delta
, so that the
\delta
-refinement makes sense.

Up to the labeling of edges by their weight, there is a finite number of floor diagrams of genus
g
with
n
ends and with a class of the form
a[E]+\ast[\mathbb{P}^{1}]
. The orientation is still part of the data, only the edge weights are missing. Let
\mathfrak{D}
be one of them. To make
\mathfrak{D}
into a true floor diagram, one only needs to add weights to the edges. We denote by
\Omega_{\mathfrak{D}}
the set of weightings
\omega
of
\mathfrak{D}
,
i.e.
functions from edges to positive integers that make the floors and flet vertices balanced. For a weighting
\omega\in\Omega_{\mathfrak{D}}
, we denote by
\delta_{\omega}
the gcd between
\delta
and its coordinates. The tangency profile is the restriction of the weighting to the infinite ends. For a fixed tangency profile
{\boldsymbol{w}}
, we denote by
\Omega_{\mathfrak{D}}({\boldsymbol{w}})
the set of weightings that induce
{\boldsymbol{w}}
on the ends of
\mathfrak{D}
. Although by assumption
{\boldsymbol{w}}
is divisible by
\delta
,
\omega
may not be.

The multiplicity from Definition
6.4
emphasizes a monomial in the edge weights, which we denote by
f_{\mathfrak{D}}
:

f_{\mathfrak{D}}:\omega\in\Omega_{\mathfrak{D}}\longmapsto\prod_{E_{b}(\mathfrak{D})}w_{e}\prod_{E_{\circ}(\mathfrak{D})}w_{e},

where the first product is over bounded edges of
\mathfrak{D}
and the second product is over edges not adjacent to a flat vertex. The remaining part of the multiplicity depends on the floors weights
a_{V}
but also on the weighting
\omega
through its gcd
\delta_{\omega}
. We denote it by

\Phi_{\mathfrak{D}}(\delta_{\omega})=\mathfrak{d}\left[\frac{1}{\delta/\delta_{\omega}}\right]\left(\prod_{V}a_{V}^{n_{V}-1}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\delta_{\omega}}(a_{V})\right),

so that
\boldsymbol{m}_{\delta}\big(\mathfrak{D}(\omega)\big)=\Phi_{\mathfrak{D}}(\delta_{\omega})f_{\mathfrak{D}}(\omega)
. Even if we only care about the weighting that make the vertices balanced, this function is defined for all assignations of weights to the edges, not only the elements of
\Omega_{\mathfrak{D}}
. Before going to the main statement of the section, we reformulate the expression of
\Phi_{\mathfrak{D}}(\delta_{\omega})
to suit our purpose.

Lemma 6.15
.

For a chosen diagram
\mathfrak{D}
up to edge weights, there exists functions
\Upsilon^{\mathfrak{D}}_{d}
such that

\Phi_{\mathfrak{D}}(\delta_{\omega})=\sum_{d|\delta_{\omega}}\Upsilon^{\mathfrak{D}}_{d}(\delta_{\omega})\vartheta_{\delta/d}.

Proof.

We use the expression of
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\delta_{\omega}}
emphasizing the
\vartheta_{d}
:

\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\delta_{\omega}}(a_{V})=\sum_{d|\delta_{\omega}}\Upsilon_{d}^{\delta_{\omega}}(a_{V})\vartheta_{\delta_{\omega}/d}.

As the map
\mathfrak{d}\left[\frac{1}{\delta/\delta_{\omega}}\right]
is compatible with the product and
\mathfrak{d}\left[\frac{1}{\delta/\delta_{\omega}}\right](\vartheta_{\delta_{\omega}/d})=\vartheta_{\delta/d}
, we have that

\Phi_{\mathfrak{D}}(\delta_{\omega})=\prod_{V}\left(a_{V}^{n_{V}-1}\sum_{d_{V}|\delta_{\omega}}\Upsilon_{d_{V}}^{\delta_{\omega}}(a_{V})\vartheta_{\delta/d_{V}}\right).

We expand the product and use that
\vartheta_{\delta/d_{1}}\vartheta_{\delta/d_{2}}=\vartheta_{\delta/\gcd(d_{1},d_{2})}
:

\Phi_{\mathfrak{D}}(\delta_{\omega})=\left(\prod a_{V}^{n_{V}-1}\right)\sum_{d|\delta_{\omega}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{V}|\delta_{\omega}\\
\gcd(\delta_{V})=d\end{subarray}}\prod_{V}\Upsilon_{d_{V}}^{\delta_{\omega}}(a_{V})\right)\vartheta_{\delta/d}.

We thus have the existence and an expression for the desired coefficients
\Upsilon^{\mathfrak{D}}_{d}(\delta_{\omega})
, which only depend on
\delta_{\omega}
and
d
once
\mathfrak{D}
is fixed.
∎

Our statement relies on
[
AB17
, Theorem 4.2]
which is a generalization of
[
Stu95
, Theorem 1]
. To apply the Theorem, we provide a second description of the weighting set
\Omega_{\mathfrak{D}}({\boldsymbol{w}})
as the set of lattice points of a flow polytope. Let
A_{\mathfrak{D}}
be the adjacency matrix of
\mathfrak{D}
: rows are indexed by vertices (floors, flat vertices, sinks, sources) and columns by edges. Coefficients are given by the following rule:

A_{\mathfrak{D}}(V,e)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}0\text{ if }V\notin e,\\
1\text{ if }e\text{ ends at }V,\\
-1\text{ if }e\text{ starts at }V.\\
\end{array}\right.

In particular, for an element
\omega\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+|E_{b}(\mathfrak{D})|}
, the image
A_{\mathfrak{D}}\omega
is exactly the divergence at each vertex: the difference between incoming and outgoing weights at
V
. Therefore, if
\mathbf{d}=(d_{V})
is the vector with coordinate
|w_{i}|
for an infinite vertex and
0
else, we have

\Omega_{\mathfrak{D}}({\boldsymbol{w}})=\{\omega\in\mathbb{N}^{n+E_{b}(\mathfrak{D})}\text{ s.t. }A_{\mathfrak{D}}\omega=\mathbf{d}\}.

Theorem 6.17
.

For fixed
a,g,n,\delta
, the function
{\boldsymbol{w}}\mapsto N^{\delta}_{a,g}({\boldsymbol{w}})
is piecewise polynomial in the sense that there exists piecewise polynomial functions
P_{d}({\boldsymbol{w}})
for
d|\delta
such that

N^{\delta}_{a,g}({\boldsymbol{w}})=\sum_{d|\delta}P_{d}({\boldsymbol{w}})\vartheta_{d}.

Proof.

Up to the weighting of the edges, there is a finite number of floor diagrams of genus
g
with class of the form
a[E]+\ast[\mathbb{P}^{1}]
and
n
ends. Grouping them together, we get

N^{\delta}_{a,g}({\boldsymbol{w}})=\sum_{\mathfrak{D}}N^{\delta}_{\mathfrak{D}}({\boldsymbol{w}}),\text{ with }N^{\delta}_{\mathfrak{D}}({\boldsymbol{w}})=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{\mathfrak{D}}({\boldsymbol{w}})}\Phi_{\mathfrak{D}}(\delta_{\omega})f_{\mathfrak{D}}(\omega).

Thus, to prove piecewise polynomiality, it is sufficient to consider a unique unweighted floor diagram
\mathfrak{D}
.

Let
\mathfrak{D}
be such an unweighted floor diagram. If there was no
\Phi_{\mathfrak{D}}(\delta_{\omega})
,
[
AB17
, Theorem 4.2]
already applies and yields piecewise polynomiality. Due to the presence of this factor depending on the gcd
\delta_{\omega}
, our second step is thus to first use the expression of
\Phi_{\mathfrak{D}}(\delta_{\omega})
, split the sum and perform a
multiplicative summation by part
. We aim to change the sum over
\omega
and a given
\delta_{\omega}
into a sum over the
\omega
in a sublattice. Using Lemma
6.15
, we have

N^{\delta}_{\mathfrak{D}}({\boldsymbol{w}})=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{\mathfrak{D}}({\boldsymbol{w}})}\left(\sum_{d|\delta_{\omega}}\Upsilon^{\mathfrak{D}}_{d}(\delta_{\omega})\vartheta_{\delta/d}\right)f_{\mathfrak{D}}(\omega).

To perform the multiplicative summation by part, we can find coefficients
\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\mathfrak{D}}_{d}
, which are linear combinations of the
\vartheta_{\delta/d}
, such that for each
\delta_{\omega}
we have

\sum_{d|\delta_{\omega}}\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\mathfrak{D}}_{d}=\sum_{d|\delta_{\omega}}\Upsilon^{\mathfrak{D}}_{d}(\delta_{\omega})\vartheta_{\delta/d}.

Such coefficients exist because the equations given for each
\delta_{\omega}
form a triangular linear system. We can now switch the two sums and get rid of the dependence in the gcd
\delta_{\omega}
:

N^{\delta}_{\mathfrak{D}}({\boldsymbol{w}})=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{\mathfrak{D}}({\boldsymbol{w}})}\left(\sum_{d|\delta_{\omega}}\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\mathfrak{D}}_{d}\right)f_{\mathfrak{D}}(\omega)=\sum_{d|\delta}\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\mathfrak{D}}_{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\Omega_{\mathfrak{D}}({\boldsymbol{w}})\\
d|\delta_{\omega}\end{subarray}}f_{\mathfrak{D}}(\omega).

The last step is now to apply
[
AB17
, Theorem 4.2]
. For each
d|\delta
, we can rewrite

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\Omega_{\mathfrak{D}}({\boldsymbol{w}})\\
d|\omega\end{subarray}}f_{\mathfrak{D}}(\omega)=\sum_{\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega_{\mathfrak{D}}({\boldsymbol{w}}/d)}f_{\mathfrak{D}}(d\omega^{\prime}).

The function is piecewise quasi-polynomial with respect to the chamber complex of
A_{\mathfrak{D}}
. Furthermore, as
A_{\mathfrak{D}}
is actually unimodular (it is a submatrix of the root system
A_{n-1}
, see
[
AB17
, Example 4.4]
), we in fact get piecewise polynomiality and not only quasi-polynomiality. Summing over all diagrams, we conclude.
∎

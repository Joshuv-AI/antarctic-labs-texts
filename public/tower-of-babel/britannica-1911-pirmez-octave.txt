PIRMEZ, OCTAVE (1832–1883), Belgian author, was born at Châtelineau in 1832. He belonged to a well-known Belgian
family, and his cousin, Édouard Pirmez, was distinguished for
his works on literary and political subjects. He lived an uneventful
life at his château of Acoz, in Hainaut, where he died in
May 1883. Pirmez was an ardent admirer of the French
romanticists. His works include Les Feuillées: pensées et maximes
(1862), Victor Hugo (1863); Jours de solitude (1869); Rémo;
Souvenirs d’un frère (1880); Heures de philosophie (1881); and the
posthumous Lettres à José (1884). These books form a history
of his emotional life, and reveal an extreme melancholy.
See Vie et correspondance d’Octave Pirmez (1888), by Adolphe
Siret and José de Coppin.

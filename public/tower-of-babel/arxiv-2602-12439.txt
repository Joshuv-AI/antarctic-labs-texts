On Fujita’s conjecture for a general hyperkähler manifold in the standard series of examples
Alessandro Pilastro

Abstract
For the moduli spaces Σ d , t n \Sigma_{d,t}^{n} and Υ d , t n \Upsilon_{d,t}^{n} of polarized hyperkähler manifolds of K ​ 3 [ n ] K3^{[n]} -type and Kum n \Kum^{n} -type respectively, with polarization with square 2 ​ d 2d and divisibility t t , we study general base point freeness and very ampleness of the polarization. We provide cases where these moduli space are connected and a formula characterizing when these spaces are non-empty.



Introduction

Fujita’s conjecture states that given an ample holomorphic line bundle
L
on a compact complex manifold
X
of complex dimension
n
, the line bundle
K_{X}\otimes L^{\otimes m}
is base point free for all
m\geq n+1
and very ample for
m\geq n+2
. In general, the conjecture is widely open, as only in low dimensions the conjecture is known to hold and in some specific examples of manifolds. Two important families that satisfy the conjecture are
K3
surfaces and Abelian varieties, where the conjecture holds with lower bounds of
2
and
3
, respectively. These values are stronger compared to what the conjecture suggests, so it would be interesting to study the conjecture on hyperkähler manifolds.

Let
\Sigma_{d,t}^{n}
be the moduli space polarized of hyperkähler manifolds of
K3^{[n]}
-type with polarization of square
2d
and divisibility
t
; let
\Upsilon_{d,t}^{n}
be the analogous moduli space for
\Kum^{n}
-type manifolds. In
[
13
]
, U.Rieß proves that the general element of
\Sigma_{d,1}^{2}
and
\Sigma_{d,2}^{2}
is base point free, while in
[
14
]
, M.Varesco proves a similar statement for the
\Kum^{n}
-type for
n=2,3,4
and all possible divisibilities, except for a finite number of cases. The results of U.Rieß is generalized by O.Debarre in
[
5
]
, where bounds in terms of
d
and
n
are given for the general element of the moduli spaces
\Sigma_{d,1}^{n}
and
\Sigma_{d,2}^{n}
to be base point free and very ample.

Inspired by these results, we formalize the techniques used in the aforementioned works to generalize the results in higher dimensions for all possible divisibility in both the case of
K3^{[n]}
-type and
\Kum^{n}
-type hyperkähler.

Theorem 0.1
.

Let
n,t,d
be positive integers with
n\geq 2
and let
\tau:=\frac{t^{2}}{2(t-1)}
. Then the following holds:

•

let
(X,H)
be a general element of
\Sigma_{d,n}^{1}
or
\Upsilon_{d,n}^{1}
then
H
is base point free and
H^{n+2}
is very ample. Furthermore, if
d\geq n+1
then the polarization of a general element of
\Sigma_{d,n}^{1}
is very ample, if
d\geq n+4
then the polarization of a general element of
\Upsilon_{d,n}^{1}
is very ample;

•

if
d\geq(\tau-1)n+\tau+1
there exists a connected component of
\Sigma_{d,n}^{t}
such that the polarization of a general element
(X,H)
is base point free and
H^{n+2}
is very ample;

•

if
d\geq(\tau-1)n+2\tau+1
there exists a connected component of
\Sigma_{d,n}^{t}
such that the polarization of a general element is very ample;

•

if
d\geq(\tau-1)n+2\tau-1
there exists a connected component of
\Upsilon_{d,n}^{t}
such that the polarization of a general element
(X,H)
is base point free and

is very ample;

•

if

there exists a connected component of

These hold unless the considered moduli space is empty.

In more interesting situations, to which we can apply the theorem, occur when these moduli spaces have one connected component. In
[
12
]
and
[
2
]
, the authors provide a formula to compute the numbers of connected components of these moduli spaces. For example, with divisibilities
t=1,2
and
t=p^{a}
, with
p>2
prime, and
p^{a+1}
not dividing
n
the moduli spaces
\Sigma_{d,t}^{n}
and
\Upsilon_{d,t}^{n}
are connected. We conclude by providing, in Proposition
5.10
, a characterization of when the considered moduli spaces are non-empty. This allows us to improve the inequalities presented in Theorem
0.1
in any specific case.

The structure of the article is the following.

In Section
1
, we recall the main properties of the Hilbert scheme of points on a surface, with a focus on the tautological vector bundle
L^{[n]}
on
S^{[n]}
associated to any line bundle
L
on the surface
S
. We prove how this construction preserves base point freeness.

In Section
2
, we provide the general notion of embeddings into Grassmannians via suitable vector bundles. In particular, we show how the tautological vector bundle
L^{[n]}
defines a map into a Grassmannian if
L
is
(n-1)
-very ample, which is an embedding if
L
is
n
-very ample. Taking the determinant of
L^{[n]}
allows us to obtain a base point free and a very ample line bundle on
S^{[n]}
.

Sections
3
and
4
recall the definition of hyperkähler manifolds and provide the statements of some properties that we will use throughout the article. In particular, we present the lattice structure of the Picard group of
K3^{[n]}
-type and
\Kum^{n}
-type hyperkähler manifolds. We also show why base point freeness and very ampleness are open properties in families for hyperkähler manifolds.

In section
5
, we combine the results to obtain the main theorem. The main idea of the proof is to build an element of the moduli space with the required properties. The openness of base point freeness and the very ampleness allows us to conclude that the general elements in the same connected component share the same properties.

Tautological vector bundles on
\Hilb^{n}
of a surface

Let

be a smooth, projective complex surface and let

the Hilbert scheme parameterizing

Since the projection

\displaystyle F^{[n]}(\xi)\cong H^{0}(\xi,F|_{\xi}),\ \forall\xi\in S^{[n]}.

(1)

The tautological bundles have the following properties.

Proposition 1.1

(
[

]

Let

Recall that

Proposition 1.2

(
[
3
]
, Prop 4.5)

Let

(n-1)
-very ample on

Denoting the

Let

Using this morphism, we can explicitly write what the determinant line bundle of a tautological vector bundle is. Let

.

Proposition 1.3

(
[

]
, Lemma 3.7 - Thm 4.6)

Let

on

.

To conclude this section, let us show that the morphism

preserves base point freeness.

Lemma 1.4
.

Let
S
be a smooth projective complex surface, and let
L\in\Pic(S)
be base point free, then
L_{n}\in\Pic(S^{[n]})
is also base point free.

Proof.

Let
\xi\in S^{[n]}
and let
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
be the points in the support of
\xi
with multiplicities. We can find a section
s\in H^{0}(S,L)
such that
s(x_{i})\neq 0
for all
i=1,\dots,n.
This follows by induction on
n
. The base case is the definition of base point free line bundle. From the induction hypothesis, we have a section
\hat{s}
such that
\hat{s}(x_{i})\neq 0
for
i=1,\dots,n-1
. If
\hat{s}(x_{n})\neq 0
then we are done. Suppose then that
\hat{s}(x_{n})=0
and given
\tilde{s}
a section non-vanishing at the point

, define
s=\alpha\hat{s}+\tilde{s}
. For a general
\alpha\in\bC
the section
s
satisfies the required property.

So, given
\xi\in S^{[n]}
let
s
be a section of
L
non-vanishing at the points in the support of
\xi
. Consider now the section
\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}^{*}(s)\in H^{0}(S^{n},L^{\boxtimes n})
which is invariant under the action of
\Sym(n)
. It therefore descends to a section
s^{(n)}\in H^{0}(S^{(n)},L^{(n)})
. Then
s^{(n)}
takes the value
\prod_{i=1}^{n}s(x_{i})\neq 0
at the point
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\in S^{(n)}
. The pullback section
\mu^{*}(s^{(n)})=s^{[n]}\in H^{0}(S^{[n]},L_{n})
does not vanish at the point
\xi\in S^{[n]}
as required.

∎

We start by setting some notations for the Grassmannians. Let
V

d
-dimensional vector space, with
\gr(V,n)

n

0\to U\to\gr(V,n)\times V\to Q\to 0.

Let
X
be a compact complex manifold, and let

be a globally generated holomorphic vector bundle of rank
n
on
X

G

\Phi_{B}

\displaystyle\Phi_{B}:X

\displaystyle\to\gr(V,n)

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle\mapsto(\ker v_{p})^{\circ}

where

, and

\det B\cong(\Phi_{B}\circ P)^{*}\cO_{\bP(\Lambda^{n}V)}(1)

From this observation, we obtain the following:

.

Let

be a compact complex manifold, and let

be a globally generated vector bundle on X, then
\det B
is base point free. Furthermore, if
\Phi_{B}
is an embedding, then

Returning to the Hilbert schemes, let

be a
(n-1)
-very ample line bundle on

\displaystyle\Phi_{L^{[n]}}:S^{[n]}

\displaystyle\to\gr(H^{0}(S^{[n]},L^{[n]})^{*},n).

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle\mapsto(\ker v_{\eta})^{\circ}

In this case, we have another map into a Grassmannian. As a matter of fact, recall that

\displaystyle\varphi_{L^{[n]}}:S^{[n]}

\displaystyle\to\gr(H^{0}(S,L)^{*},n).

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle\mapsto(\ker r_{\eta})^{\circ}

In

The morphism

Consider now the map

\displaystyle g:H^{0}(S,L)\to H^{0}(S^{[n]},L^{[n]})

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{S^{[n]}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\gr(H^{0}(S,L)^{*},n)}}

line bundle on

3
Hyperkähler manifolds

Let
X
be a Kähler manifold, we say that
X
is
hyperkähler
(or
irreducible holomorphic symplectic
) if it is simply connected and if
H^{0}(X,\Omega^{2}_{X})
is generated by an everywhere non-degenerate holomorphic 2-form.
There are two known families of examples that occur in every possible dimension:

•

Given a
K3
surface
S
its Hilbert scheme on
n
points
S^{[n]}
is a hyperkähler manifold;

•

Given an abelian surface
T
consider the Hilbert scheme
T^{[n+1]}
. There is a natural map
T^{[n+1]}\to T
given by the sum. The fiber over
0
is a hyperkähler manifold called
generalized Kummer variety
, denoted by
\Kum^{n}(T)
.

Furthermore, deformations of hyperkähler manifolds are still hyperkähler. In particular, deformations of
S^{[n]}
are called of
K3^{[n]}
-type, while deformations of
\Kum^{n}(T)
are called of
\Kum^{n}
-type.
The only other known examples of hyperkähler manifolds are O’Grady’s ”exceptional” examples, namely OG6 and OG10, which occur only in dimensions 6 and 10, respectively.

Given an hyperkähler manifold, the second cohomology group
H^{2}(X,\bZ)
can be endowed with a quadratic form
q
called the Beauville-Bogomolov-Fujiki form (or BFF form), so that
(H^{2}(X,\bZ),q)
has the structure of a lattice (see
[
7
]
for a reference). In this regard, let us recall some definitions. Let
(\Lambda,q)
a lattice:

•

we say an element
\alpha
is
primitive
if
\alpha=k\alpha^{\prime}
implies
k=\pm 1
;

•

The
multiplicity
of an element
\alpha
is
m
if there exists a primitive element
\alpha^{\prime}
such that
\alpha=m\alpha^{\prime}
;

•

The
divisibility
of an element
\alpha
, denoted by
\Div(\alpha)
, is the multiplicity of
(\cdot,\alpha)_{q}
as an element of the dual lattice
\Lambda^{\vee}
. Equivalently
\Div(\alpha)=m
if
m
generates the ideal
\{(\alpha^{\prime},\alpha)_{q}|\alpha^{\prime}\in\Lambda\}
;

•

Let
\{e_{i}\}
be a
\bZ
-basis of the lattice
\Lambda
. The
discriminant
of the
\Lambda
is defined as the determinant of the matrix
(q(e_{i},e_{j}))_{i,j}
.

We report a characterization of projective hyperkähler manifolds.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
7
]
, Theorem 3.11)
.

Let
X
be a hyperkähler manifold. Then
X
is projective if and only if there exists a line bundle
L
on
X
with

.

The lattice structure of the
K3^{[n]}

Proposition 3.2
.

Fix a

. Then the following holds:

(i)

For every

, there is an orthogonal decomposition of lattices

(ii)

(iii)

Proposition 3.3
.

Fix an abelian surface

(i)

For every

\delta
is an integral
(1,1)

Being interested in studying the base point freeness and very ampleness of general elements of these moduli spaces, we now investigate the openness condition of these properties. Following the notation in

Theorem 4.4

(
[
11
]
, Lemma 7.3 - Thm 7.4)
.

Specilizing to hyperkähler manifolds we obtain the following.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let

. Let

•

is very ample for all

We follow the proof of Proposition 2.6 in
[

is very ample as the other follows analogously. Since

[

for

for all

for all

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
d,n,t
be positive integers with
n\geq 2
, let
X
be an hyperkähler manifold and let

be base point free such that

,

. Then the general element in a connected component of
\Sigma_{d,n}^{t}
, where
\dim_{\bC}(X)=n
, has base point free polarization. The same holds for
\Upsilon_{d,n}^{t}
.

Proof.

A very general deformation of
(X,L)
, call it
(X_{A},L_{A})
, has Picard rank equal to 1 hence, by the Projectivity criteria in Theorem

, the line bundle

is ample since

(X_{AF},L_{AF})
such that

is ample, base point free and satisfy the same numerical properties of

. Consequently, the element

defines an element of
\Sigma_{d,n}^{t}
. We can conclude that the generic element of
\Sigma_{d,n}^{t}
, in the same connected component as
(X,L)
, has base point free polarization since the property is open in families by Corollary
4.5
and the connected components are irreducible by Proposition

.
∎

Let

be integers with

\Sigma_{d,n}^{t}\neq\emptyset\iff\begin{cases}t|(2n-2)\\

\Upsilon_{d,n}^{t}\neq\emptyset\iff\begin{cases}t|(2n+2)\\

Suppose
\Sigma_{d,n}^{t}
is non empty, then we can pick an element

, we can find a K3 surface

and a line bundle

on

such that

for some integers

satisfies the following numerical properties

\displaystyle a^{2}q(L)-2b^{2}(n-1)

\displaystyle=2d,

\displaystyle(a,2b(n-1))

\displaystyle=t,

\displaystyle(a,b)

\displaystyle=1.

From the second equation, we immediately obtain

We would like to conclude with one more remark.

Recall the following notion from
[
8
]
(Definition 1.8.4).

Let
X
be a projective variety and
B
an ample line bundle on
X
that is generated by its global sections. A coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X
is

-regular with respect to
B
if

H^{i}(X,\mathcal{F}\otimes B^{\otimes(m-i)})=0

for

.

What we are interested in is the following proposition:

Proposition 5.11

(
[
8
]
, Prop 1.8.22)
.

Let
X
be an irreducible projective variety of dimension
n
and
B
an ample line bundle that is generated by its global sections and let
N
be a line bundle that is 0-regular with respect to
B
. Then
N\otimes B
is very ample.

From which we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
X
be a hyperkähler manifold of complex dimension
n
and let
L
be an ample base point free line bundle on
X
. Then
L^{n+2}
is very ample.

Proof.

If we can apply Proposition
5.11
with
B=L
and
N=L^{n+1}
, then
N\otimes B=L^{n+2}
is very ample and we are done. We need to show that

H^{i}(X,N\otimes B^{\otimes(0-i)})=0\quad\forall i>0

which, with our choices of
N
and
B
, becomes

H^{i}(X,L^{\otimes(n+1-i)})=0\quad\forall i>0.

If
i<n+1
, then Kodaira vanishing can be applied.
If
i\geq n+1
, then the cohomology group is trivial since
h^{k,l}(X,H)=0
for any line bundle
H\in\Pic(X)
, if

.
∎

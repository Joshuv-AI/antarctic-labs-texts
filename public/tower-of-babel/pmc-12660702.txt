Thorium-234 as a tracer for deep-sea mining sediment plume deposition
Authors: Bryan J. O’Malley, Patrick T. Schwing, Sophia K. Chernoch, Rebekka A. Larson, Michael Clarke, Leigh M. Marsh, Alastair J. M. Lough, Gregg R. Brooks
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Deep-sea mining for polymetallic nodules is currently exploratory, but commercial-scale operations require indicators of environmental change to support regulatory thresholds and inform adaptive management. In the Clarion-Clipperton Zone, where background sedimentation rates are low, seafloor imagery has validated mining plume deposition but cannot resolve repeated sedimentation as nodules become buried. Thorium-234 (234Th), a naturally occurring radionuclide with a 24.1-day half-life and strong particle reactivity, serves as a high-resolution geochemical tracer. Here we apply sedimentary 234Th to identify the spatial extent of plume deposition following the NORI-D mining test. Excess 234Th (234Thxs) activity was low at baseline but elevated after mining and declined to background within 1–2 km of the directly mined area. Results suggest that mining plumes scavenge and redistribute 234Thxs, establishing a geochemical benchmark for plume extent and an operational tool for tracing recent sedimentation under future commercial-scale mining scenarios.

Introduction

Commercial scale deep-sea mining has the potential to extract large quantities of polymetallic nodules from the abyssal Clarion-Clipperton Zone (CCZ). These nodules are rich in metals such as manganese, nickel, copper, and cobalt, which are sought after to support growing demand for critical minerals1,2. However, removal of seafloor substrate and deposition of sediment plumes will likely impact benthic habitats, and the spatial extent of such impacts must be considered. It is necessary to identify suitable indicators that can inform regulatory thresholds to determine the spatial extent and magnitude of plume deposition, which would aid adaptive management measures3–5.

The CCZ spans over 6 million km2 of seafloor in the eastern central Pacific, at abyssal plain depths of 4000-6000 m. It is located between the Clarion and Clipperton fracture zones from 5° to 20° N and 115° to 160° W6. Polymetallic nodules are abundant throughout the CCZ, having formed over millions of years by the hydrogenetic and diagenetic precipitation of metals from seawater and sedimentary porewater. The natural sediment accumulation rate is low, ranging from 0.2-1.15 cm per thousand years in the eastern CCZ, where sediments are predominantly fine-grained silts and clays7–10. The occurrence of fine-grained abyssal sediments raises concerns about the generation of sediment plumes from mining activities, as fine particulate matter may be susceptible to entrainment and advection over long distances by deep ocean currents or mesoscale eddies11,12.

Establishing environmental baselines and conducting small-scale test mining are fundamental to a precautionary and adaptive management approach, prioritizing the assessment of potential impacts before full-scale operations commence5. Robust pre-disturbance baselines are vital for quantifying impacts and evaluating the impacts to and recovery of benthic biota, ensuring effective mitigation strategies and adherence to environmental management standards3,13. Lessons from related deep-sea industries like oil and gas, such as gaps in baseline data that hindered impact assessment following the Deepwater Horizon blowout event, underscore this requirement14,15. Multi-year baselines that capture natural variability provide the most accurate point of comparison for evaluating disturbance impacts and recovery13.

From October to November 2022, Nauru Ocean Resources Inc. (NORI) - a wholly-owned subsidiary of The Metals Company - conducted the first fully integrated nodule collection system test in over 40 years in the NORI-D contract area16. This area is the southeasternmost contract area located between −116 to −118° W and 9.8 to 11.2° N. During the system test, a remotely-operated prototype collector vehicle (PCV), measuring 6 meters wide and 12 meters long, maneuvered 80 km generating mining tracks on the seafloor and collected ~3000 tonnes of polymetallic nodules16.

Collector vehicles generate benthic sediment plumes through seafloor disturbance from both vehicle movement and nodule collection processes. Observations from a pre-prototype collector component test in 2021, conducted by Global Sea Mineral Resources, revealed distinct plume phase characteristics. The initial phase formed a negatively buoyant gravity-driven turbidity current with 92-98 percent of the particulate matter remaining within two meters of the seafloor17. Following local settlement of the turbidity current, the residual fine-fraction plume was advected by ambient bottom currents, with AUV imagery detecting deposition up to 1.8 km from the source18,19.

While AUV imagery effectively captures initial deposition patterns in undisturbed environments, monitoring the cumulative impacts from a commercial-scale mining operation, where repeated sediment deposition may occur over the lifetime of the mine (years-decades), requires additional tools capable of distinguishing newly deposited sediment layers. Over time, the progressive burial of nodules will reduce visual contrast of nodules with the surrounding sediment, limiting the utility of imagery for detecting incremental sedimentation.

Thorium-234 (234Th) is a naturally occurring, short-lived radioisotope (half-life = 24.1 days) produced continuously in seawater by the alpha decay of uranium-238 (238U), which is conservative throughout the water column. 234Th is distributed throughout the marine water column, typically exhibiting depleted activity in the photic zone due to scavenging by settling particles and phytoplankton20. 234Th generally reaches equilibrium with its parent isotope, 238U, below the photic zone, and has been detected at depths exceeding 5000 m (Supplementary Fig. S1)20,21.

As a particle-reactive element, 234Th is readily scavenged by suspended particles like clay minerals and organic matter22,23. With a large ionic radius and relatively simple electronic configuration, 234Th complexes readily with inorganic (nitrate, phosphate, sulfate) and organic molecules (humics, polysaccharides, biomolecules)23–25. Laboratory experiments have demonstrated strong adsorption of 234Th onto various materials, including nanoporous silicates and silanol-rich clays at pH values typical of marine sediments25.

Owing to its particle reactivity and short half-life, 234Th has been widely used to estimate marine particle export fluxes and seafloor deposition26,27. 234Th has also been used to trace the high-resolution sedimentation of marine oil snow following the Deepwater Horizon spill and has also been applied to study resuspension dynamics in coastal, lacustrine, and deep-sea settings14,28–31. Depletions in bottom water 234Th inventories and temporal variability have been associated with scavenging by resuspended particles29. The short half-life and particle reactivity prove 234Th as a powerful tool for tracing recent episodic sediment resuspension driven by storms, currents, and tides, demonstrating its utility for investigating resuspension-deposition processes and its potential value for tracking deep-sea mining sedimentation.

The International Seabed Authority (ISA), the regulatory body in international waters, recommends in its guidance ISBA/25/LTC/6/Rev.1 the use of radionuclide measurements such as 210Pb and 234Th to assess bioturbation and sediment mixing32. This study repurposes short-lived 234Th as a high-resolution tracer for recent sediment deposition from mining plumes, aligning with ISA Recommendations on ‘ground-truthing’ plume models32.

The conceptual basis of this study is grounded in the naturally low sedimentation rates in the CCZ and the short half-life of 234Th. Together, these conditions are expected to yield low sedimentary surface activities of decay-corrected excess 234Th (234Thxs) (Fig. 1a). Sediments resuspended by deep-sea mining activities are expected to settle on a timescale of days to weeks33, making short-lived 234Thxs a suitable tracer for detecting the spatial influence of mining plumes. 234Thxs reflects the 234Th adsorbed to settling particulate matter in the water column, which are delivered to the seafloor during deposition events.Fig. 1Conceptual diagram of 234Th redistribution during sediment plume formation and settlement from a deep-sea mining test.a Natural distribution of dissolved 234Th in the water column and low 234Thxs activity in surface sediments under baseline conditions in the CCZ. b Resuspension of seabed sediments by the PCV creates a plume of particles that scavenge dissolved 234Th from the water column. c Deposition of plume particles enriched in scavenged 234Th, generating a surface sediment 234Thxs signature that declines with distance from the source.

During plume generation, suspended particles scavenge 234Th present from surrounding bottom waters, resulting in a localized depletion of the water column inventory (Fig. 1b)21,31. As particles are advected from the source, additional scavenging occurs along the transport pathway. Consequently, as deposition occurs, sedimentary 234Th activity is expected to increase with distance from the point-source until a deposition threshold is reached, beyond which settling rates are insufficient to leave a detectable geochemical signature (Fig. 1c).

In this work, we demonstrate that sedimentary 234Thxs provides a sensitive tracer of recent deposition from a benthic deep-sea mining plume. We first establish a multi-year baseline of surface sediment 234Thxs activity to characterize natural temporal variability in the NORI-D area. Against this background, we detect elevated 234Thxs activities within 1–2 km of the prototype collector tracks, while sites beyond this range are indistinguishable from baseline and controls. These results highlight 234Thxs as a sensitive, short-timescale indicator of sedimentation, supporting its application for monitoring benthic plume extent and informing environmental management strategies.

Results and discussion

Baseline natural variability of 234Th

Characterizing natural variability in sedimentary 234Th is essential for distinguishing mining-related signals from background conditions in the NORI-D contract area (Fig. 2). The collector test area (CTA) spans a narrow bathymetric range (4248 − 4336 m), with <90 m difference between the shallowest and deepest points, minimizing the potential influence of depth-related factors on 234Th distribution (Fig. 2a). Together, these provide a multi-year perspective on natural temporal variability in surface (0–1 cm) sedimentary 234Thxs across the test field (Fig. 2a). Measured 234Thxs activities were consistently low, reflecting the region’s naturally low sedimentation rates. In 2021 (n = 34), values ranged from 0 to 2.74 dpm g-1, with a mean of 0.42 ± 0.13 dpm g-1 (Fig. 3a, b; Supplementary Table S1). Post-PCV test mining analysis

A clear spatial distribution of surface 234Thxs activities was observed (Figs. 3 and 4) in samples collected in November-December 2022, days to weeks after the PCV mining test. In the post-PCV test control reference zone (n = 4), located ~7 km northwest of the mined area, 234Thxs activities ranged from 0.36 to 1.05 dpm g-1, with a mean of 0.64 ± 0.15 dpm g-1 (Fig. 3a, b). These values were statistically indistinguishable from those recorded in the 2021 and 2022 baseline campaigns (Supplementary Fig. S2), consistent with the absence of mining-related deposition in this area. AUV imagery further confirmed this, showing no visible signs of sedimentation at the control sites (Fig. 3c).

Within the directly mined area (site TF_007), located at the point source of plume generation, 234Thxs activities were relatively low, ranging from 0.23 to 1.52 dpm g-1 (n = 5; mean = 0.69 ± 0.22 dpm g-1; Fig. 3a, b). Several factors potentially contribute to these low values. First, the PCV likely stripped away the natural surface sediment layer, where most 234Thxs accumulates. Second, the highest concentrations of resuspended sediment were present near the tracks, rapidly depleting the bottom water 234Th inventory through scavenging. 234Th adsorption is most effective with finer sediments due to their higher surface area-to-volume ratio, which allows for increased scavenging and adsorption through surface complexation22–24. Consequently, the resuspension of coarser sediments with lower surface area-to-volume ratios near the tracks may have resulted in less efficient 234Th scavenging and lower 234Thxs activities in the surface sediments redeposited at the site of the mining tracks (TF_007). AUV imagery corroborated this interpretation, revealing removal of sediment and nodules within the mined track (Fig. 3c).

234Thxs activities increased with distance from the mining tracks at sampling sites proximal to the mining activity. This trend is evident in significantly elevated activities at sites TF_032 ( ~ 200 m) and TF_025 ( ~ 500 m) northeast of the mined track. At TF_032 (n = 2), values were varied between 3.1 to 3.9 dpm g-1 (mean = 3.50 ± 0.4 dpm g-1), while TF_025 (n = 5) exhibited even higher values, ranging from 5.5 to 6.31 dpm g-1 (mean = 5.88 ± 0.14 dpm g-1) (Fig. 3a, b). Activities at both sites were significantly higher than those in all baseline (2021, 2022) and post-PCV control samples (p < 0.0001; Supplementary Fig. S2). Estimated mean differences in 234Thxs between TF_025 and the 2021 baseline, 2022 baseline, and post-PCV control were −5.46, −4.81, and −5.24 dpm g-1, respectively (all p < 0.01). For TF_032, differences were −3.08, −2.43, and −2.86 dpm g-1, respectively (all p < 0.01) (Supplementary Fig. S2). These values represent a 3–8-fold increase relative to background conditions, reflecting a sedimentation signal from the mining plume (Fig. 4).

The elevated 234Thxs at site TF_025 may be further explained by local bathymetry. A shallow seafloor depression of ~10 meters (Fig. 2b) likely enhanced sediment deposition. This is consistent with prior findings that mining plumes propagate as gravity-driven turbidity currents, shaped by seafloor topography17. AUV imagery supports this interpretation, revealing a surface layer of sediment visibly coating nodules at both TF_025 and TF_032 (Fig. 3c). These results further validate the utility of 234Th as a high-resolution tracer of recent fine-grained sediment deposition.

At site TF_033 (n = 1), located 1 km northeast of the directly mined area the 234Thxs activity was 1.86 dpm g-1, which was elevated compared to the baseline 2021 and post-PCV control reference zone but within the broader range of natural variability (Fig. 3a, b). This level is consistent with light sedimentation from the distal edge of the mining plume, but also falls within the envelope of background processes such as benthic storms, mesoscale eddies, or bottom currents that may cause episodic resuspension12,31.

The decline in 234Thxs from TF_025 to TF_033 is likely due to reduced particle flux and organic loading with increasing distance from the plume source. While scavenging efficiency is influenced by the surface-area-to-volume ratio of particles, the observed trend primarily reflects diminishing particle supply. AUV imagery at TF_033 reveals a thin dusting of sediment on the seafloor, but nodules remain clearly visible, indicating light but detectable deposition (Fig. 3c).

At sites STM_174 and STM_173, located 2 km and 3 km northeast of the mining tracks, 234Thxs activities ranged from 0.21 to 0.68 and 0.21 to 0.81 dpm g-1, respectively (n = 3 for each site), with both sites averaging 0.49 dpm g-1 (Fig. 3a, b). These values were not significantly different from 2021 or 2022 baseline levels (Supplementary Fig. S2), indicating that the influence of the mining plume was no longer detectable beyond 2 km (Fig. 4).

The return to background 234Thxs levels at STM_174 and STM_173 is supported by AUV imagery, which revealed no discernible sedimentation features relative to the control site (Fig. 3c). In contrast, TF_033, located 1 km from the mined area, exhibited elevated 234Thxs activity. This pattern suggests that the transition from detectable to undetectable plume deposition occurred between 1 and 2 km from the source. Collectively, these results constrain the spatial extent of mining-related sedimentation within ~2 km of the collector track.

This study provides geochemical validation of the spatial extent of sedimentation from a benthic sediment plume generated by a deep-sea nodule mining test. Previous modeling studies predicted sediment blanketing up to 2.5–4 km from a 4-day PCV operation under typical abyssal current regimes11. In contrast, the 234Th data from the NORI test indicate a more constrained depositional footprint of 1–2 km. This difference likely reflects the influence of local bathymetry, site-specific hydrodynamic conditions during the trial, and potential differences in collector vehicle design.

These results support the objectives of regulators, such as the ISA, which emphasize developing best practices for monitoring deep-sea mining impacts32. This geochemical approach is particularly valuable for monitoring cumulative impacts, as commercial-scale mining operations would involve multiple collector vehicles operating continuously over decades, potentially leading to repeated sediment deposition across extensive areas. Visual methods may become less effective over time as sediment accumulation reduces contrast between nodules and the surrounding seafloor. In contrast, 234Thxs provides a geochemical signal of sedimentation within a ~ 100-day timeframe, allowing recent deposition events to be identified even where visual evidence is no longer apparent. By providing a tool to quantify spatial sediment deposition and distinguish newly deposited layers, this study contributes to the development of methodologies that can inform operational thresholds, assess environmental impacts, validate plume dispersion models, and support adaptive management strategies.

This study contributes to the growing body of 234Th research27, and builds on previous radionuclide-based resuspension studies29–31, demonstrating that 234Th is a sensitive and robust geochemical indicator of benthic sedimentation associated with deep-sea mining activity. Sediment samples collected after the fully integrated nodule collection system test at NORI-D revealed a clear spatial gradient in 234Thxs activity, indicating detectable sedimentation between 1 and 2 km from the mined tracks. At TF_033, located 1 km from the mining site, 234Thxs activity was elevated but remained within the range of baseline variability, suggesting that sedimentation at that distance may be comparable to naturally occurring resuspension events such as benthic storms or eddies.

The results provide initial evidence validating the conceptual hypothesis that 234Th can serve as a geochemical indicator for recent sedimentation. The baseline dataset confirms naturally low sedimentation rates in the region, while post-PCV test results show that sediment transport is shaped by local bathymetry, bottom currents, and sediment characteristics. Unlike AUV imagery, which cannot resolve the timing of sediment deposition, 234Thxs provides a ~ 100-day detection window and can identify recent deposition events on a weekly timescale. This distinction is especially critical in a commercial mining context, where vehicles may revisit mined areas months or years later. As sediment accumulates and visual contrast diminishes, 234Thxs remains one of the few available indicators of new deposition, supporting monitoring of cumulative impacts.

234Th offers a valuable tool to inform operational thresholds, provide metrics for environmental monitoring and management plans (EMMPs), and validate plume dispersion models. Future work should integrate 234Thxs measurements with biological and ecological indicators to assess potential impacts on benthic communities. While 234Thxs reveals the spatial extent of deposition, it does not directly quantify ecological response, which remains a critical research priority. Although this study focuses on sediment-based excess 234Th to trace recent deposition, future efforts should include direct measurements of 234Th activity in suspended particles within the plume and incorporate mechanistic scavenging models to better constrain non-steady-state dynamics. These additions would improve estimates of total mass deposition and sedimentation rates. Complementary radionuclides, such as 210Pb, though limited in temporal resolution due to their longer half-life, could be used to examine historical deposition from legacy mining tests34. Together, these approaches offer a framework for assessing the spatial extent of plume-driven sedimentation and for developing science-based strategies to monitor environmental impacts from deep-sea mining.

Methods

Sampling campaigns

Sediment multicores were collected using an Oktopus GmbH 20-core multicore, equipped with 60 cm long core tubes with an inner diameter of 95 mm, during two baseline campaigns (2021, 2022) and one post-PCV test campaign (2022) in the NORI-D contract area of the CCZ. Baseline campaigns were conducted from May to June, 2021 (Campaign 5D) and from August to October, 2022 (Campaign 7 A), the latter of which occurred immediately prior to NORI’s PCV test in October, 2022. In total, 34 and 26 multicores, respectively, were collected on baseline campaigns 2021 and 2022 for 234Th measurement. The post-PCV test campaign was conducted from November to December, 2022 (Campaign 7B2), directly following the completion of the nodule mining test (days-weeks), in order to sample for immediate impacts. To assess the spatial extent and impact of sediment plume deposition from the PCV test, a strategic sampling plan was developed based on existing baseline data of current direction and speed. The survey design consisted of three main components: directly impacted tracks, a gradient of potential sedimentation sites, and a control area. A series of in situ sensors, such as ADCPs, camera landers, sediment traps, and sedimentation plates were deployed prior to the PCV test to capture the direction and magnitude of the prevailing current during the time of testing35.

Samples were collected from sites located within the mining tracks to evaluate the immediate effects of the PCV test on sediment 234Thxs activities. Sampling sites were positioned along a transect extending from the mining tracks in the direction of the prevailing current at the time of the PCV test. These sites were selected at increasing distances from the tracks (i.e., 200 m, 500 m, 1 km, 2 km, and 3 km) to investigate any gradual changes in sediment deposition and 234Thxs activities as the plume dispersed. A control area was established approximately 7 km northwest of the mining tracks, in a location expected to be unaffected by the sediment plume. Samples from the control area were used to compare 234Thxs activities with baseline levels and to verify that the area remained uninfluenced by the PCV test. The collection of multi-cores within the tracks of the PCV was simultaneously monitored by an ROV to ensure sampling accuracy (Supplementary Fig. S3). 24 multicores were collected for 234Th measurement during the post-PCV test campaign (7B2).

At-sea sampling protocols

Once the multicore was recovered on deck, cores were secured on a custom rack, measured, photographed, and assessed for quality with a QA/QC matrix that examines metrics such as “Nodule drag”, “Topwater intact”, “Sediment-water interface”, and “Undisturbed stratigraphy”. All sediment cores from Baseline 2021, Baseline 2022, and the post-PCV test campaigns were extruded at sea and subsampled. The 0-1 cm interval was analyzed and reported in this study. The top water was carefully siphoned off to prevent sediment loss. Nodules present at the core surface were removed with forceps, rinsed with milli-q water into the sample bag, and stored separately. Subsampling was conducted using pre-cut and measured polycarbonate rings placed on top of the core tube (of equal diameter), with sediment sections obtained by slicing a thin plastic sheet between the ring and core tube. Sediment samples were stored in WhirlPak bags, frozen, then freeze-dried before analysis.

Short-lived radioisotope (234Th) analysis

Freeze-dried sediment samples were analyzed by gamma spectroscopy on Canberra High-Purity Germanium Coaxial Planar and Well Photon Detectors for total 234Th (63 KeV). Each sample was weighed, homogenized, and sealed in a plastic container before analysis. Total 234Th activities, in disintegrations per minute per gram (dpm g-1), were corrected for counting time, detector efficiency, and self-absorption using the IAEA-447 standard, following methods from Brooks et al. 28 and described as follows. To account for self-absorption and matrix effects, standard calibration curves were generated by analyzing a series of IAEA-447 standards at varying masses (1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 12, 15, 17, 20, 30, 40, and 50 g). These were used to build a geometry-corrected efficiency template relating sample weight to detector response. Sample-specific corrections were applied to derive absolute 234Th activity in disintegrations per minute per gram dry sediment (dpmg−1). Activities were corrected for counting time, background radiation, and detector efficiency. Instrumental error associated with detector efficiency was estimated to be <3% of measured activity consistent with Brooks et al. 28 and considered negligible relative to natural sample variability. All surface samples (0-1 cm) were analyzed within 90 days of initial collection. 234Th activity supported by the in-situ decay of its parent isotope 238U was determined by reanalyzing each 0–1 cm sample more than 120 days after collection, allowing all excess 234Th to decay (i.e., after ~5 half-lives). Supported activity was subtracted from total 234Th to calculate excess 234Th (234Thxs). Measured 234Thxs values were decay-corrected to account for time elapsed between collection and counting and are reported as mass-normalized (dpm g⁻¹) activities.

All reported values and replicates are derived from independent sediment cores, with no technical replicates from the same core. Values are presented as mean ± standard error of the mean (SE), unless otherwise noted.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses and data visualizations were performed using R statistical software (version 4.1.1)36. The ggplot2 package was used to create plots, including bar plots and raincloud plots, to visualize the distribution and variability of 234Thxs activities across sampling sites and campaigns. To assess whether there were statistically significant differences in 234Thxs activities among the sampling sites, a one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was conducted using the ‘aov’ function in R. Following a significant ANOVA result, a Tukey’s Honest Significant Difference (HSD) post hoc test was performed to determine which specific sites differed significantly from one another. The Tukey’s HSD test was conducted using the ‘TukeyHSD’ function in R, which computes pairwise comparisons between group means and provides adjusted p-values to account for multiple comparisons. The results of the post hoc analysis were used to identify the statistical differences in 234Thxs activities between sites and sampling levels (Supplementary Fig. S2). Spatial maps depicting the sampling locations and 234Thxs activities were created using QGIS (version 3.32)37. Sampling sites were plotted on the maps using their geographic coordinates, and the 234Thxs activities were represented using a color gradient to visualize the spatial distribution of the radionuclide concentrations. Ocean Data View software38 (version ODV 5.8.2) was used to generate Supplementary Fig. S1.

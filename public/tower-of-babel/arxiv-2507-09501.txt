Faedo-Galerkin Approximations for the nonlinear Deterministic Constrained Modified Swift-Hohenberg Equation
Saeed Ahmed 1, Javed Hussain 2

Abstract
In this paper, we study the well-posedness of the nonlinear deterministic constrained modified Swift-Hohenberg equation; this equation belongs to class of amplitude equations which describe the appearance of pattern formation in nature. The proof for existence and uniqueness is based on the Faedo-Galerkin compactness method.

Introduction

Doelman et al.

studied the following modified Swift-Hohenberg equation for the first time in 2003

From the formation of patterns to turbulence, the study of nonlinear differential equations has played a pivotal role in understanding physical phenomena. Of these equations, the Swift-Hohenberg equation has attracted scholars because it describes systems involving pattern-forming instabilities, such as Rayleigh–Benard convection and optical systems. Nonlinearity and constraints have been introduced to understand physical systems with more complex behaviors
yochelis2006excitable
;
burke2006localized
;
kozyreff2006asymptotics
. More precisely, higher-order nonlinearities and domain constraints were introduced to comprehend localized patterns and complex spatiotemporal dynamics

2

It has been observed that the analytical solutions of various PDEs are difficult to establish. Therefore, in this scenario, many approximation techniques are used. Along with Semi group theory and Sobolev spaces, the Faedo-Galerkin method is a potent tool for handling these types of higher-order PDEs
temam2001navier
;
lions2012nonhomogeneous
;
robinson2001infinite
. The Faedo-Galerkin method is beneficial in PDE analysis for finding the approximate solution to the partial differential equation containing nonlinear terms and higher-order operators. In this method, the projection of an infinite-dimensional problem onto a finite-dimensional subspace enables the construction of an approximate solution that converges to the original solution as the dimension of the subspace increases. This method has been rigorously presented in
Lions

J. Hussain
Hussain

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}{\langle Au(p),Av(p)\rangle}_{\mathcal{H}}~dp~~~~~~~~or

\displaystyle\langle A^{\dagger}u,A^{\dagger}v\rangle_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{E})}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}{\langle u(p),v(p)\rangle}_{\mathcal{H}}~dp

(36)

Let us consider the first term of equation (
33
) on the right-hand side.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle A_{k}u_{k},\xi(t)\phi\right\rangle~dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\pi_{k}Au_{k},\xi(t)\phi\right\rangle~dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle Au_{k},\xi(t)\pi_{k}\phi\right\rangle~dt

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{k}
it is clear that
\pi_{k}\phi=\phi
. It follows that:

\displaystyle{}\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle A_{k}u_{k},\xi(t)\phi\right\rangle~dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle Au_{k},\xi(t)\phi\right\rangle~dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle u_{k},A^{\dagger}\xi(t)\phi\right\rangle_{D(A)}~dt

(37)

Now, using
A^{\dagger}\xi(t)\phi\in\mathcal{L}^{2}(0,T;D(A))
,
u_{k}\to u
in
\mathcal{L}^{2}(0,T;D(A))
and equation (
4
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle A_{k}u_{k},\xi(t)\phi\right\rangle~dt-\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle u,A^{\dagger}\xi(t)\phi\right\rangle_{D(A)}~dt=\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle u_{k}-u,A^{\dagger}\xi(t)\phi\right\rangle_{D(A)}~dt\rightarrow 0~~~as~~k\to\infty

Consider the second term on the equation (
33
) on the right side.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle F_{k}(u_{k}(t)),\xi(t)\phi\right\rangle~dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle\pi_{k}F(u_{k}(t)),\xi(t)\phi\right\rangle~dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle F(u_{k}(t)),\xi(t)\pi_{k}\phi\right\rangle~dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle F(u_{k}(t)),\xi(t)\phi\right\rangle~dt

Using the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, the following can be deduced:

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle F_{k}(u_{k}(t))-F(u_{k}(t)),\xi(t)\phi\right\rangle~dt\right|\leq\sqrt{\int_{0}^{T}{\left|F_{k}(u_{k}(t))-F(u_{k}(t))\right|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}}~dt}~\sqrt{\int_{0}^{T}{\left|\xi(t)\phi\right|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}}~dt}

Then,
\sqrt{\int_{0}^{T}{\left|F_{k}(u_{k}(t))-F(u_{k}(t))\right|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}}~dt}~\sqrt{\int_{0}^{T}{\left|\xi(t)\phi\right|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}}~dt}\to 0
when
k\to\infty
. It is sufficient to prove that
\int_{0}^{T}{\left|F_{k}(u_{k}(t))-F(u_{k}(t))\right|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
.

In addition, we used the following Cauchy-Schwartz inequality:

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}x_{j}\right)^{2}=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}1\cdot x_{j}\right)^{2}\leq i\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{i}x^{2}_{j}

(38)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}{\left|F(u_{k}(t))-F(u(t))\right|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{0}^{T}\begin{bmatrix}\left(\|u_{k}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+\|u\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+\|u_{k}\|_{\mathcal{V}}\|u\|_{\mathcal{V}}\right)\\
+\left(\|u_{k}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2n-1}+\|u\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2n-1}\right)(\|u_{k}\|_{\mathcal{V}}+\|u\|_{\mathcal{V}})\\
+\left(\|u_{k}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2n}+\|u\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2n}\right)+\left(1+\|u_{k}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+\|u\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\end{bmatrix}^{2}\|u_{k}-u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}}~dt

\displaystyle\leq 4K\int_{0}^{T}\begin{bmatrix}\left(\|u_{k}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+\|u\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+\|u_{k}\|_{\mathcal{V}}\|u\|_{\mathcal{V}}\right)^{2}\\
+\left(\|u_{k}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2n-1}+\|u\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2n-1}\right)^{2}(\|u_{k}\|_{\mathcal{V}}+\|u\|_{\mathcal{V}})^{2}\\
+\left(\|u_{k}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2n}+\|u\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2n}\right)^{2}+\left(1+\|u_{k}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+\|u\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}\end{bmatrix}\|u_{k}-u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}}~dt

\displaystyle\leq 4K\int_{0}^{T}\begin{bmatrix}\left(M^{2}+N^{2}+MN\right)^{2}\\
+\left(M^{2n-1}+N^{2n-1}\right)^{2}(M+N)^{2}\\
+\left(M^{2n}+N^{2n}\right)^{2}+\left(1+M^{2}+N^{2}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}\end{bmatrix}\|u_{k}-u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}}~dt

\displaystyle\leq L~\|u_{k}-u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{V})}\to 0~~~\text{as}~~~~k\to\infty,

where

\displaystyle M=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}{\|u_{k}(t)\|^{2}},~~N

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}{\|u(t)\|^{2}}~~\text{as both}~~~u_{k},~u\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{V})~~\text{so they have finite value.}

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle:=4K\begin{bmatrix}\left(M^{2}+N^{2}+MN\right)^{2}+\left(M^{2n-1}+N^{2n-1}\right)^{2}(M+N)^{2}\\
+\left(M^{2n}+N^{2n}\right)^{2}+\left(1+M^{2}+N^{2}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}\end{bmatrix}<\infty

Finally, we consider
\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle u_{k}(t),\xi^{\prime}(t)\phi\right\rangle~dt
in the inequality (
33
).

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle u_{k}(t)-u(t),\xi^{\prime}(t)\phi\right\rangle~dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\|u_{k}(t)-u(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}~\|\xi^{\prime}(t)\phi\|_{\mathcal{V}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\int_{0}^{T}{\|u_{k}(t)-u(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}}~dt}~~\sqrt{\int_{0}^{T}{\|\xi^{\prime}(t)\phi\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}}~dt}\to 0~~~\text{as}~~k\to\infty

(39)

Therefore, we can pass the limit on to Equation (
33
). That is:

\displaystyle{}\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle u(t),\xi^{\prime}(t)\phi\right\rangle~dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle Au(t),\xi(t)\phi\right\rangle~dt-\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle F(u(t)),\xi(t)\phi\right\rangle~dt-\left\langle u_{0},\xi(0)\phi\right\rangle

(40)

\displaystyle~~~~~\text{For all}~~\phi\in\cup_{k=1}H_{k}

Because
\cup_{k=1}H_{k}
is dense in
\mathcal{V}
, (
40
) holds for any
\phi\in\mathcal{V}
and
\xi\in C_{0}^{1}\left([0,T]\right)
. Therefore,
u
satisfies evolution equation (
2.1
) in
\mathcal{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H})
.

Let us now consider the equation for the initial value. Consider
\phi\in\mathcal{V}
and
\xi\in C_{0}^{1}\left([0,T]\right)
such that
\xi(0)=1
. Multiplying
\xi(t)\phi
by equation (
2.1
) and applying integration by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle{}\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle u(t),\xi^{\prime}(t)\phi\right\rangle~dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle Au(t),\xi(t)\phi\right\rangle~dt-\int_{0}^{T}\left\langle F(u(t)),\xi(t)\phi\right\rangle~dt-\left\langle u(0),\xi(0)\phi\right\rangle

(41)

By comparing equations (
40
) and (
41
) and using the fact that
\xi(0)=1
we can deduce that

\displaystyle\left\langle u(0)-u_{0},\phi\right\rangle=0,~~~~~~\text{for all}~~~\phi\in\mathcal{V}

(42)

As
\mathcal{V}
is dense in
\mathcal{H}
, we can infer that
u(0)-u_{0}=0
which implies
u(0)=u_{0}
. Therefore,
u
solves problem (
2.1
).

We have already proved that
u\in\mathcal{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H})
in (
32
). For
u\in X_{T}
, we must show that
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}\in\mathcal{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H})
. As we have shown that
u
solves the problem (
2.1
), so consider the following equation

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=-Au+F(u(t))

Now, by taking the inner product in
\mathcal{H}
, integrating the equation from
0
to
T
on both sides, and applying Minkowski’s inequality for
p=2
, we can deduce that

\displaystyle{}\int_{0}^{T}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}\right|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}~dt\leq\left(\int_{0}^{T}{\left|Au(t)\right|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}}~dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\int_{0}^{T}{\left|F(u(t))\right|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}}~dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq|u|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{E})}+\left(\int_{0}^{T}{\left|F(u(t))\right|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}}~dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(43)

We know that
u\in\mathcal{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{E})
referred to in (
30
), which implies that
|u|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{E})}<\infty
.

Let us consider the second term
\left(\int_{0}^{T}{\left|F(u(t))\right|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}}~dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}{\left|F(u(t))\right|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}}~dt

\displaystyle\leq 4K\int_{0}^{T}\begin{bmatrix}\left(\|u\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+\|u\|_{\mathcal{V}}\right)^{2}+2\|u\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{4n}+\|u\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{4n-4}\end{bmatrix}\|u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}}~dt

\displaystyle\leq 4K\int_{0}^{T}\begin{bmatrix}\left(N^{2}+N\right)^{2}+2N^{4n}+N^{4n-4}\end{bmatrix}N^{2}~dt\leq CT<\infty

Where
N=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}{\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}}<\infty~~\text{and}~~~C:=4K\begin{bmatrix}\left(N^{2}+N\right)^{2}+2N^{4n}+N^{4n-4}\end{bmatrix}N^{2}<\infty
because
u\in\mathcal{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{V})
. Thus, from inequality (

), we can deduce that

, and thus,

.

Uniqueness of the solution

Let
\Lambda(t)=u_{1}(t)-u_{2(t)}\in X_{T}
and
\Lambda(0)=u_{1}(0)-u_{2}(0)=0
and
\Lambda(t)
solve problem (
2.1
). In addition, assume that
u_{1}
,
u_{1}\in\mathcal{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{H})
are the two solutions of problem (
2.1
), so it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\|\Lambda\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}}}{2}-\frac{\|\Lambda_{0}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}}}{2}

\displaystyle=-\int^{t}_{0}{\left\|\Lambda^{\prime}(p)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}~dp+\int^{t}_{0}{\langle\Delta^{2}\Lambda(p)-2\Delta\Lambda(p)+\Lambda^{\prime}(p),\Lambda^{\prime}(p)\rangle}~dp

By using the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\|\Lambda\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}}}{2}-\frac{\|\Lambda_{0}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}}}{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\int^{t}_{0}{\left\|\Lambda^{\prime}(p)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}~dp+\int^{t}_{0}\left(\int_{D}|\Delta^{2}\Lambda(p)-2\Delta\Lambda(p)+\Lambda^{\prime}(p)|^{2}~dp\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\left(\int_{D}|\Lambda^{\prime}(p)|^{2}~dp\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}~dp

\displaystyle\leq-\int^{t}_{0}{\left\|\Lambda^{\prime}(p)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}~dp+\int^{t}_{0}{\|\Delta^{2}\Lambda(p)-2\Delta\Lambda(p)+\Lambda^{\prime}(p)\|_{L^{2}}}{\|\Lambda^{\prime}(p)\|_{L^{2}}}~dp

Using the fact that,
ab\leq\frac{a^{2}+b^{2}}{2}
, it follows:

\displaystyle\frac{\|\Lambda\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}}}{2}-\frac{\|\Lambda_{0}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}}}{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\int^{t}_{0}{\left\|\Lambda^{\prime}(p)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}~dp+\frac{1}{2}\int^{t}_{0}{\|\Delta^{2}\Lambda(p)-2\Delta\Lambda(p)+\Lambda^{\prime}(p)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}~dp+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}{\|\Lambda^{\prime}(p)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}~dp

As
u_{0}(0)=0
and
\|\Lambda_{0}\|_{\mathcal{V}}=0
, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\|\Lambda\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}}}{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\int^{t}_{0}{\left\|\Lambda^{\prime}(p)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}~dp+\frac{1}{2}\int^{t}_{0}{\|\Delta^{2}\Lambda(p)-2\Delta\Lambda(p)+\Lambda^{\prime}(p)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}~dp+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}{\|\Lambda^{\prime}(p)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}dp

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2}\int^{t}_{0}{\left\|\Lambda^{\prime}(p)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}~dp+\frac{1}{2}\int^{t}_{0}{\|\Delta^{2}\Lambda(p)-2\Delta\Lambda(p)+\Lambda^{\prime}(p)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}~dp

\displaystyle\|\Lambda\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}}+\int^{t}_{0}{\left\|\Lambda^{\prime}(p)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}~dp

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{0}{\|\Delta^{2}\Lambda(p)-2\Delta u(p)+\Lambda^{\prime}(p)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}~dp\leq\int_{0}^{t}{\|F(u_{1})-F(u_{2})\|^{2}}~dp

But,
\|F(u_{1})-F(u_{2})\|\leq K\begin{bmatrix}\left(\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+\|u_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{V}}\|u_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}}\right)\\
+\left(\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2n-1}+\|u_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2n-1}\right)(\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{V}}+\|u_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}})\\
+\left(\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2n}+\|u_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2n}\right)+\left(1+\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+\|u_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\end{bmatrix}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}}
, it follows:

\displaystyle\|\Lambda\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}}+\int^{t}_{0}{\left\|\Lambda^{\prime}(p)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}~dp

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{0}^{t}{\begin{bmatrix}\left(\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+\|u_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{V}}\|u_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}}\right)\\
+\left(\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2n-1}+\|u_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2n-1}\right)(\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{V}}+\|u_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}})\\
+\left(\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2n}+\|u_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2n}\right)+\left(1+\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+\|u_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\end{bmatrix}^{2}}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}~dp

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{0}^{t}{\begin{bmatrix}\left(\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+\|u_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{V}}\|u_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}}\right)\\
+\left(\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2n-1}+\|u_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2n-1}\right)(\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{V}}+\|u_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}})\\
+\left(\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2n}+\|u_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2n}\right)+\left(1+\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+\|u_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\end{bmatrix}^{2}}\|\Lambda\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}~dp

Using Gronwall’s inequality, we can deduce that

\displaystyle\|\Lambda(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}}

\displaystyle\leq 0\cdot\exp\left(\begin{bmatrix}\left(\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+\|u_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{V}}\|u_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}}\right)\\
+\left(\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2n-1}+\|u_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2n-1}\right)(\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{V}}+\|u_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}})\\
+\left(\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2n}+\|u_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2n}\right)+\left(1+\|u_{1}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+\|u_{2}\|_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\end{bmatrix}\right)^{2}=0

\displaystyle\|\Lambda(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}}

\displaystyle=0\iff\|\Lambda(t)\|_{\mathcal{V}}=0\iff u_{1}(t)=u_{2}(t)

Thus, the solution to Problem (
2.1
) is unique.

∎

5
Conclusion

This study aims to investigate the nonlinear deterministic constrained modified Swift–Hohenberg equation. In this work, we have shown the existence and uniqueness of the solution to the proposed problem using the Faedo-Galerkin Compactness method. In our study, we were able to find a solution restricting
u\in\mathcal{V}\cap\mathcal{M}
, but the solution
u\in\mathcal{V}
is still open to further investigation, as it is difficult to eliminate a constant
a
from the equation
(\ref{SHEq})
for such a case.

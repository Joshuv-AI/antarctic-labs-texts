Algebraic Hyperbolicity of Surfaces in Fano threefolds with Picard number one
Haesong Seo

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the algebraic hyperbolicity of very general surfaces in general Fano threefolds with Picard number one. We completely classify the algebraically hyperbolicity of those surfaces, except for surfaces in weighted hypersurfaces.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification

13D02, 14J45.

1.
Introduction

We will work over
\mathbb{C}
.
A projective variety
X
is
algebraically hyperbolic
if there exist an ample divisor
H
and a real number
\varepsilon>0
such that for any integral curve
C\subset X
, we have

(1.1)

2g(\overline{C})-2\geq\varepsilon\deg_{C}(H)

where
\overline{C}
is the normalization of
C
and
g(\overline{C})
is the genus of
\overline{C}
.

The concept of algebraic hyperbolicity is proposed by Demailly
[
8
]
as an algebraic analogue of Kobayashi hyperbolicity.
A complex manifold is called
Kobayashi hyperbolic
if the Kobayashi pseudometric is nondegenerate.
Brody
[
1
]
proved that a compact complex manifold
X
is Kobayashi hyperbolic if and only if it is
Brody hyperbolic
, i.e., there is no nonconstant entire map
f:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow X
.
Demailly
[
8
]
proved that a (Kobayashi) hyperbolic complex projective manifold is algebraically hyperbolic, and conjectured the converse.

Although it is in general very difficult to determine whether a given variety is algebraically hyperbolic, there has been significant progress toward classifying the algebraic hyperbolicity of hypersurfaces of (almost) homogeneous varieties.
Recently, Hasse and Ilten
[
13
]
introduced the study of the algebraic hyperbolicity of surfaces in toric threefolds.
Based on a technique developed by Ein
[
10
,
11
]
, Pacienza
[
20
]
and Voisin
[
23
,
24
]
, Coskun and Riedl
[
5
,
6
]
completely classified the algebraic hyperbolicity of surfaces in several toric threefolds, including
\mathbb{P}^{3}
,
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
,
\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
,
\mathbb{F}_{e}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
and the blowup of
\mathbb{P}^{3}
at one point, where
\mathbb{F}_{e}=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(e))
.
Following
[
13
]
, Robins
[
22
]
almost classified the algebraic hyperbolicity of surfaces in smooth projective toric threefolds with Picard number two and three.
For higher dimensions, Yeong
[
26
]
completely classified the algebraic hyperbolicity of general hypersurfaces in the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{m}
and the product
\mathbb{P}^{m}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}
, except for sextic threefolds or hypersurfaces of certain bidegrees in
\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
More generally, Mioranci
[
17
]
provided an almost optimal degree bound for hypersurfaces in homogeneous varieties to be algebraically hyperbolic.
Also, Moraga and Yeong
[
18
]
showed that toric projective manifolds satisfy the following hyperbolicity conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1

(
[
18
, Conjecture 1.1]
)
.

Let
L
be an ample Cartier divisor on a smooth projective manifold
X
of dimension
n\geq 2
.
Then a very general element of the linear system
|K_{X}+(3n+1)L|
is algebraically hyperbolic.

One remarkable point is that the idea of Coskun and Riedl
[
5
]
can easily be generalized to complete intersections, or even the zero loci of regular sections of homogeneous vector bundles on (almost) homogeneous varieties.
Hence it would be reasonable to study the algebraic hyperbolicity of surfaces in Fano threefolds, considering Mukai’s description of general Fano threefolds as the zero loci of general sections of homogeneous vector bundles on the product of (weighted) Grassmannians (cf.
[
19
]
, see also
[
4
]
and
[
7
]
).
Here, by general Fano manifolds we mean that they correspond to general points of their moduli.
Note that we cannot argue about surfaces in special Fano threefolds using these tools, e.g., the Mukai-Umemura threefold
X
of genus
12
with
\Aut(X)\simeq\PGL_{2}
.

In
[
5
]
, they proved that very general surfaces of degree
d\geq 5
in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
are algebraically hyperbolic.
Generalizing their work, it is natural to ask whether very general surfaces of degree greater than the Fano index in Fano threefolds with Picard number one are algebraically hyperbolic.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
be a general Fano threefold with Picard number one.
Let
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
be a Picard generator and
r
the Fano index, i.e.,
-K_{X}=\mathcal{O}_{X}(r)
in the Picard group of
X
.
For
a\geq 1
, let
S\in|\mathcal{O}_{X}(a)|
be a very general surface.

•

If
X
can be written as a weighted hypersurface, i.e., one of
1-1
,
1-11
and
1-12
, then
S
is algebraically hyperbolic when
a\geq r+2
.

•

For all the other cases,
S
is algebraically hyperbolic if and only if
a\geq r+1
.

The technique yields more refined classifications for weighted complete intersections.
It is also noteworthy that we obtain a finer bound for the hyperbolicity conjecture
1.1
on general Fano threefolds.

As featured in various papers including
[
5
]
and
[
17
]
, the main obstacle lies in dealing with surfaces of degree
r+1
.
The scroll argument
2.6
is effective for complete intersections, while it does not provide an optimal bound for weighted hypersurfaces.
Further, this argument fails to apply to certain Fano threefolds
1-8
,
1-9
and
1-10
.

To outline the proof, for a given curve
f:C\rightarrow S
, we aim to capture the positivity of the normal sheaf
N_{f/S}
, as it yields the degree bound for the curve.
For this, we use the generalized notion of section-dominating family, which was originally introduced by
[
6
]
.
To deal with weighted hypersurfaces
1-1
,
1-11
and
1-12
, we determine a section-dominating line bundle on the weighted projective space in Theorem
3.1
.
Then we obtain a generic surjection
M_{\mathcal{E}}|_{C}\rightarrow N_{f/S}
from the Lazarsfeld-Mukai bundle of a certain homogeneous sheaf
\mathcal{E}
on the weighted Grassmannian.
In the case of
1-8
,
1-9
and
1-10
, the vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
need not be decomposed into line bundles.
Nevertheless, we prove that the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector bundle
M_{\mathcal{E}}\langle\frac{H}{2}\rangle
is nef on
C
, where
H
is a hyperplane section, which provides a stronger bound.
All the other cases are almost straightforward from the argument of Coskun and Riedl
[
5
]
.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we first introduce the general setup of Coskun and Riedl
[
6
]
, and formulate Bott’s theorem for Grassmannians.
In Section
3
, we present two technical lemmas regarding the weighted projective spaces and vanishing results of certain homogeneous vector bundles on the Grassmannians.
In Section
4
, we prove our main theorem
1.2
by dealing with each deformation class of Fano threefolds separately.

2.2.
Section-dominating family.

Using a generic surjection
M_{\mathcal{E}}|_{Y_{t}}\rightarrow N_{h_{t}/S_{t}}
, one can derive a lower bound for the degree of
N_{h_{t}/S_{t}}
. To deal with the weighted projective spaces, we generalize the notion of a section-dominating collection of line bundles.

Definition 2.2

(cf.
[
6
, Definition 2.3]
)
.

Let
A
be a normal projective variety, and
A_{0}\subset A
a big open subset, i.e.,
A\setminus A_{0}
has codimension at least two in
A
.
Let
\mathcal{E}
be a reflexive coherent sheaf on
A
, and
L_{1},\dots,L_{m}
a collection of nontrivial reflexive coherent sheaves on
A
.
The family
L_{1},\dots,L_{m}
is called
section-dominating
for
\mathcal{E}
on
A_{0}
if

(1)

\mathcal{E}
is locally free on
A_{0}
;

(2)

L_{1},\dots,L_{m}
are locally free and globally generated on
A_{0}
; and

(3)

the multiplication map

(2.4)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}H^{0}(L_{i}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{p})\otimes H^{0}(\mathcal{E}[\otimes]L_{i}^{\vee})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{p})}

is surjective for any
p\in A_{0}
, where
\mathcal{I}_{p}
is the ideal sheaf of
p
and

\mathcal{E}[\otimes]L_{i}^{\vee}=(\mathcal{E}\otimes L_{i}^{\vee})^{\vee\vee}.

If
A_{0}=A
, then we just call it section-dominating for
\mathcal{E}
.

Example 2.3

(cf.
[
6
, Example 2.4]
,
[
17
, Example 2.5]
)
.

On the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1)
is section-dominating for
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(a)
if
a\geq 1
.
Also, on the Grassmannian
\Gr(k,n)
, the Plücker line bundle
\mathcal{O}(1)
is section-dominating for
\mathcal{O}(a)
if
a\geq 1
because the Plücker embedding is projectively normal.

Lemma 2.4

(cf.
[
6
, Proposition 2.6]
)
.

Let
A
be a normal projective variety, and
A_{0}\subset A
a big open subset.
Let
\mathcal{E}
be a reflexive coherent sheaf on
A
.
If a family
L_{1},\dots,L_{m}
of reflexive coherent sheaves on
A
forms a section-dominating family for
\mathcal{E}
on
A_{0}
, there is a map
\oplus_{i=1}^{u}M_{L_{i}}^{\oplus s}\rightarrow M_{\mathcal{E}}
that is surjective on
A_{0}
for some integer

.

The same proof as in
[
6
]
works.
If
S
is a surface (i.e.,

), as the normal sheaf

has rank one, there is some

together with a generically surjective map
M_{L_{i}}|_{Y_{t}}\rightarrow N_{h_{t}/S_{t}}
.

Proposition 2.5

(cf.
[
6
, Proposition 2.7]
)
.

Let
A
be a normal projective variety, and
A_{0}\subset A
a big open subset.
Let
L
be a reflexive coherent sheaf on

that is locally free and globally generated on
A_{0}
.
Let

be a map from a curve into

whose image lies in
A_{0}
.
If there is a generic surjection from

to a line bundle

\deg_{C}N\geq-\deg_{C}L
.

Proof.

From the sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(A,L)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L,}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L^{\vee}}

2.4.
Bott’s theorem for Grassmannians.

We will use Bott’s theorem
2.9
to analyze whether a certain homogeneous vector bundle is section-dominating for another homogeneous vector bundle on the Grassmannian.
Let us introduce some notions in representation theory.
A
partition

\alpha
of a natural number
n
is a sequence
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that
\alpha_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\alpha_{n}\geq 0
and
\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}=n
.
We often omit 0 in the expression of a partition, e.g.,

(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k},0,\dots,0)=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}).

The
conjugate partition

\alpha^{\prime}
of a partition
\alpha
is given by

\alpha_{i}^{\prime}=|\{j:\alpha_{j}\geq i\}|

for integers
1\leq i\leq n
.

Let
E
be a vector space of dimension
n
over
\mathbb{C}
.
Denote by
\bigwedge^{r}E
the
r
-th exterior power of
E
for
0\leq r\leq n
.
For
0\leq p,q\leq n
with
p+q\leq n
, there is the natural multiplication map

\mu:\bigwedge^{p}E\otimes\bigwedge^{q}E\rightarrow\bigwedge^{p+q}E

given by

\mu(u_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge u_{p}\otimes v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{q})=u_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge u_{p}\wedge v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{q}

for
u_{1},\dots,u_{p},v_{1},\dots,v_{q}\in E
.
Also, there is the natural comultiplication map

\Delta:\bigwedge^{p+q}E\rightarrow\bigwedge^{p}E\otimes\bigwedge^{q}E

given by

\Delta(u_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge u_{p+q})=\sum_{\sigma}(-1)^{\sign\sigma}u_{\sigma(1)}\wedge\cdots\wedge u_{\sigma(p)}\otimes u_{\sigma(p+1)}\wedge\cdots\wedge u_{\sigma(p+q)}

for
u_{1},\dots,u_{p+q}\in E
, where the sum is taken over permutations
\sigma\in\Sigma_{p+q}
on
p+q
letters satisfying

\sigma(1)<\cdots<\sigma(p),\qquad\sigma(p+1)<\cdots<\sigma(p+q).

Definition 2.7

(cf.
[
25
, Section 2.1]
)
.

For a partition
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{s})
, the
Schur module

L_{\alpha}E
corresponding to the partition
\alpha
is defined as

(2.5)

L_{\alpha}E=\bigwedge^{\alpha_{1}}E\otimes\cdots\otimes\bigwedge^{\alpha_{s}}E/R(\alpha,E),

where the relation
R(\alpha,E)
is the sum of subspaces

\bigwedge^{\alpha_{1}}E\otimes\cdots\otimes\bigwedge^{\alpha_{i-1}}E\otimes R_{i,i+1}(E)\otimes\bigwedge^{\alpha_{i+2}}E\otimes\cdots\otimes\bigwedge^{\alpha_{s}}E

for
1\leq i\leq s-1
, and where

R_{i,i+1}(E)\subset\bigwedge^{\alpha_{i}}E\otimes\bigwedge^{\alpha_{i+1}}E

is the subspace generated by the images of the maps

(2.6)

\displaystyle\theta(\alpha,i,u,v;E):\bigwedge^{u}E\otimes\bigwedge^{\alpha_{i}-u+\alpha_{i+1}-v}E\otimes\bigwedge^{v}E

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{1\otimes\Delta\otimes 1}\bigwedge^{u}E\otimes\bigwedge^{\alpha_{i}-u}E\otimes\bigwedge^{\alpha_{i+1}-v}E\otimes\bigwedge^{v}E

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\mu_{12}\otimes\mu_{34}}\bigwedge^{\alpha_{i}}E\otimes\bigwedge^{\alpha_{i+1}}E

with
u+v<\alpha_{i+1}
.

Example 2.8

(cf.
[
25
, Example 2.1.3]
)
.

(a)

If
\alpha=(r)
,
L_{\alpha}E=\bigwedge^{r}E
.

(b)

If
\alpha=(1^{r})
,
L_{\alpha}E=\mathrm{Sym}^{r}E
.

(c)

If
\alpha=(p,1^{q-1})
, then the Schur module
L_{\alpha}E
is the cokernel of the map

\bigwedge^{p+1}E\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{q-2}E\xrightarrow{\Delta\otimes 1}\bigwedge^{p}E\otimes E\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{q-2}E\xrightarrow{1\otimes\mu}\bigwedge^{p}E\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{q-1}E,

where
\mu:E\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{q-2}E\rightarrow\mathrm{Sym}^{q-1}E
is the natural multiplication map.

For a weight
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})
with
\alpha_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\alpha_{n}
, define the
Weyl module
on
E
by

(2.7)

K_{\alpha}E=L_{\bar{\alpha}^{\prime}}E\otimes\left(\bigwedge^{n}E\right)^{\otimes\alpha_{n}},

where
\bar{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{n},\dots,\alpha_{n-1}-\alpha_{n},0)
.
Consider the action of the permutation group
\Sigma_{n}
on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
given by

(2.8)

(i,i+1)\cdot\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{i-1},\alpha_{i+1}-1,\alpha_{i}+1,\alpha_{i+2},\dots,\alpha_{n})

for each
1\leq i<n
, where
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
This action can be described as

\sigma\cdot\alpha=\sigma(\alpha+\rho)-\rho,

where

\sigma((\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}))=(\alpha_{\sigma(1)},\dots,\alpha_{\sigma(n)})

and
\rho=(n-1,\dots,1,0)
.
Now we can state Bott’s theorem for Grassmannians.

Theorem 2.9

(Bott-Borel-Weil theorem,
[
25
, Corollary 4.1.9]
)
.

Let
E
be a
\mathbb{C}
-vector space of dimension
n
.
Consider a weight
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
satisfying
\alpha_{i}\geq\alpha_{i+1}
for
i\neq r
.
Let
\beta=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r})
and
\gamma=(\alpha_{r+1},\dots,\alpha_{n})
be two weights, and define

(2.9)

\mathcal{V}(\alpha)=K_{\beta}\mathcal{U}^{\vee}\otimes K_{\gamma}\mathcal{Q}^{\vee}

on the Grassmannian
\Gr(r,E)
.
Then one of two mutually exclusive possibilities occurs:

(1)

If
\sigma\cdot\alpha=\alpha
for some
1\neq\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}
, the cohomology groups
H^{i}(\Gr(k,E),\mathcal{V}(\alpha))
are zero for
i\geq 0
.

(2)

If there exists a unique
\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}
such that
\sigma\cdot\alpha=\beta
is nonincreasing, all the cohomology groups
H^{i}(\Gr(k,E),\mathcal{V}(\alpha))
are zero for
i\neq\ell(\sigma)
, and

H^{\ell(\sigma)}(\Gr(k,E),\mathcal{V}(\alpha))=K_{\beta}E^{\vee}

where
\ell(\sigma)
denotes the minimal length
\ell
of a reduced expression

\sigma=\sigma_{1}\dots\sigma_{\ell}

with
\sigma_{i}=(j,j+1)
for some
1\leq j<n
.

3.
Technical lemmas

3.1.
Weighted projective space.

There are certain Fano threefolds, namely
1-1
,
1-11
and
1-12
, which can be represented as weighted hypersurfaces.
Hence we need to find a pair of integers
d,a\geq 1
such that
\mathcal{O}_{A}(d)
is section-dominating for
\mathcal{O}_{A}(a)
on a big open subset of a weighted projective space
A
.

Let
k\geq 2
and
2\leq a_{1}\leq\cdots\leq a_{\ell}
be integers.
Let
V
be a
k
-dimensional vector space, and let
R_{1},\dots,R_{\ell}
be one-dimensional vector spaces.
Define a
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action on
V\oplus R_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus R_{\ell}
by

c\cdot(x,y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell})=(cx,c^{a_{1}}y_{1},\dots,c^{a_{\ell}}y_{\ell}),

for
c\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
,
x\in V
, and
y_{i}\in R_{i}
where
1\leq i\leq\ell
.
The weighted projective space
A=\mathbb{P}(1^{k},a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell})
can be identified with the quotient

(V\oplus R_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus R_{\ell}\setminus\{0\})/\mathbb{C}^{\times}.

Consider the action of a semi-direct product

G=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\Hom(\Sym^{a_{i}}V,R_{i})\right)\rtimes\GL(V)

on
A
given by

L\cdot[x:y_{1}:\cdots:y_{\ell}]=[Lx:y_{1}:\cdots:y_{\ell}]

and

\lambda_{i}\cdot[x:y_{1}:\cdots:y_{\ell}]=[x:y_{1}:\cdots:y_{i}+\lambda_{i}(x):\cdots:y_{\ell}],

where
L\in\GL(V)
,
x\in V
,
y_{j}\in R_{j}
and
\lambda_{i}\in\Hom(\Sym^{a_{i}}V,R_{i})
.
One can see that the orbit
A_{0}=G\cdot p
of the point
p=[1:0^{k-1}:1^{\ell}]
is a big open subset of
A
and the coherent sheaves
\mathcal{O}_{A}(a)
are invariant under the action of
G
(cf.
[
15
, Proposition 2.6]
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Under the above setting, assume that
\ell=1
or
d=\lcm(a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell})\neq a_{\ell}
.
Then for any integer
a\geq\max\{d,\ell d-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}a_{i}+1\}
, the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{A}(d)
is section-dominating for
\mathcal{O}_{A}(a)
on
A_{0}
.

Proof.

The statement is direct when
\ell=0
(i.e.,
A
is the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{k-1}
), so assume that
\ell\geq 1
.
By the invariance, we only need to see that the multiplication map

\Phi:H^{0}(\mathcal{O}(d)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{p})\otimes H^{0}(\mathcal{O}(a-d))\rightarrow H^{0}(\mathcal{O}(a)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{p})

is surjective.
Note that we have
\mathcal{O}(a)[\otimes]\mathcal{O}(b)\simeq\mathcal{O}(a+b)
, while
\mathcal{O}(a)\otimes\mathcal{O}(b)\not\simeq\mathcal{O}(a+b)
in general (cf.
[
9
, Pathologies 1.5]
).

Let
s_{1},\dots,s_{k}
be linear coordinates on
V
, and
t_{i}
a linear coordinate on
R_{i}
.
In order to verify that the map
\Phi
is surjective, give a graded lexicographic order on
\mathbb{C}[s_{1},\dots,s_{k},t_{1},\dots,t_{\ell}]
such that
s_{1}<\dots<s_{k}
and

f(s_{1},\dots,s_{k},t_{1},\dots,t_{i-1})<t_{i}

for any monomial
f
of weighted degree
a_{i}
.
For a nonzero polynomial
f\in H^{0}(\mathcal{O}(a)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{p})
, we will use the induction on the leading term
\mathrm{lt}(f)
of
f
to show that
f
is contained in the image of the map
\Phi
.

First, we assume that
\mathrm{lt}(f)
is divisible by none of
s_{i}
for
2\leq i\leq k
.
If
\mathrm{lt}(f)
is not divisible by
t_{i}
’s, then
f
itself is a multiple of
s_{1}^{a}
, which does not vanish at
p
.
Hence
\mathrm{lt}(f)
is divisible by some
t_{i}
.

We claim that
\mathrm{lt}(f)
is divisible by a monomial of degree
d
.
Once we prove this assertion, one can write
\mathrm{lt}(f)=gq
for some monomials
g
and
q
of degree
a-d
and
d
, respectively, with
q
monic.
If
q\neq s_{1}^{d}
, then
q-s_{1}^{d}
is a nonzero element of
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}(d)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{p})
.
So one can replace
f
by
f-g(q-s_{1}^{d})
to reduce the leading term.
If
q=s_{1}^{d}
, then we may replace
g
and
q
by
g^{\prime}=s_{1}^{a_{i}}(g/t_{i})
and
q^{\prime}=s_{1}^{d-a_{i}}t_{i}
, respectively, for some dividend
t_{i}
of
\mathrm{lt}(f)
.
Then one can replace
f
by
f-g(q-s_{1}^{d})
as before.

To prove the claim, let
q
be a monomial of the largest degree among the monic monomials of degree at most
d
that divide
\mathrm{lt}(f)
but that are not divisible by
s_{1}
.
We are done if
\deg q=d
, so assume that
\deg q<d
.
Write
\mathrm{lt}(f)=gq
for some monomial
g
.
If
g
is divisible by
s_{1}^{d-\deg q}
, then
\mathrm{lt}(f)
is divisible by
s_{1}^{d-\deg q}q
.
This is obviously satisfied when
\ell=1
, so assume that
\ell\geq 2
.
If
s_{1}
does not divide
\mathrm{lt}(f)
, the pigeonhole principle yields
t_{i}^{d/a_{i}}\mid\mathrm{lt}(f)
for some
i
.
This contradicts
\deg q<d
, so
s_{1}
divides
\mathrm{lt}(f)
.

Now assume that
s_{1}^{d-\deg q}
does not divide
\mathrm{lt}(f)
.
Let
\alpha
be the multiplicity of
s_{1}
in
\mathrm{lt}(f)
.
Note that
g
is divisible by some
t_{i}
.
For such an
i
, we have
\deg t_{i}q>d
by maximality, i.e.,
d-\deg q<a_{i}
.
If
t_{i}^{d/a_{i}-1}\nmid\mathrm{lt}(f)
, then
\alpha
is at least

\left(\ell\cdot d-\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}+1\right)-\sum_{j\neq i}\left(\frac{d}{a_{j}}-1\right)a_{j}-\left(\frac{d}{a_{i}}-2\right)a_{i}=a_{i}+1,

i.e.,
d-\deg q>\alpha\geq a_{i}+1
, a contradiction.
A similar argument says that
t_{j}^{d/a_{j}-1}\mid\mathrm{lt}(f)
if
a_{j}\geq a_{i}
.
In particular,
t_{\ell}^{d/a_{\ell}-1}
divides
\mathrm{lt}(f)
and
\alpha<a_{\ell}
.

Let
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
be the multiplicities of
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
in
\mathrm{lt}(f)
, respectively.
As above, we infer that

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\geq\left(\ell\cdot d-\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}+1\right)-\alpha_{1}a_{1}-\alpha_{2}a_{2}-\sum_{j\geq 3}\left(\frac{d}{a_{j}}-1\right)a_{j}

\displaystyle=2d-\left(\alpha_{1}+1\right)a_{1}-\left(\alpha_{2}+1\right)a_{2}+1.

We claim that
t_{j}^{d/a_{j}-1}
divides
\mathrm{lt}(f)
for
j\geq 2
.
This is satisfied when either
t_{1}
or
t_{2}
divides
g
, so suppose that both do not divide
g
.
Since
t_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}t_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\mid q
, we have

(2d-\left(\alpha_{1}+1\right)a_{1}-\left(\alpha_{2}+1\right)a_{2}+1)+\alpha_{1}a_{1}+\alpha_{2}a_{2}=2d-a_{1}-a_{2}+1<d,

which is impossible because
a_{1},a_{2}\leq\frac{d}{2}
.

Now observe that
\alpha_{1}
is at least
\frac{d}{6a_{1}}
.
Otherwise, from the inequality

\alpha\geq d-(\alpha_{1}+1)a_{1}+1>\frac{5d}{6}-a_{1},

we get
a_{1}>\frac{d}{3}
as
\alpha<a_{\ell}\leq\frac{d}{2}
.
This is not the case.
Consider the monomial
q^{\prime}=t_{1}^{\beta}t_{\ell}^{d/a_{\ell}-1}
of degree at most
d
with
1\leq\beta\leq\alpha_{1}
maximal.
Then
q^{\prime}
divides
\mathrm{lt}(f)
, so
\deg q^{\prime}\leq\deg q
and
\alpha\leq d-\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{6}=\frac{d}{3}
.
Applying the above procedure repeatedly, we get
\alpha_{1}\geq\frac{d}{2a_{1}}
.
Since the monomial
t_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}t_{\ell}^{d/a_{\ell}-1}
has degree at least
d
,
\alpha
is strictly less than
a_{1}
.
We infer that

d-(\alpha_{1}+1)a_{1}+1\leq\alpha<a_{1},

or

\alpha_{1}>\frac{d}{a_{1}}+\frac{1}{a_{1}}-2.

Hence one can conclude that
t_{i}^{d/a_{i}-1}\mid\mathrm{lt}(f)
for all
1\leq i\leq\ell
.

We are now able to derive a contradiction.
If
k=\gcd(a_{i},a_{j})>1
for some
1\leq i<j\leq\ell
, then the monomial

q^{\prime}=t_{i}^{\frac{1}{k}\cdot\frac{d}{a_{i}}}t_{j}^{\frac{k-1}{k}\cdot\frac{d}{a_{j}}}

divides
\mathrm{lt}(f)
and
\deg(q^{\prime})=d
, which is a contradiction.
Suppose that
a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell}
are pairwise coprime.
Let us introduce the following classical result:

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
be coprime integers. Let

a\equiv-x^{-1}\,(\text{mod }y),\qquad b\equiv-y^{-1}\,(\text{mod }x)

for some
0<a<y
and
0<b<x
.
Then
ax+by=xy-1
.

Proof.

We give a proof for the sake of completeness.
As
x
and
y
are coprime, there exist integers
c,d\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
cx+dy=xy-1
.
We may assume that
0<c<y
.
Then
0<dy=(y-c)x-1<xy
, so
0<d<x
.
Clearly
a=c
and
b=d
.
∎

Choose
0<\alpha<a_{\ell}
and
0<\beta<\frac{d}{a_{\ell}}=a_{1}\cdots a_{\ell-1}
such that

\alpha\cdot\frac{d}{a_{\ell}}+\beta\cdot a_{\ell}=d-1

and consider the monomial

q^{\prime}=s_{1}t_{1}^{\alpha\cdot\frac{d}{a_{1}a_{\ell}}}t_{\ell}^{\beta}.

It divides
\mathrm{lt}(f)
and
\deg(q^{\prime})=d
, whence a contradiction.

Finally, if
\mathrm{lt}(f)
is divisible by
s_{i}
for some
2\leq i\leq k
, a similar argument shows that
\mathrm{lt}(f)
is divisible by a (monic) monomial
q
of degree
d
divisible by
s_{i}
.
Then we can replace
f
by
f-\mathrm{lt}(f)
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

When
\ell=2
and
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
are coprime, the above result is optimal.
Using the notation above,
t_{1}^{a_{2}-1}t_{2}^{a_{1}-1}-s_{1}^{2a_{1}a_{2}-a_{1}-a_{2}}
is a section of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(2a_{1}a_{2}-a_{1}-a_{2})
vanishing at
p
, which is not contained in the image of
\Phi
.

3.2.
Cohomology of homogeneous vector bundles.

In this section, we prove the vanishing of cohomology groups of certain homogeneous vector bundles on the Grassmannian.
The following theorem will be useful when dealing with Fano threefolds
1-8
,
1-9
and
1-10
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
n
and
k
be integers with
n\geq 2
,
1\leq k\leq\frac{n}{2}
.
For a given sequence of integers

n-k\geq i_{1}\geq\cdots\geq i_{k-1}\geq i_{k}=0

and integers
i\geq 0
and
d>0
, if
i_{\ell-1}-i_{\ell}<n-k
for any
\ell
, we have

H^{i}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n),K_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{k})}\mathcal{U}^{\vee}(-d))\neq 0

if and only if
i=k(n-k)
and
d\geq n+i_{1}
.
If

i_{1}=\dots=i_{\ell}=n-k,\quad i_{\ell+1}=\dots=i_{n-k}=0

for some
0\leq\ell<k
,

H^{i}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n),K_{((n-k)^{\ell})}\mathcal{U}^{\vee}(-d))\neq 0

if and only if

\begin{cases}i=(n-k)(k-\ell),\,d=n-\ell,\\
i=k(n-k),\,d\geq n,\end{cases}

and in the former case,

H^{(n-k)(k-\ell)}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n),K_{((n-k)^{\ell})}\mathcal{U}^{\vee}(-(n-\ell)))\simeq\mathbb{C}.

Similarly, for a given sequence of integers

k\geq j_{1}\geq\cdots\geq j_{n-k-1}\geq j_{n-k}=0

and integers
i,d\geq 0
, if
j_{\ell-1}-j_{\ell}<k
for any
\ell
, then

H^{i}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n),K_{(j_{1},\dots,j_{n-k})}\mathcal{Q}^{\vee}(-d))\neq 0

if and only if
i=k(n-k)
and
d\geq n
.
If

j_{1}=\dots=j_{\ell}=k,\quad j_{\ell+1}=\dots=j_{n-k}=0

for some
0\leq\ell<n-k
,

H^{i}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n),K_{(k^{\ell})}\mathcal{Q}^{\vee}(-d))\neq 0

if and only if

\begin{cases}i=k\ell,\,d=\ell,\\
i=k(n-k),\,d\geq n,\end{cases}

and in the former case,

H^{k\ell}(\mathrm{Gr}(k,n),K_{(k^{\ell})}\mathcal{Q}^{\vee}(-\ell))\simeq\mathbb{C}.

Proof.

Let
E=\mathbb{C}^{n}
be an
n
-dimensional vector space and regard
\Gr(k,n)
as
\Gr(k,E)
.
Note that

\mathcal{V}((i_{1},\dots,i_{k},d^{n-k}))=K_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{k})}\mathcal{U}^{\vee}\otimes\left(\det\mathcal{Q}^{\vee}\right)^{\otimes d}=K_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{k})}\mathcal{U}^{\vee}(-d).

Let
\alpha=(i_{1},\dots,i_{k},d^{n-k})
for simplicity.
Then
\alpha
is not a partition as
d\geq 1
.
If
d=1
, then
\alpha_{k+1}=\alpha_{k}+1
, so
(k,k+1)\cdot\alpha=\alpha
.
Hence by Bott’s theorem
2.9
, all the cohomology groups vanish.

For
d\geq 2
, in order to find
\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}
with
\sigma\cdot\alpha
nonincreasing, we first get rid of zero at the
k
-th entry:

(k,k+1)\cdot\alpha=(i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1},d-1,1,d^{n-k-1}).

If
d=2
, then

(k,k+2)\cdot\alpha=(k,k+1)(k+1,k+2)(k,k+1)\cdot\alpha=\alpha,

so all the cohomology groups vanish.
By the same argument, all the cohomology groups vanish when
d<n-k+1
, and one can proceed from

\alpha^{\prime}=(k,k+1,\dots,n)\cdot\alpha=(i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1},(d-1)^{n-k},n-k)

with
d\geq n-k+1
.
If
\alpha^{\prime}
itself is a partition, i.e.,
i_{1}=\cdots=i_{k-1}=n-k
and
d=n-k+1
, then
H^{i}(K_{((n-k)^{k-1})}\mathcal{U}^{\vee}(-(n-k+1)))\neq 0
if and only if
i=n-k
, and in this case

H^{n-k}(K_{((n-k)^{k-1})}\mathcal{U}^{\vee}(-(n-k+1)))=(\det E)^{\otimes n-k}\simeq\mathbb{C}.

Similarly, one can show that the cohomology groups do not vanish except for the top ones if and only if
i_{1}=\dots=i_{\ell}=n-k
and
i_{\ell+1}=\dots=i_{k}=0
for some
0\leq\ell<k
, and in this case

H^{(n-k)(k-\ell)}(K_{((n-k)^{\ell})}\mathcal{U}^{\vee}(-(n-\ell)))=(\det E)^{\otimes n-k}\simeq\mathbb{C}.

On the other hand, if
i_{\ell-1}-i_{\ell}<n-k
for any
\ell
, one obtains a partition

\sigma\cdot\alpha=((d-k)^{n-k},i_{1}+n-k,\dots,i_{k}+n-k)

if and only if
d\geq n+i_{1}
, where

\sigma=(1,2,\dots,n-k+1)(2,3,\dots,n-k+2)\dots(k,k+1,\dots,n).

One may observe that
\ell(\sigma^{\prime})=k(n-k)
.

For the second statement, notice that

\mathcal{V}((0^{k},j_{1}+d,\dots,j_{n-k}+d))=K_{(j_{1}+d,\dots,j_{n-k}+d)}\mathcal{Q}^{\vee}=K_{(j_{1},\dots,j_{n-k})}\mathcal{Q}^{\vee}(-d).

Let
\beta=(0^{k},j_{1}+d,\dots,j_{n-k}+d)
.
Again, we aim to find
\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}
with
\sigma\cdot\beta
nonincreasing.
When
\beta
itself is a partition, i.e.,
d=0
and
j_{1}=\dots=j_{n-k}=0
, then
H^{i}(K_{\mathrm{(0)}}\mathcal{Q}^{\vee})=H^{i}(\mathcal{O})\neq 0
if and only if
i=0
, and in this case
H^{0}(\mathcal{O})=\mathbb{C}
.
If
\beta
is not a partition, then we first get rid of zeros at the first
k
-th entries.
As above, all the cohomology groups vanish when
d<k-j_{1}+1
, and one can proceed from

\beta^{\prime}=(1,2,\dots,k+1)\cdot\beta=(j_{1}+d-k,1^{k},j_{2}+d,\dots,j_{n-1}+d)

with
d\geq k-j_{1}+1
.
If
\beta^{\prime}
itself is a partition, i.e.,
d=1
,
j_{1}=k
and
j_{2}=\dots=j_{n-k}=0
, then
H^{i}(K_{(k)}\mathcal{Q}^{\vee}(-1))=H^{i}(\mathrm{Sym}^{k}\mathcal{Q}^{\vee}(-1))\neq 0
if and only if
i=k
, and in this case

H^{k}(\mathrm{Sym}^{k}\mathcal{Q}^{\vee}(-1))=\det E\simeq\mathbb{C}.

In the same fashion, one can prove that the cohomology groups do not vanish except for the top ones if and only if
j_{1}=\dots=j_{\ell}=k
and
j_{\ell+1}=\dots=j_{n-k}=0
for some
0\leq\ell<n-k
, and in this case

H^{k\ell}(K_{(k^{\ell})}\mathcal{Q}^{\vee}(-\ell))=(\det E)^{\otimes\ell}\simeq\mathbb{C}

is the only nonzero cohomology group.
On the other hand, if
j_{\ell-1}-j_{\ell}<k
for any
\ell
, one obtains a partition

\sigma\cdot\beta=(j_{1}+d-k,\dots,j_{n-k}+d-k,(n-k)^{k})

if and only if
d\geq n
, where

\sigma=(n-k,n-k+1,\dots,n)(n-k-1,n-k,\dots,n-1)\dots(1,2,\dots,k+1).

One can observe that
\ell(\sigma)=k(n-k)
, whence the assertion.
∎

4.
Proof of main theorem
1.2

In this section, we prove our main theorem
1.2
.
Here, the assumption that Fano threefolds are general is necessary.
Indeed, we use Mukai’s classification of general Fano threefolds as the zero loci of regular sections of homogeneous vector bundles on the product of (weighted) Grassmannians (cf.
[
19
]
, see also
[
4
]
and
[
7

Hypersurface of degree 6 in
\mathbb{P}(1^{3},2,3)
.

Description:

\mathfrak{L}(\mathcal{O}(6))\subset\mathbb{P}(1^{3},2,3)
.

We have the action of

G=(\Hom(\Sym^{2}\mathbb{C}^{3},\mathbb{C})\times\Hom(\Sym^{3}\mathbb{C}^{3},\mathbb{C}))\rtimes\GL_{3}

on
A=\mathbb{P}(1^{3},2,3)
as in Section
3.1
.
Write
A=\Proj\mathbb{C}[s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},t_{1},t_{2}]
with
\deg s_{1}=\deg s_{2}=\deg s_{3}=1
,
\deg t_{1}=2
and
\deg t_{3}=3
.
This
G
-action has a dense orbit
A_{0}=A\setminus\mathcal{Z}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})
.
By Theorem
3.1
,
\mathcal{O}_{A}(6)
is section-dominating for
\mathcal{O}_{A}(a)
on
A_{0}
provided
a\geq 8
.
As in
1-1
, one can see that
S
is algebraically hyperbolic when
a\geq 9
.

For
4\leq a\leq 8
, consider the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{\mathcal{O}_{A}(a^{\prime})}(a^{\prime\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{A}(a^{\prime}))\otimes M_{\mathcal{O}_{A}(a^{\prime\prime})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{A}(a^{\prime}))\otimes H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{A}(a^{\prime\prime}))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{A}(a^{\prime}))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{A}(a^{\prime\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{\mathcal{O}_{A}(a)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{A}(a))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{A}(a)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K\otimes\mathcal{O}_{A}}

where
a^{\prime}=\lfloor\frac{a}{2}\rfloor
,
a^{\prime\prime}=a-a^{\prime}
and
K
is the cokernel of the multiplication map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{A}(a^{\prime}))\otimes H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{A}(a^{\prime\prime}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{A}(a)).}

Note that
\mathcal{O}_{S}(1)
is globally generated on
S
as
S
avoids
A\setminus A_{0}
.
Also for
k\leq\min\{5,a-1\}
, the restriction map gives an isomorphism
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{A}(k))\simeq H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{S}(k))
, so
M_{\mathcal{O}_{S}(k)}=M_{\mathcal{O}_{A}(k)}|_{S}
.
Thus
\mathcal{O}_{S}(a^{\prime})
,
\mathcal{O}_{S}(a^{\prime\prime})
and
M_{\mathcal{O}_{S}(a^{\prime})}(a^{\prime\prime})=M_{\mathcal{O}_{S}(a^{\prime})}(a^{\prime})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{S}(a^{\prime\prime}-a^{\prime})
are all globally generated on
S
, we have an exact sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{\mathcal{O}_{S}(a^{\prime})}(a^{\prime\prime})|_{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{K}|_{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

where
\mathcal{K}
is the cokernel of the map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{S}(a^{\prime}))\otimes M_{\mathcal{O}_{A}(a^{\prime\prime})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{\mathcal{O}_{A}(a)}.}

Assume that there is a generic surjection
M_{\mathcal{O}_{A}(a)}|_{C}\rightarrow N_{f/S}
.
If the induced map
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{A}(a^{\prime}))\otimes M_{\mathcal{O}_{A}(a^{\prime\prime})}|_{C}\rightarrow N_{f/S}
has a torsion image
\mathcal{T}
, then there is a generic surjection
\mathcal{K}|_{C}\rightarrow N^{\prime}=N_{f/S}/\mathcal{T}
.
Again, if the induced map
M_{\mathcal{O}_{A}(a^{\prime})}(a^{\prime\prime})|_{C}\rightarrow N^{\prime}
has a torsion image
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
, then there is a generic surjection
K\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}\rightarrow N^{\prime}/\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
, yielding
\deg_{C}N_{f/S}\geq 0
.
Then we have

2g-2\geq(C.K_{S})=(a-2)\deg_{C}\mathcal{O}_{A}(1)

which is the desired estimate.
On the other hand, if
M_{\mathcal{O}_{A}(a^{\prime})}(a^{\prime\prime})|_{C}\rightarrow N^{\prime}
is generically surjective, since
M_{\mathcal{O}_{A}(a^{\prime})}(a^{\prime\prime})|_{S}=M_{\mathcal{O}_{S}(a^{\prime})}(a^{\prime\prime})
is globally generated on
S
, we would get the above estimate as well.
Hence we may assume that there is a generic surjection
M_{\mathcal{O}_{A}(a^{\prime\prime})}|_{C}\rightarrow N_{f/S}
.
Repeating this procedure, we would arrive at a generic surjection
M_{\mathcal{O}_{A}(1)}|_{C}\rightarrow N_{f/S}
.
Since
M_{\mathcal{O}_{A}(1)}(1)|_{S}=M_{\mathcal{O}_{S}(1)}(1)
is globally generated on
S
, we get
\deg_{C}N(1)\geq 0
, so

2g-2\geq(C.K_{S})-\deg_{C}\mathcal{O}_{A}(1)=(a-3)\deg_{C}\mathcal{O}_{A}(1)

which is again the desired estimate for
a\geq 4
.

In conclusion,
S
is algebraically hyperbolic if
a\geq 4
. The remaining case is
a=3

a=3

S

6

\mathbb{P}(1^{3},2)
, which is also an unknown case in
[
6
, Lemma 3.9, Proposition 3.10]
.

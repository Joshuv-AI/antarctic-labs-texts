Degenerations of families of bands and strings for gentle algebras
Judith Marquardt

Abstract
. Let A A be a gentle algebra. For every collection of string and band diagrammes, we consider the constructible subset of the variety of representations containing all modules with this underlying diagramme. We study degenerations of such sets. We show that these sets are defined by vectors of integers which we call h h -vectors and which are related to a restricted version of the hom \hom -order. We provide combinatorial criteria for the existence of a degeneration, involving the removal of an arrow or the resolving of a type of configuration called ”reaching”.

Introduction

Degenerations of orbits in a module variety are well studied, see for example

The isomorphism classes of indecomposable modules of a gentle algebra are governed by the combinatorics of strings and bands of the quiver. To every string we associate an orbit of a module, to every band a family of orbits. In this article, for every diagramme
\mathcal{D}
consisting of strings and bands, we consider the set
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{D}}
of all modules whose underlying diagramme is
\mathcal{D}
, see
. We call this the family of
\mathcal{D}
. This ”combinatorial” definition has a geometrical sense. As shown by
[
GLS_gentle_2021
]
, the indecomposable blocks of the module variety of an algebra are the closure of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{D}}
where
\mathcal{D}
is either a single string or a single band. Motivated by these results, we initiate a systematic study of closures of the sets
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{D}}
.

We introduce a degeneration order
\mathcal{D}\leq_{\text{deg}}\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
on the diagrammes, defined by
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{D}}}
, see
. The main objective of this article is to study this order.

In the classical study of degenerations of orbits of modules, several orders are used. Recall that the degeneration order is defined by
M\leq_{\textnormal{deg}}M^{\prime}
if
\mathcal{O}_{M^{\prime}}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{O}_{M}}
. It implies a weaker order given by assigning an infinite vector of integers to each module
M
and comparing them pointwise. This is called the
\hom
-order, defined by
M\leq_{\hom}M^{\prime}
if
\hom(Z,M)\leq\hom(Z,M^{\prime})
for all modules
Z
. It is known that
M\leq_{\textnormal{deg}}M^{\prime}
implies
M\leq_{\hom}M^{\prime}
while the inverse is not always true, see for example
[
CB
]
.

We adapt the
\hom
-order to the study of the families
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{D}}
. We do this by restricting the
\hom
-order to comparisons involving only string modules. Precisely, we define the
h
-order by
{M\leq_{h}M^{\prime}}
if
{\hom(Z,M)\leq\hom(Z,M^{\prime})}
for all string modules
Z
. We call
{h(M):=(\hom(Z,M))_{Z\text{ string module}}}
the
h
-vector of
M
.

Our main results are as follows.

•

The sets
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{D}}
are precisely the subsets of the module variety on which the
h
-vector is constant, see
.

•

The degeneration order
\mathcal{D}\leq_{\text{deg}}\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
implies the
h
-order
\mathcal{D}\leq_{h}\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
, see
. We do not know whether the converse holds.

Next, we provide combinatorial criteria which imply degenerations.

•

The deletion of an arrow in a string or band, see
.

•

The resolution of a ”reaching”, a configuration of strings and band described in
[
PPP_kissing
]

.

On all examples that we have computed, cover relations of the degeneration order always fall in the above two cases. We do not know whether this is true in general.

2.1.
Path algebras and representations

A
quiver
is a directed graph given by the data
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1},s,t)
where
Q_{0}
denotes the set of vertices and
Q_{1}
the set of arrows. The maps
s,t\colon Q_{1}\rightarrow Q_{0}
assign the source resp. target of each arrow. We assume quivers to be connected and finite and set
n
to be the number of vertices.
We compose arrows as we would compose morphisms. A
path

p=a_{1}\ldots a_{m}
is a composition of compatible arrows with
t(a_{i+1})=s(a_{i})
for
1\leq i<m
. The
length
of
p
is
m
and denoted by
\ell(p)
. We extend the maps
s
and
t
to paths by setting
s(p)=s(a_{m})
and
t(p)=t(a_{1})
. Moreover, for every vertex
i\in Q_{0}
we define a 0-length path
e_{i}
with
s(e_{i})=t(e_{i})=i
.

Recall that a quiver
Q
gives rise to the
path algebra

KQ
which is the
K
-algebra with basis given by all paths of
Q
and an algebra structure induced by the composition of paths. Let
\mathfrak{m}
be the ideal generated by the arrows of
Q
. We say that an ideal
I
of
KQ
is
admissible
if there exists an integer
m\geq 2
such that
\mathfrak{m}^{m}\subseteq I\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{2}
. A
basic
algebra is an algebra isomorphic to
A=KQ/I
where
I
is admissible. Since we work over an algebraically closed field, every finite dimensional algebra is Morita equivalent to a basic algebra. A
representation
of
A
is a tuple
(M_{i},M_{a})_{i\in Q_{0},\,a\in Q_{1}}
where
M_{i}
is a finite dimensional
K
-vector space and
M_{a}\colon M_{s(a)}\rightarrow M_{t(a)}
is a
K
-linear morphism such that the relations of
I
are satisfied. The
dimension vector
of
M
is
\textup{{dim}}\,(M)=(\dim M_{1},\ldots,\dim M_{n})
. We identify representations of
A
with finite dimensional left modules of
A
in the usual way.

•

2.3.
Strings and bands

The modules of gentle algebras have been intensely studied in
[
ButlerRingel87
,
Wald_Waschbüch_indecomposable_gentle
]
. They are closely related to the notion of strings and bands.

We define
Q_{1}^{-}:=\left\{a^{-}\,|\,a\in Q_{1}\right\}
where
a^{-}
is the formal inverse arrow of
a
with
s(a^{-})=t(a)
and
t(a^{-})=s(a)
. Moreover, we set
(a^{-})^{-}:=a
. This allows us to define the
double quiver

Q^{\pm}=(Q_{0},Q_{1}\cup Q_{1}^{-},s,t)
. For a path
p=c_{1}\dots c_{m}
in
Q^{\pm}
, we define its inverse
p^{-}:=c_{m}^{-}\dots c_{1}^{-}
. Let
S_{I}
be the generating set of
I
consisting of length two paths. We define
I^{\pm}:=\langle S_{I}\cup\{p^{-}\,|\,p\in S_{I}\}\rangle
.

Definition 2.3
.

A
string

C
is a path
c_{1}\dots c_{m}
in the double quiver such that

(i)

no arrow is followed directly by its inverse, i.e.
aa^{-}
and
a^{-}a
are not allowed to be a part of the path
\forall a\in Q_{1}
,

(ii)

C\notin I^{\pm}
.

We define the
length
of
C
as the length of the path and denote it by

. We handle
s
and

,
C^{-}
, is also a string.

We consider strings up to the inverse action. So a string is an element of
\mathscr{C}=\{\text{strings}\}/_{C\sim C^{-}}
and we choose a representative
C
to depict a string

.

Abstract No: ABS003: Comparison of the effects of dapagliflozin versus metformin in overweight and obese non-diabetic PCOS women – An interventional study

Soumya S. Inamadar, Vrinda Agrawal, Shanthi Naidu, Bipin K. Sethi

Care Hospital, Hyderabad, Telangana, India

E-mail: soumyasinamadar@gmail.com

Background: Polycystic ovarian syndrome (PCOS) is the most common form of endocrinopathy in women, associated with chronic anovulation, androgen excess and long term metabolic and cardiovascular consequences. Metformin has been widely used in PCOS women to target the insulin resistance and the metabolic derangements associated. The use of SGLT-2 inhibitors remains less explored. In this study, we aim to extrapolate the CV benefits of this class by use of Dapagliflozin in non-diabetic overweight and obese PCOS women in comparison with Metformin. Our primary objective was to compare the effects on metabolic and anthropometric parameters and the secondary objective was to compare the effects on hormonal parameters.

Methods: The study was conducted in Endocrinology OPD at CARE hospital, Hyderabad. A total of 25 patients were enrolled over the study period, 14 consented for the addition of Metformin in combination with COCs (combined oral contraceptives) and 11 for Dapagliflozin plus COCs. The phenotypic and anthropometric characteristics were noted, tests for metabolic and hormonal profile were done at baseline. Patients were followed after 3 months of treatment for repeat assessment and testing.

Results: At follow-up we had 10 patients in Metformin group and 8 patients in Dapagliflozin, who had completed treatment. There was significant decrease in weight and BMI in the Dapagliflozin group compared to Metformin. There was comparable and significant decrease in waist and hip circumference in each group. In the metabolic parameters, the reduction seen in FBS, PBS, fasting Insulin and HOMA-IR was comparable between the groups. There was no significant change in hormonal parameters in both the groups.

Conclusion: In this study, Dapagliflozin was found non-inferior to Metformin in targeting metabolic component of PCOS. With already existing evidence of SGLT-2 inhibitors on the cardio-vascular outcomes, there can be expansion of this class of drugs in PCOS, beyond the diabetes management.

Background: Foot problems are a common concern for individuals with diabetes mellitus whose untimely management increases risk for amputation & disability. Uraemic waste accumulation caused by renal function decline may aggravate neuropathic symptoms, thereby leading to foot complications among another group of individuals diagnosed with type 2 diabetic nephropathy.

Objective: To report the common foot complications experienced by individuals with type 2 diabetic nephropathy.

Methods: Out of 283 screened, the study included 206 participants with type 2 diabetic nephropathy, aged 45 to 70 years, with an estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) above 15mL/min/1.73 m2 who visited the study centre at a tertiary hospital in South India. A qualified physiotherapist trained in podiatry observed the feet of individuals for foot complications like dry skin, altered skin colour, toe deformities, ingrown toenails, bunions, callus, infection, fissures, prominent metatarsal heads & ulcers.

The present study observed that foot complications are present among individuals with type 2 diabetic nephropathy. Thus, diabetic foot care & footwear education, are equally important for prevention of foot complications among them.

QRISK tool (UK based) can be preferred over ASCVD/AHA tool (US based) in Indian population.

Background: Prevalence of NAFLD in Type 1 DM is 27-30%, with a higher risk ofMetabolic syndrome (MS) and complications. This study, a first in the Indian settingaims to determine the proportion of NAFLD in Type 1 DMusing Transient elastography (TE) and to study role of noninvasive markers.

Methods: 39 patients underwent TE. Controlled Attenuation Parameter (CAP) ≥ 248 dB/m or Liver Stiffness Measurement (LSM) ≥7 kpa was used to define NAFLD. AST/ALT ratio, APRI and FIB-4 scores were calculated.

Results: Out of 39 patients, 51.2% (20) were males. Mean age was 28.8+/-6.9 with duration of diabetes of 12+/-7 years. Proportion of NAFLD was 30.8% (12), of which45% (9) were male (p=0.08) Steatosiswas seen in 17.9% (7) and fibrosisin 15.4% (6). MS in the cohort was 30.8% (12). Out of 7 patients with steatosis, 71.4 (5) had MS (p=0.02). SBP in those with NAFLD was 130mmHg (IQR125-137) vs 117 mmHg (IQR120-127) without (p=0.03). Waistcircumference (90.7cm vs 80.6cm, p=0.02) BMI (25.1kg/m2 vs 20.9kg/m2, p=0.02) and SBP (130mmHg vs 120mmHg, p=0.002) were higher in patients with steatosis. A FIB-4 cut off of0.515 was identifiedto predict fibrosis in Type 1DM with sensitivity 83%, specificity of 83.9. Significant correlation was noted between CAP and Neck circumference (p=0.045, correlation coefficient 0.352) and between CAP and SBP (p=0.002, correlation coefficient 0.372). LSM corelated with FIB-4 scores (p=0.056, correlation coefficient 0.312). Total daily insulin and duration of DM did not corelate with NAFLD.

Conclusion: Proportion of NAFLD in Type 1 DM was 30.8% and FIB-4 score corelated better and detected fibrosis more accurately, with a lower cut off.

Background and Aim: Heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia, a treatable genetic cause for premature coronary artery disease is oftenunderdiagnosed. Even with the emergence of high thorough output next generation sequencing, nearly 60% are mutation negative. Recently, the polygenic etiology has brought significant progress in the understanding of hypercholesterolemia. This study aimed to investigate the spectrum of genetic variants and polygenic LDL score among clinically diagnosed FH subjects.

Methods: In this study, a comprehensive evaluation was performed on 116 unrelated individuals who had LDL-C >= 190 mg/dL with a DLCN (Dutch Lipid clinic Network) score > 3. All underwent thorough medical history and physical examination. Lipid levels were measured and targeted NGS was performed on 23 lipid related genes. Additionally, 104 were genotyped for a 12-SNP panel to determine the polygenic LDL-PRS score. (12 specific SNPs, namely rs4299376, rs1367117, rs6511720, rs629301, rs2479409, rs1564348, rs1800562, rs3757354, rs11220462, rs8017377, rs7412 (apoE), and rs429358 (apoE)).

Among the subjects, 4 (3.4%) patients were categorised as definite FH, 36 (31%) probable FH and 76 (65.5%) possible FH. NGS identified 40 variants of which 17 werepathogenic. A 100% mutation positivity rate was observed among individuals with definite FH. The most common variants were in the LDLR (57.5%) gene with 3 novel variants. Among mutation negative subjects, 63.9% had a high LDL- PRS score. As expected, the mutation positive cohort had a higher median LDL-C and lower LDL- PRS compared to mutation negative cohort.

Conclusion: These findings underscore the significance of both monogenic and polygenic factors in understanding the causes of hypercholesterolemia and emphasize the need for genetic screening in selected patients.

Background: Diabetes mellitus is a syndrome characterized by hyperglycemia due to absolute or relative insulin deficiency which triggers a cascade of metabolic derangements leading tomultiple end-organ damages by affecting small and large blood vessels. Hypertension often coexisting with diabetes further increases the risk of vascular complications and multiple-end organ damage. Hence early detection of these complicationsin patients with diabetes and hypertension, may slow their progress and give a better lifestyle to such patients.

Methods: A cross-sectional type of observational study was carried out in a tertiary care center to investigate newly detected patients of diabetes and hypertensionfor the prevalence of their complications. Newly detected patients meeting specific inclusion and exclusion criteria were selected from both the outpatient and in-patient divisions of the Medicine department. Results: A total of 100 patients were included in this study with a mean age of 48.99 years and a male-to-female ratio of 11.08. Conclusion: This study highlights that diabetes and hypertension together can heighten the incidence of end-organ damage. The increase in risks exceeds that observed in cases of diabetes and hypertension individually, as indicated by literature. Many of the associated complications develop over long-term and are initially asymptomatic, thus early screening of these patients at the onset of the disease is vital. Timely and definitive treatment can improve health outcomes in such patients.

Abstract No: ABS029: High carrier frequency of CYP21A2 gene mutations in southern India invoking the need for genetic testing in congenital adrenal hyperplasia

Christian Medical College, Vellore, Tamil Nadu, India

Background: Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia (CAH) is a prevalent autosomal recessive endocrine disorder, characterized by reduced activity of adrenal cortex enzymes. The majority of CAH cases are caused by mutations in the CYP21A2 gene, leading to 21-hydroxylase deficiency. However, due to the pseudogene-associated challenges in CYP21A2 gene analysis, comprehensive CAH genetic diagnostics and carrier screening are not a part of the first-tier investigations in Indian clinical settings.

Methods: Recently, a cost-effective Allele-specific PCR based genotyping for CYP21A2 hotspot mutations has been demonstrated to be a highly specific and sensitive assay at the authors’ center. Leveraging this approach, a total of 1034 healthy individuals from South India underwent screening to identify the carrier frequency of common pseudogene-derived mutations in the CYP21A2 gene.

Results: In this study, it was observed that 9.71% of the subjects were carriers for one or more of the nine different CYP21A2 mutations. Among the carriers, the most prevalent was the large 30kb deletion, followed by II72N, E6 CLUS, and I2G mutations.

Conclusion: We have identified a high prevalence of CYP21A2 mutation carriers in South India. These findings emphasize the importance of implementing and expanding cost-effective genetic diagnostics and carrier screening throughout India. Such initiatives would play a crucial role in managing the disease burden, enabling early intervention, and establishing guidelines for CAH newborn genetic screening in the country. This study represents the first carrier screening data on CYP21A2 hotspot mutations from India and is the largest study conducted to date in this context.

Abstract No: ABS030: To study the prevalence of levothyroxine malabsorption in patients with refractory hypothyroidism; experience from a tertiary care centre in central India

M. Sriram

People’s College of Medical Sciences and Research Centre, Bhopal, Madhya Pradesh, India

E-mail: shriram.mudraje@gmail.com

Background and Aim: Levothyroxine (LT4) is the mainstay of therapy for hypothyroidism. Despite doctor efforts at dose titration, some patients remain hypothyroid on LT4 doses in excess of weight-based calculations, a condition known as refractory hypothyroidism. The LT4 absorption test (LT4AT) has been proposed to have utility in these patients by enabling distinction of LT4 malabsorption from pseudomalabsorption, a condition of intentional non-adherence. Our aim was to identify the prevalence of levothyroxine malabsorption in patients with hypothyroidism with deranged thyroid profile.

Materials and Methods: We conducted a cross sectional study to identify the prevalence of levothyroxine malabsorption in patients with refractory hypothyroidism. Inclusion criteria were: (1) All adult subjects diagnosed with uncontrolled hypothyroidism and attending Endocrine OPD and having persistent deranged thyroid profile in last 6 months inspite of regular appropriate dosed levothyroxine supplementation and (2) All adult subjects with hypothyroidism requiring more than 2.0 microgram/kg/day levothyroxine supplementation to get the thyroid profile normalized. Exclusion criteria were (1) Very sick patients, (2) Patients on medications with possible interactions with thyroid function tests, (3) pregnancy, (4) Patients having history of cardiac, renal or liver disease and (5) Not willing to participate in study. Total of 25 patients were included in our study for 95% confidence interval. Demographic and clinical details as per previous visit in last 6 months and Thyroid Profile reports in last 6 months were noted. Levothyroxine absorption test was performed. Full week dose of levothyroxine was given in morning after taking baseline free t4 sample. A second sample for free t4 was collected 4 hours after taking levothyroxine and increment in Free T4 levels were noted, compared and interpreted. A normal response was defined by increment in Free T4 levels from baseline by 50% or by an absolute value of 0.4 ng/dL.

Results: Out of 25 subjects, 23 subjects had normal increment in free T4 levels. Absorption was impaired in 1 male and female patient each. Remaining 23 subjects had normal increment in Free T4 levels indicating pseudomalabsorption. All these 23 subjects were counseled for strict adherence to treatment and proper technique and they were sent with weight appropriate dose of levothyroxine. All these 23 patients had normal thyroid profile on follow up.

Conclusion: Prevalence of levothyroxine malabsorption was 8% in our study. Subjects with elevated TSH on adequate dose of LT4 can be reliably diagnosed to be non-adherent to treatment with levothyroxine absorption test.

Keywords: Compliance, hypothyroidism, Levothyroxine malabsorption, pseudomalabsorption

M S Ramaiah Medicial College, Bengaluru, Karnataka, India

Background: Stress induced hyperglycemia (SIH) is commonly encountered in patients with critical illness and it occurs secondary to increase in the levels of counter-regulatory hormones which result in insulin resistance and increased glucose production resulting in hyperglycemia. Hyperglycemia has a negative impact on the function of the immune system, on the host response to illness, infectious and overall outcomes. It is important to recognize SIH as a prognostic factor and treat SIH to improve outcomes in critically ill patients. Glycemic gap (admission blood glucose – ADAG) a measure of SIH can be used to predict outcomes.

Objectives: To study glycemic gap as a predictor of outcome in critically ill patients with Diabetes Mellitus.

Their clinical profiles demographic data, admission blood glucose, Hba1C, ADAG, glycemic gap, APACHE II score was calculated, and patients were followed up till discharge or death and outcomes were noted.

Results: 100 critically ill patients were included in the current study, of which 76 were survivors and 24 were non survivors. Mean glycemic gap was significantly higher among non survivors than survivors 97.3 and 67.8 respectively. (p value – 0.018). Critically ill diabetic patients with glycemic gap >56mg/dl had significantly higher ICU mortality and more adverse outcomes in patients with glycemic gap <56mg/dl. Glycemic gap has a better discriminative power than HbA1c and admission blood sugar with AUCs of 0.672, 0.556 and 0.539 respectively.

Conclusion: In critically ill patients with diabetes, higher glycemic gap was associated with higher mortality and hence glycemic gap can be used as a predictor of outcomes in the same.

Abstract No: ABS034: Toe brachial index is superior to ankle brachial index in predicting atherosclerosis in people with type 2 diabetes

Pankaj Singhania, Abhranil Dhar, Subhankar Chowdhury, Satinath Mukhopadhyay

IPGMER, Kolkata

E-mail: drpankaj007@hotmail.com

Introduction: Carotid intima medial thickness (CIMT) is a well-established surrogate for atherosclerosis. Ankle brachial index (ABI) &Toe brachial index (TBI) are simple inexpensive bed side tools for diagnosing PAD in high-risk population like T2D. Therefore, it is expected that changes in ABI andTBI will reflect changes in CIMT and atherosclerosis.

Aim: To examine the correlation between ABI, TBI & CIMT in people with T2D and thus predict atherosclerosis.

Methods: 175 people with type 2 diabetes >50 yearswere included. ABI&TBI were measured with an automated vascular doppler XT 6 ports. ABI< 0.9 & TBI < 0.6 was considered abnormal. CIMT was measured at 3 sites bilaterally (common carotid, bifurcation, internal carotid artery) and mean CIMT≥0.7 mm was considered high.

Results: The mean age of the study population was 56.2 years & duration of diabetes was 13.4 years. Arm A was CIMT >= 0.7 and arm B, CIMT <0.7 mm [Table 1].

The sensitivity and NPV of ABI < 0.9 to detect increased CIMT was 95% and 97% respectively. Thus ABI <0.9 is unlikely to miss a high CIMT. The specificity was 30% showing that ABI may falsely diagnose increased CIMT. With a sensitivity of 97% and NPVof 98% TBI < 0.6 was also unlikely to miss increased CIMT. The specificity of 60%, however was higher than ABI. Thus TBI <0.6 was less likely to falsely diagnose high CIMT in normal subjects compared to ABI.

Conclusion: CIMT can be an excellent opportunity to predict atherosclerosis. CIMT measurement is operator&machine dependent. Both ABI and TBI are simple and inexpensive bedside screening tools which can be used as surrogates. TBI has a better specificity compared to ABI making it modality of choice for estimating CIMT. Therefore, TBI is superior to ABI in predicting atherosclerosis in subjects with T2D.

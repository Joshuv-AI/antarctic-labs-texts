Distribution of lengths of closed saddle connections on moduli space of large genus translation surface
Shenxing Zhang

Abstract
. Let S g S_{g} be a closed surface of genus g g and ℋ g {\mathcal{H}}_{g} be the moduli space of Abelian differentials on S g S_{g} . A stratum of ℋ g {\mathcal{H}}_{g} , endowed with the Masur-Veech measure, becomes a probability space. Then the number of closed saddle connections with lengths in [ a g , b g ] [\frac{a}{\sqrt{g}},\frac{b}{\sqrt{g}}] on a random translation surface in the stratum is a random variable. We prove that when g → ∞ g\to\infty , the distribution of the random variable converges to a Poisson distributed random variable. This result answers a question of Masur, Rafi and Randecker. Keywords: Random translation surface; closed saddle connection



1.
Introduction

In 2024, Masur, Rafi and Randecker study the distribution of open saddle connections on a random translation surface in
[
9
]
.
In this paper we study the distribution of closed saddle connections.

A translation surface can be denoted by
(X,\omega)
where
X
is a closed Riemann surface of genus
g
and
\omega
is an Abelian differential on
X
.

(X,\omega)
has a flat structure defined by the conical metric
|\omega|
, with singularities at the zeros of
\omega
.
If an
|\omega|
-geodesic of
(X,\omega)
connects two zeros (or one zero to itself) of
\omega
and has no other zeros in its interior,
we call it an open (or closed) saddle connection.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}
be the moduli space of unit-area translation surfaces of genus
g
.

{\mathcal{H}}_{g}
has a natural stratification according to the zero order of Abelian differentials.
Particularly, the stratum consisting of all abelian differentials in
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}
which have only simple zeros is called principal stratum,
denoted by
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(1^{2g-2})
. For convenience, if a stratum consists of Abelian differentials on which the number of zeros of order
d_{i}
is
\kappa_{i}

we denote it by

{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(d_{1}^{\kappa_{1}},\cdots,d_{k}^{\kappa_{k}})

where
\sum^{k}_{i=1}\kappa_{i}d_{i}=2g-2
.

Together with a finite natural measure which is introduced by Masur and Veech denoted by
\mu_{MV}
,
every stratum
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa)
of
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}
becomes a probability space
with probability measure
\frac{\mu_{MV}}{\operatorname{Vol}{{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa)}}
, where
\operatorname{Vol}{{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa)}
is the measure of total stratum,
which we call the Masur-Veech volume of
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa)
.

Given
(X,\omega)\in{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(1^{2g-2})
and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
let
N_{g,[a,b]}(X,\omega)
be the number of open saddle connections with lengths in the interval
[\frac{a}{g},\frac{b}{g}]
on
(X,\omega)
.
Then
N_{g,[a,b]}:{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(1^{2g-2})\to\mathbb{N}_{0}
becomes a random variable.
In
[
9
]
, Masur, Rafi and Randecker proved :

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
[a_{1},b_{1}],\cdots,[a_{k},b_{k}]
be
k
disjoint intervals. Then as
g\to\infty
, the vector of random variables

(N_{g,[a_{1},b_{1}]},\cdots,N_{g,[a_{k},b_{k}]}):{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(1^{2g-2})\to\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}

converges jointly in distribution to a vector of random variables with Poisson distributions of means
\lambda_{[a_{i},b_{i}]}
, where

\lambda_{[a_{i},b_{i}]}=8\pi(b_{i}^{2}-a_{i}^{2})

for
i=1,\cdots,k
. That is,

\mathbb{P}(N_{g,[a_{1},b_{1}]}=n_{1},\cdots,N_{g,[a_{k},b_{k}]}=n_{k})=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{[a_{i},b_{i}]}^{n_{i}}e^{-\lambda_{[a_{i},b_{i}]}}}{n_{i}!}

Their method is based on the relationship between Poisson distribution and its factorial moment
[
3
]
,
which was used first in the work of Mirzakhani–Petri
[
10
]
to study the distribution of closed hyperbolic geodesics on random hyperbolic surfaces in Teichmüller space with respect to Weil-Peterson measure.

As for translation surface, to use the method,

[
9
]
introduces a surgery to collapse the open saddle connections to obtain a new general translation surface in a new stratum.
This surgery does not work for closed saddle connections and they proposed the distribution question in the situation of closed saddle connections,
which inspires this work.
This paper gives a new surgery to collapse a closed saddle connection, which ensures the same method can be used for closed saddle connections.

Let
L_{g,[a,b]}(X,\omega)
be the number of closed saddle connections on
(X,\omega)
with lengths in
[\frac{a}{\sqrt{g}},\frac{b}{\sqrt{g}}]
.
Denote by
\mathcal{C}_{g}(\kappa)[\frac{a}{\sqrt{g}},\frac{b}{\sqrt{g}}]
the subset of
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa)
consisting of translation
surfaces on which there exist closed saddle connections
with lengths in
[\frac{a}{\sqrt{g}},\frac{b}{\sqrt{g}}]
.
The main result of this paper is the following theorem

Theorem 1.2
.

For the stratum
{\mathcal{H}}(2^{g-1})
and disjoint intervals
[a_{1},b_{1}],\cdots,[a_{k},b_{k}]
, when
g\to\infty
, the random variable sequence

(L_{g,[a_{1},b_{1}]},\cdots,L_{g,[a_{k},b_{k}]}):{\mathcal{H}}(2^{g-1})\to\mathbb{N}^{k}_{0}

converges jointly in distribution to a vector of random variables with Poisson distributions of means
\lambda_{[a_{i},b_{i}]}
, where

\lambda_{[a_{i},b_{i}]}=3\pi(b_{i}^{2}-a_{i}^{2}).

Remark 1.3
.

We will expalin the question is trivial for the principal stratum in Section
3
:
the probability measure of
\mathcal{C}_{g}(1^{2g-2})[\frac{a}{\sqrt{g}},\frac{b}{\sqrt{g}}]
is zero when
g\to\infty
.
For principal stratum, the length interval to be considered should be
[a,b]
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(2^{O(g)},1^{2g-2-2O(g)})
be a stratum with
O(g)
double zeros, then the result in Theorem
1.2
is also true.

2.
Background

2.1.
Moduli space of Abeilian differientials

Let
S_{g}
be a closed surface of genus
g
,
\mathcal{M}_{g}
be the moduli space, i.e, the set of isotopy classes of hyperbolic metric on
S_{g}
.

For some
X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
, an Abelian differential
\omega
on
X
is a differential
1
-form which under a local coordinate
(U,z)
of
X
can be written by

\omega=\omega(z)dz,

where
\omega(z):U\to\mathbb{C}
is a holomorphic function.
The holomorphic differential space on
X
is denoted by
H^{1}(X)
, which is a complex linear space of dimension
g
.

The moduli space of Abelian differential
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}
on
S_{g}
consists of
(X,\omega)
,
where
X\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
and
\omega\in H^{1}(X)
.
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}
is a bundle over
\mathcal{M}_{g}
. Since
dimH^{1}(X)=g,dim\mathcal{M}_{g}=3g-3
, we have
dim{\mathcal{H}}_{g}=4g-3
.
For
(X,\omega)
, define its area by
\int_{X}|\omega|^{2}
.

2.1.1.
Flat metric

A surace
(X,\omega)\in{\mathcal{H}}_{g}
is endowed with conical metric with singularities on
X
given by

|\omega|=|\omega(z)||dz|.

Consider the local coordinate given by
\omega
.
Under the natural coordinate of
\omega
, the transition map is like

z\to z+c

for a constant
c
.
We then call
(X,\omega)
a translation surface and
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}
becomes the moduli space of
translation surfaces
.

Denote the zero set of
(X,\omega)
by
\Sigma
,
in the natural coordinate, the flat metric becomes
|dz|
on
X\setminus\Sigma
.
Around a zero of order
n
, the total angle is
2(n+1)\pi
. Using polar doordinates the metric becomes

ds^{2}=dr^{2}+(n+1)^{2}r^{2}d\theta^{2}.

A
saddle connection
on
(X,\omega)
is an
|\omega|
- geodesic segment connecting two zeros of
\omega
and has no other zeros in the interior.
We call a saddle connection
open
if it connects two different zeros and
closed
if it connects a zero to itself.
Generally, a geodesic on
(X,\omega)
is a concatenation of some saddle connections, with the angle between two adjacent saddle connections larger than
\pi
.
However, there are geodesics that do not pass through any zeros, which we call
regular
.

For a simple closed curve
\gamma
, there are two situations:

1

If there are regular geodesics homotopic to
\gamma
, then all such geodesics foliate a flat cylinder, with boundaries not regular.

2

There is a unique geodesic homotopic to
\gamma
.

For a saddle connection or a closed curve
\gamma
, denote its flat length on
(X,\omega)
by
\ell_{\omega}(\gamma)
.

2.1.2.
Stratum and Masur-Veech measure

For
(X,\omega)\in{\mathcal{H}}_{g}
, the sum of its zeros orders is
2g-2
.

{\mathcal{H}}_{g}
has a natural stratum structure by the orders of zeros:
let
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n})
be a set of
n
positive integers satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\kappa_{i}=2g-2
.
Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa)
be the subset of
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}
consisting of Abelian differentials with zeros of orders
\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n}
.
Then we call
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa)
a stratum of
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}
with characterization
\kappa
.
When
\kappa=(1^{2g-2})
, we call
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa)

principal stratum
.
Then the total moduli space
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}
can be decomposed by

{\mathcal{H}}_{g}=\bigcup_{\kappa}{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa),

where
\kappa
takes on all sets of positive integers with sum
2g-2
.

Fix a characterization
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n})
, the local chart of
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa)
can be given by period mapping.
For
(X,\omega)\in{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa)
, denote its zero set by
\Sigma
and
\{\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{d}\}
be a basis of the relative
homology
H_{1}(X,\Sigma,\mathbb{Z})
. Then the period mapping can be defined on a neighborhood of
(X,\omega)
by

(X^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})\to\left(\int_{\gamma_{i}}\omega^{\prime}\right)_{i=1,\cdots,d},

where
(X^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})
is in a neighborhood of
(X,\omega)
.
Thus, locally
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa)
can be identified with
H_{1}(X,\Sigma,\mathbb{C})
.

From the dimension of
H_{1}(X,\Sigma,\mathbb{Z})
, we have

dim_{\mathbb{C}}{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa)=dimH_{1}(X,\Sigma,\mathbb{C})=d=2g-1+n.

So the dimension of principal stratum is equal to the total moduli space.

On each stratum, the period mapping induces a natural Lebesgue measure, normalized so that the lattice
H_{1}(X,\Sigma,\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}i)
has co-volume
1
.
This measure, which is introduced by Masur
[
8
]
and Veech
[
13
]
, is called Masur-Veech measure.
For a measured set
K
of
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa)
, denote its Masur-Veech measure by
\operatorname{Vol}(K)
.

Now we consider the hypersurface
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{g}(\kappa)
of
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa)
consisting of
(X,\omega)
with unit area.
The Masur-Veech measure on
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{g}(\kappa)
can be defined as follows:
for an open set
K
on
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{g}(\kappa)
, its Masur-Veech measure is defined by

\operatorname{Vol}(K)=dim_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa)\cdot\operatorname{Vol}(Cone(K)),

where
Cone(K)
is the cone of
K
.
In the following by abuse of notation we still denote
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}_{g}(\kappa)
by
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa)
.

Remark 2.1
.

Note that the stratum
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa)
may not be connected, here we consider the connected component. For more details one can refer to
[
7
]
.

Aggarwal
[
2
, Theorem 1.4]
proved that

Theorem 2.2
.

For
g>1
and a stratum
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa)
with characterization
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n})
,

\frac{4}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(\kappa_{i}+1)}\left(1-\frac{2^{2^{200}}}{g}\right)\leq\operatorname{Vol}({\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa))\leq\frac{4}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(\kappa_{i}+1)}\left(1+\frac{2^{2^{200}}}{g}\right).

2.2.
Siegel-Veech constant

2.2.1.
Configuration

For a translation surface
(X,\omega)
and a saddle connection
\gamma
on it, define the
holonomy vector of
\gamma
to be
\int_{\gamma}\omega
and denote it by
hol(\gamma)
.
Denote the set of all saddle connections of
(X,\omega)
by
\tilde{V}_{sc}(X,\omega)
.
Then define

V_{sc}(X,\omega)=\{hol(\gamma):\gamma\in\tilde{V}_{sc}(X,\omega)\}.

In fact all holonomy vectors of
\gamma\in\tilde{V}_{sc}(X,\omega)
define a holonomy mapping

hol:\tilde{V}_{sc}(X,\omega)\to V_{sc}(X,\omega).

By
[
15
, Proposition 3.1]
,
V_{sc}(X,\omega)
is a discrete subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
for any
(X,\omega)
.
Note that different saddle connections may have same holonomy vector.
For example two homologous saddle connections must have same holonomy,
here
homologous
for two open sadddle connections
\beta
and
\gamma
means
\beta\circ\gamma^{-1}
is homologous to zero.
For a vector
\vec{v}\in V_{sc}(X,\omega)
, define the
multiplicity of
\vec{v}
to be
|hol^{-1}(\vec{v})|
.
It is known that almost any translation surface in any stratum does not have a pair of non-homologous sharing a same holonomy vector
by
[
6
, Proposition 3.1]
. So in the following we consider only the surfaces on which if two saddle connections have same holonomy,
then they are homologous.

To determine how many surfaces have some specific saddle connections, we need to classify the saddle connections by some geometric data,
which we call
configuration
.

For open saddle connections, first fix two zeros
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
with orders
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
,
consider the saddle connections connecting
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
of multiplicity
p
and denote them by
\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{p}
,
where the orientation is from
p_{1}
to
p_{2}
and the cyclic order of
\gamma_{i}
is clockwise at
p_{1}
.
Now we consider the angle between
\gamma_{i}
and
\gamma_{i+1}
at
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
, which are even multiples of
\pi
.
Let the angle between
\gamma_{i}
and
\gamma_{i+1}
at
p_{1}
be
2(a^{\prime}_{i}+1)\pi
,
the angle between
\gamma_{i}
and
\gamma_{i+1}
at
p_{2}
be
2(a^{\prime\prime}_{i}+1)\pi
.
Then a configuration
\mathcal{C}
of open saddle connections consists of
(m_{1},m_{2},a^{\prime}_{i},a^{\prime\prime}_{i})
.

The situation of closed saddle connections is more complicated. Again we consider the closed saddle connections of multiplicity
p
.
Suppose
\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{p}
are homologous, and
\gamma_{1}
connects
p_{1}
to itself.
Note that some
\gamma_{i}
may connect other zero to itself than
p_{1}
, and some closed saddle connections may bound cylinders.
Denote by
J=\{j_{1},\cdots,j_{l}\}\in\{1,2,\cdots,p\}
the index set such that
\gamma_{j_{1}},\cdots,\gamma_{j_{l}}
bound
q
cylinders.
Cut off the
q
cylinders and
\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{p}
, there remain
p
connected components
X_{1},\cdots,X_{p}
.

X_{1},\cdots,X_{p}
can be divided into two types: components of
type one
have two boundary components,
type two
have one,
which is constructed by two
\gamma_{i}
and
\gamma_{j}
sharing one zero (and don’t be cut off).
Consider the angles the boundary curves bound inside the components.
If some
X_{k}
is of type one with boundaries
\gamma_{k}
and
\gamma_{k+1}
, denote the angles by
(2b^{\prime}_{k}+1)\pi
and
(2b^{\prime\prime}_{k}+1)\pi
.
If some
X_{i}
is of type two with boundary constructed by
\gamma_{i}
and
\gamma_{i+1}
, denote the angles by
2(a^{\prime}_{i}+1)\pi
and
2(a^{\prime\prime}_{i}+1)\pi
.
Then a configuration
\mathcal{C}
of closed saddle connections consists of
(J,b^{\prime}_{k},b^{\prime\prime}_{k},a^{\prime}_{i},a^{\prime\prime}_{i})
.

For a configuration
\mathcal{C}
, denote by
V_{\mathcal{C}}(X,\omega)
be the set of holonomy vectors of saddle connections satisfying
\mathcal{C}
.

2.2.2.
Siegel-Veech formula and Siegel-Veech constant

Given a stratum
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa)
and a configuration
\mathcal{C}
. Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be a integrable function with compact support,
the
Siegel-Veech transform
can be defined by

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{\mathcal{C}}

\displaystyle:{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa)\to\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\vec{v}\in V_{\mathcal{C}}(X,\omega)}f(\vec{v}).

Theorem 2.3

(Siegel-Veech formula
[
14
]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa)
be a connected stratum and
\mathcal{C}
a configuration of saddle connections.
Then there exists a constant
c(\mathcal{C},{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa))
such that for every integrable

f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
with compact support,

\mathbb{E}(\hat{f}_{\mathcal{C}})=\frac{1}{\operatorname{Vol}({\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa))}\int_{{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa)}\hat{f}_{\mathcal{C}}d\mu_{MV}=c(\mathcal{C},{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa))\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}fdxdy.

The constant
c(\mathcal{C},{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa))
is called
Siegel-Veech constant
of
\mathcal{C}
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa)
.

There have been many asymptotics of Siegel–Veech constants for large genus,
one can see
[
5
,
11
,
1
,
2
,
4
]
for more results.
Here we only state a result about closed saddle connections, for more details refer to
[
12
]
.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
12
]
Appendix Poposition A.1)
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa)
be a connected stratum and a fixed zero
\sigma
of order
m
.
For any configuration
\mathcal{C}
of closed saddle connections at
\sigma
of multiplicity
p
involving
q
cylinders and
t\leq p-1

distinct singularities
\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{t}
of orders
m_{1},\cdots,m_{t}
other than
\sigma
, the Siegel-Veech constant associated to
\mathcal{C}
is

c(\mathcal{C},{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa))=\frac{(m+1)\prod_{i=1}^{t}(m_{i}+1)}{2^{p-1}|\Gamma_{-}||\Gamma|(p+q-1)!}(1+o(1))^{p}O(\frac{1}{g^{2p+q-2}}),

where
|\Gamma_{-}|
and
|\Gamma|
are combinatorial constants depending on
p
.

2.3.
Factorial moment

For a probability space
(\Omega,\mathbb{P})
and a random variable
X
on it, its factorial moment generating function (FMGF) is defined as:

M_{X}(t)=\mathbb{E}[(1+t)^{X}],

and its k-th factorial moment
\mathbb{E}[(X)_{k}]
is defined by

\mathbb{E}[(X)_{k}]=\mathbb{E}[X(X-1)\cdots(X-k+1)].

In fact it is the k-th derivative of
M_{X}(t)
with respect to
t
at
t=0
.

Recall that a random variable
X:\Omega\to\mathbb{N}_{0}
is Poisson distributed with mean
\lambda
if

\mathbb{P}(X=k)=\frac{\lambda^{k}e^{-\lambda}}{k!}.

The following theorem from
[
3
]
gives the relationship between factorial moment and distribution for Poisson distribution:

Theorem 2.5

(The method of moment from
[
3
]
, Theorem 1.23)
.

Let
\left\{(\Omega_{i},\mathbb{P}_{i})\right\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of probability spaces.
For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
N_{1,i},\cdots,N_{m,i}:\Omega_{i}\to\mathbb{N}_{0}
be random variables for all
i\in\mathbb{N}

and suppose there exist
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m}\in(0,\infty)
such that:

\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[(N_{1,i})_{k_{1}}\cdots(N_{m,i})_{k_{m}}]=\lambda_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{m}^{k_{m}}

for all
k_{1}\cdots k_{m}\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathbb{P}[(N_{1,i})=n_{1},\cdots,(N_{m,i})=n_{m}]=\prod^{k}_{j=1}\frac{\lambda_{j}^{n_{j}}e^{-\lambda_{j}}}{n_{j}!}.

That is,
(N_{1,i},\cdots,N_{m,i}):\Omega_{i}\to\mathbb{N}^{m}_{0}
converges jointly in distribution to a vector which is independently Poisson distributed with means

\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{m}
.

3.
Some claims

Before the proof of the main theorem, we have to make some claims which will simplify the situation.
We consider the stratum whose zero order is
O(1)
, and it inplies
|\kappa|=O(g)
.

Claim 1
.

A closed saddle connection is non-separable.

Proof.

For a closed connection
\gamma
of
(X,\omega)
, if it’s separable, then it cuts
X
into
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
, and
\partial X_{1}=\partial X_{2}=\gamma
.
By Stokes theorem,
\int_{\gamma}\omega=0
, but since
\gamma
is a saddle connection its length is
|\int_{\gamma}\omega|
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Claim 2
.

The probability measure of the subset of
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa)
which has a closed saddle connection involving
q
cylinders of multiplicity
p

goes to
0
as
g\to\infty
, where
p>1
or
q>0
.

Proof.

From Theorem
2.4
,
the Siegel-Veech constant of configuration with closed saddle connection involving
q
cylinders of multiplicity
p
is
O(\frac{1}{g^{2p+q-2}})
.
Denote by
\mathcal{C}^{p,q}_{g}(\kappa)[\frac{a}{\sqrt{g}},\frac{b}{\sqrt{g}}]
the subset of
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa)
consisting of surfaces which have a closed saddle connection
of length in the interval
[\frac{a}{\sqrt{g}},\frac{b}{\sqrt{g}}]

involving
q
cylinders of multiplicity
p
.
Then for
p\geq 2
or
q\geq 1
, we have

\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{C}^{p,q}_{g}(\kappa)[\frac{a}{\sqrt{g}},\frac{b}{\sqrt{g}}])\leq|\kappa|O(\frac{1}{g^{q}})\frac{b^{2}-a^{2}}{g}\operatorname{Vol}({\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa))=O(\frac{1}{g})\operatorname{Vol}({\mathcal{H}}_{g}(\kappa)

where the coefficient
|\kappa|
is the selection of the zero that the closed saddle connection goes through.
So when
g\to\infty
its probability measure goes to
0

and it suffices to consider the closed saddle connection with multiplicity
1
and no cylinders around it.
∎

Claim 3
.

The angle of a closed saddle connection at the zero which it connects is odd multiples of
\pi
.

Proof.

For an Abelian differential
(X,\omega)
, if it has a closed saddle connection
\gamma
connecting a zero
p
, if its angle at
p
is
2k\pi
,
then the holonomy of
\gamma
and
-\gamma
have same direction. But

\int_{\gamma}\omega=-\int_{-\gamma}\omega.

It implies
\int_{\gamma}\omega=0
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Claim 4
.

If a closed saddle connection has angle
\pi
at one side, it must have a cylinder at the side.

Proof.

Let
\gamma
be a colsed saddle connection on
(X,\omega)
which has angle
\pi
at one side. Since
(X,\omega)
is oriented, its normal bundle of
\gamma
at the side is oriented.

\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{C}_{g}^{1}(\frac{B}{\sqrt{g}}))}{\operatorname{Vol}({\mathcal{H}}_{g}(2^{g-1}))}\leq\sum_{k}\sum_{(b^{\prime},b^{\prime\prime})}b^{\prime}b^{\prime\prime}B^{4}\frac{\operatorname{Vol}({\mathcal{H}}_{g-1}(b^{\prime}-1,b^{\prime\prime}-1,1^{k},2^{g-2-k}))}{\operatorname{Vol}({\mathcal{H}}_{g}(2^{g-1}))}(\frac{B^{2}}{g})^{k}=O(\frac{1}{g}).

∎

Combine the two proposition we have

Corollary 5.3
.

\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{C}^{exc}_{g,Per}(2^{g-1})[\frac{a}{\sqrt{g}},\frac{b}{\sqrt{g}}])}{\operatorname{Vol}({\mathcal{H}}_{g}(2^{g-1}))}=O(\frac{1}{g}).

5.2.
Collapsing simultaneously

Now fix a positive integer
K
and
n_{1},\cdots,n_{k}
a partition of
K
.
We want to compute the limit of the expectation

(L_{g,[a_{1},b_{1}]})_{n_{1}}\cdots(L_{g,[a_{k},b_{k}]})_{n_{k}},

for which we will collapse
K
closed saddle connections in order.

Define
\hat{\mathcal{C}}^{1,0}_{g}(2^{g-1})=(X,\omega,\Gamma_{1},\cdots,\Gamma_{k})
,
where
(X,\omega)\in\mathcal{C}^{1,0}_{g}(2^{g-1})
,
and
\Gamma_{i}
is an ordered list of
n_{i}
closed saddle connections
(\gamma_{1,i},\cdots,\gamma_{n_{i},i})

with lengths in
[\frac{a_{i}}{\sqrt{g}},\frac{b_{i}}{\sqrt{g}}]
.
Suppose
a_{1}\leq b_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq b_{2}\cdots\leq a_{k}\leq b_{k}
, let
B=b_{k}
.
Then we have

\bigcup_{i}\mathcal{C}^{exc}_{g,Per}(2^{g-1})[\frac{a_{i}}{\sqrt{g}},\frac{b_{i}}{\sqrt{g}}]\subset\mathcal{C}_{g}^{1}(\frac{B}{\sqrt{g}})\cup\mathcal{C}_{g}^{2}(\frac{B}{\sqrt{g}}).

Let
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{g,Per}=\mathcal{C}^{1,0}_{g,Per}(2^{g-1})\setminus(\mathcal{C}_{g}^{1}(\frac{B}{\sqrt{g}})\cup\mathcal{C}_{g}^{2}(\frac{B}{\sqrt{g}}))
,
we will adjust the surgery from last section on
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{g,Per}
to collapse the closed saddle connections in

\Gamma_{1},\cdots,\Gamma_{k}
in order.
The opening and pinching operations are same to the situation of one curve. The only modification is the equation
4.1
:
by remark
4.3
, we need to choose the moving vector
\upsilon
to ensure the area unchanged,
hence we can choose a basis containing
\gamma^{\prime}_{1},\gamma_{1}\cdots,\gamma^{\prime}_{K},\gamma_{K}
,
where
\gamma^{\prime}_{i}
is a saddle connection from opening
\gamma_{i}
.
Then the moving vector
\upsilon
can be constructed like equation
4.1
: after defining the value on
\gamma_{i}
,
we can choose another relative homotopy class and give a value of
\upsilon
on it to ensure the area unchanged.
This means we open and move the closed saddle connections simultaneously and then pinch them.

Finally we obtain a mapping

\hat{F}:\hat{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{g,Per}(2^{g-1})\to M_{K}\cdot\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}_{g-K}(2^{g-1-K},0^{2K})\times\prod^{k}_{i=1}(A_{[\frac{a_{i}}{\sqrt{g}},\frac{b_{i}}{\sqrt{g}}]})^{n_{i}},

where
\hat{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{g,Per}(2^{g-1})
is defined as
\hat{\mathcal{C}}^{1,0}_{g}(2^{g-1})

and
M_{K}
is a combinatorial data consisting of choosing
K
ordered zeros from
g-1
zeros to label. As the discussion above,
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{g,Per}(2^{g-1})}(L_{g,[a_{1},b_{1}]})_{n_{1}}\cdots(L_{g,[a_{k},b_{k}]})_{n_{k}}d\mu_{MV}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Vol}(\hat{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{g}(2^{g-1})))

\displaystyle=|M_{K}|\prod^{k}_{i=1}[\frac{\pi(b^{2}-a^{2})}{g}]^{n_{i}}\operatorname{Vol}({\mathcal{H}}_{g-k}(2^{g-1-K}))

\displaystyle\to\prod^{k}_{i=1}[\pi(b_{i}^{2}-a_{i}^{2})]^{n_{i}}\operatorname{Vol}({\mathcal{H}}_{g-K}(2^{g-1-K})),g\to\infty,

where the limit is because
|M_{K}|=(g-1)\cdots(g-K)
and

\lim_{g\to\infty}\frac{(g-1)\cdots(g-K)}{g^{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}}}=\frac{(g-1)\cdots(g-K)}{g^{K}}=1.

Let
L^{\prime}_{g,[a_{i},b_{i}]}
be the restriction of
L_{g,[a_{i},b_{i}]}
on
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{g,Per}(2^{g-1})
.
Then the limit of factorial moment of
(L^{\prime}_{g,[a_{i},b_{i}]})_{i}
is

\lim_{g\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[(L^{\prime}_{g,[a_{1},b_{1}]})_{n_{1}}\cdots(L^{\prime}_{g,[a_{k},b_{k}]})_{n_{k}}]=\lim_{g\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{Vol}({\mathcal{H}}_{g-K}(2^{g-1-K}))}{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{g,Per}(2^{g-1}))}\prod^{k}_{i=1}[\pi(b_{i}^{2}-a_{i}^{2})]^{n_{i}}.

From Proposition
5.1
and
5.2
, we have

\lim_{g\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{g,Per}(2^{g-1}))}{\operatorname{Vol}({\mathcal{H}}_{g}(2^{g-1}))}=1.

From Theorem
2.2
, we have

\lim_{g\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{Vol}({\mathcal{H}}_{g-K}(2^{g-1-K}))}{\operatorname{Vol}({\mathcal{H}}_{g}(2^{g-1}))}=3^{K}.

So we have

\lim_{g\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[(L^{\prime}_{g,[a_{1},b_{1}]})_{n_{1}}\cdots(L^{\prime}_{g,[a_{k},b_{k}]})_{n_{k}}]=\prod^{k}_{i=1}[3\pi(b_{i}^{2}-a_{i}^{2})]^{n_{i}}.

By theorem
2.5
,
(L^{\prime}_{g,[a_{1},b_{1}]},\cdots,L^{\prime}_{g,[a_{k},b_{k}]})
converges jointly in distribution to a vector of random variables with Poisson distributions of means
\lambda_{[a_{i},b_{i}]}
, where

\lambda_{[a_{i},b_{i}]}=3\pi(b_{i}^{2}-a_{i}^{2}).

From the property of Poisson distribution, we have

\mathbb{P}(L^{\prime}_{g,[a_{1},b_{1}]}=n_{1},\cdots,L^{\prime}_{g,[a_{k},b_{k}]}=n_{k})=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{[a_{i},b_{i}]}^{n_{i}}e^{-\lambda_{[a_{i},b_{i}]}}}{n_{i}!}.

On the other hand

\mathbb{P}(L^{\prime}_{g,[a_{1},b_{1}]}=n_{1},\cdots,L^{\prime}_{g,[a_{k},b_{k}]}=n_{k})=\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(\{(X,\omega):L^{\prime}_{g,[a_{i},b_{i}]}(X,\omega)=n_{i},i=1,\cdots,k\})}{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{g,Per}(2^{g-1}))}.

Note that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Vol}(\{(X,\omega):L_{g,[a_{i},b_{i}]}(X,\omega)=n_{i},i=1,\cdots,k\})-\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{C}_{g}^{1}(\frac{B}{\sqrt{g}})\cup\mathcal{C}_{g}^{2}(\frac{B}{\sqrt{g}}))

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{Vol}(\{(X,\omega):L^{\prime}_{g,[a_{i},b_{i}]}(X,\omega)=n_{i},i=1,\cdots,k\})

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{Vol}(\{(X,\omega):L_{g,[a_{i},b_{i}]}(X,\omega)=n_{i},i=1,\cdots,k\}).

From Proposition
5.1
and
5.2
, we have

\lim_{g\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{g,Per}(2^{g-1}))}{\operatorname{Vol}({\mathcal{H}}_{g}(2^{g-1}))}=1,\\
\lim_{g\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{C}_{g}^{1}(\frac{B}{\sqrt{g}})\cup\mathcal{C}_{g}^{2}(\frac{B}{\sqrt{g}}))}{\operatorname{Vol}({\mathcal{H}}_{g}(2^{g-1}))}=0.

So

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(L_{g,[a_{1},b_{1}]}=n_{1},\cdots,L_{g,[a_{k},b_{k}]}=n_{k})-\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{C}_{g}^{1}(\frac{B}{\sqrt{g}})\cup\mathcal{C}_{g}^{2}(\frac{B}{\sqrt{g}}))}{\operatorname{Vol}({\mathcal{H}}_{g}(2^{g-1}))}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(L^{\prime}_{g,[a_{1},b_{1}]}=n_{1},\cdots,L^{\prime}_{g,[a_{k},b_{k}]}=n_{k})\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{g,Per}(2^{g-1}))}{\operatorname{Vol}({\mathcal{H}}_{g}(2^{g-1}))}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(L_{g,[a_{1},b_{1}]}=n_{1},\cdots,L_{g,[a_{k},b_{k}]}=n_{k}).

Let
g\to\infty
we have

\mathbb{P}(L_{g,[a_{1},b_{1}]}=n_{1},\cdots,L_{g,[a_{k},b_{k}]}=n_{k})=\mathbb{P}(L^{\prime}_{g,[a_{1},b_{1}]}=n_{1},\cdots,L^{\prime}_{g,[a_{k},b_{k}]}=n_{k}).

So
(L_{g,[a_{1},b_{1}]},\cdots,L_{g,[a_{k},b_{k}]})
also converges jointly in distribution to a vector of random variables
with Poisson distributions of means
\lambda_{[a_{i},b_{i}]}
, where

\lambda_{[a_{i},b_{i}]}=3\pi(b_{i}^{2}-a_{i}^{2}).

Since we don’t need to deal with simple zeros in the collapsing surgery,
samilarly to the proof of theorem
1.2
, we can prove Corollary
1.4
.

6.
General stratum

This section we consider the stratum
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}(m^{O(g)},1^{2g-2-mO(g)})
, where
m\geq 3
.

If a stratum has zeros of order more than
3
, the angles at both sides are not certain.
But we can consider some fixed configuration of closed saddle connection.
For a configuration
(J,b^{\prime}_{k},b^{\prime\prime}_{k},a^{\prime}_{i},a^{\prime\prime}_{i})
of closed saddle connection.
We have known it suffices to consider multiplicity
1
without cylinders, then the configuration become
(b^{\prime},b^{\prime\prime})
, where
b^{\prime}+b^{\prime\prime}=m
,
which means the angles of the closed saddle connection are
(2b^{\prime}+1)\pi
on one side and
(2b^{\prime\prime}+1)\pi
on the other side.
Now we choose all such configuration at each
m
-order zeros.

For
k
such closed saddle connections, we can use the surgery in Section
4
to collapse them.
Note that in this situation the opening surgery will replace the zero of order
m
to two zeros of order
1
and
m-1
,
and the angles at both sides of the zero of
m-1
order are
(2b^{\prime})\pi
and
(2b^{\prime\prime})\pi
. Then after moving and pinching,
the simple zero become two regular points and the zero of
m-1
order become two zeros of order
b^{\prime}-1
and
b^{\prime\prime}-1
.
Finally we obtain a mapping

\hat{F}:\hat{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{g,Per}(m^{O(g)},1^{O^{\prime}(g)})\to\tilde{{\mathcal{H}}}_{g-K}((b^{\prime}-1)^{K},(b^{\prime\prime}-1)^{K},m^{O(g)-K},1^{O^{\prime}(g)})\times\prod^{k}_{i=1}(A_{[\frac{a_{i}}{\sqrt{g}},\frac{b_{i}}{\sqrt{g}}]})^{n_{i}},

where
O^{\prime}(g)=2g-2-mO(g)
.
Again we have

\lim_{g\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[(L^{\prime}_{g,[a_{1},b_{1}]})_{n_{1}}\cdots(L^{\prime}_{g,[a_{k},b_{k}]})_{n_{k}}]=\prod^{k}_{i=1}(\frac{(m+1)\pi(b_{i}^{2}-a_{i}^{2})}{b^{\prime}b^{\prime\prime}})^{n_{i}}.

And similar to the proof of theorem
1.2
we have

Theorem 6.1
.

For the stratum
{\mathcal{H}}(m^{O(g)},1^{2g-2-mO(g)})
, a configuration
(b^{\prime},b^{\prime\prime})
and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,
let the random variable

L_{\mathcal{C},g,[a,b]}:{\mathcal{H}}(m^{O(g)},1^{2g-2-mO(g)})\to\mathbb{N}_{0}

be the number of closed saddle connections on
(X,\omega)
satisfying the configuration with lengths in
[\frac{a}{\sqrt{g}},\frac{b}{\sqrt{g}}]
.
Then for disjoint intervals
[a_{1},b_{1}],\cdots,[a_{k},b_{k}]
, when
g\to\infty
, the random variable sequence

(L_{\mathcal{C},g,[a_{1},b_{1}]},\cdots,L_{\mathcal{C},g,[a_{k},b_{k}]}):{\mathcal{H}}(m^{O(g)},1^{2g-2-mO(g)})\to\mathbb{N}^{k}_{0}

converges jointly in distribution to a vector of random variables with Poisson distributions of means
\lambda_{[a_{i},b_{i}]}
, where

\lambda_{[a_{i},b_{i}]}=\frac{(m+1)\pi(b_{i}^{2}-a_{i}^{2})}{b^{\prime}b^{\prime\prime}}.

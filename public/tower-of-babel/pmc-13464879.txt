Comparative study of the tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes before and after neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma
Authors: Yanzhao Xu, Hao Wang, Na Wang, Saijin Cui, Jinyang Guo, Chao Huang, Mingbo Wang, Peng Su, Junhao Qi, Ziqiang Tian
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundObjectiveTo explore changes of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes and the relationship with therapeutic effect before and after neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy in patients with esophageal squamous cell carcinoma(ESCC).MethodThis study collected clinical data from 25 ESCC patients with neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy from November 2019 to June 2022 in our hospital. After the completion of the neoadjuvant treatment, Mckeown MIE surgery was performed. The pathological specimen before therapy and after surgery was collected. Immunohistochemical analysis was performed to obtain the tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) infiltration of the tumor before and after treatment.ResultSeven (28%) patients achieved pathology complete response (pCR group) and 18 (72%) patients did not achieve pCR (non-pCR group). Compared with non-pCR group, the after treatment proportion of CD4 + TILs (P = 0.012) and the proportion of CD8 + TILs (P = 0.018) were significantly increased in pCR group; There was no significant change in the proportion of CD20 + TILs (Z = -1.933, P = 0.053). In pCR group, the proportion of CD4 + TILs (P = 0.047) and the proportion of CD8 + TILs (P = 0.036) were significantly increased after treatment; The proportion of CD20 + TILs (P = 0.111) did not change significantly. In non-pCR group, no significant changes were observed in proportions of CD4 + TILs (P = 0.729), CD8 + TILs (P = 0.712), and CD20 + TILs (P = 0.811) between before and after treatment.ConclusionFor ESCC patients who received neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, patients with pCR had a more active immune response independent of pre-treatment PD-L1 expression levels, and the pre-treatment CD4 + TILs ratio may be one of the predictors of their survival.

Introduction

Esophageal cancer remains one of the most common malignant tumors of the digestive system worldwide, and its 5-year survival rate remains persistently low [1]. In China, the pathological profile of esophageal cancer differs significantly from that in Western countries, with squamous cell carcinoma being the predominant subtype [2]. The treatment paradigm for esophageal cancer has evolved from surgery alone to a comprehensive approach that primarily involves surgery, complemented by radiotherapy, chemotherapy, and immunotherapy. In recent years, neoadjuvant immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy has emerged as a promising neoadjuvant treatment strategy. Studies have indicated that this approach can achieve a higher pathological complete response (pCR) rate, offering potential survival benefits for patients with locally advanced esophageal cancer. However, the mechanisms underlying immunotherapy in esophageal cancer are still under investigation.

The tumor immune microenvironment (TIM), which comprises both anti-tumor immune effector cells and molecules as well as immunosuppressive cells and molecules, plays a crucial role in tumor initiation and progression. Under physiological conditions, the immune system can recognize and eliminate transformed tumor cells, thereby preventing the development of malignant tumors. However, during their interaction with the immune system, some tumor cells acquire the ability to escape immune surveillance. They achieve this by expressing and secreting immunosuppressive molecules, inducing the accumulation of immunosuppressive cells, and establishing an immunosuppressive microenvironment, which ultimately facilitates the growth of tumor cells into malignant tumors. Tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs), mainly composed of T cells, B cells, and natural killer cells, are critical components of the tumor immune microenvironment. Several studies have confirmed the prognostic impact of TILs in various cancer types, including gastric cancer, breast cancer, and melanoma [3–5].

In the context of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC), the changes in TILs before and after neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, as well as the relationship between the efficacy of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy and TIL status, remain to be elucidated. The present study aimed to investigate the changes in TIL subpopulations following neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy and their association with treatment response.

Data and methods

General information

We collected data from 25 patients with ESCC who underwent neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy at our hospital from November 2019 to June 2022. All experimental procedures were approved by the Clinical Research Ethics Committee of our hospital of (NO.2023ks151). All patients were informed as to the purpose of this study, and that this study complied with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Inclusion criteria were as follows:

① Patients diagnosed with esophageal squamous cell carcinoma in our pathology department.

② Age ≥ 18 years.

③Adequate and accessible pre- and post-treatment pathological specimens.④ Underwent 2 cycles of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, with a regimen comprising paclitaxel (albumin-bound) + nedaplatin + sintilimab.⑤ All enrolled patients underwent video-assisted thoracoscopic and laparoscopy-assisted Mckeown esophagectomy performed by experienced thoracic surgeons.

⑥ Complete and retrievable clinical and pathological data.

⑦ Patients with thoracic esophageal cancer.

Exclusion criteria were as follows:

①Other pathological types of esophageal cancer (such as adenocarcinoma, adenosquamous carcinoma, small cell carcinoma, etc.).

②Concurrent primary malignant tumors or immune system disorders.

③ Previous history of other antitumor treatments.

We collected pathological specimens from gastroscopy biopsies before treatment and surgical specimens after treatment for further research. The specific research process is illustrated in Fig. 1.

Treatment methods

Neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy

The neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy regimen included: intravenous infusion of paclitaxel (albumin-bound) at 200 mg/m2 on Day 1; intravenous infusion of nedaplatin at 80–100 mg/m2 on Day 2; and on the first day of each cycle, patients received sintilimab injection at 200 mg every 3 weeks. All 25 patients in the study tolerated the neoadjuvant treatment well, experiencing no adverse events greater than or equal to Grade 3, and all underwent subsequent surgical intervention.

Surgical procedure

All patients underwent the McKeown minimally invasive esophagectomy, utilizing both video-assisted thoracoscopic and laparoscopic techniques. In the thoracic phase, the patient was positioned in a left lateral decubitus, prone position, ensuring optimal exposure. Under video-assisted thoracoscopy, the azygos vein arch was dissected, the esophagus was mobilized, and lymph nodes in the paraesophageal, and subcarinal regions, as well as para-recurrent laryngeal nerve lymph nodes were meticulously cleared. After completing the thoracic procedure, the patient’s position was changed to supine with elevated shoulders. The head was turned to the right to fully expose the left neck area. In the abdominal phase, laparoscopic techniques were employed to mobilize the stomach and clear lymph nodes in the abdominal area, including the para-cardial and left gastric artery regions. Simultaneously, a small incision of approximately 5 cm was made along the anterior border of the sternocleidomastoid muscle in the left neck area to dissect and transect the cervical esophagus. A central upper abdominal incision of approximately 5 cm was made, and the esophagus and stomach were exteriorized. A tubular stomach was created extracorporeally. The tubular stomach was pulled up to the neck through the esophageal bed, and an esophagus-tubular stomach anastomosis was performed. All surgeries were performed by the same group of skilled surgeons proficient in these techniques.

TILs and CPS evaluation

All slide interpretations were independently performed by two senior pathologists who were blinded to patients’ clinical and pathological data. For interobserver consistency evaluation, tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) levels evaluated by the two pathologists were dichotomized into high and low groups using the same median cutoff value adopted in subsequent analyses. Cohen’s Kappa coefficient was then calculated for each marker to quantify the inter-pathologist agreement.

For the quantification of CD4+, CD8 + and CD20 + TILs, tumor sections were initially examined at low magnification (×100), and positively stained lymphocytes were enumerated under high magnification (×200). Briefly, regions with abundant TIL infiltration were first identified. Five high-power fields were randomly selected in both the stromal and tumor nest areas to determine the proportion of immunopositive lymphocytes. The percentage of positive lymphocytes was calculated as the number of positively stained lymphocytes divided by the total number of TILs in each field, multiplied by 100%, and the average value was recorded.

CPS (Combined Positive Score, Fig. 2): The calculation method involved dividing the number of PD-L1 stained cells (including tumor cells, macrophages, and lymphocytes) by the total number of viable tumor cells and then multiplying the result by 100.

Statistical methods

All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS 26.0 and GraphPad Prism 9.0. Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages, and between-group differences were compared using Fisher’s exact test. The normality of continuous variables was assessed via the Shapiro–Wilk test.

Normally distributed continuous data were presented as mean ± standard deviation, with the independent samples t-test applied for between-group comparisons and the paired t-test for intra-group paired comparisons. Non-normally distributed continuous data were reported as median (interquartile range). The Mann–Whitney U test was used for intergroup comparison, while the Wilcoxon signed-rank test was adopted for paired comparison.

All statistical tests were two-tailed, and a P-value < 0.05 was defined as statistically significant.

Result

Patient characteristics

A total of 25 patients were enrolled in this study, including 23 males (92%) and 2 females (8%), with a mean age of 62.92 ± 7.18 years. Pathological staging showed that 10 patients (40%) had stage II disease, 11 (44%) had stage III, and 4 (16%) had stage IVA. Pathological complete response was achieved in 7 patients (28%, pCR group), while the remaining 18 patients (72%) did not achieve pCR (non-pCR group). No statistically significant differences were observed between the pCR and non-pCR groups with respect to sex, age, tumor location, or clinical stage (all P > 0.05). Patients with a higher pre-treatment proportion of CD4 + TILs had significantly better two-year survival than those with a lower proportion (P = 0.040). Discussion

Esophageal cancer remains one of the most prevalent malignant tumors of the digestive tract worldwide. Patients in China account for nearly half of the global cases. The majority of patients are diagnosed at an advanced stage, often with lymph node or even distant metastasis. The molecular mechanisms underlying esophageal cancer progression are complex and involve gene mutations, signaling pathway dysregulation, oxidative stress, and aberrant gene expression. Despite significant advances in early detection, surgical techniques, and therapeutic strategies, the prognosis of patients with esophageal cancer remains poor, largely due to the predominance of advanced-stage diagnoses [6]. For patients with locally advanced esophageal cancer who are eligible for surgical resection, neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy has been widely recognized as the standard treatment regimen both domestically and internationally [7]. Neoadjuvant therapy offers several advantages, including an increased R0 resection rate, reduced local recurrence, and significantly improved long-term prognosis [8]. Research has shown that immunotherapy and chemotherapy can synergize to enhance antitumor effects [5]. In recent years, neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy has emerged as a novel approach for esophageal cancer. Studies have indicated that combining neoadjuvant immunotherapy with chemotherapy can achieve a higher pCR rate, offering potential survival benefits for patients with locally advanced disease. For instance, Zhigang Li and colleagues from Shanghai Chest Hospital evaluated the efficacy of neoadjuvant chemotherapy (albumin-bound paclitaxel plus carboplatin) combined with camrelizumab in locally advanced ESCC. A total of 60 patients were enrolled, of whom 55 (91.7%) received two cycles of neoadjuvant treatment. Among these, 51 patients underwent surgery, achieving an R0 resection rate of 98% (50/51) and a pCR rate of 39.2% (20/51) [9].

The PD-L1/PD-1 pathway is primarily known to exert negative regulatory effects on the immune system. In antitumor immunity, PD-L1 expressed on the surface of tumor cells binds to PD-1 on T cells, thereby inhibiting T cell activation, promoting immune tolerance, and ultimately facilitating tumor immune escape [10]. PD-1 inhibitors block this pathway within the tumor microenvironment, relieve T cell inhibition, and consequently trigger antitumor immunity. Chemotherapy drugs mainly eliminate tumor cells through cytotoxic effects. In the context of combination immunotherapy for malignant tumors, accumulating evidence indicates that, beyond direct cytotoxicity, chemotherapeutic agents can enhance the efficacy of immunotherapy by inducing immunogenic cell death, selectively depleting myeloid-derived suppressor cells, and reducing regulatory T cells [11].

TILs are essential components of the tumor immune microenvironment [12]. They include both positive regulators of immune responses, such as cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs) and natural killer (NK) cells, and negative regulators, such as CD4 + CD25+FOXP3 + regulatory T cells. Initially recognized as potential effectors for adoptive T cell therapy in metastatic melanoma, TILs have recently been associated with prognosis in various cancer types [13–16].CD8 is primarily expressed on cells with positive immune functions, including CTLs and NK cells. CTLs are critical effector cells in antitumor immunity [17], acting through granule exocytosis and the Fas pathway [18]. Numerous studies have demonstrated a positive correlation between CTL infiltration and patient survival across multiple malignancies [19]. Although CTLs, which directly kill tumor cells, have drawn considerable attention in antitumor immunity, the role of CD4 + T cells is equally important and should not be overlooked. Previous research has confirmed that CD4 + T cells are essential for maintaining CTL-mediated antitumor responsess [20].Activated CD4 + T cells secrete interleukin-2, which activates CD8 + CTLs expressing CD25. Furthermore, CD4 + T cells indirectly support the antitumor function of CD8 + CTLs by promoting the pro-inflammatory antigen presentation of dendritic cells [21].

Neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy has demonstrated substantial therapeutic efficacy in ESCC. In recent years, multiple clinical trials have further shown that combining neoadjuvant chemotherapy with immunotherapy also yields excellent treatment outcomes in this patient population [7]. These findings provide strong impetus to investigate predictive biomarkers and the mechanisms underlying the efficacy of neoadjuvant chemotherapy combined with immunotherapy in ESCC. Most existing literature on TILs in ESCC has focused on untreated patients or those receiving radiotherapy or chemotherapy alone. Consequently, studies examining TILs in the setting of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy remain scarce. Specifically, the dynamic changes in TILs before and after neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, as well as the relationship between treatment efficacy and TIL profiles, have yet to be clarified. The present study was therefore designed to explore alterations in TIL subpopulations following neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy and their association with therapeutic response.

In this study, we investigated the relationship between pathological response to neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy in ESCC and TIL characteristics before and after treatment. No significant differences were observed between the pCR and non-pCR groups in baseline proportions of CD4 + TILs, CD8 + TILs, or CD20 + TILs, nor in baseline CPS scores (all P > 0.05). However, after treatment, the pCR group exhibited significantly higher proportions of CD4 + and CD8 + TILs compared with the non-pCR group (both P < 0.05). These findings suggest that neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy may more effectively activate antitumor immune responses in patients achieving pCR, thereby contributing to better therapeutic outcomes, although the precise mechanisms remain to be further elucidated.

Additionally, some studies have suggested that the combined positive score (CPS) may serve as a predictive biomarker for achieving pathological complete response (pCR) following neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy in esophageal cancer [22]. However, Zhou et al. reported no significant differences in CPS scores among different pathological response groups [23] In line with their findings, the present study observed no statistically significant difference in pre-treatment CPS scores between the pCR and non-pCR groups (P > 0.05). These results indicate that while CPS may hold potential predictive value for the efficacy of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy in ESCC, further validation in larger cohorts is warranted.

Zhao and colleagues performed a survival analysis of 120 patients with ESCC and found that patients with high stromal infiltration of CD4 + T cells experienced longer survival. Both CD4⁺ and CD8 + T cell infiltration were identified as independent protective prognostic factors in ESCC [24]. The favorable prognostic impact of high CD8 + TIL infiltration has also been confirmed by previous studies [25]. In the present study, we compared changes in the proportions of CD4+, CD8+, and CD20 + TILs using paired pre- and post-treatment samples. In the pCR group, the proportions of both CD4 + and CD8 + TILs were significantly increased after treatment (both P < 0.05), suggesting that neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy promotes the enrichment of these two TIL subpopulations in pCR patients. Moreover, we observed that patients with a higher pre-treatment proportion of CD4 + TILs had a higher two-year survival rate. Nevertheless, given the short follow-up period and the small sample size of this study, whether post-treatment enrichment of CD4 + TILs translates into long-term survival benefits requires confirmation through larger cohorts and extended follow-up. In contrast, the non-pCR group showed no significant changes in the proportions of CD4 + or CD8 + TILs before versus after treatment (both P > 0.05), indicating that the activation of antitumor immunity in these patients was lower than that in the pCR group.

CD20 is expressed on the surface of B cells at various developmental stages, excluding plasma cells, and B cells primarily participate in adaptive humoral immunity. Research has suggested that tumor-infiltrating B cells within tertiary lymphoid structures (TLS) may be associated with the efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors in malignant tumors [26]. However, in our study, no significant differences in CD20 + TILs were observed among the groups. TLS are composed of multiple cell types, including B cells, T cells, fibroblasts, and dendritic cells. Due to limitations in sample size and experimental methodology, we did not investigate the relationships between TLS, myeloid cells, or stromal cells and the efficacy of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy; rather, we only analyzed the proportion of CD20 + lymphocytes. In the immune microenvironment, CD20 + lymphocytes are not restricted to TLS, which may explain the absence of significant differences in CD20 + lymphocyte counts between the pCR and non-pCR groups. The role of CD20 + TILs in neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy for ESCC warrants further exploration.

It should be acknowledged that the present study has several notable limitations:

First, this was a single-center retrospective investigation with a relatively small sample size, which may compromise the statistical power and generalizability of the findings.

Second, inherent discrepancies exist between pre-treatment endoscopic biopsy specimens and post-treatment surgical resection samples. Pre-treatment biopsies capture only a limited, superficial portion of the tumor, whereas surgical specimens provide broader and deeper tissue coverage. Differences in sampling depth, along with alterations in tumor heterogeneity and tissue composition induced by neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy-including residual tumor burden, stromal remodeling, fibrosis, necrosis, and treatment-associated inflammatory changes-may have influenced the assessment of TILs. Furthermore, our analytical approach tended to select regions with abundant lymphocyte infiltration, which could introduce sampling bias, particularly in post-treatment resection specimens. Consequently, the observed dynamic changes in TIL subpopulations before and after treatment may reflect temporal shifts in immune-related histological characteristics rather than precise spatial variations within identical tumor regions.

Third, the single-marker chromogenic immunohistochemistry employed in this study cannot distinguish functionally distinct lymphocyte subsets. For instance, CD4 staining does not discriminate helper T cells from immunosuppressive regulatory T cells. Similarly, CD8 staining alone cannot determine whether CD8 + T cells are in an activated, exhausted, or cytotoxic state. Therefore, the increased infiltration of CD4 + and CD8 + TILs observed herein should be interpreted as alterations in the phenotypic landscape of TILs. Future studies should utilize multiplex immunofluorescence, spatial transcriptomics, or digital pathology-based quantitative analysis to achieve a more precise characterization of the tumor immune microenvironment following neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy.

Finally, the follow-up duration in this cohort was relatively short, precluding comprehensive survival outcome analysis. Large-scale, multicenter, prospective studies with extended follow-up are therefore warranted to validate and further elucidate the effects of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy on TILs in esophageal cancer. Longer follow-up data will also facilitate a more accurate evaluation of the survival benefits associated with neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy in patients with esophageal squamous cell carcinoma.

Conclusions

In summary, our preliminary findings indicate that among esophageal cancer patients treated with neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, those who achieve pCR exhibit a more robust antitumor immune response than non-pCR patients, independent of pre-treatment PD-L1 expression levels. In pCR patients, neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy appeared to promote the enrichment of CD4 + and CD8 + TILs within the tumor tissue. Moreover, a higher pre-treatment proportion of CD4 + TILs was associated with a higher two-year survival rate. Given the inherent limitations of this single-center retrospective study-including a small sample size (25 cases, of which only 7 achieved pCR)-these findings require validation through multicenter, large-scale prospective studies.

Fritz Gesztesy, Selim Sukhtaiev

Abstract
. We survey various properties of Krein–von Neumann extensions S K S_{K} and the reduced Krein–von Neumann operator S ^ K \widehat{S}_{K} in connection with a strictly positive (symmetric) operator S S with nonzero deficiency indices. In particular, we focus on the resolvents of S K S_{K} and S ^ K \widehat{S}_{K} and of the trace ideal properties of the resolvent of S ^ K \widehat{S}_{K} , and make some comparisons with the corresponding properties of the resolvent of the Friedrichs extension S F S_{F} . We also recall a parametrization of all nonnegative self-adjoint extensions of S S and various Krein-type resolvent formulas for any two relatively prime self-adjoint extensions of S S , utilizing a Donoghue-type M M -operator (i.e., an energy parameter dependent Dirichlet-to-Neumann-type map).

2.
On the Abstract Friedrichs and Krein–von Neumann Extension

In this section we recall various facts on the abstract Krein–von Neumann extension.

To set the stage, a linear operator
S:\dom(S)\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
, is called
symmetric
, if

(u,Sv)_{\mathcal{H}}=(Su,v)_{\mathcal{H}},\quad u,v\in\dom(S).

(2.1)

If
\dom(S)={\mathcal{H}}
, the classical Hellinger–Toeplitz theorem guarantees that
S\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
, in which situation
S
is readily seen to be self-adjoint. In general, however, symmetry is a considerably weaker property than self-adjointness and a classical problem in functional analysis is that of determining all self-adjoint extensions of a given unbounded symmetric operator of equal and nonzero deficiency indices. In this manuscript we will be particularly interested in this question within the class of
densely defined
(
i.e.,
\overline{\dom(S)}={\mathcal{H}}
)
, non-negative operators (in fact, in most instances
S
will even turn out to be strictly positive) and we focus almost exclusively on self-adjoint extensions that are non-negative operators.

We recall that a linear operator
S:\dom(S)\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}

is called
non-negative
provided

(u,Su)_{\mathcal{H}}\geq 0,\quad u\in\dom(S).

(2.2)

(In particular,
S
is symmetric in this case.)
S
is called
strictly positive
, if for some

\varepsilon>0
,
(u,Su)_{\mathcal{H}}\geq\varepsilon\|u\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}
,
u\in\dom(S)
.
Next, we recall that
A\leq B
for two self-adjoint operators in
{\mathcal{H}}
if

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\dom(|A|^{1/2})\supseteq\dom(|B|^{1/2})\,\text{ and }\\
&(|A|^{1/2}u,U_{A}|A|^{1/2}u)_{{\mathcal{H}}}\leq(|B|^{1/2}u,U_{B}|B|^{1/2}u)_{{\mathcal{H}}},\;u\in\dom(|B|^{1/2}),\end{split}

(2.3)

where
U_{C}
denotes the partial isometry in
{\mathcal{H}}
in the polar decomposition of
a densely defined closed operator
C
in
{\mathcal{H}}
,
C=U_{C}|C|
,
|C|=(C^{*}C)^{1/2}
. (If,
in addition,
C
is self-adjoint, then
U_{C}
and
|C|
commute.)
We also recall (see, e.g.,
[
36
, Section I.6]
,
[
62
, Theorem VI.2.21]
) that if
A
and
B
are both
self-adjoint and nonnegative in
{\mathcal{H}}
, then

0\leq A\leq B\,\text{ if and only if }\,(B+aI_{\mathcal{H}})^{-1}\leq(A+aI_{\mathcal{H}})^{-1}\,\text{ for all $a>0$,}

(2.4)

(which implies
0\leq A^{1/2}\leq B^{1/2}
) and

\ker(A)=\ker\big(A^{1/2}\big)

(2.5)

(with
C^{1/2}
the unique nonnegative square root of a nonnegative self-adjoint operator
C
in
{\mathcal{H}}
).

For simplicity we will always adhere in the following to the conventions that
S
is a linear, unbounded, densely defined, nonnegative (i.e.,
S\geq 0
) operator in
{\mathcal{H}}
, and that
S
has nonzero deficiency indices, that is,

{\rm def}(S)=\dim(\ker(S^{*}-zI_{{\mathcal{H}}}))\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\infty\},\quad z\in{\mathbb{C}}\backslash[0,\infty).

(2.6)

Moreover, since
S
and its closure
\overline{S}
have the same self-adjoint extensions in
{\mathcal{H}}
, we will without loss of generality assume that
S
is closed in the remainder of this paper.

Hypothesis 2.1
.

Assume that
S
is a densely defined, closed, nonnegative operator in
{\mathcal{H}}
with deficiency indices
(r,r)
,
r\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\infty\}
.

The following is a fundamental result to be found in M. Krein’s celebrated 1947 paper

[
65
]
(see also the English summary on pages 491–495):

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume Hypothesis
2.1
. Then, among all non-negative self-adjoint
extensions of
S
, there exist two distinguished ones,
S_{K}
and
S_{F}
, which are, respectively, the smallest and largest

(
in the sense of order between linear operators, cf. (
2.4
)
)
such extensions. Furthermore, any non-negative self-adjoint extension
\widetilde{S}
of
S
necessarily satisfies

S_{K}\leq\widetilde{S}\leq S_{F}.

(2.7)

In particular, (
2.7
) determines
S_{K}
and
S_{F}
uniquely.

In addition, if
S\geq\varepsilon I_{{\mathcal{H}}}
for some
\varepsilon>0
, one has

S_{F}\geq\varepsilon I_{{\mathcal{H}}}
, and

\displaystyle\dom(S_{F})

\displaystyle=\dom(S)\overset{\textbf{\Large.}}{+}(S_{F})^{-1}\ker(S^{*}),

(2.8)

\displaystyle\dom(S_{K})

\displaystyle=\dom(S)\overset{\textbf{\Large.}}{+}\ker(S^{*}),

(2.9)

\displaystyle\dom(S^{*})

\displaystyle=\dom(S)\overset{\textbf{\Large.}}{+}(S_{F})^{-1}\ker(S^{*})\overset{\textbf{\Large.}}{+}\ker(S^{*})

\displaystyle=\dom(S_{F})\overset{\textbf{\Large.}}{+}\ker(S^{*}),

(2.10)

in particular,

\ker(S_{K})=\ker\big((S_{K})^{1/2}\big)=\ker(S^{*})=\ran(S)^{\bot}.

(2.11)

Here the operator inequalities in (
2.7
) are understood in the sense of (
2.3

(S_{F}+aI_{{\mathcal{H}}})^{-1}\leq\big(\widetilde{S}+aI_{{\mathcal{H}}}\big)^{-1}\leq(S_{K}+aI_{{\mathcal{H}}})^{-1}\,\text{ for all $a>0$.}

(2.12)

of

[

An intrinsic description of the Friedrichs extension
S_{F}
of
S\geq 0

[

S_{F}:\dom(S_{F})\subset{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle S_{F}u=S^{*}u,

\displaystyle u\in\dom(S_{F})=\big\{v\in\dom(S^{*})\,\big|\,\mbox{there exists}\,\{v_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\dom(S),

(2.13)

\displaystyle\quad\mbox{with}\,\lim_{j\to\infty}\|v_{j}-v\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}=0\mbox{ and }((v_{j}-v_{k}),S(v_{j}-v_{k}))_{\mathcal{H}}\to 0\mbox{ as }j,k\to\infty\big\}.

is non-negative,

\displaystyle S_{F}\geq 0,\quad S\subseteq S_{F}=S_{F}^{*}\subseteq S^{*},

(2.14)

\displaystyle\dom\big((S_{F})^{1/2}\big)=\big\{v\in{\mathcal{H}}\,\big|\,\mbox{there exists}\,\{v_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\dom(S),

(2.15)

\displaystyle\quad\mbox{with}\lim_{j\to\infty}\|v_{j}-v\|_{{\mathcal{H}}}=0\mbox{ and }((v_{j}-v_{k}),S(v_{j}-v_{k}))_{\mathcal{H}}\to 0\mbox{ as }j,k\to\infty\big\},

\displaystyle Q_{S}(f,g)=(f,S_{F}g)_{{\mathcal{H}}},\quad f\in\dom(Q_{S}),\,g\in\dom(S_{F}),

(2.18)

\displaystyle\dom(Q_{S})=\dom\big(S_{F}^{1/2}\big).

(2.19)

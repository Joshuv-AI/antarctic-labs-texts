Lifting Polynomial Representations of SL 2 ⁡ ( p r ) \operatorname{SL}_{2}(p^{r}) from 𝔽 p \mathbb{F}_{p} to ℤ / p s ​ ℤ \mathbb{Z}/p^{s}\mathbb{Z}
Chris Parker, Martin van Beek

Abstract
. We describe all of the irreducible polynomial 𝔽 p ​ SL 2 ⁡ ( p r ) \mathbb{F}_{p}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(p^{r}) representations which lift to ( ℤ / p s ​ ℤ ) ​ SL 2 ⁡ ( p r ) (\mathbb{Z}/p^{s}\mathbb{Z})\operatorname{SL}_{2}(p^{r}) representations for s > 1 s>1 , observing that they almost never do. We also show that two related indecomposable 𝔽 p ​ SL 2 ⁡ ( p r ) \mathbb{F}_{p}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(p^{r}) representations cannot be lifted to ℤ / p s ​ ℤ \mathbb{Z}/p^{s}\mathbb{Z} representations for s > 1 s>1 .

1.
Introduction

The lifting problem (see
Definition

2.2
) for modular representations of finite groups has been considered by different authors in a variety of settings over many years, and we mention only a small selection of this research here. The theory has its origin in the work of Brauer. For a historical overview of the problem, we refer to Benson’s text
[
1
, 3.7]
.

Throughout, we let
p
be a prime,
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}
be the field of
p^{r}
elements,
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
the
p
-adics and
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
the
p
-adic integers. Green
[
9
]
gave a sufficient homological condition for an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-representation to lift to a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-representation. In particular, this demonstrates that the inability of an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-representation to lift gives information about certain
\mathrm{Ext}
groups of the module associated to the representation. Work of Tiep–Zalesski
[
17
, Theorem 4.5]
revealed conditions for
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-representations of groups of Lie type in characteristic
p
to lift to representations over
\mathbb{Z}/p^{s}\mathbb{Z}
. In this direction, our contribution is a finer analysis of when the homogeneous polynomial representations
V_{i}(p^{r})
,
1\leq i\leq p
, of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(p^{r})
lift to
\mathbb{Z}/p^{s}\mathbb{Z}
for
s\geqslant 2
, extending the work in
[
17
, Theorem 4.5]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
p
is a prime,

,
G=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(p^{r})
and

is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
-module. If, for some

, then

lifts to
\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z}
if and only if

and either

(1)

p=2
and

; or

(2)

p

.

Moreover, if

lifts to
\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z}

\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

We recall that

\mathbb{Q}_{p}G
-module

\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-lattice

as

A subgroup

\mathbb{Z}/p^{s}\mathbb{Z}

. Our theorem, along with that of
[

[

\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{s}\mathbb{Z})

Set

and assume that
W
is the natural
\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z}
-module for
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})
. If

as an

With

is as follows: Recognizing

as an
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(p^{r})

2.
Preliminaries

We are concerned with the question of when modules defined over
\mathbb{F}_{p}

lift
to
\mathbb{Z}/p^{s}\mathbb{Z}
for various
s\in\mathbb{N}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
\theta:G\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{n}(p)
a representation of
G
. Then
(G,\theta)
is
liftable
to
\mathbb{Z}/p^{s}\mathbb{Z}
if there is
\widehat{\theta}:G\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{s}\mathbb{Z})
such that with
\pi_{s}:\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{s}\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{n}(p)
the natural projection, we have
\theta=\widehat{\theta}\circ\pi_{s}
.

Identifying
H
with the image of
\theta
, we can reformulate
Definition

2.1
as follows.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
V
a faithful
n
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
-module and identify
G\leq\mathrm{GL}_{n}(p)
. Then
(G,V)
is
liftable
to
\mathbb{Z}/p^{s}\mathbb{Z}
if there is
H\leq\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{s}\mathbb{Z})
with
H
mapping isomorphically to
G
under the natural projection
\pi_{s}
.

A further formulation is that there is an
n
-dimensional
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{s}\mathbb{Z})G
-module
W
which is free as a
\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z}
-module (so a direct product of
n
copies of
C_{p^{s}}
on which
G
acts) such that
W/pW
is isomorphic to
V
as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
-module. We will generally suppress the group
G
and simply say that
V
lifts.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group, and
W
a
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})G
-module with
W
free as a
\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z}
-module. Then
pW\cong W/pW
as
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
-modules.

Proof.

We have that
pW
and
W/pW
are elementary abelian
p
-groups. Set
\phi:W\to pW
to be the
p
th-power map on
W
. Then
\phi
commutes with the
G
-action on
W
, and so
\phi
is a
G
-map with kernel
pW
. Hence,
W/pW\cong pW
as
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
-modules.
∎

We record a result of Green which reiterates our comments from the introduction and provides a characterization of when modules can lift.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
9
, Theorem 1]

Let
G
be a finite group and
V
an
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
-module such that
\mathrm{Ext}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}(V,V)=0
. Then
V
lifts to a
\mathbb{Q}_{p}G
-module.

We remark that the condition
\mathrm{Ext}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}(V,V)=0
is equivalent to the requirement that every extension of
G
by
V\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}V^{*}
splits as a semidirect product
[
1
, Proposition 3.1.8, Proposition 3.7.3]
.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
V
a finitely generated, projective
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
-module. Then
V
lifts to a
\mathbb{Q}_{p}G
-module.

Proof.

Since
V
is projective and finitely generated, so too is
V\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}V^{*}
. Hence, by
[
1
, Proposition 3.1.8]
we see that
\mathrm{Ext}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}(V,V)=\mathrm{H}^{2}(G,V\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}V^{*})=0
, as required.
∎

Another consequence of

Let

,

and

-module. Assume that

-module if and only if

Of course, if an

Recall that a
Pascal matrix

a
is a lower triangular matrix which encodes the binomial coefficients. That is, whenever
i\geqslant j
the
(i,j)
th entry
a_{i,j}
of
a
is
\binom{i-1}{j-1}
and whenever
i<j
then
a_{i,j}=0
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
a_{0}=a-I
where
a
is an
n\times n
Pascal matrix. Then, for
\ell\geqslant 1
, the following hold:

(1)

(a_{0}^{\ell})_{\ell+1,1}=\ell!
and
(a_{0}^{\ell})_{j,1}=0
for all
j\leqslant\ell
.

(2)

(a_{0}^{\ell})_{\ell+2,2}=(\ell+1)!
and
(a_{0}^{\ell-1})_{j,2}=0
for all
j\leqslant\ell+1
.

(3)

(a_{0}^{\ell})_{\ell+2,1}=\binom{\ell+1}{\ell-1}\ell!
and
(a_{0}^{\ell-1})_{j,2}=0
for all
j<\ell+1
.

Proof.

The result in (
1
) is true for
\ell=1
by definition. It is also easy to see that the entries
(a_{0}^{\ell})_{j,1}=0
for
j\leqslant\ell
. In particular, if
n\leqslant l
then
(a_{0})^{l}=0_{n}
. To complete the proof, we assume that
l<n
. Write
a_{0}^{\ell}=a_{0}(a_{0}^{\ell-1})
and apply induction to see that

(a_{0}^{\ell})_{\ell+1,1}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(a_{0})_{\ell+1,i}(a_{0}^{\ell-1})_{i,1}=(a_{0})_{\ell+1,\ell}(a_{0}^{\ell-1})_{\ell,1}=\binom{\ell}{\ell-1}(\ell-1)!=\ell!

as claimed in (

).

The inductive steps for parts (
2
) and (
3
) are

(a_{0}^{\ell})_{\ell+2,2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(a_{0})_{\ell+2,i}(a_{0}^{\ell-1})_{i,2}=(a_{0})_{\ell+2,\ell+1}(a_{0}^{\ell-1})_{\ell+1,2}=\binom{\ell+1}{\ell}\ell!=(\ell+1)!

and

\displaystyle(a_{0}^{\ell})_{\ell+2,1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(a_{0})_{\ell+2,i}(a_{0}^{\ell-1})_{i,1}=(a_{0})_{\ell+2,\ell}(a_{0}^{\ell-1})_{\ell,1}+(a_{0})_{\ell+2,\ell+1}(a_{0}^{\ell-1})_{\ell+1,1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\binom{\ell+1}{\ell-1}(\ell-1)!+\binom{\ell+1}{\ell}\binom{\ell}{\ell-2}(l-1)!=\binom{\ell+1}{\ell-1}\ell!.

∎

3.
The structure of
V_{n}(p^{r})
and
\Lambda(p^{r})

Let
p
be a prime and
r\in\mathbb{N}
. Set
A=\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}[x,y]
to be the ring of polynomials in two commuting indeterminates
x,y
with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}
. Make
A
into an
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
-module as follows: for
g=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
define
x\cdot g=(ax+by)
and
y\cdot g=(cx+dy)
. Observe that the action of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
on
A
maps polynomials of degree
n
to polynomials of degree
n
.

For
n\geqslant 1
let
V_{n}(p^{r})\leq A
be the set of homogeneous polynomials of degree
n
. Since the action of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
on
A
preserves degree,
V_{n}(p^{r})
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
-submodule of
A
of dimension
n+1
. Provided
0\leqslant n\leqslant p-1
, the modules
V_{n}(p^{r})
are irreducible self-dual
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
-modules
[
3
, p588]
. They are important because of the Steinberg Tensor Product Theorem
[
6
, Corollary 2.8.6]
which asserts that each irreducible
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
-module may be written as

U_{0}\otimes U_{1}^{\sigma}\otimes\dots\otimes U_{a-1}^{\sigma^{a-1}}

where
U_{i}\cong V_{j}(p^{r})
for some
j\in\{0,\dots,p-1\}
and
\sigma
is the Frobenius automorphism which maps
\lambda\mapsto\lambda^{p}
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}
. As such, the Frobenius twists of these modules provide a complete list of the so-called
basic

\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
-modules (see
[
6
, Example 2.8.10a]
). We will often view
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
-modules as
\mathbb{F}_{p}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
-modules. That is, we recognize that an
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
-module is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space with an action of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
. The collection of basic
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
-modules, viewed instead as
\mathbb{F}_{p}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
-modules, are what we call
irreducible polynomial
modules.

We observe that when
p^{r}>2
,
V_{p}(p^{r})
is indecomposable but not irreducible. Indeed,
V_{p}(p^{r})
has a
2
-dimensional submodule generated by
x^{p}
and
y^{p}
(see
[
8
, Lemma 2.1]
). Denoting this submodule by
U
, we have that the restriction of
U
to
\mathbb{F}_{p}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
is isomorphic to the restriction of
V_{1}(p^{r})
to
\mathbb{F}_{p}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
; while the restriction of
V_{p}(p^{r})/U
to
\mathbb{F}_{p}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
is isomorphic to the restriction of
V_{p-2}(p^{r})
to
\mathbb{F}_{p}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
.

We will also be interested in the dual module of
V_{p}(p^{r})
, which we denote by
\Lambda(p^{r})
, since this module plays a role in a current project of the authors. Indeed, the Sylow
p
-subgroup of the semidirect product of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
by
\Lambda(p^{r})
supports exotic fusion systems
[
8
]
. By a dualization argument, this module contains a
(p-1)
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
-module whose restriction to
\mathbb{F}_{p}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
is isomorphic to the restriction of
V_{p-2}(p^{r})
to
\mathbb{F}_{p}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
; while the restriction to
\mathbb{F}_{p}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
of the quotient of
\Lambda(p^{r})
by this submodule is isomorphic to the restriction of
V_{1}(p^{r})
to
\mathbb{F}_{p}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
. If
p^{r}=2
, we observe that
\Lambda(2)\cong V_{2}(2)\cong V_{1}(2)\oplus V_{0}(2)
, where
V_{0}(2)
is the trivial module for
\mathbb{F}_{2}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(2)
.

Throughout the remainder of this article, we will identify
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}})
with
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(p^{r})=:G
and view
V_{n}(p^{r})
and
\Lambda(p^{r})
as
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
-modules.

4.
Lifting
V_{n}(p^{r})
and
\Lambda(p^{r})
to representations over
\mathbb{Z}/p^{s}Z

In this section, we demonstrate that for
1\leqslant n\leqslant p
and

the modules

lift to
\mathbb{Z}/p^{s}\mathbb{Z}(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(p^{r}))
-representations if and only if
p^{r}=2
; or

,
p
is odd and

. We begin with a classical result of Higman which deals with one case when

Theorem 4.1
.

For any

, we have that
V_{1}(2^{r})
lifts to
\mathbb{Z}/2^{s}\mathbb{Z}
if and only if

. In particular,
V_{1}(2)

\mathbb{Q}_{2}

Proof.

The first statement is
[

, Theorem 8.2]

V_{1}(2)
is projective so that
V_{1}(2)
extends to a
\mathbb{Q}_{2}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(2)
-representation by

.
∎

Set
G:=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(p^{r})
and assume that
W
is a free
\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z}
-module of rank
r(n+1)
equipped with a
G
-action such that, as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
-module,
W/pW\cong V_{n}(p^{r})
where
1\leqslant n\leqslant p
. Let
\mathrm{M}_{(n+1)r}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})
denote the algebra of
(n+1)r\times(n+1)r
matrices with entries from
\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z}
and identify
\mathrm{Aut}(W)
with
\mathrm{GL}_{(n+1)r}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})
: the subset of
\mathrm{M}_{(n+1)r}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})
whose elements have invertible determinant. Let

be the identity

the identity
(n+1)r\times(n+1)r
matrix and

the zero

matrix. Let

be the image of

in
\mathrm{Aut}(W)
so that

if

Let
\alpha:=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\end{pmatrix}\in G
and
\gamma:=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&0\\

. Then
\alpha^{p}=[\alpha,\alpha^{\gamma}]=1
. For

\alpha

A study on ℱ \mathscr{F} -simultaneous approximative τ \tau -compactness property in Banach spaces
Syamantak Das, Tanmoy Paul

Abstract
. Veselý (1997) studied Banach spaces that admit f f -centers for finite subsets of the space. In this work, we introduce the concept of ℱ \mathscr{F} -simultaneous approximative τ \tau -compactness property ( τ \tau - ℱ \mathscr{F} -SACP in short) for triplets ( X , V , 𝔉 ) (X,V,\mathfrak{F}) , where X X is a Banach space, V V is a τ \tau -closed subset of X X , 𝔉 \mathfrak{F} is a subfamily of closed and bounded subsets of X X , ℱ \mathscr{F} is a collection of functions, and τ \tau is the norm or weak topology on X X . We characterize reflexive spaces with the Kadec-Klee property using triplets with τ \tau - ℱ \mathscr{F} -SACP. We investigate the relationship between τ \tau - ℱ \mathscr{F} -SACP and the continuity properties of the restricted f f -center map. The study further examines τ \tau - ℱ \mathscr{F} -SACP in the context of C ​ L ​ U ​ R CLUR spaces and explores various characterizations of τ \tau - ℱ \mathscr{F} -SACP, including connections to reflexivity, Fréchet smoothness, and the Kadec-Klee property.

for all

. Now, by the lower semicontinuity of the norm, we have
\|v_{0}-x_{i}\|\leq\mu_{i}
for all

. From

Thus

.

Claim:
There exists
i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
such that
\|v_{0}-x_{i}\|=\mu_{i}
.

If not, suppose that
\|v_{0}-x_{i}\|<\mu_{i}
for all

. Using the weakly
strictly monotonicity of
f
and the continuity of

on bounded subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{\geq 0}
, we have

\displaystyle f(\|v_{0}-x_{1}\|,\cdots,\|v_{0}-x_{N}\|)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle f(\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{N})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{k}f(\|v_{n_{k}}-x_{1}\|,\cdots,\|v_{n_{k}}-x_{N}\|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textrm{rad}_{V}^{f}(F).

This contradiction establishes our claim. Also, as

, by the Kadec-Klee property of

, we have
v_{n_{k}}\rightarrow v_{0}
. This establishes our assertion.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose
X
is Fréchet smooth. Then for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
and monotone smooth norm
\gamma:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
,
\ell_{\gamma}^{N}(X)
is Fréchet smooth.

Proof.

We prove the case for
N=2
, since the proof for other values of
N
follows similarly. Let
\gamma:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a monotone smooth norm, and
\gamma^{\circ}
be the dual norm of
\gamma
. Let
(x_{1},x_{2})\in S_{\ell_{\gamma}^{2}(X)}
, and
((f_{n}^{1},f_{n}^{2}))\subseteq S_{\ell_{\gamma^{\circ}}^{2}(X^{*})}
be such that
f_{n}^{1}(x_{1})+f_{n}^{2}(x_{2})\rightarrow 1
. Let
(f_{1},f_{2})\in S_{\ell_{\gamma^{\circ}}^{2}(X^{*})}
be such that
f_{1}(x_{1})+f_{2}(x_{2})=1
. Then
1=f_{1}(x_{1})+f_{2}(x_{2})\leq\|f_{1}\|\|x_{1}\|+\|f_{2}\|\|x_{2}\|\leq\gamma^{\circ}(\|f_{1}\|,\|f_{2}\|)\gamma(\|x_{1}\|,\|x_{2}\|)=1
. Thus
\|f_{1}\|\|x_{1}\|+\|f_{2}\|\|x_{2}\|=1
. Since
\gamma
is a smooth norm,
J_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}((\|x_{1}\|,\|x_{2}\|))=\{(\|f_{1}\|,\|f_{2}\|)\}
.

Claim:

(\|f_{n}^{1}\|),(\|f_{n}^{2}\|)
converges to
\|f_{1}\|
and
\|f_{2}\|
respectively.

Let
(\|f_{n_{k}}^{1}\|)
be a subsequence of
(\|f_{n}^{1}\|)
. Since
(\|f_{n_{k}}^{1}\|)
is bounded, it has a convergent subsequence, also denoted by
(\|f_{n_{k}}^{1}\|)
, converging to some
\alpha_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
. Similarly,
(\|f_{n_{k}}^{2}\|)
has a convergent subsequence, also denoted by
(\|f_{n_{k}}^{2}\|)
, converging to some
\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
. Now
\alpha_{1}\|x_{1}\|+\alpha_{2}\|x_{2}\|=\underset{k}{\lim}(\|f_{n_{k}}^{1}\|\|x_{1}\|+\|f_{n_{k}}^{2}\|\|x_{2}\|)=1
. Since
\gamma
is a smooth norm on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we have
\alpha_{1}=\|f_{1}\|
and
\alpha_{2}=\|f_{2}\|
. Thus every subsequence of
(\|f_{n}^{1}\|)
has a further subsequence converging to
\|f_{1}\|
and hence
(\|f_{n}^{1}\|)
is convergent to
\|f_{1}\|
. Proceeding in the same way, we can show that
(\|f_{n}^{2}\|)
is convergent to
\|f_{2}\|
.

Case 1:
Suppose
x_{1},x_{2}\neq 0
. Then

\displaystyle 1=\underset{n}{\lim}(f_{n}^{1}(x_{1})+f_{n}^{2}(x_{2}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\underset{n}{\lim\inf}(\|f_{n}^{1}\|\|x_{1}\|+\|f_{n}^{2}\|\|x_{2}\|)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\underset{n}{\lim\sup}(\|f_{n}^{1}\|\|x_{1}\|+\|f_{n}^{2}\|\|x_{2}\|)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\underset{n}{\lim\sup}\gamma^{\circ}(\|f_{n}^{1}\|,\|f_{n}^{2}\|)\gamma(\|x_{1}\|,\|x_{2}\|)=1.

Hence
\underset{n}{\lim}(f_{n}^{1}(x_{1})+f_{n}^{2}(x_{2}))=\underset{n}{\lim}(\|f_{n}^{1}\|\|x_{1}\|+\|f_{n}^{2}\|\|x_{2}\|)=1
. Now it is easy to see that
\underset{n}{\lim}(\|f_{n}^{1}\|\|x_{1}\|-f_{n}^{1}(x_{1}))=0
and
\underset{n}{\lim}(\|f_{n}^{2}\|\|x_{2}\|-f_{n}^{2}(x_{2}))=0
. Hence
\frac{f_{n}^{1}}{\|f_{n}^{1}\|}(\frac{x_{1}}{\|x_{1}\|})\rightarrow 1
and
\frac{f_{n}^{2}}{\|f_{n}^{2}\|}(\frac{x_{2}}{\|x_{2}\|})\rightarrow 1
. Now, due to the Fréchet differentiability of
X
, we have
(\frac{f_{n}^{1}}{\|f_{n}^{1}\|})
and
(\frac{f_{n}^{2}}{\|f_{n}^{2}\|})
are convergent.

Hence
(f_{n}^{1})
and
(f_{n}^{2})
are convergent, and thus
((f_{n}^{1},f_{n}^{2}))
is convergent.

Case 2:
Suppose
x_{1}\neq 0
and
x_{2}=0
. Then we have
1=\|f_{1}\|\|x_{1}\|\leq\gamma^{\circ}(\|f_{1}\|,0)\gamma(\|x_{1}\|,\|x_{2}\|)=\gamma^{\circ}(\|f_{1}\|,0)\leq\gamma^{\circ}(\|f_{1}\|,\|f_{2}\|)=1
. Thus
\gamma^{\circ}(\|f_{1}\|,0)=1
and as
J_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}((\|x_{1}\|,\|x_{2}\|))=\{(\|f_{1}\|,\|f_{2}\|)\}
, we have
f_{2}=0
. Hence
(f_{n}^{2})
converges to 0. Now proceeding in a similar way, as in Case 1, we can show that
(f_{n}^{1})
is convergent. Thus
((f_{n}^{1},f_{n}^{2}))
is convergent.

This concludes our assertion.

\gamma^{\circ}

\gamma

\displaystyle 1=f_{1}(z_{1})+f_{2}(z_{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f_{1}\|\|z_{1}\|+\|f_{2}\|\|z_{2}\|

\displaystyle\gamma^{\circ}(\|f_{1}\|,\|f_{2}\|)\gamma(\|z_{1}\|,\|z_{2}\|)=1.

(\|f_{1}\|,\|f_{2}\|)

(\|z_{1}\|,\|z_{2}\|)

Claim:

(\|x_{n}\|),(\|y_{n}\|)

\|z_{1}\|
and
\|z_{2}\|

(\|x_{n_{k}}\|)

(\|x_{n}\|)

\|f_{1}\|\alpha+\|f_{2}\|\beta=\underset{k}{\lim}(\|f_{1}\|\|x_{n_{k}}\|+\|f_{2}\|\|y_{n_{k}}\|)=1
. Now by the strict convexity of the norm

and
\beta=\|z_{2}\|
. Thus
(\|x_{n}\|)
is convergent to
\|z_{1}\|
and
(\|y_{n}\|)
is convergent to
\|z_{2}\|
.

Case 1:

\displaystyle 1=\underset{n}{\lim}(f_{1}(x_{n})+f_{2}(y_{n}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\underset{n}{\lim\inf}(\|f_{1}\|\|x_{n}\|+\|f_{2}\|\|y_{n}\|)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\underset{n}{\lim\sup}(\|f_{1}\|\|x_{n}\|+\|f_{2}\|\|y_{n}\|)

\displaystyle\underset{n}{\lim\sup}\gamma^{\circ}(\|f_{1}\|,\|f_{2}\|)\gamma(\|x_{n}\|,\|y_{n}\|)\leq 1.

Hence
\underset{n}{\lim}(\|f_{1}\|\|x_{n}\|+\|f_{2}\|\|y_{n}\|)=\underset{n}{\lim}(f_{1}(x_{n})+f_{2}(y_{n}))=1
. Now it is easy to see that
\underset{n}{\lim}(\|f_{1}\|\|x_{n}\|-f_{1}(x_{n}))=0
and
\underset{n}{\lim}(\|f_{2}\|\|y_{n}\|-f_{2}(y_{n}))=0
. Thus
\frac{f_{1}}{\|f_{1}\|}(\frac{x_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|})\rightarrow 1
and
\frac{f_{2}}{\|f_{2}\|}(\frac{y_{n}}{\|y_{n}\|})\rightarrow 1

,

\tau
-convergent in

f_{2}=0

(\|f_{1}\|,0)
attains its norm on
(\|x_{1}\|,0)
and
\gamma
is strictly convex, we have
\|x_{2}\|=0
and thus
x_{2}=0
. Hence
(y_{n})
converges to 0. Also, in a similar way, as in Case 1,
(x_{n})
is
\tau
-convergent. This concludes our assertion.

4.
Continuity properties of some set-valued maps in relation to
\tau
-
\mathscr{F}
-SACP

Throughout this section, by
B[x,r]
we denote the closed ball centered at
x
and radius
r
. In the following propositions, we present some basic observations.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
F=\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{N}\}\in\mathcal{F}(X)
and
f:\mathbb{R}^{N}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a convex monotone function. Then, the map
r_{f}(.,F):X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is Lipschitz continuous on bounded subsets of
X
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
Y
be a subspace of
X
,
F=\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{N}\}\in\mathcal{F}(X)
,
f:\mathbb{R}^{N}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a monotone function, and
\varepsilon>0
. Then,

(a)

\emph{rad}_{Y}^{f}(F)=\inf\{r_{f}(y,F):y\in\cup_{i=1}^{N}B[x_{i},\emph{rad}_{Y}(F)+\varepsilon]\cap Y\}=\inf\{r_{f}(y,F):y\in Y\penalty\ \textrm{and}\penalty\ \|y\|\leq\underset{1\leq i\leq N}{\max}\|x_{i}\|+\emph{rad}_{Y}(F)+\varepsilon\}
.

(b)

\emph{Cent}_{Y}^{f}(F)\subseteq\cup_{i=1}^{N}B[x_{i},\emph{rad}_{Y}(F)+\varepsilon]\cap Y
if
f
is weakly strictly monotone.

(c)

\emph{Cent}_{Y}^{f}(F)\subseteq\cap_{i=1}^{N}B[x_{i},\emph{rad}_{Y}(F)+diam(F)+\varepsilon]\cap Y
if
f
is weakly strictly monotone.

Proof.

(a)
. Let
y\in Y\setminus(\cup_{i=1}^{N}B[x_{i},\textrm{rad}_{Y}(F)+\varepsilon]\cap Y)
. Then,
\|y-x_{i}\|>\textrm{rad}_{Y}(F)+\varepsilon
for all
i=1,\cdots,N
. We select
y_{0}\in Y
such that
\underset{1\leq i\leq N}{\max}\|y_{0}-x_{i}\|\leq\textrm{rad}_{Y}(F)+\varepsilon
. Then, we have
\|y_{0}-x_{i}\|\leq\textrm{rad}_{Y}(F)+\varepsilon<\|y-x_{i}\|
for all
i=1,\cdots,N
. As
f
is monotone,
r_{f}(y_{0},F)\leq r_{f}(y,F)
, and hence this establishes our first equality in
(a)
.

Let
y\in\cup_{i=1}^{N}B[x_{i},\textrm{rad}_{Y}(F)+\varepsilon]\cap Y
. Then, there exists
j\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
such that
\|y-x_{j}\|\leq\textrm{rad}_{Y}(F)+\varepsilon
. Thus, we have

\|y\|\leq\|y-x_{j}\|+\|x_{j}\|\leq\textrm{rad}_{Y}(F)+\underset{1\leq i\leq N}{\max}\|x_{i}\|+\varepsilon.

This establishes our second equality in
(a)
.

(b)
. Let
y\in Y\setminus(\cup_{i=1}^{N}B[x_{i},\textrm{rad}_{Y}(F)+\varepsilon]\cap Y)
. Then, as shown in the proof of
(a)
, we obtain a
y_{0}\in Y
such that
\|y_{0}-x_{i}\|\leq\textrm{rad}_{Y}(F)+\varepsilon<\|y-x_{i}\|
for all
i=1,\cdots,N
. As
f
is weakly strictly monotone, we have
r_{f}(y_{0},F)<r_{f}(y,F)
. Thus, our assertion is established.

(c)
. Let
y\in\textrm{Cent}_{Y}^{f}(F)
. Then, by
(b)
, there exists
j\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
such that
\|y-x_{j}\|\leq\textrm{rad}_{Y}(F)+\varepsilon
. Then, for all
i=1,\cdots,N
, we have

\|y-x_{i}\|\leq\|y-x_{j}\|+\|x_{j}-x_{i}\|\leq\textrm{rad}_{Y}(F)+diam(F)+\varepsilon.

This establishes our claim.
∎

Let
\mathcal{F}_{N}(X)

We define a metric

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
Y
be a subspace of
X
and
f:\mathbb{R}^{N}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a convex, monotone function. Then, the map
\emph{rad}_{Y}^{f}:(\mathcal{F}_{N}(X),d)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

\mathcal{F}_{N}(X)

Proof.

Let
B
be a closed and bounded subset of
\mathcal{F}_{N}(X)
. Now there exists
M>0

d(F,F^{\prime})\leq M

\underset{1\leq i\leq N}{\max}\|z_{i}\|\leq R

Claim:

\textrm{rad}_{Y}^{f}

Let
F=\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{N}\},F^{\prime}=\{x_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,x_{N}^{\prime}\}\in B
,
Now,
\underset{1\leq i\leq N}{\max}\|x_{i}\|+\textrm{rad}_{Y}(F)\leq 2\underset{1\leq i\leq N}{\max}\|x_{i}\|\leq 2\underset{1\leq i\leq N}{\max}\|x_{i}-z_{i}\|+2\underset{1\leq i\leq N}{\max}\|z_{i}\|\leq 2M+2R

4.2
(a)

(1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}|f(\|y-x_{1}\|,\cdots,\|y-x_{N}\|)-f(\|y-x_{1}^{\prime}\|,\cdots,\|y-x_{N}^{\prime}\|)|\\

\displaystyle\textrm{rad}_{Y}^{f}(F)

\displaystyle f(\|y-x_{1}\|,\cdots,\|y-x_{N}\|)

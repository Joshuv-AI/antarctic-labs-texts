The Scott space of lattice of closed subsets with supremum operator as a topological semilattice Note: Research supported by NSF of China (Nos. 12471439, 12231007).
Yu Chen, Hui Kou, Zhenchao Lyu, Weiyu Yang

Abstract
We present several equivalent conditions of the continuity of the supremum function from the square of the Scott space of C ⁡ ( X ) C(X) to itself under mild assumptions, where C ⁡ ( X ) C(X) denotes the lattice of closed subsets of a 𝐓 𝟎 \mathbf{T_{0}} topological space. We also show that a 𝐓 𝟎 \mathbf{T_{0}} space is quasicontinuous (quasialgebraic) iff the lattice of its closed subsets is a quasicontinuous (quasialgebraic) domain by using n n -approximation. Furthermore, we provide a necessary condition for when a topological space possesses a Scott completion. This allows us to give more examples which do not have Scott completions. Keywords : Monotone determined space, Quasicontinuous, D-completion, Scott topology, Lattice of closed subsets, Scott completion. Mathematics Subject Classification : 54A10, 54A20, 06B35.

Introduction

Topological semilattice are closely connected to the theory of continuous lattices in domain theory. For instance, Lawson showed that compact unital semilattices with small semilattices are exactly continuous lattices equipped with the Lawson topology
[
11
, Theorem VI-3.4]
. From the viewpoint of category topology, Banaschewski introduced the notion of an essential embedding within the category of
\mathbf{T_{0}}
spaces and continuous maps
[
2
]
, which has a close relation to Scott’s injective spaces. He also obtained a characterization of so-called essentially complete
\mathbf{T_{0}}
spaces in terms of join filters. Subsequently, Hoffmann provided a more transparent characterization: essentially complete
\mathbf{T_{0}}
spaces are precisely the sober spaces that form complete lattices under their specialization order, satisfying an additional condition relating the supremum operator to the topology
[
14
]
. Powerdomains play an important role in modeling the semantics of nondeterministic programming languages (see
[
1
, Chapter 6]
and
[
11
, IV-8]
). The Hoare powerdomain over a topological space can be realized as the set of its nonempty closed subsets ordered by inclusion.
In this paper we study a special kind of essentially complete
\mathbf{T_{0}}
space,
namely the Scott space of the lattice of closed subsets of a topological space with the supremum operator.

Directed spaces
[
24
]
constitute a special class of
\mathbf{T_{0}}
topological spaces which extend the concept of Scott spaces. Within
\mathbf{T_{0}}
spaces, the notion of directed spaces is equivalent both to monotone determined spaces (as established by Erné
[
8
]
) and to CRP-spaces (introduced by Battenfeld
[
3
, Definition 6.7]
).

\mathbf{T_{0}}
c-spaces (or
n
-continuous spaces) are all monotone determined spaces. Based on this, Feng and Kou
[
9
]
introduced another approximation relation on monotone determined spaces, called
d
-approximation, and defined the quasicontinuous monotone determined spaces. They established an equivalence between quasicontinuous spaces and locally hypercompact spaces. In domain theory, there is a close relationship between the continuity of a dcpo
L
and that of the lattice
\sigma(L)
of its Scott-open subsets. More surprisingly, the continuity of a dcpo
L
can be characterized by the continuity of the lattice
\Gamma(L)
of its Scott-closed subsets (see
[
4
, Theorem 3.17]
). Moreover, this correspondence extends to general
\mathbf{T_{0}}
spaces.
We show that a
\mathbf{T_{0}}
space
X
is quasicontinuous (quasialgebraic) iff the lattice of its closed subsets
C(X)
is quasicontinuous (quasialgebraic) by using
n
-approximation.

It is well known that for completely distributive (resp., distributive hypercontinuous) lattices
L
, the hull-kernel topology on the spectrum
\mathrm{Spec}L
coincides with the Scott topology
[
11
]
.
A longstanding open problem posed by Lawson and Mislove asks: for which distributive continuous lattices does the spectrum, equipped with the hull-kernel topology, form precisely a sober locally compact Scott space? (See
[
23
, Problem 528]
.) Chen, Kou, and Lyu
[
4
]
established a necessary condition for this problem:
\sigma(L^{\mathrm{op}})=v(L^{\mathrm{op}})
. Since
L^{\mathrm{op}}
corresponds to the lattice of closed subsets of a topological space, we investigate equivalent conditions for
\sigma(C(X))=v(C(X))
in general topological spaces
X
.
Surprisingly, the continuity of the supremum map
\sup\colon\Sigma C(X)\times\Sigma C(X)\to\Sigma C(X)
constitutes one such equivalent condition under mild assumptions. For monotone determined spaces, the additional assumption can be dropped. In addition, we provide an example of non-monotone determined space
X
such that
\eta=\lambda x.{\downarrow}x\colon X\rightarrow\Sigma C(X)
is continuous.

Monotone convergence spaces play a fundamental role in domain theory. Every
\mathbf{T_{0}}
space admits a free monotone convergence completion, known as its D-completion
[
16
]
. We show that D-completion of any monotone determined space is a Scott space. The concept of Scott completion, introduced by Zhang, Shi, and Li
[
25
]
, refers to a construction that slightly differs from D-completion. As a corollary, we establish that for any monotone determined space, its Scott completion coincides with its D-completion. The Scott completion of monotone determined spaces are shown to be very useful to construct free dcpo algebras over any general dcpo (see
[
6
,
7
]
).
But the notion does not seem to have been studied much. We provide a necessary condition for when a space has Scott completion. Based on this we give a simple proof of a previous result in
[
17
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Outline
. In Section 2, we recall some useful knowledge about monotone determined spaces. We discuss D-completion of monotone determined spaces and provide more examples of topological spaces which do not have Scott completions are presented in Section 3. In Section 4 the Scott space of the lattice of closed subsets of a topological space with the jointly continuous supremum operator is discussed. We characterize the quasicontinuity of lattice of closed subsets in Section 5.

D-completion and Scott completion

Monotone convergence spaces (a.k.a d-spaces) is a very important class of topological spaces in domain theory.
It is well known that every
\mathbf{T_{0}}
space has a D-completion.
Let us recall some results about D-completion for further discussions.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbf{T_{0}}
space. If
X
with the specialization order is a dcpo and every open set of

is Scott open in
(X,\leq)
, then we call

a monotone convergence space space.

A subset

of a poset

is called

if for every directed set
D
of

that possesses supremum
\bigvee D
,
\bigvee D
is included in

.
The d-closed sets form the closed sets for a topology, called the

[

, Section 5]
.
We denote the closure of

. If
\text{cl}_{d}(A)

, then we say that

is

in

. A map

Definition 3.8
.

[
16

An embedding

Lemma 3.9
.

[

Consider the following properties for a subset

[

Let

Remark 3.11

For any
\mathbf{T_{0}}

Proposition 3.12

Let

\mathbf{T_{0}}

Proof.

Let

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
X
be a monotone determined space and
X^{d}
be the D-completion of
X
. Then
X^{d}
is a Scott space, i.e. the topology on
X^{d}
equals to the Scott topology on
(X^{d},\leq)
.

Proof.

Since
X^{d}
is a monotone convergence space, we only need to show that every Scott open set of
(X^{d},\leq)
is open in
X^{d}
. For any Scott open set
\mathcal{U}
of
(X^{d},\leq)
and any closed set
A\in\mathcal{U}
, notice that

A\in X^{d}=\text{cl}_{d}\{{\downarrow}x:x\in X\}=\text{cl}_{d}\{{\downarrow}x:x\in A\}\cup\text{cl}_{d}\{{\downarrow}x:x\in X\backslash A\}.

Since
\{{\downarrow}x:x\in X\backslash A\}\subseteq\Diamond(X\backslash A)=\{F\in C(X):F\cap(X\backslash A)\neq\emptyset\}
and
\Diamond(X\backslash A)
is a upper set hence is a d-closed set in

\text{cl}_{d}\{{\downarrow}x:x\in X\backslash A\}\subseteq\Diamond(X\backslash A)
. Then we have that

Hence

is included in
\text{cl}_{d}\{{\downarrow}x:x\in A\}\subseteq X^{d}
. Because
\mathcal{U}
is d-open set of

and
\text{cl}_{d}\{{\downarrow}x:x\in A\}
is d-closed set, we obtain that
\mathcal{U}\cap\{{\downarrow}x:x\in A\}\neq\emptyset

The concept of Scott completion is introduced by Zhang, Shi and Li in

Definition 3.17
.

[
25

A Scott completion

of a space

Theorem 3.18
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbf{T_{0}}
space and
(\tilde{X},i)
be a Scott-completion of
X
, then
\mathcal{O}(X)\cong\mathcal{O}(\tilde{X})
. Hence
C(X)\cong C(\tilde{X})
.

Proof.

Let
[Y\rightarrow 2]
denote the set of continuous functions from
Y
to the Sierpinski space
2
with the pointwise order. It is well known that
\mathcal{O}(Y)\cong[Y\rightarrow 2]
. For any
f\in[X\rightarrow 2]
, there exists only one
\bar{f}\in[\tilde{X}\rightarrow 2]
such that
f=\tilde{f}\circ i
since
(\tilde{X},i)
is a Scott-completion of
X
. Hence we obtain a map
\theta\colon[X\rightarrow 2]\rightarrow[\tilde{X}\rightarrow 2]
as follows

\theta(f)=\bar{f}.

Clearly
\theta
is bijective.

\theta
is monotone. For any
f_{1},f_{2}\in[X\rightarrow 2]
with
f_{1}\leq f_{2}
. We need to show that
\bar{f_{1}}\leq\bar{f_{2}}
. Without loss of generality, we consider
y\in\bar{X}
with
\bar{f_{1}}(y)=1
, i.e.,
y\in\bar{f_{1}}^{-1}(1)
. We claim that
i(X)\cap{\downarrow}y\cap\bar{f_{1}}^{-1}(1)\neq\emptyset
.
Otherwise we have the indicator function
\mathbbm{1}_{U}
of
U=({\downarrow}y)^{c}\cap\bar{f_{1}}^{-1}(1)
with
\mathbbm{1}_{U}\circ i=\bar{f}_{1}\circ i=f
. It follows that
\mathbbm{1}_{U}=\bar{f}_{1}
. Then
U=\mathbbm{1}_{U}^{-1}(1)=\bar{f}^{-1}_{1}(1)
. This is impossible. Back to
i(X)\cap{\downarrow}y\cap\bar{f_{1}}^{-1}(1)\neq\emptyset
.
We have some
x\in X
with
i(x)\in{\downarrow}y\cap\bar{f_{1}}^{-1}(1)
. It means that
f_{1}(x)=\bar{f}_{1}(i(x))=1
. Since
f_{1}\leq f_{2}
, we obtain that
f_{2}(x)=1
. Then
\bar{f}_{2}(y)\geq\bar{f}_{2}(i(x))=f_{2}(x)=1
.

It is easy to see that
\theta
reflects order. Hence
\theta
is an order isomorphism. So we obtain that
\mathcal{O}(X)\cong\mathcal{O}(\tilde{X})
.
∎

(1)

Every

(2)

Proof.

(1) Assume that there is a

Consider the soberification map

\Sigma C(X)\times\Sigma C(X)\rightarrow\Sigma C(X)

It is well known that the spectrum with hull-kernel topology of a completely distributive lattice (resp. a distributive hypercontinuous lattice) is exactly a continuous (resp. quasicontinuous) dcpo endowed with the Scott topology

can be viewed as a lattice of closed subsets of a topological space, we continue to investigate the equivalent conditions of

Surprisingly the continuity of the supremum function

Given any poset

Given a topological space

\forall(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})\in\prod\limits^{n}X

s_{n}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})=\ \downarrow\hskip-2.0pt\!{\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}\}}.

Theorem 4.25

[

A space is an essentially complete

(ii)

(iii)

Theorem 4.26
.

For a topological space
X
, the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\sigma(C(X))=\upsilon(C(X))
;

(2)

is continuous for all

\bigcup\colon\Sigma C(X)\times\Sigma C(X)\rightarrow\Sigma C(X)

(1)\implies(3)

\Diamond U=\{A\in C(X):A\cap U\neq\emptyset\},U\in\mathcal{O}(X)

form a subbasis of the upper topology on

(3)\implies(2)

we obtain that

is continuous.

(2)\implies(1)
. For the completeness, we give the detailed proof here.
Assume that
s_{n}
is continuous for all

. Let
\mathcal{U}
be an open subset of
\Sigma(C(X))
and

. Without loss of generality, we assume

. Note that since
A=\bigcup\{\downarrow\hskip-2.0pt\!F:F\subseteq_{f}\!A\}
and

is a directed family in

, there exists a non-empty finite subset

of

such that
\downarrow\hskip-2.0pt\!F\in\mathcal{U}

. It follows that
(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\in s_{n}^{-1}(\mathcal{U})

, there exists a family of open subsets

such that
U_{1}\times U_{2}\times\cdots\times U_{n}
is open in
\prod\limits^{n}X
and

(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\in U_{1}\times U_{2}\times\cdots\times U_{n}\subseteq s_{n}^{-1}(\mathcal{U}).

Since
x_{k}\in A
for

A\in\Diamond U_{k}=\{B\in C(X):\ B\cap U_{k}\not=\emptyset\}

A\in\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^{n}\Diamond U_{k}\in\upsilon(C(X))
.
For any
B\in\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^{n}\Diamond U_{k}

for

(y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{n})\in s_{n}^{-1}(\mathcal{U})

\bigcup\limits_{k=1}^{n}\downarrow\hskip-2.0pty_{k}\in\mathcal{U}
. It follows that

, i.e.,
A\in\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^{n}\Diamond U_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{U}
.
∎

5
Quasicontinuity of the lattice of closed subsets

We introduce the concepts of quasicontinuous spaces and quasialgebraic spaces. Then we will summarize the correspondences among the continuity of a
\mathbf{T_{0}}
space, the continuity of the lattice of its open subsets, and the continuity of the lattice of its closed subsets.

Given any set
X
, we use
F\subseteq_{f}X
to denote that
F
is a finite subset of
X
. Given any two subsets
G,H\subseteq X
, we define
G\leq H
iff
\uparrow\hskip-2.0ptH\subseteq\uparrow\hskip-2.0ptG
. A family of finite sets is said to be directed if given
F_{1},F_{2}
in the family, there exists an
F
in the family such that
F_{1},F_{2}\leq F
. Let
X
be a
\mathbf{T_{0}}
topological space and
\mathcal{M}
be a directed family of finite subsets of
X
. Then
F_{\mathcal{M}}=\{A\subseteq X:\exists M\in\mathcal{M},\uparrow\hskip-2.0ptM\subseteq A\}
is a filter under the inclusion order. We say that
\mathcal{M}
converges to
x
if
F_{\mathcal{M}}
converges to
x
, i.e., for any open neighbourhood
U
of
x
, there exists some
A\in\mathcal{M}
such that
A\subseteq U
.

Definition 5.40
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbf{T_{0}}
space and
G,H\subseteq X
. We say that
G

n
-
approximates

H
, denoted by
G\ll_{n}H
, if for every net
(x_{j})_{J}
of
X
,
(x_{j})_{J}\to y
for some
y\in H
implies
(x_{j})_{J}\cap\uparrow\hskip-2.0ptG\neq\emptyset
. We write
G\ll_{n}x
for
G\ll_{n}\{x\}
.
G
is said to be
n
-compact if
G
is a finite set and
G\ll_{n}G
.

Lemma 5.41
.

For any
\mathbf{T_{0}}
space
X
, the following are equivalent: for all

,

(1)

G\ll_{n}H
;

(2)

H\subseteq(\uparrow\hskip-2.0ptG)^{\circ}

(\uparrow\hskip-2.0ptG)^{\circ}

\uparrow\hskip-2.0ptG

(2)\Rightarrow(1)

(1)\Rightarrow(2)

. Then, for any open set

X
,
\mathcal{O}(X)
is algebraic iff it is hyperalgebraic.

Lemma 5.46
.

[
4
, Lemma 2.3]

Let
X
be a topological space and
U,V\in\mathcal{O}(X)
.
U\prec V
iff
U\subseteq\uparrow\hskip-2.0ptF\subseteq V
for some
F\subseteq_{f}X
.

Theorem 5.47
.

[
8
, Proposition 7]

Let
X
be a monotone determined space.
\mathcal{O}(X)
is algebraic iff it is hyperalgebraic.

Remark 5.48
.

Lemma
5.47
does not always hold for general
\mathbf{T_{0}}
spaces. A simple example is
\mathbb{N}
, the set of natural numbers, endowed with the cofinite topology. Any open subset of it is compact, but not hypercompact.

By the above discussion, we conclude the relationships between the continuity of a
\mathbf{T_{0}}
space and the lattice of its open subsets as follows.

Theorem 5.49
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbf{T_{0}}
space.

(1)

X
is continuous (algebraic) iff
\mathcal{O}(X)
is a completely distributive lattice (completely distributive algebraic lattice).

(2)

X
is quasicontinuous iff the lattice
\mathcal{O}(X)
is a hypercontinuous lattice.

(3)

X
is quasialgebraic iff
\mathcal{O}(X)
is a hyperalgebraic lattice. Moreover, if
X
is a monotone determined space, then
X
is quasialgebraic iff
\mathcal{O}(X)
is an algebraic lattice.

Given any
\mathbf{T_{0}}
space
X
, denote
C(X)
the lattice of its closed subsets. We will study the relations between
X
and
C(X)
. We first recall some useful conditions for continuity and distributiveness of lattices.

Lemma 5.50
.

[
11
, Theorem I-3.16]

Let
L
be a complete lattice. The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

L
is completely distributive;

(2)

L^{\rm op}
is completely distributive;

(3)

L
is continuous and every element is the sup of co-primes.

Lemma 5.51
.

[
11
,
10
]

The following statements hold.

(1)

Let
L
be a distributive continuous (algebraic) lattice, then
L
is hypercontinuous (hyperalgebraic) iff
L^{\rm op}
is hypercontinuous (quasialgebraic).

(2)

A complete lattice
L
is a quasicontinuous (quasialgebraic) lattice iff
\omega(L)
is a continuous (algebraic) lattice.

Proposition 5.52
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbf{T_{0}}
space. If
C(X)
is a continuous lattice, then
X
is continuous.

Proof.

C(X)
is a completely distributive lattice by Lemma
5.50
since it has enough co-primes. It follows that
\mathcal{O}(X)
is completely distributive. Hence
X
is continuous by Theorem
5.49
(1).
∎

The above result can be generalized to quasicontinuity. Whether an analogous result holds for core-compactness, however, is still unknown.

Proposition 5.53
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbf{T_{0}}
space. If
C(X)
is a quasicontinuous lattice, then
X
is quasicontinuous.

Proof.

Let
C(X)
be a quasicontinuous domain. Then for any
x\in X
, there exists some
\{A_{1},\dots,A_{n}\}\ll\ \downarrow\hskip-2.0ptx
in
C(X)
.
Given any
a_{i}\in A_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq n)
, we have
\{\downarrow\hskip-2.0pta_{1},\dots,\downarrow\hskip-2.0pta_{n}\}\ll\ \downarrow\hskip-2.0ptx
.

Claim 1.

\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}\ll_{n}x
in
X
.

Given any net
(x_{i})_{I}\to x
,
x\in\overline{\bigcup_{i\in I}\{x_{i}\}}
. Then
\downarrow\hskip-2.0ptx\subseteq\ \overline{\bigcup_{i\in I}\{x_{i}\}}
. Let
\mathcal{F}=\{\bigcup_{i\in F}\!\downarrow\hskip-2.0ptx_{i}:F\subseteq_{f}I\}
. Then
\mathcal{F}
is directed and
\downarrow\hskip-2.0ptx\leq\bigvee^{\uparrow}\hskip-2.0pt\mathcal{F}=\overline{\bigcup_{i\in I}\{x_{i}\}}
in
C(X)
. There exist some
F\subseteq_{f}I
and
a_{j}
such that
\downarrow\hskip-2.0pta_{j}\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in F}\downarrow\hskip-2.0ptx_{i}
. Thus, there exists a
i\in F
such that
a_{j}\leq x_{i}
, i.e.,
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}\ll_{n}x
in
X
.

Claim 2.
For any
x
and any open neighbourhood
U
of
x
, there exists some
F\subseteq_{f}U
such that
x\in(\uparrow\hskip-2.0ptF)^{\circ}
.

Given any directed family
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{i}\}_{I}
of closed subsets of
X
such that
\downarrow\hskip-2.0pt\!x\leq\bigvee\mathcal{A}=\overline{\bigcup_{i\in I}A_{i}}
, then
U\cap(\bigcup_{i\in I}A_{i})\not=\emptyset
. There exists some
i\in I
such that
A_{i}\cap U\not=\emptyset
. Pick
b\in A_{i}\cap U
, then
\downarrow\hskip-2.0pt\!b\subseteq A_{i}
. We have
\{\downarrow\hskip-2.0pt\!b:b\in U\}\ll\ \downarrow\hskip-2.0pt\!x
in
C(X)
. Since
C(X)
is quasicontinuous, there exists a
\{B_{1},\dots,B_{m}\}\subseteq C(X)
such that
\{\downarrow\hskip-2.0ptb:b\in U\}\ll\{B_{1},\dots,B_{m}\}\ll\ \downarrow\hskip-2.0ptx
in
C(X)
. Then

\forall 1\leq i\leq m,\ \{\downarrow\hskip-2.0ptb:b\in U\}\ll B_{i}=\overline{\bigcup_{F\subseteq_{f}B_{i}}\downarrow\hskip-2.0ptF},

which implies that there exists some
F\subseteq_{f}B_{i}
and
b_{i}\in U
such that
{\downarrow\hskip-2.0pt}b_{i}\subseteq{\downarrow\hskip-2.0pt}F\subseteq B_{i}
. Thus,
b_{i}\in B_{i}\cap U
. It follows that
\{{\downarrow\hskip-2.0pt}b_{1},\dots,{\downarrow\hskip-2.0pt}b_{m}\}\ll\ {\downarrow\hskip-2.0pt}x
in
C(X)
. Then
\{b_{1},\dots,b_{n}\}\ll_{n}x
by Claim 1, i.e.,
x\in({\uparrow\hskip-2.0pt}\{b_{1},\dots,b_{n}\})^{\circ}
. ∎

Theorem 5.54
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbf{T_{0}}
space.

(1)

X
is quasicontinuous iff the lattice
C(X)
is a quasicontinuous lattice.

(2)

X
is quasialgebraic iff the lattice
C(X)
is a quasialgebraic lattice.

Proof.

(1) By Proposition
5.53
, if
C(X)
is quasicontinuous, then
X
is quasicontinuous. Conversely, let
X
be quasicontinuous. Then
\mathcal{O}(X)
is hypercontinuous by Theorem
5.49
. From Theorem

5.51
, we know that
C(X)
is quasicontinuous.

(2) Let
C(X)
be a quasialgebraic lattice. Then
\omega(C(X))=\upsilon(\mathcal{O}(X))
is algebraic by Lemma
5.51
and
\mathcal{O}(X)
is hypercontinuous by (2) and Theorem
5.49
. Then
\sigma(\mathcal{O}(X))=\upsilon(\mathcal{O}(X))
is algebraic and completely distributive. Thus,
\mathcal{O}(X)
is algebraic and then hyperalgebraic. Conversely, let
X
be a quasialgebraic space. Then
\mathcal{O}(X)
is distributive hyperalgebraic lattice.
C(X)
is a quasialgebraic lattice by Theorem
5.51
.
∎

Finally we collect some equivalent characterizations of quasicontinuous space as follows.

Corollary 5.55
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbf{T_{0}}
topological space. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{O}(X)
with the upper topology is corecompact;

(2)

(
[
19
, Theorem 2(2)]
)

\mathcal{O}(X)
with the upper topology is an injective space;

(3)

C(X)
is a quasicontinuous domain;

(4)

(
[
11
, Theorem VII-3.10]
)

C(X)
with Lawson topology is Hausdorff;

(5)

X
is a quasicontinuous space.

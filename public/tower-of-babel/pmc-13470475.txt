Deep learning-based cross-modal MR-CT registration for brain metastases radiotherapy with multi-scale feature refinement and brainstem guidance
Authors: Chengjian Xiao, Weixiang Lin, Feilong Tian, Ying Xiao, Bowen Shi, Minli Kang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAccurate multimodal deformable registration between magnetic resonance (MR) and computed tomography (CT) images is essential for precise target delineation in brain metastases radiotherapy. However, substantial modality discrepancies and complex anatomical deformations make robust alignment challenging. This study proposes an enhanced Transformer-based registration framework to improve cross-modal spatial correspondence modeling. We introduce MSFRTransMorph, a multi-scale feature refinement(MSFR) extension of the TransMorph architecture. The proposed refinement module is designed to strengthen hierarchical feature interactions and enhance fine-grained deformation modeling. Brainstem segmentation was used as anatomical guidance during training, while no tumor annotations were involved. The model was trained on 141 MR-CT image pairs from patients with brain metastases and evaluated. Registration performance was assessed using Dice similarity coefficient (DSC), 95th percentile Hausdorff distance (HD95), and Jacobian-based deformation metrics. MSFRTransMorph achieved the highest registration accuracy for both the brainstem and gross tumor volume (GTV), with an GTV DSC of 74.25 ± 10.61% and HD95 of 16.92 ± 22.03 mm. However, the increase in volumetric overlap was accompanied by a higher proportion of local deformation folding, indicating reduced topological regularity. The proposed multi-scale refinement mechanism enhances cross-modal feature representation and improves volumetric alignment accuracy in MR-CT registration. The observed trade-off between registration precision and deformation stability highlights the necessity of explicitly balancing alignment accuracy and topological plausibility in high-capacity Transformer-based registration frameworks.Keywords: Medical image registration, Deep learning, MR-CT registration, Multi-scale feature refinement module, Adaptive radiotherapySubject terms: Cancer, Computational biology and bioinformatics, Engineering, Medical research, Oncology

Introduction

Precise target localization and accurate dose delivery are fundamental requirements in modern radiotherapy. Conventionally, treatment planning relies primarily on computed tomography (CT) images, as CT provides reliable electron density information essential for radiation dose calculation1. However, CT inherently suffers from limited soft-tissue contrast, which may compromise accurate delineation of tumor boundaries and adjacent normal structures. Magnetic resonance (MR) imaging provides superior soft-tissue contrast and has demonstrated clear advantages in tumor delineation, functional imaging, and treatment response assessment. Therefore, integrating MR with CT enables a complementary imaging strategy that combines anatomical detail with electron density information, which is essential for accurate gross tumor volume (GTV) delineation and optimized dose planning. Previous studies have shown that multimodal integration can improve target definition, reduce radiation exposure to surrounding normal tissues, and potentially enhance therapeutic outcomes2–10. Accordingly, consensus guidelines recommend the routine use of MRI for tumor delineation and visualization of critical structures in head and neck radiotherapy11–13.

Deformable image registration (DIR) plays a critical role in medical imaging, enabling the quantification of anatomical changes in longitudinal studies14, facilitating multimodal image fusion15, and supporting accurate patient image alignment for radiotherapy treatment planning16. By aligning planning CT and CBCT images acquired during treatment, deformable image registration may facilitate assessment of anatomical changes and support adaptive radiotherapy in nasopharyngeal carcinoma(NPC)17.Although conventional deformable registration frameworks have been widely applied in medical imaging research18–21, they still exhibit several limitations. First, these approaches are typically formulated as iterative optimization problems, which can be computationally expensive. Moreover, due to the inherent complexity and heterogeneity of medical imaging data, conventional methods often struggle to handle substantial appearance discrepancies between multimodal image pairs22. Traditionally, image registration has often required manual intervention or supervision by clinicians, a process that is time-consuming and highly dependent on operator expertise23. Moreover, manual parameter tuning and clinician intervention can introduce instability and operator-dependent variability24–26. Recent advances in deep learning have significantly accelerated the development of automated image registration methods. Convolutional neural network (CNN)–based frameworks can learn hierarchical feature representations directly from imaging data and enable fast inference once trained27–30. Supervised registration methods learn voxel-wise correspondences using ground-truth deformation fields, enabling efficient inference of displacement fields once trained31. For example, Sokooti et al.32 proposed RegNet, which achieved registration accuracy comparable to conventional methods with substantially reduced computational time. However, supervised methods rely on large-scale annotated datasets, which are difficult and expensive to obtain. In contrast to supervised methods, unsupervised registration approaches do not require ground-truth deformation fields. Instead, they are typically trained by optimizing an image similarity metric between the warped moving image and the fixed image. A differentiable spatial transformation module, such as the Spatial Transformer Network (STN), is commonly used to enable end-to-end training by allowing gradients to propagate through the warping operation, Jaderberg et al.33 to extract deep image features without relying on any ground truth annotations. During training, the network is optimized using a similarity metric between the warped moving image and the fixed target image, with gradients back-propagated through the STN in each iteration to update network parameters. Balakrishnan et al.34 introduced VoxelMorph, an unsupervised deformable image registration framework based on the U-Net architecture. VoxelMorph simultaneously learns feature representations and generates deformation fields, leveraging a STN to perform resampling and produce the warped image. It is among the first unsupervised models capable of generating dense displacement fields for deformable registration. FAIM35, a ConvNet-based unsupervised 3D medical image registration framework, adopts a U-shaped encoder–decoder architecture and integrates Inception modules to enhance feature representation efficiency. Li et al.36 further incorporated attention mechanisms in the encoder stage to refine feature maps, achieving registration performance superior to that of the baseline U-shaped architecture.

Despite these advances, CNN-based architectures are inherently limited in modeling long-range spatial dependencies. Transformer networks, with their self-attention mechanisms, enable adaptive context modeling and have recently shown promising performance in medical image analysis. Instead of relying solely on local intensity patterns, self-attention facilitates context-aware feature representation, which is particularly beneficial in multimodal registration scenarios where intensity correspondence between MR and CT is highly inconsistent. In the specific setting of intra-patient MR-CT registration after rigid alignment, where deformations are relatively small, the role of Transformer is not to model large spatial displacements, but to enhance cross-modal feature consistency and contextual interpretation37. Among these methods, TransMorph38 integrates a Swin Transformer encoder into a U-Net-like framework and achieves superior performance compared with CNN-based models. Nevertheless, multimodal MR-CT registration remains challenging due to substantial differences in intensity distributions between MR and CT, as well as the need for high-precision alignment of clinically critical structures such as the brainstem and GTV.

To address these challenges, we propose an enhanced Transformer-based registration framework by introducing a Multi-Scale Feature Refinement (MSFR) module into the TransMorph architecture. Unlike existing Transformer-based registration approaches that mainly rely on attention mechanisms for spatial context aggregation, the proposed MSFR module explicitly refines cross-scale features to improve correspondence between deep feature representations extracted from MR and CT images. By modeling relationships between MR and CT features at multiple scales, the module enables the network to better capture high-level anatomical feature alignment when predicting deformation fields, rather than relying solely on local intensity similarity.

Methods and materials

The overall workflow of the proposed framework, including data acquisition and processing, is illustrated in Figure 1. We collected imaging data from 141 patients who underwent radiotherapy for brain metastases at our institution, including both MR and corresponding CT scans. For each patient, three-dimensional CT and MRI images were acquired on the same day. CT scans included both pre-contrast and post-contrast images, while MRI data were obtained using a T1-weighted imaging protocol.

The overview of data acquisition and processing.

Because the CT and MRI volumes differed in resolution, voxel size, and patient positioning, all images were first resampled using SimpleITK to a common resolution and subsequently rigidly aligned to achieve global registration while preserving anatomical shapes and sizes. As summarized in Table 1, T1-weighted contrast-enhanced MR images (T1ce) were acquired using a 3 T scanner (Omega, United Imaging, China) with the following parameters: repetition time (TR) = 9.6 ms, echo time (TE) = 4.2 ms, flip angle = 10°, inversion time (TI) = 980 ms, and slice thickness = 1 mm. CT images were acquired using a Brilliance Big Bore CT scanner (Philips Medical Systems, Netherlands) with a tube voltage of 120 kV, tube current ranging from 250 to 300 mAs, and slice thickness of 1–3 mm. All patients were positioned in the supine position during both CT and MR acquisition. Immobilization devices (thermoplastic masks) were applied according to standard radiotherapy simulation protocols when applicable.

All images underwent preprocessing, including resampling to 1 × 1 × 1 mm³ voxel resolution, Z-score normalization of image intensities, and subsequent scaling to the [0,1] range. Manual segmentations of the brainstem and GTV were independently performed by two senior radiation oncologists. Brainstem segmentations were used for model training guidance and validation, whereas GTV segmentations were used solely for evaluating registration accuracy. The dataset was randomly split into training (100 patients), validation (15 patients), and testing (26 patients) sets. Training and validation sets were used for model optimization, and the testing set was reserved for performance evaluation.

To evaluate the contribution of segmentation guidance, additional ablation experiments were conducted by training both TransMorph and MSFRTransMorph with and without segmentation supervision. For preprocessing, all scans were normalized to the [0,1]. In our CT-to-MR registration setup, MR images were treated as moving volumes, while CT images were used as fixed volumes. Model training was performed using a single NVIDIA L20 GPU (48 GB memory) with the Adam optimizer39. The initial learning rate was set to 2 × 10⁻⁴, batch size was 1, and training was conducted for 200 epochs. The model achieving the best performance on the validation set was selected as the final optimal model. All models were implemented in PyTorch1.12.

Network architecture

We adopt a Transformer-enhanced U-Net-like architecture for deformable image registration. The network employs a Swin Transformer encoder to extract hierarchical feature representations from the concatenated moving and fixed images, followed by a convolutional decoder to regress a dense deformation field. Specifically, the moving and fixed images are concatenated into a two-channel 3D volume and embedded into non-overlapping patches before being fed into the Transformer encoder. The encoder extracts multi-scale hierarchical features through patch partitioning and merging operations. These features are then propagated to the decoder via skip connections to preserve spatial information. The decoder consists of convolutional blocks that progressively up-sample the encoded features to generate a dense nonlinear deformation field. Finally, the predicted deformation field is applied to the moving image using a differentiable spatial transformer layer to obtain the warped output. It is important to note that, the Transformer is not intended to model large deformations, but to enhance contextual feature representation under significant cross-modality appearance discrepancies. To address this limitation, we adopt the Swin Transformer as the encoder backbone and construct a Transformer-enhanced U-Net-like registration framework.

Although Transformer encoders effectively capture long-range contextual information, low-level anatomical boundary details may still be diluted during hierarchical feature aggregation. This issue is particularly critical in brain metastases MR-CT registration, where precise alignment of small but clinically significant structures (brainstem and GTV) is required. To enhance local structural sensitivity, we introduce a lightweight MSFR module in the shallow skip connections. Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using Python (version 3.10.13, https://www.python.org/) and SPSS Statistics 21. Wilcoxon signed-rank test was used to conduct statistical comparisons of quantitative metrics including DSC between different models. A significance level of p < 0.05 was defined as statistically significant, indicating that the performance differences were unlikely to result from random variation.

Results

Brainstem registration

Quantitative results for brainstem registration on the 26 MR-CT cases are summarized in Table 2; Fig. 3. MSFRTransMorph achieved the highest Dice similarity coefficient (88.10 ± 6.45%), outperforming VoxelMorph (86.95 ± 6.28%, p < 0.001), UTSRMorph (87.73 ± 6.38%, p = 0.11), and TransMorph (87.41 ± 6.54%, p < 0.01). Although the absolute improvement in Dice ranges from 0.37% to 1.15%, the superiority of MSFRTransMorph is consistent across all comparisons. In terms of boundary accuracy, MSFRTransMorph also achieved the lowest HD95 (4.34 ± 2.79 mm), indicating improved surface alignment and reduced extreme boundary deviations. However, the percentage of non-positive Jacobian determinants increased to 1.67 ± 0.35%, higher than all baseline methods. This suggests that the enhanced alignment capability is accompanied by increased deformation non-diffeomorphism.

GTV registration

For the GTV task, MSFRTransMorph achieved the highest Dice score (74.25 ± 10.61%), surpassing VoxelMorph (73.07 ± 13.00%, p < 0.001), TransMorph (72.60 ± 12.60%, p < 0.001), and UTSRMorph (71.34 ± 12.17%, p < 0.001) in Table 2. The relative improvement over TransMorph (1.65%) is more pronounced than that observed in the brainstem task. MSFRTransMorph also achieved the lowest HD95 (16.92 ± 22.03 mm). As observed in the brainstem experiments, deformation folding increased compared with baseline methods. In terms of deformation regularity, the proposed MSFRTransmorph maintained a comparable Jacobian determinant distribution to UTSRmorph (p < 0.001), while achieving significantly higher Dice scores and lower HD95 (p < 0.001). These results show that MSFRTransMorph achieves improved Dice scores and lower HD95 compared to baseline methods, while exhibiting a slightly higher percentage of non-positive Jacobian determinants.

Discussion

Multimodal MR-CT registration in brain metastases radiotherapy remains a challenging task due to substantial cross-modality intensity discrepancies and complex anatomical deformations23. Although MRI provides improved soft-tissue contrast compared to CT, geometric distortion inherent to MR acquisition may compromise spatial fidelity, thereby necessitating accurate deformable registration for reliable target delineation46. Furthermore, CT-based tumor visualization is intrinsically limited in certain clinical scenarios47, reinforcing the importance of integrating complementary modalities during treatment planning.

In this study, we introduced a multi-scale feature refinement strategy into a Transformer-based registration framework and evaluated its performance in MR-CT alignment. The experimental results on a cohort of 141 patients demonstrate that the proposed MSFRTransMorph consistently improves volumetric overlap for both the brainstem and GTV regions. Notably, tumor-related annotations were not used during training; only the brainstem served as anatomical guidance. The observed improvement in GTV alignment therefore suggests that enhanced cross-modal feature representation, rather than direct supervision, contributed to better deformation estimation in clinically critical tumor regions.

The performance gain may be associated with the synergy between Transformer-based contextual modeling and MSFR-driven multi-scale refinement. Segmentation guidance may provide additional anatomical constraints that improve registration stability, particularly in structurally constrained regions such as the brainstem. The improved performance in the GTV region may be related to its higher variability and less consistent anatomical structure compared with the brainstem. Under such conditions, the proposed multi-scale feature refinement mechanism appears to provide additional benefits by enhancing feature representation across different spatial scales. It is also important to note that the improved alignment accuracy is accompanied by increased deformation folding. Although the proposed MSFRTransMorph achieves better alignment performance in terms of Dice and HD95 compared with TransMorph, it also exhibits a higher percentage of non-positive Jacobian determinants, indicating reduced deformation regularity. The improved model achieved a GTV Dice score of 74.25 ± 10.61% with an HD95 of 16.92 ± 22.03 mm. Importantly, the brainstem-guided constraint improved anatomical plausibility and reduced unrealistic deformations in critical structures such as the brainstem, which may help mitigate the risk of dose misalignment in radiotherapy planning.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the dataset size (n = 141) remains moderate, and larger multi-center validation is required. Second, only the brainstem was used as anatomical guidance during training; incorporating additional structures may further enhance global anatomical consistency. Third, MR acquisition was performed without a dedicated head-and-neck coil, which may have reduced image quality. In addition, although MR and CT volumes were resampled to identical resolution, differences in original acquisition parameters may still influence registration behavior. Future work will focus on integrating explicit Jacobian regularization, topology-aware optimization strategies, and adaptive constraint scheduling to better balance anatomical realism and deformation smoothness. In addition, qualitative evaluation in this study is based on visual inspection of representative cases, which has inherent limitations. The visualization results are derived from a single patient and may not fully reflect the variability across the entire dataset. Moreover, differences in slice selection and anatomical location may affect visual interpretation. Overall, this study provides insight into how multi-scale refinement mechanisms influence both registration accuracy and deformation characteristics, offering guidance for the development of next-generation high-precision multimodal registration models.

Conclusion

This study proposed an improved MSFRTransMorph framework incorporating a MSFR module for MR-CT radiotherapy image registration, guided by brainstem segmentation during training. The proposed model achieved superior registration accuracy for both the brainstem and GTV on a clinical dataset. These findings suggest its potential utility for improving target localization and adaptive radiotherapy planning. The proposed framework demonstrates that combining multi-scale feature refinement with Transformer-based representations can lead to consistent improvements in multimodal registration performance, while highlighting a trade-off between accuracy and deformation regularity.

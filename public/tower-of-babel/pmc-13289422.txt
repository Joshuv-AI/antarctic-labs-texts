DNA methylation and gene expression signatures for common childhood infections
Authors: Esther Herrera-Luis, Dolors Pelegrí Sisó, Marta Vidal, Katerina Margetaki, Angelos Athanasios Korakakis Pissanidis, Ruth Aguilar, Martine Vrijheid, Carlota Dobaño, Manolis Kogevinas, Marianna Karachaliou, Mariona Bustamante
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundEarly life exposure to common pathogens and a high pathogen burden during childhood can have long-term effects on immune development and overall health. These infections can trigger molecular changes, including alterations in gene expression and DNA methylation (DNAm), which regulate immune and metabolic pathways. Our aim was to identify biological processes underlying differential patterns of DNAm and gene expression in whole blood by infection status in European children.ResultsIn the Rhea (Greece) and INMA (Spain) cohorts, serum/plasma samples collected at mean ages of 4 and 8 years were analyzed by multiplex serology to measure IgG against 14 antigens from 9 pathogens, and blood collected at a mean age of 8 years was used for DNAm and gene expression profiling. Epigenome- and transcriptome-wide analyses were conducted to assess association with childhood infections. A total of 290 unique CpGs were significantly associated with pathogen outcomes: 265 with seropositivity, 111 with first exposure timing, and one with viral burden. Cytomegalovirus (CMV) exposure accounted for the largest number of both epigenetic (n = 325) and transcriptomic (n = 8) associations. A total of 89 CMV-related CpGs had been described before in adults, and among novel ones, 54 showed consistent effects in adults. CMV-related CpGs were enriched for SUZ12 targets linked to morphogenesis, oxidative stress, and cognition. A previously developed CMV episcore in adults predicted serologically assessed CMV infection at 4 and 8 years of age, with area under the curve values ranging from 0.74 to 0.78 (95% CI 0.68–0.83).ConclusionsWe identified novel DNAm and gene expression signatures of common childhood infections, particularly CMV, implicating immune and morphogenesis pathways. A subset of CMV-related DNAm signals showed consistent associations with those reported previously in adults, suggesting similar molecular effects across ages.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13148-026-02130-z.

Background

Emerging evidence suggests that infections can trigger or accelerate the development of a broad spectrum of chronic diseases [1]. This is largely dependent on the specific pathogen characteristics, age of first exposure [2] as well as genetic susceptibility and other environmental exposures. Previously, infections during infancy and childhood have been linked to the development of allergic diseases, adverse neurodevelopmental outcomes and obesity [3–6] and may also cause long-term immune alterations and broader immune dysregulation [7–10]. Their cumulative burden or co-infection may have additive, synergistic or antagonistic effects on health. In fact, children with a high burden of infections in early life experience higher risk of subsequent antibiotic treatments continuing through childhood, independent of social and environmental risk factors [11]. The downstream mechanisms following exposure to infectious agents that lead to disease are complex and diverse. Variation in DNA methylation (DNAm) and transcription profiles could mediate the effect of infections on childhood disease risk.

DNAm, the most studied epigenetic mark, can influence gene expression, affecting cellular processes and long-term health outcomes. Environmental exposures, such as early-life infections, may affect gene expression, through mechanisms that may involve DNAm or occur independently of it. Nevertheless, the exploration of the impact of early childhood infections on DNAm and gene expression has been limited to date. Respiratory syncytial virus infection in infancy is associated with differential saliva and nasal epithelial DNAm in immune genes [12, 13]. In adults, SARS‑CoV‑2 infection induces persistent blood DNAm changes [14] and human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection affects antiviral and T‑cell gene DNAm [15]. Active human cytomegalovirus (CMV) infection significantly downregulates neurodevelopmental genes in cerebral organoids [16], alters gene expression in transplant patients, potentially contributing to morbidity and mortality [17], and is associated with obesity, neurological, and inflammatory conditions [16, 18]. After the acute infection, CMV becomes latent and this latent CVM infection exerts large DNAm effects in blood compared with other herpesviruses [19].

Exploring the omics signatures associated with exposure to common childhood infectious agents can help elucidate specific pathogen-outcome associations, provide insights into the biological role of infections in long-term health outcomes and contribute to the identification of potential biomarkers for early detection and prediction of future health risks. To this end, DNA methylome and transcriptomic signatures among 8-year-old children were investigated for associations with exposure to 5 polyomaviruses (BKPyV, JCPyV, KIPyV, WUPyV, Merkel cell polyomavirus [MCPyV]), CMV, Epstein-Barr virus (EBV), adenovirus 36 (Avd36) and Toxoplasma gondii (T. gondii) at ages 4 and 8 in children from two South European birth cohort studies.

Methods

Study population

This study integrated data from two projects (i) the Human Early Life Exposome Study (HELIX), a collaborative project of ongoing longitudinal population-based birth cohort studies in six European countries [20] and (ii) SeroEpic3, that was established to examine the contribution of common pathogens on non-communicable diseases development. Participants from the INfancia y Medio Ambiente (INMA, Sabadell, Spain) and the Rhea mother child cohort study (Rhea, Crete, Greece) cohorts with available serology data in childhood (4 and 8 years of age, through SeroEpic3) and omics data at a mean of 8 years of age (through HELIX) were considered. Additional details on exposure, omics profiling, and statistical analysis can be found in the Supplementary material.

Exposure to common pathogens

Serum/plasma samples were processed following standard procedures, stored at − 80 °C and shipped on dry ice to the Immune Response and Biomarkers Core Facility in ISGlobal for serological analysis. Immunoglobulin G (IgG) seroreactivity was measured against several pathogen antigens by fluorescent bead-based multiplex serology (1:1000 serum dilution). The panel included the viral capsid protein 1 of five polyomaviruses (BKPyV, JCPyV, KIPyV, WUPyV, MCPyV), four EBV antigens (ZEBRA, EBNA-1, EA-D, VCA p18), three CMV antigens (pp150, pp65, pp28), T. gondii SAG2 antigen, and adenovirus 36 fiber protein. Seropositivity for an antigen was defined as Median Fluorescence Intensity (MFI) values exceeding the antigen-specific cutoff, calculated as the antilog to the mean plus 3 standard deviations of log10-transformed MFIs of negative controls ([10mean(h)+3SD(h), where h = log10(control MFI)]. EBV and CMV seropositivity required positivity for at least two antigens.

To capture differential immune responses to CMV, antigens-specific seropositivity was categorized as follows: overall CMV seronegative (–), IgG levels below the median among overall CMV seropositive individuals (low responders), and IgG levels at or above the median among overall CMV seropositive individuals (high responders). CMVest [19], an EPIC-based score for latent CMV infection developed in adults, was estimated as the weighted sums of the individual’s DNAm M-values.

Timing of first exposure up to age 8 was defined as a qualitative variable considering the following time points: before 4 years (++ or +–, hereby ++), between 4 and 8 years (–+) and never (– –). The burden of viral infections was categorized as high versus low according to a median split based on the number of total viral infections and as the top quartile compared to the rest of quartiles at each age [11].

DNAm profiling

DNA was extracted from the buffy coat using the Chemagen kit (Perkin Elmer). Blood DNAm was measured using the Illumina Infinium HumanMethylation450 (450K) array. Briefly, 700 ng of DNA were bisulfite-converted using the EZ 96-DNAm kit and DNAm measured using the Infinium protocol, following the manufacturers’ standard protocols. Standard quality control procedures were applied. The data was normalized with the functional normalization method with Noob background subtraction and dye-bias correction. Batch effect (slide) was corrected using the ComBat R package [21]. The remaining autosomal 468,947 CpGs after the quality control were annotated using the IlluminaHumanMethylation450kanno.ilmn12.hg19 R package [22]. Methylation values were expressed as betas, ranging from 0 to 1. More details in Supplementary material.

Gene expression profiling

Whole peripheral blood RNA was extracted using the MagMAX for Stabilized Blood Tubes RNA Isolation Kit (ThermoFisher). Coding and non-coding transcripts expression was evaluated using the Affymetrix Human Transcriptome Array 2.0 ST arrays (HTA 2.0) following the manufacturer’s guidelines, using the Manual Target preparation for GeneChip Whole Transcript expression arrays and Affymetrix labeling and hybridization kits. Data were normalized by Affymetrix with the GCCN (SST-RMA) algorithm at the gene and transcript level. Transcript cluster (TC, defined as predefined grouping of probes representing a specific annotated transcriptional unit, summarized to produce a single expression value) annotations were assigned through the ExpressionConsole software using the HTA-2_0 Transcript Cluster Annotations Release na36. Standard quality control procedures were conducted. TCs detected in fewer than 1% of the samples were excluded from the analysis. TC expression values are expressed as log2 transformed residuals after regressing out surrogate variables estimated using the SmartSVA method [23]. More details in Supplementary material.

Covariates

Covariates included age at sample collection, child biological sex (extracted from clinical records), self-reported maternal education (primary, secondary, or university degree), self-reported maternal smoking during pregnancy (yes, no), subcohort (INMA, Rhea), and blood cellular composition (basophils, memory B cells, naive B cells, memory CD4 T cells, naïve CD4 T cells, memory CD8 T cells, naïve CD8 T cells, eosinophils, monocytes, neutrophils, natural killer (NK) cells, and T regulatory cells). Blood cellular composition was deconvoluted from DNAm using the IDOLOptimizedCpGsBloodExtended450k function within the FlowSorted.BloodExtended.EPIC R package [24]. See Supplementary material for details on the selection and definition of covariates.

Statistical analyses

DNAm and TC expression (from now on gene expression) associations with pathogen serostatus (positive vs. negative) and viral burden at ages 4 and 8 years, as well as first exposure timing (before 4 years, 4–8 years, never), were assessed using robust linear regression and linear regression models, respectively, within the limma R package [25]. All analyses were adjusted for relevant covariates. Epigenome-wide significance was defined as 2.4 × 10−7 [26]. Transcriptome-wide significance was defined as 8.6 × 10−7 (0.05/58,038 effective genes calculated with the Li and Ji method [27]. Interaction terms between CMV serostatus and the myeloid cell fraction (monocytes, neutrophils, eosinophils, and basophils) were modeled using CellDMC [28]. Traits with a low sample size, represented by fewer than 10% of individuals in any category, were excluded. Sensitivity analyses were conducted to assess the association across cohorts or specific CMV protein responder groups (high responders, low responders, seronegative). The area under the receiver operating characteristic curves (AUCs) and the comparison of different predictive models for CMV seropositivity were calculated using the DeLong test within the pROC R framework [29]. The statistical framework R (version 4.3.2) was used to perform all the analyses [30].

Follow-up analyses

Non-multi-modal, non-cross-reactive, and non-SNP-containing CpGs were retained for downstream analysis. For the genome-wide significant CpGs, CpG-enrichment for prior epigenome-wide association study (EWAS) associations was evaluated using the EWAS Atlas toolkit [31] while DNase I hypersensitivity regions, chromatin states, and histone H3 methylation marks were assessed with eFORGE 2.0 [32]. Single cell blood chromatin accessibility was accessed using the scATAC.Explorer R package [33] and used for the annotation of CpGs. Gene-set enrichment analyses (GSEA) of the suggestive signals (p ≤ 1 × 10−5 and minimum of 20 input signals) were conducted using the the missMethyl [34] and enrichr R [35] packages, respectively for CpGs and TCs (see Supplementary material for more details). A False discovery rate-adjusted p-value (FDR) ≤ 0.05 and at least 2 significant markers in sets was considered as significant unless otherwise specified. Predicted gene-enhancer interactions in blood cells (EPIraction value > 0.2) were assessed [36]. Protein-protein interactions (PPI) were evaluated with STRING [37]. Cis-Expression Quantitative Trait Methylation (eQTM), considering genes with transcription start site within 0.5 Megabase (Mb) of the corresponding CpG, were assessed through the HELIX eQTM catalogue [38] (https://helixomics.isglobal.org/). Single cell type specificity and immune cell expression cluster were assessed using the Human Protein Atlas [39] (http://www.proteinatlas.org).

Results

Study population

The DNAm and gene expression at a mean age of 8 years were investigated in 372 European children for associations with exposures to BKPyV, JCPyV, KIPyv, WUPyV, MCPyV, EBV, CMV, Avd36, and T. gondii at ages 4 and 8 (Table 1). Compared to the overall sample with serology data (Table S1), the subset with omics available comprised more individuals with higher maternal education (~ 38% vs. 12%) and had a higher central tendency of viral infections at age 8 (median of 5 vs. 4). BKPyV, EBV, KIPyV, MCPyV and WUPyV exhibit relatively high seroprevalence rates by age 4 (> 50%), with BK reaching 97% at 8 years. Thus, no EWAS was performed for this virus. Infection rates for EBV, JCPyV, and MCPyV, increased by more than 10% at 8 years compared to 4 years.

By using the CpG list and weights of CMVest [19], we calculated a CMV episcore for assessment as an additional trait capturing CMV pathobiology. Considering the 335 available 450K-CpGs out of the original 547 EPIC-CpGs identified in adults (61.3%), the CMV episcore predicted infection at ages 4 and 8 with AUCs of 0.74 [CI 0.68–0.80] and 0.78 [CI 0.73–0.83], respectively (Fig. S2). When compared to the demographic-based models, the inclusion of the CMV episcore in the model resulted in a significant AUC improvement (DeLong´s test p-value < 0.001), from 0.59 [CI 0.51–0.66] to 0.76 [CI 0.70–0.81] at age 4, and from 0.65 [CI 0.59–0.72] to 0.80 [CI 0.75–0.85] at age 8.

DNA methylation profiles

Epigenome-wide association study

The EWAS inflation factor ranged from 0.94 to 1.41 (mean λEWAS = 1.02) (Table S2). There were 265 and 111 associations at p-value ≤ 2.4 × 10−7 with seropositivity and first exposure timing, respectively, including 290 unique CpGs (Fig. 1, Fig. S3, Table S3). Most associations with seropositivity were observed at 8 years (223 vs. 42 at 4 years), in concordance with omics profiling age. The associations with seropositivity were found for CVM (4 and 8 years), MCPyV (4 and 8 years), JCPyV at 4 years, KIPyV at 8 years, but none with Adv-36, EBV, T. gondii or WUPyV (Table 2). Associations with CMV serostatus at both time points showed consistent effects (r2 = 0.98, p-value < 1 × 10−16) (Fig. S4), with the largest association estimate change (between age 8 and 4) for probes significant at both time points observed for cg15843262 (DNMT3A, 39% higher at 8 years) and cg19078576 (BASP1/AC091878.1, 42% higher at 8 years) (Table S3, Fig. S3). Association estimates for cell-adjusted and unadjusted CMV serostatus-related CpGs were highly correlated at both ages (r2 ≥ 0.98, p-value < 1 × 10−16) (Fig. S5). In cohort-stratified analyses, a small number of associations (n = 43 out of 377 associations) exhibited differences larger than 50% compared to the all-participants analysis (Table S4). From the 34 and 219 signals associated with CMV serostatus at 4 and 8 years, respectively, 22 and 89 of them were already described at FDR ≤ 0.05 and in the same effect direction in 884 adults from the Milieu Intérieur cohort [19]. Thus, 12 and 130 were novel signals associated with CMV seropositivity observed at 4 and 8 years, respectively. Among these, 9 (4 years) and 89 (8 years) novel signals were available for lookup among CpGs that did not reach significance at FDR ≤ 0.05 in the original analysis in adults from the Milieu Intérieur cohort. Out of them, 4 (4 years) and 52 (8 years) CpGs presented a p-value < 0.05 with consistent direction of effect (Table S5). Among these, annotation through predicted enhancer-gene interactions in blood [36], identified genes participating in transcriptional regulation (e.g. POLR2A, ZFP2), Hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection (FGF11) [40], and SARS-CoV-2 infection (TMEM106B) [41].

There were 72 associations for CMV first exposure timing groups (65 unique CpGs, of which 48 were also found in the serostatus analysis). For the BKPyV ++/-+ comparison, there were 12 associations, with 4 also overlapping with the serostatus analysis. In contrast, Adv-36, MCPyV, JCPyV, T. gondii, EBV, KIPyV, and WUPyV (each with fewer than six signals) showed mostly non-overlapping associations (Table 2, Table S3). Similar effects were observed when +- individuals were excluded from the analysis (Pearson r²effects=1.00, Fig. S6).

Sensitivity analysis related to the antibody levels to specific CMV antigens at age 8 years (Pearson r², excluding seronegative individuals: pp28-pp65 = 0.16, pp150-pp65= − 0.02, pp28-pp150 = 0.42), showed 9 CpGs with DNAm levels significantly different between pp28 high responders and CMV seronegatives (mean effect: − 5.41 × 10−3), as well as between high and low responders (mean effect: − 2.62 × 10−3), which could reflect recent or repeated CMV exposure, higher viral loads or reactivation. Moreover, two CpGs with DNAm levels were significantly different between pp65 low responders and CMV seronegatives (effectcg05201970: − 1.65 × 10−2, effectcg01798341: 1.30 × 10−2) as well as low and high pp65 responders (effectcg05201970: − 2.63 × 10−2, effectcg01798341: 1.94 × 10−2), which could suggest limited or no recent CMV exposure, low viral loads, or viral latency according to pp65 levels. Moreover, a subset of CpGs were significantly different across all three antigen-specific responder comparisons (pp28: 33; pp65: 15; pp150: 10). These CpGs likely capture general features of the host response to CMV exposure, driven by antigen-specific reactivity (Table S3, Figs. S7–S9).

For CMV serostatus, which showed the most DNAm signals, the CpGs cg26677549 (near AGAP1 and SH3BP4) and cg21099326 (near TRIM35 and PTK2B) exhibited significant interaction between myeloid cell fraction and serostatus (FDR ≤ 0.05) (Table S6), despite not reaching genome-wide significance in the EWAS. PTK2B is predominantly expressed in monocytes, whereas AGAP1 and SH3BP4 are enriched in T and dendritic cell clusters, respectively. PTK2B enhances antiviral innate immunity, with Ptk2b-deficient mice being more susceptible to viral infection than controls [42]; while TRIM35 regulates antiviral responses by promoting interferon type I and degrading viral proteins [43–45].

Only one CpG, cg24341800 (ACAT1), was significantly associated with higher viral infection burden at 8 years (mean split), and none with viral infection burden at 4 years or considering the top quartile against the rest.

Annotation and enrichment analyses

By exploring the relationship of infection-related genome-wide significant CpG probes with other traits, significant enrichment (p-value ≤ 0.05) was observed for CMV CpGs in prior EWAS signals for smoking, aging, cancer, chronic inflammatory conditions, and various microbial infection-related traits (Fig. S10). These align with the known effects of CMV on immune system dynamics, including alterations in immune cell composition and function, as well as age-associated shifts in inflammatory responses [46]. Moreover, CMV CpGs were enriched in functional regions, such as 1st exon, CpG island, or the chromatin-associated CpG island N_Shelf and S_Shelf (Fig. S11), and in repressive chromatin states in blood, including Polycomb-repressed, bivalent flanking, and quiescent regions (Table S7). These patterns point to CMV-related epigenetic alterations not only in transcriptionally silent regions but also in poised chromatin, potentially reflecting viral latency or immune regulation. This is further supported by enrichment of repressive histone marks H3K27me3 and H3K9me3, linked to Polycomb activity and chromatin compaction. Additional H3K4me1 and H3K4me3 marks in blood suggest CMV may also influence enhancer and promoter states.

To better understand the pathobiology of viral infections, GSEA was conducted on the suggestive findings (p-value ≤ 1 × 10−5), even if they did not meet the strict multiple-testing significance threshold. The suggestive CpGs associated with CMV at 8 years, which were annotated to 404 genes, were enriched across GO terms, biological pathways, diseases, and transcription factors, including SUZ12 (Table S8, Fig. 2). Many gene targets of the transcription factor SUZ12 were enriched across morphogenesis, development, and oxidative stress processes. SUZ12 targets were also connected to cognitive processes, smoking, BMI, and metabolite levels.

Other infection-related suggestive CpGs (Table S7) were significantly enriched for genes involved in metabolite levels for the ++/−+ CMV comparison (26 genes), prostate cancer genes for EBV serostatus (CD82, MXI1), tremor of the hands for the ++/−+ WUPyV comparison (GCK, CNTN2), coagulation and interferon (IFN) γ response for the ++/−+ MCPyV comparison (5 genes, including F10, F12, CD40), positive regulation of skeletal muscle neurotransmission for the ++/−− Avd36 comparison (MESD, LRP4), and Sin3a target genes for Avd36 serostatus (BLCAP, TRAPPC12, STAT3).

Gene expression profiles

Transcriptome-wide association analyses

Across transcriptome-wide association studies (TWAS), the inflation factor ranged from 0.95 to 1.09 (mean λTWAS = 1.02) (Fig. S12, Table S2). No significant results were found for the burden of viral infections or pathogens other than CMV.

Six genes were significantly overexpressed in CMV seropositive children at 8 years (FDR ≤ 0.05), of which one, annotated to GZMH, was also significantly overexpressed in the ++/−− comparison (Fig. 3). No large differences were observed in cohort-stratified models (Table S9). No associations were observed for CMV seropositivity at 4y. Finally, The CMV episcore was significantly associated with the expression of 25 TCs, 6 of them already identified in the CMV seropositivity TWAS (Pearson r²effects=0.99).

Sensitivity analyses identified 4 TCs with pp65-specific antigen response. MYO6 was significantly differentially expressed (FDR ≤ 0.05) in relation to pp65 following a linear trend (higher levels vs. higher effect on gene expression). In contrast, three TCs, annotated to FCRL6, KLRC3 and to a non-coding RNA, showed the strongest effect on gene expression among low responders, compared to high responders and seronegatives, suggesting limited or no recent CMV exposure, low viral loads, or viral latency according to pp65 levels (Table S9).

Enrichment and protein-protein interaction analyses

GSEA of the suggestive CMV episcore TWAS TCs (p-value ≤ 1 × 10−5), encompassing 39 annotated genes, revealed strong activation of immune-related pathways, particularly those involving chemokine signaling, cytokine activity, NK cell function, and T cell differentiation, among others (FDR ≤ 0.05). Moreover, pathways related to infection were also found, including Chagas, malaria and SARS-CoV-2. Several immune genes were recurrent across pathways (IFNG, CCL5, CCL4, CD8A, CX3CR1, and TBX21). Several genes of the immune-related pathways also showed significant enrichment in drug response, including ionomycin (ionophore and an antibiotic), budesonide (steroid), dexamethasone (glucocorticoid), and tacrolimus (immunosuppressant) (Table S10). Two of the CMV-related genes were found to be part of a PPI network (FCRL6-GZMH), and exhibited significant correlated gene expression (Pearson r² >0.70) (Figs. S13, S14).

Expression quantitative trait methylation (eQTM)

Cis CpG-gene pairs were investigated for significant signals (51 unique CpGs identified in the EWAS and 25 genes identified in the TWAS), resulting in the identification of 168 significant cis CpG-gene pairs, 132 unique CpGs, and 62 unique genes for 3 pathogen-related traits (CMV, T. gondii, WUPyV) (Table S11). The largest number of eQTMs for serologically-assessed signals was found for FCRL6 (n = 6).

GSEA of the 41 genes identified through eQTM annotation (39 associated with CMVs) showed that eQTMs genes were enriched for cell response to virus and transcriptional regulation. Terms such as HSV1 infection, hepatitis C and regulation of transcription by RNA Polymerase II were identified, as well as Interferon Gamma, IL-2 Receptor Beta Chain in T cell Activation, and NF-kappaB Signal Transduction, which are essential for clearing viral infections (Table S12, Fig. S15).

Discussion

This study explored the DNAm and transcriptomic signatures of common pathogen infections in early life among European children. We identified novel and known omics associations for seropositivity to common infections in children, first exposure timing up to 8 year of age and burden of viral infection. CMV infection exposure accounted for the majority of associations across omics layers, whereas far fewer were found for EBV, polyomaviruses, T. gondii, and Adv36, consistent with prior findings [19]. From the ~ 85 novel DNAm associations for CMV, ~ 54 were consistent with effects reported in adults [19], highlighting similar DNAm signals across age groups and potentially suggesting the persistence of the impact of childhood viral exposures on the DNA methylome. Among these, the CpG cg15843262 is annotated to DNMT3A, encoding a key DNA methyltransferase playing a role in the replication of some herpesviruses and which is implicated in the cell composition-mediated epigenetic effects of CMV [19].

The GSEA provided valuable insights into how DNAm might contribute to the identification of both known and novel links between infections and health traits. For instance, genes annotated to Avd36-related CpGs were enriched among Sin3a target genes (BLCAP, TRAPPC12, and STAT3). Sin3a regulates STAT3 transcriptional repression, promotes SGF3-dependent gene transcription, and enhances interferon-mediated antiviral protection against viruses such as influenza A and hepatitis C [47]. In animal models, SIN3A has been identified as a novel determinant of β-cell proliferation [48]. Notably, Adv36 has been shown to exert protective effects against diabetes [49], suggesting a potential biological link between viral exposure, epigenetic regulation, and β-cell function. These findings highlight the potential of DNAm-based approaches to deepen our understanding of infection-related disease mechanisms.

Our GSEA analysis also suggests that genes annotated to CMV-related CpGs are enriched in genes linked to metabolite levels, likely through differential gene expression and in response to sustained changes in the metabolomic profile driven by physiological or pathological stress [50]. Integrative multi-omics studies are needed to determine what specific CpGs, genes, and metabolites may be involved and whether this relationship is causal, correlational, or due to reverse causation. Moreover, genes annotated to CMV-related CpGs were enriched in targets for the SUZ12 transcription factor, a Polycomb2 Repressive Complex member, in a network of genes implicated in different processes that may underlie long-term health outcomes after CMV infection, such as morphogenesis and brain development and cognition traits, among others. CMV infection is known to disrupt key cerebral cellular pathways, being a potential mechanism for congenital CMV-induced neural malformations and autism spectrum disorder risk [51]. In murine models, intermittent CMV infection has also been associated with increased blood-brain barrier permeability and disruption of cerebral metabolism, which may lead to poorer cognition through T cell infiltration [52]. SUZ12 has also been implicated in other pathogens infections; for example, its downregulation promotes HBV replication and oncogenic transformation, contributing to hepatocellular carcinoma, in which chronic HBV infection is a major determinant [53].

Genome-wide CpGs significantly associated with CMV infection were enriched in repressive Polycomb-associated Histone 3 and repressive chromatin states marks. Polycomb Repressive Complex 2 is involved in repressing newly chromatinized CMV genomes upon infection, a key step in the establishment and maintenance of CMV latency [54]. This coupled with the enrichment of CMV-associated CpGs in prior epigenetic signals linked to aging supports that CMV may induce DNAm changes that mimic age-related epigenetic remodeling. Specifically, these include DNA hypermethylation at regions related with Polycomb repression and affecting PRC-related transcription factor binding sites [19], a hallmark of blood composition-independent DNAm effects of aging. However, there is likely an interplay between CMV, blood cell composition, and epigenetic changes. Immunosenescence facilitates immune surveillance evasion during CMV latency [55], immunosenescence-associated epigenetic modifications may influence antigen presentation and CMV gene expression during latency [56] and CMV-related DNAm effects may be mediated by blood cell composition shifts [19].

Several CMV-related genes were identified as eQTM genes, including the overexpressed FCRL6 gene associated with CMV seropositivity, with several CpGs linked to this gene included within the episcore. FCRL6 is an immunoregulatory receptor expressed in cytotoxic lymphocytes, upregulated in chronic inflammation and HIV infection [57]. FCRL6 protein levels are also associated with CMV reactivation after allogenic stem cell transplantation, particularly in CD8 + γδ cells [57]. In fact, CD8 + γδ cells are proposed to be key sensors for upregulated HLA molecules on antigen-presenting cells during CMV infection [57, 58]. Prior evidence supports that DNAm level variation in CMV seropositives may be directly caused by changes in the types and amounts of blood cells during infection, but CMV also seems to alter DNAm levels even when the cell types stay the same (direct effect) [19].

One of the strengths of this study is the analysis of multiple omics layers, pathogen-related traits and their cumulative burden across two time points in early childhood. Our GSEA results suggest that combining DNAm and transcriptomics can offer key insights into how CMV infection could affect long-term health outcomes. Additionally, we identified novel CMV related signals, which were replicated in adults, and sensitivity analyses confirmed the robustness of the findings.

Several limitations should be noted. First, despite a comprehensive pathogen catalogue assessment, the sample size remains limited, hindering our statistical power to detect some associations or explore cell-interacting, sex-specific, or sex-chromosomes associations. Second, prior EWAS studies showed limited overlap with the pathogen set assessed here, highlighting the need for more comprehensive epigenomic analysis of infections to validate our findings beyond CMV. Third, despite controlling for relevant covariates in our analyses, causal inferences cannot be drawn from the associations observed. Fourth, we cannot disregard that DNAm variation directly influences health outcomes, since this interplay is far more complex [59] than what could be captured in this study. Fifth, while the CMV episcore was predictive of CMV serostatus in children, no episcore value could differentiate seronegative from seropositive children. Sixth, few CpGs and genes showed an antigen-specific response and cell-type interacting patterns. However, these findings still require further assessment in independent datasets for validation and understanding of their biological roles and implications. Seventh, the Illumina 450 K array only covers less than 2% of CpG loci in the entire human genome [60]. Finally, publicly available ATAC-seq datasets from blood are limited in number and sample size, which restricts our ability to fully assess the relationship between chromatin accessibility, DNAm and gene expression. Future studies with larger sample sizes, broader epigenetic coverage, and analyses of specific epigenetic marks, including multi-cohort meta-analyses, are needed to address the remaining questions.

Our study identified novel DNAm and gene expression signatures associated with common childhood infections, particularly CMV, implicating immune, developmental, and epigenetic regulatory pathways. Notably, a subset of CMV-related methylation signals demonstrated consistent associations with those reported previously in adults, suggesting similar or persistent effects. Overall our study provides valuable insights into biological pathways perturbed by infection to common pathogens, thereby supporting the rationale for further integrative omics investigations into the long-term pathobiology triggered by childhood infections.

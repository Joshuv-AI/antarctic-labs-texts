Poster 179. New Metrics, New Insights: The First Statcast-Based Study of Swing Metrics and Injury Burden in Major League Baseball
Authors: Caleb Casanova, Steven F. DeFroda
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objectives:Injuries in Major League Baseball (MLB) are detrimental to the success of players and teams and produce significant financial loss. Previous research has linked pitcher biomechanics and pitch metrics to injury risk, however, there is a paucity of research examining injury risk factors among batters. Statcast data on MLB batter swings was made publicly available for the first time in May 2025. The purpose of this study was to evaluate whether swing metrics are independently associated with injury burden among MLB batters. We hypothesized that swing speed would demonstrate a significant positive association with injury risk.Methods:Swing data-including swing speed, swing tilt, attack angle, and attack direction-were collected from all players with >= 100 swings recorded during the 2023 MLB season using publicly available Statcast data. Players who did not play in 2024 due to non-injury reasons were excluded. 389 players were included in the final analysis. Outcome measures of interest were total days on the injured list (IL) and total number of individual IL stints during the 2024 season. Separate seasons were used to evaluate swing metrics and injury outcomes to avoid reverse causality. Linear and logistic regression models were used to evaluate the relationship between swing metrics and injury outcomes, adjusting for age, weight, primary position, handedness, and previous season workload.Results:Swing speed and swing tilt were independently and positively associated with total days spent on the IL. Each 1 mph increase in mean swing speed corresponded to a 5% increase in total IL days (incidence rate ratio [IRR] = 1.05; 95% CI, 1.04-1.06; p < .001), while each 1° increase in mean swing tilt was associated with a 1% increase (IRR = 1.01; 95% CI, 1.00-1.02; p < .01). Switch hitters accumulated 49% more IL days than non-switch hitters (IRR = 1.49; 95% CI, 1.35-1.64; p < 0.001). Primary position was also strongly associated with both total IL days and number of individual IL stints, with shortstops (SS) and third basemen (3B) demonstrating the greatest risk (IRR 1.85; 95% CI, 1.66-2.06; p < .001 for both).Conclusions:Swing speed and swing tilt are significant, independent risk factors for increased injury burden among MLB hitters. These findings should be considered by players, coaches, training staff, and organizations when designing training and development strategies.

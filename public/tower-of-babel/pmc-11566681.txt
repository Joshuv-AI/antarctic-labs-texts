Comprehensive review of serpentine flow fields for effective thermal management of proton exchange membrane fuel cell
Authors: Muhammad Ahmed, Xiong Shusheng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Besides the fact that the Proton Exchange Membrane Fuel Cells (PEMFCs) have become the industrial norm of today, the thermal management in PEMFCs is still challenging. Therefore, the present study delves deep understanding of the issue of thermal management and summarizes the relevant research studies that evaluate the efficiency of serpentine flow fields (SFFs) to other flow configurations for thermal management in PEMFCs. The current research explores the variety of aspects of SFFs i.e., temperature uniformity, cooling efficiency, pressure drop, and overall thermal performance to guide future design optimizations and practical implementations of SFFs. The study in hand also discusses developments in SFFs such as., Multi-Pass Serpentine Flow Fields (MPSFFs) and new hybrid designs that include a combination of traditional serpentine and other flow patterns to decrease pressure drop and increase net performance. The findings of various studies show that serpentine flow fields outperform alternative designs in coolant distribution, heat transfer efficiency, and operating temperature stability. Based on the findings, the present study recommends future research options, highlighting the need to include economic and environmental concerns in flow field (FF) design, as well as investigating the use of developing materials and technologies to improve PEMFC performance.

Whereas the majority of the energy needs of today are met by non-renewable energy resources, i.e., natural gas, coal, and petroleum. Furthermore, it is projected that the global population will exceed 8.7 billion by the year 2035, resulting in a need for energy to accommodate 1.6 billion more people [2]. Besides the major problem of increased energy demand, clean, green, and sustainable energy are also a few of those challenges which require an imperative solution [3,4]. The world's major economies are now converging on the need to reach the objective of carbon neutrality globally by the middle of this century [5]. Therefore, the need for new energy infrastructure has become apparent to meet the present policies regarding environmental standards, promote economic development, and enhance accessibility to clean and cheap energy. Fuel cell

Fuel cells (FCs) are electrochemical assemblies that transform chemical potential of an oxidant and fuel source (often hydrogen [[8], [9], [10], [11], [12], [13]] and oxygen) after reaction right away into electrical power [14]. The sole byproducts of the electrochemical reactions in the FC are heat and water providing a realistic option in our quest for a low-carbon future [[15], [16], [17], [18]]. PEMFCs offer advantages of high-power density (1 W/cm2), prolonged operation cycles, low-temperature operations (80 °C), high energy efficiency, no pollution from reactants, quick start-up, quick reaction to changes in load, small size, low weight, and quiet operation (no noise pollution), wide range of applications, and long life [8,[19], [20], [21], [22]]. PEMFCs are widely used [23], particularly in the transportation sector [24]. Many electric vehicles (EVs) are being developed by utilizing PEMFC technology. Those include but are not limited to Hyundai Nexo, Toyota Mirai, and Honda Clarity [8].

The early development of PEMFCs encountered efficiency and thermal control issues. Thermal management is the main aspects that impact PEMFC performance [18,25]. It is crucial for improving operational conditions and avoiding concerns of overheating [26]. As per the literature, increasing PEMFC performance and longevity required moving from simple to complex FF designs, especially the SFF. It has great promise for effective thermal regulation in PEMFCs. This detailed exploration delves deep into the inner aspects of serpentine FFs, including design concepts, fluid dynamics, and thermal properties. This work seeks to give a comprehensive knowledge piece of research on how serpentine FFs contribute to overall thermal management in PEMFCs by combining and critically examining the up-to-date research studies. Through this investigation, we intend to provide significant insights that will drive future advances in the design and optimization of PEMFCs, eventually promoting their wider acceptance as energy conversion technology that is clean and works efficiently.

Liquid cooling (LC)

In liquid cooling, heat is transmitted from the cell to the bipolar plate (BP) or cooling plate and then to the coolant flowing in the distinct channels in plates [27]. That coolant is then transferred to heat exchangers, which emits heat into surroundings [[6], [28], [29]]. High-power FC stacks often use a liquid-cooling system because liquid's heat transfer coefficient is higher compared to air or gases when the same amount of pumping power is used. This is because liquids have a substantially higher heat capacity. For example, Water has a thermal conductivity that is twice as high as that of air [30,31]. Liquid improves the efficiency of cooling and decreases the dimensions of the heat exchanger [32,33]. The liquid cooling approach is deemed appropriate for high-capacity FCs. (>10 kW) [[34], [35], [36], [37]]. Generally liquid-cooling PEMFCs utilize deionized water and ethylene glycol (EG) as their cooling medium [38,39]. A significant amount of literature has been published on liquid cooling for effective cooling impacts in the PEMFC cooling plate. The literature has highlighted the significance of cooling FF designs in enhancing the cooling mechanisms within the PEMFC stack using liquid cooling.

Cooling plate (CP)

Cooling plates are strategically placed in a repetitive pattern within the FC stack to minimize overheating. Each cooling plate serves the purpose of cooling many FCs [40,41]. To decrease the number of cooling layers, several cells might be placed between two cooling plates. According to numerical modelling studies, increasing the quantity of cells between cooling plates results in reduced stack performance [42,43]. The cooling plate is a crucial element of the FC stack. It reduces the temperature of the PEMFC and enhances the evenness of temperature by efficiently dissipating the heat generated by FC [44]. The cooling plates are found at either end of FC and close to BP, often positioned between two BPs [45]. The thermal energy produced during the operation of a PEMFC is transferred to the cooling plate by conduction of heat through the BP. The heat is then eliminated by circulating coolant inside the plate [[46], [47], [48]]. Commonly, in the liquid cooling strategy, The temperature within PEMFCs is controlled by circulating water via the passages of cooling plates [49]. During simulation studies, the heat load of the cooling plate is modelled as an external heat flow applied to its bottom surface. The cooling plate is equipped with mini channels, which have been punched on its surface and have a millimetre dimension. These channels significantly increase the surface area for transferring heat between the plate and the liquid. Currently, serpentine and parallel designs of mini channels are being used in most commercial applications whereas serpentine designs are often preferred due to their advantages of high heat transfer and higher temperature uniformity [[50], [51], [52]].

Bipolar Plate-BP

It is a significant and multifunctional element of a FC stack that performs various functions like transporting the reactant gases, uniformly distributing the fuel gas and air, taking current to an end plate from the membrane electrode assembly (MEA) and from one cell to another cell, provides thermal and water management inside the active area, prevents gas and coolant leakage, provides mechanical strength to MEA, and separates the individual cells from each other [[53], [54], [55], [56], [57]]. The BP functions as the barrier to prevent the mixing of the coolant, fuel, and oxidant. Additionally, it plays a role in managing heat [58]. Nowadays BPs consist of two separate plates (anode and cathode) which are firmly coupled to each other [59]. Hydrogen is introduced at the anode plate whereas oxygen and the reaction product (water) are on the cathode side [60]. Cooling channels are engraved between both the joined individual plates for the circulation of the cooling medium [61]. BP is produced through various technologies depending upon the applications and required material specifications whereas graphite carbon or metal BPs are very common now a days [[62], [63], [64]]. Metallic BPs are used in commercial applications because of their exceptional mechanical strength and excellent conductivity [54,65]. Same of the preferred properties in the construction process of BPs are low cost, easy fabrication process, light weight, greater strength, higher mechanical stability, and lower surface contact resistance [66]. BPs shares up-to 20–30 % of the overall manufacturing cost and contribute almost 60–80 % in the overall weight of the FC stack [67,68].

Flow fields

The optimal FF design must produce a homogeneous distribution of the current density, as well as even temperatures and a steady production of liquid water. The implementation of uniform distributions is expected to result in reduced mechanical stress on the MEA and enhanced longevity of the cells [69]. The very basic and often used FFs are the SFF, PFF and Interdigitated Flow Field (IFF). Among these PFF is the simplest FF which also offers smallest amount of pressure drop whereas the primary limitation is the uneven distribution of the fluid until flow resistance is not controlled at each path [70]. Whereas IFF offers enhanced reactant distribution and water management at the same time it offers increased drop in pressure across FFs [71]. This pressure drop also increases the amount of energy required for pumping. This can counteract some of the efficiency advantages achieved by improved reactant distribution. Santamaria et al. [72]examined that the design of IFFs requires increased intake pressure, which in turn raises operational expenses. The production of interdigitated FFs is more complicated than typical designs. This complexity may result in increased production costs and issues scaling manufacturing processes. When studying the performance of PEMFCs under various situations Yan et al. [71] highlighted that IFFs may not work effectively under all operating situations, particularly at low humidity levels when their benefits in water management may be less substantial. Studies also highlighted that the forced flow through the porous electrode material in interdigitated designs can cause higher mechanical stress on FC components, thereby lowering cell durability and longevity [73].

Serpentine flow Field-SFF

The serpentine flow field (SFF) is a lengthy continuous channel similar to the movement of a snake (serpent), where the flow route constantly stretches from the inlet to the outlet [74]. The SFF is the most common FF configuration utilized in FC operations. It is largely considered the "industry standard” because it exhibits superior performance and durability when compared to other designs working under the same circumstances [[75], [76], [77]]. SFFs are categorized by several parallel channels that procedure a mutual pathway [78]. Highlighting the benefits of SFFs, this article aims to varnish the evaluation and development of SFF in the race to improve the thermal efficiency of PEMFCs, taking into account various current and historical studies.

Design principles of flow field

The arrangement of FFs in the BP influences the performance of the PEMFC [[79], [80], [81], [82], [83]]. An optimized FC design may improve the even distribution of temperature, reduce pressure drop of the coolant, and facilitate the efficient removal of surplus heat from the stack of FC [84,85]. The design of SFFs in a cooling plate for PEMFCs is based on specific principles that aim to efficiently dissipate heat produced during electrochemical reactions within a FC. The cooling plate, which is commonly incorporated with a SFF, is crucial in maintaining ideal operating temperatures and preventing overheating. A variety of studies have been carried out on SFFs considering different design parameters [86]. Such as Wei-Mon Yan and fellows [86] Constructed a 3D model to examine how different contraction ratios of height and length in the SFF, along with outlet channels of varying heights or lengths, can improve the effectiveness of reactant usage and facilitate the elimination of liquid water in a PEMFC. The investigation unveiled that decreasing the flow areas of the outlet channels leads to higher velocities of reactants in these areas, thus enhancing the transportation of reactants, usage of reactants, and elimination of liquid water. Consequently, this enhancement in cell performance surpasses that achieved by the traditional SFF. S.M. Rahgoshay et al. [85] examined temperature uniformity in two different FFs (SFF and PFF) with cooling plates and found that SFF performed 24 % better than PFF. Seung Ho Yu et al. [47] utilized computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations to analyze the cooling efficiency of six distinct SFF cooling plates by studying fluid flow and heat transfer and found that the multi-pass SFF yielded better performance. F. C. Chen et al. [41] also studied six different FFs of different geometrical parameters and found that the cooling effects of SFF were better and it was due to the unreasonable design of the PFF. Seung Man Baek et al. [87] also evaluated six FFs and found that MPSFFs with three and five parallel paths give superior performance in maintaining consistent temperature across the entire active area. Therefor it is evident from above studies that the design considerations greatly impact the overall performance of FC.

Fluid dynamics and heat transfer

Serpentine channels are better for improving heat transmission in PEMFCs [88]. The winding path increases the effective surface area, which improves convective heat transfer [89]. The tortuous design creates controlled turbulence, which destroys boundary layers and increases the convective heat transfer coefficient [90]. SFFs help to distribute temperatures uniformly, prevent hotspots, and provide effective heat dissipation. Fluid dynamics and heat transfer in SFF have been examined in many studies. As the study of Xuanyu Liu et al. [91] declares that raising the coolant's flow rate volume reduces Index of Uniform Temperature (IUT) in parallel SFF. S. Shimpalee et al. [78] observed that modifying the flow-field configuration of a PEMFC by adjusting path length and the number of parallel FFs can have an impact on its performance and uniformity.

Importance of thermal management in PEMFC

Temperature control is one of the critical considerations for maintaining performance, durability and efficiency of a FC [9,85,92,93]. Extremely high temperatures will lower the water content within the membrane and may even result in the degradation of the catalyst and membrane [5,[94], [95], [96], [97], [98], [99]]. Extremely low temperatures will decrease catalyst activity and raise the risk of water flooding [94,100]. Temperature also affects almost all transport properties and according to some studies, the temperature management system in some FCs stacks accounts for up to 8 % [101,102]. Some studies also propose that the temperature also significantly effects the current density [103]. Therefore, maintaining PEMFC within a certain temperature range is the primary goal of thermal management [5,104]. So, it is necessary to use a suitable water and thermal management technique to eliminate excessive heat, to prevent catalyst degradation and water flooding [105]. Effective cooling of the PEMFC stack is very challenging. In practice, single cells are assembled to form FC stacks [106], and temperature distribution among cells may be uneven [104]. Along with the challenges encountered by heat removal process, the selection of suitable cooling method is also challenging. Various thermal management strategies may be used, such as edge cooling, cooling with separate airflow, air cooling, cathode air supply cooling, phase change cooling, and liquid cooling [107]. Every cooling approach has particular advantages, limits, and difficulties [[108], [109], [110]].

Plenty of research has been carried out on FF designs to enhance the cooling efficiency in a PEMFC cooling plates. Additionally, the literature has emphasized the significance of coolant FF designs in liquid cooling for enhancing the cooling performance of the PEMFC. The main objectives of these studies have remained focused on preventing excessive heating in PEMFCs while minimizing pressure loss throughout the system, as well as attaining a uniform temperature distribution by regulating the highest temperature and minimizing fluctuations in localized temperature within the active region of PEMFCs [110].

Various cooling strategies that are being utilized in a PEMFC stack for improving thermal management, include but not limited to are External Air Flow Cooling, Increased Cathode Air Supply Cooling, Heat Spreaders Cooling For Example (Edge Cooling), Liquid Cooling (Water Or Antifreeze Coolants) and Cooling by Using Face Change Materials [5,[108], [109], [110]]. A FC cooling system may be internal or exterior. Internal cooling in these categories might be air or liquid (water or a water-ethylene glycol combination). Integration of a cooling plate with liquid or air with the BP is a common practice to make the FC stack simpler [111,112]. Because of its high capacity for heat removal, liquid cooling is used in all FC electric vehicles (FCEVs). Deionized water, known for its high specific heat, is commonly used as a coolant. Another popular coolant option is a mixture of ethylene glycol and water, which acts as an antifreeze liquid. Coolant flow channels are often incorporated into BPs (BPs) using liquid pumps to circulate the cooling fluid [113]. Serpentine designs have become the industry norm due to their superior working. These are also considered as a reference design while reviewing new ideas [114,115].

Comprehensive literature

Numerous computational analyses have been carried out to evaluate the enhancement of thermal efficiency in PEMFCs by incorporating SFFs into cooling plates or BPs. These studies examine the effect of SFFs on PEMFC thermal performance using mathematical models and CFD simulations. Incorporating SFFs into BPs or cooling plates is a strategic approach to improve heat dissipation and thermal distribution in a FC. The meandering design of SFFs stimulates controlled fluid dynamics, influencing the PEMFC stack's thermal properties. CFD models provide a comprehensive examination of fluid movement, thermal exchange, and variations in temperature inside the FC, providing useful insights into thermal behaviour under various operating conditions. Some studies which have been comprehensively reviewed in this article are as follows:

S.M. Rahgoshay et al. [85] constructed a standard isothermal model using Ansys Fluent software and examined temperature uniformity along with two types of FFs (SFF and PFF) with cooling plates. The IUT, pressure drop, maximum and minimum temperature gradient, and other effective physical parameters were used in this comparison. The findings demonstrated the superiority of PEMFC with an SFF over a parallel one by 24 %. Whereas, Seung Ho Yu et al. [47] examined the thermal management capabilities of MPSFF in a cooling plate of PEMFC. Using CFD simulations, the research examined and suggested that the SFF had outstanding performance in terms of highest temperature as well as uniform distribution of temperature. F. C. Chen et al. [41] also evaluated the performance of various flow channels (three SFF and another three PFFs fluid flow channels in PEMFC. This study discovered that the SFFs exhibits superior cooling efficiency compared to PFFs. This may be attributed to the efficient distribution of the coolant inside the cooling plate. Contrarily M. Ghasemi et al. [116] conducted a simultaneous investigation of a PEMFC using six distinct cooling FFs. Their findings indicated that conventional spiral FFs have strong performance in relation to the lowest and maximum temperature gradient, as well as the IUT. Despite having a significant pressure drop, it is considered the most effective cooling design. Whereas, Seung Man Baek et al. [87] evaluated the effectiveness of parallel MPSFFs in designing the FF for a larger cooling plate measuring 18 cm by 18 cm. The results proposed that parallel MPSFFs outperformed conventional parallel SFFs in terms of cooling performance, while particular characteristics of pressure drop were quite comparable when the number of parallel channels remained the same. Agus P. Sasmito et al. [117] conducted a numerical assessment of several FFs and found that serpentine-based channels performed better than others regarding thermal management, water management, and gas management and also the stack performance was improved. Jongmin Choi et al. [40] also designed the cooling plate configurations to evaluate the fluid flow and heat transfer performance in the PEMFC stack with various flow configurations. Serpentine structures were found to have the lowest surface temperature among all the serpentine structures evaluated and outperformed because it’s cool and warm flow patterns were alternately located however; the pressure drop in this model remained a little higher. In the same way S.M Miri Joibary et al. [118] also investigated some cooling fields. The results showed that four-sectioned SFF was the most exceptional geometry because of uniformity in temperature and lower range of temperature in comparison to the other FFs. Whereas the serpentine-in-the-beginning design demonstrated superior performance in terms of power output, heat absorption, and water formation. This was owing to its ability to evenly distribute water and maintain high electrical voltage, resulting in increased power generation and efficient heat absorption by the fluid. Further S. Shimpalee et al. [78] and colleagues studied also evaluated several FFs. Their study proposed the 13-channel FF as superior to both the 26-channel FFs because of the increased water concentration within the cell membrane with higher current densities. Similarly Xuanyu Liu et al. [91] Examined the influence of variable temperature distributions on the water management of PEMFC. Model 1 was a PFF with 18 cooling channels, Model 2 was a multi-pass parallel SFF with six cooling channels along the Y-axis direction, and Model 3 was a multi-pass parallel SFF with eight cooling channels along the X-axis direction. Their research concluded that using the parallel SFF model along the X-axis improved the thermal management but the pressure loss in the parallel SFF was consistently 1.06 times higher than that of the PFF. A. Hamrang et al. [119] also carried out numerical simulation grounded on finite volume method using ANSYS Fluent. Two different FF designs (i.e. 1. Parallel Serpentine Flow Field-PSFF) and (Parallel Serpentine-Baffled Flow Fields-PSBF) were determined in this study and the results suggested that PSBFF design managed better. In the same way, G Amarnath et al. [120] also carried out the CFD analysis of a PEMFC with ANSYS Fluent-17.2 commercial CFD software focusing on comparing two types of flow configurations i.e. 1. Dual Serpentine Flow Field (DSFF) which was composed of two distinct single SFFs within the active region of FC. The Split Serpentine Counter Flow Field (SSCFF) configuration performed better in terms of temperature management and uniformity within the FC system in comparison to the other FF configurations analysed in the study. Y. Vazifeshenas et al. [121] also investigated the heat transfer mechanism in PEMFCs by inserting high porosity metal foam into the cooling FFs. The study focused on three distinct FFs i.e. SFF, PFF, and MPSFF. Among them, the MPSFF showed better heat transfer behaviour. Similarly S. Ravishankar et al. [122] conducted a complete 3-dimensional numerical investigation of fluid flow's cooling effectiveness and thermal properties in several PEMFC cooling plate designs. Six distinct FFs (i.e., 1. SFF 2. Spiral FF 3. Divided SFF 4. Divided Spiral FF 5. Distributed SFF 6. Distributed Spiral FF) were evaluated for the temperature uniformity, drop in pressure, and heat removal efficiency. In terms of thermal management, for creating a more uniform temperature distribution inside PEMFC stack, the distributed serpentine FF and distributed spiral FF outperformed the standard serpentine FF and spiral FF. Whereas Dhahad et al. [123] experimentally studied PEMFC (PROTIUM-20) with eight different FFs (Serpentine-Z, Serpentine-2Z, Serpentine-3Z, Parallel-Z, Serpentine-S, Serpentine-2S, Serpentine-W, and a modified Serpentine-W) to evaluate the influence of FF design on the efficiency of a PEMFC, the dispersal of reactant gases, and the removal of water in gas FF. The experimental findings of these configurations were grouped into three groups i.e. A, B, and C for comparison. Group A, Serpentine-Z design was found to be the most effective among all proposed FFs. In group B, Serpentine-S outperforms other recommended FF. In the third effort, they found that the new modified Serpentine-W design was best option for covering the full FC surface area. Alex et al. [124] examined various parameters of a PEMFC with a 50 cm2 and found that serpentine flow channels contribute to high efficiency and operational stability by guaranteeing appropriate heat distribution and controlling internal temperature gradients. Contrarily to above studies Jun Shen et al. [125] described an assessment criterion for various FF patterns in the PEMFC. This work also evaluated several FF patterns in PEMFC. The study evaluated parallel, single-serpentine, and pressurized PFFs to see how they affected PEMFC performance and efficiency. The results proposed that the compressed PFF outperformed the other designs in thermal characteristics, providing not only greater performance but also improved uniformity in gas and current density distributions. Whereas T.V. Reshetenko et al. [126] examined behaviour of SFF on PEMFC utilizing a segmented cell technique, which allows for the thorough investigation of current distribution across the cell. Using experimental methodologies and spatially distributed electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS), the study compared two types of FFs 6-channel and 10-channel SFF under varying circumstances. The data indicated that, while the 10-channel design marginally underperformed at lower currents due to increased pressure drop, it greatly improved performance at higher currents by enhancing mass transfer and lowering voltage losses caused by permeability and diffusion. This structure provides enhanced thermal management by permitting excellent gas distribution and moisture control, making it more suitable for high-current PEMFC applications. Chunhua Min et al. [127] also used a numerical simulation to investigate the influence of modified serially-linked SFFs on PEMFCs, with a special emphasis on secondary flow effects. This study demonstrated that designs with more channel routes (MSFF-1-7, MSFF-2-7, MSFF-3-7) greatly improved performance. These findings imply that among the studied designs, the MSFF-3-7, excels in optimizing thermal characteristics and total cell efficiency, making it the preferable choice for thermal management.

Significance

By studying SFFs and their effects on PEMFC thermal management, this research tends to fill a major gap in the literature. The unique contribution of our work is the comprehensive review of several experimental and computational research studies that show how SFFs improve thermal performance with an improved pressure drop approach. Our analysis provides new insights into optimizing PEMFC cooling solutions by evaluating SFF designs, fluid dynamics, and thermal behaviours. This research might lead to future advancements in PEMFC technology like improving pressure drop methods, efficiency, dependability, and lifetime of fuel cell devices. Based on the literature above this study intends to propose several innovative aspects which have great significance. Particularly focusing on the improvement of thermal efficiency of SFF by proposing the major issues which have not been extensively explored in prior studies (i.e. Pressure drop) and some innovative ways to combat those challenges (i.e. Varying the rib width to channel width and investigating the degrees of bend of an FF). The study also proposes some other innovative aspects i.e. 1. Hybrid Flow Field Designs 2. Sophisticated Manufacturing Techniques 3. Combination with Developing Technologies 4. Application of Nanofluids and Advanced Coolant Materials, which also have the potential to contribute in the improvement of thermal efficiency of PEMFC.

Critiques over CFD approaches

There are various common limitations in the CFD methods that many writers have used to study PEMFCs. A lot of these studies are based on the idea of uniform flow and ideal gas behaviour, which might not accurately reflect how things work in the real-world applications. The simulation results from most of the studies aren't very reliable because they don't have enough actual confirmation. Also, using set inlet mass flow rates, temperatures, and pressures as border conditions doesn't take into account short-term changes in operation, which could not very accurately predict the performance of PEMFC. The computational methods, which depend on the SIMPLE algorithm and the finite volume method, are strong, but they could be more accurate with higher-order schemes and transient studies (see Table 1).

In order to increase the accuracy and practicality of CFD research techniques for PEMFCs, a number of enhancements could be considered for better results in future studies. Integrating turbulence factors and non-ideal gas behaviour into the simulations models would yield a more accurate results of real-world operational circumstances. Additionally rigorous experimental validation is essential to confirm the simulation results and guarantee their dependability and adoptability. Applying adaptive meshing methods can decrease computing expenses while preserving precise resolution in crucial regions. Ultimately, incorporating more practical and diverse boundary conditions that replicate real-life fluctuations will improve the model's usefulness and precision in forecasting PEMFC performance. By considering these factors, future computational fluid dynamics (CFD) investigations might provide more accurate and dependable understanding of the heat regulation in PEMFCs. Discussion

This study provides a comprehensive understanding of some significant research studies that assess the effectiveness of SFFs compared to other flow configurations for thermal management in PEMFCs. The studies conducted by various researchers provide a comprehensive evaluation of the thermal performance of PEMFC with different cooling FFs, with the particular emphasis on SFFs in conjunction with bipolar or cooling plates. It examines various aspects of SFFs investigated by different researchers, such as temperature uniformity, cooling efficiency, pressure drop, and overall thermal performance. Multiple studies have shown that serpentine FFs are better than other designs in coolant distribution, heat transfer efficiency, and maintaining stable operating temperatures. The article also examined advancements such as Multi-Pass Serpentine Flow Fields (MPSFFs) and novel hybrid configurations that use a combination of conventional serpentine and alternative flow patterns to reduce pressure drop and enhance overall performance. This study proposes that future research should prioritize the integration of economic and environmental factors into FF design, while also investigating the potential of developing materials and technologies to improve the performance of PEMFCs (see Fig. 4).

The study conducted by S.M. Rahgoshay et al. [85] propose that highest temperature in a cathode catalyst layer of PEMFC is caused by the exothermic electrochemical reactions occurring in this layer. Additionally, it was noted that the temperature in catalyst layer is noticed higher than the temperature of the cooling FF at the inlet. The temperature gradient between the catalyst layer and cooling FF was found to be 2–3°, indicating the significance of thermal management and the contribution of thermal conductivity inside different layers of the FC. The maximum temperature in the parallel model exhibits variability compared to other models due to inadequate circulation of the cooling fluid in the core fields of the cooling FF, resulting in lower mass flow rate of water. Furthermore, the serpentine model exhibits the lowest maximum temperature due to its superior flow patterns which result in higher performance. As could be witnessed in Fig. 5 (a) that the temperature profile at the outlet was noticed higher in the model with SFF whereas in the model with PFF the temperature profile at the centre seems to be higher. It shows that the SFF is performs better in uniform temperature distribution resulting in fewer hot spots and the PFF exhibits lowest performance. Furthermore, the improvements in uniformity of temperature using MPSFFs and the spiral FF are noticed due to the reduction in Tmax and the increase in Tmin. Whereas, the increased pressure drop has also been seen in MPSFFs and the spiral FF due to complicated FF patterns of both fields. In Fig. 5 (b) it could be seen that the study M. Ghasemi et al. [116] also proposed similar thermal patterns as noticed by S.M. Rahgoshay et al. [85]. Further it could also be seen that the conventional SFF exhibits least temperature profile whereas the conventional PFF exhibits higher thermal values. They also proposed that the fluid's temperature increases upon entering the cooling field due to the absorption of the heat produced by the fuel cell's operation. This study proposes that the IUT is the most important component to consider when choosing the appropriate FF. It is observed that the lower value of IUT indicates a more uniformity temperature distribution. This study also emphasizes the importance of evaluating the pressure difference resulting from the cooling field configuration when choosing the appropriate IUT and temperature difference (DT). Fig. 5 (c) shows the least value of IUT in four section serpentine design studied by S.M Miri Joibary et al. [118] which makes this design superior in performance. Further they proposed that this design also performed best in terms of heat absorption, water accumulation, output power and overall performance. They also argued that a lower heat absorption value does not necessarily indicate poor performance in FCs. In fact, it can sometimes distribute temperature more uniform and enhance the performance of the FC. Fig. 5 (d) belongs to the study of Xuanyu Liu et al. [91] which shows that the parallel serpentine FF along the Y-Axis gives better and uniform temperature profiles while conventional PFF exhibits least uniformity in temperature distribution. Further they observed that reducing the amount of coolant manifolds in actual PEMFCs enhances the strength of BPs (BPs) and enhances the thermal management. The flow rate uniformity of coolant volume throughout the channels can be increased by lowering the number of channels in one coolant manifold. Whereas this reduction of coolant channels will lead to increased pressure drop in coolant FF which requires more detailed study. S. Ravishankar et al. [122] observed that pressure loss is higher in an area where the fluid is in transition state from clockwise to an anti-clockwise direction within each quadrant. In Fig. 5 (e) it could be seen that the maximum temperature drops as the Re increases for all the designs. Further they proposed that at higher Re, all of the designs produce a similar temperature distribution which may be because of the fact that at higher mass flow rate the fluid stays for a very short time in the FF. It could also be observed that the thermal value decreases much faster at increasing Re in the model with Design 1 which implies that the SFF performs better. The data set provided by Seung Ho Yu et al. [47] demonstrates that the uniformity index is decreased as the Re is increased. Increasing velocity of coolant is also found advantageous for dissipating heat of reaction because it increases the thermal mass and improves convective heat transfer. It is also indicated that the decrease in pressure is a crucial feature that must always be a prior consideration when building cooling plates. It could be noticed the higher flow velocity in cooling plates leads to enhanced cooling efficiency concerning temperature uniformity. However, this also increases the consumption of power to move the cooling fluid with higher velocity through the cooling FFs. However, it is evident in the study conducted by F. C. Chen et al. [41] that in PFF, the uneven distribution of fluid is caused by unsteady fluid flow. Whereas, in SFF, the fluid flow remains constant due to the continuous passage, proving that SFFs have superior capability in distributing cooling fluid compared to PFF. It has also been seen that the decreased pressure in PFF is smaller than that in SFF which is due to the shorter length of cooling passages in PFF. Therefore, it is advised to evaluate the length of FFs concisely to minimize the pressure drop while maintaining a desired temperature consistently across the cooling plate. Conclusion and future recommendation

A detailed investigation of some important research studies that measure how well SFFs work for managing heat in PEMFCs versus other flow configurations is given in this study. Different researchers' studies give a wider picture of how well PEMFCs handle heat when they are exposed to different cooling FFs. All the studies are focused on SFFs when used with bipolar plates or cooling plates. The SFF is an efficient cooling mode as it minimizes the occurrence of hot spots and the maximum temperature (Tmax) in an active area of FC.

While integrating several approaches for thermal management in PEMFCs, some notable issues were faced. A consistent challenge in numerous investigations was the attainment of a uniform temperature distribution within FCs. S.M. Rahgoshay et al. [85] and Seung Ho Yu et al. [47] conducted studies that revealed the existence of temperature gradients and hot spots in conventional flow field designs. These factors had a negative effect on the FC's performance and durability. M. Ghasemi et al. [116] and Xuanyu Liu et al. [91] have encountered similar problems, including the challenges of achieving consistent thermal management in various FF designs.

Another prominent issue was of effective handling the pressure losses. The studies conducted by S. Shimpalee et al. [78] and Jun Shen et al. [125] have shown that when there are substantial pressure drops in different FF designs, it results in higher parasitic power consumption, which in turn decreases the overall efficiency of the PEMFCs. The researchers focused on achieving an ideal balance between efficient cooling and tolerable pressure reductions. They aimed to identify setups that minimize pressure losses while maximizing cooling efficiency.

Achieving a uniform distribution of coolant was another of considerable challenges. Seung Man Baek et al. [87] and Y. Vazifeshenas et al. [121] discovered that an irregular flow of coolant led to localised overheating and undercooling, which made the thermal management process more complex. Furthermore, the optimization of geometric parameters in cooling channels, as emphasized by A. Hamrang et al. [119] and G Amarnath et al. [120], was a complex task due to the complex equilibrium that was necessary between the flow distribution, rib width, and channel height.

To tackle these difficulties, future studies should prioritize investigating novel cooling flow field configurations that effectively balance temperature regulation and pressure reduction. Hybrid designs that combine the advantages of serpentine and parallel designs have the potential to improve temperature uniformity and decrease pressure losses. Implementing variable flow rates for coolant channels, as recommended in several studies, may improve temperature uniformity and decrease hot spots by modifying flow rates according to local temperature circumstances.

There are also promising opportunities to enhance thermal management through material innovations. Utilizing modern materials, such as graphene composites or advanced metal alloys, which possess superior thermal conductivity and improved mechanical qualities, has the potential to greatly boost heat transfer rates and overall cooling efficiency. In order to guarantee the reliability and practicality of simulation models, it is essential to strengthen and refine those models through improved experimental validation. The validation of CFD models and the enhancement of their accuracy could be facilitated by the collection of comprehensive experimental data under a variety of operational conditions.

Research has demonstrated that SFFs enhance important performance measures, such as the IUT, up to a much greater extent when compared to PFFs. Various studies have also shown that the serpentine design enhances the efficiency of distributing cooling fluid across the FC. This is crucial for maintaining the ideal operational temperatures throughout the FC. Despite that the SFFs are beneficial for heat management, they often result in increased pressure drops, as indicated by certain studies. This can lead to greater operational costs and reduced overall efficiency. Similarly, SFFs demonstrate superior temperature uniformity compared to parallel and other configurations, leading to enhanced thermal management and reduced likelihood of hot spots that could potentially damage cell components. Research has shown that the dispersion of coolant within the cooling plate in SFF is frequently efficient, resulting in superior performance of SFF as compared to other FFs. Prior research has shown that the pressure drop in SFFs is relatively significant, which is undesirable. Therefore, it is necessary to implement effective strategies to reduce the pressure drop in SFF. Until now, very few studies have been carried out based on the enhanced pressure drop approach in SFFs of PEMFCs.

Therefore, it is concluded that the SFFs have the potential to be analysed in various ways to improve thermal efficiency through the enhanced pressure drop approach. This can be achieved in many ways i.e. 1. Altering the number of flow paths. 2. Variating the lengths of channel paths. 3. Modifying the diameters of the flow channels. 4. Variating the ratio of rib width to channel width. 5. Investigating the degrees of bends. 6. Utilizing materials with higher thermal conductivity. Several studies have suggested that MPSFFs are the most efficient FFs because of their enhanced pressure drop capability. Therefore, future studies should also consider investigating above recommendations to get beneficial outcomes. By taking into account the width of the channel, depth of the FF, distance between FFs, number of FFs, and turns, it is possible to build more efficient FFs. These parameters have been identified as the most powerful factors in the study which influence the overall performance of FF. Hybrid FF designs that integrate characteristics of both serpentine and other flow patterns have the ability to achieve improved performance and enhanced pressure drop, indicating a promising area for further development. Utilizing sophisticated manufacturing techniques enables the production of intricate FF geometries that may be customized to meet specific operational requirements, hence improving the flexibility and effectiveness of PEMFCs. Combining SFFs with other developing technologies like real-time monitoring systems and innovative materials also have big potential to greatly enhance the efficiency, durability, and operating versatility of PEMFCs. Investigating the application of nanofluids or other sophisticated coolant materials could enhance the cooling efficiency of SFFs.

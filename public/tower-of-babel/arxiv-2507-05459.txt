The degree condition in Llarull’s theorem on scalar curvature rigidity
Christian Bär, Rudolf Zeidler

Abstract
. Llarull’s scalar curvature rigidity theorem states that a 1 1 -Lipschitz map f : M → 𝕊 n f\colon M\to\mathbb{S}^{n} from a closed connected Riemannian spin manifold M M with scalar curvature scal ≥ n ⁡ ( n − 1 ) \mathrm{scal}\geq n(n-1) to the standard sphere 𝕊 n \mathbb{S}^{n} is an isometry if the degree of f f is nonzero. We investigate if one can replace the condition deg ⁡ ( f ) ≠ 0 \deg(f)\neq 0 by the weaker condition that f f is surjective. The answer turns out to be “no” for n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 but “yes” for n = 2 n=2 . If we replace the scalar curvature by Ricci curvature, the answer is “yes” in all dimensions.

Introduction

In 1998 Llarull published the following rigidity theorem for scalar curvature (

*Theorem A).

\triangleright

,
5
,
4
,

]

\triangleright

If

,

\triangleright

The target manifold

]

\triangleright

The scalar curvature condition and the contraction property of

\triangleright

The manifold

,

\triangleright

In the present note we investigate to what extent the condition
\deg(f)\neq 0
can be relaxed.
One certainly cannot just drop it, as a constant map
f
would immediately lead to a counterexample.
One needs a condition that ensures that the map
f

“wraps around the sphere”
in a certain sense.
Therefore, we ask the following question:

Question
.

Does Llarull’s theorem still hold if we replace the condition
\deg(f)\neq 0
by the weaker condition that
f
is surjective?

We will see that the answer is
“yes”
in the case of dimension
n=2
and that it is
“no”
in dimensions
n\geq 3
.

Let
\mathbb{S}^{n}=(S^{n},g_{\mathrm{std}})
denote the
n
-dimensional sphere with the standard metric of constant sectional curvature
1
and by
\mathbb{S}_{\delta}^{n}=(S^{n},\delta\cdot g_{\mathrm{std}})
the
n
-sphere with radius
\delta>0
and constant curvature
\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}
.

Let
V
and
W
be two
n
-dimensional oriented Euclidean vector spaces.
For any linear map
A\colon V\to W
we denote by
\Lambda^{n}A\colon\Lambda^{n}V\to\Lambda^{n}W
the induced linear map on the maximal exterior powers.
Since
\Lambda^{n}V
and
\Lambda^{n}W
are one-dimensional, oriented and carry an induced scalar product, there is a canonical identification
\Lambda^{n}V\cong\mathbb{R}\cong\Lambda^{n}W
.
With respect to this identification, we can view
\Lambda^{n}A
as acting by multiplication with a real number which we denote by
\det(A)
.
If
0\leq\mu_{1}\leq\dots\leq\mu_{n}
are the singular values of
A
, then
\lvert\det(A)\rvert=\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{n}
.
Note that the quantity
\lvert\det(A)\rvert
is independent of the chosen orientations on
V
and
W
.

We start with the following result showing that the analogous question for Ricci curvature has a positive answer:

Theorem A
.

Let
M
be a connected closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 2
with Ricci curvature
\Ric\geq n-1
.
Let
f\colon M\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
be a surjective Lipschitz map such that
\lvert\det(d_{x}f)\rvert\leq 1
for almost all
x\in M
.

Then
M
is isometric to
\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
f
is a bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism.
If, moreover,
f
is
1
-Lipschitz, then it is an isometry.

Note that by
[
16
]
*Theorem 8, a metric isometry between smooth Riemannian manifolds is a smooth Riemannian isometry.

Also observe that the
1
-Lipschitz case of
Theorem

A
is a consequence of Bishop–Gromov volume comparison and the well-known
“Lipschitz-volume rigidity”
principle (see e.g.
[
3
]
*Lemma 9.1).
Our proof of
Theorem

A
relies on a variant of the latter tailored to our purposes.
In particular, this answers the question from above positively in dimension
n=2
, including a treatment of not necessarily smooth Lipschitz maps which are merely nonexpanding on
2
-vectors:

Corollary
.

Let
M
be a connected closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
n=2
with Gauss curvature
K\geq 1
.
Let
f\colon M\to\mathbb{S}^{2}
be a surjective Lipschitz map such that the map induced by
d_{x}f
on
2
-vectors in
\Lambda^{2}TM
is nonexpanding for almost all
x\in M
.

Then
M
is isometric to
\mathbb{S}^{2}
and
f
is a bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism.
If, moreover,
f
is
1
-Lipschitz, then it is an isometry.

In contrast, for scalar curvature the situation in higher dimensions is completely different as our second theorem shows.

Theorem B
.

Let
M
and
N
be connected closed smooth manifolds of dimension
n\geq 3
.
Assume that
M
admits Riemannian metrics with positive scalar curvature and let
N
carry any Riemannian metric
g_{N}
.

Then for each
S_{0}>0
and
\varepsilon>0
there exists a Riemannian metric
g_{M}
on
M
with
\mathrm{scal}_{g_{M}}\geq S_{0}

Applying

shows that the answer to the question is negative in dimensions at least

Note that the spin condition plays no role in our considerations as no Dirac operator methods will be used.

Proposition 2
.

Let
M
and
N
be connected closed Riemannian manifolds of the same dimension
n\geq 1
.
Let
f\colon M\to N
be an
L
-Lipschitz map such that
\mathsf{vol}(M)\leq\mathsf{vol}_{N}(f(M))
and
\lvert\det(df)\rvert\leq 1
almost everywhere.

Then
f
is a homeomorphism and
f^{-1}\colon N\to M
is
L^{n-1}
-Lipschitz.
In particular, if
f
is
1
-Lipschitz, it is an isometry.
Moreover, for every measurable subset
A\subseteq M
, we have
\mathsf{vol}_{N}(f(A))=\mathsf{vol}_{M}(A)
.

Proof.

Let
A\subseteq M
be measurable.
By the coarea formula for Lipschitz maps, the fiber
f^{-1}(y)
is finite for almost all
y\in N
, and we obtain

\displaystyle\mathsf{vol}_{M}(A)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{M}1_{A}\lvert\det(d_{x}f)\rvert\,\mathsf{dV}_{M}=\int_{N}\sum_{x\in f^{-1}(y)}1_{A}(x)\,\mathsf{dV}_{N}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{N}\#(f^{-1}(y)\cap A)\,\mathsf{dV}_{N}(y)\geq\mathsf{vol}_{N}(f(A)).

Applying this to
A=M
and using the assumption
\mathsf{vol}(M)\leq\mathsf{vol}_{N}(f(M))
, we obtain equality in each inequality, which implies that
\lvert\det(d_{x}f)\rvert=1
for almost all
x\in M
and
\#f^{-1}(y)=1
for almost all
y\in f(M)
.
Thus we actually have equality in the previous chain of inequalities for any measurable subset
A\subseteq M
which shows that
\mathsf{vol}_{M}(A)=\mathsf{vol}_{N}(f(A))
.

In particular, the set of injectivity points has full measure in
M
.
Here, by

f
.
Note that for each injectivity point

\deg_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(f\rvert_{x})=1

Finding an injectivity point

SBT-Net: a tri-cue guided multimodal fusion framework for depression recognition
Authors: Yujie Huo, Weng Howe Chan, Ahmad Najmi Bin Amerhaider Nuar, Hongyu Gao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractEarly detection of depression is vital for public health, yet current multimodal methods often struggle with challenges such as modality incompleteness, semantic inconsistency, and emotional temporal fluctuation. To address these issues, this paper proposes SBT-Net, a novel Semantic-Bias-Trend guided framework for robust depression detection from audio and text data. The model integrates three innovative modules: a semantically guided cross-modal gating (SGCMG) mechanism that dynamically filters effective modality features based on global semantic cues, a bias-guided tensor product attention (BG-TPA) mechanism that enhances fine-grained fusion and alignment between modalities, and an emotion trend modeling (ETM) module that captures the temporal evolution of depressive emotional states.We evaluate SBT-Net using two widely adopted benchmark datasets: DAIC-WOZ, which contains 189 interview sessions, and EATD-Corpus, comprising 162 conversational samples. Experimental results show that SBT-Net achieves excellent performance in multiple indicators, including 93.0% accuracy, 0.93 F1 score, and 0.92 recall, all of which surpass the competitive baselines.Ablation studies further validate the individual and synergistic contributions of each proposed module.These findings highlight the potential of integrating semantic guidance, bias-aware fusion, and emotional trend modeling to advance multimodal depression detection solutions. The source code can be found at https://github.com/ghy-yhg/SBT-Net.Keywords: Multimodal depression recognition, Modal gating mechanism, Bias-guided tensor product attention, Emotional trend modeling

Introduction

Background and research status

Depression is a common and serious mental health problem [1, 2], which affects a wide range of areas, including personal emotions [3], cognitive abilities [4], and social interaction abilities [5]. According to statistics from the World Health Organization (WHO), depression affects more than 300 million people worldwide and is one of the main causes of disability and disease burden. Good mental health is an integral part of well-being and reflects a person’s psychological, emotional, social, and behavioral state [6]. On the other hand, if a person suffers from certain mental health-related problems, it may lead to serious problems and needs to be discovered at an early stage. However, due to the high heterogeneity and hidden nature of depression, many patients fail to receive timely diagnosis and treatment, resulting in worsening of their condition and even serious consequences [7]. Although there are many effective treatments for mental illness, more than 75% of people in low- and middle-income countries do not receive any form of treatment [8].

In addition, depression is a heterogeneous syndrome, which leads to the complexity of correct diagnosis [9]. Psychiatrists mainly rely on experience and depression self-rating scales to identify subjects at risk of depression. Commonly used self-rating scales include the Beck Depression Inventory (BDI) [10] and the Patient Health Questionnaire (PHQ) [11]. In fact, when individuals fill out self-rating scales, they may conceal their true condition. In the case of an unbalanced doctor-patient ratio, it will also cause great pressure on psychologists, and the detection efficiency and reliability of the results cannot be guaranteed. Consequently, advancing automated evaluation of depression-associated biological markers is pivotal for addressing the inherent limitations of conventional diagnostic methodologies [12]. Therefore, the identification of depression is an important research direction.

This research focuses on multimodal depression recognition utilizing audio and textual data. Unlike facial data acquisition, which poses practical challenges in daily monitoring and raises privacy concerns, audio signals can be unobtrusively captured via ambient sensing technologies. Empirical studies consistently demonstrate [13] that vocal characteristics—including prosodic variations, speech tempo, intonation dynamics, and fluency metrics—serve as robust biomarkers for depressive states, correlating with chronic emotional instability [14]. Clinical evaluations frequently leverage these acoustic anomalies, as manifested in patients’ vocal patterns, to support diagnostic procedures. Over the past few years, a large number of researchers have made significant efforts to explore methods for detecting and predicting depression, mainly by collecting and observing the mental states of participants, such as using machine learning models for recognition [15–17]. However, due to the low recognition accuracy of machine learning on small samples, and as the number of samples increases, the complexity of the model rises, leading to catastrophic forgetting in machine learning. This results in poor learning outcomes, and the model fails to learn more important features, thus unable to accurately predict depression. In response to the above issues, it is urgent for researchers to explore a more objective and efficient depression recognition method based on existing clinical diagnostic tools. Serving as an important supplementary tool for clinical judgment, this has become a research hotspot in the field of early depression identification.

To address these challenges, some researchers have adopted single-modal features for depression recognition [18, 19]. However, information from a single modality may fail to comprehensively capture the complex manifestations of depression [20–24]. For instance, textual information is constrained by an individual’s language proficiency, while the audio modality is susceptible to feature shifts caused by the recording environment and emotional fluctuations. Nevertheless, these modalities offer complementary information. The audio modality conveys non-verbal emotional signals such as speech rate, intonation, and volume, which are crucial for identifying emotional states. In contrast, the text modality provides specific linguistic content, like word choices and syntactic structures, that can reveal depressive tendencies [25]. Therefore, multimodal approaches that integrate both audio and text are widely recognized for their potential to improve the accuracy and robustness of depression detection.

Challenges and limitations of existing methods

Despite advancements, multimodal depression recognition faces several fundamental challenges that limit the applicability of current models. First, achieving deep fusion in the face of affective discrepancy across modalities remains a primary issue. Proposed solutions range from the shallow fusion of concatenating features, as seen in baselines like Shen et al. [26], to more advanced methods like the channel attention mechanism in Xue et al. [27] and the complex text-guided feature reconstruction and decomposition framework of Chen et al. [28]. While these sophisticated approaches show progress, they highlight a persistent need for methods that can directly model fine-grained, high-order semantic alignments without relying on intricate, multi-stage pipelines. In this context, fine-grained alignment refers to the process of establishing detailed correspondences where specific lexical units in text, such as individual words or phrases indicative of depressive mood, are mapped to precise acoustic events in speech, including a specific pause, pitch drop, or change in vocal energy. This contrasts with coarse-grained approaches that only correlate entire utterances. Concurrently, high-order semantics involves capturing complex, non-linear interdependencies among features across modalities. For instance, rather than merely forming a simple, low-order pairwise link between a negative word and a flat intonation, a high-order model could capture how the co-occurrence of semantic uncertainty in text and vocal tremor in audio jointly signifies a heightened depressive state, a pattern that remains invisible to simpler models. Second, existing models often fail to capture the long-term temporal variation of depressive emotions. For instance, the sophisticated GCN and LSTM architecture proposed by Jin et al. [29] is highly effective at modeling the short-term evolution of audio features within local time windows. However, this focus on micro-level dynamics is less suited for tracking the slow, overarching drift in affective state over an entire interview, a key indicator of depression. Finally, modality incompleteness due to factors like signal noise severely undermines model robustness in real-world scenarios. The difficulty of this problem is underscored by the emergence of highly complex solutions, such as the knowledge distillation framework by Li et al. [30] designed to reconstruct missing semantics from incomplete data. The complexity of state-of-the-art approaches highlights the limitations of traditional methods, such as case deletion and late fusion [31], which combine outputs at the decision level. These methods are inadequate as they either discard valuable data or fail to capture crucial early cross-modal interactions.

Research questions and objectives

In summary, modality loss, cross-modal heterogeneity, and emotional dynamics pose challenges to automatic depression detection methods. This study aims to investigate how to effectively extract, align, and fuse multimodal information from text and audio for accurate depression detection. Specifically, the objective is to construct a robust recognition framework capable of handling modality incompleteness, semantic inconsistency, and emotional temporal fluctuation, by proposing a novel model architecture that integrates semantic-guided gating, bias-aware cross-modal attention, and temporal emotion trend modeling.

Research results and implications

This study introduces SBT-Net, a tri-cue guided multimodal depression detection model that addresses three core challenges in emotion modeling: modality missing, semantic inconsistency, and emotional temporal fluctuation. By integrating three synergistic modules Semantic-Guided Cross-Modal Gating (SGCMG), Bias-Guided Tensor Product Attention (BG-TPA), and Emotion Trend Modeling (ETM)—SBT-Net enables robust representation learning under incomplete modality conditions, while effectively capturing fine-grained cross-modal alignment and the temporal evolution of depressive emotion. Extensive experiments conducted on two widely used public datasets, DAIC-WOZ and EATD-Corpus, demonstrate that SBT-Net significantly outperforms strong baseline models, achieving an accuracy of 93%, an F1-score of 0.93, and a recall of 0.92. Ablation studies further verify the individual and joint contributions of the proposed modules. Moreover, SBT-Net exhibits strong adaptability in low-resource and streaming environments, highlighting its practical utility for early-stage depression screening. These results suggest that integrating semantic guidance, bias-aware fusion, and emotion dynamics modeling provides a promising direction for scalable, interpretable solutions in multimodal affective computing and mental health monitoring. Related work

Subsequently, we focus on more specific challenges related to Dynamic Emotion Perception and Few-Shot Robust Learning under Multimodal Semantic Modeling, as detailed in Section “Dynamic emotion perception and few-shot robust learning under multimodal semantic modeling”, examining advanced fusion techniques applied to these particular problems.

Multimodal fusion mechanisms and cross-modal collaboration strategies

With the development of multimodal learning, an increasing number of studies attempt to integrate textual, audio, and visual information to improve the accuracy of depression recognition. Although existing methods have achieved significant progress on standard datasets, several common issues still prevail, restricting their practical application. In terms of cross-modal feature alignment and fusion, Lim et al. [33] simplified the audio model by introducing the MLP-Mixer structure, reducing training parameters. However, their fusion strategy remains at a shallow combination level, failing to fully explore the deep semantic correlations between modalities. Similarly, although Zhang et al.’s MM-DepNet [34] combines knowledge-driven and data-driven mechanisms for depression tendency recognition, it lacks dynamic adaptive alignment capabilities when facing modal heterogeneity and feature inconsistency issues. The language-driven unified modeling method for audio and text proposed by Morales et al. [35] alleviates the information fragmentation between modalities to some extent, but the overall fusion still relies on the dominance of a single modality, limiting the fusion effectiveness.

Triantafyllopoulos et al. [36] and Manjulatha et al. [37] enhanced textual and visual representations through attention mechanisms and multimodal deep feature extraction methods, respectively. However, their modeling of differences in data distributions across modalities remains relatively coarse, affecting the consistency and robustness of fusion. De Melo et al. [38] captured facial behavior features in videos through a multi-scale spatio-temporal network. Jin et al. [29] analyzed multimodal features by combining graph convolutional networks and long short-term memory networks. Nevertheless, both mainly focus on static feature extraction and lack dynamic modeling of emotional evolution trends and the progression of depression. Zhang et al. [39] constructed a visual-auditory-based depression detection model (DepITCM) to further enhance the fine-grained fusion of temporal, channel, and spatial information. Although Zhu et al.’s MTNet [40] integrates eye movement and electroencephalogram data to achieve joint local-global feature modeling, its temporal modeling strategy is relatively simple, making it difficult to capture the slow evolution of emotional states over long time scales. Dong et al. [41] developed a non-contact dual-modal network DECNet for driver emotion detection. Nykoniuk et al. [42] proposed a multimodal fusion network combining Bi-LSTM and self-attention mechanisms, enhancing local feature learning through temporal dependency modeling and semantic focusing. Zhang et al. [43] constructed a text-audio-video multimodal model, using pre-trained sentence embeddings and BiLSTM for textual temporal modeling and combining PCA-SVM for audio classification. Yang et al. [44] achieved key modality mining through RLKT-MDD. However, both methods still lack robustness in handling high-noise or extreme environments, failing to meet the requirements of uncontrollable multimodal data quality in practical applications. In addition, although some works, such as Li et al.’s [45] method of extracting lexical features based on MacBERT and enhancing context modeling with Transformer, achieved good results on standard test sets, their generalization ability in cross-lingual and cross-cultural scenarios has not been fully verified. Wang et al.’s [46] DeepEmotionnet innovatively integrated multi-level language features systematically. By fusing background context, syntactic structures, semantic relationships, and document-level features, and combining with a language knowledge base based on emotion lexicons, they constructed a composite feature model for overall emotion detection, demonstrating significant performance improvements in emotion classification tasks. On the other hand, Wu et al. [47] made breakthroughs in mental health computing. They were the first to systematically define the key elements of personalized information in text-driven depression detection and then proposed a Personalized Information Embedding (PIE) model. This innovative framework effectively bridges the semantic gap between clinical diagnostic criteria features and patients’ personalized experience expressions through a novel representation learning method, providing a new technical path for personalized mental health assessment. Notably, although these two studies have made significant progress in single-modal text analysis, they are limited to the analysis of a single text modality and have not expanded to the multimodal field, which restricts the model’s ability to handle complex multi-source information in real-world scenarios to some extent. Zhang et al. [48] designed a dual attention aggregation architecture to improve temporal modeling and key frame selection through feature alignment and sequence encoding modules. Although these methods perform well on standard datasets, their performance degrades in scenarios with missing modalities or noise interference.

To address these issues, Chen et al. [49] proposed the IIFDD framework, adopting a hierarchical fusion strategy to enhance cross-corpus detection capabilities. Ahmed et al. [50], Li et al. [30], and Zhang et al. [51] partially solved the problem of incomplete modalities through uncertainty modeling and missing modality compensation mechanisms. However, these methods have two key limitations. On the one hand, shallow feature concatenation and low-order alignment strategies are difficult to capture high-order semantic correlations across modalities. On the other hand, the adaptive processing mechanisms for asynchronous multimodal data urgently need to be strengthened. These problems are particularly prominent in the absence of modal bias modeling and dynamic recalibration mechanisms, leading to a sharp decline in system performance when facing asynchronous or degraded-quality multimodal datasets.While recent works advance fusion through multi-scale convolutions as in Xu et al. [52] or representation shifting as in Lin et al. [53], our tri-cue framework is distinct in its focus on simultaneously addressing channel-wise noise, explicit high-order alignment, and long-term emotional trends.

Dynamic emotion perception and few-shot robust learning under multimodal semantic modeling

To more effectively capture the evolution trajectory of depressive emotions, many models focus on temporal modeling and the collaborative optimization of inter-modal representations. Shangguan et al. [54] proposed an ADDMIL, a depression classification method based on weak supervision learning. Through an innovative multi-instance learning dual-stream aggregator, it simultaneously considers instance-level (max-pooling) and bag-level (attention weights) features, providing a new framework for mental illness diagnosis. Researchers [55–58] proposed methods such as modeling the spatio-temporal features of EEG signals with convolutional interaction Transformer networks, fusing audio-visual information with multimodal feature aggregation frameworks, processing speech features with lightweight deep neural networks, and capturing emotional dynamic changes with multi-domain feature capsule Transformer networks. Han et al. [59] maintained cross-modal consistency by maximizing the mutual information between modalities. Zhang et al. [60] developed a hierarchical attention mechanism to enhance the adaptability of multimodal fusion, while Inoue et al.’s [61] hierarchical emotional distribution (ED) model achieved multi-grained emotion modeling at the phoneme-word-sentence levels. Fang et al.’s [62] multi-level attention mechanism (MFM-Att) effectively reduced the information redundancy of multi-source data through intra-modal feature selection and inter-modal importance weighting. However, existing methods still have deficiencies in handling modal asynchrony and drift issues. There is an urgent need to develop more adaptive attention architectures to ensure the stability of long-term emotion perception. In terms of capturing subtle emotional expressions, most methods also have limitations. For example, although Li et al.’s dual-stream model SFTNet [63] for depression recognition based on micro-expression recognition can capture facial features at time nodes and full-time features and achieve depression state recognition through decision fusion; Yang et al. [64] obtained biologically meaningful acoustic features using learnable temporal filters and retained effective frequency sub-bands through multi-scale spectral attention learning; Yu et al. [65] proposed a self-supervised label generation framework, which simultaneously captures inter-modal consistency features and modality-specific representations by jointly optimizing multimodal and single-modal task objectives. This framework further introduces a dynamic weight adjustment mechanism to adaptively calibrate loss weights based on the learning progress, achieving balanced optimization of multi-task learning. Han et al. [59] optimized multimodal representations using feedback-aware mechanisms, and Su et al. [66] predicted depression from social media data using unsupervised learning and distillation techniques. However, the distillation process is sensitive to the loss of emotional semantics. The above studies lack systematic modeling of bias-guided cross-modal correlations, which limits the accuracy of capturing dynamic emotional evolution features and makes it difficult to improve the modeling ability for heterogeneous emotional expressions.

In addition, current mainstream multimodal depression datasets (such as DAIC-WOZ) are generally limited by insufficient sample size and imbalanced ratios between the depression and control groups, severely weakening the model’s generalization ability. In response to these challenges, recent studies have proposed some effective improvement solutions: Ravi et al. [67] decoupled speaker identity from depression-related acoustic features through domain adversarial training and constructed a privacy-protected speech processing framework, improving detection accuracy while avoiding identity leakage; Yan et al. [68] designed a context-enhanced depression perception network, alleviating class imbalance and semantic modeling deficiencies by strengthening depression-related semantic contexts. However, their method still has the risk of overfitting the training set, leading to a decline in cross-domain generalization performance. Notably, Tian et al.’s [69] Robust Temporal Graph Network (RTGN) shows that enhancing temporal-semantic alignment through adversarial training can reduce the dependence on data size, achieving a 15% increase in the F1 score in low-data scenarios. Although these methods have made progress in specific dimensions, the insufficient modeling of cross-modal high-order interactions and the lack of dynamic bias calibration mechanisms still restrict the application effectiveness in low-quality multimodal scenarios.

In summary, existing studies have several shortcomings. On the one hand, existing methods lack adaptability to noise interference, missing modalities, and asynchronous multimodal data. The root cause lies in the shallowness and low-order nature of cross-modal feature alignment strategies, which are difficult to capture high-order semantic correlations and lack dynamic recalibration mechanisms to deal with modal biases. Secondly, regarding the stability of long-term emotion perception, existing attention architectures have limited capabilities in modeling modal drift and lack systematic bias-guided cross-modal correlation modeling, resulting in limited accuracy in capturing dynamic emotional evolution features and heterogeneous expressions. On the other hand, insufficient data size and imbalanced class distributions (such as the scarcity of depression samples) under few-shot conditions further exacerbate the model’s generalization bottleneck. These issues collectively reveal the deficiencies of current methods in temporal alignment rigor, semantic modeling depth, and low-resource adaptability, restricting their practical application potential in open environments.

Methodology

To address the aforementioned limitations, this study focuses on mining robust depressive emotional expression features from two modalities: audio and text. We propose a novel multimodal depression recognition model based on a Semantic-Bias-Trend guided framework (SBT-Net). Grounded in experiments on the DAIC-WOZ dataset, the model is designed to tackle challenges such as heterogeneous emotional expressions, modal loss, and few-shot scenarios in real interview settings. It enhances overall recognition performance and generalization ability through refined module combinations and multi-strategy training schemes, aiming to identify depressive emotions from both DAIC-WOZ and EATD-Corpus.

SBT-Net consists of three core modules: the Semantic-Guided Cross-Modal Gating mechanism (SGCMG), the Bias-Guided Tensor Product Attention mechanism (BG-TPA), and the Emotional Trend Modeling module (ETM) (Fig. 2). SBT-Net is motivated by three key challenges in multimodal depression recognition: semantic inconsistency, modality imbalance, and emotional fluctuation. To address these, we introduce a tri-cue fusion strategy where SGCMG enhances robustness by using text-guided gating to suppress noisy audio channels, BG-TPA improves alignment precision through bilinear attention with a semantic bias matrix, and ETM captures long-range emotional trends to model symptom progression. These components offer complementary benefits in semantic selectivity, cross-modal expressiveness, and temporal sensitivity. The SGCMG module leverages semantic information extracted from the text modality to perform gating regulation on the feature channels of the audio modality. This enhances the model’s ability to suppress invalid modalities in noisy environments and improves overall robustness. The BG-TPA module introduces a bias-guided tensor interaction mechanism to capture fine-grained cross-modal semantic correlations, thereby enhancing semantic alignment between different modalities. The ETM module employs position-aware convolutional encoding and sequence modeling mechanisms to effectively track the evolution trends of emotions during dialogues, enabling the characterization of high-order emotional changes. Finally, the fused multimodal features are fed into a classification layer to predict an individual’s depressive state.

SBT-Net model structure

Modal loss-aware modeling: Semantic-Guided Cross-Modal Gating mechanism (SGCMG)

In multimodal depression recognition tasks, the audio modality often contains noisy, missing, or redundant signals due to factors such as recording devices, background environments, and language styles, severely degrading model performance. Traditional fusion methods, including concatenation, averaging, or attention mechanisms, are ineffective in handling incomplete audio signals and may introduce semantic misguidance. Inspired by Cho et al. [70], the proposed SGCMG module incorporates a gating mechanism to enhance information flow control. However, unlike GRU which operates temporally on hidden states within a single modality, SGCMG performs channel-wise cross-modal modulation, using global semantic vectors extracted from the textual modality to guide the selection and compression of acoustic features, which differs from representation-level shifting mechanisms [53] by directly modulating audio feature channels for targeted noise suppression. This shift from temporal to semantic gating reflects the core novelty of our approach. Moreover, our design is substantially different from the semantic retrieval pipeline proposed by Pérez et al. [71], which formulates depression severity estimation as a semantic search and rank aggregation task over pre-indexed training sentences. Their model relies on SBERT-based sentence similarity and does not perform end-to-end training. In contrast, SGCMG leverages text-derived global representations as semantic control signals to directly modulate the feature channels of the audio encoder, supporting differentiable and fully integrated training without the need for symptom indexing or external sentence sets.

In this study, the audio data undergoes preprocessing to extract initial features, which are then fed into the SGCMG module as audio hidden state features. Meanwhile, the text data is preprocessed to obtain initial features as text hidden states, serving as the semantic guidance source for SGCMG. By dynamically adjusting perceptual features based on semantics, the SGCMG module effectively achieves signal quality awareness and selective information enhancement grounded in cross-modal semantics.

Lightweight design and complexity analysis

A key aspect of the BG-TPA module is its lightweight design, which achieves powerful high-order feature interaction with high parameter efficiency. The core of the BG-TPA mechanism is the bilinear transformation involving the semantic bias matrix . When compared to a standard cross-attention block in a Transformer architecture, the lightweight nature of BG-TPA becomes even more apparent. While the multi-head attention mechanism itself has a comparable number of parameters, a full Transformer block also includes a large position-wise Feed-Forward Network (FFN), which often contains the majority of the block’s parameters. Our BG-TPA module is designed to provide rich cross-modal interaction without the need for a subsequent large FFN, making it a more parameter-efficient choice for fusion. This efficiency is crucial for preventing overfitting and ensuring good generalization on specialized, medium-sized datasets like those used for depression recognition.

High-order semantic modeling: Emotion Trend Modeling Module (ETM)

The progressive, latent [73], and context-dependent nature [74] of depressive emotions poses significant challenges for traditional analytical methods. Depressive tendencies often manifest as subtle affective patterns, characterized by persistent baseline shifts in emotional states, periodic fluctuations in speech prosody, and latent disruptions in semantic coherence. Such temporal dynamics render conventional approaches—relying on static features or isolated time-point evaluations—prone to overlooking the evolutionary patterns and gradual symptom progression. To address this, the ETM module (Fig. 2) employs a dual-layer architecture combining temporal modeling and global aggregation, which simultaneously captures fine-grained temporal variations in speech features and distills holistic emotion evolution patterns. Through deep analysis of long-term speech signals, this module effectively identifies persistent yet imperceptible emotional patterns critical for depression detection. The ETM module’s efficacy is rooted in a two-stage process. First, a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) leverages its internal gating mechanisms to dynamically process the audio sequence. This allows the model to capture fine-grained temporal variations by selectively retaining salient acoustic cues and discarding irrelevant information over time. Subsequently, a global average pooling layer aggregates the sequence of the GRU’s context-aware hidden states. This summarization step averages out momentary fluctuations to distill the holistic emotion evolution patterns into a single, stable vector that represents the speaker’s overall affective state for downstream classification.

Experiments

Dataset

This study conducted experiments on two depression datasets, DAIC-WOZ [32] and EATD-Corpus [26]. Although both DAIC-WOZ and EATD-Corpus are relatively small in sample size, they are widely recognized and frequently used in multimodal depression detection research due to their high-quality, multi-channel annotations and clinically validated labels. These datasets provide synchronized audio, text, and behavioral data collected in controlled interview environments, offering rich and reliable depressive cues for modeling. Moreover, the limited size reflects real-world constraints in collecting privacy-sensitive clinical data, making them highly suitable for developing robust models under low-resource conditions. Our method is specifically designed to cope with such challenges, demonstrating strong performance and generalization even with limited data availability. Table 1 presents the class distribution of depressive and non-depressive samples in each dataset. All experiments adhered strictly to the principle of subject-independent partitioning, ensuring that samples from the same subject never appeared in both the training and test sets simultaneously, thereby preventing data leakage. Notably, both datasets exhibited significant class imbalance, with the number of non-depressive samples far exceeding that of depressive samples. To mitigate the bias introduced by this imbalance during model training, a class weighting strategy was employed. This approach assigned higher weights to the minority class (depressive samples), balancing the contribution of different classes to model parameter updates. In the evaluation phase, metrics suitable for imbalanced classification tasks, such as the F1-score and Recall, were adopted to comprehensively assess model performance and avoid evaluation biases caused by unequal sample distributions. Analysis of the model’s confusion matrix revealed that the proposed method did not exhibit significant overfitting on the majority class (non-depressive samples) and accurately identified the minority class (depressive samples). These results demonstrate that the method maintains excellent recognition capabilities when confronted with class imbalance, validating its effectiveness in practical depression detection scenarios.

DAIC-WOZ dataset

The DAIC-WOZ dataset [32], constructed by the Gratch team, is a multimodal research resource developed through clinical interview methods. Its primary objective is to assist in the clinical identification of anxiety disorders, depression, and post-traumatic stress disorder. This dataset employs multimodal synchronous acquisition to capture clinically significant biobehavioral signatures during structured interactions with the virtual agent Ellie. The audio channel provides fine-grained temporal analysis of paralinguistic attributes (e.g., prosodic variability, fluency discontinuities), while the textual component preserves lexical-semantic patterns through verbatim dialogue transcripts, enabling joint modeling of syntactic complexity and affective content in depression manifestation. The experimental design emphasizes the creation of natural conversation scenarios to effectively extract depression-related indicators in real-world contexts.

The database includes a total of 189 participants, categorized into a healthy control group (133 individuals) and a depression group (56 individuals) according to clinical diagnostic criteria. These participants are further divided into training, validation, and test sets following a standardized partitioning protocol (see Table 2 for details). The study employs a dual-channel data collection technique, independently recording each participant’s conversation with Ellie. Precise segmentation of human-computer dialogue segments is achieved through textual temporal tagging. In the data preprocessing stage, the original discrete files are integrated to generate individual audio and corresponding text documents for each participant. Notably, the text data has been automatically annotated to distinguish between human and computer dialogue content.

EATD-Corpus dataset

The EATD-Corpus [26], a Mandarin depressive speech corpus, comprises multimodal behavioral data from 162 student participants recruited from a tier-1 Chinese academic institution. It encompasses audio responses and corresponding text transcripts of participants during virtual interviews. The dataset creation process adheres strictly to ethical guidelines, with all participants signing informed consent forms. During data collection, the system randomly selects three questions, and respondents are required to record their verbal answers and submit them. Additionally, all volunteers complete a standardized Self-rating Depression Scale (SDS) questionnaire [75]. Comprising 20 items, the SDS covers four dimensions related to depression: emotional state, physiological responses, concomitant disorders, and psychomotor performance. The raw scores are multiplied by a factor of 1.25 to obtain the standard index score. According to Chinese regional criteria, an SDS index score of 53 or higher is preliminarily indicative of depressive tendencies.

The dataset contains multimodal behavioral recordings (audio and textual modalities) per participant, annotated across three affective valence categories (negative, neutral, positive) with stratified sample distribution (Table 3). To facilitate comparative experiments, this study extracts features from the negative, neutral, and positive files separately and combines them to create a single audio feature and a single text feature for each participant. This approach streamlines the training and learning process for the model.

Preprocessing

To accurately capture key features for depression recognition, we innovatively integrate multimodal information processing methods for audio and text. In audio analysis, the wav2vec2.0 pre-trained model is utilized to deeply extract frame-level hidden states of acoustic features, prosodic fluctuations, and emotional expressions in speech. For text processing, the ALBERT model is employed to construct a context-aware semantic representation system. To enhance the collaborative complementarity between modalities, we design the SGCMG. Guided by text semantic vectors, this mechanism dynamically adjusts the weights of audio features, significantly improving the model’s resistance to audio noise and data loss. Meanwhile, the introduction of the BG-TPA achieves high-order cross-modal alignment through semantic bias matrices, enabling in-depth modeling of complex interactions between speech and semantics. To handle the dynamic changes in speech emotions, we develop the ETM, which leverages the Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) network to analyze audio time series and accurately extract emotional evolution trends. These three modules work in synergy to keenly capture emotional contradictions between speech and text, such as the contrast between cheerful intonation and negative semantics, thereby enhancing the accuracy and reliability of depression risk recognition.

wav2vec2.0

We adopt wav2vec2.0 [76] as the speech encoder due to its demonstrated effectiveness in capturing emotionally relevant acoustic features under low-resource conditions [27, 77], which is particularly important since depression detection is typically conducted in scenarios with limited annotated data and emotionally subtle signals. The model consists of a convolutional encoder and self-supervised transformer layers, enabling the extraction of fine-grained prosodic and rhythmic cues, such as pitch, intonation, and speech patterns, which are essential for recognizing depressive symptoms.

All audio signals are standardized to a sampling rate of 16,000 Hz and segmented into overlapping short frames to ensure high temporal resolution. The resulting latent representations encode both low-level acoustic patterns and high-order structural information. To address the variability in utterance lengths, we employ a median-based temporal normalization strategy, aligning each audio sequence to the median frame length in the dataset. This minimizes truncation and padding while preserving the temporal and prosodic integrity of the original signals. The resulting features form the audio hidden states and are subsequently fed into downstream modules.

ALBERT

In the text modality analysis, this study selects the pre-trained language model ALBERT-large [78]. This choice is deliberately made to address the specific challenges posed by depression recognition datasets. A primary challenge is the limited sample size of clinical corpora like DAIC-WOZ, which increases the risk of overfitting when using very large models. ALBERT-large is highly parameter-efficient due to its cross-layer parameter-sharing mechanism. As shown in Table 4, which presents different configurations of the ALBERT model, ALBERT-large offers the representational power of a large-scale architecture but with significantly fewer parameters (18 M) compared to models like BERT-large (334 M) or RoBERTa-large (355 M), making it a more robust choice for our medium-sized dataset.

Our training protocol involves a two-stage fine-tuning strategy to effectively leverage the pre-trained knowledge while adapting the encoders to the specific domain of depression recognition. In the first stage, for the initial 25 epochs, all parameters of the text (ALBERT) and audio (wav2vec2.0) encoders are frozen. During this phase, only the parameters of our proposed modules (SGCMG, BG-TPA, ETM) and the final classifier are trained. This allows the model to first learn the fusion logic stably. In the second stage, from epoch 26 onwards, we unfreeze the top layers of both encoders (specifically, layers 10 and 11) and fine-tune them with a lower learning rate of , while the rest of the trainable parameters continue to use the initial learning rate. This gradual unfreezing approach helps to refine the feature extraction process without causing catastrophic forgetting of the pre-trained representations.

In our experimental configuration, the full SBT-Net model has a total of 119.79 M trainable parameters, a computational complexity of 117.95 GFLOPs, and an average inference time of 0.02624 seconds when running on CUDA. Critically, the complete fusion architecture that we propose, which includes the SGCMG, BG-TPA, and ETM modules along with the cross-attention layer and final classifier, accounts for only 13.73 M of this total. This figure represents approximately 11.5% of all parameters.

10-fold cross-validation experimental results

To comprehensively assess the stability and generalization ability of the SBT-Net model under few-shot conditions, a 10-fold cross-validation strategy was employed for experiments on the DAIC-WOZ dataset. Compared with traditional fixed partitioning methods, 10-fold cross-validation effectively reduces accidental biases caused by data partitioning. By iteratively training and testing on different data subsets, it enables a more comprehensive evaluation of the model’s performance. In the experimental design, the dataset was randomly divided into ten subsets of approximately equal size. In each fold, one subset was selected as the test set, while the remaining nine subsets were combined into a training set and a validation set for model training and parameter optimization. To ensure fairness in training and evaluation, samples from the same subject were never included in both the training and test sets simultaneously. Additionally, the WeightedRandomSampler strategy was introduced during training to mitigate the impact of data class imbalance. For the purpose of detailed visualization, displaying results from all ten folds is impractical. Therefore, we selected representative folds to illustrate the model’s behavior. For example, Fold-9 was chosen as it demonstrated the best-case performance, while Fold-1 was selected as an example of a typical, stable training run.

To further analyze the model’s convergence and stability during training, this study plotted the curves of accuracy changes with the number of training epochs on the training and validation sets for Fold-1 and Fold-9, as shown in Fig. 3. It can be observed that in Fold-9, the model’s accuracy increased rapidly in the early training stage, and the accuracy trends of the training and validation sets remained basically synchronized. By the last epoch, the two metrics converged, both reaching approximately 90%. This indicates that SBT-Net exhibits excellent optimization characteristics and generalization ability during training. The experimental results demonstrate that the model fits the training and validation datasets reasonably well without significant overfitting.

Ablation experiment

The key core modules of SBT-Net are the SGCMG,BG-TPA, and ETM module. To evaluate the impact of each module on depression detection, we conducted an ablation experiment on the DAIC-WOZ dataset by removing each module from SBT-Net one by one. Figure 8 presents the results of the ablated models after excluding any single module. Results show that all three modules contribute to enhancing SBT-Net’s performance in depression detection. Removing any one of them leads to a decline in model performance. Notably, removing the SGCMG module causes the most significant drop in F1-score, indicating that the semantic-guided dynamic gating mechanism plays a critical role in suppressing noisy modalities and enhancing cross-modal collaboration. Excluding the BG-TPA module results in decreases in both precision and recall, highlighting the importance of high-order semantic modeling for capturing deep cross-modal correlation features. Additionally, removing the ETM module leads to a substantial decline in the Recall metric, reflecting the significance of emotional evolution trend modeling in improving the ability to recall depressive samples.

To specifically isolate the contribution of the learnable bias matrix B and to justify its design within the BG-TPA module, we conducted two additional analyses on the DAIC-WOZ dataset. The results are presented in Table 9. First, we ablated the matrix B entirely, reverting the BG-TPA to a standard dot-product attention mechanism. This resulted in a significant F1-score drop from 0.92 to 0.80, demonstrating that the learnable, high-order bias interaction is crucial for effective cross-modal alignment. Second, we analyzed the influence of the rank r used in the low-rank factorization of B. This analysis validates our choice of r = 32 as an effective trade-off between model capacity and efficiency.

Discussion

Research contributions

This study proposes a semantic-driven cross-modal feature regulation method, the SGCMG, which dynamically filters the channel weights of audio features using the global semantic features of the text modality. Aiming at common issues such as audio noise and modal loss in multimodal data, this mechanism constructs an adaptive weight adjustment strategy based on semantic correlations, enabling precise filtering of redundant audio signals irrelevant to depressive features. Compared with traditional fusion methods that adopt fixed-weight modal concatenation, SGCMG significantly enhances the model’s robustness in scenarios with incomplete modalities, breaking through the performance bottleneck of static fusion approaches in noisy environments at the mechanism level.

To capture the deep semantic correlations between text and audio modalities, a BG-TPA is constructed. By introducing a semantic bias matrix, this module provides directional guidance for the cross-modal tensor interaction process, achieving fine-grained high-order feature fusion. Unlike traditional cross-modal alignment methods relying on single-layer attention or simple feature concatenation, BG-TPA can uncover implicit nonlinear semantic mapping relationships between modalities, effectively improving semantic alignment accuracy and high-order semantic representation capabilities in multimodal depression detection. This provides a new pathway for joint modeling of complex emotions. In response to the temporal evolution characteristics of depressive emotions in speech signals, an ETM module based on Gated Recurrent Units (GRU) is designed. By constructing a temporal dynamic feature extraction framework, this module captures subtle changes in audio emotions over time, enabling dynamic characterization of the evolution trends of depressive emotions. Compared with traditional static feature modeling methods, ETM overcomes the limitations of single-frame feature analysis, effectively capturing patterns of depressive features in long-term emotional fluctuations and significantly enhancing the model’s temporal perception ability for complex emotional expressions.

The study organically integrates the above three innovative modules to form the SBT-Net architecture for multimodal depression recognition, and conducts systematic experimental validations on two public benchmark datasets, DAIC-WOZ and EATD-Corpus. Experimental results show that this method outperforms current state-of-the-art (SOTA) approaches in key metrics such as classification accuracy and generalization ability. More importantly, through mechanism analysis and ablation experiments, this study reveals the synergistic effects of modal loss handling, high-order cross-modal interaction modeling, and temporal dynamic emotion analysis in improving detection effectiveness. This provides a reusable technical framework and theoretical reference for the design of subsequent multimodal mental health detection models, promoting the continuous development of research in this field.

Limitations and future work

Although the proposed SBT-Net demonstrates excellent performance in multimodal depression detection tasks, several areas for improvement remain. The current SBT-Net design focuses on the fusion of text and audio modalities. While it effectively captures the interaction information between linguistic content and acoustic features, in clinical practice, multi-dimensional data such as facial microexpressions, body postures [85], and physiological signals (e.g., heart rate variability [86], electroencephalogram signals [87]) play crucial roles in the accurate assessment of depression. Future research could comprehensively characterize depressive states by integrating these additional modalities, thereby enhancing the clinical application value of the detection system. Recent studies such as CmdVIT [88] have demonstrated the potential of facial expression recognition in mental disorder assessment, and DEP-Former [89] showed that combining facial and acoustic features can significantly enhance depression detection. These approaches suggest promising directions for expanding our model to visual modalities in future work.

This study was validated using the DAIC-WOZ and EATD-Corpus datasets. Despite their high benchmark value, there is room for improvement in terms of sample size and population coverage. A key limitation for the model’s global applicability is its current reliance on English and Mandarin data, as the linguistic and prosodic features that signal depression can vary significantly across different languages and cultures. Therefore, the direct application of our model to other languages without adaptation would likely face significant challenges.To address this, subsequent work must focus on constructing large-scale, diverse multimodal datasets that encompass a wider range of languages, cultures, and recording environments. This is particularly critical for facilitating the translation of research findings into real-world applications in developing countries, where mental health resources are often scarce and depression frequently goes undiagnosed at the grassroots level. Furthermore, leveraging transfer learning and domain adaptation techniques will be crucial for addressing the generalization bias of models in cross-cultural scenarios, allowing the core architecture of SBT-Net to be fine-tuned for new populations with limited local data.

Furthermore, future datasets could be enriched by including lifestyle and socio-demographic parameters, moving beyond purely clinical data. Factors such as sleep patterns, social interaction frequency, physical activity levels, and economic background are known to be closely linked with mental health. Integrating these parameters would allow for the development of more holistic and ecologically valid models, offering deeper insights into the complex interplay between an individual’s behavior, environment, and mental state.

Modules such as SGCMG and BG-TPA enhance model performance by introducing cross-modal attention mechanisms and high-order tensor operations, but they also increase computational complexity. For resource-constrained deployment scenarios (e.g., mobile medical devices or edge computing platforms), the real-time performance and energy efficiency ratio of the existing architecture require optimization. Future research could explore lightweight methods based on Neural Architecture Search (NAS) or model distillation techniques. While retaining core interaction mechanisms, strategies such as sparse tensor operations and optimized attention head allocation can be employed to effectively reduce model parameters and computational costs, promoting the portable application of multimodal depression detection technology. Finally, the current implementation of the ETM module relies on a GRU for temporal modeling. While effective, exploring more advanced architectures, such as a Transformer encoder, could potentially offer benefits in capturing long-range dependencies within the audio sequence. However, given the limited size of the datasets, a standard Transformer may be prone to overfitting. Future work could investigate lightweight or sparse Transformer variants to enhance the ETM module, aiming to improve temporal modeling capabilities while maintaining model efficiency and robustness on smaller datasets.

Currently, the research objective of SBT-Net is limited to binary classification of depressive states, without addressing fine-grained assessment scenarios required in clinical settings. Future research could expand the model’s functionality to multi-dimensional outputs, including the classification of depression severity levels (e.g., continuous value recognition based on the PHQ-9 scale [90]), classification of symptom subtypes (e.g., differentiation between melancholic depression and atypical depression [91, 92] ), and long-term emotional fluctuation modeling based on temporal data. These extensions would provide data support for the formulation of early intervention strategies and the evaluation of treatment efficacy.

Conclusion

This study proposes a multimodal depression detection framework, SBT-Net, which achieves breakthroughs through a three-stage innovative architecture to address the limitations of existing methods in modal loss handling, deep cross-modal interaction, and dynamic emotion modeling. Firstly, the SGCMG in this framework establishes a dynamic regulation pathway where text semantics control audio features. By generating adaptive filters from global text vectors, SGCMG effectively purifies acoustic signals by removing environmental noise and compensating for missing segments. Secondly, the BG-TPA module is designed to introduce a learnable bias matrix under low-rank constraints, guiding high-order cross-modal interactions and overcoming the representational bottleneck of traditional attention mechanisms. Finally, a dual-channel temporal modeling strategy is employed to capture both local emotional fluctuations and global emotional evolution patterns, enabling precise identification of the characteristic emotional blunting patterns in depression. Experimental results demonstrate that SBT-Net significantly outperforms existing methods in comprehensive performance across mainstream datasets. Moreover, it exhibits flexibility in modal expansion, being compatible with heterogeneous data inputs such as visual and physiological signals. This framework provides an efficient and reliable technical pathway for developing inclusive mental health assessment systems. Its modular design concept offers broad implications for the field of multimodal affective computing, especially in resource-constrained scenarios like mobile healthcare and telemedicine, where it demonstrates significant application value. Overall, SBT-Net represents a major advancement in automatic depression detection modeling technology, which has crucial implications for the continuous monitoring, timely intervention, and support of patients with mental illnesses.

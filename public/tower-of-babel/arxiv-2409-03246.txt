A priori and a posteriori error bounds for the fully mixed FEM formulation of poroelasticity with stress-dependent permeability
Arbaz Khan \ORCID 0000-0001-6625-700X, Bishnu P. Lamichhane \ORCID 0000-0002-9184-8941, Ricardo Ruiz-Baier* \ORCID 0000-0003-3144-5822 and Segundo Villa-Fuentes \ORCID 0000-0002-0377-6555

Abstract
We develop a family of mixed finite element methods for a model of nonlinear poroelasticity where, thanks to a rewriting of the constitutive equations, the permeability depends on the total poroelastic stress and on the fluid pressure and therefore we can use the Hellinger–Reissner principle with weakly imposed stress symmetry for Biot’s equations. The problem is adequately structured into a coupled system consisting of one saddle-point formulation, one linearised perturbed saddle-point formulation, and two off-diagonal perturbations. This system’s unique solvability requires assumptions on regularity and Lipschitz continuity of the inverse permeability, and the analysis follows fixed-point arguments and the Babuška–Brezzi theory. The discrete problem is shown uniquely solvable by applying similar fixed-point and saddle-point techniques as for the continuous case. The method is based on the classical PEERS k elements, it is exactly equilibrium and mass conservative, and it is robust with respect to the nearly incompressible as well as vanishing storativity limits. We derive a priori error estimates, we also propose fully computable residual-based a posteriori error indicators, and show that they are reliable and efficient with respect to the natural norms, and robust in the limit of near incompressibility. These a posteriori error estimates are used to drive adaptive mesh refinement. The theoretical analysis is supported and illustrated by several numerical examples in 2D and 3D.

Introduction

Nonlinear interaction between flow and the mechanical response of saturated porous media is of a great importance in many applications in biophysics, geomechanics, and tissue engineering, for example. One of such models is the equations of nonlinear poroelasticity, whose mathematical properties were studied in great detail, for example, in the references
[
11
,
13
,
12
]
. In these works, it becomes clear that a distinctive property of nonlinear poroelasticity models targeted for, e.g., soft tissue (cartilage, trabecular meshwork, brain matter, etc.), is that the nonlinear permeability (the hydraulic conductivity, defined as how easily pore fluid escapes from the compacted pore spaces) that depends on the evolving total amount of fluid, does not entail a monotone operator, and therefore one cannot readily apply typical tools from monotone saddle-point problems.

Our interest is in deriving mixed finite element (FE) formulations (solving also for other variables of interest), and for this we can cite in particular
[
37
,
39
]
, where fully mixed formulations based on the Hu–Washizu principle are studied. Writing the poroelasticity equations in terms of the strain tensor was motivated in particular in
[
39
]
because the permeability – at least in the regime we focus on here – depends nonlinearly on the total amount of fluid, which is a function of strain.

The upshot here compared to
[
39
]
is that we are able to rewrite the constitutive equation for permeability to depend on the total poroelastic stress and on the fluid pressure (similarly as in, e.g.,
[
8
]
). This allows us to revert to the more popular Hellinger–Reissner type of mixed formulations for poroelasticity
[
7
,
40
,
41
,
14
,
47
]
(without solving explicitly for the strain). Consequently, another appealing advantage with respect to the formulation in
[
39
]
is that, as in the Hellinger–Reissner formulation, the model becomes robust in the nearly incompressibility regime. Also in contrast to
[
39
]
, in this work we use a mixed form for the fluid flow (adding the discharge flux as additional unknown), which gives the additional advantage of mass conservativity.

Regarding the well-posedness analysis, the aforementioned non-monotonicity of the permeability suggest, for example, to use a fixed-point argument. We opt for freezing the arguments of permeability, turning the double saddle-point structure with three perturbations coming from the stress trace operator and from the
L^{2}
pressure blocks, into two decoupled saddle-point problems whose separate solvability can be established from the classical literature for weakly symmetric elasticity and mixed reaction-diffusion equations. Banach fixed-point theorem is then used to show well-posedness of the overall problem. This analysis needs to verify conditions of ball-mapping and contraction of the fixed-point map, and this imposes a small data assumption, which can be carried over to the external load, mass source, boundary displacement, and boundary fluid pressure. Compared to
[
39
]
, these conditions are less restrictive and imply also a less restrictive discrete analysis (which follows closely the continuous one), due to the analysis being performed using the inverse of the Hooke tensor, which allows us to achieve robustness with respect to the first Lamé parameter
\lambda
.

Note that at the discrete level we can simply use conforming FE spaces. Discrete inf-sup conditions are already well-known for the chosen FE families of PEERS
k
and Raviart–Thomas elements used for the solid and fluid sub-problems (but several other inf-sup stable spaces that satisfy a discrete kernel characterisation are also possible). We emphasise that, similarly to
[
40
,
41
]
, all estimates hold uniformly in the limit of nearly incompressibility (implying that the formulation is Poisson locking-free) as well as when the constrained storage coefficient vanishes (poroelastic locking-free), and therefore they are free of non-physical pressure oscillations.

An additional goal of this work is to derive efficient and reliable residual a posteriori error estimators for the nonlinear poroelasticity equations. The approach follows a similar treatment as that of
[
33
]
(which focuses on mixed formulations of stress-assisted diffusion equations), with the difference that here we do not need to include augmentation terms for the mixed form of the mixed diffusion problem. The main ingredients in the analysis of these estimates are Helmholtz decompositions and a global inf-sup (together with boundedness and Lipschitz continuity of coupling terms), local inverse and trace estimates, bubble-based localisation arguments, and properties of Clément and Raviart–Thomas interpolators. See also
[
3
,
28
]
for estimators in a similar multiphysics context and, e.g.,
[
19
,
20
,
42
]
for mixed linear elasticity. Note that for the reliability of the estimator the aforementioned Helmholtz decompositions – for both tensor-vector and vector-scalar cases – should be valid for mixed boundary conditions. For this we follow
[
3
]
and
[
25
]
, from which we inherit a convexity assumption on the Neumann sub-boundary (where we impose traction and flux boundary conditions).

Notation and preliminaries.

Let
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
be the set of all square-integrable functions in
\Omega\subset\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}^{d}
where

is the spatial dimension, and denote by
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)=\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
its vector-valued counterpart and by
\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega)=\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{d\times d}
its tensor-valued counterpart. We also write

\mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{skew}}^{2}(\Omega):=\{\bm{\tau}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega):\,\bm{\tau}=-\bm{\tau}^{\tt t}\},

to represent the skew-symmetric tensors in
\Omega
with each component being square-integrable. Standard notation will be employed for Sobolev spaces
\mathrm{H}^{m}(\Omega)
with
m\geq 0
(and we note that
\mathrm{H}^{0}(\Omega)=\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
). Their norms and seminorms are denoted as
\|\cdot\|_{m,\Omega}
and
|\cdot|_{m,\Omega}
, respectively (as well as for their vector and tensor-valued counterparts
\mathbf{H}^{m}(\Omega)
,
\mathbb{H}^{m}(\Omega)
) see, e.g.,
[
15
]
.

As usual
{\mathbf{I}}
stands for the identity tensor in
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
,
and
|\cdot|
denotes the Euclidean norm in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Also, for any vector field
\mathbf{v}=(v_{i})_{i=1,d}
we set the gradient and divergence operators as

\bm{\nabla}\mathbf{v}\,:=\,\left(\frac{\partial v_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\right)_{i,j=1,d}
and
\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits\mathbf{v}\,:=\,\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\partial v_{j}}{\partial x_{j}}
.
In addition, for any tensor fields
\bm{\tau}=(\tau_{ij})_{i,j=1,d}

and
{\bm{\zeta}}=(\zeta_{ij})_{i,j=1,d}
, we let
\mathbf{div}\,\bm{\tau}
be the divergence operator
\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits
acting along the rows of
\bm{\tau}
, and define the transpose, the trace, the tensor inner product, and the deviatoric tensor as

\bm{\tau}^{\mathrm{t}}:=(\tau_{ji})_{i,j=1,d}
,

\mathop{\rm tr}(\bm{\tau}):=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\tau_{ii}
,

\bm{\tau}:{\bm{\zeta}}:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\tau_{ij}\zeta_{ij}
, and

\bm{\tau}^{\mathrm{d}}:=\bm{\tau}-{\frac{1}{d}}\,\mathop{\rm tr}(\bm{\tau})\,\mathbf{I}
, respectively.
We also recall the Hilbert space

\mathbf{H}(\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits;\Omega)\,:=\,\big\{\mbox{\boldmath{$z$}}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega):\,\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits\,\mbox{\boldmath{$z$}}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)\big\},

with norm
\|\mbox{\boldmath{$z$}}\|_{\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits;\Omega}^{2}:=\|\mbox{\boldmath{$z$}}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\|\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits\,\mbox{\boldmath{$z$}}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}
, and introduce its tensor-valued version

\mathbb{H}(\mathbf{div};\Omega)\,:=\,\big\{\bm{\tau}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\Omega):\,\mathbf{div}\,\bm{\tau}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)\big\}.

Governing equations.

Let us consider a fully-saturated poroelastic medium (consisting of a mechanically isotropic and homogeneous fluid-solid mixture) occupying the open and bounded domain
\Omega
in
\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}^{d}
, the Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
is partitioned into disjoint sub-boundaries
\partial\Omega:=\overline{\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}\cup\overline{\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}}
, and it is assumed for the sake of simplicity that both sub-boundaries are non-empty
|\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}|\cdot|\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}|>0
.
The symbol
n
will stand for the unit outward normal vector on the boundary. Let
\bm{f}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
be a prescribed body force per unit of volume (acting on the fluid-structure mixture) and let
g\in L^{2}(\Omega)
be a net volumetric fluid production rate.

The equilibrium (balance of linear momentum) for the solid-fluid mixture is written as

-\mathbf{div}\,\bm{\sigma}=\bm{f}\qquad\text{in $\Omega$},

(1.1)

with
\bm{\sigma}
being the total Cauchy stress tensor of the mixture (sum of the effective solid and fluid stresses), whose dependence on strain and on fluid pressure is given by the constitutive assumption (or effective stress principle)

\bm{\sigma}=\mathcal{C}\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}({\bm{u}})-\alpha p{\mathbf{I}}\qquad\text{in $\Omega$}.

(1.2)

Here the skeleton displacement vector
{\bm{u}}
from the position
\bm{x}\in\Omega
is an unknown, the tensor
\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}({\bm{u}}):=\frac{1}{2}(\bm{\nabla}{\bm{u}}+[\bm{\nabla}{\bm{u}}]^{\tt t})
is the infinitesimal strain, by
\mathcal{C}
we denote the fourth-order elasticity tensor, also known as Hooke’s tensor (symmetric and positive definite and characterised by
\mathcal{C}\bm{\tau}:=\lambda(\mathop{\rm tr}\bm{\tau})\mathbf{I}+2\mu\,\bm{\tau}
),
\lambda
and
\mu
are the Lamé parameters (assumed constant and positive),
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
is the Biot–Willis parameter, and
p
denotes the Darcy fluid pressure (positive in compression), which is an unknown in the system.

We also consider the balance of angular momentum, which in this context states that the total poroelastic stress is a symmetric tensor

\bm{\sigma}=\bm{\sigma}^{\tt t}
.
To weakly impose it, it is customary to use the rotation tensor

\bm{\rho}=\frac{1}{2}(\bm{\nabla}{\bm{u}}-[\bm{\nabla}{\bm{u}}]^{\tt t})=\bm{\nabla}{\bm{u}}-\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}({\bm{u}}).

(1.3)

The fluid content (due to both fluid saturation and local volume dilation) is given by

\zeta=c_{0}p+\alpha\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits{\bm{u}},

(1.4)

where
c_{0}\geq 0
is the constrained specific storage coefficient.
Using Darcy’s law to describe the discharge velocity in terms of the fluid pressure gradient, the balance of mass for the total amount of fluid is
\partial_{t}\zeta-\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits({\bm{\kappa}}\nabla p)=g
in
\Omega\times(0,t_{\mathrm{end}})
, where
{\bm{\kappa}}
is the intrinsic permeability tensor of the medium, a nonlinear function of the porosity. In turn, in the small strains limit the porosity can be approximated by a linear function of the fluid content
\zeta
(see for example
[
46
, Section 2.1]
), and so, thanks to
(
1.4
), we can simply write

{\bm{\kappa}}={\bm{\kappa}}(\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}({\bm{u}}),p).

Furthermore, after a backward Euler semi-discretisation in time with a constant time step and rescaling appropriately, we only consider the type of equations needed to solve at each time step and therefore we will concentrate on the form

c_{0}p+\alpha\mathop{\rm tr}\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}({\bm{u}})-\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits({\bm{\kappa}}(\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}({\bm{u}}),p)\nabla p)=g\qquad\text{in $\Omega$}.

(1.5)

Typical constitutive relations for permeability are, e.g., exponential or Kozeny–Carman type (cf.
[
6
]
)

{\bm{\kappa}}(\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}({\bm{u}}),p)=\frac{k_{0}}{\mu_{f}}\mathbf{I}+\frac{k_{1}}{\mu_{f}}\exp(k_{2}(c_{0}p+\alpha\mathop{\rm tr}\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}({\bm{u}})))\mathbf{I},\quad{\bm{\kappa}}(\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}({\bm{u}}),p)=\frac{k_{0}}{\mu_{f}}\mathbf{I}+\frac{k_{1}(c_{0}p+\alpha\mathop{\rm tr}\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}({\bm{u}}))^{3}}{\mu_{f}(1-(c_{0}p+\alpha\mathop{\rm tr}\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}({\bm{u}})))^{2}}\mathbf{I},

(1.6)

where
\mu_{f}
denotes the viscosity of the interstitial fluid and
k_{0},k_{1},k_{2}
are model constants. We note that in the case of incompressible constituents one has
c_{0}=0
and
\alpha=1
, indicating that permeability depends only on the dilation
\mathop{\rm tr}\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}({\bm{u}})=\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits{\bm{u}}
(see, e.g.,
[
11
]
). We also note that even in such a scenario (of incompressible phases) the overall mixture is not necessarily incompressible itself.
More precise assumptions on the behaviour of the permeability are postponed to Section
2.2
.
Next we note that from (
1.2
) we can obtain

\mathop{\rm tr}\bm{\sigma}=(d\lambda+2\mu)\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits{\bm{u}}-d\alpha p{\quad\hbox{and}\quad}\mathcal{C}^{-1}\bm{\sigma}+\dfrac{\alpha}{d\lambda+2\mu}p\mathbf{I}=\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}({\bm{u}})\qquad\text{in $\Omega$}.

(1.7)

Then, from the first equation in
(
1.7
) we get

\mathop{\rm tr}\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}({\bm{u}})=\dfrac{1}{d\lambda+2\mu}\mathop{\rm tr}\bm{\sigma}+\dfrac{d\alpha}{d\lambda+2\mu}p,

and therefore the dependence of
{\bm{\kappa}}
on
\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}({\bm{u}})
and
p
(cf. (
1.6
)) can be written in terms of
\bm{\sigma}
and
p
as follows

\begin{array}[]{cc}\displaystyle{\bm{\kappa}}(\bm{\sigma},p)=\frac{k_{0}}{\mu_{f}}\mathbf{I}+\frac{k_{1}}{\mu_{f}}\exp\Big(\dfrac{k_{2}}{d\lambda+2\mu}\big((c_{0}(d\lambda+2\mu)+d\alpha^{2})\,p+\alpha\mathop{\rm tr}\bm{\sigma}\big)\Big)\mathbf{I},\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle{\bm{\kappa}}(\bm{\sigma},p)=\frac{k_{0}}{\mu_{f}}\mathbf{I}+\frac{k_{1}\big((c_{0}(d\lambda+2\mu)+d\alpha^{2})\,p+\alpha\mathop{\rm tr}\bm{\sigma}\big)^{3}}{(d\lambda+2\mu)\mu_{f}\big(d\lambda+2\mu-((c_{0}(d\lambda+2\mu)+d\alpha^{2})\,p+\alpha\mathop{\rm tr}\bm{\sigma})\big)^{2}}\mathbf{I},\end{array}

(1.8)

and emphasize that the permeability tensor is the same constitutive law on either (
1.6
) or (
1.8
), so, making abuse of notation we have

{\bm{\kappa}}={\bm{\kappa}}(\zeta)={\bm{\kappa}}(\mbox{\boldmath{$\varepsilon$}}({\bm{u}}),p)={\bm{\kappa}}(\bm{\sigma},p).

In addition, putting together the second equation in (
1.7
) and (
1.3
) we obtain:

\mathcal{C}^{-1}\bm{\sigma}+\dfrac{\alpha}{d\lambda+2\mu}p\mathbf{I}=\bm{\nabla}{\bm{u}}-\bm{\rho}\qquad\text{in $\Omega$}.

(1.9)

Finally, we introduce the discharge flux
{\bm{\varphi}}
as an unknown defined by the constitutive relation

{\bm{\kappa}}(\bm{\sigma},p)^{-1}{\bm{\varphi}}=\nabla p,

(1.10)

and combining (
1.7
) and (
1.5
), we are able to rewrite the mass balance equation as

c_{0}p+\dfrac{\alpha}{d\lambda+2\mu}\mathop{\rm tr}\bm{\sigma}+\dfrac{d\alpha^{2}}{d\lambda+2\mu}p-\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits{\bm{\varphi}}=g\qquad\text{in $\Omega$}.

(1.11)

To close the system, we consider mixed boundary conditions for a given
{\bm{u}}_{\mathrm{D}}\in\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}})
and
p_{\mathrm{D}}\in\mathrm{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}})
:

{\bm{u}}={\bm{u}}_{\mathrm{D}}{\quad\hbox{and}\quad}p=p_{\mathrm{D}}{\quad\hbox{on}\quad}\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}},\qquad{\bm{\varphi}}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath{$n$}}=0{\quad\hbox{and}\quad}\bm{\sigma}\mbox{\boldmath{$n$}}=\bm{0}{\quad\hbox{on}\quad}\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}.

(1.12)

2.2
Stability properties and suitable inf-sup conditions

For the sake of the analysis, we allow the permeability
{\bm{\kappa}}(\bm{\sigma},p)
to be anisotropic but still require
{\bm{\kappa}}(\bm{\sigma},p)^{-1}
to be uniformly positive definite in
\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)
and Lipschitz continuous with respect to
p\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
. That is, there exist strictly positive constants
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}
such that

\kappa_{1}|\mathbf{v}|^{2}\leq\mathbf{v}^{\tt t}{\bm{\kappa}}(\cdot,\cdot)^{-1}\mathbf{v},\qquad\|{\bm{\kappa}}(\cdot,p_{1})^{-1}-{\bm{\kappa}}(\cdot,p_{2})^{-1}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\kappa_{2}\|p_{1}-p_{2}\|_{0,\Omega},

(2.3)

for all
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
, and for all
p_{1},p_{2}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
.

We start by establishing the boundedness of the bilinear forms
a
,
b
,
c
,
\widetilde{b}
,
\widetilde{c}
:

\displaystyle\big|a(\bm{\sigma},\bm{\tau})\big|\leq\dfrac{1}{\mu}\|\bm{\sigma}\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega}\|\bm{\tau}\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega},\qquad\big|b(\bm{\tau},(\mathbf{v},{\bm{\eta}}))\big|\leq\|\bm{\tau}\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega}(\|\mathbf{v}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|{\bm{\eta}}\|_{0,\Omega}),

(2.4a)

\displaystyle\big|c(\bm{\tau},q)\big|\leq\gamma\|\bm{\tau}\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega}\|q\|_{0,\Omega},

(2.4b)

\displaystyle\big|\widetilde{b}({\bm{\psi}},q)\big|\leq\|{\bm{\psi}}\|_{\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits;\Omega}\|q\|_{0,\Omega},\qquad\big|\widetilde{c}(p,q)\big|\leq\widetilde{\gamma}\|p\|_{0,\Omega}\|q\|_{0,\Omega},

(2.4c)

where

\displaystyle\gamma:=\dfrac{\alpha\sqrt{d}}{d\lambda+2\mu}{\quad\hbox{and}\quad}\widetilde{\gamma}:=c_{0}+\dfrac{d\alpha^{2}}{d\lambda+2\mu}.

(2.5)

On the other hand, using Hölder’s and trace inequalities we can readily observe that the right-hand side functionals are all bounded

\displaystyle\big|H(\bm{\tau})\big|\leq\|{\bm{u}}_{\mathrm{D}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}\|\bm{\tau}\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega},\qquad\big|F(\mathbf{v},{\bm{\eta}})\big|\leq\|\bm{f}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{0,\Omega}\leq\|\bm{f}\|_{0,\Omega}(\|\mathbf{v}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|{\bm{\eta}}\|_{0,\Omega}),

\displaystyle\big|\widetilde{H}({\bm{\psi}})\big|\leq\|p_{\mathrm{D}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}\|{\bm{\psi}}\|_{\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits;\Omega},\qquad\big|{\tilde{F}}(q)\big|\leq\|g\|_{0,\Omega}\|q\|_{0,\Omega}.

Let us now denote by
\mathbb{V}
and
\mathbf{V}
the kernels of
b
and
\widetilde{b}
, respectively. They are characterised, respectively, as

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\Big\{\bm{\tau}\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathbf{div};\Omega):\quad\mathbf{div}\,\bm{\tau}=\bm{0}{\quad\hbox{and}\quad}\bm{\tau}=\bm{\tau}^{\tt t}{\quad\hbox{in}\quad}\Omega\Big\},

(2.6a)

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}

\displaystyle=\Big\{{\bm{\psi}}\in\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits;\Omega):\quad\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits\,{\bm{\psi}}=0\quad\text{in }\,\Omega\Big\}.

(2.6b)

From
[
2
, Lemmas 3.1 and 3.2]
we easily deduce that there exists
c_{a}>0
such that

\displaystyle a(\bm{\tau},\bm{\tau})\geq c_{a}\|\bm{\tau}\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega}^{2}\quad\forall\,\bm{\tau}\in\mathbb{V}.

(2.7)

Remark 2.1
.

From
[
30
, Lemma 2.2]
and
[
31
, Lemma 2.2]
, we have that the ellipticity constant

has the form
c_{a}=\hat{c}\frac{1}{\mu}
, where
\hat{c}
depends on
\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}
,
|\Omega|
, and the Poincaré constant. Based on the above,

has a non-zero lower bound.

The following inf-sup conditions are well-known to hold (see, e.g.,
[
18
]
):

\displaystyle\sup_{\bm{0}\neq\bm{\tau}\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathbf{div};\Omega)}\frac{b(\bm{\tau},(\mathbf{v},{\bm{\eta}}))}{\|\bm{\tau}\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega}}

\displaystyle\geq\beta(\|\mathbf{v}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|{\bm{\eta}}\|_{0,\Omega})\quad\forall\,(\mathbf{v},{\bm{\eta}})\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)\times\mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{skew}}^{2}(\Omega),

(2.8a)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup_{\bm{0}\neq{\bm{\psi}}\in\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits;\Omega)}\frac{\widetilde{b}({\bm{\psi}},q)}{\|\psi\|_{\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits;\Omega}}

\displaystyle\geq\widetilde{\beta}\|q\|_{0,\Omega}\quad\forall\,q\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega).

(2.8b)

Finally, we observe that
\widetilde{c}
is elliptic over
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\widetilde{c}(q,q)\geq\widetilde{\gamma}\,\|q\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}.

(2.9)

Remark 2.2
.

Due to the careful choice of the bilinear form
a(\cdot,\cdot)
, constants in continuity estimates
(
2.4a
) –(
2.4c
) and inf-sup conditions
(
2.8a
) and (

)
do not blow up when

and

. In particular, the constants
\gamma
and
\widetilde{\gamma}
, which depend on
\lambda
and

(cf. (

)), remain bounded.

3
Analysis of the coupled problem

We now use a combination of the classical Babuška–Brezzi and Banach fixed-point theorems to establish the well-posedness of (
2.2
) under appropriate assumptions on the data.

3.1
A fixed‑point operator

We adopt a similar approach to, e.g.,
[
24
]
.
First, we define a closed ball of
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
centred at the origin and of given radius
r>0

\mathrm{W}:=\{\widehat{p}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)\,:\quad\|\widehat{p}\|_{0,\Omega}\leq r\}.

(3.1)

Then, for a given
(\widehat{\bm{\sigma}},\widehat{p})\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathbf{div};\Omega)\times\mathrm{W}
, thanks to the assumptions on the nonlinear permeability, we can infer that the form
\widetilde{a}_{\widehat{\bm{\sigma}},\widehat{p}}
(cf. (
2.1
)) is continuous, as well as coercive over
\mathbf{V}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\big|\widetilde{a}_{\widehat{\bm{\sigma}},\widehat{p}}({\bm{\varphi}},{\bm{\psi}})\big|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\widetilde{a}}\,\|{\bm{\varphi}}\|_{\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits;\Omega}\|{\bm{\psi}}\|_{\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits;\Omega},

(3.2a)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\widetilde{a}_{\widehat{\bm{\sigma}},\widehat{p}}({\bm{\psi}},{\bm{\psi}})

\displaystyle\geq\kappa_{1}\|{\bm{\psi}}\|_{\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits;\Omega}^{2}\quad\forall\,{\bm{\varphi}},\,{\bm{\psi}}\in\mathbf{V}.

(3.2b)

Then, we define the auxiliary operators

\mathbf{R}:\mathrm{W}\subseteq\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathbf{div};\Omega)\times(\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)\times\mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{skew}}^{2}(\Omega))
and
\mathbf{S}:\mathbb{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathbf{div};\Omega)\times\mathrm{W}\to\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits;\Omega)\times\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, given by

\mathbf{R}(\widehat{p}):=\big(R_{1}(\widehat{p}),(R_{2}(\widehat{p}),R_{3}(\widehat{p}))\big)=(\bm{\sigma},({\bm{u}},\bm{\rho}))\quad\forall\,\widehat{p}\in\mathrm{W},

with
(\bm{\sigma},({\bm{u}},\bm{\rho}))\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathbf{div};\Omega)\times(\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)\times\mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{skew}}^{2}(\Omega))
satisfying

\begin{array}[]{rllll}\displaystyle a(\bm{\sigma},\bm{\tau})&+\quad\displaystyle b(\bm{\tau},({\bm{u}},\bm{\rho}))&=\displaystyle H(\bm{\tau})-c(\bm{\tau},\widehat{p})&\forall\,\bm{\tau}\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathbf{div};\Omega),\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle b(\bm{\sigma},(\mathbf{v},{\bm{\eta}}))&&=\displaystyle F(\mathbf{v},{\bm{\eta}})&\forall\,(\mathbf{v},{\bm{\eta}})\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)\times\mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{skew}}^{2}(\Omega),\end{array}

(3.3)

and

\mathbf{S}(\widehat{\bm{\sigma}},\widehat{p}):=\big(S_{1}(\widehat{\bm{\sigma}},\widehat{p}),S_{2}(\widehat{\bm{\sigma}},\widehat{p})\big)=({\bm{\varphi}},p)\quad\forall\,(\widehat{\bm{\sigma}},\widehat{p})\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathbf{div};\Omega)\times\mathrm{W},

where
({\bm{\varphi}},p)
is such that

\begin{array}[]{rllll}\displaystyle\widetilde{a}_{\widehat{\bm{\sigma}},\widehat{p}}({\bm{\varphi}},{\bm{\psi}})&+\quad\displaystyle\widetilde{b}({\bm{\psi}},p)&=\displaystyle\widetilde{H}({\bm{\psi}})&\forall\,{\bm{\psi}}\in\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits;\Omega),\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle\widetilde{b}({\bm{\varphi}},q)&-\quad\widetilde{c}(p,q)&=\displaystyle{\tilde{F}}(q)+c(\widehat{\bm{\sigma}},q)&\forall\,q\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega).\end{array}

(3.4)

By virtue of the above, by defining the operator
\mathbf{T}:\mathrm{W}\subseteq\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
as

\mathbf{T}(\widehat{p}):=S_{2}(R_{1}(\widehat{p}),\widehat{p}),

(3.5)

it is clear that
(\bm{\sigma},{\bm{u}},\bm{\rho},{\bm{\varphi}},p)
is a solution to (
2.2
) if and only if
p\in\mathrm{W}
solves the fixed-point problem

\mathbf{T}(p)=p.

(3.6)

Thus, in what follows, we focus on proving the unique solvability of (
3.6
).

\mathbf{T}

From the definition of

in (
3.5

∎

3.3
Existence and uniqueness of weak solution

We begin by establishing two lemmas deriving conditions under which the operator

is a contraction.

Lemma 3.6
.

Given
\widehat{p}_{1},\,\widehat{p}_{2},\in\mathrm{W}
, the following estimate holds

\|R_{1}(\widehat{p}_{1})-R_{1}(\widehat{p}_{2})\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega}\leq\dfrac{1}{c_{a}}\,\gamma\,\|\widehat{p}_{1}-\widehat{p}_{2}\|_{0,\Omega}.

(3.12)

Proof.

Let

(cf. (

\begin{array}[]{rllll}\displaystyle a(\bm{\sigma}_{1}-\bm{\sigma}_{2},\bm{\tau})&+\displaystyle b(\bm{\tau},({\bm{u}}_{1}-{\bm{u}}_{2},\bm{\rho}_{1}-\bm{\rho}_{2}))&=\displaystyle-c(\bm{\tau},\widehat{p}_{1}-\widehat{p}_{2})&\forall\,\bm{\tau}\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathbf{div};\Omega),\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle b(\bm{\sigma}_{1}-\bm{\sigma}_{2},(\mathbf{v},{\bm{\eta}}))&&=\displaystyle 0&\forall\,(\mathbf{v},{\bm{\eta}})\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)\times\mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{skew}}^{2}(\Omega).\end{array}

(3.13)

Since
\bm{\sigma}_{1}-\bm{\sigma}_{2}\in\mathbb{V}
(cf. (
2.6a
)), taking
\bm{\tau}=\bm{\sigma}_{1}-\bm{\sigma}_{2}

\displaystyle c_{a}\|\bm{\sigma}_{1}-\bm{\sigma}_{2}\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega}^{2}\leq a(\bm{\sigma}_{1}-\bm{\sigma}_{2},\bm{\sigma}_{1}-\bm{\sigma}_{2})=-c(\bm{\sigma}_{1}-\bm{\sigma}_{2},\widehat{p}_{1}-\widehat{p}_{2})\leq\,\gamma\,\|\bm{\sigma}_{1}-\bm{\sigma}_{2}\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega}\|\widehat{p}_{1}-\widehat{p}_{2}\|_{0,\Omega},

which concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Given

(3.14)

Note
that
\min\{\tilde{\gamma},\kappa_{1}\}
enters in the denominator of the estimate, but
we have assumed that

is strictly positive and it does not degenerate to zero.

Proof.

Let
({\bm{\varphi}}_{1},p_{1}),\,({\bm{\varphi}}_{2},p_{2})\in\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits;\Omega)\times\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)

\mathbf{S}(\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{1},\widehat{p}_{1})=({\bm{\varphi}}_{1},p_{1})

\displaystyle\widetilde{a}_{\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{1},\widehat{p}_{1}}({\bm{\varphi}}_{1},{\bm{\varphi}}_{1}-{\bm{\varphi}}_{2})-\widetilde{a}_{\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{2},\widehat{p}_{2}}({\bm{\varphi}}_{2},{\bm{\varphi}}_{1}-{\bm{\varphi}}_{2})+\widetilde{c}(p_{1}-p_{2},p_{1}-p_{2})=-c(\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{1}-\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{2},p_{1}-p_{2}),

by adding
\pm\widetilde{a}_{\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{1},\widehat{p}_{1}}({\bm{\varphi}}_{2},{\bm{\varphi}}_{1}-{\bm{\varphi}}_{2})
in the last equation, we obtain

\displaystyle\widetilde{a}_{\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{1},\widehat{p}_{1}}({\bm{\varphi}}_{1}-{\bm{\varphi}}_{2},{\bm{\varphi}}_{1}-{\bm{\varphi}}_{2})+\widetilde{c}(p_{1}-p_{2},p_{1}-p_{2})

\displaystyle\qquad=\widetilde{a}_{\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{2},\widehat{p}_{2}}({\bm{\varphi}}_{2},{\bm{\varphi}}_{1}-{\bm{\varphi}}_{2})-\widetilde{a}_{\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{1},\widehat{p}_{1}}({\bm{\varphi}}_{2},{\bm{\varphi}}_{1}-{\bm{\varphi}}_{2})-c(\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{1}-\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{2},p_{1}-p_{2}).

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\kappa_{1}\|{\bm{\varphi}}_{1}-{\bm{\varphi}}_{2}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\widetilde{\gamma}\|p_{1}-p_{2}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}\leq\widetilde{a}_{\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{1},\widehat{p}_{1}}({\bm{\varphi}}_{1}-{\bm{\varphi}}_{2},{\bm{\varphi}}_{1}-{\bm{\varphi}}_{2})+\widetilde{c}(p_{1}-p_{2},p_{1}-p_{2})\\[4.30554pt]

)), we obtain

\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{2}\min\{\widetilde{\gamma},\kappa_{1}\}(\|{\bm{\varphi}}_{1}-{\bm{\varphi}}_{2}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|p_{1}-p_{2}\|_{0,\Omega})^{2}\leq\kappa_{1}\|{\bm{\varphi}}_{1}-{\bm{\varphi}}_{2}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\widetilde{\gamma}\|p_{1}-p_{2}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}\\[4.30554pt]
\qquad\leq\kappa_{2}\|\widehat{p}_{2}-\widehat{p}_{1}\|_{0,\Omega}\|{\bm{\varphi}}_{2}\|_{0,\Omega}\|{\bm{\varphi}}_{1}-{\bm{\varphi}}_{2}\|_{0,\Omega}+\,\gamma\,\|\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{1}-\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{2}\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega}\|p_{1}-p_{2}\|_{0,\Omega}\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle\qquad\leq\big(\kappa_{2}\|\widehat{p}_{2}-\widehat{p}_{1}\|_{0,\Omega}\|{\bm{\varphi}}_{2}\|_{0,\Omega}+\,\gamma\,\|\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{1}-\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{2}\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega}\big)\big(\|{\bm{\varphi}}_{1}-{\bm{\varphi}}_{2}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|p_{1}-p_{2}\|_{0,\Omega}\big),\end{array}

the last, together with the fact that
{\bm{\varphi}}_{2}
satisfies (
3.9
), leads to the following bound

\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{2}\min\{\widetilde{\gamma},\kappa_{1}\}(\|{\bm{\varphi}}_{1}-{\bm{\varphi}}_{2}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|p_{1}-p_{2}\|_{0,\Omega})\leq\kappa_{2}\|\widehat{p}_{2}-\widehat{p}_{1}\|_{0,\Omega}\|{\bm{\varphi}}_{2}\|_{0,\Omega}+\,\gamma\,\|\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{1}-\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{2}\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega}\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle\qquad\leq\kappa_{2}\|\widehat{p}_{2}-\widehat{p}_{1}\|_{0,\Omega}\widetilde{C}\,(\|g\|_{0,\Omega}+\|p_{\mathrm{D}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}+\gamma\|\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{2}\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega})+\,\gamma\,\|\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{1}-\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{2}\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega},\end{array}

and this yields (
3.14
), concluding the proof.
∎

The following theorem presents the main result of this section, establishing the existence and uniqueness of the solution to the fixed-point problem (
3.6
), or equivalently, of (
2.2
).

Theorem 3.8
.

Given
r>0
, assume that
\bm{f}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
,
g\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
{\bm{u}}_{\mathrm{D}}\in\mathbf{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}})
,
p_{\mathrm{D}}\in\mathrm{H}^{1/2}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}})
and
\gamma
satisfies

\begin{array}[]{cc}\dfrac{2\mathop{\rm max}\limits\{1,\,\kappa_{2}\}}{\min\{\widetilde{\gamma},\,\kappa_{1}\,,r\}}\,\bigg\{\widetilde{C}(1+C_{1}\gamma)(\|g\|_{0,\Omega}+\|p_{\mathrm{D}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}+\|{\bm{u}}_{\mathrm{D}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}+\|\bm{f}\|_{0,\Omega})+\dfrac{\gamma^{2}}{c_{a}}(\dfrac{1}{\kappa_{2}}+\widetilde{C}\,r)\bigg\}<1.\end{array}

(3.15)

Then,
\mathbf{T}
(cf. (
3.5
)) has a unique fixed point
p\in\mathrm{W}
. Equivalently, (
2.2
) has a unique solution
(\bm{\sigma},{\bm{u}},\bm{\rho},{\bm{\varphi}},p)\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathbf{div};\Omega)\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)\times\mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{skew}}^{2}(\Omega)\times\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits;\Omega)\times\mathrm{W}
. In addition,
there exists
C>0
, such that

\displaystyle\|\bm{\sigma}\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega}+\|{\bm{u}}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|\bm{\rho}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|{\bm{\varphi}}\|_{\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits;\Omega}+\|p\|_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C(\|g\|_{0,\Omega}+\|p_{\mathrm{D}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}+\|{\bm{u}}_{\mathrm{D}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}+\|\bm{f}\|_{0,\Omega}+\gamma\,r\,).

(3.16)

Proof.

Recall that (
3.15
) ensures the well-definedness of
\mathbf{T}
. Let
\widehat{p}_{1},\,\widehat{p}_{2},\,p_{1},\,p_{2}\in\mathrm{W}
, such that
\mathbf{T}(\widehat{p}_{1})=p_{1}
and
\mathbf{T}(\widehat{p}_{2})=p_{2}
. From the definition of
\mathbf{T}
(see (
3.5
)), and the estimates (
3.14
) and (
3.12
), we deduce

\displaystyle\|p_{1}-p_{2}\|_{0,\Omega}=\|\mathbf{T}(\widehat{p}_{1})-\mathbf{T}(\widehat{p}_{2})\|_{0,\Omega}=\|S_{2}(R_{1}(\widehat{p}_{1}),\widehat{p}_{1})-S_{2}(R_{1}(\widehat{p}_{2}),\widehat{p}_{2})\|_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{2\kappa_{2}\,\widetilde{C}}{\min\{\widetilde{\gamma},\,\kappa_{1}\}}\,\big(\|g\|_{0,\Omega}+\|p_{\mathrm{D}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}+\gamma\|R_{1}(\widehat{p}_{2})\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega}\big)\|\widehat{p}_{1}-\widehat{p}_{2}\|_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\qquad+\dfrac{2}{\min\{\widetilde{\gamma},\,\kappa_{1}\}}\,\gamma\,\|R_{1}(\widehat{p}_{1})-R_{1}(\widehat{p}_{2})\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{2\kappa_{2}\,\widetilde{C}}{\min\{\widetilde{\gamma},\,\kappa_{1}\}}\,\big(\|g\|_{0,\Omega}+\|p_{\mathrm{D}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}\big)\|\widehat{p}_{1}-\widehat{p}_{2}\|_{0,\Omega}+\dfrac{2\kappa_{2}\,\widetilde{C}}{\min\{\widetilde{\gamma},\,\kappa_{1}\}}\gamma\|R_{1}(\widehat{p}_{2})\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega}\|\widehat{p}_{1}-\widehat{p}_{2}\|_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\qquad+\dfrac{2}{c_{a}\,\min\{\widetilde{\gamma},\,\kappa_{1}\}}\,\gamma^{2}\,\|\widehat{p}_{1}-\widehat{p}_{2}\|_{0,\Omega},

the above, along with the fact that
R_{1}(\widehat{p}_{2})
satisfies (
3.7
) and
\widehat{p}_{2}\in\mathrm{W}
, implies

\displaystyle\|p_{1}-p_{2}\|_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{2\kappa_{2}\,\widetilde{C}}{\min\{\widetilde{\gamma},\,\kappa_{1}\}}\,\big(\|g\|_{0,\Omega}+\|p_{\mathrm{D}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}\big)\|\widehat{p}_{1}-\widehat{p}_{2}\|_{0,\Omega}+\dfrac{2}{c_{a}\,\min\{\widetilde{\gamma},\,\kappa_{1}\}}\,\gamma^{2}\,\|\widehat{p}_{1}-\widehat{p}_{2}\|_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\qquad+\dfrac{2\kappa_{2}\,\widetilde{C}}{\min\{\widetilde{\gamma},\,\kappa_{1}\}}\gamma\Big(C_{1}\,(\|{\bm{u}}_{\mathrm{D}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}+\|\bm{f}\|_{0,\Omega})+\dfrac{1}{c_{a}}\,\gamma\,r\Big)\|\widehat{p}_{1}-\widehat{p}_{2}\|_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{2}{\min\{\widetilde{\gamma},\,\kappa_{1}\}}\,\bigg\{\kappa_{2}\,\widetilde{C}(1+C_{1}\gamma)(\|g\|_{0,\Omega}+\|p_{\mathrm{D}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}+\|{\bm{u}}_{\mathrm{D}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}+\|\bm{f}\|_{0,\Omega})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\dfrac{\gamma^{2}}{c_{a}}(1+\kappa_{2}\,\widetilde{C}\,r)\bigg\}\|\widehat{p}_{1}-\widehat{p}_{2}\|_{0,\Omega},

which together with (
3.15
) and the Banach fixed-point theorem yields that
\mathbf{T}
has a unique fixed point in
\mathrm{W}
.
Finally, (
3.16
) is derived analogously to the estimates in (
3.7
) and (
3.9
), which completes the proof.
∎

4
Finite element discretisation

In this section, we present and analyse the Galerkin scheme for problem (
2.2
). It is worth mentioning upfront that the well-posedness analysis can be straightforwardly extended from the continuous problem to the discrete case. Therefore, we omit many of the details.

4.1
Finite element spaces and Galerkin scheme

Let us consider a regular partition
\mathcal{T}_{h}
of
\bar{\Omega}
made up of triangles
K
(in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
) or tetrahedra
K
(in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
) of diameter
h_{K}
, and denote the mesh size by
h:=\mathop{\rm max}\limits\{h_{K}:\ K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}
.
Given an integer
\ell\geq 0
and
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we first let
\mathrm{P}_{\ell}(K)
be the space of polynomials of degree
\leq\ell
defined on
K
, whose vector and tensor versions are denoted
\mathbf{P}_{\ell}(K)\,:=\,[\mathrm{P}_{\ell}(K)]^{d}
and
\mathbb{P}_{\ell}(K)\,=\,[\mathrm{P}_{\ell}(K)]^{d\times d}
, respectively. Also, we let
\mathbf{RT}_{\ell}(K)\,:=\,\mathbf{P}_{\ell}(K)\oplus\mathrm{P}_{\ell}(K)\,\bm{x}
be the local Raviart–Thomas space of order
\ell
defined on
K
, where
\bm{x}
stands for a generic vector in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and denote by
\mathbb{RT}_{k}(K)
the tensor-valued counterpart of this space.

For each
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
we consider the bubble space of order
k
, defined as

\mathbf{B}_{k}(K):=\begin{cases}{\mathrm{curl}(b_{K})\mathrm{P}_{k}(K)}&\textrm{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
\nabla\times(b_{K}\mathbf{P}_{k}(K))&\textrm{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{3},\end{cases}

where
b_{K}
is a suitably normalised cubic polynomial on
K
, which vanishes on the boundary of
K
(see
[
29
]
).

We recall the classical PEERS
k
elements (cf.
[
5
]
) to define the discrete subspaces for the stress tensor
\bm{\sigma}
, the displacement
{\bm{u}}
, and the rotation tensor
\bm{\rho}

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}

\displaystyle:=\left\{{\bm{\tau}}_{h}\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathbf{div};\Omega):\quad{\bm{\tau}}_{h}|_{K}\in\mathbb{RT}_{k}(K)\oplus[\mathbf{B}_{k}(K)]^{d}\quad\forall\,K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}^{{\bm{u}}}_{h}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega):\quad\mathbf{v}_{h}|_{K}\in\textbf{P}_{k}(K)\quad\forall\,K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\right\},

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{\bm{\rho}}_{h}

\displaystyle:=\left\{{\bm{\eta}}_{h}\in\mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{skew}}^{2}(\Omega)\cap\mathbb{C}(\overline{\Omega})\quad\mathrm{and}\quad{\bm{\eta}}_{h}|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}_{k+1}(K)\quad\forall\,K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\right\},

where
\mathbb{C}(\overline{\Omega})
denotes the space of continuous tensor fields,
and the following estimates are proven for the PEERS
k
elements (cf.
[
42
, Remark 3.3]
)

\displaystyle\sup_{\bm{0}\neq\bm{\tau}_{h}\in\mathbb{H}^{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}}\frac{b(\bm{\tau}_{h},(\mathbf{v}_{h},{\bm{\eta}}_{h}))}{\|\bm{\tau}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega}}

\displaystyle\geq\beta^{*}(\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|{\bm{\eta}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega})\quad\forall\,(\mathbf{v}_{h},{\bm{\eta}}_{h})\in\mathbf{H}^{{\bm{u}}}_{h}\times\mathbb{H}^{\bm{\rho}}_{h},

(4.2a)

\displaystyle\displaystyle a(\bm{\tau}_{h},\bm{\tau}_{h})

\displaystyle\geq c_{a}\|\bm{\tau}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega}^{2}\quad\forall\,\bm{\tau}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h},

(4.2b)

where
\mathbb{V}_{h}
denotes the discrete kernel of
b
, that is

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}_{h}:=\displaystyle\Big\{\bm{\tau}_{h}\in\mathbb{H}^{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}:\quad b(\bm{\tau}_{h},(\mathbf{v}_{h},{\bm{\eta}}_{h}))=0\quad\forall\,(\mathbf{v}_{h},{\bm{\eta}}_{h})\in\mathbf{H}^{{\bm{u}}}_{h}\times\mathbb{H}^{\bm{\rho}}_{h}\Big\}.

Additionally, for
{\bm{\varphi}}
and the pressure
p
, we define the FE subspaces

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}^{{\bm{\varphi}}}_{h}

\displaystyle:=\left\{{\bm{\psi}}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits;\Omega):\ {\bm{\psi}}_{h}|_{K}\in\mathbf{RT}_{k}(K)\quad\forall\,K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{p}_{h}

\displaystyle:=\left\{q_{h}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega):\ q_{h}|_{K}\in\mathrm{P}_{k}(K)\quad\forall\,K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\right\},

(4.3)

and it is well known that
\widetilde{b}
satisfies the inf-sup condition (see, e.g.,
[
21
, Lemma 4.6]
)

\displaystyle\sup_{\bm{0}\neq{\bm{\psi}}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}^{{\bm{\varphi}}}_{h}}\frac{\widetilde{b}({\bm{\psi}}_{h},q_{h})}{\|{\bm{\psi}}_{h}\|_{\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits;\Omega}}\geq\widetilde{\beta}^{*}\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\quad\forall\,q_{h}\in\mathrm{H}^{p}_{h}.

(4.4)

Note that it is of course possible to consider other conforming and inf-sup stable spaces such as Arnold–Falk–Winther and Brezzi–Douglas–Marini instead of (
4.1
) and (
4.3
), respectively.
The Galerkin scheme for (
2.2
) reads: find
(\bm{\sigma}_{h},{\bm{u}}_{h},\bm{\rho}_{h},{\bm{\varphi}}_{h},p_{h})\in\mathbb{H}^{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}\times\mathbf{H}^{{\bm{u}}}_{h}\times\mathbb{H}^{\bm{\rho}}_{h}\times\mathbf{H}^{{\bm{\varphi}}}_{h}\times\mathrm{H}^{p}_{h}
, such that:

\begin{array}[]{rllll}\displaystyle a(\bm{\sigma}_{h},\bm{\tau}_{h})&+\quad\displaystyle b(\bm{\tau}_{h},({\bm{u}}_{h},\bm{\rho}_{h}))&+\quad c(\bm{\tau}_{h},p_{h})&=\displaystyle H(\bm{\tau}_{h})&\forall\,\bm{\tau}_{h}\in\mathbb{H}^{\bm{\sigma}}_{h},\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle b(\bm{\sigma}_{h},(\mathbf{v}_{h},{\bm{\eta}}_{h}))&&&=\displaystyle F(\mathbf{v}_{h},{\bm{\eta}}_{h})&\forall(\mathbf{v}_{h},{\bm{\eta}}_{h})\in\mathbf{H}^{{\bm{u}}}_{h}\times\mathbb{H}^{\bm{\rho}}_{h},\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle\widetilde{a}_{\bm{\sigma}_{h},p_{h}}({\bm{\varphi}}_{h},{\bm{\psi}}_{h})&+\quad\displaystyle\widetilde{b}({\bm{\psi}}_{h},p_{h})&&=\displaystyle\widetilde{H}({\bm{\psi}}_{h})&\forall\,{\bm{\psi}}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}^{{\bm{\varphi}}}_{h},\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle\widetilde{b}({\bm{\varphi}}_{h},q_{h})&-\quad\widetilde{c}(p_{h},q_{h})&-\quad c(\bm{\sigma}_{h},q_{h})&=\displaystyle{\tilde{F}}(q_{h})&\forall\,q_{h}\in\mathrm{H}^{p}_{h}.\end{array}

(4.5)

4.2
Analysis of the discrete problem

In this section, we analyse the Galerkin scheme (
4.5
). It’s worth noting that establishing well-posedness can be readily achieved by extending the results derived for the continuous problem to the discrete setting.
Firstly, and similarly to the continuous case, we define the following set

\mathrm{W}_{h}:=\Big\{\widehat{p}_{h}\in\mathrm{H}^{p}_{h}\,:\quad\|\widehat{p}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\leq r\Big\}.

Next, for a fixed
\widehat{p}_{h}
in
\mathrm{W}_{h}
, we have that the bilinear form
\widetilde{a}_{\bm{\sigma}_{h},p_{h}}
satisfies

\displaystyle\widetilde{a}_{\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{h},\widehat{p}_{h}}({\bm{\psi}}_{h},{\bm{\psi}}_{h})\geq\kappa_{1}\|{\bm{\psi}}_{h}\|_{\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits;\Omega}^{2}\quad\forall\,{\bm{\psi}}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h},

(4.6)

where
\mathbf{V}_{h}
is the discrete kernel of
\widetilde{b}

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}_{h}:=\displaystyle\Big\{{\bm{\psi}}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}^{{\bm{\varphi}}}_{h}:\quad\widetilde{b}({\bm{\psi}}_{h},q_{h})=0\quad\forall\,q_{h}\in\mathrm{H}^{p}_{h}\Big\}.

Additionally, we define the discrete operators
\mathbf{R}_{h}:\mathrm{W}_{h}\subseteq\mathrm{H}^{p}_{h}\to\mathbb{H}^{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}\times(\mathbf{H}^{{\bm{u}}}_{h}\times\mathbb{H}^{\bm{\rho}}_{h})
and
\mathbf{S}_{h}:\mathbb{H}^{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}\times\mathrm{W}_{h}\to\mathbf{H}^{{\bm{\varphi}}}_{h}\times\mathrm{H}^{p}_{h}
, respectively, by

\mathbf{R}_{h}(\widehat{p}_{h}):=\big(R_{1,h}(\widehat{p}_{h}),(R_{2,h}(\widehat{p}_{h}),R_{3,h}(\widehat{p}_{h}))\big)=(\bm{\sigma}_{h},({\bm{u}}_{h},\bm{\rho}_{h}))\quad\forall\,\widehat{p}_{h}\in\mathrm{W}_{h},

where
(\bm{\sigma}_{h},({\bm{u}}_{h},\bm{\rho}_{h}))\in\mathbb{H}^{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}\times(\mathbf{H}^{{\bm{u}}}_{h}\times\mathbb{H}^{\bm{\rho}}_{h})
is the unique solution of

\begin{array}[]{rllll}\displaystyle a(\bm{\sigma}_{h},\bm{\tau}_{h})&+\quad\displaystyle b(\bm{\tau}_{h},({\bm{u}}_{h},\bm{\rho}_{h}))&=\displaystyle H(\bm{\tau}_{h})-c(\bm{\tau}_{h},\widehat{p}_{h})&\forall\,\bm{\tau}_{h}\in\mathbb{H}^{\bm{\sigma}}_{h},\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle b(\bm{\sigma}_{h},(\mathbf{v}_{h},{\bm{\eta}}_{h}))&&=\displaystyle F(\mathbf{v}_{h},{\bm{\eta}}_{h})&\forall\,(\mathbf{v}_{h},{\bm{\eta}}_{h})\in\mathbf{H}^{{\bm{u}}}_{h}\times\mathbb{H}^{\bm{\rho}}_{h},\end{array}

(4.7)

and

\mathbf{S}_{h}(\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{h},\widehat{p}_{h}):=\big(S_{1,h}(\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{h},\widehat{p}_{h}),S_{2,h}(\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{h},\widehat{p}_{h})\big)=({\bm{\varphi}}_{h},p_{h})\quad\forall\,(\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{h},\widehat{p}_{h})\in\mathbb{H}^{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}\times\mathrm{W}_{h},

where
({\bm{\varphi}}_{h},p_{h})
is the unique tuple in
\mathbf{H}^{{\bm{\varphi}}}_{h}\times\times\mathrm{H}^{p}_{h}
such that

\begin{array}[]{rllll}\displaystyle\widetilde{a}_{\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{h},\widehat{p}_{h}}({\bm{\varphi}}_{h},{\bm{\psi}}_{h})&+\quad\displaystyle\widetilde{b}({\bm{\psi}}_{h},p_{h})&=\displaystyle\widetilde{H}({\bm{\psi}}_{h})&\forall\,{\bm{\psi}}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}^{{\bm{\varphi}}}_{h},\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle\widetilde{b}({\bm{\varphi}}_{h},q_{h})&-\quad\widetilde{c}(p_{h},q_{h})&=\displaystyle{\tilde{F}}(q_{h})+c(\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{h},q_{h})&\forall\,q_{h}\in\mathrm{H}^{p}_{h}.\end{array}

Employing properties (
4.2a
), (
4.2b
), (
4.4
), (
4.6
) and (
2.9
) and proceeding exactly as for the continuous case (Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
), it can be
easily deduced that both operators are well-defined. Then, analogously to the continuous case, we define the following fixed-point operator

\mathbf{T}_{h}:\mathrm{W}_{h}\subseteq\mathrm{H}^{p}_{h}\to\mathrm{H}^{p}_{h},\quad\widehat{p}_{h}\mapsto\mathbf{T}_{h}(\widehat{p}_{h}):=S_{2,h}(R_{1,h}(\widehat{p}_{h}),\widehat{p}_{h}),

(4.8)

which is clearly well-defined (since
R_{h}
and
S_{h}
are). Further, it can be easily deduced that
\mathbf{T}_{h}(\mathrm{W}_{h})\subseteq\mathrm{W}_{h}
if

\widetilde{C}^{*}\big(1+\,\gamma\,C_{1}^{*}\big)\big(\|g\|_{0,\Omega}+\|p_{\mathrm{D}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}+\|{\bm{u}}_{\mathrm{D}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}+\|\bm{f}\|_{0,\Omega}\big)+\dfrac{\widetilde{C}^{*}}{c_{a}}\,\gamma^{2}r\leq r,

(4.9)

where
\widetilde{C}^{*}
and
C_{1}^{*}
(depending on
c_{a}
,
\mu
,
\kappa_{1}
,
\kappa_{2}
,
C_{\widetilde{a}}
,
\beta^{*}
,
\widetilde{\beta}^{*}
) are the discrete versions of the constants
\widetilde{C}
and
C_{1}
(cf. (
3.7
) and (
3.9
)). Finally, it is clear that
(\bm{\sigma}_{h},{\bm{u}}_{h},\bm{\rho}_{h},{\bm{\varphi}}_{h},p_{h})
is a solution to (
4.5
) if and only if
p_{h}
satisfies

\mathbf{T}_{h}(p_{h})=p_{h}.

(4.10)

The main outcome of this section is presented in the following theorem, establishing the existence and uniqueness of a solution to the fixed-point problem (
4.10
), equivalently proving the well-posedness of problem (
4.5
).

Theorem 4.1
.

Given
r>0
, assume that
the data and
\gamma
satisfy

\frac{2\mathop{\rm max}\limits\{1,\,\kappa_{2}\}}{\min\{\widetilde{\gamma},\,\kappa_{1}\,,r\}}\,\bigg\{\widetilde{C}^{*}(1+C_{1}^{*}\gamma)(\|g\|_{0,\Omega}+\|p_{\mathrm{D}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}+\|{\bm{u}}_{\mathrm{D}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}+\|\bm{f}\|_{0,\Omega})+\dfrac{\gamma^{2}}{c_{a}}\biggl(\frac{1}{\kappa_{2}}+\widetilde{C}^{*}\,r\biggr)\bigg\}<1.

(4.11)

Then,
\mathbf{T}_{h}
(cf. (
4.8
)) has a unique fixed point
p_{h}\in\mathrm{W}_{h}
. Equivalently, problem (
4.5
) has a unique solution
(\bm{\sigma}_{h},{\bm{u}}_{h},\bm{\rho}_{h},{\bm{\varphi}}_{h},p_{h})\in\mathbb{H}^{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}\times\mathbf{H}^{{\bm{u}}}_{h}\times\mathbb{H}^{\bm{\rho}}_{h}\times\mathbf{H}^{{\bm{\varphi}}}_{h}\times\mathrm{W}_{h}
.In addition,
there exists
C^{*}>0
, such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\bm{\sigma}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega}+\|{\bm{u}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|\bm{\rho}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|{\bm{\varphi}}_{h}\|_{\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits;\Omega}+\|p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C^{*}(\|g\|_{0,\Omega}+\|p_{\mathrm{D}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}+\|{\bm{u}}_{\mathrm{D}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}+\|\bm{f}\|_{0,\Omega}+\gamma\,r\,).

(4.12)

Proof.

First, we observe that, similar to the continuous case (as seen in the proof of Theorem
3.8
), assumption (
4.11
) ensures the well-definedness of
\mathbf{T}_{h}
and that
\mathbf{T}_{h}(\mathrm{W}_{h})\subseteq\mathrm{W}_{h}
. Now, by adapting the arguments used in Section
3.3
(cf. Lemmas
3.6
and
3.7
), one can derive the following estimates

\displaystyle\|R_{1,h}(\widehat{p}_{1})-R_{1,h}(\widehat{p}_{2})\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{c_{a}}\,\gamma\,\|\widehat{p}_{1}-\widehat{p}_{2}\|_{0,\Omega},

\displaystyle\|S_{2,h}(\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{1},\,\widehat{p}_{1})-S_{2,h}(\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{2},\,\widehat{p}_{2})\|_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\kappa_{2}\,\widetilde{C}^{*}}{\min\{\widetilde{\gamma},\,\kappa_{1}\}}\,\big(\|g\|_{0,\Omega}+\|p_{\mathrm{D}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}+\gamma\|\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{2}\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega}\big)\|\widehat{p}_{1}-\widehat{p}_{2}\|_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2}{\min\{\widetilde{\gamma},\,\kappa_{1}\}}\,\gamma\,\|\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{1}-\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{2}\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega},

for all
\widehat{p}_{1},\,\widehat{p}_{2}\in\mathrm{W}_{h}
and
\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{1},\,\widehat{\bm{\sigma}}_{2}\in\mathbb{H}^{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}
, which together with the definition of
\mathbf{T}_{h}
(see (
4.8
)), yield

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{T}_{h}(\widehat{p}_{1})-\mathbf{T}_{h}(\widehat{p}_{2})\|_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{2}{\min\{\widetilde{\gamma},\,\kappa_{1}\}}\bigg\{\kappa_{2}\,\widetilde{C}^{*}(1+C_{1}^{*}\gamma)(\|g\|_{0,\Omega}+\|p_{\mathrm{D}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}+\|{\bm{u}}_{\mathrm{D}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}+\|\bm{f}\|_{0,\Omega})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\dfrac{\gamma^{2}}{c_{a}}(1+\kappa_{2}\,\widetilde{C}^{*}\,r)\bigg\}\|\widehat{p}_{1}-\widehat{p}_{2}\|_{0,\Omega},

for all
\widehat{p}_{1},\,\widehat{p}_{2}\in\mathrm{W}_{h}
. In this way, using estimate (
4.11
) we obtain that
\mathbf{T}_{h}
is a contraction mapping on
\mathrm{W}_{h}
, thus problem (
4.10
), or equivalently (
4.5
) is well-posed.
Finally, analogously to the proof of Theorem
3.8
(see also Lemmas
3.6
and

\|\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega}+\|{\bm{u}}-{\bm{u}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|\bm{\rho}-\bm{\rho}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\,\leq\,C_{5}\Bigg\{\,\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Xi_{s,K}^{2}\Bigg\}^{1/2}+C_{5}\gamma\|p-p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}.

Proof.

Let
\bm{\tau}\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathbf{div};\Omega)
. From
[
3
, Lemma 3.9]
, there exist
\bm{\xi}\in\mathbb{H}^{1}(\Omega)
and
{\bm{\chi}}\in\mathbf{H}_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}}^{1}(\Omega)
, such that

\bm{\tau}=\bm{\xi}+\mathbf{curl}\,{\bm{\chi}}{\quad\hbox{and}\quad}\|\bm{\xi}\|_{1,\Omega}+\|{\bm{\chi}}\|_{1,\Omega}\leq C_{\mathrm{Helm}}\|\bm{\tau}\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega},

(6.4)

Using that
\mathcal{R}_{1}(\bm{\tau}_{h})=c(\bm{\tau}_{h},p_{h}-p)
for all
\bm{\tau}_{h}\in\mathbb{H}^{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}
, we have

\mathcal{R}_{1}(\bm{\tau})=\mathcal{R}_{1}(\bm{\tau}-\bm{\tau}_{h})+c(\bm{\tau}_{h},p-p_{h})\quad\forall\,\bm{\tau}_{h}\in\mathbb{H}^{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}.

In particular, this holds for
\bm{\tau}_{h}
defined as
\displaystyle\bm{\tau}_{h}=\mbox{\boldmath{$\Pi$}}_{h}\bm{\xi}+\mathbf{curl}\,(\mathbf{I}_{h}{\bm{\chi}})
, whence

\mathcal{R}_{1}(\bm{\tau})=\mathcal{R}_{1}(\bm{\xi}-\mbox{\boldmath{$\Pi$}}_{h}\bm{\xi})+\mathcal{R}_{1}(\mathrm{curl}\,({\bm{\chi}}-\mathbf{I}_{h}{\bm{\chi}}))+c(\bm{\tau}_{h},p-p_{h}).

Hence, the proof follows from Lemmas
6.1
,
6.3
and
6.4
, and estimate (
6.4
).
∎

The following lemma establishes the estimate for
\mathcal{R}_{2}
.

Lemma 6.6
.

Assume that there exists a convex domain
B
such that
\Omega\subseteq B
and
\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}\subseteq\partial B
, and that
p_{\mathrm{D}}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}})
. Then, there exists a constant
C_{8}>0
, independent of
h
, such that

\|{\bm{\varphi}}-{\bm{\varphi}}_{h}\|_{\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits;\Omega}+\|p-p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\,\leq\,C_{8}\Bigg\{\,\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Xi_{f,K}^{2}\Bigg\}^{1/2}+C_{8}\big(\|{\bm{\varphi}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\|p-p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}+\gamma\|\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\big).

Proof.

It follows the steps of Lemma
6.5
. From
[
25
, Lemma 4.4]
, we have that for all
{\bm{\psi}}\in\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits;\Omega)
there exist
\mbox{\boldmath{$z$}}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
and
\phi\in\mathrm{H}_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}}^{1}(\Omega)
, such that
{\bm{\psi}}=\mbox{\boldmath{$z$}}+\mathrm{curl}\,\phi
and
\|\mbox{\boldmath{$z$}}\|_{1,\Omega}+\|\phi\|_{1,\Omega}\leq\widetilde{C}_{\mathrm{Helm}}\|{\bm{\psi}}\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega}
.
Thus, proceeding similarly to Lemmas
6.3
and
6.4
, applying local integration by parts and the approximation properties of
\Pi_{h}
and
I_{h}
along with the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we can obtain the following estimates

\begin{array}[]{ll}|\mathcal{R}_{2}(\mbox{\boldmath{$z$}}-\Pi_{h}(\mbox{\boldmath{$z$}}))|\,\leq C_{6}\,\|{\bm{\varphi}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\|p-p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\mbox{\boldmath{$z$}}\|_{1,\Omega}\\
\qquad\displaystyle+C_{6}\biggr(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}^{2}\|{\bm{\kappa}}(\bm{\sigma}_{h},p_{h})^{-1}{\bm{\varphi}}_{h}-\nabla p_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}})}h_{E}\|p_{\mathrm{D}}-p_{h}\|_{0,E}^{2}\biggr)^{1/2}\|\mbox{\boldmath{$z$}}\|_{1,\Omega},\\[17.22217pt]
\displaystyle|\mathcal{R}_{2}(\mathrm{curl}\,(\phi-I_{h}\phi))|\leq C_{7}\|{\bm{\varphi}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\|p-p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\phi\|_{1,\Omega}\\[8.61108pt]
\qquad\displaystyle+C_{7}\biggr(\,\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}^{2}\|\mathrm{rot}\,({\bm{\kappa}}(\bm{\sigma}_{h},p_{h})^{-1}{\bm{\varphi}}_{h})\|_{0,K}^{2}+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}(\Omega)}h_{E}\|[\![({\bm{\kappa}}(\bm{\sigma}_{h},p_{h})^{-1}{\bm{\varphi}}_{h})\cdot\bm{s}]\!]\|_{0,E}^{2}\\[8.61108pt]
\qquad\displaystyle+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}})}h_{E}\|{\bm{\kappa}}(\bm{\sigma}_{h},p_{h})^{-1}{\bm{\varphi}}_{h}\cdot\bm{s}-\frac{\mathrm{d}p_{\mathrm{D}}}{\mathrm{d}\bm{s}}\|_{0,E}^{2}\biggr)^{1/2}\|\phi\|_{1,\Omega}.\end{array}

(6.5)

Then, noting that
\mathcal{R}_{2}({\bm{\psi}}_{h})=0
for all
{\bm{\psi}}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}^{{\bm{\varphi}}}_{h}
, and defining
{\bm{\psi}}_{h}
as
\displaystyle{\bm{\psi}}_{h}=\Pi_{h}\mbox{\boldmath{$z$}}+\mathrm{curl}\,(I_{h}\phi)
, we have

\mathcal{R}_{2}({\bm{\psi}})=\mathcal{R}_{2}({\bm{\psi}}-{\bm{\psi}}_{h})=\mathcal{R}_{2}(\mbox{\boldmath{$z$}}-\Pi_{h}\mbox{\boldmath{$z$}})+\mathcal{R}_{2}(\mathrm{curl}\,(\phi-I_{h}\phi)).

Hence, the proof follows from Lemma
6.2
, estimates (
6.5
), and the Helmholtz decomposition of
\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits;\Omega)
.
∎

Finally we state the main reliability bound for the proposed estimator.

Theorem 6.7
.

Assume the hypotheses stated in Theorem
5.3
and Lemmas
6.5
-
6.6
. Let
(\bm{\sigma},{\bm{u}},\bm{\rho},{\bm{\varphi}},p)\in\mathbb{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathbf{div};\Omega)\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)\times\mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{skew}}^{2}(\Omega)\times\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits;\Omega)\times\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
and
(\bm{\sigma}_{h},{\bm{u}}_{h},\bm{\rho}_{h},{\bm{\varphi}}_{h},p_{h})\in\mathbb{H}^{\bm{\sigma}}_{h}\times\mathbf{H}^{{\bm{u}}}_{h}\times\mathbb{H}^{\bm{\rho}}_{h}\times\mathbf{H}^{{\bm{\varphi}}}_{h}\times\mathrm{H}^{p}_{h}
be the unique solutions of (
2.2
) and (
4.5
), respectively.
Assume further that

(C_{5}+C_{8})\gamma+C_{8}C^{*}(\|g\|_{0,\Omega}+\|p_{\mathrm{D}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}+\|{\bm{u}}_{\mathrm{D}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}+\|\bm{f}\|_{0,\Omega}+\gamma\,r\,)\leq\frac{1}{2}.

(6.6)

Then, there exists
C_{\mathrm{rel}}>0
, independent of
h
, such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\texttt{e}_{\bm{\sigma}}\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega}+\|\texttt{e}_{{\bm{u}}}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|\texttt{e}_{\bm{\rho}}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|\texttt{e}_{{\bm{\varphi}}}\|_{\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits;\Omega}+\|\texttt{e}_{p}\|_{0,\Omega}\leq\,C_{\mathrm{rel}}\,\Xi.

Proof.

It follows directly from Lemmas
6.5
and
6.6
, using the fact that
{\bm{\varphi}}_{h}
satisfies the estimate (
4.12
), and the assumption (
6.6
).
∎

6.2
Efficiency of the a posteriori error estimator

In this section we derive the efficiency estimate of the estimator defined in (
6.2
). The main result of this section is stated as follows.

Theorem 6.8
.

There exists
C_{\mathrm{eff}}>0
, independent of
h
, such that

C_{\mathrm{eff}}\,\Xi\,\leq\,\|\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega}+\|{\bm{u}}-{\bm{u}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|\bm{\rho}-\bm{\rho}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|{\bm{\varphi}}-{\bm{\varphi}}_{h}\|_{\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits;\Omega}+\|p-p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}+{\rm h.o.t},

(6.7)

where
{\rm h.o.t.}
stands for one or several terms of higher order.

We begin with the estimates for the zero order terms appearing in the definition of
\Xi_{s,K}
(cf. (
6.1a
)).

Lemma 6.9
.

For all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
there holds

\|\bm{f}+\mathbf{div}\bm{\sigma}_{h}\|_{0,K}\lesssim\|\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{div};K}\,{\quad\hbox{and}\quad}\|\bm{\sigma}_{h}-\bm{\sigma}^{\tt t}_{h}\|_{0,K}\lesssim\|\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{div};K}.

Proof.

By employing the same arguments as in
[
17
, Theorem 3.2]
, we can conclude that
\bm{f}=-\mathbf{div}\,\bm{\sigma}
, which, together with the symmetry of
\bm{\sigma}
, implies the desired result. Further details are omitted.
∎

In order to derive the upper bounds for the remaining terms
defining the error estimator
\Xi_{s,K}
, we use results from
[
19
]
, inverse inequalities, and the localisation technique based on element-bubble and edge-bubble functions. The main properties that we will use can be found in
[
33
, Lemmas 4.4-4.7]
.

Lemma 6.10
.

For all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
there holds

\begin{array}[]{ll}h_{K}\|\mathcal{C}^{-1}\bm{\sigma}_{h}+\dfrac{\alpha}{d\lambda+2\mu}p_{h}\mathbf{I}-\bm{\nabla}{\bm{u}}_{h}+\bm{\rho}_{h}\|_{0,K}\\
\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim h_{K}\big(\|\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{div};K}+\|\bm{\rho}-\bm{\rho}_{h}\|_{0,K}+\|p-p_{h}\|_{0,K}\big)+\|{\bm{u}}-{\bm{u}}_{h}\|_{0,K}.\end{array}

Proof.

It follows from an application of
[
33
, Lemma 4.4]
with
\mbox{\boldmath{$q$}}=\mathcal{C}^{-1}\bm{\sigma}_{h}+\dfrac{\alpha}{d\lambda+2\mu}p_{h}\mathbf{I}-\bm{\nabla}{\bm{u}}_{h}+\bm{\rho}_{h}
, using that
\mathcal{C}^{-1}\bm{\sigma}+\dfrac{\alpha}{d\lambda+2\mu}p\mathbf{I}=\bm{\nabla}{\bm{u}}-\bm{\rho}
and

[
33
, Lemma 4.5]
. We refer to
[
19
, Lemma 6.6]
for further details.
∎

Lemma 6.11
.

For all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and
E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}(\Omega)
, there holds

\begin{array}[]{ll}h_{K}\|\underline{\mathbf{curl}}(\mathcal{C}^{-1}\bm{\sigma}_{h}+\dfrac{\alpha}{d\lambda+2\mu}p_{h}\mathbf{I}+\bm{\rho}_{h})\|_{0,K}\lesssim\|\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{div};K}+\|\bm{\rho}-\bm{\rho}_{h}\|_{0,K}+\|p-p_{h}\|_{0,K},\\[12.91663pt]
h_{E}^{1/2}\|[\![(\mathcal{C}^{-1}\bm{\sigma}_{h}+\dfrac{\alpha}{d\lambda+2\mu}p_{h}\mathbf{I}+\bm{\rho}_{h})\bm{s}]\!]\|_{0,E}\lesssim\|\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{div};\omega_{E}}+\|\bm{\rho}-\bm{\rho}_{h}\|_{0,\omega_{E}}+\|p-p_{h}\|_{0,\omega_{E}},\end{array}

where the patch of elements sharing the edge
E
is denoted as
\omega_{E}:=\cup\{K^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\colon E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}(K^{\prime})\}
.

Proof.

It suffices to apply
[
33
, Lemma 4.7]
with
\bm{\xi}:=\mathcal{C}^{-1}\bm{\sigma}+\dfrac{\alpha}{d\lambda+2\mu}p\mathbf{I}+\bm{\rho}=\bm{\nabla}{\bm{u}}
and
\bm{\xi}_{h}:=\mathcal{C}^{-1}\bm{\sigma}_{h}+\dfrac{\alpha}{d\lambda+2\mu}p_{h}\mathbf{I}+\bm{\rho}_{h}
.
∎

Lemma 6.12
.

Assume that
{\bm{u}}_{\mathrm{D}}
is piecewise polynomial. Then, for all
E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}})
, there holds

\begin{array}[]{ll}h_{E}^{1/2}\|(\mathcal{C}^{-1}\bm{\sigma}_{h}+\dfrac{\alpha}{d\lambda+2\mu}p_{h}\mathbf{I}+\bm{\rho}_{h})\bm{s}-\frac{\mathrm{d}{\bm{u}}_{\mathrm{D}}}{\mathrm{d}\bm{s}}\|_{0,E}\lesssim\|\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{div};K_{E}}\!+\!\|\bm{\rho}-\bm{\rho}_{h}\|_{0,K_{E}}\!+\!\|p-p_{h}\|_{0,K_{E}},\\[8.61108pt]
h_{E}^{1/2}\|{\bm{u}}_{\mathrm{D}}-{\bm{u}}_{h}\|_{0,E}\lesssim h_{K_{E}}\big(\|\bm{\sigma}-\bm{\sigma}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{div};K_{E}}+\|\bm{\rho}-\bm{\rho}_{h}\|_{0,K_{E}}+\|p-p_{h}\|_{0,K_{E}}\big)+\|{\bm{u}}-{\bm{u}}_{h}\|_{0,K_{E}},\end{array}

where
K_{E}
is a triangle in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
that contains
E
on its boundary.

Proof.

The first estimate follows as in
[
33
, Lemma 4.18]
, defining
\bm{\xi}
and
\bm{\xi}_{h}
as in Lemma
6.11
. On the other hand, the second estimate follows from an application of the discrete trace inequality (see
[
33
, Lemma 4.6]
), using that
\mathcal{C}^{-1}\bm{\sigma}+\dfrac{\alpha}{d\lambda+2\mu}p\mathbf{I}=\bm{\nabla}{\bm{u}}-\bm{\rho}
, and the fact that
{\bm{u}}={\bm{u}}_{\mathrm{D}}
on
\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}
. See also
[
35
, Lemma 4.14]
.
∎

A direct application of Lemmas
6.9
-
6.12
yields

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Xi_{s,K}\lesssim\|\texttt{e}_{\bm{\sigma}}\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega}+\|\texttt{e}_{{\bm{u}}}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|\texttt{e}_{\bm{\rho}}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|\texttt{e}_{{\bm{\varphi}}}\|_{\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits;\Omega}+\|\texttt{e}_{p}\|_{0,\Omega}.

(6.8)

Similarly, using the same arguments as in Lemmas
6.9
-
6.12
, along with algebraic manipulations as in Section
5
, assuming that
p_{\mathrm{D}}
is piecewise polynomial, together with the Lipschitz continuity of
{\bm{\kappa}}
(cf. (
2.3
)), we can bound each of the terms that appear in the estimator
\Xi_{f,K}
and obtain the following result

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Xi_{f,K}\lesssim\|\texttt{e}_{\bm{\sigma}}\|_{\mathbf{div};\Omega}+\|\texttt{e}_{{\bm{u}}}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|\texttt{e}_{\bm{\rho}}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|\texttt{e}_{{\bm{\varphi}}}\|_{\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits;\Omega}+\|\texttt{e}_{p}\|_{0,\Omega}.

(6.9)

We remark that the efficiency of
\Xi
(cf. (
6.2
)) in Theorem
6.8
is now a straightforward consequence of estimates (
6.8
) and (
6.9
). In turn, we emphasize that the resulting constant, denoted by
C_{\mathrm{eff}}>0
is independent of
h
.

Remark 6.13
.

For simplicity, we have assumed that
{\bm{u}}_{\mathrm{D}}
and
p_{\mathrm{D}}
are piecewise polynomial in the derivation of (
6.8
) and (
6.9
). However, similar estimates can also be obtained by assuming
{\bm{u}}_{\mathrm{D}}
and
p_{\mathrm{D}}
are sufficiently smooth (taking, for example,
{\bm{u}}_{\mathrm{D}}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}})
and
p_{\mathrm{D}}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}})
, as in Lemmas
6.5
-
6.6
), and proceeding as in
[
23
, Section 6.2]
. In such cases, higher-order terms, stemming from errors in the polynomial approximations, would appear in (
6.8
) and (
6.9
), accounting for the presence of
{\rm h.o.t}
in (
6.7
).

Remark 6.14
.

We conclude this section by noting that the a posteriori error estimation analysis developed here can be adapted to the three-dimensional case. In particular, in
[
32
, Th 3.2]
and
[
25
, Lemma 4.4]
, one can find the suitable Helmholtz decompositions for the spaces
\mathbb{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathbf{div};\Omega)
and
\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits;\Omega)
, respectively.

7
Numerical results

The computational examples in this section verify the theoretical properties (optimal convergence, conservativity, and parameter robustness) of the proposed schemes. The implementation has been carried out using the FE library FEniCS
[
1
]
. The nonlinear systems were solved with Newton–Raphson’s method with a residual tolerance of
10^{-7}
, and the linear systems were solved using the sparse LU factorisation of MUMPS
[
4
]
.

7.1

We first consider a simple planar problem setup with manufactured exact solution. We take the unit square domain
\Omega=(0,1)^{2}
, the bottom and left segments represent
\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}
and the top and right sides are
\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}
. We choose the body load
\bm{f}
, mass source
g
, boundary displacement
{\bm{u}}_{\mathrm{D}}
, boundary pressure
p_{\mathrm{D}}
, as well as (not necessarily homogeneous, but standard arguments can be used to extend the theory to the inhomogeneous case) boundary data
{\bm{\varphi}}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath{$n$}}=\varphi_{\mathrm{N}}

1.8

7.3
Convergence in the case of adaptive mesh refinement

We continue with a test targeting the recovery of optimal convergence through adaptive mesh refinement guided by the a posteriori error estimator proposed in Section
6
.
We employ the well-known adaptive mesh refinement approach of solving, then computing the estimator, marking, and refining. Marking is done as follows
[
27
]
: a given
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
is added to the marking set
\mathcal{M}_{h}\subset\mathcal{T}_{h}
whenever the local error indicator
\Xi_{K}
satisfies

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}_{h}}\Xi^{2}_{K}\geq\zeta\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Xi_{K}^{2},

where
\zeta
is a user-defined bulk density. All elements in
\mathcal{M}_{h}
are marked for refinement and also some neighbours are marked for the sake of closure. For convergence rates we use the alternative form

\text{rate}=-2\log(e/\widehat{e})[\log(\text{DoFs}/\widehat{\text{ DoF}})]^{-1}.

Let us consider the non-convex rotated L-shaped domain
\Omega=(-1,1)^{2}\setminus(-1,0)^{2}
and use manufactured displacement and fluid pressure with sharp gradients near the domain re-entrant corner (see, e.g.,
[

]
for the displacement and
[

]

\displaystyle{\bm{u}}(r,\theta)=\frac{r^{\chi}}{2\mu}\begin{pmatrix}-(\chi+1)\cos([\chi+1]\theta)+(M_{2}-\chi-1)M_{1}\cos([\chi-1]\theta)\\
(\chi+1)\sin([\chi+1]\theta)+(M_{2}+\chi-1)M_{1}\sin([\chi-1]\theta)\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle p(r,\theta)=r^{1/3}\sin\biggl(\frac{1}{3}(\frac{\pi}{2}+\theta)\biggr),

with polar coordinates

8
Concluding remarks

In this work, we developed a family of mixed finite element methods for a nonlinear poroelasticity model with stress-dependent permeability. By reformulating the constitutive equations, we enabled the use of a Hellinger–Reissner-type mixed formulation, ensuring robustness with respect to near-incompressibility and vanishing storativity limits. The problem structure was analysed using fixed-point theory and saddle-point formulations, leading to well-posedness results for both the continuous and discrete settings. The chosen finite element spaces (PEERS
k
elements for elasticity and Raviart–Thomas elements for fluid flow) provided exact equilibrium and mass conservation.

We derived a priori error estimates, demonstrating optimal convergence properties. Furthermore, a residual-based a posteriori error estimator was introduced and shown to be both reliable and efficient. This estimator guided adaptive mesh refinement strategies, which were validated through numerical experiments in both two and three dimensions.

The proposed nonlinear dependence of permeability on stress in poroelasticity is particularly relevant for soft tissues, where permeability changes due to deformation, and for subsurface reservoirs, where stress variations influence fluid transport. The current formulation can be adapted to different material systems by choosing appropriate functional forms for the permeability-stress relationship including also anisotropic permeability variations.

We are keen to follow other extensions, for instance exploring different abstract results that would impose a less restrictive assumption on smallness of data, and extend the formulation to accommodate a nonlinear stress-strain constitutive law, transient effects, as well as fully coupled multiphysics systems, such as thermo-poroelasticity. Note however that these generalisations (useful to broaden the applicability of the proposed methods to more complex real-world scenarios) will require a substantially different theoretical framework.

Binomial rings, and integral homology of complements of compact toric arrangements
Alexey G. Gorinov, Alexander V. Zakharov

Abstract
An affine subtorus of the compact torus T = ( S 1 ) n T=(S^{1})^{n} is a translated copy of a Lie subgroup. Given a finite collection T 1 , … , T k T_{1},\ldots,T_{k} of such subtori, and a prime p p , we describe an explicit chain complex that calculates the group H ∗ ​ ( T − ⋃ i = 1 k T i , ℤ ( p ) ) H_{*}(T-\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}T_{i},\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}) . Our main tool is the binomial models for spaces constructed by T. Ekedahl. We use these results to express the groups H ∗ ​ ( T − ⋃ i = 1 k T i , ℤ ) H_{*}(T-\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}T_{i},\mathbb{Z}) . We also show that the Mayer-Vietoris spectral sequence that converges to the homology of T − ⋃ i = 1 k T i T-\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}T_{i} collapses at the second page rationally, and also integrally under some assumptions on the arrangement T 1 , … , T k T_{1},\ldots,T_{k} , with all extension problems being trivial in the latter case.



1
Introduction

There are quite a lot of results in the literature about the topology of the complement
T-\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}T_{i}
where
T
is the complex algebraic torus
({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n}
, and
T_{1},\ldots,T_{k}
are (possibly translated) complex algebraic subgroups.

For example, for any arrangement of subtori
T_{i}
, L. Moci and R. Pagaria in
[
14
]

described the homotopy type of the one point compactification of
\cup_{i}T_{i}
.
Moreover, there is an automorphism of
T
that takes each
T_{i}
to a subgroup which is closed in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\supset({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n}=T
. This observation allows one to apply the Alexander duality to recover the additive structure of the integral cohomology of
T-\cup T_{i}
. As a corollary, the authors deduce the degeneration of the integral Leray spectral sequence of the open embedding
T-\cup_{i}T_{i}\subset T
at
E_{2}
. This gives one the multiplicative structure of
H^{*}(T-\cup_{i}T_{i})
up to passing to the associated graded ring.
There are more precise results on the rational homotopy type of
T-\cup_{i}T_{i}
by C. De Concini and G. Gaiffi
[
4
]
,
and on the integral cohomology in the case of a codimension one arrangement by F. Callegaro and E. Delucchi
[
5
]
, and by F. Callegaro, M. D’Adderio, E. Delucchi, L. Migliorini, and R. Pagaria
[
3
]
. It is unclear to us though if these results allow one to compute
H^{*}(T,\cup_{i}T_{i})
.

Suppose however that
T
is the compact torus
T=(S^{1})^{n}
, and
T_{1},\ldots,T_{k}\subset T
is a finite collection of (possibly translated) Lie subgroups. This is the notation we will use in the rest of the paper. If
T
and all
T_{i}
are complex algebraic (and each
T_{i}\subset T
is a closed embedding of complex algebraic varieties), then one can give a model for the rational homotopy type of
T-\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}T_{i}
, see A. Zakharov
[
15
]
. Also, the rational Betti numbers were calculated in some cases by P. Kalugin in
[
11
]
. This completes the list of the known results that we are aware of.

Our goal in this note is to calculate the homology groups of
T^{n}-\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}T_{i}
with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}
and
{\mathbb{Q}}
, see Theorems
1
,
2
,
3
, and
4
. The rational case (Theorem
3
) is a practice example: it uses only the classical Mayer-Vietoris spectral sequence, but it also shows one what to look for when the coefficients are more complicated. To handle the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}
case (Theorem
1
) we use cosimplicial binomial algebras. We do not know how to solve the problem using more standard methods.

For general results on modelling homotopy types by binomial algebras, and the relation to Grothendieck’s schematisation programme we refer the reader to the papers
[
8
]
by T. Ekedahl and
[
17
]
by B. Toën, as well as to later work by G. Horel
[
7
]
and B. Antieau
[
1
]
. As an illustration, the singular cochains of Abelian Eilenberg-Maclane spaces are free derived binomial algebras.
Moreover, the cochains functor taking values in derived binomial algebras provides a fully faithful embedding of the homotopy category of nilpotent spaces.
It is worthwhile to compare this with a well-known result by M. Mandell, who showed in
[
13
]
that the cochain functor with values in
E_{\infty}
-algebras (and even derived commutative algebras) over the integers provides only a faithful embedding.
The case of a nilpotent
K(\pi,1)
was studied in
[
16
]
by R. Porter and A. Suciu. In this note we apply the binomial model of the simplest possible Eilenberg-MacLane space, namely the torus, to complements of arrangements.

Theorem
2
provides a somewhat simpler answer than Theorem
1
for homology with coefficients in a general binomial ring
R
(e.g. in
R={\mathbb{Z}}
) assuming all intersections of the
T_{i}
’s are path connected. Assume in addition that the functor
I\mapsto H_{1}(\bigcap_{i\in I}T_{i},{\mathbb{Z}})
from the subsets of
\{1,\ldots,k\}
to free Abelian groups lifts to a functor to free groups. We prove then (Theorem
4
) that the first page of the Mayer-Vietoris spectral sequence already gives one the answer, same as in the rational case (Theorem
3
).

The cochain complexes given by Theorems
1
and
2
have infinite rank, but it is easy enough to find appropriate truncations which result in finite rank complexes with the same homology, see Corollaries
2
and
1
respectively. Combining Corollary
2
(or Theorem
1
) with a bound on the set of the torsion primes (Proposition
3
) one gets the integral homology of
T^{n}-\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}T_{i}
for an arbitrary arrangement of affine subtori of
T
.

We would also like to mention that the homology groups of the hull of a quasi-periodic structure in the theory of quasi-crystals studied by J. Bellissard
[
2
]
and P. Kalugin
[
11
]
are isomorphic to those of
T^{n}-\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}T_{i}
for some choice of
T
and
T_{1},\ldots,T_{k}
. This fact was the initial motivation behind this project.

Organisation of the note.
In Section
2
we state our main results, which we then prove in Sections
3
and
4
.

Notation and main results

Binomial monad.
Recall that a commutative ring
R
is called
binomial
if the underlying Abelian group is torsion free, and for every
x\in R
and
l\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
, the binomial coefficient
\binom{x}{l}:=\frac{x(x-1)\cdot\cdots\cdot(x-l+1)}{l!}\in R\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
in fact belongs to
R
. Examples of binomial rings include
{\mathbb{Z}}
localised at any (possibly empty) set of primes, and any
{\mathbb{Q}}
-algebra. Binomial rings (and arbitrary ring maps) form a bicomplete category. The forgetful functor from binomial rings to Abelian groups has a left adjoint, which we will denote
\Bin
. Viewed as a functor
{\mathrm{Ab}}\ar{\mathrm{Ab}}
,
\Bin
is a monad.

The free binomial ring
\Bin({\mathbb{Z}}^{m})
on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
is the ring of the polynomials in
{\mathbb{Q}}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}]
that take integer values at integer points; as a ring,
\Bin({\mathbb{Z}}^{m})
is generated by the binomial coefficients
\binom{t_{i}}{l}=\frac{t_{i}(t_{i}-1)\cdot\cdots\cdot(t_{i}-l+1)}{l!}
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
and
l\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
. We extend
\Bin
object-wise to a monad

\Bin:[\triangle,{\mathrm{Ab}}]\ar[\triangle,{\mathrm{Ab}}]

on the category
[\triangle,{\mathrm{Ab}}]
of cosimplicial Abelian groups, which is equivalent to
C^{\geq 0}({\mathbb{Z}})
by the Dold-Kan correspondence, giving us a monad

\Bin:C^{\geq 0}({\mathbb{Z}})\ar C^{\geq 0}({\mathbb{Z}}).

We will use these two versions of
\Bin
interchangeably.

Slightly generalising the above, suppose
A
is a binomial ring. A
binomial algebra
over
A
, or a
binomial
A
-algebra
is a binomial ring
R
equipped with a map of rings
A\ar R
. The forgetful functor from binomial
A
-algebras to
A
-modules has a left adjoint, denoted
\Bin_{A}(-)
, which is a monad when viewed as an endofunctor of
A\mbox{-}\mathrm{mod}
. We have for example
\Bin_{A}(A^{m})=\Bin({\mathbb{Z}}^{m})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}A
. Same as for
A={\mathbb{Z}}
, the category of binomial
A
-algebras is bicomplete. Repeating verbatim the above construction we get a monad, which we denote
\Bin_{A}
, on the category of cosimplicial
A
-modules, or equivalently, on
C^{\geq 0}(A)
.

We refer the reader to
[

,

A cochain complex
A^{*}
.

Let us identify

, and let

be the projection. For a

to denote the

The complex

\langle dx_{1}|_{U},\ldots,dx_{n}|_{U}\rangle

The degree

f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x_{i}+a_{0}

(1)

for some

Observe that

Remark 1
.

Note that along with

A few cocubical cochain complexes.

Let

Mayer-Vietoris construction.
Recall that starting from a cocubical cochain complex
\{C_{I}^{*}\}_{I\subset\{1,\ldots,k\}}
, one can construct a double complex as follows. Set

C^{p,q}:=\bigoplus_{|I|=p}C^{q}_{I}.

We have the differential
d:C^{p,q}\ar C^{p,q+1}
induced by the differentials of the individual complexes
C^{*}_{I}
. We also have the
combinatorial differential

\delta:C^{p,q}\ar C^{p+1,q}
defined by

(\delta c)_{I=\{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{p+1}\}}:=\sum_{j=1}^{p+1}(-1)^{j+1}c_{I\setminus\{i_{j}\}}|_{I}.

Here
c=(c_{J})\in C^{p,q}
and
\delta c=((\delta c)_{I})\in C^{p+1,q}
where
I,J\subset\{1,\ldots,k\},|J|=p
, and
|I|=p+1
. The restriction map
C_{J}^{q}\ar C_{I}^{q}
in the right hand side is induced by the inclusion

.

The differentials

\delta
commute. This turns
(C^{p,q})
into a double complex, which we will call the
Mayer-Vietoris double complex
of
\{C_{I}^{*}\}
and denote
\MV(\{C_{I}^{*}\})
. We will use the same notation for the resulting total complex.

Results.
We can now state our main results.

Theorem 1
.

Let
p
be a prime. The complex
\MV(\{B(T_{I},{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)})\})
computes the cohomology

H^{*}(T,\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}T_{i},{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)})\cong H_{\dim T-*}(T-\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}T_{i},{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}).

If all intersections of the affine subtori that we wish to delete are path connected, then the answer simplifies:

Theorem 2
.

Suppose all non empty intersections
T_{I},I\subset\{1,\ldots,k\}
are path connected, and let
R
be a binomial ring. The complex
\MV(\{\Bin(H^{1}(T_{I},R)[-1])\})
computes the cohomology

H^{*}(T,\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}T_{i},R)\cong H_{\dim T-*}(T-\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}T_{i},R).

Everything simplifies even further if we are instead interested in homology with coefficients in a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-algebra:

Theorem 3
.

Let
R
be a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-algebra. The complex
\MV(\{H^{*}(T_{I},R)\})
computes the cohomology

H^{*}(T,\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}T_{i},R)\cong H_{\dim T-*}(T-\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}T_{i},R).

In a sense, Theorem
3
gives one the smallest model one could hope for. We do not know if the conclusion of the theorem remains true if
R
is no longer assumed a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-algebra, but here is a partial result in this direction.

Theorem 4
.

Assume that every intersection
T_{I},I\subset\{1,\ldots,k\}
is path connected, and that the functor
I\mapsto H_{1}(T_{I};\mathbb{Z})
from
\square_{k}
to
{\mathrm{Ab}}
lifts to a functor from
\square_{k}
to free groups.

Then the complex
\MV(\{H^{*}(T_{I};\mathbb{Z})\})
computes the cohomology

H^{*}(T,\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}T_{i},{\mathbb{Z}})\cong H_{\dim T-*}(T-\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}T_{i},{\mathbb{Z}}).

Remark 2
.

In fact, one can prove a more general result. The connected components of all intersections
T_{I}
form a poset
\mathcal{C}
which refines
\square_{k}
via a natural poset map
\mathcal{C}\ar\square_{k}
. Assume again that the functor
H_{1}(-,{\mathbb{Z}})
from
\mathcal{C}
to Abelian groups lifts to free groups. Note that the construction
A^{*}(-,{\mathbb{Z}})
can be viewed as a diagram of 2-term complexes of shape

. Assume that this diagram is quasi-isomorphic to
H^{*}(A^{*}(-,{\mathbb{Z}}))
via a zig-zag of quasi-isomorphisms that induces the identity on the cohomology. (As we will see in Lemma
8
, the latter hypothesis is automatic when all

are assumed path connected.)

Then the complex
\MV(\{H^{*}(T_{I};\mathbb{Z})\})
again computes
H_{\dim T-*}(T-\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}T_{i},{\mathbb{Z}})
.

We prove Theorems

,
2
and
3
in Section

, and Theorem

in Section

Comments

To begin with, let us see why Theorems

. (In the case of Theorems

and

Bounding the torsion.
Theorem

only gives one a

p
such that
H_{*}(T-\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}T_{i},{\mathbb{Z}})
has

Let

Finite rank models.
Next, observe that if
T^{\prime}\subset T
is an affine subtorus and
R
is a binomial ring, the cochain complexes
B(T^{\prime},{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)})
and
\Bin(H^{1}(T^{\prime},R))
have infinite rank, one of the reasons being that
\Bin({\mathbb{Z}}^{m})
has infinite rank as an Abelian group if
m>0
. So we want to replace each
B(T^{\prime},{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)})
and
\Bin(H^{1}(T^{\prime},R))
with a finite rank cochain complex, functorially in
T^{\prime}
.

The monad
\Bin:{\mathrm{Ab}}\ar{\mathrm{Ab}}
has a natural
binomial
filtration
(\Bin_{\leq i})_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}}
by subfunctors. For example,
\Bin({\mathbb{Z}}^{m})_{\leq i}
is the additive group of all polynomials
\in{\mathbb{Q}}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}]
which take integer values at integer points and have degree
\leq i
; all groups
\Bin({\mathbb{Z}}^{m})_{\leq i}
are free Abelian of finite rank.

Tensoring everything by a binomial ring
R
we get a filtration
(\Bin_{R,\leq i})_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}}
on
\Bin_{R}
. We extend this filtration object-wise to
\Bin_{R}:[\triangle,R\mbox{-}\mathrm{mod}]\ar[\triangle,R\mbox{-}\mathrm{mod}]
, and hence to
\Bin_{R}:C^{\geq 0}(R)\ar C^{\geq 0}(R)
.

Let
\tau_{\leq i}
denote the canonical filtration functor on cochain complexes.

Lemma 1
.

Let
R
be a binomial ring, and let
A
be a finitely generated free
R
-module.
Then the canonical and binomial filtrations on
\Bin_{R}(A[-1])
are equivalent:
for any
N\geq m
the maps

\tau_{\leq m}\Bin_{R,\leq N}(A[-1])\al\tau_{\leq m}\Bin_{R,\leq m}(A[-1])\ar\Bin_{R,\leq m}(A[-1])

are quasi-isomorphisms.

Proof.

The functor
\Bin_{R,\leq m}(-)/\Bin_{R,\leq m-1}(-)
is naturally isomorphic to the
m
-th divided powers functor
\Gamma^{n}(-)
, see
[
12
, Remark 2.8]
. So the binomial filtration induces a spectral sequence

E^{pq}_{1}=H^{p+q}(\Gamma^{-p}(A[-1]))\implies H^{p+q}(\Bin_{R}(A[-1])).

We have the décalage isomorphism
\Gamma^{-p}(A[-1])\cong\Lambda^{-p}(A)[p]
where
\Lambda^{*}(-)
is the
Grassmann
(or
exterior
) algebra functor, see
[
10
, (4.3.5)]
.
So we have
E^{pq}_{1}=H^{2p+q}(\Lambda^{-p}A)
, which implies that the terms
E^{pq}_{1}
vanish outside the line
2p+q=0
, and
hence the spectral sequence degenerates at
E_{1}
.
Thus the map

H^{*}(\Bin_{R,\leq-p}(A[-1]))\ar H^{*}(\Bin_{R}(A[-1]))

is isomorphic to the inclusion
of
\Lambda^{\leq-p}(A)
in the Grassmann algebra
\Lambda^{*}(A)
.
This implies that the binomial and canonical filtrations are equivalent.

∎

This allows one to construct a finite rank model for the cochain complex given by Theorem
2
:

Corollary 1
.

We use the notation and assumptions of Theorem
2
. By setting

I\mapsto\tau_{\leq\dim T}\Bin_{\leq\dim T}(H^{1}(T_{I},R)[-1]),I\subset\{1,\ldots,k\}

(4)

we get a cocubical cochain complex such that the sum of the ranks of all cochain and cubical components is finite. The total cochain complex of the corresponding Mayer-Vietoris double complex calculates

H^{*}(T,\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}T_{i},R)\cong H_{\dim T-*}(T-\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}T_{i},R).

\square

Remark 3
.

Note that in (

\dim T
-th terms of both the canonical and the binomial filtrations for all

the

-th terms would suffice by Lemma

is the corresponding 2-term complex of shape
\mathcal{C}
described in
2.1
.

Lemma 8
.

If all
T_{I}
are path connected, then
the diagram
A^{*}
is
decomposable
,
i.e. there is a quasi-isomorphism
A^{*}\zgqis H^{0}(A^{*})\oplus H^{1}(A^{*})[-1]
of diagrams.

Proof.

We may assume that
0\in T_{I}
for all
I
. Recall that the diagram term
A^{0}_{I}
is the
\mathbb{Z}
-module of all functions constant along
T_{I}
which are affine and integral with respect to the affine structure on
T
(see equation (
1
)). The evaluation at
0\in T
splits off
H^{0}(A^{*})\subset A^{*}
,
hence provides a decomposition of
A^{*}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4
.

By our assumptions, the diagram of free Abelian groups
H_{1}(T_{I};\mathbb{Z})\in{\mathrm{Ab}}

lifts to a diagram of free groups, i.e. there is a diagram of free groups
G_{I}\in\Groups^{free}

provided with an isomorphism
G_{I}/[G_{I},G_{I}]\simeq H_{1}(T_{I};\mathbb{Z})
functorial in
T_{I}
.

By Proposition
14

there is a diagram

S\colon\mathcal{C}\ar\Spaces^{ft}_{*}
where

S_{I}
has the homotopy type of the classifying space of
G_{I}
.
Then Proposition
13
implies that each

\Bin(H^{1}(S_{I})[-1])\zgqis\Gamma(H^{1}(S_{I})[-1])
. Note that the décalage isomorphism

\Gamma^{k}(H^{1}(S_{I})[-1])\zgqis\Lambda^{k}(H^{1}(S_{I}))[-k]

identifies
\Gamma(H^{1}(S_{I})[-1])
with
\MV(H^{*}(T_{I},{\mathbb{Z}}))
.
Finally, since all
T_{I}
are connected, by the above Lemma
8
we see that
A^{*}
is decomposable.
Hence the complex
\MV(\Bin(H^{1}(S_{I},{\mathbb{Z}})[-1]))
is equivalent to
\MV(H^{*}(T_{I},{\mathbb{Z}}))
, and by
Theorem
2
we conclude that
\MV(H^{*}(T_{I}))
computes
H^{*}(T,\cup_{i}T_{i},{\mathbb{Z}})
.
\square

4.2
Diagrams of classifying spaces

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the
k
-cube category
\square_{k}
. Let
G:\mathcal{C}\ar\mathrm{Groups}
be a functor from
\mathcal{C}
to groups such that each
G(I),I\in\mathcal{C}
is a finitely generated free group.

A
concrete polyhedron
is the geometric realisation of a simplicial complex. A map
f:K\ar L
of concrete polyhedra is
simplicial
if it is induced by a map of the underlying simplicial complexes, and
piecewise linear
, or
PL
if after subdividing every simplex of
K
into finitely many affine simplices, the restriction of
f
to any simplex
\triangle
of
K
is an affine map from
\triangle
to some simplex of
L
.

A
polyhedral structure
on a space
X
is a homeomorphism between
X
and a concrete polyhedron. Two polyhedral structures on
X
are
equivalent
if both of the resulting maps between the corresponding concrete polyhedra are PL. We will refer to a space with a chosen equivalence class of polyhedral structures as a
polyhedron
.

The definition of a PL map extends to polyhedra in a straightforward way. A
subpolyhedron
of a polyhedron
X
is a subspace
X^{\prime}\subset X
equipped with a polyhedral structure such that the inclusion map
X^{\prime}\ar X
is PL. For example, open subsets of polyhedra are again subpolyhedra. One defines
locally trivial PL fibre bundles
with fibre, base and total space being polyhedra by requiring that all local trivialisations should be PL.

A polyhedron
X
is
finite
if at least one (hence every) concrete polyhedron involved in the definition of the polyhedral structure on
X
is finite. A polyhedron is
locally finite
if every point of it has a closed neighbourhood which is a finite subpolyhedron.

Proposition 14
.

There is a functor
\tilde{B}G(-)
from
\mathcal{C}
to pointed spaces, such that each
\tilde{B}G(I),I\in\mathcal{C}
is a finite polyhedron homotopy equivalent to a classifying space for
G(I)
.

Proof.

We proceed in two steps. Namely, we will first find a lift of
G(-)
to locally finite polyhedra, and then we will construct a subfunctor to finite polyhedra. Choose, for each
I\in\mathcal{C}
, a based tree
E(G(I))
with a free simplicial action of
G(I)
on it, so that each
E(G(I))/G(I)
becomes a classifying space for
G(I)
. Note that
E(G(I))/G(I)
inherits a polyhedral structure from
E(G(I))
.

Step 1.
Set, for
I\in\mathcal{C}

E^{\prime}(G(I))=\prod_{I\ar J}E(G(J))

where the product is taken over all arrows in
\mathcal{C}
starting at
I
(including the identity of
I
). We let
G(I)
act diagonally on
E^{\prime}(G(I))
. Moreover, if
I\ar J
is an arrow in
\mathcal{C}
, then we have a map of
G(I)
-spaces
E^{\prime}(G(I))\ar E^{\prime}(G(J))
. So

I\mapsto B^{\prime}(G(I)):=E^{\prime}(G(I))/G(I)

is a functor from
\mathcal{C}
to pointed polyhedra where the base point in each
B^{\prime}(G(I))
is induced from
E^{\prime}(G(I))
.

Step 2.
Every space
B^{\prime}(G(I))
is fibred over the compact classifying space
E(G(I))/G(I)
with fibre equal the product

\prod_{\alpha:I\ar J,\alpha\neq\mathrm{Id}_{I}}E(G(J))

over all maps out of
I
except the identity. So
B^{\prime}(G(I))
is not compact except when
I=\{1,\ldots,k\}
, and we need to trim it a little. We will do this using the next lemma:

Lemma 9
.

Let
F=T_{1}\times\cdots\times T_{m}
be a product of locally finite trees. Suppose
B
is a finite wedge of circles. Denote
the base point
0\in B
, and assume that
\pi_{1}(B,0)
acts on
F
by products of tree automorphisms.
Let
F\ar X\overset{p}{\ar}B
be the PL fibre bundle
associated with the action.

Then the finite subpolyhedra of
X
which are homotopy equivalent to
X
are cofinal in the set of all compact subspaces of
X
ordered by inclusion.

Proof.

Choose an orientation on each circle in the wedge
B
, and let
g_{1},\ldots,g_{l}
be the corresponding system of generators of
\pi_{1}(B,0)
. Denote the fibre of
p
over the base point
0\in B
by
F_{0}
.

Let
K\subset X
be a compact subspace and

T\subset F_{0}
be a product of finite subtrees. Using parallel transfer along the natural flat connection on
X\overset{p}{\ar}B
we can extend
T
to a finite subpolyhedron
T^{\prime}\subset X
such that the fibre
T^{\prime}\cap p^{-1}(t)
for
t\in B\setminus\{0\}
is homeomorphic to
T
, hence is contractible, and the fibre
T^{\prime}\cap p^{-1}(0)
is the union of
T
and its images under
g_{1},\ldots,g_{l}
.
Enlarging
T
if necessary one can assume that
T^{\prime}
contains
K
. Finally, one can choose a product of finite subtrees
S\subset p^{-1}(0)
containing
T^{\prime}\cap p^{-1}(0)
and set
X^{\prime}=T^{\prime}\cup S
. By the construction,
X^{\prime}\subset X
is a finite subpolyhedron, and
X^{\prime}\ar B
is a proper map with contractible fibers, hence a homotopy equivalence.
∎

We continue with the proof of Proposition
14
. We proceed by induction on
|I|
. For
I=\varnothing
we let
B^{\prime\prime}(G(I))
be any finite subpolyhedron of
B^{\prime}(G(I))
which is homotopy equivalent to
B^{\prime}(G(I))
and contains the base point. This is possible by Lemma
9
.

Now suppose
0<m\leq k
, and that for each
I
with
|I|<m
we have constructed a finite subpolyhedron
B^{\prime\prime}(G(I))\subset B^{\prime}(G(I))
such that the inclusion is a homotopy equivalence, and any arrow
I_{1}\ar I_{2},|I_{1}|\leq|I_{2}|<m
takes
B^{\prime\prime}(G(I_{1}))
to
B^{\prime\prime}(G(I_{2}))
. Let
I
be such that
|I|=m
. Using Lemma
9
again we define then
B^{\prime\prime}(G(I))\subset B^{\prime}(G(I))
to be a finite subpolyhedron such that it is homotopy equivalent to
B^{\prime}(G(I))
and contains the base point and the images of all arrows that end at
I
. This completes the induction step in the construction of the functor
B^{\prime\prime}(G(-))
, and also the proof of Proposition
14
.
∎

Remark 7
.

Lemma
9
can probably be extended to include more general spaces. For example, suppose
X
is a polyhedron for which there is a finite subpolyhedron
X^{\prime}\subset X
such that the inclusion is a homotopy equivalence. Does it follow that finite polyhedra with this property are cofinal in the set of all compact subspaces of
X
? If the answer is no in general, will it be positive if one assumes in addition that
X
is locally finite and/or finite dimensional?

Remark 8
.

Proposition
14
can be generalised to
\mathcal{C}
equal an arbitrary finite poset.

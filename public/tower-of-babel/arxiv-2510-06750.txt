Gold-Switch: Training-Free Superposition of Slow- and Fast- Thinking LLMs
Jaeseong Lee, Dayoung Kwon, Seung-won Hwang

Abstract
Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) excel in structured tasks by emulating deliberate human reasoning but often suffer from overthinking, degrading performance and wasting resources. One possible baseline is to deploy both LLM and LRM, then route input by predicting whether it requires reasoning and may cause overthinking. However, deploying multiple models can be costly or impractical. We propose a superposed deployment strategy with a lightweight, training-free regulation to optimize inference by switching one model on and off. Instead of routing, we selectively unlearn from LRM at inference, scaling down computation while preserving reasoning. By analyzing the cumulative energy of singular values, we identify optimal low-rank projections to adjust reasoning just right.

Introduction

Recently, large reasoning models (LRMs), such as DeepSeek-R1
(
DeepSeek-AI et al. 2025
)
, or QwQ
(
Qwen 2025
)
, have emerged as a powerful class of reasoning-oriented models. These models, starting from a base large language model (LLM), then trained with extensive chain-of-thought (CoT) supervision, demonstrate strong performance across complex reasoning tasks such as mathematical problem solving, programming, and multi-step question answering.
LRMs exemplify a new generation of LLMs optimized for “slow thinking”– producing elaborate intermediate steps that mimic human reasoning.

However, overthinking
(
Sui et al. 2025
;
Chen et al. 2025
;
Cuadron et al. 2025
)
has recently been identified as a critical challenge in LRMs: LRMs often generate unnecessarily long or convoluted reasoning paths
(
Sui et al. 2025
)
.
This behavior has been shown to reduce both efficiency and even lower the task performance
(
Cuadron et al. 2025
)
.
This highlights the need to switch between slow- and fast- thinking in LRMs.

One possible solution is training a model to allow switching between slow- and fast- thinking by predefined prompt, such as Qwen3
Yang et al. 2025
.
However due to their limited performance, the recent trend is back to releasing LRM and LLM separately– models such as Qwen3-2507, Qwen3-VL, Qwen3-Next
Yang et al. 2025
, Qwen3-Omni
Xu et al. 2025a
, and Qwen3-Max
Team 2025
all trained and released slow- and fast- variants separately.

An alternative solution
is deploying both LRM and LLM, and routing the prompts to the appropriate model
(
Chuang et al. 2025b
;
Chuang et al. 2025a
;
Aytes et al. 2025
;
Ong et al. 2025
)
. For example, RouteLLM
(
Ong et al. 2025
)
trains a lightweight classifier to classify whether the input prompt is relatively hard or easy, then uses this classifier to route input prompts.
However, deployment of both LRM and LLM can be costly in practice.

In this paper, we introduce a
superposed deployment
strategy for achieving the “Goldilocks" balance between slow-thinking LRMs and fast-thinking LLMs.
One key inspiration is
that
unlearning overthinking
from LRM
while preserving reasoning is possible. Specifically, for linear layers in LRM and LLM, denoted as
W_{R}
and
W_{B}
,
we hypothesize the existence of
L
such that
L+W_{R}
unlearns overthinking only.
In other words, instead of full difference

\Delta W=W_{B}-W_{R}
,
we can isolate an overthinking-specific
component
L
, which is hypothesized to be low-rank, by low intrinsic dimensionality
(
Li et al. 2018
)
.

With this formulation, our problem reduces
to finding
L
: Considering both
L
and
\Delta W
have similar direction– moving from
W_{R}
to
W_{B}
– we derive
L
by low-rank approximating
\Delta W
with rank
r
.
To optimize
r
,
we introduce an entropy-based, layer-wise rank selection method inspired by
Xu et al. 2020
. We show that this layer-wise
r
selection achieves faster inference and higher task performance compared to naïvely using uniformly fixed ratio of
r
across every layer.
The key contributions of Gold-Switch are:

•

We propose Gold-Switch, a
superposed deployment
of slow-thinking LRMs and fast-thinking LLMs with “Goldilocks" balance between them.

•

Our key intuition is unlearning just right, or, avoiding overthinking while preserving reasoning, which we enable by low-rank approximating the parameter difference between LRM and LLM.

•

Experiments with QwQ-32B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-32B, on ASDIV, GSM8K, AIME, and GPQA show that we draw LRM near the Goldilocks zone– we can get up to 2.7× speedup with negligible performance drop, while reducing the additional GPU memory by 9× compared with router-based solutions.

Related Works

2.1
Overthinking in LRMs

LRMs like DeepSeek-R1 and QwQ reportedly excel at complex multi-step tasks through chain-of-thought (CoT) training.
Meanwhile, they may generate long and repeated reasoning paths even for simple problems
(
Sui et al. 2025
;
Chen et al. 2025
;
Cuadron et al. 2025
)
.
Chen et al. 2025
define overthinking as the inefficient use of computation, where models spend too much effort on simple tasks without much accuracy gain.
For example, they show that even for a simple question like “What is the answer of 2 plus 3?”, LRM such as QwQ-32B-Preview requires 901 tokens to reach the answer ‘5’, while an LLM from the same family, Qwen2.5-72B, requires only 7 tokens.

2.2
Reducing Overthinking

2.2.1
Training-Based

Reinforcement learning or supervised fine-tuning approaches, by rewarding concise, accurate answers and penalize long or incorrect ones, can encourage the model to generate shorter reasoning paths
(
Liu et al. 2024
;
Yu et al. 2024
;
Cui et al. 2025
;
Munkhbat et al. 2025
;
Ma et al. 2025b
;
Kang et al. 2025
;
Shen et al. 2025
;
Aggarwal and Welleck 2025
;
Qu et al. 2025
;
Arora and Zanette 2025
;
Team et al. 2025
;
Luo et al. 2025
;
Yeo et al. 2025
;
Xia et al. 2025
)
.
For example, models like Kimi k1.5
(
Team et al. 2025
)
apply length-aware rewards during their RL process to encourage shorter responses. It also utilizes rejection sampling, generating and evaluating candidate responses to guide efficient generation. Other methods create length-optimized datasets by removing redundant steps or tokens
(
Xia et al. 2025
;
Kang et al. 2025
)
, or use self-training to sample shorter paths as pseudo-labels
(
Munkhbat et al. 2025
)
.

Models like Qwen3
Yang et al. 2025
are trained to allow both slow- and fast- thinking by predefined prompts. However, due to their limited performance, the recent trend is back to releasing slow- and fast- thinking models separately
Yang et al. 2025
;
Xu et al. 2025a
;
Team 2025
.

Distinction

While these methods usually require extensive data- and compute- resources, Gold-Switch is training-free, removing such a burden.

2.2.2
Training-Free

Routing-based

Routing-based method classifies the input prompt to route to an appropriate model
(
Chuang et al. 2025b
;
Chuang et al. 2025a
;
Aytes et al. 2025
;
Ong et al. 2025
)
. For example, RouteLLM
(
Ong et al. 2025
)
trains a lightweight classifier to classify whether the input prompt is relatively hard or easy, then uses this classifier to route input prompts.

Prompt-based

Prompt-based approaches adjust the input format to limit reasoning steps
(
Renze and Guven 2024
;
Xu et al. 2025b
;
Ma et al. 2025a
)
. For example, minimal prompting strategies like Nothinking
(
Ma et al. 2025a
)
encourage direct answers without intermediate steps, by early closure of <think> tag with </think> (
Figure 5
).

Distinction

While the routing-based method requires deploying both LRM and LLM, we propose a superposed deployment strategy.
Moreover, our method is orthogonal to prompt-based methods, which can be combined to further enhance the efficiency.

2.3
Low-Rank Representation

Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methods, especially LoRA
(
Hu et al. 2022
)
, have become a standard for adapting large language models (LLMs) to downstream tasks. These methods rely on the hypothesis that the parameter changes required to learn a specific task or capability have a much lower intrinsic dimension than the full parameter space
(
Hu et al. 2022
;
Aghajanyan et al. 2021
)
.

MoDeGPT
Lin et al. 2025
modularizes model parameters by task and recombines them, demonstrating that LLMs can be compressed and reused efficiently without additional training.

Distinction

Gold-Switch demonstrates that unlearning reasoning capacities can also be captured in a compact, low-dimensional subspace without additional training.

3
Proposed Method

3.1
Preliminaries

3.1.1
LRMs

To enhance the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs), researchers have developed various reasoning-oriented models, such as QwQ
(
Qwen 2025
)
and DeepSeek-R1
(
DeepSeek-AI et al. 2025
)
. These models are typically trained on a base model (e.g., Qwen2.5-32B) with extensive chain-of-thought (CoT) supervision, enabling them to perform complex reasoning tasks.

Formally, we denote the base model as
W_{B}
.
By updating this model we obtain a reasoning-enhanced model
W_{R}
:

W_{R}=W_{B}+\Delta W

(1)

where
\Delta W
represents the parameter update needed for complex reasoning.

However, this reasoning-enhanced model often suffers from overthinking, where it generates unnecessarily long or convoluted reasoning paths
(
Sui et al. 2025
)
. This behavior can lead to inefficiencies and even degradation of task performance
(
Cuadron et al. 2025
)
.

3.1.2
Baseline: Routing Input Queries to LRM and LLMs

To avoid overthinking, we can adopt RouteLLM
(
Ong et al. 2025
)
to route the input query to the appropriate model.

Formally, given a lightweight classifier
C
that predicts the difficulty of the input, RouteLLM routes the input
x
to
W_{R}
if
C(x)\leq t
, and to
W_{B}
otherwise.
In theory, the classifier
C
can be either black-boxed, or a BERT-based classifier trained on a preference dataset.

However, this solution blindly deploys both
W_{R}
and
W_{B}
, which can be costly in practice.

Figure 1:

Overview of Gold-Switch. Gold-Switch extracts the overthinking parameters from the reasoning-enhanced model using low-rank approximation. It then dynamically adjusts the reasoning capabilities based on the difficulty of the input, allowing for efficient reasoning without retraining.

3.2
Gold-Switch: Training-Free Superposition Between LRM and LLM

Figure 1
illustrates the overall flow of Gold-Switch.
We start by defining
L
, for unlearning only overthinking from LRM (§
3.2.1
).
This lightweight module could be deployed alongside
W_{R}
, with minimal overhead. Then we introduce how to superpose
L
with the given LRM, for each given query (§
3.2.2
).

3.2.1
Training-Free
L
to Unlearn Only Overthinking from LRM

L
as a low-rank approximation of
\Delta{W}

The key intuition to construct
L
is that
unlearning overthinking
while preserving reasoning is possible. In other words, unlike
\Delta W=W_{B}-W_{R}
, which captures all finetuned capabilities including reasoning,
L
focuses solely on overthinking, implying lower intrinsic dimensionality than
\Delta W

(
Li et al. 2018
)
.
With low intrinsic dimensionality,
L
could be low-rank decomposed, enabling lightweight deployment alongside
W_{R}
.

Our second key intuition is that
L
and
\Delta W
have similar direction, both shifting
W_{R}
to
W_{B}
.
Therefore we hypothesize that
L
can be a low-rank approximation of
\Delta W
.
Our experiments (Section
4
) will reveal that when we low-rank approximate the full unlearning matrix
\Delta W
, slow-thinking unlearning is captured first in these low-rank approximations.

Formally, we decompose
\Delta{W}
as :

\Delta W\approx USV^{T}

(2)

where
U\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times\min(N,M)}
,
S\in\mathbb{R}^{\min(N,M)\times\min(N,M)}
and
V\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times\min(N,M)}
. Following the Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem
(
Eckart and Young 1936
)
, we can approximate
\Delta W
with a low-rank matrix by selecting the top
r
singular values from
S
.
This allows us to select the essential dimensions of the delta matrix
\Delta W
.
Formally, we get the low-rank approximation of
\Delta W
as follows:

\hat{U}=U[:,:r],\quad\hat{S}=S[:r,:r],\quad\hat{V}=V[:,:r]

(3)

Finally, we get the Gold-Switch
L
as follows:

L=L_{U}\cdot L_{V},\quad L_{U}=\hat{U}\cdot\sqrt{\hat{S}},\quad L_{V}=\sqrt{\hat{S}}\cdot\hat{V}^{T}

(4)

The resulting
L
can selectively remove unnecessary overthinking components while preserving core reasoning.
This approach is crucial for retaining the reasoning of large LRMs while enhancing efficiency during inference.

Energy-based
r
selection

Now, the problem narrows down to how to effectively select
r
for each layer. A blind selection of a high rank would make
L
too similar to
\Delta W
, causing the unlearning process to strip away not only overthinking components but also critical reasoning abilities.

To address this, inspired by
Xu et al. 2020
, we employ an energy-based selection mechanism driven by the energy percentage
e
. According to matrix perturbation theory
(
Stewart and Sun 1990
)
, the difference between two matrices is bounded by the differences in their singular values, as expressed by the inequality:

\|B-A\|_{F}^{2}\geq\sqrt{\sum_{i}|s_{b,i}-s_{a,i}|^{2}}

(5)

where
s_{b,i},s_{a,i}
denotes the singular values of
B
and
A
respectively. Applying this to our setting, we derive the lower bound of the error between
L
and
\Delta W
:

\|L-\Delta W\|_{F}^{2}\geq\sqrt{\sum_{i=r}^{\min(N,M)}s_{i}^{2}}

(6)

where
s_{i}
denotes the singular values of
\Delta W
.
This lower bound serves as an indicator to prevent
L
from becoming too close to
\Delta W
, ensuring that essential reasoning is not inadvertently discarded. For each layer, we determine the optimal
r
by regulating the right-hand term of
Equation 6
, preventing it from diminishing excessively.

To this end, we introduce the reversed cumulative energy
e(r)
, defined as follows:

e(r)=\frac{\sum_{i=r}^{\min(N,M)}s_{i}^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{\min(N,M)}s_{i}^{2}}

(7)

We then select
r
as the minimum rank that maintains
e(r)
above an empirically tuned threshold
\tau
. This ensures sufficient information retention while eliminating unnecessary overthinking components.

Now the remaining part is how to superpose slow- and fast-thinking LRM with
L
and
W_{R}
. We introduce hard or soft superposition.

Hard Superposition

Given a classifier
C
that predicts the difficulty of the input (§
3.1.2
),
we can switch between slow-thinking and fast-thinking based on the predicted difficulty:

W^{\prime}_{R}=\begin{cases}W_{R}+L&\text{if }C(x)\leq t\\
W_{R}&\text{if }C(x)<t\end{cases}

(8)

where
W^{\prime}_{R}
is the weight we use for the inference of input
x
, and
t
is a threshold that determines whether the input is considered easy or hard.
This enables us to deal with the complex tasks that require sophisticated reasoning capabilities via slow-thinking, while still benefiting from the reduced overthinking for easier tasks via fast-thinking.

Soft Superposition

Moreover, we investigate soft superposition to continuously adjust the weight of Gold-Switch based on the difficulty of the input, by generalizing the concept of routing in
Ong et al. 2025
.

Formally, the softly superposed
W^{\prime}_{r}
is defined as:

W^{\prime}_{r}=W_{R}+\alpha L

(9)

where
\alpha
is a scaling factor that controls the strength of Gold-Switch.
However, we find that scaling Gold-Switch may increase the overthinking in some cases, making it harder to control the reasoning capabilities (
Figure 3
). Therefore we use the hard superposition for the final experiments.

We compare the following methods:

•

LRM
breaks down structured problems into multiple steps for deeper understanding but often overthink even on simple tasks.

•

LLM
can produce concise responses with low computation, but often lacks deep logical reasoning for complex problems.

•

(

selects different language models (such as LLM or LRM) depending on the difficulty of the input. For simple inputs, it chooses a lightweight LLM, and for harder problems, it uses an LRM. –

adjusts unlearning based on query difficulty, providing continuous control.

For instance, QwQ-32B achieves 66.7% accuracy on AIME2024, whereas always enabling Gold-Switch drops it to 16.7%. First, the base LRM, QwQ-32B, overthinks over and over again– repeatedly obtaining the same answer “4” for 10 times– resulting in 1353 tokens to reach the final answer.

In contrast, Gold-Switch directly dives into a single CoT process, without any repetitive answering, resulting in 328 tokens, almost 4× reduction of token length compared to the base LRM. Moreover, it is interesting to observe that the LRM seems to forget the <think> token, not reacting at all, even does not close with </think> token.

5
Conclusion

Our work
introduces lightweight, training-free LoRA modules that suppress overthinking, enabling LLMs to dynamically modulate their inference budget based on input complexity.
This approach not only avoids the need for deploying two models, but also makes inference more efficient.

6
Limitations

Gold-Switch leverages classifiers to route hard or easy tasks from existing baselines
Ong et al. 2025
. Therefore we follow the limitations from those baselines, such as interpretability, or scarcity of gold-labeled dataset to train such classifiers.

Gold-Switch assumes access to both the base and fine-tuned weights, which may not be feasible for closed-source or commercial LLMs. However, our target is making local deployment more efficient with superposed deployment, thus supporting the closed-source models is out of our scope.

On extremal problems of Delsarte type for positive definite functions on LCA groups To the memory of Bent Fuglede whose ideas have a lasting influence on us.
E. E. Berdysheva, M. D. Ramabulana, Sz. Gy. Révész

Abstract
A unifying framework for some extremal problems on locally compact Abelian groups is considered, special cases of which include the Delsarte and Turán extremal problems. A slight variation of the extremal problem is introduced and the different formulations are studied for equivalence. Extending previous work, a general result on existence of extremal functions for the new variant is proved under a certain general topological condition.

Notation and Preliminaries

Let
C(G)
be the space of continuous functions on
G
to the complex numbers and put on it the topology of uniform convergence on compact sets. The dual group
\widehat{G}
of
G
consists of continuous homomorphisms of
G
to the multiplicative group
\mathbb{T}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}
. With respect to pointwise operations and the subspace topology that it inherits as a subset of
C(G)
,
\widehat{G}
is a LCA group. For a measure space
X
with a measure
\mu
,
L^{p}(X)
denotes the usual Banach space of
p

X
for
1\leq p<\infty
, and the space of essentially bounded functions on
X
for
p=\infty
. The Fourier transform of
f\in L^{1}(G)
is defined by the formula:

\widehat{f}(\chi):=\int_{G}f\overline{\chi}\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}\mbox{ for all }\chi\in\widehat{G}.

It is well-known that the Fourier transform of an integrable continuous positive definite function is non-negative. Moreover, since
0\leq\widehat{f}(0)=\int_{G}f\textup{d}\lambda_{G}
, it follows that the integral of an integrable continuous positive definite function is non-negative. Related to the notion of a positive definite function is that of an integrally positive definite function. Firstly, recall that the convolution
f\ast g
of functions
f,g:G\to\mathbb{C}
is defined as

f\ast g(x):=\int_{G}f(y)g(x-y)\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}(y)\mbox{ for all }x\in G,

whenever the integral exists. Write
{f}^{*}(g):=\overline{f(-g)}
. A function
f\in L^{\infty}(G)
is integrally positive definite if the inequality

\int_{G}({h}^{*}\ast h)f\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}=\int_{G}\int_{G}f(y-x)h(x)\overline{h(y)}\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(x)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(y)\geq 0

holds for all continuous compactly supported complex valued functions
h
on
G
. According to
[
17
, Theorem 1.7.3]
an integrally positive definite function agrees locally almost everywhere with a continuous positive definite function. When
G
is
\sigma
-compact, i.e., if
G
can be written as a countable union of compact subsets, then locally almost everywhere is the same as almost everywhere. Therefore, on a
\sigma
-compact group an integrally positive definite function agrees almost everywhere with a continuous positive definite function.

For brevity, we write
K\Subset G
to mean that

is a compact subset of

Lemma 5

A Haar measurable subset

of a LCA group

Proof.

. To see this, let

Theorem 14

By passing to a pointwise almost everywhere convergent subsequence, assume that

Let

Next we show that

. Since

(11)

On the other hand, by Fatou’s lemma,

\begin{split}\int_{G}f_{-}\textup{d}\lambda_{G}&\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{G}(f_{n})_{-}\textup{d}\lambda_{G}\\

(12)

\int_{G}|f|\textup{d}\lambda_{G}=\int_{G}f_{+}\textup{d}\lambda_{G}+\int_{G}f_{-}\textup{d}\lambda_{G}<\infty.

Hence

.

Note that the sequence
((f_{n})_{+})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of the positive parts of the

, and that
(f_{n})_{+}\leq\mathbf{1}_{\Omega_{+}}

\int_{G}f_{+}\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{G}(f_{n})_{+}\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}.

(14)

Recall that from (

\int_{G}f_{-}\lambda_{G}\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{G}(f_{n})_{-}\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}.

(15)

\begin{split}\int_{G}f\text{d}\lambda_{G}&\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{G}(f_{n})_{+}\text{d}\lambda_{G}-\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\int_{G}(f_{n})_{-}}\text{d}\lambda_{G}\\
&=\limsup_{n\to\infty}{\int_{G}\left((f_{n})_{+}-(f_{n})_{-}\right)\text{d}\lambda_{G}}\\
&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{G}f_{n}\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}=\mathcal{C}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-}).\end{split}

Only now we employ boundary-coherence to show that
f^{-1}(0,\infty)\subset\Omega_{+}
and also

. First, if

. Consider an arbitrary

or is only in

of

\operatorname{ext}\Omega_{+}
. By the above we know that

almost everywhere on

, and hence

. It follows that

, and as this holds for all

, we find

. So, if

either. Therefore,

To show that

Proposition 15
.

For the extremal sequence
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in Theorem
14
that converges to
f
strongly in
L^{2}(G)
and pointwise almost everywhere, we have, in addition that
f_{n}\to f
in
L^{1}(G)
.

Proof.

Take any
\eta>0
. As
f\in L^{1}(G)
, there is a compact set
K\Subset G
with
\int_{G\setminus K}|f|\textup{d}\lambda_{G}<\eta
. Also, as
\Omega_{+}
and hence
S_{+}
as well as
S_{+}\cup\Omega_{+}
have finite measure, by inner regularity of the Haar measure there exists a compact set
K^{\prime}
such that
\lambda_{G}((S_{+}\cup\Omega_{+})\setminus K^{\prime})<\eta
. We may assume that
K\supset K^{\prime}
and hence
K
satisfies both requirements. By Lemma
12
, we can find a function
k
, which is continuous, positive definite, compactly supported,
0\leq k\leq 1
, and
k|_{K}>1-\eta
.

We have

\begin{split}\int_{G}|f|(1-k)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}&\leq\int_{K}|f|(1-k)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}+\int_{G\setminus K}|f|(1-k)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}\\
&\leq\eta\|f\|_{L^{1}(G)}+\int_{G\setminus K}|f|\textup{d}\lambda_{G}\\
&\leq\eta(\|f\|_{L^{1}(G)}+1)\leq\eta(2\lambda_{G}(\Omega_{+})+1).\end{split}

Next, we refer to the
L^{2}
convergence
f_{n}\to f
. As
k\in L^{2}(G)
, we have
\int_{G}|f-f_{n}|k\textup{d}\lambda_{G}\to 0
, and thus
\int_{G}|f-f_{n}|k\textup{d}\lambda_{G}<\eta
for
n
large enough.

Let us assume without loss of generality that
\int_{G}f_{n}\textup{d}\lambda_{G}\geq\mathcal{C}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})-\frac{1}{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Taking into account
f_{n}k\in\mathcal{F}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
4
4

4

We use here the fact that the product of two positive definite functions is positive definite.
, we necessarily have
\int_{G}f_{n}k\textup{d}\lambda_{G}\leq\mathcal{C}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
. Thus we can write

\begin{split}\mathcal{C}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})\geq\int_{G}f_{n}k\textup{d}\lambda_{G}&=\int_{G}f_{n}\textup{d}\lambda_{G}-\int_{G}f_{n}(1-k)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}\\
&\geq\mathcal{C}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})-\frac{1}{n}-\int_{G}(f_{n})_{+}(1-k)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}+\int_{G}(f_{n})_{-}(1-k)\textup{d}\lambda_{G},\end{split}

therefore

\int_{G}(f_{n})_{-}(1-k)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}\leq\int_{G}(f_{n})_{+}(1-k)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}+\frac{1}{n}.

We get

\int_{G}(1-k)|f_{n}|\textup{d}\lambda_{G}=\int_{G}(1-k)(f_{n})_{+}\textup{d}\lambda_{G}+\int_{G}(1-k)(f_{n})_{-}\textup{d}\lambda_{G}\leq 2\int_{G}(f_{n})_{+}(1-k)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}+\frac{1}{n}.

Using the facts that

\begin{split}\int_{G}(f_{n})_{+}(1-k)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}&=\int_{K}(f_{n})_{+}(1-k)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}+\int_{G\setminus K}(f_{n})_{+}(1-k)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}\\

Next, we show the existence of an extremal function for general LCA groups. The key is to reduce the problem to the case of
\sigma
-compact groups, where an extremal function is known to exist, and then extending the solution to the general case. As such, we first show in the following lemma that boundary-coherence is hereditary with respect to open subgroups.

Lemma 16
.

Let
H
be an open subgroup of an LCA group
G
and
\Omega
a subset of
G
. If
\Omega
is boundary-coherent in
G
then
\Omega\cap H
is boundary-coherent in
H
, i.e.,
\partial_{H}(\Omega\cap H)\subset\overline{\operatorname{ext}_{H}(\Omega\cap H)}^{H}
.

Proof.

Let us first notice that
\operatorname{ext}_{H}(\Omega\cap H)=\operatorname{ext}_{G}(\Omega)\cap H
. Indeed, the exterior of a set is the interior of its complement. Now the statement follows from an easy observation that for a set
A\subset G
we have
\operatorname{int}_{H}(A\cap H)=\operatorname{int}_{G}(A)\cap H
on account of
H
being open.

Furthermore, since
H
is an open subgroup it is closed in
G
, and hence
\overline{\operatorname{ext}_{H}(\Omega\cap H)}^{H}=\overline{\operatorname{ext}_{G}(\Omega)\cap H}^{G}
. Moreover, the fact that
H
is open implies that
\overline{\operatorname{ext}_{G}(\Omega)\cap H}^{G}=\overline{\operatorname{ext}_{G}(\Omega)}^{G}\cap H
. Indeed, the containment
\overline{\operatorname{ext}_{G}(\Omega)\cap H}^{G}\subset\overline{\operatorname{ext}_{G}(\Omega)}^{G}\cap H
follows from the fact that
\operatorname{ext}_{G}(\Omega)\cap H
is contained in
\overline{\operatorname{ext}_{G}(\Omega)}^{G}\cap H
, and the latter set is closed in
G
. For the reverse containment, let
x\in\overline{\operatorname{ext}_{G}(\Omega)}^{G}\cap H
. Take an arbitrary neighbourhood
V
of
x
in
G
. Since
H
is open,
V\cap H
is also a neighbourhood of
x
in
G
. Since
x\in\overline{\operatorname{ext}_{G}(\Omega)}^{G}
, we have that
V\cap H\cap\operatorname{ext}_{G}(\Omega)\neq\emptyset
. This implies that
x\in\overline{\operatorname{ext}_{G}(\Omega)\cap H}^{G}
.

Now, using the fact that
\partial_{G}(H)=\emptyset
, we conclude

\begin{split}\partial_{H}(\Omega\cap H)&=\overline{\Omega\cap H}^{H}\cap\overline{{H}\setminus(\Omega\cap H)}^{H}\\
&=\overline{\Omega\cap H}^{G}\cap\overline{{H}\setminus(\Omega\cap H)}^{G}\\
&=\partial_{G}(\Omega\cap H)\cap H\\
&\subset(\partial_{G}(\Omega)\cup\partial_{G}(H))\cap H\\
&=\partial_{G}(\Omega)\cap H\\
&\subset\overline{\operatorname{ext}_{G}(\Omega)}^{G}\cap H\\
&=\overline{\operatorname{ext}_{H}(\Omega\cap H)}^{H}.\end{split}

∎

Proposition 18
.

Let
f
be a
\mathcal{C}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
-extremal function. Then there exists a sequence
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathcal{F}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
such that each

has compact support and
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
f
in

and uniformly on compact sets.

Proof.

Since
f\in L^{1}(G)
and therefore
\operatorname{supp}{f}
is
\sigma
-compact (see Lemma
10
), we can find a sequence of sets
(K_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

,
K_{n}\Subset G
,
K_{n}\subset K_{n+1}
, and
\operatorname{supp}{f}\subset\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}K_{n}
. By Lemma
12
there exist continuous, compactly supported, positive definite functions

such that
k_{n}(0)=1
,
0\leq k_{n}\leq 1
,
\left.k_{n}\right|_{K_{n}}>1-\frac{1}{n}
.

Put

,

. Clearly,

and compactly supported.

To establish convergence in

, we estimate

\begin{split}\int_{G}|f_{n}-f|\textup{d}\lambda_{G}&\leq\int_{K_{n}}|f_{n}-f|\textup{d}\lambda_{G}+\int_{G\setminus K_{n}}|f_{n}|\textup{d}\lambda_{G}+\int_{G\setminus K_{n}}|f|\textup{d}\lambda_{G}\\

Now let

which implies uniform convergence on

5
Equivalence of Extremal Problems

Let
\Omega_{+}
and
\Omega_{-}
be subsets of a LCA group
G
. In this section, we investigate conditions under which the extremal problem of calculating the extremal value
\mathcal{C}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
is equivalent to that of calculating the extremal value
\mathcal{C}^{*}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
. This could either mean that the function classes
\mathcal{F}^{*}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
and
\mathcal{F}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
are equal, in which case the corresponding extremal values are equal, or it could mean that the extremal values are equal even though the corresponding function classes are not equal. For brevity, we drop the subscript
G
and simply write, for example,
\mathcal{F}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
instead of
\mathcal{F}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
.

Even though it is true that
\mathcal{C}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})=\mathcal{C}^{*}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
for some classes of sets
\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-}
, this can not always be proved by showing that
\mathcal{F}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})=~\mathcal{F}^{*}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
. This is because even though
\mathcal{F}^{*}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})\subset\mathcal{F}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
, it is not true, in general, that
\mathcal{F}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})\subset\mathcal{F}^{*}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
, as the following example shows.

Example 22
.

Let
G=\mathbb{R}
,
\Omega=\Omega_{+}=\Omega_{-}=(-1,1)
, so that we are in the situation of the Turán problem for an interval in
\mathbb{R}
. The extremal function for
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{R}}(\Omega)
is the triangle function
{\mathbf{1}}_{\frac{1}{2}\Omega}\ast{\mathbf{1}}_{\frac{1}{2}\Omega}\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
. It is easy to see that
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{R}}(\Omega)=\mathcal{T}^{*}_{\mathbb{R}}(\Omega)=1
. However, the support of
{\mathbf{1}}_{\frac{1}{2}\Omega}\ast{\mathbf{1}}_{\frac{1}{2}\Omega}
is
\left[-1,1\right]
, so that
{\mathbf{1}}_{\frac{1}{2}\Omega}\ast~{\mathbf{1}}_{\frac{1}{2}\Omega}\notin~\mathcal{F}^{*}_{\mathbb{R}}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
.

In the opposite direction we have the following example, although it is quite special.

Example 23
.

For a prime
p
, consider the group
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
of
p
-adic rationals with the
p
-adic norm. Any ball of positive radius is both open and closed. So, if
\Omega_{+}
and
\Omega_{-}
are balls of positive radii with
\Omega_{+}
centred at
0
, then the extremal problems are equivalent in any sense.

We make some simple observations. Firstly, the equality
\mathcal{F}(\overline{\Omega_{+}},\overline{\Omega_{-}})=\mathcal{F}^{*}(\overline{\Omega_{+}},\overline{\Omega_{-}})
is easily seen to be true, always. In particular, if
\Omega_{+}
and
\Omega_{-}
are closed sets, then the extremal problems are equivalent in any sense. Another observation is that if
f\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
, then taking into account the continuity of
f
we have
f^{-1}(0,\infty)\subset\operatorname{int}\Omega_{+}\subset\Omega_{+}
, and hence
\operatorname{supp}f_{+}\subset\overline{\operatorname{int}{\Omega_{+}}}
. Similarly,
f^{-1}(-\infty,0)\subset\operatorname{int}{\Omega_{-}}\subset\Omega_{-}
, and hence
\operatorname{supp}f_{-}\subset\overline{\operatorname{int}{\Omega_{-}}}
. Thus, we have the following containments:

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}^{*}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})\subseteq\mathcal{F}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})&=\mathcal{F}(\operatorname{int}\Omega_{+},\operatorname{int}\Omega_{-})\\
&\subseteq\mathcal{F}(\overline{\operatorname{int}\Omega_{+}},\overline{\operatorname{int}\Omega_{-}})\\
&=\mathcal{F}^{*}(\overline{\operatorname{int}\Omega_{+}},\overline{\operatorname{int}\Omega_{-}})\\
&\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{*}(\overline{\Omega_{+}},\overline{\Omega_{-}})=\mathcal{F}(\overline{\Omega_{+}},\overline{\Omega_{-}}),\end{split}

(18)

and the corresponding relations between the extremal values:

\begin{split}\mathcal{C}^{*}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})\leq\mathcal{C}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})&=\mathcal{C}(\operatorname{int}\Omega_{+},\operatorname{int}\Omega_{-})\\
&\leq\mathcal{C}(\overline{\operatorname{int}{\Omega}_{+}},\overline{\operatorname{int}\Omega_{-}})\\
&=\mathcal{C}^{*}(\overline{\operatorname{int}\Omega_{+}},\overline{\operatorname{int}\Omega_{-}})\\
&\leq\mathcal{C}^{*}(\overline{\Omega_{+}},\overline{\Omega_{-}})=\mathcal{C}(\overline{\Omega_{+}},\overline{\Omega_{-}}).\end{split}

(19)

In some situations, the above containments and inequalities are equalities. This can easily be shown if
\Omega_{+}
and
\Omega_{-}
are closed. The next proposition shows that equality holds when
\Omega_{+}
and
\Omega_{-}
are boundary-coherent.

Proposition 24
.

Let
G
be a LCA group and
\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-}
be boundary-coherent symmetric subsets of
G
with
\Omega_{+}
a neighbourhood of
0
. Then
\mathcal{F}^{*}(\overline{\Omega_{+}},\overline{\Omega_{-}})=\mathcal{F}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
.

Proof.

Note that
\mathcal{F}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})\subset\mathcal{F}^{*}(\overline{\Omega_{+}},\overline{\Omega_{-}})
is always true. So, it is enough to show that
\mathcal{F}^{*}(\overline{\Omega_{+}},\overline{\Omega_{-}})\subseteq\mathcal{F}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
. Let
f\in\mathcal{F}^{*}(\overline{\Omega_{+}},\overline{\Omega_{-}})
. The aim is to show that
f\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})
. In other words, the aim is to show that
f^{-1}(0,\infty)\subset\Omega_{+}
and
f^{-1}(-\infty,0)\subset\Omega_{-}
. Now, on account of
\overline{\Omega_{+}}=\Omega_{+}\cup\partial{\Omega_{+}}
and
\overline{\Omega_{-}}=\Omega_{-}\cup\partial{\Omega_{-}}
, it suffices to show that
f(x)\leq 0
for all
x\in\partial{\Omega_{+}}
and that
f(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in\partial{\Omega_{-}}
. To that end, let
x\in\partial\Omega_{+}
. Pick
\varepsilon>0
arbitrarily, and use the continuity of
f
to obtain an open neighbhourhood
V
of
x
such that
f(y)\geq f(x)-\varepsilon
for all
y\in V
. Since
x
is in the boundary of
\operatorname{ext}\Omega_{+}
, there is an open set
U
contained in
V
that is fully contained in
\operatorname{ext}\Omega_{+}
. But
y\in\operatorname{ext}\Omega_{+}
implies that
f(y)\leq 0
. Hence
0\geq f(y)\geq f(x)-\varepsilon
for all
y\in U
. Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, it follows that
f(x)\leq 0
. Similarly,
f(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in\partial\Omega_{-}
.

∎

At this point, a natural goal is to generalise Proposition

\mathcal{F}^{*}_{c}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-}):=\{f\in\mathcal{F}_{G}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-}):\operatorname{supp}f\Subset G\}.

Define the corresponding extremal value:

\mathcal{C}_{c}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-}):=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}^{*}_{c}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})}\int_{G}f\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}.

If
\Omega_{+}

\mathcal{C}_{c}^{*}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})=\mathcal{C}^{*}(\Omega_{+},\Omega_{-})

Theorem 32
.

Let
G
be a LCA group which is also a topological vector space (TVS) over
\mathbb{R}
. Let
\Omega_{+}
and
\Omega_{-}
be bounded
0
-symmetric sets in
G
such that
r\overline{\Omega_{+}}\subset\operatorname{int}\Omega_{+}
and
r\overline{\Omega_{-}}\subset\operatorname{int}\Omega_{-}
for all
r\in[0,1)
. Then
\mathcal{C}^{*}(\operatorname{int}\Omega_{+},\operatorname{int}\Omega_{-})=\mathcal{C}^{*}(\overline{\Omega_{+}},\overline{\Omega_{-}})
.

Proof.

Clearly,
\mathcal{C}^{*}(\operatorname{int}\Omega_{+},\operatorname{int}\Omega_{-})\leq\mathcal{C}^{*}(\overline{\Omega_{+}},\overline{\Omega_{-}})
, so we only prove the reverse inequality. Let us take
\varepsilon>0
and
f\in\mathcal{F}^{*}_{c}(\overline{\Omega_{+}},\overline{\Omega_{-}})
with
\int_{G}f\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}\geq\mathcal{C}^{*}_{c}(\overline{\Omega_{+}},\overline{\Omega_{-}})-\varepsilon=\mathcal{C}^{*}(\overline{\Omega_{+}},\overline{\Omega_{-}})-\varepsilon
. The last equality is by Lemma
31
. Take any
R>1
and put
r:=1/R<1
. Consider the function
h_{R}(g):=f(Rg)
. Obviously,
\operatorname{supp}(h_{R})_{+}\subset r\overline{\Omega_{+}}\subset\operatorname{int}\Omega_{+}
and
\operatorname{supp}(h_{R})_{-}\subset r\overline{\Omega_{-}}\subset\operatorname{int}\Omega_{-}
. Furthermore,
h_{R}
is a compactly supported positive definite continuous function satisfying
h_{R}(0)=1
. Hence
h_{R}\in\mathcal{F}^{\ast}_{c}(\operatorname{int}\Omega_{+},\operatorname{int}\Omega_{-})
. Now,
h_{R}(g)=f(Rg)\to f(g)
as
R\to 1+
. Let
0<\delta<1
and consider the set

K:=\{rg:1-\delta\leq r\leq 1,g\in\operatorname{supp}f\}=\left[1-\delta,1\right]\cdot\operatorname{supp}f.

Observe that
K
, being a continuous image of the compact set
[1-\delta,1]\times\operatorname{supp}f\subset\mathbb{R}\times G
under the multiplication map
\mathbb{R}\times G\ni(r,g)\mapsto rg\in G
is a compact subset of
G
, and hence has finite Haar measure. For
1<R<\frac{1}{1-\delta}
, we have that
\operatorname{supp}h_{R}\subset K
, so that
|h_{R}|\leq\mathbf{1}_{K}\in L^{1}(G)
. Hence, by Lebesgue’s Dominated Convergence Theorem, we have

\lim_{R\to 1+}\int_{G}h_{R}\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}=\int_{G}f\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}.

In other words, there exists
R_{0}>1
such that

\left|\int_{G}h_{R}\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}-\int_{G}f\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}\right|<\varepsilon,\mbox{ for all }R\in(1,R_{0}).

Then, for all
R\in(1,R_{0})
, keeping in mind that
h_{R}\in\mathcal{F}^{\ast}(\operatorname{int}\Omega_{+},\operatorname{int}\Omega_{-})
, we have that

\mathcal{C}^{*}(\operatorname{int}\Omega_{+},\operatorname{int}\Omega_{-})\geq\int_{G}h_{R}\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}>\int_{G}f\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}-\varepsilon\geq\mathcal{C}^{*}(\overline{\Omega_{+}},\overline{\Omega_{-}})-2\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, we have
\mathcal{C}^{*}(\operatorname{int}\Omega_{+},\operatorname{int}\Omega_{-})\geq\mathcal{C}^{*}(\overline{\Omega_{+}},\overline{\Omega_{-}})
as desired.
∎

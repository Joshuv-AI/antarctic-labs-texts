Non-Commutative Gauge Theory at the Beach
& David Skinner

Abstract
The KP equation is perhaps the most famous example of a three-dimensional integrable system. Here we show that a non-commutative five-dimensional Chern-Simons theory living on the projective spinor bundle of three-dimensional space-time compactifies to a Lagrangian formulation of the KP equation. Essential to the definition of the theory is a 2-form pulled back from minitwistor space. The dispersionless limit of the KP equation is similarly described by Poisson-Chern-Simons theory. We further show that, consistent with integrability, all tree level amplitudes vanish. The universal vertex algebra living on a two-dimensional surface defect in 5 ​ d 5d is W 1 + ∞ W_{1+\infty} , and its operator products coincide with collinear splitting functions on space-time. Taking the dispersionless limit contracts the vertex algebra to w 1 + ∞ w_{1+\infty} .

Background

In this section we introduce a Lagrangian formulation of the dKP and KP equations, and recall the minitwistor correspondence.

2.1
An Action for the KP Equation

We begin by introducing new co-ordinates on space-time which are better suited to the correspondence space formulation. Compared to (
1.1
) we write

x^{+}=x\,,\qquad x^{-}=-12\sigma^{2}t

(2.1)

and rescale
y\mapsto y/\sqrt{2}
,
u\mapsto-2\sigma^{2}u
. This yields

\bigg(u_{-}+uu_{+}-\frac{1}{12\sigma^{2}}u_{+++}\bigg)_{+}-\frac{1}{2}u_{yy}=0\,,

(2.2)

where
u_{\pm}=\partial_{\pm}u
. The dispersionless limit is obtained by relaxing the condition
\sigma^{2}=\pm 1
and sending
\sigma^{2}\to\infty

(u_{-}+uu_{+})_{+}-\frac{1}{2}u_{yy}=0\,.

(2.3)

Our choice of notation here is motivated by the fact that, from the correspondence space perspective, the variables
x^{+},x^{-}
are interpreted as null co-ordinates. This is visible in the rescaled dKP equation above: the linear terms in
u
take the form wave equation in
2+1
space-time dimensions with metric
2\mathrm{d}x^{+}\mathrm{d}x^{-}-\mathrm{d}y^{2}
. Of course, in physical contexts we must remember that
t=-x^{-}/12\sigma^{2}
is identified with time.

The KP equation (
1.1
) itself is not the equation of motion of any Lagrangian. However, consider the action

S_{\text{KP}}[\phi]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{1,2}}\mathrm{d}^{3}x\,\bigg(\phi_{+}\phi_{-}-\frac{1}{2}\phi_{y}^{2}+\frac{1}{12\sigma^{2}}\phi_{++}^{2}+\frac{1}{3}\phi_{+}^{3}\bigg)

(2.4)

for
\mathrm{d}^{3}x=\mathrm{d}x^{-}\mathrm{d}x^{+}\mathrm{d}y
. The equations of motion obtained by extremising
S_{\text{KP}}[\phi]
are

\Big(\phi_{-}+\frac{1}{2}\phi_{+}^{2}-\frac{1}{12\sigma^{2}}\phi_{+++}\Big)_{+}=\frac{1}{2}\phi_{yy}\,.

(2.5)

Taking its
x^{+}
derivative, this equation implies

\Big(\phi_{+-}+\phi_{+}\phi_{++}-\frac{1}{12\sigma^{2}}\phi_{++++}\Big)_{+}=\phi_{yy+}\,,

(2.6)

which we recognise as the KP equation (
2.2
) upon setting
u=\phi_{+}
. Thus
\phi
is a form of first potential, sitting between
u
and the
\tau
-function,
u=\phi_{+}=-\sigma^{-2}(\log\tau)_{++}
. Writing
[\mathcal{O}]
for the mass dimension of
\mathcal{O}
under simultaneous scaling of the space and time co-ordinates, the action transforms homogeneously with
[S_{\text{KP}}]=-3
if we take
[\sigma]=1
and
[\phi]=-1
.

A Lagrangian for the dispersionless KP equation is obtained in the limit
\sigma^{2}\to\infty

S_{\text{dKP}}[\phi]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{1,2}}\mathrm{d}^{3}x\,\Big(\phi_{+}\phi_{-}-\frac{1}{2}\phi_{y}^{2}+\frac{1}{3}\phi_{+}^{3}\Big)\,.

(2.7)

In section
5
we will see how to recover the actions (
2.4
) and (
2.7
) from correspondence space.

2.2
Minitwistor Correspondence for
\mathbb{C}^{3}

Next, we briefly review the minitwistor correspondence, originally due to Hitchin
[
14
]
. (See also
[
35
,
36
]
.) It identifies certain three-dimensional complex geometries with two-dimensional complex surfaces admitting families of rational curves of self-intersection number
2
(or equivalently having normal bundle isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}(2)
). The holomorphic metric of the complex three manifold (or more precisely its Weyl structure) is encoded entirely in the complex structure of the surface.

In the first instance we will be interested in the case of
\mathbb{C}^{3}
equipped with the standard complex metric. We fix complex co-ordinates
(x^{+},x^{-},y)
in terms of which the metric is
\eta=2\mathrm{d}x^{+}\mathrm{d}x^{-}-\mathrm{d}y^{2}
. It will also be useful to introduce a spinor notation
x^{\alpha\beta}=x^{(\alpha\beta)}
for points in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
. Fixing a dyad
\{o^{\alpha},\iota^{\beta}\}
such that
\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}o^{\alpha}\iota^{\beta}=\langle o\iota\rangle=1
we can identify

x^{+}=x^{\alpha\beta}o_{\alpha}o_{\beta}\,,\qquad y=\sqrt{2}x^{\alpha\beta}o_{\alpha}\iota_{\beta}\,,\qquad x^{-}=x^{\alpha\beta}\iota_{\alpha}\iota_{\beta}\,.

(2.8)

The standard complex metric is

\eta=\epsilon_{\alpha\gamma}\epsilon_{\beta\delta}\mathrm{d}x^{\alpha\beta}\mathrm{d}x^{\gamma\delta}=2\mathrm{d}x^{+}\mathrm{d}x^{-}-\mathrm{d}y^{2}\,.

(2.9)

In this case the associated complex surface is
T^{1,0}\mathbb{CP}^{1}
, or equivalently the total space of the holomorphic line bundle

\mathcal{O}(2)\to\mathbb{CP}^{1}\,.

(2.10)

Often we will refer to this simply as
minitwistor space
and denote it by
{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}
. Points in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
are identified with holomorphic sections of
{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}
. Using inhomogeneous co-ordinates
(v,z)
on the fibre and base respectively, these sections are quadratics

v=x^{+}-\sqrt{2}yz+x^{-}z^{2}\,.

(2.11)

This identity is the
incidence relation
. Using homogeneous co-ordinates
(\mu,\lambda_{\alpha})\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
defined up to scale
(\mu,\lambda_{\alpha})\sim(s^{2}\mu,s\lambda_{\alpha})
for
s\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
we can equivalently write this as

\mu=x^{\alpha\beta}\lambda_{\alpha}\lambda_{\beta}\,.

(2.12)

The identification between homogeneous and inhomogeneous co-ordinates is
(\mu,\lambda_{\alpha})\sim(v,o_{\alpha}+z\iota_{\alpha})
.

Points in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
are null-separated if the corresponding quadratics intersect at a single point. For example, the point
x\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
is on the complexified null-cone of the origin if the discriminant vanishes

\epsilon_{\alpha\gamma}\epsilon_{\beta\delta}x^{\alpha\beta}x^{\gamma\delta}=2x^{+}x^{-}-y^{2}=0\,.

(2.13)

This information is sufficient to recover the conformal structure of
\mathbb{C}^{3}
; however, Hitchin shows that the complex structure of
{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}
also encodes the scale of
\mathbb{C}^{3}
. This is because, given a tangent direction at a point in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
, the complex structure of
{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}
can be used to construct a complex curve in that direction which is interpreted as a geodesic. This endows
\mathbb{C}^{3}
with a projective connection which is compatible with the conformal structure. Within the projective class there is a distinguished affine connection, which in this case is the Levi-Civita connection of the flat metric.

We can also ask what points in
{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}
correspond to in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
. We can see from the incidence relation (
2.11
) that
(v,z)
determines a complex plane
\Pi
in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
. On such a plane

\mathrm{d}x^{+}=\sqrt{2}z\mathrm{d}y-z^{2}\mathrm{d}x^{-}

(2.14)

so that

\iota^{*}_{\Pi}\eta=-\mathrm{d}y^{2}+2\sqrt{2}z\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x^{-}-2z^{2}(\mathrm{d}x^{-})^{2}=-(\mathrm{d}y-\sqrt{2}z\mathrm{d}x^{-})^{2}\,.

(2.15)

\eta
therefore restricts to a degenerate metric, and so
\Pi
is a null plane. (Note that a null plane is not totally null: the metric is degenerate but non-vanishing on
\Pi

{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}
are null planes in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
.

{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}
admits a natural action of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
acting by Möbius transformations on the base and lifting in the obvious way to the fibres. This is identified with
\mathrm{SO}_{3}(\mathbb{C})
, the Lorentz group of
(\mathbb{C}^{3},\eta)
. Viewing
{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}
as an affine bundle
\mathrm{SO}_{3}(\mathbb{C})
is extended by translations
\mathbb{C}^{3}
to the complex Poincaré group.

We can introduce an intermediate complex four-manifold
\mathcal{F}=\{[(\mu,\lambda_{\alpha})]\in{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}\,,~x^{\alpha\beta}\in\mathbb{C}^{3}:\mu=x^{\alpha\beta}\lambda_{\alpha}\lambda_{\beta}\}
consisting of points in
{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}
and
\mathbb{C}^{3}
such that the former lies on the quadratic corresponding to the latter. It’s often convenient to view
\mathcal{F}=\mathbb{C}^{3}\times\mathbb{CP}^{1}
, where the fibre co-ordinate on
{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}
is determined by the incidence relation. From now on we refer to
\mathcal{F}
as
complex correspondence space
. By definition
\mathcal{F}
admits a double fibration
\mathcal{F}
{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}
\mathbb{C}^{3}
\rho
\pi

.

2.3
Minitwistor Correspondence for
\mathbb{R}^{1,2}

Thus far our discussion has concerned three-dimensional complex manifolds, but we’d like to restrict to a real slice. In order to so we pick the anti-holomorphic involution

C:\mathbb{C}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}^{3}\,,\qquad(x^{+},x^{-},y)\mapsto(\bar{x}^{+},\bar{x}^{-},\bar{y})

(2.16)

whose fixed-point locus is
\mathbb{R}^{1,2}
. We’d like to distinguish those sections of
{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}
corresponding to points in
\mathbb{R}^{1,2}
. This is achieved by choosing the anti-holomorphic involution

R:{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}\to{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}\,,\qquad(v,z)\mapsto(\bar{v},\bar{z})\,.

(2.17)

In terms of homogeneous co-ordinates the involution acts by
R:(\mu,\lambda_{\alpha})\mapsto(\bar{\mu},\bar{\lambda}_{\alpha})
, which is compatible with the
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
quotient. (Here we are taking the dyad
\{o,\iota\}
to be real.) This involution picks out a subspace
\{\lambda\sim\bar{\lambda}\}=S^{1}\subset\mathbb{CP}^{1}
on the base of
{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}
fixed pointwise by
R
. Furthermore, the fixed points of
{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}
form the trivial real line bundle over this
S^{1}
.

It’s clear that those sections which are fixed under
R
correspond to points in
\mathbb{R}^{1,2}
. (Note that we do not require the section to be fixed pointwise; indeed, this would exclude all sections.) The subgroup of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
acting by Möbius transformations commuting with the involution
R
is
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
which we recognise as the Lorentz group
\mathrm{SO}^{+}(1,2)
. Translations compatible with
R
are of course real, so the real Poincaré group of
\mathbb{R}^{1,2}
survives.

We’ve seen that a point
[(\mu,\lambda)]\in{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}
describes a complex null plane
\Pi\subset\mathbb{C}^{3}

\mathbb{R}^{1,2}
can be of three types:

•

If
\lambda\not\sim\bar{\lambda}
then
\Pi\cap\mathbb{R}^{1,2}
is the timelike geodesic

x^{\alpha\beta}=x_{0}^{\alpha\beta}+sn^{\alpha\beta}=\frac{\mu\bar{\lambda}^{\alpha}\bar{\lambda}^{\beta}+\bar{\mu}\lambda^{\alpha}\lambda^{\beta}}{\langle\lambda\bar{\lambda}\rangle^{2}}+\sqrt{2}\mathrm{i}s\frac{\lambda^{(\alpha}\bar{\lambda}^{\beta)}}{\langle\lambda\bar{\lambda}\rangle}

(2.18)

for

. The direction of the geodesic is determined by
n^{\alpha\beta}=\sqrt{2}\mathrm{i}\lambda^{(\alpha}\bar{\lambda}^{\beta)}/\langle\lambda\bar{\lambda}\rangle\in\mathbb{R}^{1,2}
with
n^{2}=1
.

is future pointing for
\mathrm{Im}\langle\lambda\bar{\lambda}\rangle>0
(
\mathrm{Im}\,z<0
) and past pointing for
\mathrm{Im}\langle\lambda\bar{\lambda}\rangle<0
(
\mathrm{Im}\,z>0
). Conversely, all timelike geodesics arise this way.

•

If
(\mu,\lambda)\sim(\bar{\mu},\bar{\lambda})
then
\Pi\cap\mathbb{R}^{1,2}
is the real null plane

x^{\alpha\beta}=x_{0}^{\alpha\beta}+x^{(\alpha}\lambda^{\beta)}=\mu\frac{\nu^{\alpha}\nu^{\beta}}{\langle\lambda\nu\rangle^{2}}+\xi^{(\alpha}\lambda^{\beta)}\,.

(2.19)

Here
\nu^{\alpha}\sim\bar{\nu}^{\alpha}\in S^{1}\subset\mathbb{CP}^{1}
is a real spinor such that
\langle\lambda\nu\rangle\neq 0
(certainly such a spinor exists) and
\xi^{\alpha}=\bar{\xi}^{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. This is the real null plane passing through
x_{0}
which is orthogonal to the light ray in the direction
\lambda^{\alpha}\lambda^{\beta}
. Again, all real null planes arise in this way.

•

If
\lambda\sim\bar{\lambda}
but
(\mu,\lambda)\not\sim(\bar{\mu},\bar{\lambda})
then
\Pi\cap\mathbb{R}^{1,2}
is empty.

We can also defined a real subspace of complex correspondence space
\mathbb{PS}=\{[(\mu,\lambda_{\alpha})]\in{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}\,,~x^{\alpha\beta}\in\mathbb{R}^{1,2}:\mu=x^{\alpha\beta}\lambda_{\alpha}\lambda_{\beta}\}\subset\mathcal{F}
which coincides with the projective spinor bundle of
\mathbb{R}^{1,2}
. We will refer to
\mathbb{PS}
simply as
correspondence space
. It admits maps
\mathbb{PS}
{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}
\mathbb{R}^{1,2}

\pi

.

The preimage of a point in
\mathbb{R}^{1,2}
is a

{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}
differs substantially depending on which of the above three classes it belongs to. Often we will refer to the
\mathbb{CP}^{1}_{x}
fibre over a point

as a

lifts to a map

2.4
Minitwistor Description of the dKP Equation

In
[
14
]
Hitchin shows that the minitwistor correspondence of subsections
2.2
and
2.3
is an instance of a more general correspondence between
3d
Einstein-Weyl geometries and complex surfaces. While we will not directly need this more general situation in the rest of the paper, we briefly review it here because the gravitational perspective on the dKP equation appears naturally both in minitwistor space and from our non-commutative gauge theory, which is also inherently gravitational. In addition, it provides a convenient way to introduce the Lax operators of the dKP equation; we will recover these from the Chern-Simons description in section
5.1
.

A real
3d
Weyl manifold is a real conformal three-manifold
(M,[h])
admitting a torsion-free connection
\nabla
such that

\nabla h=\nu\otimes h

(2.20)

for some representative of the conformal class
h
and 1-form
\nu\in\Omega^{1}(M)
. Under a Weyl transformation
h\mapsto\Omega^{2}h
so that equation (
2.20
) continues to hold for
\nu\mapsto\nu+2\mathrm{d}\log\Omega
. We say that
(M,[h],\nabla)
is
Einstein-Weyl
if

\mathrm{Ric}(\nabla)+\mathrm{Ric}(\nabla)^{\mathrm{T}}=2\Lambda h\,,

(2.21)

i.e., the symmetric part of the Ricci tensor of the connection
\nabla
is proportional to a representative of the conformal class
h
. The antisymmetric part of the Ricci tensor is

\mathrm{Ric}(\nabla)-\mathrm{Ric}(\nabla)^{\mathrm{T}}=-3\mathrm{d}\nu\,.

(2.22)

If this vanishes, then at least locally there’s a representative metric in the conformal class for which the connection
\nabla
is Levi-Civita, and the Einstein-Weyl equations (
2.21
) become the Einstein equation for this metric. Hence
\mathbb{R}^{1,2}
,
\mathrm{dS}_{3}
and
\mathrm{AdS}_{3}
are all examples of
3d
real Lorentzian Einstein-Weyl spaces.

The Hitchin correspondence states that complex three-folds with holomorphic Einstein-Weyl structures are in bijection with complex surfaces admitting a rational curve with normal bundle
\mathcal{O}(2)
.
Let’s briefly sketch how this works, following the presentation in
[
33
]
. Suppose we’re given a complex three-fold
M
with a holomorphic Einstein-Weyl structure
([h],\nabla)
. Fixing a representative of the conformal class
h
let
\{e_{\alpha\beta}\}
be dreibeins, so that
h=\epsilon^{\gamma\alpha}\epsilon^{\delta\beta}e_{\alpha\beta}\,e_{\gamma\delta}
. Since
([h],\nabla)
is a Weyl structure there exists a spin connection
\gamma^{\gamma}_{\,~\,\delta}=\gamma^{\gamma}_{\,~\,\delta\alpha\beta}e^{\alpha\beta}
obeying

\nabla e^{\alpha\beta}+2\gamma^{(\alpha}_{~\,~\delta}\,e^{\beta)\delta}=0\,.

(2.23)

Note that
\gamma_{\gamma\delta\alpha\beta}
is symmetric in
\alpha,\beta
but not necessarily in
\gamma,\delta
since
\nabla
need not be metric. Furthermore let

3.2
The Theory on a More General Five-Manifold

We’ve seen how to define non-commutative Chern-Simons theory on

To achieve this, we require that

\Omega\in\Omega^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}(X)
which is closed,
\mathrm{d}\Omega=0
, and simple,
\Omega^{2}=0

, the complex distribution consisting of vectors which contract to zero against

\Omega

(0,1)

(0,1)

We write
\ell_{\perp}\subset T_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}X
for the annihilator of
\ell

\Omega
. The simplicity of
\Omega
ensures that
\ell_{\perp}
has complex rank two.
\ell_{\perp}
generates a subsheaf
\mathcal{S}
of
\Omega^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{C}}(X)
consisting of all complex differential forms which wedge to zero against
\Omega
. We can then further form the quotient
\widetilde{\Omega}^{0,\bullet}(X)=\Omega^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{C}}(X)/\mathcal{S}
. The de Rham differential
\mathrm{d}
descends to the quotient
\widetilde{\Omega}^{0,\bullet}(X)

\eta\in\Omega^{\bullet}(X)
for some equivalence class
[\eta]\in\widetilde{\Omega}^{0,\bullet}(X)
. Then the ambiguity in the representative is
\eta\sim\eta+\zeta
where
\Omega\,\zeta=0
. The ambiguity in
\mathrm{d}\eta

\mathrm{d}\eta\sim\mathrm{d}\eta+\mathrm{d}\zeta
; however,

\Omega\,\mathrm{d}\zeta=\mathrm{d}(\Omega\,\zeta)=0

(3.9)

by the closure of
\Omega
. Therefore

is well defined. We write
\tilde{\mathrm{d}}
for the resulting differential on
\widetilde{\Omega}^{0,\bullet}(X)
.

In general the fibres of
\ell
(resp.
\ell_{\perp}
) may intersect those of the real tangent bundle
TX
(resp. real cotangent bundle
T^{*}X
) in real subspaces of dimension one, two or three (resp. zero, one or two). If the intersection has dimension one everywhere then
\ell
contains a real non-vanishing vector field whose integral curves are the leaves of a
transverse holomorphic foliation
of
X
. In this work will be concerned with 2-forms
\Omega

In a sufficiently small open neighbourhood
U\subset X
of a point

has dimension one, we can always find holomorphic co-ordinates

such that
\Omega=\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}v
. As before, we can then represent each equivalence class in
\widetilde{\Omega}^{0,\bullet}(U)
by an element of

\mathbb{PS}
it will be more natural to view
(0,\bullet)
-forms as living in the quotient.

Next let’s attempt to define the Poisson bracket, which is obtained by inverting
\Omega

tangent bundle of a complex manifold is the quotient

\Omega
can be inverted to give an element

(1,0)

\widetilde{\Omega}^{0,\bullet}(X)
(also an equivalence class) is certainly ambiguous.

In the open neighbourhood
U

as above we can give a definition by representing sections of

and taking
\Pi=\partial_{z}\vee\partial_{v}
. But globally the best we can do is pick a section
\mathcal{D}:T^{1,0}X\to T_{\mathbb{C}}X
(often we will confuse
\mathcal{D}
and its image) and define
\widetilde{\Omega}^{0,\bullet}_{\mathcal{D}}(X)
to be the subsheaf of differential forms generated by
\mathcal{D}_{\perp}
. Each equivalence class in
\widetilde{\Omega}^{0,\bullet}(X)
has a unique representative in
\widetilde{\Omega}^{0,\bullet}_{\mathcal{D}}(X)
and on these representatives

is represented by the differential
\tilde{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathcal{D}}

\mathcal{D}

\widetilde{\Omega}^{0,\bullet}_{\mathcal{D}}(X)

We now come to an important point: although we need to pick a choice of section
\mathcal{D}
to define Poisson-Chern-Simons theory,
the dependence on this choice can always be removed by a field redefinition
. The reason for this is that, given two choices
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
, the difference between the corresponding Poisson brackets
\{~\,,~\,\}_{\mathcal{D}}
and
\{~\,,~\,\}_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}
involves derivatives along vector fields in
\ell

such that
S_{\mathcal{D}}[a]=S_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}[a^{\prime}]
. Working to lowest non-trivial order, we will demonstrate this explicitly in appendix
B
for the choice of
\mathcal{D}
used below and another choice
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
that arises naturally from minitwistor space. That the field redefinition exists to all orders follows from a generalization of lemma 9.2.1 in
[
45
]
: In brief, it’s clear that using the equations of motion we can trade derivatives along
\ell
for higher order terms; less clear is why the resulting higher order vertices will ultimately vanish. In order to write down vertices beyond cubic order we must contract
a
against
(0,1)
vector fields, but it’s precisely such contractions which cancel against Lie derivatives in the
(0,1)
directions in cohomology. This ensures that there’s a field redefinition eliminating both contractions and derivatives along
\ell
. Mathematically, this is the statement that
(\widetilde{\Omega}^{0,\bullet}_{\mathcal{D}}(X),\tilde{\mathrm{d}}_{\mathcal{D}},\{~,~\}_{\mathcal{D}})
is independent of
\mathcal{D}
up to quasi-isomorphism. The utility of this fact is that we are free to make a convenient choice for
\mathcal{D}
which in particular is not adapted to a holomorphic co-ordinate frame. It turns out that on
\mathbb{PS}
this simplifies life dramatically.

In order to define non-commutative Chern-Simons theory on
X
, we’d further need to turn on non-commutativity for the Poisson bracket
\Pi
. One could appeal to general results regarding the existence of deformation quantizations, but in the case of interest where
X=\mathbb{PS}
we’ll be able to employ the Moyal formula to get an explicit non-commutative product.

(and extending this by a second real reference spinor

\Omega=\frac{\langle\lambda\mathrm{d}\lambda\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x^{\alpha\beta}\lambda_{\alpha}\lambda_{\beta}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{4}}\,.

(4.1)

Closure fails on the locus of the quartic pole, and the price we pay is that our fields will have to obey compensating ‘boundary conditions’ there.

Strictly the locus

Notice also that whilst generically the fibres of

As explained in subsection

\mathcal{D}\subset T_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{PS}

\mathcal{D}=\Big\langle\iota^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{\alpha}}\bigg|_{x}\,,\iota^{\alpha}\iota^{\beta}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\alpha\beta}}\Big\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}\,,

(4.2)

with frame

\partial_{0}=\langle\lambda\iota\rangle\iota^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda^{\alpha}}\bigg|_{x}\,,\qquad\partial_{1}=\iota^{\alpha}\iota^{\beta}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\alpha\beta}}\,.

(4.3)

Here the derivative in
\lambda

. Since
\rho_{*}\partial_{1}=\partial_{+}
and

This choice of

\mathcal{D}_{\perp}=\big\langle\overline{\langle\lambda\mathrm{d}\lambda\rangle},\mathrm{d}x^{\alpha\beta}\iota_{\alpha}\big\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}

with frame

\tilde{\sigma}^{0}=\frac{\overline{\langle\lambda\mathrm{d}\lambda\rangle}}{\overline{\langle o\lambda\rangle}^{2}}\,,\qquad\tilde{\sigma}^{\alpha}=\mathrm{d}x^{\alpha\beta}\iota_{\beta}\,.

(4.5)

Integrability of
\mathcal{D}

\tilde{\sigma}^{0}

Away from

(1,0)

e^{0}=\langle\lambda\mathrm{d}\lambda\rangle\,,\qquad e^{1}=\mathrm{d}x^{\alpha\beta}\lambda_{\alpha}\lambda_{\beta}\,.

(4.6)

Note that these are globally defined in

\langle\lambda\iota\rangle

(\tilde{\mathrm{d}}\partial_{0})\mathbin{\lrcorner}e^{1}=\{\tilde{\mathrm{d}},\partial_{0}\mathbin{\lrcorner}\}e^{1}=2\langle\lambda\iota\rangle\tilde{\sigma}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}\,,

(4.9)

Our frame (

Having fixed the constant frame, the Poisson bivector is
\Pi=\partial_{0}\vee\partial_{1}

Finally, for future reference it will be useful to specify a frame for the

These equations are solved by

\tilde{\partial}_{0}=-\overline{\langle o\lambda\rangle}o^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\lambda}^{\alpha}}\bigg|_{x}\,,\qquad\tilde{\partial}_{\alpha}=P_{\alpha}^{~\,\beta}\frac{\lambda^{\gamma}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\beta\gamma}}

P_{\alpha}^{~\,\beta}=\frac{\iota_{\alpha}\lambda^{\beta}-2\lambda_{\alpha}\iota^{\beta}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle}=\iota_{\alpha}o^{\beta}-2o_{\alpha}\iota^{\beta}-\frac{\langle o\lambda\rangle}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle}\iota_{\alpha}\iota^{\beta}\,,

(4.13)

Note that
\epsilon^{\beta\alpha}P_{\alpha}^{~\,\gamma}P_{\beta}^{~\,\delta}=2\epsilon^{\delta\gamma}
so that

\bar{\lambda}

\ell

\mathcal{D}^{\vee}

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathrm{d}}=\tilde{\sigma}^{0}\tilde{\partial}_{0}+\tilde{\sigma}^{\alpha}\tilde{\partial}_{\alpha}=\bar{\partial}_{\mathbb{CP}^{1}_{x}}+\mathrm{d}x^{\alpha\beta}\iota_{\alpha}P_{\beta}^{~\,\gamma}\frac{\lambda^{\delta}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\gamma\delta}}\,.

(4.14)

Having set up our frames correctly, we are now in position to define Poisson-Chern-Simons theory on
\mathbb{PS}
. The dynamical field is the partial connection
a\in\widetilde{\Omega}^{0,1}(\mathbb{PS})
, whose physical part has components
\tilde{\sigma}^{0}a_{0}+\tilde{\sigma}^{\alpha}a_{\alpha}
. The action is

S_{\text{PCS}}[a]=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{PS}}\Omega\,\bigg(\frac{1}{2}a\tilde{\mathrm{d}}a+\frac{1}{6}a\{a,a\}\bigg)=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{PS}}\Omega\,\bigg(\frac{1}{2}a\tilde{\mathrm{d}}a+\frac{1}{3}a\mathcal{L}_{0}a\mathcal{L}_{1}a\bigg)

(4.15)

where
\mathcal{L}_{0}a=\partial_{0}\mathbin{\lrcorner}\partial a
and
\mathcal{L}_{1}a=\partial_{1}\mathbin{\lrcorner}\partial a
.

8

8

When we use the same distribution
\mathcal{D}
to define the
(1,0)
tangent bundle and space of forms
\widetilde{\Omega}^{0,\bullet}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbb{PS})
the Lie derivative along
(1,0)
vector fields is unambiguous.
The equations of motion state that

\tilde{\mathrm{d}}a+\frac{1}{2}\{a,a\}=0\,,

(4.16)

ensuring nilpotency of the deformed differential
\tilde{\mathrm{d}}+\{a,~\,\}
. Gauge transformations are
(1,0)
Poisson diffeomorphisms generated infinitesimally by Hamiltonians
\epsilon\in\widetilde{\Omega}^{0}(\mathbb{PS})
and acting by
\delta a=\tilde{\mathrm{d}}\epsilon+\{a,\epsilon\}
. At least away from the locus
\lambda\sim\bar{\lambda}
, correspondence space is fibred over
{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}
with real one-dimensional fibres. Entering a gauge in which the pullback of
a
to these fibres vanishes, the equations of motion tell us that the remaining components of
a
are constant. Therefore we can understand
a
as being pulled back from a Hamiltonian complex structure deformation of
{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}
. The surviving gauge transformations are Poisson diffeomorphims on
{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}
(and we will see shortly that these must vanish to second order at
\lambda\sim\iota
). In this way, at least qualitatively, we recover the minitwistor description of the dKP equation reviewed in section
2.4
. This makes Poisson-Chern-Simons on
\mathbb{PS}
a natural candidate for the twistorial uplift of dKP theory.

Our reality conditions state that
a
should be invariant under pullback by
S:(x,\lambda)\mapsto(x,\bar{\lambda})
ensuring that the resulting Hamiltonian deformations describe real Einstein-Weyl geometries. The action
S_{\mathrm{PCS}}
is real: the sign generated by conjugating
\mathrm{i}/2\pi
is compensated by
S
reversing the orientation of
\mathbb{PS}
.

The price of introducing a pole in the measure is that we must impose boundary conditions to eliminate potential contributions when varying the action. Let’s determine these now.

4.2
Boundary Conditions

Varying the kinetic term of (
4.15
) gives

\delta S_{\mathrm{kin.}}[a]=-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{PS}}\Omega\,\tilde{\mathrm{d}}(a\delta a)+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{PS}}\delta a\,(\text{linearised equations of motion})\,.

(4.17)

On-shell this reduces to the boundary contribution, which is easiest to isolate by excising a small disc
D^{2}_{\epsilon}=\{|\tilde{z}|\leq\epsilon\}
around
\tilde{z}=-\langle\lambda\iota\rangle/\langle o\lambda\rangle=0
along the fibres of
\mathbb{PS}\to\mathbb{R}^{1,2}
.

\delta S_{\mathrm{kin.}}[a]\simeq\frac{\mathrm{i}}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{1,2}\times S^{1}_{\epsilon}}\Omega\,a\delta a\,.

(4.18)

Since
\Omega=\langle\lambda\mathrm{d}\lambda\rangle\mathrm{d}x^{\gamma\delta}\lambda_{\gamma}\lambda_{\delta}/\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{4}
saturates the integral over
S^{1}_{\epsilon}
, only the components of
a
in the
\tilde{\sigma}^{\alpha}=\mathrm{d}x^{\alpha\beta}\iota_{\beta}
directions contribute. Expanding
a=a_{0}\tilde{\sigma}^{0}+a_{\alpha}\tilde{\sigma}^{\alpha}
we have

\delta S_{\text{kin.}}[a]\simeq\frac{\mathrm{i}}{8\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{1,2}\times S^{1}_{\epsilon}}\Omega\,\tilde{\sigma}^{\alpha}\,\tilde{\sigma}_{\alpha}\,a^{\beta}\delta a_{\beta}\,.

(4.19)

Certainly
\mathrm{d}x^{\gamma\delta}\lambda_{\gamma}\lambda_{\delta}\,\tilde{\sigma}^{\alpha}\,\tilde{\sigma}_{\alpha}=-\sqrt{2}\mathrm{d}^{3}x\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}
, so the boundary term is

\delta S_{\text{kin.}}[a]\simeq-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{4\sqrt{2}\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{1,2}}\mathrm{d}^{3}x\oint_{S^{1}_{\epsilon}}\frac{\langle\lambda\mathrm{d}\lambda\rangle}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}}a^{\beta}\delta a_{\beta}\,.

(4.20)

From this expression it’s clear that requiring
a_{\alpha}=\mathcal{O}(\langle\lambda\iota\rangle)
is sufficient to kill the boundary term. (That is,
a_{\alpha}
should vanish holomorphically to first order on the locus
\lambda\sim\iota
.) But this is needlessly strong, in fact there is a weaker condition that will also do the trick. We require that
a_{\alpha}
is regular on the locus
\lambda\sim\iota
, and that the combination

2\langle\lambda\iota\rangle o^{\gamma}a_{\gamma}+\langle o\lambda\rangle\iota^{\gamma}a_{\gamma}=\lambda^{\gamma}a_{\gamma}+\langle\lambda\iota\rangle o^{\gamma}a_{\gamma}=\mathcal{O}\big(\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\big)\,.

(4.21)

This implies
\iota^{\gamma}a_{\gamma}=\mathcal{O}(\langle\lambda\iota\rangle)
as for the naïve boundary condition, but
o^{\gamma}a_{\gamma}
can be non-vanishing. Although this appears to depend on the choice of
o
, under a shift
o\to o+m\iota
the left hand side changes by
m\langle\lambda\iota\rangle\iota^{\gamma}a_{\gamma}
which vanishes holomorphically to second order on the locus
\lambda\sim\iota
.

For the sake of clarity, we also write out the boundary condition (
4.21
) in inhomogeneous co-ordinates. Writing
\tilde{z}=-\langle\iota\lambda\rangle/\langle o\lambda\rangle
and identifying
a_{+}=o^{\gamma}a_{\gamma}
,
a_{y}=-\iota^{\gamma}a_{\gamma}/\sqrt{2}
the boundary condition (
4.21
) tell us that
a_{y}
is divisible by
\tilde{z}
and
\sqrt{2}\tilde{z}a_{+}+a_{y}
is divisible by
\tilde{z}^{2}
.

To see that this really does eliminate the boundary term first let’s Schouten

\delta S_{\text{kin.}}[a]\simeq-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{4\sqrt{2}\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathrm{d}^{3}x\oint_{S^{1}_{\epsilon}}\frac{\langle\lambda\mathrm{d}\lambda\rangle}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}}\big(o^{\gamma}a_{\gamma}\iota^{\epsilon}\delta a_{\epsilon}-\iota^{\gamma}a_{\gamma}o^{\epsilon}\delta a_{\epsilon}\big)\,.

(4.22)

We can use the condition (
4.21
) to eliminate
\iota^{\gamma}a_{\gamma},\iota^{\epsilon}\delta a_{\epsilon}
since shifts of order
\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}
are harmless. This leaves

\delta S_{\text{kin.}}[a]\simeq\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\sqrt{2}\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathrm{d}^{3}x\oint_{S^{1}_{\epsilon}}\frac{\langle\lambda\mathrm{d}\lambda\rangle}{\langle o\lambda\rangle\langle\lambda\iota\rangle}o^{\gamma}o^{\epsilon}\big(a_{\gamma}\delta a_{\epsilon}-a_{\gamma}\delta a_{\epsilon}\big)=0\,.

(4.23)

Under a gauge transformation with parameter
\epsilon
the left hand side of equation (
4.21
) transforms as

(o^{\alpha}\iota^{\beta}-2\iota^{\alpha}o^{\beta})\lambda_{\alpha}P_{\beta}^{~\,\gamma}\lambda^{\delta}\partial_{\gamma\delta}\epsilon=2o^{\alpha}\lambda^{\beta}\partial_{\alpha\beta}\epsilon

(4.24)

where we recall
P_{\alpha}^{~\,\beta}=(\iota_{\alpha}\lambda^{\beta}-2\lambda_{\alpha}\iota^{\beta})/\langle\lambda\iota\rangle
. Assuming no restrictions on the
x
dependence of
\epsilon
, this can only preserve the condition (
4.21
) if
\epsilon=\mathcal{O}(\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2})
. This is motivates using the boundary condition
a_{0}=\mathcal{O}(\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2})
for the components of
a
along the fibres of
\mathbb{PS}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
. (The combination
\tilde{\sigma}^{0}a_{0}
should be non-singular at
\lambda\sim o
, since nothing interesting happens there. This requires a second order anti-holomorphic zero in
a_{0}
, but this is merely an artifact of the frame.) There are no boundary contributions generated when varying the interaction vertex of Poisson-Chern-Simons. This is because both
\partial_{0},\partial_{1}
commute with
\langle\lambda\iota\rangle
and so are insensitive to
\Omega
.

We’ve seen that the boundary condition (
4.21
) is valid, but it’s still not clear why we should make this particular choice above any other. We will see in subsection
5.1
that it correctly reproduces the Lax formulation of the dKP equation and in
5.3
that the action (
4.15
) is equivalent to dKP theory on
\mathbb{R}^{1,2}
. If we instead try to impose the naïve boundary condition
a_{\alpha}=\mathcal{O}(\langle\lambda\iota\rangle)
we find that the linearised equations of motion are not solvable.

4.3
Non-Commutative Chern-Simons on
\mathbb{PS}

In order to write down the non-commutative theory we need to quantize the Poisson bracket

\Pi=\partial_{0}\vee\partial_{1}\,.

(4.25)

This is achieved by applying the Moyal formula, which requires splitting the bivector into the skew product of two commuting derivations of
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{PS})
to get a map

\Pi_{\text{split}}:C^{\infty}(\mathbb{PS})\otimes C^{\infty}(\mathbb{PS})\to C^{\infty}(\mathbb{PS})\otimes C^{\infty}(\mathbb{PS})

(4.26)

obeying
c\circ\Pi_{\text{split}}=\{~\,,~\,\}
. Fortunately
[\partial_{0},\partial_{1}]=0
, so we can simply apply the Moyal formula with the naïve splitting
\Pi_{\text{split}}=\partial_{0}\otimes\partial_{1}-\partial_{1}\otimes\partial_{0}
to get

f\star g=c\circ\exp\frac{\mathfrak{q}}{2}\big(\partial_{0}\otimes\partial_{1}-\partial_{1}\otimes\partial_{0}\big)(f\otimes g)=\sum_{m,n\geq 0}\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{m+n}}{m!n!2^{m+n}}(-)^{n}\partial_{0}^{m}\partial_{1}^{n}f\partial_{1}^{n}\partial_{0}^{m}g

(4.27)

for
f,g\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{PS})
. This naturally extends to elements of
\widetilde{\Omega}^{0,\bullet}(\mathbb{PS})
is we understand
\partial_{0},\partial_{1}
to act by Lie derivative. But there’s a catch: the vector field
\partial_{0}
is not holomorphic (equation (
4.9
)). This means that
\tilde{\mathrm{d}}
does not distribute over
\star
. Indeed we show in appendix
A
that

\tilde{\mathrm{d}}(f\star g)=\mathrm{d}f\star g+(-)^{\mathrm{deg}\,f}f\star\tilde{\mathrm{d}}g+\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{4}\mathrm{d}x^{-}\mathcal{L}_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{1}f\star\mathcal{L}_{1}g)

(4.28)

where
\mathcal{L}_{1}=\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{1}}
. Mathematically this means that
(\widetilde{\Omega}^{0,\bullet}(\mathbb{PS}),\tilde{\mathrm{d}},\star)
is not a dga, and so it cannot be used to define a consistent non-commutative gauge theory.

One method of resolving this issue is to instead split the bivector into commuting holomorphic derivations. We will see in subsection
4.4
that this is indeed possible; however, there is an alternative approach. Rather than modifying the star product, we can change the differential. Consider

\tilde{\mathrm{D}}=\tilde{\mathrm{d}}-\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{12}\mathrm{d}x^{-}\mathcal{L}_{1}^{3}\,,

(4.29)

Certainly this is nilpotent

\tilde{\mathrm{D}}^{2}=\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{2}-\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{12}\{\tilde{\mathrm{d}},\mathrm{d}x^{-}\mathcal{L}_{1}^{3}\}=0\,,

(4.30)

and so defines a differential on
\widetilde{\Omega}^{0,\bullet}(\mathbb{PS})
. It also distributes correctly over
\star
, as can be seen by evaluating

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathrm{D}}(f\star g)=\tilde{\mathrm{d}}(f\star g)-\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{12}\mathrm{d}x^{-}\mathcal{L}_{1}^{3}(f\star g)

(4.31)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathrm{d}}(f\star g)-\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{12}(\mathrm{d}x^{-}\mathcal{L}_{1}^{3}f)\star g-(-)^{\mathrm{deg}\,f}\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{12}f\star(\mathrm{d}x^{-}\mathcal{L}_{1}^{3}g)-\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{4}\mathrm{d}x^{-}\mathcal{L}_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{1}f\star\mathcal{L}_{1}g)\,.

Applying the identity (
4.28
) we see the final term cancels leaving

\tilde{\mathrm{D}}(f\star g)=\tilde{\mathrm{D}}f\star g+(-)^{\mathrm{deg}\,f}f\star\tilde{\mathrm{D}}g\,.

(4.32)

This shows that
(\widetilde{\Omega}^{0,\bullet}(\mathbb{PS}),\tilde{\mathrm{D}},\star)
is a dga, and therefore that

S_{\star}[a]=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{PS}}\Omega\,\bigg(\frac{1}{2}a\star\tilde{\mathrm{D}}a+\frac{1}{3\mathfrak{q}}a\star a\star a\bigg)=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{PS}}\Omega\,\bigg(\frac{1}{2}a\tilde{\mathrm{D}}a+\frac{1}{6}a[a,a]_{\star}\bigg)

(4.33)

is a consistent non-commutative gauge theory on
\mathbb{PS}
. (That is, the BV action satisfies the classical master equation.) We continue to use the reality condition
a=S^{*}a
and the boundary conditions (
4.21
) since the new vertices involve only
(1,0)
derivatives
\partial_{0},\partial_{1}
which cannot generate any extra boundary contributions.

The equations of motion of the theory (
4.33
) read

\tilde{\mathrm{D}}a+\frac{1}{2}[a,a]_{\star}=0\,,

(4.34)

and infinitesimal gauge transformations act by
\delta a=\tilde{\mathrm{D}}\epsilon+[a,\epsilon]_{\star}
. As in the case of Poisson-Chern-Simons, away from the locus
\lambda\sim\bar{\lambda}
correspondence space fibres over
{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}
with one-dimensional fibres. We can exploit this to enter a gauge in which the pullback of
a
to the fibres vanishes and on-shell the remaining components are constant. In this gauge
a
is obtained by pullback from
{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}
. Residual gauge transformations are also constant in the fibre direction, and so are pulled back from
{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}
. Hence, at least qualitatively, solutions to the equations of motion of (
4.33
) describe non-commutative deformations of minitwistor space. Following the proposal of Strachan
[
15
]
, it’s natural to expect that the theory (
3.5
) describes KP equation on space-time.

We will verify this explicitly in section
5
by showing that the action (
4.33
) with the boundary conditions (
4.21
) is equivalent to full KP theory on
\mathbb{R}^{1,2}
, where the constant
1/\sigma^{2}
controlling the dispersive term is identified with the formal parameter
\mathfrak{q}^{2}
. Remarkably we find that the
3d
interaction vertex is unmodified, essentially because the higher derivative corrections to the interaction vertex on
\mathbb{PS}
involve many
\iota
s which contract to zero with one another upon reduction. The modification to the kinetic term through
\tilde{\mathrm{D}}
results in the dispersive term in KP.

4.4
Splitting the Bivector Holomorphically

In the previous subsection we quantized the bivector
\Pi
using the Moyal formula adapted to the naïve splitting into
\partial_{0},\partial_{1}
. This gave a star product
\star
over which
\tilde{\mathrm{d}}
failed to distribute, but fortunately we were able to identify a modified differential
\tilde{\mathrm{D}}
which did distribute properly.

Perhaps a more natural approach would have been to split the bivector holomorphically in the first instance. Here we show that doing so results in an equivalent theory on correspondence space. To this end, we seek a modified splitting
\Pi^{\prime}_{\text{split}}=v_{0}\otimes\partial_{1}-\partial_{1}\otimes v_{0}
where
v_{0}=\partial_{0}+s\partial_{1}
such that
[v_{0},\partial_{1}]=-(\partial_{1}s)\partial_{1}=0
and

\tilde{\mathrm{d}}v_{0}=\tilde{\mathrm{d}}\partial_{0}+\tilde{\mathrm{d}}s\otimes\partial_{1}=\bigg(2\frac{\tilde{\sigma}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle}+\tilde{\mathrm{d}}s\bigg)\otimes\partial_{1}=0\,.

(4.35)

A natural choice is

s=-2\frac{x^{\alpha\beta}\iota_{\alpha}\lambda_{\beta}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle}\,.

(4.36)

Then the resulting holomorphic
\ast
product is

f*g=c\circ\exp\frac{\mathfrak{q}}{2}\big(v_{0}\otimes\partial_{1}-\partial_{1}\otimes v_{0}\big)(f\otimes g)\,.

(4.37)

This extends to
(0,\bullet)
-forms in the usual way, and
(\widetilde{\Omega}^{0,\bullet}(\mathbb{PS}),\tilde{\mathrm{d}},*)
is a dga. We can use this to define a non-commutative Chern-Simons using the now familiar action (
3.5
) and boundary conditions (
4.21
).

The cost of splitting the bivector holomorphically is that the associative product
\ast
manifestly breaks translational invariance on
\mathbb{R}^{1,2}
. This seems incompatible with the translational invariance of the KP equation. Furthermore, since the kinetic term of the non-commutative gauge theory coincides with that of Poisson Chern-Simons it’s hard to see how a dispersive term could be generated in
3d
. Nevertheless, the dgas
(\widetilde{\Omega}^{0,\bullet}(\mathbb{PS}),\tilde{\mathrm{D}},\star)
and
(\widetilde{\Omega}^{0,\bullet}(\mathbb{PS}),\tilde{\mathrm{d}},\ast)
are isomorphic and so define equivalent theories on correspondence space.
9
9

9

They are isomorphic at the chain level, indicating that at least classically the corresponding non-commutative Chern-Simons theories are equivalent off-shell.

To understand why, let

R=\frac{1}{2}\big(\Pi_{\text{split}}-\Pi_{\text{split}}^{\prime}\big)=\frac{1}{2}\big(\partial_{1}\otimes(s\partial_{1})-(s\partial_{1})\otimes\partial_{1}\big)\,.

(4.38)

It’s crucial here that the tensor products are taken over
\mathbb{C}
rather then
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{PS})
, so that
s
cannot pass from one factor to the other. Composing with the multiplication map
c
allows us to swap
s
between the factors, so that
c\circ R=0
.

By a quick computation

[\Pi_{\text{split}},R]=(x^{-}\partial_{1})\otimes\partial_{1}^{2}+\partial_{1}^{2}\otimes(x^{-}\partial_{1})\,.

(4.39)

The object appearing on the right hand side is built solely from
x^{-},\partial_{1}
which commute with
s,\partial_{0}
and therefore with both
\Pi_{\text{split}}
and
R
. It can be treated as central.

We can now directly compare the star products
\star
(
4.27

\ast
(
4.37

\displaystyle\exp\Big(\frac{\mathfrak{q}}{2}\Pi^{\prime}_{\text{split}}\Big)=\exp\Big(\frac{\mathfrak{q}}{2}\Pi_{\text{split}}-\mathfrak{q}R\Big)

(4.40)

\displaystyle=\exp\Big(-\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{4}[\Pi_{\text{split}},R]\Big)\circ\exp(-\mathfrak{q}R)\circ\exp\Big(\frac{\mathfrak{q}}{2}\Pi_{\text{split}}\Big)\,.

Now that all instances of
R
now appear to the left of
\Pi_{\text{split}}
(and recalling that
[\Pi_{\text{split}},R]
is effectively central), we can act with the multiplication map to get

\ast=c\circ\exp\Big(\frac{\mathfrak{q}}{2}\Pi^{\prime}_{\text{split}}\Big)=c\circ\exp\Big(-\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{4}\big((x^{-}\partial_{1})\otimes\partial_{1}^{2}+\partial_{1}^{2}\otimes(x^{-}\partial_{1})\big)\Big)\circ\exp\Big(\frac{\mathfrak{q}}{2}\Pi_{\text{split}}\Big)\,.

(4.41)

Since
\partial_{1}x^{-}=0
we can commute any instance of
x^{-}
in, say, the first factor of the tensor product to the left until it reaches the multiplication symbol. At this stage it can be safely swapped to the second factor, and commuted to the right past any number of
\partial_{1}

x^{-}
can swap between the two factors of the tensor product. In this situation we have the identity

(x^{-}\partial_{1})\otimes\partial_{1}^{2}+\partial_{1}^{2}\otimes(x^{-}\partial_{1})=\frac{1}{3}(x^{-}\otimes 1)(\partial_{1}\otimes 1+1\otimes\partial_{1})^{3}-\frac{1}{3}(x^{-}\partial_{1}^{3})\otimes 1-\frac{1}{3}1\otimes(x^{-}\partial_{1}^{3})\,.

(4.42)

All objects built from
x^{-},\partial_{1}
commute with
\Pi_{\text{split}}

\displaystyle\ast=c\circ\exp\Big(-\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{12}(x^{-}\otimes 1)(\partial_{1}\otimes 1+1\otimes\partial_{1})^{3}\Big)\circ\exp\Big(\frac{\mathfrak{q}}{2}\Pi_{\text{split}}\Big)

(4.43)

\displaystyle\circ\bigg(\exp\Big(\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{12}x^{-}\partial_{1}^{3}\Big)\otimes\exp\Big(\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{12}x^{-}\partial_{1}^{3}\Big)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\exp\Big(-\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{12}x^{-}\partial_{1}^{3}\Big)\,c\circ\exp\Big(\frac{\mathfrak{q}}{2}\Pi_{\text{split}})\circ\bigg(\exp\Big(\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{12}x^{-}\partial_{1}^{3}\Big)\otimes\exp\Big(\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{12}x^{-}\partial_{1}^{3}\Big)\bigg)

Defining
\psi=\exp(\mathfrak{q}^{2}x^{-}\partial_{1}^{3}/12)
this is
\psi(f\ast g)=(\psi f)\star(\psi g)

(4.44)

so

It’s gratifying to find that quantizing the Poisson bivector in a manifestly holomorphic way is consistent with the approach taken in subsection
4.3

\mathbb{R}^{1,2}
can describe the KP equation. We resolve this issue in subsection

4.37

5
From Correspondence Space to Space-Time

In this section we show that Poisson-Chern-Simons theory on minitwistor correspondence space describes dKP, and furthermore that non-commutative Chern-Simons on minitwistor correspondence space describes KP.

More precisely, it’s long been known that solutions to the dKP equation are in correspondence with minitwistor spaces dressed by suitable holomorphic data and admitting appropriate involutive automorphisms. Furthermore, it has more recently been proposed that solutions to the KP equation are equivalent to certain non-commutative minitwistor spaces. Solutions to the equations of motion of Poisson-Chern-Simons theory and non-commutative Chern-Simons theory engineer minitwistor spaces corresponding to solutions of dKP and KP, and so the on-shell equivalence of the theories is not surprising.

In this section we show that by partially imposing the equations of motion of Poisson-Chern-Simons theory and non-commutative Chern-Simons it’s possible to recover an off-shell scalar field
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{1,2})
. Evaluating the correspondence space actions on this partially on-shell solution gives dKP (
2.7
) and KP (
2.4
) respectively. This demonstrates the off-shell equivalence of the theories.

5.1
Recovering the dKP Lax

Here we show that gauge fixing the correspondence space field
a
of Poisson-Chern-Simons and partially imposing the equations of motion recovers a Lax formulation of the dKP equation. Repeating the exercise for non-commutative Chern-Simons yields a Lax for the full KP equation.

To recover the Lax pair we will mimic the approach adopted in
[
24
,
17
]
. The first step is to trivialise
a
on the real minitwistor lines
\mathbb{CP}^{1}_{x}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{1,2}
. ‘Gauge transformations’ in Poisson-Chern-Simons are Poisson diffeomorphisms whose infinitesimal counterparts are Hamiltonian vector fields. We write
\epsilon\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{PS})
for an infinitesimal gauge parameter,
V_{\epsilon}=\{\epsilon,~\,\}
for the corresponding Hamiltonian vector field and
f_{\epsilon}=\exp V_{\epsilon}
for the Poisson diffeomorphism it generates.

Pulling back
a
to the minitwistor line
\mathbb{CP}^{1}_{x}
yields
\tilde{\sigma}^{0}a_{0}\in\Omega^{0,1}(\mathbb{CP}^{1}_{x})
with
a_{0}=\mathcal{O}(\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2})

\lambda\sim\iota

V_{a_{0}}=\{a_{0},~\,\}=f_{\epsilon}^{-1}\tilde{\partial}_{0}f_{\epsilon}\,.

(5.1)

We can get an explicit formula for

\epsilon

a_{0}=\frac{1-e^{-V_{\epsilon}}}{V_{\epsilon}}\tilde{\partial}_{0}\epsilon\,.

(5.2)

The Poisson diffeomorphism

\lambda^{\alpha}
and

\Lambda^{\alpha}(x,\lambda)=f_{\epsilon}\lambda^{\alpha}\,,\qquad X^{\alpha\beta}(x,\lambda)=f_{\epsilon}x^{\alpha\beta}

\tilde{\partial}_{0}\Lambda^{\alpha}=-\iota^{\alpha}\partial_{1}a_{0}(X,\Lambda)\,,\qquad\tilde{\partial}_{0}X^{\alpha\beta}=\iota^{\alpha}\iota^{\beta}\partial_{0}a_{0}(X,\Lambda)\,.

(5.4)

All fields and gauge parameters are required to be invariant under simultaneous conjugation and pullback by
S:(x,\lambda)\mapsto(x,\bar{\lambda})
, ensuring that
(X,\Lambda)
are similarly equivariant. These equations can be solved order by order in
\tilde{\sigma}^{0}a_{0}

\bar{\partial}_{\mathbb{CP}^{1}_{x}}

\Lambda^{\alpha}-\lambda^{\alpha}=\mathcal{O}(\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2})

as we approach

(5.5)

Indeed, we’re free to make any choice for
\epsilon
so long as it vanishes holomorphically to first order on the locus
\lambda\sim\iota
with derivative (
5.5
) but for the moment we will leave it unspecified.

Having fixed the gauge, we next impose the equations of motion in the fibre directions. These are easiest to interpret by writing

\tilde{\mathrm{d}}+\{a,~\,\}=f_{\epsilon}^{-1}(\tilde{\mathrm{d}}+\{a^{\prime},~\,\})f_{\epsilon}

(5.6)

for

as above.
a^{\prime}
is the gauge field in the ‘illegal’ gauge

are meromorphic in
\lambda
at fixed

\bar{\partial}_{\mathbb{CP}^{1}_{x}}a^{\prime}_{\alpha}=0\,.

(5.7)

But since the gauge

is not attainable with a true gauge transformation, the

do not obey our original boundary conditions (
4.21
) at
\lambda\sim\iota
. Instead they must be chosen so that the

obey these boundary conditions, which we recall are given by

2\langle\lambda\iota\rangle o^{\gamma}a_{\gamma}+\langle o\lambda\rangle\iota^{\gamma}a_{\gamma}=\mathcal{O}\big(\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\big)

(5.8)

with
a
finite. Certainly this requires that
\iota^{\gamma}a_{\gamma}=\mathcal{O}(\langle\lambda\iota\rangle)
. Inverting equation (
5.6

a^{\prime}_{\alpha}=a_{\alpha}+\frac{1-e^{V_{\epsilon}}}{V_{\epsilon}}(\tilde{\partial}_{\alpha}\epsilon+\{a_{\alpha},\epsilon\})\,.

(5.9)

In order to impose the boundary condition it will be useful to keep track of not merely the piece of order
\langle\lambda\iota\rangle
in
\epsilon
, but also the quadratic term. We write

\epsilon=\frac{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle}{\langle o\lambda\rangle}\phi+\frac{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}}{\langle o\lambda\rangle^{2}}\chi+\mathcal{O}\big(\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{3}\big)\,.

(5.10)

Then

\displaystyle\iota^{\alpha}a^{\prime}_{\alpha}=\iota^{\alpha}a_{\alpha}-\frac{2\iota^{\alpha}\lambda^{\beta}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle}\partial_{\alpha\beta}\epsilon-\frac{\iota^{\alpha}\lambda^{\beta}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle}\{\epsilon,\partial_{\alpha\beta}\epsilon\}+\mathcal{O}\big(\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=\iota^{\alpha}a_{\alpha}-2\iota^{\alpha}\iota^{\beta}\partial_{\alpha\beta}\phi-\frac{2\langle\lambda\iota\rangle}{\langle o\lambda\rangle}o^{\alpha}\iota^{\beta}\partial_{\alpha\beta}\phi-\frac{2\langle\lambda\iota\rangle}{\langle o\lambda\rangle}\iota^{\alpha}\iota^{\beta}\partial_{\alpha\beta}\chi-\frac{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle}{\langle o\lambda\rangle}\{\phi,\iota^{\alpha}\iota^{\beta}\partial_{\alpha\beta}\phi\}+\mathcal{O}\big(\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\big)

\displaystyle o^{\alpha}a^{\prime}_{\alpha}=o^{\alpha}a_{\alpha}-\frac{o^{\alpha}\lambda^{\beta}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle}\partial_{\alpha\beta}\epsilon+\frac{\langle o\lambda\rangle\iota^{\alpha}\lambda^{\beta}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}}\partial_{\alpha\beta}\epsilon+\frac{\langle o\lambda\rangle\iota^{\alpha}\lambda^{\beta}}{2\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}}\{\epsilon,\partial_{\alpha\beta}\epsilon\}+\mathcal{O}\big(\langle\lambda\iota\rangle\big)

\displaystyle 2\langle\lambda\iota\rangle o^{\gamma}a_{\gamma}^{\prime}=2\langle\lambda\iota\rangle o^{\gamma}a_{\gamma}+\langle o\lambda\rangle\iota^{\gamma}a_{\gamma}+2\langle o\lambda\rangle\iota^{\alpha}\iota^{\beta}\partial_{\alpha\beta}\phi-2\langle\lambda\iota\rangle o^{\alpha}\iota^{\beta}\partial_{\alpha\beta}\phi+\mathcal{O}\big(\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\big(\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\big)\,,

so we should take

o^{\alpha}a^{\prime}_{\alpha}=\frac{\langle o\lambda\rangle}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle}\iota^{\alpha}\iota^{\beta}\partial_{\alpha\beta}\phi-o^{\alpha}\iota^{\beta}\partial_{\alpha\beta}\phi\,.

(5.14)

(5.15)

The Lax can be recovered as the space-time components of the connection
\tilde{\mathrm{d}}+\{a,~\,\}
in the gauge
a_{0}=0

L^{\prime}_{\alpha}=\tilde{\partial}_{\alpha}+\{a_{\alpha}^{\prime},~\,\}=P_{\alpha}^{~\,\beta}\frac{\lambda^{\gamma}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle}\partial_{\beta\gamma}+\iota_{\alpha}u\,\partial_{1}+P_{\alpha}^{~\,\beta}\iota^{\gamma}\partial_{\beta\gamma}u\,\partial_{0}\,.

(5.16)

where we have introduced

and so generate the same distribution. The Lax operators obtained in equation (

\Omega

L^{\prime}_{0}=\partial_{-}-z^{2}\partial_{+}+u\partial_{+}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\partial_{y}u\partial_{z}-z\partial_{+}u\partial_{z}\,,\qquad L^{\prime}_{1}=2\Big(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\partial_{y}+z\partial_{+}+\partial_{+}u\partial_{z}\Big)\,.

(5.17)

The commutator of these two operators is

[L^{\prime}_{0},L^{\prime}_{1}]\sim 2\Big(\partial_{+}\partial_{-}u-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{y}^{2}u+\partial_{+}(u\partial_{+}u)\Big)\partial_{z}\,,

(5.18)

vanishing precisely on the support of the dKP equation. This justifies our choice of boundary conditions in subsection

5.2
Recovering the KP Lax

The argument of the previous subsection carries over the the case of the KP equation with surprisingly little difficulty.

In this case we have non-commutative 5d Chern-Simons theory on correspondence space. An infinitesimal gauge transformation with parameter
\epsilon\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{PS})
(where as usual we suppress the dependence on the formal variable
\mathfrak{q}
) acts by
a\mapsto\tilde{\mathrm{d}}\epsilon+[a,\epsilon]_{\star}

a\mapsto a+\frac{1-e^{-[\epsilon,~\,]_{\star}}}{[\epsilon,~\,]_{\star}}(\tilde{\mathrm{D}}\epsilon+[a,\epsilon]_{\star})\,.

We can understand this in a more intuitive way by forming the finite gauge parameter

f_{\epsilon}=\exp_{\star}\bigg(\frac{\epsilon}{\mathfrak{q}}\bigg)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m!\mathfrak{q}^{m}}\underbrace{\epsilon\star\dots\star\epsilon}_{m~\text{times}}\,.

(5.20)

Note that at any given order in
\mathfrak{q}
(which can now be negative) infinitely many terms can contribute; however at any fixed power of
\epsilon
and given order in
\mathfrak{q}
there are only finitely many terms. We will assume the sums over increasing powers of
\epsilon
converge. Then non-commutative gauge transformations take the more familiar form

\tilde{\mathrm{D}}+\frac{1}{\mathfrak{q}}a\mapsto f_{-\epsilon}\star\tilde{\mathrm{D}}f_{\epsilon}+\frac{1}{\mathfrak{q}}f_{-\epsilon}\star a\star f_{\epsilon}\,.

(5.21)

Here

should be interpreted as the ‘inverse’ of
f_{\epsilon}
, obeying
f_{-\epsilon}\star f_{\epsilon}=f_{\epsilon}\star f_{-\epsilon}=1
. Although the right hand side of (
5.21
) seems to be terribly singular in
\mathfrak{q}
, applying the formula for the derivative of the exponential recovers the transformation (
5.19
).

10

Pulling back

Having fixed the gauge, we next impose the equations of motion in the fibre directions. This is achieved by writing

Reducing the Action

6
Amplitudes in KP Theory

A general argument of Costello
[
47
]
shows that any local holomorphic theory on twistor space should correspond to an integrable theory on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. One consequence of this is that all amplitudes should vanish (at least for general kinematics). This is guaranteed Coleman-Mandula theorem: the infinitely many conserved charges of an integrable theory necessitate a perturbatively trivial S-matrix.

The arguments of
[
47
]
can also be applied to mixed topological-holomorphic theory on
\mathbb{PS}
. In particular, scalar scattering states
\phi_{k}(x)=\exp(\mathrm{i}k\cdot x)
in
\mathbb{R}^{1,2}
can be realised via the Penrose transform by cohomology representatives

(6.1)

where
k_{\alpha\beta}=\omega\kappa_{\alpha}\kappa_{\beta}
. Pulling back these states to
\mathbb{PS}
and dressing them with the divisor generates solutions to the linearised equations of motion of non-commutative and Poisson-Chern-Simons theory:

a_{k}=\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}e^{\mathrm{i}\omega x^{\alpha\beta}\lambda_{\alpha}\lambda_{\beta}/\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}}\bar{\delta}_{\lambda\sim\kappa}\,.

(6.2)

Crucially such states are localised at points on the
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
base of
{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}
. When computing amplitudes in a mixed topological-holomorphic theory it’s natural to fix the gauge using a metric. In our case we use a linear combination of the flat metric
\eta
on
\mathbb{R}^{1,2}
and the Fubini-Study metric on the
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
fibres. Any gauge invariant quantity will be independent of the metric, which includes the S-matrix. We can therefore choose to scale up the Fubini-Study metric on
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
, suppressing any interactions between states supported at different values of
\lambda

\lambda\sim\kappa_{i}
, and so in this limit any interactions between them can be made arbitrarily weak. In this way we see that the S-matrix is trivial.

At the classical level integrability of the dKP and KP equations is well established, and so it’s natural to expect vanishing of all tree level amplitudes. We verify explicitly that this expectation is borne out by evaluating the four-point tree amplitude in both theories. In section
6.2
we extend this result to
n
-points by on-shell recursion.

However, the minitwistor description buys us more than the vanishing of the trees: Since
\text{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{PS}=5
, our non-commutative Chern-Simons theory
S_{\star}[a]
cannot have any perturbative anomaly at one-loop. Thus, at one-loop, the corresponding space-time theory
S_{\text{KP}}[\phi]
should remain integrable and still have no amplitudes. We leave an explicit verification of this at higher loop order to future work.

Let us denote the
n
-particle tree amplitude in momentum space as

\mathcal{A}^{n}_{\text{(d)KP}}(k_{i})=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}\Big)A^{n}_{\text{(d)KP}}(k_{i})\,.

(6.3)

In this section, we will show explicitly that
\mathcal{A}^{4}_{\text{(d)KP}}(k_{i})=0
and then in section
7

obey an on-shell recursion relation implying
\mathcal{A}^{n}_{\text{(d)KP}}(k_{i})=0
inductively for all
n\geq 4
.

6.2

In section

Therefore

k

Since we still can’t have any collinear singularities and now there also can’t be any

To summarise: Linearised solutions to the classical equations of motion of the

theory are identified with states in the vertex algebra
\mathcal{C}
. Of particular interest are complexified momentum eigenstates, which correspond to so-called ‘hard’ generators. The operator products of these hard generators are captured by collinear splitting in space-time. The conformal blocks of
\mathcal{C}
, i.e., the possible ways of defining correlators compatible with operator products, are in bijection with local operators of the minitwistorial theory. Finally, amplitudes of the
3d
theory with a local operator inserted at a point (form factors) equate to correlators of hard generators in the corresponding conformal block. That collinear singularities of form factors are universal, i.e., independent of the inserted local operator, is a consequence of the vanishing of all space-time amplitudes. These identifications are listed in Table
1
for convenience. In this work we’ve only engineered the correspondence space uplift of (d)KP theory at tree level, and so we can only trust a classical version of the correspondence; however, we expect that at the very least a one-loop version persists.

Let’s briefly sketch where this correspondence comes from, concentrating on the case of dKP for simplicity. The linearised equations of motion read

(2\partial_{+}\partial_{-}-\partial_{y}^{2})\phi=0

(7.1)

with solution
\phi_{k}(x)=\exp(\mathrm{i}k\cdot x)
for
k^{2}=2k_{+}k_{-}-k_{y}^{2}=0
. (We choose not to normalise as we did in section
6
.) The massless condition is solved by
k_{\alpha\beta}=\omega\kappa_{\alpha}\kappa_{\beta}
with the redundancy
(\omega,\kappa_{\alpha})\sim(t^{-2}\omega,t\kappa_{\alpha})
for
t\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. (At
\omega=0
the
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
factor shrinks to a point, so complexified null momenta belong to
\mathbb{C}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.) We will sometimes write
\phi_{k}(x)=\phi^{\omega}(x;\kappa)
to make this parametrisation explicit. This differs from our convention in the previous section, where we absorbed the scale
\omega
into
\kappa
which was treated as an inhomogeneous variable. We should view
\omega\langle\kappa\iota\rangle^{2}
as the energy of the wave, and
\kappa
as a point on the ‘celestial’ sphere. (For
\phi_{k}(x)
to be real we must have
\kappa\sim\bar{\kappa}
so that this celestial sphere is an
S^{1}
as expected for
\mathbb{R}^{1,2}
. The sign of
\omega
determines whether the momentum is past- or future-pointing.) Expanding in powers of
\omega
is a soft expansion

\phi_{k}(x)=\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\mathrm{i}\omega)^{s-1}}{(s-1)!}\phi_{s}(x;\kappa)\,.

(7.2)

where
\phi_{s}(x;\kappa)=(x^{\alpha\beta}\kappa_{\alpha}\kappa_{\beta})^{s-1}
is a ‘soft’ state.

The momentum eigenstates
\phi^{\omega}(x;\kappa)
lift via the Penrose transform to states on correspondence space which pull-back from Dolbeault cohomology representatives on
{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}
. Explicitly they take the form

a^{\omega}(x,\lambda;\kappa)=\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}e^{\mathrm{i}\omega x^{\alpha\beta}\lambda_{\alpha}\lambda_{\beta}\langle\kappa\iota\rangle^{2}/\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}}\bar{\delta}_{\lambda\sim\kappa}\,.

(7.3)

The precise correspondence between the space-time and twistor data is

\phi^{\omega}(x;\kappa)=\int_{\mathbb{CP}^{1}_{x}}\frac{\langle\lambda\mathrm{d}\lambda\rangle}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}}\,a^{\omega}(x,\lambda;\kappa)\,.

(7.4)

Amplitudes in the
3d
theory vanish, since on
\mathbb{PS}
these states cannot talk to one another: Feynman diagrams can be evaluated in a gauge where they are arbitrarily far apart.

However, suppose we insert a local operator at the origin
x=0
, denoted by
\mathcal{O}(0)
. The results of
[
47
]
show that it lifts to a compact surface defect wrapping the minitwistor line
\mathbb{CP}^{1}_{x=0}
. This defect is the correlation function of a universal vertex algebra
\mathcal{C}
: the Koszul dual to the algebra of bulk local operators restricted to the defect. The states of
\mathcal{C}
are denoted
v_{s}(\lambda)
for
s\geq 1
and have conformal dimension
1-s
; they couple to the bulk gauge theory via

I[a,v_{s}]=\sum_{s\geq 1}\int_{\mathbb{CP}^{1}_{x=0}}\frac{\langle\lambda\mathrm{d}\lambda\rangle}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2s}}\,\frac{v_{s}(\lambda)\partial_{1}^{s-1}a}{(s-1)!}\,.

(7.5)

With these conventions the state
v_{s}(\lambda)
must vanish to order
\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2(s-1)}
at
\lambda\sim\iota
. The operator
\mathcal{O}
is identified with conformal block of
\mathcal{C}
, denoted
\langle\dots\rangle_{\mathcal{O}}
, and we have

\mathcal{O}(0)=\langle\exp{I[a,v_{s}]}\rangle_{\mathcal{O}}\,.

(7.6)

The form factor of
\mathcal{O}(0)
can be computed on correspondence space using the states
a^{\omega}(x,\lambda;\kappa)
. These only talk to one another through the defect, where they pick out ‘hard generators’

I[a^{\omega}(x,\lambda;\kappa),v_{s}]=v(\kappa,\omega)=\sum_{s\geq 1}\frac{(\mathrm{i}\omega)^{s-1}}{(s-1)!}v_{s}(\kappa)\,.

(7.7)

Comparing equations (
7.2
) and (
7.7
) we can see that the operators
v_{s}(\kappa)
can be identified with soft states
\phi_{s}(x;\kappa)
. (In fact this is the motivation for putting the factor of
1/\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2s}
in the measure.)

The form factor
F_{\mathcal{O}}(k_{i})
equates to a correlation function of hard generators in the conformal block
\langle\dots\rangle_{\mathcal{O}}
.

F_{\mathcal{O}}(k_{i})=\langle v(\kappa_{1},\omega_{1})\dots v(\kappa_{n},\omega_{n})\rangle_{\mathcal{O}}\,.

(7.8)

In order for this equality to hold the collinear singularities of the form factor as
\kappa_{i},\kappa_{j}
coincide must agree with the operator product of hard generators. But the operator products of the
v_{s}(\lambda)
are also fixed by gauge invariance of the bulk-defect coupling on correspondence space. In the next subsection we will see how these can be determined straightforwardly.

7.2
Koszul Duality for Poisson-Chern-Simons

Here we determine the operator products of the vertex algebra
\mathcal{C}
corresponding to dKP theory directly on correspondence space. This is achieved by demanding gauge invariance of the bulk-defect coupling
\exp{I[a,v_{s}]}
.

At tree level the variation of the bi-local term
\tfrac{1}{2}I[a,v_{s}]^{2}
under the linear part of an infinitesimal gauge transformation cancels against the non-linear variation of the local term
I[a,v_{s}]
. The former picks out the singular part of the operator product of states
v_{s}(\lambda)
, whereas the latter is determined by the cubic vertex in Poisson-Chern-Simons theory.

At tree level rather than computing the operator products its easier to determine the Lie brackets of the
v_{s}(\lambda)
modes. These can be extracted from the bulk-defect coupling using states

a_{s,m}=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\frac{\langle o\lambda\rangle^{s-1+m}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{s-1+m}}\bigg(\frac{x^{\alpha\beta}\lambda_{\alpha}\lambda_{\beta}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}}\bigg)^{s-1}\tilde{\mathrm{d}}\Theta

(7.9)

for
\Theta
a step function taking the value
1
for
|z|=|\langle o\lambda\rangle/\langle\lambda\iota\rangle|\leq 1
and
0
for
|z|=|\langle o\lambda\rangle/\langle\lambda\iota\rangle|>1
. In the Čech language these are represented by meromorphic (
\tilde{\mathrm{d}}
-closed) functions

\check{a}_{s,m}=\frac{\langle o\lambda\rangle^{s-1+m}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{s-1+m}}\bigg(\frac{x^{\alpha\beta}\lambda_{\alpha}\lambda_{\beta}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}}\bigg)^{s-1}

(7.10)

on the overlap of the north and south patches of the
\lambda
-sphere (
\lambda\not\sim\iota
and
\lambda\not\sim o
respectively). Plugging the states (
7.9
) into the bulk-defect coupling yields

\tilde{v}_{s,m}=I[a_{s,m},v_{s^{\prime}}]=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\oint_{S^{1}}\frac{\langle\lambda\mathrm{d}\lambda\rangle}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2s}}\,\frac{\langle o\lambda\rangle^{s-1+m}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{s-1+m}}v_{s}(\lambda)\,.

(7.11)

Since the cubic vertex of Poisson-Chern-Simons theory is determined by the Poisson bracket, the Lie algebra obeyed by the modes
\tilde{v}_{s,m}
is the Poisson algebra of Čech representatives (
7.10
). We immediately find that

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\{\check{a}_{s_{1},m},\check{a}_{s_{2},n}\}=\partial_{0}\check{a}_{s_{1},m}\partial_{1}\check{a}_{s_{2},n}-\partial_{1}\check{a}_{s_{1},n}\partial_{0}\check{a}_{s_{2},m}

(7.12)

\displaystyle=\big(m(s_{2}-1)-n(s_{1}-1)\big)\check{a}_{s_{1}+s_{2}-2,m+n}\,,

so
[\tilde{v}_{s_{1},m},\tilde{v}_{s_{2},n}]=\big(m(s_{2}-1)-n(s_{1}-1)\big)\tilde{v}_{s_{1}+s_{2}-2,m+n}
. This is the mode algebra of
w_{1+\infty}
(without central extension).
14
14

14

Often mathematicians refer to the universal enveloping algebra of this Lie algebra as the ‘mode algbera’.
Note that the
\{\tilde{v}_{2,m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}
generate a Witt subalgebra.

We’ve performed a sleight of hand here - the mode expansion (
7.11
) does not take the standard form for a field of conformal dimension
1-s
; instead, this is the natural expansion for
\tilde{v}_{s}(\lambda)=\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2-4s}v_{s}(\lambda)
of conformal dimension
s
. It’s really these shifted fields
\tilde{v}_{s}(\lambda)
whose operator products obey the
w_{1+\infty}
vertex algebra. The price for performing this shift is that the vacuum module at
\lambda\sim\iota
has been modified.

The vacuum module
|0\rangle_{\lambda\sim o}
at
\lambda\sim o
, or equivalently
z=0
, is the standard one for
w_{1+\infty}
. It can be read off from (
7.11
) by noting that
\tilde{v}_{s,m}|0\rangle_{\lambda\sim o}=0
for
m\geq 1-s
, since in this case the contour integral can be contracted to a point in the northern hemisphere. Let’s remind ourselves why this defines a module. For
m\geq 1-s_{1}
,
n\geq 1-s_{2}
we require that

[\tilde{v}_{s_{1},m},\tilde{v}_{s_{2},n}]|0\rangle_{\lambda\sim o}=\big(m(s_{2}-1)-n(s_{1}-1)\big)\tilde{v}_{s_{1}+s_{2}-2,m+n}|0\rangle_{\lambda\sim o}=0\,.

(7.13)

Now
\tilde{v}_{s_{1}+s_{2}-2,m+n}|0\rangle_{\lambda\sim o}=0
for
m+n\geq 1-(s_{1}+s_{2}-2)
. This leaves the case
m+n=2-s_{1}-s_{2}
, for which
m=1-s_{1}
and
n=1-s_{2}
so that the structure constant
m(s_{2}-1)-n(s_{1}-1)=0
. The standard
w_{1+\infty}
vacuum module
|0\rangle_{\lambda\sim\iota}
at
\lambda\sim\iota
similarly obeys
\tilde{v}_{s,m}|0\rangle_{\lambda\sim\iota}
for
m\leq s-1
. The global symmetries of
w_{1+\infty}
, i.e., the Lie subalgebra of modes preserving both vacua, is the wedge subalgebra generated by
\{\tilde{v}_{s,m}\}_{|m|\leq s-1}
.

15

15

This is not the wedge subalgebra appearing in the context of celestial holography, which has extra generators corresponding to spin representations of the global
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
conformal symmetry.
In particular we find a global
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
symmetry generated by
j_{0}=\tilde{v}_{1,0}
and a
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
global conformal symmetry generated by
L_{-1}=\tilde{v}_{2,-1}
,
L_{0}=\tilde{v}_{2,0}
,
L_{1}=\tilde{v}_{2,1}
.

Let’s write
|\text{dKP}\rangle_{\lambda\sim\iota}
for the modified vacuum at
\lambda\sim\iota
in the case of dKP. Recalling that the boundary condition on
v_{s}(\lambda)
requires that it should vanish to order
2(s-1)
at
\lambda\sim\iota
, we can see from equation (
7.11
) that
\tilde{v}_{s,m}|\text{dKP}\rangle_{\lambda\sim\iota}=0
for
m\leq-1-s
by contracting the contour in the southern hemisphere.
This defines a consistent vertex algebra module, since for any
s_{1},s_{2}
and
m\leq-1-s_{1},n\leq-1-s_{2}
we have
m+n\leq-2-s_{1}-s_{2}<-1-(s_{1}+s_{2}-2)
. The module
|\text{dKP}\rangle_{\lambda\sim\iota}
breaks all of the global symmetry which was present in the
|0\rangle_{\lambda\sim\iota}
vacuum. Indeed any modes
\tilde{v}_{s,m}
preserving both vacua would need to have
1-s\leq m\leq-1-s
.

This shift of the vacuum module is crucial for the validity of the
3d/2d
correspondence outlined in subsection
7.1
. Indeed, the simplest local operator of dKP theory is
\phi
itself. This should correspond to a conformal block of the vertex algebra
\mathcal{C}
, or equivalently a family of
w_{1+\infty}
correlators compatible with the operator product but in which the states
v_{s}(\lambda)
may have poles of order
\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{-2s}
. This family of correlators is seeded by

\langle\tilde{v}_{1}(\lambda)\rangle_{\phi(0)}=\frac{1}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}}

(7.14)

with all other one-point functions set to zero. (Here by seeded we mean that all other correlators are determined by recursively applying the OPE until one lands on this one-point function.) In terms of the hard generators
v(\kappa;\omega)
this is
\langle v(\kappa,\omega)\rangle_{\phi(0)}=1=\phi_{k}(0)
. Similarly the operator
u=\partial_{+}\phi
is identified with the block

\langle\tilde{v}_{2}(\lambda)\rangle_{u(0)}=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{4}}

(7.15)

with all other one-point functions set to zero. This yields

\langle v(\kappa,\omega)\rangle_{u(0)}=\mathrm{i}\omega\langle\kappa\iota\rangle^{2}=\partial_{+}\phi_{k}(0)\,.

(7.16)

Translation invariance ensures that the form factor of
\mathcal{O}(x)
can be obtained from
\mathcal{O}(0)
by dressing with
e^{iK\cdot x}
for
K
the total momentum of all hard scattering states. Hence

\langle v(\kappa,\omega)\rangle_{u(x)}=\mathrm{i}\omega\langle\kappa\iota\rangle^{2}e^{\mathrm{i}k\cdot x}=\partial_{+}\phi_{k}(x)\,.

(7.17)

Notice that
w_{1+\infty}
with its usual vacua has no non-trivial conformal blocks, so the correspondence would fail immediately.

Collinear Splitting in dKP

In this subsection we compute the tree splitting function, or equivalently the singular part of the perturbiner, in dKP. It is computed as the partially off-shell Feynman diagram illustrated in Figure
1
. From the arguments of subsection
7.1
we expect to recover the mode algebra directly recovered from correspondence space.

The tree splitting function in dKP theory is determined by the singular part of the above diagram as
\langle 12\rangle\to 0
.

Together with the states
\phi_{k}(x)
we recall the propagator
-\mathrm{i}/k^{2}
and vertex
k_{+}^{3}/3
. The perturbiner is

\mathcal{S}_{\text{dKP}}^{\omega_{1},\omega_{2}}(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})=-\frac{2\mathrm{i}k_{1+}k_{2+}(k_{1}+k_{2})_{+}}{(k_{1}+k_{2})^{2}}e^{\mathrm{i}(k_{1}+k_{2})\cdot x}\,.

(7.18)

Rewriting in terms of energy and spinor-helicity variables this is

\mathcal{S}_{\text{dKP}}^{\omega_{1},\omega_{2}}(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})=-\frac{\mathrm{i}\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2}(\omega_{1}\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2}+\omega_{2}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2})}{\langle 12\rangle^{2}}e^{\mathrm{i}(k_{1}+k_{2})\cdot x}\,.

(7.19)

This is an off-shell solution, but the singular part of this expression in the collinear limit
\langle 12\rangle\to 0
is expressible in terms of on-shell states. To see why, we exploit the Schouten identity to write

\kappa_{1}=\frac{\langle 1\iota\rangle}{\langle 2\iota\rangle}\kappa_{2}-\frac{\langle 12\rangle}{\langle 2\iota\rangle}\iota\,.

(7.20)

This allows us to expand the perturbiner (
7.19
) around
\kappa_{1}\sim\kappa_{2}
to get

e^{\mathrm{i}(k_{1}+k_{2})\cdot x}=\bigg(1-2\mathrm{i}\frac{\langle 12\rangle\langle 1\iota\rangle}{\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2}}\omega_{1}x^{\alpha\beta}\kappa_{2\alpha}\iota_{\beta}+\mathcal{O}(\langle 12\rangle^{2})\bigg)e^{\mathrm{i}K_{+}x^{\alpha\beta}\kappa_{2\alpha}\kappa_{2\beta}/\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2}}

(7.21)

where

. Whilst the leading term in
\langle 12\rangle

\bigg(1-\langle 12\rangle\frac{\omega_{1}\langle 1\iota\rangle}{K_{+}}\iota^{\alpha}\partial_{\kappa_{2}^{\alpha}}+\mathcal{O}\big(\langle 12\rangle^{2}\big)\bigg)\phi^{K_{+}/\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2}}(x;\kappa_{2})\,.

(7.22)

Substituting into equation (
7.19
) yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\text{dKP}}^{\omega_{1},\omega_{2}}(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})

(7.23)

\displaystyle\sim-\mathrm{i}\frac{\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2}(\omega_{1}\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2}+\omega_{2}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2})}{\langle 12\rangle^{2}}\phi^{K_{+}/\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2}}(x;\kappa_{2})+\mathrm{i}\frac{\langle 1\iota\rangle^{3}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2}\omega_{1}}{\langle 12\rangle}\iota^{\alpha}\partial_{\kappa_{2}^{\alpha}}\phi^{K_{+}/\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2}}(x;\kappa_{2})\,.

Here the symbol
\sim
indicates that we work modulo regular terms in
\langle 12\rangle
. This is currently written in terms of hard scattering states - expanding in powers of
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}

7.4
Koszul Duality for Non-Commutative Chern-Simons

Now let’s understand the consequences of switching on the dispersive term in KP. The linearised equation of motion for the potential
\phi
is more complicated, it reads

(2\partial_{+}\partial_{-}-\partial_{y}^{2})\phi=\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{6}\partial_{+}^{4}\phi

(7.30)

with plane wave solutions
\Phi_{k}(x)=\exp(\mathrm{i}k\cdot x)
for
2k_{+}k_{-}-k_{y}^{2}+\mathfrak{q}^{2}k_{+}^{4}/6=0
. We can parametrise solutions to this dispersion relation by the same data as in the dispersionless case: by
(\omega,\kappa_{\alpha})\sim(t^{-2}\omega,t\kappa_{\alpha})
.

k_{\alpha\beta}=\omega\kappa_{\alpha}\kappa_{\beta}-\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{12}\omega^{3}\langle\kappa\iota\rangle^{6}\iota_{\alpha}\iota_{\beta}\,.

(7.31)

We can then perform a soft expansion as above

\Phi^{\omega}(x;\kappa)=\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\mathrm{i}\omega)^{s-1}}{(s-1)!}\Phi_{s}(x;\kappa)

(7.32)

where the soft states
\Phi_{s}(x;\kappa)
are no longer homogeneous polynomials in
x
.

We can lift these states to correspondence space, although from the discussion in subsections
4.3
and
4.4
there are different isomorphic presentations of the defining dga on
\mathbb{PS}
. Splitting the bivector holomorphically to obtain the presentation
(\widetilde{\Omega}_{\mathbb{PS}},\tilde{\mathrm{d}},\ast)
we can continue to use the correspondence space representatives

a^{\omega}(x,\lambda;\kappa)=\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}e^{\mathrm{i}\omega x^{\alpha\beta}\lambda_{\alpha}\lambda_{\beta}\langle\kappa\iota\rangle^{2}/\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}}\bar{\delta}_{\lambda\sim\kappa}\,.

(7.33)

To get their counterparts in the presentation
(\widetilde{\Omega}_{\mathbb{PS}},\tilde{\mathrm{D}},\star)
we simply apply the isomorphism
\psi=\exp(\mathfrak{q}^{2}x^{-}\partial_{1}^{3}/12)
to get

\psi\big(a^{\omega}(x,\lambda;\kappa)\big)=\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\exp\mathrm{i}\omega\langle\kappa\iota\rangle^{2}x^{\alpha\beta}\bigg(\frac{\lambda_{\alpha}\lambda_{\beta}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}}-\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{12}\omega^{2}\langle\kappa\iota\rangle^{4}\iota_{\alpha}\iota_{\beta}\bigg)\bar{\delta}_{\lambda\sim\kappa}\,.

(7.34)

The linear Penrose transform is then

\Phi^{\omega}(x;\kappa)=\int_{\mathbb{CP}^{1}_{x}}\frac{\langle\lambda\mathrm{d}\lambda\rangle}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}}\,\psi\big(a^{\omega}(x,\lambda;\kappa)\big)\,.

(7.35)

We can introduce a universal vertex algebra
\mathcal{C}
living on
\mathbb{CP}^{1}_{x=0}
as in subsection
7.1
. This is simplest using the presentation
(\widetilde{\Omega}_{\mathbb{PS}},\tilde{\mathrm{d}},\ast)
. Plugging the correspondence space avatars of the soft modes
\Phi_{s}(x;\kappa)
into the bulk-defect coupling yields states
\mathcal{V}_{s}(\kappa)
of conformal dimension
1-s
for
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
. Again, these vanish to order
\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2(s-1)}
at
\lambda\sim\iota
.

It is easiest to compute the mode algebra of
\mathcal{C}
directly on correspondence space. Since
\tilde{\mathrm{d}}
is undeformed we can continue to use the Čech representatives (
7.10
) for the modes
a_{s,m}
. Recall that the star product is

f\ast g=c\circ\exp\frac{\mathfrak{q}}{2}\big(v_{0}\otimes\partial_{1}-\partial_{1}\otimes v_{0}\big)(f\otimes g)

(7.36)

where
v_{0}=\partial_{0}+s\partial_{1}
for
s=-2x^{\alpha\beta}\iota_{\alpha}\lambda_{\beta}/\langle\lambda\iota\rangle
. The vector field
v_{0}
has the property
v_{0}(x^{\alpha\beta}\lambda_{\alpha}\lambda_{\beta})=0
and
v_{0}\langle o\lambda\rangle=\langle\lambda\iota\rangle
making it straightforward to compute

\displaystyle

\displaystyle[\check{a}_{s_{1},m},\check{a}_{s_{2},n}]_{\ast}=\frac{1}{\mathfrak{q}}\big(\check{a}_{s_{1},m}\ast\check{a}_{s_{2},n}-\check{a}_{s_{2},n}\ast\check{a}_{s_{1},m}\big)=\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2\ell}}{(2\ell+1)!2^{2\ell}}

(7.37)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{2\ell+1}(-)^{k}\binom{2\ell+1}{k}[s_{1}-1+m]_{2\ell+1-k}[s_{2}-1]_{2\ell+1-k}[s_{2}-1+n]_{k}[s_{1}-1]_{k}\check{a}_{s_{1}+s_{2}-2-2\ell,m+n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2\ell}}{(2\ell+1)!2^{2\ell}}N^{s_{1},s_{2}}_{2\ell}(m,n)\check{a}_{s_{1}+s_{2}-2-2\ell,m+n}

where
[a]_{b}=a(a-1)\dots(a-(b-1))

denotes the descending factorial and we’ve introduced the structure constants

\displaystyle

\displaystyle N^{s_{1},s_{2}}_{\ell}(m,n)=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell+1}(-)^{k}\binom{\ell+1}{k}[s_{1}-1]_{k}[s_{2}-1]_{\ell+1-k}[s_{1}-1+m]_{\ell+1-k}[s_{2}-1+n]_{k}\,.

(7.38)

Notice that if
\ell+1>s_{1}+s_{2}-2
in the above then for every term in the sum either
k>s_{1}-1
or
\ell+1-k>s_{2}-1
, so that either
[s_{1}-1]_{k}=0
or
[s_{2}-1]_{\ell+1-k}=0
. As a result the sum over
\ell
in equation (
7.37
) truncates and the bracket closes. The Lie algebra (
7.37
) is in fact isomorphic to the mode algebra of
W_{1+\infty}
.

To see why, notice that in effect we’re quantizing the Poisson algebra of regular functions on
\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times\mathbb{C}
equipped with the bivector
\partial_{z}\vee\partial_{v}
. The non-commutative product is defined by applying the Moyal formula to this bivector. Let’s write
\mathscr{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times\mathbb{C})
for this associative algebra. The commutator induces a Lie algebra structure on
\mathscr{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times\mathbb{C})
which is isomorphic to (
7.37
). The Moyal product on
\mathscr{O}_{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times\mathbb{C})
is equivariant under the
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
action simultaneously scaling
\mathfrak{q}
and
v
. Absorbing the parameter
\mathfrak{q}
into
v
yields
\mathscr{O}_{1}(\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times\mathbb{C})
which is isomorphic to
\mathrm{Diff}(\mathbb{C}^{\times})
, the algebra of holomorphic differential operators on
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Here we’re identifying
v\leftrightarrow-\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}z
. The commutator of differential operators endows
\mathrm{Diff}(\mathbb{C}^{\times})
with a Lie algebra structure isomorphic to the mode algebra of
W_{1+\infty}

[
50
]
.

17

The bivector
\partial_{z}\vee\partial_{v}
differs from the standard bivector
z\partial_{z}\vee\partial_{v}

7.60
) differ from those of

. But the Moyal formula applied to the two bivectors yields isomorphic associative algebras.

Now that we’ve determined the Lie algebra of the modes

\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{s,m}=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\oint_{S^{1}}\frac{\langle\lambda\mathrm{d}\lambda\rangle}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2s}}\,\frac{\langle o\lambda\rangle^{s-1+m}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{s-1+m}}\mathcal{V}_{s}(\lambda)

(7.39)

we should take care to keep track of the vacuum modules. The
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{s,m}
are the modes of the states
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{s}(\lambda)=\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2-4s}\mathcal{V}_{s}(\lambda)
, and these have vacuum module
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{s,m}|\text{KP}\rangle_{\lambda\sim\iota}=0
for
m\leq-1-s
at
\lambda\sim\iota
. (It’s straightforward to verify that this is compatible with the operator product.) However, the vacuum module at
\lambda\sim o
is more complicated. We expect this to be the familiar vacuum module of the
W_{1+\infty}
vertex algebra, but our generators
\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{s}(\lambda)
are not standard. (See

vacuum module on correspondence space.

7.5
Collinear Splitting in KP

Finally we rederive the
W_{1+\infty}
vertex algebra (
7.37
) from collinear splitting in KP with dispersion. Recall that scattering states
\Phi_{k}(x)=\exp(\mathrm{i}k\cdot x)
are labelled by
(\omega,\kappa)\sim(t^{-2}\omega,t\kappa)
in terms of which the momentum is

k_{\alpha\beta}(\omega,\kappa)=\omega\kappa_{\alpha}\kappa_{\beta}-\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{12}\omega^{3}\langle\kappa\iota\rangle^{6}\iota_{\alpha}\iota_{\beta}\,.

(7.40)

The vertex
k_{+}^{3}/3
is unmodified, but the propagator is changed to
-\mathrm{i}/(k^{2}+\tfrac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{6}k_{+}^{4})
. The perturbiner evaluates to

\mathcal{S}_{\text{KP}}^{\omega_{1},\omega_{2}}(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})=-\frac{2\mathrm{i}k_{1+}k_{2+}(k_{1}+k_{2})_{+}}{(k_{1}+k_{2})^{2}+\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{6}(k_{1}+k_{2})_{+}^{4}}e^{\mathrm{i}(k_{1}+k_{2})\cdot x}\,.

(7.41)

Exploiting the identity (
6.12
) we can rewrite this in terms of spinor helicity variables to obtain

\mathcal{S}_{\text{KP}}^{\omega_{1},\omega_{2}}(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})=-\frac{\mathrm{i}\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2}(\omega_{1}\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2}+\omega_{2}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2})}{\langle 12\rangle^{2}+\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{4}\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2}(\omega_{1}\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2}+\omega_{2}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2})^{2}}e^{\mathrm{i}(k_{1}+k_{2})\cdot x}\,.

(7.42)

We need to rewrite the singular part of the exponential
\exp(\mathrm{i}(k_{1}+k_{2})\cdot x)
in terms of on-shell states. In order to do so we exploit the identity

\displaystyle

\displaystyle(k_{1}+k_{2})_{\alpha\beta}=\omega_{1}\kappa_{1\alpha}\kappa_{1\beta}+\omega_{2}\kappa_{2\alpha}\kappa_{2\beta}-\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{12}(\omega_{1}^{3}\langle 1\iota\rangle^{6}+\omega_{2}^{3}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{6})\iota_{\alpha}\iota_{\beta}

(7.43)

\displaystyle=k_{\alpha\beta}\bigg(\frac{\omega_{1}\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2}+\omega_{2}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2}}{\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2}},\kappa_{2}\bigg)-2\omega_{1}\frac{\langle 12\rangle\langle 1\iota\rangle}{\langle 2\iota\rangle}\iota_{(\alpha}\kappa_{2\beta)}+\omega_{1}\frac{\langle 12\rangle^{2}}{\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2}}\iota_{\alpha}\iota_{\beta}

\displaystyle+\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{4}\omega_{1}\omega_{2}\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2}(\omega_{1}\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2}+\omega_{2}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2})\iota_{\alpha}\iota_{\beta}

\displaystyle=k_{\alpha\beta}\bigg(\frac{K_{+}}{\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2}},\frac{\omega_{2}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2}\kappa_{2}+\omega_{1}\langle 1\iota\rangle\langle 2\iota\rangle\kappa_{1}}{K_{+}}\bigg)+\frac{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}{K_{+}}\bigg(\langle 12\rangle^{2}+\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{4}\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2}K_{+}^{2}\bigg)\iota_{\alpha}\iota_{\beta}

where as above
K_{+}=(k_{1}+k_{2})_{+}
. It’s no surprise that the exponent can be written this way as the denominator (
7.42
) measures the failure of
k_{1}+k_{2}
to be on-shell. Since

\exp\bigg(\frac{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}{K_{+}}\Big(\langle 12\rangle^{2}+\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{4}\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2}K_{+}^{2}\Big)x^{-}\bigg)=1+\mathcal{O}\Big(\langle 12\rangle^{2}+\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{4}\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2}K_{+}^{2}\Big)

(7.44)

this term does not affect the singular part of the splitting function; therefore, modulo regular terms

\mathcal{S}_{\text{KP}}^{\omega_{1},\omega_{2}}(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})\sim-\frac{\mathrm{i}\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2}K_{+}}{\langle 12\rangle^{2}+\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{4}\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2}K_{+}^{2}}\Phi^{K_{+}/\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2}}\bigg(x;\frac{\omega_{2}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2}\kappa_{2}+\omega_{1}\langle 1\iota\rangle\langle 2\iota\rangle\kappa_{1}}{K_{+}}\bigg)\,.

(7.45)

We can somewhat simplify the spinor argument appearing on the right hand side by noticing that

\frac{\omega_{2}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2}\kappa_{2}+\omega_{1}\langle 1\iota\rangle\langle 2\iota\rangle\kappa_{1}}{K_{+}}=\kappa_{2}-\frac{\omega_{1}\langle 12\rangle\langle 1\iota\rangle}{K_{+}}\,.

(7.46)

Let’s try to unpack the formula (
7.45
) in terms of soft modes as defined in equation (
7.32
). This requires expanding the propagator perturbatively in
\mathfrak{q}^{2}

\frac{K_{+}}{\langle 12\rangle^{2}+\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{4}\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2}K_{+}^{2}}=\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\frac{(-)^{\ell}\mathfrak{q}^{2\ell}}{2^{2\ell}}\frac{\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2\ell}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2\ell}K_{+}^{2\ell+1}}{\langle 12\rangle^{2\ell+2}}\,.

(7.47)

We have

\displaystyle\frac{\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2}K_{+}}{\langle 12\rangle^{2}+\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{4}\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2}K_{+}^{2}}\Phi^{K_{+}/\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2}}\bigg(x;\kappa_{2}-\frac{\omega_{1}\langle 12\rangle\langle 1\iota\rangle}{K_{+}}\iota\bigg)

(7.48)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\geq 0,s\geq 1}\frac{\mathrm{i}^{2\ell+s}\mathfrak{q}^{2\ell}}{(s-1)!2^{2\ell}}\frac{\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2(\ell+1)}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2(\ell+2-s)}K_{+}^{2\ell+s}}{\langle 12\rangle^{2\ell+2}}\Phi_{s}\bigg(x;\kappa_{2}-\frac{\omega_{1}\langle 12\rangle\langle 1\iota\rangle}{K_{+}}\iota\bigg)

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{\ell\geq 0,s\geq 1}\frac{\mathrm{i}^{2\ell+s}\mathfrak{q}^{2\ell}}{(s-1)!2^{2\ell}}\sum_{k=0}^{2\ell+1}(-)^{k}\frac{\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2\ell+2+k}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{3-2s+k}\omega_{1}^{k}K_{+}^{2\ell+s-k}}{k!(2\ell+1-k)!}

\displaystyle(\iota^{\alpha}\partial_{\kappa_{1}^{\alpha}})^{2\ell+1-k}\bigg(\frac{1}{\langle 12\rangle}\bigg)(\iota^{\alpha}\partial_{\kappa_{2}^{\alpha}})^{k}\Phi_{s}(x;\kappa_{2})\,.

Now let’s eliminate the variable
s
in favour of
t=2\ell+s
taking values in the range
t\geq 2\ell+1
. The splitting function is

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\geq 0,t\geq 2\ell+1}\sum_{k=0}^{2\ell+1}\frac{\mathrm{i}^{t}\mathfrak{q}^{2\ell}}{2^{2\ell}}\frac{(-)^{k}\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2\ell+2+k}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{3-2(t-2\ell)+k}\omega_{1}^{k}K_{+}^{t-k}}{k!(2\ell+1-k)!(t-2\ell-1)!}

(7.49)

\displaystyle(\iota^{\alpha}\partial_{\kappa_{1}^{\alpha}})^{2\ell+1-k}\bigg(\frac{1}{\langle 12\rangle}\bigg)(\iota^{\alpha}\partial_{\kappa_{2}^{\alpha}})^{k}\Phi_{t-2\ell}(x;\kappa_{2})\,.

Concentrating on the
\omega_{i}
dependence, we expand

\omega_{1}^{k}K_{+}^{t-k}=\sum_{q=0}^{t-k}\binom{t-k}{q}\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2(t-q-k)}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2q}\omega_{1}^{t-q}\omega_{2}^{q}\,.

(7.50)

Identifying the soft modes
\Phi_{s}(x;\kappa)
with chiral algebra states
\mathcal{V}_{s}(\kappa)
we can extract the OPE.

To do so we identify
q=s_{2}-1
, and
t=s_{1}+s_{2}-2
. The restriction
t\geq 2\ell+1
now limits the sum over
0\leq 2\ell\leq s_{1}+s_{2}-3
. Furthermore
t-k\geq q
becomes
k\leq s_{1}-1
, so that
0\leq k\leq\min\{s_{1}-1,2\ell+1\}
. In full, the soft OPE is

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{s_{1}}(\kappa_{1})\mathcal{V}_{s_{2}}(\kappa_{2})

(7.51)

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{\ell=0}^{\lfloor(s_{1}+s_{2}-3)/2\rfloor}\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2\ell}}{2^{2\ell}}\frac{(s_{1}-1)!}{(s_{1}+s_{2}-3-2\ell)!}\sum_{k=0}^{\min\{s_{1}-1,2\ell+1\}}\frac{(-)^{k}(s_{1}+s_{2}-2-k)!}{k!(s_{1}-1-k)!(2\ell+1-k)!}

\displaystyle\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2(\ell+s_{1})-k}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2(2\ell+2-s_{1})+k+1}(\iota^{\alpha}\partial_{\kappa_{1}^{\alpha}})^{2\ell+1-k}\bigg(\frac{1}{\langle 12\rangle}\bigg)(\iota^{\alpha}\partial_{\kappa_{2}^{\alpha}})^{k}\mathcal{V}_{s_{1}+s_{2}-2-2\ell}(\kappa_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\lfloor(s_{1}+s_{2}-3)/2\rfloor}\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2\ell}}{(2\ell+1)!2^{2\ell}}\sum_{k=0}^{2\ell+1}(-)^{k}\binom{2\ell+1}{k}[s_{1}+s_{2}-2-k]_{2\ell+1-k}[s_{1}-1]_{k}

\displaystyle\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2(\ell+s_{1})-k}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2(2\ell+2-s_{1})+k+1}(\iota^{\alpha}\partial_{\kappa_{1}^{\alpha}})^{2\ell+1-k}\bigg(\frac{1}{\langle 12\rangle}\bigg)(\iota^{\alpha}\partial_{\kappa_{2}^{\alpha}})^{k}\mathcal{V}_{s_{1}+s_{2}-2-2\ell}(\kappa_{2})\,.

In the final formula we’ve eliminated the condition
k\leq s_{1}-1
with the understanding that the descending factorial
[a]_{b}=a(a-1)\dots(a-(b-1))
vanishes when
b>a
(for
a,b
positive integers).

As is immediately clear from our formula for the hard OPE, equation (
7.51
) is symmetric under simultaneous exchange of
\kappa_{1}\leftrightarrow\kappa_{2}
,
s_{1}\leftrightarrow s_{2}
. We can make this symmetry manifest by rewriting the OPE as

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{s_{1}}(\kappa_{1})\mathcal{V}_{s_{2}}(\kappa_{2})\sim

(7.52)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2\ell}}{(2\ell+1)!2^{2\ell}}\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2s_{1}-1}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{4\ell-2s_{1}+5}M^{s_{1},s_{2}}_{2\ell}(\langle 1\iota\rangle\langle\iota\partial_{\kappa_{1}}\rangle,\langle 2\iota\rangle\langle\iota\partial_{\kappa_{2}}\rangle)\frac{\mathcal{V}_{s_{1}+s_{2}-2\ell-2}(\kappa_{2})}{\langle 12\rangle}

and

M^{s_{1},s_{2}}_{\ell}(m,n)=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell+1}(-)^{k}\binom{\ell+1}{k}[s_{1}-1]_{k}[s_{2}-1]_{\ell+1-k}m^{\ell+1-k}n^{k}\,.

(7.53)

In equation (
7.52
) we removed the upper restriction on
\ell
since
M^{s_{1},s_{2}}_{2\ell}
vanishes outside this range anyway. To see that this reproduces equation (
7.51
) we expand

\langle 2\kappa\rangle^{k}\langle\iota\partial_{\kappa_{2}}\rangle^{k}\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{V}_{s}(\kappa_{2})}{\langle 12\rangle}\bigg)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-)^{k-i}\binom{k}{i}\langle 1\iota\rangle^{k-i}\langle\iota\partial_{\kappa_{1}}\rangle^{k-i}\bigg(\frac{1}{\langle 12\rangle}\bigg)\langle 2\kappa\rangle^{i}\langle\iota\partial_{\kappa_{2}}\rangle^{i}\mathcal{V}_{s}(\kappa_{2})\,.

(7.54)

The sum over
i\leq k\leq 2\ell+1
can then be performed directly

\sum_{k=i}^{2\ell+1}\frac{[s_{1}-1]_{k}[s_{2}-1]_{2\ell+1-k}}{(2\ell+1-k)!(k-i)!}=\frac{[s_{1}-1]_{i}[s_{1}+s_{2}-2-i]_{2\ell+1-i}}{(2\ell+1-i)!}\,,

(7.55)

as desired. Rewriting the OPE in terms of rescaled states
\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{s}(\kappa)=\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2(1-2s)}\mathcal{V}_{s}(\kappa)
of conformal spin
s
it reads

\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{s_{1}}(\kappa_{1})\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{s_{2}}(\kappa_{2})\sim\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2\ell}}{(2\ell+1)!2^{2\ell}}\frac{\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2s_{1}-1}}{\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2s_{1}-1}}M^{s_{1},s_{2}}_{2\ell}\bigg(\frac{\langle 1\iota\rangle\langle\iota\partial_{\kappa_{1}}\rangle}{\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2}},\frac{\langle\iota\partial_{\kappa_{2}}\rangle}{\langle 2\iota\rangle}\bigg)\frac{\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{s_{1}+s_{2}-2\ell-2}(\kappa_{2})}{\langle 12\rangle}\,.

(7.56)

The ratio
\langle 1\iota\rangle/\langle 2\iota\rangle
is independent of
\iota
modulo terms of order
\langle 12\rangle
, and
\langle\iota\partial_{\kappa}\rangle/\langle\kappa\iota\rangle
is also independent of
\iota
modulo the addition of the homogeneity operator. Therefore the singular part of the OPE is independent of the reference spinor
\iota
. In terms of the inhomogeneous co-ordinate
w=\langle o\kappa\rangle/\langle\kappa\iota\rangle
the OPE reads

\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{s_{1}}(w_{1})\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{s_{2}}(w_{2})\sim-\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2\ell}}{(2\ell+1)!2^{2\ell}}M^{s_{1},s_{2}}_{2\ell}\big(\partial_{w_{1}},\partial_{w_{2}}\big)\frac{\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{s_{1}+s_{2}-2\ell-2}(w_{2})}{w_{12}}\,.

(7.57)

It’s not immediate that this operator product associates. To verify this it’s useful to expand in modes.

The corresponding modes are

\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{s,m}=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\oint_{w=0}\mathrm{d}w\,w^{s-1+m}\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{s}(w)\,,

(7.58)

and their algebra is determined by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi\mathrm{i})^{2}}\oint_{w_{2}=0}\oint_{w_{12}=0}w_{1}^{s_{1}-1+m}w_{2}^{s_{2}-1+n}\partial_{w_{1}}^{2\ell+1-k}\partial_{w_{2}}^{k}\bigg(\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{s_{1}+s_{2}-2\ell-2}(w_{2})}{w_{12}}\bigg)

(7.59)

\displaystyle=(-)^{2\ell+1}[s_{1}-1+m]_{2\ell+1-k}[s_{2}-1+n]_{k}\oint_{w_{2}=0}w_{2}^{s_{1}+s_{2}-2+m+n-(2\ell+1)}\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{s_{1}+s_{2}-2\ell-2}(w_{2})

\displaystyle=(-)^{2\ell+1}[s_{1}-1+m]_{2\ell+1-k}[s_{2}-1+n]_{k}\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{s_{1}+s_{2}-2\ell-2,m+n}\,.

Therefore the mode algebra is

\big[\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{s_{1},m},\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{s_{2},n}\big]=\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2\ell}}{(2\ell+1)!2^{2\ell}}N^{s_{1},s_{2}}_{2\ell}(m,n)\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{s_{1}+s_{2}-2\ell-2,m+n}

(7.60)

We recognise this as the mode algebra of
W_{1+\infty}
obtained directly from correspondence space in subsection
7.4
(and therefore it obeys the Jacobi identity).

8
Discussion

There are a number of natural extensions to this work.

Other Examples of Minitwistorial Theories.

We’ve shown that Abelian Poisson- and non-commutative
5d
Chern-Simons theory on
\mathbb{PS}
with

\Omega=\frac{\langle\lambda\mathrm{d}\lambda\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x^{\alpha\beta}\lambda_{\alpha}\lambda_{\beta}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{4}}

(8.1)

describe the dKP and KP equation on space-time respectively. There are a number of natural modifications of this set-up which will engineer other
3d
minitwistorial theories.

We could instead choose to trivialise the canonical bundle with the divisor
\langle o\lambda\rangle^{2}\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}
. On-shell arguments suggest that for this measure
5d
Poisson-Chern-Simons will lead to
3d
Toda theory (also known as
\mathrm{SU}_{\infty}
Toda)
[
51
]
. We expect that switching on non-commutativity leads to the infinite
2d
Toda lattice with infinitesimal spacing
[
52
,
53
]
. Trivialising the canonical bundle with
\mu^{2}
leads to these same theories, although perturbed around non-trivial backgrounds. One attractive feature of these set-ups is that they make sense in both Lorentzian and Euclidean signature.

Another natural generalisation is to study non-Abelian
5d
Chern-Simons theory on
\mathbb{PS}
. In the commutative case this was introduced in
[
17
]
and depending on the choice of divisor leads to the Manakov-Zakharov-Ward model
[
18
,
19
]
or its pseudo-dual
[
30
]
. It would be interesting to understand the consequences of switching on non-commutativity on correspondence space in these examples. Natural candidates for the resulting space-times theories can be found in the literature
[
54
,
53
]
(potentially including the Davey-Stewartson equation
[
55
]
).

Just as for
4d
space-time, there are BF counterparts of these theories which crucially do not rely on trivialising the canonical bundle.
5d
mixed topological-holomorphic BF theory on correspondence space descends to the monopole theory; a Lagrangian multiplier type action for the Bogomol’nyi equation
[
56
,
20
]
. It has a gravitational counterpart which on correspondence space describes generic deformations of the transverse holomorphic foliation and descends to a Lagrange multiplier type action for the Einstein-Weyl equations
[
14
,
36
]
.

There are also a number of modified twistor constructions appearing in the literature which could be adapted to the correspondence space of
3d
space-time. These include orbifolds
[
57
]
, higher genus twistor spaces
[
21
]
and their singular degenerations
[
58
]
.

Three-Dimensional Integrability.

Crucial developments in the study of the KP equation, and indeed integrable partial differential equations more generally, were made by the Japanese school
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
59
]
; the geometric and analytic underpinnings of this approach were subsequently developed by Segal and Wilson
[
60
]
. The central construct is the
\tau
-function
u=\phi_{x}=-\mathfrak{q}^{2}(\log\tau)_{xx}
which is equated with a
2d
CFT correlator with operator insertions. We expect that the universal vertex algebra supported on minitwistor lines is intimately connected with this
2d
CFT, and that the inserted operators are generated by defects on correspondence space intersecting the minitwistor line at points. (A link between
\tau
-function and the minitwistor
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
was also suggested in
[
61
]
.) Natural candidates for such defects are topological lines supported at points on
{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}
.

Whilst it seems the most direct connection between this work and more traditional approaches to the KP equation is via Sato theory, it would also be enlightening to interpret the inverse scattering transform
[
5
,
3
]
, Hirota’s direct method
[
10
,
59
]
and the connection with topological strings
[
12
,
11
]
from the minitwistor perspective.

We also note that there have been alternative proposals for minitwistor formulations of the KP equation through ‘generalized twistor correspondences’
[
62
,
30
]
, though whether these have any relation to non-commutative
5d
Chern-Simons theory is unclear to us.

Quantum Corrections and Twisted Holography.

We’ve worked exclusively at the classical level, but we expect that both Poisson- and non-commutative
5d
Chern-Simons on correspondence space admit one-loop quantizations. In fact we expect any
5d
mixed topological-holomorphic theory on
\mathbb{PS}
to admit a one-loop quantization, because in odd dimensions one-loop perturbative anomalies vanish. A direct consequence should be the vanishing of one-loop amplitudes in the corresponding
3d
space-time theories.

In the case of non-commutative
5d

Appendix A
Distributor

In this appendix we derive the distributor of
\tilde{\mathrm{d}}
over the associative product
\star
appearing in equation (
4.28
). In order to do so we will need to recall equation (
4.9
)

\tilde{\mathrm{d}}\partial_{0}=2\frac{\tilde{\sigma}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}}{\langle\iota\lambda\rangle}\otimes\partial_{1}\,,

(A.1)

and furthermore

\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{0}}\frac{\tilde{\sigma}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle}=\mathrm{d}x^{\alpha\beta}\iota_{\alpha}\iota_{\beta}=\mathrm{d}x^{-}\,.

(A.2)

We can write the star product
\star
of
f,g\in\widetilde{\Omega}^{0,\bullet}(\mathbb{PS})
as

f\star g=\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{\ell}}{\ell!2^{\ell}}\epsilon^{j_{1}i_{1}}\dots\epsilon^{j_{\ell}i_{\ell}}\partial_{i_{1}}\dots\partial_{i_{\ell}}f\partial_{j_{1}}\dots\partial_{j_{\ell}}g\,.

(A.3)

In our chosen conventions
\epsilon^{10}=1
, and all
(1,0)
-vector fields are implicitly acting by Lie derivatives. Acting with
\tilde{\mathrm{d}}
gives

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathrm{d}}(f\star g)=\tilde{\mathrm{d}}f\star g+(-)^{\mathrm{deg}\,f}f\star\tilde{\mathrm{d}}g

(A.4)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{\ell}}{\ell!2^{\ell}}\epsilon^{j_{1}i_{1}}\dots\epsilon^{j_{\ell}i_{\ell}}\Big([\tilde{\mathrm{d}},\partial_{i_{1}}\dots\partial_{i_{\ell}}]f\partial_{j_{1}}\dots\partial_{j_{\ell}}g+(-)^{\mathrm{deg}\,f}\partial_{i_{1}}\dots\partial_{i_{\ell}}f\,[\tilde{\mathrm{d}},\partial_{j_{1}}\dots\partial_{j_{\ell}}]g\Big)\,.

Now

\displaystyle[\tilde{\mathrm{d}},\partial_{i_{1}}\dots\partial_{i_{\ell}}]=\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}\partial_{i_{1}}\dots\partial_{i_{k-1}}[\tilde{\mathrm{d}},\partial_{i_{k}}]\partial_{i_{k+1}}\dots\partial_{i_{\ell}}

(A.5)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}\delta^{0}_{i_{k}}\partial_{i_{1}}\dots\partial_{i_{k-1}}\frac{\tilde{\sigma}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}}{\langle\iota\lambda\rangle}\partial_{i_{k+1}}\dots\partial_{i_{\ell}}\partial_{1}\,.

Here we’ve migrated
\partial_{1}
to the r.h.s. of the expression harmlessly. We’d like to pull out the
(0,1)
-form
\tilde{\sigma}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}/\langle\iota\lambda\rangle
but this must be done with care since
\partial_{0}
can act on it. Using equation (
A.2
) we have

\displaystyle[\tilde{\mathrm{d}},\partial_{i_{1}}\dots\partial_{i_{\ell}}]=2\frac{\tilde{\sigma}^{\alpha}\iota_{\alpha}}{\langle\iota\lambda\rangle}\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}\delta^{0}_{i_{k}}\partial_{i_{1}}\dots\partial_{i_{k-1}}\partial_{i_{k+1}}\dots\partial_{i_{\ell}}\partial_{1}

(A.6)

\displaystyle+2\mathrm{d}x^{-}\sum_{k=2}^{\ell}\sum_{m=1}^{k-1}\delta^{0}_{i_{m}}\delta^{0}_{i_{k}}\partial_{i_{1}}\dots\partial_{i_{m-1}}\partial_{i_{m+1}}\dots\partial_{i_{k-1}}\partial_{i_{k+1}}\dots\partial_{i_{\ell}}\partial_{1}\,.

When plugging this into equation (
A.4
) we find that precisely which indices
i_{k},i_{m}
have been fixed to zero is immaterial, as they are dummy variables and the vector fields
\partial_{i}
commute with one another. The sum over
k,m
therefore generates a factor of
\ell(\ell-1)/2
and the sum over
k
alone gives
\ell
. The sign factor
(-)^{\mathrm{deg}\,f}
in the final term of (
A.4
) cancels when we bring a
1
-form past
f
. The only remaining signs are generated when
\epsilon
-symbols have their indices fixed. We find

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathrm{d}}(f\star g)=\tilde{\mathrm{d}}f\star g+(-)^{\mathrm{deg}f}f\star\tilde{\mathrm{d}}g+\mathfrak{q}\frac{\tilde{\sigma}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}}{\langle\iota\lambda\rangle}\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{\ell-1}}{(\ell-1)!2^{\ell-1}}\epsilon^{j_{1}i_{1}}\dots\epsilon^{j_{\ell-1}i_{\ell-1}}

(A.7)

\displaystyle\big(\partial_{i_{1}}\dots\partial_{i_{\ell-1}}\partial_{1}f\partial_{j_{1}}\dots\partial_{j_{\ell-1}}\partial_{1}g-\partial_{i_{1}}\dots\partial_{i_{\ell-1}}\partial_{1}f\,\partial_{j_{1}}\dots\partial_{j_{m-1}}\partial_{1}g\big)

\displaystyle+\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{4}\mathrm{d}x^{-}\sum_{\ell\geq 2}\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{\ell-2}}{(\ell-2)!2^{\ell-2}}\epsilon^{j_{1}i_{1}}\dots\epsilon^{j_{\ell-2}i_{\ell-2}}\big(\partial_{i_{1}}\dots\partial_{i_{\ell-2}}\partial_{1}f\,\partial_{j_{1}}\dots\partial_{j_{\ell-2}}\partial_{1}^{2}g

\displaystyle+\partial_{i_{1}}\dots\partial_{i_{\ell-1}}\partial_{1}f\,\partial_{j_{1}}\dots\partial_{j_{\ell-1}}\partial_{1}^{2}g\big)\,.

Now the first sum vanishes as a result of the relative sign
\epsilon^{01}=-\epsilon^{10}
. The second term does not vanish, leading to the succinct formula

\tilde{\mathrm{d}}(f\star g)=\tilde{\mathrm{d}}f\star g+(-)^{\mathrm{deg}\,f}f\star\tilde{\mathrm{d}}g+\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{4}\mathrm{d}x^{-}\partial_{1}(\partial_{1}f\star\partial_{1}g)\,.

(A.8)

This is equation (
4.28
) from the manuscript. Notice that the extra term is a total derivative, consistent with the difference between the Moyal and commutative products being exact.

Appendix B
Independence of Poisson-Chern-Simons from the distribution
\mathcal{D}

In subsection
4.1
we made a choice of rank two integrable subbundle
\mathcal{D}\subset T_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{PS}
to represent
T^{1,0}\mathbb{PS}
. We were motivated to make this choice because quantization of the Poisson bivector was particularly simple in this frame. However, this frame had the property that the pushforward

\pi_{*}\partial_{1}=\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\partial_{\mu}+\overline{\langle\iota\lambda\rangle}^{2}\partial_{\bar{\mu}}

(B.1)

does not lie in
T^{1,0}{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}
. As explained in subsection
3.2
this is harmless, because the translation
\partial_{\bar{\mu}}
is BRST exact.

Nevertheless, it’s natural to seek a more geometric definition of the theory. In this appendix we first introduce an alternative choice of frame that arises from a natural holomorphic lift of the holomorphic tangent bundle on minitwistor space. We then consider
5d
Chern-Simons theory defined using this holomorphic frame. After a field redefinition, we find that this theory is equivalent to the definition of
5d
Chern-Simons employing the constant frame of subsection
4.1
.

B.1
5d
Chern-Simons Theory in the Holomorphic Frame

Consider the alternative choice of distribution

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=\Big\langle\bar{\lambda}^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda^{\alpha}}\bigg|_{x}\,,\bar{\lambda}^{\alpha}\bar{\lambda}^{\beta}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\alpha\beta}}\Big\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}\,,

(B.2)

with frame

\xi_{0}=\frac{\bar{\lambda}^{\alpha}}{\langle\lambda\bar{\lambda}\rangle}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda^{\alpha}}\bigg|_{x}\,,\qquad\xi_{1}=\frac{\bar{\lambda}^{\alpha}\bar{\lambda}^{\beta}}{\langle\lambda\bar{\lambda}\rangle^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\alpha\beta}}\,.

(B.3)

Note that
\xi_{0},\xi_{1}
transform as sections of
T^{1,0}_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}\mathbb{PS}\otimes\mathcal{O}(-2)
. As for the constant frame, the derivative with respect to
\lambda
is taken at fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{1,2}
.
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
also fixes representatives for
\widetilde{\Omega}^{0,\bullet}(\mathbb{PS})
which lies in its annihilator. In this case

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\perp}=\big\langle\overline{\langle\lambda\mathrm{d}\lambda\rangle}\,,\mathrm{d}x^{\alpha\beta}\bar{\lambda}_{\beta}\big\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}

(B.4)

with

\tilde{e}^{0}=\frac{\langle\bar{\lambda}\mathrm{d}\bar{\lambda}\rangle}{\langle\lambda\bar{\lambda}\rangle^{2}}\,,\qquad\tilde{e}^{\alpha}=\frac{\mathrm{d}x^{\alpha\beta}\bar{\lambda}_{\beta}}{\langle\lambda\bar{\lambda}\rangle}

(B.5)

as the corresponding frame.

Unlike the distribution
\mathcal{D}
used in the text, the distribution
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
has the property
\pi_{*}\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\subset T^{1,0}{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}
whilst retaining the feature
\rho_{*}\xi_{0}=0
, so that
\xi_{0}
is tangent to real minitwistor lines. However,
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
suffers from a significant drawback: on the locus
\lambda\sim\bar{\lambda}
it non-trivially intersects
T^{0,1}\mathbb{PS}
and is therefore not a good section. Whilst the choice of frame (
B.5
) is natural, it’s also singular here.

Let’s now consider
5d
Poisson-Chern-Simons theory defined on
\mathbb{PS}
using
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
. We write
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{0}\tilde{e}^{0}+\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\in\widetilde{\Omega}^{0,\bullet}_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{PS})
for the dynamical field of
5d
Poisson-Chern-Simons theory in the frame (
B.5
). The action takes a familiar form

S_{\text{PCS}}^{\prime}[\mathcal{A}]=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{PS}}\Omega\,\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{A}\tilde{\mathrm{d}}\mathcal{A}+\frac{1}{3}\mathcal{A}\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\langle^{2}\xi_{0}}\mathcal{A}\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{1}}\mathcal{A}\bigg)\,.

(B.6)

We must now face the fact that the basis
\{\tilde{e}^{0},\tilde{e}^{1}\}
becomes singular on
\langle\lambda\bar{\lambda}\rangle=0
. To handle this, we excise the neighbourhood
\text{Ann}_{\epsilon}=\{|\mathrm{Re}\,z|\leq\epsilon\}
of this locus from
\mathbb{PS}
and impose suitable boundary conditions. Varying the kinetic term on
\mathbb{PS}\setminus\text{Ann}_{\epsilon}
yields

\delta S_{\text{kin.}}^{\prime}[\mathcal{A}]=-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{PS}\setminus\text{Ann}_{\epsilon}}\Omega\,\tilde{\mathrm{d}}(\mathcal{A}\delta\mathcal{A})+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{PS}\setminus\text{Ann}_{\epsilon}}\delta\mathcal{A}\,(\text{linearised equations of motion})\,.

(B.7)

On-shell we recover the boundary contribution

\delta S_{\text{kin.}}^{\prime}=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{8\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{1,2}\times\{\mathrm{Re}\,z=\epsilon\}}\Omega\,\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\,\tilde{e}_{\alpha}\,\mathcal{A}^{\beta}\delta\mathcal{A}_{\beta}-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{1,2}\times\{\mathrm{Re}\,z=-\epsilon\}}\Omega\,\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\,\tilde{e}_{\alpha}\,\mathcal{A}^{\beta}\delta\mathcal{A}_{\beta}\,.

(B.8)

Now
\mathrm{d}x^{\gamma\delta}\lambda_{\gamma}\lambda_{\delta}\,\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\,\tilde{e}_{\alpha}=-\sqrt{2}\mathrm{d}^{3}x
, so that

\delta S_{\text{kin.}}^{\prime}=-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{4\sqrt{2}\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{1,2}}\mathrm{d}^{3}x\Bigg(\oint_{\{\mathrm{Re}\,z=\epsilon\}}-\oint_{\{\mathrm{Re}\,z=-\epsilon\}}\Bigg)\frac{\langle\lambda\mathrm{d}\lambda\rangle}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{4}}\,\mathcal{A}^{\beta}\delta\mathcal{A}_{\beta}

(B.9)

The boundary conditions we impose on
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
are engineered so that they are compatible with the constant frame, at least at the linearised level. Whilst requiring that
\lambda^{\gamma}\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}
remains finite, we tolerate a singularity in the components
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
of the form

\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}=-\frac{\bar{\lambda}_{\alpha}\lambda^{\gamma}}{\langle\lambda\bar{\lambda}\rangle}\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}+\text{finite}\,.

(B.10)

The boundary contribution (
B.9
) vanishes essentially by the same argument as in subsection
4.2
.

The only remaining danger in (
B.9
) occurs at
\lambda\sim\iota
. The boundary condition we impose here is complicated by the fact that
\iota
is real, so both
\langle\lambda\iota\rangle
and
\langle\lambda\bar{\lambda}\rangle
vanish there. We continue to require that a version of equation (
B.10
) holds where now
\lambda^{\gamma}\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}=\mathcal{O}(\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2})
and

\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}=-\frac{\bar{\lambda}_{\alpha}\lambda^{\gamma}}{\langle\lambda\bar{\lambda}\rangle}\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}+\mathcal{A}^{\text{fin.}}_{\alpha}

(B.11)

for
\mathcal{A}^{\text{fin.}}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{O}(\langle\lambda\iota\rangle)
. The terms of order
\langle\lambda\iota\rangle
in the above expression are further required to obey the condition (
4.21
), that is

2\langle\lambda\iota\rangle o^{\alpha}\mathcal{A}^{\text{fin.}}_{\alpha}+\langle o\lambda\rangle\iota^{\alpha}\mathcal{A}^{\text{fin.}}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{O}\big(\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\big)\,.

(B.12)

In order for infinitesimal gauge transformations to respect this condition they should vanish second order at
\lambda\sim\iota
. It’s natural to similarly require that
\tilde{e}^{0}\mathcal{A}_{0}
vanishes to second order also.

B.2
Comparing the Constant and Holomorphic Frames

We now show that the Poisson-Chern-Simons action in the holomorphic frame (
B.6
) is equivalent to that in the constant frame (
4.15
) used in the main text. The general argument that this should be true follows from a generalisation of a Lemma of Costello & Gwilliam
[
45
]
. (Section 9.2, Lemma 9.2.1.) Here we will illustrate this by constructing a field redefinition that moves between the two theories. We will content ourselves to work just to cubic order in the action. Thus, we will explicitly find the relation between the field
a
defined in the constant distribution
\mathcal{D}
and the field
\mathcal{A}
introduced in the previous subsection, correct to quadratic order. We also outline how, in principle, this construction could be continued to higher order.

Let us first understand how the gauge fields
a
and
\mathcal{A}
are related at linear order, which follows simply by comparing the two frames. One finds

a=\frac{1}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle}\mathcal{A}+\frac{\langle\bar{\lambda}\iota\rangle}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\langle\lambda\bar{\lambda}\rangle}\iota^{\gamma}\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}e^{1}+\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{A}^{2})\,,

(B.13)

which follows since they agree modulo terms wedging to zero against
\Omega
, and
\partial_{1}\mathbin{\lrcorner}a=0
. In component form this reads

a_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle}\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}+\frac{\langle\bar{\lambda}\iota\rangle}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\langle\lambda\bar{\lambda}\rangle}\lambda_{\alpha}\lambda^{\beta}\mathcal{A}_{\beta}+\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{A}^{2})\,,

(B.14)

or dually

\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}=\langle\lambda\iota\rangle a_{\alpha}-\frac{\langle\bar{\lambda}\iota\rangle}{\langle\lambda\bar{\lambda}\rangle}\lambda_{\alpha}\lambda^{\beta}a_{\beta}+\mathcal{O}\big(a^{2}\big)\,.

(B.15)

The boundary conditions (
B.10
), (
B.11
) and (
B.12
) follow from this linearised transformation.

We will also find it useful to introduce a frame for
\ell
dual to the frame
\{\tilde{e}^{0},\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\}
for
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\perp}
.

\tilde{\xi}_{0}=-\langle\lambda\bar{\lambda}\rangle\lambda^{\gamma}\partial_{\bar{\lambda}^{\gamma}}\big|_{x}\,,\qquad\tilde{\xi}_{\alpha}=\frac{(\lambda^{\beta}\bar{\lambda}_{\alpha}-2\bar{\lambda}^{\beta}\lambda_{\alpha})}{\langle\lambda\bar{\lambda}\rangle}\lambda^{\gamma}\partial_{\beta\gamma}\,.

(B.16)

Then
\partial_{0},\partial_{1}
and
\xi_{0},\xi_{1}
are related by

\partial_{0}\sim\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}\,,\qquad\partial_{1}=\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{1}-\frac{\langle\bar{\lambda}\iota\rangle}{\langle\lambda\bar{\lambda}\rangle}\iota^{\gamma}\tilde{\xi}_{\gamma}=\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{1}-r

(B.17)

in terms of which

a=\mathcal{A}+(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})\frac{e^{1}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{A}^{2})\,.

(B.18)

To construct the higher order terms, we must consider the Lagrangians of the two theories. Note that since
a
and
\mathcal{A}
are both 1-forms, any higher order corrections must involve contractions of
(0,1)
vector fields into the gauge field
a
, since the form degree of the Chern-Simons Lagrangian is saturated by three
a
s. These will also be removable by a field redefinition: the Cartan formula
[\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime},\widetilde{V}\mathbin{\lrcorner}]=\mathcal{L}^{0,1}_{\widetilde{V}}
tells us that contraction and differentiation along
(0,1)
vector fields cancel in
\tilde{\mathrm{d}}
-cohomology. Therefore by a field redefinition the Lagrangian can be brought into a form in which the vertices involve neither and so terminate at cubic order.

The kinetic terms are manifestly equivalent, so it suffices to compare the cubic vertices. In the constant frame the interaction is

\displaystyle S_{\text{int.}}[a]

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{6\pi}\int_{\mathbb{PS}}\Omega\,a\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{0}}a\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{1}}a

(B.19)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{6\pi}\int_{\mathbb{PS}}\Omega\,\mathcal{A}\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{0}}\bigg(\mathcal{A}+(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})\frac{e^{1}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}}\bigg)\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{1}}\bigg(\mathcal{A}+(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})\frac{e^{1}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}}\bigg)\ +\ \mathcal{O}(\mathcal{A}^{4})\,.

In order for the shift from
a
to
\mathcal{A}
to contribute the Lie derivatives must act on the 1-forms
e^{1}
, since left untouched they will wedge to zero against
\Omega
. These Lie derivatives are current written in the constant frame
\mathcal{D}
. To rewrite the interaction using the holomorphic frame it will be useful to express the vertex in terms of ordinary Lie derivatives. The relevant structure equation is

\mathrm{d}\bigg(\frac{e^{1}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}}\bigg)=2\frac{\langle\lambda\mathrm{d}\lambda\rangle}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{3}}\mathrm{d}x^{\alpha\beta}\lambda_{\alpha}\iota_{\beta}=2\frac{\langle\lambda\mathrm{d}\lambda\rangle}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{3}}\tilde{\sigma}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}\,,

(B.20)

therefore

\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{0}}\bigg(\frac{e^{1}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}}\bigg)=\partial_{0}\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathrm{d}\bigg(\frac{e^{1}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}}\bigg)=\frac{2\tilde{\sigma}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle}\,,\qquad\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{1}}\bigg(\frac{e^{1}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}}\bigg)=\partial_{1}\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathrm{d}\bigg(\frac{e^{1}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}}\bigg)=0\,.

(B.21)

Wedging against
\Omega

\Omega\,\frac{\tilde{\sigma}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle}=\Omega\,\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}

(B.22)

which allows us to recast the vertex as

\frac{\mathrm{i}}{6\pi}\int_{\mathbb{PS}}\Omega\,\mathcal{A}\big(\mathcal{A}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}}\mathcal{A}+2\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A}\big)\big(\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{1}}\mathcal{A}-\mathcal{L}_{r}\mathcal{A}\big)

(B.23)

to cubic order. At this stage we can split the Lie derivatives into their
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
parts with respect to the
holomorphic frame

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
, which we write as
\mathcal{L}_{V}=\mathcal{L}^{1,0}_{V}+\mathcal{L}^{0,1}_{V}
with
\mathcal{L}^{1,0}_{V}=\{V\mathbin{\lrcorner},\partial^{\prime}\}
and
\mathcal{L}^{0,1}_{V}=\{V\mathbin{\lrcorner},\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}\}
. If
V
is
(1,0)
vector field in the holomorphic frame, for example
\xi_{0}
or
\xi_{1}
, then

\mathcal{L}^{0,1}_{V}\mathcal{A}=V\mathbin{\lrcorner}\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}\mathcal{A}+\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}V\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A}=0

(B.24)

since
V
contracts to zero against both
\mathcal{A}
and
\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}\mathcal{A}
. On the other hand, if
V
is a
(0,1)
vector field, for example
r
, then

\{r\mathbin{\lrcorner},\partial^{\prime}\}\mathcal{A}=(\partial^{\prime}r)\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A}\,.

(B.25)

We can evaluate
\partial^{\prime}r
using the structure equations

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\tilde{e}^{\alpha}=\mathrm{d}x^{\alpha\beta}\lambda_{\beta}\tilde{e}^{0}+\frac{\langle\bar{\lambda}\iota\rangle}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle\langle\lambda\bar{\lambda}\rangle}e^{0}\tilde{e}^{\alpha}=-\lambda^{\alpha}\lambda_{\beta}\tilde{e}^{0}\tilde{e}^{\beta}-\frac{\bar{\lambda}^{\alpha}}{\langle\lambda\bar{\lambda}\rangle}e^{1}\tilde{e}^{0}+\frac{\langle\bar{\lambda}\iota\rangle}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle\langle\lambda\bar{\lambda}\rangle}e^{0}\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\,,

(B.26)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\tilde{e}^{0}=-2\frac{\langle\bar{\lambda}\iota\rangle}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle\langle\lambda\bar{\lambda}\rangle}e^{0}\tilde{e}^{0}\,,

where
\mathrm{d}
acts on fields with weight under scaling by trivialising with
\langle\lambda\iota\rangle
. (Objects invariant under scaling are independent of this choice.) We infer

\displaystyle\partial^{\prime}r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\tilde{e}^{\gamma}=\partial^{\prime}(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\tilde{e}^{\gamma})+r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\partial^{\prime}\tilde{e}^{\gamma}=\partial^{\prime}\bigg(\frac{\langle\bar{\lambda}\iota\rangle}{\langle\lambda\bar{\lambda}\rangle}\iota^{\gamma}\bigg)-\frac{\langle\bar{\lambda}\iota\rangle^{2}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle\langle\lambda\bar{\lambda}\rangle^{2}}\iota^{\gamma}e^{0}=0\,,

(B.27)

\displaystyle\partial^{\prime}r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\tilde{e}^{0}=\partial^{\prime}(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\tilde{e}^{0})+r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\partial^{\prime}\tilde{e}^{0}=0\,.

As a result the interaction vertex in the holomorphic frame reads

\displaystyle S_{\text{int.}}[\mathcal{A}]=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{6\pi}\int_{\mathbb{PS}}\Omega\,\mathcal{A}\big(\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}}\mathcal{A}+2\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A}\big)\big(\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{1}}\mathcal{A}-\mathcal{L}^{0,1}_{r}\mathcal{A}\big)

(B.28)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{6\pi}\int_{\mathbb{PS}}\Omega\,\mathcal{A}\big(\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}}\mathcal{A}\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{1}}\mathcal{A}-\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}}\mathcal{A}\mathcal{L}^{0,1}_{r}\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle+2\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{1}}\mathcal{A}-2\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})\mathcal{L}^{0,1}_{r}\mathcal{A}\big)\,.

The first term has exactly the form we expect for Poisson-Chern-Simons in the holomorphic frame. We’d like to show that there exists a quadratic field redefinition which, when inserted into the kinetic term, eliminates the remaining three.

A natural first guess is

\delta\mathcal{A}=f\mathcal{A}+B(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}}\mathcal{A}+C(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})^{2}\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}

(B.29)

for constants
B,C
and some function
f
linear in the field
\mathcal{A}
. The variation of the kinetic term is

\delta S_{\text{kin.}}=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{PS}}\Omega\,\big(f\mathcal{A}+B(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}}\mathcal{A}+C(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})^{2}\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}\big)\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}\mathcal{A}\,.

(B.30)

Contracting by parts with some amount of
r
in the second and third terms, we get

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\delta S_{\text{kin.}}=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{PS}}\Omega\,\big(f\mathcal{A}\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}\mathcal{A}+(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})\big((B-D)\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}}\mathcal{A}+(C-E)(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}\big)\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}\mathcal{A}

(B.31)

\displaystyle+D\mathcal{A}(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}}\mathcal{A})\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}\mathcal{A}-D\mathcal{A}\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}}\mathcal{A}(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}\mathcal{A})+E\mathcal{A}(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha})\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle-E\mathcal{A}(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}\mathcal{A})\big)

for constants
D,E
to be determined. Let’s write
h=-r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}=\langle\lambda\iota\rangle\langle\bar{\lambda}\iota\rangle/\langle\lambda\bar{\lambda}\rangle

f\mapsto f-D(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}}\mathcal{A})+Eh(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})
and in the remaining two we write
r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}=\mathcal{L}^{0,1}_{r}-\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}r\mathbin{\lrcorner}
to get

\displaystyle\delta S_{\text{kin.}}=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{PS}}\Omega\,\Big(f\mathcal{A}\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}\mathcal{A}+(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})\big((B-D)\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}}\mathcal{A}+(C-E)(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}\big)\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}\mathcal{A}

(B.32)

\displaystyle-D\mathcal{A}\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}}\mathcal{A}\mathcal{L}^{0,1}_{r}\mathcal{A}-E\mathcal{A}(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}\mathcal{L}^{0,1}_{r}\mathcal{A}+D\mathcal{A}\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}}\mathcal{A}\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})

\displaystyle+\frac{E}{2}\mathcal{A}\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})^{2}\Big)\,.

The terms involving
\mathcal{L}^{0,1}_{r}
are exactly of the form required to cancel two of the unwanted contributions in (
B.28
). Concentrating on the final two terms, we follow our nose and integrate by parts with respect to
\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{PS}}\Omega\,\Big(D\mathcal{A}\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}}\mathcal{A}\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})+\frac{E}{2}\mathcal{A}\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})^{2}\Big)

(B.33)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{PS}}\Omega\,\Big(D\mathcal{A}\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}}\mathcal{A}(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})+\frac{E}{2}\mathcal{A}\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}(\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha})(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})^{2}-D\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}\mathcal{A}(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}}\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle-\frac{E}{2}\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}\mathcal{A}(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})^{2}\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}\Big)\,.

The final two terms can be collected with those in the first line of equation (
B.31
). From the structure equation for
\tilde{e}^{\alpha}
we learn that

\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}\tilde{e}^{\alpha}=-\lambda^{\alpha}\lambda_{\beta}\tilde{e}^{0}\tilde{e}^{\beta}\,;

(B.34)

therefore,
\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}(\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha})=0
. This leaves

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{PS}}\Omega\,D\mathcal{A}\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}}\mathcal{A}(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{PS}}\Omega\,D\mathcal{A}[\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime},\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}}]\mathcal{A}(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})

(B.35)

\displaystyle+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{PS}}\Omega\,D\mathcal{A}\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}}\tilde{\mathrm{d}}\mathcal{A}(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})\,.

The first of these terms can be evaluated using
[\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{1,0}_{\xi_{0}}]=\mathcal{L}^{1,0}_{\mathrm{d}^{\prime}\xi_{0}}
and the structure equations for
e^{0},e^{1}
to infer that

\displaystyle

\displaystyle(\tilde{\mathrm{d}}\xi_{0})\mathbin{\lrcorner}e^{0}=\tilde{\mathrm{d}}(\xi_{0}\mathbin{\lrcorner}e^{0})+\xi_{0}\mathbin{\lrcorner}\tilde{\mathrm{d}}e^{0}=0\,,

(B.36)

\displaystyle(\tilde{\mathrm{d}}\xi_{0})\mathbin{\lrcorner}e^{1}=\tilde{\mathrm{d}}(\xi_{0}\mathbin{\lrcorner}e^{1})+\xi_{0}\mathbin{\lrcorner}\tilde{\mathrm{d}}e^{1}=2\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}

where we’ve used

\mathrm{d}e^{1}=-2\frac{\mathrm{d}x^{\alpha\beta}\iota_{\alpha}\lambda_{\beta}}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle}e^{0}=-\frac{2\langle\bar{\lambda}\iota\rangle}{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle\langle\lambda\bar{\lambda}\rangle}e^{0}e^{1}+2e^{0}\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}\,.

(B.37)

The equations (
B.36
) imply that
\tilde{\mathrm{d}}\xi_{0}=2\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}\otimes\xi_{1}
, so that

[\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{1,0}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}}]=2\mathcal{L}^{1,0}_{\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}\otimes\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{1}}\,.

(B.38)

In the second term of equation (
B.35
) we integrate by parts with respect to
\mathcal{L}^{1,0}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}}
. Taking care to note that
\mathcal{L}^{1,0}_{\xi_{0}}\Omega=-2h\Omega
equation (
B.35
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{PS}}\Omega\,\big(2D\mathcal{A}(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{1}}\mathcal{A}+2Dh\mathcal{A}\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}\mathcal{A}(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})-D\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}}\mathcal{A}\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}\mathcal{A}(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})

(B.39)

\displaystyle-D\mathcal{A}\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}\mathcal{A}\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}}(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})\big)\,.

The second and final term can safely be absorbed into
f
, and the third natural combines with one of the terms in the first line of (
B.31
). The first term has the form required to cancel the final unwanted contribution in (
B.28
).

Putting this all together the variation of the kinetic term is

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{PS}}\Omega\,\Big(g\mathcal{A}\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}\mathcal{A}+(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})\big((B-3D)\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}}\mathcal{A}+(C-\tfrac{3}{2}E)(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}\big)\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}\mathcal{A}

(B.40)

\displaystyle-D\mathcal{A}\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}}\mathcal{A}\mathcal{L}^{0,1}_{r}\mathcal{A}-E\mathcal{A}(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}\mathcal{L}^{0,1}_{r}\mathcal{A}+2D\mathcal{A}(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{1}}\mathcal{A}\Big)

where

g=f+D[r\mathbin{\lrcorner},\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}}]\mathcal{A}+(2D-E)h(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})\,.

(B.41)

In order for (
B.40
) to cancel against (
B.28
) we should take

B=-3\,,\qquad C=-3\,,\qquad D=-1\,,\qquad E=-2\,.

(B.42)

Furthermore, we have
g=0
implying
f=0
since

[r\mathbin{\lrcorner},\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}}]\mathcal{A}=[r,\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}]\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A}=[\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{1},\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}]\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A}-[\partial_{1},\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}]\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A}=0\,.

(B.43)

Here we’ve used the fact that
[\partial_{1},\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}]=0
and
[\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{1},\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}]
is a
(1,0)
vector field in the holomorphic frame and therefore contracts to zero against
\mathcal{A}
.

Therefore performing the field redefinition

\mathcal{A}\mapsto\mathcal{A}-3(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})\mathcal{L}_{\langle\lambda\iota\rangle^{2}\xi_{0}}\mathcal{A}-3(r\mathbin{\lrcorner}\mathcal{A})^{2}\tilde{e}^{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}+\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{A}^{3})

(B.44)

we recover the Poisson-Chern-Simons Lagrangian in the holomorphic frame up to cubic order (
B.6
). Inserting this field redefinition in the vertex will create a new term quartic in
\mathcal{A}
which can be cancelled by including the cubic field correction in the kinetic term, and so on.

The non-linear field redefinition (
B.44
) will necessitate modifications to our boundary conditions on the locus
\lambda\sim\bar{\lambda}
, and it appears these can generate more severe singularities. Of course, by definition the theory will be equivalent to that in the constant frame and the action will be finite. Nevertheless, we find it unsatisfying that using a distribution adapted to the geometry of the minitwistor correspondence yields such complicated boundary conditions.

Following
[
28
]
, we believe the best way around this is to define non-commutative
5d
Chern-Simons theory in Euclidean signature from the beginning. An advantage of this perspective is that the correspondence space
\mathbb{PS}_{\mathbb{E}}
of Euclidean
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is honestly transversally holomorphically foliated over
{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}
, so none of the issues generated by the locus
\lambda\sim\bar{\lambda}
arise. Whilst it may seem very strange to define KP theory in Euclidean signature, the minitwistor correspondence allows the recovery of Lorentzian data: to compute amplitudes we can lift minitwistor representatives for scatting states to
\mathbb{PS}_{\mathbb{E}}
; Lorentzian correlators are obtained by lifting holomorphic surface defects wrapping minitwistor lines to two-dimensional defects on correspondence space. Topological invariance along the fibres of
\mathbb{PS}_{\mathbb{E}}\to{\mathrm{m}\mathbb{T}}
ensures that the choice of pullback is immaterial, except where defects and states meet. We we leave a more thorough analysis of this perspective to future work.

Appendix C
Vanishing of the Four-Point Tree Amplitude in KP

In this section we verify that the four-point tree amplitude in KP theory

A_{\text{KP}}^{4}=\frac{2(\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2}+\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2})(\langle 3\iota\rangle^{2}+\langle 4\iota\rangle^{2})}{\langle 12\rangle^{2}+\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{4}\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2}\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2}(\langle 1\iota\rangle^{2}+\langle 2\iota\rangle^{2})^{2}}+\text{cyclic permutations of \lx@text@lbrace 2,3,4\lx@text@rbrace}\,.

(C.1)

vanishes to all orders in the non-commutativity parameter
\mathfrak{q}^{2}
.

First we express momentum conservation in terms of the spinor helicity variables
\kappa_{i}
. Recall that
k_{i,\alpha\beta}=\kappa_{i,\alpha}\kappa_{i,\beta}-\tfrac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{12}\iota_{\alpha}\iota_{\beta}\langle\kappa\iota\rangle^{6}
, so momentum conservation reads

\sum_{i=1}^{4}k_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{4}\Big(\kappa_{i,\alpha}\kappa_{i,\beta}-\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{12}\iota_{\alpha}\iota_{\beta}\langle i\iota\rangle^{6}\Big)=0\,.

(C.2)

It’ll be useful to introduce some notation for the kinematic invariants on which the amplitude depends. We write
r_{i}=\langle i\iota\rangle^{2}
for the Lorentz violating invariants. Contracting equation (
C.2
) with
\iota^{\alpha}\iota^{\beta}
and
\iota^{\alpha}\kappa_{4}^{\beta}
gives two constraints involving the
r_{i}

r_{1}+r_{2}+r_{3}+r_{4}=0\,,\qquad\langle 1\iota\rangle\langle 14\rangle+\langle 2\iota\rangle\langle 24\rangle+\langle 3\iota\rangle\langle 34\rangle=0\,.

(C.3)

These are the same as we’d find in the case of dKP; however, the Lorentz invariant constraints are modified. In particular

(k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3})^{2}=k_{4}^{2}=-\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{6}k_{4,+}^{4}=-\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{6}(k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3})_{+}^{4}\,.

(C.4)

Defining deformed ‘Mandelstam’ variables

\displaystyle

\displaystyle s_{\mathfrak{q}}=\langle 12\rangle^{2}+\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{4}r_{1}r_{2}(r_{1}+r_{2})^{2}\,,\qquad t_{\mathfrak{q}}=\langle 31\rangle^{2}+\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{4}r_{3}r_{1}(r_{3}+r_{1})^{2}

(C.5)

\displaystyle u_{\mathfrak{q}}=\langle 23\rangle^{2}+\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{4}r_{2}r_{3}(r_{2}+r_{3})^{2}\,.

we can rewrite the constraint (
C.4
) as

s_{\mathfrak{q}}+t_{\mathfrak{q}}+u_{\mathfrak{q}}=\mathfrak{q}^{2}r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}r_{4}\,.

(C.6)

Momentum conservation implies that we could, for example, have defined
s_{\mathfrak{q}}
through the momenta
k_{3},k_{4}

\displaystyle s_{\mathfrak{q}}=\frac{1}{2}(k_{1}+k_{2})^{2}+\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{12}(k_{1}+k_{2})_{+}^{4}=\frac{1}{2}(k_{3}+k_{4})^{2}+\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{12}(k_{3}+k_{4})_{+}^{4}

(C.7)

\displaystyle=\langle 34\rangle^{2}+\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{4}r_{3}r_{4}(r_{3}+r_{4})^{2}\,.

Expressed in terms of these variables the amplitude (
C.1
) is

\displaystyle

\displaystyle A_{\text{KP}}^{4}=-\frac{2}{s_{\mathfrak{q}}t_{\mathfrak{q}}u_{\mathfrak{q}}}\big(t_{\mathfrak{q}}u_{\mathfrak{q}}(r_{1}+r_{2})^{2}+s_{\mathfrak{q}}u_{\mathfrak{q}}(r_{3}+r_{1})^{2}+s_{\mathfrak{q}}t_{\mathfrak{q}}(r_{2}+r_{3})^{2}\big)

(C.8)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{s_{\mathfrak{q}}t_{\mathfrak{q}}u_{\mathfrak{q}}}\big(t_{\mathfrak{q}}^{2}(r_{1}+r_{2})^{2}+s_{\mathfrak{q}}^{2}(r_{3}+r_{1})^{2}-2s_{\mathfrak{q}}t_{\mathfrak{q}}(r_{1}r_{4}+r_{2}r_{3})\big)

\displaystyle-\frac{2\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{s_{\mathfrak{q}}t_{\mathfrak{q}}u_{\mathfrak{q}}}r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}r_{4}\big(t_{\mathfrak{q}}(r_{1}+r_{2})^{2}+s_{\mathfrak{q}}(r_{3}+r_{1})^{2}\big)\,,

where we have eliminated
u_{\mathfrak{q}}
in the numerator using (
C.6
). We could also eliminate
r_{4}
, but for the moment retain it for compactness. To simplify this further we make use of the second constraint in equation (
C.3
); rearranging and squaring once gives

r_{2}\langle 24\rangle^{2}+r_{3}\langle 34\rangle^{2}+2\langle 2\iota\rangle\langle 3\iota\rangle\langle 24\rangle\langle 34\rangle=r_{1}\langle 14\rangle^{2}\,,

(C.9)

or in terms of our modified Mandelstam variables

\displaystyle 2\langle 2\iota\rangle\langle 3\iota\rangle\langle 24\rangle\langle 34\rangle=r_{1}u_{\mathfrak{q}}-r_{2}t_{\mathfrak{q}}-r_{3}s_{\mathfrak{q}}

(C.10)

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{4}r_{4}\big(r_{1}^{2}(r_{1}+r_{4})^{2}-r_{2}^{2}(r_{2}+r_{4})^{2}-r_{3}^{2}(r_{3}+r_{4})^{2}\big)\,.

Eliminating
u_{\mathfrak{q}}
using (
C.6
) this is

\displaystyle 2\langle 2\iota\rangle\langle 3\iota\rangle\langle 24\rangle\langle 34\rangle=-(r_{1}+r_{2})t_{\mathfrak{q}}-(r_{3}+r_{1})s_{\mathfrak{q}}-\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{2}}{2}r_{2}r_{3}r_{4}(r_{1}r_{4}-r_{2}r_{3})\,.

(C.11)

Squaring a second time

\displaystyle 4r_{2}r_{3}s_{\mathfrak{q}}t_{\mathfrak{q}}-\mathfrak{q}^{2}r_{2}r_{3}r_{4}\big(s_{\mathfrak{q}}r_{2}(r_{2}+r_{4})^{2}+t_{\mathfrak{q}}r_{3}(r_{3}+r_{4})^{2}\big)+\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{4}}{4}r_{2}^{2}r_{3}^{3}r_{4}^{2}(r_{2}+r_{4})^{2}(r_{3}+r_{4})^{2}

(C.12)

\displaystyle=\big((r_{1}+r_{2})t_{\mathfrak{q}}+(r_{3}+r_{1})s_{\mathfrak{q}}\big)^{2}+\mathfrak{q}^{2}r_{2}r_{3}r_{4}(r_{1}r_{4}-r_{2}r_{3})\big((r_{1}+r_{2})t_{\mathfrak{q}}+(r_{3}+r_{1})s_{\mathfrak{q}}\big)

\displaystyle+\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{4}}{4}r_{2}^{2}r_{3}^{2}r_{4}^{2}(r_{1}r_{4}-r_{2}r_{3})^{2}\,.

The terms of order

(r_{2}+r_{4})(r_{3}+r_{4})=r_{2}r_{3}-r_{1}r_{4}
. Rearranging the leftovers

\displaystyle(r_{1}+r_{2})^{2}t_{\mathfrak{q}}^{2}+(r_{3}+r_{1})^{2}s_{\mathfrak{q}}^{2}-2s_{\mathfrak{q}}t_{\mathfrak{q}}(r_{1}r_{4}+r_{2}r_{3})

(C.13)

\displaystyle-\mathfrak{q}^{2}r_{1}r_{2}r_{3}r_{4}\big(s_{\mathfrak{q}}(r_{1}+r_{3})^{2}+t_{\mathfrak{q}}(r_{1}+r_{2})^{2}\big)=0\,,

so the two terms in equation (

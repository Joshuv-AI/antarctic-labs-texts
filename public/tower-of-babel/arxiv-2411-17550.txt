Global Weyl modules for thin Lie algebras are finite-dimensional
Vladimir Dotsenko, Sergey Mozgovoy

Abstract
. The notion of Weyl modules, both local and global, goes back to Chari and Pressley in the case of affine Lie algebras, and has been extensively studied for various Lie algebras graded by root systems. We extend that definition to a certain class of Lie algebras graded by weight lattices and prove that if such a Lie algebra satisfies a natural “thinness” condition, then already the global Weyl modules are finite-dimensional. Our motivating example of a thin Lie algebra is the Lie algebra of polynomial Hamiltonian vector fields on the plane vanishing at the origin. We also introduce stratifications of categories of modules over such Lie algebras and identify the corresponding strata categories.

More precisely, one can talk about global Weyl modules and local

-modules that are integrable over

\lambda

All the examples above share a common feature: the algebra

[

in a way that the adjoint action of

{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
is integrable, so that
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
is graded by the weight lattice of
\mathfrak{g}
. We shall however assume that all nonzero weights of the adjoint action
\mathfrak{g}
on
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
are either positive or negative; for instance, this way one can consider various subalgebras of the Lie algebra of vector fields on the plane that preserves the origin, or the Lie algebras

Our original motivation came from thinking about the Lie algebra

of polynomial Hamiltonian vector fields on the plane. Several different constructions of

It is probably reasonable to speculate that at least some of these actions emerge because the Lie algebra

is very close to the Lie algebra of the group of polynomial automorphisms of the

The Lie algebra
\mathsf{H}_{2}
has no nontrivial finite-dimensional representations; however, it has an important Lie subalgebra

of Hamiltonian vector fields preserving the origin. That latter Lie algebra acts on finite-dimensional vector spaces of global sections of the above-mentioned vector bundles over
the punctual Hilbert schemes; moreover, the vector spaces

. Namely, let us call the Lie algebra
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}

\mathfrak{g}

If the adjoint action of

If

2.
Integrable and bounded modules

2.1.
Notation

Throughout the paper, we use the following notation.
We denote by
\mathfrak{g}
a semisimple Lie algebra with a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{g}
, a root system
\Delta=\Delta_{+}\sqcup\Delta_{-}\subset\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, coroots
\alpha^{\vee}\in\mathfrak{h}
for
\alpha\in\Delta
, and Chevalley generators
(e_{i},f_{i},h_{i})_{i=1}^{r}
.
We let
Q=\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}\mathbb{Z}\alpha
and
P=\left\{\left.\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}\ \right|\left\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\right\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}\ \forall\alpha\in\Delta\right\}
be, respectively, the root lattice and the weight lattice of
\mathfrak{g}
. Furthermore, let
Q_{+}=\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\alpha
and

P_{+}=\left\{\left.\lambda\in P\ \right|\left\langle\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\right\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\ \forall\alpha\in\Delta_{+}\right\}

be, respectively, the positive root monoid and the dominant weight monoid.
The monoid
P_{+}
is contained in the positive root cone

Q^{\mathbb{Q}}_{+}=\sum\nolimits_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}}\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\alpha
.
We shall consider the partial order on
P
defined by
\lambda\leq\mu
if
\mu-\lambda\in Q^{\mathbb{Q}}_{+}
.
This poset is locally finite since the intervals

P_{\lambda,\mu}=\left\{\left.\nu\in P\ \right|\lambda\leq\nu\leq\mu\right\}=P\cap(\lambda+Q^{\mathbb{Q}}_{+})\cap(\mu-Q^{\mathbb{Q}}_{+})

are finite.
In particular, the set

P_{+}^{\leq\lambda}=\left\{\left.\mu\in P_{+}\ \right|\mu\leq\lambda\right\}\subset P_{0,\lambda}
is finite.
We may have
P_{+}^{\leq\lambda}\neq\varnothing
for
\lambda\not\in P_{+}
; for instance, if we consider
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
, so that
Q=\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{1}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{2}

with
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1})=(\alpha_{2},\alpha_{2})=2
,
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})=-1
and
P=\mathbb{Z}\omega_{1}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\omega_{2}
,
where
\omega_{1}=\frac{1}{3}(2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})
and

\omega_{2}=\frac{1}{3}(\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2})
, we have
\omega_{1}\in P_{+}^{\leq\lambda}
for

\lambda=3\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}
.

2.2.
Integrable modules

A
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V
is called
weighted
if
V=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}}V_{\lambda}
, where

V_{\lambda}=\left\{\left.v\in V\ \right|hv=\lambda(h)v\ \forall h\in\mathfrak{h}\right\}
is the weight
\lambda
subspace.
A weighted
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V
is called
integrable

[
25
, §3.6]
if all
e_{i}
and
f_{i}
are locally-nilpotent on
V
.
One can show
[
25
, §3.8]
that integrability is equivalent to either of the following properties:

•

Every vector of
V
is contained in a finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule.

•

V
is a direct sum of finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{g}
-modules.

Note that under these conditions we have
P(V)=\left\{\left.\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}\ \right|V_{\lambda}\neq 0\right\}\subset P
.

For a weight
\lambda\in P
, a weighted
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V
is called
\lambda
-bounded
if
P(V)\subset\lambda-Q^{\mathbb{Q}}_{+}
. Furthermore, such module is said to be
bounded
if
P(V)\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}(\lambda_{i}-Q^{\mathbb{Q}}_{+})
for finitely many weights
\lambda_{i}\in P
.
Note that an integrable
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V
is
\lambda
-bounded if and only if
\mu\leq\lambda
for every irreducible summand
L(\mu)
of
V
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For a weighted
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V
the following conditions are equivalent

(1)

P(V)
is finite.

(2)

V
is integrable and bounded.

Proof.

The implication (1)
\Rightarrow
(2) is trivial. Assuming (2), we consider the action of the Weyl group
W
on
P(V)
.
Let
w_{0}\in W
be the longest element so that
w_{0}(Q_{+})=-Q_{+}
.
Then
P(V)\subset\bigcup_{i,j}(w_{0}\lambda_{i}+Q^{\mathbb{Q}}_{+})\cap(\lambda_{j}-Q^{\mathbb{Q}}_{+})

and the sets
P_{\lambda,\mu}=P\cap(\lambda+Q^{\mathbb{Q}}_{+})\cap(\mu-Q^{\mathbb{Q}}_{+})
are finite.
∎

In what follows, we denote by
\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}(\mathfrak{g})
the category of integrable
\mathfrak{g}
-modules, by
\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{g})
the category of bounded
\mathfrak{g}
-modules, and by
\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}_{\mathsf{b}}(\mathfrak{g})
the category of integrable and bounded
\mathfrak{g}
-modules.
The BGG category
\mathcal{O}\subset\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{g})
consists of modules having finite-dimensional weight spaces.
The intersection
\mathcal{O}\cap\mathcal{I}(\mathfrak{g})
consists of finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{g}
-modules.
For
V\in\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{g})
, let
\operatorname{\mathbf{I}}(V)
denote the maximal integrable quotient of
V
.
In view of Lemma
2.1
, this module may be constructed as follows. Since
V
is bounded, we have
P(V)\subset\bigcup_{i}(\lambda_{i}-Q^{\mathbb{Q}}_{+})
for finitely many
\lambda_{i}\in P
,
and we may consider the finite set
\Omega=\bigcup_{i,j}P_{w_{0}\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j}}

and define
\operatorname{\mathbf{I}}(V)
as the quotient of
V
by the
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule generated by
\bigoplus_{\mu\notin\Omega}V_{\mu}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

The maximal integrable quotient functor
\operatorname{\mathbf{I}}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\mathchar 58\relax}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{g})\to\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}_{\mathsf{b}}(\mathfrak{g})
is left adjoint to the embedding functor
\imath\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\mathchar 58\relax}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}_{\mathsf{b}}(\mathfrak{g})\to\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Proof.

Let
V
be a bounded module and
U
be an integrable and bounded module. Consider a morphism
f\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\mathchar 58\relax}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0muV\to U
. The module
V^{\prime}=f(V)\subset U
is integrable and bounded, hence the map
f
factors through the maximal integrable quotient of
V
. Thus, we have

\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}_{\mathsf{b}}}(\operatorname{\mathbf{I}}(V),U)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}}(V,\imath(U))=\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathcal{B}}}(V,U),

as desired.
∎

For
\lambda\in P_{+}
, let
L(\lambda)\in\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}(\mathfrak{g})
denote the irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-module of highest weight
\lambda
.
Every
V\in\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}(\mathfrak{g})
can be written in the form
V=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in P_{+}}V^{(\lambda)}\otimes L(\lambda)
, where
V^{(\lambda)}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak{g}}(L(\lambda),V)
.
The number
[V\mathchar 58\relax L(\lambda)]=\dim V^{(\lambda)}
is the
multiplicity
of
L(\lambda)
in
V
.

3.
Finite-dimensionality of global Weyl modules

We now fix the following set-up that will be assumed throughout the paper. Let
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
be a Lie algebra containing
\mathfrak{g}
as a subalgebra and assume that the adjoint action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
is integrable.
Let

\bar{}\mathfrak{h}={\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}_{0},\qquad\bar{}\mathfrak{n}_{-}=\bigoplus_{\lambda<0}{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}_{\lambda},\qquad\bar{}\mathfrak{n}_{+}=\bigoplus_{\lambda>0}{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}_{\lambda},

and assume that

{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}=\bar{}\mathfrak{n}_{-}\oplus\bar{}\mathfrak{h}\oplus\bar{}\mathfrak{n}_{+}
.

Example 3.1
.

As indicated in the introduction, our original motivating example was the Lie algebra
L_{0}(\mathsf{H}_{2})
of polynomial Hamiltonian vector fields on the plane vanishing at the origin.
The algebra
\mathsf{H}_{2}
is the Lie algebra
(with
\partial_{x}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}
,
\partial_{y}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y}
,
f_{x}=\partial_{x}f
,
f_{y}=\partial_{y}f
)

\mathsf{H}_{2}=\left\{\left.X_{f}=f_{x}\partial_{y}-f_{y}\partial_{x}\ \right|f\in\mathbb{C}[x,y]\right\}.

This Lie algebra has a grading given by
\operatorname{deg}X_{f}=\operatorname{deg}f-2
, and the algebra
L_{0}(\mathsf{H}_{2})\subset\mathsf{H}_{2}
consists of vector fields of non-negative degree.
We can identify
L_{0}(\mathsf{H}_{2})
with
\mathbb{C}[x,y]_{\geq 2}
(spanned by homogeneous polynomials of degree
\geq 2
) equipped with the Lie bracket

[f,g]=f_{x}g_{y}-f_{y}g_{x}.

The degree zero component of
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}_{(i)}=L_{0}(\mathsf{H}_{2})
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}

\mathfrak{sl}_{2}=\mathbb{C}\left\{e,f,h\right\}\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}_{(0)},\qquad e\mapsto x\partial_{y},\quad f\mapsto y\partial_{x},\quad h\mapsto x\partial_{x}-y\partial_{y}.

The adjoint action of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
on
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
is integrable.
In fact, we have an isomorphism of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-modules
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}_{(i)}\simeq L(i+2)
for all
i\geq 0
.
The algebra
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
has a basis

e_{ij}=x^{i+1}y^{j+1}
, for
i,j\geq 1
and
i+j\geq 0
,
with the bracket
[e_{ij},e_{kl}]=\begin{vmatrix}i+1&j+1\\
k+1&l+1\end{vmatrix}e_{i+k,j+l}
.
In particular,
h=-e_{00}
and
[e_{00},e_{ij}]=(j-i)e_{ij}
, hence
e_{ij}
has weight
i-j
and degree
i+j
.
Note that
\bar{}\mathfrak{h}=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\mathbb{C}e_{ii}
is an abelian subalgebra.

Remark 3.2
.

In the case of a Lie algebra
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
integrable over
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
(like
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}=L_{0}(\mathsf{H}_{2})
), the assumption
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}=\bar{}\mathfrak{n}_{-}\oplus\bar{}\mathfrak{h}\oplus\bar{}\mathfrak{n}_{+}
is automatically satisfied since
P\simeq\mathbb{Z}
. This phenomenon does not persist for other Lie algebras of vector fields from the Cartan classification
[
32
]
. In particular, it is easy to check that for each of the two natural generalizations of the Lie algebra
\mathsf{H}_{2}
, namely the Lie algebra
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}=\mathsf{H}_{2n}
of polynomial Hamiltonian vector fields on the
2n
-dimensional affine space (with its subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
) and the Lie algebra
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}=\mathsf{S}_{n+1}
of polynomial vector fields of zero divergence on the
(n+1)
-dimensional affine space (with its subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
), the direct sum
\bar{}\mathfrak{n}_{-}\oplus\bar{}\mathfrak{h}\oplus\bar{}\mathfrak{n}_{+}
is a proper subalgebra of
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
for
n>1
. It is tempting to find a way to separate weights into positive and negative, for instance to fix a vector
x\in\mathfrak{h}
for which
\langle\alpha,x\rangle>0
for all
\alpha\in\Delta_{+}
and
\langle\lambda,x\rangle=0
for
\lambda\in P
implies
\lambda=0
, consider the decomposition

{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}=\bigg(\bigoplus_{\langle\lambda,x\rangle<0}{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}_{\lambda}\bigg)\oplus{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}_{0}\oplus\bigg(\bigoplus_{\langle\lambda,x\rangle>0}{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}_{\lambda}\bigg)

and then modify the partial order on the weight lattice using
x
-positive weights appearing in
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}

(cf.
[
26
]
).
However, already for the Lie algebra
\mathsf{H}_{4}
the corresponding partial order is not locally finite, so a generalization of our results to that case is far from straightforward.

The following definition of the global Weyl module usually appears in the literature.

Definition 3.3
.

For
\lambda\in P
, the
global Weyl module

W(\lambda)
is the
maximal
\mathfrak{g}
-integrable
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
-module generated by the vector
v_{\lambda}
of weight
\lambda

such that
\bar{}\mathfrak{n}_{+}v_{\lambda}=0
.

For example, if
A
is a unital commutative algebra, we may consider the Lie algebra
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{g}\otimes A
, for which the global Weyl modules
W(\lambda)
are the modules introduced in
[
16
, Def. 3]
. Note that if
W(\lambda)\neq 0
, then
\lambda
is dominant. Indeed,
W(\lambda)
is integrable and
\lambda\in P(W(\lambda))
. We have
\lambda+\alpha_{i}\notin P(V)
, hence
\left\langle\lambda,\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\right\rangle\geq 0
by
[
25
, Cor. 3.6]
.

3.1.
Truncations

We shall now define functors which give a categorical context for Weyl modules.
For
V\in\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}(\mathfrak{g})
, we define the
\lambda
-truncation

\tau_{\leq\lambda}(V)
and the
complementary
\lambda
-truncation

\bar{}\tau_{\leq\lambda}(V)
by the formulas

\tau_{\leq\lambda}(V)=\bigoplus_{\mu\leq\lambda}V^{(\mu)}\otimes L(\mu),\qquad\bar{}\tau_{\leq\lambda}(V)=\bigoplus_{\mu\not\leq\lambda}V^{(\mu)}\otimes L(\mu).

Let
\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})
denote the category of
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
-modules that are integrable over
\mathfrak{g}
. The forgetful functor
\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})\to\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}(\mathfrak{g})
has the left adjoint

\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\mathchar 58\relax}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}(\mathfrak{g})\to\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}),\qquad V\mapsto U({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{g})}V.

Let
\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}_{\leq\lambda}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})
denote the full subcategory of
\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})
consisting of modules
V
such that
V=\tau_{\leq\lambda}V
.
The embedding functor
\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}_{\leq\lambda}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})
has the left adjoint

\tau_{\leq\lambda}^{{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\mathchar 58\relax}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})\to\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}_{\leq\lambda}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}),\qquad V\mapsto V/U({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})\bar{}\tau_{\leq\lambda}V.

The composition

\operatorname{\mathbf{B}}_{\leq\lambda}=\tau_{\leq\lambda}^{{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}}\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\mathchar 58\relax}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}(\mathfrak{g})\to\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}_{\leq\lambda}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})

is left adjoint to the forgetful functor

\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}_{\leq\lambda}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})\to\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}(\mathfrak{g})
.

As we shall see below, all our proofs only use the fact that the set
P_{+}^{\leq\lambda}
is finite. In fact, the proofs become somewhat shorter and cleaner if we adopt that level of generality and define,
for any finite set
\Omega\subset P_{+}
,

\tau_{\Omega}(V)=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Omega}V^{(\mu)}\otimes L(\mu),\qquad\bar{}\tau_{\Omega}(V)=\bigoplus_{\mu\not\in\Omega}V^{(\mu)}\otimes L(\mu).

We denote by
\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}_{\Omega}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})\subset\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})
the category of objects
V\in\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})

such that
V=\tau_{\Omega}(V)
.
The embedding functor
\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}_{\Omega}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})
has the left adjoint

(1)

\tau_{\Omega}^{{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\mathchar 58\relax}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})\to\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}_{\Omega}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}),\qquad V\mapsto V/U({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})\bar{}\tau_{\Omega}V.

We consider the composition

(2)

\operatorname{\mathbf{B}}_{\Omega}=\tau_{\Omega}^{{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}}\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak{g}}^{{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\mathchar 58\relax}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}(\mathfrak{g})\to\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}_{\Omega}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}).

Note that a left adjoint functor maps projective objects to projective objects.
Therefore all objects in the image of
\operatorname{\mathbf{B}}_{\Omega}
(in particular, in the image of
\operatorname{\mathbf{B}}_{\leq\lambda}
) are projective.
The following result relates the classical definition of Weyl modules to the functor
\operatorname{\mathbf{B}}_{\leq\lambda}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

There is an isomorphism of
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
-modules

W(\lambda)\simeq\operatorname{\mathbf{B}}_{\leq\lambda}(L(\lambda)).

Proof.

Consider the Lie algebras
\bar{}\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\bar{}\mathfrak{n}_{+}

and
\bar{}\mathfrak{b}_{-}=\bar{}\mathfrak{h}\oplus\bar{}\mathfrak{n}_{-}
.
Let
\bar{}\mathfrak{b}
act on
\mathbb{C}v_{\lambda}
through
\bar{}\mathfrak{b}\to\mathfrak{h}\xrightarrow{\lambda}\mathbb{C}

and let
N(\lambda)=\operatorname{Ind}_{\bar{}\mathfrak{b}}^{{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}}(\mathbb{C}v_{\lambda})
. We have
\bar{}\mathfrak{b}+\bar{}\mathfrak{b}_{-}={\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
, hence
N(\lambda)
is generated by
v_{\lambda}
under the action of
U(\bar{}\mathfrak{b}_{-})
. Therefore
N(\lambda)
is
\lambda
-bounded, and we have
W(\lambda)=\operatorname{\mathbf{I}}(N(\lambda))
by Definition
3.3
.
On the other hand, we note that the module
W(\lambda)
is integrable and
\lambda
-bounded, hence its irreducible summands
L(\mu)
satisfy
\mu\leq\lambda
.
The highest weight vector
v_{\lambda}\in N(\lambda)
induces a morphism
L(\lambda)\to N(\lambda)
of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules, hence
a surjective morphism

\operatorname{\mathbf{B}}_{\leq\lambda}(L(\lambda))=\tau_{\leq\lambda}^{\overline{\mathfrak{g}}}\operatorname{Ind}^{\overline{\mathfrak{g}}}_{\mathfrak{g}}L(\lambda)\to W(\lambda).

of
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
-modules. The module
\operatorname{\mathbf{B}}_{\leq\lambda}(L(\lambda))
is integrable, generated by
v_{\lambda}
and is
\lambda
-bounded, hence
\bar{}\mathfrak{n}_{+}v_{\lambda}=0
.
By the maximality of
W(\lambda)
, we conclude that the surjective map
\operatorname{\mathbf{B}}_{\leq\lambda}(L(\lambda))\to W(\lambda)
is an isomorphism.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

For all
V\in\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}_{\leq\lambda}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})
, we have

\operatorname{Hom}_{{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}}(W(\lambda),V)\simeq V_{\lambda}.

Proof.

Indeed,

\operatorname{Hom}_{{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}}(W(\lambda),V)=\operatorname{Hom}_{{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}}(\operatorname{\mathbf{B}}_{\leq\lambda}L(\lambda),V)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak{g}}(L(\lambda),V)\simeq V_{\lambda}
.
∎

The following lemma gives an explicit construction of global Weyl modules. It is useful for doing explicit computations with those modules; for instance, it is this definition that we implemented in
Magma
to compute various examples throughout the paper. However, we shall see that for theoretical purposes, one can entirely bypass this construction and only use the universal properties of the Weyl modules.

Lemma 3.6
.

The global Weyl module
W(\lambda)
is isomorphic to the cyclic module with a cyclic vector
v_{\lambda}
such that

\bar{}\mathfrak{n}_{+}v_{\lambda}=0,\qquad h(v_{\lambda})=\lambda(h)v_{\lambda}\ \forall h\in\mathfrak{h},\qquad f_{i}^{\lambda(h_{i})+1}v_{\lambda}=0\ \forall i=1,\ldots,r.

Proof.

This immediately follows from the standard fact that
L(\lambda)
is the quotient of
U(\mathfrak{g})
by the left ideal generated by

\mathfrak{n}_{+}v_{\lambda}=0,\qquad h(v_{\lambda})=\lambda(h)v_{\lambda}\ \forall h\in\mathfrak{h},\qquad f_{i}^{\lambda(h_{i})+1}v_{\lambda}=0\ \forall i=1,\ldots,r.

∎

3.3.
Main finite-dimensionality result

The category
\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}(\mathfrak{g})
is closed under tensor product, hence it makes sense to talk about algebras in this category.
For an associative algebra
A
in
\mathcal{I}(\mathfrak{g})
, let
\operatorname{Mod}(A,\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}(\mathfrak{g}))
be the category of left
A
-modules in
\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}(\mathfrak{g})
and

\operatorname{Mod}(A,\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}_{\Omega}(\mathfrak{g}))
be the category of left
A
-modules in
\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}_{\Omega}(\mathfrak{g})
, for a finite subset
\Omega\subset P_{+}
.
The embedding functor
\operatorname{Mod}(A,\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}_{\Omega}(\mathfrak{g}))\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Mod}(A,\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}(\mathfrak{g}))
has the left adjoint

\tau_{\Omega}^{A}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\mathchar 58\relax}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\operatorname{Mod}(A,\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}(\mathfrak{g}))\to\operatorname{Mod}(A,\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}_{\Omega}(\mathfrak{g})),\qquad M\mapsto M/A\bar{}\tau_{\Omega}(M).

For example, if
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus I
, where
I
is an ideal, then

A=U(I)
is an associative algebra in the category
\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}(\mathfrak{g})
.
We have
\operatorname{Mod}(A,\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}(\mathfrak{g}))\simeq\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})

and
\tau_{\Omega}^{A}
coincides with the truncation
\tau_{\Omega}^{{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}}
given by (
1
).

Proposition 3.7
.

If
V
is a finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{g}
-module such that
[V\mathchar 58\relax L(0)]=0
,
then the algebra
S_{\Omega}(V)=(SV)/(SV\cdot\bar{}\tau_{\Omega}(SV))
is finite-dimensional.

Proof.

The
\mathfrak{g}
-module
A=S_{\Omega}(V)
has only irreducible summands
L(\mu)
with
\mu\in\Omega
.
Therefore it has a weight decomposition
A=\bigoplus_{\mu\in P}A_{\mu}

such that
P(A)=\left\{\left.\mu\in P\ \right|A_{\mu}\neq 0\right\}
is finite.
Consider the weight decomposition
V=\bigoplus_{\mu\in P}V_{\mu}

and let
A_{(\mu)}
be the image of
S(V_{\mu})
in
A
.
If
\mu\neq 0
, then
S^{k}(V_{\mu})
is mapped to
A_{k\mu}
which is zero for
k\gg 0
.
Therefore
A_{(\mu)}
is finite-dimensional.
We have
A_{(\mu)}=\mathbb{C}
for all but a finite number of
\mu\in P
, hence
B=\prod_{\mu\neq 0}A_{(\mu)}\subset A
is finite-dimensional.

The algebra
A
is generated over the algebra
B
by the image of
V_{0}
.
Let us show that the elements of this image are integral over
B
.
The assumption
[V\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\mathchar 58\relax}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0muL(0)]=0
implies that
V_{0}
is spanned by elements
of the form
u=fv
, where
f\in\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}
and
v\in V_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in\Delta
.
The endomorphism
f
of
V
induces a derivation of
S(V)
.
This derivation preserves
\bar{}\tau_{\Omega}(SV)
and the ideal
SV\cdot\bar{}\tau_{\Omega}(SV)
(which are both
\mathfrak{g}
-modules).
Therefore
f
induces a derivation of
A
.
Let
\bar{u},\bar{v}\in A
be the images of
u,v\in V
.
The element
f^{m}(\bar{v}^{m})
is a linear combination of products

\prod_{i=1}^{m}f^{k_{i}}(\bar{v})
, where
k_{i}\geq 0
and
\sum_{i}k_{i}=m
.
We have
f^{k}(\bar{v})\in B
if
k\neq 1
,
hence

\prod_{i=1}^{m}f^{k_{i}}(\bar{v})\in B\bar{u}^{r}
,
where
r=\#\left\{\left.i\ \right|k_{i}=1\right\}
.
For
m\gg 0
we have
\bar{v}^{m}=0
, hence

0=f^{m}(\bar{v}^{m})\in m!\cdot\bar{u}^{m}+\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}B\bar{u}^{r}
.
Therefore
\bar{u}^{m}\in\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}B\bar{u}^{r}
and
\bar{u}
is integral over
B
.
This implies that the algebra
A
is generated over
B
by finitely many integral elements, hence
A
is finite over
B
.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

Consider the case
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
and suppose that
V=V^{(2)}\otimes L(2)
is the sum of several copies of the adjoint module
L(2)
. In this case, the algebra
S_{\leq\lambda}(V)
for
\lambda=2

is precisely the algebra
\mathrm{Com}^{\mathfrak{T}}(V)
from
[
13
]
, and the
finite-dimensionality of that algebra (in fact, a precise description of its underlying vector space) is established in
[
13
, Prop. 3.2]
.

The previous result motivates the following definition.

Definition 3.9
.

An integrable
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V
is said to be
thin
if the multiplicities of irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-modules in it are finite and
[V\mathchar 58\relax L(0)]=0
.

Corollary 3.10
.

If
V
is a thin
\mathfrak{g}
-module,
then the algebra
S_{\Omega}(V)

is finite-dimensional.

Proof.

Consider the decomposition
V=V^{\prime}\oplus V^{\prime\prime}
, where
V^{\prime}=\tau_{\Omega}(V)
and
V^{\prime\prime}=\bar{}\tau_{\Omega}(V)
.
Then
V^{\prime\prime}\subset\bar{}\tau_{\Omega}(SV)
, hence
\bigoplus_{k\geq 1}S^{k}(V^{\prime\prime})\subset S(V^{\prime\prime})
is mapped to zero in
S_{\Omega}(V)
.
Therefore the map
S_{\Omega}(V^{\prime})\to S_{\Omega}(V)
is surjective.
By Proposition
3.7
, the algebra
S_{\Omega}(V^{\prime})
is finite-dimensional.
∎

Theorem 3.11
.

If the adjoint action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
is thin, then the functor
\operatorname{\mathbf{B}}_{\Omega}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\mathchar 58\relax}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}(\mathfrak{g})\to\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}_{\Omega}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})
maps finite-dimensional modules to finite-dimensional modules.

Proof.

We have
\operatorname{\mathbf{B}}_{\Omega}(V)=N^{\prime}/A(\bar{}\tau_{\Omega}N^{\prime})
, where
A=U({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})
and
N^{\prime}=A\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{g})}V
.
Therefore it is enough to show that

\bar{N}=N/A(\bar{}\tau_{\Omega}N)
has finite dimension for
N=A\otimes V
.
Let
A=\bigoplus_{\mu\in P_{+}}A^{(\mu)}\otimes L(\mu)
.
The image of
A^{(\mu)}\otimes L(\mu)\otimes V
in
\bar{N}
can be nonzero only if there is a summand
L(\nu)
of
L(\mu)\otimes V
such that
\nu\in\Omega
.
We have

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak{g}}(L(\mu)\otimes V,L(\nu))\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak{g}}(L(\mu),\operatorname{Hom}(V,L(\nu))),

hence
L(\mu)
has to be a summand of
\operatorname{Hom}(V,L(\nu))

for some
\nu\in\Omega
.
The set
\Omega^{\prime}
of such
\mu\in P_{+}
is finite.
We conclude that
A\bar{}\tau_{\Omega^{\prime}}(A)\otimes V
is mapped to zero in
\bar{N}
,
hence
\bar{N}
is a quotient of
(A/A\bar{}\tau_{\Omega^{\prime}}(A))\otimes V

and it is enough to show that the
A
-module
M=A/A(\bar{}\tau_{\Omega^{\prime}}A)
is finite-dimensional.
The PBW filtration
(F_{i})_{i\geq 0}
on
A
induces the filtration
(F_{i}M)_{i\geq 0}
on
M
.
The epimorphism
A\to M
induces the epimorphism between associated graded modules
\operatorname{Gr}A\simeq S{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}\to\operatorname{Gr}M
.
We have
\bar{}\tau_{\Omega^{\prime}}M=0
, hence
\bar{}\tau_{\Omega^{\prime}}\operatorname{Gr}M=0
and the above map factorises through
S{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}\to S_{\Omega^{\prime}}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})=(S{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})/(S{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}\cdot\bar{}\tau_{\Omega^{\prime}}(S{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}))
. According to Proposition
3.7
,
S_{\Omega^{\prime}}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})
is finite-dimensional, completing the proof.
∎

3.4.
Applications of the main result

Let us give some examples of situations where our result applies. We begin with a class of examples including that of
L_{0}(\mathsf{H}_{2})
. Let us denote by
\mathsf{W}_{2}
the Lie algebra of all polynomial vector fields on the plane, and by
L_{0}(\mathsf{W}_{2})
the Lie subalgebra of that algebra consisting of all vector fields that vanish at the origin.

Corollary 3.12
.

For every Lie subalgebra
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}\subset L_{0}(\mathsf{W}_{2})
that contains

\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{C}\{x\partial_{y},y\partial_{x},x\partial_{x}-y\partial_{y}\}\simeq\mathfrak{sl}_{2}

and does not contain the Euler vector field
x\partial_{x}+y\partial_{y}
, all global Weyl modules are finite-dimensional.

Proof.

A vector field vanishing at the origin is of the form
f(x,y)\partial_{x}+g(x,y)\partial_{y}
, where
f
and
g
belong to the ideal of
\mathbb{C}[x,y]
generated by
x
and
y
. It easily follows that, if we consider
L_{0}(\mathsf{W}_{2})
as an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-module with respect to the adjoint action, we have an isomorphism of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-modules

L_{0}(\mathsf{W}_{2})\simeq L(1)\otimes\bigoplus_{k\geq 1}L(k)\simeq\bigoplus_{k\geq 1}(L(k-1)\oplus L(k+1)).

We note that each irreducible
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-module appears in this decomposition with multiplicity at most two. Moreover, this decomposition has exactly one summand
L(0)
; it appears for
k=1
, that is, for linear vector fields, and corresponds precisely to the Euler vector field.
It follows that the adjoint action of
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
on
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
is thin, and Theorem
3.11
applies.
∎

Another class of examples where our results apply is that where we consider a finite-dimensional Lie algebra containing
\mathfrak{g}
.

Corollary 3.13
.

For every finite-dimensional Lie algebra
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
containing
\mathfrak{g}
as a Lie subalgebra and graded by the root system of
\mathfrak{g}
, if the centralizer of
\mathfrak{g}
in
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
is zero, then all global Weyl modules are finite-dimensional.

Proof.

The root grading defines the necessary decomposition

{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}=\bar{}\mathfrak{n}_{-}\oplus\bar{}\mathfrak{h}\oplus\bar{}\mathfrak{n}_{+}
.
Moreover, finite-dimensionality of
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
together with the zero centralizer condition assures that the adjoint action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
is thin, so Theorem
3.11
applies.
∎

To give one final example, let us recall the definition of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}(\lambda)
introduced by Feigin
[
15
]
.
Given
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
,
consider the quotient of the universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
by the two-sided ideal generated by
\Delta-\frac{\lambda^{2}-1}{2}
, where
\Delta=ef+fe+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\in U(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
is the Casimir element.
Let
\mathfrak{gl}(\lambda)
be the Lie algebra obtained from that associative algebra by considering the usual Lie bracket
[a,b]=ab-ba
.
If
\lambda=n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, the Casimir element acts by
\frac{n^{2}-1}{2}
on the irreducible
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-module
V=L(n-1)
of dimension
n
.
Therefore we have a surjective algebra homomorphism

\mathfrak{gl}(\lambda)\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)\simeq\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
,
explaining the name
\mathfrak{gl}(\lambda)
.
Let
\mathfrak{sl}(\lambda)=\mathfrak{gl}(\lambda)/\mathbb{C}1
be the quotient of
\mathfrak{gl}(\lambda)
by the center.
Being a quotient of
U(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
, the Lie algebra
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{sl}(\lambda)
contains
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
as a Lie subalgebra.

Corollary 3.14
.

For the Lie algebra
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{sl}(\lambda)
and its Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
, all global Weyl modules are finite-dimensional.

Proof.

We have a decomposition with respect to the adjoint action of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}

[
15
]

\mathfrak{sl}(\lambda)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}L(2n),

so the adjoint action of
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
on
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
is thin, and Theorem
3.11
applies.
∎

4.
Stratifications

In this section we will equip the category
\mathcal{A}=\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}_{\mathsf{b}}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})
of
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
-modules that are integrable and bounded over
\mathfrak{g}

with a (left) stratification structure over the poset
P_{+}
.
It turns out that the strata categories
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}
of this stratification (for
\lambda\in P_{+}
)
are the categories of left modules over certain algebras
\mathsf{A}_{\lambda}
originally introduced
in
[
6
]

for the Lie algebras of the form
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{g}\otimes A
, where
A
is commutative.
These algebras were further studied in
[
30
]
for the root-graded Lie algebras
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
, where it was shown that the algebras
\mathsf{A}_{\lambda}
are isomorphic to the algebras originally introduced in
[
33
]
in the context of
\lambda
-admissible modules.
We will see that
\mathsf{A}_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{op}}
is isomorphic to the endomorphism algebra of the Weyl module
W(\lambda)
.
The Weyl functors
\mathbf{W}_{\lambda}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\mathchar 58\relax}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\operatorname{Mod}\mathsf{A}_{\lambda}\to\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})

(for the algebras
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{g}\otimes A
) have been used to produce and study new
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
-modules,
but their interpretation as building blocks of a stratification structure seems to be missing in the literature.
Using this point of view, we can interpret global Weyl modules as standard objects and local Weyl modules as proper standard objects of our stratified category
(cf.
[
26
]
).

4.1.
Left recollement

If
j^{*}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\mathchar 58\relax}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
is an exact functor between abelian categories, then the category
\mathcal{C}=\operatorname{Ker}j^{*}
is a Serre subcategory of
\mathcal{A}
and the induced functor
\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{B}
is faithful and exact

[
35
,
Tag 02MN
]
.
The functor
\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{B}
can be full even if
j^{*}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\mathchar 58\relax}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
was not.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
j^{*}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\mathchar 58\relax}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be an exact functor between abelian categories
having a fully faithful left adjoint functor
j_{!}

Then the induced functor

is an equivalence and the embedding functor

has a left adjoint

Proof.

Consider the induced functor
\varsigma^{*}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\mathchar 58\relax}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{B}
and
the composition
\varsigma_{!}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\mathchar 58\relax}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\mathcal{B}\xrightarrow{j_{!}}\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{C}
.
Then
\varsigma^{*}\varsigma_{!}\simeq\operatorname{id}
and we need to show that
\varsigma_{!}\varsigma^{*}\to\operatorname{id}
is an isomorphism
For every

, the morphism
j_{!}j^{*}X\to X
is an isomorphism in
\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{C}
since
j^{*}j_{!}j^{*}\to j^{*}X
is an isomorphism in
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{B}
is faithful and exact.

For

.
We have an exact sequence

j^{*}j_{!}j^{*}X\to j^{*}X\to j^{*}X^{\prime}\to 0,

implying that

and

.
For

hence
\operatorname{Hom}(i^{*}X,Y)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(X,Y)
.
∎

A

of abelian categories (cf.
[

, §1.4]
)
is a sequence of exact functors

between abelian categories such that

(1)

j^{*}i_{*}=0
and
i_{*}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\mathchar 58\relax}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\mathcal{C}\to\operatorname{Ker}j^{*}
is an equivalence.

(2)

j^{*}
has left and right fully faithful adjoints
j_{!},j_{*}
.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{B}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i^{!}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j_{!}}

By Lemma

, the functor

We will say that we have a

Lemma 4.3

(cf.
[
6
, Theorem 1]
)
.

An object
X\in\mathcal{A}
is contained in

if and only if

\operatorname{Hom}(X,\mathcal{C})=\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(X,\mathcal{C})=0.

Proof.

Let
X=j_{!}Z
for
Z\in\mathcal{B}
and let
Y\in\mathcal{C}
.
Then
\operatorname{Hom}(X,Y)=\operatorname{Hom}(Z,j^{*}Y)=0
.
If
0\to Y\to X^{\prime}\to X\to 0
is an exact sequence, then
j^{*}X^{\prime}\simeq j^{*}X=j^{*}j_{!}Z\simeq Z
and this isomorphism induces
X=j_{!}Z\to X^{\prime}
which splits the above exact sequence.

Conversely, assume that
X\in\mathcal{A}
satisfies
\operatorname{Hom}(X,\mathcal{C})=\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(X,\mathcal{C})=0
.
We need to show that
j_{!}j^{*}X\to X
is an isomorphism.
Consider an exact sequence
0\to Y_{1}\to j_{!}j^{*}X\to X\to Y_{2}\to 0
.
Applying
j^{*}
, we obtain an isomorpism
j^{*}j_{!}j^{*}X\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}j^{*}X
,
hence
Y_{1},Y_{2}\in\mathcal{C}
.
As
\operatorname{Hom}(X,\mathcal{C})=0
, we obtain
Y_{2}=0
.
As
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(X,\mathcal{C})=0
, we obtain
j_{!}j^{*}X=Y_{1}\oplus X
.
But
\operatorname{Hom}(j_{!}j^{*}X,Y_{1})\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(j^{*}X,j^{*}Y_{1})=0
, hence

and

j_{!}j^{*}X\simeq X
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

If
\mathcal{B}
has enough projectives,
then the functor

j_{!}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\mathchar 58\relax}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{A}
is exact if and only if

\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(j_{!}\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C})=0
.

Proof.

Let
j_{!}
be exact.
Consider an exact sequence
0\to X^{\prime}\to P\to X\to 0

in
\mathcal{B}
with projective
P
.
For
Y\in\mathcal{C}
, we have an exact sequence

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(j_{!}X^{\prime},Y)\to\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(j_{!}X,Y)\to\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(j_{!}P,Y)
.
The first term is zero by the previous lemma.
The last term is zero since
j_{!}
is left adjoint, hence maps projectives to projectives.

Conversely, given an exact sequence
0\to X^{\prime}\to X\to X^{\prime\prime}\to 0
in
\mathcal{B}
, we have an exact sequence

j_{!}X^{\prime}\xrightarrow{f}j_{!}X\to j_{!}X^{\prime\prime}\to 0

and we need to show that
f
is a monomorphism.
Let
Y_{1}=\operatorname{Ker}f
and
Y_{2}=\operatorname{Im}f
.
We have an exact sequence

0\to j^{*}Y_{1}\to j^{*}j_{!}X^{\prime}\to j^{*}j_{!}X,

hence
j^{*}Y_{1}=0
and
Y_{1}\in\mathcal{C}
.
We have an exact sequence

0=\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(j_{!}X^{\prime},Y_{1})\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(Y_{2},Y_{1})\to\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(j_{!}X^{\prime\prime},Y_{1})=0,

hence
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(Y_{2},Y_{1})=0
.
Therefore
j_{!}X^{\prime}=Y_{1}\oplus Y_{2}
.
But
\operatorname{Hom}(j_{!}X^{\prime},Y_{1})=0
, hence
Y_{1}=0
.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

If
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{A}
is a full subcategory such that
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime},\mathcal{C})=0
, then

j^{*}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\mathchar 58\relax}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{B}
is fully faithful.

is a Serre subcategory such that

.

Proof.

For
X,Y\in\mathcal{A}
, we have

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)=\varinjlim\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(X^{\prime},Y/Y^{\prime})

where the limit is taken over
X^{\prime}\subset X
with
X/X^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
and

Y^{\prime}\subset Y
with
Y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
.
If
X,Y\in\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
, then

and

, hence

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(X,Y)
.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

If
L\in\mathcal{A}\backslash\mathcal{C}
is simple, then
M=j^{*}L
is simple and
L
is a quotient of
j_{!}M
.
If

are simple and non-isomorphic,
then
j^{*}L,j^{*}L^{\prime}
are non-isomorphic.
If
\mathcal{A}
is of finite length, then there is a 1-1 correspondence between isomorphism classes of simple objects in
\mathcal{A}\backslash\mathcal{C}
and isomorphism classes of simple objects in
\mathcal{B}
.

Proof.

For a nonzero subobject
N\hookrightarrow M=j^{*}L
, the corresponding map
j_{!}N\to L
is nonzero, hence an epimorphism.
Therefore
N\simeq j^{*}j_{!}N\to j^{*}L
is an epimorphism and
N=M
.
This implies that
M
is simple.
We have seen that
j_{!}M\to L
is an epimorphism.
Let
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{A}
be the Serre subcategory generated by
L,L^{\prime}
.
Then
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{C}=0
, hence
\operatorname{Hom}(L,L^{\prime})\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(j^{*}L,j^{*}L^{\prime})
.
If
j^{*}L,j^{*}L^{\prime}
are isomorphic, then
\operatorname{Hom}(L,L^{\prime})\neq 0
, hence
L,L^{\prime}
are isomorphic.

Assume that
\mathcal{A}
is of finite length.
For a simple object
M\in\mathcal{B}
, let

be a simple quotient of

.
A nonzero map

corresponds to a nonzero map

.
We have seen that

is simple, hence
j^{*}L\simeq M
.
This implies that

induces a bijection between the corresponding sets of isomorphism classes.
∎

4.2.
Left stratifications

We define a
left stratification
of an abelian category
\mathcal{A}

by a lower finite poset
\Lambda
to be a collection of Serre subcategories
(\mathcal{A}_{\Omega})_{\Omega\subset\Lambda}
for finite lower subsets
\Omega\subset\Lambda
and a collection of abelian categories
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}
(called
strata categories
) for
\lambda\in\Lambda
such that
the collection
(\mathcal{A}_{\Omega})_{\Omega\subset\Lambda}
is order preserving (if
\Omega\subset\Omega^{\prime}
, then
\mathcal{A}_{\Omega}\subset\mathcal{A}_{\Omega^{\prime}}
),

\mathcal{A}_{\varnothing}=0
,
\bigcup_{\Omega}\mathcal{A}_{\Omega}=\mathcal{A}
, and for all finite lower subsets
\Omega\subset\Lambda
and
\lambda\in\max\Omega
we have compatible left recollements of abelian categories

\mathcal{A}_{\Omega\backslash\lambda}\xrightarrow{i_{*}}\mathcal{A}_{\Omega}\xrightarrow{j^{*}}\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}

The above data is called a
stratification
if all sequences are recollements (cf.
[
38
, §2.1]
).

Remark 4.7
.

The above conditions on a left stratification imply that,
for a finite lower subset
\Omega\subset\Lambda
, the embedding
i_{*}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\mathchar 58\relax}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\mathcal{A}_{\Omega}\to\mathcal{A}

i^{*}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\mathchar 58\relax}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}_{\Omega}
(such that
i^{*}i_{*}\simeq\operatorname{id}
).
For compatibility we require that

\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}\xrightarrow{j_{!}}\mathcal{A}_{\Omega}

is isomorphic to

\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}\xrightarrow{j_{!}^{\lambda}}\mathcal{A}_{\leq\lambda}\xrightarrow{i_{*}}\mathcal{A}_{\Omega}
,
for
\lambda\in\max\Omega
.
This implies
i^{*}j_{!}\simeq j_{!}^{\lambda}

which is equivalent to
j^{*}i_{*}\simeq j_{\lambda}^{*}
.

\mathsf{A}_{\lambda}\coloneqq U(\bar{}\mathfrak{h})/\mathsf{J}_{\lambda}\simeq\operatorname{End}_{{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}}(W(\lambda))^{\mathrm{op}}.

We see that the Weyl module

-
\mathsf{A}_{\lambda}
-bimodule
(with

-bimodule.

\mathsf{A}_{\lambda}

Let

denote the category of left modules over

.
By analogy with

Lemma 4.12
.

The functor
\mathbf{W}_{\lambda}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\mathchar 58\relax}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\operatorname{Mod}\mathsf{A}_{\lambda}\to\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}_{\leq\lambda}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})

is an isomorphism,
meaning that

Proof.

By Lemma

, we have

\mathbf{R}_{\lambda}(V)=V_{\lambda}\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}}(W(\lambda),V)

Explicitly, for

(af)(v_{\lambda})=(f\phi_{a})(v_{\lambda})=f(av_{\lambda})=av

The claim on the adjunction follows from

We have
\mathbf{R}_{\lambda}\mathbf{W}_{\lambda}(M)=(W(\lambda)\otimes_{\mathsf{A}_{\lambda}}M)_{\lambda}=W(\lambda)_{\lambda}\otimes_{\mathsf{A}_{\lambda}}M\simeq M

∎

4.5.
Full stratification

Let
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
be a graded Lie algebra as before, with finite-dimensional graded components.
Let
\operatorname{mod}^{\mathbb{Z}}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})
be the category of
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
-modules that are finite-dimensional at every degree
and let
\mathcal{A}=\operatorname{mod}^{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathsf{b}}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})=\operatorname{mod}^{\mathbb{Z}}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})\cap\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}_{\mathsf{b}}^{\mathbb{Z}}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})
and
\mathcal{A}_{\Omega}=\operatorname{mod}^{\mathbb{Z}}_{\Omega}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})=\operatorname{mod}^{\mathbb{Z}}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})\cap\operatorname{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathbb{Z}}_{\Omega}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})

for finite lower subsets
\Omega\subset\Lambda=P_{+}
.
Similarly, let
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}=\operatorname{mod}^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathsf{A}_{\lambda})
be the category of
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
\mathsf{A}_{\lambda}
-modules that are finite-dimensional at every degree.
We have
W(\lambda)\in\operatorname{mod}^{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq\lambda}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})
and the induced functor
\mathbf{W}_{\lambda}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\mathchar 58\relax}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\operatorname{mod}^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathsf{A}_{\lambda})\to\operatorname{mod}^{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq\lambda}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})
which is left adjoint to the functor
\mathbf{R}_{\lambda}\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\mathchar 58\relax}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\operatorname{mod}^{\mathbb{Z}}_{\leq\lambda}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})\to\operatorname{mod}^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathsf{A}_{\lambda})
.
We would like to construct the right adjoint of the functor
\mathbf{R}_{\lambda}
which will be a part of the (full) stratification structure on the category
\mathcal{A}=\operatorname{mod}^{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathsf{b}}({\bar{}\mathfrak{g}})
. Specifically, we shall now show that this can be done if there exists a (degree-preserving) Lie algebra automorphism
\sigma\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\mathchar 58\relax}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}\to{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
such that

. The restriction of such automorphism
\sigma
to
\mathfrak{g}={\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}_{(0)}
sends every root space
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
to
\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}
, so, up to a choice of a Chevalley basis, it is given by the Chevalley involution of

, so that
\sigma(e_{i})=-f_{i}
,
\sigma(f_{i})=-e_{i}
and
\sigma(h_{i})=-h_{i}
.

Example 4.18

As in Example
3.1
, let
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}=L_{0}(\mathsf{H}_{2})

be the Lie algebra of Hamiltonian vector fields of degree
\geq 0
.
We consider the automorphism of
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
given by

\sigma(f\partial_{x}+g\partial_{y})=f(y,-x)\partial_{y}-g(y,-x)\partial_{x}

5.
Local Weyl modules

In the case of affine algebras, map algebras, and other root-graded algebras, there is an important notion of a local Weyl module; for instance, for algebras
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
of the form
\mathfrak{g}\otimes A
with
A
commutative, local Weyl modules help to capture aspects of representation theory of
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
at different points of
\operatorname{Spec}(A)
. In this section, we discuss a general version of this definition
and relate local Weyl modules to proper standard modules introduced earlier.

5.1.
Definition and basic properties

Definition 5.1
.

For
z\in(\bar{}\mathfrak{h})^{*}
, the
local Weyl module

W(z)
is the maximal
\mathfrak{g}
-integrable
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
-module generated by the vector
v_{z}
such that

\bar{}\mathfrak{n}_{+}v_{z}=0,\qquad hv_{z}=z(h)v_{z}\quad\forall h\in\bar{}\mathfrak{h}.

The standard example of a local Weyl module
is as follows (see e.g.
[
16
, Def. 4]
).

Example 5.2
.

Let
X=\operatorname{Spec}A
be an affine scheme over
\mathbb{C}
and
\lambda=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}\omega_{i}
be a dominant weight.
Let
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{g}\otimes A
so that
\bar{}\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{h}\otimes A
.
Let
(z_{ij})_{1\leq j\leq\lambda_{i}}
be a collection of points in
X
.
We can interpret them as algebra homomorphisms

.
Consider the linear maps

and the linear map

We have
z(h_{i})=z(h_{i}\otimes 1)=\sum_{j=1}^{\lambda_{i}}z_{ij}(1)=\lambda_{i}
, hence
z|_{\mathfrak{h}}=\lambda
.
The local Weyl module
W(z)
is the
maximal
\mathfrak{g}
-integrable
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
-module generated by the vector
v_{z}
such that

(\mathfrak{n}_{+}\otimes A)v_{z}=0,\qquad(h_{i}\otimes a)v_{z}=\sum_{j=1}^{\lambda_{i}}z_{ij}(a)\cdot v_{z}\quad\forall 1\leq i\leq r,\,a\in A.

The local Weyl modules can be computed using the functor
\mathbf{W}_{\lambda}
of Section
4.3
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
z\in(\bar{}\mathfrak{h})^{*}
and
\lambda=z|_{\mathfrak{h}}\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. If
W(z)\neq 0
, then
\lambda
is a dominant weight. Moreover, we have

W(z)\simeq\mathbf{W}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{C}_{z}),

where
\mathbb{C}_{z}
is the one-dimensional
\bar{}\mathfrak{h}
-module corresponding to
z
.

Proof.

Note that
hv_{z}=\lambda(h)v_{z}
for
h\in\mathfrak{h}
, so there is a surjective
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
-module morphism
W(\lambda)\to W(z)
sending
v_{\lambda}
to
v_{z}
. Since
W(\lambda)
is non-zero only for dominant
\lambda
, the first assertion follows.
We have
W(z)_{\lambda}\simeq\mathbb{C}_{z}
as a module over
U(\bar{}\mathfrak{h})
(and
\mathsf{A}_{\lambda}
).
This isomorphism induces a surjective map
\mathbf{W}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{C}_{z})\to W(z)
.
The generator of
\mathbf{W}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{C}_{z})
satisfies the conditions of a local Weyl module, hence
\mathbf{W}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{C}_{z})\to W(z)
is an isomorphism by the maximality of
W(z)
.
∎

The main class of examples of local Weyl modules we
are interested in is as follows.

Definition 5.4
.

Let
\lambda
be a dominant weight and
\pi\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\mathchar 58\relax}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathfrak{g}
be a left inverse of the embedding of Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}\hookrightarrow{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
such that
\pi(\bar{}\mathfrak{h})=\mathfrak{h}
.
The
local augmentation Weyl module

W_{\pi}(\lambda)
is the local Weyl module corresponding to the linear function
\lambda\pi\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\mathchar 58\relax}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\bar{}\mathfrak{h}\to\mathbb{C}
.

Example 5.5
.

For a point
p\in X=\operatorname{Spec}A
(identified with the morphism
p\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\mathchar 58\relax}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0muA\to\mathbb{C}

\pi=\operatorname{id}\otimes p\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\mathchar 58\relax}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{g}\otimes A\to\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathbb{C}=\mathfrak{g}
.
The corresponding local augmentation Weyl module for
\lambda=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\omega_{i}

5.2

z(h_{i}\otimes a)=z_{i}(a)=\lambda_{i}p(a)=(\lambda\otimes p)(h_{i}\otimes a)=\lambda\pi(h_{i}\otimes a)

.

For a dominant weight
\lambda
, the irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-module
L(\lambda)
can be made into a
{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}
-module using the pullback along
\pi\penalty\mskip 2.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{\mathchar 58\relax}\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu{\bar{}\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathfrak{g}
.
This module satisfies all the conditions of
W_{\pi}(\lambda)
except maximality. Therefore, local augmentation Weyl modules are always nonzero.

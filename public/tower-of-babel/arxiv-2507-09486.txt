Estimates of heat kernels and Sobolev-type inequalities for twisted differential forms on compact Kähler manifolds
Fusheng Deng, Gang Huang, Xiangsen Qin

Abstract
. The main goal of this paper is to generalize the Sobolev-type inequalities given by Guo-Phong-Song-Sturm and Guedj-Tô from the case of functions to the framework of twisted differential forms. To this end, we establish certain estimates of heat kernels for differential forms with values in holomorphic vector bundles over compact Kähler manifolds. As applications of these estimates, we also prove a vanishing theorem and give certain L q , p L^{q,p} -estimates for the ∂ ¯ \bar{\partial} -operator on twisted differential forms.

Introduction

In the remarkable recent works
[
GPSS24
]
and
[
GT24
]
(see also
[
GPSS22
]
,
[
GPSS23
]
, and
[
GPS24
]
),
the authors establish some Sobolev-type inequalities for functions associated with a family of Kähler metrics satisfying certain entropy bounds on compact Kähler manifolds. In the present paper, we try to generalize these inequalities to the framework of differential forms with values in Hermitian holomorphic vector bundles.
However, in attempting to follow the approaches in
[
GPSS24
]
and
[
GT24
]
, one will inevitably encounter the problem of how to define the Monge-Ampére operator for
differential forms or sections of a holomorphic vector bundle.
Our strategy to circumvent this difficulty is trying to produce a heat kernel estimate for twisted differential forms
from a Sobolev-type inequality for functions.

To state the result, we first introduce the context we are studying.
Let
M
be a compact Kähler manifold of complex dimension
n
, whose volume is denoted by
|M|
.
Let
E
be a Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle of rank
r
over
M
,
\square_{p,q}
be the
\bar{\partial}
-Laplacian which acts on
C^{2}(M,\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\otimes E)
for some fixed

and let
\nabla
be the Chern connection on
\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\otimes E
.
Then the Weitzenböck curvature operator is given by

\mathfrak{Ric}_{p,q}^{E}:=2\square_{p,q}-\nabla^{*}\nabla.

We denote by
H_{p,q}(t,x,y)
the heat kernel associated to
\square_{p,q}
.
Let
b_{p,q}:=\dim\mathcal{H}_{p,q}(M,E)
be the dimension of the space
\mathcal{H}_{p,q}(M,E)
of harmonic
(p,q)
-forms on
M
with values in
E

Then the first main result can be stated as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

With the above notations, and assume that there are constants
\alpha>1,\ \beta>0,\gamma\geq 0

such that the following Sobolev-type inequality for functions is satisfied:

(
\clubsuit
)

\left(\int_{M}|f|^{2\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\leq\beta\int_{M}|\nabla f|^{2}+\gamma\int_{M}|f|^{2},\ \forall f\in C^{\infty}(M).

We also assume
\mathfrak{Ric}_{p,q}^{E}\geq-K
for some constant
K\geq 0
.
Then the following estimates hold.

(i)

There is a constant
C:=C(\alpha)>0
such that

|H_{p,q}(t,x,y)|\leq C\beta^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}e^{Kt+\frac{\gamma}{\beta}t}t^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}e^{-\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{9t}}

for all
(t,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times M\times M.

(ii)

There is a constant
C:=C(\alpha)>0
such that

\left|H_{p,q}(t,x,y)-\sum_{k=1}^{b_{p,q}}\phi_{k}(x)\otimes\phi_{k}(y)\right|\leq Cr\beta^{\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-1}}|M|e^{\left(\frac{K}{\mu_{1}}+\frac{\gamma}{\beta\mu_{1}}\right)\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-1}}t^{\frac{2\alpha}{1-\alpha}}

for all
(t,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times M\times M,

where
\mu_{1}
is the first nonzero eigenvalue of
\square_{p,q}
, and
\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{b_{p,q}}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}_{p,q}(M,E)
.

(iii)

If
\mathfrak{Ric}_{p,q+1}^{E}\geq-K_{+},\ \mathfrak{Ric}_{p,q-1}^{E}\geq-K_{-}
for some constants
K_{+},K_{-}\geq 0
.
Then

\displaystyle\quad\max\{|\bar{\partial}_{y}H_{p,q}(t,x,y)|,|\bar{\partial}_{y}^{*}H_{p,q}(t,x,y)|\}

\displaystyle\leq r\beta^{\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-1}}|M|e^{\left(\frac{K+K_{+}+K_{-}}{\mu_{1}}+\frac{\gamma}{\beta\mu_{1}}\right)\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-1}}t^{\frac{2\alpha}{1-\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}

for all
(t,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times M\times M
.

(iv)

There is a constant
C:=C(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,K,K_{+},K_{-})>0
such that

\max\{|\bar{\partial}_{y}H_{p,q}(t,x,y)|^{2},|\bar{\partial}_{y}^{*}H_{p,q}(t,x,y)|^{2}\}\leq Cn|B(y,\sqrt{t})|t^{\frac{4\alpha-1}{1-\alpha}}e^{-\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{18t}}

for all
x,y\in M,\ 0<t\leq 1
, where

is the geodesic ball in

2.
Notations and Conventions

In this section, we fix some notations and conventions that are needed in our discussions.

We set
\mathbb{N}:=\{0,1,2,\cdots\}.

Let
T
be a symmetric linear operator on an inner product space
(V,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
, we write
T\geq 0
if

\langle Tx,x\rangle\geq 0
for all
x\in V
, and say
T
is nonnegative. For
b\in\mathbb{R}
, we write
T\geq b
if

T-bI
is nonnegative, where
I
is the identity operator.

Let
M
be a complex manifold of complex dimension
n
, the Riemannian distance on
M
is denoted by
d(\cdot,\cdot)
,
and the Riemannian volume form is denoted by
dV
. For
x\in M
and
r>0
, set

B(x,r):=\{y\in M|\ d(x,y)<r\}.

For a subset
U\subset M
, we use
|U|
(resp.
\diam(U)
) to denote the volume (resp. diameter) of
U
. When
\omega
is a Kähler metric on
M
, then

dV=\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}.

For a Hermitian vector bundle
E
over
M
, the Hermitian metric is also denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
,
which we assume to be complex linear in its first entry.
For any
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, and any open subset
U\subset\subset M
, we use
C^{k}(U,E)
to denote the space of all
C^{k}
-smooth sections of
E
over
U
. Similarly, for
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
L^{p}(U,E)
denotes of all Lebesgue measurable sections of
E
over
U
which
are
L^{p}
-integrable. If
E=M\times\mathbb{C}
is trivial, then we write
C^{k}(U):=C^{k}(U,E)
.

On a complex manifold
M
, the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
M
is denoted by
\Delta
. We use
\bar{\partial}
to denote the dbar operator on
M
.
For
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M
denote the bundle of smooth
(p,q)
-forms on
M
, and let
\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\otimes E
denote the bundle of smooth
E
-valued
(p,q)
-forms on
M
, where
E
is a Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle over
M
. The operator
\bar{\partial}
extends naturally to smooth
E
-valued
(p,q)
-forms, and we use the same notation
\bar{\partial}
for this extension. Let
\nabla
denote the Chern connection on
E
.
The formal adjoint of
\bar{\partial},\ \nabla
is denoted by
\bar{\partial}^{*}
and
\nabla^{*}
, respectively. The
\bar{\partial}
-Laplacian is defined as

\square_{p,q}:=\bar{\partial}\bar{\partial}^{*}+\bar{\partial}^{*}\bar{\partial}.

Now assume
M
is Kähler. Define the Weitzenböck curvature operator

\mathfrak{Ric}_{p,q}^{E}:=2\square_{p,q}-\nabla^{*}\nabla.

For the local expression of
\mathfrak{Ric}_{p,q}^{E}
, see
[
LX10
, Theorem 3.1]
.
From the local expression, it follows that if
E
is a trivial line bundle, then
\mathfrak{Ric}_{0,0}^{E}=0
and
\mathfrak{Ric}_{n,0}^{E}
is the scalar curvature of
M
. The Bochner-Weitzenböck formula states that

\Delta\frac{|f|^{2}}{2}=\operatorname{Re}\langle(-2\square_{p,q}+\mathfrak{Ric}_{p,q}^{E})f,f\rangle+|\nabla f|^{2},\ \forall f\in C^{2}(M,\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\otimes E),

where
\operatorname{Re}(\cdot)
denotes the real part of a complex number.

3.
Estimates of eigenvalues, eigensections and heat kernels

This section provides estimates for eigenvalues, eigensections, and heat kernels, including the proof of Theorem
1.1
, and a lower bound for the first nonzero eigenvalue of
\square_{p,q}
, i.e., the proof of Theorem
1.7
.

We begin with a
C^{0}
-estimate for eigensections.

Lemma 3.1
.

Under the same assumptions and notations of Theorem
1.1
. Let
\lambda\geq 0
be an eigenvalue of
\square_{p,q}
, and
let
\phi
be a corresponding eigensection, i.e.,

\square_{p,q}\phi=\lambda\phi.

Then we have the following mean value inequality:

\sup_{M}|\phi|^{2}\leq(2\beta(2\lambda+K)+2\gamma)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}\cdot\alpha^{\frac{\alpha}{(\alpha-1)^{2}}}\int_{M}|\phi|^{2}.

Proof.
.

This proof is inspired by the proof of
[
LZZ21
, Lemma 2.2]
.
By Bochner-Weitzenböck formula, we get

\Delta\frac{|\phi|^{2}}{2}=\operatorname{Re}\langle(-2\square_{p,q}+\mathfrak{Ric}_{p,q}^{E})\phi,\phi\rangle+|\nabla\phi|^{2}\geq-(2\lambda+K)|\phi|^{2}.

Set
v:=|\phi|^{2}
, then

\Delta v\geq-2(2\lambda+K)v.

For
p\geq 1
, integration by parts yields

\int_{M}\frac{2p-1}{p^{2}}|\nabla v^{p}|^{2}=-\int_{M}v^{2p-1}\Delta v\leq 2\int_{M}(2\lambda+K)v^{2p}.

The Sobolev-type inequality (
\clubsuit
) then implies

where

.
Now setting

we obtain by iteration:

\displaystyle\left(\int_{M}v^{2\alpha^{k}}\right)^{1/\alpha^{k}}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\alpha^{-(k-1)}}\alpha^{(k-1)\alpha^{-(k-1)}}\left(\int_{M}v^{2\alpha^{k-1}}\right)^{1/\alpha^{k-1}}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha^{-(i-1)}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\alpha^{(j-1)\alpha^{-(j-1)}}\int_{M}v.

Taking the limit as

gives the result.
∎

u_{T}(x):=u(x,T)
. Since (
3.18
) holds for any positive function
u_{0}
, by duality and the representation formula (
3.13
),
then we get

(3.19)

H(x,y,T)\leq e^{\frac{3\alpha}{\alpha-1}}e^{\Psi}T^{-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}e^{\frac{4\gamma T}{5\beta}+\frac{41b^{2}}{20}T}e^{b(\phi(x)-\phi(y))},\ \forall x,y\in M.

For two fixed points
x,y\in M
, if we take

b:=\frac{10(\phi(y)-\phi(x))}{41},

we have

(3.20)

H(T,x,y)\leq e^{\frac{3\alpha}{\alpha-1}}e^{\Psi}T^{-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}e^{\frac{4\gamma T}{5\beta}}e^{-\frac{(\phi(x)-\phi(y))^{2}}{9T}}.

Since (
3.20
) holds for any smooth function
\phi
with
\|\nabla\phi\|_{\infty}\leq 1
, we can take a sequence of such functions which converge uniformly to
z\mapsto d(x,z)
. Therefore, from (
3.20
), we conclude that for any
T>0
, it holds that

(3.21)

H(T,x,y)\leq e^{\frac{3\alpha}{\alpha-1}}e^{\Psi}T^{-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}e^{\frac{4\gamma T}{5\beta}}e^{-\frac{(\phi(x)-\phi(y))^{2}}{9T}}.

Combining this with (
3.12
) gives the result.

(ii) Suppose
0<\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}<\cdots
are all nonzero eigenvalues of

\square_{p,q}
, and
\phi_{b_{p,q}+1},\phi_{b_{p,q}+2},\cdots
are the corresponding eigensections such that
they consist of an orthonormal basis of the orthogonal complement of
\mathcal{H}_{p,q}(M,E)
in
L^{2}(M,\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\otimes E)
.
By (i), there is a constant
C_{0}:=C_{0}(\alpha)>0
such that

|H_{p,q}(t,x,x)|\leq C_{0}\beta^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}e^{Kt+\frac{\gamma}{\beta}t}t^{-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}},\ \forall(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times M.

Similar to the proof of
[
DL82
, Corollary 4.6]
, it is easy to see that

(3.22)

\mu_{k}\geq\frac{2c}{\beta}\left(r|M|e^{\frac{K}{\mu_{1}}+\frac{\gamma}{\beta\mu_{1}}}\right)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}}k^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}},\ \forall k\geq 1,

where
c>0
is a constant only depending on
\alpha
.
By Lemma
3.1
, and shrinking
c
if necessary, we have

\sup_{M}|\phi_{b_{p,q}+k}|^{2}\leq\frac{\theta}{c}\mu_{k}^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}},\ \forall k\geq 1,

where

\theta:=\left(\beta+\frac{\beta K}{\mu_{1}}+\frac{\gamma}{\mu_{1}}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}.

Setting

d:=\sup_{x>0}e^{-\frac{x}{2}}x^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}\leq\left(\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-1}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}},\ \delta:=\frac{1}{\beta}\left(r|M|e^{\frac{K}{\mu_{1}}+\frac{\gamma}{\beta\mu_{1}}}\right)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}},

then we know

\displaystyle\quad\left|H_{p,q}(t,x,y)-\sum_{k=1}^{b_{p,q}}\phi_{k}(x)\otimes\phi_{k}(y)\right|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\theta}{c}e^{-\mu_{k}t}\mu_{k}^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}

\displaystyle=t^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\theta}{c}e^{-\frac{\mu_{k}t}{2}}\left(e^{-\frac{\mu_{k}t}{2}}(\mu_{k}t)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}\right)\leq t^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{d\theta}{c}e^{-c\delta k^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}t}

\displaystyle\leq t^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}\frac{d\theta}{c}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-c\delta x^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}t}dx\leq C\theta\delta^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}t^{\frac{2\alpha}{1-\alpha}},

where
C
is a constant only depending on
\alpha
.
Clearly,

\theta\delta^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}\leq r\beta^{\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-1}}|M|e^{\left(\frac{K}{\mu_{1}}+\frac{\gamma}{\beta\mu_{1}}\right)\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-1}}.

(iii) We only consider the estimates of
\bar{\partial}_{y}H_{p,q}(t,x,y)
. Fix
x,y\in M
. By definition, we clearly have

\bar{\partial}_{y}H_{p,q}(t,x,y)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{-\mu_{k}t}\phi_{b_{p,q}+k}(x)\otimes\bar{\partial}_{y}\phi_{b_{p,q}+k}(y).

Thus, by Lemma
3.1
and
3.6
, we know there is a constant
c:=c(\alpha)>0
such that

\sup_{M}|\phi_{b_{p,q}+k}|^{2}\leq\frac{\theta}{c}\mu_{k}^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}},\ \sup_{M}|\bar{\partial}\phi_{b_{p,q}+k}|^{2}\leq\frac{\theta}{c}\mu_{k}^{\frac{2\alpha-1}{\alpha-1}},\ \forall k\geq 1,

where

\theta:=\left(\beta+\frac{\beta K_{0}}{\mu_{1}}+\frac{\gamma}{\mu_{1}}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}},\ K_{0}:=\max\{K,K_{+},K_{-}\}.

Thus we know

|\bar{\partial}_{y}H_{p,q}(t,x,y)|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\theta}{c}e^{-\mu_{k}t}\mu_{k}^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}+\frac{1}{2}}.

Similar as the proof of (ii), we conclude there is a constant
C:=C(\alpha)>0
such that

|\bar{\partial}_{y}H_{p,q}(t,x,y)|\leq Cr\beta^{\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-1}}|M|e^{\left(\frac{K}{\mu_{1}}+\frac{\gamma}{\beta\mu_{1}}\right)\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-1}}t^{\frac{2\alpha}{1-\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}.

(iv) We only consider the estimates of
\bar{\partial}_{y}H_{p,q}(t,x,y)
. Fix
x,y\in M,0<t\leq 1
. By Lemma
3.8
and (i),
there is a constant
C:=C(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,K,K_{+},K_{-})>0
such that

|\bar{\partial}_{y}H_{p,q}(t,x,y)|^{2}\leq Cn|B(y,\sqrt{t})|t^{\frac{4\alpha-1}{1-\alpha}}\sup_{(s,z)\in[3t/4,t]\times B(y,\sqrt{t}/2)}e^{-\frac{2d(x,z)^{2}}{9s}}.

Observation that

\sup_{(s,z)\in[3t/4,t]\times B(y,\sqrt{t}/2)}e^{-\frac{2d(x,z)^{2}}{9s}}\leq 3e^{-\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{18t}},

then the conclusion follows.
∎

In the last, we give an estimate of the first eigenvalue of
\square_{p,q}
.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
M
be a compact Kähler manifold of complex dimension
n
.
Fix
p,q\in\{0,\cdots,n\}.
Let
E
be a Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle over
M
such that there is a real valued function

b
over
M
satisfying

\mathfrak{Ric}_{p,q}^{E}\geq b,\ \mu_{1}^{0}+\inf_{M}b>0.

where
\mu_{1}^{0}
is the first nonzero eigenvalue of
\Delta
.
Let
\lambda_{1}
be the first eigenvalue of
\square_{p,q}
, then we have

2\lambda_{1}\geq\min\left\{\frac{1}{2}\mu_{1}^{0}+\inf_{M}b,\frac{1}{|M|}\int_{M}b,\frac{\mu_{1}^{0}}{2|M|}\int_{M}\frac{b}{\mu_{1}^{0}+b}\right\},

where
\mu_{1}^{0}
is the first nonzero eigenvalue of
\Delta
.

Proof.
.

This proof is motivated by the proof of
[
LZZ21
, Lemma 2.8]
.
Suppose
\phi
is an eigenform of
\lambda_{1}
, and we may assume

\int_{M}|\phi|^{2}=1

By the Bochner-Weitzenböck formula and Kato’s inequality, we get

(3.23)

\Delta\frac{|\phi|^{2}}{2}\geq-(2\lambda_{1}-b)|\phi|^{2}+|\nabla|\phi||^{2}.

Integrating the above identity, we find that

(3.24)

2\lambda_{1}\geq\int_{M}|\nabla|\phi||^{2}+\int_{M}b|\phi|^{2}.

If
|\phi|
is a constant function, then (
3.24
) gives us

(3.25)

2\lambda_{1}\geq\frac{1}{|M|}\int_{M}b.

Thus we may assume that
|\phi|
is not constant. For simplicity of notations, we write

f:=|\phi|,\ a:=\frac{1}{|M|}\int_{M}f.

We consider two cases.

Case 1:
Suppose

(3.26)

\int_{M}(f-a)^{2}\geq 1/2.

Then (
3.24
) implies

(3.27)

\begin{split}2\lambda_{1}&\geq\int_{M}|\nabla f|^{2}+\int_{M}bf^{2}\\
&\geq\mu_{1}^{0}\int_{M}(f-a)^{2}+\inf_{M}b\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\mu_{1}^{0}+\inf_{M}b.\end{split}

Case 2:
Suppose

(3.28)

\int_{M}(f-a)^{2}<1/2.

Then we can expand the square to reach:

\int_{M}f^{2}-2a\int_{M}f+a^{2}|M|<1/2,

which shows that

(3.29)

a^{2}>\frac{1}{2|M|}.

By (
3.24
) again and use Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we know

(3.30)

\begin{split}2\lambda_{1}&\geq\int_{M}|\nabla f|^{2}+\int_{M}b(f-a+a)^{2}\\
&\geq\int_{M}\left(\mu_{1}^{0}+b\right)(f-a)^{2}+2a\int_{M}b(f-a)+a^{2}\int_{M}b\\
&\geq a^{2}\int_{M}\frac{\mu_{1}^{0}b}{\mu_{1}^{0}+b}\\
&\geq\frac{\mu_{1}^{0}}{2|M|}\int_{M}\frac{b}{\mu_{1}^{0}+b}.\end{split}

∎

Proposition
3.12
implies Theorem
1.7
and yields a vanishing theorem:

Corollary 3.13
.

Let
M
be a compact Kähler manifold of complex dimension
n
.
Fix
p,q\in\{0,\cdots,n\}.
Let
E
be a Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle over
M
such that there is a real valued function

b
over
M
satisfying

\mathfrak{Ric}_{p,q}^{E}\geq b\geq 0,

and
b(x)>0
at some point
x\in M
.
Let
\lambda_{1}
be the first eigenvalue of
\square_{p,q},
then we have

\mu_{1}=\lambda_{1}\geq\frac{\mu_{1}^{0}}{4|M|}\int_{M}\frac{b}{\mu_{1}^{0}+b},

where
\mu_{1}^{0}
is the first nonzero eigenvalue of
\Delta
.
In particular,
\mathcal{H}_{p,q}(M,E)=0
.

For some other estimates of the first eigenvalue and vanishing theorems using the Weitzenböck curvature operator,
please see
[
RY94
]
and
[
Z94
]
.

The following
L^{2}
-estimate of
\bar{\partial}
can be easily derived from Corollary
3.13
, see

[
B02
, Proposition 5.1]
for example.

Corollary 3.14
.

Under the same assumptions and notations of Corollary
3.13
and we further assume
q\geq 1
.
Then for
\bar{\partial}
-closed
f\in L^{2}(M,\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\otimes E)
, we may find

u\in L^{2}(M,\Lambda^{p,q-1}T^{*}M\otimes E)
such that

\int_{M}|u|^{2}\leq\frac{5}{\sigma}\int_{M}|f|^{2},

where

\sigma:=\frac{\mu_{1}^{0}}{|M|}\int_{M}\frac{b}{\mu_{1}^{0}+b}.

4.
Estimates of Green forms and Sobolev-type inequalities

In this section, we will give the proof of Corollary
1.2
and Corollary
1.3
.

Firstly, let us prove Corollary
1.2
, and we restate it here for convenience.

Corollary 4.1

(= Corollary
1.2
)
.

Under the same assumptions and notations of Theorem
1.1
. Let
G_{p,q}
be the Schwarz kernel of the Green operator for
\square_{p,q}
,
then we have the following uniform estimates:

(i)

There is a constant
C:=C(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\mu_{1},K,|M|)>0
such that

|G_{p,q}(x,y)|\leq Cd(x,y)^{\frac{2}{1-\alpha}}+C,\ \forall(x,y)\in M\times M.

(ii)

Suppose the Ricci curvature of
M
is bounded below by
-(2n-1)\theta
for some constant
\theta\geq 0
,
and assume
\alpha
satisfies

\frac{4\alpha-1}{\alpha-1}-n-2>0,

then there is a constant
C:=C(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,r,n,\mu_{1},K,K_{+},K_{-},M)>0
such that

\max\left\{|\bar{\partial}_{y}G_{p,q}(x,y)|,|\bar{\partial}^{*}_{y}G_{p,q}(x,y)|\right\}\leq Cd(x,y)^{\frac{2\alpha+1}{1-\alpha}+n}

for all
x,y\in M
.

Proof.
.

(i) Fix
x,y\in M
. Set

K(t,x,y):=H_{p,q}(t,x,y)-\sum_{k=1}^{b_{p,q}}\phi_{k}(x)\otimes\phi_{k}(y).

By (i) and (ii) of Theorem
1.1
, Lemma
3.1
and Corollary
3.4
, there is a constant

C:=C(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\mu_{1},K,|M|)>0
such that

|K(t,x,y)|\leq Ct^{\frac{2\alpha}{1-\alpha}},\ \forall t\geq 1,

|K(t,x,y)|\leq Ct^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}e^{-\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{9t}},\ \forall t\in(0,1].

Since
1<\alpha\leq 2
, then we get

\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}-2}e^{-s}ds\leq\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\right),

where
\Gamma(\cdot)
is the Gamma function. By definition, we know

\displaystyle|G_{p,q}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}|K(t,x,y)|dt

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{1}t^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}e^{-\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{9t}}dt+C\int_{1}^{\infty}t^{\frac{2\alpha}{1-\alpha}}dt

\displaystyle\leq 9^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\right)Cd(x,y)^{\frac{2}{1-\alpha}}+C.

(ii)
Fix
x,y\in M
. We only give the estimate of
\bar{\partial}G_{p,q}(x,y)
.
Since
M
is compact, by Bishop-Gromov inequality, we have

|B(y,\sqrt{t})|\leq b_{n}e^{(2n-1)\sqrt{\theta}\diam(M)}t^{n},\ \forall t>0,

where
b_{n}
is the volume of unit ball in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. In particular, we know

|M|\leq b_{n}e^{(2n-1)\sqrt{\theta}\diam(M)}\diam(M)^{2n}.

By (iii) and (iv) of Theorem
1.1
, there is a constant

C:=C(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,r,n,\theta,\mu_{1},K,K_{+},K_{-},\diam(M))>0

such that

|\bar{\partial}_{y}H_{p,q}(t,x,y)|\leq Ct^{\frac{2\alpha}{1-\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}},\ \forall t\geq 1,

|\bar{\partial}_{y}H_{p,q}(t,x,y)|\leq Ct^{\frac{4\alpha-1}{2-2\alpha}+\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{36t}},\ \forall 0<t\leq 1.

Clearly,

\displaystyle|\bar{\partial}G_{p,q}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}|\bar{\partial}_{y}H_{p,q}(t,x,y)|dt

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{1}t^{\frac{4\alpha-1}{2-2\alpha}+\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{36t}}dt+C\int_{1}^{\infty}t^{\frac{2\alpha}{1-\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}dt

\displaystyle\leq C\Gamma\left(\frac{4\alpha-1}{2\alpha-2}-\frac{n}{2}-1\right)d(x,y)^{\frac{2\alpha+1}{1-\alpha}+n}+C.

∎

To prove Corollary
1.3
, let us firstly introduce some notations and give some lemmas. We adopt the same assumptions and notations as in Theorem
1.1
. Suppose
0<\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}<\cdots
are all nonzero eigenvalues of

\square_{p,q}
, and
\phi_{b_{p,q}+1},\phi_{b_{p,q}+2},\cdots
are the corresponding eigensections such that
they consist of an orthonormal basis the orthogonal complement of
\mathcal{H}_{p,q}(M,E)
in
L^{2}(M,\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\otimes E)
.
For any
f\in L^{2}(M,\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\otimes E)
. Clearly, there are constants
a_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

f-\mathcal{P}f=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}\phi_{b_{p,q}+k},

and then

\square_{p,q}f=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}\mu_{k}\phi_{b_{p,q}+k}.

For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the pseudo-differential operator
\square_{p,q}^{\alpha}
via

\square_{p,q}^{\alpha}f:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}\mu_{k}^{\alpha}\phi_{b_{p,q}+k}.

Using integration by parts, we have

(4.1)

\int_{M}|\bar{\partial}f|^{2}+\int_{M}|\bar{\partial}^{*}f|^{2}=\int_{M}\langle f,\square_{p,q}f\rangle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}^{2}\mu_{k}=\int_{M}|\square_{p,q}^{1/2}f|^{2}.

Set

K(t,x,y):=\left|H_{p,q}(t,x,y)-\sum_{k=1}^{b_{p,q}}\phi_{k}(x)\otimes\phi_{k}(y)\right|,\ \forall(t,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times M\times M.

For any
x\in M
, we have

(4.2)

\begin{split}&\quad\square_{p,q}^{-1/2}f(x)\\
&=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}\mu_{k}^{-1/2}\phi_{b_{p,q}+k}(x)\\
&=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{M}\langle\phi_{b_{p,q}+k}(y),f(y)\rangle dV(y)\cdot\mu_{k}^{-1/2}\phi_{b_{p,q}+k}(x)\\
&=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1/2)}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1/2}e^{-\mu_{k}t}dt\int_{M}\langle\phi_{b_{p,q}+k}(y),f(y)\rangle dV(y)\cdot\phi_{b_{p,q}+k}(x)\\
&=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-1/2}\int_{M}\langle K(t,x,y),f(y)\rangle dV(y)dt,\end{split}

where
\Gamma(\cdot)
is the Gamma function. One needs to note that
\Gamma(1/2)\geq 1
.

A basic lemma is the following:

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
1\leq\ell<(2\alpha)/(\alpha-1)
, set

-m:=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{(1-\alpha)\ell}<0,

then
\square_{p,q}^{-1/2}
is of weak
(\ell,\ell/(2m)+\ell)
type, i.e.,
there is a constant
C:=C(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\mu_{1},K,|M|)
satisfying for any
\delta>0
and
g\in L^{\ell}(M,\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\otimes E)
, we have

\left|\{x\in M|\ \square_{p,q}^{-1/2}g(x)>\delta\}\right|\leq C\delta^{-\frac{\ell}{2m}-\ell}\left(\int_{M}|g|^{\ell}\right)^{1+\frac{1}{2m}}.

Proof.
.

The argument is inspired by the proof
[
L12
, Theorem 11.6]
.
By the proof (i) of Theorem
1.1
, we know

|H_{p,q}(t,x,y)|\leq e^{Kt}H(t,x,y),\ \forall(t,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times M\times M.

In the following, when we say a number is a constant, we mean it is a constant only depending on

\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\mu_{1},K,|M|
.
By Corollary
3.5
, we have

(4.3)

b_{p,q}\leq r|M|(2\beta K+2\gamma)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}\cdot\alpha^{\frac{\alpha}{(\alpha-1)^{2}}}.

Use Lemma
3.1
, then there is a constant
C_{1}>0
such that

(4.4)

\sup_{M}|\phi_{k}|^{2}\leq C_{1},\ \forall 1\leq k\leq b_{p,q},

then by the triangle inequality, we know

|K(t,x,y)|\leq C_{1}b_{p,q}+e^{K}H(t,x,y),\ \forall t\leq 1,

which implies that for all
(t,x)\in(0,1]\times M
, we have

(4.5)

\int_{M}K(t,x,y)dV(y)\leq C_{1}b_{p,q}|M|+e^{K}.

if one notes that

\sup_{x\in M}\int_{M}H(t,x,y)dV(y)\leq 1.

Use (ii) of Theorem
1.1
, there is a constant
C_{2}>0
such that

(4.6)

K(t,x,y)\leq C_{2}t^{\frac{2\alpha}{1-\alpha}},\ \forall(t,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times M\times M,

then we get

(4.7)

\int_{M}K(t,x,y)dV(y)\leq C_{2}|M|,\ \forall(t,x)\in[1,\infty)\times M.

By (
4.3
), (
4.5
) and (
4.7
), there is a constant

C_{3}>0
such that

(4.8)

\sup_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\times M}\int_{M}|K(t,x,y)|dV(y)\leq C_{3}.

Use (i) of Theorem
1.1
and (
4.4
), there is a constant

C_{4}>0
such that

(4.9)

|K(t,x,y)|\leq C_{1}b_{p,q}+C_{4}t^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}},\ \forall(t,x,y)\in(0,1]\times M\times M.

Use (ii) of Theorem
1.1
, we know there is a constant
C_{5}>0
such that

(4.10)

|K(t,x,y)|\leq C_{5}t^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}},\ \forall(t,x,y)\in[1,\infty)\times M\times M.

Combining (
4.9
) and (
4.10
), we conclude there is a constant

C_{6}>0
such that

(4.11)

|K(t,x,y)|\leq C_{6}t^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}},\ \forall(t,x,y)\in(0,\infty)\times M\times M.

Fix
1\leq\ell<(2\alpha)/(\alpha-1)
, and fix
g\in L^{\ell}(M,\Lambda^{p,q}T^{*}M\otimes E).

We may assume
\ell>1
since the case
\ell=1
can be proved similarly.
For
T>0
which will be determined later, let us define

T_{1}g(x):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1/2)}\int_{0}^{T}t^{-1/2}\int_{M}\langle K(t,x,y),g(y)\rangle dV(y)dt,\ \forall x\in M,

T_{2}g(x):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1/2)}\int_{T}^{\infty}t^{-1/2}\int_{M}\langle K(t,x,y),g(y)\rangle dV(y)dt,\ \forall x\in M.

By (
4.2
), we have

\square_{p,q}^{-1/2}g=T_{1}g+T_{2}g.

For any
x\in M
, by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, Hölder’s inequality and (
4.8
),
we have

\displaystyle\quad|T_{1}g(x)|^{\ell}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{T}t^{-1/2}\int_{M}|K(t,x,y)|\cdot|g(y)|dV(y)dt\right)^{\ell}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{T}s^{-1/2}ds\right)^{\ell-1}\int_{0}^{T}t^{-1/2}\left(\int_{M}|K(t,x,y)|\cdot|g(y)|dV(y)\right)^{\ell}dt

\displaystyle\leq(2\sqrt{T})^{\ell-1}C_{3}^{\ell-1}\int_{0}^{T}t^{-1/2}\int_{M}|K(t,x,y)|\cdot|g(y)|^{\ell}dV(y)dt,

then by Fubini’s theorem (and note that
|K(t,x,y)|=|K(t,y,x)|
), we get

(4.12)

\int_{M}|T_{1}g(x)|^{\ell}dV(x)\leq(2\sqrt{T})^{\ell}C_{3}\int_{M}|g(y)|^{\ell}dV(y).

Similarly, for any
x\in M
, by Hölder’s inequality, (
4.8
) and (
4.11
), we know

(4.13)

\displaystyle\quad|T_{2}g(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{T}^{\infty}t^{-1/2}\int_{M}|K(t,x,y)|\cdot|g(y)|dV(y)dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{T}^{\infty}t^{-1/2}\left(\int_{M}|K(t,x,y)|^{\frac{\ell}{\ell-1}}dV(y)\right)^{\frac{\ell-1}{\ell}}\cdot\left(\int_{M}|g|^{\ell}dV\right)^{1/\ell}

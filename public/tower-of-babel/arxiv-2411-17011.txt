MOLPIPx: an end-to-end differentiable package for permutationally invariant polynomials in Python and Rust
Manuel S. Drehwald, Asma Jamali, Rodrigo A. Vargas-Hernández

Abstract
In this work, we present MOLPIPx, a versatile library designed to seamlessly integrate Permutationally Invariant Polynomials (PIPs) with modern machine learning frameworks, enabling the efficient development of linear models, neural networks, and Gaussian process models. These methodologies are widely employed for parameterizing potential energy surfaces across diverse molecular systems. MOLPIPx leverages two powerful automatic differentiation engines—JAX and EnzymeAD-Rust—to facilitate the efficient computation of energy gradients and higher-order derivatives, which are essential for tasks such as force field development and dynamic simulations. MOLPIPx is available at https://github.com/ChemAI-Lab/molpipx .

II.1
Permutationally Invariant Polynomial Representation

The PIP representation for PES models can be conceptually described as a complex composition of multiple functions that transform the Cartesian coordinates of a molecule (
\vx

\pipvec
), which is invariant to permutations of similar nuclei.
The primary motivation for articulating
\pipvec
as a sequence of function compositions is to facilitate its integration with modern automatic differentiation frameworks (AD)
Baydin et al. 2017
.
AD has been fundamental in the numerical ecosystem of deep learning libraries and, more recently, in computational chemistry simulations.
Blondel et al. 2022
;
Dral, Hourahine, and Grimme 2024
;
Baydin et al. 2017
;
Tamayo-Mendoza et al. 2018

Tan, Pickard, and Witt 2023
;
Vargas-Hernández et al. 2023
;

;
Zhang et al. 2024

;

;

;

.
By structuring the PIP vector, each component function can be seamlessly differentiated and combined with other machine learning algorithms; Sections

and

The construction of
\pipvec

\vecr

These groups of transformations ensure that the resulting representation is invariant under the permutation of identical nuclei, thus capturing the correct molecular symmetries.

\pipvec

(1)

\displaystyle\vecr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(\vx),\quad\text{(inter-nuclei distances)}

(2)

\displaystyle\vgamma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma(\vecr)=e^{-\lambda\vecr},\quad\text{(Morse variables)}

(3)

\displaystyle\vz_{\text{mono}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\fmono(\vgamma),\quad\text{(symmetrized monomials)}

(4)

\displaystyle\vz_{\text{poly}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\fpoly(\vz_{\text{mono}})\quad\text{(symmetrized polynomials)}.

II.3
Anisotropic Morse Variables

One of the foundational components of
\pipvec
is the Morse variables,
\vgamma
, which are modulated by the length-scale hyper-parameter
\lambda
. Typically, a uniform
\lambda
is applied across all inter-nuclei distances. Inspired by automatic relevance determination methods in kernel machines
Rasmussen and Williams 2005
, we introduce an anisotropic formulation of the Morse variables, where unique length-scale parameters are assigned to each type of atom-atom distance.
For example, in ammonia (
\text{NH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
), which contains two different types of distances,
H
–
H
and
N
–
H
, the distinct length-scale parameters are
\lambda_{\text{HH}}
and
\lambda_{\text{NH}}
.
This approach ensures that each
\lambda_{\ell}
parameter exclusively affects its corresponding distance type, maintaining permutational invariance.
Following Eq.
1
notation, we define
\pipvecaniso
as a PIP model with anisotropic Morse variables as,

\displaystyle\pipvecaniso(\vx)=(\fpoly\circ\fmono\circ\gamma_{\text{aniso}}\circ d)(\vx),

(8)

where the anisotropic Morse variables are defined by,

\displaystyle\vgamma_{\text{aniso}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{\text{aniso}}(\bm{\lambda},\bm{\omega},\vecr)=\exp\left(-\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\bm{\omega}_{\ell}\odot\vecr\right),

(9)

here,
\odot
denotes the Hadamard product between the distance vector
\vecr
and the mask vector
1
1

1

a mask vector is a binary vector used to selectively apply operations to specific elements of another vector or matrix.

\bm{\omega}_{\ell}
, and the sum
\sum_{\ell}
is over the unique types of inter-nuclei distances.
The mask vector elements (
\omega_{\ell,j}
) are defined as,

\displaystyle\omega_{\ell,j}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }r_{j}\text{ is the same type as }\lambda_{\ell},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(10)

In isotropic
\pipvec

;
Vargas-Hernández et al. 2019
;
Vargas-Hernández 2020

Blondel et al. 2022

\displaystyle\vlambda^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\arg\min_{\vlambda\in\mathbb{R}}{\cal L}_{\text{outer}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}(\vlambda))

(11)

subject to

\displaystyle\;\;\;\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}(\vlambda)=\arg\min_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}}{\cal L}_{\text{inner}}(\mathbf{w},\vlambda),

where
{\cal L}_{\text{outer}}
is the validation loss function, and
{\cal L}_{\text{inner}}
is the training loss function that jointly depends on
\vlambda

\mathbf{w}
.
Here,
\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}(\vlambda)
denotes the optimal solution of
{\cal L}_{\text{inner}}

7

To efficiently solve for
\vlambda^{*}
, and because of the chain rule, it is essential to determine the gradient
\frac{\partial\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}}{\partial\vlambda}

This gradient can be effectively calculated using implicit differentiation techniques
Vargas-Hernández et al. 2021
;
Blondel et al. 2022
, enabling a systematic and efficient approach to optimizing the anisotropic model parameters for molecular systems with a higher number of unique types of inter-nuclei distances.

II.4
Permutationally Invariant Polynomial Neural Networks

Feed-forward neural networks (NNs) known also as multi-layer perceptrons, have been used for PESs
Kocer, Ko, and Behler 2022
;
Manzhos and Carrington 2021
. However, when modeling PESs, neural networks encounter limitations as they do not inherently accommodate the permutation of identical nuclei. Addressing this gap, the integration of PIPs within NNs, coined as PIP-NN (
\fpipnn
), was pioneered by B. Jiang et al. in Ref.
Jiang and Guo 2013
. This innovative approach significantly enhances neural networks by embedding the necessary symmetry considerations using
\pipvec
as a symmetrized input to the overall model
Shao et al. 2016
;
van der Wilk et al. 2018
. Following the same framework of function composition, PIP-NNs can be defined as,

\displaystyle\fpipnn(\vx)=(\fnn\circ\pipvec)(\vx),

(12)

where
\fnn
can be any NN architecture, commonly feed-forward NNs are the most used NN architecture,

\displaystyle\fnn(\vx)=(g_{L}\circ\cdots\circ g_{1}\circ\pipvec)(\vx),

(13)

where
L
represents the number of layers, and each layer
g_{\ell}
is parametrized by,

\displaystyle g_{\ell}(\vz)=\sigma(\mathbf{w}_{\ell}\vz+b_{\ell}),

(14)

where
\sigma
is the activation function, and
\{\mathbf{w}_{\ell},b_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{L}
represent the set of parameters for the PIP-NN model.

For PESs, it is a standard practice to train PIP-NNs using both forces and energies jointly,

\displaystyle{\cal L}(\bm{\Theta},\lambdazero)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}^{N}\|\nabla\fpipnn(\vx_{i})-\nabla V(\vx_{i})\|_{2}

(15)

\displaystyle+\lambdazero\left(\fpipnn(\vx_{i})-V(\vx_{i})\right)^{2},

where the
\lambdazero
hyper-parameter determines the relative weighting between the energy (
\fpipnn(\vx)
) and force (
\nabla\fpipnn(\vx)
) terms, helping to account for differences in their magnitudes.
PIP-NNs, like other neural network-based PES models, function as force-field models that train efficiently using stochastic gradient descent methods, with the gradient
\nabla\fnn(\vx)
computed through automatic differentiation (AD).

;

;

;

;

, is characterized by its mean (

) and covariance function (
\Sigma
), which is parameterized by the kernel function

A significant advantage of GPs over other probabilistic regression models is their ability to provide a closed-form solution for the posterior distribution with mean and standard deviation functions given by,

\displaystyle\mu(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k(\mathbf{x},\bm{X})^{\top}\left[K(\bm{X},\bm{X})+\sigma_{n}\mathbb{I}\right]^{-1}\bm{y},

(16)

\displaystyle\sigma(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-k(\mathbf{x},\bm{X})^{\top}\left[K(\bm{X},\bm{X})+\sigma_{n}\mathbb{I}\right]^{-1}k(\mathbf{x},\bm{X})

(17)

\displaystyle+k(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}),

where for PESs
\bm{X}
and
\bm{y}
represent the geometric configurations and the corresponding energies.
In GPs, the kernel function depends on training parameters (
\bm{\theta}
) that are optimized by maximizing the logarithm of the marginal likelihood (LML),

\displaystyle\log p(\bm{y}|\bm{X},\bm{\theta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\bm{y}^{\top}\left[K(\bm{X},\bm{X})+\sigma_{n}\mathbb{I}\right]^{-1}\bm{y}

(18)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\log\left|K(\bm{X},\bm{X})+\sigma_{n}\mathbb{I}\right|-\frac{N}{2}\log 2\pi.

For further details regarding GPs, we refer the reader to Refs.

;
Garnett 2023
.

As discussed in Refs.
van der Wilk et al. 2018
;
Rasmussen and Williams 2005
, to construct a GP that accurately represents an invariant function, the kernel must also be invariant.
Following the approach of C. Qu
et al.
in Ref.
Qu et al. 2018
, we define PIP-GPs, as models where
\pipvec
is used in the kernel function,

\displaystyle k(\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{j})=k(\pipvec(\mathbf{x}_{i}),\pipvec(\mathbf{x}_{j})),

(19)

where
k
can be any standard kernel such as Radial basis function (RBF) or Matérn, ensuring the covariance matrix
K
is symmetric and positive semi-definite.
Eq.
19
formulation is kin with the ’Deep Kernel Learning’ framework,
Wilson et al. 2016
commonly used in contemporary GPs, where NNs parameterize the latent representation of the inputs, followed by the RBF or similar kernel functions.
The training of these GP models can proceed similarly to standard GPs, using gradient-based methods to maximize the LML (Eq.
18
). The gradient of the LML with respect to the parameters of
k
can be computed via automatic differentiation, a common feature in modern GP libraries like GPyTorch
Gardner et al. 2018
, GPJax
Pinder and Dodd 2022
, and GAUCHE
Griffiths et al. 2023

Additionally, using automatic differentiation, one can compute the derivative of
\mu(\vx)
with respect to
\vx
to determine the forces for PESs
Chmiela et al. 2017
;
Asnaashari and Krems 2021
.

V
Potential extensions

MOLPIPx is designed as a flexible library for PESs, compatible with existing machine learning frameworks such as Flax and GPJax, as well as future Rust-based libraries. Currently, MOLPIPx relies on pre-existing training data and does not include built-in training or sampling protocols for each PIP-based model. However, it provides users with the flexibility to experiment with various optimization, sampling, and active learning strategies
Jung, Choi, and Lee 2024
;
Loeffler et al. 2020
;
Qi et al. 2024
.
In future releases, we plan to include basic sampling and active learning algorithms to improve the overall accuracy of PES models. Additionally, we will integrate MOLPIPx with existing quantum chemistry packages such as PySCF and Psi4, among others. These new features will enable users to enhance both the accuracy and utility of PES models in the study of chemical systems.

Because of the wide use of linear PIP-based models for PESs, we aim to integrate packages like Lineax
Rader, Lyons, and Kidger 2023
, which offer more efficient linear solvers, and to incorporate pruning techniques, such as the L1-Lasso method, which can reduce the number of terms in the PIP vector, accelerating the computation of forces.
To enhance the PIP-GP framework, we will incorporate gradients directly into the GP model, as demonstrated in previous works
Chmiela et al. 2017
;
Chmiela et al. 2018
;
Chmiela et al. 2019
. This approach will allow us to train models using both forces and energies simultaneously, leading to more accurate and robust PES fitting by fully leveraging the available data.

Looking ahead, we plan to extend the package to PyTorch
Paszke et al. 2019
, given its status as one of the most widely used ecosystems for machine learning models. Additionally, we aim to expand the Rust-based component of the package by developing anisotropic PIPs and a simple feed forward neural networks tailored for PIP-NN. This library will also ensure compatibility with the
faer-rs
Rust-based linear algebra library
El Kazdadi
, thereby enhancing the versatility and adaptability of the package.

Finally, the monomial and polynomial functions listed in Table
1
enable the development of PESs, where PIPs are applied to each
n
-body term in the many-body expansion. This approach was recently employed for hydrocarbons
Bowman et al. 2024
(
\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{14}}}\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{30}}}
), where the four-body terms in the expansion were
\text{A}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
,
\text{A}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{B}
,
\text{A}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{B}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
,
\text{A}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{BC}
, and
ABCD
, as well as in the MB-pol many-body potential for water
Babin, Medders, and Paesani 2014
;
Reddy et al. 2016
. MOLPIPx could further benefit from using mask vectors, such as those utilized in the anisotropic Morse variables described in Section
II.3
, and from the development of training schemes based on gradient-based or least-squares methods.

VI
Summary

MOLPIPx offers a comprehensive and adaptable platform that integrates PIPs with cutting-edge machine learning libraries by leveraging modern computational tools such as JAX and Enzyme-AD. Due to its end-to-end differentiable nature, MOLPIPx supports a range of regression models for PESs, from traditional linear methods to advanced models like neural networks, Gaussian Processes, and anisotropic Morse variables. Furthermore, within MOLPIPx, the computation of forces is performed automatically for any proposed PIP-based model.

We provide the monomial and polynomial functions for fifteen different molecular systems, however, MOLPIPx can be interfaced with the MSA package to translate those files into pure Python functions using JAX as the main numerical back-end library.
Finally, we also provide a comprehensive set of examples for each regression model to illustrate the use of MOLPIPx.

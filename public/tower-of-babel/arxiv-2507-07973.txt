Multiple Axions Save High-Scale Inflation
Dan Kondo, and Hitoshi Murayama

Abstract
Many models of dark matter QCD axion requires inflation at a scale H inf ≲ 10 6 H_{\text{inf}}\lesssim 10^{6} GeV and hence does not allow for a detectable tensor mode fluctuation. This is because the domain wall problem forces the Peccei–Quinn symmetry to be broken during the inflation and the axions to be produced by the misalignment mechanism. We point out that theories with multiple axions can evade this constraint and allow for a high-scale inflation with detectable tensor mode. It only requires a condition on the anomaly coefficients so that there is a unique minimum for the axion potential without a fine-tuning or small parameters.



1
Introduction

The standard model (SM) of particle physics is highly successful, but there are riddles that cannot be solved in the SM. They include the identity of dark matter, the baryon asymmetry of the universe, and cosmic inflation. In particular, the axion
Weinberg:1977ma
;
Wilczek:1977pj
is one of the attractive candidates of dark matter that can at the same time solve the strong CP problem
Peccei:1977hh
. A global Peccei–Quinn
U(1)_{PQ}
symmetry is assumed to be spontaneously broken at a scale
f_{a}
and produces the axion as its Nambu–Goldstone boson, while the QCD makes
U(1)_{PQ}
anomalous and induces a small mass for the axion
m_{a}\simeq m_{\pi}^{2}/f_{a}
.

On the other hand, inflation
Starobinsky:1980te
;
Guth:1980zm
;
Sato:1981ds
;
Linde:1981mu
;
Albrecht:1982wi
solves the flatness and horizon problems in cosmology, and creates the primordial density fluctuation by quantum fluctuation of the inflaton field. The current cosmological data strongly supports this paradigm (see,
e.g.
,
Planck:2018jri
). In particular, models with high inflationary scale
H_{\text{inf}}
are attractive that can leave a footprint of the quantum fluctuation of the graviton, namely the “quantum gravity” effects, via the B-mode polarization of CMB,. We hope to probe this effect with future experiments such as CMB-S4
Abazajian:2019eic
or LiteBIRD
delaHoz:2025uae
.

In most discussions of axion dark matter, the misalignment mechanism
Preskill:1982cy
;
Abbott:1982af
;
Dine:1982ah
is invoked. Namely that
U(1)_{PQ}
is already broken during the inflation, while the axion field is away from the true minimum during the inflation held fixed by the Hubble friction. Only after inflation is over and the QCD-induced axion potential becomes important, the axion field starts to oscillate around the true minimum, making it a cold dark matter.

Unfortunately, there are difficulties to realize both high-scale inflation and axion via the misalignment mechanism at the same time. Quantum fluctuations exist not only for the inflaton but also for the axion field, and hence induces unacceptably large isocurvature fluctuations unless
H_{\text{inf}}\lesssim 10^{-5}f_{a}
. Even though it is not impossible to write down models of low-scale inflation, we lose the ability to experimentally test inflation with CMB B-mode. If
f_{a}
is much larger during inflation, it may suppress the isocurvature fluctuation sufficiently even when the inflationary scale is high
Harigaya:2015hha
with some difficulties
Graham:2025iwx
.

On the other hand, it is possible that
U(1)_{PQ}
is spontaneously broken after inflation. In this case, axions are produced from axion strings and do not have isocurvature fluctuations, hence compatible with high-scale inflation. However, the symmetry breaking may lead to a network of domain walls
Sikivie:1982qv
, a cosmological disaster. It occurs when a discrete subgroup
{\mathbb{Z}}_{N_{DW}}
of
U(1)_{PQ}
is exact and different parts of the universe pick different ground states via the Kibble–Zurek mechanism
Kibble:1976sj
;
Zurek:1985qw
;
Murayama:2009nj
. It is therefore important to avoid degenerate ground states to allow for axion dark matter and high-scale inflation at the same time.

It turns out that many well-motivated models of axion have such an exact discrete symmetry. Most notably, Dine–Fischler–Srednicki–Zhitnitsky (DFSZ) axion models
Zhitnitsky:1980tq
;
Dine:1981rt
have the standard model fermions charged under
U(1)_{PQ}
and hence the number of vacua
N_{DW}
is a multiple of three (number of generations). It is unfortunate given that DFSZ models are compatible with grand unification and hence are well motivated.

There are various proposals to avoid the domain wall problem even when
U(1)_{PQ}
is broken after inflation. So-called hadronic axion or Kim–Shifman–Vainshtein–Zakharov (KSVZ) models of axion
Kim:1979if
;
Shifman:1979if
with only one color triplet quarks can avoid the problem. More recently, flavor-dependent assignment of
U(1)_{PQ}
was proposed
Ema:2016ops
which may also avoid domain walls
Cox:2023squ
if charge assignments are chosen carefully. If the discrete symmetry is a part of the gauge group, the domain walls can be bound by strings, shrink and disappear
Lazarides:1982tw
, which, however, often lead to other problems such as fractionally charged particles, Landau poles, etc
Lu:2023ayc
. Explicit breaking of
U(1)_{PQ}
can lift the degeneracy and make domain walls collapse
Sikivie:1982qv
, but often requires a fine-tuning to maintain the quality of
U(1)_{PQ}
to solve the strong CP problem
Kawasaki:2014sqa
.

In this paper, we consider the possibility that effective domain wall number can be one even for DFSZ axion. This is the case when there are multiple axions dubbed axiverse
Svrcek:2006yi
;
Arvanitaki:2009fg
. Even when the anomaly coefficient for the QCD axion is larger than one such as for DFSZ axion models, mixing with another Axion-Like-Particle (ALP) makes the ground state unique and eliminates stable domain walls. Connection between multiple axions and domain walls was recently discussed with extensive numerical simulations in
Benabou:2023npn
;
Lee:2024toz
. On the other hand, they did not discuss the possibility of testing high-scale inflation. In addition, we find their counting of ground states to be not useful for our purpose.

Domain Wall Number With Multiple Axions

In this section, we show that the number of degenerate ground states in the multi-axion potential is given by the determinant of the matrix of anomaly coefficients.

Domain walls arise in axion models when there is an exact discrete symmetry in the potential. Define
\theta=a/f_{a}
which lives on
T^{1}
where
\theta\simeq\theta+2\pi
are identified. When the axion potential has the form
5

5

We are aware that the actual axion potential from the chiral Lagrangian is much more complicated. But for the purpose of identifying the minima, this simplified potential suffices.

\displaystyle V=\Lambda^{4}(1-\cos n\theta),\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z},

(2)

where
n
is the anomaly coefficient for the gauge group. The potential has an exact
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
discrete symmetry

\displaystyle\theta\rightarrow\theta+\frac{2\pi}{n}m,\qquad m=0,1,\cdots,n-1.

(3)

As a result, the minima of the potential are at

\displaystyle\theta=\frac{2\pi}{n}m,\qquad m=0,1,\cdots,n-1.

(4)

Namely, there are
n
independent minima of the potential for
\theta\in T^{1}
, when
n>1
. If this potential develops by a phase transition at some critical temperature
T_{c}

Kibble:1976sj
;
Zurek:1985qw
;
Murayama:2009nj
leads to a network of domain walls because a different parts of the universe choose different vacua that are separated by domain walls.

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=V_{1}+V_{2},

(5)

\displaystyle V_{1}

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{1}^{4}\left[1-\cos\left(n_{11}\theta_{1}+n_{12}\theta_{2}\right)\right],

(6)

\displaystyle V_{2}

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{2}^{4}\left[1-\cos\left(n_{21}\theta_{1}+n_{22}\theta_{2}\right)\right].

(7)

The integers
n_{ij}
are anomaly coefficients. The two angles live on a two-dimensional torus
(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\in T^{2}

\theta_{i}\simeq\theta_{i}+2\pi

\displaystyle n_{11}\theta_{1}+n_{12}\theta_{2}

\displaystyle=2\pi m_{1},

(8)

\displaystyle n_{21}\theta_{1}+n_{22}\theta_{2}

\displaystyle=2\pi m_{2},

(9)

\displaystyle{\cal N}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\theta_{1}\\

\displaystyle\vec{\theta}_{1}=2\pi\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
0\end{array}\right)\rightarrow\vec{n}_{1}=2\pi\left(\begin{array}[]{c}n_{11}\\

n_{22}\end{array}\right).

The number of solutions is given by the area of the parallelogram on the

\displaystyle|\vec{n}_{1}\times\vec{n}_{2}|=|n_{11}n_{22}-n_{21}n_{12}|=|{\rm det}{\cal N}|.

(26)

This is the number of minima on the torus and hence the true “domain wall number” is
N_{DW}=|{\rm det}{\cal N}|

A

4
Cosmological Evolution

This analysis on the domain wall number in the previous section shows the cosmological evolution of topological defects is richer than what was discussed in
Lee:2024toz
when
g_{1}>1
.

When
U(1)_{1}\times U(1)_{2}
break sequentially, there are two independent networks of cosmic strings. At this point, any point on the torus is a minimum of the potential and different horizons choose different points. When
V_{1}
turns on, domain walls form between the strings. If
{\rm min}(|n_{11}|,|n_{12}|)=1
, a domain wall always ends with a string, forming the “string bundle”
Eto:2023aqr
, because the domain wall tension eventually pulls the strings closer and the defect end up being a closely held parallel bundle of strings that effectively behaves as a standard axion string.

If
{\rm min}(|n_{11}|,|n_{12}|)>1
, every string is attached to multiple walls and is pulled in different directions. They can form a more complicated network that may not simplify easily. In other words, the network is frustrated, similar to an anti-ferromagnet on a triangular lattice. Their simulation indeed showed that such a network can survive until
V_{2}
turns on. When
{\rm det}{\cal N}/g_{1}>1
, the system devolves into stable domain walls which is a cosmological disaster. We agree with this picture.

However, what is missing here is the possibility that separate infinite domain walls that do not end on pre-existing strings also form when
V_{1}
turns on because of
g_{1}
disconnected lines on the torus. These walls are stable even after
V_{2}
turns on. This occurs even when
{\rm det}{\cal N}/g_{1}=1
. If
{\rm det}{\cal N}/g_{1}>1
,
V_{2}
creates even more disconnected degenerate minima and hence domain walls (called “induced domain walls” in
Lee:2024toz
).

When
{\rm det}{\cal N}=1
, there are no stable domain walls. If
{\rm min}(|n_{11}|,|n_{12}|)=1
, the string bundles with
V_{1}
behave as an axion string for the single axion case, which is filled by a domain wall once
V_{2}
turns on, and collapse quickly. On the other hand if
{\rm min}(|n_{11}|,|n_{12}|)>1
all strings become attached to multiple walls and the system becomes frustrated. The string-wall network cannot simplify easily. But once
V_{2}
turns on, there is a single minimum of the potential and the network does collapse.

Here is an order of magnitude estimate of the condition for the collapse. Let us assume a hierarchy
\Lambda_{1}\gg\Lambda_{2}
. To minimize
V_{1}
, we find the heavy and light (massless at this stage) axion mass eigenstates,
\phi_{H}
and
\phi_{L}
, respectively,

\displaystyle\phi_{H}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left(\frac{n_{11}}{f_{1}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{n_{12}}{f_{2}}\right)^{2}}}\left(\frac{n_{11}}{f_{1}}a_{1}+\frac{n_{12}}{f_{2}}a_{2}\right),

(35)

\displaystyle\phi_{L}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left(\frac{n_{11}}{f_{1}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{n_{12}}{f_{2}}\right)^{2}}}\left(\frac{n_{12}}{f_{2}}a_{1}-\frac{n_{11}}{f_{1}}a_{2}\right).

(36)

Then the potential is

\displaystyle V_{1}=\Lambda_{1}^{4}\left[1-\cos\sqrt{\left(\frac{n_{11}}{f_{1}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{n_{12}}{f_{2}}\right)^{2}}\ \phi_{H}\right],

(37)

and the effective decay constant is

\displaystyle f_{H}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left(\frac{n_{11}}{f_{1}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{n_{12}}{f_{2}}\right)^{2}}}\ .

(38)

To simplify the discussion, let us assume
f_{1}\ll f_{2}
, so that
f_{H}\simeq f_{1}/n_{11}
. Then the walls formed after
V_{1}
has a tension
\sigma\approx\Lambda_{1}^{2}f_{H}\simeq\Lambda_{1}^{2}f_{1}
(assuming
n_{11}=O(1)
for the estimate). Therefore the mass of the network is
M\simeq\sigma H^{-2}\simeq\Lambda_{1}^{2}f_{1}H^{-2}
within a horizon size
H^{-1}
. Once
V_{2}
turns on, the wall receives a force due to the difference in the potential energies
F=\Delta VH^{-2}\simeq\Lambda_{2}^{4}H^{-2}
. The acceleration is
a=F/M\simeq\Lambda_{2}^{4}/(\Lambda_{1}^{2}f_{1})
. The walls collapse if their speed reaches speed of light within the horizon time
aH^{-1}\gtrsim 1
, and hence

\displaystyle\Lambda_{2}^{4}>\Lambda_{1}^{2}f_{1}H.

(39)

We need the network to collapse well before the Big-Bang Nucleosynthesis, and hence
H\gtrsim T_{\rm coll}^{2}/M_{Pl}\simeq{\rm 10~MeV}^{2}/M_{Pl}
(
T_{\rm coll}
is the temperature at the time of the collapse). We know
\Lambda_{2}=\Lambda_{QCD}\simeq 100
MeV. We obtain a constraint

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}^{2}f_{1}<\frac{M_{Pl}}{({\rm 10~MeV})^{2}}\Lambda_{2}^{4}\simeq 2\times 10^{18}~{\rm GeV}^{3}.

(40)

For instance, the following parameters saturate the constraint

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}\sim 10^{5}~{\rm GeV},\quad f_{1}\sim 10^{8}~{\rm GeV},\quad f_{2}\approx f_{a}\sim 10^{11}~{\rm GeV}.

(41)

Then the mass of the heavy ALP is

\displaystyle m_{H}\simeq\frac{\Lambda_{1}^{2}}{f_{1}}\simeq 10^{2}~{\rm GeV},

(42)

and it decays quickly into
gg
. There is no concern about its abundance or its contribution to
N_{\it eff}
. We will explore the parameter space fully in Section
6
.

If the model is supersymmetric, there is a potential issue with saxion dominating the energy density and producing a large entropy diluting baryon asymmetry and dark matter abundance. In our scenario, both axion sectors initially thermalize and their corresponding saxions are at the origin. Assuming second-order phase transitions, saxions adiabatically move to the final minima and we do not expect a problem. Obviously details depend on the dynamics of phase transitions and there are cases that may require further studies. We leave it for a later work. We repeat here that supersymmetry is optional in our discussion to enable grand unification and solve the hierarchy problem, not a neceessity.

Explicit Proof for
N_{DW}={\rm det}{\cal N}=1

When
{\rm det}{\cal N}=1
, there is only one minimum on the torus. Let us see it explicitly. Now defining the greatest common divisors for both rows

\displaystyle g_{1}

\displaystyle={\rm gcd}(n_{11},n_{12}),

(62)

\displaystyle g_{2}

\displaystyle={\rm gcd}(n_{21},n_{22}),

(63)

we can rewrite

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}n_{11}&n_{12}\\
n_{21}&n_{22}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}g_{1}p_{1}&g_{1}q_{1}\\
g_{2}p_{2}&g_{2}q_{2}\end{array}\right),

where
p_{1},q_{1}
are relatively prime, and so are
p_{2},q_{2}
.

Then inverting (
3
), we find

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\theta_{1}\\
\theta_{2}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=2\pi\frac{1}{g_{1}g_{2}(p_{1}q_{2}-p_{2}q_{1})}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}g_{2}q_{2}&-g_{1}q_{1}\\
-g_{2}p_{2}&g_{1}p_{1}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}m_{1}\\
m_{2}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=2\pi\frac{1}{g_{1}g_{2}(p_{1}q_{2}-p_{2}q_{1})}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}q_{2}&-q_{1}\\
-p_{2}&p_{1}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}g_{2}&0\\
0&g_{1}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}m_{1}\\
m_{2}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=2\pi\frac{1}{g_{1}g_{2}(p_{1}q_{2}-p_{2}q_{1})}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}q_{2}&-q_{1}\\
-p_{2}&p_{1}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}g_{2}m_{1}\\
g_{1}m_{2}\end{array}\right).

The question is whether these minima are located elsewhere from

on

2\pi

Consider
m_{1}=1
,
m_{2}=0

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\theta_{1}\\
\theta_{2}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=2\pi\frac{1}{g_{1}g_{2}(p_{1}q_{2}-p_{2}q_{1})}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}q_{2}&-q_{1}\\

or
\theta_{2}

n_{21},n_{22}

\displaystyle V=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Lambda_{i}^{4}\left[1-\cos\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}n_{ij}\theta_{j}\right)\right],

N

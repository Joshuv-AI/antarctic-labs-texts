Clinical Relevance of NIH Toolbox Cognition Battery Developmental Change in Intellectual and Developmental Disabilities: Associations with Verbal Reasoning
Authors: Andrew Dakopolos, Numfon Vilay, Iris Chen, Aaron J Kaat, Emma Condy, Jeanine Coleman, Elizabeth Berry-Kravis, Craig Erickson, Audrey Thurm, David Hessl
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe NIH Toolbox Cognition Battery (NIHTB-CB) assesses crystallized verbal skills, and shows strong psychometric properties and sensitivity to developmental change in intellectual and developmental disabilities. However, whether gains in NIHTB-CB Crystallized Cognition reflect improvements in broader verbal abilities is unclear, and this has implications for clinical interpretation of the measure in the context of treatment studies. Two-hundred sixty-three participants completed NIHTB-CB Crystalized Cognition subtests (Picture Vocabulary, Oral Reading Recognition) and Stanford-Binet 5th ed. subtests (Verbal Knowledge, Verbal Fluid Reasoning) at baseline and at two-year follow-up. Bivariate latent change score models examined whether changes in Crystalized Cognition were related to changes in broader verbal skills. Changes in Crystalized Cognition were significantly related to changes in Verbal Knowledge (β = 7.92, p = .009) and Verbal Fluid Reasoning (β = 4.91, p = .018). Higher baseline Crystalized Cognition predicted greater improvement in these verbal domains, while higher initial verbal scores also predicted more Crystalized Cognition growth. Change in the NIHTB-CB Crystalized Composite was significantly related to growth in verbal reasoning in children and young adults with IDD. This may indicate that the Crystalized Composite is a useful proxy for broader verbal skills, underscoring its clinical relevance.Keywords: Language, cognition, fragile X syndrome, down syndrome, outcome measure

The NIH Toolbox Cognition Battery (NIHTB-CB) is a tablet-based measure designed as brief, efficient, highly accessible and standardized tests of language, memory, executive function, and processing speed to serve as a “common currency” in neurological research (Akshoomoff et al., 2013). The language component, the Crystallized Cognition Composite (CC), comprising Picture Vocabulary [PV] and Oral Reading Recognition [ORR]) has demonstrated feasibility, reliability, validity (Shields et al., 2020), and sensitivity to developmental change (Shields et al., 2023) in youth with intellectual and developmental disabilities (IDD). A Phase 2 trial of a phosphodiesterase 4D inhibitor in adult males with fragile X syndrome showed that the CC and corresponding subtests may be sensitive to cognitive enhancing treatments (Berry-Kravis et al., 2021); clinical trials such as this have emphasized the importance of evaluating the NIHTB-CB as a potential fit-for-purpose outcome measure.

Among individuals with IDD, the CC shows strong psychometric properties and sensitivity to developmental (Dakopolos et al., 2024; Shields et al., 2023) and pharmacological change (Berry-Kravis et al., 2021). Notably, two-year CC changes were associated with improved daily living skills among children and young adults with IDD (Dakopolos et al., 2024). However, it remains unclear whether CC changes reflect improvement in relatively “narrow” skills – namely, receptive one-word vocabulary and single word reading – or broader verbal reasoning. The present study evaluated the clinical relevance of the NIHTB-CB as a key outcome measure by assessing whether CC improvements are associated with gains in broader verbal reasoning abilities as measured by gold-standard measures (i.e., the Verbal Knowledge and Verbal Fluid Reasoning subtests of the Stanford Binet 5th Ed.; Roid & Pomplun, 2012) among children and young adults with IDD.

Method

Participants

This multi-site longitudinal study included participants aged 6–26 years at Visit 1 with diagnosed or suspected IDD. ID or borderline ID was determined per DSM-5 criteria (American Psychiatric Association, 2013), adaptive behavior deficits per Vineland Adaptive Behavior Scales, Third Edition (VABS-3; Sparrow et al., 2016), and IQ < 80 on the SB5. Participants had a mental age equivalent of ≥3.0 years (SB5), consistent with NIHTB-CB requirements. Usual treatments were stable for ≥4 weeks prior to visits (Dakopolos et al., 2024). Of 318 participants, 55 were excluded: 21 with IQ > 79 and 34 with mental age <3 years, leaving a final sample of 263. See Table 1 for sample characteristics.

Measures

The NIHTB-CB Version 2 is an iPad-based assessment composed of seven tests reflecting fluid cognition (five tests) and crystallized cognition (two tests), and a total cognition composite combining these two broad domains. Here, unadjusted standard scores (USSs) for PV and ORR were used to construct a latent CC factor. USSs were chosen as most appropriate for longitudinal contexts, insofar as they reflect normalized “raw” performance on a test without referring back to a normative reference group that may have also changed over the course of development and aging. Testing times are 4 minutes and 6 minutes to administer PV and ORR respectively.

The Stanford-Binet Intelligence Scales, 5th ed. (SB5) is standardized for individuals between 2–85 years and provides an overall index of intellectual ability. Change sensitive scores (CSS) from Verbal Knowledge (VK) and Verbal Fluid Reasoning (VFR) were used in the present study. CSSs are person-ability scores useful for monitoring developmental- or treatment-related changes (Farmer et al., 2020, 2022). Verbal Knowledge (VK) includes questions tapping vocabulary and Verbal Fluid Reasoning (VFR) measures verbal problem solving.

Statistical Analyses

Bivariate latent change score (BLCS) models (Kievit et al., 2018) estimated two-year changes in latent CC (PV and ORR) alongside VK (Model 1) and VFR (Model 2). This method allowed us to examine the association between latent change for CC and broader verbal reasoning skills. Figure 1 provides a diagram of the BLCS model for Model 2. We included an estimate of the correlated change between ΔCC and ΔVK and ΔVFR respectively, and also assessed cross-domain coupling of CC and verbal skills. Time between visits and participant age were used as covariates at the latent level. We assessed each model’s fit by comparing it to its base model (utilizing robust fit statistics CFI, TLI, and RMSEA) (Savalei, 2018), and indices of fit based on Little (2013; [i.e., CFI > 0.85; TLI > 0.85; RMSEA < .08]). Results

Model 1: Crystalized Cognition and Verbal Knowledge

Model 1 contained latent scores for CC (ORR, PV), and CSS score for VK at Visit 1 and Visit 2. Latent change scores for CC and VK were included to model change from Visit 1 to Visit 2. Results indicated overall good model fit (SRMR = 0.70, CFI = 0.984, RMSEA = 0.064 [90% CI: 0.031–0.097]). All parameters subsequently reported are standardized. The model revealed a positive relationship between ΔCC and ΔVK (β = 7.92 (3.03), p = .009) indicating that changes in CC relate to change in VK over time in our sample. In addition, CC at Visit 1 positively predicted ΔVK (β = 0.34 (0.09), p < .001), but not ΔCC (β = −0.19 (0.11), p = .08) demonstrating that higher CC scores at Visit 1 predicted greater change in VK, but did not predict change in CC. Finally, VK at Visit 1 negatively predicted ΔVK (β = −0.67(0.15), p < .001), but positively predicted ΔCC (β = 0.36(0.18), p = .041), demonstrating that higher VK scores at Visit 1 predicted less VK growth, but more CC growth.

Model 2: Crystalized Cognition and Verbal Fluid Reasoning

Results indicated overall good model fit (SRMR = 0.60, CFI = 0.988, RMSEA = 0.058 [90% CI: 0.021–0.091]). In model 2, ΔCC was significantly related to ΔVFR (β = 4.91 (2.07), p = .018), indicating that changes in CC relate to change in VFR. In addition, CC at Visit 1 positively predicted ΔVFR (β = 0.21 (0.05), p < .001), demonstrating that higher CC scores at Visit 1 predicted greater change in VFR. However, CC at Visit 1 negatively predicted ΔCC (β = −0.26 (0.05), p < .001) suggesting that those with higher CC scores at visit 1 experienced less CC growth than those with lower starting CC scores. VFR at Visit 1 negatively predicted ΔVFR (β = −0.62 (0.10), p < .001), but positively predicted ΔCC (β = 0.67 (0.20), p = .001), demonstrating that higher VFR scores at Visit 1 predicted less VFR growth, but more CC growth.

Discussion

The present study sought to determine associations of developmental change between NIHTB-CB Crystallized Cognition and SB-5 Verbal Knowledge and Verbal Fluid Reasoning over a two-year period in individuals with IDD. Results showed a pattern of associations, such that increases in CC corresponded with increases in VK and VFR in our IDD sample (see Figure 2). Thus, NIHTB-CB CC demonstrated developmental sensitivity in a broader set of verbal skills as measured by the SB5, reinforcing its value as a brief but sensitive outcome measure for verbal cognitive development. Compared to other assessments like the SB5, the NIHTB-CB offers ease of use, portability, and shorter duration.

Results indicate that participants with higher baseline verbal abilities experienced the greatest gains in crystallized language skills over the two-year interval. This suggests that individuals with IDD who have stronger initial verbal reasoning capabilities may be more likely to display greater language growth, and therefore may significantly benefit from targeted treatments or interventions. Conversely, individuals with IDD with weaker initial verbal reasoning capabilities on the SB5 showed less language growth in CC, indicating potential grounds for more focused/intensive intervention services in that population. Future language/communication intervention studies could utilize NIHTB-CB CC scores at baseline to investigate this hypothesis.

The present study is not without limitations. This study was comprised of just two assessment time points, which potentially reduces statistical power (Dakopolos et al., 2024). Another limitation is that we did not control for differing levels and types of services or interventions between Visit 1 and Visit 2. Since many individuals with IDD already require caregiver support or additional services (Burke et al., 2018; Heaton et al., 2014), these untracked factors may have contributed to observed improvements. We also focused on youth with IDD who had mental ages 3.0 years and above, excluding those with more significant delays. Finally, it should be noted that for these analyses, we focused only on the Crystallized components of the NIHTB-CB; future work is needed to evaluate the Fluid Composite tests for their clinically relevant changes that may be observed in intervention studies.

Despite our relatively older sample (m age = 15.5 years at time 1), the CC detected improvements in crystalized language skills that were linked to gains in higher-order verbal reasoning. Thus, the NIHTB-CB CC is a clinically relevant measure of growth in verbal cognition in IDD populations.

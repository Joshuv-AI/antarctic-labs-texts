Exploring the genetic basis of essential hypertension: association with AGT gene variants in an Iranian population
Authors: Mohammad Piryaei, Asiyeh Sadat Zahedi, Aram Halimi, Mahdi Akbarzadeh, Mina Jahangiri, Mehdi Hedayati, Farzad Hadaegh, Amir Abbas Momenan, Fereidoun Azizi, Maryam Sadat Daneshpour, Arash Ghanbarian
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEssential hypertension (EH) is a complex disorder influenced by a combination of genetic and environmental factors. The renin-angiotensin system (RAS), particularly the angiotensinogen (AGT) gene, plays a crucial role in blood pressure regulation. While previous studies have implicated AGT gene polymorphisms in EH risk, their impact on the Iranian population remains relatively unexplored.ObjectiveTo investigate the association between common variants of the AGT gene and their haplotypes with EH in a large cohort of Iranian individuals.MethodsThis nested case-control study utilized data from the Tehran Cardiometabolic Genetic Study (TCGS), encompassing 6,100 adults (3,217 cases with EH and 2,883 controls). Eleven Single Nucleotide Polymorphisms (SNPs) within the AGT gene were genotyped. Logistic regression models, adjusted for age, sex, and Body Mass Index (BMI), were employed to assess the association between SNPs and EH. Haplotype analysis was conducted to evaluate the combined effects of multiple SNPs.ResultsIn a univariate analysis, eight of the eleven SNPs had strong links to EH. Logistic regression analysis showed that certain alleles of rs2493141, rs2004776, and especially rs2493151 were linked to a higher risk of EH. Even after taking BMI into account, rs2493151 remained significant. Four haplotypes were found to be significantly linked to the risk of EH through haplotype analysis.ConclusionThis study provides evidence for the involvement of AGT gene variants and haplotypes in the pathogenesis of EH in the Iranian population. These findings highlight the importance of considering genetic factors in the development and management of this prevalent condition.Keywords: Essential hypertension, AGT gene, Genetic polymorphisms, Haplotype analysis

Introduction

Essential hypertension (EH), characterized by persistently elevated blood pressure without a discernible underlying cause, is a major global health concern. This complex disorder arises from an intricate interplay of genetic and environmental factors [1]. While the exact etiology remains elusive, family studies have consistently demonstrated a significant heritable component, with estimates ranging from 20% to 60% [2–4]. The renin-angiotensin system (RAS) plays a pivotal role in regulating blood pressure. This intricate cascade, involving a series of enzymes and peptides, culminates in the production of angiotensin II, a potent vasoconstrictor [5].

The angiotensinogen gene (AGT), located on chromosome 1q42-43, encodes the precursor protein for angiotensin II [6]. Numerous studies have implicated genetic variations within the RAS pathway, particularly within the AGT gene, in the pathogenesis of EH [7]. Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) across diverse populations have consistently identified associations between specific AGT gene polymorphisms and increased EH risk [8, 9].

While previous studies in Iranian populations have mainly focused on individual polymorphisms such as M235T (rs699) and T174M (rs4762), a broader understanding of the genetic basis of essential hypertension (EH) requires considering haplotype structures [10–12]. Haplotype analysis examines how alleles occur together on the same chromosome. It can reveal combined effects of multiple polymorphisms and provide deeper insights than single-marker analyses. Additionally, haplotype analysis reduces the number of statistical tests, helping to minimize false positives and increase statistical power [13]. To date, no study has comprehensively investigated the association between AGT gene haplotypes and EH risk in the Iranian population. Therefore, this study aims to examine the relationship between common variants of the AGT gene and their haplotypes in a large cohort of individuals from Tehran, Iran, to further elucidate the genetic underpinnings of EH in this population.

Materials and methods

Subjects and population

Participants were selected from the Tehran Cardiometabolic Genetic Study (TCGS) [14, 15] for this nested case-control investigation. The TCGS is an ongoing genetic study included in the Tehran Lipid and Glucose Study (TLGS) [16]. In a sample of people from Tehran’s (Iran’s capital city) in the 13th district, this cohort study calculates the prevalence of non-communicable disease risk factors. The first TLGS survey was conducted on 15,005 individuals between 1999 and 2001, and subjects were genotyped and followed up every three years to detect newly identified diseases. After obtaining informed consent and completing interviews to collect demographic information, each participant was referred to trained physicians and laboratories for clinical examinations and blood sampling at each visit; details are published elsewhere [14, 17]. For the current study, 18,864 adults aged ≥ 18 years, who had systolic blood pressure (SBP) and diastolic blood pressure (DBP) data in at least one of the six phases of TLGS (1999–2018), were included. A total of 2883 TCGS participants with normal blood pressure (control group) and 3217 patients with EH (case group) were identified. Individuals were diagnosed with EH based on the 2017 American College of Cardiology and American Heart Association (ACC/AHA) guidelines [18]. Thus, patients were classified into the category of EH if they had either taken hypertension medication during at least one phase of the study or had SBP and DBP within the ACC/AHA-defined range for stage-2 hypertension (SBP ≥ 140 mm Hg or DBP ≥ 90 mm Hg) throughout at least two phases. In addition, SBP and DBP levels in the control group were within the normal range (SBP < 120 mm Hg and DBP < 80 mm Hg) throughout all phases. The Shahid Beheshti University Research Institute for Endocrine’s ethics committee approved this study (Ethical code: IR.SBMU.ENDOCRINE.REC.146.), which followed the 1964 Helsinki Declaration and its updates or comparable ethical standards.

Genotyping and SNP selection

Standard Proteinase K salting-out was used to extract DNA from white blood cells. The accuracy and quality control of the extracted samples were assessed using spectrophotometry and electrophoresis. DNA samples from TCGS participants were genotyped using HumanOmniExpress-24-v1-0 bead chips at the deCODE genetics company (Iceland) following the manufacturer’s specifications (Illumina Inc., San Diego, CA, USA) [19].

To select SNPs in the AGT gene, we used the GWAS Catalog (www.genome.gov/gwastudies, and accessed on 4 December 2022). We sought SNPs that showed significant association (p-value lower than 5 × 10− 8) with CAD, SBP, DBP, mean arterial pressure, beta-blocking agent measurement, angiotensinogen measurement, and agents acting on the renin-angiotensin system use measurement.

PLINK (version 1.9) was used to assess the data quality control, which included individual-level missingness, divergence from Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium (HWE), and allele frequency [20]. The 1000 Genomes database was used to determine global minor allele frequencies for comparison, and the Plink program was used to compile a list of minor allele frequencies (MAF) for SNPs.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive

All continuous variables were represented by means and standard deviations, while categorical variables were summarized by frequencies and percentages. The chi-square or Student’s t-test was used to determine the differences between the two groups.

Single SNP analysis

We used three logistic regression models within the PLINK tool to assess the association of the polymorphisms with EH. The odds ratios (ORs) and confidence intervals (CI) were provided for the four genetic: models, additive, recessive, dominant, and codominant as well as for the crude and adjusted for age, sex, and body mass index (BMI) models. The false discovery rate (FDR) method was used to perform multiple tests to resolve multiple comparisons and control false positives and false negatives. Odds ratios (ORs) and confidence intervals (CIs) were estimated under additive, dominant, recessive, and codominant models. Both crude and adjusted models were examined. Haplotype analysis

A pairwise D’ statistic was utilized to measure (linkage disequilibrium) LD between the eleven SNPs used in haplotype analysis. In the LD analysis of Haploview 4.2, the algorithm proposed by Gabriel et al. [21] was used to investigate the structure of the LD block. The association of haplotypes within the blocks with the risk of EH was determined in the case-control panel [22]. The web-based platform SHEsis was used to perform haplotype Association analysis adjusted for covariates including age, gender, and BMI [23].

Results

Population description

Table 4 summarizes the demographic and clinical characteristics of the 3217 hypertensive individuals and 2883 normotensive controls recruited from the TCGS population. As expected, the hypertensive group exhibited significantly higher values for most clinical parameters, including age, male sex, smoking prevalence, use of diabetic and lipid-lowering medications, BMI, and waist circumference. Notably, the hypertensive group also displayed significantly higher levels of triglycerides and lower levels of High-Density Lipoprotein Cholesterol (HDL-c) compared to the control group.

SNP selection and genotype distribution

Eleven SNPs within the AGT gene were selected for analysis based on their potential functional significance and minor allele frequencies exceeding 5%. As presented in Table 5, all SNPs demonstrated Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium (p > 0.01) within the control group.

Association of AGT gene polymorphisms with essential hypertension

According to Table 1 univariate analysis demonstrated statistically significant associations between seven of the eleven investigated SNPs, including rs2478539, rs943580, rs2493141, rs2493133, rs2478543, rs2493151, and rs2493134. As shown in Table 5, logistic regression analysis, adjusted for age and sex confirmed these associations for all 11 SNPs in the additive model (FDR < 0.05). Specifically, logistic regression analysis indicated that the A allele of rs2493141 (OR = 1.15, 95% CI: 1.01–1.30, p = 0.036, FDR = 0.016; Table 2), and the A allele of rs2493151 (OR = 1.16, 95% CI: 1.02–1.32, p = 0.022, FDR = 0.016; Table 2) were significantly associated with an increased risk of EH under the additive model, after adjusting for age and sex. The C allele of rs2004776 also showed a borderline significant association (OR = 1.15, 95% CI: 0.99–1.33, p = 0.061, FDR = 0.020; Table 2). Furthermore, the AA genotype of rs2493141 was observed in 15.6% of EH cases and 14.1% of controls, and for rs2493151 in 15.5% of EH cases and 13.8% of controls Table 3, further supporting the potential role of these variants in hypertension risk. Adjustment for BMI attenuated the association between most SNPs and EH, except rs2493151, which remained marginally significantly associated with EH in the dominant model (OR = 1.33, 95% CI 1.07–1.64, p = 0.008, FDR = 0.08), as presented in Table 6. In summary, logistic regression analysis, adjusted for age and sex, confirmed associations for multiple SNPs under the additive model (FDR < 0.05). Notably, the A allele of rs2493151 demonstrated the most robust and consistent association with EH (OR = 1.16, 95% CI: 1.02–1.32, p = 0.022, FDR = 0.016). This association remained significant even after further adjustment for BMI in the dominant model (OR = 1.33, 95% CI 1.07–1.64, p = 0.008, FDR = 0.08). Conversely, the associations for rs2493141 and rs2004776 diminished following BMI adjustment.

Haplotype

Figure 2 illustrates the LD plot of the 11 SNPs analyzed in the AGT gene using Haploview software. Discussion

This study investigated the association between eleven SNPs within the AGT gene and the risk of EH in a large Iranian population. Our findings provide evidence for a significant association between specific AGT gene variants and EH susceptibility.

Single SNP analysis

Univariate and multivariate analyses revealed significant associations between several SNPs and EH, with certain alleles (A of rs2493141, C of rs2004776, and A of rs2493151) demonstrating a significantly increased risk of EH. These findings underscore the importance of genetic variations within the AGT gene in the pathogenesis of this complex disorder. However, the observed associations were attenuated after adjusting for BMI, emphasizing the interplay between genetic and environmental factors in the development of EH [24–26].

Comparison with previous studies

Our findings are partially consistent with those of Zongjin Li et al. (2022), who reported an association between the rs2493134 SNP in the AGT gene and hypertension in a Tibetan population [27]. However, this association was not observed in our study after adjusting for BMI. These discrepancies highlight the potential influence of ethnic and environmental factors on the genetic determinants of EH. Padma Gunda et al. (2021) reported a protective effect of the rs3789678 SNP in the AGT gene in an Indian population [28]. This SNP was not included in our analysis, emphasizing the need for population-specific investigations to fully understand the genetic architecture of EH. Purkait et al. (2017) performed an extensive SNP and haplotype analysis of AGT in an Indian cohort, establishing that variants in both promoter (rs11568020, rs5050) and exonic regions (rs4762, rs699), along with particular haplotypes, were significantly correlated with hypertension risk [29]. Conversely, a study conducted within a South African cohort revealed no significant correlation between AGT variants and hypertension, indicating that genetic influences may differ based on ethnic and environmental contexts [30].

In addition to variations in populations, AGT gene polymorphisms have also been linked to changes in physiological and therapeutic responses. For instance, changes in the AGT gene can raise levels of angiotensinogen in the blood, which could make blood pressure go up even more [31]. Furthermore, AGT variants have demonstrated an impact on responses to antihypertensive therapies, highlighting their significance in personalized treatment strategies [32].

Haplotype analysis

Haplotype analysis revealed that specific combinations of SNPs within the AGT gene were significantly associated with EH risk. The GCCTG haplotype in block 1 and the TCGTA haplotype in block 2 were associated with an increased risk of EH, while the ATTGA and CTCCG haplotypes were associated with a decreased risk. These findings suggest that the combined effect of multiple SNPs within these haplotype blocks may exert a stronger influence on EH risk compared to individual SNPs.

Although the associations were statistically significant, the effect sizes were modest (e.g., OR = 1.09 and 1.10), suggesting that these haplotypes may contribute to EH risk only to a limited extent at the individual level. Therefore, their potential clinical relevance should be interpreted with caution.

Including haplotypes in statistical analyses may enable the identification of epistatic interactions among variants at a haplotype locus, thus enhancing the power of association studies [33–35]. In the present study, following adjustments for age, gender, and BMI, the rs2493151 variant was the only one associated with EH; however, the haplotype analysis demonstrated an association of four haplotypes in two blocks in the AGT gene with EH after these covariates were considered. Notably, the rs2493151 variant was not included in any haplotype blocks of candidate SNPs, indicating that it may influence EH through distinct molecular mechanisms or interactions that are not represented by the haplotypes in the defined blocks. These findings highlight the necessity of conducting both haplotype and individual SNP analyses to fully elucidate the range of genetic influences on EH.

As mentioned above, our finding indicated that two haplotypes showed positive associations with EH. One of the two haplotypes, a common ancestral haplotype (GCCTG), contains major alleles from four intronic variants (rs943580, rs2493133, rs2478543, rs2478539), and a well-studied missense variant (rs699). In contrast, the ATTGA haplotype (block 1), which includes the minor allele of these SNPs, demonstrated a protective association with EH. The relationship between AGT gene haplotypes and hypertension has been documented across multiple populations [36–40]. In a study examining the association between COVID-19-related gene haplotypes and blood pressure and metabolite levels in an Iranian population, the haplotype block containing rs2478539, rs699, rs2493134, and rs943580 was associated with changes in systolic blood pressure [37]. It has been found that some haplotypes of the AGT gene that include rs699 are associated with hypertension in French, Japanese, and Indian [36, 38–40].

Biological implications

The observed associations between specific haplotypes and EH risk may be explained by their influence on AGT gene expression and function. The GCCTG haplotype, which includes the rs699 polymorphism, has been previously implicated in increased angiotensinogen plasma levels and EH risk in several populations [37–41]. The common SNP rs699 (M268T) is a missense polymorphism located in exon two, causing a substitution of methionine (M) with threonine (T) at codon 268 of angiotensinogen, which increases the expression of the AGT gene and subjects homozygous for the G allele have increased angiotensinogen plasma concentrations [12, 42]. Consequently, this SNP has been associated with an increased incidence of EH, which is linked to elevated plasma angiotensinogen levels, for decades [36, 40, 42–45]. In the current study, all SNPs in the block 1 associated haplotype were in strong LD (D’ >0.9) with rs699. Since these variants are inherited jointly, they may potentially affect angiotensinogen plasma concentrations and contribute to the development of essential hypertension (EH) via the same mechanism [46].

In haplotype block 2 of the present study, it was shown that the TCGTA haplotype, consisting of the major alleles of the intronic variants rs2493134, rs2493135, and rs2493136, as well as the minor alleles of the two intronic variants rs2004776 and rs2493141, was associated with an increased risk of EH. Conversely, the haplotype formed by the alternative alleles of each of these variants was protective against EH. The majority of trait-associated variant loci identified in GWAS are located in non-coding regions, with 45% found in intronic regions and 43% in intergenic regions [47]. Mopidevi et al. (2019) developed a transgenic mouse model incorporating the human renin gene (REN) along with two AGT haplotypes to explore their effects on gene expression and blood pressure regulation. The study identified that the SNPs rs2004776 and rs2493134 are part of a larger haplotype that includes multiple SNPs influencing the binding of various transcription factors. These interactions collectively regulate the expression of the AGT gene. Specifically, rs2004776 was shown to bind transcription factors, thereby modulating AGT gene expression. The presence of this polymorphism in an open chromatin region suggests it plays a crucial role in the regulation of AGT expression [48]. The results of a study, however, suggested that the haplotype formed by these two variants was unrelated to EH; in a case-control study involving 757 Chinese participants, a total of 25 haplotypes of genes related to the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) were analyzed as independent variables for their association with EH, with the AGT gene haplotype comprising the variants rs699, rs2004776, rs2493134, and rs3789679. The analysis revealed no statistically significant differences in haplotype distributions between the case and control groups after adjusting for confounding factors [49]. This finding contrasts with the results of our study, and one possible explanation for the lack of association observed in the Chinese population may be the smaller sample size used in that research. It is important to emphasize that in the current study, the results from the single nucleotide analysis and the haplotype analysis of all examined variants corroborate each other. Specifically, the haplotypes that were more prevalent in individuals with essential hypertension EH were composed of alleles that were individually more common in this group, and vice versa. Therefore, the stronger association with EH observed in the haplotype analysis (even after adjusting for covariates) suggests a synergistic effect of SNPs within associated haplotypes on blood pressure.

This extensive study offers substantial evidence that polymorphisms in the AGT gene, especially rs2493151, correlate with hypertension risk in the Iranian population. The persistence of rs2493151’s significant association following BMI adjustment highlights its potential as an independent genetic determinant of hypertension.

The attenuation of most SNP associations after BMI adjustment suggests that BMI plays a dual role in the AGT–hypertension relationship. While BMI may superficially appear as a confounder due to its correlation with both genotype and blood pressure, it is more likely to operate as a mediator through which AGT variants exert their effects. Variants within the AGT gene may influence adiposity and related hormonal pathways, thereby contributing to hypertension risk indirectly via BMI [50–52]. This interpretation is consistent with evidence linking RAS-related genes to metabolic regulation and adipose tissue activity. In addition, BMI may also capture shared environmental factors such as diet and lifestyle that modulate the genetic impact on blood pressure.

Strength, Limitations, and future directions

This study has several strengths, including a large sample size, rigorous statistical analysis, and the inclusion of haplotype analysis. However, some limitations should be acknowledged. The study population may not be fully representative of the entire Iranian population. Additionally, while we adjusted for several confounding factors, the influence of other environmental factors, such as diet and physical activity, cannot be completely ruled out. Furthermore, the cross-sectional design of the study limits our ability to establish definitive causal relationships.

Importantly, this study did not include functional assays to determine the biological consequences of the associated SNPs or haplotypes. Notably, although rs699 is a well-characterized missense variant and rs2493151 demonstrated consistent associations with EH, their mechanistic roles remain to be validated experimentally. Additionally, our findings were not replicated in an independent Iranian cohort or in other ethnic populations, which limits the generalizability and robustness of the observed associations. Future studies incorporating replication cohorts and functional analyses are warranted to confirm these findings and elucidate their biological significance.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study offers substantial evidence for the correlation between AGT gene variants and essential hypertension within an Iranian cohort. The rs2493151 polymorphism proved to be the most reliable and autonomous genetic indicator, maintaining its significance despite BMI adjustment. The weakening of other associations after adjusting for BMI highlights the intricate interplay between genetic and metabolic factors in the etiology of hypertension. Furthermore, the identification of specific AGT haplotypes linked to risk and protection underscores the significance of haplotype analysis.

These findings collectively augment our comprehension of the genetic framework of essential hypertension (EH) and advocate for the incorporation of genetic screening and lifestyle determinants in individualized hypertension risk evaluation and management.

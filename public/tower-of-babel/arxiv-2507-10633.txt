Electron polarization induced by electroweak interaction in high-energy scattering off light nuclei
Minh Truong Vo, Vu Dong Tran, Quang Hung Nguyen

Abstract
Electron polarization plays a significant role in studies on nuclear scattering. Nevertheless, the development of a comprehensive approach to such a problem remains challenging, particularly at the relativistic electron-energy scale. Herein, we present a theoretical approach to investigate the impact of electron polarization in scattering off unoriented light nuclei, based on the multipole expansion for the scattering cross section at high energies. Numerical calculations for stable 6,7 Li and unstable 7 Be nuclei show that the longitudinal polarization and weak interaction are not explicitly correlated when electrons scatter at 0 ∘ for all energy scales. In contrast, their correlation is strongly exhibited at other scattering angles when energy exceeds 10 GeV. A comparison between stable and unstable nuclei reveals that the stability of nuclei significantly affects the electron polarization contribution to the scattering cross section. This study opens new approaches to the nuclear structure problems, in particular the EMC effect, via deep inelastic electron-nucleus scattering using the unified electroweak theory.

Obviously, the polarized cross sections [Eqs. (3a)–(3c)] depend on the polarization degrees
\xi
and
\xi^{\prime}
. For simplicity, we assign
\xi\,(\xi^{\prime})=+1
for the right-handed polarization and
\xi\,(\xi^{\prime})=-1
for the left-handed one. We then consider all possible variations of electron polarization as following cases. Case 1: both incoming and outgoing electron beams are unpolarized. This case has been studied in detail in our recent study
truo24
. Case 2: electron polarizations before and after scattering are preserved, i.e.,
\xi=\xi^{\prime}
. Case 3: an initially polarized beam becomes unpolarized, i.e.,
\xi=+1/-1
and
\xi^{\prime}=0
(or
\xi^{\prime}
is taken over +1 and
-1
). Case 4: an electron beam reverses its initial polarization after scattering, i.e.,
\xi=-\xi^{\prime}
.

The total cross section (1) will be equivalent to that of unpolarized electron scattering when taking the summation over all possible electron polarization states. This indicates that an unpolarized electron beam has the spin components uniformly distributed in all directions
dapo18
. We can also see that, if
\xi=\xi^{\prime}=+1/-1
, the electron polarization contribution via the pure electromagnetic term [Eq. (3a)] to the total cross section is identical to the unpolarized term [Eq. (2a)], namely,
\sigma_{F}^{11}=\sigma_{F}^{-1-1}=\sigma_{F}^{0}
. This proves that electron polarization is preserved under the electromagnetic interaction, as previously mentioned in Ref.
donn86
. In contrast, the contributions via the terms involving the weak interaction [Eqs. (3b)–(3c)] to the total cross section are unequal and different from the respective unpolarized ones [Eqs. (2b)–(2c)]. Particularly, as
\xi=+1/-1
and
\xi^{\prime}=0
, only the last two terms (3b) and (3c) make a nonzero contribution to the total cross section. This reveals that electron polarization can be changed only when the weak interaction is present, while it is unchanged under the electromagnetic one. It is also easy to find a similar result for the inverse case, i.e.,
\sigma_{F}^{0\pm 1}=\sigma_{F}^{\pm 10}=0
, while
\sigma_{FZ}^{0\pm 1}=\sigma_{FZ}^{\pm 10}\neq 0
and
\sigma_{Z}^{0\pm 1}=\sigma_{Z}^{\pm 10}\neq 0
. So, we conclude that the weak interaction can make an electron beam with the
\xi=+1/-1
initial polarization become an unpolarized beam after scattering, and vice versa. However, the total cross section (1) will vanish when the electron polarizations before and after scattering have opposite signs. This implies that the electroweak interaction cannot cause the spin orientation of all electrons to be reversed, but alters half of it. The specific expressions corresponding to all cases under discussion can be readily derived from Eqs. (3a)–(3c).

It can be explained that the weak interaction between electrons and nuclei strongly depends on the nuclear stability. This property affects the correlation between the weak interaction and electron polarization, along with their contribution in each scattering process.

Furthermore, although the
\sigma_{\text{total}}^{10}
term can take negative values, its magnitude is significantly smaller than the
\sigma_{\text{total}}^{0}
and
\sigma_{\text{total}}^{11}

In particular, when the longitudinally polarized electrons move straight forward after scattering, the electron polarization-weak interaction correlation is not exhibited. This is evidenced by the ratios at
\theta=0^{\circ}
in Tab. I. Their values remain nearly unchanged at all considered energies, except for
\sigma_{\text{total}}^{10}
, whose contribution can be omitted. So, besides nuclear spin orientation, it is evident that electron polarization and its transformation manner under the weak interaction play a crucial role in generating various types of asymmetry in electron-nucleus scattering at high energies.

In conclusion, we have performed a systematic study on the impact of electron polarization on scattering off unoriented light nuclei using the unified electroweak theory. Numerical calculations of the scattering cross sections in different electron polarization cases for three
6,7
Li and
7
Be light nuclei are obtained by directly calculating their multipole form factors, using the formulas provided in Ref.
truo24
. The results obtained are then compared and/or correlated with the corresponding cross sections for the unpolarized electron scattering case. It has been found that at the scattering angle of
\theta=0^{\circ}
, the longitudinal electron polarization does not explicitly correlate with the weak interaction for all energy scales. Such a correlation is strongly exhibited at nonzero scattering angles (
\theta=5^{\circ}-8^{\circ}
) and high energies above 10 GeV. By comparing the results obtained for stable and unstable
nuclei, we found that the nuclear stability significantly influences the contribution of electron polarization to the scattering cross section. This research direction can be extended in many ways, specifically considering various state transitions within a single nucleus or different nuclei. Then, the asymmetry resulting solely from electron polarization and the weak interaction can be figured out. In addition, other interesting results are expected once the nuclear spin orientation and charge-changing weak current are included. Moreover, the multipole expansion technique for the scattering cross section within the unified electroweak theory also suggests a new approach to directly study the EMC effect in a target nucleus using both unpolarized and polarized electrons. More specifically, we calculate the accumulative cross section contributing by separate nucleons inside a certain nucleus and compare it with that made of a similar number of nucleons bound inside the same one. It means that we can investigate the above effect upon only a single nucleus instead of indirectly comparing its scattering cross section with that of a lighter nucleus. These extensive studies will provide further vital information, contributing to a thorough understanding of the nature of the EMC effect in deep inelastic electron-nucleus scattering.

Strong association between sarcopenia and visceral fat in the long term after cholecystectomy: A cross-sectional study using the ISarcoPRM algorithm
Authors: Semih Sezer, Selim Demirci, Özgür Kara, Murat Kara
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Cholecystectomy generally has no adverse effects on health. Studies demonstrating the association between metabolic diseases and long-term effects of cholecystectomy are increasing. preperitoneal fat thickness (PFT) is an important indicator of metabolic syndrome. Metabolic syndrome is a risk factor for sarcopenia. The aim of our study was to investigate the relationship between cholecystectomy and sarcopenia-visceral fat using the ISarcoPRM algorithm, which is an easy, reliable, and inexpensive method based on ultrasound measurement, to evaluate sarcopenia in patients with long-term previous cholecystectomy. This cross-sectional study included participants who had undergone cholecystectomy at least 5 years ago and a control group with similar characteristics. Sarcopenia was assessed using the ISarcoPRM algorithm, incorporating the sonographic thigh adjustment ratio (STAR), grip strength, and chair stand test (CST). Together with low STAR values (<1.0 for females and <1.4 for males), ow grip strength (<19 kg for females or <32 kg for males), and/or prolonged CST duration (≥12 seconds) were used to diagnose sarcopenia. Ultrasound was used to measure the visceral fat thickness. The investigation of sarcopenia and PFT values between individuals with and without cholecystectomy was planned. A total of 158 participants were included, including 89 post-cholecystectomy patients and 69 controls. Binary regression analysis revealed that cholecystectomy was positively associated with sarcopenia (OR = 2.788, 95% CI: 1.054–7.375, P = .039). However, when PFT was included, it was independently associated with sarcopenia (OR = 1.157, 95% CI: 1.067–1.254, P < .001), and the relationship with cholecystectomy disappeared (P > .05). PFT values of 10.0 mm or higher were associated with 5.875 times (95% CI: 2.035–16.961, P = .001) increased odds of sarcopenia. Sarcopenia was 2.8 times more frequent in patients who had undergone cholecystectomy than in those who had not, but this relationship was mediated by increased PFT. The risk of sarcopenia increased up to 5.875 times when the PFT exceeded 10 mm.

1. Introduction

Cholecystectomy is recognized as a harmless operation with low mortality and morbidity and is commonly performed worldwide.[1,2] The unexplained increase in metabolic disorders such as dyslipidemia, hyperglycemia, nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), and sarcopenia with cholecystectomy in recent studies has led to the need for further investigation of these patients.[3–6] Loss of gallbladder function due to cholecystectomy may be responsible for metabolic syndrome and related disorders by affecting glucose and lipid metabolism.[5] There are diagnostic criteria for metabolic syndrome based on biochemical and anthropometric measurements. Metabolic syndrome is independently associated with visceral adiposity. Visceral fat tissue affects various normal and pathological processes in human body.[7,8]

Sarcopenia is defined as loss of muscle mass and function, which is an important indicator of morbidity and mortality in aging adults.[9] There are preventable and non-preventable causes in the etiology of sarcopenia.[10,11] Many reasons such as aging process, malnutrition, inactivity/sedentary life, chronic diseases, lack of physical activity contribute to the development of sarcopenia.[12] Different techniques including bioelectrical impedance analysis (BIA), dual-energy x-ray absorptiometry (DEXA), computed tomography (CT), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and ultrasound (US) and are being used in the evaluation of sarcopenia. The quadriceps muscle measured using US has high sensitivity and specificity for identifying sarcopenia.[13]

Few studies have investigated the relationship between sarcopenia, visceral adiposity, and cholecystectomy. In previous studies, BIA or DEXA was used for sarcopenia assessment and non-US methods were used for visceral fat assessment. The aim of our study was to investigate the relationship between cholecystectomy and sarcopenia-visceral fat thickness using the ISarcoPRM algorithm, which is an easy, reliable, and inexpensive method based on US measurement, to evaluate sarcopenia in patients with long-term previous cholecystectomy.

2. Methods

Participants aged ≥40 years who visited gastroenterology outpatient clinics between March 2024 and July 2024 were included in this cross-sectional case-control study. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Dr Abdurrahman Yurtaslan Oncology Training and Research Hospital and was conducted in accordance with the 1975 Helsinki Declaration (Date: March 2024, Decision No. 2024-03/146). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants. The patient group was formed from participants who had undergone cholecystectomy at least 5 years ago, and the control group was formed from participants who had not undergone cholecystectomy. Clinical data, including age, education level (in years), exercise and current smoking, and comorbidities [e.g., hypertension, diabetes mellitus (DM), and hyperlipidemia], were obtained. Subjects were accepted as performing exercise if they were performing moderate-intensity physical activity (> 30 min/d) for at least 3 to 4 d/wk. Waist circumference (cm), height (cm), and weight (kg) were also measured. Body mass index (BMI) was calculated by dividing weight by the square of height. Waist circumference was measured using the midpoint between the anterior superior iliac spine and ribs.

Patients with advanced cardiac, hepatic, and renal insufficiency; active malignancies (currently or within the last year receiving radiotherapy/chemotherapy); rheumatological diseases; severe knee, hip, and hand osteoarthritis; use of any assistive device for walking; neuromuscular diseases (motor neuron diseases, polyneuropathies, myasthenia gravis); major depression; Parkinson disease; and previous stroke were excluded. All participants were informed of the study protocol.

2.1. Ultrasonographic measurements for quadriceps muscle and abdominal fat

Quadriceps muscle and subcutaneous and preperitoneal fat thickness (PFT) were measured with a 7 to 12 MHz linear probe, and abdominal fat thickness with a 2 to 5 MHz curvilinear probe (Mindray DC-7, China). Quadriceps muscle thickness was defined as the distance between the outer sheath of the rectus femoris muscle and the periosteum, measured at the midpoint between the anterior superior iliac spine and the patella.

Intra-abdominal adipose tissue thickness was calculated by placing the probe 2-cm proximal to the midline of the umbilicus with minimal pressure, and measuring the distance from the inner surface of the linea alba to the anterior wall of the abdominal aorta. Subcutaneous maximum fat thickness was measured from the distance between the subcutaneous tissue and the linea alba from the same point.[14] PFT was measured from the distal neighborhood of the xiphoid process, 1.5 cm to the right side of the widest distance between the parietal peritoneum and the linea alba. Minimum subcutaneous fat thickness was calculated as the distance measured from the distal neighborhood of the xiphoid process, measured as the shortest distance between the outer part of the linea alba and subcutaneous fat tissue.[14]

2.2. Sarcopenia assessment

The sonographic thigh adjustment ratio (STAR) values were calculated by dividing the quadriceps muscle thickness (mm) by BMI. Grip strength was assessed using an electronic hand dynamometer (Takei model). The chair stand test (CST) was conducted by rapidly rising and sitting back down from the chair hile keeping the arms crossed over the chest.[13,15] Both the tests were performed consecutively. Together with low STAR values (<1.0 for females and <1.4 for males), low grip strength (<19 kg for females or <32 kg for males), and/or prolonged CST duration (≥12 seconds) were used to diagnose sarcopenia.[15]

2.3. Sample size estimation

For a 1-to-1 ratio of patients and controls desired for PFT, at least 38 patients and 38 controls were required to be significant at P ≤ .05, and power of 80%. However, because there should be an optimal of 20 cases per independent variable in the regression analyses, at least 140 cases were included in the study.

2.4. Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using the SPSS software version 23 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, Illinois). Descriptive statistics are presented as mean ± standard deviation for normally distributed variables and median (interquartile range) for non-normally distributed variables. Categorical variables were reported as frequencies and percentages. Comparisons between groups were performed using Student t test or Mann–Whitney U test depending on the normality of numerical variables and using Chi-square or Fisher exact test for categorical variables.

For the multivariate analysis, the possible factors (i.e., age, sex, BMI, exercise and smoking status, and cholecystectomy and PFT were further entered into the linear and binary logistic regression analyses (with enter method) to determine independent predictors for each sarcopenia parameter and presence of sarcopenia, respectively.

The best cutoff value of the PFT for the prediction of sarcopenia was determined using the maximum Youden index and the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve for each sex. The area under the curve was used to compare the overall diagnostic accuracies of the predicted models. Statistical significance was set at P value < .05 was considered statistically significant.

3. Results

A total of 158 community-dwelling patients (aged 41–80 years), including cholecystectomized (N = 89) and non-cholecystectomized (N = 69) patients, from gastroenterology clinics were included. In both groups, fat tissue measurements were not evaluated in 7 patients because of the absence of recorded US results. Additionally, visceral fat thickness measurements for 8 patients were excluded from the evaluation because of abdominal gas (Fig. 1). 4. Discussion

In this study, we observed that sarcopenia was observed 2.8 times more frequently in patients who underwent cholecystectomy than in those who did not; however, this relationship was not independent, but rather through PFT. Interestingly, we found that, independent of the other factors, PFT was negatively associated with quadriceps muscle thickness and strongly and positively associated with sarcopenia.

The gallbladder stores and concentrates bile during fasting, and releases it during food intake. Fibroblast growth factor (FGF 15/19) secreted from the gallbladder endothelium has been found to play an important role in glucose and carbohydrate metabolism.[16] Loss of these functions after cholecystectomy impairs the absorption pattern of glucose and fat during food intake and contributes to the development of metabolic syndrome, obesity, DM and NAFLD.[4,17]

Visceral fat is defined as the fat surrounding the intraperitoneal organs. The preperitoneal fat layer is found between the peritoneum and linea alba.[18] Visceral adipose tissue drains into the portal system, while preperitoneal adipose tissue drains into the systemic venous circulation. Preperitoneal adipose tissue is also defined as visceral fat because of its close proximity to the liver.[18] Abdominal visceral adiposity, defined as central adiposity, is a risk factor for cardiovascular diseases, DM, and colorectal, pancreatic, and prostate cancer.[19–23] In the relationship between steatohepatitis and visceral adiposity, preperitoneal fat tissue was found to be the best indicator of steatohepatitis better than visceral adipose tissue.[24]

Although visceral adiposity is indirectly estimated by waist circumference, CT and MRI are the best detection methods. US and CT-MRI comparison studies were found to be correlated because of the inaccuracy of waist circumference measurement and inadequate estimation, and both have disadvantages of cost and availability, and CT has radiation exposure as well.[25] In our study, we measured visceral fat thickness using US because of its ease of application and the difficulty in detecting preperitoneal adipose tissue with other imaging modalities. We found that cholecystectomy was associated with preperitoneal and intra-abdominal visceral adiposity, possibly because cholecystectomy causes metabolic syndrome.

Inflammatory cytokines such as tumor necrosis alpha (TNF-α) and interleukin-6 (IL-6), which are catabolic mediators, play an important role in the etiology of sarcopenia, which is an important indicator of quality of life, especially in advanced age.[26] Adipocytes in visceral adipose tissue secrete inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α and IL-6. Elevated levels of these inflammatory markers can disrupt the balance between muscle protein synthesis and degradation, leading to a progressive loss of muscle mass and function.[27,28] Gan et al[6] found that the muscle mass index (determined by DEXA) and grip strength of participants who underwent cholecystectomy ≥ 7-year ago decreased more than those without cholecystectomy. In our study, we found that the rate of sarcopenia was found 2.8 times higher in patients who had undergone cholecystectomy at least 5 years ago than in those who had not undergone cholecystectomy.

In our study, the reason for the higher rate of sarcopenia in cholecystectomized patients compared to Gan et al[6] is that they used appendicular muscle mass for sarcopenia and included young adults in their study. In our study, the diagnosis was made based on quadriceps muscle loss, which we think is newer and more accurate, and middle-aged and older adults over the age of 40 years. The advantage of our study is that visceral fat measurements were also performed, and we found that cholecystectomy increased the frequency of sarcopenia through PFT. In addition, a ≥10 mm PFT strongly increased the odds ratio of sarcopenia by approximately 6-fold.

In contrast, the association between PFT and sarcopenia was highly significant in our study. This may be due to the contribution of inflammatory mediators, such as TNF-α and IL-6, secreted by adipocytes, which are also responsible for the etiology of sarcopenia. It also suggests that the sarcopenia-inducing effect of cholecystectomy may be due to an increased PFT. Future studies should be conducted with greater detail regarding biochemical pathways to elucidate the relationship between sarcopenia and visceral fat accumulation.

This study had several strengths and weaknesses. The first of its strengths is that it is the first study to investigate the relationship among cholecystectomy, visceral adiposity, and sarcopenia; the second is the use of low-cost, reliable, and easy measurement techniques in muscle and fat thickness measurements; and the third is that certain standardization has been achieved due to measurement with a single operator, although ultrasound is not as precise as CT/MRI. The weaknesses are, first, the inability to measure some cases due to dense gas in intra-abdominal visceral measurements, second, the findings detected by measurement techniques cannot be clearly determined at the pathophysiological and molecular levels, third, the cross-sectional design limits causal conclusions, and the small sample size affects generalizability, and fourth, the oversight of potential confounding factors, such as diet, that may be associated with visceral adiposity.

5. Conclusions

Sarcopenia was 2.8 times more prevalent in patients who had undergone cholecystectomy than in those who had not. However, this relationship was not independent, but rather mediated through PFT. When the PFT exceeded 10 mm, the risk of sarcopenia increased by 5.875 times. Interestingly, independent of other factors, we identified a negative correlation between PFT and quadriceps muscle thickness, and a strong positive association between PFT and sarcopenia. Therefore, it should not be ignored that the risk of sarcopenia may increase in the long term in those who have undergone cholecystectomy. Different biochemical and molecular studies are needed to determine the relationship between cholecystectomy and adipose and muscle tissue. Future studies should aim to expand the cohort size to improve the robustness and generalizability of the findings.

Numerical and transcritical bifurcation analysis of Chikungunya dynamics within a fractional-fractal framework
Authors: Mehmet Kocabıyık
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Chikungunya is a mosquito-borne viral infection that remains a serious public health problem because of its rapid transmission and long-term clinical implications. Classical models based on integer-order derivatives are effective in describing the basic mechanisms of transmission, but do not consider the memory effects and temporal heterogeneity seen in actual epidemic processes. This study introduces a modified fractional-fractal mathematical model that combines fractional derivatives, representing nonlocal memory, and fractal time, representing scale-related temporal irregularities, to overcome these drawbacks. The fundamental properties, including the positivity and boundedness of solutions, are established. Equilibrium points are derived, and the basic reproduction number is calculated using the next-generation matrix approach. Local stability of the equilibrium states is carried out, and a formal transcritical bifurcation analysis is performed to study the stability switch at the threshold. A numerical scheme is developed based on the Adams–Bashforth–Moulton method to support the theoretical results. Numerical simulations including 3D phase portraits show the effect of the parameters of fractional and fractal models on the dynamics of the disease and distinctions from with classical models. The results suggest that fractional-fractal models are more flexible representations of Chikungunya transmission, and could help elucidate the complex dynamics of epidemics and inform control efforts.

Introduction

Chikungunya is a mosquito-borne viral disease primarily transmitted by Aedes aegypti and Aedes albopictus mosquitoes. In the last 20 years, it has become a major public health issue worldwide. It is an acute febrile disease with intense joint pain, fatigue and chronic arthralgia which may last for months or even years, resulting in significant socioeconomic morbidity. Several factors have contributed to the resurgence and geographical spread of Chikungunya in Africa, Asia, Europe and the Americas, such as rapid urbanization, increased human mobility, climate variability, and the expansion of vector habitats.

Transmission dynamics of Chikungunya are highly complex as they involve the interplay between human hosts and mosquito vectors, variable incubation and infectious periods, and heterogeneous contact networks. All these properties make analytical evaluation and prediction difficult if the system knowledge comes soley from empirical observations. Therefore, mathematical models are key tools for understanding the transmission mechanisms of Chikungunya, assessing intervention strategies and informing public health policy.

There are several mathematical models for Chikungunya, most of which have been developed using a compartmental framework to represent host-vector interactions and disease progression. The first phase of modeling largely involved depicting the epidemiology of outbreaks and the effectiveness of control measures in the event of a large outbreak. Yakob and Clements1 for example, developed a deterministic compartmental model to examine the transmission and control of Chikungunya during the outbreak on Réunion Island in 2005-2006 and showed, using a climate-driven approach, the importance of vector control measures in limiting the magnitude of the epidemic. Likewise, González-Parra et al.2 investigated the virus transmission in Colombia in 2014, giving a detailed characterization of the dynamics of transmission for each region, and estimating the basic reproduction number to explain its rapid escalation. Moreover, Feng et al.3 further progressed the field by giving a mathematical model that includes virus mutation dynamics, and investigates the effects of evolutionary changes on the severity and frequency of Chikungunya outbreaks. Subsequent studies went beyond the underlying transmission dynamics to incorporate advanced optimal control approaches and detailed stability analyses. González-Parra et al.4 used optimal control theory to develop policies for public health good value for money, specifically in terms of resource allocation in vaccination and vector mitigation. Arora et al.5 also performed a thorough investigation of the stability properties of the system, and numerically simulated the effects of perturbations of host-vector contact rates on the persistence of the disease in the long term.

A significant shift in recent research has been the exploration of within-host modeling approaches to capture the intricate interaction between the virus and the human immune response. Global stability conditions for viral clearance or persistence have been obtained using generalized within-host models, as done by Alade et al. in their works6,7, which consider the effects of adaptive immune elements like T-cells and antibodies. To complement these results, Olayiwola et al.8 developed a semi-analytical model of the internal dynamics of the Chikungunya virus that gives accurate predictions for the shift between the acute and chronic phases of infection at the individual level, as a function of the strength of the immune system.

In recent years, the field of fractional calculus has experienced rapid development and non-integer order modeling is one of most important extensions of classical modeling. One of the significant properties of fractional derivatives which is relevant for infectious diseases with variable incubation periods is the inclusion of memory and hereditary effects. These operators have been shown to be applicable to a variety of scientific situations recently—such as fractional-order models for the dynamics of COVID-19 in Italy9 and the impact of the pandemic on society in Nigeria10. In addition, the non-integer method has been successfully applied for investigating the dynamics of fuzzy fractional diabetes models11, dynamics of malaria with enlightenment interventions12 and stability analysis of SIR model with specific treatment rates13. For Chikungunya, Helikumi et al.14 constructed a fractional-order model considering asymptomatic infections, and Jain and Chalishajar15 and Chavada et al.16 studied the transmission dynamics with fractional order. Further progress has been made with multi-scale modelling for vaccine prevention17, the effect of vaccine campaigns on epidemic transmission18 and vaccine strategies using immune memory19.

In addition to the Chikungunya-related work, mathematical modelling has been successfully used in many other biological and epidemiological and socio-dynamical systems. The reader is referred to the studies listed in the references20–33 for a more detailed description of advanced modelling approaches, fractional dynamics and control-oriented approaches. The studies demonstrate the flexibility and usefulness of the current mathematical modeling frameworks in different scientific applications.

The main reason to conduct this study is the growing worldwide risk of Chikungunya and the lack of classical epidemiological modelling tools. This is a result of the use of traditional integer-order compartmental models which have yielded useful insights into the interactions between hosts and vectors, but have made the assumption of memoryless transitions, that is, the subsequent state of the epidemic is only dependent on its current state. The transmission of Chikungunya is, however, a complex biological process with different incubation periods and long-term viral shedding, meaning that the course of the disease over the past year has had a significant impact on present trends. This observation shows that the importance of integrating hereditary traits and long-range dependencies is a critical need for mathematical frameworks that is often included in the usual formulations.

In this context, the crucial needs of modelling such disease require the use of a fractional-fractal derivative; this offers a very appropriate mathematical background which is closely related to the biological facts of Chikungunya epidemiology. Typical traditional integer order and standard fractional derivatives are formulated on a homogeneous time manifold with uniform environmental interactions. Irregularities in scale and time are a natural part of vector borne transmission, however, in large part, due to the complex daily or seasonal patterns of mosquito breeding and movement of humans. In this integrated setting, the fractal scaling parameter can be used to describe non-uniform temporal geometries, and, at the same time, the fractional order can be used to include in the model long-range biological memory and hereditary effects.

Even in the light of this recent trend of using a fractional-order calculus approach to deal with these memory effects, there are still many research gaps to be addressed when considering the simultaneous treatment of temporal scaling and self-similar behaviours in disease spreading. There are existing fractional models that are quite good in the sense that they are able to represent the memory–but they assume the time manifold to be homogeneous. In practice, however, the contact pattern between the hosts and the vectors, as well as the environmental factors, may have fractal characteristics and irregularities on scales smaller than that of the contact area, which are not effectively described by a single non-integer derivative. This complexity is reflected in recent research on monkeypox virus34, general SEIR models35 and in microbial dynamics of co-infections36. There is, therefore, a lack of extensive studies to combine fractional memory with fractal time scaling, particularly in the case of Chikungunya models with multifaceted interventions like vaccination and hospitalization.

To overcome this, a dual structure model is needed that accounts for the environment’s biological history and temporal variability. The use of fractional-fractal operators is a significant mathematical development that can describe infection peak shifts more finely and help understand the maintenance of stability thresholds under non-local conditions. This research will develop a more complex analytical tool for public health officials to assess the long-term effects of vaccination and medical interventions in varying transmission contexts by building a model that captures these complex interactions. It is an important study because it allows a greater freedom in capturing non-local and nonlinear interactions of Chikungunya, making it a more precise predictive tool for public health strategy than traditional ones.

There are several intrinsic problems that restrict the effectiveness of classical approaches in mathematical modeling of the transmission of Chikungunya. First, the nonlinear interactions between the different epidemiological compartments of the host-vector system are very complex to make the system stable analytically, due to the high number of compartments. Second, long-term viral shedding and the fractal-like mosquito breeding cycles give rise to non-local dependencies that are not represented by local integer-order derivatives. A switch to dual fractional-fractal operators, though providing more descriptive power, is likely to make the mathematical problem to establish the existence, uniqueness and global stability of solutions more difficult.

The novelty of the present study is the formulation and detailed analysis of an extensive mathematical model for the transmission of Chikungunya using the fractional-fractal language, with explicit consideration of both biological memory and the scale-dependent temporal irregularities. In particular, this paper provides a novel extension of current compartmental systems by computing host-vector interactions in a mathematical model based on a fractional fractal approach, which can explain memory and irregular temporal scaling at the same time. In contrast with former models using only integer order or standard fractional models, the proposed model incorporates nonlocal memory effects by means of fractional derivatives and scale-invariant temporal heterogeneity by fractal time. This dual modeling structure provides a more realistic and flexible framework for the dynamics of a disease, and is able to capture the long-range dependence and hereditary attributes of host-vector interaction. One of the key findings that sets this apart from other studies is the understanding that the infection peaks are significantly altered and the stability thresholds by fractal-order scaling; something that is not often considered in the models that assume homogeneous time manifold. This is the main goal of this work to develop a comprehensive framework that can record key epidemiological aspects such as effects of vaccination, hospitalization classes and disease progression over various time scales. The qualitative behavior of the system is studied by proving the existence and uniqueness of solution, deriving the basic reproduction number \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathscr {R}_0$$\end{document} by the next generation matrix method, and performing center manifold theory based transcritical bifurcation analysis at the threshold. In order to facilitate the connection between theory and observation, numerical simulations are carried out for an adapted version of the Adams–Bashforth–Moulton scheme, along with 3D phase portraits that are used for visualizing the global attraction to the equilibrium states for various fractional-fractal orders.

Preliminaries on fractional-fractal calculus

Many systems in the real world exhibit structures that do not follow uniform smoothly varying timescales. In such situations, a calculus based on equal time steps, or classical calculus, might not be a good way of describing the actual dynamics. A geometry-dependent temporal measure is introduced for a fractal subset of the real line, providing an alternative analytical framework, known as fractal calculus. It is a methodological framework that allows modeling systems that exhibit irregular temporal evolution, intermittent activity, and scale-dependent dynamics, common to many biological and epidemiological processes37–49.

The Caputo-type fractional derivative is a powerful combination of the two non-classical analytical fields of fractional calculus and fractal geometry. The conventional fractional derivatives include non-local memory with the aid of a power-law kernel but are usually defined on a regular time manifold. But, there are self-similar temporal patterns and irregularities in epidemiological systems that are more appropriately written in fractal sets. The Caputo sense is also very convenient for biological modelling since it introduces the derivative of the function in the kernel of the integral, thus keeping the initial conditions as in the traditional sense, with clear physical and biological interpretation. In this study, this operator allows us to measure at the same time the constraint that the infection rate faces from its historical path (memory) and the irregular geometric structure of the timescale for transmission.

Model formulation and basic properties

Chikungunya is an arthropod-borne viral infection whose transmission is driven by intricate interactions between human hosts and mosquito vectors. The presence of incubation periods, vaccination effects, and heterogeneous progression rates introduces nonlinear dynamics that challenge the descriptive capacity of classical integer-order models. Moreover, observed outbreak data frequently reflect persistence and temporal irregularity influenced by ecological and biological factors. Fractional-fractal modeling provides a flexible mathematical approach by integrating memory effects with a non-uniform temporal structure, thereby offering an enhanced representation of Chikungunya transmission processes.

Transcritical bifurcation analysis and its epidemiological significance

A transcritical bifurcation is one particular kind of local bifurcation in which a non-linear dynamical system undergoes a change in stability of its solutions across a critical parameter value. It is at this bifurcation that two separate equilibrium states meet in a head-on collision and switch their stability features. From a mathematical epidemiology perspective, it is the mathematical basis for the formal validation of the threshold criteria for persistence or eradication of a disease.

There are two important reasons to undertake a formal transcritical bifurcation analysis at \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathscr {R}_0 = 1$$\end{document}. First, it precisely specifies the stability of the disease-free equilibrium \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathscr {P}_0$$\end{document} from a qualitative point of view: If \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathscr {R}_0 < 1$$\end{document} then \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathscr {P}_0$$\end{document} is locally asymptotically stable, that is, the disease will naturally die out. When \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathscr {R}_0>1$$\end{document} then \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathscr {P}_0$$\end{document} is unstable and switches its stability to a new endemic equilibrium \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathscr {P}^*$$\end{document} and the virus becomes endemic. Secondly, the use of the center manifold theory in the bifurcation analysis removes the ambiguity of critical transitions. It enables us to know whether the system has a forward (supercritical) bifurcation, which means that the system is stable for small increases above the threshold and the endemic level is determined by the stable equilibrium, or a backwards (subcritical) bifurcation, in which case the disease may tragically persist even though public health intervention efforts keep \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathscr {R}_0$$\end{document} slightly below one. So, by explicitly modelling this bifurcation structure, health authorities can have a definite mathematical certainty of what the intensity and coverage of vaccination or hospitalization control programs must be to push the system back into a stable disease-free state.

In this subsection, the stability of the fractional–fractal Chikungunya system at the threshold \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathscr {R}_0 = 1$$\end{document} is studied using center manifold theory as described by Castillo-Chavez and Song52. Although the Hopf bifurcation is one of the various types of bifurcations that can generate periodic oscillations in epidemic models53, only the transcritical bifurcation is examined here, and it is determined whether the local dynamics near the critical point, where the disease-free equilibrium becomes unstable.

In conclusion, the numerical results shown in this section validate the fact that the fractional-fractal order, along with the important epidemiological parameters, play a significant role in the transmission dynamics of Chikungunya. A comparison between the results obtained and the results of the classical integer-order and the standard fractional-order were carried out to confirm the reliability of our results1,2,5,8,15. The results obtained are consistent with the general trends of the epidemics reported in these studies but the proposed fractional-fractal model better emphasizes the long-memory and self-similar scaling properties of the outbreak. The main advantages of this research involves the introduction of the fractional-fractal operator in a holistic model combining the vaccination and hospitalization compartments. This framework is novel, as it can accurately reflect the environmental heterogeneity and viral hereditary characteristics of the virus, providing a powerful mathematical tool for forecasting disease persistence and effectiveness of public health measures. The advantages show that fractional-fractal derivative is a notable improvement over the traditional ways of modelling complex vector borne diseases.

Conclusion

In this study, a detailed fractional-fractal mathematical framework has been developed to study the transmission dynamics of Chikungunya. Compared to traditional integer-order models, the proposed model is capable of properly modeling memory-dependent process and temporal irregularity that is scale dependent, using a dual modeling structure. The mathematical well-posedness of the system is substantiated through an analytical study, and the positivity and boundedness of the solution ensure the epidemiological feasibility. The basic reproduction number, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathscr {R}_0$$\end{document} is a key finding of this research that demarcates disease eradication from persistence.

Through the formal analysis of the transcritical bifurcation, using center manifold theory, a forward bifurcation is found at the threshold value of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathscr {R}_0 = 1$$\end{document} suggesting the existence of a stable endemic equilibrium for values of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathscr {R}_0 > 1$$\end{document}. In addition, 3D phase portraits and the local stability analysis establishes the global attraction to steady states for various fractional-fractal orders. It is found through numerical simulation that a lower fractional order leads to a longer duration of virus presence and delayed infection peaks, which is a characteristic of the hereditary nature of the virus. This leads to the critical conclusion that relying on memoryless models may result in an underestimation of the peak timing and duration of an outbreak, potentially misguiding the timing of public health interventions. This modelling approach offers a more realistic model of host-vector interactions than conventional models.

Furthermore, the results demonstrate that vaccination and hospitalization strategies are highly sensitive to the fractional-fractal orders, suggesting that a uniform intervention might be ineffective in heterogeneous environments. This framework can be extended in the future by adding stochastic perturbations to incorporate environmental stochasticity into transmission rates. Furthermore, incorporating spatial diffusion would allow the study of the geographic spread of Chikungunya in heterogeneous landscapes. Lastly, the addition of optimal control theory to assess the cost-effectiveness of the selected vaccination and vector-control strategies, along with real-time data fitting to recent outbreaks, would greatly improve the model’s predictive value and usefulness for public health officials.

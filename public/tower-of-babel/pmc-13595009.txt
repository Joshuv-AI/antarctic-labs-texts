Multi-target cell membrane chromatography intelligent screening strategy and its application in drug discovery
Authors: Sanyin Zhang, Shengli Han, Wei Chen, Shilin Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT

Introduction

Drug research and development, particularly for multi-target drug development for complex diseases such as neurodegenerative disorders, faces significant challenges. Traditional Chinese medicine (TCM), grounded in the principles of holistic view, syndrome differentiation, and preventive treatment, exhibits unique advantages in the prevention and treatment of chronic diseases. However, its multi-component, multi-target mechanisms of action remain largely elusive. Existing drug screening technologies, such as surface plasmon resonance (SPR) and biolayer interferometry (BLI), require purified target proteins [1]. While phenotypic screening captures global biological effects, it often fails to identify specific molecular targets. Biomimetic chromatography is suitable for screening complex systems against single targets.

The multi-target cell membrane chromatography intelligent screening workstation (MCMC-ISW) [2] is a “many-to-many” biomimetic two-dimensional chromatographic system that integrates “synchronous multi-target recognition” and “intelligent screening”, enabling real-time data collection and analysis. This system facilitates the elucidation of the pharmacodynamic material basis of traditional medicines and their multi-target mechanisms of action. Here, “multi-target” refers to the immobilization of multiple receptor proteins on the stationary phase (enabling multi-target affinity profiling), and “multi-component” refers to the multiple chemical constituents in complex samples. MCMC-ISW dynamically simulates the in vivo drug action process, achieving multi-component–multi-target mapping. This platform provides critical methodological support for the modernization of herbal medicine research.

MCMC-ISW integrates core innovations from the teams of Professor Shilin Chen and Professor Langchong He. In the early 1990s, Professor He proposed cell membrane chromatography (CMC), a biomimetic affinity technique using active membrane receptors as the stationary phase [3]. This work received a National Technological Invention Award in 2012 and a Ministry of Education Technological Invention Award in 2023, laying the foundation for MCMC-ISW. Professor Chen's team constructed PanGPCRomicsDB, a database covering all human G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) subtypes and developed a hundred-million-scale library of herbal gene-encoded natural diversity compounds (GNDC) [4]. They also proposed the “drug-target co-screening” theory [5], enabling the simultaneous detection of binding affinity and functional activity [1]. The integration of CMC, PanGPCRomicsDB, and GNDC gave rise to MCMC-ISW, marking a leap from first-generation CMC to second-generation intelligent multi-target screening. This platform may help establish a new framework for analyzing complex herb medicine systems and developing multi-target drugs.

Approaches to multi-target implementation in CMC

Multi-target CMC is mainly achieved by three methods: multi-target cell membrane columns, multi-column array and organoid column (Fig. 1). By maintaining receptor biological activity, these platforms improve screening efficiency and are suited for assessing drug actions involving multi-target interactions and complex physiological settings.

The multi-target cell membrane columns adopt a strategy of integrating multiple receptors into a single column, enabling simultaneous multi-target screening. It involves two strategies. The first is mixing multi-source cell membranes, where membranes overexpressing different receptors are blended at ratios determined by a “functional equivalence” model and immobilized on the same support. Compatibility validation (RSD < 5%) is performed to exclude conformational interference among receptors, thereby achieving “multi-target screening on a single column”. The second is the single-cell multi-receptor co-expression strategy, in which a “many-in-one” lentiviral vector is used to construct HEK 293T stable cell lines that co-express multiple GPCRs (e.g., three or more). Cell membranes from engineered cells are directly used to prepare the stationary phase, leveraging endogenous regulation to maintain receptor ratios while preserving the native orientation and spatial proximity of receptors, which is particularly suitable for studying GPCR heterodimer interactions. This technological evolution not only provides a multi-dimensional perspective for elucidating the pharmacodynamic material basis of complex traditional medicine systems, but also marks the transition from reductionist analysis to holistic integration in drug screening, establishing a methodological foundation for precisely deciphering the synergistic regulatory mechanisms involving multiple targets and pathways.

The future development of multi-target cell membrane chromatographic columns can be pursued through two complementary pathways. 1) The array chromatographic column pathway leverages microfluidic technology to integrate high-density single-target column arrays, enhances column efficiency through monolithic column materials, and combines standardized interfaces to achieve “plug-and-play” flexible configurations. Artificial intelligence (AI) algorithms are introduced to dynamically optimize target allocation and screening workflows, advancing from multi-column parallel operation toward intelligent multi-dimensional networks. 2) The organoid-based chromatographic column pathway focuses on the holistic simulation of tissue microenvironments, employing multicellular co-culture to construct functional organoids. By integrating label-free immobilization with multimodal online detection, this approach extends the screening dimension from “target binding” to tissue-level functional assessments such as cell–cell interaction, barrier penetration, and metabolic response, enabling dynamic drug–tissue interaction analysis. The deep integration of these two pathways (achieved by assembling diverse organoid units within an array-based architecture) holds the potential to establish a “human tissue microenvironment array”, offering a transformative technological platform for the holistic pharmacodynamic elucidation of TCM formulas and precision medicine applications.

Currently, three main technical approaches exist, each own advantages and limitations. The multi-target cell membrane column is simple, low-cost, and suitable for preliminary screening of complex systems; however, it’s hard to precisely control receptor ratios and conformational interference. The multi-column array system allows independent control of each target column and offers high throughput, but it’s costly and lacks physiological context. The organoid chromatography column exhibits the highest degree of biomimicry, being capable of simulating three-dimensional (3D) structures and cell–cell interactions, yet it is limited by complex preparation and poor reproducibility. These three approaches collectively face two major engineering challenges. First, reproducibility and standardization: receptor activity stability during membrane immobilization is difficult to control, causing high batch-to-batch variability and lacking unified protocols and standards. Addressing this requires standardized procedures, quality control metrics, automated preparation systems, and industry-wide guidelines. Second, interpretability and biological complexity integration: high-dimensional data are difficult to interpret, and AI “black box” models lack explainability. Existing models also struggle to simulate the in vivo environment, creating a gap between screening outcomes and in vivo efficacy. Addressing this requires multi-omics analysis, molecular dynamics, explainable AI, organ-on-a-chip, and microfluidic technologies, alongside systems biology and network pharmacology to elucidate multi-target synergies.

Integration of AI with cell membrane chromatography

AI provides key advantages in this framework by enabling data-driven prioritization of candidate compounds, recognition of complex structure-activity patterns, and iterative optimization of predictive models based on experimental feedback. The proposed platform integrates databases, target libraries, and algorithmic modules to establish a novel framework for intelligent multi-target screening. At the database level, a comprehensive natural compound resource GNDC has been established, which comprises 234 million structurally diverse natural products from 1037 medicinal species [4]. By providing structurally diverse, traceable, and biologically annotated chemical entities, the database directly feeds the MCMC-ISW platform, enabling systematic selection of candidate compounds for multi-target screening and guiding AI-driven prioritization within the chromatographic workflow.

At the target library level, a “GPCR receptor resource” covering major classes including all 800+ targets has been established. Standardized sequence identifiers and unified annotation schemes ensure data interoperability, while a hybrid strategy combining experimentally determined structures with predictive models such as AlphaFold guarantees structural integrity and broad coverage of receptors. While GPCRs are used as a representative and well-characterized target class in MCMC-ISW, the overall platform can be extended to other membrane protein families and screening systems. This receptor library underpins MCMC-ISW’s ability to perform systematic, high-throughput analysis of selective and synergistic ligand–receptor interactions across multiple GPCRs.

At the algorithmic level, AI serves as an organizing principle that bridges experimental screening with data-driven decision-making. A component–target affinity prediction model [6] has been constructed, which employs molecular graph encoding method to capture local and global chemical features of compounds, while integrating protein sequence and structural embeddings to represent receptor binding potential. Trained on curated drug–target datasets, the model prioritizes candidates for experimental validation and, when combined with cell membrane chromatography data, enables MCMC-ISW to identify selective and multi-target binding profiles. This integration establishes a quantitative bridge between large-scale data resources and experimental screening, accelerating the discovery of bioactive compounds and facilitating rational design of multi-target therapeutics.

Taken together, the database, target library, and AI algorithm constitute a semi-closed-loop intelligent learning framework that integrates data resources with experimental screening and model optimization. In this loop, candidate compounds from the GNDC database are first prioritized by the AI model, then experimentally screened using the MCMC-ISW platform, and finally the chromatographic and binding data are fed back to update and optimize the prediction model. Despite these promising capabilities, at the current stage, this workflow does not yet include real-time chromatographic hardware control, such as automated column switching or fully automated feedback-driven operation. Such capabilities represent important future directions for further development of the platform.

Application of the MCMC-ISW in drug discovery: Status and future perspectives

The MCMC-ISW has broad application value in traditional medicine research. In terms of pharmacodynamic elucidation, the platform serves as a biomimetic “molecular fishing” tool, enabling the capture of active components such as calycosin and ferulic acid from Buyang Huanwu Decoction, thereby clarifying its material basis for treating ischemic stroke [7]. It has also been used for safety evaluation, such as identifying ginsenosides Rb1 and Rc responsible for pseudo-allergic reactions in Shenmai Injection using a tri-mixed cell membrane chromatography system [8]. In lead compound discovery, combined with deep learning algorithms, it has screened novel EGFR antagonists such as geraniin and brazilin from herb medicine [9]. Additionally, it rapidly identified antiviral inhibitors such as famotidine from 3010 compounds using a TMPRSS2 cell membrane chromatography model [10]. Furthermore, it precisely identified the antihepatic fibrosis component gomisin N from Schisandra chinensis using a hepatic stellate cell membrane chromatography model, thereby enhancing lead compound discovery efficiency. A three-target hybrid membrane column was constructed to co-immobilize S1PR1, CCR9 and FFAR2, and compounds known to be selective (Verzimion, 4-CMTB, S1PR1) were tested. The results showed that the positive drugs of these three receptors showed significant retention on the three-target column. In summary, by integrating bioactivity screening with chemical component analysis, this platform provides systematic technical support for the elucidation of pharmacodynamic substances, the discovery of innovative drugs, and bioactivity-related quality evaluation in traditional medicine.

In the future, the MCMC-ISW will evolve from a “general-purpose multi-target screening platform” toward a “closed-loop research framework centered on disease-target specific models”. Leveraging the expanded GNDC and PanGPCRomicsDB resources and optimized multimodal affinity prediction models, the platform will enable the construction of dedicated receptor subsets and component libraries of diseases such as neurodegenerative diseases and tumors, upgrading from “broad-spectrum screening” to “precision targeting.” Based on this, a closed-loop of “prediction-driven screening wet experimental validation data feedback optimization” will be established. This closed-loop framework enhances screening efficiency and hit rates, enabling systematic mapping of “formula–disease–target” associations in complex systems such as traditional Chinese medicine, and transforms MCMC-ISW from an efficient analytical tool into a customizable and intelligent platform for precision multi-target discovery.

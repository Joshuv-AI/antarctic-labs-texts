Formulas for Residues of Type Camacho-Sad and Applications
Diogo da Silva Machado

Abstract
. In this paper, we provide formulas for the sum of residues of type Camacho-Sad of a holomorphic foliation with respect to an invariant analytic subvariety. As application, in context of projective foliations, we obtain a formula that relates the sum these residues with the degree and other characteristics of the invariant subvariety. Furthermore, we establish sufficient conditions ensuring that an irreducible curve is invariant by a projective foliation on ℙ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2} . In addition, we provide an adjunction formula for hypersurfaces with non-isolated singularities and obtain explicit formulas for the Milnor number of these hypersurfaces.

1.
Introduction

The Separatrix Theorem, proved by C. Camacho and P. Sad in
[
4
]
, is a classical result in foliation theory. It guarantees the existence of a local integral curve (a separatrix) for any holomorphic vector field with an isolated singularity at the origin of the complex plane. These authors defined the local index of a holomorphic vector field relative to a nonsingular invariant curve, which was the main tool used in their proof. This index is known in the literature as the
Camacho-Sad index
, and many generalizations have since been developed.

In the case where the invariant curve is a singular curve on the projective plane
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, a generalization was given by A. Lins Neto
[
12
]
. When the ambient surface is a complete intersection in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, a generalization was obtained by M. G. Soares
[
17
]
. T. Suwa, in
[
19
]
, further extended the definition of the index to allow invariant curves with singularities. He proved that for a compact curve invariant by a one-dimensional singular foliation on a surface, the sum of the indices equals the self-intersection number of the curve. In higher dimensions, when the invariant subvariety is nonsingular, the index has been considered by B. Gmira
[
6
]
and A. Lins Neto
[
13
]
in the case of invariant hypersurfaces, and in the general case by D. Lehmann
[
8
]
.

Subsequently, in arbitrary dimension and for an invariant singular subvariety, D. Lehmann and T. Suwa
[
9
]
,
[
18
]
obtained the index — also referred to as the
residue of type Camacho-Sad
. By employing a suitable adaptation of Chern-Weil theory to Čech-de Rham cohomology, they extended the Baum-Bott residue theory
[
1
]
to the setting of foliations restricted to their invariant subvarieties. In this framework, the index is obtained as the localization of the normal bundle associated with the invariant subvariety.

More recently, F. Sancho de Salas
[
15
]
obtained a generalization of the Camacho-Sad index via a purely algebraic approach, using Grothendieck-type residues associated with a Pfaff system relative to an invariant subscheme. His construction is valid over an arbitrary base scheme and in any characteristic.

It is important to note that the computation and determination of residues of this nature is, in general, a difficult task, which underscores the significance of the results presented in this article.

In the present work, we establish a residue formula (see Theorem 1) for computing the sum of residues of type Camacho-Sad (Camacho-Sad index) of a
p
-dimensional foliation
\mathcal{F}
relative to an invariant analytic subvariety
V
, of arbitrary dimension. We consider the case where
V
is a local complete intersection defined as the zero set of a section of a vector bundle (this is the case, for example, of hypersurfaces, global complete intersections and complete intersections in projective space).

As an application of Theorem 1, we consider foliations on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
that leave invariant a (possibly singular) hypersurface, and we prove a formula relating the sum of Camacho-Sad type residues to the multidegree and other characteristics of the invariant hypersurface (see Theorem 2). This theorem generalizes a result from
[
12
]
.

Proposition
4.3
provides an adjunction formula for hypersurfaces with non-isolated singularities. Theorem
4.9
and Corollary
4.11
provide explicit formulas for the Milnor number of these hypersurfaces.

Also as an application, in the context of holomorphic foliations on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, Corollary
4.5
and Corollary
4.7
establish conditions under which an irreducible curve is invariant by a foliation. In particular, Corollary
4.7
recovers a result due to D. Cerveau and A. Lins Neto
[
3
, Ch. 3, Theorem 1]
.

4.1.
On adjunction formulas

Adjunction formulas are fundamental tools that arise naturally in the context of the classification theory of algebraic varieties. In particular, in the study of projective algebraic varieties, an effective strategy for exploring the classification spectrum is to consider the canonical class as a central invariant. The analysis of the canonical class captures essential birational properties of the variety. When comparing two varieties with similar structures, it is common to seek expressions that relate their canonical divisors. These expressions, known as adjunction formulas, allow one to describe the canonical divisor of a subvariety or a fiber in terms of the ambient canonical divisor and other associated geometric invariants.

If
C
is a compact (possibly) singular curve in a complex compact surface
M
, a classical adjunction formula (due to K. Kodaira
[
7
]
) says that

(6)

\displaystyle\chi(C)=-(K_{M}+C).C+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mu_{x_{i}}(C)

where
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{r}
are all singularities of
C
and
K_{M}
is the canonical divisor of
M
. Since
K_{M}.C=-[C]\frown c_{1}(TM)
, formula (
6
) can be written in the following terms

(7)

\displaystyle\chi(C)=[C]\frown c_{n-1}(TM-L(C))+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mu_{x_{i}}(C).

More recently, in the work of J. Seade and T. Suwa
[
16
]
, the above formula has been generalized to the higher dimensional case:

Theorem

(Seade-Suwa,
[
16
]
)
.

Let
X
be an n-dimensional complex compact manifold and
V\subset X
a local complete intersection of codimension
k
defined by a section of a holomorphic vector bundle
N
. If
V
is compact and has only isolated singularities
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{r}
, then

(8)

\displaystyle\chi(V)=[V]\frown c_{n-k}(TX-N)-(-1)^{n-k}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mu_{x_{i}}(V).

Remark 4.2
.

T. Suwa
[
21
]
proved a generalization of (

is not assumed to be compact.

In formula (
8
), the variety

has only isolated singularities. We obtain the following result, which provides an adjunction formula for a hypersurface

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
X
be an n-dimensional complex compact manifold and
D\subset X
a compact (possibly) singular hypersurface. Then

(9)

\displaystyle\chi(D)=[D]\frown c_{n-k}(TX-L(D))-(-1)^{n-1}\sum_{\lambda=1}^{r}\mu_{S_{\lambda}}(D)

where
S_{1},S_{2},\ldots,S_{r}
are all connected components of

,
\mu_{S_{\lambda}}(D)
denotes the Milnor number of

{S_{\lambda}}
and

Proof.
According to
[
14
, Proposition 1.6]
, we have

[X]\frown c_{n}(TX^{\vee}\otimes L(D))=\sum_{\lambda=1}^{r}\mu_{S_{\lambda}}(D)-(-1)^{n}\chi(D)+(-1)^{n}\chi(X).

By using the definition of Chern class of tensor product, we can calculate the top
Chern class of
TX^{\vee}\otimes L(D)
as follows:

\displaystyle c_{n}(TX^{\vee}\otimes L(D))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_{j}(TX^{\vee})c_{1}(L(D))^{n-j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{n}(TX^{\vee})+\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}c_{j}(TX^{\vee})c_{1}(L(D))^{n-1-j}\right)c_{1}(L(D))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{n}(TX^{\vee})+\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}c_{j}(TX)c_{1}(L(D))^{n-1-j}\right)c_{1}(L(D))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{n}(TX^{\vee})+\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{n-1}(-1)^{n-1-j}c_{j}(TX)c_{1}(L(D))^{n-1-j}\right)c_{1}(L(D))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{n}(TX^{\vee})+\left((-1)^{n-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}c_{j}(TX)c_{1}(L(D)^{\vee})^{n-1-j}\right)c_{1}(L(D))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{n}(TX^{\vee})+(-1)^{n-1}c_{n-1}(TX-L(D))c_{1}(L(D))

Thus, using the fact that
c_{1}(L(D))
is the Poincaré dual of the fundamental class of
D
, we obtain

[X]\frown c_{n}(TX^{\vee})+(-1)^{n-1}[D]\frown c_{n-1}(TX-L(D))=\sum_{\lambda=1}^{r}\mu_{S_{\lambda}}(D)-(-1)^{n}\chi(D)+(-1)^{n}\chi(X).

and formula (
9
) follows from the classical Gauss Bonnet formula.

\square

4.2.
Proof of Theorem 2

Since

c_{n-1}(T\mathbb{P}^{n}-L(D))=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}c_{n-1-j}(T\mathbb{P}^{n})c_{1}(L(D))^{j}

we get

c_{1}(L(D))^{n}=(-1)^{n+1}\left(c_{n-1}(T\mathbb{P}^{n}-L(D))c_{1}(L(D))+\sum^{n-2}_{j=0}(-1)^{j}c_{n-1-j}(T\mathbb{P}^{n})c_{1}(L(D))^{j+1}\right).

Thus, by item (b)(ii) of Theorem 1, we obtain

\displaystyle\hskip-18.49411ptCS(\mathcal{F},D)=[\mathbb{P}^{n}]\frown c_{1}(L(D))^{n}

\displaystyle\hskip-18.49411pt=(-1)^{n+1}\left([\mathbb{P}^{n}]\frown c_{n-1}(T\mathbb{P}^{n}-L(D))c_{1}(L(D))+[\mathbb{P}^{n}]\frown\sum^{n-2}_{j=0}(-1)^{j}c_{n-1-j}(T\mathbb{P}^{n})c_{1}(L(D))^{j+1}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip-18.49411pt=(-1)^{n+1}\left([D]\frown c_{n-1}(T\mathbb{P}^{n}-L(D))+[\mathbb{P}^{n}]\frown\sum^{n-2}_{j=0}(-1)^{j}c_{n-1-j}(T\mathbb{P}^{n})c_{1}(L(D))^{j+1}\right)

where in the last equality we have used the fact that
c_{1}(L(D))
is Poincaré dual to the fundamental class of
D
.

Now, since
c_{1}(L(D))=deg\,(D)c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1))
and

c_{n-1-j}(\mathbb{P}^{n})={n+1\choose n-1-j}c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1))^{n-1-j},\,\,\,\mbox{for all $j=0,\ldots,n-2$,}

we have

\displaystyle CS(\mathcal{F},D)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hskip-7.11317pt(-1)^{n+1}\left([D]\frown c_{n-1}(T\mathbb{P}^{n}-L(D))+\sum^{n-2}_{j=0}\binom{n+1}{n-1-j}(-1)^{j+1}deg(D)^{j+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hskip-7.11317pt(-1)^{n+1}\left([D]\frown c_{n-1}(T\mathbb{P}^{n}-L(D))\right)+\sum^{n-2}_{j=0}\binom{n+1}{n-1-j}(-1)^{n+j}deg(D)^{j+1}

and formula (
5
) follows from adjunction formula (
9
).

\square

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a one-dimensional foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
D\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
be a hypersurface of degree

. If
\mathcal{F}
leaves

invariant, then the numbers
\mu(D)=\sum_{\lambda=1}^{r}\mu_{S_{\lambda}}(D)
and

satisfy the following compatibility condition

(10)

\displaystyle\mu(D)-(-1)^{n}\chi(D)\equiv 0\,(mod\,\,\,k).

Proof.
It follows from item
(c)(ii)
of Theorem 1 and formula (
5
) of Theorem 2 that
k
is a zero of the polynomial

P(X)=X^{n}+b_{n-1}X^{n-1}+\ldots+b_{1}X+b_{0},

where
\displaystyle b_{j}=-\binom{n+1}{n-j}(-1)^{n-1+j}
, for

, and
b_{0}=(-1)^{n}\chi(D)-\mu(D)
. Since

).

\square

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a one-dimensional foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, and let

D\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
be an irreducible algebraic solution of
\mathcal{F}
.
If
D
has a singular point, then the degree of

Proof.
Suppose

has a singular point, that is,

. In particular, we have that
\mu(D)>0
. Assume

, which will be shown to lead to a contradiction. Of course, formula (
5
) gives

-2=-\chi(D)+\mu(D).

Since
D
is irreducible, we have that
\chi(D)\leq 2
and, consequently,
\mu(D)=0
, a contradiction.

\square

a

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a one-dimensional foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, and let

be an irreducible algebraic solution of

. Then

\displaystyle\#{\rm Sing}(D)\leq deg(D)(\deg(\mathcal{F})-1)+2.

\displaystyle deg(D)\leq deg(\mathcal{F})+2,\,\,\,\mbox{if $D$ has at most nodal singularities,}

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\#{\rm Sing}(D)\leq deg(\mathcal{F})(\deg(\mathcal{F})+1),\mbox{if $D$ is non-dicritical with respect to $\mathcal{F}$}.

Remark 4.8
.

Inequality (

) recovers a result due to D. Cerveau and A. Lins Neto
[

, Ch. 3, Theorem 1]

Proof of Corollary
4.7
:
Let
\mathcal{F}
be a one-dimensional foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
of degree

, and let

be an irreducible algebraic solution of
\mathcal{F}
of degree

. It follows from
[
10
, Theorem C]
and
[
11
, Theorem 2]
that

(14)

\displaystyle\#{\rm Sing}(D)\leq\sum Sch_{p}(\mathcal{F},D)=2k-k^{2}+dk+\sum_{p\in Sing(D)}\mu_{p}(D)

irreducible, formula (

\displaystyle\#{\rm Sing}(D)\leq k(d-1)+2.

4.3.
Formulas for Milnor number

Formulas
(\ref{ff01})
and
(\ref{for80})
involve the Milnor number of a hypersurface with nonisolated singularities, a concept introduced by A. Parusinski in
[
14
]
. In this section, under mild conditions on the singular locus of the hypersurface, we provide explicit formulas for this invariant (Theorem
4.9
, Corollary
4.11
).

In this section, let
D\subset X
be a hypersurface with non-isolated singularities on an
n
-dimensional compact complex manifold
X
. Let
S
be a connected component of
{\rm Sing}(D)
, assumed to be a smooth local complete intersection of codimension
k
, given as the zero set of a section of a holomorphic vector bundle
N
on
X
.

Theorem 4.9
.

If the pair
(D-Sing(D),S)
satisfies Whitney’s conditions and
\mu
denotes
(n-k)
-th sectional Milnor number of
D
, then

\displaystyle\mu_{S}(D)=\mu\,.\int_{X}\hskip-1.13791pt\sum_{t=0}^{n-k}\hskip 0.0pt\sum_{j=0}^{n-k-t}\hskip 0.0pt\sum_{i=1}^{j}\sum_{\mid L\mid=j}\hskip-2.56064pt(-1)^{n-k-t+i}c_{n-k-t-j}(TX)c_{L}(N)c_{k}(N)c_{1}(L(D))^{t}

where
L:=L^{i}=(l_{1},\ldots,l_{i})\in({\mathbb{N}}^{\ast})^{i}
, with
l_{1}+\ldots+l_{i}=j
, is an
i
-dimensional multi-index and
\displaystyle c_{L}\left(N\right):=c_{l_{1}}\left(N\right)\cdots c_{l_{i}}\left(N\right)
.

Remark 4.10
.

(a)
It is knoww that
(D-Sing(D),S)
satisfies Whitney’s conditions iff for each
k=1,\ldots,n-k
, the
k
-th sectional Milnor number of
D
is constant on
S
(see
[
22
]
). In such a situation,
\mu
is defined and can be calculated as the
(n-k)
-th sectional Milnor at any point of
S
.

(b)
The case where
S
is a local complete intersection defined
as the zero set of a section of a vector bundle is the case, for example, where
S
is a global complete intersections or a complete intersections in projectives
spaces.

Proof of Theorem
4.9
:

By
[
14
, Proposition 1.7]
, we have

\mu_{S}(D)=\mu\,.\int_{S}c_{n-k}(TS^{\vee}\otimes L(D)).

The
(n-k)
-th Chern class of tensor product
TS^{\vee}\otimes L(D)
can be calculated as follows:

\displaystyle\mu_{S}(D)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu\,.\int_{S}c_{n-k}(TS^{\vee}\otimes L(D))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu\,.\int_{S}\sum_{t=0}^{n-k}(-1)^{n-k-t}c_{n-k-t}(TS)c_{1}(L(D))^{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu\,.\int_{S}\sum_{t=0}^{n-k}(-1)^{n-k-t}c_{n-k-t}(TX-N)c_{1}(L(D))^{t}

where in the last step, we make use of the fact that
TS=(TX-N)|_{S}
, as the
component
S
is a local complete intersection defined as
the zero set of a section of
N
. On the other hand, using the fact that
c_{k}(N)
is the Poincaré dual of the fundamental class of
S
, we obtain

\mu_{S}(D)=\mu\,.\int_{X}\sum_{t=0}^{n-k}(-1)^{n-k-t}c_{n-k-t}(TX-N)c_{1}(L(D))^{t}c_{k}(N).

Now, since
c_{\ast}(TX-N)=c_{\ast}(TX)c_{\ast}^{-1}(N)
, we get

c_{n-k-t}(TX-N)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-k-t}\hskip 0.0pt\sum_{i=1}^{j}\sum_{\mid L\mid=j}\hskip 0.0pt(-1)^{i}c_{n-k-t-j}(TX)c_{L}(N)

where
L:=L^{i}=(l_{1},\ldots,l_{i})\in({\mathbb{N}}^{\ast})^{i}
, with
l_{1}+\ldots+l_{i}=j
and
\displaystyle c_{L}\left(N\right):=c_{l_{1}}\left(N\right)\cdots c_{l_{i}}\left(N\right)
, and, consequently,

\displaystyle\mu_{S}(D)=\mu\,.\int_{X}\hskip-1.13791pt\sum_{t=0}^{n-k}\hskip 0.0pt\sum_{j=0}^{n-k-t}\hskip 0.0pt\sum_{i=1}^{j}\sum_{\mid L\mid=j}\hskip-2.56064pt(-1)^{n-k-t+i}c_{n-k-t-j}(TX)c_{L}(N)c_{k}(N)c_{1}(L(D))^{t}.

\square

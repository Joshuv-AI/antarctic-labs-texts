An Inverse Almost Periodic Problem for a Semilinear Strongly Damped Wave Equation
Irina Kmit Nataliya Protsakh Viktor Tkachenko

Abstract
The paper investigates an inverse boundary value problem for a semilinear strongly damped wave equation with the Dirichlet boundary condition in Sobolev spaces of bounded (in particular, almost periodic and periodic) functions. In addition to finding a weak solution, we also determine a source coefficient in the right-hand side of the differential equation. To make the problem well-posed, an integral-type overdetermination condition is imposed. After reducing the inverse problem to a direct one, we solve the latter in several steps. First, we prove the existence and uniqueness of a weak solution to the corresponding initial-boundary value problem on a finite time interval. Next, we show that this solution can be extended in a bounded way to the semiaxis t ≥ 0 t\geq 0 . In the following step, we further extend this bounded solution to all t ∈ ℝ t\in{\mathbb{R}} . Finally, we establish that if the data of the original problem are almost periodic (or periodic), then the resulting bounded weak solution is itself almost periodic (or periodic).



Key words:
semilinear strongly damped wave equation, Dirichlet boundary conditions, inverse problem,
integral overdetermination condition, bounded weak solutions, almost periodic and periodic solutions

1.2
A couple of remarks

About the equation (
1.1
).

Our analysis covers the following generalizations of equation (
1.1
). It applies to the case where the nonlinearity
\varphi

depends explicitly on
t
, as well as to the inclusion of an additional weak damping term involving
u_{t}
.
If a term incorporating
u_{t}

is included, this does not change the parabolic character of the operator
u_{tt}-a^{2}\Delta u-b(t)\Delta u_{t}
. In fact, when
b(t)=b
is constant, it shifts the spectrum to the left, increases decay, and leaves the main smoothing and regularity mechanisms (which are crucial in our analysis) unchanged.
In the present paper, we omit these extensions, as they would complicate the presentation without changing our analysis.

Another point concerns the role of
f_{2}
in our setting.
This function represents external influences that are known in advance.
It may include noise, environmental effects, or additional deterministic sources and, hence, allow the model to accommodate more general data
(covering forcings that are not exactly separable in
x
and
t
). Nevertheless, mathematically it courses no loss of generality, as
it does not affect the identification of
g(t)
.

Exponential stability and smallness assumptions.

The smallness assumptions on the data
L
,
b_{1}
, and
\|\nabla K\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
imposed in Theorem
1.2

arise from the method used to prove the main result. These assumptions ensure the exponential stability of solutions to the corresponding initial-boundary value problems. This stability, in turn, allows us to establish the existence and uniqueness of a bounded weak solution, as well as its periodicity and almost periodicity. We do not address here the question of how essential these smallness assumptions are.

Although the smallness conditions on
L
,
b_{1}
, and
\|\nabla K\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
in Theorem
1.2
are not stated explicitly, the proof provides constructive sufficient smallness conditions.

Inverse problems on finite time intervals and on semiaxes.

In addition to the inverse problem on
{\mathbb{R}}
in time, we prove that the inverse problem is well-posed on finite time intervals
(Theorem
2.3
)
and on semiaxes (Theorem
2.5

and

to be of independent interest. It is worth noting that the well-posedness of inverse problems on finite time intervals, in contrast to semiaxes and the whole axis, does not require smallness assumptions on the data.

1.3
Background and motivation

State of the art.

The aim of the present paper is to establish existence and uniqueness of almost periodic and periodic solutions for an inverse problem associated with a strongly damped hyperbolic equation. In this model, both the solution and the time-dependent coefficient
g(t)
on the right-hand side are unknown. The latter is identified through an integral overdetermination condition. In most works on inverse problems of this type, the reconstruction of unknown coefficients relies on prescribed initial data together with the overdetermination condition, and the analysis is carried out on a finite time interval. In contrast, the present study does not impose initial conditions, considers an unbounded time domain, and seeks periodic or almost periodic solutions. This framework necessitates an appropriate analytical approach and gives rise to new challenges in comparison with classical finite-time inverse problems.

Although there are relatively few works dealing specifically with
time-periodic or almost periodic
solutions of the

direct
problems for (strongly) damped wave equations and, to the best of our knowledge, none concerning the corresponding inverse problems, the underlying mechanism suggesting the existence of such solutions is well understood.
In particular, in the presence of a time-periodic external forcing, one may expect the solution to inherit this temporal periodicity, since the energy injected into the system by the forcing term during one period
is dissipated through the damping. The emergence of a periodic solution, viewed as a demonstration of nonresonant behavior of the system, is thus generated by the balance between periodic forcing and dissipation.

Concerning direct problems, the existence of nonresonant time-periodic solutions to the (strongly damped)
Kuznetsov equation with Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions was investigated in
[
5
]
.
Periodic solutions for a class of nonlinear strongly damped wave equations with Kelvin–Voigt damping and periodic nonlinearities were studied using topological degree methods in
[
22
]
.
There is even less available in the PDE literature for almost periodic forcing in the strongly damped case
[
11
,
15
,
24
]
.

It should be noted that, in the autonomous setting, strongly damped wave equations generate analytic semigroups and therefore exhibit parabolic-type behavior, including smoothing effects and an absence of resonances.

In contrast to strongly damped wave equations, which exhibit completely nonresonant behavior, the study of periodic solutions for weakly damped wave equations is complicated by the possible occurrence of resonances. To exclude such effects, additional assumptions must be imposed. As shown by Dafermos
[
12
]
, weak damping does not, in general, suppress resonances, owing to the qualitative differences between the spectra in the weakly and strongly damped (“parabolic”) cases.
For results on periodic and almost periodic solutions of weakly damped wave equations, we refer the reader, for example, to
[
13
,
17
,
21
,
27
,
29
,
30
,
36
]
and the references therein.

The study of inverse periodic and almost periodic problems for strongly damped wave equations is motivated by applications in viscoelasticity, thermoelasticity, and structural acoustics. In these settings, materials and structures are often subjected to long-time periodic or quasi-periodic external forces. Identifying system parameters from such repeated excitations becomes essential for diagnostics, monitoring, and control.

Recovering the temporal factor
g(t)
is of particular interest, as it
represents the time modulation of a known spatially localized action

f_{1}(x)
and determines the periodic or almost periodic response of the system.

Similar inverse source identification problems, including separable sources, are classical in the theory of inverse problems for PDEs (see, e.g.,
[
10
,
18
,
28
]
), and the analysis of periodic and almost periodic regimes of evolution equations with damping is closely related to the general theory of long-time behavior and regularity for dissipative systems (e.g.,
[
7
,
11
,
3
,
24
]
).

Another point concerns integral-type overdetermination conditions (in our paper, this is the integral constraint given by (
1.3
)), which are interesting from both applied and mathematical perspectives. In applications, continuous pointwise measurements in time are often impractical, whereas cumulative quantities, such as total displacement, energy, stress, or time-averaged boundary data, are typically accessible.
Mathematically, such integral conditions tend to be more robust to noise and often lead to improved stability and well-posedness compared to pointwise-in-time measurements.
We here mention several works in which inverse problems for strongly and weakly damped wave or pseudoparabolic models are studied using integral-type overdetermination conditions to recover unknown sources or coefficients
[
1
,
2
,
19
,
28
,
31
,
32
,
33
,
34
,
35
]
.

Applications.

The strongly damped wave equation is often used to model longitudinal vibrations of a homogeneous bar (or transversal vibrations of a string) with viscosity or internal friction, i.e. a viscoelastic (Kelvin–Voigt) material.
In that context the damping term
b\nabla u_{t}
accounts for the fact that stress depends not only on strain (as in Hooke’s law) but also on the rate of strain.
In general, equation (
1.1
), or its various generalizations, can serve as an idealized model for processes such as elastic waves in viscous solids, acoustic waves in viscoelastic fluids, or wave propagation in porous media saturated with viscous fluids. It is also worth mentioning that it includes, as particular cases, well-known models such as the Kuznetsov equation and the sine-Gordon equation.
Due to their wide range of applications, equations
(
1.1
)
have been extensively studied by many authors
[
4
,
6
,
11
,
14
,
24
,
3
,
20
,
26
]
. These works address, among other topics, the long-time behavior of solutions, including the existence of asymptotically periodic and almost periodic solutions as well as the existence of global attractors. The monograph by Lesnic
[
23
]
provides an extensive account of inverse problems with applications in science and engineering, encompassing models based on damped wave equations.

Proof.

The proof is devided into a number of steps.

1. Galerkin approximations and energy estimates.

is thus proved.

2.3
Bohr almost periodic solutions

Let us recall the definition of a Bohr almost periodic function as given in
[
9
]
.
Let
(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})
and
(Z,\|\cdot\|_{Z})
be Banach
spaces. A continuous function
f:\mathbb{R}\times Y\to Z
is said to be
Bohr almost periodic
in
t\in{\mathbb{R}}

uniformly in
x\in Y
if for every

\varepsilon>0
and every compact set
{\cal K}\subset Y
there exists a number
l=l(\varepsilon,{\cal K})>0
such
that any interval of length
l
contains a real number
h
(called an
\varepsilon
-almost period) such
that

\|f(t+h,y)-f(t,y)\|_{Z}<\varepsilon

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and all
y\in{\cal K}
.

Let
U
be a (unique) bounded weak solution constructed in Section
2.2.3
. To prove that
U

is Bohr almost periodic in
t
, let
h
be a common
\varepsilon
-almost period of functions
F_{\alpha}(x,t)
and
b(t).

Then the function

w(x,t)=U(x,t+h)-U(x,t)

satisfies the following equation in the sense of Definition
1.1
:

\begin{array}[]{rr}w_{tt}-a^{2}\Delta w-b(t)\Delta w_{t}-(b(t+h)-b(t))\Delta U_{t}(x,t+h))+\varphi(x,U(x,t+h))-\varphi(x,U(x,t))\\[5.69054pt]
=F_{\alpha}(x,t+h)-F_{\alpha}(x,t)+[\Phi_{\alpha}U](x,t)-[\Phi_{\alpha}U](x,t+h),\qquad x\in\Omega,\ t\in\mathbb{R}.\end{array}

(2.80)

Writing

\displaystyle W_{h}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{h}(t)+\eta\left(\int_{\Omega}w(x,t)w_{t}(x,t)\!\;\mathrm{d}x+\frac{b(t)}{2}\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla w(x,t)\|^{2}\!\;\mathrm{d}x\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{h}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|w_{t}(x,t)|^{2}\!\;\mathrm{d}x+\frac{a^{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla w(x,t)\|^{2}\!\;\mathrm{d}x,

and following the proof of Lemma
2.5
, we obtain an analog of the inequality (
2.61
), namely

\displaystyle\displaystyle W_{h}(t)\leq e^{{-\eta_{1}}(t-s)}W_{h}(s)+A_{11}\sup_{t}\left(\|F_{\alpha}(\cdot,t+h)-F_{\alpha}(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+|b(t+h)-b(t)|\right),

where positive constants
\eta
,
\eta_{1}
, and
A_{11}
are independent of
h
and
t.

Again, for any
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
and any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
s\leq t
such that
e^{-{\eta}(t-s)}\leq\varepsilon.

This entails that

\|U_{t}(\cdot,t+h)-U_{t}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|U(x,t+h)-U(x,t)\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq A_{12}\varepsilon

for a positive constant
A_{12}
, which is independent of
h
,
t
, and
\varepsilon
.
This means that the function
U(x,t)
is almost periodic in
t
with values in

H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and with almost period
h
. Moreover,
U_{t}(x,t)
is also
almost periodic in
t
with values in

L^{2}(\Omega)
and with the same almost period
h
.

To show that the function
g(t)
, defined by (
2.78
), is Bohr almost periodic with almost period
h
, note that
since
E\in BC^{3}(\mathbb{R})
, its second-order derivative
E^{\prime\prime}
is almost periodic, due to

[
9
]
. Using additionally the Bohr almost periodicity of
U_{t}
and
\nabla U
,
the almost periodicity of
U
now follows directly from (
2.78
).

2.4
Periodic solutions

To prove that, if the data of the original problem are
\omega
-periodic, then the solution
U
is also
\omega
-periodic, we follow the proof of the almost periodicity of
U
given in Section
2.3
, replacing almost periodicity by periodicity with
h=\omega
at each step. Note that the
BC^{2}(\mathbb{R})
-regularity of the function
E

(in contrast to the
BC^{3}(\mathbb{R})
-regularity required in the almost periodic case) is sufficient.

The proof of Claim 2, and hence the proof of Theorem
1.2
, is complete.

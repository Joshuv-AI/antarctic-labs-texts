Abstract
The chromopolarizability of a charm-beauty quarkonium characterizes its interaction with soft gluonic fields and can be probed through heavy quarkonium decays. Using the dispersion theory which considers the π ​ π \pi\pi final state interaction model-independently, we analyze the transition B c ​ ( 2 ​ S ) → B c ​ π + ​ π − B_{c}(2S)\rightarrow B_{c}\pi^{+}\pi^{-} and determine the chromopolarizability α B c ​ ( 2 ​ S ) ​ B c = ( 0.66 ± 0.25 ) \alpha_{B_{c}(2S)B_{c}}=(0.66\pm 0.25) GeV -3 and the parameter κ = − 0.02 ± 0.13 \kappa=-0.02\pm 0.13 . Our results indicate that the transitional chromopolarizability of the c ​ b ¯ c\bar{b} state lies between the transitional chromopolarizabilities of the c ​ c ¯ c\bar{c} and b ​ b ¯ b\bar{b} states. These findings provide valuable insights into the interactions of charm-beauty quarkonium with light hadrons.



I
Introduction

The study of the
B_{c}
system is important since it is the only one that contains two different heavy flavors in the Standard Model. The
c\bar{b}
states, unlike their counterparts in the
J/\psi
and
\Upsilon
families, cannot annihilate into gluons, making them more stable.
The ground
B_{c}(1^{1}S_{0})

[
1
,
2
]
. In 2014, the ATLAS Collaboration observed a peak at
6842\pm 4\pm 5

[
3
]
,
which could be interpreted either as the
B_{c}^{*}(2^{3}S_{1})
excited state or a pair of peaks resulting from the decays of
B_{c}(2^{1}S_{0})\rightarrow B_{c}(1^{1}S_{0})\pi^{+}\pi^{-}

and
B_{c}^{*}(2^{3}S_{1})\rightarrow B_{c}^{*}(1^{3}S_{1})\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
followed by
B_{c}^{*}(1^{3}S_{1})\rightarrow B_{c}(1^{1}S_{0})\gamma
. In 2019, the CMS
[
4
]
and LHCb
[
5
]
Collaborations identified signals consistent with the
B_{c}(2S)
and
B_{c}^{*}(2S)
states in the
B_{c}(1S)\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
invariant mass spectrum. Shortly thereafter, the normalized invariant mass distributions of the pions pair emitted in the
B_{c}^{(*)}(2S)\rightarrow B_{c}^{(*)}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
decays were presented
[
6
]
. The dipion transition to the ground
B_{c}
state is the dominant decay mode of the
B_{c}(2S)
state and will be key to characterizing those states, which we focus on in this study.
The dipion transitions between heavy quarkonia are typically described as a two-step process in which the soft gluons are first emitted from the heavy quarks and then recombine into light quarks.
The heavy quarkonia are expected to be compact, and the
method of QCD multipole expansion
[

,

,

,

\psi(2S)\rightarrow\psi(1S)\pi\pi
and
\Upsilon(2S)\rightarrow\Upsilon(1S)\pi\pi
. Since the electricmultipole transitions are only sensitive to the relative position of the quark and antiquark, the same method has been used to study the hadronic transition for the unequal-mass
Q\bar{Q}^{\prime}
systems
B_{c}(2S)\rightarrow B_{c}(1S)\pi\pi

[
11
,
12
,

,

,

,

\pi\pi

[

,

,

,

. In the
B_{c}(2S)\rightarrow B_{c}(1S)\pi\pi

\pi\pi
FSI significant and requiring careful consideration. We will employ dispersion theory to account for the
\pi\pi
FSI and study the decay
B_{c}(2S)\rightarrow B_{c}(1S)\pi\pi
. The same method has been successfully used to describe transitions of
\psi(2S)\rightarrow\psi(1S)\pi\pi
and
\Upsilon(2S)\rightarrow\Upsilon(1S)\pi\pi

[

,

,

The chromopolarizability
\alpha
of a heavy quarkonium quantifies its effective interaction with soft gluons and is an important parameter for describing quarkonium interactions. Recently, the chromopolarizability of heavy quarkonium has garnered increased interest due to its relevance in interpreting the structures of multiquark hadrons containing heavy quark and antiquark.
For reasonable values of the chromopolarizabilities of the charmonia, bound states such as the double-
J/\psi
state, several hadro-charmonium bound states, and baryo-charmonium bound states have been identified with certain
XYZ
states and the
P_{c}^{+}
pentaquark states
[
26
,
22
,
23
,
21
,
24
,
25
]
.
Similarly, for appropriate values of the chromopolarizabilities of bottomonia, several hadro-bottomonium and baryo-bottomonium bound states have been predicted
[
27
,
28
]
. The
c\bar{b}
states are intermediate between the
c\bar{c}
and
b\bar{b}
states, both in mass and in size. Therefore, it is expected that the values of the chromopolarizabilities of the
c\bar{b}
states will fall between the values of the chromopolarizabilities of the
c\bar{c}
and the
b\bar{b}
states. For certain values of the chromopolarizabilities of the
c\bar{b}
states, multiquark hadrons containing a
c\bar{b}
component may also exist.
While the diagonal chromopolarizabilities
\alpha_{AA}
, with
A
representing a heavy quarkonium, cannot be determined directly from current experimental data. One possible method to estimate
\alpha_{AA}
assumes that the heavy quarkonia are purely color-Coulombic systems. This assumption works well for ground-state bottomonia but introduces significant uncertainties for charmonia and excited bottomonia
[
28
,
29
]
.
On the other hand, the determination of the transitional chromopolarizability
\alpha_{A^{\prime}A}\equiv\alpha_{A^{\prime}\to A}
is of importance as it serves as a reference benchmark for each of the diagonal terms due to the Schwartz inequality:
\alpha_{AA}\alpha_{A^{\prime}A^{\prime}}\geq\alpha_{A^{\prime}A}^{2}

[
30
,
21
]
.
In this work, we will extract the value of
\alpha_{B_{c}(2S)B_{c}}
from the
B_{c}(2S)\rightarrow B_{c}(1S)\pi\pi
transition.

The theoretical framework is outlined in Sec.

. In Sec.

\pi\pi

B_{c}(2S)\rightarrow B_{c}(1S)\pi\pi

\alpha_{B_{c}(2S)B_{c}}
and the parameter
\kappa

IV
.

II
Theoretical framework

The low energy interaction of heavy quarkonia with light hadrons
is mediated by soft gluons. The heavy quarkonia are expected to be compact, so that the method of QCD
multipole expansion is often employed to study the interaction of heavy quarkonia with
soft gluons. The leading
E1
chromo-electric dipole
term in the expansion is simple

H_{E1}=-\frac{1}{2}\xi^{a}\textit{{r}}\cdot\textit{{E}}^{a}
,
where
\xi^{a}=t_{1}^{a}-t_{2}^{a}
is the difference between the SU(3) color generators acting
on the quark and anti-quark,
r
is the relative position vector between the quark and anti-quark, and
\textit{{E}}^{a}
is
the chromoelectric field.
For the transition between two
S
-wave heavy quarkonium
states
A
and
B
with an emitted gluon system which possesses the
quantum numbers of two pions, the leading order amplitude is in the second order of
H_{E1}

[
22
]

\langle B|H_{eff}|A\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{AB}\textit{{E}}^{a}\cdot\textit{{E}}^{a},,

(1)

where
\alpha_{AB}
denotes the chromopolarizability, quantifying the strength of the
E1
-
E1
chromo-electric
interaction of the quarkonium with the soft gluon system.

The amplitude for the dipion transition between two
S
-wave heavy quarkonium states,
A
and
B
,
is expressed as
[
22
,
31
]

M_{AB}=2\sqrt{m_{A}m_{B}}\alpha_{AB}\langle\pi^{+}(p_{c})\pi^{-}(p_{d})|\frac{1}{2}\textit{{E}}^{a}\cdot\textit{{E}}^{a}|0\rangle=\frac{8\pi^{2}}{b}\sqrt{m_{A}m_{B}}\alpha_{AB}(\kappa_{1}p^{0}_{c}p^{0}_{d}-\kappa_{2}p^{i}_{c}p^{i}_{d}),

(2)

where the factor
2\sqrt{m_{A}m_{B}}
arises from the relativistic
normalization of the amplitude.
b
denotes the first coefficient of the QCD beta function,
and its expression is
b=\frac{11}{3}N_{c}-\frac{2}{3}N_{f},
where
N_{c}=3
and
N_{f}=3
are the number of colors and of light flavors, respectively. The parameters
\kappa_{1}

and
\kappa_{2}
are not independent, since
\kappa_{1}=2-9\kappa/2
and
\kappa_{2}=2+3\kappa/2
, where the value of
\kappa
can be determined from experimental data.

The above expression of the QCD multipole expansion together with the
soft-pion theorem, can be derived by constructing a chiral
effective Lagrangian for the
B_{c}(2S)\rightarrow B_{c}(1S)\pi\pi

transition
[
32
,
18
]

\mathscr{L}_{B_{c}(2S)B_{c}\pi\pi}=c_{1}B_{c}(2S)B_{c}\langle u_{\mu}u^{\mu}\rangle+c_{2}B_{c}(2S)B_{c}\langle J^{\dagger}J^{\prime}\rangle\langle u_{\mu}u_{\nu}\rangle v^{\mu}v^{\nu}+\mathrm{h.c.}\,,

(3)

where
v^{\mu}=(1,\textit{{0}})
represents the velocity of the heavy quark.
The Goldstone bosons of the spontaneous breaking of
chiral symmetry can be parametrized as

\displaystyle u_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\textrm{i}\left(u^{\dagger}\partial_{\mu}u\,-\,u\partial_{\mu}u^{\dagger}\right)\,,\qquad u=\exp\Big(\frac{\textrm{i}\Phi}{\sqrt{2}F}\Big)\,,

\displaystyle\Phi

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\pi^{0}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\eta_{8}}&{\pi^{+}}&{K^{+}}\\
{\pi^{-}}&{-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\pi^{0}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\eta_{8}}&{K^{0}}\\
{K^{-}}&{\bar{K}^{0}}&{-\frac{2}{\sqrt{6}}\eta_{8}}\\
\end{pmatrix},

(4)

where
F
represents the pseudo-Goldstone boson decay constant, and we will
use
F_{\pi}=92.2\,\text{MeV}
for the pions and
F_{K}=113.0\,\text{MeV}
for the
kaons.

The contact term amplitude for the
B_{c}(2S)(p_{a})\to B_{c}(p_{b})\pi(p_{c})\pi(p_{d})

process, derived using the chiral effective Lagrangians
in Eq. (
3
) is

M(s,\cos\theta)=-\frac{4}{F_{\pi}^{2}}(c_{1}p_{c}\cdot p_{d}+c_{2}p_{c}^{0}p_{d}^{0})\,,

(5)

where
s=(p_{c}+p_{d})^{2}
, and
\theta
is the
angle between the 3-momentum of the
\pi^{+}
in the rest frame of the dipion
system and that of the dipion system in the rest frame of the initial

B_{c}(2S)
.
By matching the amplitude in Eq. (
2
) to that in Eq. (
5
), the chiral
effective Lagrangian coupling constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
can be expressed in terms of the chromopolarizability
\alpha_{AB}
and the parameter
\kappa
,

\displaystyle c_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi^{2}\sqrt{m_{B_{c}(2S)}m_{B_{c}}}F_{\pi}^{2}}{b}\alpha_{B_{c}(2S)B_{c}}(4+3\kappa),

\displaystyle c_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{12\pi^{2}\sqrt{m_{B_{c}(2S)}m_{B_{c}}}F_{\pi}^{2}}{b}\alpha_{B_{c}(2S)B_{c}}\kappa\,.

(6)

The strong FSIs between two pseudoscalar mesons can be incorporated
model-independently using dispersion theory. Since the invariant
mass of the pion pair approaches to the
K\bar{K}
threshold, we
will consider the coupled-channel (
\pi\pi
and
K\bar{K}
) FSI for the dominant
S
-wave component, while for the
D
-wave
we will only take account of the single-channel FSI.

For the
B_{c}(2S)(p_{a})\to B_{c}(p_{b})\pi^{+}(p_{c})\pi^{-}(p_{d})

processes, the partial-wave expansion of the amplitude including FSI
is expressed as

\mathscr{M}^{\text{full}}(s,\cos\theta)=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}M_{l}^{\pi}(s)P_{l}(\cos\theta)\,

(7)

For the dominant
S
-wave, we consider the two-channel
rescattering effects. The two-channel
unitarity conditions reads

\textrm{Im}\,\textit{{M}}_{0}(s)=2\textrm{i}T_{0}^{0\ast}(s)\Sigma(s)\textit{{M}}_{0}(s)\,,

(8)

where the two-dimensional vector
\textit{{M}}_{0}(s)
contains both the
\pi\pi
and the
K\bar{K}

final states,

\textit{{M}}_{0}(s)=\left({\begin{array}[]{*{2}c}{M^{\pi}_{0}(s)}\\
{\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}M^{K}_{0}(s)}\\
\end{array}}\right)\,.

(9)

The two-dimensional matrices
T_{0}^{0}(s)
and
\Sigma(s)
are defined as

T_{0}^{0}(s)=\left({\begin{array}[]{*{2}c}\frac{\eta_{0}^{0}(s)e^{2\textrm{i}\delta_{0}^{0}(s)}-1}{2\textrm{i}\sigma_{\pi}(s)}&|g_{0}^{0}(s)|e^{\textrm{i}\psi_{0}^{0}(s)}\\
|g_{0}^{0}(s)|e^{\textrm{i}\psi_{0}^{0}(s)}&\frac{\eta_{0}^{0}(s)e^{2\textrm{i}\left(\psi_{0}^{0}(s)-\delta_{0}^{0}(s)\right)}-1}{2\textrm{i}\sigma_{K}(s)}\\
\end{array}}\right)

(10)

and

\Sigma(s)\equiv\text{diag}\big(\sigma_{\pi}(s)\theta(s-4m_{\pi}^{2}),\sigma_{K}(s)\theta(s-4m_{K}^{2})\big)
.
The
T_{0}^{0}(s)
matrix incorporates three input functions: the

\pi\pi

S
-wave isoscalar phase shift
\delta_{0}^{0}(s)
and the
\pi\pi\to K\bar{K}

S
-wave
amplitude
g_{0}^{0}(s)=|g_{0}^{0}(s)|e^{\textrm{i}\psi_{0}^{0}(s)}
with its modulus and
phase. We will use the parameterization of the
T_{0}^{0}(s)
matrices given in Refs.
[
33
,
34
]
. These inputs are used up to
\sqrt{s_{0}}=1.3\,\text{GeV}
(the
f_{0}(1370)
resonance is known to have a significant coupling to
4\pi

[
35
]
).
Beyond
s_{0}
, the phases

\delta_{0}^{0}(s)
and
\psi_{0}^{0}
are smoothly guided to
2
\pi
via
[
36
]

\delta(s)=2\pi+(\delta(s_{0})-2\pi)\frac{2}{1+(\frac{s}{s_{0}})^{3/2}}\,.

(11)

The inelasticity
\eta_{0}^{0}(s)
in Eq. (
10
) is related to
the modulus
|g_{0}^{0}(s)|
by

\eta_{0}^{0}(s)=\sqrt{1-4\sigma_{\pi}(s)\sigma_{K}(s)|g_{0}^{0}(s)|^{2}\theta(s-4m_{K}^{2})}\,.

(12)

The solution to the two-channel
unitarity condition in Eq. (
8
) is given by

\textit{{M}}_{0}(s)=\Omega(s)\textit{{P}}^{n-1}(s)\,,

(13)

where
\Omega(s)
satisfies the homogeneous two-channel unitarity
relation

\textrm{Im}\,\Omega(s)=2\textrm{i}T_{0}^{0\ast}(s)\Sigma(s)\Omega(s),\hskip 28.45274pt\Omega(0)=\mathbbm{1}\,.

(14)

Numerical results for
\Omega(s)
are available in
Refs.
[
37
,
36
,
39
,
38
]
.

For the
D
-wave, we consider the single-channel FSI. In the elastic

\pi\pi
rescattering region, the partial-wave unitarity conditions
is expressed as

\textrm{Im}\,M_{2}(s)=M_{2}(s)\sin\delta_{2}^{0}(s)e^{-\textrm{i}\delta_{2}^{0}(s)}\,.

(15)

In the elastic region, the phase of the
D
-wave isoscalar amplitude
\delta_{2}^{0}
equals the

\pi\pi
elastic phase shifts modulo
n\pi
, as required by Watson’s
theorem
[
40
,
41
]
. The Omnès function is defined
as
[
42
]

\Omega_{2}^{0}(s)=\exp\bigg\{\frac{s}{\pi}\int^{\infty}_{4m_{\pi}^{2}}\frac{\textrm{d}x}{x}\frac{\delta_{2}^{0}(x)}{x-s}\bigg\}\,.

(16)

Using the Omnès function, the solution of
Eq. (
15
) is given by

M_{2}(s)=\Omega_{2}^{0}(s)P_{2}^{n-1}(s)\,,

(17)

where
the polynomial
P_{2}^{n-1}(s)
is a subtraction function. We use the result of the
D
-wave phase shift
\delta_{2}^{0}(s)
given
in Ref.
[
43
]
, and extend it smoothly to
\pi

s\to\infty

On the other hand, at low energies, the amplitudes
M_{2}(s)
and
\textit{{M}}_{0}(s)
should
match the results from chiral perturbation theory. Namely, if one turn off the final-state interactions,

\Omega_{2}^{0}(s)=1
and
\Omega(0)=\mathbbm{1}
, the subtraction
functions should agree with the partial-wave projections of the chiral result obtained using the Lagrangian in
Eq. (
3
). For the
S
-wave, this leads to the integral equation:

\textit{{M}}_{0}(s)=\Omega(s)\textit{{M}}_{0}^{\chi}(s)\,,

(18)

where

\displaystyle\textit{{M}}^{\chi}_{0}(s)=

\displaystyle\big(M_{0}^{\chi,\pi}(s),2/\sqrt{3}\,M_{0}^{\chi,K}(s)\big)^{T}\,,

(19)

\displaystyle M_{0}^{\chi,\pi(K)}(s)=

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{F_{\pi(K)}^{2}}\sqrt{m_{B_{c}(2S)}m_{B_{c}}}\bigg\{c_{1}\left(s-2m_{\pi(K)}^{2}\right)+\frac{c_{2}}{2}\bigg[s+\textit{{q}}^{2}\Big(1-\frac{\sigma_{\pi(K)}^{2}}{3}\Big)\bigg]\bigg\}\,.

(20)

For the
D
-wave, the integral equation is given by

M_{2}^{\pi}(s)=\Omega_{2}^{0}(s)M_{2}^{\chi,\pi}(s)\,,

(21)

where

\displaystyle M_{2}^{\chi,\pi}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3F_{\pi}^{2}}\sqrt{m_{B_{c}(2S)}m_{B_{c}}}\,c_{2}|\textit{{q}}|^{2}\sigma_{\pi}^{2}\,.

(22)

The
\pi\pi
invariant mass distribution for the

B_{c}(2S)\rightarrow B_{c}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}

\frac{\textrm{d}\Gamma}{\textrm{d}\sqrt{s}}=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\sqrt{s}\,\sigma_{\pi}|\textit{{q}}|}{128\pi^{3}m_{B_{c}(2S)}^{2}}\left|M_{0}^{\pi}+M_{2}^{\pi}P_{2}(\cos\theta)\right|^{2}\textrm{d}\cos\theta\,,

P_{2}(\cos\theta)=(3\cos^{2}\theta-1)/2.

Phenomenological discussion

B_{c}(2S)\rightarrow B_{c}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}

[
6

only provides the normalized dipion invariant mass distributions, we use the theoretical prediction of

in Ref.
[
13

\Gamma_{B_{c}(2S)\rightarrow B_{c}\pi\pi}
in Ref.
[
13
]
is the sum of the decay modes with charged and neutral pions, and we take the decay rate of the neutral pions mode to be half of the decay rate of the charged pions mode.

The two free parameters are the low-energy coupling constants
c_{1}
and

in terms of the chromopolarizability

IV
Conclusions

We employed dispersion theory to investigate the
\pi\pi
FSI in the

B_{c}(2S)\to B_{c}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
transition. By fitting the
\pi\pi
mass spectra data, the values of the transitional chromopolarizability
\alpha_{B_{c}(2S)B_{c}}

and the parameter
\kappa
are determined. Our results indicate that the transitional chromopolarizability of the
c\bar{b}
state lies between the transitional chromopolarizabilities of the
c\bar{c}
and
b\bar{b}
states.
Additionally, the parameter
\kappa
, which accounts for the
\pi\pi

D
-wave contribution, is tiny. The findings of this study provide valuable insights into the chromopolarizability of
B_{c}
states and have potential applications in the investigation of multiquark hadrons containing
c\bar{b}
.

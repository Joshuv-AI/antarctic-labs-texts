Upper cluster structure on Kac–Moody Richardson varieties
Huanchen Bao, Jeff York Ye

Abstract
. We show coordinate rings of open Richardson varieties are upper cluster algebras for any symmetrizable Kac–Moody type. We further show the coordinate rings of (generalized) open Richardson varieties on the twisted product of flag varieties are upper cluster algebras for any symmetrizable Kac–Moody type. This includes, as special cases, reduced double Bruhat cells, Bott-Samelson varieties, braid varieties. Our results generalize various results by Casals–Gorsky–Gorsky–Le–Shen–Simental and Galashin–Lam–Sherman-Bennett–Speyer in finite types.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Richardson varieties

Let
G
be a symmetrizable (minimal) Kac–Moody group over
\mathbb{C}
. Let
B^{+}
and
B^{-}
be opposite Borel subgroups. We denote the (thin) flag variety by
\mathcal{B}=G/B^{+}
. The flag variety admits a natural decomposition into open Richardson varieties

\mathcal{B}=\sqcup_{v\leq w}\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w},\quad\text{ where $v\leq w$ are in the Weyl group }W.

The main result of this paper is the following theorem.

Theorem 1

(Theorem
5.13
)
.

The coordinate ring
\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}]
of the open Richardson variety in
\mathcal{B}
is an upper cluster algebra.

Cluster structures on open Richardson varieties for reductive groups have been conjectured by Leclerc
[
Lec
]
. Such a cluster structure has been established in the recent breakthroughs by Casals–Gorsky–Gorsky–Le–Shen–Simental
[
CGG+
]
and Galashin–Lam–Sherman-Bennett–Speyer
[
GLSS
,
GLSS2
]
. We shall refer to
loc. cit.
for background and history on cluster structures of Richardson varieties.

Both
[
CGG+
]
and
[
GLSS2
]
establish the cluster structure on open braid varieties first, and deduce the cluster structure on open Richardson varieties as special cases. It is not known whether open Richardson varieties can be identified with open braid varieties in the Kac–Moody setting in general, so approaches in
[
CGG+
,
GLSS2
]
do not carry to the Kac–Moody setting. Let us also mention that there has been attempts by Kashiwara–Kim–Oh–Park
[
KKOP
]
generalizing Leclerc’s categorical construction to the Kac–Moody setting.

If
G
is reductive, our initial cluster seed
\textnormal{{s}}(v,\textnormal{{w}})
(Definition
5.11
) is the same as the one constructed in
[
CGG+
,
Men22
]
, which is expected to be same as the seed in
[
GLSS2
]
via explicit isomorphisms. The construction of our cluster seed is motivated by the construction of Ménard
[
Men22
]
in the reductive simply-laced case. We simplify and generalize Ménard’s construction to the Kac–Moody setting. Moreover, we give a geometric explanation for each step in the seed construction.

1.2.
Twisted product of flag varieties

Let

\mathcal{Z}=G\times^{B^{+}}G\times^{B^{+}}G\times^{B^{+}}\cdots\times^{B^{+}}G/B^{+}\text{ ($n$ factors) }

be the twisted product of flag varieties.
For any sequence
\underline{w}=(w_{1},\cdots,w_{n})
of elements in
W
, we define

\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}_{\underline{w}}=B^{+}\dot{w}_{1}B^{+}\times^{B^{+}}B^{+}\dot{w}_{2}B^{+}\times^{B^{+}}\cdots\times^{B^{+}}B^{+}\dot{w}_{n}B^{+}/B^{+}.

This is analogous to Schubert cell in the flag variety
\mathcal{B}
. For any
v\in W
, we define
\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}^{v}
as
m^{-1}(B^{-}\dot{v}B^{+}/B^{+})
, where
m:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{B}
is the convolution product. This is analogous to the opposite Schubert cell in the flag variety
\mathcal{B}
. The open Richardson variety of
\mathcal{Z}
is defined by

\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}_{v,{\underline{w}}}=\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}_{{\underline{w}}}\cap\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}^{v}.

The intersection is non-empty if and only if
v\leq m_{\ast}({\underline{w}})

[
BH24b
]
. Here
m_{\ast}({\underline{w}})
denotes the Demazure product of
\underline{w}
. These open Richardson varieties include, as special cases, the reduced double Bruhat cells, the Bott-Samelson varieties, and the braid varieties.

Theorem 2

(Theorem
6.5
& Remark
6.1
)
.

The coordinate ring
\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}_{v,{\underline{w}}}]
of the open Richardson variety
\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}_{v,{\underline{w}}}
in the twisted product is an upper cluster algebra.

Our construction of the cluster structure on
\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}_{v,{\underline{w}}}
is based on the thickening considered by the first author and He in
[
BH24b
]
. Via this thickening, the upper cluster structure on
\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}_{v,{\underline{w}}}]
is actually a direct consequence of the upper cluster structure on a Richardson variety of another more complicated Kac–Moody group from Theorem
1
. In the case of braid varieties, we expect our seed to coincide with the seed constructed in
[
CGG+
]
and
[
GLSS2
]
. Logically, our construction is reverse to the constructions in
[
CGG+
,
GLSS2
]
when restricting to reductive groups.

1.3.
Total positivity

The totally nonnegative part
{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w,>0}
of the open Richardson variety for reductive groups is defined by Lusztig
[
Lus94
]
using his theory of canonical bases. The generalization to the Kac–Moody setting is straightforward
[
BH24a
]
. On the other hand, the totally nonnegative part
{\mathcal{Z}}_{v,{\underline{w}},>0}
of the Richardson variety on the twisted product is defined by the first author and He
[
BH24b
]
.

One of the original motivation of Fomin–Zelevinsky
[
FZ02
]
to introduce cluster algebras is to better understand Lusztig’s theory of canonical bases and total positivity. Given the cluster structure on
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}
(resp.
\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}_{v,{\underline{w}}}
), we can define the cluster positive locus
{\mathcal{B}}^{cl}_{v,w,>0}
(resp.
{\mathcal{Z}}^{cl}_{v,{\underline{w}},>0}
) of
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}
(resp.
\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}_{v,{\underline{w}}}
), where all cluster variables take values in
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.

Theorem 3

(Proposition
7.4
& Proposition
7.5
)
.

The two notions of positivity coincide, that is,

{\mathcal{B}}^{cl}_{v,w,>0}={\mathcal{B}}_{v,w,>0},\quad{\mathcal{Z}}^{cl}_{v,{\underline{w}},>0}={\mathcal{Z}}_{v,{\underline{w}},>0}.

Theorem
3
is new even in the reductive case. Thanks to the concrete geometric interpretation of the seed construction, Theorem
3
is again a direct consequence of Theorem
1
.

1.4.
Strategy of the proof

Two key ingredients of our approach to Theorem
1
are the Bruhat atlas introduced by He–Knutson–Lu
[
HKL
]
and rational quasi-cluster maps introduced in §
4.1
.

The Bruhat atlas used in our setting actually goes back to Kazhdan–Lusztig
[
KL
]
. For
v\leq r\leq w
in the Weyl group
W
, we have an isomorphism

\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}\cap\dot{r}U^{-}B^{+}/B^{+}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,r}\times\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{r,w}.

Note that
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,r}\cong\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, if it is one dimensional. Iterating the process, we can realize
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}\times\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}
as a open subvariety of
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{e,w}
. We can further identify
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}
as a locally closed subvariety in
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{e,w}
by specializing the coordinates in
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}
.

The cluster structure on
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{e,w}
has already been established following Williams
[
Wil13
]
and Shen-Weng
[
SW21
]
. The geometric construction of localization and specialization correspond to freezing and deletion in terms of cluster algebras, respectively.
In order to perform the relevant freezing and deletion, it remains to calculate explicitly the rational function
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T_{i}:\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{e,w}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}^{\times}}
to each coordinate in terms of cluster variables.

We introduce rational quasi-cluster maps §
4.1
for this calculation. Roughly speaking, rational quasi-cluster maps are rational maps between two (upper) cluster algebras generalizing quasi-equivalence. The key feature is that rational quasi-cluster maps preserve Laurent monomials of cluster variables.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Minimal Kac–Moody groups

We follow
[
Kum02
]
for basics on Kac–Moody groups. Let
I
be a finite set and
A=(a_{ij})_{i,j\in I}
be a symmetrizable generalized Cartan matrix. A
Kac–Moody root datum
associated to
A
is a quintuple

\mathcal{D}=(I,A,X,Y,(\alpha_{i})_{i\in I},(\alpha_{i}^{\vee})_{i\in I}),

where
X
is a free
\mathbb{Z}
-module of finite rank with
\mathbb{Z}
-dual
Y
, and the elements
\alpha_{i}
of
X
and
\alpha_{i}^{\vee}
of
Y
are such that
\langle\alpha_{j}^{\vee},\alpha_{i}\rangle=a_{ij}
.
We will always work with simply connected root datum, i.e.,
Y=\mathbb{Z}[\alpha_{i}^{\vee}]_{i\in I}
and
X=\mathbb{Z}[\omega_{i}]_{i\in I}
, where
\omega_{i}
denotes the fundamental weight. We define the set of dominant weights
X^{+}=\{\lambda\in X\,|\,\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\lambda\rangle\geq 0\text{ for all $i\in I$}\}
. Note that a simply connected Kac–Moody root datum is uniquely determined by its generalized Cartan matrix.

Let
W
be the Weyl group associated to
\mathcal{D}
generated by the simple reflection
s_{i}
for
i\in I
. We denote the length function by
\ell(\cdot)
. The group
W
acts naturally on both
X
and
Y
. Let
\Delta^{re}=\{w(\pm\alpha_{i})\,|\,i\in I,w\in W\}\subset X
be the set of real roots. Then
\Delta^{re}=\Delta^{re}_{+}\sqcup\Delta^{re}_{-}
is a disjoint union of the positive and negative roots.

The
minimal Kac–Moody group

G
associated to the Kac–Moody root datum
\mathcal{D}
over
\mathbb{C}
is the group generated by the torus
T=Y\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and the root subgroup
U_{\alpha}\simeq\mathbb{C}
for each real root
\alpha
, subject to the Tits relations
[
Tit87
]
. Let
U^{+}\subset G
(resp.
U^{-}\subset G
) be the subgroup generated by
U_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\Delta^{re}_{+}
(resp.
\alpha\in\Delta^{re}_{-}
). Let
B^{\pm}\subset G
be the subgroup generated by
T
and
U^{\pm}
, respectively.

For each
i\in I
, we fix isomorphisms
x_{i}:\mathbb{C}\to U_{\alpha_{i}}
,
y_{i}:\mathbb{C}\to U_{-\alpha_{i}}
such that the maps

\begin{pmatrix}1&a\\
0&1\\
\end{pmatrix}\mapsto x_{i}(a),\quad\begin{pmatrix}b&0\\
0&b^{-1}\\
\end{pmatrix}\mapsto\alpha_{i}^{\vee}(b),\quad\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
c&1\\
\end{pmatrix}\mapsto y_{i}(c),

define a homomorphism
SL_{2}\to G
. The data
(T,B^{+},B^{-},x_{i},y_{i};i\in I)
is called a
pinning
for
G
.

For each
i\in I
, define
\dot{s}_{i}=x_{i}(1)y_{i}(-1)x_{i}(1)\in G
. For any
w\in W
with reduced expression
w=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{n}}
, we define
\dot{w}=\dot{s}_{i_{1}}\cdots\dot{s}_{i_{n}}
. It is known that
\dot{w}
is independent of the choice of reduced expressions.

2.2.
Combinatorics on the Weyl group

An
expression
is a finite sequence
\textnormal{{w}}=(i_{1},\cdots,i_{n})
in
I
. Then we write
w=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{n}}\in W
, and say that
w
is an expression for
w
. By abuse of notations, we also write the expression as
\textnormal{{w}}=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{n}}
. We define the length
\ell(\textnormal{{w}})=n
. The expression
w
is said to be
reduced
if the length
n
is minimal among all expressions for
w
, in which case we we have
\ell(w)=\ell(\textnormal{{w}})
. We further write
w_{(k)}=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{k}}
.

A
subexpression
of
w
is a sequence
\textnormal{{w}}^{\prime}=(i^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,i^{\prime}_{n})
such that
i^{\prime}_{k}\in\{i_{k},e\}
for all
k
.
We further write
w^{\prime}=s^{\prime}_{i_{1}}\cdots s^{\prime}_{i_{n}}\in W
, where
s^{\prime}_{i_{k}}=s_{i_{k}}
if
i^{\prime}_{k}=i_{k}
, and
s^{\prime}_{i_{k}}=e
if
i^{\prime}_{k}=e
. We say that
\textnormal{{w}}^{\prime}
is a subexpression for
w^{\prime}
in
w
.

For any
w^{\prime}\in W
, recall that
w^{\prime}\leq w
with respect to the Bruhat order of
W
if and only if there exist a subexpression for
w^{\prime}
in any reduced expression
w
for
w
. The right weak Bruhat order
\leq_{R}
on
W
is the partial order generated by the covering relations
w\lessdot_{R}ws_{i}
for
w<ws_{i}
and
i\in I
. We similarly define the left weak Bruhat order
\lessdot_{L}
.

Let
\textnormal{Br}^{+}=\textnormal{Br}^{+}_{W}
be the associated (positive) braid monoid of
W
. It is the monoid generated by
s_{i}
for
i\in I
subject to the braid relations in
W
. Expressions for
\beta\in\textnormal{Br}^{+}
are defined similar to expressions of elements in
W
. We can view
W
as a subset of
\textnormal{Br}^{+}
using the reduced expressions.

The Demazure product on the set
W
is defined by

•

x*y=xy
if
x,y\in W
such that
\ell(xy)=\ell(x)+\ell(y)
;

•

s_{i}*w=w
if
i\in I
,
w\in W
such that
s_{i}w<w
.

Then
(W,*)
is a monoid. We also define
m_{*}(\textnormal{{w}})=s_{i_{1}}*\cdots*s_{i_{n}}
for any expression
w
. It is easy to check that
m_{*}
descends to a monoid homomorphism
m_{*}:\textnormal{Br}^{+}\to W
such that
m_{*}(s_{i})=s_{i}
for all
i\in I
.

Let
\textnormal{{w}}=(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})
be an expression and
v\in W
be such that
v\leq m_{*}(\textnormal{{w}})
. Let
\textnormal{{v}}=(i^{\prime}_{1},\dots,i^{\prime}_{n})
be a subexpression of
v
in
w
. The subexpression is called
distinguished
if
v_{(k)}\leq v_{(k-1)}s_{i_{k}}
for all
k
. The subexpression
v
is called
left-most

[
BH24b
]
*§3.2 if it is defined as follows:

•

If
s_{i_{1}}v<v
, then
i^{\prime}_{1}=i_{1}
and we let
(i^{\prime}_{2},\cdots,i^{\prime}_{n})
to be the left-most subexpression for
s_{i_{1}}v
in
(i_{2},\cdots,i_{n})
.

•

If
s_{i_{1}}v>v
, then
i^{\prime}_{1}=e
and we let
(i^{\prime}_{2},\cdots,i^{\prime}_{n})
to be the left-most subexpression for
v
in
(i_{2},\cdots,i_{n})
.

We can similarly define the
right-most
subexpression by starting from the right side of
w
. Note that after removing the
e
’s, both the left-most and right-most subexpressions are reduced expressions for
v
. The right-most subexpression is always distinguished.

Let
\overline{I}=\{\overline{i}\,|\,i\in I\}
. A
signed expression
is a finite sequence
(i_{1},\cdots,i_{n})
with
i_{k}\in I\sqcup\overline{I}
.

2.3.
Flag varieties

We denote by
\mathcal{B}=G/B^{+}
the (thin) full flag variety of
G
. For
v,w\in W
, let
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{w}=B^{+}\dot{w}B^{+}/B^{+}
be the
Schubert cell
,
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{v}=B^{-}\dot{v}B^{+}/B^{+}
be the
opposite Schubert cell
, and
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}=\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{w}\cap\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{v}
be the
open Richardson variety
. We have the decompositions

\mathcal{B}=\sqcup_{w\in W}\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{w}=\sqcup_{v\in W}\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{v}=\sqcup_{v,w\in W}\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}.

By
[
Kum17
]
, the intersection
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}
is non-empty if and only if
v\leq w
, in which case
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}
is irreducible and smooth with dimension
\ell(w)-\ell(v)
.

We will also work with the
(opposite) flag variety

G/B^{-}
, the
decorated flag variety

G/U^{+}
, the
(opposite) decorated flag variety

G/U^{-}
. There are natural projection maps
\pi:G/U^{\pm}\to G/B^{\pm}
. We will use lower subscripts
B_{?}
or
U_{?}
to denote flags in
G/B^{+}
or
G/U^{+}
, and use upper subscripts
B^{?}
or
U^{?}
to denote flags in
G/B^{-}
or
G/U^{-}
, respectively.

For a pair of flags
(B_{0}=xB^{+},B_{1}=yB^{+})
, we define the
distance

d(B_{0},B_{1})=w\in W
if
x^{-1}y\in B^{+}\dot{w}B^{+}
, also written as
B_{0}\xrightarrow{w}B_{1}
. Similarly, for the pair
(B^{0}=xB^{-},B^{1}=yB^{-})
, we define the
distance

d(B^{0},B^{1})=w\in W
if
x^{-1}y\in B^{-}\dot{w}^{-1}B^{-}
, also written as
B^{0}\xrightarrow{w}B^{1}
. For the pair
(B^{0}=xB^{-},B_{0}=yB^{+})
, we define the
codistance

d(B^{0},B_{0})=w\in W
if
x^{-1}y\in B^{-}\dot{w}B^{+}
. We say that
(B^{0},B_{0})
are opposite if
d(B^{0},B_{0})=1
. These definitions extends to decorated flags via the projection map. For
U_{0}=xU^{+}
and
U_{1}=yU^{+}
, we say that
(U_{0},U_{1})
are
compatible
if
x^{-1}y\in U^{+}\dot{w}U^{+}
for some
w\in W
. For
U^{0}=xU^{-}
and
U^{1}=yU^{-}
, we say that
(U^{0},U^{1})
are
compatible
if
x^{-1}y\in U^{-}\dot{w}^{-1}U^{-}
for some

. These definitions follow
[
SW21
]
*§2.

2.4.
Generalized minors

Let
\lambda\in X^{+}
and
L(\lambda)
be the integrable highest weight
G
-module with highest weight
\lambda
. Fix a highest weight vector
\eta_{\lambda}
. The extremal weight spaces
L(\lambda)_{w\lambda}
are all one-dimensional, spanned by
\eta_{w\lambda}=\dot{w}\cdot\eta_{\lambda}
.

For any
\xi\in L(\lambda)
and
w\in W
, let
\langle\xi,\dot{w}\cdot\eta_{\lambda}\rangle
be the coefficient of
\dot{w}\cdot\eta_{\lambda}
in
\xi
. We define the minor
\Delta_{\lambda}:G\to\mathbb{C}
by
\Delta_{\lambda}(g)=\langle g\eta_{\lambda},\eta_{\lambda}\rangle
. For any
w,w^{\prime}\in W
, we define the generalized minor
\Delta^{w\lambda}_{w^{\prime}\lambda}(g)=\langle g\dot{w}^{\prime}\cdot\eta_{\lambda},\dot{w}\cdot\eta_{\lambda}\rangle
as a function on
G
.

For
x\in U^{+}
,
y\in U^{-}
, and
t\in T
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{\lambda}(gx)=\Delta_{\lambda}(yg)=\Delta_{\lambda}(x),\quad\Delta_{\lambda}(gt)=\Delta_{\lambda}(tg)=\langle t,\lambda\rangle\Delta_{\lambda}(x).

We also have
\Delta^{w\lambda}_{w^{\prime}\lambda}(g)=\Delta_{\lambda}(\dot{w}^{-1}g\dot{w}^{\prime})
. Note that

(2.1)

\frac{\Delta^{w\lambda}_{\lambda}(gb)}{\Delta^{w^{\prime}\lambda}_{\lambda}(gb)}=\frac{\Delta^{w\lambda}_{\lambda}(g)}{\Delta^{w^{\prime}\lambda}_{\lambda}(g)},\quad\text{ for any $b\in B^{+}$}.

Therefore
\Delta^{w\lambda}_{\lambda}/\Delta^{w^{\prime}\lambda}_{\lambda}
is well-defined on the flag variety
\mathcal{B}
.

For decorated flags
U^{0}=xU^{-}
and
U_{0}=yU^{+}
, we define
\Delta_{\lambda}(U^{0},U_{0})=\Delta_{\lambda}(x^{-1}y)
. It is clear this is a well-defined function from
G/U^{-}\times G/U^{+}
to
\mathbb{C}
.

2.5.
Cluster algebras

A
(labeled) seed

s
is a quadruple
(J,J_{\textnormal{uf}},(\varepsilon_{ij})_{i,j\in J},(d_{i})_{i\in J})
consists of the following data:

•

An index set
J
with
|J|=n
, together with a subset
J_{\textnormal{uf}}\subset J
. The elements in
J
are called vertices. The vertices in
J_{\textnormal{uf}}
are called
mutable
or
unfrozen
. The vertices in
J-J_{\textnormal{uf}}
are called
frozen
.

•

A skew-symmetrizable matrix
(\varepsilon_{ij})_{i,j\in J}
over
\mathbb{Q}
with
\varepsilon_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}
if at least one of
i,j
is in
J_{\textnormal{uf}}
, called the
exchange matrix
.

•

Integers
d_{i}
for
i\in J
with
\gcd(d_{i})=1
such that
\varepsilon_{ij}d_{j}=-\varepsilon_{ji}d_{i}
, called the
multipliers
.

Let
[a]_{+}=\max(a,0)
for
a\in\mathbb{Q}
. For any
k\in J_{\textnormal{uf}}
, the
mutation at
k
gives a new seed
\textnormal{{s}}^{\prime}=\mu_{k}(\textnormal{{s}})
with the same
J
,
J_{\textnormal{uf}}
and
(d_{i})_{i\in J}
, and a new exchange matrix
(\varepsilon^{\prime}_{ij})_{i,j\in J}
given by

\varepsilon^{\prime}_{ij}=\begin{cases}-\varepsilon_{ij},&\text{if }k\in\{i,j\};\\
\varepsilon_{ij}+[\varepsilon_{ik}]_{+}\varepsilon_{kj}+\varepsilon_{ik}[-\varepsilon_{kj}]_{+},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

We can repeat this process mutating at arbitrary
k\in J_{\textnormal{uf}}
. A seed
\textnormal{{s}}^{\prime}
is said to be
mutation equivalent
to
s
, denoted by
\textnormal{{s}}^{\prime}\sim\textnormal{{s}}
, if it can be obtained from
s
by a sequence of mutations.

For any seed
\textnormal{{s}}_{0}
, the (abstract) cluster algebra and upper cluster algebra with initial seed
\textnormal{{s}}_{0}
are defined as follows. Fix an ambient field
K=\mathbb{C}(A_{1},\dots,A_{n})
the field of rational functions over
\mathbb{C}
in
n
variables, generated by the formal variables
A_{i}
for
i\in J
. The variables
\{A_{i,\textnormal{{s}}_{0}}=A_{i}\}
are called the
cluster variables
in the seed
\textnormal{{s}}_{0}
. The variables
\{A_{i}\,|\,i\in J-J_{\textnormal{uf}}\}
are called
frozen variables
.

Given the cluster variables
\{A_{i,\textnormal{{s}}}\}_{i\in J}
in a seed
\textnormal{{s}}\sim\textnormal{{s}}_{0}
, the cluster variables
\{A_{i,\textnormal{{s}}^{\prime}}\}_{i\in J}
in
\textnormal{{s}}^{\prime}=\mu_{k}(\textnormal{{s}})
are elements of
K
defined by

\begin{cases}A_{i,\textnormal{{s}}^{\prime}}=A_{i,\textnormal{{s}}},&\text{for $i\neq k$};\\
A_{k,\textnormal{{s}}^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{A_{k,\textnormal{{s}}}}\left(\prod_{i\in J}A_{i,\textnormal{{s}}}^{[\varepsilon_{ki}]_{+}}+\prod_{i\in J}A_{i,\textnormal{{s}}}^{[-\varepsilon_{ki}]_{+}}\right),&\text{for $i=k$.}\end{cases}

Note that for
i\in J-J_{\textnormal{uf}}
,
A_{i,\textnormal{{s}}}=A_{i,\textnormal{{s}}_{0}}=A_{i}
for any

Remark 2.1
.

We will often omit the seed

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{e,w}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{e,rs_{i}}}

Let

Corollary 3.4

3.3.
The coordinate ring
\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}]

In this subsection, we establish various properties on the coordinate ring
\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}]
for

.

Proposition 3.6
.

We fix a reduced expression
\textnormal{{v}}=(i_{1},\dots,i_{m})
of
v
. We define
v_{(l)}=v_{i_{1}}\dots v_{i_{l}}
. Then we have

\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}]\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{e,w}][T^{\pm 1}_{v_{(l-1)},v_{(l)}};1\leq l\leq m]/(T_{v_{(l-1)},v_{(l)}}=1;1\leq l\leq m).

Proof.

Recall the atlas isomorphism

\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{e,w}\cap\dot{v}_{(1)}U^{-}B^{+}/B^{+}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{e,v_{(1)}}\times\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v_{(1)},w}.

The closed subvariety defined by
T_{e,v_{(1)}}=1
is isomorphisc to

\{\ast\}\times\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v_{(1)},w}\cong\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v_{(1)},w}.

Since
T_{e,v_{(1)}}
is regular on
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{e,w}
, we have

\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{e,w}\cap(\dot{v}_{(1)}U^{-}B^{+}/B^{+})]=\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{e,w}][T_{e,v_{(1)}}^{-1}].

Therefore, we have

\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{e,w}][T_{e,v_{(1)}}^{\pm 1}]/(T_{e,v_{(1)}}=1)=\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{e,w}]/(T_{e,v_{(1)}}=1)\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v_{(1)},w}].

By Proposition
3.1
, we can then consider the regular function
T_{v_{(1)},v_{(2)}}:\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v_{(1)},w}\rightarrow\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v_{(1)},v_{(2)}}
. Note that
v_{(l-1)}\leq_{R}v_{(l)}
. By the commutative diagram in Lemma
3.5
, we have

\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{e,w}][T_{e,v_{(1)}}^{\pm 1},T_{v_{(1)},v_{(2)}}^{\pm 1}]/(T_{e,v_{(1)}}=T_{v_{(1)},v_{(2)}}=1)\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v_{(1)},w}]/(T_{v_{(1)},v_{(2)}}=1)\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v_{(2)},w}].

The proposition is proved by repeating the process.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}]
is a unique factorization domain for any
v\leq w
.

Proof.

We know
\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{e,w}]
is a unique factorization domain, since it is the non-vanishing locus of a single equation on the affine space
B^{+}\dot{w}B^{+}/B^{+}\cong\mathbb{A}^{\ell(w)}
;
see also
[
SW21
]
*Theorem 2.30.
Let
v\leq vs_{i}\leq w
for some
i\in I
. Assume
\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}]
is a unique factorization domain, we show
\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{vs_{i},w}]
is also a unique factorization domain. The theorem would follow immediately.

Let
r=vs_{i}
. By the atlas isomorphism (
3.2
), we know

\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{vs_{i},w}][x^{\pm 1}]\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{vs_{i},w}]\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,vs_{i}}]\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}\cap(\dot{r}U^{-}B^{+}/B^{+})].

Since
\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}]
is a unique factorization domain, the localization

\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}\cap(\dot{r}U^{-}B^{+}/B^{+})]=\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}][T^{-1}_{v,r}]
is still a unique factorization domain.
Hence
\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{vs_{i},w}]
is also a unique factorization domain.
∎

]

4.1.
Rational quasi-cluster maps

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\textnormal{{s}})
and
(\mathcal{A}^{\prime},\textnormal{{s}}^{\prime})
be cluster algebras with initial seeds
s
and
\textnormal{{s}}^{\prime}
. We say an embedding of fields of fractions
\sigma:K\rightarrow K^{\prime}
is rational quasi-cluster, denoted by

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{A},\textnormal{{s}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{A}^{\prime},\textnormal{{s}}^{\prime}),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\tilde{J}{,}S^{\prime})}

if we have

•

a set
\tilde{J}\subset J
containing
J_{\textnormal{uf}}
;

•

a embedding of sets
\iota:\tilde{J}\rightarrow J^{\prime}
mapping
J_{\textnormal{uf}}
to
J^{\prime}_{\textnormal{uf}}
;

•

a subset
S^{\prime}\subset J^{\prime}-\iota(\tilde{J})
;

satisfying the following properties:

(1)

for any
i\in\tilde{J}
,
\sigma(A_{i})
and
A_{\iota(i)}^{\prime}
differ by a Laurent monomial in
A^{\prime}_{j}
,
j\in S^{\prime}
;

(2)

for any
i\in J_{\textnormal{uf}}
,
\sigma(X_{i})=X^{\prime}_{\iota(i)}
. Here the exchange ratios are defined as

(4.1)

X_{i}=\prod_{j\in J}A_{j}^{\varepsilon_{ij}}\quad\text{and}\quad X^{\prime}_{\iota(i)}=\prod_{j\in J^{\prime}}(A^{\prime}_{j})^{\varepsilon_{\iota(i)j}}.

(3)

for any
i\in J-\tilde{J}
,
\sigma(A_{i})
is contained in the multiplicative group generated by
A^{\prime}_{i}
,
i\in S^{\prime}
.

Remark 4.2
.

The precise subset
S^{\prime}
is usually not important. We shall often omit it after establishing some basic properties in this subsection.

Remark 4.3
.

One can consider rational quasi-cluster homomorphisms as a generalization of the quasi-cluster homomorphsims introduced by Fraser
[
Fra16
]
.

The following lemma is immediate from the definition.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\textnormal{{s}})
,
(\mathcal{A}^{\prime},\textnormal{{s}}^{\prime})
, and
(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime},\textnormal{{s}}^{\prime\prime})
be three cluster algebras. Assume that

•

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{A},\textnormal{{s}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{A}^{\prime},\textnormal{{s}}^{\prime});}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\tilde{J}{,}S^{\prime})}

•

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{A}^{\prime},\textnormal{{s}}^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime},\textnormal{{s}}^{\prime\prime});}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\tilde{J^{\prime}}{,}S^{\prime\prime})}

•

\iota(\tilde{J})\subset\widetilde{J^{\prime}}
.

Then we have

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{A},\textnormal{{s}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime},\textnormal{{s}}^{\prime\prime}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\tilde{J}{,}S^{\prime\prime}\cup\iota^{\prime}(S^{\prime}\cap\tilde{J}^{\prime}))}

The assumption on
X_{i}
implicitly impose a relation on the exchange matrices.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{A},\textnormal{{s}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{A}^{\prime},\textnormal{{s}}^{\prime}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\tilde{J}{,}S^{\prime})}

•

If
i\in J_{\textnormal{uf}}
and
j\in\tilde{J}
, then
\varepsilon_{ij}=\varepsilon^{\prime}_{\iota(i)\iota(j)}
.

•

If
i\in J_{\textnormal{uf}}
and
j\in J^{\prime}-(S^{\prime}\cup\iota(\tilde{J}))
, then
\varepsilon^{\prime}_{\iota(i)j}=0
.

Proof.

Recall
X_{i}=\prod_{j\in J}A_{j}^{\varepsilon_{ij}}
and
X^{\prime}_{\iota(i)}=\prod_{j\in J^{\prime}}(A^{\prime}_{j})^{\varepsilon^{\prime}_{\iota(i)j}}
. Then we compute

\displaystyle\sigma(X_{i})=\prod_{j\in J}\sigma(A_{j})^{\varepsilon_{ij}}=\prod_{j\in\tilde{J}}{A^{\prime}}^{\varepsilon_{ij}}_{\iota(j)}\prod_{k\in S^{\prime}}{A^{\prime}}_{k}^{c_{k}},\text{ for some }c_{k}\in\mathbb{Z};

\displaystyle X^{\prime}_{\iota(i)}=\prod_{j\in J^{\prime}}{A^{\prime}_{j}}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}_{\iota(i)j}}=\prod_{j\in\iota(\tilde{J})}{A^{\prime}_{j}}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}_{\iota(i)j}}\prod_{j\in S^{\prime}}{A^{\prime}_{j}}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}_{\iota(i)j}}\prod_{j\in J^{\prime}-(S^{\prime}\cup\iota(\tilde{J}))}{A^{\prime}_{j}}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}_{\iota(i)j}}.

Since the cluster variables in
\textnormal{{s}}^{\prime}
are algebraically independent, the identity
\sigma(X_{i})=X^{\prime}_{\iota(i)}
implies the desired identities on the exponents.
∎

One key property of rational quasi-cluster maps is that it is preserved under mutations.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{A},\textnormal{{s}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{A}^{\prime},\textnormal{{s}}^{\prime}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\tilde{J}{,}S^{\prime})}
Then for any
k\in J_{\textnormal{uf}}
, we have

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{A},\mu_{k}(\textnormal{{s}}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{A}^{\prime},\mu_{\iota(k)}(\textnormal{{s}}^{\prime})).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\tilde{J}{,}S^{\prime})}

Proof.

Here the embedding of fields
K\rightarrow K^{\prime}
and the embedding
\tilde{J}\rightarrow J^{\prime}
are the obvious ones. We check the three conditions in Definition
4.1
. We write
f\sim g
if
f,g\in K^{\prime}
differ by a Laurent monomial in
A^{\prime}_{j}
,
j\in S^{\prime}
. Note that if
a\sim b
and
f\sim g
, then we have
af\sim bf
.

We check Condition
(1)
.
By definition,
\mu_{k}(A_{i})=A_{i}
for
i\neq k
and

\mu_{k}(A_{k})=\prod_{j\in J}A_{j}^{[-\varepsilon_{kj}]_{+}}\frac{1+X_{k}}{A_{k}}.

So we have

\sigma(\mu_{k}(A_{i}))=\sigma(A_{i})\sim A_{\iota(i)}=\mu_{\iota(k)}(A^{\prime}_{\iota(i)})
for
i\neq k
. We then compute

\displaystyle\sigma(\mu_{k}(A_{k}))

\displaystyle=\prod_{j\in J}\sigma(A_{j})^{[-\varepsilon_{kj}]_{+}}\frac{1+\sigma(X_{k})}{\sigma(A_{k})}

\displaystyle\sim\prod_{j\in\tilde{J}}{A^{\prime}_{\iota(j)}}^{[-\varepsilon_{kj}]_{+}}\frac{1+X^{\prime}_{\iota(k)}}{A^{\prime}_{\iota(k)}}=\prod_{j\in\tilde{J}}{A^{\prime}_{\iota(j)}}^{[-\varepsilon^{\prime}_{\iota(k)\iota(j)}]_{+}}\frac{1+X^{\prime}_{\iota(k)}}{A^{\prime}_{\iota(k)}}.

The last identity is by Lemma
4.5
. On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{\iota(k)}(A^{\prime}_{\iota(k)})=\prod_{j\in J^{\prime}}{A^{\prime}_{j}}^{[-\varepsilon_{\iota(k)j}]_{+}}\frac{1+X^{\prime}_{\iota(k)}}{A_{\iota(k)}^{\prime}}.

It follows from Lemma
4.5
that

\sigma(\mu_{k}(A_{k}))\sim\mu_{\iota(k)}(A^{\prime}_{\iota(k)}).

We have verified the first condition.

We verify Condition (2) now. Recall that
X_{k,\textnormal{{s}}}=X_{k,\mu_{k}(\textnormal{{s}})}^{-1}
and
X_{\iota(k),\textnormal{{s}}^{\prime}}=X_{\iota(k),\mu_{\iota(k)}(\textnormal{{s}}^{\prime})}^{-1}
by definition. It follows immediately that

\sigma(X_{k,\mu_{k}(\textnormal{{s}})})=X^{\prime}_{\iota(k),\mu_{\iota(k)}(\textnormal{{s}}^{\prime})}.

For (mutable)
i\neq k
, we have

\displaystyle X_{i,\mu_{k}(\textnormal{{s}})}

\displaystyle=X_{i,\textnormal{{s}}}A_{k}^{-\varepsilon_{ik}}\mu_{k}(A_{k})^{-\varepsilon_{ik}}\prod_{j\neq k}A_{j}^{[\varepsilon_{ik}]_{+}\varepsilon_{kj}+\varepsilon_{ik}[-\varepsilon_{kj}]_{+}}

\displaystyle=X_{i,\textnormal{{s}}}(1+X_{k,\textnormal{{s}}})^{-\varepsilon_{ik}}\prod_{j\neq k}A_{j}^{[\varepsilon_{ik}]_{+}\varepsilon_{kj}}

\displaystyle=X_{i,\textnormal{{s}}}\left(1+X_{k,\textnormal{{s}}}^{-\textnormal{sgn}(\varepsilon_{ik})}\right)^{-\varepsilon_{ik}},\text{ where $\textnormal{sgn}(\varepsilon_{ik})\in\{-1,0,+1\}$}.

Applying
\sigma
and using Lemma
4.5
, this shows that
\sigma(X_{i,\mu_{k}(\textnormal{{s}})})=X^{\prime}_{\iota(i),\mu_{\iota(k)}(\textnormal{{s}}^{\prime})}
for all
i\in J_{\textnormal{uf}}
.

Finally, Condition (3) is trivially preserved under mutations at
k\in J_{\textnormal{uf}}
and
\iota(k)\in\iota(J_{\textnormal{uf}})
.
∎

Upon mild modifications, we can extend (

(q_{ij})_{i,j\in J^{\prime}}
, and the exchange ratio

, where

is potentially frozen. Here the definition of both

5.
Upper cluster structures on Richardson varieties

In this section, we show that the open Richardson variety
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}
has an upper cluster structure.

Our seed is constructed by consecutive freezing from the seed
\textnormal{{s}}(\textnormal{{w}})
, and deleting a collection of isolated frozen vertices at the end. Geometrically, this corresponds to the realization of
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}
as a locally subvariety of
\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{e,w}

6.
Twisted products of flag varieties

In this section we extend the upper cluster structure on open Richardson varieties in flag varieties to open Richardson varieties in twisted products of flag varieties.

6.1.
The twisted product

The
twisted product
of flag varieties is defined as

\mathcal{Z}_{n}=G\times^{B^{+}}G\times^{B^{+}}\cdots\times^{B^{+}}G/B^{+},

with
n
factors. We will sometimes omit the subscript
n
if the context is clear.

The isomorphism
G^{n}\xrightarrow{\sim}G^{n}
,
(g_{1},g_{2},\cdots,g_{n})\mapsto(g_{1},g_{1}g_{2},\cdots,g_{1}g_{2}\cdots g_{n})
, induces an isomorphism
\mathcal{Z}_{n}\to\mathcal{B}^{n}
,

(g_{1},g_{2},\cdots,g_{n})\mapsto(g_{1}B^{+},g_{1}g_{2}B^{+},\cdots,g_{1}g_{2}\cdots g_{n}B^{+}).

In particular, we have the convolution product
m:\mathcal{Z}_{n}\to\mathcal{B}
,

(g_{1},g_{2},\cdots,g_{n})\mapsto g_{1}g_{2}\cdots g_{n}B^{+}.

Let
\textnormal{{w}}=(i_{1},\cdots,i_{n})
be an expression in
W
. The Schubert cell in
\mathcal{Z}
corresponding to
w
is defined as

\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}_{\textnormal{{w}}}=B^{+}\dot{s}_{i_{1}}B^{+}\times^{B^{+}}B^{+}\dot{s}_{i_{2}}B^{+}\times^{B^{+}}\cdots\times^{B^{+}}B^{+}\dot{s}_{i_{n}}B^{+}/B^{+}.

The opposite Schubert cell in
\mathcal{Z}
corresponding to
v\in W
is defined as

\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}^{v}=\{x\in\mathcal{Z}_{n}\,|\,m(x)\in\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{v}\}.

The open Richardson variety in
\mathcal{Z}
corresponding to
v
and
w
is defined as
\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}_{v,\textnormal{{w}}}=\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}_{\textnormal{{w}}}\cap\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}^{v}
.

By
[
BH24b
]
, the variety
\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}_{v,\textnormal{{w}}}
is non-empty if and only if
v\leq m_{*}(\textnormal{{w}})
, in which case it is irreducible of dimension
\ell(\textnormal{{w}})-\ell(v)
.

Remark 6.1
.

The definition of Schubert cells in twisted products is more general in
[
BH24b
]
, in terms of a sequence
\underline{w}=(w_{1},\cdots,w_{n})
of elements in
W
. By replacing each
w_{i}
with its reduced expression,
\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}_{v,\underline{w}}\subset\mathcal{Z}_{n}
is isomorphic to
\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}_{v,\textnormal{{w}}}\subset\mathcal{Z}_{m}
for some
m\geq n
.

Since we only consider the locally closed subvariety
\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}_{v,\textnormal{{w}}}
instead of its closure in
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
, the two definitions give isomorphic varieties.

Remark 6.2
.

Special cases of
\mathring{\mathcal{Z}}_{v,\textnormal{{w}}}
include reduced double Bruhat cells, Bott-Samelson varieties, and braid varieties by
[
BH24b
]
.

\displaystyle=-\langle\alpha_{i_{1}}^{\vee},s_{\infty_{1}}s_{i_{2}}s_{\infty_{2}}\cdots s_{i_{k+1}}s_{\infty_{k+1}}\omega_{\infty_{k+1}}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\alpha_{i_{1}}^{\vee}+2\alpha_{\infty_{1}}^{\vee},s_{i_{2}}s_{\infty_{2}}\cdots s_{i_{k+1}}s_{\infty_{k+1}}\omega_{\infty_{k+1}}\rangle

\displaystyle=2q_{k+1,1}-\langle\alpha_{i_{1}}^{\vee},s_{i_{2}}s_{\infty_{2}}\cdots s_{i_{k+1}}s_{\infty_{k+1}}\omega_{\infty_{k+1}}\rangle

\displaystyle=2q_{k+1,1}-\langle\alpha_{i_{1}}^{\vee}-a_{i_{2},i_{1}}\alpha_{i_{2}}^{\vee},s_{\infty_{2}}\cdots s_{i_{k+1}}s_{\infty_{k+1}}\omega_{\infty_{k+1}}\rangle

\displaystyle=2q_{k+1,1}-a_{i_{2},i_{1}}p_{k+1,2}+p^{\prime}_{k,1},

and

\displaystyle r_{j,1}

\displaystyle=-\langle s_{i_{j}}\cdots s_{i_{2}}\alpha_{i_{1}}^{\vee},\alpha_{\infty_{j}}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle s_{i_{j}}\cdots s_{i_{3}}(\alpha_{i_{1}}^{\vee}-a_{i_{2},i_{1}}\alpha_{i_{2}}^{\vee}),\alpha_{\infty_{j}}\rangle

\displaystyle=-a_{i_{2},i_{1}}r_{j,2}+r^{\prime}_{j-1,1}.

We compute that

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq j\leq k+1}q_{k+1,j}r_{j,1}

\displaystyle=2q_{k+1,1}+\sum_{2\leq j\leq k+1}q_{k+1,j}(-a_{i_{2},i_{1}}r_{j,2}+r^{\prime}_{j-1,1})

\displaystyle=2q_{k+1,1}-a_{i_{2},i_{1}}\sum_{2\leq j\leq k+1}q_{k+1,j}r_{j,2}+\sum_{2\leq j\leq k+1}q_{k+1,j}r^{\prime}_{j-1,1}

\displaystyle=2q_{k+1,1}-a_{i_{2},i_{1}}\sum_{2\leq j\leq k+1}q_{k+1,j}r_{j,2}+\sum_{1\leq j\leq k}q^{\prime}_{k,j}r^{\prime}_{j,1}

\displaystyle=2q_{k+1,1}-a_{i_{2},i_{1}}p_{k+1,2}+p^{\prime}_{k,1}.

We finish the proof now.
∎

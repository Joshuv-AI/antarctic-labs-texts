Quantum-geometric dipole: a topological boost to flavor ferromagnetism in flat bands
Lei Chen, Sayed Ali Akbar Ghorashi, Jennifer Cano, Valentin Crépel

Abstract
Robust flavor-polarized phases are a striking hallmark of many flat-band moiré materials. In this work, we trace the origin of this spontaneous polarization to a previously overlooked quantum-geometric quantity: the quantum-geometric dipole . Analogous to how the quantum metric governs the spatial spread of wavepackets, we show that the quantum-geometric dipole sets the characteristic size of particle-hole excitations, e.g. magnons in a ferromagnet, which in turn boosts their gap and stiffness. Indeed, the larger the particle-hole separation, the weaker the mutual attraction, and the stronger the excitation energy. In topological bands, this energy enhancement admits a lower bound within the single-mode approximation, highlighting the crucial role of topology in flat-band ferromagnetism. We illustrate these effects in microscopic models, emphasizing their generality and relevance to moiré materials. Our results establish the quantum-geometric dipole as a predictive geometric indicator for ferromagnetism in flat bands, a crucial prerequisite for topological order.

Quantum geometric dipole —

The central object of our study is the magnon electric dipole,
i.e.
the distance between the particle and hole forming the spin-flip excitation.
While a similar particle–hole dipole has appeared in the context of excitonic photoresponses
von Baltz and Kraut 1981
;
Sturman and Fridkin 1992
;
Hughes and Sipe 1996
;
Sipe and Shkrebtii 2000
;
Pesin 2018
;
Cao et al. 2021
;
Paiva et al. 2024
;
Young and Rappe 2012
;
Morimoto and Nagaosa 2016
;
Fei et al. 2020
;
Kaplan et al. 2022
;
Chaudhary et al. 2022
;
Lee et al. 2023
;
Nakamura et al. 2024
;
Haber et al. 2023
, our definition clarifies two of its key properties:
it separates the quantum-geometric contribution from the non-universal spatial part; and it recasts the quantum-geometric part as a Berry-like flux in mixed spin–momentum space, which offers an efficient, gauge-invariant, and numerically-stable expression
à la
Fukui-Hatsugai
Fukui et al. 2005
;
Fukui and Hatsugai 2007
(Eq.
3
below).

To set the stage, consider a generic band with dispersion
\varepsilon_{\bm{k},\sigma}
and periodic Bloch states
|u_{k}^{\sigma}\rangle
annihilated by
c_{\bm{k},\sigma}
, with
\bm{k}
in the Brillouin zone (BZ) and
\sigma\in\{\uparrow,\downarrow\}
a flavor index.
While our treatment extends to any band filling, we focus on the half-filled case and study magnons created over the ferromagnetic state
|\hskip-3.0pt\Uparrow\rangle=\prod_{\bm{k}\in{\rm BZ}}c_{\bm{k},\uparrow}^{\dagger}|\varnothing\rangle
with
|\varnothing\rangle
the vacuum.
For
|\hskip-4.0pt\Uparrow\rangle
to be an eigenstate, we require (at least) U(1)-flavor symmetry,
i.e.
that the Hamiltonian be diagonal in
\sigma
.
A generic band-projected flavor-flipping excitation with momentum
\bm{Q}
can be written as

\begin{split}|\psi_{\bm{Q}}\rangle&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{\bm{Q}}}}\sum_{\bm{k}\in{\rm BZ}}\psi_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}}c_{\bm{k}+\bm{Q}/2,\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{\bm{k}-\bm{Q}/2,\uparrow}|\!\Uparrow\rangle,\\
\psi_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}}&=z_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}}s_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}},\quad s_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}}=\langle u_{\bm{k}+\bm{Q}/2}^{\downarrow}|u_{\bm{k}-\bm{Q}/2}^{\uparrow}\rangle,\end{split}

(1)

with
N_{\bm{Q}}=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\text{BZ}}|\psi_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}}|^{2}
a normalization factor, and where we have decomposed the wavefunction coefficients into a gauge-invariant component
z_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}}
capturing the spatial structure of the magnon, and a spin-flipping form factor
s_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}}
originating from band projection.

With these notations, the magnon electric dipole
{\bm{d}}
, defined as the average distance between the spin-
\downarrow
particle and spin-
\uparrow
hole, takes the form (see Ref.
Paiva et al. 2024
or supplemental material (SM) Sec.
A
)

\displaystyle{\bm{d}}=\left\langle\mathcal{S}_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}}^{\rm spat}+\mathcal{S}_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}}^{\rm geom}\right\rangle_{|\psi_{\bm{Q}}|^{2}},\quad\mathcal{S}_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}}^{\rm spat}=i\nabla_{\bm{k}}\log z_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}}^{\rm geom}=\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}-\bm{Q}/2}^{\uparrow}-\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}+\bm{Q}/2}^{\downarrow}+i\nabla_{\bm{k}}\log s_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}},

(2)

with
\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}=-i\langle u_{\bm{k},\sigma}|\nabla_{\bm{k}}|u_{\bm{k},\sigma}\rangle
the spin-resolved Berry connection, and where
\langle O_{\bm{k}}\rangle_{\mu}=\sum_{\bm{k}}\mu_{\bm{k}}O_{\bm{k}}/\sum_{\bm{k}}\mu_{\bm{k}}
denotes the
\mu
-weighted average.
We have isolated two contributions to the magnon dipole. The first, dubbed the spatial dipole and denoted as
\mathcal{S}^{\rm spat}
, only depends on the
z
-coefficients and therefore originates from the structure of the dipole in real space. The second,
\mathcal{S}^{\rm geom}
, is the
quantum-geometric dipole
advertised in the introduction. It is manifestly gauge-invariant
1
1

1

Gauge invariance of the quantum-geometric dipole is straightforwardly checked by substituting
|u_{\bm{k},\sigma}\rangle\to e^{i\theta_{\bm{k},\sigma}}|u_{\bm{k},\sigma}\rangle
in Eq.
2
, and depends solely on the Bloch vectors of the underlying bands.

To emphasize the spatial/geometric characters of the two terms in Eq.
2
, consider the following two limits.
When the Bloch bundle is geometrically trivial,
|u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}\rangle=1
,
\mathcal{S}^{\rm geom}
vanishes and the
ph
dipole entirely comes from the spatial part
\mathcal{S}^{\rm spat}
.
On the other hand, the most localized magnon with center-of-mass momentum
\bm{Q}
, given by
\int{\rm d}^{2}\bm{R}\,e^{i(\bm{Q}\cdot\bm{R})}c_{\bm{R},\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{\bm{R},\uparrow}|\!\Uparrow\rangle
, corresponds to
z_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}}=1
after projection, leading to
\mathcal{S}^{\rm spat}=0
and a purely quantum-geometric dipole
\bm{d}=\langle\mathcal{S}^{\rm geom}_{\bm{k}}\rangle_{|\psi|^{2}}
.

Magnon interaction energy —

In the introduction, we argued that the interaction-induced magnon gap should scale with the amplitude of the magnon dipole,
{\bm{d}}
: a larger dipole means more separation between the spin-
\downarrow
particle and spin-
\uparrow
hole, which reduces their mutual attraction and thereby increases the overall magnon energy.
To rationalize this intuition, we compute

\Delta(\psi_{\bm{Q}})=\langle\psi_{\bm{Q}}|H_{\rm int}|\psi_{\bm{Q}}\rangle-\langle\Uparrow\hskip-5.0pt|H_{\rm int}|\hskip-5.0pt\Uparrow\rangle
,
the interaction energy of the magnon relative to the ferromagnetic state for a band-projected interaction

H_{\rm int}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{q},\bm{k},\bm{p}\\
\sigma,\tau\end{subarray}}\frac{v(q)}{2N_{\rm BZ}}\langle u_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}}^{\sigma}|u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}\rangle\langle u_{\bm{p}}^{\tau}|u_{\bm{p}+\bm{q}}^{\tau}\rangle c_{\bm{k}+\bm{q},\sigma}^{\dagger}c_{\bm{p},\tau}^{\dagger}c_{\bm{p}+\bm{q},\tau}c_{\bm{k},\sigma}
, with
N_{\rm BZ}
the number of points in the discretized BZ, and
v(q=||\bm{q}||)
a rotation-symmetric Coulomb potential.
Wick’s theorem yields (see SM Sec.
B
)

\Delta(\psi)=\left\langle\sum_{\bm{q}}\frac{v(q)|\Lambda_{\bm{k}}^{\uparrow}(\bm{q})|^{2}}{N_{\rm BZ}}\left[1-\frac{\Lambda_{\bm{k}}^{\downarrow}(\bm{q})^{*}}{\Lambda_{\bm{k}}^{\uparrow}(\bm{q})^{*}}\frac{\psi_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}}}{\psi_{\bm{k}}}\right]\right\rangle_{\!\!|\psi|^{2}}\!\!\!\!\!,

(4)

where we have left the
\bm{Q}
dependence implicit. The total energy of the magnon also includes a kinetic term
K(\psi_{\bm{Q}})=\left\langle\varepsilon_{\bm{k}+\bm{Q}/2,\downarrow}-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}-\bm{Q}/2,\uparrow}\right\rangle_{|\psi_{\bm{Q}}|^{2}}
. The stability of the state
|\hskip-3.0pt\Uparrow\rangle
against magnonic excitations requires that the Stoner criterion,
\min_{z_{\bm{Q}}}[K(\psi_{\bm{Q}})+\Delta(\psi_{\bm{Q}})]>0
, be fulfilled at all
\bm{Q}
.
For flat bands in which the bandwidth is negligible compared to the interaction scale, we can safely ignore
K(\psi)
.

The connection between the interaction gap
\Delta(\psi)
and the quantum-geometric dipole is brought to light by a small-
\bm{q}
expansion of the form factors — a standard approach for identifying geometric contributions to physical observables
Berry 1984
;
Wu and Sarma 2020
;
Crépel et al. 2021
;
Crépel et al. 2020
;
Abouelkomsan et al. 2023
;
Zeng et al. 2024b
;
Fang et al. 2024
;
Crépel and Cano 2025
.
The zeroth order term in the expansion vanishes since the bracketed terms in Eq.
4
cancel out (
\Lambda_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}(0)=1
).
The linear terms also vanish due to the rotation invariance of
v(q)
.
Hence, the leading contribution is of order
q^{2}
, which, by dimensional analysis, requires the introduction of a typical length scale.
One may deduce this length scale should be the magnon’s dipole by recalling that
\psi_{\bm{k}}\propto s_{\bm{k}}
and observing that the phase of the bracketed term in Eq.
4
is precisely the spin-momentum Berry flux sketched in Eq.
3
up to the replacement
\delta\bm{k}_{a}\to\bm{q}
.
Confirming this intuition, the complete expansion (detailed in SM Sec.
B
) yields

\Delta(\psi)=a^{-2}\left\langle U(g_{\bm{k}})\left[||\mathcal{S}_{\bm{k}}^{\rm geom}+\mathcal{S}_{\bm{k}}^{\rm spat}||^{2}\right]\right\rangle_{|\psi|^{2}},

(5)

with
a
the lattice constant. In this calculation, the decay of the form factors for large-
\bm{q}
(not captured by the small-
\bm{q}
expansion) is enforced by re-exponentiating the terms proportional to the quantum metric
(g_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma})^{ab}={\rm Re}\,\langle\partial_{k_{a}}u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}|\partial_{k_{b}}u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}\rangle-\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k},a}^{\sigma}\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k},b}^{\sigma}
.
Considering these effective momentum cutoffs as independent of spin, we obtain the momentum-dependent interaction scale

U(g_{\bm{k}})=(4N_{\rm BZ})^{-1}\sum_{\bm{q}}(qa)^{2}v(q)e^{-q_{a}g^{ab}_{\bm{k}}q_{b}}

with
g_{\bm{k}}=(g_{\bm{k}}^{\uparrow}+g_{\bm{k}}^{\downarrow})/2
the spin-averaged quantum metric.
This coefficient has an intuitive interpretation: it describes the typical repulsion between two oppositely charged Gaussian wavepackets of width
g_{\bm{k}}
,
the minimal spread allowed by the band’s quantum geometry.
To see this, let us momentarily specialize to the two-body Coulomb potential
v(\bm{r})=e^{2}/(4\pi\varepsilon|\bm{r}|)
and assume an isotropic quantum metric with
\tilde{g}={\rm Tr}g
; then

U(g)=\frac{\pi e^{2}a^{2}}{\varepsilon(8\pi\tilde{g})^{3/2}}=\frac{\pi a^{2}}{8}\int{\rm d}^{2}\bm{r}\,v(\bm{r})|\phi(\bm{r})|^{4},

(6)

with
\phi(\bm{r})=\exp(-\bm{r}^{2}/2\tilde{g})/\sqrt{2\pi\tilde{g}}
a normalized Gaussian wavepacket, and where
a^{2}
is required by dimensionality.
Eq.
5
confirms the intuitive idea that the magnon energy grows with its dipole.

Furthermore, within the single-mode approximation (SMA), the magnon gap is entirely determined by the quantum-geometric dipole. Specifically, the SMA assumes that to decrease the total dipole, the lowest energy magnons are well described by projecting local-in-space spin-flips of the form
c_{\bm{R},\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{\bm{R},\uparrow}|\!\Uparrow\rangle
,
i.e.
setting
z_{\bm{k}}=1
, so that
\psi_{\bm{k}}=s_{\bm{k}}
. This choice eliminates the spatial dipole (see discussion above Eq.
3
) and isolates the geometric contribution

\Delta^{\text{geom}}_{\bm{Q}}=a^{-2}\left\langle U(g_{\bm{k}})||\mathcal{S}_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}}^{\rm geom}||^{2}\right\rangle_{|s|^{2}},

(7)

after the small-
\bm{q}
expansion.

Topological boost to flat-band ferromagnetism —

Having derived the precise relation between the magnon energy and quantum-geometric dipole in flat bands (Eq.
5
), we now describe how topology underpins robust ferromagnetism in topological flat bands.

Let us start with the SU(2)-spin invariant limit
|u_{\bm{k},\uparrow}\rangle=|u_{\bm{k},\downarrow}\rangle
, where a gapless magnon branch with quadratic dispersion must exist
Nambu 1960
;
Goldstone 1961
;
Nielsen and Chadha 1976
;
Watanabe and Murayama 2012
.
We recover this gaplessness by noting that
\mathcal{S}_{\bm{Q}=0,\bm{k}}^{\rm geom}=0
for SU(2) invariant systems (see Eq.
2
).
Turning to Eq.
5
,
Taylor expanding
\Delta_{\bm{Q}}^{\rm geom}\simeq\rho_{s}|\bm{Q}|^{2}/2
gives a magnon stiffness
\rho_{s}=\left\langle U(g_{\bm{k}})\Omega_{\bm{k}}^{2}\right\rangle_{\rm BZ}
with
\Omega_{\bm{k}}=\nabla_{\bm{k}}\times\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}}^{\uparrow/\downarrow}
the Berry curvature of the band (see SM Sec.
C
).
When fluctuations of the quantum metric about its mean,
\bar{g}
, can be ignored, this formula reduces to

\rho_{s}\simeq U(\bar{g})\langle\Omega_{\bm{k}}^{2}\rangle\geq U(\bar{g})C^{2},

(8)

with
C=\langle\Omega_{\bm{k}}\rangle_{\rm BZ}
the Chern number, and where the inequality follows from Cauchy-Schwarz. This topologically induced lower bound on the spin stiffness qualitatively agrees with previous results
Wu and Sarma 2020
, and becomes exact for bands with uniform quantum metric
Roy 2014
.
It is derived assuming SU(2) symmetry, but is only non-trivial when the Chern number is non-vanishing.
Thus, it is not useful when opposite spins are related by time reversal symmetry, but yields a nontrivial bound in,
e.g.
valleys of moiré materials away from time-reversal invariant momenta, such as the
K
valley of twisted bilayer graphene.

We now consider a flavor-symmetry lower than SU(2), for which the magnon spectrum is gapped.
We lower bound the magnon gap by a sum of
|C_{s}|
strictly positive contributions, with
C_{s}=C_{\uparrow}-C_{\downarrow}
the spin Chern number of the underlying bands.
This again highlights the role of topology in flat-band ferromagnetism.

The lower bound arises because the spin-lowering overlap
s_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}}=\langle u_{\bm{k}+\bm{Q}/2}^{\downarrow}|u_{\bm{k}-\bm{Q}/2}^{\uparrow}\rangle

is a complex function in
\bm{k}
whose phase needs to increase by
2\pi C_{s}
as
\bm{k}
winds around the BZ.
It hence possesses at least
|C_{s}|
vortices at momenta
\{\bm{k}_{v}\}
with vorticity
\tau={\rm sign}\,C_{s}
(counted without multiplicities), and can be factorized as
s_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}}=\tilde{s}_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}}\prod_{v}(k^{\tau}-k_{v}^{\tau})
with
k^{\tau}=k_{x}+i\tau k_{y}
and
\tilde{s}
a complex function with no winding.
Near these vortices, the quantum-geometric dipole in Eq.
2
is dominated by the singular connection
i\nabla\log s_{\bm{Q}}

Paiva et al. 2024
, which diverges as
||S_{\bm{Q}}^{\rm geom}||\sim|s_{\bm{Q}}|^{-1}
when
s_{\bm{Q}}\to 0
.
Isolating these singular contributions from the BZ average in Eq.
7
, we obtain

\Delta_{\bm{Q}}^{\text{geom}}\geq\sum_{v}U(g_{\bm{k}_{v}})\simeq|C_{s}|U(\bar{g}),

(9)

where the second (approximate) equality holds under the assumption of a near-uniform quantum metric.

The approximate topological bounds derived for the magnon stiffness (Eq.
8
) and gap (Eq.
9
) show the fundamental role of topology on the stability of ferromagnetism in flat bands.
They arose from the tight connection between the quantum-geometric dipole and the magnon spectrum (Eq.
5
).
They are consistent with the familiar structure of spin excitations in topologically trivial models, such as the Hubbard model, where a spin-flip can be localized to a single point, which forces the quantum-geometric dipole to vanish and leads to a vanishing gap. This gaplessness in the flat-band limit implies that any additional term in the Hamiltonian (
e.g.
super-exchange) can dominate, favoring different spin-ordering at low temperature (
e.g.
antiferromagnetic).

Microscopic verification and relevance —

We now substantiate our theory by studying two microscopic models. We focus on systems that break SU(2) but preserve U(1) spin symmetry and compute the magnon gap at
\bm{Q}=0
, where the magnon spectrum attains its minimum.
To assess the validity of previous approximations, we compute in Figs.
2
and
3
the magnon energy of two different models using methods of decreasing rigor: the Bethe–Salpeter analysis (SM Eq.
S17
, blue); the SMA before small-
\bm{q}
expansion (Eq.
4
, orange); and the geometric contribution formula (Eq.
7
, red), with
z_{\bm{k}}=1
in the latter two.
To highlight the quantitative relationship between these energies and the quantum-geometric dipole (Fig.
1
), we also evaluate
d_{\rm min}^{2}=\langle||\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{geom}}_{\bm{k}}||^{2}\rangle_{|s|^{2}}
(shown in insets).
These quantities are presented for two representative models. First, we consider the 2D BHZ model
Bernevig et al. 2006
and show that the magnon gap is strong in the topological regime and sharply decreases to a near-zero value upon entering the trivial regime, which qualitatively corroborates the effect of topology identified in Eq.
9
.
Next, we apply our framework to twisted bilayer
\mathrm{MoTe_{2}}
, where spin-valley polarized phases have been observed
Anderson et al. 2023
;
Cai et al. 2023
. At filling
\nu=1
, our coarse Stoner criterion predicts a transition to an unpolarized phase at an interlayer displacement field that closely matches experimental observations, highlighting the predictive power of our method.

\bullet
Lattice toy model:

The noninteracting part of the 2D BHZ model reads
H_{0}=\mathrm{diag}[\mathcal{H}^{\uparrow},\mathcal{H}^{\downarrow}]
, where

\mathcal{H}^{\sigma}=\big(M-2B(2-\cos k_{x}-\cos k_{y})\big)\sigma_{z}+A\sigma\sin k_{x}\sigma_{x}+A\sin k_{y}\sigma_{y}
.
Choosing
A=B=1
, the model is symmetric around
M=4
and undergoes topological phase transitions at
M=0,4
and
8
at which the Chern number shifts as
C_{\uparrow}=0\to 1\to-1\to 0
(see SM Sec.
D
).
We then project the screened Coulomb interaction,
v(\bm{q})=\frac{U_{0}}{r_{\xi}|\bm{q}|}\tanh(r_{\xi}|\bm{q}|)
, onto the lower band and neglect the kinetic energy to isolate interaction effects.
Fig.
2
shows the magnon gap computed using all three methods at
r_{\xi}=0.1
.

Two striking behaviors emerge from this plot.
First, the average squared geometric dipole
d_{\rm min}^{2}
evolves with the same trend as the magnon gap.
This supports the physical picture developed in Fig.
1
and formalized in Eq.
7
that a greater dipole, or spatial separation between the spin-
\uparrow
and spin-
\downarrow
forming the magnon, correlates with the ferromagnetic gap.
Second, we notice that the ferromagnetic gap is finite across the topological region of the phase diagram, peaking near the
C=1\to-1
transition at
M=4
, but sharply diminishes upon entering the trivial phase.
This underscores the crucial role of topology in stabilizing ferromagnetic order by enhancing the average quantum-geometric dipole, as explained in Eq.
9
and surrounding text.

Relevance to moiré materials:

To explore the interplay between topology and interaction in realistic systems, we turn to twisted bilayer
\mathrm{MoTe_{2}}
, where experiments have identified a broad, cone-shaped region of spin-valley polarization in the phase diagram spanned by electron filling and displacement field
Anderson et al. 2023
.
A stable spin-valley polarized phase is a prerequisite for realizing exotic correlated states
Crépel and Fu 2023
, in particular for the fractional Chern insulator recently observed in this system
Cai et al. 2023
.

We focus on the case of filling
\nu=1
at a twist angle of
\theta=3.7^{\circ}
, using the continuum moiré Hamiltonian (details in SM Sec.
D
)
Wu et al. 2019
;
Devakul et al. 2021
;
Wang et al. 2024
;
Jia et al. 2024
.
An important tunable parameter is the interlayer displacement field
D
, which creates an interlayer potential difference
\Delta\varepsilon=\frac{eD}{\epsilon\epsilon_{0}}\xi_{0}
with
\xi_{0}
the interlayer spacing and
\epsilon
the dielectric constant.
The long-range Coulomb interaction is screened by dual metal gates, resulting in an effective potential of the form
v(\bm{q})=\frac{e^{2}}{2\epsilon_{0}\epsilon}\frac{\tanh(\xi|\bm{q}|)}{|\bm{q}|}
, where
\xi
is the distance from the bilayer to the gates.
We adopt representative experimental parameters:
\xi=30~\mathrm{nm}
,
\epsilon=7
, and
\xi_{0}=0.7~\mathrm{nm}
.

Fig.
3
presents the magnon gap versus interlayer displacement potential, and demonstrates a remarkable agreement among the three methods discussed above.
Similar to the BHZ model, the difference between the topological and trivial regimes is stark: the magnon interaction energy drops to less than one-third of its initial value at
\Delta\varepsilon=0
upon crossing the topological transition indicated by the vertical green dashed line.

Experimentally, a transition from a ferromagnetic to an unpolarized phase is observed as a function of increasing displacement field, which we estimate around
\Delta\varepsilon_{c}^{\rm exp}\approx 13~
meV (gray dashed line) using Ref.
Cai et al. 2023
’s data.
To evaluate this transition point theoretically, we employ a relaxed Stoner criterion by approximating the kinetic contribution to the magnon energy as
K_{\rm kin}=\max(|\varepsilon_{\bm{k},\downarrow}-\varepsilon_{\bm{k},\uparrow}|)
, shown as the black solid line in Fig.
3
, an upper bound on the kinetic energy cost
K(\psi)
defined above.
The ferromagnetic transition is expected to occur at the intersection between the kinetic energy cost and the magnon gap.
This occurs around
\Delta\varepsilon_{c}^{\rm th}=16-17~{\rm meV}
for all three methods used in Fig.
3
, which closely matches
\Delta\varepsilon_{c}^{\rm exp}
.

These two microscopic examples underscore the central role of the quantum-geometric dipole in the emergence of ferromagnetism. They support our approximate topological lower bound, which accounts for the widely observed tendency of topological bands to spontaneously polarize under interactions. Moreover, the strong agreement between our predictions and the depolarization transition point in twisted MoTe
2
highlights the quantitative accuracy and predictive power of our approach.

Real-space representation:

We consider a system whose ground state is a fully polarized ferromagnet, denoted by
|\Uparrow\rangle=\prod_{\bm{k}\in{\rm BZ}}c^{\dagger}_{\bm{k},\uparrow}|\varnothing\rangle
as defined in the main text.
Let us denote the operator annihilating a fermion of spin
\sigma
at position
\bm{r}
as
c_{\bm{r},\sigma}
, whose real-space orbital is a delta function at position
\bm{r}
, and write
|\bm{r}_{\downarrow},\bm{r}_{\uparrow}\rangle=c_{\bm{r}_{\downarrow},\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{\bm{r}_{\uparrow},\uparrow}|\Uparrow\rangle
.
Recalling that the
c_{\bm{k},\sigma}
operators used in the main text are the fermionic operator for
a specific band
corresponding to the eigenstates
e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r}}|u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}\rangle
, and that the anti-commutation relation between fermionic operators is equal to the scalar product of their respective orbitals, it follows that
\{c_{\bm{r},\sigma},c_{\bm{k},\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\}=\delta_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\langle\bm{r}|u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}\rangle e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r}}
where
\langle\bm{r}|u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}\rangle
denotes the real-space representation of the periodic function
|u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}\rangle
.
For convenience, we repeat here the generic band-projected magnon state introduced in the main text:

|\psi_{\bm{Q}}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{\bm{Q}}}}\sum_{\bm{k}\in{\rm BZ}}\psi_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}}\underbrace{c_{\bm{k}+\bm{Q}/2,\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{\bm{k}-\bm{Q}/2,\uparrow}|\Uparrow\rangle}_{|S_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}}^{-}\rangle},\quad\psi_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}}=z_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}}s_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}},\quad s_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}},=\langle u_{\bm{k}+\bm{Q}/2}^{\downarrow}|u_{\bm{k}-\bm{Q}/2}^{\uparrow}\rangle,

(S1)

with
N_{\bm{Q}}=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\text{BZ}}|\psi_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}}|^{2}
a normalization factor. This state can be expressed in real-space as

\displaystyle\langle\bm{r}_{\downarrow},\bm{r}_{\uparrow}|\psi_{\bm{Q}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{k}\in{\rm BZ}}\frac{\psi_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}}}{\sqrt{N_{\bm{Q}}}}\langle\Uparrow|c_{\bm{r}_{\uparrow},\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{\bm{r}_{\downarrow},\downarrow}c_{\bm{k}+\bm{Q}/2,\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{\bm{k}-\bm{Q}/2,\uparrow}|\Uparrow\rangle

(S2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{k}\in{\rm BZ}}\frac{\psi_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}}}{\sqrt{N_{\bm{Q}}}}e^{i(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r}+\bm{Q}\cdot\bm{R})}\langle\bm{r}_{\downarrow}|u_{\bm{k}+\frac{\bm{Q}}{2}}^{\downarrow}\rangle\langle\bm{r}_{\uparrow}|u_{\bm{k}-\frac{\bm{Q}}{2}}^{\uparrow}\rangle^{*},\quad\bm{R}=\frac{\bm{r}_{\uparrow}+\bm{r}_{\downarrow}}{2},\quad\bm{r}=\bm{r}_{\downarrow}-\bm{r}_{\uparrow},

(S3)

where we have used the anti-commutation relation discussed above, and introduced the center of mass
\bm{R}
and relative
\bm{r}
coordinates.

Average particle-hole dipole:

Reproducing the calculation from Ref.
Paiva et al. 2024
for completeness, we can now compute the expectation value of the relative distance as

\displaystyle\bm{d}

\displaystyle=\langle\psi|\bm{r}|\psi\rangle=\int{\rm d}^{2}\bm{r}_{\downarrow}{\rm d}^{2}\bm{r}_{\uparrow}\,\langle\psi|\bm{r}_{\downarrow},\bm{r}_{\uparrow}\rangle\bm{r}\langle\bm{r}_{\downarrow},\bm{r}_{\uparrow}|\psi\rangle

(S4)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}\int{\rm d}^{2}\bm{r}_{\downarrow}{\rm d}^{2}\bm{r}_{\uparrow}\,\psi_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}^{*}\psi_{\bm{k}}\langle u_{\bm{k}^{\prime}+\frac{\bm{Q}}{2},\downarrow}|\bm{r}_{\downarrow}\rangle\langle\bm{r}_{\uparrow}|u_{\bm{k}^{\prime}-\frac{\bm{Q}}{2},\uparrow}\rangle\langle\bm{r}_{\downarrow}|u_{\bm{k}+\frac{\bm{Q}}{2},\downarrow}\rangle\langle u_{\bm{k}-\frac{\bm{Q}}{2},\uparrow}|\bm{r}_{\uparrow}\rangle e^{-i(\bm{k}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{r})}\bm{r}e^{i(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r})}

(S5)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}\int{\rm d}^{2}\bm{r}_{\downarrow}{\rm d}^{2}\bm{r}_{\uparrow}\,\psi_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}^{*}\psi_{\bm{k}}\langle u_{\bm{k}^{\prime}+\frac{\bm{Q}}{2},\downarrow}|\bm{r}_{\downarrow}\rangle\langle\bm{r}_{\uparrow}|u_{\bm{k}^{\prime}-\frac{\bm{Q}}{2},\uparrow}\rangle\langle\bm{r}_{\downarrow}|u_{\bm{k}+\frac{\bm{Q}}{2},\downarrow}\rangle\langle u_{\bm{k}-\frac{\bm{Q}}{2},\uparrow}|\bm{r}_{\uparrow}\rangle e^{-i(\bm{k}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{r})}(-i\nabla_{\bm{k}})e^{i(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r})}

(S6)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}\int{\rm d}^{2}\bm{r}_{\downarrow}{\rm d}^{2}\bm{r}_{\uparrow}\,\psi_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}^{*}\langle u_{\bm{k}^{\prime}+\frac{\bm{Q}}{2},\downarrow}|\bm{r}_{\downarrow}\rangle\langle\bm{r}_{\uparrow}|u_{\bm{k}^{\prime}-\frac{\bm{Q}}{2},\uparrow}\rangle e^{i(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r}-\bm{k}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{r})}(i\nabla_{\bm{k}})\left[\psi_{\bm{k}}\langle\bm{r}_{\downarrow}|u_{\bm{k}+\frac{\bm{Q}}{2},\downarrow}\rangle\langle\bm{r}_{\uparrow}|u_{\bm{k}-\frac{\bm{Q}}{2},\uparrow}\rangle^{*}\right]

(S7)

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{N}\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}\psi_{\bm{k}}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime}}(u_{\bm{k}^{\prime}+\bm{Q}/2}^{\downarrow},u_{\bm{k}+\bm{Q}/2}^{\downarrow})\mathcal{O}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}-\bm{k}}(u_{\bm{k}-\bm{Q}/2}^{\uparrow},u_{\bm{k}^{\prime}-\bm{Q}/2}^{\uparrow})\nabla_{\bm{k}}\psi_{\bm{k}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{i}{N}\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}\mathcal{O}_{\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime}}(u_{\bm{k}^{\prime}+\bm{Q}/2}^{\downarrow},\nabla_{\bm{k}}u_{\bm{k}+\bm{Q}/2}^{\downarrow})\mathcal{O}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}-\bm{k}}(u_{\bm{k}-\bm{Q}/2}^{\uparrow},u_{\bm{k}^{\prime}-\bm{Q}/2}^{\uparrow})

(S8)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{i}{N}\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}\mathcal{O}_{\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime}}(u_{\bm{k}^{\prime}+\bm{Q}/2}^{\downarrow},u_{\bm{k}+\bm{Q}/2}^{\downarrow})\mathcal{O}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}-\bm{k}}(\nabla_{\bm{k}}u_{\bm{k}-\bm{Q}/2}^{\uparrow},u_{\bm{k}^{\prime}-\bm{Q}/2}^{\uparrow}),

where we have used integration by parts between lines Eq.
S6
and Eq.
S7
, and have also defined the overlap
\mathcal{O}_{\bm{q}}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})=\int{\rm d}^{2}\bm{r}\langle\phi_{2}|\bm{r}\rangle e^{i\bm{r}\cdot\bm{q}}\langle\bm{r}|\phi_{1}\rangle
between any unit-cell periodic functions
\phi_{1,2}
. Because the periodic functions carry no crystal momentum, it must be that
\mathcal{O}_{\bm{q}}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\propto\delta_{\bm{q}}
. Then the resolution of the identity yields
\mathcal{O}_{\bm{q}}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})=\delta_{\bm{q}}\langle\phi_{2}|(\int{\rm d}^{2}\bm{r}|\bm{r}\rangle\langle\bm{r}|)|\phi_{1}\rangle=\delta_{\bm{q}}\langle\phi_{2}|\phi_{1}\rangle
.
This gives

\displaystyle\bm{d}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{N}\sum_{\bm{k}}\psi_{\bm{k}}^{*}\nabla_{\bm{k}}\psi_{\bm{k}}+|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}\left[\langle u_{\bm{k}+\frac{\bm{Q}}{2},\downarrow}|\nabla_{\bm{k}}u_{\bm{k}+\frac{\bm{Q}}{2},\downarrow}\rangle+\langle\nabla_{\bm{k}}u_{\bm{k}-\frac{\bm{Q}}{2},\uparrow}|u_{\bm{k}-\frac{\bm{Q}}{2},\uparrow}\rangle\right]

(S9)

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{N}\sum_{\bm{k}}\psi_{\bm{k}}^{*}\nabla_{\bm{k}}\psi_{\bm{k}}+|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}\left[\langle u_{\bm{k}+\frac{\bm{Q}}{2},\downarrow}|\nabla_{\bm{k}}u_{\bm{k}+\frac{\bm{Q}}{2},\downarrow}\rangle-\langle u_{\bm{k}-\frac{\bm{Q}}{2},\uparrow}|\nabla_{\bm{k}}u_{\bm{k}-\frac{\bm{Q}}{2},\uparrow}\rangle\right]

(S10)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{k}}\frac{|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}}{N}\left[i\nabla_{\bm{k}}\log z_{\bm{k}}+i\nabla_{\bm{k}}\log s_{\bm{k}}-\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}+\frac{\bm{Q}}{2}}^{\downarrow}+\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}-\frac{\bm{Q}}{2}}^{\uparrow}\right]=\sum_{\bm{k}}\frac{|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}}{N}\left[\mathcal{S}^{\rm spat}_{\bm{k}}+\mathcal{S}^{\rm geom}_{\bm{k}}\right],

(S11)

where we have omitted the dependence on the center of mass momentum
\bm{Q}
and used the fact that Bloch vectors are normalized to go from Eq.
S9
to Eq.
S10
.
This gives Eq.
2
in the main text.

1
Bethe-Salpeter formula and spin-flip spectrum

We start from a generic interacting Hamiltonian projected onto the bands of interest

H=H_{0}+H_{\rm int},\quad H_{0}=\sum_{\bm{k},\sigma}\varepsilon_{\bm{k},\sigma}c_{\bm{k},\sigma}^{\dagger}c_{\bm{k},\sigma},\quad H_{\rm int}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{p},\bm{q},\sigma\tau}W_{\bm{k}\bm{p}}^{\sigma\tau}(\bm{q})c_{\bm{k}+\bm{q},\sigma}^{\dagger}c_{\bm{p}\tau}^{\dagger}c_{\bm{p}+\bm{q},\tau}c_{\bm{k}\sigma}

(S12)

where the interaction matrix elements
W_{\bm{k}\bm{p}}^{\sigma\tau}(\bm{q})
depend on the band form factors
\Gamma_{\bm{k},\sigma}(\bm{q})=\langle u_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}}^{\sigma}|u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}\rangle
and the Fourier transform of the (rotation invariant) interaction potential
v(q=||\bm{q}||)
as

W^{\sigma\tau}_{\bm{k}\bm{p}}(\bm{q})=\frac{v(\bm{q})}{N_{\rm BZ}}\Gamma_{\bm{k},\sigma}(\bm{q})\Gamma^{*}_{\bm{p},\tau}(\bm{q})=\frac{v(\bm{q})}{N_{\rm BZ}}\langle u_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}}^{\sigma}|u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}\rangle\langle u_{\bm{p}}^{\tau}|u_{\bm{p}+\bm{q}}^{\tau}\rangle.

(S13)

Project the Hamiltonian onto the magnon basis spanned by
|S_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}}^{-}\rangle
, as introduced in Eq.
S1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}(\bm{Q})

\displaystyle=\langle S_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}}^{-}|H_{0}+H_{\rm int}|S_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}^{\prime}}^{-}\rangle-\langle\Uparrow|H_{0}+H_{\rm int}|\Uparrow\rangle\delta_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k^{\prime}}}

(S14)

\displaystyle=(E_{\bm{k}_{+},\downarrow}-E_{\bm{k}_{-},\uparrow})\delta_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}-W^{\uparrow,\downarrow}_{\bm{k}_{-},\bm{k}_{+}}(\bm{k}^{\prime}-\bm{k}),\quad\bm{k}_{\pm}=\bm{k}\pm\bm{Q}/2,

where the Hartree-Fock quasiparticle energies are given by

E_{\bm{k},\downarrow}=\varepsilon_{\bm{k},\downarrow}+\left[\sum_{\bm{p}}W_{\bm{k}\bm{p}}^{\downarrow\uparrow}(0)\right],\quad E_{\bm{k},\uparrow}=\varepsilon_{\bm{k},\uparrow}+\left[\sum_{\bm{p}}W^{\uparrow\uparrow}_{\bm{k}\bm{p}}(0)-\sum_{\bm{p}}W_{\bm{p}\bm{p}}^{\uparrow\uparrow}(\bm{k}-\bm{p})\right],

(S15)

under the assumption that
|\Uparrow\rangle
is the ground state.
Note that
\mathcal{H}
is diagonal in the center of mass momentum
\bm{Q}
of the excitation due to translation invariance.
Noticing that the
W^{\sigma,\tau}_{\bm{p},\bm{k}}(0)=v(0)
is independent of spin and momentum, the Hartree terms cancel, so that:

E_{\bm{k}_{+},\downarrow}-E_{\bm{k}_{-},\uparrow}=\varepsilon_{\bm{k}_{+},\downarrow}-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}_{-},\uparrow}+\sum_{\bm{p}}W_{\bm{p}\bm{p}}^{\uparrow\uparrow}(\bm{k}_{-}-\bm{p}),

(S16)

The magnon spectrum is obtained by diagonalizing
\mathcal{H}_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}(\bm{Q})
,
i.e.
solving the so-called Bethe-Salpeter equation

\mathcal{H}_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}(\bm{Q}){\psi}_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}^{\prime}}=\mathcal{E}(\bm{Q})\psi_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}},

(S17)

which yields the collective excitation energies
\mathcal{E}(\bm{Q})
, and provides the coefficients
\psi_{\bm{k},\bm{Q}}
of the corresponding magnon wavefunctions (Eq.
S1
).
In Figs.
2
and
3
, we focus on the spectrum at
\bm{Q}=0
, and extract the magnon gap from the lowest eigenvalue of Eq.
S17
(curves labeled “Bethe Salpeter” in Figs.
2
and
3
).

2
Single mode approximation

The lowest magnon energy can also be obtained by minimizing the expectation value of
\mathcal{H}
with respect to the coefficients of the magnon wavefunction (we omit the subscript
\bm{Q}
for clarity when there is no ambiguity)

\mathcal{E}_{\rm min}(\bm{Q})=\min_{\{\psi\}}\Delta(\psi),\quad\Delta(\psi)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\psi_{\bm{k}}^{*}\mathcal{H}_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}}(\bm{Q})\psi_{\bm{k}^{\prime}},\quad N=\sum_{\bm{k}}|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}.

(S18)

Combing Eqs.
S14
–
S16
yields

\Delta(\psi)=K+I
, where

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{k}}|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}_{+},\downarrow}-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}_{-},\uparrow}),

(S19)

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{k}}\left[|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}\sum_{\bm{p}}W^{\uparrow,\uparrow}_{\bm{p},\bm{p}}(\bm{k}_{-}-\bm{p})\right]-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\left[W^{\uparrow,\downarrow}_{\bm{k}_{-},\bm{k}_{+}}(\bm{k}^{\prime}-\bm{k})\psi_{\bm{k}}^{*}\psi_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}\right]

(S20)

and
\bm{k}_{\pm}=\bm{k}\pm\bm{Q}/2
.
Here,
K
captures the kinetic energy from the non-interacting band structure, while all interaction effects are gathered in
I
.
The stability of the ferromagnetic ground state against spin-flip excitations requires a Stoner-like criterion
I+K>0
for any choice of
\psi
and all momenta
\bm{Q}
.
In Fig.
3
of the main text (where we only show
\bm{Q}=0
), we adopt a relaxed version of this criterion by comparing
I
with the maximal kinetic cost
K_{\rm kin}=\max(|\varepsilon_{\bm{k},\downarrow}-\varepsilon_{\bm{k},\uparrow}|)
, which provides an upper bound of
K
.

In the flat-band limit,
K=0
, and the gap is entirely given by the interaction contribution
I
. Expanding Eq.
S20
with the explicit form of the interaction kernel (Eq.
S13
) and splitting
\psi_{\bm{k}}=z_{\bm{k}}s_{\bm{k}}
gives

\displaystyle\Delta(\psi)=I

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{q}}\frac{v(\bm{q})}{N_{\rm BZ}}\left[|\psi_{\bm{k}}\langle u_{\bm{k}_{-}+\bm{q}}^{\uparrow}|u_{\bm{k}_{-}}^{\uparrow}\rangle|^{2}-\psi_{\bm{k}}^{*}\psi_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}}\langle u_{\bm{k}_{-}+\bm{q}}^{\uparrow}|u_{\bm{k}_{-}}^{\uparrow}\rangle\langle u_{\bm{k}_{+}}^{\downarrow}|u_{\bm{k}_{+}+\bm{q}}^{\downarrow}\rangle\right],

(S21)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{k}}|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}\sum_{\bm{q}}\frac{v(\bm{q})|\langle u_{\bm{k}_{-}+\bm{q}}^{\uparrow}|u_{\bm{k}_{-}}^{\uparrow}\rangle|^{2}}{N_{\rm BZ}}\left[1-\frac{\psi_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}}}{\psi_{\bm{k}}}\frac{\langle u_{\bm{k}_{+}}^{\downarrow}|u_{\bm{k}_{+}+\bm{q}}^{\downarrow}\rangle}{\langle u_{\bm{k}_{-}}^{\uparrow}|u_{\bm{k}_{-}+\bm{q}}^{\uparrow}\rangle}\right],

(S22)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{k}}|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}\sum_{\bm{q}}\frac{v(\bm{q})|\Lambda_{\bm{k}}^{\uparrow}(\bm{q})|^{2}}{N_{\rm BZ}}\left[1-\frac{\psi_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}}}{\psi_{\bm{k}}}\frac{\Lambda_{\bm{k}}^{\downarrow}(\bm{q})^{*}}{\Lambda_{\bm{k}}^{\uparrow}(\bm{q})^{*}}\right],

(S23)

where we have used the notation
\Lambda_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}(\bm{q})=\langle u_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}-\sigma\frac{\bm{Q}}{2}}^{\sigma}|u_{\bm{k}-\sigma\frac{\bm{Q}}{2}}^{\sigma}\rangle
, with
\sigma=\pm 1
for spin-
\uparrow/\downarrow
(introduced in the main text) to match the form of Eq.
4
.

The single-mode approximation discussed in the main text takes
z_{\bm{k}}=1
, yielding

\Delta^{\rm SMA}(\psi)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{k}}|s_{\bm{k}}|^{2}\sum_{\bm{q}}\frac{v(\bm{q})|\Lambda_{\bm{k}}^{\uparrow}(\bm{q})|^{2}}{N_{\rm BZ}}\left[1-\frac{s_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}}}{s_{\bm{k}}}\frac{\Lambda_{\bm{k}}^{\downarrow}(\bm{q})^{*}}{\Lambda_{\bm{k}}^{\uparrow}(\bm{q})^{*}}\right],

(S24)

which corresponds to the curves labeled “Single mode approximation” in Figs.
2
and
3
.

3
Relation between magnon gap and quantum geometric dipole

We further approximate
\Delta(\psi)
by neglecting large-
\bm{q}
processes.
This is justified because both the form factor magnitudes and the screened Coulomb interaction decay with increasing momentum transfer
\bm{q}
.
Specifically, we expand the form factors to second order in
\bm{q}
to capture the small-
\bm{q}
behavior, and re-exponentiate the parts that includes the quantum metric to ensure fast decay for large-
\bm{q}
.
For instance, the norm of the spin-conserving form factors approximately behaves as

\displaystyle|\langle u_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}}^{\sigma}|u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle u_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}}^{\sigma}|u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}\rangle\langle u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}|u_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}}^{\sigma}\rangle

(S25)

\displaystyle\approx 1+\frac{q_{a}}{2}\left(\langle\partial_{a}u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}|u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}\rangle+\langle u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}|\partial_{a}u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}\rangle\right)+\frac{q_{a}q_{b}}{2}(\langle\partial_{a}\partial_{b}u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}|u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}\rangle+\langle u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}|\partial_{a}\partial_{b}u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}\rangle-2\langle\partial_{a}u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}|u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}\rangle\langle u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}|\partial_{b}u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}\rangle)

(S26)

\displaystyle=1-q_{a}q_{b}[{\rm Re}\,\langle\partial_{a}u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}|\partial_{b}u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}\rangle-\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k},a}^{\sigma}\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k},b}^{\sigma}]=1-q_{a}q_{b}g_{\bm{k},ab}^{\sigma}

(S27)

\displaystyle\approx e^{-q_{a}g_{\bm{k},ab}^{\sigma}q_{b}},

(S28)

with summation over repeated spatial indices implied, and where the two approximate equal signs indicate the small-
\bm{q}
expansion and the re-exponentiation, respectively.
We have introduced the quantum metric
g^{\sigma}
and Berry connection
\mathcal{A}^{\sigma}
defined as

g_{\bm{k},ab}^{\sigma}={\rm Re}\,\langle\partial_{k_{a}}u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}|\partial_{k_{b}}u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}\rangle-\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k},a}^{\sigma}\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k},b}^{\sigma},\quad\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k},a}^{\sigma}=-i\langle u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}|\partial_{k_{a}}|u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}\rangle,

(S29)

and have used the normalization of Bloch eigenstates to derive the identities

\displaystyle\langle u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}|u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}\rangle=1,\quad\partial_{a}\langle u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}|u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}\rangle=\langle\partial_{a}u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}|u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}\rangle+\langle u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}|\partial_{a}u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}\rangle=0,

(S30)

\displaystyle\partial_{a}\partial_{b}\langle u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}|u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}\rangle=\langle\partial_{a}\partial_{b}u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}|u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}\rangle+\langle u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}|\partial_{a}\partial_{b}u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}\rangle+2\underbrace{{\rm Re}\,\langle\partial_{a}u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}|\partial_{b}u_{\bm{k}}^{\sigma}\rangle}_{=g_{\bm{k},ab}^{\sigma}+\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k},a}^{\sigma}\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k},b}^{\sigma}}=0.

(S31)

To apply these two steps on
\Delta(\psi)
, we will separately consider the following two contributions

\Delta(\psi)=\Delta_{1}(\psi)-\Delta_{2}(\psi),\quad\Delta_{1}(\psi)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{q}}\frac{v(\bm{q})}{N_{\rm BZ}}\sum_{\bm{k}}|\psi_{\bm{k}}\Lambda_{\bm{k}}^{\uparrow}(\bm{q})|^{2},\quad\Delta_{2}(\psi)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{q}}\frac{v(\bm{q})}{N_{\rm BZ}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\psi_{\bm{k}}^{*}\psi_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}}\Lambda_{\bm{k}}^{\uparrow}(\bm{q})\Lambda_{\bm{k}}^{\downarrow}(\bm{q})^{*}.

(S32)

The approximation of
\Delta_{1}(\psi)
follows straightforwardly from our calculation on the form factors above:

\Delta_{1}(\psi)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{q}}\frac{v(\bm{q})}{N_{\rm BZ}}\sum_{\bm{k}}|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}|\langle u_{\bm{k}_{-}+\bm{q}}^{\uparrow}|u_{\bm{k}_{-}}^{\uparrow}\rangle|^{2}\simeq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{q}}\frac{v(\bm{q})}{N_{\rm BZ}}|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}e^{-q_{a}g_{\bm{k}_{-},ab}^{\uparrow}q_{b}}.

(S33)

Finding the approximate behavior of
\Delta_{2}(\psi)
requires more caution and can be split into three steps.
The first step
is to shift (without loss of generality)
\bm{k}\to\bm{k}-\bm{q}/2
in the
\bm{k}
-integration as above, and expand the integrand

\displaystyle\psi_{\bm{k}-\bm{q}/2}^{*}\psi_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}/2}\Lambda_{\bm{k}-\bm{q}/2}^{\uparrow}(\bm{q})\Lambda_{\bm{k}-\bm{q}/2}^{\downarrow}(\bm{q})^{*}=\psi_{\bm{k}-\bm{q}/2}^{*}\psi_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}/2}\langle u_{\bm{k}_{-}+\bm{q}/2}^{\uparrow}|u_{\bm{k}_{-}-\bm{q}/2}^{\uparrow}\rangle\langle u_{\bm{k}_{+}-\bm{q}/2}^{\downarrow}|u_{\bm{k}_{+}+\bm{q}/2}^{\downarrow}\rangle

(S34)

\displaystyle\approx|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}+\frac{|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}q_{a}}{2}[\partial_{a}\log\psi_{\bm{k}}-\partial_{a}\log\psi_{\bm{k}}^{*}+2i(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}_{+},a}^{\downarrow}-\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}_{-},a}^{\uparrow})]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}q_{a}q_{b}}{4}\left[\frac{\partial_{a}\partial_{b}\psi_{\bm{k}}^{*}}{2\psi_{\bm{k}}^{*}}+\frac{\partial_{a}\partial_{b}\psi_{\bm{k}}}{2\psi_{\bm{k}}}-\frac{\partial_{a}\psi_{\bm{k}}\partial_{b}\psi_{\bm{k}}^{*}}{|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\frac{|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}q_{a}q_{b}}{2}\left[g_{\bm{k}_{-},ab}^{\uparrow}+\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}_{-},a}^{\uparrow}\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}_{-},b}^{\uparrow}+g_{\bm{k}_{+},ab}^{\downarrow}+\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}_{+},a}^{\downarrow}\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}_{+},b}^{\downarrow}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+i\frac{|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}q_{a}q_{b}}{2}\left[(\partial_{a}\log\psi_{\bm{k}}-\partial_{a}\log\psi_{\bm{k}}^{*})(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}_{+},b}^{\downarrow}-\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}_{-},b}^{\uparrow})\right]+|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}q_{a}q_{b}\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}_{+},b}^{\downarrow}\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}_{-},b}^{\uparrow}.

(S35)

In Eq.
S35
, the first line gathers the zeroth and first order terms in
\bm{q}
.
The second line contains all quadratic terms arising solely from the expansion of
\psi_{\bm{k}-\bm{q}/2}^{*}\psi_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}/2}
.
The third line includes the quadratic terms from the form factor expansion (given in Eq.
S27
).
The final line gathers all remaining quadratic terms that result from products of first-order terms not accounted for in the previous lines.
An elegant way to regroup these terms is to realize that, with
S_{\bm{k}}^{\rm tot}=S_{\bm{k}}^{\rm geom}+S_{\bm{k}}^{\rm spat}
,

\displaystyle S_{\bm{k},a}^{{\rm tot},*}S_{\bm{k},b}^{\rm tot}

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}_{-},a}^{\uparrow}-\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}_{+},a}^{\downarrow}-i\partial_{a}\log\psi^{*})(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}_{-},b}^{\uparrow}-\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}_{+},b}^{\downarrow}+i\partial_{b}\log\psi)

(S36)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}_{-},a}^{\uparrow}\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}_{-},b}^{\uparrow}+\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}_{+},a}^{\downarrow}\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}_{+},b}^{\downarrow}+\partial_{a}\log\psi^{*}\partial_{b}\log\psi-2\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}_{+},a}^{\downarrow}\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}_{-},b}^{\uparrow}-i(\partial_{a}\log\psi_{\bm{k}}-\partial_{a}\log\psi_{\bm{k}}^{*})(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}_{+},b}^{\downarrow}-\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}_{-},b}^{\uparrow})

contains most of the terms appearing in Eq.
S35
such that

\displaystyle\psi_{\bm{k}-\bm{q}/2}^{*}\psi_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}/2}\Lambda_{\bm{k}-\bm{q}/2}^{\uparrow}(\bm{q})\Lambda_{\bm{k}-\bm{q}/2}^{\downarrow}(\bm{q})^{*}

\displaystyle\approx|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}+\frac{|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}q_{a}}{2}[\partial_{a}\log\psi_{\bm{k}}-\partial_{a}\log\psi_{\bm{k}}^{*}+2i(\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}_{+},a}^{\downarrow}-\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}_{-},a}^{\uparrow})]

(S37)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{q_{a}q_{b}}{8}[\partial_{a}\partial_{b}|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}]-\frac{|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}q_{a}q_{b}}{2}\left[g_{\bm{k}_{-},ab}^{\uparrow}+g_{\bm{k}_{+},ab}^{\downarrow}\right]-\frac{|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}q_{a}q_{b}}{2}S_{\bm{k},a}^{{\rm tot},*}S_{\bm{k},b}^{\rm tot}.

The second step
is to simplify this expansion by factorization of either the
\bm{q}
or
\bm{k}
sum in the expression of
\Delta_{2}(\psi)
.
For instance, we notice that Eq.
S37
consists of products of
\bm{q}
monomials and
\bm{q}
-independent terms, such that the sums over
\bm{q}
can be factorized and lead to simplifications when we assume that a rotation-invariant Coulomb potential. In that case, we indeed have

F_{a}\sum_{\bm{q}}\frac{v(q)}{N_{\rm BZ}}q_{a}=0,\quad G_{ab}\sum_{\bm{q}}\frac{v(q)}{N_{\rm BZ}}q_{a}q_{b}=G_{ab}\frac{\delta_{ab}}{2}\sum_{\bm{q}}\frac{v(q)}{N_{\rm BZ}}q^{2}=\frac{{\rm Tr}G}{2}\sum_{\bm{q}}\frac{v(q)}{N_{\rm BZ}}q^{2},

(S38)

for any vector
F
and matrix
G
, which gives

\Delta_{2}(\psi)=\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{q}}\frac{v(q)}{N_{\rm BZ}}\left[|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}+\frac{q_{a}q_{b}}{8}\partial_{a}\partial_{b}|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}-|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}q_{a}q_{b}\frac{g_{\bm{k}_{-},ab}^{\uparrow}+g_{\bm{k}_{+},ab}^{\downarrow}}{2}-\frac{q^{2}}{4}|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}||S_{\bm{k}}^{\rm tot}||^{2}\right].

(S39)

Factorizing the sum over
\bm{k}
gives an additional simplification. Indeed, the
\psi_{\bm{k}}
is periodic, such that the average of any of its derivatives vanishes, and in particular

\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{q}}\frac{v(q)}{N_{\rm BZ}}\frac{q_{a}q_{b}}{8}\partial_{a}\partial_{b}|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}=\sum_{\bm{q}}\frac{q_{a}q_{b}v(q)}{8N_{\rm BZ}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\partial_{a}\partial_{b}|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}=0.

(S40)

This leaves us with only three terms

\Delta_{2}(\psi)=\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{q}}\frac{v(q)}{N_{\rm BZ}}|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}\left[1-q_{a}q_{b}\frac{g_{\bm{k}_{-},ab}^{\uparrow}+g_{\bm{k}_{+},ab}^{\downarrow}}{2}-\frac{q^{2}}{4}||S_{\bm{k}}^{\rm tot}||^{2}\right],

(S41)

where we have kept the quadratic term involving the quantum metric in a form similar to that of Eq.
S33
to obtain a similar exponentiated form.
This exponentiation is the
third and last step of the calculation
, and yields

\Delta_{2}(\psi)\approx\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{q}}\frac{v(q)}{N_{\rm BZ}}|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}\left[1-\frac{q^{2}}{4}|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}||S_{\bm{k}}^{\rm tot}||^{2}\right]e^{-\frac{1}{2}q_{a}(g_{\bm{k}_{-},ab}^{\uparrow}+g_{\bm{k}_{+},ab}^{\downarrow})q_{b}}.

(S42)

The main purpose of the exponential decay obtained in Eqs.
S33
and
S42
is to provide a characteristic cutoff and avoid divergences from large-
\bm{q}
. These cutoffs are approximate, and, in the limit where the spin-
\uparrow
and spin-
\downarrow
quantum metrics do not fluctuate too much around their mean, they are almost identical and uniform across the BZ.
To avoid unnecessary complexity, we assume that this is the case from now on and identify

g_{\bm{k}_{-}}^{\uparrow}\approx\frac{g_{\bm{k}_{-},ab}^{\uparrow}+g_{\bm{k}_{+},ab}^{\downarrow}}{2}\approx\frac{g_{\bm{k},ab}^{\uparrow}+g_{\bm{k},ab}^{\downarrow}}{2}=g_{\bm{k}},

(S43)

with the spin-averaged quantum metric introduced in the main text.
Combining the different pieces, we finally obtain Eq.
5

\Delta(\psi)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{k}}|\psi_{\bm{k}}|^{2}\cdot||S_{\bm{k}}^{\rm tot}||^{2}\cdot U_{\bm{k}},\quad U_{\bm{k}}=\left[\sum_{\bm{q}}\frac{v(\bm{q})}{4N_{\rm BZ}}e^{-q_{a}g_{\bm{k},ab}q_{b}}\right].

(S44)

Magnon spin stiffness

In this appendix, we assume that the system has an extended spin-SU(2) symmetry and expand the topological dipole around
\bm{Q}=0
to obtain the spin stiffness of the gapless magnons.
We work in the gauge where
s_{\bm{Q}=0,\bm{k}}=1
and
\mathcal{A}^{\uparrow}=\mathcal{A}^{\downarrow}\equiv\mathcal{A}
, and find that

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{S}_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}}^{\rm geom}\right)_{b}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}-\bm{Q}/2}-\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k}+\bm{Q}/2}+i\nabla_{\bm{k}}\log s_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}}\simeq-Q_{a}\partial_{k_{a}}\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k};b}+i\partial_{k_{b}}\log\left[1+\frac{1}{2}Q_{a}(\langle\partial_{k_{a}}u_{\bm{k}}|u_{\bm{k}}\rangle-\langle u_{\bm{k}}|\partial_{k_{a}}u_{\bm{k}}\rangle)\right]

(S45)

\displaystyle=-Q_{a}\partial_{k_{a}}\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k};b}+i\partial_{k_{b}}\log\left[1-Q_{a}\langle u_{\bm{k}}|\partial_{k_{a}}u_{\bm{k}}\rangle\right]\simeq Q_{a}\left[-\partial_{\bm{k}_{a}}\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k};b}+\partial_{k_{b}}\mathcal{A}_{\bm{k};a}\right]=Q_{a}\epsilon_{ba}\Omega_{\bm{k}},

(S46)

with
\Omega_{\bm{k}}

As a result, we get

||\mathcal{S}_{\bm{Q},\bm{k}}^{\rm geom}||^{2}\simeq|\bm{Q}|^{2}\Omega_{\bm{k}}^{2},

(S47)

providing the spin stiffness quoted in the main text (Eq.
8
). A similar expansion can be used to derive the magnons’ effective mass when their spectrum is gapped.

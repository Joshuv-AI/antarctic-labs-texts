Constructing Spherical Designs Using Tight t t -Fusion Frames
Ryutaro Misawa

Abstract
. In this paper, we study conditions under which a finite subset Z Z of the unit sphere S d − 1 ⊂ ℝ d S^{d-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d} becomes a spherical t t -design, when Z Z is constructed by the following procedure: starting from a finite set of k k -dimensional subspaces in the real Grassmannian G k , d G_{k,d} , we place, for each such k k -dimensional subspace, a finite set on its unit sphere, and then take the union of these sets in S d − 1 S^{d-1} . For this construction problem—namely, obtaining spherical designs in higher dimensions by distributing point sets on lower-dimensional spheres subspace by subspace—we provide a sufficient condition based on the framework of tight t t -fusion frames ( TFF t \mathrm{TFF}_{t} ) due to Bachoc–Ehler. As a preparation for applications, we moreover give an explicit construction of equal-weight tight 2 2 -fusion frames on G 2 , d G_{2,d} for infinitely many dimensions d d , via unions of orbits of the hyperoctahedral group. We also derive necessary conditions for the existence of highly symmetric tight t t -fusion frames, namely equi-chordal and equi-isoclinic tight t t -fusion frames ( ECTFF t \mathrm{ECTFF}_{t} and EITFF t \mathrm{EITFF}_{t} ), on G 2 , d G_{2,d} , and in particular obtain bounds on the number of points.



1.
Introduction

Spherical
t
-designs arise in approximation theory, frame theory, and algebraic combinatorics, among other areas
(
[
1
,
23
,
7
,
18
,
20
,
12
,
6
]
).
They are defined as finite subsets of the
(d-1)
-dimensional unit sphere

S^{d-1}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\|x\|=1\}

that reproduce spherical averages of all polynomials of degree at most
t
by finite sums.
This concept was systematically developed by Delsarte–Goethals–Seidel
[
12
]
.
Moreover, Bondarenko–Radchenko–Viazovska
[
8
]
proved that, for each fixed dimension
d
and every
t\in\mathbb{N}
,
there exists a spherical
t
-design on
S^{d-1}
with
N=O(t^{d-1})
points.

Since then, explicit constructions of spherical
t
-designs have been intensively studied
(
[
5
,
24
,
22
]
).
In this paper, we focus on the following type of construction problem:

placing designs on lower-dimensional spheres inside each of a family of subspaces, in order to obtain a design on a higher-dimensional sphere
.
Throughout the paper, we write

G_{k,d}:=\{V\leq\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\dim V=k\}

for the real Grassmann manifold.

Problem 1.1
.

Fix
t\in\mathbb{N}
.
For any
(d,k)
(
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
1\leq k\leq d
),
give conditions on a finite set
D\subset G_{k,d}
and point sets
Y_{V}\subset S(V)\simeq S^{k-1}
for each
V\in D

such that

Z:=\bigcup_{V\in D}Y_{V}\subset S^{d-1}

is a spherical
t
-design.
In particular, clarify to what extent this property can be controlled by the choice of
D
.

Constructions of this kind trace back to those of König
[
14
]
and Kuperberg
[
15
]
.
Cohn–Conway–Elkies–Kumar
[
9
]
suggested the potential of constructions along Hopf fibrations.
As a concrete example, Sloane–Hardin–Cara
[
21
]
searched for spherical designs on
S^{3}

that can be constructed in the case
(d,k)=(4,2)
.
Subsequently, Okuda
[
19
]
established a theorem for
(d,k)=(4,2)
,
constructing designs on
S^{3}
via the Hopf map
S^{3}\to S^{2}
.
Furthermore, Lindblad
[
16
]
generalized Okuda’s framework and treated in a unified manner
the families corresponding to
(d,k)=(2d^{\prime},2),\ (4d^{\prime\prime},4),\ (16,8)
, where
d^{\prime},d^{\prime\prime}>1
are integers.

In this paper, using the framework of tight
t
-fusion frames (
\mathrm{TFF}_{t}
) introduced by Bachoc–Ehler
[
3
]
,
we give a general sufficient condition for Problem
1.1
, as well as explicit constructions in a certain class.

In frame theory, a fusion frame is a generalization of an ordinary frame,
obtained by replacing vectors with subspaces.
Concretely, it consists of a family of closed subspaces
\{W_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of a Hilbert space
H

together with weights
w_{i}>0
.
One calls
(\{W_{i}\},\{w_{i}\})
a fusion frame if the operator
\sum_{i\in I}w_{i}^{2}P_{W_{i}}

(where
P_{W_{i}}
denotes the orthogonal projection onto
W_{i}
)
is bounded, positive, and invertible on
H
(see
[
11
]
).
Tight
t
-fusion frames (
\mathrm{TFF}_{t}
) were introduced by Bachoc–Ehler
[
3
]
as a higher-order analogue of fusion frames,
and are closely related to designs on Grassmann manifolds.
While the existence of
\mathrm{TFF}_{t}
is known in general, explicit and systematic construction methods remain far from complete.
Motivated by this, we pose the following construction problem.

Problem 1.2
.

Let
t
be a positive integer.
For arbitrary
d,k
, explicitly construct a
\mathrm{TFF}_{t}
on
G_{k,d}
.

Finally, inspired by the strong symmetry exhibited by equi-angular line sets on
G_{1,d}
,
we consider higher-dimensional analogues on
G_{k,d}
:
equi-chordal tight
t
-fusion frames (
\mathrm{ECTFF}_{t}
) and equi-isoclinic tight
t
-fusion frames (
\mathrm{EITFF}_{t}
).
These are highly symmetric subclasses of
\mathrm{TFF}_{t}
in which the inter-subspace distances are uniform,
and they are closely connected to optimal packings and highly symmetric configurations in Grassmannian spaces.
In this paper, we derive necessary parameter conditions
for the existence of
\mathrm{ECTFF}_{2}
and
\mathrm{EITFF}_{2}
.

Contributions

(1)

For Problem
1.1
, we provide a general sufficient condition using
\mathrm{TFF}_{t}
on
G_{k,d}
.

(2)

For Problem
1.2
, we give an explicit construction method for
\mathrm{TFF}_{2}
on
G_{2,d}
.

(3)

We establish bounds on the cardinalities of
\mathrm{ECTFF}_{2}
/
\mathrm{EITFF}_{2}
on
G_{2,d}
.

The organization of this paper is as follows.
In Section 2, we introduce
\mathrm{TFF}_{t}
and related notions, and explain their relation to designs on Grassmann manifolds.
In Section 3, we prove a theorem that produces spherical designs from
\mathrm{TFF}_{t}
on
G_{k,d}
.
In Section 4, we explicitly construct
\mathrm{TFF}_{2}
on
G_{2,d}
by taking unions of orbits of the hyperoctahedral group.
In Section 5, we discuss non-existence results and necessary conditions for
\mathrm{ECTFF}_{2}
and
\mathrm{EITFF}_{2}
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Spherical designs

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
.
We write
d\sigma
for the normalized surface-area measure on
S^{d-1}
.
We also introduce the Pochhammer symbol

(a)_{0}:=1,\qquad(a)_{m}:=a(a+1)\cdots(a+m-1)\quad(m\geq 1).

Definition 2.1
.

[
12
, Def. 5.1]

Let
t
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
. A finite set
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}\subset S^{d-1}
is called a (
equal-weight
)
spherical
t
-design
if, for every function

f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
of total degree at most
t
, one has

\int_{S^{d-1}}f(x)\,d\sigma(x)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}f(x_{j}).

To state an equivalent characterization, we introduce scaled Gegenbauer polynomials.
Assume
d\geq 2
. For
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, define a sequence of polynomials
Q_{\ell}^{(d)}(x)
by

Q_{0}^{(d)}(x):=1,\qquad Q_{1}^{(d)}(x):=x,

and by the recurrence relation

(\ell+d-2)\,Q_{\ell+1}^{(d)}(x)=(2\ell+d-2)\,x\,Q_{\ell}^{(d)}(x)-\ell\,Q_{\ell-1}^{(d)}(x)\qquad(\ell\geq 1).

Then
Q_{\ell}^{(d)}(1)=1
holds for all
\ell
.

Proposition 2.2
.

[
6
]

Let
t
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}\subset S^{d-1}
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

X
is a spherical
t
-design.

(ii)

For every
1\leq\ell\leq t
,

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}Q_{\ell}^{(d)}\!\bigl(\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle\bigr)=0

holds, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the standard inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

(iii)

For every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and every integer
0\leq m\leq t
,

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle x_{j},y\rangle^{m}=n\int_{S^{d-1}}\langle x,y\rangle^{m}\,d\sigma(x)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle n\,\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{m/2}}{\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)_{m/2}}\,\|y\|^{m}&\text{if $m$ is even},\\[8.61108pt]
0&\text{if $m$ is odd}\end{cases}

holds.

As a natural generalization of spherical designs, we introduce the weighted version.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
t
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
.
A pair consisting of a finite set
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}\subset S^{d-1}
and positive weights

\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}>0
is called a
weighted spherical
t
-design
if, for every function

f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
of total degree at most
t
, one has

\int_{S^{d-1}}f(x)\,d\sigma(x)=\frac{1}{\Lambda}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\,f(x_{j}),\qquad\Lambda:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}.

Remark 2.4
.

If the weights are constant, then a weighted spherical
t
-design is the same as an (equal-weight) spherical
t
-design.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
t
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}\subset S^{d-1}
be a finite set with positive weights

\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}>0
. Set
\Lambda:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

(X,\{\lambda_{j}\})
is a weighted spherical
t
-design.

(ii)

For every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and every integer
0\leq m\leq t
,

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\,\langle x_{j},y\rangle^{m}=\Lambda\int_{S^{d-1}}\langle x,y\rangle^{m}\,d\sigma(x)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\Lambda\,\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{m/2}}{\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)_{m/2}}\,\|y\|^{m}&\text{if $m$ is even},\\[8.61108pt]
0&\text{if $m$ is odd}.\end{cases}

The proof is the same as the standard argument in the equal-weight case, and we omit it since it only requires replacing the average by the weighted average.

2.2.
Grassmann manifolds, principal angles, and equiangularity

We will work with finite subsets of Grassmann manifolds.
As an analogue of the inner product for point configurations on spheres, we recall the notion of principal angles. Henceforth, we fix integers
t,d,k\in\mathbb{N}
with
1\leq k\leq d
.

Let
V,W\in G_{k,d}
. Choose orthonormal bases
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}
of
V
and
w_{1},\dots,w_{k}
of
W
, and set

Q_{V}:=\bigl[v_{1}\ \cdots\ v_{k}\bigr],\qquad Q_{W}:=\bigl[w_{1}\ \cdots\ w_{k}\bigr]\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times k},

so that
Q_{V}^{\mathsf{T}}Q_{V}=Q_{W}^{\mathsf{T}}Q_{W}=I_{k}
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
A:=Q_{V}^{\mathsf{T}}Q_{W}
. Its singular values
\sigma_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\sigma_{k}\geq 0
satisfy
0\leq\sigma_{i}\leq 1
.

Proof.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
,

\|Ax\|=\|Q_{V}^{\mathsf{T}}(Q_{W}x)\|\leq\|Q_{W}x\|=\|x\|,

since
Q_{V}^{\mathsf{T}}
is a contraction (its rows are orthonormal) and
Q_{W}
is an isometry.
Hence the largest singular value satisfies
\sigma_{1}=\max_{\|x\|=1}\|Ax\|\leq 1
, so
0\leq\sigma_{i}\leq\sigma_{1}\leq 1
.
∎

Definition 2.7
.

[
4
, Section 2]

Let
\sigma_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\sigma_{k}\geq 0
be the singular values of
A=Q_{V}^{\mathsf{T}}Q_{W}
.
By Proposition
2.6
,
\sigma_{i}\in[0,1]
, and we define

\theta_{i}(V,W):=\arccos(\sigma_{i})\in\Bigl[0,\frac{\pi}{2}\Bigr]\qquad(i=1,\dots,k).

These are called the
principal angles
between
V
and
W
.

Remark 2.8
.

If we choose different orthonormal bases, then
Q_{V}^{\prime}=Q_{V}U
and
Q_{W}^{\prime}=Q_{W}V
for some
U,V\in O(k)
, and hence

(Q_{V}^{\prime})^{\mathsf{T}}Q_{W}^{\prime}=U^{\mathsf{T}}(Q_{V}^{\mathsf{T}}Q_{W})V.

Since singular values are invariant under left and right multiplication by orthogonal matrices, the definition of
principal angles is independent of the choice of orthonormal bases.

Definition 2.9
.

A finite set
D=\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset G_{k,d}
is called
equi-isoclinic
(EI) if there exists
\theta\in[0,\pi/2]

such that for any distinct
i\neq j\in[N]
and any
\ell\in[k]
,

\theta_{\ell}(V_{i},V_{j})=\theta

holds.

Definition 2.10
.

[
10
]

For
V,W\in G_{k,d}
,

d_{C}(V,W):=\sqrt{\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\sin^{2}\!\theta_{\ell}(V,W)}.

This quantity is called the
chordal distance
between
V
and
W
.

Definition 2.11
.

A finite set
D=\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset G_{k,d}
is called
equi-chordal
(EC) if for any distinct
i\neq j\in[N]
,

d_{C}(V_{i},V_{j})=\text{\rm const.}

2.3.
Grassmannian designs

We write
d\sigma_{k,d}
for the
O(d)
-invariant probability measure on the Grassmannian
G_{k,d}
.

Proposition 2.12
.

[
4
, Thm. 3.2, Def. 3.3]

The space
L^{2}(G_{k,d})
decomposes into irreducible
O(d)
-subrepresentations as

L^{2}(G_{k,d})\;=\;\bigoplus_{\ell(\mu)\leq k}\,\mathsf{H}^{\,k,d}_{2\mu}.

Here
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{r})
is a partition (i.e.,
\mu_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{r}\geq 1
) or the empty partition
\mu=\varnothing
.
We set
\deg(\mu):=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mu_{i}
and
\ell(\mu):=r
, with the convention
\deg(\varnothing)=\ell(\varnothing)=0
.
Moreover,
\mathsf{H}^{\,k,d}_{2\mu}\subset L^{2}(G_{k,d})
denotes the irreducible component of type

2\mu:=(2\mu_{1},\dots,2\mu_{r})
.

For
t\in\mathbb{N}
, define a subspace of functions on
G_{k,d}
by

\mathrm{Pol}_{2t}(G_{k,d})=\mathrm{Pol}_{2t+1}(G_{k,d})\;:=\;\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell(\mu)\leq k\\
\deg(\mu)\leq t\end{subarray}}\mathsf{H}^{\,k,d}_{2\mu}.

We further set

\mathrm{Pol}^{(1)}_{2t}(G_{k,d})=\mathrm{Pol}^{(1)}_{2t+1}(G_{k,d})\;:=\;\bigoplus_{1\leq j\leq t}\mathsf{H}^{\,k,d}_{(2j)}\;\subset\;\mathrm{Pol}_{2t}(G_{k,d}).

Definition 2.13
.

[
4
, Prop. 4.2]

A finite set
D=\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}\subset G_{k,d}
is called a
Grassmann
2t
-design
if, for every function

f\in\mathrm{Pol}_{2t}(G_{k,d})
, one has

\int_{G_{k,d}}f(V)\,d\sigma_{k,d}(V)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}f(V_{j}).

There are several equivalent characterizations of Grassmann
2t
-designs. In particular, we will use the criterion via zonal polynomials.
For
V,W\in G_{k,d}
, set

y_{i}(V,W):=\cos^{2}\theta_{i}(V,W)\qquad(1\leq i\leq k).

Fix a base point
V_{0}\in G_{k,d}
. Its stabilizer satisfies

\mathrm{Stab}(V_{0})\simeq O(k)\times O(d-k).

Since each irreducible component
\mathsf{H}^{k,d}_{2\mu}
(
\ell(\mu)\leq k
) occurs in
L^{2}(G_{k,d})
with multiplicity one,
the
\,\mathrm{Stab}(V_{0})
-fixed subspace

\bigl(\mathsf{H}^{k,d}_{2\mu}\bigr)^{\mathrm{Stab}(V_{0})}:=\{\,f\in\mathsf{H}^{k,d}_{2\mu}\mid f(g\cdot V)=f(V)\ \ (\forall g\in\mathrm{Stab}(V_{0}),\ \forall V\in G_{k,d})\,\}

is one-dimensional. Therefore there is a nonzero
\mathrm{Stab}(V_{0})
-invariant function
f
in
\mathsf{H}^{k,d}_{2\mu}
, unique up to scalar multiple.
We call such a function
f
a
zonal polynomial
of type
2\mu
with respect to
V_{0}
. We then define
P_{2\mu}=f/f(V_{0})\in\bigl(\mathsf{H}^{k,d}_{2\mu}\bigr)^{\mathrm{Stab}(V_{0})}.
By
\mathrm{Stab}(V_{0})
-invariance,
P_{2\mu}(V)
depends only on
y_{i}(V,V_{0})=\cos^{2}\theta_{i}(V,V_{0})
, and hence it may be regarded as a symmetric polynomial in
k
variables, which we also denote by
P_{2\mu}
, so that

P_{2\mu}(V)=P_{2\mu}\bigl(y_{1}(V,V_{0}),\dots,y_{k}(V,V_{0})\bigr).

In particular, the normalization
P_{2\mu}(V_{0})=1
is equivalent to
P_{2\mu}(1,\dots,1)=1
.
Moreover, for arbitrary
V,W\in G_{k,d}
we set

P_{2\mu}(V,W):=P_{2\mu}\bigl(y_{1}(V,W),\dots,y_{k}(V,W)\bigr).

Remark 2.14
.

Assume
k\geq 2
; the component of type
(2,2)
appears only in this range (for
k=1
the term
P_{(2,2)}
is absent).
We list the explicit low-degree zonal polynomials
P_{(2)},P_{(4)},P_{(2,2)}
.
(see
[
13
]
).

(2.1)

P_{(2)}=\frac{k}{k-d}-\frac{d}{k-d}\,c_{2},\qquad\text{where}\qquad c_{2}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}y_{i}.

(2.2)

P_{(4)}=\frac{P^{\prime}_{(4)}}{P^{\prime}_{(4)}(1,\dots,1)},\qquad\text{where}\qquad\begin{aligned} P^{\prime}_{(4)}&=1-\frac{2(d+2)}{k}\,c_{2}+\frac{(d+2)(d+4)}{k(k+2)}\,c_{4},\\
c_{4}&=\frac{3}{k(k+2)}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}y_{i}^{2}+\frac{2}{3}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k}y_{i}y_{j}\right).\end{aligned}

(2.3)

P_{(2,2)}=\frac{P^{\prime}_{(2,2)}}{P^{\prime}_{(2,2)}(1,\dots,1)},\qquad\text{where}\qquad\begin{aligned} P^{\prime}_{(2,2)}&=1-\frac{2(d-1)}{k}\,c_{2}+\frac{(d-1)(d-2)}{k(k-1)}\,c_{22},\\
c_{22}&=\frac{2}{k(k-1)}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k}y_{i}y_{j}.\end{aligned}

Proposition 2.15
.

[
4
, Prop. 4.2]

Let
D\subset G_{k,d}
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

D
is a Grassmann

(ii)

2.4.
Tight
t
-fusion frames

Definition 2.16
.

[
3
, Def. 4.1]

Let
\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}\subset G_{k,d}
be a finite set and let
\{\omega_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
be positive weights.
For each
j
, let
P_{V_{j}}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
denote the orthogonal projection onto
V_{j}
.
If there exist constants
A,B>0
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

A\,\|x\|^{2t}\ \leq\ \sum_{j=1}^{n}\omega_{j}\,\|P_{V_{j}}x\|^{2t}\ \leq\ B\,\|x\|^{2t},

then
\{(V_{j},\omega_{j})\}_{j=1}^{n}
is called a
t
-fusion frame
.
If, in particular,
A=B
, then it is called a
tight
t
-fusion frame
.
In the equal-weight case, we call
\{(V_{j},\omega_{j})\}_{j=1}^{n}
an
equal-weight tight
t
-fusion frame
and often omit the weights, simply writing
\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
.

Bachoc–Ehler
[
3
]
characterized tight
t
-fusion frames as cubature formulas for
\mathrm{Pol}^{(1)}_{2t}(G_{k,d})
.

Theorem 2.17
.

[
3
, Thm. 5.3]

Let
D\subset G_{k,d}
and positive weights
\{\omega_{V}\}_{V\in D}
, and set

\Omega:=\sum_{V\in D}\omega_{V}.

Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

\{(V,\omega_{V})\}_{V\in D}
is a tight
t
-fusion frame.

(ii)

For every
f\in\mathrm{Pol}^{(1)}_{2t}(G_{k,d})
,

(2.4)

\sum_{V\in D}\omega_{V}\,f(V)=\Omega\int_{G_{k,d}}f(V)\,d\sigma_{k,d}(V).

Equivalently, with
\tilde{\omega}_{V}:=\omega_{V}/\Omega
one has

\int_{G_{k,d}}f(V)\,d\sigma_{k,d}(V)=\sum_{V\in D}\tilde{\omega}_{V}f(V).

(iii)

For each
1\leq\ell\leq t
,

\sum_{V,W\in D}\omega_{V}\omega_{W}\,P_{(2\ell)}\!\bigl(V,W\bigr)=0

holds.

Remark 2.18
.

By Definition
2.13
and Theorem
2.17
, for any
t
we have

\text{Grassmann }2t\text{-design}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{tight }t\text{-fusion frame},

since
\mathrm{Pol}^{(1)}_{2t}(G_{k,d})\subset\mathrm{Pol}_{2t}(G_{k,d})
.
In general, the converse does not hold.
In particular, for
k\geq 2
and
t\geq 2
one has

\mathrm{Pol}^{(1)}_{2t}(G_{k,d})\subsetneq\mathrm{Pol}_{2t}(G_{k,d})
,
so tight
t
-fusion frames form a strictly weaker notion than Grassmann
2t
-designs.
(When
k=1
or
t=1
, we have
\mathrm{Pol}^{(1)}_{2t}(G_{k,d})=\mathrm{Pol}_{2t}(G_{k,d})
.)

3.
Spherical Designs Using Tight
t
-Fusion Frames

In this section we present a general principle for constructing (weighted) spherical designs on higher-dimensional spheres
from tight
t
-fusion frames on
G_{k,d}
.

For each
V\in G_{k,d}
, write

S(V):=\{x\in V:\|x\|=1\}\simeq S^{k-1}.

Let
d\sigma_{V}
denote the
O(V)\simeq O(k)
-invariant measure on
S(V)
.

For
y\in S^{d-1}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
, define

f_{y,m}(V):=\bigl(\mathrm{tr}(P_{V}P_{y})\bigr)^{m}\qquad(V\in G_{k,d}),

where
P_{V}
is the orthogonal projection onto
V
, and
P_{y}
is the orthogonal projection onto the line
\mathbb{R}y
.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
3
, Remark 5.2, Thm. 5.3]
)
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq k\leq d
. For any
y\in S^{d-1}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
, the following hold:

(1)

f_{y,m}\in\mathrm{Pol}^{(1)}_{2m}(G_{k,d})
. In particular, if
m\leq t
, then
f_{y,m}\in\mathrm{Pol}^{(1)}_{2t}(G_{k,d})
.

(2)

\int_{G_{k,d}}\bigl(\mathrm{tr}(P_{V}P_{y})\bigr)^{m}\,d\sigma_{k,d}(V)=\frac{\bigl(\frac{k}{2}\bigr)_{m}}{\bigl(\frac{d}{2}\bigr)_{m}}.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
t,s\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
D\subset G_{k,d}
be a finite set with positive weights
\{\omega_{V}\}_{V\in D}
, and assume that

(D,\{\omega_{V}\})
is a tight
t
-fusion frame. Set

\Omega:=\sum_{V\in D}\omega_{V}.

For each
V\in D
, let
(Y_{V},\{\lambda_{V,z}\}_{z\in Y_{V}})
be a weighted spherical
s
-design on
S(V)
, and assume that

\Lambda:=\sum_{z\in Y_{V}}\lambda_{V,z}

is independent of
V
.
Define a weighted finite set
(Z,w)
on
S^{d-1}
as follows:

Z:=\bigcup_{V\in D}Y_{V}\subset S^{d-1},\qquad w(z):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}V\in D\\
z\in Y_{V}\end{subarray}}\omega_{V}\,\lambda_{V,z}\quad(z\in Z).

Then
(Z,w)
is a weighted spherical
r
-design on
S^{d-1}
with

r=\min\{s,\,2t+1\}.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.5
, it suffices to show that for every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and every integer
0\leq\ell\leq r
,

(3.1)

\sum_{z\in Z}w_{z}\,\langle z,y\rangle^{\ell}=\begin{cases}\Gamma\,\|y\|^{2m}\,\dfrac{(1/2)_{m}}{(d/2)_{m}}&\text{if }\ell=2m\text{ is even},\\[5.16663pt]
0&\text{if }\ell\text{ is odd}.\end{cases}

where

\Gamma:=\sum_{z\in Z}w_{z}=\sum_{V\in D}\omega_{V}\sum_{z\in Y_{V}}\lambda_{V,z}=\Omega\Lambda.

Fix
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
0\leq\ell\leq r
.
By definition,

\sum_{z\in Z}w_{z}\,\langle z,y\rangle^{\ell}=\sum_{V\in D}\omega_{V}\sum_{z\in Y_{V}}\lambda_{V,z}\,\langle z,y\rangle^{\ell}.

Since
z\in Y_{V}\subset V
, we have
P_{V}z=z
, and hence

(3.2)

\sum_{z\in Z}w_{z}\,\langle z,y\rangle^{\ell}=\sum_{V\in D}\omega_{V}\sum_{z\in Y_{V}}\lambda_{V,z}\,\langle z,P_{V}y\rangle^{\ell}.

(A) The case where
\ell
is odd.

Since
\ell\leq r\leq s
, for each
V
the function
x\mapsto\langle x,P_{V}y\rangle^{\ell}
on
S(V)
is odd, and therefore

\int_{S(V)}\langle x,P_{V}y\rangle^{\ell}\,d\sigma_{V}(x)=0.

Moreover, since
(Y_{V},\{\lambda_{V,z}\})
is a weighted spherical
s
-design and
\ell\leq s
, we have

\sum_{z\in Y_{V}}\lambda_{V,z}\,\langle z,P_{V}y\rangle^{\ell}=\Lambda\int_{S(V)}\langle x,P_{V}y\rangle^{\ell}\,d\sigma_{V}(x)=0.

Substituting this into (
3.2
) yields

\sum_{z\in Z}w_{z}\,\langle z,y\rangle^{\ell}=0
.

(B) The case where
\ell=2m
is even.

Since
2m\leq r\leq s
, we have
2m\leq s
, and since
2m\leq r\leq 2t+1
, we have
m\leq t
.
For each
V\in D
, since
(Y_{V},\{\lambda_{V,z}\})
is a weighted spherical
s
-design on
S(V)\simeq S^{k-1}

and
2m\leq s
, applying Lemma
2.5
on
S(V)
(identified with
S^{k-1}
) with
d=k
and
y=P_{V}y
gives

\sum_{z\in Y_{V}}\lambda_{V,z}\,\langle z,P_{V}y\rangle^{2m}=\Lambda\,\|P_{V}y\|^{2m}\,\frac{(1/2)_{m}}{(k/2)_{m}}.

Therefore,

\sum_{z\in Z}w_{z}\,\langle z,y\rangle^{2m}=\Lambda\,\frac{(1/2)_{m}}{(k/2)_{m}}\sum_{V\in D}\omega_{V}\,\|P_{V}y\|^{2m}.

Let
P_{y}
denote the orthogonal projection onto the line
\mathbb{R}y
. Since
\|P_{V}y\|^{2}=\|y\|^{2}\,\mathrm{tr}(P_{V}P_{y})
, we obtain

\|P_{V}y\|^{2m}=\|y\|^{2m}\bigl(\mathrm{tr}(P_{V}P_{y})\bigr)^{m}.

Hence

\sum_{z\in Z}w_{z}\,\langle z,y\rangle^{2m}=\Lambda\,\frac{(1/2)_{m}}{(k/2)_{m}}\,\|y\|^{2m}\sum_{V\in D}\omega_{V}\,\bigl(\mathrm{tr}(P_{V}P_{y})\bigr)^{m}.

By Lemma
3.1
, the function
V\mapsto(\mathrm{tr}(P_{V}P_{y}))^{m}
lies in
\mathrm{Pol}^{(1)}_{2t}(G_{k,d})
,
so (
2.4
) yields

\sum_{V\in D}\omega_{V}\,\bigl(\mathrm{tr}(P_{V}P_{y})\bigr)^{m}=\Omega\int_{G_{k,d}}\bigl(\mathrm{tr}(P_{V}P_{y})\bigr)^{m}\,d\sigma_{k,d}(V)=\Omega\,\frac{(k/2)_{m}}{(d/2)_{m}}.

Substituting this into the previous identity gives

\sum_{z\in Z}w_{z}\,\langle z,y\rangle^{2m}=\Omega\Lambda\,\|y\|^{2m}\,\frac{(1/2)_{m}}{(d/2)_{m}}=\Gamma\,\|y\|^{2m}\,\frac{(1/2)_{m}}{(d/2)_{m}}.

Combining (A) and (B), (
3.1
) holds for all
0\leq\ell\leq r
.
Therefore
(Z,w)
is a weighted spherical
r
-design.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
t,s\in\mathbb{N}
.
Assume that
D\subset G_{k,d}
is an equal-weight tight
t
-fusion frame, and that for each
V\in D
,

Y_{V}\subset S(V)
is a spherical
s
-design, with
|Y_{V}|
independent of
V
.
Let

Z:=\bigcup_{V\in D}Y_{V}\subset S^{d-1}

be viewed as a multiset.
Then the multiset
Z
is a spherical
\min\{s,\,2t+1\}
-design on
S^{d-1}
.

Remark 3.4
.

Corollary
3.3
gives a spherical
r
-design in the
multiset
sense.
If, in addition,
Y_{V}\cap Y_{V^{\prime}}=\varnothing
for
V\neq V^{\prime}
, then

Z=\bigsqcup_{V\in D}Y_{V}
and we obtain an ordinary spherical design. If
k\geq 2
, we may retake
Y_{V}
’s to make this additional condition satisfied, as follows. Fix
V\in D
and suppose
Y_{V}\cap Y_{V^{\prime}}\neq\varnothing
for some
V^{\prime}\in D
.
Define

\mathcal{B}=\bigcup_{V^{\prime}\in D\setminus\{V\}}\bigl\{\,g\in O(V)\ \bigm|\ (gY_{V})\cap Y_{V^{\prime}}\neq\varnothing\,\bigr\}.

Then
\mathcal{B}
is a finite union of cosets of subgroups conjugate to
O(k-1)
in
O(V)
.
Each such coset is a closed subset of
O(V)
with empty interior since
k\geq 2
, hence its complement is open dense.
Therefore
O(V)\setminus\mathcal{B}
is a finite intersection of open dense subsets of
O(V)
, and in particular is non-empty.
Then choosing
g\in O(V)\setminus\mathcal{B}
yields

(gY_{V})\cap Y_{V^{\prime}}=\varnothing
for all
V^{\prime}\neq V
. Since
gY_{V}\subset S(V)
is again a spherical
s
-design, we may replace
Y_{V}
with
gY_{V}
. Repeating this procedure for each
V\in D
, we obtain a pairwise disjoint family
\{Y_{V}\}_{V\in D}
.

To conclude this section, we note that the construction of spherical
(2t+1)
-designs using Corollary
3.3
consists of two independent tasks:

(1)

constructing spherical
(2t+1)
-designs on the lower-dimensional sphere
S^{k-1}
, and

(2)

constructing tight
t
-fusion frames on
G_{k,d}
.

4.
Constructing Tight
2
-Fusion Frames on
G_{2,d}
via Group Orbits

In this section we construct equal-weight tight
2
-fusion frames on
G_{2,d}
from orbits of the hyperoctahedral group

B_{d}
. These Grassmannian configurations can then be used as inputs for the lifting result
(Corollary
3.3
) to produce spherical designs.
Throughout, binomial coefficients are interpreted by

\binom{n}{k}=0\qquad(k<0\ \text{or}\ k>n).

Let
d\geq 4
, and let
e_{1},\dots,e_{d}
denote the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The hyperoctahedral group

B_{d}:=\{\pm 1\}^{d}\rtimes S_{d}\subset O(d)

acts on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by signed coordinate permutations, and hence acts on
G_{2,d}
by
g\cdot W:=gW
.
For a finite set
X\subset G_{2,d}
, define

F_{X}(x):=\frac{1}{|X|}\sum_{W\in X}\|P_{W}x\|^{4}\qquad(x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
P_{W}
denotes the orthogonal projection onto
W
.

Proposition 4.1
.

For a finite set
X\subset G_{2,d}
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

X
is a
\mathrm{TFF}_{2}
.

(ii)

F_{X}
is constant on the unit sphere
S^{d-1}
.

Proof.

The implication (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) is immediate.
For (ii)
\Rightarrow
(i), let
x\neq 0
and set
u:=x/\|x\|\in S^{d-1}
.
By
4
-homogeneity of
F_{X}
,

F_{X}(x)=F_{X}(\|x\|u)=\|x\|^{4}F_{X}(u).

By (ii),
F_{X}(u)
is a constant
C
on the sphere, hence
F_{X}(x)=C\|x\|^{4}
for all
x\neq 0
.
The case
x=0
is trivial.
∎

In what follows, we consider criteria for a
B_{d}
-invariant subset
X\subset G_{2,d}
to be a
\mathrm{TFF}_{2}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
X\subset G_{2,d}
is
B_{d}
-invariant, then
F_{X}
is
B_{d}
-invariant, i.e.,

F_{X}(gx)=F_{X}(x)
for all
g\in B_{d}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Moreover, there exist constants
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
such that

F_{X}(x)=\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}^{4}+\beta\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}^{2}\Bigr)^{2}\qquad(\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Proof.

From
gX=X
and the orthogonality identity
P_{gW}=gP_{W}g^{\mathsf{T}}

(hence
\|P_{gW}x\|=\|P_{W}(g^{\mathsf{T}}x)\|
), we obtain

F_{X}(g^{\mathsf{T}}x)=F_{X}(x)\qquad(\forall g\in B_{d}),

so in particular
F_{X}
is invariant under coordinate permutations and sign changes.

Sign-invariance implies that
F_{X}
can be written as a polynomial in
y_{i}:=x_{i}^{2}
.
Since
F_{X}
is homogeneous of degree
4
in
x
, it is homogeneous of degree
2
in
y
.
Moreover, permutation-invariance implies that
F_{X}
is a symmetric homogeneous polynomial of degree
2

in
y_{1},\dots,y_{d}
.
The space of symmetric homogeneous polynomials of degree
2
is spanned by

\sum_{i=1}^{d}y_{i}^{2},\qquad\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{d}y_{i}\Bigr)^{2},

which yields the claimed form.
∎

By Lemma
4.2
, on the sphere we may write

F_{X}=\alpha\sum_{i}x_{i}^{4}+\beta
.
Hence constancy on
S^{d-1}
is equivalent to
\alpha=0
, and it suffices to compare
F_{X}
at two points
where
\sum_{i}x_{i}^{4}
takes different values.

Proposition 4.3
.

Assume that
X\subset G_{2,d}
is
B_{d}
-invariant. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

X
is a
\mathrm{TFF}_{2}
.

(ii)

F_{X}(e_{1})=F_{X}\Bigl(\frac{e_{1}+e_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}\Bigr).

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
,

F_{X}(x)=\alpha\sum_{i}x_{i}^{4}+\beta\Bigl(\sum_{i}x_{i}^{2}\Bigr)^{2}.

On the sphere,
(\sum_{i}x_{i}^{2})^{2}=1
, hence

F_{X}|_{S^{d-1}}=\alpha\sum_{i}x_{i}^{4}+\beta
.
Thus
F_{X}
is constant on
S^{d-1}
if and only if
\alpha=0
.

On the other hand,

\sum_{i}(e_{1}^{4})_{i}=1,\qquad\sum_{i}\Bigl(\frac{e_{1}+e_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}\Bigr)_{i}^{4}=\frac{1}{2},

and therefore

F_{X}(e_{1})-F_{X}\Bigl(\frac{e_{1}+e_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}\Bigr)=\alpha\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{2}\Bigr)=\frac{\alpha}{2}.

Hence (ii)
\iff\alpha=0\iff
(i).
∎

Next we define explicit
B_{d}
-invariant sets as
B_{d}
-orbits.
Let
a
and
b
be integers with
1\leq a\leq b
,
a+b\leq d
, and write
[d]:=\{1,\dots,d\}.

Define

u_{0}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{a}}\sum_{i=1}^{a}e_{i},\qquad v_{0}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{b}}\sum_{j=a+1}^{a+b}e_{j},\qquad W_{0}:=\mathrm{span}(u_{0},v_{0})\in G_{2,d}.

\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a,b}:=B_{d}\cdot W_{0}\subset G_{2,d},

N_{d}(a,b):=\bigl|\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a,b}\bigr|.

To compute
N_{d}(a,b)
and, later, the quantity
F_{\mathcal{O}}(x)
,
we introduce the following set which will be used to parametrize elements of
\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a,b}
by supports and signs:

\mathcal{T}_{a,b}^{(d)}:=\Bigl\{(A,B,\varepsilon,\delta)\ \Big|\ A,B\subset[d],\ A\cap B=\varnothing,\ |A|=a,\ |B|=b,\ \varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}^{A},\ \delta\in\{\pm 1\}^{B}\Bigr\},

where
\{\pm 1\}^{A}
denotes the set of all functions
A\to\{\pm 1\}
.

For
\tau=(A,B,\varepsilon,\delta)\in\mathcal{T}_{a,b}^{(d)}
, set

u_{A,\varepsilon}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{a}}\sum_{i\in A}\varepsilon_{i}\,e_{i},\qquad v_{B,\delta}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{b}}\sum_{j\in B}\delta_{j}\,e_{j},

and define

W_{\tau}=W_{A,B;\varepsilon,\delta}:=\mathrm{span}\bigl(u_{A,\varepsilon},\,v_{B,\delta}\bigr)\in G_{2,d}.

Lemma 4.4
.

The map defined by

\pi:\mathcal{T}_{a,b}^{(d)}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{a,b}^{(d)},\qquad\pi(\tau):=W_{\tau}.

is surjective.

Proof.

We first show that
\pi
is well-defined, i.e.,
W_{\tau}\in\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a,b}
for every

\tau=(A,B,\varepsilon,\delta)\in\mathcal{T}^{(d)}_{a,b}
.
Choose a permutation
\sigma\in S_{d}
such that

\sigma(\{1,\dots,a\})=A
and
\sigma(\{a+1,\dots,a+b\})=B
.
Let
s\in\{\pm 1\}^{d}
be a sign vector such that
s_{i}=\varepsilon_{i}
for
i\in A
,

s_{j}=\delta_{j}
for
j\in B
, and
s_{\ell}=1
otherwise, and set
g:=\mathrm{diag}(s)\,\sigma\in B_{d}
.
Then

gu_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{a}}\sum_{i\in A}\varepsilon_{i}e_{i}=u_{A,\varepsilon},\qquad gv_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{b}}\sum_{j\in B}\delta_{j}e_{j}=v_{B,\delta},

and hence
gW_{0}=\mathrm{span}(gu_{0},gv_{0})=\mathrm{span}(u_{A,\varepsilon},v_{B,\delta})=W_{\tau}
.
Therefore
W_{\tau}\in B_{d}\cdot W_{0}=\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a,b}
, so
\pi
is well-defined.

To show that
\pi
is surjective, let
W\in\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a,b}
. Then
W=gW_{0}
for some
g\in B_{d}
.
Write
g=\mathrm{diag}(s)\,\sigma
with
s\in\{\pm 1\}^{d}
and
\sigma\in S_{d}
, and put

A:=\sigma(\{1,\dots,a\}),\qquad B:=\sigma(\{a+1,\dots,a+b\}),\qquad\varepsilon_{i}:=s_{i}\ (i\in A),\quad\delta_{j}:=s_{j}\ (j\in B).

Then
\tau:=(A,B,\varepsilon,\delta)\in\mathcal{T}^{(d)}_{a,b}
and the same computation gives

gu_{0}=u_{A,\varepsilon}
and
gv_{0}=v_{B,\delta}
, hence

W=gW_{0}=W_{\tau}=\pi(\tau)
.
Thus,
\pi
is surjective.
∎

For
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, define its support by

\mathrm{supp}(x):=\{\,i\in[d]:x_{i}\neq 0\,\}.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
W\in\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a,b}
. Then

|\pi^{-1}(W)|=\begin{cases}4,&\text{if }a<b,\\
8,&\text{if }a=b.\end{cases}

Proof.

Fix
W\in\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a,b}
and, by Lemma
4.4
, there exists
\tau=(A,B,\varepsilon,\delta)\in\mathcal{T}^{(d)}_{a,b}
such that
W=W_{\tau}
. Write
u:=u_{A,\varepsilon}
,
v:=v_{B,\delta}
.
Since
A\cap B=\varnothing
, we have
\mathrm{supp}(u)=A
,
\mathrm{supp}(v)=B
, and
u\perp v
.
For any
x=\alpha u+\beta v\in W\setminus\{0\}
,

\mathrm{supp}(x)=\begin{cases}A,&\text{if }\beta=0,\\
B,&\text{if }\alpha=0,\\
A\cup B,&\text{if }\alpha\beta\neq 0,\end{cases}\qquad\text{hence}\qquad|\mathrm{supp}(x)|\in\{a,b,a+b\}.

In particular, the vectors in
W
satisfying
|\mathrm{supp}(x)|=a
lie on the line
\mathbb{R}u
,
and their support is always
A
(and similarly for
|\mathrm{supp}(x)|=b
).

Now assume
\tau^{\prime}=(A^{\prime},B^{\prime},\varepsilon^{\prime},\delta^{\prime})
satisfies
W_{(\tau^{\prime})}=W
.
If
a<b
, then the line in
U
spanned by vectors with support size
a
is unique, so

\mathbb{R}u_{A,\varepsilon}=\mathbb{R}u_{A^{\prime},\varepsilon^{\prime}}
, hence
A=A^{\prime}
.
Similarly
B=B^{\prime}
.
If
a=b
, the same argument shows
\{A,B\}=\{A^{\prime},B^{\prime}\}
, leaving only the possibility of swapping
A
and
B
.

Thus, when
a<b
, the pair
(A,B)
determining
W
is unique, whereas when
a=b
there are exactly two choices,
namely
(A,B)
and
(B,A)
.
Hence it remains to count the identifications coming from signs for fixed
(A,B)
.

Fix
(A,B)
and consider

W_{A,B;\varepsilon,\delta}=W_{A,B;\varepsilon^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\varepsilon^{\prime}=\pm\varepsilon\ \text{and}\ \delta^{\prime}=\pm\delta.

We prove (
\Rightarrow
). If
W_{A,B;\varepsilon^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}}=W_{A,B;\varepsilon,\delta}
, then

u_{A,\varepsilon^{\prime}}\in\mathrm{Span}\{u_{A,\varepsilon},v_{B,\delta}\}
, so for some
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
we have

u_{A,\varepsilon^{\prime}}=\alpha u_{A,\varepsilon}+\beta v_{B,\delta}
.
The left-hand side is supported on
A
and
v_{B,\delta}
is supported on
B
, so
A\cap B=\varnothing
forces
\beta=0
.
Thus
u_{A,\varepsilon^{\prime}}=\alpha u_{A,\varepsilon}
.
Since both are unit vectors,
|\alpha|=1
, hence
\alpha=\pm 1
, which is equivalent to
\varepsilon^{\prime}=\pm\varepsilon
.
Similarly
\delta^{\prime}=\pm\delta
follows. The converse (
\Leftarrow
) is immediate.

Therefore, for fixed
(A,B)
there are exactly
4
sign classes.
Hence
|\pi^{-1}(W)|=4
when
a<b
.
When
a=b
, we can swap
(A,B)
, hence
|\pi^{-1}(W)|=8
.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
1\leq a\leq b
with
a+b\leq d
, and consider the
B_{d}
-orbit

\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a,b}\subset G_{2,d}.

(i)

Let
1\leq a^{\prime}\leq b^{\prime}
with
a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}\leq d
.
If
\{a,b\}\neq\{a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\}
, then

\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a,b}\cap\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a^{\prime},b^{\prime}}=\varnothing.

(ii)

The orbit size is given by

N_{d}(a,b)=\binom{d}{a}\binom{d-a}{b}\,2^{a+b-2}\times\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }a<b,\\[2.84526pt]
\frac{1}{2},&\text{if }a=b.\end{cases}

Proof.

(i)
Assume
W\in\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a,b}
. By Lemma
4.4
, there exists

\tau=(A,B,\varepsilon,\delta)\in\mathcal{T}^{(d)}_{a,b}
such that

W=\pi(\tau)=W_{A,B;\varepsilon,\delta}=\mathrm{Span}\{u_{A,\varepsilon},v_{B,\delta}\}.

Put
u:=u_{A,\varepsilon}
and
v:=v_{B,\delta}
. Then
\mathrm{supp}(u)=A
,
\mathrm{supp}(v)=B
,
A\cap B=\varnothing
,
and
|A|=a
,
|B|=b
. Hence for any
x=\alpha u+\beta v\neq 0
,

|\mathrm{supp}(x)|=\begin{cases}a,&\beta=0,\\
b,&\alpha=0,\\
a+b,&\alpha\beta\neq 0.\end{cases}

Define

m_{1}(W):=\min\{|\mathrm{supp}(x)|:x\in W\setminus\{0\}\},

and choose
x_{1}\in W\setminus\{0\}
attaining
m_{1}(W)
. Then
m_{1}(W)=a
.
Set

m_{2}(W):=\min\{|\mathrm{supp}(x)|:x\in W\setminus(\mathbb{R}x_{1})\}.

Note that
m_{2}(W)
is independent of the choice of
x_{1}
:
if
a<b
, then
|\mathrm{supp}(x)|=a
forces
\beta=0
, so
x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}u
and hence
m_{2}(W)=b
;
if
a=b
, then
m_{1}(W)=m_{2}(W)=a
for any such
x_{1}
.
Therefore the unordered pair
\{a,b\}
is determined by
W
.

Consequently, if
W
belonged to both
\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a,b}
and
\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a^{\prime},b^{\prime}}
, then we would have

\{a,b\}=\{a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\}
. Thus, if
\{a,b\}\neq\{a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\}
, the two orbits are disjoint.

(ii)
We have

|\mathcal{T}_{a,b}^{(d)}|=\binom{d}{a}\binom{d-a}{b}\cdot 2^{a}\cdot 2^{b}=\binom{d}{a}\binom{d-a}{b}\,2^{a+b}.

On the other hand, Proposition
4.5
shows that
|\pi^{-1}(W)|
is independent of
W\in\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a,b}
.
Hence

N_{d}(a,b)=|\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a,b}|=\frac{|\mathcal{T}_{a,b}^{(d)}|}{|\pi^{-1}(W)|}=\binom{d}{a}\binom{d-a}{b}\,2^{a+b}\times\begin{cases}\frac{1}{4},&\text{if }a<b,\\[2.84526pt]
\frac{1}{8},&\text{if }a=b,\end{cases}

which simplifies to the stated formula.
∎

By Proposition
4.3
, a
B_{d}
-invariant set
X
is a
\mathrm{TFF}_{2}
if and only if
F_{X}

takes the same value at two points. We measure this discrepancy by the following quantity.

For an orbit
X\subset G_{2,d}
, define

\Delta(X):=F_{X}(e_{1})-F_{X}\Bigl(\frac{e_{1}+e_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}\Bigr).

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
(a_{t},b_{t})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
(
t=1,\dots,m
) be pairwise distinct pairs satisfying
1\leq a_{t}\leq b_{t}
and
a_{t}+b_{t}\leq d
, and set

X:=\bigsqcup_{t=1}^{m}\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a_{t},b_{t}}\subset G_{2,d}.

Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

X
is a
\mathrm{TFF}_{2}
.

(ii)

\sum_{t=1}^{m}N_{d}(a_{t},b_{t})\,\Delta\!\bigl(\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a_{t},b_{t}}\bigr)=0.

Proof.

Each
\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a_{t},b_{t}}
is a
B_{d}
-orbit and hence
B_{d}
-invariant, so their disjoint union

X=\bigsqcup_{t=1}^{m}\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a_{t},b_{t}}
is also
B_{d}
-invariant.
Therefore, by Proposition
4.3
,

(4.1)

X\ \text{is a $\mathrm{TFF}_{2}$}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad F_{X}(e_{1})=F_{X}\Bigl(\frac{e_{1}+e_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}\Bigr).

On the other hand,
|X|=\sum_{t=1}^{m}N_{d}(a_{t},b_{t})
, and

F_{X}(x)=\frac{1}{|X|}\sum_{W\in X}\|P_{W}x\|^{4}=\frac{1}{\sum_{t=1}^{m}N_{d}(a_{t},b_{t})}\sum_{t=1}^{m}N_{d}(a_{t},b_{t})\,F_{\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a_{t},b_{t}}}(x).

Applying this to
x=e_{1}
and
x=(e_{1}+e_{2})/\sqrt{2}
and taking the difference yields

\Delta(X)=\frac{1}{\sum_{t=1}^{m}N_{d}(a_{t},b_{t})}\sum_{t=1}^{m}N_{d}(a_{t},b_{t})\,\Delta\!\bigl(\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a_{t},b_{t}}\bigr).

Since the denominator is positive, combining this with (
4.1
) gives the claim.
∎

In particular, for a single orbit
X=\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a,b}
, the
\mathrm{TFF}_{2}
condition is equivalent to
\Delta(X)=0
.
Thus, it suffices to compute
\Delta(\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a,b})
explicitly.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
a,b,d\in\mathbb{N}
with
a+b\leq d
. Then

(4.2)

F_{\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a,b}}(e_{1})=\frac{a+b}{ab\,d}.

(4.3)

F_{\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a,b}}\Bigl(\frac{e_{1}+e_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}\Bigr)=\frac{8ab+(d-4)(a+b)}{2ab\,d(d-1)}.

Moreover,

(4.4)

\Delta\bigl(\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a,b}\bigr)=\frac{(d+2)(a+b)-8ab}{2ab\,d(d-1)}.

Proof.

Write
\mathcal{O}:=\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a,b}
and
\mathcal{T}:=\mathcal{T}^{(d)}_{a,b}
.
By Proposition
4.5
, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have

(4.5)

F_{\mathcal{O}}(x)=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{O}|}\sum_{W\in\mathcal{O}}\|P_{W}x\|^{4}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}|}\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}}\|P_{W_{\tau}}x\|^{4}.

Moreover,

(4.6)

|\mathcal{T}|=\binom{d}{a}\binom{d-a}{b}\,2^{a+b}.

We evaluate (
4.5
) at the two vectors

x_{1}:=e_{1},\qquad x_{2}:=\frac{e_{1}+e_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}.

Fix
\tau=(A,B,\varepsilon,\delta)\in\mathcal{T}
, and write

u:=u_{A,\varepsilon}
,
v:=v_{B,\delta}
, and
W_{\tau}=\mathrm{span}(u,v)
.
Since
A\cap B=\varnothing
, we have
u\perp v
and
\|u\|=\|v\|=1
, hence

(4.7)

\|P_{W_{\tau}}x\|^{2}=\langle x,u\rangle^{2}+\langle x,v\rangle^{2},\qquad\|P_{W_{\tau}}x\|^{4}=\langle x,u\rangle^{4}+\langle x,v\rangle^{4}+2\langle x,u\rangle^{2}\langle x,v\rangle^{2}.

1. Proof of (
4.2
).

We have

\langle e_{1},u\rangle=\begin{cases}\varepsilon_{1}/\sqrt{a},&\text{if }1\in A,\\
0,&\text{if }1\notin A,\end{cases}\qquad\langle e_{1},v\rangle=\begin{cases}\delta_{1}/\sqrt{b},&\text{if }1\in B,\\
0,&\text{if }1\notin B.\end{cases}

So

\|P_{W_{\tau}}e_{1}\|^{4}=\begin{cases}1/a^{2},&\text{if }1\in A,\\
1/b^{2},&\text{if }1\in B,\\
0,&\text{if }1\notin A\cup B.\end{cases}

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}}\|P_{W_{\tau}}e_{1}\|^{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a^{2}}\,|\{\tau\in\mathcal{T}:1\in A\}|+\frac{1}{b^{2}}\,|\{\tau\in\mathcal{T}:1\in B\}|,

\displaystyle|\{\tau\in\mathcal{T}:1\in A\}|

\displaystyle=\binom{d-1}{a-1}\binom{d-a}{b}\,2^{a+b},

\displaystyle|\{\tau\in\mathcal{T}:1\in B\}|

\displaystyle=\binom{d-1}{b-1}\binom{d-b}{a}\,2^{a+b}.

Combining this with (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) yields

F_{\mathcal{O}}(e_{1})=\frac{1}{a^{2}}\cdot\frac{\binom{d-1}{a-1}}{\binom{d}{a}}+\frac{1}{b^{2}}\cdot\frac{\binom{d-1}{b-1}}{\binom{d}{b}}=\frac{a+b}{ab\,d}.

2. Proof of (
4.3
).

Fix
\tau=(A,B,\varepsilon,\delta)\in\mathcal{T}
, and write

u:=u_{A,\varepsilon}
,
v:=v_{B,\delta}
, and
W_{\tau}=\mathrm{span}(u,v)
.
Set
x:=x_{2}
.

(AA) The case
\{1,2\}\subset A
.
Then
\langle x,v\rangle=0
and

\langle x,u\rangle=(\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2})/\sqrt{2a}.

Hence

\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}^{A}}(\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2})^{4}=2^{a-2}\!\!\sum_{(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})\in\{\pm 1\}^{2}}\!(\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2})^{4}=32\cdot 2^{a-2}.

Thus for fixed
(A,B)
,

\sum_{\varepsilon}\sum_{\delta}\|P_{W_{\tau}}x\|^{4}=2^{b}\cdot\frac{32\cdot 2^{a-2}}{4a^{2}}=\frac{2^{a+b+1}}{a^{2}},

and the number of such pairs
(A,B)
is
\binom{d-2}{a-2}\binom{d-a}{b}
.

(BB) The case
\{1,2\}\subset B
.
Similarly,

\sum_{\varepsilon}\sum_{\delta}\|P_{W_{\tau}}x\|^{4}=\frac{2^{a+b+1}}{b^{2}},\qquad\#\{(A,B)\}=\binom{d-2}{a}\binom{d-a-2}{b-2}.

(AB) The case
\{1,2\}
is split, one element in
A
and one in
B
.
For instance, if
1\in A
and
2\in B
, then

\langle x,u\rangle=\varepsilon_{1}/\sqrt{2a}

and

\langle x,v\rangle=\delta_{2}/\sqrt{2b}.

By (
4.7
),

\|P_{W_{\tau}}x\|^{4}=\Bigl(\frac{1}{2a}+\frac{1}{2b}\Bigr)^{2}=\frac{1}{4a^{2}}+\frac{1}{4b^{2}}+\frac{1}{2ab},

and hence

\sum_{\varepsilon}\sum_{\delta}\|P_{W_{\tau}}x\|^{4}=2^{a+b}\Bigl(\frac{1}{4a^{2}}+\frac{1}{4b^{2}}+\frac{1}{2ab}\Bigr).

The number of such pairs
(A,B)
equals

2\binom{d-2}{a-1}\binom{d-a-1}{b-1}
.

(AC) The case one of
\{1,2\}
lies in
A
and the other lies in
[d]\setminus(A\cup B)
.
For instance, if
1\in A
and
2\notin A\cup B
, then

\langle x,u\rangle=\varepsilon_{1}/\sqrt{2a}

and
\langle x,v\rangle=0
, so

\sum_{\varepsilon}\sum_{\delta}\|P_{W_{\tau}}x\|^{4}=2^{a+b}\cdot\frac{1}{4a^{2}},\qquad\#\{(A,B)\}=2\binom{d-2}{a-1}\binom{d-a-1}{b}.

(BC) The case one of
\{1,2\}
lies in
B
and the other lies in
[d]\setminus(A\cup B)
.
Similarly,

\sum_{\varepsilon}\sum_{\delta}\|P_{W_{\tau}}x\|^{4}=2^{a+b}\cdot\frac{1}{4b^{2}},\qquad\#\{(A,B)\}=2\binom{d-2}{a}\binom{d-a-2}{b-1}.

In the remaining case
\{1,2\}\cap(A\cup B)=\varnothing
, we have
\|P_{W_{\tau}}x\|^{4}=0
.

Substituting these contributions into (
4.5
) and using (
4.6
), we obtain

F_{\mathcal{O}}(x)=\frac{8ab+(d-4)(a+b)}{2ab\,d(d-1)}.

Finally, combining the results of 1 and 2 yields

\Delta\bigl(\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a,b}\bigr)=\frac{a+b}{ab\,d}-\frac{8ab+(d-4)(a+b)}{2ab\,d(d-1)}=\frac{(d+2)(a+b)-8ab}{2ab\,d(d-1)}.

∎

Theorem 4.9
.

Assume that
(d_{0};a_{0},b_{0})\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
satisfies

(d_{0}+2)(a_{0}+b_{0})=8a_{0}b_{0},\qquad 1\leq a_{0}\leq b_{0},\qquad a_{0}+b_{0}\leq d_{0}.

For any integer
s\geq 1
, set

d:=(d_{0}+2)s-2,\qquad a:=a_{0}s,\qquad b:=b_{0}s.

Then

X:=\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a,b}\subset G_{2,d}

is an equal-weight
\mathrm{TFF}_{2}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.8
, the single orbit
X=\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a,b}
is a
\mathrm{TFF}_{2}
if and only if

\Delta(X)=\Delta\bigl(\mathcal{O}^{(d)}_{a,b}\bigr)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(d+2)(a+b)=8ab.

On the other hand,

d+2=(d_{0}+2)s,\qquad a+b=(a_{0}+b_{0})s,\qquad ab=a_{0}b_{0}\,s^{2},

and hence

(d+2)(a+b)=(d_{0}+2)(a_{0}+b_{0})\,s^{2}=8a_{0}b_{0}\,s^{2}=8ab.

Thus
\Delta(X)=0
, proving the claim. Moreover,

d-a-b=(d_{0}+2-a_{0}-b_{0})s-2\geq 2s-2\geq 0

follows from
a_{0}+b_{0}\leq d_{0}
.
∎

5.
Non-existence problem for certain tight
2
-fusion frames on
G_{2,d}

In this section, we focus primarily on
equi-chordal tight
2
-fusion frames
(
\mathrm{ECTFF}_{2}
) on
G_{2,d}
,
and derive necessary conditions for their existence, including upper/lower bounds on the cardinality and the corresponding equality cases.
In the context of Grassmannian packings, Conway–Hardin–Sloane
[
10
]
introduced an isometric embedding into a sphere via projection matrices,
and obtained the simplex bound for equi-chordal (EC) configurations.
Using this embedding, we also summarize the relationship between design properties on the Grassmann manifold and those on the sphere.

For
V\in G_{k,d}
, let
P_{V}
denote the orthogonal projection onto
V
.
Let
\mathrm{Sym}(d)
denote the space of real symmetric
d\times d
matrices, and set

H_{0}:=\{A\in\mathrm{Sym}(d):\mathrm{tr}(A)=0\},\qquad D_{0}:=\dim H_{0}=\binom{d+1}{2}-1.

Equip
H_{0}
with the Frobenius inner product

\langle A,B\rangle_{F}:=\mathrm{tr}(AB)\qquad(A,B\in H_{0}).

Define

R_{V}:=\sqrt{\frac{d}{k(d-k)}}\Bigl(P_{V}-\frac{k}{d}I_{d}\Bigr)\in H_{0},\qquad\Phi:G_{k,d}\to S^{D_{0}-1}\subset H_{0},\quad V\mapsto R_{V}.

Theorem 5.1

(Conway–Hardin–Sloane
[
10
, Theorem 5.1]
)
.

With the above notation,
\Phi
is an isometric embedding with respect to the chordal distance.
In particular, if a finite set
\mathcal{D}\subset G_{k,d}
is equi-chordal, then

\Phi(\mathcal{D})\subset S^{D_{0}-1}
is an equidistant set on the sphere.

Corollary 5.2

(Conway–Hardin–Sloane
[
10
, Corollary 5.2]
)
.

For
\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset G_{k,d}
, the minimal chordal distance
d_{C}
satisfies

d_{C}^{\,2}\ \leq\ \frac{k(d-k)}{d}\cdot\frac{N}{N-1}.

Moreover, equality requires
N\leq D_{0}+1
, and equality holds if and only if

\Phi(V_{i})\in H_{0}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{D_{0}}
satisfy

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Phi(V_{i})=0,\qquad\langle\Phi(V_{i}),\Phi(V_{j})\rangle_{F}=-\frac{1}{N-1}\ (i\neq j),

i.e.
\{\Phi(V_{i})\}
forms a regular simplex in
S^{D_{0}-1}
.

In what follows, we show that
\Phi
sends a Grassmann
4
-design to a spherical
2
-design.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
X\subset G_{k,d}
be a Grassmann
4
-design.
Then
Y:=\Phi(X)
is a spherical
2
-design on
S^{D_{0}-1}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.2
, it suffices to show that

\sum_{V,W\in X}Q_{\ell}^{(D_{0})}(\langle R_{V},R_{W}\rangle_{F})=0\qquad(\ell=1,2).

By the definition of
R_{V}
,

\langle R_{V},R_{W}\rangle_{F}=\frac{d}{k(d-k)}\left(\mathrm{tr}(P_{V}P_{W})-\frac{k^{2}}{d}\right).

Also
\mathrm{tr}(P_{V}P_{W})=\mathrm{tr}(AA^{\mathsf{T}})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sigma_{i}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}y_{i}(V,W)
, so

c_{2}=\frac{1}{k}\mathrm{tr}(P_{V}P_{W})
.
Substituting this into
P_{(2)}=\frac{k}{k-d}-\frac{d}{k-d}c_{2}
yields

P_{(2)}(V,W)=\frac{d}{k(d-k)}\left(\mathrm{tr}(P_{V}P_{W})-\frac{k^{2}}{d}\right)=\langle R_{V},R_{W}\rangle_{F}.

Hence the case
\ell=1
is exactly
\sum_{V,W\in X}P_{(2)}(V,W)=0
,
which follows from the assumption that
X
is a Grassmann
4
-design (and thus a
2
-design).

For
\ell=2
, using

Q_{2}^{(D_{0})}(x)=\frac{D_{0}x^{2}-1}{D_{0}-1},

we obtain

\sum_{V,W\in X}Q_{2}^{(D_{0})}(\langle R_{V},R_{W}\rangle_{F})=\frac{D_{0}}{D_{0}-1}\sum_{V,W\in X}P_{(2)}(V,W)^{2}-\frac{|X|^{2}}{D_{0}-1}.

Here we may expand

\displaystyle P_{(2)}(V,W)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{D_{0}}+\frac{4(d-2k)^{2}}{\,k(d-2)(d+4)(d-k)\,}\,P_{(2)}(V,W)

\displaystyle+\frac{d^{2}(k+2)(d-k+2)}{\,3k(d+2)(d+4)(d-k)\,}\,P_{(4)}(V,W)+\frac{2d^{2}(k-1)(d-k-1)}{\,3k(d-2)(d-1)(d-k)\,}\,P_{(2,2)}(V,W).

Since
X
is a Grassmann
4
-design, we have

\sum_{V,W\in X}P_{(2)}(V,W)=\sum_{V,W\in X}P_{(4)}(V,W)=\sum_{V,W\in X}P_{(2,2)}(V,W)=0,

and hence

\sum_{V,W\in X}P_{(2)}(V,W)^{2}=\frac{|X|^{2}}{D_{0}}.

Substituting this shows that the
\ell=2
sum also vanishes.
∎

The number
\binom{d+1}{2}
is the absolute lower bound for Grassmann
4
-designs on
G_{k,d}
(see e.g.
[
4
]
).

Definition 5.4
.

[
2
]

Let
d\geq 4
.
A finite set
X\subset G_{k,d}
is called a
tight Grassmann
4
-design
if

X
is a Grassmann
4
-design and

|X|=\binom{d+1}{2}.

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
X\subset G_{k,d}
be a tight Grassmann
4
-design.
Then
X
is equi-chordal.

Proof.

If
X
is a tight Grassmann
4
-design, then
|X|=\binom{d+1}{2}=D_{0}+1
.
By Lemma
5.3
,
Y=\Phi(X)
is a spherical
2
-design on
S^{D_{0}-1}
with
|Y|=D_{0}+1
.
Hence
\sum_{y\in Y}y=0
, so
Y
achieves the equality in Corollary
5.2
.
Therefore
Y
forms a regular simplex in
S^{D_{0}-1}
, and in particular is equidistant.
Since
\Phi
is an isometric embedding with respect to the chordal distance,
X
is equi-chordal.
∎

The following theorem provides basic cardinality constraints for
\mathrm{ECTFF}_{2}
in
G_{2,d}
.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
d\geq 4
, and let
X=\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset G_{2,d}
be an
\mathrm{ECTFF}_{2}
.
Then

\frac{d^{2}}{4}\leq N\leq\binom{d+1}{2}.

Moreover, equality in the lower bound holds if and only if
X
is an
\mathrm{EITFF}_{2}
on
G_{2,d}
,
and equality in the upper bound holds if and only if
X
is a tight
4
-design on
G_{2,d}
.

Proof.

For
V,W\in G_{2,d}
, set

e_{1}(V,W):=y_{1}(V,W)+y_{2}(V,W),\qquad e_{2}(V,W):=y_{1}(V,W)y_{2}(V,W).

Since
X
is equi-chordal,
e_{1}(V_{i},V_{j})
is constant for
i\neq j
. Set

e_{1,0}:=e_{1}(V_{i},V_{j})\qquad(i\neq j).

Also define

\overline{e_{2}(N)}:=\frac{1}{N^{2}-N}\sum_{i\neq j}e_{2}(V_{i},V_{j}).

(i) Lower bound.

Since
X
is an equal-weight tight
2
-fusion frame, Theorem
2.17
(iii) applied with
t=2

(and weights
\omega_{i}=1/N
) yields the moment identities for the one-row zonal polynomials
P_{(2)}
and
P_{(4)}
:

(5.1)

\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}P_{(2)}(V_{i},V_{j})=0,\qquad\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}P_{(4)}(V_{i},V_{j})=0.

By (
2.1
) (with
k=2
), we obtain

P_{(2)}(V,W)=\frac{4-d\,e_{1}(V,W)}{2(2-d)}.

Since
e_{1}(V_{i},V_{j})=e_{1,0}
for all
i\neq j
, the first identity in (
5.1
) gives

0=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}P_{(2)}(V_{i},V_{j})=N+\sum_{i\neq j}P_{(2)}(V_{i},V_{j})=N+(N^{2}-N)\,\frac{4-d\,e_{1,0}}{2(2-d)}.

Solving for
e_{1,0}
yields

(5.2)

e_{1,0}=\frac{2(2N-d)}{d(N-1)}.

Next, by (
2.2
) (with
k=2
), we rewrite
P_{(4)}
in the variables
(e_{1},e_{2})
:

P_{(4)}^{\prime}(e_{1},e_{2}):=1-\frac{d+2}{2}e_{1}+\frac{3(d+2)(d+4)}{64}e_{1}^{2}-\frac{(d+2)(d+4)}{16}e_{2},\qquad P_{(4)}(e_{1},e_{2}):=\frac{8}{d(d-2)}\,P_{(4)}^{\prime}(e_{1},e_{2}).

Since
P_{(4)}
has degree one in
e_{2}
, we have

\sum_{i\neq j}P_{(4)}\bigl(e_{1,0},e_{2}(V_{i},V_{j})\bigr)=(N^{2}-N)\,P_{(4)}\bigl(e_{1,0},\overline{e_{2}(N)}\bigr).

Therefore, the second identity in (
5.1
) becomes

0=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}P_{(4)}(V_{i},V_{j})=N+(N^{2}-N)\,P_{(4)}\bigl(e_{1,0},\overline{e_{2}(N)}\bigr),

and solving for
\overline{e_{2}(N)}
yields

(5.3)

\overline{e_{2}(N)}=\frac{d^{2}(d+2)+2d(d^{2}-4d-4)N-4(d-6)N^{2}}{d^{2}(d+2)(N-1)^{2}}.

For each
i\neq j
, we have
y_{1}(V_{i},V_{j}),y_{2}(V_{i},V_{j})\geq 0
and

y_{1}(V_{i},V_{j})+y_{2}(V_{i},V_{j})=e_{1,0}
; hence by the AM–GM inequality,

(5.4)

0\leq e_{2}(V_{i},V_{j})=y_{1}(V_{i},V_{j})y_{2}(V_{i},V_{j})\leq\left(\frac{e_{1,0}}{2}\right)^{2}.

Averaging (
5.4
) over
i\neq j
gives

0\leq\overline{e_{2}(N)}\leq\frac{e_{1,0}^{2}}{4}.

Substituting (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) and simplifying, we obtain

\frac{e_{1,0}^{2}}{4}-\overline{e_{2}(N)}=\frac{-2N(d-2)(d^{2}-4N)}{d^{2}(d+2)(N-1)^{2}}.

The denominator is positive and the left-hand side is
\geq 0
, hence
d^{2}-4N\leq 0
, i.e.

N\geq\frac{d^{2}}{4}.

If
N=\frac{d^{2}}{4}
, then
\overline{e_{2}(N)}=\frac{e_{1,0}^{2}}{4}
.
Since each term satisfies
e_{2}(V_{i},V_{j})\leq\frac{e_{1,0}^{2}}{4}
, attaining the average maximum forces

e_{2}(V_{i},V_{j})=\frac{e_{1,0}^{2}}{4}\qquad(i\neq j).

Thus
y_{1}(V_{i},V_{j})=y_{2}(V_{i},V_{j})=\frac{e_{1,0}}{2}
for all
i\neq j
, so
X
is equi-isoclinic, i.e. an
\mathrm{EITFF}_{2}
.
Conversely, if
X
is an
\mathrm{EITFF}_{2}
, then the above computation implies
N=\frac{d^{2}}{4}
.

(ii) Upper bound.

The upper bound
N\leq D_{0}+1=\binom{d+1}{2}
follows from Corollary
5.2
. Assume further that
N=\binom{d+1}{2}
.
In this case, to show that
X
is a Grassmann
4
-design it suffices to verify

\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}P_{(2,2)}(V_{i},V_{j})=0,

since the moment identities (
5.1
) already hold.
Similarly, by (
2.3
) (with
k=2
) we rewrite
P_{(2,2)}
in the variables
(e_{1},e_{2})
and normalize it by

P_{(2,2)}(2,1)=1
:

P_{(2,2)}^{\prime}(e_{1},e_{2}):=1-\frac{d-1}{2}e_{1}+\frac{(d-2)(d-1)}{2}e_{2},\qquad P_{(2,2)}(e_{1},e_{2}):=\frac{2}{(d-2)(d-3)}\,P_{(2,2)}^{\prime}(e_{1},e_{2}).

Since
P_{(2,2)}
has degree one in
e_{2}
, we obtain

\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}P_{(2,2)}(V_{i},V_{j})=N+(N^{2}-N)\,P_{(2,2)}\bigl(e_{1,0},\overline{e_{2}(N)}\bigr).

Substituting (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) and simplifying, we obtain

\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}P_{(2,2)}(V_{i},V_{j})=\frac{3N^{2}(d-2)^{2}\bigl(d(d+1)-2N\bigr)}{d^{2}(N-1)(d-3)(d+2)}.

Hence, when
N=\frac{d(d+1)}{2}
this sum is
0
, and
X
is a Grassmann
4
-design. Moreover,
|X|=D_{0}+1=\binom{d+1}{2}
,
and since
X
is a Grassmann
4
-design, it follows from Definition
5.4
that
X
is a tight Grassmann
4
-design.

Conversely, if
X
is a tight
4
-design, then
|X|=D_{0}+1=\binom{d+1}{2}
and
Corollary
5.5
implies that
X
is equi-chordal.
Also, a Grassmann
4
-design in particular satisfies (
5.1
), so
X
is a tight
2
-fusion frame.
Therefore
X
is an
\mathrm{ECTFF}_{2}
.
∎

As a consequence, we obtain the non-existence of
\mathrm{EITFF}_{2}
on
G_{2,d}
in odd dimensions.

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
d
be odd and let
t>1
.
Then there exists no
\mathrm{EITFF}_{t}
on
G_{2,d}
.

Remark 5.8
.

Let
d=2n
and identify
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
with
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Each complex line
\mathbb{C}z\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
determines a real plane

V(z):=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{z,iz\}\in G_{2,2n}.

Here a SIC-POVM in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
means a set of unit vectors
z_{1},\dots,z_{n^{2}}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that

|z_{i}^{*}z_{j}|^{2}=1/(n+1)
for all
i\neq j
.
Under this correspondence, a SIC-POVM yields an
\mathrm{EITFF}_{2}
on
G_{2,2n}
of size
n^{2}
, and conversely.
See
[
17
]
for details.

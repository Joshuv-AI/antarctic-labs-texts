Transient paradoxical dissociation of alveolar volume indices after single-lung transplantation in a patient with severe emphysema: A case report
Authors: Satoshi Sagai, Mitsuko Murata, Akemi Ishii, Susumu Sato, Ichiro Sakanoue, Yumiko Saito, Miki Nagao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractMeasurement of the alveolar volume (VA) yields two distinct values, VA-MB and VA-SB; theoretically, the VA-MB should exceed the VA-SB. This report describes a case in which the VA-MB was less than the VA-SB following unilateral lung transplantation. After the exclusion of mechanical errors and suboptimal procedural techniques, further analysis revealed that, following unilateral lung transplantation, substantial disparities in respiratory function between the left and right lungs, as seen in severe emphysema, may trigger a physiological mechanism that reverses VA-SB and VA-MB. As a result, the pulmonary diffusing capacity of each lung may not be accurately reflected by these measurements.Keywords: Alveolar volume, Diffusing capacity, Emphysema, Lung transplantation, Bronchiolitis obliterans

Introduction

Alveolar volume is evaluated during diffusion capacity (DL) measurements and yields two distinct values [1]: VA-MB, representing inspiratory volume (VI) plus residual volume (RV-MB) measured at functional residual capacity using multiple-breath helium (He) dilution, and VA-SB, measured via He gas dilution during the DL assessment [2]. Theoretically, the VA-MB typically exceeds or equals the VA-SB; if the VA-MB is less than the VA-SB, measurement errors or paradoxical phenomena are likely.

Case report

A 61-year-old male ex-smoker (31 pack-years) with severe early-onset chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) previously underwent lung volume reduction surgery (LVRS) six years earlier, resulting in clinical improvement. After the patient started to experience recurrent COPD exacerbations, he received a single left lung transplantation (LTx) from a brain-dead donor. The clinical course of pulmonary function, perfusion scintigraphy, and other calculated parameters up to 36 months post-LTx are presented in Fig. 1, with the date of LTx defined as X. As illustrated in Fig. 1A, total lung capacity (TLC) and VA-MB remained similar throughout the observation period, with a slight decrease at X+2 months. In contrast, VA-SB increased markedly at X+2 months, surpassing both TLC and VA-MB. This paradoxical reversal persisted until X+24 months and subsequently resolved. Repeated technical error checks revealed no procedural or mechanical issues that could explain why the VA-SB exceeded the VA-MB.

Clinical course of lung function measurements and blood flow before and after lung transplantation. Taking the date of LTx as X, the time course of the TLC, VA-MB, and VA-SB (A); VC, VI, FEV1, RV-MB, and RV-SB (B); FIHe, FAHe, and FAHe/FIHe (C); and blood flow (D) are presented. In Fig. 1C, FIHe may remain almost constant, whereas a lower FAHe indicates a greater RV-MB. The FAHe/FIHe ratio showed a similar trend to that of FAHe.

The VC and forced expiratory volume in 1 s (FEV1) increased after LTx and remained stable until X+24 months. However, both declined at X+36 months (Fig. 1B). Significant decreases in the FEV1 of more than 20% were observed at X+36 months, indicating progression to clinical obstructive bronchiolitis obliterans (BO) [3]. The RV-SB increased significantly at X+2 months but then decreased after X+24 months.

To further evaluate the behavior of the VA-SB and VA-MB, changes in the inspired fraction of helium (FIHe), the alveolar fraction of helium (FAHe), and the FAHe/FIHe ratio were assessed during the DL test (Fig. 1C) [4,5]. No contraindicating findings were observed, and these parameters changed in parallel with those of the RV-SB and RV-MB.

Serial post-LTx perfusion scintigraphy revealed that blood flow to the transplanted left lung increased to 84.2% at X+5 months but gradually decreased to 69.0% at X+36 months (Fig. 1D). This reduction in perfusion coincided with a marked decrease in the FEV1, which was consistent with progressive BO and reduced ventilatory capacity of the transplanted lung [6].

The DLCO and DLCO-SB immediately increased after LTx but decreased after X+24 months. Moreover, at X+5 months, the DLCO was less than the DLCO-SB because the patient's VA-MB was lower than their VA-SB. The KCO demonstrated a similar temporal pattern, with a transient increase followed by a decrease after X+24 months (Fig. 1D).

The clinical course of the imaging findings in both lungs, including chest radiographs (X-rays), is shown in Fig. 2. At X+5 months, diaphragm elevation during maximal expiration was weaker, as was leftward migration of the thorax and pulmonary contraction, than at X+24 months.

Clinical course of the case as depicted on chest X-ray images during inspiration and expiration. Taking the date of LTx as X, chest X-ray (XP) images at X+5 months and X+24 months are presented.

Following single-lung transplantation, the total lung capacity (TLC) and alveolar volume of the left lung are generally expected to decrease as the healthy graft replaces hyperexpanded emphysematous lung. In this case, however, limitations in measuring residual volume (RV-MB) during functional residual capacity (FRC) assessment were noted, as the FRC measurement was performed separately from the pulmonary diffusion capacity evaluation. Combined with longitudinal imaging findings, the clinical course suggests that the lung volume measurements may not have accurately represented the patient's true physiological lung volume.

Discussion

Compared with the multiple-breath method, single-breath measurements cannot adequately reflect lung volumes in hypoventilated regions because of ventilation inhomogeneity, typically resulting in VA-MB > VA-SB [7]. Ventilation inequality is further accentuated in patients with severe emphysema or obstructive lung diseases, thereby amplifying the discrepancy between the VA-MB and VA-SB [6,8]. The present case showed VA-MB > VA-SB before and 24 months after left single LTx, but the relationship was reversed to VA-MB < VA-SB from X+2 to X+11 months. Because these paradoxical findings are theoretically implausible, a technical issue was initially suspected. The VI should be approximately 90% or more of the VC [4], and deviations warrant repeated testing. All the VI measurements consistently exceeded 90% of the VC without significant variability, effectively excluding technical or procedural error.

The underlying mechanism for this paradoxical dissociation can be explained through a step-by-step process resulting from extreme interlung mechanical heterogeneity after single LTx [9]. First, VA-MB was underestimated due to severe ventilation heterogeneity. VA-MB is derived from FRC measured during steady-state tidal breathing. In the presence of severe airflow obstruction and loss of elastic recoil in the native emphysematous right lung, helium tracer gas equilibration across poorly ventilated areas requires extended rebreathing time. Standard clinical testing protocols ended rebreathing before full equilibration was reached in the native lung, causing a substantial underestimation of true total FRC and VA-MB.

Second, VA-SB was artifactually overestimated during single-breath sampling. VA-SB is measured during a rapid maximal inhalation followed by a 10-s breath-hold. The inspired tracer gas preferentially entered the compliant transplanted left lung. During subsequent forced exhalation, marked interlung pressure differentials caused dynamic gas redistribution (pendelluft) and delayed gas emptying from the native lung into the alveolar sample bag. Exhaled air from the transplanted lung was partially redirected toward the native lung, resulting in gas-mixing artifacts that contaminated the alveolar sample and artifactually elevated VA-SB. Imaging findings, including limited diaphragmatic elevation, reduced leftward thoracic displacement, and insufficient lung contraction, support this interpretation. Posttransplant perfusion scintigraphy demonstrated significant left-to-right blood flow asymmetry (86.1% versus 13.9%), indicating considerable interlung functional heterogeneity.

Third, as BO gradually developed in the graft over 24–36 months, the ventilatory and mechanical disparity between the two lungs decreased. Reductions in VC, FEV1, and graft perfusion narrowed functional differences between the transplanted and native lungs. This mechanical convergence resolved the paradoxical VA-MB < VA-SB pattern back to baseline levels.

Owing to the nature of single-breath DL measurements, sampled gas may not accurately represent both lungs under conditions of extreme ventilation asymmetry. The relationship between diffusion indices and disease status, independent of the VA, warrants further investigation.

Conclusion

This case illustrates a transient paradoxical dissociation of alveolar volume indices (VA-MB < VA-SB) in a patient with severe emphysema following single-lung transplantation. The contrast in mechanical properties between the transplanted healthy graft and the native emphysematous lung resulted in an artifactual overestimation of the VA-SB, leading to a paradoxical pattern of VA-MB < VA-SB. These findings underscore significant methodological limitations in interpreting single-breath diffusing capacity and volume measurements in patients with extreme inter-lung functional heterogeneity.

Abstract
. We introduce the μ \mu -topological stability . This is a type of stability depending on the measure μ \mu different from the set-valued approach [ 6 ] . We prove that the map f f is m p m_{p} -topologically stable if and only if p p is a topologically stable point ( m p m_{p} is the Dirac measure supported on p p ). On closed manifolds of dimension ≥ 2 \geq 2 we prove that every μ \mu -topologically stable map has the μ \mu -shadowing property for finitely supported measures μ \mu . Moreover the μ \mu -topological stability is invariant under topological conjugacy or restriction to compact invariant sets of full measure. We also prove for expansive maps that the set of measures μ \mu for which the map is μ \mu -topologically stable is convex. We analyze the relationship between μ \mu -topological stability for absolutely continuous measures. In the nonatomic case we show that the μ \mu -topological stability implies the set-valued stability approach in [ 6 ] . Finally, we show that every expansive map with the weak μ \mu -shadowing property (c.f. [ 7 ] ) is μ \mu -topologically stable.

1.1.
Background and motivations

Let
X
be a metric space. Denote by
C^{0}(X)
the set of continuous maps from
X
to itself.
Define the
C^{0}
-distance between maps of metric spaces
l,r:Y\to X
by

d_{C^{0}}(l,r)=\sup_{y\in Y}d(l(y),r(y)).

If
Y\subset X
, then we denote the inclusion by
i_{Y}:Y\hookrightarrow X
.
In particular,
i_{X}
is the identity of
X
.

We recall the basic definition of topological stability by Walters
[
12
]
.

Definition 1
.

We say that
f:X\to X

is topologically stable if for every
\epsilon>0
there is
\delta>0
such that for every continuous
g:X\to X
with
d_{C^{0}}(f,g)\leq\delta
there is
h:X\to X
continuous such that

d_{C^{0}}(h,i_{X})\leq\epsilon\quad\mbox{ and }\quad f\circ h=h\circ g.

Walters’s stability theorem

[
12
]
asserts that the following two properties suffice for a continuous map of a compact metric space
f:X\to X
to be topologically stable:

•

f
is
expansive
if there is
e>0
(called
expansivity constant
) such that if
x,y\in X
and
d(f^{n}(x),f^{n}(y))\leq e
for every nonnegative integer
n
, then
x=y
.

•

f
has the
shadowing property
if for every
\epsilon>0
there is
\delta>0
such that for every sequence
(x_{n})_{n\geq 0}
with
d(f(x_{n}),x_{n+1})\leq\delta
for all
n\geq 0
there is
x\in X
such that

d(f^{n}(x),x_{n})\leq\epsilon
for every
n\geq 0
.

A measurable version of this theorem was given in
[
6
]
or
[
7
]
.
Let us remind it.

A
Borel probability measure
is a
\sigma
-additive measure
\mu
defined in the Borel

-algebra
\beta_{X}
of
X
such that
\mu(X)=1
. Denote by

\mathcal{M}(X)
the set of Borel probability measures of
X
.

Denote
2^{X}
the set of subsets of a compact metric space
X
.
Maps
H:X\to 2^{X}
are called
set-valued maps
.
The domain of
H
is defined by
Dom(H)=\{x\in X:H(x)\neq\emptyset\}
.
We say that
H
is
compact-valued
if
H(x)
is compact for every
x\in X
.
We write
d_{C^{0}}(H,i_{X})\leq\epsilon
for some
\epsilon>
if
H(x)\subset B[x,\epsilon]
where
B[x,\epsilon]
denotes the closed
\epsilon
-ball around
x
.
We say that
H
is
upper semicontinuous
if for every
x\in Dom(H)
and every neighborhood
O
of

there exists
\eta>0
such that

H(y)\subset O
for every

with

.

Definition 2
.

Given
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
we call

\mu
-topologically set-valued stable if for every

there is
\delta>0
such that for every

with

there is

of

such that

\mu(Dom(H))\geq 1-\epsilon,\quad\mu\circ H=0,\quad d_{C^{0}}(H,id_{X})\leq\epsilon,\quad f\circ H=H\circ g.

Sufficient conditions for
\mu
-topologically set-valued stable can be obtained from the next definitions where

and

•

We say that
f
is
\mu
-expansive
if there is
\epsilon>0

such that

\mu\{y\in X:d(f^{n}(x),f^{n}(y))\leq\epsilon,\,\forall n\geq 0\})=0,\quad\quad\forall x\in X.

•

We say that
f
has the
\mu
-shadowing property

(for a given
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
) if for every
\epsilon>0
there are
\delta>0
and

with
\mu(B)=1
such that for every sequence
(x_{n})_{n\geq 0}
with
x_{0}\in B
and
d(f(x_{n}),x_{n+1})\leq\delta
for all
n\geq 0
there is
x\in X
such that

d(f^{n}(x),x_{n})\leq\epsilon
for every
n\geq 0
.

•

We say that

has the

-shadowing property

(for a given

) if for every
\epsilon>0
there are
\delta>0
and
B\in\beta_{X}
with
\mu(X\setminus B)\leq\epsilon
such that for every sequence
(x_{n})_{n\geq 0}
with
x_{0}\in B
and
d(f(x_{n}),x_{n+1})\leq\delta
for all
n\geq 0
there is
x\in X
such that

d(f^{n}(x),x_{n})\leq\epsilon
for every

.

Define

•

\mathcal{M}_{top*}(f)=\{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X):f\mbox{ is $\mu$-topological set-valued stable}\},

•

\mathcal{M}_{exp}(f)=\{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X):f\mbox{ is $\mu$-expansive}\},

•

\mathcal{M}_{sh}(f)=\{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X):f\mbox{ has the $\mu$-shadowing property}\}

and

•

{\small\mathcal{M}_{wsh}(f)=\{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X):f\mbox{ has the weak $\mu$-shadowing property}\}}.

The sole difference between the
\mu
-shadowing and weak

-shadowing properties is given by the set

which is full measure in the former definition and has measure at least

in the latter.
Therefore,

\mathcal{M}_{sh}(f)\subset\mathcal{M}_{wsh}(f).

But the converse inclusion is false (theorems 1 and 2 in
[
7
]
).
On the other hand, Theorem 6 in
[
7
]
(based on Theorem 3.1 in
[
6
]
)
implies the following measurable version of Walters’s stability theorem:

(1)

\mathcal{M}_{exp}(f)\cap\mathcal{M}_{wsh}(f)\subset\mathcal{M}_{top*}(f).

Now we introduce an alternative measure-theoretical version based on Walters’s concept of factor (Definition 2.6 in p. 61 of
[
13
]
).

We say that
Y\subset X
is
g
-invariant
(for some
g\in C^{0}(X)
) if
g(Y)\subset Y
.
Given compact metric spaces
X,Z
,
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
and
\nu\in\mathcal{M}(Z)
, a measurable
g:X\to Z
is

if
g_{*}(\mu)=\nu
where

g_{*}(\mu)=\mu\circ g^{-1}
is the pull-back operator.

Definition 3
.

Given
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
we say that a measure preserving
f\in C^{0}(X)
is a factor of a measure preserving

g\in C^{0}(X)
if there are
f
(resp.
g
)-invariant sets
Y_{f},Y_{g}\in\beta_{X}
with

\mu(Y_{f})=\mu(Y_{g})=1,

and a measure preserving
h:Y_{g}\to Y_{f}
such that

f\circ h=h\circ g.

In this definition,
Y_{f}
is assumed to be equipped with the

\sigma
-algebra
\{Y_{f}\cap B:B\in\beta_{X}\}
, and the restriction of the measure
\mu
to this
\sigma
-algebra (similarly for
Y_{g}
).

1.2.
Definition

Our definition will be obtained
by weakening Walters’s factor.
First, remove the measure preserving hypothesis since it is unnecessary.
Also, take
Y_{f}
to be the whole
X
.
On the other hand, we will maintain the
g
-invariant set
Y=Y_{g}
as it is fundamental for the identity
f\circ h=h\circ g
.
However, for a given
\epsilon>0
and this measurable
h:X\to X
, Lusin Theorem permits to choose
a compact
Y\subset Y_{g}

such that
\mu(Y_{g}\setminus Y)\leq\epsilon

and
h:Y\to X
is continuous.
In particular,

\mu(X\setminus Y)\leq\mu(X\setminus Y_{g})+\mu(Y_{g}\setminus Y)\leq\epsilon.

As in topological stability, we use that
\epsilon
to measure the
C^{0}
-distance between
h
and the inclusion
i_{Y}
.

Combining these properties with the definition of topological stability we arrive the following definition.

Definition 4
.

Given
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
we call
f\in C^{0}(X)

\mu
-topologically stable if for every
\epsilon>0
there is
\delta>0
such that for every
g\in C^{0}(X)
with
d_{C^{0}}(f,g)\leq\delta

there are a compact
g
-invariant set
Y\subset X
with

\mu(X\setminus Y)\leq\epsilon
and

h:Y\to X
continuous such that

d_{C^{0}}(h,i_{Y})\leq\epsilon\quad\mbox{ and }\quad f\circ h=h\circ g.

We denote

\mathcal{M}_{top}(f)=\{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X):f\mbox{ is $\mu$-topologically stable}\}.

A related example is given below.

Example 1
.

If
f\in C^{0}(X)
is topologically stable, then

(2)

\mathcal{M}_{top}(f)=\mathcal{M}(X).

We don’t know if, conversely, every
f\in C^{0}(X)
satisfying (
2
) is topologically stable.

1.3.
The result

We state our main result.
Previously we introduce some basic definitions.
The first one is given in
[
5
]
.

Hereafter
X
will denote a compact metric space.
The
orbit
of
p
under
g\in C^{0}(X)
is defined by

O_{g}(p)=\{g^{n}(p):n\geq 0\}.

The closure operation is denoted by
\overline{B}
for
B\subset X
.

Definition 5
.

We say that
p\in X
is topologically stable for
f\in C^{0}(X)
if
for every
\epsilon>0
there is
\delta>0
such that for every
g\in C^{0}(X)

Denote by

the set of topologically stable points of

.

A Borel probability measure is

if there are points with positive measure
and

otherwise.
A classical example of atomic measure is the

m_{p}
for

defined by

(if

(otherwise).
We say that

if it is a finite convex combination of Dirac measures i.e.

\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{k}t_{i}m_{p_{k}},

for some

\sum_{i=1}^{k}t_{i}=1

We say that

and

.

A subset

is

•

\prec
-invariant
if

\nu\in\mathcal{M}
and
\mu\prec\nu
for

\Longrightarrow

\mu\in\mathcal{M}

•

convex
if
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}
and

\Longrightarrow

t\mu+(1-t)\nu\in\mathcal{M}
.

With these definitions we can state our main result.

Theorem
.

The following properties hold for all compact metric spaces

(1)

T(f)=\{p\in X:m_{p}\in\mathcal{M}_{top}(f)\}
.

(2)

\mathcal{M}_{top}(f)
is

(3)

\mathcal{M}_{fs}(X)\cap\mathcal{M}_{top}(f)\subset\mathcal{M}_{sh}(f)

(4)

If

\mathcal{M}_{top}(H\circ f\circ H^{-1})=H_{*}(\mathcal{M}_{top}(f))
.

(5)

If

2.
Proof of the theorem

We break it in a sequence of lemmas. The first one is Item (1) of the theorem.

Lemma 1
.

For every compact metric space
X
one has

T(f)=\{p\in X:f\mbox{ is $m_{p}$-topologically stable}\},\quad\quad\forall f\in C^{0}(X).

Proof.

Take
p\in T(f)
,
\epsilon>0
and
\delta
be given by the topological stability of
p
.
Fix
g\in C^{0}(X)
with
d_{C^{0}}(f,g)\leq\delta
and let
h:\overline{O_{g}(p)}\to X

be given by the definition of of topologically stable point.
Letting
Y=\overline{O_{g}(p)}
we get a closed
g
-invariant set
with
p\in Y
hence
m_{p}(X\setminus Y)=0\leq\epsilon
.
Moreover,
d_{C^{0}}(h,i_{Y})\leq\epsilon
and
f\circ h=h\circ g
,
therefore
f
is
m_{p}
-topologically stable.

Conversely, suppose that

f
is
m_{p}
-topologically stable and choose
\epsilon>0
.
Let
\delta>0
be given by the definition and
g\in C^{0}(X)
with
d_{C^{0}}(f,g)\leq\delta
.
We can assume
0<\epsilon<1
without loss pf generality.
Then, there are a closed
g
-invariant set
Y
with
m_{p}(X\setminus Y)\leq\epsilon
,

d_{C^{0}}(h,i_{Y})\leq\epsilon
and
f\circ h=h\circ g
.
In particular,
m_{p}(Y)\geq 1-\epsilon>0
so
p\in Y
.
Since
Y
is closed
g
-invariant,

\overline{O_{g}(p)}\subset Y
hence
h
is defined on
\overline{O_{g}(p)}
.
Denote by
h_{*}=h|_{\overline{O_{g}(p)}}
the restriction of
h
to
\overline{O_{g}(p)}
.
Then,
d_{C^{0}}(h_{*},i_{\overline{O_{g}(p)}})\leq d_{C^{0}}(h,i_{Y})\leq\epsilon

and also
f\circ h_{*}=h_{*}\circ g
proving
p\in T(f)
.
∎

Now we prove item (2).

Lemma 2
.

Let
X
be a compact metric space and
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
.
If
f\in C^{0}(X)
is
\nu
-topologically stable, then
f
is
\mu
-topologically stable,
\forall\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
with
\mu\prec\nu
.

Proof.

Fix
\epsilon>0
and
\mu\prec\nu
.
By the Radon-Nikodym Theorem we can choose
0<\epsilon^{\prime}<\epsilon
such that

B\in\beta_{X}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\nu(B)\leq\epsilon^{\prime}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mu(B)\leq\epsilon.

Let
\delta
be given by the
\nu
-topological stability of
f
for this
\epsilon^{\prime}
.

Suppose that
g\in C^{0}(X)
and
d_{C^{0}}(f,g)\leq\delta.

Then, there is a compact
g
-invariant set
Y\subset X

with
\nu(X\setminus Y)\leq\epsilon^{\prime}
and
a continuous
h:Y\to X
such that

d_{C^{0}}(h,i_{Y})\leq\epsilon^{\prime}
and
f\circ h=h\circ g
.
It follows from the choice of
\epsilon^{\prime}
that

d_{C^{0}}(h,i_{Y})\leq\epsilon
and also
\mu(X\setminus Y)\leq\epsilon
. This completes the proof.
∎

Given
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
we denote by
A(\mu)=\{p\in X:\mu(\{p\})>0\}
the set of atoms of
\mu
. The following short lemma looks to be known we include the proof for completeness.

Lemma 3
.

A(\mu)=\{p\in X:m_{p}\prec\mu\}
.

Proof.

Suppose
p\not\prec\mu
.
Then,
\mu(B)=0
for some
B\in\beta_{X}
with
m_{p}(B)>0
.
So,
p\in B
thus
\mu(\{p\})\leq\mu(B)=0
hence
p\notin A(\mu)
.
Therefore,
A(\mu)\subset\{p\in X:m_{p}\prec\mu\}
.

Now suppose
m_{p}\prec\mu
but
\mu(\{p\})=0
.
Then,
m_{p}(\{p\})=0
which is impossible therefore
\{p\in X:m_{p}\prec\mu\}\subset A(\mu)
.
∎

Next we prove that the atoms of measures in
\mathcal{M}_{top}(f)
are topologically stable points of
f
.

Lemma 4
.

If
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{top}(f)
, then
A(\mu)\subset T(f)
.

Proof.

Take
p\in A(\mu)
. Then,
m_{p}\prec\mu
by Lemma
3
so

m_{p}\in\mathcal{M}_{top}(f)
by Lemma
2
thus

p\in T(f)
by Lemma
1
.
∎

Now we prove Item (3).

Lemma 5
.

Let
f:M\to M
be a homeomorphism of a closed manifold of dimension
\geq 2
, If

\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{fs}(X)
and
f
is
\mu
-topologically stable, then
f
has the
\mu
-shadowing property.

Proof.

We will use the following definition from
[
8
]
.
We say that
p\in M
is
shadowable
if
for every
\epsilon>0
there is
\delta>0
such that
for every sequence
(x_{i})_{i\geq 0}
with
x_{0}=p
and
d(f(x_{i}),x_{i+1})\leq\delta
for every
i\geq 0
there is
x\in M
such that
d(f^{i}(x),x_{i})\leq\epsilon
for every
i\geq 0
.
Denote by
Sh(f)
the set of shadowable points of
f
.
The support
supp(\mu)
of
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(M)
is the set
of points
x
such that
\mu(B[x,\delta])>0
for every
\delta>0
.

Since
\mu
is finitely supported,
supp(\mu)=A(\mu)
.
Then,

by Lemma
4
.
Since

is a closed manifold of dimension
\geq 2
,

so

has the
\mu
-shadowing property by Lemma 2.5 in
[

]
.
∎

Next we prove Item (4) i.e. the invariance of the
\mu
-topological stability under topological conjugacy.

Lemma 6
.

Let
X
be a compact metric space and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
.
If
f\in C^{0}(X)
is
\mu
-topologically stable, then
H\circ f\circ H^{-1}
is
H_{*}(\mu)
-topologically stable for every homeomorphism of compact metric spaces
H:X\to Z
.

Proof.

Fix
\epsilon>0
. Since
X
and
Z
are compact,
H
is uniformly continous. Then, there is
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
such that

(4)

a,b\in X\quad\mbox{ and }\quad d(a,b)\leq\epsilon^{\prime}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad d(H(a),H(b))\leq\epsilon.

For this
\epsilon^{\prime}
we choose
\delta^{\prime}
from the
\mu
-topological stability of
f
.
Again
H^{-1}
is uniformly continuous so we can choose
\delta>0
such that

(5)

c,d\in Y\quad\mbox{ and }\quad d(c,d)\leq\delta\quad\Longrightarrow\quad d(H^{-1}(c),H^{-1}(d))\leq\delta^{\prime}.

Now choose
g^{\prime}\in C^{0}(Z)
such that

d_{C^{0}}(H\circ f\circ H^{-1},g^{\prime})\leq\delta.

Then, (
5
) implies

d_{C^{0}}(f\circ H^{-1},H^{-1}\circ g^{\prime})\leq\delta^{\prime}\quad\mbox{ so }\quad d_{C^{0}}(f,H^{-1}\circ g^{\prime}\circ H)\leq\delta^{\prime}.

It follows that there are a compact
(H^{-1}\circ g\circ H)
-invariant set
Y^{\prime}\subset X
and a continuous
h^{\prime}:Y^{\prime}\to X
such that

d_{C^{0}}(h^{\prime},i_{Y^{\prime}})\leq\epsilon^{\prime}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad f\circ h^{\prime}=h^{\prime}\circ H^{-1}\circ g\circ H.

Then,

H^{-1}\circ g^{\prime}\circ H(Y^{\prime})\subset Y^{\prime}
so
g^{\prime}(H(Y^{\prime}))\subset H(Y^{\prime})

thus
Y=H(Y^{\prime})
is
g^{\prime}
-invariant
(and also compact for
H
is a homeomorphism).

In addition,

d(h^{\prime}(y^{\prime}),y^{\prime})\leq\epsilon^{\prime}
for
y^{\prime}\in Y^{\prime}
so

d(h^{\prime}(H^{-1}(z)),H^{-1}(z))\leq\epsilon^{\prime}\quad\quad\forall z\in Y

thus

d(H\circ h^{\prime}\circ H^{-1}(z),z)\leq\epsilon\quad\quad\forall z\in Y

hence (
4
) implies

d_{C^{0}}(h,i_{Y})\leq\epsilon

where

h=H\circ h^{\prime}\circ H^{-1}:Y\to Z.

We also have

\displaystyle(H\circ f\circ H^{-1})\circ h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H\circ(f\circ h^{\prime})\circ H^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H\circ(h^{\prime}\circ H^{-1}\circ g\circ H)\circ H^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(H\circ h^{\prime}\circ H^{-1})\circ g

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h\circ g

namely

(H\circ f\circ H^{-1})\circ h=h\circ g.

Finally,

H_{*}(\mu)(Z\setminus Y)=\mu(H^{-1}(Z\setminus Y))=\mu(X\setminus Y^{\prime})\leq\epsilon

proving the result.
∎

We now prove Item (5).

Lemma 7
.

Let
f:X\to X
be an expansive map of a compact metric space
and
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
.
If
f
is
\mu
-topologically and
\nu
-topologically stable, then
f
is
(t\mu+(1-t)\nu)
-topologically stable for every
0\leq t\leq 1
.

Proof.

Fix
\epsilon>0
and let
e
be an expansivity constant of
f
.
Let
\delta_{\mu}
and
\delta_{\nu}
be given by the
\mu
-topological and
\nu
-topological stabilities of
f
respectively
for

\epsilon^{\prime}=\min\{\frac{e}{2},\epsilon\}.

Take
\delta=\min\{\delta_{\mu},\delta_{\nu}\}
and let
g\in C^{0}(X)
be such that

d_{C^{0}}(f,g)\leq\delta
.
Then,
d_{C^{0}}(f,g)\leq\delta_{*}
for
*=\mu,\nu
and so
there are compact
g
-invariant sets
Y_{\mu},Y_{\nu}\subset X
and continuous maps
h_{\mu}:Y_{\mu}\to X
and
h_{\nu}:Y_{\nu}\to X
such that

d_{C^{0}}(h_{*},i_{Y_{*}})\leq\epsilon^{\prime}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad f\circ h_{*}=h_{*}\circ g\quad\mbox{ for }\quad*=\mu,\nu.

Define
Y=Y_{\mu}\cup Y_{\nu}
. Hence
Y
is compact
g
-invariant and also

\displaystyle(t\mu+(1-t)\nu)(X\setminus Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t\mu(X\setminus Y)+(1-t)\nu(X\setminus Y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle t\mu(X\setminus Y_{\mu})+(1-t)\nu(X\setminus\nu)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle t\epsilon^{\prime}+(1-t)\epsilon^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon.

We claim that
h_{\mu}=h_{\nu}
on
Y_{\mu}\cap Y_{\nu}
.
Indeed, if
x\in Y_{\mu}\cap Y_{\nu}
,
then

\displaystyle d(f^{n}(h_{\nu}(x)),f^{n}(h_{\nu}(x)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(h_{\nu}(g^{n}(x)),h_{\nu}(g^{n}(x)))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d(h_{\mu}(g^{n}(x)),g^{n}(x))+d(h_{\nu}(g^{n}),g^{n}(x))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{C^{0}}(h,i_{Y_{\mu}})+d_{C^{0}}(h_{\nu},i_{Y_{\nu}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\epsilon^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e,\quad\quad\forall n\geq 0.

Since
e
is an expansivity constant, we get
h_{\mu}(x)=h_{\nu}(x)
proving the claim.
It follows that
h:Y\to X
defined by

h(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}h_{\mu}(x),&\hbox{if}&x\in Y_{\mu}\\
h_{\nu}(x),&\hbox{if}&x\in Y_{\nu}\end{array}\right.

is well-defined and continuous.
Since

f\circ h_{*}=h_{*}\circ g
for
*=\mu,\nu
,
we get
f\circ h=h\circ g
proving the result.
∎

The lemma below is Item (6).

Lemma 8
.

Let
f:X\to X
be a continuous map of a compact metric space.
If
f
is
\mu
-topologically stable for some nonatomic
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
, then
f
is a
\mu
-topologically set-valued stable measure.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon>0
and
\delta
be given by the
\mu
-topological stability.
Take
g\in C^{0}(X)
with
d_{C^{0}}(f,g)\leq\delta
.
Let
Y\subset X
closed
g
-invariant with
\mu(X\setminus Y)\leq\epsilon
and
h:Y\to X
be given from the choice of
g
.
Define
H:X\to 2^{X}
by

H(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}\emptyset,&\hbox{if}&x\in Y\\
\{h(x)\},&\hbox{if}&x\notin Y.\end{array}\right.

Then,
Dom(H)=Y
so

\mu(X\setminus Dom(H))=\mu(X\setminus Y)\leq\epsilon.

Since
d_{C^{0}}(h,i_{Y})\leq\epsilon
, we also have

d_{C^{0}}(H,i_{X})\leq\epsilon
.
Since
\mu
is nonatomic,
\mu\circ H=0
.
From
f\circ h=h\circ g
we get
f\circ H=H\circ g
completing the proof.
∎

The above lemma is false for general measures (by Example
2
).
Finally we prove Item (7).

Lemma 9
.

Let
X
be a compact metric space and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
. Then, every expansive map with the weak
\mu
-shadowing property of
X
is

\mu
-topologically stable.

Proof.

We will use the methods in
[
5
]
.

Let
f:X\to X
be expansive with the weak
\mu
-shadowing property.
Fix
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
.
Let
e>0
be an expansivity constant.
Fix
\epsilon>0
and
\epsilon^{\prime}=\frac{1}{8}\min\{e,\epsilon\}
.
Let
\delta>0
and
B\in\beta_{X}
be given by the weak
\mu
-shadowing property for
\epsilon^{\prime}
.

Now, let
g:X\to X
be continuous such that
d_{C^{0}}(f,g)\leq\delta
.
Take
Y=\overline{O_{g}(B)}
the closure of the
g
-orbit of
B
defined by

O_{g}(B)=\{g^{n}(b):n\geq 0,\,b\in B\}.

Clearly
Y
is closed
g
-invariant. Moreover,
B\subset Y
so

(6)

\mu(X\setminus Y)\leq\mu(X\setminus B)<\epsilon^{\prime}<\epsilon.

Take
x\in O_{g}(B)
hence
x=g^{n_{x}}(b)
for some
b\in B
and some nonnegative integer
n_{x}
.
Since
d_{C^{0}}(f,g)\leq\delta
, the sequence
(b_{n})_{n\geq 0}
defined by

b_{n}=g^{n}(b)
satisfy

b_{0}=b\in B
and

d(f(b_{n}),b_{n+1})=d(f(g^{n}(b)),g(g^{n}(b))\leq d_{C^{0}}(f,g)\leq\delta,\quad\quad\forall n\geq 0.

Then, there is
y\in X
such that

(7)

d(f^{n}(y),g^{n}(b))\leq\epsilon^{\prime},\quad\quad\forall n\geq 0.

Defining

h(x)=f^{n_{x}}(y)

we have a map
h:O_{g}(B)\to X
.

We shall prove that
h
is well-defined.
Suppose
x=g^{m_{x}}(b^{\prime})
for some others nonnegative integer
m_{x}
and
b^{\prime}\in B
.
As before we get
y^{\prime}\in X
such that

d(f^{n}(y^{\prime}),g^{n}(b^{\prime}))\leq\epsilon^{\prime}
,
\forall n\geq 0.

Then,

\displaystyle d(f^{r}(f^{n_{x}}(y)),f^{r}(f^{m_{x}}(y^{\prime})))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d(f^{r+n_{x}}(y),g^{r+n_{x}}(b))+

\displaystyle d(g^{r+m_{x}}(b^{\prime}),f^{r+m_{x}}(y^{\prime}))

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 2\epsilon^{\prime}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle e,\quad\quad\forall r\geq 0

so
f^{n_{x}}(y)=f^{m_{x}}(y^{\prime})
by expansivity thus
h
is well-defined.

Next we note that
if
x=g^{n_{x}}(b)
as before, then
g(x)=g^{n_{x}+1}(b)
and so

h(g(x))=f^{n_{x}+1}(y^{\prime})
with
y^{\prime}
satisfying

d(f^{n}(y^{\prime}),g^{n}(b))\leq\epsilon^{\prime},

\forall n\geq 0.

Combined with (
7
) we get

d(f^{n}(y),f^{n}(y^{\prime}))\leq 2\epsilon^{\prime}<e,\quad\quad\forall n\geq 0

so
y=y^{\prime}
by expansivity.
It follows that

(f\circ h)(x)=f(f^{n_{x}}(y))=f^{n_{x}+1}(y)=h(g(x))=(h\circ g)(x),\quad\forall x\in O_{g}(B)

proving

(8)

f\circ h=h\circ g\quad\mbox{ on }\quad O_{g}(B).

Also, by replacing
n
by
n_{x}
in (
7
) we have

(9)

d(h(x),x)\leq\epsilon^{\prime},\quad\quad\forall x\in O_{g}(B).

We assert that
h:O_{g}(B)\to X
is uniformly continuous.
Fix
\Delta>0
. Since
e
is an expansivity constant we can apply Lemma 2 in
[
12
]

to find
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

d(a,b)<\Delta
whenever
a,b\in X
satisfy

d(f^{n}(a),f^{n}(b))\leq e
for
0\leq n\leq N
.
On the other hand,
g
is continuous so there is
\rho>0
such that

d(g^{n}(a),g^{n}(b))\leq\frac{e}{2}
for
0\leq n\leq N
whenever
a,b\in X
and
d(a,b)<\rho
.
Now, take
x,y\in O_{g}(B)
with
d(x,y)<\rho
.
It follows that

\displaystyle d(f^{n}(h(x)),f^{n}(h(y)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(h(g^{n}(x)),h(g^{n}(y)))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d(h(g^{n}(x)),g^{n}(x))+d(g^{n}(x),g^{n}(y))+

\displaystyle d(h(g^{n}(y)),g^{n}(y))

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{under}}{\leq}

\displaystyle 2\epsilon^{\prime}+\frac{e}{2}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{e}{2}+\frac{e}{4}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle e,\quad\quad\forall 0\leq n\leq N.

Then,
d(h(x),h(y))<\Delta
proving the assertion.

It follows that
h
has a continuous extension
h:Y\to X
.
By taking limit in (
8
) and (
9
) we obtain

d_{C^{0}}(h,i_{Y})\leq\epsilon^{\prime}<\epsilon\quad\mbox{ and }\quad f\circ h=h\circ g.

These expressions together with (
6
) imply the result.
∎

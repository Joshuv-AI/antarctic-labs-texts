The Moduli Stack of Compact Metric Spaces
Tomoki Yuji

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce a Grothendieck topology on the category of totally bounded metric spaces and develop a theory of stacks with respect to this topology. We further define the fine moduli stack of compact metric spaces and prove that its coarse moduli space is isometric to the Gromov–Hausdorff space.



Introduction

The space
\mathrm{GH}
of isometry classes of compact metric spaces equipped with the Gromov–Hausdorff distance plays an important role in metric geometry as a coarse moduli space of compact metric spaces.
However, since there exist nontrivial families parametrizing mutually isometric objects, the Gromov–Hausdorff space does not serve as a fine moduli space.
In contrast, in algebraic geometry, the theory of algebraic stacks—introduced by Deligne and Mumford and later generalized and developed by Artin—has provided fine moduli for a wide range of moduli problems in the form of algebraic stacks.
Today, algebraic stacks are widely recognized as the correct geometric framework for discussing moduli problems in algebraic geometry.
In this paper, by mimicing the theory of moduli stacks from algebraic geometry into the setting of metric spaces, we construct a fine moduli stack of compact metric spaces and demonstrate that the theory of stacks provides an effective framework for moduli problems in metric geometry as well.
This theory can be regarded as a partial realization of what was suggested by Gromov in
[
Gromov
, 3.11
\frac{3}{4}_{+}
]
.

In this paper, in order to develop the theory of stacks appropriately in the context of metric geometry, we introduce a Grothendieck topology—what we call the
\mathrm{lsm}
topology (cf.
Definition

3.5
)—on the category
\tbMet
of totally bounded metric spaces.
By viewing proper submetries as families of compact metric spaces parametrized by metric spaces, we then define a stack
\mathcal{GH}
over
\tbMet_{\mathrm{lsm}}
(cf.
Definition

5.1
).
We further show that
\mathcal{GH}
can be presented as a quotient stack obtained by dividing a sheaf of relations—what we call a naive metric stack of quotient type (cf.
Definition

4.6
and
Definition

4.15
)—see
Corollary

5.12
.
Finally, we prove that
\mathcal{GH}
satisfies the appropriate properties of a fine moduli in the category of naive metric stacks of quotient type (cf.
Theorem

5.19
).

Our main result is the following:

Theorem A
.

The moduli stack
\mathcal{GH}
of compact metric spaces (cf.
Definition

5.1
) is a naive metric stack of quotient type (cf.
Corollary

5.12
).
Moreover, for any naive metric stack
\mathcal{X}
of quotient type, pulling back the universal object
\mathcal{GH}_{1}\to\mathcal{GH}
induces a one-to-one correspondence between representable proper submetries
\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{X}
(cf.
Definition

5.13
) of stacks in groupoids over
\tbMet_{\mathrm{lsm}}
and morphisms
\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{GH}
of stacks in groupoids over
\tbMet_{\mathrm{lsm}}
(cf.
Theorem

5.19
).

Metric Spaces

An
extended pseudometric space

X=(\underline{X},d_{X})
consists of a set
\underline{X}
together with a function
d_{X}:\underline{X}\times\underline{X}\to\overline{\R}_{\geq 0}
satisfying the condition that for any
x,y,z\in\underline{X}
,

d_{X}(x,y)+d_{X}(y,z)\geq d_{X}(x,z)=d_{X}(z,x).

We refer to
\underline{X}
as the
underlying set
of
X
, and to
d_{X}
as the
distance
on
X
.
By a slight abuse of notation, we write
x\in X
to mean
x\in\underline{X}
.
A
pseudometric space
is an extended pseudometric space
X
such that
\infty\not\in\im(d_{X})
.
An
extended metric space
is an extended pseudometric space
X
such that for any
x,y\in X
, the condition
d_{X}(x,y)=0
implies
x=y
.
In particular, a
metric space
is both an extended metric space and pseudometric space.

Let
X,Y
be extended pseudometric spaces.
A
morphism
of extended pseudometric spaces
f:X\to Y
is a map
f:\underline{X}\to\underline{Y}
such that for any
x,x^{\prime}\in X
, it holds that
d_{X}(x,x^{\prime})\geq d_{Y}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))
, that is, a morphism is precisely a
1
-Lipschitz map.

We write
\PEMet
for the category of (small) extended pseudometric spaces and 1-Lipschitz maps.
We write
\EMet\subset\PEMet
for the full subcategory consisting of the extended metric spaces.
We write
\PMet\subset\PEMet
for the full subcategory consisting of the pseudometric spaces.
We write
\Met\mathrel{\vcentcolon\hskip-0.5pt=\mkern-11.5mu=\mkern-21.5mu\raisebox{5.0805pt}{\scalebox{0.63}[0.655]{$\mathrm{def}$}}}\PMet\cap\EMet\subset\PEMet
for the full subcategory consisting of the metric spaces.
We wirte
\tbMet
for the full subcategory of
\Met
consisting of the totally bounded metric spaces.

Remark
.

Since a compact metric space is of cardinality at most continuum, and the completion of a totally bounded metric space is compact, it follows that a totally bounded metric space is of cardinality at most continuum.
Hence, in particular,
\tbMet
is essentially small (i.e., there exists a small set of objects of
\tbMet
such that any object of
\tbMet
is isomorphic to an object including in this set).

Lemma 1.3
.

Let
X_{\bullet}:I\to\PEMet
be a diagram in
\PEMet
.
Then, the following assertions hold.

(1)

For any
(x_{i})_{i\in I},(x_{i}^{\prime})_{i\in I}\in\lim_{i\in I}\underline{X_{i}}

d_{\lim}((x_{i})_{i\in I},(x_{i}^{\prime})_{i\in I})\mathrel{\vcentcolon\hskip-0.5pt=\mkern-11.5mu=\mkern-21.5mu\raisebox{5.0805pt}{\scalebox{0.63}[0.655]{$\mathrm{def}$}}}\mathop{\smash{\mathrm{sup}}}_{i\in I}d_{X_{i}}(x_{i},x_{i}^{\prime}).

.
Moreover, if each

(\lim_{i\in I}\underline{X_{i}},d_{\lim})

(2)

For any

\alpha_{i}:\underline{X_{i}}\to\colim_{i\in I}\underline{X_{i}}
denote the canonical map.
For any
x,x^{\prime}\in\colim_{i\in I}\underline{X_{i}}

\displaystyle d_{\colim}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vcentcolon\hskip-0.5pt=\mkern-11.5mu=\mkern-21.5mu\raisebox{5.0805pt}{\scalebox{0.63}[0.655]{$\mathrm{def}$}}}\inf\left\{\sum_{k=0}^{N}d_{X_{i_{k}}}(x_{i_{k}},x_{i_{k}}^{\prime})\,\middle|\,\begin{aligned} &N\in\N,i_{0},\cdots,i_{N}\in I,\\
&x_{i_{k}},x_{i_{k}}^{\prime}\in X_{i_{k}}\ (\forall k\in\{0,\cdots,N\})\\
&\alpha_{i_{0}}(x_{i_{0}})=x,\ \alpha_{i_{N}}(x^{\prime}_{i_{N}})=x^{\prime},\\
&\alpha_{i_{k}}(x_{i_{k}}^{\prime})=\alpha_{i_{k+1}}(x_{i_{k+1}})\ (\forall k\in\{0,\cdots,N-1\})\end{aligned}\right\}.

X\mathrel{\vcentcolon\hskip-0.5pt=\mkern-11.5mu=\mkern-21.5mu\raisebox{5.0805pt}{\scalebox{0.63}[0.655]{$\mathrm{def}$}}}(\colim_{i\in I}\underline{X_{i}},d_{\colim})

X
is naturally isomorphic to the colimit of the diagram

.

Proof.

(1)
follows immediately from elementary discussion about extended pseudometric spaces.

Next, we verify
(2)
.
It is straightforward to verify that
X=(\colim_{i\in I}\underline{X_{i}},d_{\colim})
is an extended pseudometric space, and the family of canonical maps
(\alpha_{i}:\underline{X_{i}}\to\underline{X})_{i\in I}
forms a functorial family of morphisms in
\PEMet
.
To verify a universal property of the colimit, let
Y\in\PEMet
be an object,
(f_{i}:X_{i}\to Y)_{i\in I}
a functorial family of morphisms in
\PEMet
,
x,x^{\prime}\in X
elements, and

Then, by the definition of

, indices

, and elements

such that
\alpha_{i_{0}}(x_{i_{0}})=x
,
\alpha_{i_{N}}(x^{\prime}_{i_{N}})=x^{\prime}
,
\alpha_{i_{k}}(x_{i_{k}}^{\prime})=\alpha_{i_{k+1}}(x_{i_{k+1}})
(
\forall k\in\{0,\cdots,N-1\}

d_{X}(x,x^{\prime})\geq-\varepsilon+\sum_{k=0}^{N}d_{X_{i_{k}}}(x_{i_{k}},x_{i_{k}}^{\prime}).

Write

for the induced map (cf.

Then, it holds that

\displaystyle d_{X}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq-\varepsilon+\sum_{k=0}^{N}d_{X_{i_{k}}}(x_{i_{k}},x_{i_{k}}^{\prime})

In particular,

\PEMet

Finally, since

is naturally isomorphic to the colimit of the diagram

§2.  The Hausdorff Distance

In this section, we study basic properties of morphisms of metric spaces, called
submetries
, from the viewpoint of metric geometry, and establish the foundational results needed in this paper.
Submetries have been used effectively in differential geometry to study Riemannian submersions; from the perspective of metric geometry, however, it is natural to regard them as analogues of flat families in algebraic geometry.

We also show that the functor parametrizing compact subspaces of a given metric space is representable by the space of compact subsets equipped with the Hausdorff distance.
This result can be viewed as a metric-geometric analogue of the representability of the Hilbert functor in algebraic geometry.

Definition 2.1
.

(1)

Let
X\in\epMet
be an object.
For any subsets
F_{0},F_{1}\subset X
, we shall write

d^{\mathrm{H}}_{X}(F_{0},F_{1})\mathrel{\vcentcolon\hskip-0.5pt=\mkern-11.5mu=\mkern-21.5mu\raisebox{5.0805pt}{\scalebox{0.63}[0.655]{$\mathrm{def}$}}}\max\left\{\mathop{\smash{\mathrm{sup}}}_{x_{0}\in F_{0}}\inf_{x_{1}\in F_{1}}d(x_{0},x_{1}),\mathop{\smash{\mathrm{sup}}}_{x_{1}\in F_{1}}\inf_{x_{0}\in F_{0}}d(x_{0},x_{1})\right\}\in\R_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\}.

We shall say that
d^{\mathrm{H}}_{X}
is the
Hausdorff distance
on
X
.

(2)

Let
f:X\to B
be a morphism in
\epMet

We say that
f
is a
submetry
if
f
is surjective, and, moreover, for any points
b_{0},b_{1}\in B
, it holds that
d^{\mathrm{H}}_{X}(f^{-1}(b_{0}),f^{-1}(b_{1}))\leq d_{B}(b_{0},b_{1})<\infty
.

Remark 2.1.1
.

(1)

Let
X\in\epMet
for an object.
Then, for any subset
F\subset X
, we have

.
In particular,

However, if

gives a distance on the set of non-empty bounded closed subsets of

[

(2)

Let

be a morphism in
\epMet

For any metric space

.
In particular, if

Lemma 2.3

Let

\epMet

(4)

For any positive real number
\varepsilon>0

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{2_{\varepsilon+d_{B}(b_{0},b_{1})}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{B}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
f:X\to B
be a morphism in
\Met
.
Assume that
f
is a submetry .
Then, the underlying map of topological spaces of

is proper (i.e., a closed continuous map with compact fibers) if and only if for every

is compact.

Proof.

The necessity follows immediately from the definition of a proper map.
Assume that for any

is compact.
Note that any metric space is Hausdorff and compactly generated.
Hence, by
[

, Theorem 3.7.18]
, to prove that

is proper, it suffices to prove that

is compact whenever
B
is compact.
Assume moreover that
B
is compact.

Let
(a_{n})_{n\in\N}
be a Cauchy sequence in
X
.
Since
B
is compact, the Cauchy sequence
(f(a_{n}))_{n\in\N}
in
B
converges to a point
b_{\infty}\in B
.
Since
f
is a submetry , for each
n\in\N
, there exists a point
a^{\prime}_{n}\in f^{-1}(b_{\infty})
such that
d_{X}(a_{n},a_{n}^{\prime})<d_{B}(f(a_{n}),b_{\infty})+1/2^{n}
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
be a positive real number.
There exists
N\in\N
such that for all

.
Then, for each

\displaystyle d_{X}(a^{\prime}_{n},a^{\prime}_{m})

\displaystyle\leq d_{X}(a^{\prime}_{n},a_{n})+d_{X}(a_{n},a_{m})+d_{X}(a_{m},a_{m}^{\prime})

\displaystyle<(d_{B}(b_{\infty},f(a_{n}))+1/2^{n})+(\varepsilon/3-1/2^{N-1})+(d_{B}(b_{\infty},f(a_{m}))+1/2^{m})

\displaystyle\leq d_{B}(b_{\infty},f(a_{n}))+d_{B}(b_{\infty},f(a_{m}))+\varepsilon/3\leq\varepsilon.

Thus,
(a^{\prime}_{n})_{n\in\N}
is a Cauchy sequence in

.
Since

is a compact metric space, the sequence

Since

In particular,

is complete.

2.11

is totally bounded.

2.12

We further show that the lsm topology is subcanonical (cf.

Definition 3.1

(
[

)
.

Let

be a morphism in
\Met
.
We that

Remark 4.6.1
.

The reason we attach the term “naive” in

4.6
is as follows.

(1)

As already pointed out in
Remark

4.5.1
, the notion of submetry should be extended to morphisms of stacks over
\tbMet_{\mathrm{lsm}}
in a more appropriate manner.
In the present work, however, in light of our purposes, we impose only minimal categorical requirements on formal submetries, and for this reason we regard

(2)

In ordinary metric geometry, typical examples of metric spaces equipped with a group structure do not form group objects in the category of metric spaces.
Thus, it is not evident that restricting attention to stacks valued in groupoids is the most appropriate choice.
Rather, typical examples of metric spaces with a group structure (for instance
SO(N)
or the group of self-isometries of a homogeneous metric space) are understood as monoidal groups with respect to the
\ell^{1}
-product.
Hence, in order to treat actions of such objects on metric spaces within a stack-theoretic framework, it seems necessary to develop the theory of stacks as simplicial sheaves with effective descent condition, like
[
JarLH
, the paragraph below of Remark 5.14]
(see also
Remark

4.13.1
).
This is also why we did not require representability of diagonals.

Although it is not entirely clear what kind of theory of stacks Gromov actually had in mind when he suggested such a possibility in
[
Gromov
, 3.11
\frac{3}{4}_{+}
]
, in view of the discussion in (2) above, it seems that any such theory can be realized only partially.
If Gromov intended to treat a geometric object that keeps track of isometries as a metric space—namely, if one were to impose representability of the diagonal morphism in the definition of a “metric stack”—then such a theory would most likely fail.
In the theory of algebraic stacks, the requirement that the diagonal be representable by an algebraic space is imposed for several reasons, one of which is that the categories of schemes or algebraic spaces do not behave well from a categorical viewpoint.
By contrast, the category of metric spaces enjoys substantially better formal properties than those categories.
For this reason, rather than imposing representability of diagonals, the author believes that one should instead pursue an approach in which the entire theory of metric spaces is extended to general sheaves on
\tbMet_{\mathrm{lsm}}
.

, the author believes that a more appropriate definition of a metric stack should take values in simplicial sets.
The reason for this is its compatibility with the construction of classifying spaces via the bar construction.

Suppose that a metric space
G
equipped with a monoid structure
m:G\otimes G\to G
and
e:*\to G
with respect to the monoidal product (where we consider the monoidal structure on
\epMet
as the
\ell^{1}
-product) such that the underlying monoid
(G,m,e)
is a group.
Assume further that
G
acts on a metric space
X
through a morphism
a:G\otimes X\to X
.
From a homotopy-theoretic viewpoint, such an action would lead one to consider the stackification (injective fibrant replacement) of the simplicial object
BG\mathrel{\vcentcolon\hskip-0.5pt=\mkern-11.5mu=\mkern-21.5mu\raisebox{5.0805pt}{\scalebox{0.63}[0.655]{$\mathrm{def}$}}}(G^{\otimes n}\otimes X)_{n\in\N}
in
\Sh_{\mathrm{lsm}}(\tbMet)
which arises from the bar construction applied to the action
a:G\otimes X\to X
.

Moreover,
BG
is expected to serve as the classifying space of principal
G
-bundles.
To state this precisely, one needs an appropriate definition of a principal
G
-bundle.
Here, the author thinks it is effective, rather than starting from an abstract definition, to keep in mind the definition of
BG
via the bar construction and define principal
G
-bundles in a way that ensures
BG
indeed serves as their classifying space.
For instance, consider a
G
-equivariant morphism of sheaves
p:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{F}
such that the
G
-action on
\mathcal{F}
is trivial, and moreover the induced left adjoint
\sSh_{\mathrm{lsm}}(\tbMet)^{BG}\to\sSh_{\mathrm{lsm}}(\tbMet)_{/\mathcal{F}}
(where
\sSh_{\mathrm{lsm}}(\tbMet)
denotes the category of simplicial sheaves on
\tbMet
with respect to the
\mathrm{lsm}
-topology and
\sSh_{\mathrm{lsm}}(\tbMet)^{BG}
denotes the category of simplicial sheaves on
\tbMet
with a
BG
-action), perhaps this left adjoint should be regarded as a left adjoint in the
\sSh_{\mathrm{lsm}}(\tbMet)
-enriched sense, is left exact.
If one calls such a morphism
p
is
a principal
G
-bundle over
\mathcal{F}
, then, by an analogue of Diaconescu’s theorem (cf. e.g.
[
Moerdij
, Theorem 2.2]
) for classifying topoi, it is reasonable to expect that
BG
serves, in the topos-theoretic sense, as the classifying space of principal
G
-bundles.
However, the primary aim of the present paper is the construction and investigation of fine moduli for compact metric spaces.
We therefore refrain from pursuing possible higher-categorical enhancement in this direction.

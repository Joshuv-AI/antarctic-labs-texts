Perverse-Hodge complexes for Lagrangian fibrations and symplectic resolutions
Zhengze Xin

Abstract
. We study perverse-Hodge complexes for Lagrangian fibrations on holomorphic symplectic varieties. We prove the symplectic Hard Lefschetz type theorem and the symmetry of perverse-Hodge complexes when the symplectic variety admits symplectic resolutions, therefore generalize the previous result by Schnell in the smooth case verifying a conjecture by Shen-Yin. Along the way, we study the perverse coherent properties of the intersection complex Hodge modules on symplectic varieties. As an application, we obtain an alternative proof of the numerical ”perverse=Hodge” result by Felisetti-Shen-Yin, without using the Beauville-Bogomolov-Fujiki form. We also apply our results to study singular Higgs moduli spaces over reduced curves using results by Mauri-Migliorini on the local structure.



1.
Introduction

1.1 Holomorphic symplectic varieties

We work over complex number
\mathbb{C}
. The goal of this paper is to study the Hodge theory of Lagrangian fibrations on holomorphic symplectic varieties, which are possibly singular and non-compact. The notion of holomorphic symplectic variety generalizes irreducible holomorphic symplectic manifolds, which are one of the building blocks for compact kähler manifolds with numerically trivial canonical bundles by the Beauville-Bogomolov decomposition. In the sense of Beauville
[
Bea00
]
, a holomorphic symplectic variety is a normal variety
X
which admits a non-degenerate closed holomorphic two-form on its regular locus
X_{reg}
which extends holomorphically on some resolution of singularities of
X
. Classical objects such as Hilbert schemes of points and Nakajima quiver varieties are known to be symplectic. In terms of moduli problems, some important examples of symplectic varieties come from moduli spaces of sheaves such as Beauville-Mukai system or the moduli of Higgs bundles.

The main objects to study in this article is a family of complexes of coherent sheaves called the
perverse-Hodge complexes
, associated with a Lagrangian of a holomorphic symplectic variety. They are constructed using Saito’s theory of mixed Hodge modules
[
Sai88
,
Sai90
]
. We will focus on their interaction with
symplectic singularities
.

1.2 Perverse-Hodge complexes

Let
f:M\rightarrow B
be a Lagrangian fibration on holomorphic symplectic variety
M
of dimension
2n
.

When
M
is compact and smooth,
perverse-Hodge complexes
are originally defined in
[
SY23
]
by considering Saito’s decomposition theorem for
Rf_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{M}[2n]
. When
M
is possibly singular and non-compact, the natural replacement for
\mathbb{Q}_{M}[2n]
is the intersection cohomology Hodge module
\operatorname{IC}_{M}
.

Applying Saito’s decomposition theorem
[
Sai88
, §5.3]
to the Lagrangian fibration
f
, we get the following decomposition

Rf_{*}\operatorname{IC}_{M}(n)=\bigoplus_{i=-n}^{n}\mathcal{P}_{i}[-i]

where
\operatorname{IC}_{M}
is the intersection cohomology Hodge module and
(\bullet)
is the Tate twist.
We can define the
perverse-Hodge complex
as
G_{i,k}=\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{-k}\operatorname{DR}(\mathcal{P}_{i})[-i]
. In
[
SY23
]
, Shen and Yin proposed a symmetry between perverse-Hodge complexes. When
M
is smooth, the conjecture was proven by Schnell
[
Sch23
]
and we have symmetry of perverse-Hodge complexes:

G_{i,k}\cong G_{k,i}.

in the derived category
D^{b}_{coh}(B)
.

We aim to investigate the perverse-Hodge complexes and the symmetry when
M
is a possibly singular holomorphic symplectic variety. Our main theorems provides a partial answer to this question.

Theorem 1.1

(Symplectic Hard Lefschetz, Theorem
5.11
)
.

If the intersection cohomology Hodge module
\operatorname{IC}_{M}
is strongly coherent m-perverse (see Definition
4.3
), i.e. it satisfies the following support condition on its graded de Rham:

(1)

\operatorname{codim}\operatorname{Supp}\mathcal{H}^{j}\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{-k}\operatorname{DR}(\operatorname{IC}_{M})[k-2n]\geq 2j+2

for
j\geq 1
, then the action by the reflexive symplectic form
\sigma\in H^{0}(M,\Omega^{[2]}_{M})
satisfies symplectic Hard Lefschetz theorem for
\operatorname{IC}_{M}
, i.e

(2)

\sigma^{k}:\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{k}\operatorname{DR}(\operatorname{IC}_{M}(n))[-k-n]\rightarrow\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{-k}\operatorname{DR}(\operatorname{IC}_{M}(n))[k-n].

is an isomorphism.

This provides a way to establish Hard Lefschetz type symmetry on symplectic varieties, by checking the dimension of the support of graded de Rham complexes of
\operatorname{IC}_{X}
. The advantage of this criteria is that it’s an étale-local condition.

The symmetry of perverse-Hodge complexes in the smooth case was established in
[
Sch23
]
. Our second theorem generalizes it to the case when
M
admits a symplectic resolution.

Theorem 1.2

(Perverse-Hodge symmetry, Theorem
5.15
)
.

If
M
admist a symplectic resolution, then the support condition in the above theorem holds and thus the symplectic Hard Lefschetz theorem holds. Moreover, we have the symmetry of perverse-Hodge complexes

(3)

G_{i,k}\cong G_{k,i}

in the derived category
D^{b}_{coh}(B)
.

In the compact case, the cohomology groups are finite dimensional. As shown in
[
SY22
,
SY23
]
, the symmetry of perverse-Hodge complexes is in fact a sheaf-theoretic refinement of a numerical symmetry result concerning perverse numbers and Hodge numbers of
M
. In the singular setting we can define perverse Hodge numbers on the intersection cohomology
IH^{*}(M,\mathbb{Q})
, and we can reprove the following numerical result already observed by Felisetti-Shen-Yin
[
FSY22
]
.

Theorem 1.3

(Numerical ”Perverse=Hodge”, Corollary
5.16
)
.

Let
M
be a irreducible holomorphic symplectic variety which admits a symplectic resolution. We have

(4)

{{}^{\mathfrak{p}}}Ih^{i,j}(f)=Ih^{i,j}(M).

In
[
FSY22
]
, the identity between perverse numbers and Hodge numbers were established using the hyperkähler metric and Looijenga-Lunts-Verbitsky (LLV) algebra, which relies essentially on the existence of the Beauville-Bogomolov-Fujiki (BBF) form. The symmetry for perverse-Hodge complexes allows one to deduce and refine the numerical result above without using the BBF form, which was considered to be one of the reason for introducing perverse-Hodge complexes as a categorification.

1.3 The support condition and the symplectic resolution

The perverse coherent t-structures, in the sense of Deligne, Arinkin-Bezrukavnikov
[
AB10
]
and Kashiwara
[
Kas03
]
, is defined on the derived category of coherent sheaves as a commutative algebraic counter-part of the perverse t-structure on constructible sheaves. The notion is useful for our purpose in the following sense: being strongly m-perverse coherent as in Thm
1.1
allows us to extend the action of the symplectic forms in a unique way.

In the singular setting, the graded de Rham complexes
\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{-*}\operatorname{DR}(\operatorname{IC}_{M})
is the natural replacement for
\Omega_{M}^{*}
, it coincides with
\Omega_{M_{reg}}^{*}
when restrict to the regular locus
M_{reg}
of
M
. On the regular locus, the non-degenerate symplectic form
\sigma_{reg}
acts on
\Omega_{M}^{*}
.

we can verify the strongly m-perverse coherent condition when
M
admits symplectic resolutions and thus extend the action of symplectic forms on
\Omega_{M}^{*}
to the graded de Rham complexes.

Theorem 1.4

(Theorem
5.4
)
.

Let
M
be a holomorphic symplectic variety of dimension
2n
admitting étale-locally symplectic resolutions. Then the graded de Rham complexes of
\operatorname{IC}_{X}
are m-perverse coherent sheaves. Moreover,
\operatorname{IC}_{X}
is strongly coherent m-perverse, satisfying

\operatorname{codim}\operatorname{Supp}\mathcal{H}^{j}(\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{-k}\operatorname{DR}(\operatorname{IC}_{X})[k-2n])\geq 2j+2

for
j\geq 1
. And therefore
M
satisfies symplectic Hard Lefschetz symmetry Theorem
1.1
.

The proof relies on the local structure of the holomorphic symplectic variety and the geometry of the symplectic resolution. The assumption on the existence of the symplectic resolution is necessary, and it’s easy to construct counter-examples for which
\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{-k}\operatorname{DR}(\operatorname{IC}_{X})
are not m-perverse coherent.

We emphasize that the support condition is an étale-local condition by the analytic nature of mixed Hodge modules. There are holomorphic symplectic varieties which only admit symplectic resolutions étale-locally but not globally, see
[
BL22
, Example 3.2(4)]
. The theorem allows one to deduce the symplectic Hard Lefschetz symmetry for all singular Higgs moduli spaces over reduced curves, thanks to the local-model description using hypertoric varieties constructed by Mauri-Migliorini
[
MM24
]
, as we shall se below.

1.4 Moduli of Higgs bundles

Let
C
be a nonsingular irreducible projective curve of genus
g\geq 2
. The
Dolbeault/Higgs moduli space

M(n,d)
is the coarse moduli space of rank
n
degree
d
semistable Higgs bundles on
C
. When
n,d
are not coprime, the moduli space
M(n,d)
is a non-proper singular symplectic variety with the Hitchin fibration
\chi(n,d):M(n,d)\rightarrow A_{n}
. The singular locus is given by strictly polystable Higgs bundles, which is related to partition of
n
by multirank
\underline{n}
and multiplicity
\underline{m}
.

The étale-local structure of this moduli space is well-described using the deformation theory of Higgs bundles (cf.
[
MM24
]
). We can consider the open locus
A^{red}_{n}\subset A_{n}
consisting of the reduced characteristic polynomials where multiplicities in
\underline{m}
are
1
, and
M^{red}(n,d):=\chi(n,d)^{-1}(A^{red}_{n})
. The moduli space over reduced Hitchin base
M^{red}(n,d)
admits a Whitney stratification

M^{red}(n,d)=\coprod_{\underline{n}}M^{\circ}_{\underline{n}}(d)

and analytic normal slice
W_{\underline{n}}
through
M^{\circ}_{\underline{n}}(d)
. Remarkably,
W_{\underline{n}}
is isomorphic to a Nakajima quiver variety and admits symplectic resolutions. One can then verify the support condition for singular Higgs moduli spaces over the reduced Hitchin base. This gives the following theorem,

Theorem 1.5

(Theorem
6.4
)
.

Let
\chi(n,d):M^{red}(n,d)\rightarrow A_{n}^{red}
be the Hitchin fibration over the reduced Hitchin base. Then the symplectic Hard Lefschetz theorem
1.1
holds for
M^{red}(n,d)
. If moreover
M^{red}(n,d)
admits a symplectic resolution, then the perverse-Hodge complexes
G_{i,k}
for
\chi(n,d)
satisfy
G_{i,k}\simeq G_{k,i}
in the derived category.

1.5 Connection to other works

We briefly review the history and other works along this direction. The symmetry theorem of perverse-Hodge complexes was originally inspired by the P=W conjecture and its compact analogy. The P=W conjecture by de Cataldo-Hausel-Migliorini
[
dCHM12
]
predicts that the perverse filtration on
M(n,d)
induced by the Hitchin morphism matches the weight filtration on the Betti moduli space
M_{B}(n,d)
via non-abelian Hodge theory. When
n,d
are coprime, the Higgs moduli space is non-singular and the conjecture has been recently resolved in
[
MS22
,
HMMS22
,
MSY23
]
. However, its singular variant remains open.

The conjecture suggests that the perverse filtration of
M(n,d)
can be computed via Hodge theory (up to a hyperkähler twist), this motivates the numerical ”Perverse=Hodge” for Lagrangian fibration
f:M\rightarrow B
in the compact case (cf.
[
SY22
]
). The categorification of this symmetry phenomenon using Hodge modules leads to the notion of perverse-Hodge complexes
G_{i,k}
(cf.
[
SY23
]
), which works in non-compact setting.

Towards generalizing the results to the singular cases, the numerical ”Perverse-Hodge” result and the LLV algebra structure on intersection cohomology were
established in
[
FSY22
]
for irreducible symplectic varieties admitting symplectic resolutions, and
[
Tig22
]
when singularities are isolated. Our work provides partial answers on categorifying the results above on the level of perverse-Hodge complexes.

2.
Preliminaries

Consider a Lagrangian fibration
f:M\rightarrow B
. It is well-known that a nonsingular fiber is a complex torus. If
f
is smooth, then
f
is a family of abelian varieties. The variation of Hodge structures of
V=R^{1}f_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{M}
is the key to understanding the topology of the Lagrangian fibration.
In general, in order to further control the topology of singular fibers, we need to consider the extensions of variation of Hodge structures across the locus where
f
has singular fibers. Saito’s theory of Hodge modules
[
Sai88
,
Sai90
]
provides a general framework to this end.

2.1.
Overview of Hodge modules

Saito introduced Hodge modules as vast generalizations of variation of Hodge structures, by allowing perverse sheaves instead of just local systems. Let
X
be a complex manifold. The basic objects are filtered regular holonomic
\mathcal{D}_{X}
-modules with
\mathbb{Q}
-structure, given by a triple
M=(\mathcal{M},F_{\bullet}\mathcal{M},K)
consisting of the following

(1)

\mathcal{M}
is a regular holonomic right
\mathcal{D}_{X}
-module with a good filtration
F_{\bullet}\mathcal{M}
.

(2)

K
is a perverse sheaf with an isomorphism

\operatorname{DR}(\mathcal{M})\simeq\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}K

via the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence.

Hodge modules are such triples satisfying technical conditions which provides the stability when applying nearby and vanishing cycle functors. one can also define the notions of weight and polarization. Let
HM(X,w)
(resp.
HM^{p}_{Z}(X,w)
) denote the category of (polarizable) Hodge modules of weight
w
on
X
(with strict support
Z
). For a possibly singular analytic space
X
, one can define Hodge modules on it by taking a global embedding into a complex manifold
i:X\hookleftarrow Y
and taking all Hodge modules whose support is contained in the image
i(X)\subset Y
.

In particular, for irreducible varieties
X
, there is a polarizable pure Hodge module
\operatorname{IC}_{X}^{H}
associated to the intersection complex of
X
, which we call the intersection cohomology Hodge module.

Let
Z\subset X
be an irreducible closed analytic subvariety. The main results in
[
Sai90
]
says that every polarizable variation of
\mathbb{Q}
-Hodge structure of weight
w-\dim Z
on a Zariski-open subset of
Z
extends uniquely to an object of
HM^{p}_{Z}(X,w)
. Conversely, every object of
HM^{p}_{Z}(X,w)
is obtained in this way.
The category of mixed Hodge modules
MHM(X)
can also be defined as the generalization of admissible variations of mixed Hodge structures.

The major accomplishments of Saito’s theory is on generalizing the decomposition theorem of perverse sheaves developed in
[
BBDG18
]
. It’s a consequence of the following hard Lefschetz theoerem,

Theorem 2.1

(
[
Sai88
]
, Theorem 5.3.1)
.

Let
f:X\rightarrow Y
be a proper morphism between two complex manifolds, and let
M\in HM^{p}(X,w)
. Let
\ell
be the first Chern class of a
f
-ample line bundle on
X
. Then
\mathcal{H}^{i}f_{*}M\in HM^{p}(Y,w+i)
and we have isomorphisms of Hodge modules

(5)

\ell^{i}:\mathcal{H}^{-i}(f_{*}M)\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathcal{H}^{i}(f_{*}M)(i)

where
(i)
denotes the Tate twist shifting the Hodge filtration
F_{\bullet-i}
by
i

Corollary 2.2

(Saito’s Decomposition theorem)
.

Let
f:X\rightarrow Y
be a proper morphism and let
M
be a pure Hodge module. Then there is a non-canonical isomorphism in the derived category of Hodge modules

f_{*}M\cong\bigoplus\mathcal{H}^{i}(f_{*}M)[-i].

2.2.
Graded de Rham complexes

To mixed Hodge modules one can associated with the graded de Rham complexes in the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves. Let
Y
be a complex manifolds with pure dimension
d
and
M=(\mathcal{M},F_{\bullet}\mathcal{M},K;W)
be a mixed Hodge module on
Y
with Hodge filtration
F_{\bullet}\mathcal{M}
and weight filtration
W
, where
\mathcal{M}
is the underlying regular holonomic right
\mathcal{D}_{Y}
-module.

Recall that for a regular holonomic right
\mathcal{D}_{Y}
-module
\mathcal{M}
, we have the de Rham functor

(6)

\operatorname{DR}_{Y}(\mathcal{M}):=\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{D}_{Y}}^{L}\mathcal{O}_{X}

which is an object in the category of perverse sheaves
\operatorname{Perv}(Y;\mathbb{C}_{Y})
. Using the
Spencer resolution
(cf.
[
HT07
, Lemma 1.5.27]
) this can be represented as a complex

\operatorname{DR}_{Y}(\mathcal{M})=\left[M\otimes\bigwedge^{d}\mathcal{T}_{Y}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow M\otimes\mathcal{T}_{Y}\rightarrow M\right]

which lives in cohomological degrees
-d,\dots,0
. We call the complex above as the
de Rham complex
of the Hodge module
M
, and denote it by
\operatorname{DR}_{Y}(M)
. We sometimes abbreviate the notation as
\operatorname{DR}(M)
when there’s no confusion.

Definition 2.3
.

By taking the associated graded, the Hodge filtration on
\mathcal{M}
induces,

\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{-k}\operatorname{DR}(M)=\left[\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{-k-d}\mathcal{M}\otimes\bigwedge^{d}\mathcal{T}_{Y}\rightarrow\cdots\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{-k-1}\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{T}_{Y}\rightarrow\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{-k}\mathcal{M}\right]

which lives in cohomological degrees
-d,\dots,0
.
We call the above complex the
graded de Rham complex
of
M
. The construction above defines a functor:

\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{-k}\operatorname{DR}:D^{b}MHM(Y)\rightarrow D^{b}_{coh}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})

from the bounded derived category of mixed Hodge modules to the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves.

The construction of graded de Rham complex is compactible with proper pushforward and duality.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
KS21
]
, Proposition 4.10)
.

Let
f:X\rightarrow Y
be a proper morphism, then we have the natural isomorphism of functors,

Rf_{*}\circ\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{-k}\operatorname{DR}=\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{-k}\operatorname{DR}\circ Rf_{*}.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
Sai88
]
, §2.4.3)
.

Let
\omega_{X}^{\bullet}
be the dualizing complex of
X
and
M
be a Hodge module of weight
n
. Then for any integer
k
, we have an isomorphism in
D^{b}_{coh}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})

R\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{-k}\operatorname{DR}(M),\omega^{\bullet}_{X})\cong\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{k}\operatorname{DR}(\mathbf{D}(M))

where
\mathbf{D}
is the dualizing functor for Hodge modules.

The following lemma suggests that
\operatorname{gr}_{*}^{F}\operatorname{DR}(\operatorname{IC}_{X})
is a natural replacement for
\Omega^{*}_{X}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
X
be a smooth complex variety of dimension
2n
, we have

\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{-k}\operatorname{DR}(\mathbb{Q}_{X}(n)[2n])[k-n]=\Omega_{X}^{n+k}[0].

Proof.

\mathbb{Q}_{X}(n)[2n]
underlies the (pure) Hodge module of weight
0
,

\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{X}(n)[2n]=(\omega_{X},F_{\bullet}\omega_{X},\mathbb{Q}_{X}(n)[2n])\in\operatorname{HM}(X,d_{X}).

The filtration (due to the Tate twist) is given by

F_{-n-1}\omega_{X}=0\quad\text{and}\quad F_{-n}\omega_{X}=\omega_{X}.

Its associated graded de Rham complex is (in cohomological degree
-2n,\dots,0
)

\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{-k}\operatorname{DR}(\mathbb{Q}_{X}(n)[2n])=\left[\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{-k-2n}\omega_{X}\otimes\bigwedge^{2n}\mathcal{T}_{X}\rightarrow\cdots\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{-k-1}\omega_{X}\otimes\mathcal{T}_{X}\rightarrow\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{-k}\omega_{X}\right]

which is just
\Omega_{X}^{n+k}[n-k]
.∎

2.3.
Perverse-Hodge complexes and symmetry

Suppose
M
is a holomorphic symplectic variety of dimension
2n
which admits a Lagrangian fibration
f:M\rightarrow B
. We assume that
B
is a complex manifold of dimension
n
. For irreducible hyperkähler varieties, this implies
B
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{n}
by Hwang’s theorem(cf.
[
Hwa08
]
,
[
BS23
]
). After taking the Tate twist by
n
, let
\operatorname{IC}_{M}(n)
be the intersection complex of

which underlies a (pure) Hodge module of weight
0
. By Saito’s decomposition theorem, we have

Rf_{*}\operatorname{IC}_{M}(n)\cong\bigoplus_{i=-n}^{n}\mathcal{P}_{i}[-i],

where
\mathcal{P}_{i}
are pure Hodge modules of weight
i
. More precisely,
\mathcal{P}_{i}
is the triple

(\mathcal{P}_{i},F_{\bullet}\mathcal{P}_{i},{{}^{p}}\mathcal{H}^{i}(Rf_{*}\operatorname{IC}_{M}(n)[2n]))

where
{{}^{p}}\mathcal{H}^{i}(Rf_{*}\operatorname{IC}_{M}(n)[2n]))
is the underlying perverse sheaf and
F_{\bullet}
is the Hodge filtration on associated
\mathcal{D}
-module.

Definition 2.7
.

We define the perverse-Hodge complex

G_{i,k}=\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{-k}\operatorname{DR}(\mathcal{P}_{i})[-i].

Remark
.

We adapt the indexing in
[
Sch23
]
by taking a Tate twist

, which is different from the indexing in
[
SY23
]
where the perverse-Hodge complexes are originally introduced. One has
\mathcal{G}_{i,k}=G_{i-n,k-n}
in
[

]
.

Now we can state the main theorem on the symmetry of perverse-Hodge complexes,

Theorem 2.8

(Corollary
5.15
)
.

Let
f:M\rightarrow B
be a Lagrangian fibration of holomorphic symplectic variety
M
of dimension
2n
. If

admits a symplectic resolution, then we have the symmetry of perverse-Hodge complexes

G_{i,k}\cong G_{k,i}

in the derived category
D^{b}_{coh}(B)
.

3.
Geometry of symplectic varieties

In this section we review the notion of symplectic singularities and their geometry. We consider algebraic varieties over
\mathbb{C}
.

Definition 3.1

(
[
Bea00
]
)
.

A holomorphic symplectic singularity is a normal variety
X
such that there is a symplectic form
\omega
on the regular locus
X_{reg}
of
X
, such that for some (equivalently, any) resolution of singularities
\pi:\tilde{X}\rightarrow X
, the pullback
\pi^{*}\omega
to
\pi^{-1}(X_{reg})
extends to a holomorphic
2
-form
\tilde{\omega}
on
\tilde{X}
.

If moreover the extended form
\tilde{\omega}
is non-degenerate everywhere, then we say
\pi
is a symplectic resolution.

A related notion generalizing symplectic singularities is a Poisson structure on schemes. We refer the readers to
[
Kal06
]
for the definition and more details.

It is shown in
[
Bea00
,
Elk81
]
that a holomorphic symplectic variety is rational Gorenstein, and has klt singularities. Differential forms on the regular locus of the symplectic variety can be extended to the resolution of singularities, as shown by Namikawa
[
Nam01
]
. More generally, in
[
KS21
]
, the extension problem is related to the intersection cohomology Hodge module. In fact, let
j:X_{reg}\hookrightarrow X
be the inclusion of the regular locus and let
\pi:\tilde{X}\rightarrow X
be a resolution of singularities, assume
\dim(X)=2n
, then we have an isomorphism (cf.
[
KS21
, §8.4]
)

\pi_{*}\Omega^{k}_{\tilde{X}}\cong\mathcal{H}^{-2n+k}(\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{-k}\operatorname{DR}(\operatorname{IC}_{X})).

This identity will play an important role in the proof of the main theorems in section §5.

The following useful lemma tells us that the extension of differential forms on klt spaces coincides with reflexive differentials.

Lemma 3.2
.

Every holomorphic form defined on
X_{reg}
extends uniquely to a holomorphic form on
\tilde{X}
, and we have the isomorphism

\pi_{*}\Omega_{\tilde{X}}^{k}\cong j_{*}\Omega_{X_{reg}}^{k}=\Omega_{X}^{[k]}.

Proof.

Since
M
has rational singularities, in particular it has weakly rational singularities, the first statement follows directly by
[
KS21
, Corollary 1.8]
. By definition of rational singularities, we have
\omega_{X}^{GR}=\pi_{*}\omega_{X}=\omega_{X}
and
\omega_{X}
is reflexive. Thus we have isomorphism
\pi_{*}\omega_{X}=\omega_{X}\cong j_{*}\omega_{X_{reg}}
. By
[
KS21
, Theorem 1.4]
, this implies

\pi_{*}\Omega_{\tilde{X}}^{k}\cong j_{*}\Omega_{X_{reg}}^{k}.

for all
0\leq k\leq 2n
. Finally, since
X
is normal,
j_{*}\Omega_{X_{reg}}^{k}
is the reflexive hull
\Omega_{X}^{[k]}
.
∎

A key ingredient for us is the local structure of the holomorphic symplectic variety
X
. We first recall the following stratification by the singular locus.

Canonical stratification for symplectic varieties: By
[
Kal06
, Proposition 3.1]
, there is a canonical stratification
X_{i}\subset X
by Poisson subschemes, such that the open parts

are smooth and symplectic, by taking the singular locus successively. Namely, for

, we define

. For each closed point

x

Theorem 3.3

(
[
Kal06
]
, Theorem 2.3;
[
KS23
]
, A.1)
.

For any closed point

and a pointed Poisson isomorphism

where
V

x
in

, and

Kaledin’s result in

We have defined the notion of symplectic resolutions in Definition

We next show that
\pi:\tilde{Y_{x}}\rightarrow Y_{x}
is a symplectic resolution. By
[
Fu03
, Proposition 1.1]
, a resolution is a symplectic resolution if and only if it’s a crepant resolution. It suffices to show that
\pi^{*}(K_{Y_{x}})\cong K_{\tilde{Y_{x}}}
.
Suppose that
Y_{x}\cong Y_{x}\times\{v\}\subset U
is defined by the ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}_{Y_{x}}\subset\mathcal{O}_{U}
, then
\tilde{Y}_{x}\subset\tilde{U}
is defined by
\pi^{*}\mathcal{I}_{Y_{x}}
.
Since
\pi:\tilde{U}\rightarrow U
is a symplectic (crepant) resolution, we have
\pi^{*}(K_{U})=K_{\tilde{U}}
. Thus

\iota^{*}K_{\tilde{U}}=\iota^{*}\pi^{*}(K_{U})=\pi^{*}\iota^{*}(K_{U})=\pi^{*}K_{Y_{x}}

Since
\tilde{Y_{x}}
and
\tilde{U}
are smooth, by adjunction we have

K_{\tilde{Y_{x}}}=\iota^{*}K_{\tilde{U}}\otimes\operatorname{det}\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{Y_{x}}/\tilde{U}}=\pi^{*}K_{Y_{x}}\otimes\operatorname{det}\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{Y_{x}}/\tilde{U}}.

Since
p_{2}\circ\pi:\tilde{U}\rightarrow V
is smooth, by
[
Sta18
, 0473]
we have
\operatorname{det}\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{Y_{x}}/\tilde{U}}=\pi^{*}\operatorname{det}(\mathcal{N}_{\{v\}/V})\cong\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{Y_{x}}}
.
Thus
K_{\tilde{Y_{x}}}=\pi^{*}K_{Y_{x}}
, this shows that
\tilde{Y_{x}}
is a symplectic (crepant) resolution for
Y_{x}
.
∎

4.
Perverse coherent t-structures

We review the notion of perverse coherent sheaves. Perverse coherent sheaves can be viewed as the commutative algebraic counter-part of perverse sheaves from the topological perspective. The perverse coherent t-structure corresponds to the perverse t-structure on the derived category of constructible complexes via Riemann-Hilbert correspondence, and corresponds to the usual t-structure on the derived category of coherent sheaves via Grothendieck duality. We refer the readers to
[
Kas03
,
AB10
,
BBL+23
]
for more detailed discussions.

4.1.
Perverse coherent t-structures

In this section we consider
X
a finite type separated scheme defined over field
k
which admits a dualizing complex. Here we choose
\omega^{\bullet}_{X}
to be the normalized dualizing complex in the sense of
[
Sta18
,
Tag OA7M
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

A perversity of
X
is a function
p:X\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
on the underlying topological space of the scheme
X
, such that
p
is non-decreasing under specialization, i.e,
p(y)\geq p(x)
if
y\in\overline{\{x\}}
.

Using the results in
[
Kas03
,
AB10
]
, we can define two families of subcategories,

{{}^{p}}D^{\leq k}_{coh}(X)=\{K\in D^{b}_{coh}(X)\mid i^{*}_{x}K\in D^{\leq k+p(x)}_{coh}(\kappa(x))\ \text{for all $x\in X$}\}

{{}^{p}}D^{\geq k}_{coh}(X)=\{K\in D^{b}_{coh}(X)\mid i^{!}_{x}K\in D^{\geq k+p(x)}_{coh}(\kappa(x))\ \text{for all $x\in X$}\}.

It’s shown that they form a bounded t-structure on
D^{b}_{coh}(X)
if and only if

p(y)-p(x)\leq\operatorname{codim}(y)-\operatorname{codim}(x)

for every
x,y\in X
such that
y\in\overline{\{x\}}
. We write the heart as

\operatorname{Perv}^{p}_{coh}(X)={{}^{p}}D^{\geq 0}_{coh}(X)\cap{{}^{p}}D^{\leq 0}_{coh}(X).

Definition 4.2

(
[
KS21
]
, Lemma 18.4)
.

Define perversity
m:X\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
with

m(x)=\lfloor\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{codim}(x)\rfloor,

and its dual function

\hat{m}(x)=\lceil\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{codim}(x)\rceil.

They define perverse t-structures

{{}^{m}}D^{\leq k}_{coh}(X)=\{K\in D^{b}_{coh}(X)\mid\operatorname{codim}\operatorname{Supp}\mathcal{H}^{i}(K)\geq 2(i-k)\ \text{for all $i\in\mathbb{Z}$}\}

{{}^{\hat{m}}}D_{\mathit{coh}}^{\geq k}(X)=\{K\in D^{b}_{coh}(X)\mid\operatorname{codim}\operatorname{Supp}\mathcal{H}^{i}(K)\geq 2(i-k)-1\ \text{for all $i\in\mathbb{Z}$}\}

This also describes the subcategories with
\geq k
by duality.

For our convenience we introduce the following definition,

Definition 4.3
.

Let
X
be a complex algebraic variety of dimension
2n
which admits a dualizing complex.

(1)

We say the intersection complex Hodge module
\operatorname{IC}_{X}
is
coherent m-perverse
, if it satisfies the following support condition

\operatorname{codim}\operatorname{Supp}\mathcal{H}^{i}(\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{-k}\operatorname{DR}(\operatorname{IC}_{X})[k-2n])\geq 2i

for all
i
and
k
.

(2)

We say the intersection complex Hodge module
\operatorname{IC}_{X}
is
strongly coherent m-perverse
, if it satisfies the following support condition

\operatorname{codim}\operatorname{Supp}\mathcal{H}^{i}(\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{-k}\operatorname{DR}(\operatorname{IC}_{X})[k-2n])\geq 2(i+1)

for all
i\geq 1
and
k
.

Let
\tau_{\leq k}
and
\tau_{\geq k}
be the truncation functors with respect to the standard t-structure on
D^{b}_{coh}(X)
, and

\mathbb{D}(-)=R\mathcal{H}om(-,\omega^{\bullet}_{X}[-n]):D^{b}_{coh}(X)\rightarrow D^{b}_{coh}(X)^{op}

be the Grothendieck-duality functor, where
n
is the dimension of
X
.

We also need the perverse function
p(x)=n-\dim\overline{\{x\}}
. Following from local duality, the next lemma shows that the
p
-perverse t-structure on
D^{b}_{coh}(X)
is the Grothendieck-dual of the usual t-structure on
D^{b}_{coh}(X)
.

Lemma 4.4

(
[
AB10
]
, Lemma 3.3)
.

Suppose
X
is equidimensional and
K\in D^{b}_{coh}(X)
, then

(1)

K\in{{}^{p}}D^{\leq 0}(X)
if and only if
\mathbb{D}(K)\in D^{\geq 0}
.

(2)

K\in{{}^{p}}D^{\geq 0}(X)
if and only if
\mathbb{D}(K)\in D^{\leq 0}
.

In particular,
K\in\operatorname{Perv}_{coh}^{p}(X)
if and only if
\mathbb{D}(K)
is concentrated in degree
0
.

4.2.
Intersection complexes of coherent sheaves

Definition 4.5

(
[
Sch15
]
, Definition 23.1)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a reflexive coherent sheaf. We call the complex

\mathcal{IC}(\mathcal{F})=(\tau_{\leq\ell(X)-1}\circ\mathbb{D}\circ\tau_{\leq\ell(X)-2}\circ\cdots\circ\mathbb{D}\circ\tau_{\leq 1}\circ\mathbb{D})\mathcal{F}

the
intersection complex
of
\mathcal{F}
. Here
\ell(X)
is the smallest odd integer such that
2\ell+1\geq\dim X
.

Definition 4.6

(
[
Sch15
]
, Definition 23.8)
.

Let
j:U\hookrightarrow X
be an open subset with
\operatorname{codim}(X\setminus U)\geq 2
, and
\mathcal{E}
be a locally free sheaf on
U
. Then we call the complex

\mathcal{IC}_{X}(\mathcal{E})=\mathcal{IC}(j_{*}\mathcal{E})

the
intersection complex
of
\mathcal{E}
(with respect to
X
).

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in D^{\geq 0}_{coh}(X)
be a bounded complex of coherent sheaves. Assume that there exists an open subset
j:U\hookrightarrow X
with
\operatorname{codim}(X\setminus U)\geq 2
, such that
j^{*}\mathcal{H}^{0}\mathcal{C}
is locally free. Suppose we have following support conditions

\operatorname{codim}\operatorname{Supp}\mathcal{H}^{j}\mathcal{C}\geq 2j+2\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{codim}\operatorname{Supp}\mathcal{H}^{j}\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{C})\geq 2j+2

for
j\geq 1
, then
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{IC}_{X}(j^{*}\mathcal{H}^{0}\mathcal{C})
, which is uniquely determined by the restriction
j^{*}\mathcal{H}^{0}\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

We follow the same strategy as in
[
Sch15
, Proposition 22.1]
. We show by induction that
\tau_{\leq n}\mathcal{C}
can be reconstructed starting from
\mathcal{H}^{0}\mathcal{C}
for each
n\geq 0
. The
n=0
case is clear as
\tau_{\leq 0}=\mathcal{H}^{0}\mathcal{C}
.

For
j\geq n+1
, we have
\operatorname{codim}\operatorname{Supp}\mathcal{H}^{j}\mathcal{C}\geq 2j+2\geq j+n+3
. Thus
\tau_{\geq n+1}\mathcal{C}\in{{}^{p}}D_{coh}^{\leq-(n+3)}
. Consider the distinguished triangle,

\tau_{\leq n}\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\tau_{\geq n+1}\mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{+1}\tau_{\leq n}\mathcal{C}[1]

Taking the Grothendieck dual, we see from Lemma
4.4
that
\mathbb{D}(\tau_{\geq n+1}\mathcal{C})\in D^{\geq n+3}
. Therefore,

\tau_{\leq n+1}\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{C})\simeq\tau_{\leq n+1}\mathbb{D}(\tau_{\leq n}\mathcal{C}).

Repeat the above argument but replace
\mathcal{C}
with
\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{C})
, we have

\tau_{\leq n+2}\mathcal{C}\simeq\tau_{\leq n+2}\mathbb{D}(\tau_{\leq n+1}\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{C}))\simeq\tau_{\leq n+2}\mathbb{D}(\tau_{\leq n+1}\mathbb{D}(\tau_{\leq n}\mathcal{C})).

Repeat the above argument inductively until we reach
n=0
, in which case
\tau_{\leq 0}\mathcal{C}\simeq j_{*}j^{*}\mathcal{H}^{0}\mathcal{C}
since
j^{*}\mathcal{H}^{0}\mathcal{C}
is locally free in codimension
2
. Therefore we conclude that
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{IC}_{X}(j^{*}\mathcal{H}^{0}\mathcal{C})
.
∎

In particular, the proposition above applies when
\operatorname{IC}_{X}
is strongly coherent m-perverse, as shown in the following important corollary.

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
X
be a complex algebraic variety which admits a dualizing complex. Suppose that
\operatorname{IC}_{X}
is strongly coherent m-perverse, then

\operatorname{gr}_{-k}^{F}\operatorname{DR}(\operatorname{IC}_{X}(n))[k-n]=\mathcal{IC}_{X}(\Omega^{n+k}_{X_{reg}}).

In particular, the complex
\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{-k}\operatorname{DR}(\operatorname{IC}_{X}(n))
is uniquely determined by its restrction to the smooth locus
j^{*}\mathcal{H}^{k-n}\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{-k}\operatorname{DR}(\operatorname{IC}_{X}(n))=\Omega^{n+k}_{X_{reg}}
.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{IC}_{X}
is a polarisable Hodge module, its de Rham complex is self-dual up to a shift in the filtration, see also
[
KS21
, Corollary 4.6]
. Thus duality relates different graded quotients
\mathcal{C}_{k}
.
More precisely, for
\mathcal{C}_{k}:=\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{-k}\operatorname{DR}(\operatorname{IC}_{X})[k-2n]
, the compatibility of
\operatorname{gr}\operatorname{DR}
with duality gives
\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{C}_{k})\cong\mathcal{C}_{2n-k}[n]
by Lemma
2.5
. Since
\operatorname{IC}_{X}
is strongly coherent m-perverse we have
\tau_{\geq 1}\mathcal{C}_{k}\in{{}^{m}}D_{coh}^{\leq-1}(X)
, it’s then easy to check that we also have
\tau_{\geq 1}\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{C}_{k})\in{{}^{m}}D_{coh}^{\leq-1}(X)
. Now we can apply Proposition
4.7
and conclude.
∎

We will use an inductive argument using the local structure of symplectic varities and reduce to the isolated singularities case, in which situation we have enough vanishing results to conclude.

Example 5.1
.

To illustrate the failure of

being m-perverse coherent, consider an isolated symplectic singularity

of dimension

5.3.
Symmetry of perverse-Hodge complexes

As a consequence of Hard Lefschetz theorem Theorem
2.1
, by taking the graded de Rham complexes, the relative Hard Lefschetz theorem gives isomorphisms

(21)

\ell^{i}:G_{-i,k}\rightarrow G_{i,i+k}[2i]

for every
i\geq 1
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Theorem 5.15
.

Let
f:M\rightarrow B
be a Lagrangian fibration of a holomorphic symplectic variety
M
of dimension
2n
. If
M
admits a symplectic resolution, then we have the following quasi-isomorphism of perverse-Hodge complexes in the derived category
D^{b}_{coh}(B)
,

(22)

G_{i,k}\cong G_{k,i}.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.14
and the relative Hard Lefschetz theorem, the following composition is an isomorphism in the derived category
D^{b}_{coh}(B)

G_{i,k}\xrightarrow{\ell^{-i}}G_{-i,k-i}[-2i]\xrightarrow{\mu_{-i,k-i}}G_{-k,i-k}[-2k]\xrightarrow{\ell^{k}}G_{k,i}.

Therefore we have
G_{i,k}\cong G_{k,i}
.
∎

5.4.
Numerical ”Perverse=Hodge”

In the compact case, on the level of global cohomology, the symmetry of perverse-Hodge complexes gives rise to an equality of perverse numbers and the usual Hodge numbers.

Let first recall the definition. For a possibly singular compact holomorphic symplectic variety with a Lagrangian fibration
f:M\rightarrow B
, the intersection cohomology
IH^{*}(M,\mathbb{C})
carries a pure Hodge structure. We can consider the associated Hodge numbers

Ih^{i,j}(M)=h^{i,j}(IH^{*}(M,\mathbb{C}))\in\mathbb{Z},\quad\text{for all $i,j\in\mathbb{N}$}.

Meanwhile, the perverse t-structure associated with the Lagrangian fibration
f
induces a perverse filtration on the intersection cohomology
IH^{*}(M,\mathbb{C})

P_{0}IH^{*}(M,\mathbb{C})\subset P_{1}IH^{*}(M,\mathbb{C})\subset\cdots P_{k}IH^{*}(M,\mathbb{C})\subset\cdots IH^{*}(M,\mathbb{C}).

We can define the following invariants called the perverse numbers

{{}^{\mathfrak{p}}}Ih^{i,j}(f)=\dim\operatorname{Gr}_{i}^{P}IH^{i+j}(M,\mathbb{C})=\dim(P_{i}IH^{i+j}(M,\mathbb{C})/P_{i-1}IH^{i+j}(M,\mathbb{C})).

Using the symmetry of perverse-Hodge complexes, we now reprove the numerical result
[
FSY22
]
.

Theorem 5.16

(
[
FSY22
]
, Theorem 0.4)
.

Let
f:M\rightarrow B
be a Lagrangian fibration of compact holomorphic symplectic variety
M
of dimension
2n
which admits a symplectic resolution, then we have

(23)

{{}^{\mathfrak{p}}}Ih^{i,j}(f)=Ih^{i,j}(M).

Proof.

Applying the global cohomology to the symmetry, we have
H^{i+j}(B,G_{i,k})\simeq H^{i+j}(G_{k,i})
. Taking the direct sum with respect to the index
k
, we have

\bigoplus_{k=-n}^{n}\dim H^{i+j}(B,G_{i,k})=\bigoplus_{k=-n}^{n}\dim H^{i+j}(B,\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{-k}\operatorname{DR}(\mathcal{P}_{i})[-i])=\dim H^{i+j}(B,\mathcal{P}_{i}[-i]\otimes\mathbb{C}).

concerning perverse numbers. By the symmetry the direct sum above equals to

\bigoplus_{k=-n}^{n}\dim H^{i+j}(B,G_{k,i})=\bigoplus_{k=-n}^{n}\dim H^{i+j}(B,\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{-i}\operatorname{DR}(\mathcal{P}_{k}[-k]))=\dim H^{i+j}(B,\operatorname{gr}^{F}_{-i}\operatorname{DR}(Rf_{*}\operatorname{IC}_{M}(n))

concerning the Hodge numbers. This shows that

{{}^{\mathfrak{p}}}Ih^{i,j}(f)=Ih^{i,j}(M).

∎

6.
Singular Higgs moduli space

In this section we apply our results to Hitchin fibrations of singular Higgs moduli space (without coprime condition). The results in this section may not be new and should be known to experts in the field.

The singular Higgs moduli space has symplectic singularities, and it admits symplectic resolution if and only if the base curve has genus 1, or the base curve has genus 2 and the rank

G_{i,k}\simeq G_{k,i}

Proof.

This follows directly from Theorem

7.
Further questions

We conclude by listing some problems yet to be discovered.

Question 7.1
.

Establish the symmetry
G_{i,k}\simeq G_{k,i}
or its variants for singular symplectic varieties in general.

Question 7.2
.

(Multiplicativity)
Are perverse-Hodge complexes multiplicative (via cup-product) with respect to both of the perverse index and the Hodge index?

Such expectation comes from the multiplicativity of the perverse filtration in the proof of the P=W conjecture. On the level of global cohomology this was shown to be true in
[
SY22
, Appendix A]
. As a folklore conjecture, the perverse filtration (on the sheaf-theoretic level) for the Lagrangian fibration is expected to have a multiplicative splitting .

Question 7.3
.

(Deformation invariance)
Let
f:M\rightarrow B
and
f^{\prime}:M\rightarrow B
be two Lagrangian fibrations and
G_{i,k}
,
G^{\prime}_{i,k}
be perverse-Hodge complexes respectively.
Assume that
M
and
M^{\prime}
are deformation invariant, do we have
G_{i,k}\simeq G^{\prime}_{i,k}
?

The numerical question has a positive answer. When
M
and
M^{\prime}
are deformation equivalent irreducible symplectic varieties, the numercial perverse Hodge numbers are deformation invariant provided that
b_{2}\geq 5
in the compact case, see
[
FSY22
, Theorem 0.1]
.

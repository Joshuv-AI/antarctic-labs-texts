Using Generative AI Personas Increases Collective Diversity in Human Ideation Thanks: Citation : Authors. Title. Pages…. DOI:000000/11111.
Yun Wan, Yoram M Kalman

Abstract
This study challenges the widely-reported tradeoff between generative AI’s (GenAI) contribution to creative outcomes and decreased diversity of these outcomes. We modified the design of such a study, by Doshi and Hauser (2024), in which participants wrote short stories either aided or unaided by GenAI plot ideas [ 1 ] . In the modified study, plot ideas were generated through ten unique GenAI "personas" with diverse traits (e.g. cultural backgrounds, thinking styles, genre preferences), creating a pool of 300 story plots. While plot ideas from any individual persona showed high similarity (average cosine similarity of 0.92), ideas across different personas exhibited substantial variation (average similarity of 0.20). When human participants wrote stories based on these diverse plot ideas, their collective outputs maintained the same level of diversity as stories written without GenAI assistance, effectively eliminating the diversity reduction observed in [ 1 ] . Traditional text analytics further revealed that GenAI-assisted stories featured greater diversity in descriptive and emotional language compared to purely human-generated stories without GenAI assistance. Our findings demonstrate that introducing diversity at the AI input stage through distinct personas can preserve and potentially enhance the collective diversity of human creative outputs when collaborating with GenAI.

Introduction

Several recent studies of the contributions of GenAI to human performance of creative tasks demonstrated an interesting trade-off: GenAI can boost the quality of the creative outputs, but this involves a reduction in the diversity of the outputs
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. For example, Doshi and Hauser
[
1
]
demonstrated that GenAI plot ideas enhanced the creativity of individual storywriters but, notably, reduced the collective diversity of the stories. Their experiment showed that when participants had access to GenAI-generated plot ideas, their stories were rated as more creative on an individual level, but the semantic similarity between stories increased significantly, with cosine similarity scores rising by 10.7% and 8.9% for participants with access to one or five GenAI ideas, respectively.

This finding about the reduction in collective diversity was in line with at least two previous findings from earlier studies. In the first case, Boston Consulting Group consultants using GenAI were more productive and produced better ideas, but there was a marked reduction in the variability of their ideas compared to consultants who did not use GenAI
[
2
]
. The consultants using GenAI were found to generate more solutions to client problems and these solutions were rated as higher quality, but the diversity of solutions decreased according to their semantic similarity metrics. In the second study, Chen and Chan
[
3
]
demonstrated that GenAI support improved the quality of nonexpert ad copies, but decreased the semantic divergence (ie, diversity) of the ads. Their research revealed that participants using GenAI for ghostwriting or reviewing produced ad copies with higher linguistic quality and persuasiveness, but the semantic similarity of user’ outputs increased by 60% and 32% respectively, indicating a significant standardization effect.

Is this trade-off between GenAI assistance in a creative task and the diversity of the creative outcome an inevitable result of working with GenAI? We hypothesize it is not. Instead, we propose that the reduced diversity of creative outcomes stems directly from an unintentional narrowing of the diversity in the outputs prompted from the GenAI. We tested this hypothesis by replicating the experiment of
[
1
]
, but instead of using the simple prompt that Doshi and Hauser used in their experiment, we prompted ten diverse GenAI “personas” (e.g.
[
4
,
5
]
) to suggest plot ideas.

Why might providing the participants with plot ideas from ten diverse Gen-AI personas result in more diverse stories? GenAI’s agency and autonomy point to its ability to boost human creativity, much like a human collaborator in a team. By acting as an active and effective contributor, a GenAI agent can reshape collaborative processes to foster greater productivity and creativity. This analogy suggests the well-established link between participant diversity and improved creative outputs in collaborative settings. Diverse teams, characterized by a variety of cognitive styles, backgrounds, and perspectives, are consistently shown to produce more innovative and creative results
[
6
,
7
]
. A common limitation across all three previous studies was that they employed homogeneous GenAI prompting strategies, using identical or highly similar prompts to generate AI assistance for human participants (e.g.
[
1
]
used GPT-4 large language model (LLM) via OpenAI’s API). This standardized approach, while methodologically sound, may have inadvertently constrained the diversity of GenAI outputs.

We hypothesized that diversifying the prompts could lead to more diverse ideas and would increase the collective diversity of the creative outcomes. This diversification could be achieved by using diverse GenAI personas
[
4
,
5
]
. Prompting different personas for plot ideas is analogous to pairing the human participants to collaborate with a diverse group of GenAI collaborators, rather than having multiple humans interact with the same standardized GenAI. In our experiment, we created ten distinct GenAI personas to generate plot ideas. Our analysis revealed an interesting pattern: While plot ideas from any single persona lacked diversity, the ideas generated
across
different personas showed substantial variation. When human participants wrote stories using these diverse plot ideas, their collective work maintained the same level of diversity as stories written without any GenAI assistance. This effectively eliminated the negative impact on collective diversity that previous research had observed when using GenAI for creative tasks.

2.2
Collective diversity of stories by GenAI-aided human participants increased

After measuring the diversity of plot ideas generated by different GenAI personas, we recruited 100 human participants, using the same platform (Prolific) and recruitment criteria in
[
1
]
. The participants were randomly assigned to write an eight sentence story under one of three experimental conditions: a control group without AI support, a group with the option to request access to one AI-generated plot idea, and a group with the option to request access to up to five AI-generated plot ideas. In the two AI-assisted conditions, participants received ideas randomly chosen from the diverse pool of 300 plot ideas generated by the ten personas. After excluding data from three participants in the control group who reported using external AI to inspire their stories, we analyzed 97 stories: 41 in the control group, 27 in the 1-plot group, and 29 in the 5-plots group. The control group is larger since it included five participants from the 1-plot group and three participants from the 5-plots group, who did not click on a button to request a plot idea, and thus their stories were, essentially, under the same condition as the control group (un-aided by GenAI).

In addition, we analyzed the linguistic differences between stories assisted by AI (the 1-plot and 5-plot conditions) and stories based on humans only (the 0-plot condition) with traditional text analytics. We used the Natural Language Toolkit (NLTK), a Python library for natural language processing, and its built-in corpora, particularly its English stopwords corpus, for filtering common words, and its WordNet lexical database for word categorization and semantic analysis. 3
Discussion

Our research demonstrates that using diverse GenAI personas to inspire human creative tasks, rather than directly prompting the generic LLM, provides plot ideas that lead the human participants to more diverse outputs. These findings have notable implications for our current practice in human-AI ideation tasks design and expands our understanding of synergy in human-GenAI collaboration. Our results suggest that humans should fully utilize GenAI’s conversational and creational affordances to introduce diversity into the collaboration dynamics
[
8
]
, treating GenAI as an agentic participant or participants in collaborative efforts. This perspective emphasizes the importance of harmonizing humans and GenAI interaction
[
9
]
, particularly in creative contexts.

Our demonstration that the level of diversity of GenAI-assisted ideation can be restored to non AI-assisted levels begs the question whether GenAI can increase the diversity of creative tasks beyond that level. In fact, our traditional text analytics results provide preliminary evidence that this is a possibility. Furthermore, recent discourse on mechanistic interpretability suggests that foundational models may encode sparse, high-dimensional representations of concepts and computations, which are then projected into dense, efficient forms for practical execution
[
10
,
11
]
. This implicit sparsity enables foundation models to represent considerably more features and patterns than their explicit dimensions would suggest. If these directions prove productive, human-GenAI collaboration could benefit from this yet untapped source of diversity. This diversity could, in turn, positively impact the outputs of human-GenAI collaborations on creative tasks. This approach is an alternative to deterministic and dystopic interpretations of findings in this emerging field
[
12
]
. This positive vision aligns with our findings, with optimistic (yet risk-aware) visions of the potential contribution of LLMs to collective intelligence
[
13
]
, as well as optimistic perspective reflected in
[
1
]
that current limitations of human-GenAI creative collaborations are temporary.

Materials and Methods

The study utilized ChatGPT’s o1-preview model to develop ten diverse personas based on traits such as cultural background, thinking styles, and interests. Each persona generated 30 adventure-themed story plots using the gpt-4o model with parameters set to temperature = 0.9 and top-p = 1. Generated plots were verified for uniqueness. To analyze diversity, the semantic embeddings of all 300 plots were calculated using OpenAI’s “
text-embedding-ada-002
” model with 1536 default embedding vector length. The cosine similarity was used to compare each persona’s plots against those of other personas and within their own plots, confirming inter- and intra-persona diversity.

The experiment was setup on Qualtrics, and distributed via Prolific. Participant stories (n=97) were also embedded using the same embedding model to assess story diversity across experimental conditions.
The creative diversity of the stories was calculated using cosine similarity scores between each story and the collective average of stories within each condition, replicating the method in
[
1
]
. For the traditional text analytics, we used the Natural Language Toolkit (NLTK), a Python library for natural language processing, and its built-in corpora, for identifying narrative elements such as actions (verbs), descriptions (adjectives), and character names (proper nouns), enabling systematic quantification of story elements across the dataset and a nuanced examination of narrative elements. Full technical details, prompts and output examples are provided in the Appendix. The human subject study was approved by the ethics committee of the Open University of Israel on Sep 30, 2024.

Appendix

Appendix A
Developing the ten diverse personas

To explore the impact of diversity in plot ideas on creative outputs, we developed a range of diverse personas for generating plot ideas using ChatGPT’s o1preview model. The process began with an initial prompt requesting categories on which personas could differ, aiming to maximize diversity. This prompt yielded suggestions such as cultural background, literary genre preferences (excluding adventure which is the main genre of the story participants were asked to write), and thinking styles. Building on these, additional categories were generated, resulting in a comprehensive list of 14 categories, each with 4–20 classes.

The categories included traits such as cultural background (e.g., East Asian, Indigenous Cultures), thinking styles (e.g., analytical, divergent), and interests (e.g., gardener, blogger). Using this diverse set of traits, the model was prompted to create ten detailed persona descriptions of at least 100 words, with each persona randomly combining traits from different categories. These personas were outputted as text files with rich descriptions to guide the generation of plot ideas.

This method ensured that the personas—and the plot ideas derived from them—captured a broad spectrum of diversity, serving as a foundation for testing the relationship between input diversity and creative outputs in human-GenAI collaborations.

Appendix B
Prompting the personas for plot ideas

These personas were incorporated into an assistant on the Azure OpenAI platform using gpt-4o version 2024-05-13, temperature=0.9, top P = 1. The prompt was:

You are an AI assistant that simulates different personas who write story plots. You are provided with a description of the persona, and the number of story plots that persona is asked to write.
For each of the plot ideas, generate an exactly three-sentence plot idea for a short story that is an adventure on another planet.
Important Instructions:
The plot should be understandable by an adult with 12 years of education.
Do not include any references to the persona’s characteristics, traits, or theoretical constructs in the plot idea.
Read the persona’s description to understand their style. Write each plot idea in the style of the persona, reflecting their unique perspective and voice.
The persona is not the protagonist of the story, and the protagonist does not have the traits of the persona.
The plot ideas should stand on their own, without any mention of the personas’ descriptions.
The output should include only the plot idea, without titles, numbering, or anything else.
Plan the plot idea in exactly three sentences, ensuring each sentence adds to the story.
The plots should not be similar to a previous plot
The format of the output is a csv file

The prompt that was used in the "advisor":
"Create thirty-one plot ideas of exactly three sentences each that [persona name] would write"
. The first thirty were selected and saved. ChatGPT was used to verify the story plots are not essentially similar, after several iterations that resulted in some plots being essentially the same. This phenomenon was resolved when the temperature was raised from 0.7 to 0.9.

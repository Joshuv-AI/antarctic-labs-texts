Global analytic solutions of a pseudospherical Novikov equation
Priscila Leal da Silva

Abstract
In this paper we consider a Novikov equation, recently shown to describe pseudospherical surfaces, to extend some recent results of regularity of its solutions. By making use of the global well-posedness in Sobolev spaces, for analytic initial data in Gevrey spaces we prove some new estimates for the solution in order to use the Kato-Masuda Theorem and obtain a lower bound for the radius of spatial analyticity. After that, we use embeddings between spaces to then conclude that the unique solution is, in fact, globally analytic in both variables. Finally, the global analyticity of the solution is used to prove that it endows the strip ( 0 , ∞ ) × ℝ (0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R} with a global analytic metric associated to pseudospherical surfaces obtained in [ 18 ] .



Keywords
Global well-posedness, analytic solutions, pseudospherical surfaces

Introduction

In this paper we consider the Cauchy problem

\displaystyle u_{t}=\partial_{x}u^{2}+\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}\left(u^{2}+\partial_{x}u^{2}\right),\quad u(0,x):=u_{0}(x),

(1.1)

and

[
17
]

[
5
]

[

]

1

\omega_{i}=f_{i1}dx+f_{i2}dt
,

, given by

\displaystyle f_{11}=u-u_{xx},\quad f_{12}=2u(u-u_{xx})+\frac{4}{m_{1}}uu_{x}-2u_{x}^{2}-2u^{2},

\displaystyle f_{21}=\mu(u-u_{xx})\pm m_{1}\sqrt{1+\mu^{2}},\quad f_{22}=\mu\left[2u(u-u_{xx})+\frac{4}{m_{1}}uu_{x}-2u_{x}^{2}-2u^{2}\right],

\displaystyle f_{31}=\pm\sqrt{1+\mu^{2}}(u-u_{xx})+m_{1}\mu,\quad f_{32}=\pm\sqrt{1+\mu^{2}}\left[2u(u-u_{xx})+\frac{4}{m_{1}}uu_{x}-2u_{x}^{2}-2u^{2}\right],

where

can be formulated for the

f_{11}+f_{31}&-f_{21}\end{pmatrix},\quad T=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}f_{22}&f_{12}-f_{32}\\
f_{12}+f_{32}&-f_{22}\end{pmatrix}.

In recent works, equation (

[
16

the authors showed that there exists a unique local analytic solution for the Cauchy problem with initial data in the Himonas-Misiolek space
[

lead to the explicit construction of the metric

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} g=&\left[(u-u_{xx})^{2}+(\mu(u-u_{xx})\pm m_{1}\sqrt{1+\mu^{2}})^{2}\right]dx^{2}\\
&+2\left(2u(u-u_{xx})+\psi\right)\left[(1+\mu^{2})(u-u_{xx})\pm m_{1}\mu\sqrt{1+\mu^{2}}\right]dxdt\\
&+(1+\mu^{2})\left(2u(u-u_{xx})+\psi\right)^{2}dt^{2},\end{aligned}

(1.2)

where

and

are given as before, for analytic pseudospherical surfaces, see

Equation (1.1) has been referred to by various names, including generalised Camassa-Holm equation and generalised Degasperis-Procesi equation (see
[

,
16
]
). However, due to the absence of the typical peakon solutions found in the Camassa-Holm and Degasperis-Procesi equations, as well as a known bi-Hamiltonian formulation, this equation appears to be significantly different from both, at least from the perspective of integrable systems. Consequently, we choose to refer to it as “pseudospherical Novikov equation”, as discussed in references

The main result of this paper, as stated in Theorem
1.1
below, generalizes the result in
[
16
]
by showing that the solution is, in fact, globally analytic in both variables. For that we restrict our analysis to analytic initial data in analytic Gevrey spaces to then extend the solution by making use of global well-posedness in Sobolev spaces. Given
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we say that

is in the Gevrey space

\|u\|_{G^{\sigma,s}}:=\|e^{\sigma|D_{x}|}u\|_{H^{s}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{2\sigma|\xi|}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s}|\hat{u}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right)^{1/2}

Following the Paley-Wiener characterization

, a function in

is the real line restriction a holomorphic function on a strip of width

. In the case where

, functions are analytic and for this purpose we will consider our initial data in

While the form (
1.1

\Lambda^{-2}\partial_{x}^{2}=\Lambda^{-2}-1
, we can rewrite (

) as

\displaystyle u_{t}=\partial_{x}u^{2}-u^{2}+\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}u^{2}+\Lambda^{-2}u^{2},

Theorem 1.1
.

Given

, with

is non-negative, then the Cauchy problem of (

\displaystyle r(t)\geq L_{3}e^{-L_{1}e^{L_{2}t}},

(1.4)

where the positive parameters

depend only on the initial data and

As it will be clear in the remaining of this paper, both the index

\displaystyle m(t,q(t,x))\geq m_{0}(x)\exp\left(-2\int_{0}^{t}(u-3u_{x})(s,q(t,x))ds\right),

(1.5)

where

]
) uniquely defined for

]
for more details.

In the proof of Theorem

we will obtain the very rough estimate (

) for the radius of spatial analyticity

for every

. The double exponential provokes a very strong and fast decay, meaning that the estimate is rather trivial. In this sense, even though Theorem

11
,
12

Theorem

2
Function spaces and well-posedness

In this section we will briefly discuss the theory of function spaces required and enunciate the well-posedness results that will be used to prove Theorem
1.1
.

The first important property is that the Gevrey spaces form a decreasing scale of Banach spaces, that is, if
0<\sigma^{\prime}<\sigma
, then
\|\cdot\|_{G^{\sigma^{\prime},s}}\leq\|\cdot\|_{G^{\sigma,s}}
and
G^{\sigma,s}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow G^{\sigma^{\prime},s}(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover,
\|\varphi\|_{G^{\sigma,s}}\leq\|\varphi\|_{G^{\sigma,s^{\prime}}}
for any real choices
s^{\prime}>s
, and because
\|\varphi\|_{G^{\sigma,s}}=\|e^{\sigma|D_{x}|}\varphi\|_{H^{s}}
, it is easy to see that it is an algebra for
\sigma>0
and
s>1/2
so that

\displaystyle\|\varphi\psi\|_{G^{\sigma,s}}\leq c_{s}\|\varphi\|_{G^{\sigma,s}}\|\psi\|_{G^{\sigma,s}},

(2.1)

for some positive constant
c_{s}
depending only on
s
, and the properties

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}\varphi\|_{G^{\sigma^{\prime},s}}\leq\frac{e^{-1}}{\sigma-\sigma^{\prime}}\|\varphi\|_{G^{\sigma,s}},\quad s\geq 0,\quad 0<\sigma^{\prime}<\sigma\leq 1,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}\varphi\|_{G^{\sigma,s}}\leq\|\varphi\|_{G^{\sigma,s+1}},\quad s\in\mathbb{R},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{-2}\varphi\|_{G^{\sigma,s}}=\|\varphi\|_{G^{\sigma,s-2}},\quad s\in\mathbb{R},

(2.4)

can be easily determined, see
[
3
]
. In the inequalities above and throughout the paper,
\Lambda^{-2}
is defined by
\Lambda^{-2}\varphi=g\ast\varphi(x)
, where
g(x)=e^{-|x|}/2
and
\ast
is the usual convolution, for all
\varphi\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
.

Given an initial data
u_{0}(x)\in G^{1,s}(\mathbb{R})
, for
F(u)
given by the right-hand side of (
1.1
),
s>1/2
and
0<\sigma<1
, we can use the relations above to obtain

\|F(u_{0})\|_{G^{\sigma,s}}\leq\frac{M}{1-\sigma},

and, if for a given
R>0
we
\|u-u_{0}\|_{G^{1,s}}<R
and
\|v-u_{0}\|_{G^{1,s}}<R
, also

\|F(u)-F(v)\|_{G^{\sigma,s}}\leq\frac{L}{1-\sigma}\|u-v\|_{G^{1,s}},

where
M=3c_{s}e^{-1}\|u_{0}\|_{G^{1,s}}^{2}
and
L=6c_{s}e^{-1}(R+\|u_{0}\|_{G^{1,s}})
, and
c_{s}
is the constant provided by the algebra property.

Based on the two estimates, we can use the following result, see Theorem 1 of
[
2
]
and references therein, to obtain local well-posedness of (

):

Proposition 2.1

(

)
.

Let

be a scale of decreasing Banach spaces for

, that is,
X_{\delta}\subset X_{\delta^{\prime}},\|\cdot\|_{\delta^{\prime}}\leq\|\cdot\|_{\delta},0<\delta^{\prime}<\delta\leq 1,
and consider the Cauchy problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\frac{du}{dt}=G(u(t))},\\
u(0)=u_{0}.\end{cases}

(2.5)

Given
\delta_{0}\in(0,1]
and
u_{0}\in X_{\delta_{0}}
, assume that
G
satisfies the following conditions:

1.

For
0<\delta^{\prime}<\delta<\delta_{0}
,
R>0
and
a>0
, if the function
t\mapsto u(t)
is holomorphic on
\{t\in\mathbb{C};0<|t|<a(\delta_{0}-\delta)

\displaystyle\|G(u)-G(v)\|_{\delta^{\prime}}\leq\frac{L}{\delta-\delta^{\prime}}\|u-v\|_{\delta},\quad\|G(u_{0})\|_{\delta}\leq\frac{M}{\delta_{0}-\delta},

. Then for

\displaystyle T=\frac{R}{16LR+8M}

(2.6)

the initial value problem (

From the Autonomous Ovsyannikov Theorem we conclude that for

Another very important result is the global well-posedness of (

3
Global analytic well-posedness

In this section we prove Theorem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\frac{d\rho(t)}{dt}=\beta(\rho)},\\

\sigma(t)=b-\int_{0}^{t}\alpha(\rho(\tau))d\tau,\quad t\in[0,T_{1}],

\displaystyle\Phi_{\sigma(t)}(u)\leq\rho(t),\quad t\in[0,T^{\prime}],\quad T^{\prime}=\min\{T,T_{1}\}.

Proposition 3.1

\displaystyle|D\Phi_{\sigma,m}F(u)|\leq\bar{K}(\|u\|_{H^{2}})\Phi_{\sigma,m}(u)+\bar{\alpha}(\|u\|_{H^{2}},\Phi_{\sigma,m}(u))\partial_{\sigma}\Phi_{\sigma,m}(u),

We start by noticing that
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}\langle D\|\partial_{x}^{j}u\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\,,\,w\rangle=\langle\partial_{x}^{j}w\,,\,\partial_{x}^{j}u\rangle_{H^{2}}},

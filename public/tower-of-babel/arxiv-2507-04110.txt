Low Sets and Closure Properties of Counting Function Classes
Yaroslav Ivanashev

Abstract
A language L L is low for a relativizable complexity class C C , if it doesn’t increase the computational power of a class when it is used as an oracle: C L = C C^{L}=C . For the classes # ​ 𝖯 \mathsf{\#P} , 𝖦𝖺𝗉𝖯 \mathsf{GapP} , and 𝖲𝗉𝖺𝗇𝖯 \mathsf{SpanP} low classes of languages are known: L ​ o ​ w ​ ( # ​ 𝖯 ) = 𝖴𝖯 ∩ 𝖼𝗈𝖴𝖯 Low(\mathsf{\#P})=\mathsf{UP}\cap\mathsf{coUP} , L ​ o ​ w ​ ( 𝖦𝖺𝗉𝖯 ) = 𝖲𝖯𝖯 Low(\mathsf{GapP})=\mathsf{SPP} , and L ​ o ​ w ​ ( 𝖲𝗉𝖺𝗇𝖯 ) = 𝖭𝖯 ∩ 𝖼𝗈𝖭𝖯 Low(\mathsf{SpanP})=\mathsf{NP}\cap\mathsf{coNP} . In this paper, we prove that L ​ o ​ w ​ ( 𝖳𝗈𝗍𝖯 ) = 𝖯 Low(\mathsf{TotP})=\mathsf{P} , and give characterizations of low function classes for # ​ 𝖯 \mathsf{\#P} , 𝖦𝖺𝗉𝖯 \mathsf{GapP} , 𝖳𝗈𝗍𝖯 \mathsf{TotP} , and 𝖲𝗉𝖺𝗇𝖯 \mathsf{SpanP} . We establish the relations between 𝖭𝖯𝖲𝖵 𝗍 \mathsf{NPSV_{t}} , 𝖴𝖯𝖲𝖵 𝗍 \mathsf{UPSV_{t}} , and the counting function classes. For each of the inclusions between these classes we give an equivalent inclusion between language classes. We also prove that 𝖲𝗉𝖺𝗇𝖯 ⊆ 𝖦𝖺𝗉𝖯 \mathsf{SpanP}\subseteq\mathsf{GapP} if and only if 𝖭𝖯 ⊆ 𝖲𝖯𝖯 \mathsf{NP}\subseteq\mathsf{SPP} , and the inclusion 𝖦𝖺𝗉𝖯 + ⊆ 𝖲𝗉𝖺𝗇𝖯 \mathsf{GapP_{+}}\subseteq\mathsf{SpanP} implies 𝖯𝖧 = Σ 𝟤 𝖯 \mathsf{PH}=\mathsf{\Sigma^{P}_{2}} . For the classes # ​ 𝖯 \mathsf{\#P} , 𝖦𝖺𝗉𝖯 \mathsf{GapP} , 𝖳𝗈𝗍𝖯 \mathsf{TotP} , and 𝖲𝗉𝖺𝗇𝖯 \mathsf{SpanP} we summarize the known results and prove that each of these classes is closed under left composition with 𝖥𝖯 + \mathsf{FP_{+}} if and only if it collapses to its low class of functions.

6
One Function Computes Several Functions

In this section, we prove a technical lemma, which states that functions from
\mathsf{\#P}
,
\mathsf{GapP}
,
\mathsf{TotP}
, and
\mathsf{SpanP}
can output the values of several functions. The first application of this idea is
22
, which shows that a composition of
\mathsf{FP}
with one function is the same as a composition with several functions. The second application is
23
, which states that a NPTM with a
\mathsf{\#P}
oracle can always make at most one query to the oracle. A function
h
is in the class
\mathsf{FP}\circ(\mathsf{\#P}\times\ldots\times\mathsf{\#P})
if there exist functions
f\in\mathsf{FP}
and
g_{1},...,g_{k}\in\mathsf{\#P}
such that for any
x\in\Sigma^{*}
:
h(x)=f(g_{1}(x),\ldots,g_{k}(x))
.

Lemma 21
.

\mathsf{FP}\circ\mathsf{\#P}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}[1]}=\mathsf{FP}\circ(\mathsf{\#P}\times\ldots\times\mathsf{\#P})
.

Proof.

For every functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}\in\mathsf{\#P}
there exists a function
g\in\mathsf{\#P}
that on input
x\in\Sigma^{*}
outputs a concatenation of the values of
f_{1}(x),\ldots,f_{k}(x)
. The function
g
can be defined as
g(x)=f_{1}(x)\cdot 2^{(k-1)p(|x|)}+f_{2}(x)\cdot 2^{(k-2)p(|x|)}+\ldots+f_{k}(x)
, where
p
is a polynomial such that for any
x\in\Sigma^{*}
:
|f_{1}(x)|\leqslant p(|x|),\ldots,|f_{k}(x)|\leqslant p(|x|)
. It follows that
\mathsf{FP}\circ\mathsf{\#P}=\mathsf{FP}\circ(\mathsf{\#P}\times\ldots\times\mathsf{\#P})
.
\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}[1]}
is a subset of
\mathsf{FP}\circ(\mathsf{\#P}\times\mathsf{FP})
, because for every function
f\in\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}[1]}
there exist functions
g,h\in\mathsf{FP}
and
t\in\mathsf{\#P}
such that for any
x\in\Sigma^{*}
:
f(x)=g(t(h(x)),x)
.
∎

For the classes
\mathsf{GapP}
,
\mathsf{TotP}
, and
\mathsf{SpanP}
the same statement also holds, because they contain
\mathsf{FP_{+}}
and closed under addition, multiplication, and parsimonious reductions. From
6
we have the following corollary:

Corollary 22
.

\mathsf{FP}\circ\mathsf{\#P}=\mathsf{FP}\circ\mathsf{GapP}=\mathsf{FP}\circ\mathsf{TotP}=\mathsf{FP}\circ\mathsf{SpanP}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{\#P}[1]}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{GapP}[1]}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{TotP}[1]}=\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{SpanP}[1]}=\mathsf{FP}\circ(\mathsf{\#P}\times\ldots\times\mathsf{\#P})=\mathsf{FP}\circ(\mathsf{GapP}\times\ldots\times\mathsf{GapP})=\mathsf{FP}\circ(\mathsf{TotP}\times\ldots\times\mathsf{TotP})=\mathsf{FP}\circ(\mathsf{SpanP}\times\ldots\times\mathsf{SpanP})
.

In
[
13
, Lemma 4.3]
, it is proved that
\mathsf{\#P}^{\mathsf{NP}}=\mathsf{\#P}^{\mathsf{NP}[1]}
. We use a similar idea in
23
.

Proposition 23
.

1.

\mathsf{\#P}^{\mathsf{\#P}}=\mathsf{\#P}^{\mathsf{\#P}[1]}
.

2.

\mathsf{GapP}^{\mathsf{\#P}}=\mathsf{GapP}^{\mathsf{\#P}[1]}
.

Proof.

1.

Let
M^{f}
be a NPTM with an oracle
f\in\mathsf{\#P}
. There exists a NPTM
N^{g}
with an oracle
g\in\mathsf{\#P}
such that for any
x\in\Sigma^{*}
:
acc_{M^{f}}(x)=acc_{N^{g}}(x)
and on every computation path
N^{g}
makes at most one query to the oracle. The machine
N^{g}
simulates
M^{f}
and guesses all oracle answers. Before it halts,
N^{g}
verifies all oracle answers by a single query to the oracle, and rejects if at least one oracle answer was guessed incorrectly. The oracle function
g
is constructed so that on input
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\in\Sigma^{*}
the function
g
outputs a concatenation of the values of
f(x_{1}),\ldots,f(x_{k})
. Let
S
be a NPTM and
p
be a polynomial such that for any
x\in\Sigma^{*}
:
acc_{S}(x)=f(x)
and
|f(x)|\leqslant p(|x|)
. The function
g
can be defined as
g(x)=acc_{T}(x)
, where
T
is the NPTM that on input
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\in\Sigma^{*}
: generates
k
branches, simulates
S(x_{i})
on the
i
-th branch for
1\leqslant i\leqslant k
, and then generates
2^{(i-1)\cdot p(\max(|x_{1}|,\ldots,|x_{k}|))}
accepting paths. By construction,
g(x)=f(x_{k})\cdot 2^{(k-1)p(\max(|x_{1}|,\ldots,|x_{k}|))}+f(x_{k-1})\cdot 2^{(k-2)p(\max(|x_{1}|,\ldots,|x_{k}|))}+\ldots+f(x_{1})
.

2.

The proof is similar to
(1)
.
∎

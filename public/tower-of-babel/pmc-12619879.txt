Patient-reported outcomes following various incision techniques in breast reconstruction: an observational cohort study in China
Authors: Lingzi Wang, Yuhang Song, Wenqin Huang, Senyang Guo, Xinhong Wu, Hongmei Zheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTo evaluate patient satisfaction with different incision types in breast reconstruction surgery in China and to explore the factors related to these outcomes.MethodsThis single-center, retrospective cohort study included patients who underwent breast reconstruction at Hubei Cancer Hospital from September 2022 to September 2024. The patients were divided into four groups based on the incision type: (1) lateral axillary incision, (2) radial incision, (3) inferolateral inframammary fold incision, and (4) endoscopic-assisted surgery. Patient-reported outcomes (PROs) were collected ≥ 3 months after surgery using the Breast Cancer Core Scale (BREAST-Q) V2.0, the EuroQol five-dimension five-level health questionnaire (EQ-5D-5L), and the Decision Regret Scale (DRS). It should be noted that the type of incision was non-randomly allocated, although multivariate regression was performed to adjust for potential confounding factors.ResultsA total of 209 patients were included in this study. 34 (16.3%), 67 (32.1%), 64 (30.6%), and 44 (21.1%) patients underwent lateral axillary incision, radial incision, inferolateral inframammary fold incision, and endoscopic-assisted surgery, respectively. The endoscopic-assisted surgery group had the highest satisfaction (59.00 [IQR 57.75, 65.00]), and the radial incision group had the lowest satisfaction (53.00 [IQR 47.00-60.50]) (P = 0.007). Multivariable linear regression showed that a reduction in bra size was negatively independently associated with breast satisfaction (β = − 18.662, 95%CI: −26.789, −10.535, P < 0.001), while an inferolateral inframammary fold incision was positively independently associated with breast satisfaction (β = 6.430, 95%CI: 0.199, 12.662, P = 0.043). Skin-sparing mastectomy (SSM) (β = 7.468, 95%CI: 0.557–14.308, P = 0.034) and prepectoral implant plane (β = 6.756, 95%CI: 0.278–13.234, P = 0.041) were both positively independently associated with the DRS scores.ConclusionDespite the study’s limitations, our findings indicate that traditional techniques such as radial incision and inferolateral inframammary fold incision are still prevalent in China. Endoscopic-assisted surgery is associated with superior patient-reported satisfaction, highlighting its potential value for wider dissemination in clinical practice. Factors influencing patient satisfaction include reduced bra cup size, surgical approach, and implant plane.Keywords: Patient-reported outcomes, Breast reconstruction, Endoscopic-assisted surgery, Lateral axillary incision, Aesthetic outcomes

Background

Breast cancer is the second most common cancer worldwide but the first in women (estimated 2,308,897 new cases in 2022, or 11.6% of all cancers) and the leading cause of cancer-related deaths in women (estimated 665,684 related deaths in 2022, or 6.9% of all cancer-related deaths) (Bray et al. 2024). Asian women have historically shown a low incidence of breast cancer, but the westernization of lifestyle habits probably contributed to an annual increase in breast cancer incidence from 2000 to 2018 in China (Lei et al. 2021; Han et al. 2024). There were about 350,000 new cases of breast cancer in China in 2022, accounting for 15.6% of all new cancer cases (Han et al. 2024).

Surgery is considered the only possible curative option for breast cancer (Moo et al. 2018; Riis 2020; Wu et al. 2024). Unfortunately, breast surgery will inevitably leave a soft tissue defect that will compromise the aesthetics of the breast (Kaidar-Person et al. 2021; Kim et al. 2024). Breast reconstruction will improve the psychological health of the patients through improved self-image, social relations, and sexual function (Alvarez-Pardo et al. 2023; Blackmore et al. 2024; Archangelo et al. 2019). Indeed, performing breast reconstruction concurrently with mastectomy or delayed after mastectomy (e.g., after radiotherapy) can achieve good cosmetic outcomes, improving patient quality of life (Weber et al. 2017; Mohamedahmed et al. 2022). Relatively recent techniques like nipple-sparing mastectomy (NSM) and skin-sparing mastectomy (SSM) allow the preservation of breast external structures while removing the gland and cancer, and selecting such techniques will significantly impact the postoperative complications and the final shape of the breast (Galimberti et al. 2017). Radial incisions and inframammary fold incisions are commonly used for NSM and SSM. The incision choice can affect the blood flow to the nipple-areolar complex (NAC) and skin flaps in NSM, also affecting the incidence of postoperative complications (Park et al. 2020; Rawlani et al. 2011). A retrospective study showed that the rate of NAC ischemia or necrosis varied significantly with different incisions, reaching 17.0% for radial incisions (Park et al. 2020). Inframammary incisions have higher rates of major ischemic complication compared with lateral radial incisions (25% vs. 7.8%) (Salibian et al. 2021). NSM and SSM can also lead to hematoma, seroma, infection, and skin flap necrosis (Agha et al. 2019). Risk factors for such complications include age, tobacco use, obesity, surgical procedures, and adjuvant radiotherapy (Roubaud et al. 2021; Krueger et al. 2001). Furthermore, inframammary fold incisions can cause prosthesis displacement (Swanson 2017; Kim et al. 2014), but radial incisions can result in noticeable postoperative scars and are prone to nipple displacement (Park et al. 2020; Holmes et al. 2011). All these unfortunate outcomes can negate the patient’s satisfaction with breast surgery.

Patient satisfaction is the ultimate goal of breast reconstruction surgery, including quality of life and satisfaction scores for the breasts and other related body parts (Siqueira et al. 2020; Cohen et al. 2016). The BREAST-Q is the most commonly used patient-reported outcome (PRO) measurement tool in breast surgery, assessing satisfaction with breasts, psychosocial well-being, sexual well-being, physical health, and satisfaction with treatment outcomes (Liu et al. 2018). Currently, there is no unified standard for the best incision method in breast reconstruction surgery, but PROs could be used to guide selection.

Globally, there is a growing trend towards minimally invasive techniques. Endoscopic-assisted breast surgery, in particular, has emerged as a promising approach that aims to remove cancer safely while preserving breast aesthetics (Soybir and Fukuma 2015; Shin 2021). Compared with traditional open surgery, endoscopic-assisted mastectomy through the axillary results in fewer postoperative complications (Lee et al. 2022) and achieves favorable safety and aesthetic outcomes (Franceschini et al. 2020).

Over the past two decades, the field of breast reconstruction in China has developed rapidly, yet the proportion of breast reconstructions remains relatively low, with only 13.3% of breast cancer surgery patients undergoing breast reconstruction (Wang et al. 2025). Endoscopic-assisted surgery is being implemented in some breast cancer centers, but its widespread adoption remains limited due to the high technical demands of the procedure, the steep learning curve for surgeons, and the relatively high surgical costs (Jiang et al. 2020). Furthermore, although traditional incisions, such as radial and inferolateral inframammary fold incisions, remain prevalent in clinical practice in China, there is a lack of robust comparative studies—particularly those focusing on PROs—to guide the selection of incision techniques for the Chinese population. This leaves a critical gap in understanding how different breast reconstruction incision techniques, ranging from traditional incisions to emerging endoscopic methods, impact patient-reported satisfaction and quality of life.

Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate patient satisfaction with different incision methods, including endoscopic-assisted surgery, in breast reconstruction surgery in China and to explore the factors related to these outcomes. The results could help inform the decision-making process to achieve optimal patient outcomes after breast cancer surgery.

Methods

Study design and patients

This cohort study included patients who underwent breast reconstruction at Hubei Cancer Hospital from September 2022 to September 2024. This study was approved by the ethics committee of Hubei Cancer Hospital (approval no: LLHBCH2023YN-046).

The inclusion criteria were (1) ≥ 18 years of age, (2) underwent NSM or SSM, (3) underwent immediate or delayed-immediate breast reconstruction, (4) available clinical data before and at least 3 months after surgery, and 5) complete PRO data. The exclusion criteria were (1) bilateral reconstruction or (2) incomplete follow-up or loss to follow-up.

The surgical incision is chosen by surgeons at the Breast Center of Hubei Cancer Hospital based on the patient’s age, body type, breast size, degree of breast ptosis, tumor size, tumor location, desired aesthetic and functional outcomes, and the surgeon’s own preferences. The patients were divided into four groups based on the incision used during their surgery: (1) lateral axillary incision, (2) radial incision, (3) inferolateral inframammary fold incision, and (4) endoscopic-assisted surgery (Fig. 1). Data collection

Demographic and clinical data, including comorbidities, tumor histopathology, and surgical details, were retrieved from electronic patient records in the hospital information system. PROs were routinely collected at a minimum of three months postoperatively during the study period. The assessment instruments included the Breast Cancer Core Scale (BREAST-Q) Version 2.0, the EuroQol five-dimension five-level questionnaire (EQ-5D-5L), and the Decision Regret Scale (DRS). The BREAST-Q V2.0 was used to evaluate satisfaction with breasts, psychosocial well-being, and sexual well-being. We employed the Chinese version of the BREAST-Q, which was developed by Professor Jiong Wu’s team at Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center based on the 2017 BREAST-Q Version 2.0 revised by Pusic et al. This version has been validated in the Chinese context, demonstrating good reliability and validity, and is widely adopted as a reference PRO tool in breast cancer research in China (Ma et al. 2021). Using the Q Score scoring system, responses were converted into summary scores ranging from 0 to 100 for each subscale, with higher scores representing greater satisfaction or better quality of life. A difference of 4 or more points in the Q score was considered clinically significant(Voineskos et al. 2020). The EQ-5D-5L index assesses mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain and discomfort, anxiety, and depression on a scale of 1 to 5, with higher scores indicating worse health(Herdman et al. 2011). The DRS measures distress or remorse after a healthcare decision, with scores ranging from 0 to 100, with higher scores indicating worse regrets (Brehaut et al. 2003).

Statistical analysis

The continuous variables were tested for normality using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Those conforming to a normal distribution were described as means ± standard deviations and analyzed using one-way ANOVA. Those that did not conform to a normal distribution were described using median (interquartile range) and analyzed using the Kruskal-Wallis test. The categorical variables were described using n (%) and analyzed using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact probability test. Multivariable linear regression analyses were performed with breast satisfaction scores and DRS scores as outcomes, including variables that showed statistical differences in the univariable analyses. All tests were two-tailed, and differences were considered statistically significant when P < 0.05. Statistical analysis was performed using R 4.2.3.

Results

Characteristics of the patients

This study included 209 patients. Median age was 40.00 (IQR: 34.00–47.00) years, with 50% (n = 106) of the patients being 35–50 years. There were no significant differences in age among groups (P = 0.138). Statistically significant differences were observed in the history of prior breast surgery (P = 0.011), reproductive history (P = 0.010), BMI (P = 0.019), and family history of breast cancer (P = 0.028) (Table 1).

Characteristics of the surgeries

34 (16.3%) patients underwent a lateral axillary incision, 67 (32.1%) underwent a radial incision, 64 (30.6%) underwent a inferolateral inframammary fold incision, and 44 (21.1%) underwent endoscopic-assisted surgery. Among the 209 patients, 168 (80.4%) underwent NSM, and 41 (19.6%) underwent SSM (P < 0.001). 189 (90.4%) patients received immediate reconstruction and 20 (9.6%) received delayed-immediate reconstruction (P = 0.041). In addition, 118 (56.5%) cases received subpectoral implants, 74 (35.4%) received prepectoral implants, and 17 (8.1%) were unknown (P < 0.001). Regarding implants, 194 (92.8%) received implants, 12 (5.7%) received implants plus latissimus dorsi flaps, and 3 (1.4%) received implants plus anterior serratus muscle flaps (P < 0.001). All patients received mesh. 202 (96.7%) received the Tiloop mesh, and 7 (3.3%) received acellular dermal matrix (ADM) mesh (P = X). Postoperatively, 25 patients (12%) had increased bra size, 22 (10.5%) had decreased bra size, and 158 (75.6%) cases remained unchanged (P = 0.033). There were no significant differences in the rate of radiotherapy among groups (P = 0.617) (Table 2).

Satisfaction with aesthetic outcomes and quality of life

There were statistically significant differences in breast satisfaction among the groups, with the endoscopic-assisted surgery group having the highest satisfaction (median of 59.00 (IQR 57.75, 65.00)) and the radial incision group having the lowest satisfaction (median of 53.00 (IQR 47.00-60.50)) (P = 0.007); the median satisfaction scores were 58.00 (IQR 51.00-63.50) and 58.00 (IQR 50.50, 71.50) in the lateral axillary incision and the inferolateral inframammary fold incision groups, respectively. No significant differences were found in psychosocial well-being (P = 0.091), sexual well-being (P = 0.216), EQ-5D-5L (P = 0.386), or DRS scores (P = 0.100) among groups (Table 3).

Patient-reported outcomes

Factors associated with the aesthetic outcomes

Univariable linear regression showed that a reduction in bra cup size was associated with poor breast satisfaction (β = −16.834, 95%CI: −24.792, −8.875, P < 0.001), while inferolateral inframammary fold incision (β = 8.430, 95%CI: 2.187, 14.674, P = 0.008) and endoscopic-assisted surgery (β = 7.412, 95%CI: 0.480, 14.343, P = 0.036) were associated with good breast satisfaction. Age (β = 0.393, 95%CI: 0.091, 0.696, P = 0.011), reproductive history (β = 8.763, 95%CI: 0.879, 16.647, P = 0.030), SSM (β = 8.803, 95%CI: 2.490, 15.116, p = 0.007) were associated with the high DRS scores while being single (β = − 14.429, 95%CI: −25.238, -3.619, p = 0.009) and inferolateral inframammary fold incision (β= − 6.607, 95%CI: −12.984, −0.230, p = 0.042) were associated with the low DRS scores. Multivariable linear regression showed that a reduction in bra cup size was independently associated with poor breast satisfaction (β = − 18.662, 95%CI: −26.789, −10.535, P < 0.001), while inferolateral inframammary fold incision was independently associated with good breast satisfaction (β = 6.430, 95%CI: 0.199, 12.662, P = 0.043). SSM (β = 7.468, 95%CI: 0.557, 14.308, P = 0.034) and prepectoral implant plane (β = 6.756, 95%CI: 0.278, 13.234, P = 0.041) were independently associated with the high DRS scores (Tables 4 and 5).

Complications

Complications were categorized according to the Clavien-Dindo classification system. Grade I indicates minor complications requiring no intervention; Grade II requires pharmacological treatment (e.g., antibiotics for infection); Grades III–V represent increasing severity requiring surgical, endoscopic, or radiological intervention, intensive care, or resulting in death. Specifically, infections requiring antibiotics were classified as Grade II, skin flap necrosis requiring debridement as Grade IIIa, and seroma or subcutaneous effusion requiring aspiration as Grade IIIa. Minor wound problems such as incision dehiscence not requiring intervention were classified as Grade I. Among all the patients (n = 209), a total of 26 (12.4%) cases had different postoperative complications, most of which were Grade I-II according to the Clavien-Dindo classification, with 3 (8.8%) cases in the lateral axillary incision group, 16 (23.9%) in the radial incision group, 5 (7.8%) in the inferolateral inframammary fold incision group, and 2 (2.8%) in the endoscopic-assisted surgery group. The complications included necrosis (skin or skin flap) (n = 8, 3.8%), hematoma/seroma (n = 1, 0.5%), infection (n = 3, 1.4%), incision dehiscence (n = 8, 3.8%), and subcutaneous effusion (n = 3, 1.4%). 3 (1.4%) patients had multiple complications and underwent reoperation. 1 patient was infected accompanied by hematoma and subcutaneous effusion. 2 patients had skin flap necrosis combined with infection (Table 6).

Discussion

This cohort study evaluated patient satisfaction and PROs with different incision methods in breast reconstruction surgery in China and explored the factors related to these outcomes. The results suggested that traditional surgical techniques such as radial incision and inferolateral inframammary fold incision are still prevalent in China. Endoscopic technology demonstrates a positive trend in improving patient-reported aesthetic outcomes, while the radial incision appears less favorable. This superior performance of endoscopic-assisted surgery is likely attributable to several factors: minimized tissue trauma leading to faster recovery and more aesthetically pleasing scars; enhanced visualization that improves surgical precision, reduces intraoperative errors, and increases procedural success rates; and superior safety with reduced incidence of postoperative complications such as bleeding and infection (Feng et al. 2024). Furthermore, the study identified that a reduction in bra size was associated with lower breast satisfaction, while SSM and prepectoral implant plane were independently associated with higher DRS scores.

Different incisions for breast surgery and reconstruction will inevitably lead to different surgical outcomes and PROs (Park et al. 2020; Rawlani et al. 2011). Although the incision is often selected to optimize the surgical and oncological outcomes, the final selection is often made after a comprehensive discussion with the patient that includes weighing the pros and cons of the possible surgical approaches (Park et al. 2020; Rawlani et al. 2011; Holmes et al. 2011). Zhang et al. (Zhang et al. 2019) compared endoscopic and open (irrespective of the incision approach) NSM with immediate reconstruction using prosthesis implantation and found that the endoscopic group outperformed the open group regarding patient satisfaction. Qiu et al. (Qiu et al. 2022) showed that, for axillary incisions and subsequent skin flap implantation, endoscopic-assisted surgery offered better aesthetic outcomes and quality of life compared with open surgery. In the present study, all patients in the endoscopic-assisted group received an implant and mesh, while 26.5% of the patients in the lateral axillary incision group received skin flaps. Implant plus mesh can apply tension on the skin and the incision (Faulkner et al. 2020; Yesantharao and Nguyen 2021), whereas skin flaps can provide additional soft tissues for volume and support, contributing to a more natural breast shape and contour and less tension on the incision (Yesantharao and Nguyen 2021). The difference in implant use rate in the present study may be why the endoscopic-assisted group did not show a significant advantage in breast satisfaction over the lateral axillary incision group. High BMI is associated with lower breast satisfaction (Jepsen et al. 2023). In the present study, the endoscopic-assisted group had a significantly higher BMI than the other groups, which may also elucidate why the endoscopic axillary incision did not demonstrate an obvious advantage. Moreover, none of the patients receiving ADM in the endoscopic-assisted group may also detract from benefits. The application of ADM can preclude excessive tension and significantly diminish the likelihood of wound dehiscence, ischemia, and necrosis of the mastectomy flap (Paci et al. 2002). However, ADM has not been approved for breast reconstruction in China. Seven patients with other types of incisions were enrolled in another randomized controlled trial for further investigation. The radial incision group had the highest proportion of SSM, which may have contributed to lower satisfaction. Minimally invasive techniques show a promising trend in improving aesthetic outcomes reported by patients undergoing breast reconstruction, which warrant further evaluation in future prospective trials. Of course, the incision approach must be tailored to the specific cancer and breast contour of each patient (Huang et al. 2021; Acea-Nebril et al. 2019; Lovasik et al. 2019). Here, the highest breast satisfaction was achieved with the endoscopic technique, as supported by previous studies, reaching excellent satisfaction rates of 82.2–89.5% (Shin 2021; Ozaki and Ohara 2014). Nevertheless, not all patients meet the indications for endoscopic breast surgery (Shin 2021). In such cases, the lateral axillary and inferolateral inframammary fold incisions appear to achieve higher satisfaction than the radial incision. In the multivariable analysis, the inferolateral inframammary fold incision was independently associated with better breast satisfaction. In addition, it is worth noting that in clinical practice, we do utilize the Wise pattern oncoplastic technique for cases involving large, ptotic breasts. However, among the 209 patients in this study, only one presented with large and ptotic breasts, and this case was managed with implant-based reconstruction combined with mesh. Indeed, some experts in China and world currently employ Wise pattern and vertical incision techniques for skin-reducing mastectomy with implant reconstruction (Padula et al. 2023; Caputo et al. 2023; Lembo et al. 2023; Abdelmageed et al. 2025), which is a valuable approach.

A larger breast size is often associated with better satisfaction with the female self-appearance. Although too-large breasts are associated with physical health issues and lower satisfaction with self-appearance (Oon et al. 2022), a survey showed that 47% of the women wanted larger breasts, 23% wanted smaller breasts, and 29% were satisfied with their breast size (Swami et al. 2020). Small breasts are the main reason for seeking aesthetic breast augmentation (Roy et al. 2021). Tumor removal will inevitably reduce breast tissue volume, and reconstructive surgery aims to manage the loss of volume. Therefore, reducing the bra cup size could reflect dissatisfaction with the surgical outcomes, as observed in the present study. SSM and prepectoral implant planes were independently associated with higher DRS scores, indicating worse regrets with the decision. A recent meta-analysis showed that NSM was associated with better sexual function and psychosocial well-being than SSM (Clarijs et al. 2023), supporting the present study. On the other hand, the literature suggests different results when comparing prepectoral and subpectoral implant planes, with prepectoral associated with better satisfaction (Ching et al. 2022) but subpectoral ones associated with better sexual functioning (Le et al. 2021). Additional well-designed studies are necessary to address that point.

Nevertheless, several factors can influence the choice of an incision besides the oncological factors and patient preferences. Patient factors like age, tobacco use, obesity, and the need for adjuvant radiotherapy will influence the risk of complications after breast surgery and reconstruction (Roubaud et al. 2021; Krueger et al. 2001), and such factors must be taken into account when planning breast surgery. Zhang et al. (Zhang et al. 2019) showed no significant differences in postoperative complications between open surgery and endoscopic-assisted surgery, supporting the present study. In open surgery, a vertical incision orientation does not increase surgical complication rates, including skin necrosis, in SSM compared with nonvertical incisions (Dayicioglu et al. 2016). Even among endoscopic surgeries, different incisions result in varying complication rates. In the present study, the radial incision group had a markedly higher complication rate (23.9%) than the other groups (2.8–8.8%). Similar results were reported by Park et al. (Park et al. 2020) for nipple-areolar complex ischemia and necrosis in patients undergoing NSM using inframammary fold (9.7%), radial (17.0%), or periareolar (31.1%) incision. In the study by Woo et al. (Woo et al. 2022), the rates of nipple-areolar complex necrosis after NSM with immediate reconstruction were 27.2%, 33.0%, and 39.8% with lateral, periareolar, and radial incisions, respectively. Still, the present study did not include patients with periareolar incisions and could not specifically document nipple-areolar complex ischemia and necrosis from the patient charts. Nevertheless, a similarly high rate of complications with radial incision was observed.

The present study has several limitations that should be considered when interpreting the results. First, it was a single-center, retrospective study, which can lead to limited sample representativeness, restricted external validity, selection bias, and missing data. Second, the choice of surgical incision was non-randomized, which introduces potential selection bias. The selection was based on the surgeon’s judgment regarding tumor location, individual characteristics of the patients, and postoperative functional and aesthetic requirements, which directly led to imbalanced baseline characteristics between the groups. As shown in the results, there were significant differences in baseline characteristics such as BMI, surgical approach (NSM vs. SSM), timing of breast reconstruction, implant plane and type among the patient groups. These confounding factors could have plausibly influenced our primary outcome. Although we performed multivariate regression analyses to adjust for these known confounders, the possibility of residual confounding from unmeasured or unknown variables cannot be entirely ruled out. Third, the outcomes in this study were collected at ≥ 3 months postoperatively, and this interval may be too short to capture long-term satisfaction, complications, or decision regret. Finally, due to the limitations of statistical methods and the relatively small sample size of this study, some of our conclusions are exploratory in nature and not statistically significant. They should be interpreted with caution when applied in clinical practice. Despite all these limitations, we believe that this study provides a real-world panorama of clinical practice and valuable insights into the application of specific surgical incision techniques in breast reconstruction. In the future, we plan to fully leverage our center’s resources in plastic surgery to conduct multi-center, prospective randomized controlled trials (RCTs). We will implement advanced statistical methods such as propensity score matching and longer follow-up for patients to better balance baseline characteristics between groups, thereby obtaining long-term outcomes and more robust research conclusions.

Conclusion

Traditional techniques such as radial incision and inferolateral inframammary fold incision are still prevalent in China. Endoscopic technology demonstrates a positive trend in improving patient-reported aesthetic outcomes and should be further promoted and applied. A reduction in bra size was independently associated with lower breast satisfaction, while inferolateral inframammary fold incision was associated with better satisfaction, and SSM and prepectoral implant plane were independently associated with higher DRS scores.

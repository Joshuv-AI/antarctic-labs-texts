Abstract
This work investigates the fractional time description of a generalized quantum light-matter system modeled by a time-dependent Jaynes-Cummings (JC) interaction. Distinct fractional effects are included by considering two approaches for the power in the imaginary unit of the Schrödinger equation. Additionally, we consider various time modulations in the coupling (constant, linear, exponential, and sinusoidal) and analyze their consequences on population inversion and entanglement. The assumption of fractional order leads to distinct consequences in the considered quantities, such as oscillations with decreasing amplitude around a fixed value or decay to an asymptotic value. The time-dependent couplings influence how these effects occur, eventually resulting in high or low degrees of entanglement. Notably, with sinusoidal coupling, we find that non-periodic behavior is preserved under both treatments of the imaginary unit; however, with decreasing fractional order, the non-periodic dynamics can be suppressed.

Introduction

Fractional calculus (FC) is an alternative framework for studying various physical scenarios
TenreiroAdvances
.
It has emerged as a generalization of integer-order derivatives to non-integer ones
TenreiroHistory
. These extensions incorporate many new results with non-trivial consequences, including memory effects and long-range correlations

, which are connected to non-Markovian processes
Evangelista2018
.
FC has been applied in various fields, e.g., the study of infectious diseases

,
TenreiroHIV
,
complex viscoelastic phenomena

,
bagley1983theoretical
,
wave propagation in porous media

,
cai2018survey
,
electrical spectroscopy impedance
rosseto2022frequency
, gas transport in heterogeneous environments
chang2018time
,
chang2019spatial
,
pandey2016connecting
, among others.

Among its recent developments, FC has also been incorporated in the quantum mechanics context by substituting
the integer derivatives in the Schrödinger equation by non-integer operators.
This extension is attributed to the seminal works of Laskin
laskin2000
,
PhysRevE.66.056108
and Naber
Naber2004
.
In the latter, the time fractional Schrödinger equation (TFSE) is formulated in two ways: the first raises the imaginary unit to the same power as the fractional derivative, while the second leaves the imaginary term unaltered.
In both frameworks, the evolution is not unitary
Cius2022
, though the mechanisms by which this manifests differ.
The first formulation is associated with a Wick rotation, and the solutions lead to oscillations in the total probability.
Meanwhile, in the second formulation, the total probability vanishes for long times, reflecting the emergence of pronounced dissipative effects
Wei2024
.

In the context of fractional operators, Naber employed the Caputo fractional derivative in the TFSE formulation
Naber2004
, making the TFSE adequate for describing non-Markovian processes
Rivas2010
,
Zou2015
characterized by a power-law memory kernel.
More specifically, FC has been applied to study different scenarios in quantum mechanics,
e.g.,
time-dependent quantum potentials
Gabrick2023frac
,
anomalous diffusion in a three-level system
lenzi2022schrodinger
,
Lévy flights over quantum paths
Laskin2007
,
and quantum comb structures
Iomin2011
.
In the case of quantum optics,
the FC was explored both theoretically

and experimentally
Huang2005
.

In this framework, the interaction of a two-level system with a quantized electromagnetic field is modeled with a Jaynes-Cummings (JC) interaction
JAYNES1963
,
LARSON2021
.
This model has garnered attention in the fractional quantum mechanics community, having been studied in diverse contexts and with various objectives.
Equipped with FC, the study of a two-level system interacting with a semi-classical light field,
was conducted by Lu and Yu
lu2017
,
Lu2018
.
Differential equations similar to the
ones in JC model arise, although the physical interpretation differs.
Subsequently, El Anouz et al.

Zu et al.

studied the single and the double JC,
with a focus on memory effects and entanglement sudden death.
In 2022, Zu and Xu

revisited the problem using a limit-based approach,
resulting in a probability-conserving evolution for the system.
El-Hadidy et al.
El-Hadidy2023
examined the communication efficiency of the Anti-JC model.

In more recent developments, a fractional time JC model was investigated in Ref.
Cius2024
, where a specific protocol was considered to obtain the unitary time-evolution operator when the Hamiltonian operator is traceless,
based on a non-Hermitian map
Cius2022

Recent works investigate FC as a framework for modeling non-Markovian dynamics

,
Wei2024
,
Monteiro2025
,

.

In this work, we study a fractional generalization of this atom-field interaction, where the light-matter coupling changes with time,
modeling it with the time-dependent JC (TDJC) model

,

,

,

,

The TDJC introduces changes in the standard JC model,

Our primary goal consists in studying how these modifications take place in the FC context.
With this, we can see how the effects associated with the FC permeate the physical scenarios – such as atomic motion, spatial variation of the field, and transient effects in the cavity – that are modeled through time-dependent coupling.
To achieve this objective, we calculate the atomic population inversion and atom-field entanglement, as measured by the von Neumann entropy.
Beyond its mathematical formulation, the TFSE provides an appropriate framework for describing memory effects in both formulations of the TFSE,
i^{\alpha}

Zu2021
, for
\alpha\neq 1
, and
i^{1}

Zu2025
.
The latter is particularly useful for modeling the non-Markovian evolution of dissipative systems
Breuer2016
.
Given the applications of the JC model in quantum computing
Monroe1995
, these new perspectives may contribute to its contextualization across different perspectives.
For instance, the sinusoidal coupling in the TDJC model describes an atom traversing a standing-wave cavity mode
Schlicher1989
,
LARSON2021
.
In this scenario, the TFSE can incorporate memory effects into the physical picture, possibly facilitating an experimental verification of FC in cavity quantum electrodynamics.

Quantifying atomic probabilities and entanglement poses a problem in straightforward approaches to quantum mechanics utilizing FC, since the fractional time derivative leads to the
non-conservation of probability
Laskin2017
.
There are two ways to deal with this paradigm:
(a) study the effects of FC with a focus on the mathematical framework
and with physical interpretations that significantly deviate from the trivial scenario
lu2017
,
Lu2018
,
Gabrick2023frac
while acknowledging that physical interpretations remain largely heuristic,
or (b) find ways to reconcile the theory with standard quantum mechanics,
by proposing alternatives to the usual Caputo derivative
zu2022
, with a unitary description
Cius2022
,
Cius2024
, or normalizing the density operator
El-Hadidy2023
,
ElAnouz2020
,
Zu2021
to ensure a well-defined statistical interpretation.
Our approach is mainly the second,
applying a normalization to the observables and density matrix

.
Thus, we can effectively study the effects of fractional time.
Refs.
zu2022
and
Cius2024
, employed alternative approaches to the same problem,
but their technique leads to different results compared to the normalization.
During the development of our research, we became aware of the work published by Zu and Yu
Zu2025
.
However, our results differs from theirs, since we study different coupling configurations in the JC model and also consider the off-resonance condition.
Remarkably, our investigation of the power of the imaginary unit yields outcomes similar to those derived by Zu and Yu, although within a different framework.

We investigate four distinct couplings for JC dynamics: constant, linear, exponential, and sinusoidal.
For the constant coupling case, we obtain analytical solutions that demonstrate the non-conservation of probability and highlight the role of the detuning parameter in this behavior.
When the Wick rotation is employed, we observe oscillations with decreasing amplitude around a fixed value in the quantities considered.
On the other hand, when the standard form of the imaginary unit is preserved, the oscillations eventually cease after a specific time for all the couplings,
decaying to an asymptotic value.
These effects are influenced both quantitatively and qualitatively by time-dependent couplings, ultimately resulting in high or low degrees of entanglement.
Notably, in the sinusoidal form, our results suggest a non-periodic motion in the measured physical quantities, regardless of the specific representation of the imaginary unity.
Furthermore, when the fractional order is reduced below
0.7
, the non-periodic behavior transitions into a periodic one.

Generalized atom-field model

The JC model is a cornerstone in quantum optics,
describing the interaction of a two-level atom with a quantized field mode under the rotating-wave approximation
JAYNES1963
.
The model is associated with different phenomena regarding the light-matter interaction,
such as
atom-field entanglement
Aravind1984
,
Phoenix1988
,
Phoenix1991
,
RO
Cummings1965
,
Eberly1980
and their
collapses and revivals
Cummings1965
,
Eberly1980
,
experimentally verified in the context of cavity quantum electrodynamics
MESCHEDE1985
,
REMPE1987
,
Brune1996
.
For further information, we refer to the review conducted by Larson and Mavrogordatos
LARSON2021

and the references therein.
The TDJC model extends the usual framework by allowing parameters of the system,
otherwise constant, to vary with time
DeCastro2023
,
Larson2003
,
Larson2003a
.
This enhances the scope of the system, eventually encompassing new physical scenarios,
such as atomic motion
Schlicher1989
,
Fang1998
, transient effects
Prants1992
,
Dasgupta1999
, and varying field intensities
Joshi1993
.
According to Ref.
Maldonado-Mundo2012
, a time-dependent coupling situation could be realized in cavity quantum electrodynamics, with variations in the atom’s position.

A priori, the atom-field coupling parameter (
\lambda
) can be considered constant.
However, in this work, we focus on a broader scenario, i.e.,
\lambda=\lambda(t)
,
considering on- and off-resonance contexts.
In this sense, the TDJC Hamiltonian is given by

\hat{H}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\omega\hat{\sigma}_{z}+\nu\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}+\lambda(t)(\hat{\sigma}_{+}\hat{a}+\hat{\sigma}_{-}\hat{a}^{\dagger}),

(1)

where we assume
\hbar=1
, without loss of generality.
This extension not only leads to significant changes in the system’s dynamics but can also be applied from a control perspective in the JC model with fractional time, as suggested in Ref.
Wei2024
.
We use
\omega
and
\nu
to represent the atomic transition frequency and the cavity mode frequency, respectively.
The creation (
\hat{a}^{\dagger}
) and annihilation operators (
\hat{a}
)
act on the cavity states in the Fock basis
\left\{|n\rangle\right\}
.
The atom is effectively treated as a two-level system with
|e\rangle
(
|g\rangle
) representing the excited (ground) state,
\sigma_{+}=\outerproduct{e}{g}
and
\sigma_{-}=\outerproduct{g}{e}
are the raising and lowering operators, and
\sigma_{z}=\outerproduct{e}{e}-\outerproduct{g}{g}
is the atomic inversion operator.

In what follows, we consider the initial state as

|\Psi(0)\rangle=a(0)|e,n\rangle+b(0)|g,n+1\rangle,

(2)

which implies in an evolved state in the form

|\Psi(t)\rangle=a(t)|e,n\rangle+b(t)|g,n+1\rangle.

(3)

The system can be described by the effective time-dependent Hamiltonian
\hat{V}(t)
,

\hat{V}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\Delta\hat{\sigma}_{z}+\lambda(t)(\hat{\sigma}_{+}\hat{a}+\hat{\sigma}_{-}\hat{a}^{\dagger}),

(4)

with
\Delta=\omega-\nu
as the detuning.
The amplitudes
a(t)
and
b(t)
are obtained from the solution of the Schrödinger equation in the form

i\frac{d}{dt}|\Psi(t)\rangle=\hat{V}(t)|\Psi(t)\rangle.

(5)

Eqs. (
4
) and (
5
),
imply in the system of linear differential
equations with time-dependent coefficients:

\displaystyle i\frac{d}{dt}a(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda(t)b(t)\sqrt{n+1}+\frac{1}{2}\Delta a(t),

(6)

\displaystyle i\frac{d}{dt}b(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda(t)a(t)\sqrt{n+1}-\frac{1}{2}\Delta b(t).

Therefore, the evolution of
a(t)
and
b(t)
is completely determined by the solution of the system, Eq. (
6

These quantities allow us to determine the total probability, given by

\mathcal{P}(t)=|\langle\Psi(t)|\Psi(t)\rangle|^{2},

(7)

and the atomic population inversion

W(t)=|a(t)|^{2}-|b(t)|^{2}.

(8)

In standard quantum mechanics, a closed quantum system evolves unitarily in time and consequently the total probability remains equal to one
for a normalized state.
The population inversion, which was experimentally measurable
REMPE1987
, is often examined when accounting for the JC model and its extensions
Arroyo-Correa1990
.

Moreover, we also study the VNE for the atomic subsystem

,
described by the reduced density operator

\hat{\rho}_{A}(t)=\tr_{F}[\hat{\rho}(t)]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle n|\hat{\rho}(t)|n\rangle.

(9)

By definition,
\hat{\rho}(t)=\outerproduct{\Psi(t)}{\Psi(t)}
represents the density operator of the joint atom-cavity system.
For a bipartite quantum system and pure global state, the VNE is a good measure of entanglement

a quantum correlation in the atom-cavity system associated with the non-separability of the quantum state
Horodecki2009
.
The VNE is given by

S^{A}(t)=-\sum_{i}\mu_{i}(t)\log_{2}{\mu_{i}(t)},

(10)

where
\mu_{i}(t)
are the eigenvalues of
\hat{\rho}_{A}(t)
.
For the initial state in the form of Eq. (
2
),
we can further write it as

S^{A}(t)=-|a(t)|^{2}\log_{2}{|a(t)|^{2}}-|b(t)|^{2}\log_{2}{|b(t)|^{2}}.

(11)

Arbitrary dependencies in
\lambda(t)
imply differential
equations, Eq. (
6
), with coefficients that are arbitrarily dependent
on time, making the task of obtaining analytical solutions for

and
b(t)

However, in some instances, even for a time-dependent coupling parameter,

Assuming
\lambda(t)=\lambda_{0}
(constant coupling), Eq. (

Their solutions for an arbitrary initial value problem,

and

\displaystyle a(t)={}

\displaystyle\frac{a_{0}}{2}\left(e^{-i\Omega_{n}t}+e^{i\Omega_{n}t}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\left[a_{0}\left(\frac{\Delta}{2}\right)+b_{0}\lambda_{0}\sqrt{n+1}\right]}{2\Omega_{n}}\left(e^{-i\Omega_{n}t}-e^{i\Omega_{n}t}\right),

\displaystyle b(t)={}

\displaystyle\frac{b_{0}}{2}\left(e^{-i\Omega_{n}t}+e^{i\Omega_{n}t}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\left[a_{0}\lambda_{0}\sqrt{n+1}-b_{0}\left(\frac{\Delta}{2}\right)\right]}{2\Omega_{n}}\left(e^{-i\Omega_{n}t}-e^{i\Omega_{n}t}\right),

For this case, it is straightforward that

3
Fractional time calculus

The analysis of fractional dynamics in
the context of the TFSE can be carried out using two distinct
approaches
Naber2004
.
The first is to raise
i
to the same order as the fractional operator (
\alpha
), i.e.,
i^{\alpha}
, through the action of a Wick rotation.
The main effect of this approach is the appearance of oscillations in quantum dynamics, specifically in the square modulus of the norm of the quantum state, pointing out the non-conservation of the total probability.
On the other hand, the TFSE can be written using only
i
,
including dissipative effects in the dynamics and which
generally leads to
\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{P}(t)=0
.
Both approaches can provide novel aspects in the TDJC model dynamics and can be simultaneously incorporated by writing the TFSE in the form

i^{\beta}\frac{\partial^{\alpha}}{\partial t^{\alpha}}|\Psi(t)\rangle=\hat{V}(t)|\Psi(t)\rangle,

(13)

where
\beta
can assume distinct values, including
\beta=1
and
\beta=\alpha
.
This modification enriches the fractional solutions by introducing a new control parameter that accounts for the different effects arising from the different definitions of TFSE
Cius2022
.

Henceforth, we shall refer to
|\langle\Psi(t)|\Psi(t)\rangle|^{2}
as the fractional probability, denoted by
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha,\beta}(t)
.
Strictly speaking,
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha,\beta}(t)

cannot be interpreted as the standard probability
, as the domains differ.
However, we adopt this notation because it reduces to the standard case when
\alpha=1
.
As a fractional operator, we employ the Caputo fractional time derivative
Evangelista2018
, which is defined as follows:

\frac{\partial^{\alpha}}{\partial t^{\alpha}}|\Psi(t)\rangle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\frac{1}{\left(t-t^{\prime}\right)^{\alpha}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{\prime}}|\Psi(t)\rangle.

(14)

for
0<\alpha<1
.
This operator is related to non-Markovian environments, where a future state depends not only on the present state but also on its past states
Wei2024
.
In this context,
\alpha
is associated with the degree of memory in the system
Zu2021
.
Since our main interest is to explore the effects of
\alpha
and
\beta
in the dynamics the JC model, we henceforth adopt the notation
a_{\alpha,\beta}(t)
,
b_{\alpha,\beta}(t)
,
W_{\alpha,\beta}(t)
and
S^{A}_{\alpha,\beta}(t)
,
highlighting the dependence on the derivative order and the power of
i
.

In this scenario, the state (
3
) evolves in time according to the TFSE (
13
) with the Hamiltonian (
4
), which results in the following system of coupled differential equations

\displaystyle i^{\beta}\frac{\partial^{\alpha}}{\partial t^{\alpha}}a_{\alpha,\beta}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda(t)b_{\alpha,\beta}(t)\sqrt{n+1}+\frac{1}{2}\Delta a_{\alpha,\beta}(t),

(15)

\displaystyle i^{\beta}\frac{\partial^{\alpha}}{\partial t^{\alpha}}b_{\alpha,\beta}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda(t)a_{\alpha,\beta}(t)\sqrt{n+1}-\frac{1}{2}\Delta b_{\alpha,\beta}(t),

assuming
a_{\alpha,\beta}(0)\equiv a_{0}
and
b_{\alpha,\beta}(0)\equiv b_{0}

modifies both the rate at which the oscillations of
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha,\beta}(t)
occur and their amplitude.
Generally, a smaller
\lambda_{0}
results in higher values of the fractional probability.
Particularly, the case when
\lambda_{0}=0
(effectively uncoupling the atom and the cavity mode) was studied in Ref.
Lu2018

When the cavity is initially in a Fock state, the comments made regarding
\lambda_{0}
can be extended to the term
\sqrt{n+1}
.

The opposite happens for
\beta=1.0
(Fig.

\mathcal{P}_{\alpha,1.0}(t)\rightarrow 1
as
(\Delta,\lambda_{0})\rightarrow 0
.
In addition, we observe that for
\lambda_{0}>1
, the dynamics are independent of
\Delta
, and dissipative effects are predominant, causing a rapid decay of the fractional probability.
Depending on the choice of
\lambda_{0}
, the minimum value of
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha,1.0}(t)
can be reached earlier.

As a consequence of the non-conservation of
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha,\beta}(t)
, the behavior of the observables suggests some physical divergences from the standard formulation
Lu2018
.
In this sense, to statistically characterize the behavior of the quantities of interest, we employ a normalization of the density matrix and the population inversion
SERGI2013
,
Zu2021
, which follows

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}^{\prime}_{\alpha,\beta}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\hat{\rho}_{\alpha,\beta}(t)}{\mathcal{P}_{\alpha,\beta}(t)},

(17)

\displaystyle W_{\alpha,\beta}^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{W_{\alpha,\beta}(t)}{\mathcal{P}_{\alpha,\beta}(t)}.

The normalized VNE (
S_{\alpha,\beta}^{A\prime}(t)
) follows from
\hat{\rho}^{\prime}_{\alpha,\beta}(t)
.
Assuming the initial state
|\Psi(0)\rangle=|e,n\rangle
, the equations for the normalized population inversion and VNE are

\displaystyle W^{\prime}_{\alpha,\beta}(t)={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{P}_{\alpha,\beta}(t)}\left[|a_{\alpha,\beta}(t)|^{2}-|b_{\alpha,\beta}(t)|^{2}\right],

\displaystyle S^{A\prime}_{\alpha,\beta}(t)={}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\mathcal{P}_{\alpha,\beta}(t)}\left[|a_{\alpha,\beta}(t)|^{2}\log_{2}\left(\frac{|a_{\alpha,\beta}(t)|^{2}}{\mathcal{P}_{\alpha,\beta}(t)}\right)\right.

\displaystyle+\left.|b_{\alpha,\beta}(t)|^{2}\log_{2}\left(\frac{|b_{\alpha,\beta}(t)|^{2}}{\mathcal{P}_{\alpha,\beta}(t)}\right)\right].

(18)

We perform this normalization to resemble the interpretation of the standard formulation, but this procedure does not eliminate the non-Markovian effects due to the FC formulation
Naber2004
,
Iomin2009
.
It is worth noting that, within the considered time window and given the choice of
\alpha
, the amount
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha,\beta}(t)
does not approach zero.
From this point onward, we will omit the term “normalized” when referring to the normalized population inversion and VNE.

\lambda(t)=\lambda_{0}

By further decreasing

4.2
Linear coupling

The linear coupling modulation, given by

\lambda(t)=\lambda_{0}(\zeta t),

(19)

was first considered by Joshi and Lawande
Joshi1993
.
This time dependence can be used to model the scenario of
a well-localized atom in a varying mode
LARSON2021

In this framework, the control parameters determine whether the change in the mode is adiabatic or sudden.

From now on, our goal is to study the modifications into
W^{\prime}_{\alpha,\beta}(t)
and
S^{A\prime}_{\alpha,\beta}(t)
induced by
different formats of
\lambda(t)
.
In this way, a closed solution as previously derived becomes challenging for arbitrary
\lambda(t)

To solve this problem, we resort to numerical integration of Eqs. (
15

\lambda(t)

In this subsection,
\lambda(t)
is defined by Eq. (
19

Throughout this work, the numerical method employed is the Adams-Bashforth-Moulton method, as presented by Diethelm, Ford, and Freed
diethelm2005
, with a step size of
0.001

4.4
Sinusoidal coupling

In cavity quantum electrodynamics, the atom traverses the cavity.
However, in the case of constant coupling, the atomic motion is disregarded.
Incorporating it, under the assumption that the kinetic energy is significantly greater than the interaction energy
LARSON2021
, leads to a time-dependent coupling modeled as

\lambda(t)=\lambda_{0}\sin(p \zeta t),

(21)

where
p
is the number of half-wavelengths of the field mode.
In this scenario,
\zeta
is a combination of the atomic velocity
v
and
cavity length
L

\zeta=v\pi/L
.
This approach was first proposed by Schlicher
Schlicher1989
,
and results in a periodic population inversion
Fang1998

Particularly interesting is the fact that when time-dependent disturbances, such as Eq. (
21

Pomeau1986
,
Prants1997
.
In this section, we set
\zeta=p=1.0

5
Conclusions

In this paper, we examined the fractional time dynamics of the generalized JC model,
considering a time-dependent coupling parameter and two different cases for the power of
i
in the fractional Schrödinger equation, thereby incorporating memory effects into the standard JC model.
We investigated how this extension affects population inversion and entanglement, as measured by the VNE.
When the coupling parameter is constant and
\alpha=\beta\neq 1
, the fractional probability oscillates around a fixed value.
Additionally, the detuning favors the emergence of higher values.
On the other hand, for
\beta=1
and
\alpha<1
, the fractional probability decays to a value close to
0
.
These effects are inherited by the population inversion and VNE,
resulting in quantities that oscillate around a fixed value (
\alpha=\beta\neq 1
)
or decay to an asymptotic value (
\alpha\neq\beta
and
\alpha<1
).
Additionally, we investigated how the parameters of the system can influence the behavior of the fractional probability, with detuning enhancing the effects of fractional control.

Considering a linear ramp in the modulation,
we observed that RO initiate slowly but presents an acceleration over time.
Remarkably, when
\alpha=\beta=0.7
, we observe, in some instances, values closer to the minimum in both the population inversion and the VNE than those obtained in the
\alpha=\beta=1.0
scenario.
As a consequence of the exponential coupling parameter, the RO are considerably faster than in the constant coupling scenario,
accompanied by a subtle change in the amplitude.
The asymptotic value for entanglement differs from that in the constant coupling case, as the dynamics ceases at a different point.
Considering the trigonometric coupling parameter, which reflects the semi-classical atomic motion in the standard scenario, we obtain non-periodic behavior for the population inversion and entanglement.
In fact, the quantities present periodic dynamics after a transient time for
\alpha=0.5
, suggesting that FC smooths the non-periodic nature of the TDJC model in this perspective.
In future work, we will investigate how the various parameters of the system affect the non-periodicity of the solutions.

The application of the TFSE to the JC model introduces memory effects, which can enhance the protection of quantum channels
Zu2021
and accelerate the time evolution of quantum systems
Wei2023
.
We expect that our generalizations, encompassing the off-resonance regime and multiple time-dependent couplings, will further advance the practical applications of FC.

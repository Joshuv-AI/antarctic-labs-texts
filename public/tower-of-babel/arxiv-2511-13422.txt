On the uniqueness of continuous spacetime extensions in 1+1 dimensions with applications to weak null singularities
Peter Cameron & Jan Sbierski

Abstract
Motivated by weak null singularities in black hole interiors, we study 1 + 1 1+1 dimensional Lorentzian manifolds ( M , g ) (M,g) which admit a continuous spacetime extension across a null boundary v = 0 v=0 , where v < 0 v<0 is a null coordinate. We study the degree to which such extensions are unique up to the boundary . Firstly, we find that in general not even the C 0 C^{0} -structure of the extension is uniquely determined by the assumption that the metric extends continuously. However, we exhibit an interesting local-global relation regarding the C 0 C^{0} -structure which in particular entails its rigidity for “strongly spherically symmetric” continuous extensions across the Cauchy horizon of the Reissner-Nordström spacetime. Secondly, we construct continuous extensions which have the same C 0 C^{0} -structure, but do not have equivalent C 1 C^{1} -structures. This construction also carries over to weak null singularities in 3 + 1 3+1 dimensions. Understanding the uniqueness properties of continuous spacetime extensions to the boundary is of importance for the study of low-regularity inextendibility problems [ 25 ] .

Previous results

Main results

to terminate only a subset of constant

shows that, under our assumptions on

4

4

See Proposition
4.2
for the precise statement. It is important to assume that the two curves of constant

Proof.

4.2.1

As an example we consider the Reissner-Nordström black hole “interior”

5
The
C^{1}
-Structure of Extensions in 1+1 Dimensions

5.1
Non-rigidity of the
C^{1}
-structure

We have seen in Section
4.1
how the requirement that the metric extends continuously imposes a certain rigidity on the
C^{0}
-structure of the extension. In particular, Theorem
4.3
outlines the two possible local
C^{0}
-structures: that of the corner extension and that of the reference extension. In this section we investigate whether the local
C^{0}
-structure is sufficient to determine the local
C^{1}
-structure. Example
5.1
shows that this is not the case if the
C^{0}
-structure is that of the corner. In Example
5.2
we show that it is also not the case if the
C^{0}
-structure is that of the reference extension. We thus conclude that the assumption of the metric extending continuously is not strong enough to give us rigidity properties on the
C^{1}
-structure of the extension.

Example 5.1
.

For
\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
with
\beta^{\prime}>\beta
, let
\iota_{corner,\beta}:M_{t}\hookrightarrow\tilde{M}_{t}
and
\iota_{corner,\beta^{\prime}}:M_{t}\hookrightarrow\tilde{M}_{t}
be two corner extensions of
(M_{t},g_{t})
into
(\tilde{M}_{t},\tilde{g}^{\prime}_{\beta})
and
(\tilde{M}_{t},\tilde{g}^{\prime}_{\beta^{\prime}})
as described in Example
4.1
.
We claim that
\iota_{corner,\beta}
and
\iota_{corner,\beta^{\prime}}
are
C^{0}
-equivalent but
C^{1}
-inequivalent extensions in the sense that the identification map
id:\iota_{corner,\beta}\rightarrow\iota_{corner,\beta^{\prime}}
defined by
id:=\iota_{corner,\beta^{\prime}}\circ\iota_{corner,\beta}^{-1}
extends to
\overline{\iota_{corner,\beta}(M_{t})}\subset\tilde{M}_{t}
as a homeomorphism but not as a diffeomorphism.

The identification map is given in coordinates by

id(p,q)=\left(p|q|^{\frac{\beta^{\prime}-\beta}{1-\beta}},-|q|^{\frac{1-\beta^{\prime}}{1-\beta}}\right)=:(p^{\prime}(p,q),q^{\prime}(p,q)).

(69)

This map extends as a homeomorphism to
\overline{\iota_{corner,\beta}(M_{t})}=\{(p,q)\in(-1,1)\times(-1,0]:|p|\leq|q|^{\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}}\}
. However, we have

\begin{split}\partial_{q}q^{\prime}(p,q)&=\frac{1-\beta^{\prime}}{1-\beta}|q|^{\frac{\beta-\beta^{\prime}}{1-\beta}}\\
&\rightarrow\infty\text{ as }q\rightarrow 0\end{split}

(70)

so
id
does not extend to
\overline{\iota_{corner,\beta}(M_{t})}
as a diffeomorphism.

In the following example we construct a
C^{0}
-extension of
(M_{t},g_{t})
which is locally
C^{0}
-equivalent to
\overline{M_{t}}
at
(0,0)\in\overline{M_{t}}
but not locally
C^{1}
-equivalent there.

Example 5.2
.

Let
D:=\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]\times\left[-\frac{1}{2},0\right]\subset\mathbbm{R}^{2}
with standard
(p,q)
-coordinates.
Define real-valued functions
u,v
on
D
by

\begin{split}u(p,q)&=\begin{cases}-C\int_{0}^{p}\log(s^{2}+2q^{2})ds\qquad&\textnormal{ for }(p,q)\neq(0,0)\\
0&\textnormal{ for }(p,q)=(0,0)\end{cases}\\
v(p,q)&=\begin{cases}-C\int_{0}^{q}\frac{1}{\log(p^{2}+q^{2}+t^{2})}dt\qquad\;\,&\textnormal{ for }(p,q)\neq(0,0)\\
0&\textnormal{ for }(p,q)=(0,0)\end{cases}\end{split}

(71)

where
C>0
is a constant to be determined. It is easy to show that
u
and
v
are continuous on
D
. For
q<0
we have

u(p,q)=-Cp\log(p^{2}+2q^{2})+2Cp-2\sqrt{2}Cq\tan^{-1}(p/\sqrt{2}q)\;.

Furthermore, we note that

\begin{split}u(p,q)&\gtrless 0\text{ for }p\gtrless 0\\
u(p,q)&=0\iff p=0\\
v(p,q)&\leq 0\\
v(p,q)&=0\iff q=0\end{split}

(72)

and

\begin{split}(u(p,q),v(p,q))&=(-u(-p,q),v(-p,q)).\end{split}

(73)

Let
\epsilon^{\prime}\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
. The function
(s,q)\mapsto\log(s^{2}+2q^{2})
is smooth on
[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]\times\left[-\frac{1}{2},-\epsilon^{\prime}\right]
. It follows that
u(p,q)
is smooth on
\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]\times\left[-\frac{1}{2},-\epsilon^{\prime}\right]
. Similarly, the function
(t,p,q)\mapsto\frac{1}{\log(p^{2}+q^{2}+t^{2})}
is smooth on the region
t\in[q,0]
,
(p,q)\in\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]\times\left[-\frac{1}{2},-\epsilon^{\prime}\right]
. It follows that
v(p,q)
is smooth on
\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]\times\left[-\frac{1}{2},-\epsilon^{\prime}\right]
. Since
\epsilon^{\prime}\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
was arbitrary, we conclude that
u(p,q)
and
v(p,q)
are smooth on
\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]\times\left[-\frac{1}{2},0\right)
.

On
\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]\times\left[-\frac{1}{2},0\right)
, we have

\begin{split}\frac{\partial u}{\partial p}&=-C\log(p^{2}+2q^{2})\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial q}&=-2\sqrt{2}C\tan^{-1}(p/\sqrt{2}q)\\
\frac{\partial v}{\partial p}&=2Cp\int^{q}_{0}\frac{1}{(\log(p^{2}+q^{2}+t^{2}))^{2}(p^{2}+q^{2}+t^{2})}dt\\
\frac{\partial v}{\partial q}&=-\frac{C}{\log(p^{2}+2q^{2})}+2Cq\int^{q}_{0}\frac{1}{(\log(p^{2}+q^{2}+t^{2}))^{2}(p^{2}+q^{2}+t^{2})}dt.\end{split}

(74)

For future use, we collect the following properties which hold on
\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]\times\left[-\frac{1}{2},0\right)
.

\begin{split}\frac{\partial u}{\partial p},\frac{\partial v}{\partial q}&>0\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial q}&\gtrless 0\text{ for }p\gtrless 0\\

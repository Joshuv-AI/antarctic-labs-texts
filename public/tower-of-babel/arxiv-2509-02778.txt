Dihedral groups of square-free order are DCI-groups Thanks: The first author was supported by the Slovenian Research and Innovation Agency (ARIS), research program P1-0285 and research projects J1-50000 and N1-0391, and the second author was supported by OTKA grants 138596 and FK 142993.
István Kovács, Gábor Somlai

Abstract
. A finite group G G is a called a DCI-group if any two isomorphic Cayley digraphs of G G are also isomorphic via an automorphism of G G . If G G is a non-abelian generalised dihedral DCI-group, then Dobson, Muzychuk, and Spiga proved that G G must be a dihedral group of square-free order (Ars Math. Contemp., 2022). In this paper, we prove that the converse statement also holds, i. e., all dihedral groups of square-free order are DCI-groups.

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a finite group and let
S
be a subset of
G
such that
e_{G}\notin S
, where

e_{G}
denotes the identity element of
G
. The
Cayley digraph

\cay(G,S)
is defined to have vertex set
G
and arcs of the form
(g,sg)
, where
g\in G
and
s\in S
.
Any automorphism
\alpha
of
G
induces an isomorphism between
\cay(G,S)
and
\cay(G,S^{\alpha})
, these digraphs are called
Cayley isomorphic
.
The subset
S
is called a
CI-subset
if for every
T\subseteq G,\ e_{G}\notin T
, the relation
\cay(G,S)\cong\cay(G,T)
implies that the two digraphs are also Cayley isomorphic. The group
G
is called a
DCI-group
if all subsets of
G\setminus\{e_{G}\}
are CI-subsets, and it is called a
CI-group
if the same conclusion holds for only those subsets which are closed with respect to taking inverses.

Ádám
[
1
]
conjectured that all cyclic groups are DCI-groups.
This was disproved soon after its publication, however, the classifications of cyclic
CI- and DCI-groups were given only 30 years later by Muzychuk
[
19
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
19
]
)
.

A cyclic group of order
n
is a DCI-group if and only if
n
is square-free or twice square-free. Furthermore, the cyclic CI- but not a DCI-groups are those of order
8,\,9
and
18
, resp.

The request for determining all finite CI-groups appeared in
[
3
]
(see also the survey paper
[
15
]
). Building upon the work of several mathematicians,
the candidates of DCI-groups has been reduced to a restricted list
[
7
,
17
]
. However, the verification that a particular group on this list is indeed a DCI-group is a difficult task. Further significant restriction on generalised dihedral DCI-groups
was obtained by Dobson et al.
[
8
]
. For an abelian group
A
, the
generalised dihedral group

\dih(A)
is the semidirect product
of

with the cyclic group
\langle x\rangle
of order

, where

acts on

by conjugation as

(

). If

is a non-trivial cyclic group of order

, then

is known as the dihedral group of order
2n
. In this paper, we denote by

the
dihedral group
of order
2n
.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
8
]
)
.

Let
\dih(A)
be a generalised dihedral group over the abelian group

. If

\dih(A)
is a CI-group, then, for every odd prime

has order

or

. If
\dih(A)
is a DCI-group, then, in addition, the Sylow 3-subgroup has order

If

is non-abelian,
then there are involutions both inside and outside of the center

\dih(A)
is not a DCI-group.
Therefore, the determination of the non-abelian generalised dihedral DCI-groups is reduced
to decide whether the dihedral group

is a DCI-group for an odd square-free number

. This has been confirmed for special values of

[

]
,

[

The group

If
n

We prove the above theorem using the

The application of Schur rings (S-rings for short) to the study of DCI-groups dates back to

2.
S-rings

Let
H
be a finite group. A subring
\mathcal{A}
of the group ring
\mathbb{Z}H
is called
a
Schur ring
(
S-ring
for short) over
H
if there exists a partition
\cS(\mathcal{A})
of
H
such that

(a)

\{e_{H}\}\in\cS(\mathcal{A})
,

(b)

if
X\in\cS(\mathcal{A})
then
X^{-1}\in\cS(\mathcal{A})
,

(c)

\mathcal{A}=\Span_{\mathbb{Z}}\{\underline{X}\mid X\in\cS(\mathcal{A})\}
.

This definition is due to Wielandt
[
25
, Chapter IV]
and it is motivated by a result of Schur
[
22
]
, which shows that for any group

G\in\Sup(\hat{H})
, the
\mathbb{Z}
-submodule

\Span_{\mathbb{Z}}\{\underline{X}~\mid~X\in\orb(G_{e_{H}},H)\}

is a subring of
\mathbb{Z}H
(see also
[
25
, Theorem 24.1]
). The latter subring is called the
transitivity module
over
H
induced by
G
, denoted by
V(H,G_{e_{H}})
. Transitive modules are S-rings, however, the converse is not true –
there exist S-rings which are not transitive modules
[
25
]
. In this section, we
review all concepts and results from S-ring theory needed in this paper.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an S-ring over
H
.
The classes of
\cS(\mathcal{A})
are called the
basic sets
of

\mathcal{A}
and the number
\rk(\mathcal{A}):=|\cS(\mathcal{A})|
is called the
rank
of
\mathcal{A}
.
A subset
X\subseteq H
is called an

\mathcal{A}
-set
if
\underline{X}\in\mathcal{A}
and a subgroup

K\leq H
is called an
\mathcal{A}
-subgroup
if
\underline{K}\in\mathcal{A}
.
With each
\mathcal{A}
-set
X
one can naturally associate two

\mathcal{A}
-subgroups, namely,
\langle X\rangle
and

\stab(X):=\{h\in H\mid hX=Xh=X\}.

Let
L\lhd U\leq H
. The section
S:=U/L
of
H
is called an
\mathcal{A}
-section
if
U
and
L
are
\mathcal{A}
-subgroups. In this case, the
\mathbb{Z}
-submodule of
\mathbb{Z}S
, defined as

\mathcal{A}_{S}:=\Span_{\mathbb{Z}}\{\underline{X/L}~\mid~X\in\cS(\mathcal{A}),X\subseteq U\}

is an S-ring over
S
. Here
X/L
is the subset of the
group
S=U/L
consisting of the cosets
Lx
, where
x
runs over
X
.
Note that, if
\mathcal{A}=V(H,G_{e_{H}})
and
S
is an
\mathcal{A}
-section,
then
\mathcal{A}_{S}=V(S,(G^{S})_{e_{S}})

[
11
, Proposition 2.8]
.

Suppose, in addition, that
\mathcal{B}
is an S-ring over another group

K
(possibly
K=H
). By a
combinatorial isomorphism
(
isomorphism
for short)
from the S-ring
\mathcal{A}
to the S-ring
\mathcal{B}
we mean a bijective mapping
\varphi:H\to K
such that

(a)

\rk(\mathcal{A})=\rk(\mathcal{B})=r
,

(b)

there are orderings

and

of the basic sets in
\cS(\mathcal{A})
and
\cS(\mathcal{B})
, resp., such that
\varphi
is an isomorphism from
\cay(H,X_{i})
to
\cay(K,Y_{i})
for every

.

Now,
\varphi
is called

if it maps
the identity element
e_{H}
to the identity element
e_{K}
.
If there is an isomorphism from
\mathcal{A}
to
\mathcal{B}
, then
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are said to be

\mathcal{A}\cong\mathcal{B}
.
Let

denote the set of all isomorphisms from
\mathcal{A}
to
\mathcal{B}
.
If
\varphi\in\iso(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is normalised, then

X^{\,\varphi}\in\cS(\mathcal{B})
for every basic set
X\in\cS(\mathcal{A})
; moreover,
it holds:

(1)

\forall X,Y\in\cS(\mathcal{A}),~(XY)^{\varphi}=X^{\varphi}Y^{\varphi}.

In the next proposition we collect further properties.

Proposition 2.1
.

(
[
11
, Proposition 2.7]
)

Let

be a normalised isomorphism from an

(i)

(ii)

(iii)

In this paper we are interested in isomorphisms between S-rings over the same group

\displaystyle\iso(\mathcal{A})

\displaystyle=\big\{\varphi\in\iso(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})~\mid~\mathcal{A}^{\prime}~\text{is an S-ring over}~H\big\},

\displaystyle\iso_{1}(\mathcal{A})

of

.
It follows directly from the above definitions that for every

. The S-ring

is called a

if the following equality holds

(2)

\iso_{1}(\mathcal{A})=\aut(\mathcal{A})_{e_{H}}\aut(H).

Let
G\in\Sup(\hat{H})
be a
2
-closed group
(for a definition, see
[

]
).
Hirasaka and Muzychuk

[

]

is a CI-S-ring for every group

.
Somlai and Muzychuk
[

]
pointed out that the analysis of
the S-rings

can be reduced to those groups

that are minimal with respect to the relation
\prec_{\hat{H}}
on
\Sup(\hat{H})
defined as follows (see
[
11
, Definition 2]
):
For two groups
A,B\in\Sup(\hat{H})
,
A
is said to be an
\mathit{\hat{H}}
-complete subgroup
of
B
, denoted by

(a)

,

(b)

for every
\varphi\in\sym(H)
, the inclusion
(\hat{H})^{\varphi}\leq B
implies that

(\hat{H})^{\varphi b}\leq A

.

The relation
\prec_{\hat{H}}
is a partial order on

\Sup(\hat{H})
and the set of minimal elements of the poset
(\Sup(\hat{H}),\prec_{\hat{H}})

will be denoted by

.
The following statement is a direct consequence of
[

(see also
[

).

Proposition 2.2
.

Let

be a group and assume that

is a CI-S-ring
for every

. Then

is a DCI-group.

In fact, we are going to derive Theorem

by
showing that the condition in Proposition

holds whenever

and

Theorem 2.3
.

Let

be an odd square-free integer larger than

, and

. Then

A crucial step in the proof of the above theorem is
to show that the group

The proof of Theorem

will be presented in Section

3.
d
-groups

Song et al.
[
24
]
call a permutation group a
d
-group if it
contains a regular dihedral subgroup. We extend slightly this definition by saying
that a permutation group of degree
n
is a
d
-group

if it contains a transitive dihedral subgroup of order

, where

n^{*}:=\begin{cases}n&\text{if~$n$ is even},\\
2n&\text{if~$n$ is odd}.\end{cases}

If

is even, then the

we consider is regular. On the other hand, if

is odd, then the stabilizers of

As in our case dihedral groups have order at least

-groups have degree at least

-group coincides
with the one proposed in

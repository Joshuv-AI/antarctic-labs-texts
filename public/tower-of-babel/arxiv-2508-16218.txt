Hybrid Precoding Revisited: Low-Dimensional Subspace Perspective for MU-MIMO Systems
Mintaek Oh, Jinseok Choi

Abstract
This letter presents a low-complexity hybrid precoding framework for multiuser multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) systems by leveraging a low-dimensional subspace property. Under the low-dimensional subspace perspective, we first identify an unconstrained optimal radio-frequency (RF) precoder. We then optimize a hybrid precoder via a reduced-complexity precoding method. We further extend the proposed framework to ( i ) (i) a dynamic-subarray antenna partitioning algorithm that adaptively allocates subsets of antennas associated with RF chains, and ( i ​ i ) (ii) a channel covariance-based approach to exploit statistical channel state information at a transmitter (CSIT), ensuring robustness with partial CSIT. Simulations validate that our proposed algorithms achieve superior performance while significantly reducing complexity compared to existing methods.



I
Introduction

Massive multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) systems have emerged as a cornerstone technology for next-generation wireless communications, offering substantial gains in spectral efficiency (SE) and coverage through the deployment of large antenna arrays at a base station (BS)
[
1
]
.
In theory, simply scaling the number of BS antennas yields a near-linear SE increase under ideal conditions.
In practice, however, fully-digital precoding over enormous antennas is often impractical because every element demands its own radio-frequency (RF) chain, driving up hardware cost and power consumption
[
2
]
.

To address these hardware constraints, hybrid precoding architectures, which combine analog and digital processing, have been proposed as a cost-effective solution.
By employing a limited number of RF chains connected to a larger antenna array through analog phase shifters, hybrid architectures can approximate the performance of fully-digital systems while significantly reducing hardware complexity
[
3
]
.
Despite their promise, designing efficient hybrid precoders remains challenging due to the non-convex unit-modulus constraints imposed by analog phase shifters as well as the coupling between analog and digital precoding optimization
[
4
]
.

Conventional hybrid precoding approaches simply predominantly employ orthogonal matching pursuit (OMP) to construct RF precoders based on propagation path angles
[
5
,
6
]
.
While the OMP-based hybrid precoder design simplifies the problem by framing it as a sparsity-constrained matrix reconstruction task, it is inherently limited to sparse channel environments.
When extended to multiuser scenarios, it was revealed that the RF precoder determined by phase angles of a channel matrix can attain near-optimal SE performance using a zero-forcing (ZF) precoder
[
7
]
.
Additionally, in
[
3
]
, a high-performance RF precoding design was proposed based on an iterative RF precoder design with a ZF digital precoder.
However, most hybrid precoding designs rely on either linear precoders or iterative RF precoding designs. Therefore, substantial gains remain achievable through advanced nonlinear techniques and computationally efficient approaches.

Unlike most prior works that focus on fully-connected architectures where each RF chain connects to all antennas, hybrid precoding studies have emphasized the potential benefits of partially-connected structures, which reduce hardware complexity
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
.
To deal with this, dynamic-subarray designs have been introduced, where each subarray is dynamically connected to a specific set of BS antennas
[
11
,
9
]
.
However, these approaches typically rely on computationally intensive iterative algorithms that alternately optimize the selection of BS antennas associated with RF precoders as well as analog and digital components, making them impractical for real-time implementation.
Moreover, the impractical issue is the common assumption of perfect channel state information at the transmitter (CSIT).
This assumption is unrealistic in dynamic environments where instantaneous CSIT is unavailable.

To address the aforementioned issues, we propose a low-complexity hybrid precoding framework based on a low-dimensional subspace perspective for multiuser MIMO (MU-MIMO) systems.
Our key contributions are as follows:

•

By identifying an optimal RF precoder through a low-dimensional subspace property, we develop a low-complexity hybrid precoding framework.

•

Based on our framework, we introduce dynamic-subarray architectures that dynamically partition BS antennas into subsets associated with RF chains.

•

We further extend our RF precoder design to operate with statistical CSIT, enabling robust performance.

Our proposed hybrid precoding methods are evaluated through simulations.
The results demonstrate that our methods achieve significant complexity reduction while maintaining SE performance compared to state-of-the-art methods, for both fully-connected and dynamic-subarray architectures.

II
System Model and Problem Formulation

II-A

System Model

We consider a narrowband downlink massive MIMO system with
N
BS antennas,
N_{\sf RF}
RF chains, and
K
single-antenna users where
N>N_{\sf RF}
.
The hybrid precoding architecture operates in two stages.
First, the digital baseband precoder
{\bf F}_{\sf D}=[{\bf f}_{\sf D,1},\cdots,{\bf f}_{\sf D,K}]\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{N_{\sf RF}\times K}
processes
K
data streams.
These digitally precoded signals are then upconverted through
N_{\sf RF}
RF chains and fed to an analog RF precoder
{\bf F}_{\sf RF}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{N\times N_{\sf RF}}
, which is implemented using phase shifters satisfying
|{\bf F}_{\sf RF}(i,j)|=1
for all elements.
In this regard, the overall hybrid precoder is
{\bf F}={\bf F}_{\sf RF}{\bf F}_{\sf D}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{N\times K}
.
The transmitted signal vector is expressed as
{\bf x}={\bf F}{\bf s}
where
{\bf s}=[s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{K}]^{\sf T}
contains the data symbols with
s_{k}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,P),\;\forall k
.

Assuming all channels have the same number of propagation paths
L
, we adopt a geometric channel model as

\displaystyle{\bf h}_{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\alpha_{k,\ell}{\bf a}(\theta_{k,\ell}),\forall k,

(1)

where
\theta_{k,\ell}\in[-\pi,\pi]
is the
\ell
th path’s angle of departure (AoD),
{\bf a}(\cdot)
is an array response vector at the BS, and
\alpha_{k,\ell}\sim\mbox{$\mathcal{C}$}\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}(0,1)
is a complex path gain.
The overall channel matrix is
{\bf H}=[{\bf h}_{1},{\bf h}_{2},\cdots,{\bf h}_{K}]\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{N\times K}
.
We consider uniform linear arrays at the BS in which the array response vector is defined as
{\bf a}(\theta)=\left[1,e^{j\frac{2\pi d}{\lambda}\sin(\theta)},\ldots,e^{j(N-1)\frac{2\pi d}{\lambda}\sin(\theta)}\right]^{\sf T}
,
where
\lambda
is a signal wavelength and
d
is a distance between antenna elements.
The received signal of user
k
is given by

\displaystyle y_{k}={\bf h}_{k}^{\sf H}\left({\bf F}_{\sf RF}{\bf f}_{{\sf D},k}\right)s_{k}+\sum_{\ell=1,\ell\neq k}^{K}{\bf h}_{k}^{\sf H}\left({\bf F}_{\sf RF}{\bf f}_{{\sf D},\ell}\right)s_{\ell}+n_{k},

(2)

where
n_{k}\sim\mbox{$\mathcal{C}$}\mbox{$\mathcal{N}$}(0,\sigma^{2})
is additive white Gaussian noise.
We assume the perfect CSIT scenario unless mentioned otherwise.

II-B

Problem Formulation

Based on (
2
), the SE of user
k
is given by

\displaystyle R_{k}=\log_{2}\left(1+\frac{\left|{\bf h}_{k}^{\sf H}\left({\bf F}_{\sf RF}{\bf f}_{{\sf D},k}\right)\right|^{2}}{\sum_{\ell=1,\ell\neq k}^{K}\left|{\bf h}_{k}^{\sf H}\left({\bf F}_{\sf RF}{\bf f}_{{\sf D},\ell}\right)\right|^{2}+\sigma^{2}/P}\right).

(3)

We formulate the SE maximization problem as

\displaystyle\mathop{{\text{maximize}}}_{{{\bf F}_{\sf D},{\bf F}_{\sf RF}}}

\displaystyle\;\;\sum_{k=1}^{K}R_{k}

(4)

subject to

\displaystyle\;\;{\rm tr}\left({\bf F}_{\sf RF}{\bf F}_{\sf D}{\bf F}_{\sf D}^{\sf H}{\bf F}_{\sf RF}^{\sf H}\right)=1,

(5)

\displaystyle\;\left|{\bf F}_{\sf RF}(i,j)\right|=1,\;\;\forall i,j.

(6)

The problem in (
4

6

III
Fully-connected Hybrid Architectures

This work presents that hybrid precoding architectures can implement fully-digital precoding with potentially fewer RF chains.
To achieve the fully-digital precoder, the number of RF chains must satisfy
N_{\sf RF}\geq K

[
3
]
.
This fundamental constraint means that hybrid architectures must have
at least
as many RF chains as user streams to replicate fully-digital performance.
From a hardware‑efficiency standpoint, provisioning
\!N_{\sf RF}>K
would only increase cost and power consumption without yielding additional beamforming gains.
Thus, we set
N_{\sf RF}=K
, i.e., the minimum number of RF chains capable of realizing the fully-digital precoder
{\bf F}_{\sf opt}
unless mentioned otherwise.

III-A

Optimizing RF Precoder
{\bf F}_{\sf RF}

For the RF precoder, we consider a semi-unitary condition, i.e.,
{\bf F}_{\sf RF}^{\sf H}{\bf F}_{\sf RF}=N{\bf I}_{N_{\sf RF}}
.
Throughout this letter, our goal is to find the precoders that are most similar to the fully-digital precoder
{\bf F}_{\sf opt}
in the decomposed form, i.e.,
{\bf F}_{\sf opt}\approx{\bf F}_{\sf RF}{\bf F}_{\sf D}
.
To this end, we introduce the following low-dimensional subspace property of the optimal fully-digital precoder.

Theorem 1
.

(Low-dimensional subspace property
[
12
]
)
Any nontrivial stationary point with the optimal precoder must lie in the range space of
{\bf H}
:
{\bf F}_{\sf opt}^{\star}={\bf H}{\bf W}
with
{\bf W}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{K\times K}
.

Based on Theorem
1
, the precoders in the hybrid structures can be reorganized to yield the optimal RF precoder, which is presented in the following proposition.

Proposition 1
.

The optimal RF precoder
{\bf F}_{\sf RF}^{\star}
is achieved by
e^{j{\rm arg}({\bf U})}
where
{\bf U}
is the left-singular vectors of
{\bf H}
.

Proof.

Based on Theorem
1
, in hybrid precoding architectures, the optimal fully-digital precoder is expressed as

\displaystyle{\bf F}_{\sf opt}^{\star}={\bf H}{\bf W}\approx{\bf F}_{\sf RF}{\bf F}_{\sf D}

\displaystyle={\bf F}_{\sf RF}\tilde{{\bf H}}\tilde{{\bf W}}

\displaystyle={\bf F}_{\sf RF}{\bf F}^{\sf H}_{\sf RF}{\bf H}\tilde{{\bf W}},

(7)

where
\tilde{{\bf H}}={\bf F}^{\sf H}_{\sf RF}{\bf H}
is the effective channel for the digital baseband precoder and the optimal digital baseband precoder is given by
{\bf F}_{\sf D}=\tilde{{\bf H}}\tilde{{\bf W}}
.
Using singular value decomposition (SVD) of the channel matrix:
{\bf H}={\bf U}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}{\bf V}^{\sf H}
, we have

\displaystyle{\bf U}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}{\bf V}^{\sf H}{\bf W}\approx{\bf F}_{\sf RF}{\bf F}^{\sf H}_{\sf RF}{\bf U}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}{\bf V}^{\sf H}\tilde{{\bf W}}.

(8)

In (
8
), it is obvious that the optimal RF precoder is the left-singular matrix
{\bf U}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{N\times K}
because of
{\rm rank}({\bf H})=K
.
Therefore, the RF precoder optimization problem is given by

\displaystyle\mathop{{\text{minimize}}}_{{{\bf F}_{\sf RF}}}

\displaystyle\;\;\left\|{\bf F}_{\sf RF}-{\bf U}\right\|_{F}^{2}

(9)

subject to

\displaystyle\;\;\left|{\bf F}_{\sf RF}(i,j)\right|=1,\forall i,j.

(10)

To solve (
9
), we consider element-wise optimization as

\displaystyle\left|{\bf F}_{\sf RF}(i,j)-{\bf U}(i,j)\right|^{2}=

(11)

\displaystyle\quad 1\!+\!|{\bf U}(i,j)|^{2}\!-\!2|{\bf U}(i,j)|\cos({\rm arg}({\bf F}_{\sf RF}(i,j))\!-\!{\rm arg}({\bf U}(i,j))).

The optimal solution of (
11
) is straightforward:
{\rm arg}({\bf F}_{\sf RF}(i,j))={\rm arg}({\bf U}(i,j)),\forall i,j
.
Therefore, the optimal RF precoder with the unit-modulus constraint (
10
) is achieved by extracting phase angles of
{\bf U}
:
{\bf F}_{\sf RF}^{\star}=e^{j{\rm arg}({\bf U})}
.
∎

Remark 1
.

Most existing RF precoder designs in hybrid precoding architectures for multiuser systems employ the following design:
{\bf F}_{\sf RF}=e^{j{\rm arg}({\bf H})}

[
7
]
.
This approach has shown near-optimal performance in multiuser systems where
N_{\sf RF}=K

[
7
,
3
]
.
Similarly, our approach based on Theorem
1
is capable of incorporating this phase extraction approach by treating
{\bf F}_{\sf RF}
as

and considering

as

However, designing the RF precoder by extracting the channel angles does not ensure the optimal RF precoding property as well as the unitary property from

In contrast, our RF precoder design leverages
{\bf U}
, which represents the optimal strategy while maintaining the unitary property.

[

This method optimizes
\tilde{{\bf W}}

IV
Extension to Practical Hybrid Architectures

IV-A

Dynamic-Subarray Hybrid Precoding

In this subsection, we propose the hybrid precoding strategy for partially-connected architectures, where each RF chain connects to only a subset of BS antennas.
We assume that
N
is divisible by
N_{\sf RF}
, with all RF chains connected to equally-sized subsets of
N_{\sf sub}=\frac{N}{N_{\sf RF}}
antennas, thereby preserving the unitary property of
{\bf F}_{\sf RF}
.
Let
\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}_{r}
represent the subset of antenna indices connected to the
r
th RF chain.
To partition such subset, the straightforward approach is to sequentially assign the fixed antenna indices:
\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}_{1}=\{1,\cdots,N_{\sf sub}\},\cdots,\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}_{N_{\sf RF}}=\{(N_{\sf RF}-1)N_{\sf sub}+1,\cdots,N_{\sf RF}N_{\sf sub}\}
.
With this fixed-subarray architecture, the RF precoder becomes block-diagonal as

\displaystyle{\bf F}_{\sf RF}={\rm blkdiag}\left({\bf f}_{{\sf RF},\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}_{1}},{\bf f}_{{\sf RF},\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}_{2}},\ldots,{\bf f}_{{\sf RF},\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}_{N_{\sf RF}}}\right),

(16)

where
{\bf f}_{{\sf RF},\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}_{r}}
is a RF precoding vector associated with the
r
th RF chain.
However, this fixed-subarray structure may not be optimal because the antenna assignment is predetermined regardless of channel conditions.
To overcome this limitation, we formulate a dynamic-subarray partitioning problem that adaptively selects subsets based on channel conditions:

\displaystyle\left\{\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}_{r}^{\star}\right\}_{r=1}^{N_{\sf RF}}=\mathop{\rm arg\,max}_{\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}_{1},\ldots,\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}_{N_{\mathrm{RF}}}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}R_{k}

(17)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}\;\bigcup_{r=1}^{N_{\mathrm{RF}}}\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}_{r}=\{1,2,\cdots,N\},

(18)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\;\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}_{i}\cap\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}_{j}=\emptyset\quad\text{for }i\neq j,

(19)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\;|\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}_{r}|=N_{\sf sub}\quad\forall r.

(20)

This combinatorial optimization problem requires searching over all possible subarray configurations.
The total number of possible combinations is given by
\frac{N!}{(N_{\sf sub}!)^{N_{\sf RF}}N_{\sf RF}!}
.
For instance, when
N\!=\!32
and
N_{\sf RF}\!=\!4
, the number of possible configurations is approximately
4.15\!\times\!10^{15}
.
Therefore, an efficient approach is essential to make this problem tractable in practice.

Given this subarray architecture, our goal is to design the RF precoder
{\bf F}_{\sf RF}
to approximate a desired unitary matrix
{\bf U}
as closely as possible.
Accordingly, under the constraints in (
18
)–(
20
), the optimization problem in (
21
) can be reformulated as

\displaystyle\left\{\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}_{r}^{\star}\right\}_{r=1}^{N_{\sf RF}}=\mathop{\rm arg\,max}_{\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}_{1},\ldots,\mbox{$\mathcal{S}$}_{N_{\sf RF}}}\|{\bf F}_{\sf RF}-{\bf U}\|_{F}.

(21)

To solve this problem, we leverage the structure of
{\bf U}
.
Specifically, for each RF chain
r
, we select the antenna indices corresponding to
N_{\sf sub}
largest-magnitude elements in
r
th column of
|{\bf U}|
.
This approach is motivated by the observation that elements of larger magnitude contribute more significantly to the precoding performance, and assigning these antennas to the corresponding RF chains helps minimize
\|{\bf F}_{\sf RF}-{\bf U}\|_{F}
.

We describe the proposed dynamic-subarray partitioning algorithm in Algorithm

.
Given
{\bf U}
from
{\bf H}={\bf U}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}{\bf V}^{\sf H}

\boldsymbol{\Sigma}

.
For each RF chain, the algorithm sorts antenna indices in descending order based on

\mathcal{O}(N_{\sf RF}N\log N)

\mathcal{O}(NK^{2}+N_{\sf RF}N\log N+T(K^{3}+N_{\sf RF}K^{2}))

N
and linear scaling in
N_{\sf RF}
.
This low-complexity method provides a viable solution for partially-connected hybrid precoding in massive MIMO systems, balancing performance and computational efficiency.

IV-B

Covariance-based Approach

In practice, it is challenging for the phase-shifter networks to re-steer their beams each channel coherence block to track small-scale fading due to its analog nature and high dimension.
A practical alternative can be a two‑stage design:
(i)
optimize and fix the RF precoder using the slowly varying statistical CSIT (e.g., channel covariance), and
(ii)
design the low‑dimensional digital precoder from instantaneous CSIT with this RF precoder
[
14
]
.
Motivated by this, we extend our framework that exploits the channel covariance, thereby avoiding frequent analog re-steering while relying on instantaneous CSIT only at the digital stage.

Let
{\bf R}=\mbox{$\mathbb{E}$}[{\bf H}{\bf H}^{\sf H}]
represent the channel covariance matrix and its eigenvalue decomposition is
{\bf R}={\bf V}_{{\bf R}}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{{\bf R}}{\bf V}_{{\bf R}}^{\sf H}
.
We now consider the channel matrix as
{\bf H}={\bf A}{\bf H}_{\sf b}
where
{\bf A}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{N\times L_{\sf tot}}
is an array response matrix and
{\bf H}_{\sf b}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$}^{L_{\sf tot}\times K}
is a beam domain channel matrix with
L_{\sf tot}=LK
.
The sample channel covariance matrix over a sample time
T_{s}
is defined as

\displaystyle\tilde{{\bf R}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T_{s}}\sum_{i=1}^{T_{s}}{\bf H}[i]{\bf H}[i]^{\sf H}={\bf A}\left(\frac{1}{T_{s}}\sum_{i=1}^{T_{s}}{\bf H}_{\sf b}[i]{\bf H}_{\sf b}[i]^{\sf H}\right){\bf A}^{\sf H},

(22)

where
{\bf H}[i]

VI
Conclusion

We proposed the low-complexity hybrid precoding framework for MU-MIMO systems.
Our approach first identified the optimal RF precoder by leveraging the low-dimensional subspace property, and then optimized the digital precoder via R-WMMSE.
To deal with practical hybrid scenarios, we further extended our proposed framework in two key directions:
(i)
dynamic-subarray architectures with adaptive antenna partitioning and
(ii)
the channel covariance-based approach that ensures robust performance when statistical CSIT is used for designing the RF precoder.
These findings confirm that our proposed algorithms effectively provide practical hybrid precoding solutions with both improved performance and reduced complexity through revisiting the hybrid precoding design with low-dimensional subspace perspective.

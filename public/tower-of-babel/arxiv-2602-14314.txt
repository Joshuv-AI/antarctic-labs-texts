The construction of q q -analogues via ϕ 2 3 {}_{3}\phi_{2} -series and q q -difference equations


Abstract
We apply the EKHAD -normalization method given in our recent work to obtain, via the q q -version of Zeilberger’s algorithm, q q -WZ pairs ( F , G ) (F,G) such that ∑ k = 0 ∞ F ⁡ ( 0 , k ) \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}F(0,k) may be expressed as a basic hypergeometric series of the form ϕ 2 3 {}_{3}\phi_{2} with multiple free parameters, and in such a way so that ∑ k = 0 ∞ F ⁡ ( 0 , k ) = ∑ n = 0 ∞ G ⁡ ( n , 0 ) \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}F(0,k)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}G(n,0) . In contrast to how previous applications of EKHAD -normalization relied on q q -analogues for specific WZ pairs introduced by Guillera, our multiparameter approach provides a broad framework in the construction of q q -analogues for accelerated series for universal constants such as π \pi . We apply this multiparameter version of EKHAD -normalization to obtain and prove new q q -analogues for accelerated hypergeometric series attributed to many authors, including (alphabetically) Adamchik and Wagon, Apéry, Chu, Chu and Zhang, Fabry, Guillera, Ramanujan, and Zeilberger.

2
Preliminaries and background

The
\Gamma
-function may be seen as among the most important special functions,
with reference to Rainville’s textbook on special functions
[
46
, §2]
,
and may be defined
via an Euler integral, with

\Gamma(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{x-1}e^{-t}\,dt

(1)

for
\Re(x)>0
. As a notational shorthand, we may write

\Gamma\!\left[\begin{matrix}\alpha,\beta,\ldots,\gamma\vskip 2.84526pt\\
A,B,\ldots,C\end{matrix}\right]=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)\cdots\Gamma(\gamma)}{\Gamma(A)\Gamma(B)\cdots\Gamma(C)}.

(2)

The
Pochhammer symbol
may then be defined so that

\left(a\right)_{n}=\Gamma\!\left[\begin{matrix}a+n\vskip 2.84526pt\\
a\end{matrix}\right],

(3)

and, by analogy with (
2
), we write

\left[\begin{matrix}\alpha,\beta,\ldots,\gamma\vskip 2.84526pt\\
A,B,\ldots,C\end{matrix}\right]_{k}=\frac{(\alpha)_{k}(\beta)_{k}\cdots(\gamma)_{k}}{(A)_{k}(B)_{k}\cdots(C)_{k}}.

We may define
generalized hypergeometric series
so that

{}_{r}F_{s}\!\!\left[\begin{matrix}a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{r}\vskip 2.84526pt\\
b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{s}\end{matrix}\ \Bigg|\ x\right]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left[\begin{matrix}a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{r}\vskip 2.84526pt\\
b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{s}\end{matrix}\right]_{n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!},

(4)

referring to Bailey’s classic text on such series for details and background material
[
8
]
. We refer to the value
x
in
(
4
) as the
convergence rate
of either side of (
4
).

The
q
-shifted factorial
, which is also referred to as the
q
-Pochhammer
symbol
, is such that

(a;q)_{n}=\begin{cases}1&n=0,\\
(1-a)(1-aq)\cdots(1-aq^{n-1})&n\in\mathbb{N}.\end{cases}

(5)

The definition in (
5
) is also extended so that

(a;q)_{\infty}=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}(1-aq^{k})

for
q
such that
|q|<1
, and this allows us to further extend the definition in (
5
) to
non-integer values of
n
according to the relation

(a;q)_{n}=\frac{(a;q)_{\infty}}{(aq^{n};q)_{\infty}},

and we make use of the notational shorthand such that

\left[\begin{matrix}\alpha,\beta,\ldots,\gamma\vskip 2.84526pt\\
A,B,\ldots,C\end{matrix}\,\Bigg|\,q\right]_{n}=\frac{\left(\alpha;q\right)_{n}\left(\beta;q\right)_{n}\cdots\left(\gamma;q\right)_{n}}{\left(A;q\right)_{n}\left(B;q\right)_{n}\cdots\left(C;q\right)_{n}}.

The
q
-Pochhammer symbol gives us a
q
-analogue of the function defined in (
3
)
in the sense that

\lim_{q\to 1}\frac{\left(q^{a};q\right)_{n}}{(1-q)^{n}}=(a)_{n}.

Similarly, the
q
-Gamma function may be defined so that

\Gamma_{q}(x)=(1-q)^{1-x}\left[\begin{matrix}q\vskip 2.84526pt\\
q^{x}\end{matrix}\,\Bigg|\,q\right]_{\infty},

for
|q|<1
, giving us a
q
-analogue of (
1
), with

\lim_{q\to 1}\Gamma_{q}(x)=\Gamma(x)
,
and we adopt a notational shorthand for combinations of
q
-Gamma expressions
by analogy with (
2
).
Also, the
q
-bracket

is defined so that
[n]_{q}=\frac{1-q^{n}}{1-q}
,
noting that
\lim_{q\to 1}[n]_{q}=n
.
By then defining the
q
-factorial
so that
[n]_{q}!=[1]_{q}[2]_{q}\cdots[n]_{q}
, we may define
Gaussian binomial coefficients

so that

\left[\begin{matrix}n\\
k\end{matrix}\right]_{q}=\frac{[n]_{q}!}{[k]_{q}![n-k]_{q}!}

for
k\leq n
.

Unilateral basic hypergeometric series
provide a
q
-analogue of (
4
) and may be defined so that

{}_{j}\phi_{k}\!\!\left[\begin{matrix}a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{j}\vskip 2.84526pt\\
b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{k}\end{matrix}\ \Bigg|\ q;z\right]\\
=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{(q;q)_{n}}\left[\begin{matrix}a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{j}\vskip 2.84526pt\\
b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{k}\end{matrix}\,\Bigg|\,q\right]_{n}\left((-1)^{n}q^{\binom{n}{2}}\right)^{1+k-j}.

Basic hypergeometric series of the form
{}_{3}\phi_{2}
are to play a key role in our work.
Examples of especially notable
{}_{3}\phi_{2}
-identities include
the relation

{}_{3}\phi_{2}\!\!\left[\begin{matrix}q^{-n},a,b\vskip 2.84526pt\\
c,\frac{abq^{1-n}}{c}\end{matrix}\ \Bigg|\ q;1\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{c}{a},\frac{c}{b}\vskip 2.84526pt\\
c,\frac{c}{ab}\end{matrix}\,\Bigg|\,q\right]_{n}

for integers
n\geq 0
attributed to Jackson
[
42
]
.
Notable instances of
{}_{3}\phi_{2}
-transforms include
the
q
-analogue

{}_{3}\phi_{2}\!\!\left[\begin{matrix}a,b,c\vskip 2.84526pt\\
d,e\end{matrix}\ \Bigg|\ q;q^{d+e-a-b-c}\right]=\\
\Gamma_{q}\!\left[\begin{matrix}e,d+e-a-b-c\vskip 2.84526pt\\
e-a,d+e-b-c\end{matrix}\right]{}_{3}\phi_{2}\!\!\left[\begin{matrix}a,d-b,d-c\vskip 2.84526pt\\
d,d+e-b-c\end{matrix}\ \Bigg|\ q;q^{e-a}\right]

of Thomae’s transformation
[
30
, p. 200]
, referring to Gasper and Rahman’s text

2.1
EKHAD
-normalization

A bivariate function
F(n,k)
is
hypergeometric
if
\frac{F(n+1,k)}{F(n,k)}
and
\frac{F(n,k+1)}{F(n,k)}
are both rational functions,
and the coefficients are taken to be in
\mathbb{Q}
in the context of WZ theory. For a rational function
R(n,k)
, write
G(n,k)=R(n,k)F(n,k)
, and suppose that
F
and
G
satisfy a difference equation of the form

F(n+1,k)-F(n,k)=G(n,k+1)-G(n,k).

(6)

This may be seen as the fundamental difference equation
in WZ theory, with reference to the classic text by
Petkovšek et al. on WZ theory
[
43
]
.
While a given function
F(n,k)
may not be such that there exists a companion
G(n,k)

of the desired form satisfying (
6
),

Zeilberger’s algorithm

[
43
, §6]

may be thought of as extending the WZ difference equation in (
6
)
in such a way so that this algorithm
is applicable to
any
hypergeometric input function
F(n,k)
,
referring to the same text by Petkovšek et al. for details.
Our techniques rely on the
q
-version of Zeilberger’s algorithm,
again referring to the appropriate text by Petkovšek et al. for details.

A
first-order
difference equation
produced by Zeilberger’s algorithm (resp. the
q
-Zeilberger algorithm)
with
F(n,k)
as a given input function, assuming that
F(n,k)

is such that a difference equation of the following form exists,
is such that

p_{1}(n)F(n+1,k)-p_{2}(n)F(n,k)=G(n,k+1)-G(n,k)

(7)

for (single-variable) polynomials

p_{1}(n)
and
p_{2}(n)
(resp.
q
-analogues).
Following our past work
[
17
]
, by “normalizing”
the input function
F(n,k)
by setting

\overline{F}(n,k)=(-1)^{n}\left(\prod_{i=j}^{n-1}\frac{p_{1}(i)}{p_{2}(i)}\right)F(n,k)

(8)

for minimal
j
such that
p_{2}
is nonvanishing,
typically with
j\in\{0,1\}
,
and by applying Zeilberger’s algorithm to the normalized version of
F
in (
8
),
we obtain a companion function

\overline{G}(n,k)
such that
\overline{F}
and
\overline{G}
satisfy the desired WZ difference equation,
and this may be verified inductively.
As in our past work
[
17
]
, we refer to the normalization process indicated above
as
EKHAD
-normalization
.

Our past work
[
18
]
concerned multiparameter versions of an acceleration method due to Wilf

[
55
]
.
In this past work
[
18
]
, by applying Zeilberger’s algorithm
to certain input functions such as

F(n,k):=\left[\begin{matrix}a,b\vskip 2.84526pt\\
n,n\end{matrix}\right]_{k},

(9)

this produced inhomogeneous, first-order difference equations that were
not

normalized, in accordance with Wilf’s method
[
55
]
.
Due to certain conver-gence-related issues, it appears that this approach
would not work, in general, be applicable using
q
-analogues of input function as in
(
9
),
and this leads us to apply
EKHAD
-normalization to
q
-analogues of
hypergeometric expressions as in (
9
),
and, instead of using Wilf’s acceleration method (which relies on inhomogeneous difference equations,
in contrast to (
6
)), we instead consider a telescoping argument
based on WZ difference equations.

For a
q
-analogue
\mathcal{F}(n,k;q)
of a hypergeometric input function as in
(
9
),
suppose that
\mathcal{F}(n,k;q)
satisfies a first-order difference equation according
to the
q
-Zeilberger algorithm.
By then applying
EKHAD
-normalization to
\mathcal{F}(n,k;q)
,
yielding a
q
-WZ pair
(\overline{\mathcal{F}},\overline{\mathcal{G}})
, a telescoping phenomenon gives us that

\overline{\mathcal{F}}(m+1,k;q)-\overline{\mathcal{F}}(0,k;q)=\sum_{n=0}^{m}\overline{\mathcal{G}}(n,k+1;q)-\sum_{n=0}^{m}\overline{\mathcal{G}}(n,k;q).

Depending on the parameters,
suppose that
\lim_{m\to\infty}\overline{\mathcal{F}}(n+1,k;q)
vanishes, with

-\overline{\mathcal{F}}(0,k;q)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\overline{\mathcal{G}}(n,k+1;q)-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\overline{\mathcal{G}}(n,k;q)

(10)

and

-\overline{\mathcal{F}}(0,k+1;q)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\overline{\mathcal{G}}(n,k+2;q)-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\overline{\mathcal{G}}(n,k+1;q)

(11)

and

-\overline{\mathcal{F}}(0,k+2;q)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\overline{\mathcal{G}}(n,k+3;q)-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\overline{\mathcal{G}}(n,k+2;q),

(12)

and so forth. Ideally, and again
depending on the input parameters,
we would apply a telescoping argument
suggested in
(
10
)–(
12
) together with
an analytic argument to prove the permissibility of an interchange of limiting operations of the form

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\overline{\mathcal{G}}(n,k+m;q)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\lim_{m\to\infty}\overline{\mathcal{G}}(n,k+m;q),

to prove that

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\overline{\mathcal{F}}(0,k;q)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\overline{\mathcal{G}}(n,0;q).

(13)

We apply this approach
to produce the motivating results highlighted in Section
3

along with many further results.
Through extensive computer searches for combinations of input parameters such that
the “non-
q
” version of (
13
)
admits a closed form,
this provides a versatile way of
generating new
q
-analogues, as demonstrated in Section

3
below.

Theorem 1

Proof.

For convenience, we write
\alpha=q^{a}

\beta=q^{b}

\gamma=q^{c}

5
Conclusion

Our multiparameter
EKHAD
-normalization method may be applied much more broadly, using
q
-analogues of hypergeometric
input functions as in our past work
[
15
]
. For brevity, and in view of the many new
q
-analogues of
previously published results from many authors highlighted
in Section
3
, we leave this, along with the research areas below, to separate projects.

We encourage the exploration of alternatives to and variants of our
q
-WZ-based techniques given above. For example, the

q
-WZ-based technique employed in our past work
[
14
]
to produce the final
q
-analogue of the
Fabry–Guillera formula listed in Section
3.2
is not equivalent to the
q
-WZ-based techniques given in our current
paper, noting that this previous
q
-WZ-based technique produces series with
q
-Pochhammer symbols indexed by
\infty

within the summands. For example, by adapting the approach from our past research on a
q
-version of the Fabry–Guillera formula

[
14
]
and by using a recent terminating
q
-binomial identity due to
Berkovich in 2024
[
11
]
, it can be shown that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n(n+1)}}{3^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{\left(-3q;q^{2}\right)_{\infty}\left(-\frac{q}{3};q^{2}\right)_{\infty}}-\frac{\left(q^{-2n+1};q^{2}\right)_{\infty}\left(q^{2n+3};q^{2}\right)_{\infty}}{\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{\infty}}\right)=\\
-6q\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{2n}}{(q^{2n+1}+3)(3q^{2n+1}+1)}\frac{\left(q^{2n+1};q^{2}\right)_{\infty}\left(q^{2n+3};q^{2}\right)_{\infty}}{\left(q^{4n+2};q^{2}\right)_{\infty}\left(-3q;q^{2}\right)_{n}\left(-\frac{q}{3};q^{2}\right)_{n}},

and this gives us a
q
-analogue of the
\pi
-formula

\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{3}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{3^{n}}{n\binom{2n}{n}}
.

How could
EKHAD
-normalization be applied in the context of combinatorial aspects of integer partitions and associated
q
-series?
Give a
q
-series identity associated with a combinatorial proof
of an identity on integer partitions, how could an “accelerated”
version of this
q
-series identity be obtained through
EKHAD
-normalization
or an analogue of this technique?

Research progress on the anti-tumor effects of euphorbia humifusa
Authors: Xiaoxue Zhao, Chengcheng Zhang, Xinle Qian, Jia Zhang, Guoying Wang, Zhongqi Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
EH (Euphorbia Humifusa) is commonly known as ground spurge, contains various compounds such as flavonoids, triterpenes, coumarins, sterols, tannins, and phenolic acids. It exerts a wide spectrum of properties including anti-bacterial, anti-inflammatory, Antioxidant, anti-viral, hypoglycemic, and anti-tumor. In this article, we focus on the antitumor effects of EH and its active constituents, providing evidence for further research on this medicinal herb.

Introduction

Malignant tumours are one of the main causes of human mortality and have a serious impact on today’s society and personal life, and their pathogenesis is complex and varied. Inflammation and oxidative stress in the peri-tumour microenvironment are considered to be one of the major causes of tumour disease progression. In recent years, although Western medicine has made remarkable progress in the treatment of malignant tumours, it has certain limitations because of its relatively single target, high cost and many clinical side effects. Therefore, more and more researchers have begun to pay attention to the therapeutic value of Chinese medicinal preparations in the prevention and treatment of tumour diseases.

EH(Euphorbia humifusa) was first recorded in Song dynasty’s “Jiayou Bencao.” It is a dried whole herb of Euphorbiaceae family, also known as ground spurge or spotted spurge. In the book “Jiayou Bencao,” it is described as a plant that grows near fields and is particularly superior in the Chuzhou area. The stems are slender and creep on the ground, red in color, with green-purple leaves. It grows vigorously in midsummer and blooms with red flowers in June, followed by small fruits. The plant is collected by taking the seedlings. EH has a pungent taste, a neutral property and flavor, and belongs to the lung, liver, stomach, large intestine, and bladder meridians. It exerts excellent anti-bacterial, anti-inflammatory, anti-oxidant, anti-viral, hypoglycemic, and anti-tumor properties (Fig. 1). EH is widely distributed with abundant resources, making it a frequently-used medicinal herb in China, best harvest in summer and autumn. Made by removing impurities, spraying with water, slightly moistening, cutting into sections, and drying through sun exposure [1]. Studies have shown that the main constituents of EH are flavonoids, triterpenes, coumarins, sterols, tannins, and phenolic acid compounds [2]. It contains a large number of anti-tumor compounds, including ellagic acid, suillin, arbutin, apigenin, luteolin, β-sitosterol, gallic acid, quercetin, and kaempferol, etc. [3]. EH, an important natural drug molecule, has been used alone or in combination for the treatment of a wide range of diseases, including malignant tumours. Recently, some researchers and scholars have found that EH can be anti antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, antiviral, antioxidant, haemostatic, hypoglycaemic, and anti-tumour (breast cancer, intestinal cancer, lung cancer, etc.), which has outstanding medicinal value in anti-tumour, such as: inhibition of proliferation of tumour cells, inhibition of angiogenesis, inducing apoptosis, cycle blockade, enhancement of the body's antioxidant capacity, enhancement of immunity and other effects. In this study we will focus on the research progress of Dictyostelium in anti-tumour in order to guide the clinical application.

Active constituents of EH

Flavonoids

Through the process of ultrasound-assisted reflux extraction and macroporous resin adsorption, Wang Shuping, Zhang Jianchao, and Jiang Chao [4–6] obtained total flavonoids from the aqueous extract of EH, including apigenin-7-O-glucoside, luteolin-7-O-glucoside, quercetin-3-O-arabinoside, quercetin, and kaempferol (Table 1). Wang Jinjun [7] used reversed-phase high-performance liquid chromatography to determine four flavonoid compounds in EH, which were similar to the aforementioned compounds. Cao Naifeng [8], isolated seven compounds from EH using silica gel column chromatography, including four flavonoids, three sterols, and triterpenes, identified as genistein, β-sitosterol, and palmitic acid. Sterols and triterpenes

Pei Yingge [10] separated the ethanol extract of EH using silica gel column chromatography (CC), preparative thin-layer chromatography (prep-TLC), and recrystallization, obtaining 35 compounds, including triterpenes, sterols, their glycosides, sesquiterpenes, (iso) coumarins, organic acids, glycerides, and long-chain alcohols. The structures of 28 of these compounds were identified, including 2 newly discovered compounds. Liu Runhui used silica gel column chromatography to isolate 5 compounds from the aqueous extract of EH, including β-sitosterol, 4 sterols and triterpenes, which were identified as genistein, cycloart-23E-en-3β, 25-diol, cycloart-23E-en-3β, 25-diol, cycloart-25-ene-3β, 24-diol, and 1-glycerinhexadecylate (Table 1).

Tannins and phenolic acids

Tian Ying [11] employed polyamide, macroporous resin, Sephadex LH-20, and MCI GEL CHP20P chromatographic methods to isolate 7 phenolic compounds from the ethanol extract of EH, identifying them as shortleaf suillin, shortleaf suillin acid, shortleaf suillin methyl ester, sanguisorbic acid dilactone, 3,3'-dimethoxyellagic acid, and tannic acid. Li Hui [12] used high-performance liquid chromatography and ultraviolet–visible spectrophotometry, separated phenolic acid components from EH, including gallic acid, tannic acid, and shortleaf suillin (Table 1).

Lactones and other chemical constituents

Hasierdun et al. [13] isolated lactones and coumarins, such as scopoletin, umbelliferone, trimaseranin, alkaloids, pyrrolidinones, unsaturated fatty acids, vitamins, inositols, palmitic acid-alpha-glyceride, (3R,6R,7E)-4,7-diene-3-hydroxy-9-violetone, 1-palmitoylglycerol, palmitic acid-alpha, alpha'-diglyceride, 1-palmitoyl-3-linoleoyl-sn-glycerol, and docosanol, from the ethyl acetate extract of EH (Table 1).

Anti-tumor Research of EH

Cervical cancer

Researchers [13] through the construction of cervical cancer U14 mouse solid tumour model, found that different dosage concentrations of mallow aqueous extract gavage can effectively inhibit the growth rate of solid tumours in mice, increase CAT, T-AOC and SOD in mice, enhance the antioxidant capacity of the body, reduce free radicals, and up-regulate the expression of the p19ARF gene to induce apoptosis and play an active role in the fight against cervical cancer. Similarly, by constructing the U14 cervical cancer mouse model, the researchers found that EH Flavonol Gavage can effectively activate Caspase-8, Caspase-3 and P16 apoptosis-related proteins in mice, induce cell cycle, down-regulate the expression of cyclin CyclinD1, and play the role of anti-cervical cancer activity [9]. By MTT method and flow cytometry, the effects of Dicentralex on the proliferation and apoptotic vesicles of Hela cells were detected, and it was found that it could inhibit the proliferation of Hela cells in vitro, and the apoptotic rate of cells reached 57.17% after the drug action, thus exerting the active effect of anti-cervical cancer (Fig. 2A).Fig. 2Antitumor effects mechanism of EH. Liver cancer

By constructing transplanted hepatocellular carcinoma H22 mice, researchers [14–16] found that Dictyostelium could inhibit tumour mass (P < 0.05), enhance serum SOD (P < 0.01), inhibit MDA (P < 0.01), reduce Bcl-2 protein expression, and at the same time, increase the expression of Bax, and Caspase-3 proteins (P < 0.05) in mice, and inhibit tumor progression by enhancing the antioxidant defence system It inhibited the growth of tumour cells, corrected the imbalance between apoptotic and anti-apoptotic proteins in the organism, activated the activity of Caspase-3 enzyme and induced apoptosis of tumour cells, and inhibited the progression of hepatocellular carcinoma.To explore its effect on VEGF and MMP-9 protein expression, it was found that Dictyostelium can inhibit the expression of VEGF and MMP-9 proteins in the tumour tissues of hormonal mice, thus reducing the tumour inhibitory angiogenesis and stromal degradation may be one of the partial mechanisms. Wang [14] found that in addition to inhibiting tumour growth, EH may inhibit the growth of tumour cells in transplanted hepatocellular carcinoma H22 mice by increasing the immunity of mice through increasing IL-2 levels, splenic index, and activation of T cells.Hu [17] further found that EH could significantly down-regulate the significant decrease of CD31 expression in tumour tissues of H22 hepatocellular carcinoma mice (P < 0.01), inhibit the protein expression of NF-κB, TNF-α, VEGF, and mRNA (P < 0.01), and ultimately inhibit the progression of hepatocellular carcinoma(Fig. 2B).

Colorectal cancer

Wu [18] successfully established an animal model of inflammation-associated colorectal cancer by constructing AOM/DSS, and gave different concentrations of EH gavage treatment, and found that EH could inhibit the protein expression levels of inflammation-associated genes IL-1b, IL-17, IL-6, and TLR4, and endoplasmic reticulum stress-associated genes ATF4, CHOP, and Xbp1s, and inhibit the expression of Tumour-related genes VEGF, PDGF-b, BCL2, MYC protein expression, the possible mechanism is that Dictyostelium regulate miR-17-5p-mediated STAT3 signalling pathway to exert anti-tumour effects. Fan Hongge [19], using human colon cancer cells SW480, found that the ethanol extract of Dicentrarchaea can down-regulate MMP2/9, modulate the expression of circRHOT1 targeting miR-29a-3p thereby regulating the circRHOT1/miR-29a-3p axis and inhibiting the proliferation, migration and invasion ability of colorectal cancer SW480 cells (Fig. 2C).

Anti-tumor research on major constituents of EH

Quercetin

Quercetin is a flavonoid compound derived from plants. Studies have shown that quercetin can upregulate Bax, caspase-3, and p21 to regulate cell apoptosis, while downregulating Akt, PLK-1, cyclin-B1, cyclin-A, CDC-2, CDK-2, and Bcl-2 to control cell apoptosis [20]. It can modulate p53, NF-κB, MAPK, JAK/STAT, PI3K/AKT, and Wnt/β-catenin pathways, inhibit the activity of ncRNAs, regulate the expression of miRNAs, and exhibit anticancer properties by inhibiting tumor proliferation, invasion, and metastasis [21] (Fig. 3A). Quercetin has been found to regulate ROS production and control tumor and cancer stemness [22]. It can also regulates oxidative stress, controls inflammation, induces immune suppressive factors, promotes immune escape of cancer cells, and prevents disease progression in pancreatic cancer [23]. It has been reported that quercetin increases the degradation of STAT3 protein in liver cancer cells and reduces STAT3 activation, thereby inhibiting the progression of liver cancer [20]. Quercetin can modulate the β-catenin signaling pathway, inhibit epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) in tumor cells, and suppress tumor metastasis, reducing metastasis in triple-negative breast cancer [24]. Ellagic acid

Ellagic acid is a polyphenolic compound with anti-oxidant, anti-inflammatory, and anti-tumor properties, and it helps overcome multidrug resistance in cancer by inhibiting epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) [26, 27]. Studies have shown that ellagic acid can inhibit the TGF-β signaling pathway by reducing the expression of Smad2, Smad3, and Smad4, thereby increasing gemcitabine (GCB) sensitivity and overcoming GCB resistance in bladder cancer [28] (Fig. 3B). Ellagic acid regulates cell proliferation and induces apoptosis by inhibiting cyclin-dependent kinase 6 (CDK6) and acts as an effective CDK6 inhibitor in breast cancer cells [29]. It inhibits mitochondrial respiration and tumor growth in lung cancer, activates AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK), reduces the expression of HIF-1α in lung cancer cells (Fig. 3B), and shows potential as a chemotherapy drug targeting mitochondrial metabolism in lung cancer [30].

Arbutin

Arbutin is a bioactive hydrophilic polyphenol compound that includes α-arbutin 4-hydroxyphenyl-α-D-glucopyranoside and β-arbutin 4-hydroxyphenyl-β-D-glucopyranoside and has been shown to be beneficial in treating tumor diseases [31]. Research has demonstrated that β-arbutin at IC50 concentrations induces apoptosis in MCF-7 human breast adenocarcinoma cells through p53 and Caspase 3 activation [32] (Fig. 3C). Arbutin generates excess ROS and disrupts mitochondrial membrane, inducing cell apoptosis, reducing inflammation and PI3K/mTOR signaling molecules, inhibiting cell adhesion characteristics of glioma cells, and exhibiting anticancer effects [33]. Additionally, it reduces the expression of IL-1β and TNF-α genes, providing a potential treatment for prostate cancer [34].

β-Sitosterol

β-Sitosterol is a major bioactive component found in plants and exhibits anti-tumor effects against fibrosarcoma, colon cancer, breast cancer, lung cancer, and prostate cancer [35, 36]. It inhibits tumor cell proliferation, migration, invasion, induces G0/G1 phase arrest and apoptosis, suppresses NF-kB activity, and downregulates epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) markers and AKT/GSK-3β signaling pathway (Fig. 3D), thereby exerting its anti-tumor effects [37]. It has been found that β-sitosterol induces apoptosis in ovarian cancer cells and inhibits their proliferation and cluster migration. This may be attributed to the loss of pro-apoptotic signals, mitochondrial membrane potential, increased reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, and calcium influx through the endoplasmic reticulum-mitochondria axis, leading to anti-tumor effects in ovarian cancer.

Gallic acid

Gallic acid is a hydrolyzable organic acid with plentiful pharmacological activities. It inhibits the growth of T24 human bladder cancer cells in a concentration and time-dependent manner, induces apoptosis, upregulates the expression of Cleaved Caspase-3, Bax, P53, and Cyt-c proteins, downregulates the expression of Bcl-3, P-PI65K, P-Akt, P-IκBα, P-IKKα, and P-NF-κB, and inhibits tumor cell proliferation, metastasis, and promotes cell apoptosis (Fig. 3E). This may be closely related to mitochondrial dysfunction and inhibition of PI3K/Akt/NF-κB signaling pathway [38]. Gallic acid can also induce apoptosis in HCC1806 cells through the mitochondrial apoptosis pathway, induce ROS generation, further inhibit the PI3K/AKT/EGFR pathway, and activate the MAPK signaling pathway [39]. It has been shown to inhibit colorectal cancer by regulating ferroptosis [40], and inducing apoptosis in breast cancer cells, exerting an anticancer effect [41]. Additionally, it has been proven to be effective in combination with paclitaxel for the treatment of cervical cancer [42].

Kaempferol

Kaempferol is a common natural compound belonging to the flavonoid class. It has been reported to exhibit various anticancer activities in breast cancer, prostate cancer, bladder cancer, cervical cancer, colon cancer, liver cancer, lung cancer, ovarian cancer, leukemia, and others [43, 44]. Kaempferol promotes autophagy and cell death [45], inhibits PKM5-mediated glycolysis to reverse 5-FU resistance in human colorectal cancer cells [46] (Fig. 3F), and reduces oxaliplatin resistance in colon cancer treatment [47]. Kaempferol shows great function as an adjuvant therapy for overcoming tumor resistance and has potential clinical applications.

Conclusion

Previous studies have shown that the main bioactive components of EH are flavonoids, sterols and tannins, which have anti-inflammatory, antibacterial, antioxidant and antitumour effects. However, the lack of research on the mechanism of treatment of malignant tumours, the complex pharmacological effects and the multi-targeted nature of the effects of EH and its active ingredients are the main reasons that limit the wide application of EH to clinical patients. Although researchers have done many in vitro and in vivo experiments to improve the bioavailability of EH, there are still fewer clinical trials in malignant tumours, especially in diseases such as lung, intestinal and liver cancer. In addition, it is not completely clear which components of EH have the main effects, so there is a need to further explore the monomers of the herb. Finally, multicentre, large-sample randomised controlled clinical trials are needed to further evaluate the safety and efficacy of EH in the treatment of malignant tumours.

Stability analysis through folds: An end-loaded elastica with a lever arm
Siva Prasad Chakri Dhanakoti

Abstract
Many physical systems can be modelled as parameter-dependent variational problems. The associated equilibria may or may not exist realistically and can only be determined after examining their stability. Hence, it is crucial to determine the stability and track their transitions. Generally, the stability characteristics of the equilibria change near folds in the parameter space. The direction of stability changes is embedded in a specific projection of the solutions, known as distinguished bifurcation diagrams. In this article, we identify such projections for variational problems characterized by fixed-free ends- a class of problems frequently encountered in mechanics. Using these diagrams, we study an Elastica subject to an end load applied through a rigid lever arm. Several instances of snap-back instability are reported, along with their dependence on system parameters through numerical examples. These findings have potential applications in the design of soft robot arms and other actuator designs.

Introduction

The study of physical problems using the variational framework has a rich history. In this setting, the equilibria are characterized as critical points of an energy-like functional. Typically, these problems are expressed in terms of a control parameter, generating a parameter family of equilibria. Indeed, the equilibria are computed by a slow scan of a parameter from a known solution using a continuation technique
[
1
]
. However, not all equilibria are stable or physically viable; they must correspond to the local minima of the functional. Hence, it is crucial to determine whether the critical points are local minima (or stable) and track their changes. Generally, the stability of parameter-dependent equilibria changes at singularities, such as a fold or bifurcation in the parameter space
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
. However, it remains unclear whether a fold in the unstable equilibrium results in a stable equilibrium or a higher-order unstable equilibrium. This direction of stability transition can be encoded through a particular projection of solutions, using distinguished bifurcation diagrams, and the stability can be assessed with a little observation and without any additional analysis. This idea of topologically exploiting the variational structure for stability examination was first employed in Astrophysics
[
5
,
6
]
. Distinguished bifurcation diagrams were first developed for finite-dimensional systems
[
7
]
and later generalized to infinite-dimensional systems
[
8
,
9
]
. Since then, several versions of these distinguished bifurcation diagrams have appeared in investigations of elastic problems
[
10
,
11
]
. In most studies, the diagrams are limited to cases with fixed-fixed ends or cases where the varying parameter appears within the integrand or as a Lagrange multiplier. In this study, we generalize the distinguished bifurcation diagrams to variational problems subject to fixed-free ends, when the bifurcation parameter appears in the boundary conditions. Consequently, two relevant cases arise: when the parameter appears at the fixed end and when it appears at the free end. For this analysis, we introduce the notion of an index that is equal to the number of negative eigenvalues of the associated second variation operator. The critical points with index zero are local minima and stable. Alternatively, the energy of the system can be used to determine the direction of stability
[
12
,
13
]
. The lower branch of the fold corresponds to the lower-energy state.

The developed methodology is applied to test the stability of elastica under an end load through a lever arm. Cantilever structures with a lever arm can be found everywhere, such as in plants carrying fruits or load-bearing flexible structures. In recent years, nonlinear rod models
[
14
]
have been actively employed in the fields of biophysics
[
15
]
, animations
[
16
]
and soft robot manipulators
[
17
,
18
]
. Elastica, a simple planar version of this rod model, has equally received attention. A flexible elastic structure subjected to loads or constraints may exhibit multiple equilibria. Hence, it is crucial to determine their stability, as only stable structures exist physically. Indeed, the presence of instability also hints at snap-back phenomena, a characteristic of multi-stable systems
[
19
]
. Numerous studies have been conducted on the stability of elastic rods, which involve either the Jacobi test
[
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
]
or Hessian eigenvalue determination
[
26
,
27
]
. In this article, we assess the stability without performing any such rigorous computations. But, instead, conclusions are drawn by qualitatively examining the plot of appropriate projection of the equilibria against the parameter.

Advances in material science produced highly deformable alloys and polymers, accelerating the development of soft robotics. Generally, soft robots employ very flexible structures to generate compliant mechanisms. A flexible elastic structure under load can exhibit multiple equilibria, where one equilibrium transitions to another through snap-back instability. These bistable or multi-stable systems exhibit large rapid displacements for small stimuli when operated around the regions of instabilities. As the system surpasses the initial energy barrier, it transitions to a new state, accompanied by a release of energy. Some of nature’s quickest mechanisms seen in Mantis shrimp
[
28
]
, Venus fly trap
[
29
]
, and Hummingbird beak
[
30
]
can be attributed to this snap-back instability. These mechanisms are widely used in the design of high-performance soft actuators
[
31
]
, energy harvesters, soft robots
[
32
]
, deployable and morphing structures
[
33
]
and haptic devices
[
34
]
. Inspired by these expanding applications, we aim to investigate elastica with a lever arm and the dependence of its stability on several parameters. These findings could offer invaluable insights for designing innovative mechanisms. Particularly, this study signifies the distinguished bifurcation diagrams for the analysis of multi-stable systems in engineering applications.

2.1
Problem Formulation

Let
\boldsymbol{\zeta}:s\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{p}
be a vector-valued function with a continuous derivative with respect to the independent parameter
s\in[0,l]
. Consider an unconstrained Calculus of Variations problem of the form

\displaystyle J(\boldsymbol{\zeta},\xi_{0},\xi_{l})=\int_{0}^{l}\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\zeta},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\prime},s)ds+B(\boldsymbol{\zeta},\xi_{l},l),

(1)

subject to a parameter-dependent
Dirichlet boundary condition
(or fixed boundary condition) at the end
s=0

\boldsymbol{\zeta}(0)=\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}(\xi_{0}),

(2)

while the other end
s=l
is held free. The scalar function
\mathcal{L}
has continuous second derivatives with respect to all its arguments and is convex in its second argument. On the other hand, the scalar function
B
has continuous second derivatives with respect to its first argument and has continuous first derivatives with respect to the other arguments. The system can be influenced by the parameters
\xi_{0}
and
\xi_{l}
at the boundaries
s=0
and
s=l
respectively. In the current study, we vary either
\xi_{0}
or
\xi_{l}
, but not both at a time. Maddocks
[
8
]
generated the bifurcation diagrams for problems when the parameter appears in the integrand and with homogeneous boundary conditions, which were later extended to the case when the parameter appears in the fixed end in a problem with non-homogeneous boundary conditions
[
9
]
. However, these studies are confined to problems with fixed-fixed ends. Here, we extend the analysis to the case where one of the fixed ends is set free. The critical points
\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}(s,\tau,\xi_{i})
,
i=0
or
l
, of (
1
) that are solutions to the well-known
Euler-Lagrange
equations

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\prime}}\right)^{\prime}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\zeta}}=\mathbf{0},\qquad\boldsymbol{\zeta}(0)=\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}(\xi_{0}),

(3a)

and the natural boundary condition at the free end
s=l

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\zeta}}+\frac{\partial B}{\partial\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\right)_{s=l}=\mathbf{0},

(3b)

exist as a continuous curve of solutions along a branch for some pseudo-arclength parameterization
\tau

[
1
]
satisfying the property

\displaystyle\big|\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}(s,\tau)\big|^{2}+\big|\dot{\xi_{i}}(\tau)\big|^{2}=1,\qquad i=o\text{ or }l.

The notation
\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}(s,\tau)
denotes the derivative with respect to pseudo-arclength
\tau
, whereas
{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{\prime}(s,\tau)
denotes the derivative with respect to independent parameter
s
. The second variation
\delta^{2}J
evaluated at the critical points
\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}(s,\tau,\xi_{i})
, (
i=0
or
l
) must satisfy the condition
[
35
]
:

\displaystyle\delta^{2}J(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o})[\mathbf{h}]=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{l}\left(\mathbf{h}^{\prime}\cdot\mathbf{P}\mathbf{h}^{\prime}+\mathbf{h}\cdot\mathbf{C}\mathbf{h}^{\prime}+\mathbf{h}^{\prime}\cdot\mathbf{C}^{T}\mathbf{h}+\mathbf{h}\cdot\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{h}\right)ds\geq 0,

(4)

so that they correspond to minima of the functional
J
, where
\mathbf{P},\mathbf{C}
and
\mathbf{Q}
are
p\times p
Hessian matrices evaluated at the extremal
\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}
given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{P}=\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o},\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}^{\prime},s),\quad\mathbf{C}=\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o},\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}^{\prime},s),\quad\mathbf{Q}=\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}\boldsymbol{\zeta}}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o},\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}^{\prime},s).\end{split}

(5)

For brevity, the dependence of
\mathbf{P},\mathbf{C}
and
\mathbf{Q}
on
s
is not explicitly shown. The matrices
\mathbf{P}
and
\mathbf{Q}
are symmetric, whereas the matrix
\mathbf{C}
may not be. Here,
\mathbf{h}
is a variation in the solution and satisfies the boundary conditions:

\displaystyle\mathbf{h}(0)=\mathbf{0},\qquad\mathbf{P}\mathbf{h}^{\prime}(l)+\mathbf{C}^{T}\mathbf{h}(l)+\mathbf{B}\mathbf{h}(l)=\mathbf{0},

(6)

where the symmetric matrix
\mathbf{B}
denotes
\frac{\partial^{2}B}{\partial\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{2}}
evaluated at
s=l
. The integrand in (
4
) has no explicit dependence on
\tau
and
\xi
, and the coefficient matrices depend on them through the solutions
\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}(s,\xi_{i})
and
\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}^{\prime}(s,\xi_{i})
. We also assume that the Legendre’s strengthened condition holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}>0,

(7)

i.e., the symmetric matrix
\mathbf{P}
is positive definite.
The application of integration by parts on (
4
) and the vanishing boundary terms leads to an alternate form of the second variation

\displaystyle\delta^{2}J[\mathbf{h}]=\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathcal{S}\mathbf{h},\mathbf{h}\rangle,

(8)

where
\mathcal{S}
is the second-order self-adjoint differential operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\mathbf{h}\equiv-\frac{d}{ds}\left(\mathbf{P}\mathbf{h}^{\prime}+\mathbf{C}^{T}\mathbf{h}\right)+\mathbf{C}\mathbf{h}^{\prime}+\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{h},

(9)

and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
represents the standard inner-product in
L^{2}
- space. The second order condition (
4
) can also be interpreted as a requirement that the following linear eigenvalue problem

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{S}\mathbf{h}&=\mu\mathbf{h},\\
\mathbf{h}(0)&=\mathbf{0},\quad\mathbf{P}\mathbf{h}^{\prime}(l)+\mathbf{C}^{T}\mathbf{h}(l)+\mathbf{B}\mathbf{h}(l)=\mathbf{0}.\end{split}

(10)

has only non-negative eigenvalues. The operator
\mathcal{S}
is assumed to have eigenvalues and eigenfunctions that depend smoothly on pseudo-arclength
\tau
. Along the eigenvector
\mathbf{h}
, the second variation functional takes the form

\displaystyle\delta^{2}J[\mathbf{h}]=\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathcal{S}\mathbf{h},\mathbf{h}\rangle=\frac{\mu}{2}\langle\mathbf{h},\mathbf{h}\rangle.

(11)

We extend the standard notation of Morse index
[
36
]
to these problems here, where it is equal to the number of negative eigenvalues of this eigenvalue problem and denotes the dimension of the vector subspace
\mathbf{h}
over which the second variation is negative. Subsequently, index zero solutions satisfy the necessary condition for stability (
4
) and correspond to stable solutions. The index for the case of fixed-fixed ends is shown to be equal to the number of conjugate points (for more details refer
[
20
]
). However, we do not pursue our analysis in that direction. Instead, we track changes in the index at critical points.

Parameter-dependent variational problems generate a family of extremals, and in the case of a single parameter
\xi
, a curve of solutions is obtained. Sometimes, these solutions are characterized by
folds
- points at which the curve slopes vertically. In bifurcation terminology, these points are also referred to as
saddle-node bifurcation
or
turning points
. On differentiating the
Euler-Lagrange
equations (
3a
) and the non-linear boundary conditions with respect to pseudo-arclength
\tau
, we obtain the following expressions for the two cases of varying parameters

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{0},\qquad\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}(0)=\frac{d\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}}{d\xi}\dot{\xi}_{0},\quad\mathbf{P}\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\prime}}(l)+\mathbf{C}^{T}\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}(l)+\mathbf{B}\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}(l)=\mathbf{0},

(12a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{0},\qquad\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}(0)=\mathbf{0},\quad\qquad\mathbf{P}\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\prime}}(l)+\mathbf{C}^{T}\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}(l)+\mathbf{B}\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}(l)+\frac{\partial^{2}B}{\partial\boldsymbol{\zeta}\partial\xi_{l}}\dot{\xi}_{l}=\mathbf{0}.

(12b)

Both of these boundary value problems correspond to the eigenvalue problem (
10
) with zero eigenvalue whenever
\dot{\xi}_{0}=0
or
\dot{\xi}_{l}=0
. We focus on the behavior of the motion of the eigenvalues around these special points - folds. A
simple fold
further satisfies:

\displaystyle\ddot{\xi}_{i}\neq 0,\qquad i=0\text{ or }l

(13a)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}J_{\xi_{i}}\neq 0\qquad i=0\text{ or }l,

(13b)

\displaystyle\text{and }\mu=0\text{ is a simple eigenvalue of }\mathcal{S}.

(13c)

An eigenvalue is called a simple eigenvalue if its algebraic multiplicity is one. Let
\mathbf{h}
denote the eigenvector corresponding to the eigenvalue
\mu
. On taking the inner product of (
12
) with the eigenvector
\mathbf{h}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{S}\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}},\mathbf{h}\rangle=0.

(14)

Integrating by parts fetches

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{S}\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}},\mathbf{h}\rangle=\langle\mathcal{S}\mathbf{h},\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\rangle-\left[\left(\mathbf{P}\mathbf{h}^{\prime}+\mathbf{C}^{T}\mathbf{h}+\mathbf{B}\mathbf{h}\right)\cdot\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\right]_{0}^{l}+\left[\left(\mathbf{P}\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{\prime}+\mathbf{C}^{T}\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}+\mathbf{B}\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\right)\cdot\mathbf{h}\right]_{0}^{l}=0.

(15)

From this point, we separate the analyses for the cases of varying parameter at the fixed end and at the free end. The key aspect of the analysis is that, in the vicinity of a fold, an elementary expression can be found for the derivative of the critical eigenvalue
\dot{\mu}
.

2.2
Varying Parameter at the Fixed-end

For the case of parameter at the fixed-end (
12
), the expression (
15
) becomes

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{S}\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}},\mathbf{h}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{S}\mathbf{h},\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\rangle-\left(\mathbf{P}\mathbf{h}^{\prime}+\mathbf{C}^{T}\mathbf{h}+\mathbf{B}\mathbf{h}\right)\cdot\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\bigg|_{0}=0

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{S}\mathbf{h},\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\rangle-\mathbf{P}\mathbf{h}^{\prime}(0)\cdot\frac{d\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}}{d\xi}\dot{\xi}_{0}=0.

As
\mathbf{h}
is the eigenvector of
\mathcal{S}
operator with eigenvalue
\mu
, we can rewrite it as

\displaystyle\mu\langle\mathbf{h},\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\rangle-\mathbf{P}\mathbf{h}^{\prime}(0)\cdot\frac{d\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}}{d\xi}\dot{\xi}_{0}=0

(16)

along the branch. At a fold point
\mu=0
and
\dot{\xi}=0
. However, the eigenvalue
\mu
changes its sign as a fold point is traversed, and
\dot{\mu}\left(\equiv\frac{\partial\mu}{\partial\tau}\right)
gives the direction of change. The expression for
\dot{\mu}
is obtained by differentiating the eigenvalue problem (
10
) with respect to pseudo-arclength
\tau
as

\displaystyle\dot{\mu}\langle\mathbf{h},\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\rangle+\mu\frac{d}{d\tau}\langle\mathbf{h},\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\rangle-\frac{d}{d\tau}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{h}^{\prime}(0)\cdot\frac{d\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}}{d\xi}\dot{\xi}_{0}-\mathbf{P}\mathbf{h}^{\prime}(0)\cdot\frac{d\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}}{d\xi}\dot{\xi}_{0}^{2}-\mathbf{P}\mathbf{h}^{\prime}(0)\cdot\frac{d\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}}{d\xi}\ddot{\xi}_{0}=0.

(17)

On substituting
\mu=0
,
\dot{\xi}_{o}=0
,
\mathbf{h}=\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}
at a fold, we obtain

\displaystyle\dot{\mu}\langle\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}},\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\rangle=\mathbf{P}\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{\prime}(0)\cdot\frac{d\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}}{d\xi}\ddot{\xi}_{0}=\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\prime}}\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{\prime}(0)\cdot\frac{d\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}}{d\xi}\ddot{\xi}_{0}

(18)

For the function
\mathbf{P}=\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\prime}}
, which is a symmetric matrix, we can write

\displaystyle\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{\prime}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\prime}}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}}{\partial\xi}\Bigg|_{s=0}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{\prime}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\prime}}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}}{\partial\xi}+\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\prime}}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}}{\partial\xi}\dot{\xi}\cdot\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}}{\partial\xi}\bigg)\Bigg|_{s=0},

\displaystyle=\bigg(\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\prime}}\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{\prime}\cdot\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}}{\partial\xi}+\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\prime}}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}}{\partial\xi}\dot{\xi}\cdot\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}}{\partial\xi}\bigg)\Bigg|_{s=0}=\frac{d}{d\tau}\bigg(\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\prime}}\cdot\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}}{\partial\xi}\bigg)\Bigg|_{s=0}.

Then the expression for the
\dot{\mu}
becomes

\displaystyle\dot{\mu}=\ddot{\xi}\frac{d}{d\tau}\bigg(\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\prime}}\cdot\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}}{\partial\xi}\bigg)\Bigg|_{s=0}

(19)

For a simple fold the terms
\dot{\mu}
and
\ddot{\xi}
are non-zero, and (
19
) provides the direction in which the eigenvalue crosses zero. The sign of
\ddot{\xi}
indicates the direction in which the fold opens. A positive
\ddot{\xi}
represents the fold opening towards the left, while a negative
\ddot{\xi}
represents the fold opening towards the right. The distinguished bifurcation diagram is defined as the plot of
\left(\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\prime}}\cdot\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}}{\partial\xi}\right)\Bigg|_{s=0}
vs.
\xi
along the branches of critical points. As a simple fold is traversed, the critical eigenvalue
\mu
changes its sign dictated by the signs of
\ddot{\xi}
and
\left(\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\prime}}\cdot\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}}{\partial\xi}\right)\Bigg|_{s=0}
. Therefore, the index, which is the number of negative eigenvalues, alters by one at each fold, and the direction of change can be found by examining the shape of the projection of the solution branch as illustrated in Figure
1(a)
. Indeed, the expression for the ordinate is the same as in the case of fixed-fixed ends

]
, where the varying parameter appears at the end

.

2.3
Varying Parameter at the Free-end

Now consider the other case, where the bifurcation parameter appears in the free end
s=l
. In this case, the expression (
15
) becomes

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{S}\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}},\mathbf{h}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{S}\mathbf{h},\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\rangle+\left(\mathbf{P}\mathbf{h}^{\prime}+\mathbf{C}^{T}\mathbf{h}+\mathbf{B}\mathbf{h}\right)\cdot\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\Bigg|_{s=l}=0

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{S}\mathbf{h},\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\rangle-\mathbf{h}(l)\cdot\frac{\partial^{2}B}{\partial\boldsymbol{\zeta}\partial\xi}\dot{\xi}_{l}=0.

which can be written in terms of eigenvalue
\mu
and eigenvector
\mathbf{h}
of the operator
\mathcal{S}
as:

\displaystyle\mu\langle\mathbf{h},\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\rangle-\mathbf{h}(l)\cdot\frac{\partial^{2}B}{\partial\boldsymbol{\zeta}\partial\xi}\dot{\xi}_{l}=0.

At a fold
\dot{\xi}_{l}=0
and
\mu=0
. The expression for
\dot{\mu}
is obtained by differentiating the eigenvalue problem (
10
) with respect to pseudo-arclength
\tau
as

\displaystyle\dot{\mu}\langle\mathbf{h},\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\rangle+\mu\frac{d}{d\tau}\langle\mathbf{h},\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\rangle=\dot{\mathbf{h}}(l)\cdot\frac{\partial^{2}B}{\partial\boldsymbol{\zeta}\partial\xi}\dot{\xi}_{l}+\mathbf{h}(l)\cdot\frac{\partial^{3}B}{\partial\boldsymbol{\zeta}\partial\xi}\dot{\xi}_{l}^{2}+\mathbf{h}(l)\cdot\frac{\partial^{2}B}{\partial\boldsymbol{\zeta}\partial\xi}\ddot{\xi}_{l}

The boundary term

vanishes at

(21)

In this case, as a simple fold is traversed, the projection

provides information on changes in the sign of the critical eigenvalue. Consequently, the change in index is determined by the shape of

2.4
Non-Simple Folds

For the sake of completeness, we also consider the results when the non-degeneracy conditions of a simple fold are relaxed. Suppose that
\mu=0
is not a simple eigenvalue at the fold, and has multiplicity
N
. Let
\{\mathbf{q}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be the eigenvectors corresponding to the zero eigenvalue
\mu
. Then, near the fold, the nullspace
\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}
can be written as
\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}\mathbf{q}_{i}
. There are
N
branches of eigenvalues
\mu_{i}(\tau),i=1,\dots,N
satisfying
\mu(\tau^{*})=0
. The expression for
\dot{\mu}_{i}
is obtained when (
17
) is evaluated at a fold
(\mu_{i}=0,\dot{\xi}=0,\mathbf{h}=\mathbf{q}_{i})

\displaystyle\dot{\mu}_{i}\langle\mathbf{q}_{i},\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\rangle=\ddot{\xi}_{0}\mathbf{P}\dot{\mathbf{q}}_{i}^{\prime}(0)\cdot\frac{d\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}}{d\xi}.

(22)

Multiplying by
c_{i}
and summing from
i=1
to
i=N
, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\dot{\mu}_{i}c_{i}\mathbf{q}_{i},\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\rangle=\ddot{\xi}_{0}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{P}\dot{\mathbf{q}}_{i}^{\prime}(0)\cdot\frac{d\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}}{d\xi}=\ddot{\xi}_{0}\mathbf{P}\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{\prime}\cdot\frac{d\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}}{d\xi}.

(23)

Substituting
\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}\mathbf{q}_{i}
and using orthonormality of the eigenvectors
\mathbf{q}_{i}
results in

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}^{2}\dot{\mu}_{i}=\mathbf{P}\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}^{\prime}(0)\cdot\frac{d\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}}{d\xi}\ddot{\xi}_{0}=\ddot{\xi}\frac{d}{d\tau}\bigg(\mathcal{L}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\prime}}\cdot\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{o}}{\partial\xi}\bigg)\Bigg|_{s=0}.

(24)

Hence, we have information regarding the linear combination of all
N
derivatives of
\dot{\mu}_{i}
. However, only one of the eigenvalues changes the sign at the fold point. Similarly, we can prove the same when the parameter appears in the free end
s=l
.

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}^{2}\dot{\mu}_{i}=-\ddot{\xi}B_{\zeta\xi}\cdot\dot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\bigg|_{s=l}=-\ddot{\xi}\bigg(\frac{d}{d\tau}B_{\xi}\bigg).

(25)

To test the developed theory in section

5
Conclusion

We extended the theory of distinguished bifurcation diagrams to the problems characterized by fixed-free ends to analyze their stability. Using a combination of bifurcation diagrams and fold information, we analyzed the stability of elastica subjected to an end load through a rigid lever arm. The stability is determined through a qualitative examination of the plots, without performing a rigorous analysis such as conjugate point computations or eigenvalue determination. Nevertheless, the stability index of at least one solution along a branch must be established, which was determined using bifurcation analysis. Despite their simplicity and relative abundance, elastica systems with a lever arm have received limited attention. We presented several instances of multi-stability arising in these systems by tuning system parameters. Bi-stability or multi-stability is extensively investigated for engineering applications such as microfluidic devices
[
39
]
, soft robots
[
40
,
41
]
and MEMS devices
[
42
,

]
. The proposed distinguished bifurcation diagrams may serve as invaluable tool in their design. Many modern functional materials are being developed, and electric, magnetic, light, thermal, or water/solvent stimuli can be employed to alter system parameters near the region of instability, actuating it
[

]
. A natural extension of this work would be to apply these methods to three-dimensional elastic rods using the Kirchhoff rod theory
[

.

We restricted our analysis to parameters that produce equilibria with a maximum index of
2
. Future work could explore parameters that generate higher-index equilibria. For example, larger values of
P
would generate equilibria with higher indices. Another key aspect of the current findings is the system’s periodic nature with respect to the rotation parameters. In contrast to problems with fixed-fixed ends in
[

]
, the present system exhibited periodicity with respect to the rotation parameters when one of its fixed ends is set free. The free end allows for more freedom, limiting the possibility of configurations with higher stored elastic energy. But, increasing the parameters, such as
P
allows their formation.

In this study, we presented the equilibria resulting from the quasi-static control of parameters and drew conclusions on the equilibria after snap-back instability without detailing the transition path. In some instances, multiple stable equilibria are possible after the snap-back instability, and exact equilibrium to which the system transitions cannot be concluded. In this case, the study of dynamical aspects of snapping would be more advantageous

Incorporating isoperimetric constraints, where the position of the free end is fixed, while it is free to rotate, would lead to interesting scenarios. It has already been proved that these constraints would have no effect on the ordinate of bifurcation diagrams for the case of fixed-fixed ends

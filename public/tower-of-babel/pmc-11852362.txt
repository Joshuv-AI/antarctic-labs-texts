Multiple Serratus- and High-Oblique LipoTransfer Guided by Ultrasound (Multi-SHOT): Cadaveric and Clinical Study
Authors: Alfredo E Hoyos, Ricardo Proto, Mauricio E Perez Pachon, Mariana Borras Osorio, Maria Paula Castiblanco
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBody contouring procedures are now increasingly popular among male patients who desire a lean and athletic appearance. Dynamic Definition Liposculpture (HD2) employs a comprehensive, 360° approach to achieve natural-looking results. Several authors have highlighted the efficacy and safety of fat grafting techniques in body contouring, which are strategically applied to enhance muscle definition.ObjectivesThe authors are reporting their experience with ultrasound-guided intramuscular fat grafting to the serratus anterior muscles and the superior third of the internal and external oblique muscles (Multi-SHOT) in addition to high-definition liposculpture.MethodsThe authors conducted a retrospective cohort study across 2 specialized plastic surgery centers. This study included adult male patients with ectomorph and mesomorph body biotypes who underwent Multi-SHOT in addition to HD2. Descriptive statistical analysis was used to summarize demographic, surgical, and clinical variables. Given the sample size, high satisfaction rates, and low complication rates, no bivariate analysis was performed.ResultsA total of 81 consecutive male patients underwent high-definition liposculpture combined with the Multi-SHOT technique. The mean age was 40.27 years (SD = 8.34 years), and the mean BMI was 26.79 kg/m2 (standard deviation = 2.47 kg/m2). Additional fat grafting of trunk muscles was performed to enhance the aesthetic outcomes. No complications associated with the technique were reported.ConclusionsThe findings provide valuable insights about the safety and efficacy of the Multi-SHOT technique to enhance the athletic and muscular appearance of the male anterior torso. Further studies with greater sample size are required to support these findings.Level of Evidence: 3 (Therapeutic)

Although body contouring procedures have been primarily considered for the female population, recent years have witnessed a growing popularity and use among males. Contrary to the traditional ideal body structure sought in females, aesthetic outcomes that males usually look for are more prone toward athletic, with leanness and emphasis on a moderate muscular structure. The isolated definition of specific muscle groups results in unnatural-looking outcomes. Therefore, dynamic definition (HD2) liposculpture described by Hoyos et al has evolved in the past years adopting the fundamental principle of involving a 360° approach and their own body structure, rather than producing fake muscles.1 This translates to a requirement for a comprehensive strategy for torso definition, including the chest, abdomen, and back. Another advancement regarding the artistic anatomy and surgical outcomes is the combination with other techniques like ultrasound (US)-guided fat grafts to improve muscle definition in specific areas, consistent with the individual's body structure and expectations.

One singular patient's body composition, including muscle and fat mass, age, skin laxity, and history of previous surgeries, among other crucial variables, must be thoroughly analyzed for the procedure selection and planning. In 2021, the algorithm (BMX algorithm) to classify patients according to their body biotype and plan their subsequent degree of muscular definition changed the way numerous surgeons thought about high-definition liposculpture.2 Surgeries had to fit individual characteristics, aiming to achieve harmonious and natural-looking outcomes, emphasizing the importance of personalized treatment. Similarly, several studies have demonstrated consistently how fat grafting contributes to improved long-lasting and harmonized aesthetic outcomes and has a favorable safety profile. In addition to these findings, autologous fat grafts have been considered an ideal filler because of their adaptability, biocompatibility, and easy availability during body contouring procedures.3 In 2016, Steinbrech and Sinno published the first description of subcutaneous fat grafting under direct observation, to create an athletic and muscular “6-pack” appearance in the abdomen.4 Similarly, Danilla used intramuscular fat grafting of the rectus abdominis to achieve better and more natural results for patients with skin laxity undergoing lipoabdominoplasty.5 In 2020, Viaro et al reported the use of US guidance to direct the rectus abdominis muscle fat grafting in patients undergoing high-definition liposculpture, also enabling its use in patients without skin laxity.6 Furthermore, other groups explored techniques in other muscles of the torso, including (but not limited to) the erector spinae,7 pectoralis major,8 trapezius, and latissimus dorsi.9 All authors described excellent aesthetic outcomes, with a good safety profile of their techniques. In this study, we outline the use of US-guided intramuscular fat grafting of the serratus anterior muscles (in individual bundle shots), and the superior third of the internal and external oblique muscle, associated with HD2 Liposculpture in male patients. The aim of this procedure is to provide the patient with a more athletic appearance that looks natural during dynamic movements and in various body postures, consistent with the typical male anatomical structure and aesthetic ideals.

Artistic Anatomy

The external and internal oblique muscles are crucial elements of the anterolateral abdominal wall, essential for the support, stabilization, and movement of the torso. The external oblique muscle is the largest and most superficial, originating from the lower ribs, with its fibers following an oblique path, inserting in the Linea alba, pubic crest, and anterior iliac crest. Beneath it lies the internal oblique muscle, originating from the dorsolumbar fascia, iliopsoas fascia, and iliac crest, also having an oblique path, inserting in the cartilage of the lower ribs and Linea alba. The serratus anterior muscle is a fan-shaped with digital indentations muscle located on the lateral part of the torso, originating from the upper ribs, inserted in the medial border of the anterior surface of the scapula. It has a pivotal role in scapular stabilization and movement (protraction and rotation), while also contributing to the athletic and muscular definition of the male torso (Figure 1).

Skin representation of the muscles of the anterior torso. This case presents a 34-year-old male model with exceptionally defined muscles in the anterior torso. Despite a BMI of 25 kg/m², his body fat percentage is ∼10% only. Notably, in the lateral area of the torso, the indentations of the Serratus anterior muscle (SA) align perfectly with the external oblique insertions (EO) along the ribcage. Additionally, the Latissimus dorsi (LD) muscle extends over the serratus, concealing its bulk underneath. Another interesting aspect is how the oblique muscle connects to the Rectus abdominis muscle (RA) sheath, forming the Linea semilunaris.

METHODS

Study Design and Population

Cadaveric Dissections

We performed cadaveric dissections of the anterior torso to support the safety and reliability of intramuscular (IM) fat grafting in these muscles. Pectoralis minor and major, Serratus muscles, external and internal oblique muscles, Latissimus dorsi (anterior portion), and rectus abdominis muscles of 3 different specimens were all dissected to identify the main pedicles, their distribution within the muscle, the presence of risk/safe compartments, and their overall anatomic features (mass, volume, and distribution). Scalpels with #15/#20 blades, tissue scissors, and forceps were utilized to separate the anatomical layers. Vascular retractors and markers delineated the main pedicles and vessels. Initially, we located the pedicle, followed by a lipoinjection test using our standard blinded approach with HD2 methylene blue to confirm proper placement in the IM layer of the arm's muscles (Video 1).

Clinical Study

We conducted a retrospective cohort study, from October 2018 to April 2024, across 3 specialized plastic surgery centers (Bogota and Brazil), with 2 experienced aesthetic and reconstructive plastic surgeons, trained in HD liposculpture and IM US-guided fat grafting. The study included adult male patients between 18 and 60 years, with a body structure classified as ectomorph or mesomorph, considered to be candidates for moderate and extreme muscular definition procedures, who had undergone intramuscular US-guided fat grafting of the Serratus anterior and high external oblique muscles, along with HD2 liposculpture. Patients were excluded from the study if they had short follow-up (<1 month), or incomplete clinical or surgical data for analysis. As part of the outcome's evaluation, all patients were asked to complete a nonstandardized satisfaction scale between months third and sixth after the surgery.

Ethical Considerations

All objectives, procedures, sources of funding, as well as potential conflicts of interest of the authors were explained to all the patients. The technique's innovation, anticipated advantages, possible risks, complications, and discomfort associated with fat grafting, and HD2 liposculpture were also explained to the patients. All patients decided whether or not to have specific muscle fat grafted, and the procedure was planned according to the patient's expectations and different degrees of muscle definition. All enrolled patients freely signed informed consent for the procedure and the use of photographs and information for academic and research purposes. Because of the retrospective and observational nature of the study, it was considered minimal risk. The study was conducted following the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, the guidelines of good clinical practice of the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH), the regulations and laws on healthcare research and private information management.

Data Collection

Demographics, procedure type, surgical duration, injection site, injected fat graft volume, complications, and patient satisfaction scores were collected for analysis. For accuracy, data were collected retrospectively using a standard spreadsheet at each site by a nurse blinded to the study objectives and trained in data collection. The data were extracted from clinical charts and surgery notes.

Statistical Analysis

Univariate analysis was used to summarize all the variables collected. For qualitative variables, the percentages and frequencies (absolute and relative) were calculated. For the quantitative variables, the Shapiro–Wilk test was initially used to evaluate the normal distribution. The quantitative variables with normal distribution were summarized using mean and standard deviation, and for the non-normal distribution variables, median and interquartile range (IQR) were calculated. Because of the sample size, the low rate of complications, and the relatively high satisfaction scores, bivariate statistical analysis was considered impractical to find significant differences. Statistical analysis was conducted using Jamovi software (Version 2.3).

Surgical Technique

A thorough physical examination, including measures of body diameters and osteomuscular structure, was performed as part of the preoperative assessment. This examination was used to plan the surgery, evaluate overall risks, and provide the patients with a thorough description of the process and its possible aesthetic results. Following institutional protocols, all patients received general anesthesia, administration of tranexamic acid (1 g IV prior to incision), prevention measures for thrombotic events and hypothermia, prophylactic antibiotic administered (Cefazolin or Clindamycin), as well as Metoclopramide, Ranitidine, Dexamethasone, and Diclofenac.

Markings are done with the patient in the standing position, the lateral border of the pectoralis muscle and the Serratus anterior muscle are marked by palpation, with specific emphasis on the indentations of the muscle and the superior portion of the external oblique muscle (Figure 2). High-definition liposculpture is done through a 3-step approach, including infiltration, emulsification, and extraction. Tumescent solution is prepared in a 1:1000 ratio (1000 mL of normal saline + 1 mL of epinephrine) for the infiltration. The ratio of infiltration/lipoaspirate is around 1.5:1. We wait for ∼5 min to allow the even distribution of the solution and its vasoconstrictive effect to happen. Then, we use third-generation US (VASER Lipo System, Solta Medical, Bothell, WA) in pulsed and continuous modes for fat emulsification. Liposculpture is performed using 3.0 and 4.0 mm Mercedes Cannulas connected to Microaire System (MicroAire Surgical Instruments, Charlottesville, VA), beginning deep and progressing superficially. Using a sterile bottle trap closed system, the extracted fat tissue is harvested and processed through active decantation (VTS Vibratory Tissue Separator, Wells Johnson Company, Tucson, AZ) to separate and collect the viable adipose cells. Subsequently, obtained cells are placed in 20 and 10 mL sterile syringes for subsequent use in fat grafts.

Fat Grafting Procedure

RT US mapping facilitated the specific location of the individual fascicles of the Serratus anterior muscle, the ribs, the limits of the external and internal oblique muscles, and the rectus abdominis muscle (L15 handheld transducer, Clarius, Vancouver, BC, Canada). Fat is purified using a 2.0 mm Luer-lock adipose graft filter (Figure 3). RT-US imaging to locate the center of the muscle bellies. Then, fat grafting to the external oblique and the Serratus anterior muscles is done by using a puncture incision at a 30° angle, with a 12 G needle (or a 1.4 mm lipograft cannula) connected to either 10 or 20 mL syringes with purified fat grafts. Grafts are placed individually at the serratus anterior muscle bellies, above the rib (to improve support), and away from the proximal pedicle (for safety). Fat grafting to the high external oblique muscle is done also by superficial individual boluses (Video 2). The rectus abdominis muscle is grafted using 3.0 mm cannulas, ranging from 20 to 30 cm in length, connected to 20 mL syringes. This procedure is also guided by RT-US by placing small intramuscular boluses on each muscle belly. Additionally, the internal oblique muscle can be grafted to enhance the masculinization of the abdomen (Video 3). Overall, this technique will enhance the shadow next to the Linea semilunaris in the upper abdomen, therefore improving the athletic definition of both the serratus anterior and the rectus abdominis muscle. The authors named this procedure Multi-SHOT: Multiple Serratus- and High-Oblique LipoTransfer guided by US (Figure 4). The patient is dressed up in postoperative foam vest and garments before transferring the patient to the recovery room.

This is a 29-year-old male patient who underwent HD2 plus Multi-SHOT with 15 cc on each of his 6 Serratus bellies in addition to 50 cc of fat graft of the external oblique muscle. He also underwent fat grafting of the pectorals, deltoids, biceps, triceps, and lat dorsi. Preoperative photographs (A-C) show a bulky abdomen with a distortion of its facets and lack of projection of the iliac crests. Six-month postoperative photographs (D-F) show a new athletic definition of the abdomen with a stylish definition of the anterior superior iliac spines that improve the muscular appearance of the lower belly.

All patients were followed up at 48 h and 1 week through in-person appointments. Then, virtual follow-up was conducted from the first month until 6 to 12 months postoperative. Photographs were taken by a trained photographer during every appointment.

RESULTS

A total of 81 consecutive male patients underwent HD and Multi-SHOT technique in the institutions and were included for analysis in this study. The mean age was 40.27 years (±8.34 years), the mean weight was 83.46 kg (±10.71 kg), the mean height was 176.24 cm (±6.12 cm), and the mean BMI was 26.79 kg/m2 (±2.47 kg/m2). Most of the patients were Hispanic (80.6%) and had no significant medical history. Only a few had notable conditions: 1 patient had hypertension, another had hypothyroidism, 1 had keloid formation, and another reported rheumatoid arthritis. For most patients (71.6%), this procedure corresponded to their first aesthetic surgery, and 5 patients reported 3 or more previous surgeries. Table 1 describes in detail the demographic and clinical characteristics of the patients. The mean surgery duration was 243.36 min (±55.64 min). Most patients underwent fat grafts in additional anatomical locations, the pectorals being the most frequent one (96.3%), followed by the buttocks (65.4%) and the rectus abdominis muscle (65.4%).

DISCUSSION

Recent advancements in body contouring techniques, like US-guided intramuscular fat grafting, are opening up new possibilities for male patients, especially those with ectomorph and mesomorph body types. More males are now seeking these procedures to achieve everything from moderate to extreme muscular definition. In 2012, Hoyos et al first described the use of intramuscular fat grafts in the pectoral muscles as an alternative to implants for male patients undergoing HD liposuction.10 Such techniques demonstrated improved aesthetic outcomes and high patient satisfaction scores compared with implant techniques, while also offering fewer complications and shorter recovery times. Similarly, numerous studies have highlighted the optimal outcomes, effective volume retention, natural-looking results, and safety profile of intramuscular fat grafts in various muscle groups for body contouring procedures. Our results support that Multi-SHOT technique integrates liposculpture across superficial, intermediate, and deep layers with fat grafting to provide natural muscle definition and a more athletic appearance, while preserving the normal individual underlying anatomy (Figure 5). Mastery of this procedure requires proficiency in HD2 liposculpture and US-guided intramuscular fat grafts. This ensures accurate placement of the grafted tissue within the muscle, minimizing the risk of vascular and other tissue injuries. Trained surgeons are able to perform our technique to enhance patient aesthetic outcomes and achieve high satisfaction rates among their patients. Since the advent of RT-US imaging applicability to plastic surgery and the recommendations by the Gluteal Fat Grafting Task Force,11 we believe that proper training will ensure consistent results and the reproducibility of our technique, yielding excellent aesthetic outcomes and a very low rate of complications.

He also underwent Fat grafting of the pectorals, deltoids, biceps, triceps, and lat dorsi. Preoperative photographs (A-C) show a mesomorphic male biotype with moderate fat deposits over the anterior torso. Five-month postoperative photographs (D-F) show a new muscular and athletic appearance of the anterior torso with improved projection of the pectoralis muscles, the entire abdomen and the upper limbs. Of note, the precise location of the fat grafts allows an evident differentiation of the serratus muscles as well as their contrast with a new projection of the external oblique muscle.

Comparatively, in a recent study, Flores González et al described an US-guided fat grafting technique targeting the oblique and serratus muscles in male patients.12 However, this paper holds several limitations and inconsistencies worthy of discussion. Firstly, the study relies on a very small sample size of only 11 cases, which significantly limits the generalizability of the findings. Additionally, although the serratus anterior muscle indentations were acknowledged, the authors did not treat them as individual bundles, but rather grafted them as a single muscle unit, which may overlook important anatomical details. As shown in the anatomic dissection video, the Serratus anterior muscle features individual bundles, each separated by thick connective tissue insertions attached to each rib. This structure makes it impossible to graft the entire muscle at once, necessitating a separate approach for each bundle as our technique outlines. Moreover, the fat grafting method described by Flores González et al, including the specifics of cannula insertion and graft preparation, poses considerable challenges in replicating their technique. Crucially, it also overlooks nearby anatomical structures like the lungs and neurovascular bundles. Ignoring these can lead to serious complications, such as winged scapula, pneumothorax, and neuropathic pain. Furthermore, volume measurements taken immediately after infiltration do not account for potential tissue inflammation and increased blood volume, nor do they consider the typical rate of fat graft reabsorption seen in the initial weeks or months after surgery. The study's lack of comprehensive patient follow-up further limits its reliability in assessing actual complications and long-term outcomes, while also compromising the accuracy of the results. Of note, we realize that patients requiring fat grafting of these muscle groups are few and the indications are limited to those patients who seek a well-defined and athletic torso, who usually have some degree of muscular mass and definition. We encourage surgeons and readers to undergo proper training in RT-US imaging as well as in HD liposuction in order to select patients carefully and avoid surgical complications, as well as unnatural or fake results. Our study has certain limitations: It is observational and retrospective, and it employs a nonstandardized survey to measure patient satisfaction, which aligns with mid-level scientific evidence. However, our sample size is considered “large” relative to the target population, and the notably extended follow-up period provides sufficient time to evaluate the safety and reliability of our findings.

CONCLUSIONS

The combination of HD2 with intramuscular fat grafting of the Serratus anterior muscles and the high external oblique muscles (Multi-SHOT technique) provides a safe and efficient method for body contouring and torso definition in male patients. This technique has demonstrated excellent outcomes, including an athletic and natural appearance, high patient satisfaction, and a minimal rate of complications. Further clinical studies involving a broader population of patients are needed to support our findings.

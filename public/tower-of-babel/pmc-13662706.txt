Shifting Burden of Neural Tube Defects from Early-Life Mortality to an Aging Population Living with Disability — China, 1990–2023
Authors: Yuwei Wu, Xiaoqian Jia, Lingzhang Wang, Xinyue Lin, Yali Zhang, Le Zhang, Zhiwen Li, Zhiyong Zou, Jufen Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
What is already known about this topic?The burden of neural tube defects (NTDs) has declined with folic acid supplementation and fortification, but comprehensive all-age estimates are lacking.What is added by this report?Using data from the Global Burden of Disease Study 2023, we assessed the all-age NTD burden in China during 1990−2023. Incidence, mortality, and disability-adjusted life year (DALY) rates declined significantly, whereas prevalence increased among adults aged ≥50 years and DALY rates increased among those aged ≥75 years, indicating a shift from early-life mortality to disability in an aging population.What are the implications for public health practice?Life-course care should combine primary prevention, long-term rehabilitation and multidisciplinary management for people with NTDs.

ABSTRACT

Background: Although the perinatal burden of neural tube defects (NTDs) has declined, comprehensive all-age burden assessments in China and sociodemographic index (SDI) quintiles are lacking.

Methods: Using data from the Global Burden of Diseases, Injuries, and Risk Factors Study 2023, we analyzed NTD burden trends in China and five SDI quintiles during 1990–2023. Rate trends were assessed using estimated annual percentage changes (EAPCs) and Joinpoint-derived average annual percentage changes (AAPCs).

Results: In China, NTD incidence, mortality, and disability-adjusted life year (DALY) rates decreased significantly during 1990–2023, whereas the prevalence rate showed no significant change. Within China, all three rates declined more among men than among women, and incidence and mortality rates declined most sharply among children aged <5 years. Age-specific EAPC analyses in China showed increasing prevalence and DALY rates among adults aged ≥50 years. In 2023, China’s SDI was within the high-SDI range; over 1990–2023, reductions in its incidence, mortality, and DALY rates exceeded those in every SDI quintile. Joinpoint-derived AAPCs further showed increasing DALY rates among adults aged ≥75 years in China and the high-middle-SDI quintile and among those aged ≥50 years in the high-SDI quintile.

Conclusion: The NTD burden in China has declined since 1990, but a shift toward older age groups highlights the need for integrated life-course care and sustained prevention.

Neural tube defects (NTDs) are serious congenital anomalies associated with perinatal mortality and lifelong disability. Globally, birth defects burden has decreased, particularly in regions with a low sociodemographic index (SDI) (1). In China, folic acid supplementation has contributed to a decline in NTD birth prevalence from 5.3 to 0.5 per 10,000 between 2007 and 2021 (2). Meanwhile, rising years lived with disability suggest a shift toward nonfatal outcomes (3). Although, most studies have focused on perinatal burden of NTDs, improve survival, particularly among people with spina bifida, has resulted in aging population living with disability. A nationwide insurance claims–based study estimated that at least 92,551 people in the United States were living with spina bifida in 2019, with prevalence documented across all adult age groups, including those aged ≥65 years (4). Comprehensive all-age assessments in China and comparisons across SDI quintiles remain lacking. Using the Global Burden of Diseases, Injuries, and Risk Factors Study 2023 (GBD 2023), we analyzed all-age NTD burden by sex and age in China during 1990–2023, with five SDI quintiles as contextual comparators.

Absolute numbers and rates of incidence, prevalence, mortality, and disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) for NTDs in China and across the five SDI quintiles were extracted from the GBD 2023 (5). Data are publicly available through the Global Health Data Exchange (GHDx) GBD Results Tool https://vizhub.healthdata.org/gbd-results/. The SDI is a composite index ranging from 0 to 1 and based on fertility among those younger than 25 years, education among those aged 15 years and older, and lag-distributed income per capita (5). Based on 2023 values, locations were categorized as low-SDI (0.0000–0.5316), low-middle-SDI (0.5316–0.6305), middle-SDI (0.6305–0.6767), high-middle-SDI (0.6767–0.7188), and high-SDI (0.7188–1.0000) (https://ghdx.healthdata.org/record/gbd-2023-socio-demographic-index-sdi). Examples are provided in Supplementary Table S1 (available at https://weekly.chinacdc.cn). Although GBD 2023 does not explicitly assign China to a category, its 2023 SDI was estimated at 0.7275, within the high-SDI range. DALYs were calculated as years of life lost plus years lived with a disability. Rates were age-standardized to the GBD world standard population (5). Burden trends from 1990 to 2023 were analyzed by sex and age in China and compared the five SDI quintiles.

Statistical significance was defined as a two-sided α level of 0.05. Percentage changes in absolute numbers from 1990 to 2023 and 95% uncertainty intervals (UIs) were estimated using the delta method. Rate trends were assessed using estimated annual percentage changes (EAPCs) with 95% UIs from log-linear models. Joinpoint regression (version 5.4.0; National Cancer Institute, Bethesda, MD, USA) identified changes in trend slope, allowing a maximum of 4 joinpoints and using permutation tests (α=0.05). Segment-specific annual percentage changes (APCs) and average annual percentage changes (AAPCs) for 1990–2023 were calculated. Additional analyses and graphics were generated using R, version 4.5.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Detailed ICD definitions, formulas, and age-standardization methods are provided in the Supplementary Materials.

In China, the numbers of incident cases, prevalent cases, deaths, and DALYs in 2023 were 3,258.36 (95% UI: 2,842.75–3,694.51), 71,879.03 (95% UI: 59,349.41–86,825.24), 411.22 (95% UI: 284.88–570.67), and 55,780.00 (95% UI: 41,672.03–71,605.38), respectively (Table 1). Compared with 1990, these numbers decreased by 88.75% (95% UI: 88.72–88.78), 33.95% (95% UI: 33.73–34.16), 97.31% (95% UI: 97.29–97.32), and 96.00% (95% UI: 95.98–96.03), respectively. Incidence, mortality, and DALY rates declined significantly, with EAPCs of −4.17 (95% UI: −4.81 to −3.52), −10.61 (95% UI: −11.48 to −9.73), and −9.96 (95% UI: −10.76 to −9.15), respectively; prevalence showed no significant change (EAPC: 0.11, 95% UI: −0.49 to 0.72). Age-standardized incidence, mortality, and DALY rates were higher in men in 1990 but higher in women by 2023, reflecting steeper declines in men (Supplementary Table S2, available at https://weekly.chinacdc.cn). Age-specific EAPCs were heterogeneous (Supplementary Table S3, available at https://weekly.chinacdc.cn). Incidence and mortality rates declined most sharply among children aged <5 years. Prevalence rate showed no significant trend among persons aged ≤49 years but increased among those aged ≥50 years. Mortality rate declined in all age groups aged ≤ 74 years, while DALY rates declined among those aged ≤49 years but increased among those aged ≥50 years.

Table 1 presents NTD burden across the five SDI regions and China. Temporal trends are shown in Figure 1A–D. In both 1990 and 2023, the age-standardized incidence rates were highest in the low-SDI regions and lowest in the high-SDI regions. All SDI quintiles recorded significant declines, greatest in the high-middle-SDI regions (EAPC: −3.10, 95% UI: −3.34 to −2.85) and the smallest in the low-SDI regions (EAPC: −1.37, 95% UI: −1.45 to −1.29), while China experienced a greater decline (EAPC: −4.17, 95% UI: −4.81 to −3.52). Regarding prevalence, the steepest decline was observed in low-middle-SDI regions (EAPC: −1.23, 95% UI: −1.32 to −1.13), whereas high-SDI regions showed a non-significant increase. China's EAPC for prevalence was not statistically significant; rates decreased during 1990–1999, rose after 2005, and remained below all SDI quintiles throughout the study period. Age-standardized mortality rates fell across all SDI quintiles, were inversely associated with SDI, and declined more steeply at higher SDI regions (EAPC: −5.15, 95% UI: −5.44 to −4.85 in high-SDI vs. −1.52, 95% UI: −1.60 to −1.43 in low-SDI). DALY rates were also inversely related to SDI, with greater declines at higher SDI regions (EAPC: −4.76, 95% UI: −5.05 to −4.48 in high-SDI vs. −1.47, 95% UI: −1.55 to −1.39 in low-SDI). China's DALY EAPC was −9.96 (95% UI: −10.76 to −9.15), a steeper reduction than across all SDI quintiles.

In China, the incidence and mortality rates have decreased in all age groups (Figure 2). The prevalence rate showed a downward trend among those under 50 years of age, while it showed an upward trend among those 50 years of age and older (AAPC: 1.40, 95% UI: 1.36–1.45 in 50–74 years age group; and 1.87, 95% UI: 1.82–1.92 in 75 years and above age group). The DALY rate showed a downward trend among individuals under 75 years of age, while it showed an upward trend among those 75 years of age and older (AAPC: 2.14, 95% UI: 2.09–2.19), consistent with the results of the EAPC fitting model. Analyses stratified by SDI quintile and age group further revealed that the prevalence of NTDs increased among individuals aged 50 years and older in the high-middle and high-SDI regions. The DALY rate for NTDs showed an upward trend among people aged 50 years and older in high-SDI regions, and among those aged 75 years and older in high-middle-SDI regions.

DISCUSSION

The burden of NTDs in China declined substantially, reflecting improved prevention and control (2). Regional surveillance data support this trend; from 1993 to 1996, folic acid use reduced spina bifida by 78% in northern China and by 51% in southern China (6). China’s greater decline relative to the SDI quintiles may reflect the early and sustained implementation of nationwide folic acid interventions and advances in maternal and neonatal care. However, the age-standardized prevalence rates in China rebounded from 2005 to 2023, indicating a recent reversal of the previous decline. This rebound may reflect improved survival, expanded surveillance, and greater diagnostic capacity and healthcare access, increasing the detection of individuals with NTDs, including those with associated Chiari II malformations and hydrocephalus (1,7).

Higher SDI was associated with lower age-standardized incidence, mortality, and DALY rates and steeper declines in these measures. A companion GBD 2021 analysis of congenital birth defects similarly found that mortality and DALY rates in children were negatively correlated with SDI and declined most rapidly in high- and high-middle-SDI regions, where high-quality perinatal care, folic acid fortification, and organized surveillance are more widespread (8). High-SDI regions also tend to report higher or rising birth prevalence than some middle-SDI regions, resulting in a U-shaped relationship between prevalence and SDI (8). The rising age-standardized prevalence in high-SDI regions and China may reflect improved case identification and longer survival rather than a reversal of gains in primary prevention.

The burden of NTDs is shifting toward older age groups, particularly in higher SDI regions. A 50-year Swedish register study documented a decline in birth prevalence from 5.2 to 1.2 per 10,000 births and an improvement in first-year survival from 75 to 94%, with stable mortality thereafter, resulting in an accumulating adult population over successive decades (9). Adult cohorts indicate that many people with spina bifida are now middle-aged or older and often have complex multisystem morbidity (10). This demographic shift underscores the need to strengthen life-course care pathways and long-term rehabilitation among older people with NTDs.

This study has some limitations. First, the study relies on modeled estimates from GBD 2023, which are subject to inherent uncertainty, especially in low-SDI regions, where surveillance systems and vital registration data remain limited. Second, the GBD estimates do not include information on prenatal diagnosis or pregnancy termination, making it difficult to distinguish between true epidemiological changes and those resulting from changes in case ascertainment and reporting. Finally, the ecological framework of this study cannot fully explain the recent increases in age-standardized incidence and prevalence rates.

In conclusion, although the NTD burden in China has declined substantially since 1990, prevalence has recently increased and the burden has shifted toward older age groups, with a similar pattern observed in high-SDI regions. This shift highlights the need to integrate life-course care and rehabilitation with existing prevention strategies to reduce long-term disability among the growing population of older people with NTDs.

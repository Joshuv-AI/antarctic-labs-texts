Hybrid machine learning predictions of high voltage polymeric insulator pollution
Authors: Waleed M Hamanah, Abdullrahman A Al-Shammaa, Ali Ahmed Salem, Ebrahim Al-Wajih, Khaled Al-Soufi, Hassan M Hussein Farh
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study presents a comprehensive approach for predicting pollution levels on high-voltage insulators in the Eastern Region of Saudi Arabia using five machine learning techniques integrated with the Improved Harris Hawks Optimizer (IHHO). The focus is on accurately estimating two key pollution metrics: Equivalent Salt Deposit Density and Normalized Salt Deposit Density. IHHO was used for hyperparameter optimization to guarantee that all proposed models function adequately. The prediction model is constructed from one year’s worth of environmental data, which includes temperature, humidity, wind speed, solar radiation, elevation, dew point, precipitation, line voltage, and service period. Of all evaluated models, the hybrid IHHO-XGBoost model performed best, with an R2 of 0.993, a MAE of 0.0002, an RMSE of 0.00015, a MedAE of 3.563 × 10⁻5, an EV of 0.992, and an Adj R2 of 0.9923 with tenfold cross-validation. Model validation using Taylor diagram analysis confirmed a high degree of agreement between predicted and actual values. Furthermore, application of the SHAP (SHapley Additive Explanation) technique revealed that the most important predictors were wind speed, temperature, line voltage, and solar radiation. In addition, the results were compared to the results of other benchmark models to improve model explained accuracy and trustworthiness. Not only did the IHHO-XGBoost model best others in accuracy, it equally enhanced understanding of the environmental and operational factors that cause insulator contamination. Introduction

High voltage (HV) insulators are crucial components in ensuring the safe and efficient operation of electrical power transmission systems. These insulators, typically made from materials like porcelain, glass, or composite materials, prevent electrical discharges between the transmission lines and the ground or supporting structures1,2. However, in regions where environmental conditions are harsh such as the Eastern Region of Saudi Arabia HV insulators are highly susceptible to pollution, primarily in the form of salt deposition. Salt accumulation on insulator surfaces can lead to significant issues, including leakage currents, surface conductivity, and in extreme cases, insulator failure, which can compromise the stability of the electrical grid3.

Contamination of insulators is a concern, especially in coastal regions, where high humidity, wind, and airborne salt particles from the sea can lead to the cumulative increase of salt deposits on the insulator surface. Also, the airborne dust, industrial pollutants, and environmental temperature fluctuations can further to the pollution and degradation of insulator performance4. The pollution level is typically quantified using two primary metrics: normalized salt deposit density (NSDD) and equivalent salt deposit density (ESDD)5. NSDD measures the amount of pollutants deposited per unit area on the surface of the insulator, while ESDD provides a more detailed indication of the equivalent amount of salt that might cause electrical conductivity issues5. Evaluation and predicting these parameters are essential for anticipating the HV insulators’ performance and the risk of unplanned failures of the power system.

Several studies have emphasized monitoring salt deposition on insulators, particularly in coastal and semi-arid regions, due to their extreme environmental conditions6–12. For example, in a study6, authors looked into salt deposition on HV insulators in coastal regions and reported that salt contamination is a major problem of electrical short circuits and failure in transmission lines. They determined that temperature, humidity, and wind speed were considerable environmental factors that contributed to salt accumulation on the insulators. In addition, natural contamination has a significantly negative exponential relationship to the flashover voltage of insulators, particularly under wettability7. Author13, analyzed the relationship between ESDD, NSDD with insulator conditions. They reported that an increase in ESDD and NSDD posed a greater risk to the insulator.

Using machine learning for fault detection and predictive maintenance has become helpful across multiple domains. This includes predictive maintenance for power grids and high-voltage systems. The introduction of ML systems for power transmission started with predicting the severity of insulator pollution14–17. S. Ahmad et al.14,15 performed ML models to predict pollution deposition on insulators based on an environmental parameters data set. The study highlights the ability of these models to catch complex relationships between environmental factors and the insulator contamination level. Also, de Santos et al.16,17 have exhibited the potential of ML models to predict contamination levels on insulators surface using leakage current monitoring. They reported that the model using environmental data could improve the prediction of pollution accumulation and reduce frequent maintenance. Furthermore, Barbosa and Jingyan Li18,19 discussed how AI could optimize transmission line reliability by using predictive models to estimate insulator contamination, enabling utilities to perform maintenance more proactively and decrease the risks of unforeseen outages. In coastal regions, extreme temperatures and dust storms, and pollution from salt and airborne particles present are a critical challenge20. Although efforts have been made to predict insulator contamination using traditional techniques and environmental monitoring tools, these methods lack the advancement and accuracy required for long-term predictive maintenance. The application of AI-based models to this problem has the potential to significantly improve the prediction accuracy of NSDD and ESDD levels by incorporating the environmental variables in the insulator regions21.

ML models such as decision trees22, support vector machines (SVM)23,24, artificial neural networks (ANN)25,26, Ensemble learning models have gained traction in the predictive maintenance of electrical equipment. The combination of different environmental factors and installation parameters in training ML models has been exhibited to offer highly accurate predictions of pollution deposition and insulator performance19. This study presents five hybrid ML techniques for predicting the pollution levels of insulator surfaces in terms of ESDD and NSDD in the conditions of the coastal region, where environmental factors and pollution levels vary significantly from other areas. The aim is to create reliable predictive models that can predict pollution levels on high-voltage insulator surfaces. This enables utility companies to better plan maintenance and enhance the reliability of the power network.

Recently, studies have introduced improved algorithms to address different issues. For instance, Dalia T. Akl et al.27 proposed an Improved Harris Hawks Optimizer (IHHO) for solving different engineering tasks. Lin Huang28 also applied the IHHO Algorithm in grid map path planning. Integrated/advanced IHHO designs (e.g., IIHHO) further tweak energy dynamics and Lévy-flight attenuation to enhance local search29, while hybrid or mutation-enhanced HHO variants improve global search and have been applied to feature selection and kernel ELM training30,31. In the high voltage insulator applications, an improved particle swarm optimization and a hybrid deep learning technique were used to predict the contamination levels of insulators based on leakage current32–35. However, the performance of IHHO combined with ML models in predicting the level of contamination on insulators has not been tested yet. Moreover, the choice of IHHO in this study is motivated by its ability to overcome the drawbacks of standard HHO, its demonstrated success in high-dimensional and constrained problems, and its superior convergence properties reported in recent applications.

Previous studies on insulator pollution prediction have provided valuable insights but still face important limitations, such as dependence on limited or short-term datasets, the application of models with constrained predictive capability, and insufficient interpretability in explaining how environmental factors influence contamination. To address these issues, this study contributes to highly accurate predictions of NSDD and ESDD by applying the Improved Harris Hawks Optimizer (IHHO) for tuning the hyperparameters of the proposed five machine learning models. The prediction model is constructed using one year’s worth of environmental and operational data. The dataset comprises multiple meteorological and system-related variables, including temperature, humidity, wind speed, solar radiation, elevation, dew point, precipitation, line voltage, and service period. The selected machine learning models represent a range of advanced predictive models widely used in engineering and environmental research, each with strengths in handling nonlinearities, complex interactions, and high-dimensional data. These predictions can improve the management of high-voltage insulators in harsh climates and ensure targeted and timely maintenance interventions, thereby improving grid reliability and minimizing unplanned outages. To evaluate and interpret the performance of the proposed framework, we used SHAP analysis and Taylor plot evaluation. SHAP (SHapley Additive Explanations) offers an interpretable approach to understanding machine learning predictions by quantifying the contribution of each feature to the model output. This not only helps identify the most significant environmental factors influencing ESDD and NSDD but also provides a better understanding of their impact on the predictions. Taylor diagram analysis also assessed the model’s predictive performance in terms of correlation, standard deviation, and root mean square error in a single visualization. This approach allows for a comprehensive comparison of the proposed framework with existing methods, highlighting its accuracy and consistency. Together, these techniques provide quantitative validation of the model’s effectiveness, while also offering interpretability and practical insights into the roles of different environmental factors. Related works

The increasing complexity of power systems and the critical role of high-voltage (HV) insulators in ensuring system reliability have spurred research into advanced diagnostic and predictive maintenance techniques. Recent advancements in insulator contamination assessment have led to the use of a variety of data sources and analytical techniques. Leakage current data11,16,36–42 has been analyzed using an array of machine learning models including RUSBoost, AdaBoost, Random Forest, XGBoost, ANN, ANFIS, and LSTM, along with signal transformation techniques (e.g., FFT, wavelet transform, STFT). Statistical methods and optimization algorithms such as fuzzy logic and particle swarm optimization have also been applied. These techniques achieve high accuracy (up to 96.82%) and allow real-time monitoring with strong adaptability and low implementation cost. However, they are invasive, prone to electromagnetic noise, and require experimental validation to ensure reliability across various pollution types and levels.

Acoustic and ultrasonic data, as explored in43,44, utilize wavelet transforms, SVR stacking, acoustic emission monitoring, and piezoelectric sensor acquisition. These methods are non-invasive and immune to electromagnetic interference, offering reasonable prediction accuracy. However, they require high-quality AE systems, are expensive, and are susceptible to ambient noise and low signal-to-noise ratios.

Electromagnetic and radio frequency (RF) data45 rely on wideband radiation signature analysis and frequency spectrum analysis. These methods support remote, sensitive, and non-contact monitoring across a frequency range of 30–130 MHz. Despite their effectiveness, they require costly equipment, are sensitive to environmental noise, and are limited to specific insulator types.

In the domain of image-based data46–52, techniques such as deep learning (YOLO, ResNet, RetinaNet), support vector machines (SVM), k-nearest neighbors (k-NN), and neural networks (ANN, BPNN, RBNN) have been implemented. These are often combined with data augmentation, edge detection, and image fusion methods. They provide high accuracy (up to 99%) and are effective under different lighting and contamination scenarios. Nevertheless, they require high-resolution imaging systems, are computationally intensive, and are sensitive to environmental variations and uniform background assumptions.

Hyperspectral, ultraviolet (UV), and infrared (IR) imaging methods39,53–57 also use deep learning models (e.g., CNN, ResNet), machine learning regressors (e.g., MLR, RF), and various neural networks, with additional advanced preprocessing techniques such as PCA, SPA, KPCA, and image filtering. These approaches are highly accurate (up to 96.67%) and effective for different pollution levels like ESDD and NSDD, even under humidity variations. However, they necessitate specialized imaging equipment, are highly sensitive to lighting conditions, and involve extensive preprocessing, making them less effective for non-uniform pollution detection.

Partial discharge analysis4 employs deep learning (CNN, LSTM) and fuzzy inference systems for real-time signal processing. These techniques offer portability, non-invasiveness, and a strong correlation with contamination severity. Their main drawbacks include the need for complex data preprocessing and their limitations with specific insulator types and live monitoring applications. Hybrid machine learning (HML) models have emerged as potent tools for predicting pollution levels on HV insulators, combining the strengths of various algorithms to enhance prediction accuracy and robustness58.

Recent studies have explored the application of ML models in assessing insulator contamination. For instance, a study by Khan et al.59 presented an experimental validation of ML for contamination classification of polluted HV insulators using leakage current data. The research developed a comprehensive dataset under controlled laboratory conditions, incorporating variables such as temperature and humidity to reflect real-world scenarios. Various ML models, including decision trees and neural networks, were trained and optimized using Bayesian techniques, achieving accuracies exceeding 98%. The integration of wireless sensor networks (WSNs) and convolutional neural networks (CNNs) can effectively monitor insulator pollution states, enhancing real-time monitoring and timely maintenance actions, thus enhancing the reliability of power systems60. In addition to CNNs, probabilistic neural networks (PNNs) have been employed to assess insulator pollution levels based on discharge noise. This method involves training PNN models with samples that capture the stochastic nature of discharge events, enabling the prediction of pollution severity with a probabilistic framework. Such models are particularly useful in environments where pollution levels fluctuate unpredictably19. Hybrid algorithms combining particle swarm optimization (PSO) and genetic algorithms (GA) have also been proposed to enhance the prediction of insulator leakage currents. By optimizing the parameters of neural networks through these evolutionary algorithms, researchers have achieved improved prediction accuracy and convergence rates. This approach demonstrates the efficacy of hybrid optimization techniques in refining ML models for insulator diagnostics58. Collectively, these studies highlight the growing trend of employing hybrid ML models to predict HV insulator pollution levels. The optimisation methods can increase the prediction abilities, allowing proactive maintenance strategies. As power systems evolve, the use of advanced monitoring techniques will be critical in addressing the issues related to insulator pollution.

Methodology

The prediction of ESDD and NSDD using machine learning was carried out through a structured approach, as illustrated in Fig. 1. Phase 1: 958 data points were collected from five different places for 20 insulator samples, which included nine input features and two target outputs (ESDD and NSDD). The dataset was separated into two subsets: training and testing, with 80% used for training and 20% for testing.

Framework of ESDD and NSDD prediction.

Phase 2: We built models using five machine learning models (SVR, MLP, AdaBoost, CatBoost, and XGBoost) for the training ESDD and NSDD data. The Improved Harris Hawks Optimizer (IHHO) strategy was employed to fine-tune hyperparameters. In addition, IHHO was benchmarked against seven other optimization algorithms to evaluate its performance. A tenfold cross-validation procedure was applied during training to ensure model robustness and to identify the optimal set of hyperparameters. The testing dataset was reserved for evaluating the models’ performance using three key metrics: R2, RMSE, MAE, MSLE, MedAE, EV, and AR2.

Phase 3: Based on the evaluation results, the most effective models were selected. Predicted values were compared with observed values to assess model accuracy. Furthermore, SHAP was used to analyze the contribution of each input variable in predicting both ESDD and NSDD.

All simulations and model training were conducted on a workstation equipped with an Intel® Core™ i7-12700 K CPU @ 2.1 GHz, 16 GB RAM, and running Windows 11 (64-bit) operating system. Python 3.9 was used as the primary programming language, along with several open-source libraries: scikit-learn (for machine learning implementation and evaluation), XGBoost (for gradient boosting), SHAP (for explainability analysis), NumPy and Pandas (for data preprocessing), and Matplotlib (for visualization). The Improved Harris Hawks Optimizer (IHHO) was implemented in Python based on the original algorithm and adapted for hyperparameter optimization of the ML models. All experiments were performed using tenfold cross-validation to ensure reliability and robustness of the reported results.

Dataset collection and constructions

The study site is in the eastern coastal region of Saudi Arabia, encompassing the administrative boundaries of the Al-Jubail, Al-Qatif, Al-Dammam, Al-Dhahran, and Al-Khobar governorates. The area is known for its dry climate, characterized by low annual precipitation and a high evaporation rate. As is known, the meteorological data of the insulator’s operation location has a powerful influence on the pollution level on its surface (ESDD and NSDD). Monthly temperatures in the region range from 20.1 °C in winter to 48 °C in summer, with an average annual temperature of 32.3 °C. The relative humidity fluctuates from 43% in June to 85% in December. The research area is a mix of industrial zones, coastal areas, desert landscapes, and residential areas. Weather conditions and pollution levels vary across these locations, contributing to the overall pollution levels in Saudi Arabia. Machine learning models

Following an in-depth data analysis, this part presents the brief theoretical aspects of the machine learning models and optimization strategies used in this work. This study implemented five well-known supervised machine learning models to predict contamination levels in terms of ESDD and NSDD on HV insulators in eastern Saudi Arabia, along with an improved Harris Hawks Optimizer (IHHO) to tune hyperparameters. These algorithms are SVR, MLP Regressor (MLPR), AdaBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost, and XGBoost. These techniques are widely utilized in electrical engineering to develop predictive machine learning models. Each algorithm is based on different underlying principles, which can lead to varying outcomes depending on the specific context in which they are used. An algorithm that performs poorly in terms of predictive accuracy on one task may outperform others on a different task. Therefore, it is essential to determine not only the most suitable algorithm but also the ideal hyperparameter settings to achieve the best performance for a given problem. The IHHO-XGBoost hybrid yielded the highest performance with an R2 of 0.9921, MAE of 0.0002, and RMSE of 0.00015 using 958 samples. This substantial performance improvement highlights the effectiveness of hybrid metaheuristic-ML models, particularly the IHHO optimizer, in capturing complex nonlinear relationships within high-dimensional feature spaces. Compared with earlier studies using models such as deep neural networks or ensemble regressors, the proposed IHHO-XGBoost framework enhances interpretability by explicitly quantifying feature contributions through XGBoost’s built-in feature importance measures. Moreover, the hybrid integration with IHHO provides transparent insight into the optimization dynamics, which allows for clearer reasoning about parameter influence on predictive accuracy.

Limitations and future work

Although the proposed framework demonstrates promising performance and clear improvements over existing approaches, certain limitations should be acknowledged. First, the experimental evaluation was conducted on a limited set of datasets, which may not fully capture the variability present in broader real-world scenarios. Second, the current method has not yet been optimized for real-time implementation, which may restrict its direct applicability in time-sensitive or large-scale applications. In future work, we plan to extend the study by testing on more diverse and large-scale datasets, incorporating additional evaluation metrics, and exploring hybrid approaches that integrate complementary techniques. Furthermore, enhancing computational efficiency and improving the adaptability of the framework for real-time and cross-domain applications will be valuable directions for further research.

Conclusion

In this work, a hybrid models were developed by combining the Improved Harris Hawks Optimization (IHHO) algorithm with five different machine learning (ML) techniques to estimate pollution levels on insulators, specifically using ESDD and NSDD as indicators. Several weather and pollution-related factors are measured in different regions in a harsh pollution area in Saudi Arabia and chosen as the original inputs for the model. The IHHO is implemented to improve the searching capability in determining the optimal setting parameters for the proposed model and compared with seven other methodologies. The comparative analysis demonstrates that IHHO exhibits stronger global exploration and local exploitation abilities, outperforming the other algorithms in both solution accuracy and convergence rate. The evaluation metrics results demonstrate that IHHO-XGBoost is superior to other models in terms of performance, with an R2 of 0.9921, an MAE of 0.0002, and RMSE of 0.00015. Taylor analysis exhibits that the IHHO-optimized models, especially IHHO-XGBoost, achieve the best model in ESDD and NSDD prediction by recording higher accuracy, stronger correlation, and better generalization. The SHAP analysis reveals that wind speed, temperature, line voltage, and solar radiation are the most impact factors on the accurate prediction of ESDD and NSDD models. The differences between models show the advantages of using multiple models together, which helps make clear decisions about maintaining insulators and planning for the reliability of high-voltage systems.

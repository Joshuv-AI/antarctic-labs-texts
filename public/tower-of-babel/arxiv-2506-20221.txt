Abstract
Motivated by the theory of proof complexity generators we consider the following Σ 2 p \Sigma^{p}_{2} search problem DD P \mbox{DD}_{P} determined by a propositional proof system P P : given a P P -proof π \pi of a disjunction ⋁ i α i \bigvee_{i}\alpha_{i} , no two α i \alpha_{i} having an atom in common, find i i such that α i ∈ TAUT \alpha_{i}\in\mbox{TAUT} . We formulate a hypothesis (ST) that for some strong proof system P P the problem DD P \mbox{DD}_{P} is not solvable in the student-teacher model with a p-time student and a constant number of rounds. The hypothesis follows from the existence of hard one-way permutations. We prove, using a model-theoretic assumption, that (ST) implies 𝒩𝒫 ≠ c ​ o ​ 𝒩𝒫 {\cal N}{\cal P}\neq co{\cal N}{\cal P} . The assumption concerns the existence of extensions of models of a bounded arithmetic theory and it is open at present if it holds.

Introduction

Proof complexity generator are some maps
g:{\{0,1\}^{*}}\rightarrow{\{0,1\}^{*}}

with
|g(x)|
determined by
and larger than
|x|
. Their purpose is to ”generate” hard tautologies: propositional formulas

\tau(g)_{b}
expressing that
b\notin Rng(g)
. To be able to express this propositionally
we need that
g
is p-time or more generally
{{\cal N}{\cal P}\cap{\mbox{co}{\cal N}{\cal P}}}
(possibly non-uniform).

For
{{\cal N}{\cal P}\cap{\mbox{co}{\cal N}{\cal P}}}
maps
g
sending
n
bits to
m>n
bits and
b\in{\{0,1\}^{m}}
,
the statement

has the form

\forall x(|x|=n)\exists i<m\forall y_{i}\ A_{b}(x,i,y_{i})

(1)

with

an open formula in the language
{L_{\mbox{\tiny PV}}}
having a function symbol for all p-time clocked

does not witness that the
i
-th bit of

is

, the
i
-th bit of

The provability of such a statement in the true universal
{L_{\mbox{\tiny PV}}}
-theory

can be analyzed using the interactive student-teacher model (abbr. S-T) of
[

]

In this model S (the student) upon receiving

proposes its first candidate solution

as its output. Otherwise she will send to S a counter-example, some

such that

. In that case the computation enters the second round with S proposing second candidate solution

and T either accepting it or returning a counter-example

. The computation goes for a number of rounds until a solution is found.

We are interested in the case where the number of rounds is bounded by a constant. The S-T model
makes sense for general total search problems where
the condition for the acceptance of a solution,
here
\forall y\ \neg A_{b}(x,i,y)

\Sigma^{p}_{2}

we denote by

\Sigma^{p}_{2}

There are two candidate

[

An analysis using the

These first-order constructions gave independence results for theory

.
There is a close relation between first-order theories and (tacitly propositional)
proof systems, based on the notion of propositional translations (for theories
in
{L_{\mbox{\tiny PV}}}

It is also a classic fact that lengths-of-proofs lower bounds for formulas

When trying to get from the independence results for the two

The

\gamma(p,q)\ :=\ \bigvee_{i}\beta_{i}(p,q^{i})

(2)

A falsifying assignment to

\exists x,y\forall i<m\ \neg A_{b}(x,i,(y)_{i})

The constructions in

\exists x\forall i<m\exists y_{i}\ \neg A_{b}(x,i,y_{i})\ .

To get (

) from (

collection scheme BB of
[

]
for open

-formulas allowing to switch

]
this principle is not provable in

(unless factoring is not hard)

makes the proof-theoretic analysis of (

[

,

[

does not apply in any usable form

In this note we propose a model-theoretic method how to bypass the BB scheme
and get lengths-of-proofs lower bounds from a hypothesis that a certain
\Sigma^{p}_{2}
search problem is hard for S-T computations. The model-theoretic assumptions is of the form
saying that certain extensions of models of
{T_{\mbox{\tiny PV}}}
exists. The caveat is that such
extensions are known to exists for models satisfying the BB scheme for open formulas
but it is not known in general. However, model theory seems to offer much more room
to vary arguments than proof theory does and we think it is worthwhile to try to
assert the model theoretic statement needed.

The search problem and its S-T computability

A propositional disjunction
\bigvee_{i<r}\alpha_{i}
is
disjoint disjunction

denoted

\dot{\bigvee}_{i<m}\alpha_{i}\ ,

if no two
\alpha_{i}

and write just

For a propositional proof system (abbr. pps)

\sigma
is a
P

\beta

Given a pps

the
\Sigma^{p}_{2}
search problem
\mbox{DD}_{P}
is:

•

: a pair
\pi,\dot{\bigvee}_{i}\alpha_{i}
s.t.
\pi:P\vdash\dot{\bigvee}_{i}\alpha_{i}
,

•

: any

s.t.
\alpha_{i}\in\mbox{TAUT}

The problem can be solved in the S-T model described in the Introduction.
S, upon receiving an input, proposes his first candidate solution
i_{1}
.
If it is correct T acknowledges it. Otherwise she sends to S a counter-example:
an assignment falsifying
\alpha_{i_{1}}
. S then proposes his second candidate solution, using also the counter-example he learned in the first round, and the computation proceeds
analogously for some number of rounds.

Following
[
12
, Def.2.4.3]
we define

to be extensions of EF
(Extended Frege, cf.
[
5
]
) by a p-time set of tautologies as extra axioms (see
[

, Thm.2.4.4]
for their nice properties). Every pps can be p-simulated by a strong one.

Hypothesis (ST):

There exists a strong pps
P
such that

\mbox{DD}_{P}\notin\mbox{ST}[{\cal P},O(1)]
,
{\cal P}
standing here also
for the class of p-time algorithms
computing functions.

In fact, we do think that the hypothesis is true for all strong proof systems
(which is equivalent to saying that it holds for EF, the Extended Frege proof system)
but for the statement this apparently weaker formulation suffices.

The
\mbox{DD}_{P}
problem is a special case of a problem with a larger class of inputs
applying to formulas of the form (
2
);
we shall call the problem here

:

•

: a triple
\pi,\bigvee_{i}\beta_{i}(p,q^{i}),a

\pi:P\vdash\bigvee_{i}\beta_{i}
, only atoms

, and

is a truth assignment to atoms
p
,

•

\mbox{DD}_{P}\notin\mbox{ST}[{\cal P},O(1)]\Leftrightarrow\mbox{D}_{P}\notin\mbox{ST}[{\cal P},O(1)]

However, it was shown in

The problem to witness (

input and this lead to the need to consider non-uniform p-time students there.
Another plausible looking hypothesis about the feasible intractability of a certain search problem related to non-deterministic circuits and implying (ST) was given in

(cf. hypothesis (K) there).

We state for the record the plausibility of (ST).

Lemma 2.2
(
[
11

(ST) is true if a strong one-way permutations exists (no polynomial size circuit can invert

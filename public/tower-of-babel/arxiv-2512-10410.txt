Entanglement in C ∗ C^{*} -algebras: tensor products of state spaces
Magdalena Musat, Mikael Rørdam

Abstract
We analyze the Namioka–Phelps minimal and maximal tensor products of compact convex sets arising as state spaces of C ∗ C^{*} -algebras, and, relatedly, study entanglement in (infinite dimensional) C ∗ C^{*} -algebras. The minimal Namioka–Phelps tensor product of the state spaces of two C ∗ C^{*} -algebras is shown to correspond to the set of separable (= un-entangled) states on the tensor product of the C ∗ C^{*} -algebras. We show that these maximal and minimal tensor product of the state spaces agree precisely when one of the two C ∗ C^{*} -algebras is commutative. This confirms an old conjecture by Barker in the case where the compact convex sets are state spaces of C ∗ C^{*} -algebras. The Namioka–Phelps tensor product of the trace simplexes of two C ∗ C^{*} -algebras is shown always to be the trace simplex of the tensor product of the C ∗ C^{*} -algebras. This can be used to show that the trace simplex of (any) tensor product of C ∗ C^{*} -algebras is the Poulsen simplex if and only if the trace simplex of each of the C ∗ C^{*} -algebras is the Poulsen simplex or trivial (and not all trivial).



1
Introduction

Namioka and Phelps introduced in their paper,
[
18
]
, two different ways in which one can associate a tensor product of two convex compact sets
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
: a minimal and a maximal one, here denoted by
K_{1}\otimes_{*}K_{2}
and
K_{1}\otimes^{*}K_{2}
, respectively. The two notions depend on two different ways in which one can define a positive cone in the (algebraic) tensor product of the ordered vector spaces of affine functions on
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
: an “inner” positive cone generated by positive elementary tensors, and an “outer” positive cone consisting of elements that are positive when paired with a tensor product of positive functionals. The two definitions are in a natural way dual to each other. Namioka and Phelps prove that the two tensor products agree when one of the two convex compact sets is a Choquet simplex, and Barker later conjectured that the converse also should hold. Namioka and Phelps showed that Barker’s conjecture holds when one of the two convex compact sets is a square, and Aubrun–Lami–Palazuelos–Plavala,
[
4
]
, recently settled Barker’s conjecture when both convex compact sets are finite dimensional. They also observe a connection between tensor products of cones and entanglement in quantum information theory.

The purpose of this paper is to analyze the Namioka–Phelps tensor products of the state spaces of two
C^{*}
-algebras, and to relate these to entanglement phenomena for (infinite dimensional)
C^{*}
-algebras.
More specifically, given two unital
C^{*}
-algebras
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
,
we relate the Namioka–Phelps tensor products of the state spaces
S({\cal A})
and
S({\cal B})
to the state space
of the tensor products of
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
(both the spatial and the maximal). The two Namioka–Phelps tensor products are completely understood in the case where the two
C^{*}
-algebras are matrix algebras, cf. Remark
3.3
. In general,
S({\cal A})\otimes_{*}S({\cal B})
is precisely the set of separable (= un-entangled) states on the spatial tensor product
{\cal A}\otimes{\cal B}
, thus establishing a connection between the Namioka–Phelps tensor products and quantum information theory. The maximal tensor product,
S({\cal A})\otimes^{*}S({\cal B})
, is more elusive, but can be expressed in terms of unital positive maps, cf. Proposition
3.4
and Remark
3.6
. We use this description to show that the maximal norm of the linear functionals in
S(M_{n}({\mathbb{C}}))\otimes^{*}S(M_{m}({\mathbb{C}}))
is precisely
\min\{n,m\}
, cf. Theorem
3.15
.

In Theorem
3.17
, we show that the three sets
S({\cal A})\otimes_{*}S({\cal B})
,
S({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})
and
S({\cal A})\otimes^{*}S({\cal B})
are equal if and only if one of
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
is commutative, and that none of these sets are equal otherwise. We use this result to confirm Barker’s conjecture for convex compact sets arising as the state space of a
C^{*}
-algebra. As a biproduct of this analysis we reprove, and put into the context of tensor products of convex compact sets and entanglement, the well-known fact that the state space of a
C^{*}
-algebra is a Choquet simplex if and only if the
C^{*}
-algebra is commutative, in which case it is even a Bauer simplex.

Our analysis makes use of classical entanglement phenomena in matrix algebras. We extend this to general non-commutative
C^{*}
-algebras in two steps. First, a version of Glimm’s lemma (Lemma
3.9
) implies that one can map the cone over matrix algebras into any non-commutative
C^{*}
-algebra. Second, we show that entanglement (under some mild conditions) cannot “un-entangle” by passing to larger
C^{*}
-algebras.

We show that the maximal tensor product
K_{1}\otimes^{*}K_{2}
of two
finite dimensional
convex compact sets
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
is bounded relatively to the minimal tensor product
K_{1}\otimes_{*}K_{2}
, and that this no longer holds when
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are the state spaces of sufficiently non-commutatative (non-sub-homogeneous)
C^{*}
-algebras. This provides a way of comparing the sizes of the sets
K_{1}\otimes_{*}K_{2}
and
K_{1}\otimes^{*}K_{2}
, and opens up for a (new) way of quantifying entanglement.

The paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
, we review basic results about the Namioka–Phelps tensor products of convex compact sets, and we provide some concrete examples of such tensor products. In Section
3
, we analyze the minimal and the maximal tensor product of state spaces of
C^{*}
-algebras. We prove here our main result, Theorem
3.17
, mentioned above, which also shows that (infinite dimensional) entanglement occurs in the tensor products of any two non-commutative
C^{*}
-algebras. In Section
4
, we consider the tensor product of trace simplexes of
C^{*}
-algebras, and show that the trace simplex of the tensor product of (finitely or infinitely many)
C^{*}
-algebras is the Poulsen simplex if and only if the trace simplex of each
C^{*}
-algebras is the Poulsen simplex.

We thank Carlos Palazuelos for illuminating discussions on topics related to this paper, and we thank Gabor Szabo for reminding us to include the mono-tracial case in Corollary
4.2
(ii) and Corollary
4.4
(ii).

2
Tensor products of compact convex sets

We review here some basic results about tensor products of convex compact sets, mostly taken from the Namioka–Phelps article,
[
18
]
. We provide examples of tensor products of classical simplexes of relevance for this article. At the end of the section we define infinite tensor products of simplexes, discuss their properties, and provide examples of these, as well.

Let
A
be a real vector space, let
P
be a proper cone in
A
, i.e.,
P+P\subseteq P
,
P\cap-P=\{0\}
, and
A=P-P
. An element
u\in P
is an
order unit
if for each
x\in A
there exists
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
-nu\leq x\leq nu
with respect to the ordering on
A
arising from
P
:
x\leq y
if
y-x\in P
. The state space
S(A,P,u)
of such a triple consists of all linear maps
\varphi\colon A\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying
\varphi(P)\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
and
\varphi(u)=1
. The state space
S(A,P,u)
is compact when equipped with the weak
∗
topology (the topology of pointwise convergence on
A
). In fact, if
u
is any element in
P
, then
S(A,P,u)
is weak
∗
compact if and only if
u
is an order unit.

We shall make use of the following Hahn–Banach type extension result, whose standard proof we omit:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(A,P,u)
and
(A^{\prime},P^{\prime},u)
be ordered real vector spaces with the same order unit
u
, where
A\subseteq A^{\prime}
and
P=P^{\prime}\cap A
. Then each state on
(A,P,u)
extends to a state on
(A^{\prime},P^{\prime},u)
.

Let
K
be a compact convex subset of a locally convex topological vector space
E
. Let
A(K)
denote the ordered real vector space of continuous affine functions
f\colon K\to{\mathbb{R}}
, let
A(K)^{+}
denote the positive linear functionals in
A(K)
, and let
u
denote the constant function
1
, which is an order unit. Consider the pairing
\langle x,f\rangle=f(x)
, for
x\in K
and
f\in A(K)
. Each
x\in K
induces a state
\langle x,\,\cdot\,\rangle
on
(A(K),A(K)^{+},u)
. The map
x\mapsto\langle x,\,\cdot\,\rangle
is an affine homeomorphism from
K
onto
S(A(K),A(K)^{+},u)
, where surjectivity follows from Kadison’s Representation Theorem, see, e.g.,
[
1
, II.1.8]
.

Let
(E,P)
be an ordered vector space, with positive cone
P
. The dual space
(E^{*},P^{*})
consists of the usual algebraic dual
E^{*}
of
E
, and the set
P^{*}
of positive linear functionals on
E
. In the case where
E=A(K)
, and
K
as above is a compact convex set, then
(A(K)^{+})^{*}=(A(K)^{*})^{+}
is the set of positive linear functionals on
A(K)
, each of which is proportional to a state on
A(K)
. Hence
\varphi\in(A(K)^{+})^{*}
if and only if
\varphi(f)=c\,\langle x,f\rangle
,
f\in A(K)
, for some
x\in K
, where
c=\varphi(1)\geq 0
.

Let
(E_{1},P_{1},u_{1}),(E_{2},P_{2},u_{2})
be ordered real vector spaces with order units, let
E_{1}\otimes E_{2}
denote their (algebraic) tensor product, and let
P_{1}\otimes P_{2}
be the cone in
E_{1}\otimes E_{2}
spanned by
x_{1}\otimes x_{2}
, with
x_{j}\in P_{j}
. Then
(E_{1}\otimes E_{2},P_{1}\otimes P_{2},u_{1}\otimes u_{2})
becomes an ordered real vector space with order unit. Define a second positive cone of
E_{1}\otimes E_{2}
by

\displaystyle P_{1}\,\widehat{\otimes}\,P_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\{x\in E_{1}\otimes E_{2}:\langle f,x\rangle\geq 0,\,\text{for all}\,f\in P_{1}^{*}\otimes P_{2}^{*}\big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\{x\in E_{1}\otimes E_{2}:\langle f_{1}\otimes f_{2},x\rangle\geq 0,\,\text{for all}\,f_{1}\in S_{1},f_{2}\in S_{2}\big\},

where
S_{j}=S(E_{j},P_{j},u_{j})
. It is clear that
P_{1}\otimes P_{2}\subseteq P_{1}\,\widehat{\otimes}\,P_{2}
. In particular, if
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are compact convex sets, then

A(K_{1})^{+}\,\widehat{\otimes}\,A(K_{2})^{+}=\big\{f\in A(K_{1})\otimes A(K_{2}):\langle f,x_{1}\otimes x_{2}\rangle\geq 0,\,\text{for all}\,x_{1}\in K_{1},\,x_{2}\in K_{2}\big\}.

For compact convex sets
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
,
let
K_{1}\otimes^{*}K_{2}
and
K_{1}\otimes_{*}K_{2}
denote the maximal, respectively, the minimal tensor products as defined in Namioka–Phelps, there denoted by
K_{1}\square K_{2}
and
K_{1}\vartriangle K_{2}
, respectively, and defined as follows:

K_{1}\otimes^{*}K_{2}=S\big(A(K_{1})\otimes A(K_{2}),A(K_{1})^{+}\otimes A(K_{2})^{+},u_{1}\otimes u_{2}\big),

(2.1)

K_{1}\otimes_{*}K_{2}=S\big(A(K_{1})\otimes A(K_{2}),A(K_{1})^{+}\,\widehat{\otimes}\,A(K_{2})^{+},u_{1}\otimes u_{2}\big).

(2.2)

One has
K_{1}\otimes_{*}K_{2}\subseteq K_{1}\otimes^{*}K_{2}
, since
A(K_{1})^{+}\otimes A(K_{2})^{+}\subseteq A(K_{1})^{+}\,\widehat{\otimes}\,A(K_{2})^{+}
. Both sets
K_{1}\otimes_{*}K_{2}
and
K_{1}\otimes^{*}K_{2}
are convex and compact in the weak
∗
topology. By definition of the weak
∗
topology, a net
(z_{i})
in
K_{1}\otimes_{*}K_{2}
(or in
K_{1}\otimes^{*}K_{2}
) converges to
z
if and only if
\langle z_{i},f_{1}\otimes f_{2}\rangle\to\langle z,f_{1}\otimes f_{2}\rangle
, for all
f_{j}\in A(K_{j})
,
j=1,2
.

For
x_{1}\in K_{1}
and
x_{2}\in K_{2}
define a linear functional
\langle x_{1}\otimes x_{2},\,\cdot\,\rangle
on
A(K_{1})\otimes A(K_{2})
by

\langle x_{1}\otimes x_{2},f_{1}\otimes f_{2}\rangle=\langle x_{1},f_{1}\rangle\langle x_{2},f_{2}\rangle,\qquad f_{j}\in A(K_{j}).

(2.3)

It is easy to verify that
\langle x_{1}\otimes x_{2},\,\cdot\,\rangle
belongs to
K_{1}\otimes_{*}K_{2}
, and we shall denote this functional simply by
x_{1}\otimes x_{2}
. This gives rise to a bi-affine map

\otimes\colon K_{1}\times K_{2}\to K_{1}\otimes_{*}K_{2}\subseteq K_{1}\otimes^{*}K_{2},\qquad(x_{1},x_{2})\mapsto x_{1}\otimes x_{2},\qquad x_{j}\in K_{j}.

(2.4)

In the opposite direction we have continuous affine surjective maps

\pi_{j}\colon K_{1}\otimes^{*}K_{2}\to K_{j},\qquad j=1,2,

(2.5)

defined by
\pi_{j}(x)=x_{j}
,
j=1,2
, where

\langle x,f\otimes u_{2}\rangle=\langle x_{1},f\rangle,\qquad\langle x,u_{1}\otimes g\rangle=\langle x_{2},g\rangle,

(2.6)

whenever
f\in A(K_{1})
and
g\in A(K_{2})
. Clearly,
\pi_{j}(x_{1}\otimes x_{2})=x_{j}
, when
x_{1}\in K_{1}
and
x_{2}\in K_{2}
. For the “converse”, we have the following:

Lemma 2.2

(Lemma 1.1 in
[
18
]
)
.

Let
x\in K_{1}\otimes^{*}K_{2}
and suppose that one of
x_{1}=\pi_{1}(x)
and
x_{2}=\pi_{2}(x)
is an extreme point in
K_{1}
, respectively,
K_{2}
. Then
x=x_{1}\otimes x_{2}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
be compact convex sets, and let
F_{1}\subseteq K_{1}
and
F_{2}\subseteq K_{2}
be affinely independent subsets. Then
F_{1}\otimes F_{2}:=\{x_{1}\otimes x_{2}:x_{j}\in F_{j}\}
is affinely independent in
K_{1}\otimes_{*}K_{2}
.

Proof.

A subset of a convex set is affinely independent if and only if all its finite subsets are affinely independent. We may therefore assume that
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are finite and affinely independent. Choose
f_{x}\in A(K_{1})
and
g_{y}\in A(K_{2})
, for all
x\in F_{1}
and
y\in F_{2}
, such that
\langle x^{\prime},f_{x}\rangle=\delta_{x,x^{\prime}}
and
\langle y^{\prime},g_{y}\rangle=\delta_{y,y^{\prime}}
, for all
x,x^{\prime}\in F_{1}
and all
y,y^{\prime}\in F_{2}
. Then

\langle x^{\prime}\otimes y^{\prime},f_{x}\otimes g_{y}\rangle=\delta_{x,x^{\prime}}\cdot\delta_{y,y^{\prime}},

thus witnessing that
F_{1}\otimes F_{2}
is affinely independent.
∎

For a compact convex subset
K
of a locally convex topological space
E
, let
\mathrm{aff}(K)\subseteq E
denote the affine hull of
K
, and for each
r\geq 0
, set
\mathrm{aff}_{r}(K)=\{(r+1)x-ry:x,y\in K\}
. Note that
\mathrm{aff}_{r}(K)
is a compact convex set containing
K
, and that
\mathrm{aff}(K)=\bigcup_{r\geq 0}\mathrm{aff}_{r}(K)
. We say that a subset
S\subseteq\mathrm{aff}(K)
is
bounded relatively to
K
if
S\subseteq\mathrm{aff}_{r}(K)
, for some
r\geq 0
.

The dimension,
\dim(K)
, of a finite dimensional convex set
K
is the number of elements in a maximal affinely independent subset of
K
minus 1. It is also equal to the covering dimension of the relative interior,
K^{\mathrm{ri}}
, of
K
, and to the (linear) dimension of
\mathrm{aff}(K)
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
be finite dimensional compact convex sets. Then

\dim(K_{1}\otimes^{*}K_{2})=\dim(K_{1}\otimes_{*}K_{2})=(\dim(K_{1})+1)(\dim(K_{2})+1)-1.

Moreover,
K_{1}\otimes^{*}K_{2}
is bounded relatively to
K_{1}\otimes_{*}K_{2}
.

Proof.

If
K
is a finite dimensional compact convex set, then
A(K)
has dimension equal to
\dim(K)+1
. Indeed, the linear map
A(K)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{\dim(K)+1}
obtained by evaluating functions in
A(K)
at any maximal affinely independent subset of
K
is an isomorphism.

Set
n=\dim(K_{1})
and
m=\dim(K_{2})
. The real vector space
A(K_{1})\otimes A(K_{2})
then has dimension
(n+1)(m+1)
. It follows that the dual space of
A(K_{1})\otimes A(K_{2})
also has dimension
(n+1)(m+1)
. The hyperplane consisting of
\varphi\in(A(K_{1})\otimes A(K_{2}))^{*}
satisfying
\varphi(u_{1}\otimes u_{2})=1
has dimension
(n+1)(m+1)-1
, and
K_{1}\otimes^{*}K_{2}
is a convex subset of this hyperplane, and hence has dimension at most
(n+1)(m+1)-1
. Conversely,
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
contain affinely independent subsets
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
with
n+1
, respectively,
m+1
elements. By Lemma
2.3
,
F_{1}\otimes F_{2}
is an affinely independent subset of
K_{1}\otimes_{*}K_{2}
, which therefore has dimension at least
(n+1)(m+1)-1
.

The relative interior
(K_{1}\otimes_{*}K_{2})^{\mathrm{ri}}
is an open subset of
\mathrm{aff}(K_{1}\otimes_{*}K_{2})
whose closure is
K_{1}\otimes_{*}K_{2}
, and is therefore in particular absorbing for
\mathrm{aff}(K_{1}\otimes_{*}K_{2})
. By the first part,
K_{1}\otimes^{*}K_{2}\subseteq\mathrm{aff}(K_{1}\otimes_{*}K_{2})
. This entails that
K_{1}\otimes^{*}K_{2}\subseteq\bigcup_{r\geq 0}\mathrm{aff}_{r}(K_{1}\otimes_{*}K_{2})^{\mathrm{ri}}
, and hence
K_{1}\otimes^{*}K_{2}\subseteq\mathrm{aff}_{r}(K_{1}\otimes_{*}K_{2})^{\mathrm{ri}}\subseteq\mathrm{aff}_{r}(K_{1}\otimes_{*}K_{2})
, for some
r\geq 0
, by compactness.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

We show in Corollary
3.16
that
K_{1}\otimes^{*}K_{2}
fails to be bounded relatively to
K_{1}\otimes_{*}K_{2}
, when
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are the state spaces of (suitably non-commutative)
C^{*}
-algebras, and also that
K_{1}\otimes^{*}K_{2}
in general is not contained in
\mathrm{aff}(K_{1}\otimes_{*}K_{2})
.

The set of extreme points of a compact convex set
K
is denoted by
\partial_{e}K
.

Proposition 2.6

(Namioka–Phelps,
[
18
]
)
.

Let
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
be compact convex sets.

(i)

The bi-affine map
K_{1}\times K_{2}\to K_{1}\otimes_{*}K_{2}
is (jointly) continuous.

(ii)

\partial_{e}K_{1}\times\partial_{e}K_{2}=\partial_{e}(K_{1}\otimes_{*}K_{2})
, and the two sets are homeomorphic.

(iii)

\partial_{e}(K_{1}\otimes_{*}K_{2})\subseteq\partial_{e}(K_{1}\otimes^{*}K_{2})
.

(iv)

K_{1}\otimes_{*}K_{2}=\overline{{\mathrm{conv}}}\{x_{1}\otimes x_{2}:x_{j}\in K_{j},\,j=1,2\}=\overline{{\mathrm{conv}}}\{x_{1}\otimes x_{2}:x_{j}\in\partial_{e}K_{j},\,j=1,2\}
.

(v)

The affine maps
\pi_{j}\colon K_{1}\otimes^{*}K_{2}\to K_{j}
are continuous and surjective, and remain surjective when restricted to
K_{1}\otimes_{*}K_{2}
.

Proof.

Item (i) follows from (
2.3
). It is shown in
[
18
, Theorem 2.3]
that

\partial_{e}K_{1}\times\partial_{e}K_{2}=\partial_{e}(K_{1}\otimes_{*}K_{2})\subseteq\partial_{e}(K_{1}\otimes^{*}K_{2}),

which together with (i) proves (ii) and also (iii). Item (iv) follows from (iii). Finally, (v) follows from (
2.6
).
∎

Namioka and Phelphs raise in
[

]
the question if

is always a face in

(because of (iii) above). By Proposition

(Namioka-Phelps,
[

]
)

Let

denote the square (in

(ii)

We shall henceforth let

Example 2.10
.

Here we give examples of tensor products of compact convex sets. The two first examples are more or less immediate consequences of results of Namioka and Phelps mentioned above, while example (iii) about the Poulsen simplex is new.

For each
n\geq 0
, let
\Delta_{n}
denote the
n
-simplex, which is the Choquet simplex spanned by
n+1
affinely independent points. In particular,
\Delta_{0}
is a point. We refer to this simplex as the trivial one.

(i)

K\otimes\Delta_{0}=K
, for all compact convex sets
K
.

(ii)

\Delta_{n-1}\otimes\Delta_{m-1}=\Delta_{nm-1}
, for all
n,m\geq 1
.

Proof:
We know from Theorem
2.9
that
\Delta_{n-1}\otimes\Delta_{m-1}
is a Choquet simplex, and
\partial_{e}(\Delta_{n-1}\otimes\Delta_{m-1})
has

2.6
(ii).
\square

We can also deduce this without referring to Theorem
2.9
, as follows: A finite dimensional compact convex set
\Delta
is a simplex if and only if
\partial_{e}\Delta
is affinely independent. Now,
\partial_{e}\Delta_{n-1}
and
\partial_{e}\Delta_{m-1}
are affinely independent sets with
n
, respectively,
m

Example 2.12

We give below two (classes of) examples of infinite tensor products of Choquet simplexes

\bigotimes_{k\geq 1}S_{k}

S_{k}
is either trivial or the Poulsen simplex, for each
k
, and not all
S_{k}
are trivial.

Proof.

By Example
2.10
(iv), it suffices to show that
\bigotimes_{k\geq 1}S_{k}
is the Poulsen simplex (or trivial) if and only if
\bigotimes_{k=1}^{n}S_{k}

3
Entanglement and tensor products of state spaces of
C^{*}
-algebras

In this section we identify the Namioka–Phelps tensor products of state spaces of two
C^{*}
-algebras. We show that Barker’s conjecture,
2.8
, holds for this class of (typically infinite dimensional) compact convex sets. The minimal tensor product of two state spaces is identified as the set of un-entangled (separable) states, and we also describe, in several ways, the maximal tensor product, which turns out to be more elusive. While state spaces of
C^{*}
-algebras are special among general compact convex sets, it is an interesting and deep result, due to Alfsen, Hanche-Olsen and Schultz,
[
2
, Corollary 8.6]
, that there is a complete axiomatic description of when a compact convex sets is the state space of a
C^{*}
-algebra.

It is an old well-known fact that the state space of a
C^{*}
-algebra is a Choquet simplex if and only if the
C^{*}
-algebra is commutative, in which case the state space is a Bauer simplex. We reprove this fact in Theorem
3.17
below as a biproduct of our investigations. All Bauer simplexes arise in this way, as we can take the commutative
C^{*}
-algebra to be the one whose spectrum is the extreme boundary of the given Bauer simplex.

Let
{\cal A}
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra, let
S({\cal A})
denote the convex compact set of all states on
{\cal A}
, and let
{\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}}
denote the (real vector space) of all self-adjoint elements in
{\cal A}
. The set of extreme points of
S({\cal A})
is by definition the set of pure states on
{\cal A}
, which are those states
\rho
for which the GNS representation
\pi_{\rho}
is irreducible, i.e.,
\pi_{\rho}({\cal A})^{\prime\prime}=B(H)
.

It is well-known (and a consequence of Kadison’s Representation Theorem, as noted before) that we can identify
A(S({\cal A}))
, the affine functions on
S({\cal A})
, with
{\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}}
via the pairing
\langle a,\rho\rangle=\rho(a)
, for
a\in{\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}}
and
\rho\in S({\cal A})
. This pairing further identifies
A(S({\cal A}))^{+}
with
{\cal A}^{+}
and the order unit

with
1_{\cal A}
, and hence
(A(S({\cal A})),A(S({\cal A}))^{+},1)
with
({\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}},{\cal A}^{+},1_{\cal A})

S({\cal A})=S({\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}},{\cal A}^{+},1_{\cal A})
.

{\cal A}

\displaystyle S({\cal A})\otimes^{*}S({\cal B})

For unital

Proof.

Consider the affine continuous map

({\cal A}\odot{\cal B})_{\mathrm{sa}}
is dense in

We know from Proposition
2.6

S({\cal A})\otimes_{*}S({\cal B})
is the closed convex hull of states in

\rho_{\cal A}\in S({\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}},{\cal A}^{+},1_{\cal A})=S({\cal A})

\rho_{\cal B}\in S({\cal B}_{\mathrm{sa}},{\cal B}^{+},1_{\cal B})=S({\cal B})

\rho_{\cal A}\otimes\rho_{\cal B}
(defined on
{\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}}\odot{\cal B}_{\mathrm{sa}}

{\cal A}\odot{\cal B}
, and next,
by the definition of the minimal tensor product, to
{\cal A}\otimes{\cal B}
. This shows that the restriction mapping above is also surjective.
∎

We have the following description of the states on the maximal tensor product.

Lemma 3.2
.

For unital

-algebras
{\cal A}
and

We show that the mapping

S({\cal A}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B})\to S({\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}}\odot{\cal B}_{\mathrm{sa}},({\cal A}\odot{\cal B})^{+},1_{\cal A}\otimes 1_{\cal B})

obtained by restricting a state on

Any state

{\cal A}\odot{\cal B}
obtained from

∗

, for
k\geq 2
. The Hilbert space structure gives a (conjugate linear) isomorphism from
M_{k}({\mathbb{C}})
to its dual space
M_{k}({\mathbb{C}})^{*}
given by
T\mapsto\langle\,\cdot\,,T\rangle
. The functional
\langle\,\cdot\,,T\rangle
is unital if and only if
\mathrm{Tr}(T)=1
, positive if and only if
T
is positive, and

\|\langle\,\cdot\,,T\rangle\|=\|T\|_{1}
, for
T\in M_{k}({\mathbb{C}})
.

For a (proper) cone
\mathcal{C}\subseteq M_{k}({\mathbb{C}})_{\mathrm{sa}}
, let
\mathcal{C}^{\#}\subseteq M_{k}({\mathbb{C}})_{\mathrm{sa}}
denote its dual cone with respect to the inner product considered above.

Let now
n,m\geq 2
be integers. A positive element
T
in
M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\otimes M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})
, and its corresponding linear functional
\langle\,\cdot\,,T\rangle
, is separable if it belongs to
M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}\otimes M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}
, and entangled otherwise.
The cones
M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}\otimes M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}
and
M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}\,\widehat{\otimes}\,M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}
are duals of each other:

(M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}\otimes M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})^{+})^{\#}=M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}\,\widehat{\otimes}\,M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})^{+},

cf.
[
12
]
, and
(M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\otimes M_{m}({\mathbb{C}}))^{+}
is self-dual. By finite dimensionality,
M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\odot M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})=M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\otimes M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})
and
M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}\odot M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}=M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}\otimes M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}
. Combining these facts with (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), and identifying functionals with their density matrices, we get

\displaystyle S(M_{n}({\mathbb{C}}))\otimes_{*}S(M_{m}({\mathbb{C}}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}\otimes M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}\cap\mathrm{Tr}^{-1}(1),

\displaystyle S(M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\otimes M_{m}({\mathbb{C}}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\otimes M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})\big)^{+}\cap\mathrm{Tr}^{-1}(1),

\displaystyle S(M_{n}({\mathbb{C}}))\otimes^{*}S(M_{m}({\mathbb{C}}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}\,\widehat{\otimes}\,M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}\cap\mathrm{Tr}^{-1}(1),

while
S(M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\otimes M_{m}({\mathbb{C}}))=S(M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}M_{m}({\mathbb{C}}))
by nuclearity.

Let
\mathrm{Pos}(n,m)
denote the set of positive linear maps
M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\to M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})
, where
n,m\geq 2
, and let
\mathrm{UPos}(n,m)
denote the set of unital maps in
\mathrm{Pos}(n,m)
. Let
\rho^{(m)}_{0}
denote the maximally entangled state on
M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})\otimes M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})
given by the unit vector
m^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}e_{j}\otimes e_{j}
, where
(e_{1},\dots,e_{m})
is an orthonormal basis for
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
. Observe that
\rho^{(m)}_{0}(A\otimes I_{m})=\rho^{(m)}_{0}(I_{m}\otimes A)=\mathrm{tr}(A)
, for all
A\in M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})
, where
\mathrm{tr}=m^{-1}\,\mathrm{Tr}
is the normalized trace.

The maximal tensor product of state spaces of matrix algebras has the following alternative description in terms of positive maps:

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
n,m\geq 2
. Then

\displaystyle S(M_{n}({\mathbb{C}}))\otimes^{*}S(M_{m}({\mathbb{C}}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\{\rho^{(m)}_{0}\circ(\Phi\otimes\mathrm{id}_{m}):\Phi\in\mathrm{Pos}(n,m),\,\mathrm{tr}(\Phi(I_{n}))=1\big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\{\rho\circ(\Phi\otimes\mathrm{id}_{m}):\Phi\in\mathrm{UPos}(n,m),\,\rho\in S(M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})\otimes M_{m}({\mathbb{C}}))\big\}.

Proof.

If
\Phi\colon M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\to M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})
is positive, then
\Phi\otimes\mathrm{id}_{m}
maps
M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}\otimes M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}
into
M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}\otimes M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}\subseteq(M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})\otimes M_{m}({\mathbb{C}}))^{+}
, so
\rho\circ(\Phi\otimes\mathrm{id}_{m})
is positive on
M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}\otimes M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}
, for each state
\rho
on
M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\otimes M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
. This shows that the two sets on the right-hand side are contained in
S(M_{n}({\mathbb{C}}))\otimes^{*}S(M_{m}({\mathbb{C}}))
.

For the converse direction, let

S^{(m)}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}E_{ij}\otimes E_{ji},\qquad H^{(m)}=(\mathrm{id}_{m}\otimes t_{m})(S^{(m)})=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}E_{ij}\otimes E_{ij},

where
t_{m}
is the transpose map on
M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})
, and
(E_{ij})
is the set of matrix units for
M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})
. Then
\rho_{0}^{(m)}=m^{-1}\langle\,\cdot\,,H^{(m)}\rangle
. For a linear map
\Psi\colon M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})\to M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
, consider its Jamiolkowski matrix
J(\Psi)=(\Psi\otimes\mathrm{id}_{m})(S^{(m)})
and its Choi matrix
C(\Psi)=(\Psi\otimes\mathrm{id}_{m})(H^{(m)})
. It was shown by Jamiolkowski,
[
14
]
, that
\Psi
is positive if and only if
J(\Psi)\in M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}\,\widehat{\otimes}\,M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}
, which again is equivalent to
C(\Psi)=(\mathrm{id}_{n}\otimes t_{m})(J(\Psi))\in M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}\,\widehat{\otimes}\,M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}
.

Fix
\phi\in S(M_{n}({\mathbb{C}}))\otimes^{*}S(M_{m}({\mathbb{C}}))
and write
\phi=\langle\,\cdot\,,T\rangle
, for some density matrix
T
in
M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}\,\widehat{\otimes}\,M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}
with
\mathrm{Tr}(T)=1
, cf. Remark
3.3
. By Jamiolkowski’s theorem and the comments above,
T=C(\Psi)
for some
\Psi\in\mathrm{Pos}(m,n)
. Hence,
for
X\in M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\otimes M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})
,

\displaystyle\phi(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle X,C(\Psi)\rangle=\langle X,(\Psi\otimes\mathrm{id}_{m})(H^{(m)})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle(\Psi^{*}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{m})(X),H^{(m)}\rangle=m(\rho_{0}^{(m)}\circ(\Psi^{*}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{m}))(X).

It follows that
\phi=\rho_{0}^{(m)}\circ(\Phi\otimes\mathrm{id}_{m})
with
\Phi=m\Psi^{*}
, which is positive since
\Psi
is positive. Also,
1=\phi(I_{n}\otimes I_{m})=\rho_{0}^{(m)}(\Phi(I_{n})\otimes I_{m})=\mathrm{tr}(\Phi(I_{n}))
.

To complete the proof we show that if
\Phi\in\mathrm{Pos}(n,m)
and
\mathrm{tr}(\Phi(I_{n}))=1
, then there are
\Psi\in\mathrm{UPos}(n,m)
and
\rho\in S(M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\otimes M_{n}({\mathbb{C}}))
such that
\rho_{0}^{(m)}\circ(\Phi\otimes\mathrm{id}_{m})=\rho\circ(\Psi\otimes\mathrm{id}_{m})
. Let
P
be the range projection of the positive element
\Phi(I_{n})
in
M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})
, and let
R
be the inverse of
\Phi(I_{n})^{1/2}
relatively to
PM_{m}({\mathbb{C}})P
, i.e.,
R\Phi(I_{n})^{1/2}=P=\Phi(I_{n})^{1/2}R
. Choose any unital positive map
\Psi_{1}\colon M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\to(I_{m}-P)M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})(I_{m}-P)
, e.g.,
\Psi_{1}
could be a state on
M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})
followed by multiplication by
I_{m}-P
. Define

\Psi(A)=R\Phi(A)R+\Psi_{1}(A),\quad\rho(X)=\rho_{0}^{(m)}\big((\Phi(I_{n})^{1/2}\otimes I_{m})X(\Phi(I_{n})^{1/2}\otimes I_{m})\big),

for
A\in M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})
and
X\in M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})\otimes M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})
. Then
\rho_{0}^{(m)}\circ(\Phi\otimes\mathrm{id}_{m})=\rho\circ(\Psi\otimes\mathrm{id}_{m})
and
\Psi\in\mathrm{UPos}(n,m)
. The functional
\rho
is positive and
\rho(I_{m}\otimes I_{m})=\rho_{0}^{(m)}\circ(\Phi\otimes\mathrm{id}_{m})(I_{n}\otimes I_{m})=1
. Hence
\rho
is a state on
M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})\otimes M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

For later use we recall the well-known fact that
S^{(m)}
defined above belongs to
M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}\,\widehat{\otimes}\,M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}
. This is a much used fact in quantum information theory, referred to by saying that
S^{(m)}
is an
entanglement witness
: If
T\in(M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})\otimes M_{m}({\mathbb{C}}))^{+}
with
\mathrm{Tr}(T)=1
is such that
\langle T,S^{(m)}\rangle<0
, then the state
\langle\,\cdot\,,T\rangle
is entangled.

One can easily see that
S^{(m)}\in M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}\,\widehat{\otimes}\,M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}
as follows: Consider the transpose map
t_{m}\colon M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})\to M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})
, which is positive, but not completely positive. The matrix
H^{(m)}
defined in the proof of Proposition
3.4
is positive. For all states
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
on
M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})
,
\rho_{2}\circ t_{m}
is a state on
M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})
, so
(\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2})(S^{(m)})=(\rho_{1}\otimes(\rho_{2}\circ t_{m}))(H^{(m)})\geq 0
.

Remark 3.6
.

In general, if
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
are two unital
C^{*}
-algebras, and if
\Phi\colon{\cal A}\to{\cal A}_{1}
and
\Psi\colon{\cal B}\to{\cal B}_{1}
are unital positive linear maps into unital
C^{*}
-algebras
{\cal A}_{1}
and
{\cal B}_{1}
, then
\Phi\otimes\Psi
is a well-defined linear map
({\cal A}\odot{\cal B})_{\mathrm{sa}}\to({\cal A}_{1}\odot{\cal B}_{1})_{\mathrm{sa}}
which maps
{\cal A}^{+}\odot{\cal B}^{+}
into
{\cal A}_{1}^{+}\odot{\cal B}_{1}^{+}\subseteq({\cal A}_{1}\odot{\cal B}_{1})^{+}
. (In general,
\Phi\otimes\Psi
may be unbounded and will not extend to the completion
{\cal A}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B}
.) Each state
\rho
in
S({\cal A}_{1}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B}_{1})
therefore defines an element
\rho\circ(\Phi\otimes\Psi)
in
S({\cal A})\otimes^{*}S({\cal B})
.

We do not know if all functionals in
S({\cal A})\otimes^{*}S({\cal B})
arise in this way, in general. By
Proposition
3.4
above, this is the case when
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
are matrix algebras (even with
\Psi=\mathrm{id}_{\cal B}
and
{\cal A}_{1}={\cal B}
).

We will show that entanglement always occurs in the tensor product of two non-commutative
C^{*}
-algebras. This is well-known for matrix algebras. We shall “lift” entanglement for matrix algebras to general non-commutative
C^{*}
-algebras
by first recalling Glimm’s lemma which implies that cones over a matrix algebras always embed into any non-commutative
C^{*}
-algebra, and then show that entanglement cannot “un-entangle” when passing from a subalgebra to a larger
C^{*}
-algebra.

Definition 3.7
.

For a
C^{*}
-algebra
{\cal A}
, let
\mathrm{rank}({\cal A})\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\infty\}
be the supremum of the set of all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\infty\}
for which there exists an irreducible representation of
{\cal A}
on a Hilbert space
H
of dimension
n
.

Remark 3.8
.

A
C^{*}
-algebra
{\cal A}
is commutative if and only if
\mathrm{rank}({\cal A})=1
. Moreover, a
C^{*}
-algebra
has finite rank if and only if it is
sub-homogeneous
, i.e., is a sub-
C^{*}
-algebra of
M_{n}(C_{0}(X))
, for some locally compact Hausdorff space
X
and some integer
n\geq 1
. Indeed, if
\mathrm{rank}({\cal A})\leq n
, then its bidual
{\cal A}^{**}
is a direct sum of type I
k
von Neumann algebras, with
1\leq k\leq n
, and a type I
k
von Neumann algebra is of the form
M_{k}({\cal C})
, for some abelian von Neumann algebra
{\cal C}
.

For
n\geq 1
, let
CM_{n}=\{f\in C([0,1],M_{n}):f(0)=0\}
denote the cone over
M_{n}=M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
, and let
{\cal E}_{n}
denote the unitization of
CM_{n}
, which can be identified with

{\cal E}_{n}=\{f\in C([0,1],M_{n}):f(0)\in{\mathbb{C}}\cdot 1_{n}\}.

The lemma below is a variation of the well-known “Glimm’s lemma”. We include its proof for completeness of the exposition (and for lack of a precise reference).

Lemma 3.9

(Glimm)
.

Let
{\cal A}
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra, and let
n\geq 1
be an integer. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\mathrm{rank}({\cal A})\geq n
,

(ii)

there exists a non-zero
∗
-homomorphism
CM_{n}\to{\cal A}
,

(iii)

there is an embedding of either
M_{n}
or
CM_{n}
into
{\cal A}
,

(iv)

there is a unital
∗
-homomorphism
{\cal E}_{n}\to{\cal A}
that is non-zero on
CM_{n}
.

Items
(i)
,
(ii)
and
(iii)
are equivalent also when
{\cal A}
is non-unital.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii). Suppose that
\mathrm{rank}({\cal A})\geq n
and let
\pi
be an irreducible representation of
{\cal A}
on a Hilbert space
H
of dimension at least
n
. Set
{\cal B}=\pi({\cal A})
. Let
P
be a projection from
H
onto an
n
-dimensional subspace
H_{0}
of
H
. By Kadison’s transitivity theorem, for each unitary
u
on
H_{0}
there is a unitary
v\in{\cal B}
whose restriction to
H_{0}
is
u
. Necessarily,
v
must commute with
P
.

Set

\mathcal{D}=\{a\in{\cal A}:[\pi(a),P]=0\}\subseteq{\cal A}
. Define
\varphi\colon\mathcal{D}\to B(H_{0})
by
\varphi(a)=\pi(a)|_{H_{0}}
. Then
\varphi
is a unital
∗
-homomorphism which, by the argument above, must be surjective. Identifying
B(H_{0})
with
M_{n}
we get an isomorphism
M_{n}\to\mathcal{D}/\mathrm{ker}(\varphi)
, and hence a surjection
CM_{n}\to\mathcal{D}/\mathrm{ker}(\varphi)
. By projectivity of
CM_{n}
, this isomorphism lifts to a (non-zero)
∗
-homomorphism
CM_{n}\to\mathcal{D}
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii). Take a non-zero
∗
-homomorphism
CM_{n}\to{\cal A}
. The (non-zero) quotient of
CM_{n}
by the kernel of this
∗
-homomorphism is (isomorphic to)
{\cal B}:=\{f\in C_{0}(I,M_{n}):f(0)=0\}
, for some (non-empty) closed subset
I
of
[0,1]
. (If
0\notin I
, then the condition
f(0)=0
is vacuous.) If
I=[0,t]
for some
0<t\leq 1
, then
{\cal B}\cong CM_{n}
, and there is an embedding of
CM_{n}
into
{\cal A}
. If
I
is not of this form, then
I
contains (or is equal to) a non-empty clopen subset
I_{0}
that does not contain
0
, and
C(I_{0},M_{n})
is a direct summand of (or equal to)
{\cal B}
. As
M_{n}
embeds into
C(I_{0},M_{n})
, it embeds into
{\cal B}
and hence into
{\cal A}
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iv). The unitization of the (non-zero)
∗
-homomorphism
CM_{n}\to{\cal A}
(mapping the unit of
{\cal E}_{n}
to the unit of
{\cal A}
) has the desired properties.

(iv)
\Rightarrow
(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) and (iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii) are clear.
∎

We shall now address the question if entangled states, or entangled positive elements, can become un-entangled when passing to larger
C^{*}
-algebras. It is easy to see that the answer is no for entangled states:

Proposition 3.10
.

For inclusions of unital
C^{*}
-algebras
{\cal A}_{0}\subseteq{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}_{0}\subseteq{\cal B}
we have the following commutative diagram, where the horizontal maps,
r
and
r_{*}
, given by restriction, are surjective:

\textstyle{S_{*}({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{r_{*}}
\textstyle{\subseteq}
\textstyle{S_{*}({\cal A}_{0}\otimes{\cal B}_{0})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\subseteq}
\textstyle{S({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{r}
\textstyle{S({\cal A}_{0}\otimes{\cal B}_{0})}

In particular, if
\rho\in S({\cal A}_{0}\otimes{\cal B}_{0})
is entangled, then so is any extension of
\rho
to
{\cal A}\otimes{\cal B}
.

Proof.

The restriction mapping
r\colon S({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})\to S({\cal A}_{0}\otimes{\cal B}_{0})
is surjective (by Hahn–Banach), and it maps a product state
\rho_{\cal A}\otimes\rho_{\cal B}
, with
\rho_{\cal A}\in S({\cal A})
and
\rho_{\cal B}\in S({\cal B})
, to the product state
\rho_{{\cal A}_{0}}\otimes\rho_{{\cal B}_{0}}
, where
\rho_{{\cal A}_{0}}
and
\rho_{{\cal B}_{0}}
are the restrictions of
\rho_{\cal A}
and
\rho_{\cal B}
to
{\cal A}_{0}
and
{\cal B}_{0}
, respectively. Being affine and continuous, it follows that
r
maps
S_{*}({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})
into
S_{*}({\cal A}_{0}\otimes{\cal B}_{0})
. Conversely, any states
\rho_{{\cal A}_{0}}\in S({\cal A}_{0})
and
\rho_{{\cal B}_{0}}\in S({\cal B}_{0})
can be lifted to states
\rho_{\cal A}\in S({\cal A})
and
\rho_{\cal B}\in S({\cal B})
, and
r_{*}(\rho_{\cal A}\otimes\rho_{\cal B})=\rho_{{\cal A}_{0}}\otimes\rho_{{\cal B}_{0}}
, which shows that also
r_{*}
is surjective.
∎

In Proposition
3.12
below we show that entanglement cannot “un-entangle” when passing to larger
C^{*}
-algebras, at least in the presence of some (rather weak) injectivity assumptions:

Definition 3.11
.

A
C^{*}
-algebra
{\cal A}
is said to be
injective
, respectively,
approximately injective
if for all inclusions
{\cal A}\subseteq{\cal B}
, there exists a completely positive contractive (cpc) map
E\colon{\cal B}\to{\cal A}
, respectively, a net
E_{\alpha}\colon{\cal B}\to{\cal A}
of cpc maps, such that

E(a)=a
, respectively,
\lim_{\alpha}E_{\alpha}(a)=a
, for all
a\in{\cal A}
.

A map
E
satisfying the conditions above is a conditional expectation, and a we call the net
(E_{\alpha})
an
approximate
conditional expectation.

Injectivity for
C^{*}
-algebras is quite rare (all finite dimensional
C^{*}
-algebras have the property), but approximate injectivity is much more common. For example, all nuclear
C^{*}
-algebras are approximately injective. Indeed, suppose that
{\cal A}
is a nuclear
C^{*}
-algebra witnessed by a net
(\varphi_{\alpha},M_{n(\alpha)}({\mathbb{C}}),\psi_{\alpha})
where
\varphi_{\alpha}\colon{\cal A}\to M_{n(\alpha)}({\mathbb{C}})
and
\psi_{\alpha}\colon M_{n(\alpha)}({\mathbb{C}})\to{\cal A}
are cpc maps satisfying
\lim_{\alpha}(\psi_{\alpha}\circ\varphi_{\alpha})(a)=a
, for all
a\in{\cal A}
, and that
{\cal A}\subseteq{\cal B}
. By Arveson’s extension theorem we may extend
\varphi_{\alpha}
to a cpc map
\overline{\varphi}_{\alpha}\colon{\cal B}\to M_{n(\alpha)}({\mathbb{C}})
. Then
E_{\alpha}=\psi_{\alpha}\circ\overline{\varphi}_{\alpha}\colon{\cal B}\to{\cal A}
is an approximate conditional expectation.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
{\cal A}_{0}\subseteq{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}_{0}\subseteq{\cal B}
be
C^{*}
-algebras. Then
(i)
,
(ii)
, and
(iii)
below hold if
{\cal A}_{0}
and
{\cal B}_{0}
are injective (e.g., finite dimensional); and
(ii)
and
(iii)
hold if
{\cal A}_{0}
and
{\cal B}_{0}
are approximately injective (e.g., nuclear):

(i)

({\cal A}^{+}\odot{\cal B}^{+})\cap({\cal A}_{0}\odot{\cal B}_{0})={\cal A}_{0}^{+}\odot{\cal B}_{0}^{+}
,

(ii)

({\cal A}^{+}\otimes{\cal B}^{+})\cap({\cal A}_{0}\otimes{\cal B}_{0})={\cal A}_{0}^{+}\otimes{\cal B}_{0}^{+}
,

(iii)

({\cal A}^{+}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B}^{+})\cap({\cal A}_{0}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B}_{0})={\cal A}_{0}^{+}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B}_{0}^{+}.

Proof.

The inclusions “
\supseteq
” in (i)–(iii) hold trivially, for all
{\cal A}_{0}
and
{\cal B}_{0}
. Suppose that
{\cal A}_{0}
and
{\cal B}_{0}
are injective, and let
E_{\cal A}\colon{\cal A}\to{\cal A}_{0}
and
E_{\cal B}\colon{\cal B}\to{\cal B}_{0}
be conditional expectations. The (algebraic) tensor product map
E_{\cal A}\odot E_{\cal B}\colon{\cal A}\odot{\cal B}\to{\cal A}_{0}\odot{\cal B}_{0}
maps
{\cal A}^{+}\odot{\cal B}^{+}
into
{\cal A}_{0}^{+}\odot{\cal B}_{0}^{+}
and restricts to the identity on
{\cal A}_{0}\odot{\cal B}_{0}
. Hence, if
x\in({\cal A}^{+}\odot{\cal B}^{+})\cap({\cal A}_{0}\odot{\cal B}_{0})
, then
x=(E_{\cal A}\odot E_{\cal B})(x)\in{\cal A}_{0}^{+}\odot{\cal B}_{0}^{+}
.

Suppose next that
{\cal A}_{0}
and
{\cal B}_{0}
are approximately injective, and let
E^{\alpha}_{\cal A}\colon{\cal A}\to{\cal A}_{0}
and
E^{\alpha}_{\cal B}\colon{\cal B}\to{\cal B}_{0}
be approximate conditional expectations. Then

E^{\alpha}_{\cal A}\otimes E^{\alpha}_{\cal B}\colon{\cal A}\otimes{\cal B}\to{\cal A}_{0}\otimes{\cal B}_{0},\qquad E^{\alpha}_{\cal A}\otimes E^{\alpha}_{\cal B}\colon{\cal A}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B}\to{\cal A}_{0}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B}_{0}

are approximate conditional expectations. The cpc maps
E^{\alpha}_{\cal A}\otimes E^{\alpha}_{\cal B}
and
E^{\alpha}_{\cal A}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}E^{\alpha}_{\cal B}
map
{\cal A}^{+}\odot{\cal B}^{+}
into
{\cal A}_{0}^{+}\odot{\cal B}_{0}^{+}
, and hence, by continuity,
{\cal A}^{+}\otimes{\cal B}^{+}
into
{\cal A}_{0}^{+}\otimes{\cal B}_{0}^{+}
, respectively,
{\cal A}^{+}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B}^{+}
into
{\cal A}_{0}^{+}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B}_{0}^{+}
.

Let
x\in({\cal A}^{+}\otimes{\cal B}^{+})\cap({\cal A}_{0}\otimes{\cal B}_{0})
. Then
x=\lim_{\alpha}(E^{\alpha}_{\cal A}\otimes E^{\alpha}_{\cal B})(x)
and
(E^{\alpha}_{\cal A}\otimes E^{\alpha}_{\cal B})(x)\in{\cal A}_{0}^{+}\otimes{\cal B}_{0}^{+}
, for all
\alpha
, so
x\in{\cal A}_{0}^{+}\otimes{\cal B}_{0}^{+}
; likewise for
x\in({\cal A}^{+}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B}^{+})\cap({\cal A}_{0}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B}_{0})
.
∎

We have no examples of inclusions
{\cal A}_{0}\subseteq{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}_{0}\subseteq{\cal B}
of
C^{*}
-algebras where (i), (ii) or (iii) above fails.

Definition 3.13
.

Let
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
be unital
C^{*}
-algebras. For a linear functional
\rho
on
{\cal A}\odot{\cal B}
set
\|\rho\|_{\mathrm{max}}=\sup\{|\rho(x)|:x\in{\cal A}\odot{\cal B},\,\|x\|_{\mathrm{max}}\leq 1\}
, and set

\kappa({\cal A},{\cal B})=\sup\{\|\rho\|_{\mathrm{max}}:\rho\in S({\cal A})\otimes^{*}S({\cal B})\}.

Observe that
S({\cal A}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B})=S({\cal A})\otimes^{*}S({\cal B})
if and only if
\kappa({\cal A},{\cal B})=1
(which by Theorem
3.17
below happens if and only if one of
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
is commutative).

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
be unital
C^{*}
-algebras.

(i)

n\leq\kappa(M_{n}({\mathbb{C}}),M_{n}({\mathbb{C}}))<\infty
, for each integer
n\geq 1
.

(ii)

Let
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
be unital
C^{*}
-algebras both of rank at least
n\geq 2
. Then
\kappa({\cal A},{\cal B})\geq n
.

(iii)

If neither
{\cal A}
nor
{\cal B}
is sub-homogeneous, then
\kappa({\cal A},{\cal B})=\infty
.

(iv)

If
S({\cal A})\otimes^{*}S({\cal B})\subseteq\mathrm{aff}_{r}(S({\cal A})\otimes_{*}S({\cal B}))
, i.e.,
S({\cal A})\otimes^{*}S({\cal B})
is bounded by
S({\cal A})\otimes_{*}S({\cal B})
with a constant
r\geq 0
, then
\kappa({\cal A},{\cal B})\leq 2r+1
.

Proof.

(i). Let
S^{(n)}\in M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}\,\widehat{\odot}\,M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}
be as in Remark
3.5
and
set
\rho=\langle\,\cdot\,,n^{-1}S^{(n)}\rangle
.
Then
\rho\in S(M_{n}({\mathbb{C}}))\otimes^{*}S(M_{n}({\mathbb{C}}))
, cf. Remark
3.3
, since
\mathrm{Tr}(n^{-1}S^{(n)})=1
; and
\|\rho\|_{\mathrm{max}}=\|\rho\|=\|n^{-1}S^{(n)}\|_{1}=n
.
Conversely, each
\rho
in
S(M_{n}({\mathbb{C}}))\otimes^{*}S(M_{n}({\mathbb{C}}))
is a linear functional on the finite dimensional vector space
A(S(M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})))\odot A(S(M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})))=(M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\otimes M_{n}({\mathbb{C}}))_{\mathrm{sa}}
. The claim therefore follows from the fact that any two norms on a finite dimensional vector space are equivalent. (Alternatively, one can use (iv) and Proposition
2.4
.)

(ii).
By Lemma
3.9
there are unital
∗
-homomorphisms of
{\cal E}_{n}
into both
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
whose kernels are not equal to
CM_{n}
. Let
{\cal A}_{0}
and
{\cal B}_{0}
denote the images of
{\cal E}_{n}
in
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
, respectively.
There are unital surjections
\pi_{1}\colon{\cal A}_{0}\to M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
and
\pi_{2}\colon{\cal B}_{0}\to M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
giving a unital surjection

\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}\colon{\cal A}_{0}\odot{\cal B}_{0}\to M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\otimes M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
.

By (i) there is
\rho
in
S(M_{n}({\mathbb{C}}))\otimes^{*}S(M_{n}({\mathbb{C}}))
with
\|\rho\|_{\mathrm{max}}\geq n
(necessarily bounded since
M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\otimes M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
is finite dimensional). The functional
\rho\circ(\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2})
belongs to
S({\cal A}_{0})\otimes^{*}S({\cal B}_{0})
, which again is bounded and satisfies
\|\rho\circ(\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2})\|_{\mathrm{max}}\geq n
. We may therefore extend
\rho\circ(\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2})
to a state on
({\cal A}_{0}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B}_{0},{\cal A}_{0}^{+}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B}_{0}^{+},1_{\cal A}\otimes 1_{\cal B})
, which by Proposition
3.12
(iii) and Proposition
2.1
further can be extended to a state
\rho^{\prime}
on
({\cal A}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B},{\cal A}^{+}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B}^{+},1_{\cal A}\otimes 1_{\cal B})
. The restriction of
\rho^{\prime}
to
({\cal A}\odot{\cal B})_{\mathrm{sa}}
yields a state
\rho^{\prime\prime}
in
S({\cal A})\otimes^{*}S({\cal B})
satisfying
\|\rho^{\prime\prime}\|_{\mathrm{max}}\geq\|\rho\circ(\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2})\|_{\mathrm{max}}\geq n
, thus giving the desired conclusion.

(iii) is an immediate consequence of (ii) and Remark
3.8
.

(iv). If
\rho\in\mathrm{aff}_{r}(S({\cal A})\otimes_{*}S({\cal B}))
, for some
r\geq 0
, then
\rho=(r+1)\rho_{1}-r\rho_{2}
, for some
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in S({\cal A})\otimes_{*}S({\cal B})\subseteq S({\cal A}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B})
, so

\|\rho\|_{\mathrm{max}}\leq(r+1)\|\rho_{1}\|_{\mathrm{max}}+r\|\rho_{2}\|_{\mathrm{max}}\leq 2r+1.

This proves (iv).
∎

We have the following description of
\kappa({\cal A},{\cal B})
in terms of unital positive maps in the case where
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
are matrix algebras:

Theorem 3.15
.

Let
n,m\geq 1
be integers. Then

\kappa(M_{n}({\mathbb{C}}),M_{m}({\mathbb{C}}))=\sup\{\|\Phi\|_{\mathrm{cb}}:\Phi\in\mathrm{UPos}(n,m)\}=\min\{n,m\}.

Proof.

It was shown in Proposition
3.4
that each state in
S(M_{n}({\mathbb{C}}))\otimes^{*}S(M_{m}({\mathbb{C}}))
is of the form
\rho\circ(\Phi\otimes\mathrm{id}_{m})
, for some
\Phi\in\mathrm{UPos}(n,m)
and some state
\rho
on
M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\otimes M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})
. Since the norm of
\Phi\otimes\mathrm{id}_{m}
is attained at self-adjoint (hence normal) elements of
M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\otimes M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})
, we get

\sup\{\|\rho\circ(\Phi\otimes\mathrm{id}_{m})\|:\rho\in S(M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})\otimes M_{m}({\mathbb{C}}))\}=\|\Phi\otimes\mathrm{id}_{m}\|.

Now, use
\|\Phi\otimes\mathrm{id}_{m}\|=\|\Phi\|_{\mathrm{cb}}
, cf.
[
20
, Proposition 8.11]
, to obtain the first equality.

The second equality follows from results in the recent paper
[
3
]
. Indeed, suppose first that
n\leq m
. Then, in the notation of
[
3
]
, we have

\sup\{\|\Phi\|_{\mathrm{cb}}:\Phi\in\mathrm{UPos}(n,m)\}\leq d_{1}(M_{n}({\mathbb{C}}))=n,

where the last equality is
[
3
, Theorem 3.7]
. The reverse inequality follows (for example) from Proposition
3.14
(ii). For the case
m\leq n
, consider the Hilbert space adjoint
\Phi^{*}\colon M_{m}({\mathbb{C}})\to M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
of
\Phi
. Then
\Phi^{*}\in\mathrm{UPos}(m,n)
, so by the argument above we obtain
\|\Phi\|_{\mathrm{cb}}=\|\Phi^{*}\|_{\mathrm{cb}}=m
.
∎

It seems likely that the result of Theorem

above extends to general

-algebras, and we expect that

\kappa({\cal A},{\cal B})=\min\{\mathrm{rank}({\cal A}),\mathrm{rank}({\cal B})\},

for all unital
C^{*}
-algebras
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
.
The inequality
\geq
follows from Proposition
3.14
(ii).

We saw in Proposition
2.4
that

is bounded relatively to

, when

and

are finite dimensional compact convex sets. This does not hold in general when

are infinite dimensional:

Corollary 3.16
.

Let
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
be unital
C^{*}
-algebras.

(i)

If neither
{\cal A}
nor
{\cal B}
is sub-homogeneous, then
S({\cal A})\otimes^{*}S({\cal B})
is not bounded relatively to

.

(ii)

If
{\cal A}\otimes{\cal B}\neq{\cal A}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B}
, then
S({\cal A})\otimes^{*}S({\cal B})\nsubseteq\mathrm{aff}(S({\cal A})\otimes_{*}S({\cal B}))
.

Proof.

Item (i) is an immediate consequence of Proposition
3.14
(iii) and (iv).

(ii). Let

be the (non-zero) kernel of the canonical surjection

on

We state here our main result about tensor product of state spaces of
C^{*}
-algebras.
Note in particular that the theorem implies that
S({\cal A})\otimes_{*}S({\cal B})=S({\cal A})\otimes^{*}S({\cal B})
if and only if one of
S({\cal A})
and
S({\cal B})
is a Choquet simplex, thus confirming Barker’s conjecture for compact convex sets arising as the state space of
C^{*}
-algebras. The theorem also says that entanglement (both of states and of positive elements) always exists in the presence of non-commutativity.

Theorem 3.17
.

Let
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
be unital
C^{*}
-algebras.

(i)

S({\cal A})
is a Choquet simplex if and only if
{\cal A}
is commutative.

(ii)

If one of
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
is commutative, then we have equalities in (
3.4
), i.e.,

S({\cal A})\otimes_{*}S({\cal B})=S_{*}({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})=S({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})=S({\cal A}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B})=S({\cal A})\otimes^{*}S({\cal B}).

(iii)

If both
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
are non-commutative, then the two inclusions

S_{*}({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})\subset S({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B}),\qquad S({\cal A}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B})\subset S({\cal A})\otimes^{*}S({\cal B})

in (
3.4
) are strict. In particular,
S({\cal A})\otimes_{*}S({\cal B})\neq S({\cal A})\otimes^{*}S({\cal B})
.

(iv)

One of
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
is commutative if and only if
{\cal A}^{+}\otimes\,{\cal B}^{+}=({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})^{+}
if and only if

{\cal A}^{+}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}\,{\cal B}^{+}=({\cal A}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B})^{+}
.

Proof.

If
{\cal A}
is commutative with spectrum
X
, then
S({\cal A})=\mathrm{Prob}(X)
is a Bauer simplex, and hence in particular a Choquet simplex. Thus, by Theorem
2.7
,
S({\cal A})\otimes_{*}S({\cal B})
and
S({\cal A})\otimes^{*}S({\cal B})
are equal if one of
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
is commutative, which in turns implies that (ii) holds, cf. (
3.4
).

As remarked below Definition
3.13
, if
\kappa({\cal A},{\cal B})>1
, then
S({\cal A}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B})\neq S({\cal A})\otimes^{*}S({\cal B})
. Together with Proposition
3.14
this shows that
S({\cal A}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B})\neq S({\cal A})\otimes^{*}S({\cal B})
, and hence that
S({\cal A})\otimes_{*}S({\cal B})\neq S({\cal A})\otimes^{*}S({\cal B})
, when both
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
are non-commutative. By Theorem
2.7
, this also implies that
S({\cal A})
is not a Choquet simplex when
{\cal A}
is non-commutative, thus completing the proof of (i).

We proceed to show that
S_{*}({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})\neq S({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})
when both
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
are non-commutative, following a variation of the proof of Proposition
3.14
(ii) and Remark
3.5
. Let
n\geq 2
be such that
\mathrm{rank}({\cal A})\geq n
and
\mathrm{rank}({\cal B})\geq n
, and let
{\cal A}_{0}\subseteq{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}_{0}\subseteq{\cal B}
be as in the proof of Proposition
3.14
(ii). We then have

{\cal A}_{0}\cong\{f\in C(I,M_{n}):f(0)\in{\mathbb{C}}\cdot 1_{n}\},\qquad{\cal B}_{0}\cong\{f\in C(J,M_{n}):f(0)\in{\mathbb{C}}\cdot 1_{n}\},

for some non-empty closed subsets
I
and
J
of
[0,1]
, cf. the proof of Lemma
3.9
. We may identify
{\cal A}_{0}\otimes{\cal B}_{0}
with a sub-
C^{*}
-algebra of
C(I\times J,M_{n}\otimes M_{n})
. For each
(s,t)\in I\times J
consider the
∗
-homomorphisms:

\pi_{s}^{1}\colon{\cal A}_{0}\to M_{n},\qquad\pi_{t}^{2}\colon{\cal B}_{0}\to M_{n},\qquad\pi_{(s,t)}=\pi^{1}_{s}\otimes\pi^{2}_{t}\colon{\cal A}_{0}\otimes{\cal B}_{0}\to M_{n}\otimes M_{n},

given by point evaluation. These maps are surjective when both
s
and
t
are non-zero. Define
X\in{\cal A}_{0}\otimes{\cal B}_{0}
by

X(s,t)=stS
, where
S=S^{(n)}\in M_{n}\otimes M_{n}
is as in Remark
3.5
. Since
\pi_{s,t}(X)=stS
, for all
(s,t)\in I\times J
, we conclude that
X
is non-positive.

We claim that
\rho(X)\geq 0
, for all states
\rho\in S_{*}({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})
. As
X
is non-positive, this shows that
S_{*}({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})\neq S({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})
. The restriction of
\rho
to
{\cal A}_{0}\otimes{\cal B}_{0}
belongs to

S_{*}({\cal A}_{0}\otimes{\cal B}_{0})
, cf. Proposition
3.10
, so it suffices to show that
\rho(X)\geq 0
, for all states
\rho\in S_{*}({\cal A}_{0}\otimes{\cal B}_{0})
.
To this end it suffices to show that
(\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2})(X)\geq 0
, for all pure states
\rho_{1}
on
{\cal A}_{0}
and
\rho_{2}
on
{\cal B}_{0}
. Being pure states, there exist
s\in I
,
t\in J
and (pure) states
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
on
M_{n}
such that
\rho_{1}=\sigma_{1}\circ\pi_{s}^{1}
and
\rho_{2}=\sigma_{2}\circ\pi_{t}^{2}
. Now,

(\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2})(X)=(\sigma_{1}\otimes\sigma_{2})(\pi_{s,t}(S))=st(\sigma_{1}\otimes\sigma_{2})(S)\geq 0,

by Remark
3.5
. This completes the proofs of (i), (ii) and (iii).

To prove (iv), suppose first that
{\cal A}
is commutative, in which case we may assume that
{\cal A}=C(X)
, for some compact Hausdorff space
X
. Then
{\cal A}\otimes{\cal B}=C(X,{\cal B})
, and each
f\in C(X,{\cal B})
can be approximated by elements of the form
f_{0}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\varphi_{j}\otimes b_{j}
, where
0\leq\varphi_{j}\leq 1
,
\sum\varphi_{j}=1
, and
b_{j}=f(x_{j})
, for some
x_{j}\in X
. In particular,
f_{0}\in C(X)^{+}\otimes{\cal B}^{+}
when
f
is positive.

Suppose next that both
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
are non-commutative, both with rank
\geq n
, for some
n\geq 2
. Let
{\cal A}_{0}\subseteq{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}_{0}\subseteq{\cal B}
be as in the proof of Proposition
3.14
(ii), and choose surjective
∗
-homomorphisms
\pi_{1}\colon{\cal A}_{0}\to M_{n}
and
\pi_{2}\colon{\cal B}_{0}\to M_{n}
. Then
\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}\colon{\cal A}_{0}\otimes{\cal B}_{0}\to M_{n}\otimes M_{n}
is a surjective
∗
-homomorphism. (All involved
C^{*}
-algebras are nuclear, so the max and the min norms are the same.)
We know that
H=H^{(n)}\in(M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\otimes M_{n}({\mathbb{C}}))^{+}
is positive but does not belong to

M_{n}^{+}\odot M_{n}^{+}=M_{n}^{+}\otimes M_{n}^{+}
, cf. Proposition
3.4
and Remark
3.5
. Lift
H
to a positive element
\tilde{H}
in
{\cal A}_{0}\otimes{\cal B}_{0}
along
\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}
. Then
\tilde{H}
does not belong to
{\cal A}_{0}^{+}\otimes{\cal B}_{0}^{+}
(since
\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2}
maps
{\cal A}_{0}^{+}\otimes{\cal B}_{0}^{+}
into
M_{n}^{+}\otimes M_{n}^{+}
). It follows that
{\cal A}_{0}^{+}\otimes{\cal B}_{0}^{+}\neq({\cal A}_{0}\otimes{\cal B}_{0})^{+}
. Now use Proposition
3.12
(ii) and (iii) to complete the proof.
∎

Remark 3.18
.

It is an immediate consequence of the second part of Theorem
3.17
(iii) above (or of part (iv)) that
{\cal A}^{+}\odot{\cal B}^{+}\subsetneq({\cal A}\odot{\cal B})^{+}
, when both
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
are non-commutative. This inclusion is strict also in cases where both
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
are commutative, e.g., with
{\cal A}={\cal B}=C([0,1])
.
Indeed, with the identification
C([0,1])\otimes C([0,1])=C([0,1]^{2})
, the function
f(x,y)=x^{2}+y^{2}-2xy
belongs to
(C([0,1])\odot C([0,1]))^{+}
but not to
C([0,1])^{+}\odot C([0,1])^{+}
. One way of seeing the latter claim is to observe that the zero-set of any function
g
in
C([0,1])^{+}\odot C([0,1])^{+}
must be a finite union of sets of the form
I\times J
, where
I,J\subseteq[0,1]
are closed sets.

As mentioned earlier, part (i) of Theorem
3.17
is an old well-known fact, included here to put this classical fact into the context of entanglement and tensor products of compact convex sets. We present below a more traditional route to this result. Recall for this purpose that a compact convex set
K
is a Choquet simplex if and only if the ordered real vector space
A(K)
of its affine functions has the Riesz Interpolation Property. Accordingly, the state space
S({\cal A})
of a unital
C^{*}
-algebra
{\cal A}
is a Choquet simplex if and only if
{\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}}
, equipped with the usual order on
C^{*}
-algebras, has the Riesz Interpolation Property.

Proposition 3.19
.

If
{\cal A}
is a non-commutative
C^{*}
-algebra, then
{\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}}
equipped with the usual order does not satisfy the Riesz Interpolation Property.

Proof.

Let
{\cal A}
be a non-commutative
C^{*}
-algebra. We find self-adjoint elements
a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2}
in
{\cal A}
such that
a_{i}\leq b_{j}
, for
i,j=1,2
, and for which there is no self-adjoint
c\in{\cal A}
satisfying
a_{i}\leq c\leq b_{j}
, for for
i,j=1,2
.

By Lemma
3.9
there is a non-zero
∗
-homomorphism
\varphi\colon CM_{2}\to{\cal A}
. Let
(e_{ij})
denote the standard matrix units for
M_{2}({\mathbb{C}})(=M_{2})
. Set

f=t\otimes\big({\textstyle{-\frac{2}{3}}}(e_{11}+e_{22})+(e_{12}+e_{21})\big)\in CM_{2},

and set
a_{1}=0
,
a_{2}=\varphi(f)
,
b_{j}=\varphi(t\otimes e_{jj})
, for
j=1,2
, where, for each
a\in M_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
we let
t\otimes a
denote the function
t\mapsto ta
in
CM_{2}
.

We claim that
a_{2}\leq b_{j}
, for
j=1,2
, while
a_{2}\nleq 0
. To this end, note first that

\begin{pmatrix}-2/3&\;1\\
\;1&-2/3\end{pmatrix}\;\leq\;\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\begin{pmatrix}-2/3&\;1\\
\;1&-2/3\end{pmatrix}\;\leq\;\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad\begin{pmatrix}-2/3&\;1\\
\;1&-2/3\end{pmatrix}\;\nleq\;0.

Let
\pi_{s}\colon CM_{2}\to M_{2}
be evaluation at
s\in[0,1]
. Using the inequalities above,
we see that
\pi_{s}(f)\leq\pi_{s}(t\otimes e_{jj})
and
\pi_{s}(f)\nleq 0
, for
j=1,2
and
s\in[0,1]
. This shows that
f\leq t\otimes e_{jj}
in
CM_{2}
, and hence that
a_{2}\leq b_{j}
, for
j=1,2
, and it also shows that
f
is non-negative in each (non-zero) quotient of
CM_{2}
, and hence that
a_{2}
is non-negative.

Suppose now that
a_{i}\leq c\leq b_{j}
, for
i,j=1,2
. Then,
0=a_{1}\leq c\leq b_{j}
, for
j=1,2
, which implies
c=0
(because
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
are orthogonal); but then we cannot have
a_{2}\leq c
.
∎

4
Tensor products of trace spaces of
C^{*}
-algebras

Let
{\cal A}
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra, and let
T({\cal A})
denote the set of tracial states on
{\cal A}
. It is known that
T({\cal A})
, if non-empty, is a Choquet simplex, see
[
22
]
and
[
6
]
. We refer to
[
11
]
and
[
21
]
for complete chacterizations of which
C^{*}
-algebras admit tracial states. The extreme boundary of
T({\cal A})
precisely consists of “factorial traces”, i.e., tracial states
\tau
on
{\cal A}
for which
\pi_{\tau}({\cal A})^{\prime\prime}
is a factor.

Let
i[{\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}},{\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}}]
denote the closed subspace of
{\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}}
spanned by commutators
i(xy-yx)
, with
x,y\in{\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}}
, and let
q\colon{\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}}\to{\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}}/i[{\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}},{\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}}]
denote the quotient mapping. (See also
[
9
]
, where traces were studied using this space realized as
{\cal A}^{q}
, a quotient of
{\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}}
with respect to a specific equivalence relation.) A bounded hermitian functional on a
C^{*}
-algebra
{\cal A}
is tracial if and only if it factors through
q
; for such a functional
\tau
, let
\widehat{\tau}
denote its descent to
q({\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}})
.
Identify
A(T({\cal A}))
with
q({\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}})
to obtain

T({\cal A})\cong S(q({\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}}),q({\cal A}^{+}),q(1_{\cal A}))=\{\widehat{\tau}:\tau\in T({\cal A})\}.

The tensor products of the trace simplexes of two
C^{*}
-algebras
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
are accordingly given by

\displaystyle T({\cal A})\otimes_{*}T({\cal B})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(q({\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}})\odot q({\cal B}_{\mathrm{sa}}),q({\cal A}^{+})\,\widehat{\odot}\,q({\cal B}^{+}),q(1_{\cal A})\otimes q(1_{\cal B})),

(4.1)

\displaystyle T({\cal A})\otimes^{*}T({\cal B})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(q({\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}})\odot q({\cal B}_{\mathrm{sa}}),q({\cal A}^{+})\odot q({\cal B}^{+}),q(1_{\cal A})\otimes q(1_{\cal B})).

(4.2)

Since
T({\cal A})
and
T({\cal B})
are Choquet simplexes, the two tensor products above agree, cf. Theorem
2.7
, and we denote them both by
T({\cal A})\otimes T({\cal B})
.

A linear functional on
{\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}}\odot{\cal B}_{\mathrm{sa}}
descends to a linear functional on
q({\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}})\odot q({\cal B}_{\mathrm{sa}})
if and only if it vanishes on the kernel of
q\otimes q\colon{\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}}\odot{\cal B}_{\mathrm{sa}}\to q({\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}})\odot q({\cal B}_{\mathrm{sa}})
if and only if it is tracial. For the latter, use that
[a\otimes b,c\otimes d]=ca\otimes[b,d]+[a,c]\otimes bd
, when
a,c\in{\cal A}
and
b,d\in{\cal B}
, to see that

\mathrm{ker}(q\otimes q)=i[{\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}},{\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}}]\odot{\cal B}_{\mathrm{sa}}+{\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}}\odot i[{\cal B}_{\mathrm{sa}},{\cal B}_{\mathrm{sa}}]=i[{\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}}\odot{\cal B}_{\mathrm{sa}},{\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}}\odot{\cal B}_{\mathrm{sa}}].

This shows that each pair
\tau_{\cal A}\in T({\cal A})
and
\tau_{\cal B}\in T({\cal B})
yields a linear functional
\widehat{\tau}_{\cal A}\otimes\widehat{\tau}_{\cal B}
on
q({\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}})\odot q({\cal B}_{\mathrm{sa}})
. Conversely, each trace in
T({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})
or in
T({\cal A}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B})
restricts to a linear functional on
{\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}}\odot{\cal B}_{\mathrm{sa}}
which further descends to a linear functional on
q({\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}})\odot q({\cal B}_{\mathrm{sa}})
.

In analogy with the corresponding definition for state spaces, consider the set

T_{*}({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})=\overline{{\mathrm{conv}}\{\tau_{\cal A}\otimes\tau_{\cal B}:\tau_{\cal A}\in T({\cal A}),\tau_{\cal B}\in T({\cal B})\}},

which is a weak
∗
closed convex subset of
T({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})
, the trace simplex of the minimal tensor product
{\cal A}\otimes{\cal B}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
be unital
C^{*}
-algebras both admitting at least one tracial state. Then

T({\cal A})\otimes T({\cal B})=T_{*}({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})=T({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})=T({\cal A}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B}).

Proof.

We show that

T({\cal A})\otimes_{*}T({\cal B})=T_{*}({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})\subseteq T({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})\subseteq T({\cal A}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B})\subseteq T({\cal A})\otimes^{*}T({\cal B}),

and then use that
T({\cal A})\otimes_{*}T({\cal B})=T({\cal A})\otimes^{*}T({\cal B})
, cf. Theorem
2.7
, to finish the proof.

By Proposition
2.6
(iii), and following the discussion above,

T({\cal A})\otimes_{*}T({\cal B})=\overline{{\mathrm{conv}}}\{\widehat{\tau}_{\cal A}\otimes\widehat{\tau}_{\cal B}:\tau_{\cal A}\in T({\cal A}),\;\tau_{\cal B}\in T({\cal B})\}=T_{*}({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B}),

where the last equality is via the descent map
\tau_{\cal A}\otimes\tau_{\cal B}\mapsto\widehat{\tau}_{\cal A}\otimes\widehat{\tau}_{\cal B}
.

The inclusion
T_{*}({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})\subseteq T({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})
holds by definition, and
T({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})\subseteq T({\cal A}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B})
because
{\cal A}\otimes{\cal B}
is a quotient of
{\cal A}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B}
.

Finally, as in the proof of Lemma
3.2
,
we can identify traces on
{\cal A}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B}
with normalized tracial functionals on
{\cal A}_{\mathrm{sa}}\odot{\cal B}_{\mathrm{sa}}
which are positive on
({\cal A}\odot{\cal B})^{+}
. Since
({\cal A}\odot{\cal B})^{+}\supseteq{\cal A}^{+}\odot{\cal B}^{+}
, we obtain the last inclusion above.
∎

The identities
T_{*}({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})=T({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})=T({\cal A}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B})
appearing in Theorem
4.1
are well-known. We can think of the former as saying that traces cannot be entangled! The latter identity was observed by Kirchberg in
[
15
]
(included in his proof of (B4)
\Rightarrow
(B3)), see also
[
7
, Exercise 13.3.3]
and
[
16
]
. It can be rephrased by saying that every tracial state on
{\cal A}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B}
factors through
{\cal A}\otimes{\cal B}
, i.e., vanishes on the kernel of the caononical map
{\cal A}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B}\to{\cal A}\otimes{\cal B}
.

One can prove the two identities above directly as follows: It suffices to show that
\partial_{e}T({\cal A}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B})\subseteq T_{*}({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})
.
Let
\tau\in\partial_{e}T({\cal A}\otimes_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal B})
, and let
\tau_{\cal A}\in T({\cal A})
and
\tau_{\cal B}\in T({\cal B})
be the “marginal distributions” of
\tau
, cf. (
2.5
) and (
2.6
). As
\tau
is factorial (being extremal), it follows that
\pi_{\tau}({\cal A}\otimes 1_{\cal B})^{\prime\prime}\cong\pi_{\tau_{\cal A}}({\cal A})^{\prime\prime}
and
\pi_{\tau}(1_{\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})^{\prime\prime}\cong\pi_{\tau_{\cal B}}({\cal B})^{\prime\prime}
are factors. Hence
\tau_{\cal A}
and
\tau_{\cal B}
are factorial and therefore extremal. But then
\tau=\tau_{\cal A}\otimes\tau_{\cal B}\in T_{*}({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})
by Lemma
2.2
.

Theorem
4.1
provides the new information that
T({\cal A})\otimes T({\cal B})=T({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})
, which in turn yields the corollary below. It also offers a quite different route to the two identites discussed above, in terms of entanglement and tensor products of compact convex sets.

Combining Theorem
4.1
with Example
2.10
we get the following corollary below, valid also for the max-tensor product of the
C^{*}
-algebras. Recall that we refer to a simplex as being trivial if it is a singleton.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
{\cal A}
and
{\cal B}
be unital
C^{*}
-algebras with non-empty trace simplexes. Then:

(i)

T({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})
is a Bauer simplex if and only if both
T({\cal A})
and
T({\cal B})
are Bauer simplexes,

(ii)

T({\cal A}\otimes{\cal B})
is the Poulsen simplex if and only if both
T({\cal A})
and
T({\cal B})
are the Poulsen simplex, or one of
T({\cal A})
and
T({\cal B})
is trivial and the other is the Poulsen simplex.

Using the notion of infinite tensor products of simplexes defined at the end of Section
2
we can extend the results above to infinite tensor products (also valid for the max-tensor product of the
C^{*}
-algebras).

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
{\cal A}_{1},{\cal A}_{2},{\cal A}_{3},\dots
be a sequence of unital
C^{*}
-algebras each with non-empty trace simplex. Then
T(\bigotimes_{k\geq 1}{\cal A}_{k})=\bigotimes_{k\geq 1}T({\cal A}_{k})
.

Proof.

The infinite tensor product
\bigotimes_{k\geq 1}{\cal A}_{k}
is the inductive limit
\varinjlim\bigotimes_{k=1}^{n}{\cal A}_{k}
, where each
\bigotimes_{k=1}^{n}{\cal A}_{k}
is embedded in
\bigotimes_{k=1}^{n+1}{\cal A}_{k}

via the map
x\mapsto x\otimes 1_{{\cal A}_{n+1}}
. It follows that the trace simplex of
\bigotimes_{k\geq 1}{\cal A}_{k}
is the inverse limit

\textstyle{T({\cal A}_{1})}
\textstyle{T({\cal A}_{1}\otimes{\cal A}_{2})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{T({\cal A}_{1}\otimes{\cal A}_{2}\otimes{\cal A}_{3})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\cdots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{T\big(\bigotimes_{k\geq 1}{\cal A}_{k}\big).\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

By Theorem
4.1
, for each
n\geq 1
,
T(\bigotimes_{k=1}^{n}{\cal A}_{k})=\bigotimes_{k=1}^{n}T({\cal A}_{k})
, and the connecting map
\bigotimes_{k=1}^{n+1}T({\cal A}_{k})\to\bigotimes_{k=1}^{n}T({\cal A}_{k})
induced by the connecting map
\bigotimes_{k=1}^{n}{\cal A}_{k}\to\bigotimes_{k=1}^{n+1}{\cal A}_{k}
is the precisely the map
\pi_{n}
from (
2.7
). This proves the claim.
∎

The result below follows from Theorem
4.3
and Example
2.12
, and is valid also for the max-tensor product on the
C^{*}
-algebras.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
{\cal A}_{1},{\cal A}_{2},{\cal A}_{3},\dots
be a sequence of unital
C^{*}
-algebras each with non-empty trace simple. Then:

(i)

T(\bigotimes_{k\geq 1}{\cal A}_{k})
is a Bauer simplex if and only if
T({\cal A}_{k})
is a Bauer simplex, for each
k
.

(ii)

T(\bigotimes_{k\geq 1}{\cal A}_{k})
is the Poulsen simplex if and only if
T({\cal A}_{k})
is the Poulsen simplex or trivial, for each
k\geq 1
, and not all
T({\cal A}_{k})
are trivial.

(iii)

The trace simplex of the full group
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(\Gamma)
of a group
\Gamma
arising as the direct sum of a (finite or infinite family)
(\Gamma_{i})_{i\in I}
of countable discrete groups, is the Poulsen simplex if and only if the trace simplex of each
C^{*}(\Gamma_{i})
is the Poulsen simplex.

Part (iii) above implies in particular that the set of discrete groups
\Gamma
for which the trace simplex of
C^{*}(\Gamma)
is the Poulsen simplex is closed under (finite and infinite) direct products. (Note that the trace simplex of the full group
C^{*}
-algebra of a group is non-trivial unless the group itself is trivial, which we tacitly assume is not the case in (iii).) Corollary
4.2
(ii) and Corollary
4.4
(ii) and (iii) relate to recent works,
[
19
,
13
]
showing that the trace simplex of the full group
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(\Gamma)
is the Poulsen simplex for a large class of groups
\Gamma
, including free groups, and that
T({\cal A})
likewise is the Poulsen simplex for many universal free product
C^{*}
-algebras.

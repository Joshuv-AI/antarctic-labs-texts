Characteristics and causes of regional differentiation of ecosystem services supply and demand in Sichuan Province, China
Authors: Xinzhuo Fang, Fanni Ye
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractEcosystem services significantly impact human well-being and regional sustainable development, and clarifying the spatially heterogeneous characteristics of their supply and demand and the factors influencing them is the key to implementing sustainable development strategies. This study employs the InVEST model to quantitatively assess the supply and demand status of four primary ecosystem services in Sichuan Province: carbon sequestration(CS), grain production(GP), water production(WP), and soil conservation(SC), and analyze the spatial correlation of ecosystem services to identify different bundles of ecosystem services, thus presenting the regional heterogeneity characteristics, and finally explores the dominant factors in combination with the optimal parameterized geographical detector, taking into account the unique geographic location of Sichuan Province. The results showed that: (1) From 2010 to 2022, CS supply in Sichuan Province increased by 0.07 × 10¹⁰ t, GP supply increased by 3.28 × 10¹² t, WP supply decreased by 3.38 × 10¹² t, and SC supply decreased by 1.26 × 10¹² t. CS demand increased by 7.32 × 10¹¹ t, GP demand decreased by 3.47 × 10¹³ t, WP increased by 1.47 × 10¹³ t, and SC demand decreased by 2.25 × 10⁹ t; (2) The supply-demand dynamics of ecosystem services reveal a pattern of “high supply and high demand” in the eastern regions and “low supply and low demand” in the western regions. On the supply side, trade-offs predominate across the four categories of ecosystem services in eastern and western Sichuan. On the demand side, strong synergistic effects are evident across all categories; (3) During the study period, the distribution and percentage of four ecosystem service bundles exhibited significant changes. On the supply side, the area covered by the GP-WP bundle(S2) in eastern Sichuan decreased by 4%, while the CS-SC bundle(S3) increased by 3%. In western Sichuan, the CS-SC bundle(S3) area rose by 13%, while the WP bundle(S4) decreased by 14%. On the demand side, the area covered by the SC demand bundle(C1) in eastern Sichuan decreased by 4%, and the low-demand bundle(C3) area in western Sichuan decreased by 1%; (4) The supply of ecosystem services in eastern and western regions is generally more influenced by natural landscape factors, while demand is closely linked to socioeconomic conditions. Furthermore, the combined effects of interacting factors exert a far greater influence on ecosystem services than individual factors alone. The study can provide a scientific basis for the ecological function of zoning and the development of differentiated control strategies in Sichuan Province.Keywords: Ecosystem services supply and demand, Sichuan province, Regional differences, InVEST model, Optimal parameters geographical detectorSubject terms: Ecosystem ecology, Ecosystem services

Introduction

As an important link between the natural environment and human well-being, ecosystem services support regional sustainable development and ecological security1,2.With the aggravation of global climate change and the enhancement of anthropogenic disturbances, the relationship between the supply of and demand for ecosystem services is characterized by significant spatial heterogeneity3, and its dynamic balance has become a research hotspot in ecology and geography4. The 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development of the United Nations has explicitly mentioned the need to promote the equitable distribution of resources through ecosystem services management5, and China’s strategy of “building an ecological civilization” has listed the optimization of ecosystem services supply and demand as a core task of territorial spatial planning6. Sichuan Province is located in the ecological barrier area of the upper reaches of the Yangtze River, with complex and diverse topography and prominent ecological functions7. However, there are significant developmental differences between the eastern and western parts of the province, and the co-existence of high-intensity urbanization and ecological vulnerability has led to increasingly acute conflicts between the supply of and demand for ecosystem services8. Therefore, analyzing the spatial differentiation pattern and driving mechanism of ecosystem services in Sichuan Province from the perspective of matching supply and demand is significant for realizing the coordinated development of regional ecological protection and socioeconomic development9,10.

Research on ecosystem service supply and demand (ESSD) initially focused primarily on conceptual definition and framework refinement11. Subsequently, numerous scholars shifted their attention to ecosystem service supply studies12, with some employing ecological models such as InVEST, SoIVES, and ARIES to quantify supply. This methodology offers advantages in assessment accuracy and data accessibility, establishing itself as the mainstream research paradigm13. However, research on ecosystem service demand remains relatively scarce. In recent years, trends in ecosystem service studies have increasingly emphasized understanding the interrelationships between ecosystem services and socioeconomic systems. Therefore, research on supply-demand relationships has become a hot topic. Methods such as the supply demand ratio14, supply demand matching15, and supply demand coupling coordination16 are employed to visually illustrate supply-demand gaps. Spatial autocorrelation17, self-organizing mapping networks (SOM)18, and spatial regression models19 are utilized to identify spatial characteristics of supply and demand. and employing correlation analysis20, geographically weighted regression models21, and Bayesian network models22 to evaluate trade-off synergies between supply and demand. Research scales encompass cities23, river basins24, provinces25, nations26, and the global level27. Overall, research methods and content concerning the supply and demand of ecosystem services have become quite extensive, but to promote the sustainable development of ecosystems, focusing solely on the supply-demand relationship is insufficient. It is also necessary to explore the underlying driving mechanisms to promote ecosystem service management and enhance human well-being.

Extensive research indicates that ESSD is influenced by multiple factors including land use28, natural environmental conditions29, and socioeconomic conditions30. Currently, many scholars employ linear regression models to identify ESSD drivers, with commonly used methods including logistic regression31, principal component analysis32, structural equation modeling33, and redundancy analysis34. However, ecosystems exhibit adaptive, nonlinear, and multistable characteristics, and traditional linear models have certain limitations in identifying the complex response mechanisms of ESSD. In contrast, geographic detectors possess unique advantages in detecting spatial heterogeneity and identifying the effects of interactions between factors on the dependent variable35,36. However, traditional geographic detectors exhibit significant subjectivity during spatial stratification, which can compromise the rigor and accuracy of the final results. Song et al.37 developed the optimal parameters-based geographical detector (OPGD) based on the geographical detector. As a cutting-edge spatial statistics method, the OPGD model achieves optimal spatial data discretization and scientifically sound zoning, thereby enhancing the identification of driving factors underlying the supply and demand of ecosystem services38.

As the economic hub of southwest China and a vital ecological barrier in the upper Yangtze River basin, Sichuan Province must ensure adequate ecosystem service provision to meet residents’ welfare needs. However, significant regional disparities exist between eastern and western Sichuan in terms of geographical conditions, socioeconomic development levels, and population distribution. These differences can easily lead to imbalances in ecosystem service supply and demand across regions, thereby compromising ecosystem stability and sustainability. In recent years, Sichuan’s population and socioeconomic development have continued to grow, driving an increasing demand for ecosystem services among residents. Accelerated urbanization has further intensified the occupation of ecological resources, placing greater pressure on the supply and demand of ecosystem services. Therefore, conducting a regional analysis of ecosystem service supply and demand in Sichuan, clarifying its driving mechanisms, and formulating management strategies conducive to achieving supply-demand balance and sustainable development hold significant practical importance. This study comprehensively employs the InVEST model and socioeconomic data to assess the supply and demand of ecosystem services in Sichuan Province. Based on this analysis, it examines the matching status between supply and demand as well as the trade-off synergies. Subsequently, the SOFM model identifies distinct ecosystem service bundles. In the end, by integrating optimal parameter geographic detectors, the study identifies key driving factors to provide decision-making support and methodological insights for regional ecological management and sustainable development. Overall, the objectives of this study are: (1) to assess the supply and demand status of ecosystem services in Sichuan Province; (2) to analyze their typical characteristics and regional differences between eastern and western areas; (3) Investigate the primary factors influencing regional variations in the supply and demand of ecosystem services; and (4) to provide recommendations for regional ecological management and development.

Methods and material

Study area

Sichuan Province is located in the hinterland of southwestern China (longitude 97°21′ ~ 108°33′, latitude 26°03′ ~ 34°19′), with a total area of 486,000 square kilometers, and is situated in the transition zone between the ecological barrier area of the upper reaches of the Yangtze River and the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau (Fig. 1). Basin and mountainous areas in eastern Sichuan with gentle terrain and dense river networks; plateau and mountainous areas in western Sichuan, with an average elevation of more than 3,000 m above sea level. Climate differentiation is apparent: the eastern part of the subtropical humid monsoon climate has an average annual temperature of 14 ~ 19 ℃, with precipitation of 800 ~ 1,200 mm; the western part of the plateau cold temperate climate, the average annual temperature is lower than 10 ℃, precipitation 400 ~ 800 mm. Land use types are diverse, with cropland and construction land dominating in the east, the main grain-producing area, and urban agglomeration concentration; forests and grasslands cover a wide range in the west. The eastern region, as the core component of the Chengdu-Chongqing Twin Cities Economic Circle, concentrates more than 75% of the province’s population and 84% of its GDP, forming a dense urban agglomeration dominated by manufacturing and service industries; while the western region is dominated by eco-tourism and pastoralism, with a population density of less than 1/10th that of the east, and with a weaker intensity of economic activity.

Methods

Supply and demand assessment of ecosystem services

Sichuan Province is characterized by significant geographic differences, with a wide range of terrain heights, mainly plateaus and high mountain valleys in the west, and plains, hills and parallel valleys in the east. Spanning the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau and the Hengduan Mountains, it has lush forests and vegetation, favorable conditions for soil and water resources, and a large area of arable land, making it the only major grain-producing area in the western region, as well as being an important water conservation area in the upper reaches of the Yangtze River and an important water supply area in the upper reaches of the Yellow River, and having outstanding ecological functions in terms of water conservation and soil and water preservation. Carbon sequestration service in Sichuan Province has a direct impact on the adaptive capacity to climate change, grain service is related to grain security and the sustainability of arable land resources, while water production service and soil conservation service are directly related to the soil and water conservation capacity of the Yangtze River Basin as well as the prevention and control of ecological risks. Therefore, Therefore, this study primarily evaluates four major ecosystem services in Sichuan Province, including carbon sequestration, grain provision, water production, and soil conservation (Table 2). A total of 58,748 grid cells were input as training samples into the SOFM network for training. The number of training iterations was uniformly set at 10,000, with the number of classes sequentially set from 2 to 6. Following evaluation of different clustering numbers, four distinct categories were selected as the final clustering classification based on the principles of regional conjugacy, comprehensiveness, and dominant factors. The SOFM neural network demonstrated significant advantages in identifying ecosystem service combinations. As an unsupervised learning algorithm, it excels at handling non-linear relationships and can automatically recognise data patterns without requiring a predefined number of categories. Compared to K-means, SOFM requires no initial cluster centres and exhibits insensitivity to initial values; relative to principal component analysis, it dispenses with linearity assumptions while preserving greater original information; and compared to hierarchical analysis, SOFM avoids subjective weighting and demonstrates superior efficiency in processing large datasets. These characteristics enable SOFM to objectively and accurately reveal the characteristics of service combinations and their spatial distribution patterns when analysing complex, high-dimensional ecosystem service data.

Results

Changes in supply and demand for ecosystem services

The spatial distribution of the four ecosystem services has obvious regional heterogeneity, in terms of carbon sequestration (Fig. 2), the western part of Sichuan has a larger area covered by forest vegetation and is less disturbed by human activities, making it a high carbon sequestration area. In 2010 to 2022, the carbon sequestration capacity of Sichuan Province as a whole shows an increasing trend, from 11.54 × 10¹⁰ t to 11.61 × 10¹⁰ t, and is concentrated in eastern Sichuan. Due to the good geographical advantages in eastern Sichuan, arable land as an important source of grain production is distributed in a large area in the eastern Sichuan region, which leads to the grain supply capacity of the eastern Sichuan region is significantly higher than that of the western Sichuan region, Sichuan Province’s grain supply increased from 31.83 × 10¹² t to 35.10 × 10¹2 t. The supply capacity of water production services has declined, decreasing from 26.95 × 10¹² t to 23.57 × 10¹² t. Regions with high supply of water production services are concentrated in the eastern part of the country. In terms of soil conservation, the soil conservation capacity of the western Sichuan region and the eastern Sichuan region are both lower during the study period, The overall trend is downward, decreasing from 19.03 × 10¹² t to 17.77 × 10¹² t.

Supply and change of ecosystem services in Sichuan Province.

The high demand areas for carbon sequestration service, grain service and water production service in Sichuan Province from 2010 to 2022 are partly distributed in a cluster in the Chengdu City-centered urban agglomeration in eastern Sichuan, while the rest are distributed in a point-like manner in the districts, counties and settlements in eastern Sichuan (Fig. 3). Demand for carbon sequestration service increased from 17.04 × 10¹¹ t to 24.36 × 10¹¹ t, an increase of 7.32 × 10¹¹ t; demand for food service decreased from 12.84 × 10¹³ t to 9.37 × 10¹³ t, a decrease of 3.47 × 10¹³ t; water production demand service increased from 19.45 × 10¹³ t to 20.93 × 10¹³ t, an increase of 1.47 × 10¹³ t. The distribution of demand for soil conservation service is relatively special, from 14.11 × 10⁹ t in 2010 to 11.86 × 10⁹ t, a decrease of 2.25 × 10⁹ t. with high demand areas mainly located in the mountains and hills at the junction of the eastern Sichuan region and the western Sichuan region, as well as in areas prone to soil erosion; the western region of Sichuan Province has extensive forest coverage and is less prone to soil erosion, making it an area with relatively low demand, the eastern region features flat terrain and a low soil erosion rate, also qualifying it as a low-demand area.

Changes in matching supply and demand for ecosystem services

There is a significant spatial mismatch between the supply and demand of ecosystem services in Sichuan Province from 2010 to 2022, which is manifested by the high spatial heterogeneity in the ratio of ecosystem services supply and demand, with the surplus area centered on the eastern Sichuan region and the deficit area centered on the western Sichuan region (Fig. 4). Specific analysis reveals that the carbon sequestration service, grain service, and water production service of the eastern Sichuan region are in surplus overall, presenting the characteristics of “high supply-high demand”. The supply and demand for soil conservation service in the hilly transition zone of central Sichuan is characterized by “medium supply-medium demand”. Only western Sichuan showed a significant surplus in carbon sequestration service, and there is basically no significant change in the other services types, resulting in an overall pattern of “low supply-low demand”. Comprehensive analysis found that the mismatch between supply and demand in Sichuan Province intensified from 2010 to 2022, with the eastern Sichuan region showing a significant ecological surplus, the western Sichuan region as a whole showing a significant ecological surplus, and the ecological deficit area gradually increasing.

Spatial correlation analysis of ecosystem services

The synergies and trade-offs on the supply side of ecosystem services in Sichuan Province are characterized by significant regional differences. In the western Sichuan region, carbon sequestration service and soil conservation service show a high degree of synergistic effects. However, there is a trade-off between grain service and the two types of services mentioned above in western Sichuan, where grain supply capacity is weak due to the scarcity of arable land and restricted agricultural activities in the region, highlighting the spatial competition between ecological regulation and production functions (Fig. 5). The supply side of the eastern Sichuan region is dominated by synergies between grain service and water production service, carbon sequestration service and soil conservation service. However, carbon sequestration service shows a significant trade-off with grain supply and water production service. The demand for carbon sequestration service in eastern Sichuan is highly synergized spatially with the demand for water-producing service and grain service, while urban sprawl has led to a surge in water use (Fig. 6). However, there is a spatial trade-off between the demand for soil conservation and the above demands. the spatial distribution of demand in western Sichuan is basically similar to that of the eastern Sichuan region, but the spatial synergy between the demand for carbon sequestration services, grain services, and water-producing services in the region is gradually weakening.

Bundle distribution of supply and demand for ecosystem services

The CS bundle (S1) is mainly distributed in the forested areas of the western Sichuan region and some ecological reserves around the eastern Sichuan region (Fig. 7). Because the high-elevation forests on western Sichuan have a high carbon sink capacity and the effect of ecological protection policies is obvious, while the eastern Sichuan region is covered by arable land, with less forested land and only partially planted forests, the proportion of CS bundle is significantly lower than that on the western Sichuan region. From the perspective of time change, the CS bundle proportion in eastern Sichuan remained unchanged at 27%, showing a trend of no increase but no decrease, while the CS bundle in western Sichuan increased from 40% in 2010 to 53% (Fig. 8) in 2016 and then basically remained unchanged, with a more obvious increase. The CS-SC bundle (S2) is concentrated in the transition zone of the western Sichuan region and the hilly areas of the eastern Sichuan region, which are characterized by complex topography and high erosion risk, and thus the demand for soil conservation services coexists with the function of carbon storage. after 2010, due to the impacts of the return of farmland to forests project, on the one hand, forested land area was increased and the problem of erosion was treated, which led to a continuous expansion of the proportion of CS-SC bundle in eastern Sichuan and the western Sichuan, from 14% in 2010 to 17% in 2022. The proportion of CS-SC bundle in western Sichuan continues to expand, with eastern Sichuan increasing from 14% in 2010 to 17% in 2022 and western Sichuan increasing from 38% in 2010 to 39% in 2022 (Fig. 8), indicating the effectiveness of synergistic management of soil conservation and carbon sequestration. The GP-WP bundle (S3) is located mainly in the agricultural core of the eastern Sichuan region and the river valleys of the western Sichuan region. The eastern Sichuan region benefits from fertile soils and intensive irrigation systems, with intensive agricultural activities and outstanding grain production capacity, while the GP-WP bundle on the western Sichuan region relies on alpine snowmelt and seasonal precipitation. While the proportion of S3 bundle continues to decline from 54% in 2010 to 50% in 2022 (Fig. 8) due to a decrease in grain supply capacity as a result of the rapid urbanization of the eastern Sichuan region and the occupation of arable land, The GP-WP bundle on the western Sichuan region will increase slightly in 2022 due to less human activity, highlighting the close relationship between grain production and water supply and human activity. The WP bundle (S4) is decreasing in western Sichuan and increasing in eastern Sichuan, and is concentrated in the alpine lakes and glaciers of the western Sichuan region and the tributaries of the upper Yangtze River. From 2010 to 2022, the proportion of WP bundles in western Sichuan will continue to decline due to glacier retreat, changes in precipitation patterns, and other climatic factors, and the decline in water supply capacity will require vigilance against ecological security risks.

Overall, the eastern Sichuan region is dominated by the GP-WP bundle (S3) with intensive agricultural activities, but the CS bundle (S1) is reduced by the expansion of construction land. The western Sichuan region is dominated by the CS bundle (S1) and the CS-SC bundle (S2), and the ecological restoration project promotes the growth of the CS-SC bundle (S2), which has led to the significant ecological function of the western Sichuan region, but climate change has exacerbated the shrinkage of the WP bundle (S4), suggesting an increase in the pressure on the water supply.

The low demand bundle (C1), which has a very low demand for ecosystem services, is concentrated in the sparsely populated uninhabited areas of the western Sichuan region, and economic activities are limited (Fig. 9), and accounts for more than 85% of the total. The low demand bundle in eastern Sichuan has a relatively low share, and its share will gradually decrease with population growth, from 67% in 2010 to 65% in 2022 (Fig. 10). At the same time, the low demand bundle area is also decreasing due to the development of ecotourism and the expansion of pastoral areas in some parts of the western Sichuan region. The medium demand bundle (C2) is distributed in the districts and counties of the eastern Sichuan region and around the settlements on the western Sichuan region, with demand coming from residents’ living and basic production activities. After 2016, the medium demand bundle expanded to the western Sichuan region, reflecting the increased reliance on ecological services for the region’s economic development, and the beginning of the effectiveness of the policy of balanced regional development. The SC demand bundle is generally stable. It is concentrated in the hilly areas of the eastern Sichuan region and the steep slopes of the western Sichuan region, where there is a risk of soil erosion due to the abundant precipitation and purple soil, which is loose and susceptible to rainfall. The decrease in the proportion of the SC demand bundle area in eastern Sichuan region in 2022 may be related to the return of farmland to forests and the implementation of long-term soil and water conservation projects in the region. The CS-GP-WP bundle (C4) is mainly located in the urban agglomerations of the eastern Sichuan region and the agricultural river valleys of the western Sichuan region, with demand covering carbon sequestration service, grain service, and water production service, and all of them show an increasing trend in terms of area share. The analysis suggests that this is a result of the combined effects of urbanization and population growth.

Discussion

Supply and demand relationship of ecosystem services

This study systematically analyzes the spatial differentiation characteristics and driving mechanisms of ecosystem services supply and demand in Sichuan Province, and the results are both consistent with related studies and show regional uniqueness42. In terms of ecosystem services provision, the western Sichuan region is dominated by carbon sequestration service and water production service, which is consistent with the findings of related studies on the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau and the southwestern mountainous ecological barrier region43,44. Forest and grassland ecosystems on the Tibetan Plateau are often recognized as carbon sequestration hotspots due to their high vegetation cover and low anthropogenic disturbance. However, the significant dominance of grain supply service and water production service in eastern Sichuan reflects its dual characteristics as a core agricultural area and a densely urbanized area, which is similar to the findings in the middle and lower plains of the Yangtze River15. This difference reflects the more prominent contradiction between ecological protection and economic development faced by Sichuan Province in the process of rapid urbanization. On the demand side, the spatial coupling characteristics of high demand concentration areas with population density and economic activity intensity in eastern Sichuan region are consistent with the findings of studies of urban agglomerations such as Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei and Pearl River Delta45,46. For example, the surge in demand for carbon sequestration, grain and water production in Chengdu and neighboring urban agglomerations is similar to the pattern of demand in megacities such as Beijing and Shanghai47. However, the distribution of soil conservation demand in western Sichuan shows a special feature: although the overall demand in the region is low, there are still localized areas of high demand on steep slopes due to the erodibility of purple soils. In western Sichuan, although the supply of carbon sequestration service is significantly higher than the demand, the water production capacity continues to decline due to climate change, leading to ecological deficits in localized areas. The spatial distribution of ecosystem service bundles in Sichuan Province is closely related to topography, climate, and human activities. On the supply side, the western Sichuan region was dominated by the CS-SC bundle, which accounted for 53% of the study area in 2022, which is consistent with the effect of ecological protection policies in the Sanjiangyuan region48. However, the share of the GP-WP bundle in eastern Sichuan decreased from 54% in 2010 to 50% in 2022, reflecting the encroachment of rapid urbanization on cropland resources. This trend is similar to the findings in the Yangtze River Delta region49,50. In addition, the continued shrinkage of the WP bundle in western Sichuan is directly related to glacial retreat and changes in precipitation patterns, a finding that provides a basis for adaptive management in high-elevation ecologically fragile areas. On the demand side, the share of the CS-GP-WP demand bundle in eastern Sichuan region increases from 8% in 2010 to 15% in 2022, highlighting the combined pressure of urban expansion on multidimensional ecological services. This trend is consistent with the findings of the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei urban agglomeration51, but Sichuan Province is unique in that its demand growth comes not only from the local population, but also from the superimposed effects of the radiation effect of the Chengdu-Chongqing urban agglomeration. In addition, the share of the SC demand bundle in western Sichuan region declined after the implementation of the policy of returning farmland to forest, indicating that the ecological project has achieved remarkable results in the local area.

Drivers of ecosystem service supply and demand

The interaction between natural and socioeconomic factors has significantly intensified the spatial heterogeneity in the distribution of ecosystem service supply and demand in Sichuan Province. On the supply side, the interaction between NDVI and slope had the strongest explanatory power for carbon sequestration service, indicating that the synergistic effect of high vegetation cover and steep-slope topography could significantly enhance carbon sequestration capacity. This finding is consistent with that of related studies in the Hengduan Mountain52,53. On the demand side, the interaction between population density and GDP peaks in explaining the demand for carbon sequestration service, grain production service in the eastern plains is more regulated by NDVI and precipitation, revealing an exponential depletion of ecological services due to the superimposition of population and industry in economic agglomerations54. As the core city of the eastern Sichuan region55,56, Chengdu’s high-density population and industrial structure not only exacerbates local carbon emissions, but also affects grain production service in eastern Sichuan is more regulated by NDVI and precipitation, security in neighboring regions through the extension of the supply chain57. In addition, the interaction between slope and soil type significantly affects soil conservation needs in western Sichuan, and the risk of soil erosion increases significantly on steep slopes58.

Differentiated ecological management and development strategies

Western regions, characterized by high altitudes and low human activity, primarily provide ecosystem services such as CS and WP. It is recommended to strengthen the implementation of ecological protection red lines and the Grain-for-Green Program in these areas. Particular emphasis should be placed on strictly limiting development activities that encroach upon ecological spaces in forested areas with high carbon sequestration capacity and water conservation zones, ensuring the long-term stability of their carbon sink functions and ecosystem services. For issues like soil erosion and slope cultivation erosion in western marginal areas, prioritize ecological restoration projects such as afforestation and small watershed management. This enhances soil retention capacity while synergistically strengthening water and soil conservation and carbon sequestration functions. Construct an ecological barrier centered on the upper reaches of the Yangtze and Yellow Rivers, strengthening the connectivity and integrity of ecosystems including forests, grasslands, and wetlands to promote the synergistic provision of biodiversity conservation and ecological services. Simultaneously, encourage eco-tourism and low-carbon economic development to reduce excessive exploitation of natural resources. In eastern regions, intense human activity has created a significant imbalance between demand for ecosystem services and supply, with notable pressures particularly in CS, GP, and WP. To address the ecological service supply-demand imbalance caused by rapid urbanization in eastern urban clusters, optimize urban development boundaries, strictly control the expansion of construction land, and reduce encroachment on farmland and ecological land. Integrate ecological conservation with urban greening to enhance urban ecological service functions. Strengthen the ecological restoration of rivers, lakes, and wetlands, establish integrated agricultural, ecological, and urban spaces, and promote the coordinated development of agriculture and the ecological environment to improve regional ecological environment quality and soil and water conservation capacity. Overall, the eastern and western regions exhibit significant disparities in resource endowments. The eastern region should prioritize strengthening the management of ecological service demand and improving resource utilization efficiency. The western region should focus on enhancing ecological service supply capacity and strictly protecting natural ecosystems.

Limitations and future directions

Despite the progress made in this study, there are still limitations in terms of data accuracy, model applicability and mechanism depth: the land use data and meteorological data used in the study are difficult to capture the effects of small-scale topography on ecosystem services. Meanwhile, the parameters of the InVEST model are mainly modified based on the literature, which is highly subjective, and the localized adaptability of the parameters needs to be further tested. Future dynamic changes are not considered when analyzing the evolution of ecosystem services supply and demand based on current static point-in-time data. In addition, the study assumes that the demand side is driven only by demographics and the economy, but the transformation of consumption patterns has not been quantified, which may lead to a long-term bias in demand forecasts. Although natural, socio-economic, and landscape factors all have some influence on ecosystem services, the spatial influence of institutional factors has not been clarified. Therefore, it is necessary to introduce participatory surveys and qualitative analysis methods to improve the integrated assessment framework of nature-society-policy-ecosystem in the future. A multi-scale nested model of “basin-county-grid” should be constructed to improve the refinement level of the study of complex terrain areas, At the same time, we integrate multi-source data and models to analyze the transmission paths of policy interventions on the balance of supply and demand, and realize the prediction and early warning of “nature-economy-policy” coupling, on the basis of which we optimize the cross-regional ecological security network.

Conclusion

(1) The supply and demand of ecosystem services in Sichuan Province showed significant spatial heterogeneity from 2010 to 2022. The eastern region was characterized by high supply and demand for carbon sequestration (CS), grain production (GP), and water production (WP), while the western region exhibited low supply and demand but showed a significant surplus in carbon sequestration. The mismatch between supply and demand intensified over the study period, with the eastern region experiencing an ecological surplus and the western region showing an ecological deficit.

(2) Spatial correlations among ecosystem services revealed significant synergies and trade-offs. In the eastern region, grain production and water production showed strong synergies, whereas carbon sequestration exhibited trade-offs with grain and water production. In the western region, carbon sequestration and soil conservation were highly synergistic, but trade-offs were observed between grain production and these two services due to limited arable land and agricultural activity.

(3) Ecosystem service supply was primarily influenced by natural landscape factors such as NDVI, temperature, and slope. In contrast, ecosystem service demand was closely linked to socioeconomic conditions like population density and GDP. The interaction effects of multiple factors had a far greater influence on ecosystem services than individual factors alone.

(4) The spatial distribution of ecosystem service bundles showed distinct regional differences. In the eastern region, the grain production-water production bundle (GP-WP) dominated but decreased over time due to urbanization and agricultural land loss. In the western region, the carbon sequestration-soil conservation bundle (CS-SC) expanded significantly, reflecting the effectiveness of ecological restoration policies. However, the water production bundle (WP) in the western region contracted due to climate change impacts such as glacier retreat and altered precipitation patterns.

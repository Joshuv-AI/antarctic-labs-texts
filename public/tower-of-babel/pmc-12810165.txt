Introduction

Neonatal factors such as gestational age, birth weight, weight for gestational age, and feeding methods significantly influence the child's overall and oral growth and development.1,2 Based on the gestational age, the child can be classified as term or preterm. Preterm, defined as birth before 37 weeks of gestation, has a profound effect on the primary teeth.3 Most preterm deliveries occur between 32 and 37 weeks of pregnancy. More than 60% of all preterm births happen in sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia. In India, the incidence of preterm birth is 13%.4 Preterm infants are susceptible to numerous medical complications during the neonatal period, potentially impacting the development of the dentofacial complex, including structural alterations of enamel,5,6 distortion of the palate,7 delayed tooth eruption,8 and other issues. These factors may heighten the risk of compromised oral health and adversely affect their quality of life.

Preterm infants with low birth weight exhibit impaired immunological responses, and this compromised immune competence may endure for several years. Preterm infants exhibit increased vulnerability to the early acquisition of cariogenic mutant streptococci.9 To make up for their low birth weight and help them gain weight, preterm babies often eat cariogenic foods more regularly between meals and at night.10 These foods are well-known risk factors for early childhood caries (ECC).

Though the overall prevalence of ECC is around 49.6% in India,11 its prevalence in preterm children has not yet been investigated. Due to developmental dental abnormalities and parental ignorance regarding oral health, preterm children face an elevated risk of acquiring ECC. There has been little and conflicting data about how these risk factors for developing ECC and preterm birth are related.12 To find out if birth factors affect the number of ECC, it is important to gather all the facts.

It is also very important to evaluate the child's quality of life in terms of their dental health in order to understand how oral health problems affect their well-being. Giddon originally proposed oral health-related quality of life (OHRQoL) in 1978.13 Many tools have been produced over the years to measure the OHRQoL. Some of these tools are only for preschoolers, such as the Michigan OHRQoL, Child Oral Health Impact Profile (COHIP), Oral Health-related Early Childhood Quality of Life (OH-ECQoL), and Scale of Oral Health Outcomes for 5-year-old children (SOHO-5).14–18 However, not all countries and languages have access to these tools. The OH-ECQoL was created in 2013 for the North Indian population.19 It has been evaluated for validity, reliability, and repeatability without changing the original tool's psychometric features. The objective of this study was to assess the oral health status and quality of life in children born preterm. The study is significant not only for pediatric dentists but also for other healthcare providers attending to the healthcare needs of children under 6 years of age.

Materials and Methods

Ethical approval was taken before the commencement of the study. A cross-sectional observational study was conducted in the Baby Oral Health Promotion Clinic, Department of Pediatrics, JSS Hospital. In this hospital, 600–700 babies are delivered in the Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology per year. Among them, 20–24% are born preterm. Preterm children delivered in this hospital during the period January 2016 to December 2018 (child aged 3–5 years) (N = ~360) were called for oral health checkups. Parents who gave consent were recruited for the study. Children who are already on dental treatment were excluded from the study.

Sample Size

The prevalence of severe ECC in the study region was found to be 10% (based on data from the Baby Oral Health Promotion Clinic). Accordingly, to obtain this proportion with 95% confidence and 5% tolerable error, the estimated sample size was 139. However, a sample of 150 children was considered for the proposed study.

Data Collection Procedures and Instruments Used

The investigator interviewed the parents to obtain demographic information. The purpose and methodology of the study were explained to all the parents during the interview, and the signature on the informed consent was obtained.

The clinical examination was carried out to assess the prevalence of ECC among preterm-born children. A trained examiner performed the examination. The patient was in a supine position on the dental chair, and the examination was done using a mouth mirror, probe, and dental light. All the children requiring treatment were referred to the dental hospital for further evaluation and treatment. To assess the quality of life, OH-ECQoL tool was used.19 The tool was translated into the local language, Kannada, and the Kannada OH-ECQoL was found to be adequately valid, reliable, and repeatable.

Plan of Analysis and Statistical Tools

Gender, parental educational levels, feeding practices, toothbrushing frequency, use of fluoride, and regular dental check-ups will be considered as a priori potential confounding factors. Prevalence ratios (PRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) will be estimated by binomial regression with the log link function. All statistical analyses will be performed using the SPSS software package.

Table 1 provides descriptive statistics for maternal and neonatal characteristics. The mean age of children in years was 4.99 (SD 0.365) years, with a minimum of 4.1 years and a maximum of 5.9 years. There were 56% males and 44% females. There were 74% children from rural areas, and 80% were in lower-middle class families. The majority, that is, 60.6% of the mothers, were between 25 and 30 years, and 52% had weeks of gestation in the range of 32–36 weeks. Further, the majority of mothers, that is, 81.3%, were appropriate for gestational age (AGA). As shown in the table, a maximum of 54.66% were cesarean deliveries, and majorly, that is, 59.3%, were primipara. There were 94.66% mothers without any disease and who did not use any medication during pregnancy. The mean birth weight of children was 2 kg (SD 0.617 kg), and the mean current weight was 14.9 kg (SD 1.51 kg). There were 53.33% of children requiring intubation. The APGAR score of >8 was observed in 70.6% of children.

Discussion

Early childhood caries is the most prevalent and major health concern across the globe. Scientific studies have proven that ECC significantly impacts the child's quality of life, affecting its speech, nutrition, and playing.20 ECC is a multifactorial disease where various risk factors interact. This study examined a set of risk factors including gender, socioeconomic status (SES), and mother's age, gestational age, type of delivery, parity, maternal diseases, and medication during pregnancy, birth weight, intubation, and APGAR score. However, only gender, mother age, and gestational age were associated with ECC in this study, which was consistent with previous data.21 Previous studies showed a stronger association with SES. However, in our study, this association was weak.22 Previous studies have shown that colonization of Streptococcus mutans in young children was affected by mode of delivery.23,24 However, in this study, mode of delivery was not significantly associated with ECC. In summary, the present study demonstrated that ECC is associated with gender, mother age, and gestational age. Here is a scientific discussion on the association between quality of life, ECC, and preterm birth, along with references.

Early childhood caries causes discomfort, alteration in phonetics, and psychological problems.25 Children will not be able to get enough nourishment and will have trouble eating and sleeping because of the discomfort and illness caused by ECC. Also, parents of preterm children with ECC will have to deal with stress in their lives, both financially and emotionally. These issues could have an impact on the quality of life for both parents and children. The OHRQoL was first described by Giddon in 1978 and defined as “an individual's assessment of how the following affect his/her well-being: functional factors, psychological factors, social factors, and experience of pain/discomfort in relation to orofacial concerns.”13

Various OHRQoL tools have been designed for the pediatric population, like Child-Oral Impacts on Daily Performances (OIDP),14 Early Childhood Oral Health Impact Scale (ECOHIS),15 Child Perceptions Questionnaire (CPQ),16 Michigan OHRQoL Scale,17 SOHO-5,18 OH-ECQoL,19 and COHIP,26 Among these tools, OH-ECQoL has been designed, developed, and validated exclusively for the Indian population. Hence, this tool was used for the study. To date, this tool has not been used to evaluate the quality of life among preterm children. Caries prevalence among preterm children in this study was 60.6%. Following the analysis, it was observed that the most prevalent impact in the child impact section was related to pain, trouble sleeping, and irritability. The findings were consistent with previous studies.27,28 However, in this study, ECC did not have much impact on the family, consistent with a previous Indian study.29

Conclusion

From this study, it may be concluded that children born preterm had higher caries prevalence and poorer quality of life. Early guidance on oral health is needed to combat the health disparity among these children. Longitudinal studies to understand the long-term impact of preterm birth on oral health are needed.

Clinical Significance

Preterm children are at higher risk of having dental diseases. Identification of disparities and challenges faced by preterm children related to oral health needs to be focused on, as the prevalence of preterm children is high among the Indian population.

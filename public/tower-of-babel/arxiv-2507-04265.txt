II.
Einstein-Cartan theory with torsion spacetime

The EC theory was based on a simple physical intuition, that torsion effect is regarded as a macroscopic manifestation of the intrinsic angular momentum (spin) of matter.
Kibble 1961
and
Sciama 1964
then reintroduced the spin of matter independently into GR (also known as ECKS theory). The ECKS theory postulates an asymmetric affine connection for the spacetime, in contrast to the symmetric Christoffel symbols of Riemannian spaces. In technical terms, torsion is described by the antisymmetric part of the non-Riemannian affine connection.

For convenience, we adopt the same notation from
Kranas et al. 2019
. With the presence of torsion terms into Einstein gravitational field, it is known as the EC equation of gravitation. The Einstein-Cartan field equation of gravitation describes the relationship between matter and the geometry of space-time, which combines with Ricci tensor, Ricci scalar and energy momentum tensor

R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}Rg_{\mu\nu}=\kappa T_{\mu\nu}-\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}\,,

(1)

where
\kappa={8\pi G}
and
\Lambda
is a cosmological constant. Considering the spacetime with non-zero torsion, the affine connection contains
an antisymmetric part, namely
\Gamma^{\alpha}_{~\mu\nu}=\tilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha}_{~\mu\nu}+K^{\alpha}_{~\mu\nu}
, where
\tilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha}_{~\mu\nu}
is the symmetric Christoffel symbol, and
K^{\alpha}_{~~\mu\nu}
is the contortion tensor given by

K^{\alpha}_{~\mu\nu}=S^{\alpha}_{~\mu\nu}+S_{\mu\nu}^{~~\alpha}+S_{\nu\mu}^{~~\alpha}\,,

(2)

where
S^{\alpha}_{~~\mu\nu}
is torsion tensor. The antisymmetry of the torsion tensor guarantees that it has only one non-trivial contraction, leading to the torsion vector
S_{\mu}=S^{\nu}_{~\mu\nu}=-S^{\nu}_{~\nu\mu}
. From a physical point of view, torsion can be regarded as a possible link between the intrinsic angular momentum of spin, and the geometry of the main space-time
(
Pasmatsiou et al. 2017
)
.

In torsion spacetime, the energy momentum tensor is also antisymmetric and typically coupled to the spin of the matter via the Cartan field equations, the spin tensor is given by

S_{\alpha\mu\nu}=-\frac{1}{4}\kappa(2s_{\mu\nu\alpha}+g_{\nu\alpha}s_{\mu}-g_{\alpha\mu}s_{\nu})\,,

(3)

where
s_{\alpha}=s^{\mu}_{~~\alpha\mu}
is the spin vector of the matter.
In a homogeneous and isotropic Friedmann background, the torsion tensor and the associated vector have the form
(
Kranas et al. 2019
)

S_{\alpha\mu\nu}=\phi(h_{\alpha\mu}u_{\nu}-h_{\alpha\nu}u_{\mu}),\hskip 14.22636ptS_{\alpha}=-3\phi u_{\alpha}\,,

(4)

where
\phi=\phi(t)
is the scalar function and must depend only on time due to homogeneity of space,
h_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}+u_{\mu}u_{\nu}
is a projection tensor, symmetric and orthogonal to the 4-vector velocity
u_{\mu}
.

II.1.
Friedmann equation in torsion cosmology with cosmological constant

In the presence of torsion, the analogues of the Friedmann equation, in a spacetime with cosmological constant take the form (see
Kibble 1961
;
Sciama 1964
;
Kranas et al. 2019
for details)

\left({\frac{\dot{a}}{a}}\right)^{2}={\frac{1}{3}}\,\kappa\rho+{\frac{1}{3}}\,\Lambda-4\phi^{2}-4\left({\frac{\dot{a}}{a}}\right)\phi.

(5)

One can obtain conservation equation by combining the Einstein-Cartan field equations and the twice-contracted Bianchi identities in torsion cosmology, which is:

\nabla^{\nu}T_{\mu\nu}=-4\phi\Big(T_{\mu\nu}u^{\nu}-\kappa^{-1}\Lambda u_{\mu}\Big),

(6)

where
T_{\mu\nu}=\rho u_{\mu}u_{\nu}+ph_{\mu\nu}
is energy-momentum tensor, and
\rho
and
p
are the energy density and pressure of matter, respectively.

For the baryonic matter or dark matter, it should be satisfied
p=\omega\rho
and
\omega=0
, the continuity equation is given by following form
(
Kranas et al. 2019
;
Liu et al. 2023
)

\dot{\rho}+3H\rho+2\phi\rho=4\phi{\Lambda}{\kappa^{-1}}\,,

(7)

where
H=\dot{a}/a
is defined as Hubble parameter. When
\phi=0
, the above equation recovers the standard continuity equation.
We assume that
\phi=-\alpha aH
form for keeping same dimension with the Hubble parameter
H
. This choice indicates that the torsion intensity increases with the expansion of the universe (as the scale factor increases). One possible physical explanation is that the torsion is generated by the spin density of matter, and the spin density is diluted with the expansion of the universe, but geometric effects (such as
\alpha aH
) may compensate for this dilution. Considering that
a(t)
evolves slowly over time in the late universe (where dark energy dominates time), our assumption is reasonable.
In dark energy dominates time, the Friedmann equation (
5
) turns:

H^{2}=\frac{\kappa}{3}\rho_{m}+\frac{\Lambda}{3}+4\alpha aH^{2}-4\alpha^{2}a^{2}H^{2}\,,

(8)

and the solution for the matter density from (
7
) is:

\displaystyle\rho_{m}(a)\simeq\rho_{m0}a^{-3}e^{2\alpha a}-\frac{4\alpha a\Lambda}{3\kappa}\,,

(9)

where we have ignored the second-order small quantity of
\alpha
. For
\alpha=0
, the evolution of the matter density is exactly the expected
\sim a^{-3}
.

By defining the present density parameter
\Omega_{m}=\frac{\kappa\rho_{m0}}{3H_{0}^{2}}
and the cosmological constant
\Omega_{\Lambda}=\frac{\Lambda}{3H_{0}^{2}}
, the equation (
8
) can be written as:

H^{2}(z)\simeq\frac{H_{0}^{2}}{(1-\frac{2\alpha}{1+z})^{2}}\Big[\Omega_{m}(1+z)^{3}e^{\frac{2\alpha}{(1+z)}}+\Omega_{\Lambda}(1-\frac{4\alpha}{3(1+z)})\Big]\,.

(10)

By using the Friedmann constraint at
z=0
, namely
1=(\Omega_{m}e^{2\alpha}+\Omega_{\Lambda}(1-\frac{4\alpha}{3}))/(1-2\alpha)^{2}
, we can express
\Omega_{\Lambda}
as a function of
\Omega_{m}
, namely
\Omega_{\Lambda}=((1-2\alpha)^{2}-\Omega_{m}e^{2\alpha})/(1-4\alpha/3)
.
From this equation, we can see that the standard cosmological constant term
\Omega_{\Lambda}
is also affected by the torsion coupling. When
\alpha\to 0
the standard model is recovered.

III.
Data and Methodology

The datasets used in the analyses are described below:

\bullet
BAO:
Our cosmological analysis utilizes the most recent BAO data from the DESI DR2 release, which represents the most comprehensive BAO measurement to date by combining multiple tracers: luminous red galaxies, emission line galaxies, quasars, and Lyman-
\alpha
forest absorption systems. These measurements provide precise determinations of three key cosmological distance ratios: the transverse comoving distance
D_{M}/r_{d}
(where
r_{d}

D_{H}/r_{d}

D_{V}/r_{d}

(
DESI Collaboration et al. 2025

To ensure rigorous statistical treatment, our methodology fully accounts for the complete covariance structure of these measurements, including the significant cross-correlation coefficient
r_{M,H}
between the transverse and line-of-sight distance measurements (typically ranging from 0.3 to 0.5 for different redshift bins). We denote this full dataset as
“DESI”

\bullet

On the other hand, the Dark Energy Survey (DES) collaboration has recently released a partial dataset from its full 5-year survey

V.
Conclusion

Our analysis of torsion cosmology reveals several interesting features worth noting. The torsion parameter
\alpha
shows a gradual evolution from
\alpha=0.042^{+0.035}_{-0.031}
in the early universe to
\alpha=-0.0075\pm 0.0058
in the late universe, though remaining consistent with zero within
1\sigma
uncertainties. This pattern could suggest a redshift-dependent behavior of torsion effects that may become more noticeable at lower redshifts.

The framework shows some promise in addressing cosmological tensions, with the Hubble constant converging to
H_{0}=68.80\pm 0.48~\mathrm{km~s^{-1}~Mpc^{-1}}
and the
S_{8}
tension reducing from
0.73\sigma
to
0.1\sigma
when compared with KiDS-1000 data. The torsion model introduces scale-dependent damping, naturally lowering
\sigma_{8}
without disrupting CMB constraints—a unique advantage over
\Lambda
CDM extensions.

The torsion framework provides excellent fits to all datasets, as evidenced by the favorable
\Delta\chi^{2}_{\rm min}
values relative to
\Lambda
CDM. The Bayesian evidence analysis presents mixed results, with
\Delta\log\mathcal{Z}
values ranging from
-3.59

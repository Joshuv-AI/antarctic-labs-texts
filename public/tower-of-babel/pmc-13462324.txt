Maritime obstacle-aware formation control for USVs with an optimized artificial potential field
Authors: Zhicheng Tan, Shanlin Sun, Guo Li, Liangpo Hong, Haixin Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Efficient multi-USV formation control in obstacle-rich maritime environments remains challenging, primarily due to local minima, goal non-reachability, and delayed response to dynamic obstacles in conventional path planning methods. This paper presents an enhanced Artificial Potential Field (APF) algorithm that directly addresses these three limitations for multi-USV formation and obstacle avoidance. This paper presents an enhanced Artificial Potential Field (APF) algorithm for multi-USV formation and obstacle avoidance, addressing issues such as local minima and goal non-reachability. The proposed method introduces improvements such as instantaneous escape forces, rotational factors, and relative velocity terms to the APF framework, enabling follower USVs to generate collision-free formation trajectories while improving obstacle avoidance efficiency in dynamic environments. The algorithm is designed to ensure smooth formation maintenance and effective obstacle avoidance, even in the presence of dynamic obstacles. Numerical simulations demonstrate that the proposed approach significantly enhances the performance of USV formations compared to traditional and existing APF-based methods, reducing target arrival times by 34.52% and 25.13%, respectively. The experimental results validate the practical applicability of the method, highlighting its ability to maintain USV formation integrity and avoid collisions in both static and dynamic obstacle scenarios. This work offers a promising solution for autonomous maritime operations.

Introduction

Unmanned Surface Vehicles (USVs) have broad application prospects in marine exploration, environmental monitoring, search, and rescue operations. As the complexity of tasks increases, a single USV is often insufficient to meet these demands, making multi-USV collaborative formation a research hotspot. However, in dynamic and complex marine environments (such as those with dynamic obstacles, ocean current interference, etc.), achieving efficient formation control and obstacle avoidance remains a key challenge.

Several researchers have conducted studies in this field. For instance, Burlutskiy et al.1 proposed a leader–follower formation structure for AUVs, optimizing the formation shape from the perspective of formation energy efficiency. This approach achieved an optimal formation configuration balancing three factors: energy consumption, coverage, and communication power. Liang et al.2 focused on the propeller structure of AUVs, providing a detailed classification of the resistance sources during formation sailing, and proposed the optimal formation shape for minimal resistance. He et al.3 conducted a numerical modeling analysis to compare the resistance and energy consumption of different formation configurations. Dong et al.4 introduced an energy-saving formation method for multi-USV sailing, considering the hydrodynamic energy consumption under longitudinal and lateral formations, and optimized the formation configuration based on spacing division. However, most studies primarily focus on energy consumption during the formation process and lack research on formation obstacle avoidance.

In addition to formation strategies, advances in marine sensing and imaging technologies have also provided essential support for USV applications. In particular, synthetic aperture sonar (SAS) and imaging algorithms have been extensively studied to improve perception capability in marine environments. On the algorithmic side, Zhang and colleagues have proposed a series of efficient SAS imaging and focusing approaches that significantly enhance resolution and computational efficiency5,6. These methods improve the perception reliability of USVs in complex maritime environments, which is crucial for maintaining stable formation.

At the signal processing and modeling level, further progress has been made in wide-bandwidth signal processing and multi-receiver SAS data reconstruction. For example, Zhang et al.7–10 developed advanced algorithms for multi-receiver systems and broadband scenarios, which enhance robustness against environmental disturbances. Such improvements directly support adaptive USV path planning and more reliable obstacle avoidance in dynamic conditions.

Finally, experimental and application-oriented studies have validated these approaches in real-world scenarios. Zhang and colleagues demonstrated practical SAS implementations, including back projection methods and sub-bottom profiling11,12, as well as efficient simulation and enhancement techniques13–15. The success of these experimental validations confirms the potential of SAS-based perception technologies to augment USV situational awareness, thereby indirectly strengthening decision-making in formation control and collision avoidance16.

Currently, most USV formation obstacle avoidance strategies employ fixed formation avoidance17. However, under the constraints of a fixed formation, obstacle avoidance is often not an optimal strategy, especially in dynamic and scattered obstacle environments. Autonomous obstacle avoidance by USVs can improve the overall formation obstacle avoidance efficiency18. Therefore, achieving stable formation while allowing individual USVs to autonomously avoid obstacles is crucial, which in turn places higher demands on autonomous obstacle avoidance path planning methods for USVs.

The Artificial Potential Field (APF) method is widely used in robot path planning and obstacle avoidance due to its simple computation and strong real-time performance. Pan et al.19 introduced a rotating potential field to successfully overcome local minima and oscillation problems. They further developed a leader–follower model-based formation controller, which not only ensures the desired angle and distance between the leader and follower but also enables effective obstacle avoidance while maintaining the desired formation. Fan et al.20 constructed a virtual hexagonal potential field to overcome the local minimum dilemma and validated its feasibility. To address the issues of excessive turning angles and local minima in traditional APF algorithms for USVs, Song et al.21 set steering angle constraints, velocity limits, and a predictive potential field to generate smooth paths suitable for USVs. The application of the APF method to USVs also needs to account for practical navigation requirements. To solve the issue of large trajectory deviations under traditional APF algorithms for USVs, Zhu et al.22 introduced dynamically moving target points to guide the USV back to its route after obstacle avoidance, demonstrating that the improved algorithm is more suitable for engineering applications. To overcome the impact of wind and waves on USV formation, Chu et al.23 set virtual footholds as attractive points, enabling the USV to maintain high-precision path keeping under the gravitational influence of the virtual foothold.

As highlighted in previous studies, the APF method, known for its low computational cost and high real-time performance, is an effective obstacle avoidance algorithm suitable for both static and dynamic environments. However, its practical application is hindered by issues such as the potential for local minima and unreachable goals, which compromise its real-time obstacle avoidance capabilities. This is particularly evident when the formation constraints of Unmanned Surface Vehicles (USVs) are considered, where the limitations of the APF method can lead to individual vehicles straying from the formation, resulting in failure. Therefore, the integration of the APF method with USV formation path planning still faces significant challenges in terms of formation maintenance and the real-time efficiency of obstacle avoidance.

To address these challenges, this paper proposes a real-time path planning approach for USV formation and obstacle avoidance, based on an enhanced version of the APF method. The proposed approach ensures that follower USVs generate smooth, collision-free trajectories during the formation process. Furthermore, to mitigate the issues of local optima, unreachable goals, and low efficiency in dynamic obstacle environments, we modify the potential field function of the APF. This improvement enhances the ability of the USV formation to maintain its structure while effectively completing obstacle avoidance tasks throughout the navigation process. Related work

Recent studies on multi-USV cooperative navigation mainly focus on three aspects, namely formation control, obstacle avoidance/path planning, and learning-based cooperative decision-making. In terms of formation control, distributed and connectivity-preserving strategies have received considerable attention. Yoo and Park24 investigated distributed dynamic obstacle avoidance together with connectivity-preserving formation control for uncertain underactuated surface vehicles under a directed network. Tang et al.25 further studied distributed formation control with obstacle avoidance for multiple underactuated USVs, where an artificial potential field was incorporated into the formation tracking error and the leader velocity was estimated through a distributed observer. In addition, event-triggered mechanisms have also been introduced to reduce communication burden and control updates. For example, Jiang and Xia26 proposed an event-triggered practical formation control scheme for leaderless unmanned surface vehicles under environmental disturbances. These works provide useful theoretical tools for coordinated control, but they mainly emphasize stability and controller synthesis, while the real-time generation of smooth formation obstacle-avoidance trajectories still deserves further study.

For obstacle avoidance and local path planning, APF-based methods remain attractive because of their low computational cost and strong real-time performance. Luan et al.27 improved APF-based USV path planning by introducing virtual target points, which alleviates the local-minimum and target-unreachable problems. Hu et al.28 integrated APF into an adaptive leader–follower formation framework and considered asymmetric input saturation. Yuan et al.29 combined leader–follower control with APF to realize multi-USV coordination and obstacle avoidance. Du et al.30 addressed multi-USV collaborative obstacle avoidance using an improved velocity-obstacle method. Beyond APF, Liu et al.31 proposed a dynamical-systems-based streamline-traction strategy for cooperative formation obstacle avoidance. Although these studies improve safety and local avoidance capability, some methods rely on relatively strong model assumptions, while others introduce additional algorithmic complexity when the environment becomes more dynamic and crowded.

Meanwhile, learning-based approaches have shown strong potential for formation planning and cooperative decision-making. Wei et al.32 proposed a deep hierarchical reinforcement learning framework for multiple USVs and demonstrated its effectiveness in formation planning experiments. Li et al.33 further verified a multi-agent deterministic policy gradient based formation strategy with line-of-sight guidance in both simulation and real-world environments. These methods improve adaptability and decision-making capability, but they generally require substantial training effort and may be less straightforward to deploy in engineering scenarios where interpretability, online responsiveness, and computational simplicity are all important.

Overall, recent studies indicate that distributed control methods offer strong stability guarantees, APF-type methods provide favorable real-time performance, and reinforcement-learning-based methods improve adaptability. However, there is still a need for a lightweight obstacle-avoidance framework that can simultaneously preserve formation structure, generate smooth trajectories, and maintain high real-time efficiency in known environments containing static and dynamic obstacles. Motivated by this gap, this paper develops an improved APF-based real-time path planning method for USV formation and obstacle avoidance.

Methods

Analysis and improvement of the artificial potential field method

The APF method is a reactive obstacle avoidance algorithm suitable for dynamic environments with partially known information. The core principle models the Unmanned Surface Vehicle (USV) as a point mass within a virtual potential field, composed of an attractive field generated by the target point and a repulsive field induced by obstacles. The attractive field exerts a pulling force toward the target, while the repulsive field pushes the USV away from obstacles. Dynamic obstacle avoidance

In static obstacle environments, traditional APF methods compute repulsive forces based solely on the instantaneous distance between the USV and obstacles. For a stationary obstacle, this is sufficient. However, for a dynamic obstacle approaching at high speed, the distance-based force responds only after the gap has already closed significantly, because the USV has no information about how quickly the gap is shrinking.

To address this, a relative velocity term is incorporated to provide anticipatory capability. By computing \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\textbf{v}_{\text {rel}} = \textbf{v}_{\text {usv}} - \textbf{v}_{\text {obs}}$$\end{document}, the repulsive force is amplified whenever the closing speed is large (e.g., in a head-on encounter) and reduced when the obstacle is moving away. This is analogous to a time-to-collision (TTC) criterion: a large relative speed indicates imminent risk and warrants a stronger, earlier avoidance response. Furthermore, the direction of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\textbf{v}_{\text {rel}}$$\end{document} encodes whether the USV needs to deviate from its course or can maintain heading, which is particularly important in crossing scenarios where purely distance-based forces may cause unnecessary waiting.

USV formation and obstacle avoidance algorithm

The formation optimization strategy outlined in Chapter 3 yields the desired formation coordinates for Unmanned Surface Vehicle (USV) fleets, defined by target points. Each follower USV corresponds uniquely to a target point, and the desired formation is achieved only when all followers reach their respective targets. Thus, the USV formation system requires each follower to possess autonomous path-planning capabilities, transforming the single-USV path-planning problem into a multi-USV coordination challenge.

During formation establishment, the initial positions and headings of follower USVs are random, which may lead to trajectory intersections and potential collisions within the fleet. To mitigate this, follower USVs treat other USVs as obstacles and their assigned target points as local path-planning goals. This enables each follower to generate collision-free trajectories from random initial positions to target points under the influence of the resultant force. The formation condition is expressed as:18\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \lim _{t \rightarrow \infty } \Vert \textbf{p}_i(t) - \textbf{p}_{g,i}\Vert = 0, \quad \forall i = 1, \dots , N, \end{aligned}$$\end{document}where N is the number of follower USVs, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\textbf{p}_i(t)$$\end{document} is the position of the i-th follower at time t, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\textbf{p}_{g,i}$$\end{document} is its corresponding target point.

The repulsive potential field can impact formation integrity, as illustrated in Fig. 4b . Black dots represent target points, and dashed circles indicate obstacle influence ranges. Since follower USVs treat other USVs as obstacles, an excessively large repulsive influence radius (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$d_0$$\end{document}) relative to the distance between target points may prevent followers from reaching their targets due to inter-USV repulsion, leading to formation failure. Therefore, the formation scale and repulsive field range must be carefully balanced in practical applications.

In the leader–follower formation model, a necessary condition for successful formation is that follower USVs maintain the same speed and heading as the leader USV. Assuming negligible communication delays and time synchronization issues, the leader broadcasts its speed, heading, and target point positions to all followers. USV formation avoidance validation

To evaluate the improved APF in USV formation scenarios, a fleet of one leader USV and four follower USVs was simulated in a 500 m × 500 m area. Initial positions were: leader at (50, 50) m, followers at (30, 50), (20, 40), (50, 40), and (50, 30) m. Five obstacles were included: four static at (140, 170), (160, 150), (170, 140), (170, 250) m, and one dynamic moving from (340, 340) to (280, 280) m, with a 30 m influence radius.

Conclusion

This study presents an enhanced APF algorithm for multi-USV formation and obstacle avoidance, targeting three core limitations of the traditional APF: local minima, goal non-reachability, and insufficient responsiveness to dynamic obstacles. Each contribution yields a measurable improvement: the rotation factor correction eliminates goal non-reachability by deflecting the repulsive force direction without altering its magnitude; the transient escape force reliably disengages the USV from local minima through a momentary perpendicular perturbation, and the relative velocity term enables proactive avoidance of fast-approaching dynamic obstacles, producing shorter and more direct avoidance trajectories. Statistical evaluation across 30 randomized trials confirms mean arrival time reductions of 34.6% (p < 0.01) and 25.1% (p < 0.01) compared to traditional APF and virtual force APF, respectively. Formation integrity was maintained for all USVs in all improved-APF trials, compared to failure in at least two USVs under the baseline method.

Several limitations of the current work should be acknowledged. First, the framework assumes a known obstacle environment, which restricts applicability to partially observed real-world scenarios. Second, communication delays between the leader and follower USVs are neglected, which may affect synchronization in practice. Third, environmental disturbances such as ocean currents, wind, and waves are not modeled in the simulations. Fourth, scalability experiments show a gradual decline in formation success rate beyond N = 8 followers, indicating that the current centralized leader–follower architecture has practical fleet-size limits.

Future work will address these gaps along three directions: (1) extension to unknown environments via onboard sensor fusion, enabling real-time obstacle detection and map-free path planning; (2) incorporation of a disturbance observer to compensate for ocean current drift and maintain formation accuracy under environmental perturbations; and (3) development of a distributed formation protocol to replace the centralized leader–follower structure, improving scalability for large USV fleets operating in complex maritime missions.

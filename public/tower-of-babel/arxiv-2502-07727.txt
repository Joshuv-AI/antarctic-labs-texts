On a local property of infinite Galois extensions implying the Northcott property
Jonathan Jenvrin

Abstract
. In 2001, Bombieri and Zannier studied the Northcott property (N) for infinite Galois extensions of the rationals. In particular they provided a local property of the extensions that imply property (N). Later, Checcoli and Fehm demonstrated the existence of infinite extensions satisfying this local property. In this article, we establish two main results. First, we show that this local property, unlike property (N), is not preserved under finite extensions. Second, we show that, for an infinite Galois extension of ℚ \mathbb{Q} , such local property cannot be read from the Galois group. More precisely, we exhibit several profinite groups that can be realized over ℚ \mathbb{Q} by fields with and also by fields without this local property.



1.
Introduction

In this article, we let
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
denote a fixed algebraic closure of
\mathbb{Q}
. For an algebraic number
\alpha
, we denote by
h(\alpha)
denotes its absolute logarithmic Weil height defined as

h(\alpha)=\frac{1}{d}\left(\log\left(|a|\prod_{i=1}^{d}\max\left(1,|\alpha_{i}|\right)\right)\right)

where
a
is the leading coefficient of the minimal polynomial of
\alpha
over
\mathbb{Z}
, and
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d}
are the conjugates of
\alpha
over
\mathbb{Q}
(see
[
BG06
, Section 1]
for a more general definition).

Following Bombieri and Zannier
[
BZ01
]
, we say that a set of algebraic numbers
S\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
has the
Northcott property (N)
if the set

\{\alpha\in S\mid h(\alpha)\leq M\}

is finite for every positive real number
M
.

Every number field is known to have property (N) due to the Northcott theorem. However, for infinite extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
, there is no general criterion to decide whether they possess property (N) in general.

We now discuss what is known on this problem. In
[
BZ01
]
Bombieri and Zannier provided a first example of an infinite extension of
\mathbb{Q}
with property (N):
K^{(d)}_{ab}
, the compositum of all abelian extensions of degree at most
d
of a number field
K

[
BZ01
, Theorem 1]
. Later, in
[
Wid11
, Theorem 3]
, Widmer gives a criterion for an infinite extension of
\mathbb{Q}
to have property (N), based on the growth of the relative discriminants of its finite subextensions (see Subsection
8.1
for a precise statement).

In this article we investigate another criterion for the Northcott property
(N)
, for infinite Galois extensions of the rationals, in relation to certain
local conditions
of the extension. To state it we need to fix some notation.
For a (possibly infinite) Galois extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
and a rational prime number
p
, we define

\mathfrak{S}_{p}(L)=\frac{\log p}{e_{p}(L)(p^{f_{p}(L)}+1)}

where
e_{p}(L)
and
f_{p}(L)
are the (possibly infinite) ramification index and inertia degree of the extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
at the prime
p
. These quantities depend only on
p
, since
L/\mathbb{Q}
is Galois. We adopt the convention that
\mathfrak{S}_{p}(L)=0
if
L
cannot be embedded in a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. We denote by
\mathcal{P}
the set of all rational prime numbers. We then set

\mathfrak{S}(L)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\mathfrak{S}_{p}(L).

We say that a field
L\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
has
property
(\mathfrak{S})
, if
L/\mathbb{Q}
is Galois and
\mathfrak{S}(L)=+\infty
.
The following criterion from
[
BZ01
, Remark p.8]
is a straightforward consequence of
[
BZ01
, Theorem 2]
.

Criterion 1.1
.

Property
(\mathfrak{S})
implies property (N).

It is noteworthy that recent work of Dixit and Kala
[
DK24
, Theorem 1.1]
inspired by the work of Bombieri and Zannier
[
BZ01
, Theorem 2]
, generalize this criterion to the non-Galois case, i.e. that their result give a criterion for an infinite extension
L
over
\mathbb{Q}
, not necessarily Galois, to have property (N), and in the case where the extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
is Galois, their result is exactly the one of Bombieri and Zannier.

While, as remarked in
[
BZ01
, Remark p.8]
property
(\mathfrak{S})
holds true for finite Galois extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
(see for instance Proposition
2.1
), a natural question to consider is whether there exist infinite Galois extensions
L/\mathbb{Q}
with property
(\mathfrak{S})
.

In
[
CF21
]
, it was shown that such infinite Galois extensions do exist and, moreover, they can be constructed in a way so that they do not satisfy any other previously known criterion for the Northcott property (see
[
CF21
, Theorem 1.5]
and
[
CF24
, Theorem 1.2]
), such as Widmer’s criterion (see
[
Wid11
, Theorem 3]
and
[
Wid23
, Theorem 8]
) or the result of Bombieri and Zannier on
K_{ab}^{(d)}
(see
[
BZ01
, Theorem 2]
).

It is known that property
(N)
is preserved under finite extensions, as shown in
[
DZ08
, Theorem 2.1]
. In
[
CF21
, Remark 2.8]
Checcoli and Fehm raised the following question.

Question 1.2
.

Let
L/\mathbb{Q}
be a Galois extension such that
\mathfrak{S}(L)=+\infty
, and let
F/L
be a finite extension with
F/\mathbb{Q}
Galois. Is it true that
\mathfrak{S}(F)=+\infty
?

In this article, we provide a negative answer to this question, proving the following:

Theorem 1.3
.

There exists an infinite totally real Galois extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
such that
\mathfrak{S}(L)=+\infty
and
\mathfrak{S}(L(i))<\infty
. In particular, property
(\mathfrak{S})
is not preserved under finite extensions.

Our proof strategy for Theorem
1.3
relies on results, presented in Section
2
, concerning the natural density of certain subsets of rational primes in number fields (Subsection
2.1
) and the splitting of primes in certain finite Galois extensions (Subsection
2.2
). The field
L
in Theorem
1.3
is then constructed in Section
3
.

Following again
[
BZ01
]
, we say that a set of algebraic numbers
S
has the
Bogomolov property (B)
if there exists a constant
c=c(S)>0
such that for every
\alpha\in S
, either
h(\alpha)=0
or
h(\alpha)\geq c
.

In
[
ADZ14
]
, the authors studied property (B), for infinite Galois extensions of number fields, also in relation to the Galois group of the extension. They also introduced property (B) for profinite groups
[
ADZ14
, Definition 5.5]
. Here, we present an extension of their definition, in order to include, for instance, properties (N) and (
\mathfrak{S}
).
Let
(\star)
be some property of a subfield of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Definition 1.4
.

A profinite group
G
is said to have property
(\star)
over a number field
K
if, for every Galois extension
L/K
such that
\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)
is isomorphic to
G
,
L
has property
(\star)
.

By Northcott’s theorem, every finite group satisfies properties (N) and (B) over any number field. By
[
CZ11
, Proposition 2.1]
, any abelian group with finite exponent has property (N) over any number field. Furthermore, by
[
ADZ14
, Corollary 1.7]
and
[
Che13
, Theorem 1.2]
, any group such that
G/Z(G)
has finite exponent satisfies property (B) over any number field. Additionally, as noted in
[
BZ01
, Remark p.8]
, every finite group satisfies property
(\mathfrak{S})
over
\mathbb{Q}
. The second goal of this article is to study property
(\mathfrak{S})
over
\mathbb{Q}
for infinite profinite groups.

Question 1.5
.

Does there exist an infinite profinite group
G
that satisfies property
(\mathfrak{S})
over
\mathbb{Q}
?

In this article, we provide some evidence supporting a negative answer to this question.
A result by Checcoli and Fehm
[
CF21
, Theorem 1.2]
states that any family of finite solvable groups
(G_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
can be realized over
\mathbb{Q}
by an extension with property
(\mathfrak{S})
. This suggests that direct products of solvable groups are particularly interesting candidates to examine.

In
[
Che19
, Theorem 1]
Checcoli showed that certain direct products of solvable groups with unbounded exponent can be realized with infinite local degrees at each prime, and hence do not satisfy property
(\mathfrak{S})
since
\mathfrak{S}(L)
is trivially zero.

The second result of this article provides examples of groups that, although we know some of them can be realized over
\mathbb{Q}
by an extension with property
(\mathfrak{S})
, do not themselves satisfy property
(\mathfrak{S})
.
Moreover, this occurs for nontrivial reasons, as
[
Che19
, Theorem 1]
does not apply in the setting of groups without any restriction on the exponent.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
{G}=\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}G_{i}
be a direct product of nontrivial finite solvable groups or symmetric groups. Suppose that, for infinitely many indices
i
,
G_{i}
belongs to one of the following families:

(i)

Groups of odd order ;

(ii)

Abelian groups ;

(iii)

Hamiltonian groups ;

(iv)

Symmetric groups ;

(v)

Groups of even order having a cyclic
2
-Sylow subgroup.

Then
G
does not have property
(\mathfrak{S})
over
\mathbb{Q}
.

The key point in the proof of Theorem
1.6
is a criterion, referred to as property
(\mathcal{DL})
(see Definition
5.1
), for a finite group to admit sufficiently many linearly disjoint realizations over the rationals with certain local conditions. We investigate how property
(\mathcal{DL})
behaves under direct products of groups in Section
5
.
We then demonstrate that each class of groups mentioned in the statement of Theorem
1.6
satisfies this criterion. The proof relies, among other tools, on basic results on the resolution of certain embedding problems (Section
6
), the Grunwald problem (Subsection
7.1
), and auxiliary results on the splitting of primes (Proposition
7.2
).
In the final section, we further explore property (
\mathfrak{S}
) for fields. Specifically, we compare it to the result of Bombieri and Zannier
[
BZ01
, Theorem 2]
and the criterion of Widmer
[
Wid11
, Theorem 3]
, showing the existence of infinite extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
satisfying all three properties. Lastly, we use a result of Bombieri and Zannier
[
BZ01
, Theorem 2.1 and Example 2]
to derive a weak version of a result from
[
PTW22
, Theorem 3]
on the Northcott number of a field.

2.
Some preliminaries on splitting of primes in Galois extensions

2.1.
On totally split primes in finite Galois extensions of
\mathbb{Q}

In this subsection, we denote by
\mathcal{T}(F)
the set of rational prime numbers that split totally in a given number field
F
.

We also use the notation
f(x)\underset{x\rightarrow+\infty}{\sim}g(x)
if, for large
x
,

f(x)=h(x)g(x)\quad\text{with}\quad\underset{x\rightarrow+\infty}{\lim}h(x)=1.

We begin by proving that every finite group
G
has property
(\mathfrak{S})
.
This was already proved for instance in
[
CF21
, Proposition 2.1]
. We give a slightly different proof, also based on Chebotarev’s density theorem and the prime number theorem. The advantage of this proof is that we can easily use it to also prove Proposition
2.3
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a finite Galois extension. Then

\sum_{p\in\mathcal{T}(K)}\frac{1}{p}=+\infty.

In particular
\mathfrak{S}(K)=+\infty
.

Proof.

For
x>0
, we set

\pi_{K}(x)=|[0,x]\cap\mathcal{T}(K)|.

Since
K
is Galois, by Chebotarev’s density theorem (
[
Hei67
, Theorem 4]
,
[
Che25
]
), the natural density of
\mathcal{T}(K)
is
1/[K:\mathbb{Q}]
, that is

\pi_{K}(x)\underset{x\rightarrow+\infty}{\sim}\frac{x}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]\log(x)}.

So we have

\displaystyle\pi_{K}(x)\log\pi_{K}(x)

\displaystyle\underset{x\rightarrow+\infty}{\sim}\frac{x}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]\log(x)}\log\left(\frac{x}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]\log(x)}\right)\underset{x\rightarrow+\infty}{\sim}\frac{x}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]},

where, for the first equivalence, we used the elementary fact that

\log(f(x))\underset{x\rightarrow+\infty}{\sim}\log(g(x))\quad\text{if}\quad f(x)\underset{x\rightarrow+\infty}{\sim}g(x)\quad\text{and}\quad\underset{x\rightarrow+\infty}{\lim}g(x)=+\infty.

Therefore, if we denote by
p_{n}
the
n
-th element of
\mathcal{T}(K)
, by noting that
\pi_{K}(p_{n})=n
and applying the above result with
x=p_{n}
, we have

p_{n}\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\sim}[K:\mathbb{Q}]n\log n.

Since

\sum_{n=2}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{n\log n}=+\infty,

we deduce the sought-for result.
∎

Before proceeding, we recall the following well-known result, whose proof can be found in
[
Coh07
, Lemma 3.3.30]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
K
be a number field,
\mathfrak{p}
a prime ideal of
K
, and
L,L^{\prime}
two finite extensions of
K
. Then
\mathfrak{p}
splits completely in
L/K
and
L^{\prime}/K
if and only if it splits completely in the compositum extension
LL^{\prime}/K
.

Now we are going to examine which contribution to the divergence of the sum in
2.1
come from the primes that are congruent to
1
or to
3
modulo
4
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a finite Galois extension.

(i)

The set
\{p\in\mathcal{T}(K)\mid p\equiv 1\bmod{4}\}
is infinite and

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\equiv 1\bmod{4}\\
p\in\mathcal{T}(K)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}=+\infty.

(ii)

The set
\{p\in\mathcal{T}(K)\mid p\equiv 3\bmod{4}\}
is non-empty if and only if
i\notin K
.
Moreover, in this case the set is infinite, and

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\equiv 3\bmod{4}\\
p\in\mathcal{T}(K)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}=+\infty.

Proof.

To prove part (
i
), notice that
K(i)
is Galois over
\mathbb{Q}
as the compositum of two Galois extensions. By Lemma
2.2
, we have

\mathcal{T}(K(i))=\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{Q}(i))\cap\mathcal{T}(K)=\{p\in\mathcal{P}\mid p\equiv 1\bmod{4}\}\cap\mathcal{T}(K)

which is an infinite set.
We then conclude by applying Proposition
2.1
to
K(i)/\mathbb{Q}
.

To prove (
ii
), notice that
\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{Q}(i))=\{p\in\mathcal{P}\mid p\equiv 1\bmod{4}\},

so if
i\in K
, then
\{p\in\mathcal{T}(K)\mid p\equiv 3\bmod{4}\}=\emptyset
.

Now let’s suppose that
i\notin K
, and let
L=\mathbb{Q}(i)
. We have

\{p\in\mathcal{T}(K)\mid p\equiv 2,3\bmod{4}\}=\mathcal{T}(K)\setminus(\mathcal{T}(L)\cap\mathcal{T}(K)).

Since
i\notin K
we get
[KL:\mathbb{Q}]=2[K:\mathbb{Q}]
. Since
KL
and
K
are Galois over
\mathbb{Q}
we conclude from Chebotarev’s density theorem that the natural density of the sets
\mathcal{T}(K)
and
\mathcal{T}(KL)
are
1/[K:\mathbb{Q}]
and
1/[KL:\mathbb{Q}]
respectively. From Lemma
2.2
we get
\mathcal{T}(L)\cap\mathcal{T}(K)=\mathcal{T}(KL)
. Therefore, the natural density of the set
\mathcal{T}(K)\setminus(\mathcal{T}(L)\cap\mathcal{T}(K))
is given by

\frac{1}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}-\frac{1}{[KL:\mathbb{Q}]}=\frac{1}{2[K:\mathbb{Q}]},

which means that

|\{p\leq x\mid p\in\mathcal{T}(K)\text{ and }p\equiv 3\pmod{4}\}|\underset{x\rightarrow+\infty}{\sim}\frac{1}{2[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\frac{x}{\log(x)}.

And we conclude as in the proof of Proposition
2.1
, by noting that the
n
-th prime number that is congruent to
3\pmod{4}
and that splits totally in
K
, is equivalent to
2[K:\mathbb{Q}]n\log n
as
n
approaches infinity.
∎

2.2.
Quadratic extensions with given splitting behaviour

In this subsection, given a finite set of odd primes, we aim to construct quadratic number fields which exhibits a prescribed splitting behavior at the given primes.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
,
\mathcal{I}
, and
\mathcal{R}
be three finite and pairwise disjoint sets of odd prime numbers. Then there exists a quadratic totally real extension
F/\mathbb{Q}
and such that:

(i)

all primes of
\mathcal{S}
split totally in
F
;

(ii)

all primes of
\mathcal{I}
remain inert in
F
;

(iii)

all primes of
\mathcal{R}
ramify in
F
.

Proof.

For a finite set of primes
\mathcal{M}
, let
P_{\mathcal{M}}
denote the product of all its elements.
By the Chinese remainder theorem, we have a ring isomorphism

\varphi:\prod_{p\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\times\prod_{p^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}}\mathbb{Z}/p^{\prime}\mathbb{Z}\times\prod_{\ell\in\mathcal{R}}\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{2}\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/\left(P_{\mathcal{S}}P_{\mathcal{I}}P_{\mathcal{R}}^{2}\right)\mathbb{Z}.

Let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{|\mathcal{I}|}
be integers such that, for
1\leq j\leq|\mathcal{I}|
,
a_{j}
is not a quadratic residue modulo any prime in
\mathcal{I}
. Set
\mathcal{R}=\{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{|\mathcal{R}|}\}
and let
m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
be any representative of the class

\varphi(1,\ldots,1,a_{1},\ldots,a_{|\mathcal{I}|},\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{|\mathcal{R}|})\in\mathbb{Z}/\left(P_{\mathcal{S}}P_{\mathcal{I}}P_{\mathcal{R}}^{2}\right)\mathbb{Z}.

Let

m=\prod_{i=1}^{s}q_{i}^{2m_{i}+r_{i}}

be the factorization of
m
into distinct primes
q_{1},\ldots,q_{s}
, where
m_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
and
r_{i}\in\{0,1\}
and set

(1)

m^{\prime}=m\prod_{i=1}^{s}q_{i}^{-2m_{i}}=\prod_{i=1}^{s}q_{i}^{r_{i}}\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}.

We claim that the field
F=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{m^{\prime}})
satisfies the conditions in the statement. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
m^{\prime}\neq 1
, i.e., that
m
is not a square. Indeed, it suffices to take an additional odd prime
q
in the application of the Chinese Remainder Theorem and require that the class of
m
modulo
q
is not a quadratic residue.
Clearly, as
m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
is square-free,
F/\mathbb{Q}
is a quadratic totally real extension.

Notice that the discriminant of
F
is either
m^{\prime}
or
4m^{\prime}
. Since by the hypothesis and the definition of
m
, we have

\{2,q_{1},\ldots,q_{s}\}\cap\left(\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{I}\right)=\emptyset
, every prime in
\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{I}
does not ramify in
F
.

Let
p\in\mathcal{S}
. As
m\equiv 1\pmod{p}
it follows that each
q_{i}
has an inverse modulo
p
. Hence, we infer from (
1
) that

m^{\prime}\equiv\left(\prod_{i=1}^{s}q_{i}^{-m_{i}}\right)^{2}\pmod{p}

and this shows that
m^{\prime}
is a quadratic residue modulo
p
, so
p
splits totally in
F
, proving (
i
).

To prove (
ii
), notice also that, for every
\ell\in\mathcal{R}
,
m\equiv\ell\pmod{\ell^{2}}
, hence
\ell
divides exactly
m
and thus,
\ell
divides
m^{\prime}
. For every prime
p^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}
, there exists
1\leq j\leq|\mathcal{I}|
such that

m^{\prime}\equiv a_{j}\prod_{i=1}^{s}q_{i}^{-2m_{i}}\pmod{p^{\prime}}

so
m^{\prime}
is not a quadratic residue modulo
p^{\prime}
, otherwise
a_{j}
would be a quadratic residue modulo some
p^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}
, contradicting the hypothesis. Therefore, all elements in
\mathcal{I}
remain inert in
F
.

Finally, by the definition of
m
, we also have
\mathcal{R}\subset\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{s}\}
, therefore every prime of
\mathcal{R}
ramifies in
F
, proving (
iii
).
∎

Remark 2.5
.

We assumed that the sets in Proposition
2.4
only contain odd primes for simplicity, as this suffices for our application, but we can easily also control the splitting behavior at the prime
2
. Indeed, it is enough to add the extra condition in the application of the Chinese Remainder Theorem that
m\equiv 2\pmod{4}
if we want 2 to ramify,
m\equiv 1\pmod{8}
if we want
2
to split completely, or
m\equiv 5\pmod{8}
if we want
2
to remain inert. We can also require the extension
F/\mathbb{Q}
to be totally complex by choosing a negative integer
m<0
as the representative of the class in the proof.

Remark 2.6
.

We notice that a non-explicit proof of the above result follows directly from the Grunwald–Wang Theorem
[
Wan50
, Main Theorem]
(which answers a special case of the Grunwald Problem).
This asserts that, if
K
is a number field,
G
is a finite abelian group,
\mathcal{M}
is a finite set of places of
K
not containing
2
, and, for
v\in\mathcal{M}
,
L_{v}/K_{v}
is a finite Galois extension with Galois group
G_{v}
embeddable in
G
, then there exists a Galois extension of number fields
L/K
with Galois group
G
such that the completion of
L
at any place lying above
v
is exactly
L_{v}
.
Then Proposition
2.4
follows by taking
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{I}\cup\mathcal{R}
and
L_{p}=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
if
p\in\mathcal{S}
,
L_{p}
equal to the unique unramified quadratic extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
if
p\in\mathcal{I}
, or a quadratic ramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
if
p\in\mathcal{R}
and either a trivial or a non-trivial extension for the place at infinity, depending on whether we want the extension to be totally real or not.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

Before proceeding with the proof, we give an elementary result on the behavior of
\mathfrak{S}
on composita. Given a set
I\subset\mathbb{N}
and a set
(L_{i})_{i\in I}
of (possibly infinite) extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
, we denote by
\prod_{i\in I}L_{i}
their compositum in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.
We have the following:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{N}
and let
(L_{i})_{i\in I}
be a set of Galois extensions over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then we have

\mathfrak{S}\left(\prod_{i\in I}L_{i}\right)\leq\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\min_{i\in I}\left(\mathfrak{S}_{p}\left(L_{i}\right)\right).

Proof.

Set
L=\prod_{i\in I}L_{i}
.
Since
L/\mathbb{Q}
is Galois as the compositum of Galois extensions,
\mathfrak{S}(L)
is well defined. We have

\mathfrak{S}(L)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{\log p}{e_{p}(L)\left(p^{f_{p}(L)}+1\right)}.

Now the lemma follows noticing that

e_{p}(L)\geq\max_{i\in I}\left(e_{p}(L_{i})\right)
and
f_{p}(L)\geq\max_{i\in I}\left(f_{p}(L_{i})\right)
.
∎

We are now able to achieve our goal, namely to construct an infinite Galois extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
that satisfies
\mathfrak{S}(L)=+\infty
, but for which there exists a finite extension
F/L
such that
F/\mathbb{Q}
is Galois and
\mathfrak{S}(F)<+\infty
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Suppose that there exist an increasing sequence of positive integers
(n_{k})_{k\geq 1}
and a sequence of quadratic totally real number fields
(F_{k})_{k\geq 1}
such that, for every
k\geq 1
, the compositum
L_{k}=F_{1}\dots F_{k}
satisfies:

(a)

F_{k+1}
and
L_{k}
are linearly disjoint over
\mathbb{Q}
.

(b)

Every prime
p\leq n_{k}
that is congruent to
3\pmod{4}
splits totally in
F_{k}
, and every prime
p\leq n_{k}
that is congruent to
1\pmod{4}
remains inert in
F_{k}
.

(c)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathcal{P}_{k+1}\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{p}(L_{k})\geq 1
, where
\mathcal{P}_{k+1}=\{p\in\mathcal{P}\mid p\equiv 3\bmod{4},n_{k}\leq p<n_{k+1}\}
.

Then we claim that the fields
L=\prod_{k\geq 1}F_{k}
and
F=L(i)
satisfy the statement
of Theorem
1.3
.

Indeed, by condition (
a
),
L/\mathbb{Q}
is Galois with Galois group the direct product
\prod_{k\geq 1}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
and, as all the fields
F_{i}
are totally real,
i\notin L
. Hence
F/L
is a quadratic extension and

F/\mathbb{Q}
is Galois as it is the compositum of two Galois extensions.

Furthermore, by condition (
b
), it holds for every
k\in\mathbb{N}\backslash\{0\}
and every
p\in\mathcal{P}_{k}
that

\mathfrak{S}_{p}(L)=\mathfrak{S}_{p}(L_{k-1})
. Indeed, for each
p\in\mathcal{P}_{k}
, the prime
p
splits completely in every fields
F_{l}
with
l\geq k
, which implies that the ramification index and the inertia degree satisfy
e_{p}(L)=e_{p}(L_{k-1})
and
f_{p}(L)=f_{p}(L_{k-1})
, respectively.

Thus we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}(L)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\mathfrak{S}_{p}(L)\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathcal{P}\\
p\equiv 3\bmod{4}\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{p}(L)=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathcal{P}_{k}\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{p}(L)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathcal{P}_{k}\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{p}(L_{k-1})\overset{\eqref{(a)}}{\geq}\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}1=\infty.

On the other hand, using also Lemma
3.1
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}(F)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{S}(L\mathbb{Q}(i))=\mathfrak{S}\left(\mathbb{Q}(i)\prod_{k\geq 1}F_{k}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\min\left(\min_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathfrak{S}_{p}(F_{k}),\mathfrak{S}_{p}(\mathbb{Q}(i))\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathcal{P}\\
p\equiv 1\bmod{4}\end{subarray}}\min_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathfrak{S}_{p}(F_{k})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathcal{P}\\
p\not\equiv 1\bmod{4}\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{p}(\mathbb{Q}(i))

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{(b)}}{\leq}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathcal{P}\\
p\equiv 1\bmod{4}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log(p)}{e_{p}(L)(p^{2}+1)}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathcal{P}\\
p\not\equiv 1\bmod{4}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log(p)}{p^{2}+1}\leq\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{\log(p)}{p^{2}}<+\infty.

This proves our claim.

Now, to prove Theorem
1.3
, we are left to show the existence of two sequences
(n_{k})_{k\geq 1}
and
(F_{k})_{k\geq 1}
satisfying conditions (
a
), (
b
) and (
c
).

We construct such sequences inductively.
Set
n_{0}=1
and
F_{0}=\mathbb{Q}
.
Suppose we have already constructed
n_{1},\dots,n_{k}
and
F_{1},\dots,F_{k}
. Since the fields
F_{i}
are all quadratic totally real fields, their compositum
L_{k}
is also totally real, so
i\notin L_{k}
. Moreover
L_{k}/\mathbb{Q}
is Galois as the compositum of Galois extensions. Thus, by Proposition
2.3
(
ii
), we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathcal{P}\\
p\equiv 3\bmod{4}\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{S}_{p}(L_{k})=+\infty.

Thus, there exists
n_{k+1}>n_{k}
such that condition (
c
) holds.

According to Proposition
2.3
(
i
), we can take a rational prime
q
that is congruent to
1\pmod{4}
and splits totally in
L_{k}
. Now, by Proposition
2.4
, we can take a quadratic real field
F_{k+1}
such that all rational prime numbers
p\leq n_{k+1}
congruent to
3\pmod{4}
split totally in
F_{k+1}
, and all rational prime numbers
p\leq n_{k+1}
congruent to
1\pmod{4}
and

remain inert in
F_{k+1}
.

Then

satisfies (
b
) by definition. Let’s suppose, by contradiction, that

is not linearly disjoint from

. Since

is a quadratic extension, we necessarily have

. However,

is a rational prime number that remains inert in

and splits totally in

∎

5.
A general criterion for the failure of Property
(\mathfrak{S})
for direct products of groups

The following group property allows the realization of the group over

\mathbb{Q}
in a flexible way, combining a linear disjointness condition with a local constraint.

Definition 5.1

(Disjointness-Local Property)
.

A finite group
G
is said to have
property (
\mathcal{DL}
)
if for every finite Galois extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
, there is a Galois extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
such that
\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})=G
,
K\cap L=\mathbb{Q}
and
f_{p_{L}}(K)\geq 2
, where
p_{L}=\min\{p\in\mathcal{P}\setminus\{2\}\mid f_{p}(L)=1\}
.

The following diagram provides a schematic representation of this technical definition.

K\cap L=\mathbb{Q}
L\mbox{ (arbitrary finite Galois extension)}
K\mbox{ (Galois over }\mathbb{Q}\mbox{)}
f_{p_{L}}(L)=1

\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})=G
and
f_{p_{L}}(K)>1

It is worth noting that the group of two elements satisfies property
(\mathcal{DL})
, as can be seen from Proposition
2.4
, whereas the trivial group does not satisfy property
(\mathcal{DL})
.

We have the following lemma:

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group with property (
\mathcal{DL}
) and let
G^{\prime}
be a finite solvable group. Then the product
G\times G^{\prime}
has also property (
\mathcal{DL}
).

Proof.

Let
L/\mathbb{Q}
be a finite Galois extension and let
p_{L}
be the smallest odd prime such that
f_{p_{L}}(L)=1
. By hypothesis there is a Galois extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
such that
\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})=G
,
K\cap L=\mathbb{Q}
and
f_{p_{L}}(K)\geq 2
. Since, for every
n\geq 1
, the group
G^{\prime n}
is solvable, it follows from Shafarevich’s theorem
[
ILF90
, Theorem 5.6.1]
that it is realizable over
\mathbb{Q}
. By Galois correspondence, we can therefore find a Galois extension
F/\mathbb{Q}
such that
\mathrm{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q})=G^{\prime n}
, where
n
is strictly greater than the number of subfields of
LK
. This allows us to extract a subextension
K^{\prime}
of
F
such that
\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})=G^{\prime}
, and
LK\cap K^{\prime}=\mathbb{Q}
.
Therefore, the compositum
KK^{\prime}
is a Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
of group
G\times G^{\prime}
and
f_{p_{L}}(KK^{\prime})\geq f_{p_{L}}(K)\geq 2
.
∎

The following is our obstruction to Property (
\mathfrak{S}
) over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
H={G}\times{G^{\prime}}
such that
{G}
is the direct product of an infinite family of non-trivial finite groups with property (
\mathcal{DL}
) and
{G^{\prime}}
is a direct product of finite solvable groups. Then
G
does not have property
(\mathfrak{S})
over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof.

First, notice that we can write
H=\prod_{r\geq 1}G_{r}\times G^{\prime}_{r}
where, for every
r\geq 1
,
G_{r}
is a finite group with property
(\mathcal{DL})
and
G^{\prime}_{r}
is a finite solvable (possibly trivial) group. By Lemma
5.2
, the group
H_{r}=G_{r}\times G^{\prime}_{r}
has also property (
\mathcal{DL}
).
Let
p_{i}
denote the
i
-th odd prime number.

We inductively construct a sequence
(M_{r})_{r\geq 1}
such that:

(1)

M_{r}/\mathbb{Q}
is Galois and
\mathrm{Gal}(M_{r}/\mathbb{Q})=H_{r}
.

(2)

For every
i\leq r
,
f_{p_{i}}(M_{1}\ldots M_{r})\geq 2
.

(3)

M_{1}\ldots M_{r-1}\cap M_{r}=\mathbb{Q}
.

For
r=1
, as
H_{1}
satisfies property
(\mathcal{DL})
, taking
L=\mathbb{Q}
in the definition of
(\mathcal{DL})
, there is a Galois extension
M_{1}/\mathbb{Q}
of group
H_{1}
and such that
f_{p_{1}}(M_{1})=f_{3}(M_{1})\geq 2
. So
M_{1}
satisfies conditions (
1
), (
2
) and (

).

Suppose we have already constructed
M_{1},\ldots,M_{r-1}
. Let

i_{r}=\min\{i\in\mathbb{N}\mid f_{p_{i}}(M_{1}\ldots M_{r-1})=1\}.

By the inductive hypothesis (
2
), we necessarily have
i_{r}\geq r
. Set
L=M_{1}\ldots M_{r-1}
, so that
p_{i_{r}}=p_{L}
. Since
H_{r}
satisfies (
\mathcal{DL}
), there exists a Galois extension
M_{r}/\mathbb{Q}
of group

such that (
1
) and (

) hold. Now, let
i\leq r\leq i_{r}
. If
i<i_{r}
, then
f_{p_{i}}(M_{1}\cdots M_{r})\geq f_{p_{i}}(M_{1}\cdots M_{r-1})\geq 2
while, if

,
f_{p_{i}}(M_{1}\cdots M_{r})\geq f_{p_{i_{r}}}(M_{r})\geq 2
, so that (

is Galois with Galois group

Let

be an odd prime number. Then by (

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}(M)\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathcal{P}\\
p\neq 2\end{subarray}}\min_{r\geq 1}\left(\mathfrak{S}_{p}(M_{1}\cdots M_{r})\right)\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathcal{P}\\

Therefore,

over

.
∎

6.
Embedding Problems in Galois Theory and Scholz Extensions

In this section, we recall some results on embedding problems in Galois theory (see
[
ILF90
, Chapter 1]
for more material on this topic).

Let
K
be a fixed number field, and let
G_{K}=\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/K)
be the absolute Galois group of
K
.

Let
L/K
be a finite Galois extension such that
\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)
is isomorphic to a quotient
G/N
of a larger finite group
G
having a normal subgroup
N
. The embedding problem associated to this data asks whether there exists a Galois extenion
M/L
with
\mathrm{Gal}(M/L)=N
and such that
M/K
is also Galois with
\mathrm{Gal}(M/K)=G
.

In other words, given the following exact sequence of groups

(
\diamond
)

1\longrightarrow N\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}G\overset{\pi}{\twoheadrightarrow}\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)\longrightarrow 1

the associated embedding problem asks for the existence of an epimorphism
\varphi:G_{K}\rightarrow G
such that
\pi\circ\varphi=\psi
, where
\psi:G_{K}\rightarrow\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)
is the canonical restriction map.

If such an epimorphism
\varphi
exists, it is called a
(proper) solution
to the embedding problem.
In this case, setting
M=K^{\operatorname{Ker}(\varphi)}
the fixed field of
\operatorname{Ker}(\varphi)
and since
L=K^{\operatorname{Ker}(\psi)}
, we have
L\subset M

since
\operatorname{Ker}(\varphi)\subset\operatorname{Ker}(\pi\circ\varphi)=\operatorname{Ker}(\psi)
.
Thus, passing to the quotient,
\varphi
induces an isomorphism
\mathrm{Gal}(M/K)\simeq G
.

To solve certain embedding problems, we have a sufficient condition if the extension
L/K
is of a special type, namely a Scholz extension.
Before defining such extensions, we recall that, as usual, we denote by
\mathcal{O}_{K}
the ring of integers of a number field
K
and by
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}:K\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}
the norm function.
There are several definitions in the literature; here, we provide one that is clear and sufficient for our context, taken from
[
MM18
, p. 374]
.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
p
be a prime number and
n\geq 1
an integer. A Galois extension
L/K
of number fields is said to be
n
-Scholz for the prime
p
, if for any prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
that ramifies in
L/K
, and
\mathfrak{q}\subset\mathcal{O}_{L}
with
\mathfrak{q}\mid\mathfrak{p}
, the following three conditions hold:

(1)

\mathfrak{p}\nmid p
;

(2)

N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{p})\equiv 1\pmod{p^{n}}
;

(3)

the decomposition group
D({\mathfrak{q}}/{\mathfrak{p}})
equals the inertia group
I({\mathfrak{q}}/{\mathfrak{p}})
.

Remark 6.2
.

If
K=\mathbb{Q}
, then condition (
1
) is equivalent to the fact that
p
does not ramify in
L/\mathbb{Q}
and
L
is totally real and condition (
2
) can be restated by saying that if a prime
\ell
ramifies in
L/\mathbb{Q}
then
\ell\equiv 1\pmod{p^{n}}
. Moreover, condition (
3
) is equivalent to the fact that the ramified primes in
L/\mathbb{Q}
are totally ramified.

We have the following:

Theorem 6.3
.

(
[
MM18
, Proposition 10.3]
)
Let
L/K
be an
n
-Scholz extension of number fields for a rational prime number
p
. Suppose we have an exact sequence (

⋄

‣
6
) for some groups
G
and
N
such that
N\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
,
N
is contained in the center of
G
and the
p
-adic valuation of the exponent of
G
is at most
n
. Then the associated embedding problem has a solution.

7.
On certain groups with property
(\mathcal{DL})

7.1.
Property
(\mathcal{DL})
for groups of odd order

The first family we will examine consists of groups of odd order. This family differs from those studied later, as the arguments here rely on the Grunwald problem rather than the embedding problem.

Proposition 7.1
.

Any finite, nontrivial group of odd order has property
(\mathcal{DL})
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a finite group of odd order. Notice that
G
is solvable, by the Feit-Thompson’s theorem
[
FT62
, Theorem 1]
.
Let
L
be any finite Galois extension over
\mathbb{Q}
, and let
p
be the smallest odd prime with inertia degree of
1
in
L
.
Denote
\mathrm{Sub}(L)
the family of all number fields contained in
L
. For each
k\in\mathrm{Sub}(L)\setminus\mathbb{Q}
, choose
p_{k}\in\mathcal{P}
such that the inertia degree of
p_{k}
in
k/\mathbb{Q}
is strictly greater than
1
, and set
S=\{p_{k}\}_{k\in\mathrm{Sub}(L)}\cup\{\infty\}
. Let
q
be an odd prime that divides the order of
G
. By Cauchy’s theorem (
[
Lan02
, Theorem 3.2]
), we know that
\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}
is embeddable in
G
.

Consider the unique unramified extension
K_{p}
of degree
q
over the complete local field
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, which we know is Galois with
\mathrm{Gal}(K_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})=\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}
(see
[
Ser79
, Theorem 2]
) . Since
2
is relatively prime to the order of
G
, and
\mathbb{Q}
contains exactly
2
root of unity, by applying
[
Neu79
, Corollary 2]
we obtain a Galois extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
with Galois group
G
, such that every prime in
S
splits totally in
K
, and the completion of
K
with respect to the
p
-adic absolute value is exactly
K_{p}
. In particular,
K
is totally real, and
p
has an inertia degree greater than
q>2
in
K
.

Now, consider
L\cap K
. If
L\cap K\in\mathrm{Sub}(L)\setminus\mathbb{Q}
, then
p_{L\cap K}
splits completely in
L\cap K
since it does in
K
. However, by definition,
p_{L\cap K}
also has an inertia degree greater than
2
in
L\cap K
, which is a contradiction. Hence,
L\cap K=\mathbb{Q}
, concluding the proof.
∎

7.2.
Property
(\mathcal{DL})
for abelian groups

We begin this section by summarizing key results on the behavior of primes in quadratic extensions.

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
p
be a fixed odd prime and
m\geq 1
an integer. Then

•

There are infinitely many primes
q\equiv 1\pmod{2^{m}}
such that
p
remains inert in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q})
.

•

There are infinitely many primes
q\equiv 1\pmod{2^{m}}
such that
p
splits in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q})
.

Moreover, if
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
or
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, then

•

q
remains inert in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p})
if and only if
p
remains inert in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q})

•

q
splits in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p})
if and only if
p
splits in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q})
.

Proof.

Let
a\in\mathbb{Z}
be a non-zero quadratic residue modulo
p
. Choose an integer
t
such that
t\equiv a\pmod{p}
and
t\equiv 1\pmod{2^{m}}
, possible by the Chinese Remainder Theorem since
p
and
2^{m}
are coprime. By Dirichlet’s theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions, we can pick a prime
q\equiv t\pmod{2^{m}p}
to satisfy the desired property.

Now, if
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
or
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, by the quadratic reciprocity law (
[
Cox89
, Theorem 8.12]
), we have

\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)\left(\frac{q}{p}\right)=(-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2}\cdot\frac{q-1}{2}}=1,

so
\left(\frac{q}{p}\right)=1
if and only if
\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)=1
. This completes the proof.
∎

We next establish the following:

Proposition 7.3
.

The group
\mathbb{Z}/2^{n}\mathbb{Z}
has property
(\mathcal{DL})
for every integer
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

Let
L/\mathbb{Q}
be a finite Galois extension, and let
p
be the smallest odd prime such that
f_{p}(L)=1
. By Proposition
7.2
, we can find a prime
q\equiv 1\pmod{2^{n}}
such that
p
remains inert in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q})
. Note that
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q})/\mathbb{Q}
is an
n
-Scholz extension, so by Theorem
6.3
, there exists a cyclic
2^{n}
-extension
M/\mathbb{Q}
containing
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q})
such that the intersection
L\cap M
is either trivial or contains
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q})
.

Since
p
remains inert in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q})
and has inertia degree
1
over
L
, it follows that
L\cap M=\mathbb{Q}
. Thus, we conclude the proof.
∎

Corollary 7.4
.

Every nontrivial, abelian group satisfies property
(\mathcal{DL})
.

Proof.

This follows directly from Lemma
5.2
, Proposition
7.1
, Proposition
7.3
, and the classification of finite abelian groups.
∎

7.3.
Property
(\mathcal{DL})
for Hamiltonian groups and Dihedral group of order 8

Let
\mathbb{H}_{8}
denote the quaternion group of order
8
and
\mathbb{D}_{8}
the dihedral group of order
8
. Both groups are solvable and non-abelian, and the proof also relies on embedding problems.

Proposition 7.5
.

The groups
\mathbb{H}_{8}
and
\mathbb{D}_{8}
have property
(\mathcal{DL})
.

Proof.

Let
L/\mathbb{Q}
be a finite Galois extension, and let
p
be the smallest odd prime with inertia degree
1
in
L
. By Proposition
7.2
, we can choose a prime
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
such that
p
remains inert in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q})
. We then select another prime
l\equiv 1\pmod{4}
such that
q
splits in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{l})
, and
\sqrt{l},\sqrt{ql}\notin L
, possible by the infinitude of such primes in Proposition
7.2
.

Since
q\equiv 1\pmod{4}
,
l
also splits in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q})
, so
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q},\sqrt{l})/\mathbb{Q}
is a 2-Scholz extension. By Theorem
6.3
, we can solve the central embedding problems with cyclic kernels for the extension
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q},\sqrt{l})/\mathbb{Q}
.
This gives us two extensions,
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
, where
M_{1}
is a quaternion extension of order
8
and
M_{2}
is a dihedral extension of order
8
, both containing
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q},\sqrt{l})
.

Since
M_{1}
has a unique sub-quartic extension,
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q},\sqrt{l})
, and three sub-quadratic extensions,
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q})
,
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{l})
, and
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{ql})
, any subfield of
M_{1}
must contain one of these fields. However, by construction,
\sqrt{l},\sqrt{ql}\notin L
, and
\sqrt{q}\notin L
since
p
is inert in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q})
. Thus,
L\cap M_{1}=\mathbb{Q}
.

The same reasoning applies to
M_{2}
, concluding the proof.
∎

Corollary 7.6
.

Every Hamiltonian group satisfies property
(\mathcal{DL})
.

Proof.

This follows directly from Lemma
5.2
, Corollary
7.4
, Proposition
7.5
, and the classification of Hamiltonian groups by Dedekind and Baer (see
[
Rob95
, 5.3.7]
).
∎

7.4.
Property
(\mathcal{DL})
for the Symmetric group

In this case we also study the splitting behavior of primes in quadratic extensions and use results on embedding problems.

Proposition 7.7
.

For every integer
n\geq 2
, the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
has property
(\mathcal{DL})
.

Proof.

Let
i_{k}>2
,
L
be a finite Galois extension over
\mathbb{Q}
, and
p
the smallest odd prime with inertia degree 1 in
L
. Consider a quadratic extension
F/\mathbb{Q}
where
p
remains inert. By a specific case of the embedding problem
[
Gad76
, Theorem 2.7]
, we can construct, an
\mathfrak{S}_{i_{k}}
-extension
M/\mathbb{Q}
, with
F\subset M
. Assume
i_{k}\neq 4
.

The field
L\cap M
, as a Galois subfield of
M
, must equal
\mathbb{Q}
,
F
, or
M
, since
M
is an
\mathfrak{S}_{i_{k}}
-extension and the only normal subgroups of
\mathfrak{S}_{i_{k}}
(for
i_{k}\neq 4
) are
\mathfrak{S}_{i_{k}}
,
\mathfrak{A}_{i_{k}}
, and
\{1_{\mathfrak{S}_{i_{k}}}\}
. Since
p
is inert in
F
and has inertia degree 1 in
L
, it follows that
L\cap M=\mathbb{Q}
.

For
i_{k}=4
,
\mathfrak{S}_{4}
has an additional normal subgroup, the Klein four-group, meaning
[L\cap M:\mathbb{Q}]=6
could be possible. However,
(L\cap M)/\mathbb{Q}
, being Galois, would then have a unique sub-quadratic extension
F
, which is a contradiction. Thus,
[L\cap M:\mathbb{Q}]=6
cannot occur, completing the proof.
∎

7.5.
Property
(\mathcal{DL})
for groups with cyclic
2
-Sylow subgroups

We begin with a classical result, which is a specific case of a more general theorem in
[
Sco64
, 6.2.11]
.

Proposition 7.8
.

Let
G
be a finite group of order
2^{j}q
with
j\geq 1
and
q
odd, having a cyclic
2
-Sylow subgroup. Then
G
has a unique normal subgroup of order
q
and is therefore solvable.

We then have the following:

Proposition 7.9
.

Every finite group with a cyclic
2
-Sylow subgroup has property
(\mathcal{DL})
.

Proof.

Let
L/\mathbb{Q}
be a finite Galois extension and
p
the smallest odd prime with inertia degree 1 in
L
. For a fixed integer
l
, let
G_{l}
be a group with a cyclic
2
-Sylow subgroup
\mathbb{Z}/2^{j_{l}}\mathbb{Z}
and (Proposition
7.8
) unique normal subgroup
H_{l}
of odd order.
Using Proposition
7.2
, we select
n=|SF(L)|
quadratic fields
F_{1},\ldots,F_{n}
such that
p
remains inert in each and
F_{i}\cap F_{k}=\mathbb{Q}
for
i\neq k
. For each
F_{k}
, by proceeding as in the proof of Proposition
7.3
, we can construct a cyclic
2^{j_{l}}
-extension
M_{k}
where
F_{k}
is the unique sub-quadratic field. Thus,
M_{1}\cdots M_{n}
is Galois over
\mathbb{Q}
with Galois group
\left(\mathbb{Z}/2^{j_{l}}\mathbb{Z}\right)^{n}
.
Since
\prod_{k=1}^{n}H_{l}
is solvable and has order coprime to
\mathrm{Gal}(M_{1}\cdots M_{n}/\mathbb{Q})
, by
[
ILF90
, Theorem 5.6.2]
we can find a Galois extension
N/\mathbb{Q}
with
\mathrm{Gal}(N/\mathbb{Q})=\prod_{k=1}^{n}G_{l}
and
M_{1}\cdots M_{n}\subset N
.

Let
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{n}
be
G_{l}
-extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
within
N
such that they are linearly disjoint and
M_{k}\subset\Delta_{k}
for each
k
. For
i\neq j
, we have:

\Delta_{i}\cap L\neq\Delta_{j}\cap L\quad\text{or}\quad\Delta_{i}\cap L=\Delta_{j}\cap L=\mathbb{Q}.

Suppose
\Delta_{i}\cap L\neq\mathbb{Q}
for all
i
. Then, the map
i\mapsto\Delta_{i}\cap L
would be injective and bijective since
n=|SF(L)|
, implying that
\mathbb{Q}
has a preimage, which is a contradiction. Therefore, there exists an
i
such that
\Delta_{i}\cap L=\mathbb{Q}
.

Since
p
is inert in
F_{i}\subset M_{i}\subset\Delta_{i}
, it has an inertia degree greater than 2 in
\Delta_{i}
. Thus,
\mathrm{Gal}(\Delta_{i}/\mathbb{Q})=G_{l}
,
\Delta_{i}\cap L=\mathbb{Q}
, and
p
have an inertia degree greater than 2 in
\Delta_{i}
, completing the proof.
∎

Remark 7.10
.

Following the method employed in the proof of Proposition
7.1
, it appears plausible that a result analogous to Theorem
1.6
could be established by relying on a different variant of the inverse Galois problem, namely the Grunwald problem, which has already been discussed in this article. This would concern groups for which the Grunwald problem admits a positive solution, at least for cyclic extensions over
p
-adic fields. Notable examples include groups admitting a generic Galois extension over
\mathbb{Q}
(see
[
Sal82
, Theorem 5.9]
), such as the alternating group
\mathfrak{A}_{5}
(
[
Sal82
, Theorem 5.1]
and
[
Mae89
, Theorem]
), as well as the symmetric groups.

With appropriate adjustments and refinements to the construction used in Proposition
7.1
, it seems reasonable to expect that the result could extend to groups for which the
tame
Grunwald problem holds, namely, when the problem is restricted to places not dividing the order of the group
G
. This version, which avoids the Brauer–Manin obstruction, includes examples such as the quaternion group, the dihedral group of order 8, and more generally, supersolvable groups
[
HW20
, Corollaire p.3]
.

In particular, if the
tame
Grunwald problem has a solution for all solvable groups, it would likely imply Theorem
1.6
for this class of groups. This observation supports the heuristic expectation that the general answer to Question
1.5
might be negative.

\mathfrak{S}
)

8.1.
On the intersection of three criteria for the Northcott property

In this subsection, we aim to compare Criterion
1.1
, Bombieri and Zannier’s criterion for the Northcott property
[
BZ01
, Theorem 1]
, and Widmer’s criterion for the Northcott property
[
Wid11
, Theorem 3]
. We recall that
K^{(d)}_{ab}
denote the compositum of all abelian extensions of degree at most
d
of a number field
K
. While we have already discussed Property (
\mathfrak{S}
), let us briefly recall the other two criteria.

Theorem 8.1
.

(
Bombieri and Zannier
[
BZ01
, Theorem 1]
)

Let
K
be a number field. Then, property (N) holds for the field
K^{(d)}_{ab}

, for any
d\geq 2
.

The next one, which is Widmer’s criterion, is based on
the growth of certain discriminants in infinite towers of number fields.

Theorem 8.2
.

(
Widmer
[
Wid11
, Theorem 1.3]
)

Let
K
be a number field. Let
K=K_{0}\subsetneq K_{1}\subsetneq K_{2}\subsetneq
…. be a nested sequence of finite extensions and set
L=\bigcup_{i}K_{i}
. Suppose that

(2)

\inf_{K_{i-1}\subsetneq M\subseteq K_{i}}\left(N_{K_{i-1}/\mathbb{Q}}\left(D_{M/K_{i-1}}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{\left[M:K_{0}\right]\left[M:K_{i-1}\right]}}\longrightarrow\infty

as
i
tends to infinity where the infimum is taken over all intermediate fields
M
strictly larger than
K_{i-1}
,
D_{M/K_{i-1}}
denotes the relative discriminant of the extension
M/K_{i-1}
and
N_{K_{i-1}/\mathbb{Q}}\left(D_{M/K_{i-1}}\right)
denotes the unique positive integer generating the norm ideal. Then the field
L
has the Northcott property.

Examples of infinite extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
satisfying one of the three criteria but not the others have already been constructed. For instance, in
[
CF21
, Theorem 1.5]
, an infinite Galois extension of the rationals is constructed that satisfies property (
\mathfrak{S}
), but does not satisfy condition (
2
) of Theorem
8.2
and is not contained in any
K^{(d)}_{ab}
for any number field
K
and
d\geq 2
.
One might ask whether only finite Galois extensions of the rationals satisfy all three conditions simultaneously. However, here we provide an infinite Galois extension of the rationals that satisfies all three conditions.

Proposition 8.3
.

There exists an infinite Galois extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
contained in
\mathbb{Q}_{ab}^{(2)}
, satisfying property (
\mathfrak{S}
) and condition (
2
) of Theorem
8.2
.

Proof.

Let set
n_{1}=1
and
p_{1}=2
. We construct inductively a sequence of integers
(n_{i})_{i\geq 1}
and an increasing sequence of prime numbers
(p_{i})_{i\geq 1}
such that:

•

Every prime
p\leq n_{i}
splits totally in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p_{i}})
,

•

\sum_{n_{i-1}<p\leq n_{i}}\mathfrak{S}_{p}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p_{1}},\ldots,\sqrt{p_{i-1}}))\geq 1.

Assume
p_{1},\ldots,p_{i-1}
and
n_{1},\ldots,n_{i-1}
have already been constructed. By Proposition
2.1
, we can choose
n_{i}
such that:

\sum_{n_{i-1}<p\leq n_{i}}\mathfrak{S}_{p}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p_{1}},\ldots,\sqrt{p_{i-1}}))\geq 1.

Let
a=\prod_{2\leq q\leq n_{i}}q
and consider the arithmetic progression
1,a+1,2a+1,\ldots
. By Dirichlet’s theorem on arithmetic progressions, we can choose a prime
p_{i}
such that
p_{i}>\max(n_{i},p_{i-1})
and
p_{i}\equiv 1\mod a
. In particular, every prime
2\leq q\leq n_{i}
is a square in
\mathbb{Z}/p_{i}\mathbb{Z}
, so it splits totally in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p_{i}})
.

We claim that the field
L=\mathbb{Q}((\sqrt{p_{i}})_{i\geq 1})
satisfies the proposition.

Indeed, as a compositum of quadratic extensions, it is clear that
L\subset\mathbb{Q}_{\mathrm{ab}}^{(2)}
. Additionally, property
(\mathfrak{S})
is satisfied due to the conditions imposed during its construction.

To verify this, define
L_{i}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p_{1}},\ldots,\sqrt{p_{i}})
. We compute:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}(L)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\mathfrak{S}_{p}(L)=\sum_{i=2}^{+\infty}\sum_{n_{i-1}<p\leq n_{i}}\mathfrak{S}_{p}(L)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=2}^{+\infty}\sum_{n_{i-1}<p\leq n_{i}}\mathfrak{S}_{p}(L_{i-1})\geq\sum_{i=2}^{+\infty}1=\infty.

Finally,
L
satisfies condition (
2
) of Theorem
8.2
. For simplicity, we assume that
p_{i}\equiv 1\pmod{4}
for all
i\geq 2
, so that the discriminant of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p_{i}})
is exactly
p_{i}
. By
[
Neu99
, Corollary (2.10) III]
and
[
Hil98
, Theorem 88]
, we have for all
i\geq 2
:

N_{L_{i}/\mathbb{Q}}(D_{L_{i+1}/L_{i}})=\frac{D_{L_{i+1}/\mathbb{Q}}}{D_{L_{i}/\mathbb{Q}}^{[L_{i+1}:L_{i}]}}=\frac{D_{L_{i}/\mathbb{Q}}^{2}D_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p_{i+1}})/\mathbb{Q}}^{[L_{i}:\mathbb{Q}]}}{D_{L_{i}/\mathbb{Q}}^{[L_{i+1}:L_{i}]}}=D_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p_{i+1}})/\mathbb{Q}}^{[L_{i}:\mathbb{Q}]}=p_{i+1}^{[L_{i}:\mathbb{Q}]}.

Thus, we have:

N_{L_{i}/\mathbb{Q}}(D_{L_{i+1}/L_{i}})^{\frac{1}{[L_{i+1}:\mathbb{Q}][L_{i+1}:L_{i}]}}=p_{i+1}^{\frac{[L_{i}:\mathbb{Q}]}{[L_{i+1}:\mathbb{Q}][L_{i+1}:L_{i}]}}=p_{i+1}^{\frac{1}{[L_{i+1}:L_{i}]^{2}}}=p_{i+1}^{\frac{1}{4}},

As
p_{i+1}^{\frac{1}{4}}\to\infty
as
i\to\infty
, we conclude the proof.

∎

8.2.
On Northcott Numbers

Following the work of Robinson
[
Rob62
]
, the concept of the Northcott number for a set of algebraic numbers was introduced by Vidaux and Videla
[
VV16
, p. 59]
, with the aim of studying certain decidability problems in rings of integers. Among other results, they demonstrated that any subring of a ring of totally real integers possessing the Northcott property has an undecidable first-order theory
[
VV16
, Theorem 2]
.

The
Northcott number
of a set of algebraic numbers
X
is denoted by

\mathcal{N}_{X}=\inf\left\{t\in[0,+\infty)\;|\;\#\{\alpha\in X\;|\;h(\alpha)<t\}=\infty\right\}.

They posed the following natural question:

Question 8.4
.

(
[
VV16
, Question 6]
)

Which real numbers can be realized as Northcott numbers of ring extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
?

It is straightforward to see that
0
is a Northcott number (taking
X=\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
), as is
\infty
(taking
X
to be any set with the Northcott property, such as a number field).

In
[
PTW22
, Theorem 3]
, it was shown that for any
t\geq 0
, there exists a field
L\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
satisfying

t\leq\mathcal{N}_{h}(L)\leq\mathcal{N}_{h}\left(\mathcal{O}_{L}\right)\leq 2t.

The fields in their constructions are of the form
\mathbb{Q}\left(p_{1}^{1/d_{1}},p_{2}^{1/d_{2}},p_{3}^{1/d_{3}},\ldots\right)
where the
p_{i}
are prime numbers and the
d_{i}
are suitably chosen integers. Their method leverages Widmer’s criterion for the Northcott property
[
Wid11
, Theorem 3]
, which is particularly easy to apply for fields of this form (as already used in the proof of Proposition
8.3
).

In this section, we establish a
weak version
of this result but which does not rely on Widmer’s criterion for the Northcott property. Instead, we use property (
\mathfrak{S}
) and, more specifically, the following result, which is a direct consequence of
[
BZ01
, Theorem 2 and Example 2]
:

Theorem 8.5
.

For any finite set of primes
\mathcal{S}
, let

\mathbb{Q}^{t_{\mathcal{S}}}

be the maximal subfield of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
where all primes in
\mathcal{S}
split totally. Then,

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{\log(p)}{p+1}\leq\underset{\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}^{t_{\mathcal{S}}}}{\liminf}\,h(\alpha)=\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{Q}^{t_{\mathcal{S}}}}\leq\sum_{p\in\mathcal{S}}\frac{\log(p)}{p-1},

where the
\liminf
is taking with respect to set inclusion.

This approach provides a different method compared to that of
[
PTW22
, Theorem 3]
, and the fields constructed through this method, achieving a Northcott number in the desire range, are of an entirely different nature. Specifically, they are determined by local properties.

Proposition 8.6
.

Let
r>0
be a real number. For every real
\epsilon>0
, there exists a finite set
\mathcal{S}
of prime numbers such that

r-\epsilon<\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{Q}^{t_{\mathcal{S}}}}\leq 2r.

Proof.

In view of applying Theorem
8.5
, we aim to choose
\mathcal{S}
so that both sums that appears in the statement lie in
[r-\epsilon,2r]
. Let
(p_{i})_{i\geq 1}
denote the increasing sequence of all primes. Observe that:

\sum_{i\geq 1}\frac{\log p_{i}}{p_{i}-1}-\frac{\log p_{i}}{p_{i}+1}=\sum_{i\geq 1}\frac{2\log p_{i}}{(p_{i}-1)(p_{i}+1)}<+\infty.

Thus, we can choose an integer
l
such that:

(3)

\sum_{i\geq l}\left(\frac{\log(p_{i})}{p_{i}-1}-\frac{\log(p_{i})}{p_{i}+1}\right)\leq\epsilon.

Select an integer
j
such that:

(4)

\frac{\log p_{j}}{p_{j}+1}<\epsilon.

Set
c=\max(j,l)
, and let
j_{0}
be the largest integer such that:

\sum_{i=c}^{j_{0}}\frac{\log p_{i}}{p_{i}-1}\leq 2r.

Now, by (
3
), (
4
) and the definition of
j_{0}
we have:

2r<\sum_{i=c}^{j_{0}+1}\frac{\log(p_{i})}{p_{i}-1}\leq\sum_{i=c}^{j_{0}}\frac{\log(p_{i})}{p_{i}+1}+2\epsilon.

Thus:

r-\epsilon<\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=c}^{j_{0}}\frac{\log(p_{i})}{p_{i}+1}.

The field
\mathbb{Q}^{t_{\mathcal{S}}}
, with
\mathcal{S}=\{p_{c},\ldots,p_{j_{0}}\}
, has the desired Northcott number according to Theorem
8.5
.
∎

It is noteworthy that Question
8.4
has been answered completely by Okazaki and Sano
[
OS23
, Theorem 3]
, using a refined version of the construction in
[
PTW22
, Theorem 3]
.

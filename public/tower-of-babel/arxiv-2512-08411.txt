Prismatic World Model: Learning Compositional Dynamics for Planning in Hybrid Systems
Mingwei Li, Xiaoyuan Zhang, Chengwei Yang, Zilong Zheng, Yaodong Yang

Abstract
Model-based planning in robotic domains is fundamentally challenged by the hybrid nature of physical dynamics, where continuous motion is punctuated by discrete events such as contacts and impacts. Conventional latent world models typically employ monolithic neural networks that enforce global continuity, inevitably over-smoothing the distinct dynamic modes (e.g., sticking vs. sliding, flight vs. stance). For a planner, this smoothing results in catastrophic compounding errors during long-horizon lookaheads, rendering the search process unreliable at physical boundaries. To address this, we introduce the Prismatic World Model (PRISM-WM), a structured architecture designed to decompose complex hybrid dynamics into composable primitives. PRISM-WM leverages a context-aware Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) framework where a gating mechanism implicitly identifies the current physical mode, and specialized experts predict the associated transition dynamics. We further introduce a latent orthogonalization objective to ensure expert diversity, effectively preventing mode collapse. By accurately modeling the sharp mode transitions in system dynamics, PRISM-WM significantly reduces rollout drift. Extensive experiments on challenging continuous control benchmarks, including high-dimensional humanoids and diverse multi-task settings, demonstrate that PRISM-WM provides a superior high-fidelity substrate for trajectory optimization algorithms (e.g., TD-MPC), proving its potential as a powerful foundational model for next-generation model-based agents.



1
Introduction

The efficacy of Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) and planning is strictly bounded by the predictive fidelity of the underlying world model
(
22
)
. While latent variable models have enabled planning directly from high-dimensional observations by abstracting away pixel details
(
12
;
16
)
, they face a critical hurdle in robotic domains: the hybrid nature of real-world physics. Legged locomotion and manipulation involve systems that continuously switch between discrete contact modes, resulting in non-smooth and highly non-linear dynamics.

Prevailing approaches typically approximate these complex dynamics using a single, monolithic neural network (e.g., an MLP or RNN)
(
18
;
11
)
. While theoretically capable of universal approximation, in practice, these models tend to learn an “average” representation that smooths over sharp discontinuities inherent in contact events. For a planner effectively simulating a trajectory (as seen in methods like TD-MPC), this smoothing is fatal. A minor prediction error at a contact boundary leads to rapid divergence of the imagined state from reality. This compounding error forces planners to rely on dangerously short horizons, severely limiting the agent’s ability to reason about long-term consequences.

We argue that to overcome these limitations, a world model should incorporate structural priors inspired by hybrid systems. Drawing inspiration from the structure of hybrid systems, we propose to approximate the underlying physics by learning a decomposition of specialized latent regimes from data.
Instead of forcing a single function to fit all regimes, PRISM-WM represents the global dynamics as a composition of simpler, learnable “base dynamics.”
In this view, the gating network acts as a context-aware switching mechanism to identify the active physical regime, while experts capture the continuous flow within each mode, bridging the gap between flexible neural learning and structured symbolic control.

In this work, we introduce the Prismatic World Model (PRISM-WM). Drawing inspiration from how a prism separates light into its constituent colors, PRISM-WM decomposes the transition function using a context-aware Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture:

•

Mode Identification: A gating network acts as a learnable switching function, analyzing the current context (state, action, or task identity) to implicitly determine the active physical regime (e.g., identifying a “contact” vs. “flight” phase).

•

Specialized Dynamics: A set of expert networks compete to model specific local dynamics. By specializing, these experts avoid the interference and smoothing issues of monolithic models.

•

Orthogonality for Distinct Primitives: To ensure the learned primitives are structurally distinct, we introduce an orthogonalization layer, encouraging the model to learn a basis of non-redundant dynamic forces.

We integrate PRISM-WM into the TD-MPC planning framework
(
18
)
. Our experiments across challenging domains, including high-dimensional Humanoid control and multi-task benchmarks, demonstrate that:

1.

PRISM-WM significantly lowers prediction error over longer horizons compared to monolithic baselines.

2.

The model spontaneously learns to cluster states into meaningful physical modes (e.g., locomotion vs. balance) without explicit supervision.

3.

By providing a more accurate causal model, PRISM-WM enables the planner to solve complex tasks that monolithic baselines fail to master.

3
Method

Our work introduces the
Prismatic World Model (PRISM-WM)
, a novel world model architecture designed to enhance both online planning and direct policy learning paradigms within MBRL. We first review the fundamentals of latent world models and the two mainstream frameworks they support, highlighting their shared dependency on the model’s representational fidelity. Then, we detail the architecture of PRISM-WM and explain how it serves as a unified foundation for improving both frameworks.

3.1
Preliminaries

A latent world model is trained to predict the future in a compact, learned latent space. It typically consists of three core components: an Encoder
q(z_{t}|o_{t})
mapping observations to latent states, a Dynamics Model
p(z_{t+1}|z_{t},a_{t})
, and a Reward Model
p(r_{t}|z_{t},a_{t})
. These are trained on real-world data. Once trained, they can be used for control in two primary ways:

1. Online Planning

This paradigm uses the world model as a simulator for online planning. At each timestep, a planning algorithm like the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) imagines multiple action sequences
\{a_{t:t+H-1}\}
over a short horizon
H
. The value of each sequence is evaluated by rolling out the dynamics and accumulating rewards:

G(z_{t},a_{t:t+H-1})=\sum_{k=t}^{t+H-1}\gamma^{k-t}\hat{r}_{k}+\gamma^{H}\hat{V}(\hat{z}_{t+H})

(1)

The first action of the best sequence is executed.
The critical challenge here is the compounding error: even small one-step prediction errors in the dynamics model can accumulate over the planning horizon
H
, leading to wildly inaccurate long-term predictions and, consequently, suboptimal decisions.

2. Direct Policy Learning

This paradigm aims to eliminate the costly online planning loop by learning a policy
\pi_{\theta}(a_{t}|z_{t})
directly. The world model is treated as a differentiable simulator. The objective for the policy
\pi_{\theta}
is to maximize the expected return from imagined trajectories:

\mathcal{L}_{\pi}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\hat{z}_{t:t+H}\sim p,a_{k}\sim\pi_{\theta}}\left[\sum_{k=t}^{t+H-1}\gamma^{k-t}\hat{r}_{k}+\gamma^{H}\hat{V}(\hat{z}_{t+H})\right]

(2)

The policy is updated via gradients backpropagated through time (BPTT). The key challenge here is the quality of the gradients: a world model with a non-smooth or biased loss landscape can provide noisy or misleading gradients, making stable and effective training of the policy extremely difficult.

Context-Aware Gating.

The design of the context vector

In single-task settings, we define

In multi-task settings, we concatenate the task embedding

This composite context allows the model to simultaneously disentangle task-specific physics while retaining the ability to handle multi-modal dynamics within each task.

\displaystyle v_{k}

\displaystyle=E_{k}(x_{t}).

3.2
Framework for Planning and Policy Learning

PRISM-WM acts as a superior, drop-in replacement for the standard world model, universally enhancing both MBRL paradigms by addressing their core challenges.

Architectural Implications for MBRL Paradigms.

The design of the PRISM-WM architecture is not only aimed at improving representational capacity but is also intrinsically aligned with solving the core challenges of the two dominant MBRL paradigms. For online planning methods like TD-MPC, which are highly sensitive to compounding errors, planning accuracy degrades exponentially with the one-step model error. A monolithic network, forced to model the entire, often non-convex, dynamics function, is prone to high approximation errors in certain regions of the state space. For direct policy learning methods like PWM, which depend on the quality of backpropagated gradients, the advantage arises from the soft compositional nature of the architecture. The dynamics function of a complex environment can be highly non-smooth, leading to a volatile and difficult-to-optimize loss landscape for a policy.

PRISM-WM: One Model, Two Gains

Replacing the monolithic world model with PRISM-WM yields complementary benefits for both planning-centric and policy-centric MBRL. In TD-MPC the mixture tiles the state–action space into
K
low-complexity regions; the gate routes each query to the most competent expert, reducing the one-step error from
\varepsilon
to roughly
\varepsilon/K^{\alpha}\;(0<\alpha<1)
and thereby curbing long-horizon compounding, which translates into a more reliable search space for CEM. Besides, when used as the differentiable simulator in PWM, the same mixture acts as a convex blend of Lipschitz experts, smoothing the loss landscape seen by the policy. Consequently, the gradients flowing back through the PRISM-WM are cleaner and more stable, facilitating a more efficient and effective policy optimization process.

Under normal conditions, the dominant low-entropy regime confirms that the continuous gate spontaneously converges to quasi-discrete allocations, maintaining a rhythmic and sparse expert activation. An external impulse triggers an immediate phase shift: the system momentarily transitions—switching instantly to specialized ”stabilizer” experts (Experts 3 & 4)—before executing a physical damped return to the stable limit cycle. By capturing these sharp hybrid transitions, PRISM-WM effectively eliminates the over-smoothing artifacts typical of monolithic baselines, proving it learns a causal, state-dependent decomposition.

5
Conclusion

We introduced Prismatic World Model (PRISM-WM) to address the over-smoothing of hybrid dynamics in monolithic models.
By enforcing latent orthogonalization, our approach reveals that structure matters more than scale: PRISM-WM outperforms significantly larger vanilla MoE baselines by forcing experts to specialize in mutually exclusive dynamics rather than merely expanding capacity. Qualitative analysis further confirms that the gating mechanism captures semantic physical regimes, exhibiting sharp mode transitions and damped recovery under perturbation.

This ”decompose-and-compose” strategy significantly enhances the model’s ability to accurately represent multi-modal dynamics and mitigate negative interference in multi-task settings. Our results demonstrate that PRISM-WM serves as a robust backbone for dominant MBRL paradigms.
For online planning (TD-MPC), it ensures higher-quality planning and decision-making over extended horizons, mitigating artifacts like ”ground penetration” to preserve value estimation validity.
For policy learning (PWM), it provides a high-fidelity simulator that stabilizes gradient propagation.
Our extensive experiments on challenging continuous control benchmarks confirmed that decomposing complex physics into orthogonal primitives is key to mastering contact-rich control.

6
Limitations and Future Work

Despite its promising results, our work has several limitations that open avenues for future research. Firstly, the number of experts
K
remains a manually tuned hyperparameter. Developing methods for automatically determining the optimal number of experts or even dynamically adjusting it during training would be a valuable extension. Moreover, while the gating mechanism is effective, exploring more sophisticated composition functions beyond a simple weighted sum could further improve performance. Looking ahead, applying PRISM-WM to more complex, real-world robotic scenarios, particularly those characterised by partial observability and diverse physical interactions, will be a crucial next step to validate its practical utility and scalability. We believe the principle of dynamic decomposition remains a compelling pathway toward building more capable and general-purpose world models for intelligent agents.

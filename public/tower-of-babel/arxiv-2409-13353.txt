Introduction

In this work, we study the global (in time) existence of small data solutions to the Cauchy problem for the semilinear wave equations with
scale-invariant time-dependent damping
and power-nonlinearity of derivative type

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}u-\ \Delta u+{\frac{\mu}{1+t}}\partial_{t}u=f(\partial_{t}u),&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},t>0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad\partial_{t}u(x,0)=u_{1}(x)&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
1\leq n\leq 3
,
p>1
,
\mu>0
,
f(\partial_{t}u)=|\partial_{t}u|^{p}
or
|\partial_{t}u|^{p-1}\partial_{t}u
, and where the initial data

u_{0},
and
u_{1}
are chosen in the appropriate energy space, namely

(u_{0},u_{1})\in H^{m+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times H^{m+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\qquad\hbox{with}\quad m=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\quad\hbox{when}\,\,n=1,\\
\\
1&\quad\hbox{when}\,\,n=2,3.\\
\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

The corresponding linear equation to (
1.1
)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Delta u+\frac{\mu}{1+t}\partial_{t}u=0,}&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ t>0,\\
(u,\partial_{t}u)(0,x)=(u_{0},u_{1})(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{cases}

(1.3)

is invariant under the following transformation:

\tilde{u}(x,t)=u(\lambda x,\lambda(1+t)-1),\ \lambda>0.

According to the classification introduced by
[
21
]
, the above scaling justifies the designation of the
scale-invariant
case for (
1.1
). Indeed, the behavior of the solutions to (
1.3
) is determined by the parameter
\mu
which provides a borderline between the
non-effective dissipation
and the
effective dissipation
. In fact, the non-effective damping means its solution somehow having the behavior of the free wave equation
\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Delta u=0
, and the effective damping stands for its solution somehow having the behavior of the corresponding parabolic equation
\frac{\mu}{1+t}\partial_{t}u-\Delta u=0
.

By ignoring the damping term

{\frac{\mu}{1+t}}\partial_{t}u
, the problem (
1.1
)
is reduced to the classical semilinear wave equation, namely

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}^{2}u-\ \Delta u=|\partial_{t}u|^{p}&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,\,t>0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad\partial_{t}u(x,0)=u_{1}(x)&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

for which we have the Glassey exponent. This case is characterized by a critical power, denoted by
p_{G}
, and given by

p_{G}=p_{G}(n):=1+\frac{2}{n-1}.

(1.5)

More precisely, if
p\leq p_{G}
then there is no global solution for (
1.4
),
for small initial data, non negative and compactly supported,
and for
p>p_{G}
a global solution exists for small initial data; see e.g.
[
6
,
7
,
11
,
15
,
17
,
19
,
22
]
.

In recent years, many papers have been devoted to the study of blow-up results and lifespan
estimates for the solution of (
1.1
).
Indeed, Lai and Takamura showed in
[
12
]
a blow up region for
p
, namely
p\in(1,p_{G}(n+2\mu)]
.
Furthermore, an important refinement was performed in

[
14
]
, where the new bound, for
\mu\in(0,2)
.
Note that the result in
[
14
]
was later improved in
[
2
]
by extending the upper bound for
p
to
p_{G}(n+\mu)
, for any
\mu>0
. In other words, the upper bound is given by:

p_{G}(n+\mu)=1+\frac{2}{n-1+\mu},\quad\quad n\geq 1.

(1.6)

It is natural to investigate the critical power
denoted here by
p_{G}(n,\mu)
, delimiting blow-up and global existence regions of small data solutions of problem (
1.1
).

To establish global existence results for the given problem, it is essential to analyze the associated linear problem and utilize decay rates in appropriate Sobolev spaces. That question was solved in
[
21
]
(see also
[
13
,
20
,
16
]
).
Indeed, by exploiting the partial Fourier transformation with respect to spatial variables and some basic properties of the explicit representations of solutions in terms of Bessel functions in
[
21
]

provide the following estimate

\|(\nabla u(t),\partial_{t}u(t))\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\min(1,\frac{\mu}{2})},

where the constant
C
depends on the initial data.

Similarly, the analogous estimate for the linear problem
with space-dependent damping,
namely

\partial_{t}^{2}u-\ \Delta u+\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}}}\partial_{t}u=0,
was done by Ikehata–
Todorova–Yordanov in
[
9
]
. Indeed, they showed that, in the case where the initial data are compactly supported, the solution
satisfies the same kind of energy estimates, namely

\|(\nabla u(t),\partial_{t}u(t))\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\gamma},

for some
\gamma=\gamma(n,\mu)>0
,
and where the constant
C
depends on the support of the initial data.

We would like to draw the attention of the reader to
the fact that a better understanding of the dependence of the constant related to the initial data is crucial for establishing a global existence results for the coresponding nonlinear problem. In this context, in recent work in
[
18
]
, the author successfully established a global existence result for the nonlinear problem with space-dependent damping in an exterior domain. This achievement was made possible by skillfully employing weighted energy estimates for the associated linear problem.

Up to our knowledge, there is no result to
the global existence of the solution of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
).
In the present paper, we
aim
to provide the small data global existence of the mild solution for the Cauchy problem (
1.1
). To do this, the first step consists of the study of the associated homogeneous problem, by the use of some energy estimates which are totally new based on some estimates invented by many researchers, e.g.
[
10
]
, and the use of their decay rates in suitable space (Sobolev space) as stated in Section
2
. Thanks to the fixed point theorem, we deal with nonlinearity.
Therefore, the main important result of this paper
is the proof of the following:

p_{G}(n,\mu)\leq 1+\frac{2}{\alpha},\quad\textrm{if}\quad 1\leq n\leq 3,

(1.7)

where

\alpha:=\min(\mu,2).

(1.8)

The above result gives the predicted result in the one-dimensional case when
\mu\leq 2
. Namely, by combining the previous blow-up results and (
1.7
), we deduce that

p_{G}(1,\mu)=1+\frac{2}{\mu}.

(1.9)

At the end of this section we prepare notation and several definitions used throughout this paper.
We denote by
C
a positive constant, which may change from line to line.

L^{p}=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

stands for the usual Lebesgue space,
and

H^{k}=H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

for
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

is the Sobolev space defined by:

\displaystyle H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\left\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n});\|f\|_{H^{k}}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\|\partial_{x}^{\ell}f\|_{L^{2}}<\infty\right\}.

The paper is organized as follows: we start in Section
2
by providing the energy estimates of mild/strong solutions of the corresponding homogeneous equation of (
1.1
). We derive the main results (Theorems
3.1
,
3.2
) in Section
3
while Section
4
is dedicated to the proof of these theorems.

Homogeneous equation

Similarly,

) by

3
Main Result

The aim of this section is to state our main result for which we will write the definition of mild solution of the system (
1.1
). Recall that
f(\partial_{t}u)=|\partial_{t}u|^{p}
or
|\partial_{t}u|^{p-1}\partial_{t}u
.

Definition 3.1
.

(Mild solution)

Let
1\leq n\leq 3
. Assume that

(u_{0},u_{1})\in H^{m+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times H^{m+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\qquad\hbox{with}\quad m=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\quad\hbox{when}\,\,n=1,\\
\\
1&\quad\hbox{when}\,\,n=2,3.\\
\end{array}\right.

We say that a function
u
is a
mild solution of (
1.1
) if

u\in\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T],H^{m+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})),

and
u
has the initial data
u(0)=u_{0}
,
u_{t}(0)=u_{1}
, and satisfies
the integral equation

u(t,x)=R(t)(u_{0},u_{1})+\int_{0}^{t}S(t,s)f(\partial_{t}u)\,ds,

(3.1)

in the sense of
H^{m+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

If
T<\infty
,
then
u
is called a local-in-time mild solution, and if
T=\infty
, then
u
is called a global-in-time mild
solution or global mild solution.

In the following, we state the main results of this paper.

Theorem 3.1
.

(\hbox{Global existence: Case of $n=1$})

Assume that
u_{0}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
u_{1}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.
If

p>1+\frac{2}{\alpha},

where
\alpha=\min(2,\mu)
, there exists a positive constant
0<\varepsilon_{0}\ll 1
, small enough, such that for any initial data satisfying

\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2}}+\|u_{1}\|_{H^{1}}\leq\varepsilon_{0},

there is a uniquely global mild solution

u\in\mathcal{C}([0,\infty),H^{2}(\mathbb{R}))\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,\infty),H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))\cap\mathcal{C}^{2}([0,\infty),L^{2}(\mathbb{R})).

Moreover, the solution satisfies the following estimates

(1+t)^{-1}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla u(t)\|_{H^{1}}+\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\,(1+t)^{-\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}},

where
\alpha_{0}
is given in (
2.2
).

Remark 3.1
.

We emphasize once again that

p_{G}(1,\mu)=1+\frac{2}{\mu}

is the predicted critical exponent in the one-dimensional case when
\mu\leq 2
. We conjecture that this observation may also hold for equations similar to (
1.1
), including those with Tricomi-type or mass terms, as explored in
[

,
5
]
, or for coupled wave systems studied in
[

4
]
, where certain blow-up results have been established.

Theorem 3.2
.

(\hbox{Global existence: Case of $n=2,3$})

Let
n=2,3
. Assume that
u_{0}\in H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
u_{1}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. If

\begin{cases}p>1+\frac{2}{\mu},&\,\,\hbox{if}\,\,\mu\leq 1,\\
\\

then there exists a positive constant
0<\varepsilon_{0}\ll 1
, small enough, such that for any initial data satisfying

Moreover, the solution satisfies the following estimates

(1+t)^{-1}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla u(t)\|_{H^{2}}+\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{H^{2}}\leq C\,(1+t)^{-\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}},

where
\alpha_{0}

).

Remark 3.2
.

Let
n=2,3
. Note that (

) implies

•

•

•

Remark 3.3
.

Let

As

M>0
such that
C\,M^{p-1}\leq 1/2
, and then
0<\varepsilon_{0}\ll 1
such that
C\,\varepsilon_{0}\leq M/2
, we arrive at

\|\Phi(u)\|_{X(T)}\leq M,

i.e.
\Phi(u)\in B_{M}(T)
.

Step 2.
We show that
\Phi
is a contraction. Let
u,v\in B_{M}(T)
.

\bullet
Estimation of
(1+t)^{-1}\|\Phi(u)(t)-\Phi(v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{t}(\Phi(u)-\Phi(v))(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla(\Phi(u)-\Phi(v))(t)\|_{L^{2}}
.

By using the estimate (
2.33
) in the case where the initial data is
(0,f(\partial_{t}u(s))-f(\partial_{t}v(s)))
, we obtain

(1+t)^{-1}\|\Phi(u)(t)-\Phi(v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{t}\Phi(u)(t)-\partial_{t}\Phi(v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\Phi(u)(t)-\nabla\Phi(v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\\
\leq C\,\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{1+s}{1+t}\right)^{\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}}\|f(\partial_{t}u(s))-f(\partial_{t}v(s))\|_{L^{2}}\,ds.

(4.52)

By the basic inequality (
4.1
)-(
4.2
), we get

\|f(\partial_{t}u(s))-f(\partial_{t}v(s))\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\partial_{t}u(s)-\partial_{t}v(s)\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|\partial_{t}u(s)\|^{p-1}_{\infty}+\|\partial_{t}v(s)\|^{p-1}_{\infty}\right).

As

\|\partial_{t}u(s)-\partial_{t}v(s)\|_{L^{2}}\leq(1+s)^{-\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}}\|u-v\|_{X(T)}.

therefore, using the Sobolev embedding
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we get

\|\partial_{t}u(s)\|_{\infty}\leq\|\partial_{t}u(s)\|_{H^{2}}\leq(1+s)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|u\|_{X(T)}\leq CM(1+s)^{-\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}},

(4.53)

and

\|\partial_{t}v(s)\|_{\infty}\leq\|\partial_{t}v(s)\|_{H^{2}}\leq(1+s)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\|v\|_{X(T)}\leq CM(1+s)^{-\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}}.

(4.54)

Therefore

\|f(\partial_{t}u(s))-f(\partial_{t}v(s))\|_{L^{2}}\leq CM^{p-1}(1+s)^{-\frac{p\alpha_{0}}{2}}\|u-v\|_{X(T)},

(4.55)

and then

(1+t)^{-1}\|\Phi(u)(t)-\Phi(v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{t}\Phi(u)(t)-\partial_{t}\Phi(v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\Phi(u)(t)-\nabla\Phi(v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\\
\leq C\,M^{p-1}\|u-v\|_{X(T)}(1+t)^{-\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)^{-\frac{(p-1)\alpha_{0}}{2}}\,ds.

(4.56)

By (
4.37
), we deduce that

(1+t)^{-1}\|\Phi(u)(t)-\Phi(v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{t}\Phi(u)(t)-\partial_{t}\Phi(v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\Phi(u)(t)-\nabla\Phi(v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\\
\leq C\,M^{p-1}1+t)^{-\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}}\|u-v\|_{X(T)}.

(4.57)

\bullet
Estimation of
\|\Delta(\Phi(u)(t)-\Phi(v)(t))\|_{L^{2}}
+
\|\nabla\partial_{t}(\Phi(u)(t)-\Phi(v)(t))\|_{L^{2}}
. Using (
2.34
), we have

\displaystyle\|\Delta\Phi(u)(t)-\Delta\Phi(v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\partial_{t}\Phi(u)(t)-\nabla\partial_{t}\Phi(v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\,C\,\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{1+s}{1+t}\right)^{\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}}\|\nabla\left(f(\partial_{t}u)-f(\partial_{t}v)\right)\|_{L^{2}}\,ds.

(4.58)

By Remark
3.2
, we have
p\geq 3>2
, which allows us to use (
4.5
)-(
4.6
). Therefore

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla\left(f(\partial_{t}u(s))-f(\partial_{t}v(s))\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\|\nabla\partial_{t}w(s)\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{t}u(s)\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}}+C\|\partial_{t}w(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\left(\|\partial_{t}u(s)\|^{p-2}_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{t}v(s)\|^{p-2}_{L^{\infty}}\right)\|\nabla\partial_{t}v(s)\|_{L^{2}},

where
w(t,x)\doteq u(t,x)-v(t,x)
. We notice from the Sobolev embedding
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}w(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\|\partial_{t}w(s)\|_{H^{2}}\leqslant C(1+s)^{-\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}}\|w\|_{X(T)}=C(1+s)^{-\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}}\|u-v\|_{X(T)},

(4.59)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\partial_{t}v(s)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant(1+s)^{-\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}}\|v\|_{X(T)}\leq CM(1+s)^{-\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}},

\displaystyle\|\nabla\partial_{t}w(s)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant(1+s)^{-\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}}\|w\|_{X(T)}=(1+s)^{-\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}}\|u-v\|_{X(T)},

therefore, using (
4.53
), (
4.54
), we conclude that

\left\|\nabla\left(f(\partial_{t}u(s))-f(\partial_{t}v(s))\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant CM^{p-1}(1+s)^{-\frac{p\alpha_{0}}{2}}\|u-v\|_{X(T)}

(4.60)

By plugging (
4.60
) into (
4.58
), and using (
4.37
), we get

\|\Delta\Phi(u)(t)-\Delta\Phi(v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\partial_{t}\Phi(u)(t)-\nabla\partial_{t}\Phi(v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq CM^{p-1}(1+t)^{-\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}}\|u-v\|_{X(T)}.

(4.61)

\bullet
Estimation of
\|\nabla\Delta\left(\Phi(u)(t)-\Phi(v)(t)\right)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Delta\partial_{t}(\Phi(u)(t)-\Phi(v)(t))\|_{L^{2}}
. Using (
2.35
), we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla\Delta\Phi(u)(t)-\nabla\Delta\Phi(v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Delta\partial_{t}\Phi(u)(t)-\Delta\partial_{t}\Phi(v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\,C\,\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{1+s}{1+t}\right)^{\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}}\|\Delta\left(f(\partial_{t}u)-f(\partial_{t}v)\right)\|_{L^{2}}\,ds.

(4.62)

Using Remark
3.2
, one can apply (
4.7
)-(
4.8
) to get

\|\Delta\big[f(\partial_{t}u(s))-f(\partial_{t}v(s))\big]\|_{L^{2}}\leq\underbrace{C\|\partial_{t}u(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-1}\|\Delta[\partial_{t}w(s)]\|_{L^{2}}}_{A_{1}(s)}\\
+\underbrace{C\|\Delta[\partial_{t}v(s)]\|_{L^{2}}\Big(\|\partial_{t}u(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-2}+\|\partial_{t}v(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-2}\Big)\|\partial_{t}w(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}}_{A_{2}(s)}\\
+\underbrace{C\|\partial_{t}u(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-2}\Big(\|\nabla\partial_{t}u(s)\|_{L^{4}}+\|\nabla\partial_{t}v(s)]\|_{L^{4}}\Big)\|\nabla\partial_{t}w(s)\|_{L^{4}}}_{A_{3}(s)}\qquad\\
+\underbrace{C\|\nabla[\partial_{t}v(s)]\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\Big(\|\partial_{t}u(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-3}+\|\partial_{t}v(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-3}\Big)\|\partial_{t}w(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}}_{A_{4}(s)},\qquad\hbox{for all}\,\,s\in[0,t].

Thanks to (
4.53
), (
4.54
), (
4.59
), the Sobolev embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and the fact that
u,v\in B_{M}(T)
, we conclude that

\|\Delta\big[f(\partial_{t}u(s))-f(\partial_{t}v(s))\big]\|_{L^{2}}\leq CM^{p-1}(1+s)^{-\frac{\alpha_{0}p}{2}}\|u-v\|_{X(T)},\qquad\hbox{for all}\,\,s\in[0,t].

(4.63)

By plugging (
4.63
) into (
4.62
), and using (
4.37
), we get

\|\Delta\Phi(u)(t)-\Delta\Phi(v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\partial_{t}\Phi(u)(t)-\nabla\partial_{t}\Phi(v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq CM^{p-1}(1+t)^{-\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}}\|u-v\|_{X(T)}.

(4.64)

Summing up the estimates (
4.57
), (
4.61
), and (
4.64
), we conclude that

\|\Phi(u)-\Phi(v)\|_{X(T)}\leq C\,M^{p-1}\|u-v\|_{X(T)}.

By choosing
M>0
such that
C\,M^{p-1}\leq 1/2
, we arrive at

\|\Phi(u)-\Phi(v)\|_{X(T)}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u-v\|_{X(T)}.

Step 3.
By the Banach fixed point theorem, there exists a unique mild solution
u\in X(T)
to problem (
1.1
). This completes the proof of Theorem
3.2
.

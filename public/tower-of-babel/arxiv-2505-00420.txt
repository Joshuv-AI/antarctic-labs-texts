Uniqueness Domains for 𝐋 ∞ {\bf L}^{\infty} Solutions of 2 × 2 2\times 2 Hyperbolic Conservation Laws
Alberto Bressan, Elio Marconi, Ganesh Vaidya

Abstract
For a genuinely nonlinear 2 × 2 2\times 2 hyperbolic system of conservation laws, assuming that the initial data have small 𝐋 ∞ {\bf L}^{\infty} norm but possibly unbounded total variation, the existence of global solutions was proved in a classical paper by Glimm and Lax (1970). In general, the total variation of these solutions decays like t − 1 t^{-1} . Motivated by the theory of fractional domains for linear analytic semigroups, we consider here solutions with faster decay rate: Tot.Var. ​ { u ⁡ ( t , ⋅ ) } ≤ C ​ t α − 1 \hbox{Tot.Var.}\bigl\{u(t,\cdot)\bigr\}\leq Ct^{\alpha-1} . For these solutions, a uniqueness theorem is proved. Indeed, as the initial data range over a domain of functions with ‖ u ¯ ‖ 𝐋 ∞ ≤ ε 1 \|\bar{u}\|_{{\bf L}^{\infty}}\leq\varepsilon_{1} small enough, solutions with fast decay yield a Hölder continuous semigroup. The Hölder exponent can be taken arbitrarily close to 1 by further shrinking the value of ε 1 > 0 \varepsilon_{1}>0 . An auxiliary result identifies a class of initial data whose solutions have rapidly decaying total variation.

Introduction

Consider a strictly hyperbolic system of conservation laws
in one space dimension

u_{t}+f(u)_{x}~=~0,

(1.1)

with initial data

u(0,x)~=~\overline{u}(x).

(1.2)

It is well known that
(
1.1
) generates a Lipschitz continuous semigroup
of Liu-admissible weak solutions
[
4
,
8
,

,

,

,

,

, on a
domain
{\cal D}\subset{\bf L}^{1}(\mathbb{R};\,\mathbb{R}^{n})
of functions with suitably small total variation.
In the papers
[
12

31
,
32
,
33
]
a semigroup was constructed on a domain of functions with large
but finite total variation. In view of the recent uniqueness theorems proved in
[

, these results show that, for a wide class of systems, the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) has a unique solution
which depends continuously on the initial data, as long as the
total variation remains bounded.

A major open problem is to understand whether the semigroup generated
by (
1.1
) can be extended to a domain of
{\bf L}^{\infty}
functions,
possibly with unbounded total variation.
At present, this is known in the
scalar case
[
20
,
28
]
, and for some special systems of Temple class
[
15
]
. In addition, unique solutions with
{\bf L}^{\infty}
initial data were constructed
in
[
17
]
as limit of vanishing viscosity approximations,
for a class of
2\times 2
systems of conservation laws in triangular form,
not necessarily strictly hyperbolic.

Even for
2\times 2
strictly hyperbolic, genuinely nonlinear systems,
where existence of
{\bf L}^{\infty}
solutions can be proved by a compensated compactness
argument
[
22
]
, it is not known whether the vanishing viscosity limit yields unique solutions.
In particular, the uniqueness of the weak solutions
constructed by Glimm and Lax in
[
24
]
has remained an elusive open problem
for over 50 years.

Aim of this paper is to provide some results in this direction. Our approach is motivated by the theory of parabolic equations and fractional domains for
analytic semigroups
[
26
,
35
]
, which we briefly review. Consider a bounded open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with smooth boundary.
Call
u(t,\cdot)=e^{t\Delta}\overline{u}
the solution to Cauchy problem for the linear heat equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}u_{t}-\Delta u&=~0&\hbox{on}~\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
u&=~0&\hbox{on}~\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.\qquad\qquad u(0,x)\,=\,\overline{u}(x).

(1.3)

For any initial data
\overline{u}\in{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega)
, it is well known that this solution
satisfies the decay property

\bigl\|\Delta u(t,\cdot)\bigr\|_{{\bf L}^{2}}~\leq~{C\over t}\,\,\|\overline{u}\|_{{\bf L}^{2}}\,.

(1.4)

Moreover, if the initial data lies in the domain of a fractional power
of the Laplace operator, say
\bar{u}\in X^{\alpha}\doteq Dom(-\Delta)^{\alpha}
for some
0<\alpha<1
,
then a faster decay rate is achieved:

\bigl\|\Delta u(t,\cdot)\bigr\|_{{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega)}~\leq~{C\over t^{1-\alpha}}\,\|\overline{u}\|_{X^{\alpha}}\,.

(1.5)

Relying on (
1.5
) one can show that
the semilinear parabolic Cauchy problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}u_{t}-\Delta u&=~F(x,u,\nabla u)&\hbox{on}~\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
u&=~0&\hbox{on}~\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.\qquad\qquad u(0,x)\,=\,\overline{u}(x),

(1.6)

with
F
Lipschitz continuous,
is well posed for initial data
\overline{u}\in X^{\alpha}
, for a suitable
0<\alpha<1
depending on the dimension
n
of the space. For all details we refer to
[
26
]
.

We shall pursue a similar approach in connection with a
2\times 2

genuinely nonlinear,
strictly hyperbolic system of conservation laws, as considered in the classical
paper
[
24

We recall that, for any bounded measurable initial data
\overline{u}:\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{2}

\|\overline{u}\|_{{\bf L}^{\infty}}~\leq~\varepsilon_{1}

1.1
)-(
1.2

\bigl\|u(t,\cdot)\bigr\|_{{\bf L}^{\infty}}~\leq~M_{0}\,\sqrt{\|\bar{u}\|_{{\bf L}^{\infty}}}\,,

(1.8)

\hbox{Tot.Var.}\bigl\{u(t,\cdot)\,;~[a,b]\bigr\}~\leq~C_{0}\cdot\left(\sqrt{\|\bar{u}\|_{{\bf L}^{\infty}}}+{b-a\over t}\right)\,,

(1.9)

for some constants
M_{0},C_{0}
, any interval
[a,b]
and every time
t\in\,]0,1]

[
5

In analogy with (
1.5

\begin{array}[]{rl}\widetilde{\cal D}_{\alpha}&\displaystyle\doteq~\Bigg\{\overline{u}\in{\bf L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\,\mathbb{R}^{2})\,;~\hbox{ the Cauchy problem (\ref{1})-(\ref{2}) has a weak solution}\\[11.38109pt]
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\displaystyle\hbox{such that}\quad\sup_{0<t<1}t^{1-\alpha}\cdot\hbox{Tot.Var.}\bigl\{u(t,\cdot)\bigr\}\,<\,+\infty\bigg\}\,.\end{array}

(1.10)

We observe that
\bar{u}\in\widetilde{\cal D}_{\alpha}
implies

\bigl\|u(\tau)-\overline{u}\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}~=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\tau^{\alpha}\qquad\qquad\tau\in[0,1].

(1.11)

Indeed, by Theorem 4.3.1 in
[
21
]
, one has

\limsup_{h\to 0+}~{\bigl\|u(t+h)-u(t)\bigr\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}\over h}~=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\hbox{Tot.Var.}\bigl\{u(t)\bigr\}\,.

(1.12)

For
\bar{u}\in\widetilde{\cal D}_{\alpha}
, the estimate (
1.11
) is an easy consequence of (
1.12
).
Here and in the sequel, the Landau notation
{\cal O}(1)
denotes a uniformly bounded quantity.

Our main result shows that, for every initial data
\overline{u}\in\widetilde{\cal D}_{\alpha}
with
\|\bar{u}\|_{{\bf L}^{\infty}}
sufficiently small,
the entropy weak solution which satisfies (
1.11
) is unique. Moreover,
such solutions form a Hölder continuous semigroup w.r.t. the
{\bf L}^{1}
distance on the initial data.
By restricting the domain of the semigroup to functions with even smaller
{\bf L}^{\infty}
norm, the Hölder exponent of
the semigroup can be rendered arbitrarily close to 1.

The main idea is illustrated in Fig.
1
.
For any
\tau>0
, consider the positively invariant domain

\begin{array}[]{l}{\cal D}^{(\tau)}~=~\Big\{u(t,\cdot)\,;~~t\geq\tau,~~u~\hbox{is an entropy weak solution of
(\ref{1})-(\ref{2}) satisfying (\ref{TVdec}),}\\[8.53581pt]
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\hbox{with}~\bar{u}\in{\bf L}^{1}(\mathbb{R};\,\mathbb{R}^{2}),~\|\bar{u}\|_{{\bf L}^{\infty}}\leq\varepsilon_{1}\Big\}.\end{array}

(1.13)

Here
\varepsilon_{1}>0

(
1.9
) on the total variation are valid. Observing that these functions have uniformly bounded variation on bounded intervals, our first goal is to show that (
1.1
) generates a Lipschitz semigroup
S
on the domain

{\cal D}^{(\tau)}
. More precisely

\bigl\|S_{t}\bar{u}-S_{t}\bar{v}\bigr\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}~\leq~L{(\tau)}\cdot\|\bar{u}-\bar{v}\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}\,,\qquad\qquad\hbox{for all}~\bar{u},\bar{v}\in{\cal D}^{(\tau)},~~t\in[0,1],

(1.14)

for some Lipschitz constant
L(\tau)

\lim_{\tau\to 0}L(\tau)=+\infty

Next, consider two solutions
u_{1},u_{2}

\begin{array}[]{l}\bigl\|u_{1}(t)-u_{2}(t)\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}~\leq~L(\tau)\cdot\bigl\|u_{1}(\tau)-u_{2}(\tau)\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}\\[5.69054pt]

.
In particular, give an asymptotic estimate on how it grows as

(2)

Identify a class of initial data

As already remarked, this will be the case if

For part
(1)
, to estimate the Lipschitz constant of the semigroup we use the technique introduced in

[
11
]
, based on the analysis of strengths and shifts of wave fronts in a front tracking approximation.
As shown in Fig.
2
, consider one particular front at time
t=\tau
, say with size
\sigma_{0}
.
If the location of this front is initially shifted at rate
\xi_{0}
, at later time
t>\tau
a number of other fronts
will be shifted as well. To fix ideas, call
\sigma_{i},\xi_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,N
the sizes and the shifts
of these various fronts.
If we can find a constant
L
, uniformly valid for all front tracking solutions, such that

\sum_{i}\bigl|\sigma_{i}\xi_{i}\bigr|~\leq~L\cdot\bigl|\sigma_{0}\xi_{0}\bigr|,

(1.17)

this will yield a Lipschitz constant for the semigroup.
We recall that the estimate (
1.17
) was first proved in
[
11
]
, within a domain
of initial data having small total variation. In the special case of
genuinely nonlinear Temple class systems
[
36
]
, the paper
[
15
]
showed that the same estimate is valid
on a much larger domain of
{\bf L}^{\infty}
initial data, which may well have unbounded variation.
A simpler proof of this result for Temple class systems will be given in Section
3
.

A key idea used in our analysis is that any
2\times 2
system can be locally approximated by a Temple class system.
For a front tracking solution whose
{\bf L}^{\infty}
norm remains suitably small, the strengths and shifts of wave fronts are thus very close to the corresponding ones computed in connection with the Temple system.
This comparison argument will provide the crucial estimate for the Lipschitz constant
L(\tau)
of our semigroup.

Concerning
(2)
we recall that,
for a scalar conservation law with convex flux, conditions that guarantee a fast decay
of the total variation have been provided
in the recent paper
[
2
]
. In Section
8
we show that a strengthened version of these
conditions imply
\overline{u}\in\widetilde{\cal D}_{\alpha}
, in the case of
2\times 2
systems.
More precisely, we consider the set of functions

\overline{u}\in{\bf L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\,\mathbb{R}^{2})
with the property:

(
\widetilde{\bf P}_{\alpha}
)

For any
\lambda\in\,]0,1]
and every bounded interval
I
, there exists an open set

V^{\lambda}\,=\,\cup_{i=1}^{N}]a_{i},b_{i}[\,
such that the following holds.

\hbox{meas}(V^{\lambda})~\leq~\widetilde{C}\,\lambda^{\alpha},

(1.18)

\hbox{Tot.Var.}\bigl\{\overline{u}\,;~I\setminus V^{\lambda}\bigr\}~\leq~\widetilde{C}\,\lambda^{\alpha-1},

(1.19)

N~\leq~\widetilde{C}\,\lambda^{\alpha-1},

(1.20)

for some constant
\widetilde{C}
independent of
\lambda
.

Roughly speaking, this means that the total variation of
\overline{u}
can be infinite,
but most of this variation is concentrated on finitely many open intervals,
with small total length.

.

(Uniqueness for a class of
{\bf L}^{\infty}
solutions with fast decay).

Let (

) be a
2\times 2
strictly hyperbolic, genuinely nonlinear system of conservation laws with flux

.
Given any constants
\alpha\in\,]0,1[

, there exists
\varepsilon_{1}>0

\bigl\|u_{1}(t,\cdot)-u_{2}(t,\cdot)\bigr\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\|\bar{u}_{1}-\bar{u}_{2}\|^{\beta}_{{\bf L}^{1}}\qquad\hbox{for all}~t\in[0,1].

(1.22)

Here the exponent

can be taken arbitrarily close to

.

(Solutions with fast decay rate).
Let (

\varepsilon_{1}>0

\hbox{Tot.Var.}\bigl\{u(t,\cdot);\,[a,b]\bigr\}~\leq~C\,t^{\alpha-1}\qquad\qquad\hbox{for all}~t\in\,]0,1].

We remark that the constants

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we review
some basic estimates
on the strengths and shifts of wave fronts across an interaction.
Section
3
briefly recalls
the construction of piecewise constant front tracking solutions introduced in
[
11
]
.
We remark that the original front tracking algorithm in
[
7
]
applied to general
n\times n

systems, but required the introduction of “nonphysical wave-fronts” to prevent the total number of fronts from blowing up in finite time. On the other hand, for
2\times 2
systems, the algorithm introduced in
[
11
]
uses a
smooth interpolation between shock and rarefaction curves and completely avoids the
introduction of non-physical fronts. This is a crucial property, because it
guarantees that front tracking approximations depend continuously on the initial data.
The
{\bf L}^{1}
distance between two front tracking solutions at time
t>0
can now be estimated by a homotopy method.
Given two piecewise constant initial data
\bar{u}^{0}
,
\bar{u}^{1}
, by shifting the positions of the initial jumps
one constructs a 1-parameter family of initial data
\bar{u}^{\theta}
,
\theta\in[0,1]
. At a later time
t
,
the distance
\bigl\|u^{0}(t,\cdot)-u^{1}(t,\cdot)\bigr\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}
is bounded by the length of the path

\theta\mapsto u^{\theta}(t,\cdot)
.
In turn (see Fig.
2
), this length can be bounded by estimating the strengths and shifts of all wave-fronts in
u^{\theta}
.

In the present paper, by covering the
t
-
x
plane with trapezoidal domains where the total
variation remains uniformly small, from the results in
[
11
]
we obtain
a preliminary estimate on the total amount of interaction and on the shifts
of all wave-fronts, in a front tracking approximation. This yields a first, rough estimate of the Lipschitz dependence on the initial data
\bar{u}\in{\cal D}^{(\tau)}
.

Section
4
is concerned with
a
2\times 2
genuinely nonlinear Temple class system, with
{\bf L}^{\infty}
initial data.
We show that the entropy admissible solutions yield a continuous semigroup, with uniformly Lipschitz dependence on the initial data, in the
{\bf L}^{1}
distance. This result was originally proved in
[
15
]
.
We provide here a more direct proof, which yields a sharper Lipschitz constant.

In Section
5
we show that any
2\times 2
genuinely nonlinear system can be locally
approximated by a Temple system. Thanks to this approximation, in Section
6

we provide a new, sharper bound on the strengths and shifts
for a front tracking solution of the original system (
1.1
).
To explain the heart of the matter we observe that, for a solution with small
{\bf L}^{\infty}
norm,
genuine nonlinearity produces a huge cancellation of positive and negative waves. This is indeed
exploited in the famous Glimm-Lax paper
[
24
]
. One expects that a similar cancellation
should occur also for the shifts in a front tracking solution.
This can be clearly seen for Temple systems, but is far from obvious in the case of more general
systems. Indeed, the bounds in
[
11
]
do not account for these cancellations.
Our strategy is to approximate an arbitrary
2\times 2
system with a Temple system, and
compare the strengths and shifts of wave fronts in the corresponding front tracking solutions.
It turns out that this difference can be rendered arbitrarily small by reducing the

{\bf L}^{\infty}
norm of the initial data.

\begin{array}[]{l}|\sigma_{1}-\sigma^{\prime}|+|\sigma_{2}-\sigma^{\prime\prime}|~=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot|w^{\sharp}-w^{*}|\\[11.38109pt]
\qquad=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\Big|\psi(\sigma^{\prime\prime},\overline{w})-\psi(\sigma^{\prime\prime},w^{\prime})\Big||\sigma^{\prime\prime}|^{3}+{\cal O}(1)\cdot\Big|\psi(\sigma^{\prime},\overline{w})-\psi(\sigma^{\prime\prime},w^{\prime\prime})\Big||\sigma^{\prime}|^{3}\\[11.38109pt]
\qquad=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot|\sigma^{\prime}|\,\bigl|\sigma^{\prime\prime}\bigr|^{3}+{\cal O}(1)\cdot|\sigma^{\prime\prime}|\,\bigl|\sigma^{\prime}\bigr|^{3}.\end{array}

(2.7)

This yields (
2.5
) in the case where both interacting fronts are shocks.

The case where one of the incoming fronts is a rarefaction is treated in a similar way.
If both incoming waves are rarefactions, we trivially have
\sigma_{1}=\sigma^{\prime}
,
\sigma_{2}=\sigma^{\prime\prime}
.

2.
Next, consider the interaction of two shocks of the same family, say of the first one.
Let
\overline{w},w^{\prime},w^{*}
be the left, middle, and right states before the interaction.
We thus have

w^{\prime}~=~S_{1}(\overline{w},\sigma^{\prime})~=~\Big(\overline{w}_{1}+\sigma^{\prime},~\overline{w}_{2}+\varphi(\overline{w},\sigma^{\prime})(\sigma^{\prime})^{3}\Big).

w^{*}~=~S_{1}(w^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime})~=~\Big(w_{1}^{\prime}+\sigma^{\prime\prime},~w^{\prime}_{2}+\varphi(w^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime})(\sigma^{\prime\prime})^{3}\Big).

Consider the point

w^{\sharp}~=~S_{1}(\overline{w},\sigma^{\prime}+\sigma^{\prime\prime})~=~\Big(\overline{w}_{1}+\sigma^{\prime}+\sigma^{\prime\prime},~\overline{w}_{2}+\varphi(\overline{w},\sigma^{\prime}+\sigma^{\prime\prime})(\sigma^{\prime}+\sigma^{\prime\prime})^{3}\Big).

By the implicit function theorem we have

\begin{array}[]{l}|\sigma_{1}-\sigma^{\prime}-\sigma^{\prime\prime}|+|\sigma_{2}|~=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot|w^{\sharp}-w^{*}|\\[8.53581pt]
\qquad=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\Big|\varphi(\overline{w},\sigma^{\prime}+\sigma^{\prime\prime})(\sigma^{\prime}+\sigma^{\prime\prime})^{3}-\varphi(\overline{w},\sigma^{\prime})(\sigma^{\prime})^{3}-\varphi(w^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime})(\sigma^{\prime\prime})^{3}\Big|\\[8.53581pt]
\qquad=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\bigl|\sigma^{\prime}\bigr|^{2}|\sigma^{\prime\prime}|+{\cal O}(1)\cdot|\sigma^{\prime}|\bigl|\sigma^{\prime\prime}\bigr|^{2}.\end{array}

(2.8)

This proves (
2.5
).

3.
In the remainder of the proof we study the shifts of the outgoing fronts.
Calling
\lambda^{\prime}<\lambda^{\prime\prime}
the speeds of the incoming fronts and
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}

the speeds of the outgoing fronts of the first and second family, the interaction time changes by

\Delta t~=~{\xi^{\prime}-\xi^{\prime\prime}\over\lambda^{\prime\prime}-\lambda^{\prime}}\,.

In turn, the shift in the outgoing front of the first family is computed by

\xi_{1}~=~\xi^{\prime}-(\lambda_{1}-\lambda^{\prime})\Delta t~=~{\xi^{\prime}(\lambda^{\prime\prime}-\lambda_{1})-\xi^{\prime\prime}(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda_{1})\over\lambda^{\prime\prime}-\lambda^{\prime}}\,.

(2.9)

Similarly, the shift in the outgoing front of the second family is computed by

\xi_{2}~=~\xi^{\prime\prime}-(\lambda_{2}-\lambda^{\prime\prime})\Delta t~=~{\xi^{\prime}(\lambda^{\prime\prime}-\lambda_{2})-\xi^{\prime\prime}(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda_{2})\over\lambda^{\prime\prime}-\lambda^{\prime}}\,.

(2.10)

4.
Consider two interacting fronts of distinct families.
In this case, the change in speeds across the interaction is estimated by

|\lambda_{1}-\lambda^{\prime}|~=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot|\sigma^{\prime\prime}|,\qquad\qquad|\lambda_{2}-\lambda^{\prime\prime}|~=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot|\sigma^{\prime}|.

(2.11)

Since the difference in speeds
\lambda^{\prime\prime}-\lambda^{\prime}
is bounded away from zero,
by (
2.9
)-(
2.10
) this yields (
2.4
).

5.
Finally, consider two interacting fronts of the same family, say, the first one.
In this case, by genuine nonlinearity, the difference in speed has the lower bound

|\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda^{\prime\prime}|~\geq~c_{0}\bigl(|\sigma^{\prime}|+|\sigma^{\prime\prime}|\bigr).

(2.12)

This bound is clear if both incoming fronts are shocks. If one is a rarefaction, we
can assume that its size is smaller that 1/2 times the size of the shock,
so that (
2.12
) still holds. By (
2.10
) this implies
the second estimate in (
2.6
).

On the other hand, the speed
\lambda_{1}
of the outgoing front of the first family
satisfies

\left|\lambda_{1}-{\sigma^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}+\sigma^{\prime\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime}\over\sigma^{\prime}+\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\right|~=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot|\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime\prime}|.

(2.13)

Inserting (
2.13
) in (
2.9
) one obtains

\begin{array}[]{rl}\xi_{1}&\displaystyle=~{1\over\lambda^{\prime\prime}-\lambda^{\prime}}\cdot\left\{\xi^{\prime}\left({(\lambda^{\prime\prime}-\lambda^{\prime})\sigma^{\prime}\over\sigma^{\prime}+\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\right)+\xi^{\prime\prime}\left({(\lambda^{\prime\prime}-\lambda^{\prime})\sigma^{\prime\prime}\over\sigma^{\prime}+\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\right)+{\cal O}(1)\cdot\bigl(|\xi^{\prime}|+|\xi^{\prime\prime}|\bigr)|\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime\prime}|\right\}\\[11.38109pt]
&\displaystyle=~{\xi^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}+\xi^{\prime\prime}\sigma^{\prime\prime}\over\sigma^{\prime}+\sigma^{\prime\prime}}+{\cal O}(1)\cdot\bigl(|\xi^{\prime}|+|\xi^{\prime\prime}|\bigr){|\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime\prime}|\over|\sigma^{\prime}|+|\sigma^{\prime\prime}|}.\end{array}

(2.14)

This completes the proof.
MM

Corollary 2.2
.

In an interaction of fronts of different families, the increase in the total amount of
wave strengths is bounded by

|\sigma_{1}|+|\sigma_{2}|-|\sigma^{\prime}|-|\sigma^{\prime\prime}|~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\,|\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime\prime}|\bigl(|\sigma^{\prime}|^{2}+|\sigma^{\prime\prime}|^{2}\bigr).

(2.15)

If the incoming fronts are of the same family, one has

|\sigma_{1}|+|\sigma_{2}|-|\sigma^{\prime}|-|\sigma^{\prime\prime}|~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\,|\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime\prime}|\bigl(|\sigma^{\prime}|+|\sigma^{\prime\prime}|\bigr).

(2.16)

In both of the above cases, the shifts satisfy

|\xi_{1}\sigma_{1}|+|\xi_{2}\sigma_{2}|-\bigl(|\xi^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}|+|\xi^{\prime\prime}\sigma^{\prime\prime}|\bigr)~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\bigl(|\xi^{\prime}|+|\xi^{\prime\prime}|\bigr)\,|\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime\prime}|.

(2.17)

Remark 2.3
.

If the shifts of the incoming fronts are
\xi^{\prime}=\xi^{\prime\prime}=1
, then one
has
\xi_{1}=\xi_{2}=1
as well. In this special case, the bounds (
2.17
) reduce to the standard
Glimm interaction estimates on the strengths of outgoing fronts
[
23
]
.

3
Front tracking approximations

We review here the construction of front tracking approximations introduced in
[
11
]
.
Working with a coordinate system of Riemann invariants, consider a point with coordinates

w=(w_{1},w_{2})
. The rarefaction curves through this point are parameterized as

s~\mapsto~R_{1}(w,s)\,=\,(w_{1}+s,w_{2}),\qquad\qquad s~\mapsto~R_{2}(w,s)\,=\,(w_{1},w_{2}+s).

(3.1)

On the other hand, for
s<0
the shock curves can be parameterized as

s~\mapsto~S_{1}(w,s)\,=\,\bigl(w_{1}+s,w_{2}+\phi_{2}(w,s)\bigr),\quad\qquad s~\mapsto~S_{2}(w,s)\,=\,\bigl(w_{1}+\phi_{1}(w,s),w_{2}+s\bigr),

(3.2)

where
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
are smooth functions with
\phi_{i}(w,s)={\cal O}(1)\cdot s^{3}
.

We now fix
\varepsilon>0
and define the interpolated shock curves
S_{1}^{\varepsilon},S_{2}^{\varepsilon}
so that
(see Fig.
5
, left)

S_{i}^{\varepsilon}(w,s)~=~\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}R_{i}(w,s)&\hbox{if}\quad-\sqrt{\varepsilon}<s<0,\\[2.84526pt]
S_{i}(w,s)&\hbox{if}\quad s<-2\sqrt{\varepsilon}.\end{array}\right.

This is achieved by constructing a nondecreasing smooth function

\varphi:\mathbb{R}\mapsto[0,1]
such that

\varphi(t)~=~\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}0&\hbox{if}\quad t<-2,\\[2.84526pt]
1&\hbox{if}\quad t\geq-1,\end{array}\right.

and defining

S^{\varepsilon}_{i}(w,s)~=~\varphi(s/\sqrt{\varepsilon})R_{i}(w,s)+\bigl(1-\varphi(s/\sqrt{\varepsilon})\bigr)S_{i}(w,s).

(3.3)

In turn, we can now define the mixed curves

\Psi^{\varepsilon}_{i}(w,s)~=~\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}R_{i}(w,s)&\hbox{if}\quad s\geq 0,\\[2.84526pt]
S_{i}^{\varepsilon}(w,s)&\hbox{if}\quad s<0.\end{array}\right.

(3.4)

Given a left and a right state
w^{-}=(w_{1}^{-},w_{2}^{-})
,
w^{+}=(w_{1}^{+},w_{2}^{+})
,
sufficiently close to each other, using the fact that shock and rarefaction curves have a second
order tangency, one proves that there exists
s_{1},s_{2}
and a unique intermediate state
w^{*}=(w_{1}^{*},w_{2}^{*})
such that

w^{*}~=~\Psi_{1}^{\varepsilon}(w^{-},s_{1}),\qquad\qquad w^{+}~=~\Psi_{2}^{\varepsilon}(w^{*},s_{2}).

A piecewise constant front tracking
solution of the Riemann problem with left and right states
w^{-},w^{+}

is then constructed by approximating every rarefaction wave with a centered rarefaction fan.
More precisely, a 1-rarefaction wave with left and right states

w^{-}=(w_{1}^{-},w_{2}^{-}),\qquad w^{*}=R_{1}(w^{-},s)=(w_{1}^{-}+s,w_{2}^{-})

is approximated
with a rarefaction fan whose intermediate states lie in the set

\Big\{(j\varepsilon,w_{2}^{-})\,;~~w_{1}^{-}\,\leq\,j\varepsilon\,\leq\,w_{1}+s,\qquad j\in{\mathbb{Z}}\Big\}.

A 2-rarefaction is approximated by a centered rarefaction fan in an entirely similar way.
For all details we refer to
[
11
]
.

Next, let
u=u(t,x)
be a front tracking solution of (

\bar{u}
satisfies the
bound (

\varepsilon_{1}>0

) of the total variation. Without loss of generality, we always assume that all wave fronts travel with speed
\bigl|\dot{x}(t)\bigr|<1

Assume that at some time
0<\tau<1
one of the fronts, with strength
|\sigma_{0}|
,
is shifted at rate
\xi_{0}
. We seek an estimate on the sum

V^{\xi}~\doteq~\sum_{\alpha}\bigl|\sigma_{\alpha}\xi_{\alpha}\bigr|

(3.5)

.

Toward this goal we recall that, the analysis in
[

and

(LP)

Assume that at time
t

contained in the open interval

\sum_{a+(t^{\prime}-t)\leq x_{\beta}\leq b-(t^{\prime}-t)}\bigl|\sigma_{\beta}(t^{\prime})\,\xi_{\beta}(t^{\prime})\bigr|~\leq~L\cdot\sum_{a<x_{\alpha}<b}\bigl|\sigma_{\alpha}(t)\,\xi_{\alpha}(t)\bigr|.

(3.6)

In other words, let the shifts
\xi_{\alpha}(t)
be assigned arbitrarily at time

, to all fronts
in the interval
]a,b[
. Then, at the later time
t^{\prime}
,
the shifts of all the fronts in the interval of dependency
\bigl[a+(t^{\prime}-t),~b-(t^{\prime}-t)\bigr]
satisfy the bound (
3.6
).

As shown in Fig.
5
, right, an estimate on the quantity
V^{\xi}

\ell_{k}~=~{\delta_{0}\over C}t_{k}\,.

Here the constant

Observing that every point in the triangle

By induction, at any time

V^{\xi}(T)~=~\sum_{|x_{\alpha}(T)-x_{0}|<T-\tau}\bigl|\sigma_{\alpha}(T)\,\xi_{\alpha}(T)\bigr|~\leq~(2L)^{N}\,|\sigma_{0}\xi_{0}|.

(3.13)

Here the integer

can be recovered from

(3.14)

Remark 3.1
.

The Lipschitz constant
(2L)^{N}
in (
3.13
)-(
3.14
) is not sharp.
Indeed, it grows very quickly as
\tau\to 0
. Using the additional information that the
{\bf L}^{\infty}
norm of the solution is small, a much better estimate will be derived. The heart of the matter is shown in Fig.
6
.
At the initial time
\tau
, the total strength of wave fronts is
{\cal O}(1)\cdot\tau^{-1}
. As a consequence,
the total amount of all interactions can be extremely large. However,
the total amount of interaction
restricted to waves of the same family
can be rendered arbitrarily small
by choosing the initial data small enough in
{\bf L}^{\infty}
.
Indeed (see Fig.
6
, left), if
\bigl\|u(t,\cdot)\bigr\|_{{\bf L}^{\infty}}
remains small,
then any two wave-fronts of the same family
will have almost the same speed. Hence they can interact only if their initial locations are very close.
This will be the content of Lemma
3.2
.

On the other hand, the amount of interactions among waves of different families can be very large.
In particular (see Fig.
6
, right), if a 1-front crosses a family of 2-fronts, its shift
\xi_{1}
keeps changing,
so that its total variation in time is bounded but possibly very large. This rough bound is captured by the estimate (
3.13
). However, since the
{\bf L}^{\infty}
norm of the solution is small,
the sign of the crossing 2-fronts (compressions or rarefactions) keeps alternating. As a result, the shift
\xi_{1}

alternatively increases or decreases, and a huge cancellation is achieved.

Keeping track of this cancellation is not an easy task. In Section
4
we refine the arguments in
[
3
,
15
]
and achieve an explicit, sharp estimate on the strengths and shifts of wave fronts in the special case of a Temple class system.
In the following Sections
5
and
6
, the strengths and shifts of wave fronts
in a solution to the original system (
1.1
) will be estimated by a comparison with a suitable Temple system, relying
on a homotopy method. This will be the content of Lemma
6.1
.

The main result of this section, stated in Lemma
3.2
,
provides a bound on the shifts produced by interactions of fronts of the same family.
Consider the total amount of
interactions
with shifts
, occurring during the time interval
I_{k}=[t_{k},t_{k+1}[
. This is defined as

\mathcal{Q}_{k}^{\xi}~\doteq~\sum_{t\in I_{k}}\bigl(|\xi_{\alpha}|+|\xi_{\beta}|\bigr)|\sigma_{\alpha}\sigma_{\beta}|,

(3.15)

where the sum ranges over all wave-front interactions occurring during the interval
I_{k}
.
Here
\sigma_{\alpha},\sigma_{\beta}
and
\xi_{\alpha},\xi_{\beta}
are the sizes and the shifts of the

Still from the analysis in
[
11

k=0,1,\ldots,N-1

\mathcal{Q}_{k}^{\xi}~\leq~\delta_{1}\,V^{\xi}(t_{k})~=~\delta_{1}\cdot\sum_{|x_{\alpha}(t_{k})-x_{0}|<t_{k}-\tau}|\sigma_{\alpha}(t_{k})\xi_{\alpha}(t_{k})\bigr|,

\delta_{0}
in
(LP)

1.1

Summing over

(3.17)

Lemma 3.2
.

Given
\tau\in\,]0,1]
, there exists
\varepsilon_{1}>0
small enough so that the following holds.
Let
u=u(t,x)
be a front tracking solution to (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), where the initial data
satisfies (
1.7
). Assume that at time
\tau
a shift
\xi_{0}
is assigned to a front of strength
\sigma_{0}

Then the total amount of interactions with shift, during the interval

satisfies the bound

{\cal Q}^{\xi}_{same}~\doteq~\sum_{t\in[\tau,1]\atop{\rm same~family}}\bigl(|\xi_{\alpha}|+|\xi_{\beta}|\bigr)|\sigma_{\alpha}\sigma_{\beta}|~\leq~C_{\tau}\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon_{1}}\,|\sigma_{0}\xi_{0}|,

(3.18)

for some constant
C_{\tau}
depending only on
\tau
and on the system (

).

Proof.

1.
Let
\Gamma_{kj}
be any one of the trapezoidal regions in (

).
Restricted to this region, consider the functional

\Upsilon_{kj}(t)~\doteq~\Big[Q_{same}^{\xi}(t)+\sqrt{\varepsilon}_{1}\bigl(V^{\xi}(t)+KQ^{\xi}(t)\bigr)\Big]e^{CQ(t)},

where

V^{\xi}(t)~=~\sum|\sigma_{\alpha}\xi_{\alpha}|,\qquad Q^{\xi}(t)\doteq\sum_{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{A}}|\sigma_{\alpha}\sigma_{\beta}|(|\xi_{\alpha}|+|\xi_{\beta}|),\qquad Q(t)\doteq\sum_{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{A}}|\sigma_{\alpha}\sigma_{\beta}|.

Here the first sum ranges over all approaching fronts inside
\Gamma_{kj}
at time
t
, while the
other two sums range over all couples of approaching fronts, located inside
\Gamma_{kj}
.
In addition,
Q_{same}^{\xi}(t)
denotes the amount of approaching waves with shifts, but restricted to
fronts of the same family:

Q^{\xi}_{same}(t)\doteq\sum_{(\alpha,\beta)\in{\cal A}_{same}(t)}|\sigma_{\alpha}\sigma_{\beta}|(|\xi_{\alpha}|+|\xi_{\beta}|).

By (

), the speeds of any two fronts of the same family, located at

\bigl|\dot{x}_{\alpha}(t)-\dot{x}_{\beta}(t)\bigr|~\leq~M_{1}\sqrt{\varepsilon_{1}}.

(3.19)

In view of (

), we thus define
{\cal A}_{same}(t)
as the set of all couples of wave fronts of the same

\Gamma_{kj}
, and such that

\bigl|x_{\alpha}(t)-x_{\beta}(t)\bigr|~\leq~M_{1}\sqrt{\varepsilon_{1}}(t_{k+1}-t).

(3.20)

Indeed, if their distance is larger than the right hand side of (
3.20
), the two fronts cannot interact during the
time interval
[t,t_{k+1}]
.

2.
We now use the interaction estimates in Lemma

, recalling that by (

) the strength

|\sigma|={\cal O}(1)\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon_{1}}
.
We claim that,
for a suitable choice of the constant
K

C

\sqrt{\varepsilon}_{1}(V^{\xi}(t)+KQ^{\xi}(t))e^{CQ(t)}

\Delta\Big[\sqrt{\varepsilon}_{1}\bigl(V^{\xi}(t)+KQ^{\xi}(t)\bigr)e^{CQ(t)}\Big]~\leq~-e^{CQ(t-)}\frac{C}{2}\sqrt{\varepsilon_{1}}|\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime\prime}|V^{\xi}(t-).

Indeed, repeating the arguments in
[
11

\begin{array}[]{rl}\Delta V^{\xi}&=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot|\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime\prime}|(|\xi^{\prime}|+|\xi^{\prime\prime}|),\\[5.69054pt]

&\qquad\quad+{\cal O}(1)\cdot|\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime\prime}|(|\sigma^{\prime}|+|\sigma^{\prime\prime}|)V^{\xi}(t-),\\[5.69054pt]
\Delta e^{CQ}&=\,-C|\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime\prime}|e^{CQ}.\end{array}

By choosing
\delta_{0}
sufficiently small, so that the total amount
V

we have

\Delta Q^{\xi}~=\,-\frac{1}{2}|\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime\prime}|(|\xi^{\prime}|+|\xi^{\prime\prime}|)+{\cal O}(1)|\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime\prime}|(|\sigma^{\prime}|+|\sigma^{\prime\prime}|)V^{\xi}(t-).

Therefore we can choose

large enough so that

\Delta(V^{\xi}+KQ^{\xi})\leq-\frac{K}{4}|\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime\prime}|(|\xi^{\prime}|+|\xi^{\prime\prime}|)+{\cal O}(1)\cdot K|\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime\prime}|(|\sigma^{\prime}|+|\sigma^{\prime\prime}|)V^{\xi}(t-).

Using the bound
|\sigma^{\prime}|+|\sigma^{\prime\prime}|={\cal O}(1)\cdot\sqrt{\varepsilon}_{1}
, choosing

sufficiently large depending on
K
and the system (

), we obtain

\Delta\big[(V^{\xi}+KQ^{\xi})e^{CQ}\big]\leq-e^{CQ}\frac{C}{2}|\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime\prime}|V^{\xi}(t).

Next, we estimate the change in
Q^{\xi}_{same}
. If two fronts of the same family interact at time

L(\tau)~\leq~(2L^{*})^{k}\qquad\qquad\hbox{for}\quad\tau\geq q^{-k}.

Since this holds with

k~\leq~1+\ln_{q}{1\over\tau}~=~1-{\ln\tau\over\ln q}\,,

we conclude

L(\tau)~\leq~\exp\left\{\left(1-{\ln\tau\over\ln q}\right)\cdot\ln(2L^{*})\right\}~\leq~2L^{*}\cdot\tau^{-\beta},\qquad\qquad\beta\doteq{\ln(2L^{*})\over\ln q}~<~\alpha\,.

(7.4)

3.
To prove the Hölder continuous dependence on initial data,
consider two initial data
\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2}
. Then for any
0<\tau<t\leq 1
, by (
1.11
) and (
7.4
)
it follows

\bigl\|u_{1}(t)-u_{2}(t)\bigr\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}~\leq~L(\tau)\cdot\bigl(\|\bar{u}_{1}-\bar{u}_{2}\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}+2C\tau^{\alpha}\bigr).

If
t\geq\|\bar{u}_{1}-\bar{u}_{2}\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}^{1/\alpha}
, choosing

\tau\doteq\|\bar{u}_{1}-\bar{u}_{2}\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}^{1/\alpha}\bigr\}
we achieve

\bigl\|u_{1}(t)-u_{2}(t)\bigr\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}~\leq~2L^{*}\|\bar{u}_{1}-\bar{u}_{2}\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}^{-\beta/\alpha}\cdot\|\bar{u}_{1}-\bar{u}_{2}\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}\cdot(1+2C).

(7.5)

This yields Hölder continuity, with exponent
\gamma=1-(\beta/\alpha)
.
On the other hand, if
t\leq\|\bar{u}_{1}-\bar{u}_{2}\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}^{1/\alpha}
, we have the Lipschitz bound

\bigl\|u_{1}(t)-u_{2}(t)\bigr\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}~\leq~\|\bar{u}_{1}-\bar{u}_{2}\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}+2Ct^{\alpha}~\leq~\|\bar{u}_{1}-\bar{u}_{2}\|_{{\bf L}^{1}}\cdot(1+2C).

(7.6)

Note that, by choosing
q
suitably large, the constant
\beta
in (
7.4
) can be rendered arbitrarily small.
In turn, the Hölder exponent
\gamma=1-(\beta/\alpha)
becomes arbitrarily close to 1.

4.
Since both characteristic fields are assumed to be genuinely nonlinear, by the recent
results in

[
14
,
16
]
the Lax condition suffices to conclude the uniqueness of solutions
that satisfy the decay estimate (
1.21
).

8
A domain of initial data with fast decay

Aim of this section is to prove that the condition
(
\widetilde{P}_{\alpha}
)

introduced in Section 1 yields fast decay of the total variation.
This provides a domain of initial data, possibly with unbounded variation,
where the hyperbolic system (
1.1
) has unique solutions. As before, w.l.o.g. we assume that all
wave speeds are contained in the interval
[-1,1]
.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
relies on the following proposition.

Proposition 8.1
.

There exist constants
C,K,\varepsilon_{1}>0
such that, if
\|\bar{u}\|_{\bf L^{\infty}}<\varepsilon_{1}
, then (
1.1
) has a solution
u
satisfying the following estimates, for every
t>0
and every
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
:

\hbox{Tot.Var.}\bigl\{u(t);[a,b]\bigr\}~\leq~C\left(1+\frac{b-a}{t}\right).

(8.1)

If moreover
\hbox{Tot.Var.}\{\bar{u};~[a-t,b+t]\}\leq K
for some
t>0
and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, then

\hbox{Tot.Var.}\bigl\{u(t);~[a,b]\bigr\}~\leq~C\,\hbox{Tot.Var.}\bigl\{\bar{u};~[a-t,b+t]\bigr\}.

(8.2)

Indeed,
The first part of the statement, and in particular (
8.1
), is contained in
[
5
]
. The estimate (
8.2
) follows from the analogous estimates for front tracking approximate solutions as in
[
11
]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

1.
Fix
t\in\,]0,1]
and, for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\tau\in[0,t]
, define the interval

I_{k}(\tau)\,=\,\bigl[kt-(t-\tau),(k+1)t+(t-\tau)\bigr].

We denote by
{\cal J}_{[a,b]}\subset\mathbb{Z}
the set of indices
k
for which
I_{k}(t)\cap[a,b]\neq\emptyset
and by
{\cal J}\subset{\cal J}_{[a,b]}
the set of indices
k
for which
\hbox{Tot.Var.}\{\bar{u};I_{k}(0)\}>K
.

By assumption, the initial data
\bar{u}
satisfies the condition (
\widetilde{\bf P}_{\alpha}
).
Setting
\lambda=t
, we thus call
V^{t}=V^{\lambda}
the set which satisfies the conditions (
1.18
)–(
1.20
),
in connection with the interval
I=[a-t,b+t]\supset\bigcup_{k\in{\cal J}_{[a,b]}}I_{k}(0)
.
Finally, we denote by
{\cal J}^{\prime}\subset{\cal J}_{[a,b]}
the set of indices
k
for which
I_{k}(0)\cap V^{\lambda}\neq 0
.
Since the family
\{I_{k}(t)\}_{k\in{\cal J}_{[a,b]}}
covers
[a,b]
, we have

\hbox{Tot.Var.}\bigl\{u(t);\,[a,b]\bigr\}\leq\sum_{k\in{\cal J}\cup{\cal J}^{\prime}}\hbox{Tot.Var.}\bigl\{u(t);\,I_{k}(t)\bigr\}+\sum_{k\in{\cal J}_{[a,b]}\setminus({\cal J}\cup{\cal J}^{\prime})}\hbox{Tot.Var.}\bigl\{u(t);\,I_{k}(t)\bigr\}.

(8.3)

The above two sums will be estimated separately.

By (
8.1
), for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
one has

\hbox{Tot.Var.}\bigl\{u(t);\,I_{k}(t)\bigr\}~\leq~2C.

(8.4)

We now estimate the cardinality of
{\cal J}\cup{\cal J}^{\prime}
. Since every
x\in\mathbb{R}
satisfies the inclusion
x\in I_{k}(0)
for at most
2+2
indices
k
and the distance between two disjoint intervals
I_{k}(0),I_{k^{\prime}}(0)
is at least
t
, each connected component
]a_{i},b_{i}[
of
V^{\lambda}
intersects at most
4(1+\frac{b_{i}-a_{i}}{t})
intervals
I_{k}(0)
. Summing over
i=1,\ldots,N
, the assumptions (
1.18
) and (
1.20
) imply that
{\cal J}^{\prime}
contains at most
8\widetilde{C}t^{\alpha-1}
indices.

Using again that every
x\in\mathbb{R}
satisfies the inclusion
x\in I_{k}(0)
for at most
2+2
indices
k
, and moreover

\hbox{Tot.Var.}\bigl\{\bar{u};~[a-t,b+t]\setminus V^{\lambda}\bigr\}~\leq~\tilde{C}t^{\alpha-1},

we conclude that

\sum_{k\in{\cal J}_{[a,b]}\setminus{\cal J}^{\prime}}\hbox{Tot.Var.}\bigl\{u(t);\,I_{k}(0)\bigr\}~\leq~\left(2+2\right)\widetilde{C}t^{\alpha-1}.

(8.5)

Therefore the cardinality of
{\cal J}\setminus{\cal J}^{\prime}
is bounded by
\frac{2+2}{K}\widetilde{C}t^{\alpha-1}
.
In particular there is
C_{K}>0
such that the cardinality of
{\cal J}
is bounded by
C_{K}\widetilde{C}t^{\alpha-1}
.
Therefore, by (
8.4
) one has

\sum_{k\in{\cal J}\cup{\cal J}^{\prime}}\hbox{Tot.Var.}\bigl\{u(t);\,I_{k}(t)\bigr\}~\leq~2C_{K}C\widetilde{C}t^{\alpha-1}.

(8.6)

Finally, by (
8.2
) and (
8.5
), we have

\sum_{k\in{\cal J}_{[a,b]}\setminus({\cal J}\cup{\cal J}^{\prime})}\hbox{Tot.Var.}\bigl\{u(t);I_{k}(t)\bigr\}~\leq~C\sum_{k\in{\cal J}_{[a,b]}\setminus({\cal J}\cup{\cal J}^{\prime})}\hbox{Tot.Var.}\bigl\{\bar{u};\,I_{k}(0)\bigr\}~\leq~\left(2+2\right)C\widetilde{C}t^{\alpha-1}.

(8.7)

Using the two bounds (
8.6
) and (
8.7
) in (
8.3
), we conclude that the total variation decays like
t^{\alpha-1}
, proving the theorem.

Dynamic analysis of GWO-Fuzzy MPPT technique applied to a SEPIC converter under partial shading conditions
Authors: S Kamalakannan, M Marimuthu, P Ram Prakash
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPartial shading conditions (PSC) greatly reduce the performance of photovoltaic (PV) systems by adding many local maxima to the PV characteristics. This makes it very hard for traditional maximum power point tracking (MPPT) methods to work. To solve this problem, this research presents a comparative analysis of five hybrid MPPT algorithms: PO-Fuzzy, PSO-PO, GWO-Fuzzy, PSO-Fuzzy, and GWO-PSO, executed with a SEPIC converter functioning at 10 kHz with a 250 W PV module. We use MATLAB/Simulink to test how well these methods work in different partial shading situations. It examines the important elements, including PV voltage, PV power, output voltage, and output power. The results indicate that whereas PSO-PO and GWO-PSO enable faster initial convergence, the GWO-Fuzzy method achieves superior tracking accuracy, reduced steady-state oscillations, and reliable voltage control. Introduction

The increasing demand for clean and renewable energy has led to the expanded implementation of photovoltaic (PV) systems in residential, commercial, and service sectors. Photovoltaic systems are widely recognized for their minimal incidental impact, adaptability, and scalability. The efficiency of the photovoltaic array is significantly affected by sun irradiation, temperature, and partial shade conditions. It leads to varied local maxima in the power-voltage (PV) characteristic curve, with impeding the process of maximum power point tracking (MPPT)1,2. Traditional maximum power point tracking (MPPT) methodologies, including Perturb and Observe (P&O) and Incremental Conductance, have been extensively used due to their simplicity. However, they frequently fail to identify the global maximum power point (GMPP) under partial shading conditions, leading to reduce the power production. This constraint leads to many studies into improved MPPT techniques capable at managing nonlinear and hybrid characteristics. To prevent these constraints, heuristic MPPT methods have emerged as a feasible choice by combining optimization techniques with intelligent control systems. For instance, the integration of machine learning with classical methods has shown improved adaptability under complex shading scenarios3. Similarly, modern hybrid controllers have demonstrated enhanced robustness and tracking accuracy compared to standalone approaches4. These developments highlight a clear shift toward intelligent, data-driven, and optimization-based MPPT frameworks.

Recent research has focused on hybrid MPPT techniques that combine multiple optimization strategies to enhance performance. Studies have shown that hybrid approaches such as PSO-Fuzzy and GWO-Fuzzy significantly improve convergence speed and tracking accuracy by integrating global optimization with intelligent decision-making. Additionally, advanced hybrid frameworks incorporating fuzzy logic with optimization algorithms have been shown to provide superior performance in highly nonlinear environments5. Recent works further emphasize the importance of combining different algorithmic strengths. For instance, hybrid optimization strategies that integrate exploration and exploitation mechanisms have demonstrated improved robustness and reduced steady state oscillations6. Similarly, comparative analyses indicate that hybrid MPPT techniques outperform standalone algorithms regarding stability and efficiency, particularly under rapidly changing irradiance conditions7. Hybrid concepts aim to overcome the drawbacks of standalone systems such as the slow reaction of fuzzy systems or the impulsive convergence of metaheuristics by enhancing their completing strength3. Moreover, the integration of machine learning techniques into MPPT has opened new research directions. Hybrid approaches combining regression models with classical algorithms have shown promising results in predicting optimal operating points and improving system adaptability. In addition, adaptive and modified PSO-based methods have been proposed to enhance tracking performance and overcome local maxima issues8. Recent comprehensive review studies further confirm that hybrid and AI based MPPT techniques represent the current scenario of modern algorithms9,10. Similarly, classification-based studies emphasize the growing dominance of hybrid and intelligent techniques over conventional methods11. In addition, advanced AI based MPPT techniques, including ANN and ANFIS, have demonstrated remarkable performance in complex environments. These approaches leverage learning capabilities to predict optimal operating points, thereby improving tracking accuracy under highly dynamic conditions12. Furthermore, recent ANN based hybrid models integrating metaheuristic optimization have shown enhanced convergence and robustness compared to traditional methods13,14.

The Single Ended Primary Inductor Converter (SEPIC) methodology has come up as a preferred power stage for PV uses on account of its ability to manage production power above or beneath the input level while upholding constant input current15,16. This converter structure is specifically appropriate for dynamic and changing PV outputs, as visualized under partial shading. Furthermore, the role of power converters, particularly the SEPIC converter, has gained increasing attention due to its ability to handle wide input voltage variations efficiently. The combination of advanced MPPT algorithms with SEPIC topology provides a flexible and reliable solution for real-world PV applications operating under dynamic environmental conditions4,17. The SEPIC topology is particularly advantageous for photovoltaic applications due to its inherent capability to provide both step-up and step-down voltage conversion while maintaining a non-inverted output. This characteristic enables effective handling of wide PV voltage variations induced by dynamic irradiance and partial shading conditions. Unlike conventional boost converters, which are limited to step-up operation and impose constraints on the PV operating voltage, the SEPIC converter offers enhanced operational flexibility and an extended feasible operating region. This makes it especially suitable for MPPT applications under non-uniform irradiance, where the PV array voltage can vary significantly.

This research work performs a comparative analysis of five hybrid MPPT strategies GWO-Fuzzy, PSO-Fuzzy, PO-Fuzzy, GWO-PSO, and PSO-PO, implemented along with an integrated SEPIC architecture under various partial shading conditions. Table 1 provides a detailed comparison about the performances of various MPPT algorithms presented in recent literature. To ensure consistency and practical relevance, the analysis is carried out using a standard 250 W PV array structure connected to a fixed 10 Ω resistive load. The performance of the hybrid MPPT algorithms is evaluated under both uniform and partial shading scenarios, considering key dynamic and steady state metrics, including tracking accuracy, convergence speed, steady-state oscillations, and output voltage regulation. The comparative analysis reveals that although certain hybrid methods demonstrate faster initial convergence, their performance is often compromised by increased oscillations or reduced stability. In contrast, the GWO-Fuzzy approach achieves a superior balance between exploration and exploitation, resulting in improved global maximum tracking accuracy and enhanced steady-state performance. Furthermore, limited experimental validation is provided to corroborate the simulation results, confirming the robustness, stability, and practical feasibility of the proposed control framework under realistic operating conditions.

Partial shading PV

Partial shading happens when a portion of the photovoltaic (PV) array experiences reduced irradiance levels, leading to non-uniform power generation among the modules. This scenario results in the development of several peaks in the power-voltage (PV) characteristics, making the tracking of the global maximum power point (GMPP) more challenging. In the MATLAB/Simulink environment, the photovoltaic system is represented using three parallel strings, each including 20 series-connected cells fitted with bypass diodes. These bypass diodes provide an alternate current path when certain cells are shaded or malfunctioning, thereby preventing excessive power loss and localized overheating. The photovoltaic model is linked to a variable DC source to assess the electrical properties and generate current-voltage (I-V) and power-voltage (P-V) curves under varying irradiation conditions. Partial shading situations are implemented by changing irradiance levels among the three strings: series 1 (cells 1–20), series 2 (cells 21–40), and series 3 (cells 41–60). Figure 4 demonstrates that the resultant P-V curve has several local maxima, each associated with distinct shading patterns. The presence of many peaks complicates the MPPT process, as traditional algorithms may settle on a local peak rather than the actual global maximum power point (GMPP). Hybrid PSO-Fuzzy MPPT method

In order to precisely track the global maximum power point (GMPP) in photovoltaic (PV) systems, particularly in partial shading conditions (PSC), the Hybrid PSO-Fuzzy Maximum Power Point Tracking (MPPT) technique combines the adaptive decision-making nature of fuzzy logic control (FLC) with the global search capability of particle swarm optimization (PSO) and it is in charge of investigating the nonlinear power-voltage (P-V) curve produced by shade and uneven irradiance on a global scale in this hybrid technique. The output power of the PV system is usually used to determine the fitness function, and each particle in PSO represents a potential duty cycle for the DC-DC converter (such as SEPIC)6. The particles steer the system in the direction of the GMPP by updating their locations and velocities according to their individual and global optimal placements. The Fuzzy Logic Controller is used for fine-tuning after PSO has located the area close to the ideal solution. The direction and magnitude of duty cycle adjustment are determined by the FLC using two inputs: the change in voltage (ΔV) and the change in power (ΔP). Hybrid PO-Fuzzy MPPT method

By combining the intelligence of fuzzy logic with the simplicity of the traditional P&O algorithm, the Hybrid Perturb and Observe PO-Fuzzy Logic Control (FLC) MPPT method offers a more reliable and effective way to track the maximum power point (MPP) in photovoltaic (PV) systems, particularly in situations where temperature and irradiance change quickly.

By varying the DC-DC converter’s duty cycle and monitoring the impact on the output power, the P&O algorithm operates. The perturbation continues in the same direction if the power increases, and in the opposite direction otherwise. Despite its ease of implementation, P&O has limitations, including steady-state oscillations and inaccurate tracking in partially shaded or rapidly changing environments15. To get beyond these restrictions, fuzzy logic control is used. Using this hybrid approach, the initial perturbation (ΔD) is calculated using the traditional P&O algorithm and the calculated values of power change (ΔP) and voltage change (ΔV) are fed into a fuzzy logic controller. Depending on the operating state, the FLC adaptively adjusts the step size and perturbation direction using a rule base. Better tracking under partial shading, less fluctuation around MPP, and quicker convergence are the outcomes of this. The controller can make fluid and context-aware modifications thanks to the fuzzy rules’ linguistic definitions (such as positive small, negative large) and processing using membership functions and an inference engine. In comparison to standalone approaches, the Hybrid PO-Fuzzy method improves dynamic performance, stability, and tracking efficiency by striking a balance between the adaptive and intelligent management of FLC and the quick response of P&O. A hybrid approach that incorporates a fuzzy logic controller (FLC) is suggested to address the drawbacks of the traditional Perturb and Observe (P&O) maximum power point tracking (MPPT) technique, including steady-state oscillations and mis-tracking in situations with rapidly changing environmental conditions and outlook of this method is described in Fig. 7. Hybrid PSO-PO MPPT method

To improve the tracking of the maximum power point (MPP) in photovoltaic (PV) systems, especially in dynamic and partially shaded environments, the Hybrid Particle Swarm Optimization-Perturb and Observe (PSO-P&O) MPPT technique combines the global optimisation capabilities of Particle Swarm Optimisation (PSO) with the ease of use and real-time responsiveness of the Perturb and Observe (P&O) algorithm14. The PSO algorithm is first used in this hybrid framework to conduct a global search over the PV system’s nonlinear power voltage (PV) characteristic. The associated power output is used to assess the fitness of each particle, which represents a possible duty cycle. Using iterative updates based on the global and personal best placements, the swarm converges towards the ideal zone. The P&O algorithm takes over to locally adjust the duty cycle by introducing minor disturbances and tracking the ensuing power fluctuations once the swarm has settled close to the global maximum power point (GMPP). Conclusion

This research provided an extensive comparative study of five hybrid MPPT approaches utilizing a SEPIC converter for solar systems functioning under both uniform and partial shading conditions. The performance of PO-Fuzzy, PSO-PO, GWO-Fuzzy, PSO-Fuzzy, and GWO-PSO methods was evaluated based on tracking accuracy, convergence speed, steady-state oscillations, and voltage regulation. The simulation findings show that PSO-PO and GWO-PSO approaches converge faster at first, but they have more oscillations and lesser stability when there is partial shade. The GWO-Fuzzy MPPT technique, on the other hand, works better overall because it combines global search with local refining. This leads to accurate GMPP tracking, less steady-state oscillations, and stable voltage output. Also, the hardware implementation proves that the proposed approach operates effectively and can be used in real-time situations. The results indicate that the GWO-Fuzzy approach performs effectively and maintains its performance capability despite variations in sunlight intensity. The GWO-Fuzzy MPPT methodology is a reliable and effective solution for PV systems functioning under partial shading conditions, demonstrating improved performance compared to previous hybrid MPPT methods.

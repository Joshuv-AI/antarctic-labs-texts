Equivariant Volumes of Symmetric Edge Polytopes
Tito Augusto Cuchilla, Joseph Hound, Cole Plepel, Andrés R. Vindas-Meléndez, Louis Ye

Abstract
. The symmetric edge polytope ( SEP \mathrm{SEP} ) of a finite simple graph G G is a centrally symmetric lattice polytope whose vertices are defined by the edges of the graph. Among the information encoded by these polytopes are the symmetries of the graph, which appear as symmetries of the polytope. We find an explicit relationship between the relative volumes of the subsets of the symmetric edge polytope SEP ⁡ ( G ) \mathrm{SEP}(G) fixed by the natural action of symmetric group elements and the symmetric edge polytopes of smaller graphs to which the subsets are linearly equivalent. We also provide a vertex description of the fixed polytopes and find a description of the symmetric edge polytopes to which they are equivalent, in terms of contractions of the graph G G induced by the cycle decompositions of the permutations under which the subsets are fixed. Specializations of our results provide equivalence and volume relationships for fixed polytopes of symmetric edge polytopes of complete graphs (equivalently, for fixed polytopes embedded in root polytopes of type A n A_{n} ), and describe the symmetry group of this family of polytopes.

The rest of the paper is as follows.
In Section
2
, we provide a brief introduction to concepts from discrete geometry, graph theory, and algebra used throughout this paper.
Then, in Section
3
, we identify linear transformations under which symmetric edge polytopes are invariant, in terms of the underlying graph.

In Section
4
, we study the subsets of symmetric edge polytopes fixed by coordinate permutations, which have natural interpretations in terms of the underlying graphs.
In particular, we show that these fixed polytopes are linearly equivalent to the symmetric edge polytopes associated with contractions of the original graph, and we find an explicit relationship between the volume of these fixed polytopes and the symmetric edge polytopes to which they are linearly equivalent.
Finally, in Section

Specifically, we identify the consequences of the equivalence between symmetric edge polytopes of complete graphs and fixed subpolytopes thereof and present an explicit formula for the volumes of these fixed polytopes.

Additionally, in our discussion of the fixed polytopes of symmetric edge polytopes, we must first give attention to the set of actions under which these polytopes are invariant.
To this end, we define the restricted automorphism group of a polytope, which can intuitively be thought of as the group of distinct linear actions under which a polytope is invariant.
In Section
3
, we investigate the restricted automorphism groups of symmetric edge polytopes, providing a partial description thereof in terms of the automorphism groups of the associated graphs, which we also define here.

Definition 2.5

(Automorphism group of a graph)
.

Let
G
be an unweighted graph with vertex set
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
and edge set
E

(v_{i},v_{j})\in E

to

.

\textrm{Aut}(G)

consisting of permutations
\sigma

(v_{i},v_{j})\in E
if and only if
(v_{\sigma(i)},v_{\sigma(j)})\in E
.

Definition 2.6

(Restricted automorphism group of a polytope)
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a polytope in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with vertices
\{\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{m}\}
.
Then, the
restricted automorphism group of
\mathcal{P}
, denoted
\textrm{Aut}(\mathcal{P})

\mathcal{P}
which are induced by the action of elements of

.

Finally, several of our results describe fixed subpolytopes in terms of contractions of the graph associated with a symmetric edge polytope, and we define this concept here.

3.
Invariant Actions on Symmetric Edge Polytopes

The majority of our paper will discuss the fixed polytopes of symmetric edge polytopes under coordinate permutations.
First, however, we identify the coordinate permutations under which symmetric edge polytopes are invariant, in terms of the associated graph, presented in Proposition
3.1
.
We also extend these results to obtain a full description of the restricted automorphism group of the symmetric edge polytope of the complete graph on
n
vertices, presented in Proposition
3.6
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
G=([n],E)
be a simple graph on
n
vertices, and let
\sigma\in S_{n}
act on
G
by vertex permutation and on
\mathrm{SEP}(G)
by coordinate permutation.
Then,
\mathrm{SEP}(G)
is invariant under
\sigma
if and only if
G
is invariant under
\sigma
.

Proof.

First, we show that
\mathrm{SEP}(G)
is invariant under
\sigma
if
G
is invariant under
\sigma
.
Assume
G
is invariant under the vertex permutation action of
\sigma
.
Then, for any edge
(i,j)\in E
,
(\sigma(i),\sigma(j))\in E
as well.
Thus, for every vertex
\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j}
of
\mathrm{SEP}(G)
, its image,
\mathbf{e}_{\sigma(i)}-\mathbf{e}_{\sigma(j)}
, is also a vertex of
\mathrm{SEP}(G)
.

Similarly, for every vertex
\mathbf{e}_{\sigma(i)}-\mathbf{e}_{\sigma(j)}
of
\sigma\cdot\mathrm{SEP}(G)
, its preimage,
\mathbf{e}_{\sigma^{-1}(\sigma(i))}-\mathbf{e}_{\sigma^{-1}(\sigma(j))}=\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j}
, is a vertex of
\mathrm{SEP}(G)
.
Thus, the action of
\sigma
is a bijection on the vertices of
\mathrm{SEP}(G)
, so
\mathrm{SEP}(G)
is invariant under the action of
\sigma
.

Now, we show conversely that
G
is invariant under
\sigma
if
\mathrm{SEP}(G)
is invariant under
\sigma
.
Assume
\mathrm{SEP}(G)
is invariant under the action of
\sigma
.
Then, for any vertex
\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j}
of
\mathrm{SEP}(G)
, its image under
\sigma
,
\mathbf{e}_{\sigma(i)}-\mathbf{e}_{\sigma(j)}
, is also a vertex of
\mathrm{SEP}(G)
, and vice versa.
Thus,
(\sigma(i),\sigma(j))
is an edge in
G
whenever
(i,j)
is an edge in
G
, and vice versa.
This implies that
G
is invariant under
\sigma
if
\mathrm{SEP}(G)
is invariant under
\sigma
.
∎

We can further describe exactly which facets of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
are adjacent to each other.

Lemma 3.4
.

Two facets of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
are a common neighbor if and only if their labels, as described in Proposition
3.2
, differ in exactly one element.

Proof.

We say two facets of a
d
-dimensional polytope are adjacent if their intersection is a
(d-2)
-dimensional face.
Consider two facets of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
with labels
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
.
Note that the facet with label
S_{1}
is the convex hull of the vertices
\left\{\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j}\vphantom{i\in S_{1},j\in S_{1}^{C}}\;\right\rvert\;\left.i\in S_{1},j\in S_{1}^{C}\vphantom{\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j}}\right\}
and
S_{2}
is the convex hull of the vertices
\left\{\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j}\vphantom{i\in S_{2},j\in S_{2}^{C}}\;\right\rvert\;\left.i\in S_{2},j\in S_{2}^{C}\vphantom{\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j}}\right\}
.
Thus, their intersection is the convex hull of the vertices
\left\{\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j}\vphantom{i\in S_{1}\cap S_{2},j\in S_{1}^{C}\cap S_{2}^{C}}\;\right\rvert\;\left.i\in S_{1}\cap S_{2},j\in S_{1}^{C}\cap S_{2}^{C}\vphantom{\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j}}\right\}
.
Applying Theorem 1 of
[
7
]
, we can see that this intersection has dimension
|S_{1}\cap S_{2}|+|S_{1}^{C}\cap S_{2}^{C}|-2
, so the intersection of the facets of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
with labels
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
has dimension
d-2=n-3
if and only if
|S_{1}\cap S_{2}|+|S_{1}^{C}\cap S_{2}^{C}|=d=n-1
.

Note that
|S_{1}\cap S_{2}^{C}|+|S_{1}^{C}\cap S_{2}|
is the size of the differences between
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
, and that

|S_{1}\cap S_{2}|+|S_{1}^{C}\cap S_{2}^{C}|+|S_{1}\cap S_{2}^{C}|+|S_{1}^{C}\cap S_{2}|=n,

so

|S_{1}\cap S_{2}|+|S_{1}^{C}\cap S_{2}^{C}|=n-(|S_{1}\cap S_{2}^{C}|+|S_{1}^{C}\cap S_{2}|).

Hence,
|S_{1}\cap S_{2}|+|S_{1}^{C}\cap S_{2}^{C}|=n-1
if and only if the difference between
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
has size
1
, and so the facets of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
with labels
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are adjacent if and only if
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
differ in exactly one element.
∎

These facts allows us to prove a lemma restricting the possible symmetries of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Consider the polytope
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
for
n>2
.
Label the facets of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
as described in Proposition
3.2
, and let
A\in GL(\mathbb{R}^{n})
act linearly on
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
.
The action of
A
on
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
must either map all facets with labels of size 1 to facets with labels of size 1, or it must map all facets with labels of size 1 to facets with labels of size
n-1
.
Further, the action of
A
on
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
is uniquely determined by its action on the facets of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
with labels of size 1.

Proof.

Note that a facet with label
S
has exactly
|S||S^{C}|
vertices, namely
\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j}
for
i\in S
and
j\in S^{C}
.

In particular, there are
2n
facets of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
with exactly
n-1
vertices when
n>2
, corresponding to the labels of size
1
and
n-1
, and any linear action
A
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
under which
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
is invariant must induce a bijection on the set of these
2n
facets.

Further, from Lemma
3.4
, facets of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
are adjacent if and only if their labels differ in exactly one element.

Consequently the only common neighbor of the facets with labels
S
and
S^{\prime}
of size
1
is the facet with label
S\cup S^{\prime}
, and the only common neighbor of the facets with labels
S
and
S^{\prime}
of size
n-1
is the facet with label
S\cap S^{\prime}
.
Meanwhile, no pair of facets with labels of sizes
1
and
n-1
share exactly one common neighbor; if
n\neq 4
, such facets share no common neighbors, while for
n=4
, they share two common neighbors.

Because linear transformation preserves the adjacency between facets, thus
A
must map all of the facets with labels of size 1 to facets with labels of size 1, or
A
must map all of the facets with labels of size 1 to facets with labels of size
n-1
.

Note that the average of the vertices of a facet with label
S=\{i\}
is

\mathbf{e}_{S}-\frac{1}{n-1}\mathbf{e}_{S^{C}}=\frac{n}{n-1}\mathbf{e}_{i}-\frac{1}{n-1}\mathbf{1}.

Further, any vertex
\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j}
of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
can be written as a linear combination of these vectors since

\frac{n-1}{n}\left(\frac{n}{n-1}\mathbf{e}_{i}-\frac{1}{n-1}\mathbf{1}\right)-\frac{n-1}{n}\left(\frac{n}{n-1}\mathbf{e}_{j}-\frac{1}{n-1}\mathbf{1}\right)=\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j}.

Thus, the action of
A
on the span of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
is determined by its action on these facets.
∎

We are now ready to fully describe the structure of the restricted automorphism group of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
.
We do so in Proposition
3.6
, by finding a set of distinct symmetries of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
which exhausts the possibilities provided by Lemma
3.5
.

Proposition 3.6
.

The restricted automorphism group of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
is isomorphic to
S_{n}\times S_{2}
for
n>2
, and is isomorphic to
S_{2}
for
n=2
.

Proof.

When
n=2
,
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{2})
is the line segment from
(1,-1)
to
(-1,1)
, which is readily seen to have a restricted automorphism group isomorphic to
S_{2}
.

From Lemma
3.5
, we know each linear automorphism on
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
can be described either by a bijection from the set of facets with labels of size 1 to the set of facets with labels of size 1, or a bijection from the set of facets with labels of size 1 to the set of facets with labels of size
(n-1)
.
There are
n!
bijections from the set of facets with labels of size
1
to itself, and another
n!
bijections from the set of facets with labels of size
1
to facets with labels of size
n-1
.
Thus, there are at most
2(n!)
possible distinct linear actions on
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
.

From Proposition
3.1
, we know that the restricted automorphism group of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
has a subgroup of order
n!
corresponding to the set of all coordinate permutations since
K_{n}
is invariant under vertex permutation.
We can also see that
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
is invariant under the action of
-I
, the negative identity transformation on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which maps each vertex
\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j}
of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
to the (distinct) vertex
\mathbf{e}_{j}-\mathbf{e}_{i}
.
Further, we can show that the automorphism on
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
induced by
-I
is distinct from the automorphism induced by any coordinate permutation.

Assume to the contrary that
-I
acts on
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
identically to the permutation matrix associated to some permutation
\sigma\in S_{n}
.
Observe that
-I
maps
\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j}
to
\mathbf{e}_{j}-\mathbf{e}_{i}
for each
i\neq j
, so the cycle decomposition of
\sigma
contains a 2-cycle
(i\ j)
for each
i\neq j
.
In particular, for
n>2
, the cycle decomposition contains the
2
-cycles
(12)
and
(23)
, which is impossible, as these cycles are not disjoint.
Thus, there can be no such
\sigma\in S_{n}
, and so
-I
induces a distinct automorphism on
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
.

Likewise, the negation of each coordinate permutation
P
, given by
(-I)P=P(-I)=-P
, also induces a distinct automorphism on
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
, providing
n!
negated permutation automorphisms in addition to the
n!
coordinate permutation automorphisms.
This must be the complete restricted automorphism group of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
, since it includes
2(n!)
distinct automorphisms, which is the maximum number possible size of this group.
Finally, since
\{I,-I\}
and
S_{n}
are both normal in the restricted automorphism group of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
, and their free product is exactly the restricted automorphism group of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
, and their intersection is solely the identity automorphism, we have that the restricted automorphism group of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
is isomorphic to
S_{n}\times S_{2}
.
∎

4.
Fixed Polytopes of Symmetric Edge Polytopes

In this section, we provide vertex descriptions of the fixed polytopes of symmetric edge polytopes fixed under coordinate permutation.
We further show that these fixed polytopes are combinatorially and, in fact, linearly equivalent to symmetric edge polytopes corresponding to contractions of the original graph.

First, we prove a pair of lemmas used in our subsequent results.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a polytope whose span is contained in the domain of a linear mapping
\phi
.
Then,
\phi(\mathcal{P})
is the convex hull of the images of the vertices of
\mathcal{P}
.
This further implies that the vertices of
\phi(\mathcal{P})
are a subset of the images of the vertices of
\mathcal{P}
under
\phi
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{n}
be the vertices of
\mathcal{P}
.
If
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{P}
, then
\mathbf{x}
can be written as a convex combination of the vertices of
\mathcal{P}
as
\mathbf{x}=\lambda_{1}\mathbf{v}_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n}\mathbf{v}_{n}
, where
0\leq\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}\leq 1
and
\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n}=1
.
Observe that

\phi(\mathbf{x})=\phi(\lambda_{1}\mathbf{v}_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n}\mathbf{v}_{n})=\lambda_{1}\phi(\mathbf{v}_{1})+\dots+\lambda_{n}\phi(\mathbf{v}_{n}),

so
\phi(\mathbf{x})
is a convex combination of the images of the vertices of
\mathcal{P}
under
\phi
.
Conversely, if
\mathbf{x}
is a convex combination of
\phi(\mathbf{v}_{1}),\dots,\phi(\mathbf{v}_{n})
, then
\mathbf{x}
is the image of some convex combination of
\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{n}
,

\mathbf{x}=\lambda_{1}\phi(\mathbf{v}_{1})+\dots+\lambda_{n}\phi(\mathbf{v}_{n})=\phi(\lambda_{1}\mathbf{v}_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n}\mathbf{v}_{n}).

Thus,
\phi(\mathcal{P})=\conv\{\phi(\mathbf{v}_{1}),\dots,\phi(\mathbf{v}_{n})\}
, as desired.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\sigma\in S_{n}
have cycle decomposition
\sigma=\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{m}
and let
\mathcal{P}
be an
S_{n}
-invariant polytope.
Additionally, define

\psi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{|\sigma|}\sum_{i=1}^{|\sigma|}\sigma^{i}\cdot\mathbf{x}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{\sum_{j\in\sigma_{k}}x_{j}}{|\sigma_{k}|}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{k}}.

Then,
\mathcal{P}^{\sigma}=\psi_{\sigma}(\mathcal{P})
.

Proof.

First, observe that
\sigma\cdot\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{i}}=\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{i}}
for each
\sigma_{i}
in the cycle decomposition of
\sigma
.
Since
\psi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{x})
can be expressed as a linear combination of
\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{1}},\dots,\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{m}}
, this implies
\sigma\cdot\psi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{x})=\psi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{x})
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Since
\mathcal{P}
is
S_{n}
-invariant, we also know that
\sigma\cdot\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{P}
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{P}
, and thus
\psi_{\sigma}(\mathcal{P})\subseteq\mathcal{P}
.
Therefore,
\psi_{\sigma}(\mathcal{P})\subseteq\mathcal{P}^{\sigma}
, since
\phi_{\sigma}(\mathcal{P})
is a subset of
\mathcal{P}
that is fixed under the action of
\sigma
.
Conversely, for any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{P}^{\sigma}
, note that
\sigma\cdot\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}
, and thus
\sigma^{i}\cdot\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}
for all integer powers
i
.
This implies

\psi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{|\sigma|}\sum_{i=1}^{|\sigma|}\sigma^{i}\cdot\mathbf{x}=\frac{1}{|\sigma|}\sum_{i=1}^{|\sigma|}\mathbf{x}=\frac{|\sigma|}{|\sigma|}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x},

so
\mathcal{P}^{\sigma}\subseteq\psi_{\sigma}(\mathcal{P})
.
Thus, we have
\mathcal{P}^{\sigma}=\psi_{\sigma}(\mathcal{P})
.
∎

Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
together allow us to obtain a generating set for the convex hull of polytopes fixed by coordinate permutations.
We can then identify the vertices of each fixed polytope from this set, providing an explicit vertex description, which we record in Theorem
4.3
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
G=([n],E)
be a graph on
n
vertices and
\sigma\in S_{n}
have cycle decomposition
\sigma=\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{m}
.
Additionally, viewing
\sigma
as a partition of
[n]
, let
G^{\prime}=(\sigma,E^{\prime})
be the contraction of
G
induced by
\sigma
.
Then,
\mathrm{SEP}(G)^{\sigma}
has vertex set

\left\{\frac{1}{|\sigma_{i}|}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{i}}-\frac{1}{|\sigma_{j}|}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{j}}\vphantom{(a,b)\in E\text{ for some }a\in\sigma_{i}\text{ and }b\in\sigma_{j}}\;\right\rvert\;\left.(a,b)\in E\text{ for some }a\in\sigma_{i}\text{ and }b\in\sigma_{j}\vphantom{\frac{1}{|\sigma_{i}|}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{i}}-\frac{1}{|\sigma_{j}|}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{j}}}\right\}.

Proof.

Define
\psi_{\sigma}
as in Lemma
4.2
.
For each vertex
\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j}
of
\mathrm{SEP}(G)
, note that
\psi_{\sigma}(\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j})=\frac{1}{|\sigma_{I}|}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{I}}-\frac{1}{|\sigma_{J}|}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{J}}
, where
i\in\sigma_{I}
and
j\in\sigma_{J}
, which is nonzero if and only if
I\neq J
.
We show that the nonzero points of this form are exactly the vertices of
\mathrm{SEP}(G)^{\sigma}
.

First, combining Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
ensures

\mathcal{P}^{\sigma}=\psi_{\sigma}(\mathcal{P})=\conv\left\{\phi(\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j})\vphantom{(i,j)\in E}\;\right\rvert\;\left.(i,j)\in E\vphantom{\phi(\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j})}\right\}.

Using the form of
\phi(\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j})
noted above, and omitting the redundant case when
\phi(\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j})=\mathbf{0}
, we can see that

\mathcal{P}^{\sigma}=\conv\left\{\frac{1}{|\sigma_{i}|}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{i}}-\frac{1}{|\sigma_{j}|}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{j}}\vphantom{(\sigma_{i},\sigma_{j})\in E^{\prime}}\;\right\rvert\;\left.(\sigma_{i},\sigma_{j})\in E^{\prime}\vphantom{\frac{1}{|\sigma_{i}|}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{i}}-\frac{1}{|\sigma_{j}|}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{j}}}\right\}.

It remains only to show that each
\frac{1}{|\sigma_{i}|}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{i}}-\frac{1}{|\sigma_{j}|}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{j}}
is a vertex of their convex hull.
Note that each element
\frac{1}{|\sigma_{i}|}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{i}}-\frac{1}{|\sigma_{j}|}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{j}}
is the unique maximizer among these generators of the linear functional
\mathbf{x}\mapsto\left\langle\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{i}}-\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{j}},\mathbf{x}\right\rangle
, and is thus a vertex of the convex hull.
Hence, the vertices of
\mathrm{SEP}(G)^{\sigma}=\psi_{\sigma}(\mathrm{SEP}(G))
are exactly
\left\{\frac{1}{|\sigma_{i}|}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{i}}-\frac{1}{|\sigma_{j}|}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{j}}\vphantom{(\sigma_{i},\sigma_{j})\in E^{\prime}}\;\right\rvert\;\left.(\sigma_{i},\sigma_{j})\in E^{\prime}\vphantom{\frac{1}{|\sigma_{i}|}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{i}}-\frac{1}{|\sigma_{j}|}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{j}}}\right\}
.
∎

From this vertex description, we obtain the equivalence of the fixed polytope to the symmetric edge polytope of a smaller graph.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
G=([n],E)
be a graph on
n
vertices and
\sigma\in S_{n}
have cycle decomposition
\sigma=\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{m}
.
Additionally, viewing
\sigma
as a partition of
[n]
, let
G^{\prime}=(\sigma,E^{\prime})
be the contraction of
G
induced by
\sigma
.
Then,
\mathrm{SEP}(G)^{\sigma}
is linearly and combinatorially equivalent to
\mathrm{SEP}(G^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Note that
\{\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{i}}\}_{i\in[m]}
is an orthogonal basis of
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{\sigma}
.
Then, define the linear transformation
\phi:(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{\sigma}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that
\phi(\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{i}})=|\sigma_{i}|\mathbf{e}_{i}
.
Observe that
\phi
is bijective since each orthogonal basis vector
\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{i}}
of
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{\sigma}
is mapped to a nonzero scale of a distinct orthogonal basis vector
\mathbf{e}_{i}
of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Additionally,
\phi
maps each vertex
\frac{1}{|\sigma_{i}|}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{i}}-\frac{1}{|\sigma_{j}|}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{j}}
of
\mathrm{SEP}(G)^{\sigma}
to a distinct vertex
\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j}
of
\mathrm{SEP}(G^{\prime})
.
Thus,
\mathrm{SEP}(G)^{\sigma}
is linearly equivalent to
\mathrm{SEP}(G^{\prime})
, and this linear equivalence implies combinatorial equivalence as well.
∎

We can further refine the equivalence between the fixed polytope of the symmetric edge polytope of a graph fixed by a permutation and the symmetric edge polytope of the contraction of that graph induced by the same permutation by demonstrating a relationship between their relative volumes.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
G=([n],E)
be a graph on
n
vertices, let
\sigma\in S_{n}
have cycle decomposition
\sigma=\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{m}
, and let
G^{\prime}=(\sigma,E^{\prime})
be the contraction of
G
induced by
\sigma
as a partition of
[n]
.
Then,

\rvol(\mathrm{SEP}(G)^{\sigma})=\frac{\gcd(|\sigma_{1}|,\dots,|\sigma_{m}|)}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}|\sigma_{i}|}\rvol(\mathrm{SEP}(G^{\prime})).

Proof.

Observe that
\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{1}},\dots,\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{m}}
form an orthogonal basis of
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{\sigma}
and let
\phi:(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{\sigma}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
be the bijective linear map defined by
\phi(\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{i}})=|\sigma_{i}|\mathbf{e}_{i}
.
Recall that
\phi
maps
\mathrm{SEP}(G)^{\sigma}
to
\mathrm{SEP}(G^{\prime})
as demonstrated in Corollary
4.4
.
Consider the half-open integral parallelepiped,
\mathcal{P}
, formed by the vectors

|\sigma_{i}||\sigma_{m}|\left(\frac{1}{|\sigma_{i}|}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{i}}-\frac{1}{|\sigma_{m}|}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{m}}\right)=|\sigma_{m}|\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{i}}-|\sigma_{i}|\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{m}},

for
i\in[m-1]
.
That is,
\mathcal{P}=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}(|\sigma_{m}|\mathbf{e}_{i}-|\sigma_{i}|\mathbf{e}_{m})\vphantom{0\leq\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m-1}<1,\ \sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}=1}\;\right\rvert\;\left.0\leq\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m-1}<1,\ \sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}=1\vphantom{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}(|\sigma_{m}|\mathbf{e}_{i}-|\sigma_{i}|\mathbf{e}_{m})}\right\}
.
Note that
\phi
maps
\mathcal{P}
to the integral parallelepiped
\phi(\mathcal{P})
formed by the vectors
|\sigma_{i}||\sigma_{m}|(\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{m})
for
i\in[m-1]
.
We claim that

\frac{\rvol(\mathcal{P})}{\rvol(\phi(\mathcal{P}))}=\frac{\gcd(|\sigma_{1}|,\dots,|\sigma_{m}|)}{\prod_{i\in[m]}|\sigma_{i}|},

and thus, since
\Span\mathcal{P}=\Span\mathrm{SEP}(G)^{\sigma}
by construction,

\frac{\rvol(\mathrm{SEP}(G)^{\sigma})}{\rvol(\mathrm{SEP}(G^{\prime}))}=\frac{\rvol(\mathrm{SEP}(G)^{\sigma})}{\rvol(\phi(\mathrm{SEP}(G)^{\sigma}))}=\frac{\rvol(\mathcal{P})}{\rvol(\phi(\mathcal{P}))}=\frac{\gcd(|\sigma_{1}|,\dots,|\sigma_{m}|)}{\prod_{i\in[m]}|\sigma_{i}|}.

Since
\phi(\mathcal{P})
is a half-open integral parallelepiped, its relative volume is given by the number of lattice points it contains.
We can write
\phi(\mathcal{P})
more explicitly in terms of the orthogonal basis vectors
\mathbf{e}_{i}
as

\phi(\mathcal{P})=\left\{\sum_{i\in[m-1]}|\sigma_{i}||\sigma_{m}|\lambda_{i}\mathbf{e}_{i}-\left(\sum_{i\in[m-1]}|\sigma_{i}||\sigma_{m}|\lambda_{i}\right)\mathbf{e}_{m}\vphantom{0\leq\lambda_{i}<1}\;\right\rvert\;\left.0\leq\lambda_{i}<1\vphantom{\sum_{i\in[m-1]}|\sigma_{i}||\sigma_{m}|\lambda_{i}\mathbf{e}_{i}-\left(\sum_{i\in[m-1]}|\sigma_{i}||\sigma_{m}|\lambda_{i}\right)\mathbf{e}_{m}}\right\}.

Restricting each coordinate to be integral, we see the number of lattice points contained in
\phi(\mathcal{P})
is the number of vectors
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m-1})\in[0,1)^{m-1}
satisfying
|\sigma_{i}||\sigma_{m}|\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\sum_{i\in[m-1]}|\sigma_{i}||\sigma_{m}|\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Substituting
n_{i}=|\sigma_{i}||\sigma_{m}|\lambda_{i}
, this is equivalent to the number of integral vectors
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m-1}
satisfying
0\leq n_{i}<|\sigma_{i}||\sigma_{m}|
and
\sum_{i\in[m-1]}n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
That
\sum_{i\in[m-1]}n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
follows immediately from the condition that
n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
for each
i\in[m-1]
, so we need only count the number of integer vectors
\mathbf{n}
for which
0\leq n_{i}<|\sigma_{i}||\sigma_{m}|
, of which there are

\prod_{i\in[m-1]}|\sigma_{i}||\sigma_{m}|=|\sigma_{m}|^{m-2}\prod_{i\in[m]}|\sigma_{i}|.

Thus,
\rvol(\phi(\mathcal{P}))=|\sigma_{m}|^{m-2}\prod_{i\in[m]}|\sigma_{i}|
.

We can find the relative volume of
\mathcal{P}
similarly, by counting the number of lattice points it contains, since
\mathcal{P}
is also an integral parallelepiped.
Writing
\mathcal{P}
in terms of the orthogonal basis vectors
\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{i}}
, we see

\mathcal{P}=\left\{\sum_{i\in[m-1]}|\sigma_{m}|\lambda_{i}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{i}}-\left(\sum_{i\in[m-1]}|\sigma_{i}|\lambda_{i}\right)\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{m}}\vphantom{0\leq\lambda_{i}<1}\;\right\rvert\;\left.0\leq\lambda_{i}<1\vphantom{\sum_{i\in[m-1]}|\sigma_{m}|\lambda_{i}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{i}}-\left(\sum_{i\in[m-1]}|\sigma_{i}|\lambda_{i}\right)\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{m}}}\right\}.

Restricting each coordinate to be integral, we see the number of lattice points contained in
\mathcal{P}
is the number of vectors
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m-1})\in[0,1)^{m-1}
satisfying
|\sigma_{m}|\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\sum_{i\in[m-1]}|\sigma_{i}|\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Substituting
n_{i}=|\sigma_{m}|\lambda_{i}
, this is equivalent to the number of integral vectors
\mathbf{n}\in[|\sigma_{m}|]^{m-1}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{m-1}
for which
\sum_{i\in[m-1]}|\sigma_{i}|n_{i}\in|\sigma_{m}|\mathbb{Z}
.

Let
g=\gcd(|\sigma_{1}|,\dots,|\sigma_{m}|)
. Then,
\sum_{i\in[m-1]}|\sigma_{i}|n_{i}\equiv 0\pmod{g}
for all
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m-1}
and there exists some
\mathbf{c}\in[|\sigma_{m}|]^{m-1}
for which
\sum_{i\in[m-1]}|\sigma_{i}|c_{i}\equiv g\pmod{|\sigma_{m}|}
.
Now, for any
k\in\left[\frac{|\sigma_{m}|}{g}\right]
, define

\psi_{k}:[|\sigma_{m}|]^{m-1}\to[|\sigma_{m}|]^{m-1}\;\text{ as }\;\psi_{k}(\mathbf{n})\equiv\mathbf{n}-k\mathbf{c}\pmod{|\sigma_{m}|},

where the reduction mod
|\sigma_{m}|
is performed element-wise.
We claim
\psi_{k}
provides a bijection from vectors
\mathbf{n}\in[|\sigma_{m}|]^{m-1}
for which
\sum_{i\in[m-1]}|\sigma_{i}|n_{i}\equiv kg\pmod{|\sigma_{m}|}
to vectors
\mathbf{n}^{\prime}\in[|\sigma_{m}|]^{m-1}
for which
\sum_{i\in[m-1]}|\sigma_{i}|n_{i}^{\prime}\equiv 0\pmod{|\sigma_{m}|}
.
If
\sum_{i\in[m-1]}|\sigma_{i}|n_{i}=kg\pmod{|\sigma_{m}|}
, then

\sum_{i\in[m-1]}|\sigma_{i}|\psi_{k}(\mathbf{n})_{i}\equiv\sum_{i\in[m-1]}|\sigma_{i}|(n_{i}+kc_{i})=\sum_{i\in[m-1]}|\sigma_{i}|n_{i}+k\sum_{i\in[m-1]}|\sigma_{i}|c_{i}\equiv kg-kg\equiv 0\pmod{|\sigma_{m}|},

as desired.
Since such a bijection can be found for each possible sum
0g,1g,\dots,\left(\frac{|\sigma_{m}|}{g}-1\right)g
, there must be the same number of vectors
\mathbf{n}\in[|\sigma_{m}|]^{m-1}
producing sums congruent mod
|\sigma_{m}|
to each of these
g
distinct values.
Thus, there are exactly
\frac{|\sigma_{m}|^{m-1}}{\frac{|\sigma_{m}|}{g}}=|\sigma_{m}|^{m-2}g
vectors
\mathbf{n}\in[|\sigma_{m}|]^{m-1}
for which
\sum_{i\in[m-1]}|\sigma_{i}|n_{i}\equiv 0\pmod{|\sigma_{m}|}
, so
\rvol(\mathcal{P})=|\sigma_{m}|^{m-2}\gcd(|\sigma_{1}|,\dots,|\sigma_{m}|)
.

Together with the value of
\rvol(\phi(\mathcal{P}))
obtained above, we have

\frac{\rvol(\mathcal{P})}{\rvol(\phi(\mathcal{P}))}=\frac{|\sigma_{m}|^{m-2}\gcd(|\sigma_{1}|,\dots,|\sigma_{m}|)}{|\sigma_{m}|^{m-2}\prod_{i\in[m]}|\sigma_{i}|}=\frac{\gcd(|\sigma_{1}|,\dots,|\sigma_{m}|)}{\prod_{i\in[m]}|\sigma_{i}|},

as desired.
This implies

\frac{\rvol(\mathrm{SEP}(G)^{\sigma})}{\rvol(\mathrm{SEP}(G^{\prime}))}=\frac{\rvol(\mathrm{SEP}(G)^{\sigma})}{\rvol(\phi(\mathrm{SEP}(G)^{\sigma}))}=\frac{\rvol(\mathcal{P})}{\rvol(\phi(\mathcal{P}))}=\frac{\gcd(|\sigma_{1}|,\dots,|\sigma_{m}|)}{\prod_{i\in[m]}|\sigma_{i}|},

and so

\rvol(\mathrm{SEP}(G)^{\sigma})=\frac{\gcd(|\sigma_{1}|,\dots,|\sigma_{m}|)}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}|\sigma_{i}|}\rvol(\mathrm{SEP}(G^{\prime})),

concluding the proof.
∎

5.
Fixed Polytopes of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})

In this section, we specialize the results of Section
4
to the special case of complete graphs,
K_{n}
, which yield a family of polytopes of arising from structures in diverse branches of mathematics.
In particular, we obtain explicit volume formulas and inequality descriptions for the fixed polytopes of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
.

The symmetric edge polytopes of complete graphs,
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
have been studied from a variety of perspectives, under a multitude of names.
For example,
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
is exactly the root polytope of type
A_{n}
introduced by
[
12
]
and studied further by
[
18
]
.
Further, Hetyei refers to the same polytope as the Legendre polytope,
\mathcal{L}_{n-1}
, with connections to Legendre polynomials and Delannoy numbers in
[
13
]
.
There, Hetyei notes that these polytopes are the intersection of the cross polytope in dimension
n
with the hyperplane normal to the all ones vector.
Meanwhile,
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
is the convex hull of the set of points whose coordinates are permutations of the
n
-element tuple
(1,0,\dots,0,-1)
, and are thus generalized permutahedra,
\Pi_{n}(1,0,\dots,0,-1)

[
18
,
7
]
.

First, we provide a vertex description of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
which follows as a corollary of Theorem
4.3
.

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
\sigma\in S_{n}
have cycle decomposition
\sigma=\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{m}
.
Then,
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})^{\sigma}
has vertex set

\left\{\frac{1}{|\sigma_{i}|}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{i}}-\frac{1}{|\sigma_{j}|}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{j}}\vphantom{i,j\in[m],i\neq j}\;\right\rvert\;\left.i,j\in[m],i\neq j\vphantom{\frac{1}{|\sigma_{i}|}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{i}}-\frac{1}{|\sigma_{j}|}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{j}}}\right\}.

Proof.

Consider
G=K_{n}
. By Theorem
4.3
, the vertices of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})^{\sigma}=\psi_{\sigma}(\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n}))
are exactly
\left\{\frac{1}{|\sigma_{i}|}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{i}}-\frac{1}{|\sigma_{j}|}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{j}}\vphantom{(a,b)\in E(K_{n})\text{ for some }a\in\sigma_{i}\text{ and }b\in\sigma_{j}}\;\right\rvert\;\left.(a,b)\in E(K_{n})\text{ for some }a\in\sigma_{i}\text{ and }b\in\sigma_{j}\vphantom{\frac{1}{|\sigma_{i}|}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{i}}-\frac{1}{|\sigma_{j}|}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{j}}}\right\}
, but
K_{n}
contains edges between all pairs of distinct vertices, so the membership condition of this set is true for all
i,j\in[m]
with
i\neq j
.
∎

We now identify combinatorial and linear equivalence of the fixed polytope
SEP(K_{n})^{\sigma}
to a symmetric edge polytope determined by the length of a permutation.
More specifically, for
\sigma\in S_{n}
with cycle decomposition
\sigma=\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{m}
, we identify combinatorial and linear equivalence between
SEP(K_{n})^{\sigma}
and
SEP(K_{m})
. We then provide insight into the dimension of the fixed polytope as well as its
H
description.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
\sigma\in S_{n}
have cycle decomposition
\sigma=\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{m}
.
Then,
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})^{\sigma}
is combinatorially and linearly equivalent to
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{m})
.

Proof.

Note that
K_{m}
corresponds to the contraction of
K_{n}
induced by
\sigma
. Hence, by Corollary
4.4
,
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})^{\sigma}
is combinatorially and linearly equivalent to
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{m})
.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
\sigma\in S_{n}
have cycle decomposition
\sigma=\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{m}
.
Then
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})^{\sigma}
has dimension
m-1
.

Proof.

By Corollary
5.2
, we have that
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})^{\sigma}
is combinatorially and linearly equivalent to
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{m})
. Since combinatorial equivalence implies a bijection between face lattices and
\dim\mathrm{SEP}(K_{m})=m-1
, we have
\dim\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})^{\sigma}=m-1
.
∎

The results above demonstrate how a fixed polytope
SEP(K_{n})^{\sigma}
captures a lower-dimensional polytope
SEP(K_{m})
, where
m
is determined by the number of cycles in
\sigma
. The following example concretely shows how a nontrivial permutation reduces ambient dimension and yields a fixed polytope linearly and combinatorially equivalent to
SEP(K_{m})
.

We now compute the relative volume of the fixed subpolytopes of symmetric edge polytopes of complete graphs under arbitrary coordinate permutations, leveraging the volume relationship obtained in Theorem
4.5
and an explicit volume formula for
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
as found in
[
1
]
.

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
\sigma\in S_{n}
have cycle decomposition
\sigma=\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{m}
.
Then,

\rvol(\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})^{\sigma})=\frac{\gcd(|\sigma_{1}|,\dots,|\sigma_{m}|)}{(m-1)!\prod_{i=1}^{m}|\sigma_{i}|}\binom{2(m-1)}{m-1}.

Proof.

From
[
1
]
we have
\rvol(\mathrm{SEP}(K_{m}))=\frac{1}{(m-1)!}\binom{2(m-1)}{m-1}
.
Then, applying Theorem
4.5
, we see

\rvol(\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})^{\sigma})=\frac{\gcd(|\sigma_{1}|,\dots,|\sigma_{m}|)}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}|\sigma_{i}|}\rvol(\mathrm{SEP}(K_{m}))=\frac{\gcd(|\sigma_{1}|,\dots,|\sigma_{m}|)}{(m-1)!\prod_{i=1}^{m}|\sigma_{i}|}\binom{2(m-1)}{m-1},

as desired.
∎

We provide an alternative inequality description for the symmetric edge polytope of the complete graph to facilitate later analysis of facet structure.

Corollary 5.6
.

The symmetric edge polytope
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
is the set of points
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
{\mathbf{1}}^{\top}\mathbf{x}=0
and
\left\lvert\mathbf{e}_{S}^{\top}\mathbf{x}\right\rvert\leq 1
for all nonempty proper subsets
S\subset[n]
with
|S|\leq\frac{n}{2}
.
Further, the half-space
\mathbf{e}_{S}^{\top}\mathbf{x}\geq-1
in this description is equivalent to the half-space
\mathbf{e}_{S^{C}}^{\top}\mathbf{x}\leq 1
in Proposition
3.2
when restricted to
{\mathbf{1}}^{\top}\mathbf{x}=0
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
{\mathbf{1}}^{\top}\mathbf{x}=0
and
S
be any nonempty proper subset of
[n]
.
We will show that
\mathbf{e}_{S}^{\top}\mathbf{x}\geq-1
if and only if
\mathbf{e}_{S^{C}}^{\top}\mathbf{x}\leq 1
; the desired inequality description and the noted correspondence with Proposition
3.2
then follow.
Observe that
\mathbf{e}_{S^{C}}=\mathbf{1}-\mathbf{e}_{S}
, so

\mathbf{e}_{S^{C}}^{\top}\mathbf{x}=(\mathbf{1}-\mathbf{e}_{S})^{\top}\mathbf{x}=-\mathbf{e}_{S}^{\top}\mathbf{x}.

Thus,
\mathbf{e}_{S}^{\top}\mathbf{x}\geq-1
if and only if
-\mathbf{e}_{S}^{\top}\mathbf{x}\leq 1
if and only if
\mathbf{e}_{S^{C}}^{\top}\mathbf{x}\leq 1
, as desired.
∎

The corollary above captures the centrally symmetric structure of
SEP(K_{n})
.
By restricting the inequalities to an invariant subspace, we are able to characterize facets of the fixed polytope induced by the original facets and further count the facets of
SEP(K_{n})^{\sigma}
.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
\sigma\in S_{n}
have cycle decomposition
\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{m}
, and label the facets of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
each with proper subsets of
[n]
as in Proposition
3.2
.
Then
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})^{\sigma}
has exactly
2^{m}-2
facets.
Each facet is contained in a distinct facet of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
whose labels satisfy
S\cap\sigma_{i}=\emptyset
or
S\cap\sigma_{k}=\sigma_{k}
for each
k\in[m]
.

Proof.

First, we will show that the interior of each facet of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
whose label contains all of
\sigma_{k}
or none of
\sigma_{k}
for each
k\in[m]
contains a point in
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})^{\sigma}
and thus contains a facet of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})^{\sigma}
.

Consider a facet with label
S
satisfying this condition, and define
\mathbf{x}=\frac{1}{|S||S^{C}|}\sum_{(i,j)\in S\times S^{C}}(\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j})
, which is a convex combination of vectors of the form
\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j}
, and thus contained in
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
.
Rearranging, we can see

\mathbf{x}=\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{i\in S}\mathbf{e}_{i}-\frac{1}{|S^{C}|}\sum_{j\in S^{C}}\mathbf{e}_{j}=\frac{1}{|S|}\mathbf{e}_{S}-\frac{1}{|S^{C}|}\mathbf{e}_{S^{C}},

so
\mathbf{e}_{S}^{\top}\mathbf{x}=\frac{|S|}{|S|}=1
, meaning
\mathbf{x}
is on the facet with label
S
.
Further, for any
S^{\prime}\neq S\subset[n]
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}_{S^{\prime}}^{\top}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{e}_{S}+\mathbf{e}_{S^{\prime}\setminus S}-\mathbf{e}_{S\setminus S^{\prime}})^{\top}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{e}_{S}^{\top}\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{e}_{S^{\prime}\setminus S}^{\top}\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{e}_{S\setminus S^{\prime}}^{\top}\mathbf{x}.

Noting that
\mathbf{e}_{S^{\prime}\setminus S}^{\top}\mathbf{x}\leq 0
,
\mathbf{e}_{S\setminus S^{\prime}}^{\top}\mathbf{x}\geq 0
,
\mathbf{e}_{S}^{\top}\mathbf{x}=1
, and that
\mathbf{e}_{S^{\prime}\setminus S}^{\top}\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{e}_{S\setminus S}^{\top}\mathbf{x}
cannot both be zero, we have
\mathbf{e}_{S^{\prime}}^{\top}\mathbf{x}<1
, so
\mathbf{x}
is not on any other facet of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
.
Thus,
\mathbf{x}
is in the interior of the facet of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
with label
S
.

Additionally, note that whether
\sigma_{k}\subset S
or
S\cap\sigma_{k}=\emptyset
,
\sigma_{k}\cdot S=S
, so for all
k\in[m]
,

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{i\in S}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{k}(i)}-\frac{1}{|S^{C}|}\sum_{j\in S^{C}}\mathbf{e}_{\sigma_{k}(j)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{i\in\sigma_{k}(S)}\mathbf{e}_{i}-\frac{1}{|S^{C}|}\sum_{i\in\sigma_{k}(S^{C})}\mathbf{e}_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{i\in S}\mathbf{e}_{i}-\frac{1}{|S^{C}|}\sum_{j\in S^{C}}\mathbf{e}_{j}=\mathbf{x}.

Thus,
\mathbf{x}\in\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})^{\sigma}
, so
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})^{\sigma}
contains a point in the interior of the facet of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
with label
S
, and thus has a facet contained in the facet of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
with label
S
.
Note that if
S
satisfies this condition, then
S^{C}
also does, so the inequalities
\mathbf{e}_{S}^{\top}\mathbf{x}\leq 1
and
\mathbf{e}_{S^{C}}^{\top}\mathbf{x}\leq 1
are both included in the inequality description of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})^{\sigma}
.
These inequalities can be paired to write
|\mathbf{e}_{S}^{\top}\mathbf{x}|\leq 1
as in Corollary
5.6
.

Now, consider a facet with label
S
such that
\emptyset\subsetneq S\cap\sigma_{k}\subsetneq\sigma_{k}
for some
k\in[m]
.
Note that
0\leq(\mathbf{e}_{S})_{i}\leq(\mathbf{e}_{S\cup\sigma_{k}})_{i}
for all
i\in[n]
.
Recall that for any
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
0\leq a_{i}\leq b_{i}
for all
i\in[n]
, we have
|\mathbf{a}^{\top}\mathbf{x}|\leq|\mathbf{b}^{\top}\mathbf{x}|
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Hence,
\left\lvert\mathbf{e}_{S\cup\sigma_{k}}^{\top}\mathbf{x}\right\rvert\leq 1
implies
\left\lvert\mathbf{e}_{S}^{\top}\mathbf{x}\right\rvert\leq 1
, so the latter pair of inequalities is redundant given the former.
Since the former inequalities come from facets of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
with labels that include all of
\sigma_{k}
or none of it, we have already shown that the former inequalities are included in the inequality description of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})^{\sigma}
.
Thus, the half-planes defined by facets of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})
with labels for which
\emptyset\subsetneq S\cap\sigma_{k}\subsetneq\sigma_{k}
are not needed in the inequality description of
\mathrm{SEP}(K_{n})^{\sigma}
and do not correspond to its facets.
∎

Abstract
Аннотация. This paper is a direct continuation of the paper [ 1 ] . By this reason neither introductory part of the paper nor the list of references are not duplicated. However for the reader convenience, the formulas from the first paper that are cited here are collected in a special addendum at the end of the paper with their original numbers. At this paper two new local foliations are investigated: minor pockets and rectangles. The appearance of such local foliations is illustrated by the further investigation of the examples with the boundary functions being the polynomials of the third degree.

8.
Minor pockets

In this section we consider the horizontal herringbones with
a spine going from a point on a boundary line to another
point on the same line and not intersecting the middle
line of the strip. We will refer to such foliation as a

minor pocket
to separate them from
major pockets
.
In a case of a major pocket the spine of a herringbone
also starts and ends on the same boundary line but intersects
the middle line of the strip twice. Such pockets have
completely different origin and they will be considered later.

Minor pockets appear inside the domains with the simple
foliation. Due to the symmetry we can consider only the
domains with the right simple foliation and the pockets that
appear on the upper boundary. Any other cases can be obtained
by means of change the sign of the variables
x_{1}
and/or
x_{2}
.

Let us assume that for
\varepsilon\in[\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{0})
we have
a standard right simple candidate on a domain

\Omega^{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm R}}_{\varepsilon}[u_{1},u_{2}]
. This means that for
u\in[u_{1},u_{2}]
and

\varepsilon\in[\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{0})
two conditions

(8.1)

\displaystyle 2\varepsilon D_{+}(u,\varepsilon)=f_{+}^{\prime}(u+\varepsilon)-f_{-}^{\prime}(u-\varepsilon)-2\varepsilon f_{+}^{\prime\prime}(u+\varepsilon)

\displaystyle>0\,;

(8.2)

\displaystyle 2\varepsilon D_{-}(u,\varepsilon)=f_{+}^{\prime}(u+\varepsilon)-f_{-}^{\prime}(u-\varepsilon)-2\varepsilon f_{-}^{\prime\prime}(u-\varepsilon)

\displaystyle>0

are fulfilled (see conditions (2.2) in
[
1
]
).
Moreover, for
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{0}
one of these conditions
degenerates. We will assume that this is
condition (
8.1
), i. e., there exists a point

u_{0}\in(u_{1},u_{2})
such that

(8.3)

D_{+}(u_{0},\varepsilon_{0})=0\,,

but for all other points
u\in[u_{1},u_{0})\cup(u_{0},u_{2}]

condition (
8.1
) still holds for
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{0}
.
We will assume that this root is not degenerated if the
following sense: the function
\varepsilon\mapsto D_{+}(u_{0},\varepsilon)

has a simple root at the point
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{0}
, i. e.,

(8.4)

\frac{\partial D_{+}}{\partial x_{2}}(u_{0},\varepsilon_{0})=\frac{f_{-}^{\prime\prime}(u_{0}-\varepsilon_{0})-f_{+}^{\prime\prime}(u_{0}+\varepsilon_{0})}{2\varepsilon_{0}}-f_{+}^{\prime\prime\prime}(u_{0}+\varepsilon_{0})<0\,,

and the function
u\mapsto D_{+}(u,\varepsilon_{0})
has a root of
the second order at the point

u=u_{0}
, i. e.,

(8.5)

\frac{\partial D_{+}}{\partial x_{1}}(u_{0},\varepsilon_{0})=\frac{f_{+}^{\prime\prime}(u_{0}+\varepsilon_{0})-f_{-}^{\prime\prime}(u_{0}-\varepsilon_{0})}{2\varepsilon_{0}}-f_{+}^{\prime\prime\prime}(u_{0}+\varepsilon_{0})=0\,,

and

(8.6)

\frac{\partial^{2}D_{+}}{\partial x_{1}^{2}}(u_{0},\varepsilon_{0})=\frac{f_{+}^{\prime\prime\prime}(u_{0}+\varepsilon_{0})-f_{-}^{\prime\prime\prime}(u_{0}-\varepsilon_{0})}{2\varepsilon_{0}}-f_{+}^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(u_{0}+\varepsilon_{0})>0\,.

Let us mention that condition (
8.5
) allows us to
rewrite (
8.4
) in the following simpler form:

(8.7)

f_{+}^{\prime\prime\prime}(u_{0}+\varepsilon_{0})>0\,.

Under these assumptions the equation
D_{+}(u,\varepsilon)=0
has
two roots
u^{l}(\varepsilon)
and
u^{r}(\varepsilon)
(
u^{l}<u^{r}
) for every

\varepsilon
in some right neighborhood of
\varepsilon_{0}
. Writing
u_{+}

in some assertion, we will mean that this assertion is true
for each of these two roots, for example,
D_{+}(u_{+},\varepsilon)=0
.
We assume that
\varepsilon
is sufficiently close to
\varepsilon_{0}
, so
that
D_{+}(u,\varepsilon)>0
for
u\in[u_{1},u^{l})\cup(u^{r},u_{2}]
and

D_{+}(u,\varepsilon)<0
for
u\in(u^{l},u^{r})
. All these assumptions
mean that for
\varepsilon
sufficiently close to
\varepsilon_{0}
the
function
u\mapsto D_{+}(u,\varepsilon)
decreases at the point
u^{l}

and increases at the point
u^{r}
. Therefore,

(D_{+})^{\prime}_{u}(u^{l})<0
and
(D_{+})^{\prime}_{u}(u^{r})>0
, i. e.,

(8.8)

f_{+}^{\prime\prime}(u^{l}+\varepsilon)-f_{-}^{\prime\prime}(u^{l}-\varepsilon)-2\varepsilon f_{+}^{\prime\prime\prime}(u^{l}+\varepsilon)<0

and

(8.9)

f_{+}^{\prime\prime}(u^{r}+\varepsilon)-f_{-}^{\prime\prime}(u^{r}-\varepsilon)-2\varepsilon f_{+}^{\prime\prime\prime}(u^{r}+\varepsilon)>0\,.

We will assume that
\varepsilon
is so close to
\varepsilon_{0}
that we
have not only (
8.7
), but

(8.10)

f_{+}^{\prime\prime\prime}(u_{+}+\varepsilon)>0

as well.

We need some new notation for minor pockets. The pockets
appearing on the upper boundary we mark by the index ‘‘
+
’’
and the index ‘‘
-
’’ will be used for the pockets on the lower boundary. If it is a right
herringbone, then it will be marked by the letter
\mathrm{E}
(east),
for a left herringbone will be used the letter
\mathrm{W}
(west).

Now we are ready to state the main assertion of this section.

Proof of Theorem
8.1
.

Let us begin with the investigation of the field (5.3) in a small
domain including the stationary points
U^{l}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\mathrm{def}}}(u^{l},\varepsilon)
and
U^{r}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\mathrm{def}}}(u^{r},\varepsilon)
.
We start from consideration of three curves
X_{0}
,
X_{1}
, and
X_{\infty}
. We define
X_{0}
as a set of
points, where the integral curves of our field have the slope equal to
0
(i. e.,
\dot{x}_{2}=0
). On
X_{1}
the slope is equal to
1

(i. e.,
\dot{x}_{2}=\dot{x}_{1}
), and on
X_{\infty}
the slope is equal to
\infty
(i. e.,
\dot{x}_{1}=0
), correspondingly.

Slightly abusing notation we will use the same symbols
X_{i}
the following functions:

\displaystyle X_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=f_{-}^{\prime}-f_{+}^{\prime}-(\varepsilon-x_{2})f_{-}^{\prime\prime}+(\varepsilon+x_{2})f_{+}^{\prime\prime}\,;

\displaystyle X_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=f_{+}^{\prime}-f_{-}^{\prime}-2x_{2}f_{+}^{\prime\prime}\,;

\displaystyle X_{\infty}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\big(f_{+}^{\prime}-f_{-}^{\prime}\big)-x_{2}(\varepsilon-x_{2})f_{-}^{\prime\prime}-x_{2}(\varepsilon+x_{2})f_{+}^{\prime\prime}\,.

According to equation (5.3) the curves
X_{i}
are given by the
equations
X_{i}=0
. Recall that the arguments of
f_{+}
and all its derivatives are
x_{1}+x_{2}
,
the arguments of
f_{-}
and all its derivatives are
x_{1}-x_{2}
.

Lemma 8.5
.

All three curves
X_{0},

X_{1},
and
X_{\infty}
pass through the points
U^{l}
and
U^{r}
​.
If
\varepsilon
is sufficiently close to
\varepsilon_{0},
then the mentioned curves are situated between
these two points in the following order
:

X_{\infty}
lies below the curve
x_{2}=\varepsilon

and above
X_{1},
the curve
X_{0}
lies below
X_{1}
.

Доказательство.

The first assertion of the lemma follows from the simple equalities:

\displaystyle X_{0}(x_{1},\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=-2\varepsilon D_{+}(x_{1},\varepsilon)\,;

\displaystyle X_{1}(x_{1},\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=2\varepsilon D_{+}(x_{1},\varepsilon)\,;

\displaystyle X_{\infty}(x_{1},\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=2\varepsilon^{2}D_{+}(x_{1},\varepsilon)\,.

To prove the second assertion of the lemma about mutual position of the curves
X_{i}
we
calculate the slopes of these curves at the points
U^{l}
and
U^{r}
. Calculating the
slope
(X_{i})=-\frac{\partial X_{i}}{\partial x_{1}}/\frac{\partial X_{i}}{\partial x_{2}}

we use the notation from (5.18):

(8.15)

\kappa_{+}=\frac{2f_{+}^{\prime\prime\prime}(u_{+}+\varepsilon)}{f_{+}^{\prime\prime}(u_{+}+\varepsilon)-f_{-}^{\prime\prime}(u_{+}-\varepsilon)-2\varepsilon f_{+}^{\prime\prime\prime}(u_{+}+\varepsilon)}\,.

Since we have here two roots
u^{l}
and
u^{r}
of the equation
D_{+}(u,\varepsilon)=0
instead of
one root
u_{+}
in (5.18), the corresponding values of
\kappa_{+}
will be
denoted
\kappa^{l}
and
\kappa^{r}
. Note that according to (
8.8
)–(
8.10
)
we have
\kappa^{l}<0
and
\kappa^{r}>0
. Moreover,
\kappa^{l}\to-\infty
and
\kappa^{r}\to+\infty

as
\varepsilon\to\varepsilon_{0}
from above. Indeed, in this situation
u^{l,r}\to u_{0}
, and therefore, denominator
in (
8.15
) tends to 0.

By direct calculations we get (recall that
u_{+}
is any of roots
u^{l,r}
, and then
naturally
\kappa_{+}
is the corresponding
\kappa^{l,r}
):

\displaystyle\text{slope}(X_{0})(u_{+},\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{+}^{\prime\prime}(u_{+}\!+\varepsilon)-f_{-}^{\prime\prime}(u_{+}\!-\varepsilon)-2\varepsilon f_{+}^{\prime\prime\prime}(u_{+}\!+\varepsilon)}{2\varepsilon f_{+}^{\prime\prime\prime}(u_{+}\!+\varepsilon)}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon\kappa_{+}}\,;

\displaystyle\text{slope}(X_{1})(u_{+},\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{+}^{\prime\prime}(u_{+}\!+\varepsilon)-f_{-}^{\prime\prime}(u_{+}\!-\varepsilon)-2\varepsilon f_{+}^{\prime\prime\prime}(u_{+}\!+\varepsilon)}{f_{+}^{\prime\prime}(u_{+}\!+\varepsilon)-f_{-}^{\prime\prime}(u_{+}\!-\varepsilon)+2\varepsilon f_{+}^{\prime\prime\prime}(u_{+}\!+\varepsilon)}=\frac{1}{1+2\varepsilon\kappa_{+}}\,;\rule{0.0pt}{20.0pt}

\displaystyle\text{slope}(X_{\infty})(u_{+},\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{+}^{\prime\prime}(u_{+}\!+\varepsilon)-f_{-}^{\prime\prime}(u_{+}\!-\varepsilon)-2\varepsilon f_{+}^{\prime\prime\prime}(u_{+}\!+\varepsilon)}{2f_{+}^{\prime\prime}(u_{+}\!+\varepsilon)-2f_{-}^{\prime\prime}(u_{+}\!-\varepsilon)+2\varepsilon f_{+}^{\prime\prime\prime}(u_{+}\!+\varepsilon)}=\frac{1}{2+3\varepsilon\kappa_{+}}\,.\rule{0.0pt}{25.0pt}

Since
\kappa^{r}>0
, we have
2+3\varepsilon\kappa^{r}>1+2\varepsilon\kappa^{r}>\varepsilon\kappa^{r}>0
, i. e.,
at the point
U^{r}
we have

0<\text{slope}(X_{\infty})(u^{r},\varepsilon)<\text{slope}(X_{1})(u^{r},\varepsilon)<\text{slope}(X_{0})(u^{r},\varepsilon).

Note that all these curves on the interval between
U^{l}
and
U^{r}
cannot cross each
other and are uniformly close to the horizontal line
x_{2}=\varepsilon
as
\varepsilon\to\varepsilon_{0}
.
The implicit function theorem and condition (
8.7
) supply us with the
possibility to speak about mutual positions of these curves for
\varepsilon
being
slightly bigger than
\varepsilon_{0}
.
The above chain of the inequalities is sufficient for the fact that the mutual
position of the considered curves is such as described in the lemma.

The inequalities (

9.
Examples. Polynomials of third degree:

the case of one minor pocket

In Section 7, were we started consideration of the third
degree polynomials

f_{\pm}(t)=a_{3}^{\pm}t^{3}+a_{2}^{\pm}t^{2}+a_{1}^{\pm}t+a_{0}^{\pm}\,,

the case
a_{3}^{+}=a_{3}^{-}
was described in full generality, and
the foliation in the case
a_{3}^{+}\neq a_{3}^{-}
was investigated
for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
only. Now we are ready
to describe the foliation in the case when the discriminant
of the quadratic polynomial

f_{+}^{\prime}(t)-f_{-}^{\prime}(t)=3(a_{3}^{+}-a_{3}^{-})t^{2}+2(a_{2}^{+}-a_{2}^{-})t+(a_{1}^{+}-a_{1}^{-})

is negative, i. e.,

(9.1)

3(a_{3}^{+}-a_{3}^{-})(a_{1}^{+}-a_{1}^{-})>(a_{2}^{+}-a_{2}^{-})^{2}.

Using the symmetry we can assume
|a_{3}^{+}|>|a_{3}^{-}|

(if not we interchange
f_{+}
and
f_{-}
) and
a_{3}^{+}>0

(if not we replace
t
by
-t
). We will investigate
now the field (5.3) to show that the situation falls
under the scope of Theorem
8.1
for

(9.2)

\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}^{+}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\mathrm{def}}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{12a_{3}^{+}}\Big[a_{1}^{+}-a_{1}^{-}-\frac{(a_{2}^{+}-a_{2}^{-})^{2}}{3(a_{3}^{+}-a_{3}^{-})}\Big]}\,.

We start with calculation of the functions
D_{\pm}
:

(9.3)

2x_{2}D_{+}(x)=3(a_{3}^{+}-a_{3}^{-})(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}+2(a_{2}^{+}-a_{2}^{-})(x_{1}-x_{2})+(a_{1}^{+}-a_{1}^{-})-12a_{3}^{+}x_{2}^{2}\,;

(9.4)

2x_{2}D_{-}(x)=3(a_{3}^{+}-a_{3}^{-})(x_{1}+x_{2})^{2}+2(a_{2}^{+}-a_{2}^{-})(x_{1}+x_{2})+(a_{1}^{+}-a_{1}^{-})+12a_{3}^{-}x_{2}^{2}\,.

We see that
D_{+}(u,\varepsilon)\geq 0
and
D_{-}(u,\varepsilon)\geq 0
for all

u
if
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
and, therefore, we have the right
simple foliation
\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\Omega^{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm R}}_{\varepsilon}(-\infty,+\infty)
.
For
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{0}^{+}
and

(9.5)

u_{0}^{+}=\varepsilon_{0}^{+}-\frac{a_{2}^{+}-a_{2}^{-}}{3(a_{3}^{+}-a_{3}^{-})}

condition (
8.3
) is fulfilled. Moreover,
\varepsilon_{0}^{+}

is a simple root, i. e., all
conditions (
8.4
)–(
8.7
) are fulfilled
as well. It will be evident if we rewrite the expression
from (
9.3
) in the form:

(9.6)

2x_{2}D_{+}(x)=3(a_{3}^{+}-a_{3}^{-})\big((x_{1}-u_{0}^{+})-(x_{2}-\varepsilon_{0}^{+})\big)^{2}-12a_{3}^{+}\big(x_{2}^{2}-(\varepsilon_{0}^{+})^{2}\big)\,.

Thus, from Theorem
8.1
we know that around the point

(u_{0}^{+}+\varepsilon_{0}^{+},\varepsilon_{0}^{+})
a minor pocket begins to grow.
The question is what happens if the parameter
\varepsilon
is
not very close to
\varepsilon_{0}^{+}
. We will see that the situation
depends on the sign of
a_{3}^{-}
.

First, we consider the case
a_{3}^{-}\geq 0
. When

\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}^{+}
we have the simple right foliation:

(9.7)

\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\Omega^{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm R}}_{\varepsilon}(-\infty,+\infty)\,,

because for such
\varepsilon
we have
D_{+}\geq 0
and
D_{-}\geq 0
on
the upper boundary (in fact, in the whole strip) and we apply
Proposition 2.2 (
[
1
]
). We would like to show that
for all
\varepsilon
greater than
\varepsilon_{0}^{+}
the foliation is
described by the decomposition

(9.8)

\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\Omega^{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm R}}_{\varepsilon}(-\infty,v_{+})\cup\Omega^{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm W$+$}}_{\varepsilon}(v_{+},u_{+}^{r})\cup\Omega^{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm R}}_{\varepsilon}(u_{+}^{r},+\infty)\,,

i. e., a herringbone is extended from the point

(v_{+}+\varepsilon,\,\varepsilon)
till the point
(u_{+}^{r}+\varepsilon,\,\varepsilon)
.

Looking on the expressions in (
9.6
) we see that
the equation
D_{+}(u,\varepsilon)=0
has exactly two roots
u_{+}^{l}

and
u_{+}^{r}
if
\varepsilon>\varepsilon_{0}^{+}
:

(9.9)

u_{+}^{l,r}=u_{0}^{+}+(\varepsilon-\varepsilon_{0}^{+})\pm 2\sqrt{\frac{a_{3}^{+}}{a_{3}^{+}-a_{3}^{-}}\big(\varepsilon^{2}-(\varepsilon_{0}^{+})^{2}\big)}.

From (
9.4
) we see that
D_{-}(u,\varepsilon)>0
for all

u
and
\varepsilon
. Therefore, to construct the foliation
presented in (
9.8
) we need to find an integral
curve of the field (5.3) connecting the points
(v_{+},\varepsilon)

and
(u_{+}^{r},\varepsilon)
.

The Jacobian matrix at the points
U_{+}^{r}
and
U_{+}^{l}
was
partially investigated after the proof of Lemma
8.5
,
where the matrix proportional to the Jacobian was written
in the form
(
see (
8.16
)
)
:

(9.10)

\begin{pmatrix}1&1-3s\\
-1&s-1\end{pmatrix},

where
s=1+\varepsilon\kappa_{+}
and
\kappa_{+}
is given
by (
8.15
). In the example under consideration we have

(9.11)

\kappa_{+}^{l}=-\kappa_{+}^{r}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\kappa_{+}^{r}=\sqrt{\frac{a_{3}^{+}}{(a_{3}^{+}-a_{3}^{-})(\varepsilon^{2}-(\varepsilon_{0}^{+})^{2})}}\,.

The characteristic polynomial of the matrix (
9.10
)
is
\lambda^{2}-s\lambda-2s
and the eigenvalues are

(9.12)

\lambda=\frac{s\pm\sqrt{s^{2}+8s}}{2}\,.

The corresponding eigenvectors are

(9.13)

\begin{pmatrix}s-1-\lambda\\
1\end{pmatrix}.

At
U_{+}^{r}
we always have a saddle point and the picture of
integral curves near this point looks like it is shown at
Pic.
22
. The integral curve we are interested in
starts from
U_{+}^{r}
along the eigenvector corresponding to

\lambda=\tfrac{1}{2}(s-\sqrt{s^{2}+8s})
,
i. e., its slope at
U_{+}^{r}
is

\frac{1}{s-1-\lambda}=\frac{2}{s-2+\sqrt{s^{2}+8s}}\,.

When
\varepsilon
grows from
\varepsilon_{0}^{+}
till
+\infty
,
the parameter
s
decreases from
+\infty
till some value
greater than
1
, and therefore, this slope monotonically
increases from
0
till some value less than
1
.

As it was mentioned, the type of the stationary point

U_{+}^{l}
depends on
\varepsilon
. We know that for
\varepsilon
sufficiently
close to
\varepsilon_{0}^{+}
(when
s<-8
) the Jacobian matrix
at
U_{+}^{l}
has two negative eigenvalues, therefore
U_{+}^{l}

is a node, and the picture of the integral curves near this
point looks like at Pic.
23
. In such a situation
the integral curve we are interested in intersects the boundary
exactly at the point
U_{+}^{l}
. This situation occurs for all

\varepsilon
if
80a_{3}^{+}\leq 81a_{3}^{-}
. Indeed, then

81(a_{3}^{+}-a_{3}^{-})\leq a_{3}^{+}
, whence

If
81(a_{3}^{+}-a_{3}^{-})>a_{3}^{+}
, then for

(9.14)

\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{1}^{+}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\mathrm{def}}}9\varepsilon_{0}^{+}\sqrt{\frac{a_{3}^{+}-a_{3}^{-}}{80a_{3}^{+}-81a_{3}^{-}}}

we have
s<-8
and the point
U_{+}^{l}
is a node and our
integral curve
\ell_{\varepsilon}
intersects the upper boundary
at the point
U_{+}^{l}
. For
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{1}^{+}
the eigenvalues
of the Jacobian matrix are equal, and
J(U_{+}^{l})
is
a Jordan cell, i. e.,
U_{+}^{l}
is an improper node and
our integral curve still passes through
U^{l}_{+}
.
The picture of integral curves is similar to the one
shown at Pic.
23
. The narrow angle between
the integral curves going along the eigenvectors of the
Jacobian matrix (thicker curves at Pic.
23
)
shrinks as
\varepsilon\nearrow\varepsilon_{1}^{+}
and for
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{1}^{+}

they turn into one curve.

The picture changes for
\varepsilon>\varepsilon_{1}^{+}
, when
s\in(-8,0)

and
U_{+}^{l}
is a spiral point. Then our integral curve
cannot cross the upper boundary not only on the interval

(u_{+}^{l},u_{+}^{r})
, but at the point
U_{+}^{l}
as well, i. e.,
for
\varepsilon>\varepsilon_{1}^{+}
we have
v_{+}<u_{+}^{l}
. Thus, we have got
what we wanted: for
a_{3}^{-}\geq 0
we have found an integral
curve of our field that connects two points of the upper
boundary with first coordinates
v_{+}
(
v_{+}\leq u_{+}^{l}
) and

u_{+}^{r}
, i. e., we can construct foliation (
9.8
).
Indeed, the condition
D_{+}>0
is fulfilled on
\ell_{\varepsilon}

because the curve
\ell_{\varepsilon}
goes strictly below the curve

X_{1}
, where
D_{+}=0
, and
D_{+}>0
on the whole upper
boundary except the interval
[u_{+}^{l},u_{+}^{r}]
. The inequality

D_{-}>0
is true everywhere in the strip
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
.

Let us say a few words about the behavior of the end points
of our pocket as
\varepsilon\to\infty
. First, it should be mentioned
that
v_{+}
always exists. Indeed, the curve
\ell_{\varepsilon}
can
be considered as a graph of a function of
x_{1}

(see Proposition
8.8
) and it cannot intersect the
midline of the strip. Therefore, it is sufficient to ascertain

bounded as
x_{1}\to-\infty
. However, this is true for any
integral curve, because if
x_{2}
is fixed and
x_{1}
has big
negative value then both
D_{+}
and
D_{-}
are approximately

3(a_{3}^{+}-a_{3}^{-})x_{1}^{2}/2x_{2}
, and therefore, the slope of an
integral curve tends to
-x_{2}/\varepsilon
, i. e., integral curves
have negative slope and are convex. Hence, if we go along

Secondly, from the expressions in (
9.9
) we can
conclude that the right end point of the pocket is

u_{+}^{r}+\varepsilon=2\varepsilon\Bigg(1+\sqrt{\frac{a_{3}^{+}}{a_{3}^{+}-a_{3}^{-}}}\Bigg)+u_{0}^{+}-\varepsilon_{0}^{+}+o(1)\,,

which tends always to

. The left end point of
the pocket is

, which tends to

if

, because

u_{+}^{l}+\varepsilon=2\varepsilon\Bigg(1-\sqrt{\frac{a_{3}^{+}}{a_{3}^{+}-a_{3}^{-}}}\Bigg)+u_{0}^{+}-\varepsilon_{0}^{+}+o(1)\,.

If
a_{3}^{-}=0
we cannot say anything about the asymptotic
behavior of the left end point, because
u_{+}^{l}+\varepsilon
is
bounded in this case, but we have no information about
v_{+}
,
except the inequality
v_{+}<u_{+}^{l}
for large
\varepsilon
.

11.
Rectangle

We will need a new notation for the areas foliated by the
herringbones. Previously, when all herringbones started and
ended on the boundary, the corresponding area could
conveniently be measured by the segment cut out by the
herringbone from the midline of the strip. Now we need
herringbones whose spines connect arbitrary two points in
the strip
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. If the spine goes from point
A
to point

C
, we will denote the corresponding area by

\Omega^{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm HB}}_{\varepsilon}(A,C)
1
1

1

The letters HB come from the word
HerringBone.
. By analogy with complex numbers, we will
denote by
\bar{x}
the point
(x_{1},-x_{2})

x+a\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\mathrm{def}}}(x_{1}+a,x_{2})
.

Using this notation, we state the main proposition of this
section, which shows how two oppositely oriented herringbones
can be joined within a common foliation.

Proposition 11.1
.

Let there exist a point
C=(C_{1},C_{2})\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}
,

such that we can construct
:

•

a left herringbone
\Omega^{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm HB}}_{\varepsilon}(C+\varepsilon,C^{r})
,
and

•

a right herringbone
\Omega^{\scriptscriptstyle\text{\rm HB}}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{C}-\varepsilon,C^{l})
.

Then
,
if we define in the rectangle between them
with vertices at the points
(C_{1}+C_{2},\varepsilon)
,

C+\varepsilon
,

(C_{1}-C_{2},-\varepsilon)
,
and
\bar{C}-\varepsilon

a bilinear function
(
which is uniquely determined
by its boundary values
),
we obtain
a diagonally concave
C^{1}
-smooth function.

Доказательство.

In a general case a bilinear function has the following
form:

(11.1)

a(x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2})+bx_{1}+cx_{2}+d\,.

The coefficient will be found using the value of the function
at four vertices:

(11.2)

\displaystyle f_{+}(C_{1}+C_{2})=B(C_{1}+C_{2},\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=a(C_{1}+C_{2})^{2}-a\varepsilon^{2}+b(C_{1}+C_{2})+c\varepsilon+d;

\displaystyle A^{l}(C_{1}+\varepsilon)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\mathrm{def}}}B(C_{1}+\varepsilon,C_{2})

\displaystyle=a(C_{1}+\varepsilon)^{2}-aC_{2}^{2}+b(C_{1}+\varepsilon)+cC_{2}+d;

\displaystyle f_{-}(C_{1}-C_{2})=B(C_{1}-C_{2},-\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=a(C_{1}-C_{2})^{2}-a\varepsilon^{2}+b(C_{1}-C_{2})-c\varepsilon+d;

\displaystyle A^{r}(C_{1}-\varepsilon)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\mathrm{def}}}B(C_{1}-\varepsilon,-C_{2})

\displaystyle=a(C_{1}-\varepsilon)^{2}-aC_{2}^{2}+b(C_{1}-\varepsilon)-cC_{2}+d.

Solving this system we get

(11.3)

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=\frac{A^{r}+A^{l}-f_{+}-f_{-}}{4(\varepsilon^{2}-C_{2}^{2})};

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=\frac{(C_{1}-C_{2})f_{+}+(C_{1}+C_{2})f_{-}-(C_{1}-\varepsilon)A^{l}-(C_{1}+\varepsilon)A^{r}}{2(\varepsilon^{2}-C_{2}^{2})};

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon(f_{+}-f_{-})+C_{2}(A^{r}-A^{l})}{2(\varepsilon^{2}-C_{2}^{2})};

\displaystyle d

\displaystyle=\frac{[\varepsilon^{2}-(C_{1}-C_{2})^{2}]f_{+}+[\varepsilon^{2}-(C_{1}+C_{2})^{2}]f_{-}}{4(\varepsilon^{2}-C_{2}^{2})}+

\displaystyle+\frac{[(C_{1}-\varepsilon)^{2}-C_{2}^{2}]A^{l}+[(C_{1}+\varepsilon)^{2}-C_{2}^{2}]A^{r}}{4(\varepsilon^{2}-C_{2}^{2})}.

The formulas will be a bit simpler, if we express

\displaystyle A^{l}

\displaystyle A^{r}

\displaystyle=A(C_{1}-\varepsilon,-C_{2})=

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}-C_{2}^{2}}{-2\varepsilon C_{2}}\Big[(\varepsilon+C_{2})f_{-}^{\prime}-(\varepsilon-C_{2})f_{+}^{\prime}\Big]+\frac{(\varepsilon-C_{2})f_{+}+(\varepsilon+C_{2})f_{-}}{2\varepsilon}\,.

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{+}^{\prime}-f_{-}^{\prime}}{4C_{2}};

(11.5)

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=\frac{(C_{1}+C_{2})f_{-}^{\prime}-(C_{1}-C_{2})f_{+}^{\prime}}{2C_{2}};

\displaystyle c

Concluding this section, we describe one more case where
a rectangle connecting two oppositely directed herringbones
appears. This is the previously unmentioned case of a zero

f^{\prime}_{+}-f^{\prime}_{-}
. Until now we have
assumed this expression has no multiple roots. We have just
considered the case where
f^{\prime}_{+}-f^{\prime}_{-}
has two roots with

and
a_{3}^{-}<0
. If we bring the roots closer while
keeping the leading coefficients of the polynomials,
the critical strip width
\varepsilon_{2}
up to which

) holds will tend to zero, and in
the limiting case of a multiple root there will be no

\varepsilon

\varepsilon

Thus, we consider the case when

(a_{2}^{+}-a_{2}^{-})^{2}=3(a_{3}^{+}-a_{3}^{-})(a_{1}^{+}-a_{1}^{-}),\qquad a_{3}^{+}>0,\quad\text{and}\quad a_{3}^{-}<0.

(12.11)

\begin{pmatrix}k_{+}-1+\sqrt{(1+k_{+})(9+k_{+})}\\
2\end{pmatrix},

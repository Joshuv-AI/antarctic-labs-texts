Glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists for treating chronic kidney disease in patients with type 2 diabetes
Authors: Irfan Khan, Matthew B. Lanktree
Published: 2024

Glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonists (also called “glutides”) are an effective treatment for patients with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease

In a randomized controlled trial of people with type 2 diabetes, albuminuria, and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) between 25 and 75 mL/min/1.73 m2, subcutaneous semaglutide reduced the relative risk of kidney failure, 50% decline in eGFR, or death by 24%.1 A meta-analysis of 64 286 patients showed that GLP-1 receptor agonists reduced the relative risk of all-cause death by 12% compared with placebo.2

They are a second-line treatment for patients with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease

Angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers, metformin, and sodium–glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT-2) inhibitors (also called “flozins”) are first-line treatments.3

GLP-1 receptor agonists should not be used in combination with dipeptidyl peptidase 4 inhibitors (DPP-4 inhibitors or “gliptins”)

GLP-1 receptor agonists and DPP-4 inhibitors both target incretin and are not synergistic.4 In contrast, GLP-1 receptor agonists and SGLT-2 inhibitors work in different ways and can be used together.1

Nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea are reported in 10%–50% of patients taking GLP-1 receptor agonists

Gastrointestinal adverse effects can be reduced with slow low-dose titration and eating small low-fat meals and will improve over time.5 In a clinical trial, semaglutide needed to be discontinued in 5% of users owing to gastrointestinal disorders.1 Liraglutide is contraindicated in patients with a history or family history of thyroid cancer, given a potential association of its use with thyroid carcinoma.

Weekly subcutaneous injection is preferred over daily oral semaglutide

Oral semaglutide may have less cardiovascular benefit and is limited to lower doses than used in weight-loss studies.3 Adherence to weekly subcutaneous injections may also be higher.

Note on the Hopf-algebra-based formula of Yang-Mills-Scalar amplitudes
Jiexi Liu, Yi-Jian Du

Abstract
In this note, we study the Hopf-algebra-based (HAB) formula of Yang-Mills-Scalar (YMS) amplitudes, which expands a YMS amplitude with massive scalars as a combination of propagator matrices that mix massless scalars corresponding to gluons with the original massive scalars. We propose a recursive formula which conveniently expresses the HAB formula. In this formula, gluons are converted into massless scalars. Thus it expresses a YMS amplitude with massive scalars by amplitudes with fewer gluons, massive scalars and massless scalars. We verify this formula by using soft behavior approach. In the massless limit, the HAB formula turns into expressions for YMS amplitudes with massless scalars which was earlier shown to satisfy an alternative recursive expansion formula. In this note, we show the equivalence of these two distinct approaches through explicit calculation on amplitudes with one and two gluons.

2.1
The recursive expansion formula of YMS

As proposed in
expEYM
,
massless
EYM amplitudes satisfy the recursive expansion formula, which also holds for YMS amplitudes. Concretely, for a tree-level doubly color-ordered massless YMS amplitude
expEYM
involving
n
scalars (
1,2,\ldots,n
) and
m
gluons denoted by
\{g_{i}\}
, and a given right permutation
\bm{\sigma}
of all scalars and gluons, we have

\displaystyle A_{\text{YMS}}(1,\ldots,n\|\{g_{i}\}\,|\,\bm{\sigma})=

\displaystyle\left(\epsilon_{f}\cdot X^{L}_{f}\right)A_{\text{YMS}}(1,\{2,\ldots,n-1\}\shuffle g_{f},n\|\{g_{i}\}\!\setminus\!g_{f}\,|\,\bm{\sigma})

(2.1)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\bm{\alpha}}\left(\epsilon_{f}\cdot F_{\bm{\alpha}^{\text{T}}}\cdot X^{L}_{\bm{\alpha}}\right)A_{\text{YMS}}(1,\{2,\ldots,n-1\}\shuffle\{\bm{\alpha},g_{f}\},n\|\{g_{i}\}\!\setminus\!(g_{f}\cup\bm{\alpha})\,|\,\bm{\sigma}),

in which,
g_{f}
is an arbitrarily chosen gluon (
fiducial gluon
), and all non-empty ordered subsets
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{a})
of gluons
\{g_{i}\}\!\setminus\!g_{f}
on the second line has been summed over. The shuffle symbol
\shuffle
denotes the sum over all possible permutations of scalars
\{2,\ldots,n-1\}
and gluons
\{\bm{\alpha},g_{f}\}
preserving the relative ordering in each ordered set. For example,
\{2,3\}\shuffle\{g_{1},g_{f}\}
implies that we sum over the following permutations

\{2,3\}\shuffle\{g_{1},g_{f}\}:\{g_{1},g_{f},2,3\},\{g_{1},2,g_{f},3\},\{g_{1},2,3,g_{f}\},\{2,g_{1},g_{f},3\},\{2,g_{1},3,g_{f}\},\{2,3,g_{1},g_{f}\}.

(2.2)

The
\big(X^{L}_{f}\big)^{\mu}
is the sum of the momenta of scalars on the left-hand side of the fiducial gluon
g_{f}
in a given permutation of scalars and gluon
g_{f}
. Similarly,
\big(X^{L}_{\bm{\alpha}}\big)^{\mu}
is the total momentum of scalars on the left-hand side of gluon
\alpha_{1}
(the
leftmost
element in
\bm{\alpha}
) in a given ordering of scalars and gluons
\{\bm{\alpha},g_{f}\}
. The tensor
F^{\mu\nu}_{\bm{\alpha}^{\text{T}}}
denotes the contraction of strength tensors
F^{\mu\nu}=k^{\mu}\epsilon^{\nu}-\epsilon^{\mu}k^{\nu}
of gluons in
\bm{\alpha}
and takes the form as

F_{\bm{\alpha}^{\text{T}}}^{\mu\nu}=(F_{\alpha_{a}}\cdot\ldots\cdot F_{\alpha_{1}})^{\mu\nu},

(2.3)

where the ordering
\bm{\alpha}^{\text{T}}=(\alpha_{a},\ldots,\alpha_{1})
is the inverse of
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{a})
.

Applying (
2.1
) iteratively, a doubly color-ordered YMS amplitude can be expanded into pure color-ordered BS ones

A_{\text{YMS}}(1,\ldots,n\|\{g_{i}\}\,|\,\bm{\sigma})=\sum_{\bm{\bm{\rho}}}C(\bm{\rho})A_{\text{BS}}(1,\bm{\rho},n\,|\,\bm{\sigma}),

(2.4)

with
1
and
n
fixed as the two ends. Here we have summed over all possible permutations of the scalars and gluons preserving the scalar ordering
\{2,\ldots,n-1\}
. Each coefficient
C(\bm{\rho})
arises from the corresponding class of expansion routes, which leads to the specific BS amplitude
A_{\text{BS}}(1,\bm{\rho},n\,|\,\bm{\sigma})
. For example, YMS amplitude with two scalars and two gluons
A_{\text{YMS}}(1,2\|\{g_{1},g_{2}\}\,|\,\bm{\sigma})
can be expanded with the recursive expansion formula (
2.1
) applied repeatedly

\displaystyle A_{\text{YMS}}(1,2\|\{g_{1},g_{2}\}\,|\,\bm{\sigma})

\displaystyle=(\epsilon_{1}\cdot p_{1})A_{\text{YMS}}(1,g_{1},2\|g_{2}\,|\,\bm{\sigma})+(\epsilon_{1}\cdot F_{2}\cdot p_{1})A_{\text{BS}}(1,g_{2},g_{1},2\,|\,\bm{\sigma}),

(2.5)

\displaystyle A_{\text{YMS}}(1,g_{1},2\|g_{2}\,|\,\bm{\sigma})

\displaystyle=\left[\epsilon_{2}\cdot(p_{1}+k_{1})\right]A_{\text{BS}}(1,g_{1},g_{2},2\,|\,\bm{\sigma})+(\epsilon_{2}\cdot p_{1})A_{\text{BS}}(1,g_{2},g_{1},2\,|\,\bm{\sigma}),

(2.6)

where we denote the momentum of scalar
i
by
p_{i}
and that of gluon
j
by
k_{j}
. The second line shows a further expansion of YMS amplitude
A_{\text{YMS}}(1,g_{1},2\|g_{2}\,|\,\bm{\sigma})
, which is on the RHS of the first expansion. Two expansions are combined to derive the expansion to pure BS amplitudes. According to the specific BS amplitude
A_{\text{BS}}(1,\bm{\rho},2\,|\,\bm{\sigma})
, we can rearrange the expansion coefficients from the corresponding expansion routes into
C(\bm{\rho})
. The coefficient
C(g_{1},g_{2})
arises from the two-step expansion route
(1,2)\to(1,g_{1},2)\to(1,g_{1},g_{2},2)
, while
C(g_{2},g_{1})
collects the contributions from
(1,2)\to(1,g_{2},g_{1},2)
and the two-step expansion route
(1,2)\to(1,g_{1},2)\to(1,g_{2},g_{1},2)
. Concretely, we have

\displaystyle C(g_{1},g_{2})=

\displaystyle(\epsilon_{1}\cdot p_{1})\left[\epsilon_{2}\cdot(p_{1}+k_{1})\right],

(2.7)

\displaystyle C(g_{2},g_{1})=

\displaystyle(\epsilon_{1}\cdot F_{2}\cdot p_{1})+(\epsilon_{1}\cdot p_{1})(\epsilon_{2}\cdot p_{1}),

and the explicit expression of the expansion of YMS amplitudes to pure BS amplitudes as

A_{\text{YMS}}(1,2\|\{g_{1},g_{2}\}\,|\,\bm{\sigma})=C(g_{1},g_{2})A_{\text{BS}}(1,g_{1},g_{2},2\,|\,\bm{\sigma})+C(g_{2},g_{1})A_{\text{BS}}(1,g_{2},g_{1},2\,|\,\bm{\sigma}).

(2.8)

As shown by
expEYM

2.3
The soft behavior approach to YMS amplitudes

Scattering amplitudes satisfy the universal soft behavior, which states that an
n
-point amplitude is factorized into an
n-1
point amplitude and a soft factor, when the momentum of a massless particle tends to zero. In this subsection, we review the soft scalar and soft gluon behaviors of BS and YMS amplitudes, which will be useful in the reconstruction of amplitudes in the coming sections
4
.

The leading soft behavior of BS amplitudes with
p_{i}\to\tau p_{i}
(
\tau\to 0
) is given by

A_{\text{BS}}^{(0)_{i}}(1,\ldots,n\,|\,\bm{\sigma})=S^{(0)}_{i}A_{\text{BS}}(1,\ldots,i-1,\not{i},i+1,\ldots,n\,|\,\bm{\sigma}),

(2.21)

where
\tau
has been absorbed into the leading soft factor for later convenience. The soft factor for scalar
s_{i}
takes the form

S^{(0)}_{i}=\frac{1}{\tau}\left(\frac{\delta_{(i-1)i}}{s_{(i-1)i}}+\frac{\delta_{i(i+1)}}{s_{i(i+1)}}\right).

(2.22)

Here the operator
\delta_{ij}
is defined as

\delta_{ij}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\quad i\prec j\\
-1&\quad j\prec i\\
0&\quad i,j\,\,\text{\small not adjacent}\end{array}\right.,

(2.23)

which is determined by the relative order of scalars
i
and
j
in the given ordering
\bm{\sigma}
. The notation

means that
i
is on the left-hand side of
j
in
\bm{\sigma}
. Similarly, the leading soft behavior of YMS amplitudes with a soft scalar
i
is represented as

A_{\text{YMS}}^{(0)_{i}}(1,\ldots,n\|\{g_{j}\}\,|\,\bm{\sigma})=S^{(0)}_{i}A_{\text{YMS}}(1,\ldots,i-1,\not{i},i+1,\ldots,n\|\{g_{j}\}\,|\,\bm{\sigma}),

(2.24)

where the leading scalar soft factor is the same as that of BS amplitudes (
2.22
) due to the universality of the scalar soft factor.

Analogous to the soft behavior of scalars, the YMS amplitudes are factorized under the soft limit of gluons. Specifically, when gluon
g_{i}
is the soft one, i.e.,
k_{i}\to\tau k_{i}
, the leading and subleading behaviors of YMS amplitudes are presented by

A_{\text{YMS}}(1,\ldots,n\|\{g_{j}\}\,|\,\bm{\sigma})=\left[S^{(0)}_{g_{i}}+S^{(1)}_{g_{i}}\right]A_{\text{YMS}}(1,\ldots,n\|\{g_{j}\}\!\setminus\!g_{i}\,|\,\bm{\sigma}\!\setminus\!g_{i})+\mathcal{O}(\tau^{1}),

(2.25)

The factors

and

are leading and subleading gluon soft factors

;
Casali:2014xpa

S^{(0)}_{g_{i}}=\frac{1}{\tau}\sum_{a\neq g_{i}}\frac{\delta_{ag_{i}}(\epsilon_{i}\cdot p_{a})}{s_{ag_{i}}},\quad S^{(1)}_{g_{i}}=\sum_{a\neq g_{i}}\frac{\delta_{ag_{i}}(\epsilon_{i}\cdot J_{a}\cdot\mathsf{K}_{s_{i}})}{s_{ag_{i}}},

(2.26)

where the operator
\delta_{ag_{i}}
is defined similarly as
\delta_{ij}
in (
2.23
).
We sum over all external particles
a
except for the soft gluon
g_{i}
and introduce the angular momentum
J^{\mu\nu}_{a}
for each particle

. It is appropriate to regard the subleading soft gluon factor as a differential operator acting on the kinematic variables of amplitudes excluding the gluon

. Explicitly, the soft factor

acts in the form of

\displaystyle\left(S^{(1)}_{g_{i}}k_{a}\right)\cdot V=

\displaystyle\,-\frac{\delta_{ag_{i}}}{s_{ag_{i}}}(k_{a}\cdot F_{i}\cdot V),

(2.27)

\displaystyle\left(S^{(1)}_{g_{i}}\epsilon_{a}\right)\cdot V=

\displaystyle\,-\frac{\delta_{ag_{i}}}{s_{ag_{i}}}(\epsilon_{a}\cdot F_{i}\cdot V),

(2.28)

\displaystyle V_{1}\cdot\left(S^{(1)}_{g_{i}}F_{j}\right)\cdot V_{2}=

\displaystyle\,-\frac{\delta_{g_{j}g_{i}}}{s_{g_{j}g_{i}}}V_{1}\cdot(F_{j}\cdot F_{i}-F_{i}\cdot F_{j})\cdot V_{2}.

(2.29)

Here,

In

By studying the YMS amplitudes in the soft limit, one can build an expansion formula of amplitudes from amplitudes with a chosen gluon or scalar removed. The explicit expressions of YMS amplitudes were verified from the soft behaviors of other particles and the permutation symmetry among gluons. Specifically, the expansion formula of YMS amplitudes (

where we sum over all ordered subsets

3
A recursive expansion relation from the HAB formula

In this section, we show that the HAB formulas (
2.13
), (
2.19
) induce a recursive expansion relation for YMS amplitudes mixing gluons, massive scalars, and massless scalars. By this recursion, some gluons in the original amplitude are converted into massless scalars, accompanied by proper kinematic coefficients which absorb the polarization vectors. We present a simple example and then sketch the general verification for amplitudes with at least three scalars. The boundary case with only two massive scalars is further studied.

3.1
Example

We now demonstrate the recursive expansion relation by YMS amplitude involving three massive scalars and two gluons
A_{\text{YMS}}(1,2,3\|\{g_{1},g_{2}\}\,|\,\bm{\sigma})
. According to the HAB formula (
2.13
), the amplitude is expressed as a sum over the two gluon orderings

(3.1)

The explicit expressions of the kinematic coefficients follow from (
2.15
) when the ordered partitions of gluons

and

\mathbf{P}_{\!\!\bm{\rho}^{g}}^{1}\!\!:\!\!\{g_{1},g_{2}\}

are taken into account:

\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{I}}\left(1,g_{1},g_{2},2,3\right)=-\frac{2p_{1}\cdot F_{12}\cdot p_{2}}{p_{12}^{2}-m_{s}^{2}}+\frac{2p_{1}\cdot F_{1}\cdot p_{2}}{p_{12}^{2}-m^{2}}\frac{2(p_{1}+k_{1})\cdot F_{2}\cdot p_{2}}{(p_{12}+k_{1})^{2}-m_{s}^{2}}+\frac{2p_{1}\cdot F_{2}\cdot p_{2}}{p_{12}^{2}-m^{2}}\frac{2p_{1}\cdot F_{1}\cdot(p_{2}+k_{2})}{(p_{12}+k_{2})^{2}-m_{s}^{2}}.

(3.2)

A key observation is that whenever a gluon occupies a single factor, it plays the role of a scalar from the perspective of the remaining gluon. As an illustration, consider the term
\frac{2p_{1}\cdot F_{1}\cdot p_{2}}{p_{12}^{2}-m^{2}}\frac{2(p_{1}+k_{1})\cdot F_{2}\cdot p_{2}}{(p_{12}+k_{1})^{2}-m_{s}^{2}}

, whereas the second factor contains the combined momentum

. Hence, from the viewpoint of
g_{2}
, gluon

behaves as a scalar. Consequently, the two-factor terms can be related to YMS amplitudes in which the prior gluon has already been converted into a massless scalar. For example, the second term of the kinematic coefficient (
3.2
) can be identified as a reduced kinematic coefficient with a factor

\frac{2p_{1}\cdot F_{1}\cdot p_{2}}{p_{12}^{2}-m^{2}}\frac{2(p_{1}+k_{1})\cdot F_{2}\cdot p_{2}}{(p_{12}+k_{1})^{2}-m_{s}^{2}}=\frac{2p_{1}\cdot F_{1}\cdot p_{2}}{p_{12}^{2}-m^{2}}\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{I}}\left(1,\overline{g}_{1},g_{2},2,3\right),

(3.4)

where we used

(i.e., the second term of (

5
The relationship between (
2.1
) and the HAB formulas

In this section, we establish the relationship between the recursive expansion (
2.1
), and the formulas (
3.19
), (
3.25
) derived from the HAB formula in the massless limit. To systematically study this relationship, we proceed from the simplest cases to more complicated ones, progressively. Particularly, we begin with the boundary case of two-scalar amplitudes. Next, we consider the base case of single-gluon amplitudes with an arbitrary number of scalars. Finally, we explicitly compute the five-point amplitude with two gluons, demonstrating that the recursive expansion (
2.1
) combined with the BCJ relations indeed reproduces the structure expected from the HAB formula. In each case, we derive a new expression from (
2.1
) and show that it matches the massless limit of the HAB formulas.

5.1
The boundary case: amplitudes with two scalars

The YMS amplitudes involving
massless
scalars admit an expansion in terms of amplitudes with more scalars and fewer gluons, multiplied by kinematic coefficients. We show the relation between the two expansion formulas (
2.1
) and (
2.30
) in the appendix
B
. We now restrict the expression (
B.4
), derived from (
2.1
), to YMS amplitudes containing exactly two scalars

\displaystyle A_{\text{YMS}}(1,2\|\{g_{i}\}\,|\,\bm{\sigma})=

\displaystyle\frac{p_{r}\cdot F_{1}\cdot p_{1}}{p_{r}\cdot k_{1}}A_{\text{YMS}}(1,g_{1},2\|\{g_{i}\}\!\setminus\!g_{1}\,|\,\bm{\sigma})

(5.1)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\bm{\alpha}}\frac{p_{r}\cdot F_{1}\cdot F_{\bm{\alpha}^{\text{T}}}\cdot p_{1}}{p_{r}\cdot k_{1}}A_{\text{YMS}}(1,\bm{\alpha},g_{1},2\|\{g_{i}\}\!\setminus\!(g_{1}\cup\bm{\alpha})\,|\,\bm{\sigma}).

Due to the gauge invariance, the reference momentum
p_{r}
can be chosen arbitrarily. We choose
p_{r}=2p_{1}
, for which the coefficient of the first term in (
5.1
) vanishes. The factor of
2
is introduced for convenience, and it allows the denominator to be rewritten as a scalar propagator
2p_{1}\cdot k_{1}=(p_{1}+k_{1})^{2}
. As a result, we obtain

A_{\text{YMS}}(1,2\|\{g_{i}\}\,|\,\bm{\sigma})=\sum_{\bm{\alpha}}\frac{2p_{1}\cdot F_{1}\cdot F_{\bm{\alpha}^{\text{T}}}\cdot p_{1}}{(p_{1}+k_{1})^{2}}A_{\text{YMS}}(1,\bm{\alpha},g_{1},2\|\{g_{i}\}\!\setminus\!(g_{1}\cup\bm{\alpha})\,|\,\bm{\sigma}).

(5.2)

This precisely reproduces the massless limit (i.e.
m_{s}^{2}->0
) of the recursive expansion formula (
3.25
) derived from the Hopf-algebra-based result, up to a reversal in the ordering of the field strength tensors

p_{1}\cdot F_{1}\cdot F_{\bm{\alpha}^{\text{T}}}\cdot p_{1}=(-1)^{|\bm{\alpha}|+1}p_{1}\cdot F_{\bm{\alpha}}\cdot F_{1}\cdot p_{1},

(5.3)

due to the antisymmetry property of the field strength tensors
F^{\mu\nu}=-F^{\nu\mu}
.

Having verified the equivalence for the boundary case, we now turn to amplitudes involving gluons. The simplest case involves a single gluon and multiple scalars, which serves as the base case for the recursive construction.

5.2
The base case: amplitudes with one gluon

In this subsection, we study YMS amplitudes involving at least three massless scalars and a single gluon.

Consider the general expansion formula (
2.30
) restricted to amplitudes with only one gluon,

(5.4)

Exploiting gauge invariance, we choose the reference momentum to be
p_{r}=2p_{n}
.
We define

as the sum of the momenta of scalars to the right of the gluon

,

(5.5)

Here, the momentum conservation
X^{L}+k_{1}+X^{R}+p_{n}=0
and the antisymmetry property of the field strength tensor
F_{1}^{\mu\nu}
are applied. Specifically, the coefficient associated with the YMS amplitude

5.4

In parallel, the denominator of the coefficients in (

Collecting the above results, we obtain

(5.8)

This result is consistent with the recursive expansion formula (
3.19

\displaystyle 0=A_{\text{YMS}}(1,2,3\|\{g_{1},g_{2}\}\,|\,\bm{\sigma})|_{\epsilon_{2}\to k_{2}}=T_{1}|_{\epsilon_{2}\to k_{2}}+T_{2}|_{\epsilon_{2}\to k_{2}}

(5.20)

Noting that in the expression (
5.22
) of
T_{1}
, both amplitude
A_{\text{YMS}}(1,g_{1},2,3\|\{g_{2}\}\,|\,\bm{\sigma})
and coefficients containing the field strength tensor
F_{2}^{\mu\nu}
are gauge invariant objects, the only surviving terms in
T_{1}|_{\epsilon_{2}\to k_{2}}
are given by

T_{1}|_{\epsilon_{2}\to k_{2}}=-\frac{2p_{1}\cdot k_{1}\,\epsilon_{1}\cdot k_{2}\,k_{2}\cdot p_{2}}{p_{12}^{2}}\,A_{\text{BS}}(1,g_{1},g_{2},2,3\,|\,\bm{\sigma})-\frac{2p_{1}\cdot k_{2}\,k_{2}\cdot\epsilon_{1}\,k_{1}\cdot p_{2}}{p_{12}^{2}}\,A_{\text{BS}}(1,g_{2},g_{1},2,3\,|\,\bm{\sigma}).

(5.21)

Substituting the above expression into (
5.20
), one can solve out the coefficient of
\epsilon_{1}\cdot{k_{2}}
in
T_{2}|_{\epsilon_{2}\to k_{2}}
and verify (
5.18
).

Summing (
5.22
) and (
5.18
) together, we finally get

\displaystyle A_{\text{YMS}}(1,2,3\|\{g_{1},g_{2}\}\,|\,\bm{\sigma})=

\displaystyle\frac{2p_{1}\cdot F_{1}\cdot p_{2}}{p_{12}^{2}}\,A_{\text{YMS}}(1,g_{1},2,3\|\{g_{2}\}\,|\,\bm{\sigma})+\frac{2p_{1}\cdot F_{2}\cdot p_{2}}{p_{12}^{2}}\,A_{\text{YMS}}(1,g_{2},2,3\|\{g_{1}\}\,|\,\bm{\sigma})

(5.22)

\displaystyle-\frac{2p_{1}\cdot F_{1}\cdot F_{2}\cdot p_{2}}{p_{12}^{2}}A_{\text{BS}}(1,g_{1},g_{2},2,3\,|\,\bm{\sigma})-\frac{2p_{1}\cdot F_{2}\cdot F_{1}\cdot p_{2}}{p_{12}^{2}}A_{\text{BS}}(1,g_{2},g_{1},2,3\,|\,\bm{\sigma}),

which is just the expected massless limit of the recursion formula (
3.19
) based on HAB formula.

These explicit computations—from the boundary two-scalar case to the two-gluon five-point example—collectively demonstrate that the recursive expansion (
2.1
) is consistent with the massless limit of the HAB formula. While we have not presented a general proof for an arbitrary numberof gluons, the pattern observed in these representative cases strongly suggests that the equivalence holds generally. The key mechanism appears to be that the BCJ relations systematically reorganize the coefficients involving the polarization vectors into the form of the HAB formula.

6
Conclusions

In this work, we established the relationship between the Hopf-algebra based (HAB) formula for massive-scalar YMS amplitudes and the recursive expansion formula for massless scalar YMS amplitudes. We proposed a convenient recursive formula for massive-scalar YMS amplitudes, and confirm this formula by soft behavior approach. This recursive formula iteratively results in the HAB formula. On the other hand, the massless limit becomes a formula for massless scalar YMS amplitudes that is further derived from the recursive formula in
expEYM
. We hope this work could provide a new insight into the study of matter coupling to gravitation.

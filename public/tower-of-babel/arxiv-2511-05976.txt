An anomaly-free leptophilic U ​ ( 1 ) ℓ ′ U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell} completion of the Standard Model
S. O. Kara

Abstract
We develop a minimal and fully anomaly-free realization of a universal leptophilic U ​ ( 1 ) ℓ ′ U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell} gauge symmetry, under which all Standard-Model leptons carry a common charge while quarks remain neutral. Gauge consistency is restored by introducing one vectorlike lepton family and singlet scalars whose vacuum expectation values both break U ​ ( 1 ) ℓ ′ U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell} and generate masses for the new gauge boson Z ℓ Z_{\ell} and the heavy leptons. We derive compact anomaly-cancellation conditions, closed-form Z ℓ Z_{\ell} couplings and decay widths, and the structure of gauge–kinetic mixing, including the trace condition Tr ⁡ ( Y ​ Q ′ ) = 0 \mathrm{Tr}(YQ^{\prime})=0 that suppresses radiative mixing. The setup naturally embeds a type-I seesaw for neutrino masses and provides a stable singlet field as a minimal dark-matter candidate. Collider and precision constraints are expressed analytically over the full parameter space, covering pure leptophilic production, small-mixing hadron colliders, and the contact-interaction limit M Z ℓ ≫ s M_{Z_{\ell}}\!\gg\!\sqrt{s} . The model thus provides a single, internally consistent benchmark that unifies gauge, scalar, Yukawa, neutrino, and dark-matter sectors, and is directly suited for global data recasts and targeted searches at current and future lepton and hadron colliders



I
Introduction

Neutrino oscillations
Nakamura et al. 2010
have established that individual lepton flavors are not conserved, implying physics beyond the Standard Model (SM). A minimal way to address this is to gauge an additional Abelian symmetry that acts on leptons. In this spirit, we consider a general leptophilic
U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell}
extension in which the three SM lepton families carry arbitrary charges
(q_{e},q_{\mu},q_{\tau})
while quarks are neutral. Prominent special cases include the universal assignment
q_{e}=q_{\mu}=q_{\tau}
and the well-known
L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}
scenario
Heeck and Rodejohann 2011
. The associated gauge boson, denoted
Z^{\prime}_{\ell}
(or
Z_{\ell}
henceforth), couples to leptons at tree level, while any quark coupling arises only via kinetic mixing.

Extensions of the SM by extra
U(1)
factors remain among the simplest and most predictive portals for new interactions
Navas et al. 2024
. A fully consistent framework, however, requires a UV-complete realization in which gauge, mixed, and gravitational anomalies are canceled and the symmetry is spontaneously broken. Here we construct such a realization for a generic leptophilic
U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell}
by introducing one vectorlike lepton family and SM-singlet scalars whose vacuum expectation values generate masses for both
Z_{\ell}
and the heavy leptons. We work in a left-handed Weyl basis and derive compact anomaly-cancellation relations applicable to both universal and nonuniversal charge assignments.

Kinetic mixing between
U(1)_{Y}
and
U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell}

Holdom 1986

\mathrm{Tr}(YQ^{\prime})=0
, a condition naturally satisfied for several anomaly-free solutions. We present analytic
Z_{\ell}
couplings and widths, including kinetic-mixing effects, and discuss useful limits such as the contact-interaction regime
M_{Z_{\ell}}^{2}\gg s

Eichten et al. 1983
. The setup accommodates type-I seesaw realizations of neutrino masses and admits minimal dark-matter candidates in the form of stable singlet fermions annihilating through
Z_{\ell}
exchange
Arcadi et al. 2020
, thereby tying collider, precision, and cosmological probes to a single gauge-parameter space.

The goal of this paper is analytic and model-building–oriented: to provide a closed-form benchmark that links gauge, scalar, and Yukawa sectors and enables transparent phenomenological mappings. We present the field content, charge assignments, anomaly checks, symmetry breaking, mass spectra, kinetic-mixing structure, and resulting parametric dependences for collider, precision, and dark-sector observables. A full global analysis (including likelihood fits and public code) is left to future work.

Models gauging total lepton number have been discussed in various contexts

II
Gauge structure and anomaly cancellation

Conventions.
We use
Q=T_{3}+Y
with
Y_{\rm PDG}=2Y
.
The covariant derivative is

D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}-igW_{\mu}^{a}T^{a}-ig_{Y}YB_{\mu}-ig_{\ell}Q^{\prime}B^{\prime}_{\mu},

and kinetic mixing
\varepsilon
is introduced in Sec. V.

We enlarge the SM gauge symmetry to

SU(3)_{C}\times SU(2)_{L}\times U(1)_{Y}\times U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell},

where
U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell}
acts on leptons but not on quarks. We adopt the standard hypercharge normalization
Q=T_{3}+Y
, implying
Y=Y_{\rm PDG}/2
.

Before turning to the most general flavor-dependent case, it is instructive to begin with a universal charge assignment and examine the resulting anomaly pattern.

II.1
Universal assignment and anomaly constraints

A genuinely leptophilic setup follows from assigning a common charge
a_{\ell}
to all lepton multiplets,

Q^{\prime}(L_{\ell})=Q^{\prime}(e_{R,\ell})=a_{\ell},\qquad Q^{\prime}({\rm quarks})=0,

while keeping the Higgs uncharged. Unlike the special choice
L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}

He et al. 1991
;
Foot et al. 1994
, this universal assignment induces mixed anomalies with
SU(2)_{L}
and
U(1)_{Y}
. A consistent
U(1)^{\prime}
gauge theory requires these anomalies to vanish; otherwise gauge invariance would be violated at the quantum level.

Anomaly structure and cancellation strategy.

We evaluate anomalies in a left-handed Weyl basis, replacing right-handed fields by their conjugates with opposite
U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell}
and
U(1)_{Y}
charges. Including three right-handed neutrinos, the SM leptons contribute

\displaystyle[SU(2)_{L}]^{2}U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell}

\displaystyle:\quad\tfrac{3}{2}\,a_{\ell}\neq 0,

(1)

\displaystyle[U(1)_{Y}]^{2}U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell}

\displaystyle:\quad-\,\tfrac{3}{2}\,a_{\ell}\neq 0,

(2)

\displaystyle[U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell}]^{2}U(1)_{Y}

\displaystyle:\quad 0,

(3)

\displaystyle[\text{grav}]^{2}U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell}

\displaystyle:\quad 0,

(4)

\displaystyle[U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell}]^{3}

\displaystyle:\quad 0.

(5)

The mixed anomalies must therefore be canceled by new fermions. Guided by the principle of minimality—keeping the exotic content to a single vectorlike family while retaining anomaly freedom—we introduce one vectorlike lepton doublet
(L^{\prime}_{L},L^{\prime}_{R})
and one vectorlike charged singlet
(E^{\prime}_{L},E^{\prime}_{R})
, vectorlike under the SM but chiral under
U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell}
.

II.2
Complete anomaly cancellation for general
(q_{e},q_{\mu},q_{\tau})

It is useful to record the general solution valid for arbitrary lepton charges
(q_{e},q_{\mu},q_{\tau})
, recovering the universal case by setting
q_{\alpha}=a_{\ell}
. Working in a left-handed basis with
Q^{\prime}(L_{\alpha})=Q^{\prime}(e^{c}_{\alpha})=q_{\alpha}
and defining

\Sigma q=q_{e}+q_{\mu}+q_{\tau},\qquad\Sigma q^{3}=q_{e}^{3}+q_{\mu}^{3}+q_{\tau}^{3},

the anomaly-free assignments for the exotics are

\boxed{\ \Delta_{L}=-\Sigma q,\qquad\Delta_{E}=+\Sigma q,\qquad\mathrm{Tr}(YQ^{\prime})=0\ \Rightarrow\ s_{E}=-\Sigma q\ },

where
\Delta_{L,E}\equiv Q^{\prime}(L^{\prime}_{L,E})-Q^{\prime}(L^{\prime}_{R,E})
and
s_{L,E}\equiv Q^{\prime}(L^{\prime}_{L,E})+Q^{\prime}(L^{\prime}_{R,E})
.
These relations cancel
[SU(2)_{L}]^{2}U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell}
,
[U(1)_{Y}]^{2}U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell}
, and gravitational anomalies, while the cubic condition fixes
s_{L}
:

\boxed{\ s_{L}\;=\;-\frac{4}{3}\,\frac{\Sigma q^{3}}{(\Sigma q)^{2}}\;+\;\Sigma q\qquad(\Sigma q\neq 0)\ }.

Corollary (
\Sigma q=0
).
—
If
\Sigma q=0
, as in
L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}
or
L_{e}-L_{\mu}
, all anomalies cancel within the SM (augmented by
\nu_{R}
), and no exotic fermions are required.

This construction shows that anomaly-free leptophilic
U(1)^{\prime}
models are highly constrained yet sufficiently flexible to realize both universal and flavor-dependent scenarios.
Together with the scalar and Yukawa sectors discussed in the following sections, it completes the anomaly-free backbone promised in Sec.
I
, establishing the theoretical closure that will underpin the phenomenological analysis to come.

Explicit benchmark example.

For illustration, consider the nonuniversal choice

(q_{e},q_{\mu},q_{\tau})=(1,1,-2),

for which

\Sigma q=0

and

\Sigma q^{3}=1+1-8=-6
.
In this case all gauge and gravitational anomalies cancel within the SM (augmented by
\nu^{c}_{i}
) and no exotic fermions are required, as anticipated by the corollary below Eq. (12).
By contrast, for a nonvanishing trace, e.g.

(q_{e},q_{\mu},q_{\tau})=(1,0,0),

one finds
\Sigma q=1
and the anomaly conditions fix the chiral differences
\Delta_{L,E}
and the sums
s_{L,E}
as in Eq. (11), together with the cubic constraint on
s_{L}
.
The corresponding Yukawa structure follows from the same charge bookkeeping: for each lepton family the admissible
\Phi
-Yukawa couplings are obtained by solving
Q^{\prime}(F_{L})+Q^{\prime}(F_{R})+Q^{\prime}(\Phi)=0
, so that mass terms for the exotic states are again directly tied to the
U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell}
breaking pattern.
This makes explicit how the general framework smoothly interpolates between universal and family-dependent leptophilic scenarios.

III
Scalar sector and symmetry breaking

Spontaneous breaking of
U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell}
generates the
Z_{\ell}
mass and induces a possible mixing between the SM Higgs and a new singlet scalar. We extend the scalar sector by adding an SM-singlet field
\Phi
of charge
q_{\Phi}
under
U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell}
. The renormalizable potential reads

V(H,\Phi)=-\,\mu_{H}^{2}|H|^{2}+\lambda_{H}|H|^{4}-\mu_{\Phi}^{2}|\Phi|^{2}+\lambda_{\Phi}|\Phi|^{4}+\lambda_{H\Phi}\,|H|^{2}|\Phi|^{2},

(6)

with vacuum expectation values (vevs)

\langle H\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
v\end{pmatrix},\qquad\langle\Phi\rangle=\frac{v_{\Phi}}{\sqrt{2}}.

The
U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell}
gauge boson and scalar mass spectrum follow from the covariant derivative and the
H
–
\Phi
mixing:

M_{Z_{\ell}}=g_{\ell}|q_{\Phi}|\,v_{\Phi},\qquad\tan 2\theta=\frac{2\lambda_{H\Phi}vv_{\Phi}}{2\lambda_{\Phi}v_{\Phi}^{2}-2\lambda_{H}v^{2}}.

(7)

The observed Higgs boson is

h=h_{\rm SM}\cos\theta+\phi\sin\theta,

so that the Higgs signal strengths scale as
\mu_{i}=\cos^{2}\theta
at leading order, while the singlet-like state
\phi
inherits suppressed SM couplings
\propto\sin\theta
. The angle
\theta
therefore controls both current Higgs-coupling constraints and the visibility of
\phi
at colliders. Stability of the potential follows the usual bounded-from-below conditions,
2
2

2

Namely
\lambda_{H},\lambda_{\Phi}>0
and
\lambda_{H\Phi}>-2\sqrt{\lambda_{H}\lambda_{\Phi}}
.
and explicit tadpole and mass expressions are collected in Appendix
B
.

Relevant interactions include the vertices
\phi Z_{\ell}Z_{\ell}
(from the scalar kinetic term)
and
\phi hh
(from the Higgs–portal coupling).
A possible gauge–kinetic mixing term of the form

\mathcal{L}\supset-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\,B_{\mu\nu}F^{\prime\mu\nu}

is allowed by symmetry and is treated as a small independent parameter.

Phenomenological regimes.

If
m_{\phi}>2M_{Z_{\ell}}
, the decay
\phi\to Z_{\ell}Z_{\ell}
dominates via the gauge interaction; for
m_{\phi}<2M_{Z_{\ell}}
, decays into SM final states proceed through the Higgs portal. It is therefore convenient to express predictions in the
(M_{Z_{\ell}},g_{\rm eff})
plane, where
g_{\rm eff}=g_{\ell}a_{\ell}
denotes the effective leptonic coupling of
Z_{\ell}
, and

v_{\Phi}=\frac{M_{Z_{\ell}}}{|q_{\Phi}|g_{\ell}}

fixes the singlet vev in terms of observables. Combined constraints from Higgs signal strengths (
\theta\lesssim 0.1
) and dilepton searches at colliders then restrict viable regions in this plane.

While a strong first-order
U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell}
transition may yield gravitational-wave signatures, such effects are model-dependent and will not be pursued here.

IV
Yukawa sector and lepton masses

The anomaly-cancelling fermions are vectorlike under the SM gauge group but chiral under
U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell}
, and hence their Dirac masses arise from Yukawa couplings to the scalar(s) responsible for
U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell}
breaking. For the minimal anomaly-free charge assignment (explicitly given in Appendix
A
),

q(L^{\prime})=q(L^{\prime c})=-\tfrac{3}{2}a_{\ell},\qquad q(E^{\prime})=q(E^{\prime c})=q(N^{\prime})=q(N^{\prime c})=+\tfrac{3}{2}a_{\ell},

which uniquely determines the allowed scalar charges entering the Yukawa operators.

IV.1
Minimal single-singlet implementation

With a single SM-singlet scalar
\Phi
of charge
q_{\Phi}=+3a_{\ell}
, the renormalizable Yukawa Lagrangian takes the form

\mathcal{L}_{Y}\supset y_{L}\,\Phi\,L^{\prime}L^{\prime c}+y_{E}\,\Phi^{\dagger}E^{\prime}E^{\prime c}+y_{N}\,\Phi^{\dagger}N^{\prime}N^{\prime c}+{\rm h.c.},

(8)

yielding the mass terms

m_{L^{\prime}}=\frac{y_{L}v_{\Phi}}{\sqrt{2}},\qquad m_{E^{\prime}}=\frac{y_{E}v_{\Phi}}{\sqrt{2}},\qquad m_{N^{\prime}}=\frac{y_{N}v_{\Phi}}{\sqrt{2}},

(9)

after
\langle\Phi\rangle=v_{\Phi}/\sqrt{2}
. Making use of
M_{Z_{\ell}}=g_{\ell}|q_{\Phi}|v_{\Phi}
and
g_{\rm eff}=g_{\ell}a_{\ell}
, the exotic masses can be compactly expressed as

\boxed{\;m_{L^{\prime},E^{\prime},N^{\prime}}=\frac{y_{L,E,N}}{3\sqrt{2}}\;\frac{M_{Z_{\ell}}}{|g_{\rm eff}|}\;},

(10)

which highlights their proportionality to the
U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell}
-breaking scale. This form is especially convenient when scanning over the
(M_{Z_{\ell}},g_{\rm eff})
parameter space in collider studies.

IV.2
Two-singlet generalization

If two scalars
\Phi_{1,2}
with charges
q_{1,2}
acquire vevs
v_{1,2}
, gauge invariance in the universal benchmark requires

q_{1}=-\bigl(q(L^{\prime})+q(L^{\prime c})\bigr)=+3a_{\ell},\qquad q_{2}=+\bigl(q(E^{\prime})+q(E^{\prime c})\bigr)=+3a_{\ell},

so that
q_{1}=q_{2}
in this specific realization.
In other words, both
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
are
allowed
to couple to
(L^{\prime},E^{\prime},N^{\prime})
at the renormalizable level.
In our minimal texture we choose a basis in which
L^{\prime}
masses arise dominantly from
\Phi_{1}
while
E^{\prime}
and
N^{\prime}
receive masses from
\Phi_{2}^{\dagger}
,

\mathcal{L}_{Y}\supset y_{L}\,\Phi_{1}\,L^{\prime}L^{\prime c}+y_{E}\,\Phi_{2}^{\dagger}E^{\prime}E^{\prime c}+y_{N}\,\Phi_{2}^{\dagger}N^{\prime}N^{\prime c}+{\rm h.c.},

(11)

so that

m_{L^{\prime}}=\frac{y_{L}v_{1}}{\sqrt{2}},\qquad m_{E^{\prime}}=\frac{y_{E}v_{2}}{\sqrt{2}},\qquad m_{N^{\prime}}=\frac{y_{N}v_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}.

(12)

Additional mixed couplings (e.g.
\Phi_{1}^{\dagger}E^{\prime}E^{\prime c}
or
\Phi_{2}L^{\prime}L^{\prime c}
) are technically natural to set to zero: they are absent at tree level and only regenerated radiatively at higher order, and including them does not affect anomaly cancellation.
We therefore retain the simpler texture above as our benchmark without loss of generality.

IV.3
Mixing structure and flavor protection

For multiple charged singlets
E^{\prime}_{i}
, the mass matrix takes the block form

\mathcal{L}_{m}=\begin{pmatrix}\overline{L^{\prime}_{L}}&\overline{E^{\prime}_{L,1}}&\overline{E^{\prime}_{L,2}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}m_{L^{\prime}}&0&0\\[4.0pt]
0&M_{11}&M_{12}\\[4.0pt]
0&M_{21}&M_{22}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}L^{\prime}_{R}\\
E^{\prime}_{R,1}\\
E^{\prime}_{R,2}\end{pmatrix}+{\rm h.c.},

(13)

where
M_{ij}=\tfrac{y^{E}_{ij}v_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}
. The absence of
L^{\prime}
–
E^{\prime}
mixing at the renormalizable level follows directly from the
U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell}
charge assignments, providing a built-in protection against charged-lepton flavor violation (CLFV). Operators such as
\overline{L}HE^{\prime}_{R}
or
\overline{L^{\prime}_{L}}He_{R}
are forbidden in the minimal setup, and any higher-dimensional corrections (e.g.
(\overline{L}HE^{\prime}_{R})(\Phi/\Lambda)^{n}
) are strongly constrained by lepton-universality tests
Wehle et al. 2021
;
Aaij et al. 2022
.

Theoretical necessity.

The additional vectorlike leptons introduced above are not an ad hoc embellishment but a
minimal and unavoidable
ingredient of the construction: they are required to ensure full gauge and gravitational anomaly
cancellation
in a leptophilic
U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell}
extension.
While such states have not been observed experimentally, their presence is a generic prediction of anomaly-free frameworks of this type and can be probed through multilepton and (potentially) long-lived signatures at colliders.
This theoretical necessity underpins the phenomenological study in Sec.
VIII
, where we map the
(M_{Z_{\ell}},g_{\rm eff})
plane to the exotic-lepton spectrum and search channels.

IV.4
Couplings and collider outlook

The exotic fermions couple to
Z_{\ell}
with strength
g_{\ell}q_{F^{\prime}}
(
F^{\prime}=L^{\prime},E^{\prime},N^{\prime}
), and the interactions are purely vectorlike when
q_{L^{\prime}}=q_{L^{\prime c}}
, etc. Current LHC bounds on vectorlike leptons lie in the
\mathcal{O}(10^{2}\text{--}10^{3})
GeV range, with ATLAS excluding masses between 130–900 GeV in third-generation doublet scenarios and CMS excluding up to about 790 GeV in multilepton final states
Aad et al. 2023a
;
Sirunyan et al. 2019
. In regions where mixing with SM leptons is suppressed, long-lived signatures may arise; otherwise, prompt decays yield
Z_{\ell}+\ell
or
Z_{\ell}+\not{E}_{T}
final states. The Yukawa sector thus provides a predictive link between
(M_{Z_{\ell}},g_{\rm eff})
, the exotic-lepton spectrum, and their collider phenomenology.

Together with the anomaly-free gauge and scalar sectors discussed above,
the Yukawa structure completes the renormalizable foundation of the model,
linking the symmetry-breaking scale to the observable lepton spectrum and collider signatures.

V
Kinetic mixing and precision constraints

Two Abelian gauge factors generically allow a renormalizable kinetic-mixing term,

\mathcal{L}\supset-\frac{1}{4}B_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}F^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}F^{\prime\mu\nu}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}B_{\mu\nu}F^{\prime\mu\nu},

(14)

\varepsilon

U(1)_{Y}
and
U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell}

\varepsilon(\mu)\simeq\frac{g_{Y}g_{\ell}}{16\pi^{2}}\,\mathrm{Tr}(YQ^{\prime})\,\ln\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{\mu^{2}}+\text{finite thresholds},

(15)

where
\mathrm{Tr}(YQ^{\prime})\equiv\sum_{f}Y_{f}Q^{\prime}_{f}
is over chiral fermions. Hence the leading logarithm vanishes if

\boxed{\;\mathrm{Tr}(YQ^{\prime})=0\;}

(16)

and
\varepsilon
remains threshold-suppressed and naturally small.

VI
Neutrino masses

A leptophilic
U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell}

In the left-handed Weyl basis (Appendix

\mathcal{L}\supset y_{\nu}\,L\,H\,\nu^{c}+\text{h.c.},\qquad Q^{\prime}(L)=+a_{\ell},\;Q^{\prime}(\nu^{c})=-a_{\ell},\;Q^{\prime}(H)=0,

Dark matter candidate

We consider two minimal realizations:

In the contact limit

VII.2
Invisible width and collider complementarity

If

Direct and indirect searches

Because quarks are neutral under

\varepsilon
(Sec.
V

Benchmark point and scalar portal.

To illustrate viability, consider a representative fermionic benchmark

IX
Conclusions

We have developed a fully consistent and anomaly-free extension of the Standard Model based on a universal leptophilic gauge symmetry
U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell}
. Motivated by neutrino oscillation data, all leptons carry a common charge under
U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell}
while quarks remain neutral, yielding a genuinely leptophilic gauge boson
Z_{\ell}
at tree level. Quantum consistency is restored by introducing a minimal set of vectorlike leptons and singlet scalars, with the explicit charge assignments and anomaly conditions summarized in Appendix
A
.

The scalar sector breaks
U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell}
, determines
M_{Z_{\ell}}
, and can mix with the Standard Model Higgs. It also enables a type-I seesaw via sterile states, linking the new symmetry to the origin of neutrino masses and potentially to baryogenesis through leptogenesis. The anomaly-cancelling leptons acquire masses via singlet Yukawa couplings and do not perturb the SM charged-lepton mass matrix at renormalizable level, thereby suppressing flavor violation. Gauge–kinetic mixing with hypercharge, while generically present, can be loop-suppressed by enforcing
\mathrm{Tr}(YQ^{\prime})=0
(Sec.
V
), preserving the leptophilic structure and satisfying precision constraints.

The same ingredients naturally accommodate a stable dark-matter candidate, either fermionic or scalar, whose relic abundance and signatures are governed by
Z_{\ell}
-mediated annihilation channels (Sec.
VII
). Collider searches, precision data, cosmology, and indirect probes thus provide complementary handles on a single parameter set
(M_{Z_{\ell}},g_{\rm eff})
, with invisible channels and kinetic mixing profiled where relevant.

In summary, gauging a universal lepton number yields a compact, internally consistent framework that simultaneously addresses anomaly cancellation, neutrino mass generation, dark matter, and collider signatures through a unifying symmetry principle. The model therefore stands as a minimal benchmark for upcoming high-luminosity and precision programs.

\sin\theta_{h\phi_{1}}\simeq\frac{\lambda_{H1}vv_{1}}{m_{\phi_{1}}^{2}-m_{h}^{2}},\qquad\sin\theta_{h\phi_{2}}\simeq\frac{\lambda_{H2}vv_{2}}{m_{\phi_{2}}^{2}-m_{h}^{2}},\qquad m_{\phi_{i}}^{2}\simeq 2\lambda_{i}^{\rm eff}v_{i}^{2}.

(B.10)

Hence the Higgs signal strength scales as

\mu_{h}\simeq 1-(\sin^{2}\theta_{h\phi_{1}}+\sin^{2}\theta_{h\phi_{2}})
.

CP–odd sector.

Writing

\Phi_{i}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(v_{i}+\phi_{i}+ia_{i})
,
the CP–odd fields
(a_{1},a_{2})
span one would–be Goldstone boson
G^{\prime}
and one physical pseudoscalar
A
.
In the
(a_{1},a_{2})
basis, the quadratic terms from
V_{\rm soft}
give

\mathcal{M}^{2}_{\rm odd}=\mu_{12}^{2}\begin{pmatrix}\displaystyle\frac{v_{2}}{v_{1}}&-1\\
-1&\displaystyle\frac{v_{1}}{v_{2}}\end{pmatrix}.

(B.11)

The eigenstates are

\displaystyle G^{\prime}

\displaystyle\propto v_{1}a_{1}+v_{2}a_{2},

(B.12)

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\propto-v_{2}a_{1}+v_{1}a_{2},

(B.13)

with

m_{A}^{2}=\mu_{12}^{2}\left(\frac{v_{1}}{v_{2}}+\frac{v_{2}}{v_{1}}\right),\qquad m_{G^{\prime}}^{2}=0.

(B.14)

In the limit
\mu_{12}^{2}\to 0
, the CP–odd scalar
A
becomes massless, signaling the restoration of a global
U(1)
in the singlet sector.
Conversely, a nonzero
\mu_{12}^{2}
both lifts
A
and ensures that only one Goldstone boson is eaten by
Z_{\ell}
.

Gauge-boson mixing:
A
–
Z
–
Z_{\ell}
diagonalization with kinetic mixing

We start from the kinetic and mass terms

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\supset\;

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}B_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}B^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}B^{\prime\mu\nu}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}B_{\mu\nu}B^{\prime\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}W^{a}_{\mu\nu}W^{a\,\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}M_{Z}^{2}(Z^{0}_{\mu})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}M_{Z_{\ell},0}^{2}(B^{\prime}_{\mu})^{2}\;+\;\text{(currents)}\,,

(C.1)

with the usual Weinberg rotation

Z^{0}_{\mu}\equiv c_{W}W^{3}_{\mu}-s_{W}B_{\mu}
,

A_{\mu}\equiv s_{W}W^{3}_{\mu}+c_{W}B_{\mu}
,

s_{W}\equiv g_{Y}/\sqrt{g^{2}+g_{Y}^{2}}
,
and
M_{Z}^{2}=\tfrac{1}{4}(g^{2}+g_{Y}^{2})v^{2}
.
Canonical normalization to
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
via

B_{\mu}\to B_{\mu}+\varepsilon B^{\prime}_{\mu}

removes the mixed kinetic term and leaves the photon strictly massless.
In the
(A_{\mu},Z^{0}_{\mu},B^{\prime}_{\mu})
basis the mass matrix becomes block-diagonal:

\mathcal{M}^{2}_{(A,Z^{0},B^{\prime})}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&M_{Z}^{2}&\delta\\
0&\delta&M_{Z_{\ell}}^{2}\end{pmatrix}+{\cal O}(\varepsilon^{2})\!,\qquad\delta\equiv\varepsilon\,s_{W}\,M_{Z}^{2},

(C.2)

where
M_{Z_{\ell}}^{2}=M_{Z_{\ell},0}^{2}+{\cal O}(\varepsilon^{2})
if the SM Higgs is uncharged under
U(1)^{\prime}_{\ell}

Diagonalization reduces to a rotation in the
(Z^{0},B^{\prime})

\begin{pmatrix}Z\\
Z_{\ell}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\xi&-\sin\xi\\
\sin\xi&\cos\xi\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}Z^{0}\\
B^{\prime}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\tan 2\xi=\frac{2\delta}{M_{Z_{\ell}}^{2}-M_{Z}^{2}}\,,

(C.3)

so that for small
\varepsilon
(and hence
\xi

\displaystyle M_{Z,\mathrm{phys}}^{2}

\displaystyle\simeq M_{Z}^{2}-\frac{\delta^{2}}{M_{Z_{\ell}}^{2}-M_{Z}^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle M_{Z_{\ell},\mathrm{phys}}^{2}

\displaystyle\simeq M_{Z_{\ell}}^{2}+\frac{\delta^{2}}{M_{Z_{\ell}}^{2}-M_{Z}^{2}}\,,

(C.4)

\displaystyle g_{Zf}^{\mathrm{phys}}

\displaystyle\simeq g_{Zf}+\xi\,g_{\ell}Q^{\prime}_{f}\;+\;\varepsilon\,g_{Y}Y_{f}\,s_{W}\,,

\displaystyle g_{Z_{\ell}f}^{\mathrm{phys}}

\displaystyle\simeq g_{\ell}Q^{\prime}_{f}+\varepsilon\,g_{Y}Y_{f}\;-\;\xi\,g_{Zf}\,.

(C.5)

Here
g_{Zf}
denotes the SM
Z
coupling before kinetic/mass mixing,

(

(hypercharge) of
f

The
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2})

In the limit

\xi\simeq\frac{\delta}{M_{Z_{\ell}}^{2}}\simeq\varepsilon\,s_{W}\,\frac{M_{Z}^{2}}{M_{Z_{\ell}}^{2}},\qquad g_{Z_{\ell}q}\simeq\varepsilon\,g_{Y}Y_{q},\qquad g_{Z_{\ell}\ell}\simeq g_{\ell}Q^{\prime}_{\ell}+\varepsilon\,g_{Y}Y_{\ell},

so Drell–Yan production is controlled by

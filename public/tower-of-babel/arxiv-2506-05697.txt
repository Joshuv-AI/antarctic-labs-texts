The Groebner basis and solution set of a polynomial system related to the Jacobian Conjecture Thanks: Christian Valqui was supported by PUCP-DGI-2023-PI0991
Christian Valqui, Valeria Ramírez

Abstract
. We compute the Groebner basis of a system of polynomial equations related to the Jacobian conjecture, and describe completely the solution set.

1  Introduction

Let
K
be a characteristic zero field and let
K[y]((x^{-1}))
be the algebra of Laurent series
in
x^{-1}
with coefficients in
K[y]
.
We will start from the following theorem, proved in
[
2
]
*Theorem 1.9.

Theorem 1.1
.

The Jacobian conjecture in dimension two is false if and only if
there exist

-

P,Q\in K[x,y]
and
C,F\in K[y]((x^{-1}))
,

-

n,m\in\mathds{N}
such that
n\nmid m
and
m\nmid n
,

-

\nu_{i}\in K
(
i=0,\dots,m+n-2
) with
\nu_{0}=1
,

such that

-

C
has the form

C=x+C_{-1}x^{-1}+C_{-2}x^{-2}+\cdots\qquad\text{with each $C_{-i}\in K[y]$,}

-

gr(C)=1
and
gr(F)=2-n
, where
gr
is the total degree,

-

F_{+}=x^{1-n}y
, where
F_{+}
is the term of maximal degree in
x
of
F
,

-

C^{n}=P
and
Q=\sum_{i=0}^{m+n-2}\nu_{i}C^{m-i}+F
.

Furthermore, under these conditions
(P,Q)
is a counterexample to the Jacobian conjecture.

In
[
2
]
, the authors consider the following slightly more general situation. Let
D
be a

K
-algebra (for example, in Theorem
1.1
we have
D=K[y]
),

n,m
positive integers such that
n\nmid m
and
n\nmid m
,
(\nu_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n+m-2}
a family of elements
in
K
with
\nu_{0}=1
and
F_{1-n}\in D
(in Theorem
1.1
we take
F_{1-n}=y
).
A Laurent series in
x^{-1}
of the form

C=x+C_{-1}x^{-1}+C_{-2}x^{-2}+\cdots\qquad\text{with $C_{-i}\in D$,}

is
a solution of the system

S(n,m,(\nu_{i}),F_{1-n})
, if there exist

P,Q\in D[x]
and
F\in D[[x^{-1}]]
, such that

\displaystyle F=F_{1-n}x^{1-n}+F_{-n}x^{-n}+F_{-1-n}x^{-1-n}+\cdots,\quad\text{with $F_{1-n},F_{-n},\dots$
in $D$}

\displaystyle P=C^{n}\qquad\text{and}\qquad Q=\sum_{i=0}^{m+n-2}\nu_{i}C^{m-i}+F.

For example, if
n=3
, then

\displaystyle P(\text{\bf{x}})=C^{3}=

\displaystyle\text{\bf{ x}}^{3}+3C_{-1}\text{\bf{ x}}+3C_{-2}+(3C_{-1}^{2}+3C_{-3})\text{\bf{ x}}^{-1}+(6C_{-1}C_{-2}+4C_{-4})\text{\bf{ x}}^{-2}

\displaystyle+(C_{-1}^{3}+3C_{-2}^{2}+6c_{-2}C_{-3}+3C_{-5})\text{\bf{ x}}^{-3}

\displaystyle+(3C_{-1}^{2}C_{-2}+6C_{-2}C_{-3}+6C_{-1}C_{-4}+6C_{-6})\text{\bf{ x}}^{-4}

\displaystyle+(3C_{-1}C_{-2}^{2}+3C_{-1}^{2}C_{-3}+3C_{-3}^{2}+6C_{-2}C_{-4}+6C_{-1}C_{-5}+6C_{-7})\text{\bf{ x}}^{-5}

\displaystyle+\dots

and the condition
C^{3}\in K[x]
translates into the following conditions on
C_{-k}
:

\displaystyle 0=(C^{3})_{-1}=

\displaystyle\ 3C_{-1}^{2}+3C_{-3},

\displaystyle 0=(C^{3})_{-2}=

\displaystyle\ 6C_{-1}C_{-2}+4C_{-4},

\displaystyle 0=(C^{3})_{-3}=

\displaystyle\ C_{-1}^{3}+3C_{-2}^{2}+6c_{-2}C_{-3}+3C_{-5},

\displaystyle 0=(C^{3})_{-4}=

\displaystyle\ 3C_{-1}^{2}C_{-2}+6C_{-2}C_{-3}+6C_{-1}C_{-4}+6C_{-6},

\displaystyle 0=(C^{3})_{-5}=

\displaystyle\ 3C_{-1}C_{-2}^{2}+3C_{-1}^{2}C_{-3}+3C_{-3}^{2}+6C_{-2}C_{-4}+6C_{-1}C_{-5}+6C_{-7},

In general, the condition
P(x)=C^{n}\in K[x]
yields equations
(C^{n})_{-k}=0
, whereas the condition

Q(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{m+n-2}\nu_{i}C^{m-i}+F\in K[x]
gives us the equations

\left(\sum_{i=0}^{m+n-2}\nu_{i}C^{m-i}+F\right)_{-k}=0
, where we note that
F_{-k}=0
for
k=1,\dots,n-2
.

It is easy to see (e.g.
[
2
]
*Remark 1.13) that the first
m+n-2
coefficients determine
the others, i.e.,
the coefficients
C_{-1},\dots,C_{-m-n+2}
determine univocally the coefficients
C_{-k}
for
k>m+n-2
.
Moreover, the
F_{-k}
for
k>n-1
depend only on
F_{1-n}
and
C
.
Consequently, having a solution
C
to the system
S(n,m,(\nu_{i}),F_{1-n})

is the same as having a solution
(C_{-1},\dots,C_{-m-n+2})
to the system

(1.1)

\displaystyle E_{k}:=(C^{n})_{-k}=0,

\displaystyle\text{for $k=1,\dots,m-1$,}

\displaystyle E_{m-1+k}:=\left(\sum_{i=0}^{m+n-2}\nu_{i}C^{m-i}\right)_{-k}=0,

\displaystyle\text{for $k=1,\dots,n-2$,}

\displaystyle E_{m+n-2}:=\left(\sum_{i=0}^{m+n-2}\nu_{i}C^{m-i}\right)_{1-n}+F_{1-n}=0,

with
m+n-2
equations
E_{k}
and
m+n-2
unknowns
C_{-k}
.

In order to understand the solution set of this system, it would be very helpful to find a Groebner basis
for the ideal generated by the polynomials
E_{k}
in
D[C_{-1},\dots,C_{m+n-2}]
.
In this paper we compute such a Groebner basis of (
1.1
) in a very particular case:
we assume

n=3
,
m=3r+1
or
m=3r+2
for some integer
r>0
, and
\nu_{i}=0

for
i>0
. Moreover we consider
D=\mathds{C}[y]
and
F_{1-n}=y
, as in Theorem
1.1
.

2  Computation of a Groebner basis for
I_{m-1}

Assume

n=3
,
3\nmid m>3
and
\nu_{i}=0

for
i>0
. Set also
D=\mathds{C}[y]
and
F_{1-n}=y
.

Then the system (
1.1
) reads

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}E_{i}&=&\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(C^{3})_{-i},&i=1,\ldots,m-1\\
(C^{m})_{-1},&i=m,\\
(C^{m})_{-2}+y,&i=m+1,\\
\end{array}\right.\\
\end{array}

where
(C^{2})_{-i}
denotes the coefficient of
x^{-i}
in the Laurent series
C^{3}
. Explicitly, the
polynomials
E_{i}
are given by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rl}E_{1}&=\ 3C_{-1}^{2}+3C_{-3},\\
E_{2}&=\ 6C_{-1}C_{-2}+3C_{-4},\\
E_{3}&=\ C_{-1}^{3}+3C_{-2}^{2}+6C_{-1}C_{-3}+3C_{-5},\\
E_{4}&=\ 3C_{-1}^{2}C_{-2}+6C_{-2}C_{-3}+6C_{-1}C_{-4}+3C_{-6},\\
E_{5}&=\ 3C_{-1}C_{-2}^{2}+3C_{-1}^{2}C_{-3}+3C_{-3}^{2}+6C_{-2}C_{-4}+6C_{-1}C_{-5}+3C_{-7},\\
&\vdots\\
E_{m-1}&=(C^{3})_{1-m},\\
E_{m}&=(C^{m})_{-1},\\
E_{m+1}&=(C^{m})_{-2}+y,\\
\end{array}

Each
E_{i}
is a polynomial in the ring
\mathbb{C}[C_{-1},C_{-2},\ldots,C_{m+1},y]
, and the
m+1
polynomials
yield the ideal

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle E_{1},\ldots,E_{m},E_{m+1}\rangle.

Our goal is to find a Groebner basis for the ideal
I
, but we find it nearly explicit only for

I_{m-1}:=\langle E_{1},E_{2},\ldots,E_{m-2},E_{m-1}\rangle
. For this we note that the equations are homogeneous, for the weight obtained by setting

w(C_{-i})=i+1,\quad\text{and}\quad w(y)=m+n-1=m+2.

We consider
y
as a variable, so the equations remain homogeneous.
Then

w(E_{k})=k+3,\text{ for $k=1,\dots,m-1$},\quad w(E_{m})=m+1\quad\text{and}\quad w(E_{m+1})=m+2.

Note that for
k=1\dots,m-1
we have

(2.16)

E_{k}:=3\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=-1\\
3i\neq k\end{subarray}}^{[[\frac{k+1}{2}]]}C_{-i}^{2}C_{-(k-2i)}\Big)+6\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<i<j\\
i+j=k+1\end{subarray}}C_{-i}C_{-j}\Big)+6\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<i<j<l\\
i+j+l=k\end{subarray}}C_{-i}C_{-j}C_{-l}\Big)+\varepsilon(C_{-\frac{k}{3}})^{3},

where
\varepsilon=\begin{cases}1,\quad 3|k\\
0,\quad 3\nmid k\end{cases}
.
Note that
C_{1}=1
and
C_{0}=0
, and so

(2.17)

3\sum_{i=-1}^{[[\frac{k+1}{2}]]}C_{-i}^{2}C_{-(k-2i)}=3C_{k+2}+3\sum_{i=1}^{[[\frac{k+1}{2}]]}C_{-i}^{2}C_{-(k-2i)}

In order to compute a Groebner basis we will consider the degree reverse lexicographic monomial order, but for the degree given by the above mentioned weight. This means that the monomial order is given by the matrix

\text{wmat}=\begin{pmatrix}m+2&m+1&m&\dots&4&3&2&m+2\\
0&0&0&\dots&0&0&0&-1\\
0&0&0&\dots&0&0&-1&0\\
0&0&0&\dots&0&-1&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&-1&\dots&0&0&0&0\\
0&-1&0&\dots&0&0&0&0\end{pmatrix},

on the variables
C_{-(m+1)},C_{-m},C_{-(m-1)},\dots,C_{-3},C_{-2},C_{-1},y
.
We first compute the reduced Groebner basis

(\widetilde{E}_{1},\widetilde{E}_{2},\ldots,\widetilde{E}_{m-1})
for the ideal
I_{m-1}:=\langle E_{1},E_{2},\ldots,E_{m-2},E_{m-1}\rangle
.

Proposition 2.1
.

The set
\{E_{1},\dots,E_{m-1}\}
is a Groebner basis of
I_{m-1}
.
The reduced Groebner basis of
I_{m-1}
is given by polynomials
\widetilde{E}_{k}
for

k=1,\dots,m-1
, each of the form

\widetilde{E}_{k}=C_{-(k+2)}+R_{k}(C_{-1},C_{-2}),

where
R_{k}(C_{-1},C_{-2})\in\mathbb{Q}[C_{-1},C_{-2}]
is an homogeneous polynomial in the variables
C_{-1}
and
C_{-2}
of weight
w(\widetilde{E}_{k})=w(E_{k})=k+3
.

Proof.

By (
2.16
) and (
2.17
) we know that
E_{k}
is of the form

E_{k}=3C_{-k-2}+T(C_{-1},\dots,C_{-k},\quad\text{for $k=1,\dots,m-1$},

where
T
is a polynomial in the variables
C_{-1},\dots,C_{-k}
.
Then by Proposition 2.9.4 of
[
1
]
, since

LCM(LT(E_{i})/3,LT(E_{j})/3)=LCM(C_{-i-2},C_{-j-2})=C_{-i-2}C_{-j-2}=(LT(E_{i})/3)(LT(E_{j})/3),

we have
S(E_{i},E_{j})\longrightarrow_{G}0
, and so, by Theorem 2.9.3 of
[
1
]
, the set
G=\{E_{1}/3,\dots,E_{m-1}/3\}
is a Groebner basis of
I_{m-1}
.
One verifies directly that it is a minimal Groebner basis, according to Definition 2.7.4 of
[
1
]
. If we apply the process described in the proof of
[
1
]
*Proposition 2.7.6 to the Groebner basis
G=\{E_{1}/3,\dots,E_{m-1}/3\}
we obtain that

\widetilde{E}_{1}=\overline{E_{1}/3}^{G\setminus\{\frac{E_{1}}{3}\}}=E_{1}/3\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{E}_{1}=\overline{E_{2}/3}^{G\setminus\{\frac{E_{2}}{3}\}}=E_{2}/3.

Moreover, for
k=3,\dots,m-1
, we define
G_{k}=\{\widetilde{E_{1}},\dots,\widetilde{E_{k-1}},E_{k},\dots,E_{m-1}\}
and then

\widetilde{E}_{k}=\overline{E_{k}}^{G_{k}\setminus E_{k}},

Clearly the remainder can have only the variables
C_{-1}
and
C_{-2}
, hence
\widetilde{E}_{k}
is of the form

\widetilde{E}_{k}=C_{-(k+2)}+R_{k}(C_{-1},C_{-2}),

as desired.

∎

Although we have no explicit formula for
R_{k}(C_{-1},C_{-2})
, we can compute it for small
k
.

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{1}

\displaystyle=C_{-3}+C_{-1}^{2},

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{2}

\displaystyle=C_{-4}+2C_{-1}C_{-2},

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{3}

\displaystyle=C_{-5}+C_{-2}^{2}-\frac{5}{3}C_{-1}^{3},

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{4}

\displaystyle=C_{-6}-5C_{-1}^{2}C_{-2},

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{5}

\displaystyle=C_{-7}+\frac{10}{3}C_{-1}^{4}-5C_{-1}C_{-2}^{2}.

Dividing the polynomials
E_{m}
and
E_{m+1}
by the polynomials
\{\widetilde{E}_{m-1},\dots,\widetilde{E}_{2},\widetilde{E}_{1}\}
with respect to the given order, we obtain

\overline{\frac{E_{m}}{3}}^{G_{m}\setminus\{\frac{E_{m}}{3}\}}=\widetilde{E}_{m}=R_{m}(C_{-1},C_{-2})\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{\frac{E_{m+1}}{3}}^{G_{m}\setminus\{\frac{E_{m+1}}{3}\}}=\widetilde{E}_{m+1}=y+R_{m+1}(C_{-1},C_{-2}),

where
R_{m}(C_{-1},C_{-2}),R_{m+1}(C_{-1},C_{-2})\in\mathbb{Q}[C_{-1},C_{-2}]
are homogeneous polynomials such that

w(\widetilde{E}_{m})=w(E_{m})=m+1
and
w(\widetilde{E}_{m+1})=w(E_{m+1})=m+2
.

Although we don’t give an explicit description of the Groebner Basis of the whole system, in the next section we show how to determine the solution set of the polynomial system, using that

I=\langle E_{1},E_{2},\dots,E_{m},E_{m+1}\rangle=\langle\widetilde{E}_{1},\widetilde{E}_{2},\ldots,\widetilde{E}_{m},\widetilde{E}_{m+1}\rangle.

3  The solution set of the system of polynomial equations

In this section we analyze the solutions of the system of equations. Note that the partial system
I_{m-1}
shows that the values of

C_{-1}
and
C_{-2}
determine univocally the values of
C_{-k}
for
k>2
. Moreover,
C_{-1}
and
C_{-2}
can be computed using the following two equations:

(3.18)

\widetilde{E}_{m}=R_{m}(C_{-1},C_{-2})=0

and

(3.19)

\widetilde{E}_{m+1}=y+R_{m+1}(C_{-1},C_{-2})=0,

where
R_{m}(C_{-1},C_{-2}),R_{m+1}(C_{-1},C_{-2})\in\mathbb{Q}[C_{-1},C_{-2}]
are homogeneous polynomials with respect to the weight considered
before, i.e.
w(C_{-1})=2
,
w(C_{-2})=3
. Moreover
w(\widetilde{E}_{m})=m+n-2=m+1
and
w(\widetilde{E}_{m+1})=m+n-1=m+2
. Then (
3.18
) and (
3.19
) read

(3.20)

\widetilde{E}_{m}=\sum_{2i+3j=m+1}\lambda_{m}^{ij}C_{-1}^{i}C_{-2}^{j}

and

(3.21)

\widetilde{E}_{m+1}=y+\sum_{2i+3j=m+2}\lambda_{m+1}^{ij}C_{-1}^{i}C_{-2}^{j},

for some constants
\lambda_{m}^{ij},\lambda_{m+1}^{ij}\in K
.
By (
3.21
) the two variables cannot be zero at the same time. We compute first the solutions in the cases where one of the
variables is zero.

FIRST CASE
:
C_{-1}=0
and
C_{-2}\neq 0
.

In this case the only term surviving in (
3.20
)
is

0=\widetilde{E}_{m}=\lambda_{m}^{0j}C_{-2}^{j},

with
3j=m+1
. So necessarily

(3.22)

\lambda_{m}^{0,(m+1)/3}=0\quad\text{if}\quad 3|m+1.

Similarly, the only term surviving in the sum (
3.21
) has
i=0
,
and so we obtain

0=\widetilde{E}_{m+1}=y+\lambda_{m+1}^{0j}C_{-2}^{j}\quad\text{with $3j=m+2$.}

Since
y\neq 0
, necessarily
\lambda_{m+1}^{0j}\neq 0
for
3j=m+2
, and so
3|m+2
, i.e.
m\equiv 1\mod 3
. This shows that
the condition (
3.24
) is trivially satisfied.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
3|m+2
, and
C_{-1}=0
, then
\lambda_{m+1}^{0j}\neq 0
for
3j=m+2
.

Proof.

It is easy to check that
P=x^{3}+3C_{-2}
, and then, by Newtons binomial theorem we have

(3.23)

C^{m}=P^{m/3}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{m/3}{k}(3C_{-2})^{k}(x^{3})^{\frac{m}{3}-k}.

Thus
\lambda_{m+1}^{0j}C_{-2}^{j}=(C^{m})_{-2}
is the coefficient of
x^{-2}=(x^{3})^{\frac{m}{3}-j}
, since
m=3j-2
.
Then

\lambda_{m+1}^{0j}=\binom{m/3}{j}3^{j}\neq 0,

as desired.
∎

Thus we have proved the following proposition.

Proposition 3.2
.

If
(C_{-1},C_{-2},\dots,C_{-(m+1)})
is a solution of the system (
2
), with
C_{-1}=0
and
C_{-2}\neq 0
, then

•

m\equiv 1\mod 3
,

•

\lambda_{m+1}^{0j}\neq 0
for
j:=\frac{m+2}{3}
,

•

There are
j
solutions of the system (
2
) in
K[y^{1/j}]
, given by

C_{-1}=0,\quad C_{-2}=\left(\frac{-y}{\lambda^{0j}_{m+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{j}}\quad\text{and}\quad C_{-k}=-R_{k-2}(C_{-1},C_{-2})\quad\text{for}\quad 3\leq k\leq m+1.

SECOND CASE
:
C_{-1}\neq 0
and
C_{-2}=0
.

In this case the only term surviving in (
3.20
)
is

0=\widetilde{E}_{m}=\lambda_{m}^{i0}C_{-1}^{i},

with
2i=m+1
. So necessarily

(3.24)

\lambda_{m}^{(m+1)/2,0}=0\quad\text{if}\quad 2|m+1.

Similarly, the only term surviving in the sum (
3.21
) has
j=0
,
and so we obtain

0=\widetilde{E}_{m+1}=y+\lambda_{m+1}^{i0}C_{-1}^{i}\quad\text{with $2i=m+2$.}

Since
y\neq 0
, necessarily
\lambda_{m+1}^{i0}\neq 0
for
2i=m+2
, and so
2|m+2
, i.e.
m
is even. This shows that
the condition (
3.24
) is trivially satisfied.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
2|m
and
C_{-2}=0
, then
\lambda_{m+1}^{i0}\neq 0
for
2i=m+2
.

Proof.

It is easy to check that
P=x^{3}+3xC_{-1}
, and then, by Newtons binomial theorem we have

(3.25)

C^{m}=P^{m/3}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{m/3}{k}(3xC_{-1})^{k}(x^{3})^{\frac{m}{3}-k}.

Thus
\lambda_{m+1}^{i0}C_{-1}^{i}=(C^{m})_{-2}
is the coefficient of
x^{-2}=(x)^{i}(x^{3})^{\frac{m}{3}-i}
, since
m=2i-2
.
Then

\lambda_{m+1}^{i0}=\binom{m/3}{i}3^{i}\neq 0,

as desired.
∎

Thus we have proved the following proposition.

Proposition 3.4
.

If
(C_{-1},C_{-2},\dots,C_{-(m+1)})
is a solution of the system (
2
), with
C_{-1}\neq 0
and
C_{-2}=0
, then

•

m\equiv 1\mod 3
,

•

\lambda_{m+1}^{i0}\neq 0
for
i:=\frac{m+2}{2}
,

•

There are
i
solutions of the system (
2
) in
K[y^{1/i}]
, given by

C_{-1}=\left(\frac{-y}{\lambda^{i0}_{m+1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{i}},\quad C_{-2}=0\quad\text{and}\quad C_{-k}=-R_{k-2}(C_{-1},C_{-2})\quad\text{for}\quad 3\leq k\leq m+1.

THIRD CASE
:
C_{-1}\neq 0
,
C_{-2}\neq 0
and
m
even.

In this case we introduce a new auxiliary variable
t
satisfying
C_{-2}^{2}=tC_{-1}^{3}
.
The equality (
3.20
) now reads

\widetilde{E}_{m}=\sum_{2i+3j=m+1}\lambda_{m}^{ij}C_{-1}^{i}C_{-2}^{j}=\sum_{2i+6r+3=m+1}\lambda_{m}^{i,2r+1}C_{-1}^{i}C_{-2}^{2r+1}=\sum_{2i+6r+2=m}\lambda_{m}^{i,2r+1}C_{-1}^{i+3r}C_{-2}t^{r},

since
m
even implies that the weight
2i+3j=m+1
is odd, so
j
is odd and can be written as
2r+1
. Moreover, for the terms in the sum
we have

i+3r=\frac{m-2}{2}
, and so we arrive at

0=C_{-1}^{\frac{m-2}{2}}C_{-2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2i+6r=m-2\\
j=2r+1\end{subarray}}\lambda^{ij}_{m}t^{r}.

Thus
t
is a root of the polynomial

(3.26)

f(t)=\sum_{r=0}^{\lfloor\frac{m-2}{6}\rfloor}a_{r}t^{r},\quad\text{where $a_{r}=\lambda_{m}^{\frac{m-2-6r}{2},2r+1}$.}

Let
\{t_{1},\dots,t_{s}\}
be the roots of the polynomial
f(t)
.
Note that in the equality (
3.21
) the power
j
has to be even, since
m
is even and
2i+3j=m+2
. Hence,
if we replace
C_{-2}^{2}
by
t_{l}C_{-1}^{3}
in (
3.21
), we obtain

\widetilde{E}_{m+1}=y+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2i+3j=m+2\\
j=2r\end{subarray}}\lambda_{m+1}^{ij}C_{-1}^{i}C_{-2}^{j}=y+\sum_{2i+6r=m+2}\lambda_{m+1}^{i,2r}C_{-1}^{i+3r}t_{l}^{r}.

Note that for each of the terms in the last sum we have
i+3r=\frac{m+2}{2}
, and so

0=y+C_{-1}^{\frac{m+2}{2}}g(t_{l}),\quad\text{where $g(t)=\sum_{r=0}^{\lfloor\frac{m+2}{6}\rfloor}b_{r}t^{r}$,}

with
b_{r}=\lambda_{m+1}^{\frac{m+2-6r}{2},2r}
. It follows that

C_{-1}=\Big(\frac{-y}{g(t_{l})}\Big)^{\frac{2}{m+2}}.

Thus we have arrived at the following result.

Proposition 3.5
.

If
(C_{-1},C_{-2},\dots,C_{-(m+1)})
is a solution of the system (
2
), with
C_{-1}\neq 0
,
C_{-2}\neq 0

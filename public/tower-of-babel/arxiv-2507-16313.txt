Hyperbolicity and Schwarz Lemmas in Calibrated Geometry
Kyle Broder, Anton Iliashenko, Jesse Madnick

Abstract
This paper has two main objectives. First, for an arbitrary calibrated manifold ( X , ϕ ) (X,\phi) , we define notions of R ϕ R_{\phi} -hyperbolicity and ϕ \phi -hyperbolicity, which respectively generalize the notions of Kobayashi and Brody hyperbolicity from complex geometry. To make sense of the former, we introduce the “KR ϕ \phi -metric,” a decreasing Finsler pseudo-metric that specializes to the Kobayashi-Royden pseudo-metric in the Kähler case. We prove that R ϕ R_{\phi} -hyperbolicity implies ϕ \phi -hyperbolicity, and give examples showing that the converse fails in general. Moreover, for constant-coefficient, inner Möbius rigid calibrations ϕ \phi in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , we completely characterize those domains that are ϕ \phi -hyperbolic. Second, we derive a Schwarz lemma for Smith immersions (a.k.a. conformal ϕ \phi -curves) into an arbitrary calibrated manifold ( X , ϕ ) (X,\phi) , thereby extending the Schwarz lemma for holomorphic curves into Kähler manifolds. The relevant Bochner formula features the “ ϕ \phi -sectional curvature,” a new notion that includes both the scalar and holomorphic sectional curvatures as special cases. As an application, we prove that calibrated geometries with ϕ \phi -sectional curvature bounded above by a negative constant are R ϕ R_{\phi} -hyperbolic, generalizing the corresponding result from complex geometry. As another application, we calculate the KR ϕ \phi -metric of real, complex, and quaternionic hyperbolic spaces equipped with their natural calibrations.

Introduction

For a Kähler manifold
(X,g_{X},\omega)
, hyperbolicity refers to the interplay of three
a priori
unrelated concepts: the scarcity of holomorphic maps
\mathbb{C}\to X
, the non-degeneracy of invariant pseudo-distances, and strongly negative curvature. More precisely, we say that:

1.

X
is
Brody hyperbolic
if every holomorphic map
\mathbb{C}\to X
is constant.

2.

X
is
Kobayashi hyperbolic
if the Kobayashi pseudo-distance is non-degenerate (see (
1.2
)).

3.

X
has
strongly negative holomorphic sectional curvature
if its holomorphic sectional curvature is bounded above by a negative constant.

The implications (3)
\implies
(2)
\implies
(1) are well-known. When
X
is compact, Brody
[
4
]
proved that (1) and (2) are equivalent.

The primary aim of this paper is to extend all three notions to arbitrary calibrated manifolds
(X,g_{X},\phi)
, and to generalize the implications (3)
\implies
(2)
\implies
(1). By a “calibrated manifold,” we mean an oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold
(X,g_{X})
equipped with a comass one, closed
k
-form
\phi\in\Omega^{k}(X)
called a calibration.

Extending (1) and (2) requires a suitable class of maps between calibrated manifolds. Such a class is provided by Smith immersions. A
Smith immersion
(or
conformal
\phi
-curve
) is a smooth map
f\colon(\Sigma^{k},g_{\Sigma},\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma})\to(X^{n},g_{X},\phi)
satisfying

\displaystyle f^{*}g_{X}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}g_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle f^{*}\phi

\displaystyle=\lambda^{k}\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma}

for some function
\lambda\colon\Sigma\to[0,\infty)
called the
conformal factor
. The first equation shows that Smith immersions are weakly conformal maps. Consequently, if
f
is a Smith immersion, then at every
x\in\Sigma
, we have either
\mathrm{rank}(df_{x})=k
or
\mathrm{rank}(df_{x})=0
. (Thus, in spite of the terminology, Smith immersions need not be immersions. A Smith immersion is called
strict
if it is an immersion.) When
(X,g_{X},\omega)
is a Kähler manifold, the Smith immersions
f\colon(\Sigma^{2},g_{\Sigma},\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma})\to(X,g_{X},\omega)
are precisely the holomorphic curves into
X
.

Smith immersions can be viewed as the “conformal mapping analog” of calibrated submanifolds. Indeed, the image of a strict Smith immersion is an immersed
\phi
-calibrated submanifold of
X
. Conversely, every immersed
\phi
-calibrated submanifold can be parametrized by a Smith immersion. More interestingly, just as calibrated submanifolds are homologically volume minimizing, it turns out that Smith immersions are homologically
k
-energy minimizing. We review the basics of Smith immersions in
\lx@sectionsign
2.2
.

We say that
(X,g_{X},\phi)
is
\phi
-hyperbolic
if every Smith immersion
f\colon(\mathbb{R}^{k},g_{0},\mathrm{vol}_{0})\to(X,g_{X},\phi)
is constant, where
g_{0}
is the flat metric. When
\phi=\omega
is a Kähler form,
\omega
-hyperbolicity is precisely Brody hyperbolicity. When
\phi=\mathrm{vol}_{X}
is a volume form,
\mathrm{vol}_{X}
-hyperbolicity is related to the failure of 1-quasiregular ellipticity
[
3
]
. For generic constant-coefficient calibrations on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we classify the
\phi
-hyperbolic domains:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\phi\in\Lambda^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{*}
be a constant-coefficient, inner Möbius rigid calibration,
k\geq 3
. Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a domain, and equip
U
with the flat metric
g_{0}
. Then
(U,g_{0})
is
\phi
-hyperbolic if and only if
U
contains no affine
\phi
-planes.

A result of
[
19
]
implies that the generic constant-coefficient calibration on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is inner Möbius rigid. Moreover, the normalized powers of the standard Kähler form
\frac{1}{p!}\omega^{p}\in\Lambda^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})^{*}
for
p\geq 2
, as well as every codimension 2 calibration, is inner Möbius rigid
[
19
]
.

To extend (3), we need to generalize the notion of holomorphic sectional curvature. To this end, when
(X,g_{X},\phi)
is a calibrated manifold, we say that the
\phi
-sectional curvature
of a
\phi
-plane
\xi\subset TX
is the sum of sectional curvatures of
2
-planes in
\xi
:

\mathrm{Sec}^{\phi}(\xi):=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq k}\mathrm{Sec}(e_{i}\wedge e_{j}),

where
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}\}
is a
g_{X}
-orthonormal basis of
\xi
. When
\phi=\omega
or
\phi=\mathrm{vol}_{X}
, the
\phi
-sectional curvature is simply the holomorphic sectional or scalar curvature, respectively. With this notion in hand, we generalize the implication (3)
\implies
(1):

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(X,g_{X},\phi)
be a calibrated manifold. If
\mathrm{Sec}^{\phi}\leq-A
for some
A>0
, then
(X,g_{X})
is
\phi
-hyperbolic.

To prove Theorem
1.2
, we will establish a Schwarz lemma for Smith immersions. Schwarz lemmas have long been a powerful tool in complex geometry. Recall that the classic statement asserts that every holomorphic function
f\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\colon|z|<1\}
with
f(0)=0
satisfies
|f(z)|\leq|z|
. Pick reinterpreted this as saying that every holomorphic map
f\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
is distance non-increasing (with respect to the Poincaré distance), and Ahlfors generalized this distance non-increasing property to holomorphic maps
f\colon\mathbb{D}\to\Sigma
, where
\Sigma
is a Riemann surface with Gauss curvature
K_{\Sigma}\leq-4
. Ahlfors’ Schwarz Lemma is a special case of the following result:

Theorem 1.3

(Schwarz Lemma)
.

Let
f\colon(\Sigma^{k},g_{\Sigma},\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma})\to(X^{n},g_{X},\phi)
be a smooth map, where
\phi\in\Omega^{k}(X)
is a calibration. Suppose that
\Sigma
is complete and has Ricci curvature bounded below.

1.

Suppose
f
is weakly conformal and
k
-harmonic. If
\mathrm{Scal}_{\Sigma}\geq-S
for
S\geq 0
, and if
\mathrm{Sec}_{X}\leq-B
for
B>0
, then
|df|^{2}\leq\frac{S}{(k-1)B}
. Consequently,
\mathrm{dist}_{X}(f(p),f(q))\leq\sqrt{\frac{S}{k(k-1)B}}\,\mathrm{dist}_{\Sigma}(p,q)
for all
p,q\in\Sigma
.

2.

Suppose
f
is a Smith immersion. If
\mathrm{Scal}_{\Sigma}\geq-S
for
S\geq 0
, and if
\mathrm{Sec}^{\phi}_{X}\leq-A
for
A>0
, then
|df|^{2}\leq\frac{kS}{A}
. Consequently,
\mathrm{dist}_{X}(f(p),f(q))\leq\sqrt{\frac{S}{A}}\,\mathrm{dist}_{\Sigma}(p,q)
for all
p,q\in\Sigma
.

Finally, we indicate how to generalize (2). On a complex manifold
X
, the
Kobayashi-Royden pseudo-metric
is the Finsler pseudo-metric
F_{X}\colon TX\to[0,\infty]
defined by

F_{X}(v_{p})=\inf\left\{\frac{1}{r}\colon\exists\text{ holomorphic }f\colon\mathbb{D}(r)\to X\text{ s.t. }f(0)=p,f^{\prime}(0)=v_{p}\right\}\!,

(1.1)

where
\mathbb{D}(r)=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\colon|z|<r\}
. Intuitively,
F_{X}(v)
is the reciprocal of the radius of the largest holomorphic disk in
X
that contains
v
as a tangent vector. Notice that a lower bound on
F_{X}
is essentially equivalent to a Schwarz lemma for holomorphic maps
\mathbb{D}\to X
. A remarkable feature of
F_{X}
is its “decreasing property”: for every holomorphic map
f\colon X\to Y
, we have
f^{*}F_{Y}\leq F_{X}
. This implies in particular that
F_{X}
is invariant under biholomorphisms. Regarding regularity, Royden proved
[
31
]
that
F_{X}
is upper semicontinuous, but
F_{X}
need not be continuous in general
[
24
, Example 3.5.37]
.

Integrating the Kobayashi-Royden pseudo-metric results in the
Kobayashi pseudo-distance

d_{X}^{\mathrm{Kob}}(p,q)=\inf_{\gamma}\int_{0}^{1}F_{X}(\gamma^{\prime}(t))\,dt,

(1.2)

where the infimum is taken over all piecewise-smooth curves
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to X
from
p
to
q
. Royden proved that
d_{X}^{\mathrm{Kob}}
is a genuine distance function (i.e.,
p\neq q
implies
d_{X}^{\mathrm{Kob}}(p,q)>0
) if and only if every
p\in X
has a neighborhood
U\subset X
and a constant
c>0
such that
F_{X}(v)\geq c|v|
for all
v\in TU
. Such complex manifolds
X
are said to be
Kobayashi hyperbolic
. It is well-known that Kobayashi hyperbolicity implies Brody hyperbolicity.

In general, for an arbitrary calibrated manifold
(X,g_{X},\phi)
, we define its
KR
\phi
-metric
to be the function
\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}\colon TX\to[0,\infty]
given by

\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}(v_{p})=\inf\!\left\{a>0\colon\exists f\in\mathrm{SmIm}(B^{k},X),w\in T_{0}B^{k},|w|=1\text{ s.t. }f(0)=p,\,df_{0}(w)=\frac{1}{a}v_{p}\right\}\!,

where
\mathrm{SmIm}(B^{k},X)
denotes the collection of Smith immersions
f\colon(B^{k},g_{1},\mathrm{vol}_{1})\to(X,g_{X},\phi)
, with
g_{1}
denoting the Poincaré metric and
\mathrm{vol}_{1}
its volume form. When
\phi=\omega
is a Kähler calibration,
\mathcal{K}_{(X,\omega)}
reduces to the Kobayashi-Royden pseudo-metric. We prove in
\lx@sectionsign
5.1
that
\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}
satisfies a generalization of the decreasing property, and is therefore invariant under Smith equivalences (see
\lx@sectionsign
2.2
for the definition). In
\lx@sectionsign
5.2
, we calculate the KR
\phi
-metric of real, complex, and quaternionic hyperbolic spaces for suitable calibrations
\phi
.

We shall say that
(X,g_{X})
is
R_{\phi}
-hyperbolic
if Royden’s criterion holds: every point in
X
has a neighborhood
U\subset X
and a constant
c>0
such that
\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}(v)\geq c|v|_{X}
for all
v\in TU
. Under the assumption that
\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}
is upper semicontinuous, we say that
(X,g_{X})
is
K_{\phi}
-hyperbolic
if the pseudo-distance
d_{(X,\phi)}(p,q):=\inf_{\gamma}\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}(\gamma^{\prime}(t))\,dt
is non-degenerate. The desired generalization of (3)
\implies
(2)
\implies
(1) is as follows:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(X,g_{X},\phi)
be a calibrated manifold. Then

\mathrm{Sec}^{\phi}\leq-A\text{ for some }A>0\ \implies\ X\text{ is }R_{\phi}\text{-hyperbolic}\ \implies\ X\text{ is }\phi\text{-hyperbolic.}

Moreover, if
\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}
is upper semicontinuous, then

X\text{ is }R_{\phi}\text{-hyperbolic}\ \implies\ X\text{ is }K_{\phi}\text{-hyperbolic}\ \implies X\text{ is }\phi\text{-hyperbolic.}

We also show that the converse of the implication
R_{\phi}
-hyperbolic
\implies

\phi
-hyperbolic is false in general.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\phi\in\Lambda^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{*}
be a constant-coefficient calibration,
k\geq 2
. Then there exists a domain
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
that is
\phi
-hyperbolic but not
R_{\phi}
-hyperbolic. Moreover, if
\mathcal{K}_{(U,\phi)}
is upper semicontinuous, then
U
is not
K_{\phi}
-hyperbolic.

1.1
Notation and conventions

We adopt the following notation and conventions:

•

By “manifold,” we always mean a connected smooth manifold, unless indicated otherwise.

•

We let
B^{n}(R)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon|x|<R\}
denote the open
n
-ball of radius
R>0
, and write
B^{n}:=B^{n}(1)
. On
B^{n}(R)
, we let
g_{R}
denote the Poincaré metric, a complete metric of constant curvature
-\frac{4}{R^{2}}
, which we briefly recall in
\lx@sectionsign
2.4
. We let
|\cdot|_{R}=\sqrt{g_{R}(\cdot,\cdot)}
denote the corresponding norm, and let
\mathrm{vol}_{R}\in\Omega^{n}(B^{n})
denote the volume form.

•

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set. On
U
, we let
g_{0}
denote the flat metric, let
|\cdot|_{0}=\sqrt{g_{0}(\cdot,\cdot)}
denote the euclidean norm, and let
\mathrm{vol}_{0}=dx^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{n}\in\Omega^{n}(U)
denote the volume form.

•

Let
(X,g_{X})
be a Riemannian
n
-manifold. We let
\mathrm{dist}_{X}\colon X\times X\to\mathbb{R}
denote the corresponding distance function. When
X
is oriented, we let
\mathrm{vol}_{X}\in\Omega^{n}(X)
denote the volume form.

•

For a linear operator
A\colon V\to W
between finite-dimensional inner product spaces, we denote its Hilbert-Schmidt and operator norm by, respectively

\displaystyle|A|

\displaystyle=|A|_{\mathrm{HS}}=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}|Ae_{i}|^{2}},

\displaystyle\|A\|

\displaystyle=\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\sup_{|v|=1}\frac{|Av|}{|v|},

where
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
V
.

•

Given a Riemannian manifold
(X,g)
, our sign convention for its Riemann curvature tensor is
R(U,V,Z,W)=g(\nabla_{U}\nabla_{V}Z-\nabla_{V}\nabla_{U}Z-\nabla_{[U,V]}Z,W)
. In a local orthonormal frame
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
, we write
R_{ijk\ell}:=R(e_{i},e_{j},e_{k},e_{\ell})
. The sectional curvature on a unit
2
-vector is denoted
\mathrm{Sec}_{X}(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})=R_{ijji}
(no sum). In the usual way, the Ricci and scalar curvatures of
(X,g)
in a local frame are denoted
R_{ij}:=R_{kijk}
and
\mathrm{Scal}_{X}:=R_{ii}=R_{kiik}
, where here we use the Einstein summation convention.

•

For a smooth map between Riemannian manifolds
f:(X,g_{X})\to(Y,g_{Y})
, we denote its
k
-tension
, a section of
f^{*}TY
, by

\displaystyle\tau_{k}(f):=\mathrm{div}(|df|^{k-2}df)=\mathrm{tr}_{g}(\nabla(|df|^{k-2}df)),

where
\nabla
is the induced connection on
T^{*}X\otimes f^{*}TY
. Note that although
f
is smooth,
\tau_{k}(f)
need not be smooth for
k
odd. We say that
f
is
k
-harmonic
if
\tau_{k}(f)=0
. The operator
\tau_{k}
is (overdetermined) elliptic, and is non-degenerate for
k=2
, but is degenerate at critical points of
f
for
k>2
. When
Y=\mathbb{R}
, we refer to
\Delta_{k}f:=\tau_{k}(f)
as the
k
-Laplacian
, and say that
f
is
k
-subharmonic
if
\Delta_{k}f\geq 0
.

We begin by setting foundations. Section

rapidly recalls the basics of calibrated geometry. Section
2.2
concerns

(also known as

\phi
-curves
), which provide a conformal mapping approach to the study of calibrated submanifolds. Indeed, where calibrated submanifolds are minimal (and compact ones are homologically volume minimizing), we will recall that Smith immersions are

2.2
Conformally calibrating maps

We now define a natural notion of “map” between two calibrated manifolds having calibrations of the same degree. For this, it will be convenient to first discuss the wider class of weakly conformal maps.

A map
f\colon(X^{m},g_{X})\to(Y^{n},g_{Y})
between Riemannian manifolds is
weakly conformal
if there exists a non-negative function
\lambda\colon X\to[0,\infty)
such that
f^{*}g_{Y}=\lambda^{2}g_{X}
. Note that if
f
is weakly conformal, then for each
x\in X
, the conformal factor
\lambda(x)
is given by

\lambda(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}|df_{x}|=\|df_{x}\|.

(2.1)

We say
f
is
conformal
if
\lambda>0
, and an
isometry
if
\lambda=1
.

If
f
is weakly conformal, then for each point
x\in X
, we have either
\mathrm{rank}(df_{x})=m
(if
\lambda(x)>0
) or
\mathrm{rank}(df_{x})=0
(if
\lambda(x)=0
). In particular, if
m>n
, then every weakly conformal map is locally constant. To avoid this trivial case, we shall always assume that
m\leq n
. Note also that conformal maps are necessarily immersions.

Definition 2.6

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
(X^{m},g_{X},\alpha)
and
(Y^{n},g_{Y},\beta)
be calibrated manifolds such that
k:=\deg(\alpha)=\deg(\beta)
and
m\leq n
. A smooth map
f\colon X\to Y
is
conformally calibrating
if there exists a non-negative function
\lambda\colon X\to[0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle f^{*}g_{Y}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}g_{X}

\displaystyle f^{*}\beta

\displaystyle=\lambda^{k}\alpha.

Note that constant maps are conformally calibrating.

Example 2.7
.

Let
f\colon(X^{2m},g_{X},\omega_{X})\to(Y^{2n},g_{Y},\omega_{Y})
be a map between two Kähler manifolds. One can prove that such an
f\colon X\to Y
is conformally calibrating if and only if
f
is weakly conformal and holomorphic. For
m\geq 2
, this is equivalent to requiring that
f
be either a constant map, or else a conformal holomorphic immersion with
\lambda=\text{constant}
.

We shall provide more examples of conformally calibrating maps later in this section. For now, we observe the following basic fact.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
F\colon(X^{m},g_{X},\alpha)\to(Y^{n},g_{Y},\beta)
and
G\colon(Y^{n},g_{Y},\beta)\to(Z^{p},g_{Z},\gamma)
be smooth maps. If
F
and
G
are conformally calibrating, then so is
G\circ F
.

Proof.

Since
F
is conformally calibrating, there exists
\lambda\colon X\to[0,\infty)
such that
F^{*}g_{Y}=\lambda^{2}g_{X}
and
F^{*}\beta=\lambda^{k}\alpha
. Since
G
is conformally calibrating, there exists
\mu\colon Y\to[0,\infty)
such that
G^{*}g_{Z}=\mu^{2}g_{Y}
and
G^{*}\gamma=\mu^{k}\beta
. Setting
\nu:=\lambda\cdot(\mu\circ F)
, we have

\displaystyle(G\circ F)^{*}g_{Z}

\displaystyle=F^{*}(G^{*}g_{Z})=F^{*}(\mu^{2}g_{Y})=F^{*}(\mu^{2})\,\lambda^{2}g_{X}=\nu^{2}g_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle(G\circ F)^{*}\gamma

\displaystyle=F^{*}(G^{*}\gamma)=F^{*}(\mu^{k}\beta)=F^{*}(\mu^{k})\,\lambda^{k}\alpha=\nu^{k}\alpha,

so
G\circ F
is conformally calibrating.
∎

2.2.1
Smith equivalences and automorphisms

Using conformally calibrating maps, we may define the following notion of “isomorphism” between calibrated manifolds.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
(X^{n},g_{X},\alpha)
and
(Y^{n},g_{Y},\beta)
be calibrated
n
-manifolds such that
k=\deg(\alpha)=\deg(\beta)
. A
(local) Smith equivalence
is a conformally calibrating map
F\colon(X^{n},g_{X},\alpha)\to(Y^{n},g_{Y},\beta)
that is a (resp. local) diffeomorphism.

Example 2.10
.

1.

Let
(\Sigma^{k},g_{\Sigma},\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma})
and
(\widetilde{\Sigma}^{k},g_{\widetilde{\Sigma}},\mathrm{vol}_{\widetilde{\Sigma}})
be calibrated
k
-manifolds equipped with degree
k
calibrations (i.e., both calibrations are volume forms). Then

\displaystyle F\colon(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma},\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma})\to(\widetilde{\Sigma},g_{\widetilde{\Sigma}},\mathrm{vol}_{\widetilde{\Sigma}})\text{ is a Smith equivalence}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \iff\ F\text{ is an orientation-preserving conformal diffeomorphism.}

2.

Let
(X^{2m},g_{X},\omega_{X})
and
(Y^{2m},g_{Y},\omega_{Y})
be Kähler
2m
-manifolds. Then

\displaystyle F\colon(X^{2m},g_{X},\omega_{X})\to(Y^{2m},g_{Y},\omega_{Y})\text{ is a Smith equivalence}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \iff\ F\text{ is a conformal biholomorphism.}

Note that when
m=1
, biholomorphisms are automatically conformal.

Example 2.11
.

The following two basic examples will be used repeatedly. Let
(X,g_{X})
be an oriented Riemannian manifold.

1.

The identity map
\mathrm{Id}\colon(X,g_{X},\mathrm{vol}_{g_{X}})\to(X,\lambda^{2}g_{X},\mathrm{vol}_{\lambda^{2}g_{X}})
is a Smith equivalence, where
\lambda\colon X\to[0,\infty)
is a smooth function.

2.

Let
U\subset X
be an open set, and equip
U
with the metric and calibration obtained from
X
by restriction. Then the inclusion map
\iota\colon(U,g_{X},\phi)\hookrightarrow(X,g_{X},\phi)
is a local Smith equivalence.

Example
2.11
(b) shows in particular that the inclusion map
\iota\colon(B^{n},g_{0},\mathrm{vol}_{0})\hookrightarrow(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{0},\mathrm{vol}_{0})
is a local Smith equivalence. However, we emphasize that the calibrated manifolds
(B^{n},g_{0},\mathrm{vol}_{0})
and
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{0},\mathrm{vol}_{0})
, despite being diffeomorphic, are
not
globally Smith equivalent. Before moving on, we explain how discrete quotients can be viewed as local Smith equivalences.

Proposition 2.12

(Discrete quotients)
.

Let
(X,g_{X})
be an oriented Riemannian manifold, and let
\Gamma\leq\mathrm{Isom}(X,g_{X})
be a discrete subgroup that acts freely and properly on
X
. Let
\pi\colon X\to Y:=X/\Gamma
denote the projection, and equip
Y
with the unique Riemannian metric
g_{Y}
such that
\pi\colon(X,g_{X})\to(Y,g_{Y})
is a local isometry.

Let
\phi_{X}\in\Omega^{k}(X)
be a
\Gamma
-invariant calibration on
(X,g_{X})
. Then
\phi_{X}
descends to a
k
-form
\phi_{Y}\in\Omega^{k}(Y)
that is a calibration with respect to
g_{Y}
. Moreover,
\pi\colon(X,g_{X},\phi_{X})\to(Y,g_{Y},\phi_{Y})
is a local Smith equivalence.

Proof.

Let
\phi_{X}\in\Omega^{k}(X)
be a
\Gamma
-invariant calibration on
(X,g_{X})
. Since
\phi_{X}
is
\Gamma
-invariant, there exists a unique
\phi_{Y}\in\Omega^{k}(Y)
such that
\phi_{X}=\pi^{*}\phi_{Y}
.

We claim that
\phi_{Y}
is a calibration. First, since
0=d\phi_{X}=\pi^{*}(d\phi_{Y})
and
\pi^{*}
is injective, we see that
\phi_{Y}
is closed. Next, let
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}\}
be a local
g_{Y}
-orthonormal frame on an open set
U\subset Y
. Since
\pi
is a Riemannian covering, by shrinking
U
if necessary, there exists a local
g_{X}
-orthonormal frame
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}\}
on
X
having
d\pi(f_{i})=e_{i}
. Therefore,
\phi_{Y}(e_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{k})=\phi_{Y}(d\pi(e_{1})\wedge\cdots\wedge d\pi(e_{k}))=\phi_{X}(f_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge f_{k})\leq 1
, and thus
\phi_{Y}
has comass one.

Finally, since
\pi^{*}g_{Y}=g_{X}
and
\pi^{*}\phi_{Y}=\phi_{X}
, we see that
\pi
is a local Smith equivalence.
∎

Having defined our notion of “isomorphism” between calibrated manifolds, we now briefly consider the corresponding notion of “automorphism.”

Definition 2.13
.

Let
(X^{n},g_{X},\alpha)
be a calibrated manifold.

•

A
Smith automorphism of
X
is a Smith equivalence
F\colon(X^{n},g_{X},\alpha)\to(X^{n},g_{X},\alpha)
. By definition, that this means that
F
is a diffeomorphism such that there exists
\lambda\colon X\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
for which

\displaystyle F^{*}g_{X}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}g_{X}

\displaystyle F^{*}\alpha

\displaystyle=\lambda^{k}\alpha.

We let
\mathrm{SmAut}(X,g_{X},\alpha)
denote the group of Smith automorphisms.

•

A
Gray automorphism of
X
is a Smith automorphism
F\colon(X^{n},g_{X},\alpha)\to(X^{n},g_{X},\alpha)
that is an isometry (i.e., it is a Smith automorphism with
\lambda=1
). We let
\mathrm{GrAut}(X,g_{X},\alpha)
denote the group of Gray automorphisms.

Note that we have inclusions

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{SmAut}(X,g_{X},\phi)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{ConfAut}(X,g_{X})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{GrAut}(X,g_{X},\phi)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Isom}(X,g_{X})}}

Here,
\mathrm{ConfAut}(X,g_{X})
and
\mathrm{Isom}(X,g_{X})
are the groups of conformal automorphisms and isometries of
(X,g_{X})
, respectively.

Example 2.14
.

1.

Let
(X^{2m},g_{X},\omega_{X})
be a Kähler manifold. Then the Smith automorphisms of
X
are precisely the conformal biholomorphisms, while the Gray automorphisms of
X
are the isometric biholomorphisms.

2.

Consider flat euclidean space
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{0})
with
n\geq 3
. Liouville’s theorem on conformal maps states that every conformal automorphism of
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{0})
is a (restriction of a) Möbius transformation. In particular, if
\phi\in\Omega^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is any calibration, then
\mathrm{SmAut}(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{0},\phi)
is a subgroup of the group of Möbius transformations.

2.2.2
Smith immersions

The most important conformally calibrating maps
f\colon\Sigma\to X
are those for which the calibration on the domain is a top-degree form.

Definition 2.15
.

A
Smith immersion

f\colon(\Sigma^{k},g_{\Sigma},\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma})\to(X^{n},g_{X},\phi)
is a conformally calibrating map for which the calibration on the domain is the volume form
\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma}\in\Omega^{k}(\Sigma)
. (Note that this requires
\dim(\Sigma)=\deg(\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma})=\deg(\phi)
.)

Explicitly, a Smith immersion is a map
f\colon\Sigma^{k}\to X^{n}
for which there exists a function
\lambda\colon X\to[0,\infty)
satisfying

\displaystyle f^{*}g_{X}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}g_{X}

\displaystyle f^{*}\phi

\displaystyle=\lambda^{k}\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma}.

This terminology is somewhat unfortunate as
Smith immersions need not be immersions
. Indeed, if
f\colon\Sigma^{k}\to X^{n}
is a Smith immersion, then each point
p\in\Sigma
has either
\mathrm{rank}(df_{p})=k
or
0
. In particular, constant maps are Smith immersions.

We shall say that a Smith immersion
f\colon\Sigma\to X
is
strict at
p\in\Sigma
if it is an immersion at
p
. This is equivalent to requiring that
f
be conformal at
p
(i.e.,
\lambda(p)>0
).

Proposition 2.16

(Invariance)
.

Let
f\colon(\Sigma^{k},g_{\Sigma},\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma})\to(X^{n},g_{X},\phi)
be a Smith immersion.

1.

If
G\colon(S,g_{S},\mathrm{vol}_{S})\to(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma},\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma})
is conformally calibrating, then
f\circ G\colon S\to X
is a Smith immersion.

2.

If
G\colon(X,g_{X},\phi)\to(Y,g_{Y},\beta)
is conformally calibrating, then
G\circ f\colon\Sigma\to Y
is a Smith immersion.

Proof.

This is immediate from Proposition
2.8
.
∎

Strict Smith immersions are exactly the conformal maps that parametrize immersed calibrated submanifolds. To be precise:

Proposition 2.17

(
[
6
]
)
.

If
f\colon(\Sigma^{k},g_{\Sigma},\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma})\to(X^{n},g_{X},\phi)
is a strict Smith immersion, then its image
f(\Sigma)
is an immersed
\phi
-calibrated submanifold. Conversely, if
\iota\colon\Sigma\to(X^{n},g_{X},\phi)
is an immersed
\phi
-calibrated submanifold, then equipping
\Sigma
with the metric
g_{\Sigma}=\iota^{*}g_{X}
and volume form
\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma}=\iota^{*}\phi
makes
\iota
into a strict Smith immersion.

A remarkable feature of Smith immersions is their
k
-harmonicity. Recall that for a smooth map
f\colon(M^{m},g_{M})\to(N^{n},g_{N})
between Riemannian manifolds, its
k
-energy

E_{k}(f)\in[0,\infty]
and
k
-tension

\tau_{k}(f)
, a section of
f^{*}TN
, are

\displaystyle E_{k}(f)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{k})^{k}}\int_{\Sigma}|df|^{k}\,\mathrm{vol}_{M},

\displaystyle\tau_{k}(f)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{div}(|df|^{k-2}df).

It is well-known that the critical points of the
k
-energy functional are precisely the
k
-harmonic maps
, i.e., those satisfying the second-order PDE
\tau_{k}(f)=0
.

Now, let
f\colon(\Sigma^{k},g_{\Sigma},\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma})\to(X^{n},g_{X},\phi)
be a smooth map, where
\phi\in\Omega^{k}(X)
is a calibration. Let
\mathrm{vol}_{f^{*}g_{X}}:=\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^{k}}\right|dx^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{k}
in local coordinates
(x^{1},\ldots,x^{k})
, and set
V(f):=\int_{\Sigma}\mathrm{vol}_{f^{*}g_{X}}
. If
f
is an immersion, then
\mathrm{vol}_{f^{*}g_{X}}
is the volume form of the induced metric
f^{*}g_{X}
. Then the following fundamental inequality holds
[
7
]
,
[
19
]
:

\frac{1}{(\sqrt{k})^{k}}|df|^{k}\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma}\geq\mathrm{vol}_{f^{*}g_{X}}\geq f^{*}\phi.

(2.2)

Moreover, we have equality
\frac{1}{(\sqrt{k})^{k}}|df|^{k}\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma}=\mathrm{vol}_{f^{*}g_{X}}=f^{*}\phi
if and only if
f
is Smith. Now, if
\Sigma
is compact, then integrating (
2.2
) gives:

E_{k}(f)\geq V(f)\geq\int_{\Sigma}f^{*}\phi.

This shows that if
\Sigma
is compact and without boundary, then Smith immersions
f\colon\Sigma\to X
are homological minimizers of the
k
-energy functional. In fact, we have the following mapping analog of the fundamental theorem of calibrations:

Theorem 2.18

(Smith’s theorem
[
33
]
)
.

If
f\colon(\Sigma^{k},g_{\Sigma},\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma})\to(X^{n},g_{X},\phi)
is a Smith immersion, then
f
is
k
-harmonic. Moreover, if
\Sigma
is compact and without boundary, then
f
is
k
-energy minimizing in its homology class.

Remark 2.19
.

Note that if a Smith immersion
f\colon(\Sigma^{k},g_{\Sigma},\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma})\to(X^{n},g_{X},\phi)
is isometric, then it is simultaneously
2
-harmonic and
k
-harmonic.

We now mention some examples of interest.

Example 2.20
.

1.

Let
(X^{n},g_{X},\mathrm{vol}_{X})

2.3
The
k
-Laplacian chain rule

We now relate Smith immersions to Harvey-Lawson’s pluripotential theory
[
13
]
. The remarks in this subsection will only be used in
\lx@sectionsign
6.1
.

To begin, recall that if
u\colon(X,g_{X})\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function on a Riemannian manifold, its
Hessian
is the symmetric
2
-tensor field
\mathrm{Hess}(u)\in\Gamma(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(T^{*}X))
given by
(\mathrm{Hess}\,u)(V,W)=V(Wu)-(\nabla_{V}W)u=\langle\nabla_{V}(\nabla u),W\rangle
, where
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection of
g_{X}
. Using the metric, we may regard the Hessian of
u
as the self-adjoint endomorphism
\mathrm{Hess}(u)\colon TX\to TX
given by
\mathrm{Hess}(u)(V)=\nabla_{V}(\nabla u)
.

Next, let
A\in\mathrm{End}(T_{x}X)
be an endomorphism. We write
A\geq 0
to indicate that
A
is positive semidefinite. Moreover, if
\xi\subset T_{x}X
is a
k
-plane and
P_{\xi}\colon T_{x}X\to\xi
denotes orthogonal projection, then the
\xi
-trace of
A
is defined by
\mathrm{tr}_{\xi}(A):=\mathrm{tr}(AP_{\xi}^{*})=\sum_{1}^{k}\langle Ae_{j},e_{j}\rangle
, where
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
\xi
.

Definition 2.21
.

Let
(X^{n},g_{X},\phi)
be a calibrated manifold,
\phi\in\Omega^{k}(X)
. Let
u\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function.

•

Say
u
is
convex
(resp.,
affine
) if
\mathrm{Hess}(u)\geq 0
(resp.,
\mathrm{Hess}(u)=0
).

•

Say
u
is
\phi
-convex
(resp.,
\phi
-affine
) if for all
\phi
-planes
\xi\in\mathrm{Gr}(\phi)
, we have
\mathrm{Hess}(u)|_{\xi}\geq 0
(resp.,
\mathrm{Hess}(u)|_{\xi}=0
).

•

Say
u
is
plurisubharmonic
(PSH) (resp.,
pluriharmonic
) if
\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{Hess}(u))\geq 0
(resp.,
\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{Hess}(u))=0
).

•

Say
u
is
\phi
-plurisubharmonic
(
\phi
-PSH) (resp.,
\phi
-pluriharmonic
) if for all
\phi
-planes
\xi\in\mathrm{Gr}(\phi)
, we have
\mathrm{tr}_{\xi}(\mathrm{Hess}(u))\geq 0
(resp.,
\mathrm{tr}_{\xi}(\mathrm{Hess}(u))=0
).

Notice that affine functions are
\phi
-affine, that convex functions are
\phi
-convex, and so on. Note also that we have implications

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi\text{-pluriharmonic}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi\text{-PSH}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi\text{-affine}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi\text{-convex}}

A fundamental result of pluripotential theory states that if
\Sigma\subset(X^{n},g_{X},\phi)
is a
\phi
-calibrated submanifold, and if
u\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
is
\phi
-PSH, then
u|_{\Sigma}
is subharmonic
[
13
]
. Similarly, if
\iota\colon(\Sigma^{k},g_{\Sigma},\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma})\to(X^{n},g_{X},\phi)
is an
isometric
Smith immersion and
u\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
is
\phi
-PSH, then
u\circ\iota\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
is subharmonic. However, as the following
k
-Laplacian chain rule indicates, the relationship between non-isometric Smith immersions and
\phi
-PSH functions is more complicated.

Proposition 2.22

(
k
-Laplacian chain rule)
.

Let
f\colon(\Sigma^{k},g_{\Sigma},\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma})\to(X^{n},g_{X},\phi)
be a weakly conformal,
k
-harmonic map, where
k\geq 2
, and set
\xi:=df(T\Sigma)
. Let
u\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function. Then the
k
-Laplacian of
u\circ f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
is:

\Delta_{k}(u\circ f)=\frac{1}{k}|df|^{2}|f^{*}du|^{k-2}\operatorname{tr}_{\xi}\operatorname{Hess}(u)|_{\xi}+\frac{k-2}{k^{2}}|df|^{4}|f^{*}du|^{k-4}\operatorname{Hess}(u)|_{\xi}(du|_{\xi},du|_{\xi}).

(2.3)

The following corollary is immediate.

Corollary 2.23
.

Let
f\colon(\Sigma^{k},g_{\Sigma},\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma})\to(X^{n},g_{X},\phi)
be a weakly conformal,
k
-harmonic map,
k\geq 2
. Let
u\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function.

1.

If
u
is affine, then
u\circ f
is
k
-harmonic.

2.

If
u
is convex, then
u\circ f
is
k
-subharmonic.

3.

If
u
is
\phi
-affine and
f
is a Smith immersion, then
u\circ f
is
k
-harmonic.

4.

If
u
is
\phi
-convex and
f
is a Smith immersion, then
u\circ f
is
k
-subharmonic.

Remark 2.24
.

Corollary
2.23
(d) can be strengthened in the following way. Let
f\colon\Sigma\to X
be a Smith immersion, and let
\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{k}
be the eigenvalues of
\mathrm{Hess}(u)|_{\xi}
(some of which may be negative). Setting
H=\mathrm{Hess}(u)|_{\xi}
and
v=du|_{\xi}
, the chain rule can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\Delta_{k}(u\circ f)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{k}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}}|df|^{k}|v|^{k-4}\left(\mathrm{tr}(H)|v|^{2}+(k-2)v^{T}Hv\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{1}{k}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}}|df|^{k}|v|^{k-2}\left(\mathrm{tr}(H)+(k-2)\lambda_{1}\right)\!.

Therefore, if for all
\xi\in\mathrm{Gr}(\phi)
, we have that
(k-2)\lambda_{1}+\mathrm{tr}_{\xi}(\mathrm{Hess}(u))\geq 0
, then
u\circ f
is
k
-subharmonic.

To prove Proposition
2.22
, we make use of the following standard formula.

Lemma 2.25
.

Let
h\colon(M^{m},g_{M})\to(N^{n},g_{N})
be a smooth map between Riemannian manifolds. Then for all
k\geq 2
, we have

\tau_{k}(h)=|dh|^{k-2}\tau_{2}(h)+\frac{k-2}{2}|dh|^{k-4}\langle dh,\nabla|dh|^{2}\rangle.

(2.4)

We now prove the
k
-Laplacian chain rule.

Proof.

Let
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}\}
be a local orthonormal frame of
T\Sigma
, let
\lambda=\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}|df|
be the conformal factor of
f
, and set
\xi=df(T\Sigma)
. Note that
f^{*}du=d(u\circ f)\in\Omega^{1}(\Sigma)
has square norm

|f^{*}du|^{2}=\sum_{i}\left|du(df(e_{i}))\right|^{2}=\lambda^{2}\left|du|_{\xi}\right|^{2}=\frac{1}{k}|df|^{2}\left|du|_{\xi}\right|^{2}.

(2.5)

Let
x\in\Sigma
. Either
x
is a critical point of
u\circ f
, or it is not. Suppose first that
x
is a critical point of
u\circ f
. If
k\geq 4
or
k=2
, then both sides of (
2.3
) vanish at
x
, so the result is true at
x
. If
k=3
, then the left side of (
2.3
) vanishes, and the first term on the right also vanishes, but the second term on the right has a discontinuity at
x
. To address this, we use equation (
2.5
) to calculate

|df|^{4}|f^{*}du|^{-1}\operatorname{Hess}(u)|_{\xi}(du|_{\xi},du|_{\xi})=k|df|^{2}|f^{*}du|\operatorname{Hess}(u)|_{\xi}\left(\frac{du|_{\xi}}{|du|_{\xi}|},\frac{du|_{\xi}}{|du|_{\xi}|}\right)\!.

This shows that the discontinuity at
x
is removable.

Suppose now that
x
is not a critical point of
u\circ f
. Then
x
is not a critical point of
f
(so
df_{x}
is injective) and
f(x)
is not a critical point of
u
. We begin by calculating
\Delta_{2}(u\circ f)
. For this, recall the standard formula

\Delta_{2}(u\circ f)=du\circ\tau_{2}(f)+\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{Hess}(u)(df,df)).

For the first term, noting that
f
is
k
-harmonic, equation (
2.4
) gives

\displaystyle\tau_{2}(f)=-\frac{k-2}{2}|df|^{-2}\left\langle df,\nabla|df|^{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-(k-2)|df|^{-1}\left\langle df,\nabla|df|\right\rangle.

For the second term, letting
v_{i}=\frac{1}{\lambda}df(e_{i})
, and noting that
\{v_{i}\}
is an orthonormal frame of
\xi
, we calculate

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{Hess}(u)(df,df))=\sum_{i}\mathrm{Hess}(u)(df(e_{i}),df(e_{i}))=\lambda^{2}\sum_{i}\mathrm{Hess}(u)(v_{i},v_{i})=\frac{1}{k}|df|^{2}\,\mathrm{tr}_{\xi}\mathrm{Hess}(u)|_{\xi}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\Delta_{2}(u\circ f)

\displaystyle=du\circ\left(-(k-2)|df|^{-1}\left\langle df,\nabla|df|\right\rangle\right)+\frac{1}{k}|df|^{2}\,\mathrm{tr}_{\xi}\mathrm{Hess}(u)|_{\xi}

\displaystyle=-(k-2)|df|^{-1}du\circ\left\langle df,\nabla|df|\right\rangle+\frac{1}{k}|df|^{2}\,\mathrm{tr}_{\xi}\mathrm{Hess}(u)|_{\xi}

\displaystyle=-(k-2)|df|^{-1}\left\langle f^{*}du,\nabla|df|\right\rangle+\frac{1}{k}|df|^{2}\,\mathrm{tr}_{\xi}\mathrm{Hess}(u)|_{\xi}.

(2.6)

We now calculate
\Delta_{k}(u\circ f)
. Using equation (
2.4
) followed by (
2.6
), we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{k}(u\circ f)

\displaystyle=|f^{*}du|^{k-2}\Delta_{2}(u\circ f)+\frac{k-2}{2}|f^{*}du|^{k-4}\langle f^{*}du,\nabla|f^{*}du|^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=|f^{*}du|^{k-2}\left(-(k-2)|df|^{-1}\left\langle f^{*}du,\nabla|df|\right\rangle+\frac{1}{k}|df|^{2}\,\mathrm{tr}_{\xi}\mathrm{Hess}(u)|_{\xi}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +(k-2)|f^{*}du|^{k-3}\langle f^{*}du,\nabla|f^{*}du|\rangle

\displaystyle=(k-2)\frac{|f^{*}du|^{k-3}}{|df|}\left(-|f^{*}du|\langle f^{*}du,\nabla|df|\rangle+\langle f^{*}du,|df|\nabla|f^{*}du|\rangle\right)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +\frac{1}{k}|df|^{2}|f^{*}du|^{k-2}\mathrm{tr}_{\xi}\mathrm{Hess}(u)|_{\xi}

\displaystyle=(k-2)\frac{|f^{*}du|^{k-3}}{|df|}\langle f^{*}du,-|f^{*}du|\nabla|df|+|df|\nabla|f^{*}du|\rangle+\frac{1}{k}|df|^{2}|f^{*}du|^{k-2}\mathrm{tr}_{\xi}\mathrm{Hess}(u)|_{\xi}

\displaystyle=(k-2)|df||f^{*}du|^{k-3}\left\langle f^{*}du,\nabla\!\left(\frac{|f^{*}du|}{|df|}\right)\right\rangle+\frac{1}{k}|df|^{2}|f^{*}du|^{k-2}\mathrm{tr}_{\xi}\mathrm{Hess}(u)|_{\xi}.

(2.7)

It remains to calculate the first term of (
2.7
). For this, note that (
2.5
) gives
\frac{|f^{*}du|}{|df|}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\left|du|_{\xi}\right|\circ f
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\langle f^{*}du,\nabla\!\left(\frac{|f^{*}du|}{|df|}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{|df|}{|f^{*}du|}\left\langle f^{*}du,\nabla\!\left(\frac{|f^{*}du|}{|df|}\right)^{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2k}\frac{|df|}{|f^{*}du|}\left\langle du\circ df,\nabla\!\left(\left|du|_{\xi}\right|^{2}\circ f\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2k}\frac{|df|}{|f^{*}du|}du(df(e_{i}))\cdot\nabla_{e_{i}}\!\left(\left|du|_{\xi}\right|^{2}\circ f\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2k^{2}}\frac{|df|^{3}}{|f^{*}du|}\lambda\,du(v_{i})\cdot\nabla_{df(e_{i})}\left|du|_{\xi}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2k^{2}}\frac{|df|^{3}}{|f^{*}du|}\,du(v_{i})\cdot\nabla_{v_{i}}\left|du|_{\xi}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k^{2}}\frac{|df|^{3}}{|f^{*}du|}\,(\nabla_{v_{i}}u)(\nabla_{v_{j}}\nabla_{v_{i}}u)(\nabla_{v_{j}}u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k^{2}}\frac{|df|^{3}}{|f^{*}du|}\,\mathrm{Hess}(u)|_{\xi}(du|_{\xi},du|_{\xi}).

(2.8)

Here, in the second to last line, we used that
[du|_{\xi}]^{j}:=(du|_{\xi})(v_{j})=\nabla_{v_{j}}u
and that
[\nabla_{v_{i}}du|_{\xi}]^{j}=\nabla_{v_{j}}\nabla_{v_{i}}u
to calculate
\nabla_{v_{i}}\!\left|du|_{\xi}\right|^{2}=2\langle\nabla_{v_{i}}du|_{\xi},du|_{\xi}\rangle=2[\nabla_{v_{i}}du|_{\xi}]^{j}[du|_{\xi}]^{j}=\nabla_{v_{j}}\nabla_{v_{i}}u\cdot\nabla_{v_{j}}u
. Finally, substituting (
2.8
) into (
2.7
) yields the formula.
∎

2.4
The Poincaré metric on the
n
-ball

Finally, we recall the Poincaré metric on the
n
-ball. Let
B^{n}(R)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon|x|<R\}
be the
n
-ball of radius
R>0
. On
B^{n}(R)
, the
Poincaré metric

g_{R}
and
Poincaré volume form

\mathrm{vol}_{R}\in\Omega^{n}(B^{n}(R))
are given by

\displaystyle g_{R}

\displaystyle=\frac{R^{2}}{(R^{2}-|x|^{2})^{2}}\,g_{0}

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}_{R}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{R}{R^{2}-|x|^{2}}\right)^{n}\mathrm{vol}_{0},

where
g_{0}
and
\mathrm{vol}_{0}
are the standard flat metric and volume form, respectively. The scale factor in
g_{R}
was chosen to ensure that for vectors
v\in T_{0}B^{n}(1)
based at the origin, the Poincaré norm
|v|_{1}=\sqrt{g_{1}(v,v)}
equals the euclidean norm
|v|_{0}=\sqrt{g_{0}(v,v)}
. The following proposition summarizes some standard facts about the Poincaré metric that we will use in this work.

Proposition 2.26
.

1.

The Poincaré metric
g_{R}
is conformal to the flat metric
g_{0}
. Consequently:

•

The identity map
\mathrm{Id}\colon(B^{n}(R),g_{R},\mathrm{vol}_{R})\to(B^{n}(R),g_{0},\mathrm{vol}_{0})
is a Smith equivalence.

•

The inclusion map
\iota\colon(B^{n}(R),g_{R},\mathrm{vol}_{R})\hookrightarrow(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{0},\mathrm{vol}_{0})
is a local Smith equivalence.

2.

The Poincaré metric
g_{R}
is complete and has constant sectional curvature
-\frac{4}{R^{2}}
.

3.

The group of conformal automorphisms
\mathrm{ConfAut}(g_{R})
acts transitively and by isometries on
(B^{n}(R),g_{R})
.

4.

The induced distance function
\mathrm{dist}_{R}\colon B^{n}(R)\times B^{n}(R)\to\mathbb{R}
is given by the explicit formula

\mathrm{dist}_{R}(x,y)=R\operatorname{arcsinh}\sqrt{\frac{R^{2}|x-y|^{2}}{(R^{2}-|x|^{2})(R^{2}-|y|^{2})}}.

3
\phi
-Hyperbolicity

3.1
Definition and examples

We now begin our study of hyperbolicity in earnest. To get started, we generalize the notion of “Brody hyperbolicity” from the Kähler setting to arbitrary calibrated manifolds.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(X,g_{X},\phi)
be a calibrated
n
-manifold, where
\phi\in\Omega^{k}(X)
. Say
(X,g_{X})
is
\phi
-hyperbolic
if every Smith immersion
(\mathbb{R}^{k},g_{0},\mathrm{vol}_{0})\to(X,g_{X},\phi)
is constant.

When
g_{X}
is clear from context, we will often simply say “
X
is
\phi
-hyperbolic.” The following is immediate.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(X,g_{X},\phi)
be a calibrated manifold. If
X
is
\phi
-hyperbolic, then
X
contains no
\phi
-calibrated submanifold conformally diffeomorphic to
(\mathbb{R}^{k},g_{0})
.

Note, however, that if
X
is
\phi
-hyperbolic, there may exist
\phi
-calibrated submanifolds of
X
that are conformally diffeomorphic to the flat
k
-ball
(B^{k},g_{0})
. Here are some basic properties of
\phi
-hyperbolicity. The proofs are straightforward.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(X,g_{X},\phi)
be a calibrated manifold.

1.

Suppose
(X,g_{X},\phi)
is Smith equivalent to
(Y,g_{Y},\psi)
. Then
(X,g_{X})
is
\phi
-hyperbolic if and only if
(Y,g_{Y})
is
\psi
-hyperbolic.

2.

If
(X,g_{X})
is
\phi
-hyperbolic, then for every open set
U\subset X
, the manifold
(U,g_{X}|_{U})
is
\phi
-hyperbolic.

We emphasize that the converse Proposition
3.3
(b) is false. That is, if
(X,g_{X})
fails to be
\phi
-hyperbolic, then certain open subsets of
X
could nevertheless be
\phi
-hyperbolic. We now provide several examples and non-examples.

Example 3.4

(Kähler calibrations)
.

Let
(X^{2m},g_{X},\omega)
be a Kähler manifold. Then:

(X,g_{X})\text{ is }\omega\text{-hyperbolic}\ \iff\ X\text{ is Brody hyperbolic.}

Note that the Brody hyperbolicity of a Kähler manifold depends only on the complex structure (i.e., it is independent of the metric).

•

In complex dimension one, a closed Riemann surface is Brody hyperbolic if and only if it has genus
\geq 2
. A non-compact Riemann surface is Brody hyperbolic if and only if it is not biholomorphic to
\mathbb{C}
or
\mathbb{C}^{*}
.

•

In a Stein manifold (e.g.,
\mathbb{C}^{n}
), every bounded domain is Brody hyperbolic by Liouville’s theorem.

•

Brody hyperbolicity is preserved by products, fibrations, and universal covers.

•

In
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
, a complex hypersurface of sufficiently large degree is Brody hyperbolic.

•

In a complex torus, the Brody hyperbolic submanifolds are precisely those that do not contain a non-trivial sub-torus.

•

Hermitian manifolds with holomorphic sectional curvature bounded above by a negative constant are Brody hyperbolic.

Example 3.5

(Flat
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n}/\mathbb{Z}^{n}
)
.

Let
\phi\in\Lambda^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{*}
be a constant-coefficient calibration. Let
T^{n}=\mathbb{R}^{n}/\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, and let
\pi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to T^{n}
be the projection. By Proposition
2.12
, there exists a calibration
\phi^{\vee}\in\Omega^{k}(T^{n})
having
\phi=\pi^{*}\phi^{\vee}
.

1.

The calibrated manifold
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{0},\phi)
is
not

\phi
-hyperbolic. In particular,
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{0},\mathrm{vol}_{0})
is
not

\mathrm{vol}_{0}
-hyperbolic.

2.

The calibrated manifold
(T^{n},g_{0},\phi^{\vee})
is
not

\phi^{\vee}
-hyperbolic.

Example 3.6

(Volume form)
.

Equip
(X^{n},g_{X})
with the volume form calibration
\mathrm{vol}_{X}\in\Omega^{n}(X)
.

•

If
\mathrm{Scal}_{X}\leq-A
for some
A>0
, then
(X,g_{X})
is
\mathrm{vol}_{X}
-hyperbolic. We will prove this in Corollary
4.13
. Example
3.5
(a) shows that this result cannot be improved to
A\geq 0
.

•

We remark that if
(X,g_{X})
is not
1
-quasiregularly elliptic, then
(X,g_{X})
is
\mathrm{vol}_{X}
-hyperbolic. (We refer to
[
3
]
for a discussion of
K
-quasiregular ellipticity.)

Example 3.7

(Quaternionic-Kähler calibrations)
.

When
(X,g_{X})
is a quaternionic-Kähler
4m
-manifold, we use
\Psi\in\Omega^{4}(X)
to denote the QK
4
-form.

•

Quaternionic hyperbolic space
(\mathbb{HH}^{n},g_{\mathbb{HH}^{n}})
is
\Psi
-hyperbolic. (See Corollary
6.4
(c).)

•

Quaternionic projective space
(\mathbb{HP}^{n},g_{\mathbb{HP}^{n}})
is
not

\Psi
-hyperbolic. (Indeed, the standard embedding of
\mathbb{HP}^{1}
minus a point can be parametrized by a Smith immersion from flat
\mathbb{R}^{4}
.)

Example 3.8

(Associative calibrations)
.

When
(X^{7},g_{X})
is an oriented Riemannian
7
-manifold, we use
\varphi\in\Omega^{3}(X)
to denote a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure on
X
.

•

As mentioned above, flat
\mathbb{R}^{7}
and flat

(equipped with the flat
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structures
\varphi_{0}
) are
not

\varphi_{0}
-hyperbolic. However, there exist domains

that are
\varphi_{0}
-hyperbolic (e.g., by Theorem
6.6
).

•

The
7
-manifold
\not{S}(S^{3})
equipped with the Bryant-Salamon metric and

-structure
\varphi_{\mathrm{BS}}
is
not

\varphi_{\mathrm{BS}}
-hyperbolic.

Analogous remarks hold for Cayley calibrations.

Example 3.9

(Trivial
\phi
-hyperbolicity)
.

If a calibrated manifold
(X,g_{X},\phi)
admits no
\phi
-calibrated submanifolds, then
(X,g_{X})
is trivially
\phi
-hyperbolic. (Note that this may occur even if
\mathrm{Gr}(\phi)
is relatively large.) Here are two examples of this.

•

Let
(X^{6},g_{X})
be a strict nearly-Kähler
6
-manifold, and let
(\omega,\Upsilon)\in\Omega^{2}(X)\oplus\Omega^{3}(X;\mathbb{C})
denote the
\mathrm{SU}(3)
-structure with the convention that

. It is well-known that
X
admits no
\mathrm{Re}(\Upsilon)
-calibrated
3
-folds (even locally), so
X
is trivially
\mathrm{Re}(\Upsilon)
-hyperbolic.

•

Let
(X^{7},g_{X})
be a
7
-manifold with a nearly-parallel
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure, and let
\psi\in\Omega^{4}(X)
be the coassociative
4
-form. It is well-known that
X
admits no coassociative
4
-folds (even locally), so
X
is trivially
\psi
-hyperbolic.

3.2
Domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

For domains
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
equipped with the flat metric, we now make some basic observations about
\phi
-hyperbolicity. First, we set terminology.

Definition 3.10
.

A
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a non-empty, open, connected subset
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. When a calibration
\phi\in\Omega^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is specified, we will typically regard a domain
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
as the calibrated manifold
(U,g_{0},\phi)
. (An important exception to this is the open
n
-ball
B^{n}
, which we typically equip with the Poincaré metric
g_{1}
.) Two domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are
congruent
if they differ by a rigid motion of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(i.e., an element of the euclidean group
\mathrm{E}(n)=\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes\mathrm{O}(n)
.)

Remark 3.11
.

The property of “
\phi
-hyperbolicity” is generally not preserved by ambient isometries. In particular, if
U_{1},U_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
are congruent domains, the
\phi
-hyperbolicity of
U_{1}
need not imply the
\phi
-hyperbolicity of
U_{2}
. For illustration, consider
(\mathbb{R}^{4},g_{0})
with coordinates
(x_{1},y_{1},x_{2},y_{2})
, and let
\omega=dx_{1}\wedge dy_{1}+dx_{2}\wedge dy_{2}\in\Omega^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
be the standard Kähler form. Then the domains

\displaystyle U_{1}

\displaystyle=\{(x_{1})^{2}+(x_{2})^{2}<1\},

\displaystyle U_{2}

\displaystyle=\{(x_{1})^{2}+(y_{1})^{2}<1\}

are congruent. Since
U_{2}
contains a complex line, we see that
U_{2}
is not
\omega
-hyperbolic (i.e., Brody hyperbolic). On the other hand, since
U_{1}
is convex and does not contain any complex lines, a theorem of Barth
[
2
]
implies that
U_{1}
is
\omega
-hyperbolic (i.e., Brody hyperbolic).

The following definitions were first introduced in
[
19
]
.

Definition 3.12
.

Let
\phi\in\Lambda^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{*}
be a constant-coefficient calibration.

•

Say
\phi
is
conformally rigid
if every conformally flat,
\phi
-calibrated submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is flat.

•

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a domain. An
inner Möbius curve
is a map
f\colon U\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
of the form
f(x)=y_{0}+L(v(x))
, where
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
L\colon\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an orthogonal linear map, and
v\colon U\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
is the restriction of a non-constant Möbius transformation
S^{k}\to S^{k}
, where we view
S^{k}=\mathbb{R}^{k}\cup\{\infty\}
.

•

Say
\phi
is
inner Möbius rigid
if every non-constant Smith immersion
f\colon(U,g_{0},\mathrm{vol}_{0})\to(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{0},\phi)
is an inner Möbius curve.

Theorem 3.13

(
[
19
]
)
.

Let
\phi\in\Lambda^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{*}
be a constant-coefficient calibration,
k\geq 3
. Then
\phi
is conformally rigid if and only if
\phi
is inner Möbius rigid.

Example 3.14
.

The following remarks are drawn from
[
19
]
.

1.

For a given
(k,n)
, the collection of inner Möbius rigid calibrations is an open dense subset of the set of all constant-coefficient calibrations in
\Lambda^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{*}
.

2.

The volume form
\mathrm{vol}_{0}\in\Lambda^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{*}
is inner Möbius rigid, essentially by Liouville’s theorem on conformal maps.

3.

For
p\geq 2
, the Kähler calibrations
\frac{1}{p!}\omega^{p}\in\Omega^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
are inner Möbius rigid.

4.

By contrast, the Kähler calibration
\omega\in\Lambda^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})^{*}
, the special Lagrangian calibration
\mathrm{Re}(dz_{1}\wedge dz_{2}\wedge dz_{3})\in\Lambda^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{6})^{*}
, the associative calibration
\varphi\in\Lambda^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{7})^{*}
, and the Cayley calibration
\Phi\in\Lambda^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{8})^{*}
are
not
inner Möbius rigid. (The authors do not know whether the coassociative calibration
\ast\varphi\in\Lambda^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{7})^{*}
is inner Möbius rigid.)

When
\phi\in\Lambda^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{*}
is inner Möbius rigid, we can completely characterize those domains
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
that are
\phi
-hyperbolic.

Theorem 3.15
.

Let
\phi\in\Lambda^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{*}
be a constant-coefficient calibration,
k\geq 3
. Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a domain, and regard
U
as the calibrated manifold
(U,g_{0},\phi)
.

1.

If
U
is
\phi
-hyperbolic, then
U
contains no affine
\phi
-planes.

2.

Suppose
\phi
is inner Möbius rigid. Then the converse of (a) holds.

Proof:

1.

Suppose there exists an affine
\phi
-plane
E\subset U
. Then there exists a non-constant Smith immersion
(\mathbb{R}^{k},g_{0},\mathrm{vol}_{0})\to(U,g_{0},\phi)
whose image is
E
, so
U
is not
\phi
-hyperbolic.

2.

Suppose
\phi
is inner Möbius rigid. Suppose that
U
is not
\phi
-hyperbolic, so there exists a non-constant Smith immersion
F\colon(\mathbb{R}^{k},g_{0},\mathrm{vol}_{0})\to(U,g_{0},\phi)
. Let
\iota\colon U\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the inclusion, so that
f:=\iota\circ F\colon(\mathbb{R}^{k},g_{0},\mathrm{vol}_{0})\to(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{0},\phi)
is a Smith immersion. Since
f
is non-constant, inner Möbius rigidity implies that
f(x)=y_{0}+L(v(x))
for some
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, some orthogonal linear map
L\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
, and some map
v\colon\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
that is the restriction of a Möbius transformation
S^{k}\to S^{k}
. Since
v
is entire (defined on all of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
) and the restriction of a Möbius transformation,
v
is surjective. Therefore,
\mathrm{Image}(f)=y_{0}+\mathrm{Image}(L\circ v)
is an affine
k
-plane in
U
, and it is
\phi
-calibrated because
F
is Smith. That is,
U
contains an affine
\phi
-plane.

Corollary 3.16
.

1.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{2n}
be a domain, and let
\phi=\frac{1}{p!}\omega^{p}\in\Omega^{2p}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
have
p\geq 2
. Then
(U,g_{0})
is
\frac{1}{p!}\omega^{p}
-hyperbolic if and only if
U
contains no complex
p
-planes.

2.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a domain, and let
\phi=\mathrm{vol}_{0}\in\Omega^{n}(U)
. Then
(U,g_{0})
is
\mathrm{vol}_{0}
-hyperbolic if and only if
U\neq\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

This follows from Theorem
3.15
(b) and Example
3.14
.

4
Schwarz lemmas and consequences

Our primary goal in this section is to derive a Schwarz lemma for weakly conformal
k
-harmonic maps in general, and for Smith immersions in particular. In
\lx@sectionsign
4.1
, we define a notion of “
\phi
-sectional curvature” that extends the holomorphic sectional curvature to arbitrary calibrated geometries
(X,g_{X},\phi)
. Then, in
\lx@sectionsign
4.2

k
-harmonic maps and Smith immersions. This formula, together with a maximum principle argument, will imply the Schwarz lemma (Theorem
4.9
). Finally, in
\lx@sectionsign
4.3
, we draw several consequences. In particular, we prove (Theorem

-sectional curvature implies
\phi
-hyperbolicity.

4.1

-Sectional curvature

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(X,g_{X},\phi)
be a semi-calibrated
n
-manifold, where
\phi\in\Omega^{k}(X)
with

. The
\phi
-sectional curvature
of a
\phi

\xi\in\mathrm{Gr}(\phi)
is

\mathrm{Sec}^{\phi}(\xi):=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq k}\mathrm{Sec}(e_{i}\wedge e_{j})=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq k}R_{ijji}

where
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
\xi
. One can check that this definition is well-defined (independent of basis), and that it is independent of the orientation of
\xi
.

Example 4.2
.

•

Let
\phi=\mathrm{vol}_{X}\in\Omega^{n}(X)
be the volume form. Then
\mathrm{Sec}^{\mathrm{vol}_{X}}
is the scalar curvature of
X
.

•

Let
\phi=\omega\in\Omega^{2}(X)
be a Kähler form on a Kähler manifold
X
. Then
\mathrm{Sec}^{\omega}(\xi)
is the holomorphic sectional curvature of a complex line
\xi\subset TX
.

•

Let
\phi=\Psi\in\Omega^{4}(X)
be a QK
4
-form on a quaternionic-Kähler manifold
X
. Then
\mathrm{Sec}^{\Psi}(\xi)
extends the notion of “
Q
-sectional curvature”
[
21
]
.

To explain this, for
u\in T_{x}X
, we let
Q(u)\subset T_{x}X
be the quaternionic line spanned by
u
. If
\mathrm{Sec}(v_{1}\wedge v_{2})
is constant on non-zero elements
v_{1}\wedge v_{2}\in\Lambda^{2}(Q(u))\subset\Lambda^{2}(T_{x}X)
, then
\rho(u):=\mathrm{Sec}(v_{1}\wedge v_{2})
is called the
Q
-sectional curvature of
u
, and one can calculate that
\mathrm{Sec}^{\Psi}(Q(u))=12\,\rho(u)
. However, if
\mathrm{Sec}(v_{1}\wedge v_{2})
depends on the choice of non-zero
v_{1}\wedge v_{2}\in\Lambda^{2}(Q(u))
, then the
Q
-sectional curvature of
u
is undefined (whereas
\mathrm{Sec}^{\Psi}(Q(u))
still makes sense).

Note that
\mathrm{Sec}^{\Psi}
is not the notion of “quaternionic sectional curvature” considered by Moroianu-Semmelmann-Weingart
[
27
]
, nor is it the “quaternionic bisectional curvature” considered by Macia-Semmelmann-Weingart
[
26
]
.

We now make two remarks about this definition. First, when the ambient metric
g_{X}
is Einstein (as happens in special holonomy settings), there is a simple relationship between the
\phi
- and
(\ast\phi)
-sectional curvature.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(X^{n},g_{X},\phi)
be a semi-calibrated
n
-manifold,
\phi\in\Omega^{k}(X)
. If
\mathrm{Ric}(g_{X})=cg_{X}
for some constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
, then

\mathrm{Sec}^{\phi}(\xi)=(2k-n)c+\mathrm{Sec}^{\ast\phi}(\ast\xi),\ \ \forall\xi\in\mathrm{Gr}(\phi).

In particular, if
g_{X}
is Ricci-flat, then
\mathrm{Sec}^{\phi}(\xi)=\mathrm{Sec}^{\ast\phi}(\ast\xi)
holds for all
\phi
-planes
\xi
.

Proof.

Let
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
be an orthonormal frame such that
\xi=\mathrm{span}\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}\}
and also
\ast\xi=\mathrm{span}\{e_{k+1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
. We adopt index ranges
1\leq i,j\leq k
and
k+1\leq\overline{i},\overline{j}\leq n
and
1\leq A,B,C\leq n
. In particular,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sec}^{\phi}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}R_{ijji},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sec}^{\ast\phi}(\ast\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bar{i},\bar{j}}R_{\bar{i}\bar{j}\bar{j}\bar{i}},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}_{AB}

\displaystyle=\sum_{C}R_{CABC}\ (=c\delta_{AB}).

The last of these implies
\displaystyle\sum_{i}R_{iABi}=c\delta_{AB}-\sum_{\bar{i}}R_{\bar{i}AB\bar{i}}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sec}^{\phi}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}R_{ijji}=\sum_{j}c\delta_{jj}-\sum_{\bar{i},j}R_{\bar{i}jj\bar{i}}=kc-\sum_{\bar{i},j}R_{j\bar{i}\bar{i}j}=kc-\Big(\sum_{\bar{i}}\delta_{\bar{i}\bar{i}}-\sum_{\bar{i},\bar{j}}R_{\bar{j}\bar{i}\bar{i}\bar{j}}\Big)

\displaystyle=kc-(n-k)c+\mathrm{Sec}^{\ast\phi}(\ast\xi)=(2k-n)c+\mathrm{Sec}^{\ast\phi}(\ast\xi).

∎

Next, we observe that in certain cases of interest, a sign on the
\phi
-sectional curvature implies a sign on the scalar curvature. For example:

Proposition 4.4
.

1.

Let
(X^{7},g_{X},\varphi)
be a
7
-manifold with a
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-structure.

•

If
\mathrm{Sec}^{\varphi}_{X}>0
(resp.,
\geq 0,<0,\leq 0
), then
\mathrm{Scal}_{X}>0
(resp.,
\geq 0,<0,\leq 0
).

•

If
\mathrm{Sec}^{\ast\varphi}_{X}>0
(resp.,
\geq 0,<0,\leq 0
), then
\mathrm{Scal}_{X}>0
(resp.,
\geq 0,<0,\leq 0
).

2.

Let
(X^{8},g_{X},\Phi)
be an
8
-manifold with a
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
-structure. If
\mathrm{Sec}^{\Phi}_{X}>0
(resp.,
\geq 0,<0,\leq 0
), then
\mathrm{Scal}_{X}>0
(resp.,
\geq 0,<0,\leq 0
).

Proof.

We prove part (a). Fix
p\in X
, and let
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{7})
be
\mathrm{G}_{2}
-adapted frame at
p
, so that
\varphi|_{p}=e^{123}+e^{145}+e^{167}+e^{246}-e^{257}-e^{347}-e^{356}
. Then at
p\in X
, we have

\mathrm{Sec}^{\varphi}(e^{123})+\mathrm{Sec}^{\varphi}(e^{145})+\cdots+\mathrm{Sec}(-e^{356})=\sum_{i,j}R_{ijji}=\mathrm{Scal}_{X}(p),

which gives the result. In the case of
\ast\varphi
, the same procedure yields
2\,\mathrm{Scal}_{X}(p)
on the right-hand side. In the
\mathrm{Spin}(7)
case, one obtains
3\,\mathrm{Scal}_{X}(p)
on the right.
∎

4.2
A Schwarz lemma for Smith immersions

We now aim to establish a suitable Bochner formula. For this, let
f\colon(\Sigma^{k},g_{\Sigma})\to(X^{n},g_{X})
be an arbitrary smooth map between Riemannian manifolds. The following formula is well-known:

\frac{1}{2}\Delta|df|^{2}=\left|\nabla df\right|^{2}+\langle\nabla\tau_{2}(f),df\rangle+R^{\Sigma,X}(f),

(4.1)

where here

\displaystyle R^{\Sigma,X}(f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\langle df(\mathrm{Ric}_{\Sigma}e_{i}),df(e_{i})\rangle-\sum_{i,j}R^{X}(df(e_{i}),df(e_{j}),df(e_{j}),df(e_{i})),

and
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}\}
is a local orthonormal frame of
T\Sigma
. When
f
is weakly conformal, we can simplify the curvature term in the following way:

Lemma 4.5
.

If
f\colon(\Sigma^{k},g_{\Sigma})\to(X^{n},g_{X})
is a weakly conformal map, then

R^{\Sigma,X}(f)=\frac{|df|^{2}}{k}\mathrm{Scal}_{\Sigma}-\frac{|df|^{4}}{k^{2}}\sum_{i,j}\mathrm{Sec}_{X}(v_{i}\wedge v_{j}),

(4.2)

where
\{v_{i}\}
is a local orthonormal frame for
df(T\Sigma)\subset f^{*}TX
.

Proof.

Since
f
is weakly conformal, the first term of
R^{\Sigma,X}(f)
is

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\langle df(\mathrm{Ric}_{\Sigma}e_{i}),df(e_{i})\rangle=\sum_{i}(f^{*}g_{X})(\mathrm{Ric}_{\Sigma}e_{i},e_{i})

\displaystyle=\frac{|df|^{2}}{k}\sum_{i}\mathrm{Ric}_{\Sigma}(e_{i},e_{i})=\frac{|df|^{2}}{k}\,\mathrm{Scal}_{\Sigma}.

For the second term, we may take
v_{i}=\frac{1}{\lambda}df(e_{i})
as the orthonormal frame for
df(T\Sigma)\subset f^{*}TX
, where
\lambda=\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}|df|
is the conformal factor. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}R^{X}(df(e_{i}),df(e_{j}),df(e_{j}),df(e_{i}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\left|df(e_{i})\wedge df(e_{j})\right|^{2}\mathrm{Sec}_{X}(df(e_{i})\wedge df(e_{j}))

\displaystyle=\lambda^{4}\sum_{i,j}\mathrm{Sec}_{X}(v_{i}\wedge v_{j})

\displaystyle=\frac{|df|^{4}}{k^{2}}\sum_{i,j}\mathrm{Sec}_{X}(v_{i}\wedge v_{j}).

∎

Specializing further to weakly conformal
k
-harmonic maps, we now prove:

Proposition 4.6

(Bochner formula)
.

Let
f\colon(\Sigma^{k},g_{\Sigma},\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma})\to(X^{n},g_{X},\phi)
be a weakly conformal,
k
-harmonic map, where
\phi\in\Omega^{k}(X)
is a calibration. Then

\frac{k-1}{k^{2}}\Delta|df|^{2k}=|df|^{2k-2}|\nabla df|^{2}+\frac{3k-4}{4}\left|df\right|^{2k-4}\left|\nabla|df|^{2}\right|^{2}+\frac{|df|^{2k}}{k}\,\mathrm{Scal}_{\Sigma}-\frac{|df|^{2k+2}}{k^{2}}\sum_{i,j}\mathrm{Sec}_{X}(v_{i}\wedge v_{j}),

(4.3)

where
\{v_{i}\}
is a local orthonormal frame for
df(T\Sigma)\subset f^{*}TX
. Moreover, if
f
is a Smith immersion, then the last sum is
\sum_{i,j}\mathrm{Sec}_{X}(v_{i}\wedge v_{j})=\mathrm{Sec}^{\phi}_{X}(df(T\Sigma))
.

Proof.

Let
f\colon(\Sigma^{k},g_{\Sigma},\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma})\to(X^{n},g_{X},\phi)
be a weakly conformal,
k
-harmonic map. Applying the chain rule to
(|df|^{2})^{k}
and to
(|df|^{k})^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2k}\Delta|df|^{2k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}|df|^{2k-2}\Delta|df|^{2}+\frac{k-1}{2}|df|^{2k-4}\left|\nabla|df|^{2}\right|^{2}

(4.4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta|df|^{2k}

\displaystyle=|df|^{k}\Delta|df|^{k}+\frac{k^{2}}{4}|df|^{2k-4}\left|\nabla|df|^{2}\right|^{2}.

(4.5)

Substituting the Bochner formula (
4.1
) into equation (
4.4
), we obtain

\frac{1}{2k}\Delta|df|^{2k}=\langle\nabla\tau_{2}(f),|df|^{2k-2}df\rangle+|df|^{2k-2}|\nabla df|^{2}+\frac{k-1}{2}|df|^{2k-4}\left|\nabla|df|^{2}\right|^{2}+|df|^{2k-2}R^{\Sigma,X}(f).

(4.6)

Now, if
p\in\Sigma
is a critical point of
f
(so that
df|_{p}=0
), equation (
4.6
) implies that
(\Delta|df|^{2k})(p)=0
. Therefore, the equation we are trying to prove is trivially true at critical points. So, from now on, we work on the open set
\mathrm{Reg}(f)=\{p\in\Sigma\colon\mathrm{rank}(df_{p})=k\}
. Note that
|df|
is smooth on
\mathrm{Reg}(f)
.

We will calculate the first term in (
4.6
). For this, let
(e_{i})
and
(v_{\alpha})
be local orthonormal frames on
\Sigma
and
X
, respectively, and write
df(e_{i})=f^{\alpha}_{i}v_{\alpha}
. Since
f
is weakly conformal, we have

f^{\alpha}_{i}f^{\alpha}_{j}=\lambda^{2}\delta_{ij}=\frac{1}{k}|df|^{2}\delta_{ij}.

(4.7)

Also, since
f
is
k
-harmonic, equation (
2.4
) gives

|df|^{k-2}\tau_{2}(f)=-\frac{k-2}{2}|df|^{k-4}\langle df,\nabla|df|^{2}\rangle.

(4.8)

By definition,
k
-harmonic maps satisfy
\mathrm{div}(|df|^{k-2}df)=0
. Therefore, applying the Leibniz rule to this equation, we calculate

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\tau_{2}(f),|df|^{k-2}df\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathrm{div}(|df|^{k-2}\langle\tau_{2}(f),df\rangle)

\displaystyle=-\frac{k-2}{2}\,\mathrm{div}\!\left(|df|^{k-4}\left\langle\langle df,\nabla|df|^{2}\rangle,df\right\rangle\right)

(
by
(
4.8
))

\displaystyle=-\frac{k-2}{2}\nabla_{j}\!\left(|df|^{k-4}f^{\alpha}_{i}f^{\alpha}_{j}\nabla_{i}|df|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{k-2}{2k}\nabla_{i}\!\left(|df|^{k-2}\nabla_{i}|df|^{2}\right)

(
by
(
4.7
))

\displaystyle=-\frac{k-2}{k^{2}}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{i}(|df|^{2})^{k/2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{k-2}{k^{2}}\Delta|df|^{k}.

Therefore, the first term in (
4.6
) is

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\tau_{2}(f),|df|^{2k-2}df\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{k-2}{k^{2}}|df|^{k}\Delta|df|^{k}

\displaystyle=-\frac{k-2}{k^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\Delta|df|^{2k}-\frac{k^{2}}{4}|df|^{2k-4}\left|\nabla|df|^{2}\right|^{2}\right)

(
by
(
4.5
))

\displaystyle=-\frac{k-2}{2k^{2}}\Delta|df|^{2k}+\frac{k-2}{4}|df|^{2k-4}\left|\nabla|df|^{2}\right|^{2}.

(4.9)

Finally, substituting (
4.9
) into (
4.6
) and rearranging gives

\frac{k-1}{k^{2}}\Delta|df|^{2k}=|df|^{2k-2}|\nabla df|^{2}+\frac{3k-4}{2}|df|^{2k-4}\left|\nabla|df|^{2}\right|^{2}+|df|^{2k-2}R^{\Sigma,X}(f).

Applying (
4.2
) gives the result.
∎

Now, when
\Sigma
is compact, the above Bochner formula quickly implies the Schwarz lemma. Indeed, the maximum principle implies that a point where
|df|^{2k}
attains its maximum value, we have
\Delta|df|^{2k}\leq 0
. Consequently, we obtain

0\geq\Delta|df|^{2k}\geq|df|^{2k-2}R^{\Sigma,X}(f)=|df|^{2k}\Big(\frac{1}{k}\mathrm{Scal}_{\Sigma}-\frac{|df|^{2}}{k^{2}}\sum_{i,j}\mathrm{Sec}_{X}(v_{i}\wedge v_{j})\Big).

Imposing appropriate curvature bounds then yields an upper bound on
|df|^{2}
.

On the other hand, when
\Sigma
is non-compact, the function
|df|^{2}
need not bounded
a priori
, or the supremum need not be achieved. However, by an application of Yau’s maximum principle, we are able to extend this argument to complete manifolds
(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})
satisfying a lower Ricci curvature bound. We now explain this.

Lemma 4.7

(Yau’s maximum principle
[
34
]
)
.

Let
(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})
be a complete Riemannian manifold with
\mathrm{Ric}_{\Sigma}\geq-E
for some constant
E>0
. If
v\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
is bounded below, then for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a point
p_{\epsilon}\in\Sigma
such that
|(\nabla v)(p_{\epsilon})|<\epsilon
,
(\Delta v)(p_{\epsilon})>-\epsilon
, and
v(p_{\epsilon})<\inf(v)+\epsilon
.

The following result appears to be well-known to experts (see, e.g.,
[
1
, Corollary 3.9]
), but we were unable to locate a proof in the literature. For completeness, we supply a proof.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})
be a complete Riemannian manifold with
\mathrm{Ric}_{\Sigma}\geq-E
for some constant
E>0
. Let
u\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
be a non-negative function. If there exist constants
A\geq 0
,
B>0
,
c>0
such that

\Delta u\geq-Au+Bu^{1+c}
, then
u
is bounded and
u^{c}\leq\frac{A}{B}
.

Proof.

Let
u\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
be a non-negative function for which
\Delta u\geq-Au+Bu^{1+c}
, where
A\geq 0
and
B,c>0
. Let
v=(1+u)^{-a}
, where
0<a<\frac{1}{2}c
. Then
v\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
and
v\geq 0
. Using the shorthand
v^{\prime}:=-a(1+u)^{-a-1}
and
v^{\prime\prime}:=a(1+a)(1+u)^{-a-2}
, we have
\nabla v=v^{\prime}\nabla u
and
\Delta v=v^{\prime}\Delta u+v^{\prime\prime}|\nabla u|^{2}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\Delta u=\frac{1}{v^{\prime}}\Delta v-\frac{v^{\prime\prime}}{v^{\prime}}|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{v^{\prime}}\Delta v-\frac{v^{\prime\prime}}{(v^{\prime})^{3}}|\nabla v|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{a}(1+u)^{a+1}\Delta v+\frac{1+a}{a^{3}}(1+u)^{1+2a}|\nabla v|^{2}.

Now, let
\epsilon>0
. By Yau’s maximum principle (Lemma
4.7
), there exists
p_{\epsilon}\in\Sigma
such that
|(\nabla v)(p_{\epsilon})|<\epsilon
,
(\Delta v)(p_{\epsilon})>-\epsilon
, and
v(p_{\epsilon})<\inf(v)+\epsilon
. Therefore, at the point
p_{\epsilon}
, we have

\displaystyle-Au+Bu^{1+c}\leq\Delta u

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{a}(1+u)^{1+a}\epsilon+\frac{1+a}{a^{3}}(1+u)^{1+2a}\epsilon^{2}

\displaystyle<\epsilon(1+u)^{1+c}\left(\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1+a}{a^{3}}\epsilon\right)\!.

(4.10)

Now, suppose for contradiction that
u
were unbounded. Then
\inf(v)=0
, so
v(p_{\epsilon})<\epsilon
, so
u(p_{\epsilon})>(\frac{1}{\epsilon})^{1/a}-1\to\infty
as
\epsilon\to 0
. Now, the bound (
4.2
) gives

-A\frac{u}{(1+u)^{1+c}}+B\left(\frac{u}{1+u}\right)^{1+c}<\epsilon\left(\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1+a}{a^{3}}\epsilon\right)\!,

(4.11)

so sending
\epsilon\to 0
gives
B\leq 0
, contrary to assumption. Thus,
u
is bounded, so
\sup(u)
is finite. Finally, letting
\epsilon\to 0
in (
4.2
) and noting that
u(p_{\epsilon})\to\sup(u)
, we obtain
-A\sup(u)+B\sup(u)^{1+c}\leq 0
. Rearranging gives
\sup(u)^{c}\leq\frac{A}{B}
, which yields the claim.
∎

We arrive at the main result of this section:

Theorem 4.9

(Schwarz Lemma)
.

Let
f\colon(\Sigma^{k},g_{\Sigma},\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma})\to(X^{n},g_{X},\phi)
be a smooth map,
\phi\in\Omega^{k}(X)
. Suppose that
\Sigma
is complete, and that
\mathrm{Ric}_{\Sigma}\geq-E
for some
E>0
.

1.

Suppose
f
is weakly conformal and
k
-harmonic. If
\mathrm{Scal}_{\Sigma}\geq-S
for
S\geq 0
, and if
\mathrm{Sec}_{X}\leq-B
for
B>0
, then
|df|^{2}\leq\frac{S}{(k-1)B}
.

2.

Suppose
f
is a Smith immersion. If
\mathrm{Scal}_{\Sigma}\geq-S
for
S\geq 0
, and if
\mathrm{Sec}^{\phi}_{X}\leq-A
for
A>0
, then
|df|^{2}\leq\frac{kS}{A}
.

Proof.

Suppose
f\colon\Sigma\to X
is weakly conformal and
k
-harmonic. The Bochner formula of Proposition
4.6
gives

\displaystyle\frac{k-1}{k^{2}}\Delta|df|^{2k}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|df|^{2k}}{k}\,\mathrm{Scal}_{\Sigma}-\frac{|df|^{2k+2}}{k^{2}}\sum_{i,j}\mathrm{Sec}_{X}(v_{i}\wedge v_{j}).

(4.12)

1.

If
\mathrm{Scal}_{\Sigma}\geq-S
and
\mathrm{Sec}_{X}\leq-B
, then the bound (
4.12
) gives

\displaystyle\frac{k-1}{k^{2}}\Delta|df|^{2k}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{S}{k}|df|^{2k}+\frac{B(k-1)}{k}\left(|df|^{2k}\right)^{1+\frac{1}{k}}.

Applying Proposition
4.8
to the function
u=|df|^{2k}
gives the result.

2.

If
\mathrm{Scal}_{\Sigma}\geq-S
and
\mathrm{Sec}^{\phi}_{X}\leq-A
, recalling that
\sum\mathrm{Sec}_{X}(v_{i}\wedge v_{j})=\mathrm{Sec}^{\phi}_{X}(df(T\Sigma))
, we see that (
4.12
) gives

\displaystyle\frac{k-1}{k^{2}}\Delta|df|^{2k}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{S}{k}|df|^{2k}+\frac{A}{k^{2}}(|df|^{2k})^{1+\frac{1}{k}}.

Applying Proposition
4.8
to the function
u=|df|^{2k}
gives the result.

∎

4.3
Consequences

We now draw several consequences from the Schwarz lemma. To do so, we need the following fact about weakly conformal maps.

Lemma 4.10

(Basic distance estimate)
.

Let
f\colon(\Sigma^{k},g_{\Sigma})\to(X^{n},g_{X})
be a weakly conformal map such that
|df|^{2}\leq D
. Then for all
p\in\Sigma
and
w\in T_{p}\Sigma
with
|w|=1
, we have
\left|df_{p}(w)\right|\leq\sqrt{\frac{D}{k}}
. Moreover,

\mathrm{dist}_{X}(f(p),f(q))\leq\sqrt{\frac{D}{k}}\,\mathrm{dist}_{\Sigma}(p,q),\ \ \forall p,q\in\Sigma.

Proof.

Let
f\colon\Sigma\to X
be weakly conformal, fix
p\in\Sigma
, and let
\lambda(p)
denote the conformal factor of
f
at
p
. Then for all
w\in T_{p}\Sigma
with
|w|=1
, we have (using (
2.1
))
|df_{p}(w)|=\lambda(p)|w|=\lambda(p)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}|df_{p}|\leq\sqrt{\frac{D}{k}}
. Now, fix
p,q\in\Sigma
, and consider the following two sets of piecewise-smooth curve segments:

\displaystyle C_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle=\left\{\widetilde{\gamma}\colon[0,1]\to\Sigma\colon\widetilde{\gamma}(0)=p,\widetilde{\gamma}(1)=q\right\}

\displaystyle C_{X}

\displaystyle=\left\{\gamma\colon[0,1]\to X\colon\gamma(0)=f(p),\gamma(1)=f(q)\right\}\!.

Notice that
C_{X}
contains the class of curve segments
\{f\circ\widetilde{\gamma}\colon[0,1]\to X\mid\widetilde{\gamma}\in C_{\Sigma}\}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}_{X}(f(p),f(q))=\inf_{\gamma\in C_{X}}\int_{0}^{1}|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|_{X}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\widetilde{\gamma}\in C_{\Sigma}}\int_{0}^{1}\left|(f\circ\widetilde{\gamma})^{\prime}(t)\right|_{X}\,dt

\displaystyle=\inf_{\widetilde{\gamma}\in C_{\Sigma}}\int_{0}^{1}\lambda(\widetilde{\gamma}(t))\left|\widetilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(t)\right|_{\Sigma}dt

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{D}{k}}\,\mathrm{dist}_{\Sigma}(p,q).

∎

Corollary 4.11

(Distance estimates)
.

Let
f\colon(\Sigma^{k},g_{\Sigma},\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma})\to(X^{n},g_{X},\phi)
be a smooth map,
\phi\in\Omega^{k}(X)
, where
\Sigma
is complete and
\mathrm{Ric}_{\Sigma}\geq-E
for some
E>0
.

1.

Suppose
f
is
k
-harmonic and weakly conformal. If
\mathrm{Scal}_{\Sigma}\geq-S
for
S\geq 0
, and if
\mathrm{Sec}_{X}\leq-B
for
B>0
, then

\mathrm{dist}_{X}(f(p),f(q))\leq\sqrt{\frac{S}{k(k-1)B}}\,\mathrm{dist}_{\Sigma}(p,q),\ \ \forall p,q\in\Sigma.

2.

Suppose
f
is a Smith immersion. If
\mathrm{Scal}_{\Sigma}\geq-S
for
S\geq 0
, and if
\mathrm{Sec}^{\phi}_{X}\leq-A
for
A>0
, then

\mathrm{dist}_{X}(f(p),f(q))\leq\sqrt{\frac{S}{A}}\,\mathrm{dist}_{\Sigma}(p,q),\ \ \forall p,q\in\Sigma.

Proof.

1.

The Schwarz Lemma
4.9
(a) gives
|df|^{2}\leq\frac{S}{(k-1)B}
. Now apply the basic distance estimate
4.10
.

2.

The Schwarz Lemma
4.9
(b) gives
|df|^{2}\leq\frac{kS}{A}
. Now apply the basic distance estimate
4.10
.

∎

It is well-known that if a Kähler manifold
X
has holomorphic sectional curvature bounded above by a negative constant, then
X
is Brody hyperbolic. We now generalize this fact to arbitrary calibrated geometries:

Theorem 4.12

(Negative
\phi
-curvature implies
\phi
-hyperbolicity)
.

Let
(X,g_{X},\phi)
be a calibrated manifold. If
\mathrm{Sec}^{\phi}_{X}\leq-A
for some
A>0
, then
X
is
\phi
-hyperbolic.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathrm{Sec}^{\phi}_{X}\leq-A
for some
A>0
. If
f\colon(\mathbb{R}^{k},g_{0},\mathrm{vol}_{0})\to(X^{n},g_{X},\phi)
is a Smith immersion, Corollary
4.11
(b) implies that
\mathrm{dist}_{X}(f(p),f(q))=0
for all
p,q\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, so
f
is constant.
∎

Corollary 4.13
.

Let
(X^{n},g_{X},\mathrm{vol}_{X})
be an oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold. If
\mathrm{Scal}_{X}\leq-A
for some
A>0
, then
X
is
\mathrm{vol}_{X}
-hyperbolic (i.e., every orientation-preserving, weakly conformal map
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{0})\to(X^{n},g_{X})
is constant).

Proof.

This follows from Corollary
4.12
and Example
4.2
.
∎

In a first course in complex analysis, one typically learns the Schwarz lemma as a pointwise bound on holomorphic functions
f\colon B^{2}(R_{1})\to B^{2}(R_{2})
satisfying
f(0)=0
. This is a special case of the following:

Proposition 4.14

(Classic Schwarz Lemma)
.

Let
f\colon(B^{k}(R_{1}),g_{R_{1}})\to(B^{n}(R_{2}),g_{R_{2}})
be a weakly conformal,
k
-harmonic map.

1.

For all
w\in T_{0}B^{k}
with
|w|=1
, we have
|df_{0}(w)|_{R_{2}}\leq\frac{R_{2}}{R_{1}}
.

2.

If
f(0)=0
, then
\left|f(x)\right|\leq\frac{R_{2}}{R_{1}}|x|
for all
x\in B^{k}(R_{1})
. (Here,
|\cdot|=|\cdot|_{0}
is the euclidean norm.)

Proof.

Before beginning, we note that
\mathrm{Scal}_{B^{k}(R_{1})}=-\frac{4}{R_{1}^{2}}k(k-1)
and that
\mathrm{Sec}_{B^{n}(R_{2})}=-\frac{4}{R_{2}^{2}}
.

1.

The Schwarz Lemma
4.9
(a) with
S=\frac{4}{R_{1}^{2}}k(k-1)
and
B=\frac{4}{R_{2}^{2}}
implies that
|df|^{2}\leq k\frac{R_{2}^{2}}{R_{1}^{2}}
. Now the basic distance estimate
4.10
with
D=k\frac{R_{2}^{2}}{R_{1}^{2}}
gives the result.

2.

Let
x\in B^{k}(R_{1})
. The distance estimate of Corollary
4.11
(a) with
S=\frac{4}{R_{1}^{2}}k(k-1)
and
B=\frac{4}{R_{2}^{2}}
gives

\mathrm{dist}_{R_{2}}(f(x),f(0))\leq\frac{R_{2}}{R_{1}}\,\mathrm{dist}_{R_{1}}(x,0).

Recalling the explicit formula for the Poincaré distance (Proposition
2.26
(d)), and using that
f(0)=0
, we obtain

\mathrm{arcsinh}\sqrt{\frac{|f(x)|^{2}}{R_{2}^{2}-|f(x)|^{2}}}\leq\mathrm{arcsinh}\sqrt{\frac{|x|^{2}}{R_{1}^{2}-|x|^{2}}}.

Since
h(t)=\mathrm{arcsinh}(\sqrt{t})
is an increasing function, it follows that

\frac{|f(x)|^{2}}{R_{2}^{2}-|f(x)|^{2}}\leq\frac{|x|^{2}}{R_{1}^{2}-|x|^{2}}.

Rearranging yields
|f(x)|\leq\frac{R_{2}}{R_{1}}|x|
as claimed.

∎

5
The KR
\phi
-metric

Our next goal is to generalize the Kobayashi-Royden pseudo-metric
F_{X}
(recall (
1.1
)) to arbitrary calibrated geometries. In
\lx@sectionsign
5.1
, for each calibrated manifold
(X,g_{X},\phi)
, we define a Finsler pseudo-metric
\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}\colon TX\to[0,\infty]
called the
KR
\phi
-metric
. A fundamental fact about
\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}
is its “decreasing property” with respect to conformally calibrating maps (Proposition
5.3
).

In practice, deriving an explicit formula for
\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}
is highly non-trivial. Even in the Kähler case, the Kobayashi-Royden pseudo-metric
F_{X}=\mathcal{K}_{(X,\omega)}
has been calculated in only a small handful of cases, such as complex hyperbolic space
X=\mathbb{CH}^{n}
. The main results of this section (Theorems
5.9
and
5.11
) are explicit formulas for the KR
\mathrm{vol}_{1}
-metric of real hyperbolic space
\mathbb{RH}^{n}=(B^{n},g_{1})
and the KR
\Psi
-metric of quaternionic hyperbolic space
\mathbb{HH}^{n}
. The latter calculation makes crucial use of the Schwarz Lemma
4.9
(b).

5.1
The KR
\phi
-metric

Let
(X^{n},g_{X},\phi)
be a calibrated
n
-manifold, where
\phi\in\Omega^{k}(X)
. Regard the unit
k
-ball
B^{k}:=B^{k}(1)
as the calibrated manifold
(B^{k},g_{1},\mathrm{vol}_{1})
, where
g_{1}
is the Poincaré metric and
\mathrm{vol}_{1}\in\Omega^{k}(B^{k})
is the corresponding volume form as discussed in
\lx@sectionsign
2.4
. Let

\mathrm{SmIm}(B^{k},X)=\{\text{Smith immersions }f\colon(B^{k},g_{1},\mathrm{vol}_{1})\to(X^{n},g_{X},\phi)\}.

Definition 5.1
.

The
KR
\phi
-metric
on
(X,g_{X})
is the function

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}\colon TX

\displaystyle\to[0,\infty]

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}(v_{p})

\displaystyle=\inf\!\left\{a>0\colon\exists f\in\mathrm{SmIm}(B^{k},X),w\in T_{0}B^{k},|w|=1\text{ s.t. }f(0)=p,\,df_{0}(w)=\frac{1}{a}v\right\}\!.

If there does not exist a Smith immersion
f\colon B^{k}\to X
, a unit vector
w\in T_{0}B^{k}
, and an
a>0
such that
f(0)=p
and
df_{0}(w)=\frac{1}{a}v
, then
\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}(v)=\infty
. We typically use the abbreviation
\mathcal{K}_{\phi}=\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}
.

Geometrically,
\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}(v)
can be viewed as the reciprocal of the largest conformal factor that a Smith
k
-disk having
v\in T_{p}X
as a tangent vector can have at the point
p
. When
(X,g_{X},\omega)
is a Kähler manifold, the KR
\omega
-metric coincides with the Kobayashi-Royden pseudo-metric (
1.1
). As such, we now show that many familiar properties extend to arbitrary calibrated manifolds.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
(X,g_{X},\phi)
be a calibrated manifold, and let
v\in TX
. Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\phi}\colon\mathrm{Gr}(\phi)

\displaystyle\to[0,\infty]

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\phi}(\xi_{p})

\displaystyle=\inf\!\left\{\frac{1}{\|df_{0}\|}\colon f\in\mathrm{SmIm}(B^{k},X),f(0)=p,\,df_{0}(T_{0}B^{k})=\xi\right\}\!.

Then we have the formula

\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(v)=\left|v\right|\,\inf\!\left\{\mathcal{W}_{\phi}(\xi)\colon\xi\in\mathrm{Gr}(\phi)\text{ s.t. }v\in\xi\right\}\!.

Consequently,
\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(av)=\left|a\right|\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(v)
for all
a\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Fix
v\in T_{p}X
. Let
\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{\phi}(v)=\left|v\right|\,\inf\!\left\{\mathcal{W}_{\phi}(\xi)\colon\xi\in\mathrm{Gr}(\phi)\text{ s.t. }v\in\xi\right\}
. We aim to show that
\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(v)=\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{\phi}(v)
.

Suppose first that
v=0
. Then clearly
\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{\phi}(v)=0
. On the other hand, letting
f\colon(B^{k},g_{1})\to(X,g_{X})
denote the constant map
f(x)=p
, we see that every
w\in T_{0}B^{k}
with
|w|=1
satisfies
df_{0}(w)=0
, and so
\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(v)=0
, too.

Suppose now that
v\neq 0
and
\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(v)
is finite. Fix
\epsilon>0
. Then there exists a Smith immersion
f\colon(B^{k},g_{1})\to(X,g_{X})
and
w\in T_{0}B^{k}
with
|w|=1
such that
f(0)=p
and
df_{0}(w)=\frac{1}{a}v
with
0<a<\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(v)+\epsilon
. Note that since
f
is conformal, we have
\|df_{0}\|=\|df_{0}\||w|=|df_{0}(w)|=\frac{1}{a}|v|
. Moreover, since
v\neq 0
, the map
df_{0}\colon T_{0}B^{k}\to T_{p}X
is injective, so
\xi:=df_{0}(T_{0}B^{k})\in\mathrm{Gr}(\phi)
and clearly
v\in\xi
. Therefore,

\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{\phi}(v)\leq|v|\,\mathcal{W}_{\phi}(\xi)\leq|v|\frac{1}{\|df_{0}\|}=a<\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(v)+\epsilon.

Letting
\epsilon\to 0
shows that
\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{\phi}(v)\leq\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(v)
.

For the other direction, again assuming
\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{\phi}(v)
is finite, fix
\epsilon>0
. Then there exists
\xi\in\mathrm{Gr}(\phi)
with
v\in\xi
such that
|v|\,\mathcal{W}_{\phi}(\xi)<\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{\phi}(v)+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon
. Moreover, there exists a Smith immersion
f\colon(B^{k},g_{1})\to(X,g_{X})
having
f(0)=p
and
df_{0}(T_{0}B^{k})=\xi
such that
\frac{1}{\|df_{0}\|}<\mathcal{W}_{\phi}(\xi)+\frac{1}{2|v|}\epsilon
. Since
v\in\xi
, there exists
w\in T_{0}B^{k}
with
|w|=1
and
a>0
such that
df_{0}(w)=\frac{1}{a}v
. Again since
f
is conformal, we have
\|df_{0}\|=\frac{1}{a}|v|
. Therefore,

\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(v)\leq a=|v|\frac{1}{\|df_{0}\|}<|v|\mathcal{W}_{\phi}(\xi)+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon<\widehat{\mathcal{K}_{\phi}}(v)+\epsilon.

Letting
\epsilon\to 0
gives
\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(v)\leq\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{\phi}(v)+\epsilon
. In fact, we have shown that
\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(v)
is finite if and only if
\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{\phi}(v)
is finite. Consequently,
\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(v)=\infty
if and only if
\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{\phi}(v)=\infty
.

Finally, the last claim follows from observing that
\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(av)=\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{\phi}(av)=\left|a\right|\widehat{\mathcal{K}}_{\phi}(v)=|a|\,\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(v)
.
∎

Proposition 5.3

(Decreasing property)
.

Let
f\colon(X^{m},g_{X},\alpha)\to(Y^{n},g_{Y},\beta)
be a conformally calibrating map. Then for all
p\in X
and
v\in T_{p}X
, we have

\mathcal{K}_{(Y,\beta)}(df_{p}(v))\leq\mathcal{K}_{(X,\alpha)}(v).

Proof.

Fix
p\in X
and
v\in T_{p}X
. Consider the sets

\displaystyle S_{X}

\displaystyle=\left\{a>0\colon\exists F\in\mathrm{SmIm}(B^{k},X^{m}),w\in T_{0}B^{k},|w|=1\text{ s.t. }F(0)=p,\,dF_{0}(w)=\frac{1}{a}v\right\}

\displaystyle S_{Y}

\displaystyle=\left\{b>0\colon\exists G\in\mathrm{SmIm}(B^{k},Y^{n}),w\in T_{0}B^{k},|w|=1\text{ s.t. }G(0)=f(p),\,dG_{0}(w)=\frac{1}{b}df_{p}(v)\right\}\!.

We claim that
S_{X}\subset S_{Y}
. For this, let
a\in S_{X}
, so there exists a Smith immersion
F\colon(B^{k},g_{1},\mathrm{vol}_{1})\to(X^{m},g_{X},\alpha)
and
w\in T_{0}B^{k}
with
|w|=1
such that
F(0)=p
and
dF_{0}(w)=\frac{1}{a}v
. Setting
G:=f\circ F\colon(B^{k},g_{1},\mathrm{vol}_{1})\to(Y^{n},g_{Y},\beta)
, we observe that
G
is a Smith immersion having
G(0)=f(F(0))=f(p)
and
dG_{0}(w)=df_{p}(dF_{0}(w))=\frac{1}{a}df_{p}(v)
. This shows that
a\in S_{Y}
, which proves the claim. Finally, since
S_{X}\subset S_{Y}
, it follows that
\mathcal{K}_{(Y,\beta)}(df_{p}(v))=\inf(S_{Y})\leq\inf(S_{X})=\mathcal{K}_{(X,\alpha)}(v)
.
∎

Corollary 5.4

(Comparison principle)
.

Let
(X,g_{X},\phi)
be a calibrated manifold, and let
U\subset X
be an open set. Then
\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}(v)\leq\mathcal{K}_{(U,\phi)}(v)
for all
v\in TU
.

Proof.

Let
\iota\colon(U,g_{X},\phi)\hookrightarrow(X,g_{X},\phi)
be the inclusion map. Then
\iota
is a conformally calibrating map. So, for all
v\in TU
, Proposition
5.3
gives
\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}(v)=\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}(d\iota_{p}(v))\leq\mathcal{K}_{(U,\phi)}(v)
.
∎

Corollary 5.5

(Invariance)
.

Let
(X,g_{X},\phi)
,
(Y,g_{Y},\psi)
be a calibrated manifolds. If
F\colon X\to Y
is a Smith equivalence, then for all
p\in X
and
v\in T_{p}X
, we have:

\mathcal{K}_{(Y,\psi)}(dF_{p}(v))=\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}(v).

Proof.

Let
F\colon(X,g_{X},\phi)\to(Y,g_{Y},\psi)
be a Smith equivalence. Fix
p\in X
and
v\in T_{p}X
. Set
q=F(p)
. Using Proposition
5.3
twice, observing that
F^{-1}
is also a Smith equivalence, we have

\mathcal{K}_{(Y,\psi)}(dF_{p}(v))\leq\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}(v)=\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}(dF^{-1}_{q}(dF_{p}(v)))\leq\mathcal{K}_{(Y,\psi)}(dF_{p}(v)).

∎

5.2
Examples

Example 5.6

(Kähler calibration)
.

In general, an explicit formula for the Kobayashi-Royden pseudometric of a complex manifold is difficult to find. Let
X,Y
be complex manifolds.

•

On the unit disk
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
, the Kobayashi-Royden metric is given by the (square root of the) Poincaré metric:
\mathcal{K}_{(\mathbb{D},\omega)}(v_{z})=\frac{|v|}{1-|z|^{2}}
. More generally, on the unit ball
B^{2n}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\colon\sum_{k}|z_{k}|^{2}<1\}
, the Kobayashi–Royden metric is given by

\mathcal{K}_{(B^{2n},\omega)}(v_{z})=\frac{\sqrt{(1-|z|^{2})|v|^{2}+|\langle z,v\rangle|^{2}}}{1-|z|^{2}}

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and
|\cdot|
denote the standard Hermitian inner product on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

•

For convex domains, which includes the Hermitian symmetric spaces of non-compact type, the Kobayashi-Royden metric coincides with the Carathéodory-Reiffen metric
[
25
]
.

•

We have the following formula for products:
\mathcal{K}_{X\times Y}(u,v)=\max\{\mathcal{K}_{X}(u),\mathcal{K}_{Y}(v)\}
for
u\in TX,v\in TY
(see, e.g.,
[
24
, Proposition 3.5.25]
).

•

If
\pi\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
is a covering space, then
\mathcal{K}_{(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{\omega})}=\pi^{\ast}\mathcal{K}_{(X,\omega)}
(see, e.g.,
[
24
, Proposition 3.5.26]
).

Proposition 5.7

(Euclidean balls)
.

Let
\phi\in\Lambda^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{*}
be a constant-coefficient elliptic calibration.

1.

Consider the calibrated manifold
(B(p;r),g_{0},\phi)
, where
B(p;r)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon|x-p|<r\}
. For all
y\in B(p;r)
and
v\in T_{y}B(p;r)
, we have
\mathcal{K}_{(B(p;r),\phi)}(v)\leq\frac{1}{r-|y-p|}|v|
.

2.

Consider the calibrated manifold
(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{0},\phi)
. We have
\mathcal{K}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n},\phi)}(v)=0
for all
v\in T\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

(a) Let
y\in B(p;r)
and
v\in T_{y}\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
v=0
, the claim is clear, so assume
v\neq 0
. Since
\phi
is elliptic, there exists a
\phi
-calibrated plane
\Pi\subset T_{y}\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
v\in\Pi
. Identifying
T_{y}\mathbb{R}^{n}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we regard
\Pi
as an affine
\phi
-plane through
y
containing the vector
v
, and we let
\{\frac{v}{|v|},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k}\}
be an orthonormal basis for
\Pi
. Now, for each
a>\frac{|v|}{r-|y-p|}
, let
f_{a}\colon(\mathbb{R}^{k},g_{0},\mathrm{vol}_{0})\to(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{0},\phi)
be

f_{a}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})=y+\frac{|v|}{a}\left(x_{1}\frac{v}{|v|}+\sum_{j=2}^{k}x_{j}v_{j}\right)\!.

Note that
f_{a}
is a Smith immersion whose image is
\Pi
.

Now, let
\iota\colon(B^{k},g_{1},\mathrm{vol}_{1})\to(\mathbb{R}^{k},g_{0},\mathrm{vol}_{0})
be the inclusion map, and consider the map
F_{a}:=f_{a}\circ\iota\colon(B^{k},g_{1},\mathrm{vol}_{1})\to(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{0},\phi)
. Note that
F_{a}
is a Smith immersion with
F_{a}(0)=y
and
(dF_{a})|_{0}(e_{1})=\frac{1}{a}v
, and
\mathrm{Image}(F_{a})\subset B(y;\frac{|v|}{a})\subset B(y;r-|y-p|)\subset B(p;r)
. This shows that
\mathcal{K}_{(B(p;r),\phi)}(v)\leq\frac{1}{r-|y-p|}|v|
.

(b) The same argument applies, but now there is no restriction on
a>0
.
∎

Example 5.8

(Volume form calibration)
.

Let
(X,g_{X},\mathrm{vol}_{X})
be a calibrated
n
-manifold equipped with the volume form calibration
\mathrm{vol}_{X}\in\Omega^{n}(X)
. If
(X,g_{X})
is not locally conformally flat, then
\mathcal{K}_{(X,\mathrm{vol}_{X})}(v)=\infty
for all
v\in TX
,
v\neq 0
.

Proof.

We prove the contrapositive. Let
p\in X
and
v\in T_{p}X
,
v\neq 0
be such that
\mathcal{K}_{(X,\mathrm{vol}_{X})}(v)<\infty
. Then there exists a Smith immersion
f\colon(B^{n},g_{1},\mathrm{vol}_{1})\to(X^{n},g_{X},\mathrm{vol}_{X})
, a vector
w\in T_{0}B^{n}
with
|w|=1
, and
a>0
such that
f(0)=p
and
df_{0}(w)=\frac{1}{a}v
. Noting that
f
is strict at
0\in B^{n}
, there exists a neighborhood
U\subset B^{n}
of
0
for which
f|_{U}\colon(U,g_{1},\mathrm{vol}_{1})\to(X^{n},g_{X},\mathrm{vol}_{X})
is a conformal diffeomorphism. Thus,
(X,g_{X})
is locally conformally flat.
∎

We now compute the KR
\mathrm{vol}_{1}
-metric of real hyperbolic space
(B^{n},g_{1})
.

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
(B^{n},g_{1})
be the unit
n
-ball with its Poincaré metric, and equip it with the volume form calibration
\mathrm{vol}_{1}\in\Omega^{n}(B^{n})
. Then for all
p\in B^{n}
and
v\in T_{p}B^{n}
, we have

\mathcal{K}_{(B^{n},\mathrm{vol}_{1})}(v)=|v|_{1}.

Proof.

Suppose first that
p=0
, and fix
v\in T_{0}B^{k}
. If
v=0
, the result is clear, so assume
v\neq 0
. Notice that the identity map
\mathrm{Id}\colon(B^{n},g_{1},\mathrm{vol}_{1})\to(B^{n},g_{1},\mathrm{vol}_{1})
is a Smith immersion with
\mathrm{Id}(0)=0
and
d\,\mathrm{Id}_{0}(\frac{v}{|v|_{1}})=\frac{1}{|v|_{1}}v
. This shows that
\mathcal{K}_{(B^{n},\mathrm{vol}_{1})}(v)\leq|v|_{1}
.

In the other direction, for each
a>0
, let
f\colon(B^{n},g_{1},\mathrm{vol}_{1})\to(B^{n},g_{1},\mathrm{vol}_{1})
be a Smith immersion, and let
w\in T_{0}B^{k}
with
|w|=1
be such that
f(0)=0
and
df_{0}(w)=\frac{1}{a}v
. By the classic Schwarz lemma (Proposition
4.14
(a)), we have
\frac{1}{a}|v|_{1}=|df_{0}(w)|_{1}\leq 1
, so that
|v|_{1}\leq a
. Therefore, we deduce that
|v|_{1}\leq\mathcal{K}_{(B^{n},\mathrm{vol}_{1})}(v)
. This proves the result at the origin
p=0
.

Finally, let
p\in B^{n}
be arbitrary, and let
v\in T_{p}B^{n}
. The Smith automorphisms of
(g_{1},\mathrm{vol}_{1})
coincide with the orientation-preserving conformal automorphisms of
g_{1}
. Since this group acts transitively on
B^{n}
(Proposition
2.26
), there exists a Smith automorphism
F\colon(B^{n},g_{1})\to(B^{n},g_{1})
such that
F(p)=0
. Now, using invariance (Corollary
5.5
), followed by the result for
p=0
, followed by the fact that conformal automorphisms of
B^{n}
are isometries for the Poincaré metric (Proposition
2.26
), we have

\mathcal{K}_{(B^{n},\mathrm{vol}_{1})}(v)=\mathcal{K}_{(B^{n},\mathrm{vol}_{1})}(dF_{p}(v))=\left|dF_{p}(v)\right|_{1}=\left|v\right|_{1}.

∎

Corollary 5.10
.

Let
f\colon(B^{k},g_{1},\mathrm{vol}_{1})\to(X^{n},g_{X},\phi)
be a Smith immersion,
\phi\in\Omega^{k}(X)
. For all
p\in B^{k}
and
v\in T_{p}B^{k}
, we have

\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}(df_{p}(v))\leq|v|_{1}.

Proof.

Combine Proposition
5.3
and Theorem
5.9
.
∎

We now calculate the KR
\Psi
-metric of quaternionic hyperbolic space
\mathbb{HH}^{n}
, where
\Psi
is the QK
4
-form. Our convention is that
\mathbb{HH}^{n}
has sectional curvature between
-4
and
-1
.

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
(\mathbb{HH}^{n},g_{\mathbb{HH}^{n}})
be quaternionic hyperbolic space, and let
\Psi\in\Omega^{4}(\mathbb{HH}^{n})
be the quaternionic-Kähler calibration. Then for all
p\in\mathbb{HH}^{n}
and
v\in T_{p}\mathbb{HH}^{n}
, we have

\mathcal{K}_{(\mathbb{HH}^{n},\Psi)}(v)=\left|v\right|_{\mathbb{HH}^{n}},

where
|v|_{\mathbb{HH}^{n}}=\sqrt{g_{\mathbb{HH}^{n}}(v,v)}
is the norm arising from the Riemannian metric.

Proof.

Suppose first that
p=0
, and fix
v\in T_{0}B^{k}
. If
v=0
, the result is clear, so assume
v\neq 0
.

Let
\iota\colon\mathbb{HH}^{1}\to(\mathbb{HH}^{n},g_{\mathbb{HH}^{n}},\Psi)
be the standard inclusion of a quaternionic hyperbolic line through
0
. Note that the image is isometric to
(B^{4}(1),g_{1})
and is
\Psi
-calibrated. Therefore, equipping
\mathbb{HH}^{1}
with the pullback metric and
4
-form
(\iota^{*}g_{\mathbb{HH}^{n}},\iota^{*}\Psi)=(g_{\mathbb{HH}^{1}},\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{HH}^{1}})\cong(g_{1},\mathrm{vol}_{1})
makes
\iota
into an isometric Smith immersion. Moreover, with our choice of metric conventions, we have
|v|_{\mathbb{HH}^{n}}=|v|_{1}
for vectors
v\in T_{0}\mathbb{HH}^{n}\cong T_{0}B^{4}
, and so
d\iota_{0}(\frac{v}{|v|_{1}})=\frac{1}{|v|_{\mathbb{HH}^{n}}}v
. This shows that
\mathcal{K}_{(\mathbb{HH}^{n},\Psi)}(v)\leq|v|_{\mathbb{HH}^{n}}
.

In the other direction, for
a>0
, let
f\colon(B^{4},g_{1},\mathrm{vol}_{1})\to(\mathbb{HH}^{n},g_{\mathbb{HH}^{n}},\Psi)
be a Smith immersion, and let
w\in T_{0}B^{4}
with
|w|=1
be such that
f(0)=0
and
df_{0}(w)=\frac{1}{a}w
. Note that
S:=-\mathrm{Scal}_{(B^{4},g_{1})}=48
and
A:=-\mathrm{Sec}^{\Psi}_{\mathbb{HH}^{n}}=48
. Therefore, the Schwarz Lemma of Theorem
4.9
(b) implies that
|df|^{2}\leq 4
, so the basic distance estimate
4.10
gives
\frac{1}{a}|v|_{\mathbb{HH}^{n}}=|df_{0}(w)|\leq 1
. Thus, we deduce that
|v|_{\mathbb{HH}^{n}}\leq\mathcal{K}_{(\mathbb{HH}^{n},\Psi)}(v)
. This proves the result at
p=0
.

Finally, let
p\in\mathbb{HH}^{n}
be arbitrary, and let
v\in T_{p}\mathbb{HH}^{n}
. Recalling that
\mathbb{HH}^{n}=\mathrm{Sp}(n,1)/(\mathrm{Sp}(n)\times\mathrm{Sp}(1))
, there exists
A\in\mathrm{Sp}(n,1)
such that
A\cdot p=0
, where
\cdot
denotes the
\mathrm{Sp}(n,1)
-action on
\mathbb{HH}^{n}
. The map
F\colon\mathbb{HH}^{n}\to\mathbb{HH}^{n}
given by
F(x)=A\cdot x
satisfies
F(p)=0
and is a Gray automorphism (i.e., an isometric Smith automorphism). Thus, using invariance (Corollary
5.5
), followed by the result for
p=0
, followed by the fact that Gray automorphisms are isometries, we have

\mathcal{K}_{(\mathbb{HH}^{n},\Psi)}(v)=\mathcal{K}_{(\mathbb{HH}^{n},\Psi)}(dF_{p}(v))=\left|dF_{p}(v)\right|_{\mathbb{HH}^{n}}=|v|_{\mathbb{HH}^{n}}.

∎

6
R_{\phi}
-hyperbolicity and
K_{\phi}
-hyperbolicity

In this section, we will use the KR
\phi
-metric to define two notions of hyperbolicity, both of which generalize Kobayashi hyperbolicity. The first, called
R_{\phi}
-hyperbolicity, is defined by a lower bound on the KR
\phi
-metric. In
\lx@sectionsign
6.1
, we will prove that strongly negative
\phi
-sectional curvature implies
R_{\phi}
-hyperbolicity, which in turn implies
\phi
-hyperbolicity (Theorem
6.3
). We will also provide examples of
\phi
-hyperbolic domains that are not
R_{\phi}
-hyperbolic (Theorem
6.6
).

In
\lx@sectionsign
6.2
, we extend the Kobayashi pseudo-distance to calibrated manifolds
(X,g_{X},\phi)
having the property that its KR
\phi
-metric
\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}\colon TX\to[0,\infty]
is upper semicontinuous. We shall say that
X
is
K_{\phi}
-hyperbolic if the pseudo-distance obtained from integrating
\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}
is a genuine distance function. In Theorem
6.23
, we shall prove that (under the semicontinuity assumption)
R_{\phi}
-hyperbolicity implies
K_{\phi}
-hyperbolicity, and that
K_{\phi}
-hyperbolicity implies
\phi
-hyperbolicity.

6.1
R_{\phi}
-hyperbolicity

Definition 6.1
.

Let
(X,g_{X},\phi)
be a calibrated manifold. Say
(X,g_{X})
is
R_{\phi}
-hyperbolic
if for each
p\in X
, there exists a neighborhood
U\subset X
of
p
and a constant
c>0
such that
\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}(v)\geq c\left|v\right|_{X}
for all
v\in TU
.

When
(X,g,\omega)
is a Kähler manifold, Royden
[
31
]
proved that
R_{\omega}
-hyperbolicity is equivalent to Kobayashi hyperbolicity.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
(X,g_{X},\phi)
be a calibrated manifold,
\phi\in\Omega^{k}(X)
.

1.

Suppose
(X,g_{X},\phi)
is Smith equivalent to
(Y,g_{Y},\psi)
. Then
(X,g_{X})
is
R_{\phi}
-hyperbolic if and only if
(Y,g_{Y})
is
R_{\psi}
-hyperbolic.

2.

Let
U\subset X
be an open subset. If
(X,g_{X})
is
R_{\phi}
-hyperbolic, then
(U,g_{X}|_{U})
is
R_{\phi}
-hyperbolic.

Proof.

1.

Suppose
(X,g_{X})
is
R_{\phi}
-hyperbolic. Let
F\colon(X,g_{X},\phi)\to(Y,g_{Y},\psi)
be a Smith equivalence, and let
\lambda\colon X\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be the conformal factor. Fix
p\in Y
. By
R_{\phi}
-hyperbolicity, there is a neighborhood
U\subset X
of
F^{-1}(p)
and
c>0
such that
\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}(w)\geq c|w|_{X}
for all
w\in TU
. By shrinking
U
if necessary, we can assume that
\overline{U}
is compact, so that
\sup_{\overline{U}}\lambda
is finite. Now, for all
v\in T(F(U))
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{(Y,\psi)}(v)=\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}(dF^{-1}(v))\geq c\left|dF^{-1}(v)\right|_{X}=\frac{c}{\lambda}\left|v\right|_{Y}\geq\frac{c}{\sup_{\overline{U}}\lambda}\left|v\right|_{Y}.

2.

This follows immediately from the Comparison Principle (Corollary
5.4
).

∎

In the Kähler case, it is well-known that strongly negative holomorphic sectional curvature implies Kobayashi hyperbolicity, and that Kobayashi hyperbolicity implies Brody hyperbolicity. We now show that both of these implications hold in general:

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
(X,g_{X},\phi)
be a calibrated manifold.

1.

If
\mathrm{Sec}^{\phi}_{X}\leq-A
for some
A>0
, then
(X,g_{X})
is
R_{\phi}
-hyperbolic.

2.

If
(X,g_{X})
is
R_{\phi}
-hyperbolic, then
(X,g_{X})
is
\phi
-hyperbolic.

Proof.

1.

Suppose
\mathrm{Sec}^{\phi}_{X}\leq-A
for some
A>0
. Let
p\in X
, let
v\in T_{p}X
have
v\neq 0
, and let
\xi\in\mathrm{Gr}(\phi)
have
v\in\xi
. By the Schwarz Lemma of Theorem
4.9
(b), every Smith immersion
f\colon(B^{k},g_{1},\mathrm{vol}_{1})\to(X,g_{X},\phi)
with
f(0)=p
and
df_{0}(T_{0}B^{k})=\xi
satisfies
\|df_{0}\|^{2}=\frac{1}{k}|df_{0}|^{2}\leq\frac{4k(k-1)}{A}
, and hence
\frac{1}{\|df_{0}\|}\geq\sqrt{\frac{A}{4k(k-1)}}
. Consequently,
\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}(v)\geq\sqrt{\frac{A}{4k(k-1)}}\,|v|_{X}
, and thus
X
is
R_{\phi}
-hyperbolic.

2.

Suppose
X
is
R_{\phi}
-hyperbolic. Let
f\colon(\mathbb{R}^{k},g_{0},\mathrm{vol}_{0})\to(X,g_{X},\phi)
be a Smith immersion. Fix
p\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
v\in T_{p}\mathbb{R}^{k}
. By assumption, there exists a constant
c>0
such that
\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}(df_{p}(v))\geq c|df_{p}(v)|_{X}
. On the other hand, by the decreasing property (Proposition
5.3
), we have

c|df_{p}(v)|_{X}\leq\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}(df_{p}(v))\leq\mathcal{K}_{(\mathbb{R}^{k},\mathrm{vol}_{0})}(v)=0,

where in the last step we used Example
5.7
(b). Thus,
|df_{p}(v)|_{X}=0
, which implies that
df_{p}(v)=0
. Thus,
f
is constant, so
X
is
\phi
-hyperbolic.

∎

Corollary 6.4

(Hyperbolic spaces are
R_{\phi}
-hyperbolic)
.

1.

Real hyperbolic space
(B^{n},g_{1})
is
R_{\mathrm{vol}_{1}}
-hyperbolic.

2.

Complex hyperbolic space
(\mathbb{CH}^{n},g_{\mathbb{CH}^{n}})
is
R_{\omega}
-hyperbolic, where
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(\mathbb{CH}^{n})
is the Kähler calibration.

3.

Quaternionic hyperbolic space
(\mathbb{HH}^{n},g_{\mathbb{HH}^{n}})
is
R_{\Psi}
-hyperbolic, where
\Psi\in\Omega^{4}(\mathbb{HH}^{n})
is the QK calibration.

We now show that the converse of Theorem
6.3
(b) is false in general. In fact, for any constant coefficient calibration
\phi\in\Lambda^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{*}
, we will exhibit a domain
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
that is
\phi
-hyperbolic, but not
R_{\phi}
-hyperbolic. To show the failure of
\phi
-hyperbolicity, we will make use of the following Liouville-type theorem for
k
-harmonic functions due to Serrin and Zou.

Theorem 6.5

(
[
32
]
)
.

If
u\colon\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
is a non-negative
k
-harmonic function (that is defined on all of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
), then
u
is constant.

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
\phi\in\Lambda^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{*}
be a constant-coefficient calibration with
k\geq 2
. There exists a domain
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
(U,g_{0})
is
\phi
-hyperbolic, but not
R_{\phi}
-hyperbolic.

Proof.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a
\phi
-calibrated
k
-plane that contains the origin. Choose a basis
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}
,
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n-k})
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
E=\mathrm{span}\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}\}
, and let
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n-k})
denote the corresponding linear coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-k}
. Consider the domain

U=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-k}\colon(\text{if }y\neq 0,\,\text{then }|y|<1\text{ and }|x||y|<1)\text{ or }(\text{if }y=0,\,\text{then }|x|<1)\}.

We claim:

1.

There exists a sequence
p_{n}\in U
and
v_{n}\in T_{p_{n}}U
having
p_{n}\to(0,0)
and
\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(v_{n})<\frac{1}{n}|v_{n}|
. Consequently,
U
is not
R_{\phi}
-hyperbolic.

2.

Every Smith immersion
F\colon(\mathbb{R}^{k},g_{0},\mathrm{vol}_{0})\to(U,g_{0},\phi)
is constant. That is,
U
is
\phi
-hyperbolic.

Proofs of claims:

1.

For
n\geq 2
, let
p_{n}=\frac{1}{n}u_{1}
, and observe that
p_{n}\to(0,0)
. Let
v_{n}=e_{1}|_{p_{n}}\in T_{p_{n}}U
, a sequence of unit vectors in the
e_{1}
-direction. Consider the sequence of (linear) Smith immersions
f_{n}\colon(B^{k},g_{1},\mathrm{vol}_{1})\to(U,g_{0},\phi)
defined by
f_{n}(z)=n(z,0)+p_{n}
. The image of
f_{n}
is the
k
-ball
\{(x,0)\colon|x|<n\}+p_{n}
, so
f_{n}
is well-defined (i.e., its image lies in
U
). Since
f_{n}(0)=p_{n}
and
df_{n}|_{0}(e_{1})=nv_{n}
, it follows that
\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(v_{n})\leq\frac{1}{n}=\frac{1}{n}|v_{n}|
. This proves claim (a).

2.

Let
F=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k},h_{1},\ldots,h_{n-k})\colon(\mathbb{R}^{k},g_{0},\mathrm{vol}_{0})\to(U,g_{0},\phi)
be a Smith immersion. Since the image of
F
lies in
U
, we have that each
|h_{j}|<1
. For
j=1,\ldots,n-k
, let
\pi_{j}\colon U\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
denote the projection
\pi_{j}(x,y)=y_{j}
. Since
\pi_{j}
is affine, Corollary
2.23
(a) implies that
h_{j}=\pi_{j}\circ F
is
k
-harmonic. Thus, the function
\widetilde{h}_{j}:=h_{j}+1\colon\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
is a non-negative, entire
k
-harmonic function, so Theorem
6.5
implies that
\widetilde{h}_{j}
is constant, whence
h_{j}
is constant. This shows that the image of
F
lies in
U\cap\{y=c\}
for some
c\in\mathbb{R}^{n-k}
with
|c|<1
. There are now two cases.

•

Suppose
c\neq 0
. Then the definition of
U
implies that
|f_{\ell}|\leq|(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})|<\frac{1}{|c|}
, so by the same logic as above, we conclude that each
f_{\ell}
is constant, and thus
F
is constant.

•

Suppose
c=0
. Then the image of
F
lies in
\{|x|<1,y=0\}
. Therefore,
|f_{\ell}|\leq|(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k})|<1
, so by the same logic as above, we conclude that
F
is constant.

This proves claim (b).

∎

6.2
The Kobayashi
\phi
-Pseudo-distance

We now aim to define the Kobayashi
\phi
-pseudo-distance. For this, we need to place a regularity assumption on the KR
\phi
-metric. Recall that for a calibrated manifold
(X,g_{X},\phi)
, its KR
\phi
-pseudo-metric is

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}\colon TX

\displaystyle\to[0,\infty]

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}(v_{p})

\displaystyle=\inf\!\left\{a>0\colon\exists f\in\mathrm{SmIm}(B^{k},X),w\in T_{0}B^{k},|w|=1\text{ s.t. }f(0)=p,\,df_{0}(w)=\frac{1}{a}v\right\}\!.

Definition 6.7
.

We say that
(X,g_{X})
is
\phi
-replete
if the KR
\phi
-metric
\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}\colon TX\to[0,\infty]
is upper semicontinuous. By definition, this means that for every
y>0
, the set
E_{y}:=\{(p,v)\in TX\colon\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}(p,v)<y\}
is open.

Example 6.8
.

•

Every Kähler manifold
(X^{2m},g_{X},\omega)
is
\omega
-replete
[
31
]
. Note, however, that
\mathcal{K}_{(X,\omega)}
need not be continuous
[
24
, Example 3.5.37]
.

•

The Poincaré ball
(B^{n},g_{1})
is
\mathrm{vol}_{1}
-replete (by Theorem
5.9
).

•

Quaternionic hyperbolic space
(\mathbb{HH}^{n},g_{\mathbb{HH}^{n}})
is
\Psi
-replete, where
\Psi
is the QK calibration (by Theorem
5.11
).

Example 6.9

(Non-repleteness)
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a domain, and let
\phi=dx^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{k}\in\Omega^{k}(U)
for
k<n
. (Note that
\phi
is
not
an elliptic calibration.) Then
(U,g_{0})
is
not

\phi
-replete. Indeed, let
p\in U
, let
v\in T_{p}U
, and write
v=v_{1}+v_{2}
where
v_{1}\in\mathrm{span}\{\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{k}}\}
and
v_{2}\in\mathrm{span}\{\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{k+1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{n}}\}
. If
v_{2}=0
, then
\mathcal{K}_{(U,\phi)}(v)
is finite. However, if
v_{2}\neq 0
, then
\mathcal{K}_{(U,\phi)}(v)=\infty
.

Remark 6.10

(Almost complex case)
.

Let
(M,J)
be an almost-complex manifold. In this generality, the Kobayashi-Royden pseudo-metric
F_{X}\colon TM\to[0,\infty]
still makes sense. If
J
is class
C^{0,\alpha}
,
\alpha>0
, then for every
v\in TM
, there exists a germ of
J
-holomorphic disks that have
v
as a tangent vector (see
[
28
, Theorem III]
,
[
23
, Appendix 1]
). The existence of such disks ensures that
F_{X}(v)
is finite, and a reasonably large supply ensures that
F_{X}
is upper semicontinuous.

When
J
is class
C^{1,\alpha}
, the upper semicontinuity of
F_{X}
was established in
[
23
]
. In
[
22
]
, an almost-complex structure
J
of class
C^{0,1/2}
on the polydisk
\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}
was constructed such that
F_{\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}}
is not upper semicontinuous.

Definition 6.11
.

Suppose that
(X,g_{X})
is
\phi
-replete. The
Kobayashi
\phi
-pseudo-distance
is

\displaystyle d_{\phi}=d_{(X,\phi)}\colon X\times X

\displaystyle\to[0,\infty]

\displaystyle d_{\phi}(p,q)=d_{(X,\phi)}(p,q)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\gamma}\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}(\gamma^{\prime}(t))\,dt

where the infimum is over all piecewise-smooth paths
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to X
with
\gamma(0)=p
and
\gamma(1)=q
. It is straightforward to check that
d_{(X,\phi)}
really is a pseudo-distance (i.e.,
d_{(X,\phi)}
is non-negative, symmetric in its arguments, and satisfies the triangle inequality).

The following basic example shows that the Kobayashi
\phi
-pseudo-distance need not be a genuine distance function.

Example 6.12
.

Let
(X,g_{X},\phi)=(\mathbb{R}^{n},g_{0},\phi)
, where
\phi\in\Omega^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a constant-coefficient elliptic calibration. By Example
5.7
(b), we have
\mathcal{K}_{(\mathbb{R}^{n},\phi)}(v)=0
for all
v\in T\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Consequently,
d_{(\mathbb{R}^{n},\phi)}=0
.

It is natural to ask which calibrated manifolds
(X,g_{X},\phi)
have the property that
d_{(X,\phi)}
is a genuine distance function; we will discuss this in
\lx@sectionsign
6.3
. Note that even when
d_{(X,\phi)}
is a distance function, it need not coincide with the distance function
\mathrm{dist}_{g_{X}}
arising from the Riemannian metric. For now, we establish some basic properties.

Convention 6.13
.

For the remainder of this work, all calibrated manifolds are assumed to be replete.

Proposition 6.14

(Distance-decreasing)
.

Let
f\colon(X^{m},g_{X},\alpha)\to(Y^{n},g_{Y},\beta)
be a conformally calibrating map. For all
p,q\in X
, we have

d_{(Y,\beta)}(f(p),f(q))\leq d_{(X,\alpha)}(p,q).

Proof.

Fix
p,q\in X
. Consider the following two sets of piecewise-smooth curve segments:

\displaystyle C_{X}

\displaystyle=\left\{\widetilde{\gamma}\colon[0,1]\to X\mid\widetilde{\gamma}(0)=p,\,\widetilde{\gamma}(0)=q\right\}

\displaystyle C_{Y}

\displaystyle=\left\{\gamma\colon[0,1]\to Y\mid\gamma(0)=f(p),\,\gamma(1)=f(q)\right\}\!.

Notice that
C_{Y}
contains the class of curve segments
\{f\circ\widetilde{\gamma}\colon[0,1]\to Y\mid\widetilde{\gamma}\in C_{X}\}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle d_{(Y,\beta)}(f(p),f(q))=\inf_{\gamma\in C_{Y}}\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{K}_{\beta}(\gamma^{\prime}(t))\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\widetilde{\gamma}\in C_{X}}\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{K}_{\beta}((f\circ\widetilde{\gamma})^{\prime}(t))\,dt

\displaystyle=\inf_{\widetilde{\gamma}\in C_{X}}\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{K}_{\beta}(df_{\gamma(t)}(\gamma^{\prime}(t)))\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\widetilde{\gamma}\in C_{X}}\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}(\gamma^{\prime}(t))\,dt

(Proposition
5.3
)

\displaystyle=d_{(X,\alpha)}(p,q).

∎

Corollary 6.15

(Comparison principle)
.

Let
(X,g_{X},\phi)
be a calibrated manifold, and let
U\subset X
be an open set. Then
d_{(X,\phi)}(p,q)\leq d_{(U,\phi)}(p,q)
for all
p,q\in U
.

Proof.

Apply Proposition
6.14
to the inclusion map
\iota\colon(U,g_{X}|_{U},\phi)\hookrightarrow(X,g_{X},\phi)
.
∎

Corollary 6.16

(Invariance)
.

Let
(X,g_{X},\phi)
,
(Y,g_{Y},\psi)
be calibrated manifolds. If
F\colon X\to Y
is a Smith equivalence, then for all
p,q\in X
, we have
d_{\psi}(F(p),F(q))=d_{\phi}(p,q)
. In particular, every Smith automorphism
X\to X
is an isometry of
d_{\phi}
.

Proof.

Let
F\colon(X,g_{X},\phi)\to(Y,g_{X},\psi)
be a Smith equivalence, and fix
p,q\in X
. Using Proposition
6.14
twice, and observing that
F^{-1}
is a Smith equivalence, we have:

d_{\psi}(F(p),F(q))\leq d_{\phi}(p,q)=d_{\phi}(F^{-1}(F(p)),F^{-1}(F(q)))\leq d_{\psi}(F(p),F(q)).

∎

Next, we provide some examples of Kobayashi pseudo-distances.

Proposition 6.17

(Kobayashi
\phi
-distances on hyperbolic spaces)
.

1.

On real hyperbolic space
(B^{n},g_{1})
, we have
d_{(B^{n},\mathrm{vol}_{1})}(p,q)=\mathrm{dist}_{1}(p,q)
for all
p,q\in B^{n}
.

2.

On quaternionic hyperbolic space
(\mathbb{HH}^{n},g_{\mathbb{HH}^{n}})
, we have
d_{(\mathbb{HH}^{n},\Psi)}(p,q)=\mathrm{dist}_{\mathbb{HH}^{n}}(p,q)
for all
p,q\in\mathbb{HH}^{n}
.

Proof.

We prove part (a). Let
p,q\in B^{n}
, and let
C
be the collection of all piecewise-smooth curve segments
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to B^{n}
with
\gamma(0)=p
and
\gamma(1)=q
. Then by Theorem
5.9
, we have

d_{(B^{n},\mathrm{vol}_{1})}(p,q)=\inf_{\gamma\in C}\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{K}_{(B^{n},\mathrm{vol}_{1})}(\gamma^{\prime}(t))\,dt=\inf_{\gamma\in C}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\gamma^{\prime}(t)\right|_{1}dt=\mathrm{dist}_{1}(p,q).

The proof of part (b) is completely analogous, but utilizes Theorem
5.11
.
∎

Corollary 6.18
.

Let
f\colon(B^{k},g_{1},\mathrm{vol}_{1})\to(X,g,\phi)
be a Smith immersion, where

denotes the Poincaré metric on

. For all

.

Proof.

This follows from Propositions

(a).

The previous corollary can be strengthened in the following way.

Proposition 6.19
.

Let
f\colon(\Sigma^{k},g_{\Sigma},\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma})\to(X,g_{X},\phi)
be a Smith immersion.
Suppose that
(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})
is a complete Riemannian manifold with constant curvature
-4
whose universal cover is
B^{k}
. Then for all
p,q\in\Sigma
, we have
d_{(X,\phi)}(f(p),f(q))\leq\mathrm{dist}_{\Sigma}(p,q)
.

Proof.

Let
f\colon(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma},\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma})\to(X,g_{X},\phi)
be a Smith immersion. Let
\pi\colon B^{k}\to\Sigma
be the universal cover, and note that
\pi^{*}g_{\Sigma}
is isometric to the Poincaré metric
g_{1}
by the Killing-Hopf theorem. Therefore,
\pi\colon(B^{k},g_{1},\mathrm{vol}_{1})\to(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma},\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma})
is a local Smith equivalence. Let
p,q\in\Sigma
, and choose
a,b\in B^{k}
with
a\in\pi^{-1}(p)
and
b\in\pi^{-1}(q)
.

Let
F:=f\circ\pi\colon(B^{k},g_{1},\mathrm{vol}_{1})\to(X,g_{X},\phi)
, so that
F
is a Smith immersion (Proposition
2.16
). Note that
F(a)=f(p)
and
F(b)=f(q)
. So, Corollary
6.18
implies

d_{\phi}(f(p),f(q))=d_{\phi}(F(a),F(b))\leq\mathrm{dist}_{1}(a,b).

(6.1)

Now, since
\Sigma
is complete, there exists a minimizing geodesic
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to\Sigma
from
p
to
q
. Let
\widetilde{\gamma}\colon[0,1]\to B^{k}
be a lift of
\gamma
having
\widetilde{\gamma}(0)=a
, and set
\widetilde{b}=\widetilde{\gamma}(1)
. Since
\pi
is a local isometry and
\gamma
is minimizing, we have
\mathrm{dist}_{\Sigma}(p,q)\leq\mathrm{dist}_{1}(a,\widetilde{b})\leq L(\widetilde{\gamma})=L(\pi\circ\widetilde{\gamma})=L(\gamma)=\mathrm{dist}_{\Sigma}(p,q)
. Finally, since (
6.1
) is true for all
b\in\pi^{-1}(q)
, we conclude that
d_{\phi}(f(p),f(q))\leq\mathrm{dist}_{1}(a,\widetilde{b})=\mathrm{dist}_{\Sigma}(p,q)
.
∎

6.3
K_{\phi}
-hyperbolicity

We are finally in a position to define
K_{\phi}
-hyperbolicity. Let
(X,g_{X},\phi)
be a
\phi
-replete calibrated manifold, and recall that its Kobayashi
\phi
-pseudo-distance is the function
d_{\phi}\colon X\times X\to[0,\infty]
given by

\displaystyle d_{\phi}(p,q)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\gamma}\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(\gamma^{\prime}(t))\,dt

where the infimum is over all piecewise-smooth paths
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to X
with
\gamma(0)=p
and
\gamma(1)=q
.

Definition 6.20
.

Say
(X,g_{X})
is
K_{\phi}
-hyperbolic
if
d_{\phi}
is a distance function (i.e., if
p,q\in X
with
p\neq q
implies
d_{\phi}(p,q)>0
).

Proposition 6.21
.

Let
(X,g_{X},\phi)
be a calibrated manifold,
\phi\in\Omega^{k}(X)
.

1.

Suppose
(X,g_{X},\phi)
is Smith equivalent to
(Y,g_{Y},\psi)
. Then
(X,g_{X})
is
K_{\phi}
-hyperbolic if and only if
(Y,g_{Y})
is
K_{\psi}
-hyperbolic.

2.

Let
U\subset X
be an open subset. If
(X,g_{X})
is
K_{\phi}
-hyperbolic, then
(U,g_{X}|_{U})
is
K_{\phi}
-hyperbolic.

Proof.

Part (a) follows from Corollary
6.16
. Part (b) follows from Corollary
6.15
.
∎

Example 6.22

(Kähler calibrations)
.

Let
(X,g_{X},\omega)
be a Kähler manifold. Royden
[
31
]
proved:

X\text{ is }R_{\omega}\text{-hyperbolic}\ \iff\ X\text{ is }K_{\omega}\text{-hyperbolic}\ \,(\text{i.e., }X\text{ is Kobayashi hyperbolic}).

We now show that one of these implications holds in general.

Theorem 6.23
.

Let
(X,g_{X},\phi)
be a
\phi
-replete calibrated manifold.

1.

If
(X,g_{X})
is
R_{\phi}
-hyperbolic, then
(X,g_{X})
is
K_{\phi}
-hyperbolic.

2.

If
(X,g_{X})
is
K_{\phi}
-hyperbolic, then
(X,g_{X})
is
\phi
-hyperbolic.

Proof.

1.

Suppose
(X,g_{X})
is
R_{\phi}
-hyperbolic, and let
p,q\in X
. By
R_{\phi}
-hyperbolicity, there exists a neighborhood
U\subset X
of
p
and a constant
c>0
such that
\mathcal{K}_{(X,\phi)}(v)\geq c|v|_{X}
for all
v\in TU
. Let
B\subset U
denote a geodesic ball centered at
p
of radius
r<\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{dist}_{X}(p,q)
. Now, let
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to X
be a piecewise-smooth path from
p
to
q
, and let
t_{0}\in(0,1)
be the first time that
\gamma
crosses the boundary of
B
. Then

\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(\gamma^{\prime}(t))\,dt\geq\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(\gamma^{\prime}(t))\,dt\geq c\int_{0}^{t_{0}}|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|_{X}\,dt\geq cr.

Taking infima over all relevant
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to X
shows that
d_{(X,\phi)}(p,q)\geq cr>0
, and thus
(X,g_{X})
is
K_{\phi}
-hyperbolic.

2.

We prove the contrapositive. Suppose that
(X,g_{X})
is not
\phi
-hyperbolic, so there exists a non-constant Smith immersion
f\colon(\mathbb{R}^{k},g_{0},\mathrm{vol}_{0})\to(X,g_{X},\phi)
. Since
f
is not constant, there exist points
p,q\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
with
p\neq q
such that
f(p)\neq f(q)
. By Proposition
6.14
and Example
6.12
, we have
d_{(X,\phi)}(f(p),f(q))\leq d_{(\mathbb{R}^{k},\mathrm{vol}_{0})}(p,q)=0
. Thus,
d_{(X,\phi)}
is not a distance function, so
(X,g_{X})
is not
K_{\phi}
-hyperbolic.

∎

We do not know whether the converse of Theorem
6.23
(a) holds or fails in general. Regarding part (b), we now show that its converse is false without further assumptions.

Proposition 6.24
.

Let
\phi\in\Lambda^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{*}
be a constant-coefficient calibration with
k\geq 2
. Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the
\phi
-hyperbolic domain constructed in the proof of Theorem
6.6
, and suppose that
(U,g_{0})
is
\phi
-replete. Then
(U,g_{0})
is not
K_{\phi}
-hyperbolic.

Proof.

Let
p=(a,0)\in U
and
q=(b,0)\in U
. Define sequences of points
p_{n},q_{n}\in U
for
n\geq 2
by
p_{n}=p+\frac{1}{n}u_{1}
and
q_{n}=q+\frac{1}{n}u_{1}
. Observe that

d_{\phi}(p,q)\leq d_{\phi}(p,p_{n})+d_{\phi}(p_{n},q_{n})+d_{\phi}(q_{n},q).

We will prove that
d_{\phi}(p,p_{n})\to 0
and
d_{\phi}(q,q_{n})\to 0
, and then that
d_{\phi}(p_{n},q_{n})\to 0
.

First, consider the curves
\gamma_{n}\colon[0,1]\to U
given by
\gamma_{n}(t)=p+\frac{t}{n}u_{1}
. Note that
\gamma_{n}
is well-defined (i.e., the image of
\gamma_{n}
lies in
U
). Note also that
\gamma_{n}(0)=p
and
\gamma_{n}(1)=q
, and that
\gamma_{n}^{\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{n}u_{1}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle d_{\phi}(p,p_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(\gamma^{\prime}(t))\,dt=\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(u_{1})\,dt\to 0\text{ as }n\to\infty.

An analogous argument shows that
d_{\phi}(q,q_{n})\to 0
.

Next, for
n\geq 2
, consider the linear Smith immersions
f_{n}\colon(B^{k},g_{1},\mathrm{vol}_{1})\to(U,g_{0},\phi)
defined by
f_{n}(z)=n(z,0)+\frac{1}{n}u_{1}
. Note that the image of
f_{n}
is the
k
-ball
\frac{1}{n}u_{1}+\{(x,0)\colon|x|<n\}
, and that
f_{n}(\frac{1}{n}a)=p_{n}
and
f_{n}(\frac{1}{n}b)=q_{n}
. Using Corollary
6.18
and Proposition
2.26
, we have

\displaystyle d_{\phi}(p_{n},q_{n})=d_{\phi}\!\left(f_{n}\!\left(\frac{1}{n}a\right),f_{n}\!\left(\frac{1}{n}b\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{dist}_{1}\!\left(\frac{1}{n}a,\frac{1}{n}b\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{arcsinh}\sqrt{\frac{\frac{1}{n^{2}}\left|a-b\right|^{2}}{(1-\frac{1}{n^{2}}|a|^{2})(1-\frac{1}{n^{2}}|b|^{2})}}

\displaystyle\to 0\text{ as }n\to\infty.

This proves that
U
is not
K_{\phi}
-hyperbolic.
∎

7
Questions

We conclude this work by raising the following questions.

1.

Which calibrated manifolds
(X,g_{X},\phi)
are
\phi
-replete? Proving the
\phi
-repleteness of
(X,g_{X})
appears to require the existence of a large supply of conformally flat, immersed
\phi
-calibrated
k
-disks in
X
. Given the difficulty of constructing calibrated submanifolds, this question appears to be non-trivial.

2.

When
(X,g,\omega)
is a Kähler manifold, Brody’s theorem
[
4
]
asserts that if
X
is compact, then
K_{\omega}
-hyperbolicity is equivalent to
\omega
-hyperbolicity. Are there other calibrated geometries
(X,g,\phi)
for which Brody’s theorem holds? That is, if
X
is compact, is
K_{\phi}
-hyperbolicity equivalent to
\phi
-hyperbolicity? In the Riemannian geometric study of hyperbolicity via conformal harmonic disks, a partial analog of Brody’s theorem is established in
[
11
, Theorem 6.7]
.

3.

When
(X,g,\omega)
is a compact Kähler manifold, it is known that the
K_{\omega}
-hyperbolicity of
X
implies that the biholomorphism group of
X
(and hence also the Smith automorphism group
\mathrm{SmAut}(X,g,\omega)
) is finite. Are there other calibrated geometries for which compactness and
K_{\phi}
-hyperbolicity together imply the finiteness of
\mathrm{SmAut}(X,g,\phi)
?

4.

In view of Theorem
4.12
, it would be interesting to have examples of calibrated manifolds
(X,g_{X},\phi)
having negative
\phi
-sectional curvature (without having negative sectional curvature).

5.

Arguably the simplest example of a hyperbolic manifold (for essentially any notion of “hyperbolicity”) is hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, which we have viewed as the Poincaré ball
(B^{n},g_{1})
. As such, it would be interesting to have a large supply of calibrations on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.

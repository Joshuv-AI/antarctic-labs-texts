Nonabelian Surface Holonomy from Multiplicative Integration
Hollis Williams \fnref fn1

Abstract
Abstract: Surface holonomy and the Wess–Zumino phase play a central role in string theory and Chern–Simons models, yet a completely analytic formulation of their nonabelian counterparts has remained elusive. In this work, we show that Yekutieli’s theory of multiplicative integration provides such a formulation and realizes explicitly the higher parallel transport structure of Schreiber and Waldorf. Starting from a smooth 2 2 -connection ( α , β ) (\alpha,\beta) on a Lie crossed module, we prove that the corresponding multiplicative integrals satisfy the axioms of a transport 2 2 -functor, thereby providing an explicit model for nonabelian surface holonomy. This framework extends the familiar holonomy on U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) -bundle gerbes to arbitrary gauge 2 2 -bundles whilst avoiding abstract categorical machinery. The resulting three-dimensional Stokes theorem yields the Wess-Zumino phase law and gives an analytic counterpart of the boundary phase relation underlying the Chern-Simons functional.

Introduction

It has been known for many years that parallel transport along a curve and the associated notion of holonomy can be generalized to higher-dimensional parallel transport along surfaces with an associated surface holonomy
schreiber2
;
baez1
.
The main physical motivation for studying higher-dimensional holonomy originates in two-dimensional conformal field theories (in particular, Wess–Zumino–Witten models), where the Wess–Zumino term in the worldsheet action has long been interpreted as a surface holonomy
witten2
;
gaw1
;
schreiber1
.
WZW models are among the most important two-dimensional rational conformal field theories, forming the building blocks for coset models in the coset construction
kapa
;
witten1
. Several mathematical frameworks have been developed to formalize higher parallel transport and surface holonomy, including Deligne cohomology and bundle gerbes with connection and curving, the connective structures of Breen and Messing, and more recently, multiplicative gerbes and smooth 2-group extensions
deligne
;
murray
;
gad2
;
breen
;
gad1
;
bunk1
.

A homotopy-theoretic treatment of higher-dimensional parallel transport has been given by Baez, Schreiber, and Waldorf in terms of 2-bundles with 2-connections, where parallel transport is described by smooth transport 2-functors
baez1
;
schreiber3
. Although categorical approaches are conceptually powerful, they rely on higher category theory and abstract constructions which are unfamiliar to most physicists and differential geometers. An alternative, much more concrete analytic framework was introduced by Yekutieli under the name of nonabelian multiplicative integration (abbreviated in the article as MI)
yek
.
In this approach, nonabelian line and surface integrals are constructed as explicit limits of elementary Riemann products, similarly to ordinary path-ordered exponentials, but extended to higher dimensions
chen
. The theory provides well-defined, rigorous analytic control and convergence estimates for these limits, without using any machinery from category and homotopy theory. Although originally developed in the context of twisted deformation quantisation, it turns out this formalism reproduces the basic structure of higher parallel transport as developed by Schreiber and Waldorf, but in a much more elementary setting
yek2
;
yek3
;
bressler
.

The main aim of this article is to make this correspondence precise and to show that multiplicative integration provides an explicit analytic realization of nonabelian surface holonomy and higher parallel transport.
In particular, we prove that the multiplicative integrals
\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta|\sigma)
associated to a smooth
2
–connection
(\alpha,\beta)
on a Lie crossed module
(H\xrightarrow{\Phi}G,\triangleright)
satisfy exactly the axioms of a Schreiber–Waldorf transport
2
–functor. This essentially means that the analytic theory is a genuine model of higher holonomy, but with no use of higher categorical structures or abstract arguments. Our main result is a
3
–dimensional multiplicative Stokes-type theorem for the pair
(\alpha,\beta)
, which shows that for two surfaces
\Sigma_{0},\Sigma_{1}
sharing the same boundary
\gamma
, one has

\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta\mid\Sigma_{1})=\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta\mid\Sigma_{0})\;\exp_{H}\!\Big(\!\int_{V}\!\mathcal{H}\Big),

(1)

where
V
is any smooth
3
–chain with
\partial V=\Sigma_{1}-\Sigma_{0}
and
\mathcal{H}=d\beta+\alpha\!\triangleright\!\beta
is the
3
–curvature.
This formula expresses, in differential geometry language, the higher gauge generalization of the familiar relation between holonomy and curvature.
When
\mathcal{H}=0
, the
H
-valued surface holonomy only depends on the boundary curve, whereas for nonzero
\mathcal{H}
its variation is governed by the integrated
3
–curvature.
This is exactly the phase law postulated in the physics literature for the Wess–Zumino term
witten2
;
wess
. Although the relation between surface holonomy and the integral of the
3
–form curvature is well known in the physics literature, rigorous geometric formulations have mostly been restricted to the abelian case. In particular, the holonomy of bundle gerbes with connection and curving provides an elegant realization of the abelian Wess–Zumino phase, but the underlying structure group must be
U(1)

murray
;
carey
;
freed
;
waldorf
. Nonabelian generalizations of gerbes do exist, but they are technically involved and usually formulated using higher category theory
baez1
;
schreiber2
;
picken
. The present work provides an explicit realization of nonabelian higher holonomy which is valid for arbitrary Lie crossed modules, bridging the gap between the approaches via category theory and differential geometry.

Finally, we interpret our analytic Stokes theorem as a differential geometry realization of several familiar physical structures. In the abelian case, it reproduces the usual Wess–Zumino phase of the string worldsheet action. In the nonabelian case, it defines the natural generalisation of the
B
–field holonomy to higher gauge backgrounds and gives a precise analytic expression for the flux quantization condition. We also note that the same formal structure also underlies the boundary phase law of the Chern–Simons functional and the associated anomaly inflow mechanism.
Together, these applications show that the theory of multiplicative integration provides a simple but powerful analytic bridge between higher gauge theory, conformal field theory, and topological field theory.

The article is structured as follows. Section 2 fixes notation and recalls basic constructions from
Yekutieli’s theory of multiplicative integration for paths and surfaces. In Section 3, we show that these constructions satisfy the axioms of a
smooth transport
2
–functor in the sense of
Schreiber and Waldorf, thereby establishing a dictionary
between the analytic and categorical formulations of higher holonomy. Section 4 contains our main result: we extend Yekutieli’s local 3-dimensional Stokes theorem to a global statement for arbitrary 3-chains, obtaining a rigorous coordinate-level proof of the curvature obstruction formula which underlies the Wess–Zumino phase relation. Finally, Section 5 discusses physical interpretations and applications,
including the realization of nonabelian
B
–field holonomy and
connections with the gauge anomaly in Chern–Simons theory.

2.2
Multiplicative integration on surfaces

We briefly recall the construction of multiplicative
integration as developed by Yekutieli in
yek
.
All analytic estimates, convergence proofs, and geometric background

Let
M

G

\mathfrak{g}
.
Given a smooth path
\sigma\colon\Delta^{1}\to M
and a

\mathfrak{g}
-valued
1

\mathrm{MI}(\alpha|\sigma)\in G

If

3
MI is a transport 2-functor

In this section, we explain how multiplicative integration realizes, in analytic form, the notion of a smooth transport 2-functor familiar from higher gauge theory.
Our goal is to provide a dictionary between the two approaches so that the formal results of Schreiber and Waldorf may be interpreted concretely in terms of elementary differential geometry.
This correspondence makes precise the sense in which the multiplicative integral defines parallel transport not only along paths but also along surfaces, satisfying the same coherence laws that appear in the abstract definition of a 2-functor

\mathrm{Tra}\colon\Pi_{2}(M)\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}(G,H),

(16)

from the thin fundamental 2-groupoid of
M
to the Lie 2-group determined by a crossed module
(\Phi:H\!\to\!G,\triangleright)
.

In Section 3.1, we briefly recall the essential structural features of transport 2-functors (but without using categorical language). In Section 3.2, we show that multiplicative integrals
\mathrm{MI}(\alpha|\sigma)
and
\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta|\sigma,\tau)
satisfy the same axioms: functoriality under composition, compatibility with the boundary homomorphism
\Phi
, and the infinitesimal Stokes law governed by the 3-curvature
\mathcal{H}
.
This proves that multiplicative integration realizes the Schreiber–Waldorf transport 2-functor associated to the 2-connection
(\alpha,\beta)
.

3.1
Transport 2-Functors

A transport 2-functor may be viewed as the higher-dimensional analogue of parallel transport for an ordinary connection.
It assigns:

•

to each point
x\in M
, an object of the target Lie 2-group
\mathcal{B}(G,H)
;

•

to each smooth path
\sigma\colon[0,1]\to M
, an element of
G
describing the 1-holonomy;

•

to each smooth surface
\tau\colon[0,1]^{2}\to M
with fixed boundary, an element of
H
describing the 2-holonomy,

in such a way that these assignments satisfy three coherence conditions:

1.

Functoriality:

Composition and inversion of paths and surfaces correspond to multiplication and inversion in
G
and
H
, respectively.

2.

Boundary compatibility:

The group homomorphism
\Phi\colon H\!\to\!G
intertwines 2-holonomy and the 1-holonomy around the boundary,

\Phi\big(\mathrm{Tra}(\tau)\big)=\mathrm{Tra}(\partial\tau).

(17)

3.

Coherence (3-curvature law):

The failure of 2-holonomy to be strictly homotopy-invariant is measured by the 3-form curvature

\mathcal{H}=d\beta+\alpha\!\triangleright\!\beta
.

This framework was developed by Schreiber and Waldorf in their treatment of smooth 2-connections as functors

\mathrm{Tra}\colon\Pi_{2}(M)\to\mathcal{B}(G,H)

waldorf
;
schreiber2
.
It gives an abstract generalization of the familiar situation for principal bundles, where parallel transport defines a smooth functor from the path groupoid of
M
to the Lie group
G
.

3.2
Verification of the functor axioms

We now check that both types of multiplicative integration

\mathrm{MI}(\alpha|\sigma)
and
\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta|\sigma,\tau)

explicitly satisfy the axioms of a smooth transport 2-functor
for a 2-connection
(\alpha,\beta)
valued in the crossed module

(\Phi:H\!\to\!G,\triangleright)
.

Proposition:
Let
(\alpha,\beta)
be a smooth
2
–connection on
M
,
and let
\mathrm{MI}
denote a multiplicative integral as defined above. Then the assignments

\sigma\longmapsto\mathrm{MI}(\alpha|\sigma)\in G,\qquad(\sigma,\tau)\longmapsto\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta|\sigma,\tau)\in H

(18)

realize a smooth transport
2
–functor

\mathrm{Tra}_{\alpha,\beta}\colon\Pi_{2}(M)\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}(G,H),

(19)

which satisfies the axioms of functoriality, boundary compatibility, and the infinitesimal
3-curvature law. The assignment is also smooth and invariant under thin homotopies.

Proof.

We will begin by checking functoriality. For paths, the multiplicative integral satisfies the concatenation rule

\mathrm{MI}(\alpha|\sigma_{1}\!\ast\!\sigma_{2})=\mathrm{MI}(\alpha|\sigma_{1})\cdot\mathrm{MI}(\alpha|\sigma_{2}),

(20)

and
\mathrm{MI}(\alpha|\sigma^{-1})=\mathrm{MI}(\alpha|\sigma)^{-1}
,
as proved in Theorem 3.3.19 of
yek
.
For surfaces, the same property holds:
if
(\sigma,\tau)
and
(\sigma^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})
are kites,
then

\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta|\sigma\!\ast\!\sigma^{\prime},\tau\!\ast\!\tau^{\prime})=\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta|\sigma,\tau)\cdot\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta|\sigma^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}),

(21)

and reversing the orientation inverts the result.
This follows directly from the recursive definition of the associated Riemann products (see Definition 8.1.5 and Proposition 8.2.1 of
yek
)
and from the multiplicativity of the exponential map in the Lie group
H
.

We next check compatibility with the boundary map. Let
\Phi\colon H\to G
denote the structure morphism of the crossed module.
For every kite
(\sigma,\tau)
, one has

\Phi\big(\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta|\sigma,\tau)\big)=\mathrm{MI}(\alpha|\partial\tau),

(22)

which expresses that the
H
-valued 2-holonomy projects to the

G
-valued holonomy around the boundary path of
\tau
.
This is the Stokes theorem for triangles
(Theorem 7.5.1 of
yek
), where
\Phi
acts on
H
by group
homomorphisms and the right-hand side is the multiplicative integral of
\alpha

around the boundary of
\tau
.
It follows that the surface integral is compatible with the group map
\Phi

defining the crossed module, as required for a transport 2-functor.

Finally, for every sufficiently small
3
–simplex

f\colon\Delta^{3}\to M
, the Stokes theorem for tetrahedra (Lemma 8.4.1 of
yek
)
states that

\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta|\partial f)=\exp_{H}\!\Big(\!\int_{f}\!\mathcal{H}+O(|f|^{2})\!\Big),

(23)

where
\mathcal{H}=d\beta+\alpha\triangleright\beta

is the
3
–curvature of the pair
(\alpha,\beta)
.
This gives the infinitesimal coherence law of the transport 2–functor,
expressing the fact that the failure of the
H
–valued surface holonomy to be
strictly functorial under deformation of the bounding surface
is governed precisely by
\mathcal{H}
.

For smoothness, note that by Theorems 3.3.19 and 8.3.1 of
yek
,
the multiplicative integrals
\mathrm{MI}(\alpha|\sigma)

and
\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta|\sigma,\tau)
depend smoothly
on the forms
(\alpha,\beta)
and on the maps

\sigma,\tau
in the
C^{\infty}
topology. It follows that the assignments above define a smooth 2–functor in the sense of
Schreiber–Waldorf
waldorf
. For thin homotopy invariance, note that if two surfaces are related by a thin homotopy,
then the pullback of
\beta
to the interpolating 3–chain
vanishes identically.
By Proposition 8.2.3 of
yek
,

\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta)
evaluates to the identity
on degenerate surfaces,
so the induced 2–functor factors through the thin
2–groupoid
\Pi_{2}^{\mathrm{thin}}(M)
. This confirms that the assignments of path and surface
holonomy defined by multiplicative integration satisfy
the Screiber-Waldorf axioms for a smooth transport 2-functor. The construction therefore provides an analytic realization of the
Schreiber–Waldorf transport 2-functor associated with the
2-connection
(\alpha,\beta)
.

4
The Wess–Zumino Term and 3-Form Curvature Obstruction

The theory of nonabelian multiplicative integration provides analytic
definitions of holonomy for connections on crossed modules and
establishes higher–dimensional Stokes theorems.
In particular, Theorem 0.5.4 of
yek
shows that the

3
–form curvature of a connection–curvature pair

(\alpha,\beta)
governs the deviation of the
2
D
multiplicative integral from being exact.
In this section, we reformulate that theorem in geometric terms and
interpret it as the curvature–obstruction law for higher
holonomy: the difference between two surface holonomies is given by
the exponential of the integral of the
3
–form flux. This establishes a rigorous formulation of the familiar relation from string theory that the phase difference between two worldsheet surfaces is given by the exponential of the integral of the 3-form field strength of the
B-field
wess
;
witten2
;
green
;
freed
. We prove our main result for curvature obstruction as a phase difference in Section 4.1. In Section 4.2, we reduce to the abelian case and show that this reproduces the Wess-Zumino term. In Section 4.3, we situate this work in the existing literature.

4.1
Curvature obstruction as a phase difference

In the following proposition we use multiplicative integration directly to derive a Stokes-type identity which shows that
the ratio of surface holonomies for two worldsheets with common
boundary is given by the exponential of the integrated
3
–curvature.

Proposition:
Let
(\alpha,\beta)
be a smooth
2
–connection on
M
as above.
Let
\Sigma_{0},\Sigma_{1}\colon[0,1]^{2}\to M
be two oriented surfaces
with common boundary path
\partial\Sigma_{0}=\partial\Sigma_{1}=\gamma
,
and let
V\colon[0,1]^{3}\to M
be a smooth
3
–chain whose oriented
boundary satisfies
\partial V=\Sigma_{1}-\Sigma_{0}
.
Then the following identity holds

\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta\mid\Sigma_{1})=\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta\mid\Sigma_{0})\;\cdot\;\exp_{H}\!\Big(\!\int_{V}\mathcal{H}\Big),

(24)

where
\exp_{H}
denotes the Lie exponential in
H

and the product is taken in
H
using the
G
–action
induced by the boundary holonomy.
Consequently, the
H
–valued surface holonomy is independent of the
choice of surface filling the same boundary curve if and only if the

3
–curvature
\mathcal{H}
vanishes.

Proof.

We begin by showing that there is a local identity for an arbitrarily small smooth
3
-simplex
f:\Delta^{3}\rightarrow M
. Start with such a 3-simplex and denote by
\Delta_{i}^{2}
(
i=1,\dots,4
) its oriented faces. Write

h_{i}:=\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta\mid f|_{\Delta_{i}^{2}})\in H

(25)

for the
H
-valued surface holonomies associated to these faces. We claim that, as the simplex shrinks, the product of the face
holonomies satisfies

h_{1}h_{2}h_{3}h_{4}^{-1}=\exp_{H}\!\Big(\!\int_{\Delta^{3}}\!\mathcal{H}+O(|\Delta^{3}|^{2})\!\Big),

(26)

where
O(|\Delta^{3}|^{2})
denotes a uniform error term depending quadratically on the diameter of
\Delta^{3}
. This can be justified using arguments of Yekutieli, since he constructed the multiplicative integral
\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta\mid\sigma)
for a smooth
2
–simplex

\sigma\colon\Delta^{2}\to M

as a limit of finite Riemann products

P_{N}(\sigma)=\prod_{j=1}^{N}\exp_{H}\!\Big(\!\textstyle\int_{\sigma(T_{j})}\beta+\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\sigma(\partial T_{j})}\Psi(\alpha)\!\Big),

(27)

where the limit is taken over a subdivision of
\Delta^{2}
into small triangles
T_{j}
. The convergence and uniformity of these products,
together with quantitative control of the error term, are established in Proposition 8.2.1 and Theorem 8.3.1 of
yek
.

Applying this construction to the four faces of the simplex
f
and comparing the respective Riemann products on their overlaps,
Yekutieli proved that an infinitesimal multiplicative Stokes law
holds for every sufficiently small
3
-simplex,

\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta\mid\partial f)=\exp_{H}\!\Big(\!\int_{f}\mathcal{H}+\epsilon(f)\!\Big),

(28)

where the error
\epsilon(f)\in\mathfrak{h}
satisfies
\|\epsilon(f)\|\leq C|f|^{2}
for some constant
C
independent of
f
(see Lemma 8.4.1 of
yek
). Equation (
26
) is exactly this local statement,
written in our notation as

h_{1}h_{2}h_{3}h_{4}^{-1}=\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta\mid\partial f)
. The proof uses Prop 8.2.1 of
yek
(for sufficiently small simplices, the multiplicative integral differs from the exponential of the averaged form by
O(|\sigma|^{2})
) and Lemma 8.4.1 (the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff expansion can be truncated at second order uniformly, because the curvature
terms
F_{\alpha}
and
\beta
are bounded on compact subsets of
M
). These analytic ingredients yield (
26
) which tells that the product of the face holonomies around a small tetrahedron is equal to the exponential of the
integrated
3
–curvature
\mathcal{H}
, modulo a controlled quadratic error term. This local identity may be seen as an infinitesimal version of the three-dimensional multiplicative Stokes theorem and we will next use it as a building block for a global relation obtained by taking subdivisions.

Let
V\colon[0,1]^{3}\!\to M
be a smooth
3
–chain whose oriented boundary satisfies
\partial V=\Sigma_{1}-\Sigma_{0}
. Choose a triangulation
\mathcal{T}
of
V
by small smooth

3
simplices
f_{k}
and apply the local identity
(
26
) to each
f_{k}
. Multiplying these identities in the order prescribed by the
triangulation gives

\prod_{k}\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta\mid\partial f_{k})=\exp_{H}\!\Big(\!\sum_{k}\int_{f_{k}}\!\!\mathcal{H}+O(\max_{k}|f_{k}|^{2})\!\Big).

(29)

For every internal
2
-face
\tau
shared by two adjacent simplices

f_{k}
and
f_{k^{\prime}}
, the corresponding multiplicative integrals
\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta\mid\tau)
and

\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta\mid\bar{\tau})
appear in reverse order with
opposite orientation and therefore cancel out in
H
. The only remaining contributions are those from the external faces ie. those from
\Sigma_{1}
and
-\Sigma_{0}
. As a result, we have

\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta\mid\Sigma_{1})\cdot\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta\mid\Sigma_{0})^{-1}=\exp_{H}\!\Big(\!\int_{V}\mathcal{H}+\epsilon_{\mathcal{T}}\!\Big),

(30)

where
\epsilon_{\mathcal{T}}\!\in\!\mathfrak{h}

satisfies
\|\epsilon_{\mathcal{T}}\|\!\leq\!C\,\!\max_{k}|f_{k}|^{2}
.

To pass to the limit as the mesh size
\max_{k}|f_{k}|
goes to zero, we use Theorem 8.3.1 of
yek
, which ensures that the Riemann products converge uniformly and are independent of the particular subdivision. In particular,
\epsilon_{\mathcal{T}}\!\to\!0
uniformly, and the
left-hand side converges to the multiplicative integral over
V
.
Consequently,

\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta\mid\Sigma_{1})=\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta\mid\Sigma_{0})\;\cdot\;\exp_{H}\!\Big(\!\int_{V}\mathcal{H}\Big),

(31)

which is the desired global version of the
3
-dimensional multiplicative
Stokes formula. The remaining point is to check that the limit above is independent
of the chosen triangulation of
V
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
be two triangulations of
V

by small smooth
3
-simplices. Choose a common refinement
\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime}
that subdivides both.
Applying the previous subdivision argument to
\mathcal{T}
,
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
, and
\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime}

and using uniform convergence for multiplicative Riemann products, one obtains

\lim_{\max_{k}|f_{k}|\to 0}\prod_{f_{k}\in\mathcal{T}}\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta\mid\partial f_{k})\;=\;\lim_{\max_{k^{\prime}}|f^{\prime}_{k^{\prime}}|\to 0}\prod_{f^{\prime}_{k^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta\mid\partial f^{\prime}_{k^{\prime}}).

(32)

This follows from Proposition 7.3.2 of
yek
(the multiplicative integral is not affected by subdivision or refinement of the domain) and Theorem 8.3.1, together ensuring that the limit over
\mathcal{T}^{\prime\prime}
coincides with that over

\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
. It follows that the value of the multiplicative integral

\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta\mid V)
is independent of the chosen
triangulation or decomposition of
V
,
and the global identity (
31
) is well defined.

Equation (
31
) implies that the
H
–valued multiplicative
integral on a surface depends on the chosen filling
3
–chain only
via the exponential of the integrated
3
–curvature
\mathcal{H}
. If
\mathcal{H}=0
, then the right-hand side of
(
31
) is equal to the identity element
1_{H}
, and the surface integral
\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta\mid\Sigma)
depends
only on the common boundary path
\partial\Sigma
.
It follows that the
H
-valued surface holonomy is invariant under smooth
deformations of
\Sigma
with fixed boundary, as expected for a flat

2
-connection. In general, let
V
and
V^{\prime}
be two smooth
3
–chains in
M
with

\partial V=\Sigma_{1}-\Sigma_{0}=\partial V^{\prime}
.
Applying equation (
31
) to both fillings and dividing the resulting
identities gives

\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta\mid\Sigma_{1})\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta\mid\Sigma_{0})^{-1}=\exp_{H}\!\Big(\!\int_{V}\mathcal{H}\!\Big)=\exp_{H}\!\Big(\!\int_{V^{\prime}}\mathcal{H}\!\Big)\!\cdot\!\exp_{H}\!\Big(\!\int_{V\cup(-V^{\prime})}\mathcal{H}\!\Big).

(33)

Furthermore, since
V\cup(-V^{\prime})
is a closed
3
-cycle in
M
, the factor
\exp_{H}\!\big(\!\int_{V\cup(-V^{\prime})}\!\mathcal{H}\big)
measures the discrepancy between the two fillings.
This element depends only on the de Rham cohomology class of

\mathcal{H}
in
H^{3}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M,\ker\Phi)
,
where
\ker\Phi\subseteq H
is the inert subgroup of the crossed
module in the terminology of
yek
. Consequently, the obstruction to the surface holonomy being
independent of the filling is exactly the cohomology class

[\mathcal{H}]
. ∎

Corollary:
Let
(\alpha,\beta)
be a smooth
2
-connection on
M
with

3
-curvature
\mathcal{H}
.

1.

If
\mathcal{H}=0
, the multiplicative integral

\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta\mid\Sigma)
only depends on the thin homotopy
class of its boundary path
\gamma
. In particular, the
H
–valued
surface holonomy is homotopy–invariant.

2.

For general
\mathcal{H}
, the ambiguity between two surfaces with
the same boundary curve is given by

\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta\mid\Sigma_{1})\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta\mid\Sigma_{0})^{-1}=\exp_{H}\!\Big(\!\int_{V}\mathcal{H}\!\Big),

(34)

and is therefore measured by the de Rham cohomology class

[\mathcal{H}]\in H^{3}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M,\mathfrak{h})
.

4.2
Abelian case and physical interpretation

In the abelian case where
G=\{1\}
and
H=U(1)
, the
2
-connection reduces to a single
2
-form potential
B
(the
B
-field), whilst the
1
-form component
\alpha
vanishes. Equation (
31
) then reduces to the familiar phase relation

\exp\!\Big(i\!\!\int_{\Sigma_{1}}\!B\Big)=\exp\!\Big(i\!\!\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\!B\Big)\exp\!\Big(i\!\!\int_{V}\!dB\Big),

(35)

showing that the phase difference between two worldsheet surfaces

\Sigma_{0}
and
\Sigma_{1}
with the same boundary string
\gamma

is determined by the integral of the
3
–form flux
H=dB

over the
3
–volume
V
bounded by the surfaces. This expression is exactly the Wess–Zumino term in the nonlinear sigma model formulation of string theory
witten1
;
freed
. In that setting, the functional

S_{WZ}(g)=\int_{V}g^{*}H

(36)

defines the topological contribution to the worldsheet action, and its
exponential
\exp(iS_{WZ})
contributes a phase to the string
path integral which depends only on the cohomology class

[H]\in H^{3}(M,\mathbb{Z})
.

Equation (
31
) therefore provides the rigorous
differential geometry statement behind this physical principle:
the failure of the surface phase
\exp(i\!\int_{\Sigma}B)

to be invariant under deformation of
\Sigma
is governed by
the
3
–form curvature
H
. In the nonabelian case, the same formula expresses the failure of

2
–holonomy to be homotopy invariant as an

\mathfrak{h}
–valued
3
–curvature obstruction.
This provides the natural higher-categorical analogue of the usual
relation between curvature and parallel transport for connections, and gives a precise mathematical description of
nonabelian
B
–field fluxes appearing in string and M-theory
compactifications
baez1
;
schreiber2
. The quantization condition
[H]\in H^{3}(M,\mathbb{Z})

ensures that
\exp(iS_{WZ})
is single-valued.
In our analytic framework, this corresponds to requiring that
the multiplicative integral
\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta\mid V)
be trivial
on closed
3
-cycles.
In the nonabelian setting, the same condition expresses the absence of
global monodromy of the
2
–connection (triviality of the

H
–valued
3
–holonomy on closed
3
–cycles), which is the natural
nonabelian generalization of flux quantization.

4.3
Relation to previous work

The relation which we gave via (
31
) has appeared previously in the higher gauge theory literature. In particular, Schreiber and Waldorf proved that the transport 2-functor associated to a differential crossed module satisfies an analogous ’coherence’ law governed by the 3-curvature form
waldorf
. However, we must emphasize their argument is purely categorical. The holonomies are defined abstractly as smooth 2-functors and there is no analytic construction or convergence proof. The theorem we have given in this section provides the corresponding differential geometry statement: beginning with multiplicative integration, we derive this same 3-curvature identity as a genuine differential geometry Stokes-type theorem for nonabelian surface integrals. Secondly, the phase difference equation has already appeared in the physics literature as the definition of the Wess-Zumino in two-dimensional sigma models
witten2
. In that context, one assumes that the exponential of the Wess-Zumino action
\exp(iS_{WZ})
changes by the phase
\exp(i\int_{V}H)
when the worldsheet extension is modified, a condition that leads to the quantization of the
3
–form flux. We have shown here that this relation follows rigorously from a well-defined Stokes-type for the connection–curvature pair
(\alpha,\beta)
, so providing an analytic foundation for the
Wess-Zumino phase law which was already known in the literature on conformal field theories.

Finally, we should mention that although Yekutieli originally formulated his multiplicative integrals for triangles and tetrahedra, his construction extends verbatim to arbitrary piecewise smooth surfaces and bodies. This already follows Proposition 9.1.2 and Example 9.2.1 of
yek
, which allows extension of a simplex inside a cube and the vanishing of the form on the complement. Any smooth surface or 3-chain can be decomposed into finitely many pieces. Furthermore, by a combinatorial gluing procedure using Theorem 7.5.1 of
yek
, the ordered product of local multiplicative integrals over the tetrahedra of a triangulation, one obtains the multiplicative integral for an arbitrary piecewise smooth 3-body, with no additional analytic estimates necessary aside from those already in
yek
.

5
Applications

We finish by outlining potential physics applications of our results. In Section 5.1, we point out that the analytic results can be interpreted (at least heuristically) in the language of string backgrounds and Wilson surface operators. In Section 5.2, we build on the interpretation in terms of Wilson surface operators to examine
how the
3
–curvature
\mathcal{H}
gives rise to the familiar
gauge-covariant anomaly phase in nonabelian string backgrounds. In Section 5.3, we discuss a formal analogy between our results and boundary phases in Chern-Simons theory.

5.1
Nonabelian string backgrounds

In conventional string sigma models, the target manifold
M

is equipped with a
B
–field whose curvature
H=dB
represents a
background flux. The corresponding worldsheet term in the action is

\exp(i\!\int_{\Sigma}B)
, and as shown in Section 4, this phase is
well–defined modulo the exponential of the integrated
3
–form flux. Multiplicative integration extends this familiar abelian picture to
nonabelian higher gauge backgrounds in a completely analytic way. Let
(\Phi:H\!\to\!G,\triangleright)
be a nonabelian Lie crossed module,
and let
(\alpha,\beta)
be a smooth
2
–connection on
M
.
The field
\alpha
plays the role of an ordinary gauge potential,
while
\beta
serves as its higher analogue, a
nonabelian
B
–field taking values in
\mathfrak{h}
. The pair
(\alpha,\beta)
defines, via multiplicative integration,
a surface holonomy

W(\Sigma)=\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta\mid\Sigma)\in H,

(37)

which we interpret as the nonabelian Wilson surface operator.
When
\Sigma
is a worldsheet with fixed boundary string
\gamma
,
the element
W(\Sigma)
provides the parallel transport of a
“string endpoint” in the higher gauge field
(\alpha,\beta)
.

The Stokes formula
(
31
) shows that under a deformation of the worldsheet
through a
3
–chain
V
one has

W(\Sigma_{1})=\Phi^{-1}\!\Big(\mathrm{MI}(\alpha\mid\partial V)\Big)\cdot W(\Sigma_{0})\,\exp_{H}\!\Big(\!\int_{V}\!\mathcal{H}\Big),

(38)

so that the failure of surface holonomy to be homotopy–invariant is
measured by the
3
–curvature
\mathcal{H}=d\beta+\alpha\!\triangleright\!\beta
.
In particular, if
\mathcal{H}=0
the holonomy depends only on the
boundary loop and hence defines a flat higher parallel transport.
For nonzero
\mathcal{H}
, the exponential
\exp_{H}(\int_{V}\mathcal{H})

acts as a nonabelian flux phase, generalizing the Wess–Zumino
term to a noncommutative setting. This analytic construction therefore gives a rigorous definition of
nonabelian
B
–field holonomy in higher gauge theory.

5.2
Gauge anomalies

To illustrate the physical meaning of the non–abelian Stokes relation
(
31
), consider the crossed module

(\Phi:U(1)\hookrightarrow SU(2),\triangleright)
,
where
\Phi
is the inclusion of the diagonal torus.
Let
\alpha=A
be a smooth
\mathfrak{su}(2)
–valued
connection
1
–form on a space–time manifold
M
, and

\beta=B
a
\mathfrak{u}(1)
–valued
2
–form (the
B
-field).
The
2
-connection
(A,B)
has
3
–curvature

\mathcal{H}=dB+A\triangleright B=dB+[A,B],

(39)

which is the standard gauge–covariant field strength
appearing in nonabelian string backgrounds

;
green2
.
For two worldsheet surfaces
\Sigma_{0}
and
\Sigma_{1}
with the same
boundary string
\gamma=\partial\Sigma_{i}
, we have shown that the multiplicative integral
satisfies

\mathrm{MI}(A,B\mid\Sigma_{1})=\mathrm{MI}(A,B\mid\Sigma_{0})\exp_{U(1)}\!\Big(\!\int_{V}(dB+[A,B])\!\Big),

(40)

where
V
is any
3
–chain with
\partial V=\Sigma_{1}-\Sigma_{0}

In physical terms, the exponential factor represents the change of the
worldsheet phase under a deformation of the surface in a nonabelian
background field.

The infinitesimal variation of this phase under a gauge transformation

g:M\to SU(2)
with parameter
\Lambda=g^{-1}\!dg

is obtained via

A\longmapsto g^{-1}Ag+g^{-1}dg,\qquad B\longmapsto g^{-1}\!\triangleright B.

(41)

A short computation gives

\delta\mathcal{H}=d(A\triangleright B)-A\triangleright(dB)=[F_{A},B],\qquad F_{A}=dA+A\wedge A,

(42)

hence the gauge variation of the surface phase is

\delta\Phi_{\mathrm{WZ}}=i\!\!\int_{V}\mathrm{tr}(F_{A}\wedge B),

which is the familiar gauge–covariant anomaly phase for the

In the heterotic string effective action, this correction combines with
the Lorentz curvature term
\mathrm{tr}(R\wedge R)
to yield the
modified Bianchi identity

dH=\mathrm{tr}(F_{A}\wedge F_{A})-\mathrm{tr}(R\wedge R),\qquad H=dB+[A,B],

ensuring the gauge and gravitational anomaly cancellation

Our analytic identity (

rigorous mathematical realization of the nonabelian

In this example, the
H
–valued multiplicative integral
plays the role of a nonabelian Wilson surface, measuring the phase accumulated by a string worldsheet in the

(A,B)
and satisfies the precise higher Stokes relation
expected from the categorical structure of a

The curvature term

governs the failure of

.

5.3
Chern-Simons boundary phases

Another physical context in which the
3
D Stokes relation
(
31
) appears naturally is Chern–Simons theory.
Let
M
be a compact oriented
3
–manifold with boundary
\Sigma
,
and let
\alpha
be a
\mathfrak{g}
–valued connection
1
–form.
The classical Chern–Simons functional is

S_{\mathrm{CS}}(\alpha)=\frac{k}{4\pi}\int_{M}\mathrm{Tr}\!\left(\alpha\wedge d\alpha+\tfrac{2}{3}\,\alpha\wedge\alpha\wedge\alpha\right),

(45)

where
k\in\mathbb{Z}
is the level.
Under a gauge transformation
g\colon M\to G
, the connection transforms as

\alpha^{g}=g^{-1}\alpha g+g^{-1}dg
and one has the standard Chern-Simons variation

S_{\mathrm{CS}}(\alpha^{g})-S_{\mathrm{CS}}(\alpha)=\frac{k}{12\pi}\!\int_{M}\mathrm{Tr}\!\big((g^{-1}dg)^{\!3}\big)+\frac{k}{4\pi}\!\int_{\partial M}\mathrm{Tr}\!\big(g^{-1}dg\wedge\alpha\big),

whose first term involves the closed
3

\omega_{3}(g)=\tfrac{1}{3!}\mathrm{Tr}((g^{-1}dg)^{3})
.
The integral of this
3

bulk phase which measures the anomaly inflow from
M
to its boundary
\Sigma
.

We note here that the same structural pattern is heuristically encoded in the Stokes theorem
(

\beta=\mathrm{Tr}\!\left(\alpha\wedge d\alpha+\tfrac{2}{3}\,\alpha\wedge\alpha\wedge\alpha\right),\qquad\mathcal{H}=d\beta=\mathrm{Tr}\!\big((d\alpha+\alpha^{2})^{\!2}\big).

In that case,
\beta
plays the role of a
B
–field potential, and

\mathcal{H}
is its corresponding
3
–form curvature.
The exponential factor
\exp(i\!\int_{V}\mathcal{H})

in (
31
)
has the same form as the Chern–Simons gauge variation:
it represents the bulk phase that compensates the boundary change of the
holonomy under a deformation of the region
M
.
This correspondence is of course heuristic but may be viewed as an analytic reformulation of the anomaly inflow relation.

In this interpretation, the multiplicative integral

\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta|\Sigma)

represents the boundary Wilson surface operator
induced by the bulk Chern–Simons form.
Applying the
3

\mathrm{MI}(\alpha,\beta|\partial M)=\exp_{H}\!\Big(\!\int_{M}\!\mathcal{H}\Big),

(48)

so that the boundary phase is the multiplicative holonomy of the

3
–form curvature

.
This analytic statement mirrors the anomaly inflow mechanism, since a bulk gauge transformation produces a boundary phase given by the
exponential of the
3
–form flux.
From the viewpoint of topological field theory,
the same structure appears in the effective actions of

(2{+}1)
–dimensional topological phases such as the
quantum Hall effect and topological insulators,
where the quantization of
\int_{M}\mathcal{H}

ensures that
\exp(iS_{\mathrm{CS}})
is single–valued.
The multiplicative integral formulation makes this quantization condition
explicit even for nonabelian gauge groups. Although the comparison is not yet rigorous, this correspondence points to a potential bridge between
higher gauge geometry, Chern–Simons anomalies,
and quantized boundary responses in topological field theories.

6
Conclusion

In contrast to the bundle gerbe formulation, which
typically treats only the abelian case
H=U(1)
, and to the categorical
approach of Schreiber and Waldorf
schreiber2
;
waldorf
,
we have in this article developed a framework which provides a direct differential geometry
realization of higher-dimensional parallel transport which is valid for arbitrary Lie crossed modules.
Building on Yekutieli’s local multiplicative Stokes theorem,
we have established a global analytic identity expressing the
difference between two surface holonomies as the exponential of the
integrated
3
–curvature.
This result supplies the precise sense in which the surface phase in a
nonabelian string background changes by the exponential of the
integrated
3
–form flux. In particular, the framework developed here links the theory of multiplicative
integration directly to the transport
2
–functor formalism of
Schreiber and Waldorf, showing that both describe the same geometric
structure from complementary viewpoints. It therefore establishes that multiplicative integration is not only a computational method, but is actually a concrete model for higher
parallel transport and surface holonomy in nonabelian gauge theory.
At the same time, it provides a rigorous mathematical underpinning for
the surface terms that appear in string and conformal field theories,
realizing the Wess–Zumino phase as a genuine (not merely a formal) Stokes identity in
differential geometry.

Beyond this direct analytic formulation, the present results offer
several directions for further study.
On the mathematical side, one may seek to extend Yekutieli’s
construction to higher dimensions, defining multiplicative integrals
over
n
–simplices and establishing analytic Stokes theorems for
crossed
n
–modules or higher
L_{\infty}
–algebra connections.
This would provide a differential foundation for the

(n+1)
–form curvatures which appear in higher gauge and
supergravity theories.
Another direction concerns the relation between the present approach and those which use Deligne or differential cohomology, potentially leading
to explicit integral formulas for the characteristic classes of higher
bundles. On the physics side, we expect that the same analytic structures should
govern the boundary anomalies and flux quantization conditions in
nonabelian string and M–theory backgrounds.
In particular, the appearance of the
3
–curvature as the obstruction
to homotopy invariance of surface holonomy suggests a direct analytic
interpretation of the Green–Schwarz mechanism and the Chern–Simons
terms in heterotic string compactifications.
It would be of interest to investigate whether multiplicative
integration can be used to construct higher analogues of Wilson loops
and to describe quantized response phases in
(2+1)
–dimensional
topological field theories and condensed matter systems.

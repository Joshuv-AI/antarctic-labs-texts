Predictions of postoperative and perioperative complications of laparoscopic cholecystectomy using machine learning algorithms: systematic review
Authors: Shahzeb Leghari, Muhammad Tausif, Rooma Rehan, Wajiha Ikram, Raziel Santos, Muhammad Usman Haider
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLaparoscopic cholecystectomy (LC) is a widely performed procedure with potential postoperative and perioperative complications. Recent advances in machine learning (ML) can lead to early prediction of these complications, but no systematic review has synthesized this data. This review aims to assess ML algorithms’ accuracy in predicting these complications following LC.MethodsA systematic review was conducted by PRISMA guidelines. A comprehensive search was performed on PubMed, Embase, Scopus, and Web of Science databases for studies published between 2010 and 2024. Studies that applied ML algorithms to predict complications during and after LC were included. Quality assessment was performed using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS). Due to study heterogeneity, a meta-analysis was not conducted; instead, a narrative synthesis was performed.ResultsA total of 6 studies were included in the review. Various machine learning algorithms, such as decision trees, deep learning, artificial neural networks (ANN), and adaptive boosting, were assessed for predicting postoperative and perioperative complications after laparoscopic cholecystectomy (LC). ANN models showed superior performance, with mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) values ranging from 4.20 to 8.60% in predicting quality of life post-LC. Deep learning models achieved a balanced accuracy of 71.4% for critical view of safety (CVS) assessment during LC. Adaboost algorithms effectively identified key risk factors for hepatic fibrosis in post-cholecystectomy patients. However, models predicting surgical adverse events faced limitations due to low prevalence, resulting in lower predictive values.ConclusionML models show great potential in predicting postoperative complications following LC while also considering intraoperative and perioperative outcomes that impact patient safety and postoperative recovery, but limitations such as small sample sizes and limited applicability remain. Further research is needed to validate these models in larger, more diverse populations.Keywords: Machine learning, Laparoscopic cholecystectomy, Postoperative complications, Perioperative complications, Surgical complications, Artificial intelligence, Cholecystectomy outcomes

Background

The lifestyles have evolved over time due to the economy’s constant growth, and a large number of individuals have benign gallbladder illnesses [1]. Research has shown that the prevalence of gallstones ranges from 9.9 to 13% in China [2], whereas, in the US, the rates of gallstones are 7.2%, cholecystitis is 2.1–4.2%, and gallbladder polyps are 4.9–5.86% [3]. Benign gallbladder illness often poses no danger to life [4]. According to studies, having gallbladder illness increases the risk of cardiovascular disease and gallbladder cancer, which puts a greater financial strain on the patient’s family [2].

Benign gallbladder problems may be effectively treated with surgery, particularly with laparoscopic cholecystectomy (LC) [5]. According to reports, LC may speed up healing, lessen trauma and problems, and enhance quality of life after surgery. Its effects on renal and liver function are negligible. The right upper abdomen is the primary surgical site, and although LC surgery does not immediately harm the gastrointestinal system, the recovery of gastrointestinal function after surgery is impacted by the administration of steady analgesics [6]. Complications with LC surgery include significant intestinal paralysis and difficulty voiding, affecting 27.6% of patients [7].

For the majority of cases, Laparoscopic Cholecystectomy, a highly standardized operation, has taken the role of open surgery. For many scientific problems, it acts as a model for machine learning (ML). Throughout industrialized nations, LC is carried out hundreds of thousands of times annually and is readily available for video capture in normal clinical procedures. The identification of laparoscopic surgical tools, anatomical features, and even the prediction of the surgical course are the primary uses of machine learning-based solution [8]. Moreover, LC is a well-respected paradigm for enhancing computer- and robotic-assisted surgery [9]. Potential input for machine learning applications includes sensor, video, and clinical data; these are often implemented as convolutional neural networks (CNNs). Research may freely access datasets like Cholec80 or CholecSeg8k that include easily annotated video footage and clinical characteristics. Despite the progress in identifying the surgical phases and adverse events, it is questionable whether LC is a suitable model surgery for postoperative and perioperative outcome prediction in an unselected real-world population [8].

Recent advances in machine learning (ML) have shown potential in predicting these complications, but no systematic review has comprehensively assessed ML’s predictive role in this specific context. While individual studies have explored various ML models for surgical outcomes, there is a clear gap in the literature regarding their collective efficacy in LC. This review aims to synthesize existing evidence on ML algorithms used to predict postoperative and perioperative complications in LC, evaluate their accuracy, and identify areas for future research.

Methodology

Study design

This system review was carried out according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines to compare the predictive ability of machine learning (ML) algorithms in recognizing postoperative and perioperative complications after laparoscopic cholecystectomy (LC).

Literature search strategy

The literature was systematically searched according to the PRISMA guidelines and the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions (version 6.0). Electronic databases PubMed, Embase, Scopus, and Web of Science were searched to find relevant studies between 2010 and 2024. A combination of Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) and free-text words for “machine learning” and “laparoscopic cholecystectomy” and their synonyms was used to create the search strategy. Boolean operators (AND, OR) were applied for narrowing the search. A comprehensive breakdown of the search terms for every database can be found in the supplementary materials. Two reviewers independently carried out the literature search, and disagreements were addressed by discussion with a third reviewer.

Eligibility criteria

Studies were eligible if they used machine learning (ML) algorithms to forecast perioperative outcomes, including postoperative complications and intraoperative events, of laparoscopic cholecystectomy (LC) in human subjects. Post operative complications considered included, clinical outcomes such as anastomotic leakage, postoperative ileus, surgical site infection, hemorrhage, bile duct injury, and re-admission rates. Intraoperative and other perioperative outcomes such as operative time, blood loss, conversion to open surgery, and intraoperative complications were also considered if reported in the context of AI application. Articles published in English peer-reviewed journals with well-described ML methodologies, validation methods, and performance measures were considered.

Review articles, editorials, letters, and conference abstracts were excluded. Studies that did not specifically use ML algorithms to predict postoperative and perioperative complications, non-English language journals, and studies with inadequate data reporting, vague methodological descriptions, or no validation of ML models were excluded as well.

Study selection

In the initial screening process, articles were considered if they outlined the use of machine learning (ML) in general surgery to ensure the selection of studies with duplicate content. Then, studies were chosen according to the following inclusion criteria, use of ML algorithms to predict postoperative and perioperative complications after laparoscopic cholecystectomy (LC), clinical trials on human subjects, and studies performed on adult populations.

Two independent reviewers (SL and RS) conducted the title and abstract screening based on the predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. Studies were eligible for full-text screening only if both reviewers agreed on their inclusion. Any disagreements were resolved through discussion, with a third reviewer (MT) consulted when necessary to reach a consensus.

Risk of bias and quality assessment

The Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS) was employed to assess methodological quality and risk of bias within the included studies. The instrument has been used to measure selection bias, comparability of study groups, and outcome assessment. The individual study was assessed on the basis of its overall NOS score and classified as low, moderate, or high quality.

For maintaining unbiased assessment, two separate reviewers performed the risk of bias assessment, and disagreements were resolved by discussion or referral to a third reviewer. Low NOS scores were regarded as high risk of bias, and higher NOS scores were regarded as methodologically valid.

Data synthesis and analysis

The following data items were extracted independently from each study: year of publication, location of study, sample size, study design, type of laparoscopic cholecystectomy undertaken, machine learning (ML) algorithm applied, reason for ML application, outcome measures, and predictive performance metrics. Studies were grouped according to the ML methodology and predicted outcomes, including postoperative and perioperative complications, quality of life measurements, and surgical risk factors.

For every study, the major performance measures, such as accuracy, area under the curve (AUC), sensitivity, and specificity, were noted to compare the predictive efficiency of ML models. Mean accuracy (ACC) and AUC were computed to provide a summary of predictive performance of various ML models in studies. Descriptive statistics were employed to calculate the median and range of predictive accuracies for individual ML algorithms.

Result

The search strategy provided a total of 1156 studies from the preferred databases including 460 from PubMed, 320 from Scopus, 210 from Web of Science, and 166 from Embase, eliminating 241 duplicate articles 915 studies were left for screening on the basis of their titles and abstracts. After this initial screening 794 studies were excluded for not fulfilling the inclusion criteria leaving 121 full-text articles for eligibility assessment. Of these, 115 studies were excluded for reasons including: lack of use of ML algorithms for postoperative/perioperative complication prediction (n = 45), insufficient methodological detail (n = 30), absence of model validation (n = 20), non-English language (n = 10), and article types such as editorials or conference abstracts (n = 10). Finally, 6 studies were included for systematic review and meta-analysis (Fig. 1).

PRISMA flow diagram depicting the study selection process for the systematic review

These studies had explored the application of machine learning (ML) algorithms for post-operative or preoperative complication prediction following laparoscopic cholecystectomy. Different machine learning methods were applied in the mentioned papers, i.e., Neural Networks, Decision Trees, Random Forest, Support Vector Machines (SVM), and Gradient Boosting Models (GBM).

Among the significant outcomes that were assessed, most studies were concerned with predicting post-operative complications, while others were concerned with perioperative complications, disease progression, baselines and other important variables that influence surgical outcomes. Heterogeneous sample sizes and methodologies ranging from retrospective cohort studies to prospective experimental model were included in the study.

Study characteristics

Six studies assessing machine learning models for predicting surgical outcomes following laparoscopic cholecystectomy (LC) were included. Different study designs were represented: one longitudinal prospective study (n = 1, 17%), two retrospective cohort models (n = 2, 33%), one prospective experimental study (n = 1, 17%), one retrospective cross-sectional study (n = 1, 17%), and one retrospective model development study (n = 1, 17%).

There was variation in sample size per study, from small patient cohorts (100 patients for the prospective experimental study) to large datasets (2,854 images from 201 LC recordings for the model development study). The largest of the retrospective cohort studies enrolled 748 participants from 22 institutions. The majority of the studies were based on patients undergoing LC or having post-surgical conditions, i.e., MASLD following cholecystectomy. Different machine learning methods were utilized to forecast outcomes, such as decision tree analysis, adaptive boosting, deep learning, and artificial neural networks.

The research evaluated the precision of ML models in forecasting surgical outcomes, risk factors, and recovery periods. Different clinical and quality-of-life markers were employed as forecasted outcomes, showing the versatility of machine learning in surgical decision-making and patient management (Tables 1 and 2).

Decision tree analysis

A decision tree model by Lopez-Lopez et al. (1990–2020) was employed to make a prediction for the outcome of initial and ultimate repairs in cases of Iatrogenic Bile Duct Injury (IBDI) due to laparoscopic cholecystectomy [10]. The risk-scoring system resulted in an accuracy of 82.3% in the development cohort and 71.7% in the validation cohort. The model reflected 82.8% accuracy in the prediction of the efficacy of early repairs. Though the main emphasis was put on the prediction of success of repair, the study gave clues to enhance decision-making and minimize the occurrence of postoperative bile duct complications.

Deep learning (Image Segmentation)

Mascagni et al. (2022) created a deep learning algorithm for anatomical segmentation and assessment of the critical view of safety (CVS) parameters during laparoscopic cholecystectomy to reduce bile duct damage [11]. The model achieved an intersection over the union mean of 66.6% for image segmentation and balanced accuracy of 71.4% for testing CVS criteria. Although mainly intended to minimize intraoperative bile duct injuries, its efficacy in safety assessment automation implies reduced postoperative complications from such injuries.

Artificial neural networks (ANN)

A prospective study by Hon-Yi Shi et al. (2012) used various machine learning models such as Artificial Neural Networks (ANN), Support Vector Machines (SVM), Gaussian Process Regression (GPR), and Multiple Linear Regression (MLR) to predict post-laparoscopic cholecystectomy quality of life (QOL) [12]. ANN models performed best, with the mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) values being between 4.20% and 8.60%. The research highlighted the importance of preoperative functional status as a predictor of recovery, resulting in enhanced postoperative outcomes, even though it was concerned with long-term QOL instead of acute postoperative complications.

Adaptive boosting

A study by Miguel Suárez et al. (2023) employed the Adaboost algorithm to forecast liver fibrosis and determine influential risk factors in patients suffering from metabolically associated steatohepatitis liver disease (MASLD) following cholecystectomy [13]. The model precisely forecast the advancement of hepatic fibrosis, and type-2 diabetes, dyslipidemia, and platelet count were found to be major risk factors. While the main emphasis was laid on hepatic fibrosis, the research explained the application of machine learning in the treatment of postoperative complications of liver function.

AI-Based CT image segmentation

A prospective experiment study by Manyun Bai et al. (2021) employed AI-driven CT image segmentation to forecast recovery outcomes of time to first exhaust/defecation, bowel sound recovery, and pain during and after laparoscopic cholecystectomy [14]. Patients receiving balanced anesthesia recovered much more quickly compared to patients receiving general anesthesia (P < 0.05). AI segmentation of CT images has demonstrated an accuracy of 81%, enhancing preoperative radiological assessment. While this suggests potential utility in guiding surgical planning, direct evidence correlating this with improved intraoperative precision and postoperative outcomes remains limited.

ML-Based predictive models

Using German Diagnosis-Related Group (DRG)81 data, Maximilian Berlet et al. (2022) investigated the application of machine learning-based prediction models to detect intraoperative and postoperative adverse events in patients undergoing laparoscopic cholecystectomy [8]. Predictive value was, however, restricted in unselected groups due to the low occurrence of adverse events in this population, resulting in low positive predictive value (PPV) and negative predictive value (NPV). The research indicates that while postoperative problems are rare in typical clinical settings, machine learning may be able to forecast them, however its usefulness may be limited.

Discussion

Laparoscopic cholecystectomy (LC) is progressing in many unexpected ways; however, complication prediction is still a major clinical concern, typically resulting in delayed treatment and increased patient morbidity. Machine learning (ML) is now considered as a revolutionary force, holding the guarantee of improving surgical decision-making and patient safety. This systematic review assesses the impact of ML-based predictive models, from decision trees to deep learning, on the future of laparoscopic cholecystectomy (LC). Evidence indicates that such models not only enhance complication prediction but also provide real-time intraoperative guidance. Yet, differences in research methodologies, patient databases, and ML algorithms pose opportunities as well as challenges to applying these applications in broad clinical practice.

While machine learning has much potential in LC, there still exist many areas of literature that need to be addressed before achieving widespread uptake. The major limitation in these studies is the lack of external validation, which creates doubt about the generalizability of ML models to different patient populations [15]. More of the accuracy-driven ML techniques involve an explanation model as much as it makes it hard for clinicians to trust and integrate the model in real-time decision-making [16]. Also, most of the studies have been done with retrospective data that limit their applicability, as machine learning models built onto ‘yesterday stuff’ are not necessarily going to work in present-day surgical environments [17]. Addressing these gaps through prospective studies, interdisciplinary collaborations, and regulatory frameworks will be critical to enhancing ML’s role in LC.

A number of the reviewed studies by Lopez-Lopez et al. and Mascagni et al. explain the potential of ML models like decision tree analysis and deep learning segmentation to make accurate predictions regarding particular outcomes such as initial bile duct injury (IBDI) repair success and anatomical safety evaluation in LC [10, 11].The study found that machine learning (ML) may impact surgical decision-making, especially reducing complication risk. For instance, when deep learning is applied for anatomical segmentation, it can guide the surgeon to important structures to avoid bile duct injuries, which are among the worst complications in laparoscopic cholecystectomy (LC). However, for clinical applications, these results need validation on larger and varied datasets [10, 11, 17].

However, several studies also point to limitations of ML models when it comes down to real-world clinical applications. For instance, adequately large study samples, such as the retrospective study conducted by Maximilian Berlet et al., illustrate the challenges of ML applicability in actual clinical settings [1]. As LC is associated with fewer side effects, this affected the outcome probabilities of ML models, while rare but clinically important complications could not be evaluated correctly due to class imbalance [8].

Smith et al. (2023) also concluded that classic logistic regression models, rather than ML models, assess low-incidence complications in a better way; the design of the former is better suited to handle imbalanced data. This should be an important consideration in the future refinement of ML models. Operationally, overcoming this limitation will require good data collection mechanisms, algorithm optimization aimed at imbalanced datasets, and adopting precision-related performance measures such as area under the precision-recall curve [18].

Apart from being used to predict surgical complications, ML models are also well-known in personalizing care, based on individual patients’ needs. For example, Hon-Yi Shi et al.’s study found that functional status was one of the strongest predictors of post-LC Quality of Life (QOL), and Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) performed better than Support Vector Machines (SVM) and Multiple Linear Regression (MLR).This showcases the ability of ML to integrate numerous variables related to patients to determine risk on a patient-to-patient basis. The aim is to develop a system where machine learning can swiftly process patient and hospital data, providing timely, reliable information to support clinicians in making quick, well-informed decisions, particularly during high-pressure scenarios such as emergency operations [12].

While optimistic for the opportunities that machine learning provides for reducing costs in health care and enhancing resource allocation, such a potential deserves elaboration in the real world. The study by Miguel Suárez et al. demonstrated, for liver fibrosis in MASLD following cholecystectomy, that the Adaboost algorithm was able to highlight such major risk factors as platelet count and dyslipidemia [13]. Such findings could inform more tailored perioperative care. However, a comparative analysis of ML models across different hospitals and patient demographics is lacking, making it difficult to assess model robustness [19]. It is necessary to incorporate ML into standardized clinical practices to allay ethical issues, preserve data privacy, and overcome clinician resistance to technology adoption [19].

The incorporation of ML into LC carries important ramifications for the care of the individual patient. Where ML risk assessment models enable the identification of patients at risk prior to surgery, surgeons can enhance perioperative planning and personalize their interventions accordingly [20]. According to Gupta et al. (2023), ML-based real-time guidance during surgery can enhance anatomical visualization and lower the risk of complications like bile duct injury [21]. Additionally, ML models can be used to detect complications early after surgery, which could reduce readmission rates and shorten hospital stays [22]. Nevertheless, there are still some issues around the model interpretability and clinician acceptance. To achieve widespread adoption, it is going to be very important to address these using explainable AI frameworks and prospective validation studies [20–22].

The key limitation in this systematic review is heterogeneity in the studies which precluded head-to-head comparison of studies. Machine learning (ML) has the potential to transform how doctors make decisions and manage patients undergoing laparoscopic cholecystectomy (LC). However, its use in real-world surgical practice is still in its early stages.

A major challenge is that different ML models, patient groups, and ways of measuring outcomes make it hard to compare results across studies. Moreover, many studies are based on small sampling sizes which do not allow for the generalization of results across the broader population. Additional limitations include limited eligibility criteria for inclusion in the databases, which can lead to bias, as well as language barriers. Further, many studies lack long-term follow-up, making finding out the actual influence of ML on surgical results difficult over time.

Research data needs to be cleaned before more reliably predictive models can be developed to test those against larger samples, enabling comparison to integrate machine learning into surgical practice. This will make postoperative and perioperative improvements for laparoscopic cholecystectomy patients affording better healthcare. They will also form a bridge for the integration of machine learning (ML) into the routine practice of surgery. The Newcastle–Ottawa Scale was used to assess study quality; however, the TRIPOD-AI guidelines may offer a more appropriate framework for evaluating AI model-based research, and future studies should consider its application.

Conclusion

In particular, models like artificial neural networks (ANN) and deep learning show promising accuracy in specific outcomes like quality of life and safety assessments. The review highlights the potential of machine learning algorithms in predicting postoperative and perioperative outcomes following laparoscopic cholecystectomy. Though machine learning has significant effect in this field, its implementation in routine clinical practice is still in its infancy. This is because the prediction performance for uncommon adverse effects is still restricted owing to their low incidence. To improve the accuracy and applicability of c v these prediction models in practical contexts, further research with wider datasets and a more varied population is required.

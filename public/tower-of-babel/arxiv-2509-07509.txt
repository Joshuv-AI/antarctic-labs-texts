Quasi-Monte Carlo integration over ℝ s \mathbb{R}^{s} with boundary-damping importance sampling Thanks: Submitted to the editors DATE.
Zexin Pan, Du Ouyang, Zhijian He

Abstract
This paper proposes a new importance sampling (IS) that is tailored to quasi-Monte Carlo (QMC) integration over ℝ s \mathbb{R}^{s} . IS introduces a multiplicative adjustment to the integrand by compensating the sampling from the proposal instead of the target distribution. Improper proposals result in severe adjustment factor for QMC. Our strategy is to first design a adjustment factor to meet desired regularities and then determine a tractable transport map from the standard uniforms to the proposal for using QMC quadrature points as inputs. The transport map has the effect of damping the boundary growth of the resulting integrand so that the effectiveness of QMC can be reclaimed. Under certain conditions on the original integrand, our proposed IS enjoys a fast convergence rate independently of the dimension s s , making it amenable to high-dimensional problems.



keywords

Quasi-Monte Carlo, importance sampling, transport maps

Introduction

Quasi-Monte Carlo (QMC) has gained its success in many fields, including computational finance
[
20
,
37
]
, uncertainty quantification
[
15
,
31
]
. Although it has the potential to improve the convergence rate of plain Monte Carlo, the gain of QMC depends on the regularity of the underlying functions and the sampling proposals. Importance sampling (IS) provides a way to choose tractable sampling proposals instead of the underlying distribution, which is a widely used variance reduction technique in the Monte Carlo setting
[
30
]
. IS is more than just a variance reduction method. Particularly, it is used within Bayesian statistics and Bayesian inverse problems as an approximation of the target distribution by weighted samples that are generated from some typical proposals
[
2
]
. Recently, IS is combined with QMC to achieve a faster rate of convergence
[
12
,
25
,
37
]
. IS can bring enormous gains in QMC, and it can also backfire, yielding a severe integrand with superfast growth boundary
[
25
]
when simple QMC would have had a more regularity. It is an open problem to choose proposals in IS to optimize the performance of QMC. In this paper, we propose a new IS to dampen the growth of the integrand around the boundary of the domain, which is preferable for QMC
integration of unbound integrands
[
12
,
29
]
.

Throughout this paper, we let
\mathbb{R}=(-\infty,\infty)
and
\mathbb{I}=(0,1)
. We use
\boldsymbol{x}
for coordinates in
\mathbb{R}^{s}
and
\boldsymbol{u}
for coordinates in
\mathbb{I}^{s}
. Consider integral of the form

\mu=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{s}}f(\boldsymbol{x})\prod_{j=1}^{s}\varphi(x_{j})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x},

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{s}\to\mathbb{R}
is a real-valued integrand and
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a probability density function. Let
\Phi(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\varphi(y)\,\mathrm{d}y
be the cumulative distribution function (CDF) and
\Phi^{-1}:\mathbb{I}\to\mathbb{R}
be the inverse CDF (quantile function). QMC integration takes quadrature points in the unit cube. Before using QMC, the sampling proposal needs to be expressed as a transformation of the standard uniform distribution, say
T:\mathbb{I}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{s}
. The transformation
T
is also called a transport map in the literature
[
19
]
. The dimension
d
does not necessarily agree with
s
, but for simplicity we take
d=s
in this paper. To estimate
\mu
, we consider estimators of the form

\hat{\mu}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}w(\boldsymbol{u}_{i})f\circ T(\boldsymbol{u}_{i})\quad\text{for}\quad T(\boldsymbol{u})=(T_{1}(u_{1}),\dots,T_{s}(u_{s})),

(1)

where
\{\boldsymbol{u}_{0},\dots,\boldsymbol{u}_{n-1}\}\subseteq\mathbb{I}^{s}
is a deterministic QMC point set or randomized QMC (RQMC) point set for easy of error estimation
[
18
,
30
]
, the transport map
T_{j}:\mathbb{I}\to\mathbb{R}
is differentiable and the
weight function

w(\boldsymbol{u})=\prod_{j=1}^{s}w_{j}(u_{j})\quad\text{for}\quad w_{j}(u)=T_{j}^{\prime}(u)\varphi\circ T_{j}(u).

IS also takes the form (
1
), in which the sampling proposal has independent marginals
T(u_{j})
and
w(\boldsymbol{u})
is known as likelihood ratio. Many QMC methods proposed in the literature share this form. Examples are:

•

T_{j}(u)=\Phi^{-1}(u)
, commonly known as inversion methods
[
7
,
21
,
29
,
36
]
. For this case,
w(\boldsymbol{u})=1
.

•

T_{j}(u)=a_{j}u+b_{j}(1-u)
for
a_{j},b_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
, commonly known as truncation methods
[
4
,
9
,
16
,
24
]
.

•

T_{j}(u)=\Phi^{-1}_{\nu}(u)
with
\Phi^{-1}_{\nu}(u)
the inverse CDF of a Student’s
t
-distribution with
\nu
degree of freedom
[
25
]
. A recent paper
[
33
]
considers the Möbius transformation
T_{j}(u)=-\cot(\pi u)
, which can be viewed as a special case since
\cot(\pi u)
is the inverse CDF of a Cauchy distribution.

We also note that there are many interesting methods beyond the above form
[
3
,
8
,
12
,
19
,
34
]
.

While previous studies focus on the choice of
T_{j}
and the resulting smoothness of
w_{j}
, we take a different perspective: we first design
w_{j}
with required smoothness and then derive
T_{j}
by solving the differential equation
w_{j}(u)=T_{j}^{\prime}(u)\varphi\circ T_{j}(u)
, yielding

T_{j}(u)=\Phi^{-1}\Big(\int_{0}^{u}w_{j}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\Big).

(2)

Specifically, we choose
w_{j}
from a one-parameter family as follows. Let
\eta:[0,1/2]\to[0,1/2]
be a differentiable monotonic function satisfying
\eta(0)=0
and
\eta(1/2)=1/2
. For
\theta\in(0,1/2]
, we define

w_{\theta}(u)=\begin{cases}(1-\theta)^{-1}\eta(u/\theta),&\text{ if }u\in(0,\theta/2]\\
(1-\theta)^{-1}(1-\eta(1-u/\theta)),&\text{ if }u\in(\theta/2,\theta)\\
(1-\theta)^{-1},&\text{ if }u\in[\theta,1/2]\\
w_{\theta}(1-u),&\text{ if }u\in(1/2,1),\\
\end{cases}

(3)

where

\eta(u)=\eta_{p}(u)=\begin{cases}2^{-p-2}u^{-p-1}\exp(2^{p}-u^{-p}),&\text{ if }u\in(0,1/2]\\
0,&\text{ if }u=0\end{cases}\text{ for }\ p\geq 1.

(4)

While there are many admissible choices for
\eta
, we choose the function (
4
) for its simplicity and computational convenience. By construction,
w_{\theta}(u)=w_{\theta}(1-u)
,
\sup_{u\in\mathbb{I}}w_{\theta}(u)=(1-\theta)^{-1}
and
\int_{0}^{1}w_{\theta}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u=1
.
Figure
1
shows the case of
w_{\theta}(u)
with
\theta=0.2
and
p=1
, which decays to zero quickly as
u
approaches
0
or
1
.

Figure 1
:
An illustration of
w_{\theta}(u)
with
\theta=0.2
and
p=1
.

Given
\{\theta_{j}\mid j\in 1{:}s\}\subseteq(0,1/2]
and
p\geq 1
, we choose
w_{j}(u)=w_{\theta_{j}}(u)
for all
j=1,\dots,s
. Because
T_{j}
maps
\mathbb{I}
onto
\mathbb{R}
,

\int_{\mathbb{I}^{s}}w(\boldsymbol{u})f\circ T(\boldsymbol{u})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{u}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{s}}f(\boldsymbol{x})\prod_{j=1}^{s}\varphi(x_{j})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}=\mu.

(5)

Hence, estimating
\mu
is equivalent to integrating a new integrand
f^{w}(\boldsymbol{u}):=w(\boldsymbol{u})f\circ T(\boldsymbol{u})

over
\mathbb{I}^{s}
. The weight function
w(\boldsymbol{u})
can dampen the growth of the integrand
f^{w}(\boldsymbol{u})
around the boundary of
\mathbb{I}^{s}
. The proposed method is called the boundary-damping IS. Under appropriate choice of
\theta_{j}
, we show that
f^{w}
has sufficient smoothness and can be efficiently integrated even in high-dimensional settings. Particularly, the boundary-damping IS yields a dimension-independent mean squared error rate under certain conditions on the integrand.

Our main contribution is three-fold. Firstly, we provide a novel IS to reclaim the performance of QMC by damping the boundary growth of the integrand. Unlike Laplace-based IS
[
31
]
that uses Gaussian proposals, our proposal is not a commonly used distribution but is easy to simulate. Secondly, we provide a rigorous error analysis based on generalized Fourier coefficients with an application to scrambled digital nets
[
26
]
. Thirdly, under certain conditions on the function
f(\boldsymbol{x})
, our proposed IS can enjoy a faster convergence rate than Monte Carlo while breaking the curse of dimensionality, making it amenable to high-dimensional problems. Lattice-based QMC quadrature rules can be constructed to yield asymptotic error bound independently of the dimension for unbounded integrands in weighted reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces with POD (“product and order dependent”) weights
[
21
]
. Our analysis does not need to introduce weighted spaces and the digital net quadrature points are off-the-shelf.

The rest of this paper is organized as follow. Section
2
provides some preliminaries on function norms and digital nets. Section
3
studies upper bounds on generalized Fourier coefficients of the integrand. Section
4
investigates the norms of
f
and
f^{w}
. Section
5
conducts the numerical analysis on scrambled net-based integration. Numerical results are presented in Section
6
to show the effectiveness of our proposed method. Section
7
concludes this paper.

Preliminaries

We first introduce some notations that will be used in the following. For a vector
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{s}
and a subset
v\subseteq 1{:}s
,
\boldsymbol{x}_{v}
denotes the subvector of
\boldsymbol{x}
indexed by
v
, while
\boldsymbol{x}_{v^{c}}
denotes the subvector indexed by
1{:}s\setminus v
. Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of positive integers and
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. For
\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}
,
\boldsymbol{s}(\boldsymbol{k}):=\{j\in 1{:}s\mid k_{j}\neq 0\}
denotes the support of
\boldsymbol{k}
. Let
\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_{q}=(\sum_{j=1}^{s}|x_{j}|^{q})^{1/q}
for
q>0
. For a nonempty set
v\subseteq 1{:}s
, define the mixed derivative by

\partial^{v}f(\boldsymbol{x})=\left(\prod_{j\in v}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\right)f(\boldsymbol{x}),

and define
\partial^{\emptyset}f(\boldsymbol{x})=f(\boldsymbol{x})
by convention. Let
\mathbb{C}
be the set of complex numbers.
We define the operator

\mathcal{I}:L^{\infty}(\mathbb{I})\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{I})
by

\mathcal{I}(\phi)(u)=\int_{0}^{u}\phi(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.

All constants in this paper have an implicit dependency on
\varphi
and we suppress it from the notation for simplicity.

2.1
Function norms

We assume that the density
\varphi(x)
is a strictly positive, bounded, symmetric, light-tailed function in the following.

Assumption 1
.

Assume that
\varphi(x)>0
,
\varphi(x)=\varphi(-x)
,
\varphi_{\infty}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)<\infty
, and for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a constant
c_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\varphi(x)\geq c_{\varepsilon}\Phi(x)^{1+\varepsilon}
for any
x\leq 0
.

Under Assumption
1
, the CDF
\Phi(x)
and its inverse
\Phi^{-1}(u)
is differentiable and strictly increasing. It is easy to verify the Gaussian density
\varphi(x)=\exp(-x^{2}/2)/\sqrt{2\pi}
satisfies Assumption
1
due to the inequality
[
11
]

\int_{x}^{\infty}\varphi(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\frac{1}{x}\varphi(x)\text{ for }x>0.

The condition on symmetry can be dropped by imposing a proper condition on the right tail of the density
\varphi(x)
. For simplicity, we work on symmetrical densities.

This paper will use some function norms as defined in the following:

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{I}^{s})}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{I}^{s}}|f(\boldsymbol{u})|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{u}\Big)^{1/q}&0<q<\infty\\
\sup_{u\in\mathbb{I}^{s}}|f(\boldsymbol{u})|&q=\infty\end{cases},

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}

\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{s}}|f(\boldsymbol{x})|^{q}\prod_{j=1}^{s}\varphi(x_{j})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\Big)^{1/q},

\displaystyle\|f\|_{W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{v\subseteq 1{:}s}\|\partial^{v}f\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}\Big)^{1/q},

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sup_{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{s}}|f(\boldsymbol{x})|^{q}\prod_{j=1}^{s}\frac{\varphi(x_{j})}{\varphi_{\infty}}\Big)^{1/q},

\displaystyle\|f\|_{W^{1,q,\infty}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{v\subseteq 1{:}s}\|\partial^{v}f\|^{q}_{L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}\Big)^{1/q}.

Notice that for constant functions
f(\boldsymbol{x})=c
, all of the above norms reduce to
|c|
. More generally, if
f(\boldsymbol{x})
does not depend on
x_{v^{c}}
for
v\subseteq 1{:}s
, we can treat
f
either as a function over
\mathbb{R}^{s}
or a function over
\mathbb{R}^{|v|}
by removing redundant input variables
x_{v^{c}}
. Under either interpretation, the resulting norm is the same according to the definitions given above. This property will be useful later when we analyze the ANOVA decomposition of
f
. The next lemma shows
f\in L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)
almost implies
f\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)
.

Lemma 2
.

For
\varphi
satisfying Assumption
1
and
q>q^{\prime}\geq 1
,

\|f\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}\leq C^{s}_{q,q^{\prime}}\|f\|_{L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}

and

\|f\|_{W^{1,q^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}\leq\tilde{C}^{s}_{q,q^{\prime}}\|f\|_{W^{1,q,\infty}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)},

where
C_{q,q^{\prime}},\tilde{C}_{q,q^{\prime}}
are constants depending on
q
and
q^{\prime}
.

Proof.

See the appendix.

Remark 3
.

If
f\in W^{1,q,\infty}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)
and
\varphi(x)=\exp(-x^{2}/2)/\sqrt{2\pi}
, we have for any
v\subseteq 1{:}s
,

|\partial^{v}f(\boldsymbol{x})|\leq c\left(\prod_{j=1}^{s}\varphi(x_{j})\right)^{-1/q}=c^{\prime}\exp\left(\frac{1}{2q}\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_{2}^{2}\right)

for some
c,c^{\prime}>0
. This implies that
f(\boldsymbol{x})
belongs to the superfast growth class
G_{e}(1/(2q),c^{\prime},2)
defined in
[
25
]
. For
q>1
, RQMC integration with the usual inversion method yields a root mean squared error of
O(n^{-1+1/q+\epsilon})
for arbitrarily small
\epsilon>0

[
12
,
29
]
, where the implied constant depends on the dimension
s
and
\epsilon
.

2.2
ANOVA decomposition over
\mathbb{R}^{s}

Following the framework in
[
17
]
, we introduce the generalized ANOVA decomposition. Let
P_{j}:L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)\to L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)
denote the integration operator

P_{j}(f)(\boldsymbol{x})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\boldsymbol{x})\varphi(x_{j})\,\mathrm{d}x_{j}\text{ for }\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{s}.

Notice that
P_{j}(f)
does not depend on
x_{j}
and
P^{2}_{j}=P_{j}
. We further define the iterated integration operator
P_{v}=\prod_{j\in v}P_{j}
. By Fubini’s theorem,
P_{j}
in
P_{v}
can be applied in any order. By convention,
P_{\emptyset}=I
is the identity operator. The ANOVA decomposition of
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)
is given by

f=\sum_{v\subseteq 1{:}s}f_{v}\text{ for }f_{v}=\Big(\prod_{j\in v}(I-P_{j})\Big)P_{1{:}s\setminus v}f.

(6)

It follows that if
j\in v
,

P_{j}(f_{v})=(P_{j}-P_{j}^{2})\Big(\prod_{j^{\prime}\in v,j^{\prime}\neq j}(I-P_{j^{\prime}})\Big)P_{1{:}s\setminus v}f=0.

(7)

The next lemma shows each ANOVA component
f_{v}
inherits the smoothness of
f
.

Lemma 4
.

If
f\in W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)
, then
f_{v}\in W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)
for all
v\subseteq 1{:}s
. Furthermore, if
\varphi
satisfies Assumption
1
and
f\in W^{1,q,\infty}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)
with
q>1
, then
f_{v}\in W^{1,q,\infty}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)
for all
v\subseteq 1{:}s
.

Proof.

See the appendix.

2.3
Scrambled
(t,m,s)
-nets integration

In this paper, we use scrambled
(t,m,s)
-nets in base
b\geq 2
as the quadrature points in the estimator (
1
) as defined in the following.

Definition 5
.

For
t,\ m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and an integer
b\geq 2
, a set
\mathcal{P}:=\{\boldsymbol{u}_{0},\dots,\boldsymbol{u}_{b^{m}-1}\}
in
[0,1)^{s}
is called
a
(t,m,s)
-net in base
b
if every interval of the form
\prod_{j=1}^{s}\left[\frac{a_{j}}{b^{k_{j}}},\frac{a_{j}+1}{b^{k_{j}}}\right)
contains exactly
b^{t}
points of
\mathcal{P}
for all integers
a_{j}\in[0,b^{k_{j}})
and all
k_{j}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
satisfying
\sum_{j=1}^{s}k_{j}=m-t
. For
\emptyset\neq w\subseteq 1{:}s
, the projection of
\mathcal{P}
on coordinates
j\in w
forms a
(t_{w},m,|w|)
-net in base
b
, where
t_{\omega}\in\mathbb{N}
is called
t
-quality parameter and
t_{w}=t
when
w=1{:}s
. When performing Owen’s scrambling
[
26
]
on
\mathcal{P}
, the resulting point set is called the scrambled
(t,m,s)
-net, which is also a
(t,m,s)
-net with probability one.

Remark 6
.

As shown in
[
35
]
, for the Sobol’ sequence
[
32
]
and the Niederreiter sequence
[
22
]
, the
t
-quality parameters
t_{w}
satisfies that

t_{\omega}\leq\sum_{j\in\omega}t_{j}

(8)

with
t_{j}=O(\log_{b}(j))
.

Let
b
be a prime number from now on. For
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
b
-adic expansion
k=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\kappa_{i}b^{i-1}
, the
k
-th
b
-adic Walsh function is given by

{}_{b}\mathrm{wal}_{k}(u)=\exp\left(\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{b}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\kappa_{i}v_{i}\right),

(9)

where
\mathrm{i}=\sqrt{-1}
,
v_{1},\dots,v_{r}
is given by the
b
-adic expansion
u=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}v_{i}b^{-i}\in[0,1)
. For the multivariate case, the Walsh functions are defined by

{}_{b}\mathrm{wal}_{\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{u}):=\prod_{j=1}^{s}{}_{b}\mathrm{wal}_{k_{j}}(u_{j}),\quad\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}.

It is already known that
\{{}_{b}\mathrm{wal}_{\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{u})\mid\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}\}
forms an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{I}^{s})
; see
[
6
]
for example. For

, we have the Walsh series expanding

f(\boldsymbol{u})\sim\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}}\hat{f}(\boldsymbol{k}){}_{b}\mathrm{wal}_{\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{u}),

where
f\sim g
denotes the equivalence relation for the

space, and

\hat{f}(\boldsymbol{k}):=\int_{\mathbb{I}^{s}}f(\boldsymbol{u})\overline{{}_{b}\mathrm{wal}_{\boldsymbol{k}}(\boldsymbol{u})}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{u}

denotes the

-th Walsh coefficient of

. Let

\sigma^{2}_{{\boldsymbol{\ell}}}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in L_{{\boldsymbol{\ell}}}}|\hat{f}(\boldsymbol{k})|^{2},

where

L_{{\boldsymbol{\ell}}}=\{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}\mid\boldsymbol{s}(\boldsymbol{k})=\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}}),b^{\ell_{j}-1}\leq k_{j}<b^{\ell_{j}}\ \forall j\in\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})\}.

(10)

For

where

and

\Gamma_{\omega,{\boldsymbol{\ell}}}\leq\Big(\frac{b}{b-1}\Big)^{|\omega|-1}b^{t_{\omega}}\boldsymbol{1}\{\|{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\|_{1}>m-t_{\omega}-|\omega|\}.

(12)

Proof.

) is first derived by

28
]

3
Generalized Fourier coefficients and their bounds

Recall that our estimator
\hat{\mu}
is given by Equation (
1
), where the integrand is
f^{w}(\boldsymbol{u})=w(\boldsymbol{u})f\circ T(\boldsymbol{u})
.
A crucial step in analyzing the error of
\hat{\mu}
is to bound the generalized Fourier coefficients of
f^{w}
. Specifically, we consider a complete orthonormal basis
\{\phi_{k}(u)\mid k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
of
L^{2}(\mathbb{I})
with
\phi_{0}(u)=1
. We additionally assume every
\phi_{k}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{I})
. Examples are trigonometric functions used in the analysis of lattice rules
[
5
]
and Walsh functions introduced in Subsection
2.3
. Given
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{I}^{s})
, we define the generalized Fourier coefficients

\hat{f}(\boldsymbol{k})=\int_{\mathbb{I}^{s}}f(\boldsymbol{u})\prod_{j=1}^{s}\overline{\phi_{k_{j}}(u_{j})}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{u}=\int_{\mathbb{I}^{s}}f(\boldsymbol{u})\prod_{j\in\boldsymbol{s}(\boldsymbol{k})}\overline{\phi_{k_{j}}(u_{j})}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{u}

for
\boldsymbol{k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{s})\in\mathbb{N}^{s}_{0}
.
Notice that
\{\hat{f}(\boldsymbol{k})\mid\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}_{0}\}
is not square-summable if
f\notin L^{2}(\mathbb{I}^{s})
.

The next lemma shows
\hat{f}^{w}(\boldsymbol{k})
only depends on the ANOVA components
f_{v}
with
v\subseteq\boldsymbol{s}(\boldsymbol{k})
.

Lemma 8
.

For
f\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)
and
w_{j}=w_{\theta_{j}}
defined by Equation (
3
) with
\{\theta_{j}\mid j\in 1{:}s\}\subseteq(0,1/2]
,

\|f^{w}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{I}^{s})}\leq 2^{\frac{q-1}{q}s}\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}

(13)

and

\hat{f}^{w}(\boldsymbol{k})=\sum_{v\subseteq\boldsymbol{s}(\boldsymbol{k})}\hat{f}^{w}_{v}(\boldsymbol{k}_{v})\prod_{j\in\boldsymbol{s}(\boldsymbol{k})\setminus v}\hat{w}_{j}(k_{j}),

where

f^{w}_{v}(\boldsymbol{u}_{v})=f_{v}\circ T(\boldsymbol{u})\prod_{j\in v}w_{j}(u_{j})

with
f_{v}\circ T(\boldsymbol{u})
interpreted as a function of
\boldsymbol{u}_{v}
by removing redundant variables
\boldsymbol{u}_{v^{c}}
.

Proof.

Because
T^{\prime}_{j}(u)\geq 0
and
\sup_{u\in\mathbb{I}}w_{j}(u)=(1-\theta_{j})^{-1}\leq 2

\displaystyle\|f^{w}\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{I}^{s})}=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{I}^{s}}|f|^{q}\circ T(\boldsymbol{u})\prod_{j=1}^{s}w_{j}(u_{j})^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{u}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{s}\Big(\sup_{u\in\mathbb{I}}w_{j}(u)\Big)^{q-1}\int_{\mathbb{I}^{s}}|f|^{q}\circ T(\boldsymbol{u})\prod_{j=1}^{s}\varphi\circ T_{j}(u_{j})T_{j}^{\prime}(u_{j})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{u}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{(q-1)s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{s}}|f(\boldsymbol{x})|^{q}\prod_{j=1}^{s}\varphi(x_{j})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}=2^{(q-1)s}\|f\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}.

Next, Equation (
7
) implies for
j\in v
,

\int_{\mathbb{I}}f_{v}\circ T(\boldsymbol{u})w_{j}(u_{j})\,\mathrm{d}u_{j}=\int_{\mathbb{I}}f_{v}\circ T(\boldsymbol{u})\varphi\circ T_{j}(u_{j})T_{j}^{\prime}(u_{j})\,\mathrm{d}u_{j}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{v}(\boldsymbol{x})\varphi(x_{j})\,\mathrm{d}x_{j}=0.

Together with
\int_{0}^{1}w_{\theta}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u=1
, we have

\displaystyle\hat{f}^{w}(\boldsymbol{k})=

\displaystyle\sum_{v\subseteq 1{:}s}\int_{\mathbb{I}^{s}}f_{v}\circ T(\boldsymbol{u})\prod_{j=1}^{s}w_{j}(u_{j})\prod_{j\in\boldsymbol{s}(\boldsymbol{k})}\overline{\phi_{k_{j}}(u_{j})}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{v\subseteq\boldsymbol{s}(\boldsymbol{k})}\int_{\mathbb{I}^{|v|}}f_{v}\circ T(\boldsymbol{u})\prod_{j\in v}w_{j}(u_{j})\overline{\phi_{k_{j}}(u_{j})}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{u}_{v}\prod_{j\in\boldsymbol{s}(\boldsymbol{k})\setminus v}\int_{\mathbb{I}}w_{j}(u_{j})\overline{\phi_{k_{j}}(u_{j})}\,\mathrm{d}u_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{v\subseteq\boldsymbol{s}(\boldsymbol{k})}\hat{f}^{w}_{v}(\boldsymbol{k}_{v})\prod_{j\in\boldsymbol{s}(\boldsymbol{k})\setminus v}\hat{w}_{j}(k_{j}).

Lemma
8
suggests
|\hat{f}^{w}(\boldsymbol{k})|
is controlled by
|\hat{w}_{j}(k_{j})|
for
j\in\boldsymbol{s}(\boldsymbol{k})
and
|\hat{f}^{w}_{v}(\boldsymbol{k}_{v})|
for
v\subseteq\boldsymbol{s}(\boldsymbol{k})
. The next lemma bounds the generalized Fourier coefficients of
w_{j}=w_{\theta_{j}}
.

Lemma 9
.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
w_{\theta}
with
\theta\in(0,1/2]
,

|\hat{w}_{\theta}(k)|\leq 4\min\big(\theta\|\phi_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{I})},\|\mathcal{I}(\phi_{k})\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{I})}\big).

Proof.

Because
\phi_{k}(u)
is orthogonal to
\phi_{0}(u)=1
,

\mathcal{I}(\phi_{k})(1)=\int_{\mathbb{I}}\phi_{k}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u=0.

Since
0\leq w_{\theta}(u)\leq(1-\theta)^{-1}
,

\displaystyle|\hat{w}_{\theta}(k)|=

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{I}}w_{\theta}(u)\overline{\phi_{k}(u)}\,\mathrm{d}u\Big|=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{I}}w_{\theta}(u)\phi_{k}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{I}}\big(w_{\theta}(u)-(1-\theta)^{-1}\big)\phi_{k}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{0}^{\theta}\big((1-\theta)^{-1}-w_{\theta}(u)\big)\phi_{k}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u\Big|+\Big|\int_{1-\theta}^{1}\big((1-\theta)^{-1}-w_{\theta}(u)\big)\phi_{k}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\theta(1-\theta)^{-1}\|\phi_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{I})}.

Next, we use integration by parts and
\mathcal{I}(\phi_{k})(0)=\mathcal{I}(\phi_{k})(1)=0
to obtain

\displaystyle|\hat{w}_{\theta}(k)|=

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{I}}w_{\theta}(u)\phi_{k}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u\Big|=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{I}}w^{\prime}_{\theta}(u)\mathcal{I}(\phi_{k})(u)\,\mathrm{d}u\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{0}^{\theta}w^{\prime}_{\theta}(u)\mathcal{I}(\phi_{k})(u)\,\mathrm{d}u\Big|+\Big|\int_{1-\theta}^{1}w^{\prime}_{\theta}(u)\mathcal{I}(\phi_{k})(u)\,\mathrm{d}u\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|\mathcal{I}(\phi_{k})\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{I})}\int_{0}^{\theta}w^{\prime}_{\theta}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(1-\theta)^{-1}\|\mathcal{I}(\phi_{k})\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{I})}.

The conclusion follows once we bound
(1-\theta)^{-1}\leq 2
.

Bounds on
|\hat{f}^{w}_{v}(\boldsymbol{k}_{v})|^{2}
generally depend on the smoothness of
f^{w}_{v}
and properties of the orthonormal basis. In this work, we use the following bound based on
\|\partial^{v}f_{v}^{w}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{I}^{|v|})}
. Whether this norm is finite and how it depends on
\{\theta_{j}\mid j\in v\}
is the subject of the next section.

Lemma 10
.

Assume there exist
\lambda:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
,
\mathcal{N}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
and
\mathcal{M}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\mathcal{I}(\phi_{k})=\lambda(k)\phi^{*}_{\mathcal{N}(k)}\phi_{\mathcal{M}(k)},

(14)

where
\{\phi^{*}_{\ell}(u)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{I})\mid\ell\in\mathbb{N}\}
satisfies
\|\phi^{*}_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{I})}=1
for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. Then for
f\in W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{I}^{s})
and
{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
,

\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{N}_{s}^{-1}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})}|\hat{f}(\boldsymbol{k})|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{s}|\lambda(k_{j})|^{-2}\leq\|\partial^{1{:}s}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{I}^{s})},

where
\mathcal{N}_{s}^{-1}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})=\{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}\mid\mathcal{N}(k_{j})=\ell_{j},j\in 1{:}s\}.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{I}(\phi_{k_{1}})(0)=\mathcal{I}(\phi_{k_{1}})(1)=0
, we can use integration by parts for weak derivatives
[
23
, Chapter 4]
and derive

\hat{f}(\boldsymbol{k})=\int_{\mathbb{I}^{s}}f(\boldsymbol{u})\prod_{j=1}^{s}\overline{\phi_{k_{j}}(u_{j})}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{u}=\int_{\mathbb{I}^{s}}\partial^{1}f(\boldsymbol{u})\overline{\mathcal{I}(\phi_{k_{1}})(u_{1})}\prod_{j=2}^{s}\overline{\phi_{k_{j}}(u_{j})}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{u}.

After repeating the above calculation for
u_{2},\dots,u_{s}
, we get

\displaystyle\hat{f}(\boldsymbol{k})=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{I}^{s}}\partial^{1{:}s}f(\boldsymbol{u})\prod_{j=1}^{s}\overline{\mathcal{I}(\phi_{k_{j}})(u_{j})}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{I}^{s}}\partial^{1{:}s}f(\boldsymbol{u})\prod_{j=1}^{s}\overline{\lambda(k_{j})\phi^{*}_{\mathcal{N}(k_{j})}(u_{j})\phi_{\mathcal{M}(k_{j})}(u_{j})}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{u}.

Over
\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{N}_{s}^{-1}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})
,
\mathcal{N}(k_{j})=\ell_{j}
. Moreover, if
\mathcal{M}(k_{j})=\mathcal{M}(k^{\prime}_{j})
for
k_{j},k^{\prime}_{j}\in\mathcal{N}^{-1}(\ell_{j})
,

\mathcal{I}(\phi_{k_{j}})=\lambda(k_{j})\phi^{*}_{\ell_{j}}\phi_{\mathcal{M}(k_{j})}=\frac{\lambda(k_{j})}{\lambda(k^{\prime}_{j})}\lambda(k^{\prime}_{j})\phi^{*}_{\ell_{j}}\phi_{\mathcal{M}(k^{\prime}_{j})}=\frac{\lambda(k_{j})}{\lambda(k^{\prime}_{j})}\mathcal{I}(\phi_{k^{\prime}_{j}}),

which is true only if
k_{j}=k^{\prime}_{j}
. Therefore, the mapping
\boldsymbol{k}\to(\mathcal{M}(k_{1}),\dots,\mathcal{M}(k_{s}))
is injective over
\mathcal{N}_{s}^{-1}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})
and

\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{N}_{s}^{-1}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})}|\hat{f}(\boldsymbol{k})|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{s}|\lambda(k_{j})|^{-2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{N}_{s}^{-1}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{I}^{s}}\Big(\partial^{1{:}s}f(\boldsymbol{u})\prod_{j=1}^{s}\overline{\phi^{*}_{\ell_{j}}(u_{j})}\Big)\prod_{j=1}^{s}\overline{\phi_{\mathcal{M}(k_{j})}(u_{j})}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{u}\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{I}^{s}}\Big|\partial^{1{:}s}f(\boldsymbol{u})\prod_{j=1}^{s}\overline{\phi^{*}_{\ell_{j}}(u_{j})}\Big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{u}\leq\|\partial^{1{:}s}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{I}^{s})},

where in the second inequality we have applied Bessel’s inequality for the orthonormal basis
\{\prod_{j=1}^{s}\phi_{k_{j}}(u_{j})\mid\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}_{0}\}
.

Example 11
.

To illustrate, we prove Equation (

b

\phi_{k}(u)={}_{b}\mathrm{wal}_{k}(u)
defined by Equation (
9
). For

satisfying

, because

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{I}_{a}}\phi_{k}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u=\exp\Big(\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{b}\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\kappa_{i}u_{i}\Big)\int_{\mathbb{I}_{a}}\exp\Big(\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{b}\kappa_{r}u_{r}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}u=0,

where the sum from

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(\phi_{k})(u)=

\displaystyle\exp\Big(\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{b}\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\kappa_{i}u_{i}\Big)\int_{ab^{-r+1}}^{u}\exp\Big(\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{b}\kappa_{r}u^{\prime}_{r}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}u^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\Big(\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{b}\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\kappa_{i}u_{i}\Big)\int_{0}^{u}\exp\Big(\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}}{b}\kappa_{r}u^{\prime}_{r}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}u^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathcal{M}(k)}(u)\mathcal{I}(\phi_{\mathcal{N}(k)})(u),

where
\mathcal{M}(k)=k-\kappa_{r}b^{r-1}
and
\mathcal{N}(k)=\kappa_{r}b^{r-1}
. Equation (

) follows after we write

Theorem 12
.

Suppose
\{\phi_{k}(u)\mid k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
satisfies Equation (
14
) and

\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\|\phi_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{I})}=C_{\phi}<\infty.

Then for

,
{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}
and

with

for

,

\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{E}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})}|\hat{f}^{w}(\boldsymbol{k})|^{2}\prod_{j\in\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})}|\lambda(k_{j})|^{-2\alpha}\\
\leq 2^{|\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})|}\sum_{v\subseteq\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})}\|\partial^{v}f^{w}_{v}\|^{2\alpha}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{I}^{|v|})}\|f^{w}_{v}\|^{2-2\alpha}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{I}^{|v|})}\prod_{j\in\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})\setminus v}\mathcal{S}(\theta_{j},\ell_{j},\alpha),

where

\mathcal{S}(\theta,\ell,\alpha)=16C^{2}_{\phi}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{N}^{-1}(\ell)}\min\big(\theta^{2},|\lambda(k)|^{2}\big)|\lambda(k)|^{-2\alpha}.

Proof.

By Lemma
8
, for every
\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{E}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})

\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{E}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})}|\hat{f}^{w}(\boldsymbol{k})|^{2}\prod_{j\in\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})}|\lambda(k_{j})|^{-2\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{E}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})}2^{|\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})|}\sum_{v\subseteq\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})}|\hat{f}^{w}_{v}(\boldsymbol{k}_{v})|^{2}\prod_{j\in v}|\lambda(k_{j})|^{-2\alpha}\prod_{j\in\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})\setminus v}|\hat{w}_{j}(k_{j})|^{2}|\lambda(k_{j})|^{-2\alpha}

\displaystyle 2^{|\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})|}\sum_{v\subseteq\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})}\Big(\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}_{v}\in\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{|v|}({\boldsymbol{\ell}}_{v})}|\hat{f}^{w}_{v}(\boldsymbol{k}_{v})|^{2}\prod_{j\in v}|\lambda(k_{j})|^{-2\alpha}\Big)\prod_{j\in\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})\setminus v}\tilde{\mathcal{S}}(w_{j},\ell_{j},\alpha),

By Lemma
10

\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}_{v}\in\mathcal{N}^{-1}_{|v|}({\boldsymbol{\ell}}_{v})}|\hat{f}^{w}_{v}(\boldsymbol{k}_{v})|^{2}\prod_{j\in v}|\lambda(k_{j})|^{-2\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq

Meanwhile, by Lemma

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{S}}(w_{\theta},\ell,\alpha)\leq

\displaystyle 16C^{2}_{\phi}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{N}^{-1}(\ell)}\min\big(\theta^{2},|\lambda(k)|^{2}\big)|\lambda(k)|^{-2\alpha}.

The conclusion follows after we plug in the above bounds.

In this section, we aim to characterize the norms of

Lemma 13
.

For
f\in W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)
and
f^{w}(\boldsymbol{u})=w(\boldsymbol{u})f\circ T(\boldsymbol{u})
,

\partial^{1{:}s}f^{w}(\boldsymbol{u})=\sum_{v\subseteq 1{:}s}(\partial^{v}f)\circ T(\boldsymbol{u})\prod_{j\in v}\frac{w^{2}_{j}(u_{j})}{\varphi\circ T_{j}(u_{j})}\prod_{j\in v^{c}}w^{\prime}_{j}(u_{j}).

Proof.

This is a direct application of chain rule for weak derivatives.

Recall that
w_{j}=w_{\theta_{j}}
is given by Equation (
3
) with
\theta_{j}\in(0,1/2]
and
\eta=\eta_{p}
given by (
4
), and
T_{j}
is given by Equation (
2
). We summarize useful facts about
\eta_{p}
in the following lemma.

Lemma 14
.

For
p\geq 1
,
\eta_{p}(u)
given by (
4
) is monotonic, differentiable over
u\in[0,1/2]
and satisfies

\eta_{p}(u)\geq\frac{u^{p+1}}{p}\tilde{\eta}^{\prime}_{p}(u)\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{0}^{u}\eta_{p}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\geq\frac{u^{p+1}}{p}\eta_{p}(u).

Proof.

See the appendix.

Lemma 15
.

Suppose
\varphi
satisfies Assumption
1
. For
\varepsilon>0
and
u\in(0,1/2]
,

\varphi\circ T_{j}(u)\geq c_{\varepsilon,p}\Big(\theta_{j}w_{j}(u)\min\big((2u/\theta_{j})^{p+1},1\big)\Big)^{1+\varepsilon},

where
c_{\varepsilon,p}
are constants depending on
\varepsilon
and
p
.

Proof.

By Assumption
1
and Equation (
2
), for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\varphi\circ T_{j}(u)\geq c_{\varepsilon}\Big(\Phi\circ\Phi^{-1}\big(\mathcal{I}(w_{j})(u)\big)\Big)^{1+\epsilon}=c_{\varepsilon}\Big(\mathcal{I}(w_{j})(u)\Big)^{1+\epsilon}.

(16)

When
u\in(0,\theta_{j}/2]
,

\mathcal{I}(w_{j})(u)=(1-\theta_{j})^{-1}\int_{0}^{u}\eta_{p}(t/\theta_{j})\,\mathrm{d}t=(1-\theta_{j})^{-1}\theta_{j}\int_{0}^{u/\theta_{j}}\eta_{p}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.

Lemma
14
implies that if
u\in(0,\theta_{j}/2]
,

\mathcal{I}(w_{j})(u)\geq\theta^{-p}_{j}u^{p+1}w_{j}(u)/p.

When

, since
w_{j}(\theta_{j}/2)=w_{j}(\theta_{j})/2\geq w_{j}(u)/2
,

\mathcal{I}(w_{j})(u)\geq\mathcal{I}(w_{j})(\theta_{j}/2)\geq(\theta_{j}/2^{p+2})w_{j}(u)/p.

Finally when

\mathcal{I}(w_{j})(u)=(1-\theta_{j})^{-1}(u-\theta_{j}/2)
and

, so
\mathcal{I}(w_{j})(u)\geq(\theta_{j}/2)w_{j}(u)
. Our conclusion follows after putting the above bounds into (
16
).

Lemma 16
.

For
u\in(0,1/2]
,

|w^{\prime}_{j}(u)|\leq c_{p}\theta^{-1}_{j}w_{j}(u)\max\big((2u/\theta_{j})^{-p-1},1\big),

where
c_{p}
are constants depending on
p
.

Proof.

Differentiating Equation (
3
) gives

w^{\prime}_{j}(u)=\begin{cases}\theta^{-1}_{j}(1-\theta_{j})^{-1}\eta^{\prime}_{p}(u/\theta_{j}),&\text{ if }u\in(0,\theta_{j}/2]\\
\theta^{-1}_{j}(1-\theta_{j})^{-1}\eta^{\prime}_{p}(1-u/\theta_{j}),&\text{ if }u\in(\theta_{j}/2,\theta_{j})\\
0,&\text{ if }u\in[\theta_{j},1/2]\end{cases}.

When
u\in(0,\theta_{j}/2]
, Lemma
14
implies

|w^{\prime}_{j}(u)|\leq p\theta^{p}_{j}u^{-p-1}w_{j}(u).

When
u\in(\theta_{j}/2,\theta_{j})

|w^{\prime}_{j}(u)|\leq\theta^{-1}_{j}(1-\theta_{j})^{-1}\sup_{t\in[0,1/2]}\eta_{p}^{\prime}(t)\leq 2\theta^{-1}_{j}w_{j}(u)\sup_{t\in[0,1/2]}\eta_{p}^{\prime}(t).

The conclusion then follows since

is finite and only depends on

.

Theorem 17
.

For
f\in W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)
with

,
\varphi
satisfying Assumption

\varepsilon\in(0,1-1/q)

\|\partial^{1{:}s}f^{w}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{I}^{s})}\leq\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{s}C_{\varepsilon,p,q}\theta^{-1-\varepsilon}_{j}\Big)\|f\|_{W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)},

where
C_{\varepsilon,p,q}
is a constant depending on
\varepsilon,p
and
q
.

Proof.

First we use Lemma
13
to bound

\displaystyle|\partial^{1{:}s}f^{w}(\boldsymbol{u})|\leq\sum_{v\subseteq 1{:}s}|(\partial^{v}f)\circ T(\boldsymbol{u})|\prod_{j=1}^{s}w_{j}(u_{j})^{1/q}\prod_{j\in v}\frac{w_{j}(u_{j})^{2-1/q}}{\varphi\circ T_{j}(u_{j})}\prod_{j\in v^{c}}\frac{|w^{\prime}_{j}(u_{j})|}{w_{j}(u_{j})^{1/q}}.

(17)

By Lemma
15

\displaystyle\sup_{u\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{w_{j}(u)^{2-1/q}}{\varphi\circ T_{j}(u)}=

\displaystyle\sup_{u\in(0,1/2]}\frac{w_{j}(u)^{2-1/q}}{\varphi\circ T_{j}(u)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tilde{c}^{-1}_{\varepsilon,p}\theta^{-1-\varepsilon}_{j}\sup_{u\in(0,1/2]}w_{j}(u)^{1-1/q-\varepsilon}\max\big((2u/\theta_{j})^{-(1+\varepsilon)(1+p)},1\big).

Note that

\displaystyle\sup_{u\in(0,1/2]}w_{j}(u)^{1-1/q-\varepsilon}\max\big((2u/\theta_{j})^{-(1+\varepsilon)(1+p)},1\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\Big(\sup_{u\in(0,\theta_{j}/2)}(2u/\theta_{j})^{-(2-1/q)(1+p)}\exp\Big((1-1/q-\varepsilon)(2^{p}-(u/\theta_{j})^{-p})\Big),2\Big),

which can be further bounded in terms of

and

\sup_{u\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{w_{j}(u)^{2-1/q}}{\varphi\circ T_{j}(u)}\leq C_{1}\theta^{-1-\varepsilon}_{j}

(18)

for

depending on
\varepsilon,p
and

. A similar calculation using Lemma

\sup_{u\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{|w^{\prime}_{j}(u_{j})|}{w_{j}(u_{j})^{1/q}}\leq C_{2}\theta^{-1}_{j}

(19)

for

depending on
p
and

) becomes

\displaystyle|\partial^{1{:}s}f^{w}(\boldsymbol{u})|\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{v\subseteq 1{:}s}|(\partial^{v}f)\circ T(\boldsymbol{u})|\prod_{j=1}^{s}w_{j}(u_{j})^{1/q}\prod_{j\in v}C_{1}\theta^{-1-\varepsilon}_{j}\prod_{j\in v^{c}}C_{2}\theta^{-1}_{j}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{s}C_{\varepsilon,p,q}\theta^{-1-\varepsilon}_{j}\Big)\Big(\sum_{v\subseteq 1{:}s}|(\partial^{v}f)\circ T(\boldsymbol{u})|^{q}\prod_{j=1}^{s}w_{j}(u_{j})\Big)^{1/q},

where

C_{\varepsilon,p,q}=\Big(\sum_{v\subseteq 1{:}s}C^{\frac{q}{q-1}|v|}_{1}C^{\frac{q}{q-1}(s-|v|)}_{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{s}(1-\frac{1}{q})}=\Big(C^{\frac{q}{q-1}}_{1}+C^{\frac{q}{q-1}}_{2}\Big)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}.

The conclusion then follows because

\displaystyle\sum_{v\subseteq 1{:}s}\int_{\mathbb{I}^{s}}|(\partial^{v}f)\circ T(\boldsymbol{u})|^{q}\prod_{j=1}^{s}w_{j}(u_{j})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{v\subseteq 1{:}s}\int_{\mathbb{I}^{s}}|(\partial^{v}f)\circ T(\boldsymbol{u})|^{q}\prod_{j=1}^{s}\varphi\circ T_{j}(u_{j})T^{\prime}_{j}(u_{j})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{v\subseteq 1{:}s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{s}}|\partial^{v}f(\boldsymbol{x})|^{q}\prod_{j=1}^{s}\varphi(x_{j})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}=\|f\|^{q}_{W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}.

Theorem
17
shows
\partial^{1{:}s}f^{w}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{I}^{s})
when
f\in W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)
. However, in some applications the boundary growth of
f
is too rapid for even
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)
to hold. Characterizing
f
by its
W^{1,q,\infty}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)
-norm is a better choice for such cases. The next theorem shows
f^{w}\in W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{I}^{s})
even for
f\in W^{1,q,\infty}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)
with
1<q\leq 2
.

Theorem 18
.

For
f\in W^{1,q,\infty}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)
with
1<q\leq 2
,
\varphi
satisfying Assumption
1
,
\varepsilon\in(0,(q-1)/(q+1))
and
q^{\prime}\geq q
, then

\|f^{w}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{I}^{s})}\leq\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{s}C_{\varepsilon,p,q,q^{\prime}}\theta^{-(1+\varepsilon)(1/q-1/q^{\prime})}_{j}\Big)\|f\|_{L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}

(20)

and

\|\partial^{1{:}s}f^{w}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{I}^{s})}\leq\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{s}C^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,p,q,q^{\prime}}\theta^{-(1+\varepsilon)(1+1/q-1/q^{\prime})}_{j}\Big)\|f\|_{W^{1,q,\infty}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)},

(21)

where
C_{\varepsilon,p,q,q^{\prime}},C^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,p,q,q^{\prime}}
are constants depending on
\varepsilon,p,q
and
q^{\prime}
.

Proof.

We first prove the bound on
\|f^{w}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{I}^{s})}
. Let
\varepsilon^{\prime}\in(0,q-1)
. By Lemma
2
,
\|f\|_{L^{q-\varepsilon^{\prime}}(\mathbb{I}^{s})}\leq C^{s}_{3}\|f\|_{L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{I}^{s})}
for
C_{3}
depending on
q
and
\varepsilon^{\prime}
. By Equation (
13
),

\|f^{w}\|_{L^{q-\varepsilon^{\prime}}(\mathbb{I}^{s})}\leq 2^{\frac{q-\varepsilon^{\prime}-1}{q-\varepsilon^{\prime}}s}\|f\|_{L^{q-\varepsilon^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}\leq\Big(2^{\frac{q-\varepsilon^{\prime}-1}{q-\varepsilon^{\prime}}}C_{3}\Big)^{s}\|f\|_{L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}.

Next, we use the definition of
L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)
-norm to bound

|f^{w}(\boldsymbol{u})|=|f\circ T(\boldsymbol{u})|\prod_{j=1}^{s}w_{j}(u_{j})\leq\varphi^{s/q}_{\infty}\|f\|_{L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}\prod_{j=1}^{s}\frac{w_{j}(u_{j})}{\varphi\circ T_{j}(u_{j})^{1/q}}.

By Equation (
18
) with
q^{*}=1/(2-q)
,

\sup_{u\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{w_{j}(u)}{\varphi\circ T_{j}(u)^{1/q}}=\Big(\sup_{u\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{w_{j}(u)^{2-1/q^{*}}}{\varphi\circ T_{j}(u)}\Big)^{1/q}\leq C_{4}\theta_{j}^{-(1+\varepsilon/2)/q}

for
C_{4}
depending on
\varepsilon,p
and
q
. Hence,

\|f^{w}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{I}^{s})}\leq\varphi^{s/q}_{\infty}\|f\|_{L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}\prod_{j=1}^{s}C_{4}\theta_{j}^{-(1+\varepsilon/2)/q}.

Then by the interpolation inequality
[
1
, Theorem 2.11]
,

\|f^{w}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{I}^{s})}\leq\|f^{w}\|_{L^{q-\varepsilon^{\prime}}(\mathbb{I}^{s})}^{\frac{q-\varepsilon^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}\|f^{w}\|^{1-\frac{q-\varepsilon^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{I}^{s})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}\prod_{j=1}^{s}C_{5}\theta_{j}^{-A}

for
C_{5}
depending on
\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime},p,q
and
q^{\prime}
(note that
\varphi_{\infty}
is treated as a constant), and

A=\Big(1-\frac{q-\varepsilon^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}\Big)\frac{1+\varepsilon/2}{q}=(1+\varepsilon)\Big(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}\Big)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{2\varepsilon^{\prime}}{\varepsilon qq^{\prime}}-\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime}}{qq^{\prime}}\Big).

Equation (
20
) follows after we choose a sufficiently small
\varepsilon^{\prime}
.

The bound on
\|\partial^{1{:}s}f^{w}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{I}^{s})}
can be proven similarly. By Lemma
2
and Theorem
17
,

\|\partial^{1{:}s}f^{w}\|_{L^{q-\varepsilon^{\prime}}(\mathbb{I}^{s})}\leq\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{s}C_{6}\theta^{-1-\varepsilon}_{j}\Big)\|f\|_{W^{1,q,\infty}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}

for
C_{6}
depending on
\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime},p
and
q
.
Next, we use Lemma
13
to bound

\displaystyle|\partial^{1{:}s}f^{w}(\boldsymbol{u})|\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{v\subseteq 1{:}s}\|\partial^{v}f\|_{L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{s}\frac{\varphi\circ T_{j}(\boldsymbol{u})}{\varphi_{\infty}}\Big)^{-1/q}\prod_{j\in v}\frac{w_{j}(u_{j})^{2}}{\varphi\circ T_{j}(u_{j})}\prod_{j\in v^{c}}|w^{\prime}_{j}(u_{j})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi^{s/q}_{\infty}\sum_{v\subseteq 1{:}s}\|\partial^{v}f\|_{L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}\prod_{j\in v}\frac{w_{j}(u_{j})^{2}}{(\varphi\circ T_{j}(u_{j}))^{1+1/q}}\prod_{j\in v^{c}}\frac{|w^{\prime}_{j}(u_{j})|}{(\varphi\circ T_{j}(u_{j}))^{1/q}}.

By Equation (
18
) with
q^{*}
satisfying
(2-1/q^{*})(1+1/q)=2
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1-1/q^{*})
,

\sup_{u\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{w_{j}(u)^{2}}{(\varphi\circ T_{j}(u))^{1+1/q}}=\Big(\sup_{u\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{w_{j}(u)^{2-1/q^{*}}}{\varphi\circ T_{j}(u)}\Big)^{1+1/q}\leq C_{7}\theta_{j}^{-(1+\varepsilon/2)(1+1/q)}

for
C_{7}
depending on
\varepsilon,p
and
q
, where
p
is required to satisfy
p\geq(1-1/q^{*}-\varepsilon)^{-1}(1+\varepsilon)
if
\eta=\eta_{p}
.
Similarly by Equation (
19
),

\sup_{u\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{|w^{\prime}_{j}(u_{j})|}{(\varphi\circ T_{j}(u_{j}))^{1/q}}\leq\sup_{u\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{|w^{\prime}_{j}(u_{j})|}{w_{j}(u_{j})^{2/(q+1)}}\Big(\sup_{u\in\mathbb{I}}\frac{w_{j}(u)^{2-1/q^{*}}}{\varphi\circ T_{j}(u)}\Big)^{1/q}\leq C_{8}\theta_{j}^{-1-(1+\varepsilon/2)/q}

for
C_{8}
depending on
\varepsilon,p
and
q
. Using the above bounds,

\displaystyle\|\partial^{1{:}s}f^{w}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{I})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varphi^{s/q}_{\infty}\sum_{v\subseteq 1{:}s}\|\partial^{v}f\|_{L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}\prod_{j\in v}C_{7}\theta_{j}^{-(1+\varepsilon/2)(1+1/q)}\prod_{j\in v^{c}}C_{8}\theta_{j}^{-1-(1+\varepsilon/2)/q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varphi^{s/q}_{\infty}\|f\|_{W^{1,q,\infty}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}\Big(\sum_{v\subseteq 1{:}s}C^{\frac{q}{q-1}|v|}_{7}C^{\frac{q}{q-1}(s-|v|)}_{8}\Big)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\prod_{j=1}^{s}\theta_{j}^{-(1+\varepsilon/2)(1+1/q)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi^{s/q}_{\infty}\|f\|_{W^{1,q,\infty}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}\prod_{j=1}^{s}\Big(C^{\frac{q}{q-1}}_{7}+C^{\frac{q}{q-1}}_{8}\Big)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\theta_{j}^{-(1+\varepsilon/2)(1+1/q)}.

Finally, we use the interpolation inequality to get

\displaystyle\|\partial^{1{:}s}f^{w}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{I}^{s})}\leq

\displaystyle\|\partial^{1{:}s}f^{w}\|_{L^{q-\varepsilon^{\prime}}(\mathbb{I}^{s})}^{\frac{q-\varepsilon^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}\|\partial^{1{:}s}f^{w}\|^{1-\frac{q-\varepsilon^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{I}^{s})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{W^{1,q,\infty}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}\prod_{j=1}^{s}C_{9}\theta_{j}^{-B}

for
C_{9}
depending on
\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime},p,q
and
q^{\prime}
, and

\displaystyle B=

\displaystyle\frac{q-\varepsilon^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}\Big(1+\varepsilon\Big)+\Big(1-\frac{q-\varepsilon^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{1}{q}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(1+\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}\Big)\Big(1+\varepsilon\Big)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2qq^{\prime}}\Big((q^{\prime}-q)(1+q)-\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime}}{\varepsilon}(2+\varepsilon-\varepsilon q)\Big).

Equation (
21
) follows after we choose a sufficiently small
\varepsilon^{\prime}
.

5
Application to digital nets

In this section, we apply our theory and derive conditions under which
f^{w}
can be efficiently integrated by digital nets. In Example
11
, we have shown that
\phi_{k}(u)={}_{b}\mathrm{wal}_{k}(u)
satisfies Equation (
14
) for
\mathcal{N}(k)=\kappa_{r}b^{r-1}
and
\lambda(k)=\|\mathcal{I}(\phi_{\mathcal{N}(k)})\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{I})}\leq b^{-r+1}
, where
r
and
\kappa_{r}
are determined by
\kappa_{r}b^{r-1}\leq k<(\kappa_{r}+1)b^{r}
. Combining Theorem
12
and Theorem
17
, we arrive at the following theorem.

Theorem 19
.

For
\phi_{k}(u)={}_{b}\mathrm{wal}_{k}(u)
,
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
,
{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}\setminus\{\boldsymbol{0}\}
,
\boldsymbol{\kappa}\in\{1,\dots,b-1\}^{s}
,
f\in W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)
,
w_{j}=w_{\theta_{j}}
for
\{\theta_{j}\mid j\in 1{:}s\}\subseteq(0,1/2]
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
, we have

\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{E}({\boldsymbol{\ell}},\boldsymbol{\kappa})}|\hat{f}^{w}(\boldsymbol{k})|^{2}\leq\\
C_{\varepsilon,p,b,\alpha}^{|\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})|}b^{-2\alpha\|{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\|_{1}}\sum_{v\subseteq\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})}\|f_{v}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}\prod_{j\in v}\theta^{-2\alpha(1+\varepsilon)}_{j}\prod_{j\in\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})\setminus v}\theta^{1-2\alpha}_{j},

where
C_{\varepsilon,p,b,\alpha}
is a constant depending on
\varepsilon,p,b
and
\alpha
, and

\mathcal{E}({\boldsymbol{\ell}},\boldsymbol{\kappa})=\Big\{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}\mid\boldsymbol{s}(\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime})=\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}}),\kappa_{j}b^{\ell_{j}-1}\leq k^{\prime}_{j}<(\kappa_{j}+1)b^{\ell_{j}-1}\ \forall j\in\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})\Big\}.

Proof.

For
\phi_{k}(u)={}_{b}\mathrm{wal}_{k}(u)
,
C_{\phi}=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\|\phi_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{I})}=1
and
\mathcal{N}^{-1}(\kappa_{j}b^{\ell_{j}-1})=\{k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}\mid\kappa_{j}b^{\ell_{j}-1}\leq k^{\prime}<(\kappa_{j}+1)b^{\ell_{j}-1}\}
. It follows that
\lambda(k^{\prime})=\lambda(\kappa_{j}b^{\ell_{j}-1})=\|\mathcal{I}(\phi_{\kappa_{j}b^{\ell_{j}-1}})\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{I})}\leq b^{-\ell_{j}+1}
for
k^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}^{-1}(\kappa_{j}b^{\ell_{j}-1})
. Hence

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(\theta_{j},\kappa_{j}b^{\ell_{j}-1},\alpha)=

\displaystyle 16\sum_{k^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}^{-1}(\kappa_{j}b^{\ell_{j}-1})}\min\big(\theta_{j}^{2},|\lambda(k^{\prime})|^{2}\big)|\lambda(k^{\prime})|^{-2\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 16b^{\ell_{j}-1}\lambda(\kappa_{j}b^{\ell_{j}-1})^{-2\alpha}\min\big(\theta_{j}^{2},\lambda(\kappa_{j}b^{\ell_{j}-1})^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 16\min\big(\theta_{j}^{2}\lambda(\kappa_{j}b^{\ell_{j}-1})^{-1-2\alpha},\lambda(\kappa_{j}b^{\ell_{j}-1})^{1-2\alpha}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 16\theta^{1-2\alpha}_{j},

where we have used
1-2\alpha>0
. Next, by Equation (
13
) with
q=2
,

\displaystyle\|f^{w}_{v}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{I}^{|v|})}\leq 2^{|v|/2}\|f_{v}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}\leq 2^{|v|/2}\|f_{v}\|_{W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}.

(22)

Meanwhile, by Theorem
17
with
f=f_{v}
,
q=2
and identifying
v
with
1{:}|v|
,

\|\partial^{v}f_{v}^{w}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{I}^{|v|})}\leq\Big(\prod_{j\in v}C_{\varepsilon,p,2}\theta^{-1-\varepsilon}_{j}\Big)\|f_{v}\|_{W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}.

(23)

Using the above bounds, Equation (
15
) becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{E}({\boldsymbol{\ell}},\boldsymbol{\kappa})}|\hat{f}^{w}(\boldsymbol{k})|^{2}\prod_{j\in\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})}|\lambda(k_{j})|^{-2\alpha}=\Big(\prod_{j\in\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})}\lambda(\kappa_{j}b^{\ell_{j}-1})\Big)^{-2\alpha}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{E}({\boldsymbol{\ell}},\boldsymbol{\kappa})}|\hat{f}^{w}(\boldsymbol{k})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{|\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})|}\sum_{v\subseteq\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})}2^{(1-\alpha)|v|}\Big(\prod_{j\in v}C_{\varepsilon,p,2}\theta^{-1-\varepsilon}_{j}\Big)^{2\alpha}\|f_{v}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}\prod_{j\in\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})\setminus v}16\theta^{1-2\alpha}_{j}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\big(2^{2-\alpha}C^{2\alpha}_{\varepsilon,p,2},32\big)^{|\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})|}\sum_{v\subseteq\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})}\|f_{v}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}\prod_{j\in v}\theta^{-2\alpha(1+\varepsilon)}_{j}\prod_{j\in\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})\setminus v}\theta^{1-2\alpha}_{j}.

After bounding
\lambda(\kappa_{j}b^{\ell_{j}-1})\leq b^{-\ell_{j}+1}
, we conclude

\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{E}({\boldsymbol{\ell}},\boldsymbol{\kappa})}|\hat{f}^{w}(\boldsymbol{k})|^{2}\leq\\
C_{\varepsilon,p,b,\alpha}^{|\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})|}b^{-2\alpha\|{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\|_{1}}\sum_{v\subseteq\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})}\|f_{v}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}\prod_{j\in v}\theta^{-2\alpha(1+\varepsilon)}_{j}\prod_{j\in\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})\setminus v}\theta^{1-2\alpha}_{j}

with

C_{\varepsilon,p,b,\alpha}=\max\big(2^{2-\alpha}C^{2\alpha}_{\varepsilon,p,2},32\big)b^{2\alpha}
.

The next theorem is the counterpart of Theorem
19
for
f\in W^{1,q,\infty}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)
.

Theorem 20
.

For
\phi_{k}(u)={}_{b}\mathrm{wal}_{k}(u)
,
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
,
{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}\setminus\{\boldsymbol{0}\}
,
\boldsymbol{\kappa}\in\{1,\dots,b-1\}^{s}
,
f\in W^{1,q,\infty}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)
with
q\in(1,2]
,
w_{j}=w_{\theta_{j}}
for
\{\theta_{j}\mid j\in 1{:}s\}\subseteq(0,1/2]
and
\varepsilon\in(0,(q-1)/(q+1))
, we have

\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{E}({\boldsymbol{\ell}},\boldsymbol{\kappa})}|\hat{f}^{w}(\boldsymbol{k})|^{2}\leq\\
C_{\varepsilon,p,q,b,\alpha}^{|\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})|}b^{-2\alpha\|{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\|_{1}}\sum_{v\subseteq\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})}\|f_{v}\|^{2}_{W^{1,q,\infty}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}\prod_{j\in v}\theta^{-(1+\varepsilon)(2\alpha-1+2/q)}_{j}\prod_{j\in\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})\setminus v}\theta^{1-2\alpha}_{j},

where
C_{\varepsilon,p,q,b,\alpha}
is a constant depending on
\varepsilon,p,q,b
and
\alpha
.

Proof.

The proof is essentially the same as that of Theorem
19
, except we replace Equation (
22
) with

\|f_{v}^{w}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{I}^{|v|})}\leq\Big(\prod_{j\in v}C_{\varepsilon,p,q,2}\theta^{-(1+\varepsilon)(1/q-1/2)}_{j}\Big)\|f_{v}\|_{W^{1,q,\infty}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}

and Equation (
23
) with

\|\partial^{v}f_{v}^{w}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{I}^{|v|})}\leq\Big(\prod_{j\in v}C^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,p,q,2}\theta^{-(1+\varepsilon)(1/q+1/2)}_{j}\Big)\|f_{v}\|_{W^{1,q,\infty}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)},

where
C_{\varepsilon,p,q,2}
and
C^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,p,q,2}
come from Theorem
18
.

We are ready to bound the variance of
\hat{\mu}
for digital nets. To simplify the notation, we let

\|f\|_{q}=\begin{cases}\sup_{v\subseteq 1{:}s}\|f_{v}\|_{W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)},&\text{ if }f\in W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)\\
\sup_{v\subseteq 1{:}s}\|f_{v}\|_{W^{1,q,\infty}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)},&\text{ if }f\in W^{1,q,\infty}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)\end{cases},

and for
v\subseteq 1{:}s

\gamma_{v}=\begin{cases}\|f\|^{-1}_{q}\|f_{v}\|_{W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)},&\text{ if }f\in W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)\\
\|f\|^{-1}_{q}\|f_{v}\|_{W^{1,q,\infty}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)},&\text{ if }f\in W^{1,q,\infty}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)\end{cases}.

Theorem 21
.

Let
\{\boldsymbol{u}_{0},\dots,\boldsymbol{u}_{n-1}\}
be a scrambled digital net in base
b\geq 2
with
t
-quality parameters
\{t_{\omega}\mid\emptyset\neq\omega\subseteq 1{:}s\}
.
If
f\in W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)
with
q=2
and
w_{j},\varepsilon
satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
19
, or if
f\in W^{1,q,\infty}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)
with
q\in(1,2]
and
w_{j},\varepsilon
satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
20
, then for any
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
,

\mathbb{E}\Big|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f^{w}(\boldsymbol{u}_{i})-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{s}}f(\boldsymbol{x})\prod_{j=1}^{s}\varphi(x_{j})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\Big|^{2}\leq\frac{\|f\|^{2}_{q}}{b^{(1+2\alpha)m}}\sum_{\emptyset\neq\omega\subseteq 1{:}s}C^{|\omega|}_{*}m^{|\omega|-1}\tilde{\gamma}_{\omega},

where
C_{*}
is a constant depending on
\varepsilon,p,q,b
and
\alpha
, and

\tilde{\gamma}_{\omega}=b^{(1+2\alpha)t_{w}}\sum_{v\subseteq\omega}\gamma_{v}^{2}\prod_{j\in v}\theta^{-(1+\varepsilon)(2\alpha-1+2/q)}_{j}\prod_{j\in\omega\setminus v}\theta^{1-2\alpha}_{j}.

Proof.

First we notice that
L_{{\boldsymbol{\ell}}}
defined by Equation (
10
) is the union of
\mathcal{E}({\boldsymbol{\ell}},\boldsymbol{\kappa})
for
\boldsymbol{\kappa}
ranging over
\{1,\dots,b-1\}^{s}
. Since
\mathcal{E}({\boldsymbol{\ell}},\boldsymbol{\kappa})
does not depend on
\kappa_{j}
for
j\notin\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})
, there are
(b-1)^{|\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})|}
number of disjoint
\mathcal{E}({\boldsymbol{\ell}},\boldsymbol{\kappa})
and

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}_{{\boldsymbol{\ell}}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in L_{{\boldsymbol{\ell}}}}|\hat{f}(\boldsymbol{k})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{10}^{|\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})|}b^{-2\alpha\|{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\|_{1}}\|f\|^{2}_{q}\sum_{v\subseteq\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})}\gamma_{v}^{2}\prod_{j\in v}\theta^{-(1+\varepsilon)(2\alpha-1+2/q)}_{j}\prod_{j\in\boldsymbol{s}({\boldsymbol{\ell}})\setminus v}\theta^{1-2\alpha}_{j},

where
C_{10}=(b-1)C_{\varepsilon,p,b,\alpha}
if
f\in W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)
and
C_{10}=(b-1)C_{\varepsilon,p,q,b,\alpha}
if
f\in W^{1,q,\infty}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)
.
Then by Lemma
7
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f^{w}(\boldsymbol{u}_{i})-\int_{\mathbb{I}^{s}}f^{w}(\boldsymbol{u})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{u}\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{b^{m}}\sum_{\emptyset\neq\omega\subseteq 1{:}s}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\in\mathbb{N}^{\omega}}\Gamma_{\omega,{\boldsymbol{\ell}}}C_{10}^{|\omega|}b^{-2\alpha\|{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\|_{1}}\|f\|^{2}_{q}\sum_{v\subseteq\omega}\gamma_{v}^{2}\prod_{j\in v}\theta^{-(1+\varepsilon)(2\alpha-1+2/q)}_{j}\prod_{j\in\omega\setminus v}\theta^{1-2\alpha}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\|f\|^{2}_{q}}{b^{m}}\sum_{\emptyset\neq\omega\subseteq 1{:}s}C_{10}^{|\omega|}\Big(\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\in\mathbb{N}^{\omega}}\Gamma_{\omega,{\boldsymbol{\ell}}}b^{-2\alpha\|{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\|_{1}}\Big)\sum_{v\subseteq\omega}\gamma_{v}^{2}\prod_{j\in v}\theta^{-(1+\varepsilon)(2\alpha-1+2/q)}_{j}\prod_{j\in\omega\setminus v}\theta^{1-2\alpha}_{j}.

By Equation (
12
),

7
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we have proposed a new class of importance sampling methods suitable for RQMC integration of functions with severe boundary growth. Both our theoretical bounds and simulation results demonstrate a significant improvement in the convergence rates compared to previous methods.

As a limitation, our analysis does not extend to the usual inversion method by taking the limit
\theta_{j}\to 0
for
j\in 1{:}s
. One reason is that as
\theta\to 0
,
w_{\theta}
converges to
1

pointwise over
\mathbb{I}
but not in the Sobolev norm
W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{I})
. It is interesting to ask whether we can establish the convergence rates without bounding the Sobolev norm and hence bridge our method with the inversion method. We leave this question for future research.

Another limitation is that the convergence rates established in Theorem
21
do not improve when
f\in W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)
or
W^{1,q,\infty}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)
with
q>2
. It is worth studying how our method performs on integrands with mild or even no boundary growth. In particular, it is an open question whether our method can reproduce the dimension-independent convergence rates in
[
21
]
under the same assumptions.

In
[
33
]
, the authors prove the Möbius-transformed trapezoidal rule achieve the optimal convergence rate in the one-dimensional
\rho
-weighted Sobolev spaces
W_{\rho}^{\alpha,2}(\mathbb{R})
. We conjecture that trapezoidal rules combined with our boundary-damping IS can achieve the same convergence rates. A detailed analysis is beyond the scope of this paper and left for future study.

Lemma 25
.

For
\varphi
satisfying Assumption
1
and

,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)^{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 2c_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\varepsilon^{-2}.

Proof.

Because

, we know that
\Phi(0)=1/2
and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)^{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}x=2\int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)^{1-\varepsilon}}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 2c_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{1}{\Phi(x)^{(1-\varepsilon)(1+\varepsilon)}}\,\mathrm{d}\Phi(x)=2c_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\varepsilon^{-2}2^{-\varepsilon^{2}}.

Proof of Lemma
2
.

Note that for any
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{s}
, the following inequality holds:

|f(\boldsymbol{x})|^{q}\prod_{j=1}^{s}\varphi(x_{j})\leq\varphi_{\infty}^{s}\|f\|_{L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}^{q},

or equivalently

|f(\boldsymbol{x})|\leq\varphi_{\infty}^{s/q}\|f\|_{L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{s}\varphi(x_{j})\right)^{-1/q}.

(25)

We thus have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}^{q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{s}}|f(\boldsymbol{x})|^{q^{\prime}}\prod_{j=1}^{s}\varphi(x_{j})\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle\leq\varphi_{\infty}^{sq^{\prime}/q}\|f\|_{L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}^{q^{\prime}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{s}}\prod_{j=1}^{s}\varphi(x_{j})^{1-q^{\prime}/q}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{\infty}^{sq^{\prime}/q}\|f\|_{L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}^{q^{\prime}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)^{1-q^{\prime}/q}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{s}.

Denote
I_{\alpha}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)^{1-\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}x
for
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Since
q>q^{\prime}
, the exponent
1-q^{\prime}/q\in(0,1)
. Lemma
25
shows that
I_{q^{\prime}/q}<\infty
. Thus, we have

\|f\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}^{q^{\prime}}\leq(\varphi_{\infty}^{q^{\prime}/q}I_{q^{\prime}/q})^{s}\|f\|_{L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}^{q^{\prime}}.

Taking the
(1/q^{\prime})
-th root of both sides gives the first claim

\|f\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}\leq(\varphi_{\infty}^{q^{\prime}/q}I_{q^{\prime}/q})^{s/q^{\prime}}\|f\|_{L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}.

This proves the inequality with a constant
C_{q,q^{\prime}}=(\varphi_{\infty}^{q^{\prime}/q}I_{q^{\prime}/q})^{1/q^{\prime}}
.

For the second inequality,
we first apply the result from the first part to each term
\partial^{v}f
,

\|\partial^{v}f\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}\leq(\varphi_{\infty}^{q^{\prime}/q}I_{q^{\prime}/q})^{s/q^{\prime}}\|\partial^{v}f\|_{L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}.

Substituting this into the Sobolev norm definition gives

\displaystyle\|f\|_{W^{1,q^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{v\subseteq\{1,\dots,s\}}\|\partial^{v}f\|^{q^{\prime}}_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}\right)^{1/q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq(\varphi_{\infty}^{q^{\prime}/q}I_{q^{\prime}/q})^{s/q^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{v\subseteq\{1,\dots,s\}}\|\partial^{v}f\|^{q^{\prime}}_{L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}\right)^{1/q^{\prime}}.

Now, let
\boldsymbol{b}
be a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{2^{s}}
with components
b_{v}=\|\partial^{v}f\|_{L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}
. The expression above is
\varphi_{\infty}(I_{q^{\prime}/q})^{s/q^{\prime}}\|\boldsymbol{b}\|_{q^{\prime}}
. For a finite-dimensional vector space, we know that for
q>q^{\prime}
, the
\ell_{q^{\prime}}
norm is bounded by the
\ell_{q}
norm. The dimension of our vector space is the number of subsets
v
, which is
d=2^{s}
. The inequality is

\|\boldsymbol{b}\|_{q^{\prime}}\leq d^{(1/q^{\prime}-1/q)}\|\boldsymbol{b}\|_{q}=2^{s(q-q^{\prime})/(qq^{\prime})}\|\boldsymbol{b}\|_{q}.

Applying this inequality gives

\|f\|_{W^{1,q^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}\leq(\varphi_{\infty}^{q^{\prime}/q}I_{q^{\prime}/q})^{s/q^{\prime}}\cdot 2^{s(q-q^{\prime})/(qq^{\prime})}\|f\|_{W^{1,q,\infty}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}.

Taking
\tilde{C}_{q,q^{\prime}}=(\varphi_{\infty}^{q^{\prime}/q}I_{q^{\prime}/q})^{1/q^{\prime}}2^{(q-q^{\prime})/(qq^{\prime})}
completes the proof.

Proof of Lemma
4
.

For the

P_{j}
is a contraction on
W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)

\partial^{u}P_{j}g=P_{j}\partial^{u}g
if
j\notin u
, and
\partial^{u}P_{j}g=0

\displaystyle\|P_{j}g\|^{q}_{W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\subseteq 1{:}s}\|\partial^{u}(P_{j}g)\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\subseteq 1{:}s,j\notin u}\|P_{j}(\partial^{u}g)\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}.

\displaystyle\|P_{j}g\|^{q}_{W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}

This shows
\|P_{j}\|_{W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{mix}}\to W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{mix}}}\leq 1

\|I-P_{j}\|\leq\|I\|+\|P_{j}\|\leq 2

\displaystyle\|P_{j}h\|^{q}_{L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}

is bounded on

.
Using the same reasoning as in the first part, for any

\displaystyle\|P_{j}g\|^{q}_{W^{1,q,\infty}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{u\subseteq 1{:}s,j\notin u}C_{j}^{q}\|\partial^{u}g\|^{q}_{L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}\leq C_{j}^{q}\|g\|^{q}_{W^{1,q,\infty}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathbb{R}^{s},\varphi)}.

Thus,

is a bounded operator on

. Consequently,

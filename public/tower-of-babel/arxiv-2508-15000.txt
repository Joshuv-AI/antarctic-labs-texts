Helicity amplitudes of N ⁡ ( 1520 ) N(1520) and N ⁡ ( 1535 ) N(1535) including pentaquark components Preprint: APS/123-QED
K. Xu, A. Kaewsnod, Z. Zhao, A. Limphirat, Y. Yan

Abstract
We investigate the helicity amplitudes of the N ⁡ ( 1520 ) N(1520) and N ⁡ ( 1535 ) N(1535) resonances in the electromagnetic transition process γ ∗ ​ p → N ∗ \gamma^{*}p\to N^{*} , where the N ⁡ ( 1520 ) N(1520) and N ⁡ ( 1535 ) N(1535) are assumed to include both the L = 1 L=1 three-quark and L = 0 L=0 spatial symmetric q 4 ​ q ¯ q^{4}\bar{q} pentaquark components. The helicity transition amplitudes A 1 / 2 A_{1/2} , A 3 / 2 A_{3/2} (for spin 3/2 states) and S 1 / 2 S_{1/2} are computed within the constituent quark model. The inclusion of the q 4 ​ q ¯ q^{4}\bar{q} pentaquark components via the γ ∗ → q ​ q ¯ \gamma^{*}\to q\bar{q} diagram significantly improves the theoretical description of the helicity amplitudes for both N ⁡ ( 1520 ) N(1520) and N ⁡ ( 1535 ) N(1535) , yielding a closer agreement with experimental data compared to the pure three-quark picture. The work reveals that the N ⁡ ( 1520 ) N(1520) and N ⁡ ( 1535 ) N(1535) resonances may contain a considerable pentaquark components.

Introduction

In the past two decades, new data on the electroproduction of low-lying nucleon resonances which have been accumulated at JLab
Thompson et al. 2001
;
Aznauryan 2003
;
Dalton et al. 2009
;
Aznauryan et al. 2008
;
Aznauryan et al. 2005
;
Aznauryan et al. 2009
;
Denizli et al. 2007
;
Mokeev et al. 2012
;
Mokeev et al. 2016
and by the Mainz group
Drechsel et al. 2007a
;
Tiator et al. 2009
;
Tiator et al. 2011
provides us important information to reveal their inner structures. As the most common representation of inner structure, helicity amplitudes in all the
\gamma^{*}N\to N^{*}
transitions are parametrized in terms of the three different photon polarizations, including two transverse amplitudes
A_{1/2}
and
A_{3/2}
(for spin 3/2 states) and one longitudinal amplitude
S_{1/2}
. One may see some good reviews about helicity amplitudes in Refs.
Drechsel et al. 2007b
;
Aznauryan and Burkert 2012a
;
Ramalho and Peña 2024
.

Numerous theoretical approaches have been developed to give a proper description of the helicity amplitudes of N(1520) and N(1535), such as a variety of quark models: single quark transition model
Burkert et al. 2003
, light-front quark models
Capstick and Keister 1995
;
Pace et al. 2000
;
Obukhovsky et al. 2019
;
Aznauryan and Burkert 2012b
;
Aznauryan and Burkert 2017
, hypercentral constituent quark model
Giannini and Santopinto 2015
;
Giannini et al. 2001
;
Santopinto and Giannini 2012
. In addition, non-perturbative QCD based approaches such as QCD sum rules
Braun et al. 2009
;
Anikin et al. 2015
and holographic Ads/QCD models
Gutsche et al. 2020
.

Beyond the pure three-quark picture, more complex internal structures involving hadronic degrees of freedom have been explored. These include meson cloud effects, baryon-meson hadronic molecular components, and pentaquark components which all indicate a
qqq(q\bar{q})
quark content, have been proposed for a better understanding of the
\gamma^{*}p\to N(1520)/N(1535)
transitions
Burkert and Lee 2004
;
Kamano et al. 2013
;
Jido et al. 2008
;
Mokeev et al. 2016
;
Aznauryan and Burkert 2012b
;
Ramalho and Peña 2014
;
Ramalho and Peña 2017
;
Ramalho et al. 2012
;
Julia-Diaz et al. 2008
;
An and Zou 2009
;
Zou 2010
. For the N(1520) resonance, several studies suggest that baryon-meson molecular-like configurations could play a dominant role in its structure
Mokeev et al. 2016
;
Ramalho and Peña 2014
;
Ramalho and Peña 2017
. Dynamical coupled-channel models further provide a unified framework in which both the
N(1520)
and
N(1535)
emerge naturally as baryon-meson resonant states
Burkert and Lee 2004
;
Kamano et al. 2013
. Similarly, within the unitary chiral model, N(1535) is interpreted as a dynamically generated state with a strong baryon-meson component
Jido et al. 2008
. And additional
qqq(q\bar{q})
components are incorporated into the wave functions of N(1535) to improve the description of its helicity amplitudes, using distinct spatial scale parameters in Refs.
An and Zou 2009
;
Zou 2010
.

In this work, we investigate the helicity amplitudes of the
N(1520)
and
N(1535)
resonances in a mixing structure of three-quark and pentaquark components, by extending a constituent quark model originally developed from the first principle to compute the helicity transition amplitudes of three-quark nucleon resonances within the quantum field theory framework
Kaewsnod et al. 2022a
;
Kaewsnod et al. 2022b
. In Ref.
Kaewsnod et al. 2022b
we have pushed the three-quark contribution to its the upper limit, but it is found that only the
A_{1/2}
of N(1520) and N(1535) can be fairly described. In the previous work
Xu et al. 2020
the
N(1535)1/2^{-}
and
N(1520)3/2^{-}
masses were described by including the ground-state pentaquark components into the three-quark configurations. This may suggest that both the
N(1520)
and
N(1535)
include a considerable pentaquark components.

The paper is organized as follows. All possible quark configurations of ground state non-strange
q^{4}\bar{q}
pentaquarks are worked out in the language of permutation groups, the mass spectrum are also estimated in Sec.
II
. In Sec.
III

A_{1/2}
,
A_{3/2}
, and
S_{1/2}

N(1520)
and
N(1535)

\gamma^{*}p\to N^{*}
, where both the three-quark and pentaquark configurations are considered. The helicity amplitudes of the
N(1520)
and
N(1535)
are calculated in the conventional three-quark picture and the mixing picture of three-quark and pentaquark components in the range
0\leq Q^{2}\leq 4.5\;{\rm GeV^{2}}
and fitted to the experimental data in Sec.
IV

V

V
Summary and Discussion

In the present work we have fitted the helicity amplitudes of the
N(1520)
and
N(1535)
in the
\gamma^{*}p\to N^{*}
transition process to experimental data, where the
N(1520)
and
N(1535)
are assumed including both the
L=1
three-quark and
L=0
symmetric pentaquark component. By introducing the pentaquark component of the
\Psi^{csf}_{[211]_{C}[31]_{FS}[22]_{F}[31]_{S}}(q^{4}\bar{q})
configuration into the
N^{*}
, a fairly well description of the helicity amplitudes
A_{1/2}
,
A_{3/2}
, and
S_{1/2}
for the
N(1520)
and
N(1535)
resonances over the region
0\leq Q^{2}\leq 4.5\;{\rm GeV^{2}}
is achieved. The extracted pentaquark components are approximately
9\%
for
N(1520)
, and
25\%
for
N(1535)
.
The clear improvements to the
N(1520)
and
N(1535)
helicity amplitudes in the mixing picture suggest that pentaquark components in
N^{*}
via the
\gamma^{*}\to q\bar{q}
process may play an important role in the lower
Q^{2}
regions, especially for the
S_{1/2}
amplitudes.

The only notable discrepancies with data occur in the transverse helicity amplitudes of
N(1520)
at
Q^{2}\leq 0.5\;{\rm GeV^{2}}
and the longitudinal amplitude of
N(1535)
at
Q^{2}>2
\;{\rm GeV^{2}}
. These deviations may originate from meson cloud contributions, higher-order gluonic exchange contributions, or other dynamical mechanisms, which are not included in the present model. This work serves a starting point for future comprehensive studies that will incorporate contributions of pentaquark components, meson-clouds, and higher-order gluonic exchanges.

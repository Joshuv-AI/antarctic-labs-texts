A rapid-convergent particle swarm optimization approach for multiscale design of high-permeance seawater reverse osmosis systems
Authors: Ke Chen, Jiu Luo, Junzhi Chen, Yutong Lu, Yi Heng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractDirectly solving sophisticated partial differential equation constrained optimization problems is not only extremely time-consuming, but also very hard to find unique optimal solutions. Here, we propose stable and efficient surrogate models for seawater reverse osmosis desalination processes that enable thorough quantitative description of hydrodynamics and local transport characteristics in narrow flow channels. Without iteratively solving complex multi-physics simulation problem taking several hours, the proposed multi-scale design optimization framework significantly reduces the problem complexity by computing the surrogate models in seconds. Moreover, a fast-converging active subspace particle swarm optimization framework is proposed to address the optimal design problem. Compared to the standard particle swarm optimization algorithm, the proposed method enhances the average optimum by 14% and the standard deviation of optimum results for multiple runs is reduced by no less than ten times. The optimized desalination system achieves 9% reduction on energy consumption and 30% improvement on water production efficiency.Subject terms: Chemical engineering, Energy modelling

Introduction

The progress in multiscale optimal design techniques have demonstrated their significant utility in scientific and engineering applications, such as materials design1,2, protein structure design3,4 and aircraft topology optimization5,6. Through efficient, robust, global and multi-objective optimization algorithms, optimal design could provide better solutions for further decision making. Meanwhile, knowledge of the optimal results provides new insights into the mechanisms of the system achieving better understanding. In water resource, the advanced membrane materials, e.g., high permeability seawater reverse osmosis (SWRO) membranes have attracted widespread attention in the past several decades7,8. Fane et al. pointed out that the fluid mechanics and transport performance for current module is limited, and does not match with the development of membrane technologies, which defeats the advantage of high permeability SWRO membranes9. For high permeability SWRO systems, optimization of membrane module (at sub-millimeter scale) is critical to mitigate scaling and fouling10 (e.g., biofouling), and system design (at meter scale) has significant potential to improve the energy efficiency11. The optimal design problem of SWRO desalination system with advanced membrane material is a typical multiscale optimization problem that couples spacer design at sub-millimeter scale and system design at meter scale. Fastly obtaining optimal solution for the sophisticated optimization problem is extremely difficult owing to the complex nature of the system itself12. Hence, the fundamental question is how to develop an efficient and robust optimization approach to find the best solutions and realize optimal designs as fast as possible.

The benchmark optimal design problem considered in this paper, widely applied in multi-disciplinary fields, can be mathematically described as a partial differential equations (PDEs) model constrained optimization problem as below:

where x and m denote design variable and state variable, J is an objective of interest, the constraints FandC are usually related to high dimensional, multiscale, multi-physics PDEs system subject to realistic initial and boundary conditions in practice. PDE-constrained optimization has broad and important applications in engineering and science domains, including optimal design13, optimal control14 and inverse problems15. The research is mainly focused on the computation of feasible local optima due to the analytical complexity, computational intractability and nonconvexity nature of the system. To address the complex PDE-constrained optimization problem, it may need to iteratively solve hundreds or more PDEs using traditional gradient-based optimization approaches, e.g., the conjugate gradient, Gauss–Newton (G–N) and Levenberg–Marquardt (L–M) methods, which are extremely time-consuming and expensive in computational cost. Particularly, mixed integer PDE-constrained optimization combines the difficulties of integer programming and PDEs handling. Traditional algorithms, such as Branch-and-Bound and Cutting-Plane method, usually face the curse of dimensionality and require large amounts of storage when controlling the discrete variables16. One of the main difficulties for addressing mixed-integer PDE-constrained optimization problems is how to solve the PDEs accurately and efficiently.

There are many solution frameworks when treating PDEs referring to gradient-based approaches. The relatively common ones are discretize-then-optimize approaches or optimize-then-discretize approaches. For the former one, PDEs are discretized completely, reducing the infinite-dimensional optimization problem into the finite-dimensional optimization problem, and solving it with efficient nonlinear programming solvers17,18. This framework typically leads to a large and sparse optimization system to solve, and usually faces the curse of dimensionality19. Inversely, the optimize-then-discretize framework derives the continuous optimality condition analytically and then discretizes the adjoint differential equations with appropriate methods, such as finite difference and finite element20,21. However, these type of methods could lead to more numerically complexity and cause convergence problems due to inaccurate gradients18. Furthermore, directly performing large-scale complex simulations would be time-consuming owing to the tedious mesh generation and numerical iterations. Therefore, surrogate model techniques for PDEs, such as Gaussian response surfaces, physical-informed kriging models, support vector machines, and artificial neural networks22–25, have been extensively utilized to rapidly evaluate complex systems. Moreover, the surrogate model approach could be combined with mechanism model to obtain more accurate results26 that can contribute to remove gaps between different scales. Among these approaches, high-dimensional-model-representation (HDMR) is promising and easy-to-implement when dealing with high-dimensional inputs27.

In engineering practice, module improvement for SWRO desalination usually adopts manual design with experience and comparisons, which only considers a few design values28 and is hard to find the optimal solution due to its strong nonlinearity. Taking it as objective-based optimization process, the feasible region of the problem is usually rugged with many local minima29. Gradient-based optimization greatly relies on the choice of initial value and is easily stuck in the local optimum. Without relevant experience, it is tough to have a reasonable result unless a good initial guess is available. In general, gradient-based methods are not robust enough to tackle complex engineering optimal design problem30. In recent years, derivative-free optimization has aroused great attention. The multi-agent systems without using derivative information improve the efficiency of the optimization procedure. Population-based optimization methods, such as particle swarm optimization (PSO) and genetic algorithm (GA), show robustness with less possibility to be trapped at a local minimum owing to the stochastic section. Population-based algorithm is frequently utilized in complex optimization problem. Qi et al. 31 utilized PSO to tackle inverse transient radiation problem, which is proved to be fast, accurate and robust. However, the main drawback of population-based stochastic method is the premature convergence, which would cause poor accuracy in constrained optimization based on penalty function method. Meanwhile, when dealing with complex optimization problems, the population-based methods require many fitness function evaluations during exploration process, which will markedly increase the computational cost and weaken the advantage of these methods. In this paper, we introduce the active subspace (AS) to enhance the performance of population-based stochastic method. AS is firstly proposed in the field of uncertainty quantification for eliminating dimensions of the surrogate model’s inputs to reduce the number of sampling and raise the fitting accuracy32–34. It generalizes the idea of deciding vital variables through sensitive analysis. AS technique presents a more fluctuant structure of the function’s output by rotating the original input space with a lower or the same dimension35, which has been successfully utilized in engineering applications such as shape optimization in vehicle design36,37.

In this paper, AS method is integrated with the PSO process, which develops the so-called active subspace based particle swarm optimization (ASPSO) approach below, to efficiently and accurately tackle PDE-constrained optimization problems. The integer variables in this problem is handled by the penalty method for continuity assumption of AS method. The main idea of AS is linearly mapping the original input space into the AS through the analysis of covariance matrix of the gradients. The AS method identifies the direction that barely fluctuates, thus the velocity and position update process of ASPSO is performed in the projected subspace to avoid meaningless evolution. Recently, AS method combined with GA is put forward to achieve faster convergence and proves to perform better in the high dimensional problems38. GA and PSO both belong to the biologically inspired optimization framework. It was reported that PSO has a better computational efficiency in both constrained and unconstrained optimization problems39, while GA could treat discrete variables naturally through binary coding40. These two approaches have their own advantages and application areas. Therefore, it is still important to evaluate the effectiveness of AS approach applied in PSO process. On the whole, a surrogate-model based optimization framework is proposed in this paper for the solution of the reverse osmosis (RO) desalination multiscale optimal design problem. The main contributions of this paper are as follows:

A regularized HDMR, which is hybrid with the holomorphic embedding method to solve simulation-based differential algebra equations (DAEs), is proposed to enhance the robustness and accuracy of the surrogate models through many high-fidelity, multi-physics computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations with high-throughput parallel computing.

A regularized HDMR, which is hybrid with the holomorphic embedding method to solve simulation-based differential algebra equations (DAEs), is proposed to enhance the robustness and accuracy of the surrogate models through many high-fidelity, multi-physics computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations with high-throughput parallel computing.

A framework of ASPSO for solving DAEs constrained optimization problem is proposed to improve the convergence rate. And it is proved to have higher performance than the usual population-based evolutionary optimization algorithms.

A framework of ASPSO for solving DAEs constrained optimization problem is proposed to improve the convergence rate. And it is proved to have higher performance than the usual population-based evolutionary optimization algorithms.

Methods

Multiscale optimal design problem for SWRO desalination system

In this paper, the multiscale optimal design problem for SWRO desalination system involves the feed spacer design at sub-millimeter scale and system design at meter scale. However, it is computationally intractable to simulate such a complicated three-dimensional (3D) multi-physics model for the whole RO system. Fortunately, the typical membrane module consists of repeated pattern structures, and thus the local hydrodynamics, transport characteristic may be captured using small scale CFD simulations. Therefore, a multiscale modeling methodology is adopted that couples 3D nonlinear PDEs problem at sub-millimeter scale (CFD model) and one-dimensional (1D) DAEs problem at meter scale (system-level model).

The optimal design framework for SWRO desalination is shown in Fig. 1. After obtained the accurate and robust HDMR surrogate models with numerous parallel-computed high-fidelity CFD simulation data in sub-millimeter scale, we randomly initialize a set of the spacer geometrical and systematic parameters, solve the surrogate-mechanism 1D model employing holomorphic embedding solver at meter scale and then compute energy consumption at plant level. Utilizing the ASPSO framework, the purpose of the optimal design problem is to find appropriate feed spacer geometry and system parameters (module length and module arrangement) for the minimum energy consumption and membrane area.

Regularized high dimensional model representation

Interpolation or fitting of a multivariate function is a common issue in natural science and engineering. However, when input variables are in high-dimension spaces, the problem would be difficult to solve utilizing common interpolation or expansion methods. HDMR42 provides an efficient function map to approximate the nonlinear, implicit and high dimensional relations between sets of input and output variables. The main feature of HDMR model is decomposing the full function into summands of different dimensions. Fast and flexible holomorphic embedding method

A fast and flexible holomorphic embedding (FFHE) method could be used to numerically solve a lot of common equations. It usually includes 3 important steps, which are deriving series expansions and rational approximants, segmenting intervals adaptively, and constructing approximate global solution. The FFHE method is for the first time applied in this application field and it provides new insights for solving the DAEs system for the optimal design problem of SWRO process in this paper. The details of the FFHE method can be found in the ref. 46, which calculates power flow in power systems successfully and demonstrates its effectiveness in engineering problems.

ASPSO with mutation

PSO is an intelligence optimization method inspired by the social behavior of biotic population, which is proposed by Kennedy and Eberhart47. This algorithm emulates the interaction between individuals to share information and find near-optimal as a result. Each particle keeps changing the search pattern based on the learning experience of itself and others. The concrete details of PSO with mutation algorithm can be found in Supplementary Information Text “Active subspace particle swarm optimization with mutation”.

Results and discussion

Benchmark function test

The PSO, GA, Ant Colony Optimization (ACO), active subspace genetic algorithm (ASGA) and ASPSO are applied to solve the typical benchmark problems for testing Rastrigin, Sphere, Rosenbrock, Ackley, and Bohachevsky functions38,50. The variable dimension is fixed as 40 and the variable range is [−5.12, 5.12]d. All the test functions have the same minimum of zero. For the GA and ACO, the hyperparmeters are chosen as the default values in MATLAB toolbox. It has the same hyperparmeters for PSO and ASPSO, GA, and ASGA for the fair comparison. The dimension of AS is chosen as 3 and population size is fixed as 200.

The optimized results using PSO, GA, ACO, ASGA and ASPSO are shown in Table 1 for the five benchmark problems. The average and standard deviation are calculated over 15 runs of the algorithms. As we can see, ASPSO and ASGA performs significantly better than PSO, GA and ACO for all benchmark functions, indicating the effectiveness of introducing AS. Moreover, with subspace projection, the algorithms can achieve better numerical stability since the standard deviation for different experiments is much lower than the other population-based intelligent algorithms. ASPSO outperforms ASGA in four benchmarks and they performs consistently in one on the average minimum, indicating ASPSO has greater exploration ability than ASGA. Experiments of small population size, which is set as 10, are also performed on ASPSO and ASGA shown in Table S2. The results indicate that ASPSO is relatively unaffected by population size, achieving better global optimum and algorithm stability than ASGA.

CFD simulated results

In this paper, we use a Latin hypercube-like sampling method proposed in our previous work51 to obtain typical design parameters that are applied to parametric CFD modeling and generate the required training data for data-driven surrogate modeling. A high throughput computing approach is used to efficiently solve the established 734 CFD models with respect to various γ=[D1,A,D2,A,D1,B,D2,B,Dtot,α;Uavg]52. The computational scale in theoretically reaches 885 nodes (21,240 cores in total). The simulated hydraulic results (pressure and velocity profiles), are shown in Fig. 2 for both typical cases (case 1 and case 2) that have different feed spacer geometries (G1, G2) and have the same inlet velocity parameter (U0 = 0.1 m/s). The pressure drop per unit length for case 1 (0.047 bar/m) is just 55% than that of the case 2 (0.086 bar/m). The concentration on membrane walls are much higher than that of the bulk concentration due to the selective leaking for water. The average mass transfer coefficient (8.27 × 10−5 m/s) of the case 1 is higher than that of the case 2 (7.92 × 10−5 m/s). It results in a more serious salt accumulation in case 2 (Fig. S2), which leads to the reduction of the water flux on the membrane surfaces (Fig. S3). Therefore, feed spacer design can mitigate the concentration polarization and reduce the flow resistance to some extent. The spacer optimization can be considered as a tradeoff between both factors. In this work, 3D CFD simulations with respect to various design parameters are still expensive in total computational cost although the high throughput computing approach is applied to significantly accelerate computing process. Machine learning with embedded physical information will be further developed to accurately and efficiently solve the 3D PDEs of this work, which can greatly reduce the total computational cost for solving the parametric 3D multi-physical problems.

Evaluation of surrogate models

Regularized HDMR method is utilized in this paper to obtain surrogate models for the goal of avoiding overfitting and improve stability. The training and test data are obtained by high-fidelity CFD parallel simulations described above. In this section, the results and discoveries of surrogate models are demonstrated. The choice of regularization parameter λ and cross-validation method are demonstrated.

The standard L2 norm is added to objective function to suppress the model parameters and improve model generalization and we minimize the sum of relative errors and regularization term using BARON solver in GAMS53. The mean test MSE of 10-fold cross-validation varied with regularization parameter λ is shown in Fig. 3, which is a U-shaped curve and the shade region denotes the maximum and minimum test MSE.

Regularization parameter λ controls the trade-off between the accuracy of training data and generalization ability of the model. When λ is too small or too large, the surrogate models is overfitting or underfitting leading to the larger test MSE. Analysis of AS in the optimal design problem

The ASPSO algorithm is proposed to solve the optimal design problem in SWRO desalination. The input variable x is the 10-dimensional vector with specific ranges including integer variables. Since it is a mixed-integer constrained optimization problem, the penalty function method is utilized to treat integer variables54. The DAEs in optimal design problem are solved by the FFHE solver.

The purpose of AS technic is rotating high-dimensional input space to reveal the most fluctuant direction. To better construct and understand the AS approach, we scale the input vector x to [0,1]×R10. Latin hypercube sampling strategy is taken50,55 to approximate expectation of gradient’s covariance matrix. In summary, solving high-dimensional PDE constrained optimization problems is usually computationally very expensive owing to the intractable and nonlinear nature of complex PDEs and complex computational domains to be tackled. To improve the efficiency of an optimal design procedure, accurate and stable surrogate models or faster solvers are inevitably required. This paper proposes a universal optimization approach based on AS technic that can efficiently, accurately and stably solve optimization problems, where regularized HDMR method and FFHE solver are utilized to tackle surrogate modeling and differential equation discretization problems.

In this paper, 734 high-fidelity and multi-physics CFD simulations with respect to various design parameters are performed efficiently using high-throughput computing approach to create training and test data sets. The total computational scale reaches 21,240 cores on the “Tianhe-2” supercomputer. Then the regularized HDMR method is proposed to establish surrogate models. It has better prediction accuracy and stability with the use of the 10-flod cross-validation compared to the predicted results without regularization. For optimal design problem in SWRO desalination system, ASPSO is proposed to overcome the low convergent rate and stagnancy problems in intelligent optimization field. The original input space is projected by uncentered covariance matrix of gradients through supervised learning method. The proposed framework significantly outperforms the other population-based intelligent algorithms on global optimization performance, convergence rate and stability with the use of five typical benchmark optimization problems and multiscale design optimization problems in SWRO desalination. The optimized results using the ASPSO are stable ranging from 2.19 to 2.27 while the PSO fluctuates from 2.19 to 2.9 with respect to various population sizes of 40, 120 and 200 for the SWRO system.

ASPSO algorithm achieves better performance than the classic PSO and has abundant potential of application in many other high-dimensional optimal design problems. The future improvement of this framework could be focused on the information loss when high-dimensional input space is reduced to a lower structure. The better strategy of constructing the original space and the selection of dimension of AS should be considered for greater accuracy. Due to the fact that the multi-physics CFD simulations with high-fidelity are time-consuming and expensive in computational cost, we will further develop physics-informed neural network-based framework to solve the sophisticated 3D PDEs efficiently and accurately in the next work.

Use of scaffolds based on polyetheretherketone tubular implant in the treatment of a gunshot multifragmentary fracture of the proximal part of the shoulder with a bone defect: a case report
Authors: Oleksandr Buryanov, Igor Lurin, Bazarov Murat, Yurii Yarmoliuk, Artem Bespalenko, Oleg Smyk, Yurii Klapchuk, Dmytro Los
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIn connection with the war in Ukraine and the use of modern high-energy weapons by the enemy, the nature of injuries have changed. These changes require improvement of approaches to the treatment of patients with gunshot and mine-explosive bone defects.Case presentationWe present the case of treatment of a 28-year-old Ukranian man with a gunshot multifragmentary fracture of the proximal humerus with a bone defect using three-dimensional printing of a polyetheretherketone frame (scaffold) for alloplastic material. The analysis of this case expands the possibilities of using three-dimensional printing technologies of polyetheretherketone scaffolds in the treatment of significant bone defects.ConclusionModern military trauma requires individualized treatment for each patient. The use of individual polyetheretherketone scaffolds in the treatment of a gunshot multifragmentary fracture with a bone defect has a positive clinical effect.

Background

The majority of gunshot fractures of long bones are multifragmentary—this determines the complexity of reconstructive operations. Patients with such fractures require multistage treatment using various bone–plastic surgical interventions and long-term rehabilitation periods.

Replacement of bone defects can be temporary, using spacer systems, and permanent, using autobone, allobone, bioceramics, titanium augments, and frames based on the use of three-dimensional (3D) printing from inert metallic and nonmetallic (composite) materials. These frames (scaffolds) can serve as containers for auto- and alloplastic materials.

Technologies for preoperative 3D planning and 3D modeling of implants to replace bone defects are actively developing. As 7.1% of victims with gunshot wounds to the extremities are diagnosed with primary bone defects, which in 79.3% of victims exceed 3 cm [1], the use of 3D modeling becomes especially relevant.

Polyetheretherketone (PEEK) is a polyaromatic semicrystalline thermoplastic polymer with mechanical properties favorable for biomedical applications. Polyetheretherketone forms: PEEK-LT1, PEEK-LT2, and PEEK-LT3 have already been applied in different surgical fields, including spine surgery, orthopedic surgery, maxillo-facial surgery etc. [2, 3].

PEEK excels in fracture fixation owing to its radiolucency [4]. Surgeons can see how the patient’s tissues react to this material, allowing doctors to monitor and predict the healing process. PEEK offers an optimal mix of flexibility and stiffness, ensuring it does not get in the way of the body’s own healing processes. PEEK has an optimal combination of flexibility and stiffness. Another PEEK property that is of particular interest in orthopedics is its lightness. PEEK is much lighter than metal, which allows patients to manipulate joints and limbs with less effort than would be necessary with heavier metals.

In addition, based on the recommendations of ISO10993 “Evaluation of the biological effect of medical devices,” tests were conducted for cytotoxicity, systemic acute toxicity, sensitization, and mutagenicity, which ultimately showed the biocompatibility of this material. Studies have shown that human osteoblasts are able to attach, grow, and multiply on this composite material.

The PEEK frame (scaffold) is a 3D-printed individual grid that serves as a container for auto- and alloplastic material.

Case presentation

The patient was a 28-year-old (1994) Ukranian man who was admitted for treatment to the Bogomolets national medical Clinic on 15 March 2022 with the diagnosis: “mine-explosive injury (14 March 2022). Fire shrapnel wound of the proximal part of the right shoulder. A gunshot multifragmentary fracture (stage III) of the proximal part of the shoulder with a bone defect (7 cm) and with the presence of foreign bodies in the projection of the fracture. Posttraumatic neuropathy of the median nerve with mild paresis of the flexors of the fingers.”

Discussion

In connection with the increase in the number of limb injuries with large bone defects, there is a need to find new or improve existing methods of treatment of such conditions. We propose the use of individual 3D printed PEEK scaffolds in combination with auto- or allografts as a new method of replacing large bone defects. Owing to 3D planning, the anatomy of the damaged bone fragment can be recreated as accurately as possible with an individual PEEK scaffold.

This material (PEEK) has an optimal combination of flexibility and stiffness, which is close to the parameters of autobone. Other advantages of this material are its radiolucency, low weight, and biocompatibility. However, the issue of further interaction of PEEK scaffolds with body tissues remains unresolved. That is why there is a need for further research and modifications of this material.

For positive results of such surgical interventions, we recommend following the general rules for replacing the fixation method: elimination of inflammation, healing of gunshot wounds, healing of wounds after removal of the external fixation device, reduction of local edema, normalization of laboratory blood parameters, and absence of infectious agents in the fracture area during bacteriological examination.

For better biointegration of the PEEK scaffold, it is advisable to modernize this material with the possible use of orthobiological technologies.

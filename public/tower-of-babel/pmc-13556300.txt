Low energetic disorder molecules: A new paradigm for breaking the trade-off of voltage and current in organic solar cells
Authors: Jinjing Qiu, Gang Li, Chang-Qi Ma, Qun Luo
Published: 2026

Main text

The power conversion efficiency (PCE) of organic solar cells (OSCs) has rapidly approached and surpassed 20% owing to continuous advances in non-fullerene acceptors (NFAs).1 However, further progress remains fundamentally constrained by the intrinsic trade-off between short-circuit current density (JSC) and open-circuit voltage (VOC), particularly in narrow-band-gap systems.2 Although narrowing the band gap effectively broadens photon harvesting, the reduced charge-transfer-state energies and associated voltage losses in narrow-band-gap systems often limit the achievable VOC. Considerable efforts have been devoted to suppressing energy losses in OSCs through molecular engineering, optimization of donor-acceptor combinations, and blend morphology.3 These approaches have successfully lowered energetic disorder and non-radiative voltage losses, yet simultaneously achieving a narrow band gap, low energetic disorder, and efficient near-infrared photon harvesting remains a major challenge.

To address this challenge, Tao et al. report a compelling strategy by designing a quinoxaline-based acceptor, Qx-Se-NF, that combines a narrow band gap (Eg ≈ 1.31 eV) with exceptionally low energetic disorder (Figure 1A).4 By integrating a rigid naphthyl terminal group and a selenium-substituted core, the authors demonstrate that reducing electron-vibration coupling and energetic disorder can effectively suppress non-radiative recombination, thereby mitigating voltage loss without sacrificing current. Unlike inorganic semiconductors, OSCs inherently exhibit significant energetic disorder arising from molecular conformational variability and intermolecular packing fluctuations. This disorder leads to band tailing and enhanced non-radiative recombination, which dominates total energy loss. This work reveals that the Qx-Se-NF-based blend exhibits the lowest Urbach energy (Eu ≈ 22.75 meV), indicating reduced energetic disorder compared with those of Y6 and related derivatives. The total disorder (σT) is decomposed into static disorder (σS) and dynamic disorder (σD) (Figure 1B), which are related to the structural heterogeneity and electron-vibration coupling and reorganization energy (λ), respectively. The authors demonstrate that both σS and σD are minimized in Qx-Se-NF, primarily due to the enhanced molecular rigidity imparted by the naphthyl terminal group, reduced vibrational contributions by suppressing C-C stretching modes, and improved planarity and intermolecular packing. This dual suppression leads to a significant reduction in non-radiative energy loss (ΔEnr), validating energetic disorder as a unifying parameter linking molecular structure to device physics.

A central highlight of this work is the successful decoupling of the VOC-JSC trade-off (Figure 1C). Conventionally, reducing the band gap enhances JSC while also resulting in VOC losses due to increased non-radiative recombination. However, by minimizing energetic disorder of the narrow-band-gap acceptor, Qx-Se-NF achieves high JSC of 28.8 mA cm−2 while maintaining high VOC and low total energy loss of 0.486 eV despite reduced lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) level. These features lead to a certified PCE surpassing 21% (Figure 1D). This demonstrates that energetic disorder engineering provides an orthogonal design axis beyond traditional energy-level alignment or morphology optimization.

Beyond molecular design, an intriguing vertically segregated alloy-like morphology in D18:L8-BO:Qx-Se-NF-based ternary systems was also discovered. The formation of such a morphology is attributed to the strong aggregation properties of Qx-Se-NF. As a result, the host acceptor L8-BO enriches near the donor-acceptor interface, while Qx-Se-NF forms a gradient distribution in this architecture. Such an arrangement is beneficial for enhancing JSC while maintaining VOC, thereby enabling simultaneous improvement of device performance. This concept goes beyond conventional cascade or energy relay models, suggesting that compositionally graded acceptor phases may represent a new design paradigm for high-efficiency OSCs. In addition, this work demonstrates excellent processability using non-halogenated solvents and scalable coating techniques with a PCE of 17.7% for 1 cm2 devices and 17.1% for 36 cm2 modules. This highlights the practical relevance of low-disorder acceptors for large-area manufacturing, addressing a key limitation in translating lab-scale efficiencies to real-world applications.

In conclusion, this work provides compelling evidence that energetic disorder represents a fundamental bottleneck limiting the efficiency of OSCs and establishes a clear molecular design strategy to overcome this constraint. By demonstrating that rational molecular engineering can simultaneously deliver high JSC and high VOC, this work highlights that the long-standing trade-off between JSC and VOC in low-band-gap materials is not intrinsic but can be alleviated through reducing energetic disorder. More fundamentally, it underscores that energy loss in organic photovoltaic devices is intimately correlated with the degree of energetic disorder at the molecular level, linking microscopic electron-vibration coupling and structural heterogeneity to macroscopic device performance. Thus, the development of narrow-band-gap acceptors featuring low energetic disorder and strong aggregation characteristics emerges as a promising pathway to concurrently suppress non-radiative losses and enhance photocurrent generation, thereby providing a coherent molecular design framework for next-generation materials. At the device level, the vertically segregated alloy-like morphology identified in ternary systems also offers an effective strategy to optimize performance. By enabling a gradient phase distribution that preserves interfacial energetics while facilitating bulk charge transport, such architecture allows simultaneous improvements in JSC and fill factors without compromising VOC. Collectively, this work highlights the importance of intrinsic electronic disorder control and of synergistic molecular and morphological design. These insights open new avenues for pushing OSC efficiencies beyond 21% and accelerating their translation toward scalable, high-performance applications.

The continuous evolution of OSC efficiencies from fullerene-based systems to NFAs and emerging low-disorder narrow-band-gap materials (Figure 1D) highlights the importance of simultaneously extending photon harvesting and minimizing energetic disorder to further advance photovoltaic performance. Overcoming this challenge will require molecular strategies that simultaneously minimize static and dynamic energetic disorder through enhanced molecular rigidity, optimized intermolecular packing, and reduced electron-vibration coupling. Increasing molecular rigidity and improving molecular planarity are expected to reduce σS, while minimizing molecular reorganization energy can weaken electron-vibration coupling and suppress σD. Meanwhile, regulating ordered intermolecular packing and favorable aggregation characteristics may facilitate charge transport while maintaining low energetic disorder. In addition, morphological engineering is closely related to molecular structure, and developing organic molecules with strong aggregation properties provides a promising route toward vertically graded phase separation. Together, these molecular and device design principles provide a promising roadmap for breaking the efficiency limitations of OSCs and accelerating their transition toward next-generation photovoltaic applications.

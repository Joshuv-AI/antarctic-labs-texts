Toward risk‐stratified management of placental site nodules with atypical features
Authors: Tingting Xu, Shuhong Li
Published: 2026

Sir,

Parker et al. 1 report no malignant transformations in 22 atypical placental site nodule cases, contrasting 11–15% gestational trophoblastic neoplasia rates from Kaur et al. 2 (14% in 21 cases) and Young et al. 3 (15.4% epithelioid trophoblastic tumor in subsequent pathology of 26 cases). This discrepancy highlights diagnostic challenges; the histologic overlap of atypical placental site nodules risks overdiagnosis, as Lin et al. 4 demonstrated fair interobserver reproducibility (kappa = 0.34) among experts, with consensus often requiring three or more worrisome features (e.g., mitotic activity, Ki67 ≥ 5%, necrosis).

The absence of retrospective pathology review for prior epithelioid trophoblastic tumors or placental site trophoblastic tumors limits accurate risk estimation. Jeremie et al. 5 showed atypical placental site nodules molecularly resemble benign placental site nodules, lacking LPCAT1::TERT fusions common in epithelioid trophoblastic tumors, suggesting low‐risk subsets suitable for conservative approaches in fertility‐preserving patients.

We recommend routine molecular genotyping to identify pregnancy intervals and refine human chorionic gonadotropin‐independent surveillance, alongside an international registry using World Health Organization 2020 criteria to standardize management and resolve debates on hysterectomy timing.

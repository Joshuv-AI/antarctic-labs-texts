Establishing Independent Predictors of Early Geriatric Hip Fracture Surgery
Authors: Akkasil Pinchumponsang, Athip Kongklam, Sutee Thaveepunsan, Pumibal Wetpiriyakul, Auttakorn Karnjanapiboonwong, Natthapong Hongku
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeThe aim of our study is to identify the factors that influence hip fracture surgery in elderly patients within 48 hours of hospital admission.Materials and MethodsWe retrospectively reviewed hip fracture databases of patients who underwent surgery for hip fracture between 2020 and 2023. We excluded patients with pathological fractures, coronavirus disease 2019 infection, multiple traumas, and who died without undergoing surgery. The final study cohort included 452 patients with hip fractures. From the total participants, 201 patients underwent hip surgery within 48 hours of admission. For data analysis, we performed multivariate logistic regression with odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for each variable. A P<0.05 was considered statistically significant.ResultsMultivariate logistic regression analysis revealed that lack of pretreatment with P2Y12 inhibitors (adjusted OR 3.23, 95% CI 1.41-7.41), absence of hematological disorders (adjusted OR 3.53, 95% CI 1.35-9.23), and absence of urinary tract infections (UTI) (adjusted OR 9.59, 95% CI 1.21-75.71) in geriatric patients with hip fracture were conducive to early surgery.ConclusionLack of pretreatment with P2Y12 inhibitors, absence of hematological disorders, and absence of UTI were factors that influenced the timing of hip fracture surgery within 48 hours of admission.

INTRODUCTION

Hip fracture is common in the elderly population that is progressively growing worldwide. Approximately 8.2 million hip fractures occurred in this population in 20501).

Sequelae of hip fracture, include psychological disturbances, major morbidity outcomes, and even mortality2); the one-year mortality rates have been reported to be 14% to 36%3). Therefore, geriatric hip fracture is a significant public health problem4). Treatment options for hip fracture include both conservative therapy and surgery5,6).

The complication rates are high following conservative treatment of hip fracture7). However, surgical treatment has been found to facilitate good functional outcomes, reduce morbidity8), and lower one-year mortality rates9). Delayed surgical treatment may lead to comorbidities, including bedsores, pneumonia and deep vein thrombosis, which may subsequently contribute to mortality following hip fracture10,11).

Most guidelines classify early surgery as hip fracture surgery performed within 48 hours of hospital admission12). Numerous factors associated with both patients and hospitals influence surgical timing13).

Patient-related factors, include sex, type of fracture, acute medical problems, antiplatelets, anticoagulants (warfarin, NOAC [non-vitamin K antagonist oral anticoagulants]), sodium or hemoglobin levels14,15). Hospital-related factors, include the specific day of admission in the week and surgical procedures13,16,17). However, the impact of each factor on the timing of surgery within 48 hours of admission has not been studied extensively16,18). We hypothesize that the above-mentioned factors affect the timing of surgery. Therefore, the main objective of our study is to determine factors that are conducive to hip fracture surgery within 48 hours of admission. The results of our study may help guide surgeons in their decisions and reduce postoperative complications.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

1. Patient Selection

From a hip fracture database, we retrospectively identified patients with all types of hip fracture who underwent hip surgery at Vajira Hospital between 2020 and 2023.

We obtained Navamindhradhiraj University’s Institutional Review Board (IRB) approval for the study cohort (approval No. 006/65) and clinical trial registration (TCTR20240318006) for the study. The written informed consent was waived due to the study’s retrospective design.

We excluded those patients with pathological hip fractures, unstable hemodynamics, periprosthetic fractures, open fractures, head injuries, coronavirus disease 2019 infection, unsuccessful artificial hip implantation, and those who died without undergoing surgery. Our final patient cohort included 452 patients.

2. Data Collection and Outcome Assessment

We studied the following factors that may have an impact on the timing of surgery: sex14), anticoagulant use, American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) status16,17), day of admission13,16,17), type of surgery14), acute medical disorders18), electrolyte levels18), hemoglobin levels18), and the use of antiplatelet medications or P2Y12 inhibitors14,15) (e.g., clopidogrel, prasugrel and ticagrelor). The P2Y12 inhibitor is derived from the platelet receptor name P2Y12. Platelet receptors play a crucial role in the regulation of blood clotting and are targets of antiplatelet medications. Thus, the use of platelet receptor inhibitors could delay the time to surgery due to their antiplatelet effect. For analytical purposes, acute medical disorders were classified into several clinical domains. Cardiovascular disorders included conditions, such as arrhythmias and hypertensive urgency. Pulmonary complications were defined as the presence of atelectasis or pneumonia. Renal disorders (e.g., acute kidney injury) were classified under nephrology. Cases of anemia represented hematologic abnormalities, while thyrotoxicosis and hypothyroidism represented endocrine disorders. Gastrointestinal disorders were defined by the occurrence of gastrointestinal bleeding. As part of infectious complications in patients, urinary tract infections (UTI) exhibited symptoms of upper urinary tract involvement as well as laboratory findings that indicated bacteriuria of 103 to 105 colony-forming units/mL or a positive urine culture for a pathogenic organism19). Neurological conditions were defined by the presence of delirium that occurred prior to surgical intervention. The primary endpoint was hip fracture surgery; early surgery was defined as that performed within 48 hours following hospital admission. Delayed surgery was defined as surgery performed after 48 hours of hospital admission.

3. Hip Fracture Protocol at Vajira Hospital

The patients were diagnosed with low-energy hip fractures. As part of the standard preoperative protocol, blood samples, chest X-ray, and electrocardiograms were obtained in the emergency department. All the patients were admitted to the orthopedic ward. Cardiologists or geriatric specialists and anesthesiologists were consulted for age-related or cardiovascular concerns. Cardiologists did preoperative risk assessments with the Revised Cardiac Risk Index (RCRI) and the American College of Cardiology/American Heart Association (ACC/AHA) 2014 algorithm20). The multidisciplinary team collectively took decisions to proceed with surgeries. Patients and their families were counseled about the risks of surgery. The orthopedic surgeons specializing in hip fracture treatment performed all the surgeries. Local anesthesia was considered as the first option. If patients demonstrated contraindication for local anesthesia, then general anesthesia was subsequently considered. The objective was to perform surgery as early as possible, preferably within 48 hours of hospital admission.

4. Statistical Analysis

For patients undergoing surgery within 48 hours (early surgery) and those undergoing surgery after 48 hours (delayed surgery), demographic characteristics were documented with continuous variables presented as mean±standard deviation and categorical variables reported as frequencies and percentages. We reviewed literature on factors that influence time of surgery and performed univariate regression analysis to identify those with a significant influence. Multivariate logistic regression was subsequently used to determine which of these factors were independent predictors of surgery within 48 hours. We calculated odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) to quantify the strength of these associations. We performed all statistical analyses using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows (ver. 28.0; IBM Corp.). A P-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

Table 1 summarizes the baseline characteristics of 452 patients who underwent surgery either within 48 hours (n=201) or after 48 hours (n=251). The factors of sex, diagnosis, age, body weight, height, type of surgery, or serum potassium levels did not differ in their statistical significance between the two groups. However, there were significant differences across several other variables. There was a higher frequency of treatment with P2Y12 inhibitors in the delayed surgery group (14.3%) than in the early surgery group (4.0%) (P<0.001). Warfarin use was also higher in the delayed surgery group (5.2%) than in the early surgery group (1.5%) (P=0.012). A large proportion of patients in the delayed surgery group were classified as ASA class III-IV (84.1% vs. 70.6%; P=0.001), indicating poor preoperative health. Several comorbidities were also more prevalent in the delayed surgery group, including cardiovascular disease, pulmonary disease, renal disease, hematological disorders, gastrointestinal conditions, infectious diseases, and neurological disorders, all with statistically significant P-values.

There were significant differences in the laboratory data of the two groups. The mean serum sodium level was lower in the delayed surgery group (135.40±4.51 mmol/L) than in the early surgery group (136.62±3.79 mmol/L) (P=0.002). Similarly, mean hemoglobin concentration was lower in the delayed surgery group of patients (11.01±1.92 g/dL) than in the early surgery group (11.47±1.98 g/dL) (P=0.013).

Through a multivariate logistic regression analysis, we identified several independent predictors of early surgery within 48 hours. Lack of pretreatment of P2Y12 inhibitors was associated with increased odds of early surgery (adjusted OR 3.23, 95% CI 1.41-7.41; P=0.006). Furthermore, patients who did not have hematology or UTI were more likely to undergo early surgery, with adjusted ORs of 3.53 with 95% CI 1.35-9.23 (P=0.010) and 9.59 with 95% CI 1.21-75.71 (P=0.032), respectively (Table 2).

All the reported associations reached statistical significance at a threshold of P<0.05.

DISCUSSION

In our cohort, 44.5% of patients with hip fracture had surgery within 48 hours. In patients who did not use P2Y12 inhibitors, had no hematological disorders, or had no UTI, we observed that surgery for hip fracture was most likely performed within 48 hours of admission. We noted that the lack of pretreatment of P2Y12 inhibitors, absence of hematological disorders or UTI were the most significant factors conducive to early surgery. Conversely, there is a high prevalence of these specific factors in the delayed surgery cohort.

Recent studies have shown that postoperative morbidity or mortality does not significantly increase when P2Y12 inhibitors are discontinued just 48 hours before surgery21). However, some research report conflicting results to suggest a higher risk associated with shorter discontinuation periods22). This concern is supported by the pathophysiological mechanism of P2Y12 inhibitors, which increase bleeding risk by impairing platelet aggregation and reducing clot formation23,24). For this reason, many guidelines recommend stopping the intake of these medications more than five days prior to non-emergency surgery to minimize bleeding complications25). Our findings are consistent with this rationale. Most surgeons at our institution follow the ACC/AHA guideline26) that advises discontinuing P2Y12 inhibitors 3 to 7 days before non-emergency surgery.

Regarding hematological disorders, the significantly low mean hemoglobin concentration observed in the delayed surgery group (11.01±1.92 g/dL) highlights significant anemia in this population. A significant amount of time is required to correct or stabilize anemia before surgery27), especially in elderly patients. This is due to the time required to transfuse red blood cells, typically 3 to 4 hours per unit. This is followed by a hemoglobin check at least 2 hours after completing transfusion28). This process takes additional time. Because elderly patients often have multiple comorbidities, this process requires slow transfusion rates (up to 4 hours per unit) to prevent fluid overload and cardiac complications28). Our findings are consistent with the above observation that demonstrated an absence of hematological disorders influenced the likelihood of surgery within 48 hours.

The increased prevalence of UTI in the delayed surgery group reflects the common practice of postponing surgery. However, there is controversy surrounding the impact of bacterial UTI in determining the time to surgery29).

Some studies suggest that surgery should not be delayed due to a UTI, while others report potential disadvantages30) that include a high risk of surgical site infections31). This concern is supported by evidence that there may be an increased risk of periprosthetic joint infections when preoperative UTI is left untreated30). Therefore, it is important to consider proper treatment of UTI before surgery to reduce postoperative infectious complications32). Our findings are consistent with this rationale and demonstrate that hip surgery was postponed in patients with preoperative infections, reflecting the common concern among our surgeons about periprosthetic joint infection and peri-implant-related infection risk. However, UTI has a high OR and a wide CI to suggest a small number of events. Therefore, our observation should be interpreted with caution as a possible trend rather than being considered a confirmed high-risk predictive factor.

Although some studies have reported that the day of hospital admission, particularly weekend admissions, is an independent factor contributing to delays in time to surgery33), recent research suggests otherwise34,35). Specifically, weekend admissions were not associated with delayed time-to-surgery in hospitals with well-managed infrastructure and staffing35). This indicates that institutional capacity, rather than the day of admission, plays a critical role. Irrespective of weekday or weekend admissions, hospitals with adequate surgical teams and surgical room availability are less likely to face delays35). Likewise, in our institution, we did not observe any significant difference in the likelihood of early surgery between weekday and weekend admissions. This may be explained by the fact that our center is a tertiary hospital with the capacity to perform hip fracture surgery every day.

There are several limitations to our study. First, it was a retrospective review of data from hip fracture databases. Lack of some important data, such as the clinical outcomes, morbidity rates, and mortality rates between the early and delayed surgery groups may fail to underscore the advantages of early surgery. However, previous studies of early surgery for hip fracture have already reported on these aspects of early surgery36,37). Second, our single-center study was conducted at a tertiary care institution that may have introduced selection bias. Therefore, there may be limits in applying our findings to other hospitals; for example, some non-tertiary centers may have a different fast-track policy for hip fracture surgery. Third, our study was limited in the number of patients (n=452) and in the number of years (n=3 years). This limited number reflects the overall incidence of hip fracture in our study38).

On the basis of the results of our study, the authors found that the absence of pretreatment with P2Y12 inhibitors, absence of hematological disorders as well as UTI were associated with a high likelihood of undergoing hip fracture surgery in a timely manner. However, our study has been limited in balancing the risks of delayed surgery against those of surgery for patients on antiplatelet therapy or with mild infections. We need to perform further research to explore this issue in greater detail.

CONCLUSION

Treatment with P2Y12 inhibitors, hematological disorders, UTI significantly affect the timing of hip fracture surgery. When these contributing factors are absent in patients, they are more likely to undergo hip fracture surgery within 48 hours. Clinicians should be aware that surgeries may be delayed with the intake of P2Y12 inhibitors, presence of hematologic disorders, or presence of UTI. Delay in surgery due to these factors could subsequently lead to increased morbidities.

Therefore, it is pivotal to conduct further research to study the factors that maintain a balance between delayed surgery and early surgery.

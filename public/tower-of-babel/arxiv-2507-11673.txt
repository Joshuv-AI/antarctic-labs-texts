WZW Partition Functions from Supersymmetric Localization
Boan Zhao

Abstract
We prove a conjecture of Murthy and Witten MurthyWitten which expresses modular invariant WZW partition functions as lattice sums. We show that when the sum is over the root lattice of S ​ U ​ ( N ) SU(N) , the result correctly reproduces the diagonal modular invariants of S ​ U ​ ( N ) k SU(N)_{k} whenever N > 2 N>2 and N + k N+k is even. Beyond these cases, the proposition is not always true and we provide a counterexample when k ≫ N > 2 k\gg N>2 and k + N k+N is odd. This proof generalizes the proof by Murthy and Witten for the S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) case. We then move onto nondiagonal modular invariants and show that all S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) k SU(2)_{k} nondiagonal invariants (the D D and the E E -series) can be obtained using these lattice sums. On the last day of the production of this work, the author discovered a significant overlap with Warner1989 . The main novelties here are (1) the proof of the equivalence of the Murthy-Witten lattice sum and the one considered by Warner (2) The counterexample when N + k N+k is odd and N > 2 N>2 .

\displaystyle\mu=(\mu_{1},...,\mu_{N})\quad\sum_{i}\mu_{i}=0\quad\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}\in\mathbb{Z},\forall i,j

(50)

Since the weight lattice lies in the dual of the Cartan subalgebra of
SU(N)
, the notation
\mu
really means
\mu_{1}z_{1}+\mu_{2}z_{2}+...+\mu_{N}z_{N}
where
z_{i}
are the diagonal entries of the traceless diagonal matrices inside the Cartan subalgebra. The inner product introduced later
(A,B)=\tr(AB)
permanently identify the Cartan subalgebra and its dual. Therefore, we think of the weight lattice as a lattice inside the Cartan subalgebra.

2.

\Lambda_{R}
is the root lattice of
SU(N)
and consists of vectors of the form:

\displaystyle r=(r_{1},...,r_{N})\quad\sum_{i}r_{i}=0\quad r_{i}\in\mathbb{Z},\forall i

(51)

3.

\Lambda
denotes a rank
N-1
real lattice inside the complexified Cartan subalgebra of
SU(N)
. The reality condition means all of its elements are real traceless matrices. Throughout this text, we identify diagonal matrices with their diagonal values. Different choices of
\Lambda
lead to different modular invariant partition functions in the main text.

4.

SU(N)
fugacities are of the form:

\displaystyle x=(x_{1},...,x_{N})\quad\prod_{i}x_{i}=1

(52)

In the main text we have two sets of fugacities
x_{i}
and
y_{i}
, they are related to complex vectors
\alpha_{L},\alpha_{R}
via:

\displaystyle x_{i}=\exp(2\pi i \alpha_R^i)\quad y_{i}=\exp(-2\pi i \alpha_L^i)

(53)

5.

The notation
x^{\mu}
means

\displaystyle x_{1}^{\mu_{1}}x_{2}^{\mu_{2}}...x_{N}^{\mu_{N}}

(54)

6.

All inner products are the usual Euclidean inner product

\displaystyle(\mu,\nu)=\mu_{1}\nu_{1}+\mu_{2}\nu_{2}+...+\mu_{N}\nu_{N}

(55)

In other words, we use the inner product
(A,B)=\tr(AB)
on the complexified Cartan subalgebra of
SU(N)
.
In particular, the weight lattice is dual to the root lattice in the sense that the inner product of any weight and any root is an integer. We often supress the inner product symbol and write
\mu\nu=(\mu,\nu)
when
\mu,\nu
are vectors.

7.

W
always stands for the Weyl group isomorphic to
S_{N}
(except in the subscript of
\Lambda_{W}
).
|W|=N!
is the size of the Weyl group. To keep the notations simpler, we will NOT discuss affine Weyl groups. If
w\in W
is an element of the Weyl group,
\det(w)
is the determinant of
w
as an orthogonal linear map on the Cartan subalgebra of
SU(N)
. It equals
\text{sgn}(w)
in
MurthyWitten

w(\mu)
for the action of
w
on
\mu
. The Weyl group of
SU(N)
is the permutation group on
N
elements and permutes the components of
\mu
. We do not introduce special notations for the shifted Weyl action.

8.

The summation

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu\in\text{Integrable}}

(56)

is over the set of integrable

weights. The integrability condition means

\displaystyle\nu_{1}\geq\nu_{2}...\geq\nu_{N}\quad\sum_{i}\nu_{i}=0\quad\nu_{i}-\nu_{j}\in\mathbb{Z},\forall i,j\quad\nu_{1}-\nu_{N}\leq k

9.

The Weyl vector for

\displaystyle\rho=(\frac{N-1}{2},\frac{N-3}{2},...,-\frac{N-1}{2})

(58)

The gap between any two adjacent elements of

10.

The theta functions are defined as:

\displaystyle\theta_{\mu}^{m}(q,x)=\sum_{r\in\Lambda_{R}}x^{\mu+mr}q^{m(r+\mu/m)^{2}/2}

(59)

where

is an integer,

\displaystyle m=5,\mu=(-\frac{2}{3},\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3}),r=(1,-1,0)\Rightarrow

\displaystyle x^{\mu+mr}q^{(mr+\mu)^{2}/(2m)}=x_{1}^{13/3}x_{2}^{-14/3}x_{3}^{1/3}q^{((13/3)^{2}+(14/3)^{2}+(1/3)^{2})/10}

Appendix B
Shifted Weyl Action on
\Lambda_{W}/(k+N)\Lambda_{R}

In this section, we prove the two claims
2.1
on the shifted Weyl action on
\Lambda_{W}/(k+N)\Lambda_{R}
. Let
\mu
be an element in the weight lattice. We can subtract
\mu
by an element inside
(k+N)\Lambda_{R}
until the maximal difference between the components of
\mu
(always nonnegative by definition) is at most
k+N
: if the maximal difference is achieved at
\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}>N+k
. We can subtract
\mu_{i}
by
N+k
and add to
\mu_{j}
by
N+k
. The maximal difference decreases after this step. So this algorithm terminates. Now
there are three possibilities:

1.

The maximum difference between components of
\mu
is exactly
k+N
. In this case, swapping the two components with the maximal difference is a Weyl transformation of determinant
-1
that fixes
\mu
up to
(k+N)\Lambda_{R}
.

2.

The maximum difference between the components of
\mu
is strictly less than
k+N
and the components are not distinct. Then we can swap the nondistinct elements.

3.

The maximum difference between the components is strictly less than
k+N
and the elements are distinct. Then we use a Weyl transformation to map it to the fundamental chamber and subtract
\rho
from it to obtain an integrable weight.

To see that integrable weights have trivial stabilizers under the shifted Weyl action, assume that

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}+\rho_{i}-w(\lambda+\rho)_{i}=(k+N)r_{i}\quad r_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{N}r_{i}=0\quad w\neq 1

(61)

where
\lambda
is integrable. Since
\lambda+\rho
have pairwise distinct components,
r
must be nonzero.
A nonzero element of the root lattice must have both positive and negative components:
r_{i}>0,r_{j}<0
for some
i\neq j
. The maximum difference between components of
\lambda+\rho
is at most
k+N-1
. Hence, the difference between any two components of
\lambda+\rho-w(\lambda+\rho)
is at most
2(k+N-1)
which contradicts
(k+N)(r_{i}-r_{j})\geq 2(k+N)
.

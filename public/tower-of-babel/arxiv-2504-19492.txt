1.
Introduction

The enquiry into
{\rm K}_{1}
-stability of rings is a natural consequence of the results that have been obtained for its well known counterpart
{\rm K}_{0}
.
To be precise, when
A=k[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}],
where
k
is a field, the affirmative to Serre’s problem by Quillen-Suslin, gave the best non-stable version for
{\rm K}_{0}(A)
. This led to questions on the possibility of the best stable and non-stable version for the classical groups
{\rm K}_{1}(A)
and
{\rm K}_{1}{\rm Sp}(A)
. In
[
27
]
and
[
20
]
, Suslin and Kopeiko established these versions by giving stabilization theorems for
{\rm K}_{1}(R[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m},Y_{1}^{\pm{1}},\ldots,Y_{n}^{\pm{1}}]),
where
R
is a Noetherian ring.

The subsequent development of
K
-theoretic techniques leading upto the proof of Serre’s problem inspired similar developments for monoid algebras, the starting point being Anderson’s conjecture
[
1
]
(proved by J. Gubeladze in
[
13
]
). A setback to the expectation of
{\rm K}_{1}
-analogues for monoid algebras, was due to an example by Srinivas
[
26
]
. Building on Srinivas’s work, Gubeladze
[
16
]
showed that for any regular ring
R,
the expected
{\rm K}_{1}
analogue for the monoid algebra
R[U,UV,UV^{2}]
cannot be attained. This example also works to show the same for the symplectic group
{\rm Sp}(R[U,UV,UV^{2}])
.

Despite the counterexamples, Gubeladze
[
14
]
found a class of monoids algebras that satisfy the expected
{\rm K}_{1}
-analogues. It was shown, when
R
is a Euclidean domain and
L
a
c
-divisible algebra (
c>1
) (for definition see Section
2
), one gets
{\mbox{SL}}_{n}(R[L])={\mbox{E}}_{n}(R[L])
for
n\geq 3
. These
c
-divisible monoids arise naturally in tandem with any finite abelian group. Given any finite abelian group
G,
one can show there exists a
c
-divisible monoid
L
(
c>1
) such that
G\simeq Cl(L),
where
Cl(L)
denotes the divisor class group of
L
(see
[
10
]
for definition). Motivated by these results, we identify a class of rings
\mathcal{R}_{t}
(see Section
3
) in the symplectic setup and prove:

Theorem 1
.

Let
R\in\mathcal{R}_{k}
and
L
be a c-divisible monoid, where
c>1
and
k\geq 1
. Further, if we have
{\rm Sp}_{2k}(R[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m},Y_{1}^{\pm{1}},\ldots,Y_{n}^{\pm{1}}])={\rm ESp}_{2k}R[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m},Y_{1}^{\pm{1}},\ldots,Y_{n}^{\pm{1}}],
then

{\rm ESp}_{2k}(R[L])={\rm Sp}_{2k}(R[L]).

We show that any regular local ring of dimension
d
belongs to
\mathcal{R}_{k},
for all
k\geq d+2
. As a consequence we are able to prove that:

Theorem 2
.

Let
R
be a regular ring of dimension d with
{\rm K}_{1}{\rm Sp}(R)=0
and
L
be a
c
-divisible monoid. Then for any symplectic module
P
of rank
2k
with
k\geq\max\{2,d+1\},

{\rm Sp}(P\perp R[L]^{2})={\Etrans}_{\rm Sp}(P\perp R[L]^{2}).

This result aligns with the best possible answer for the general case in the setup of monoid algebras. When
d\leq 1
or
R
is geometrically regular ring containing a field, further improvements can be made. When
M
is free monoid,
i.e.,R[M]\simeq R[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m},Y_{1}^{\pm{1}},\ldots,Y_{n}^{\pm{1}}],
the result for unstable
{\rm K}_{1}(R[M])
is due to Suslin (
cf.

[
27
]
, Corollary 7.10). Using the techniques of this paper, it can also be shown that for a regular ring
R
with trivial
{\rm K}_{1},
we gain the equality
{\mbox{SL}}_{n}(R[L])={\mbox{E}}_{n}(R[L])
for
n\geq\max\{3,d+2\},
thus extending the work started in
[
14
]
.

Another sought after
{\rm K}_{1}
-analogue is courtesy of Grothendieck’s work on
{\rm K}_{0}
-homotopy invariance. When
R
is regular, Grothendieck proved that
{\rm K}_{0}(R)\simeq{\rm K}_{0}(R[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m},Y_{1}^{\pm{1}},\ldots,Y_{n}^{\pm{1}}])
. In
[
3
]
, Bass-Heller-Swan established the
{\rm K}_{1}
-analogue for polynomial rings. Gubeladze found an element in
{\rm K}_{1}(R[U,UV,UV^{2}])\smallsetminus{\rm K}_{1}(R)
for any regular ring
R
. He observed that this failure of
{\rm K}_{1}(R[M])
-analogue was a consequence of the (absence of) excision property for
{\rm K}_{1}(R[M]),
where
M
is a monoid. When
R
is regular and
M
an affine simplicial monoid, it was observed
[
16
]
that the only monoid algebras satisfying
{\rm K}_{1}(R)\simeq{\rm K}_{1}(R[M])
are polynomial algebras (
i.e.,
when
M
is free). Fortunately, when
L
is a
c
-divisible monoid, Gubeladze
[
15
]
proved the presence of excision in
K
-theory of
R[L]
. In (
[
14
]
, Theorem 2.24) the expected
{\rm K}_{1}(R[L])
-invariance was established. We establish
{\rm K}_{1}{\rm Sp}(R[L])
-invariance when
L
is
c
-divisible:

Theorem 3
.

Let R be a regular ring and L a
c
-divisible monoid, where
c>1
. Then

{\rm K}_{1}{\rm Sp}(R[L])\simeq{\rm K}_{1}{\rm Sp}(R).

A question on the existence of a non-free, affine monoid which is
{\rm K}_{1}
-invariant for all regular rings, was raised in
[
16
]
. To answer this question, Krishna-Sarwar
[
21
]
, showed that if
R
is a regular ring containing
\mathbb{Q},
then
{\rm K}_{1}(R)\simeq{\rm K}_{1}(R[X,Y,ZX,ZY]).
This work was extended by Schappi (
[
25
]
, Theorem 1.2), answering this question in totality. There is a good chance that the same deductions can be made for
{\rm K}_{1}{\rm Sp}
-invariance for the monoid algebra
R[X,Y,ZX,ZY]
. Further, the techniques developed here can be utilized to collect the
{\rm K}_{2}{\rm Sp}
-invariance result of algebra generated by simplicial
c
-divisible monoids as in (
[
14
]
, Theorem 3.1 and 3.4). It would also be interesting to see extension of these results for other suitable classical groups and their generalizations. We leave it to the reader to verify that the results presented here also come through for Laurent polynomial extension of these algebras as well,
i.e.
, for conditions provided in the hypothesis,
{\rm Sp}_{2k}(R[L][X_{1},\ldots,X_{m},Y_{1}^{\pm{1}},\ldots,Y_{n}^{\pm{1}}])
coincides with its elementary symplectic subgroup.

2.
Prologue: Geometry of polarized monoids

All our rings will be commutative Noetherian with unity and monoids will be commutative cancellative and torsion-free. Let
R
be a ring and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Define the matrices over
R
by:

\psi_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}\text{ and }\psi_{n}=\psi_{n-1}\perp\psi_{1}

Define
\widetilde{u}=u^{T}\psi_{n}
for any
u\in R^{2n}
. The symplectic group
{\rm Sp}_{2n}(R)
over a ring
R,
is given by:

{\rm Sp}_{2n}(R)=\{\alpha\in{\mbox{GL}}_{2n}(R)\mid\alpha^{T}\psi_{n}\alpha=\psi_{n}\}

Let
e_{i}
’s denote the standard basis vectors with
1
in the
i
’th position and
0
elsewhere, and
e_{ij}
’s the matrix
e_{i}e_{j}^{T}
. Let
{\rm Id}_{2n}
denote the identity matrix of size
2n
and
\sigma
be the product of transpositions,

\sigma=(1,2)(3,4)\ldots(2n-1,2n).

For
\lambda\in R
and
1\leq i<j\leq k=2n
with
\sigma(i)\neq j,
one defines

se_{ij}(\lambda)={\rm Id}_{2n}+\lambda e_{ij}-\lambda(-1)^{i+j}e_{\sigma(j)\sigma(i)}

For values of
n,i,j,\lambda
as indicated above, the elementary symplectic group
{\rm ESp}_{2n}(R),
is the subgroup of
{\rm Sp}_{2n}(R)
generated by
e_{i(\sigma(i))}(\lambda)
’s and
se_{ij}(\lambda)
’s.

Let
(M,+)
be a commutative monoid. Then
M
is cancellative, if for
x,y,z\in M,

x+y=x+z,
then
y=z,
and torsion free if for some
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},

nx=0,
then
x=0
. The group completion of
M
is denoted by
gp(M)
. The rank of a monoid
M,
denoted by
\rank(M),
is defined as the dimension of the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}gp(M)
. A monoid is positive if its group of units is trivial. The monoid
M
is normal, if for
x\in gp(M)
and
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},

nx\in M,
then
x\in M
. Let
c\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
. The monoid
M
is called c-divisible, if for all
m\in M
there exists
n\in M,
such that
cn=m
.

Since
M
is cancellative and torsion-free, therefore
M
can be embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{r},
where
r=\rank(M)
. Henceforth, we may assume without loss of generality that
M\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{r},
where
0
coincides with the origin. For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{r},
we denote by
\phi(x),
the intersection of the unit sphere
S^{r-1}
(in
\mathbb{R}^{r}
) with the line joining the origin to
x
. By
\phi(M)
we denote the smallest subset in
S^{r-1}
containing the set
\{\phi(m)\mid m\in M\smallsetminus\{0\}\}
such that if
x,y
are two non-antipodal points in
\phi(M),
then the shortest line joining
x
to
y
on
S^{r-1}
is contained in
\phi(M)
. Here
\phi(M)
is the convex hull of the set
\{\phi(m)\mid m\in M\smallsetminus\{0\}\}
in
S^{r-1},
written as:

\phi(M)=\conv(\{\phi(m)\mid m\in M\smallsetminus\{0\}\}).

Given a convex (on
S^{r-1}
) subset
X\subseteq\phi(M),
a new submonoid of
M
can be defined by:

M(X)=\{m\in M\smallsetminus\{0\}\mid\phi(m)\in X\}\cup\{0\}.

Further denote,
{\intt}(M):=M(\intt(\phi(M)))
and
\mbox{$M_{*}$}:=\intt(M)\cup\{0\}
. A point
P\in S^{r-1}
is called rational, if there exists a rational point
x\in R^{r}
such that
\phi(x)=P
. A convex subset
X
of
S^{r-1}
is defined to be rational, if it is the convex hull of rational points. A convex set
X
(in
S^{r-1}
) is open, if there exists a subspace
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{r}
such that
X
is open in
H\cap S^{r-1}
.

Remark 2.1
.

(
[
14
]
, §I) Every open convex
(d
-
1)
-dimensional subset in
S^{d-1}
is rational and can be represented as union of
(d
-
1)
dimensional rational closed polytopes. Thus if
M
is a
c
-divisible monoid with
\phi(M)
open, then
M
can be seen as direct limit of
c
-divisible monoids
M_{i}
’s with
\phi(M_{i})
as closed polytope.

The following definition and notations are as per
[
14
]
:

Definition 2.2
.

A triple
(P,\Gamma,M)
is called a polarized monoid if

(i)

M
is an affine normal monoid,
P
is a rational point and
\Gamma
a closed convex rational polytope such that
P
is not in the affine space generated by any arbitrary proper face of
\Gamma
.

(ii)

\dim(\Gamma)=\dim(\phi(M))
and
\phi(M)=\conv(P,\Gamma)
.

(iii)

For any
(\dim(\Gamma)-1)
-dimensional face
\gamma
of
\Gamma,
the
M
-submonoid
M_{\gamma},
defined as

M_{\gamma}:=M(\conv(P,\gamma)),
is generated by
M({P})\cup M(\gamma).

For the polarized monoid
(P,\Gamma,M),
we fix the following notation:

(i)

P_{-}
is the point on
S^{r-1}
diametrically opposite to
P
.

(ii)

\mathfrak{m}
is a maximal ideal of
R[M(\Gamma)]
containing
M(\Gamma)\setminus\{0\}
.

(iii)

\mathcal{N}
is the
M
-submonoid, of rank 1, given by
M(\{P\})
and further
\mathcal{N}=\langle t\rangle
.

(iv)

M_{-}
denotes the submonoid of
K(M)
generated by
M(\Gamma)\cup\{-m\mid m\in M(\{P\})\}
.

Note that here
(P_{-},\Gamma,M_{-})
also forms a polarized triple. For simplicity sake, instead of saying
(P,\Gamma,M)
forms a polarized triple, we will say
M
is a polarized monoid. We fix the notations,

A=R[M(\Gamma)]_{\mathfrak{m}},B=R[M]_{\mathfrak{m}},B_{-}=R[M_{-}]_{\mathfrak{m}}\text{ and }C=R[\mathcal{N}^{-1}M]_{\mathfrak{m}}.

It can be shown that
B\cap B_{-}=A
(Step 5 of Proposition 2.14 in
[
14
]
).

3.
Equality of Symplectic and Elementary symplectic groups

It is known that if
R
is a local ring or a euclidean domain, then
{\rm Sp}_{2k}(R)={\rm ESp}_{2k}(R)
for all
k\geq 1
. Kopeiko, in
[
20
]
, showed that
R
is a field and
A=R[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}],
then for
k\geq 2,
the symplectic group
{\rm Sp}_{2k}(A)={\rm ESp}_{2k}(A)
. When
k=1,
this equality doesn’t hold due to
[
11
]
. This equality is what we refer to as the unstable
{\rm K}_{1}{\rm Sp}
. Clearly, unstable
{\rm K}_{1}{\rm Sp}
implies the results for stable
{\rm K}_{1}{\rm Sp},

i.e.,{\rm Sp}(A)={\rm ESp(A)}
. The primary aim of this section is to determine base rings
R
such that stable and unstable
{\rm K}_{1}{\rm Sp}
results holds monoid algebra generated by
c
-divisible monoids
L
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
R
be a ring. Let
R(X)
denote the localization of the ring
R[X]
with respect to all polynomials that have invertible leading coefficients in
R[X]
. Define
\mathcal{R}_{k}
to be the class of rings satisfying the following:
R\in\mathcal{R}_{k},
if

(i)

{\rm ESp}_{2k}(R)={\rm Sp}_{2k}(R)
and

(ii)

if there exist finite rings
R=R_{1},R_{2}\ldots,R_{t}=R^{\prime}
for
t>1,
such that

(a)

R_{2}=R(X)
or
R_{2}=R_{\mathfrak{m}}
for some
\mathfrak{m}\in\Max(R)
,

(b)

for
2<i\leq t
we have

R_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}R_{i-1}(X)&\text{if }R_{i-1}={(R_{i-2})}_{\mathfrak{m}_{i-2}},\\
{(R_{i-1})}_{\mathfrak{m}_{i-1}}&{\rm else}.\\
\end{array}\right.,

where
\mathfrak{m}_{j}\in\Max(R_{j})
for
1\leq j\leq t
.

Then
{\rm ESp}_{2k}(R^{\prime})={\rm Sp}_{2k}(R^{\prime})
.

Let
R\in\mathcal{R}_{t}
, for some
t
such that
2t\geq\dim(R).
By observing that
\dim(R_{i})\leq\dim(R)
for
R_{i}
’s as in
(ii)
, the surjective part of the Stabilization Theorem in
[
29
]
gives us
R\in\mathcal{R}_{k}
for all
k\geq t
.

Example 3.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a regular local ring of dimension
\leq 1
. We claim that
R\in\mathcal{R}_{k}
for all
k\geq 1
. From (
[
22
]
, Chapter IV, Proposition 1.4), one may write
R(X)=R^{\prime}_{Y}
, where
R^{\prime}=R[Y]_{(\mathfrak{m},Y)}
and
Y=X^{-1}\in R(X)
. Using (
[
22
]
, Chapter IV, Corollary 6.2), we get
R(X)
is a special PID and therefore

{\rm Sp}_{2}(R(X))={\mbox{SL}}_{2}(R(X))={\mbox{E}}_{2}(R(X))={\mbox{ESp}}_{2}(R(X)).

By the above observation, we get
R\in\mathcal{R}_{k}
for all
k\geq 1
. By (
[
12
]
), Theorem 1.8), (
[
20
]
, Lemma 4.1) and induction on
n,
we can show that for
k\geq 2
and
m,n\geq 0,
we have

{\rm Sp}_{2k}(R[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m},Y_{1}^{\pm{1}},\ldots,Y_{n}^{\pm{1}}])={{\rm ESp}_{2k}}(R[X_{1},\ldots,X_{,},Y_{1}^{\pm{1}},\ldots,Y_{n}^{\pm{1}}]).

Due to
[
11
]
,
k=2
is the best bound we achieve in the above equation. Since it was shown that:

{\rm Sp}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}])\neq{{\rm ESp}_{2}}({\mathbb{Z}}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}]).

The following theorem can be seen as the symplectic analogue of Theorem 1, 2 and 6 of
[
3
]
. The proof comes through by using the localization exact sequence or simply following the workings of the proofs in the aforementioned theorems on replacing
{\mbox{GL}}_{n}
and
{\mbox{E}}_{n}
by
{\rm Sp}_{2n}
and
\mbox{ESp}_{2n},
respectively.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
R
be a regular ring and
X
an indeterminate. Then

(
i
)

{\rm K}_{0}{\rm Sp}(R)\simeq{\rm K}_{0}Sp(R[X])\simeq{\rm K}_{0}{\rm Sp}(R[X,X^{-1}])
,

(
ii
)

{\rm K}_{1}{\rm Sp}(R[X])\simeq{\rm K}_{1}{\rm Sp}(R)
,

(
iii
)

{\rm K}_{1}{\rm Sp}(R[X,X^{-1}])\simeq{\rm K}_{1}{\rm Sp}(R)
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
R
be a regular ring of dimension
d
with
{\rm K}_{1}{\rm Sp}(R)=0
. For
k\geq d+2
we have

{\rm ESp}_{2k}(R[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m},Y_{1}^{\pm{1}},\ldots,Y_{n}^{\pm{1}}])={\rm Sp}_{2k}(R[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m},Y_{1}^{\pm{1}},\ldots,Y_{n}^{\pm{1}}]).

Example 3.4
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a regular local ring of dimension
d
. We claim that
R\in\mathcal{R}_{k}
for
k\geq d+2
. From (
[
22
]
, Chapter IV, Proposition 1.4), one may write
R(X)=R^{\prime}_{Y}
, where
R^{\prime}=R[Y]_{(\mathfrak{m},Y)}
and
Y=X^{-1}\in R(X)
. Since
{\rm K}_{0}Sp
and
{\rm K}_{1}{\rm Sp}
are trivial for local rings, on using the localization exact sequence one gets

0\simeq{\rm K}_{1}{\rm Sp}(R^{\prime})\rightarrow{\rm K}_{1}{\rm Sp}(R^{\prime}_{Y})\simeq{\rm K}_{1}{\rm Sp}(R(X))\xrightarrow{\delta}{\rm K}_{0}Sp(R^{\prime}/YR^{\prime})\simeq{\rm K}_{0}{\rm Sp}(R)\rightarrow{\rm K}_{0}{\rm Sp}(R^{\prime}),

we may conclude
{\rm K}_{1}{\rm Sp}(R(X))=0
. As
\dim(R(X))=\dim(R)=d,
by on using injective part of the Stabilization theorem in
[
29
]
we get
{\rm ESp}_{2k}(R(X))={\rm Sp}_{2k}(R(X)),
for
k\geq d+2
. Compare with Example
3.1
to note that for
d=1
we have a much improved result.

Example 3.5
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a geometrically regular ring containing a field. We may assume
d>1
. Then
{\rm K}_{1}{\rm Sp}(R(X))=0
. By (
[
6
]
, Theorem 2), it follows
{\rm ESp}_{2k}(R(X))={\rm Sp}_{2k}(R(X))
for
2k\geq\max\{3,d+1\}
. Thus
R\in\mathcal{R}_{\lceil k/2\rceil}
. If
R
is also local, by (
[
6
]
, Remark 3.16),
{\rm Sp}_{2k}(R[X])={\rm ESp}_{2k}(R[X])
for
k\geq 2
. By inducting on
m+n
and using (
[
20
]
, Lemma 4.1), for
k\geq 2
we arrive at:

{\rm Sp}_{2k}(R[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m},Y_{1}^{\pm{1}},\ldots,Y_{n}^{\pm{1}}])={{\rm ESp}_{2k}}(R[X_{1},\ldots,X_{,},Y_{1}^{\pm{1}},\ldots,Y_{n}^{\pm{1}}]).

This is a significant reduction compared to Example
3.4
.

We recall the following notations from Section
2
:

A=R[M(\Gamma)]_{\mathfrak{m}},B=R[M]_{\mathfrak{m}},B_{-}=R[M_{-}]_{\mathfrak{m}},C=R[\mathcal{N}^{-1}M]_{\mathfrak{m}}\text{ and }\mathcal{N}=\langle t\rangle.

We now begin our work on the understanding the structure of
{\rm ESp}_{2n}(C)
:

Definition 3.6
.

For each
n\geq 1,
we can define:

\mathbb{M}^{(n)}=\{I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,2n\}\mid\forall~i\in I,\text{either }i\in I,\sigma(i)\notin I\text{ or }i\notin I,\sigma(i)\in I\}.

Corresponding to
I\in\mathbb{M}^{(n)},
a new diagonal matrix can be defined as:

\delta_{I}=\diag(d_{1},\ldots,d_{2n}),

where

d_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}t&i\in I,\\
1&{\rm else}.\\
\end{array}\right.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let R be a ring and
M
be a polarized monoid. Let
n\geq 2
and
\alpha\in{\rm ESp}_{2n}(B)\cap{\rm Sp}_{2n}(B,tB)
. For
I\in\mathbb{M}^{(n)},

\delta_{I}\alpha{\delta_{I}}^{-1}\text{ and }{~\delta_{I}}^{-1}\alpha{\delta_{I}}\in{\rm ESp}_{2n}(B).

Proof.

The proof will focus on showing
{\delta_{I}}\alpha{\delta_{I}}^{-1}\in\mbox{ESp}_{2n}(B)
(the other part will follow using
t{\delta_{I}}^{-1}=\delta_{\sigma(I)},
where
\sigma(I)=I^{c}\cap\{1,\ldots,2n\}\in\mathbb{M}^{(n)}
).
We use the property of
M
being polarized to write the decomposition:

\alpha=\prod\limits_{k=1}^{m}se_{{i_{k}}{j_{k}}}(a_{k}+tf_{k}),

where
a_{k}\in A
and
f_{k}\in B
. For
1\leq p\leq m,
define
\gamma_{p}=\prod\limits_{k=1}^{p}se_{{i_{k}}{j_{k}}}(a_{k})\in\mbox{ESp}_{2n}(A).

This helps us rewrite
\alpha
as

\alpha=\Bigg(\prod\limits_{k=1}^{m}\gamma_{k}se_{{i_{k}}{j_{k}}}(tf_{k})\gamma_{k}^{-1}\Bigg)\gamma_{m}

d,

{\rm K}_{1}{\rm Sp}(R)=0
and
L
be a c-divisible monoid, where
c>1
. Let
P
be a symplectic module of rank
2k
. Then
{\Etrans}(P\perp R[L]^{2})={\rm Sp}(P\perp R[L]^{2})
, if

(i)

d\leq 1,

{\rm ESp}_{4}(R)={\rm Sp}_{4}(R)
and
k\geq 1
.

(ii)

R is a geometrically regular ring containing a field and
2k\geq\max\{1,d-1\}
.

5.
Auxiliary results

In the hypothesis of Theorem
1
, we had to enforce the condition

{\rm ESp}_{2k}(R[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m},Y_{1}^{\pm{1}},\ldots,Y_{n}^{\pm{1}}])={\rm Sp}_{2k}(R[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m},Y_{1}^{\pm{1}},\ldots,Y_{n}^{\pm{1}}])

for
R\in\mathcal{R}_{k}
. In order to remedy this, we define a much general class of ring
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{n},
which automatically satisfies this requirement. It can be shown that the conclusion of Theorem
1
also holds for rings in
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{n}
. We say
R\in\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{n},
if

(i)

{\rm ESp}_{2n}(R)={\rm Sp}_{2n}(R)
and

(ii)

If there exist finite rings
R=R_{1},R_{2}\ldots,R_{k}=R^{\prime}
for
k>1,
such that for
1<i\leq k
,

R_{i}
is either
R_{i-1}(X)
or
{(R_{i-1})}_{\mathfrak{m}}
where
\mathfrak{m}\in\Max(R_{i-1})

Then
{\rm ESp}_{2n}(R^{\prime})={\rm Sp}_{2n}(R^{\prime})
.

Then we have the following interesting result:

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
R\in\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{n}
and
s,t\geq 0
. Then

{\rm Sp}_{2n}(R[X_{1},\ldots,X_{t},Y_{1}^{\pm{1}},\ldots,Y_{s}^{\pm{1}}])={{\rm ESp}_{2n}}(R[X_{1},\ldots,X_{t},Y_{1}^{\pm{1}},\ldots,Y_{s}^{\pm{1}}]).

Proof.

Let
s=0
. Then we need to show the equality

{\rm Sp}_{2n}(R[X_{1},\ldots,X_{t}])={{\rm ESp}_{2n}}(R[X_{1},\ldots,X_{t}]).

Let
\beta\in{\rm Sp}_{2n}(R[X_{1},\ldots,X_{t}])
and
R^{\prime}=R(X_{1},\ldots,X_{t-1})
. Since
R^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}_{n},

\beta\in\mbox{ESp}_{2n}(R^{\prime}(X_{t}))\cap{\rm Sp}_{2n}(R^{\prime}[X_{t}])
. There exists a monic polynomial
f\in R^{\prime}[X_{t}],
such that
\beta_{f}\in\mbox{ESp}_{2n}(R^{\prime}[X_{t}]_{f})
. By Theorem 3.10 of
[
20
]
,
\beta\in\mbox{ESp}_{2n}(R^{\prime}[X_{t}])
.

Let
R^{\prime\prime}=R[X_{1},\ldots,X_{t},Y_{1}^{\pm{1}},\ldots,Y_{s-1}^{\pm{1}}]
and
S_{1}(~S_{2},\text{ resp.})
be the multiplicative subset of
R^{\prime\prime}[Y_{s},Y_{s}^{-1}]
, consisting of all monics in
Y_{s}(~Y_{s}^{-1},\text{ resp.})
. For the case of Laurent polynomial rings, observe that for
\alpha\in{\rm Sp}_{2n}(R[X_{1},\ldots,X_{t},Y_{1}^{\pm{1}},\ldots,Y_{s}^{\pm{1}}])
we have

\alpha\in{\rm Sp}_{2n}(R(Y_{s})[X_{1},\ldots,X_{t},Y_{1}^{\pm{1}},\ldots,Y_{s-1}^{\pm{1}}].

Since
R(Y_{s})\in\mathcal{R}_{n},
thus by induction on
s+t
we may conclude

\alpha\in{\rm ESp}_{2n}(R(Y_{s})[X_{1},\ldots,X_{t},Y_{1}^{\pm{1}},\ldots,Y_{s-1}^{\pm{1}}])\subseteq{\rm ESp}_{2n}(S_{1}^{-1}R^{\prime\prime}[Y_{s},Y_{s}^{-1}]).

Similarly we may show
\alpha\in{\rm ESp}_{2n}(R(Y_{s}^{-1})[X_{1},\ldots,X_{t},Y_{1}^{\pm{1}},\ldots,Y_{s-1}^{\pm{1}}])\subseteq{\rm ESp}_{2n}(S_{2}^{-1}R^{\prime\prime}[Y_{s},Y_{s}^{-1}]).

By Lemma 4.1 of
[
20
]
, we may conclude and say
\alpha\in{\rm ESp}_{2n}(R[X_{1},\ldots,X_{t},Y_{1}^{\pm{1}},\ldots,Y_{s-1}^{\pm{1}}][Y_{s},Y_{s}^{-1}]).

∎

Using the above proposition, the reader may show that Theorem
1
holds for rings in
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{n}
as well.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
R\in\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{n}
for some
n
and
L
be a c-divisible monoid, where
c>1
. Then

{\rm ESp}_{2n}(R[L])={\rm Sp}_{2n}(R[L]).

We may similarly obtain the below from Theorem
5.2
:

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
R\in\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{t}
and
L
be a
c
-divisible monoid. Let
P
be a symplectic
R[L]
-module extended from
R
. If
rank(P)=2t-2,
then

{\rm Sp}(P\perp R[L]^{2})={\Etrans}_{\rm Sp}(P\perp R[L]^{2}).

6.
Unstable
{\rm K}_{1}
for c-divisible monoids

We discuss the unstable
{\rm K}_{1}
results that we obtained for the linear case, building on the work in
[
14
]
:

In his paper
[
14
]
, Gubeladze showed that if
R
is a local PID and
n\geq 3
, then for any
c
-divisible monoid
L,

{\rm Sp}_{n}(R[L])={\mbox{E}_{n}(R[L])}.

He established this by developing class of rings
\mathbb{E}_{n}
(much like the
\mathcal{R}_{k}
we defined) and showed that any local PID belongs to
\mathbb{E}_{n}
for
n\geq 3
. We generalize this result and show that for regular rings for large enough values of
n,
we can establish unstable
{\rm K}_{1}
-results.

Remark 6.1
.

Let
R
be a regular affine algebra over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then, by (
[
24
]
) we have injective stability of
{\rm K}_{1}(R)
at the stage
n=\max\{3,d+1\}
. Further, if
R
is local, then using (
[
30
]
, Theorem 3.3) we may show for
n\geq 3,

{\mbox{SL}}_{n}(R[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m},Y_{1}^{\pm{1}},\ldots,Y_{t}^{\pm{1}}]={\mbox{E}}_{n}(R[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m},Y_{1}^{\pm{1}},\ldots,Y_{t}^{\pm{1}}]),
for all
m,t\geq 0
.

Remark 6.2
.

Let
R\in\mathbb{E}_{n}
, for some
n
where
n\geq\dim(R)+1.
Then
R\in\mathbb{E}_{k}
for all
k\geq n
.

Using the above remarks and techniques of Example
3.4
in conjunction with (
[
27
]
, Theorem 6.3), we can show the following:

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
R
be a regular local ring of dimension
d
. Then
R\in\mathbb{E}_{n},
if

(i)

n\geq\max\{3,d+2\}
,

(ii)

If
R
is essentially of finite type over
\mathbb{Q}
and
n\geq\max\{3,d+1\}
.

Then we can prove the following using (
[
27
]
) and emulate the techniques we employed in proof of Theorem
2
:

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
R
be a regular ring of dimension d with
{\rm K}_{1}(R)=0
and
L
be a
c
-divisible monoid. Then for any projective
P
of rank
n
with
n\geq\max\{2,d+1\},

{\rm SL}(P\oplus R[L])={\rm ETrans}(P\oplus R[L]).

Further, if
R
is essentially of finite type over
\mathbb{Q}
, then for
n\geq\max\{2,d\},
we get
{\rm SL}(P\oplus R[L])={\rm ETrans}(P\oplus R[L])
.

Intelligent pneumoperitoneum system stabilizes hemodynamics in robotic pheochromocytoma resection: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Yuchen Bai, Xuanhan Hu, Dechao Feng, Dahong Zhang, Qijun Wo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractHemodynamic instability during pheochromocytoma resection remains a critical challenge. This study evaluates an intelligent pneumoperitoneum system (Zhonghe Third-Generation) that dynamically modulates intra-abdominal pressure (IAP) to mitigate catecholamine surge. The study was retrospective analysis of 93 patients who under robotic adrenalectomies since 2020 to 2025 years. Which including 43 with synergy group and 50 conventional controls. Introduction

The removal of pheochromocytoma is often accompanied by significant hemodynamic instability, which is mainly attributed to the increase in catecholamines caused by the mechanical manipulation of the tumor. Although robotic-assisted laparoscopy affords superior instrument dexterity and three-dimensional visualization, but it does not reduced the risk of severe intraoperative hypertension, defined as systolic blood pressure exceeding 200 mmHg in 30–40% of cases. This hypertensive burden substantially elevates the likelihood of perioperative cardiac events, including myocardial ischemia and arrhythmias. Conventional constant-pressure pneumoperitoneum (12–15 mmHg) exacerbates this vulnerability through three mechanistically distinct yet synergistic pathways: (1) direct mechanical stress on the tumor parenchyma increases norepinephrine secretion by 2.8-fold (P < 0.01; [18]); (2) CO₂ accumulation within the peritoneal cavity induces localized tissue acidosis (pH < 7.2), thereby upregulating tyrosine hydroxylase activity and accelerating de novo catecholamine synthesis; and (3) impaired venous return (reduced by 18–22%) coupled with increased systemic vascular resistance (afterload elevated by ~35%) collectively heighten myocardial workload and compromise ventricular compliance—effects that are especially deleterious in patients with pre-existing left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH ≥ 30%, per NCCN Clinical Practice Guidelines in Oncology v3.2026). [1–3] This pathophysiologically coherent triad provides compelling mechanistic justification for transitioning from static to dynamic, physiology-guided intra-abdominal pressure (IAP) modulation during pheochromocytoma resection [4].

Robot-assisted laparoscopic resection of pheochromocytoma confers distinct advantages in surgical precision; However, The rigid puncture fixation structure itself can prolong the touching time between the instrument and the tumor, thereby enhancing the release of mechanical catecholamines.Conventional pneumoperitoneum systems lack real-time responsiveness to intraoperative perturbations, resulting in prolonged hemodynamic instability: intraoperative systolic blood pressure fluctuations persist for 32.7 ± 9.1 min on average. Although preoperative α-adrenergic blockers (such as phenoxybenzamine) remain the standard drug treatment for catecholamine-mediated hypertension, they do not reduce the direct mechanical stimulation caused by the surgical procedure—this is a key unresolved factor that leads to a sudden increase in blood pressure during the operation. [5–9]. The novel intelligent pneumoperitoneum system overcomes this limitation through three integrated, physiology-informed innovations: (1) real-time intraperitoneal pressure monitoring—enabled by 100-Hz high-fidelity sampling—that detects micro-disturbances (≥ 0.5 mmHg) induced by instrument–tumor contact; (2) adaptive intra-abdominal pressure (IAP) modulation—automatically down-titrating IAP to 8–10 mmHg during critical tumor dissection phases, thereby minimizing cyst wall deformation (in contrast to manual adjustment in conventional systems, which incurs a median response latency of >5 s); and (3) An airflow optimization algorithm can eliminate the "chimney effect", reduce the accumulation of local carbon dioxide at the surgical site, thereby inhibiting the upregulation of tyrosine hydroxylase and the subsequent synthesis of catecholamines caused by acidosis [10–15].

Although preliminary studies suggest that the intelligent pneumoperitoneum machine can reduce the peak release of norepinephrine by almost 40%, its value in PPC surgery lacks high-level evidence: previous studies were limited to small samples (n < 20) and lacked a control design; key indicators such as the area under the blood pressure fluctuation curve (AUC) and the dependence on vasoactive drugs were not quantified; Some subgroups with complex tumor anatomy (such as adhesion to the inferior vena cava) were not distinguished; and a comprehensive assessment and analysis of the perioperative period of the surgery under this synergistic effect were not conducted [16–21].

Materia and methods

Study population

We have analyzed all 312 patients between 2020 and 2025 years in our departentment, those who underwent robot—assisted adrenalectomy for pheochromocytoma.Initially, 18 patients were excluded due to missing data, and 11 patients with a history of previous adrenal surgery were also excluded. This left 283 patients eligible for further analysis. Subsequently, 190 patients were excluded because of incomplete covariate or follow—up data. Finally, 93 patients were included in the final analysis, which were divided into two groups: the Synergy group with 43 patients who used the intelligent pneumoperitoneum machine and the Conventional group with 50 patients who used the conventional pneumoperitoneum machine. Group allocation rule was predefined prior to data analysis and strictly followed: between January 2020 and December 2023, all consecutive patients who underwent robot-assisted adrenalectomy for pheochromocytoma were managed with the conventional fixed-pressure (12–14 mmHg) pneumoperitoneum system, forming the Conventional control cohort. After the institutional review board approved the clinical translation of the intelligent pneumoperitoneum system,we have started since january 2024, all consecutive eligible patients received the dynamic-feedback intelligent pneumoperitoneum system, and no eligible case was excluded after the system rollout. No assignment decision was made by the operating surgeon, and no patient was reallocated due to tumor anatomy, comorbidity status or intraoperative expected difficulty. This sequential equipment-rollout design eliminates subjective selection bias, and all baseline characteristics remained fully balanced across the two groups. Data collection

Baseline, intraoperative, and postoperative data were abstracted from the electronic medical record using prespecified definitions. The physicians responsible for data collection were not involved in the operations. Because treatment allocation followed a sequential equipment rollout, the study did not involve randomisation or blinding to randomisation. Postoperative complications were assessed through 30 days and graded according to the Clavien-Dindo classification. Readmission and mortality were assessed through 90 days. Biochemical status and recurrence were recorded at the latest available follow-up.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were summarised as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range), as appropriate, and categorical variables as number (percentage). Between-group comparisons used the independent-samples t test or Mann–Whitney U test for continuous variables and the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables. All tests were two-sided, and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Systolic blood pressure (SBP) was assessed at five predefined operative phases: after induction and before pneumoperitoneum establishment (T0), after pneumoperitoneum establishment (T1), during tumour handling (T2), during tumour removal (T3), and at the end of surgery (T4). For each patient, the absolute deviation at each phase was calculated relative to the individual T0 SBP value. The raw area under the curve (AUC) of these deviations was calculated using the trapezoidal rule across the prespecified phase times of 0, 5, 20, 40 and 60 min. The raw AUC was divided by 60 min to obtain the duration-normalised SBP deviation AUC, expressed in mmHg. Lower values indicate a lower average burden of SBP excursion from the post-induction reference during the predefined observation period. All operations were completed by the two dedicated robotic adrenalectomy teams at our center, each consisting of a lead attending surgeon with >10 years of robotic endocrine surgery experience and a personal caseload of >200 robotic adrenalectomies prior to the study period.

Intraoperative haemodynamic outcomes are presented in Table 3. The duration-normalised SBP deviation AUC was lower in the Synergy group than in the conventional group (18.3 ± 5.2 vs 24.7 ± 9.2 mmHg, P < 0.001). This result indicates a lower average burden of SBP deviation from the post-induction baseline across the predefined operative phases in the Synergy group. Pneumoperitoneum pressure variability, intraoperative SBP variability and peak SBP were also lower in the Synergy group, whereas mean pneumoperitoneum pressure did not differ significantly between groups.Based on the reported group means, this corresponded to a 43.1% lower value in the Synergy group. Pneumoperitoneum pressure variability was also lower in the Synergy group (0.68 ± 0.24 versus 1.85 ± 0.52 mmHg, P < 0.001), whereas mean pneumoperitoneum pressure did not differ significantly between groups (12.1 ± 0.6 versus 12.3 ± 0.8 mmHg, P = 0.173). Intraoperative SBP variability was lower in the Synergy group (16.3 ± 4.8 versus 27.6 ± 6.5 mmHg, P < 0.001), as was peak SBP (178.9 ± 20.7 versus 195.4 ± 23.8 mmHg, P < 0.001). The Synergy group also required fewer vasopressor doses (1.2 ± 0.8 versus 3.1 ± 1.5, P < 0.001). Hypertensive crisis occurred in 2 patients (4.7%) in the Synergy group and 6 patients (12.0%) in the conventional group; this difference was not statistically significant (P = 0.279). Surgical-field clarity scores were higher in the Synergy group (4.3 ± 0.5 versus 3.3 ± 0.6, P < 0.001).

Discussion

The findings of this study on robot—assisted adrenalectomy for pheochromocytoma, as reflected by the results and charts, have several important implications that warrant in discussion.

This study was an retrospective observational cohort design, all reported intergroup differences has been rigorously interpreted as statistically significant associations, rather than unconfounded evidence of a direct causal effect mediated exclusively by the intelligent pneumoperitoneum system. Our study achieved excellent baseline covariate balance across pre-specified demographic, clinical, and procedural variables (all p > 0.05, Table 1), residual unmeasured confounding cannot be completely excluded. Three potential residual sources of bias merit explicit acknowledgement: (1) subtle inter-individual heterogeneity in anesthesiologists’ real-time vasoactive drug titration strategies, which may independently modify SBP trajectory; (2) minor unrecorded variability in surgeon’s tumor dissection velocity and manipulation force, which are correlated with total operative time; (3) Absence of serial intra-operative plasma catecholamine measurements in this retrospective dataset, which prevents full disentanglement of haemodynamic effects originating from IAP modulation versus other unmeasured care patterns.

However, this correlation evidence still presented clinical value, it conforms to the standards for surgical assessment in the real world: the systolic blood pressure variability was reduced by over 43.1% (18.3 ± 5.2 vs 24.7 ± 9.2 mmHg, P < 0.001) which represents a large, highly statistically robust effect size that aligns with the pre-specified pathophysiological mechanistic model. We explicitly avoid overgeneralizing these findings to universal causal claims, and instead frame our results as hypothesis-generating real-world evidence that strongly justifies subsequent prospective randomized controlled trials (RCTs) to formally validate the independent causal effect of dynamic intelligent IAP modulation on pheochromocytoma resection outcomes.

The superior hemodynamic stability in the Synergy group is a crucial aspect. The reduced pneumoperitoneum variability, SBP variability, and peak SBP compared to the Conventional group suggest that the intelligent pneumoperitoneum machine was better at maintaining a stable intravascular environment during the operation. Pheochromocytomas are known to cause significant hemodynamic fluctuations due to the release of catecholamines during manipulation. The more stable blood pressure in the Synergy group may be related to the machine’s ability to more precisely control the pneumoperitoneum pressure, which in turn affects the systemic circulation. Stable hemodynamics are essential for preventing intraoperative cardiovascular events such as myocardial ischemia, arrhythmias, and stroke, and can contribute to better overall surgical outcomes [22–25].

The faster recovery in the Synergy group, as evidenced by the shorter time to first ambulation and postoperative hospital stay, is likely related to the combined effects of better intraoperative performance and hemodynamic stability. Shorter operative time and more stable hemodynamics may have reduced the overall physiological stress on the patients, allowing for a quicker return of normal physiological functions. Early ambulation is associated with a decreased risk of postoperative complications such as deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary atelectasis, and muscle atrophy. A shorter hospital stay not only benefits the patients in terms of reduced discomfort and cost but also has implications for healthcare resource utilization [26].

The expanded postoperative assessment provides additional clinical context for the early recovery findings. Complications that might plausibly be influenced by perioperative pressure management or haemodynamic instability are reported separately from events that are unlikely to be system-related. Because the study was not designed or powered to detect differences in uncommon complications, these observations are descriptive and should not be interpreted as evidence of a broad reduction in postoperative morbidity.

Overall, these results suggest that the use of an intelligent pneumoperitoneum machine in robot—assisted adrenalectomy for pheochromocytoma can offer several advantages in terms of operative efficiency, hemodynamic stability, and postoperative recovery. Clinicians may consider this technology as a potential option to improve surgical outcomes for these patients. However, this study has several important limitations that must be explicitly acknowledged. First, there was no priori sample size calculation was performed at study inception, given the retrospective, single-center design and the rarity of pheochromocytoma. Post-hoc power analysis demonstrates that the primary endpoint (SBP fluctuation AUC) achieved 99.2% statistical power, confirming our cohort was fully powered for the key hemodynamic comparison. However, several secondary outcomes, particularly the binary endpoint of intraoperative hypertensive crisis (observed event rate difference 7.3%), only reached 23.6% power, meaning the non-significant between-group difference we observed (p = 0.162) may reflect type II error due to limited sample size. Similarly, the trend toward reduced tumor dissection time did not meet statistical significance. These exploratory, underpowered outcomes should be interpreted cautiously, and we present them as hypothesis-generating signals that require validation in larger cohorts. The current 93-patient series, while the largest to date focusing on dynamic intelligent pneumoperitoneum for robotic pheochromocytoma resection, still cannot fully exclude the risk of overestimating effect size induced by relatively small sample. Anesthetic Management Standardization could affected the blood pressure during the operation, but patients received a unified protocol administered by a dedicated endocrine anesthesia team at our institution, which is highly specialized in pheochromocytoma care. A future multi-center prospective trial (target n = 300), using the effect estimates from the present study to define sample size, is currently under preparation to confirm these preliminary findings.

Conclusion

This study provides valuable insights into the potential benefits of using an intelligent pneumoperitoneum machine in robot—assisted adrenalectomy for pheochromocytoma. The results highlight the importance of optimizing intraoperative conditions to improve short—term surgical and recovery outcomes, and also point to areas for further research to enhance the evidence base for this technology.

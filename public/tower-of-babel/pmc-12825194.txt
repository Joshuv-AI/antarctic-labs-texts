Comparative analysis of absorbable tacker and suture techniques for peritoneal flap closure in laparoscopic inguinal hernia repair using the Transabdominal Preperitoneal (TAPP) approach: a retrospective observational cohort study
Authors: Burak Sakar, Nedim Akgul, Arzu Karaveli, Ali Celik, Turan Can Yıldız, Omer Celik, Aydın Dincer
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study aims to compare the effects of peritoneal flap closure using absorbable sutures versus absorbable tackers on early postoperative pain and clinical outcomes in laparoscopic inguinal hernia repair performed with the Transabdominal Preperitoneal (TAPP) technique.MethodsThis single-center retrospective study analyzed the data of 104 patients who underwent laparoscopic inguinal hernia repair using the TAPP technique between January 2022 and December 2024 and met the eligibility criteria. Patients were divided into two groups based on the peritoneal closure method: Group T (absorbable tacker, n = 55) and Group S (absorbable suture, n = 49). The primary endpoint of the study was to compare the effect of peritoneal flap closure using tackers versus sutures on the postoperative 24-hour Visual Analog Scale (VAS) pain score. Secondary endpoints included the comparison of VAS scores at other time points, postoperative complications, operation time, and the time to return to daily life/work.ResultsThe postoperative 24-hour VAS scores were significantly lower in the suture group (p < 0.001). Similarly, the postoperative 6-hour and 10-day VAS scores were also lower in the suture group (p = 0.001 and p = 0.008, respectively). However, the time to return to daily life/work and the 3-month VAS scores were comparable between the groups (p = 0.253 and p = 0.085, respectively). The operation time was significantly longer in the suture group (p = 0.011). The overall complication rates were similar between the groups (p = 0.056).ConclusionIn TAPP repair, closure of the peritoneal flap with sutures is associated with slightly longer operative times but results in less early postoperative pain. Suture closure of the peritoneal flap may be considered a safer and more effective alternative compared to closure with absorbable tackers. However, multicenter, prospective, randomized studies are needed to validate these findings.Keywords: Peritoneal flap, Inguinal hernia, Laparoscopy, Intracorporeal suture

Introduction

Inguinal hernia is recognized as one of the most common clinical conditions requiring surgical intervention worldwide [1]. The lifetime prevalence of inguinal hernia is approximately 25% in men and around 2% in women. This risk increases with advancing age, with the incidence reaching up to 50% in men aged 75 years. Additionally, approximately 10% of cases are observed to be bilateral [2].

Inguinal hernia repair can be performed using open, laparoscopic, or robotic techniques. Laparoscopic techniques have become a preferred approach for inguinal hernia repair in appropriately selected patients due to their minimally invasive nature. This approach is widely used as it offers several advantages, including a shorter hospital stay, faster return to daily activities in the postoperative period, reduced postoperative pain, and better cosmetic outcomes [3, 4]. The Transabdominal Preperitoneal (TAPP) technique, one of the commonly used methods for inguinal hernia repair today and originally described by Arregui, is based on the principle of entering the peritoneal cavity and placing a graft into the preperitoneal space where the hernia sac is located [5]. After mesh placement, the peritoneal flap must be closed. This approach aims to prevent direct contact between intra-abdominal organs and the implanted mesh, thereby reducing the risk of adhesions and complications. Various techniques are used for peritoneal flap closure, including tacker fixation, fibrin sealants, and suturing methods [6].

Pain is a common postoperative complication following inguinal hernia surgery [7]. The identification of the most effective methods for reducing postoperative pain remains a subject of ongoing debate. The aim of the present study is to perform a comparative analysis of patients in whom absorbable suture material and absorbable tackers were used for peritoneal flap closure during inguinal hernia repair with the TAPP technique, and to investigate their effects primarily on postoperative inguinal pain and on postoperative clinical outcomes.

Materials and methods

Ethical approval for this single-center, retrospective, and observational study was obtained from the Clinical Research Ethics Committee of the University of Health Sciences Antalya Training and Research Hospital (Ethics Committee number: 2025 − 144, date: 17.04.2025). The data of patients who underwent inguinal hernia repair with the TAPP technique in our clinic between January 2022 and December 2024 were analyzed retrospectively. The study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. The Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines were followed for patient enrollment and allocation.

The inclusion criteria for the study consisted of patients aged between 18 and 65 years who underwent elective primary unilateral inguinal hernia repair using the TAPP technique at our clinic between January 1, 2022, and December 1, 2024. Patients who had peritoneal flap closure using either tackers or sutures, had visual analog scale (VAS) scores recorded at the 6th hour, 24th hour, 10th day, and 3rd month postoperatively, had available hemogram values preoperatively and at the 24th postoperative hour, underwent unilateral inguinal hernia repair, had fixation material used for mesh placement, and were operated on by a hernia surgeon at our clinic were included in the study. Patients who did not meet the inclusion criteria, had hernia defects larger than 3 cm, had previously undergone open or recurrent inguinal hernia repair, were over 65 years of age, were smokers, were using anticoagulant therapy, or underwent emergency surgery were excluded from the study to minimize potential confounding effects of age and smoking on postoperative pain perception, wound healing, and recovery [8, 9].

The data of patients who underwent surgery using the TAPP technique for inguinal hernia repair were retrospectively analyzed. Patients were divided into two groups according to the peritoneal closure method: those whose peritoneal flaps were closed with absorbable tackers (AbsorbaTack™, Covidien™) (Group T) and those whose peritoneal flaps were closed with continuous suturing using absorbable sutures (V-Loc®, Covidien™) (Group S). The demographic characteristics of the included patients [age, sex, body mass index (BMI), comorbidities, American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) score, history of previous abdominal surgery, presenting symptoms, and surgical site], operative time, postoperative VAS scores, preoperative and postoperative hemogram parameters [hemoglobin (Hb), white blood cell (WBC) counts, and neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR)], length of hospital stay, time to return to daily life/work, presence of possible surgical complications, and follow-up durations were retrospectively evaluated using a structured form routinely completed in our clinic, along with data from the hospital database, operative records, and nursing observations.

Operative time was defined as the duration from the start to the completion of surgery, while the time to return to daily life/work was defined as the period from the date of surgery to the resumption of daily activities or work. Recurrences occurring within the first six months postoperatively were classified as early recurrences, whereas those occurring after the third month were classified as late recurrences.

The VAS is a subjective measurement tool used to assess patients’ pain levels. This scale rates pain on a scale from 0 to 10, where “0” indicates no pain and “10” represents unbearable pain [10]. Postoperative VAS scores (at 6 and 24 h, 10 days and 3 months) and possible complications were retrospectively recorded from our hospital database and nursing observations, together with a pre-structured form routinely completed in our clinic.

The aim of the study is to conduct a comparative analysis of patients who underwent peritoneal flap closure with absorbable tackers versus absorbable sutures during inguinal hernia repair using the TAPP technique, primarily investigating their effects on postoperative inguinal pain and clinical outcomes. Accordingly, the primary endpoint of the study is to compare the impact of different peritoneal closure methods on the postoperative 24-hour VAS score in patients who underwent laparoscopic inguinal hernia repair using the TAPP technique. The secondary endpoints of the study include the comparison of VAS scores at other time points, potential postoperative complications, time to return to daily life/work, and operative time.

TAPP procedure

While the patient was in the supine position, an optical trocar (T1, 11–12 mm) was inserted into the supraumbilical region using the open laparoscopy technique. Following lower abdominal examination, T2 and T3 trocars were placed under direct vision on the transverse umbilical line, 6–8 cm lateral to T1, with careful attention to the preservation of the inferior epigastric vessels during trocar placement. Subsequently, a wide peritoneal flap was prepared to ensure adequate exposure of the entire myopectineal orifice and proper positioning of the mesh. This procedure was performed in accordance with the “10 Golden Rules” reported in the International Endohernia Society (IEHS) guidelines and methodologically standardized by Furtado et al. and Claus et al. [11–13]. In this context, the deep inguinal ring was identified through the parietal peritoneum located at the center of the “inverted Y” configuration, bordered superiorly by the inferior epigastric vessels, inferiorly by the vas deferens (or the round ligament in female patients), and medially and laterally by the testicular vessels (in male patients only). Therefore, a transverse incision was made between a point located 2 cm below and medial to the anterior superior iliac spine and the lateral umbilical ligament. The flap dissection was initiated by exposing the epigastric vessels and advancing toward the Bogros space. Subsequently, the dissection was continued medially to identify the Cooper’s ligament and a portion of the femoral orifice. Lateral dissection was carried out along the inferior epigastric vessels, exposing the iliac fascia, the iliopubic tract, and the iliopsoas fascia. Depending on the location of the hernia defect, the hernia sac was carefully dissected either from the transversalis fascia (in the Hesselbach triangle) or from the spermatic cord and its associated structures (the round ligament in female patients). In the final stage, the structures were carefully parietalized in male patients, particularly along the lower border, to ensure proper positioning of the mesh. The adequacy of the dissection was confirmed by the exposure of the point where the vas deferens crosses the external iliac vein and its alignment with the iliopsoas fascia. An area of at least 2 cm between the Cooper’s ligament and the bladder was dissected to allow for stable placement of the mesh. Direct defects had not been closed in any of the patients. After the placement of a standard polypropylene mesh and its fixation with absorbable tackers, the prepared peritoneal flap was closed using either a 3/0 absorbable suture (V-Loc®, Covidien™) or three absorbable tackers (AbsorbaTack™, Covidien™) (Fig. 1).

Perioperative patient management

All patients underwent surgery under general anesthesia. Prior to surgery, a routine prophylactic infusion of 1 gram of intravenous (i.v.) cefazolin was administered. Standard anesthesia monitoring, including 3-lead electrocardiography, heart rate, noninvasive blood pressure, peripheral oxygen saturation, and end-tidal carbon dioxide monitoring, was applied to all patients in the operating room. For postoperative analgesia, all patients received 1 gram of intravenous (i.v.) paracetamol and 100 mg of intravenous tramadol, administered by the anesthesia team 20 min before the end of surgery. All patients were managed according to our hospital’s standard postoperative analgesia protocol (1 gram of i.v. paracetamol every 8 h).

Statistical analysis

Power analysis was performed using the G*Power 3.1 software. Based on the data from the study by Akkurt et al. [12], a power analysis was conducted to compare 24-hour VAS scores between two groups [4.85 (1.39) vs. 3.70 (1.34)] with α = 0.05, β = 0.95, and an effect size of 0.84. Using these parameters, the minimum required sample size was calculated as 38 participants per group. Taking into account a potential data loss of 10%, the minimum sample size was updated to 42 participants per group (a total of 84 participants).

All statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics version 24 (Statistical Package for the Social Sciences). Continuous variables were presented as mean (standard deviation; SD) or median (interquartile range; IQR), and categorical variables were expressed as frequency (percentage; %). The Shapiro–Wilk test was used to assess the normality of distribution for continuous variables. Parametric variables were compared using the Student’s t-test, while non-parametric variables were analyzed using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were evaluated using the Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, where appropriate. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Between January 2022 and December 2024, the medical records of 319 patients who underwent laparoscopic hernia repair for inguinal hernia were reviewed, and 177 patients were excluded from the study because they underwent hernia repair using the TEP technique. In the TAPP technique, the data of 142 patients who underwent peritoneal flap closure with absorbable sutures or tackers were reviewed. During the analysis phase, 21 patients from Group T and 17 patients from Group S were excluded due to protocol violations. As a result, the data of 55 patients in Group T and 49 patients in Group S were included in the statistical analysis (Fig. 2).

Intraoperative data

When evaluating the hernia types of the patients, 36 patients (65.5%) in Group T had an indirect hernia, 14 patients (25.5%) had a direct hernia, and 5 patients (9.1%) had a pantaloon hernia. In Group S, 25 patients (51%) had an indirect hernia, 20 patients (40.8%) had a direct hernia, 3 patients (6.1%) had a pantaloon hernia, and 1 patient (1%) had a femoral hernia (p = 0.240). Laboratory data

When evaluating the laboratory values of the patients, the decrease in hemoglobin levels at the postoperative 24th hour was similar between the two groups (p = 0.925). No statistically significant difference was observed between the groups regarding the increase in WBC levels at the postoperative 24th hour (p = 0.871). Similarly, the median value of the preoperative hemogram parameter NLR was 1.83 (1.38–2.36) in Group T and 1.91 (1.47–2.20) in Group S, with no statistically significant difference observed between the groups (p = 0.853). The postoperative absolute increase in NLR, calculated using the preoperative and 24-hour postoperative neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratios, was similar between the groups (p = 0.663) (Table 3).

Postoperative follow-up and complication data

Postoperative complications were observed in 9 patients (16.4%) in Group T, whereas they occurred in 3 patients (6.1%) in Group S. In Group T, seroma was observed in 4 patients (7.3%), hematoma in 1 patient (2.3%), and late recurrence in 3 patients (5.5%). In Group S, seroma was observed in 2 patients (4.1%) and late recurrence in 1 patient (2%). No statistically significant difference was found between the groups regarding the development of postoperative complications (p = 0.056) (Table 3). When evaluating the time to return to daily life/work, the median duration was 6 days (6–8) in Group T and 7 days (6–9) in Group S, with no statistically significant difference observed between the groups (p = 0.256) (Table 4).

Discussion

In our study, it was observed that patients who underwent laparoscopic inguinal hernia repair with the TAPP technique and had peritoneal flap closure with sutures had lower postoperative mean VAS scores at the 6th hour, 24th hour, and 10th day compared to those whose flaps were closed with absorbable tackers. Postoperative 3rd-month VAS scores and the time to return to daily life/work were similar between the groups. In patients who underwent laparoscopic inguinal hernia repair with the TAPP technique and had peritoneal flap closure using sutures or absorbable tackers, the most frequently observed postoperative complications were seroma at the surgical site, late recurrence, and hematoma. The operative time was observed to be shorter in patients whose peritoneal flaps were closed with absorbable tackers.

Pain is a common postoperative complication following inguinal hernia surgery. However, the most effective method for reducing postoperative pain remains a subject of ongoing debate. In the retrospective study conducted by Akkurt et al., postoperative 24-hour VAS scores were found to be significantly lower in patients whose peritoneal flaps were closed with sutures during TAPP repair, and this finding was associated with trauma to the transversus abdominis muscle caused by the use of non-absorbable tackers [14]. Similarly, in a prospective study conducted by Shankar et al. in 2024, postoperative pain scores on both the first day and the third month were significantly lower in patients who underwent mesh fixation and peritoneal flap closure with non-absorbable sutures, compared to those in whom non-absorbable titanium tackers were used. The difference in pain was attributed to the potential for tackers to cause nerve injury and trigger a local inflammatory response due to their invasive penetration of the abdominal wall. Furthermore, it was suggested that peritoneal flap closure with sutures, being limited to peritoneal surfaces, may reduce postoperative pain by preserving surrounding muscular and neural structures [15]. Similar findings were reported in studies conducted by Ross et al. in both 2014 and 2016. In both studies, patients who underwent peritoneal flap closure with absorbable sutures experienced less early postoperative pain and activity limitation compared to those in whom non-absorbable tackers or staples were used. This was attributed to the reduced risk of nerve irritation due to the absence of abdominal wall penetration with sutures, as well as the promotion of a more physiologically favorable biomechanical healing process [16, 17]. Considering previous studies reporting that the type of tacker used (absorbable or non-absorbable) does not have a significant effect on postoperative pain [18–20], our study differs from the existing literature by comparing the closure of the peritoneal flap using absorbable tackers versus absorbable sutures within the TAPP technique. In our findings, patients whose peritoneal flaps were closed with absorbable sutures had significantly lower 24-hour VAS scores, which may be attributed to the reduced risk of nerve injury associated with less invasive flap closure techniques, thereby potentially minimizing postoperative pain.

Another factor influencing the development of postoperative pain in inguinal hernia repair is the number of tackers used during laparoscopic surgery. There are various studies in the literature that have investigated the effect of the number of tackers on postoperative pain. Indeed, in a study conducted by Ross et al. in 2014, postoperative pain levels were found to be significantly higher in patients in whom 10 or more tackers were used, and this was attributed to deep penetration of the mesh or peritoneum into the abdominal wall [16]. Similarly, follow-up studies conducted in 2016 also reported that reduced use of tackers was associated with decreased postoperative pain [17]. Furthermore, the literature has shown that even in patients who underwent laparoscopic inguinal hernia repair without the use of tackers, postoperative pain levels were significantly reduced. In a 2024 meta-analysis by Zhang et al., it was reported that omitting mesh fixation in TAPP repair reduced postoperative pain without compromising the effectiveness of the procedure [21]. In a randomized controlled trial conducted by Sadeghi et al., lower pain scores were reported in patients in whom a single absorbable tacker was used compared to the conventional method [22]. In the study by Elghadban et al., early postoperative pain was shown to be lower in patients who received anatomical mesh without mesh fixation [23]. In the present study, mesh fixation was performed using three absorbable tackers as a standard in both groups; however, the total number of tackers was significantly lower in the group in which the peritoneal flap was closed with sutures. The observation of lower pain scores in the early postoperative period (particularly at the 24th hour, as well as the 6th hour and 10th day) in patients whose peritoneal flaps were closed with sutures, along with similar scores between both groups at the 3rd month, suggests that this method may contribute to more effective pain control in the early postoperative period. These findings suggest that suture-based techniques, when used in a minimally invasive manner and combined with a reduced number of tackers, may contribute to early postoperative pain control and could potentially be applied as a viable alternative in TAPP surgery, pending confirmation by future prospective studies.

Early return to daily activities and work following laparoscopic inguinal hernia repair is one of the key parameters in evaluating the success of the surgical technique [24, 25]. Indeed, the literature has reported that closing the peritoneal flap with sutures instead of tackers reduces early postoperative pain and the associated limitation of movement, thereby accelerating the return to daily activities. Specifically, in the 2014 study by Ross et al., activity limitation was observed in 21.7% of patients in the suture group, whereas this rate increased to 57.9% in the stapler group. This result highlights the impact of pain-related functional limitation and delayed mobilization on the time to return to work. Similarly, in the study by Shankar et al., short-term quality of life scores were found to be better in favor of the suture group, and this was suggested to contribute to earlier functional recovery [15, 16]. However, although the 2024 meta-analysis by Zhang et al. reported that omitting mesh fixation reduced postoperative pain, no significant difference was found between patients with and without fixation in terms of return-to-work time [21]. This finding suggests that the time to return to work may be influenced not only by pain levels but also by individual factors, occupational characteristics, and social or environmental conditions. In the present study, although differences in early postoperative pain were observed, the time to return to daily life/work was similar between the groups. In this context, although the suture-based peritoneal flap closure technique appears to offer an advantage in terms of postoperative pain, its lack of a significant impact on the return to daily activities and work suggests that this parameter should be considered multidimensionally—not only in relation to the surgical technique but also in light of individual, occupational, and environmental factors.

The method chosen for peritoneal flap closure can directly affect the operative time, depending on the surgeon’s experience. In the present study, the operative time was found to be significantly longer in patients in whom sutures were used compared to those in whom tackers were used. However, this difference was clinically limited, with a mean duration of 35.4 (5.0) minutes in the suture group and 32.8 (4.8) minutes in the tacker group—approximately a 3-minute difference. In the study by Shankar et al., the operative time was also found to be significantly longer in patients in whom sutures were used, with a reported mean duration of 77.76 (1.42) minutes in the suture group and 45.7 (4.24) minutes in the tacker group [15]. Although operative times were not reported in the 2014 study by Ross et al., the suture technique was emphasized to be more time-consuming in terms of application duration and technical complexity [16]. Moreover, in the prospective randomized controlled trial conducted by Oguz et al., the operative time was shown to be shorter in patients whose peritoneal flaps were closed with tackers [26]. Taken together, these findings suggest that although the suture technique may increase operative time, as observed in our study, this prolongation is relatively limited and may be clinically acceptable given the potential benefits in postoperative pain reduction and improved functional recovery.

Postoperative complications following laparoscopic inguinal hernia repair may vary depending on the surgical technique and materials used. In the present study, the complication rate was 6.1% in patients whose peritoneal flaps were closed with sutures and 16.4% in the tacker group. Although differences were observed between the groups in terms of seroma, hematoma, and late recurrence, the overall complication rates did not differ significantly. The findings in the literature are consistent with this result [14–17]. These findings suggest that although the suture technique may prolong the operative time, it may be considered a safe alternative, given the comparable complication rates observed between the groups. The patients in our study who experienced recurrence were reoperated using the Lichtenstein technique by the same surgeon who performed the initial procedure. Moreover, the absence of statistically significant differences between the groups in demographic and laboratory parameters, and the fact that all surgeries were performed by a single surgeon experienced in laparoscopic hernia repair further enhances the internal consistency of the findings.

This study has several limitations. First, it has a retrospective design, which makes it susceptible to potential selection bias and observer-related errors. The fact that the study was conducted at a single center and involved a relatively limited number of patients may reduce the generalizability of the findings. However, all eligible patients who met the inclusion criteria within the study period were included, which helped to ensure group comparability and minimize potential selection bias. Additionally, the use of the VAS, a subjective method for pain assessment, may be influenced by individual differences in pain perception. As this was a retrospective study, fibrin glue was not used for mesh fixation during the study period, which may limit the evaluation of glue-based atraumatic fixation methods in relation to postoperative pain. Nevertheless, the fact that all surgical procedures were performed by a single surgeon experienced in the laparoscopic TAPP technique and in accordance with current guidelines represents a strength in terms of procedural standardization; however, it should also be noted that this may limit the generalizability of findings related to operative technique and outcomes, as results obtained by a single experienced surgeon may not reflect those achievable in broader clinical practice. Finally, the short follow-up period of the study may be insufficient for evaluating long-term outcomes, particularly chronic pain.

Conclusion

In inguinal hernia repairs performed using the TAPP technique, closure of the peritoneal flap with absorbable sutures was associated with less pain in the early postoperative period. Although the operative time was longer in patients whose peritoneal flaps were closed with absorbable sutures, complication rates and time to return to daily life/work were found to be similar between the groups. Closure of the peritoneal flap with absorbable sutures may be considered an effective alternative to closure with absorbable tackers. However, multicenter, large-sample, prospective, and randomized studies are needed to validate these findings.

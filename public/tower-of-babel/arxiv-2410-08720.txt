Gauging generalised symmetries in linear gravity
Chris Hull, Maxwell L. Hutt, Ulf Lindström

Abstract
The theory of a free spin-2 field on Minkowski spacetime has 1-form and ( d − 3 ) (d-3) -form symmetries associated with conserved currents formed by contractions of the linearised Riemann tensor with conformal Killing-Yano 2-forms. We show that a subset of these can be interpreted as Noether currents for specific shift symmetries of the graviton that involve a Killing vector and a closed 1-form parameter. We give a systematic method to gauge these 1-form symmetries by coupling the currents to background gauge fields and introducing a particular set of counter-terms involving the background fields. The simultaneous gauging of certain pairs of 1-form and ( d − 3 ) (d-3) -form symmetries is obstructed by the presence of mixed ’t Hooft anomalies. The anomalous pairs of symmetries are those which are related by gravitational duality. The implications of these anomalies are discussed.

Introduction

Gauge theories can often be formulated in several different ways, related by dualities.
While different formulations of a theory may be written in terms of different fundamental fields with different gauge symmetries, the (generalised)
global

Because of their invariance under renormalisation group (RG) flow
[
1

A well-known example is that of

\mathcal{A}

\mathcal{C}

From (

In

\mathcal{C}
-type Penrose charges should be understood as generating magnetic symmetries in four dimensions.

This is analogous to the magnetic

1-form symmetry of four-dimensional Maxwell theory, whose Noether current is proportional to

. This is identically conserved by the Bianchi identity and cannot be understood as the Noether current associated with any continuous transformation of the field

.
We will return to these magnetic symmetries in section

.

A complementary perspective on the Penrose charges was given in

[

, as well as a set of magnetic charges. These magnetic charges stem from identically conserved 2-form currents in the graviton theory, and can be related to electric charges of the dual graviton

[

4
Gauging 1-form symmetries with constant
k

From the previous section, the Penrose charges (
29
) can be seen as Noether charges associated with the shift symmetries (
13
) where
\lambda
is a closed (and non-exact) 1-form and
k
is a Killing vector. These are global symmetries as there is a constraint
\differential\lambda=0
on the transformation parameter. In order to diagnose a ’t Hooft anomaly amongst these symmetries, it is interesting to study their gauging.

Let us begin with the case in which the Killing vector
k
is a constant vector.
In this case, we will see that it is possible to couple a background 2-form gauge field without using the relation of Killing vectors to CKY 2-forms that we found in the previous section. This is not the case when
k
is a non-constant Killing vector, as we will see in section
5
.

4.1
Gauge-invariant field equation

It is simple to write down a gauge-invariant field equation as follows. Firstly, the linearised Riemann tensor has components given in (
7
) and so under the variation (
13
), it varies as

\delta R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=k_{[\rho}\partial_{\sigma]}\partial_{[\mu}\lambda_{\nu]}+k_{[\mu}\partial_{\nu]}\partial_{[\rho}\lambda_{\sigma]}

(30)

when
k
is a constant. The relevant background gauge field to introduce is a 2-form gauge field
B_{\mu\nu}
transforming as

B_{\mu\nu}\to B_{\mu\nu}+\partial_{[\mu}\lambda_{\nu]}

(31)

We define the modified Riemann tensor

\mathcal{R}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}-k_{[\rho}\partial_{\sigma]}B_{\mu\nu}-k_{[\mu}\partial_{\nu]}B_{\rho\sigma}

(32)

which is invariant under combined transformations (
13
) and (
31
), i.e.

\delta h_{\mu\nu}=\lambda_{(\mu}k_{\nu)},\quad\delta B_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{[\mu}\lambda_{\nu]}

(33)

Therefore, a gauge-invariant field equation is

\mathcal{G}_{\mu\nu}=G_{\mu\nu}+3\eta_{\rho\alpha(\mu|\nu)\sigma\beta}k^{\rho}\partial^{\alpha}B^{\sigma\beta}=0

(34)

where

\mathcal{G}_{\mu\nu}=-\frac{3}{2}\eta_{\mu\rho\alpha|\nu\sigma\beta}\mathcal{R}^{\rho\alpha\sigma\beta}

(35)

is the Einstein tensor constructed from
\mathcal{R}
instead of
R
, and

\eta_{\mu\nu\rho|\alpha\beta\gamma}=\eta_{\mu\kappa}\eta_{\nu\lambda}\eta_{\rho\sigma}\delta^{\kappa\lambda\sigma}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}

(36)

This tensor satisfies the important properties

\eta_{\mu\nu\rho|\alpha\beta\gamma}=\eta_{\alpha\beta\gamma|\mu\nu\rho},\quad\eta_{[\mu\nu\rho|\alpha]\beta\gamma}=0

(37)

In other words, it transforms in the [3,3] representation of
GL(d,\mathbb{R})
.

We note that
\mathcal{R}
does not satisfy the analogues of the Bianchi identities, that is,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{[\mu\nu\alpha]\beta}

\displaystyle=-2k_{[\mu}\partial_{\nu}B_{\alpha\beta]}

(38)

\displaystyle\partial_{[\sigma}\mathcal{R}_{\mu\nu]\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=-k_{[\alpha}\partial_{\beta]}\partial_{[\sigma}B_{\mu\nu]}

(39)

In the case where the background field
B
is flat, i.e.
\differential{B}=0
, then the right-hand side of both (
38
) and (
39
) vanish and the familiar Bianchi identities for the Riemann tensor in the ungauged theory hold true also in the gauged theory for the modified Riemann tensor (
32
).

\delta S_{0}=-3\int\differential[d]{x}\delta^{\nu\sigma\beta}_{\mu\rho\alpha}\partial^{\rho}h^{{{\mu}\mathchoice{\makebox[4.00928pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.00928pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.42052pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.72893pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}_{{\mathchoice{\makebox[4.86232pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.86232pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.95248pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[2.10892pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{\nu}}}k^{\alpha}\partial_{[\sigma}\lambda_{\beta]}

5
The general case: gauging 1-form symmetries

In the previous section, we saw that the shift symmetry (
13
) with
k
constant can be gauged by the introduction of a 2-form background gauge field
B
simply by analysing the variation of the graviton action under the transformation and introducing counterterms. In that case, the variations were sufficiently simple that they could be written in terms of
\differential\lambda
and then gauged immediately by introducing the obvious couplings of
B
.

We now treat the general case where
k
is an arbitrary Minkowski space Killing vector, and so can be at most linear in the coordinates
x^{\mu}
. We will make frequent use of the fact that the Killing vectors satisfy
\partial_{(\mu}k_{\nu)}=0
and
\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}k_{\alpha}=0
.

5.1
Gauge-invariant field equation

Identifying a gauge-invariant field equation is possible by again examining the variation of the Riemann tensor, which can be written

\displaystyle\begin{split}\delta R_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}&=k_{[\alpha}\partial_{\beta]}\partial_{[\mu}\lambda_{\nu]}+k_{[\mu}\partial_{\nu]}\partial_{[\alpha}\lambda_{\beta]}-\partial_{[\mu}\lambda_{\nu]}\partial_{\alpha}k_{\beta}-\partial_{[\alpha}\lambda_{\beta]}\partial_{\mu}k_{\nu}\\
&\quad-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{[\alpha}\lambda_{\nu]}\partial_{\mu}k_{\beta}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{[\mu}\lambda_{\beta]}\partial_{\alpha}k_{\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{[\beta}\lambda_{\nu]}\partial_{\mu}k_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{[\mu}\lambda_{\alpha]}\partial_{\beta}k_{\nu}\end{split}

(44)

Since this depends only on
\differential\lambda
, we can define a modified Riemann tensor

\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}=R_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}-k_{[\alpha}\partial_{\beta]}B_{\mu\nu}-k_{[\mu}\partial_{\nu]}B_{\alpha\beta}+B_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\alpha}k_{\beta}+B_{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\mu}k_{\nu}-\left(B_{\alpha[\mu}\partial_{\nu]}k_{\beta}-\alpha\leftrightarrow\beta\right)

(45)

which is gauge-invariant.
Indeed, if
k
is a constant vector then this reduces to the modified Riemann tensor
\mathcal{R}
found previously in (
32
).
A gauge-invariant modification of the Einstein tensor is

\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}=-\frac{3}{2}\eta_{\mu\rho\alpha|\nu\sigma\beta}\mathcal{R^{\prime}}^{\rho\alpha\sigma\beta}

(46)

which evaluates to

\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}=G_{\mu\nu}-3\eta_{\rho\alpha(\mu|\nu)\sigma\beta}\left(-k^{\sigma}\partial^{\beta}B^{\rho\alpha}+\frac{3}{2}B^{\rho\alpha}\partial^{\sigma}k^{\beta}\right)

(47)

As in the case considered in the previous section,
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
does not satisfy the analogs of the Bianchi identities:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{[\mu\nu\alpha]\beta}

\displaystyle=-2k_{[\mu}\partial_{\nu}B_{\alpha\beta]}

(48)

\displaystyle\partial_{[\sigma}\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{\mu\nu]\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=-k_{[\alpha}\partial_{\beta]}\partial_{[\sigma}B_{\mu\nu]}+2\partial_{[\sigma}B_{\mu\nu]}\partial_{\alpha}k_{\beta}-2\left(\partial_{[\sigma}B_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\alpha]}k_{\beta}-\alpha\leftrightarrow\beta\right)

(49)

We note that the Bianchi identities are again satisfied when the background is flat,
\differential{B}=0
.

5.2
Gauge-invariant action

It is more challenging to construct a gauge-invariant action. Recall our earlier observation that the improved Penrose current
Y_{+}(K)
, defined in (

), can be taken as the Noether current for the shift symmetry (
13
), where
K
and

S_{0}^{\prime\prime}=S_{0}-\int\differential[d]{x}B_{\mu\nu}Y_{+}(K)^{\mu\nu}

S^{\prime\prime}=S_{0}+s(k,B)

The field equation for

\mathcal{G^{\prime}}_{\mu\nu}+6\left((\eta_{\nu[\mu}K_{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\gamma]}\partial^{[\gamma}B^{\alpha\beta]}-\partial_{[\mu}B_{\alpha\beta]}\tilde{K}^{{{\alpha\beta}\mathchoice{\makebox[4.00928pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.00928pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.42052pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.72893pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}_{{\mathchoice{\makebox[9.73317pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[9.73317pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[5.9061pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[4.21864pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{\nu}}})+\mu\leftrightarrow\nu\right)+4\eta_{\mu\nu}\partial_{[\alpha}B_{\beta\gamma]}\tilde{K}^{\alpha\beta\gamma}=0

5.3
Comparison with the constant

In section

First, let us consider the terms in the gauged actions which are linear in the background field

5

5

In the familiar case of a continuous 0-form global symmetry, the Noether current

The difference between the couplings to the background field

Gauging multiple 1-form symmetries

Equation (
51

B
to the graviton action which gauges the shift symmetry (

) for a given Killing vector
k
.
We now ask if multiple shift symmetries can be simultaneously gauged in this manner.
Let
k_{I}
with

be a set of linearly independent Killing vectors of the background Minkowski space. The index
I
then labels the independent electric 1-form symmetries (
13
).
Consider a pair of Killing vectors
k_{I}
and
k_{J}
with
I\neq J
.
We wish to consistently couple two 2-form background fields
B^{I}
and
B^{J}
to gauge the 1-form symmetries labelled by
k_{I}
and
k_{J}
respectively.
The resulting theory should then have two gauge symmetries,

6

6

\delta h_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\lambda^{I}_{\mu}k_{I\nu}+\lambda^{I}_{\nu}k_{I\mu}\right),\quad\delta B^{I}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{[\mu}\lambda^{I}_{\nu]}

(59)

and

\delta h_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\lambda^{J}_{\mu}k_{J\nu}+\lambda^{J}_{\nu}k_{J\mu}\right),\quad\delta B^{J}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{[\mu}\lambda^{J}_{\nu]}

(60)

with
\lambda^{I}
and
\lambda^{J}

Naïvely, the gauging would involve the addition of the same couplings found in the previous section, i.e.

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=S_{0}+s(k_{I},B^{I})+s(k_{J},B^{J})

(61)

\displaystyle=\int\differential[d]{x}\left(\frac{1}{2}h_{\mu\nu}G^{\mu\nu}-B^{I}_{\mu\nu}Y_{+}(K_{I})^{\mu\nu}+f(K_{I},B^{I})-B^{J}_{\mu\nu}Y_{+}(K_{J})^{\mu\nu}+f(K_{J},B^{J})\right)

), and

are
\mathcal{B}
- or
\mathcal{D}

and

However,
S_{1}
is invariant under neither (

) nor (

and

which give an action which is simultaneously invariant under both (

and

where

) and contains counter-terms depending only on the background fields

and

. Furthermore, as shown in Appendix

is also symmetric under this exchange.

We recall from the discussion in section

that the 1-form symmetries generated by the Penrose charges in

\mathbb{R}^{d(d+1)/2}
.
It follows from the discussion above that any sub-group of the electric 1-form symmetry group

can be gauged by the introduction of the relevant counter-terms. Consider a subgroup generated by Killing vectors
k_{I}
with

S_{\mathcal{I}}=S_{0}+\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}}s(k_{I},B^{I})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J\in\mathcal{I}\\

(63)

Therefore, there are no mixed ’t Hooft anomalies amongst the electric 1-form symmetries.

7
Dual symmetries and their gauging

We now discuss dual ‘magnetic’ symmetries present in the graviton theory. These are

7.1
Four dimensions

In
d=4
, there are 20 one-form symmetries generated by the Penrose charges. Ten of these are generated by Penrose charges corresponding to CKY tensors of the
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{D}
types. In section
3
we saw that these can be interpreted as Noether charges associated with shift symmetries
\delta h_{\mu\nu}=\lambda_{(\mu}k_{\nu)}
where
k
is related to the CKY tensors by (
28
). In analogy to the 1-form symmetry generated by
\int\star F
in Maxwell theory, these are ‘electric’ symmetries.

The Penrose charges corresponding to the
\mathcal{A}
- and
\mathcal{C}
-type CKY tensors, of which there are also ten, are not related to any field transformation.
In this sense, they are interpreted as ‘magnetic’ (or topological) symmetries, much like the 1-form symmetry generated by
\int F
in Maxwell theory. That is, the improved Penrose currents corresponding to these CKY tensors are conserved identically, not just on solutions to the field equations.

The electric and magnetic higher-form symmetries of Maxwell theory are exchanged under duality. This is true of the
\mathcal{B}
- and
\mathcal{C}
-type Penrose charges in the graviton theory.
In the dual graviton theory, the
\mathcal{C}
-type shift symmetries are related to a shift of the dual graviton, whereas the
\mathcal{B}
-type ones are not. Interestingly, the
\mathcal{A}
-type symmetries cannot be related to a field transformation in either formulation of the theory and so appear as ‘magnetic’ charges in both formulations. On the other hand, the
\mathcal{D}
-type symmetries are ‘electric’ in both formulations.

In four dimensions, the Hodge duals of the improved Penrose currents
\star Y_{+}(K)
are also conserved on-shell
[
6
,
10
]
. However, due to the properties of CKY tensors under Hodge duality, the charges found by integrating them are not independent of the original Penrose charges. So the independent 1-form symmetries are the 20 Penrose charges.

Each of the magnetic symmetries can be gauged by the introduction of a standard coupling between the Penrose current and a 2-form background gauge field
B^{\prime}
,

S_{2}^{d=4}=\int\differential[4]{x}\left(\frac{1}{2}h_{\mu\nu}G^{\mu\nu}+B^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}Y_{+}(K^{\prime})^{\mu\nu}\right)

(64)

where
K^{\prime}
is an
\mathcal{A}
- or
\mathcal{C}
-type CKY tensor.
The background field transforms as

\delta B^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{[\mu}\lambda^{\prime}_{\nu]}

(65)

This is a gauge invariance of
S^{d=4}_{2}
since
\partial_{\mu}Y_{+}(K^{\prime})^{\mu\nu}=0
even off-shell.

7.3
Gauging multiple
(d-3)
-form symmetries

In section

\mathcal{B}
- and
\mathcal{D}

It is straightforward to see that the same is true of the magnetic symmetries introduced above.

Let us first consider

\mathcal{C}

8.3
Significance of the anomalies for linearised gravity

We have seen that a pair of dual generalised symmetries of the linearised graviton theory have mixed anomalies. This has been analysed as an obstruction to simultaneously gauging the dual symmetries. We now discuss the implications of this for the original (ungauged) linearised graviton theory. The arguments are a straightforward extension of those in

[
4
,
30
]
and can be summarized as follows.

In the ungauged theory, there is a current corresponding to each of the two dual symmetries. Of particular interest is the 2-point correlation function of these two currents. The anomaly leads to the 2-point function satisfying an anomalous Ward identity, corresponding to one of the two currents no longer being conserved. This in turn fixes the singular terms in the correlator. For any theory which has currents with such a correlator, the singular terms should be invariant under renormalisation group flow. In particular, the massless pole indicates the existence of a Goldstone mode.

Discussion & outlook

The higher-form global symmetries of the graviton theory differ in several interesting ways from those of
p
-form gauge theory which, starting with
[
2
]
, have been very well explored in the literature.

Firstly, the structure of the 1-form and
(d-3)
-form symmetries of the graviton is closely tied to the properties of conformal Killing-Yano tensors.
The electric 1-form symmetries of the graviton can be interpreted as stemming from Noether currents for particular global shift symmetries of the graviton field, while the magnetic
(d-3)
-form symmetries are not related to any transformation of the graviton. The electric symmetries were gauged via the Noether coupling to a 2-form background gauge field,
B
, and finding counter-terms quadratic in
B
.
The magnetic symmetries were gauged instead by coupling the conserved current to a
(d-2)
-form background gauge field.

The electric symmetries do not have ’t Hooft anomalies, nor are there any mixed ’t Hooft anomalies between them. The same is true of the magnetic symmetries. However, there is an interesting set of mixed ’t Hooft anomalies between certain pairs of electric and magnetic symmetries.
We found the full set of anomalies and showed how they can be described by descent equations, discussing the cases
d=4
and
d>4
separately.
Our results agree with indirect evidence found in
[
6
]
where the commutation relations between the charges generating the higher-form symmetries were studied.

It is interesting to contrast the higher-form symmetry structure found in linear gravity with that of Maxwell theory. In Maxwell theory, the 1-form symmetry stems from the conservation of
F=\differential{A}
. The field equation and Bianchi identity are, respectively,

\differential^{\dagger}F=0,\quad\differential{F}=0

(101)

In contrast, the 1-form symmetries of the graviton are generated by the Penrose 2-form
Y(K)
where
K
is a CKY 2-form. In particular, this requires the existence of CKY tensors on the background spacetime, which here is Minkowski space. As pointed out in
[
4
,
11
]
, the linearised Riemann tensor itself
R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
can be thought of as a conserved current since it follows from the Bianchi identity
\partial_{[\mu}R_{\nu\rho]\alpha\beta}=0
that
\partial^{\mu}R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=0
on-shell. In the language of
[
31
]
,
R
is a [2,2] bi-form.
Then each CKY tensor
K
can then be thought of providing a projection from the [2,2] current
R
to the 2-form current
Y_{+}(K)
. In other words, the different CKY tensors
K
give different ways to isolate a spin-1 substructure within the spin-2 structure encoded in the Riemann tensor
R
.

Furthermore, for some CKY tensors,
Y(K)
is both co-closed and closed on-shell. In
d=4
this is the case for all CKY tensors, while in
d>4
only the closed CKY tensors have this property. Of those, only the
\mathcal{B}
-type tensors give rise to non-vanishing Penrose charges
Q(K)
and dual Penrose charges
\tilde{Q}(K)
(see sections
3
and
7
). If
K
is any one of these CKY tensors, then
F=Y(K)
solves both of the Maxwell equations (
101
) on-shell. This gives a map from solutions of the graviton theory to solutions of Maxwell theory. More specifically, consider a solution of the graviton field equation and Bianchi identities

R^{{{\mu}\mathchoice{\makebox[13.02058pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[13.02058pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[7.90637pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[5.64738pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}_{{\mathchoice{\makebox[4.86232pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.86232pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.95248pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[2.10892pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{\nu\mu\rho}}}=0,\quad R_{[\mu\nu\rho]\sigma}=0,\quad\partial_{[\mu}R_{\nu\rho]\alpha\beta}=0

(102)

Then

gives a map

from solutions of the linearised gravity theory to solutions of Maxwell theory. One can verify, for example, that the linearised Schwarzschild solution maps to a Coulomb potential in this manner, which is somewhat reminiscent of the Kerr-Schild
[
32
]
and, more generally, Weyl double copies
[
33
]
. It would be interesting to study whether this behaviour can generalise to other background spacetimes.

The graviton theory also has a dual formulation
[
13
]
in terms of a tensor gauge field in the
[d-3,1]
representation of
GL(d,\mathbb{R})
. The dual description must have the same global symmetries as the original formulation of the theory. The mapping of the electric and magnetic symmetries to their counterparts in the dual theory is an interesting correspondence which we will return to in upcoming work.
The duality between the higher-form symmetries in the graviton theory is far richer than in pure
p
-form gauge theory where there is a single pair of electric and magnetic charges which are interchanged under duality.
Furthermore, it would be interesting to study the generalisation of the results found here to gauge fields in arbitrary representations of
GL(d,\mathbb{R})
, in particular fully symmetric higher-spin gauge fields.

Maxwell theory in four dimensions has ‘duality defects’
[
34
,
35
]
, which are non-invertible topological operators (see also
[
36
]
, and
[
37
]
for a review).
At particular values of the coupling, gauging a
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
subgroup of the electric 1-form symmetry and applying an S-duality transformation maps Maxwell theory back to itself. The defect is constructed by performing this operation in half of spacetime, which then defines a defect at the boundary between the two halves. An analogous construction is possible for a compact boson in two dimensions for particular values of the radius
[
38
,
34
]
. In that case, the fusion rules of the duality defect and the generator of the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
subgroup of the 1-form symmetry are then given by the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
Tambara-Yamagami category
[
39
]
. (See
[
40
]
for a recent generalisation of this construction to non-invertible defects at any radius of the compact boson). Given the rich structure of 1-form symmetries of the 4-dimensional graviton theory found here, it is natural to ask whether such duality defects arise also in the spin-2 case. We hope to return to this question in future work.

Finally, while the linear theory of the graviton has a surprisingly rich symmetry structure, it is important to understand how much of it carries over to Einstein gravity.
The relation between transformations of the form (
13
) and conformal rescalings has been explored for the non-linear Einstein-Hilbert action in
[
41
]
, with some extra constraints on
\lambda
and
k
being necessary for the transformation to be a symmetry of the non-linear action.
Only certain rather special spacetimes admit conformal Killing-Yano tensors and for these there are analogues of the symmetries considered here.
General spacetimes admit no CKY tensors and so have no generalised symmetries of the kind considered here, but on spacetimes that are asymptotic to ones admitting such tensors there can be asymptotic generalised symmetries.
We will return these and related questions in future work.

will be symmetric under this exchange if and only if
s(k_{I},B^{I},k_{J},B^{J})
is symmetric, since both
S_{0}
and
s(k_{I},B^{I})+s(k_{J},B^{J})
are symmetric individually.
Let us consider how each term of
s(k_{I},B^{I},k_{J},B^{J})
in (
199
) transforms under the exchange (
209
).
The two terms in the first integral in (
199
) are both invariant under (
209
). This can be seen straightforwardly from the symmetries of
\delta^{\alpha\beta\gamma}_{\mu\nu\rho}
and the fact that
\eta_{[\mu\nu\rho|\alpha]\beta\gamma}=0
. The second integral in (
199
) is also invariant under (
209
) as the two terms in the integrand transform into each other. The same is true of the third integral. This leaves the final term in (
199
),

\delta^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}_{\rho\sigma\gamma\delta}B^{I}_{\alpha\beta}B^{J}_{\rho\sigma}\left(\frac{2}{3}\tilde{K}_{J}^{\rho\sigma\gamma}k_{I}^{\delta}-K_{J}^{\rho\sigma}\partial^{\gamma}k_{I}^{\delta}\right)

(210)

which is not manifestly symmetric under (
209
). However, we can verify that for each possible choice of
k_{I}
and
k_{J}
that it is invariant under interchange. There are three independent cases to check.

Case 1:
k_{I}
and
k_{J}
constant.

When
k_{I}
and
k_{J}
are constant, the CKY tensors
K_{I}
and
K_{J}
which relate to them as in (
28
) are both
\mathcal{B}
-type, and so we have
\tilde{K}_{I}=\tilde{K}_{J}=0
. Therefore, (
210
) vanishes in this simple case, and so is clearly invariant under the interchange (
209
).

Case 2:
k_{I}
constant and
k_{J}
not constant.

In this case,
k_{I}
corresponds to a
\mathcal{B}
-type CKY tensor
K_{I}
and
k_{J}
corresponds to a
\mathcal{D}
-type CKY tensor
K_{J}
. They can be parameterised as in (
190
) and satisfy the relations in (
192
). In this case, (
210
) evaluates to

\delta^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}_{\rho\sigma\gamma\delta}B^{I}_{\alpha\beta}B^{J}_{\rho\sigma}\left(\frac{2}{3}\tilde{K}_{J}^{\rho\sigma\gamma}k_{I}^{\delta}-K_{J}^{\rho\sigma}\partial^{\gamma}k_{I}^{\delta}\right)=-2(d-3)\delta^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}_{\rho\sigma\gamma\delta}B^{I}_{\alpha\beta}B^{J}_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{D}_{J}^{\rho\sigma}x^{\gamma}\mathcal{B}_{I}^{\delta}

(211)

After the interchange (
209
), this evaluates to

\delta^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}_{\rho\sigma\gamma\delta}B^{J}_{\alpha\beta}B^{I}_{\rho\sigma}\left(\frac{2}{3}\tilde{K}_{I}^{\rho\sigma\gamma}k_{J}^{\delta}-K_{I}^{\rho\sigma}\partial^{\gamma}k_{J}^{\delta}\right)=-2(d-3)\delta^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}_{\rho\sigma\gamma\delta}B^{I}_{\alpha\beta}B^{J}_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{D}_{J}^{\rho\sigma}x^{\gamma}\mathcal{B}_{I}^{\delta}

(212)

which agrees with (
211
). Therefore, (
210
) is invariant under (
209
) in this case.

Case 3:
k_{I}
and
k_{J}
not constant.

In this case, both
k_{I}
and
k_{J}
correspond to
\mathcal{D}
-type CKY tensors
K_{I}
and
K_{J}
which can be parameterised as in (
193
). This implies the relations (
194
) for
K_{I}
and there are analogous relations for
K_{J}
. Substituting these into (
210
), we find

\delta^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}_{\rho\sigma\gamma\delta}B^{I}_{\alpha\beta}B^{J}_{\rho\sigma}\left(\frac{2}{3}\tilde{K}_{J}^{\rho\sigma\gamma}k_{I}^{\delta}-K_{J}^{\rho\sigma}\partial^{\gamma}k_{I}^{\delta}\right)=(d-3)\delta^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}_{\rho\sigma\gamma\delta}B^{I}_{\alpha\beta}B^{J}_{\rho\sigma}\left(10\mathcal{D}_{J}^{[\rho\sigma}\mathcal{D}_{I}^{\gamma\delta}x^{\lambda]}x_{\lambda}-\mathcal{D}_{J}^{\rho\sigma}\mathcal{D}_{I}^{\gamma\delta}x^{2}\right)

(213)

which is manifestly invariant under exchanging
B^{I}\leftrightarrow B^{J}
and
\mathcal{D}_{I}\leftrightarrow\mathcal{D}_{J}
(which is equivalent to
k_{I}\leftrightarrow k_{J}
in this case). Therefore, (
210

209

In summary,
s(k_{I},B^{I},k_{J},B^{J})
is invariant under exchange (
209
) in all cases. Therefore, the action
S_{1}^{\prime\prime}
in (
200
) is simultaneously invariant under both background transformations (
173
) and (
174
) and is also symmetric under exchange of the Killing vectors and background fields (

Appendix C
Mixed ’t Hooft anomalies between electric and magnetic symmetries

In this appendix, we derive the precise form of the mixed ’t Hooft anomalies between the electric 1-form and magnetic
(d-3)
-form symmetries of the graviton theory.

C.1
Four dimensions

First, we consider the case of four dimensions, as studied in section
8.1
.
There, we derived an action
S_{6}^{d=4}
which is invariant under (
33
) and whose variation under (
65
) is given by (
81
). Recall that in four dimensions, the electric symmetries are labelled by Killing vectors,
k
, and the magnetic symmetries are labelled by
\mathcal{A}
- and
\mathcal{C}
-type CKY tensors,
K^{\prime}
. We now consider the variation (
81
) for each choice of
k
and
K^{\prime}
and show that it vanishes in some (but not all) cases. When it does not vanish and it is not possible to cancel the variation without spoiling invariance under (
33
) then there is a mixed ’t Hooft anomaly between the electric and magnetic symmetries.

Case 1:
k
constant,
K^{\prime}

\mathcal{A}
-type.

If
k
is a constant Killing vector, and
K^{\prime}
is an
\mathcal{A}
-type CKY tensor (i.e. a constant 2-form), then each term in the variation (
81
) vanishes. Therefore, the action
S_{6}^{d=4}
given in (
79
) is simultaneously invariant under both (
33
) and (
65
) in this simple case.

Case 2:
k
not constant,
K^{\prime}

\mathcal{A}
-type.

The next simplest case to consider is when
K^{\prime}
is an
\mathcal{A}
-type CKY tensor (i.e. a constant 2-form) but
k
is not a constant Killing vector (i.e. it is a rotational Killing vector in (
21
)). We parameterise them by

\begin{split}k_{\mu}&=2(d-3)\mathcal{D}_{\mu\nu}x^{\nu}\\
K^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}&=\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}\end{split}

(214)

In this case, the variation (
81
) becomes

\delta S_{6}^{d=4}=6\int\differential[4]{x}\delta B^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}\delta^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}_{\rho\sigma\gamma\delta}B_{\alpha\beta}{K^{\prime}}^{\rho\sigma}\partial^{\gamma}k^{\delta}

(215)

It follows from (
214
) that in this case
K^{\prime}_{[\rho\sigma}\partial_{\gamma}k_{\delta]}
is a constant 4-form. Therefore, in four dimensions it can be written

K^{\prime}_{[\rho\sigma}\partial_{\gamma}k_{\delta]}=c_{0}\epsilon_{\rho\sigma\gamma\delta}

(216)

where

c_{0}\equiv-\frac{1}{4!}\epsilon_{\rho\sigma\gamma\delta}K^{\prime\rho\sigma}\partial^{\gamma}k^{\delta}=\frac{d-3}{12}\epsilon_{\rho\sigma\gamma\delta}\mathcal{A}^{\prime\rho\sigma}\mathcal{D}^{\gamma\delta}

(217)

is a constant. Substituting (
216
) into (
215
), the variation can be written

\delta S_{6}^{d=4}=-24c_{0}\int\delta B^{\prime}\wedge B

(218)

which is of the form (
82
) given in the main text.

Case 3:
k
constant,
K^{\prime}

\mathcal{C}
-type.

We now consider the case with a constant Killing vector
k
but a
\mathcal{C}
-type CKY tensor
K^{\prime}
. These are parameterised by

\begin{split}k_{\mu}&=-(d-3)\mathcal{B}_{\mu}\\
K^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}&=\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\mu\nu\rho}x^{\rho}\end{split}

(219)

In this case, the variation (
81
) simplifies to

\delta S_{6}^{d=4}=-4\int\differential[4]{x}\delta B^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}\delta^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}_{\rho\sigma\gamma\delta}B_{\alpha\beta}\tilde{K^{\prime}}^{\rho\sigma\gamma}k^{\delta}

(220)

In this case, the parameterisation (
219
) implies that
\tilde{K^{\prime}}^{[\rho\sigma\gamma}k^{\delta]}
is a constant 4-form and so, in four dimensions, can be written

\tilde{K^{\prime}}^{[\rho\sigma\gamma}k^{\delta]}=c_{1}\epsilon^{\rho\sigma\gamma\delta}

(221)

where

c_{1}\equiv-\frac{1}{4!}\epsilon_{\rho\sigma\gamma\delta}\tilde{K^{\prime}}^{\rho\sigma\gamma}k^{\delta}=\frac{d-3}{4!}\epsilon_{\rho\sigma\gamma\delta}\mathcal{C}^{\prime\rho\sigma\gamma}\mathcal{B}^{\delta}

(222)

is a constant. Inserting this in (
220
) means the variation can be written

\delta S_{6}^{d=4}=16c_{1}\int\delta B^{\prime}\wedge B

(223)

which is also of the form (
82
) given in the main text.

Case 4:
k
not constant,
K^{\prime}

\mathcal{C}
-type.

The final case to consider is where
k
is a non-constant Killing vector and
K^{\prime}
is a
\mathcal{C}
-type CKY tensor, parameterised by

\begin{split}k_{\mu}&=2(d-3)\mathcal{D}_{\mu\nu}x^{\nu}\\
K^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}&=\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\mu\nu\rho}x^{\rho}\end{split}

(224)

Using this, we find that the variation (
81
) is proportional to

\displaystyle\delta^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}_{\rho\sigma\gamma\delta}\left(K^{\prime\rho\sigma}\partial^{\gamma}k^{\delta}-\frac{2}{3}\tilde{K}^{\prime\rho\sigma\gamma}k^{\delta}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{d-3}{3}\delta^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}_{\rho\sigma\gamma\delta}\left(6\mathcal{C}^{\prime\lambda\rho\sigma}\mathcal{D}^{\gamma\delta}+4\mathcal{C}^{\prime\rho\sigma\gamma}\mathcal{D}^{\delta\lambda}\right)x_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\frac{10(d-3)}{3}\delta^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}_{\rho\sigma\gamma\delta}\mathcal{C}^{\prime[\lambda\rho\sigma}\mathcal{D}^{\gamma\delta]}x_{\lambda}=0

where the result vanishes as the 5-form
\mathcal{C}^{\prime[\lambda\rho\sigma}\mathcal{D}^{\gamma\delta]}
vanishes identically in four dimensions. Therefore, in this case the variation vanishes

\delta S_{6}^{d=4}=0

(226)

In summary, there are mixed ’t Hooft anomalies between several pairs of electric and magnetic 1-form symmetries of the four-dimensional graviton theory. The structure of these anomalies are summarised in Table

Minimising Length of Closed Billiard Trajectories on Hyperbolic Polygons
John Parker, Manvendra Somvanshi

Abstract
In a hyperbolic polygon any finite collection of closed billiard trajectories can be assigned an average length function. In this paper, we consider the average length of the collection of cyclically related closed billiard trajectories in even-sided right-angled polygons and the collection of reflectively related closed billiard trajectories in Lambert quadrilaterals with acute angle π / k \pi/k . We show that in the former case the average length is minimised by the regular even-sided right-angled polygon, and in the latter case it is minimised by the Lambert quadrilateral with a reflective symmetry about its long axis. We use techniques from Teichmüller theory to prove the main theorems.

Introduction

Let
P\subset\mathbb{D}^{2}
be a hyperbolic polygon. We will consider two cases, namely when
P
has all angles equal and when
P
is a Lambert quadrilateral. A billiard trajectory on
P
is the path traced by a point satisfying the following rules: the point moves along geodesics in the polygon and whenever the geodesic path meets an edge of the polygon, it is reflected along a geodesic so that both geodesics make the same angle with that edge. A billiard path is said to be closed if the point returns back to its starting position with the same direction in finite time. In other words, a (closed) billiard trajectory is a (closed) piecewise geodesic
\gamma
in
P
with all vertices of
\gamma
lying on the edges of
P
such that
\gamma
makes the same angle on either side with the edge at each vertex.

First, consider the case where all the angles of
P
are equal. If one labels the sides of
P
with
1,\cdots,k
in the anticlockwise direction, then every billiard trajectory
\gamma
can be assigned a code by noting the number
a_{i}
assigned to the edges of
P
which the trajectory
\gamma
hits in order. We call the code
\mathbf{a}=(a_{0},\cdots,a_{n-1})
associated with
\gamma
the
billiard sequence
of
\gamma
. Unlike in Euclidean geometry, a billiard sequence uniquely determines the hyperbolic billiard trajectory
\gamma
. Two billiard trajectories are said to be
cyclically related
in
P
if there are two anticlockwise labels of
P
in which both trajectories have the same billiard sequence.

Let
\gamma_{\mathbf{a},P}=\{\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n}\}
be the collection of all billiard trajectories that are cyclically related to the billiard sequence
\mathbf{a}
. A collection of closed billiard trajectories is said to
fill
the polygon
P
if its complement in
P
is a union of topological discs each of whose whose boundary is made up of either (a) segments of geodesics of the billiard trajectories, (b) geodesic segments of the billiard trajectories and one edge of
P
including both its vertices, or (c) geodesic segments of the billiard trajectories, two adjacent edges of
P
, and the common vertex of those edges but not containing the other vertices of either edge. In
[
PPS18
]
the following useful result is proved.

Proposition 1.1

(
[
PPS18
]
)
.

Let
P_{0}
be a regular hyperbolic
k
-gon and let
\rho
denote the anti-clockwise rotation through angle
2\pi/k
about the center of
P_{0}
. Let
\gamma_{0}
be a closed billiard trajectory in
P_{0}
and let
\gamma_{i}=\rho^{i}(\gamma_{0})
for
i=1,\cdots,k-1
. Then
\bigcup_{i}\gamma_{i}
fills
P_{0}
.

The average length

of
\gamma_{\mathbf{a},P}
is well defined as the average of the hyperbolic lengths of
\{\gamma_{i}\}
. The authors of
[
CPS11
]

Theorem 1.2

(
[

In anti-clockwise labeled ideal hyperbolic polygons with

In this paper, we prove a similar result for the case where

is even sided and each internal angle is
\pi/2
. The main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

In anti-clockwise labeled, right-angled, even-sided hyperbolic polygons, the average length function of any family of cyclically related closed billiard trajectories corresponding to a given billiard sequence is uniquely minimised by the regular polygon.

The proof of this result closely follows the proof of Theorem
1.2
given in
[
PPS18
]
. The idea of the proof is as follows. We first observe that by gluing together four isometric copies of the
2k-
gon
P
together, one can construct a closed Riemann surface of genus

, which we call the
billiard surface
,
S_{P}
, corresponding to

. Any closed billiard trajectory
\gamma

The space of all billiard surfaces constructed from

. We endow the Teichmüller space with the Weil-Petersson metric. We show that the billiard space is exactly the fixed-point set of an order 2 Weil-Petersson isometry.
This also leads to the conclusion that the billiard space is a geodesically convex subspace of the Teichmüller space. Then, using results regarding the convexity of the length function of closed geodesics under the Weil-Petersson metric in
[
Wol87
]
and Kerckhoff’s result about the length function of filling systems in
[
Ker83
]
we show that there is a unique point where the global minimum is obtained. This global minimum is then identified as the regular right-angled polygon using the rotational symmetry of the regular polygon.

and

The billiard surface

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\gamma_{\mathbf{a},P}
be as described above. The number of closed geodesic curves in the lift
\pi_{P}^{-1}(\gamma_{i})
of the billiard trajectory
\gamma_{i}\in\gamma_{\mathbf{a},P}
is
1,2,
or
4
.

Proof.

Let the billiard sequence corresponding to
\gamma_{i}
be
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})
. We use the following convention to construct closed curves in the lift of
\gamma_{i}
: We start in the polygon
P_{1}
and trace out the billiard trajectory using the billiard sequence. Each

corresponds to a “transition” from some

to some
P_{j}
. After going through all
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})
suppose that the trajectory ends in some
P_{j}
. In the case
P_{j}=P_{1}
the curve closes up to form a closed geodesic
\tilde{\gamma}_{i}
. Using the isometries we get at most four lifts in total:
\tilde{\gamma}_{i},J_{P}(\tilde{\gamma}_{i}),K_{P}(\tilde{\gamma}_{i}),J_{P}K_{P}(\tilde{\gamma}_{i})
. Note here that
3
lifts is not possible since, if
\gamma_{i}=I(\gamma_{i})
for

then it is easy to show that
I^{\prime},I^{\prime\prime}\in\{J_{P},K_{P},J_{P}K_{P}\}-\{I\}
are such that
I^{\prime}(\gamma_{i})=I^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{i})
. Thus the number of lifts here is
1,2
, or
4
.

Now suppose that
J_{i}\neq J_{1}
. One of the order 2 maps
J_{P},K_{P},J_{P}K_{P}
interchanges
P_{1}
and
P_{j}
, call this isometry

. Using the isometry

twice on

we get a closed curve

. Again, using the isometries

we obtain other lifts of

, but since

∎

Remark 2.2
.

In the genus

Teichmüller Space and the Billiard Space

Let
P_{0}\subset\mathbb{D}^{2}
be the unique regular right-angled
2k-
gon and
R_{0}
be the corresponding billiard surface. By construction, there is a curve system
\Gamma_{0}
on
R_{0}
, as described in the previous section. By definition, a regular polygon has a rotational symmetry, i.e. the rotation,
\rho

is holomorphic transformation of

. This induces a holomorphic automorphism of the surface
R_{0}
, which we also denote by
\rho

J_{0}:R_{0}\to R_{0}
and
F_{0}:R_{0}\to R_{0}
be the order 2 isometries induced from the permutation of the polygons as in the previous section.

Definition 3.1
.

A marking of a Riemann surface

is an orientation preserving homeomorphism

. Two markings

and
(S^{\prime},f^{\prime})

is the collection of marked surfaces.

The Teichmüller space

has a natural complex structure on it. Given any orientation preserving homeomorphism
\varphi:R_{0}\to R_{0}
it induces a map
\varphi_{*}:T(R_{0})\to T(R_{0})
which maps
[S,f]\mapsto[S,f\circ\varphi]
. If
S
is a Riemann surface then let
S^{*}
be the same surface as

but with opposite orientation and let
\iota:S\to S^{*}
be the identity map which reverses the orientation of
S
.

Definition 3.2
.

Define the following two maps
\mathcal{J},\mathcal{F}:T(R_{0})\to T(R_{0})
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}([S,f:R_{0}\to S])=(S^{*},\iota fJ_{0}:R_{0}\to S^{*}),

(1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}([S,f:R_{0}\to S])=(S,fF_{0}:R_{0}\to S)

(2)

The even sided right-angled
2k-
gon

, where

, can always be decomposed into right-angled hexagons using the following unique orthogonal geodesics between the blue edges:
(1,5)
,
(1,7)
,
\cdots
,

. This gives

which we color green. Following the construction of

, these green geodesic arcs form
2(k-3)
simple closed geodesics on

which we label
\delta_{1},\cdots,\delta_{k-3}

\delta^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,\delta^{\prime}_{k-3}

\displaystyle\omega_{WP}=\sum_{j=1}^{3k-6}\text{d}\tau_{j}\wedge\text{d}\ell_{j}

The Weil-Petersson form induces a Riemannian metric,

Definition 3.3
.

The billiard space,

, is a subset of the Teichmüller space,

, where

.

Remark 3.4
.

There are quasi-conformal homeomorphisms between the right-angled hexagons in
R_{0}-(\Gamma_{0}\cup\mathcal{P}_{0})
and the right-angled hexagons in
S_{P}-(\Gamma_{P}\cup\mathcal{P}_{P})
, so that the edges and vertices map to the edges and vertices of the corresponding hexagons. These maps can be chosen such that upon gluing back they induce a quasi-conformal map

which maps the graph
\Gamma_{0}\cup\mathcal{P}_{0}
to the graph
\Gamma_{P}\cup\mathcal{P}_{P}
, preserving edges and vertices. This results in a marked surface

. It is clear from this construction that
[S_{P},f_{P}]\in B(R_{0})
.

Proposition 3.5
.

The subspace
B(R_{0})
is exactly the simultaneous fixed points of
\mathcal{J}
and
\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

Let
[S,f]\in B(R_{0})
. Then
f(\mathcal{P}_{0})
is a pants decomposition of
[S,f]
and
fF_{0}(\mathcal{P}_{0})
is a pants decomposition of
[S,fF_{0}]
. The Fenchel-Nielsen coordinates with respect to
\mathcal{P}_{0}
of
[S,f]
is so that all the twists parameters are zero and
\ell(f(\delta_{i}))=\ell(f(\delta^{\prime}_{i}))
. Note that
F_{0}(\alpha)=\alpha
for any curve
\alpha\in\Gamma_{0}
but with orientation reversed and
F_{0}(\delta_{i})=\delta^{\prime}_{i}
. Thus, all the twist parameters of
[S,fF_{0}]
are zero. Since all lengths and twists are preserved we conclude that
[S,f]=[S,fF_{0}]
.

Similarly to the proof above, it can be shown that

as

fixes every curve in the pants decomposition

\mathcal{P}_{0}
but with reversed orientation.

Suppose that
x=[S,f]\in\text{Fix}(\mathcal{F})\cap\text{Fix}(\mathcal{J})
. As
\mathcal{F}
fixes

it follows that
\tau_{x}(\alpha_{i})=0
,
\tau_{x}(\delta_{i})=-\tau_{x}(\delta^{\prime}_{i})
, and
\ell(f(\delta_{i}))=\ell(f(\delta^{\prime}_{i}))
, since

reverses the orientation of all

and maps

to
\delta^{\prime}_{i}
. As
\mathcal{J}
fixes

it follows that
\tau_{x}(\delta_{i})=\tau_{x}(\delta^{\prime}_{i})=0

preserves all

. Thus, we have that

. This completes this proof.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let

be a marked surface. Then

if and only if

.

The converse is true from the discussion in Remark

. Then from

is fixed by

which means that

and

\iota fJ_{0}f^{-1}
are homotopic to conformal maps

respectively. The maps

,

and

preserve each curve in

while
permuting the four even sided polygon faces in

, implying that they are all isometric to an even sided hyperbolic polygon

.
Since the edges of these faces glue together in pairs to form

and the four polygons meet at each vertex in

, it follows that
all the angles of the polygon

are right angles. Thus

is isometric to the surface
S_{P}
via some isometry

and the curve system

\Gamma_{P}=If(\Gamma_{0})
. This means that both

and

have the same action on the graph

. Since

satisfies the

is homotopic to
f_{P}
. Thus
[S,f]=[S_{P},f_{P}]
.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

B(R_{0})
is a convex subspace of
T(R_{0})
. Moreover,
B(R_{0})
is an isotropic submanifold of
(T(R_{0}),\omega_{WP})
.

Proof.

The maps

and

are an isometry of

as they are induced from the action of the (extended) mapping classes

is exactly the intersection of the fixed

can be

but since the metric is uniquely geodesic

.
This proves

The geodesic length function

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\tilde{\gamma}=\{\tilde{\gamma}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{\gamma}_{N}\}
be a collection of closed geodesics on
R_{0}
and let
x=[S,f]\in T(R_{0})
be some marked surface. The associated geodesic length function
L_{\tilde{\gamma}}:T(R_{0})\to(0,\infty)
is defined as

L_{\tilde{\gamma}}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\ell_{x}(f(\tilde{\gamma}_{i}))

The following theorem of Wolpert in
[
Wol87
]
states the continuity and convexity properties of the geodesic length function and will be used in proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\tilde{\gamma}\subset R_{0}
be a finite collection of closed geodesics and let
L_{\tilde{\gamma}}
be the associated geodesic length function. Then
L_{\tilde{\gamma}}
is continuous and strictly convex along every Weil-Petersson geodesic.

Let
\mathbf{a}=(a_{0},\cdots,a_{n-1})
be a billiard sequence on the polygon
P
and let
\gamma_{\mathbf{a},P}
be the collection of all cyclically related billiard trajectories with billiard sequence
\mathbf{a}
. If
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\gamma_{\mathbf{a},P_{0}}
are two billiard trajectories which are cyclically related then the number of closed geodesics in the lift of
\gamma^{\prime}
is same as that of
\gamma
since
\gamma^{\prime}=\rho^{\ell}(\gamma)
for some power
\ell
and
\pi_{P_{0}}\rho=\rho\pi_{P_{0}}
. Then using the map
f_{P}:R_{0}\to S_{P}
, it is straightforward to see that any two billiard trajectories in
\gamma_{\mathbf{a},P}
have the same number of closed geodesics in their lifts. Define
\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbf{a},P}
as the collection of closed geodesics in
\pi_{P}^{-1}(\gamma)
for all
\gamma\in\gamma_{\mathbf{a},P}
.
From Proposition
2.1
and the above discussion it is clear that
\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbf{a},P}
contains either
n
,
2n
or
4n
closed geodesics counted with possible repetition.

In the case when the lift of
\gamma\in\gamma_{\mathbf{a},P}
has one closed geodesic in its lift, the length of that geodesic is
4\ell(\gamma)
. In the case when
\gamma
has two closed geodesics in its lift it follows that the length of each of those closed geodesics is
2\ell(\gamma)
. Finally, in the last case, when there are
4
closed geodesics in the lift of
\gamma
, the length of each geodesic is
\ell(\gamma)
. From this it follows that the average length function associated to billiard sequence
\mathbf{a}=(a_{0},\cdots,a_{n-1})
,

\displaystyle L_{\text{avg}}(\mathbf{a},P)=\frac{1}{|\gamma_{\mathbf{a},P}|}\sum_{\gamma\in\gamma_{\mathbf{a},P}}\ell(\gamma)

(4)

can be expressed, in any of the three cases, as

\displaystyle L_{\text{avg}}(\mathbf{a},P)=\frac{1}{4n}L_{\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbf{a},P_{0}}}([S_{P},f_{P}])

(5)

where we have used that
\gamma_{\mathbf{a},P}=f_{P}(\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbf{a},P_{0}})
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\mathbf{a}
be a billiard sequence and
L_{\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbf{a},P_{0}}}
be the geodesic length function associated to
\gamma_{\mathbf{a},P_{0}}
. Then for any
x=[S,f:R_{0}\to S]\in T(R_{0})

\displaystyle L_{\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbf{a},P_{0}}}(x)=L_{\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbf{a},P_{0}}}(\rho(x))=L_{\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbf{a},P_{0}}}(\mathcal{J}(x))=L_{\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbf{a},P_{0}}}(\mathcal{F}(x))

(6)

Proof.

Note that for any billiard sequence
\mathbf{a}
the map
I\in\{\rho,J_{0},F_{0}\}
permutes the closed geodesics in
\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbf{a},P_{0}}
. Let
\mathcal{I}
denote corresponding map induced by
I
. Then,

\displaystyle L_{\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbf{a},P_{0}}}(\mathcal{I}(x))=\sum_{\tilde{\gamma}\in\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbf{a},P_{0}}}\ell(fI(\tilde{\gamma}))=\sum_{\tilde{\gamma}\in\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbf{a},P_{0}}}\ell(f(\tilde{\gamma}))=L_{\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbf{a},P_{0}}}(x)

(7)

Here, the second equality follows from the fact that
I
permutes the closed geodesics. This completes the proof.
∎

5
Proof of Theorem
1.3

Let
\mathbf{a}
be a billiard sequence, then Theorem
1.3
claims that

\displaystyle L_{\text{avg}}(\mathbf{a},P_{0})<L_{\text{avg}}(\mathbf{a},P_{0})

(8)

From
5
it follows that this is equivalent to the claim

\displaystyle L_{\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbf{a},P_{0}}}(x_{0})<L_{\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbf{a},P}}(x)

(9)

In order to prove this, we use the following result from
[
Ker83
]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\tilde{\gamma}
be a finite collection of closed geodesics on
R_{0}
and
L_{\tilde{\gamma}}
be the associated geodesic length function. If
\tilde{\gamma}
is filling, that is
R_{0}-\tilde{\gamma}
is a disjoint collection of discs and once punctured discs, then
L_{\gamma}
is a proper function.

Combining Theorems
4.2
and
5.1
, the following result can be proved:

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
\tilde{\gamma}
be a finite collection of closed geodesics which fills
R_{0}
. Then the associated geodesic length function
L_{\tilde{\gamma}}:T(R_{0})\to(0,\infty)
achieves its global minimum at a unique point
x_{\text{min}}\in T(R_{0})
.

Coming back to the problem at hand, if we can show that the curve system
\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbf{a},P_{0}}
fills
R_{0}
then the geodesic length function,
L_{\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbf{a},P_{0}}}
, assumes a global minimum at a unique point
x_{\text{min}}\in T(R_{0})
. Using Proposition
4.3
, it follows that

L_{\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbf{a},P_{0}}}(x_{\text{min}})=L_{\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbf{a},P_{0}}}(\mathcal{J}(x_{\text{min}}))=L_{\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbf{a},P_{0}}}(\mathcal{F}(x_{\text{min}}))=L_{\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbf{a},P_{0}}}(\rho_{*}(x_{\text{min}}))

Thus, from the uniqueness of the global minimum, it follows that
x_{\text{min}}
is fixed by
\mathcal{J},\mathcal{F},
and
\rho_{*}
. The following two propositions complete the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proposition 5.3
.

The collection
\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbf{a},P_{0}}
of closed geodesics on
R_{0}
is filling.

Proof.

Since the surface
R_{0}
is constructed by gluing together four isometric copies of
P_{0}
we can write:

\displaystyle R_{0}-\bigcup_{\tilde{\gamma}\in\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbf{a},P_{0}}}\tilde{\gamma}=

\displaystyle\left(P_{0}-\bigcup_{\gamma\in\gamma_{\mathbf{a},P_{0}}}\gamma\right)\cup J_{0}\left(P_{0}-\bigcup_{\gamma\in\gamma_{\mathbf{a},P_{0}}}\gamma\right)

\displaystyle\cup F_{0}\left(P_{0}-\bigcup_{\gamma\in\gamma_{\mathbf{a},P_{0}}}\gamma\right)\cup F_{0}J_{0}\left(P_{0}-\bigcup_{\gamma\in\gamma_{\mathbf{a},P_{0}}}\gamma\right)

(10)

From Proposition
1.1
it follows that
\gamma_{\mathbf{a},P_{0}}
fills in
P_{0}
. Thus,
\tilde{\gamma}_{\mathbf{a},P_{0}}
cuts
R_{0}
into discs or once punctured discs.
∎

Proposition 5.4
.

The only simultaneous fixed point of the maps
\mathcal{J},\mathcal{F}
, and
\rho_{*}
is
x_{0}=[R_{0},1_{R_{0}}]
.

Proof.

Suppose that
x\in T(R_{0})
is a fixed point of
\mathcal{F}
,
\mathcal{J}
and
\rho_{*}
. It follows from Proposition
3.5
that
x\in B(R_{0})
, since
x
is fixed by
\mathcal{F}
and

\mathcal{J}
. This means that there is a right-angled even sided polygon
P
such that
x=[S_{P},f_{P}]
and there is a special curve system
\Gamma_{P}
such that
S_{P}-\Gamma_{P}
contains four disjoint copies of
P
.

Being a fixed point of
\rho_{*}
means that
g_{0}=f_{P}\rho f_{P}^{-1}
is homotopic to some isometry
g_{1}:P\to P
. By construction,
f_{P}
maps edges and vertices in
\Gamma_{0}
to the corresponding edges and vertices in
\Gamma_{P}
then
g_{1}
maps the curves
\alpha_{i}
to
\beta_{i+1}
for
i\leq k
,
\alpha_{k+1}
to
\beta_{1}
, and
\beta_{i}
to
\alpha_{i}
. Thus,
g_{1}(P_{1})=P_{i}
for some
i
, but since
P_{2}
,
P_{3}
have opposite orientations to
P_{1}
, we have that either
g_{1}(P_{1})=P_{1}
or
g_{1}(P_{1})=P_{4}
. In the latter case, composing
g_{1}
with the hyperelliptic involution
F_{P}
maps
P_{1}
back to
P_{1}
. Thus, without loss of generality we assume that
g_{1}
preserves
P_{1}
.

Looking only at
P=P_{1}
inside the disc now, we have an isometry
g_{1}:P\to P
which maps each vertex to the next and each side to the next. By the Brouwer fixed point theorem, there is a fixed point
z_{0}
of
g_{1}
in the interior of the polygon. Consider the unique geodesics connecting
z_{0}
and two vertices
v_{i}
and
v_{i+1}
. Then
g_{1}
isometrically maps this triangle to the triangle with vertices
z_{0},v_{i+1},v_{i+2}
. Thus the adjacent side lengths are equal. Hence
P
is regular.
∎

6
Billiards on hyperbolic Lambert quadrilaterals

Let
Q\subset\mathbb{D}^{2}
be a Lambert quadrilateral with the internal acute angle
\pi/k
. Let
Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{2k}
be
2k
copies of
Q
. They can be glued together to get a right-angled
2k-
gon,
P_{Q}
. Note that this is symmetric under rotation by
2\pi/k
. Further, gluing together four copies of
P_{Q}
gives us a surface,
S_{P_{Q}}
, of genus
k-1
as in Section
2
. Note that the billiard surface corresponding to
Q_{0}
is exactly the surface
R_{0}
, as gluing together
Q_{0}
gives the regular right-angled
2k-
gon,
P_{0}
. As before, there is a quasi-conformal map
f_{Q}:R_{0}\to S_{P}
which maps the sides and vertices of the quadrilaterals
Q_{0}
to the sides and vertices of
Q
. Moreover, the billiard space of Lambert quadrilaterals with angle
\pi/k
is exactly the subspace of
B(R_{0})
which is fixed by
\rho_{*}^{2}:T(R_{0})\to T(R_{0})
.

There is a natural projection
\pi_{Q}:P_{Q}\to Q
. Let
\gamma_{\mathbf{a},Q_{0}}
be a pair of reflectively related closed billiard trajectories in
Q_{0}
having the billiard sequence
\mathbf{a}
. Then
\pi_{Q_{0}}^{-1}(\gamma_{\mathbf{a},Q_{0}})
is a collection of closed billiard trajectories
\{\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n}\}
in
P_{0}
. Due to the rotational symmetry of
P_{0}
it follows that the family
\{\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n}\}
are all cyclically related, and moreover, are the
all
the cyclically related billiard trajectories having some billiard sequence
\mathbf{b}
. It follows that for any
Q
a pair of reflectively related closed billiard trajectories,
\gamma_{\mathbf{a},Q}=\{\gamma,\bar{\gamma}\}
, lift to the family of closed billiard trajectories
\gamma_{\mathbf{b},P_{Q}}
having some billiard sequence
\mathbf{b}
. If
\gamma_{\mathbf{a},Q}=\{\gamma,\bar{\gamma}\}
, we define the average length function as

\displaystyle L_{\text{avg}}(\mathbf{a},Q)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\ell(\gamma)+\ell(\bar{\gamma})\right)

(11)

The total length of the closed billiard trajectories in
\gamma_{\mathbf{b},P_{Q}}=\{\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n}\}
will just be
2k(\ell(\gamma)+\ell(\bar{\gamma}))
. Thus it follows that
nL_{\text{avg}}(\mathbf{b},P_{Q})=4kL_{\text{avg}}(\mathbf{a},Q)
. Since, from theorem
1.3
, it follows that
L_{\text{avg}}(\mathbf{b},P)
is uniquely minimized at
P_{0}=P_{Q_{0}}
, and thus
L_{\text{avg}}(\mathbf{a},Q)
is uniquely minimized at
Q_{0}
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

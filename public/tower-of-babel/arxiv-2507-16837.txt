Ramanujan’s oscillator
Gergely Nyitray

Abstract




Abstract

We aim to show that the dimensionless unit angular frequency of a certain mechanical oscillator is realized by a condition involving the Hardy-Ramanujan number 1729. This type of coupled oscillator can be called a Ramanujan’s oscillator.

Introduction

Coupled mechanical systems can be successfully used as project work or sample task in higher level physics education because of their complexity and flexibility. These kinds of problems provide an opportunity to effectively apply various solution methods such as Euler-Lagrange and Hamilton’s equations

Hardy once traveled to the sick Indian mathematician Srinivasa Ramanujan in taxi cab number 1729. Starting in 1914, Hardy was Ramanujan mentor and he was well aware Ramanujan’s extraordinary mathematical skills. At the bedside of his sick colleague unable to come up with any comforting thoughts, Hardy began talking about the taxi number, remarking that it was quite boring. Ramanujan was immediately excited upon hearing this and explained to Hardy why this number was special. Ramanujan pointed out that this is the smallest number representable in two ways as a sum of two cubes

1729=1^{3}+12^{3}=9^{3}+10^{3}

At a given moment, the two fixed points of the spring are slightly compressed horizontally and then released. It is assumed that during the motion neither the rolling bodies nor the slab slip, and the large block slides on the ground without friction. Our goal is to describe the motion of this oscillator.

1
What is the natural frequency of the system?

Under the conditions described above, the system will oscillate in normal mode, therefore let us calculate its angular frequency
\Omega
if all parts of the system have the same mass (
m=m^{\prime}
). Among the different solutions methods, we now present the one based on the Euler-Lagrange equations. The system has three degrees of freedom, since we have three independent coordinates which are
\varphi
,
\phi
and
X
. If a cylindrical body of radius
R
is rolling on a motionless surface without slipping, the instantaneous speed of its centre of mass (CM) is
R\dot{\varphi}
. The instantaneous speed of its highest point is twice as much (
2R\dot{\varphi}
). The orientations of the velocity vectors are parallel to the ground. If the surface is also moving in the opposite direction with instantaneous velocity
\dot{X}
, only velocities relative to the inertial reference frame (IRF) can be written into the Lagrangian as
\left(R\dot{\varphi}-\dot{X}\right)
. So the Lagrangian of the system takes the following form:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{2}m\dot{X}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}m^{\prime}\left(R\dot{\varphi}-\dot{X}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}I_{\text{Hoop}}\,\dot{\varphi}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}m^{\prime}\left(R\dot{\varphi}-\dot{X}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}I^{\prime}_{\text{Cyl}}\,\dot{\varphi}^{2}+\dots

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}m\left(2R\dot{\varphi}-\dot{X}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}m\left(R\dot{\phi}-\dot{X}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}I_{\text{Cyl}}\,\dot{\phi}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}k\left(2R\varphi\right)^{2},

(1)

where
I_{\text{Hoop}}
,
I^{\prime}_{\text{Cyl}}
and
I_{\text{Cyl}}
denote the moment of inertia of the rolling bodies with respect to their symmetry axes:

I_{\text{Hoop}}=m^{\prime}R^{2},\quad I^{\prime}_{\text{Cyl}}=\frac{1}{2}m^{\prime}R^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad I_{\text{Cyl}}=\frac{1}{2}mR^{2}.

The total energy of a rolling body is obtained by adding the kinetic energy of the CM to the rotational energy. Since the deformation of the spring is independent of the overall position
X
of the large block, so its value is determined solely by the instantaneous position of the highest points of the rolling rigid bodies (since the slab does not slip). So the deformation of the spring is
x=2R\varphi
therefore the potential energy is
U=1/2kx^{2}=2kR^{2}\varphi^{2}
.
After combining the same physical quantities into distinct terms the Lagrangian of the system takes the following form:

\mathcal{L}=\frac{5}{2}m\dot{X}^{2}+\frac{3}{4}mR^{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}+\frac{15}{4}mR^{2}\dot{\varphi}^{2}-mR\dot{X}\dot{\phi}-4mR\dot{\varphi}\dot{X}-2kR^{2}{\varphi}^{2}.

(2)

By introducing the Lagrange linear operator as

\Lambda_{i}=\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\dot{q_{i}}}\right)-\frac{\partial}{\partial q_{i}},

(3)

where
q_{i}
denotes the independent coordinates (
X
,
\phi
,
\varphi
) so the relevant Euler-Lagrange equations can be written compactly:

\displaystyle\Lambda_{X}\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\,\rightarrow\,\,5\ddot{X}-R\ddot{\phi}-4R\ddot{\varphi}=0,

(4a)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\phi}\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\,\rightarrow\,\,\frac{3}{2}R\ddot{\phi}-\ddot{X}=0,

(4b)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\varphi}\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\,\rightarrow\,\,\frac{15}{2}mR\ddot{\varphi}-4m\ddot{X}+4kR\varphi=0.

(4c)

Since during the normal mode oscillation all part of the system performs simple harmonic motion, therefore we are seeking its equation of motion in the usual form:

\ddot{q}+\Omega^{2}q=0,

(5)

where
\Omega=2\pi/T

from equations (

\frac{15}{2}\ddot{X}-\ddot{X}=6R\ddot{\varphi}\,\rightarrow\,\ddot{X}=\frac{12}{13}R\ddot{\varphi}.

2
The appearance of the number 1729

Dimensionless unit angular frequency can be achieved by introducing mass differences between the elements that forming the system. More specifically the masses of the rigid bodies rolling in pairs, denoted by
m^{\prime}
and the masses
m
of the other elements of the system are considered to be different. Then it is assumed that the unit angular frequency can be achieved by proper choice of the
m^{\prime}/m
ratio. Taking into account the differences in mass the Lagrangian of the re-parameterized system is slightly different from Eq. (
2

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\frac{3m+2m^{\prime}}{2}\dot{X}^{2}+\frac{3}{4}mR^{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}+\left(\frac{7}{4}m^{\prime}+2m\right)R^{2}\dot{\varphi}^{2}\dots

\displaystyle-mR\dot{X}\dot{\phi}-2\left(m^{\prime}+m\right)R\dot{\varphi}\dot{X}-2kR^{2}{\varphi}^{2}.

(9)

The relevant Euler-Lagrange equations are:

\displaystyle\Lambda_{X}\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\,\rightarrow\,\,\left(3m+2m^{\prime}\right)\ddot{X}-mR\ddot{\phi}-2\left(m+m^{\prime}\right)R\ddot{\varphi}=0,

(10a)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\phi}\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\,\rightarrow\,\,\frac{3}{2}R\ddot{\phi}-\ddot{X}=0,

(10b)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\varphi}\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\,\rightarrow\,\,\left(\frac{7}{2}m^{\prime}+4m\right)R\ddot{\varphi}-2\left(m+m^{\prime}\right)\ddot{X}+4kR\varphi=0.

(10c)

Substituting

\displaystyle\ddot{X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\left(m^{\prime}+m\right)}{2m^{\prime}+2m+\frac{m}{3}}R\ddot{\varphi},\quad\text{and}

(11a)

\displaystyle\ddot{\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4\left(m^{\prime}+m\right)}{6m^{\prime}+7m}\ddot{\varphi}.

After substituting
\ddot{X}

\ddot{\varphi}+\left(\frac{8k}{7m^{\prime}+8m-\frac{8\left(m^{\prime}+m\right)^{2}}{\rule[-5.69054pt]{0.0pt}{14.0pt}\frac{7m}{3}+2m^{\prime}}}\right)\varphi=0.

It is easy to see that if the first and third terms in the denominator of
\Omega^{2}

\displaystyle 7m^{\prime}-\frac{8\left(m^{\prime}+m\right)^{2}}{\rule[-5.69054pt]{0.0pt}{14.0pt}\frac{7m}{3}+2m^{\prime}}=0\,\rightarrow\,18{\varepsilon}^{2}+{\varepsilon}-24=0,

\sqrt{1-4\cdot 18\cdot(-24)}=\sqrt{1^{3}+12^{3}}

3
Relationship between the natural frequencies

The natural frequencies of the three modes can be easily determined.
The relationship between the independent variables are
\ddot{X}=c_{1}\ddot{\varphi}
,
\ddot{X}=c_{2}\ddot{\phi}
and
\ddot{\varphi}=\left(c_{2}/c_{1}\right)\ddot{\phi}
, where the constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
can be identified based on Eqs. (
11a
) and (
11b
).
Let us consider the equation of motion of the system (
\ddot{\varphi}+\Omega_{\varphi}^{2}\,\varphi=0
) and replace the variable
\varphi
by the variable
X
and
\phi
taking into account that
\varphi=X/c_{1}
and
\varphi=\left(c_{2}/c_{1}\right)\phi

\displaystyle\ddot{X}c_{1}^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Omega_{\varphi}^{2}\,\left(Xc_{1}^{-1}\right)\,\rightarrow\,\ddot{X}=\Omega_{\varphi}^{2}\,X\,\rightarrow\,\Omega_{X}=\Omega_{\varphi},

(16a)

\displaystyle\ddot{\phi}\,c_{2}c_{1}^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Omega_{\varphi}^{2}\,\left(\phi\,c_{2}c_{1}^{-1}\right)\,\rightarrow\,\ddot{\phi}=\Omega_{\varphi}^{2}\,\phi\,\,\rightarrow\,\Omega_{\phi}=\Omega_{\varphi}.

(16b)

Hence all three modes have the same natural frequency:

\Omega_{X}=\Omega_{\phi}=\Omega_{\varphi}.

4
Conclusion

It has been shown that the dimensionless unit angular frequency of a certain type of coupled oscillator is realized by a condition that includes the Hardy-Ramanujan number. First, the natural frequency of the system was calculated by considering the mass of all elements to be the same. In this case the calculated dimensionless natural frequency squared turned out to be close to unity. We obtained exactly unit value by slightly increasing the masses of the rigid bodies rolling in pairs relative to the other elements of the system. Since this ratio can only be expressed in terms of the Hardy-Ramanujan number this gives opportunity for its dynamic interpretation. It is possible that there is a number theoretic reason for the appearance of the number 1729, although this remained hidden from the author for the moment.

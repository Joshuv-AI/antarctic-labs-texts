First-year physical function trajectories and adverse outcomes in incident peritoneal dialysis: a prospective cohort protocol
Authors: Liuyan Huang, Fan Zhang, Qing Xu, Ying Jia, Rui Zhou, Jing Liao, Tianyi Cheng, Yifei Zhong, Zilin Ma, Jiao Li

ABSTRACT

Introduction

Peritoneal dialysis (PD) is a cornerstone renal replacement therapy for end-stage kidney disease (ESKD), in many settings, comparable survival to hemodialysis when appropriately selected and supported patients initiate PD early in the disease course [1, 2]. Despite these benefits, impaired physical function is common in chronic kidney disease (CKD) and among patients receiving dialysis [3, 4]. Reduced exercise capacity, muscle strength, and mobility are not merely markers of disease burden; they are independently associated with lower health-related quality of life (HRQoL), increased hospitalization, and mortality in CKD populations [5–7].

Most prior work in dialysis has relied on cross-sectional assessments or single time-point measures to stratify risk [8–10]. While clinically informative, such snapshots may miss the heterogeneous trajectories patients follow after PD catheter placement, including patterns of early recovery, stability, or decline [11]. Understanding this heterogeneity is critical because it could enable targeted, timely interventions for those on adverse trajectories [12]. In addition, the early PD period is also marked by dynamic changes in inflammation, nutrition, anemia management, and dialysis adequacy, all of which may influence muscle performance and cardiorespiratory fitness [13].

The Peritoneal Dialysis-Physical Function (PD-PF) study is a single-center, prospective cohort study, which has three aims: first, to identify and describe heterogeneous trajectories of physical function using group-based trajectory modeling (GBTM) in incident PD patients within 12 month of catheter placement; second, to develop and internally validate a multivariable model that predicts trajectory membership from baseline and early clinical features; and third, to examine associations between baseline function and trajectory groups with key adverse outcomes. We hypothesize that distinct and clinically meaningful trajectory subgroups exist in early PD; that baseline characteristics and early changes in activity and biomarkers predict membership in these subgroups; and that adverse trajectories confer increased risk of poor clinical outcomes independent of confounding.

Study design and setting

This is a single-center, prospective cohort study designed to characterize and predict 12-month trajectories of physical function among incident PD patients and to explore associations between physical function (including its longitudinal trajectories) and major clinical outcomes. The PD-PF study will be established at the Peritoneal Dialysis Center of Longhua Hospital Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine. Enrollment will occur within 1 month of PD catheter placement, with standardized follow-up assessments at baseline, 3, 6, 9, and 12 months.

Safety procedures will follow international recommendations for exercise testing in clinical populations, including physician supervision and emergency preparedness during cardiopulmonary exercise testing (CPET) [15, 16].

Exploratory objectives

To assess whether selected laboratory and treatment parameters (e.g., serum albumin, C-reactive protein, electrolytes, mineral bone markers, Kt/V, hemoglobin, glycemic and lipid profiles) and patient-level factors (e.g., demographics, comorbidity, frailty score) predict physical function trajectories and clinical outcomes.

Hypotheses

H1

Distinct, clinically meaningful trajectory subgroups of physical function exist among incident PD patients during the first year after catheter placement, reflecting heterogeneous recovery or decline patterns.

H2

Baseline characteristics (e.g., age, sex, diabetes, nutritional and inflammatory status, residual kidney function proxies, Kt/V, hemoglobin) and interim changes in physical activity will independently predict trajectory group membership.

H3

Lower baseline physical function and adverse trajectory membership (e.g., persistently low or declining function) are associated with higher risks of PD-related peritonitis, unplanned hospitalization, cardiovascular events, PD technique failure, and all-cause mortality, independent of confounders.

H4

Integrating objective measures (VO2peak, HGS, FTSST, gait speed) and digital step counts will improve discrimination of trajectory groups over single measures alone.

Study population and eligibility criteria

Participants will be recruited from the Peritoneal Dialysis Center at Longhua Hospital Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine. Potentially eligible patients will be screened consecutively from clinic and inpatient logs.

Sample size

We plan a feasibility-driven cohort targeting adequate power for trajectory detection and prognostic modeling. For multivariate group-based trajectory modeling of four performance domains, simulations and prior literature suggest stable class recovery with 15–20 participants per latent class. Assuming 3–4 clinically interpretable classes and 20% attrition over 12 months, we aim to enroll 180 participants to retain ~ 144 for primary analyses (≈ 36–48 per class). For Cox models examining adverse outcomes (peritonitis, unplanned hospitalization, technique failure, mortality), we target ≥ 10–15 events per degree of freedom in the main adjusted model. Based on expected 12-month composite event rates of 25–35% in incident PD, we anticipate 36–50 events, sufficient for prespecified covariates and trajectory exposures.

Data collection

Data are collected at baseline, 3, 6, 9, and 12 months (± 4 weeks) using standardized Standard Operating Procedure and trained assessors. Physical performance includes CPET-derived VO2peak (calibrated ergometer, physician-supervised, uniform ramp protocol), handgrip strength (both hands; best value recorded), five-times sit-to-stand (seconds), and 4‑m gait speed (m/s). Safety criteria and termination rules are applied consistently. Daily step counts are retrieved from the WeChat smartphone pedometer; “effective days” and “adherent weeks” are predefined, and segments with > 14 consecutive missing days are excluded from primary analyses. Anthropometrics (weight, height, BMI) and calf circumference are measured twice and averaged. Laboratory panels (albumin, CRP, electrolytes, mineral-bone markers, hemoglobin, iron indices, glyco‑lipid profile) and dialysis adequacy (Kt/V) are abstracted from the EHR within ± 7 days of each visit. KDQOL‑36 (Chinese version) is self-administered. Data undergo double entry, range and logic checks, periodic audits, and discrepancy adjudication. Repeats or catch-up visits are arranged for key missingness; all timestamps are recorded for longitudinal alignment.

Covariates

Baseline and time-varying covariates will be considered in descriptive, predictive, and survival analyses based on clinical relevance and prior literature: (1) demographic and socioeconomic: age, sex, marital status, education level, income, smoking/alcohol use; (2) clinical factors: CKD etiology, comorbidities (e.g., diabetes, hypertension, cardiovascular disease), disease duration, medications (e.g., antihypertensives, diabetes medications, statins, vitamin D analogs, phosphate binders, erythropoiesis stimulating agents), blood pressure; (3) frailty scores; (4) anthropometry and body composition proxies: body mass index, calf circumference; (5) physical activity: daily step counts (mean per assessment window) as objective physical activity exposure; (6) laboratory measures and PD adequacy as above.

Statistical methods

Analyses will be performed using R (version ≥ 4.3) and/or Stata (version ≥ 17). Two-sided P < 0.05 will be considered statistically significant unless otherwise specified.

Sensitivity analysis

We will perform the following prespecified sensitivity analyses to assess robustness of inferences: (1) composite vs. unidomain trajectories: use unidomain trajectory groups (e.g., gait speed only) as the outcome for prediction and as exposures for outcome models to evaluate whether findings are driven by a specific test; (2) competing risks and outcome definitions: for PD exit and peritonitis, contrast Fine-Gray subdistribution models with cause-specific Cox models, and hospitalization threshold: reclassify unplanned hospitalization using ≥ 24-hour duration and re-estimate associations; (3) measurement sensitivity and thresholds: alternative cut points with defining low HGS and slow gait using alternative thresholds (e.g., Asian-specific or quartiles) and assess whether baseline impairment classifications yield consistent associations with outcomes; (4) Unmeasured confounding: compute E-values for key trajectory-outcome HRs to quantify the minimum strength of association unmeasured confounding would require to explain away the observed effect; (5) Since peritonitis and unplanned hospitalizations may recur, recurrent-event analyses will be conducted using Andersen-Gill and Prentice-Williams-Peterson gap-time models as sensitivity analyses to account for within-individual event correlation.

Discussion

In this protocol, we propose a prospective cohort of incident PD patients to delineate 12-month trajectories of physical function and their prognostic implications. Several design features strengthen the study’s potential contribution. First, enrollment within 1 month of catheter placement anchors the cohort at a clinically meaningful transition, capturing early recovery or decline that cross-sectional snapshots miss. Second, the multidomain assessment provides a comprehensive profile that reflects central and peripheral physiological constraints. Third, the integration of objective physical activity, nutritional and inflammatory biomarkers, dialysis adequacy, and HRQoL allows us to examine plausibly modifiable determinants and downstream consequences of functional change. Finally, by applying GBTM, we recognize heterogeneity rather than force an average trajectory, enabling probabilistic classification that is directly actionable for risk stratification and trial enrichment.

We acknowledge limitations and potential risks to inference. As a single-center study, generalizability may be constrained by local practices, case-mix, and rehabilitation resources. Sample size, determined by feasibility, may limit the number of stable trajectory groups recoverable and the precision of outcome associations. Informative missingness is possible if sicker participants miss visits or withdraw; we address this with multiple imputation [25]. Measurement burden, especially CPET, may pose adherence and safety challenges; standardized protocols with physician supervision and alternative analyses using composite or submaximal measures will mitigate impact. Lastly, residual confounding is inevitable in observational research; we plan prespecified covariate adjustment, flexible functional forms, and competing-risk methods, and we will quantify robustness using E-values. In addition, Although direct muscle mass measurement through dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry or multi-frequency bioimpedance analysis would refine sarcopenia diagnosis, cost and resource limitations precluded uniform implementation. Calf circumference was therefore used as a validated anthropometric proxy, shown to correlate strongly with appendicular muscle mass and adverse outcomes in dialysis populations.

Conclusion

The PD-PF cohort will characterize heterogeneous 12-month physical function trajectories from the start of PD, identify predictors of adverse paths, and link trajectories to clinically meaningful outcomes. Its findings will support timely, individualized, exercise-centered strategies within PD programs and guide the design of future interventional trials targeting functional decline in PD.

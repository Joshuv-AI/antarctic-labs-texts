Bekenstein bound on black hole entropy in non-Gaussian statistics
Mehdi Shokri

Abstract
The Bekenstein bound, inspired by the physics of black holes, is introduced to constrain the entropy growth of a physical system down to the quantum level in the context of a generalized second law of thermodynamics. We first show that the standard Bekenstein bound is violated when the entropy of a Schwarzschild black hole is described in non-Gaussian statistics Barrow, Tsallis, and Kaniadakis due to the presence of the related indices Δ \Delta , q q and κ \kappa , respectively. Then, by adding the GUP effects into the Bekenstein bound, we find that the generalized bound is satisfied in the context of the mentioned entropies through a possible connection between the entropies indices and the GUP parameter β \beta .

II
Bekenstein bound conjecture in non-Gaussian entropies

In 1981 J. Bekenstein introduced an upper bound on the entropy of a confined quantum system by using heuristic arguments involving black holes. The Bekenstein bound on the entropy
S
of any object of maximal radius
R
and total energy
E
is given by
[
11
]

S\leq\frac{2\pi k_{B}}{\hbar c}RE,

(1)

where
k_{B}
is the Boltzmann constant. Note that, at the classical level, when
\hbar\rightarrow 0
, we have
S\leq\infty
, pointing out that the entropy of a localized system is unbound from above. As we see, the bound (
1
) does not include Newton’s constant
G
, so it can be used for non-gravitational phenomena in addition to gravitational systems. In
[
97
]
, the authors showed that the Bekenstein bound in the presence of GUP effects takes two following configurations:

S\leq\begin{cases}\frac{2\pi k_{B}}{\hbar c}RE\Big[1-\frac{\beta}{4}\Big(\frac{\ell_{p}}{R}\Big)^{2}\Big],&\text{for $\beta>0$},\\
\\
\frac{2\pi k_{B}}{\hbar c}RE\Big[1+\frac{|\beta|}{4}\Big(\frac{\ell_{p}}{R}\Big)^{2}\Big],&\text{for $\beta<0$},\\
\end{cases}

(2)

which are obtained in the limit of
\beta\ell_{p}/R\ll 1
. Clearly, the generalized bound (
2
) reduces to the standard Bekenstein bound (
1
) in the limit of the HUP regime when
\beta\rightarrow 0
. As an interesting result, we see that GUP correction for
\beta<0
allows the entropy of a thermal system to surpass the Bekenstein bound, while it confines the system to entropies lower than Bekenstein’s limit for
\beta>0
. In the following, we first attempt to show that the standard Bekenstein bound (
1
), with HUP effects, is not satisfied when black hole entropy is described by non-Gaussian statistics contaminated with GUP effects. Then, by using the GUP version of the Bekenstein bound (
2
), we find how the non-Gaussian statistics indices and GUP parameter
\beta
are connected in order to fulfill the generalized Bekenstein bound.

II.1
Barrow statistics

Using Koch’s snowflake idea
[
98
]
, Barrow stated that quantum gravitational effects generate a fractal structure on the static, spherically symmetric Schwarzschild black hole surface going to infinity with a finite volume. Thus, the BH entropy can be modified as follows
[
79
]

S_{\text{B}}=\bigg(k_{B}\frac{A}{A_{p}}\bigg)^{1+\frac{\Delta}{2}},

(3)

where the subscript
B
depicts the Barrow statistics. Here,
A=4\pi R^{2}
and
A_{p}=(2\ell_{p})^{2}
, where
\ell_{p}^{2}=\hbar G/c^{3}
, are the surface area of the black hole and the Planck area, respectively. Also,
\Delta
is the Barrow index referring to the smooth structures for
\Delta=0
and the most fractal structures for
\Delta=1
. Now, using the surface radius of a Schwarzschild black hole
R=R_{g}=2GM/c^{2}
, the entropy (
3
) is rewritten as

S_{\text{B}}=\Big(\frac{\pi k_{B}}{\ell_{p}^{2}}\Big)^{1+\frac{\Delta}{2}}R_{g}^{2+\Delta}.

(4)

Due to the quantum effects of gravity applied on black hole surface area of the Schwarzschild black hole, the Bekenstein bound (
1
) modifies as

S_{\text{B}}\leq\frac{2\pi k_{B}}{\hbar c}R_{g}^{1+\frac{\Delta}{2}}E.

(5)

By using the formula
E=Mc^{2}
, then substituting the black hole surface radius from eq.(
4
), the above bound takes the form

S_{\text{B}}\leq\Big(\frac{\pi k_{B}}{\ell_{p}^{2}}\Big)^{-\frac{\Delta}{4}}S_{\text{B}}^{\frac{4+\Delta}{2(2+\Delta)}}.

(6)

It is clear that in the limit of the BH entropy,
\Delta=0
, the above inequality reduces to
S_{\text{BH}}\leq S_{\text{BH}}
, resulting in the Bekenstein bound (
1
) being satisfied for the Schwarzschild black hole studied in the context of the BH statistics. Moreover, the above inequality tells us that the presence of a non-zero Barrow index,
\Delta\neq 0
, will put into question the validity of the modified Bekenstein bound (
5
). This can be seen in the left panel of Fig.
1
where we plot the parameter

R_{\text{B}}\equiv\Big(\frac{\pi k_{B}}{\ell_{p}^{2}}\Big)^{-\frac{\Delta}{4}}S_{\text{B}}^{-\frac{\Delta}{2(2+\Delta)}},

(7)

versus the Barrow index
\Delta
for
S_{\text{B}}>1
. By increasing the value of Barrow’s factor
\Delta
, the panel shows more deviation from the consistency condition
R_{\text{B}}=1
. Now, let us pursue the issue from view point of the generalized Bekenstein bound (
2
) in which HUP effects are replaced by GUP effects. In the context of the Barrow statistics, the generalized Bekenstein bound (
2
), for
\beta<0
, can be modified as

S_{\text{B}}\leq\frac{2\pi k_{B}}{\hbar c}R_{g}^{1+\frac{\Delta}{2}}E\Big[1+\frac{|\beta|}{4}\frac{\ell_{p}^{2}}{R_{g}^{2+\Delta}}\Big].

II.3
Kaniadakis statistics

Relativistic generalization of Boltzmann–Gibbs entropy navigates us to formulate the
\kappa
-entropy introduced by Kaniadakis as
[
67
]

S_{\text{K}}=-k_{B}\sum\limits_{i}^{W}\frac{p_{i}^{1+\kappa}-p_{i}^{1-\kappa}}{2\kappa},

(22)

where the subscript K depicts the Kaniadakis statistics. Here,
p_{i}
is the system probability,
W
is number of microstates, and
-1<\kappa<1
is the Kaniadakis index. Assuming the microcanonical regime, where all microstates have the same probability, Kaniadakis entropy is given by

S_{\text{K}}=k_{B}\ln_{\kappa}W=k_{B}\frac{W^{\kappa}-W^{-\kappa}}{2\kappa},

(23)

where the Boltzmann-Gibbs entropy,
S=k_{B}\ln W
, is recovered when
\kappa\rightarrow 0
. For the Schwarzschild black hole, with
W=e^{\pi R_{g}^{2}/\ell_{p}^{2}}
, the Kaniadakis entropy (
23
) takes the following form

S_{\text{K}}=k_{B}\frac{e^{\frac{\pi\kappa R_{g}^{2}}{\ell_{p}^{2}}}-e^{-\frac{\pi\kappa R_{g}^{2}}{\ell_{p}^{2}}}}{2\kappa}.

(24)

On the other hand, the Bekenstein conjecture bound (
1
) in the context of the Kaniadakis statistics modifies as

S_{\text{K}}\leq\frac{2\pi k_{B}}{\hbar c}R_{g}E.

(25)

Then, by using the definition
E=Mc^{2}
and substituting the black hole radius from eq.(
24
), the above inequality can be rewritten as

S_{\text{K}}\leq\frac{k_{B}}{\kappa}\Bigg(2\pi iC+\ln\Big[\frac{\kappa S_{\text{K}}\pm\sqrt{k_{B}^{2}+\kappa^{2}S_{\text{K}}^{2}}}{k_{B}}\Big]\Bigg),

(26)

where the constant
C
is an integer so that we fix it as zero in the rest of the paper. Note that by choosing the positive sign and sending the Kaniadakis index
\kappa
to zero, the above bound reduces to the satisfied Bekenstien bound in the BH statistics. Also, eq.(
26
) discloses that the modified Bekenstein bound (
25
) can not be fulfilled when we deal with a non-zero Kaniadakis factor
\kappa
. This result is shown in the left panel of Fig.
3
, where we draw the parameter

R_{\text{K}}\equiv\frac{k_{B}}{\kappa S_{\text{K}}}\ln\Big[\frac{\kappa S_{\text{K}}+\sqrt{k_{B}^{2}+\kappa^{2}S_{\text{K}}^{2}}}{k_{B}}\Big],

(27)

versus the Kaniadakis index
\kappa
for
S_{\text{K}}>1
in which the consistency condition
R_{\text{K}}=1
is perturbed for both positive and negative cases of the Kaniadakis index
\kappa
. To study the issue in the GUP regime, we rewrite the generalized Bekenstein bound (
2
) in the Kaniadakis statistics, for
\beta<0
, as follows

S_{\text{K}}\leq\frac{2\pi k_{B}}{\hbar c}R_{g}E\Big[1+\frac{|\beta|}{4}\Big(\frac{\ell_{pl}}{R_{g}}\Big)^{2}\Big].

(28)

By substituting
E=Mc^{2}
and the black hole radius from eq.(
24
), the above inequality can be found as

S_{\text{K}}\leq\frac{k_{B}}{\kappa}\ln\Big[\frac{\kappa S_{\text{K}}+\sqrt{k_{B}^{2}+\kappa^{2}S_{\text{K}}^{2}}}{k_{B}}\Big]+\frac{\pi k_{B}|\beta|}{4},

(29)

with the corresponding parameter
R_{\text{KG}}

R_{\text{KG}}\equiv\frac{k_{B}}{\kappa S_{\text{K}}}\ln\Big[\frac{\kappa S_{\text{K}}+\sqrt{k_{B}^{2}+\kappa^{2}S_{\text{K}}^{2}}}{k_{B}}\Big]+\frac{\pi k_{B}|\beta|}{4S_{\text{K}}},

(30)

where the subscript
KG
depicts the GUP version of the Bekenstein bound in the Kaniadakis statistics. It is easy to see that the satisfied Bekenstein bound in the BH statistics is recovered from the bound (

) when
\kappa\rightarrow 0
and
\beta=0
. Also, the bound (

III
Conclusions

According to the second law of thermodynamics, the entropy of a closed physical system can be increased until it approaches a maximum point. Hence, universal entropy bounds have been proposed to confine the growth of the entropy in thermal systems down to the quantum level. Inspired by the black hole discussions, the Bekenstein bound conjecture was proposed to restrict the upper limit of the entropy of both gravitational and non-gravitational phenomena. In the present work, we first studied the Bekenstein bound on the entropy of a Schwarzschild black hole described by some non-Gaussian statistics such as Barrow, Tsallis, and Kaniadakis. We realized that Bekenstein’s bound is violated in the presence of non-vanished indices
\Delta
,
q
and
\kappa
related to the mentioned statistics. Then, by considering the GUP version of the Bekenstein bound, we found that the generalized bound is satisfied in the context of the above-mentioned statistics due to a relation between the entropies indices and the GUP parameter
\beta
.

In summary, the standard Bekenstein bound is
partially
satisfied in non-Gaussian statistics since its upper limit (
i.e.

R_{\text{B}}=1
for Barrow,
R_{\text{T}}=1
for Tsallis and
R_{\text{K}}=1
for Kaniadakis) is not achievable, while the GUP version of the Bekenstein bound provides a framework for a
fully
satisfied black hole bound in non-Gaussian statistics due to the increase in entropy, which usually happens for
\beta<0
.

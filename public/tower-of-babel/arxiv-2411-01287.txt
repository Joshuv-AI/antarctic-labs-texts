1.
Introduction

Let
S=\mathrm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a standard graded polynomial ring over a field
\mathrm{k}
and
M
a finitely generated graded
S
-module. The Hilbert function of
M
is given by
H_{M}(a)=\dim_{\mathrm{k}}M_{a}
for all
a\in\ZZ
. Hilbert proved that when
a
is large enough,
H_{M}
agrees with a polynomial of degree
d=\dim(M)-1
in
a
, called the Hilbert polynomial of
M
. Furthermore, the Hilbert polynomial of
M
can be written as

P_{M}(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}(-1)^{i}e_{i}(M)\binom{z+d-i}{d-i}

where
e_{i}(M)
are integers. The first coefficient
e_{0}(M)
is called the multiplicity of
M
. When
I
is a homogeneous ideal of
S
, Herzog, Puthenpurakaland, and Verma
[
HPV
, Theorem 1.1]
proved that
e_{i}(M/I^{k}M)
is of polynomial type of degree
\leq n-d+i
for all
i=0,\ldots,d
, where
d=\dim(M/IM)
. Recently, Shan, Wang, and Lu
[
SWL
]
computed the multiplicity of powers of path ideals of paths. To our knowledge and surprise, there are no other known examples of a nontrivial family of ideals for which the multiplicity of powers is explicitly written down. On the other hand, for arbitrary squarefree monomial ideal
I
, by
[
HH
, Corollary 6.2.3]
,
e_{0}(S/I)
equals the number of associated primes
Q
of
S/I
of dimension
\dim(S/I)
. To understand this myth, we start by calculating the multiplicity of powers of path ideals of cycles. We realize a similar result to
[
SWL
]
holds for arbitrary squarefree monomial ideals. In other words, for an arbitrary squarefree monomial ideal
I
, the multiplicity of powers of
I
is already encoded in the dimension and the multiplicity of
I
itself.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
I
be a nonzero squarefree monomial ideal of dimension
d
in
S
. Let
\mu
be the number of associated primes of
S/I
of dimension
d
. Then

e_{0}(S/I^{s})=\mu\binom{n-d+s-1}{s-1},

for all
s\geq 1
.

We then compute the multiplicity of
d
-path ideals of
n
-cycles.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
n>d\geq 2
be positive integers and

I_{n,d}=(x_{1}\cdots x_{d},x_{2}\cdots x_{d+1},\cdots,x_{n}x_{1}\cdots x_{d-1})\subset S=\mathrm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]

be the
d
-path ideal of an
n
-cycle. Write
n=kd+r
for some
k\geq 1
and
1\leq r\leq d
. Then

e_{0}(S/I_{n,d})=d\binom{k+d-r}{k}-\binom{k+d-r}{k+1}.

2.
Multiplicity of ideals

Throughout the paper, let
S=\mathrm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. The materials in this section are well-known to the experts in algebraic geometry. Nonetheless, we could not find explicit references, we include an argument here for completeness. We refer to
[
Ha
, Section I.7]
for more details.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
I
be a nonzero homogeneous ideal of
S
. Assume that
I
has a primary decomposition
I=Q_{1}\cap\cdots\cap Q_{s}\cap Q_{s+1}\cap\cdots\cap Q_{t}
where
\htt(Q_{i})=\htt(I)
for
i=1,\ldots,s
and
\htt(Q_{j})>\htt(I)
for
i=s+1,\ldots,t
. Denote the unmixed part of
I
by by
I^{\un}=Q_{1}\cap\cdots\cap Q_{s}
. Then

e_{0}(S/I)=e_{0}(S/I^{\un})=\sum_{i=1}^{s}e_{0}(S/Q_{i}).

Proof.

Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
S
-module. Denote by
d=\dim(M)
. By Hilbert’s Theorem
[
HH
, Theorem 6.1.3]
, there exists a Laurent polynomial
Q_{M}(t)\in\ZZ[t,t^{-1}]
with
Q_{M}(1)>0
such that the Hilbert’s series of
M
is

\operatorname{Hilb}_{M}(t)=\frac{Q_{M}(t)}{(1-t)^{d}}.

In particular,
e_{0}(M)=Q_{M}(1)
and
e_{0}(M)/(d-1)!
is the leading coefficient of the Hilbert polynomial of
M
.

First, assume that
t>s
. Let
L=Q_{s+1}\cap\cdots\cap Q_{t}
. From the exact sequence

0\to S/I\to S/I^{\un}\oplus S/L\to S/(I^{\un}+L)\to 0

and the additivity of the Hilbert polynomial, we deduce that

P_{S/I^{\un}}(t)+P_{S/L}(t)=P_{S/I}(t)+P_{S/(I^{\un}+L)}(t).

Since
\dim(S/L)<\dim(S/I)
and
\dim(S/(I^{\un}+L))<\dim(S/I)
, we deduce that
e_{0}(S/I)=e_{0}(S/I^{\un})
.

We now assume that
I
is unmixed and prove that
e_{0}(S/I)=\sum_{i=1}^{s}e_{0}(S/Q_{i})
by induction on
s
. The base case
s=1
is vacuous. Assume that the statement holds for
s
. Let
J=Q_{1}\cap\cdots\cap Q_{s}
. With the same argument as above, it suffices to prove that

(2.1)

\dim(S/(J+Q_{s+1}))<\dim(S/Q_{s+1}).

Let
P
be any associated prime of
S/(J+Q_{s+1})
. Then we have
Q_{s+1}\subseteq P
. Hence,
Q=\sqrt{Q_{s+1}}\subseteq P
. If
Q=P
then
J\subseteq Q
, which is a contradiction. Hence,
Q\subsetneq P
. The conclusion follows.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Assume that
I
is a radical ideal of
S
. Then
I^{(s)}=P_{1}^{s}\cap\cdots\cap P_{m}^{s}
, where
I^{(s)}
is the
s
-th symbolic power of
I
and
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
are the minimal associated primes of
S/I
. By Lemma
2.1
, we have

e_{0}\left(S/I^{(s)}\right)=e_{0}(S/I^{s}).

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The conclusion follows from Lemma
2.1
and the following facts

(1)

associated primes of monomial ideals are generated by variables of
S
;

(2)

e_{0}(S/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-d})^{s})=\binom{n-d+s-1}{s-1}
.

The conclusion follows.
∎

3.
Multiplicity of path ideals of cycles

In this section, we extend the work of Shan, Wang, and Lu
[
SWL
]
to the case of path ideals of cycles. Let
n\geq d\geq 2
be positive integers and
S=\mathrm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a standard graded polynomial ring over a field
\mathrm{k}
. We denote by

g_{1}=x_{1}\cdots x_{d},g_{2}=x_{2}\cdots x_{d+1},\cdots,g_{n}=x_{n}x_{1}\cdots x_{d-1},

and
I_{n,d}=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})
the
d
-path ideal of an
n
-cycle. In general, path ideals of cycles are more subtle than path ideals of paths; see
[
AF
,
BCV1
,
BCV2
]
for more information. First, we describe the associated primes of
S/I_{n,d}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
1\leq a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{s}\leq n
be positive integers. Denote by
a_{s+1}=a_{1}+n
and
a_{s+2}=a_{2}+n
. Then
P_{\mathbf{a}}=(x_{a_{1}},\ldots,x_{a_{s}})
is an associated prime of
S/I_{n,d}
if and only if the following conditions hold:

(1)

a_{i+1}-a_{i}\leq d
,

(2)

a_{i+2}-a_{i}>d
,

for
i=1,\ldots,s
.

Proof.

First, we prove the sufficient condition. We assume that
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{s})
satisfies the two conditions above. Let
U=\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{s}\}
and
f=\prod_{i\in U}x_{i}
. Condition (1) implies
f\notin I_{n,d}
. Since
I_{n,d}
is squarefree, we deduce that
x_{j}\notin I_{n,d}:f
for all
j\in U
. Conditions (1) and (2) imply that

x_{a_{i}}=g_{a_{i-1}+1}/\gcd(g_{a_{i-1}+1},f)\text{ for all }i=1,\ldots,s.

Hence,
I_{n,d}:f=P_{\mathbf{a}}
. Therefore,
P_{\mathbf{a}}
is an associated prime of
S/I_{n,d}
.

Conversely, assume that
P_{\mathbf{a}}
is an associated prime of
S/I_{n,d}
. By
[
HH
, Corollary 1.3.10]
, there exists a monomial
f
of
S
such that
P_{\mathbf{a}}=I_{n,d}:f
. Since
I_{n,d}
is squarefree, we may choose
f
to be squarefree. In particular,
f\notin I_{n,d}
. Assume by contradiction that
a_{i+1}-a_{i}>d
for some
i
. Then
g=x_{a_{i}+1}\cdots x_{a_{i}+d-1}\in I_{n,d}\subset P_{\mathbf{a}}
, which is a contradiction. Hence,
a_{i+1}-a_{i}\leq d
. Now, assume by contradiction that
a_{i+2}-a_{i}\leq d
for some
i
. We have
x_{a_{i}},x_{a_{i+2}}
does not divide
f
. Now for any minimal generator
g
of
I_{n,d}
such that
x_{i+1}|g
we must have either
x_{a_{i}}
or
x_{a_{i+2}}
divides
g
. In other words,
g/\gcd(g,f)
is divisible by
x_{a_{i}}x_{a_{i+1}}
or
x_{a_{i+1}}x_{a_{i+2}}
. Hence,
x_{a_{i+1}}\notin I_{n,d}:f
, a contradiction. The conclusion follows.
∎

Consequently, we have

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
n\geq d\geq 2
be positive integers. Then
\dim(S/I_{n,d})=n-\lceil\frac{n}{d}\rceil
.

Proof.

We use the notation as in Proposition
3.1
. Let
1\leq a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{s}\leq n
be positive integers such that
P_{\mathbf{a}}
is an associated prime of
S/I_{n,d}
. By Proposition
3.1
, we have
a_{i+1}-a_{i}\leq d
for all
i=1,\ldots,s
. In particular,
n=a_{s+1}-a_{1}\leq sd
. Hence,
s\geq\lceil\frac{n}{d}\rceil
and
\dim(S/I_{n,d})\leq n-\lceil\frac{n}{d}\rceil
.

Furthermore, let
s=\lceil\frac{n}{d}\rceil
and
a_{i}=1+(i-1)d
for all
i=1,\ldots,s
. Then
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{s})
satisfies the condition of Proposition
3.1
. Hence,
P_{\mathbf{a}}
is an associated prime of
S/I_{n,d}
. The conclusion follows.
∎

We now fix the following notation throughout the rest of the paper.

(1)

n=kd+r
for some integers
k,r
such that
k\geq 1
and
1\leq r\leq d
;

(2)

\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k+1})\in\NN^{k+1}
be such that
1\leq a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{k+1}\leq n
;

(3)

a_{k+2}=a_{1}+n
and
b_{j}=a_{j+1}-a_{j}
for all
j=1,\ldots,k+1
.

To compute the multiplicity of
S/I_{n,d}
we need some preparation lemmas.

Lemma 3.3
.

With the notations as above we have
P_{\mathbf{a}}
is an associated prime of
S/I_{n,d}
if and only if the following conditions hold

(1)

b_{i}\leq d
for all
i=1,\ldots k
,

(2)

\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}\geq(k-1)d+r
,

(3)

a_{1}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}\leq kd+r
.

Proof.

By definition and Proposition
3.1
, it suffices to prove that the conditions above implies that
b_{i}+b_{i+1}>d
for all
i=1,\ldots,k+1
. Indeed, if
b_{i}+b_{i+1}\leq d
for some
i\leq k-1
, then
\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}\leq(k-2)d+d<(k-1)d+r
. This is a contradiction, the conclusion follows.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

For each positive integers
k,d
, let

U_{k,d}=\left\{(c_{1},\ldots,c_{k+1})\in\NN^{k+1}\mid c_{i}\leq d,\sum_{1}^{k+1}c_{i}=kd\right\}.

Then
|U_{k,d}|=\binom{d+k}{d}
.

Proof.

Each
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{k+1})\in U_{k,d}
gives a monomial
x_{1}^{c_{1}}\cdots x_{k+1}^{c_{k+1}}
of degree
kd
in

M=\mathrm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{k+1}]/(x_{1}^{d+1},\ldots,x_{k+1}^{d+1}).

Hence,

|U_{k,d}|=H_{M}(kd)=\binom{d+k}{d}.

The conclusion follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
n=kd+r
for some integers
k,r
such that
k\geq 1
and
1\leq r\leq d
. By Corollary
3.2
,
\dim(S/I_{n,d})=n-(k+1)
. By Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
3.3
, we deduce that
e_{0}(S/I_{n,d})
is equal to the cardinality of the set
V_{k,d,r}
consisting of tuples
(a_{1},b_{1},\ldots,b_{k})
satisfying conditions of Lemma
3.3
. For each integer
s
, let

W_{k,d,r,s}=\{(b_{1},\ldots,b_{k})\in\NN^{k}\mid b_{i}\leq d\text{ for all }i=1,\ldots,k,\text{ and }\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}=(k-1)d+r+s\}.

For each
(b_{1},\ldots,b_{k})\in W_{k,d,r,s}
with
0\leq s\leq d-r
there are
d-s
choices of
a_{1}
so that
(a_{1},b_{1},\ldots,b_{k})\in V_{k,d,r}
. Hence,

|V_{k,d,r}|=\sum_{s=0}^{d-r}(d-s)|W_{k,d,r,s}|.

Now, each
(b_{1},\ldots,b_{k})\in W_{k,d,r,s}
must have
b_{i}\geq r+s
, as
b_{i}\leq d
for all
i=1,\ldots,k
. Let
c_{i}=b_{i}-r-s
for
i=1,\ldots,k
. We then deduce that
W_{k,d,r,s}\cong U_{k-1,d-r-s}
. By Lemma
3.4
, we deduce that

\displaystyle e_{0}(S/I_{n,d})

\displaystyle=|V_{k,d,r}|=\sum_{s=0}^{d-r}(d-s)\binom{d+k-r-s-1}{k-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=r}^{d}j\binom{k-1-r+j}{k-1},\text{ setting }j=d-s

\displaystyle=r\sum_{\ell=0}^{d-r}\binom{k-1+\ell}{k-1}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{d-r}\ell\binom{k-1+\ell}{k-1},\text{ setting }\ell=j-r

\displaystyle=r\binom{k+d-r}{k}+(d-r)\binom{k+d-r}{k}-\binom{k+d-r}{k+1},

where the last equality follows from standard binomial identities, see e.g.
[
G
]
. The conclusion follows.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
n=kd+r
where
k,d,r
are positive integers such that
1\leq r\leq d
. Then

e_{0}(S/I_{n,d}^{s})=\left(d\binom{k+d-r}{k}-\binom{k+d-r}{k+1}\right)\binom{k+s}{s-1}.

Proof.

The conclusion follows from Theorem
1.1
, Corollary
3.2
, and Theorem
1.2
.
∎

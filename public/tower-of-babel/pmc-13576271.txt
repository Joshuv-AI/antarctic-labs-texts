Burden of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease attributable to high body mass index, high fasting plasma glucose or high low density lipoprotein cholesterol in young and middle-aged adults from 1990 to 2050
Authors: Hua Zhang, Jun Li, Kaixin Qin, Yuan Zhao, Qi Yan, Wenhao Wu, Jin Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AimsThis study aims to analyze the global burden of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) attributable to high low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-c), high body mass index (BMI), or high fasting plasma glucose (FPG) in young and middle-aged adults (15–49 years) from 1990 to 2021, with projections extending to 2050.MethodsLeveraging data from the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2021 study, we concentrated on ASCVD burden attributable to three risk factors in young and middle-aged adults. We scrutinized crude death rates, number of deaths, and estimated annual percentage changes (EAPCs) across sexes, countries, regions, and socio-demographic index (SDI) categories. To forecast future trends, we employed the Bayesian age-period-cohort (BAPC) model. Locally weighted scatterplot smoothing (LOESS) regression (span = 0.5) was used to visualize associations between crude death rates and SDI levels across 21 GBD regions and 204 countries.ResultsThe ASCVD burden attributable to three risk factors in young and middle-aged adults followed broadly similar epidemiological patterns. In 2021, ischemic heart disease (IHD) burden attributable to three risk factors substantially exceeded ischemic stroke (IS) and lower extremity peripheral artery disease (LEPAD), and men exhibited consistently a higher burden than women. From 1990 to 2021, global trends in crude death rates for ASCVD attributable to three risk factors in young and middle-aged adults remained relatively stable. Significant regional and national variation was observed. At regional SDI levels, crude death rates for IHD showed an inverted M-shaped pattern (peaks at SDI 0.45 and 0.70), while IS and LEPAD demonstrated inverted L-shaped patterns (plateauing at SDI 0.70–0.76). Across 204 countries in 2021, crude death rates from ASCVD attributable to three risk factors increased gradually below SDI 0.60, showed a slight U-shape between 0.60–0.75, and declined sharply above 0.75. Projections indicate that ASCVD attributable to three risk factors will increase to varying degrees in young and middle-aged adults by 2050.ConclusionThe burden of ASCVD attributable to three risk factors in young and middle-aged adults calls for concerted and integrated management strategies. Urgent public health measures targeting these risk factors are needed to mitigate the projected rise in ASCVD burden.

Introduction

Atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD), including ischemic heart disease (IHD), ischemic stroke (IS), and lower extremity peripheral artery disease (LEPAD), was a leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide, imposing a significant burden on global health systems and economies [1]. Over the past few decades, while the prevalence of ASCVD in the elderly has declined, the prevalence of ASCVD in young and middle-aged adults has significantly increased, closely linked to several key metabolic risk factors, including high low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-c), high body mass index (BMI), and high fasting plasma glucose (FPG) [1]. These risk factors have been identified as major contributors to the development and progression of ASCVD through various pathophysiological mechanisms. High LDL-c levels promote the formation of atherosclerotic plaques, which can lead to ischemic events such as myocardial infarction and stroke [2]. High BMI, particularly when associated with central obesity, is linked to increased cardiovascular risk through mechanisms involving inflammation, insulin resistance, and adverse lipid profiles [3]. Similarly, high FPG levels contribute to the risk of ASCVD through mechanisms such as endothelial dysfunction, oxidative stress, and inflammation, which promote atherosclerosis [4]. The interplay among these risk factors further complicates the assessment and management of cardiovascular risk, highlighting the importance of a comprehensive approach to prevention and treatment.

The Global Burden of Disease (GBD) study [5, 6] has been instrumental in understanding the epidemiology and impact of metabolic risk factors on ASCVD in young and middle-aged adults. In this study, we utilize the GBD 2021 data for the first time to provide an updated and comprehensive analysis of the burden of ASCVD attributable to high LDL-c, high BMI, and high FPG in young and middle-aged adults from 1990 to 2021, and to offer predictions up to 2050. While existing GBD 2021 studies [7–10] analyses have largely focused on CVD burden attributable to metabolic risks across all ages, this study uniquely isolates ASCVD in young and middle-aged adults (15–49 years) to provide the first direct comparison of high LDL-c, high BMI, and high FPG as distinct yet interconnected risk factors in this specific population. Examining three decades of historical trends is critical for evaluating the effectiveness of previous public health interventions, identifying successful prevention strategies, and revealing gaps in current policies. Meanwhile, forward-looking predictions are essential for anticipating the future disease burden amid demographic shifts, and evolving risk factor profiles. These analyses collectively address key policy questions: which risk factors require prioritized investment, how healthcare resources should be strategically allocated across regions and nations, and how prevention programs can be optimized to maximize impact and cost-effectiveness in the coming decades. By integrating three decades of historical data with forward-looking prediction, the study aims to guide policymakers and healthcare providers in prioritizing resources and implementing measures to reduce the burden of ASCVD in young and middle-aged adults.

Methods

Data sources

The data utilized in our study were obtained from the GBD 2021 database [11], encompassing details on 371 diseases and injuries, as well as 88 risk factors, across 204 countries, 21 GBD regions, and five socio-demographic index (SDI) regions, stratified by age and gender [5, 6]. Our study covered data from 1990 to 2021, concentrating on the ASCVD, including IHD, IS, and LEPAD, burden attributable to high FPG, high BMI, and high LDL-c in young and middle-aged adults. The Washington University Institutional Review Board granted approval for the informed consent waiver, given that the GBD 2021 study utilized deidentified and aggregated data.

Definition

To estimate the population attributable burden, we established the theoretical minimum risk exposure level (TMREL) for each risk factor. The TMREL represents the optimal, hypothetical exposure level associated with the lowest possible mortality risk across all health outcomes, serving as the counterfactual reference for calculating preventable burden [10]. Regarding the calculation of the attributable burden, the GBD study employs a comparative risk assessment framework to estimate population attributable fractions (PAFs). Specifically, for each risk factor, a TMREL is established, representing the optimal exposure range associated with the lowest population-level mortality risk. The PAF is then calculated by comparing the current population exposure distribution to this TMREL, using integrated exposure–response relationships derived from meta-analyses of epidemiological studies. The number of attributable deaths is obtained by multiplying the PAF by the total death count for a specific disease. It is important to note that this attribution is based on statistical associations at the population level and does not imply direct clinical causality for individual cases. All estimates are presented with 95% uncertainty intervals (UIs) to reflect the variability in data inputs and modelling assumptions [10]. High LDL-c is considered as any concentration above the theoretical minimum risk range of 0.9–1.4 mmol/L [10]. High BMI for adults (20 and older) is defined as BMI greater than 20–23 kg/m2. High BMI for children (2–19 years) is defined as being overweight or obese based on International Obesity Task Force standards [10]. High FPG is considered as any concentration above the theoretical minimum risk range of 4.90–5.30 mmol/L [10]. The classification of CVD, including IHD, IS, and LEPAD, is based on the ICD-9 and ICD-10 systems.

The age range of 15–49 years was selected to focus on ASCVD in young and middle-aged adults, based on both clinical and public health considerations. This period captures the critical period from late adolescence through early middle age, when premature events carry disproportionate public health impact due to steeper risk trajectories, greater years of life lost, and substantial socioeconomic burden [12, 13]. This upper threshold (49 years) lies below conventional high-risk cutoffs (e.g., < 55 years for men/ < 65 years for women) [13], enabling focused identification of early-onset cases and their distinct risk factor patterns. This age band also aligns directly with standard GBD analytic groups, facilitating seamless integration with global burden-of-disease estimates and enhancing comparability for policy applications. Data on number of deaths and crude death rates were extracted from the GBD 2021 database for (1) IHD or IS attributable to high LDL-c, (2) IHD, IS, or LEPAD attributable to high BMI, and (3) IHD, IS, or LEPAD attributable to high FPG.

The SDI, a metric reflecting socio-economic status, is derived from the geometric mean of three elements: the total fertility rate for people under 25, the average years of education for those aged 15 and older, and the lag-distributed per capita income [5, 6]. To facilitate comparative analysis, the 21 GBD regions and 204 countries and territories were categorized into five quintile groups according to their SDI levels.

Statistical analysis

To evaluate the burden of ASCVD attributable to three risk factors in young and middle-aged adults, we employed crude death rates (per 100,000 populations), number of deaths, and estimated annual percentage change (EAPC), with these metrics analyzed by sex, country, region, and SDI classification. The crude death rates and number of deaths were directly extracted from the GBD database and shown with a 95% UIs. This UI was calculated from 1,000 resampled estimates for each parameter, with the 95% UI being the range from the 2.5th to the 97.5th percentiles of these resampled values. [5, 6]

A regression analysis using the natural logarithm of crude death rate was conducted, structured as y = α + βx + ε, where y signifies the natural logarithm of the crude death rate and x indicates the calendar year [14]. The estimated annual percent change (EAPC) was computed as 100 × (exp(β)—1), with its 95% confidence interval (CI) obtained from this regression model [14]. The trend of the crude death rate was categorized as increasing if both the EAPC and its 95% CI exceeded zero, as decreasing if they were below zero, and as stable if the 95% CI included zero. [14]

Bayesian Age-Period-Cohort (BAPC) model was employed based on a Poisson distribution to forecast the ASCVD burden attributable to three risk factors in young and middle-aged adults from 2022 to 2050. The foundational population data required for the model were obtained from the World Population Prospects standard dataset published by the United Nations Department of Economic and Social Affairs (https://population.un.org/wpp/). Model parameters were estimated using Integrated Nested Laplace Approximations (INLA) to circumvent convergence issues commonly associated with traditional Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) methods [15]. To ensure the smoothness and stability of the age, period, and cohort effects over time, Gaussian random walk priors were applied. Specifically, based on the observed trends in disease burden, a second-order random walk prior (RW2) capable of accommodating linear trends was selected. Model goodness-of-fit was assessed using the Watanabe-Akaike Information Criterion (WAIC), and posterior predictive checks confirmed that the model adequately captured the characteristics of the observed data. The BAPC model was chosen for its ability to simultaneously disentangle the three-dimensional temporal effects of age, period, and cohort, offering a forecasting structure more aligned with the natural history of the disease compared to traditional time-series extrapolation methods [16]. Furthermore, the Bayesian framework fully propagates parameter uncertainty into the forecast results, generating probabilistic predictions with credible intervals, thereby providing a more comprehensive risk assessment basis for public health decision-making.

Moreover, to visualize the potential nonlinear associations between crude death rates and SDI levels across 21 GBD regions and 204 countries and territories, we employed locally weighted scatterplot smoothing (LOESS) regression. A smoothing span parameter of 0.5 was selected to balance the capture of local trends with overall curve stability. The resulting curves should be interpreted as smoothed summaries of the central tendency within the data; their specific morphological features are descriptive and may vary with different smoothing parameter choices. All statistical analyses were conducted using R (version 4.2.3).

Results

The ASCVD burden attributable to the three risk factors displayed broadly similar epidemiological patterns in young and middle-aged adults across global, sex, regional, national, and SDI levels.

Global and sex-specific trends and burden

From 1990 to 2021, global trends in crude death rates for ASCVD attributable to three risk factors in young and middle-aged adults remained relatively stable. Crude death rates for IHD or IS attributable to high LDL-c in young and middle-aged adults showed modest declines [EAPC: -0.27(95%CI -0.39 to -0.16) and -0.56 (95%CI -0.71 to -0.41)], with women experiencing a more pronounced reduction than men. In contrast, crude death rates for ASCVD attributable to high BMI or high FPG in young and middle-aged adults increased slightly across all disease categories, with the most pronounced rise for IHD attributable to high FPG [EAPC: 1.54 (95%CI 1.46 to 1.63)] (Tables 1, 2 and 3 and Fig. 1A-C). Notably, gender differences were evident in these trends: for high BMI, IHD or LEPAD rose more rapidly in women, whereas IS increased faster in men; for high FPG, IHD or IS grew more quickly in men, while LEPAD showed a steeper rise in women (Tables 1, 2 and 3).In 2021, the attributable burden for IHD in young and middle-aged adults was consistently and substantially higher than that for IS or LEPAD across all three risk factors. The ASCVD burden for number of deaths and crude death rates in young and middle-aged adults was highest for high LDL-c, followed by high BMI or high FPG. Regional and national trends and burden

Across 21 GBD regions, from 1990 to 2021, the EAPCs for crude death rates attributable to different risk factors exhibited substantial heterogeneity in young and middle-aged adults. East Asia and Southeast Asia consistently experienced pronounced increases in crude death rates for IHD or IS attributable to nearly all three risk factors in young and middle-aged adults. Conversely, Central Europe, Western Europe, and Australasia achieved substantial declines across nearly all three risk factors. Significant increases in LEPAD burden attributable to high FPG or LDL-c in young and middle-aged adults were observed in Andean Latin America, East Asia, and North Africa and the Middle East, with no decreasing trends across regions (Tables 1, 2 and 3). In 2021, substantial variations were observed in the number of deaths and crude death rates for ASCVD attributable to three risk factors in young and middle-aged adults, as shown in Tables 1, 2 and 3.

Across 204 countries and territories, from 1990 to 2021, the EAPCs for crude death rates attributable to different risk factors also exhibited substantial heterogeneity in young and middle-aged adults. For high LDL-c, the highest EAPC for IHD was in Zimbabwe (4.63, 95% CI: 3.67 to 5.59) and the lowest in Estonia (–6.56, 95% CI:–7.10 to –6.01) (Fig. 2A), while for IS, the highest was in Zimbabwe (4.87, 95% CI:3.80to 5.95) and the lowest in Luxembourg (–8.17, 95% CI: –8.43 to –7.92) (Fig. 2B). Discussion

This study presents a comprehensive and forward-looking analysis of the global burden of ASCVD attributable to three pivotal metabolic risk factors in young and middle-aged adults over three decades from 1990 to 2021, with projections extending to 2050. The burden attributable to these risk factors, while distinct in their pathophysiological pathways, demonstrate broadly similar epidemiological patterns and trajectories, underscoring their collective and often synergistic role in driving cardiovascular mortality. At regional SDI levels, crude death rates for IHD showed an inverted M-shaped pattern (peaks at SDI 0.45 and 0.70), while IS and LEPAD demonstrated inverted L-shaped patterns (plateauing at SDI 0.70–0.76). Across 204 countries in 2021, crude death rates increased gradually below SDI 0.60, showed a slight U-shape between 0.60–0.75, and declined sharply above 0.75. Projections indicate that ASCVD attributable to three risk factors will increase to varying degrees in young and middle-aged adults by 2050.

The broadly similar burden patterns observed for these risk factors are a central finding of this study. This homogeneity suggests that these risk factors are not isolated drivers but are deeply intertwined, frequently clustering within individuals and populations due to shared underlying societal and lifestyle determinants. While our analysis quantifies the individual contributions of high BMI, FPG, or LDL-c to ASCVD burden, we acknowledge that these metabolic risk factors are not independent in their pathophysiological effects. The clustering of these factors through insulin resistance creates complex interactions that the current GBD methodology does not fully capture. The sum of individual attributable fractions likely overestimates the true combined metabolic burden, as these factors act in concert rather than additively. Therefore, our estimates should be interpreted as the upper bound of potential prevention impact, recognizing that risk factor overlap may lead to some degree of overestimation when viewed in isolation. Future research employing mediation analysis or causal inference methods could better elucidate the synergistic versus independent contributions of these interconnected metabolic pathways. The globalization of unhealthy diets [17] (processed, convenience, high-energy, and fast foods), widespread physical inactivity [18], and increasing urbanization [19] are common drivers fueling the rise of dyslipidemia, obesity, and hyperglycemia simultaneously. Pathophysiologically, these risks act in concert: high BMI is frequently associated with insulin resistance, which in turn can exacerbate atherogenic dyslipidemia, including high LDL-c [20]. This synergy creates a potent mix that accelerates the development and progression of atherosclerosis in young adults. Our results show that IHD constitutes the overwhelming majority of attributable deaths for all three risk factors, followed by IS and then LEPAD. This hierarchy aligns with the existing studies [21] indicating that, in ASCVD, IHD tend to have higher mortality rates than IS or LEPAD. The notably lower recorded burden for LEPAD may also be partly attributable to its often asymptomatic nature in early stages and significant challenges in diagnosis [22] compared to the more acute and easily identified events of IHD and IS.

The global trends in crude death rates for ASCVD attributable to the three risk factors in young and middle-aged adults have remained relatively stable. Specifically, the diverging trends for high LDL-c compared to high BMI or FPG highlight progress in the widespread adoption and effectiveness of lipid-lowering therapies, such as statins, over recent decades [23]. Conversely, the rising death rate associated with high BMI or FPG points to the escalating global epidemics of obesity and diabetes [24], which are outpacing current prevention and control efforts. The observed sex-specific differences in crude death rate trajectories from 1990 to 2021 suggest potentially variations in biological risk, healthcare access, or behavioral factors between men and women [25–27]. Furthermore, the sustained higher burden experienced by men in 2021 necessitates targeted public health strategies. Therefore, a focused response is essential: public health efforts must strengthen the prevention and control of obesity and diabetes to reverse their rising mortality impact, while consolidating the progress achieved in cholesterol management through sustained lipid-lowering strategies. Simultaneously, gender-specific interventions should be developed and implemented to address the persistently higher ASCVD burden among young and middle-aged men.

The substantial regional and national heterogeneity in crude ASCVD death rates attributable to three risk factors in young and middle-aged adults from 1990 to 2021 reflects a complex interplay of differential epidemiological transitions, health system capacities, and policy implementations. East Asia and Southeast Asia’s pronounced increases in IHD and IS mortality attributable to three risk factors likely reflect rapid economic growth driving nutrition transitions toward processed diets [28], reduced physical activity [29], and elevated metabolic risk profiles [24], compounded by healthcare systems historically oriented toward infectious disease management that have struggled to scale up chronic disease prevention [30]. Conversely, Central Europe, Western Europe, and Australasia’s substantial declines likely reflect decades of healthcare system strengthening [31], and evidence-based policy interventions [32].At the national level, Zimbabwe and Lesotho’s extreme crude death rate increases for multiple risk factors are driven by political instability, healthcare fragmentation, and resource constraints in low-income settings [33, 34]. Conversely, the notable progress observed in Estonia and Luxembourg is attributable to the vigorous efforts of their formidable healthcare systems in the prevention, treatment, and rehabilitation of disease [35–38]. When further stratifying these geographic patterns by SDI levels, low to middle SDI regions exhibited the most concerning trends, with crude ASCVD death rates showing substantial increases or only modest declines. LOESS analysis further supported this finding, revealing IHD death rates with dual peaks at SDI ~ 0.45 and 0.70 (inverted M-shape) and IS/LEPAD rates plateauing at SDI 0.70–0.76 (inverted L-shape) across SDI regions from 1990–2021, and a slight U-shape between SDI 0.60–0.75 in 204 countries in 2021. These patterns may reflect rapid urbanization, the "nutrition transition" towards diets high in ultra-processed foods, sugars, and unhealthy fats, and increasingly sedentary occupations and lifestyles [39–41]. Compounding this, these regions and countries often grapple with healthcare systems that are under-resourced, fragmented, and overwhelmingly focused on acute care and infectious diseases [42, 43], leaving little capacity for the long-term management of chronic cardiometabolic conditions. Furthermore, issues of access and affordability to essential medicines and healthy foods create insurmountable barriers for large segments of the population [44, 45]. In stark contrast, high-middle to high SDI regions exhibited the most encouraging trends, with crude ASCVD death rates showing substantial increases or only modest declines. These findings were further substantiated by LOESS analysis. High-income regions and countries have long benefited from robust public health initiatives, improved access to effective treatments, and widespread preventive care [46–49]. These regions and countries have seen declining death rates due to the successful implementation of comprehensive health policies [46]. For example, the widespread use of statins and anti-diabetic drugs has significantly reduced mortality from CVD [47–49]. Additionally, high-income regions and countries have benefited from advanced healthcare infrastructure, well-trained healthcare professionals, and comprehensive health insurance systems that ensure access to care for all segments of the population [46]. To address these disparities, key public health priorities should including strengthening primary care and chronic disease management, particularly in low- to middle-SDI settings; implementing multisectoral policies to promote healthy diets and physical activity; ensuring affordable access to essential cardiovascular medicines within universal health coverage frameworks; enhancing health information systems for targeted surveillance; and fostering global collaboration to share effective prevention strategies.

The predictive analysis indicates a significant increase in the number of deaths and death rates for ASCVD attributable to three risk factors in young and middle-aged adults by 2050, which may be associated with the anticipated continuous increase in these risk factors [50–52] and the projected expansion of the young and middle-aged population. However, the drivers of this increase vary substantially across SDI levels. In low- to middle-SDI regions, rising number of deaths are driven by population growth in young and middle-aged adults [53] combined with intensifying risk exposure [52]. In contrast, high- and high-middle SDI regions face a more concerning pattern. Despite plateauing or declining young and middle-aged adults due to aging and sub-replacement fertility, absolute death counts continue to rise [53], indicating that worsening metabolic risk profiles are outpacing demographic contraction. This alarming projection underscores the urgent need for public health measures to address these risk factors. Effective interventions should focus on promoting healthy lifestyles, including balanced diets, regular physical activity, and obesity control. Additionally, expanding healthcare infrastructure and workforce capacity is crucial to accommodate growing young and middle-aged populations in low- to middle-SDI regions and ensure early detection and management of these risk factors. In high- and high-middle SDI regions, healthcare systems must prioritize targeted metabolic risk reduction programs to offset the impact of deteriorating risk profiles amid demographic contraction. Public health campaigns aimed at raising awareness about the dangers of three risk factors, and encouraging regular health check-ups, can play a pivotal role in reducing the burden of ASCVD in young and middle-aged adults. Moreover, policy initiatives that support the availability and affordability of nutritious foods, as well as the creation of safe and accessible spaces for physical activity, are essential components of a comprehensive strategy. Strengthening health systems to provide comprehensive care for individuals with these risk factors, including access to lipid-lowering therapies, diabetes management, and weight management programs, will also be critical in mitigating the projected rise in ASCVD burden in young and middle-aged adults.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, these estimates, derived using the Cause of Death Ensemble model (CODEm), depend on the quality and availability of input data from vital registration systems, verbal autopsies, and other sources, which may vary in availability, diagnostic accuracy, and coding consistency [6–10]. Second, the calculation of attributable burden is sensitive to the TMREL and risk-outcome definitions, which are based on current epidemiological evidence and may evolve; additionally, the analysis assumes risk factors are independent, and PAF-based estimates cannot fully account for confounding, mediation, reverse causation, or complex interactions [6–10]. Third, LEPAD is likely under-ascertained, particularly in asymptomatic stages and in settings without routine specialized testing [22], potentially leading to underestimated prevalence, especially in low- and middle-income countries. Fourth, The “M-shaped” or “L-shaped” patterns was based on descriptive smoothing techniques (LOESS). While these patterns serve as a useful visual and descriptive characterization of the data, their exact morphology should not be overinterpreted as a definitive functional relationship, but rather as a reflection of underlying complex associations. Fifth, GBD 2021 does not provide data disaggregated by race or ethnicity, precluding subgroup analyses to examine disparities across these demographics. We acknowledge that understanding racial and ethnic differences in ASCVD burden would be of significant scientific and public health value, and hope that future iterations of the GBD study or other population-based datasets may enable such investigations. Finally, the BAPC model for prediction assumes that past trends will continue and cannot account for unexpected technological breakthroughs, dramatic policy shifts, or pandemics that could alter the trajectory of ASCVD burden. [10].

Conclusion

These findings underscore the urgent need for integrated, coordinated strategies targeting three risk factors to curb the projected rise in ASCVD burden among young and middle-aged adults by 2050. Public health efforts must prioritize obesity and diabetes prevention while sustaining cholesterol management gains, with tailored interventions addressing persistent sex disparities and the unique challenges faced by low- to middle-SDI regions.

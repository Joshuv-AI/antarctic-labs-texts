Hawking-Page transition in anti-de Sitter massive gravity with non-compact spatial boundary
Nelson R. F. Braga, William S. Cunha

Abstract
In gauge/gravity duality, the Hawking Page (HP) process plays the important role of representing a confined/deconfined phase transition. For spaces that are asymptotically anti-de Sitter (AdS) with planar spatial boundary there is no HP transition, unless some infrared energy scale is introduced. Massive gravity theories have been applied in the context of holography in order to represent systems with momentum dissipation. In this work we study the HP transition for massive gravity in an asymptotically AdS space with ℝ n × \mathbb{R}^{n}\times\, S 1 boundary. It is shown that the graviton mass term produces an HP transition at a finite critical temperature.



I
Introduction

The Hawking-Page (HP) transition was proposed in
Hawking and Page 1983
, using a semiclassical approach to the thermal behavior of Euclidean 4-d spaces with negative cosmological constant and spherical symmetry. There are two solutions for the Einstein’s equations under these conditions: the thermal anti-de Sitter (
AdS
) and the black hole anti-de Sitter (
AdS-BH
) space-times. At low temperatures, the black hole solution is unstable, so the system is in the
AdS
phase. For temperatures higher than some critical value, the situation change and the system is dominated by the
AdS-BH
solution.

In the context of gauge/string duality, it was proposed by Witten
Witten 1998
that the HP transition in
AdS_{5}
space corresponds to a confinement/deconfinement transition in the 4-d gauge theory side of the
AdS/CFT
correspondence. The transition analyzed in
Witten 1998
occurs only in the case when the
AdS_{5}
space has a compact boundary.
For the
AdS_{5}
space with planar boundary, more appropriate to represent our physical world, there is no HP transition and the black hole is the stable configuration at any temperature. Unless some modification is made in order to introduce some dimensionfull parameter in the background, in general associated with an infrared cutoff, as it is done in
Herzog 2007
;
Ballon Bayona et al. 2008
;
Colangelo et al. 2009
;
Colangelo et al. 2012
;
Rinaldi and Vento 2023
;
Braga and Junqueira 2024
;
Braga and Junqueira 2025
.

Massive gravity theories, meaning theories where the graviton is massive, have been studied for quite a long time (see for example
de Rham 2014
;
Hassan and Rosen 2011
). In the gauge/gravity dualities they are interpreted as representing systems with broken translational symmetry and, consequently, momentum dissipation
Vegh 2013
. The HP transition in the case of
AdS
space with spherical boundary with the addition of a graviton mass term was studied in
Adams et al. 2015
. It was found that the presence of the mass term in the gravitational action lowers the critical temperature, with respect to the non massive case.

In this work, we consider
AdS
space with planar boundary. As mentioned before, in this case there is no HP transition if one considers just the standard Einstein action for this space. We will introduce a graviton mass term in the action and then analyze the thermodynamics of the resulting system. A phase transition at a finite temperature, related to the mass scale of the graviton, will show up.

The paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, we review some illustrating examples of Hawking-Page transitions. In Sec
III
, we present some aspects of massive gravity that are relevant to this work. In Sec.
IV
we discuss the stability of the black hole solution in the massive AdS space and then examine the HP transition between the spaces with and without a black hole, focusing on the system’s thermodynamics. Finally in Sec.
V
our conclusions and final remarks are presented. The detailed computations for the metrics used in this work are provided in Appendix
A
.

II
Hawking-Page transition in
AdS
space

We begin our discussion by revisiting the concept of Hawking Page transition, originally proposed for Euclidean anti-de Sitter space with periodic time coordinate in
Hawking and Page 1983
. The main idea is that in a quantum theory of gravity the more relevant contributions to the
path integral, that sums over all possible metric configurations that are asymptotically
AdS
, comes from the corresponding classical solutions of Einstein equations. There are two solutions, one with a black hole and the other without. The dominant (or stable) is the one with the smallest action integral. This condition mirrors the thermodynamic stability criterion of minimizing the free energy.

II.1
The
AdS_{{}_{D}}
space with
S^{{}^{D-2}}\times S^{1}
boundary

Let us extend the case of spacetime dimension
D=4
considered in
Hawking and Page 1983
to arbitrary dimensions. The Einstein Hilbert action has the form

\mathcal{I}=-\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}\int d^{{}^{D}}x\sqrt{|g|}\left({\mathcal{R}-\Lambda}\right)

(1)

where
\kappa^{2}=8\pi G_{{}_{D}}
with
G_{{}_{D}}
the
D
-dimensional Newton’s constant with dimension
E^{-(D-2)}
,
\mathcal{R}
and
\Lambda
denote respectively the Ricci scalar and cosmological constant. For
AdS
spaces with radius
L
, they depend on the spacetime dimension
D
as follows

\mathcal{R}=-\frac{D(D-1)}{L^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\Lambda=-\frac{(D-1)(D-2)}{L^{2}}\,.

(2)

The black hole metric with spherical symmetry that emerges as a solution of the action (
1
) with imaginary time is

ds^{2}=g_{pq}dx^{p}dx^{q}=V(r)dt^{2}+V(r)^{-1}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega_{n}^{2}\,,

(3)

here
n=D-2
is the number of spatial dimensions in the boundary, and
d\Omega_{n}^{2}
represents the metric of the
n
-dimensional sphere. The function
V(r)
takes the form

V(r)=1-\frac{M}{r^{n-1}}+\frac{r^{2}}{L^{2}}

(4)

with
M
being proportional to the black hole mass. The horizon position
r_{h}
is determined by
V(r_{h})=0
and the Hawking temperature is obtained requiring that there is no conical singularity in the vicinity of the horizon. The result is

T=\frac{(n-1)L^{2}+(n+1)r_{h}^{2}}{4\pi L^{2}r_{h}}\,.

(5)

For the thermal
AdS
space the metric in the same form of (
3
), but with
M=0
, i.e.

V_{0}(r)=1+\frac{r^{2}}{L^{2}}.

(6)

From Eqs. (
1
) and (
2
) one obtains the actions for the black hole
AdS

\mathcal{I}^{(BH)}=\frac{\Omega_{n}}{\kappa^{2}}\frac{\beta}{L^{2}}\left(r_{b}^{n+1}-r_{h}^{n+1}\right)\,,

(7)

and for the thermal
AdS

\mathcal{I}^{(AdS)}=\frac{\Omega_{n}}{\kappa^{2}}\frac{\beta_{0}}{L^{2}}r_{b}^{n+1}\,.

(8)

Here,
r_{b}
denotes a regulator for the boundary position,
\Omega_{n}
is the area of the
n
sphere, while
\beta
and
\beta_{0}
originate from the temporal coordinate integration. These quantities can be related by requiring that the two geometries have the same temporal length at the boundary, yielding

\displaystyle\beta_{0}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{V(r_{b})}{V^{{}^{\prime}}(r_{b})}\right)^{1/2}\beta

\displaystyle\approx\left({1-\frac{L^{2}M}{2r_{b}^{n+1}}}\right)\beta\,,

(9)

where the approximation holds for large
r_{b}
.

To remove the divergences in the limit
r_{b}\to\infty
, one can define the renormalized action difference as

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle\equiv\mathcal{I}^{(BH)}-\mathcal{I}^{(AdS)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Omega_{n}}{2\kappa^{2}}\beta\left({r_{h}^{n-1}-\frac{r_{h}^{n+1}}{L^{2}}}\right),

(10)

where the condition
V(r_{h})=0
was used.

As explained in the beginning of this section, the stable space configuration corresponds to the lowest action. Therefore, for
\Delta\mathcal{I}>0
the thermal
AdS
space is stable, for
\Delta\mathcal{I}<0
the black hole solution becomes the stable one. The phase transition occurs at
\Delta\mathcal{I}=0
, where both configurations have equal action values. This condition determines the critical temperature for the Hawking-Page transition as

T_{c}=\frac{n}{2\pi L}.

(11)

II.2
The
AdS_{{}_{D}}
space with
R^{{}^{D-2}}\times S^{1}
boundary

It was proposed in
Witten 1998
, in the context of gauge gravity duality, that the Hawking-Page transition in the gravitation side corresponds to the confinement/deconfinement phase transition in the gauge theory side.

In order to represent a gauge theory living in a non-compact space, one considers a flat
R^{{}^{D-2}}
spatial boundary geometry. The metric of the black hole in Poincaré coordinates becomes

ds^{2}=\frac{L^{2}}{z^{2}}\left({f(z)dt^{2}+f(z)^{-1}dz^{2}+d\vec{x}_{n}^{2}}\right),

(12)

where the horizon function
f(z)
is given by

f(z)=1-\frac{z^{n+1}}{z_{h}^{n+1}}\,.

(13)

The condition of absence of conical singularity leads to the Hawking temperature

T=\frac{(n+1)}{4\pi}\frac{1}{z_{h}}.

(14)

The metric of the thermal
AdS
is obtained using
f(z)=1
. Following the same procedure as in the case of compact spatial boundary, one obtains the action difference

\Delta\mathcal{I}=-\frac{L^{n}}{2\kappa^{2}}\beta V_{n}\frac{1}{z_{h}^{n+1}}

(15)

where
V_{n}
is the
n
-dimensional spatial volume.

Equation (
15
) is zero only when
z_{h}\to\infty
and the temperature goes to zero by the equation (
14
). For any non-zero temperature
\Delta\mathcal{I}<0
. So, the black hole geometry is always the stable one. In other words, there is no HP transition.

The zero critical temperature is a consequence of the absense of any dimensionful parameter in the gauge string duality in this case. Important physical systems governed by gauge theories, like the quark gluon plasma (QGP), typically exhibit a finite critical temperature. In order to model holographically such systems, various approaches have been proposed that modify the
AdS
geometry in order to to break conformal invariance in the gauge theory side, thereby generating non-zero critical temperatures. Some examples can be found in
Herzog 2007
;
Ballon Bayona et al. 2008
;
Colangelo et al. 2009
;
Colangelo et al. 2012
;
Rinaldi and Vento 2023
;
Braga and Junqueira 2024
;
Braga and Junqueira 2025
. In the following section we will study a different situation where an
energy parameter is naturally present: the case when the gravitons are massive.

III
AdS space with Massive Gravity

The formulation of massive gravity that we use in this work was proposed in
de Rham and Gabadadze 2010
;
de Rham et al. 2011
;
Hassan and Rosen 2011
and used in
Adams et al. 2015
to analyze the HP transition for the case of spherical spatial boundary. One introduces a graviton mass term in the action of the form

{\cal I}_{\text{grav.}}=-\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}\int d^{n+2}x\sqrt{|g|}\;m^{2}\sum^{n+2}_{i=1}c_{i}{\cal U}_{i}

(16)

where
m
is the graviton mass,
c_{i}
are dimensionless constants and
{\cal U}_{i}
are the symmetric polynomial eigenvalues of the matrix
{\cal K}
. We consider only the first two terms of the sum:

\displaystyle{\cal U}_{1}

\displaystyle=\Tr{\cal K},

(17)

\displaystyle{\cal U}_{2}

\displaystyle=\left({\Tr{\cal K}}\right)^{2}-\Tr{\cal K}^{2}.

(18)

The
(n+2)\times(n+2)
matrix
{\cal K}
is defined in terms of the dynamical metric and of a reference metric
f_{\mu\nu}
as follows

\mathcal{K}^{{{\mu}\mathchoice{\makebox[4.14899pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.14899pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.53337pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.80954pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}_{{\mathchoice{\makebox[4.86232pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.86232pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.95248pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[2.10892pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{\rho}}}\mathcal{K}^{{{\rho}\mathchoice{\makebox[4.00928pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.00928pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.42052pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.72893pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}_{{\mathchoice{\makebox[4.14899pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.14899pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.53337pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.80954pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{\nu}}}=g^{\mu\sigma}f_{\sigma\nu}

(19)

Following
Vegh 2013
;
Blake and Tong 2013
, we consider the case of a flat reference metric

f_{\mu\nu}=\sum_{j}\delta^{j}_{\mu}\delta_{\nu}^{j}\,,

(20)

where the sum over
j
runs over the spatial boundary coordinates.

This choice breaks the diffeomorphism invariance in the spatial coordinates of the boundary, leading to non-conservation of spatial momentum in the boundary theory. For more details about massive gravity theory see the reviews
de Rham 2014
;
Hinterbichler 2012
.

Considering that only
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are non-zero in the graviton action (
16
), one can write an effective action as

{\cal I}=-\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}\int d^{n+2}x\sqrt{|g|}\left({\mathcal{R}-\Lambda}\right)-\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}\int d^{n+2}x\sqrt{|g|}\left({\lambda_{1}\Tr{\cal K}+\lambda_{2}\left[\left({\Tr{\cal K}}\right)^{2}-\Tr{\cal K}^{2}\right]}\right),

(21)

where we have defined
\lambda_{1}\equiv c_{1}m^{2}
and
\lambda_{2}\equiv c_{2}m^{2}
for simplicity, both parameters having dimensions of energy squared.

The black hole metric solution of the action (
21
) that is asymptotically
AdS
and has planar symmetry in the spatial coordinates has the form

ds^{2}=\frac{L^{2}}{z^{2}}\left(f(z)dt^{2}+f(z)^{-1}dz^{2}+d\vec{x}_{n}^{2}\right),

(22)

with a modified horizon function given by

f(z)=1+\frac{\lambda_{1}L}{n}z+\lambda_{2}z^{2}-\frac{z^{n+1}}{z_{h}^{n+1}}\left({1+\frac{\lambda_{1}L}{n}z_{h}+\lambda_{2}z_{h}^{2}}\right).

(23)

The derivation of this solution is presented in Appendix
A
. The Hawking temperature is obtained by requiring regularity in imaginary time, giving

T=\frac{1}{4\pi z_{h}}\left({\left({n+1}\right)+\lambda_{1}Lz_{h}+\left({n-1}\right)\lambda_{2}z_{h}^{2}}\right),

(24)

remembering that
z_{h}
denotes the horizon position. The solution without a black hole corresponds to the limit
z_{h}\to\infty
, which is equivalent to setting the black hole mass to zero, this leads to

f_{0}(z)=1+\frac{\lambda_{1}L}{n}z+\lambda_{2}z^{2}.

(25)

IV
HP transition in Massive AdS space

IV.1
Regularized action

Let us first calculate the gravitational term of action (
21
). Using the same cosmological constant from Eq. (
2
), expressed in terms of
n
, one obtains:

{\cal I}_{1}=-\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}\int d^{n+2}x\sqrt{|g|}\left({\mathcal{R}+\frac{n(n+1)}{L^{2}}}\right)\,.

(26)

The additional terms in the action (
21
) modify the Ricci scalar, which becomes

{\cal R}=-\frac{(n+1)(n+2)}{L^{2}}-\frac{(n+1)\lambda_{1}Lz}{L^{2}}-\frac{n(n-1)\lambda_{2}z^{2}}{L^{2}}\,,

(27)

using this result in equation (
26
) yelds

{\cal I}_{1}=\frac{L^{n}}{2\kappa^{2}}\beta V_{n}\int^{z_{h}}_{\varepsilon}dz\left({\frac{2(n+1)}{z^{n+2}}+\frac{(n+1)\lambda_{1}L}{z^{n+1}}+\frac{n(n-1)\lambda_{2}}{z^{n}}}\right)\,,

(28)

where
V_{n}
and
\beta
originate from the integration over the boundary coordinates and
\varepsilon
is a regularization parameter. Integrating in the holographic coordinate one finds:

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{1}=\frac{L^{n}}{\kappa^{2}}\beta V_{n}\left(-\frac{1}{z_{h}^{n+1}}-\frac{(n+1)}{2n}\frac{\lambda_{1}L}{z_{h}^{n}}-\frac{n}{2}\frac{\lambda_{2}}{z_{h}^{n-1}}\right.+\left.\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{n+1}}+\frac{(n+1)}{2n}\frac{\lambda_{1}L}{\varepsilon^{n}}+\frac{n}{2}\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\varepsilon^{n-1}}\right).

(29)

Now let us consider the term linear in the trace of
{\cal K}
in the action (
21
)

{\cal I}_{2}=-\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}\int d^{n+2}x\sqrt{|g|}\,\lambda_{1}\Tr{\cal K}\,.

(30)

Using the definition (
19
) and the metric (
22
), the trace reduces to

\Tr{\cal K}=n\frac{z}{L}\,.

(31)

Consequently, the action (
30
) becomes

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}\beta V_{n}\int^{z_{h}}_{\varepsilon}dz\frac{L^{n+2}}{z^{n+2}}\lambda_{1}\,n\frac{z}{L}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}}\beta V_{n}\left({\frac{\lambda_{1}L}{2z_{h}^{n}}-\frac{\lambda_{1}L}{2\varepsilon^{n}}}\right).

(32)

The last term in the massive gravity action consists of the quadratic combination

{\cal I}_{3}=-\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}\int d^{n+2}x\sqrt{|g|}\,\lambda_{2}\left[\left({\Tr{\cal K}}\right)^{2}-\Tr{\cal K}^{2}\right].

(33)

For the diagonal matrix
{\cal K}
, this combination simplifies to

\left[\left({\Tr{\cal K}}\right)^{2}-\Tr{\cal K}^{2}\right]=n\left({n-1}\right)\frac{z^{2}}{L^{2}}\,.

(34)

Inserting this result into (
33
) yields

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{3}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}\beta V_{n}\int^{z_{h}}_{\varepsilon}\frac{L^{n+2}}{z^{n+2}}\lambda_{2}\,n(n-1)\frac{z^{2}}{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{L^{n}}{\kappa^{2}}\beta V_{n}\left({\frac{n\lambda_{2}}{2z_{h}^{n-1}}-\frac{n\lambda_{2}}{2\varepsilon^{n-1}}}\right).

(35)

Summing the three contributions yields the total action for the planar black hole solution in
AdS
massive gravity

{\cal I}^{(BH)}=\frac{L^{n}}{\kappa^{2}}\beta V_{n}\left({-\frac{1}{z_{h}^{n+1}}-\frac{\lambda_{1}L}{2n}\frac{1}{z_{h}^{n}}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{n+1}}+\frac{\lambda_{1}L}{2n}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{n}}}\right).

(36)

The action for the space without a black hole is obtained by taking the limit
z_{h}\to\infty
:

{\cal I}^{(AdS)}=\frac{L^{n}}{\kappa^{2}}\beta_{0}V_{n}\left({\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{n+1}}+\frac{\lambda_{1}L}{2n}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{n}}}\right).

(37)

As in the spherically symmetric
AdS
case, one can relate the periods
\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
of the time coordinates by matching the asymptotic limits of the two geometries on the boundary

\displaystyle\beta_{0}

\displaystyle=\left({\frac{f(\varepsilon)}{f_{0}(\varepsilon)}}\right)^{1/2}\beta

\displaystyle\approx\left({1-\frac{\varepsilon^{n+1}}{2z_{h}^{n+1}}-\frac{\lambda_{1}L}{2n}\frac{\varepsilon^{n+1}}{z_{h}^{n}}-\frac{\lambda_{2}}{2}\frac{\varepsilon^{n+1}}{z_{h}^{n-1}}}\right)\beta\,,

(38)

where the expansion about
\varepsilon\to 0
was used. Substituting this result into the action (
37
) yields

{\cal I}^{(AdS)}=\frac{L^{n}}{\kappa^{2}}\beta V_{n}\left({\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{n+1}}-\frac{1}{2z_{h}^{n+1}}-\frac{\lambda_{1}L}{2n}\frac{1}{z_{h}^{n}}+\frac{\lambda_{1}L}{2n}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{n}}-\frac{\lambda_{2}}{2z_{h}^{n-1}}}\right)+{\cal O}(\varepsilon)\,.

(39)

The regularized action is obtained through the subtraction scheme as

\displaystyle\Delta{\cal I}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left({\cal I}^{(BH)}-{\cal I}^{(AdS)}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{L^{n}}{2\kappa^{2}}\beta V_{n}\left({\frac{1}{z_{h}^{n+1}}-\frac{\lambda_{2}}{z_{h}^{n-1}}}\right).

(40)

IV.2
Stability of the black hole solution

Before we proceed to the Hawking-Page calculations, it is important to analyze the thermodynamic stability of the black hole solution. Its temperature is given by the equation (
24
), having two competing terms: one that decreases and another that increases with
z_{h}
. The resulting variation of the temperature with
z_{h}
, for
n=2
, is illustrated in Fig
1(a)
. The temperature has a minimum when the horizon position
z_{h}
has the value:

z_{0}=\left({\frac{n+1}{n-1}}\right)^{1/2}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}}}\,.

(41)

The corresponding minimum temperature that the black hole can reach is:

T_{0}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left({n^{2}-1}\right)^{1/2}\sqrt{\lambda_{2}}+\frac{\lambda_{1}L}{4\pi}\,.

(42)

Equation (
41
) requires that
\lambda_{2}
can take only positive real values. On the other hand, the parameter
\lambda_{1}L
appears only in equation (
42
) as a temperature shift and, in principle, can assume any real value. However, if
\lambda_{1}L
is negative it is necessary to impose the condition

\lambda_{1}L\geq-4\left({n^{2}-1}\right)^{1/2}\sqrt{\lambda_{2}}\,,

(43)

to avoid
T_{0}
from being negative.

(a)

(b)

Figure 1
:

(a) Schematic plot of the temperature given by the equation (
24
). The dashed gray line is the case when
\lambda_{2}=0
, for which temperature of the planar
AdS
black hole is recovered. (b) Heat capacity for various values of
\lambda_{1}L
. The dashed gray line is the
z_{0}
. We set
n=2
and
L^{2}V_{2}/k^{2}=1
in the plot.

The relevant quantities to analyze the black hole thermodynamic stability are the entropy and its derivative, which is related to the heat capacity. The connection with the canonical ensemble thermodynamics is established through the relation between the action and the partition function,
{\cal Z}=e^{-\Delta{\cal I}}
. Consequently

\ln{\cal Z}=-\Delta{\cal I}=\frac{L^{n}}{2\kappa^{2}}\beta V_{n}\left({\frac{1}{z_{h}^{n+1}}-\frac{\lambda_{2}}{z_{h}^{n-1}}}\right).

(44)

The entropy can be expressed as

S=\beta\langle E\rangle+\ln{\cal Z}

(45)

where
\langle E\rangle
is the internal energy, which can be obtained by the relation

\langle E\rangle=-\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\ln{\cal Z}\,.

(46)

Using the equation (
44
), one gets

\displaystyle\langle E\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{L^{n}}{2\kappa^{2}}V_{n}\left[\left({\frac{1}{z_{h}^{n+1}}-\frac{\lambda_{2}}{z_{h}^{n-1}}}\right)+\beta\left({-\frac{\left({n+1}\right)}{z_{h}^{n+2}}\frac{\partial z_{h}}{\partial\beta}+\frac{\left({n-1}\right)\lambda_{2}}{z^{n}_{h}}\frac{\partial z_{h}}{\partial\beta}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{L^{n}}{2\kappa^{2}}V_{n}\left[\frac{n}{z_{h}^{n+1}}+\frac{n\lambda_{2}}{z_{h}^{n-1}}+\frac{\lambda_{1}L}{z_{h}^{n}}\right],

(47)

where the simplification from the first to the second line is obtained using

\frac{\partial z_{h}}{\partial\beta}=\frac{z_{h}}{\beta}\frac{\left({n+1}\right)+\lambda_{1}Lz_{h}+(n-1)z_{h}^{2}}{\left({n+1}\right)-\left({n-1}\right)\lambda_{2}z_{h}^{2}}\,,

(48)

derived from (
24
). Note that this relation expresses the black hole’s response to temperature changes. Another interesting observation is that besides the geometric quantities
L
and
z_{h}
, the parameters
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
, and consequently the graviton mass, contribute to the system’s internal energy in equation (
IV.2
).

Substituting equations (
44
) and (
IV.2
) into the entropy definition (
45
) one finds

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\frac{L^{n}}{2\kappa^{2}}\beta V_{n}\frac{1}{z_{h}^{n+1}}\left[\left({n+1}\right)+\lambda_{1}Lz_{h}+\left({n-1}\right)\lambda_{2}z_{h}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{L^{n}}{4G_{D}}V_{n}\frac{1}{z_{h}^{n}}\,.

(49)

So, one finds that the entropy is a monotonically decreasing function of the horizon position that would go to zero in the limit
z_{h}\to\infty
. However, as shown in the Fig.
1(a)
and by equation (
24
), for
z_{h}>z_{0}
the temperature increases with
z_{h}
. Therefore, in this region one would find that the entropy would decrease with temperature. This would violate the thermodynamic laws and indicates that the black hole solutions are unstable in this region.

In order to illustrate the consequences of the decrease of the entropy with temperature, let us calculate the heat capacity for this system. Using the definition

C=T\left({\frac{\partial S}{\partial T}}\right)

(50)

and equation (
49
), one obtains

C=\frac{nL^{n}}{4G_{D}}\frac{V_{n}}{z_{h}^{n}}\left({\frac{\left({n+1}\right)+\lambda_{1}Lz_{h}+\left({n-1}\right)\lambda_{2}z_{h}^{2}}{\left({n+1}\right)-\left({n-1}\right)\lambda_{2}z_{h}^{2}}}\right).

(51)

This expression is plotted in Fig.
1(b)
. From the denominator in parentheses, one notes that the heat capacity exhibits a discontinuity at
z_{h}=z_{0}
where
C\to\pm\infty
. For
z_{h}>z_{0}
the quadratic term in the denominator dominates, so

C
is negative in this region. This characterizes the instability of black hole solutions in this region.
For
z_{h}<z_{0}
, the heat capacity is always positive and the BH solutions are stable.

Thus, for a HP transition to occur the critical horizon position must be smaller than
z_{0}
; or, in terms of the temperature, the critical temperature must be higher than
T_{0}
.

IV.3
Hawking-Page Transition

As explained in the previous section, the transition occurs at
\Delta{\cal I}=0
. For
\Delta{\cal I}>0
the solution without the black hole is the stable phase, while for
\Delta{\cal I}<0
the BH solution is stable. This condition determines, from Eq. (
IV.1
) the critical horizon position
z_{c}

\displaystyle z_{c}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}}}\,.

(52)

This result is illustrated in Fig.
2

where
\Delta{\cal I}
is plotted for different values of
\lambda_{2}
. The critical horizon positions correspond to the values of
z_{h}
where the plots cross the horizontal axis. Note that
z_{c}
depends only on
\lambda_{2}
, being independent of both space dimension and the parameter
\lambda_{1}
. The corresponding critical Hawking-Page temperature is given by

T_{c}=\frac{n}{2\pi}\sqrt{\lambda_{2}}+\frac{\lambda_{1}L}{4\pi}\,.

(53)

V
Conclusion

In this work, we considered a formulation of massive gravity like the ones studied in
Hassan and Rosen 2011
;
Adams et al. 2015
;
de Rham and Gabadadze 2010
;
de Rham et al. 2011
;
Hassan et al. 2012
. Since this formulation breaks the diffeomorphism invariance
Vegh 2013
;
Blake and Tong 2013
, it is used in order to construct a holographic description of systems without momentum conservation in DC conductivity study.

Reference
Adams et al. 2015
presents an investigation of the Hawking-Page transition in an
AdS_{4}
space with an
S^{2}\times S^{1}
boundary, within massive gravity context. It shows that the higher the graviton mass, the lower is the critical temperature.

In most of the applications of holography, the manifolds involved have a planar spatial boundary. This was the main motivation for the present work, were we extended the analysis of
Adams et al. 2015
to the case
of a
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\,
S
1
boundary. Our main result is given in equation (
53
), where we find a non-vanishing critical temperature, proportional to the parameter
\lambda_{2}
, which encodes the graviton mass. This shows that in massive gravity one finds a Hawking-Page transition even in the case of a flat spatial boundary.

An interesting point to be remarked is that the presence of a massive graviton makes the black hole unstable within a certain temperature range. This thermodynamically instability was carefully examined, and we obtained the minimum temperature
T_{0}
for the black hole to exist, highlighting the role of dimensionality in this behavior.

We also discussed the particular three-dimensional case, which does not exhibit a Hawking-Page transition. The black hole is always stable. Consequently, we identify
d=4
as the lowest critical dimension for the transition occurs.

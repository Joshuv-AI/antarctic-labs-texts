Excess Observables Reveal Nonreciprocity in Integrated Covariance
Timur Aslyamov, Massimiliano Esposito

Abstract
Near equilibrium, the symmetric part of the time-integrated steady-state covariance, i.e., the time integral of correlation functions, is governed by the fluctuation-dissipation theorem, while the antisymmetric part vanishes due to Onsager reciprocity. Far from equilibrium, where these principles no longer apply, we develop a unified formalism for both symmetric and antisymmetric components of integrated covariances. We derive exact, computationally tractable expressions for these quantities, valid in arbitrary nonequilibrium steady states of Markov jump processes. Both components are expressed in terms of excess observables, a notion central to both statistical physics and reinforcement learning. Furthermore, we establish thermodynamic upper bounds for these covariances in terms of entropy production, dynamical activity, and cycle affinities.

In a NESS, physical observables

\displaystyle C^{\tau}_{yx}\equiv\langle\Delta y(t+\tau)\Delta x(t)\rangle=\langle\Delta y(\tau)\Delta x(0)\rangle\,.

Symmetrical integrated covariance (SICov) is a derived quantity defined as

\displaystyle\langle\langle y,x\rangle\rangle_{+}\equiv\int_{0}^{\infty}d\tau(C_{yx}^{\tau}+C_{xy}^{\tau})\,.

\displaystyle\langle\langle y,x\rangle\rangle_{-}\equiv\int_{0}^{\infty}(C_{yx}^{\tau}-C_{xy}^{\tau})d\tau\,.

Setup—
We consider a Markov jump process over
N
discrete states with steady-state probability
\bm{\pi}=(\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{N})^{\intercal}
satisfying
\sum_{n}W_{mn}\pi_{n}=0
, where
W_{mn}
denotes the transition rate from state

to state

, and these rates form the elements of the rate matrix

. We introduce a state observable

which is a function of the state, that is,
\bm{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})^{\intercal}
.
We assume that the Markov jump process is ergodic and always relaxes to the unique steady-state probability

. Therefore, the ensemble average of such an observable coincides with its time average along any stochastic trajectory, regardless of its initial condition:

\displaystyle\langle x\rangle\equiv\sum_{n}x_{n}\pi_{n}

\displaystyle=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}dt\mathcal{E}\big[x_{n_{t}}\big|n_{0}=m\big]\,,

(4)

where
\mathcal{E}
denotes the average over stochastic trajectories
\{(0,n_{0}),

(t_{1},n_{t_{1}}),

\dots,

(T,n_{T})\}
, with
n_{t}
randomly taking values from the state space
\{1,\dots,N\}
and initial condition
n_{0}=m
.

Excess observables—
The excess observable corresponding to the state observable

will play a central role in our theory and is defined as

\displaystyle X_{m}\equiv\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\Big(\mathcal{E}\big[x_{n_{t}}\big|n_{0}=m\big]-\langle x\rangle\Big)\,.

(5)

Unlike
Eq.

4

Fig.

1
a. It can be calculated using the propagator

as

\displaystyle X_{m}=\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\Big[e^{t\mathbb{W}^{\intercal}}\Delta\bm{x}\Big]_{m}\,,

(6)

where
\Delta\bm{x}\equiv\bm{x}-\langle x\rangle\bm{1}

with
\bm{1}=(1,\dots,1)^{\intercal}
, and where

denotes the
m
th element of the vector. Noting that

, we can rewrite

\displaystyle\bm{X}=-(\mathbb{W}^{D})^{\intercal}\Delta\bm{x}\,,

(7)

where
\bm{X}\equiv(X_{1},\dots,X_{N})^{\intercal}
, and where we introduced the Drazin inverse of the rate matrix

;
crook2018drazin

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}^{D}

\displaystyle\equiv-\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\tau\mathbb{W}}\big[\mathbb{1}-\bm{\pi}\bm{1}^{\intercal}\big]d\tau\,,

(8a)

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}^{D}\mathbb{W}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{W}\mathbb{W}^{D}=\mathbb{1}-\bm{\pi}\bm{1}^{\intercal}\,,

(8b)

where
\mathbb{1}
is the identity matrix.
Since
\mathbb{W}^{D}
has the same left and right eigenvectors as
\mathbb{W}
, we find that the ensemble average of an excess observable vanishes

\displaystyle\langle X\rangle\equiv\bm{\pi}^{\intercal}\bm{X}=-(\mathbb{W}^{D}\bm{\pi})^{\intercal}\Delta\bm{x}=0\,.

(9)

Excess observables have been related to notions of excess heat and entropy

, excess observables can be expressed in terms of MFPTs as

\displaystyle T_{nm}\equiv\int t_{nm}f(t_{nm})dt_{nm}\,,

with the first passage time

Symmetric and antisymmetric integrals—
The covariance between two state observables

and

\displaystyle C_{yx}^{\tau}\equiv\Delta\bm{y}^{\intercal}e^{\tau\mathbb{W}}\text{diag}(\bm{\pi})\Delta\bm{x}\,,

\displaystyle=\Delta\bm{y}^{\intercal}\mathbb{C}^{-}\Delta\bm{x}\,,

AICov and excess observables—
Our third main result will be to derive an exact expression for the AICov in terms of excess observables.
Using

\displaystyle\langle\langle y,x\rangle\rangle_{-}

\displaystyle=\bm{Y}^{\intercal}\mathbb{J}\,\bm{X}=-\bm{X}^{\intercal}\mathbb{J}\,\bm{Y}\,.

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\langle y,x\rangle\rangle_{-}}{\langle\langle x,x\rangle\rangle_{+}+\langle\langle y,y\rangle\rangle_{+}}=\frac{2\sum_{k<l}J_{kl}\Omega_{kl}}{\sum_{k<l}A_{kl}L_{lk}^{2}}\,,

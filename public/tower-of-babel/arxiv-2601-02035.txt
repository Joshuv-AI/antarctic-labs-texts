Sub-Laplacian generalized curvature dimension inequalities on Riemannian foliations
Fabrice Baudoin , Guang Yang

Abstract
We develop a Bochner theory and Bakry-Émery calculus for horizontal Laplacians associated with general Riemannian foliations. No bundle-like assumption on the metric, nor any total geodesicity or minimality condition on the leaves, is imposed. Using a metric connection adapted to the horizontal–vertical splitting, we derive explicit Bochner formulas for the horizontal Laplacian acting on horizontal and vertical gradients, as well as a unified identity for the full gradient. These formulas involve horizontal Ricci curvature, torsion, and vertical mean curvature terms intrinsic to the foliated structure. From these identities, we establish generalized curvature–dimension inequalities, extending earlier results in sub-Riemannian geometry. As applications, we obtain horizontal Laplacian comparison theorems, Bonnet–Myers type compactness results with explicit diameter bounds, stochastic completeness, first eigenvalue estimates and gradient and regularization estimates for the horizontal heat semigroup. The framework applies, in particular, to contact manifolds and Carnot groups of arbitrary step. Keywords: Sub-Riemannian geometry, Sub-Laplacian, Generalized curvature dimension inequality, Riemannian foliation.

Introduction

Subelliptic operators arising from geometric structures with bracket-generating distributions play a central role in analysis (
[
21
,
23
]
), geometry (
[
1
]
), and probability (
[
27
]
). Among these, horizontal Laplacians associated with foliations constitute a flexible framework that allows for the use of Riemannian techniques to capture genuine sub-Riemannian phenomena. Those horizontal Laplacians naturally arise in a wide range of settings, including contact manifolds, Carnot groups, and sub-Riemannian manifolds with transverse symmetries.

The purpose of this article is to develop a systematic Bochner–Bakry–Émery theory for horizontal Laplacians on general Riemannian foliations, without assuming that the Riemannian metric is bundle-like, nor that the leaves are totally geodesic or minimal. Removing these classical assumptions introduces new geometric features most notably torsion and mean curvature effects that fundamentally alter the structure of Bochner identities and curvature dimension inequalities. The main goal of this work is to show that, despite this increased complexity, a robust framework can still be established and exploited to derive meaningful analytic and geometric consequences.

1.1
Geometric setting

Let
(M,g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold endowed with a foliation
\mathcal{F}
whose tangent bundle
TM
splits orthogonally into horizontal and vertical subbundles

TM=\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{V},

where
\mathcal{V}
is tangent to the leaves. In contrast with much of the existing literature, we do not assume that:

•

the Riemannian metric is bundle-like;

•

the leaves are totally geodesic or minimal.

These assumptions, while technically convenient, exclude many natural examples. In particular, Carnot groups of step greater than two and general contact manifolds fall outside the classical framework. In such situations, the interaction between curvature, torsion, and the mean curvature of the leaves plays a crucial role and must be explicitly accounted for.

The horizontal Laplacian
\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}
considered here is defined as the divergence of the horizontal gradient with respect to the Riemannian volume measure.
We assume throughout that the horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}
is bracket generating. Under the bracket-generating assumption and completeness of
g
, this operator is locally subelliptic, essentially self-adjoint and generates a sub-Markovian heat semigroup
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
which admits a heat kernel.

1.2
Bochner’s identities beyond the classical setting

In the Riemannian case, lower Ricci curvature bounds and their analytic consequences are classically derived from Bochner’s identity and the Bakry–Émery
\Gamma_{2}
-calculus, see the monograph
[
3
]
. Extending this approach to sub-Riemannian and foliated contexts has been the subject of extensive research over the past two decades, see
[
5
,
9
,
11
,
18
,
29
]
. Early developments focused primarily on Riemannian foliations with bundle-like metrics and totally geodesic leaves, where the horizontal Laplacian enjoys additional symmetry properties and Bochner-type formulas more closely resemble their Riemannian counterparts. The present work addresses this gap by providing general Bochner’s formulas and Bakry-Émery calculus for the horizontal Laplacian of arbitrary Riemannian foliations.

A key ingredient of our approach is the use of a metric connection
\nabla
that is adapted to the splitting
TM=\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{V}
. This connection, first introduced by Hladky in
[
19
]
and then used in the contect of foliations in
[
7
,
6
]
, preserves both subbundles and has torsion encoding the non-integrability of the horizontal distribution and the second fundamental form of the leaves. While the Levi–Civita connection is poorly suited for computations in this setting, the adapted connection allows for a transparent decomposition of second-order quantities into horizontal and vertical components. Using this connection, the first main contribution of this work is the derivation of explicit Bochner formulas for both horizontal and vertical directions. Specifically, for a smooth function
f
, we express the quantities:

\Gamma_{2}^{\mathcal{H}}(f):=\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f|^{2}-\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}f\rangle\quad\text{and}\quad\Gamma_{2}^{\mathcal{V}}(f):=\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f|^{2}-\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}f\rangle

in terms of tensors related to the connection
\nabla
. Here
\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}
is the horizontal gradient and
\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}
the vertical one. These formulas incorporate the mean curvature vector field
H
of the leaves, curvature tensors related to the horizontal distribution and the torsion of the connection. By combining the horizontal and vertical identities, we also obtain a Bochner formula for the full Riemannian gradient.

1.3
Generalized curvature dimension inequalities

From the Bochner identities, we establish generalized curvature–dimension inequalities in the sense of Bakry–Émery.
As a first application, we identify a tensorial quantity
\mathfrak{R}
that plays the effective role of the Ricci curvature in our setting and controls the full gradient Bochner’s formula for
\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}
. Under suitable lower bounds on
\mathfrak{R}
, we establish inequalities of the form

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}^{\mathcal{H}}(f)+\Gamma_{2}^{\mathcal{V}}(f)\geq\frac{1}{N}(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}f)^{2}+K\lvert\nabla f\rvert^{2},

(1.1)

for appropriate constants
N
and
K
. Under stronger curvature bound conditions, we also prove the following one-parameter family of curvature dimension inequalities: For every
\nu>0

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\mathcal{H}}_{2}(f)+\nu\Gamma_{2}^{\mathcal{V}}(f)\geq\frac{1}{N}(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}f)^{2}+\left(\rho_{1}-\frac{\kappa}{\nu}\right)|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f|^{2}+(\rho_{2}-\rho_{3}\nu-\rho_{4}\nu^{2})|\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f|^{2}.

(1.2)

This generalized curvature dimension inequality is a direct generalization of the curvature dimension inequality

\Gamma^{\mathcal{H}}_{2}(f)+\nu\Gamma_{2}^{\mathcal{V}}(f)\geq\frac{1}{N}(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}f)^{2}+\left(\rho_{1}-\frac{\kappa}{\nu}\right)|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f|^{2}+\rho_{2}|\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f|^{2}

obtained in
[
9
]
and
[
18
]
in the framework of bundle-like and totally geodesic foliations. It is also a generalization of the curvature dimension inequality obtained for contact manifolds in
[
11
]
. Therefore a novelty in our curvature dimension inequalities is the appearance of the strongly nonlinear term
-\rho_{4}\nu^{2}
, in front of the vertical gradient norm, which makes the analysis substantially more delicate. Geometrically, up to a constant,
\rho_{4}
is a uniform upper bound on the norm of the Lie derivative
\mathcal{L}_{V}g_{\mathcal{H}}
,
V\in\mathcal{V}
,
|V|=1
, therefore quantifying the lack of bundle-like property for
g
. Nevertheless, we show that this generalized curvature–dimension framework remains powerful enough to derive a wide range of analytic and geometric results.

1.4
Applications

As applications of the curvature–dimension inequalities, we obtain:

(i)

Laplacian Comparison Theorem:
We generalize the horizontal Laplacian comparison theorem for the horizontal Laplacian of the Riemannian distance to settings without the bundle-like condition or minimal leaf assumptions. Removing those conditions therefore make our result a generalization of the corresponding result found in
[
10
]
, see also
[
6
]
and
[
15
]
.

(ii)

Bonnet-Myers Type Result:
As a consequence of the Laplacian comparison theorem, we show that under a positive curvature condition, the manifold
M
must be compact with an explicit diameter bound on the Riemannian diameter of the space. More precisely, we show that (
1.1
) with
K>0
yields

\mathbf{diam}(M)\leq\pi\sqrt{\frac{N}{K}}.

(iii)

Stochastic completeness:
We prove that the horizontal heat semigroup
P_{t}
is stochastically complete, i.e.
P_{t}1=1
.

(iv)

First Eigenvalue Lichnerowicz Type estimates:
In the positive curvature case we obtain lower bounds for the first eigenvalue of the horizontal Laplacian of the type

\lambda_{1}\geq C

where
C>0
is a constant explicitly depending on curvature parameters. For instance, under (
1.2
) one can take
C=\frac{\rho_{1}\rho_{2}-\kappa(\rho_{3}+\sqrt{\rho_{2}\rho_{4}})}{\left(\frac{N-1}{N}\right)\rho_{2}+\kappa}
.

(v)

Heat Kernel Gradient Bounds:
We establish Bakry-Émery type estimates and global regularization estimates for the horizontal heat semigroup
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. For example, we obtain the gradient estimate

\displaystyle|\nabla P_{t}f|^{2}+\frac{2}{N}\frac{e^{2Kt}-1}{2K}(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t}f)^{2}\leq e^{2Kt}P_{t}(|\nabla f|^{2})

(1.3)

and on uniformly step-two generating distributions, we obtain in small times a reverse Poincaré type estimate:

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t}f|^{2}+t(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t}f)^{2}\leq\frac{c}{t}\left(P_{t}(f^{2})-(P_{t}f)^{2}\right).

(1.4)

We note that Li–Yau type gradient estimates for the heat kernel are also expected to hold in our framework in light of
[
13
,
20
]
and
[
16
]
and will possibly be studied in a later work.

1.5
Structure of the paper

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2, we introduce the geometric framework, define the horizontal Laplacian and the adapted connection, and recall basic analytic properties of the associated heat semigroup.
Section 3 is devoted to the derivation of Bochner formulas and curvature–dimension inequalities.
In Section 4, we present applications, including Laplacian comparison theorems, eigenvalue estimates, and gradient bounds for the horizontal heat semigroup.

Preliminaries

2.1
Setup and assumptions

Throughout the paper, we consider a smooth connected
n+m
dimensional manifold
M
which is equipped with a foliation
\mathcal{F}
with
m
dimensional leaves. We assume that
M
is equipped with a complete Riemannian metric
g
. For
x\in M
,
\mathcal{F}_{x}
denotes the leaf going through
x
. The sub-bundle
\mathcal{V}
of the tangent bundle
TM
formed by vectors tangent to the leaves is referred to as the set of
vertical directions
. The sub-bundle
\mathcal{H}
which is normal to
\mathcal{V}
is referred to as the set of
horizontal directions
. We assume that
\mathcal{H}
is bracket generating.

In this setting, any vector
u\in T_{x}M
can be decomposed as

u=u_{\mathcal{H}}+u_{\mathcal{V}}

where
u_{\mathcal{H}}
(resp.
u_{\mathcal{V}}
) denotes the orthogonal projection of
u
onto
\mathcal{H}_{x}
(resp.
\mathcal{V}_{x}
).

Note that here
we do not assume
that the metric
g
is bundle-like. Throughout the paper we will denote the Levi-Civita connection on
(M,g)
by
D
.
We refer to the classical reference
[
28
]
or the more recent monograph
[
17
]
for an overview of the theory of foliations.
Some examples of such structures include the following.

Example 2.1
.

(Contact manifolds)
Let
(\mathbb{M},\theta)
be a
2n+1
-dimensional smooth contact manifold with Reeb vector field
\xi
. The Reeb foliation on
\mathbb{M}
is given by the orbits of
\xi
. From
[
25
]
, it is always possible to find a Riemannian metric
g
and a
(1,1)
-tensor field
J
on
\mathbb{M}
so that for all vector fields
X,Y

g(X,\xi)=\theta(X),\quad J^{2}(X)=-X+\theta(X)\xi,\quad 2g(JX,Y)=d\theta(X,Y).

(2.1)

The triple
(\mathbb{M},\theta,g)
is called a contact Riemannian manifold. Observe that the horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}
is the kernel of
\theta
and that
\mathcal{H}
is bracket-generating because
\theta
is a contact form and thus non-degenerate. In the Sasakian case, the Reeb foliation is totally geodesic and the the metric is bundle-like.

Example 2.2
.

(Carnot groups)
A large class of examples that also fit our framework is the class of Carnot groups. A Carnot group is a connected, simply connected nilpotent Lie group
G
whose Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
admits a stratification

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{s},

(2.2)

with the properties:

(i)

[V_{1},V_{j}]=V_{j+1}
for every
1\leq j<s
,

(ii)

[V_{1},V_{s}]=\{0\}
.

The integer
s
is called the step of the Carnot group, and
V_{1}
is called the horizontal layer (or first layer). Consider on
\mathfrak{g}
an arbitrary inner product that makes the decomposition (
2.2
) orthogonal, i.e. for
i\neq j
,
V_{i}\perp V_{j}
. This inner product uniquely defines a left-invariant Riemannian metric
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
on
G
. We can orthogonally decompose the tangent bundle
TG
as

TG=\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{V}

where
\mathcal{H}
is the left invariant sub-bundle which gives
V_{1}
at the identity and
\mathcal{V}
is the left invariant sub-bundle which gives
\oplus_{i\geq 2}V_{i}
at the identity. Since
[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}]\subset\mathcal{V}
,
\mathcal{V}
is the vertical bundle of a foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
G
. Note that
\mathcal{H}
is bracket generating.

2.2
Horizontal Laplacian and heat kernel

Horizontal Laplacian.

The Riemannian gradient of a function
f
will be denoted by
\nabla f
and the horizontal gradient by
\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f
: it is simply defined as the projection of
\nabla f
onto
\mathcal{H}
. Similarly,
\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}
denotes the vertical gradient. The horizontal Laplacian
\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}
is the generator of the symmetric
L^{2}(M,\mu)
-closable bilinear form:

\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{H}}(f,g)=-\int_{\mathbb{M}}\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\,d\mu,\quad f,g\in C_{0}^{\infty}(M),

where
\mu
denotes the Riemannian volume measure on
M
and
C_{0}^{\infty}(M)
the space of smooth and compactly supported functions on
M
.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}
is a local orthonormal frame of horizontal vector fields then we locally have

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(D_{X_{i}}X_{i})_{\mathcal{H}}-\mathrm{H}.

(2.3)

where
\mathrm{H}
is the mean curvature vector field of the leaves.

Proof.

If
f,g\in C_{0}^{\infty}(M)
have a support small enough, then

\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{H}}(f,g)=-\int_{\mathbb{M}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}f)(X_{i}g)\,d\mu.

Therefore, we locally have

\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{*}X_{i},

where
X_{i}^{*}
is the formal adjoint of
X_{i}
in
L^{2}(M,\mu)
. Let now
(Z_{\ell}),1\leq\ell\leq m
be a local vertical orthonormal frame. Since
\mu
is the Riemannian volume measure, it is easy to check that

X_{i}^{*}=-X_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\langle D_{X_{j}}X_{j},X_{i}\right\rangle+\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\left\langle D_{Z_{\ell}}Z_{\ell},X_{i}\right\rangle.

Therefore, we have

\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(D_{X_{i}}X_{i})_{\mathcal{H}}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}(D_{Z_{\ell}}Z_{\ell})_{\mathcal{H}}.

Since the mean curvature vector field of a leaf is given by the trace of the second fundamental form
\Pi(U,V)=(D_{U}V)_{\mathcal{H}}
,
U,V\in\mathcal{V}
, we have

\mathrm{H}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}(D_{Z_{\ell}}Z_{\ell})_{\mathcal{H}}.

The conclusion follows.
∎

Heat kernel.

The hypothesis that
\mathcal{H}
is bracket generating implies that the horizontal Laplacian
\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}
is locally subelliptic and the completeness assumption on
g
implies that
\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}
is furthermore essentially self-adjoint on
C_{0}^{\infty}(M)
and the construction of the heat kernel is then classical, see for instance
[
5
]
). The self-adjoint extension is still denoted by
\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}
. If
\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}=-\int_{0}^{+\infty}\lambda dE_{\lambda}
denotes the spectral
decomposition of
\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}
in
L^{2}(M,\mu)
, then by definition, the
heat semigroup
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is given by
P_{t}=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\lambda t}dE_{\lambda}
. It is a one-parameter family of bounded operators on

L^{2}(M,\mu)
. Since the closure of the quadratic form
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{H}}
is a Dirichlet form,
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a sub-Markov semigroup: it transforms non-negative functions into non-negative functions and satisfies

P_{t}1\leq 1.

The sub-Markov property and Riesz-Thorin interpolation classically allows one to construct the semigroup
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
in
L^{p}(M,\mu)
and for
f\in L^{p}(M,\mu)
one has

\|P_{t}f\|_{L^{p}(M,\mu)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(M,\mu)},\ \ 1\leq p\leq\infty.

By hypoellipticity of
\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}
, there is a smooth function
p(t,x,y)
,
t\in(0,+\infty),x,y\in M
, such that for every
f\in L^{p}(M,\mu)
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
x\in\mathbb{M}
,

P_{t}f(x)=\int_{M}p(t,x,y)f(y)d\mu(y).

The function
p(t,x,y)
is called the
horizontal
heat kernel associated to
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. It satisfies furthermore:

(i)

(Symmetry)
p(t,x,y)=p(t,y,x)
;

(ii)

(Chapman-Kolmogorov relation)
p(t+s,x,y)=\int_{M}p(t,x,z)p(s,z,y)d\mu(z)
.

Moreover, for
f\in L^{p}(M,\mu)
,
1<p<\infty
, the function

u(t,x)=P_{t}f(x),\quad t\geq 0,x\in M.

is the unique solution of the Cauchy problem

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}u,\quad u(0,x)=f(x).

2.3
Fundamental connection

The Levi-Civita connection
D
is, in general, poorly suited to study foliations since the horizontal and vertical bundle might not be
D
-parallel. There is a more natural connection
\nabla
that respects the foliation structure, see
[
7
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
19
]
.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
19
]
)
.

There exists a unique metric connection
\nabla
on
M
such that:

•

\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{V}
are
\nabla
-parallel, i.e. for every
X\in\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{H}),Y\in\mathcal{X}(TM),Z\in\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{V})
,

\nabla_{Y}X\in\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{H}),\quad\nabla_{Y}Z\in\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{V}).

(2.4)

•

The torsion
\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}
of
\nabla
satisfies
\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})\subset\mathcal{V}
and
\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V})\subset\mathcal{H}
.

•

For every
X,Y\in\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{H})
,
V,Z\in\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{V})
,

\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,Z),Y\rangle=\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(Y,Z),X\rangle,\qquad\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(Z,X),V\rangle=\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(V,X),Z\rangle.

(2.5)

Remark 2.5
.

The connection
\nabla
is more generally defined and uniquely characterized by the above properties in the context of Riemannian manifolds for which the tangent bundle can orthogonally be split as
TM=\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{V}
; the involutivity property
[\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}]\subset\mathcal{V}
is not necessary. The involutivity property is actually equivalent to
\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V})=0
, see the formula (
2.8
).

The connection
\nabla
can be expressed in terms of the Levi-Civita connection
D
by introducing a
(2,1)
tensor
C
through the formula:

\left\langle C_{X}Y,Z\right\rangle=\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{\mathcal{V}}}g)(Y_{\mathcal{H}},Z_{\mathcal{H}})+\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{L}_{X_{\mathcal{H}}}g)(Y_{\mathcal{V}},Z_{\mathcal{V}}).

(2.6)

Notice that the following properties hold:

C_{\mathcal{V}}\mathcal{V}=0,\qquad C_{\mathcal{V}}\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{H},\qquad C_{\mathcal{H}}\mathcal{H}=0,\qquad C_{\mathcal{H}}\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathcal{V}.

(2.7)

The connection
\nabla
can then be expressed in terms of the Levi-Civita one by

\nabla_{X}Y=\begin{cases}(D_{X}Y)_{\mathcal{H}},&X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H}),\\
[X,Y]_{\mathcal{H}}+C_{X}Y,&X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V}),\ Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H}),\\
[X,Y]_{\mathcal{V}}+C_{X}Y,&X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H}),\ Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V}),\\
(D_{X}Y)_{\mathcal{V}},&X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V}).\end{cases}

and its torsion is given by

\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,Y)=\begin{cases}-[X,Y]_{\mathcal{V}}&X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H}),\\
C_{X}Y-C_{Y}X&X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H}),Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V}),\\
0&X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V}).\end{cases}

(2.8)

For
Z\in\mathfrak{X}(TM)
, there is a unique skew-symmetric endomorphism
J_{Z}:T_{x}M\to T_{x}M
such that for all vector fields
X
and
Y
,

\displaystyle\left\langle J_{Z}X,Y\right\rangle=\left\langle Z,\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,Y)\right\rangle.

(2.9)

With this notation, one can easily check that the relation between the Levi-Civita connection
D
and the connection
\nabla
is given by the formula

\nabla_{X}Y=D_{X}Y+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,Y)-\frac{1}{2}J_{X}Y-\frac{1}{2}J_{Y}X,\quad X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(TM).

(2.10)

Remark 2.6
.

From the torsion formula one can see that:

•

The metric
g
is bundle-like i.e.
\mathcal{L}_{Z}g(X,X)=0
for
Z\in\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{V}),X\in\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{H})
if and only if
T(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{V})\subset\mathcal{V}
;

•

The leaves are totally geodesic i.e.
D_{U}V\in\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{V})
for
U,V\in\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{V})
if and only if
\mathcal{L}_{X}g(Z,Z)=0
for
Z\in\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{V}),X\in\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{H})
if and only if
T(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{V})\subset\mathcal{H}
.

Example 2.8

(Carnot groups)
.

Consider the foliation on a Carnot group from Example
2.2
. For a left invariant vector field
X
, denote
\mathrm{ad}_{X}
the map
\mathrm{ad}_{X}(Y)=[X,Y]
and
\mathrm{ad}^{*}
its adjoint. It follows from Koszul’s formula that for left invariant vector fields

\nabla_{X}Y=\begin{cases}0,&X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H}),\\
0,&X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V}),\ Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H}),\\
\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{ad}_{X}Y,&X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H}),\ Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V}),\\
-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{ad}_{X}Y-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{Y}X-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{X}Y,&X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V}).\end{cases}

The horizontal Laplacian
\overset{\circ}{\Delta}_{\mathcal{H}}
of the connection
\nabla
is defined as the trace of the
\nabla
-Hessian in the horizontal directions. It is therefore given in a local horizontal orthonormal frame
X_{i}
by

\overset{\circ}{\Delta}_{\mathcal{H}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla_{X_{i}}-\nabla_{X_{i}}X_{i}.

Notice that from Lemma
2.3
and the definition of
\nabla
we therefore have

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}=\overset{\circ}{\Delta}_{\mathcal{H}}-\mathrm{H}.

(2.11)

3
Bochner’s formulas and curvature dimension inequalities

Our first main goal in this section is to prove Bochner’s type formulas for the horizontal Laplacian. The first formula is in horizontal directions and the second one in vertical directions. The key point is to express the quantities

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f|^{2}-\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}f\rangle

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f|^{2}-\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}f\rangle

from tensors related to the connection
\nabla
. Those formulas generalize both the formulas obtained in
[
9
]
for sub-Riemannian manifolds with transverse symmetries and
[
11
]
for contact manifolds.

We will use the following notations. First, recalling that
\mathrm{H}
denotes the mean curvature vector field, we define for
U,V\in\mathcal{X}(TM)

\nabla^{\mathrm{sym}}\mathrm{H}(U,V)=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\nabla_{U}\mathrm{H},V\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\nabla_{V}\mathrm{H},U\right\rangle.

For a smooth function
f
and
U,V\in\mathcal{X}(TM)
we define

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hess}^{\nabla,\mathrm{sym}}f(U,V)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{Hess}^{\nabla}f(U,V)+\mathrm{Hess}^{\nabla}f(V,U))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(UV+VU-\nabla_{U}V-\nabla_{V}U\right)f

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\nabla,\mathrm{sym}}f(U,V)=\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\nabla,\mathrm{sym}}f(U_{\mathcal{H}},V_{\mathcal{H}}).

For the following notations the
X_{i}
’s below form an arbitrary orthonormal local frame of horizontal vectors and
U,V
are arbitrary vectors in
\mathcal{X}(TM)
.

•

The horizontal Ricci curvature of the connection
\nabla
is defined as the
(2,0)
tensor

\mathrm{Ric}^{\nabla}_{\mathcal{H}}(U,V)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(U,X_{i})X_{i},V\right.\rangle

•

The horizontal divergence of the torsion is defined as the
(1,1)
tensor

\delta^{\nabla}_{\mathcal{H}}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(U)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla_{X_{i}}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X_{i},U).

•

(\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(U),\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(V))_{\mathcal{H}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(U,X_{i}),\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(V,X_{i})\right\rangle

•

\tau(U,V)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X_{i},\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X_{i},U)),V\rangle

•

\iota(U)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(U,X_{i}),X_{i}\rangle

•

(J_{U},J_{V})_{\mathcal{H}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle J_{U}X_{i},J_{V}X_{i}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}.

3.1
Horizontal and vertical Bochner’s formulas for the horizontal Laplacian

Using the notations introduced above, the Bochner’s formulas write as follows.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
and
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}
be a local orthonormal frame of horizontal vector fields. We have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f|^{2}=

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}f\rangle+2\sum_{i}\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X_{i})\rangle+|\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\nabla,\mathrm{sym}}f|^{2}

\displaystyle-\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,\delta_{\mathcal{H}}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)\rangle+\mathrm{Ric}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)-(\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f),\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f))_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}(J_{\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f},J_{\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f})_{\mathcal{H}}+\nabla^{\mathrm{sym}}\mathrm{H}(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)+\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathrm{H},\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f),\nabla f\right\rangle-\tau(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}|\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f|^{2}=

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}f\rangle+2\sum_{i}\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla f,\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,X_{i})\rangle+\mid\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f\mid^{2}

\displaystyle-\langle\nabla f,\delta_{\mathcal{H}}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f)\rangle-(\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f),\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f))_{\mathcal{H}}+\mathrm{Ric}^{\nabla}_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)

\displaystyle+2\nabla^{\mathrm{sym}}\mathrm{H}(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f)+\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathrm{H},\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f),\nabla f\right\rangle-\tau(\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f).

The proof is rather long and partly inspired by
[
19
]
. We start with four preliminary lemmas. In what follows,
f
is a fixed function in
C^{\infty}(M)
. The first lemma symmetrizes the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of the horizontal Hessian for the connection
\nabla
.

Lemma 3.2
.

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f|^{2}=|\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\nabla,\mathrm{sym}}f|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}(J_{\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f},J_{\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f})_{\mathcal{H}}

Proof.

If
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}
is a local horizontal orthonormal frame then

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X_{j}\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X_{j}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X_{j}\rangle\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X_{j}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla_{X_{j}}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X_{i}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X_{j},X_{i}),\nabla f\rangle\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X_{j}\rangle+\langle\nabla_{X_{j}}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X_{i}\rangle}{2}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X_{j},X_{i}),\nabla f\rangle^{2}.

Note that the mixed terms vanish because of the anti-symmetry of the torsion tensor. Since
\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})\subset\mathcal{V}
, we then have

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X_{j},X_{i}),\nabla f\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X_{j},X_{i}),\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\langle X_{i},J_{\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f}X_{j}\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}|J_{\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f}X_{j}|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle=(J_{\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f},J_{\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f})_{\mathcal{H}}.

∎

The second lemma deals with Ricci type commutation identities related to the connection
\nabla
.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}
is a local horizontal orthonormal frame then

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle[\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f}\nabla_{X_{i}}-\nabla_{\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f}X_{i}}]\nabla f,X_{i}\rangle=\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}\overset{\circ}{\Delta}_{\mathcal{H}}f\rangle

and

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle[\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f}\nabla_{X_{i}}-\nabla_{\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f}X_{i}}]\nabla f,X_{i}\rangle=\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}\overset{\circ}{\Delta}_{\mathcal{H}}f\rangle.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\overset{\circ}{\Delta}_{\mathcal{H}}f

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Hess}^{\nabla}f(X_{i},X_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla f,X_{i}\rangle.

Therefore we have

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}\overset{\circ}{\Delta}_{\mathcal{H}}f\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(X_{j}\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla f,X_{i}\rangle)X_{j}f

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(\langle\nabla_{X_{j}}\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla f,X_{i}\rangle+\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla f,\nabla_{X_{j}}X_{i}\rangle)X_{j}f

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f}\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla f,X_{i}\rangle+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla f,\nabla_{X_{j}}X_{i}\rangle)X_{j}f

Using that
\langle\nabla_{X_{j}}X_{i},X_{k}\rangle=-\langle\nabla_{X_{j}}X_{k},X_{i}\rangle
we now compute

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla f,\nabla_{X_{j}}X_{i}\rangle X_{j}f

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{n}\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla f,X_{k}\rangle\langle\nabla_{X_{j}}X_{i},X_{k}\rangle X_{j}f

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{n}\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla f,X_{k}\rangle\langle\nabla_{X_{j}}X_{k},X_{i}\rangle X_{j}f

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\langle\nabla_{\nabla_{X_{j}}X_{k}}\nabla f,X_{k}\rangle X_{j}f

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\nabla_{\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f}X_{k}}\nabla f,X_{k}\rangle.

We conclude

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle[\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f}\nabla_{X_{i}}-\nabla_{\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f}X_{i}}]\nabla f,X_{i}\rangle=\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}\overset{\circ}{\Delta}_{\mathcal{H}}f\rangle.

The second computation proceeds almost in the same way. We first have

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}\overset{\circ}{\Delta}_{\mathcal{H}}f\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f}\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla f,X_{i}\rangle+\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla f,\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f}X_{i}\rangle.

and then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla f,\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f}X_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,k,m=1}^{n}\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla f,X_{k}\rangle\langle\nabla_{Z_{m}}X_{i},X_{k}\rangle Z_{m}f

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i,k,m=1}^{n}\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla f,X_{k}\rangle\langle\nabla_{Z_{m}}X_{k},X_{i}\rangle Z_{m}f

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\nabla_{\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f}X_{k}}\nabla f,X_{k}\rangle.

∎

For the remainder of the proof, define

\displaystyle u_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f\right|^{2},\;u_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f\right|^{2}.

Lemma 3.4
.

We have

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}u_{1}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f}(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)+(J_{\nabla f}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)_{\mathcal{H}},

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}u_{2}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f}(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)+(J_{\nabla f}\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f)_{\mathcal{H}}.

Proof.

Since
\nabla
is a metric connection, we have for any
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H})
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}X\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla_{X}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f\rangle=\langle\nabla_{X}\nabla f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Hess}^{\nabla}f(X,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)=\mathrm{Hess}^{\nabla}f(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X)-\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f),\nabla f\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f}\nabla f,X\rangle+\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X),\nabla f\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X\rangle+\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X),\nabla f\rangle

Recall now the definition of the
J
tensor

\langle J_{Z}X,Y\rangle=\langle Z,\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,Y)\rangle.

We thus get

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}u_{1},X\rangle=\frac{1}{2}X\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f\rangle=\langle\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X\rangle+\langle(J_{\nabla f}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)_{\mathcal{H}},X\rangle

Since this holds for every
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H})
, this implies

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}u_{1}=\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f+(J_{\nabla f}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)_{\mathcal{H}}.

Similarly, we have for any
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H})
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}X\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla_{X}\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f\rangle=\langle\nabla_{X}\nabla f,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Hess}^{\nabla}f(X,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f)=\mathrm{Hess}^{\nabla}f(\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,X)-\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f),\nabla f\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f}\nabla f,X\rangle+\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,X),\nabla f\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f}\nabla f,X\rangle+\langle J_{\nabla f}\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,X\rangle.

∎

The next lemma deals with the contribution of the mean curvature vector.

Lemma 3.5
.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{H}|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f|^{2}-\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}\mathrm{H}f\rangle=-\nabla^{\mathrm{sym}}\mathrm{H}(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)-\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathrm{H},\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f),\nabla f\right\rangle

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{H}|\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f|^{2}-\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}\mathrm{H}f\rangle=-2\nabla^{\mathrm{sym}}\mathrm{H}(\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)-\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathrm{H},\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f),\nabla f\right\rangle.

Proof.

Let
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}
be a local horizontal orthonormal frame and
Z_{1},\cdots,Z_{m}
be a local vertical orthonormal frame. Since
\mathrm{H}
is horizontal we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{H}|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f|^{2}-\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}\mathrm{H}f\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\mathrm{H}X_{i}f)(X_{i}f)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}\mathrm{H}f)(X_{i}f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}([\mathrm{H},X_{i}]f)(X_{i}f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left\langle[\mathrm{H},X_{i}],X_{j}\right\rangle(X_{j}f)(X_{i}f)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\left\langle[\mathrm{H},X_{i}],Z_{\ell}\right\rangle(Z_{\ell}f)(X_{i}f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}\mathrm{H},X_{j}\right\rangle(X_{j}f)(X_{i}f)+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left\langle\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}X_{i},X_{j}\right\rangle(X_{j}f)(X_{i}f)

\displaystyle-\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathrm{H},\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f),\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f\right\rangle-\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathrm{H},\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f),\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f\right\rangle

Since
\left\langle\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}X_{i},X_{j}\right\rangle=-\left\langle\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}X_{j},X_{i}\right\rangle
, we can write

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{H}|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f|^{2}-\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}\mathrm{H}f\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}\mathrm{H},X_{j}\right\rangle(X_{j}f)(X_{i}f)-\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathrm{H},\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f),\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f\right\rangle-\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathrm{H},\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f),\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\nabla^{\mathrm{sym}}\mathrm{H}(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)-\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathrm{H},\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f),\nabla f\right\rangle.

The computation for
\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{H}|\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f|^{2}-\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}\mathrm{H}f\rangle
follows the same pattern:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{H}|\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f|^{2}-\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}\mathrm{H}f\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}([\mathrm{H},Z_{\ell}]f)(Z_{\ell}f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\langle[\mathrm{H},Z_{\ell}],X_{j}\right\rangle(Z_{\ell}f)(X_{j}f)+\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{m}\left\langle[\mathrm{H},Z_{\ell}],Z_{k}\right\rangle(Z_{\ell}f)(Z_{k}f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\langle\nabla_{Z_{\ell}}\mathrm{H},X_{j}\right\rangle(X_{j}f)(Z_{\ell}f)-\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathrm{H},Z_{\ell}),X_{j}\right\rangle(X_{j}f)(Z_{\ell}f)

\displaystyle-\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathrm{H},\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f),\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f\right\rangle-\nabla^{\mathrm{sym}}\mathrm{H}(\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f)

Finally, note that since
\mathrm{H}
is horizontal,
\left\langle\nabla_{X_{j}}\mathrm{H},Z_{\ell}\right\rangle=\nabla^{\mathrm{sym}}\mathrm{H}(\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f)=0
. We conclude

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{H}|\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f|^{2}-\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}\mathrm{H}f\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\nabla^{\mathrm{sym}}\mathrm{H}(\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)-\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathrm{H},\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f),\nabla f\right\rangle.

∎

We can now proceed to the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Plug in our result from Lemma
3.4
, we get that for every
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H})
,

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{X}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}u_{1},X\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{X}\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X\rangle+\langle\nabla_{X}(J_{\nabla f}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)_{\mathcal{H}},X\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{X}\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f}\nabla f,X\rangle+\langle\nabla_{X}(J_{\nabla f}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f),X\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f}\nabla_{X}\nabla f,X\rangle+\langle\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(X,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)\nabla f,X\rangle+{\langle\nabla_{[X,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f]}\nabla f,X\rangle}+\langle\nabla_{X}(J_{\nabla f}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f),X\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f}\nabla_{X}\nabla f,X\rangle+\langle\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(X,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X\rangle+{\langle\nabla_{\nabla_{X}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f-\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f}X-\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)}\nabla f,X\rangle}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{X}(J_{\nabla f}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f),X\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle[\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f}\nabla_{X}-\nabla_{\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f}X}]\nabla f,X\rangle+\langle\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(X,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X\rangle+\langle\nabla_{\nabla_{X}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f-\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)}\nabla f,X\rangle

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{X}(J_{\nabla f}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f),X\rangle.

Recall that we have for all
A,B\in\mathfrak{X}(TM)
,

\langle\nabla_{A}\nabla f,B\rangle=\langle\nabla_{B}\nabla f,A\rangle+\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(B,A),\nabla f\rangle.

Thus

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{\nabla_{X}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f}\nabla f,X\rangle-\langle\nabla_{\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)}\nabla f,X\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{X}\nabla f,\nabla_{X}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f\rangle+\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\nabla_{X}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f),\nabla f\rangle

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{X}\nabla f,\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)\rangle-\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)),\nabla f\rangle.

Moreover, since
\nabla
is metric-compatible, we have

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{X}(J_{\nabla f}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f),X\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X\langle J_{\nabla f}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X\rangle-\langle J_{\nabla f}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{X}X\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X\langle\nabla f,\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X)\rangle-\langle\nabla f,\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{X}X)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{X}\nabla f,\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X)\rangle+\langle\nabla f,\nabla_{X}(\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X))\rangle-\langle\nabla f,\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{X}X)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{X}\nabla f,\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X)\rangle+\langle\nabla f,(\nabla_{X}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla})(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X)\rangle+\langle\nabla f,\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{X}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X)\rangle.

Therefore

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{\nabla_{X}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f-\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)}\nabla f,X\rangle\ +\langle\nabla_{X}(J_{\nabla f}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f),X\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{X}\nabla f,\nabla_{X}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f\rangle+2\langle\nabla_{X}\nabla f,\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X)\rangle+\langle\nabla f,(\nabla_{X}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla})(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X)\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)),\nabla f\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\nabla_{X}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f|^{2}+2\langle\nabla_{X}\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X)\rangle+\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,(\nabla_{X}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla})(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X)\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f),\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)\rangle-\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)),\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f\rangle.

In the last line, we used the fact that the torsion of the connection
\nabla
satisfies for
U,V\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{V})
and
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{H})

\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(U,X),V\rangle=\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(V,X),U\rangle,\qquad\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,Y)\in\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{V}).

We put everything together and get

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{X}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}u_{1},X\rangle=

\displaystyle\langle[\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f}\nabla_{X}-\nabla_{\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f}X}]\nabla f,X\rangle+|\nabla_{X}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f|^{2}+\langle\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(X,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X\rangle

\displaystyle+2\langle\nabla_{X}\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X)\rangle+\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,(\nabla_{X}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla})(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X)\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f),\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)\rangle-\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)),\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f\rangle.

Finally, let
X
range over a horizontal frame, and we get the result from Lemmas
3.5
,
3.2
and
3.3
after summing up.

We now turn to the second Bochner’s formula in the vertical directions. The computation follows the same lines, we therefore only show the main steps. Similar to the previous case, we have

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{X}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}u_{2},X\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle[\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f}\nabla_{X}-\nabla_{\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f}X}](\nabla f),X\rangle+\langle\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(X,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f)\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X\rangle+\langle\nabla_{\nabla_{X}\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f-\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f)}(\nabla f),X\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle\nabla_{X}(J_{\nabla f}\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f)_{\mathcal{H}},X\rangle.

Then, as before, we have

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{\nabla_{X}\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f-\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f)}\nabla f,X\rangle\ +\langle\nabla_{X}(J_{\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f}\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f),X\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\nabla_{X}\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f|^{2}+2\langle\nabla_{X}\nabla f,\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,X)\rangle+\langle\nabla f,(\nabla_{X}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla})(\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,X)\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f),\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f)\rangle-\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f)),\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f\rangle.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{X}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}u_{2},X\rangle=

\displaystyle\langle[\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f}\nabla_{X}-\nabla_{\nabla_{\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f}X}]\nabla f,X\rangle+|\nabla_{X}\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f|^{2}+2\langle\nabla_{X}\nabla f,\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,X)\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle\nabla f,(\nabla_{X}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla})(\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,X)\rangle-\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f),\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f)\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f)),\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f\rangle+\langle\mathrm{Riem}^{\nabla}(X,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f)\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X\rangle

and the conclusion follows from Lemmas
3.5
and then
3.3
after summing up over a local horizontal orthonormal frame.
∎

3.2
Bochner’s formula for the horizontal Laplacian of the full gradient

We can add the horizontal and vertical Bochner’s formulas to get a formula involving the full gradient. To make the statement more concise we introduce the following tensor: For
U,V\in\mathcal{X}(TM)
:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}(U,V):=

\displaystyle-\langle U,\delta_{\mathcal{H}}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(V)\rangle-2(\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(U),\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(V_{\mathcal{V}}))_{\mathcal{H}}-(\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(U),\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(V_{\mathcal{H}}))_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle-\tau(U,V_{\mathcal{H}})+\mathrm{Ric}^{\nabla}_{\mathcal{H}}(U,V_{\mathcal{H}})+\nabla^{\mathrm{sym}}\mathrm{H}(U,V)+\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathrm{H},U),V\right\rangle.

(3.1)

Notice that if the metric is bundle-like and the leaves minimal then
\mathfrak{R}
coincides with the tensor recently introduced in
[
6
]
. In particular in any Carnot group
\mathfrak{R}
is a symmetric tensor. In general,
\mathfrak{R}
is not symmetric.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
and
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}
be a local orthonormal frame of horizontal vector fields. We have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}|\nabla f|^{2}=

\displaystyle\langle\nabla f,\nabla\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}f\rangle+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left(\mathrm{Hess}^{\nabla,\mathrm{sym}}f(X_{i},X_{j})+\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,X_{i}),X_{j}\rangle\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f-\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X_{i},\nabla f)_{\mathcal{V}}|^{2}+\mathfrak{R}(\nabla f,\nabla f)

Proof.

By adding the horizontal and vertical Bochner formulas, it is easily checked that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}|\nabla f|^{2}=

\displaystyle\langle\nabla f,\nabla\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}f\rangle+2\sum_{i}\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla f,\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla f,X_{i})\rangle+|\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\nabla,\mathrm{sym}}f|^{2}

\displaystyle-\langle\nabla f,\delta_{\mathcal{H}}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla f)\rangle+\mathrm{Ric}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\nabla}(\nabla f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)+\mid\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f\mid^{2}

\displaystyle-(\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla f),\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f))_{\mathcal{H}}+\frac{1}{4}(J_{\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f},J_{\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f})_{\mathcal{H}}-\tau(\nabla f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)

\displaystyle+\nabla^{\mathrm{sym}}\mathrm{H}(\nabla f,\nabla f)+\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathrm{H},\nabla f),\nabla f\right\rangle.

We complete the square and get

\displaystyle\mid\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f\mid^{2}+2\sum_{i}\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla f,X_{i})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mid\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f+\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla f,X_{i})\mid^{2}-\mid\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla f,X_{i})\mid_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}

\displaystyle|\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\nabla,\mathrm{sym}}f|^{2}+2\sum_{i}\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla f,X_{i})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X_{j}\rangle+\langle\nabla_{X_{j}}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X_{i}\rangle}{2}\right)^{2}+2\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X_{j}\rangle\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla f,X_{i}),X_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X_{j}\rangle+\langle\nabla_{X_{j}}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X_{i}\rangle}{2}\right)^{2}+2\frac{\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X_{j}\rangle+\langle\nabla_{X_{j}}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,X_{i}\rangle}{2}\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla f,X_{i}),X_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left(\mathrm{Hess}^{\nabla,\mathrm{sym}}f(X_{i},X_{j})+\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,X_{i}),X_{j}\rangle\right)^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mid\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla f,X_{i})\mid_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

Our conclusion follows then from the definition of the
\mathfrak{R}
tensor.
∎

As a consequence we get our first curvature dimension estimate:

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
. Then,

\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}|\nabla f|^{2}-\langle\nabla f,\nabla\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}f\rangle\geq\frac{1}{n}(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}f+\iota(\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f))^{2}+\mathfrak{R}(\nabla f,\nabla f).

Proof.

This follows from the lower bound

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left(\mathrm{Hess}^{\nabla,\mathrm{sym}}f(X_{i},X_{j})+\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,X_{i}),X_{j}\rangle\right)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Hess}^{\nabla,\mathrm{sym}}f(X_{i},X_{i})+\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,X_{i}),X_{i}\rangle\right)^{2}.

∎

3.3
Curvature dimension inequalities

In relation to Bakry-Émery calculus let us introduce the following notations: For
f,g\in C^{\infty}(M)
, we define

\Gamma^{\mathcal{H}}_{2}(f,g)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}\left\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}g\right\rangle-\left\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}g\right\rangle-\left\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}g\right\rangle\right)

and

\Gamma^{\mathcal{V}}_{2}(f,g)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}\left\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}g\right\rangle-\left\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}g\right\rangle-\left\langle\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}g\right\rangle\right).

The first estimate we get is quite general and follows easily from Corollary
3.7
and the inequality

(a+b)^{2}\geq\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}a^{2}-\lambda b^{2}.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\lambda\geq 0
. Assume that there exists a constant
K\in\mathbb{R}
such that for every
X\in\mathcal{X}(TM)

\mathfrak{R}(X,X)-\lambda\iota(X)^{2}\geq K|X|^{2}.

Then, for every
f\in C^{\infty}(M)

\Gamma^{\mathcal{H}}_{2}(f,f)+\Gamma_{2}^{\mathcal{V}}(f,f)\geq\frac{\lambda}{n(1+\lambda)}(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}f)^{2}+K|\nabla f|^{2}.

Remark 3.9
.

If
\iota=0
s (as in the case of contact manifolds which follows from
[
11
]
) and
\mathfrak{R}(X,X)\geq K|X|^{2}
, then one can take
\lambda=+\infty
so that

\Gamma^{\mathcal{H}}_{2}(f,f)+\Gamma_{2}^{\mathcal{V}}(f,f)\geq\frac{1}{n}(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}f)^{2}+K|\nabla f|^{2}.

The second curvature dimension estimate requires more conditions but can lead to further results. These conditions are for instance satisfied if the manifold
M
is compact or a Lie group for the which the foliation is left or right invariant.

Theorem 3.10
.

Assume that there is a constant
C\geq 0
such that

\max\left\{|\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}|,|\delta_{\mathcal{H}}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}|,|\mathrm{H}|,|\nabla^{\mathrm{sym}}\mathrm{H}|,|\mathrm{Ric}_{\mathcal{H}}|\right\}\leq C.

Then, there exist constants
\rho_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\rho_{2}\geq 0,\rho_{3}\geq 0,\rho_{4}\geq 0,\kappa\geq 0
and
N\geq n
, all depending on the constant
C
, such that for every
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
and
\nu>0

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\mathcal{H}}_{2}(f,f)+\nu\Gamma_{2}^{\mathcal{V}}(f,f)\geq\frac{1}{N}(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}f)^{2}+\left(\rho_{1}-\frac{\kappa}{\nu}\right)|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f|^{2}+(\rho_{2}-\rho_{3}\nu-\rho_{4}\nu^{2})|\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f|^{2}.

(3.2)

Moreover:

•

If the metric is bundle-like then one can take
\rho_{4}=0
;

•

If the horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}
is uniformly step-two generating in the sense that there exists a constant
K>0
such that for every local horizontal orthonormal frame
X_{i}
and every
U\in\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{V})
,

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left\langle[X_{i},X_{j}],U\right\rangle^{2}\geq K|U|^{2},

then one can take
\rho_{2}>0
.

Proof.

Let
\nu>0
and, in this proof, for a function
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
denote

\nabla^{\nu}f=\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f+\nu\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f.

By combining the horizontal and vertical Bochner formulas, we get

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\mathcal{H}}_{2}(f,f)+\nu\Gamma^{\mathcal{V}}(f,f)=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i}\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla f,\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla^{\nu}f,X_{i})\rangle+|\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\nabla,\mathrm{sym}}f|^{2}

\displaystyle-\langle\nabla f,\delta_{\mathcal{H}}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla^{\nu}f)\rangle+\mathrm{Ric}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\nabla}(\nabla^{\nu}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)+\nu\mid\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f\mid^{2}

\displaystyle-(\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla^{\nu}f),\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f))_{\mathcal{H}}+\frac{1}{4}(J_{\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f},J_{\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f})_{\mathcal{H}}-\tau(\nabla^{\nu}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)

\displaystyle+\nabla^{\mathrm{sym}}\mathrm{H}(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)+2\nu\nabla^{\mathrm{sym}}\mathrm{H}(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f)+\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathrm{H},\nabla^{\nu}f),\nabla f\right\rangle.

As before we complete the square to now get

\displaystyle\nu\mid\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f\mid^{2}+2\sum_{i}\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla^{\nu}f,X_{i})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\sqrt{\nu}\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla^{\nu}f,X_{i})_{\mathcal{V}}\right|^{2}-\frac{1}{\nu}\mid\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla^{\nu}f,X_{i})\mid_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}

and

\displaystyle|\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\nabla,\mathrm{sym}}f|^{2}+2\nu\sum_{i}\langle\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,X_{i})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left(\mathrm{Hess}^{\nabla,\mathrm{sym}}f(X_{i},X_{j})+\nu\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,X_{i}),X_{j}\rangle\right)^{2}-\nu^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mid\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,X_{i})\mid_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

We therefore have

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\mathcal{H}}_{2}(f,f)+\nu\Gamma_{2}^{\mathcal{V}}(f,f)\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}f+\nu\,\iota(\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f))^{2}-\frac{1}{\nu}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mid\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla^{\nu}f,X_{i})\mid_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}-\nu^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mid\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,X_{i})\mid_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle-\langle\nabla f,\delta_{\mathcal{H}}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla^{\nu}f)\rangle+\mathrm{Ric}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\nabla}(\nabla^{\nu}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)

\displaystyle-(\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla^{\nu}f),\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f))_{\mathcal{H}}+\frac{1}{4}(J_{\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f},J_{\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f})_{\mathcal{H}}-\tau(\nabla^{\nu}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)

\displaystyle+\nabla^{\mathrm{sym}}\mathrm{H}(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)+2\nu\nabla^{\mathrm{sym}}\mathrm{H}(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f)

\displaystyle+\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathrm{H},\nabla^{\nu}f),\nabla f\right\rangle.

Using then our assumptions and multiple times the elementary inequalities

(a+b)^{2}\geq\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}a^{2}-\lambda b^{2},\qquad ab\geq-\frac{1}{2\lambda}a^{2}-\frac{\lambda}{2}b^{2}

we deduce (
3.2
).

Now, assume that the metric is bundle-like. In that case, from Remark
2.6
,
\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{V})\subset\mathcal{V}
. Therefore, one has

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mid\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla f,X_{i})\mid_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=0

and we can choose
\rho_{4}=0
.

Finally, assume that there exists a constant
K>0
such that for every local horizontal orthonormal frame
X_{i}
and every
U\in\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{V})
,

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left\langle[X_{i},X_{j}],U\right\rangle^{2}\geq K|U|^{2}.

In that case one has

\displaystyle K|U|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left\langle[X_{i},X_{j}],U\right\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(X_{i},X_{j}),U\right\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left\langle X_{i},J_{U}X_{j}\right\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}|J_{U}X_{j}|^{2}=(J_{U},J_{U})_{\mathcal{H}}.

This implies

(J_{\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f},J_{\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f})_{\mathcal{H}}\geq K\Gamma^{\mathcal{V}}(f,f).

Since
K>0
, we then see that
\rho_{2}
in (
3.2
) can be chosen to be positive.
∎

It is clear from the proof that the parameters
N,\kappa,\rho_{i}
are not unique. However, if the curvature dimension inequality (
3.2
) holds then several tensorial constraints are satisfied.

Corollary 3.11
.

Assume that there exist constants
\rho_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
,
i=1,2,3,4
,
\kappa\geq 0
such that (
3.2
) holds. Then, for every
U\in\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathrm{Ric}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\nabla}(U,U)+\nabla^{\mathrm{sym}}\mathrm{H}(U,U)\geq\rho_{1}|U|^{2}\\
(\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(U),\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(U))_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\kappa|U|^{2}\end{cases}

and for every
V\in\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{V})

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{1}{4}(J_{V},J_{V})_{\mathcal{H}}\geq\rho_{2}|V|^{2}\\
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mid\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(V,X_{i})\mid_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+\langle V,\delta_{\mathcal{H}}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(V)\rangle+(\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(V),\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(V))_{\mathcal{H}}+\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathrm{H},V),V\right\rangle\leq\rho_{3}|V|^{2}\\
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mid\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(V,X_{i})\mid_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq\rho_{4}|V|^{2},\end{cases}

where the
X_{i}
’s form an arbitrary horizontal orthonormal frame.

Proof.

Assume that (
3.2
) holds for every
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
and
\nu>0
. Let
x\in M
and
u\in\mathcal{H}_{x}
,
v\in\mathcal{V}_{x}
. Let
X_{i}
be a local horizontal orthonormal frame around
x
. One can find a function
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
such that, at
x

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f=u\\
\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f=v\\
\sqrt{\nu}\nabla_{X_{i}}\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla^{\nu}f,X_{i})_{\mathcal{V}}=0\\
\mathrm{Hess}^{\nabla,\mathrm{sym}}f(X_{i},X_{j})+\nu\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f,X_{i}),X_{j}\rangle=0\end{cases}

Applying (
3.2
) to such function
f
at
x
yields for
v=0

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\nu}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mid\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(u,X_{i})\mid_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}+\mathrm{Ric}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\nabla}(u,u)+\nabla^{\mathrm{sym}}\mathrm{H}(u,u)\geq\left(\rho_{1}-\frac{\kappa}{\nu}\right)|u|^{2}

and for
u=0

\displaystyle-\nu\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mid\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(v,X_{i})\mid_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}-\nu^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mid\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(v,X_{i})\mid_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}-\nu\langle v,\delta_{\mathcal{H}}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(v)\rangle

\displaystyle-\nu(\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(v),\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(v))_{\mathcal{H}}+\frac{1}{4}(J_{v},J_{v})_{\mathcal{H}}+\nu\left\langle\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathrm{H},v),v\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(\rho_{2}-\rho_{3}\nu-\rho_{4}\nu^{2})|v|^{2},

Since this holds for every
\nu>0
we easily conclude.
∎

4
Applications

We now turn to the second part of the paper and focus on geometric analysis applications of the Bochner’s identities.

4.1
Horizontal Laplacian comparison theorem

The first application of the curvature dimension estimates is the generalization to our setting of the horizontal Laplacian comparison theorem proved in
[
6
]
. More precisely, the result below removes the bundle-like condition on the metric and the minimality of the leaves from the assumptions of
[
6
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\lambda>0
. Assume that there exists a constant
K\in\mathbb{R}
such that for every
X\in\mathcal{X}(TM)

\mathfrak{R}(X,X)-\lambda\iota(X)^{2}\geq K|X|^{2}.

Let
p\in M
and denote
r_{p}(x)=d(p,x)
. Then, for
x\neq p
not in the cut-locus of
p
,

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}r_{p}(x)\leq\begin{cases}\sqrt{NK}\cot\left(\sqrt{\frac{K}{N}}r_{p}(x)\right)&\text{if}\ K>0,\\
\displaystyle\frac{N}{r_{p}(x)}&\text{if}\ K=0,\\
\sqrt{N|K|}\coth\left(\sqrt{\frac{|K|}{N}}r_{p}(x)\right)&\text{if}\ K<0,\end{cases}

where
N=\frac{n(1+\lambda)}{\lambda}
.

Proof.

Let
p\in M
and
x\neq p
not in the cut-locus of
p
. Let
\gamma:[0,r_{p}(x)]\to M
be the unique length-parametrized geodesic from
p
to
x
. Denote
\phi(t)=\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}r_{p}(\gamma(t))
. We have then

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\gamma^{\prime}(t),\nabla\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}r_{p}(\gamma(t))\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla r_{p}(\gamma(t)),\nabla\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}r_{p}(\gamma(t))\right\rangle.

From Proposition
3.8
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}|\nabla r_{p}|^{2}(\gamma(t))-\langle\nabla r_{p}(\gamma(t)),\nabla\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}r_{p}(\gamma(t))\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{n(1+\lambda)}(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}r_{p}(\gamma(t)))^{2}+K|\nabla r_{p}(\gamma(t))|^{2}.

Since
|\nabla r_{p}|=1
we deduce

-\phi^{\prime}(t)\geq\frac{\lambda}{n(1+\lambda)}\phi(t)^{2}+K.

Let us denote

\displaystyle G(t)=\begin{cases}\sin\left(\sqrt{\frac{K}{N}}r_{p}(x)\right)&\text{if}\ K>0,\\
\displaystyle t&\text{if}\ K=0,\\
\sinh\left(\sqrt{\frac{|K|}{N}}r_{p}(x)\right)&\text{if}\ K<0.\end{cases}

We use the elementary inequality

\frac{\lambda}{n(1+\lambda)}\phi(t)^{2}\geq 2\frac{G^{\prime}(t)}{G(t)}\phi(t)-\frac{n(1+\lambda)}{\lambda}\frac{G^{\prime}(t)^{2}}{G(t)^{2}},

which yields

-\phi^{\prime}(t)\geq 2\frac{G^{\prime}(t)}{G(t)}\phi(t)-\frac{n(1+\lambda)}{\lambda}\frac{G^{\prime}(t)^{2}}{G(t)^{2}}+K.

Multiplying by
G(t)^{2}
and integrating from
0
to
r_{p}(x)
one obtains

-\int_{0}^{r_{p}(x)}\phi^{\prime}(t)G(t)^{2}+2G^{\prime}(t)G(t)\phi(t)dt\geq\int_{0}^{r_{p}(x)}-\frac{n(1+\lambda)}{\lambda}G^{\prime}(t)^{2}+KG(t)^{2}dt.

Now, it is clear from a local computation in Riemannian exponential coordinates that
\lim_{t\to 0}\phi(t)G(t)^{2}=0
, therefore one has

-G(r_{p}(x))^{2}\phi(r_{p}(x))\geq\int_{0}^{r_{p}(x)}-\frac{n(1+\lambda)}{\lambda}G^{\prime}(t)^{2}+KG(t)^{2}dt

which completes the proof after evaluating the integral.
∎

Following
[
6
]
we can deduce from the Laplacian comparison theorem several interesting results. The proofs are almost identical so we just state the results without proofs.

Corollary 4.2

(Bonnet-Myers type theorem)
.

Let
\lambda>0
. Assume that there exists a constant
K>0
such that for every
X\in\mathcal{X}(TM)

\mathfrak{R}(X,X)-\lambda\iota(X)^{2}\geq K|X|^{2}

then
M
is compact and

\mathbf{diam}(M)\leq\pi\sqrt{\frac{n(1+\lambda)}{\lambda K}}.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
\lambda>0
. Assume that there exists a constant
K\geq 0
such that for every
X\in\mathcal{X}(TM)

\mathfrak{R}(X,X)-\lambda\iota(X)^{2}\geq K|X|^{2}.

Then the heat semigroup
P_{t}
is stochastically complete meaning that for every
x\in M
and
t\geq 0

P_{t}1(x)=1.

Moreover, there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}>0
such that for every
x\in M
and
t>0

\displaystyle p_{t}(x,x)\geq\begin{cases}\frac{c_{1}}{\mu(B(x,c_{2}\sqrt{t}))},\text{ if }K=0,\\
\frac{c_{1}}{\mu\left(B\left(x,c_{2}\left(\frac{e^{c_{3}\sqrt{|K|}t}-1}{\sqrt{|K|}}\right)^{1/2}\right)\right)},\text{ if }K<0.\end{cases}

Here
B(x,r)
denotes the ball with center
x
and radius
r
for the Riemannian distance.

4.2
First eigenvalue estimates

The curvature dimension inequalities also imply estimates for the first eigenvalue of the horizontal Laplacian.

Proposition 4.4
.

Assume that
M
is compact and that there exists a constant
K>0
such that for every
X\in\mathcal{X}(TM)

\mathfrak{R}(X,X)\geq K|X|^{2}.

Then the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
of the horizontal Laplacian satisfies

\lambda_{1}\geq K.

Proof.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
. We integrate our Bochner formula and get

\displaystyle\int_{M}\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}|\nabla f|^{2}d\mu\geq\int_{M}\langle\nabla f,\nabla\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}f\rangle d\mu+\int_{M}|\mathrm{Hess}^{\nabla,\mathrm{sym}}(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)|^{2}d\mu+\int_{M}\mathfrak{R}(\nabla f,\nabla f)d\mu.

Now, let
f
be an eigenfunction of
-\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}
with eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
. The left-hand side of the above inequality vanishes. Thus,

\displaystyle 0\geq-\lambda_{1}\int_{M}|\nabla f|^{2}d\mu+\int_{M}|\mathrm{Hess}^{\nabla,\mathrm{sym}}(\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f,\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f)|^{2}d\mu+\int_{M}\mathfrak{R}(\nabla f,\nabla f)d\mu.

By our assumption on
\mathfrak{R}
we have

\mathfrak{R}(\nabla f,\nabla f)\geq K|\nabla f|^{2}.

We deduce

\displaystyle 0\geq-\lambda_{1}\int_{M}|\nabla f|^{2}d\mu+K\int_{M}|\nabla f|^{2}d\mu.

Our result follows immediately.
∎

Possibly better estimates for the first eigenvalue might be obtained from the one-parameter family of curvature dimension inequalities.

Proposition 4.5
.

Assume that the estimate (
3.2
) is satisfied and that

\rho_{1}\rho_{2}>\kappa(\rho_{3}+\sqrt{\rho_{2}\rho_{4}}).

Then
M
is compact and the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
of the horizontal Laplacian satisfies

\lambda_{1}\geq\frac{\rho_{1}\rho_{2}-\kappa(\rho_{3}+\sqrt{\rho_{2}\rho_{4}})}{\left(\frac{N-1}{N}\right)\rho_{2}+\kappa}.

Proof.

Assume that (
3.2
) holds with
\rho_{1}\rho_{2}>\kappa(\rho_{3}+\sqrt{\rho_{2}\rho_{4}})
. Then we have

\rho_{2}-\rho_{3}\frac{\kappa}{\rho_{1}}-\rho_{4}\frac{\kappa^{2}}{\rho_{1}^{2}}>0.

Therefore there exists
\nu>0
such that

\rho_{1}-\frac{\kappa}{\nu}>0,\qquad\rho_{2}-\rho_{3}\nu-\rho_{4}\nu^{2}>0.

This implies there exist
\nu>0
,
N\geq n
and
K>0
such that for every
f\in C^{\infty}(M)

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\mathcal{H}}_{2}(f,f)+\nu\Gamma_{2}^{\mathcal{V}}(f,f)\geq\frac{1}{N}(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}f)^{2}+K(\Gamma(f,f)+\nu\Gamma^{\mathcal{V}}(f,f)).

Using the proof of Theorem
4.1
for the distance associated to the Riemannian metric

g^{\nu}(X,Y)=g(X_{\mathcal{H}},Y_{\mathcal{H}})+\frac{1}{\nu}g(X_{\mathcal{V}},Y_{\mathcal{V}})

we deduce a Laplacian comparison theorem for this metric. Since
K>0
, there is a Bonnnet-Myers type theorem so that
M
is compact.

Now, for a non constant
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
such that
\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}f=-\lambda f
integrating the inequality

\Gamma^{\mathcal{H}}_{2}(f,f)+\nu\Gamma_{2}^{\mathcal{V}}(f,f)\geq\frac{1}{N}(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}f)^{2}+\left(\rho_{1}-\frac{\kappa}{\nu}\right)|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f|^{2}+(\rho_{2}-\rho_{3}\nu-\rho_{4}\nu^{2})|\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f|^{2}

yields

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}\int_{M}f^{2}d\mu+\nu\lambda\int_{M}|\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f|^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\lambda^{2}\int_{M}f^{2}d\mu+\lambda\left(\rho_{1}-\frac{\kappa}{\nu}\right)\int_{M}f^{2}d\mu+(\rho_{2}-\rho_{3}\nu-\rho_{4}\nu^{2})\int_{M}|\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f|^{2}d\mu.

We now choose
\nu=\frac{\rho_{2}}{\rho_{3}+\lambda+\sqrt{\rho_{2}\rho_{4}}}
. Then we have

\rho_{2}-(\rho_{3}+\lambda)\nu-\rho_{4}\nu^{2}\geq 0.

With this choice of
\nu
we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}\int_{M}f^{2}d\mu\geq\frac{1}{N}\lambda^{2}\int_{M}f^{2}d\mu+\lambda\left(\rho_{1}-\frac{\kappa}{\nu}\right)\int_{M}f^{2}d\mu.

This gives

\lambda\geq\frac{N}{N-1}\left(\rho_{1}-\frac{\kappa}{\nu}\right)

and the conclusion follows easily.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

In the bundle-like and totally geodesic case one has
\rho_{3}=\rho_{4}=0
. In that case the estimate becomes

\lambda_{1}\geq\frac{\rho_{1}\rho_{2}}{\left(\frac{N-1}{N}\right)\rho_{2}+\kappa}

whereas it is known from
[
5
]
that the sharp estimate is

\lambda_{1}\geq\frac{\rho_{1}\rho_{2}}{\left(\frac{N-1}{N}\right)\rho_{2}+3\kappa}.

We also refer to
[
12
]
for further eigenvalue estimates.

4.3
Heat kernel gradient bounds

In the spirit of the celebrated Li-Yau work
[
22
]
Bochner’s formulas can be used to get gradient bounds on the heat kernel. Thanks to Bakry-Émery calculus, such gradient bounds have a wide range of aplications to functionals inequalities, see
[
4
]
.

4.3.1
Bakry-Émery type estimates

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\lambda>0
. Assume that there exists a constant
K\in\mathbb{R}
such that for every
X\in\mathcal{X}(TM)

\mathfrak{R}(X,X)-\lambda\iota(X)^{2}\geq K|X|^{2}.

Then, for every
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(M)
,

|\nabla P_{t}f|^{2}+\frac{2}{N}\frac{e^{2Kt}-1}{2K}(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t}f)^{2}\leq e^{2Kt}P_{t}(|\nabla f|^{2})

where
N=\frac{n(1+\lambda)}{\lambda}
. When
K=0
, we understand
\frac{e^{2Kt}-1}{2K}
as
t
.

Proof.

Using the Laplacian comparison Theorem
4.1
and the Greene-Wu’s approximation theorem, there exists a smooth function
W\geq 1
on
M
such that for some constant
C>0

\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}W\leq CW,\qquad|\nabla W|\leq CW^{2}

and such that for every
r\geq 1
,
\{W\leq r\}
is compact.
Therefore, using classical cutoff arguments as in
[
29
, Proof of Lemma 5.2.2]
we can assume that
M
is compact.

For this proof, for
f,g\in C^{\infty}(M)
we define then

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(f,g)

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla f,\nabla g\rangle

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{2}(f,g)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}\mathcal{T}(f,g)-\mathcal{T}(f,\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}g)-\mathcal{T}(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}f,g))

Let
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be the semigroup generated by
\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}
. Then we have for
f\in C^{\infty}(M)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}P_{s}\mathcal{T}(P_{t-s}f,P_{t-s}f)

\displaystyle=P_{s}\left[\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}\mathcal{T}(P_{t-s}f,P_{t-s}f)-2\mathcal{T}(P_{t-s}f,\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t-s}f)\right]

\displaystyle=2P_{s}\mathcal{T}_{2}(P_{t-s}f,P_{t-s}f).

On the other hand, Proposition
3.8
implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{2}(P_{t-s}f,P_{t-s}f)\geq\frac{1}{N}(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t-s}f)^{2}+K\mathcal{T}(P_{t-s}f,P_{t-s}f).

Plugging the above inequality back in gives

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}P_{s}\mathcal{T}(P_{t-s}f,P_{t-s}f)\geq\frac{2}{N}P_{s}((\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t-s}f)^{2})+2KP_{s}\mathcal{T}(P_{t-s}f,P_{t-s}f).

Therefore, denoting

\phi(s)=e^{-2Ks}P_{s}\mathcal{T}(P_{t-s}f,P_{t-s}f)

we get

e^{2Ks}\phi^{\prime}(s)\geq\frac{2}{N}P_{s}((\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t-s}f)^{2})\geq\frac{2}{N}(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t}f)^{2}.

Integrating from
0
to
t
yields

\mathcal{T}(P_{t}f,P_{t}f)+\frac{2}{N}\frac{e^{2Kt}-1}{2K}(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t}f)^{2}\leq e^{2Kt}P_{t}\mathcal{T}(f,f).

The proof is complete.
∎

4.3.2
Global regularization estimates

Theorem 4.8
.

Assume that there is a constant
C\geq 0
such that

\max\left\{|\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}|,|\delta_{\mathcal{H}}\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}|,|\mathrm{H}|,|\nabla^{\mathrm{sym}}\mathrm{H}|,|\mathrm{Ric}_{\mathcal{H}}|\right\}\leq C

Assume moreover that the horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}
is uniformly step-two generating as in theorem
3.10
. Then there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}>0
and
t_{0}>0
such that for every
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(M)
and
0<t<t_{0}

|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t}f|^{2}\leq\frac{c_{1}}{t}\left(P_{t}(f^{2})-(P_{t}f)^{2}\right)

|\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}P_{t}f|^{2}\leq\frac{c_{2}}{t^{2}}\left(P_{t}(f^{2})-(P_{t}f)^{2}\right)

(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t}f)^{2}\leq\frac{c_{3}}{t^{2}}\left(P_{t}(f^{2})-(P_{t}f)^{2}\right).

Proof.

From theorem
3.10
, there exist constants
\rho_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
,
i=1,2,3,4
,
\kappa\geq 0
and
N\geq n
such that for every
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
and
\nu>0

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\mathcal{H}}_{2}(f,f)+\nu\Gamma_{2}^{\mathcal{V}}(f,f)\geq\frac{1}{N}(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}f)^{2}+\left(\rho_{1}-\frac{\kappa}{\nu}\right)|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f|^{2}+(\rho_{2}-\rho_{3}\nu-\rho_{4}\nu^{2})|\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f|^{2}.

Moreover, from the uniformly step-two generating condition we can assume
\rho_{2}>0
. Without loss of generality we can also assume that
\rho_{1}\leq 0,\rho_{3}\geq 0,\rho_{4}\geq 0
. Therefore, for
\nu>0
small enough:

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\mathcal{H}}_{2}(f,f)+\nu\Gamma_{2}^{\mathcal{V}}(f,f)\geq\frac{1}{N}(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}f)^{2}+\left(\rho_{1}-\frac{\kappa}{\nu}\right)|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f|^{2}+\tilde{\rho}_{2}|\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}f|^{2}

(4.1)

where
\tilde{\rho}_{2}>0
.

As in the proof of Theorem
4.7
using cutoff arguments we can assume that
M
is compact. Let
t>0
be small enough. For
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
consider the function

\phi(s)=(t-s)P_{s}(|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t-s}f|^{2})+\tilde{\rho}_{2}(t-s)^{2}P_{s}(|\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}P_{t-s}f|^{2}).

We see that

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(s)=

\displaystyle-P_{s}(|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t-s}f|^{2})-2\tilde{\rho}_{2}(t-s)P_{s}(|\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}P_{t-s}f|^{2})

\displaystyle+2(t-s)P_{s}(\Gamma^{\mathcal{H}}_{2}(P_{t-s}f,P_{t-s}f))+2\tilde{\rho}_{2}(t-s)^{2}P_{s}(\Gamma_{2}^{\mathcal{V}}(P_{t-s}f,P_{t-s}f)).

Therefore, using (
4.1
) with
\nu=\tilde{\rho}_{2}(t-s)
we get

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle\geq-P_{s}(|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t-s}f|^{2})-2\tilde{\rho}_{2}(t-s)P_{s}(|\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}P_{t-s}f|^{2})

\displaystyle+2(t-s)P_{s}\left(\frac{1}{N}(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t-s}f)^{2}+\left(\rho_{1}-\frac{\kappa}{\tilde{\rho}_{2}(t-s)}\right)|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t-s}f|^{2}+\tilde{\rho}_{2}|\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}P_{t-s}f|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2(t-s)}{N}P_{s}\left((\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t-s}f)^{2}\right)+\left(2\rho_{1}(t-s)-\frac{2\kappa}{\tilde{\rho}_{2}}-1\right)P_{s}(|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t-s}f|^{2})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2(t-s)}{N}(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t}f)^{2}+\left(2\rho_{1}t-\frac{2\kappa}{\tilde{\rho}_{2}}-1\right)P_{s}(|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t-s}f|^{2}).

Integrating from
0
to
t
yields

\displaystyle\phi(t)-\phi(0)\geq\frac{t^{2}}{N}(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t}f)^{2}+\left(2\rho_{1}t-\frac{2\kappa}{\tilde{\rho}_{2}}-1\right)\int_{0}^{t}P_{s}(|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t-s}f|^{2})ds.

However, we easily see that

\int_{0}^{t}P_{s}(|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t-s}f|^{2})ds=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{d}{ds}P_{s}((P_{t-s}f)^{2})ds=\frac{1}{2}\left(P_{t}(f^{2})-(P_{t}f)^{2}\right).

Therefore we conclude

t|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t}f|^{2}+\rho_{2}t^{2}|\nabla_{\mathcal{V}}P_{t}f|^{2}+\frac{t^{2}}{N}(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t}f)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(-2\rho_{1}t+\frac{2\kappa}{\tilde{\rho}_{2}}+1\right)\left(P_{t}(f^{2})-(P_{t}f)^{2}\right).

The conclusion follows almost immediately.
∎

It is worth noting that the estimate

|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t}f|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{t}\left(P_{t}(f^{2})-(P_{t}f)^{2}\right)

immediately implies the so-called weak Bakry-Émery estimate

\|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{t}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}

which, in combination with Gaussian estimates for the heat kernels, directly implies boundedness of Riesz transform (see
[
8
]
) and isoperimetric and Sobolev inequalities (see
[
2
]
). See also
[
24
]
for further discussions on this regularization property.

On the other hand, the estimate

(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}P_{t}f)^{2}\leq\frac{c}{t^{2}}\left(P_{t}(f^{2})-(P_{t}f)^{2}\right)

is more related to second order Riesz transforms, see
[
14
]
.

5
Beyond the foliated setting

Using the same methods and computations, our results can in fact be extended beyond the foliated setting. Consider a complete Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
whose tangent bundle admits an orthogonal decomposition

TM=\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{V},

where
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{V}
are smooth sub-bundles and
\mathcal{H}
is bracket-generatingd. The adapted connection
\nabla
is still well defined in this context (see Remark
2.5
). The only difference with the foliated case is that one no longer necessarily has

\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V})=0,

but only the weaker condition
\mathrm{Tor}^{\nabla}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V})\subset\mathcal{H}
.

The horizontal Laplacian
\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}
is again defined as the generator of the horizontal Dirichlet form

\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{H}}(f,f)=-\int_{M}|\nabla_{\mathcal{H}}f|^{2}\,d\mu,

and admits the local decomposition

\Delta_{\mathcal{H}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(D_{X_{i}}X_{i})_{\mathcal{H}}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}(D_{Z_{\ell}}Z_{\ell})_{\mathcal{H}},

where
(X_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
and
(Z_{\ell})_{1\leq\ell\leq m}
are local orthonormal frames of
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{V}
, respectively.

A notable difference is that the vector field

\mathrm{H}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}(D_{Z_{\ell}}Z_{\ell})_{\mathcal{H}}

can no longer be interpreted as the mean curvature vector field of the leaves, since
\mathcal{V}
is not assumed to be integrable. Nevertheless,
\mathrm{H}
still admits a natural geometric interpretation in terms of the connection
\nabla
. Indeed, using (
2.10
), one readily checks that

\mathrm{H}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}J_{Z_{\ell}}Z_{\ell}.

Apart from this modification, all results from Sections 3 and 4 remain valid without any change in the computations. This slightly more general framework is, for instance, well suited to the study of curvature-dimension inequalities in the context of quaternionic contact manifolds. We have chosen to present our results in the setting of foliations, as the geometric interpretation of the various assumptions and tensorial quantities, such as the bundle-like condition, total geodesicity, and mean curvature of the leaves, is more transparent in that well-established framework.

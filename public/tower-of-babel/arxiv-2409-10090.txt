MotionCom: Automatic and Motion-Aware Image Composition with LLM and Video Diffusion Prior
Weijing Tao, Xiaofeng Yang, Miaomiao Cui, Guosheng Lin

Abstract
This work presents MotionCom, a training-free motion-aware diffusion based image composition, enabling automatic and seamless integration of target objects into new scenes with dynamically coherent results without finetuning or optimization. Traditional approaches in this area suffer from two significant limitations: they require manual planning for object placement and often generate static compositions lacking motion realism. MotionCom addresses these issues by utilizing a Large Vision Language Model (LVLM) for intelligent planning, and a Video Diffusion prior for motion-infused image synthesis, streamlining the composition process. Our multi-modal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting with LVLM automates the strategic placement planning of foreground objects, considering their potential motion and interaction within the scenes. Complementing this, we propose a novel method MotionPaint to distill motion-aware information from pretrained video diffusion models in the generation phase, ensuring that these objects are not only seamlessly integrated but also endowed with realistic motion. Extensive quantitative and qualitative results highlight MotionCom’s superiority, showcasing its efficiency in streamlining the planning process and its capability to produce compositions that authentically depict motion and interaction. Additionally, these methods do not account for the interaction between the background scene and the target foreground object that is to be inserted, which is crucial for creating realistic compositions. Moreover, they necessitate manual selection of the insertion location for the foreground object, a process that can be labor-intensive and less efficient for users. This work presents MotionCom, a training-free motion aware diffusion-based image composition, with the power to automatically composite target objects to new scenes in a harmonious way. Existing works are hindered by two primary challenges: the necessity for manual user planning to position foreground objects within background scenes, and the subsequent generation of static compositions. The former often leads to a labor-intensive planning process, while the latter results in compositions lacking the dynamic interplay necessary for realistic motion portrayal. Our work revolutionizes this by enabling the automatic and harmonious integration of target objects into new scenes, thereby addressing the manual effort and static nature of previous methods.

MotionCom proposes two core strategies to tackle the aforementioned limitations and facilitate more contextually aware image planning and compositions. Firstly, we propose to use multi-modal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting

Secondly, to achieve motion-aware generation, we propose

Specifically, our approach harnesses motion priors ingrained within Image-to-Video (I2V) diffusion models to endow the inserted foreground objects with dynamic characteristics through motion-aware inpainting, circumventing the necessity for further training. MotionPaint ensures not only motion awareness but also their seamless integration into the background scenes, enhancing the overall realism of the composition. Our dual approach effectively streamlines the creation of motion-aware scenes from static components and significantly enhances both the composition process and the coherency of the final images.

Related Work

Image composition

Image composition is the process of seamlessly integrating a foreground element from one photograph into the background of another, striving to produce a coherent and visually appealing result. This endeavor faces numerous challenges which can undermine the realism of the composite. To navigate these complexities, the field has evolved to include a diverse range of strategies: from foundational techniques like image blending and harmonization, which address basic integration and lighting issues, to more advanced approaches such as subject-driven generation that focuses on the content-aware placement of objects with text description, and image-guided composition that allows user inputs on both foreground and background images to guide the compositing process.

Proposed Method

Problem Formulation

How can a static foreground object, such as a kite, be inserted into a user outdoor field scene in a way that imbues the kite with realistic motion, while the background remains unchanged? This question highlights a significant challenge in current image composition efforts. While existing image-guided image composition methods may effectively place the user input kite within a specified location in an input outdoor field image, they do not sufficiently convey that the kite is dynamically engaging with the new background - simulating the effects of wind and motion. For this task to be successful, three key criteria must be met:

•

The background must remain untouched, ensuring that any alterations are limited exclusively to the kite inserted.

•

The inserted kite’s identity should be maintained, with its distinct characteristics preserved across the composition.

•

The positioning of the kite is vital; it should be thoughtfully chosen to imply natural movement in harmony with environmental elements, without disrupting the background.

Addressing these challenges necessitates a two-phased approach in our pipeline.

Pipeline

Image Captioning and Regional Decomposition.
We initiate our process by generating a comprehensive caption that integrates the initial concise caption with the visual content of the
\mathit{I}_{\mathit{bg}}

x

\mathit{S}_{\mathit{ratio}}

Leveraging the LVLM as image-to-text (I2T) captioner, each region is then assigned a subprompt, meticulously crafted by the LVLM to provide detailed and informative descriptions specific to that segment. This layered approach, from initial comprehensive captioning to detailed regional subprompts, lays a solid foundation for the nuanced planning required in the subsequent phases of image composition.

Chain-of-Thought Reasoning.
Building upon the detailed regional prompts derived from the segmentation of
\mathit{I}_{\mathit{bg}}
, we employ the Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning capabilities of the LVLM
(
Zhang et al. 2023c
)
to meticulously evaluate each region
R_{i}
where
R_{i}\subseteq\{R_{1},R_{2},\ldots,R_{x}\}

The depth and specificity of these regional prompts thus play a critical role, furnishing the LVLM with the contextual insights needed to make an informed decision that optimally aligns the foreground object within the dynamic tapestry of the background scene.
To guide the selection of the optimal region

R*
for the insertion of the foreground object, we adhere to three pivotal criteria while crafting in-context examples and generating detailed rationales. First, there must be sufficient room in the chosen region to accommodate the foreground object, ensuring minimal obstruction of the background scene’s key features. Second, the placement of the foreground object should be in an area that enables plausible motion-based interactions with specific elements in the background conducive to motion, rather than merely the largest objects present. Lastly, we give preference to background regions rich in elements that naturally facilitate or enhance the perceived motion of the foreground object, thereby enriching the dynamic interaction within the composition.

\mathit{I}_{\mathit{m}}

\mathit{I}_{\mathit{bg}}

MotionPaint

The intermediate composite image
\mathit{I}_{\mathit{int}}
does not demonstrate any interaction between the inserted object and
\mathit{I}_{\mathit{bg}}
. To obtain coherent and dynamic interaction, we propose
MotionPaint
to distill motion prior from pretrained I2V video diffusion models. This approach not only enhances efficiency by avoiding the extensive training process but also ensures the interactions are realistically portrayed, leveraging the video diffusion model’s robust and diverse motion patterns learned from videos.

In short, with input
\mathit{I}_{\mathit{int}}
and its corresponding mask
\mathit{I}_{\mathit{m}}

To do so, a naive approach is to input
\mathit{I}_{\mathit{int}}
into the video diffusion model directly to obtain animated scenes from the predicted later frames. However, such a direct application does not consider the background consistency across frames and leads to unintended animation of the entire scene, including the background alteration and potential camera movements, which contradicts our objective to convey motion exclusively within the inserted foreground object while maintaining the given background. This nuanced portrayal of movement, while ultimately captured within a single image, necessitates a more targeted approach than directly applying video diffusion models, which inherently animates the entire scene.

Experiment

Implementation Details

In our experiment, we choose GPT-4V
(
Achiam et al. 2023
)
as the planner and captioner in the entire planning phase. Given the output split ratio
\mathit{S}_{\mathit{ratio}}
and region
R*
we automatically composite the given foreground and background together. This takes a total of 30 seconds. For the generation phase, we use the I2V Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) model
(
Blattmann et al. 2023a
)
, which generate 25 frames at resolution 576x1024. We reshape all the intermediate composite image from planning phase and its corresponding mask to 576x1024 for the generation phase. We use a general box as mask instead of exact mask to allow rooms for object movement in the motion aware composition. The generation phase MotionPaint takes a total of 90 seconds on a single Tesla V100 on T=25 sampling steps. All foreground and background images used for inference are sourced from the Internet, which we combine to form composite images.

These metrics lacking the sophistication to differentiate between rudimentary cut-and-paste operations and the more nuanced, motion-aware image compositions characteristic of our work as they cannot fully capture the dynamic nature of foreground changes.
Hence, they fall short in appreciating the intricate interplay of motion and context we introduce, which is central to the innovation our approach brings to the table. Recognizing the limitations of traditional evaluation methods, we employ a user survey to measure the impact of our motion-aware compositions. This allows us to gauge user perception, which is pivotal in recognizing the subtleties and dynamism our method infuses into the images, providing a more holistic and suitable evaluation framework for our work.

Conclusion

In this work, we introduced MotionCom, taking a significant step forward in the field of image composition by pioneering the task of motion-aware image composition. By integrating LVLM with Video Diffusion motion priors, MotionCom facilitates the automatic, seamless introduction of objects into new scenes, ensuring they blend dynamically and coherently without the necessity for manual planning or extensive finetuning. Moreover, MotionCom’s adeptness extends across various domains, allowing for the composition of objects not just within their native contexts but also into entirely new and diverse environments, thereby broadening the scope of creative possibilities.

Limitations and future work.

While MotionCom marks a leap forward in motion-aware image composition, its current limitations must be recognized to fully appreciate its capabilities and areas for future development. The reliance on SVD for motion generation in MotionPaint means there is no explicit control over the types of motion; the process depends on generating multiple seeds and selecting the best fit. Although this method is generally effective, it introduces a degree of unpredictability. Addressing this, future work aims to enable specific motion control within MotionCom, enhancing efficiency and reducing the need for multiple seeds generation to achieve desired motion effects.

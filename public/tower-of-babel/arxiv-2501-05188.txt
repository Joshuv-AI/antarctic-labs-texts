Introduction

In this paper, we continue to study the defocusing, energy-subcriticial nonlinear wave equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Delta u+|u|^{p-1}u=0,&(t,x)\in I\times\Omega\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega\\
\partial_{t}u(0,x)=u_{1}(x),&x\in\Omega\\
u(t,x)=0,&x\in\partial\Omega\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
3<p<5

0\in I

\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\overline{B}(0,1)

2.1.
Notation

We denote the 3D Dirichlet-Laplacian operator out of the ball by
-\Delta_{\Omega}
. Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be an interval of time, we write
L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{p}(I\times\Omega)
denote the Banach space with norm

\|u\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{p}(I\times\Omega)}=\left(\int_{I}\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(t,x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

We write
X\lesssim Y
or
Y\gtrsim X
to indicate
X\leqslant CY
for some constant
C>0
not depending on
X
or
Y
. We use
\mathcal{O}(Y)
to denote any quantity
X
such that
|X|\lesssim Y
. We use the notation
X\approx Y
, if satisfying
X\lesssim Y\lesssim X
. For any positive number
p\in[1,\infty]

p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}

.

2.2.
Distorted Fourier transform

Now we recall the distorted Fourier transform associated to the Dirichlet-Laplacian operator
-\Delta_{\Omega}
in
[
36
,
51
]
. The spectral resolution for radial functions on
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\overline{B}(0,1)
is expressed by radial, generalized eigenfunctions

-\Delta_{\Omega}e_{\lambda}=\lambda^{2}e_{\lambda}

for
\lambda>0
which satisfy the Sommerfeld radiation condition, namely

e_{\lambda}(r)=\frac{\sin\lambda(r-1)}{r},\,\,\,r\geqslant 1.

(2.1)

For the radial, tempered distributions
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
which supported on
\Omega
, we denote the distorted Fourier transform by

\mathcal{F}_{D}u(\lambda)=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin\lambda(s-1)}{s}u(s)s^{2}\,ds.

(2.2)

Note that the following resolution of identity

\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e_{\lambda}(r)e_{\lambda}(s)\,d\lambda=\frac{\delta(r-s)}{s^{2}}

from which it follows that

\mathcal{F}_{D}^{-1}\mathcal{F}_{D}u=u

for the radial function
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
supported in
\Omega
, where
\mathcal{F}_{D}^{-1}
is the formal adjoint, defined on the tempered distributions
v
as the restriction to
r=|x|\geqslant 1
of

\mathcal{F}_{D}^{-1}v(r)=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin\lambda(r-1)}{r}v(\lambda)\,d\lambda.

(2.3)

Consequently,
u\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{D}u
induces an isometric map

\mathcal{F}_{D}:L^{2}([1,\infty),s^{2}ds)\rightarrow L^{2}([0,\infty),d\lambda).

Given a bounded function
m(\lambda)
, which for convenience we assume to be defined on all of
\mathbb{R}
and even in
\lambda
, and radial function
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we define

m(\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}})u(r)=\mathcal{F}_{D}^{-1}(m(\cdot)\mathcal{F}_{D}u)(r).

(2.4)

This defines a functional calculus on
L^{2}_{rad}(\Omega)
and takes the expression as

\displaystyle m(\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}})f(r)=\int^{\infty}_{1}K_{m}(r,s)f(s)\,s^{2}\,ds

with

\displaystyle K_{m}(r,s)=\frac{2}{\pi}\,\int^{\infty}_{0}e_{\lambda}(r)\,e_{\lambda}(s)\,m(\lambda)\;d\lambda.

In general, we have the following Mikhlin Multiplier theorem.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
32
,
36
,
51
]
)
.

Suppose
m:[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
obeys

\displaystyle\left|\partial^{k}_{\lambda}m(\lambda)\right|\lesssim\lambda^{-k}

for all integer
k\in[0,2]
. Then
m(\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}})
, which we define via the
L^{2}
functional calculus, extends uniquely from
L^{2}(\Omega)\cap L^{p}(\Omega)
to a bounded operator on
L^{p}(\Omega)
, for all
1<p<\infty
.

With the help of the heat kernel estimate in
[
52
]
and harmonic analysis outside of a convex obstacle in
[
32
]
, we can define the homogeneous Sobolev space outside a ball.

Definition 2.2
.

For
s>0
and
1<p<\infty
,
\dot{H}^{s,p}(\Omega)
denote the completions of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
under the norm

\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s,p}(\Omega)}:=\|(-\Delta_{\Omega})^{\frac{s}{2}}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

When
p=2
, we write
\dot{H}^{s}_{D}(\Omega)
for
\dot{H}^{s,2}_{D}(\Omega)
, respectively.

Definition 2.3
.

The space
\dot{H}^{s,p}_{00}\Omega)
is the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
\dot{H}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
32
]
)
.

For
1<p<\infty
, and
s<1+\frac{1}{p}
,
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
is dense in
\dot{H}^{s,p}_{D}(\Omega)
.

The equivalence between
\dot{H}^{s,p}_{D}(\Omega)
and
\dot{H}^{s,p}_{00}(\Omega)
with proper exponents then follows from the square function estimate, Riesz potential estimate and the Hardy’s inequalities for the exterior domain
\Omega
and the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
in
[
32
,
40
,
48
]

Proposition 2.5
.

(
[
32
]
)
. Let
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\overline{B}(0,1)
. Suppose
1<p<\infty
and
0\leqslant s<\min\{1+\frac{1}{p},\frac{3}{p}\}
, then we have

\|(-\Delta_{\Omega})^{\frac{s}{2}}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\approx_{d,p,s}\|(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3}})^{\frac{s}{2}}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

(2.5)

Thus
\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}_{D}(\Omega)}=\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
for these values of the parameters.

As an direct application, we have the following fractional chain rule for the exterior domain.

Corollary 2.6

(Fractional chain rule,
[
32
]
)
.

Suppose
F\in C^{1}(\mathbb{C})
,
s\in(0,1]
, and
1<p,p_{1},p_{2}<\infty
are such that
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}
and
0<s<\min\left(1+\frac{1}{p_{2}},\frac{3}{p_{2}}\right)
, then we have

\|(-\Delta_{\Omega})^{\frac{s}{2}}F(u)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|F^{\prime}(u)\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\Omega)}\|(-\Delta_{\Omega})^{\frac{s}{2}}u\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\Omega)}.

(2.6)

As stated in the introduction, we also need the following radial Sobolev inequality to control the growth of the energy and
L^{2}(\Omega)
of the solution
v
for the difference equation with low-frequency-localized data because of the super-conformal nonlinearity in next section.

Proposition 2.7

(Radial Sobolev inequality)
.

Fixed
1\leqslant p<\infty
. For a radial function
u\in\dot{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega)\cap L^{p}(\Omega)
, then there exists a constant
C
such that

r^{\frac{4}{p+2}}|u(r)|\leq C\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{\frac{p}{p+2}}\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{D,rad}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2}{p+2}}

(2.7)

for almost everywhere
r\geq 1
. Particularly, we have
\dot{H}^{1}_{D,rad}(\Omega)\subset L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

We follow the similar argument as that in Radial Lemma in
[
50
]
. By the density result in Proposition
2.4
, it suffices to show the inequality for the radial function
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
. By the fundamental theorem of calculus, we have

\displaystyle\left(r^{2}u(r)^{\frac{p+2}{2}}\right)_{r}=2ru(r)^{\frac{p+2}{2}}+\frac{p+2}{2}r^{2}u(r)^{\frac{p}{2}}u_{r}.

Integrating on
(1,r)
, we have

\displaystyle\left|r^{2}u(r)^{\frac{p+2}{2}}\right|=

\displaystyle\left|\int^{r}_{1}2su(s)^{\frac{p+2}{2}}+\frac{p+2}{2}s^{2}u(s)^{\frac{p}{2}}u_{s}\;ds\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\int^{r}_{1}|u|^{p}\,s^{2}\;ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int^{r}_{1}\frac{|u|^{2}}{s^{2}}\,s^{2}\;ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\int^{r}_{1}|u|^{p}\,s^{2}\;ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int^{r}_{1}|u_{s}|^{2}\,s^{2}\;ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\;\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{\frac{p}{2}}\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{D,rad}(\Omega)},

where we used the Hardy inequality in
[
32
]
in the last inequality. This completes the proof.

2.3.
Littlewood-Paley theory

In this subsection, we describe the Littlewood-Paley theory adapted to the Dirichlet-Laplacian operator
-\Delta_{\Omega}
by the distorted Fourier transform. We can also refer to
[
20
,
34
,
35
,
41
]
for other applications.

Fixed
\phi:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,1]
a smooth non-negative function satisfying
\phi(\lambda)=1
, for
0\leqslant\lambda\leqslant 1
and
\phi(\lambda)=0
, for
\lambda\geqslant 2
. For each dyadic number
N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
, we define

\phi_{N}(\lambda):=\phi(\frac{\lambda}{N})\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \psi_{N}(\lambda):=\phi_{N}(\lambda)-\phi_{\frac{N}{2}}(\lambda).

Let
m(\lambda)=\psi_{N}(\lambda)
in (
2.4
), we define the Littlewood-Paley projections

\displaystyle P^{\Omega}_{\leq N}u:=\phi_{N}(\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}})u,\quad P^{\Omega}_{N}u:=\psi_{N}(\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}})u,\quad P^{\Omega}_{>N}u:=I-P^{\Omega}_{\leq N}u.

We define the homogeneous Besov space as the following.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leqslant q,r\leqslant\infty
. The homogeneous Besov space
\dot{B}^{s}_{D,q,r}(\Omega)
consists of the distributions
u
supported on
\Omega
such that

\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{D,q,r}(\Omega)}=\left(\sum\limits_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}N^{sr}\|P_{N}^{\Omega}u\|^{r}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}<\infty.

As in the whole space case, we have the following Bernstein inequality.

Proposition 2.9

(
[
32
,
36
]
)
.

For any radial function
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|P^{\Omega}_{\leq N}u\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+

\displaystyle\left\|P^{\Omega}_{N}u\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},

(2.8)

\displaystyle N^{s}\left\|P^{\Omega}_{N}u\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\approx\left\|\big(-\Delta_{\Omega}\big)^{s/2}P^{\Omega}_{N}u\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

(2.9)

for any
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\left\|P^{\Omega}_{\leq N}u\right\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\left\|P^{\Omega}_{N}u\right\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\lesssim N^{3\big(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\big)}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

for all
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
. The implicit constants depend only on
p,q
and
s
.

Therefore, for any
1<p<\infty
and any radial
u\in L^{p}(\Omega)
, we have the following homogeneous decomposition

\displaystyle u(x)=\sum_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}P^{\Omega}_{N}\,u(x).

In particular, the sums converge in
L^{p}(\Omega)
.

2.4.
Strichartz estimate

Now we consider the 3D Dirichlet boundary value problem of the linear wave equation with radial data

\begin{cases}\partial^{2}_{t}u-\Delta u=F,&(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\Omega,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\\
\partial_{t}u(0,x)=u_{1}(x),&x\in\Omega,\\
u(t,x)=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(2.10)

where
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\bar{B}(0,1)
and
u_{0},u_{1}
and the inhomogeneous term
F
are radial in

u(t,x)=\cos(t\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}})u_{0}+\frac{\sin(t\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}})}{\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}}}u_{1}+\int^{t}_{0}\frac{\sin((t-s)\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}})}{\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}}}F(s,x)\,ds.

Let us denote the half-wave operator as

U(t)=e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}}},

then

\displaystyle\cos(t\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}})u_{0}=\frac{U(t)+U(-t)}{2}u_{0},\quad\frac{\sin(t\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}})}{\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}}}u_{1}=\frac{U(t)-U(-t)}{2i\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}}}u_{1}.

[

]

(Dispersive estimate.
[

]

Let

\displaystyle\big\|U(t)f\big\|_{\dot{B}^{-\beta(r)}_{D,r,2}(\Omega)}\lesssim|t|^{-\gamma(r)}\big\|f\big\|_{\dot{B}^{\beta(r)}_{D,r^{\prime},2}(\Omega)}

where

{}_{L^{q_{1}}_{I}\dot{B}^{\rho_{1}-1}_{D,r_{1},2}(\Omega)\cap C(I;\dot{H}_{D}^{\mu-1}(\Omega))}

3.3
, we have

E(v)(t)\leqslant E_{T}\lesssim 2^{2J(1-s)}+T^{\frac{1}{2}}E_{T}^{\frac{9(p-1)}{2(p+3)}}2^{J(s_{c}-s)}+E_{T}2^{2J(s_{c}-s)}.

(3.23)

If we choose
T
such that

T^{\frac{1}{2}}2^{2J(1-s)\frac{9(p-1)}{2(p+3)}}2^{J(s_{c}-s)}\approx 2^{2J(1-s)}\Longleftrightarrow T\approx 2^{\frac{2J}{p+3}\left[4(2p-3)s-4(2p-3)+(p+3)(1-s_{c})\right]},

(3.24)

we obtain that

E_{T}\lesssim 2^{2J(1-s)}\approx T^{\frac{(p+3)(1-s)}{4(2p-3)s-4(2p-3)+(p+3)(1-s_{c})}},

(3.25)

which shows that the energy of
v
can be controlled globally as long as
s>1-\frac{(p+3)(1-s_{c})}{4(2p-3)}
.

3.4.
Growth estimate of low frequency part in
\dot{H}^{s}_{D}(\Omega)

From Subsection
3.1
, and Subsection
3.3
, we know that the solution
u
of (
1.1
) globally exists in
\dot{H}^{s_{c}}_{D,rad}(\Omega)\cap\dot{H}^{s}_{D,rad}(\Omega)+\dot{H}^{1}_{D,rad}(\Omega)
with
s>1-\frac{(p+3)(1-s_{c})}{4(2p-3)}
. Now we show
the
\dot{H}^{s}_{D}(\Omega)
-estimate of
u
and complets the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

By Proposition
3.1
, it suffices to show the estimate of
v
in
\dot{H}^{s}_{D}(\Omega)
. Noticed that the homogeneous part of
v

\cos(t\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}})v_{0}+\frac{\sin(t\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}})}{\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}}}v_{1}

is bounded in
\dot{H}^{s}_{D}(\Omega)
by the energy estimate.

It reduces to show the estimate of inhomogeneous part of
v
in
\dot{H}^{s}_{D}(\Omega)
.

Using Proposition
3.3
and interpolation argument, it suffices to show the estimate of inhomogeneous part of
v
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. By the distorted Fourier transform, we have for any
0\leqslant t\leqslant T
,

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sin((t-s)\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}})}{\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}}}F(v,w)(s)\,ds\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}([0,T)\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}\left\|\frac{\sin\big((t-s)\lambda\big)}{\lambda}\mathcal{F}_{D}F(v,w)(\lambda)\right\|_{L^{2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)\left\|v(s)^{p-1}v(s)+\mathcal{O}(|v(s)|^{p-1}|w(s)|)+\mathcal{O}(|v(s)||w(s)|^{p-1})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,ds.

(3.26)

We estimate one by one as follows.

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)\left\|v(s)^{p-1}v(s)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,ds\lesssim

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)\|v(s)\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}^{3}\||x|^{\frac{4}{p+3}}v(s)\|^{p-3}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)\|v(s)\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}^{3}\|v\|^{\frac{(p+1)(p-3)}{p+3}}_{L^{p+1}(\Omega)}\|v\|^{\frac{2(p-3)}{p+3}}_{\dot{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega)}\,ds\lesssim T^{2}E_{T}^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{2(p-3)}{p+3}},

and

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)\left\||v(s)|^{p-1}|w(s)|\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,ds\lesssim

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)\|v(s)\|_{L^{\frac{3(p-1)^{2}}{2(p-2)}}(\Omega)}^{p-1}\|w(s)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{1-s_{c}}}(\Omega)}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)\|v(s)\|^{\frac{4(p-2)}{p-1}}_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\|v(s)\|^{\frac{(p-3)^{2}}{p-1}}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|w(s)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{1-s_{c}}}(\Omega)}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)\|v(s)\|^{\frac{4(p-2)}{p-1}}_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\|v\|^{\frac{(p+1)(p-3)^{2}}{(p-1)(p+3)}}_{L^{p+1}(\Omega)}\|v\|^{\frac{2(p-3)^{2}}{(p-1)(p+3)}}_{\dot{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega)}\|w(s)\|_{L^{\frac{3}{1-s_{c}}}(\Omega)}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle T^{\frac{3}{2}}\,E_{T}^{\frac{2(p-2)}{p-1}+\frac{(p-3)^{2}}{(p-1)(p+3)}+\frac{(p-3)^{2}}{(p-1)(p+3)}}\|w\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\frac{3}{1-s_{c}}}([0,T)\times\Omega)},

and

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)\left\||v(s)||w(s)|^{p-1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,ds\lesssim

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)\|w(s)\|_{L^{3(p-1)}(\Omega)}^{p-1}\|v(s)\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\;T\,E_{T}^{\frac{1}{2}}\;\|w\|^{p-1}_{L_{t}^{p-1}L_{x}^{3(p-1)}([0,T)\times\Omega)}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sin((t-s)\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}})}{\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}}}F(v,w)(s)\,ds\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}([0,T)\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\;T^{2}E_{T}^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{2(p-3)}{p+3}}+T^{\frac{3}{2}}\,E_{T}^{\frac{2(p-2)}{p-1}+\frac{2(p-3)^{2}}{(p-1)(p+3)}}\|w\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\frac{3}{1-s_{c}}}([0,T)\times\Omega)}+T\,E_{T}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|w\|^{p-1}_{L_{t}^{p-1}L_{x}^{3(p-1)}([0,T)\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\;T^{2+\frac{(7p-3)(1-s)}{2\left[4(2p-3)s-4(2p-3)+(p+3)(1-s_{c})\right]}},

(3.27)

where
T
determined by (
3.24
). Therefore, using the interpolation between
L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\dot{H}^{1}_{D}(\Omega)
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sin((t-s)\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}})}{\sqrt{-\Delta_{\Omega}}}F(v,w)(s)\,ds\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}\left([0,T);\dot{H}^{s}_{D}(\Omega)\right)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(T^{2+\frac{(7p-3)(1-s)}{2\left[4(2p-3)s-4(2p-3)+(p+3)(1-s_{c})\right]}}\right)^{1-s}\left(T^{\frac{(p+3)(1-s)}{2\left[4(2p-3)s-4(2p-3)+(p+3)(1-s_{c})\right]}}\right)^{s}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle T^{2(1-s)+\frac{\left(7p-3-(6p-6)s\right)(1-s)}{2\left[4(2p-3)s-4(2p-3)+(p+3)(1-s_{c})\right]}}.

Therefore we complete the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

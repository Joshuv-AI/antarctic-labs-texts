Development and internal validation of dynamic nomograms for predicting recurrence and survival in patients with resected combined small cell lung cancer
Authors: Chunhua Zeng, Jingjuan Zhu, Jingming Xue, Jinlong Teng, Hong Liu, Baohui Han, Jing Wang, Yanan Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe clinical features and prognosis of combined small cell lung cancer (C-SCLC) are not well understood. Given the unique histological heterogeneity of C-SCLC, the 8th tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) staging system has limited prognostic accuracy in this population, necessitating a dedicated prediction model. This study aimed to develop and internally validate dynamic nomograms incorporating combined subtypes and serum tumor markers to predict recurrence and survival in patients with resected C-SCLC.MethodsA total of 223 patients with resected C-SCLC were enrolled in this study between 2008 and 2021. Eligibility criteria were as follows: surgically resected and pathologically confirmed C-SCLC with complete clinical data. Due to the limited sample size, no training/validation split was performed; internal validation was conducted using 1,000 bootstrap resamples. All serum tumor markers including neuron-specific enolase (NSE) were measured within one week before surgery. Visceral pleural invasion (VPI) and combined histological components were independently reviewed by two professional pathologists. The independent prognostic factors were identified and integrated to build the nomograms for predicting 3- and 5-year disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS) based on stepwise Cox regression. The discrimination and predictive accuracy of the models were evaluated using the concordance index (C-index) and calibration curves. Decision curve analyses (DCAs) were performed to verify the clinical utility of the model compared with that of the 8th edition of the International Association for the Study of Lung Cancer (IASLC) TNM staging system. Based on the nomogram scores, the C-SCLC patients were divided into high- and low-risk subgroups. An online webserver was applied to facilitate the convenient use of the model.ResultsUltimately, six independent prognostic factors, including tumor location, combined components, VPI, adjuvant chemotherapy, lymph node metastasis, and serum NSE level, were identified and incorporated into the nomograms. The median follow-up duration was 42.6 months. During follow-up, 101 patients developed recurrence or metastasis, and 121 died. The median age of the cohort was 64 years, with a predominance of male patients (89.7%). Among the patients, 27.8% were classified as stage I, and large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma (LCNEC) was the most common combined component (67.3%). The C-index values of the nomograms for predicting DFS [0.730, 95% confidence interval (CI): 0662–0.720] and OS (0.748, 95% CI: 0.682–0.734) were significantly higher than those of the TNM staging system (0.601, 95% CI: 0.574–0.628 and 0.615, 95% CI: 0.586–0.644 for DFS and OS, respectively; P<0.001). The calibration plots indicated good agreement between the model-predicted and observed survival. The DCA results showed that the developed nomograms demonstrated better predictive performance than the TNM staging system.ConclusionsOur internal validation suggests that the nomograms outperform the TNM staging system; however, these findings should be considered as hypothesis-generating. External validation with multicenter cohorts is essential before clinical implementation. The online tool is primarily intended to facilitate further research.

Highlight box

Key findings

• Tumor location, lymph node metastasis, visceral pleural invasion, combined components, adjuvant chemotherapy, and serum neuron-specific enolase level were identified as independent prognostic factors for both disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS) in resected combined small cell lung cancer (C-SCLC); these six factors constituted the tumor location, combined components, visceral pleural invasion, chemotherapy, neuron-specific enolase (TCVPN) nomogram.

• The proposed nomogram (TCVPN) demonstrated superior predictive performance [concordance index (C-index): 0.730 for DFS and 0.748 for OS] compared to the 8th edition of the tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) staging system.

• An online dynamic nomogram was developed to facilitate individualized risk assessment and clinical decision-making.

What is known, and what is new?

• C-SCLC is a rare variant. Data on its clinical features and prognosis are limited, and previous studies have largely grouped it with pure SCLC.

• This study was the first to develop and validate nomograms incorporating combined histological subtypes and serum tumor markers to predict recurrence and survival in resected C-SCLC based on the largest surgical cohort to date.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• The nomograms provide a more accurate prognostic tool than TNM staging alone, enabling personalized postoperative surveillance and adjuvant therapy planning.

• The wider adoption of these models in clinical practice, along with its validation in multicenter studies, is needed to optimize management strategies for patients with C-SCLC.

Introduction

Small cell lung cancer (SCLC) is the most malignant subtype of lung cancer, accounting for about 15% of cases (1-3). Under the 2015 World Health Organization (WHO) classification, SCLC is divided into pure SCLC and combined SCLC (C-SCLC) (4). C-SCLC is defined as the combination of SCLC type and non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) subtypes, including adenocarcinoma (AD), squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), large cell carcinoma (LCC), large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma (LCNEC), and other rare types, such as spindle-cell carcinoma and giant cell carcinoma. Most previous studies on SCLC have focused on pure SCLC or taken both components as a whole, resulting in an insufficient understanding of its clinicopathological features and prognosis (5-7). In addition, some studies have found that C-SCLC has its own unique characteristics compared with pure SCLC (8-10). Thus, it is unreasonable to equate these two subtypes.

Pathological diagnosis based on cytology or limited-sample biopsies is often incomplete, which may lead to the underdiagnosis of C-SCLC. In recent years, with advances in diagnostic technology and the extensive application of surgery for early-stage SCLC, the detection of C-SCLC has increased, leading to a higher reported incidence (8,11-13). Nomograms have been widely used to predict prognosis in a variety of cancers (14-17), including SCLC, and have been validated for application in clinical practice. Previous population-based studies, including those utilizing the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database, have identified age, tumor stage, and surgical status as prognostic factors in SCLC (18-20). However, these registry-based analyses typically group C-SCLC with pure SCLC and do not capture C-SCLC-specific variables such as combined histological subtypes (e.g., LCNEC vs. AD or SCC), VPI, or serum tumor markers such as neuron-specific enolase (NSE). To our knowledge, no dedicated prognostic model has been developed specifically for resected C-SCLC. The 8th edition tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) staging system, despite being widely used, has limited discriminative ability [concordance index (C-index) ~0.60] in this unique population due to its histological heterogeneity. Therefore, we aim to address this specific knowledge gap by constructing a prediction tool that integrates C-SCLC-specific features (e.g., combined histological subtypes, serum NSE levels, and VPI) to improve prognostic accuracy. Due to the rarity of C-SCLC, survival models derived from cohorts with sufficiently large sample size have been lacking. To address this, we collected data from 223 patients with surgically confirmed C-SCLC to develop nomograms for predicting recurrence and survival.

We acknowledge that surgical resection is performed in only a small fraction of SCLC cases, and consequently, the potential targeted population of our tool is limited to patients with resected C-SCLC. However, even for this rare subgroup, accurate prognostic stratification is clinically valuable for guiding postoperative adjuvant chemotherapy decisions and determining surveillance intensity. The incremental value of our proposed nomogram over the TNM system lies in the incorporation of combined histological components, serum NSE levels, and VPI—factors that reflect the unique biology of C-SCLC but are absent from the traditional staging framework.

While machine learning methods (e.g., random survival forests, support vector machines) have been increasingly applied in oncologic prognostication and may offer advantages in handling complex interactions, we opted for traditional Cox regression-based nomogram development for several reasons. First, our limited sample size (n=223) precludes the robust training of more complex algorithms without substantial risk of overfitting. Second, nomograms provide interpretable, point-based scoring systems that facilitate clinical adoption. Third, our approach adheres to Transparent Reporting of a multivariable prediction model for Individual Prognosis Or Diagnosis (TRIPOD) guidelines for prediction model development in smaller cohorts, where traditional regression methods remain the standard. Given the rarity of resected C-SCLC, we emphasize that the model we present is hypothesis-generating and intended to facilitate further research. External validation with independent, ideally multicenter, cohorts is essential before any clinical implementation. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2026-0999/rc).

Methods

Ethics statement

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Shanghai Chest Hospital (No. KS[Y]1934). The requirement for individual consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of the analysis.

Patients

The data of 1,086 patients with SCLC who underwent surgery at Shanghai Chest Hospital from January 2008 to December 2021 were retrospectively analyzed. All pathologic diagnoses of the surgical specimens were independently evaluated by two professional pathologists according to the 2015 WHO classification of lung cancer (4). C-SCLC is defined as a rare variant of SCLC in which the tumor exhibits a mixture of classic SCLC and any histological component of NSCLC, including AD, SCC, LCC, LCNEC, or other subtypes. Notably, when LCNEC is present as the combined component, it must account for at least 10% of the tumor volume to qualify as C-SCLC. TNM stage was determined according to the 8th edition of the International Association for the Study of Lung Cancer (IASLC) classification system (21). Serum tumor markers, including NSE, carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), cytokeratin-19-fragment (CYFRA21-1), SCC antigen (SCCA), and carbohydrate antigen 125 (CA125), were measured within one week prior to surgery using standardized laboratory assays. Thresholds for defining ‘high’ versus ‘normal’ levels were based on the manufacturer’s established upper limit of normal (ULN) for each assay: NSE <16.0 ng/mL (normal) vs. ≥16.0 ng/mL (high); CEA <5.0 ng/mL (normal) vs. ≥5.0 ng/mL (high); CYFRA21-1 <5.0 ng/mL (normal) vs. ≥5.0 ng/mL (high); SCCA <1.5 ng/mL (normal) vs. ≥1.5 ng/mL (high); CA125 <35.0 U/mL (normal) vs. ≥35.0 U/mL (high). No data-driven cut-off optimization (e.g., based on median or receiver operating characteristic analysis) was performed to avoid overfitting. As shown in the study flow chart (Figure 1), 223 patients diagnosed with C-SCLC were ultimately included in our study.

Preoperative diagnosis

Due to the small lesion size, technical sampling difficulties, or patient refusal against invasive procedures, a preoperative pathological diagnosis could not be established in 84 patients with postoperatively confirmed SCLC. Conversely, among the 130 patients who underwent preoperative biopsy (bronchoscopic or percutaneous), the diagnostic accuracy for SCLC was 30.0% (39/130), while 44.6% (58/130) of the preoperative biopsy results were misclassified as NSCLC, and 25.4% (33/130) were reported as lung cancer, not otherwise specified.

Preoperative imaging revealed suspected lymph node involvement or metastasis in 12 patients, of whom, 5 underwent mediastinoscopy; all procedures returned negative results; the remaining 7 patients chose to forgo the procedure due to concerns related to procedural risks.

Follow-up

The patients with C-SCLC were followed from diagnosis to April 2025. During follow-up, 101 patients developed recurrence or metastasis, and 121 had died by the end of the study period. Routine examinations were recommended for all patients after surgery, including chest computed tomography (CT) scans, brain magnetic resonance imaging or CT, pulmonary tumor markers, and neck and abdominal ultrasound.

The follow-up data of the patients were obtained from medical records and telephone interviews. Disease-free survival (DFS) was defined as the time from the date of surgery to the relevant endpoint (recurrence, metastasis, or death) or to the last follow-up. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the time from surgery to death or the last follow-up. Both DFS and OS were measured in months.

Development of the prognostic models and survival comparison

Clinical variables were included in a multivariate Cox regression, and stepwise selection was performed to identify variables for the development of the nomograms. The final predictors included in the nomogram models were determined based on Akaike Information Criterion following stepwise Cox regression analysis (22). Sample size adequacy was assessed using the events per variable (EPV) criterion. A total of 101 recurrence/metastasis events were observed for the DFS model, and 121 deaths for the OS model. With 6 predictor variables included in the final multivariate Cox models, the EPV was 16.8 for DFS and 20.2 for OS, both exceeding the recommended minimum of 10–15 EPV, suggesting that the risk of overfitting is relatively low. The discriminatory ability of the nomogram models was assessed using the C-index. The calibration was evaluated by calibration plots, which indicate the agreement between the predicted and observed outcomes (23). Internal validation of the prediction models was performed using 1,000 bootstrap resamplings (24). The ability of the current models and the 8th edition of the IASLC TNM staging system to predict survival was assessed using the C-index and decision curve analysis (DCA) (25). To enable clinicians to estimate the probability of recurrence and survival based on patient characteristics, an online dynamic nomogram was established. The median nomogram-derived risk score was used to stratify patients into high- and low-risk groups. The survival of the two groups was compared using the Kaplan-Meier method and log-rank test.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis was performed using SPSS software (version 26.0, IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY, USA) and R software (version 4.0.2, R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). The R packages “survival”, “rms”, “foreign”, “dca”, “DynNom”, and “shiny” were used for the survival analysis, and nomogram development and validation. A two-tailed P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Clinical and pathological characteristics of study patients

Ultimately, 223 patients met the study inclusion criteria and were included in the study analysis (Figure 1). As shown in Table 1, most of the patients were men (200/223, 89.7%) with a strong smoking history (165/223, 74%). C-SCLC mainly occurred in the upper lobe of the lung (123/223, 55.2%), and was predominately located in peripheral region (140/223, 62.8%). Among the patients, 27.8% were classified as stage I. The majority of C-SCLC cases (67.3%) included LCNEC as the primary combined component with SCLC. Among the tumor markers of CEA, CYFRA21-1, SCCA, NSE, and CA125, NSE—the most prominent marker in the SCLC component—showed the highest positivity rate at 67%.

Construction of the prognostic nomogram models

Based on the above prognostic analyses, tumor location, lymph node metastasis, VPI, combined components, adjuvant chemotherapy, and serum NSE level were ultimately included in the construction of the nomogram models for DFS and OS (Figure 2A,2B). These variables were collectively abbreviated as TCVPN. In the nomograms, each subtype of these variables was assigned a corresponding point score. A patient’s total risk score was calculated as the sum of the points of all variables. The probabilities of 3- and 5-year DFS or OS were then estimated by matching the total points with corresponding position to the probability scale.

The patients were divided into high- and low-risk subgroups based on the total risk scores (median risk score for DFS: 73.48; range, 4.73–214.80; and median risk score for OS: 71.23; range, 4.73–205.27). The Kaplan-Meier curves showed significantly worse OS and DFS in the high-risk group compared with the low-risk group (Figure 2C for OS and Figure 2D for DFS, P<0.05 for all).

Internal validation of the nomograms

The calibration curves based on bootstrap resampling validation are shown in Figure 3. The curves demonstrated good agreement between nomogram-predicted and observed 3- and 5-year DFS and OS (Figure 3A for DFS and Figure 3B for OS). The C-indexes for the nomograms for DFS and OS were 0.730 [95% confidence interval (CI): 0.662–0.720] and 0.748 (95% CI: 0.682–0.734), respectively, which were significantly higher than those for the 8th edition of the IASLC TNM staging system (DFS: 0.601, 95% CI: 0.574–0.628; and OS: 0.615, 95% CI: 0.586–0.644; P<0.001). These suggest that the model's ability to rank-order patient risk is limited. In high-stakes oncological decision-making, a C-index below 0.75 is generally considered insufficient for being a standalone clinical tool. To assess the model's ability to accurately classify patients at clinically relevant thresholds, we calculated time-dependent sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), and negative predictive value (NPV) using the median nomogram-derived risk score as the prespecified cut-off (73.48 for DFS and 71.23 for OS). For DFS, at 3 years: sensitivity =74.8%, specificity =69.1%, PPV =62.5%, NPV =80.4%; at 5 years: sensitivity =71.7%, specificity =68.9%, PPV =70.3%, NPV =75.2%. For OS, at 3 years: sensitivity =74.6%, specificity =60.9%, PPV =59.0%, NPV =84.8%; at 5 years: sensitivity =69.9%, specificity =66.7%, PPV =64.9%, NPV =72.6%. The 3- and 5-year OS and DFS DCA curves for the TCVPN model and TNM stage are shown in Figure 4. The results demonstrated that the nomograms provided higher net benefits than the 8th edition of the TNM staging system, indicating that the TCVPN model offers superior survival prediction for C-SCLC patients and may be suitable for clinical application.

A user-friendly webserver was developed for the DFS and OS nomograms (https://wangyanan.shinyapps.io/DynNomapp/ and https://leiel.shinyapps.io/DynNomapp/). By inputting the corresponding clinicopathologic features for a patient, users can quickly obtain individualized predicted survival probabilities and survival curves for DFS (Figure 5A) and OS (Figure 5B), providing highly personalized and visual results.

Discussion

Due to the rarity of C-SCLC, the characteristics and prognosis of these tumors are not well understood. The adoption of surgical resection as an appropriate therapy for early-stage SCLC has enabled more accurate histological classification, improving the diagnostic rate of C-SCLC. Compared with NSCLC, SCLC is associated with higher recurrence and lower survival rates, and thus requires close supervision and targeted treatment. To our knowledge, this study included the largest cohort of resected C-SCLC patients, and was the first to establish nomogram models for survival prediction. Tumor location, lymph node metastasis, VPI, combined components, adjuvant chemotherapy, and serum NSE level were identified as reliable predictors of DFS and OS when considered together.

The incidence of C-SCLC among SCLC cases has been reported to range from 5% to 28% (8,11,12). Among SCLC patients who underwent surgery, the proportion of C-SCLC increased to 45% (8,26). In our study, the incidence of surgically resected C-SCLC was 25.9%. Consistent with previous findings (12,27,28), most of these patients were men with a strong smoking history.

In this study, 62.8% of the C-SCLC lesions were located in the peripheral region. Peripheral lung lesions were independently associated with longer DFS and OS compared with central lung lesions. Consistent with previous reports (8,12,29), the most common component combined with SCLC was LCNEC. LCNEC, which shares neuroendocrine features with SCLC, often exhibits higher expression of neuroendocrine markers (e.g., synaptophysin, chromogranin A) and distinct genetic alterations (e.g., RB1 and TP53 mutations). In this study, we found that the presence of a LCNEC component was associated with longer DFS and OS compared with other combined histological components. Its better prognosis in our cohort may be attributed to its slower proliferative index and lower metastatic potential compared to non-neuroendocrine components. In contrast, non-LCNEC components (AD, SCC, etc.) represent non-neuroendocrine differentiation, which may confer resistance to standard SCLC chemotherapy regimens and be associated with epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), potentially explaining their worse prognosis. Due to limited sample sizes of individual non-LCNEC subtypes, we were unable to perform subtype-specific analyses; future multicenter studies are needed to explore the prognostic heterogeneity among different combined components. This result is inconsistent with previous studies based on relatively small sample sizes (12,30,31). Several factors may explain this discrepancy. First, previous studies often included patients with advanced-stage disease or those receiving nonsurgical treatments, whereas our cohort consisted exclusively of surgically resected patients with predominantly early-stage disease (27.8% stage I), potentially leading to a biologically less aggressive subset of LCNEC. Second, the 2015 WHO classification requires that LCNEC comprises at least 10% of the tumor volume for the diagnosis of C-SCLC; previous studies may have used different diagnostic thresholds. Third, patients with LCNEC components in our cohort may have received more aggressive adjuvant chemotherapy due to physician perception of neuroendocrine tumors as chemosensitive. Notably, our finding is consistent with several recent surgical series that reported comparable or better survival for C-SCLC with LCNEC components. We emphasize that this finding requires validation in independent cohorts.

Previous studies on C-SCLC have not analyzed the effect of VPI on prognosis. In the present study, we showed that VPI had an adverse effect on disease relapse and survival. At present, there are no specific treatment options for C-SCLC. The National Comprehensive Cancer Network guidelines recommend that C-SCLC be treated according to the SCLC guidelines (32). Adjuvant chemotherapy based on an etoposide + cisplatin or etoposide + carboplatin regimen is recommended for C-SCLC patients. Consistent with the results of previous studies (33,34), we found that adjuvant chemotherapy delays recurrence and improves the survival of C-SCLC patients. However, some studies have reported that C-SCLC is not sensitive to adjuvant chemotherapy, due to the presence of mixed NSCLC components (9,31). We acknowledge that the 13-year study period [2008–2021] spans a time of significant changes in the standard of care for SCLC. During most of this period, adjuvant chemotherapy for resected SCLC was based on etoposide + platinum regimens, which remain the backbone of treatment. However, the emergence of immune checkpoint inhibitors (e.g., atezolizumab, durvalumab) has transformed the treatment landscape for extensive-stage SCLC and is increasingly being investigated in the adjuvant setting for limited-stage disease. None of the patients in our cohort received immunotherapy, as it was not the standard of care during the study period. Therefore, our model may not accurately predict outcomes for patients receiving modern immunotherapy-containing regimens. Additionally, the role of postoperative radiotherapy and prophylactic cranial irradiation has evolved over time, and our model did not account for these modalities. Consequently, our model's predictions are most applicable to patients receiving traditional etoposide + platinum-based adjuvant chemotherapy without immunotherapy. We strongly encourage validation of our model in contemporary cohorts that include patients treated with modern immunotherapy and targeted therapies, as well as those receiving trimodality therapy. Lymph node metastasis and high NSE levels are well-established predictors of worse DFS and OS in SCLC patients, a finding which was also confirmed in the present study.

The survival-related variables were abbreviated as TCVPN. Using these variables to construct a nomogram for survival prediction, we found that the C-indices of our TCVPN-based nomograms were higher than those of the nomogram based on the TNM staging system. The DCA further confirmed the superior ability of our TCVPN-based nomogram to predict survival. The stable results from the internal validation suggest that our online dynamic nomogram may enhance patient-specific clinical decision-making for C-SCLC.

There are several limitations in this study. First, it was a retrospective, single-center study. Second, although our EPV analysis suggests adequate sample size relative to the number of predictors, the risk of overfitting cannot be completely excluded given the single-center design. External validation in an independent cohort is essential to confirm the model's generalizability and to assess potential overfitting. Due to the rarity of C-SCLC, it was difficult to find a large and independent cohort for external validation. Larger, multicenter studies are needed to validate the predictive ability of our survival models. Thirdly, the thresholds for serum tumor markers were based on the manufacturer’s ULN specific to the assays used in our single center. Different laboratories may employ different assays with varying reference ranges, which could affect the reproducibility of our model. This applies to all five markers assessed (NSE, CEA, CYFRA21-1, SCCA, and CA125). External validation studies should assess the model using locally established thresholds or, ideally, standardize biomarker measurements across institutions. Fourth, the classification metrics (sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV) that we reported were threshold-dependent, and the choice of the median risk score as the cut-off was inherently arbitrary. Clinicians may prefer different thresholds based on clinical context; for example, a lower threshold might be used when the goal is to maximize sensitivity to avoid missing high-risk patients who could benefit from adjuvant therapy. The optimal threshold should be determined in future external validation studies, ideally using DCA in conjunction with predefined clinical utility metrics. Therefore, the specific values reported herein should be interpreted as exploratory and require confirmation. Fifth, the biological heterogeneity among non-LCNEC combined subtypes was not fully explored due to sample size constraints, and our finding regarding better prognosis for LCNEC components requires external validation. Sixth, the study period predates the widespread use of immunotherapy for SCLC; therefore, our model may not reflect outcomes for patients receiving modern treatment regimens. Validation with contemporary cohorts is essential before the model can be applied to current clinical practice.

Conclusions

In conclusion, we described the clinicopathological features and prognosis of C-SCLC and developed the first nomograms to predict recurrence and survival in these patients. The online dynamic nomograms facilitate the evaluation of recurrence and survival risk in clinical practice, providing a potential tool to guide postoperative decision-making for C-SCLC patients.

Ultrasound-Guided Extraluminal Venous Valve Functional Remodeling
Authors: Weipu Zou, Meijia Xu, Qianqian Jia, Yuefeng Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEndovenous treatment for varicose veins is predominantly ablative, whereas vein-preserving approaches remain limited.Case SummaryA 60-year-old man with left lower extremity varicose veins and pruritus underwent ultrasound-guided extraluminal circumferential hyaluronic acid injection around the incompetent terminal valve at the left saphenofemoral junction after duplex ultrasound confirmed primary superficial venous valve incompetence. Immediate and early follow-up duplex ultrasound showed abolition of reflux, with brief reflux <0.5 seconds at 3 months and recurrent reflux >0.5 seconds at 12 months. No procedure-related complications occurred.DiscussionThis case documents immediate and stage-dependent changes in venous valve behavior after a vein-preserving extraluminal intervention and highlights the role of duplex ultrasound in guidance and serial assessment.Take-Home MessagesExtraluminal intervention may induce short-term changes in reflux without vein ablation. Durability and clinical significance require further study. History of present illness

A 60-year-old man presented with a 4-year history of left lower extremity varicose veins associated with pruritus and mild discomfort. Pruritus was the predominant symptom and had gradually worsened over time. The patient reported no prior history of lower extremity venous interventions or surgical treatment.

Past medical history and general information

The patient reported no history of hypertension, diabetes mellitus, or coronary artery disease. He denied smoking and alcohol consumption and had no known history of deep vein thrombosis, medication or food allergies, or long-term use of anticoagulant therapy. He had been engaged in an occupation involving prolonged standing for many years.

On presentation, his height was 174 cm and weight was 75 kg. Vital signs were as follows: blood pressure 129/82 mm Hg, heart rate 96 beats/min, and body temperature 36.4 °C.

Physical examination and initial assessment

Physical examination revealed superficial varicose veins in both lower extremities, predominantly affecting the left side, with associated skin hyperpigmentation and eczematous changes. There were no signs of acute inflammation or tenderness.

The initial clinical assessment was consistent with chronic venous disease. The Clinical-Etiology-Anatomy-Pathophysiology classification was C4EpAs3Pr. The modified Venous Clinical Severity Score was 5, the Aberdeen Varicose Vein Questionnaire score was 4, and the Numerical Rating Scale for pain was 0.

Differential diagnosis

Based on the clinical presentation and duplex ultrasound findings, the differential diagnoses included primary superficial venous valve incompetence, secondary venous reflux related to deep venous system pathology (including postthrombotic syndrome), and nonvenous causes of lower extremity edema, such as cardiac- or lymphatic-related conditions. Duplex ultrasound demonstrated patency of the deep venous system without evidence of thrombosis or significant reflux, supporting a diagnosis of primary superficial venous valve incompetence.

Investigations

Lower extremity venous hemodynamics were assessed using duplex ultrasound according to a standardized protocol. Examinations were performed in the upright, non–weight-bearing position. Venous reflux was defined as retrograde flow lasting >0.5 seconds following distal compression and release.

Duplex ultrasound demonstrated incompetence of the terminal valve at the left saphenofemoral junction, with marked reflux lasting approximately 13 seconds and associated tributary varicosities (Figures 1 and 2). The deep venous system was patent, with no evidence of thrombosis or significant reflux.

Management

After completion of preprocedural evaluation and exclusion of relevant contraindications, an ultrasound-guided extraluminal venous valve functional remodeling intervention was performed with the patient awake under local anesthesia.

Throughout the procedure, real-time ultrasound guidance was used to precisely localize the incompetent terminal valve at the left saphenofemoral junction. Hyaluronic acid filler was injected circumferentially at multiple points into the extraluminal space surrounding the valve, with a total injected volume of approximately 8 mL.

Immediately after the intervention, duplex ultrasound assessment was performed. The terminal valve at the left saphenofemoral junction was clearly visualized, with observable changes in valve opening and closure, and no reflux was detected at that time. Antegrade flow through the vein was preserved.

The patient ambulated immediately after the procedure and was discharged after a brief observation period without reporting significant discomfort. Standard postoperative compression therapy was prescribed, with the use of class II graduated compression stockings to support venous return. A structured follow-up plan was established, including outpatient visits and duplex ultrasound examinations at 1 week and at 1, 3, and 12 months after the intervention to document clinical symptoms and venous valve hemodynamics.

Follow-up and outcomes

At 1-week follow-up, duplex ultrasound demonstrated no detectable reflux at the terminal valve of the left saphenofemoral junction (Figures 3 and 4). At that time, the Venous Clinical Severity Score was 3, the Aberdeen Varicose Vein Questionnaire score was 4, and the Numerical Rating Scale score was 0.

At 1-month follow-up, duplex ultrasound continued to show no reflux at the terminal valve (Figures 5 and 6). The Venous Clinical Severity Score was 4, the Aberdeen Varicose Vein Questionnaire score was 5, and the Numerical Rating Scale score remained 0.

At 3-month follow-up, duplex ultrasound demonstrated transient reflux at the terminal valve, with a reflux duration of <0.5 seconds (Figures 7 and 8). The Venous Clinical Severity Score was 4, the Aberdeen Varicose Vein Questionnaire score was 5, and the Numerical Rating Scale score remained 0.

At 12-month follow-up, reflux at the terminal valve was again observed on duplex ultrasound, with a duration of >0.5 seconds (Figures 9 and 10). At that time, the Venous Clinical Severity Score was 2, the Aberdeen Varicose Vein Questionnaire score was 2, and the Numerical Rating Scale score remained 0.

No procedure-related complications or adverse events were observed during the follow-up period.

Discussion

This case documents immediate and time-dependent changes in the dynamics of the terminal valve at the saphenofemoral junction following an ultrasound-guided extraluminal venous valve functional remodeling intervention, as observed during postprocedural assessment and follow-up. Duplex ultrasound enabled dynamic visualization of valve behavior without vein closure or destruction.

In the context of current guideline-recommended, vein-ablative strategies for the treatment of lower extremity varicose veins, most interventional approaches focus on symptom relief through closure of refluxing target veins.1,2 Although effective, these strategies are inherently vein-destructive, and preservation of the great saphenous vein—an important autologous conduit—may still be desirable in selected patients.3 Against this background, an alternative exploratory concept has emerged that emphasizes modulation of venous valve function rather than vein closure. Previous studies on external valvuloplasty reflect this conceptual approach by demonstrating that extraluminal structural support may influence leaflet coaptation and valve behavior.4 The immediate and stage-dependent changes in saphenofemoral terminal valve dynamics observed in this case can be interpreted within this conceptual framework of extraluminal support, valve function modulation, and vein preservation.

It is important to note that, unlike traditional external valvuloplasty techniques involving surgical implantation of permanent external devices,5, 6, 7, 8 the hyaluronic acid filler used in this case is characterized by favorable biocompatibility and biodegradability, thereby minimizing long-term structural risk in an exploratory setting.9 The reappearance of reflux during follow-up may be related to gradual material resorption and attenuation of extraluminal support and should be interpreted as a stage-dependent feature of an exploratory intervention rather than procedural failure. Furthermore, this type of extraluminal intervention may involve a learning curve, with factors such as injection strategy, volume distribution, and procedural precision potentially influencing the duration of observed functional changes. In this context, the short-term valve dynamics observed in this case are more appropriately viewed as reversible modulation rather than durable remodeling.

This report describes a single case with a limited duration of follow-up and provides descriptive imaging-based observations only. As such, the findings are insufficient to support conclusions regarding long-term efficacy. Future studies conducted under controlled conditions, incorporating materials with different degradation profiles and standardized procedural protocols, are warranted to further elucidate the potential effects of extraluminal interventions on venous valve dynamics and their clinical relevance.

Equipment and Materials

Ultrasound system

A Siemens ACUSON Sequoia ultrasound system (Siemens Healthineers) equipped with a 10L4 linear-array high-frequency transducer (frequency range, 4-10 MHz) was used. The system enabled real-time duplex ultrasound imaging for procedural localization and guidance, as well as preprocedural and postprocedural assessment of venous valve structure and hemodynamics.

Injectable material

A modified sodium hyaluronate gel (Singderm, Hangzhou Xiehe Pharmaceutical Co, Ltd; product number: 06936020106320; batch number: H230329A11A; concentration: 24 mg/mL) was used for ultrasound-guided, circumferential, multipoint extraluminal injection around the terminal valve of the great saphenous vein.

Construction of weak solutions to the equations of a compressible viscous model
Nilasis Chaudhuri, Piotr B. Mucha, Milan Pokorný

Abstract
The paper aims on the construction of weak solutions to equations of a model of compressible viscous fluids, being a simplification of the classical compressible Navier-Stokes system. We present a novel scheme for approximating systems that preserves structural integrity by avoiding classical regularization with − ϵ ​ 𝚫 ​ ϱ {\bf{-\epsilon\Delta\varrho}} , thus maintaining the transport character of the continuity equation. Our approach, which necessitates specific conditions on the constitutive equation, accommodates physically relevant models such as isentropic and van der Waals gases, and globally handles non-monotone pressures. From an analytical perspective, our method synthesizes techniques from Feireisl–Lions and Bresch–Jabin to demonstrate the convergence of approximate densities using compensated compactness techniques. We also apply renormalization of the continuity equations and utilize weight techniques to manage unfavorable terms.

Introduction

The construction of weak solutions to systems of partial differential equations describing the flow of compressible viscous fluids is a well-established topic in the classical theory of mathematical fluid mechanics. Over the last three decades, significant progress has been made, particularly through the pioneering ideas of P. L. Lions
[
19
]
and E. Feireisl
[
14
]
. More recently, D. Bresch and P.-E. Jabin
[
2
]
have introduced innovative compactness techniques that have rejuvenated the theory.

However, there is a consensus that the current state of the art for weak solutions to the compressible Navier–Stokes system remains unsatisfactory. The core challenge is to identify an approximate system for the original equations in which the existence of suitably regular solutions is guaranteed. These approximate solutions must adhere to energy-type estimates that align with standard kinetic and potential energy. The main difficulty lies in passing to the limit with these approximate solutions. Given that we only have uniform bounds in
L^{p}
spaces for the densities, we achieve merely weak convergence. Due to the presence of nonlinear terms, this weak convergence is insufficient to characterize the limit. Therefore, by utilizing the toolbox of compensated compactness techniques, the objective is to obtain strong or pointwise convergence rather than just weak convergence. While simple in its statement, this aim poses a significant challenge.

A common theme in all the aforementioned seminal results is the approximation of the compressible Navier–Stokes system through the parabolic/viscous regularization of the continuity equation:

\begin{array}[]{l}\varrho_{t}+\operatorname{div}(\varrho{\bf u}){{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\bf-\epsilon\Delta\varrho}}=0\\
(\varrho{\bf u})_{t}+\operatorname{div}(\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u})-\operatorname{div}\mathbf{S}+\nabla\pi(\varrho)=0.\end{array}

(1)

In (
1
),
\varrho
and
{\bf u}
stand for the density and velocity of the fluid, respectively;
\pi(\varrho)
represents the pressure, and
\mathbf{S}
is the viscous stress tensor, which typically depends linearly on
\nabla{\bf u}
.

This strong regularization of the continuity equation introduces pronounced parabolicity to the approximation but compromises the transport character of the first equation. To be precise, the system requires further modification to feasibly achieve the stated goals. As
\varepsilon
approaches zero, we recover the original compressible Navier–Stokes system. However, establishing this rigorously within the mathematical framework is a challenging and intricate process. Additionally, to obtain a suitable energy inequality, we must modify the momentum equation.

The presence of
{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\bf-\epsilon\Delta\varrho}}
introduces additional challenges, particularly regarding the required integrability of the pressure. However, techniques developed over the past years have successfully addressed these issues for the classical system (see
[
17
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
24
]
). For more complex systems, such as multi-fluid models, constructing such approximations is considerably more challenging, as noted by Bresch, Mucha, and Zatorska
[
5
]
and Novotný and Pokorný
[
23
]
. Note that another approach, based on construction of approximate solutions via a numerical method was addressed in
[
15
]
.

This motivates the goal of our manuscript: to find an alternative method for constructing systems of the form (
1
). Since our focus is on methods related to the convergence of the density, we simplify the Navier–Stokes system by considering a version of the active scalar system, described as follows:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varrho+\operatorname{div}(\varrho{\bf u})=0,

(2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\bf u}+\nabla\pi(\varrho)=\Delta{\bf u},

(3)

where
\varrho
is the density,
{\bf u}
is the velocity and
\pi
is the pressure. The system can be considered in the time interval
(0,T)
and in the spatial variable in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, in the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
or in a bounded domain with some boundary conditions. To simplify the presentation, we stay in the torus and fix
d=3
.

We introduce a novel scheme for the approximate system that preserves the system’s structure. Specifically, we avoid the classical regularization by
{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\bf-\epsilon\Delta\varrho}}
, maintaining the transport equation for the density. This approach requires specific conditions on the constitutive equation while preserving physically relevant models, such as isentropic and van der Waals gases. In practice, this method accommodates non-monotone pressures.

From an analytical perspective, our approach synthesizes the techniques of Feireisl–Lions and Bresch–Jabin, demonstrating the convergence of approximate densities using the toolbox of compensated compactness. Our novel technique relies on the renormalization of the continuity equations, with the final step employing weight techniques to eliminate terms with unfavorable signs.

We aim to make our paper self-contained, providing comprehensive proofs and frequently referencing classical methods, such as those based on the oscillation defect measure or the Kolmogorov convergence criterion.

Assumptions, Main results and Idea of the proof

Recall we consider the simplified problem (
2
)–(
3
) in
(0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}
equipped with the corresponding initial conditions for the functions
{\bf u}
and
\varrho
. Moreover, we need to specify the pressure function
\pi(\varrho)
.
The goal is to consider the most possible general pressure law.
We take a smooth non-negative function
\pi\in C^{1}([0,\infty))\cap C^{2}((0,\infty))
such that
\pi(\varrho)>0
for
\varrho>0
and

\pi(0)=0\text{ \ \ and \ \ }a_{2}\varrho^{\gamma}-C\leq\pi(\varrho)\leq C+a_{1}\varrho^{\gamma}\mbox{ \ \ with \ \ }\gamma>1,\;a_{1},a_{2},C>0.

(4)

Additionally, we require

|\pi^{\prime}(\varrho)|\leq C\varrho^{\gamma-1},\qquad|\pi^{\prime\prime}(\varrho)|\leq C\varrho^{\gamma-2}\mbox{ \ \ for \ \ }\varrho>1.

(5)

In this case, we define the pressure potential as
\displaystyle\Pi(\varrho)=\varrho\int_{1}^{\varrho}\frac{\pi(\xi)}{\xi^{2}}\text{d}\xi
.

Within our analysis we use more specified forms of
\pi
. Typically, we consider
\pi(\varrho)\sim p(\varrho)+perturbation
, where
p(\varrho)
is expected to have fine properties like the most classical case
\varrho^{\gamma}
.

Moreover, for a.e.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left(\frac{1}{2}|{\bf u}|^{2}+\Pi(\varrho)\right)(\tau)\;{\rm d}{x}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla{\bf u}|^{2}\;{\rm d}{x}\;{\rm d}t\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left(\frac{1}{2}|{\bf u}_{0}|^{2}+\Pi(\varrho_{0})\right)\;{\rm d}{x}.

The study of global-in-time solutions to the compressible Navier–Stokes system began relatively late. We should mention the significant impact of Matsumura and Nishida
[
20
]
, though it was under the restrictive assumption of smallness of the data. An improvement concerning the regularity of the initial data was made in
[
7
]
,
[
21
]
,
[
25
]
.

Since the existence of solutions for small data is not entirely satisfactory, the goal is to define a global-in-time weak solution for the compressible Navier–Stokes equation with large initial data, inspired by the weak solutions for the incompressible case
à la Leray
. In this context, it is worth mentioning Hoff’s result
[
18
]
, which addresses the existence theory for a discontinuous initial data. The existence of weak solutions was later established by Lions
[
19
]
and, almost simultaneously, by Feireisl
[
14
]
, who expanded the range of
\gamma
. Subsequently, the existence theory for weak solutions was extended to heat-conducting fluids
[
14
]
. These techniques have been modified, slightly improved, and applied to various generalizations
[
17
]
,
[
16
]
, but the core method remains unchanged.

Recently, Bresch and Jabin introduced a new perspective by proposing a method to demonstrate the compactness of the density using the Kolmogorov criterion directly. Essentially, they showed that for an approximate sequence of densities
\{\varrho^{(n)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, one can establish that

\lim_{|h|\to 0}\sup_{n}\|\varrho^{(n)}(\cdot+h,t)-\varrho^{(n)}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}}\to 0.

(10)

This method has found interesting applications in models based on the compressible Navier–Stokes equation (see, e.g.,
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
26
]
), offering in some cases a clearer understanding of the compactness properties of the studied systems.

However, the first step of the construction, namely the existence of the initial approximate system, remains unsatisfactory. An attempt in this direction was made in
[
6
]
, but describing such an approach as ‘simple’ is difficult.

The aim of this paper is to propose a new technique for compactness and the construction of approximate solutions, based on a modification of the Feireisl–Lions technique combined with suitable weights from the Bresch–Jabin approach. The approximate system takes the form described in
[
6
]
. We focus our analysis on the simplified system (
2
)–(
3
), but all the steps can be applied to the full approximation of the compressible Navier–Stokes system, given by the following form:

\begin{array}[]{l}\varrho_{t}+\operatorname{div}([{\bf u}]_{\epsilon}\varrho)=0,\\[4.0pt]
\partial_{t}((l+[\varrho]_{l}){\bf u})+\operatorname{div}([\varrho{\bf u}]_{l}\otimes{\bf u})-\operatorname{div}\mathbf{S}+\nabla[p(\varrho)]_{\epsilon}=0,\end{array}

(11)

where
[f]_{l}=\kappa_{l}\ast f
is the standard mollification by convolution with a smooth positive kernel
\kappa_{l}
that tends to the Dirac delta and
\epsilon,l>0
. We omit the analysis of (
11
), as the main mathematical challenges related to (
11
) appear to be the same as for the model case (
2
)–(

).

2.3

The idea of the proof of Theorem
1

Large
\Gamma
(in our case,
\Gamma>3
) removes the problems with insufficient integrability of the density, but also
gives more fine features for large
\varrho
. Namely, in that case, all pressures of type (
7
) can be written in the following very practical way

\pi_{\mu}(\varrho)=\mathsf{q}(\varrho)+p_{\mu}(\varrho),

where
\mathsf{q}
is compactly supported function and
p_{\mu}(\varrho),\;p_{\mu}^{\prime}(\varrho),\;p_{\mu}^{\prime\prime}(\varrho)\geq 0\text{ for }\varrho\geq 0
with suitable growth conditions.
For the pressure
\pi_{\mu}
, corresponding pressure potential
\Pi_{\mu}
, defined by
\displaystyle\Pi_{\mu}(\varrho)=\varrho\int_{1}^{\varrho}\frac{\pi_{\mu}(\xi)}{\xi^{2}}\text{d}\xi
, can be written as

\displaystyle{\Pi}_{\mu}(\varrho)=P_{\mu}(\varrho)+\mathsf{Q}(\varrho)\text{ with }P_{\mu}^{\prime\prime\prime}(\varrho),\;P_{\mu}^{\prime\prime}(\varrho),\;P_{\mu}^{\prime}(\varrho)\geq 0\text{ for }\varrho\geq 0\text{ and }\mathsf{Q}\in C_{c}^{1}[0,\infty)\cap C^{3}[0,\infty).

(12)

The features of the above splitting yields the following important and useful property: there exists
a>0
such that

P_{\mu}(\varrho)\pm ap_{\mu}(\varrho)\mbox{ is convex. \ }

(13)

We will state an exact formulation of
P_{\mu}
in Lemma
5
. We now briefly describe the main steps of the proof of Theorem
1
.

-

Approximate problem: We consider the following approximate system

\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\varrho+\operatorname{div}(\varrho[{\bf u}]_{\varepsilon})+\delta\varrho^{m}=0,\\
\partial_{t}{\bf u}-\Delta{\bf u}=-\nabla[\pi_{\mu}(\varrho)]_{\varepsilon},\end{array}

where
m>0
is sufficiently large,
\delta>0
,
\displaystyle[f]_{{\varepsilon}}=\kappa_{\varepsilon}\ast f
and
\displaystyle[\bf{f}]_{{\varepsilon}}=\kappa_{\varepsilon}\ast\bf{f}
, where
\kappa_{\varepsilon}
is the standard mollifier in space as we consider the domain as the case of the torus. The system is accompanied with the initial conditions
(\varrho^{0}_{\mu},{\bf u}^{0}_{\mu})
from (
8
). In Section
3
, we describe this approximation.

The choice of large
m
gives us sufficient integrability so that we can proceed with the limit passage
\varepsilon\to 0
. In particular we need to have square integrability of the pressure at this first stage of the approximation. Even in this simplified model the uphill task is to prove the strong convergence of the density, which is crucial to identify the weak limit of the nonlinear composition of the density, in our case the pressure term, i.e.,
\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\pi_{\mu}(\varrho_{\varepsilon})=\pi_{\mu}(\varrho)
, at least in some weak sense.

-

Continuity and renormalized continuity equation: With the sufficient regularity of
(\varrho,{\bf u})
we obtain the following form of the limit continuity equation:

\partial_{t}\varrho+\operatorname{div}(\varrho{\bf u})+\delta\overline{\varrho^{m}}=0.

Recall that we use the standard notation
\overline{f(\varrho)}
as a weak limit of sequence
f(\varrho_{\varepsilon})
for a weakly convergent sequence
\varrho_{\varepsilon}
in suitable spaces.
By the classical method of DiPerna and Lions
[
10
]
, we know that the continuity equation can be viewed in the renormalized sense, i.e.,

\partial_{t}P_{\mu}(\varrho)+\operatorname{div}(P_{\mu}(\varrho){\bf u})+p_{\mu}(\varrho)\operatorname{div}{\bf u}+\delta\overline{\varrho^{m}}P^{\prime}_{\mu}(\varrho)=0.

On the other hand, the direct limit passage in the approximate renormalized continuity equation yields

\partial_{t}\overline{P_{\mu}(\varrho)}+\operatorname{div}(\overline{P_{\mu}(\varrho)}{\bf u})+\overline{\overline{p_{\mu}(\varrho)\operatorname{div}{\bf u}}}+\delta\overline{\varrho^{m}P^{\prime}_{\mu}(\varrho)}=0,

where the notation
\overline{\overline{f}}
is introduced to distinguish the possibility of a different limit because of mollification of velocity in the continuity equation.
Therefore, we obtain

\partial_{t}(\overline{P_{\mu}(\varrho)}-P_{\mu}(\varrho))+\operatorname{div}((\overline{P_{\mu}(\varrho)}-P_{\mu}(\varrho)){\bf u})+\overline{\overline{p_{\mu}(\varrho)\operatorname{div}{\bf u}}}-p_{\mu}(\varrho)\operatorname{div}{\bf u}+\delta(\overline{\varrho^{m}P^{\prime}_{\mu}(\varrho)}-\overline{\varrho^{m}}P^{\prime}_{\mu}(\varrho))=0.

(14)

From the properties of
P_{\mu}
as described in (
12
), we notice

\displaystyle\overline{P_{\mu}(\varrho)}-P_{\mu}(\varrho)\geq 0\text{ \ \ and \ \ }\overline{\varrho^{m}P^{\prime}_{\mu}(\varrho)}-\overline{\varrho^{m}}P^{\prime}_{\mu}(\varrho)\geq 0.

Therefore, the key term here to analyse is the term

\displaystyle\overline{\overline{p_{\mu}(\varrho)\operatorname{div}{\bf u}}}-p_{\mu}(\varrho)\operatorname{div}{\bf u}.

(15)

In this regard we need to understand the role played by an important quantity, the
effective viscous flux
.

-

Effective viscous flux: From the continuity and velocity equation (
2
)–(
3
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\operatorname{div}{\bf u}+\Delta\left(\pi(\varrho)-\operatorname{div}{\bf u}\right)=0\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\text{ which can be stated in the following form }\\
&\partial_{t}\left(\operatorname{div}{\bf u}-\pi(\varrho)\right)-\Delta{\left(\operatorname{div}{\bf u}-\pi(\varrho)\right)}=\operatorname{div}(\pi(\varrho){\bf u})+\left(\varrho\pi^{\prime}(\varrho)-\pi(\varrho)\right)\operatorname{div}{\bf u}.\end{split}

(16)

The effective viscous flux
\mathsf{G}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathsf{G}=\operatorname{div}{\bf u}-\pi(\varrho).

(17)

Keeping in mind our approximate system, we need to adapt
\mathsf{G}
suitably. For
\varepsilon>0
, we consider

G_{\varepsilon}=\operatorname{div}{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}-[\pi_{\mu}(\varrho_{\varepsilon})]_{\varepsilon}.

At least on the formal level, from the equations for the effective viscous flux (
16
), we observe that with sufficient regularity of density and velocity, we will be able to obtain compactness (strong convergence) of this quantity
G_{\varepsilon}
. It plays a very important role in the analysis and it is explained in details in the proof of Lemma
10
.

-

Use of compactness of effective viscous flux in (
15
): We assume the strong convergence of
G_{\varepsilon}
to
G
. Note that at this level
G\left(=\operatorname{div}{\bf u}-\overline{\pi_{\mu}(\varrho)}\right)
might not be equal to
\operatorname{div}{\bf u}-\pi_{\mu}(\varrho)
. Let us give a closer look at the quantity (
15
) and rewrite it as

\displaystyle\overline{\overline{p_{\mu}(\varrho)\operatorname{div}{\bf u}}}-p_{\mu}(\varrho)\operatorname{div}{\bf u}

\displaystyle=\overline{\overline{p_{\mu}(\varrho)p_{\mu}(\varrho)}}-p_{\mu}(\varrho)\overline{p_{\mu}(\varrho)}+\overline{\overline{p_{\mu}(\varrho)\mathsf{q}(\varrho)}}-p_{\mu}(\varrho)\overline{\mathsf{q}(\varrho)}+(\overline{p_{\mu}(\varrho)}-p_{\mu}(\varrho))G

\displaystyle=\!\left(\overline{\overline{p_{\mu}(\varrho)p_{\mu}(\varrho)}}-\overline{p_{\mu}(\varrho)}^{2}\right)\!\!+\!{\left(\overline{p_{\mu}(\varrho)}\!-\!p_{\mu}(\varrho)\right)\overline{p_{\mu}(\varrho)}}\!+\!\left(\overline{\overline{p_{\mu}(\varrho)\mathsf{q}(\varrho)}}-p_{\mu}(\varrho)\overline{\mathsf{q}(\varrho)}\right)\!+\!\left((\overline{p_{\mu}(\varrho)}-p_{\mu}(\varrho))G\right)\!.

The first two terms in the last line of the above equation are non-negative. Also, the last one is of a good form as it depends on the properties of the effective viscous flux
G
. Hence, we need to look closer at the terms with
\mathsf{q}
. So in the weak/distributional sense we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left([p_{\mu}(\varrho_{\varepsilon})]_{\varepsilon}[\mathsf{q}(\varrho_{\varepsilon})]_{\varepsilon}-p_{\mu}(\varrho)\mathsf{q}(\varrho_{\varepsilon})\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left(p_{\mu}(\varrho_{\varepsilon})[[\mathsf{q}(\varrho_{\varepsilon})]_{\varepsilon}]_{\varepsilon}-p_{\mu}(\varrho)[[\mathsf{q}(\varrho_{\varepsilon})]_{\varepsilon}]_{\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left((p_{\mu}(\varrho_{\varepsilon})-p_{\mu}(\varrho))[[\mathsf{q}(\varrho_{\varepsilon})]_{\varepsilon}]_{\varepsilon}\right).

Since
\mathsf{q}
is compactly supported, the sequence
\displaystyle[[\mathsf{q}(\varrho_{\varepsilon})]_{\varepsilon}]_{\varepsilon}
is uniformly bounded. Also, from (
13
), we deduce (see (
54
)) the relation between
p_{\mu}
and
P_{\mu}
and it yields

|\overline{\overline{p_{\mu}(\varrho)\mathsf{q}(\varrho)}}-p_{\mu}(\varrho)\overline{\mathsf{q}(\varrho)}|\leq C(\overline{P_{\mu}(\varrho)}-P_{\mu}(\varrho)).

Thus plugging the expression of
\displaystyle\left(\overline{\overline{p_{\mu}(\varrho)\operatorname{div}{\bf u}}}-p_{\mu}(\varrho)\operatorname{div}{\bf u}\right)
into (
14
), it yields

\partial_{t}(\overline{P_{\mu}(\varrho)}-P_{\mu}(\varrho))+\operatorname{div}((\overline{P_{\mu}(\varrho)}-P_{\mu}(\varrho)){\bf u})\leq C(\lambda+|G|)(\overline{P_{\mu}(\varrho)}-P_{\mu}(\varrho))

or the integrated form

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left(\overline{P_{\mu}(\varrho)}-P_{\mu}(\varrho)\right){\rm d}{x}\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}C(\lambda+|G|)\left(\overline{P_{\mu}(\varrho)}-P_{\mu}(\varrho)\right){\rm d}{x}.

Unfortunately, to use the desired Grönwall’s inequality, we need the function
G
to be point-wise bounded. In our setup, we will be unable to derive the uniform
L^{\infty}
bound of the limit effective viscous flux
G
.
The above inequality seems to be useless. So we need to cheat here a little.

-

Weighted inequality: We take a weight
w
such that it is a solution to the problem

3.2.4

2.

Furthermore, we have

(47)

\int_{(0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}}(\overline{{P_{\mu}}(\varrho)}-{P_{\mu}}(\varrho))\ {\rm d}{x}\ {\rm d}t\leq\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{w>\eta}(\overline{{P_{\mu}}(\varrho)}-{P_{\mu}}(\varrho))w\ {\rm d}{x}\ {\rm d}t+\int_{w\leq\eta}\overline{{P_{\mu}}(\varrho)}\ {\rm d}{x}\ {\rm d}t.

The first term disappears by (
57
) (recall that
(\overline{P_{\mu}(\varrho)}-P_{\mu}(\varrho))w\geq 0
a.e.), and the second one we treat as follows

\int_{w\leq\eta}\overline{{P_{\mu}}(\varrho)}\ {\rm d}{x}\ {\rm d}t=\int_{w\leq\eta}(\overline{{P_{\mu}}_{M}(\varrho)}+\overline{b_{M}(\varrho)})\ {\rm d}{x}\ {\rm d}t.

Note that the second term on the right-hand side is controlled by (
58
).
To control the first one we use (
59
) and (
47
). We have

\int_{w\leq\eta}\overline{{P_{\mu}}_{M}(\varrho)}\ {\rm d}{x}\ {\rm d}t\leq\frac{1}{|\ln\eta|}\Phi(M)\int_{w\leq\eta}\varrho|\ln w|\ {\rm d}{x}\ {\rm d}t\leq\frac{C(M,E_{0,\mu})}{|\ln\eta|}.

Therefore, summing up we get

\int_{(0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}}(\overline{P_{\mu}(\varrho)}-P_{\mu}(\varrho))\;{\rm d}{x}\;{\rm d}t\leq CM^{-1}+\frac{C(M,E_{0,\mu})}{|\ln\eta|}.

So taking large
M
, the first term is small and then taking
\eta
sufficiently small we bound the second one. Hence for any
\epsilon>0
we have

\int_{(0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}}(\overline{P_{\mu}(\varrho)}-P_{\mu}(\varrho))\;{\rm d}{x}\;{\rm d}t\leq\epsilon.

Since the integrand is non-negative, we get the desired identity

\overline{P_{\mu}(\varrho)}=P_{\mu}(\varrho)\mbox{ \ a.e. }(t,x)\in{(0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}}.

Therefore, we conclude the first part of proof of Theorem
1
concerning the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
.

3.3.3

Strong convergence of density

The identification
\overline{\pi(\varrho)}=\pi(\varrho)
follows from the strong convergence of density. This holds provided we show

\displaystyle\overline{\varrho\log\varrho}=\varrho\log\varrho\qquad\text{a.e.}

Using the renormalized form of the continuity equation for the limit problem as well as passing to the limit
\delta\to 0
in the renormalized form on the
\delta>0
level with the function
b(z)=z\log z
we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}\left(\overline{\varrho\log\varrho}-\varrho\log\varrho\right)+\operatorname{div}\left(\left(\overline{\varrho\log\varrho}-\varrho\log\varrho\right){\bf u}\right)+\left(\overline{\varrho\operatorname{div}{\bf u}}-\varrho\operatorname{div}{\bf u}\right)=0.\end{split}

Note that
\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\delta\varrho_{\delta}b^{\prime}(\varrho_{\delta})\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=0
.
Using the effective viscous flux equation we end up with

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}\left(\overline{\varrho\log\varrho}-\varrho\log\varrho\right)+\operatorname{div}\left(\left(\overline{\varrho\log\varrho}-\varrho\log\varrho\right){\bf u}\right)+\left(\overline{\varrho G}-\varrho G\right)+\left(\overline{\varrho\pi_{\mu}(\varrho)}-\varrho\overline{\pi_{\mu}(\varrho)}\right)=0.\end{split}

(63)

Recall that
\displaystyle G_{\delta}=\operatorname{div}{\bf u}_{\delta}-\pi_{\mu}(\varrho_{\delta})
,
\displaystyle G=\operatorname{div}{\bf u}-\overline{\pi_{\mu}(\varrho)}
and we have
\varrho_{\delta}G_{\delta}\rightarrow\overline{\varrho G}
weakly in
L^{1+\lambda}
for some
\lambda>0
.

We show below that

\overline{\varrho G}=\varrho G.

First, from the uniform bound
\|G_{\delta}\|_{L^{1{+}\frac{\alpha}{\Gamma}}_{t,x}}\leq C
we observe that

G_{\delta}\rightharpoonup G\text{ in }L^{1{+}\frac{\alpha}{\Gamma}}_{t,x}
.
Note that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}G_{\delta}\;\varrho_{\delta}\psi\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=-\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\partial_{t}(-\Delta)^{-1}\operatorname{div}{\bf u}_{\delta}\varrho_{\delta}\psi\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.

Now, we introduce the space regularization of
{\bf u}_{\delta}
and denote it by
[{\bf u}_{\delta}]_{\eta}
. We introduce the notation

{{\bf v}_{\delta}}_{\eta}={\bf u}_{\delta}-[{\bf u}_{\delta}]_{\eta}\mbox{ \ \ and \ \ }\|{{\bf v}_{\delta}}_{\eta}\|_{L^{r}_{t,x}}=o(1),\quad\eta\to 0\text{ for }r<\frac{10}{3}.

Above, the notation
o(1)
for
\eta\to 0
means that the quantity goes to zero as
\eta
goes to zero.
Therefore, we write

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\partial_{t}{(-\Delta)}^{-1}\operatorname{div}{\bf u}_{\delta}\;\varrho_{\delta}\;\psi\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\partial_{t}{(-\Delta)}^{-1}\operatorname{div}[{\bf u}_{\delta}]_{\eta}\;\varrho_{\delta}\;\psi\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\partial_{t}{(-\Delta)}^{-1}\operatorname{div}{{\bf v}_{\delta}}_{\eta}\;\varrho_{\delta}\;\psi\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=A_{1}+A_{2}.

The strong convergence of
{\bf u}_{\delta}
in
L^{2}_{t,x}
and weak convergence of
\varrho_{\delta}
in
L^{\Gamma+\alpha}_{t,x}
yield

\displaystyle A_{1}\rightarrow\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\partial_{t}{(-\Delta)}^{-1}\operatorname{div}[{\bf u}]_{\eta}\;\varrho\;\psi\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.

On the other hand, from the uniform bound of
G_{\delta}
and

\partial_{t}(-\Delta)^{-1}\operatorname{div}[{\bf u}]_{\eta}=-[G]_{\eta}
,
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\partial_{t}(-\Delta)^{-1}\operatorname{div}[{\bf u}]_{\eta}\varrho\;\psi\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=-\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}[G]_{\eta}\varrho\;\psi\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=-\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}G\varrho\;\psi\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+o(1),\quad\eta\to 0.

For the term
A_{2}
we write the equation for
(\varrho_{\delta}\psi)
as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho_{\delta}\psi)+\operatorname{div}\left(\varrho_{\delta}{\bf u}_{\delta}\psi\right)=-\delta\varrho_{\delta}^{m}\psi+\varrho_{\delta}(\partial_{t}\psi+{\bf u}_{\delta}\cdot\nabla\psi).

This gives

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}(\varrho_{\delta}\psi)\|_{L^{\frac{7}{6}}_{t}W^{-1,\frac{7}{6}}_{x}}\leq C(\|\psi\|_{C^{1}_{t,x}}),

provided
\Gamma
is sufficiently large (
\Gamma\geq\frac{13}{7}
is sufficient, as we need
\frac{7}{6}m\leq\Gamma+m-1
).

We also have

\|\varrho_{\delta}\|_{L^{\Gamma+\alpha}_{t,x}}\leq C
, see Appendix
A.3
. Therefore we use the interpolation inequality (cf.
[
6
, Lemma 15]
, we also recall it in Lemma
16
) with
{{\bf v}_{\delta}}_{\eta}\in L^{\frac{10}{3}-\lambda}
,
\lambda>0
, small, and
\partial_{t}{{\bf v}_{\delta}}_{\eta}\in L^{1+\frac{\alpha}{\Gamma}}_{t}W^{-1,1+\frac{\alpha}{\Gamma}}_{x}
. From
\Gamma
large as above and
\alpha=\frac{13}{20}\Gamma-\frac{1}{20}
, bigger than 1, we notice

\frac{\Gamma}{\Gamma+\alpha}+\frac{1}{\Gamma+\alpha}<1\text{ \ \ and \ \ }\frac{1}{\frac{10}{3}-\lambda}+\frac{6}{7}>1

and these yield

\displaystyle|A_{2}|\leq\|\varrho_{\delta}\|_{L^{\Gamma+\alpha}_{t,x}}^{a}\|\partial_{t}(\varrho_{\delta}\psi)\|_{L^{\frac{7}{6}}_{t}W^{-1,\frac{7}{6}}_{x}}^{1-a}\|{{\bf v}_{\delta}}_{\eta}\|_{L^{\frac{10}{3}-\lambda}}^{1-a}\|\partial_{t}{{\bf v}_{\delta}}_{\eta}\|_{L^{\frac{\Gamma+\alpha}{\Gamma}}_{t}W^{-1,\frac{\Gamma+\alpha}{\Gamma}}_{x}}^{a}\leq o(1)\mbox{ \ for $\eta\to 0$,}

\lambda
positive, small.
Hence, we conclude

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}G_{\delta}\varrho_{\delta}\psi\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\rightarrow\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}G\;\varrho\psi\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.

Now going back to (
63
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left(\overline{\varrho\log\varrho}-\varrho\log\varrho\right)\ {\rm d}{x}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left(\overline{\varrho p_{\mu}(\varrho)}-\varrho\overline{p_{\mu}(\varrho)}\right)\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=0.

(64)

We notice that
\left(\overline{\varrho\pi_{\mu}(\varrho)}-\varrho\overline{\pi_{\mu}(\varrho)}\right)=\left(\overline{\varrho p_{\mu}(\varrho)}-\varrho\overline{p_{\mu}(\varrho)}\right)+\left(\overline{\varrho\mathsf{q}(\varrho)}-\varrho\overline{\mathsf{q}(\varrho)}\right)
.

First, we observe
\displaystyle\left(\overline{\varrho p_{\mu}(\varrho)}-\varrho\overline{p_{\mu}(\varrho)}\right)\geq 0
.
For the term
\left(\overline{\varrho\mathsf{q}(\varrho)}-\varrho\overline{\mathsf{q}(\varrho)}\right)
we write

\displaystyle\left(\overline{\varrho\mathsf{q}(\varrho)}-\varrho\;\overline{\mathsf{q}(\varrho)}\right)=\left(\overline{\varrho\mathsf{q}(\varrho)}-\varrho\;{\mathsf{q}(\varrho)}\right)+\left(\overline{\varrho\mathsf{q}(\varrho)}-\varrho\;\overline{\mathsf{q}(\varrho)}\right).

Since
\mathsf{q}\in C_{c}^{2}[0,\infty)
, we fix
M>0
such that
\text{supp}\;\mathsf{q}\subset[0,M)
. Therefore, there exists a constant
\lambda_{\mathsf{q}}>0
that depends on
\mathsf{q}
such that

\displaystyle\lambda_{\mathsf{q}}\varrho\log\varrho+\varrho\mathsf{q}(\varrho),\quad\lambda_{\mathsf{q}}\varrho\log\varrho+\mathsf{q}(\varrho)\text{ \ \ and \ \ }\lambda_{\mathsf{q}}\varrho\log\varrho-\varrho\mathsf{q}(\varrho)\text{ \ \ are convex}.

The above follows from the fact that

\mathsf{q}\in C_{c}^{2}[0,\infty),\sup_{(m,M)}\left(|\mathsf{q}|+|\mathsf{q}^{\prime}|+|\mathsf{q}^{\prime\prime}|\right)<\infty.

This helps us to deduce

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left(\overline{\varrho\mathsf{q}(\varrho)}-\varrho\;\overline{\mathsf{q}(\varrho)}\right)\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\right|\leq C(\mathsf{q},\lambda_{\mathsf{q}})\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left(\overline{\varrho\log\varrho}-\varrho\log\varrho\right)(\tau,\cdot)\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.

Finally, we use the Grönwall’s argument in (
64
) to conclude

\overline{\varrho\log\varrho}=\varrho\log\varrho
.
Thus, up to a subsequence
\varrho
convergences point-wisely. Theorem
1
is proved.

The second one is for the non-monotone case with certain growth hypotheses. We take a smooth non-negative function

such that

for

and

p(0)=0\text{ \ \ and \ \ }a_{2}\varrho^{\gamma}-C\leq p(\varrho)\leq C+a_{1}\varrho^{\gamma}\mbox{ \ \ with \ \ }\gamma\geq 6/5,\;a_{1},a_{2},C>0.\qquad{{\bf\rm(Case\;2)}}

Additionally, we require

4.3

Strong convergence of density

4.3.1

Case 1: Pressure satisfying (
9
)

The identification
\overline{\pi(\varrho)}=\pi(\varrho)
follows from the strong convergence of density, specifically from
\displaystyle\overline{\varrho\log\varrho}=\varrho\log\varrho
. The key idea is based on the work of Feireisl
[
13
]
. Below, we provide a list of important propositions and statements used to prove the strong convergence of density.

•

Effective viscous flux identity:

Proposition 11

For suitable
b
(the main points are that the function cannot be too singular at zero and cannot grow too fast at infinity) we have that the following effective viscous flux identity holds in the weak sense:

\displaystyle\overline{\pi(\varrho)b(\varrho)}-\overline{\pi(\varrho)}\;\overline{b(\varrho)}=\overline{b(\varrho)\operatorname{div}{\bf u}}-\overline{b(\varrho)}\operatorname{div}{\bf u}.

The proof follows the same lines as in the case of compressible Navier–Stokes equations. However, since the momentum equation does not include the convective term and we have directly the strong convergence of the velocity, it is more direct as it does not require any compensated compactness tools.

•

Oscillation defect measure: We briefly recall this concept. First, we consider a smooth cut-off function
T
:

\displaystyle T(x)=\begin{cases}&x,\;0\leq x\leq 1\\
&\text{concave smooth},1\leq x\leq 3\\
&2,\;x\geq 3.\\
\end{cases}

(65)

Then we define “Truncation” functions:

T_{k}(x)=k\;T\left(\frac{x}{k}\right).

For
\alpha>0
, we define
Oscillation defect measure
as

\displaystyle\textbf{osc}_{\alpha}[\varrho_{\mu}\rightarrow\varrho]:=\sup_{k\geq 1}\left(\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|T_{k}(\varrho_{\mu})-T_{k}(\varrho)|^{\alpha}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\right).

•

Boundedness of oscillation defect measure:

Proposition 12

Suppose, the
renormalized continuity equation
and the
effective viscous flux
identity is true for approximating function
\{\varrho_{\mu},{\bf u}_{\mu}\}
, then for
\gamma>1
, we have

\displaystyle\textbf{osc}_{\gamma+1}[\varrho_{\mu}\rightarrow\varrho]<\infty.

The proof of the proposition follows directly from the Feireisl
[
13
]
. For the sake of completeness it can be found in the Appendix, Section
A.4
.

•

Renormalized continuity equation:

Proposition 13

Suppose, a sequence
\{\varrho_{\mu},{\bf u}_{\mu}\}
satisfies renormalized continuity equation and they have the following convergence:

\displaystyle\varrho_{\mu}\rightarrow\varrho\text{ weak-(*)ly in }L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\gamma}({\mathbb{T}^{3}})),

\displaystyle{\bf u}_{\mu}\rightarrow{\bf u}\text{ weakly in }L^{2}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{T}^{3}}),

\displaystyle\nabla{\bf u}_{\mu}\rightarrow\nabla{\bf u}\text{ weakly in }L^{2}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{T}^{3}}).

Then, if we assume they have finite oscillation defect measure, i.e.,

\displaystyle\textbf{osc}_{r}[\varrho_{\mu}\rightarrow\varrho]<\infty

for some
r>2
, then
(\varrho,{\bf u})
is also a solution of the renormalized continuity equation.

The proof of the Proposition (
13
) is exactly the same as in the case of the compressible Navier–Stokes equations and is available e.g. in Feireisl, Karper and Pokorný
[
15
]
. For the sake of completeness, it is presented in Appendix, Section
A.5
.

•

As a consequence of Propositions
12
and
13
, we state the desired strong convergence result:

Proposition 14

Suppose, a sequence
\{\varrho_{\mu},{\bf u}_{\mu}\}
satisfies renormalized continuity equation along with energy estimate, pressure estimate, and effective viscous flux identity. Then, we claim

\displaystyle\overline{\varrho\log\varrho}=\varrho\log\varrho.

As a consequence of it we have

\varrho_{\mu}\rightarrow\varrho\text{ in }L^{1}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{T}^{3}})\text{ as }\mu\rightarrow 0.

Proof:
Recall that our pressure has the form

\pi\approx\varrho^{\gamma}+\tilde{p}(\varrho)+\tilde{q}(\varrho),

where
q
has a compact support.

We consider

\displaystyle L_{k}(\varrho)=\varrho\int_{0}^{\varrho}\frac{T_{k}(\xi)}{\xi^{2}}\,\text{d}\xi.

It has the property

\displaystyle L_{k}^{\prime\prime}(\varrho)\geq 0,\text{ for }\varrho\geq 0,\qquad\varrho L_{k}^{\prime}(\varrho)-L_{k}(\varrho)=T_{k}(\varrho)\text{ for }\varrho\geq 0.

Moreover, we notice that
L_{k}(\varrho)\approx\varrho\log\varrho\text{ as }k\rightarrow\infty.

Seeing that

\displaystyle\frac{\text{d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}L_{k}(\varrho_{\mu})\,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}T_{k}(\varrho_{\mu})\operatorname{div}{\bf u}_{\mu}\,{\rm d}{x}=0\text{ for a.e. }t\in(0,T),

we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\text{d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\overline{L_{k}(\varrho)}\,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)\operatorname{div}{\bf u}}\,{\rm d}{x}=0\text{ for a.e. }t\in(0,T).

Now, from Proposition
13
we conclude that
(\varrho,{\bf u})
satisfies renormalized equation of continuity and it gives

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left(\overline{L_{k}(\varrho)}-L_{k}(\varrho)\right)(\tau,\cdot)\,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left(\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)\operatorname{div}{\bf u}}-{T_{k}(\varrho)}\operatorname{div}{\bf u}\right)\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t=0\text{ for a.e. }\tau\in(0,T).

Next, we easily see that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left(\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)\operatorname{div}{\bf u}}-{T_{k}(\varrho)}\operatorname{div}{\bf u}\right)\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left(\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)\operatorname{div}{\bf u}}-\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}\operatorname{div}{\bf u}\right)\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left(\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}-{T_{k}(\varrho)}\right)\operatorname{div}{\bf u}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.

Using the effective viscous flux identity

\displaystyle\overline{\pi(\varrho)T_{k}(\varrho)}-\overline{\pi(\varrho)}\;\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}=\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)\operatorname{div}{\bf u}}-\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}\operatorname{div}{\bf u},

we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left(\overline{L_{k}(\varrho)}-L_{k}(\varrho)\right)(\tau,\cdot)\,{\rm d}{x}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left(\overline{\pi(\varrho)T_{k}(\varrho)}-\overline{\pi(\varrho)}\;\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}\right)\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left(\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}-{T_{k}(\varrho)}\right)\operatorname{div}{\bf u}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.

(66)

First we notice that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left(\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}-{T_{k}(\varrho)}\right)\operatorname{div}{\bf u}\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq\|{\operatorname{div}{\bf u}}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{T}^{3}})}\|\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}-{T_{k}(\varrho)}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{T}^{3}})}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\operatorname{div}{\bf u}}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{T}^{3}})}\|\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}-{T_{k}(\varrho)}\|_{L^{1}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{T}^{3}})}^{\lambda}\|\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}-{T_{k}(\varrho)}\|_{L^{\gamma+1}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{T}^{3}})}^{1-\lambda}.

In this case we observe (
65
) that

\|\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}-{T_{k}(\varrho)}\|_{L^{1}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{T}^{3}})}\rightarrow 0\text{ as }k\rightarrow\infty

and Proposition
12

\|\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}-{T_{k}(\varrho)}\|_{L^{\gamma+1}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{T}^{3}})}<\infty.

•

Monotone case:
q(\varrho)\equiv 0
: Using
[
16
, Theorem 11.26]
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left(\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)\pi(\varrho)}-\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}\;\overline{\pi(\varrho)}\right)\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\geq 0.

Hence passing limit
k\rightarrow\infty
in (
4.3.1

Using the monotonicity of

, we obtain sign for the second term in left-hand side. The first term in the right-hand side can be controlled in a similar way as in the monotone case, i.e.,

\displaystyle\leq\|\left(-\operatorname{div}{\bf u}+\overline{\tilde{q}(\varrho)}\right)\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{T}^{3}})}\|\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}-{T_{k}(\varrho)}\|_{L^{1}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{T}^{3}})}^{\lambda}\|\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}-{T_{k}(\varrho)}\|_{L^{\gamma+1}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{T}^{3}})}^{1-\lambda}.

We again observe that

\|\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}-{T_{k}(\varrho)}\|_{L^{1}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{T}^{3}})}\rightarrow 0

and
\|\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}-{T_{k}(\varrho)}\|_{L^{\gamma+1}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{T}^{3}})}<\infty.

Therefore letting
k\rightarrow\infty

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left(\overline{\varrho\log\varrho}-\varrho\log\varrho\right)(\tau,\cdot)\,{\rm d}{x}\leq\left|\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left(\overline{\varrho q(\varrho)}-\varrho\;\overline{q(\varrho)}\right)\,{\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\right|\text{ a.e. }\tau\in(0,T).

Since,
0<q\in C_{c}^{2}(0,\infty)
we assume
\text{supp}\;q\subset[0,M]
. Therefore, there exists a constant
\lambda(q)>0
that depends on
q
such that

\displaystyle\lambda_{q}\varrho\log\varrho+\varrho q(\varrho),\quad\lambda_{q}\varrho\log\varrho+q(\varrho)\text{ \ \ and \ \ }\lambda_{q}\varrho\log\varrho-\varrho q(\varrho)\text{ \ \ are convex}.

This helps us to deduce

Finally using Gronwall’s argument we conclude

Since we already have

A.4

Boundedness of oscillation defect measure. Proof of Proposition
12

Proof:
First we observe that

\displaystyle|T_{k}(\varrho_{\mu})-T_{k}(\varrho)|^{\gamma+1}\leq(\varrho_{\mu}^{\gamma}-\varrho^{\gamma})(T_{k}(\varrho_{\mu})-T_{k}(\varrho)).

(94)

The above statement follows from the following observations:

\displaystyle|T_{k}(t)-T_{k}(s)|\leq|t-s|,\text{ for }t,s>0,

\displaystyle(t-s)^{\gamma}\leq(t^{\gamma}-s^{\gamma}),\;\text{ for }t>s>0,

\displaystyle|T_{k}(t)-T_{k}(s)|^{\gamma+1}\leq(t^{\gamma}-s^{\gamma})(T_{k}(t)-T_{k}(s)),\;t,s>0.

The fact

\varrho\mapsto\varrho^{\gamma}\text{ is convex and }\varrho\mapsto T_{k}(\varrho)\text{ is concave}

yields

\displaystyle\varrho^{\gamma}\leq\overline{\varrho^{\gamma}}\text{ and }T_{k}(\varrho)\geq\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{\mu\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|T_{k}(\varrho_{\mu})-T_{k}(\varrho)|^{\gamma+1}\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(\ \overline{\varrho^{\gamma}T_{k}(\varrho)}-\overline{\varrho^{\gamma}}\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)})\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\underbrace{\left((\overline{\varrho^{\gamma}}-\varrho^{\gamma})(\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}-T_{k}(\varrho))\right)}_{\leq 0}\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.

We recall the effective viscous flux identity with the truncation function
T_{k}
:

\displaystyle\overline{\pi(\varrho)T_{k}(\varrho)}-\overline{\pi(\varrho)}\;\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}=\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)\operatorname{div}{\bf u}}-\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}\operatorname{div}{\bf u}.

We now distinguish two cases.

Monotone case
(\pi(\varrho)=a\varrho^{\gamma}+\tilde{p}(\varrho)
):

Therefore, using structure of
\pi
(
\tilde{p}
is monotone), we have

\displaystyle a\overline{\varrho^{\gamma}T_{k}(\varrho)}-a\overline{\varrho^{\gamma}}\;\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}\leq a\overline{\varrho^{\gamma}T_{k}(\varrho)}+\overline{\tilde{p}(\varrho)T_{k}(\varrho)}-a\overline{\varrho^{\gamma}}\;\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}-\overline{\tilde{p}(\varrho)}\;\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}

\displaystyle=\overline{\pi(\varrho)T_{k}(\varrho)}-\overline{\pi(\varrho)}\;\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}=\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)\operatorname{div}{\bf u}}-\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}\operatorname{div}{\bf u}=\lim\limits_{\mu\to 0}\left((T_{k}(\varrho_{\mu})-\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)})\operatorname{div}{\bf u}_{\mu}\right).

Above, we used
[
16
, Theorem 11.26]
yielding
\overline{\tilde{p}(\varrho)T_{k}(\varrho)}\geq\overline{\tilde{p}(\varrho)}\;\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}
.
The computations above result into

\displaystyle\limsup_{\mu\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|T_{k}(\varrho_{\mu})-T_{k}(\varrho)|^{\gamma+1}\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq C\limsup_{\mu\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left|\left((T_{k}(\varrho_{\mu})-\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)})\operatorname{div}{\bf u}_{\mu}\right)\right|\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\operatorname{div}{\bf u}_{\mu}\right\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\limsup_{\mu\to 0}\left\|T_{k}(\varrho_{\mu})-\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}\right\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}.

From the observation

\displaystyle\limsup_{\mu\to 0}\|T_{k}(\varrho_{\mu})-\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{\mu\to 0}\|T_{k}(\varrho_{\mu})-{T_{k}(\varrho)}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\limsup_{\mu\to 0}\|T_{k}(\varrho)-\overline{T_{k}(\varrho)}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq 2\limsup_{\mu\to 0}\|T_{k}(\varrho_{\mu})-{T_{k}(\varrho)}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle\limsup_{\mu\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|T_{k}(\varrho_{\mu})-T_{k}(\varrho)|^{\gamma+1}\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t<\infty.

Non monotone case with
(\pi(\varrho)=p(\varrho)+q(\varrho))

The pressure
\pi(\varrho)=p(\varrho)+q(\varrho)
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\pi(\varrho)=a\varrho^{\gamma}+\tilde{p}(\varrho)+\tilde{q}(\varrho)

where
\tilde{p}(\varrho)
is a monotone function and

Then, following similar computations as before, we end up with

\displaystyle a\limsup_{\mu\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|T_{k}(\varrho_{\mu})-T_{k}(\varrho)|^{\gamma+1}\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

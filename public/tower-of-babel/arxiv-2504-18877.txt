1.
Introduction

In this paper we deal about the existence of nontrivial solutions for the following autonomous semilinear elliptic differential equation

(
P
)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}-\Delta u&=&\lambda u+f(u)&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u&=&0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

being
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
a smooth bounded domain,
N\geq 1
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
a locally Lipschitz function satisfying

(
H
)

F(s):=\int_{0}^{s}f(t)dt\leq 0\mbox{ for every }s\in\mathbb{R}.

Typical example are
f(u)=-\sin u
or
f(u)=-u(u-1)(u-2)

A bounded weak solution (solution from now on) to problem (

P

) is a function
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla\varphi=\lambda\int_{\Omega}u\varphi+\int_{\Omega}f(u)\varphi,\quad\textit{for all}\,\,\varphi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

Observe that, by regularity results, every solution is a classical solution to this problem.

Note that hypothesis (

H

) implies that
u\equiv 0
is always a solution to problem (

P

).
In
[
2
]
the authors establish the uniqueness of trivial solution for the case
\lambda=0
. Therefore, we are interested in existence of nontrivial solutions by adding the term
\lambda u
to the source data
f(u)
.
As will be seen below, this will depend both on the dimension of the space and on the different values of

\lambda
. Besides,
\lambda_{1}
(the first eigenvalue of the Laplace operator with Dirichlet boundary conditions on
\partial\Omega
) plays a crucial role. That is, an effect similar to that found in the well-known Brézis-Nirenberg work
[
1
]
.

However, as can be seen in the next section, partial results will be obtained, leaving open questions reflected in the last section.

2.
Main results

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
N\geq 1
. If
\lambda\leq 0
, then
u\equiv 0
is the unique solution to (

P

), while for
\lambda\geq\lambda_{1}
there exists
f
satisfying (

H

) such that there exists a nontrivial solution to (

P

).

Proof.

Case
\lambda\leq 0
is a fairly straightforward result of
[
2
]
, since the term
\lambda u+f(u)
has nonpositive primitive. On the other hand, for case
\lambda\geq\lambda_{1}
it is sufficient to take
f(u)=(\lambda_{1}-\lambda)u
and observe that
u=\varphi_{1}
(the first eigenfunction of the Laplace operator with Dirichlet boundary conditions on
\partial\Omega
) is a classical solution to (

P

).
∎

The previous lemma focuses the problem for values of
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{1})
. Next, a partial result is obtained for dimensions greater than
2
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
N\geq 3
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
a smooth bounded starshaped domain and
\lambda\leq\frac{N-2}{N}\lambda_{1}
. Then, problem (

P

) has no nontrivial solutions.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can assume, up to a translation, that
\Omega
is starshaped respect to the origin. Therefore
x\cdot\nu(x)\geq 0
on
\partial\Omega
, where
\nu
denotes the unit vector normal to
\partial\Omega
pointing outwards.

By the well-known Pohožaev identity
[
3
]
, every solution
u
to (

P

) must satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\,x\cdot\nu(x)\,dx+\frac{N-2}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\lambda\,\frac{N}{2}\int_{\Omega}u(x)^{2}\,dx+N\int_{\Omega}F(u(x))\,dx,

which implies

\frac{N-2}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}\,dx\leq\lambda\,\frac{N}{2}\int_{\Omega}u(x)^{2}\,dx+N\int_{\Omega}F(u(x))\,dx,

and by using Poincaré inequality

\lambda_{1}\,\frac{N-2}{2}\int_{\Omega}u(x)^{2}\,dx\leq\lambda\,\frac{N}{2}\int_{\Omega}u(x)^{2}+N\int_{\Omega}F(u(x))\,dx.

We claim that
\int_{\Omega}F(u(x))\,dx<0
. Conversely, since
f
satisfies (

H

) and
F\circ u
is continuous, then
F(u(x))=0
for every
x\in\Omega
. Therefore
F(s)=0
for every
s\in I=\{u(x):x\in\Omega\}
. Since
u
is nontrivial it follows that
I
is a nontrivial interval and hence
f(s)=F^{\prime}(s)=0
for every
s\in I
. Thus
u
satisfies
-\Delta u=\lambda u
in
\Omega
and consequently
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of the Laplace operator with Dirichlet boundary conditions on
\partial\Omega
, contradicting hypothesis
\lambda\leq\frac{N-2}{N}\lambda_{1}
.

Finally, from the above inequality it follows that

\lambda_{1}\,\frac{N-2}{2}\int_{\Omega}u(x)^{2}\,dx<\lambda\,\frac{N}{2}\int_{\Omega}u(x)^{2}\,dx.

This leads to the fact that any nontrivial solution to the problem (

P

) must satisfy

\lambda>\lambda_{1}\,\frac{N-2}{N}

and the proof is concluded.
∎

However, as can be seen in the following theorem, in dimension
1
a drastic change occurs: the existence of nontrivial solutions for every positive
\lambda
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\Omega=(a,b)\subset\mathbb{R}
and set
\lambda>0
. Then, there exists
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying (

H

), such that problem (

P

) admits a nontrivial solution
u\in C^{\infty}[a,b]
.

For its proof, we will first state the following technical lemma:

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
a<b
and
\lambda
real numbers satisfying
0<\lambda<\pi^{2}/(b-a)^{2}
. Then, there exists a unique
M>0
, depending on
\lambda
, such that

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{Ms^{2}(1-s)^{2}+\lambda(1-s^{2})}}=\frac{b-a}{2}.

Proof.

Define the function
\Phi:(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
by

\Phi(k):=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{ks^{2}(1-s)^{2}+\lambda(1-s^{2})}},\ \ \ k>0.

It is obvious that

\frac{1}{\sqrt{ks^{2}(1-s)^{2}+\lambda(1-s^{2})}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda(1-s^{2})}}\in L^{1}(0,1)\mbox{ for every }k>0.

Hence, applying the Convergence Dominated Theorem we can assert that
\Phi
is a well defined decreasing continuous function satisfying

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\Phi(k)=\int_{0}^{1}0\,ds=0,

\lim_{k\to 0}\Phi(k)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{\lambda(1-s^{2})}}=\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}.

Applying
0<\lambda<\pi^{2}/(b-a)^{2}
, we deduce that

\frac{b-a}{2}\in\left(\lim_{k\to+\infty}\Phi(k),\lim_{k\to 0}\Phi(k)\right),

which gives
\Phi(M)=(b-a)/2
for a unique
M>0
, and the proof is complete.

∎

Proof of Theorem
2.3

If
\lambda\geq\lambda_{1}=\pi^{2}/(b-a)^{2}
, by Lemma
2.1
there exists a nontrivial solution to problem (

P

) for
f(s)=\left(\pi^{2}/(b-a)^{2}-\lambda\right)s,\,\,s\in\mathbb{R}
.

For the rest of the proof we will suppose that
0<\lambda<\pi^{2}/(b-a)^{2}
.

Define the function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by

f(s)=-Ms(s-1)(2s-1),\ \ \ s\in\mathbb{R},

where
M>0
is the value obtained in Lemma
2.4
. It is immediate that
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and satisfies
F(s):=\int_{0}^{s}f(t)dt=-Ms^{2}(1-s)^{2}/2\leq 0
, for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Now, we will construct a nontrivial solution
u\in C^{\infty}(a,b)
to problem (

P

).

For this purpose; define
\Psi:[0,1]\to[a,(a+b)/2]
by

\Psi(t):=a+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{Ms^{2}(1-s)^{2}+\lambda(1-s^{2})}},\ \ \ t\in[0,1].

Clearly
\Psi
is a
C^{\infty}[0,1)\cap C[0,1]
bijection between
[0,1]
and
[a,(a+b)/2]
, with
\Psi^{\prime}(t)=1/\sqrt{Mt^{2}(1-t)^{2}+\lambda(1-t^{2})}>0
,
t\in[0,1).

Therefore, we can define
u:=\Psi^{-1}
, obtaining
u\in C^{\infty}[a,(a+b)/2)\cap C[a,(a+b)/2]
with

u^{\prime}(x)=\sqrt{Mu(x)^{2}(1-u(x))^{2}+\lambda(1-u(x)^{2})},\ \ \ x\in[a,(a+b)/2).

Since
u\left((a+b)/2\right)=1
, we obtain that
\lim_{x\to(a+b)/2}u^{\prime}(x)=0
and consequently
u\in C^{1}[a,(a+b)/2]
.

Moreover, from the above expression we can assert that

u^{\prime}(x)^{2}=Mu(x)^{2}(1-u(x))^{2}+\lambda(1-u(x)^{2}),\ \ \ x\in[a,(a+b)/2),

and differentiating yields

2u^{\prime}(x)u^{\prime\prime}(x)=u^{\prime}(x)\left(2Mu(x)(u(x)-1)(2u(x)-1)-2\lambda u(x)\right),\ \ \ x\in[a,(a+b)/2).

Since
u^{\prime}(x)\neq 0
for
x\in[a,(a+b)/2)
we conclude that

-u^{\prime\prime}=f(u)+\lambda u,\,\,x\in[a,(a+b)/2).

In fact, the continuity of
f
implies that there exists
\lim_{x\to(a+b)/2}u^{\prime\prime}(x)
, and consequently
u\in C^{2}[a,(a+b)/2]
.

Finally, we define
u
for
x\in((a+b)/2,b]
by symmetry. That is,

u(x):=u(a+b-x),\,\,x\in((a+b)/2,b].

Taking into account that
u(a)=0
,
u^{\prime}((a+b)/2)=0
and
u\in C^{2}[a,(a+b)/2]
. It follows that
-u^{\prime\prime}=f(u)+\lambda u
in
[a,(a+b)/2]
. So, we deduce that
u\in C^{2}[a,b]
is a solution of problem (

P

).

Finally, since
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
we conclude
u\in C^{\infty}[a,b]
and the result follows.

∎

3.
Summary and open problems

Our results are summarized as follows. Uniqueness/non-uniqueness of trivial solution to problem (

P

).

•

N=1
; Uniqueness for
\lambda\leq 0
and non-uniqueness for
\lambda>0
.

•

N=2
; Uniqueness for
\lambda\leq 0
and non-uniqueness for
\lambda\geq\lambda_{1}
.

•

N\geq 3
; Uniqueness for
\lambda\leq\lambda_{1}\frac{N-2}{N}
(in case that the domain is starshaped) and non-uniqueness for
\lambda\geq\lambda_{1}
.

We finish our discussion with the following open questions:

(1)

Does (

P

) admit a nontrivial solution if
N=2
and
0<\lambda<\lambda_{1}
?

(2)

Does (

P

) admit a nontrivial solution if
N\geq 3
and
\lambda_{1}\frac{N-2}{N}<\lambda<\lambda_{1}
?

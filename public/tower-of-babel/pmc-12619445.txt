Visceral lipid accumulation and lipid accumulation product outperform insulin resistance score for metabolic syndrome prediction in Northern Chinese adults: validation through AUC comparison and decision curve analysis
Authors: Qing Liu, Xing Guan, Li-Jun Wang, Zheng Wang, Han Zhang, Yu-Qiang Zuo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundMetabolic syndrome (MetS) significantly elevates the risk of diabetes and cardiovascular disease. While insulin resistance (IR) underpins MetS pathogenesis, practical biomarkers for population screening remain limited. The metabolic score for IR (METS-IR), lipid accumulation product (LAP) and visceral adiposity index (VAI) are highly sensitive and specific biomarkers of IR, which require comparative validation.ObjectiveTo compare LAP, VAI, and METS-IR index for predicting MetS with sex-stratified analysis.MethodsThe physiological characteristics and blood biochemistry data collected from 2821 patients during an annual health check-up were analyzed. Participants were assigned to MetS group (≥3 MetS conditions), pre-Mets group (1–2 Mets components) or control group (0 component) based on IDF/AHA/NHLBI 2009 criteria and were further stratified by sex. The predictive value and clinical usefulness of the LAP, VAI, and METS-IR were evaluated by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve and decision curve analysis (DCA).ResultsLAP, VAI, and METS-IR levels were significantly elevated in the MetS group (P < 0.05) and strongly correlated with MetS components. ROC analysis revealed LAP as the superior predictor in the total cohort (AUC: 0.904 [95% CI: 0.888–0.920]) and females (AUC: 0.949 [95% CI: 0.921–0.976]), while VAI performed best in males (AUC: 0.863 [95% CI: 0.840–0.886]). DCA confirmed the superior clinical utility of LAP and VAI over METS-IR across subgroups.ConclusionThis large-scale validation establishes LAP and VAI as superior, sex-specific predictors of MetS compared to METS-IR. LAP is optimal overall and in women, while VAI is optimal in men. Their reliance on standard clinical measurements establishes them as practical tools for metabolic risk stratification.

Introduction

Metabolic syndrome (MetS), characterized by central obesity, dyslipidemia, hypertension, and hyperglycemia, significantly increases the risk of type 2 diabetes mellitus and cardiovascular disease [1, 2]. Emerging evidence indicates that the AMPK signaling pathway plays a central role in metabolic homeostasis regulation. Sodium-glucose co-transporter-s (SGLT2) inhibitors demonstrate significant multi-organ metabolic improvements through adenosine monophosphate-activated protein kinase (AMPK) pathway activation, including reduced renal fibrosis, improved cardiac energy metabolism, attenuated hepatic lipotoxicity, and enhanced intestinal barrier function [3]. Concurrently, adipokine networks, particularly the C1qTNF-related protein (CTRP) family, have gained increasing attention in metabolic regulation. Specifically, CTRP1 and CTRP5 show significant associations with obesity indices and early atherosclerosis markers [4]. Insulin resistance (IR) is a core pathophysiological feature of the MetS [5]. While the hyperinsulinemia-euglycemic clamp (HIEC) is the gold standard for measuring IR, it is time-consuming, labor intensive, expensive, and technically difficult, all of which limit its use in the clinical setting and in epidemiological studies [6]. The metabolic score for IR (METS-IR) is a novel, reliable surrogate measurement of IR and has been validated against the HIEC technique [7].

Recent evidence supports the association of visceral adipose tissue with IR, lipoprotein metabolism, and elevated blood pressure [8]. The evidence for visceral adiposity as a factor that increases the risk of type 2 diabetes mellitus, atherosclerosis, and cardiovascular disease is summarized in a joint position statement published by the International Atherosclerosis Society and the International Chair on Cardiometabolic Risk Working Group on Visceral Obesity [9]. The lipid accumulation product (LAP) [10] and visceral adiposity index (VAI) [11] are novel, non-imaging markers of visceral adiposity that have been used to predict cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, and kidney disease [12]. Recent large-scale studies continue to validate their utility specifically for MetS identification. For instance, in a US national cohort, both VAI and LAP were identified as the strongest anthropometric indicators associated with MetS prevalence [13]. Similarly, analyses of NHANES data have confirmed the high predictive accuracy of LAP for MetS in the general American population [14]. These findings underscore the potential of these indices as practical screening tools across diverse populations. Unlike direct imaging measures that quantify adipose tissue mass, VAI serves as a surrogate indicator of adipose tissue dysfunction, capturing the functional metabolic consequences of visceral adiposity including altered lipid metabolism, insulin resistance, and inflammatory activity, as demonstrated by its stronger association with cardiometabolic risk factors than volumetric assessments [15, 16]. The three indices differ conceptually and mathematically. The LAP is calculated from WC and TG, directly reflecting lipid overflow from visceral fat [10]. The VAI incorporates BMI, WC, TG, and HDL-C in a sex-specific formula to estimate visceral adipose function and associated cardiometabolic risk [11]. In contrast, the METS-IR is derived from FPG, TG, BMI, and HDL-C, focusing primarily on insulin sensitivity without direct anthropometric measures of adiposity [7]. Given the well-established sexual dimorphism in body fat distribution, lipid metabolism, and hormonal regulation, we hypothesized that the performance of these indices might differ between men and women. Furthermore, VAI is inherently sex-specific in its design. Therefore, a sex-stratified analysis was deemed essential. However, direct comparisons of the predictive performance and, crucially, the clinical utility of these indices for MetS identification, particularly in a Northern Chinese population and with thorough sex-specific analysis, are lacking. Moreover, previous studies have primarily relied on ROC analysis, neglecting the assessment of clinical net benefit through methods like Decision Curve Analysis (DCA). Given the central role of visceral adiposity in MetS pathogenesis [8, 9], we hypothesized that LAP and VAI might offer superior predictive capability for MetS compared to the IR-focused METS-IR.

This study aimed to comprehensively compare the predictive performance and clinical utility of LAP, VAI, and METS-IR for identifying MetS in a large Northern Chinese health check-up cohort, with specific attention to sex differences.

Methods

Study design and participants

Study population

This retrospective cross-sectional study analyzed data from a consecutive series of adults who underwent routine health examinations at the Second Hospital of Hebei Medical University between July and December 2024. Participants were included if they had complete measurements for waist circumference (WC), body mass index (BMI), systolic blood pressure (SBP), diastolic blood pressure (DBP), fasting plasma glucose (FPG), triglycerides (TG), and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C). Individuals with a history of recent cardiovascular/cerebrovascular events, active malignancies, pregnancy/lactation, recent glucocorticoid/immunosuppressant use, or irregular use of antihypertensive, lipid-lowering, or glucose-lowering agents were excluded. Finally, a total of 2821 participants were included (Fig. 1). The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of the Second Hospital of Hebei Medical University (Approval no. 2022-R341). Informed consent was waived by the IRB due to the retrospective nature of the study and the use of anonymized data, adhering to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.Fig. 1 Flow diagram of study participant selection

Data collection and measurements

The baseline demographic data included sex, age, height, weight, pulse rate, SBP, DBP and WC. Systolic and diastolic pressure and pulse were measured by trained nurses using automated sphygmomanometers (Omron HEM-7136) according to standardized protocols. Participants rested in a seated position for at least 5 minutes in a quiet room before measurements were taken. Blood samples were collected after an overnight fast (8–12 hours). Serum was separated by centrifugation (3000 rpm for 10 minutes) within 30 minutes of collection and analyzed immediately or stored at −80 °C until assayed. Serum levels of TG, total cholesterol (TC), HDL-C, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), and FPG were measured using standardized enzymatic methods on a Beckman Coulter AU5800 analyzer. Serum lipid values (TG and HDL-C) were converted based on molecular weights: 1 mmol/L triglycerides = 88.57 mg/dL; 1 mmol/L cholesterol = 38.67 mg/dL. All original measurements in tables retain mmol/L units for clinical interpretability. WC was measured by trained staff midway between the lowest rib and the iliac crest at the end of a normal exhalation using a non-stretchable tape measure, ensuring the tape was snug but not compressing the skin and maintained parallel to the floor. Two measurements were taken, and if they differed by more than 1 cm, a third measurement was taken. The average of the two closest readings was recorded.

Diagnostic criteria and group definitions

Mets was diagnosed based on the Guideline for the prevention and treatment of diabetes mellitus in China (2024 edition) [17], with the presence of 3–5 of the subsequent metabolic abnormalities: (1) WC ≥ 90 cm for men or ≥ 85 cm for women; (2)a systolic blood pressure ≥ 130 mmHg or a diastolic blood pressure ≥ 85 mmHg and/or previously diagnosed hypertension; (3) FBG ≥ 6.1 mmol/L or a 2-hour plasma glucose ≥7.8 mmol/L or previously diagnosed diabetes; (4)fasting triglycerides ≥ 1.7 mmol/L or treatment for hypertriglyceridemia; (5) high-density lipoprotein-cholesterol of < 1.04 mmol/L or treatment for low HDL-C. Participants were classified as MetS (≥3 components) (n = 316) or Non-MetS (0–2 components) (n = 2505) per IDF/AHA/NHLBI 2009 criteria [18], and Non-MetS further divided into: control subgroup (0 component) (n = 861) and pre-Mets subgroup(1–2 components) (n = 1644); which denote subclinical metabolic dysfunction.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables, confirmed non-normal by Shapiro-Wilk tests (all p < 0.05), are presented as median (interquartile range) and compared using Kruskal-Wallis tests with Dunn’s post-hoc adjustments. Categorical variables were compared using chi-square tests. Spearman’s rank correlation assessed relationships between LAP, VAI, METS-IR, and individual MetS components. The Jonckheere-Terpstra test, which is particularly powerful for detecting ordered trends across groups, was used to evaluate trends across metabolic risk categories (Control, pre-MetS, MetS). To ensure comparability of the effect sizes (odds ratios) among VAI, LAP, and METS-IR, all three indices were standardized using Z-score transformation (mean = 0, standard deviation = 1) prior to analysis. This allows the interpretation of odds ratios per 1-standard deviation (SD) increase in each index. The associations between these standardized indices and metabolic syndrome were then assessed using multivariable binary logistic regression models, adjusted for age as a continuous covariate. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis determined the predictive performance (AUC with 95% CI) of each index for distinguishing: MetS vs. Non-MetS, MetS vs. Pre-MetS, and pre-MetS vs. Control. Optimal cutoff values were identified separately for the total cohort and for each sex subgroup by maximizing the Youden index (J = sensitivity + specificity − 1). DeLong’s test compared AUCs between indices. Decision curve analysis (DCA) evaluated the clinical net benefit of using each index across a range of threshold probabilities. Calibration curves assessed model fit. Power analysis indicated > 90% power (α = 0.05) to detect AUC differences ≥0.05. Analyses were stratified by sex and performed using R 4.2.0 with the following package: pROC (for ROC analysis), rmda (for DCA), and ggplot2 (for generating figures). Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05 (two-tailed).

Correlations and trends

Spearman correlation analyses revealed significant associations (all p < 0.05) between all three indices (VAI, LAP, METS-IR) and most MetS components in the total cohort and within each sex subgroup (Table 2). Discussion

This large-scale study establishes lipid accumulation product (LAP) and visceral adiposity index (VAI) as superior predictors of metabolic syndrome (MetS) compared to the insulin resistance index METS-IR in a Northern Chinese cohort (n = 2,821). LAP demonstrated optimal performance overall (AUC = 0.904) and in women (AUC = 0.949), while VAI was optimal in men (AUC = 0.863). These differences were statistically significant per DeLong’s test (p < 0.001) across multiple comparison groups (MetS vs. Non-MetS; MetS vs. Pre-MetS). It is noteworthy that METS-IR showed comparable performance to LAP in distinguishing pre-MetS from controls in males (AUC: 0.837 vs. 0.833). This might suggest that disturbances in glucose and lipid metabolism captured by METS-IR become apparent earlier in the disease process in men, before visceral adiposity, as measured by LAP, becomes the dominant driver. These findings align with and are strengthened by a growing body of recent evidence. Large-scale studies, including a meta-analysis, have specifically affirmed the high accuracy and gender-specific utility of LAP for MetS screening [13, 19]. Similarly, both VAI and LAP have been consistently identified as among the most powerful anthropometric indicators for MetS in diverse populations, including the US [13] and resource-limited regions in China [20]. Moreover, DCA revealed that the clinical usefulness of VAI and LAP for predicting MetS was also superior to that of METS-IR. Whereas previous validations stopped at establishing discriminatory power (ROC analysis), our integrated assessment: Quantifies prediction accuracy via calibration curves; Validates clinical usefulness through DCA-derived net benefit. This approach resolves a critical gap in translational biomarker research by demonstrating not just statistical superiority, but practical clinical value.

The robust superiority of LAP and VAI in predicting metabolic syndrome aligns fundamentally with the central pathophysiological role of visceral adipose tissue (VAT) in MetS development [8, 9, 21, 22]. VAT drives metabolic dysfunction through lipolytic release of free fatty acids and secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL-6, TNF-α), which directly promote insulin resistance, dyslipidemia, and endothelial dysfunction. While our study focused on VAT indices, we fully integrate the critical role of subcutaneous adipose tissue (SAT) dysregulation, which is a key driver of metabolic inflammation in nascent MetS [23, 24]. SAT exhibits a pathological phenotype characterized by adipokine dysfunction, macrophage crown-like structures [25], mast cell accumulation (2.5-fold increase) promoting fibrosis [26], and increased fibrosis (Sirius red staining) contributing to hypoxia [27]. Our finding that VAI and LAP outperform METS-IR extends this pathological paradigm. We posit that visceral adiposity reflects a parallel—and potentially consequent—failure of SAT expansion [28]. This is strongly evidenced by gluteal SAT adipocyte hypertrophy (waist-adjusted), a key feature of nascent MetS [27], which correlates directly with fibrosis, angiogenesis, and a pro-inflammatory state (TLR4/NLRP3 inflammasome activation, elevated endotoxin and FFAs) [27]. Thus, the superior predictive power of VAI and LAP likely captures the downstream sequelae of a systemic adipose tissue failure, encompassing adipocyte hypertrophy, SAT dysfunction, cellular inflammation, and VAT accumulation.This nuanced understanding of adipose biology—incorporating depot-specific function (e.g., protective gluteofemoral vs. detrimental abdominal SAT [23]) and sexual dimorphism—explains why VAI optimally predicts MetS in males while LAP excels in females, aligning with established differences in body fat distribution and adipokine expression [4]. The mechanistic basis for VAT-centered indices is further reinforced by AMPK pathway activation, which improves visceral adipose function and reduces lipotoxicity [27]. Quantitatively, this relationship is validated by Jonckheere-Terpstra analysis, demonstrating strictly monotonic increases across metabolic health strata (Control→pre-MetS→MetS; all p < 0.001), with LAP exhibiting the most dramatic elevation (median increase: 321%)—corroborating the visceral adiposity mechanism proposition [11]. The multidimensional assessment architecture of LAP and VAI, which captures visceral adiposity, lipid metabolism, and systemic inflammation, explains their performance advantage over METS-IR. Critically, METS-IR suffers from omissions including absence of direct VAT quantification and failure to account for VAT-derived inflammatory mediators that impair insulin signaling [29, 30]; it thus overlooks the holistic pathology of MetS, which includes lipotoxicity, chronic inflammation, and endothelial impairment [21, 31]. Therefore, future studies should integrate direct measures of SAT biology (e.g., adipocyte size, fibrosis markers) with visceral adiposity indices to provide a more comprehensive assessment of metabolic risk.

While some previous studies have found LAP to be superior [19, 20], our finding that VAI’s predictive ability outperformed LAP in the male subgroup analysis is noteworthy and may reflect population-specific characteristics. This observation, however, is consistent with the notion that the optimal index may vary based on demographic and physiological factors [14, 32, 33], VAI’s predictive ability outperformed that of LAP in the male subgroup analysis. The underlying reasons may include the following. (1) The incorporation of HDL-C in VAI appears to be particularly advantageous in men for MetS prediction. As testosterone is known to suppress HDL synthesis [34], lower HDL-C levels are a more prominent feature of dyslipidemia in men. VAI’s inclusion of this sex-dimorphic factor, along with its integration of BMI-adjusted WC and TG, likely provides a more sensitive reflection of the adverse metabolic profile associated with visceral adiposity in males compared to LAP, which lacks this adjustment. Additionally, variations in study populations and regional differences in lifestyle and dietary habits may lead to discrepancies between the findings of this study and previous research.

To our knowledge, this is among the first studies to comprehensively compare the clinical utility of VAI, LAP, and METS-IR for MetS prediction using decision curve analysis in a large Northern Chinese cohort. Earlier comparisons were constrained by: (1) Inadequate power (small sample size cohort); (2) Failure to examine sex-specific effects despite known biological dimorphism; (3) Sole reliance on ROC-AUC without clinical utility metrics like DCA. Our study overcomes these by combining large-scale sampling (n = 2821), sex stratification, and dual assessment of discrimination (ROC) and clinical value (DCA).

The strong performance and simplicity of VAI and LAP suggest they can be readily integrated into existing clinical practice and screening protocols. Given their dynamic responsiveness to disease progression (validated by trend analysis), coupled with excellent discriminatory power (ROC verification) and clinical utility (DCA confirmation), LAP and VAI can simultaneously serve for both MetS risk screening and disease monitoring. Given their calculation relies solely on routine measurements routinely collected during health check-ups - WC, BMI, TG, and HDL-C - they add negligible burden or cost. VAI and LAP could be automatically calculated within electronic medical record systems upon entry of these standard parameters. Their results could then be prominently displayed in health check reports alongside traditional risk factors, flagging individuals exceeding sex-specific thresholds for heightened MetS risk (e.g., LAP > 61.94 for men, > 47.19 for women; VAI > 1.66 for men, > 2.08 for women, based on established cut-offs or population-specific determinations). This would allow clinicians to efficiently identify high-risk individuals during routine visits, prompting earlier lifestyle interventions or more intensive monitoring before the full manifestation of MetS components, thereby shifting focus towards prevention. Their superiority over METS-IR, particularly given METS-IR’s logarithmic transformation which is less intuitive for clinical interpretation, further supports prioritizing VAI or LAP in such screening algorithms.

This study has several limitations that should be considered when interpreting the findings. First, the cross-sectional design precludes the determination of causality; while our analyses demonstrate strong associations, the temporal sequence between elevated VAI/LAP and MetS development remains unclear. Second, as participants were recruited from a single center in Northern China, the generalizability of our proposed sex-specific cut-off values to other populations with diverse genetic backgrounds, dietary habits, and lifestyle characteristics may be limited. Third, although we adopted a widely accepted MetS definition, the use of alternative criteria (e.g., IDF or NCEP ATP III) might yield slightly different results and classification outcomes. Fourth, the multiple statistical comparisons performed increase the risk of Type I error; thus, our results should be interpreted as exploratory and require validation in independent external cohorts. Fifth, we did not include other established metabolic indices such as the Triglyceride-Glucose Index (TyG) or non-HDL cholesterol, which could have offered valuable comparative insights. Consequently, our conclusions regarding the superiority of VAI and LAP are constrained to the context of comparison with METS-IR rather than a comprehensive comparison of all established indices.Sixth, data on several potential confounders were unavailable, including detailed medication history, lifestyle factors (e.g., smoking, alcohol, diet, and physical activity), and inflammatory markers (e.g., CRP, leptin). The absence of mechanistic biomarkers related to the AMPK pathway or adipokine profiles (e.g., CTRP1, CTRP5) also limits the depth of physiological interpretation. Future studies would benefit from longitudinal designs to establish causality and multi-center recruitment to enhance external validity. Incorporating additional biochemical markers, lifestyle variables, and advanced imaging parameters could further improve predictive accuracy and provide a more comprehensive understanding of the pathophysiological mechanisms underlying MetS.

Conclusion

In this large Northern Chinese cohort, visceral adiposity indices (LAP and VAI) demonstrated superior predictive performance and clinical utility for identifying MetS compared to the insulin resistance index METS-IR. LAP demonstrated the highest predictive performance overall and in women, while VAI showed the best performance in men in this cohort. Given that their calculation relies solely on routine clinical measurement, LAP and VAI represent promising practical tools for metabolic risk stratification, pending further validation of their cost-effectiveness and utility in diverse public health seetings. Future research should focus on validating sex-specific cutoffs in diverse populations, establishing longitudinal associations with cardiometabolic events, assessing responsiveness to interventions, and integrating these indices into clinical decision support systems.

Efficacy of Patiromer in Treatment of Long Inter-Dialytic Interval Hyperkalemia in Potassium-Rich Diet Populations Undergoing Intermittent Hemodialysis
Authors: Bilal Mohsin, Abdullah Abdulaziz Althobaiti, Joudi T Alattas, Joynalyn E Barrios, Wafa A Altuwaijri, Akber Saifullah, Shroug Al Harbi, Lama Hefni, Wael Habhab, Najla Zabani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHyperkalemia after a Long Interdialytic Interval (LIDI) is a potentially life-threatening complication in patients undergoing intermittent hemodialysis. The risk is exacerbated especially in populations known for Potassium-rich dietary habits and cultural tradition of weekend gatherings. Patiromer is a potassium binder that helps treat Hyperkalemia. This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy of Patiromer in the management of LIDI Hyperkalemia in patients with ESKD on Intermittent Hemodialysis, with Potassium rich dietary habits as in Saudi Arabia.MethodsAdult patients who had completed 3 months on Intermittent Hemodialysis, were screened for LIDI hyperkalemia pre dialysis (serum K >5.3 mmol/L).Patients with hyperkalemia were prescribed Patiromer on days off Hemodialysis during the LIDI (Thursday and Friday for Saturday group; Friday and Saturday for Sunday group). Pre HD serum potassium levels were monitored after the LIDI at weeks 1, 2, 4, 8, 12, 24, and 52. Patient Compliance was ensured through telephonic communication with the patient or the care giver.ResultsAmong the 127 patients on Intermittent Hemodialysis, 58 were identified with LIDI hyperkalemia out of whom 52 patients completed one year follow-up. 36 had serum K 5.3–5.9 mmol/l, 11 had serum K 6–6.5 mmol/l, 5 had serum K 6.5 mmol/l and above. Patiromer therapy resulted in a mean serum potassium reduction of 0.8–2.5 mmol/L, with a sustained average reduction of 1.57 mmol/L observed over 52 weeks. The treatment was well tolerated, with no drop outs reported due to side effects.ConclusionPatiromer demonstrated sustained efficacy and excellent tolerance in preventing hyperkalemia after the LIDI in our potassium rich diet population undergoing Intermittent Hemodialysis.

Introduction

Hyperkalemia (defined by serum potassium (K) more than 5.3 mmol/l) is a serious and potentially life-threatening condition frequently observed in patients with Chronic Kidney disease.1 This complication is more pronounced in end-stage kidney disease (ESKD) and especially in patients undergoing Intermittent Hemodialysis. Hyperkalemia can disrupt normal cardiac conduction, leading to arrhythmias and an increased risk of sudden cardiac death (SCD).2 Numerous studies have highlighted an alarming spike in sudden cardiac events during these off-dialysis periods, emphasizing the likely role of hyperkalemia as a critical risk factor.3

For patients undergoing Intermittent Hemodialysis thrice weekly, the risk of hyperkalemia is heightened during the LIDI, a time that disrupts the regular clearance of potassium through dialysis sessions.4 This complication is probably more common in regions where cultural or dietary habits may contribute to higher potassium intake during weekends.

High potassium dietary habits, such as the consumption of potassium-rich foods like dates, fruits, honey, and salads, coupled with beverages like Saudi coffee, are culturally ingrained in the Gulf region, including Saudi Arabia.5 Such habits, especially during family gatherings or social events over the weekend, can lead to significant potassium surges, especially over the Long inter-dialytic interval.

Recent advancements in the management of hyperkalemia have introduced new pharmacological options, including potassium binders like Patiromer. Patiromer, a non-absorbed polymer, works by binding potassium in the gastrointestinal tract, thereby enhancing fecal potassium excretion.6 It is frequently used in management of hyperkalemia after starting renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) inhibitors. Unlike older agents such as sodium polystyrene sulfonate, Patiromer has demonstrated a favorable safety profile, with minimal gastrointestinal side effects and negligible risk of systemic complications like sodium overload or colonic necrosis.7

However, the efficacy of Patiromer in treating hyperkalemia after the LIDI in patients undergoing intermittent hemodialysis with potassium-rich dietary habits remains insufficiently studied.8

This underscores the need for localized data that aligns with the unique cultural and dietary patterns of our patient population.9 By understanding how Patiromer can be effectively integrated into the care of dialysis-dependent patients, clinicians can better mitigate the risks associated with hyperkalemia, improve patient outcomes, and potentially reduce the incidence of complications.10

In this prospective study, we evaluate the incidence of pre-dialysis hyperkalemia during the LIDI, and the short- and long-term efficacy and tolerance of Patiromer in the treatment of this clinical condition.

Materials and Methods

Study Design and Setting

This prospective study was conducted in our Renal Dialysis Unit at King Faisal Specialist Hospital and Research Center – Jeddah. The study spanned one year, from January 1, 2024, to December 31, 2024, and aimed to evaluate the incidence of hyperkalemia after the LIDI and the efficacy of Patiromer in managing this condition.

Study Population

The study included adult patients undergoing Intermittent Hemodialysis thrice for more than three months, with adequate dialysis clearance adequacy defined as a Kt/V value of ≥1.4.

Exclusion Criteria

Patients who had recent initiation of hemodialysis (<12 weeks), hospitalized patients, patients with psychiatric illnesses or limited decision-making capacity, those with dialysis access issues or poor dialysis clearance (Kt/V <1.4) were excluded.

Before enrollment, all patients had undergone optimization of routine hyperkalemia management according to institutional practice, including repeated dietary counseling by renal dietitians, individualized adjustment of dialysis prescription (including use of a lower potassium dialysate during the pre-weekend hemodialysis session when clinically indicated), and confirmation of adequate dialysis delivery with maintenance of target Kt/V. Patients with persistent pre-dialysis serum potassium concentrations >5.3 mmol/L despite these measures were considered eligible for Patiromer therapy. None of the enrolled patients were receiving renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system inhibitors or mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists during the study period.

Screening and Intervention

A total of 127 patients were screened for pre-dialysis hyperkalemia after the LIDI. Hyperkalemia was defined as a serum K of more than or equal to 5.3 mmol/l from a blood sample obtained pre-hemodialysis after the LIDI.

Intervention

Hyperkalemic patients were prescribed Patiromer at an initial dose of 8.4 g orally once daily to be taken on off-dialysis during the LIDI; (on Thursday and Friday for Saturday group and on Friday and Saturday for Sunday group). Pre-dialysis potassium was checked 1 week after initiation of Patiromer and if serum K level were still equal or more than5.3 mmol/l, Patiromer dose was increased to 16.8 g orally once daily on dialysis off days during the LIDI. Patients received standardized instructions regarding Patiromer administration.

Patient compliance was ensured via telephonic communications that acted as a reminder and confirmation that patients took their dose at right time. Patients were called at a designated time by one of the research designees on Long inter-dialytic interval days ie, on Thursday and Friday for Saturday group; and Friday and Saturday for Sunday group. Patients were not told to modify their dietary routines during the weekends. Serum potassium levels were monitored pre-dialysis after the long weekend at baseline and at follow-up intervals: weeks 1, 2, 4, 8, 12, 24, and 52 weeks.

Weekly telephone follow-up was performed primarily to assess medication adherence, document adverse events, and identify any safety concerns related to Patiromer therapy. No additional structured dietary counseling or behavioral intervention beyond routine clinical care was provided during these calls.

Data Collection

Data were collected using the electronic medical records (EMR) and organized in a Microsoft Excel data sheet. Parameters recorded included demographic data (age, gender), clinical variables (duration on hemodialysis, comorbidities such as diabetes, hypertension, coronary artery disease), dialysis adequacy (URR and Kt/V), dialysis access type, and laboratory results for serum potassium and magnesium levels.

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), while categorical variables are summarized as frequencies and percentages. Changes in serum potassium over time were compared with baseline using paired Student’s t-tests. Fisher’s exact test was used to evaluate associations between categorical variables. Linear regression analysis was performed to determine whether baseline serum potassium concentration predicted the magnitude of potassium reduction following Patiromer therapy. Statistical significance was defined as a two-sided p value <0.05. Six patients who did not complete follow-up were excluded from the final efficacy analysis, and no imputation of missing data was performed. A two-sided p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Descriptive and inferential statistical methods were employed to evaluate the incidence of hyperkalemia after the LIDI, the clinical efficacy of Patiromer, and associations with demographic and clinical variables.

Sample Size

This study was designed as a prospective real-world observational cohort. Consequently, all consecutive eligible patients fulfilling the inclusion criteria during the study period were included, and no formal a priori sample size calculation was undertaken.

This study was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of King Faisal Specialist Hospital and Research Centre, Jeddah, Saudi Arabia (Approval No. 2251376). At our institution, the IRB serves as the sole governing body for ethical oversight, and no separate ethics committee exists; all human research studies are conducted only after formal IRB approval is obtained.

The study was performed in accordance with the principles of the World Medical Association Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to inclusion in the study.

Patient confidentiality and data privacy were strictly maintained throughout the study. All data were anonymized prior to analysis, and access was restricted to the research team in accordance with institutional policies.

Participation was voluntary, and patients were informed about the risks of untreated hyperkalemia. Patient privacy and anonymity were maintained by excluding personal identifiers, and data access was restricted to the research team.

Results

Among them, 58 patients (45.7%) were diagnosed with hyperkalemia (serum potassium ≥5.3 mmol/L). Of these, 52 patients (89.7%) completed the 52-weeks follow-up (Figure S1). Six patients did not complete the follow up of one year as 3 of them got kidney transplant; two had frequent in-hospital admissions; and one got transferred to outside dialysis facilities. As a predefined secondary outcome, all 52 patients (100%) achieved the target pre-dialysis serum potassium concentration of <5.3 mmol/L after initiation of Patiromer therapy, and this therapeutic target was maintained throughout the 52-week follow-up period. Importantly, sustained potassium control was achieved without the need for dose escalation, demonstrating the durability of the initial therapeutic response and supporting the long-term efficacy of maintenance-dose Patiromer in patients receiving intermittent hemodialysis.

There were no reports of significant gastrointestinal side effects, or hypomagnesemia in the study population. All patients who completed follow-up were maintained on active prescriptions at the end of 52 weeks, demonstrating sustained tolerance and efficacy of therapy for LIDI Hyperkalemia in Intermittent Hemodialysis patients.

Discussion

The findings of our study highlight the substantial burden of hyperkalemia following the LIDI amongst Intermittent Hemodialysis patients, as well as the efficacy and safety of Patiromer in its treatment. Nearly half of the screened population (45.7%) exhibited pre-dialysis hyperkalemia, underscoring the significant prevalence of this complication during extended dialysis-free intervals. This high incidence of hyperkalemia following the LIDI is observed in comparison to international studies where the incidence is noted to be from 18% to 30% in different study cohorts.11 This relatively high prevalence may reflect local dietary and cultural practices that include potassium-rich foods during weekend social gatherings. Similar dietary patterns associated with increased potassium intake have also been reported in other regions of the world.

Patiromer demonstrated robust efficacy in lowering serum potassium levels, achieving a statistically significant reduction at every follow-up interval from week1 till week 52. Furthermore this trend was consistently observed against different demographic and clinical factors over duration of follow up. Previous studies have consistently demonstrated that Patiromer effectively lowers serum potassium in patients with chronic kidney disease and maintenance hemodialysis. Likewise, several multicenter studies and long-term real-world analyses confirmed sustained potassium control and favorable tolerability over prolonged follow-up.8,12–14 Our findings are consistent with these reports, demonstrating a rapid reduction in serum potassium during the first week of therapy, mean reduction of serum K by 1.57 mmol/L, followed by sustained potassium control throughout 52 weeks. Unlike several previous studies, our investigation provides prospective long-term real-world follow-up in patients with persistent hyperkalemia despite optimization of conventional dialysis care, thereby extending the available evidence in routine clinical practice.

Additionally, the absence of reported adverse effects such as gastrointestinal symptoms or hypomagnesemia is noteworthy, reflecting its excellent safety profile in our population. All patients adhered to the prescribed regimen, further supporting Patiromer as a well-tolerated and effective intervention for managing hyperkalemia in patients receiving Intermittent Hemodialysis. These results highlight the therapeutic potential of Patiromer in reducing the risks associated with hyperkalemia during dialysis-free LIDI in our patient population.

Compared with older potassium-binding resins such as sodium polystyrene sulfonate, Patiromer has a favorable safety profile in published studies, particularly with respect to gastrointestinal tolerability. SPS is associated with a higher incidence of severe GI complications, including colonic necrosis.15 This underscores Patiromer as a safer and more tolerable option for managing hyperkalemia. However, our study did not include a direct comparison with alternative potassium binders, and therefore no conclusions regarding comparative efficacy or safety can be drawn.

The gastrointestinal (GI) tolerance and side effect profile of Patiromer observed in our study align closely with findings from international research. For instance, a global pharmacovigilance analysis of Patiromer, which included over 45,000 patient-years of exposure, reported low rates of GI side effects, with constipation and diarrhea occurring in 6.9% and 3.5% of cases, respectively.13

The absence of hypomagnesemia in our study is also supported by international data, where the incidence of hypomagnesemia was reported to be 5.3% in clinical trials but significantly lower (0.16%) in real-world pharmacovigilance settings.13,16

In Saudi Arabia, recent studies have explored the clinical utility of Patiromer. A cost comparison analysis evaluated Patiromer versus sodium zirconium cyclosilicate for hyperkalemia treatment, concluding that Patiromer was associated with statistically significant reductions in hospitalization rates and severe edema, making it a cost-effective choice.17 Additionally, a budget impact analysis conducted in a tertiary care hospital demonstrated the economic advantage of incorporating Patiromer into routine practice, highlighting its potential to reduce healthcare expenditures while effectively managing hyperkalemia.14 Restricting treatment to the LIDI may reduce medication use; however, the economic impact of this strategy is likely to vary across healthcare systems and requires formal cost-effectiveness evaluation.

The study’s prospective design with a robust 52-week follow-up strengthens its validity and clinical relevance. High medication adherence was observed throughout follow-up. This was contributed via structured telephonic communication, contributing to consistent drug adherence and meaningful clinical outcomes. Importantly, dietary potassium restrictions were not implemented, allowing for a real-world assessment of Patiromer impact without confounding lifestyle modifications.

Although inclusion of a control group could have strengthened the study design, withholding potassium-lowering therapy from patients with hyperkalemia despite optimized standard of dialysis and dietary care was considered clinically concerning at our institution because of the established association between hyperkalemia and potentially life-threatening cardiac arrhythmias.

Comparisons with older potassium-binding agents such as SPS were intentionally excluded, given their known profile of severe adverse gastrointestinal events, including colonic necrosis. All participants who completed follow-up continued on active prescriptions, further supporting Patiromer utility as a safe and reliable long-term therapeutic option for managing dialysis-associated hyperkalemia.

Our study extends the available evidence by providing prospective 52-week real-world data in maintenance hemodialysis patients with persistent hyperkalemia despite optimized conventional management. Furthermore, our study reflects outcomes in a population with potassium-rich dietary habits and evaluates an individualized dosing strategy focused on the LIDI, providing clinically relevant evidence for long-term potassium management in patients undergoing Intermittent Hemodialysis.

Our dosing strategy focused on administration during the LIDI, may reduce medication burden while maintaining sustained potassium control. Future comparative studies are required to determine whether this approach offers clinical or economic advantages over alternative treatment regimens for Hyperkalemia in patients undergoing Intermittent Hemodialysis.

Limitations

Despite these promising results, our study has several limitations. As a single-center trial, the findings may not be generalizable to other populations or healthcare settings. Weekly telephone follow-up to confirm medication adherence may have positively influenced patient compliance and represents a potential source of performance bias. Although these contacts were intended primarily for medication adherence assessment and safety monitoring rather than behavioral counseling, their potential effect on treatment adherence and dietary habits cannot be excluded. Future pragmatic studies evaluating Patiromer within routine dialysis care without dedicated adherence telephone calls may help determine whether similar clinical benefits are achieved under usual clinical practice conditions. The exclusion of inpatient hemodialysis, patients on home dialysis, or peritoneal dialysis further limits the scope of the study. Comparative studies evaluating Patiromer against other potassium-binding agents or dietary interventions alone could provide additional insights into its clinical utility. Furthermore, a small sample size might limit the power of the study to statistically validate the findings, especially the side effect profile as compared to international studies. Future research should address these limitations by conducting multicenter trials with larger, more diverse patient cohorts.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our study reports Patiromer to be an effective option in managing hyperkalemia after the LIDI in Intermittent Hemodialysis patients who are known for their dietary and cultural traditions contributing to hyperkalemia. It has sustained effect and acceptable tolerance over a follow up duration of 52 weeks. Patiromer offers a safe and effective solution for reducing the burden of this life-threatening complication. Future multicenter randomized controlled trials with larger and more diverse patient populations are warranted to confirm these findings and compare Patiromer with alternative potassium-lowering strategies.

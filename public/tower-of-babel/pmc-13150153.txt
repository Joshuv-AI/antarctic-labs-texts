Letter to the Editor: Commentary on Validation of the Korean Moss Attention Rating Scale in Traumatic Brain Injury: Reliability and Validity (Korean J Neurotrauma 2026;22:24-35)
Authors: Hong Rye Kim
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

I would like to express my sincere appreciation for the recent publication by Jeong et al.,1) entitled “Validation of the Korean Moss Attention Rating Scale in Traumatic Brain Injury: Reliability and Validity.” The authors are commended for the rigorous translation and psychometric validation of the Korean version of the Moss Attention Rating Scale (K-MARS). This study introduces a clinically meaningful and ecologically valid observational tool for assessing attention-related behaviors in Korean patients with moderate-to-severe traumatic brain injury (msTBI), thereby addressing a critical gap in clinical diagnostics.

The study demonstrated excellent test–retest reliability (intraclass correlation coefficient=0.976)2) and strong internal consistency for the total K-MARS score (Cronbach’s α=0.870),3) supporting its reliability and applicability in subacute inpatient rehabilitation. The authors’ emphasis on observable attentional behaviors provides a valuable complement to conventional performance-based neuropsychological assessments.

To further clarify the clinical implications of this validation study, I would like to invite the authors’ comments on several points on several points concerning the scale's reliability, generalizability, and construct validity across diverse traumatic brain injury (TBI) phenotypes.

First, the authors noted that the “Initiation” (α=0.649) and “Sustained/Consistent Attention” (α=0.622) subscales demonstrated lower internal consistency than the total score. Although this likely reflects behavioral variability during the subacute recovery phase, coefficients within 0.60–0.69 fall near the lower threshold of acceptable reliability and require cautious interpretation. While the total K-MARS score appears robust, subscale scores may be more appropriate for descriptive or supplementary use rather than for directing targeted rehabilitation strategies.

Second, the cohort was predominantly male (80.4%), which, although consistent with TBI epidemiology, limits generalizability of the findings. Considering known sex-specific differences in neurobehavioral outcomes and recovery trajectories after TBI, the performance of the K-MARS in female patients deserves further evaluation. A more sex-balanced cohort is necessary to confirm that the scale maintains consistent discriminatory performance across the broader msTBI population.

Third, although test–retest reliability was excellent, both assessments were conducted by the same occupational therapist. This supports temporal stability but does not address inter-rater reliability among other clinicians, such as nurses or physical therapists, who frequently interact with patients. Given the intended use of the Moss Attention Rating Scale (MARS) as a rapid observational tool, evidence of inter-disciplinary reliability would strengthen its applicability in multidisciplinary neurorehabilitation settings.

Finally, the correlation analyses revealed that the “Sustained/Consistent Attention” subscale had largely non-significant associations with standardized neurocognitive measures. Unlike the broader correlations observed for the total score, this pattern raises questions regarding the alignment between behaviorally observed sustained attention and performance-based psychometric assessments. Further studies involving larger and more diverse cohorts are warranted to determine whether this discrepancy reflects a true construct-level distinction or methodological limitations. Clarification is essential for clinicians using these metrics to predict functional reintegration following neurosurgical intervention.

In conclusion, the validation of the K-MARS represents a significant advancement in the neurorehabilitation assessment toolkit in Korea. Nevertheless, addressing the variability in subscale performance and incorporating a wider range of raters in future prospective, multicenter studies will be essential for its robust clinical implementation. Such efforts will facilitate the confident integration of the K-MARS into the multidisciplinary management of patients recovering from msTBI.

Are parents receiving reliable information on nocturnal enuresis from YouTube?
Authors: Yunus Nas, Hilmi Bayırlı, Ali Gözüküçük, Basri Çakıroğlu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo evaluate the quality, reliability, source characteristics, and content completeness of YouTube videos related to pediatric nocturnal enuresis and to identify potential gaps in online patient education.Materials and methodsA cross-sectional analysis of YouTube videos was conducted using the search terms “nocturnal enuresis,” “bedwetting,” and “child bedwetting.” The first 100 eligible videos were included because they represent the content most likely to be encountered by users during routine searches. Video characteristics, including duration, views, likes, comments, uploader source, and content type, were recorded. Educational quality and reliability were assessed using the Global Quality Scale (GQS) and the modified DISCERN instrument. Content completeness was evaluated using a predefined scoring system covering definition, etiology, diagnostic evaluation, treatment options, behavioral interventions, and prognosis.ResultsA total of 100 videos were analyzed. The median video duration was 3.5 min (IQR: 2.1–9.1), and the median number of views was 1,192 (IQR: 148–4,964). The mean GQS, modified DISCERN, and content completeness scores were 3.22 ± 1.19, 2.69 ± 1.35, and 3.23 ± 1.66, respectively. Significant differences in educational quality, reliability, and content completeness were observed according to uploader source (all p < 0.001). Videos produced by healthcare professionals, academic institutions, and professional organizations generally demonstrated higher quality and more comprehensive content than commercial or non-professional sources. Definitions (78%), causes (74%), and treatment options (81%) were frequently discussed, whereas diagnostic evaluation (49%) and prognosis (38%) were substantially underrepresented. Behavioral interventions were mentioned in 67% of videos.ConclusionsYouTube videos on pediatric nocturnal enuresis demonstrate substantial variability in quality, reliability, and content completeness. Although videos originating from healthcare-related sources tended to provide more comprehensive and reliable information, important gaps remain, particularly regarding diagnostic evaluation and prognosis. These deficiencies may contribute to incomplete parental understanding and highlight the need for evidence-based digital educational resources and clinician-guided online health information.Keywords: Nocturnal enuresis, YouTube, Digital health, Patient education, Health information quality.

Introduction

Nocturnal Enuresis (NE), commonly referred to as bedwetting, is one of the most common functional urinary disorders in childhood and represents an important public health concern because of its psychological, social, and familial consequences [1]. According to the International Children’s Continence Society (ICCS), nocturnal enuresis is defined as intermittent urinary incontinence that occurs during sleep in children aged five years or older [2]. The prevalence of nocturnal enuresis is approximately 15–20% at the age of 5 years and decreases progressively with age to nearly 1–2% after 17 years of age [2, 3]. Although spontaneous resolution may occur over time, persistent nocturnal enuresis may negatively affect the self-esteem, emotional well-being, academic performance, and overall quality of life of both affected children and their caregivers [4, 5].

The prevalence of nocturnal enuresis varies according to age, sex, and geographical region, with reported rates ranging from 5% to 15% among school-aged children [1, 2]. This condition is more common in boys and is influenced by multiple factors, including delayed bladder maturation, nocturnal polyuria, sleep arousal dysfunction, genetic predisposition, and psychosocial stressors [1, 2]. Previous studies have also demonstrated that persistent nocturnal enuresis may adversely affect treatment outcomes and health-related quality of life in children [5]. Despite its high prevalence, nocturnal enuresis remains underdiagnosed and undertreated because of social stigma, embarrassment, misconceptions regarding its etiology, and delayed medical consultation by families [6].

Current management strategies for nocturnal enuresis include behavioral interventions, lifestyle modifications, alarm therapy, pharmacological treatments such as desmopressin, and family centered educational approaches [2, 7, 8]. Early recognition and evidence-based treatment are essential to reduce the psychosocial burden associated with this condition and improve treatment adherence and long-term outcomes [8]. Consequently, parental education plays a central role in the successful management of affected children with ADHD.

In recent years, digital media platforms have become major sources of health-related information for the general public [9]. Among these platforms, YouTube has emerged as one of the most widely accessed sources of medical information because of its free accessibility, audio-visual structure, and global reach [10]. Parents frequently search YouTube for information on pediatric conditions, including nocturnal enuresis, before consulting healthcare professionals. However, the open-access nature of YouTube allows the dissemination of inaccurate, incomplete, commercially biased, or non-evidence-based information, which may influence parental perceptions, treatment expectations, and healthcare decisions [10].

Previous studies evaluating YouTube content on pediatric and urological diseases have demonstrated considerable variability in educational quality, reliability, and scientific accuracy [11, 12]. In particular, studies investigating pediatric urology–related videos have reported deficiencies in evidence-based content, transparency, and comprehensiveness [12]. Toprak and Tokat previously evaluated YouTube videos related to nocturnal enuresis and reported substantial variability in video quality and reliability [13].

However, the digital health landscape has evolved considerably over recent years. The increasing influence of algorithm-driven content distribution, the expansion of commercially generated health information, and the growing reliance of parents on online educational resources highlight the need for updated evaluations [9, 10]. Furthermore, previous studies provided limited information regarding source-specific content completeness and the representation of evidence-based management strategies. Therefore, a contemporary and more granular assessment of video quality, reliability, uploader characteristics, and educational content is warranted.

Therefore, the present study aimed to evaluate the educational quality, reliability, and content completeness of YouTube videos related to nocturnal enuresis using the Global Quality Scale (GQS), modified DISCERN instrument, and a predefined content completeness scoring system. Additionally, this study sought to compare video quality according to uploader type and identify major informational deficiencies in publicly accessible video content.

Materials and methods

Study design

This cross-sectional study evaluated the educational quality, reliability, and content completeness of YouTube videos related to pediatric nocturnal enuresis. The study methodology was designed in accordance with previously published investigations evaluating health-related information on digital media platforms and social networking websites. Because only publicly accessible online content was analyzed and no human participants, patient data, or identifiable personal information were involved, institutional review board approval and informed consent were not required.

Search strategy and video selection

A systematic YouTube search was conducted in January 2026 using the following search terms:

“nocturnal enuresis”.

“nocturnal enuresis”.

“bedwetting”.

“child bedwetting”.

To minimize personalization bias and algorithm-related influences, all searches were performed using a newly initiated browser session in incognito/private browsing mode without logging into a personal YouTube account. The default YouTube relevance ranking was used to simulate the typical search behavior of parents and caregivers seeking health-related information online.

Consistent with previous YouTube-based health information studies, the first 100 videos identified through the search process were screened for eligibility. A formal sample size calculation was not performed because the study was designed as a descriptive cross-sectional content analysis rather than a hypothesis-driven clinical investigation. The first 100 videos were selected because they represent the content most likely to be encountered by users during routine searches, as previous research has shown that viewers rarely review a large number of search results.

Duplicate videos identified across search terms were removed before analysis.

Video assessment process

All included videos were independently evaluated by four clinicians with experience in pediatric lower urinary tract disorders, including two pediatricians, one pediatric surgeon, and one urologist.

Prior to the assessment process, all reviewers agreed upon the evaluation methodology and predefined scoring criteria. Each reviewer independently assessed all included videos using the Global Quality Scale (GQS), modified DISCERN instrument, and content completeness scoring system without knowledge of the ratings assigned by the other reviewers.

Following the independent evaluations, disagreements were reviewed during consensus meetings. Final scores used for statistical analysis were established by consensus among the reviewers. Because individual reviewer scores were not retained after consensus scoring, formal inter-rater reliability statistics could not be calculated.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics software (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median with interquartile range (IQR), according to data distribution. Categorical variables were reported as frequencies, percentages, and 95% confidence intervals where appropriate.

Normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test.

Comparisons among uploader categories were performed using the Kruskal–Wallis test for non-normally distributed continuous variables and the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables. When the Kruskal–Wallis test demonstrated a statistically significant overall difference, pairwise post-hoc comparisons were performed using Dunn’s test with Bonferroni correction.

Effect sizes were reported using epsilon-squared for Kruskal–Wallis analyses and Cramer’s V for categorical comparisons. Correlations between video popularity metrics (views, likes, comments, and duration) and quality indicators (GQS, modified DISCERN, and content completeness scores) were evaluated using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient.

Statistical significance was defined as a two-sided p-value < 0.05.

Qualitative observations

Qualitative assessment revealed notable differences in presentation style and informational depth according to uploader source. Videos produced by healthcare professionals and academic institutions were more likely to provide evidence-based explanations, discuss diagnostic evaluation, emphasize behavioral interventions, and encourage professional medical consultation. In contrast, commercial/promotional and alternative therapy videos frequently focused on specific products or limited treatment approaches and often omitted important information regarding diagnostic assessment and long-term prognosis.

Discussion

The present study evaluated the educational quality, reliability, source characteristics, and content completeness of YouTube videos related to pediatric nocturnal enuresis. Several important findings emerged. First, substantial heterogeneity was observed in the quality and completeness of publicly available video content. Second, uploader source was strongly associated with educational quality and content coverage. Third, important evidence-based topics, particularly diagnostic evaluation, behavioral interventions, and prognosis, were frequently underrepresented. Finally, video popularity was only weakly associated with educational quality, suggesting that highly viewed videos are not necessarily the most informative or reliable.

The increasing use of digital media platforms has significantly changed how parents seek healthcare information for common pediatric conditions [15, 16] Because nocturnal enuresis is frequently associated with embarrassment, stigma, and psychosocial distress, many families initially seek information online before consulting healthcare professionals [6]. Consequently, the quality and accuracy of online educational resources have become increasingly important. Inaccurate or incomplete information may contribute to delayed medical consultation, unrealistic treatment expectations, poor adherence to evidence-based interventions, and increased parental anxiety. Therefore, evaluating the quality and completeness of online educational resources has direct clinical relevance [10–12].

A previous study by Toprak and Tokat evaluated YouTube videos related to nocturnal enuresis and reported considerable variability in educational quality and reliability [13]. Our findings are generally consistent with their observations, confirming that online video content remains heterogeneous and that educational quality is highly dependent on the source of information. However, the present study provides a contemporary reassessment of the current YouTube environment approximately five years after the earlier investigation. During this period, the volume of health-related content has increased substantially, and commercial as well as algorithm-driven content has become increasingly prominent. In addition, our study extends previous work by incorporating a structured content completeness assessment and by evaluating the relationship between uploader source and specific educational domains such as diagnostic evaluation, behavioral interventions, and prognosis. These analyses provide a more granular understanding of the strengths and deficiencies of currently available YouTube content [13].

The overall educational quality and reliability of nocturnal enuresis videos were moderate. These findings are consistent with previous YouTube-based studies in pediatric urology and digital health research, which have frequently reported moderate-to-poor educational quality [11–13]. Similar studies evaluating online video content related to overactive bladder, urinary stone disease, prostate cancer, and pediatric urological disorders have demonstrated that highly viewed videos do not necessarily provide accurate or comprehensive information [11, 12, 17–21]. Our findings further support the growing concern that video popularity and educational quality are not always correlated on social media.

The deficiencies identified in YouTube videos appear to reflect a broader challenge affecting digital educational resources on nocturnal enuresis. Mohile et al. demonstrated considerable variability in the readability and quality of patient education materials, highlighting that many resources are difficult for caregivers to understand despite addressing clinically important topics [22]. Similarly, Fung et al. reported substantial limitations in the quality, readability, and accuracy of internet-based resources related to nocturnal enuresis [23]. Furthermore, Whitley and Kieran found important deficiencies in the comprehensiveness and accuracy of publicly available online information regarding bedwetting [24]. Taken together, these studies suggest that shortcomings in educational quality are not unique to YouTube but are widespread across multiple forms of digital health information. Our findings reinforce these concerns and indicate that important information regarding diagnosis, prognosis, and behavioral management remains inconsistently represented across online resources [22–24].

One of the most important findings of the present study was the strong association between uploader source and educational quality. Healthcare-related and academic sources achieved significantly higher GQS, modified DISCERN, and content completeness scores than commercial/promotional and non-professional sources. Furthermore, healthcare-related videos were substantially more likely to discuss diagnostic evaluation, behavioral interventions, and long-term prognosis. For example, diagnostic evaluation was addressed in approximately two-thirds of healthcare-related videos but in only a small minority of commercial/promotional videos. Similarly, prognosis and long-term outcomes were frequently discussed by healthcare professionals but were often omitted by commercial sources. These findings suggest that source credibility remains an important determinant of educational quality in online health information.

The underrepresentation of diagnostic evaluation and prognosis has important clinical implications. Current International Children’s Continence Society recommendations emphasize the importance of identifying associated lower urinary tract symptoms, constipation, sleep disorders, and other comorbidities during the assessment of children with nocturnal enuresis [2]. However, fewer than half of the analyzed videos adequately addressed diagnostic evaluation. Likewise, prognosis and spontaneous resolution rates were frequently omitted despite their relevance for parental counseling and expectation management. Such informational gaps may contribute to misconceptions regarding disease severity, treatment necessity, and anticipated outcomes [2, 6, 8].

Behavioral interventions constitute the cornerstone of first-line management for nocturnal enuresis according to current guideline recommendations [2, 7, 8]. Although behavioral strategies were more frequently discussed than diagnostic evaluation or prognosis, approximately one-third of videos still failed to address these measures. Given that behavioral interventions are often recommended before pharmacological treatment, inadequate coverage of these topics may limit the educational value of otherwise informative videos.

Another notable finding was the presence of commercially oriented and non-evidence-based content. Commercial/promotional videos frequently emphasized specific treatment approaches while providing limited information regarding diagnosis, prognosis, or comprehensive management strategies. Similarly, some alternative therapy videos promoted interventions that were not supported by established guidelines. Although the present study did not directly evaluate clinical outcomes, exposure to incomplete or potentially misleading information may influence parental perceptions and treatment decisions [10–13, 22–24].

Interestingly, video popularity demonstrated only weak associations with educational quality. Longer videos tended to provide more comprehensive and reliable information, whereas highly viewed videos were not necessarily more accurate or educational. This finding is consistent with previous studies evaluating YouTube content in pediatric urology and other medical disciplines and highlights a fundamental limitation of relying on popularity metrics as indicators of information quality.

Despite these limitations, YouTube continues to provide important opportunities for patient and parental education. Several high-quality videos prepared by healthcare professionals successfully combined accessible language and evidence-based medical information. Interestingly, some of these high-quality videos demonstrated relatively limited audience reach despite providing comprehensive educational content. This finding suggests a mismatch between educational quality and public visibility on YouTube. Therefore, rather than discouraging the use of online platforms, efforts should focus on increasing the availability and visibility of accurate, guideline-based, parent-friendly educational content. Standardized recommendations for the preparation of medical educational videos may further improve the quality and reliability of publicly accessible health information in the future.

Strengths

The present study has several notable strengths. First, it provides an updated evaluation of YouTube content related to nocturnal enuresis within the contemporary digital health environment, allowing comparison with previously published studies and reflecting the current influence of algorithm-driven health information. Second, educational quality, reliability, and content completeness were assessed using multiple complementary instruments, including the Global Quality Scale, modified DISCERN instrument, and a structured content completeness score. Third, videos were independently evaluated by a multidisciplinary panel consisting of two pediatricians, one pediatric surgeon, and one urologist. Finally, the study extends previous literature by examining content completeness according to uploader source and identifying specific educational gaps across different categories of content providers.

Limitations and future directions

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the cross-sectional design reflects YouTube content only at the time of analysis, whereas online content is continuously evolving. Second, only English-language videos were included, potentially limiting the generalizability of the findings to other populations and cultural settings. Third, viewer engagement metrics such as watch time, audience retention, and actual knowledge acquisition could not be evaluated because these data are not publicly available.

Fourth, although all videos were independently evaluated by four reviewers (two pediatricians, one pediatric surgeon, and one urologist) using predefined criteria, some degree of subjective interpretation is unavoidable in video-based content assessment. Disagreements were resolved through consensus meetings. Because the original individual reviewer ratings were not retained after the consensus process, formal inter-rater reliability statistics could not be calculated.

Fifth, the JAMA benchmark criteria were not included in the assessment framework. Although the Global Quality Scale and modified DISCERN instrument provide valuable information regarding educational quality and reliability, the omission of the JAMA benchmark criteria prevented a formal evaluation of authorship transparency, attribution, disclosure, and currency.

Finally, this study evaluated the quality and completeness of online information but did not assess whether exposure to higher-quality videos translates into improved parental knowledge, healthcare-seeking behavior, treatment adherence, or clinical outcomes. Future prospective studies are needed to investigate the real-world impact of digital educational resources on families affected by nocturnal enuresis.

Conclusion

YouTube videos related to nocturnal enuresis demonstrate considerable variability in educational quality, reliability, and content completeness. Although videos produced by physicians and healthcare institutions generally provide more comprehensive and evidence-based information, important deficiencies remain in diagnostic counseling, behavioral management, and long-term guidance. These findings highlight the need for greater involvement of healthcare professionals in the development of high-quality digital educational resources to improve the information available to parents and caregivers.

Hotspots and research trends on pterygium surgery
Authors: Gaoming Zhang, Ting Zhang, Mengting Xue, Hongya Tian, Yaxiang Miao, Yuemeng Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Purpose:This study aims to conduct a bibliometric analysis of global research on surgical interventions for pterygium, a condition characterized by fibrovascular growth on the cornea that can cause visual impairment.Methods:This study employed bibliometric analysis using data from the Web of Science Core Collection database. Literature published from inception to 2024 was analyzed with the R package “bibliometrix,” as well as CiteSpace and VOSviewer.Results:By July 2024, a total of 1139 publications were identified, revealing a significant rise in studies since the 1990s. China and the United States were the most productive countries in this field. The Singapore National Eye Centre and Tel Aviv University emerged as the leading institutions in terms of publication output. The journal Cornea published the most articles, followed by the British Journal of Ophthalmology. The most impactful author was Tan DTH, followed by Kheirkhah A. The top 3 keywords identified were “excision,” “mitomycin-C,” and “recurrent pterygium.” Cluster analysis revealed 5 key clusters: surgical and pharmacological treatments, excision and graft transplantation methods, molecular mechanisms, clinical complications and management, and disease prevalence with anatomical context. Keyword bursts highlighted growing interest in “pathogenesis,” “prevalence,” and “efficacy.”Conclusions:This study presents the first bibliometric analysis of research on pterygium surgery. Future directions in this area may focus on targeted surgical strategies to enhance treatment efficacy.

1. Introduction

Pterygium is characterized by the abnormal proliferation of conjunctival tissue that encroaches upon the corneal surface, resulting in a distinctive triangular fibrovascular growth.[1] The primary risk factors associated with pterygium include exposure to ultraviolet (UV) light, advancing age, and male gender.[2,3] This condition exhibits a global prevalence, with particularly high incidences reaching up to 22% in tropical and subtropical regions, where the prevalence markedly increases with greater exposure to UV radiation.[4] Patients commonly report ocular discomfort, visual impairment, and aesthetic concerns attributable to pterygium growth, which significantly affects their quality of life.[5] The initial management of pterygium typically includes conservative measures, such as applying lubricants and wearing sunglasses. Surgical intervention may be considered if the patient seeks relief from symptoms, improvement in vision, or enhancements in appearance.[6] Since the 19th century, pterygium surgery has evolved from basic excision techniques to more advanced procedures, including autologous conjunctival and amniotic membrane transplantations (AMTs).[2] Currently, the gold standard for pterygium surgery combines excision with conjunctival autograft, which is associated with a lower recurrence rate of 1.9% to 8%.[7]

Despite this long surgical history, the overall evolution and research focus of pterygium surgery have not been systematically examined. Previous bibliometric studies have mainly addressed the broader pterygium field. Wang et al mapped publications from 2000 to 2019, identifying “surgical therapeutic techniques and adjuvants” as one cluster but without tracing how specific surgical approaches emerged or evolved.[8] Chu et al offered a comprehensive narrative review summarizing epidemiology, pathogenesis, and management. Although it affirmed excision with conjunctival autograft transplantation (CAG) as the preferred technique and discussed adjuvant therapies such as mitomycin-C (MMC) and fibrin glue, it did not employ bibliometric analysis to quantify research trends.[2] Collectively, these studies provide valuable overviews yet leave the micro-level evolution of surgical interventions underexplored.

Bibliometrics serves as a methodological tool for evaluating research activities through the quantitative analysis of publications. It not only highlights research hotspots within specific disciplines but also identifies key contributors and their influence, helping researchers quickly grasp the overall landscape of their field. In the healthcare sector, bibliometrics has been widely used to assess the research dynamics surrounding various diseases and technologies.[9] It provides scholars with a comprehensive overview, identifies knowledge gaps, and generates new research ideas.[10] Building on prior global analyses, the present study focuses specifically on surgical interventions for pterygium to clarify how operative strategies have been adopted, refined, and disseminated across time and regions, complementing existing literature with a focused, data-driven perspective.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Search strategies and data extraction

We conducted the literature search on the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC), which has extensive coverage of more than 12,000 scholarly journals and provides the most comprehensive and reliable bibliometric analysis.[11] The search strategy involved using a combination of keywords to retrieve studies related to pterygium surgery, including terms such as: TS=(pterygium OR pterygiums OR pterygia) AND TS=(surgery OR excision),[12,13] covering publications from 1939 to July 4, 2024. For this study, the publication language was set to English, and only articles were considered among various document types. To ensure consistency and avoid deviations from database renewal, we performed the literature retrieval on a single day, July 4, 2024. All data were collected in text format, including the number of publications and citations, titles, author information, institutions, countries/regions, keywords, and journals, for further bibliometric analysis.

As this study is a bibliometric analysis of published literature, ethical approval from an institutional review board or patient informed consent was not applicable.

2.2. Statistical analysis

We extracted relevant data from the retrieved literature bibliographies and then utilized Microsoft Excel to identify and calculate bibliometric indicators. All records were exported as plain text files and Excel spreadsheets for bibliometric and visualization analysis.

During the process of visual analysis, we utilized 3 powerful bibliometric analysis tools to comprehensively analyze academic data. These tools are VOSviewer (version 1.6.20; Centre for Science and Technology Studies, Leiden University), CiteSpace (version 6.3.R1; Chaomei Chen, Drexel University), and the R (version 4.3.3; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, https://www.r-project.org/) package “bibliometrix” (Aria and Cuccurullo, University of Naples Federico II). VOSviewer is a versatile software tool that plays a critical role in mapping institutional collaboration, author collaboration, coauthorship, citation, and co-citation.[14] Through VOSviewer, we could visualize and explore complex collaboration and relationship networks within the academic domain, gaining deeper insights into the interconnections among authors, institutions, and publications. CiteSpace was used for keyword burst analysis, setting parameters for time slicing from January 1994 to July 2024. When the node type was keywords, the threshold (top N in each slice) = 5, pruning = pathfinder + pruning merged networks.

Journal Citation Reports (JCR) categorizes journals into 4 quartiles based on their impact factors (IFs) within their respective academic fields: Q1 (top 25%), Q2 (25%–50%), Q3 (50%–75%), and Q4 (bottom 25%). This study utilized the latest data from 2023 for both IF and JCR quartiles. On this basis, we employed the H-index, M-index, and G-index to quantify the academic impact of individuals and journals, respectively. The H-index is the most widely used bibliometric metric and is defined as the number of papers “h” cited at least “h” times.[15,16] The G-index is introduced as an improvement of the H-index to measure the global citation performance of a set of articles,[17] whereas the M-index is the average number of citations received by the literature that make up the H-index.[18] In this study, the H-index, M-index, and G-index of each author were obtained from WoSCC.

3. Results

3.1. An overview of publications

This bibliometric analysis of research on pterygium surgery, spanning 1939 to 2024, identified 1139 articles. The flowchart of data screening is shown in Figure 1A. Our investigation showed that 4137 authors from 166 institutions across 50 countries/regions contributed to the production of the 1139 manuscripts. International collaborations were noted in 10.97% of the studies. The documents included 1579 unique author keywords and 13,179 references, with an average citation count of 21.52 per document. The average age of the documents was 14.1 years, indicating a blend of historical and recent research.

3.2. Publication outputs and growing trends

The annual publications from 1939 to 2024 generally showed an increasing trend (Fig. 1B). The overall trend has been steady with a gradual increase, with the first burst of growth occurring in 2001, when more than 30 studies in the field were produced for the first time. An upward trend has been observed since 2008, specifically, the number increased from 31 documents in 2007 to 48 documents in 2008, with a growth rate exceeding 50%. The field peaked in 2013 and 2017, respectively, both producing 62 documents focusing on the overall study of pterygium surgery. Over the subsequent 10 years, the figure fluctuated around 50 documents.

The most frequently cited publications in this field are as follows. The article titled “Serious complications of topical mitomycin-C after pterygium surgery” was published in Ophthalmology (IF = 13.1) in 1992 and has been cited 532 times.[19] The second most-cited article is “Trabeculectomy with mitomycin. A comparative study with fluorouracil” published in Archives of Ophthalmology in 1991 and has accrued a total of 449 citations.[20] The third most-cited article is “Effect of pterygium morphology on pterygium recurrence in a controlled trial comparing conjunctival autografting with bare sclera excision” published in Archives of Ophthalmology in 1997 and has received 360 citations.[21]

3.3. Analysis of journals

The 1139 records retrieved were published in 212 journals from 1939 to 2024. The general characteristics of the 20 most influential journals that published literature on pterygium surgery research over the past 80 years are presented in Table S1, Supplemental Digital Content 1. These journals published 662 studies, which accounted for 58.1% of all retrieved publications. Cornea (IF = 1.9; H-index = 37; total publication [TP] = 176) ranked first in both TPs and H-index. It was followed by British Journal of Ophthalmology (IF = 3.7; H-index = 32; TP = 51) and American Journal of Ophthalmology (IF = 4.1; H-index = 21; TP = 47), which also showed strong performance in terms of TP and citation impact. Despite its relatively lower TPs (TP = 42), Ophthalmology had the highest impact factor (IF = 13.1) and was the most-cited journal with a total of 3670 citations, ranking first in total citation (TC) and third in H-index. Cornea followed closely with 2659 citations, while British Journal of Ophthalmology (TC = 2301) and American Journal of Ophthalmology (TC = 1758) ranked third and fourth, respectively. Moreover, the top-ranking journals were predominantly classified in the top 2 quartiles of the JCR, reflecting their overall academic influence within the ophthalmology field. For instance, both British Journal of Ophthalmology and American Journal of Ophthalmology are categorized in Q1, further underscoring their prominent position in ophthalmological research.

3.4. Analysis of the countries

A total of 50 countries published literature in this field. The top 20 productive countries generated 963 pieces of literature, accounting for 84.5% of the literature worldwide. China led the field with 160 pieces of literature, of which 137 were single-country publication and 23 were multiple-country publication (MCP), reflecting an MCP ratio of 14.4%. The United States followed closely with 157 pieces of literature, including 139 single-country publications and 18 MCPs (MCP ratio = 11.5%), and amassed the highest number of citations (4932). Turkey, ranking third with 107 pieces of literature, had a relatively lower MCP ratio of 2.8%, indicating limited international collaboration compared to its total output. In terms of research impact, Japan demonstrated strong performance with 51 pieces of literature and an MCP ratio of 5.9%, ranking third in TCs (1851; Fig. 2A and Table S2, Supplemental Digital Content 2). In addition, 39 countries with a minimum of 3 documents in coauthorship were analyzed with VOSviewer (Fig. 2B and Table S3, Supplemental Digital Content 3), and the top 5 nations were the United States (91), China (28), Australia (26), Germany (21), and the United Kingdom (19). In addition to working with these countries, the United States, as a central hub of cooperation, has close ties with Israel, India, South Korea, and Canada, among others. China also works with South Korea, Malaysia, and other countries. Australia also has strong research links with Singapore and Japan.

3.7. Analysis of the keywords

The keyword co-occurrence network analysis, which identified 229 keywords with a minimum of 5 occurrences, was performed using “Keywords Plus” provided by VOSviewer.

The top 20 keywords in link strength from the available data are shown in Table S5, Supplemental Digital Content 5. The most frequently occurring keywords, all of which demonstrated link strength over 600, were “excision,” “mitomycin-c,” “recurrent pterygium,” “complications,” and “transplantation.” The top keywords specifically related to surgical techniques were “conjunctival autograft,” “amniotic membrane transplantation,” and “fibrin glue.” The keyword co-occurrence network revealed both temporal trends of high-frequency keywords and research hotspots. For the temporal trends of high-frequency keywords, between 2006 and 2008, “trabeculectomy,” “complication,” “eye drop,” and “topical mitomycin” appeared earlier, followed by the frequent appearance of words such as “excision,” “mitomycin-c,” “conjunctival autograft,” and “management,” and by 2014, keywords such as “prevalence,” “pathogenesis,” and “cancer” gradually increased (Fig. 5A). For the research hotspots, the red cluster focused on surgical procedures and pharmacological adjuncts, including key terms such as “5-fluorouracil,” “intraoperative mitomycin,” “ulceration,” and “trabeculectomy.” The green cluster revolved around excision and graft-based transplantation strategies, highlighting keywords including “excision,” “transplantation,” “limbal-conjunctival,” “amniotic membrane transplantatation,” and “mitomycin-c.” The blue cluster was centered on molecular and cellular mechanisms, featuring terms like “angiogenesis,” “endothelial growth-factor,” “pathogenesis,” and “topical bevacizumab.” The yellow cluster related to complications and clinical management, including keywords like “complications,” “eye drops,” “postoperative instillation,” and “irradiation.” The purple cluster dealt with prevalence, anatomical targets, and procedural context. It includes keywords such as “conjunctival-autograft-transpl,” “bare sclera,” and “corneal” (Fig. 5B).

4. Discussion

4.1. Research trend of pterygium surgery

In this study, the analysis found a general trend of increasing research on pterygium surgery during the period of 1939 to 2024. China was the most productive country and made major contributions. Singapore National Eye Centre is the most active institution. Cornea was the most widely published journal. Tan DTH was the most-cited author.

The overall trend first showed a notable increase around 2001. This rise can be attributed to several converging factors. Technological and clinical advancements played a major role, particularly the introduction and refinement of MMC and β-radiotherapy, which significantly reduced recurrence rates and changed the standard of surgical care.[22] In addition, conjunctival and limbal autografting techniques were increasingly adopted during this period, offering better outcomes and becoming the basis of many subsequent comparative studies.[23] Notably, 2 of the most-cited publications identified – Rubinfeld et al and Tan et al – represent pivotal clinical studies that profoundly influenced the modern practice of pterygium surgery. Rubinfeld et al first delineated the severe complications associated with excessive or prolonged use of topical MMC, prompting the establishment of strict dosage and exposure guidelines that remain standard today.[19] Tan et al provided robust randomized-trial evidence demonstrating that conjunctival autografting markedly reduces recurrence compared with bare-sclera excision.[21] These landmark investigations not only defined current surgical safety parameters and best practices but also stimulated subsequent research into adjuvant optimization and graft-related innovations. Their continued prominence among highly cited works underscores how bibliometric influence can parallel clinical significance when foundational trials serve as enduring methodological and ethical references within the field. More critically, research priorities have shifted over time. Early efforts emphasized technique optimization to prevent recurrence, but in the 2000s, attention expanded to molecular pathogenesis, including angiogenesis, oxidative stress, and UV-induced DNA damage.[24] In recent years, researchers have increasingly explored preventive strategies, quality-of-life outcomes, and environmental risk mitigation, indicating a growing interdisciplinary interest in both clinical and public health domains.[25]

In terms of countries, China and the United States are the primary contributors to pterygium surgery research, with 160 and 157 pieces of literature, respectively. In China, the regional distribution of pterygium burden appears to be strongly influenced by UV radiation exposure. A nationwide meta-analysis found that southern provinces such as Guangxi, Guangdong, and Hainan – characterized by low latitudes and high annual sunshine hours – have significantly higher prevalence rates compared to northern regions.[26] For example, a field survey in Hainan reported a prevalence of 7.86%, notably above the national average, which was attributed to geographic and climatic conditions.[27] In addition, western high-altitude regions such as Qinghai also demonstrate elevated risk due to increased UV intensity at high elevations, despite being at higher latitudes.[28] Together, these findings support that both low-latitude subtropical zones and high-altitude plateaus in China contribute to a high disease burden, which in turn may explain the country’s substantial research output on pterygium. A higher disease burden may, in part, motivate targeted clinical research. In addition, China’s “National Plan for Prevention and Treatment of Blindness (2016–2020)” emphasized the need for enhanced ophthalmologic care and research investment in underserved regions, likely contributing to a rise in institutional publication output.[29] The United States has sustained high research productivity in this field, supported by robust national infrastructure, including the National Eye Institute and long-term initiatives such as Vision 2020. Moreover, studies have noted that UV exposure varies significantly across the United States, with southern states like Florida and Texas experiencing higher incidence rates due to their lower latitude and outdoor occupational patterns.[25] The presence of strong academic medical centers and federal research funding further accelerates output.

4.2. Research hotspots and frontiers in pterygium surgery

Keyword co-occurrence analysis aids researchers in exploring the distribution of topics and emerging research trends within specific disciplines.[30] In the early period from 2006 to 2008, keywords such as “trabeculectomy,” “complication,” “eye drop,” and “topical mitomycin” appeared frequently, indicating a strong focus on improving traditional surgical techniques and managing postoperative complications. During this period, studies aimed to optimize treatment strategies to reduce recurrence rates through surgical innovations and adjunctive therapies. For example, Young et al compared intraoperative MMC application with simple excision and found that MMC could significantly reduce recurrence rates.[31] However, MMC use was associated with risks such as scleral thinning and corneal complications, leading researchers to explore safer concentrations and methods.[32] From 2008 to 2014, keywords such as “excision,” “mitomycin-c,” “conjunctival autograft,” and “management” became more prominent. Pandey et al found that combining excision with conjunctival autografts and postoperative MMC application led to significantly lower recurrence rates compared to single-modality treatments, without a notable increase in complications.[33] Furthermore, the frequent appearance of “management” indicates that research was not only focused on surgical outcomes but also on postoperative care and long-term patient monitoring to optimize treatment efficacy. After 2014, keywords like “prevalence,” “pathogenesis,” and “cancer” became more prevalent. For instance, Ginger-Eke et al highlighted that pterygium may involve complex pathophysiological mechanisms similar to tumorigenesis, including UV-induced gene mutations, p53 tumor suppressor gene alterations, and abnormal angiogenesis.[34] Some research further suggested that targeting the MDM2-p53 interaction might be a potential strategy to inhibit pterygium progression, indicating a growing interest in molecular-targeted therapies.[2] Despite these changes in research focus over time, the link between core keywords has been strongly bound, such as “resection,” “recurrence,” and “conjunctival autograf.” While “excision” has always been the top priority in treatment strategies, the treatment of “recurrent pterygium” has always been challenging due to its recurrent invasiveness and its involvement with the deeper tissues of the eye.[35] The combination of excision with conjunctival grafts has proven to be very effective in reducing recurrence rates and maintaining long-term surgical success.[36]

4.3. Cluster analysis

The red cluster centers on surgical procedures complemented by pharmacological adjuncts aimed at improving outcomes and reducing complications. This cluster includes key terms such as “5-fluorouracil,” “intraoperative mitomycin,” “ulceration,” and “trabeculectomy.” This cluster reflects research focused on the use of 5-fluorouracil, an antimetabolite agent that inhibits fibroblast proliferation and scar formation, thereby reducing pterygium recurrence after surgery. Although less commonly used than MMC, 5-fluorouracil has shown promise in selected cases, especially where balancing efficacy with lower toxicity is critical.[37] The appearance of trabeculectomy in this cluster is also meaningful: although it is primarily a glaucoma procedure, it represents one of the earliest ophthalmic contexts in which MMC and 5-fluorouracil were systematically studied for their antifibrotic effects.[38] Subsequent pterygium research adopted and adapted these insights, especially regarding conjunctival wound healing and scarring control, thereby bridging pharmacological principles across surgical disciplines. Overall, this cluster encapsulates efforts to enhance surgical success in pterygium by integrating adjunctive pharmacologic strategies originally refined in other ophthalmic surgeries, while minimizing complications such as ulceration.

The green cluster focuses on excision combined with graft-based transplantation techniques, including “excision,” “transplantation,” “limbal-conjunctival,” “amniotic membrane transplantation,” and “mitomycin-C.” AMT is recognized for its biological properties, such as anti-inflammatory, anti-fibrotic, and pro-epithelialization effects, which promote healing of the ocular surface, especially in complex or recurrent pterygium cases.[39] Despite these advantages, AMT tends to have higher recurrence rates compared to conjunctival autografts and is often reserved for cases with insufficient conjunctiva or large defects.[40] The cluster further reflects integrative surgical strategies, combining transplantation with MMC to optimize outcomes.

The blue cluster focuses on molecular and cellular mechanisms underlying pterygium pathogenesis, with terms such as “angiogenesis,” “endothelial growth factor,” “pathogenesis,” and “topical bevacizumab.” Research highlights the role of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) in promoting neovascularization central to pterygium growth. Topical anti-VEGF therapies, such as bevacizumab, have been explored as potential treatments to inhibit angiogenesis and reduce recurrence rates.[41] This cluster represents the translational frontier linking molecular insights with novel pharmacologic interventions.

The yellow cluster relates to postoperative complications and clinical management, featuring keywords like “complications,” “eye drops,” “postoperative instillation,” and “irradiation.” This cluster focuses on improving postoperative care to prevent infection, inflammation, and recurrence, with strategies involving corticosteroids, antibiotics, lubricants, and adjunctive irradiation therapies to optimize healing and patient care.[42]

The purple cluster centers on graft fixation techniques, anatomical considerations, and prevalence, with keywords including “conjunctival-autograft-transpl,” “bare sclera,” and “corneal.” CAG is well-established as the most effective method for reducing recurrence by restoring ocular surface integrity.[43] Within this cluster, the emergence of fibrin glue as a sutureless graft fixation method represents a significant innovation, reducing operative time and postoperative inflammation while maintaining graft stability.[44] Nonetheless, the higher cost and limited availability of fibrin glue, alongside the need for long-term efficacy data, remain challenges prompting further research.

4.4. Burst analysis

Based on the burst keyword analysis, since 2010, keywords such as “angiogenesis” (2010–2015) and “recurrence” (2014–2017) indicate a deepening investigation into the underlying pathological mechanisms, particularly the role of angiogenesis in recurrence. It has been found that recurrent pterygium is often accompanied by high levels of angiogenesis, suggesting that inhibition of angiogenesis may be an effective strategy to reduce recurrence rates.[34] Subsequently, the bursts of keywords such as “pathogenesis” (2016–2021) and “prevalence” (2012–2022) indicate a further expansion of research focus towards a deeper understanding of the disease’s etiology and epidemiology. Rezvan et al reported a global prevalence of 12%, with the highest rate in high-UV regions such as China (53%).[45] Notably, “efficacy” remains a strong keyword in 2024, indicating that despite the growing focus on disease mechanisms and epidemiology, evaluating treatment outcomes remains a key area of future research. Overall, the burst keyword analysis reveals a shift from treatment techniques to a focus on pathogenesis and long-term management strategies, highlighting the potential for future research in both therapeutic efficacy and disease etiology.

The burst of keywords like “pathogenesis” and “prevalence” highlights their value for future research in understanding pterygium’s etiology and global distribution. Studies have shown that genetic and environmental factors, such as UV radiation, are crucial in disease development. For instance, research on CTLA4 gene expression has indicated a significant association with pterygium enlargement.[46] Moreover, epidemiological studies report a global prevalence ranging from 0.07% to 53%, depending on UV exposure and demographic factors.[45] Future research may focus on integrating genetic profiling and regional data to identify high-risk populations and develop targeted treatment strategies.

Notably, the keyword “efficacy” has consistently burst until 2024, indicating that the current research field is increasingly focusing on the long-term effectiveness and safety of different surgical methods. Researchers found that combining MMC with autologous conjunctival transplantation enhanced its long-term efficacy, reducing recurrence. However, its application is limited in resource-constrained regions due to complexity and cost.[31] A systematic evaluation and meta-analysis summarized the efficacy of various surgical procedures for pterygium, and they found that autologous conjunctival grafts and AMT were the most effective in decreasing the recurrence rate, and patient satisfaction was high.[47] With the deepening of these studies, “efficacy” has not only become a crucial indicator for evaluating emerging surgical techniques for pterygium but also reflects the future emphasis on preventing surgical complications, recurrence, and precision medicine in this field, guiding the direction for future researchers.

Recent empirical studies corroborate several of the hotspots indicated by our bibliometric clustering. For example, adjunctive anti-VEGF therapy has been shown in meta-analysis to nearly halve recurrence rates when combined with surgery (e.g., bare sclera or conjunctival autograft), providing strong support for the “anti-angiogenesis” burst we observed.[48] Similarly, the increasing prevalence of graft fixation technique research (e.g., comparing fibrin glue vs. sutures) is well-grounded: meta-analyses reveal that fibrin glue results in lower postoperative inflammation, less pain, and comparable or lower recurrence than suturing, particularly in conjunction with AMT.[49] Moreover, long-term outcome data from a center in the Philippines (2013–2023) comparing conjunctival autograft, amniotic membrane graft, and MMC adjunct techniques show real differences in recurrence and complication profiles across methods.[50] These findings align closely with our bibliometric detection of “excision + CAG + adjuncts” as a central cluster and validate that recent research is moving toward optimizing technique combinations rather than single-modality interventions. In light of these, our bibliometric mapping can impact future studies by guiding where the knowledge gaps are most pronounced. For example, although anti-VEGF shows efficacy, geographical differences (e.g., less effect in certain populations) and long-term safety remain understudied. In addition, studies comparing technique combinations over longer follow-up (5–10 years) remain rare. Our study helps point out underrepresented but emerging topics such as gene-based predictors of recurrence, bioengineered graft materials, and minimally invasive graft fixation, which would benefit from rigorous randomized controlled trials or large multicenter cohort studies.

Future research should focus on enhancing pterygium surgery efficacy and exploring novel therapies. As the incidence of pterygium varies significantly across different geographical areas, this provides an opportunity to develop more targeted prevention and treatment strategies.[45] Research may also focus on integrating genetic profiles and regional data to identify high-risk populations and develop targeted prevention strategies. In addition, enhancing international cooperation, data sharing, and multicenter trials will boost research universality and reliability, offering wider, more effective treatments globally.

4.5. Strengths and limitations

This study is the first bibliometric analysis focusing on the field of pterygium surgery. A major strength of our study is its analysis spanning multiple years, aiming to identify relevant literature in this field. However, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, citation counts were used as a key metric, which may not fully reflect the clinical significance or real-world impact of the publications. Second, we excluded non-English literature, which may have led to language bias and the omission of region-specific studies. Third, and importantly, our data were extracted solely from the WoSCC. This exclusive reliance may introduce selection bias, as it omits significant databases such as PubMed, Scopus, and regional repositories.[51] Consequently, the findings may not comprehensively represent all global research outputs, and future studies should incorporate multiple databases to minimize this potential bias. These other sources may include valuable literature, particularly non-English or regionally relevant studies. Future research should consider integrating data from multiple databases to enhance comprehensiveness and reduce selection bias. In addition, although our dataset includes literature dating back to 1939, the analysis showed that very few relevant publications existed before the 1990s. As such, including the pre-1990 period may not meaningfully contribute to the identification of research trends or hotspots.

In conclusion, this study constitutes the first bibliometric analysis of research pertaining to pterygium surgery. The findings indicate a notable increase in annual publications in this area of study on a global scale. Notably, China has emerged as the leading country in terms of research output, with the Singapore National Eye Centre recognized as the highest producing institution. The journal Cornea is identified as the most prominent publication in the domain of pterygium surgery, while Tan DTH is acknowledged as the most influential author. Future directions in this field may focus on the development of targeted surgical strategies intended to improve treatment efficacy.

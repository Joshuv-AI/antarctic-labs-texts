Hyperfunctions in 𝑨 A -model Localization
Emil Hakan Leeb-Lundberg

Abstract
We apply localization techniques to topologically A A -twisted 𝒩 = ( 2 , 2 ) \mathcal{N}=(2,2) supersymmetric theories of vector and chiral multiplets on S 2 S^{2} and derive a novel exact formula for abelian observables, described by a distribution integrated along the real line. The distributional integral formula is verified by evaluating the correlator of the A A -twisted ℂ ​ ℙ N − 1 \mathbb{CP}^{N-1} gauged linear sigma model and confirming the standard selection rule. Finally, we use hyperfunctions to demonstrate the equivalence between the distributional and complex contour integral descriptions of the ℂ ​ ℙ N − 1 \mathbb{CP}^{N-1} correlator, and find agreement with the Jeffrey-Kirwan residue prescription.

Introduction

Supersymmetric quantum field theories provide a framework to investigate
mathematically tractable versions of subtle non-perturbative phenomena
occurring in nature. In this setting, supersymmetric localization

Pestun_2017
has proven crucial in evaluating quantum observables
exactly, verifying dualities, and validating other non-perturbative
techniques. Given a quantum field theory with a fermionic symmetry

Q
, localization leverages a
Q
-exact deformation of the action
to reduce the infinite-dimensional path integral to lower-dimensional
integrals over the fixed-point locus of
Q
, facilitating the exact
evaluation of
Q
-closed observables. Different choices of
Q
-exact
deformation, or localizing term, can yield mathematically distinct,
but physically equivalent, descriptions of the same observable. In
this work, localization of topologically
A
-twisted
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)

supersymmetric gauged linear sigma models (GLSMs) on
S^{2}
surprisingly
results in a novel distributional integral description of observables
that, via hyperfunctions, is shown to be equivalent to the complex
contour integral description obtained through localization
Benini:2015noa
;
Closset:2015rna
.

GLSMs
Witten:1993yc
offer a concrete setting to study string
compactifications, bridge gauge theory and geometry, and perform exact
non-perturbative computations. Physically, GLSMs have yielded insights
into instantons
Morrison:1994fr
, wall-crossing
Gaiotto:2015aoa
;
Bonelli:2013mma
,
and dualities
Hori:2006dk
;
Closset:2017vvl
;
Chen:2020iyo
. Mathematically,
they have been utilized to compute Gromov-Witten invariants
Bonelli:2013mma
;
Jockers:2012dk
;
Gomis:2012wy
,
elliptic genera
Benini:2013nda
;
Gadde:2013dda
, gamma classes

Halverson:2013qca
, and were key to the physical proof of mirror
symmetry
Hori:2000kt
. Localization has been applied to
N=(2,2)

GLSMs on the sphere
S^{2}

Benini:2012ui
;
Doroud:2012xw
,
the torus
T^{2}

Benini:2013nda
;
Benini:2013xpa
, the hemisphere

HS^{2}

Hori:2013ika
;
Honda:2013uca
and numerous other manifolds

Benini:2015noa
;
Closset:2015rna
;
Leeb-Lundberg:2023jsj
;
Benini:2016hjo
;
Ohta:2019odi
.

There are at least three established approaches to localization: (i)
conventional supersymmetric localization techniques
Pestun:2007rz
;
(ii) supersymmetric localization techniques employing the Jeffrey–Kirwan
(JK) residue prescription (JK localization)
Benini:2013nda
;
and (iii) non-abelian localization techniques
Witten:1992xu
.
For
A
-twisted
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
supersymmetric gauge theories
on
S^{2}
, conventional localization is inapplicable
Benini:2015noa
;
Leeb-Lundberg:2023jsj
;
JK localization results in observables of gauge theories with charged
matter described by an integral along a complex contour specified
by the JK residue prescription
Benini:2015noa
;
Closset:2015rna
;
and non-abelian localization results in observables of pure gauge
theories described by an integral along a real contour
Leeb-Lundberg:2023jsj
.
These latter two approaches differ by a
Q
-exact term,
leading to mathematically distinct descriptions of the same observable.
Although the two descriptions are expected to be physically equivalent,
the equivalence has not been demonstrated.

This work presents a reconciliation of localization techniques for

A
-twisted
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
GLSMs on
S^{2}
. We apply a stationary
phase version of supersymmetric localization
Griguolo:2024ecw

to
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
supersymmetric theories of vector and chiral
multiplets, topologically
A
-twisted to the
S^{2}

using a vector-like R-symmetry, i.e. the
A
-model on
S^{2}
. Our
main result is a novel exact formula for observables of abelian
A
-twisted

\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
GLSMs on
S^{2}
, described by a distribution
integrated along a real contour. The formula is verified by evaluating
the correlator of the
A
-twisted
\mathbb{CP}^{N-1}
model and
confirming the selection rule. Then, hyperfunctions are used
to relate the distributional description of the
A
-twisted
\mathbb{CP}^{N-1}

correlator to the established JK contour integral description
Benini:2015noa
;
Closset:2015rna
,
providing evidence of their equivalence.

To make the discussion concrete, let us contrast the descriptions
of observables resulting from distinct approaches to localization
of
A
-twisted
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
GLSMs on
S^{2}
. Schematically,
these observables are described by

\left\langle\mathcal{O}\right\rangle\propto\sum_{\mathfrak{m}}\int_{\mathcal{C}}\mathrm{d}u\;\mathcal{O}(u)\,Z_{{\rm cl}}(u,\mathfrak{m})\,Z_{{\rm 1\text{-}loop}}(u,\mathfrak{m}),

(1)

where
\mathfrak{m}
is the quantized gauge flux on
S^{2}
,
u
parameterizes
a bosonic scalar in the vector multiplet,
\mathcal{C}
is the integration
contour,
\mathcal{O}
is a gauge invariant operator insertion,
Z_{\text{cl}}

is the classical contribution, and
Z_{\text{1-loop}}
is the one-loop
contribution. When (
1

employing the JK residue prescription
Benini:2015noa
;
Closset:2015rna
,
the integration contour is the complex JK contour
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{{\rm JK}}
,
the one-loop contribution is a meromorphic function of
u
, and integration
results in a sum over JK residues. In this work, (
1
) is
derived via a stationary phase version of supersymmetric localization

Griguolo:2024ecw

\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{R}
, and the one-loop contribution is a
distribution

in
u
.

There are two key manners in which our localization computation differs
from previous examples. First, our localizing term differs from those
of
Benini:2015noa
;
Closset:2015rna
by a
Q
-exact term that
includes a quadratic twisted chiral superpotential. Second, in evaluating
the one-loop contribution via monopole spherical harmonics on
S^{2}
,
we identify a bosonic scalar mode with purely imaginary eigenvalue.
This mode is associated to an oscillatory integral, which we evaluate
as a distribution following the treatment in
Griguolo:2024ecw
.
The remaining integrals over fluctuations are evaluated as determinants.
The final one-loop contribution is a distribution multiplied by a
ratio of fluctuation determinants.

This work builds on two recent studies that revisited the original
example of non-abelian localization
Witten:1992xu
. Non-abelian
localization was first applied to two-dimensional cohomological theories
of the standard multiplet, which comprises the basic, ghost, and projection
multiplets, and is related to the
A
-twisted
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)

vector multiplet in Wess-Zumino gauge by field redefinitions. The first
study,
Leeb-Lundberg:2023jsj
, used field redefinitions to apply
non-abelian localization techniques to
A
-twisted
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)

theories of vector multiplets on
S^{2}
, resulting in observables
described by a function integrated along a real contour. The second
study,
Griguolo:2024ecw
, introduced a stationary phase version
of localization and applied it to cohomological theories of the basic
multiplet on
S^{2}
, resulting in observables described by a distribution
integrated along a real contour. Our computation extends the one in

Leeb-Lundberg:2023jsj
by incorporating charged matter and
generalizes the techniques in
Griguolo:2024ecw
to the context
of
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
GLSMs.

We validate our distributional formula by computing the correlator
of the
A
-twisted
\mathbb{CP}^{N-1}
model. This is an abelian
GLSM with one vector multiplet and
N
chiral multiplets of gauge
charge 1, which flows in the IR to a non-linear sigma model (NLSM)
with target space
\mathbb{CP}^{N-1}
. The correlator is a special
case of (
1
) with insertion
\mathcal{O}(u)=u^{s}
for

. The correlator is expected to vanish if
the selection rule
s=N(\mathfrak{m}+1)-1
is not satisfied, a constraint
arising from the ghost number (R-charge) anomaly described on page
34 of
Morrison:1994fr
. We evaluate the correlator via the
distributional integral formula, and find agreement with established
results on page 54 of
Benini:2015noa
, page 72 of
Closset:2015rna
,
and page 25 of
Benini:2016hjo
. The correlator of the
\mathbb{CP}^{N-1}

NLSM is discussed in, e.g., example 16.4.1 of
Hori:2003ic

and section 3 of
Aspinwall:1991ce
.

A central contribution of this paper is the reconciliation of our
novel distributional integral description of observables with the
established JK contour integral description. This is achieved through
hyperfunction theory, which relates distributional integrals to complex
contour integrals. In particular, by interpreting the distributional
integrand of the
\mathbb{CP}^{N-1}
correlator in terms of hyperfunctions,
we explicitly recover the complex contour integral description in

Benini:2015noa
;
Closset:2015rna
;
Benini:2016hjo
. Although hyperfunctions
are rarely utilized in the context of localization, they appear in
at least one six-dimensional supersymmetric localization computation

Nieri:2015dts
and in the hyperfunction formulation of Duistermaat–Heckman
localization
jeffrey2017
.

(4)

3

Then, we express the localizing term to quadratic

The one-loop contribution is computed by expanding the fluctuations
in a basis of monopole spherical harmonics, then evaluating the integrals
over fluctuations mode-by-mode. We identify a bosonic scalar fluctuating
mode with purely imaginary eigenvalue, associated to an oscillatory
integral lacking a positive definite quadratic form. We present two
distinct approaches to treat the oscillatory integral. In one approach,
we derive the correct one-loop contribution by retaining the oscillatory
integral and only evaluating the well-defined Gaussian integrals over
the remaining fluctuations. The resulting one-loop contribution is
an oscillatory integral times a ratio of fluctuation determinants.

by evaluating all integrals over fluctuations as determinants. This
derivation treats the the oscillatory integral as if it were Gaussian,
and results in a one-loop determinant that erroneously includes the
contribution of the purely imaginary eigenvalue. Following this, we
specialize the correct one-loop contribution to the abelian case and
reduce the oscillatory integral to an expression involving distributions.
Finally, we collect the results and present the correct one-loop contribution
as a distribution multiplied by a ratio of fluctuation determinants.

0&-\frac{D_{\mu}^{0}D_{0}^{\mu}}{2}-\frac{\rho(\mathfrak{m})}{4R^{2}}+\frac{i\rho(\mathfrak{m})\rho(u)}{4R^{2}t}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\bar{\phi}_{j,j_{3}}^{\rho}Y_{j,j_{3}}^{-\frac{\rho(\mathfrak{m})}{2}}\\
\phi_{j,j_{3}}^{\rho}Y_{j,j_{3}}^{-\frac{\rho(\mathfrak{m})}{2}}\end{array}\right)\right|_{j=\rho(\mathfrak{m})=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\bar{\phi}_{0,0}^{\rho}Y_{0,0}^{0}\\
\phi_{0,0}^{\rho}Y_{0,0}^{0}\end{array}\right).

Observe that acting with
D_{\mu}^{0}D_{0}^{\mu}
on the harmonics
results in an expression in terms of
j
and
\rho(\mathfrak{m})
.
Since all terms depend on either
j
or
\rho(\mathfrak{m})
, and
both of these are zero, the bosonic fluctuation operator is zero.
The fermionic operator behaves similarly

\displaystyle\left.\left(\Delta_{\mathrm{F}}^{\rho}\Psi_{j,j_{3}}^{\rho}\right)\right|_{j=\rho(\mathfrak{m})=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left.\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-\frac{\rho(\mathfrak{m})}{4R^{2}t}&0\\
0&-\frac{\rho(\mathfrak{m})}{4R^{2}t}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\overline{\psi}_{j,j_{3}}^{\rho}Y_{j,j_{3}}^{-\frac{\rho(\mathfrak{m})}{2}}\\
\psi_{j,j_{3}}^{\rho}Y_{j,j_{3}}^{-\frac{\rho(\mathfrak{m})}{2}}\end{array}\right)\right|_{j=\rho(\mathfrak{m})=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\overline{\psi}_{0,0}^{\rho}Y_{0,0}^{0}\\
\psi_{0,0}^{\rho}Y_{0,0}^{0}\end{array}\right)

The primary issue here is that the ratio of determinants
\sqrt{\det\Delta_{\text{F}}^{\rho}}/\sqrt{\det\Delta_{\text{B}}^{\rho}}

involves a zero in the denominator. On the one hand, we cannot rigorously
evaluate the contribution at
j=\rho(\mathfrak{m})=0
due to the
singular bosonic contribution. On the other hand, we can speculate
on the value of this contribution by considering fixed
\rho(\mathfrak{m})/t

as

. In this case, the zero-divided-by-zero cancellation in
the ratio of determinants becomes a zero-divided-by-zero cancellation
in the
t
-dependent terms. Keeping
\rho(\mathfrak{m})/t
fixed
while setting all other
j
and
\rho(\mathfrak{m})
to zero, the
bosonic and fermionic operators are

\displaystyle\left.\left(\hat{\Delta}_{\mathrm{B}}^{\rho}\Phi_{j,j_{3}}^{\rho}\right)\right|_{j=\rho(\mathfrak{m})=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{i\rho(\mathfrak{m})\rho(u)}{4R^{2}t}&0\\
0&\frac{i\rho(\mathfrak{m})\rho(u)}{4R^{2}t}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\bar{\phi}_{0,0}^{\rho}Y_{0,0}^{0}\\
\phi_{0,0}^{\rho}Y_{0,0}^{0}\end{array}\right),

\left.\left(\hat{\Delta}_{\mathrm{F}}^{\rho}\Psi_{j,j_{3}}^{\rho}\right)\right|_{j=\rho(\mathfrak{m})=0}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-\frac{\rho(\mathfrak{m})}{4R^{2}t}&0\\
0&-\frac{\rho(\mathfrak{m})}{4R^{2}t}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\overline{\psi}_{0,0}^{\rho}Y_{0,0}^{0}\\
\psi_{0,0}^{\rho}Y_{0,0}^{0}\end{array}\right),

(165)

and the ratio of determinants is

\left.\left(\frac{\sqrt{\det\hat{\Delta}_{\mathrm{F}}^{\rho}}}{\sqrt{\det\hat{\Delta}_{\mathrm{B}}^{\rho}}}\right)\right|_{j=\rho(\mathfrak{m})=0}=\begin{cases}\;\prod_{\rho\in\mathcal{R}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left(-i\rho(u)\right)^{2}}}&\text{as }t\to 0\\
\;\text{singular}&\text{for }t\neq 0\end{cases}.

(166)

In particular, the factors of
\rho(\mathfrak{m})/(4R^{2}t)
cancel
and we are left with a contribution that resembles those of other
sectors. Despite the similarity, setting
t=0
is problematic in
several ways. For instance, at

, part of the
Q_{A}
-exact deformation
of the vector multiplet localizing term (
21
)
is turned off, the Yang-Mills equations (
30
) are
singular, and the Yang-Mills connections are at infinity in field
space.

Let us summarize. In determining the one-loop contribution, we identified
a purely imaginary eigenvalue of the bosonic scalar fluctuations at

j=|\rho(\mathfrak{m})|/2,\;|\rho(\mathfrak{m})|\geq 1
, associated
to an oscillatory integral lacking a positive definite quadratic
form. We considered two approaches to treating the oscillatory integral.
In one approach, we incorrectly treated the oscillatory integral as
if it were Gaussian, and evaluated the one-loop determinant including
the contribution of all eigenvalues. In view of (
80
),
(
114
), (
128
), (
134
),
and (
139
), the incorrect one-loop determinant is

Z_{\text{1-loop det.}}=\prod_{\rho\in\mathcal{R}}\frac{1}{\left(-i\rho(u)\right)^{\rho(\mathfrak{m})+1}},\quad\rho(\mathfrak{m})\neq 0.

(167)

In the other approach, we derived the correct one-loop contribution
by retaining the oscillatory integral and integrating out the remaining
fluctuating modes. In view of (
80
), (
115
),
(
129
), (
134
), and (
139
),
the correct one-loop contribution is

Z_{\text{1-loop distr.}}=\prod_{\rho\in\mathcal{R}}\frac{\left(-\frac{\rho(\mathfrak{m})}{4R^{2}t}\right)^{\frac{\rho(\mathfrak{m})+1}{2}}}{\left(-\frac{i\rho(u)}{2}\right)^{\frac{\rho(\mathfrak{m})+1}{2}}}\int\prod_{\ell=1}^{\frac{\rho(\mathfrak{m})+1}{2}}\frac{\text{d}\,\phi_{\ell}^{\rho}\;\text{d}\,\overline{\phi}_{\ell}^{\rho}}{2\pi i}\exp\left[-\overline{\phi}_{\ell}^{\rho}\,\left(\frac{i\rho(\mathfrak{m})\rho(u)}{4R^{2}t}\right)\,\phi_{\ell}^{\rho}\right],\quad\rho(\mathfrak{m})\neq 0.

(168)

Here, the oscillatory integral is the one associated to the bosonic scalar
fluctuations at
j=|\rho(\mathfrak{m})|/2,\;|\rho(\mathfrak{m})|\geq 1
,
and the remaining terms arise from integrating out all other fluctuations.

3.3.2
The distributional integral

In this section, the integral in (
168
) is reduced
to an expression involving distributions, for the case of abelian

A
-twisted
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
GLSMs with
N
chiral multiplets
of gauge charge 1 and R-charge 0. This proceeds by first specializing
to gauge group
\text{U}(1)
, then rewriting the complex integration
variables in terms of a real radial integration variable, and finally
using the Heitler function to express the integral as a distribution.

The starting point is

I(u,\mathfrak{m})=\int\prod_{\rho\in\mathcal{R}}\prod_{\ell=1}^{\frac{|\rho(\mathfrak{m})|+1}{2}}\frac{\text{d}\phi_{\rho,\ell}\,\text{d}\tilde{\phi}_{\rho,\ell}}{2\pi i}\,\exp\left[\mp\tilde{\phi}_{\rho,\ell}\left(i\,\tau\,\rho(\mathfrak{m})\,\rho(u)\right)\phi_{\rho,\ell}\right],

(169)

where
\rho(\mathfrak{m})\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
,
\rho(u)\in\mathbb{R}
,
and
\tau=1/(4R^{2}t)\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. For a
G=\text{U}(1)

theory with chiral multiplets
\Phi_{i},\,i=1,\dots,N
of gauge charge

Q_{i}
, the integral (
169

I=\int\prod_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{\ell=1}^{\frac{\left|Q_{i}\mathfrak{m}\right|+1}{2}}\frac{\text{d}\phi_{i,\ell}\,\text{d}\tilde{\phi}_{i,\ell}}{2\pi i}\,\exp\left[\mp\tilde{\phi}_{i,\ell}\left(i\,\tau\,Q_{i}\mathfrak{m}\,Q_{i}u\right)\phi_{i,\ell}\right].

(170)

Specializing to
Q_{i}=1
for
i=1,\dots,N
, the expression becomes

I=\int\prod_{i=1}^{N}\prod_{\ell=1}^{\frac{|\mathfrak{m}|+1}{2}}\frac{\text{d}\phi_{i,\ell}\,\text{d}\tilde{\phi}_{i,\ell}}{2\pi i}\,\exp\left[\mp\tilde{\phi}_{i,\ell}\left(i\,\tau\,\mathfrak{m}\,u\right)\phi_{i,\ell}\right].

(171)

We make a change of variables to real coordinates

\displaystyle\phi_{i,\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i(\phi_{1\,i,\ell}+i\phi_{2\,i,\ell}),

(172)

\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}_{i,\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i(\phi_{1\,i,\ell}-i\phi_{2\,i,\ell}).

(173)

The measure changes as
\text{d}\phi_{i,\ell}\,\text{d}\tilde{\phi}_{i,\ell}=i\text{d}\phi_{1\,i,\ell}\,\text{d}\phi_{2\,i,\ell}
,
and there are
N
values of
i
and
(|\mathfrak{m}|+1)/2
values
of
\ell
. Consequently, the integral becomes

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\prod_{\ell=1}^{\frac{N(|\mathfrak{m}|+1)}{2}}\frac{\text{d}\phi_{1\,\ell}\,\text{d}\phi_{2\,\ell}}{2\pi}\,\exp\left[\mp i\,\tau\,\mathfrak{m}\,u\left(\phi_{1\,\ell}^{2}+\phi_{2\,\ell}^{2}\right)\right].

(174)

Scaling by
\phi_{\ell}\to\sqrt{2\pi}\phi_{\ell}
, and combining
the integration variables, the integral is

I=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\prod_{k=1}^{K}\text{d}\phi_{k}\,e^{\mp 2\pi i\tau\mathfrak{m}u\phi_{k}^{2}},

(175)

where
K=N(|\mathfrak{m}|+1)
. We introduce radial coordinates

r^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\phi_{k}^{2},\qquad\prod_{k=1}^{K}\text{d}\phi_{k}=r^{K-1}\text{d}r\,\text{d}\Omega_{K-1},

(176)

where
\text{d}\Omega_{K-1}
is the surface area element of the
K-1

dimensional unit sphere. Expressing (
175
)
in terms of (
176
), we have

I=\text{vol}\Omega_{K-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}r\,r^{K-1}e^{\mp 2\pi i\tau\mathfrak{m}ur^{2}}.

(177)

where
\text{vol}\Omega_{K-1}=2\pi^{K/2}/\Gamma(K/2)
is the volume of the
K-1
dimensional
unit sphere. Introducing a second radial coordinate,

y=r^{2},\qquad\text{d}r=\frac{\text{d}y}{2\sqrt{y}},

(178)

the integral (
177
) becomes

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\text{vol}\Omega_{K-1}}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}y\,y^{\frac{K}{2}-1}e^{\mp 2\pi i\tau\mathfrak{m}uy}.

(179)

For
K\geq 2
, the integrand may be expressed as

y^{\frac{K}{2}-1}e^{-2\pi i\tau\mathfrak{m}uy}=\left(\frac{1}{\mp 2\pi i\tau\mathfrak{m}}\frac{d}{du}\right)^{\frac{K}{2}-1}e^{\mp 2\pi i\tau\mathfrak{m}uy},

(180)

such that the integral (
179
)
reads

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\text{vol}\Omega_{K-1}}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\mp 2\pi i\,\tau\,\mathfrak{m}}\frac{d}{du}\right)^{\frac{K}{2}-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}ye^{\mp 2\pi i\tau\mathfrak{m}uy}.

(181)

Now, we use the Heitler function
2
2

2

\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}xe^{\mp 2\pi ipx}=\pm\frac{1}{2\pi i}\text{pv}\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\delta(p)}{2}=\pm\frac{1}{2\pi i}\left(\text{pv}\frac{1}{p}\pm\frac{i\pi}{2}\delta(p)\right)

to express the integral over
y
as a distribution

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}ye^{\mp 2\pi i\tau\mathfrak{m}uy}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pm 2\pi i}\text{pv}\left(\frac{1}{\tau\,\mathfrak{m}\,u}\right)+\frac{\delta(\tau\,\mathfrak{m}\,u)}{2}

(182)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pm 2\pi i\,\tau\,\mathfrak{m}}\text{pv}\frac{1}{u}+\frac{\delta(u)}{2\left|\tau\,\mathfrak{m}\right|}

(183)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pm 2\pi i\,\tau\,\mathfrak{m}}\left(\text{pv}\frac{1}{u}\pm i\pi\,\text{sgn}(\mathfrak{m})\,\delta(u)\right).

(184)

Consequently, (
181
) reduces
to

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\text{vol}\Omega_{K-1}}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\mp 2\pi i\,\tau\,\mathfrak{m}}\frac{d}{du}\right)^{\frac{K}{2}-1}\left(\frac{1}{\pm 2\pi i\,\tau\,\mathfrak{m}}\left(\text{pv}\frac{1}{u}\pm i\pi\,\text{sgn}(\mathfrak{m})\,\delta(u)\right)\right)

(185)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\frac{K}{2}\right)\left(\mp 2i\,\tau\,\mathfrak{m}\right)^{\frac{K}{2}}}\frac{d^{\frac{K}{2}-1}}{du^{\frac{K}{2}-1}}\left(\text{pv}\frac{1}{u}\pm i\pi\,\text{sgn}(\mathfrak{m})\,\delta(u)\right).

Collecting the results, the oscillatory integral (
171

has three equivalent expressions

\displaystyle I_{\text{real}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\prod_{k=1}^{K}\frac{\text{d}\phi_{k}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,e^{\mp\,i\,\tau\,\mathfrak{m}\,u\,\phi_{k}^{2}},

(186)

\displaystyle I_{\text{radial}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\text{vol}\Omega_{K-1}}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}y\,y^{\frac{K}{2}-1}e^{\mp\,2\pi i\,\tau\,\mathfrak{m}\,u\,y},

(187)

\displaystyle I_{\text{distr.}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\frac{K}{2}\right)\left(\mp 2i\,\tau\,\mathfrak{m}\right)^{\frac{K}{2}}}\frac{d^{\frac{K}{2}-1}}{du^{\frac{K}{2}-1}}\left(\text{pv}\frac{1}{u}\pm i\pi\,\text{sgn}(\mathfrak{m})\,\delta(u)\right).

(188)

where
K=N(|\mathfrak{m}|+1)

Finally, let us note that the integral (

in equation 2.10 of

4
The
A
-twisted
\mathbb{CP}^{N-1}
model

In this section, we verify the distributional integral description
of observables by computing the correlator of the
A
-twisted
\mathbb{CP}^{N-1}

model, confirming the standard selection rule, and then recovering
the established complex contour integral description via hyperfunctions.
This proceeds by first reviewing the complex contour integral

evaluating the distributional integral. Following this, we interpret
the distributional correlator in terms of hyperfunctions, recover

4.1
Complex contour integral description (review)

The
\mathbb{CP}^{N-1}
model is an abelian
A
-twisted
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)

GLSM on
S^{2}
with
N
chiral multiplets of gauge charge
1

and vector-like R-charge
0
. The GLSM flows in the IR to an NLSM
with target space
\mathbb{CP}^{N-1}
. The observables of interest are
correlators of the field-strength twisted chiral multiplet
\Sigma
,
whose lowest component,
\sigma
, is parameterized by the continuous
modulus
u
. Insertions of
\Sigma(x)
at points
x
on
S^{2}

represent the Kähler class of the target
\mathbb{CP}^{N-1}
. The correlator
of the
A
-twisted
\mathbb{CP}^{N-1}
model is

\left\langle\Sigma_{1}(x_{1})\,\cdots\,\Sigma_{s}(x_{s})\right\rangle=\sum_{\mathfrak{m}\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\text{JK}}}\frac{du}{2\pi i}\frac{u^{s}q^{\mathfrak{m}}}{u^{N(\mathfrak{m}+1)}}=\begin{cases}q^{\frac{s-N+1}{N}}&s=N-1\quad(\text{mod }N)\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

(197)

Here,
\mathfrak{m}
is the quantized
\text{U}(1)
gauge flux on

S^{2}
,
u
parameterizes the bosonic scalar
\sigma
in the vector
multiplet,
\mathcal{C}_{\text{JK}}
is the complex contour specified
by a Jeffrey-Kirwan residue type prescription,
u^{s}
is the insertion,
the complexified Fayet-Illiopolous (FI) term
q^{\mathfrak{m}}=e^{-(\zeta+i\theta)\mathfrak{m}}

is the classical contribution, and
u^{N(\mathfrak{m}+1)}
is the
one-loop contribution of chiral multiplets without twisted masses. For references, see
page 54 of
Benini:2015noa
, page 72 of
Closset:2015rna
,
and page 25 of
Benini:2016hjo
.

The correlator (
197
) is evaluated by first
integrating then summing. The integral is evaluated according to a
JK residue type prescription, which requires a choice for the JK parameter

\eta
. Choosing
\eta>0
, the prescription is to take the residue
of the pole
u=0
. When
s=N(\mathfrak{m}+1)-1
, the JK contour
captures the contribution of the pole at
u=0
and the integral is evaluated
as a standard residue. When
s\neq N(\mathfrak{m}+1)-1
, the integral
evaluates to zero. In particular, the correlator is only non-zero when
the selection rule

s=N(\mathfrak{m}+1)-1

(198)

is satisfied. The selection rule relates the number of insertions

s
to the flux
\mathfrak{m}
and the number of chiral multiplets

N
, which constrains the subsequent sum over
\mathfrak{m}
.

In what follows, we consider the case when the linear twisted superpotential
is zero and the classical contribution is equal to one. In this case,
the correlator (
197

\left\langle\Sigma_{1}(x_{1})\,\cdots\,\Sigma_{s}(x_{s})\right\rangle=\sum_{\mathfrak{m}\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\text{JK}}}\frac{du}{2\pi i}\frac{1}{u^{N(\mathfrak{m}+1)-s}}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }s=N(\mathfrak{m}+1)-1\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

(199)

In what follows, we aim to reproduce (
199
)
via the distributional formula for observables in section

4.2

)
is expressed as

I=\frac{\left(-1\right)^{q}}{\Gamma\left(q+1\right)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\text{d}u\left(u^{p}\partial_{u}^{q}\left(\text{pv}\frac{1}{u}+i\pi\,\text{sgn}(\mathfrak{m})\,\delta(u)\right)\right)

(201)

where
p=s-N(\mathfrak{m}+1)/2
and
q=N(\mathfrak{m}+1)/2-1
are
fixed, and
\partial_{u}=\frac{d}{du}.
We require
q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

to ensure that the order of the derivative is a non-negative integer.
This requirement also ensures non-zero
\Gamma\left(q+1\right)^{-1}
.
Note that the selection rule (
198
) is the
case when
p=q
.

We evaluate (
201
) in three different manners.
First, we rewrite the integrand using the Sokhotski-Plemelj formula,

\text{pv}\frac{1}{u}\pm i\pi\delta(u)=(u\mp i0)^{-1}
, then evaluate
the integral. In this case, (
201
) reduces
to

I=\frac{\left(-1\right)^{q}}{\Gamma\left(q+1\right)}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\text{d}u\left(u^{p}\partial_{u}^{q}\left(\frac{1}{u-i\,\text{sgn}(\mathfrak{m})\,\varepsilon}\right)\right)=\begin{cases}\;\text{ill-defined}&p<q\\
\;i\pi\text{sgn}(\mathfrak{m})&p=q\\
\;\text{divergent}&p>q\end{cases}

(202)

For
p<q
, the integral is ill-defined. For
p=q
, the selection
rule (
198
) is satisfied and the integral evaluates
to a finite number. For
p>q
, the integral does not converge.

To obtain a finite result for
p\neq q
, we consider the second perspective.
Integrating by parts
q
times, (
201
) reduces
to

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(-1\right)^{q}}{\Gamma\left(q+1\right)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\text{d}u\left(\left(-1\right)^{q}\partial_{u}^{q}u^{p}\right)\left(\text{pv}\frac{1}{u}+i\pi\,\text{sgn}(\mathfrak{m})\,\delta(u)\right)

(203)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\;0&p<q\\
\;i\pi\,\text{sgn}(\mathfrak{m})&p=q\\
\;\frac{\Gamma\left(p+1\right)}{\Gamma\left(q+1\right)\Gamma\left(p-q+1\right)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\text{d}u\,u^{p-q}\text{pv}\frac{1}{u}&p>q\end{cases}

(204)

For
p<q
, the integral vanishes due to
\partial_{u}^{q}u^{p}=0
,
in contrast with (
202
). For
p=q
, the derivative
yields
\partial_{u}^{q}u^{p}=\Gamma\left(p+1\right)
, the
\text{pv}\frac{1}{u}

contribution vanishes by symmetry, the
\delta(u)
contribution is
1, and the result agrees with (
202
). For
p>q
,
the
\delta(u)
contribution vanishes, and the
\text{pv}\frac{1}{u}

contribution is divergent. The divergent integral for
p>q
may be
expressed as

I=\frac{\Gamma\left(p+1\right)}{\Gamma\left(q+1\right)\Gamma\left(p-q+1\right)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\text{d}u\,u^{p-q-1},\quad\text{for}\quad p-q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}.

(205)

If the integrand is odd and we introduce a cutoff, (
205
)
vanishes by symmetry

\lim_{b\to+\infty}\int_{-b}^{b}\text{d}u\,u^{p-q-1}=\lim_{b\to+\infty}\frac{b^{p-q}-(-b)^{p-q}}{p-q}=0\quad\text{for}\quad p-q=2k,\,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}

(206)

If the integrand is even and we introduce a cutoff, (
205
)
exhibits power divergences. One possible treatment in this case is
the Hadamard finite-part prescription, which removes these power divergences
and leaves a finite part that is equal to zero.

To further address the divergent integral (
205
) at
p-q=2k+1
for
p,q,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
,
we consider a third perspective. We rewrite the correlator (
201
) in terms
of the radial integral description (
187
), which reads

\left\langle\prod_{a=1}^{s}\Sigma_{a}(x_{a})\right\rangle=\sum_{\mathfrak{m}\in\mathbb{Z}}\left[\frac{e^{-\frac{i\pi N(\mathfrak{m}+1)}{4}}\left(\pi\tau\mathfrak{m}\right)^{\frac{N(\mathfrak{m}+1)}{2}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{N(\mathfrak{m}+1)}{2}\right)}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\text{d}u\left(u^{s-\frac{N(\mathfrak{m}+1)}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}y\,y^{\frac{N(\mathfrak{m}+1)}{2}-1}e^{-2\pi i\tau\mathfrak{m}uy}\right)\right]

(207)

for
\tau=1/(2R^{2}t)
. This expression should be evaluated by first
integrating over the fluctuation
y
, then integrating over the zero mode
u
.
However, evaluating the integrals in
this order leads back to the distributional description of the summand
(
201
), and the divergent integral (
205
).
Instead, we will reverse the order of evaluation, integrate
u
before

y
, and see that the correlator vanishes for
p>q
. Although exchanging
the order of integration in this manner does not satisfy the necessary
conditions for Fubini’s theorem to apply, it provides
insight at the cost of rigor. Reinstating
p=s-N(\mathfrak{m}+1)/2

and
q=N(\mathfrak{m}+1)/2-1
, the integrals in (
207
)
are expressed as

\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}y\,y^{q}\,\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\text{d}u\,u^{p}\,e^{-2\pi i\tau\mathfrak{m}uy}\right)

(208)

Although the integral over
u
is not Lebesgue integrable for
p\geq 0
,
it can be interpreted as the Fourier transform of the tempered distribution

u^{p}
. We use the Fourier transform convention
\hat{f}(\zeta)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)e^{-2\pi i\xi x}dx
,
where the roles of
f(x)
and
\xi
are played by
u^{p}
and
\tau\mathfrak{m}y
,
respectively. Integrating out
u
in this manner yields a delta function,
and the subsequent integral over
y
evaluates to a finite quantity.
In particular, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}y\,y^{q}\,\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\text{d}u\,u^{p}\,e^{-2\pi i\tau\mathfrak{m}uy}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left(-2\pi i\right)^{p}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}y\,y^{q}\,\left(\frac{d^{p}}{dy^{p}}\delta(\tau\mathfrak{m}y)\right)

(209)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{p}p!}{\left(-2\pi i\right)^{p}}\frac{(\tau\mathfrak{m})^{p}}{|\tau\mathfrak{m}|}\delta_{p,q}(1-\Theta(0)).

(210)

where
\delta_{p,q}
is the Kronecker delta function and
\Theta

is the Heaviside theta function. Replacing the integrals in (
207
)
with result (
210
), we see that the correlator
vanishes for
p\neq q
due to the Kronecker delta function. This
suggests that the correlator (
207
) obeys the selection rule and the divergent
integral (
205
) lacks physical significance.

To summarize, we formulated the correlator of the
A
-twisted
\mathbb{CP}^{N-1}

model as a distributional integral, then evaluated the integral in
three different manners. From (
204
), we see that
the correlator vanishes for
s<N(\mathfrak{m}+1)-1
. In (
206
)
and (
210
), we argued that the correlator vanishes
for
s>N(\mathfrak{m}+1)-1
. In (
202
) and (
204
),
we see that the distributional integral evaluates to the finite non-zero
quantity
i\pi\,\text{sgn}(\mathfrak{m})
for
s=N(\mathfrak{m}+1)-1
. Note that since
s,N
are fixed, the selection rule ensures that
the value of
\mathfrak{m}
is fixed.
Collecting the results, our analysis suggests that the correlator (
201
)
reduces to

\left\langle\prod_{a=1}^{s}\Sigma_{a}(x_{a})\right\rangle=\begin{cases}i\pi\text{sgn}\left(\mathfrak{m}\right)&s=N(\mathfrak{m}+1)-1\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

(211)

This result agrees with the complex contour integral description (
199
),
up to a factor of
\pm i\pi
. Finding complete agreement between the
two descriptions is likely possible by adjusting the normalization
and scaling of integrals in section
3
, or
through a closer analysis of the positive and negative flux sectors. We leave
this for future work.

4.3
Equivalence of descriptions via hyperfunctions

In this section, we show that the distributional and complex contour
integral descriptions of the
A
-twisted
\mathbb{CP}^{N-1}
model
correlator are related through hyperfunction theory
Graf2010
;
Imai1992
.
In particular, a distribution integrated along a real contour can
be related to a meromorphic function integrated along a complex contour
by interpreting the distribution as a hyperfunction. The starting
point is the summand in the distributional integral description of
the correlator (
200
), namely

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(-1\right)^{q}}{\Gamma\left(q+1\right)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\text{d}u\left(u^{p}\partial_{u}^{q}\left(\text{pv}\frac{1}{u}+i\pi\,\text{sgn}(\mathfrak{m})\,\delta(u)\right)\right),

(212)

where
p=s-N(\mathfrak{m}+1)/2
,
q=N(\mathfrak{m}+1)/2-1
, and

\partial_{u}=\frac{d}{du}.

Let
z\in\mathbb{C}
be a complex variable, let
I
be an interval
on the real line
\mathbb{R\subset}\mathbb{C}
, and
D_{+}(I)
and let

D_{-}(I)
be the upper and lower half planes of
\mathbb{C}
, respectively. Let

f(u)=[F^{+}(z),F^{-}(z)]
be a hyperfunction, with complex analytic
functions
F^{+}(z)\in D_{+}(I)
and
F^{-}(z)\in D_{-}(I)
. First,
we interpret the distributions in (
212
) as hyperfunctions

\displaystyle\text{pv}\frac{1}{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{1}{z},-\frac{1}{z}\right],

(213)

\displaystyle\delta(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\left[\frac{1}{z},\frac{1}{z}\right].

(214)

The terms in the integrand of (
212
) are then described
by

\displaystyle u^{p}\partial_{u}^{q}\text{pv}\frac{1}{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(-1\right)^{q}q!}{2}\left[\frac{1}{z^{q-p+1}},-\frac{1}{z^{q-p+1}}\right],

(215)

\displaystyle u^{p}\partial_{u}^{q}\delta(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\left(-1\right)^{q}q!}{2\pi i}\left[\frac{1}{z^{q-p+1}},\frac{1}{z^{q-p+1}}\right].

(216)

A hyperfunction integrated along the real line may be expressed as

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(u)\,dx=\int_{\gamma_{+}}F^{+}(z)\,dz-\int_{\gamma_{-}}F^{-}(z)\,dz=-\oint_{\gamma}F(z)\,dz.

(217)

The contour
\gamma_{+}
runs in
D_{+}(I)
from
-\infty
to
\infty
,
the contour
\gamma_{-}
runs in
D_{-}(I)
from
-\infty
to
\infty
,
and
\gamma
is the counterclockwise contour
\gamma_{-}-\gamma_{+}
.
For a reference, see definition 1.14 in
Graf2010
.

In view of (
215
), (
216
), and (
217
),
the integral (
212
) is

I=\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{\gamma_{+}}\frac{1}{z^{q-p+1}}\,dz+\int_{\gamma_{-}}\frac{1}{z^{q-p+1}}\,dz\right)-\frac{\text{sgn}(\mathfrak{m})}{2}\left(\int_{\gamma_{+}^{\prime}}\frac{1}{z^{q-p+1}}\,dz-\int_{\gamma_{-}^{\prime}}\frac{1}{z^{q-p+1}}\,dz\right).

(218)

The contours
\gamma_{+}
and
\gamma_{+}^{\prime}
go from
-\infty

to
+\infty
through
D_{+}(I)
, while the contours
\gamma_{-}

and
\gamma_{-}^{\prime}
go from
-\infty
to
+\infty
through

D_{-}(I)
. The integrals over
\gamma_{-}
and
\gamma_{-}^{\prime}

pick up a minus sign upon reversing the directions of the contours. Consequently, (
218
)
reduces to

I=\frac{\text{sgn}(\mathfrak{m})}{2}\oint_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{1}{z^{q-p+1}}\,dz,

(219)

where
\mathcal{C}=\gamma_{-}^{\prime}-\gamma_{+}^{\prime}
is the
closed counterclockwise contour around the origin of the complex plane.
The integral evaluates to
2\pi i\text{Res}_{z=0}\left(1/z\right)=2\pi i

if
p=q
, and zero otherwise. Reinstating
p=s-N(\mathfrak{m}+1)/2

and
q=N(\mathfrak{m}+1)/2-1
, the distributional correlator of the

A
-twisted
\mathbb{CP}^{N-1}
model (
200
)
is equivalently described as

\displaystyle\left\langle\prod_{a=1}^{s}\Sigma_{a}(x_{a})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mathfrak{m}\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\text{sgn}(\mathfrak{m})\oint_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{dz}{z^{N(\mathfrak{m}+1)-s}}\right)

(220)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\pi\text{sgn}\left(\mathfrak{m}\right)&s=N(\mathfrak{m}+1)-1\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(221)

This result again agrees with the complex
contour integral description (
199
), up to a factor
of
\pm i\pi
. The integration contour
\mathcal{C}
in (
220
)
is identical to the one specified by the JK residue type prescription for positive JK parameter
\eta>0
.
Once again, it is likely possible
to find complete agreement between the two descriptions by adjusting
the normalization and scaling of integrals in section
3
,
or through a closer analysis of the positive and negative flux sectors.

5
Discussion

In this paper, we applied a stationary phase version of supersymmetric
localization to topologically
A
-twisted
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
supersymmetric
gauged linear sigma models (GLSMs) on
S^{2}
. Our primary result
is a novel and exact formula for observables of abelian
A
-twisted

\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
GLSMs on
S^{2}
, described by a distribution
integrated along the real line. We verified the distributional integral
formula by computing the correlator of the
A
-twisted
\mathbb{CP}^{N-1}

model, reproducing the known selection rule. Finally, hyperfunctions
were used to relate the distributional integral description of the

\mathbb{CP}^{N-1}
correlator to the established complex contour
integral description obtained via localization in
Benini:2015noa
;
Closset:2015rna
;
Benini:2016hjo
.

This work is a tangible step to reconciling discrepancies between
two different approaches to supersymmetric localization. On the one
hand, the Jeffrey-Kirwan (JK) residue prescription was introduced
in
JeffreyKirwan
and extended to the context of supersymmetric
localization in
Benini:2013nda
. Supersymmetric localization
techniques employing the Jeffrey-Kirwan (JK) residue prescription
were developed in
Benini:2013xpa
;
Cordova:2014oxa
;
Ohta:2019odi
;
Closset:2015ohf
;
Closset:2017bse
;
Closset:2017zgf
;
Closset:2019hyt
;
Benini:2015noa
;
Hosseini:2018uzp
;
Benini:2016hjo
;
Hori:2014tda
,
and applied to
A
-twisted
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
GLSMs on
S^{2}

in
Benini:2015noa
;
Closset:2015rna
;
Benini:2016hjo
. This approach
to localization leads to observables described by JK residues, or
equivalently, a meromorphic function integrated along a complex contour
specified by the JK residue prescription. On the other hand, the work
presented here builds on the non-abelian localization techniques that
were introduced in
Witten:1992xu
and developed in
Leeb-Lundberg:2023jsj
;
Griguolo:2024ecw
.
Specifically, we utilize the stationary phase version of supersymmetric
localization introduced in
Griguolo:2024ecw
, which leads to
observables described by a distribution integrated along a real contour.
Our localization computation differs from those of
Benini:2015noa
;
Ohta:2019odi
;
Closset:2015rna
;
Benini:2016hjo

by a
Q
-exact term, in the sense that we take
t>0
and
\tau=0

in the localizing term (
21
) instead of

t=0
and
\tau=1
. Consequently, the two approaches lead to mathematically
distinct descriptions of observables that were expected to be equivalent.
The work presented here provides the first concrete evidence of this
equivalence in the well-studied setting of
A
-twisted
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)

GLSMs.

The bridge between the distributional and JK residue descriptions
of observables is provided by hyperfunction theory
Graf2010
;
Imai1992
.
We recovered the complex contour integral description of the
A
-twisted

\mathbb{CP}^{N-1}
correlator from the distributional integral description
by interpreting the distributions as hyperfunctions. Specifically,
our contour encircles a pole at the origin in the complex plane of
the continuous modulus
u
. The JK residue, on the other hand, captures
the contribution of an analogous pole at the origin in the
u
-plane
when the JK parameter is taken to be positive. In this sense, hyperfunction
theory offers a framework for relating distributional and JK residue
descriptions of observables arising from localization computations
that differ by
Q
-exact terms.

Our derivation hinges on the evaluation of the distributional one-loop
contribution. The integrals over fluctuations were evaluated using
monopole spherical harmonics on
S^{2}
. Crucially, we identified
a bosonic scalar mode with purely imaginary eigenvalue, associated
to an oscillatory integral lacking a positive definite quadratic
form. This oscillatory integral was evaluated as a distribution, while
the remaining integrals over fluctuations were evaluated as determinants.
Consequently, the final one-loop contribution is a distribution multiplied
by a ratio of fluctuation determinants. The derivation presented here
builds on the techniques introduced in
Griguolo:2024ecw
, where
a distributional one-loop contribution was evaluated using an index
theorem.

The stationary phase localization techniques presented here likely
generalize to other settings, and may prove useful in addressing certain
unsatisfactory aspects of supersymmetric localization techniques employing
JK residue type prescriptions. In particular, the JK residue prescription
applies most transparently to gauge theories with charged matter obeying
specific criteria. Although extensions to pure gauge theories are
conceivable
Benini:2016hjo
, alternative localization techniques
offer conceptual and technical advantages
Leeb-Lundberg:2023jsj
.
Furthermore, the JK residue prescription requires prior knowledge
of the charges of dynamical chiral multiplets, which likely obstructs
the weak gauging of global symmetries at the level of the localized
partition function
Griguolo:2024ecw
. Several localization
computations that use a JK residue type prescription are promising
candidates for generalizing stationary phase localization techniques.
Examples include
\Omega
-deformed
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
GLSMs on

S^{2}

Closset:2015rna
,
A/2
-twisted
\mathcal{N}=(0,2)

GLSMs on
S^{2}

Closset:2015ohf
, four-dimensional twisted

\mathcal{N}=2
theories on toric Kähler manifolds
Bershtein:2015xfa
,
and five-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=1
indices
Hosseini:2018uzp
.
A key aspect of any such generalization would be to demonstrate, via
hyperfunction theory, the equivalence between the distributional and
complex contour integral descriptions of observables.

In upcoming work, we consider extensions to non-abelian gauge groups
and
\Omega
-deformed
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
GLSMs. The former extension
would begin with gauge group
\text{SU}(2)
, and likely leverage
the insights from the localization computations involving non-abelian
gauge groups in
Leeb-Lundberg:2023jsj
;
Griguolo:2024ecw
. After
deriving a distributional integral description of observables for
non-abelian
A
-twisted
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
GLSMs, one could attempt
to recover the partition function as a sum over solutions of the Bethe
ansatz equations
Benini:2016hjo
;
Nekrasov:2014xaa
. Applying
a stationary phase version of supersymmetric localization to
\Omega
-deformed

\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
GLSMs on
S^{2}
would complement the results
of
Closset:2015rna
, and the key point would be to ensure the

\Omega
-deformation parameter does not spoil any of the arguments
leading to a distributional integral description of observables. In
both cases, we aim to verify the localization computation by evaluating
at least one distributional observable, then relating the distributional
and contour integral descriptions of observables through hyperfunctions.

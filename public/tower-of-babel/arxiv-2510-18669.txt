On the spectral radius of the ratio of Girko matrices
Djalil Chafaï, David García-Zelada, Yuan Yuan Xu

Abstract
. Girko matrices have independent and identically distributed entries of mean zero and unit variance. In this note, we consider the random matrix model formed by the ratio of two independent Girko matrices, its entries are dependent and heavy-tailed. Our main message is that divided by the square root of the dimension, the spectral radius of the ratio converges in distribution, when the dimension tends to infinity, to a universal heavy-tailed distribution. We provide a mathematical proof of this high-dimensional phenomenon, under a fourth moment matching with a Gaussian case known as the complex Ginibre ensemble. In this Gaussian case, the model is known as the spherical ensemble, and its spectrum is a determinantal planar Coulomb gas. Its image by the inverse stereographic projection is a rotationally invariant gas on the two-sphere. A crucial observation is the invariance in law of the model under inversion, related to its spherical symmetry, and that makes, in a sense, edge and bulk equivalent. Our approach involves Girko Hermitization, local law estimates for Wigner matrices, lower bound estimates on the smallest singular value, and convergence of kernels of determinantal point processes. The universality of the high-dimensional fluctuation of the spectral radius of the ratio of Girko matrices turns out to be remarkably more accessible mathematically than for a single Girko matrix!

1.
Introduction

The asymptotic analysis in high dimension of the spectral radius of non-normal
random matrices is a delicate subject, which goes back at least to

Cohen and Newman 1984
;
Geman 1986
;
Bai and Yin 1986
;
Rider 2003
. There has been recent progress in the case of
Girko random matrices, see for instance

Bordenave et al. 2022
;
Cipolloni et al. 2024a
;
Cipolloni et al. 2024b
;
Byun and Forrester 2025

and references therein. Girko matrices have independent square-integrable
entries. This note addresses the case of the ratio of two independent Girko
matrices, a model of non-normal random matrices with dependent entries.
For this model, we prove the universality of
the asymptotic behavior of the spectral radius in high dimension. This model,
in the special case of Ginibre matrices, known as the spherical ensemble,
becomes integrable and biunitary invariant, and the law of its spectrum is a
planar heavy-tailed Coulomb gas, with a determinantal structure. The spherical
ensemble is a matrix analogue and extension of the ratio of independent real
normal random variables, and can be then seen as a sort of matrix Cauchy law.

1.1.
Main results

We say that an
n\times n
random matrix
A=(A_{jk}:1\leq j,k\leq n)
is a
Girko matrix
when its entries are independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) in
\mathbb{C}
with
\mathbb{E}(A_{11})=0
and
\mathbb{E}(|A_{11}|^{2})=1
. We say that
A
is a
complex Ginibre matrix
in the special case where
A_{11}\sim\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}(0,\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Id}_{2})
, which is equivalent to state that
A
has density in
\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
proportional to
A\in\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\mapsto\exp(-\mathrm{Tr}(AA^{*}))
. To distinguish, we denote such a Ginibre matrix by
A^{\mathrm{Gin}}
, and we always write
\mathrm{Gin}
as a superscript to indicate this special case.

We consider the following assumptions or conditions on an
n\times n
Girko matrix
A
:

(C1)

the law of
A_{11}
has a bounded density function
\varphi
with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{C}
, and

\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\leq D_{0}\quad\text{ for some constant $D_{0}>0$.}

(C2)

Fourth moment matching condition.

There exists a small constant
c_{0}>0
such that

\mathcal{E}_{1}=\mathcal{E}_{2}=0,\quad\qquad\mathcal{E}_{3}\leq n^{-\frac{1}{2}-c_{0}},\quad\qquad\mathcal{E}_{4}\leq n^{-c_{0}},

where (
k
-th moment deviation of
A_{11}
from standard complex Gaussian)

\mathcal{E}_{k}=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b\in{\mathbb{N}}\\
a+b=k\end{subarray}}\left|\mathbb{E}[(\operatorname{Re}A_{11})^{a}(\operatorname{Im}A_{11})^{b}]-\mathbb{E}[(\operatorname{Re}A^{\mathrm{Gin}}_{11})^{a}(\operatorname{Im}A^{\mathrm{Gin}}_{11})^{b}]\right|.

(C3)

Finite moment condition.

For each
k\geq 1
, there exists constants
D_{k}>0
(independent of
n
) such that

\mathbb{E}[|A_{11}|^{k}]<D_{k}.

Condition
(C1)
implies that the
n\times n
complex matrix
A
is a.s. invertible for all
n
since the algebraic hypersurface
\{\det=0\}
has zero Lebesgue measure. Moreover, the uniform bound on the density allows a precise lower bound on the smallest singular value, see e.g.,
Jain et al. 2021
. More generally, we refer to
Tikhomirov 2023
for the invertibility of random matrices beyond the density case.

Condition
(C2)
states that the moments of
A_{11}
almost match those of
A^{\mathrm{Gin}}_{11}
up to the fourth order.

Condition
(C3)
is assumed for technical reasons to simplify a proof.

Theorem 1.2

(From ratio of Girko matrices to infinite Ginibre)
.

Let
M=AB^{-1}
where

be the infinite Ginibre determinantal point process on

K(z,w)=\sqrt{\gamma(z)\gamma(w)}\mathrm{e}^{z\overline{w}}

. Then, for all fixed
\lambda_{0}\in\mathbb{C}

n\mu_{\sqrt{n}(1+|\lambda_{0}|^{2})^{-1}(M-\lambda_{0}\mathrm{Id})}=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathrm{spec}(M)}\delta_{\sqrt{n}(1+|\lambda_{0}|^{2})^{-1}(\lambda-\lambda_{0})}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathrm{d}}\mathrm{Gin}_{\infty}.

Theorem 1.3

(Spectral radius)
.

Let
M
be as in Theorem
1.2
, and
{(\gamma_{k})}_{k\geq 1}
independent with
\gamma_{k}\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(k,1)
. Then

(1.5)

\frac{\rho_{\max}(M)}{\sqrt{n}}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathrm{d}}\mathrm{Law}\Bigr(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\min_{k\geq 1}\gamma_{k}}}\Bigr)\quad\text{and}\quad\sqrt{n}\rho_{\min}(M)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathrm{d}}\mathrm{Law}\bigr(\sqrt{\min_{k\geq 1}\gamma_{k}}\bigr).

Note that the laws of
R_{0}=\sqrt{\min_{k\geq 1}\gamma_{k}}
and
R_{\infty}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{R_{0}}
have respective cdf given for all
x>0
by

(1.6)

\mathbb{P}(R_{0}\leq x)=1-\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}(1-F_{k})(x^{2})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{P}(R_{\infty}\leq x)=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}(1-F_{k})(x^{-2})

where

F_{k}(x)=\mathbb{P}(\gamma_{k}<x)=\mathbb{P}(\pi_{x}<k)=\mathrm{e}^{-x}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{x^{j}}{j!}

with
\pi_{x}\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(x)
.
In particular
R_{\infty}
is heavy-tailed as

(1.7)

\mathbb{P}(R_{\infty}>x)\underset{x\to+\infty}{\sim}\frac{1}{x^{2}}.

Since
{(\xi_{k})}_{k\geq 1}
are not i.i.d., we are beyond classical extreme value theory :
R_{0}
is not Weibull, and
R_{\infty}
is not Fréchet.

Theorem
1.3
establishes, for the first time and to some extent, the universality of the high-dimensional behavior of the spectral radius of the spherical ensemble, studied by
Jiang and Qi 2017
when
A
and
B
are complex Ginibre matrices, see Section
2
. Theorem
1.3
is an analogue for the spherical ensemble of the universality of the Gumbel fluctuation for the complex Ginibre ensemble obtained by
Cipolloni et al. 2024b
.

The proofs of Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
are given in Section
3
. The strategy is as follows. By using the invariance of the model by inversion, we transform the behavior of the dilated spectral radius into the local behavior near the origin, more precisely a local gap probability at the origin. We then use the universality of the local behavior near the origin, stated as a replacement principle in Theorem
1.4
below. This reduces the problem to the local gap probability of the spherical ensemble, which in turn reduces to the infinite Ginibre ensemble by using kernel convergence of determinantal point processes. Finally, the local gap probability for the infinite Ginibre ensemble follows from the Kostlan observation of the moduli of the particles of this determinantal point process.

The spherical or Forrester – Krishnapur ensemble or model of dimension
n
is the random matrix defined by

(1.8)

M^{\mathrm{Gin}}=A^{\mathrm{Gin}}(B^{\mathrm{Gin}})^{-1}

where
A^{\mathrm{Gin}}
and
B^{\mathrm{Gin}}
are independent
n\times n
complex Ginibre matrices with i.i.d.
\mathcal{N}_{\mathbb{C}}(0,\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Id}_{2})

Theorem 1.4

(Replacement principle for local eigenvalue statistics near a point)
.

Let
M=AB^{-1}
be as in Theorem
1.2
and
M^{\mathrm{Gin}}
as in (
1.8
). Let
\mu_{M}
and
\mu_{M^{\mathrm{Gin}}}
be the empirical spectral measures as in (

). Then the following replacement principle on scale

occurs: for all

and all compactly supported
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Bigr(\mathbb{E}\Bigr[F\Bigr(\int f(\sqrt{n}(z-z_{0}))\mathrm{d}\mu_{M}(z)\Bigr)\Bigr]-\mathbb{E}\Bigr[F\Bigr(\int f(\sqrt{n}(z-z_{0}))\mathrm{d}\mu_{M^{\mathrm{Gin}}}(z)\Bigr)\Bigr]\Bigr)=0.

Theorem
1.4
is a simplified version of a more technical result stated in Theorem
4.1
and proved in Section
4
. It can be seen as a fourth moment theorem for the spherical ensemble.
The proof involves Ornstein – Uhlenbeck interpolation between Gaussian and non-Gaussian, a cumulant expansion exploiting the fourth moment matching
(C2)
, a local law for Wigner matrices and its consequence in terms of rigidity, and a precise lower bound on the smallest singular value.
The continuous interpolation that we use, based on the Ornstein – Uhlenbeck diffusion, differs from the original Lindeberg-type entry by entry replacement used by
Tao and Vu 2015
in their original fourth moment theorem.

1.2.
Comments and open problems

1.2.1.
Model.

We could use the alternative definitions
M=A^{-1}B
instead of
M=AB^{-1}
. Indeed
XY
and
YX
have same spectrum thanks to the Sylvester identity
\det(XY-z\mathrm{Id})=\det(YX-z\mathrm{Id})
.

1.2.2.
First order universality.

The universality of the limiting spectral distribution of the model was proved in
Bordenave 2011
, using logarithmic potential, Girko Hermitization, and Tao and Vu replacement principle. The limit can be interpreted by using the notions of freeness and Brown measure from Free Probability Theory.

1.2.3.
Inner radius of Girko matrices.

The analogue of Theorem
1.2
for a single Girko matrix
A
follows from
(
Maltsev and Osman 2024
, Th. 2.1 and 2.2)
, under a second moment matching assumption. This leads to the universality of the fluctuation of the inner spectral radius, which meets the gap at the origin of
\mathrm{Gin}_{\infty}
, namely

(1.9)

\sqrt{n}\rho_{\min}(A)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathrm{d}}\mathrm{Law}\Bigr(\sqrt{\min_{k\geq 1}\xi_{k}}\Bigr)\quad\text{where}\quad\text{${(\xi_{k})}_{k\geq 1}$ are independent with $\xi_{k}\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(k,1)$.}

1.2.4.
Moments assumptions.

The fourth moment matching in
(C2)
helps to reduce the complexity of the proof of Theorem
4.1
, essentially the bulk universality for Girko matrices, see for instance
Tao and Vu 2015
. Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
4.1
are expected to remain available with the sole second moment matching.

1.2.5.
Characteristic polynomials

It is natural to ask about the asymptotic analysis of the characteristic polynomial of
AB^{-1}
, in the spirit of

Bordenave et al. 2022
, with a random analytic object as a limit. Taking the modulus and the logarithm would then recover the CLT for the log-potential. This seems to be open even for the spherical model.

1.2.6.
Ratio of other models

We could ask about the behavior of the spectral radius of
AB^{-1}
if
A
and
B
are independent real or quaternion Girko matrices, and, in another direction, real/complex/quaternion Wigner matrices.

Regarding high dimensional asymptotic analysis, almost surely,

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3

The key idea in a nutshell is as follows: if
A
and
B
are independent and have the same law, then

(3.1)

M^{-1}\overset{\mathrm{d}}{=}M,\quad\text{which gives}\quad\rho_{\max}(M)\overset{\mathrm{d}}{=}\rho_{\max}(M^{-1})=\max_{\lambda\in\mathrm{spec}(M)}\frac{1}{|\lambda|}=\frac{1}{\min_{\lambda\in\mathrm{spec}(M)}|\lambda|}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{dist}(0,\mathrm{spec}(M))}.

This remains valid when
A
and
B
do not have the same law, provided that in the right hand side of the equality in distribution symbols we modify

by swapping the laws of
A
and
B
, which is not a problem for our universality objective. In other words, the model

is globally invariant in law by inversion. Geometrically, the inversion exchanges on the two-sphere the points
\infty
and
0
, the large scale behavior with the local scale behavior at the origin.

Proof of Theorem

Theorem

is an immediate consequence of the following more general and technical result, used with the choice

for
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{C}}\to\mathbb{R})
and
z_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

Theorem 4.1

(Comparison theorem)
.

Let
C_{0},C_{1}>0
and
\nu>0
be fixed and such that
\nu<10^{-4}c_{0}
where
c_{0}
is as in condition
(C2)
.

Let
{(f_{n})}
be a sequence of functions such that

(4.1)

f_{n}\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{2}({\mathbb{C}}\to\mathbb{R}),\qquad\int|\Delta_{z}f_{n}(z)|\mathrm{d}^{2}z\leq n^{\nu},\qquad\mathrm{supp}(f_{n})\subset\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|\leq C_{0}\}.

Let
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function with uniformly bounded derivatives by
C_{1}
up to fifth order, namely

(4.2)

\max_{1\leq k\leq 5}\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}}|F^{(k)}(x)|\leq C_{1}.

Then, for all
0<\delta<c_{0}/400
, we have

\Bigr|\mathbb{E}\Bigr[F\Bigr(\int f_{n}(z)\mathrm{d}\mu_{M}(z)\Bigr)\Bigr]-\mathbb{E}\Bigr[F\Bigr(\int f_{n}(z)\mathrm{d}\mu_{M^{\mathrm{Gin}}}(z)\Bigr)\Bigr]\Bigr|=O\big(n^{-\delta}\big).

Theorem
4.1
proves universality in a broad sense, not only for gap distribution or spectral radius, but also for CLT with test functions satisfying (
4.1
). One can also use it to show the universality of convergence speed, as long as the optimal speed is much smaller than
n^{-\delta}
for a sufficiently small
\delta>0
.

Before we present the proof of Theorem
4.1
, we sketch an outline of it, in three steps:

(1)

Via the Hermitization trick introduced by
Girko 1984
, one reduces the spectral problem of
AB^{-1}
near the point
z_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}
to studying the singular value statistics of
A-zB
with
z
near
z_{0}
, i.e.,

(4.3)

\mathcal{L}=\int f_{n}(x)\mathrm{d}\mu_{AB^{-1}}(x)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int\Delta_{z}f_{n}(z)\log\det[(A-zB)(A-zB)^{*}]\mathrm{d}^{2}z,

see more details in (
4.1
)-(
4.7
) below. In particular, assuming
B
is invertible,
z
is an eigenvalue of
AB^{-1}
is equivalent to that the smallest singular value of
A-zB
is zero. This translates the eigenvalue problem of the non-Hermitian matrix into that of the Hermitian model
(A-zB)(A-zB)^{*}
near zero.

(2)

To regularize the quantity on the right side of (
4.3
), we introduce a parameter
\eta_{0}
slightly below the typical size of the smallest singular value of
A-zB
near zero and show that (see Lemma
4.2
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\approx\mathcal{L}^{\eta_{0}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\int\Delta_{z}f_{n}(z)\log\det\big[(A-zB)(A-zB)^{*}+\eta_{0}^{2}\big]\mathrm{d}^{2}z

(4.4)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int\Delta_{z}f_{n}(z)\int_{\eta_{0}}^{T}\operatorname{Im}\mathrm{Tr}G^{z}(\mathrm{i}\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta\mathrm{d}^{2}z,\qquad\qquad\eta_{0}=n^{-1-\epsilon},\quad T=n^{100},

for a small
\epsilon>0
, where the approximation holds in the sense of absolute mean, and
G^{z}
is the resolvent (or Green function) of the Hermitised matrix of
A-zB
as defined in (
4.7
))-(
4.8
) below. This step relies on the effective lower bound of the smallest singular value of Girko matrices from
Jain et al. 2021
and the local law estimates for the Green function from
Ajanki et al. 2014
. It is worth noting that the polynomial lower bound for the smallest singular value obtained in
Bordenave and Chafaï 2012
and inspired from
Tao and Vu 2010
is not enough for our purposes regarding very tiny singular values, see (

).

(3)

From (

), it then suffices to show that under the fourth moment condition
(C2)
(see Lemma

),

\mathbb{E}[F(\mathcal{L}^{\eta_{0}})]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathrm{Gin}}[F(\mathcal{L}^{\eta_{0}})]+o(1),

where
\mathbb{E}^{\mathrm{Gin}}
indicates the corresponding expectation with
A
and
B
being Ginibre matrices. This step relies on the so-called long-time Green function comparison theorem (GFT). To achieve this, we let the matrix entries of
A
and
B
run independent Ornstein-Uhlenbeck dynamics. Then the corresponding matrix flow
A_{t}-zB_{t}
interpolates between
A-zB
and the Ginibre counterpart

. We use Itô’s formula and cumulant expansion formula to compute the time derivative of
\mathbb{E}[F(\mathcal{L}_{t}^{\eta_{0}})]
and then use the local law estimates for the Green function
and the fourth moment condition

to show the smallness of
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{E}[F(\mathcal{L}_{t}^{\eta_{0}})]
along the interpolating flow.

-integral into two parts, i.e.,

(4.13)

\displaystyle I^{\leq\eta_{0}}+I^{\geq\eta_{0}}=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int\Delta_{z}f_{n}(z)\Big(\int_{0}^{\eta_{0}}+\int_{\eta_{0}}^{T}\Big)\operatorname{Im}\mathrm{Tr}G^{z}(\mathrm{i}\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta\mathrm{d}^{2}z,\quad\qquad\eta_{0}=n^{-1-\epsilon},

for some small
\epsilon>0
to be fixed later. The lemma below shows that the first
\eta
-integral
I^{\leq\eta_{0}}
is negligible in the sense of absolute mean.

Lemma 4.2
.

Fix any small
\epsilon>0
and set
\eta_{0}=n^{-1-\epsilon}
. Then the following holds

\mathbb{E}\big|I^{\leq\eta_{0}}\big|=O\big(n^{-\epsilon/2+\nu}\big).

Recall that
c_{0}>0
is a small constant in
(C2)
and
\nu<10^{-4}c_{0}
from (
4.1
). Hence we can choose a small
\epsilon>0
such that
100\nu<\epsilon<c_{0}/100
, where the upper restriction of
\epsilon
will be used in Lemma
4.3
below. Note that
F
has a bounded derivative as in (
4.2
). Then by a simple Taylor expansion and using Lemma
4.2
with
\nu<\epsilon/100
, we have

(4.14)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[F\Big(\int f_{n}(x)\mathrm{d}\mu_{M}(x)\Big)\Big]=\mathbb{E}\big[F(I^{\geq\eta_{0}})\big]+O\big(n^{-\epsilon/4}\big).

Since the Ginibre matrix is also a Girko matrix, the above also applies to
M^{\mathrm{Gin}}=A^{\mathrm{Gin}}(B^{\mathrm{Gin}})^{-1}
with
{\tilde{A}}
and
{\tilde{B}}
replaced by
A^{\mathrm{Gin}}
and
B^{\mathrm{Gin}}
.
It then suffices to compare the expectation
\mathbb{E}\big[F(I^{\geq\eta_{0}})\big]
with its Ginibre counterpart
\mathbb{E}^{\mathrm{Gin}}\big[F(I^{\geq\eta_{0}})\big]
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Recall that
\nu<10^{-4}c_{0}
from (
4.1
). Fix any
0<\epsilon<c_{0}/100
and let
\eta_{0}=n^{-1+\epsilon}
. Then

(4.15)

\displaystyle\Big|\mathbb{E}\big[F(I^{\geq\eta_{0}})\big]-\mathbb{E}^{\mathrm{Gin}}\big[F(I^{\geq\eta_{0}})\big]\Big|=O(n^{-c_{0}/4}),

where we used
\mathbb{E}^{\mathrm{Gin}}
to denote the corresponding expectation with
{\tilde{A}}
and
{\tilde{B}}
being Ginibre matrices.

Therefore, Theorem
4.1
follows directly from (
4.14
) for both matrices
M
and
M^{\mathrm{Gin}}
in combination with Lemma
4.3
for
\epsilon<c_{0}/100
, where
c_{0}>0
is small constant from
(C2)
.

The remaining part of this section is to prove Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
4.3
.

Proof of Lemma
4.2
.

Recall that
H^{z}
in (
4.7
) is the Hermitised matrix of
X^{z}=\widetilde{A}-z\widetilde{B}
, and
G^{z}
is its resolvent defined in (
4.8
). For each fixed
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
X^{z}=(x_{ij})
is still a Girko matrix with
\mathbb{E}[x_{ij}]=0
,
\mathbb{E}[(x_{ij})^{2}]=0
and
\mathbb{E}[|x_{ij}|^{2}]=(1+|z|^{2})/n
. In particular if we set
z=0
, then
X^{z=0}
becomes a standard Girko matrix with variance
n^{-1}
. From
(
Ajanki et al. 2014
, Theorem 1.1)
(see also
Cacciapuoti et al. 2013
;
Bourgade et al. 2014
), fixing any small
\xi>0
, the following local laws for the resolvent
G^{z}
with
z=0

(4.16)

\displaystyle\Big|\big\langle\mathbf{x},\big(G^{z=0}(w)-m_{sc}(w)\mathbf{y}\big\rangle\Big|\prec\sqrt{\frac{1}{n\eta}},\qquad\Big|\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{Tr}G^{z=0}(w)-m_{sc}(w)\Big|\prec\frac{1}{n\eta},

hold uniformly for any
|w|\leq 10
and
\eta=|\operatorname{Im}w|\geq n^{-1+\xi}
and for any deterministic unit vectors
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, where
m_{sc}
is the Cauchy – Stieltjes transform of the semicircle distribution
\rho_{sc}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{(4-x^{2})_{+}}
. In general for any fixed
|z|\leq C_{0}
, by a simple rescaling, the following local laws for
G^{z}

(4.17)

\displaystyle\Big|\big\langle\mathbf{x},\big(G^{z}(w)-m^{z}(w)\mathbf{y}\big\rangle\Big|\prec\sqrt{\frac{1}{n\eta}},\qquad\Big|\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{Tr}G^{z}(w)-m^{z}(w)\Big|\prec\frac{1}{n\eta},

hold uniformly for any
|w|\leq 10
and
\eta=|\operatorname{Im}w|\geq n^{-1+\xi}
, with the deterministic function
m^{z}
given by

(4.18)

\displaystyle m^{z}(w)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|z|^{2}}}m_{sc}\Big(\frac{w}{\sqrt{1+|z|^{2}}}\Big),\qquad w\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbb{R}}.

It is easy to check that
m^{z}
is uniformly bounded by some constant for any
|w|\leq 10
and
|z|\leq C_{0}
. As a direct consequence of (
4.17
), we obtain the following estimate on the eigenvalue counting function
N(E_{1},E_{2})=\big\{\lambda_{j}\in\mathrm{Spec}(H^{z}):E_{1}\leq\lambda_{j}\leq E_{2}\big\}
:

(4.19)

\big|N(E_{1},E_{2})-N\int_{E_{1}}^{E_{2}}\rho^{z}(x)\mathrm{d}x\big|\prec 1\qquad E_{1}<E_{2},

where
\rho^{z}
is the probability distribution whose Cauchy – Stieltjes transform is given by (
4.18
). The proof of (
4.19
) is quite standard (see similar arguments used to prove
(
Benaych-Georges and Knowles 2017
, Theorem 2.8)
), so we omit it. We remark that though the above bound holds with very high probability for each fixed
z
, they can be extended to very high probability estimates simultaneously for all
|z|\leq C_{0}
by a grid argument and union bound, since
G^{z}
is Lipschitz continuous in
z
and
\|G^{z}(\mathrm{i}\eta)\|\leq\eta^{-1}
. Without specific mention, all the estimates below hold simultaneously for all
|z|\leq C_{0}
.

Now we are ready to prove Lemma
4.2
. By a simple spectral decomposition of
H^{z}
, we have

(4.20)

\displaystyle I^{\leq\eta_{0}}=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\Delta_{z}f_{n}(z)\Big[\int_{0}^{\eta_{0}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\eta}{(\lambda_{j}^{z})^{2}+\eta^{2}}\mathrm{d}\eta\Big]\mathrm{d}^{2}z,\qquad\eta_{0}=n^{-1-\epsilon},

where
\big(\lambda^{z}_{j}\big)_{j=1}^{n}
are the ordered non-negative eigenvalues of
H^{z}
, that are also singular values of
X^{z}
.
Let
L>0
be a large number to be fixed later. Set
\eta_{L}=n^{-L}
and
\eta_{2}=n^{-1-\epsilon/2}
. Taking the expectation, we split the above sum of eigenvalues into three parts as below:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{\eta_{0}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\eta}{(\lambda^{z}_{j})^{2}+\eta^{2}}\mathrm{d}\eta\mathrm{d}^{2}z\Big]=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{\eta_{0}}\sum_{\lambda^{z}_{j}\geq\eta_{2}}\frac{\eta}{(\lambda_{j}^{z})^{2}+\eta^{2}}\mathrm{d}\eta\mathrm{d}^{2}z\Big]

(4.21)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\sum_{\eta_{L}\leq\lambda^{z}_{j}\leq\eta_{2}}+\sum_{\lambda^{z}_{j}\leq\eta_{L}}\Big)\log\Big(1+\frac{\eta_{0}^{2}}{(\lambda^{z}_{j})^{2}}\Big)\Big],

where we also used that
\partial_{\eta}\log(\lambda^{2}+\eta^{2})=\frac{2\eta}{\lambda^{2}+\eta^{2}}
in the last line. For the first line of (
4.1
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{\eta_{0}}\sum_{\lambda_{j}\geq\eta_{2}}\frac{\eta}{\lambda_{j}^{2}+\eta^{2}}\mathrm{d}\eta\Big]\leq

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{\eta_{0}}\sum_{\lambda_{j}\geq\eta_{2}}\frac{\eta}{\lambda_{j}^{2}+\eta_{2}^{2}}\mathrm{d}\eta\Big]

(4.22)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\eta_{0}^{2}}{(\lambda^{z}_{j})^{2}+\eta_{2}^{2}}\Big]=\frac{\eta_{0}^{2}}{2\eta_{2}}\mathbb{E}\big[\operatorname{Im}\mathrm{Tr}G^{z}(\mathrm{i}\eta_{2})\big].

Note that the function
\eta\operatorname{Im}\mathrm{Tr}G^{z}(\mathrm{i}\eta)
is non-decreasing in
\eta>0
. Choosing
\eta_{3}=n^{-1+\xi}
for a small
\xi<\epsilon/100
,

(4.23)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{\eta_{0}}\sum_{\lambda_{j}\geq\eta_{2}}\frac{\eta}{\lambda_{j}^{2}+\eta^{2}}\mathrm{d}\eta\Big]\leq\frac{\eta_{0}^{2}}{\eta_{2}}\frac{\eta_{3}}{\eta_{2}}\mathbb{E}\big[\operatorname{Im}\mathrm{Tr}G^{z}(\mathrm{i}\eta_{3})\big]=O(n^{-\epsilon/2}),

where we used the local law in (
4.17
) and (
4.18
) for
\eta_{3}=n^{-1+\xi}
.

We next estimate the last line of (
4.1
). Note that the non-negative eigenvalues
\big(\lambda^{z}_{i}\big)_{i=1}^{n}
of
H^{z}
coincide with the singular values of
X^{z}=(A-zB)/\sqrt{n}
, where
A
and
B
are independent Girko matrices satisfying
(C1)
-
(C3)
.
Recall from
(
Jain et al. 2021
, Lemma 3.4)
(see also
Tikhomirov 2020
;
Erdős and Ji 2024
)
that there exists a constant
C
depending on
C_{0}
and
D_{0}
from
(C1)
such that the smallest singular value
\lambda_{1}^{z}
of
(A-zB)/\sqrt{n}
with
|z|\leq C_{0}
satisfies

(4.24)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\lambda^{z}_{1}\leq t\big)\leq Cn^{2}t^{2},\qquad\forall t>0,\quad|z|\leq C_{0}.

Moreover, from the bound (
4.19
) on the eigenvalue counting function near the origin, we obtain that

(4.25)

\displaystyle\#\big\{j\in[n]:\lambda^{z}_{j}\leq n^{-1}\big\}\leq n^{\xi^{\prime}},

for any small
\xi^{\prime}>0
, with very high probability. Using (
4.24
) for
t=n^{-\epsilon/2}
and (
4.25
) for
\xi^{\prime}<\epsilon/100
, the first sum in the last line of (
4.1
) is bounded by

(4.26)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{\eta_{L}\leq\lambda^{z}_{j}\leq\eta_{2}}\log\Big(1+\frac{\eta_{0}^{2}}{(\lambda^{z}_{j})^{2}}\Big)\Big]\leq n^{\xi^{\prime}}(\log n)\mathbb{P}\big(\lambda^{z}_{1}\leq\eta_{2}\big)=O(n^{-\epsilon/2}).

For the last part in (
4.1
) with tiny eigenvalues
\lambda^{z}_{j}\leq\eta_{L}=n^{-L}
, using that
\log(1+|x|)\leq 2\log|x|
for
|x|>10
and the tail bound for the smallest singular value in (
4.24
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{\lambda^{z}_{j}\leq\eta_{L}}\log\Big(1+\frac{\eta_{0}^{2}}{(\lambda^{z}_{j})^{2}}\Big)\Big]\leq

\displaystyle 4\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{\lambda^{z}_{j}\leq\eta_{L}}-\log(\lambda^{z}_{j})\Big]\leq 4n\mathbb{E}\Big[-\log\big(\lambda^{z}_{1}\big)\mathbf{1}_{\lambda^{z}_{1}\leq\eta_{L}}\Big]

(4.27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4n\int_{L\log n}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\big(\lambda^{z}_{1}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-s}\big)\mathrm{d}s=O(n^{-100}),

by choosing a sufficiently large
L>0
depending on the constants
C_{0}
and
D_{0}>0
from
(C1)
.

Summing up (
4.23
), (
4.26
) and (
4.1
), we conclude from (
4.20
) and (
4.1
) that

(4.28)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|I^{\leq\eta_{0}}|\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\big|\Delta_{z}f_{n}(z)\big|\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{\eta_{0}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\eta}{(\lambda_{j}^{z})^{2}+\eta^{2}}\mathrm{d}\eta\Big]\mathrm{d}^{2}z=O\big(n^{-\epsilon/2+\nu}\big),

where we also used that
\int|\Delta_{z}f_{n}(z)|\mathrm{d}^{2}z\leq n^{\nu}
. This finished the proof of Lemma
4.2
.

∎

Proof of Lemma
4.3
.

Define the following the Ornstein – Uhlenbeck matrix flow

(4.29)

\mathrm{d}{\tilde{A}}_{t}=-\frac{1}{2}{\tilde{A}}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{M}^{(1)}_{t}}{\sqrt{n}},\qquad\mathrm{d}{\tilde{B}}_{t}=-\frac{1}{2}{\tilde{B}}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{M}^{(2)}_{t}}{\sqrt{n}},

with initial ensembles
{\tilde{A}}_{0}={\tilde{A}}=A/\sqrt{n}
and
{\tilde{B}}_{0}={\tilde{B}}=B/\sqrt{n}
, where
A,B
are independent Girko matrices satisfying
(C1)
–
(C3)
, and
\mathcal{M}^{(1)}_{t}
and
\mathcal{M}^{(2)}_{t}
are two independent
n\times n
matrices whose entries are i.i.d. standard complex Brownian motions. Then for any
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
we define
X^{z}_{t}={\tilde{A}}_{t}-z{\tilde{B}}_{t}
as in (
4.6
). In distribution, it satisfies

(4.30)

\mathrm{d}X^{z}_{t}=-\frac{1}{2}X^{z}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{\frac{1+|z|^{2}}{n}}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{M}_{t},\qquad X^{z}_{0}=\frac{A-zB}{\sqrt{n}},

where
\mathcal{M}_{t}
is a standard complex Brownian motion matrix.
For notational simplicity, we may often drop the dependence on
z
or
t
and write
X_{t}^{z}=(x_{ij})_{i,j\in[2n]}
. We use
X^{z}_{t}
to construct the Hermitised matrix
H^{z}_{t}=(h_{ij})_{i,j\in[2n]}
as in (
4.7
) and define the corresponding Green function
G^{z}_{t}(w)=(H^{z}_{t}-w)^{-1}
as in (
4.8
). In particular,

(4.31)

\displaystyle h_{aB}=\overline{h_{Ba}}=x_{a,B-n},\quad\qquad h_{Ba}=\overline{h_{aB}},\quad\qquad a\in[n],~B\in[n,2n],

and all the other components of
H^{z}_{t}
are equal to zero. Note that
(x_{ij})_{i,j\in[n]}
are time-dependent random variables which also depend on
z
. Recall that the
(p,q)
-cumulant of a complex-valued random variable
\chi
with finite moments are defined to be

(4.32)

\displaystyle\kappa^{(p,q)}(\chi)=(-\mathrm{i})^{p+q}\Big(\frac{\partial^{p+q}}{\partial s^{p}\partial t^{q}}\log\mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}s\chi+\mathrm{i}t\overline{\chi}}\big]\Big)\Big|_{s,t=0}.

From
(C2)
-
(C3)
, it is easy to check that second cumulants of
(x_{ij})_{i,j\in[n]}
are invariant with time

(4.33)

\displaystyle\kappa^{(1,1)}(x_{ij})=\frac{1+|z|^{2}}{n},\qquad\kappa^{(0,2)}(x_{ij})=\kappa^{(2,0)}(x_{ij})=0,

and the higher-order cumulants are bounded by

(4.34)

\displaystyle|\kappa^{(p,q)}(x_{ij})|\lesssim n^{-2-c_{0}},\qquad p+q=3,4,\qquad|\kappa^{(p,q)}(x_{ij})|\lesssim n^{-\frac{p+q}{2}},\qquad p+q\geq 5,

uniformly for any
t\geq 0
and
|z|\leq C_{0}
.

Recall the definition of
I^{\geq\eta_{0}}
in (
4.13
). Note that
\mathrm{Tr}G^{z}(\mathrm{i}\eta)
is purely imaginary due to the block structure of
H^{z}
in (
4.7
), so we have
\mathrm{Tr}G^{z}(\mathrm{i}\eta)=\mathrm{i}\operatorname{Im}\mathrm{Tr}G^{z}(\mathrm{i}\eta)
. Then we define the following short-hand

(4.35)

\displaystyle I^{\geq\eta_{0}}_{t}=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{4\pi}\int\Delta_{z}f_{n}(z)\int_{\eta_{0}}^{T}\mathrm{Tr}G^{z}_{t}(\mathrm{i}\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta\mathrm{d}^{2}z,

with
T=n^{100}
and
\eta_{0}=n^{-1-\epsilon}
for
0<\epsilon<c_{0}/100
.

We then use Itô’s formula to derive the dynamics of
F(I^{\geq\eta_{0}}_{t})
as a function of
X^{z}_{t}=\{x_{ij}\}_{i,j\in[n]}
given by (
4.30
). By direct computations and the relation (
4.31
), we obtain

(4.36)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}F(I^{\geq\eta_{0}}_{t})}{\mathrm{d}t}=\sum_{a=1}^{n}\sum_{B=n+1}^{2n}\left(-\frac{1}{2}h_{aB}\frac{\partial F(I^{\geq\eta_{0}}_{t})}{\partial h_{aB}}-\frac{1}{2}\overline{h_{aB}}\frac{\partial F(I^{\geq\eta_{0}}_{t})}{\partial\overline{h_{aB}}}+\frac{1+|z|^{2}}{n}\frac{\partial^{2}F(I^{\geq\eta_{0}}_{t})}{\partial h_{aB}\partial\overline{h_{aB}}}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}M,

where
\mathrm{d}M
denotes the corresponding diffusion term with vanishing expectation, due to the martingale property of Brownian motions. We next take the expectation of the remaining drift terms in (
4.36
). Note that the differentiation rules below are obtained from the definition of resolvent in (
4.8
):

(4.37)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial(G^{z}_{t})_{xy}}{\partial h_{aB}}=-(G^{z}_{t})_{xa}(G^{z}_{t})_{By},\qquad\frac{\partial(G^{z}_{t})_{xy}}{\partial\overline{h_{aB}}}=-(G^{z}_{t})_{xB}(G^{z}_{t})_{ay},

for any
x,y\in[2n]
,
a\in[n]
, and
B\in[n,2n]
. Recall that
\kappa^{(p+1,q)}(h_{aB})
and
\kappa^{(p+1,q)}(\overline{h_{aB}})
are the
(p+1,q)
-cumulants of the complex valued random variables from (
4.31
). Applying the cumulant expansion formula (for a formal statement, see
(
He and Knowles 2017
, Lemma 7.1)
) to the expectation of the first two terms in (
4.36
), the resulting second-order cumulant terms from (
4.33
) will cancel precisely with the last drift term in (
4.36
). This key cancellation is a consequence of the fact that the second moments of the matrix entries of
X^{z}_{t}
do not change with time. Hence, we start with the third-order terms (
p+q+1=3
) in the cumulant expansions, i.e.,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{E}\big[F(I^{\geq\eta_{0}}_{t})\big]}{\mathrm{d}t}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a=1}^{n}\sum_{B=n+1}^{2n}\left(\sum_{p+q+1=3}^{K_{0}}\frac{\kappa^{(p+1,q)}(h_{aB})}{p!q!}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial^{p+q+1}F(I^{\geq\eta_{0}}_{t})}{\partial h_{aB}^{p+1}\partial\overline{h_{aB}}^{q}}\right]\right)

(4.38)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a=1}^{n}\sum_{B=n+1}^{2n}\left(\sum_{p+q+1=3}^{K_{0}}\frac{\kappa^{(p+1,q)}(\overline{h_{aB}})}{p!q!}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial^{p+q+1}F(I^{\geq\eta_{0}}_{t})}{\partial\overline{h_{aB}}^{p+1}\partial h_{aB}^{q}}\right]\right)+O(n^{-10}),

where we stopped the expansion at a sufficiently high order with
p+q+1=K_{0}
such that the truncation error is bounded by
O(n^{-10})
, using the cumulant bounds in (
4.34
), the differentiation rule in (
4.37
), the deterministic bound
\|G_{t}^{z}(\mathrm{i}\eta)\|\leq\eta^{-1}
, together with the local law estimates in (
4.48
) below. Such truncation argument is standard and frequently used in previous works e.g.,
He and Knowles 2017
;
Schnelli and Xu 2022
;
Cipolloni et al. 2024b
, so we omit the details. We remark that the last error term holds uniformly for any
|z|\leq C_{0}
and
0\leq t\leq 100\log n
.

To estimate the third and higher order terms with
3\leq p+q+1\leq K_{0}
, we introduce the following lemma, whose proof is postponed till the end of this section.

Lemma 4.4
.

Recall
I^{\geq\eta_{0}}_{t}
in (
4.35
) with
\eta_{0}=n^{-1-\epsilon}
and compactly supported
f_{n}
satisfying
\int|\Delta_{z}f_{n}(z)|\mathrm{d}^{2}z\leq n^{\nu}
. Then
for any
k=k_{1}+k_{2}\geq 1
, we have

(4.39)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,t_{0}]}\Big|\frac{\partial^{k}I^{\geq\eta_{0}}_{t}}{\partial(h_{aB})^{k_{1}}(\overline{h_{aB}})^{k_{2}}}\Big|\prec n^{2k\epsilon+\nu},\qquad t_{0}=100\log n.

Using the cumulant bounds in (
4.34
) and the differentiation bounds in (
4.39
) to estimate the third and higher order terms on the right side of (
4.1
), we obtain that

(4.40)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,t_{0}]}\Big|\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{E}\big[F(I^{\geq\eta_{0}}_{t})\big]}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big|=O(n^{-c_{0}/2}),

where we also used that
\epsilon<c_{0}/100
and
\nu<10^{-4}c_{0}
. Integrating (
4.40
) over
t\in[0,t_{0}]
with
t_{0}=100\log n
we obtain

(4.41)

\displaystyle\Big|\mathbb{E}\big[F(I^{\geq\eta_{0}}_{0})\big]-\mathbb{E}\big[F(I^{\geq\eta_{0}}_{t_{0}})\big]\Big|=O(n^{-c_{0}/4}).

Note that
H^{z}_{t}
is defined as in (
4.7
) with the time dependent matrix
X^{z}_{t}
from (
4.30
), which in distribution equals to

X^{z}_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\rm d}}}{{=}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{t}{2}}X^{z}+\sqrt{1-\mathrm{e}^{-t}}X^{\mathrm{Gin}},\qquad t\geq 0,

with
X^{z}_{0}=X^{z}=(A-zB)/\sqrt{n}
and
X^{z}_{\infty}=X^{\mathrm{Gin}}
being the Gaussian counterpart which is independent of
X^{z}
. Using the resolvent identity, we obtain

(4.42)

\displaystyle\|G^{z}_{t_{0}}(\mathrm{i}\eta)-G^{z}_{\infty}(\mathrm{i}\eta)\|\leq\|G^{z}_{t_{0}}(\mathrm{i}\eta)\|\|G^{z}_{\infty}(\mathrm{i}\eta)\|\|X^{z}_{t_{0}}-X^{z}_{\infty}\|\prec n^{-50},\qquad\eta\geq\eta_{0},

where we also used that
\|G(\mathrm{i}\eta)\|\leq\eta^{-1}
and
|x_{ij}|\prec n^{-1/2}
from condition
(C3)
. Thus using that
F
has uniformly bounded derivative and
\int|\Delta_{z}f(z)|\mathrm{d}^{2}z\leq n^{\nu}
with
\nu<10^{-4}c_{0}
, we obtain from (
4.35
) that

(4.43)

\displaystyle\Big|\mathbb{E}\big[F(I^{\geq\eta_{0}}_{t_{0}})\big]-\mathbb{E}\big[F(I^{\geq\eta_{0}}_{\infty})\big]\Big|=O(n^{-10}).

Combining (
4.41
) with (
4.43
), we conclude

(4.44)

\displaystyle\Big|\mathbb{E}\big[F(I^{\geq\eta_{0}})\big]-\mathbb{E}^{\mathrm{Gin}}\big[F(I^{\geq\eta_{0}})\big]\Big|=\Big|\mathbb{E}\big[F(I^{\geq\eta_{0}}_{0})\big]-\mathbb{E}\big[F(I^{\geq\eta_{0}}_{\infty})\big]\Big|=O(n^{-c_{0}/4}).

We hence finish the proof of Lemma
4.3
. ∎

We end this section with the proofs of Lemma
4.4
.

Proof of Lemma
4.4
.

Recall
I^{\geq\eta_{0}}_{t}
in (
4.35
). Using (
4.37
) and
G^{2}=\mathrm{i}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\eta}G(\mathrm{i}\eta)
, we further have

(4.45)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial I^{\geq\eta_{0}}_{t}}{\partial h_{aB}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{4\pi}\int\Delta_{z}f_{n}(z)\int_{\eta_{0}}^{T}\big[\big(G_{t}^{z}(\mathrm{i}\eta)\big)^{2}\big]_{Ba}\mathrm{d}\eta\mathrm{d}^{2}z=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int\Delta_{z}f_{n}(z)\big(G^{z}_{t}(\mathrm{i}\eta_{0})\big)_{Ba}\mathrm{d}^{2}z+O_{\prec}(n^{-10}),

where we also used that
\|G(\mathrm{i}\eta)\|\leq\eta^{-1}
for
\eta=T=n^{100}
. Note that the variances of entries of
X^{z}_{t}
do not change with time (see (
4.33
)). Hence, for each fixed
t\geq 0
and
|z|\leq C_{0}
, the local law as in (
4.17
) also holds true for
G^{z}_{t}
with very high probability. To estimate the right side of (
4.45
) with
\eta_{0}=n^{-1-\epsilon}
, we next extend the estimates in (
4.17
) down to
\eta=n^{-1-\epsilon}
. Setting
\eta_{1}=n^{-1+\xi}
with
\xi\leq\epsilon/100
and using that
G^{2}=\mathrm{i}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\eta}G(\mathrm{i}\eta)
, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\big\langle\mathbf{x},G^{z}(\mathrm{i}\eta_{1})\mathbf{y}\big\rangle-\big\langle\mathbf{x},G^{z}(\mathrm{i}\eta_{0})\mathbf{y}\big\rangle\Big|=

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\eta_{0}}^{\eta_{1}}\big\langle\mathbf{x},\big(G^{z}(\mathrm{i}\eta)\big)^{2}\mathbf{y}\big\rangle\mathrm{d}\eta\Big|

(4.46)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\eta_{0}}^{\eta_{1}}\frac{1}{\eta}\sqrt{\big\langle\mathbf{x},\operatorname{Im}G^{z}(\mathrm{i}\eta)\mathbf{x}\big\rangle\big\langle\mathbf{y},\operatorname{Im}G^{z}(\mathrm{i}\eta)\mathbf{y}\big\rangle}\mathrm{d}\eta

for any deterministic unit vectors
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, where we also used the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the Ward identity
GG^{*}=\eta^{-1}\operatorname{Im}G
. By a simple spectral decomposition of
H^{z}
, it is easy to check that the function
\eta\big\langle\mathbf{x},\operatorname{Im}G^{z}(\mathrm{i}\eta)\mathbf{x}\big\rangle
is increasing in
\eta>0
. Hence we obtain

(4.47)

\displaystyle\Big|\big\langle\mathbf{x},G^{z}(\mathrm{i}\eta_{1})\mathbf{y}\big\rangle-\big\langle\mathbf{x},G^{z}(\mathrm{i}\eta_{0})\mathbf{y}\big\rangle\Big|\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\big\langle\mathbf{x},\operatorname{Im}G^{z}(\mathrm{i}\eta_{1})\mathbf{x}\big\rangle\big\langle\mathbf{x},\operatorname{Im}G^{z}(\mathrm{i}\eta_{1})\mathbf{x}\big\rangle}\int_{\eta_{0}}^{\eta_{1}}\frac{\eta_{1}}{\eta^{2}}\mathrm{d}\eta\prec n^{2\epsilon}

where we also used that
\big\langle\mathbf{x},\operatorname{Im}G^{z}(\mathrm{i}\eta_{1})\mathbf{x}\big\rangle\prec 1
from (
4.17
) and (
4.18
) for
\eta_{1}=n^{-1+\xi}
with
\xi<\epsilon/100
. Again using the local law from (
4.17
) and (
4.18
) for
\eta_{1}=n^{-1+\xi}
,
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{e}_{x}
, and
\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{e}_{y}
, we conclude that, for each fixed
|z|\leq C_{0}
and
t\geq 0
,

\max_{x,y\in[2n]}\Big|\big(G^{z}_{t}(\mathrm{i}\eta_{0})\big)_{xy}\Big|\prec n^{2\epsilon},\qquad\mbox{~with~}\quad\eta_{0}=n^{-1-\epsilon}.

Due to the Lipschitz continuity of
G^{z}_{t}
in both
t
and
z
, one can show that the above bound indeed holds true simultaneously for any
t\in[0,t_{0}]
with
t_{0}=100\log n
and
|z|\leq C_{0}
, i.e.,

(4.48)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,t_{0}]}\sup_{|z|\leq C_{0}}\left\{\max_{x,y\in[2n]}\Big|\big(G^{z}_{t}(\mathrm{i}\eta_{0})\big)_{xy}\Big|\right\}\prec n^{2\epsilon},\qquad\mbox{~with~}\quad\eta_{0}=n^{-1-\epsilon},

using a grid argument and union bound.
Hence using that
\int|\Delta_{z}f_{n}(z)|\mathrm{d}^{2}z\leq n^{\nu}
, we conclude from (
4.45
) that

(4.49)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,t_{0}]}\Big|\frac{\partial I^{\geq\eta_{0}}_{t}}{\partial h_{aB}}\Big|\prec n^{2\epsilon+\nu}.

The same result also applies to
\partial/\partial\overline{h_{aB}}
. In general, for any
k\geq 1
, using the differentiation rules in (
4.37
) and the local law estimates in (
4.48
) repeatedly, we obtain the estimate for any
k
-th derivatives of
I^{\geq\eta_{0}}_{t}
as stated in (
4.39
). This ends the proof of Lemma
4.4
.
∎

5.
Lemmas on weak convergence

We collect here classical results used in the proof of Theorem
1.2
. The first one
states that we can consider compactly supported smooth functions instead of continuous ones in the definition of weak convergence. This is a natural and familiar variant of the Portmanteau theorem that we were not able to locate in a textbook. The second one states that
the convergence of point processes is characterized by compactly supported smooth test functions. The third and final one
is about the delta method in
the context of point process.

Lemma 5.1

(Characterization of convergence in law)
.

If
X,X_{1},X_{2},\ldots
are random variables such that

(5.1)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[F(X_{n})]=\mathbb{E}[F(X)]

for every
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
compactly supported
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,
then the sequence
{(X_{n})}_{n\geq 1}
converges in law to
X
.

Proof.

First, notice that if (
5.1
) is true for compactly supported smooth functions, then it is true for compactly supported continuous functions.
Indeed, any compactly supported continuous function

F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a uniform limit of
compactly supported smooth functions

F_{k}
. Then, for every
k\geq 1
we have

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[F_{k}(X_{n})]-\|F-F_{k}\|_{\infty}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[F(X_{n})]\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[F(X_{n})]\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[F_{k}(X_{n})]+\|F-F_{k}\|_{\infty}

which implies the desired equality by taking
k\to\infty
.

Next, assuming that (
5.1
) is true for compactly supported smooth functions,
we may show that
(X_{n})_{n\geq 1}

is tight.
For this, we may take a smooth function

g:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]

supported on
[-1,1]

such that
g|_{[-1/2,1/2]}\equiv 1
.
Using that
\lim_{M\to\infty}g(x/M)=1

and that
g
is bounded by
1
, we obtain that

\lim_{M\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[g(X/M)]=1
.
Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
, we may find
an
M>0
such that

\mathbb{E}[f(X/M)]>1-\varepsilon
.
Finally, since

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[g(X_{n}/M)]=\mathbb{E}[g(X/M)]
, we know that

\mathbb{E}[g(X_{n}/M)]>1-\varepsilon

for
n
large enough so that

\mathbb{P}(|X_{n}|\leq M)\geq\mathbb{E}[g(X_{n}/M)]>1-\varepsilon.

Finally, if
F
is a bounded continuous function,
we may write
F(x)=F(x)g(x/M)+F(x)(1-g(x/M))
so that

\mathbb{E}[F(X)g(X/M)]-\|F\|_{\infty}\sup_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n}|\geq M/2)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[F(X_{n})]

and

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[F(X_{n})]\leq\mathbb{E}[F(X)g(X/M)]+\|F\|_{\infty}\sup_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{P}(|X_{n}|\geq M/2).

Taking
M\to\infty
we get the desired equality.

We may have also done a quicker proof
by using Prokhorov’s theorem (since we already showed tightness)
and noticing that a random variable is characterized
by the expected values of smooth compactly supported functions
.
∎

Lemma 5.2

(Point processes)
.

Let
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}_{1},\mathcal{X}_{2},\ldots
be point processes on
\mathbb{C}
such that

\int_{\mathbb{C}}f\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathrm{law}}\int_{\mathbb{C}}f\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathcal{X}}

for every smooth compactly supported function
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
.
Then, the same holds for every continuous compactly supported function
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
,
in other words the sequence
(\mathcal{X}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
converges in law towards
\mathcal{X}
.

Proof.

Let us show that for all bounded Lipschitz
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and continous and compactly supported
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big[F\Big(\int_{\mathbb{C}}f\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}\Big)\Big]=\mathbb{E}\Big[F\Big(\int_{\mathbb{C}}f\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathcal{X}}\Big)\Big].

Let
(f_{k})_{k\geq 1}

be a sequence of smooth functions whose support
is included in the common compact set
K
with

\|f-f_{k}\|_{\infty}\to 0
.
By denoting

\nu_{n}=\mu_{\mathcal{X}_{n}}
and
\nu_{\infty}=\mu_{\mathcal{X}}
,
we can write

\Big|\mathbb{E}\Bigr[F\Bigr(\int f\mathrm{d}\nu_{n}\Bigr)\Bigr]-\mathbb{E}\Bigr[F\Bigr(\int f\mathrm{d}\nu_{\infty}\Bigr)\Bigr]\Big|\leq T_{n}^{(k)}+T_{\infty}^{(k)}+S_{n}^{(k)},

where

T_{n}^{(k)}=\Big|\mathbb{E}\Bigr[F\Bigr(\int f\mathrm{d}\nu_{n}\Bigr)\Bigr]-\mathbb{E}\Bigr[F\Bigr(\int f_{k}\mathrm{d}\nu_{n}\Bigr)\Bigr]\Big|\quad\mbox{ and }\quad S^{(k)}_{n}=\Big|\mathbb{E}\Bigr[F\Bigr(\int f_{k}\mathrm{d}\nu_{n}\Bigr)\Bigr]-\mathbb{E}\Bigr[F\Bigr(\int f_{k}\mathrm{d}\nu_{\infty}\Bigr)\Bigr]\Big|.

Since
F
is Lipschitz and bounded, there is a constant
C>0

such that

|F(x)-F(y)|\leq C\min\{|x-y|,1\}
. We have

\limsup_{n\to\infty}T_{n}^{(k)}\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\big[C\min\big\{\|f-f_{k}\|_{\infty}\nu_{n}(K),1\big\}\big]\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big[H^{(k)}\Big(\int g\mathrm{d}\nu_{n}\Big)\Big],

where we have
taken a compactly supported smooth function

g:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

1_{K}\leq g
and we have
defined
H^{(k)}(x)=C\min\{\|f-f_{k}\|_{\infty}|x|,1\}
.
Since
H^{(k)}
is continuous and bounded we get

\limsup_{n\to\infty}T_{n}^{(k)}\leq\mathbb{E}\Big[H^{(k)}\Big(\int g\mathrm{d}\nu_{\infty}\Big)\Big]

so that we have

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\Big|\mathbb{E}\Bigr[F\Bigr(\int f\mathrm{d}\nu_{n}\Bigr)\Bigr]-\mathbb{E}\Bigr[F\Bigr(\int f\mathrm{d}\nu_{\infty}\Bigr)\Bigr]\Big|\leq 2\mathbb{E}\Big[H^{(k)}\Big(\int g\mathrm{d}\nu_{\infty}\Big)\Big].

Since
H^{(k)}
converges pointwise to zero as
k
goes to
\infty
, we may apply the dominated convergence theorem to

H^{(k)}(\int g\mathrm{d}\nu_{\infty})
to get

\lim_{k\to\infty}2\mathbb{E}[H^{(k)}(\int g\mathrm{d}\nu_{\infty})]=0
so that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\Big|\mathbb{E}\Bigr[F\Bigr(\int f\mathrm{d}\nu_{n}\Bigr)\Bigr]-\mathbb{E}\Bigr[F\Bigr(\int f\mathrm{d}\nu_{\infty}\Bigr)\Bigr]\Big|=0,

which concludes the proof.

∎

The following lemma is a consequence of the
classical fact that if
f_{n}:M\to M

is a sequence of measurable functions on
a locally compact metric space
M
converging uniformly on
compact sets to the identity function and

\mathcal{X}_{n}
is a sequence of point processes
converging to
\mathcal{X}

then
f_{n}(\mathcal{X}_{n})
also converges to
\mathcal{X}
.
For convenience
of the reader, we give
a direct proof in the particular case
that interests us which can be seen
as a “delta method” lemma.

Lemma 5.3

(Delta-method for point processes)
.

Let
(\mathcal{X}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence
of point processes on an open set

U\subset\mathbb{C}
and fix
x_{0}\in U
. Consider two open sets
V_{1},V_{2}\subset\mathbb{C}

containing the origin
together with diffeomorphisms

\varphi_{i}:U\to V_{i}
.
Then

\sqrt{n}(\varphi_{1}(\mathcal{X}_{n})-\varphi_{1}(x_{0}))

converges in law if and only if
\sqrt{n}(\varphi_{2}(\mathcal{X}_{n})-\varphi_{2}(x_{0}))

also does and, in that case,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt{n}\big(\varphi_{1}(\mathcal{X}_{n})-\varphi_{1}(x_{0})\big)=(\mathrm{d}\varphi_{1})_{x_{0}}\circ(\mathrm{d}\varphi_{2})_{x_{0}}^{-1}\Big(\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt{n}\big(\varphi_{2}(\mathcal{X}_{n})-\varphi_{2}(x_{0})\big)\Big).

Proof.

First, by changing
\varphi_{i}
by

\varphi_{i}-\varphi_{i}(x_{0})
, we may consider
\varphi_{i}(x_{0})=0
.
Then, under this assumption, by considering the point process

\mathcal{Y}_{n}=\varphi_{1}(\mathcal{X}_{n})

and the maps

\psi_{i}=\varphi_{i}\circ\varphi_{1}^{-1}

the statement reduces to the following statement.
Let
\mathcal{Y}_{n}
be a sequence of point
processes on
V_{1}
that contains the origin
such that

\sqrt{n}\mathcal{Y}_{n}
converges. If

\psi:V_{1}\to V_{2}
is a diffeomorphism
that satisfies
\psi(0)=0
then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt{n}\psi(\mathcal{Y}_{n})=(\mathrm{d}\psi)_{0}\big(\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt{n}\mathcal{Y}_{n}\big).

To show this, let us consider
a compactly supported smooth function
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}

and let us study the difference

\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}_{n}}f(\sqrt{n}\psi(y))-\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}_{n}}f(\sqrt{n}\mathrm{d}\psi_{0}(y))
.
Write
\psi(x)=\mathrm{d}\psi_{0}(x)+\|x\|r(x)
, where
the limit of
r(x)
as
x
goes to zero is zero.
Since
f
is smooth and compactly supported
it is Lipschitz with
some Lipschitz constant
C>0
and its
support is contained in some disk
\mathcal{D}
.
Using that
\psi^{-1}
is Lipschitz near
the origin we can take a disk

\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}

such that

\psi^{-1}(\mathcal{D}/\sqrt{n})\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}/\sqrt{n}

and we may also assume

\mathrm{d}\psi_{0}^{-1}(\mathcal{D}/\sqrt{n})\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}/\sqrt{n}
.
Consider a compactly supported continuous
function

g:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying
1_{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}}\leq g
. We may write

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}_{n}}f(\sqrt{n}\psi(y))-\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}_{n}}f(\sqrt{n}\mathrm{d}\psi_{0}(y))\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}_{n}}C\sqrt{n}|\psi(y)-\mathrm{d}\psi_{0}(y)|1_{y\in\psi^{-1}(\mathcal{D}/\sqrt{n})\cup\mathrm{d}\psi_{0}^{-1}(\mathcal{D}/\sqrt{n})}

(5.2)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}_{n}}C\sqrt{n}\|y\||r(y)|1_{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}/\sqrt{n}}(y).

If
R
is the radius of
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
,
we know that

\sqrt{n}\|y\|1_{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}/\sqrt{n}}(y)\leq R
.
Since
\lim_{x\to 0}r(x)=0
,
we know that
r(y)1_{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}/\sqrt{n}}(y)\leq\varepsilon_{n}

for a sequence
\varepsilon_{n}
that goes to
zero. Then we can bound
(
5.2
) by

CR\varepsilon_{n}\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}_{n}}g(\sqrt{n}y)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{\mathrm{law}}0.

This implies the result.
∎

Colorless image processing technique combining chromaticity transfer and image feature extraction in visual graphic design
Authors: Tingting Wu, Bing Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study presents a verifiable framework for high-fidelity colorless image processing by integrating depth-guided chrominance transfer with multi-level feature extraction. To address critical limitations of traditional methods—color inconsistency, edge bleeding, and insufficient feature matching accuracy—we establish a dual-channel processing pipeline for RGB-D images. This study presents a high-fidelity colorless image processing framework through depth-guided chrominance transfer and multi-level feature extraction. The method introduces three key innovations: a weighted non-local Laplacian algorithm for cross-modal consistency, a multi-level feature hierarchy bridging pixel statistics to semantic understanding, and a brightness-guided edge preservation mechanism. Rigorous testing on benchmark datasets (Middlebury Stereo, MIT-Adobe FiveK, BSDS500, Flickr Million) under standardized preprocessing confirms its efficacy. The algorithm reduces color reconstruction error to 0.020 ± 0.003—a 60% improvement over the Welsh method—with a Turbo-pixel 800 edge fitting accuracy of 0.93 and over-segmentation of only 8%. The three-level feature system boosts matching recall to 91.4%±2.1% from 62.3%, while cutting semantic alignment error from 12.4 to 3.1 ± 0.7 pixels. On MIT-Adobe FiveK, it achieves a PSNR of 38.5 ± 0.4 dB, SSIM of 0.942 ± 0.008, and 9.4 FPS, outperforming diffusion-based colorization by 3.7 dB PSNR with 37% faster speed. All results are statistically significant (p < 0.05) and fully reproducible. This work provides a transparent, efficient, and robust solution for visual graphic design, establishing a new benchmark for colorless image processing.Keywords: Visual graphic design, Chromaticity transfer, Image feature extraction, Colorless image processing, Deep learningSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

In visual graphic design, colorless images such as grayscale or monochrome images (i.e. input objects in image colorization tasks) are widely used in posters, brand identities, and photographic art due to their unique artistic expressiveness and concise visual language1,2. However, traditional achromatic image processing techniques often rely on simple desaturation or threshold segmentation methods, resulting in loss of image detail and insufficient tonal gradation. This makes it difficult to meet the high demands for visual expressiveness in modern design3,4. In recent years, with the advancement of computer vision and image processing technologies, enhancement methods based on chrominance transfer and image feature extraction have provided new approaches for processing colorless images5,6. Chromaticity transfer technology enhances visual depth by mapping the color statistics of the reference image to the target image, which has been established in previous works7,8. Our method extends this principle by integrating deep guidance.

In recent years, the development of technologies such as 3D visualization, video enhancement, generative adversarial networks, and image feature extraction has provided diverse means for visual content processing. Chen et al. upgraded 2D graphic design to 3D virtual visualization technology, constructing a design platform with panoramic information and 3D rendering capabilities, optimizing the image generation process, and improving the visual quality of images9. Wang et al. analyzed video sequence images and designed a motion flow propagation algorithm based on global matching and Transformer encoder, which improved the quality of video restoration while reducing computational complexity10. Elnabawy R H et al. addressed the challenges of manually drawing traditional clip art and its difficulty in automated generation by proposing a generative adversarial network (GAN)-based clip art generation model. This model could automatically convert photographs of objects into clip art. The results showed that the accuracy of the model was 58% and 54% higher than that of Cycle GAN and Google Image Search, respectively11. Image feature extraction optimizes the detail representation of colorless images across dimensions such as structure, texture, and contrast. Yang Z et al. designed a visual communication system to address the issue of poor motion continuity in animated characters within virtual reality. Experimental results demonstrated that the system generated more realistic and coherent animated characters than existing methods12. Wang F et al. proposed an enhancement method based on Sobel operator filtering to address the issues of time-consuming processing, detail loss, and poor visual quality in traditional flat advertising image enhancement techniques. Experiments demonstrated that this method could significantly enhance images within a short timeframe while maintaining high entropy values to preserve rich details13. Liu Z et al. proposed a deep learning-based GAN for visual saliency feature extraction to address the challenges of insufficient training samples and imprecise visual feature extraction in graphic design plagiarism detection. Experimental results indicated that the model outperformed various similarity metrics, effectively enhancing the accuracy and reliability of design plagiarism detection14. The above studies have made significant progress in the fields of 3D scene reconstruction, dynamic video processing, automated graphics generation, and image detail optimization. However, they mainly focus on the processing of color images or specific visual content, and have not systematically solved the coloring problem of colorless images, especially the lack of joint utilization of depth geometric information and multi-level semantic features. Therefore, this study proposes an organic fusion of deep geometric information and multi-level semantic features to construct a coloring method that can assign reasonable and visually rich colors to colorless images while maintaining the original structure. The research scope focuses on the automatic coloring task of grayscale/monochrome images, driven by the gap between the existing methods’ blank space in depth semantic joint utilization and the modern design’s demand for high expressive coloring.

This study addresses three key challenges in colorless image processing (i.e. grayscale/monochrome image coloring): color inconsistency caused by isolated pixel processing in coloring results (manifested as color bleeding, mottled effects, and other artifacts), limited matching accuracy due to single level feature extraction, and insufficient utilization of depth color correlation. Among them, single-level feature extraction refers to relying solely on single level features for matching, such as using only pixel intensity or texture descriptors, lacking the ability to represent images across scales from low-level statistics to high-level semantics. The key innovations of the proposed method are reflected in three aspects: (1) theoretical innovation, proposing a new idea of using depth information as geometric constraints to guide color propagation, elevating depth from additional features to structural priors, and solving the problem of inconsistent cross modal features. (2) Technological innovation, constructing an optimization framework that combines weighted non local Laplacian and locally consistent sparse models to achieve joint modeling of deep constraints, multi-level features, and neighborhood collaboration. (3) Applied innovation, aimed at colorless image coloring tasks, establishes a complete processing chain from depth guidance, superpixel segmentation to brightness post filtering, achieving collaborative enhancement between structural fidelity and color richness. These innovations collectively alleviate traditional limitations by ensuring global-to-local consistency through superpixel constraints, guaranteeing matching accuracy via sparse representations, and enhancing detail rendering through brightness guidance, forming an integrated multi strategy comprehensive processing scheme. Modern visual design has put forward higher requirements for the color expression of colorless images, while traditional coloring methods have obvious limitations in detail preservation, structural consistency, and semantic accuracy, and urgently need new technological paths to break through. This study aims to achieve high fidelity coloring of colorless images (grayscale/monochrome), striking a balance between structural integrity and color richness, and proposing a new scheme that differs from existing RGB-D stitching, global color transfer, and isolated sparse encoding paradigms.

The core scientific contribution of this study lies in: (1) revealing the geometric constraint effect of depth information in color propagation, and theoretically solving the structural distortion problem caused by cross modal feature inconsistency by constructing a depth color joint weighting model. (2) A multi-level feature hierarchical representation framework based on superpixel units has been established, which organically integrates pixel statistics, texture description, and semantic understanding. The system improves the accuracy and semantic coherence of color matching in complex scenes. (3) The guiding mechanism of brightness gradient on chromaticity propagation was elucidated. By coupling the optimization objectives of brightness and chromaticity, color overflow and edge blurring were theoretically suppressed, achieving a synergistic enhancement of structural integrity and color richness.

Methods and materials

Deep guidance-based chrominance transfer technology

Colorless image processing relies on three theoretical principles: cross modal information complementarity, the necessity of multi-scale feature representation, and the inevitability of global local collaborative optimization. Based on these principles, the framework integrates depth information, superpixel structure, multi-level features, sparse encoding alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) Optimization and brightness filtering are organically integrated, and each component cooperates with each other to form a complete inference chain. Specifically, depth information provides geometric constraints and assigns structural priors to superpixel segmentation. Hyperpixels organize pixels into structural units, enabling multi-level feature extraction to be performed on semantic regions and compensating for the insufficient accuracy of single level feature matching. Multi level features can provide rich representations for sparse coding, but isolated coding is prone to local inconsistency. Therefore, graph Laplacian regularization is introduced to force sparse coding similarity of neighboring superpixels, and ADMM efficiently solves this constraint optimization. Finally, brightness post filtering utilizes the original brightness gradient to guide chromaticity propagation, compensating for the edge blurring that may be caused by sparse reconstruction. Each module is interconnected, achieving collaborative optimization from global to local, and from coarse to fine.

The selection of weighted non local Laplacian algorithm and Turbo pixel superpixel algorithm is driven by the following three practical considerations to compensate for the shortcomings of traditional methods. First, traditional local Laplacian filters and SLIC superpixels suffer from edge oversegmentation and insufficient texture adaptation in complex scenes. The weighted nonlocal Laplacian algorithm introduces a novel weighting function that integrates both color similarity and depth similarity, enabling cross-modal fusion that prevents color bleeding while preserving structural coherence. Second, real-time processing demands necessitate computational efficiency. The computational experiments were performed on the university’s shared high-performance computing cluster, which provides authorized access to NVIDIA Tesla V100 GPUs for interdisciplinary research projects, ensuring legitimate access to the required computational resources. Turbo-pixels significantly outperform SLIC in edge retention with comparable processing time, forming dense irregular grids that accurately follow complex boundaries rather than imposing geometrically uniform segments. Third, the integration of these two algorithms establishes a synergistic “global-local” optimization framework: the nonlocal Laplacian provides global color-depth correlation constraints, while Turbo-pixel superpixels serve as structure-aware units for local feature extraction and sparse representation, collectively enabling precise color reconstruction from coarse to fine.

In the field of visual graphic design, colorless image processing technology is a critical component for achieving high-quality visual effects15,16. Traditional methods primarily rely on single-color information or simple feature matching, making it challenging to meet color reconstruction demands in complex scenes17,18. With the widespread adoption of RGB-D image capture devices, the correlation between depth information and color features offers new possibilities for enhancing processing performance19,20. However, existing techniques still exhibit significant shortcomings in maintaining color consistency, controlling edge sharpness, and achieving feature matching accuracy. This often results in processing artifacts such as color bleeding, edge blurring, and texture distortion21,22. To address these challenges, this study proposes an innovative approach that integrates depth-guided chrominance transfer with local consistency feature extraction. The methodology is transparently detailed through specific algorithmic formulations and visual workflows. This method employs a dual optimization strategy: the first stage establishes a fundamental chromaticity distribution through depth guidance. The second stage refines edges using local consistency constraints and luminance guidance, forming a closed-loop optimization process. Traditional chromaticity transfer methods rely solely on color information, overlooking the strong correlation between depth features and color. This study constructs a joint processing framework using RGB-D images generated by active depth sensors (such as structured light cameras or stereo matching devices) that have achieved pixel level registration as input, as shown in Fig. 1.

Image feature extraction and matching based on local consistency

Depth-guided chrominance transfer techniques have achieved preliminary color reconstruction by modeling depth-color correlations to address fundamental cross-modal matching challenges. However, these methods retain two critical limitations: First, isolated pixel processing leads to local color inconsistencies, particularly manifesting as mottled effects in texturally complex regions. Second, the lack of understanding regarding image semantic structure causes color bleeding at critical edges27,28. To this end, the study proposes a local consistency-based image feature extraction and matching methodology comprising four core technologies: Superpixel segmentation technology establishes structure-aware processing units. Multi-level feature extraction integrates low-level intensity statistics, mid-level texture descriptors, and high-level semantic features: The base layer captures pixel-level intensity statistics to ensure color accuracy. The middle layer uses texture descriptors to maintain structural consistency. The top layer incorporates semantic features from a pre-trained saliency detection network to preserve semantic coherence. Feature vectors from all three levels are concatenated into a unified descriptor for each superpixel. The local consistency sparse representation model introduces three key innovations that fundamentally differ from traditional isolated sparse coding. First, a graph Laplacian regularizer incorporates spatial adjacency constraints directly into the objective function, ensuring adjacent superpixels with similar features share similar sparse codes. Second, a learnable adaptive weighting matrix dynamically balances contributions from different feature modalities (intensity, texture, saliency) based on local context to prevent any single feature from dominating. Third, an entropy-based sparsity constraint promotes structured sparsity patterns aligned with image semantics, enhancing both representation efficiency and interpretability beyond simple element-wise sparsity. Governed by the novel objective function in Eq. 4, which simultaneously minimizes reconstruction error while enforcing local consistency and structured sparsity, this model forms a critical part of the coarse-to-fine processing chain. The local consistency-based image feature extraction and matching methodology is illustrated in Fig. 2.

Method system of image feature extraction and matching based on local consistency.

In Fig. 2, the target and reference images are first segmented into 800 superpixel structural units using the Turbo-pixel superpixel algorithm (Turbo-pixel). Extract three-level features for each superpixel, including a 25 dimensional intensity histogram, a 64 dimensional DAISY texture reduced by PCA, and a 1-dimensional saliency response mean, cascaded to form a 90 dimensional unified descriptor. The reference image branch constructs a feature dictionary based on this. In the matching stage, a local consistency sparse model is adopted, which constrains the encoding similarity of neighboring superpixels through the Turalaplus term and introduces entropy regularization to promote structured sparsity. The model is decomposed by ADMM into three sub problems: sparse reconstruction, graph smoothing, and entropy constraint, which are solved alternately. The maximum number of iterations is 50, and the convergence threshold is coefficient variation norm below 0.0001. The obtained sparse coefficients are weighted to generate the initial chromaticity field, and finally the edge sharpened coloring result is output through brightness guided joint filtering (with edge preservation intensity set to 0.5). To address pixel-level processing inefficiencies, traditional methods employ simple linear iterative clustering (SLIC) for segmentation, yet suffer from edge oversegmentation29,30. Therefore, the study selects the Turbo-pixel, which significantly enhances processing efficiency while maintaining edge accuracy by adjusting the number of seed points and iteration counts. By integrating pixel-level intensity statistics, local texture descriptions, and global saliency features, it achieves cross-scale feature representation from micro to macro levels, thereby enhancing matching accuracy and color consistency in complex scenes. In summary, the proposed colorless image processing technique employs a combined methodology of depth-guided chrominance transfer and multi-level feature extraction. The core technologies include: first, the RGB-D dual-channel processing framework is established, achieving cross-modal fusion of depth and color through the weighted nonlocal Laplacian algorithm. Second, Turbo-pixel superpixel segmentation is utilized to establish structure-aware units, integrating three levels of features: intensity histogram, DAISY texture, and saliency detection. Next, a sparse representation model for local consistency is designed, solving the constrained convex optimization problem via the ADMM optimization algorithm. Finally, a brightness-guided edge preservation mechanism is introduced, coupling chrominance and luminance gradients to prevent edge bleeding. This method ensures local consistency through superpixel constraints and guarantees matching accuracy with a sparse representation. It also enhances detail rendering via brightness guidance. Together, these features form a complete processing chain from coarse to fine. The input of the proposed colorless image processing method is a registered grayscale/monochrome image and its corresponding depth map (depth channel in RGB-D), which only contains brightness and geometric structure, and lacks color information. The goal is to transfer reasonable colors from the reference image, reconstruct color channels, and ultimately output visually rich and structurally complete color images through multi-level feature extraction (intensity, texture, saliency) and cross modal matching. The features such as saliency and texture mentioned in the article are obtained through computational analysis of the input data, and are not pre-existing additional input information. The input data of the proposed colorless image processing method only includes brightness and geometric structure, lacking color information. Features such as saliency heatmaps, DAISY textures, and intensity histograms are obtained through computational analysis of the input image, rather than pre-existing additional information. Therefore, the purpose of preprocessing (i.e. feature extraction) is to obtain descriptive features such as saliency and texture from limited inputs, laying the foundation for subsequent color transfer.

Experimental datasets

To ensure the reproducibility and verifiability of the experimental results, this study utilized four publicly available benchmark datasets. The key specifications, purposes, and preprocessing details for each dataset are comprehensively outlined below.

Middlebury Stereo Standard RGB-D Dataset: 100 samples were selected from this dataset to validate the depth-guided chrominance transfer technology. The images were used at their native resolution as provided by the benchmark to maintain the integrity of depth-color correlations. Direct Link: https://vision.middlebury.edu/stereo/data/.

MIT-Adobe FiveK Dataset: This dataset was employed to evaluate the performance of the brightness-guided edge preservation mechanism. All images were standardized to a resolution of 512 × 512 pixels to ensure consistent processing conditions. Direct Link: https://data.csail.mit.edu/graphics/fivek/.

Berkeley Segmentation Dataset 500 (BSDS500): It was utilized for comparing the performance of SLIC and Turbo-pixel superpixel segmentation algorithms, providing a ground truth for evaluating edge fitting accuracy and segmentation quality. Direct Link: https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Research/Projects/CS/vision/bsds/.

Flickr Million Dataset: A curated subset of this large-scale collection was used for conducting extensive feature ablation experiments on the proposed multi-level feature system, testing its robustness across diverse visual content. Direct Link: https://multimediacommons.org/.

All images across the datasets were preprocessed by converting the color space to CIE 1976 L*a*b*, which is optimal for color processing tasks. Access to these datasets (Middlebury, MIT-Adobe FiveK, BSDS500, Flickr Million) was obtained through official academic licensing agreements managed by the university’s research data management office, ensuring full compliance with terms of use for non-commercial academic research. Standard training/validation/test splits for each public dataset were followed to align with established benchmarking practices.

Although the above datasets are not specifically designed for colorless image processing or RGB-D coloring, their core characteristics make them widely used as benchmarks in their respective research fields. Specifically, the Middlebury dataset is used to evaluate depth estimation accuracy in the fields of stereo matching and 3D reconstruction due to its high-quality depth and color registration. The MIT Adobe FiveK dataset is used for training and evaluating automatic color grading models in the fields of image enhancement and tone mapping due to the pairing of its original images with images adjusted by multiple experts. The BSDS500 dataset is used in the field of image segmentation and boundary detection to evaluate the algorithm’s ability to segment natural image structures due to its fine manual boundary annotation of multiple sets. The Flickr Million dataset is used in the field of large-scale image retrieval and multimedia analysis to test the generalization performance of algorithms in diverse scenarios due to its large scale and content diversity. The use of the above dataset in this study is consistent with its standard usage, providing a reliable proxy benchmark for the evaluation of each module31. Experimental parameter selection strategy

The experimental parameter selection strategy is based on theoretical analysis, combined with grid search and cross validation for systematic multi criteria optimization. The parameter values follow three core principles: maximizing performance metrics, ensuring robustness under perturbations, and maintaining computational efficiency. The depth color joint weighting parameter = 0.2 in weighted non local Laplacian is determined by entropy analysis of the depth color joint distribution, and is determined within the range of 0.1-1.0 by minimizing color reconstruction error and maximizing structural similarity, in accordance with the maximization of mutual information criterion in information theory. The configuration of Turbo pixel superpixel quantity is 800, determined based on the marginal benefit decreasing curve of edge fitting accuracy with increasing segmentation density, taking into account both structural fidelity and computational cost. In the local consistency parameter, the spatial bandwidth = 0.3 and the feature bandwidth = 0.7 are derived from the spatial feature joint distribution kernel density estimation within the superpixel neighborhood, satisfying the theoretical constraints of intra class compactness and inter class separation. The regularization parameters of \varepsilon = 0.5 and α = 0.1 are used to achieve a balance between sparse reconstruction error and Turaplas smoothness through L-curve analysis, which conforms to the inflection point selection criterion of Pareto optimal frontier in convex optimization theory. The edge preservation strength of brightness guided filtering, P= 0.5, is derived from the coupling strength model of brightness gradient and chromaticity propagation. Based on the theory of total variation minimization, the optimal compromise is achieved between preserving edge sharpness and suppressing noise. All parameters were validated on a combined dataset using 5-fold cross validation to ensure their generalization ability and theoretical consistency under diverse image content.

Discussion and conclusion

In-depth analysis of superior performance mechanisms

The proposed method achieved performance improvement through the collaboration of three core modules. First, the weighted nonlocal Laplacian algorithm reduces color reconstruction error to 0.020 ± 0.003-a 60% improvement over traditional methods-by integrating depth-color correlations and spatial adjacency constraints. This prevents color bleeding while maintaining structural coherence. Second, Turbo-pixel 800 superpixel configuration achieves an edge fitting accuracy of 0.93 and reduces boundary confusion by 44.9% compared to SLIC 500 by adaptively following complex edges rather than imposing geometric regularity. Third, the three-level feature fusion system achieves a matching recall rate of 91.4% ± 2.1% through hierarchical learning: pixel-level intensity statistics (25-bin histogram), DAISY descriptors (64-dimensional PCA-reduced), and saliency detection for semantic guidance.

The essential difference between the method proposed by the research institute and existing technologies lies not in incremental performance improvement, but in three paradigm shifts. Firstly, traditional RGB-D fusion considers depth as an additional feature channel, while the proposed method uses weighted non local Laplacian to use depth as a geometric constraint for color propagation, achieving structure guided coloring rather than feature concatenation. Secondly, traditional color transfer relies on global statistics or shallow descriptors. The proposed method constructs a three-level feature system (intensity, DAISY, saliency) under superpixel units, elevating the matching from pixel level to semantic aware structured representations. The third traditional sparse optimization method for isolated encoding of local blocks proposes a sparse pattern with graph Laplacian forcing neighborhood coefficient coordination and entropy regularization inducing semantic alignment, moving from “independent sparsity” to “uniform sparsity”.

Limitations and technical boundaries

Despite these advancements, three limitations persist. Computational time increases non-linearly with superpixel count (reaching 7.9s for 800 superpixels), and this delay is difficult to meet real-time requirements in graphic design scenarios that require real-time feedback, such as interactive color editing and real-time preview, limiting the direct application of this method in real-time graphic design software. DAISY descriptor dimensionality reduction may lose fine-grained features in complex textures. Performance is sensitive to the quality of the reference image - when the resolution of the reference image is below 256 × 256 pixels, the SSIM decreases by more than 0.09 and drops below 0.85, indicating a significant decline in structural fidelity and difficulty in accurately reconstructing fine textures. These limitations are particularly pronounced in dynamic scenes and mobile environments. In addition, the proposed method relies on multiple manually designed components (depth color weighting function, Turbo superpixel parameters, three-level feature combination) and high-performance GPU resources, which may limit its generalization. The difference in the depth noise characteristics of different sensing devices will also affect the color stability. The 7.9 s single image processing and GPU dependency make it difficult to deploy in resource constrained scenarios such as mobile terminals and embedded devices. Therefore, the follow-up work should introduce domain adaptive strategy to reduce the impact of different sensor depth noise distribution differences on color stability and improve the generalization ability of the model by learning the cross dataset invariant representation between depth features and color reconstruction. At the same time, for scenarios where computing resources are limited, model pruning is used to remove redundant parameters in sparse coding and feature extraction networks. Combined with knowledge distillation, the coloring ability of the current complex model is transferred to a lightweight network, compressing the processing time of single images while maintaining reconstruction quality, making the method applicable to resource limited environments.

Future applications and research directions

Potential applications include grayscale optimization for brand logos, monochrome photography enhancement, and intelligent coloring systems using GANs for style transfer. In virtual reality, the method can improve grayscale 3D model rendering. Future research will focus on: (1) GPU acceleration of ADMM algorithm for faster processing, (2) lightweight network architectures for mobile deployment, and (3) adaptive cross-modal fusion mechanisms to balance depth and color features based on scene complexity.

Experimental verifiability and reproducibility summary

The claimed performance advantages are grounded in a verifiable foundation: statistical significance (p < 0.01), empirical ablation studies, detailed parameter specifications, and consistent results across five diverse datasets. The commitment to releasing code, data, and configurations ensures the findings are practically reproducible.

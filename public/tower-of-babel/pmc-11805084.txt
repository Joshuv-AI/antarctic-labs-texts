Digital Health Literacy and Surgical Information - Seeking Behaviour: A Cross Sectional Survey
Authors: Mayank Asmit, Bhartendu Kumar, Tauseef Kibria, Soofia Firdaus, Md Khalid Tanweer, Sadaf Kibria
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:In an era marked by technological advancements and increased digital information accessibility, digital health literacy has emerged as a pivotal factor in healthcare decision-making.Methods:A cross-sectional survey involving 400 participants was conducted. Digital health literacy, information sources, frequency of surgical information seeking, and factors influencing information-seeking behavior were assessed. Data analysis included descriptive statistics and inferential tests.Results:The study found diverse digital health literacy levels, with 45% in the moderate category. Social media (60%) and healthcare websites (70%) were primary information sources. A significant portion (30%) frequently sought surgical information online. Trust in online information (mean = 3.85), perceived usefulness (mean = 4.12), and access barriers (mean = 2.95) played crucial roles. Digital health literacy influenced information seeking, with moderate literacy individuals (60%) being the most engaged.Conclusion:Understanding digital health literacy and information-seeking behavior is vital for patient empowerment and healthcare decision-making. Tailored interventions should target trust-building, perceived usefulness, and access barriers.

INTRODUCTION

People’s capacity to find and understand health-related information online has become essential for making informed healthcare decisions in an era of rapid technological innovation and increased access to digital information. The ability to acquire, comprehend, assess, and utilize digital health information is known as digital health literacy, and it is essential for enabling people to make decisions about their health and medical treatments that are well-informed.[12] It is crucial to comprehend people’s digital health literacy while discussing surgical information-seeking behavior, as this will help to enhance patient participation, collaborative decision-making, and overall health outcomes.

In recent times, Muzaffarpur, Bihar, an Indian location with distinct healthcare prospects and problems, has seen notable progress in digital technologies and internet usage. People now look for health-related information, including details on surgeries and treatments, in a completely different manner because of the widespread use of cellphones and internet access. Despite the promising benefits of digital health literacy in this context, it’s essential to assess the current landscape to identify knowledge gaps and areas where interventions can be designed to enhance individuals’ digital health literacy. Prior research has emphasized the significance of health literacy within the surgical setting.[34]

MATERIALS AND METHODS

The study participants included residents of Muzaffarpur, Bihar, who were aged 18 years and older. A stratified random sampling method was employed to ensure representation from different age groups and educational backgrounds. The sample size of 400 was calculated to achieve a 95% confidence level with a 5% margin of error.

Data collection instrument

A structured questionnaire was developed to collect data on participants’ digital health literacy, sources of digital health information, frequency of seeking surgical information online, and factors influencing their online information-seeking behavior. The questionnaire was designed based on validated scales and adapted to the local context.

Data collection procedure

Data collection was conducted over a specified period from January 2023 to December 2023. The survey was administered in person, through online platforms, and via telephonic interviews to maximize participation.

RESULTS

The participants were categorized into four age groups. The majority (45%) fall in the age range of 31-45, followed by 30% in the 18-30 age group, 20% in the 46-60 age group, and 5% aged 61 and above. Participants’ education levels are categorized, with 40% having a Bachelor’s degree, 25% with a Master’s degree, 25% having completed high school, and 10% holding a Doctoral degree. The digital health literacy levels of participants were assessed. A significant portion (45%) falls into the “Moderate” category, 30% have “Low” digital health literacy, and 25% possess “High” digital health literacy.

60% of participants use social media platforms as a source of health-related information, 70% rely on dedicated healthcare websites for health information, 40% participate in online health-related forums, 30% use healthcare apps to access health information, 20% follow health-related blogs for information, and 10% indicated other sources. 10% of participants never seek surgical information online. 30% seek information once a month or less, 30% seek information 2-3 times a month, and 30% seek information once a week or more.

Table 1 examines factors that influence participants’ information-seeking behavior, with scores ranging from 1 (low influence) to 5 (high influence). Participants, on average, have a moderate level of trust in online health information, with a mean score of 3.85 and a standard deviation of 0.75. The perceived usefulness of online health information is relatively high, with an average score of 4.12, and a standard deviation of 0.68. Participants face some barriers to access online health information, as indicated by a mean score of 2.95, and a standard deviation of 0.84.

DISCUSSION

In the context of a rapidly evolving digital landscape, the study’s results provide insight into the surgical information-seeking behavior and digital health literacy of individuals in Muzaffarpur, Bihar. In a time when digital information is easily accessible, it is critical to comprehend how people interact with online health resources in order to improve healthcare decision-making and advancing patient-centered care.[12]

The participants in our research had a wide range of digital health literacy levels, with a sizable percentage falling into the intermediate literacy range. This suggests a respectable ability to get, comprehend, assess, and use digital health information. These results are in line with other studies that stressed the importance of digital health literacy as a prerequisite for making well-informed decisions.[15]

This research shows how popular social media and medical websites are in Muzaffarpur as the primary sourcesto get information about digital health. This underscores the significance of these platforms as vital sources of health knowledge, and is consistent with worldwide trends of people looking for health-related information online.[6] But, it also emphasizes how crucial it is to guarantee the veracity and authenticity of health information disseminated on these platforms, especially given the Indian context.[7]

The results of this study have several ramifications for Muzaffarpur, Bihar’s healthcare system. In order to meet the population’s varied degrees of digital health literacy, specialized health communication techniques are first required. Targeted efforts need to concentrate on removing access obstacles, raising perceived utility, and strengthening confidence in online health information.

CONCLUSION

In summary, during a period of rapidly increasing digital technology, this research offers important insights on the surgical information-seeking behavior and digital health literacy of people in Muzaffarpur, Bihar. The results highlight the significance of digital health literacy in fostering informed healthcare choices and underscore the role of various factors that influence information-seeking behavior.

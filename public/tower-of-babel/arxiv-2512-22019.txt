Mass Spectra of Λ Q ​ Σ ¯ Q \Lambda_{Q}\bar{\Sigma}_{Q} Bound States
Xuan-Heng Zhang, Cong-Feng Qiao

Abstract
Recently, the BESIII Collaboration indicate that no Λ c ​ Σ ¯ c \Lambda_{c}\bar{\Sigma}_{c} bound-state with a mass near threshold in the range 4715 4715 – 4735 ​ MeV 4735~\mathrm{MeV} was observed. In order to determine the plausible mass region of the bound states in this structure, we calculate the mass spectrum of the Λ c ​ Σ ¯ c \Lambda_{c}\bar{\Sigma}_{c} molecular configuration with the method of QCD sum rules. Two linearly independent interpolating currents are constructed, and contributions from nonperturbative condensates up to dimension 12 are included in the numerical results. Consequently, we obtain the masses of the candidate bound states with quantum numbers J P = 0 − , 0 + , 1 − , 1 + J^{P}=0^{-},\,0^{+},\,1^{-},\,1^{+} . Our results show that the central values of the Λ c ​ Σ ¯ c \Lambda_{c}\bar{\Sigma}_{c} bound-state masses lie around the 5.8 ​ GeV 5.8~\mathrm{GeV} region, consistent with the findings reported by the BESIII Collaboration. Furthermore, we compute the mass spectrum of the Λ b ​ Σ ¯ b \Lambda_{b}\bar{\Sigma}_{b} bound states with quantum numbers J P = 0 − , 0 + , 1 − , 1 + J^{P}=0^{-},\,0^{+},\,1^{-},\,1^{+} , which could be served as hidden-bottom candidates in the experimental detecting.

II.2.2
Phenomenological Side

In the phenomenological framework, the contributions from the ground state and the excited states (including the continuum spectrum) can be separated as

\rho_{X,J^{P}}^{\text{Phen}}(s)=\lambda_{X,J^{P}}^{2}\,\delta\!\left(s-M_{X,J^{P}}^{2}\right)+\theta\!\left(s-s_{0}\right)\rho_{X,J^{P}}(s),

(15)

where
m_{X,J^{P}}
denotes the mass of the ground state, and
s_{0}
is the threshold parameter, which characterizes the onset of the excited states and the continuum spectrum. The decay constants
\lambda_{X,J^{P}}
of the ground state are defined as

\displaystyle\lambda_{X,0^{\pm}}

\displaystyle=\bra{\Omega}j(x)\ket{X},

(16)

\displaystyle\lambda_{X,1^{\pm}}\varepsilon_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\bra{\Omega}j_{\mu}(x)\ket{X},

which reflect the coupling of the interpolating currents and the ground state hadronic states.

By applying the dispersion relation, the phenomenological representation of the correlation function can be written as

\Pi_{X,J^{P}}^{\text{Phen}}(q^{2})=\frac{\lambda_{X,J^{P}}^{2}}{M_{X,J^{P}}^{2}-q^{2}}+\int_{s_{0}}^{\infty}\differential s\,\frac{\rho_{X,J^{P}}(s)}{s-q^{2}},

(17)

where the first term corresponds to the pole contribution of the ground state, while the second term accounts for the contributions from the excited states and the continuum spectrum.

II.3
Hadronic Mass and Decay Constant

According to the hypothesis of quark-hadron duality, the correlation functions obtained from the OPE representation and the phenomenological representation should be consistent. In particular, the spectral densities from the two sides are expected to be approximately equal above the continuum threshold parameter
s_{0}
. Based on this assumption, we can combine Eqs. (
14
) and (
17

\lambda_{X,J^{P}}^{2}\,\mathrm{e}^{-M_{X,J^{P}}^{2}/M_{B}^{2}}=\int_{s_{\min}}^{s_{0}}\differential s\,\rho_{X,J^{P}}^{\text{OPE}}(s)\,\mathrm{e}^{-s/M_{B}^{2}}.

(18)

From the sum rule given in Eq. (
18
), the mass of the ground-state hadron
X
can be expressed as

M_{X,J^{P}}(s_{0},M_{B}^{2})=\sqrt{-\frac{L_{X,J^{P},1}(s_{0},M_{B}^{2})}{L_{X,J^{P},0}(s_{0},M_{B}^{2})}},

(19)

where

\displaystyle L_{X,J^{P},0}(s_{0},M_{B}^{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{s_{\min}}^{s_{0}}\differential s\,\rho^{\text{OPE}}(s)\,\mathrm{e}^{-s/M_{B}^{2}},

(20)

\displaystyle L_{X,J^{P},1}(s_{0},M_{B}^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial(M_{B}^{-2})}L_{X,J^{P},0}(s_{0},M_{B}^{2}).

Furthermore, the decay constant can be extracted from Eq. (
18

\lambda_{X,J^{P}}(s_{0},M_{B}^{2})=\sqrt{\mathrm{e}^{M_{X,J^{P}}^{2}(s_{0},M_{B}^{2})/M_{B}^{2}}\,L_{X,J^{P},0}(s_{0},M_{B}^{2})}.

In establishing the theoretical framework of QCDSR, two additional parameters,

M_{B}
is minimized. The region in which the mass exhibits minimal sensitivity to these parameters is referred to as the Borel window. A reliable Borel window must also satisfy two additional conditions. First, since the quantity being extracted is the mass of the ground-state hadron, the pole contribution associated with the ground state should dominate the spectral density, while the contributions from excited states and the continuum should remain suppressed. We define the ratio of the pole contribution as

R^{\text{PC}}_{X,J^{P}}=\frac{L_{X,J^{P},0}(s_{0},M_{B}^{2})}{L_{X,J^{P},0}(\infty,M_{B}^{2})}.

(23)

For hexaquark states, the spectral density carries a high dimension, and the power of
s
is correspondingly large, which could suppress the pole contribution significantly. As a result, a Borel window with a pole contribution satisfying
R^{\text{PC}}_{X,J^{P}}>15\%
can be regarded as reliable

;
45
;
57

n

R^{\langle\mathcal{O}_{n}\rangle}_{X,J^{P}}=\left|\frac{L^{\langle\mathcal{O}_{n}\rangle}_{X,J^{P},0}(s_{0},M_{B}^{2})}{L_{X,J^{P},0}(s_{0},M_{B}^{2})}\right|,

(24)

where

L^{\langle\mathcal{O}_{n}\rangle}_{X,J^{P},0}(s_{0},M_{B}^{2})=\int_{s_{\min}}^{s_{0}}\differential s\ \rho^{\langle\mathcal{O}_{n}\rangle}(s)\mathrm{e}^{-s/M_{B}^{2}}.

(25)

In this work, the OPE is carried out up to the condensates of dimension 12. For the case of

IV
Decay Modes Analyses

In experimental studies, determining the internal structure of a hadronic state requires not only to observe its mass and quantum numbers, but also reconstructing the state from its decay products. Moreover, in realistic experimental environments, the observed
XYZ
resonances are often not pure states. Instead, they are mixtures of several hadronic configurations with similar masses and identical quantum numbers. To distinguish among them, it is necessary to examine their possible decay channels. Therefore, it is important to analyze the potential decay modes of the hexaquark states of the type
\Lambda_{Q}\bar{\Sigma}_{Q}
, so as to provide useful guidance for their experimental identification.

For the molecular-type
\Lambda_{Q}\bar{\Sigma}_{Q}
hexaquark states, the dominant decay mode proceeds through strong interactions directly into the corresponding baryons
\Lambda_{Q}
and
\bar{\Sigma}_{Q}
. According to the latest PDG data
33
, the baryon–antibaryon threshold of
\Lambda_{c}\bar{\Sigma}_{c}
is approximately
4.71
GeV, while that of
\Lambda_{b}\bar{\Sigma}_{b}
is around
11.43
GeV. Therefore, the masses of the
\Lambda_{Q}\bar{\Sigma}_{Q}
states lie above their respective thresholds, allowing them to decay directly into the corresponding baryon–antibaryon pairs. Another dominant decay mode is the three-body strong decay, in which the final states consist of three mesons. The corresponding decay mechanism is illustrated in Fig.

V
Discussion and Conclusions

In summary, in this work we have employed the QCDSR method to calculate the masses and decay constants of the ground hidden-charm and hidden-bottom
\Lambda_{Q}\bar{\Sigma}_{Q}
bound states. Two linearly independent hadronic interpolating currents are constructed for this purpose, and nonperturbative contribution up to dimension 12 is considered. Possible decay modes of these states have also been analyzed. The results indicate that the baryonium currents constructed from the Type-I baryonic currents yield reliable Borel windows for the
\Lambda_{Q}\bar{\Sigma}_{Q}
states with
J^{P}=0^{-}
and
1^{-}
. In contrast, the currents constructed from the Type-II baryonic currents provide reliable Borel windows for all four quantum-number assignments
J^{P}=0^{-},0^{+},1^{-},1^{+}
of the
\Lambda_{Q}\bar{\Sigma}_{Q}
ground states. All extracted masses lie above the corresponding baryon–antibaryon thresholds.

In our calculation, the central value of the
\Lambda_{c}\bar{\Sigma}_{c}
bound state lies in the range of
5.8
GeV, which is more than
1
GeV above its baryon–antibaryon threshold. Therefore, the absence of any detection of this state by BESIII near the threshold
6
is consistent with our theoretical interpretation. To observe this bound state, it is suggested that experiments can increase the center-of-mass energy and search for it in the region around
5.8
GeV. The theoretical results
22
referenced in the experiment are not consistent with our calculations, and the primary reason for this discrepancy may lie in the different binding mechanisms. The binding mechanism used in the Bethe-Salpter equation involves the single-meson exchange potential between the baryon and antibaryon, which is a hadronic-level binding mechanism and depends on the chosen potential model, leading to a near threshold mass. In contrast, the possible binding through QCDSR may yield by gluon condensates and mixed condensates, operating at the quark level, and then connects to the hadronic level via quark-hadron duality. Moreover, QCDSR directly reflects the molecular structure through hadronic interpolating currents, which determine the different configurations of condensate terms. Therefore, the results of QCDSR calculations depend on the choice of interpolating currents. The reliable mechanism behind the formation of molecular states from baryon-antibaryon pairs requires further study to specifically determine the mass of the molecular state, while also providing deeper insight into the nonperturbative hadronization process.2

It should also be noted that Ref.
14
systematically studied hidden-charm hexaquark states. In their results, only the
J^{P}=0^{+},1^{+}
hidden-charm baryonium states were found, with some of their masses falling in the range of
5.5
–
6.0
GeV, which is consistent with our findings. The main differences between that work and the present study are twofold: First, the
\Sigma
baryon interpolating currents used in the two works are different, leading to the construction of distinct baryonium currents that could couple to different states, which is why the
J^{P}=0^{-},1^{-}
hidden-charm baryonium states
\Lambda_{c}\bar{\Sigma}_{c}
are found in this work and obtained different mass values. Second, this work takes into account a larger number of nonperturbative condensate contributions, such as the dimension-10 condensate
\langle q\bar{q}\rangle^{2}\langle G^{2}\rangle
and the dimension-11 condensate
\langle q\bar{q}\rangle^{2}\langle qG\bar{q}\rangle
, which were not considered in their work. From our calculations, it is evident that the contribution of
\langle q\bar{q}\rangle^{2}\langle qG\bar{q}\rangle
is greater than
10\%
in many states, and can even approach
20\%
. This has a significant impact, directly affecting the existence of
J^{P}=0^{-},1^{-}
states.

For the hidden-bottom baryonium states
\Lambda_{b}\bar{\Sigma}_{b}
, no possible signals have been observed experimentally so far. The states we have calculated would be detected in experiments such as STCF, LHCb, ATLAS, BelleII, and others. They can serve as candidates for hidden-bottom resonances in the
12
GeV region.

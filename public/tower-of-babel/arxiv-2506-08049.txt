Physics-Informed Teleconnection-Aware Transformer for Global Subseasonal-to-Seasonal Forecasting
Tengfei Lyu, Weijia Zhang, Hao Liu

Abstract
Subseasonal-to-seasonal (S2S) forecasting, which predicts climate conditions from several weeks to months in advance, presents significant challenges due to the chaotic dynamics of atmospheric systems and complex interactions across multiple scales. Current approaches often fail to explicitly model underlying physical processes and teleconnections that are crucial at S2S timescales. We introduce TelePiT , a novel deep learning architecture that enhances global S2S forecasting through integrated multi-scale physics and teleconnection awareness. Our approach consists of three key components: (1) Spherical Harmonic Embedding, which accurately encodes global atmospheric variables onto spherical geometry; (2) Multi-Scale Physics-Informed Neural ODE, which explicitly captures atmospheric physical processes across multiple learnable frequency bands; (3) Teleconnection-Aware Transformer, which models critical global climate interactions through tactfully injecting teleconnection patterns into the self-attention. Extensive experiments demonstrate that TelePiT significantly outperforms state-of-the-art data-driven baselines and operational numerical weather prediction systems, with remarkable improvements for atmospheric variables including a 57.7% reduction in RMSE for 2-meter temperature compared to previous best models.

Our main contributions are as follows:
Firstly, we pioneer the explicit integration of physical constraints across multi-scale climate dynamics via Multi-Scale Physics-Informed ODE, simultaneously capturing atmospheric processes operating at different frequencies while preserving fundamental physical laws.
Secondly, we introduce the first explicit modeling of teleconnections through a novel Teleconnection-Aware Transformer, which captures the complex, non-local correlations between distant geographic regions that critically influence forecasting in S2S scales.
Finally, extensive experiments demonstrate that

significantly outperforms both state-of-the-art data-driven baselines and operational numerical weather prediction systems, consistently delivering enhanced forecasting accuracy and reliability at global scales.

3.1
Spherical Harmonic Embedding

To accurately represent global atmospheric variables on the sphere, we introduce a learnable spherical harmonic-inspired positional embedding.
For a grid point at latitude
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\theta_{i}\in[-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}]
and longitude
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\phi_{j}\in[-\pi,\pi]
, we construct positional encodings using frequency-scaled sinusoidal functions:

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{E}_{lat}(i,2k)=\sin\big((k+1)\theta_{i}\big),\quad\mathbf{E}_{lat}(i,2k+1)=\cos\big((k+1)\theta_{i}\big),

(2)

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{E}_{lon}(j,2q)=\sin\big((q+1)\phi_{j}\big),\quad\mathbf{E}_{lon}(j,2q+1)=\cos\big((q+1)\phi_{j}\big),

(3)

where
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}k\in\{0,1,...,\frac{d_{lat}}{2}-1\}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}q\in\{0,1,...,\frac{d_{lon}}{2}-1\}
. These encodings are initialized as learnable parameters
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{E}_{lat}\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times d_{lat}}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{E}_{lon}\in\mathbb{R}^{W\times d_{lon}}
where
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}d_{lat}+d_{lon}=D_{emb}
.
The sinusoidal functions provide a natural way to represent the periodic structure of spherical coordinates, particularly for longitude which wraps around the globe
atkinson2012spherical
;
cohen2018spherical
.

Figure 1

The framework of TelePiT for global subseasonal-to-seasonal forecasting.

We process the latitude dimension as a sequence of tokens while aggregating information along the longitude dimension. For each latitude
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}i
, we compute a zonal average:

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{u}_{i}=\frac{1}{W}\sum_{j=1}^{W}X_{i,j}\in\mathbb{R}^{C}
.
This zonal averaging operation is physically justified as atmospheric patterns often exhibit stronger correlations along latitude bands, particularly for large-scale circulation features
baldwin2003stratospheric
;
kidston2015stratospheric
. The features are then projected to a higher-dimensional latent space through a learnable transformation:

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{h}_{i}=\mathbf{W}\mathbf{u}_{i}+\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{emb}}
,
where
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{emb}\times C}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{emb}}
are learnable parameters.

To incorporate spherical harmonic-inspired positional embedding, we combine the latitude embedding with the global average of longitude embeddings:

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{p}_{i}^{lat}=\mathbf{E}_{lat}(i,:)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{lat}},\kern 5.0pt\mathbf{p}^{lon}=\frac{1}{W}\sum_{j=1}^{W}\mathbf{E}_{lon}(j,:)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{lon}},\kern 5.0pt\mathbf{p}_{i}=[\mathbf{p}_{i}^{lat},\mathbf{p}^{lon}]\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{emb}}.

(4)

The final embedding is computed with the complete embedded sequence obtained by stacking:

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{z}_{i}=\mathbf{W}_{proj}(\mathbf{h}_{i}+\mathbf{p}_{i})+\mathbf{b}_{proj}\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{emb}},\kern 5.0pt\mathbf{Z}=[\mathbf{z}_{1},\mathbf{z}_{2},\ldots,\mathbf{z}_{H}]\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times D_{emb}}.

(5)

The theoretical foundation of this connects to spherical function representation theory. Mathematically, our zonal averaging and embedding method implements learnable approximation to spherical harmonic decomposition. Traditionally, a function
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}f(\theta,\phi)
on sphere can be decomposed as:

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}f(\theta,\phi)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}a_{\ell m}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi)
,
where
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi)
are the spherical harmonic functions and
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}a_{\ell m}
are expansion coefficients
atkinson2012spherical
. In particular, when
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}m=0
, spherical harmonics reduce to zonal harmonics that depend solely on latitude
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\theta
, which precisely corresponds to our zonal averaging operation. This approach extracts a sequence of length
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}H
as input tokens for subsequent modules, preserving the inductive bias of spherical geometry while enabling the model to adaptively learn optimal representations for atmospheric variables.

3.2
Multi-Scale Physics-Informed Neural ODE

Learnable Multi-Scale Decomposition

Atmospheric processes span multiple scales that interact in complex ways, creating a fundamental challenge for S2S forecasting.
For example, when predicting Northern Hemisphere winter circulation, stratospheric polar vortex dynamics (planetary-scale phenomena) exhibit significant downward influence on tropospheric weather patterns (regional-scale outcomes) through scale-bridging mechanisms operating over 2-3 week
baldwin2001stratospheric
;
kidston2015stratospheric
.
These cross-scale interactions, also documented in observational studies
butler2019predictability
;
vitart2014evolution
, embody critical physical processes that must be explicitly resolved to overcome current predictability limitations.

To explicitly capture this inherent multi-scale structure, we implement an
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}L
-level learnable wavelet decomposition
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathcal{W}
operating along the latitude dimension of
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{Z}
. At each level, the current signal is split into a coarse approximation and a detail component, mirroring the discrete wavelet transform but with learnable filters. Formally, let
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{A}_{0}=\mathbf{Z}\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times D_{emb}}
be the input at the finest scale. For each level
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\ell=1,2,\cdots,L
, we apply a neural transform:

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}[\mathbf{A}_{\ell},\mathbf{D}_{\ell}]=\textit{split}\Big(\text{MLP}_{\ell}(\mathbf{A}_{\ell-1}),\text{dim}=2\Big)
, where
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\text{MLP}_{\ell}:\mathbb{R}^{D_{emb}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2D_{emb}}
is a two-layer perceptron with GELU activation, and the
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\textit{split}(\cdot,\text{dim}=2)
operation divides the output features into two equal halves. This yields an
approximation

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{A}_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times D_{emb}}
and a
detail

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{D}_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times D_{emb}}
. The
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{A}_{\ell}
carries the smoother (low-frequency) information, while
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{D}_{\ell}
captures the variations (high-frequency details) removed at level
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\ell
.
After
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}L
levels, we obtain a final coarse approximation
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{A}_{L}
and detail components
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\{\mathbf{D}_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{L}
. For convenience, we denote the set of multi-scale features as:

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathcal{X}=\{\mathbf{X}^{(0)},\mathbf{X}^{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{X}^{(L)}\}
,
where
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{X}^{(0)}:=\mathbf{A}_{L}
represents the lowest-frequency band, and
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{X}^{(\ell)}:=\mathbf{D}_{L+1-\ell}
for
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\ell=1,\cdots,L
(ordering from low to high frequency). Each
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{X}^{(\ell)}\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times D_{emb}}
maintains the same dimensions as the original embedding.

This multi-scale decomposition aligns with the established understanding in atmospheric science that different physical processes dominate at different scales
hoskins1985use
, enabling our learnable approach to adaptively determine the optimal decomposition basis for S2S forecasting.

Physics-Informed Neural ODE

To incorporate physical prior knowledge of atmospheric dynamics
kashinath2021physics
, we evolve each frequency band using a physics-informed neural ODE. Drawing from fluid dynamics principles, we formulate an ODE in the latent space
vermaclimode
that combines fundamental atmospheric processes with data-driven corrections to handle complex, unresolved dynamics.

For each multi-scale component
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{X}^{(\ell)}=[\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2},\cdots,\mathbf{x}_{H}]\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times D_{emb}}
, we define an ODE that evolves the latent state through time:

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\frac{d\mathbf{x}_{i}}{dt}=\gamma\cdot\tanh\Bigg({\color[rgb]{0.0078,0.4648,0.7422}\underbrace{\boldsymbol{\nu}\odot(\mathbf{x}_{i+1}-2\mathbf{x}_{i}+\mathbf{x}_{i-1})}_{\smash{\text{diffusion}}}}+{\color[rgb]{0.8477,0.2617,0.082}\underbrace{\boldsymbol{\mu}\odot\frac{\mathbf{x}_{i+1}-\mathbf{x}_{i-1}}{2}}_{\smash{\text{advection}}}}+{\color[rgb]{0.1875,0.2461,0.625}\underbrace{\boldsymbol{f}}_{\smash{\text{forcing}}}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0.5391,0.4805}\underbrace{\alpha\cdot\text{MLP}(\mathbf{x}_{i})}_{\smash{\text{neural correction}}}}\Bigg)

(6)

This equation incorporates four physically meaningful components:

•

Diffusion term
:
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}{\color[rgb]{0.0078,0.4648,0.7422}\boldsymbol{\nu}\odot(\mathbf{x}_{i+1}-2\mathbf{x}_{i}+\mathbf{x}_{i-1})}
approximates the second-order diffusion operator along latitude, representing the meridional diffusion of atmospheric properties. The learnable parameter
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{emb}}
modulates diffusion strength for each feature dimension.

•

Advection term
:
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}{\color[rgb]{0.8477,0.2617,0.082}\boldsymbol{\mu}\odot\frac{\mathbf{x}_{i+1}-\mathbf{x}_{i-1}}{2}}
approximates the first-order spatial derivative, representing meridional transport by mean flow. The parameter
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{emb}}
controls advection intensity.

•

Forcing term
:
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}{\color[rgb]{0.1875,0.2461,0.625}\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{emb}}}
represents external forcing, analogous to radiative and diabatic processes in the climate system.

•

Neural correction
:
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}{\color[rgb]{0,0.5391,0.4805}\alpha\cdot\text{MLP}(\mathbf{x}_{i})}
learns complex, nonlinear dynamics not captured by simplified physical terms. Parameter
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\alpha
balances the physical terms with data-driven corrections.

For numerical stability and physical realism, we apply boundary conditions
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{x}_{0}=\mathbf{x}_{H+1}=0
(zero-padding at the poles) and constrain the rate of change using a hyperbolic tangent function.
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\gamma
is a small scaling factor that moderates the rate of change to prevent numerical instabilities.
We employ the Euler method with a fixed step size
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\Delta t
:

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{x}_{i}(t+\Delta t)=\mathbf{x}_{i}(t)+\Delta t\cdot\frac{d\mathbf{x}_{i}}{dt}\Big|_{t}
.
We evolve each frequency band
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{X}^{(\ell)}
independently from time
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}t=0
to
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}t=T
(where
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}T
represents one forecast step in latent space), yielding evolved representations
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{(\ell)}
. This scale-dependent evolution reflects physical reality that atmospheric processes operate differently across scales, diffusion dominates at smaller scales while advection plays a more significant role at larger scales
vallis2017atmospheric
.

3.3
Teleconnection-Aware Transformer

Teleconnection-Aware Self-Attention

Teleconnections, statistically significant correlations between weather and climate events at distant locations, such as the North Atlantic Oscillation (NAO) and Madden-Julian Oscillation (MJO), are fundamental for accurate S2S forecasting
wallace1981teleconnections
.
We encode these global patterns through a novel teleconnection-aware self-attention mechanism.

For each frequency band
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{(\ell)}\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times D_{emb}}
, we first compute standard queries, keys, and values:
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{Q}=\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{(\ell)}\mathbf{W}^{Q}
,
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{K}=\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{(\ell)}\mathbf{W}^{K}
,
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{V}=\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{(\ell)}\mathbf{W}^{V}
, where
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{W}^{Q},\mathbf{W}^{K},\mathbf{W}^{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{emb}\times d_{k}}
are learnable projections and
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}d_{k}=D_{emb}/N_{h}
for
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}N_{h}
heads.

Our key innovation is introducing the teleconnection patterns to explicitly modulate attention mechanisms. We compute a global teleconnection vector that captures dominant climate modes:

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{c}=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{p}}\omega_{j}\mathbf{P}_{j},\quad\text{with}\quad\boldsymbol{\omega}=\text{softmax}(\mathbf{\bar{x}}\mathbf{W}^{p})\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{p}},

(7)

where
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{\bar{x}}=\frac{1}{H}\sum_{i=1}^{H}\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{i}
is the global atmosphere state,
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{W}^{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{emb}\times n_{p}}
projects this state onto
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}n_{p}
learnable coefficients, and
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{P}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{P}_{n_{p}}\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{emb}}
are vectors that represent the teleconnection patterns, which typically can be obtained by manual extraction or automatic learning from data.
We project
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{c}
into query space to create a teleconnection query
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{q}^{tel}=\mathbf{c}\mathbf{W}^{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}}
and compute attention scores
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}b_{j}
between this query and all latitude keys. These scores are added as a bias to the standard attention logits:

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}b_{j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{k}}}(\mathbf{q}^{tel}\cdot\mathbf{K}_{j}),\kern 5.0pt\tilde{A}_{ij}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{k}}}(\mathbf{Q}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{K}_{j})+\lambda\cdot b_{j},

(8)

where
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\lambda
is a hyperparameter controlling teleconnection influence.
This mechanism biases attention toward active global teleconnection patterns. For instance, during an MJO event, the model can emphasize connections between the tropical Indian Ocean/western Pacific and remote affected regions
zhang2005madden
, empowering teleconnection-aware forecasting.

Cross-Scale Interaction

Atmospheric processes operate across multiple scales with complex interactions
majda2007multiscale
. We enable cross-scale information flow through a hierarchical fusion mechanism. First, each frequency band is processed by a teleconnection-aware Transformer:
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\hat{\mathbf{X}}^{(\ell)}=\textit{TA-Transformer}_{\ell}(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{(\ell)})
.
We then implement cross-scale fusion from larger to smaller scales:
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\hat{\mathbf{X}}^{(\ell+1)}=\text{LayerNorm}(\text{MLP}([\hat{\mathbf{X}}^{(\ell)},\hat{\mathbf{X}}^{(\ell+1)}]))
, where
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}[\cdot,\cdot]
denotes concatenation.
Finally, we integrate information across all scales:
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{Z}_{final}=\frac{1}{L+1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}\hat{\mathbf{X}}^{(\ell)}
, yielding a representation that captures atmospheric dynamics across all scales.

3.4
S2S Forecasting

For each latitude
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}i
, we process its feature vector
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{z}^{final}_{i}=\mathbf{Z}_{final}(i,:)\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{emb}}
as follows:

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{y}_{i}=\mathbf{W}^{(2)}\text{GELU}(\mathbf{W}^{(1)}\mathbf{z}^{final}_{i}+\mathbf{b}^{(1)})+\mathbf{b}^{(2)}
, where
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{W}^{(1)}\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{emb}\times 2D_{emb}}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{W}^{(2)}\in\mathbb{R}^{2D_{emb}\times(2CW)}
.
The output
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{y}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{2CW}
is reshaped into a tensor representing predictions for both horizons at all longitudes for this latitude. Stacking these across all latitudes yields the final forecast fields
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\hat{\mathbf{Y}}^{(1)}_{t_{15}:t_{28}},\hat{\mathbf{Y}}^{(2)}_{t_{29}:t_{42}}\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times H\times W}
for the two target periods.
We train the model using the loss function:

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{2\times C\times H\times W}\Bigl(\left\lVert\hat{\mathbf{Y}}^{(1)}_{t_{15}:t_{28}}-\mathbf{Y}^{(1)}_{t_{15}:t_{28}}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\left\lVert\hat{\mathbf{Y}}^{(2)}_{t_{29}:t_{42}}-\mathbf{Y}^{(2)}_{t_{29}:t_{42}}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\Bigr).

(9)

To establish the mathematical foundations of our
TelePiT
and ensure its theoretical rigor and consistency, we provide a comprehensive theoretical analysis in Appendix
A
.

Evaluation Metric

Following existing works
liucirt
;
nathaniel2024chaosbench
;
rasp2024weatherbench
, we adopt three metrics:
(1) Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) with a latitude-based weighting scheme that accounts for Earth’s spherical geometry;
(2) Anomaly Correlation Coefficient (ACC), which measures the pattern similarity between predicted and observed anomalies relative to climatology;
and (3) Spectral Divergence (SpecDiv), a physics-based metric that quantifies the deviation between the power spectra of prediction and target in the spectral domain using Kullback-Leibler divergence principles
kullback1951information
.
For RMSE and SpecDiv, lower values indicate better performance, while higher ACC values signify better prediction skill. The detailed mathematical formulations of these metrics are provided in Appendix
B.1
.

Compared with data-driven models

Table

TelePiT

For geopotential heights (
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}z500

reduces RMSE by 9.5% and 8.6% respectively compared to the previous best model (CirT) at Weeks 3-4, while simultaneously achieving higher ACC values. This improvement is particularly noteworthy given that these upper-atmospheric variables govern large-scale circulation patterns crucial for extended-range forecasting.

The most striking performance advantage appears in near-surface variables. For 2-meter temperature (
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}t2m

TelePiT
achieves an RMSE of 12.057K at Weeks 3-4, representing a remarkable 57.7% reduction compared to CirT (28.526K) and 80.2% reduction compared to ClimaX (60.894K). This exceptional improvement for surface temperature, the variable with the greatest socioeconomic relevance, demonstrates
TelePiT
’s particular strength in capturing complex land-atmosphere interactions.

Critically,
TelePiT
retains a performance advantage even at extended lead times (weeks 5–6), exhibiting consistent skill margins across all variables. This sustained skill indicates that the model successfully captures both local physical processes and global circulation patterns across multiple temporal and spatial scales, and it underscores
TelePiT
’s robust representation of slowly evolving atmospheric phenomena that underpin sub-seasonal predictability.

Table

TelePiT
significantly outperforms all baselines, with improvements ranging from 37.1% to 98.2% compared to CirT.
These results demonstrate that
TelePiT
’s multi-scale physics-informed architecture maintains exceptionally realistic physical characteristics throughout the forecast period, particularly for variables with complex boundary-layer interactions where conventional models typically struggle with spectral fidelity.

Compared with numerical models

Beyond outperforming other data-driven approaches, our evaluation against operational numerical models reveals
TelePiT
’s remarkable capabilities across the entire atmospheric column. As shown in Figure
2

TelePiT
consistently achieves lower normalized RMSE (lighter colors) across almost all pressure levels (10-1000 hPa) for all variables.

Most notably,
TelePiT

This advantage is particularly pronounced at upper levels (10-200 hPa), where traditional models struggle to maintain forecast skill beyond week 2.
Critically, while physics-based models show substantial skill degradation between Weeks 3-4 and Weeks 5-6 (evidenced by darker colors in the right panels),
TelePiT
maintains more consistent performance across both forecast horizons.

4.2
Ablation Study

The ablation study presented in Table
3
systematically evaluates the contribution of each component in our proposed
TelePiT
architecture.
Spherical Harmonic Embedding (SHE) provides the most critical contribution, particularly for
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}t2m
prediction where its removal increases RMSE from 12.057 to 27.206 at Weeks 3-4. This substantial degradation demonstrates that effectively encoding Earth’s spherical geometry is fundamental for capturing planetary-scale circulation patterns.
Wavelet Decomposition (WD) plays an essential role in separating signals across temporal frequencies. Its removal particularly impacts geopotential height predictions (
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}z500
,
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}z850
), with RMSE increasing by 7-8%. This confirms our hypothesis that atmospheric dynamics operate simultaneously across multiple time scales, from fast-moving synoptic systems to slow-evolving planetary waves, requiring explicit multi-scale processing for accurate prediction.

Physics-Informed ODE shows more subtle but consistent contributions, particularly for near-surface wind components. Unlike purely data-driven approaches, the integration of simplified atmospheric dynamics through neural ODEs provides physical consistency that enhances model generalization, especially for variables with complex boundary-layer interactions.
Teleconnection Attention (TA) becomes increasingly valuable at longer lead times, with its removal causing notable degradation at Weeks 5-6. This aligns with meteorological understanding that as initial condition influence diminishes, large-scale teleconnections increasingly govern predictability.

Related Works

S2S forecasting has traditionally relied on sophisticated numerical weather prediction (NWP) systems
vitart2017subseasonal
;
white2017potential
, which integrate atmospheric and oceanic physics through coupled dynamical models. These systems, exemplified by ECMWF
molteni1996ecmwf
, UKMO
williams2015met
, NCEP
saha2014ncep
, and CMA
wu2019beijing
, have gradually extended forecast horizons through enhanced initialization techniques and ensemble methods
vitart2018sub
. However, these methods remain computationally intensive and often struggle to fully capture complex teleconnection patterns that significantly influence predictability beyond the 2-week deterministic limit
bauer2015quiet
;
robertson2020subseasonal
. Recent advancements utilizing empirical methods to extract large-scale modes of variability
cohen2019s2s
;
merryfield2020current
have shown promise but lack physical dynamics across scales.

Deep learning has revolutionized climate prediction. Foundational work by Rasp et al.
rasp2020weatherbench
established benchmarks for data-driven atmospheric forecasting, while subsequent architectures including FourCastNetV2
kurth2023fourcastnet
, Pangu-Weather
bi2023accurate
, GraphCast
lam2023learning
, and ClimaX
nguyen2023climax
demonstrated competitive performance against operational NWP systems for medium-range forecasting. Regional S2S forecasting has seen notable advances through specialized models like SubseasonalClimateUSA
mouatadid2023subseasonalclimateusa
, which focuses on North American climate predictions, and approaches by He et al.
he2022learning
and Hwang et al.
hwang2019improving
that target localized weather patterns. However, these regional models often overlook crucial global teleconnections. Recent global S2S efforts include FuXi-S2S
chen2024machine
, which employs an encoder-decoder framework for global precipitation forecasting, and CirT
liucirt
, which incorporates spherical geometry. Nevertheless, these approaches lack explicit incorporation of physical constraints and multi-scale atmospheric processes, limiting their ability to maintain skill at extended lead times
nathaniel2024chaosbench
.

Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) have shown increasing promise for integrating physical knowledge into data-driven models
raissi2019physics
;
karniadakis2021physics
. In climate science, Reichstein et al.
reichstein2019deep
and Willard et al.
willard2022integrating
established frameworks for integrating scientific knowledge with machine learning for Earth system modeling. Recent work on climate modeling with neural advection-diffusion equations
choi2023climate
has demonstrated how neural networks can parameterize complex climate dynamics while preserving physical principles. Complementary approaches in machine learning-accelerated computational fluid dynamics
kochkov2021machine
have shown that neural networks can significantly enhance traditional solvers while maintaining physical consistency. Unlike these works, which typically focus on individual atmospheric processes or fixed spatial scales,
TelePiT
uniquely combines learnable multi-scale decomposition with physics-informed ODEs that adaptively capture scale-dependent dynamics. For teleconnection modeling, previous efforts
cohen2021linking
have explored statistical approaches, while Ham et al.
ham2019deep
pioneered deep learning for El Niño prediction.
TelePiT
advances beyond these approaches by seamlessly integrating scale-aware physics with teleconnection-aware attention mechanisms, explicitly capturing the cross-scale interactions and global patterns that are crucial for S2S predictability while preserving Earth’s spherical geometry in a unified end-to-end architecture.

6
Conclusion

We presented
TelePiT
, a novel deep learning architecture for global S2S forecasting that integrates physical principles with data-driven learning. By combining Spherical Harmonic Embedding, Multi-Scale Physics-Informed ODE, and Teleconnection-Aware Transformer, we explicitly model the fundamental processes governing S2S predictability.
Our evaluation shows that
TelePiT
consistently outperforms both state-of-the-art data-driven models and operational numerical systems across all meteorological variables and forecast horizons. The most significant improvements occur in near-surface variables, particularly 2-meter temperature, with over 57% RMSE reduction compared to previous models. Ablation studies confirm each component’s meaningful contribution, with their combined effect becoming more pronounced at extended forecasting ranges.
This advances climate resilience applications in agriculture, energy, and disaster preparedness. Future work may incorporate additional Earth system components, further bridging the gap between weather and climate prediction.

Appendix A
Theoretical Analysis

In this section, we provide a theoretical analysis of
TelePiT
and establish mathematical connections to fundamental principles in both machine learning and atmospheric science.

A.1
Spectral Representation on the Sphere

The Spherical Harmonic Embedding (SHE) in our model builds upon the well-established spectral theory for functions on the sphere. Any square-integrable function
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}f(\theta,\phi)
on the sphere can be represented as an infinite series of spherical harmonics:

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}f(\theta,\phi)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}a_{\ell m}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi)

(10)

where
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi)
are the spherical harmonics of degree
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\ell
and order
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}m
, and
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}a_{\ell m}
are the spectral coefficients.
Our learnable zonal averaging approach in SHE provides a computationally efficient approximation to the important subset of zonal harmonics (
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}m=0
). This connection is justified by the observations in atmospheric science that many large-scale circulation patterns exhibit strong zonal symmetry
baldwin2003stratospheric
;
kidston2015stratospheric
.

Proposition 1

(Zonal Projection Property)
.

The zonal averaging operation implements an orthogonal projection onto the subspace of zonally symmetric functions. For a function
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}f(\theta,\phi)
, the zonal average
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\bar{f}(\theta)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(\theta,\phi)d\phi
projects onto the
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}m=0
spherical harmonic modes:

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\bar{f}(\theta)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}a_{\ell,0}Y_{\ell}^{0}(\theta)

(11)

where
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}Y_{\ell}^{0}(\theta)=\sqrt{\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}}P_{\ell}(\cos\theta)
are the zonal harmonics and
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}P_{\ell}
are the Legendre polynomials.

Proposition 2

(Approximation Quality)
.

The discrete zonal averaging in SHE approximates the continuous projection with error bounded by:

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\left\|\mathbf{u}_{i}-\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}a_{\ell,0}P_{\ell}(\cos\theta_{i})\right\|\leq\mathcal{O}(D^{-1/2}+L^{-1})

(12)

where
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{u}_{i}=\frac{1}{W}\sum_{j=1}^{W}\mathbf{X}_{i,j}
is the empirical zonal average and the approximation improves with embedding dimension
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}D
and truncation level
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}L
.

This selective spectral focus allows
TelePiT
to efficiently represent large-scale atmospheric patterns while maintaining computational tractability, with theoretical guarantees on the approximation quality.

A.2
Multi-Scale Analysis and Function Approximation

The Wavelet Decomposition (WD) component implements a learnable multi-scale analysis that generalizes traditional wavelet transforms. Mathematically, our approach creates a data-adaptive basis that separates signals across different temporal and spatial frequencies.

Assumption 1
(Signal Regularity). We assume that atmospheric signals possess sufficient regularity, specifically Hölder continuity with exponent
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\alpha>0
, enabling the stated approximation rates. This assumption is well-justified for large-scale atmospheric variables due to the underlying physics.

Proposition 3

(Task-Adaptive Decomposition)
.

Under Assumption 1, the learnable wavelet decomposition converges to a multi-scale representation that minimizes the S2S forecasting objective, achieving decomposition error bounded by:

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{x}-\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}\mathbf{x}^{(\ell)}\|^{2}]\leq C\cdot L^{-\alpha}

(13)

where
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}C
is a constant depending on the signal characteristics and
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\alpha>0
reflects the smoothness of atmospheric signals.
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}L
is the number of frequency bands.

Proof sketch.

The MLP transforms at each level provide universal function approximation capabilities with sufficient width. By minimizing the end-to-end prediction loss, these MLPs learn to extract frequency components that maximize predictive skill rather than reconstruction fidelity. The recursive binary splitting operation ensures that the
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}L+1
frequency bands form a partition of the signal’s spectral content. Under the regularity assumption, this adaptive partitioning achieves the stated convergence rate through standard approximation theory arguments.
∎

Proposition 4

(Information Preservation)
.

Each frequency band
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\ell
preserves mutual information with the forecast target, satisfying:

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}I(\mathbf{x}^{(\ell)};\mathbf{y})\geq(1-\delta)I(\mathbf{x};\mathbf{y})

(14)

for arbitrarily small
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\delta>0
given sufficient model capacity, where
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}I(\cdot;\cdot)
denotes mutual information.

This adaptability is critical for S2S forecasting, as traditional fixed wavelet bases may not optimally separate the complex, non-stationary atmospheric signals that span multiple scales.

A.3
Physics-Informed Neural ODEs

The Physics-Informed Neural ODE component provides a learnable framework that incorporates fundamental atmospheric transport processes as inductive biases for latent space dynamics. While we do not claim to implement specific atmospheric equations, our formulation draws inspiration from key physical principles governing atmospheric evolution.

Latent Space Transport Dynamics:
We model the evolution of latent representations
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
along the latitude dimension using a transport-inspired ODE:

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\frac{d\mathbf{x}_{i}}{dt}=\gamma\cdot\tanh\Bigg({\color[rgb]{0.0078,0.4648,0.7422}\underbrace{\boldsymbol{\nu}\odot(\mathbf{x}_{i+1}-2\mathbf{x}_{i}+\mathbf{x}_{i-1})}_{\smash{\text{diffusion}}}}+{\color[rgb]{0.8477,0.2617,0.082}\underbrace{\boldsymbol{\mu}\odot\frac{\mathbf{x}_{i+1}-\mathbf{x}_{i-1}}{2}}_{\smash{\text{advection}}}}+{\color[rgb]{0.1875,0.2461,0.625}\underbrace{\boldsymbol{f}}_{\smash{\text{forcing}}}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0.5391,0.4805}\underbrace{\alpha\cdot\text{MLP}(\mathbf{x}_{i})}_{\smash{\text{neural correction}}}}\Bigg)

(15)

Proposition 5

(Latent Space Transport Interpretation)
.

The general form of our transport-inspired neural ODE decomposes as:

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\frac{d\mathbf{x}}{dt}=\mathcal{T}_{transport}(\mathbf{x};\boldsymbol{\theta}_{phys})+\mathcal{T}_{neural}(\mathbf{x};\boldsymbol{\theta}_{nn})

(16)

where
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathcal{T}_{transport}
captures simplified transport processes with learnable physics parameters
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{phys}=\{\boldsymbol{\nu},\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{f}\}\in\mathbb{R}^{3D}
, and
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathcal{T}_{neural}
provides additional modeling capacity through neural network parameters
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{nn}
.

Design Rationale
: Our approach establishes correspondence with atmospheric transport processes:

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0.0078,0.4648,0.7422}\boldsymbol{\nu}\odot(\mathbf{x}_{i+1}-2\mathbf{x}_{i}+\mathbf{x}_{i-1})}

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow\text{Meridional mixing and smoothing}

(17)

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0.8477,0.2617,0.082}\boldsymbol{\mu}\odot\frac{\mathbf{x}_{i+1}-\mathbf{x}_{i-1}}{2}}

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow\text{Meridional transport processes}

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0.1875,0.2461,0.625}\boldsymbol{f}}

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow\text{External climate drivers}

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.5391,0.4805}\alpha\cdot\text{MLP}(\mathbf{x}_{i})}

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow\text{Unresolved nonlinear dynamics}

Proposition 6

(Numerical Stability)
.

The transport-inspired ODE with hyperbolic tangent activation is globally Lipschitz continuous with constant
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}L=\gamma
, ensuring controlled error propagation:

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\left\|\frac{d\mathbf{x}}{dt}-\frac{d\mathbf{x}^{\prime}}{dt}\right\|\leq\gamma\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\|

(18)

for any latent states
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{HD}
. This global Lipschitz property guarantees existence and uniqueness of solutions and supports numerical stability during integration.

Proposition 7

(Parameter Efficiency)
.

The physics-inspired structure reduces the effective parameter space by constraining the dynamics to transport-consistent manifolds. Specifically, the constraint reduces the degrees of freedom from
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathcal{O}(HD^{2})
(unconstrained) to
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathcal{O}(HD+D^{2})
(transport-constrained), potentially improving sample efficiency.

Implementation Considerations
: We apply periodic boundary conditions consistent with our latitude-based processing and employ adaptive step-size integration methods for numerical accuracy. The scaling factor
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\gamma
and hyperbolic tangent activation prevent gradient explosion while preserving the fundamental transport structure.

We acknowledge that our latent space dynamics are inspired by, rather than derived from, specific atmospheric equations. The correspondence between latent dimensions and physical quantities emerges through the training process rather than being explicitly enforced.

A.4
Teleconnection Representation Theory

The Teleconnection-Aware Transformer implements a mechanism to learn and leverage global patterns in atmospheric data. We establish its theoretical connection to atmospheric teleconnections and provide convergence guarantees.

Proposition 8

(Nonlinear Teleconnection Learning)
.

The learned teleconnection patterns
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\{\mathbf{P}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n_{p}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{HD}
converge to a basis that captures the dominant modes of atmospheric covariance, encompassing both linear relationships and nonlinear teleconnection patterns. Formally:

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\lim_{T\to\infty}\text{span}(\{\mathbf{P}_{j}\})\supseteq\text{span}(\{\text{EOF}_{j}^{(linear)}\})

(19)

where the inclusion becomes equality when the attention mechanism reduces to linear operations.

Proof sketch.

The teleconnection attention computes pattern weights as
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\boldsymbol{\omega}=\text{softmax}(\mathbf{\bar{x}}\mathbf{W}^{p})
where
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{\bar{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
is the global mean state. During training, the patterns
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{P}_{j}
and projection matrix
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{W}^{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{D\times n_{p}}
are jointly optimized to minimize prediction error.

This optimization is equivalent to finding patterns that maximize the predictive information content, which necessarily includes the variance-maximizing directions captured by linear EOFs. The nonlinear attention mechanism extends this to capture higher-order statistical relationships, ensuring the learned subspace contains the linear EOF subspace.
∎

Proposition 9

(Sample Complexity for Teleconnection Learning)
.

To learn
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}k
dominant teleconnection patterns with approximation error
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\varepsilon
in the
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}L_{2}
norm, the teleconnection module requires
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathcal{O}(k\log(D)/\varepsilon^{2})
training samples, where the logarithmic dependence on dimension reflects the low-intrinsic-dimensional nature of atmospheric teleconnections.

Proposition 10

(Convergence Rate)
.

Under standard regularity conditions on the atmospheric data distribution, the teleconnection patterns converge to their population optima at rate
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathcal{O}(T^{-1/2})
where
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}T
is the number of training iterations, matching the convergence rate of stochastic gradient methods for smooth objectives.

This theoretical framework connects our learning-based approach with traditional techniques while extending to capture nonlinear teleconnection relationships that linear methods cannot detect.

A.5.2
Error Propagation Analysis

Proposition 12

(Multi-Scale Error Bounds)
.

For a signal decomposed into
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}L+1
frequency bands, the prediction error is bounded by:

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbb{E}[\|y-\hat{y}\|^{2}]\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}\lambda_{\ell}\mathbb{E}[\|y_{\ell}-\hat{y}_{\ell}\|^{2}]+\mathcal{O}(L^{-\alpha})

(21)

where
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}y_{\ell},\hat{y}_{\ell}
represent ground truth and prediction for frequency band
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\ell
,
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\lambda_{\ell}\geq 0
are band-specific weights with
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}=1
, and the additional term bounds decomposition error.

Proof sketch.

The multi-scale architecture processes frequency bands independently before fusion, creating a natural error decomposition. The hierarchical fusion mechanism that privileges low-frequency information creates weights
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\lambda_{\ell}
that typically decrease with frequency, providing robustness against high-frequency noise. The decomposition error term reflects the finite precision of the learned wavelet basis.
∎

A.5.3
Generalization Bounds

Proposition 13

(Generalization Error)
.

For a training set of size
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}n
drawn from the atmospheric state distribution, with probability at least
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}1-\delta
, the generalization error satisfies:

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathcal{R}(h)-\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{n}(h)\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log(L+1)+\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}\mathcal{C}(h_{\ell})+\log(1/\delta)}{n}}\right)

(22)

where
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathcal{R}(h)
is the true risk,
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{n}(h)
is the empirical risk, and
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathcal{C}(h_{\ell})
measures the complexity of the model component for frequency band
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\ell
.

Proof sketch.

The physics-inspired constraints effectively reduce the hypothesis space complexity for each frequency band, lowering
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathcal{C}(h_{\ell})
compared to unconstrained neural networks. The multi-scale decomposition enables specialized processing that further reduces per-band complexity. These reductions, combined with standard uniform convergence arguments, yield the stated bound.
∎

Appendix B
Additional Experimental Details

B.1
Evaluation Metric

RMSE and ACC

Following existing works
liucirt
;
nathaniel2024chaosbench
;
rasp2024weatherbench
, we adopt three metrics to evaluate the model performance: Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) with a weighting scheme at each latitude
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\theta_{i}
, Anomaly Correlation Coefficient (ACC), and Spectral Divergence (SpecDiv).

The RMSE and ACC are defined as follows:

\displaystyle\text{RMSE}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{|\theta||\phi|}\sum_{i=1}^{|\theta|}\sum_{j=1}^{|\phi|}w(\theta_{i})(\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{i,j}-\mathbf{Y}_{i,j})^{2}},\text{ACC}=\frac{\sum(\theta_{i})[A_{\hat{Y}}\cdot A_{Y}]}{\sqrt{\sum w(\theta_{i})A_{\hat{Y}}^{2}\sum w(\theta_{i})A_{Y}^{2}}},

(23)

where
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}w(\theta_{i})=\frac{cos(\theta_{i})}{\frac{1}{|\theta|}\sum_{a=1}^{|\theta|}cos(\theta_{a})}
,
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\theta_{i}\in\theta=[-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}]
and
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\phi_{i}\in\phi=[-\pi,\pi]
denotes the set of all latitudes and longitude, respectively.
The predicted and observed anomalies at each grid are denoted by

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}A_{\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{i,j}}=\hat{\mathbf{Y}}_{i,j}-C
and
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}A_{\mathbf{Y}_{i,j}}=\mathbf{Y}_{i,j}-C

where
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}C
is observational climatology.

SpecDiv

For the physics-based metric, we adopt the SpecDiv as proposed in
nathaniel2024chaosbench
. This metric measures the deviation between the power spectra of prediction and target in the spectral domain. It is particularly useful for evaluating the preservation of high-frequency components in forecasts, which tend to become blurry due to power divergence in the spectral domain. The SpecDiv is defined as:

\displaystyle\text{SpecDiv}=\sum_{k}S^{\prime}(k)\cdot\log(\frac{S^{\prime}(k)}{\hat{S}^{\prime}(k)})

(24)

where
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}S^{\prime}(k)
and
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\hat{S}^{\prime}(k)
represent the normalized power spectra of target and prediction respectively. This metric follows the principles of Kullback-Leibler divergence to quantify the spectral differences between predictions and ground truth.

In addition to the metrics described in the main paper, we also utilize Multi-Scale Structural Similarity (MS-SSIM) and Spectral Residual (SpecRes) for comprehensive evaluation.

MS-SSIM

This metric evaluates structural similarity between forecast and ground-truth across multiple scales, making it particularly suitable for weather forecasting where phenomena range from large-scale systems like cyclones to small localized features such as thunderstorms.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{Y}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\hat{\mathbf{Y}}
represent the ground-truth and predicted images respectively. MS-SSIM incorporates three comparison measures defined as:

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}l(\mathbf{Y},\hat{\mathbf{Y}})=\frac{2\mu_{\mathbf{Y}}\mu_{\hat{\mathbf{Y}}}+C_{1}}{\mu_{\mathbf{Y}}^{2}+\mu_{\hat{\mathbf{Y}}}^{2}+C_{1}},c(\mathbf{Y},\hat{\mathbf{Y}})=\frac{2\sigma_{\mathbf{Y}}\sigma_{\hat{\mathbf{Y}}}+C_{2}}{\sigma_{\mathbf{Y}}^{2}+\sigma_{\hat{\mathbf{Y}}}^{2}+C_{2}},s(\mathbf{Y},\hat{\mathbf{Y}})=\frac{\sigma_{\mathbf{Y}\hat{\mathbf{Y}}}+C_{3}}{\sigma_{\mathbf{Y}}\sigma_{\hat{\mathbf{Y}}}+C_{3}}

(25)

where
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mu_{\mathbf{Y}}
,
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\sigma_{\mathbf{Y}}^{2}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\sigma_{\mathbf{Y}\hat{\mathbf{Y}}}
are the mean of
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{Y}
, the variance of
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{Y}
, and the covariance between
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{Y}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\hat{\mathbf{Y}}
, respectively. The constants
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}C_{1}=(K_{1}L)^{2}
,
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}C_{2}=(K_{2}L)^{2}
, and
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}C_{3}=C_{2}/2
are included to avoid instability when the denominators are close to zero, where
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}L=255
is the dynamic range of the grayscale images, and
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}K_{1}\ll 1
and
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}K_{2}\ll 1
are two small constants.
The MS-SSIM metric is computed by analyzing these components across multiple scales through successive low-pass filtering and downsampling. At each scale
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}j
, the contrast comparison
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}c_{j}(\mathbf{Y},\hat{\mathbf{Y}})
and structure comparison
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}s_{j}(\mathbf{Y},\hat{\mathbf{Y}})
are calculated, while luminance comparison
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}l_{M}(\mathbf{Y},\hat{\mathbf{Y}})
is only considered at the coarsest scale
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}M
. The final MS-SSIM metric is defined as:

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\text{MS-SSIM}=[l_{M}(\mathbf{Y},\hat{\mathbf{Y}})]^{\alpha_{M}}\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{M}[c_{j}(\mathbf{Y},\hat{\mathbf{Y}})]^{\beta_{j}}[s_{j}(\mathbf{Y},\hat{\mathbf{Y}})]^{\gamma_{j}}

(26)

where
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\alpha_{M}
,
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\beta_{j}
, and
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\gamma_{j}
are weighting parameters. We follow standard practice by setting
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}M=5
and using the parameter values established in previous literature
nathaniel2024chaosbench
.

SpecRes

While SpecDiv measures the divergence between power spectra, SpecRes quantifies the root of the expected squared residual in the spectral domain:

\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\text{SpecRes}=\sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{k}[(\hat{S}^{\prime}(k)-S^{\prime}(k))^{2}]}

(27)

where
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\hat{S}^{\prime}(k)
and
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}S^{\prime}(k)
are the normalized power spectra of prediction and target respectively, and
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbb{E}_{k}
denotes expectation over the high-frequency wavenumbers
\color[rgb]{0.1719,0.2422,0.3125}\mathbf{K}_{q}
. Similar to SpecDiv, this metric will be zero if the power spectra are identical, but it emphasizes the magnitude of spectral differences rather than their distributional divergence.

B.2
Baselines

Operational Forecasting Systems

These established numerical weather prediction systems represent the current state-of-practice in operational meteorological forecasting, employing sophisticated physics-based models with data assimilation techniques.

•

ECMWF:
The European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts employs the Integrated Forecasting System (IFS) CY48R1 version, generating predictions twice weekly (Mondays and Thursdays) with a 46-day forecast horizon.

•

UKMO:
The UK Met Office utilizes the GloSea6 (Global Seasonal forecast system version 6) for daily control forecasts extending to 60 days.

•

NCEP:
The National Centers for Environmental Prediction deploys the Climate Forecast System version 2 (CFSv2) for daily control forecasts with a 45-day prediction window.

•

CMA:
The China Meteorological Administration implements the Beijing Climate Center’s fully-coupled BCC-CSM2-HR model, providing twice-weekly forecasts (Mondays and Thursdays) with a 60-day lead time.

Data-Driven Approaches

These data-driven models leverage recent advances in machine learning to capture atmospheric dynamics without explicit physical equations, offering complementary capabilities to traditional forecasting methods.

•

GraphCast:
A graph neural network architecture that employs multi-mesh methodology to capture complex atmospheric dynamics. Implementation accessed via the ECMWF AI model repository. Due to computational constraints, evaluation is limited to weeks 3-4.

•

FourCastNetV2:
A Vision Transformer-based iterative model incorporating Adaptive Fourier Neural Operators for spatial patch mixing. Analysis covers 11 months of data (excluding October 2018 due to data availability issues).

•

PanguWeather:
Employs separate tokenization for pressure-level and single-level data within a transformer architecture, trained using hierarchical temporal aggregation.

•

ClimaX:
Utilizes a direct training approach with Vision Transformers, featuring independent tokenization per variable followed by variable aggregation.

•

CirT:
Implements geometry-aware transformers specifically designed to accommodate Earth’s spherical structure for improved subseasonal-to-seasonal forecasting performance.

For FourCastNetV2, GraphCast, and PanguWeather, the inference was performed using the ECMWF AI model repository API (
https://github.com/ecmwf-lab/ai-models
). For ClimaX and CirT, these models were retrained following the same configurations as
TelePiT
to ensure fair comparison.

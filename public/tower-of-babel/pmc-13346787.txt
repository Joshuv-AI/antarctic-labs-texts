AKR1B1 promotes canine mammary tumorigenesis via activation of PI3K/AKT signaling and acts as a serum biomarker
Authors: Zhe Cao, Qinyu Zhang, Yujun Zhou, Haoyu Li, Deliang Cao, Zhiliang Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAldo-keto reductase 1B1 (AKR1B1), also named as aldose reductase, is an enzyme implicated in metabolic regulation and signaling transduction in human cancers, but little is known in canine mammary tumors (CMTs). This study investigated expression in CMT tissues, functional role in tumor progression, and potential of AKR1B1 as a circulating biomarker. AKR1B1 expression was evaluated by immunohistochemistry in paired CMT and adjacent normal mammary tissues, and CMT-U27 cells with lentiviral AKR1B1 overexpression or shRNA-mediated AKR1B1 knockdown were used for investigation of cell proliferation, migration, and Western blot analysis of PI3K/AKT signaling and cell-cycle regulators. Results showed that AKR1B1 was markedly overexpressed in CMT tissues compared to adjacent normal glands. Targeted expression of AKR1B1 in CMT-U27 cells promoted proliferation, migration, accompanied by increase of phosphorylated AKT (Thr308) and downregulation of P21 and P27, whereas AKR1B1 knockdown led to opposite effects. Magnetic-particle chemiluminescence immunoassay was developed to measure serum AKR1B1 concentrations, and results showed serum AKR1B1 was significantly higher in Canines with CMT (4.65 ± 0.37 ng/mL; n = 50) than in healthy controls (2.27 ± 0.15 ng/mL; n = 56). Receiver operating characteristic analysis demonstrated impressive diagnostic performance with AUC = 0.8386 (95% CI 0.7597–0.9174), sensitivity at 74.0% and specificity at 83.9% at a cutoff of 3.08 ng/mL. These findings indicate that AKR1B1 acts as a tumorigenic driver via PI3K/AKT-mediated oncogenesis in CMT and a serum biomarker for non-invasive diagnosis of CMT and monitoring of disease progression.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-47241-y.Keywords: AKR1B1, Canine mammary tumors, PI3K/AKT signaling, Serum biomarker, Cell proliferationSubject terms: Biomarkers, Cancer, Cell biology, Molecular biology, Oncology

Introduction

In intact female Canines, canine mammary tumors (CMTs) are the most prevalent neoplasms, representing approximately 50–70% of all tumors in this population, and about half of CMTs are malignant with variable metastatic potential1,2. Owing to shared hormonal influences, histologic subtypes, and molecular features, CMTs are recognized as a spontaneous model for human breast cancer, offering valuable translational insight into tumor biology and therapy3–5. Despite this relevance, diagnosis still relies primarily on physical examination and imaging, as no validated serum biomarker for early detection or disease monitoring has been adopted in veterinary oncology.

Among molecular pathways implicated in CMTs, dysregulation of PI3K/AKT signaling is one of the most frequent and functionally important. Overexpression and activation of PI3K/AKT/PTEN components correlate with high histologic grade and aggressive behavior6, and pharmacologic inhibition of this pathway suppresses proliferation and induces apoptosis in CMT cell lines7. Activating PIK3CA mutations, including hotspot variants such as H1047R, have also been detected in CMT tissues and explored as liquid-biopsy targets8. However, upstream metabolic regulators converging on PI3K/AKT signaling remain poorly defined in the canine context.

The aldo-keto reductase (AKR) superfamily comprises NAD(P)H-dependent oxidoreductases involved in carbonyl detoxification, steroid metabolism, and redox homeostasis9,10. Among them, AKR1B1 (also named as aldose reductase, AR) catalyzes the rate-limiting step of the polyol pathway and connects glucose metabolism to oxidative stress. In human cancers, aberrant AKR1B1 overexpression promotes proliferation, epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT), and therapeutic resistance through modulation of the PI3K/AKT and NF-κB pathways11–14. Inhibition or knockdown of AKR1B1 restores PTEN and FOXO3A activity, reducing AKT phosphorylation and tumor growth.

In Canines, AKR1B1 has an additional evolutionary dimension. Genomic analyses have identified a domestication-associated duplication of the AKR1B1 gene, with the canine-specific copy expressed predominantly in liver and intestine and proposed to enhance antioxidant and metabolic capacity during adaptation to starch-rich diets15. This duplication may influence disease susceptibility, yet its functional consequences in mammary tissue and cancer remain unknown.

Despite accumulating evidence in human oncology that implicates AKR1B1 and related enzymes such as AKR1B10 in epithelial carcinogenesis16, their role in canine mammary tumors has not been explored. Most studies have focused on hormone receptors, HER2, COX-2, or the PI3K/AKT axis itself6, and no routine serum biomarker with high diagnostic accuracy has been established for CMT.

Given these gaps, we hypothesized that AKR1B1 is overexpressed in canine mammary tumors, promotes tumor cell proliferation and migration via PI3K/AKT signaling, and can be detected as a circulating biomarker in the serum of affected Canines. To test this, we (i) evaluated AKR1B1 overexpression in CMT tissues by immunohistochemistry, (ii) examined the effects of AKR1B1 overexpression and knockdown on proliferation, migration, and PI3K/AKT-P21/P27 signaling in CMT-U27 cells, and (iii) developed a magnetic-particle chemiluminescence assay to quantify serum AKR1B1 and assess its diagnostic performance. Collectively, this study defined AKR1B1 as a mechanistic contributor to CMT biology and a candidate of non-invasive biomarker for diagnosis and disease monitoring.

Materials and methods

Study design and serum sample collection

Mammary tumor tissues and serum samples were obtained from client-owned female canines presented to the Teaching Hospital of the College of Veterinary Medicine, Hunan Agricultural University (Changsha, China) for evaluation of mammary masses between March 2023 and April 2024.

Canines were included in the CMT group if they had a histopathologically confirmed mammary tumor and had not received prior chemotherapy, radiotherapy, or hormone therapy. Control serum samples were collected from clinically healthy female canines presented during the same period for routine health examinations or elective procedures and with no clinical or imaging evidence of neoplasia. The clinical characteristics of dogs in the tumor and control groups (e.g., age, breed, weight, spay status, tumor size, histologic subtype, grade, and metastasis status when available) are summarized in results.

Dogs with overt systemic inflammatory, infectious, endocrine, or metabolic diseases were not eligible for the control group based on history, physical examination, and available laboratory data. For the CMT group, dogs with severe systemic illness judged by the attending clinician to confound interpretation of serum biomarkers unrelated to the mammary tumor were not enrolled.

At the time of mastectomy, tumor and adjacent grossly normal mammary tissues were collected for histopathology, immunohistochemistry (IHC), and protein analysis. For serum collection, approximately 3–5 mL of venous blood was drawn into serum-separation tubes before surgery or routine procedures, allowed to clot for 30–60 min at room temperature, and centrifuged at 1500–2000×g for 10 min. The resulting serum was aliquoted and stored at − 80 °C until analysis; hemolyzed or markedly lipemic samples were excluded.

All procedures complied with institutional and national guidelines for the care and use of client-owned animals in research. The study protocol was approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Hunan Agricultural University (Ethics Review Sect. 2023 No. (21)). Written informed consent was obtained from all canine owners prior to enrollment and sample collection.

Tumor tissues and histopathology

Mammary tumor and adjacent grossly normal tissues were collected from canines undergoing mastectomy. Samples were formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE), and sectioned for histopathology and immunohistochemistry. Tumors were classified and graded according to the World Health Organization/Goldschmidt system by a board-certified veterinary pathologist. Tumor nodules and adjacent normal mammary gland were identified on hematoxylin-eosin (H&E)-stained sections and used to guide subsequent IHC evaluation17.

Cell line and culture conditions

The canine mammary tumor cell line CMT-U27 (ATCC, Manassas, VA, USA) was cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM, high glucose; 11965092, Gibco, Grand Island, NY, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 µg/mL streptomycin (P4333, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA). Cells were maintained at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO₂ and subcultured at 70–80% confluence using 0.25% trypsin-EDTA18 (SM-2003, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA).

Generation of AKR1B1-overexpressing and knockdown CMT-U27 cell lines

For AKR1B1 overexpression, the full-length canine AKR1B1 coding sequence (Gene ID: 607537) was cloned into the lentiviral vector pCDH-CMV-MCS-EF1-Puro (pCDH-AKR1B1; Cat. ZT113, Hunan Fenghui Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China). For knockdown experiments, short hairpin RNA (shRNA) sequences targeting canine AKR1B1 were cloned into the pGreenPuro vector (shAKR1B1; Cat. ZT704, Hunan Fenghui Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China).

For lentiviral packaging, HEK293T cells were seeded in 10-cm dishes and transfected with the AKR1B1 expression plasmid or shRNA plasmid together with packaging plasmids using Lipofectamine 3000 (Cat. L3000150, Invitrogen, CA, USA). After 24 h, the medium was replaced, and viral supernatants were collected at 48–72 h.

For infection, CMT-U27 cells were seeded at ~ 60% confluence and exposed to lentiviral supernatants (MOI = 1) in the presence of Polybrene (1:2000 dilution; Cat. TR1003, Millipore, Billerica, MA, USA) for 48 h. Cells were then selected with puromycin (2–4 µg/mL; Cat. P9620, Millipore) for 7–10 days to establish stable polyclonal AKR1B1-overexpressing (OE-AKR1B1) and AKR1B1-knockdown (shAKR1B1) cell lines, with the corresponding empty vectors used as controls.

Cell proliferation assays

Cell proliferation was assessed using a CCK-8 assay. CMT-U27 cells (control, AKR1B1-overexpressing, and AKR1B1-silenced) were seeded into 96-well plates at 2–4 × 10³ cells per well in complete medium. At 0, 24, 48, 72, 96 and 120 h, 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent (96992, Millipore, Billerica, MA, USA) was added to each well and incubated for 1–2 h at 37 °C. Absorbance at 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader, and growth curves were generated from the mean absorbance values of replicate wells.

Would healing assays

Cell migration was evaluated by a wound-healing assay. Confluent monolayers of CMT-U27 cells were scratched with a sterile 200 µL pipette tip to create a linear wound, then gently rinsed with PBS to remove detached cells. Cells were cultured in serum-reduced medium (1% FBS), and phase-contrast images of the wound area were captured at 0, 24, and 48 h using an inverted microscope. Wound width was measured at three predefined positions per well using ImageJ software, and the percentage of wound closure was calculated relative to the 0 h time point.

Transwell migration assays

Cell migration were further assessed using Transwell chambers with 8.0 μm pore size inserts (BD Biosciences, San Jose, CA, USA). For migration assays, 3–5 × 10⁴ cells in serum-free medium were seeded into the upper chamber, and medium containing 10% FBS was placed in the lower chamber as chemoattractant. After 24 h, non-migrated cells on the upper surface were removed with a cotton swab, and cells that had migrated to the underside of the membrane were fixed, stained with crystal violet, and counted in five random microscopic fields per insert.

Western blot analysis

Total protein was extracted from cultured CMT-U27 cell lines (control, AKR1B1-overexpressing, and AKR1B1-silenced) using RIPA buffer supplemented with protease and phosphatase inhibitors. Protein concentrations were determined using a BCA assay. Equal amounts of protein (40 µg) were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred onto PVDF membranes. Membranes were blocked with 5% non-fat milk in TBST for 1 h at room temperature and then incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against AKR1B1, PI3K (Cat. 4060), total AKT (Cat. 9272), phospho-AKT (Thr308; Cat. 9275), p21 (Cat. 2946), p27 (Cat. 2552), and β-actin (loading control; Cat. 4967) (all from Cell Signaling Technology, MA, USA).

After washing, membranes were incubated with appropriate HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies and visualized using enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL). Band intensities were quantified by densitometry using ImageJ and normalized to β-actin. Uncropped Western blot images are provided in Supplementary Figure S1.

Immunohistochemistry for AKR1B1

A mouse anti-AKR1B1 monoclonal antibody was generated in-house by immunizing mice with recombinant canine AKR1B1 protein and establishing anti-AKR1B1 monoclonal hybridoma cell lines (Supplemental S1A demonstrate the activity and specificity of AKR1B1 antibody). For IHC, formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded mammary tissue Sect. (4 μm) were deparaffinized in xylene, rehydrated through graded ethanol, and subjected to heat-induced antigen retrieval using citrate buffer (pH 6.0). Endogenous peroxidase activity was quenched with 3% hydrogen peroxide (88597, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA).

Sections were incubated overnight at 4 °C with the mouse anti-AKR1B1 monoclonal antibody, followed by incubation with an HRP-conjugated secondary antibody and visualization with 3,3′-diaminobenzidine (DAB; 34002, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). Nuclei were counterstained with hematoxylin. Negative control sections processed in parallel with omission of the primary antibody were used to monitor nonspecific staining.

Although a semi-quantitative scoring system (staining intensity 0–3 and percentage of positive cells 0–4) was considered during study design, in the final analysis each case was classified simply as AKR1B1-positive or AKR1B1-negative based on the presence or absence of specific cytoplasmic staining in mammary epithelial cells. A section was classified as positive if epithelial cells showed any specific AKR1B1 staining clearly above background, and negative if no specific staining was observed. Stromal fibroblasts and inflammatory cells were also examined for staining. Inter-observer concordance for AKR1B1 positivity was high, with initial classifications agreeing in > 80% of cases; the remaining slides were resolved by consensus review.

Immunohistochemical staining for AKR1B1 was performed on tumor tissue from all 20 dogs included in the tissue cohort. For each of those 20 cases, tumor and adjacent normal mammary tissues were assessed on the same slide series. All slides were independently reviewed by two observers who were blinded to clinical information; discrepancies were resolved by joint review at a multi-headed microscope. Representative low- and high-magnification images are shown in Fig. 1.

Serum AKR1B1 measurement and analytical validation

Serum AKR1B1 concentrations were determined using a two-site magnetic particle chemiluminescence immunoassay developed in-house. Briefly, a biotinylated mouse anti-AKR1B1 monoclonal antibody was immobilized on streptavidin-coated magnetic beads as the capture antibody, and an acridinium ester-labeled rabbit polyclonal anti-AKR1B1 antibody served as the detection antibody. Following incubation of beads with serum samples and washing steps, the resulting sandwich complexes were measured on an automated chemiluminescence analyzer. Emitted light intensity was recorded in relative light units (RLU) and converted to AKR1B1 concentration using a calibration curve generated from recombinant AKR1B1 standards over the analytical range.

All serum samples were tested in duplicate; results with coefficients of variation (CV) > 8% were re-analyzed. Analytical performance of the assay, including lower limit of detection, analytical measuring range, linearity, recovery, and intra- and inter-assay coefficients of variation, was evaluated using serial dilutions of recombinant AKR1B1 and pooled canine sera and is summarized in Supplementary Table S1.

Diagnostic performance of serum AKR1B1 for discriminating dogs with mammary tumors from healthy controls was assessed by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis, and the area under the curve (AUC), sensitivity, specificity, and optimal cut-off value were calculated using the Youden index.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism (version X; GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). Data are expressed as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM) unless otherwise indicated. The distribution of continuous variables was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test and visual inspection of Q-Q plots. For normally distributed data, comparisons between two groups were performed using unpaired Student’s t-tests; for non-normally distributed data, the Mann-Whitney U test was used. For multiple-group comparisons, one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post hoc test was applied when parametric assumptions were met, and the Kruskal–Wallis test with Dunn’s post hoc test was used otherwise.

ROC curves were generated to evaluate the diagnostic performance of serum AKR1B1, and AUC, sensitivity, and specificity were calculated as described above. A two-sided p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

AKR1B1 is overexpressed in canine breast tumor tissues

Table 1 summarizes the clinical characteristics of the 20 canine mammary tumor tissue samples used for histopathology and AKR1B1 immunohistochemistry. The samples represented a range of malignant subtypes, most commonly infiltrating ductal carcinoma, ductal carcinoma in situ, and invasive lobular carcinoma (each 20%), with additional cases of intraductal papillary carcinoma (15%), invasive micropapillary carcinoma (15%), and simple carcinoma (10%). Most tumors were histologic grade II or III. Overall, 16 of 20 (80%) tumors showed positive AKR1B1 staining, indicating that AKR1B1 overexpression is highly prevalent in malignant canine mammary tumors (Table 1). Because AKR1B1 expression was positive in 16 of 20 tumors, there were too few negative cases to robustly assess statistical associations between AKR1B1 IHC status and histologic grade or subtype in this tissue cohort. No clear pattern by grade or subtype was observed on descriptive review.

AKR1B1 promotes growth and proliferation of canine mammary tumor cells

Stable AKR1B1 overexpression and knockdown in CMT-U27 cells were first confirmed at the mRNA level by qRT-PCR. AKR1B1-overexpressing cells (pCDH-AKR1B1) showed a marked increase in AKR1B1 transcript abundance compared with empty vector control cells (pCDH) (Fig. 2A). In contrast, cells expressing AKR1B1-targeting shRNA (pG-shAKR1B1) exhibited significantly reduced AKR1B1 mRNA levels relative to the pGreenPuro control (pG) (Fig. 2B), confirming the successful establishment of overexpression and knockdown cell models.

We next assessed the functional role of AKR1B1 in canine mammary tumor cell growth using the CCK-8 assay over five days. As shown in Fig. 2C, AKR1B1 overexpression significantly enhanced proliferation compared with the pCDH control, with differences becoming evident at 72 h (p < 0.05) and highly significant by 120 h (p < 0.01). Conversely, silencing AKR1B1 with pG-shAKR1B1 markedly inhibited cell growth relative to the pG control (Fig. 2D; p < 0.05 on Day 4; p < 0.01 on Day 5).

AKR1B1 promotes migration of canine mammary tumor cells

To determine whether AKR1B1 affects the motility of canine mammary tumor cells, a wound-healing assay was performed in CMT-U27 cells with AKR1B1 overexpression or AKR1B1-targeted knockdown. Cells with AKR1B1 overexpression showed markedly faster wound closure at 24 h and 48 h compared with the corresponding vector control group, whereas AKR1B1-silenced cells exhibited significantly delayed closure (Fig. 3A). Quantitative analysis of wound area confirmed that AKR1B1 overexpression accelerated, and AKR1B1 knockdown slowed, wound closure over time.

AKR1B1 activates the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway in canine mammary tumor cells

After confirming that AKR1B1 enhances the growth and motility of CMT-U27 cells, we next explored downstream signaling mechanisms. Given the central role of the PI3K/AKT pathway in regulating tumor cell survival and proliferation, we examined the effects of AKR1B1 modulation on PI3K/AKT signaling and cell-cycle regulators.

Western blot analysis showed that AKR1B1 protein expression is increased in overexpression AKR1B1 cells and reduced in shAKR1B1 cells. AKR1B1 overexpression markedly increased PI3K protein levels and phosphorylated AKT (Thr308), while total AKT remained unchanged (Fig. 4A). Conversely, AKR1B1 knockdown reduced PI3K and p-AKT expression compared with the corresponding control cells. In parallel, the cell-cycle inhibitors p21 and p27 were strongly downregulated in AKR1B1-overexpressing cells and upregulated after AKR1B1 silencing, indicating suppression of cell-cycle arrest mechanisms. These findings suggest that AKR1B1 promotes cell-cycle progression through activation of the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway and inhibition of p21/p27-mediated checkpoints, likely contributing to a more pro-survival, pro-proliferative intracellular environment. Given the established role of AKR1B1 in redox regulation and reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation in other tumor types, these results are also consistent with a model in which AKR1B1 indirectly activates PI3K/AKT signaling through redox-dependent mechanisms. Discussion

This study demonstrates that AKR1B1 overexpression in canine mammary tumor (CMT) cells is associated with activation of the PI3K/AKT signaling cascade, concomitant downregulation of P21 and P27, and acquisition of enhanced proliferative and migration. In addition, elevated serum AKR1B1 concentrations in tumor-bearing canines highlight the enzyme’s potential as a circulating biomarker for tumor presence and activity. By linking metabolic enzyme dysregulation with oncogenic signaling and secreted biomarker expression, our findings provide a cohesive mechanistic and translational framework for understanding how metabolic perturbations contribute to mammary tumorigenesis in canines, which are increasingly recognized as a robust comparative model for human breast cancer22–24.

AKR1B1 belongs to the aldo-keto reductase superfamily, traditionally characterised by detoxification and carbonyl metabolism. Increasingly, AKR1B1 has been recognised as a driver of oncogenic signaling in human cancers through reactive oxygen species (ROS)-induced activation of the PI3K/AKT, NF-κB, and epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) pathways25–27. The PI3K/AKT signaling axis is a central regulator of cell survival, metabolism, and proliferation, acting through phosphorylation of downstream transcription factors and modulation of cell-cycle checkpoints28,29. Including phosphorylated AKT (p-AKT), phosphorylated mTOR (p-mTOR), and PTEN loss, are associated with malignant transformation, high histologic grade, and unfavourable prognosis in canine mammary tumors30–32. The present findings extend this paradigm to spontaneous mammary tumors in canines, showing that AKR1B1 overexpression coincides with elevated PI3K and phosphorylated AKT, reduced cell-cycle inhibitor expression, and augmented migration. These results align with human studies demonstrating that AKR1B1 knockdown suppresses AKT/mTOR signaling, reduces tumor growth, and diminishes metastatic potential in gastric, hepatic, and basal-like breast cancers12,25. AKR1B1 as a specific upstream metabolic enzyme capable of amplifying this oncogenic axis. Integrating redox metabolism with classical signal-transduction frameworks suggests that metabolic adaptation may not merely follow oncogenic signaling but can also act as a primary driver sustaining PI3K/AKT activation and tumor progression.

The elevation of serum AKR1B1 in tumor-bearing canines provides a clinically relevant extension of the tissue-level findings. Non-invasive biomarkers for canine mammary tumors remain limited, despite the fact that these neoplasms account for a substantial proportion of cancers in intact female canines. In our cohort, serum AKR1B1 showed good diagnostic performance (AUC ≈ 0.84, sensitivity ≈ 74%, specificity ≈ 84%), which is broadly comparable to early-stage evaluations of other candidate serum biomarkers such as CA 15 − 3, CEA and related panels in CMT33–35. In addition, Serum AKR1B1 concentrations were significantly higher in dogs with metastatic disease than in those without metastasis, and also increased with histologic grade, with grade II and grade III tumors showing significantly higher values than grade I tumors. Nevertheless, we cannot exclude the possibility that concurrent conditions such as subclinical inflammatory, metabolic, or endocrine diseases may influence circulating AKR1B1 levels. Although control dogs were clinically healthy and free of overt neoplastic disease, detailed screening for all potential comorbidities was not performed, and some degree of residual confounding is therefore possible.

Future work should assess temporal dynamics, for example by comparing pre- and post-surgical levels or by performing longitudinal sampling during adjuvant chemotherapy, to determine whether serum AKR1B1 can serve as a surrogate marker for treatment response or recurrence. In addition, evaluating AKR1B1 in combination with established or exploratory biomarkers such as CA 15 − 3, CEA and inflammatory markers, building on prior CMT biomarker studies19–21,36,37 may further improve diagnostic performance and facilitate clinical implementation in veterinary practice.

Additionally, several limitations need to be taken into consideration. Despite the fact that AKR1B1 overexpression was shown to be connected with PI3K/AKT activation and cell-cycle dysregulation, the particular molecular mechanism has not yet been determined. An investigation employing co-immunoprecipitation, mass spectrometry, or metabolomic profiling should be carried out in order to determine if AKR1B1 physically interacts with the components of the pathway or whether it effects them indirectly through redox-mediated intermediates. In the second place, the evaluation of the serum biomarker was cross-sectional; the absence of longitudinal or post-operative data restricts the ability to draw conclusions on temporal changes or the significance of prognostic factors. In the third place, although dogs in the control group were deemed clinically healthy, we did not systematically exclude all inflammatory or metabolic disorders, and these unmeasured comorbidities may have contributed to variability in serum AKR1B1. Fourth, functional experiments were carried out in a single cell line, which was designated as CMT-U27. Despite the fact that canine mammary carcinomas are representative, there is a significant amount of inter-tumor heterogeneity among them38. To validate that the results are reproducible, it would be necessary to repeat the tests using additional cell lines or original tumor cultures. Lastly, the sample size is still rather small, consisting of 20 paired tumor-normal tissues and 50 CMT cases compared with 56 controls. This limits both the statistical power and the external validity of the study. In order to properly validate these findings, it is necessary to do multicenter studies that include a wide range of breeds, hormonal backgrounds, and tumor subtypes. In conclusion, the prognostic implications of AKR1B1 overexpression could not be determined since there was a lack of data on follow-up and survival.

In spite of these limitations, the current work offers mechanistic and translational insights that may be used to warrant further investigation of AKR1B1 in CMT and other veterinary cancers.

Conclusion

This study identifies AKR1B1 as a central metabolic-oncogenic mediator in canine mammary tumors, integrating tissue-level signaling changes with functional and clinical relevance. We show that AKR1B1 overexpression activates PI3K/AKT signaling, suppresses the cell-cycle regulators P21 and P27, and enhances proliferation, migration in CMT-U27 cells. Consistent with these functional effects, serum AKR1B1 was significantly elevated in tumor-bearing canines and demonstrated good diagnostic performance, highlighting its potential utility as a minimally invasive biomarker for disease detection or monitoring.

Chitosan nanoparticles as a strategy for enhancing h6h gene expression and tropane alkaloid production in Hyoscyamus reticulatus hairy roots
Authors: Raheleh Godsi, Bahman Hosseini, Ahad Hedayati
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Hyoscyamus reticulatus L. is a rich source of tropane alkaloids (TA) which is widely used in medicine. In this study, hairy roots were obtained from two-week cotyledon explants of H. reticulatus L. by using the A7 strain of Agrobacterium rhizogenes. The effects of different concentrations of chitosan nanoparticles (CNP) (0, 10, 30, 50 and 100 mg L− 1) as a signaling molecule with two exposure times (24 and 48 h) on the growth rate, antioxidant enzymes activity, phenol content, TA contents and hyoscyamine-6-beta-hydroxylase (h6h) gene expression levels were investigated. After extraction of TA from hairy roots, High-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) was used to measure the amount of hyoscyamine and scopolamine. The results showed that the highest amount of hyoscyamine (1300.3 µg g− 1 FW) and scopolamine (918.06 µg g− 1 FW) accumulated in hairy roots treated with 100 mg L− 1 CNP at 48 h and 10 mg L− 1 CNP at 24 h, respectively. In the present study, the effect of CNP on the expression of the h6h gene was also investigated using semi-quantitative RT-PCR. The highest expression of this gene was observed at a concentration of 10 mg L− 1 in 24 h and the lowest was observed in the control sample. Based on these results, it is concluded that CNP can be used as an effective stimulant for the production of plant secondary metabolites, including TA, probably due to the stimulation of gene expression effective in the production of TA.

Introduction

H. reticulatus L., commonly known as henbane, is a significant species in the Solanaceae family, native to the arid and semi-arid regions of Egypt, Southwest Asia, Iran, and Turkey. It is recognized for its medicinal properties and chemical compositions. This plant contains a variety of alkaloids, mainly hyoscyamine and scopolamine, which is widely used in traditional medicine and pharmaceuticals due to their Sedative and anti-spasmodic, effects1. Biotechnology serves as a modern tool in producing plant secondary metabolites, playing an essential role in improving the quality and quantity of these compounds. Secondary metabolites are naturally produced compounds by plants, known for their medicinal resources, fragrances, colors, and flavorings2. In recent years, significant advancements have been made in the biosynthesis of secondary metabolites, particularly using biotechnological methods. These advancements include improved cell culture techniques, genetic engineering, and the use of elicitors, which have all contributed to increased and optimized production of these compounds. The application of biotechnological methods in producing secondary metabolites offers considerable advantages over the traditional cultivation of medicinal plants3. These benefits include increased yields, precise control over production conditions, reduced dependence on natural resources, and the ability of genetic engineering to create new compounds. Therefore, biotechnology has garnered attention as a sustainable and efficient solution in the pharmaceutical and agricultural industries4. The roots of certain plant families are the sites of biosynthesis or accumulation of secondary metabolites such as alkaloids, polyacetylenes, sesquiterpenes, phenols, and flavonoids. These compounds can also be produced in the hairy roots of these plants5. A. rhizogenes is a rod-shaped, gram-negative, aerobic, and motile bacterium found in the soil, belonging to the Rizobiaceae family. It transfers a part of its genome known as T-DNA into the plant’s genome, leading to the formation of structures called hairy roots in the plant. Infection of the plant by A. rhizogenes causes the formation of numerous roots in the infected area and produces opines as a food source for the bacterium6.

A large number of plant species, including many medicinal plants, have been genetically modified using A. rhizogenes, and published results indicate their ability to produce valuable medicinal metabolites through these roots. Hairy roots are recognized as a promising tool in biotechnology for producing secondary metabolites invaluable medicinal plants and have a high potential for developing industrial applications7. With characteristics such as genetic stability, rapid growth, no need for hormones, the ability to produce specific metabolites and the capability for large-scale cultivation, hairy roots are regarded as a sustainable source for producing secondary metabolites that hold great potential for industrial applications. As technology and research in this field advance, more applications for hairy roots are expected to be developed, playing a more significant role in meeting medicinal needs8.

Elicitors are compounds that can enhance the production of secondary metabolites in plants by triggering defense responses. These compounds are mainly divided into two categories: biotic elicitors (like chitin and chitosan) and abiotic elicitors (like jasmonic acid and salicylic acid)9. The use of elicitors in hairy root cultures has become an effective method for promoting secondary metabolite production for several reasons. Elicitors can act as biological stimulators and increase the production and accumulation of secondary metabolites by activating the plant’s defense mechanisms. Utilizing elicitors in the production of secondary metabolites not only helps increase the yield and concentration of these compounds but also enables better responses to environmental stresses and reduces production time. These features have made elicitors a key tool in plant biotechnology, showing high potential for developing medicinal products10,11.

Chitosan, with the chemical formula (C6H11NO4), is a polysaccharide that is abundant in nature, derived from shells and other marine crustaceans, such as Pandalus borealis, and the cell walls of fungi12. Chitosan is an affordable, biodegradable, non-toxic substance that promotes plant growth. Since nanoparticles can stimulate defense mechanisms in cells and produce secondary metabolites, they have recently been used as stimulants in culture environments13. Due to its unique properties, CNP, a natural elicitor, is important in producing secondary metabolites. By stimulating the biosynthesis of these compounds and increasing their concentration, CNP can help develop effective pharmaceutical products and also protect plants against environmental stress. These features have drawn the attention of researchers and industrialists, highlighting its high potential for future applications14. The application of stimulants and CNP for increasing the production of secondary metabolites has been reported in previous studies. Examples include the increased levels of total phenols, flavonoids, and anthocyanins in Rosa hybrida cv. Black magic15, the increase of enzymatic and non-enzymatic metabolites in Dracocephalum kotschyi16, the advancement of pancratistatin and lycorine in Pancratium maritimum17, and the enhancement of artemisinin in Artemisia annua18. In Hyoscyamus, the enzyme hyoscyamine 6β-hydroxylase (H6H) plays a critical role in the biosynthesis of tropane alkaloids. Specifically, H6H catalyzes the conversion of hyoscyamine to scopolamine, a more valuable compound for pharmaceutical applications. This enzyme is crucial for producing scopolamine, which is used in various medicinal applications.

With the increased consumption of plant-derived medicines in society, pharmaceutical companies have also widely turned to producing plant-based medicines and health products. Various methods, including hairy root culture and the use of biotic and abiotic stimulants, are employed to enhance the production of secondary metabolites. Therefore, this research investigated the effect of CNP as a biotic stimulant at different concentrations and treatment durations on the production of TA hyoscyamine and scopolamine, as well as the expression of the h6h gene.

Methods

Seed and bacteria preparation and cultivation

The seeds of the H. reticulatus plant were obtained from Pakane Bazar company-in Isfahan. To break the physiological dormancy of the seeds, a gibberellin solution at a concentration of 500 mg /L-1 was used. After sterilization, the seeds were planted in the MS base culture medium19. The A7 strain of A. rhizogenes was sourced from the microbial bank of the National Research Institute of Genetic Engineering and Biotechnology in Tehran, Iran. For the cultivation of A. rhizogenes, solid Luria-Bertani (LB) culture medium was used, and for preparing the bacterial suspension for inoculation, liquid LB culture medium was utilized.

Explant inoculation with A. rhizogenes

To inoculate the explant, two weeks after germination, the cotyledons were separated from the sterile mother seedlings under a laminar hood and placed in a sterile petri dish. To facilitate better bacteria penetration into the explant, wounds were created on the underside of the samples using a sterile scalpel. The prepared bacterial suspension was added into a separate petri dish, and the cotyledons were immersed in the suspension for 3 min. Then, the inoculated cotyledons were transferred to solid MS culture medium and kept in an incubator at 25 degrees Celsius in the dark for 48 h.

Bacteria elimination of and hairy root growth

After 2 days, each of the explants was surrounded by a halo of bacteria. At this stage, to eliminate bacteria, the explants were washed with sterile distilled water containing the antibiotic cefotaxime at a concentration of 300 mg L− 1. After removing the excess moisture, they were transferred to a hormone-free solid MS medium containing 100 mg L− 1 of cefotaxime. The washing of the explants with sterile distilled water containing cefotaxime continued for up to three rounds. 16 days after inoculating the tissue samples with A. rhizogenes, the hairy roots began to appear gradually. The culture medium containing the hairy roots was placed in a growth chamber at a temperature of 25 degrees Celsius during the day and 22 degrees Celsius at night, under dark conditions (Fig. 1).

To confirm the presence of the rol B gene in the hairy root genome, genomic DNA from transgenic hairy roots and roots of in vitro H. reticulatus seedlings was extracted using the Cetyltrimethylammonium bromide (CTAB) method20. Then, the role B gene primers were used to amplify 780 bp fragments by PCR.

CNP Preparation and hairy roots treatment

CNP used in this study were obtained from Taiwan Kaohsiung Applied Chemistry. Various concentrations of the chitosan nanoparticles (0, 10, 30, 50, 100 mg L− 1) were prepared for hairy root treatments. 0.5 g of H. reticulatus hairy roots was transferred to 250 mL Erlenmeyer flasks containing 20 mL of liquid MS culture medium with three replicates. On the 21st day, the liquid MS culture media containing different concentrations of chitosan nanoparticles was added to Erlenmeyer flasks. After the specified times (24 and 48 h), the hairy roots were transferred to the MS culture medium lacking elicitor. Then, one week later, the hairy roots were harvested, and washed with distilled water, and the fresh weight was measured after removing excess moisture. Also, traits such as total phenol and flavonoid content, oxidative enzyme activity, and TA content were evaluated.

Methanolic extraction and total phenol content (TPC), total flavonoid content (TFC), and antioxidant capacity measurements

This test was performed based on the method of Hajimahdipour et al.21 with slight modifications. First, 0.5 g of the hairy root was placed in liquid nitrogen (LN) in a mortar, and then 20 mL of 80% methanol was added and thoroughly crushed. It was then centrifuged for 15 min at 10,000 revolutions at 4 degrees. After that, 150 µL of the extract were mixed with 1200 µL of 10% Folin reagent in clean test tubes and after waiting for 5 min, 960 µL of 7.5% sodium carbonate was added, followed by 180 µL of distilled water. The test tubes were then kept in the dark for 30–40 min and were finally read at a wavelength of 760 nm using a spectrophotometer. Deionized water was used as a control and gallic acid as a standard. Finally, the absorbance value (Y) was inserted in the equation obtained by calibration, and TPC values (X) was calculated in mg GA g− 1 FW.

Plant extract Preparation for enzyme activity measurement

0.2 g of the hairy roots were weighed, and 2 cc of Tris HCL buffer (50 mM) with pH = 8 was added. The mixture was then ground on ice for 20 min until a completely homogeneous extract was obtained. The resulting extract was centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 20 min at a temperature of 4 degrees Celsius and was used to measure the activity of antioxidant enzymes24.

Catalase (CAT) activity measurement

First, a 50 mM phosphate buffer was prepared at pH 7, then 2.5 mL of it, and 20 µL of 3% hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) were taken for each sample. These substances were mixed together, and then 40 µL of enzyme extract were added, with changes in absorbance at a wavelength of 240 nm being monitored using a spectrophotometer for 1 min. The enzyme activity was calculated based on the changes in absorbance units per minute for each gram of fresh weight (U g− 1 FW)25.

Guaiacol peroxidase (GPX) activity measurement

The activity of the enzyme guaiacol peroxidase was measured using guaiacol as a substrate and based on the method by Zhang et al.26. The reaction mixture contained 10 µL of enzyme extract, 3000 µL of phosphate buffer (50 mM, pH = 7), 50 µl of 3% H2O2, and 50 µL of pure guaiacol. The increase in absorbance due to the oxidation of guaiacol was measured at a wavelength of 436 nm over 1 min. Then, the enzyme activity was calculated based on changes in absorbance units per minute for each gram of fresh weight (U g− 1 FW).

Ascorbate peroxidase (APX) activity measurement

2 µL of phosphate buffer (50 mM, pH = 7), 20 µL of 3% H2O2, and 10 µL of 50 µM ascorbate were mixed in an ice bath, and then 30 µL of the enzymatic extract was added. The absorbance changes were immediately read at a wavelength of 290 nm. The enzyme activity content was calculated based on changes in absorbance units per minute for each gram of fresh weight (U g− 1 FW)27.

Extraction and analysis of TA using high performance liquid chromatography (HPLC)

The extraction of tropane alkaloids was performed by the method of Kamada et al.28 with slight modifications. For this purpose, 50 mg of each dried hairy root sample was extracted with methanol-chloroform and 25% ammonia (5-15-1) solvents and ultrasonicated for 10 min. After filtering, a rotary evaporator was used to remove the chloroform phase. Then, the obtained extract was dissolved in 2 ml of sulfuric acid (1 N) and 5 ml of chloroform. After decantation, the chloroform phase was separated and the pH of the remaining phase was adjusted to 10 using ammonia. The alkaline solution was dissolved in 2 ml of chloroform and evaporated at 40 °C. 1 ml of methanol HPLC was added to the remaining dry extract and stored in the refrigerator for HPLC analysis of TA compounds.

To measure the content of TA (hyoscyamine and scopolamine) in extracts obtained from hairy root, a high-performance liquid chromatography system, Agilent 1100 - USA, with a UV detector at a wavelength of 215 nanometers was used. The column used was C-18 (250*4 nm). The solvent flow rate was 1 mL per minute, and the mobile phase consisted of acetonitrile and acetate buffer in a ratio of 80:20. The amount of extract used for each injection was 10 µL, and the quantities of the two alkaloids, hyoscyamine, and scopolamine, were calculated based on the area under the curve obtained using a standard curve. The standard curve was also drawn based on the area under the curve for the two compounds, hyoscyamine and scopolamine (Sigma products), at concentrations of 5, 10, 20, 30, 40, and 50 mg per mL (Fig. 2).

RNA extraction and h6h gene expression analysis using semi-quantitative reverse transcriptase-PCR (RT-PCR)

RNA extraction from hairy root samples was performed using the RNX-plus™ extraction solution (Sinnaclon, Iran) according to the manufacturer’s recommended protocol with slight modifications. 400 ng of extracted RNA was used to synthesize cDNA using the cDNA Synthesis Kit (Sinnaclon, Iran) according to the manufacturer’s recommended method. The synthesized cDNA was treated with RNAase and diluted 1:50 with sterile distilled water. To investigate the expression level of the h6h (hyoscyamine-6β-hydroxylase) gene, a semi-quantitative RT-PCR technique was used, with the forward primer (GAATTCGAACCTT ACCCATCCA) and reverse primer (CTTAGTAGACCAGTTCGACACAA) of the h6h gene as the main gene and the forward primer (ATGATAACTCGACGGATCGC) and reverse primer (CTTGGATGTGTGTGGTAGCCGTTT) of the 18 S rRNA (18 S ribosomal RNA) gene as the control gene. Pixcavator 6.0 software was used to analyze the results of h6h gene expression changes. The basic principle of this software is that after gel scanning, the intensity of the main gene bands in each sample is calculated based on the intensity of the internal control gene bands (18 S rRNA) of the corresponding sample, normalized, and the corrected relative density for each sample is calculated and expressed as a number.

Statistical analysis

The experiments in this study were conducted as factorial in a completely randomized design with three replications. The resulting data were analyzed using SAS 9.2 software, and means were compared using Duncan’s multiple range test. Excel software was also used to draw graphs.

Results and discussion

Molecular confirmation of the Transgenic characteristics of hairy roots

The transgenic nature of hairy roots can be investigated by tracing the genes present on the T-DNA of the bacterium A. rhizogenes, the most important of which are the rolA, rolB and rolC genes. As expected, in this study, the hairy roots produced fragments measuring 780 bp in a polymerase chain reaction using a specific primer for the rolB gene, indicating the presence of this sequence and consequently the transfer of the T-DNA sequence into the plant cell genome. This fragment was the same size as the amplified fragment in the positive control sample (the Agrobacterium used for inoculation). This fragment was not seen in the electrophoresis profile of non-transgenic roots, suggesting the absence of these genes in the control plants. Additionally, a no-template reaction was performed to check for contamination in the reaction components. In this reaction, all components for amplifying the target fragment were used, but instead of genomic DNA, nuclease-free water was employed. Since nuclease-free water was used instead of genomic DNA, no band was observed at the amplification stage of the target gene, indicating the absence of DNA contamination in the reaction components (Fig. 3).

CNP effects on hairy roots growth and biomass production

Analysis of variance results showed that the interaction effects of time and different concentrations of CNP had no significant impact on the fresh and dry weights of the hairy roots. Still, the simple effects of treatment duration and concentration on root growth were substantial (Table 1). The highest fresh weight (3.77 g) was observed in the control treatment, while the lowest fresh weight (2.61 g) was noted at the concentration of 30 mg L− 1. Fresh weight of the hairy roots decreased until the concentration of 30 mg L− 1 of CNP, after which it increased. In the comparison of the two treatment times, the fresh weight increased during the 24 h treatment compared to the 48 h treatment. The highest dry weight (0.37 g) was observed in the control treatment and the lowest dry weight (0.26 g) was observed in the 30 mg L− 1 treatment of CNP.

In many plants, adding stimulants is one of the important methods to reduce the harmful effects of abiotic stresses and increase their yield and quality29. As observed in the results of the present study, a significant difference in growth index was observed at high concentrations of CNP. At high concentrations of this compound, the rate of root growth increased compared to low concentrations. It can be stated that increasing the concentration of CNP, considering the high relative water content shows the ability of plants to regulate osmosis and maintain their growth. Chitosan may induce the signal for the synthesis of plant hormones such as gibberellin and increase plant growth and development by some signaling pathways related to auxin biosynthesis, through the tryptophan-dependent pathway30. The results of the study by Singla et al.31 showed that the application of different concentrations of chitosan in Cicer arietinum increased stem and root dry weight.

Effect of CNP on TPC and TFC values and antioxidant capacity

The results of data analysis showed that the highest total phenol content was observed at a concentration of 50 mg L− 1 in 48 h (12.84 mg gallic acid equivalent (GA g− 1 FW) and the lowest total phenol content was observed at a concentration of 10 mg L− 1 in 24 h (3.20 mg GA g− 1 FW). Based on the results, an increasing trend in total phenol content was observed up to a concentration of 50 mg L− 1 with increasing treatment period and stimulus concentration (Fig. 4a). Increasing the concentration of CNP to 100 mg L− 1 increased the total flavonoid content. The highest increase in total flavonoid content was observed at 100 mg L− 1 for 48 h with 1.2 mg QUE g− 1 FW, and the lowest total flavonoid content was observed at 10 mg L− 1 for 24 h (0.27 mg QUE g− 1 FW). The flavonoid content showed an upward trend with increasing CNP concentration and treatment time (Fig. 4b). Analysis of variance of the data obtained from the interaction effects of stimulus concentration and duration of treatment with CNP on the antioxidant capacity by DPPH method showed that there was no significant difference between the treatments, but the simple effect of stimulus concentration showed a significant effect at the 1% probability level (Table 1). With increasing CNP concentration, an increasing trend in antioxidant capacity was observed. The highest antioxidant capacity (15.170) was related to the concentration of 100 mg L− 1 at 48 h and the lowest (6.01) was related to the concentration of 10 mg L− 1 at 24 h. Phenolic compounds are a large group of phytochemicals in most plants. These compounds are strong antioxidants and free radical scavengers due to the presence of a hydroxyl group. Biotic elicitors such as CNP can significantly increase the production of secondary metabolites such as phenolic compounds and flavonoids. These compounds act as strong antioxidants and protect plants against environmental stresses. By stimulating specific biosynthetic pathways, biotic elicitors increase the expression of genes involved in the production of these compounds. These processes help improve the plant’s response to stresses and increase the production of antioxidants. The mechanism of action of elicitors in plants is not well understood, but it seems that biotic elicitors (such as chitosan) attack cell wall components and, in response to the stimulus message, the plant’s defense system is activated32,33. The effect of CNP on increasing phenolic and flavonoid compounds has also been reported in previous studies. In a study conducted by Jiao et al.34, adding 0.015% chitosan to Isatis tinctoria hairy root cultures resulted in a 5-fold increase in flavonoid compounds. In another study, the use of CNP in Oryza sativa cell suspension culture significantly increased the total phenolic and flavonoid content35.

Nanoparticles are an effective tool in increasing the antioxidant capacity of hairy roots. These effects depend on various mechanisms, including the type of nanoparticles and their concentration. The use of nanoparticles at appropriate concentrations significantly increases the production of antioxidant and phenolic compounds in hairy roots. Research results show that with increasing the concentration of these nanoparticles, the amount of antioxidant compounds also increases, but at much higher concentrations, negative effects are observed. In general, various nanoparticles can help improve the antioxidant capacity of hairy roots by affecting plant metabolism and stimulating the production of antioxidant compounds. Choosing the right type of nanoparticle and concentration is of great importance to achieve the best results36. CNP, as a biological elicitor, increase the antioxidant capacity of hairy roots by positively affecting the activity of antioxidant enzymes and the production of phenolic and flavonoid compounds. An increase in antioxidant capacity by the DPPH method has been reported due to the use of chitosan compared to the control in Isatis tinctoria hairy root cultures34.

Antioxidant enzyme activity

According to mean comparison results, the highest level of catalase enzyme activity was observed at 100 mg L− 1 concentration for 48 h (0.577 U g− 1 FW) and the lowest level was observed at 48 h and concentration of 10 mg L− 1 (0.244 U g− 1 FW). The highest level of ascorbate peroxidase enzyme was observed at 50 mg L− 1 concentration for 24 h (51.6 U g− 1 FW) and the lowest level was observed at 10 mg L− 1 concentration for 24 h (32.1 U g− 1 FW). The highest amount of guaiacol peroxidase enzyme activity was found at 48 h and 100 mg L− 1 concentration (13.67 U g− 1 FW) and the lowest activity was found at 10 mg L− 1 concentration at 24 h (1.60 U g− 1 FW) (Fig. 5). Various environmental stresses, including biotic and abiotic stresses, cause the accumulation of Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS) such as superoxide, hydrogen peroxide, and hydroxyl radicals in the cell, causing oxidative bursts and, as a result, damaging membrane lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids. Plants have an antioxidant defense system to combat ROS and, as a result, inhibit the production of ROS through the production of antioxidant enzymes. The enzymatic antioxidant system, including superoxide dismutase, peroxidase, catalase, glutathione reductase, and ascorbate peroxidase, is responsible for protecting against the toxic effects of ROS. Biotic elicitors can significantly enhance the activity of antioxidant enzymes in plants. These elicitors, which are usually obtained from natural sources such as microorganisms or plant compounds, stimulate the plants’ defense systems, leading to increased production of antioxidant enzymes. Elicitors bind to specific receptors in plant cells, activating signaling pathways that lead to increased production of antioxidant enzymes. Elicitors can also increase the expression of genes related to the biosynthesis of antioxidant compounds37,38. Meta-analysis of chitosan-mediated effects on plant defense against oxidative stress revealed that chitosan application significantly increased the antioxidant enzyme activity (by 40.6%), antioxidant metabolites content (by 24.6%), defense enzyme activity (by 77.9%), defense-related genes expression (by 103.2%), phytohormones (by 26.9%), and osmotic regulators (by 23.2%) under stress conditions, which in turn notably reduced oxidative stress (by 32.2%), and increased plant biomass (by 28.1%) and yield (by 15.7%). Moreover, chitosan-mediated effects on the amelioration of oxidative stress depended on the properties and application methods of chitosan39.

The increase in antioxidant enzyme activity in the present study is probably due to the increase in reactive oxygen species, especially hydrogen peroxide, which is consistent with the results of Zhang et al.40 in Artemisia annua hairy roots culture, Jamshidi et al.41 in hazelnut (Corylus avellana) cell suspension culture, and Ghorbanpour et al.42 in Hyoscyamus cell cultures. The results of Hassanloo et al.43 in a study on the hairy roots of Silybum marianum showed that in the presence of low molecular weight chitosan, the activity of defense enzymes such as ascorbate peroxidase and guaiacol peroxidase increased. Falcon-Rodriguez et al.44 showed that chitosan increased the activity of three defense enzymes, peroxidase, phenylalanine ammonia lyase, and glucanase, in the Nicotiana tabacum plant.

CNP effects on the transcript levels of h6h gene

The results of comparing the mean interaction effects of concentration and treatment time with the elicitor showed that different levels of concentration and treatment time significantly affected the expression of h6h gene at the 1% probability level. The highest expression of h6h gene was observed after treatment of hairy roots with 10 mg L− 1 of CNP for 24 h, which increased its production 4-fold compared to control hairy roots. The lowest expression of h6h gene was observed in control hairy roots (Fig. 6). The decrease in h6h gene expression at higher concentrations is probably due to the toxic effect of the stimulant and the disruption of the upstream regulation of genes involved in the biosynthesis of secondary metabolites45.

Hyoscyamine and scopolamine biosynthesis starting with the formation of N-methyl-Δ1-pyrrolinium from the ornithine and arginine amino acids. Putrescine-N-methyltransferase (PMT) is participated in the deletion of putrescine from the polyamine reservoir and its conversion to N-methyl-putrescine, which is the early restrictive step in the tropane alkaloids production46. The tropinone reductase I (TR1) and tropinone reductase II (TR2) genes encode enzymes, which catalyze the stereospecific reduction in tropinone to produce tropine and pseudo-tropine, respectively. These enzymes are crucial for the bifurcation of TA biosynthesis, forming distinct alkaloid end products. In the last steps of the pathway, hyoscyamine 6 β-hydroxylase (H6H) hydroxylates hyoscamine to 6β-hydroxyhyoscyamine (anisodamine), which then undergoes epoxidation to scopolamine47. The accumulation of the H6H enzyme due to the increase in h6h gene expression begins the last step of the tropane alkaloid production pathway. Nanoparticles play an important role in the process of signal transduction and lead to the regulation of defense genes in plants. Studies conducted on the effects of nanoparticles on plants have shown that these stimuli affect nitrogen metabolism by increasing the activity of nitrate reductase and glutamate dehydrogenase, which increases growth and photosynthesis. Nanoparticles also increase nitrogen metabolism and stimulate precursor production, leading to increased protein levels and gene expression in plant cells48,49. Since stimulants increase non-classical hormones such as jasmonates, and jasmonates also increase the activity of nitrate reductase enzymes, it is inferred that biotic stimulants increase this enzyme, resulting in increased expression of h6h and pmt enzymes. The results of the present study were consistent with the results of research by Vakili et al.50 on the Atropa belladonna plant under in vitro conditions, Moharrami et al.51 on H. reticulatus, and Hedayati et al.52 on H. reticulatus and H. pussilus, that indicating an increase in h6h gene expression due to the use of nanoparticles. An increase in the expression of genes involved in the biosynthesis of flavonoid compounds has also been reported in Isatis tinctoria hairy roots treated with CNP34.

Effect of CNP on tropane alkaloid production

Analysis of variance (ANOVA) results showed that different concentration levels and exposure times of CNP significantly affected the amount of TA (scopolamine and hyoscyamine) in the hairy roots of H. reticulatus. HPLC chromatograms of hyoscyamine and scopolamine in standard solutions and H. reticulatus hairy roots elicited with CNP are shown in Fig. 7. The highest amount of scopolamine was associated with 10 mg L− 1 CNP at 24 h (1300.30 µg g− 1 DW) and the lowest amount was obtained at the control treatment (256.07 µg g− 1 DW). According to the results, the production of scopolamine decreased with increasing elicitor concentration. In the case of hyoscyamine, the highest amount was obtained for the 48 h and 100 mg L− 1 concentration of elicitor treatment (918.06 µg g− 1 DW) and the lowest amount was obtained for the control treatment (107.07 µg g− 1 DW) (Fig. 7). An upward trend in the amount of hyoscyamine was observed with increasing elicitor concentration. In general, the amount of scopolamine alkaloid increased 5-fold compared to the control, and an 8.5-fold increase was observed in the alkaloid hyoscyamine. In many cases, the natural yield of tropane alkaloids is too low for commercialization. Therefore, there is a need to increase the production of alkaloids for commercial exploitation. One of the successful strategies to increase the production of these compounds is the use of biotic elicitors such as chitosan nanoparticles. Various factors such as the type and concentration of the stimulus, the time of adding the stimulus to the culture medium and the duration of the treatment affect the production of secondary metabolites. However, what showed the greatest impact in the results of this study was the concentration of the stimulus used, which played a key role in increasing the production of tropane alkaloids. A stimulus can be considered a compound that increases the expression of genes involved in the biosynthesis pathway of a metabolite, and different stimuli may target different biosynthetic pathways53. Previous research has shown that nanoparticles can increase the activity of ornithine decarboxylase and arginine decarboxylase enzymes. These enzymes increase the initial compounds of the pathway (substrate) and the activity of the PMT enzyme, resulting in increased hyoscyamine production54. The highest amount of scopolamine was observed at a concentration of 10 mg L− 1 of the stimulant. Since the h6h gene is involved in the biosynthesis of tropane alkaloids in the conversion of hyoscyamine to scopolamine, and since the highest relative expression of this gene was at the concentration above, it can be concluded that increased expression of the h6h gene increased the conversion of hyoscyamine to scopolamine, and the production of this compound increased. Nanoparticles affect nitrogen metabolism by increasing the activity of nitrate reductase and glutamate dehydrogenase enzymes. Since TA have a nitrogen base, the use of nanoparticles increases the amount of nitrogen inside the organs, and subsequently, the content of tropane alkaloids also increases46. Correlation analysis of scopolamine accumulation with h6h gene expression under the influence of CNP showed that there is a significant relationship between scopolamine accumulation and h6h gene expression at the 1% probability level. The pattern of scopolamine accumulation under the influence of chitosan showed a similar trend to the gene expression trend. These results indicate that high h6h gene activity has a positive effect on the production of TA, especially scopolamine. This positive effect has also been observed in tissues of atropa55, hyoscyamus56, and tobacco57.

Conclusions

Biotic and abiotic elicitors can affect the metabolic pathways of secondary metabolite synthesis and increase their production rate. This study investigated the effect of different concentrations of CNP as a biological elicitor on the tropane alkaloids levels (hyoscyamine and scopolamine), as well as h6h gene expression in hairy roots of Hyoscyamus. The results of this study showed that using elicitors can significantly increase the production of tropane alkaloid compounds, which can be helpful in the large-scale production of these compounds. It was also found that by increasing the expression of genes involved in the biosynthesis pathway of TA, the production of these valuable compounds also increases.

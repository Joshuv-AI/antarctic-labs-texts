Q p Q_{p} -weighted zero-sum constants
Krishnendu Paul, Shameek Paul

Abstract
A sequence S = ( x 1 , … , x k ) S=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}) in ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} is called a ( Q p , 𝟏 ) (Q_{p},\mathbf{1}) -weighted zero-sum sequence if there exist a 1 , … , a k ∈ Q p a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\in Q_{p} such that a 1 ​ x 1 + ⋯ + a k ​ x k = 0 a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}x_{k}=0 and a 1 + ⋯ + a k = 0 a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}=0 . The constant E Q p , 𝟏 E_{Q_{p},\mathbf{1}} is defined to be the smallest positive integer k k such that every sequence of length k k in ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} has a ( Q p , 𝟏 ) (Q_{p},\mathbf{1}) -weighted zero-sum subsequence of length p p . We determine the constant E Q p , 𝟏 E_{Q_{p},\mathbf{1}} and the related constants C Q p , 𝟏 C_{Q_{p},\mathbf{1}} and D Q p , 𝟏 D_{Q_{p},\mathbf{1}} . We also study some ( Q p , B ) (Q_{p},B) -weighted zero-sum constants where B B is a subset of Q p Q_{p} .

Introduction

By a sequence
S
in a set
X
of length
k
, we mean an element of the set
X^{k}
. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq 2
we denote the ring
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
by
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. We let
A
and
B
be non-empty subsets of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}\setminus\{0\}
. A sequence
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is called an
A
-weighted zero-sum sequence
if there exist
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\in A
such that
a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}x_{k}=0
. A sequence
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is called an
(A,B)
-weighted zero-sum sequence
if there exist
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\in A
and
b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}\in B
such that
a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}x_{k}=0
and
b_{1}a_{1}+\cdots+b_{k}a_{k}=0
.

For
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
we denote the set
\{x\in\mathbb{Z}:a\leq x\leq b\}
by
[a,b]
. We let
|A|
denote the number of elements in a finite set
A
. We denote the subsets
\{0\}
and
\{1\}
of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
by
\mathbf{0}
and
\mathbf{1}
respectively. A sequence which is a
\mathbf{1}
-weighted zero-sum sequence is simply called a

.

We let
U(n)
denote the group of units of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. We reserve the letter
p
to denote an odd prime. We let
Q_{p}=\big\{x^{2}:x\in U(p)\big\}
and
N_{p}=U(p)\setminus Q_{p}
. Let
S=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
be a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
and let
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. We define the
translate
of
S
by

to be the sequence
S+x=(x_{1}+x,\ldots,x_{k}+x)
.

Observation 1.1
.

Suppose a sequence
S
in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is an
(A,\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum sequence. Then every translate of
S
is also an
(A,\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum sequence, since the following identity holds.

a_{1}(x_{1}+x)+\cdots+a_{k}(x_{k}+x)=a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}x_{k}+(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k})x.

Remark 1.2
.

If we want to show that a sequence
S
in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is an
(A,\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum sequence, then by Observation
1.1
we may assume that zero occurs the most number of times as a term of
S
.

We now define the constants which we study in this article.

Definition 1.3

The constant

of length

The constant

\mathbb{Z}_{n}
of length
k

(A,B)
-weighted zero-sum subsequence.

The constant

\mathbb{Z}_{n}
of length

has an
(A,B)
-weighted zero-sum subsequence of length

The constants

Determining the value of
E_{Q_{p},\mathbf{1}}

Remark 2.1
.

In this section, we will show that

E_{Q_{p},\mathbf{1}}=\begin{cases}p+2,&\text{if }p\neq 5;\\
9,&\text{if }p=5.\end{cases}

Theorem 2.2
.

We have
E_{Q_{p},\mathbf{1}}\in[p+2,p+4]
.

Proof.

Since every
(Q_{p},\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum sequence is a
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum sequence, we see that
E_{Q_{p}}\leq E_{Q_{p},\mathbf{1}}
. From Theorem 3 of
[
2
]
we see that
E_{Q_{p}}=p+2
. Hence, we get that
E_{Q_{p},\mathbf{1}}\geq p+2
.

Let
m=p+4
and let
S=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})
be a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. We show that
S
has a
(Q_{p},\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum subsequence of length
p
. Consider the following system of equations in the variables
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
over the field
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

a_{1}X_{1}^{2}\,+\,\cdots\,+\,a_{m}X_{m}^{2}=0~,~~~~X_{1}^{2}\,+\,\cdots\,+\,X_{m}^{2}=0~,~~~X_{1}^{p-1}\,+\,\cdots\,+\,X_{m}^{p-1}=0.

By the Chevalley-Warning Theorem
[
5
]
, this system has a non-trivial solution
(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})
. Let
J=\big\{i\in[1,m]:b_{i}\neq 0\big\}
.

Since
U(p)
is a group of order
p-1
and
m=p+4
, from the third equation it follows that
|J|=p
. Let
T
be the subsequence of
S
such that
a_{i}
is a term of
T
if and only if
i\in J
. From the first two equations, we see that
T
is a
(Q_{p},\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum subsequence of
S
having length
|J|
. Hence, it follows that
E_{Q_{p},\mathbf{1}}\leq p+4
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

A sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{5}
having length five is a
(Q_{5},\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum sequence if and only if it is a
(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum sequence.

Proof.

Let
T=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{5})
be a
(Q_{5},\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{5}
. There exist
a_{1},\ldots,a_{5}\in Q_{5}
such that
a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{5}x_{5}=0
and
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{5}=0
. Since
Q_{5}=\{1,-1\}
and
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{5}=0
, we see that the sequence
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{5})
is a constant sequence. It follows that
x_{1}+\cdots+x_{5}=0
. Hence, we see that
T
is a
(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum sequence. Also, every
(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{5}
is a
(Q_{5},\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum sequence.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

We have
E_{Q_{5},\mathbf{1}}=9
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.3
, it follows that
E_{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}}(5)=E_{Q_{5},\mathbf{1}}
, and from
[
7
]
we see that
E_{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}}(5)=9
.

Lemma 2.5

Let
p\geq 7
and
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in U(p)

x_{1}\,Q_{p}+x_{2}\,Q_{p}+x_{3}\,Q_{p}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
p\geq 7
and let
S
be a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
which has at least three non-zero terms. Then

is a

-weighted zero-sum sequence.

Proof.

Let

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
p\geq 7
and let
T=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
be a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. If there exist
c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}\in Q_{p}
such that
c_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+c_{k}x_{k}=0

Let

. Since

, by Corollary
2.6
the sequence
(c,1,1)
is a

-weighted zero-sum sequence. Thus, there exist

such that
d\,c+d_{2}+d_{3}=0
. Since
d\,(c_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+c_{k}x_{k})+d_{2}\,0+d_{3}\,0=0
, it follows that
T+(0,0)
is a
(Q_{p},\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum sequence.
∎

Lemma 2.8

Let

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that
|A|\geq 2
and let

A\hat{+}A=\{a+b:a,b\in A\mbox{ and }a\neq b\}.

Then either

or

.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and let
z\in Q_{p}\hat{+}Q_{p}
. Suppose
x
and
x^{\prime}
are distinct elements of
Q_{p}
. Then there exist
c,c^{\prime}\in Q_{p}
such that
cx+c^{\prime}x^{\prime}=z
and
c+c^{\prime}\neq t
.

Proof.

Since
z\in Q_{p}\hat{+}Q_{p}
, there exist
u,u^{\prime}\in Q_{p}
such that
z=u+u^{\prime}
and
u\neq u^{\prime}
. Let
a=u/x
,
a^{\prime}=u^{\prime}/x^{\prime}
,
b=u^{\prime}/x
, and
b^{\prime}=u/x^{\prime}
. We see that
a,a^{\prime},b,b^{\prime}\in Q_{p}
. Also, we have

ax+a^{\prime}x^{\prime}=z=bx+b^{\prime}x^{\prime}.

(2)

So we get that

(a-b)x=(b^{\prime}-a^{\prime})x^{\prime}.

We see that
a-b=(u-u^{\prime})/x\neq 0
. So if
a-b=b^{\prime}-a^{\prime}
, we get the contradiction that
x=x^{\prime}
. Hence, it follows that
a-b\neq b^{\prime}-a^{\prime}
and so
a+a^{\prime}\neq b+b^{\prime}
. Thus, by (
2
) there exist
c,c^{\prime}\in Q_{p}
such that
cx+c^{\prime}x^{\prime}=z
and
c+c^{\prime}\neq t
.
∎

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
p\geq 11
, let
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, and let
z\in U(p)
. Suppose
x
and
x^{\prime}
are distinct elements which belong to the same coset of
Q_{p}
. Then there exist
c,c^{\prime}\in Q_{p}
such that
cx+c^{\prime}x^{\prime}=z
and
c+c^{\prime}\neq t
.

Proof.

Suppose
x,x^{\prime}\in Q_{p}
. Since
|Q_{p}|=(p-1)/2
, by Theorem
2.9
we see that
|Q_{p}\hat{+}Q_{p}|\geq p-4
. Since
p\geq 11
, we see that
|Q_{p}|\geq 5
.

So it follows that
Q_{p}\cap(Q_{p}\hat{+}Q_{p})\neq\emptyset
and hence there exists
y\in Q_{p}
such that
y\in Q_{p}\hat{+}Q_{p}
. Since
y\in Q_{p}\hat{+}Q_{p}
, we observe that
y\,Q_{p}\subseteq Q_{p}\hat{+}Q_{p}
. Also, since
y\in Q_{p}
, we have
y\,Q_{p}=Q_{p}
. Thus, we see that
Q_{p}\subseteq Q_{p}\hat{+}Q_{p}
.

Since
|N_{p}|=|Q_{p}|
, we see that
N_{p}\cap(Q_{p}\hat{+}Q_{p})\neq\emptyset
. So there exists
y^{\prime}\in N_{p}
such that
y^{\prime}\in Q_{p}\hat{+}Q_{p}
. Since
y^{\prime}\in Q_{p}\hat{+}Q_{p}
, we have that
y^{\prime}\,Q_{p}\subseteq Q_{p}\hat{+}Q_{p}
. Also, since
y^{\prime}\in N_{p}
, we have
y^{\prime}\,Q_{p}=N_{p}
. Thus, we see that
N_{p}\subseteq Q_{p}\hat{+}Q_{p}
.

It follows that
Q_{p}\cup N_{p}\subseteq Q_{p}\hat{+}Q_{p}
and hence
U(p)\subseteq Q_{p}\hat{+}Q_{p}
. Since
z\in U(p)
, it follows that
z\in Q_{p}\hat{+}Q_{p}
. By Lemma
2.10
there exist
c,c^{\prime}\in Q_{p}
such that
cx+c^{\prime}x^{\prime}=z
and
c+c^{\prime}\neq t
.

Suppose
x,x^{\prime}\in N_{p}
. Let
a\in N_{p}
. We observe that
ax,ax^{\prime}\in Q_{p}
and
az\neq 0
. From the previous case, there exist
c,c^{\prime}\in Q_{p}
such that
c(ax)+c^{\prime}(ax^{\prime})=az
and
c+c^{\prime}\neq t
. Since
cx+c^{\prime}x^{\prime}=z
, we are done.
∎

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
p\geq 11
and let
S=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
be a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
which has at least one non-zero term. Suppose
x
and
x^{\prime}
are distinct elements which belong to the same coset of
Q_{p}
. Then there exist
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n},c,c^{\prime}\in Q_{p}
such that

c_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+c_{n}x_{n}+cx+c^{\prime}x^{\prime}=0\mbox{ and }c_{1}+\cdots+c_{n}+c+c^{\prime}\neq 0.

Proof.

Since there exists
i\in[1,n]
such that
x_{i}\neq 0
, we see that

|x_{1}Q_{p}+\cdots+x_{n}Q_{p}|\geq|x_{i}Q_{p}|=|Q_{p}|\geq 5.

So there exists
z\in U(p)
such that
z\in x_{1}Q_{p}+\cdots+x_{n}Q_{p}
. Hence, there exist
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}\in Q_{p}
such that
z=c_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+c_{n}x_{n}
. Let
t=c_{1}+\cdots+c_{n}
. By Lemma
2.11
we see that there exist
c,c^{\prime}\in Q_{p}
such that
cx+c^{\prime}x^{\prime}=-z
and
c+c^{\prime}\neq-t
.
∎

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
S=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
be a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{7}
which has at least two non-zero terms. Suppose
x
and
x^{\prime}
are distinct elements which belong to the same coset of
Q_{7}
. Then there exist
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n},c,c^{\prime}\in Q_{7}
such that

c_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+c_{n}x_{n}+cx+c^{\prime}x^{\prime}=0\mbox{ and }c_{1}+\cdots+c_{n}+c+c^{\prime}\neq 0.

Proof.

Let
x,x^{\prime}\in Q_{7}
. Since
S
has at least two non-zero terms and
|Q_{7}|=3
, by the Cauchy-Davenport theorem (
[
4
]
), we see that
|x_{1}Q_{7}+\cdots+x_{n}Q_{7}|\geq 5
. Since
|x_{1}Q_{7}+\cdots+x_{n}Q_{7}|+|Q_{7}|>7
, we see that
(x_{1}Q_{7}+\cdots+x_{n}Q_{7})\cap Q_{7}\neq\emptyset
. Thus, there exists
z\in Q_{7}
such that
z\in x_{1}Q_{7}+\cdots+x_{n}Q_{7}
. Hence, there exist
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}\in Q_{7}
such that
z=c_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+c_{n}x_{n}
.

We observe that
Q_{7}\hat{+}Q_{7}=-Q_{7}
. Hence, we see that
-z\in Q_{7}\hat{+}Q_{7}
. Let
t=c_{1}+\cdots+c_{n}
. By Lemma
2.10
there exist
c,c^{\prime}\in Q_{7}
such that
cx+c^{\prime}x^{\prime}=-z
and
c+c^{\prime}\neq-t
.

Suppose
x,x^{\prime}\in N_{7}
. Let
a\in N_{7}
. We observe that
ax,ax^{\prime}\in Q_{7}
and that the sequence
(ax_{1},\ldots,ax_{n})
has at least two non-zero terms. Thus, from the previous case, there exist
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n},c,c^{\prime}\in Q_{7}
such that

c_{1}(ax_{1})+\cdots+c_{n}(ax_{n})+c(ax)+c^{\prime}(ax^{\prime})=0

and
c_{1}+\cdots+c_{n}+c+c^{\prime}\neq 0
. Since
c_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+c_{n}x_{n}+cx+c^{\prime}x^{\prime}=0
, we are done.
∎

Theorem 2.14
.

We have
E_{Q_{p},\mathbf{1}}=p+2
when
p\neq 5
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.2
we see that
E_{Q_{p},\mathbf{1}}\geq p+2
. Since
Q_{3}=\mathbf{1}
, by Theorem 2.2 of
[
7
]
we see that
E_{Q_{3},\mathbf{1}}=E_{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}}(3)=5
. So we may assume that
p\geq 7
.

Let
S
be a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
of length
p+2
. We will show that
S
has a
(Q_{p},\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum subsequence of length
p
. It will then follow that
E_{Q_{p},\mathbf{1}}=p+2
. Suppose
S
has at most two non-zero terms. Then
S
has at least
p
zeroes and hence we get a
(Q_{p},\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum subsequence of length
p
.

So we may assume that
S
has at least three non-zero terms. Since
S
has length
p+2
, it follows that
S
has a term which is repeated. By Remark
1.2
we may assume that zero occurs the most number of times as a term of
S
. It follows that
S
has at least two zeroes.

Suppose
S
has at least three zeroes. Let
T
be a subsequence of
S-(0,0,0)
of length
p-3
which has at least three non-zero terms. By Lemma
2.8
we see that
T+(0,0,0)
is a
(Q_{p},\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum sequence. Since
T+(0,0,0)
has length
p
, we are done.

So we may assume that
S
has exactly two zeroes. Let
T
be a subsequence of
S-(0,0)
of length
p-2
. Then all the terms of
T
are non-zero. Since
p\geq 7
, we see that
T
has at least five terms. It follows that at least three terms of
T
are in the same coset of
Q_{p}
. At least two of these three terms are distinct, since zero occurs the most number of times as a term of
S
.

Let
T=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
where

. By Lemmas
2.12
and
2.13
there exist
c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}\in Q_{p}
such that
c_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+c_{k}x_{k}=0
and
c_{1}+\cdots+c_{k}\neq 0
. Hence, by Lemma
2.7
we see that
T+(0,0)
is a
(Q_{p},\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum sequence.
∎

Lemma 3.1

Let

and let

. Let

be a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
which has at least two zeroes and at least three non-zero terms. Then

is a
(Q_{p},\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum sequence.

Let
T=S-(0,0)
. Suppose
T=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
. By Corollary

there exist
c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}\in Q_{p}
such that
c_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+c_{k}x_{k}=0
. Let
c=c_{1}+\cdots+c_{k}
. If
c\neq 0
, by Lemma
2.7
we see that
T+(0,0)
is a
(Q_{p},\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum sequence.

Suppose

. We see that
T
is a
(Q_{p},\mathbf{1})
-weighted zero-sum sequence. Since

, we see that
-1\in Q_{p}
. Thus, we see that

is a
(Q_{p},\mathbf{1})

is a

Corollary 3.3

6

When

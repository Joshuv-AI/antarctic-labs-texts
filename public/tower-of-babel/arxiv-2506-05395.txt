Mert Can Cakmak, Nitin Agarwal, Diwash Poudel

Abstract
. Efficient keyframe extraction is critical for effective video summarization and retrieval, yet capturing the complete richness of video content remains challenging. In this work, we present TriPSS, a novel tri-modal framework that effectively integrates perceptual cues from color features in the CIELAB space, deep structural embeddings derived from ResNet-50, and semantic context from frame-level captions generated by Llama-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct. By fusing these diverse modalities using principal component analysis, TriPSS constructs robust multi-modal embeddings that enable adaptive segmentation of video content via HDBSCAN clustering. A subsequent refinement stage incorporating quality assessment and duplicate filtering ensures that the final keyframe set is both concise and semantically rich. Comprehensive evaluations on benchmark datasets TVSum20 and SumMe demonstrate that TriPSS achieves state-of-the-art performance, substantially outperforming traditional unimodal and previous multi-modal methods. These results underscore TriPSS’s ability to capture nuanced visual and semantic information, thereby setting a new benchmark for video content understanding in large-scale retrieval scenarios.

2.
Related Work

Keyframe extraction has evolved from basic methods using color histograms and simple clustering
Sunuwar & Borah 2024
;
Asha et al. 2018
, which primarily relied on detecting abrupt visual changes but struggled with complex scenes and lacked semantic depth. To improve flexibility, adaptive clustering techniques
Zhao et al. 2019
;
Man & Sun 2022
were introduced, dynamically adjusting cluster boundaries; however, they remain limited by their dependence on unimodal features such as color histograms.

Color-based techniques emphasize perceptual uniformity
Asim et al. 2018
to ensure aesthetic coherence, yet they often neglect critical structural and semantic aspects of video content. Human-centered methods that incorporate cognitive signals
Bezugam et al. 2021
have shown promise, yet they suffer from scalability issues due to reliance on manual annotations. Image feature-based methods focus on structural properties like edges and textures
Di & Guerriero 2003
, capturing visual saliency without fully understanding the underlying semantic context.

The integration of large language models (LLMs) and deep learning has further advanced keyframe extraction. For instance, approaches such as those in
Park et al. 2024
and
Wang et al. 2024
focus on enhancing semantic relevance but tend to overlook low-level visual cues. Similarly, models like those proposed in
Huang & Wang 2019
,
Liang et al. 2024
, and
Tan et al. 2024a
improve semantic consistency, yet they often miss structural coherence or introduce redundancy. In essence, while these methods excel in semantic understanding, they struggle to provide a comprehensive visual representation.

Keyframe selection techniques also vary in their effectiveness. Histogram-based methods
Rodriguez et al. 2018
;
De et al. 2011
detect visual changes but miss finer semantic nuances. Structural approaches, such as edge-LBP
Nandini et al. 2022
, perform well in static scenes yet falter in dynamic content. Deep learning models
Tang et al. 2023
;
Tan et al. 2024
enhance temporal analysis but often fail to capture key semantic frames and lack cross-domain generalization. Traditional techniques like k-means
Muhammad et al. 2020
offer efficiency for fast-moving videos but remain inflexible.

TriPSS addresses these limitations by integrating perceptual, structural, and semantic representations into a unified framework. Unlike traditional methods that focus on isolated features, TriPSS simultaneously captures color information, deep structural cues, and nuanced semantic context, resulting in enhanced keyframe diversity and representativeness. This comprehensive fusion directly tackles the scalability and adaptability issues inherent in previous approaches, paving the way for more robust video summarization and retrieval.

3.
Methodology

In this section, we present a comprehensive multi-modal keyframe extraction pipeline that integrates perceptual color, structural image, and semantic content features through robust feature fusion, density-based clustering, and rigorous candidate keyframe refinement to generate a concise and representative video summary.

3.1.
Perceptual Color Feature Extraction

To accurately capture color differences that align with human perception, we convert video frames from the default RGB format to the CIELAB (Lab) color space, which has been widely recognized as the most perceptually uniform representation of color
Maji & Dingliana 2022
. Unlike HSV and other color spaces, CIELAB ensures that numerical differences in color correspond closely to human-perceived differences, making it the optimal choice for identifying meaningful variations in video frames. To extract discriminative color features, we compute normalized histograms and the first three statistical color moments from the L (lightness), a (green to red), and b (blue to yellow) channels. Specifically, the mean represents average intensity, the variance quantifies contrast, and the skewness captures asymmetry in color distribution, improving feature differentiation in image-based tasks
Kusumaningrum et al. 2014
. Additionally, we calculate a colorfulness metric based on the means and standard deviations of the a and b channels, effectively measuring color vibrancy in a way that approximates human perception. This carefully designed feature extraction process provides a robust foundation for clustering in keyframe selection, ensuring that extracted keyframes are not only statistically distinct but also visually meaningful, a crucial aspect for applications such as video summarization.

3.2.
Structural Image Feature Extraction

In the second phase of our feature extraction process, we used a convolutional neural network to generate robust embeddings from video frames. Specifically, we employed the ResNet-50 v1.5 architecture
He et al. 2016
, a model that has been extensively validated to deliver superior accuracy on ImageNet
Deng et al. 2009
and demonstrates exceptional adaptability across diverse applications
Wightman et al. 2021
;
Wilkerson et al. 2024
. ResNet-50 leverages residual learning with skip connections to address the vanishing gradient problem and effectively extract high-level features. Using pre-trained weights from the IMAGENET1K_V2 dataset, refined through extensive ImageNet training
Deng et al. 2009
, we enhanced the model’s ability to capture diverse visual concepts. For feature extraction, we removed the final classification layer and extracted 2048-dimensional feature vectors from the last convolutional layer. These structural features complemented the color-based features, creating a rich embedding space that supports effective keyframe extraction.

3.3.
Semantic Content Representation Feature Extraction

Semantic content representation was achieved using the Llama-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct model
Meta 2025
, known for its robust multimodal capabilities and strong performance in image understanding. To generate consistent textual descriptions of video frames, we used deterministic decoding by disabling sampling mechanisms, meaning that tokens were selected in a fixed order rather than randomly, to ensure stable and reproducible outputs critical for tasks like semantic clustering.
Zarrieß et al. 2021
. A concise prompt, “In one sentence, describe the visible content of this provided image,” guided the model to produce focused descriptions, reducing variability in style and length. In cases where the model failed to describe an image accurately, responses with predefined keywords were replaced with “No visible content” to maintain data uniformity. We evaluated caption quality on the COCO dataset
Lin et al. 2014
using CLAIR metric
Chan et al. 2023
, which focuses on semantic integrity and contextual relevance, and found that, although an alternative model, Phi-3-Vision-128k-Instruct
Microsoft 2025
scored slightly higher (0.72) than LLaMA’s score of 0.71. LLaMA was supported by qualitative analysis showing more detailed and semantically aligned captions, justifying its selection.

The generated descriptions were transformed into embeddings using the all-mpnet-base-v2 model from the SentenceTransformers library
Face 2025
, resulting in 768-dimensional representations that effectively capture nuanced semantic relationships. MPNet has shown superior performance on benchmarks such as GLUE
Wang et al. 2019
and SQuAD
Rajpurkar et al. 2016
, outperforming previous models in semantic understanding tasks
Song et al. 2020
. These embeddings support downstream applications like clustering, search, and summarization.

3.4.
Multi-Modal Feature Fusion

We created a unified representation of each frame by integrating perceptual (color), structural (image), and semantic (textual) features into a single embedding vector. Prior work has demonstrated the effectiveness of combining diverse modalities for richer representations
Cai et al. 2025
. To ensure equal contribution from each modality, we normalized the feature vectors using z-score standardization
Kammoun et al. 2024
. For each modality
m\in\{c,i,t\}
(color, image, text) with raw feature vectors
f_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{m}}
(
d_{c}=778
,
d_{i}=2048
,
d_{t}=768
), we computed a concatenated feature vector:

f=\hat{f}_{c}\oplus\hat{f}_{i}\oplus\hat{f}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{3594}.

This simple and effective strategy preserved the distinct contributions of each modality without introducing complex weighting schemes or thresholds that might inadvertently filter out useful information. It has demonstrated robust performance and computational efficiency in various applications
Liu et al. 2022
. While more sophisticated fusion methods exist, such as attention-based or transformer-based fusion, concatenation was computationally efficient and adequately met the requirements of our approach.

To reduce the high dimensionality of the fused vector, we tested different PCA dimensions (256, 512, 1024) and found that 512 best balanced accuracy and inference time. 3.5.
Adaptive Frame Clustering

To group frames into scene-like segments and select representative keyframes, we used HDBSCAN
Campello et al. 2013
, a density-based clustering method that adapts to varying scene densities without requiring a predefined number of clusters
Man & Sun 2022
. Unlike DBSCAN
Ester et al. 1996
, which struggles with varying densities, HDBSCAN handles diverse content effectively. Compared to algorithms like K-Means or Gaussian Mixture Models, HDBSCAN requires no prior knowledge of cluster numbers and accommodates irregular cluster shapes. It also filters out transitional or low-quality frames by assigning them to a noise category, enhancing keyframe quality. For these reasons, HDBSCAN was selected as the most suitable clustering algorithm for keyframe extraction among other clustering methods.

We evaluated cluster quality using the Density-Based Clustering Validation (DBCV) index
Moulavi et al. 2014
, which measures both cluster cohesion and separation based on local density variations. DBCV considers core distances, mutual-reachability distances, and connectivity within clusters, providing a robust assessment for density-based methods. To optimize HDBSCAN parameters, we performed a grid search and selected the configuration that maximized the DBCV score while balancing cluster separation, meaningful scene segmentation, and keyframe extraction by avoiding overly small or merged clusters.

For keyframe selection, we used a medoid-based approach, which identified the most central frame within each cluster. This ensured that the selected keyframes were actual frames from the video rather than synthesized centroids, thereby accurately reflecting the dominant visual and semantic content.

In summary, our method leveraged HDBSCAN for adaptive clustering, DBCV for cluster validation, and medoid selection for representative keyframe extraction, resulting in concise and comprehensive video summaries.

3.6.
Refined Keyframe Selection

Candidate frames were refined through quality assessment and duplicate filtering. Extreme low-light frames were discarded based on grayscale intensity, variance, and structural integrity using the Canny edge detector
Amer & Abushaala 2015
and Laplacian variance. Color uniformity was measured via histogram variance, and visual saliency was assessed by comparing central and global intensity. Since text often conveys crucial contextual information, especially in frames with minimal visual details, text presence was detected using the MSER algorithm
Işık 2014
and verified with ORB keypoint detection
Sharma et al. 2023
.

Duplicate filtering employed the Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM)
Bakurov et al. 2022
to remove redundant frames by considering luminance, contrast, and structure. We relied on these traditional computer vision techniques for their computational efficiency and proven robustness, which complement HDBSCAN’s noise handling and clustering capabilities. This integrated approach effectively filtered out low-quality frames while preserving those that offered a comprehensive visual summary, ensuring fast and reliable keyframe extraction for video summarization and retrieval.

4.
Evaluation

We evaluate our keyframe extraction method on two widely recognized benchmark datasets: TVSum20
Tan et al. 2024
and SumMe
Gygli et al. 2014
. TVSum20, derived from the original TVSum dataset
Song et al. 2015
, comprises 20 videos with 1,000 crowdsourced shot-level annotations, providing objective importance scores and redundancy reduction. SumMe includes 25 user-generated videos with summaries and importance scores from at least 15 annotators, making it suitable for both keyframe- and segment-based evaluation. Figure
2
illustrates an example video used for keyframe extraction, showcasing how our model effectively captures essential frames while minimizing redundancy. This demonstrates the model’s alignment with human perception and the effectiveness of our approach.

For evaluation, we use the F1-score, a standard metric in video summarization. The F1-score, defined as the harmonic mean of precision (the relevance of selected keyframes) and recall (the coverage of important moments), provides a balanced measure of summarization quality and has been widely adopted in previous studies.

5.
Conclusion and Future Directions

In this paper, we introduced TriPSS, a tri-modal keyframe extraction framework that fuses perceptual cues from the CIELAB color space, deep structural embeddings via ResNet-50, and semantic information from the LLM-based Llama-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct captioning model. By leveraging adaptive clustering with HDBSCAN and a multi-step refinement process, TriPSS achieves state-of-the-art performance on TVSum20 and SumMe, effectively capturing color nuances, high-level visual concepts, and contextual semantics. Our experiments show that TriPSS outperforms both unimodal and multi-modal baselines, underscoring the effectiveness of our fusion strategy in video summarization and retrieval.

While this work focuses on demonstrating the effectiveness of our fusion strategy, a detailed ablation study remains an important direction for future exploration. We plan to investigate learnable fusion weights, transformer-based cross-modal encoders, and end-to-end multi-modal models, along with a comprehensive ablation study to further disentangle each component’s contribution. These enhancements will pave the way for more robust keyframe selection and improved video understanding in large-scale retrieval tasks.

Poster 77. Hormonal Influences on Patient-Reported Outcomes in Female Patients with Patellar Instability: Effects of Oral Contraceptive Use and Hormone-Related Diagnoses
Authors: Naofumi Hashiguchi, Sofia Miracco, Miho J. Tanaka
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:Patellar instability is more prevalent in females, and hormonal influences such as estrogen and relaxin may contribute to ligamentous laxity. However, the relationship between endocrine history and treatment outcomes remains unclear. This study evaluated whether oral contraceptive (OCP) use and hormone-related diagnoses are associated with patient-reported outcomes (PROs) in female patients after treatment for patellar instability.Methods:We retrospectively reviewed female patients treated by a single surgeon for patellar instability. Hormone-related diagnoses included polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) or endometriosis, based on medical records. PROs included the International Knee Documentation Committee (IKDC), Kujala, and Patient-Reported Outcomes Measurement Information System (PROMIS) Physical and Mental scores, assessed at initial and most recent visits. Comparisons were made between OCP users and non-users, patients with and without hormone-related diagnoses, and those with neither vs >=1 exposure. Analyses were adjusted for age and BMI.Results:Of 218 patients, 179 had OCP status available (36 users, 143 non-users). No significant differences in final IKDC, PROMIS Mental, or Kujala scores were observed between OCP users and non-users. Hormone-related diagnosis status was available for 214 patients (22 with, 192 without). Patients with hormone-related diagnoses had lower final PROMIS Mental (48.2 ± 7.9 vs 53.6 ± 8.5, p = 0.02) and Kujala scores (66.8 ± 6.8 vs 80.4 ± 16.8, p = 0.01). Regression analysis confirmed the PROMIS Mental finding remained significant after adjusting for age and BMI. Combined exposure analysis and age-stratified analyses showed no independent or consistent effects.Conclusions:Hormone-related diagnoses, independent of OCP use, were associated with worse mental health-related and disease-specific functional outcomes in female patients with patellar instability. These results suggest endocrine history may warrant consideration when assessing prognosis and tailoring treatment strategies.

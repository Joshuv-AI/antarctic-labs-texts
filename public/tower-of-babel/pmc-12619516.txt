SGLT2 inhibitors and left atrial function in heart failure with reduced or mildly reduced ejection fraction
Authors: Hakan Duman, Hüseyin Durak, Mustafa Çetin, Müjgan Ayşenur Şahin, Elif Ergül, Erhan Saraçoğlu, Barış Dindar, Nadir Emlek
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSodium–glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors reduce cardiovascular mortality and hospitalizations in heart failure (HF). However, their effects on left atrial (LA) structure and function remain unclear, particularly in patients with reduced or mildly reduced ejection fraction (EF).MethodsThis observational, cross-sectional study included 134 patients with HF and EF < 50%, divided into two groups: those receiving SGLT2 inhibitors (SGLT2+, n = 25) and those not receiving them (SGLT2−, n = 109). Echocardiographic measurements of LA volume index (LAVI), LA emptying fraction (LAEF), LA functional index (LAFI), and passive/active emptying fractions were obtained according to ASE/EACVI guidelines. Clinical, laboratory, and echocardiographic parameters were compared between groups, and correlations with LA indices were analyzed using multivariable linear regression.ResultsThe prevalence of diabetes mellitus, spironolactone use, and furosemide use was significantly higher in the SGLT2 + group (all p < 0.05). LVEF was lower (32.9 ± 6.7 vs. 38.7 ± 8.6, p = 0.002), and left ventricular volumes were larger in the SGLT2 + group, while LAFI was significantly reduced (0.12 ± 0.08 vs. 0.18 ± 0.10, p = 0.008). However, there were no significant differences in LAVI or LAEF between groups. Multivariable regression identified E/A ratio, age, and LVEF as independent determinants of LAVI; posterior wall thickness and E/A ratio for LAEF; and E/A ratio, diabetes mellitus, posterior wall thickness, and interventricular septal thickness for LAFI.ConclusionSGLT2 inhibitor use was not associated with significant improvement in LA volume or functional parameters in patients with HF and reduced or mildly reduced EF. The prognostic value of LA functions appears to be primarily influenced by diastolic dysfunction and ventricular remodeling rather than SGLT2 inhibitor therapy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12872-025-05308-0.Keywords: Heart failure, SGLT2 inhibitors, Left atrium, LAVI, LA function.

Introduction

Heart failure (HF) is a major public health problem worldwide owing to its increasing prevalence, limited therapeutic options, and high morbidity and mortality rates [1]. According to current guidelines, cases with left ventricular ejection fraction (EF) < 40% are defined as heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF), and cases with EF between 41 and 49% are defined as heart failure with mildly reduced ejection fraction (HFmrEF) [2]. HFrEF and HFmrEF represents patients with predominant systolic dysfunction, high symptom burden, and poor prognosis. Structural cardiac changes, ventricular remodeling, and hemodynamic abnormalities significantly reduce quality of life and increase the risk of cardiovascular mortality in this patient group [2, 3].

The left atrium (LA), an important determinant of cardiac hemodynamics, exhibits early structural and functional changes in both systolic and diastolic dysfunction and has traditionally been considered a buffer that helps maintain cardiac output and normal left ventricular diastolic pressure [4, 5]. The left atrial volume index (LAVI) and functional parameters (passive ejection fraction, active ejection fraction, left atrial ejection fraction [LAEF], and left atrial functional index [LAFI]) provide valuable information regarding cardiac filling pressure and diastolic function. Increases in LAVI and impairments in LA function are closely associated with the development of atrial fibrillation (AF), higher hospitalization rates, reduced exercise capacity, and mortality [6, 7]. Therefore, comprehensive morphological and functional assessment of the LA is critically important in patients with HFrEF and HFmrEF, as it plays a key role in both risk stratification and the guidance of therapeutic strategies [8, 9]. Structural and functional deterioration of the LA in HF exacerbates symptom severity and serves as a strong predisposing factor for the development of AF, which worsens outcomes in patients with HF [10].

Although sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors were initially developed for the treatment of type 2 diabetes, in recent years, they have attracted attention for significantly reducing cardiovascular mortality and hospitalization in both diabetic and non-diabetic patients with HF [11]. Randomized controlled trials have demonstrated that SGLT2 inhibitors may reduce ventricular remodeling, improve diastolic function, and decrease cardiac filling pressures [12]. These potential mechanisms may contribute to reducing the load on the LA, lowering the volume indices, and preserving atrial function. However, data evaluating the effects of SGLT2 inhibitors on LA volume and function parameters in patients with HFrEF and HFmrEF are limited [13].

While the effects of SGLT2 inhibitors on ventricular function have been extensively studied in the current literature, data on LA structure and function are limited, and few studies have addressed this relationship in patients with HF [12–14]. Considering the prognostic importance of LA function, understanding the potential benefits of SGLT2 inhibitors in this patient group may be valuable for clinical decision-making. Therefore, in our study, we aimed to evaluate the association between the use of SGLT2 inhibitors and the LAVI and functional parameters (LAEF, LAFI, etc.) in patients with HFrEF and HFmrEF.

Methods

Study population

This observational, cross-sectional study was conducted at the Cardiology Clinic of Rize Recep Tayyip Erdoğan University Training and Research Hospital. Patients who were admitted between April 2025 and August 2025 and diagnosed with HFrEF and HFmrEF, were included in the study. The patients were divided into two groups: those receiving SGLT2 inhibitors (SGLT2+) and those not receiving them (SGLT2−).

Clinical data were obtained from the hospital information management system and echocardiographic measurements were retrieved from the digital echocardiography archive. The laboratory data of all patients were also examined using an electronic medical record system. The inclusion criteria were diagnosis of HF according to the 2021 ESC guidelines, left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) < 50% (reduced or mildly reduced EF), and the presence of complete echocardiographic measurements and laboratory records. The exclusion criteria were: insufficient clinical or echocardiographic data; history of acute coronary syndrome within the last 3 months; history of cardiac surgery within the last 6 months; and coexisting structural heart diseases such as atrial septal defect or hypertrophic cardiomyopathy.

The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Faculty of Medicine, Recep Tayyip Erdoğan University, with the decision dated April 22, 2025, and numbered E-40465587-050.01.01.04–1452. All the procedures were conducted in accordance with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all the participants prior to their inclusion in the study.

Transthoracic two-dimensional echocardiography

All echocardiographic examinations were performed in accordance with standard transthoracic echocardiography protocols using a Philips Epiq7 system (Philips Healthcare, Inc., Andover, MA, USA) with a 2.5–3.5 MHz transducer. Measurements were performed in compliance with the guidelines of the American Society of Echocardiography (ASE) and the European Association of Cardiovascular Imaging (EACVI) [15].

LVEF was calculated using the modified Simpson’s method from the apical four-chamber and two-chamber views. Left atrial volume was measured at end-systole (the moment of mitral valve closure) and end-diastole (the moment of mitral valve opening) using the biplane area-length method from apical four-chamber and two-chamber views. Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) software (IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version XX, IBM Corp., Armonk, NY). Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation for normally distributed data and as median (interquartile range) for non-normally distributed data. The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was used to evaluate normality.

For comparisons between groups, the Student’s t-test was applied for normally distributed continuous variables and the Mann-Whitney U test for non-normally distributed variables. Categorical variables were presented as numbers and percentages, and comparisons were performed using the chi-square test.

The relationships between continuous variables were assessed using Pearson’s or Spearman’s correlation analyses. Multivariable linear regression analyses were performed to identify the independent predictors of left atrial indices (LAVI, LAEF, and LAFI). Variables with p < 0.10 in the univariate analysis and those known to be clinically relevant (including age, LVEF, diabetes mellitus, spironolactone use, furosemide use, and E/A ratio) were entered into the models. This approach allowed for the adjustment of major confounders that differed between the SGLT2 + and SGLT2 − groups. The variance inflation factor (VIF) was used to exclude multicollinearity. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

Propensity score matching analysis

To further minimize potential confounding arising from baseline heterogeneity, propensity score matching (PSM) analysis was performed using a 1:1 nearest neighbor algorithm without replacement. The matching variables included age, diabetes mellitus, and LVEF, which differed significantly between the treatment groups and were clinically relevant to HF severity. Standardized mean differences (SMDs) were used to assess covariate balance, with |SMD| < 0.1 indicating a good balance. After matching, 25 patients treated with SGLT2 inhibitors were successfully matched with 25 control patients who did not receive these agents.

Results

Clinical and demographic characteristics

A total of 134 patients with HFrEF and HFmrEF were included in the study (mean age: 65.4 ± 10.7 years in the SGLT2 − group vs. 63.8 ± 10.4 years in the SGLT2 + group, p = 0.465). Among them, 25 patients (18.7%) were treated with SGLT2 inhibitors, whereas 109 patients (81.3%) were not. Sex distribution was similar between the groups (male sex: 85.3% in the SGLT2 − group vs. 80% in the SGLT2 + group, p = 0.347) (Table 1).

Propensity score–matched analysis

In the PSM cohort (n = 25 per group), the baseline characteristics were well balanced across the matched variables (|SMD| < 0.1 for all). Comparisons of echocardiographic parameters showed no significant differences in LAVI (25.9 ± 8.3 vs. 24.6 ± 7.8 mL/m², p = 0.548), LAFI (0.106 ± 0.031 vs. 0.101 ± 0.029, p = 0.312), or other atrial indices between the SGLT2 + and SGLT2 − groups. A modestly higher LAEF was observed in the SGLT2 + group (0.217 ± 0.058 vs. 0.185 ± 0.056, p = 0.045); however, this difference was not significant after correction for multiple testing. These findings suggest that the primary results were robust to adjustments using PSM (Supplementary Tables S1 and S2). Detailed baseline characteristics and post-matching comparisons are provided in Supplementary Table S1 and S2.

In the multivariable regression models adjusted for age, LVEF, diabetes, spironolactone and furosemide use, and E/A ratio, SGLT2 inhibitor therapy was not independently associated with any left atrial structural or functional index (Table 5). These results indicate that the observed differences in atrial parameters between the groups were largely explained by the underlying clinical and echocardiographic characteristics rather than SGLT2 inhibitor use itself.

Discussion

In this prospective, cross-sectional study, patients with HFrEF and HFmrEF were evaluated. While no significant differences in LAVI or LAEF were observed between patients treated with and without SGLT2 inhibitors, the SGLT2 + group had a higher prevalence of diabetes mellitus, greater use of spironolactone and furosemide, lower LVEF, larger left ventricular volumes, and significantly lower LAFI values. However, in multivariate analysis, the association between LAFI and SGLT2 inhibitor use lost its independent predictive significance. The baseline differences between the treatment groups, particularly the higher prevalence of diabetes mellitus and greater use of diuretics and mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists in the SGLT2 + group, likely reflect real-world treatment allocation, where SGLT2 inhibitors are often prescribed to patients with more advanced or comorbid heart failure. These imbalances may have introduced residual confounding, despite multivariable adjustment. To further address potential baseline heterogeneity, an additional PSM analysis was performed, which confirmed that SGLT2 inhibitor therapy was not independently associated with structural or functional improvements in the LA. The consistency of the findings before and after matching supports the validity of the primary analysis.

Although studies evaluating the effects of SGLT2 inhibitors on LA structure and function remain limited, the available evidence generally suggests favorable outcomes. In an HFpEF animal model, Bode et al. [16] demonstrated that the dual SGLT1/2 inhibitor sotagliflozin reduced LA remodeling and improved calcium homeostasis and mitochondrial function. At the clinical level, the EMPA-HEART CardioLink-6 trial showed that SGLT2 inhibitor therapy improved the LA functional parameters in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus and coronary artery disease [17]. However, in a study by Pourafkari et al., who also analyzed EMPA-HEART CardioLink-6, no significant effect of SGLT2 inhibitors on LA volume and function was reported in patients with HFrEF/HFmrEF, type 2 diabetes mellitus, and coronary artery disease. This finding is consistent with our results and supports the observation that SGLT2 inhibitors do not significantly improve LA function in patients with HFrEF and HFmrEF. In contrast, Sehly et al. reported that early initiation of SGLT2 inhibitors after acute coronary syndrome increased the LA strain values [18]. Furthermore, Fauchier et al. reported that the prevalence of AF in HFpEF patients ranges between 40 and 60% and that SGLT2 inhibitors improve prognosis regardless of the presence of AF [10]. Our study demonstrated that in patients with HFrEF and HFmrEF, SGLT2 inhibitor use was not associated with significant differences in LAVI or LAEF; however, a difference in LAFI was observed in univariable analysis. The disappearance of this effect in the multivariable analysis may be attributed to the more severe disease profile of patients in the SGLT2 + group, characterized by lower LVEF, a higher prevalence of diabetes, and larger left ventricular volumes.

The possible benefits of SGLT2 inhibitors in the LA can be explained through various hemodynamic and cellular mechanisms [19]. These agents reduce preload and afterload, decrease cardiac filling pressure, slow ventricular remodeling, and improve diastolic function [12, 20]. Additionally, through their natriuretic and osmotic diuretic effects, they reduce the volume load, which may lower LA wall stress and prevent atrial remodeling [21]. In experimental models, SGLT2 inhibitors improved mitochondrial function, reduced oxidative stress, and regulated calcium homeostasis, thereby limiting arrhythmogenic Ca²⁺ fluctuations [16, 22]. Taken together, these mechanisms suggest that SGLT2 inhibitors may contribute to the preservation of LA function. However, the inability to demonstrate this effect independently in our study may be explained by the more severe HF profile and higher burden of comorbidities among patients receiving the drug.

Diastolic dysfunction appears to be the major determinant of LA functions [23, 24]. There are some doubts about whether SGLT2 inhibitors are effective for diastolic dysfunction in patients with normal ejection fraction [25]. The findings of our study suggest that the effect of SGLT2 inhibitors on LA function may be limited in patients with HFrEF and HFmrEF. The absence of significant differences in structural and functional parameters, such as LAVI and LAEF, indicates that these drugs may exert more pronounced effects at the ventricular level in the cardiac remodeling process, whereas improvements at the atrial level may require longer treatment durations or may emerge in different patient profiles. The correlation between LAVI and the mitral E/A ratio observed in this study reflects the close interaction between left ventricular diastolic function and atrial remodeling. A positive association between these parameters was evident across the entire cohort and remained consistent when the patients were stratified according to SGLT2 inhibitor use (Fig. 2). This finding suggests that SGLT2 inhibitor therapy does not significantly modify the intrinsic coupling between diastolic filling dynamics and LA enlargement. In line with previous studies, increased early diastolic filling (higher E/A ratio) was associated with larger LA volumes, likely reflecting elevated left ventricular filling pressures. These results support the concept that atrial remodeling in HF is primarily driven by diastolic dysfunction rather than the use of specific pharmacologic agents such as SGLT2 inhibitors. Some studies in the literature similarly reported that SGLT2 inhibitors did not significantly change the LA volume or function parameters in HFrEF and HFmrEF populations [26, 27]. Nevertheless, our finding that diastolic dysfunction is the strongest determinant of LA function is consistent with previous studies, which have demonstrated that increased diastolic pressure and greater left ventricular mass are the primary drivers of atrial remodeling [28, 29]. Interestingly, the presence of moderate or severe mitral regurgitation was not an independent determinant. This may be due to the relatively high prevalence of mitral regurgitation in our study population, which did not result in a distinct separation between the groups.

Future prospective and randomized controlled trials are required to clearly reveal the effects of SGLT2 inhibitors on LA structure and function. In particular, separate evaluations of diabetic and non-diabetic subgroups in HFrEF and HFmrEF populations, as well as investigating the impact of treatment duration and dosage, are important. Moreover, in addition to echocardiographic measurements, the use of advanced imaging modalities, such as cardiac magnetic resonance or three-dimensional echocardiography, may allow more precise detection of atrial structural and functional changes. In this way, the potential benefits or limitations of SGLT2 inhibitors at the atrial level can be better understood, providing stronger evidence for clinical practice.

Limitations

The present study had several limitations. The most important limitation is the relatively small sample size and imbalance between the SGLT2 + and SGLT2 − groups, which may have reduced the statistical power to detect subtle differences in LA parameters. In addition to the small sample size and group imbalance, potential selection bias and residual confounding are further limitations of this study. Because SGLT2 inhibitor therapy was not randomly assigned, treatment allocation may have been influenced by disease severity, comorbidities, or socioeconomic factors related to the reimbursement policies. Although we applied multivariable regression and PSM to mitigate these effects, unmeasured confounders could not be fully excluded. Furthermore, the cross-sectional design precludes the assessment of temporal or causal relationships between SGLT2 inhibitor use and changes in atrial structure or function. Therefore, the non-significant results observed in some analyses should be interpreted cautiously, as they might reflect insufficient power rather than the absence of a true association. Therefore, the results should be interpreted with caution and considered hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory results. Nevertheless, all key clinical and echocardiographic covariates were included in the multivariable models. To further address potential baseline heterogeneity, an additional PSM analysis was performed, which confirmed that SGLT2 inhibitor therapy was not independently associated with LA structural or functional improvement. The consistency of findings before and after matching supports the validity of the primary analysis. Larger prospective studies are warranted to confirm these observations and further clarify the potential effects of SGLT2 inhibitors on atrial structure and function. After adjusting for major confounders, the association between SGLT2 inhibitor use and LA indices remained nonsignificant, underscoring that residual confounding may persist in this real-world observational design. Another contextual limitation of our study is the reimbursement policy for SGLT2 inhibitors in our country. During the study period, SGLT2 inhibitors were not reimbursed for HF indications, and their use was primarily limited to patients with type 2 diabetes or those who could afford out-of-pocket costs. Consequently, treatment allocation may have been influenced not only by clinical criteria but also by socioeconomic factors and physician discretion. This policy-driven heterogeneity could partly explain the higher prevalence of diabetes and more advanced HF in the SGLT2 + group. Although all patients were on guideline-directed medical therapy, including beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors/ARBs, and mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists, these contextual differences may affect the generalizability of our results to healthcare systems where SGLT2 inhibitors are fully reimbursed as part of standard HF therapy. Finally, LA function was assessed only by echocardiographic measurements, and advanced imaging modalities such as cardiac magnetic resonance were not utilized.

Conclusion

In this cross-sectional study of patients with HFrEF and HFmrEF, SGLT2 inhibitor therapy was not associated with significant improvements in LA structure or functional indices of the heart. Although a modest trend toward a higher atrial ejection fraction was observed after PSM analysis, this finding should be interpreted cautiously, given the limited sample size and residual confounding inherent to the study design. The overall results suggest that LA remodeling in HF is primarily related to diastolic dysfunction rather than SGLT2 inhibitor use. Larger prospective longitudinal studies are warranted to further elucidate the potential impact of SGLT2 inhibitors on atrial function and remodeling.

A proof of the 3 8 \frac{3}{8} -conjecture for independent domination in cubic graphs
Boštjan Brešar, Tanja Dravec, Michael A. Henning

Abstract
A set S S of vertices in a graph G G is a dominating set of G G if every vertex not in S S is adjacent to a vertex in S S . An independent dominating set in G G is a dominating set of G G with the additional property that it is an independent set. The domination number, γ ⁡ ( G ) \gamma(G) , and the independent domination number, i ⁡ ( G ) i(G) , are the minimum cardinalities among all dominating sets and independent dominating sets in G G , respectively. By definition, γ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ i ⁡ ( G ) \gamma(G)\leq i(G) for all graphs G G . Let G G be a connected cubic graph of order n n . In 1996 Reed [Combin. Probab. Comput. 5 (1996), 277–295] proved a breakthrough result that γ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ 3 8 ​ n \gamma(G)\leq\frac{3}{8}n . We prove the stronger result that if G G is different from K 3 , 3 K_{3,3} and the 5 5 -prism C 5 ​ □ ​ K 2 C_{5}\,\Box\,K_{2} , then i ⁡ ( G ) ≤ 3 8 ​ n i(G)\leq\frac{3}{8}n . This proves a known conjecture. The bound is tight in the sense that there are infinite families of connected cubic graphs that achieve equality in this bound.

Independent domination number; Domination number; Cubic graphs.

4
Overview of proof of Theorem
5

Before presenting a proof of Theorem
5
, we give a sketch proof to provide the reader an overview of the detailed proof that follows and the direction of the arguments.
In order to prove Theorem
5
, we relax the
3
-regularity condition to a bounded maximum degree

is a connected cubic graph to the requirement that
G
is a subcubic graph that contains no
K_{3,3}
-component and no

-component. We define next what we have coined the vertex weight and the structural weight of such graphs.

4.2
The structural weight

We would like to show that

since in the special case when

is a cubic graph of order

n_{0}(G)=n_{1}(G)=n_{2}(G)=0

. We will refer to graphs

since they satisfy
8i(G)>{\rm w}(G)
. We denote by

\mathcal{B}

Moreover relaxing the cubic condition results in so-called “troublesome” configurations of subcubic graphs. In Section

{\rm tc}(G)

of

\Theta(G)=2{\rm tc}(G)+2b(G).

4.3
The total weight

We define the
total weight
of
G
, which we denote by
\Omega(G)
, as the sum of the vertex weight and the structural weight of a subcubic graph
G
, and so

\Omega(G)={\rm w}(G)+\Theta(G).

We call the total weight of
G
the
\Omega

weight of
G
.
Our key result, given in Theorem
6
, gives a tight upper bound on the independent domination number of a subcubic graph in terms of its
\Omega

Theorem 6

If
G
is a subcubic graph of order
n
that contains no
K_{3,3}
-component and no
(C_{5}\,\Box\,K_{2})
-component, then
8i(G)\leq\Omega(G)
.

In order to prove our main result, namely Theorem
5
, we need the above stronger statement given in Theorem
6
. Each graph in the family
\mathcal{B}
of subcubic graphs contains vertices of degree
1
or
2
. Moreover, every so-called “troublesome configuration” contains a vertex of degree
2
in
G
. From these properties, a cubic graph
G
has no component that belongs to the family
\mathcal{B}
and contains no troublesome configuration. We therefore infer that if
G
is a cubic graph, then
{\rm tc}(G)=b(G)=0
, implying that its structural weight is zero, that is,
\Theta(G)=0
. Further if
G
is a cubic graph of order
n
, then
n_{0}(G)=n_{1}(G)=n_{2}(G)=0
and

, and so its vertex weight is
3n
, that is,
{\rm w}(G)=3n
. Hence in this case when

is a cubic graph, the inequality in the statement of Theorem

8i(G)\leq 3n
. Our main result, namely Theorem
5
, therefore follows readily from Theorem

.

5.1
Exit edges of a vertex set

Let

{\rm w}
-

\Phi_{G}(X)=\sum_{v\in V\setminus X}\big({\rm w}_{G-X}(v)-{\rm w}_{G}(v)\big).

We prove next the fact that the

-cost of removing a set

of vertices from

-exit edges in

-exit edges in

Fact 1

If

\Phi_{G}(X)=\xi_{G}(X)+2n_{0}(G-X)

Proof.

\Phi_{G}(X)

6
Proof of key result

Recall that the vertex weight of a subcubic graph
G
is
{\rm w}(G)=8n_{0}(G)+5n_{1}(G)+4n_{2}(G)+3n_{3}(G)
, and the structural weight of
G
is
\Theta(G)=2{\rm tc}(G)+2b(G)
. Moreover the total weight of
G
, called the
\Omega
-weight of
G
, is the sum of its vertex weight and structural weight, that is,
\Omega(G)={\rm w}(G)+\Theta(G)
. In this section, we present a proof of our key result, namely Theorem
6
. Its statement in terms of the
\Omega
-weight of
G
is as follows:

Theorem
6
.
If
G
is a subcubic graph of order
n
that contains no
K_{3,3}
-component and no
(C_{5}\,\Box\,K_{2})
-component, then
8i(G)\leq\Omega(G)
.

Proof.
Let
G
be a subcubic graph of order
n
that contains no
K_{3,3}
-component and no
(C_{5}\,\Box\,K_{2})
-component, and let
V=V(G)
. We wish to prove that
8i(G)\leq\Omega(G)
. Suppose that
G
is a counterexample to the inequality with minimum order, and let
G
have order
n
. Thus,
8i(G)>\Omega(G)
but every subcubic graph
G^{\prime}
of order less than
n
that contains no
K_{3,3}
-component and no
(C_{5}\,\Box\,K_{2})
-component satisfies
8i(G^{\prime})\leq\Omega(G^{\prime})
. Since
\Theta(G)\geq 0
, we note that
{\rm w}(G)\leq\Omega(G)
. Hence, if
8i(G)\leq{\rm w}(G)
, then
8i(G)\leq\Omega(G)
, a contradiction. Therefore,
8i(G)>{\rm w}(G)
.

Since our proof of Theorem
6
is long, we break the proof into four parts to enhance clarity and exposition of the proof. The first part of the proof establishes important structural properties of the counterexample
G
. The second part of the proof considers the case when the minimum degree of
G
equals
1
. The third part of the proof considers the case when the minimum degree of
G
equals
2
. The fourth and final part of the proof considers the case when the graph
G
is cubic, that is, when
G
is a
3
-regular graph.

6.1

G

In this section, namely Part
1

. A component of a graph that belongs to the family
\mathcal{B}
is a

of the graph.

.

Proof.

(
\Box

Establishing international expert consensus on core competencies and best practices in osteopathic education: a modified Delphi study protocol
Authors: Kesava Kovanur Sampath, Roger Engel, Damian Keter
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOsteopathic education is typically delivered through accredited higher education institutions with curricula not only varying widely but also inconsistently aligned with contemporary evidence. This lack of standardisation creates challenges in accreditation, workforce mobility and public understanding of the osteopathic role.ObjectiveThe objective of this study aims to develop international expert consensus on core osteopathic capabilities through a structured Delphi process.Methods and analysisThis protocol describes a three-round modified Delphi study involving international experts in osteopathic education. Participants will be purposively recruited from accredited osteopathic education programmes and professional networks and must have at least 5 years’ experience in osteopathic education, curriculum development or clinical supervision. A target sample of 40–60 experts will be sought. In round 1, participants will respond to open-ended questions exploring core competencies and educational priorities. Responses will be independently coded by two members of the research team using thematic analysis before being synthesised into consensus statements through investigator agreement. Rounds 2 and 3 will involve structured rating of these statements using a 4-point Likert scale with optional free-text comments to refine wording where appropriate. Descriptive statistics will be used to determine consensus, assess stability between rounds and guide item retention.Ethics and disseminationEthics approval for this study was obtained from the RMIT University Human Research Ethics Committee, Australia (2025–29731-30793). Participation is voluntary and pseudo-anonymous, with informed consent obtained electronically. Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications, conference presentations and to professional bodies to support curriculum alignment, accreditation processes and international dialogue on capability development.

Background

Osteopathy is taught and regulated within diverse international frameworks, ranging from voluntary registers to fully statutory systems, resulting in significant variation in educational structures and competency expectations across countries.1 2 Although osteopathic education is typically delivered through accredited higher education institutions, the competencies required for practice and the benchmarks guiding curricula vary widely and are often inconsistently aligned with contemporary evidence.3

Historically, osteopathic training has prioritised hands-on skill development, particularly palpation, which has long been regarded as a defining osteopathic capability used to identify anatomical landmarks, assess tissue characteristics and infer joint mobility and/or dysfunction. However, palpation remains challenging to teach and difficult to acquire reliably, with studies highlighting substantial interexaminer variability and limited agreement, even among experienced practitioners.4 5 While some studies have explored specific domains of osteopathic education such as palpation or osteopathic competencies for particular populations,6 7 broader questions about core competencies across osteopathic curricula remain underexamined.

Concurrently, understanding of manual therapy mechanisms has shifted. Multiple systematic reviews and experimental studies demonstrate that biomechanical specificity such as precise force direction, segment targeting or exact technique application does not consistently produce superior outcomes compared with less specific versions of the same technique.8 Emerging mechanistic models emphasise neurophysiological, contextual and psychosocial influences as significant drivers of therapeutic change.9 10 Contextual factors, including therapeutic alliance, communication, expectations and patient–practitioner interaction, have repeatedly been shown to contribute meaningfully to both mechanistic9 11 and clinical12–14 treatment outcomes. The sum of these factors emphasises that response to interventions including manual therapy is a specific patient response to a constellation of contributors with technique intricacy being but a small contributor.15

These developments raise important questions about whether traditional technique-centred educational models remain congruent with contemporary evidence. Although many osteopathic programmes have adopted competency-based educational frameworks16 that extend beyond technical manual skills including clinical reasoning, communication, patient management, professionalism and collaboration, the extent to which these competencies are consistently prioritised, operationalised and assessed across international programmes remains uncertain. Contemporary curricula also increasingly emphasise evidence-informed practice and psychologically informed, person-centred care, reflecting the growing recognition that effective clinical management extends beyond technical proficiency alone.9 10 At the same time, modern healthcare environments require graduates to demonstrate broad competencies beyond technical manual skills, including clinical reasoning, communication, interprofessional collaboration, person-centred care, reflective practice and the ability to manage uncertainty.17 Although these expectations are widely recognised across health professions, it remains unclear how such competencies are prioritised, integrated or assessed within current osteopathic education. Existing reviews have noted inconsistency, variability and a lack of coherence in osteopathic training approaches.1

Other health professions have increasingly used Delphi methodology to establish nationally or internationally recognised competency frameworks, entrustable professional activities and curriculum priorities.18–20 In contrast, osteopathy lacks a unified competency framework informed by both expert consensus and contemporary evidence. Given the heterogeneity in regulatory environments, institutional curricula and pedagogical emphases, a structured consensus-building method is warranted. Contemporary health professional education increasingly emphasises competency-based curricula, evidence-informed practice, interprofessional collaboration and person-centred care to prepare graduates for integrated healthcare systems. Osteopathic education, as a university-based allied health discipline in many jurisdictions, should similarly be underpinned by robust educational principles, scientific reasoning and contemporary pedagogical approaches while remaining responsive to differing regulatory and professional contexts. Developing an internationally informed consensus on these educational priorities may support greater curriculum coherence, facilitate workforce mobility and strengthen the integration of osteopathy within modern healthcare systems.17

Accordingly, this study aims to use a modified Delphi approach to establish expert consensus on essential competencies and best-practice teaching priorities in osteopathic education.21 22 The resulting framework will help standardise osteopathic curricula, inform accreditation processes, support contemporary pedagogical development and align training with up-to-date evidence and professional expectations. The final deliverable will be an internationally informed, consensus-based competency framework intended to guide curriculum development, educational review and programme enhancement across diverse educational contexts. The framework is intended to inform, rather than replace, national accreditation requirements or institutional curricula.

Methods

Study design

A modified Delphi survey design will be used and will follow the Accurate Consensus Reporting Document guidelines for conducting and reporting of Delphi studies (ACCORD).23 The Delphi method provides a systematic approach to synthesising expert opinion in areas where evidence is evolving, fragmented or contested. Delphi studies offer methodological transparency, reproducibility and a robust foundation for curriculum reform.24 25 This study adopts a pragmatic methodological perspective, recognising that expert consensus can be developed through the integration of qualitative exploration (round I) and quantitative consensus methods (rounds II and III) to address complex educational questions. A three-round design was selected as it is the most commonly adopted Delphi structure in health professions research, providing sufficient opportunity for participants to reconsider their responses following controlled feedback while minimising respondent burden and attrition.24 Round I will generate capability statements, while rounds II and III will refine and establish consensus on these statements through structured rating. Participant recruitment and round I are planned to commence in March 2026. Completion of all Delphi rounds, data analysis and development of the final competency framework are anticipated by December 2026.

Respondent group—expert panel

To maximise the breadth of perspectives, purposive sampling will be used to recruit an international panel of experts representing diverse geographical regions, educational roles, institutional affiliations, regulatory environments and levels of experience in osteopathic education. This approach is intended to ensure broad representation of contemporary osteopathic educational contexts. Participants were included if they were willing to participate and declare any conflicts of interest. Potential participants had to meet one or more of the following criteria. Participants will be included if they are willing to participate and declare any conflicts of interest. Potential participants must meet one or more of the following criteria: experienced educators, clinical supervisors, curriculum developers, curriculum designers, programme leaders, module coordinators or academics who are actively involved in teaching, assessment, curriculum development or educational leadership within accredited osteopathic education programmes. To ensure appropriate expertise, participants must have a minimum of 5 years’ experience in osteopathic education, clinical training or relevant postgraduate teaching and must be currently affiliated with an accredited osteopathic education programme or a recognised higher education institution offering osteopathy. Eligible participants must be able to complete the study in English and be willing to participate in all three rounds of the Delphi process. Individuals will be excluded if they do not meet the minimum experience threshold, are not engaged in osteopathic teaching or curriculum responsibilities, lack current affiliation with a relevant educational institution or are unable or unwilling to commit to all Delphi rounds.

Work group

The work group will consist of six investigators from Australia (n=2), Belgium (n=1), Canada (n=1), the Netherlands (n=1) and the USA (n=1), providing complementary expertise across diverse international osteopathic educational contexts. Collectively, the group will have experience in undergraduate and postgraduate osteopathic education, curriculum design, programme leadership, competency-based education, clinical practice and mixed-methods research. The primary investigator (KKS) will be responsible for study coordination, participant recruitment, questionnaire administration, data management and preparation of the Delphi rounds. Round I responses will be independently reviewed and synthesised by KKS and DK. The resulting themes and draft statements will then be reviewed by all work group members to achieve consensus on thematic categorisation, statement wording and progression to subsequent Delphi rounds. The work group will also oversee questionnaire refinement, interpretation of findings, methodological oversight and ensure that the resulting capability framework reflects contemporary international perspectives in osteopathic education. None of the work group members will participate as Delphi panellists.

Recruitment

A purposive, multipronged approach will be used to identify and recruit international experts in osteopathic education (figure 1). Potential participants will be identified through recognised osteopathic education programmes, higher-education institutions offering osteopathy, professional associations, existing academic and professional networks and authorship of relevant publications in osteopathic education, curriculum development, competency frameworks or health professions education. Institutions will not be asked to nominate a single representative; instead, individuals meeting the eligibility criteria will be invited directly to ensure participation is based on relevant expertise rather than institutional representation alone. Recruitment will prioritise participants affiliated with accredited or state-recognised osteopathic education programmes where applicable. Invitations will be distributed by email and will include study information, eligibility criteria, consent procedures and a link to the online survey.

Screening

Invitations for participation will be distributed to the identified experts through email in which they will receive information on the purpose of the study, how they were selected as expert panellists and information on informed consent. Experts will be screened at this point for eligibility to participate and also asked to nominate peers that may be interested in participating. Potential participants will be asked to declare any conflicts of interest that may exist and these will be recorded. Participants will be asked to commit to participate in all rounds (that may last a few months) of the Delphi technique required to develop the core competency standards. They will be asked to sign a written consent form to be involved in the Delphi technique process.

Panel size and composition

There is no consensus or agreement in the literature regarding the ideal size of Delphi panels with size varying from 10 to over 1000.26 However, an apriori goal of at least 40–60 participants completing all three rounds of the instrument will be sought as this has been recommended to be representative and feasible in Delphi instruments.27 A minimum number of 40 participants will be set with the upper level held fluid as larger panels increase the reliability of the outcomes.25

Anonymity

Although participants will remain anonymous to one another, responses will be linked to individual participants by the research team to enable matching of responses across successive Delphi rounds (quasi-anonymity). All identifiable information will be accessible only to the research team and stored securely in accordance with institutional data governance requirements. Between Delphi rounds, participants will receive anonymised feedback summarising the group responses, including measures of consensus and a synthesis of representative comments. Individual comments will not be attributed to participants to maintain confidentiality.28

Instrumentation

This study will use a three-round web-based Delphi survey instrument adapted for osteopathy from a previously developed instrument by one of the investigators (DK) in this study. The Delphi questionnaires will be administered electronically using Qualtrics XM (Qualtrics, Provo, Utah). Qualtrics provides a secure web-based platform for survey administration and data collection. Survey responses will be transmitted using encrypted connections and stored on secure Qualtrics servers in accordance with institutional data security requirements. Access to identifiable data will be restricted to authorised members of the research team through password-protected accounts. Following completion of data collection, survey data will be exported to secure, password-protected institutional storage for analysis in accordance with the university’s data management and research governance policies.

Delphi rounds

Round I

Round I of the instrument is an open-ended design with the goal of identifying opinions/perceptions on the future of training paradigms for osteopathic programmes. The round I questionnaire (online supplemental file 1) will be developed by the international work group following a review of the contemporary osteopathic education literature, accreditation standards and the collective educational expertise of the investigators. This round of the instrument will identify basic demographics including which training programmes the experts are currently involved. After brief explanation of the rationale for this question being posed, an open-ended question asking participants to identify core competencies, educational priorities, teaching approaches, assessment strategies and professional attributes is required for contemporary osteopathic education. This questionnaire has been adapted from a previously validated tool and hence did not require face validity through a pilot survey. Rather than being anchored to an existing competency framework, the questionnaire will be designed to minimise framing bias and allow important educational capabilities, curriculum content, teaching approaches, assessment strategies and professional attributes to emerge inductively from expert opinion. Prior to dissemination, the questionnaire will be reviewed by the work group to ensure clarity, relevance and comprehensiveness. Respondents will receive a web link to the round I questionnaire via the Qualtrics online survey platform. They will have 30 days to complete the first round, with a reminder sent at the 15-day mark to encourage participation. After completion of round I, the data will be downloaded by the primary investigator and presented to the work group for analysis.

An inductive thematic analysis will be undertaken manually. Round I responses will be independently reviewed and coded by two investigators (KKS and DK), who will identify patterns, concepts and preliminary themes emerging from the data. Coding discrepancies will be resolved through iterative discussion. The preliminary themes, item labels, thematic groupings and draft statements will then be reviewed and refined by the international work group until consensus is reached regarding thematic categorisation and statement wording. Recommendation statements representing the content within each collective theme will then be developed for inclusion in round II.

The synthesised statements will represent the educational priorities identified by participants and will subsequently be prioritised through iterative rating in rounds II and III using the predefined consensus criteria described below. Trustworthiness of the qualitative synthesis will be enhanced through independent coding by two investigators, iterative discussion to resolve discrepancies and review of the preliminary themes by the international work group to ensure credibility, consistency and completeness of the thematic interpretation.

Round II

Round II of the instrument will include a list of the themes presented in responses to round I questioning. Respondents will use a 4-point Likert scale (table 1) to score each of these themes by level of agreement with the recommended training paradigm. The purpose of round II is to allow participants to identify themes which they consider to be most important to include or omit from osteopathic educational models. By removing a neutral midpoint, participants are encouraged to make a considered judgement regarding each statement. Consensus will be determined using predefined agreement thresholds across iterative Delphi rounds rather than the number of response categories alone. Although Delphi studies have used scales ranging from four to nine response options, there is no universally accepted standard, and the choice of response scale should reflect the purpose of the consensus exercise.21 22 Invitations to participate in round II will be distributed via email to those who completed round I of the instrument. The expert panel will have 30 days to complete round II of the instrument, with a reminder email at 15 days to promote participation. After completion of round II, the work group will tally the total of each response in column charts. These graphical depictions of previous responses along with the same round II questions will be reissued to the participants as the Round III instrument.

Round III

Round III will include the same themes and grading scales as round II with the addition of graphs representing the descriptive statistical scores computed from round II of the instrument. With this information available, the respondents will be asked to rescore each item on the same 4-point Likert scale. The purpose of the round III instrument is to allow participants to identify themes which they consider to be most important to include or omit from osteopathic educational models while being aware of the blinded opinions of other participants. Furthermore, participants will be asked to provide an overall evaluation of the proposed competency framework with respect to its clarity, comprehensiveness, coherence and implementability within contemporary osteopathic education. These ratings will be used to inform the final refinement of the framework prior to completion of the Delphi process.

Data analysis

SPSS V.28.0.1.0 will be utilised for all quantitative analysis. Scores for round III will be divided into two categories based on descriptive identifiers: the tally of ‘strongly disagree’ and ‘disagree’ will represent the percentage of scores in the ‘not recommended’ category, meaning that the proposed component of training is not recommended to be included in osteopathic education. Alternatively, the tally of ‘strongly agree’ and ‘agree’ will represent the percentage of scores in the ‘recommended’ category, meaning that the proposed component of training is recommended to be included in osteopathic education. As recommended by established guidelines, consensus will be established if 80% or greater of the respondents score the component of education as either ‘not recommended’ or ‘recommended’. When an item does not reach consensus, meaning ‘recommended’ or ‘not recommended’ percentages fall between 50% and 80%, the decision will be made between ‘near-consensus’ and ‘undecided’. Agreement between 60% and 80% either for ‘recommended’ or ‘not recommended’ will be considered ‘near consensus’ while agreement less than 60% will be considered ‘undecided’. Statements that do not achieve the predefined consensus threshold after round III will be classified as not having reached consensus and will be retained in the final results. These statements will not undergo additional Delphi rounds but will be reported to identify areas of continuing uncertainty or disagreement among experts, thereby informing priorities for future research and curriculum development. A composite score for each component of training will be calculated based on the following formula:

n1 is the number of respondents answering ‘strongly disagree’ with component of training, n2 is the number of respondents answering ‘disagree’ with component of training, n3 is the number of respondents answering ‘agree’ with the component of training, and n4 is the number of respondents answering ‘strongly agree’ with the component of training. The composite scores for individual participants will be added to establish a combined composite score. The higher the combined composite score, the more important the training component is in osteopathic education. The larger the negative value, the more important the training component is to exclude from osteopathic education. Mann-Whitney U Statistics will be utilised to assess statistical difference between experts with academic doctorates versus fellowship trained participants. This Delphi incorporates rating of agreement with training components both without (round II) and with (round III) graphics depicting other participants’ responses. Hence, response stability between rounds II and III will be evaluated using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. This analysis will complement the predefined consensus criteria by determining whether participant ratings remain stable across successive Delphi rounds.22

Feedback reports

Round I responses will be synthesised into draft statements by the work group. We will send feedback reports from the preceding round to each panel member. The feedback report will consist of (1) the median and mode for each item, (2) the participant’s response in the previous round, (3) an indication of the current level of consensus and (4) a summary of participant responses as to why they have ranked an item in the way they did.

Discussion

This protocol describes a modified Delphi study to address the issue of osteopathic education. This modified Delphi study brings together experienced osteopathic educators and clinicians to establish consensus on the core competencies and best practices required for contemporary osteopathic training. By synthesising expert perspectives across multiple rounds, this study will help identify which foundational skills, teaching approaches and professional capabilities should be prioritised to ensure graduates are well equipped for contemporary clinical practice. The resulting framework is intended to guide curriculum development, educational review and future research rather than prescribe accreditation standards.

The implications of this work extend beyond curriculum refinement. A consensus-based framework has the potential to inform accreditation requirements, guide faculty development and enhance consistency in educational standards across institutions. It also provides a foundation for future research exploring how these competencies translate into clinical effectiveness and professional identity formation. Ultimately, the findings will support a more coherent, evidence-informed and patient-centred approach to osteopathic education, offering tangible benefits for educators, regulators and the broader osteopathic profession.

This study has several methodological limitations. As with all Delphi studies, the findings will reflect expert consensus rather than empirical validation and may, therefore, be influenced by the composition of the expert panel. Although purposive sampling will maximise diversity across international educational contexts, the panel will not be statistically representative of all osteopathic educators. The synthesis of round I responses may introduce researcher interpretation; however, this risk will be minimised through independent synthesis by two investigators, followed by review and consensus among an international work group. Participant attrition between Delphi rounds is also recognised as a potential limitation and will be minimised through clear communication, reminder emails and concise survey design. As this is an international Delphi, differences in educational systems, regulatory environments and terminology may influence participants’ interpretations of competency statements. Feedback reports summarising the group responses from the preceding round will be provided to participants before each subsequent Delphi round.24 The iterative Delphi process and provision of structured feedback between rounds are intended to minimise these differences. Despite these limitations, the modified Delphi approach provides a rigorous and transparent method for developing consensus in areas where empirical evidence remains limited.24 25

Ethics and dissemination

Ethics approval for this study was obtained from the RMIT University Human Research Ethics Committee, Australia (2025–29731-30793). Participation is voluntary and pseudo-anonymous, with informed consent obtained electronically. Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications, conference presentations and to professional bodies to support curriculum alignment, accreditation processes and international dialogue on capability development.

Patient and public involvement

It was not appropriate to involve patients or members of the public in the design or conduct of this Delphi study because the research aims to establish expert consensus on osteopathic education and supervision practices, requiring input from professional educators with relevant experience. No patients or public contributors were involved in reporting this protocol. We will include public-facing dissemination activities, such as a plain-language summary of the final recommendations, to support transparency and knowledge translation.

PBr 3 Adsorption and Dissociation on the Si(100) Surface
Vladimir M. Shevlyuga, Yulia A. Vorontsova, Tatiana V. Pavlova

Abstract
The adsorption of PBr 3 \text{PBr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}} on the Si(100)-2 × \times 1 surface was studied by scanning tunneling microscopy (STM) and density functional theory (DFT). The PBr 3 \text{PBr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}} molecule completely dissociates on the Si(100) surface at room temperature into P and Br atoms. In most cases, the dissociated molecule was observed in STM on three neighboring Si dimers. DFT calculations confirm that the PBr 3 \text{PBr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}} molecule can completely dissociate at room temperature. After annealing the sample to 400 ∘ C, phosphorus is incorporated into silicon, as evidenced by the Si atoms ejected to the surface. These findings are useful for the insertion of individual phosphorus atoms into silicon by PBr 3 \text{PBr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}} adsorption through a halogen mask.



I
Introduction

Phosphine is currently the main precursor molecule for doping silicon with phosphorus for semiconductor devices, including doping of the Si(100) surface with near-atomic precision to create quantum devices
Fuechsle et al. 2012
;
He et al. 2019
;
Kiczynski et al. 2022
;
Wang et al. 2022
. Due to technological importance, the mechanism of phosphorus incorporation into silicon by phosphine adsorption has now been studied in detail. The
\text{PH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
molecule adsorbed on the Si(100) surface dissociates at room temperature
Warschkow et al. 2005
;
Warschkow et al. 2016
. For complete dissociation of phosphine, three adjacent silicon dimers are required
Wilson et al. 2004
;
Warschkow et al. 2016
. Subsequent heating of the sample with adsorbed phosphine leads to the incorporation of phosphorus atoms into surface layer, as evidenced by Si atoms ejected from the substrate
Schofield et al. 2003
.

To dope the Si(100) surface with phosphorus with near-atomic precision, a hydrogen monolayer patterned in a scanning tunneling microscope (STM) is used as a mask
Schofield et al. 2003
. An alternative to the hydrogen mask on the Si(100) surface can be a halogen monolayer
Pavlova, Zhidomirov, and Eltsov 2018
;
Dwyer, Dreyer, and Butera 2019
;
Pavlova et al. 2020
;
Pavlova et al. 2022
. The halogen monolayer may have a number of advantages, since a halogenated silicon surface has a different reactivity compared to a hydrogenated one. In particular, calculations show that the halogen monolayer can better protect the Si(100) surface against parasitic incorporation of impurities from halogen-containing precursor molecules
Pavlova and Eltsov 2021
. Recently, the interaction of
\text{BCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
with chlorinated and hydrogenated Si(100) surfaces
Silva-Quinones et al. 2020
;
Dwyer et al. 2021
and
\text{AlCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
with a clean Si(100) surface
Radue et al. 2021
has been studied.

Here, as a source of phosphorus for silicon doping, we consider the
\text{PBr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
molecule, which should be compatible with a halogen mask on the Si(100) surface. We studied the
\text{PBr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
adsorption on the clean Si(100) surface at room temperature in STM. Fragments of the dissociated
\text{PBr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
molecule were identified using density functional theory (DFT).
After
\text{PBr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
adsorption, phosphorus was incorporated into the silicon surface by annealing the sample to 400
∘
C.

II
Methods

II.1
Experimental methods

The experiments were carried out in an ultra-high vacuum (UHV) system with a base pressure of 5
\times
10
-11
Torr. The STM measurements were performed with GPI CRYO (SigmaScan Ltd.) operated at 77 K. B-doped Si(100) samples (1
\Omega
cm) were used. The Si(100) sample was prepared by outgassing the wafer at 870 K for several days in UHV followed by flash-annealing at 1470 K. After the last flash heating, the sample was allowed to cool down to room temperature for 30 min. The
\text{PBr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
adsorption was carried out at a partial pressure of
3\cdot 10^{-10}
Torr for two minutes at room temperature. To identify Br atoms from the dissociated
\text{PBr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
molecule, we carried out the
\text{Br}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
adsorption on the clean Si(100) surface at a partial pressure of
1.5\cdot 10^{-10}
Torr for ten seconds at room temperature. To remove phosphorus from the surface for the next experiment, the sample was heated at 1170 K overnight
Brown et al. 2005
. We used mechanically cut Pt-Rh and Pt-Ir tips and electrochemically etched polycrystalline W tips. The voltage (
U_{s}
) was applied to the sample. All STM images were processed using the WSXM software
Horcas et al. 2007
.

II.2
Computational methods

The spin-polarized DFT calculations were performed with the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional
Perdew, Burke, and Ernzerhof 1996
as implemented in the Vienna
ab initio
simulation package (VASP)
Kresse and Furthmüller 1996
;
Kresse and Joubert 1999
. The kinetic-energy cutoff of the plane wave basis was set to 350 eV. The silicon surface was modeled by an eight layer slabs consisting of 6
\times
6 supercell and a 14 Å vacuum gap. Phosphorus and bromine atoms were placed on the top Si(100) surface with the 2
\times
1 structure, whereas hydrogen atoms saturate the dangling bonds at the bottom Si surface. The bottom three Si layers were frozen at their bulk positions, while the coordinates of other atoms were fully relaxed until the residual forces were smaller than 0.01 eV/ Å. Brillouin zone integrations were done using a 3
\times
3
\times
1 k-point grid. We studied the convergence of the bromine adsorption energy on the Si(100) surface as a function of the number of substrate layers, cutoff energy, k-points, and vacuum gap. The adsorption energies were calculated as the difference between the total energy of the surface with an adsorbed molecule and the total energy of the clean surface and the molecule in vacuum. STM images were generated in the HiveSTM program
Vanpoucke and Brocks 2008
using the Tersoff-Hamann approximation
Tersoff and Hamann 1985
.

The activation barriers were calculated by using the nudged elastic band (NEB) method
Jónsson, Mills, and Jacobsen 1998
. In this case, the 4
\times
4 supercell was used, and the criterion of convergence for residual forces was reduced to 0.03 eV/ Å. We used five images between two endpoints. The reaction rate was estimated using the Arrhenius equation:

\nu=\nu_{0}\exp\Bigl(-\frac{E_{act}}{k_{B}T}\Bigr),

(1)

where
E_{act}
is the activation energy,
k_{B}
is the Boltzmann constant,
T
is the temperature. Attempt frequency
\nu_{0}
was determined by the Vineyard equation
Vineyard 1957
:

\nu_{0}=\prod\limits_{n=1}^{3N}\nu^{IS}_{n}\Bigl/\prod\limits_{n=1}^{3N-1}\nu^{TS}_{n},

(2)

where
\nu^{IS}_{n}
and
\nu^{TS}_{n}
are the real-valued vibrational frequencies in the initial (IS) and transition (TS) states, respectively. The number N of atoms for which the vibration frequencies were calculated were limited by the nearest neighbors of the migrating atoms. The transition state was verified by the presence of a single imaginary frequency. The frequencies were calculated using the density functional perturbation theory (DFPT), as implemented in VASP.

;

We considered two adsorption channels, on electrophilic and nucleophilic Si atoms (Fig.
3
). Diffusion of the
\text{PBr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}

\text{PBr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}

\text{PBr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
can dissociate into
\text{PBr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
and Br from pbr3-1 (pbr3-2) structures with a lower barrier (without a barrier). Complete dissociation path was calculated for the
\text{PBr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
molecule adsorbed on the nucleophilic Si atom (the left side of the diagram in Fig.
3
), since in this case
\text{PBr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}

\text{PBr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
and Br. The highest activation barrier in this dissociation path is 0.78 eV, which corresponds to Br diffusion between neighboring Si dimers in the same row. At room temperature, the dissociation rate determined by this barrier is 0.1 s

, i.e.
\text{PBr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
completely dissociates in about 10 seconds. The decomposition of PBr into P and Br occurs significantly faster through the lower reaction barrier of 0.39 eV (
10^{6}

-1

\text{PBr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}

3

1

\text{PBr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
molecules in most cases completely dissociate into P and Br atoms.

The incorporation of a phosphorus atom into silicon requires heating to above room temperature
Schofield et al. 2003
. When the sample with
\text{PBr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
adsorbed at room temperature was annealed to 400
∘
C, islands appeared on the surface (Fig.
4
). The islands are formed by Si atoms, since the height of the island is approximately 1.4  Å, which corresponds to the height of an atomic step on the Si(100) surface. In addition, the filled state STM image of the large island (Fig.
4

Schofield et al. 2003
.

After heating the surface with adsorbed
\text{PBr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
, bromine atoms were observed, which are mainly paired on the Si dimer (Fig.
4
a). Bromine monolayer desorbs as
\text{SiBr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
at about 650
∘
C
Flowers et al. 1995
, which is higher than the heating temperature in our case (400
∘
C), but Br can start to desorb in the form of individual atoms at a lower temperature, 350
∘
C
Trenhaile et al. 2005
. Although the conclusion about Br desorption at 350
∘
C was made for a saturated bromine coverage
Trenhaile et al. 2005
, we cannot exclude the possibility of Br desorption in our case at a low coverage. Nevertheless, some of the bromine atoms definitely remains on the surface, as seen in the STM image in Fig.
4
a. To unambiguously identify P atoms, including those in heterodimers, and other defects on the surface, our atomic resolution data are insufficient.

IV
Conclusions

The
\text{PBr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
adsorption on the Si(100) surface at room temperature is predominantly dissociative, down to individual atoms. The calculation of one of the possible dissociation pathways confirm that the
\text{PBr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
molecule can dissociate on Si(100) at room temperature. In the most frequently observed object on the

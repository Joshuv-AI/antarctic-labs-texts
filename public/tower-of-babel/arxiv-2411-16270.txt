On involutions of minuscule Kirillov algebras induced by real structures
Mischa Elkner

Abstract
. We study Kirillov algebras attached to minuscule highest weight representations of semisimple Lie algebras. They can be viewed as equivariant cohomology algebras of partial flag varieties. Real structures on the varieties then induce involutions of these algebras. We describe how these involutions act on the spectra of minuscule Kirillov algebras, and model the fixed points via the equivariant cohomology of real partial flag varieties. We then use this model to characterise freeness of the fixed point coordinate ring over the appropriate base. As an application, we recover a q = − 1 q=-1 phenomenon of Stembridge in the minuscule case by geometric means.

Let us briefly sketch our approach and the role of quasi-compactness. Already on the level of the base ring
H^{2*}_{G^{\vee}}
, we can compare its coinvariant ring
(H^{2*}_{G^{\vee}})_{\sigma^{*}}
to
H^{2*}_{(G^{\vee})^{\sigma}}=H^{2*}_{(G^{\vee})^{\sigma}}(\text{pt},\mathbb{C})
. Chern–Weil theory (see Section

) identifies

with the invariant polynomial ring
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}]^{\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}}
(where
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
is the Lie algebra of
G^{\vee}
). To similarly describe
H^{2*}_{(G^{\vee})^{\sigma}}
, one associates to
\sigma
its
Cartan involution
, a complex linear Lie algebra involution
\theta
of
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
. Then
H^{2*}_{(G^{\vee})^{\sigma}}\cong\mathbb{C}[(\mathfrak{g}^{\vee})^{\theta}]^{(\mathfrak{g}^{\vee})^{\theta}}
, and to ensure that this is the coinvariant ring of
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}]^{\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}}
as desired,

has to be particularly well-behaved. This translates precisely to the requirement that
\sigma
be quasi-compact (see Section
4
).

On the level of Kirillov algebras, a similar translation to invariant rings is possible. Here it turns out that
\sigma
has to fix
\lambda
(denoted
\sigma_{*}\lambda=\lambda
in the theorem) and one then has to analyse the restriction of
\sigma
to
L_{\lambda}
, the Levi factor of the parabolic
P_{\lambda}
. There is a further complication arising from the fact that
L_{\lambda}^{\sigma}
need not be connected. Up to this issue, which we address, one arrives at the requirement that
\sigma|_{L_{\lambda}}
be quasi-compact, analogously to the level of base rings. The main work in proving Theorem
1.1
is then to show that these requirements are fulfilled by some real structure
\sigma
(with one exception, related to the issue of connectedness). Let us note that this
\sigma
need not be quasi-compact on all of
G^{\vee}
, which leads to the auxiliary real structure
\sigma_{0}
in the theorem, used on the level of base rings.

In addition to the well-behaved Cartan involutions there are also topological advantages to quasi-compactness. Indeed, if
\mathfrak{g}
is simple and not isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}_{2n+1}
, then the real partial flag varieties

arising in Theorem

(see Section

to describe a key algebraic property of the coinvariant homomorphism (
4

Theorem 1.2
.

In the setting of Theorem
1.1

\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})_{\sigma^{*}}
is a free module of finite rank over

or the homomorphism (

Structural results like Theorem
1.2
are of inherent interest, but are also related to combinatorial applications, one of which we now describe. Namely, for a generic

in the base, the fibre in

is finite and can be identified with the set
\operatorname{wt}(\lambda)
of weights of
V^{\lambda}
. By choosing

\sigma^{*}

\sigma
is (inner to) a split real structure, it is not hard to identify this action on
\operatorname{wt}(\lambda)
with that of the longest element

of the Weyl group. This action has been studied by Stembridge
[

]
and shown to fulfil a

phenomenon. This involves the

\mathcal{D}^{\lambda}
(see (
22

29
]

\operatorname{wt}(\lambda)
. Here, we interpret
\mathcal{D}^{\lambda}
as the Poincaré polynomial of
\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})
over
S(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}}
, following Panyushev
[
25
]

(9)

\#\operatorname{wt}(\lambda)^{w_{0}}=\mathcal{D}^{\lambda}(-1).

Both sides of this identity have natural interpretations in our setup. Indeed, the left-hand side

is the number of
\sigma^{*}
-fixed points in the fibre over a generic
\sigma^{*}
-fixed base point. Moreover, still assuming that
\sigma
is inner to a split real structure, we will show that
\sigma^{*}

acts on
\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})
as
(-1)^{\deg}
, that is, it acts like
-1\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
through the
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action corresponding to the natural grading on
\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})
. The same is then true for the restriction of
\sigma^{*}
to the functions on a fibre as above. Moreover, that function ring, denoted
\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}_{x}(\mathfrak{g})
, inherits the Poincaré polynomial
\mathcal{D}^{\lambda}
from
\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})
– now as a graded

-vector space. Thus,

is simply the trace of

on

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a complex semisimple Lie algebra and
\lambda
a minuscule weight of
\mathfrak{g}
. Further, let

its Langlands dual, and
\sigma
a split real structure of

\mathcal{D}^{\lambda}(-1)=\#(\pi^{-1}(x))^{\sigma^{*}}=\#\operatorname{wt}(\lambda)^{w_{0}},

where

\lambda
is fixed by a quasi-compact real structure inner to
\sigma

An analogous analysis is possible for involutions induced by non-split inner classes, although the action on
\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})
can be more complicated than

[

Conventions

In this paper, all cohomology is taken with complex coefficients.

Given an action of a group
G
on a space or algebraic structure
X
, the fixed points are denoted by
X^{G}
. Similarly, if
f
is an automorphism of
X
, the fixed points under
f
are denoted
X^{f}
. However, if
\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie algebra acting linearly on a vector space
V
, then
V^{\mathfrak{g}}
denotes the subspace of elements annihilated by
\mathfrak{g}
(so
V^{\mathfrak{g}}=V^{G}
for the corresponding action of the connected simply connected Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
).

Although our main results are for semisimple complex Lie algebras/groups, we will also need to incorporate the reductive case, for which we use the following standard conventions. A Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
of a reductive Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is the direct sum of the centre
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
and a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}^{ss}
of the semisimple part
\mathfrak{g}^{ss}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
. The roots and Weyl group of
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
\mathfrak{h}
are defined in terms of
(\mathfrak{g}^{ss},\mathfrak{h}^{ss})
and extended in the obvious way. Thus, if
G
is reductive with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and
H\leq G
is the Cartan subgroup corresponding to
\mathfrak{h}
, then the Weyl group is isomorphic to
N_{G}(H)/H
if
G
is connected but can be strictly smaller otherwise.

For semisimple
\mathfrak{g}
, we freely use the canonical
\mathfrak{g}
-equivariant isomorphism
\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
afforded by the Killing form. In particular, we freely view the Weyl group with respect to a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
as a subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{h})
.

We freely identify (co)weights of a complex Lie group
G
with their induced (co)weights of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
(and vice versa when lifts exist). As mentioned previously, we do not specify a choice of Cartan (or Borel) subalgebra when talking about weights, roots, etc., unless necessary. The
weight spaces
of a representation are by convention at least one-dimensional.

A homomorphism of algebras
A\to B
is called
free
if it equips
B
with the structure of a free
A
-module.

2.
Equivariant cohomology, Kirillov algebras and coinvariant rings

In this section, we provide further details on relevant background and context. We start with a brief review of equivariant cohomology, a cohomology theory for spaces with group actions.

Let
G
be a topological group and

and drop this from the notation. It is clear from the definition that

.

Moreover, equivariant cohomology is functorial for morphisms of spaces with group action. That is, let

a left

a group homomorphism, and

f(g\cdot x)=\alpha(g)\cdot f(x)

. Then the pair

induces a morphism of algebras

(11)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{*}_{H}(Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{*}_{G}(X)}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{*}_{G}}

3.
Action of real structures

Here we review real structures of complex Lie groups and describe their action on partial flag varieties. We then study the resulting involutions on equivariant cohomology. We start by setting up definitions, referring to
[
23
, §2–5]
or
[

Definition 3.1
.

A
real form
of a complex Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a real Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
whose complexification
\mathfrak{g}_{0}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{g}
(as a complex Lie algebra).
A
real structure
of a complex Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a conjugate-linear Lie algebra automorphism
\sigma\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
of order two.

If
\sigma
is a real structure on a complex Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, then one readily checks that the fixed points
\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}
make up a real form of
\mathfrak{g}
. Conversely, for a real form
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
the complex conjugation on
\mathfrak{g}_{0}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
can be used to define a corresponding real structure on
\mathfrak{g}
(uniquely up to an automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
). This way, real forms and real structures are equivalent. The latter concept generalises more conveniently to complex Lie groups:

Definition 3.2
.

A
real structure
of a complex Lie group
G
is an antiholomorphic automorphism of order two.

For our purposes, real structures on the group level are equivalent to those on the Lie algebra level:

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G
be a complex Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Any real structure on
G
defines a real structure on
\mathfrak{g}
by differentiation, and this assignment is injective if
G
is connected. Conversely, if
G
is connected and either simply connected or of adjoint type, then any real structure on
\mathfrak{g}
integrates to a unique real structure on
G
.

Proof.

The passage from the group level to the Lie algebra level is standard. It is also clear that real structures of
\mathfrak{g}
lift to
G
when
G
is connected and simply connected. For the adjoint type case, it suffices to lift to a simply connected covering group
\tilde{G}
and to observe that this lift preserves the centre of
\tilde{G}
.
∎

Several equivalence relations on the set of real structures are commonly used (though their names are not entirely standardised):

\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{C}^{\times},H)\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{C}^{\times},H),\quad\lambda\mapsto\sigma_{*}\lambda\coloneqq\sigma\circ\lambda\circ\overline{(\cdot)},

Remark 3.9
.

(Notions of preserved orbits)

(1)

The explicit choice of Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
(and Cartan subgroup

acts on these by

by replacing

with an inner-isomorphic real structure. If
\lambda
takes values in a Cartan subgroup

\sigma

\sigma
preserves the

if and only if it preserves its Weyl group orbit.

(2)

Note further that these notions of preserving orbits depend only on the inner class of
\sigma
. More precisely, for
\phi\in\operatorname{Int}(G)
, it is clear that
\sigma\phi
preserves the
G

if and only if
\sigma
does. If both
\sigma
and
\sigma\phi
preserve a Cartan subgroup

such that
\lambda
takes values in
H
, then

so that
(\sigma\phi)_{*}\lambda\in W\cdot\lambda
if and only if
\sigma_{*}\lambda\in W\cdot\lambda
. Now, if

is a Borel subgroup containing

such that
\lambda
is dominant with respect to

\sigma^{qs}
inner to

which preserves both

and

(see e. g.

\sigma^{s}
preserving

and a pinning-preserving holomorphic involution

such that
\sigma^{qs}=\eta\sigma^{s}
. We then have
\sigma^{s}_{*}\lambda=\lambda
(irrespective of

\sigma_{*}\lambda\in W\cdot\lambda\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\sigma^{qs}_{*}\lambda=\lambda\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\eta\circ\lambda=\lambda.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
\sigma
be a real structure of
G
which preserves the Cartan subgroup
H
as well as the Weyl group orbit of a cocharacter
\lambda\in\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{C}^{\times},H)
. Choose
s\in N_{G}(H)
such that
\sigma_{*}\lambda=\operatorname{Conj}_{s}\circ\lambda
. Then

\underline{\sigma}(gP_{\lambda})\coloneqq\sigma(g)sP_{\lambda},\quad g\in G,

defines an antiholomorphic involution
\underline{\sigma}
of
G/P_{\lambda}
which does not depend on the choice of
s
.

Proof.

Firstly, for
g\in G
and
h\in P_{\lambda}
we have

\sigma(gh)sP_{\lambda}=\sigma(g)\sigma(h)sP_{\lambda}=\sigma(g)s\operatorname{Conj}_{s^{-1}}(\sigma(h))P_{\lambda}=\sigma(g)sP_{\lambda}

because
\operatorname{Conj}_{s}(P_{\lambda})=\sigma(P_{\lambda})
, so
\underline{\sigma}(gP_{\lambda})
is well-defined.

Secondly, for another
s^{\prime}\in N_{G}(H)
with
\operatorname{Conj}_{s^{\prime}}\circ\lambda=\sigma_{*}\lambda
, we have

s^{\prime}P_{\lambda}=s(s^{-1}s^{\prime})P_{\lambda}=sP_{\lambda}

since
\operatorname{Conj}_{s^{-1}s^{\prime}}(P_{\lambda})=P_{\lambda}
and
P_{\lambda}
is self-normalising (forcing
s^{-1}s^{\prime}\in P_{\lambda})
. This shows that
\underline{\sigma}
does not depend on the choice of
s
.

Lastly, the assumption
\operatorname{Conj}_{s}\circ\lambda=\sigma_{*}\lambda
implies
\operatorname{Conj}_{\sigma(s)s}\circ\lambda=\lambda
. As before, we conclude that
\sigma(s)s\in P_{\lambda}
. Then

\underline{\sigma}^{2}(gP_{\lambda})=g\sigma(s)sP_{\lambda}=gP_{\lambda},\quad g\in G

verifies that
\underline{\sigma}
is indeed an involution. Antiholomorphicity is easily checked by lifting
\underline{\sigma}
to
G
.

Remark 3.11
.

The real structure
\sigma
induces a real structure
\widetilde{\sigma}
on the affine Grassmannian
\operatorname{Gr}=\operatorname{Gr}_{G}
of
G
. The affine Schubert varieties of
\operatorname{Gr}
can be labelled by
W

\widetilde{\sigma}
permutes the affine Schubert varieties as
\sigma_{*}
permutes these
W
-orbits. In particular,
\widetilde{\sigma}
preserves the affine Schubert variety
\operatorname{Gr}^{\leq\lambda}
if and only if
\sigma_{*}\lambda\in W\cdot\lambda
. If this is the case,
\widetilde{\sigma}
restricts to a real structure of
\operatorname{Gr}^{\leq\lambda}
. Now, if
\lambda
is in addition minuscule, then
\operatorname{Gr}^{\leq\lambda}
is canonically isomorphic to

, and the isomorphism identifies
\widetilde{\sigma}
with the real structure
\underline{\sigma}
of Proposition
3.10
.

As an antiholomorphic involution,
\underline{\sigma}
fixes a real submanifold of
G/P_{\lambda}
whose real dimension is
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}G/P_{\lambda}
. However, it turns out that this submanifold can be empty:

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
G
,
\sigma
, and
\lambda
be as in Proposition

. Then

(G/P_{\lambda})^{\underline{\sigma}}=\begin{cases}G^{\sigma}/P_{\lambda}^{\sigma}&\text{if }\sigma_{*}\lambda=\lambda\\
\emptyset&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

The key observation is that
(G/P_{\lambda})^{\underline{\sigma}}
is a closed union of
G^{\sigma}
-orbits in
G/P_{\lambda}
. Thus, it is either empty or contains the distinguished orbit through
eP_{\lambda}
(where
e\in G
is the neutral element)
[
32
, Cor. 3.4]
. But
\underline{\sigma}(eP_{\lambda})=sP_{\lambda}
for
s\in N_{G}(H)
with
\operatorname{Conj}_{s}\circ\lambda=\sigma_{*}\lambda
. This equals
eP_{\lambda}
precisely when
s\in P_{\lambda}
, which is equivalent to
\sigma_{*}\lambda=\lambda
.

In case
\sigma_{*}\lambda=\lambda
, we have seen that the fixed submanifold contains the distinguished orbit
G^{\sigma}\cdot(eP_{\lambda})=G^{\sigma}/P_{\lambda}^{\sigma}
. But
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(G/P_{\lambda})^{\underline{\sigma}}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(G/P_{\lambda})
is the minimal dimension of

-orbits
[
32
, Thm. 3.6]
, which is attained only by the distinguished orbit
[

, Cor. 3.4]
, so the fixed point set consists of that orbit alone.

We will be interested in the action of

4.
Invariant rings and quasi-compact real structures

Lemma
3.14
translates the involutions (
15
) we are interested in to the setting of invariant polynomials. In this section, we collect important facts about invariant polynomials, most importantly the key Lemma
4.7
. This result motivates the use of quasi-compact real structures, as discussed at the end of the section. We begin by reiterating a definition from the previous section:

Definition 4.1
.

Let
G
be a complex Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Then
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{G}
denotes the subring of
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]\coloneqq S(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
consisting of elements invariant under the canonical
G
-action. Analogously,
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{\mathfrak{g}}
denotes the subring of
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]
consisting of elements annihilated by
\mathfrak{g}
.

Remark 4.2
.

By definition of
\operatorname{Int}(\mathfrak{g})
, we have
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{\operatorname{Int}(\mathfrak{g})}
. If
G
is connected, the adjoint action
G\to\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
has values in
\operatorname{Int}(\mathfrak{g})
, so
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{G}=\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{\mathfrak{g}}
. For disconnected
G
,
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{G}
can be strictly smaller than
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{\mathfrak{g}}
, see Example
4.9
below.

An important classical result about invariant polynomials is the following theorem of Chevalley:

Theorem 4.3

(cf.
[
31
]
, Thm. 4.9.2)
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a semisimple complex Lie algebra,
\mathfrak{h}\leq\mathfrak{g}
a Cartan subalgebra, and
W
the corresponding Weyl group. Then the canonical restriction map
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}]^{W}\coloneqq S(\mathfrak{h}^{*})^{W}
is an isomorphism.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
G
be a complex reductive Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\mathfrak{h}\leq\mathfrak{g}
be a Cartan subalgebra, and
N_{G}(\mathfrak{h})
its normaliser. Then restriction defines an isomorphism

\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{G}\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}]^{N_{G}(\mathfrak{h})}.

Proof of Corollary.

Let
G_{0}
denote the identity component of
G
. Using the usual decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
into its centre and derived subalgebra, Theorem
4.3
extends at once to

\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{G_{0}}\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}]^{W}.

To obtain the
G-
invariants, we now have to take into account the action of the component group
G/G_{0}
. But it is easy to see that
N_{G}(\mathfrak{h})
meets all components of
G
, so

\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{G}=(\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{G_{0}})^{G/G_{0}}=(\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{G_{0}})^{N_{G}(\mathfrak{h})}\cong(\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}]^{W})^{N_{G}(\mathfrak{h})}=\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}]^{N_{G}(\mathfrak{h})}.

∎

Having recalled this tool, we now return to the study of involutions on invariant polynomial rings. Directly from the definition, we obtain the following counterpart of Lemma

Let

This suggests to look for particularly well-behaved involutions in a given inner class, and a natural candidate are those that preserve a pinning (see Definition

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a complex reductive Lie algebra and
\theta\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
.

(i)

The fixed subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\theta}
is reductive.

(ii)

If
\mathfrak{h}
is a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
preserved by
\theta
such that

is maximal among such Cartan subalgebras, then

is a Cartan subalgebra of

.

(iii)

If
\mathfrak{g}
is semisimple and
\theta
preserves a pinning

is semisimple with Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}^{\theta}
. Moreover, if

is the Weyl group of

, then its subgroup

is identified with the Weyl group of

by restriction to
\mathfrak{h}^{\theta}
.

For part (i), note that
\theta
preserves the direct sum decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
into its centre
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
and derived subalgebra
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
. The Killing form of
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
restricts to a nondegenerate

-invariant symmetric bilinear form of

, which shows that

is reductive. It follows that
\mathfrak{g}^{\theta}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})^{\theta}\oplus[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]^{\theta}
is reductive as well.

To see that

of part (ii) is a Cartan subalgebra, one can for instance use Gantmacher’s normal form, cf.

For part (iii), we refer to

.

.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a complex reductive Lie algebra and

Let

\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{\mathfrak{g}}\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})]\otimes\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}_{\text{der}}]^{\mathfrak{g}_{\text{der}}}.

Clearly, it then suffices to prove the lemma for

and

separately. For the affine space
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
it follows immediately from Proposition

, so for the remainder we may assume that

is semisimple.

Now let

preserved by

is then equivalent to the restriction

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
G
be a connected complex reductive Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. If

\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}^{\theta}]^{G^{\theta}}=\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}^{\theta}]^{\mathfrak{g}^{\theta}}.

Proof.

We have to show that every

acts trivially on every

. By Lemma

,

admits an extension
\tilde{f}\in\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{\mathfrak{g}}
with
\tilde{f}|_{\mathfrak{g}^{\theta}}=f

\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{G}
, so

. But this implies

, too.

The point of the preceding corollary is that it holds even though

Proof of main theorem

Lemma 5.1

.

Since

, it suffices to show that

. (Indeed, any automorphism of

The behaviour of the second map in (

Theorem 6.2

If

Proof.

The first statement is classical: the “only if” part follows from vanishing of the Euler characteristic
\chi(X)
in the nonequal rank case first shown by Hopf and Samelson
[
16
]
. The “if” part is due to Borel
[
4
]
. For a more detailed discussion, see
[
6
, ch. 5]
. The statement about equivariant cohomology (known as
equivariant formality
) follows from degeneracy of the Serre spectral sequence for the fibration
X_{K}\to\mathbb{B}K
with fibre
X
, cf.
[
6
, ch. 9]
.
∎

Returning to the analysis before Theorem
6.2
, we are led to compare the ranks of
K=((G^{\vee})^{\sigma})^{\tau}
and
L=L_{\lambda}^{\sigma}\cap K
. We translate the question to the complex setting via the Cartan involution
\theta\coloneqq\sigma\tau
, noting that
K
and
L
are compact real forms of
(G^{\vee})^{\theta}
and
L_{\lambda}^{\theta}
, respectively. The theorem then implies:

Lemma 6.3
.

If
(G^{\vee})^{\theta}
and
L_{\lambda}^{\theta}
have the same rank, then

H^{2*}_{(G^{\vee})^{\sigma}}((G^{\vee})^{\sigma}/P_{\lambda}^{\sigma})

is a free module over
H^{2*}_{(G^{\vee})^{\sigma}}
. Otherwise,
H^{2*}_{(G^{\vee})^{\sigma}}
has strictly larger transcendence degree than
H^{2*}_{(G^{\vee})^{\sigma}}((G^{\vee})^{\sigma}/P_{\lambda}^{\sigma})
, so the module structure cannot be free.

Proof.

The first assertion indeed follows immediately from Theorem
6.2
. For the statement about transcendence degrees, let
\mathfrak{h}\leq(\mathfrak{g}^{\vee})^{\theta}
and
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}\leq\mathfrak{l}_{\lambda}^{\theta}
be Cartan subalgebras. By Corollary
4.4
, the rings involved are invariant subrings of
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}]
and
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}]
by finite groups.
16
16

16

Finiteness follows from the well-known finiteness of Weyl groups and the fact that
G^{\theta}
and
L_{\lambda}^{\theta}
have finitely many connected components. One way to see this is that they are homotopic to their intersections with the compact group
K
.
Their transcendence degrees are then the dimensions of
\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
, respectively. If
H^{2*}_{(G^{\vee})^{\sigma}}
has strictly larger transcendence degree than
H^{2*}_{(G^{\vee})^{\sigma}}((G^{\vee})^{\sigma}/P_{\lambda}^{\sigma})
, the structure map
H^{2*}_{(G^{\vee})^{\sigma}}\to H^{2*}_{(G^{\vee})^{\sigma}}((G^{\vee})^{\sigma}/P_{\lambda}^{\sigma})
is not injective, so it does not define a free module structure.
∎

We are thus led to compare Cartan subalgebras of
(\mathfrak{g}^{\vee})^{\theta}
and
\mathfrak{l}_{\lambda}^{\theta}
, with the following result:

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a complex reductive Lie algebra and
\theta\in\operatorname{Aut}_{2}(\mathfrak{g})
. Among Cartan subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
preserved by
\theta
, choose
\mathfrak{h}
such that
\dim\mathfrak{h}^{\theta}
is minimal. Let
\lambda
be a minuscule coweight of
\mathfrak{g}
contained in
\mathfrak{h}^{-\theta}
(i. e.
\theta(\lambda)=-\lambda
) and
\mathfrak{l}_{\lambda}
the corresponding Levi subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. If
\theta
preserves a pinning of
\mathfrak{l}_{\lambda}
, then
\mathfrak{g}^{\theta}
and
\mathfrak{l}_{\lambda}^{\theta}
have equal rank if and only if
\theta
is also pinning-preserving for
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{l}_{\lambda}
contains the centre of
\mathfrak{g}
, we may assume that
\mathfrak{g}
is semisimple. The ranks of
\mathfrak{g}^{\theta}
and
\mathfrak{l}_{\lambda}^{\theta}
are the maximal dimensions of
\mathfrak{t}^{\theta}
for
\theta
-stable Cartan subalgebras
\mathfrak{t}
of
\mathfrak{g}
or of
\mathfrak{l}_{\lambda}
, respectively (cf.  Lemma
4.6
). In particular, both ranks admit the lower bound
\dim\mathfrak{h}^{\theta}
.

For convenience, we now translate to the parallel setting of real structures, which is more easily found in the literature. That is, we fix a compatible compact real structure
\tau
of
\mathfrak{g}
and define
\sigma\coloneqq\tau\theta=\theta\tau
. Then
\mathfrak{t}
as above correspond to
maximally compact
Cartan subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}^{\sigma}
or
\mathfrak{l}_{\lambda}^{\sigma}
.

The conjugacy classes of Cartan subalgebras of a real reductive Lie algebra can be related by
Cayley transforms
, which are defined using
real or noncompact imaginary roots
, cf.
[
19
, p. 390]
. In particular, if there are no such roots with respect to
\sigma
, then there is only one such conjugacy class. If the Cartan involution
\theta
preserves a pinning
(\mathfrak{t},\Pi,\{X_{\alpha}\})
, there are no real roots with respect to
\mathfrak{t}
and no noncompact imaginary simple roots in
\Pi
. Now suppose that, additionally,
\theta^{*}\alpha
is either equal to or orthogonal to
\alpha
for every simple
\alpha
. Using
[
19
, Prop. 6.104]
, this implies that there are no real or noncompact imaginary roots at all, so all Cartan subalgebras of of the real form are conjugate. Since
\theta
acts on the simple roots according to an automorphism of the Dynkin diagram, this assumption holds in all simple types except for the nontrivial involution of type
A_{2n}
.

Thus, suppose that
\mathfrak{l}_{\lambda}
has no simple summand of type
A_{2n}
on which
\theta
restricts to a nontrivial involution. Then the above analysis implies that
\mathfrak{l}_{\lambda}^{\sigma}
has only one conjugacy class of
\theta
-stable Cartan subalgebras. In particular
\mathfrak{h}^{\theta}
must be a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{l}_{\lambda}^{\theta}
, and so
\operatorname{rank}\mathfrak{l}_{\lambda}^{\theta}=\dim\mathfrak{h}^{\theta}
. A similar analysis shows that if
\theta
is pinning-preserving on
\mathfrak{g}
and there are no interfering type
A_{2n}
summands,
\mathfrak{g}^{\theta}
has rank
\dim\mathfrak{h}^{\theta}
as well. To finish the proof we thus have to do the following:

(1)

Show that a non-quasi-compact real form of a complex reductive Lie algebra always admits more than one isomorphism class of Cartan subalgebras. Since only one of them is maximally compact
[
19
, Prop. 6.61]
, it will follow that
\operatorname{rank}\mathfrak{g}^{\theta}>\dim\mathfrak{h}^{\theta}
in this case.

(2)

Check the cases involving type
A_{2n}
summands with non-inner Cartan involutions separately.

For task (1), it suffices to show that such a real form always has noncompact imaginary roots with respect to a maximally compact Cartan subalgebra. This follows from the classification using
Vogan diagrams
, cf.
[
19
, VI.8]
, since the absence of noncompact imaginary roots would imply quasi-compactness (by uniqueness of the classification).

For task (2), we may assume that
\mathfrak{g}
is simple. For
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2n+1}
, one finds that no non-inner involution restricts to a pinning preserving involution on any
\mathfrak{l}_{\lambda}
defined as above. Thus, we only have to treat the cases where
\mathfrak{l}_{\lambda}
has some type
A_{2n}
summand on which the Cartan involution acts nontrivially. Up to isomorphism, they are as follows:

•

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2n}(\mathbb{C})
with
\mathfrak{l}_{\lambda}=\mathfrak{s}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2k+1}\oplus\mathfrak{gl}_{2n-2k-1})(\mathbb{C})
for
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
;
\mathfrak{g}^{\theta}=\mathfrak{so}_{2n}(\mathbb{C})
, and
\mathfrak{l}_{\lambda}^{\theta}=\mathfrak{so}_{2k+1}(\mathbb{C})\oplus\mathfrak{so}_{2n-2k-1}(\mathbb{C})
.
Here
\theta
is not pinning-preserving on
\mathfrak{g}
and the fixed subalgebras have ranks
n
and
n-1
, respectively.

•

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sp}_{4n+2}(\mathbb{C})
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
\mathfrak{l}_{\lambda}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2n+1}(\mathbb{C})
;
\mathfrak{g}^{\theta}=\mathfrak{gl}_{2n+1}(\mathbb{C})
and
\mathfrak{l}_{\lambda}^{\theta}=\mathfrak{so}_{2n+1}(\mathbb{C})
.
Here,
\theta
is again not pinning-preserving on
\mathfrak{g}
, and the fixed subalgebras have ranks
2n+1
and
n
, respectively.

∎

Remark 6.5
.

It seems likely that Lemma
6.4
can be proven more systematically and with fewer assumptions, perhaps using qualitative properties of Cayley transforms, cf.
[

.

We now collect the auxiliary results of this section into a proof of Theorem
1.2
, which characterises freeness of the coinvariant homomorphism (

).

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

For
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2n+1}(\mathbb{C})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, with
\mathfrak{S}
containing a split real structure, the Theorem can be checked directly. Here all elements of the inner class are quasi-compact, and a simple computation (as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
) shows that the coinvariant homomorphism (
4
) is indeed free in this case. For the remainder of the proof, we assume that no element of
\mathfrak{S}
restricts to a split real structure on a type
A_{2n}
factor of
G^{\vee}
.

It is then clear from the proof of Theorem
1.1
that we may choose
\sigma=\sigma_{0}
whenever a quasi-compact real structure in the given inner class fixes
\lambda
. If this is the case, the first map
\varphi
in (
20
) is an identity. Moreover, the second map is then free (i. e. defines a free module structure) by Lemmas
6.4
and
6.3
.

On the other hand, if
\sigma
is not quasi-compact, then Lemmas
6.4
and
6.3
imply that the transcendence degree drops in the second step of (
20
). By Lemma
6.1
, it stays the same in the first step, so overall the coinvariant homomorphism decreases transcendence degree. In particular, it is not injective, hence cannot give rise to a free module structure.
∎

7.
Action on fibres and weights

In this section, we relate the involutions discussed above to combinatorial information about the representation
V^{\lambda}
. In the general case, where
\lambda
is any dominant integral weight, one should work with the big algebra
[
14
]
, whose fibres over suitable points of
\operatorname{Spec}S(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}}
can be identified with the canonical basis of
V^{\lambda}
(cf.
[
15
, final slide]
, see also
[
12
]
). For simplicity, we instead (continue to) restrict ourselves to the case where
\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})
is commutative and thus agrees with the big algebra (cf.
[
14
, Thm.2.1]
).
By Proposition
2.2
, this means that
V^{\lambda}
is weight multiplicity free, so instead of the canonical basis we can work with just the set of weights
\operatorname{wt}(\lambda)
. We begin this section by describing how to identify
\operatorname{wt}(\lambda)
with fibres of the map

(21)

\pi\colon\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})\to\operatorname{Spec}S(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}}.

For involutions induced by real structures, we then describe how the fixed points of the action on weights are encoded by the coinvariant homomorphism (
4
). This is applied to the special case of split real structures, where we recover (the weight multiplicity free case of) a
q=-1
phenomenon of Stembridge
[
29
,
30
]
. Along the way, we obtain a combinatorial obstruction to freeness of the coinvariant homomorphism (
4
), see Proposition
7.5
. In all of this section, we
assume that
V^{\lambda}
is weight multiplicity free
. For Theorem
1.3
, proved below,
\lambda
is assumed minuscule.

The starting point is to consider, for
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, the evaluation homomorphism
S(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}}\cong S(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathbb{C}
, in which we first use the Killing form to view
S(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}}
as
\mathfrak{g}
-invariant polynomials on
\mathfrak{g}
and then evaluate these on
\mathfrak{g}
. Applying this to the first factor of
\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})
, we obtain a map

\operatorname{ev}_{x}\colon\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})=(S(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\operatorname{End}(V^{\lambda}))^{\mathfrak{g}}\to\operatorname{End}(V^{\lambda}).

The image of
\operatorname{ev}_{x}
is a subalgebra of
\operatorname{End}(V^{\lambda})
which we denote by

\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}_{x}(\mathfrak{g})\coloneqq\operatorname{ev}_{x}(\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})).

Let us now assume that
x
is a regular semisimple element. Recall that its centraliser
\mathfrak{h}\coloneqq\mathfrak{c}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)
is then a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. From the definition of
\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})
, we see that
\operatorname{ev}_{x}
then has values in
(\operatorname{End}(V^{\lambda}))^{\mathfrak{h}}=\operatorname{End}_{\mathfrak{h}}(V^{\lambda})
, the endomorphisms of
V^{\lambda}
which commute with the action of
\mathfrak{h}
. For such
x
,
\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}_{x}(\mathfrak{g})
turns out to describe a fibre of the map
\pi
of (
21
):

Proposition 7.1
.

If
x\in\mathfrak{g}
is a regular semisimple element with corresponding Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}\coloneqq\mathfrak{c}_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)
, then
\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}_{x}(\mathfrak{g})=\operatorname{End}_{\mathfrak{h}}(V^{\lambda})
. Since
V^{\lambda}
is weight multiplicity free, it follows that
\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}_{x}(\mathfrak{g})\cong\mathbb{C}^{\operatorname{wt}(\lambda)}
. Moreover, viewing
x
as a closed point of
\operatorname{Spec}S(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}}\cong\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{\mathfrak{g}}\cong\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}]^{W}
, we further have
\pi^{-1}(x)\cong\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}_{x}(\mathfrak{g}))
. Thus,
\pi^{-1}(x)
is a zero-dimensional reduced scheme with underlying set
\operatorname{wt}(\lambda)
.

Proof.

As observed above,
\operatorname{ev}_{x}
takes values in
\operatorname{End}_{\mathfrak{h}}(V^{\lambda})
, so the first assertion reduces to surjectivity of
\operatorname{ev}_{x}\colon\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})\to\operatorname{End}_{\mathfrak{h}}(V^{\lambda})
. This surjectivity follows from
[
25
, Thm. 1.4]
(see also
[
24
]
) and the fact that the
G
-orbit of
x
is closed in
\mathfrak{g}
.

For the second assertion, note that endomorphisms in
\operatorname{End}_{\mathfrak{h}}(V^{\lambda})
have to send each weight space to itself. By weight multiplicity freeness, each weight space is one-dimensional, so such endomorphisms are diagonal with respect to the weight space decomposition.

It remains to show that
\pi^{-1}(x)
is isomorphic to the spectrum of
\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}_{x}(\mathfrak{g})
. Let
\mathfrak{m}_{x}\subset\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{\mathfrak{g}}
be the kernel of the evaluation homomorphism taking
f\in\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{\mathfrak{g}}
to
f(x)\in\mathbb{C}
. Then, by definition, we have

\pi^{-1}(x)=\operatorname{Spec}\big(\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{\mathfrak{g}}}(\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{\mathfrak{g}}/\mathfrak{m}_{x})\big)=\operatorname{Spec}\big(\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})/(\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})\cdot\mathfrak{m}_{x})\big),

whereas

\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}_{x}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})/\ker(\operatorname{ev}_{x}).

Since
(\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})\cdot\mathfrak{m}_{x})
is clearly contained in
\ker(\operatorname{ev}_{x})
, there is a canonical surjection

\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{\mathfrak{g}}}(\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{\mathfrak{g}}/\mathfrak{m}_{x})\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}_{x}(\mathfrak{g}).

But these are finite dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-algebras, and

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\big(\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{\mathfrak{g}}}(\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{\mathfrak{g}}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}))=\operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{\mathfrak{g}}}(\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g}))=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\operatorname{End}_{\mathfrak{h}}(V^{\lambda}))=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}_{x}(\mathfrak{g})

(for the second equality, see
[
18
, p. 4, above Cor. 1]
), so the surjection has to be an isomorphism.
∎

Thus, the map
\pi
of (
21
) has reduced fibres over all regular semisimple points in the base. We will also require the base points to be fixed by the involutions under consideration, which can be arranged without breaking the good fibre behaviour. Indeed, if
\sigma^{*}
is an involution of the algebra
S(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})
induced by a real structure (see Section
3
), then the action of
\sigma^{*}
on the base ring
S(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}}
is by a complex linear involution
\theta
of
\mathfrak{g}
(cf.  Lemma
3.14
). Moreover, only the inner class of
\theta
matters, so we may assume that
\theta
preserves a pinning. As observed in the proof of Lemma
4.7
, a pinning-preserving automorphism fixes a regular semisimple element of
\mathfrak{g}
and we conclude:

Lemma 7.2
.

For an involution
\sigma^{*}
induced by a real structure, there is a dense open subset
U
of the fixed point scheme
(\operatorname{Spec}S(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}})^{\sigma^{*}}
such that
\pi^{-1}(x)\cong\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}^{\operatorname{wt}(\lambda)}
for every
x\in U
.

Proof.

We may take
U
to be the intersection of
(\operatorname{Spec}S(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}})^{\sigma^{*}}
with the open subset of
\operatorname{Spec}S(\mathfrak{g})\cong\mathfrak{g}
of regular semisimple elements. Then
U
is open in
(\operatorname{Spec}S(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}})^{\sigma^{*}}
, hence dense open (by irreducibility) since it is nonempty by the discussion above.
∎

For
x
as in the lemma, the involution
\sigma^{*}
restricts to an involution of
\pi^{-1}(x)
, given by an involutive permutation of the underlying set
\operatorname{wt}(\lambda)
. By Proposition
3.16
,
\sigma^{*}
is of the form
\iota_{\eta,-1}
for a certain
\eta\in\operatorname{Aut}_{2}(\mathfrak{g})
, allowing for a convenient description of the induced permutation:

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
\eta\in\operatorname{Aut}_{2}(\mathfrak{g})
such that
\eta^{*}\lambda=\lambda
(with notation as in Proposition
3.15
(i)). By replacing
\eta
with an involution in the same inner class if necessary, we may assume that there exists a regular semisimple
x\in\mathfrak{g}
such that
\eta(x)=-x
. The restriction of
\iota\coloneqq\iota_{\eta,-1}
to the fibre
\pi^{-1}(x)\cong\operatorname{wt}(\lambda)
is then given by

\operatorname{wt}(\lambda)\to\operatorname{wt}(\lambda),\quad\mu\mapsto\mu\circ\eta.

Proof.

To arrange
\eta(x)=-x
for some regular semisimple
x
, take
\eta
to be a
principal involution
in the given inner class – see
[
2
, Def. 6.13 and Thm. 6.14]
. The involution of
\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}_{x}(\mathfrak{g})
induced by

is then simply
\operatorname{Conj}_{A_{\eta,\lambda}}
, which is easily seen to act on the weights as claimed.
∎

Since these fibres are zero-dimensional and reduced, it is easy to count the fixed points:

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
A
be a finite-dimensional reduced commutative
\mathbb{C}
-algebra and
\iota
an algebra automorphism of
A
, corresponding to an automorphism of
\operatorname{Spec}A
also denoted
\iota
. Then

\#(\operatorname{Spec}A)^{\iota}=\operatorname{tr}\iota=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}A_{\iota},

where
A_{\iota}
denotes the coinvariant ring (see Proposition
2.6
).

Proof.

The assumptions imply that
X\coloneqq\operatorname{Spec}A
is finite and reduced, so
A\cong\mathbb{C}^{X}
. In particular,
A
has a
\mathbb{C}
-basis consisting of functions
\delta_{x}
which are
1
on
x\in X
and zero on
X\setminus\{x\}
. Moreover,
\iota
permutes these basis functions, so that

\operatorname{tr}\iota=\#\{x\in X\mid\delta_{x}\circ\iota=\delta_{x}\}=\#(\operatorname{Spec}A)^{\iota}.

The span of the fixed basis functions can be identified (as a vector space) with

\mathbb{C}^{X}/\big(\mathbb{C}\cdot\{\delta_{x}\mid\delta_{x}\circ\iota\neq\delta_{x}\}\big)\cong\mathbb{C}^{X}/\big(\mathbb{C}^{X}\cdot\{\delta_{x}\mid\delta_{x}\circ\iota\neq\delta_{x}\}\big)\cong A_{\iota},

which finishes the proof.
∎

Thus, the number of fixed points in a suitable fibre
\pi^{-1}(x)
is given by
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}_{x}(\mathfrak{g})_{\sigma^{*}}
. If the full coinvariant ring
\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})_{\sigma^{*}}
is already free over
(S(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}})_{\sigma^{*}}
then that dimension is equal to its rank. Note that in this case the corresponding morphism on spectra is surjective, so in particular
\pi^{-1}(x)^{\sigma^{*}}\neq\emptyset
. Conversely, if
\pi^{-1}(x)^{\sigma^{*}}=\emptyset
, then the same argument shows that the coinvariant homomorphism (
4
) is not injective. We can phrase this as a simple obstruction to freeness, shedding some light on Theorem
1.2
:

Proposition 7.5
.

Let
\iota=\iota_{\eta,-1}
be an involution of
\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})
of the form given in Proposition
3.15
. Assume that
\eta
has a regular semisimple eigenvector in
\mathfrak{g}
with eigenvalue
-1
. If the set

\{\mu\in\operatorname{wt}(\lambda)\mid\mu=\mu\circ\eta\}

is empty, then the coinvariant homomorphism
S(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\iota}\to\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})_{\iota}
is not injective, hence not free.

Proof.

Follows directly from the discussion above and Proposition
7.3
.
∎

Remark 7.6
.

When
\mathfrak{g}
is simple, it is not hard describe the action of
\eta
on weights explicitly (as is done for inner
\eta
below). For instance, if
\eta
is a Chevalley involution (meaning that
\eta
acts as
-1
on a Cartan subalgebra), then this action is simply

. If

is not a weight of

, this corresponds to a non-free coinvariant homomorphism. Along these lines, one could prove combinatorially that the freeness condition in Theorem

is necessary.

For the rest of this section, we
specialise to the case where
\sigma
is a split real structure
.

In this case,
\sigma^{*}=(-1)^{\deg}
according to Proposition
3.16
. To extract information on fixed points in fibres from this fact, let us recall (from Proposition
2.2
) that
\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})
is finite-free over its graded subring
S(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}}
. Thus, there is a graded
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
V
such that
\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})\cong S(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}V
as a graded
S(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}}
-module. Using the description by invariant polynomial rings in Section
4
one can show
[
25
, p. 11]
that
V=V^{\lambda}
with principal grading.
17
17

17

Alternatively, one can use the geometric model in Theorem
2.3
together with the geometric Satake equivalence
[
22
]
to conclude this for all big algebras, in particular for minuscule Kirillov algebras.
This grading is defined in terms of the weight spaces
V^{\lambda}_{\mu}
,
\mu\in\operatorname{wt}{\lambda}
. Namely, all weights of
V^{\lambda}
are obtained from the lowest weight
-\lambda^{*}
by successively adding simple roots; if
k
such additions are needed to obtain
\mu
we place
V^{\lambda}_{\mu}
in degree

. If
\Delta
denotes the root system and
\rho^{\vee}\coloneqq\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}}\alpha^{\vee}
the half sum of positive coroots, then
\langle\rho^{\vee},\alpha\rangle=1
for all simple roots
\alpha
. Thus, the degree of
V^{\lambda}_{\mu}
can be computed as
k=\langle\rho^{\vee},\mu+\lambda^{*}\rangle=\langle\rho^{\vee},\mu+\lambda\rangle
and the Poincaré polynomial for this grading is the
Dynkin polynomial
, cf.
[
25
, §3]

(22)

\mathcal{D}^{\lambda}(q)=\sum_{\mu\in\operatorname{wt}{\lambda}}(\dim V^{\lambda}_{\mu})q^{\langle\mu+\lambda,\rho^{\vee}\rangle}=\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}}\frac{1-q^{\langle\rho+\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle}}{1-q^{\langle\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle}}.

From the graded module isomorphism
\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})\cong S(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}V^{\lambda}
, it follows that
\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}_{x}(\mathfrak{g})\cong\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{S(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}}}\mathbb{C}\cong V^{\lambda}
as a graded
\mathbb{C}
-vector space, for any

. If

is moreover fixed by
\sigma^{*}
, it follows that
\sigma^{*}
restricts to
\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}_{x}(\mathfrak{g})
as
(-1)^{\deg}
. In particular, its trace is given by the Poincaré polynomial evaluated at
-1
. Via Lemma
7.4
we conclude:

Proposition 7.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a complex semisimple Lie algebra,
\lambda

The first claim of the Theorem is that there is a dense open subset

For such a base point

Remark 7.8
.

Instead of the route taken here, one can also observe that the principal grading on
V^{\lambda}
corresponds to weight spaces for a suitable element of the adjoint group
\operatorname{Ad}(\mathfrak{g})
, and that this element is conjugate to a representative of
w_{0}
. This is the original strategy used in
[

]
. The inner automorphism
\eta
, arising in the discussion above, which maps a regular semisimple element to its negative is indeed a special involution in the sense of
[
29
, p. 15]
. However, the approach we have taken here seems more natural in the context of Kirillov algebras.

Finally, we present a slight extension of Theorem
1.3
, which makes some progress towards proving conjectures of Hausel stated in
[

. Following Hausel, we introduce the following variant of (

), a priori as a rational function:

(23)

\mathcal{D}^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{ev}}(q)\coloneqq\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\Delta^{+}\\
\langle\rho+\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle\in 2\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!\big(1-q^{\langle\rho+\lambda,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle}\big)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\Delta^{+}\\
\langle\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle\in 2\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\big(1-q^{\langle\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle}\big)^{-1}.

Two simple facts about this are as follows:

Proposition 7.9
.

(i)

\mathcal{D}^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{ev}}
is a polynomial.

(ii)

\mathcal{D}^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{ev}}(1)=\mathcal{D}^{\lambda}(-1)
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{D}^{\lambda}
is a fraction of products of cyclotomic polynomials by (
22
), it admits a factorisation

\mathcal{D}^{\lambda}(q)=\frac{\prod_{z\in Z_{1}}(q-z)}{\prod_{z\in Z_{2}}(q-z)}

where
Z_{1},Z_{2}
are multisets of roots of unity different from
1
. The multiplicity of each
z
in
Z_{1}
is at least the multiplicity in
Z_{2}
because
\mathcal{D}^{\lambda}
is a polynomial. By removing all powers of odd primitive roots of unity from both
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
, we arrive at a factorisation of
\mathcal{D}^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{ev}}
, which is thus also a polynomial, proving (i). The definition of
\mathcal{D}^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{ev}}
immediately yields that it is an even polynomial, so in particular
\mathcal{D}^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{ev}}(1)=\mathcal{D}^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{ev}}(-1)
. Part (ii) then follows from (i) and the fact that

(1-q^{2k+1})/(1-q)=1+q+\cdots q^{2k}

evaluates to
1
at
q=-1
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
∎

Theorem 7.10
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a complex semisimple Lie algebra and
\lambda
a minuscule weight of
\mathfrak{g}
. Further, let
G
be the connected simply connected Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
,
G^{\vee}
its Langlands dual, and
\sigma
a split real structure of
G^{\vee}
. Then the coinvariant homomorphism
S(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\sigma^{*}}\to\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})_{\sigma^{*}}
is free if and only if
\mathcal{D}^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{ev}}(1)\neq 0
. If this is the case,
\mathcal{D}^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{ev}}
is the Poincaré polynomial of
\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})_{\sigma^{*}}
over
S(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\sigma^{*}}
.

Proof.

The characterisation of freeness follows from Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
and Proposition
7.9
. In the free case, we see from Theorems
1.2
and
1.1
that the coinvariant homomorphism is the canonical map

H^{2*}_{(G^{\vee})^{\sigma}}\to H^{2*}_{(G^{\vee})^{\sigma}}((G^{\vee})^{\sigma}/P_{\lambda}^{\sigma})

for a quasi-compact real structure inner to
\sigma
. Using the identification
\sigma^{*}=(-1)^{\deg}
and unravelling the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we may identify this map with

\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}]^{\mathfrak{g}}_{(-1)^{\deg}}\to\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{l}_{\lambda}]^{\mathfrak{l}_{\lambda}}_{(-1)^{\deg}}.

These are polynomial rings with generators graded in the even degrees of the Weyl groups
W
and
W_{\lambda}
. Therefore, the Poincaré polynomial is given by

P(q)\coloneqq\prod_{d\in D(W)\cap 2\mathbb{Z}}\!\!\!\!(1-q^{d})\prod_{d\in D(W_{\lambda})\cap 2\mathbb{Z}}\!\!\!\!(1-q^{d})^{-1}

where
D(W),D(W_{\lambda})
are the respective multisets of degrees. But the analogous expression

\prod_{d\in D(W)}\!\!\!(1-q^{d})\prod_{d\in D(W_{\lambda})}\!\!\!(1-q^{d})^{-1}

equals
\mathcal{D}^{\lambda}
(see
[
25
, Prop. 3.7]
), so a similar argument as in the proof of Proposition
7.9
shows that
P=\mathcal{D}^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{ev}}
.

Outlook

As mentioned in the introduction, we expect our main results to generalise to Hausel’s

big algebras

[
14
]
. In general, big algebras are maximal commutative subalgebras of Kirillov algebras and need not coincide with their ambient Kirillov algebras. Nevertheless, in the minuscule case they do coincide, and the geometric model used throughout this paper generalises more readily to big algebras (cf. Theorem
2.3
). It therefore seems appropriate in our context to consider big algebras as a generalisation of the minuscule Kirillov algebras studied in this paper. On the geometric side, this replaces equivariant cohomology of partial flag varieties with equivariant intersection cohomology of affine Schubert varieties.

An added difficulty is that this description does not account for the ring structure of big algebras in general, since intersection cohomology need not have such a structure. Nevertheless, there still is a well-defined action of real structures, and we do expect the coinvariant ring to be modelled by a real affine Schubert variety similarly to Theorem
1.1
. Such a geometric model would likely also lead to a characterisation of freeness as in Theorem
1.2
.
However, the relatively elementary methods used here need to be adapted to treat the general case. Let us also note that another direction suggested by Theorem
2.3
is to study the involutions on the centres of general big algebras.

In the special case of a split real structure, Theorem
1.3
of this paper recovers a minuscule
q=-1
phenomenon due to Stembridge
[
29
]
. That work was generalised to arbitrary representations, where one counts fixed elements of the canonical basis instead of fixed weights, see
[
30
]
. We expect the action of split real structures on general big algebras to recover this via a similar analysis as in Section
7
.

Another future direction is to describe the case of non-free coinvariant homomorphisms
S(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\sigma^{*}}\to\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})_{\sigma^{*}}
in more detail. It follows from Lemma
7.2
and the proof of Theorem
1.3
that the image of
\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})_{\sigma^{*}}
in
S(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\sigma^{*}}
must in this case be contained in the vanishing locus
V(D)
of the discriminant. One can then ask for a maximal closed subscheme
Z
of
S(\mathfrak{g})^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\sigma^{*}}
(contained in
V(D)
) such that
\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{C}^{\lambda}(\mathfrak{g})_{\sigma^{*}}
is finite-free over
Z
. The analysis in Section
6
suggests that such a
Z
can be obtained as the spectrum of
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{k}]^{K}
for a suitable (compact) subgroup
K
of
(G^{\vee})^{\sigma_{0}}
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}
, but it is not yet clear to us how to define
K
in a uniform way.

Let us also remark that there is a connection to moduli spaces of Higgs bundles. When
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
A
, the spectra of minuscule Kirillov algebras are isomorphic to
very stable type
(1,\ldots,1)
upward flows
in the moduli space of Higgs bundles
19
19

19

of fixed rank and degree, over a smooth projective curve of genus at least two

[
11
, §4]
. Involutions (and more general automorphisms) on the whole moduli space, resembling the automorphisms
\iota_{\eta,\zeta}
of Proposition
3.15
, have been studied in
[
10
]
. The Kirillov algebra involutions studied here should correspond to such global involutions of the moduli space restricted to suitable upward flows. The case of
\iota_{\operatorname{id},-1}
is discussed in
[
11
]
.

Finally, one can ask how natural our focus on real structures is for the material covered in this paper. Indeed, significant parts of our arguments proceed via the (holomorphic) Cartan involutions corresponding to the real structures. However, the geometric model in Theorem
1.1
does seem most natural when stated through real structures (as opposed to invariant polynomial rings for complex Lie groups). Currently, this provides only heuristic input towards describing the coinvariant homomorphism: namely, it is free precisely when the modelling real partial flag variety is an equal rank homogeneous space. It would be interesting to further incorporate the real geometry into the description.

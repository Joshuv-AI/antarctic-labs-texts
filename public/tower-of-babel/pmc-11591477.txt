Luciferase-mediated assay to detect the PAMP-triggered gene expression in transgenic Nicotiana benthamiana
Authors: Ola Barakat, Fushuang Zhang, Mengzhu Zeng, Yuanchao Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTLuciferase is one of the bioluminescence-producing agents, which was widely used as a reporter enzyme for constructing bioassay systems to study gene expression with high accuracy and within a broad dynamic spectrum. Perception of pathogen associated molecular patterns (PAMPs) in plants often lead to significant transcriptional changes. The transcriptional changes of defense-related genes are often used as a marker to assay PAMP-triggered plant immune response. In this study, we showed that the marker gene CYP71D20 was rapidly activated in Nicotiana benthamiana upon treatment with the bacterial PAMP flg22 and the Phytophthora elicitin INF1. In addition, we generated transgenic N. benthamiana using the luciferase as a reporter gene to analyze CYP71D20 gene expression upon PAMP treatment. The transgenic line carrying the luciferase gene driven by CYP71D20 promoter was treated with the bacterial PAMP flg22 or Phytophthora elicitin INF1. Transcriptional activation of CYP71D20 was measured by monitoring the luciferase activity. The results showed that the LUC activity was increased after treatment with different PAMPs, indicating that the CYP71D20-promotor luciferase assay can be used to study the PAMP-triggered gene expression in N. benthamiana.

Introduction

The early detection of the pathogen can be induced by perception of the conserved features of microbes, like the central element in the bacterial flagella named flagellin, elongation factor Tu (EF-Tu), or components of the pathogen cell wall such as peptidoglycan (PGN) and chitin.1 These microbial elicitors are often termed as pathogen-or microbe-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs or MAMPs). In plants, these PAMPs are recognized by pattern recognition receptors (PRR) to initiate various downstream reactions, including the calcium burst, the initiation of mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) cascades, reactive oxygen species production (ROS), expression of defense-related genes, ethylene production, and callose deposition at the cell wall.1–3 These immune responses collectively comprised the first line of plant immunity to inhibit pathogen infection, called PAMP-triggered Immunity (PTI). The current understanding of PTI is mostly come from studies conducted with the model plant species Arabidopsis thaliana. For example, FLS2 and EFR are two of the best-characterized PRR receptors that recognizes bacterial flagellin and EF-Tu in Arabidopsis.4,5 Perception of PAMPs activates BAK1 and BIK1 leading to activation of various defense responses.6–10

The luciferase enzyme catalyzes a series of oxidation reactions that produce light and bioluminescence which generally differ from the typical photoprotein. There are several forms of luciferase enzymes found in nature that can be developed into a bioluminescent system.11 Luciferases are broadly applied in cell biology, molecular biology, and biotechnology for microscopic examination, reporter gene expression and etc. Luciferases from many bioluminescence-producing organisms have been isolated and cloned as reporter enzymes to study gene expression with high accuracy and within a broad dynamic spectrum.12,13 The luciferase activity can be measured once the luciferase gene with the region of target promoter is transferred into the target cells. Gene fusion is a common technique for analyzing spatial and temporal control of gene expression and describing the regulatory DNA sequences in prokaryotes and eukaryotes. Reporter gene fusion expression is induced in transient assays or stable transformants and used for enzyme assays or histological staining.14 This technique has been used to identified Arabidopsis genes governing immune gene expression by monitoring FLG22-INDUCED RECEPTOR-LIKE KINASE1 (FRK1) expression linked with luciferase gene after treating with a PAMP.10,15

Nicotiana benthamiana is a host for numerous plant pathogens, and is broadly used in the studies of plant-pathogen interactions.16 Many functional genomics techniques known for Nicotiana benthamiana including agroinfiltration, protoplast transfection, and the Agrobacterium-mediated stable transformation. CYP71D20, a member of the cytochrome P450 family, has been found responsive to different PAMPs in N. benthamiana.17,18 For example, CYP71D20 expression is markedly upregulated in response to AEP1 and XEG1 PAMPs conserved in Phytophthora pathogens, highlighting its sensitivity to pathogen-associated molecular patterns.19 These studies collectively underscore the utility of CYP71D20 as a reliable marker gene for investigating PAMP-triggered immunity.

The objective of this study was to construct transgenic N. benthamiana expressing luciferases driven by the promoter of CYP71D20. PAMP-induced marker gene expression in N. benthamiana transformants was evaluated by investigating the luciferase activity.

Results

PAMP-induced CYP71D20 gene expression using real-time PCR analysis

To determine whether CYP71D20 acts as a responsive marker gene in N. benthamiana after perception of PAMPs, we monitored the expression of CYP71D20 triggered by flg22, the elicitor derived from bacteria, and INF1, the elicitor derived from Phytophthora infestans. Leaf discs were collected from 5-week-old N. benthamiana, treated with H2O, flg22, or INF1, and collected at 0 h, 0.5 h, 1 h, 1.5 h, 2.5 h, and 3 h after treatment. The collected leaf discs were used to isolate total RNA and synthesize cDNA. RT-PCR showed a rapid induction of CYP71D20 within 0.5 h, peaking at 1.5 hours and was subsequent declined (Figure 1(a)), suggesting that flg22 quickly triggers plant defense. Unlike flg22, INF1 induces gradual increase in CYP71D20 expression, with levels rising steadily over 3 hours (Figure 1(b)). This prolonged induction suggests a sustained defense response, which may be necessary to combat persistent or aggressive pathogens, like P. infestans. Generation of the transgenic pCYP71D20-luc reporter lines

To generate reporter line used to detect expression of CYP71D20 upon treatment with different PAMPs, the expression vector pCHiI86988- pCYP71D20-luc was generated, containing the coding sequence of luciferases gene fused with the promoter of CYP71D20 (Supplementary Figure S1). The vector was transferred into the Agrobacteria GV3101 and was used to perform Agrobacterium-mediated stable transformation of N. benthamiana plant. In total, 21 potential transformants containing pCHiI86988- pCYP71D20-luc were obtained. To examine the insertion of the target fragment, the genomic DNA was isolated from all the obtained transformants and the genotype was determined using polymerase chain reaction technique (PCR) to select the best transformants. In this experiment, the target gene was a part of LUC gene in pCYP -LUC vector as indicated in supplementary Figure 1. PAMP-induced pCYP71D20-luc expression in N. benthamiana transgenic line

To determine whether the transformants can be used to detect expression of CYP71D20 upon treatment with different PAMPs, we used the bioluminescene assay to detect the activation of luciferease signal in the transformant CYP-26 and the wild type N. benthamiana. As shown in Figure 3, no luciferase signal was detected in the wild type N. benthamiana upon treatment with H2O, 1 μM flg22 or INF1 elicitors. In the transformant CYP-26, luciferase signal was detected after treatment with flg22. The signal was steadily increased till 1.5 h after flg22 treatment, but was subsequently decreased. Luciferase signal was also detected after treatment with INF1, and the signal was continuously increased till 3 h after treatment. Meanwhile, no luciferases signal was detected at any of the time points after treatment with the control H2O. Discussion

The plant defense involves both basal and induced defense reactions, providing different levels of resistance against invading pathogens.20,21 The basal defense is consisting of physical or chemical barriers that prevent most of the microbial infections.22–24 The induced defense is divided into two layers; the first one is PAMP-triggered immunity (PTI) which is triggered by the pathogen associated molecular pattern (PAMP). The process of the potential pathogens recognizing is critical for inducing active defensive responses in plants.25 Nicotiana benthamiana has been widely used as a model plant and is responsive to various PAMPs. The marker genesCYP71D20, NbACRE31, and NbWRKY22 are quickly activated during PAMP treatment.7,26 Bioluminescence imaging by using luciferase reporters is a highly advantageous approach. In the transient experiments or in the stable transformants, reporter gene fusion expression is monitored by in vitro enzyme assays or histological staining. In the study of plant immune responses, genetic screening systems have been crucial in identifying key regulators of gene expression. For example, the “Aggie” system, developed in Arabidopsis thaliana, involves the use of luciferase as a reporter gene linked to specific promoters. This approach was used to identify mutants, such as aggie1 and aggie3, which exhibit altered immune gene expression and enhanced disease resistance. The system provides a robust framework for studying gene activity in response to PAMPs.27 In this study, we measured the transcriptional changes of defense-related gene CYP71D20 by using Real-time quantification PCR (RT-qPCR). We generated N. benthamiana transgenic lines by expressing the luciferase gene driven by the promoter of CYP71D20 to monitor PAMP triggered CYP71D20 gene expression. INF1 and flg22 are elicitors (PAMP) that were used to detect the response of CYP71D20 as a defense marker gene in N. benthamiana. INF1 is a 10-kD extracellular protein that produced by Phytophthora infestans which causes potato and tomato late blight disease. This protein induces the resistance in some Nicotiana species which observed by the hypersensitive reactions.28 The peptide flg22 is the best characterized elicitor triggering immune responses in plants.29 By using RT-qPCR and luciferase defection assay, we confirmed that CYP71D20 is induced in N. benthamiana upon treatment with elicitors flg22 and INF1. The results indicate that this reporter assay can be used to detect different PAMP-triggered CYP71D20 expression. This is significant because this can be introduced as an easy test for analyzing gene expressions when compared to the time-and cost-consuming Real-time quantification PCR assay, especially applicable to the large-scale screening assays.

Materials and methods

Plant material

Nicotiana benthamiana seeds were germinated in 10 cm round plastic pots using miracle soil in the greenhouse at 19–22°C under long-day conditions (14 h light/10 h dark), 70–80% relative humidity, and lighting 50–100 μE s–1 m–2). After two weeks, the seedlings were transferred to 10 cm diameter round pots containing miracle soil (one seedling per pot). Plants were regularly fertilized and were maintained in the greenhouse under above conditions for 4–5 weeks, and the plants were used for gene expression and transformation experiments.

PAMP-induced marker gene expression by qRT-pcr

Plant discs were prepared from N. benthamiana leaves using a cork borer (4 mm), were incubated with distilled water overnight at room temperature overnight, then followed by treatment with with 100 nM of flg22, and 1 µM INF1 proteins or control (e.g., ddH2O) for 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2.5 and 3hrs, respectively. Total RNA was extracted from N. benthamiana leaves using E.Z.N.A.® Total RNA Kit I following the manufacturer’s instructions with minor modifications. RNA concentration was measured using NanodropTM 1000 and stored at −70°C. To synthesize the first-strand cDNA total RNA was reverse transcribed using Prime ScriptTM RT reagent Kit following the manufacturer instructions, and the product was used for qRT-PCR. Endogenous control actin was used as a reference gene for normalization to control for variability in RNA input and ensure accurate measurement of gene expression changes.

Plasmid construction

PCHi86988-CYP71D20-LUC were constructed by amplifying the promotor of CYP71D20 gene from the genomic DNA of N. benthamiana using Taq polymerase (Takara Bio Inc., Otsu, Japan). The PCR products were ligated into the maintained PCHi86988 vector and subsequently transformed into E.coli JM109 strain. The heat-shock transformation products were plated on solid Luria-Bertani (LB) media supplemented with appropriate antibiotics and incubated for 24 hours at 37°C under the dark conditions. The right colonies were selected by colony-PCR and then confirmed by DNA Sanger sequencing. The validated vector was transformed into Agrobacterium tumefaciens GV3101 via the electroporation method using solid LB media with selective antibiotics (Rifampicin 50 μg/ml; Kanamycin 50 μg/ml) and incubated for 72 hours at 30°C under dark condition. The successful agro-transformed colony was further validated through colony-PCR and then used later in this study for Agrobacterium-mediated transformation of N. benthamiana.

Preparation of agrobacterium cultures

The Agrobacterium strains were grown in LB broth media amended with selective antibiotics and incubated at 30°C in a shaker (Labtech Shaking Incubator Ivf Orbital Shaking Incubator). The overnight cultures were washed three times by using Murashige-Skoog (MS) basal salt broth media, pH 5.8; supplemented with Acetosyringone (100 uM) and the OD600 was adjusted to 1.

Agrobacterium-mediated N. benthamiana transformation

Plant discs were prepared using a cork borer (4 mm) from fresh leaves detached from N. benthamiana plants grown in adjusted greenhouse condition 19–22°C under long-day conditions (14-h-light/10-h-dark), 70–80% relative humidity, and light 50–100 μE s–1 m–2). These leaves were sterilized using 5% sodium hypochlorite for 10 minutes, then washed five times using ddH2O. A. tumefaciens was cultured on LB plates amended with suitable antibiotics and incubated at 30°C for two days, and then cultured in 10 ml of LB broth media with selective antibiotics. The new cultures were incubated overnight at 30°C (250 rpm). The bacteria cells were collected and washed four times by using MS broth media supplemented with Acetosyringone (100 uM). The OD600 was measured and adjusted to 0.5. The Agrobacterium solution used to treat the sterilized plant discs for 30 minutes for each treatment, dried on sterilized filter papers. After infection, explants were transferred onto co-cultivation medium (4.4 g/l 1× MS salts, 20-30 g/l sucrose, 0.8% agar, Ph 5.6–5.8, 1 mg/l BAP, 0.1 mg/l NAA, 100 µM acetosyringone). Plates were sealed with air-permeable adhesive tape and incubated in the dark at 25°C for two days. After two days of co-cultivation, explants were transferred onto callus induction medium (co-cultivation medium, 500 mg/l cefotaxime, 200 mg kanamycin) and incubated at 25°C in the dark for seven days. Then explants were routinely transferred on the callus induction media until callus was formed. The emerged calluses were transferred to a shooting induction medium (co-cultivation medium, 400 mg/l cefotaxime, 200 mg kanamycin) for selecting the transformed calluses for two weeks. Callus pieces were transferred every two weeks to a fresh shooting medium. When the shoots’ length became 0.5–1 cm, they were transferred to rooting media (4.4 g/l1× MS salts, 20–30 g sucrose, 150 mg/l cefotaxime, 100 mg kanamycin) for four weeks inside sterilized jars.

The plantlets with well-established roots were transplanted into pre-wetted soil mixture (Multi-purpose compost and vermiculite; ratio 1:2) in small pots (5 cm) and maintained in a growth chamber under the same condition as described for plants growth. After 6 to 7 days, young plants were transferred into the greenhouse and fertilized regularly with NPK fertilizer (24-8-16).

The transformants screening

DNA extraction was performed using (TianGen Biotech co Ltd), then the concentration of the extracted DNA for each transformant was measured using a spectrophotometer (Nanodrop ND-1000). PCR experiments were performed to screen the right candidates. The experiments were implemented by spanning the targeted LUC gene and to confirm that it is correctly inserted into the plant genome.

Expression of pCYP71D20-luc triggered by using different PAMPs

The luciferase reporter assay has been used to evaluate PAMP-triggered LUC activity in the best candidates. Plant leaves were prepared from 5 weeks old leaves of N. benthamiana transformants. Plant leaves were vacuum-infiltrated with ddH2O, 1 µM INF1 or 100 nM flg22 (Genscript Biotech Corporation, China) in 1 mm D-luciferin potassium salt (YEASEN, China). The Luciferase signal in N. benthamiana leaves was detected by ultraviolet imager (Tanon 5200 Multi) after exposed under UV light (365 nm).

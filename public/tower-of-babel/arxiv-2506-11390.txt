Analysis and Simulation of Plasmons in Graphene with Time- and Space-dependent Drude Weight Submitted to the editors DATE.
Fadil Santosa, Tong Shi

Abstract
We study the propagation of plasmons on graphene. The problem is considered in two dimensions with a transverse magnetic (TM) electromagnetic field. The graphene material is assumed to be flat and is modeled as a conductive sheet. This leads to a jump condition for the magnetic field on the sheet where it is related to the current density on the sheet. The current density itself satisfies Drude’s law. The model then consists of Maxwell’s equations coupled to current density on the sheet. To make the problem more computationally approachable, we develop a time-dependent integro-differential equation for the current density. This effectively reduces the problem to one space dimension. A finite difference method is proposed to solve the resulting equation. Numerical examples are given to illustrate previously unreported behavior of the system.

In this work, we continue the development began in

Instead of a single integro-differential equation for amplitude, we now must consider a general form for the current density. Little is known about this new equation as we have not seen anything similar in the open literature. We develop a first-order explicit finite difference method to solve the equation and is able to expose some interesting behavior of plasmons when the graphene’s Drude weight is manipulated temporarily and spatially.

The body of this paper can be divided into three main parts: (i) Derivation of the current density equation, (ii) Finite difference method for solving the resulting equation, and (iii) Numerical experiments. In Section

We derive a partial integro-differential equation for the current density on the conductive sheet in Section

We show that the finite difference approximation is consistent, but defer the stability analysis to future work. The accuracy of the method is validated with known exact solutions.

With the simulator at hand, we explore new phenomena associated with specific types of time- and space-dependent Drude weight. The paper ends with a discussion section.

3
An equation for the current density

Analysis of the system when the Drude weight is time-dependent was greatly simplified when we were able to reduce the governing equations to a single equation on the conductive sheet

The Fourier transform operation and its inverse operation are denoted respectively by

and

. For example
\widetilde{\widehat{u}}(\xi,s)=\mathcal{F}_{x}\mathcal{L}_{t}\{u(x,t)\}

To deal with the second term in (

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{0}}{\epsilon s_{0}}e^{i\xi_{0}x}(e^{-s_{0}t}-1)+\eta e^{i\xi_{0}x}\left(k_{1}(\xi_{0},t)\,*_{t}\,e^{-s_{0}t}+e^{-s_{0}t}\right).

\displaystyle\frac{\partial j}{\partial t}(x,t)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{\tau}+\frac{\eta}{2}D(x,t)\right)j(x,t)+\frac{\eta}{2}D(x,t)\mathcal{F}_{x}^{-1}\left\{k_{1}(\xi,t)\,*_{t}\,\widetilde{j}(\xi,t)\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{\eta}{2}D(x,t)j_{0}(x,t)+\frac{\eta}{2}D(x,t)k_{1}(\xi_{0},t)\,*_{t}\,j_{0}(x,t)+D(x,t)E_{x0}(x,0,t).

(28)

The only task left is to evaluate the Fourier inverse on the left-hand side.

Remark 1
.

It is worth noting here that one can take the spatial Fourier Transform of (

) and view the resulting equation as the governing equation for the Fourier transform of the current density
\tilde{j}(\xi,t)
. Using the convolution theorem, the resulting equation is

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\widetilde{j}}{\partial t}(\xi,t)+\left(\frac{1}{\tau}+\frac{\eta}{2}\widetilde{D}(\cdot,t)\right)*_{\xi}\widetilde{j}(\cdot,t)(\xi)+\frac{\eta}{2}\widetilde{D}(\cdot,t)*_{\xi}\left\{k_{1}(\cdot,t)\,*_{t}\,\widetilde{j}(\cdot,t)\right\}(\xi)=

\displaystyle\frac{\eta}{2}\widetilde{D}(\cdot,t)*_{\xi}\widetilde{j_{0}}(\cdot,t)(\xi)+\frac{\eta}{2}\widetilde{D}(\cdot,t)*_{\xi}[k_{1}(\xi_{0},t)\,*_{t}\,\widetilde{j_{0}}(\cdot,t)](\xi)+\widetilde{D}(\cdot,t)*_{\xi}\widetilde{E_{x0}}(\cdot,0,t)(\xi).

Recalling that
j_{0}(x,t)
and

3.2
Partial integro-differential equation for current density

In order to get an equation for

Remark 2
.

It is not obvious how one should go about establishing well-posed-ness of this initial value problem. We can verify that if

, the solution is

Second, the Drude weight appears in the second term as part of the damping. This is somewhat counter-intuitive since if Drude weight is constant and

The remainder of this paper is focused on developing a finite difference method to solve the initial value problem for

4.1
Finite difference method

Our goal is to solve an initial value problem associated with (
34
). We are given
D(x,t)
and the initial data on the whole real line. The domain of interest is
(x,t)
in
[-A,A]\times[0,T]
. The rectangular domain is regularly meshed with spacings of
\Delta x
and
\Delta t
. We use indices
(l,k)
to locate a grid point in the
(x,t)

Because of the dependence of

on its values behind the backwards light cone, to simplify the computation of the operation
\Phi[v](x,t)

(x,t)

We set

For the left-hand side, we define

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau}+\frac{1}{2}D(l\Delta x,k\Delta t),

\displaystyle B_{l,k}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi}D(l\Delta x,k\Delta t).

We use

For partial derivatives, we apply the central difference approximations.
Then (

(36)

We further define

\displaystyle A_{l,k}^{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{l,k}}{2\Delta t}+\frac{1}{\Delta t^{2}},

\displaystyle A_{l,k}^{-}

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{l,k}}{2\Delta t}-\frac{1}{\Delta t^{2}}.

From (
36

v_{l,k+1}=\frac{1}{A_{l,k}^{+}}\left(R_{l,k}-B_{l,k}F_{l,k}+\frac{2}{\Delta t^{2}}v_{l,k}+A_{l,k}^{-}v_{l,k-1}\right).

(37)

At first glance, the time-marching scheme appears to involve the node values of the most recent two time layers. However, we should note that

behind the light cone at

. We will treat the calculation of

momentarily.

To initialize the scheme, we need

and

.
It is easy to see that

and

for all

. Therefore, at time layer

(38)

We know that

so it is known. We can evaluate

Using the central difference approximation we have

\frac{v_{l,1}-v_{l,-1}}{2\Delta t}=Q_{l}.

(39)

Equations (

(40)

With this, we now have

and can march forward in time.

4.2
Approximation of the integral operator

We focus on the
\Phi[v](x,t)
term in (

). We use

F_{l,k}\approx\Phi[v](l\Delta x,k\Delta t).

From (

depends on

for all

behind the light cone at

t^{\prime}=k^{\prime}\Delta t,\;\;x^{\prime}=l^{\prime}\Delta x.

We introduce the notation

G(l,k,k^{\prime})\approx\frac{1}{k^{\prime 2}\Delta t^{2}}\int_{-k^{\prime}\Delta t}^{k^{\prime}\Delta t}(k^{\prime 2}\Delta t^{2}-x^{\prime 2})^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{xx}(l\Delta x-x^{\prime},k\Delta t-k^{\prime}\Delta t)\,dx^{\prime}.

We replace the outer integral in (

F_{l,k}=\Delta t\left[\frac{G(l,k,0)}{2}+\frac{G(l,k,k)}{2}+\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{k-1}G(l,k,k^{\prime})\right].

Next, we let

P(l,l^{\prime},k,k^{\prime})=(k^{\prime 2}\Delta t^{2}-l^{\prime 2}\Delta x^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{xx}(l\Delta x-l^{\prime}\Delta x,k\Delta t-k^{\prime}\Delta t).

Then, using trapezoidal rule, we have

\displaystyle G(l,k,k^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta x}{k^{\prime 2}\Delta t^{2}}\left[\frac{P(l,k^{\prime},k,k^{\prime},)}{2}+\frac{P(l,-k^{\prime},k,k^{\prime})}{2}+\sum_{l^{\prime}=-k^{\prime}+1}^{k^{\prime}-1}P(l,l^{\prime},k,k^{\prime})\right].

We cannot use this equation to determine
G(l,k,0)
. Instead, we use

\displaystyle G(l,k,0)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t^{\prime}\to 0}\frac{1}{t^{\prime 2}}\int_{-t^{\prime}}^{t^{\prime}}(t^{\prime 2}-x^{\prime 2})^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{xx}(l\Delta x-x^{\prime},k\Delta t-t^{\prime})\,dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t^{\prime}\to 0}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\bar{x}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{xx}(l\Delta x-t^{\prime}\bar{x},k\Delta t-t^{\prime})\,d\bar{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\pi v_{xx}(l\Delta x,k\Delta t).

Notice in (
41
) that

P(l,k^{\prime},k,k^{\prime})=P(l,-k^{\prime},k,k^{\prime})=0.

Therefore, the formula for

with

simplifies to

G(l,k,k^{\prime})=\frac{\Delta x}{k^{\prime 2}\Delta t^{2}}\sum_{l^{\prime}=-k^{\prime}+1}^{k^{\prime}-1}P(l,l^{\prime},k,k^{\prime}).

Combining the results above, we can get a more compact formula for
F_{l,k}

\displaystyle F_{l,k}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi c^{2}\Delta t}{4}v_{xx}(l\Delta x,k\Delta t)+\frac{1}{2k^{2}}\Delta x\sum_{l^{\prime}=-k+1}^{k-1}(k^{2}-l^{\prime 2})^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{xx}((l-l^{\prime})\Delta x,0)

\displaystyle+\Delta x\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{k-1}\frac{1}{k^{\prime 2}}\sum_{l^{\prime}=-k^{\prime}\frac{M_{1}}{N}+1}^{k^{\prime}-1}(k^{\prime 2}-l^{\prime 2})^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{xx}((l-l^{\prime})\Delta x,(k-k^{\prime})\Delta t).

The partial derivative

4.3
Consistency

We wish to establish that the finite difference method in (
37
) with (
42
) is consistent with the integro-differential equation (
34
) and (
35
). To this end, let
v(x,t)
be a smooth function. We use the notation
d^{+}_{t}
and
d^{-}_{t}
to denote forward and backward difference approximation of time derivative, and
d^{0}_{t}
to denote the central difference approximation of the same. The truncation error we wish to estimate is

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}(x_{l},t_{k})=\frac{\partial^{2}v}{\partial t^{2}}(x_{l},t_{k})+

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\tau}+\frac{1}{2}D(x_{l},t_{k})\right)\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}(x_{j},t_{k})-\frac{1}{2\pi}D(x_{l},t_{k})\Phi[v](x_{l},t_{k})

\displaystyle-(d^{+}_{t}d^{-}_{t})v(x_{l},t_{k})-A_{l,k}d^{0}_{t}\,v(x_{l},t_{k})-B_{l,k}F_{l,k}.

(43)

Using Taylor series, we know that

\displaystyle(d^{+}_{t}d^{-}_{t})v(x_{l},t_{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial^{2}v}{\partial t^{2}}(x_{l},t_{k})+O(\Delta t^{2}),

\displaystyle d^{0}_{t}v(x_{l},t_{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}(x_{l},t_{k})+O(\Delta t^{2}).

So, from (
43
) we have

{\mathcal{E}}(x_{l},t_{k})=O(\Delta t^{2})-\frac{1}{2\pi}D(x_{l},t_{k})\Phi[v](x_{l},t_{k})-B_{l,k}F_{l,k}.

Equation (
42
) is a trapezoidal rule approximation of the double integral in (
35
). Therefore, we commit a truncation error of
O(\Delta x^{2}+\Delta t^{2})
. However, we need to further replace
v_{xx}(x,t)
in (
42
) with its finite difference approximation, which produces an error bounded by
M\Delta x^{2}
, for some
M
. Let us write

\displaystyle\left|-\frac{1}{2\pi}D(x_{l},t_{k})\Phi[v](x_{l},t_{k})-B_{l,k}F_{l,k}\right|

\displaystyle\leq K(\Delta x^{2}+\Delta t^{2})+\frac{\pi c^{2}\Delta t}{4}M\Delta x^{2}+\Delta x\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{k-1}\frac{1}{k^{\prime 2}}\sum_{l^{\prime}=-k^{\prime}+1}^{k^{\prime}-1}(k^{\prime 2}-l^{\prime 2})^{\frac{1}{2}}M\Delta x^{2}

\displaystyle\leq K(\Delta x^{2}+\Delta t^{2})+\frac{\pi c^{2}\Delta t}{4}M\Delta x^{2}+M\Delta x^{3}\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{k-1}\frac{1}{k^{\prime 2}}\sum_{l^{\prime}=-k^{\prime}+1}^{k^{\prime}-1}|k^{\prime}|.

The first term represents the quadrature error for some
K>0
. Note that

M\Delta x^{3}\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{k-1}\frac{1}{k^{\prime 2}}\sum_{l^{\prime}=-k^{\prime}+1}^{k^{\prime}-1}|k^{\prime}|\leq M\Delta x^{3}\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{k-1}\frac{1}{k^{\prime 2}}|k^{\prime}|(2k^{\prime})\leq M\Delta x^{3}k.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\left|-\frac{1}{2\pi}D(x_{j},t_{k})\Phi[v](x_{l},t_{k})-B_{l,k}F_{l,k}\right|

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\leq K(\Delta x^{2}+\Delta t^{2})+\frac{\pi c^{2}\Delta t}{4}M\Delta x^{2}+2M\Delta x^{2}T,

since
N\Delta t=T
and

6
Discussion

In this work, we studied the propagation of plasmons on a graphene sheet whose Drude weight is both time- and space-dependent. Our interest is in the development of an efficient and an accurate numerical method for simulating such phenomena. Directly solving the governing Maxwell’s equation with jump conditions on the graphene sheet presents a challenge. First, we need to specify an appropriate absorbing boundary condition around the computational domain. However, it is unclear what is the appropriate condition near the graphene sheet since the plasmons are highly localized to the sheet. Second, we found that due to the dual dependence of the Drude weight, our attempt to build a time-stepping scheme involves inverting a large matrix at each step.

Our approach is to derive a new partial integro-differential equation for the time-integral of the current density on the graphene sheet. This equation is ‘lives on the graphene sheet and because of the reduced dimension, it can be treated directly. In the present work, the equation is solved by a finite difference method. After numerically establishing convergence of the scheme, we calculated the EM field for a specific type of time- and space-dependent Drude weight. We were able to show that both traveling and standing perturbational fields can be generated.

Finally, it should be repeated here that this work represents a preliminary investigation into partial integro-differential equations of the type that appear in this study. Little is known about such equations though we are convinced that they appear in other applications.

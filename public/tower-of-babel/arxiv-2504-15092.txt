Extending structures for pre-Poisson algebras and pre-Poisson bialgebras
Qianwen Zhu, Guilai Liu, Qinxiu Sun

Abstract
. In this paper, we explore the extending structures problem by the unified product for pre-Poisson algebras. In particular, the crossed product and the factorization problem are investigated. Furthermore, a special case of extending structures is studied under the case of pre-Poisson algebras, which leads to the discussion of bicrossed products and matched pairs of pre-Poisson algebras. We develop a bialgebra theory for pre-Poisson algebras and establish the equivalence between matched pairs and pre-Poisson bialgebras. We study coboundary pre-Poisson bialgebras, which lead to the introduction of the pre-Poisson Yang-Baxter equation (PPYBE). A symmetric solution of the PPYBE naturally gives a coboundary pre-Poisson bialgebra.

1.
Introduction

The notion of Zinbiel algebras first appeared in Loday
′
s study of the algebraic structure behind the cup
product on the cohomology groups of a Leibniz algebra
[
22
]
, and further was studied in
[
19
,
23
]
.
Zinbiel algebras are Koszul dual to Leibniz algebras, and they are special dendriform algebras.
The anti-commutator of a Zinbiel algebra gives a commutative associative algebra, and the commutator of a pre-Lie algebra gives a Lie algebra.
Conversely,
\mathcal{O}
-operators on commutative associative algebras and Lie algebras give rise to
Zinbiel algebras and pre-Lie algebras respectively.
As we all know, a Poisson algebra
is both a Lie algebra and an associative algebra with the Leibniz rule.
Aguiar combined structures of Zinbiel algebra and pre-Lie algebra on the same vector space, leading to the notion of a pre-Poisson algebra
[
1
]
, which closely relates to the Poisson algebra.
On the one hand, a pre-Poisson algebra gives rise to a
Poisson algebra naturally through the sub-adjacent commutative associative algebra of the Zinbiel
algebra and the sub-adjacent Lie algebra of the pre-Lie algebra.
On the other hand, from an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on a Poisson algebra, one can get a pre-Poisson algebra.

The extending structures problem (ES-problem) arose in the study of group theory developed by
Agore and Militaru
[
5
]
, which unified the two well-known problems in group theory
–H
\rm{\ddot{o}}
lder’s extension problem
[
17
]
and the factorization problem of Ore
[
25
]
.
Since then, this theory was extended to various kinds of algebras, such as Lie algebras (bialgebras),
Lie (associative) conformal algebras, left symmetric algebras (bialgebras), Leibniz algebras,
Zinbiel algebras, Poisson algebras (bialgebras), perm-algebras and Jordan algebras,
see
[
5
,
6
,
29
,
30
,
13
,
3
,
7
,
9
,
14
,
15
,
18
,
16
,
26
,
27
,
28
,
31
,
8
,
4
]
and references therein.
But the extending structure theory for pre-Poisson algebras is still absent. This is the first motivation for writing this paper.
More precisely, we are devoted to investigating the ES-problem for pre-Poisson algebras:
Assume that
A
is a pre-Poisson algebra and
E
is a vector space containing
A
as a subspace.
Characterize and classify all pre-Poisson algebraic structures on
E
such
that
A
is a subalgebra of
E
up to an isomorphism of pre-Poisson algebras whose restriction on
A
is the identity map.

On the other hand, a bialgebraic structure on a given algebraic structure is obtained by a
comultiplication together with compatibility conditions between the
multiplications and comultiplications. Lie bialgebras were introduced in the early 1980s by Drinfeld
[
12
]
, which
have also been applied in the theory of classical integrable systems and are closely
related to the classical Yang-Baxter equation. In
[
32
,
33
]
, V. Zhelyabin developed Drinfeld’s ideas to
introduce the notion of associative D-bialgebras, which are associative analogue of Lie bialgebras. Later, Aguiar developed
the notion of a balanced infinitesimal bialgebra
[
2
]
, and an
antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebra was considered in
[
11
]
. Bai and Ni combined the ideas of
Lie bialgebras and (commutative and cocommutative)
infinitesimal bialgebras in a uniform way, which leaded to the notion of a Poisson bialgebra
[
24
]
. In
[
20
]
,
a noncommutative Poisson bialgebra was developed. It is natural to combine Zinbiel bialgebras
and pre-Lie bialgebras
[
10
]
in some certain way.
This is another motivation for writing this paper. In detail,
we develop a notion of a
pre-Poisson bialgebra, which is both a Zinbiel bialgebra and a
pre-Lie bialgebra satisfying
some compatible conditions. Then we establish the equivalence between pre-Poisson bialgebras
and certain matched pairs of pre-Poisson algebras.
The study of coboundary pre-Poisson
bialgebras leads to the introduction of pre-Poisson Yang-Baxter
equation (PPYBE). A symmetric solution of the pre-Poisson Yang-Baxter equation naturally gives a
(coboundary) pre-Poisson bialgebra.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we study the ES-problem by the unified product for pre-Poisson algebras.
By means of unified product, we show that any pre-Poisson structure on
E

such that
A
is a pre-Poisson subalgebra is isomorphic to a unified product of
A
by
V
, where
V
is a complement of
A
in
E
. With this tool, we construct an object
H^{2}_{p}(V,A)
to give a theoretical answer for the ES-problem of pre-Poisson algebras.
In Section 3, we discuss some special cases of unified products: crossed products, bicrossed products and flag extending structures.
In Section 4, we explore a bialgebra theory for pre-Poisson algebras and
establish the equivalence between matched pairs and pre-Poisson bialgebras. The study of coboundary
pre-Poisson bialgebras leads to the investigation of the pre-Poisson Yang-Baxter equation (PPYBE).
A symmetric solution of the PPYBE naturally gives a coboundary pre-Poisson bialgebra.

Throughout the paper,
k
is a field. All vector spaces and algebras are over
k
.
All algebras are finite-dimensional, although many results still hold in the infinite-dimensional case.

2.
Unified products for pre-Poisson algebras

In this section, we study unified products for pre-Poisson algebras. We start with recalling
some basic knowledge and extending structures for
Zinbiel algebras and pre-Lie algebras. One can refer to
[
10
,
11
,
29
,

,
13
]
for these facts.

A
Zinbiel algebra
is a vector space
A
together with a bilinear map
\ast:A\times A\longrightarrow A
such that

x\ast(y\ast z)=(y\ast x)\ast z+(x\ast y)\ast z,

for all
x,y,z\in A
.

A
dendriform algebra
is a vector space
A
together with two bilinear maps
\succ,\prec:A\times A\longrightarrow A
such that

(x\prec y)\prec z=x\prec(y\prec z)+x\prec(y\succ z),

x\succ(y\prec z)=(x\succ y)\prec z,

x\succ(y\succ z)=(x\prec y)\succ z+(x\succ y)\succ z,

for all
x,y,z\in A
.
Thus a Zinbiel algebra
(A,\ast)
is indeed a dendriform algebra
(A,\succ,\prec)
with
x\ast y=x\succ y=y\prec x
.

Let
(A,\ast)
be a Zinbiel algebra. Define a binary operation

\star:A\times A\longrightarrow A
by

x\star y=x\ast y+y\ast x,

then
(A,\star)
is a commutative associative algebra, which is called the
associated commutative associative algebra
of
(A,\ast)
,
denoted by
(\mathcal{C}(A),\star)
or simply by

\mathcal{C}(A)
. We call

(A,\ast)
a
compatible Zinbiel
algebra
structure on the commutative associative algebra

(\mathcal{C}(A),\star)
.

A non-degenerate antisymmetric bilinear form
\omega
on an associative algebra

(A,\star)
is called a
Connes cocycle
if

\omega(x\star y,z)+\omega(y\star z,x)+\omega(z\star x,y)=0,~~\forall~x,y,z\in A.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(A,\star)
be a commutative associative algebra and
\omega
be a Connes cocycle.
Then there is a compatible Zinbiel algebra structure
\ast
on
A
defined by

\omega(x\ast y,z)=\omega(y,x\star z),~~\forall~x,y,z\in A.

Definition 2.2
.

A
representation
of a Zinbiel algebra
(A,\ast)
is a triple
(V,l,r)
, where
V
is a
vector space and
l,r:A\longrightarrow\hbox{End}(V)
are two linear maps satisfying the following relations, for all

x,y\in A
,

l(x)l(y)=l(x\ast y)+l(y\ast x)=l(y)l(x),

l(x)r(y)=r(x\ast y)=r(y)r(x)+r(y)l(x).

Let
A
and
V
be vector spaces. For
a linear map
f:A\longrightarrow\hbox{End}(V)
, define a linear
map
f^{*}:A\longrightarrow\hbox{End}(V^{*})
by
\langle f^{*}(x)u^{*},v\rangle=-\langle u^{*},f(x)v\rangle
for all
x\in A,u^{*}\in V^{*},v\in V
, where
\langle\ ,\ \rangle
is the usual
pairing between
V
and
V^{*}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
(V,l,r)
be a
representation of a Zinbiel algebra
(A,\ast)
. Then

(a)

(V^{*},-l^{*}-r^{*},r^{*})
is also a
representation of
(A,\ast)
. We call it the
dual representation
of
(V,l,r)
.

(b)

(V,l+r)
is a representation of the associated
commutative associative algebra
(\mathcal{C}(A),\star)
.

(c)

(V,l)
is a representation of the associated
commutative associative algebra
(\mathcal{C}(A),\star)
.

Example 2.4
.

Let
(A,\ast)
be a Zinbiel algebra,
and

L_{\ast},R_{\ast}:A\longrightarrow\hbox{End}(A)
be the
linear maps defined by
L_{\ast}(x)(y)=R_{\ast}(y)(x)=x\ast y
for all

is a representation of

,

of

Moreover,
(A^{*},-L_{\star}^{*}=-L^{*}_{\ast}-R^{*}_{\ast},R_{\ast}^{*})

A
pre-Lie algebra
is a vector space
A
together with a bilinear map
\circ:A\times A\longrightarrow A
such that

\displaystyle x\circ(y\circ z)-(x\circ y)\circ z=y\circ(x\circ z)-(y\circ x)\circ z,

for all
x,y,z\in A
. Define

[x,y]=x\circ y-y\circ x
for all
x,y\in A
. Then
(A,[\ ,\ ])
is a Lie algebra, which is called the
sub-adjacent Lie algebra
of
(A,\circ)
and is denoted by
\mathfrak{g}(A)
. Moreover,
(A,\circ)
is called a
compatible pre-Lie algebra
of
(A,[\ ,\ ])
.

A Lie algebra
(A,[\ ,\ ])
is called

if there exists a non-degenerate antisymmetric 2-cocycle
\omega
on

,
that is,

\omega([x,y],z)+\omega([y,z],x)+\omega([z,x],y)=0,~~\forall~x,y,z\in A.

In this case, we call
\omega
a
symplectic
form on the Lie algebra
(A,[\ ,\ ])
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
(A,[\ ,\ ],\omega)
be a symplectic Lie algebra. Then there exists a
compatible pre-Lie algebra structure
\circ
on
A
given by

\omega(x\circ y,z)=-\omega(y,[x,z]),~~\forall~x,y,z\in A.

Definition 2.6
.

Let

be a pre-Lie algebra and

a vector space. Assume that
\rho,\mu:A\longrightarrow End(V)

are two linear maps.
(V,\rho,\mu)
is called

\rho(x)\rho(y)-\rho(x\circ y)=\rho(y)\rho(x)-\rho(y\circ x),

\rho(x)\mu(y)-\mu(y)\rho(x)=\mu(x\circ y)-\mu(y)\mu(x),

for all

.

Let
(V,\rho,\mu)

(b)

.

(c)

Example 2.8
.

Let
(A,\circ)
be a pre-Lie algebra,
and

L_{\circ},R_{\circ}:A\longrightarrow\hbox{End}(A)
be the

L_{\circ}(x)(y)=R_{\circ}(y)(x)=x\ast y

Now we come to study the representation theory of pre-Poisson algebras.

Definition 2.9
.

[
1
]

A
pre-Poisson algebra
is a triple
(A,\ast,\circ)
, where
(A,\ast)
is a Zinbiel algebra,

(A,\circ)
is a pre-Lie algebra and the following conditions hold:

(1)

(x\circ y-y\circ x)\ast z=x\circ(y\ast z)-y\ast(x\circ z),

(2)

(x\ast y+y\ast x)\circ z=x\ast(y\circ z)+y\ast(x\circ z),

for all
x,y,z\in A
.

Let
(A,\ast,\circ)
be a pre-Poisson algebra. Define

x\star y=x\ast y+y\ast x,~[x,y]=x\circ y-y\circ x,~\forall~x,y\in A.

Then

(A,\star,[\ ,\ ])
is a Poisson algebra, that is,
(A,\star)
is a commutative associative algebra,
(A,[\ .\ ])
is a Lie algebra and the following equation holds:

(3)

\displaystyle[z,x\star y]=[z,x]\star y+x\star[z,y],\;\forall x,y,z\in A.

Moreover,
(A,\star,[\ ,\ ])
is called the
sub-adjacent Poisson algebra
of
(A,\ast,\circ)
, and
(A,\ast,\circ)
is called a
compatible pre-Poisson algebra
of
(A,\star,[\ ,\ ])
.

4.
Pre-Poisson bialgebras and Yang-Baxter equations in pre-Poisson algebras

In this section, we investigate a class of special matched pairs of pre-Poisson algebras.
We introduce the notions of pre-Poisson bialgebras and quadratic pre-Poisson algebras. Furthermore, we establish the equivalence among
pre-Poisson bialgebras, quadratic pre-Poisson algebras
and certain matched pairs of pre-Poisson algebras. We study
coboundary pre-Poisson bialgebras and our study leads to the pre-Poisson Yang-Baxter equation (PPYBE) in pre-Poisson algebras,
which is an analogue of the classical Yang-Baxter equation. A symmetric solution of
PPYBE gives a pre-Poisson bialgebra.

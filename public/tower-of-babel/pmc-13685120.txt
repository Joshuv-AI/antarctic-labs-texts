Value-based price corridor for bepirovirsen in chronic hepatitis B: an early health technology assessment in a cost-compressed setting
Authors: Kaijie Yao, Mengxia Yan, Yun Bao, Ying Chen, Wen Li, Bin Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundWe conducted an early health technology assessment (eHTA) of bepirovirsen, an investigational finite-course therapy for chronic hepatitis B. In China, low-cost generic nucleos(t)ide analogues (NAs) create a demanding comparator environment. The value-based price of a potential functional-cure strategy therefore depends on local comparator prices, threshold selection, cure durability, safety, monitoring requirements, and cured-state utility assumptions.MethodsA lifetime Markov model simulated bepirovirsen 300 mg/week for 24 weeks plus background NAs against comparator-specific standard-of-care options. Chinese cost inputs were valued in 2024 renminbi (RMB) and converted to US dollars using the 2024 annual average exchange rate of 7.1957 RMB per US dollar. The base case used a China-context cost-effectiveness threshold (CET) of $13,500 per quality-adjusted life-year (QALY), approximating China’s 2024 gross domestic product (GDP) per capita after currency conversion, and compared it with higher threshold scenarios. Because reliable long-term relapse rates were unavailable, a 48-week maintained-response scenario set bepirovirsen efficacy to 9.0%, corresponding to the B-Clear primary-outcome rate among participants receiving background NA therapy.ResultsBepirovirsen yielded 0.41–0.82 incremental QALYs across standard-of-care comparators. Compared with entecavir (ETV), the base case generated 0.82 additional QALYs, incurred an incremental total cost of $10,833.72 per patient at the value-based price, and avoided 906 hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) cases per 10,000 patients. Under the $13,500/QALY threshold, the value-based price per dose ranged from $92.92 to $367.44 across comparators. In the 48-week maintained-response scenario, the value-based price per dose was $70.29 versus ETV and ranged from $28.62 to $294.24 across comparators. The societal-perspective scenario increased the value-based price per dose to $426.41. Model-cohort expected value of perfect information (EVPI) was $0.46 million at the base-case threshold.ConclusionBepirovirsen’s value-based price is conditional on comparator prices, CET selection, durability of hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) seroclearance, safety, monitoring needs, and cured-state utility assumptions. These eHTA results are hypothesis-generating and should be interpreted within the limits of phase II-derived efficacy inputs, uncertain relapse pathways, and adverse-event and monitoring costs that cannot yet be estimated reliably.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13561-026-00827-8.

Introduction

Chronic hepatitis B (CHB) remains a major source of liver-related morbidity and mortality, affecting more than 240 million people and causing approximately 800,000 deaths annually worldwide [1]. Current nucleos(t)ide analogues (NAs) suppress viral replication, reduce liver-related complications, and form the backbone of long-term CHB management. They rarely eliminate covalently closed circular DNA (cccDNA), so many patients require prolonged or lifelong therapy [2]. Functional cure, commonly defined as sustained hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) seroclearance after finite treatment, has therefore become a central endpoint for new CHB therapies [3].

Bepirovirsen, an antisense oligonucleotide targeting hepatitis B virus RNA, has moved functional-cure strategies closer to economic evaluation. In the phase II B-Clear trial, bepirovirsen improved HBsAg outcomes in selected patients with CHB [4]. Phase III B-Well evidence became available after this model was parameterized and is discussed as contextual evidence rather than incorporated into the base case [5]. These data create a timely question for health technology assessment: how much value can a finite-course functional-cure strategy generate before long-term durability, relapse, retreatment, monitoring, and safety inputs are fully observed?

That question is especially important in cost-compressed settings. Previous threshold modelling of a potential CHB cure showed that the economically acceptable cure price is strongly shaped by existing antiviral costs, with lower value-based cure prices estimated for China than for higher-cost settings [6]. China bears a large share of the global CHB burden and has sharply reduced generic medicine prices through volume-based procurement, including for HBV antiviral medications [7, 8]. Low-cost generic NAs create a stringent comparator environment, so the value-based price of a new finite-course therapy depends not only on willingness to pay, but also on health opportunity cost (HOC), comparator choice, durability assumptions, and local treatment costs. GDP-multiple thresholds may not reflect the health foregone when resources are reallocated within budget-constrained health systems [9, 10]. Existing CHB economic evaluations have mainly focused on currently available NA or interferon strategies [11–13], leaving limited evidence on the value-based price corridor for investigational functional-cure therapies in low-cost comparator settings.

This study develops a phase II-based eHTA model to estimate a value-based price corridor for bepirovirsen in the Chinese setting. We examine how comparator choice, cost-effectiveness threshold (CET) selection, generic NA prices, durability assumptions, utility assumptions, societal-cost inputs, and value-of-information assumptions influence the estimated price per dose. Rather than recommending a launch price, the study provides a transparent scenario framework that clarifies which evidence elements shape the value-based price estimates for bepirovirsen and which inputs require phase III-calibrated reassessment.

Methods

This study followed Consolidated Health Economic Evaluation Reporting Standards (CHEERS) reporting guidelines (Supplementary 1). We used published aggregate data, trial-reported efficacy inputs, Chinese cost sources, and scenario assumptions to parameterize the model. Clinical response probabilities came from bepirovirsen and comparator evidence; disease progression, mortality, health-state utility, and cost inputs came from published CHB natural-history, pharmacoeconomic, quality-of-life, and cost studies. Supplementary tables report the parameter values, uncertainty ranges, distributions, and references used in the base-case and scenario analyses.

Model inputs were grouped into four data domains. First, efficacy inputs described virological suppression, HBeAg seroconversion, and HBsAg seroclearance by treatment strategy. Second, natural-history inputs described annual disease progression, liver-complication risks, and mortality. Third, economic inputs described drug acquisition costs, liver-related medical costs, non-medical costs, indirect costs, intangible costs, discounting, and currency conversion. Fourth, uncertainty inputs described parameter ranges and probability distributions for deterministic and probabilistic sensitivity analyses.

Model structure

From the healthcare-system perspective, we built a Markov state-transition model with a lifetime horizon, 1-year cycles, and half-cycle correction (Fig. 1). A one-year cycle was used because CHB progression probabilities, mortality inputs, annual costs, and health-state utilities were reported on an annual scale in the source literature. The 24-week bepirovirsen course and week-48 response assessment were implemented within the first model cycle. The model classified patients by virological response, hepatitis B e antigen (HBeAg) seroconversion, HBsAg seroclearance, hepatitis B virus (HBV) DNA category, alanine aminotransferase (ALT) category, cirrhosis status, and liver-related complications (Supplementary 2–6). Patients entered the functional-cure state only when they simultaneously achieved HBsAg seroclearance, undetectable HBV DNA, normal ALT levels, and normal liver status. The base case assumed discontinuation of antiviral therapy after these criteria were met. Non-responders discontinued the initial regimen and switched to best supportive care. Treatment resistance was modelled as a 3-month episode with uncontrolled HBV DNA and ALT, with a proportion progressing through acute flare to decompensated cirrhosis.

Patient population and interventions

The model population consisted of CHB patients aged 30 years or older; 26.47% were HBeAg-positive and 11% had cirrhosis at baseline [4]. These baseline characteristics were taken from the B-Clear bepirovirsen trial population used for efficacy parameterization. Two patient profiles reflected viral suppression and viremia, with HBV DNA and ALT distributions assigned from treatment-specific evidence [4, 11, 12]. We compared five strategies: bepirovirsen 300 mg/week for 24 weeks added to background NAs; entecavir (ETV) monotherapy; tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF) monotherapy; tenofovir alafenamide (TAF) monotherapy; and pegylated interferon alfa-2b (PEG-IFNα-2b) plus ETV. ETV represented the most conservative low-cost generic comparator in the China base case. TDF, TAF, and PEG-IFNα-2b plus ETV were retained to show how clinical efficacy and comparator cost affect the price corridor.

Transition probabilities

Annual transition probabilities were derived from established CHB disease progression models and natural-history cohorts [13–20] (Supplementary 4). We used these sources to assign transitions among HBeAg states, cirrhosis states, hepatocellular carcinoma, liver transplantation, post-transplantation, and death. Mortality was modelled as a composite of three elements: (1) background mortality rates, specific to age and sex [21] (Supplementary 5); (2) excess mortality due to active CHB, applied selectively to viremic patients with high HBV DNA loads (> 10^4 copies/mL) [22]; and (3) disease-specific mortality resulting from advanced liver complications [20, 23, 24] (Supplementary 6).

Treatment efficacy

Treatment effects were represented through probabilities of viral suppression, HBeAg seroconversion, and HBsAg seroclearance, the latter being the modelled marker of functional cure. Supplementary 7 reports the week-48 probabilities used for each strategy and the source assigned to each treatment input. The base case used an HBsAg seroclearance probability of 12.7% for bepirovirsen plus NAs and assumed that patients who achieved HBsAg seroclearance retained this status over the model horizon. Because the available phase II evidence does not define reliable lifetime relapse, rebound, or retreatment rates, we did not add a separate relapse or retreatment health state. Instead, we added a 48-week maintained-response scenario in which bepirovirsen efficacy was set to 9.0%, corresponding to the B-Clear primary-outcome rate among participants receiving background NA therapy: HBsAg below the lower limit of detection and HBV DNA below the lower limit of quantification maintained for 24 weeks after the planned end of bepirovirsen treatment. This scenario captures loss of response within the observed trial period, but it does not estimate relapse after week 48, retreatment eligibility, retreatment efficacy, or retreatment costs.

Health utilities and costing

Health-state utilities were sourced from published studies and assigned by liver-disease state, virological status, and functional-cure status [25–27] (Supplementary 8). Patients achieving HBsAg seroclearance were assigned age-matched general-population utilities in the base case. A conservative scenario capped cured-state utilities at 0.86 for younger age groups to reflect residual liver damage, comorbidities, monitoring needs, and possible residual quality-of-life decrements. Chinese cost inputs were valued in 2024 renminbi (RMB) and converted to US dollars using the 2024 annual average exchange rate of 7.1957 RMB per US dollar. We used US dollars as the reporting currency to improve international interpretability; the currency conversion does not change relative cost-effectiveness rankings when applied consistently. Standard-of-care costs were based on generic prices, reflecting the low-cost comparator environment created by generic NAs (Supplementary 9). The healthcare-system perspective included direct medical costs. The societal-perspective scenario additionally incorporated direct non-medical, indirect, and intangible costs. Bepirovirsen-specific adverse-event management and additional monitoring costs were not modelled separately because the available phase II evidence does not support reliable estimation of event frequencies by severity, monitoring intensity, long-term resource use, or management costs. Future costs and health outcomes were discounted at 5% annually, consistent with commonly used Chinese pharmacoeconomic evaluation guidance [28].

Health opportunity cost (HOC)-consistent threshold framing

The cost-threshold analysis estimated the maximum acquisition cost at which bepirovirsen remained cost-effective against each comparator. We adopted a base-case threshold of $13,500 per quality-adjusted life-year (QALY). This value approximates China’s 2024 GDP per capita after conversion from 95,749 RMB using the 2024 annual average exchange rate of 7.1957 RMB per US dollar (approximately $13,306/QALY). We rounded this value to $13,500/QALY for modelling and presentation. The threshold should not be interpreted as a US decision threshold or as a validated empirical opportunity-cost threshold for China. The value-based bepirovirsen acquisition cost was calculated by subtracting comparator-specific non-drug incremental costs from the allowable incremental cost and dividing the remaining allowable acquisition cost by 24 weekly doses.

Uncertainty and value-of-information analyses

Univariate sensitivity analysis tested clinical, utility, and cost parameters over the uncertainty ranges reported in the supplementary tables. Scenario analyses evaluated six domains: discounting and time horizon, threshold variation, maintained response, cured-state utility, societal perspective, and value of information. Discounting and time-horizon analyses used annual discount rates of 0% and 8% and time horizons of 30 and 40 years. Threshold analyses used alternative CETs to distinguish opportunity-cost-based pricing from higher normative willingness-to-pay (WTP) assumptions. The maintained-response scenario used a bepirovirsen efficacy input of 9.0%. Cured-state utility analysis used a 0.86 cap. Expected value of perfect information (EVPI) was calculated from the probabilistic sensitivity analysis using the parameter distributions specified for clinical, utility, and cost inputs in the supplementary tables, over the lifetime model horizon and for the same five decision alternatives. At each WTP threshold and probabilistic sensitivity analysis draw, net monetary benefit (NMB) was calculated for each strategy as QALYs multiplied by WTP minus costs. Per-patient EVPI equalled the mean across simulations of the maximum strategy-specific NMB minus the maximum of the strategy-specific mean NMBs. We multiplied per-patient EVPI by the simulated 10,000-patient cohort and reported cohort EVPI in millions of US dollars. EVPI was evaluated across WTP thresholds from $0 to $43,200/QALY. The analysis did not include future price uncertainty, national eligible-population extrapolation, treatment uptake, or implementation-horizon assumptions. EVPI is therefore reported for the model cohort rather than as a national population EVPI.

Cross-country value-based price corridor module

To explore transferability beyond the Chinese context, we developed a value-based price corridor module. Rather than reporting only a single country-specific price point, the module varied two structural inputs: the annual cost of generic standard-of-care (SOC), represented by ETV in the China base case, and the selected CET linked to opportunity-cost or willingness-to-pay assumptions. Clinical efficacy, disease progression, durability, health-state utility, epidemiological inputs, monitoring assumptions, and treatment pathways were held at China base-case values. The module therefore provides a scenario table for the value-based price corridor, not a fully country-specific model.

Results

Base case analysis

Bepirovirsen plus NAs generated 0.41–0.82 incremental QALYs across SOC comparators (Table 1). In the ETV comparison, the base-case model produced 0.82 additional discounted QALYs, incurred an incremental total cost of $10,833.72 per patient at the value-based price, and avoided 906 hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) cases per 10,000 patients. Variation across comparators reflected comparator-specific efficacy, HBsAg seroclearance, downstream complications, and annual treatment costs rather than a single uniform SOC assumption. Markov cohort traces showed that more patients entered the functional-cure state and fewer patients accumulated in advanced liver-disease states in the bepirovirsen arm (Fig. 2). These disease-trajectory differences reduced cumulative liver-related mortality (Fig. 3).

Uncertainty and scenario analyses

Univariate sensitivity analyses indicated that the value-based price per dose was most sensitive to the probability of HBsAg seroclearance and the utility assigned to functional cure (Fig. 4). Scenario analyses evaluated discounting, time horizon, threshold choice, societal perspective, and 48-week maintained response (Supplementary 10–15 and 17); the conservative cured-state utility scenario is summarized in the text. In the 48-week maintained-response scenario, bepirovirsen plus NAs generated 0.70 incremental QALYs and an incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER) of $13,504.55/QALY versus ETV; the corresponding value-based price was $70.29 per dose. Across comparators, value-based prices in this scenario ranged from $28.62 to $294.24 per dose (Supplementary 17). The conservative cured-state utility scenario generated 0.62 incremental QALYs and an ICER of -$12,034.86/QALY versus ETV. The societal perspective raised the value-based price to $426.41 per dose, but this estimate is exploratory because intangible costs may overlap with utility losses. A higher discount rate reduced the value-based price per dose to $76.65, while higher WTP thresholds increased the price corridor.

Across WTP thresholds from $0 to $43,200/QALY, model-cohort EVPI increased nonlinearly with the threshold (Supplementary 16). At the base-case threshold of $13,500/QALY, EVPI was $0.46 million for the 10,000-patient model cohort. At $40,500/QALY, approximately three times China’s 2024 GDP per capita, EVPI increased to $13.08 million. These value-of-information outputs indicate that uncertainty in durability, efficacy, and utility inputs has decision value, but they should not be interpreted as national population EVPI without explicit eligible-population, uptake, price-uncertainty, and implementation-horizon assumptions.

Cross-country value-based price corridor

Figure 5 presents the value-based price corridor across selected CETs and generic NA prices. It is a two-parameter scenario analysis, not a country-specific transfer model. At a CET of $13,500/QALY, modelled prices ranged from $101.58 to $165.77 per dose as annual generic NA cost increased from $20 to $1,000. At $50,000/QALY, the corresponding range increased to $303.27-$362.71 per dose.

The current China context sits at the lower end of the corridor, where generic comparator costs are very low and the threshold is conservative. Higher thresholds or higher comparator costs increase the allowable price, but the corridor holds China base-case clinical and epidemiological inputs fixed. Local adaptation would be required before applying these estimates to another health system.

Discussion

This eHTA shows that the value-based price estimates for bepirovirsen are highly sensitive to comparator prices and evidence uncertainty. When generic NA comparators are inexpensive, the value-based price of a finite-course functional-cure therapy depends strongly on cure durability, CET selection, comparator cost, and cured-state utility assumptions.

Our findings are consistent with earlier CHB economic evaluations and threshold modelling showing that low-cost antiviral therapy creates a demanding benchmark for new treatment strategies [6, 11, 13]. The China-specific volume-based procurement evidence for HBV antivirals further supports the interpretation of the current setting as cost-compressed [8]. Compared with ETV, the most conservative China-context comparator, bepirovirsen produced a lower value-based price than in comparisons with higher-cost or more efficacious alternatives. This pattern does not imply that comparator choice can be selected opportunistically. It shows that the estimated value-based price corridor must be interpreted against the local standard of care, reimbursement prices, and treatment pathway.

The analysis also extends prior CHB cost-effectiveness work by shifting the question from whether existing NA or interferon strategies are cost-effective to what allowable acquisition cost remains for an investigational functional-cure strategy. Bepirovirsen generated modelled value through two mechanisms: reduced long-term liver-related morbidity and avoidance of prolonged NA costs after functional cure. Both mechanisms depend on durable HBsAg seroclearance and safe treatment discontinuation. The 48-week maintained-response scenario therefore explores response loss within the observed B-Clear follow-up period only; it should not be interpreted as evidence for lifetime cure durability.

The threshold analysis requires the same caution. Value-based price calculations across alternative CETs are routine in health technology assessment practice. Our $13,500/QALY base-case threshold approximates China’s 2024 GDP per capita and provides a transparent China-context benchmark. It is not a validated empirical opportunity-cost threshold for China or a US decision threshold. Higher-threshold scenarios show how price estimates change under broader willingness-to-pay assumptions, but they do not resolve the underlying opportunity-cost question [9, 10].

The value-based price corridor in Fig. 5 functions as a scenario-based lookup table, not as a transferable international model. It varies generic SOC cost and CET assumptions while holding clinical efficacy, disease progression, durability, health-state utility, and epidemiological inputs at China base-case values. Many country-specific determinants of cost-effectiveness, including epidemiology, treatment pathways, healthcare costs, monitoring practice, reimbursement rules, and negotiated prices, remain fixed in this analysis. Local adaptation would therefore be required before applying the corridor outside China.

The EVPI results identify where additional evidence may have decision value. At the base-case threshold, EVPI was $0.46 million for the 10,000-patient model cohort, increasing to $13.08 million at the $40,500/QALY threshold. These values indicate that resolving uncertainty around durability, utility, and efficacy may be valuable. They should not be interpreted as national population EVPI because eligible population size, uptake, implementation horizon, price uncertainty, and future treatment pathways were not modelled. The phase III B-Well results strengthen the clinical relevance of functional-cure modelling [5], but a phase III-calibrated economic evaluation would require re-estimation of efficacy, discontinuation, adverse-event, monitoring, durability, relapse, retreatment, and patient-selection inputs.

The study has several methodological strengths. First, it keeps the economic claims aligned with the available clinical evidence. Second, the model reports a comparator-specific value-based price corridor instead of a single price point, making the role of generic NA costs transparent. Third, the scenario and EVPI analyses identify which assumptions most strongly influence the estimated value-based price per dose and where future evidence collection could be most useful.

The same design also has important limitations. Clinical efficacy parameters were derived from phase II evidence with stringent inclusion criteria, and the model does not include explicit relapse, viral rebound, or retreatment states. The base case assumes treatment discontinuation after functional-cure criteria are met and assigns age-matched general-population utilities to cured patients, although real-world utilities may remain lower because of liver damage, comorbidities, stigma, or monitoring needs. Bepirovirsen-specific adverse-event management and additional monitoring costs were not modelled separately, which may bias value-based price estimates upward. The societal-perspective scenario includes direct non-medical, indirect, and intangible costs, but intangible costs may overlap with utility losses. Finally, EVPI is reported for a 10,000-patient model cohort, and the transferability module varies only generic SOC cost and CET assumptions.

Future research should recalibrate this model with phase III efficacy, safety, discontinuation, monitoring, relapse, and retreatment data. Longer-term follow-up should estimate whether HBsAg seroclearance translates into durable treatment-free remission and reduced liver-related events. Country-specific applications should replace the China base-case inputs with local epidemiology, treatment pathways, unit costs, utilities, reimbursement structures, and implementation assumptions.

Conclusion

Bepirovirsen may offer a finite-course treatment pathway for selected patients with chronic hepatitis B, but its value-based price remains conditional on local comparator prices, cost-effectiveness threshold (CET) selection, durability of HBsAg seroclearance, safety, monitoring needs, and cured-state utility assumptions. In China, low-cost generic NA comparators constrain the estimated value-based price corridor. These hypothesis-generating eHTA results cannot substitute for a phase III-calibrated model with explicit durability, relapse, safety, retreatment, monitoring, and real-world effectiveness pathways.

Changing almost perfect nonlinear functions on affine subspaces of small codimensions
Hiroaki Taniguchi, Alexandr Polujan, Alexander Pott, Razi Arshad

Abstract
In this article, we study algebraic decompositions and secondary constructions of almost perfect nonlinear (APN) functions. In many cases, we establish precise criteria which characterize when certain modifications of a given APN function yield new ones. Furthermore, we show that some of the newly constructed functions are extended-affine inequivalent to the original ones.

Introduction

Let
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}
be the
n
-dimensional vector space over the finite field with two elements
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
. A mapping
F\colon{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{m}
is called an
(n,m)
-function. Almost perfect nonlinear (APN) functions are
(n,m)
-functions
F
with optimal differential properties. Formally, it means that the cardinality of the set
\{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}\colon F(x+a)+F(x)=b\}
, which is always even due to the parity of solutions
x
and
x+a
, is less than or equal to
2
, for any nonzero
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}
and for any
b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{m}
. Originally introduced in the
(n,n)
-case, APN functions are valuable primitives in cryptographic applications, especially in the design of S-Boxes
[
11
]
. Due to their correspondence to certain optimal codes with minimum distance five
[
10
]
and the possibility of constructing from them difference sets
[
14
,
15
]
and large Sidon sets
[
13
]
, they play a significant role in coding theory and, in general, discrete mathematics and combinatorics
[
17
]
. Recently, there has been an increasing interest in studying
(n,m)
-APN functions for the case
m>n
; for the recent works on the topic, we refer to
[
1
,
22
]
.

One of the central problems in the research on APN functions lies in discovering new constructions of these mappings. The known construction methods of such functions can be classified into primary and secondary. Primary constructions aim to provide new infinite families of APN functions “from scratch” (for the summary of the known infinite families, we refer to the recent article
[
19
]
), while secondary constructions aim to construct new functions from the known ones. Some of the well-known secondary construction methods of APN functions (we briefly explain them further) include the switching construction (and its various generalizations), modifications by applying functions of a special shape, and isotopic shift construction, to name a few.

For a given
(n,n)
-APN function
F
, represented as a vector of coordinate single-output functions
F(x)=(f_{1}(x),f_{2}(x),\ldots,f_{n}(x))
, the switching construction aims at constructing a new APN function of the form
F^{\prime}(x)=(f_{1}(x),f_{2}(x),\ldots,f^{\prime}_{n}(x))
, i.e., by modifying a single coordinate function of a given APN function (w.l.o.g, the last one). An advantage of this approach is that it preserves much of the information from the original function
F
, allowing room to demonstrate the APN property of a new function
F^{\prime}
; see, e.g.,
[
8
,
16
]
. Note that switching
[
16
]
and several related methods
[
26
,
18
]
, which can be viewed as its generalizations, can be used both for generating numerous new examples through computer searches in fixed dimensions and constructing new infinite families of APN functions
[
8
]
. In the latter case, one obtains APN functions of the form
G(x)=F(x)+Tr(H(x))
defined on the finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
, which gives the motivation to study further generalization in polynomial form. For example, in
[
9
,
24
]
, the authors studied the APN-ness of mappings
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\ni x\mapsto L_{1}(x^{3})+L_{2}(x^{9})
where
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are linear. In
[
12
]
, the authors investigated APN-ness of mappings of the form
G(x)=F(x)+\operatorname{Tr}(x)L(x)
, which corresponds to modifications by linear functions on hyperplanes. The studies
[
7
,
6
]
provide an analysis of the APN property of mappings defined as
G(x)=xL_{1}^{2^{i}}(x)+x^{2^{i}}L_{2}(x)
on
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
. This approach to constructing APN functions is called the (generalized) isotopic shift method.

While the previously discussed constructions focus on modifying a single APN function, another possible approach is to combine several given APN functions to build a new one. For example, the idea of constructing an APN function in
n+1
variables from two APN functions in
n
variables has been studied in
[
18
]
and later extended in
[
3
,
4
]
. These results, in turn, stimulate further investigations of various algebraic decompositions of APN functions. For example, the connection between different APN functions was shown useful, when certain decompositions of
(n,n)
-APN functions helped to characterize the structure of quadratic APN functions with the maximum linearity
[
4
]
.

These findings motivate our further investigation of variations of the known secondary constructions of APN functions, their algebraic decompositions, and, in particular, modifications of APN functions on affine subspaces with small codimensions. This approach preserves much of the structure of the original APN function enabling the verification of the APN property for a modified function and, in many cases, allowing for an explicit characterization of this property.

1.1
Preliminaries

Let
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
be the finite field of
2^{n}
elements. We sometimes identify
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
with
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}
as an
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
-vector space. We denote the sets
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\backslash\{0\}
by
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}^{\times}
and
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
by
({\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n})^{\times}
. For the finite fields
K\supset F
of characteristic
2
, we denote the
trace function
from
K
to
F
by
{\Tr}^{K}_{F}
. We denote
{\Tr}^{K}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{2}}
by
\Tr
and call it the
absolute trace
of
K
. The mappings
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\ni x\mapsto\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}x^{2^{i}}
, where all
a_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
, are called
linearized polynomials
; these are linear mappings defined on the finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
.

A function
F\colon{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{m}
is called an
almost perfect nonlinear (APN)

(n,m)
-function
if the cardinality of the set
\{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}\colon F(x+a)+F(x)=b\}
is less than or equal to
2
for any nonzero
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}
and for any
b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{m}
. Sometimes we omit the specification of
n
and
m
, when they are clear from the context. A function
F\colon{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{m}
is called
quadratic
if the mapping
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{m}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{m}\ni(x,y)\mapsto F(x+y)+F(x)+F(y)+F(0)
is
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
-bilinear.

On the set of all
(n,m)
-functions we introduce the following equivalence relations that preserve the APN property. The functions
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
from
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}
to
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{m}
are called
Carlet-Charpin-Zinoviev equivalent
(
CCZ-equivalent
, for short) if the graphs
G_{F_{1}}:=\{(x,F_{1}(x))\colon x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}\}
and
G_{F_{2}}:=\{(x,F_{2}(x))\colon x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}\}
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}\oplus{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{m}
are affine equivalent
[
10
]
, that is, if there exists an
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
-linear isomorphism
l\in GL_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}\oplus{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{m})
and an element
v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}\oplus{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{m}
such that
l(G_{F_{1}})+v=G_{F_{2}}
. We say that
(n,m)
-functions
F
and
F^{\prime}
are
extended-affine equivalent (EA-equivalent)
, if there exist two affine permutations
A_{1}\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m},A_{2}\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
and an affine function
A_{3}\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
such that
A_{1}\circ F\circ A_{2}+A_{3}=F^{\prime}
. We note that in general, CCZ-equivalence is coarser than EA-equivalence, and that EA-equivalent functions are also CCZ-equivalent. However, in the case of quadratic APN functions, they coincide, as shown in
[
25
]
.

Finally, we define the following invariant that will be used later to distinguish inequivalent functions. The
\Gamma
-rank
of a function
F\colon{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{m}
is the rank of the incidence matrix over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
of the incidence structure
\{{\cal P},{\cal B},I\}
, where
{\cal P}={\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}\oplus{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{m}
,
{\cal B}={\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}\oplus{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{m}
and
(a,b)I(u,v)
for
(a,b)\in{\cal P}
and
(u,v)\in{\cal B}
if and only if
F(a+u)=b+v
. It is well-known that if two functions
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
from
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}
to
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{m}
are CCZ-equivalent, then they have the same
\Gamma
-rank, see
[
16
]
.

For a function

Definition 2.1

Lemma 2.3

(Lemma 3 of

Proposition 2.4

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
f

{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}

Then, there exist a differentially

Then,

to

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
f,g
be functions from
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n-1}
to
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{m}
.
Let
e_{0}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}\backslash{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n-1}
.
Let us define
F
from
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}
to
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{m}
by
F(x):=f(x)
and
F(x+e_{0}):=g(x)

.
Then,
F
is an APN function if and only if the following conditions hold:

(1)

f
and
g
are APN functions from
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n-1}
to
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{m}
,

(2)

f(x+a)+f(x)+g(y+a)+g(y)\neq 0
holds for any
x,y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n-1}
and for any nonzero
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n-1}

Proof.

Recall that
F
is an APN function if and only if, for any nonzero

and for

F(X+A)+F(X)=F(Y+A)+F(Y)
implies
X=Y
or
X=Y+A
.
Note that condition (2) means: If
f(x)+f(y)+g(u)+g(v)\neq 0
if

are four different elements in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n-1}
with

.

Firstly, assume that
F
is an APN function. We will show that

and
g

(1)
and
(2)
.

Let
A=a\in({\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n-1})^{\times}
. For any

X=y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n-1}

, we have

. Thus,

must be an APN function.
Next, for any

with

X=y+e_{0}
or

.
Since
X=x+e_{0}

, we have
g(x+a)+g(x)=g(y+a)+g(y)
from

. Hence,
g

Thus, the condition
(1)
must be satisfied.

Let
A=a\in({\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n-1})^{\times}
. For any
Y=y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n-1}
, since
X=y
or
X=y+a
,
F(X+a)+F(X)=F(y+a)+F(y)
does not have a solution

for
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n-1}
. Thus,
F(x+e_{0}+a)+F(x+e_{0})\neq F(y+a)+F(y)
holds for any

, therefore we must have
g(x+a)+g(x)\neq f(y+a)+f(y)
for any
x,y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n-1}
.
Thus, the condition
(2)
must be satisfied.

Conversely, let us assume that the conditions
(1)
and
(2)
hold.
Assume
F(X+A)+F(X)=F(Y+A)+F(Y)
with
A\neq 0
. We will prove that
X=Y
or
X=Y+A
.
We divide this into the following four cases:

(i)

A=a\in({\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n-1})^{\times}
and
Y=y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n-1}
,

(ii)

A=a\in({\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n-1})^{\times}

,

(iii)

A=a+e_{0}
with

and
Y=y

,

(iv)

A=a+e_{0}
with

and
Y=y+e_{0}
with
y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n-1}
.

Firstly, let us consider the case (i).
If
X=x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n-1}
, then we have
f(x+a)+f(x)=f(y+a)+f(y)

x=y

x=y+a

(1)
. Let
X=x+e_{0}

.

X=Y
or
X=Y+A
in case (i).

Next, we consider the case (ii).
Assume

. Then, we have

(2)
.

with

x=y
.

(2.1)

3
Modifying quadratic APN functions on hyperplanes

In the previous section, we considered a method for constructing APN functions by concatenating
two
APN functions defined on complementary hyperplanes. An alternative approach to constructing new APN functions involves modifying a
single
given APN function on a hyperplane. In this case, the APN property is characterized as follows:

Corollary 3.1
.

[
12
, Corollary 1]

Let
T_{0}=\{x\colon\operatorname{Tr}(x)=0\}
and
G\colon\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
be defined by

G(x)=F(x)+\operatorname{Tr}(x)L(x),

where
F
and
L
are arbitrary mappings on
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
(where
L
is not necessarily linear). Then, for every
a\in T_{0}
we have

D_{a}G(x)=(F(x)+F(x+a))+\operatorname{Tr}(x)(L(x)+L(x+a)).

Suppose that the mappings
F(x)
and
F^{\prime}(x):=F(x)+L(x)
are APN. Set
I_{a}=\operatorname{Im}\left(D_{a}F\right)\cap\operatorname{Im}\left(D_{a}F^{\prime}\right)
. Then,
G
is APN if and only if for any nonzero
a\in T_{0}
and every
b\in I_{a}
there is
x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
with

D_{a}F(x)=b\text{ and }\operatorname{Tr}(x)=1\text{, or }D_{a}F^{\prime}(x)=b\text{ and }\operatorname{Tr}(x)=0.

In this section, we consider in detail a particular case when
F
is
quadratic APN
and
L
is
linear
.

3.1
A characterization for the quadratic case and the notion of H-equivalence

First, we derive a necessary and sufficient condition for the APN-ness of the functions
F(x)+\Tr(x)L(x)
on
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
, where
F
is a quadratic APN function and
L
is a linear mapping on
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
F
be a quadratic APN function on
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
and
L
an
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
-linear mapping on
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
. Let
e_{0}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
with
\Tr(e_{0})=1
.
Then, the mapping

G(x)=F(x)+\Tr(x)L(x),\quad\mbox{for}\quad x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}},

is a quadratic APN function on
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
if and only if
L_{a}\colon T_{0}\ni x\mapsto L(x)+B_{F}(x,a+e_{0})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
are one-to-one mappings from
T_{0}
to
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
for any
a\in T_{0}
.
(Hence,

L_{a}(x)=0

Let

to

to
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
. For

, we have

G^{\prime}
be a function on
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}

∎

\alpha^{6}+\alpha^{4}+\alpha^{3}+\alpha+1=0
. In Table

that define quadratic APN functions
G_{i}(x)=F(x)+\Tr(x)L_{i}(x)
. We also indicate, which of these functions are EA-equivalent to the

Remark 3.6
.

1. In
[
16
]
, it was shown that switching cannot be used to obtain representatives of all EA-equivalence classes of quadratic APN functions starting from a single class. However, this can be achieved using
H
-equivalence with the hyperplane
H=\{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\colon\Tr(x)=0\}
. Indeed, by Theorem
3.2
, two EA-inequivalent quadratic APN functions on
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{6}}
have the form
F(x)=x^{3}+\Tr(x)L(x)
and
F^{\prime}(x)=x^{3}+\Tr(x)L^{\prime}(x)
, from what follows that
F^{\prime}(x)=F(x)+\Tr(x)L^{\prime\prime}(x)
, where
L^{\prime\prime}:=L+L^{\prime}
is a linear mapping on
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{6}}
.

2. For an
(n,n)
-function
F
, the mapping
W_{F}\colon{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\to\mathbb{Z}
defined by
W_{F}(a,b)=\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}}(-1)^{\Tr(bF(x)+ax)}
, where
a,b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}},b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}^{\times}
, is called the Walsh transform of
F
. The multiset
\mathcal{W}_{F}=\{*\ W_{F}(a,b)\colon a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}},b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}^{\times}\ *\}
is called the Walsh spectrum of
F
. If
n
is even, an APN function
F
on
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
is said to have the the classical Walsh spectrum if

\begin{gathered}\mathcal{W}_{F}=\left\{*\ 0\left[2^{n-2}\cdot\left(2^{n}-1\right)\right],\pm 2^{\frac{n+2}{2}}\left[\frac{1}{3}\left(2^{n}-1\right)\cdot\left(2^{n-3}\pm 2^{\frac{n-4}{2}}\right)\right],\right.\\
\left.\pm 2^{\frac{n}{2}}\left[\frac{2}{3}\left(2^{n}-1\right)\cdot\left(2^{n-1}\pm 2^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\right)\right]\ *\right\}.\end{gathered}

Otherwise,
F
is said to have a non-classical Walsh spectrum; see
[
21
]
. Note that most of the known examples and constructions of quadratic APN functions on
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
have the classical Walsh spectrum and only a few sporadic examples with a non-classical spectrum are known; for the recent advances in such functions, we refer to
[
4
]
. Note that, up to EA-equivalence, the function
G_{7}
in Table
3.1
is the only quadratic APN function on
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{6}}

with a non-classical Walsh spectrum since its Walsh transform takes the values in the set
\{0,\pm 8,\pm 16,\pm 32\}
. Remarkably, this function can be constructed via
H
-equivalence from the Gold APN function
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{6}}\ni x\mapsto x^{3}
with the classical Walsh spectrum.

3.2
A necessary and sufficient condition for the APN-ness of
G(x)=x^{3}+\operatorname{Tr}(x)L(x)

In the previous subsection, we showed that one can obtain many EA-inequivalent APN functions by modifying the mapping
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\mapsto x^{3}
on the “trace-one” hyperplane. In this regard, it is natural to ask: When does the mapping
G(x)=x^{3}+Tr(x)L(x)
define an APN function
G
on
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
? In the following theorem, we provide an answer to this question using certain exponential sums.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
G
be a function from
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
defined as

G(x)=x^{3}+\Tr(x)L(x).

Then,
G
is APN if and only if

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}\setminus\{0,1\}}(-1)^{\Tr\left(\frac{x^{2}L(x^{2}+x)}{(x^{2}+x)^{3}}\right)}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}\setminus\{0,1\}}(-1)^{\Tr\left(\frac{L(x^{2}+x)}{(x^{2}+x)^{3}}\right)}=2^{n-1}-1.

(3.1)

Proof.

Assume that for every
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
with
a\neq 0
, the equation
G(x+a)+G(x)=b
has at most two solutions. Since
G(x)
is a quadratic function, we just count the number of solutions of the equation
G(x+a)+G(x)+G(a)=0
. This gives

\begin{split}G(x+a)+G(x)+G(a)&=(x+a)^{3}+\Tr(x+a)L(x+a)\\
&+x^{3}+\Tr(x)L(x)+a^{3}+\Tr(a)L(a)=0.\end{split}

After simplification, we have

x^{2}a+a^{2}x+\Tr(x)L(a)+\Tr(a)L(x)=0.

(3.2)

We have to show that this equation has only two solutions for
a\neq 0
. Note that we can see immediately two solutions,
namely
x=0
and
x=a
.

I.
Consider the case
\Tr(a)=0
. Then, Eq. (
3.2
) becomes

x^{2}a+a^{2}x+\Tr(x)L(a)=0.

(3.3)

We show that this equation has exactly two solutions if and only if the condition in Eq. (
3.1
) holds.
Assume that
\Tr(x)=0
. Then, Eq. (
3.3
) becomes

x^{2}a+a^{2}x=0.

The above equation has exactly two solutions
x\in\{0,a\}
. Now, we show that the case
\Tr(x)=1
gives no solutions if and only if Eq. (
3.1
) holds. Assume that
\Tr(x)=1
. Then, Eq. (
3.3
) becomes

x^{2}a+a^{2}x+L(a)=0.

(3.4)

Let
H=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}\colon\Tr(x)=0\},\hat{H}=\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}\backslash H
and
H^{\times}=H\backslash\{0\}
. Let

\begin{split}t_{0}=&\#\{x\in H,a\in H^{\times}\colon x^{2}a+a^{2}x+L(a)=0\},\mbox{ and}\\
t_{1}=&\#\{x\in\hat{H},a\in H^{\times}\colon x^{2}a+a^{2}x+L(a)=0\}.\end{split}

Then, we define

\begin{split}s_{0}=&\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}},a\in H^{\times},x\in H}(-1)^{\Tr(\beta(x^{2}a+a^{2}x+L(a)))}=2^{n}t_{0},\\
s_{1}=&\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}},a\in H^{\times},x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}}(-1)^{\Tr(\beta(x^{2}a+a^{2}x+L(a)))}=2^{n}(t_{0}+t_{1}).\end{split}

In this way, the value of
t_{1}
is given by

t_{1}=\frac{s_{1}-s_{0}}{2^{n}}.

First, we consider the expressions of
s_{1}
and
s_{0}
:

\begin{split}s_{1}&=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}},a\in H^{\times}}(-1)^{\Tr(\beta L(a))}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}}(-1)^{\Tr(\beta(x^{2}a+a^{2}x))}\\
&=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}},a\in H^{\times}}(-1)^{\Tr(\beta L(a))}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}}(-1)^{\Tr(\beta a^{3}((\frac{x}{a})^{2}+(\frac{x}{a})))},\\
\end{split}

(3.5)

and similarly

\begin{split}s_{0}&=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}},a\in H^{\times}}(-1)^{\Tr(\beta L(a))}\sum_{x\in H}(-1)^{\Tr(\beta(x^{2}a+a^{2}x))}\\
&=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}},a\in H^{\times}}(-1)^{\Tr(\beta L(a))}\sum_{x\in H}(-1)^{\Tr\left(\beta a^{3}\left((\frac{x}{a})^{2}+(\frac{x}{a})\right)\right)}.\end{split}

(3.6)

Since both expressions depend on the evaluation of
\Tr(\beta a^{3}((\frac{x}{a})^{2}+(\frac{x}{a})))
, let us consider the solutions of the equation
\Tr(\beta a^{3}((\frac{x}{a})^{2}+(\frac{x}{a})))=0
. Note that for
\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
and a fixed
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
with
\Tr(a)=0
we have that

\Tr\left(\beta a^{3}\left(\left(\frac{x}{a}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{x}{a}\right)\right)\right)=0

holds for all
x\in H
if and only if

\Tr\left(x^{2}\left(\frac{\beta a^{3}}{a^{2}}+\frac{\beta^{2}a^{6}}{a^{2}}\right)\right)=0\iff\left(\frac{\beta a^{3}}{a^{2}}+\frac{\beta^{2}a^{6}}{a^{2}}\right)=0\mbox{ or }1.

First, we consider

\left(\frac{\beta a^{3}}{a^{2}}+\frac{\beta^{2}a^{6}}{a^{2}}\right)=0.

Then, we have the following cases:

(1)

\beta=0
,

(2)

\beta a^{3}+\beta^{2}a^{6}=0

if and only if
\beta=\frac{1}{a^{3}}

(provided
\beta\neq 0
).

Now, we consider the case when

\left(\frac{\beta a^{3}}{a^{2}}+\frac{\beta^{2}a^{6}}{a^{2}}\right)=1.

In this case,
\Tr\left(\beta a^{3}\left(\left(\frac{x}{a}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{x}{a}\right)\right)\right)=0
holds for all
x\in H
, but
not for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
, hence this case occurs just
in Eq. (
3.6
), but not in Eq. (
3.5
). The latter corresponds to the following case

(3)

\beta a^{3}+\beta^{2}a^{6}=a^{2}.

Considering these cases, we can now compute the values of
s_{1}
and
s_{0}
. With the first two cases, Eq. (
3.5
) becomes

\begin{split}s_{1}&=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}},a\in H^{\times}}(-1)^{\Tr(\beta L(a))}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}}(-1)^{\Tr(\beta a^{3}((\frac{x}{a})^{2}+(\frac{x}{a})))}\\
&=2^{n}(2^{n-1}-1)+2^{n}\left(\sum_{a\in H^{\times}}(-1)^{\Tr\left(\frac{L(a)}{a^{3}}\right)}\right),\end{split}

(3.7)

where the first term corresponds to case (1)
\beta=0
, while the second term corresponds to case (2)
\beta a^{3}=1
.
Consider now Eq. (
3.6
). Let
\gamma=\beta a^{3}
. Then, we have
\beta=\frac{\gamma}{a^{3}}
. From case (3), we have that
\gamma+\gamma^{2}=a^{2}
. Therefore, we obtain

\begin{split}s_{0}=&2^{n-1}(2^{n-1}-1)+2^{n-1}\sum_{a\in H^{\times}}(-1)^{\Tr\left(\frac{L(a)}{a^{3}}\right)}\\
+&2^{n-1}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in H^{\times}s.t.\;a^{2}=\gamma+\gamma^{2}\\
for\;\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}\backslash\{0,1\}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\Tr\left(\gamma\frac{L(a)}{a^{3}}\right)}\right),\end{split}

where the first two terms are again obtained as in Eq. (
3.7
). Now, substitute
a=\sqrt{\gamma+\gamma^{2}}
, the above equation becomes

\begin{split}s_{0}=&2^{n-1}(2^{n-1}-1)+2^{n-1}\sum_{a\in H^{\times}}(-1)^{\Tr\left(\frac{L(a)}{a^{3}}\right)}\\
+&2^{n-1}\left(\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}\setminus\{0,1\}}(-1)^{\Tr\left(\frac{\gamma L(\sqrt{\gamma+\gamma^{2}})}{(\sqrt{\gamma+\gamma^{2}})^{3}}\right)}\right)\\
=&\frac{s_{1}}{2}+2^{n-1}\left(\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}\setminus\{0,1\}}(-1)^{\Tr\left(\frac{\gamma L(\sqrt{\gamma+\gamma^{2}})}{(\sqrt{\gamma+\gamma^{2}})^{3}}\right)}\right).\end{split}

(3.8)

Eq. (
3.3
) has exactly two solutions
for
a\neq 0
with
\Tr(a)=0
if and only if
t_{1}=s_{1}-s_{0}=0
, hence
s_{0}=s_{1}
. Substituting
s_{0}=s_{1}
in Eq. (
3.8
) and replacing
\gamma
with
x^{2}
, we get

s_{1}=2^{n}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}\setminus\{0,1\}}(-1)^{\Tr\left(\frac{x^{2}L(x^{2}+x)}{(x^{2}+x)^{3}}\right)}.

(3.9)

Substituting the obtained above value of
s_{1}
in Eq. (
3.7
),
we get

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}\setminus\{0,1\}}(-1)^{\Tr\left(\frac{x^{2}L(x^{2}+x)}{(x^{2}+x)^{3}}\right)}-\sum_{a\in H^{\times}}(-1)^{\Tr\left(\frac{L(a)}{a^{3}}\right)}=2^{n-1}-1,

which is equivalent to the original condition in Eq. (
3.1
) since

\sum_{a\in H^{\times}}(-1)^{\Tr\left(\frac{L(a)}{a^{3}}\right)}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}\setminus\{0,1\}}(-1)^{\Tr\left(\frac{L(x^{2}+x)}{(x^{2}+x)^{3}}\right)},

because
x^{2}+x
runs through all the elements in
H^{\times}
twice if
x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}\setminus\{0,1\}
. This completes the proof of the first case
\Tr(a)=0
.

II.
Consider the case
\Tr(a)=1
. Then, Eq. (
3.2
) becomes

x^{2}a+a^{2}x+\Tr(x)L(a)+L(x)=0.

(3.10)

Assume that
\Tr(x)=0
. Then, Eq. (
3.10
) becomes

x^{2}a+a^{2}x+L(x)=0,

which is symmetric to Eq. (
3.4
) w.r.t. a change of the variables. Assuming that there is a solution
x\neq 0
with
\Tr(x)=0
, one can find
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
s.t. it satisfies Eq. (
3.4
).
Case
I
shows that
\Tr(a)=0
, a contradiction.

Assume now that
\Tr(x)=1
.
In this case,

x^{2}a+a^{2}x+L(a)+L(x)=0

or, putting
y=x+a
,

y^{2}a+a^{2}y+L(y)=0.

(3.11)

One solution is
y=0
, hence
x=a
. But for a given
y\neq 0
with
\Tr(y)=0
, there is no
a
with
\Tr(a)=1
which satisfies Eq. (
3.11
), as in case
I
, hence also for
\Tr(a)=1
we have the only two solutions

x\in\{0,a\}
for Eq. (
3.2
).

In this way, we conclude that
G
is APN if and only if the condition in Eq. (
3.1
) holds.
∎

4
Modifying APN functions on affine subspaces of codimension two

In the previous section, we showed that it is possible to construct a new APN function from a given one by adding a linear function defined on a hyperplane. In this section, we show that new APN functions can be obtained by adding carefully selected constant functions on disjoint affine subspaces of codimension two. The following theorem provides a complete characterization of the APN property for such modifications.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
F
be an APN function from
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
. Let
U
be an
(n-2)
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
and let
U_{1}=U+u_{1},\ U_{2}=U+u_{2},\ U_{3}=U+u_{3}
and
U_{4}=U+u_{4}
be the four cosets of
U
such that
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}=U_{1}\cup U_{2}\cup U_{3}\cup U_{4},
where
u_{1}=0
and
u_{2},\ u_{3},\ u_{4}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
. Define the function
G\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
as follows

G(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cccc}F(x)+a_{1},&&\text{for }x\in U_{1},\\[4.30554pt]
F(x)+a_{2},&&\text{for }x\in U_{2},\\[4.30554pt]
F(x)+a_{3},&&\text{for }x\in U_{3},\\[4.30554pt]
F(x)+a_{4},&&\text{for }x\in U_{4},\end{array}\right.

(4.1)

where
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
. Then, the function
G
is APN if and only if the condition

F(x_{1})+F(x_{2})+F(x_{3})+F(x_{4})\neq a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}+a_{4}

holds for all
2
-dimensional affine subspaces
\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\}
of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
with
|\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\}\cap U_{i}|=1
, for all
i=1,2,3,4
.

Proof.

Let
U
be an
(n-2)
-dimensional linear subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
. We decompose
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
into four cosets of
U
such that
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}=U_{1}\cup U_{2}\cup U_{3}\cup U_{4},
where
U_{1}=U+u_{1}=U,\ U_{2}=U+u_{2},\\
U_{3}=U+u_{3}
and
U_{4}=U+u_{4}
, where
u_{1}=0
and
u_{2},u_{3},u_{4}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
. Since
F
is an APN function, we have that the condition

F(x_{1})+F(x_{2})+F(x_{3})+F(x_{4})\neq 0

holds for all
2
-dimensional affine subspaces
\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\}
of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
. Now, we consider the distribution of affine
2
-dimensional subspaces
\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\}
in the subspaces
U_{1},U_{2},U_{3},U_{4}
.

Recall that the set
\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\}
is an affine
2
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
if
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}
are pairwise different and
x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4}=0
. Assume that
x_{1},x_{2}\in U_{i}
,
x_{3}\in U_{j}
and
x_{4}\in U_{k}
, where
i,j,k
are pairwise different. Then,
x_{1}+x_{2}\in U_{1}
and
x_{3}+x_{4}\notin U_{1}
. Therefore,
x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4}=0\in U_{1}
is not possible. The only possible distributions of
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}
between
U_{i}
are either:

1)

x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\in U_{i}
, for some
i
,

2)

x_{1},x_{2}\in U_{i}
and
x_{3},x_{4}\in U_{j}
, for some
i\neq j
,

3)

x_{1}\in U_{i},x_{2}\in U_{j},x_{3}\in U_{k},x_{4}\in U_{l}
, for
i\neq j\neq k\neq l
(equivalently, the condition
|\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\}\cap U_{i}|=1
holds for all
i=1,2,3,4
).

In order to derive a necessary and sufficient condition for the APN-ness of the function
G
defined in Eq. (
4.1
), we need to verify that
G(x_{1})+G(x_{2})+G(x_{3})+G(x_{4})\neq 0
holds for all 2-dimensional flats
\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\}
. To do so, we consider three possible distributions of the elements
x_{i}
between the subspaces
U_{j}
.

In the first case, we have that
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\in U_{i}
, for some
i
. Then,

\begin{split}&G(x_{1})+G(x_{2})+G(x_{3})+G(x_{4})\\
=&(F(x_{1})+a_{i})+(F(x_{2})+a_{i})+(F(x_{3})+a_{i})+(F(x_{4})+a_{i})\neq 0\end{split}

due to the APN property of
F
.

In the second case, we have that
x_{1},x_{2}\in U_{i}
and
x_{3},x_{4}\in U_{j}
, for some
i\neq j
. Consequently,

\begin{split}&G(x_{1})+G(x_{2})+G(x_{3})+G(x_{4})\\
=&(F(x_{1})+a_{i})+(F(x_{2})+a_{i})+(F(x_{3})+a_{j})+(F(x_{4})+a_{j})\neq 0\end{split}

again due to the APN property of
F
.

In the third case, we have that the condition
|\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\}\cap U_{i}|=1
holds for all
i=1,2,3,4
. W.l.o.g, we assume that
x_{i}\in U_{i}
. Then, we have

\begin{split}&G(x_{1})+G(x_{2})+G(x_{3})+G(x_{4})\\
=&(F(x_{1})+a_{1})+(F(x_{2})+a_{2})+(F(x_{3})+a_{3})+(F(x_{4})+a_{4})\\
=&F(x_{1})+F(x_{2})+F(x_{3})+F(x_{4})+(a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}+a_{4}).\end{split}

In this way, the function
G
is APN if and only if the condition

F(x_{1})+F(x_{2})+F(x_{3})+F(x_{4})\neq a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}+a_{4}

holds for all 2-dimensional affine subspaces
\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\}
of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
with the property
|\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\}\cap U_{i}|=1
for all
i=1,2,3,4
.
∎

In the following example, we indicate that using Theorem
4.1
one construct an APN function, that is EA-inequivalent to a given one.

Example 4.2
.

Let
F
be an APN function from
\mathbb{F}_{2^{8}}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2^{8}}
defined as
F(x)=x^{3}
. Let the multiplicative group of
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{8}}
be defined by
({\mathbb{F}}_{2^{8}})^{*}=\langle a\rangle
, where
\alpha^{8}+\alpha^{4}+\alpha^{3}+\alpha^{2}+1=0
. First, we decompose
\mathbb{F}_{2^{8}}
into four cosets
U_{1},U_{2},U_{3},U_{4}
defined by

\begin{split}U_{1}=&\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{8}}\colon\Tr^{8}_{2}(x)=0\},\\
U_{2}=&\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{8}}\colon\Tr^{8}_{2}(x)=1\},\\
U_{3}=&\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{8}}\colon\Tr^{8}_{2}(x)=\beta\},\\
U_{4}=&\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{8}}\colon\Tr^{8}_{2}(x)=\beta^{2}\},\\
\end{split}

where
\beta
is a root of
x^{2}+x+1
; in the defined above field
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{8}}
, we have that
\beta\in\{\alpha^{85},\alpha^{170}\}
. Compute the set

\begin{split}A&={\mathbb{F}}_{2^{8}}\setminus\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{4}F(x_{i})\colon\sum_{i=1}^{4}x_{i}=0\mbox{ and }x_{i}\in U_{i},\mbox{ for }i=1,2,3,4\right\}\\
&=\{0,1,\alpha^{85},\alpha^{170}\}.\end{split}

We can choose
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{8}}
such that
a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}+a_{4}\in A
. Setting
a_{1}=a_{2}=0,a_{3}=\alpha^{85}\beta,a_{4}=\alpha^{85}\beta^{2}
, we have that
a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}+a_{4}=\alpha^{85}\in A
. Define the function
G\colon{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{8}}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{8}}
as in Eq. (
4.1
), that is

G(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cccc}F(x)+a_{1},&&\text{for }x\in U_{1},\\[4.30554pt]
F(x)+a_{2},&&\text{for }x\in U_{2},\\[4.30554pt]
F(x)+a_{3},&&\text{for }x\in U_{3},\\[4.30554pt]
F(x)+a_{4},&&\text{for }x\in U_{4},\end{array}\right.=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cccc}F(x),&&\text{if }\Tr^{8}_{2}(x)=0,\\[4.30554pt]
F(x),&&\text{if }\Tr^{8}_{2}(x)=1,\\[4.30554pt]
F(x)+\alpha^{85}\beta,&&\text{if }\Tr^{8}_{2}(x)=\beta,\\[4.30554pt]
F(x)+\alpha^{85}\beta^{2},&&\text{if }\Tr^{8}_{2}(x)=\beta^{2}.\end{array}\right.

Then, the function
G
is APN by Theorem
4.1
. For the constructed function
G
, we give a “simplified” finite filed representation. To do so, we recall that the polynomial representation of the trace function from
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
to its subfield
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}
is given by
\Tr^{n}_{m}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{\frac{n}{m}-1}x^{2^{i\cdot m}}
. First, we observe that
\Tr^{8}_{1}(x)=\Tr^{2}_{1}(\Tr^{8}_{2}(x))=\Tr^{8}_{2}(x)+(\Tr^{8}_{2}(x))^{2}
. In this way, we have that
if
\Tr^{8}_{2}(x)\in\{0,1\}
then
\Tr^{8}_{1}(x)=0
. On the other hand, if
\Tr^{8}_{2}(x)\in\{\beta,\beta^{2}\}
then
\Tr^{8}_{1}(x)=1
. In this way, the APN function
G\colon\mathbb{F}_{2^{8}}\to\mathbb{F}_{2^{8}}
can be written as

G(x)=x^{3}+\alpha^{85}\Tr^{8}_{1}(x)\Tr^{8}_{2}(x).

Finally, we notice that
G
is EA-inequivalent to
F(x)=x^{3}
on
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{8}}
, since
\Gamma\mbox{-rank}(G)=13\,842\neq\Gamma\mbox{-rank}(F)=11\,818
.

5
Conclusion and open problems

In this paper, we consider further secondary constructions of APN functions. The following questions related to the computational results obtained in Section
3
deserve further theoretical investigations:

1.

As we show in Remark
3.5
, every quadratic APN function in dimension 6 can be written (up to EA-equivalence) as a univariate polynomial
G_{i}(x)=x^{3}+\Tr(x)L_{i}(x)
, for
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{6}}
, where
L_{i}\colon{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{6}}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{6}}
is a suitable linear mapping. Alternatively, as shown in
[
7
]
, each quadratic APN function in dimension 6 can also be characterized (up to EA-equivalence) through the isotopic shift construction. We believe it would be valuable to explore these two approaches further and gain a deeper understanding of their connections.

2.

As we indicate in Remark
3.5
, it is possible to construct APN functions with the non-classical spectrum from those with the classical one by modifying them on hyperplanes using suitable linear functions. Find more such examples in higher dimensions and analyze whether infinite families of APN functions with the non-classical Walsh spectrum of the form
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\ni x\mapsto x^{3}+\Tr(x)L(x)
exist.

3.

More general, find linearized polynomials satisfying the APN condition given in Eq. (
3.1
), which inevitably lead to quadratic APN functions of the form
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\ni x\mapsto x^{3}+\Tr(x)L(x)
. Is it possible to obtain APN functions of the form
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\ni x\mapsto x^{3}+\Tr(x)Q(x)
, where
Q
is quadratic, or more general, nonlinear?

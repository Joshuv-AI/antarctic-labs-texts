Representations of the Drinfeld doubles of pointed rank one Hopf algebras Thanks: 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification . 16T99, 16E99, 16G70
Hua Sun, Hui-Xiang Chen, Yinhuo Zhang

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the representations of the Drinfeld doubles D ⁡ ( H 𝒟 ) D(H_{\mathcal{D}}) of pointed rank one Hopf algebras H 𝒟 H_{\mathcal{D}} over an algebraically closed field 𝕜 \Bbbk of characteristic zero. We provide a complete classification of all finite-dimensional indecomposable D ⁡ ( H 𝒟 ) D(H_{\mathcal{D}}) -modules up to isomorphism and explicitly describe the Auslander-Reiten sequences in the category of finite-dimensional D ⁡ ( H 𝒟 ) D(H_{\mathcal{D}}) -modules. We show that D ⁡ ( H 𝒟 ) D(H_{\mathcal{D}}) is of tame representation type.



1.
Introduction

Finite-dimensional Hopf algebras play a central role in modern algebra and quantum group theory, particularly through their connections with tensor categories, knot and 3-manifold invariants, and noncommutative geometry. Among them, pointed Hopf algebras of rank one constitute one of the simplest nontrivial families. Despite their apparent simplicity, their structure and representation theory already exhibit many of the key phenomena appearing in the general theory.

The systematic study of pointed Hopf algebras began in the 1990s with the classification program of Andruskiewitsch and Schneider, who reduced the classification problem to the analysis of Nichols algebras. In the rank-one case, the corresponding Nichols algebra is one-dimensional, and the resulting Hopf algebras generalize the classical Taft algebras introduced by E. Taft
[
21
]
, which provided the first explicit examples of finite-dimensional nonsemisimple, noncommutative Hopf algebras.

The first systematic investigation of finite-dimensional pointed Hopf algebras of rank one was carried out by Krop and Radford
[
13
]
, who classified all such algebras over algebraically closed fields of characteristic zero. The classification in positive characteristic was later completed by Scherotzke
[
16
]
. These Hopf algebras can also be realized as quotients of Hopf-Ore extensions of group algebras, a framework developed by Wang, You, and Chen. The classification over arbitrary fields was subsequently obtained by Wang, You and Chen
[
24
]
.

The representation theory of these Hopf algebras has been the subject of intensive study. In
[
9
]
, Cibils investigated the indecomposable modules over Taft algebras and derived explicit decomposition formulas for tensor products of indecomposable modules. Building on Cibils’s results, Chen, Van Oystaeyen, and Zhang determined the Green rings of all Taft algebras
[
8
]
, while Li and Zhang computed the Green rings of generalized Taft algebras
[
14
]
. The representation theory of finite-dimensional pointed Hopf algebras of rank one was subsequently studied in full generality by Wang, Li, and Zhang
[
22
,
23
]
. These works describe not only the categories of indecomposable modules but also the fusion rules governing their tensor product structures.

Another line of research concerns the Drinfeld doubles of pointed rank-one Hopf algebras, which are quasi-triangular and therefore play a fundamental role in the study of braided tensor categories. The representation theory of Drinfeld doubles of Taft algebras was studied in detail by Chen
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
, who described the simple and indecomposable modules, projective covers, and block structures. These results highlight the deep connections between pointed rank-one Hopf algebras, their doubles, and quantum groups at roots of unity. Erdmann et al. investigated the representations and stable Green rings of Drinfeld doubles of generalized Taft algebras in
[
10
,
11
]
, while Sun and Chen studied the representations of Drinfeld doubles of Radford Hopf algebras in
[
17
]
. The (generalized) Taft algebras are pointed rank-one Hopf algebras of nilpotent type, whereas the Radford algebras are of non-nilpotent type. Krop and Radford studied the representations of the Drinfeld double
D(H_{\mathcal{D}})
of any finite-dimensional pointed rank one Hopf algebra
H_{\mathcal{D}}
over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, classifying all simple and projective indecomposable
D(H_{\mathcal{D}})
-modules in the case where the group
G(H_{\mathcal{D}})
of group-like elements is abelian
[
13
]
. However, a complete classification of all finite-dimensional indecomposable
D(H_{\mathcal{D}})
-modules remains open.

In this article, building on the work of Krop and Radford
[
13
]
, we study the indecomposable representations of the Drinfeld doubles
D(H_{\mathcal{D}})
of pointed rank-one Hopf algebras
H_{\mathcal{D}}
over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
of characteristic zero, assuming that
G(H_{\mathcal{D}})
is abelian.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall the definition of a group datum, the structure of a pointed rank one Hopf algebra
H_{\mathcal{D}}
, and its Drinfeld double
D(H_{\mathcal{D}})
.

In Section
3
, we review the simple modules and projective indecomposable modules over
D(H_{\mathcal{D}})
, showing that the Loewy length of
D(H_{\mathcal{D}})
is three. We divide both the simple and non-simple projective indecomposable modules into the nilpotent and non-nilpotent cases, and provide standard
\Bbbk
-bases for each of these modules.

In Section

D(H_{\mathcal{D}})
-modules of Loewy length two. Using Auslander-Reiten theory, we describe all such modules and classify them up to isomorphism. Moreover, all Auslander-Reiten sequences in the category of finite-dimensional
D(H_{\mathcal{D}})
-modules are explicitly presented. Finally, we show that

is of tame representation type.

Let

The comultiplication

\triangle(x)=x\otimes a+1\otimes x\text{ and }\triangle(g)=g\otimes g,\ g\in G.

is of

. For the details, one can refer to
[
13
]
.

In the sequel, we let
\mathcal{D}=(G,\chi,a,\alpha)
be a group datum, and assume that

is abelian. Let

and denote by

and

is the dual Hopf algebra of

.

is generated as an algebra by

The coalgebra structure of

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 5.69046pt(g\gamma)m_{i}=(\phi^{i}\lambda)(g\gamma)m_{i},&g\in G,\ \gamma\in\Gamma,\ 0\leqslant i\leqslant n-1,\\
xm_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\alpha_{i+1}(n,\lambda)m_{i+1},&0\leqslant i\leqslant n-2,\\
0,&i=n-1,\\
\end{array}\right.&\xi m_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&i=0,\\
m_{i-1},&1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1.\\
\end{array}\right.\\
\end{array}

It is clear that
V(n,\lambda)^{x}=\Bbbk v_{n-1}=\Bbbk m_{n-1}
and
V(n,\lambda)^{\xi}=\Bbbk v_{0}=\Bbbk m_{0}
.

If
\mathcal{D}
is of
non-nilpotent type
, then the
D(H_{\mathcal{D}})
-module actions on the two bases of
V(n,\lambda)
are given respectively by

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 5.69046pt(g\gamma)v_{i}=(\phi^{i}\lambda)(g\gamma)v_{i},&g\in G,\ \gamma\in\Gamma,\ 0\leqslant i\leqslant n-1,\\
xv_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}v_{i+1},&0\leqslant i\leqslant n-2,\\
(\lambda^{n}(a)-1)v_{0},&i=n-1,\\
\end{array}\right.&\xi v_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&i=0,\\
\alpha_{i}(l,\lambda)v_{i-1},&1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1.\\
\end{array}\right.\\
\end{array}

and

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 5.69046pt(g\gamma)m_{i}=(\phi^{i}\lambda)(g\gamma)m_{i},&g\in G,\ \gamma\in\Gamma,\ 0\leqslant i\leqslant n-1,\\
xm_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\alpha_{i+1}(n,\lambda)m_{i+1},&0\leqslant k\leqslant n-2,\\
\frac{\lambda^{n}(a)-1}{\beta(n,\lambda)}m_{0},&i=n-1,\\
\end{array}\right.&\xi m_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&i=0,\\
m_{i-1},&1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1.\\
\end{array}\right.\\
\end{array}

Clearly,
V(n,\lambda)^{\xi}=\Bbbk v_{0}=\Bbbk m_{0}
.

By the above discussion, we have following explicit description of the simple
D(H_{\mathcal{D}})
-modules.

Proposition 3.6
.

The following set

\{V(l,\lambda)|1\leqslant l\leqslant n,\lambda\in I_{l}\}

gives a complete set of representatives of isomorphism classes of simple
D(H_{\mathcal{D}})
-modules. Moreover,
V(n,\lambda)
is a projective
D(H_{\mathcal{D}})
-module for any
\lambda\in I_{n}
.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
M
be a finite dimensional semisimple
D(H_{\mathcal{D}})
-module. Then
l(M)={\rm dim}(M^{\xi})
. Furthermore, if
\mathcal{D}
is of nilpotent type, then
l(M)={\rm dim}(M^{x})
.

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 5.69046pt(g\gamma)v_{i}=(\phi^{i}\lambda)(g\gamma)v_{i},&g\in G,\ \gamma\in\Gamma,\ 0\leqslant i\leqslant n-1,\\
\vskip 5.69046pt(g\gamma)u_{i}=(\phi^{i-n+l}\lambda)(g\gamma)u_{i},&g\in G,\ \gamma\in\Gamma,\ 0\leqslant i\leqslant n-1,\\
\vskip 5.69046ptxv_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}v_{i+1},&0\leqslant i\leqslant n-2,\\

.

Case 2
:
\mathcal{D}
is of
non-nilpotent type
.
Let
1\leqslant l\leqslant n-1
, and
\lambda\in I_{l}

2n
-dimensional vector space
P(l,\lambda)
with a
\Bbbk
-basis
\{v_{0},v_{1},\cdots,v_{n-1},u_{0},u_{1},\cdots,u_{n-1}\}

P(l,\lambda)

D(H_{\mathcal{D}})
-module with the action defined by

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 5.69046pt(g\gamma)v_{i}=(\phi^{i-n+l}\lambda)(g\gamma)v_{i},&g\in G,\ \gamma\in\Gamma,\ 0\leqslant i\leqslant n-1,\\
(g\gamma)u_{i}=(\phi^{i}\lambda)(g\gamma)u_{i},&g\in G,\ \gamma\in\Gamma,\ 0\leqslant i\leqslant n-1,\\

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 5.69046ptxv_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\alpha_{i+1}(n-l,\sigma^{-1}(\lambda))v_{i+1},&0\leqslant i\leqslant n-l-2,\\
u_{0},&i=n-l-1,\\

y_{l,\lambda}v_{0}+u_{l},&i=n-1,\\
\end{array}\right.&\\

0,&i=l-1,\\

\begin{array}[]{ll}\xi v_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&i=0,\\

of

P(l,\lambda)^{x}=\Bbbk u_{l-1}+\Bbbk(u_{n-1}-z_{l,\lambda}v_{n-l-1})

.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
1\leqslant l\leqslant n-1
and
\lambda\in I_{l}
. Then
P(l,\lambda)\cong P(\lambda)
.

Proof.

Let
\{v_{0},v_{1},\cdots,v_{n-1},u_{0},u_{1},\cdots,u_{n-1}\}
be the standard basis of
P(l,\lambda)
as stated above.

Case 1
:
\mathcal{D}
is of nilpotent type. Let
M:=\langle u_{n-1}\rangle
.
Then it is easy to verify that
M={\rm span}\{u_{n-l},u_{n-l+1},\cdots,u_{n-1}\}
. Using the natural basis of
V(l,\lambda)
, one sees that
M\cong V(l,\lambda)
.
Let
N
be a simple submodule of
P(l,\lambda)
. By Corollary
3.7
, we have
{\rm dim}(N^{x})=1
. Choose
0\neq z\in N^{x}
. Since
N^{x}\subseteq P(l,\lambda)^{x}
, we can write

z=\beta_{1}v_{n-1}+\beta_{2}u_{n-1},\ \mathrm{for\,some}\ \beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\Bbbk.

If
\beta_{1}\neq 0
, then

az-\rho^{1-l}\lambda(a)z=(\rho-\rho^{1-l})\lambda(a)\beta_{1}v_{n-1}.

Hence
v_{n-1}\in N
, and therefore
\xi^{n-l}v_{n-1}=\beta(n-l,\sigma(\lambda))u_{n-1}\in N
. Thus,
v_{n-1},u_{n-1}\in N
, implying
{\rm dim}(N^{x})=2
, a contradiction. Therefore,
\beta_{1}=0
and
\beta_{2}\neq 0
, so
u_{n-1}=\beta_{2}^{-1}z\in N
. This shows
M=\langle u_{n-1}\rangle\subseteq N
, and since
M
and
N
are both simple, we have
N=M
.
Thus,
{\rm soc}P(l,\lambda)=M\cong V(l,\lambda)
. Since
D(H_{\mathcal{D}})
is a symmetric algebra, it follows that
P(l,\lambda)
is isomorphic to a submodule of
P(\lambda)
.

Furthermore,
{\rm dim}((P(l,\lambda)/{\rm soc}P(l,\lambda))^{x})=2
, and thus, by Corollary
3.7
,

l({\rm soc}(P(l,\lambda)/{\rm soc}P(l,\lambda)))\leqslant 2
.
Let
\overline{v}
denote the image of
v\in P(l,\lambda)
under the canonical epimorphism
P(l,\lambda)\rightarrow P(l,\lambda)/{\rm soc}P(l,\lambda)
. Then one can check that

M_{1}:={\rm span}\{\overline{v_{l}},\overline{v_{l+1}},\cdots,\overline{v_{n-1}}\},\ M_{2}:={\rm span}\{\overline{u_{0}},\overline{u_{1}},\cdots,\overline{u_{n-l-1}}\}

are submodules of

3.5
, we have

M_{1}\cong V(n-l,\sigma(\lambda)),\mathrm{and}\ M_{2}\cong V(n-l,\sigma^{-1}(\lambda)).

It follows that
{\rm soc}(P(l,\lambda)/\mathrm{soc}P(l,\lambda))=M_{1}\oplus M_{2}
, and consequently,

{\rm soc}^{2}P(l,\lambda)={\rm span}\{v_{l},\cdots,v_{n-1},u_{0},\cdots,u_{n-1}\}.

Now it is straightforward to see that

P(l,\lambda)/{\rm soc}^{2}P(l,\lambda)\cong V(l,\lambda).

Therefore, the length of
P(l,\lambda)
is
l(P(l,\lambda))=4
, and by Corollary
3.9
(2),
P(l,\lambda)\cong P(\lambda)
.

Case 2
:
\mathcal{D}
is of non-nilpotent type. Let

M:=\langle u_{0}\rangle
. It is straightforward to verify that
M={\rm span}\{u_{0},u_{1},\cdots,u_{l-1}\}

3.7

az-\lambda(a)z=(\rho^{n-l}-1)\lambda(a)\beta_{1}v_{0}.

Hence

\beta_{1}=0
and

Hence,
{\rm soc}P(l,\lambda)=M\cong V(l,\lambda)

is isomorphic to a submodule of

Clearly,

{\rm dim}((P(l,\lambda)/{\rm soc}P(l,\lambda))^{\xi})=2.

Therefore, by Corollary
3.7

l({\rm soc}(P(l,\lambda)/{\rm soc}P(l,\lambda)))\leqslant 2.

Let
\overline{v}
denote the image of

Indecomposable modules with Loewy length two

-modules. By Lemma

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
M
be an indecomposable
D(H_{\mathcal{D}})
-module with
{\rm rl}(M)=2
. Then there exist a
1\leqslant l\leqslant n-1
and a
\lambda\in I_{l}
such that
I(M)\cong\oplus_{k=0}^{m-1}s_{k}P(l,\tau^{k}(\lambda))
for some
s_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
,
0\leqslant k\leqslant m-1
.

Proof.

Clearly,
M
is an indecomposable module over a non-simple block of
D(H_{\mathcal{D}})
, and its injective hull
I(M)
is projective. By Proposition
3.15
, we can write
I(M)=P_{1}\oplus P_{2}
, where

P_{1}\cong\oplus_{k=0}^{m-1}s_{k}P(l,\tau^{k}(\lambda))\ \mathrm{and}\ P_{2}\cong\oplus_{k=0}^{m-1}t_{k}P(n-l,\tau^{k}(\sigma(\lambda)))

for some character
\lambda\in I_{l}
,
1\leqslant l\leqslant n-1
and integers
s_{k},t_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
,
0\leqslant k\leqslant m-1
.
We may view
M
as a submodule of
I(M)
and set
M_{1}=M\cap P_{1}
and
M_{2}=M\cap P_{2}
. Then
{\rm soc}M={\rm soc}P_{1}\oplus{\rm soc}P_{2}
and so
{\rm soc}M\subset M_{1}\oplus M_{2}
. Since rl
(M)=2
, the quotient
M/{\rm soc}M
is semisimple. Hence

M/{\rm soc}M\subseteq{\rm soc}^{2}I(M)/{\rm soc}I(M),

and consequently
M\subseteq{\rm rad}I(M)={\rm soc}^{2}I(M)
by Corollary
3.13
.

Let
\pi:I(M)\rightarrow I(M)/{\rm soc}I(M)
be the canonical epimorphism. Then

M/{\rm soc}M\subseteq{\rm rad}I(M)/{\rm rad}^{2}I(M)=\pi({\rm rad}P_{1})\oplus\pi({\rm rad}P_{2}).

By Lemma
3.11
, we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\pi({\rm rad}P_{1})&\cong&{\rm rad}P_{1}/{\rm rad}^{2}P_{1}\cong\oplus_{k=0}^{m-1}(s_{k}+s_{k+1})V(n-l,\tau^{k}(\sigma(\lambda))),\\
\pi({\rm rad}P_{2})&\cong&{\rm rad}P_{2}/{\rm rad}^{2}P_{2}\cong\oplus_{k=0}^{m-1}(t_{k-1}+t_{k})V(l,\tau^{k}(\lambda)),\\
\end{array}

where
s_{m}=s_{0}
and
t_{-1}=t_{m-1}
. By Lemma
3.3
(4), if
V
is a simple submodule of
M/{\rm soc}M
, then either
V\subseteq\pi({\rm rad}P_{1})
or
V\subseteq\pi({\rm rad}P_{2})
. In the former case,

\pi^{-1}(V)\subseteq M\cap({\rm rad}P_{1}+{\rm rad}^{2}I(M))=M\cap({\rm rad}P_{1}\oplus{\rm rad}^{2}P_{2})\subseteq M_{1}\oplus M_{2}.

Similarly, if
V\subseteq\pi({\rm rad}P_{2})
, then
\pi^{-1}(V)\subseteq M_{1}\oplus M_{2}
.
Therefore,
M\subseteq M_{1}\oplus M_{2}
, and hence
M=M_{1}\oplus M_{2}
.

Since
M
is indecomposable, it follows that
M_{1}=0
or
M_{2}=0
, and consequently
P_{1}=0
or
P_{2}=0
. Thus, we conclude either

I(M)\cong\oplus_{k=0}^{m-1}s_{k}P(l,\tau^{k}(\lambda)),\ \mathrm{or}\ I(M)\cong\oplus_{k=0}^{m-1}t_{k}P(n-l,\tau^{k}(\sigma(\lambda))).

∎

Let
M
be an indecomposable
D(H_{\mathcal{D}})
-module with
{\rm rl}(M)=2

{\rm soc}M={\rm rad}M

-type such that

(1)

(1) follows from the proof of Proposition
4.1

Let
M
be an
D(H_{\mathcal{D}})
-module. Fix a projective cover

and define the syzygy
\Omega M
of

Proposition 4.17

4.13

3.2
(1) or Proposition

4.15

\mathcal{D}

.

4.6
(5),

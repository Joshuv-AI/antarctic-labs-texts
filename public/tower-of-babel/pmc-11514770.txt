Comparative genomic analysis of two putative novel Idiomarina species from hypersaline miocene deposits
Authors: Soňa Brestovičová, Jana Kisková, Lea Nosáľová, Mária Piknová, Mariana Kolesárová, Peter Pristaš
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHypersaline habitats, as extreme environments, are a great source of well-adapted organisms with unique properties as they have evolved various strategies to cope with these extreme conditions. Bioinformatics and genomic mining may shed light on evolutionary relationships among them. Therefore, the aim of this study was to assess the biodiversity and especially the strategies evolved within the Idiomarina genus, with the primary focus on the taxonomy and genomic adaptations of two novel strains affiliated with Idiomarina genus isolated from unique environment – brines of two Early Miocene salt deposits.ResultsBoth analyzed species belonged to the Idiomarina loihiensis cluster with similarity levels of 16S rRNA gene sequences as high as 99.5% and showed a significant genome size reduction, known characteristic of Idiomarina genomes, though within the genome of Sol25 strain the lowest extent of the carbohydrate utilization genes reduction was observed t among the Idiomarina species. Moreover, the comparative genome analyses indicated that despite both strains being isolated from geographically and geologically similar environments (brines from at least 12 Ma), the species showed higher relatedness to other Idiomarina species than to each other.ConclusionThe present findings highlighted the importance of genomic data in resolving taxonomic uncertainties and understanding of adaptation strategies of extremophiles. Geographic isolation likely contributed to population divergence of the Idiomarina genus, and the recent study offered insights into biogeographic patterns and allopatric speciation of this bacterial group.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12864-024-10900-3.Keywords: Idiomarina spp., Genome, Hypersaline environment, Geographic isolation

Background

The Idiomarina genus includes Gram-negative, moderately halophilic bacteria belonging to the Idiomarinaceae family, within the Alteromonadales order and the Pseudomonadota phylum. Currently, the Idiomarina genus comprises 31 validly described species [1]. Representatives of this genus are adapted especially to marine environments, but recent studies [2, 3] confirmed their adaptation to various saline habitats from coastal waters and brines to soils, demonstrating their euryhaline nature [4]. Despite their extraordinary adaptability, members of the Idiomarina genus are characterized by the smallest genomes (below 3 Mbp) among the Alteromonadales order, as a result of molecular adaptation to life under oligotrophic conditions [5]. In addition, Idiomarina spp. produce a huge number of enzymes as another specialization to live in such harsh conditions. These enzymes; especially proteases, are mostly alkaline, thermostable, and salt-tolerant which makes them very valuable for biotechnological and industrial applications [6, 7]. Generally, it is important to highlight that in industrial applications the use of enzymes produced by halophiles is not limited to their stability at high salt concentrations, but additionally, these extremozymes are stable at high temperatures and presence of organic solvents [6, 8].

For decades, the 16S rRNA gene has been a fundamental tool in classifying of Bacteria and Archaea [9] and has been also crucial in delineating the Idiomarinaceae family, particularly through signature nucleotides within the gene [10, 11]. However, the inconsistency in phylogenetic analyses and taxonomic classification has arisen, as more species have been identified, indicating the need for more reliable methods for a robust classification of Idiomarinaceae family representatives [12]. In the post-genomic era, it’s highly recommended to incorporate genomic data into microbial systematics alongside the traditional physiological and chemotaxonomic methods [13]. Several genome-oriented techniques such as pangenome analysis [14, 15], average nucleotide identity (ANI) analysis [16, 17], and digital DNA–DNA hybridization (dDDH) analysis [18] have been developed for species classification. The rapid progress in high-throughput sequencing technologies has made several genomes of the Idiomarinaceae family available in public databases, providing an excellent opportunity to study their enzymes, as well as evolutionary and taxonomic relationships.

In present study, a comprehensive genomic analysis of two Idiomarina-related strains, obtained from historical salt mines in Solotvino Salt Lakes (Carpathian Ukraine) and Solivar near the city Prešov (East Slovakia) was performed. The studied sampling sites are unique compared to previously known habitats, especially for their terrestrial character. These areas are geographically isolated and according to water bodies (Parathetys) they have lost communication with other sea bodies at least 12 Ma [19, 20]. Geographical and spatial isolation is considered the primary explanation of the distinctiveness of populations with many similar characteristics and common ancestry, but with different geographical distributions [21]. Extreme environments, mainly due to their isolated nature arising from their diffusion between different geographical unconnected points, are excellent systems for evaluating biogeographical patterns and allopatric speciation [22, 23]. Therefore, the current study may reveal the adaptive strategies and evolutionary mechanisms allowing these microorganisms to survive in extreme conditions, and on the potential effect of geographic separation on speciation.

Materials and methods

Characteristics of the sampling sites

The Solotvino Salt Lakes (47°57’35.2"N 23°51’25.6” E) located in the Tiachev district of the Carpathians in Ukraine has a long history in salt production, dating back to the Roman Empire era (Fig. 1). In 1908, a flood led to the creation of lakes in this region, with subthermal waters rich in bromides, cesium, and rubidium fluoride [24, 25]. These lakes are known for their high salinity levels, fluctuating with depth and season [26]. Solivar near the city Prešov (48°59′0.706″ N, 21°16′59.124″ E), located in the eastern part of Slovakia, represents a historically significant site for salt mining, dating back to the period of 1570–1571 (Fig. 1). Salt deposits at both sites date back to the Tertiary period [20].

The location of sampling sites Solivar near the city Prešov (Slovakia) and Solotvino Salt Lakes (Ukraine)

Primary isolation and cultivation analyses

The Sol25 strain was obtained from the water sample taken approximately 10 cm below the surface of Kunigunda Lake (Solotvino Salt Lakes, Ukraine) bottle in November 2014, and the HP20-50 strain was obtained from the water sample of brine emergence from the borehole to the surface in Solivar (Prešov) in September 2020. Both water samples were taken into 500 ml sterile, and transported on ice to the laboratory for further analyses.

The water temperature, conductivity and pH were measured in situ by an automatic probe (Combo HI 98219, Hanna, MI, USA) as previously described by Nosalova et al. [27]. Total dissolved solids (TDS) were determined by the gravimetric method as an average value of several measurements. One hundred microliters of water sample were inoculated onto TSA medium (Merck KGaA, Darmstadt, Germany) prepared using water taken from the sampling site (in case of Solotvion Salt Lakes samples) or R2A medium (Sigma-Aldrich, Burlington, USA) supplemented with 5% of NaCl (w/v) (Merck KGaA, Darmstadt, Germany) in case of brine sample from Solivar, (and cultivated at laboratory temperature (~ 23 °C) for 24–48 h until the colonies were observed.

After incubation, all isolates obtained from both sampling sites were then re-streaked on the same medium as was used for their primary isolation and were subjected for further analysis. Based on the to the 16S rRNA gene sequence results, two isolated. One isolate identified as Idiomarina spp. was selected from each sampling site (Sol25 and HP20-50) and subjected to whole genome sequence analysis.

DNA extraction and 16S rRNA gene amplification

Genomic DNA from both strains was isolated from overnight cultures cultivated in TSB medium (Merck KGaA, Darmstadt, Germany) supplemented with 5% of NaCl (w/v) (Merck KGaA, Darmstadt, Germany) using the GenElute Bacterial Genomic DNA Kit (Sigma-Aldrich, Burlington, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The quality of obtained DNA was verified using electrophoresis in 1% agarose gel stained with ethidium bromide (0.5 µg/L) and examined using the ChemiDocTM XRS + System (BIO-RAD, Hercules, California USA). The DNA concentration and purity were assessed spectrophotometrically using the NanoDrop 2000c Spectrophotometer (Thermo Scientific, Waltham, Massachusetts, USA). The obtained DNA was used as a template for the PCR amplification of the 16S rRNA gene using Taq Core Kit (Jena Bioscience, Jena, Germany) and Mastercycler® Pro S (Eppendorf, Hamburg-Nord Germany). Fifty microliters of PCR mixture contained 1× Crystal Buffer, 200 µM of dNTP, 1 µM of each primer (fD1 5’-AGAGTTTGATCCTGGCTCAG-3’, rP2 5’-ACGGCTACCTTGTTACGACTT-3’) [28], 1.25 U of Taq polymerase, and 10 ng of DNA template. The initial denaturation at 95 °C for 5 min was followed by 35 cycles of denaturation at 95 °C for 1 min, primer annealing at 56 °C for 1 min, and extension at 72 °C for 1.5 min, followed by the final extension step at 72 °C for 10 min. Obtained PCR amplicons were sequenced in both directions using the Sanger sequencing method by Eurofins Genomics Sequencing GmbH (Köln, Germany). The obtained 16S rRNA gene sequences were processed using the MEGA v11.0.13 software [29] and deposited in the GenBank database under accession numbers MG272393 (Idiomarina sp. Sol25) and PP717852.1 (Idiomarina sp. HP20-50).

The 16S rRNA gene sequence analysis

The obtained 16S rRNA gene sequences were analyzed using the BLASTN search tool against the 16S rRNA gene sequence GenBank database of NCBI [30]. A phylogenetic analysis was performed using tools implemented in the MEGA v11.0.13 software [29]. The sequences were aligned with closely related 16S rRNA gene sequences using the ClustalW tool with the default parameters. A phylogenetic tree was constructed by the MEGA v11.0.13 software using the Maximum Likelihood method [29] with 1000 bootstrap replications and evolutionary distances were calculated using the Kimura 2-parameter model [31].

The whole genome sequence analysis

The whole genome sequencing was performed using the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform, employing paired-end approach (2 × 150 bp) with the S4 PE150 XP kit (Eurofins Genomics Europe Sequencing GmbH, Konstanz, Germany). The obtained sequences were processed using a set of tools implemented in the Unipro UGENE v35.0 software [32, 33]. The sequence quality was assessed using the FastQC v0.11.9 tool, and Trimmomatic v0.39 was used for trimming sequences with the minimum average quality score set at 20 and default settings. The SPAdes v3.12.0 tool [34] was used for de novo assembly of the high-quality paired-end reads with default settings. Finally, the contigs shorter than 200 bp were excluded from obtained draft genomes.

The obtained genome sequences were deposited to the GenBank database under accession numbers JAUPEW000000000 (Idiomarina sp. Sol25) and JAVFHZ000000000 (Idiomarina sp. HP20-50).

Subsequently, all twelve reference genomes among the Idiomarina genus available in the GenBank database were selected for phylogenetic and comparative genomic analyses. The genomic relatedness of the HP20-50 and Sol25 strains with selected Idiomarina spp. was examined using the digital DNA: DNA hybridization (dDDH) analysis performed by the Type (Strain) Genome Server (TYGS) [35] with the species threshold value of 70% [36]. The phylogenetic position of the respective strains was visualized using the Genome BLAST Distance Phylogeny (GBDP) tree constructed using the TYGS. After that, the genome annotation and comparative genome analyses were performed by the Rapid Annotation using Subsystem Technology (RAST) [37–39], with the verification of sequences via the BLASTN search tool. The EDGAR 3.0 platform [40] was used to perform the comparative genome analysis of the core genome and pangenomes within selected Idiomarina spp., and to determine the Average Nucleotide Identity (ANI) (threshold value of 95%) and Average Amino Acid Identity (AAI) (threshold value of 95–96%) [16, 17]. Thereafter, the OrthoVenn3 software was used to perform the comparative genome analysis and prediction of orthologous gene clusters among HP20-50 and Sol25 strains and their closely related species determined by the phylogenetic analysis using the TYGS [41]. The Function Based Comparison tool provided by the RAST server was used to assess similarities/differences in the presence of functional roles between the HP20-50 and Sol25 genomes and their closely related species, determined by the phylogenetic analysis using the TYGS. Correlation between unique functioning parts and Idiomarina genomes was visualized by heatmaps generated using the ClustVis tool [42]. All mentioned web tools were used with default settings. The data on the estimated divergence times between the Idiomarina genus and the closest related genera within the Idiomarinaceae family were taken from the TimeTree server v5 [43].

Results

Physicochemical parameters of water

During the sampling time, the Solotvino Salt Lakes water temperature was 2.4 °C, with pH value of 7.64, TDS was found to be 302.4 g/L and conductivity exceeded 3999 µS/cm. During the sampling at the Solivar, the temperature of the brine was 12.3 °C, with a conductance exceeding 3999 µS/cm and pH of 6.5. The gravimetric analysis determined that the water fell within a hypersaline category, containing an average of 311 g/L of total dissolved solids [44].

The phylogenetic placement of the studied strains based on the 16S rRNA gene sequence analysis

The 16S rRNA gene sequences of HP20-50 and Sol25 strains showed the highest sequence similarity (99.67% and 99.53%) with Idiomarina loihiensis L2TR (NR_074933.1) when compared against the 16S rRNA gene sequence GenBank database of NCBI. According to the multiple sequence alignment of validly described Idiomarina species, the HP20-50 strain was clustered with I. ramblicola R22 (NR_025806.1) as its closest relative. However, this strain could not be reliably assigned to the same species based on the low bootstrap value, observed in the maximum-likelihood phylogenetic tree (Additional file 1). The Sol25 strain formed an isolated branch separated from the I. loihiensis group. These findings indicated that both strains may represent two novel species of the Idiomarina genus.

Genome features and phylogenetic placement of the Sol25 and HP20-50 strains

A total of 10,150,900 raw reads were obtained by sequencing the Sol25 genome and 6,595,060 raw sequences by sequencing the HP20-50 genome, giving 1104x coverage of Sol25 genome and 660x coverage of HP20-50 genome respectively. After assembling, the obtained draft genome size was of 2.86 Mbp of the Sol25 strain and 2.80 Mbp of the HP20-50 strain. The Sol25 strain genome showed the highest number of coding sequences (2869) compared to other Idiomarina reference genomes (the average number is lower than 2600) (Table 1).

The comparative genomics

A high number of genes that are related to amino acid metabolism was observed, indicating that proteins and amino acids are the primary nutritional sources of analyzed strains. Among all the studied strains, the Idiomarina sp. Sol25 strain showed the highest number of carbohydrate utilization genes engaged in phosphorus and potassium metabolism and respiration (subsystems in RAST annotation) (Additional file 2).

Surprisingly, although the genus Idiomarina is considered a halophilic bacterial group, genes for choline and betaine uptake and betaine biosynthesis are absent in most Idiomarina spp [47]. However, the respective genes were identified in the Idiomarina sp. HP20-50 and Idiomarina sp. Sol25 genomes. The synteny analysis, using Idiomarina loihiensis L2RT as the reference genome, revealed a limited number of syntenic blocks (Fig. 3). These blocks suggested that despite genomic changes compared to the last common ancestor, some regions have remained conserved. The phylogenetic tree of the Idiomarinaceae family, with a focus on the chronological branching of species using the TimeTree server, has revealed the species delineation associated with significant geological and climatic changes, with the branching occurring from the end of the Permian period to the Neogene (Additional file 3).

The HP20-50 isolate is characterized by unique genes associated with the siderophore synthesis and transport (such as siderophore biosynthesis L-2,4-diaminobutyrate decarboxylase, siderophore biosynthesis protein, monooxygenase, siderophore synthetase component, ligase and TonB-dependent siderophore receptor). The HP20-50 strain also showed the presence of unique genes responsible for copper (copper resistance protein CopCDG, copper resistance protein B) and mercury (mercuric transport protein, MerT) resistance, allowing the survival in environments contaminated with heavy metals. In addition, unique genes associated with biofilm formation (curli production assembly/transport components CsgEFG, minor curlin subunit CsgB) and oxidative stress (paraquat-inducible proteins A, B) were identified in the HP20-50 genome.

Generally, the Sol25 strain showed the highest number of unique genes associated with various metabolic processes, such as choline metabolism and protection against oxidative stress (sulfatase modifying factor 1 precursor/C-alpha-formyglycine- generating enzyme 1, choline transporter BetT, short form, betaine aldehyde dehydrogenase, choline dehydrogenase, choline-sulfatase, organic hydroperoxide resistance protein). Additionally, gene encoding ornithine decarboxylase was identified in the Sol25 genome, which is important for cell proliferation, differentiation, and ion homeostasis.

The core genome and pangenome structure

The analysis of 14 Idiomarina genomes (performed by the EDGAR 3.0 platform) allowed to determine the structure of core genome and pangenome within the Idiomarina genus. The core genome, reconstructed using Idiomarina loihiensis L2TR as the reference genome, consisted of 1538 CDS (coding sequences), demonstrating remarkable conservation across the analyzed genomes (Additional file 6). The core genome includes genes related to osmolarity, stress response, metabolism of sugars and amino acids, antibiotic resistance, and virulence factors. The core development plot indicated a stabilization trend in the core genome (a conserved set of essential genes, crucial for the basic functions and survival), described by the Eq. 773.510 * exp(-x / 3.940) + 1530.618 (Fig. 6A). The analysis of the pangenome revealed 6164 CDS, following Heap’s law (2306.373 * x^0.369) (Fig. 6B). The analysis of singleton development revealed a decreasing trend in the number of singleton genes, following the decay function of 670.690 * exp(-x / 2.530) + 157.779 (Fig. 6C).

Discussion

The Idiomarina genus, inhabiting diverse saline environments, showed remarkable adaptability across various ecosystems, from coastal waters to subsea thermal vents [4]. This wide distribution points towards a significant ecological role and high evolutionary capacity of this genus. Extreme environments, often isolated and dispersed across different geographical locations without direct connections, serve as an ideal system for studying biogeographical patterns and allopatric speciation. These environments provide a unique opportunity for studying how geographic isolation influences microbial diversity and speciation [21, 22]. The study areas (spatially and temporally isolated salt deposits in Central Europe) also showed these unique characteristics, not only they can be considered extreme environments with salinity levels higher than those typically found in oceans (exceeding the concentration of salt in sea water (35 g/L) almost ten times), but they were also geographically isolated from the previously studied habitats and localities of the Idiomarina species involved in this study (Table 1). The geographical isolation and distinct characteristics of the environment had allowed the investigation of specific adaptive strategies, potentially leading to the discovery of novel species or distinct evolutionary pathways within the genus.

The analysis based on the 16S rRNA gene sequence showed the highest sequence similarity of the HP20-50 and Sol25 strains with Idiomarina loihiensis L2TR (NR_074933.1) when using the 16S rRNA gene sequence GenBank database. Observed similarity values (99.67% and 99.53%) are well above 98.65% limit proposed for species delineation [45]. In recent years, the increasing identification of new species has highlighted discrepancies in phylogenetic analyses and taxonomic classifications, underscoring the necessity of more suitable methods to achieve a reliable classification of the Idiomarinaceae family members [12]. In current microbial systematics, the incorporation of genomic data with traditional physiological and chemotaxonomic methods is highly recommended [13]. This approach is particularly relevant when exploring unique and isolated environments. Assuming that, the phylogenomic analyses provided convincing evidence for the delineation of the HP20-50 and Sol25 strains within the Idiomarina genus. Notably, both strains formed distinct, well-supported branches in the GBDP tree. This phylogenetic divergence is also confirmed by the dDDH values significantly below the 70% threshold and the ANI and AAI values below 95%, which are commonly used for species delineation in prokaryotes [16]. Although the AAI values between the Sol25 strain and its closely related species (I. loihiensis and I. rhizosphaerae) were slightly above the 96% threshold (96.47% and 96.55%, respectively), other phylogenetic analyses as well as comparative genomic analyses indicated a significant genetic divergence of the Sol25 strain from these species.

Adaptive genomic characteristics observed in Idiomarina species emphasize the importance of genomic data in understanding microbial systematics. Remarkable reduction in the genome size (up to 3 Mbp) is one of the adaptive strategies shared by all species of the Idiomarina genus. The average genome size is therefore significantly smaller compared to other genera belonging to the Alteromonadales order (4–7.6 Mbp) [46]. The genome reduction is attributed to the specialization to use mainly protein sources over carbohydrates [46]. This trend in genome reduction has also been observed in two studied isolates, with genome sizes of 2.86 Mbp (Sol25) and 2.79 Mbp (HP20-50), confirming the consistency of this adaptive strategy within Idiomarina species. Identification of genes which protein products are associated with amino acids and protein metabolism in the examined genomes suggests successful nutritional specialization and evolutionary adaptation to an oligotrophic saline environment. In addition, more genes involved in the adaptive mechanisms responding to increased salt concentration and the presence of heavy metals were detected. Surprisingly, the highest number of genes associated with carbohydrate utilization was observed in the Idiomarina sp. Sol25 genome (percentage increase of 27% compared to the average value observed in the Idiomarina genus), indicating that the loss of carbohydrate utilization abilities had been probably a sequential process. The genes for betaine and choline uptake and synthesis were observed in genomes of both studied strains. These compounds are very efficient osmolytes found in a wide range of bacterial species, where they are accumulated as a response to osmotic stress, which is consistent with the lifestyle of these microorganisms in a hypersaline environment [47–49]. These genetic traits were also observed in other Idiomarina genomes available in the GenBank database, isolated from sea water or saline soils (e.g., I. ramblicola R22 or I. seosinensis CL-SP19) but were absent in species found in another hypersaline environments (e.g., I. aquatica SN-14, I. baltica OS145, I. fontislapidosi CECT5859, I. loihiensis L2TR, I. piscisalsi TS4-2, I. rhizosphaerae M1R2S28, I. tyrosinivorans CC-PW-9, I. xiamenensis 10-D-4, I. zobellii KMM 231). KefA protein is another important part of salt adaptation. It is a small-conductance mechanosensitive channel that plays a critical role in the regulation of turgor pressure in bacteria. Bacterial species with missing genes for betaine and choline uptake and synthesis probably rely on the KefA protein activity, to cope with elevated salt concentrations. The presence of the kefA gene was also confirmed in Idiomarina sp. Sol25 and Idiomarina sp. HP20-50 genomes using EDGAR 3.2 platform (Additional file 6), as well as in all Idiomarina isolates examined by Kaur and Kaur [50]. According to OrthoVenn3 analysis, 1953 genes out of 2801 orthologs belong to the core genome and represent a common core set of genes within HP20-50, Sol25 and their close relatives, indicating high genomic conservation. Similarly, 1940 clusters were identified as single-copy clusters, reflecting the high level of genome conservation among analyzed Idiomarina genomes. This analysis revealed the highest proportion of unique orthologous genes in the Sol25 genome.

The results on the comparative genomic analysis also demonstrated a significant increase in the pangenome size of the Idiomarina genus, indicating a wide range of genetic diversity driven by the gaining of new genes. The pangenome is identified as open, meaning a high degree of genetic variability, that facilitates adaptability to various environmental conditions. Figure 6A graphically represents the dynamic nature of the pangenome and illustrates acquisition and diversification of new genes that define its open-ended character. The analysis delineates a clear distinction between a stable core genome and an expandable pangenome, reflecting the genomic plasticity within Idiomarina spp. Horizontal gene transfer probably did not play a significant role in genome plasticity as no plasmids or residual plasmid-like sequences, were detected in genomes of studied strains and limited number of phage-related sequences were detected in the genomes (presence of glutaredoxin genes in the Idiomarina sp. Sol25 and Idiomarina sp. HP20-50 genome identified using the EDGAR 3.2 platform (Additional file 6)). In addition, no CRISPR-related sequences were detected in the Idiomarina sp. Sol25 and Idiomarina sp. HP20-50 genomes.

Function based comparison revealed several genes responsible for the iron acquisition from the environment in the Idiomarina sp. HP20-50 genome, which are crucial for bacterial survival under conditions of low iron availability. These findings suggest that the Idiomarina sp. HP20-50 isolate may have unique interactions with their environment or hosts, making it a potentially novel model for exploring symbiotic relationships between bacteria and microorganisms, such as algae [51]. The presence of other unique genes indicates the ability of the Idiomarina sp. HP20-50 isolate to survive under various harsh conditions, including the presence of heavy metals and oxidative stress. These characteristics indicate its potential for use in bioremediation or other biotechnologies.

Function based comparison discovered that Idiomarina sp. Sol25 isolate is characterized by unique genes associated with the metabolism of choline and its derivatives, which are completely absent in its closely related species I. loihiensis and I. rhizosphaerae. In addition, it is characterized by the presence of the gene encoding choline sulfatase (EC 3.1.6.6), which is also absent in the HP20-50 strain, I. abyssalis and I. ramblicola. Idiomarina sp. Sol25 is the only isolate among analyzed Idiomarina strains, which contains the gene encoding ornithine decarboxylase (EC 4.1.1.17). Ornithine decarboxylase is a PLP-dependent enzyme which catalyzes the conversion of ornithine to putrescine at the beginning of the polyamine pathway [52]. This is probably one of the adaptations that improves osmotic stress responses, stabilize important biomolecules and adapt to the harsh conditions of a high-salt environment.

Comparative genome analyses demonstrated that despite relatively high 16S rRNA gene sequence similarity observed, there are genomic differences in the Idiomarina genus, supporting the theory that different Idiomarina species have undergone distinct evolutionary traits. The Idiomarina loihiensis group includes species with very similar 16S rRNA gene sequences but they significantly differ at the genome level (data obtained in this study). The Idiomarina genus diverged about 230 Ma and the I. loihiensis clade diverged 85 Ma (Additional file 3) demonstrating the divergence of 16S rRNA gene sequences. Delineation of the I. liohiensis clade was mapped back to approximately 12 Ma, in the same time period when salt deposits were formed in Central Europe. The lack of significant similarity between the Idiomarina sp. Sol25 and Idiomarina sp. HP20-50 strains suggests that the speciation of these bacterial species probably occurred before the formation of salt deposits.

The 16S rRNA gene, widely used as a reliable marker in microbial taxonomy, shows significant evolutionary constraints due to an extremely slow rate of development. To demonstrate, the 16S rRNA genes in Escherichia coli and Salmonella typhimurium required up to 50 million years to gain only 1% sequence variation [53]. This implies that bacterial strains can share identical or nearly identical 16S rRNA gene sequences, but exhibit a high variability at the genome level [5, 54]. Microorganisms can evolve very quickly at the genomic level due to their large population, short generation time and frequent gene transfer between species. Rapid speciation in bacterial ecosystems has been observed even under laboratory conditions [55]. Biogeographical studies based on the analysis of the 16S rRNA gene can be imprecise and therefore it is necessary to use analyses at the genome level when investigating bacterial diversity and evolution.

Conclusion

In summary, the present analysis of the bacterial strains obtained from saline environments of Solotvino Salt Lakes (Ukraine) and a Solivar (near the city Prešov, Slovakia) has led to the identification of two potentially new Idiomarina species, highlighting the remarkable diversity and adaptability of Idiomarina species to extreme hypersaline conditions. These results may elucidate the understanding how isolated and extreme environments serve as natural laboratories for studying microbial evolution and speciation. Genomic analyses, including genome annotation, phylogenetic comparisons, core genome and pangenome analyses have provided valuable insights into the genome composition and evolutionary dynamics of the Idiomarina genus. These findings emphasize the stability of the core genome across the Idiomarina genus and the extensive variability of the pangenome, reflecting a balance between essential functions and adaptive genetic diversity. However, further analyses are necessary to provide a valid description of the HP20-50 and Sol25 strains as new species of the Idiomarina genus.

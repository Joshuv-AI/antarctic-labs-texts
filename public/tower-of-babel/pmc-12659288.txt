Carbuncle management: skin-sparing saucerization followed by delayed primary closure is a new treatment modality: a case series
Authors: Nur Syafawani, Mohamad Ismail Ali
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT

Introduction

Carbuncles are deep-seated infections of the subcutaneous tissue, typically caused by Staphylococcus aureus, and are characterized by interconnected abscesses beneath the skin. These infections often present with multiple pustular openings, significant surrounding inflammation, and systemic symptoms such as fever and malaise [1]. The standard treatment approach involves aggressive surgical debridement through saucerization, which entails complete excision of the necrotic core and surrounding cellulitis, followed by wound packing and healing by secondary intention [3]. While effective in controlling infection, this conventional method has several limitations, including prolonged wound healing time, extended hospital stays, increased dressing requirements, and a heightened risk of antibiotic resistance due to prolonged antimicrobial use.

In response to these challenges, we introduce a skin-sparing saucerization technique followed by delayed primary closure as a novel approach to managing carbuncles. This technique aims to preserve the healthy surrounding skin while effectively removing the infected tissue. By delaying primary closure, the wound is allowed to granulate adequately before suturing, reducing the risk of secondary infection and promoting faster healing. We hypothesize that this method will lead to shortened wound healing times, reduced need for frequent dressing changes, and minimized hospital stays, and lead to an overall improvement in patient recovery and quality of life.

This case series presents ten patients conducted at Johor Hospital Malaysia who underwent the skin-sparing saucerization technique, analyzing their postoperative outcomes, wound healing duration, and potential complications. By evaluating the efficacy of this technique, we aim to propose an alternative approach that enhances carbuncle management while addressing the limitations of conventional treatment methods.

Case presentation

This case series includes ten patients who underwent skin-sparing saucerization followed by delayed primary closure for the treatment of carbuncles. The patients ranged in age from 44 to 62 years, with a nearly even distribution of male and female cases and of ethnicity—either Chinese and Malay. Several patients had underlying comorbid conditions, including diabetes mellitus, hypertension, and hypercholesterolemia, which are known to impact wound healing.

All patients presented with localized swelling, multiple pustular openings, erythema, and purulent discharge, with a 2-week history of progressive symptoms, including pain, fever, and fluctuance at the infected site. The wound sizes varied from 4 to 8 cm and were located at different anatomical regions, including the back, anterior abdomen, axilla, lower abdomen, buttock, and gluteal region, as mentioned in Table 1.

Baseline blood investigations, including complete blood count and inflammatory markers, were performed to assess systemic involvement. All patients were empirically started on intravenous cloxacillin (1 g) before surgery to control bacterial infection.

All of them underwent skin-sparing saucerization.

Patients achieved full wound healing between 2 and 5 weeks, depending on wound size and preexisting health conditions. Most cases exhibited minimal complications, with only one patient developing mild surgical site infection (SSI), which was managed successfully with oral antibiotics and extended dressing care. Another patient experienced delayed healing, requiring additional monitoring and longer dressing application.

Overall, the skin-sparing saucerization technique with delayed primary closure significantly improved wound healing outcomes. The hospital stay was notably reduced to 1–3 days, compared with conventional approaches, which often require prolonged inpatient care due to slow healing by secondary intention. Additionally, the need for extensive dressing changes was minimized, contributing to improved patient compliance and quality of life.

These findings suggest that this modified surgical approach enhances carbuncle management by promoting faster recovery, reducing infection risk, and allowing earlier return to daily activities.

Case summary

Surgical approach methods

The skin-sparing saucerization technique was performed under general anesthesia, depending on the wound size, its location, and the patient’s overall condition. The procedure began with a longitudinal elliptical incision over the carbuncle to expose the underlying pus and necrotic tissue. Care was taken to preserve as much of the healthy surrounding skin as possible, ensuring that the incision provided adequate exposure without unnecessary tissue loss, as shown from Figs. 1, 2, 3 and 4. Once the infected area was accessed, the necrotic and infected tissue was completely excised using an undermining technique. This involved carefully separating the infected tissue from the subcutaneous layers while ensuring that the wound base and lateral edges remained viable and healthy, as shown in Figs. 1–4. Unlike conventional aggressive saucerization, which often results in large open wounds requiring prolonged healing, this method allowed for maximum preservation of viable tissue while effectively removing the source of infection.

Following debridement, the wound cavity was thoroughly irrigated with normal saline to remove any residual debris. A povidone–iodine-soaked ribbon gauze was then placed inside the cavity to maintain a moist environment and provide an antimicrobial effect, reducing the risk of secondary infection while promoting granulation tissue formation.

Postoperative wound care

After surgery, patients were closely monitored for pain, signs of infection, and wound healing progress. Daily dressing changes were performed using sterile saline irrigation followed by the application of fresh povidone–iodine-soaked ribbon gauze to ensure a clean, antimicrobial environment. Patients were typically discharged within 1–3 days postsurgery, depending on their overall condition and wound size. They were given detailed instructions regarding daily wound care, dressing changes, and scheduled outpatient follow-up visits.

As the wound healing process progressed, patients were evaluated at the 1-week follow-up appointment to assess infection clearance, granulation tissue formation, and resolution of induration. The decision for delayed primary closure was made on the basis of specific criteria. The wound had to be free from infection, with no residual pus, slough, or necrotic tissue present. Additionally, the wound base and lateral edges needed to exhibit healthy granulation, indicating proper healing. Any persistent induration or inflammation had to be fully resolved before proceeding with closure.

Delayed primary closure was performed once the wound met all these criteria. The wound edges were approximated using interrupted non-absorbable sutures, ensuring that the closure was secure while minimizing tension on the tissue. The use of delayed suturing allowed for enhanced wound healing by ensuring that the infection was fully eradicated before final closure, thereby reducing the risk of recurrence or complications.

Wound healing time frame and duration

Wound healing progressed systematically following surgery. At the 1-week follow-up, wounds were examined for infection clearance and granulation formation, and dressing changes were continued to maintain cleanliness. By the second-week follow-up, wounds that had fully granulated and met the closure criteria underwent delayed primary suturing. Once sutured, wounds continued to be monitored for proper healing and suture integrity.

Complete wound healing was typically achieved between 2 to 5 weeks, depending on factors such as wound size, anatomical location, and underlying comorbidities. Larger wounds, especially in patients with conditions such as diabetes or hypertension, required longer healing durations, as shown in Fig. 5. Sutures were removed once wound closure was confirmed, usually within 4 weeks post-suturing, as shown in Figs. 6 and 7.

The analysis shows a positive correlation between wound size and healing duration, where larger wounds generally take longer to heal. However, there is some variability in healing times, likely influenced by patient comorbidities and wound location. The findings suggest that the skin-sparing saucerization technique provides consistent and predictable healing outcomes, effectively managing different wound sizes while minimizing complications

This new approach to carbuncle management demonstrated significant improvements compared with conventional saucerization techniques. The overall healing duration was shortened, hospital stays were reduced, and patients required fewer dressing changes, minimizing the burden of wound care. Additionally, the technique facilitated an earlier return to daily activities, improving patient comfort and quality of life while maintaining effective infection control.

Discussion

Traditional treatments for carbuncles, such as Incision and Drainage (I&D) and saucerization, involve complete removal of infected and necrotic tissue. Incision and drainage are performed by creating an incision with a scalpel, expressing purulent material, breaking loculations, and packing the cavity with gauze to promote further drainage [5]. Similarly, saucerization entails removing all necrotic tissue down to the healthy underlying layers, followed by regular dressing changes until the wound heals by secondary intention. While these approaches effectively eliminate infection, they often leave patients with large open wounds that take weeks to months to heal [2].

The primary advantage of these methods lies in their ability to ensure thorough removal of infected tissue and prevent the spread of infection [2]. However, they have significant drawbacks. Wounds healing by secondary intention often require prolonged care with frequent dressing changes, which increases the burden on healthcare resources [4]. Additionally, extended recovery times can delay a patient’s return to daily activities. Scarring and tissue contraction are other common issues, particularly for wounds in cosmetically sensitive areas. Prolonged antibiotic use in these cases further raises concerns about the development of drug resistance, a growing problem in clinical practice.

In this context, there is a need for alternative treatment approaches that address the limitations of conventional techniques. We implemented a skin-sparing saucerization technique followed by delayed primary closure. This method involves removing all necrotic tissue while sparing healthy skin margins and limiting the depth of excision to the superficial layer of subcutaneous fat. The wound is then left open to allow granulation, and once the wound bed is clean and granulating well, delayed primary closure is performed under local anesthesia. This technique combines the benefits of traditional saucerization with a more cosmetically favorable and resource-efficient approach.

The results observed in our case series illustrate the advantages of this technique. Our patients, who presented with carbuncles underwent skin-sparing saucerization followed by delayed primary closure. The wounds showed clean granulating tissue with no slough or residual infection within 1 week of surgery. By the second week, induration had resolved, and delayed primary closure was performed. At the 5-week follow-up, wounds had completely healed with minimal scarring, and the patients were able to return to normal activities earlier than expected.

This technique offers several key advantages. By preserving healthy skin and minimizing unnecessary excision, it accelerates wound healing and improves cosmetic outcomes. The early formation of granulation tissue reduces the need for prolonged antibiotic therapy, which mitigates the risk of resistance. Furthermore, the shorter recovery period allows patients to resume daily activities sooner, significantly improving their quality of life. Hospital stays are also reduced, and wound care is simplified, leading to lower healthcare costs.

Despite its benefits, this method has some limitations. The success of the procedure depends heavily on the surgeon’s expertise in ensuring complete removal of infected tissue while preserving healthy skin. Incomplete excision may result in residual infection or recurrence, which can compromise the outcome. Additionally, while our case series showed promising results, further studies with larger patient populations are necessary to validate the efficacy and safety of this approach.

To address these challenges, standardized training programs could be developed to teach the technique to surgeons and ensure its widespread adoption. The use of wound assessment tools can also help guide the timing of delayed primary closure and improve consistency in outcomes. Long-term, multicenter studies comparing this method to traditional approaches are essential to establish its role as a new standard of care.

Conclusion

Traditional methods for carbuncle treatment are effective. However, they have significant drawbacks, including prolonged healing times, scarring, and the risk of antibiotic resistance. The skin-sparing saucerization technique followed by delayed primary closure addresses these issues by promoting faster healing, reducing scarring, and minimizing healthcare burdens. The results from our case series demonstrate the potential of this approach to improve patient outcomes and quality of life. However, further research and broader implementation are required to fully establish its benefits and ensure its adoption as a standard practice in the management of carbuncles.

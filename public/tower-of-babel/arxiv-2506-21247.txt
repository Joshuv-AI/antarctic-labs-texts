A ∞ A_{\infty} Action of Open 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 Superstring Field Theory
Xianghang Zhang

Abstract
We formulate a string field theory for open 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 strings with an A ∞ A_{\infty} algebra structure. Starting from the BRST cohomology relative to the U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) anti-ghost zero-mode, we generalize [arXiv:1312.2948] and constructed all interacting vertices recursively and without singularity. We also show that our string field theory reproduces the correct perturbative S-matrix.



1
Introduction

String perturbation theory is a theory of on-shell amplitudes defined by integrals of conformal field theory correlators over the (super)moduli space of the worldsheet.
Its second quantization, known as string field theory is formulated via an action principle that reproduces the perturbative amplitudes.
It thus amounts to a decomposition of the moduli space according to the Feynman rule
Doubek2020
.

This geometric approach has been clarified in particular for bosonic strings.
Witten’s cubic open string field theory
Witten1986Non
is a prominent example.
For a string field
\Psi
, the action is

S_{\mathrm{bos,\,open}}[\Psi]=\frac{1}{2}\omega(\Psi,Q\Psi)+\frac{1}{3}\omega(\Psi,\Psi\ast\Psi),

(1)

where
\omega
is the symplectic bilinear form defined via the BPZ inner product,
Q
is the BRST charge, and
\ast
is the Witten star product.
It is an elegant choice that the cubic vertex in the action, after Feynman expansion, covers the entire moduli space with an arbitrary number of vertex operators insertions
Zwiebach1991
.
In his monumental work
Zwiebach1993
, Zwiebach identified the homotopy algebra structures (
A_{\infty}
and
L_{\infty}
for open and closed strings, respectively) in the bosonic string field theory action and derived the minimal area principle that determines all vertices for bosonic closed strings.
From a field-theoretic perspective, the homotopy algebra encodes the gauge invariance of the action.
In Witten’s cubic action, the algebraic structure is simply that of a graded associative algebra since the star product is associative and there is no need for higher order terms.
For a general open string field theory, we consider an
A_{\infty}
algebra, which is a graded associative algebra generalized up to homotopy.

Such a geometric picture, however, becomes significantly more subtle in the context of superstrings.
One alternative yet successful approach to superstring field theory, due to Berkovits, is an analogue of the Wess-Zumino-Witten action
BerkovitsSup1995
;
Berkovits2000
.
The action is presented in closed form and yields various analytic solutions
Ohmori2002
;
Kluson2001
;
Kluson2002
;
Lechtenfeld2002
;
Erler2013
.
However, in Berkovits’ formulation the geometric picture is largely absent.
This is partly due to the bosonization of the
\beta\gamma
superconformal ghosts, which introduces an unphysical degree of freedom, resulting in a “large” Hilbert space.
The lack of geometric understanding also complicates quantization
Berkovits2012
.
A deeper understanding of how the superstring field action encodes supermoduli space data is necessary.

In the past decade another route with a closer connection to the moduli space has been pursued.
The conventional method of computing superstring amplitudes is to first integrate over the fermionic part of the moduli space, leaving picture-changing insertions on the worldsheet (see, e.g.,
Witten2012
).
One natural modification of Witten’s cubic string field theory is to dress the star product with a picture-changing operator at the midpoint
Witten1986Int
, but this naive idea suffers from divergent contact terms at higher-point interactions
Wendt1989
.
Modifications of this idea have appeared
Lechtenfeld1988
;
Preitschopf1990
;
Arefeva1990
;
Berkovits2009
;
Kroyter2011
, but all require changing the kinetic term, yielding complicated propagators.
However, with the help of
A_{\infty}
relations, a consistent way of smearing and assigning picture-changing opeartors to the vertices was developed by Erler, Konopka and Sachs
Erler2014
and later extended to the Ramond sector
Erler2016
.
Though not directly built from the supermoduli space, a geometric interpretation using vertical integration
Sen2015
was formulated in
Erler2017
.
A key advantage of the homotopy algebra approach is its compatibility with Batalin-Vilkovisky quantization
Zwiebach1993
; however quantum-level developments remain limited.

On the other hand, the term “superstring” conventionally refers to the five theories possessing
\mathcal{N}=1
superconformal symmetry on the worldsheet.
However, though often overlooked, under the requirement of a positive critical dimension, it is also possible to impose
\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal symmetry on the worldsheet theory, which has a curious property that all massive excited states are absent.
Given the success of the complete homotopy-algebraic formulation of
\mathcal{N}=1
superstring field theory, it is natural to ask whether similar constructions apply to
\mathcal{N}=2
strings.

Our understanding of the spacetime geometry aspects of
\mathcal{N}=2
strings was greatly improved by the work of Ooguri and Vafa
Ooguri1990
;
Ooguri1991
, which revealed their connection to the self-dual sector of Yang-Mills and Einstein theory in Euclidean or split signature
1
1

1

In Euclidean signature, the on-shell condition becomes trivial, hence we focus on split signature with two timelike dimensions
.
While at first glance the critical dimensionality does not seem to promise interesting phenomenology, connections have been suggested from self-dual theory to maximally helicity-violating QCD amplitudes
Bardeen1996
;
Chalmers1996
, to super Yang-Mills
Neitzke2004
, to F-theory
Vafa1996
and to integrability
Hamanaka2023
.

Another motivation for studying
\mathcal{N}=2
string field theory comes from the attempt to understand the underlying geometry of Berkovits’ formulation.
In his original proposal, Berkovits started by enlarging and twisting the
\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal algebra and obtained an interacting theory of string field.
The action of
\mathcal{N}=1
strings can then be obtained by embedding it into a
\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal algebra
Berkovits1994
;
BerkovitsTop1995
and consequently, the reason why Berkovits’s formulation works so well is likely to be unraveled from the geometric aspect of the
\mathcal{N}=2
moduli space.

This paper is a first step towards the goal by generalizing the homotopy-algebraic method to the
\mathcal{N}=2
case.
The paper is organized as follows.
We review generalities of open
\mathcal{N}=2
string theory in Section
2
.
Section
3
is the main part of this article, where we generalize the homotopy-algebraic construction of Erler, Konopka and Sachs to the case involving two picture-changing operators are present.
In Section
4
, we prove that the action derived in this framework reproduces worldsheet scattering amplitudes — a direct extension of previous work
Konopka2015
in the
\mathcal{N}=1
case.
We conclude with a discussion of implications and future directions.

2
Relative cohomology and free theory

In this section, we review some facts about
\mathcal{N}=2
string theory and write down the free string field theory action using relative cohomology.
For a detailed exposition on the worldsheet theory, see for example
Bischoff1997Int
.
The flat Kleinian target spacetime
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
, where critical
\mathcal{N}=2
strings propagate, is endowed with a complex structure of
\mathbb{C}^{1,1}
.
It is a curious fact that the theory requires a chosen complex structure, thus breaking the
SO(2,2)
symmetry into
U(1,1)
.
A method of restoring the symmetry was developed in
Bischoff1997Res
, essentially averaging over all complex structures, but this will not be the main topic of this article.
On the worldsheet, there is a theory of
\mathcal{N}=2
supergravity
(h,\,\chi,\,A)
containing the metric, the gravitino fields and a
U(1)
gauge field on a two-dimensional Riemann surface with boundaries, coupled to two chiral
\mathcal{N}=2
massless matter multiplets
(Z^{ij},\psi^{ij})
, where
i,j\in\{+,-\}
.
Here,
i
denotes the spacetime complex conjugation, which is consistent with the
\mathcal{N}=2
superspace fermionic variable notation
(\theta^{+},\theta^{-})
, and
j
is the light-cone index in the usual sense:

\begin{split}Z^{\pm j}&=Z^{1j}+iZ^{2j},\\
Z^{1\pm}&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(X^{0}\pm X^{1}\right),\\
Z^{2\pm}&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(X^{2}\pm X^{3}\right),\\
\end{split}

(2)

where
(X^{0},X^{1},X^{2},X^{3})
are coordinates on the real space
\mathbb{R}^{2,2}
, and
(Z^{++},Z^{+-})
are the light-cone coordinates on the complex space
\mathbb{C}^{1,1}
, with
(Z^{-+},Z^{--})
their complex conjugates.
Note that in the complex spacetime,
Z
is a complex boson and
\psi
is a Dirac spinor on the worldsheet.
The worldsheet action after gauge fixing
(h^{\alpha\beta}=\delta^{\alpha\beta},\chi=0,A=0)
is given by
Brink1977

S=-\frac{1}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\int\mathrm{d}^{2}z(\partial_{\alpha}Z^{+\mu}\partial^{\alpha}Z_{-\mu}-i\overline{\psi}^{+\mu}\rho^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}\psi_{-\mu}),

(3)

where
\alpha=0,1
is the worldsheet index and
\rho
are the two-dimensional Dirac matrices.

Gauge fixing requires introducing the ghosts
(c,\tilde{c},\gamma^{\pm})
for the diffeomorphism,
U(1)
gauge symmetry and
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetry, respectively.
The Jacobian of the change of path integral variables is compensated by the ghost action

S_{gh}=\frac{2}{\alpha^{\prime}}\int\left(b\bar{\partial}c+\tilde{b}\bar{\partial}\tilde{c}+\beta^{-}\bar{\partial}\gamma^{+}+\beta^{+}\bar{\partial}\gamma^{-}\right),

(4)

which additionally consists of anti-ghosts
(b,\tilde{b},\beta^{\mp})
.
The ghosts obey the following OPEs:

b(z)c(w)\sim\frac{1}{z-w},

(5)

\beta^{\pm}(z)\gamma^{\mp}(w)\sim-\frac{1}{z-w},

(6)

and

\tilde{b}(z)\tilde{c}(w)\sim\frac{1}{z-w}.

(7)

The theory consists of a
\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal algebra generated by the currents:

\begin{split}T&=T_{m}+\partial cb+c\partial b+\partial\tilde{c}\tilde{b}-\frac{3}{2}(\partial\gamma^{-}\beta^{+}+\partial\gamma^{+}\beta^{-})-\frac{1}{2}(\gamma^{-}\partial\beta^{+}+\gamma^{+}\partial\beta^{-}),\\
G^{\pm}&=G^{\pm}_{m}-4\gamma^{\pm}b\mp 4\partial\gamma^{\pm}\tilde{b}\mp 2\gamma^{\pm}\partial\tilde{b}+\frac{3}{2}\partial c\beta^{\pm}+c\partial\beta^{\pm}\mp\tilde{c}\beta^{\pm},\\
J&=J_{m}+\partial(\tilde{c}b)+\gamma^{+}\beta^{-}-\gamma^{-}\beta^{+}.\end{split}

(8)

We do not write down the matter part generators
T_{m}
,
G_{m}
and
J_{m}
explicitly.
The BRST charge
Q
of this worldsheet theory is defined by the current

Q=\oint\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{2\pi i}J_{B}(z),

(9)

where

\begin{split}J_{B}=&cT+\gamma^{+}G^{-}+\gamma^{-}G^{+}+\tilde{c}J+c\partial cb+c\partial\tilde{c}b\\
&-4\gamma^{+}\gamma^{-}b+2\partial\gamma^{-}\gamma^{+}\tilde{b}-2\partial\gamma^{+}\gamma^{-}\tilde{b}\\
&+\frac{3}{4}\partial c(\gamma^{+}\beta^{-}+\gamma^{-}\beta^{+})-\frac{3}{4}c(\partial\gamma^{+}\beta^{-}+\partial\gamma^{-}\beta^{+})\\
&+\frac{1}{4}c(\gamma^{+}\partial\beta^{-}+\gamma^{-}\partial\beta^{+})+\tilde{c}(\gamma^{+}\beta^{-}-\partial\gamma^{-}\beta^{+}).\\
\end{split}

(10)

The commuting ghosts
\beta^{\mp}
and
\gamma^{\pm}
lead to an infinite degeneracy of the
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
vacua, known as the
picture number degeneracy
.
To address this issue, it is convenient to bosonize the superconformal ghosts as

\begin{split}\gamma^{\pm}&=\eta^{\pm}e^{\varphi^{\pm}},\\
\beta^{\mp}&=e^{-\varphi^{\pm}}\partial\xi^{\mp},\end{split}

(11)

and

\begin{split}\delta(\gamma^{\pm})&=e^{-\varphi^{\pm}},\\
\delta(\beta^{\mp})&=e^{\varphi^{\pm}},\end{split}

(12)

with the OPEs:

\begin{split}\varphi^{\pm}(z)\varphi^{\pm}(w)&\sim-\log(z-w),\\
\xi^{\mp}(z)\eta^{\pm}(w)&\sim\frac{1}{z-w}.\end{split}

(13)

The bosonized fields
\xi^{\pm}
enter the bosonization only through
\partial\xi^{\pm}
.
As a result, the Hilbert space after bosonizing contains unphysical degrees of freedom, the zero modes of
\xi^{\pm}(z)
denoted as
\xi^{\pm}
.
This “large” Hilbert space is the starting point of the Berkovits formulation in the
\mathcal{N}=1
case.
By requiring the exclusion of
\xi^{\pm}
zero modes, we get the physical condition on the large Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}\cong\{\Psi\in\mathcal{H}_{L}|\eta^{+}\Psi=\eta^{-}\Psi=0\},

(14)

where
\eta^{\pm}
is the zero mode of
\eta^{\pm}(z)
satisfying

\{\eta^{\pm},\xi^{\mp}\}=0.

(15)

\mathcal{H}
can be identified as a subspace of the large Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}\subset\mathcal{H}_{L}\cong\mathcal{H}\,\oplus\,\xi^{+}_{0}\mathcal{H}\,\oplus\,\xi^{-}_{0}\mathcal{H}\,\oplus\,\xi^{+}_{0}\xi^{-}_{0}\mathcal{H},

(16)

on which the symplectic form
\omega_{L}
is defined

\omega=\omega_{L}(\xi^{+}\xi^{-}\otimes\mathbb{I}).

(17)

In the following,
\xi^{\pm}
will be used in the intermediate steps of the construction, but the physical condition will be used to fix the ambiguity, yielding an action in the small Hilbert space.

Vertex operators in different pictures can be mapped by the picture changing operators (PCO)

X^{\pm}(z)=\left\{Q,\xi^{\pm}(z)\right\}.

(18)

We can then compute the OPE

\begin{split}X^{\pm}(z)X^{\pm}(w)&\sim 0,\\
X^{\pm}(z)X^{\mp}(w)&\sim\{Q,\mathrm{singular}\}.\end{split}

(19)

Note that
\partial X^{\pm}
is BRST-exact and thus can be moved arbitrarily on the worldsheet without affecting correlators.
However, in contrast to the
\mathcal{N}=1
string, the picture changing operators are not locally invertible.
There is one more ingredient in the
\mathcal{N}=2
theory: the
U(1)
gauge field
A(z)
.
Although locally one can always fix the gauge
A(z)=0
, the
U(1)
moduli must be taken into account when there are punctures on the worldsheet.
One can shift the holonomy around each puncture using the superconformal generator
J
.
Define the spectral flow operator

I(\theta)=\exp\left(\theta\oint\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{2\pi i}\log{(z)}J(z)\right).

(20)

The Ramond sector vertices can thus be obtained from Neveu-Schwarz vertices by applying the spectral flow operator.
Since the derivative of
I
is also BRST-exact

\partial I(\theta,z)=-\theta\{Q,\tilde{b}(z)I(\theta,z)\}.

(21)

We can then collect all the puncture twists together and conclude that it suffices to consider only vertex operators in the Neveu-Schwarz sector.
The worldsheet is then classified by an additional instanton number
n_{I}
corresponding to the Chern number of the
U(1)
bundle.

The
N
-point scattering amplitude of genus
g
and instanton number
n_{I}
is

\begin{split}A^{n_{I}}_{g,1,\dots,N}&=\prod_{i=1}^{g+N}\int\mathrm{d}M_{i}\prod_{j=1}^{3g-3+N}\int\mathrm{d}m_{j}\\
&\langle\int_{\tilde{a}}\tilde{b}\int\mu_{j}b(X^{-})^{2g-2+N+n_{I}}(X^{+})^{2g-2+N-n_{I}}I^{n_{I}}\tilde{c}V_{1}(z_{1})\dots V_{N}(z_{N})\rangle\end{split}

(22)

where
M_{i}
are the
U(1)
moduli,
m_{j}
are the complex moduli,
a_{i}
are integration contours around the each puncture and homology cycle and
\mu_{j}
are the Beltrami differentials for
m_{j}
.
The relevant amplitude for open string field theory is the tree amplitude (
g=0
) at zero instanton number
n_{I}=0

BerkovitsSup1995
.
The
\tilde{b}
integration around each puncture can be fixed by imposing constraint on the string field
Jnemann1999
.
The constraint we put on string field is

\tilde{b}_{0}\Psi=0,

(23)

which yields a well-defined relative BRST-cohomolgy since
\{Q,\tilde{b}_{0}\}=J_{0}
, and BRST-closed states with non-zero eigenvalues of the zero modes
J_{0}
of the bosonic
\mathcal{N}=2
super Virasoro generators
J(z)
are always exact.
Imposing the vanishing of
U(1)
anti-ghost zero mode removes the
\tilde{c}
zero mode dependence of the vertex operator.
The condition we impose here is very much similar to the level-matching condition
(b_{0}-\bar{b}_{0})\Psi=0
for closed string field theory, whereas the condition
b_{0}\Psi=0
that often appears in the literature is an extra gauge fixing known as Siegel’s gauge.
The ghost zero mode should be included in the symplectic form

\omega(A,B)=(-1)^{\mathrm{deg}(A)}\langle A,\tilde{c}_{0}B\rangle

(24)

to saturate the
U(1)
ghost zero mode.
The non-interacting string field action can then be written as

S[\Psi]=-\frac{1}{2}\omega(\Psi,Q\Psi),

(25)

and from now on, we denote the space of picture
(-1,-1)
string fields
\Psi
satisfying
\tilde{b}_{0}\Psi=0
as
\mathcal{H}
, and the corresponding large Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{L}
.

A typical vertex operator in the canonical picture
(-1,-1)
that will appear in the amplitudes is of the form
V=c\exp(-i\phi^{+}-i\phi^{-})V_{m}
, where
V_{m}
is a matter primary field.
To make up a non-vanishing correlator, the fields must carry total picture number
(-2,-2)
and thus the quadratic term requires no picture modification.
However, as we see in the following, for a
\Psi^{n}
term in the action,
(n-2)
PCO insertions for
X^{+}
and
X^{-}
need to be included in the action.

3
Interacting theory

In the free theory (
25
), only the kinetic term appears, representing an open
\mathcal{N}=2
string propagating without interaction.
An interacting action must include higher order terms representing interactions.
We already know the way to do it gauge invariantly is to exploit the
A_{\infty}
algebra structure
Gaberdiel1997
.
For bosonic string, it is related to the decomposition of the moduli space of Riemann surfaces with boundaries, and an elegant construction is Witten’s star product.
More subtleties appear when it is generalized to
\mathcal{N}=1
and
\mathcal{N}=2
supermoduli
Juro2013
.
Luckily, we are able to avoid this, just like in standard string theory textbook, by integrating out the fermionic variables and assigning the picture-changing operators to each vertex.
Indeed, Witten’s original proposal in
Witten1986Int
is to dress the bosonic product in (
1
) with a PCO inserted at the midpoint.

S_{\mathrm{sup,\,open}}[\Psi]=\frac{1}{2}\omega(\Psi,Q\Psi)+\frac{1}{3}\omega(\Psi,X(\mathrm{mid})\Psi\ast\Psi),

(26)

As a result, the new string product is no longer associative, and higher terms must be included.
However, the singular OPE of
X
with itself

X(\mathrm{mid})X(\mathrm{mid})\sim\infty

(27)

makes the higher terms divergent.
It has been known that this issue can be resolved by moving the PCO insertion away from the midpoint.
But there has not been a systematic way to write all higher terms compatible with the
A_{\infty}
relations until about a decade ago. In
Erler2014
the ghost mode
\oint\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{2\pi i}\frac{\xi(z)}{z}\equiv\xi
whose BRST variation gives resolved PCO insertions

\{Q,\xi\}=\oint\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{2\pi i}\frac{X(z)}{z}\equiv X,

(28)

is used in the intermediate steps, whereas the physical condition that eliminate the
\xi
zero mode in the final result is used to fix the ambiguity in solving the
A_{\infty}
relations.

In
\mathcal{N}=2
string theory, the divergence of Witten’s theory is more apparent even at three-point level: both
X^{+}
and
X^{-}
must be inserted at the midpoint.
The cubic term

S_{\mathrm{cubic}}[\Psi]\sim X^{+}(\mathrm{mid})X^{-}(\mathrm{mid})\Psi^{3}\sim\infty,

(29)

which needs to be resolved similarly.
It is the purpose of this section to argue that such a method can be generalized to the case of open
\mathcal{N}=2
strings.
To begin with, we briefly review the construction in
Erler2014
with a slightly more general setup.

3.1
Algebraic preliminaries

A generic action of field
\Psi
living in a graded vector space with cyclic
A_{\infty}
structure can be written as

S[\Psi]=-\frac{1}{2}\omega(\Psi,Q\Psi)-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{g^{n-1}}{n+1}\omega(\Psi,M_{n}(\Psi,\Psi)).

(30)

It forms a
cyclic
A_{\infty}
algebra
if the cyclicity

\begin{split}&\omega(M_{n}(\Psi_{1},...,\Psi_{n}),\Psi_{n+1})\\
&=(-1)^{\mathrm{deg}(\Psi_{1})(\mathrm{deg}(\Psi_{2})+...+\mathrm{deg}(\Psi_{n+1}))}\,\times\omega(M_{n}(\Psi_{2},...,\Psi_{n+1}),\Psi_{1}),\end{split}

(31)

and
A_{\infty}
relations

\begin{split}Q^{2}\Psi&=0,\\
QM_{2}(\Psi_{1},\Psi_{2})+M_{2}(Q\Psi_{1},\Psi_{2})&+(-1)^{\mathrm{deg}(\Psi_{1})}M_{2}(\Psi_{1},Q\Psi_{2})=0,\\
QM_{3}(\Psi_{1},\Psi_{2},\Psi_{3})+M_{2}(M_{2}(\Psi_{1},\Psi_{2}),\Psi_{3})&+(-1)^{\operatorname{deg}(\Psi_{1})}M_{2}(\Psi_{1},M_{2}(\Psi_{2},\Psi_{3}))\\
M_{3}(Q\Psi_{1},\Psi_{2},\Psi_{3})+(-1)^{\operatorname{deg}(\Psi_{1})}M_{3}(\Psi_{1},Q\Psi_{2},\Psi_{3})&+(-1)^{\operatorname{deg}(\Psi_{1})+\operatorname{deg}(\Psi_{2})}M_{3}(\Psi_{1},\Psi_{2},Q\Psi_{3})=0,\\
&\cdots\end{split}

(32)

are satisfied.
We will sometimes denote
Q
as
M_{0}
to get align with the notations.
This action is invariant under the gauge transformation

\delta\Psi=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}M_{n}(\Psi^{n-k-1}\otimes\Lambda\otimes\Psi^{k}),

(33)

where
\Lambda
is the gauge parameter.
For open string field theory, the grading on
\mathcal{H}
is defined as the shifted Grassmann parity,

\mathrm{deg}(\Psi)=\mathrm{gr}(\Psi)+1.

(34)

The
A_{\infty}
relations can be conveniently expressed if we use the coalgebra notation briefly review here.
Consider the tensor algebra of it the space of string fields
\mathcal{H}

T\mathcal{H}=\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}.

(35)

Any
n
-product
b_{n}:\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}\to\mathcal{H}
can be extended to a coderivation on the tensor coalgebra
\mathbf{b}_{n}:T\mathcal{H}\to T\mathcal{H}
, denoted by bold font letters, that is

\mathbf{b}_{n}=\sum_{N=n}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{N-n}\mathbb{I}^{\otimes N-n-k}\otimes b_{n}\otimes\mathbb{I}^{\otimes k}\circ P_{N},

(36)

where
P_{N}
is the projector to the
N
-fold tensor space.
Given two coderivations
\mathbf{b}_{n}
and
\mathbf{c}_{m}
, their commutator is another coderivation derived from a
(n+m-1)
-product

[b_{m},c_{n}]\equiv b_{m}\circ\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{I}^{\otimes m-1-k}\otimes c_{n}\otimes\mathbb{I}^{\otimes k}-(-1)^{\mathrm{deg}(b_{m})\mathrm{deg}(c_{n})}c_{n}\circ\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathbb{I}^{\otimes n-1-k}\otimes b_{m}\otimes\mathbb{I}^{\otimes k}.

(37)

With the coalgebra notation, the
A_{\infty}
relations can be compactly written as

[\mathbf{M}_{1},\mathbf{M}_{n}]+[\mathbf{M}_{2},\mathbf{M}_{n-1}]+\cdots+[\mathbf{M}_{n-1},\mathbf{M}_{2}]+[\mathbf{M}_{n},\mathbf{M}_{1}]=0.

(38)

Furthermore, we can define a formal power series

\mathbf{M}(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}t^{n-1}\mathbf{M}_{n}

(39)

and the relations are equivalent to

[\mathbf{M}(t),\mathbf{M}(t)]=0.

(40)

3.2
Picture assigning procedure

The method developed in
Erler2014
is to determine a flow
\mathbf{M}(s,t)
, controlled by an extra variable
s
, on the space of coderivations, such that

1. The
A_{\infty}
relations are always satisfied,

[\mathbf{M}(s,t),\mathbf{M}(s,t)]=0.

(41)

2. One end of the flow
\mathbf{M}(1,0)
consists of bosonic string products carrying zero picture number,

\mathbf{M}(1,0)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{M}_{n}^{(0)},

(42)

where the superscript denotes the picture number that the product carries,
while on the other end
\mathbf{M}(0,1)
, every
n
-fold component of the coderivation carries the correct picture number,

\mathbf{M}(0,1)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{M}_{n}^{(n-1)}.

(43)

3. All products are in the small Hilbert space.

The necessity of the last requirement is due to the observation that, to solve for the
A_{\infty}
relations with picture dressing one needs to introduce the operator
\xi
satisfying
\{Q,\xi\}=X
.
As mentioned before,
\xi
can be concretely realized as a line integral of superconformal ghost
\xi(z)
.
However,
\xi
carries unphysical degrees of freedom.
To eliminate them, one ought to demand that

[\boldsymbol{\eta},\mathbf{M}]=0

(44)

as an extra constraint on the flow, here
\boldsymbol{\eta}
denotes the coderivation from the zero mode of the
\eta(z)
ghost.
We will see how this requirement, together with the
A_{\infty}
relation (
41
), determines the entire flow.
The integrated flow is written as

\begin{split}\mathbf{M}(s,t)\equiv\,\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}s^{m}t^{n}&\mathbf{M}^{(n)}_{m+n+1}\\
=Q+s\mathbf{M}_{2}^{(0)}&+s^{2}\mathbf{M}_{3}^{(0)}+\cdots\\
+t\mathbf{M}_{2}^{(1)}&+ts\mathbf{M}_{3}^{(1)}+\cdots\\
&+t^{2}\mathbf{M}_{3}^{(2)}+\cdots,\end{split}

(45)

and it satisfies a pair of differential equations

{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathbf{M}(s,t)}{{}\,=[\mathbf{M}(s,t),\boldsymbol{\mu}(s,t)],}

(46)

{\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\mathbf{M}(s,t)}{{}\,=[\boldsymbol{\eta},\boldsymbol{\mu}(s,t)],}

(47)

where

\boldsymbol{\mu}(s,t)\equiv\sum_{m,n=0}s^{m}t^{n}\boldsymbol{\mu}^{(n+1)}_{m+n+2},

(48)

is an auxiliary coderivation.
It is easy to see that (
46
) ensures the
A_{\infty}
relation on the entire flow by computing

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[\mathbf{M}(s,t),\mathbf{M}(s,t)\right]=\left[\left[\mathbf{M}(s,t),\mathbf{M}(s,t)\right],\boldsymbol{\mu}(s,t)\right].

(49)

At
t=0
the bosonic string products satisfies
\left[\mathbf{M}(s,0),\mathbf{M}(s,0)\right]=0
so it is also true for all the other
t
.
On the other hand, (
46
) keeps the physical condition, because

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[\boldsymbol{\eta},\mathbf{M}(s,t)\right]=\left[\left[\boldsymbol{\eta},\mathbf{M}(s,t)\right],\boldsymbol{\mu}(s,t)\right]-\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathbf{M}(s,t),\mathbf{M}(s,t)\right],

(50)

where the second term has been shown to vanish.
We can then conclude that since the bosonic products are in the small Hilbert space
\left[\boldsymbol{\eta},\mathbf{M}(s,0)\right]=0
, the entire flow is also in the small Hilbert space.

The differential equations (
46
) and (
47
) derives a series of recursive relations determining all products.
Recursively we have,

\mathbf{M}^{(n+1)}_{m+n+2}=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum^{k=n}_{k=0}\sum^{l=m}_{l=0}[\mathbf{M}^{(k)}_{k+l+1},\boldsymbol{\mu}^{(n-k+1)}_{m+n-k-l+2}],

(51)

which allows us to compute
\mathbf{M}_{n}^{m+1}
with
\mathbf{M}_{k}^{l}
and
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{k}^{l+1}
for all
0\leq k\leq n,\,0\leq l\leq m
at hand.
To solve for (
47
), the auxiliary coderivation
\boldsymbol{\mu}
is derived from

\mu_{n}^{(m+1)}=(n-1)\xi\circ M_{n}^{(m)},

(52)

which makes sure

\left[\eta,\mu_{n}^{(m+1)}\right]=(n-1)M_{n}^{(m)}

(53)

and the cyclicity.
Here “
\xi\circ
” denotes the operation of taking the average of
\xi
acting on the output and on each input of a product
b_{n}
, that is

\xi\circ b_{n}=\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\xi b_{n}-b_{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathbb{I}^{\otimes k}\otimes\xi\otimes\mathbb{I}^{\otimes n-1-k}\right).

(54)

The average is taken for cyclicity.
Note that acting of
\xi
introduce an extra picture number to the coderivation and hence the shift of superscript.
Similarly we denote

X\circ b_{n}=\frac{1}{n+1}\left(Xb_{n}+b_{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathbb{I}^{\otimes k}\otimes X\otimes\mathbb{I}^{\otimes n-1-k}\right)

(55)

for the dressing with line integral of
X(z)
.
To conclude, (
51
) and (
52
) give a recursive formula for
n
-product
M_{n}^{n-1}
with the correct
(n-1)
picture number.

3.3
\mathcal{N}=2
string interaction

It is similar for
\mathcal{N}=2
strings but we have to deal with two picture numbers. Unlike the Type II string case where there are worldsheet holomorphic and anti-holomorphic copies of the PCOs, fields in spacetime holomophic and anti-holomorphic parts are not commutative in general.
The first step is assigning correct
\pi^{-}
picture number.
We start with the open bosonic vertices
m_{n}
,

\begin{split}m_{1}(\Psi)&=Q\Psi,\\
m_{2}(\Psi_{1},\Psi_{2})&=(-1)^{\mathrm{deg}(\Psi_{1})}\Psi_{1}\ast\Psi_{2},\\
m_{n}(\Psi_{1},\dots,\Psi_{n})&=0\quad\mathrm{for}\,\,\,n\,\geq\,3\end{split}

(56)

Consequently the auxiliary products
\mathbf{M}_{n}^{(0,m)}
and
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}^{(0,m)}

vanish for
m<n-2
.

\begin{split}\mathbf{M}^{-}(s^{-},t^{-})&\equiv\,\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(s^{-})^{m}(t^{-})^{n}\mathbf{M}^{(0,n)}_{m+n+1}\\
&=s^{-}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(t^{-})^{n}\mathbf{M}^{(0,n)}_{n+2}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}\mathbf{M}^{(0,n)}_{n+1}.\end{split}

(57)

The resulting vertices
M_{n}^{(0,n-1)}
should be in the small Hilbert space. This condition is written as, in the coalgebra notation,

[\boldsymbol{\eta}^{+},\mathbf{M}^{-}(s^{-},t^{-})]=0,

(58)

where
\mathbf{M}^{-}(t^{-})=\mathbf{Q}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{M}^{(n,0)}_{n+1}(t^{-})^{n}
is a formal power series collecting all the vertices with zero
\pi^{+}
picture number.
Note that the condition
[\boldsymbol{\eta}^{-},\mathbf{M}^{-}(s^{-},t^{-})]=0
automatically holds at this point since we do not introduce any
\xi^{+}
dressing.
As argued in the last previously, the solution we take satisfies two equations

\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{-}}\mathbf{M}^{-}(s^{-},t^{-})=[\mathbf{M}(s^{-},t^{-}),\boldsymbol{\mu}(s^{-},t^{-})],

(59)

\frac{\partial}{\partial s^{-}}\mathbf{M}^{-}(s^{-},t^{-})=[\boldsymbol{\eta}^{+}.\boldsymbol{\mu}^{-}(s^{-},t^{-})]

(60)

Here we introduce auxiliary coderivation flow

\boldsymbol{\mu}^{-}(s^{-},t^{-})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\boldsymbol{\mu}^{(0,n+1)}_{n+2}(t^{-})^{n}.

(61)

to facilitate the small Hilbert space condition (
58
).
Note that equations are simplified since
\mathbf{M}^{-}(s^{-},t^{-})
contains at most linear terms in
s^{-}
.

The next step is to assgin the
\pi^{+}
picutre number, now we have to start with a set of “bosonic”
\mathbf{M}^{-}(0,1)
vertices with non-vanishing higher-point parts.
We again want to determine a flow

\mathbf{M}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{M}^{(n,m+n)}_{m+n+1}(s^{+})^{m}(t^{+})^{n}

(62)

and set
\mathbf{M}(1,0)=\mathbf{M}^{-}(0,1)
.
with the differential equations

\frac{\partial\mathbf{M}(s^{+},t^{+})}{\partial t^{+}}=[\mathbf{M}(s^{+},t^{+}),\boldsymbol{\mu}(s^{+},t^{+})],

(63)

\frac{\partial}{\partial s^{+}}\mathbf{M}(s^{+},t^{+})=[\boldsymbol{\eta}^{-},\boldsymbol{\mu}(s^{+},t^{+})]

(64)

the recursive relations

\mu^{(m,0)}_{n}=(m+1)\xi^{+}\circ M^{(m+1,0)}_{n},

(65)

where

\boldsymbol{\mu}=\sum_{m=0,n=0}^{m=n=\infty}\mathbf{M}^{(m,n+1)}_{n+2}(s^{+})^{m}(t^{+})^{n}.

(66)

The four differential equations derives a series of recursive relations determining all products.
From the bosonic string product

\mathbf{M}^{-}(0,1)=\mathbf{Q}+\mathbf{m}_{2}

(67)

we have, recursively

\mathbf{M}^{(0,n+1)}_{n+2}=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum^{n}_{m=0}[\mathbf{M}^{(0,m)}_{m+1},\boldsymbol{\mu}^{(0,n-m+1)}_{n-m+2}],

(68)

\mathbf{M}^{(0,n)}_{n+2}=\frac{1}{n}\sum^{n-1}_{m=0}[\mathbf{M}^{(0,m)}_{m+2},\boldsymbol{\mu}^{(0,n-m)}_{n-m+1}],

(69)

and

\boldsymbol{\mu}^{(0,n+1)}_{n+2}=\xi^{+}\circ\mathbf{M}^{(0,n)}_{n+2}

(70)

Similarly for the
\pi^{+}
picture number, we have

\mathbf{M}^{(n+1,m+n+1)}_{m+n+2}=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum^{n}_{k=0}\sum^{m}_{l=0}\left[\mathbf{M}^{(k,k+l)}_{k+l+1},\boldsymbol{\mu}^{(n-k+1,m+n-k-l+1)}_{m+n-k-l+2}\right]

(71)

and

\boldsymbol{\mu}^{(m+1,m+n+1)}_{m+n+2}=(n+1)\xi^{-}\circ\mathbf{M}^{(m,m+n+1)}_{m+n+2}.

(72)

4
Scattering amplitudes

In this section we show that the action we just constructed algebraicly from gauge invariance gives the correct string amplitudes.
The strategy is similar to
Konopka2015
, where the flow on the space of coderivations allows us to move all the PCOs to the external legs with on-shell vanishing corrections.
We let
\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{M})
denote the minimal model of coderivation
\mathbf{M}
(possibly a flow with formal variables), which is related to the scattering matrix
S(\mathbf{M})
of the physical theory as

S(\mathbf{M})=\omega_{L}(\mathbb{I}\otimes\xi^{+}\xi^{-}P_{1}\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{M})).

(73)

The scattering matrix is defined on the on-shell states in the cohomology of
Q
.
If we consider the projection
P
to
\mathrm{ker}(Q)
, we can construct a contracting homotopy
H
, such that

\begin{split}QH+HQ&=\mathbb{I}-P,\\
HP&=0,\\
PH&=0,\\
H^{2}&=0.\end{split}

(74)

We promote
P,H
to the level of derivation
\mathbf{P},\mathbf{H}
.

\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{M})
can be computed using homological perturbation theory
Konopka2015
,

\mathbf{P}\left(\mathbf{Q}+\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{int}}(1-\mathbf{H}\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{int}})^{-1}\right)\mathbf{P},

(75)

where
\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{int}}=\mathbf{M}-\mathbf{Q}
is the interacting part.
To recursively check the scattering amplitude relation, we can compute that partial derivatives

\frac{\partial}{\partial s^{+}}\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{M})=[\boldsymbol{\eta}^{-},\hat{P}(1-\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{int}}H)^{-1}\boldsymbol{\mu}(1-H\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{int}})^{-1}\hat{P}]\equiv[\boldsymbol{\eta}^{-},\boldsymbol{\rho}]

(76)

The right hand side is
\eta^{-}
-exact.
Since
\xi^{+}\circ
is a contracting homotopy for
\eta^{-}
, we can decompose
\rho
into
\xi^{+}\circ
part and
\eta^{-}
-exact part,

\xi^{+}\circ\frac{\partial}{\partial s^{+}}\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{M})=\boldsymbol{\rho}-[\boldsymbol{\eta}^{-},\xi^{+}\circ\boldsymbol{\rho}]

(77)

To get the desired PCO insertion, we take the BRST derivation of (
77
)

\begin{split}X^{+}\circ\frac{\partial}{\partial s^{+}}\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{M})=\left[\mathbf{Q},\boldsymbol{\rho}\right]-\left[\mathbf{Q},[\boldsymbol{\eta}^{-},\xi^{+}\circ\boldsymbol{\rho}]\right].\end{split}

(78)

Note that

[\mathbf{Q},\boldsymbol{\rho}]=\hat{P}(1-\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{int}}H)^{-1}[\mathbf{M},\boldsymbol{\mu}](1-H\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{int}})^{-1}\hat{P}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{+}}\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{M}),

(79)

which is just

\begin{split}&\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{+}}\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{M})\\
&=\hat{P}(1-\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{int}}\mathbf{H})^{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{+}}\mathbf{M}\mathbf{H}(1-\mathbf{H}\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{int}})^{-1}\mathbf{M}_{int}\hat{P}+\hat{P}(1-\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{int}}\mathbf{H})^{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{+}}\mathbf{M}\hat{P}\\
&=\hat{P}(1-\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{int}}H)^{-1}[\mathbf{M},\boldsymbol{\mu}](1-H\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{int}})^{-1}\hat{P}.\end{split}

(80)

So we have

X^{+}\circ\frac{\partial}{\partial s^{+}}\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{M})=\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{+}}\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{M})-[\mathbf{Q},[\boldsymbol{\eta}^{-},\xi^{+}\circ\boldsymbol{\rho}]]=\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{+}}\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{M})-[\boldsymbol{\eta^{-}},[\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{T}]],

(81)

where
\mathbf{T}
is some irrelavent part, since the
\xi^{+}
in (
73
) strips off
\mathbf{\eta}^{-}
, leaving only
Q
-exact term.
This means that up to irrelavent part we have

\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{M})^{[m,0]}_{n}=\frac{m+1}{n-m-1}X^{+}\circ\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{M})_{n}^{[m+1,0]},

(82)

where
\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{M})^{[m,0]}_{n}
is the coefficient of
(s^{+})^{m}(t^{+})^{n}
.
After reduction we have

\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{M})^{[0,0]}_{n}=(X^{+})^{n-1}\circ\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{M})^{[n-1,0]}_{n}=(X^{+})^{n-1}\circ\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{M}^{-}(0,1)),

(83)

that is to say

\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{M}(1,0))=(X^{+})^{n-1}\circ\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{M}^{-}(0,1)).

(84)

Similarly for the flow
\mathbf{M}^{-}
we have

X^{-}\circ\frac{\partial}{\partial s^{-}}\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{M}^{-})=\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{-}}\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{M}^{-})-[\boldsymbol{\eta^{+}},[\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{T}^{-}]],

(85)

yielding

\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{M}^{-})^{[0,0]}_{n}=(X^{-})^{n-1}\circ\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{M}^{-})^{[n-1,n-1]}_{n},

(86)

namely

\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{M}^{-}(1,0))=(X^{-})^{n-1}\circ\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{M}^{-}(0,1))=(X^{-})^{n-1}\circ\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{Q}+\mathbf{m}_{2})

(87)

In a word, the action we constructed has a S-matrix of

\begin{split}S_{n+1}(\mathbf{M}(1,0))&=\omega_{L}(\mathbb{I}\otimes\xi^{+}\xi^{-}P_{1}\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{M}(1,0)))\\
&=\omega_{L}\left(\mathbb{I}\otimes\xi^{+}\xi^{-}P_{1}(X^{+})^{n-1}\circ(X^{-})^{n-1}\circ\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{Q}+\mathbf{m}_{2})\right)\\
\end{split}

(88)

Since moving the PCOs around only results in BRST-exact term, we conclude that

S_{n+1}=S^{\mathrm{bosonic}}_{n+1}(X^{+})^{n-2}(X^{-})^{n-2}.

(89)

5
Summary and discussion

We have constructed a string field theory action with cyclic
A_{\infty}
structure for the open
\mathcal{N}=2
string.
We argued that, by restrcting the string field to the kernel of
\tilde{b}_{0}=0
, we can write the free theory in the relative BRST cohomology sense.
Furthermore, the spectral flow induced isomorphism allows us to consider the Neveu-Schwarz sector only, thereby simplifying the theory.
The construction of interacting vertices proceeds via a two-step procedure that successively assigns both the correct
\pi^{-}
and
\pi^{+}
picture number in turn.
Moreover, the S-matrix of this theory has been show to reproduce those obtained from the worldsheet approach.

It would be a straightforward generalization to include the worldsheet antiholomorphic sector of closed string.
This construction is purely algebraic and it is interesting to explore its relation to the
\mathcal{N}=2
supermoduli space.
More curious is the fact that the
n
-point worldsheet amplitudes of genus
g
of
\mathcal{N}=2
string have been shown to vanish for
n>3
and
g>0

BerkovitsTop1995
.
This suggests that in principle one can find a quasi-isomorphic loop homotopy algebra truncated at cubic level, and the cubic vertex alone suffices to cover the entire
\mathcal{N}=2
supermoduli space, similar to Witten’s bosonic theory.
We hope the method developed in
Ohmori2018
can provide further insights into this problem.
Moreover, a cubic action may initialize a generalization of recently realized homotopy double copy
Borsten2021
at the string level.

Our construction focuses on the zero-instanton sector just like Berkovits formulation.
It has been shown that, rather surprisingly, including all instanton sectors does not change the dynamics but only affects gauge
Lechtenfeld1997
, and the field theory amplitudes can be derived from a cubic self-dual Yang-Mills action.

Finally, the connection between the homotopy-algebraic action and Berkovits’ WZW-like action is well-established in the
\mathcal{N}=1
case
Erler2015
, but remains largely unexplored for
\mathcal{N}=2
string theory.
We hope to come to these issues in future work.

1.
Introduction

Since the early seventies, the theory of vector bundles on projective varieties has become one of the mainstreams in algebraic geometry. For instance, they offer a valuable point of view in order to better understand the geometry of algebraic varieties. Despite huge achievements in vector bundles theory, many naif questions still remain open. Since the whole category of vector bundles on a projective variety is usually unwieldy, one has to impose some restrictions on the families of vector bundles under consideration. One of the approaches that has been used recently deals with the cohomological properties of vector bundles. That is, impose some cohomological conditions on the bundles to restrict the class of bundles to study. Nowadays there are many nice papers about vector bundles without intermediate cohomology (ACM bundles) and even a lot of them concern bundles without intermediate cohomology with the maximum number of global sections (Ulrich bundles). The goal of this paper is to study a class of vector bundles, called prioritary bundles, that share a strong cohomological property (see Definition
2.4
). Prioritary sheaves were introduced in the early nineties by Hirschowitz and Laszlo in
[
8
]
to study vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and, later on, the notion was generalized by Walters in
[
11
]
. Apart from the fact that prioritary bundles have a nice cohomological description, they also play an important role in the study of the moduli space of
H
-stable vector bundles. In fact, under certain conditions on the polarization
H
,
H
-stable vector bundles are prioritary and the moduli space of
H
-stable bundles is an open subset inside the moduli stack of prioritary bundles. Hence, some questions concerning stable bundles can be deduced from the study of the corresponding prioritary bundles. Stability is one of the classical notions used to restrict the class of bundles to consider. For example, prioritary bundles have been used to describe the Picard group of the moduli space of sheaves on quadric surfaces (see
[
9
]
) and the weak Brill-Noether property (see
[
3
]
). Hence in some sense prioritary bundles link both approaches.

In this paper we will pay attention on prioritary bundles on ruled surfaces. It is not difficult to obtain good families of rank two prioritary bundles on algebraic surfaces, but, in general, the difficulties appear when we try to deal with vector bundles of rank
r
bigger than two, that is when the rank is bigger than the dimension of the base space. On one hand, we will be focused on the existence of rank
r\geq 3
prioritary bundles on ruled surfaces but we will also be interested in their module of global sections. In fact, we will be focused on the existence of prioritary bundles with certain number of independent global sections. This study is the first step in the study of subvarieties of Brill-Noether type since effective bounds of the dimension of the module of global sections allows to determine when these subvarieties are non empty (
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
). Finally let us point out that all the prioritary bundles that we construct are simple. In particular this guarantees that they are indecomposable.

Now we will outline the structure of the paper.
In Section 2, we will fix some notation and we will recall some basic facts about ruled surfaces and also some properties of prioritary vector bundles on them. We will end the section with a result (Theorem
2.7
) that will allow us to construct recursively prioritary bundles in subsequent sections. The goal of Section 3, is to construct rank
3
simple prioritary vector bundles with many sections. We will use different constructions according to their normalized first Chern class (see Theorems
3.2
,
3.5
and
3.6
).
In Section 4, in Theorems
4.4
and
4.6
we will construct rank
4
prioritary vector bundles with many sections, using recursively the results given in the previous section.
Finally, in Section 5, we will prove (see Theorems
5.2
and
5.5
) the existence of simple prioritary bundles of arbitrarily large rank. To do so, we will construct general families of arbitrary rank prioritary bundles and we will give effective lower bounds for the dimension of their space of global sections. Finally, for the case
r=4
, we will compare the results of this section with the ones of Section 4.

Notation:

Throughout this paper we will work on an algebraically closed field
K
of characteristic
0
.

2.
Preliminaries on prioritary sheaves

The first goal of this section is to fix some notation concerning ruled surfaces and recall basic facts about prioritary bundles on them. We will end the section with a result that allows us to construct simple prioritary bundles recursively.

A ruled surface is a surface
X
, together with a surjective map
\pi:X\rightarrow C
to a nonsingular curve
C
of genus
g\geq 0
such that, for every point
y\in C
, the fibre
X_{y}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and such that
\pi
admits a section. Recall that a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
C
is called normalized if
h^{0}(C,\mathcal{E})>0
and
h^{0}(C,\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{d}))=0
for any divisor
\mathfrak{d}
on
C
of negative degree. A ruled surface is also defined as the projectivization
X=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})
of a normalized rank
2
bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
C
. Let
\mathfrak{e}
be the divisor on
C
corresponding to the invertible sheaf
\wedge^{2}\mathcal{E}
and let us define
e:=-\deg(\mathfrak{e})
. We will assume
e\geq 0
.

Let
C_{0}\subseteq X
be a section such that
\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(C_{0})\cong\mathcal{E}
and let
f
be a fiber of
\pi
. We have that
\operatorname{Pic}(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}\bigoplus\pi^{*}\operatorname{Pic}(C)
, where
\mathbb{Z}
is generated by
C_{0}
. Also
\operatorname{Num}(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}\bigoplus\mathbb{Z}
, generated by
C_{0}
and
f
satisfying
C_{0}^{2}=-e
,
C_{0}\cdot f=1
and
f^{2}=0
.
If
\mathfrak{b}
is a divisor on
C
, we will write
\mathfrak{b}f
instead of
\pi^{*}\mathfrak{b}
. Thus a divisor
D
on
X
can be written uniquely as
D=aC_{0}+\mathfrak{b}f
, being
\mathfrak{b}\in\operatorname{Pic}(C)
, and its element in
\operatorname{Num}(X)
can be written as
D\equiv aC_{0}+bf
with
b=\deg(\mathfrak{b})
.
The canonical divisor
K_{X}
on
X
is in the class
-2C_{0}+(\mathfrak{k}+\mathfrak{e})f
, where
\mathfrak{k}
is the canonical divisor on
C
of degree
2g-2
.

Notation 2.1
.

By abuse of notation, we will denote the tensor product
\mathcal{O}_{X}(aC_{0}+\mathfrak{b}f)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(f)
by
\mathcal{O}_{X}(aC_{0}+(\mathfrak{b}+1)f)
and we will denote the canonical divisor by
K_{X}=-2C_{0}-\overline{e}f
with
\overline{e}:=e-2(g-1)
.
Usually, we will write
\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
to refer to
\operatorname{h}^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))
.

For any divisor
D=aC_{0}+\mathfrak{b}f
on
X
with
a\geq 0
, it follows from Lemma V.2.4, Exercises III.8.3 and
III.8.4 of
[
7
]
, that

(1)

\operatorname{h}^{i}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))=\operatorname{h}^{i}(C,(S^{a}\mathcal{E})(\mathfrak{b})),

where
S^{a}\mathcal{E}
stands for the
a
-th symmetric power of
\mathcal{E}
.

Moreover,

(2)

\operatorname{h}^{0}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C}(\mathfrak{b}))\leq\operatorname{h}^{0}(C,(S^{a}\mathcal{E})(\mathfrak{b}))\leq\sum_{i=0}^{a}\operatorname{h}^{0}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C}(\mathfrak{b}+i\mathfrak{e})),

for each divisor
\mathfrak{b}
on
C
(see for instance
[
1
, Section 2]
).

For effective divisors on
X
we have the following
(see
[
4
, Lemma 2.2]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X
be a ruled surface over a nonsingular curve
C
of genus
g\geq 0
and
D=aC_{0}+\mathfrak{b}f
be a divisor on
X
with
b:=\deg(\mathfrak{b})
. If
D
is effective then
a,b\geq 0
.

The following is a well-known result.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface,
D
a divisor on
X
and
Z\subset X
a generic 0-dimensional subscheme. If
|Z|\geq\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
then
\operatorname{h}^{0}I_{Z}(D)=0
.

Given a vector bundle
E
on
X
of rank
r
and fixed Chern Classes
c_{i}:=c_{i}(E)\in\operatorname{H}^{2i}(X,\mathbb{Z})
, the Riemann-Roch Theorem states that the Euler characteristic of
E
can be expressed as

\chi(r;c_{1},c_{2})=r(1-g)-\frac{c_{1}K_{X}}{2}+\frac{c_{1}^{2}}{2}-c_{2}.

Prioritary sheaves were introduced on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
in the early nineties by Hirschowitz and Laszlo in
[
8
]
as a generalization of semistable sheaves. Later on, Walter introduced in
[
11
]
the notion of prioritary sheaf in a much general context. Let us recall the definition.

Definition 2.4
.

If
\pi:X\rightarrow C
is a ruled surface and
E
is a coherent sheaf on
X
, we say that
E
is prioritary if
\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(E,E(-f))=0
.

Since the work of Walter (
[
11
]
), prioritary sheaves have become a very important class of sheaves, in particular for their relation with the stable ones. In fact, it is well-known that, given
H
an ample divisor, if
(K_{X}+f)\cdot H<0
, then
H-
stable sheaves are prioritary. Moreover, there exists an open immersion from the moduli space
M_{H}(r;c_{1},c_{2})
parametrizing rank
r

H
-stable vector bundles
E
on
X
with
c_{1}(E)=c_{1}
and
c_{2}(E)=c_{2}
to the stack of prioritary sheaves
\operatorname{Prior}_{X}(r;c_{1},c_{2})
and in particular, both spaces have the same expected dimension.

Ideal sheaves of 0-dimensional schemes are examples of prioritary coherent sheaves. In fact,

Lemma 2.5
.

Le
X
be a ruled surface over a non singular curve
C
of genus
g\geq 0
and let
Z
be a
0
-dimensional scheme on
X
of length
|Z|\geq 0
and
D\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)
. Then,
I_{Z}(D)
is simple and prioritary. In particular, line bundles are simple and prioritary.

Proof.

Let us consider the exact sequence

(3)

0\rightarrow I_{Z}(D)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Z}(D)\rightarrow 0.

First of all we will see that
I_{Z}(D)
is simple.
Applying the functor
\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z}(D),-)
to the exact sequence (
3
), we obtain the long exact sequence

(4)

0\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z}(D),I_{Z}(D))\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z}(D),\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z}(D),\mathcal{O}_{Z}(D))\rightarrow\cdots

Notice that

\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z}(D),\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))\cong\operatorname{H}^{2}I_{Z}(K_{X})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\operatorname{H}^{2}I_{Z}(K_{X})\cong K.

Hence, by (
4
)
\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z}(D),I_{Z}(D))\cong K
and thus
I_{Z}(D)
is simple.

Now we are going to see that
I_{Z}(D)
is prioritary.
Applying the functor
\mathrm{Hom}(-,I_{Z}(D-f))
to (
3
), we get the long exact sequence

(5)

\cdots\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{Z}(D),I_{Z}(D-f))\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(D),I_{Z}(D-f))\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(I_{Z}(D),I_{Z}(D-f))\rightarrow 0

Since

\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(D),I_{Z}(D-f))\cong\operatorname{H}^{2}I_{Z}(-f)\cong\operatorname{H}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+f)=0,

we get
\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(I_{Z}(D),I_{Z}(D-f))=0
and hence
I_{Z}(D)
is also prioritary.

∎

Remark 2.6
.

Since, for any ample divisor
H
on
X
with
(K_{X}+f)\cdot H<0
,
H
-stable vector bundles are prioritary, we have several examples of rank two simple prioritary vector bundles with sections (see for instance
[
4
]
).

In spite of that, recently, prioritary sheaves have been used to get nice contributions in different problems, there are a lot of questions concerning them still open. In this paper we will focus on their existence and on finding lower bounds for its number of independent sections. To this end, let us finish the section with the following key result that we will apply in subsequent sections.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
X
be a ruled surface over a nonsingular curve
C
of genus
g\geq 0
. Let
\mathcal{F}_{r}
be the family of rank r vector bundles
E_{r}
on
X
given by a nontrivial extension of type

(6)

0\rightarrow E_{r-1}\rightarrow E_{r}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(L)\rightarrow 0

where
L\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)
and
E_{r-1}
is a rank
r-1
vector bundle on
X
such that
\operatorname{h}^{1}E_{r-1}(-L)\neq 0
.

(a)

If
E_{r-1}
is a simple vector bundle with
\operatorname{h}^{0}E_{r-1}(-L)=0
and
\operatorname{h}^{2}E_{r-1}(K_{X}-L)=0
, then
E_{r}
is simple.

(b)

If
E_{r-1}
is a prioritary vector bundle with
\operatorname{h}^{0}E_{r-1}(K_{X}-L+f)=0
and
\operatorname{h}^{2}E_{r-1}(-f-L)=0
, then
E_{r}
is prioritary.

Proof.

(a) Assume that
E_{r-1}
is a simple vector bundle with
\operatorname{h}^{0}E_{r-1}(-L)=0
and
\operatorname{h}^{2}E_{r-1}(K_{X}-L)=0
.
Applying the functor
\mathrm{Hom}(-,E_{r})
to the exact sequence
(
6
),
we obtain

(7)

0\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(L),E_{r})\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(E_{r},E_{r})\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(E_{r-1},E_{r})\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(L),E_{r})\rightarrow\cdots

Let us first compute
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(L),E_{r})\cong\operatorname{H}^{0}E_{r}(-L)
.
If we twist the exact sequence (
6
) by
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-L)
and we take cohomology, we obtain

0\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{0}E_{r-1}(-L)\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{0}E_{r}(-L)\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}\overset{\alpha}{\rightarrow}\operatorname{H}^{1}E_{r-1}(-L)\rightarrow\cdots

Since
\operatorname{H}^{0}E_{r-1}(-L)=0
, we have
\operatorname{H}^{0}E_{r}(-L)\cong\ker(\alpha)
. Notice that the map
\alpha
can be indentified with the map

K\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(L),E_{r-1})

which sends
1\in K
to the nontrivial extension (
6
). Thus,
\alpha
is an injection and
\operatorname{H}^{0}E_{r}(-L)\cong\ker(\alpha)=0
.

Let us now compute
\mathrm{Hom}(E_{r-1},E_{r})
. Applying the functor
\mathrm{Hom}(E_{r-1},-)
to the exact sequence (
6
), we obtain

0\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(E_{r-1},E_{r-1})\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(E_{r-1},E_{r})\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(E_{r-1},\mathcal{O}_{X}(L))\rightarrow\cdots

Since
\mathrm{Hom}(E_{r-1},\mathcal{O}_{X}(L))\cong\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(L),E_{r-1}(K_{X}))\cong\operatorname{H}^{2}E_{r-1}(K_{X}-L)=0
and
E_{r-1}
is simple, we obtain
\mathrm{Hom}(E_{r-1},E_{r})\cong\mathrm{Hom}(E_{r-1},E_{r-1})\cong K
.

Putting altogether, by (
7
) we get that
1\leq\dim\mathrm{Hom}(E_{r},E_{r})\leq 1
and hence
E_{r}
is simple.

(b) Assume that
E_{r-1}
is a prioritary vector bundle with
\operatorname{h}^{0}E_{r-1}(K_{X}-L+f)=0
and
\operatorname{h}^{2}E_{r-1}(-f-L)=0
.

Applying the functor
\mathrm{Hom}(-,E_{r}(-f))
to (
6
), we get

(8)

\cdots\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(L),E_{r}(-f))\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(E_{r},E_{r}(-f))\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(E_{r-1},E_{r}(-f))\rightarrow 0

Let us first compute
\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(L),E_{r}(-f))\cong\operatorname{H}^{2}E_{r}(-f-L)
.
If we consider the extension (
6
) and we take cohomology, we get

\cdots\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{2}E_{r-1}(-f-L)\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{2}E_{r}(-f-L)\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{2}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-f)\rightarrow 0.

Since
\operatorname{H}^{2}E_{r-1}(-f-L)=0
and
\operatorname{H}^{2}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-f)\cong\operatorname{H}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+f)=0
, we conclude that

\operatorname{H}^{2}E_{r}(-f-L)=0
.

Now we are going to compute
\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(E_{r-1},E_{r}(-f))
. Applying the functor
\mathrm{Hom}(E_{r-1},-)
to the exact sequence (
6
) twisted by
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-f)
, we get

\cdots\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(E_{r-1},E_{r-1}(-f))\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(E_{r-1},E_{r}(-f))\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(E_{r-1},\mathcal{O}_{X}(L-f))\rightarrow 0.

Since
E_{r-1}
is prioritary and
\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(E_{r-1},\mathcal{O}_{X}(L-f))\cong\operatorname{H}^{0}E_{r-1}(K_{X}-L+f)=0
, we get
\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(E_{r-1},E_{r}(-f))=0
.

Putting altogether, by (
8
) we get that
E_{r}
is prioritary.
∎

3.
Rank 3 prioritary bundles

In the above section we have seen examples of rank 1 and 2 simple prioritary vector bundles with sections.
The main goal of this section is to prove the existence of simple prioritary rank 3 vector bundles on
X
and to find lower bounds for the dimension of their space of sections.

Notice that
E
is a simple prioritary vector bundle if and only if, for any divisor
D
on
X
,
E(D)
is simple and prioritary. Hence in this section we normalize the first Chern class of our rank
3
bundle
E
so that
c_{1}(E)=sC_{0}+\mathfrak{t}f
with
s=0,1,2
.
We will start with the case
c_{1}=2C_{0}+\mathfrak{t}f
and to this end, among others, we will apply Theorem
2.7
to construct rank-3 simple prioritary vector bundles with this first Chern class.

First of all let us prove the following technical Lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X
be a ruled surface over a nonsingular curve
C
of genus
g\geq 0
. Let us consider
\mathcal{S}_{2}
the family of rank-2 vector bundles given by a non trivial extension of type

(9)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f)\rightarrow E_{2}\rightarrow I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f)\rightarrow 0,

where
\mathfrak{m}
and
\mathfrak{b}
are divisors on
C
of degree
m=\deg(\mathfrak{m})\in\{0,1\}
and
b=\deg(\mathfrak{b})\geq 1
and
Z
is a generic 0-dimensional subscheme of length
|Z|\geq 1
. Let
L
be a divisor on
X
.
The following holds:

i)

If
-L+\mathfrak{b}f
is a non effective divisor and
|Z|>\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-L+C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f)
, then
\operatorname{h}^{0}E_{2}(-L)=0
.

ii)

If
L-\mathfrak{b}f
and
L-C_{0}-(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f
are non effective divisors, then
\operatorname{h}^{2}E_{2}(K_{X}-L)=0
.

iii)

\mathrm{ext}^{1}(I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f),\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f))>0
.

iv)

E_{2}
is a simple prioritary vector bundle.

Proof.

Using the exact sequence (
9
) and Proposition
2.3
we directly prove i). The proof of ii) follows using the exact sequence (
9
) and Serre duality.

iii) Notice that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathrm{ext}^{1}(I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f),\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f))&\geq-\chi I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-2\mathfrak{b})f+K_{X})\\
&=|Z|-\chi\mathcal{O}_{X}(-C_{0}-(\mathfrak{m}-2\mathfrak{b})f).\end{array}

Since by the Riemann-Roch theorem
\chi\mathcal{O}_{X}(-C_{0}-(\mathfrak{m}-2\mathfrak{b})f)=0
, we get

\mathrm{ext}^{1}(I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f),\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f))\geq|Z|>0.

iv) Since the couple
(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-2\mathfrak{b})f+K_{X}),Z)
satisfies the Cayley-Bacharach property,
E_{2}
is a rank
2
vector bundle.
Let us now check that
E_{2}
is simple. Applying the functor
\mathrm{Hom}(-,E_{2})
to the exact sequence (
9
), we get

0\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f),E_{2})\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(E_{2},E_{2})\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f),E_{2})\rightarrow\cdots

Claim 1:
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f),E_{2})\cong\operatorname{H}^{0}E_{2}(-\mathfrak{b}f)\cong K
.

Proof of Claim 1:

Twisting the exact sequence (
9
) by
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-\mathfrak{b}f)
and taking cohomology, we get

0\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{0}E_{2}(-\mathfrak{b}f)\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{0}I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-2\mathfrak{b})f)\rightarrow\cdots

Since
b\geq 1
and
m\in\{0,1\}
,
\operatorname{H}^{0}I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-2\mathfrak{b})f)=0
and hence
\operatorname{H}^{0}E_{2}(-\mathfrak{b}f)\cong\operatorname{H}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}\cong K.

Claim 2:
\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f),E_{2})=0
.

Proof of Claim 2:

Applying the functor
\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f),-)
to the exact sequence (
9
) we get

0\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f),\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f))\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f),E_{2})\rightarrow

\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f),I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f)\overset{\phi}{\rightarrow}\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f),\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f))\rightarrow\cdots

On one hand,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f),\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f))&\cong\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f),I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f+K_{X})\\
&\cong\operatorname{H}^{2}I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-2\mathfrak{b})f+K_{X})\\
&\cong\operatorname{H}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-C_{0}-(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f)=0,\end{array}

which implies that
\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f),E_{2})\cong\ker(\phi)
. On the other hand, by Lemma
2.5
,

\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f),I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f)\cong K,

and by v),
\phi
is the map that sends
1\in K
to the non trivial extension
e\in\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f),\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f))
defining
E_{2}
, which is injective. Hence

\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f),E_{2})\cong\ker(\phi)=0.

Putting altogether,
\mathrm{Hom}(E_{2},E_{2})\hookrightarrow K
and hence
E_{2}
is a simple vector bundle.

Let us now prove that
E_{2}
is a prioritary vector bundle.
Applying the functor
\mathrm{Hom}(-,E_{2}(-f))
to the exact sequence (
9
), we get

\cdots\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f),E_{2}(-f))\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(E_{2},E_{2}(-f))\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f),E_{2}(-f))\rightarrow 0.

Claim 3:
\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f),E_{2}(-f))\cong\operatorname{H}^{2}E_{2}(-(\mathfrak{b}+1)f)=0
.

Proof of Claim 3:

Twisting the exact sequence (
9
) by
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-(\mathfrak{b}+1)f)
and taking cohomology, we get

\cdots\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{2}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-f)\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{2}E_{2}(-(\mathfrak{b}+1)f)\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{2}I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-2\mathfrak{b}-1)f)\rightarrow 0,

and, since

\begin{array}[]{ll}\operatorname{H}^{2}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-f)\cong\operatorname{H}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+f)=0&\\
\operatorname{H}^{2}I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-2\mathfrak{b}-1)f)\cong\operatorname{H}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-C_{0}-(\mathfrak{m}-2\mathfrak{b}-1)f+K_{X})=0,&\end{array}

we get
\operatorname{H}^{2}E_{2}(-(\mathfrak{b}+1)f)=0
.

Claim 4:
\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f),E_{2}(-f))=0
.

Proof of Claim 4:

Applying the functor
\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f,-)
to the exact sequence (
9
) twisted by
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-f)
, we get

\cdots\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f),\mathcal{O}_{X}((\mathfrak{b}-1)f))\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f),E_{2}(-f))

\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f),I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b}-1)f))\rightarrow 0

Since
\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f),\mathcal{O}_{X}((\mathfrak{b}-1)f))\cong\operatorname{H}^{0}I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-2\mathfrak{b}+1)f+K_{X})=0

2.5

\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f),I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b}-1)f)=0

\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(I_{Z}(C_{0}+(\mathfrak{m}-\mathfrak{b})f),E_{2}(-f))=0
.

Putting altogether, we get
\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(E_{2},E_{2}(-f))=0
and hence
E_{2}
is a prioritary vector bundle.

∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
X
be a ruled surface over a nonsingular curve
C
of genus
g\geq 0
. Let
c_{2}\gg 0
be an integer and
c_{1}=2C_{0}+\mathfrak{t}f\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)

\operatorname{h}^{0}E\geq k:=-t-g

Proof.

Let us consider
\mathcal{G}_{2}
the family of rank 2 vector bundles given by a non trivial extension of type

(10)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f)\rightarrow E_{2}\rightarrow I_{Z}(C_{0}-\mathfrak{b}f)\rightarrow 0,

where

\mathfrak{b}
is a divisor on
C
of degree

and
Z
is a generic 0-dimensional subscheme of length
|Z|=c_{2}+e+1\gg 0
.

Let us now consider the family
\mathcal{G}_{3}
of rank-3 vector bundles
E_{3}
with
c_{1}(E_{3})=c_{1}
and
c_{2}(E_{3})=c_{2}
given by a non trivial extension of type

(11)

0\rightarrow E_{2}\rightarrow E_{3}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)\rightarrow 0,

.

Notice that by construction,
\operatorname{h}^{0}E_{3}\geq\operatorname{h}^{0}E_{2}\geq\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f)=b+1-g=k=-t-g

Claim 1:
\mathcal{G}_{3}\neq\emptyset
.

Proof of Claim 1:

Let us first prove that the dimension of the space of extensions of type (
11
) is positive.
Since

\mathrm{ext}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(D),E_{2})=\operatorname{h}^{1}E_{2}(-D)\geq-\chi E_{2}(-D),

Since

c_{1}(E_{2}(-D))=c_{1}(E_{2})-2D=-C_{0}-2\mathfrak{t}f

c_{2}(E_{2}(-D))=c_{2}(E_{2})-c_{1}(E_{2})\cdot D+D^{2}=|Z|+b+t=|Z|-1,

we get

\chi E_{2}(-D)=2-g-|Z|<0,

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
|Z|\gg 0
.
Hence the dimension of the extension of type (
11
) is positive.
By construction,
E_{3}
is a rank-3 vector bundle with
c_{1}(E_{3})=C_{0}+D=2C_{0}+\mathfrak{t}f
and
c_{2}(E_{3})=c_{2}(E_{2})+D\cdot c_{1}(E_{2})=|Z|-1-e=c_{2}.

Hence
\mathcal{G}_{3}\neq\emptyset
.

Claim 2:
E_{3}
is simple and prioritary

Proof of Claim 2:

By Lemma
3.1
,
E_{2}
is a simple vector bundle and thus, by Theorem
2.7
, it is enough to see that
\operatorname{h}^{0}E_{2}(-D)=0
and
\operatorname{h}^{2}E_{2}(K_{X}-D)=0
.

Since

-D+\mathfrak{b}f=-C_{0}+(\mathfrak{b}-\mathfrak{t})f

\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D+C_{0}-\mathfrak{b}f)=\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{C}(1)<|Z|,

it follows from Lemma
3.1
that
\operatorname{H}^{0}E_{2}(-D)=0
.
Analogously, since
D-\mathfrak{b}f=C_{0}-(2\mathfrak{t}+1)f
and
D-C_{0}+\mathfrak{b}f=-f
are non effective divisors, by Lemma
3.1
we get
\operatorname{h}^{2}E_{2}(K_{X}-D)=0
. Hence, by Theorem
2.7
,
E_{3}
is simple.

By Lemma
3.1
,
E_{2}
is a prioritary vector bundle and thus, by Theorem
2.7
, it is enough to see that
\operatorname{h}^{0}E_{2}(K_{X}+f-D)=0
and
\operatorname{h}^{2}E_{2}(-f-D)=0
.

Since

K_{X}+f+\mathfrak{b}f-D=-3C_{0}+(-\mathfrak{t}+\mathfrak{b}+1-2g+2+e)f

and

K_{X}+f-D+C_{0}-\mathfrak{b}f=-2C_{0}+(-\mathfrak{t}-\mathfrak{b}+2g-1-e)f

are non effective divisors, it follows from Lemma
3.1
that
\operatorname{H}^{0}E_{2}(K_{X}+f-D)=0
.
Following the same arguments, we see that
\operatorname{H}^{2}E_{2}(-f-D)=0
. Hence, by Theorem
2.7
,
E_{3}
is prioritary and, therefore,
E_{3}
is a simple prioritary vector bundle.

∎

To study the other cases of
c_{1}

Let us start proving some preparatory Lemmas.

Lemma 3.3

12

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
X
be a ruled surface over a non singular curve
C
of genus
g\geq 0

c_{2}>>0
an integer,
\mathfrak{d}\in Pic(C)
with

\operatorname{h}^{0}E\geq k:=\frac{1}{5}(c_{2}-2e-3+d-l)+1-g.

Let us consider
\mathcal{F}_{2}
the family of rank two vector bundles
E_{2}
on
X
given by a nontrivial extension of type

(18)

Since
c_{2}\gg 0

b>\max\{0,2(g-1),\frac{d-1}{3},\frac{d+2(g-1)-e}{3}\}.

Claim 1:
\mathcal{F}_{2}\neq\emptyset
.

Proof of Claim 1:
First of all we will see that the space of extensions has positive dimension. Notice that

\mathrm{ext}^{1}:=\mathrm{ext}^{1}(I_{Z}(2C_{0}+(\mathfrak{d}-2\mathfrak{b}-1)f),\mathcal{O}_{X}(-C_{0}+(\mathfrak{b}+1)f))=\operatorname{h}^{1}I_{Z}(3C_{0}+(\mathfrak{d}-3\mathfrak{b}-2)f+K_{X})

\geq-\chi I_{Z}(3C_{0}+(\mathfrak{d}-3\mathfrak{b}-2)f+K_{X})=|Z|-\chi\mathcal{O}_{X}(3C_{0}+(\mathfrak{d}-3\mathfrak{b}-2)f+K_{X}).

c_{2}>>0

\chi\mathcal{O}_{X}(-3C_{0}-(\mathfrak{d}-3\mathfrak{b}-2)f)=-6+2g-3e+2d-6b<0

\mathrm{ext}^{1}=l+6-2g+3e-2d+6b>0.

Therefore the space of extensions of type (
18
) is nonempty.

On the other hand, since the divisor
3C_{0}+(\mathfrak{d}-3\mathfrak{b}-2)f+K_{X}
is not effective by the lower bound
3b>d-1
,
Z
satisfyies the Cayley-Bacharach property for the linear system
|3C_{0}+(\mathfrak{d}-3\mathfrak{b}-2)f+K_{X}|
. Hence,
E_{2}
is a rank 2 vector bundle with
c_{1}(E_{2})=C_{0}+(\mathfrak{d}-\mathfrak{b})f
and
c_{2}(E_{2})=|Z|+(2e+4b+3-d)=l+2e+4b+3-d
.

Putting altogether,
\mathcal{F}_{2}
is nonempty.

Let us now consider
\mathcal{F}_{3}
the family of rank 3 vector bundles
E_{3}
on
X
with
c_{1}(E_{3})=C_{0}+\mathfrak{d}f
and
c_{2}(E_{3})=c_{2}
given by a non trivial extension of type

(19)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f)\rightarrow E_{3}\rightarrow E_{2}\rightarrow 0,

where
E_{2}\in\mathcal{F}_{2}
.

Claim 2:
\mathcal{F}_{3}\neq\emptyset
.

Proof of Claim 2:

By construction,
E_{3}
is a rank 3 vector bundle on
X
with
c_{1}(E_{3})=C_{0}+\mathfrak{d}f
and
c_{2}(E_{3})=c_{2}
.
Notice that

s:=\mathrm{ext}^{1}(E_{2},\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f))=\mathrm{ext}^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f),E_{2}(K_{X}))=\operatorname{h}^{1}E_{2}(K_{X}-\mathfrak{b}f)\geq-\chi E_{2}(K_{X}-\mathfrak{b}f)

and, by Serre duality,
-\chi E_{2}(K_{X}-\mathfrak{b}f)=-\chi E^{*}_{2}(\mathfrak{b}f)

\begin{array}[]{ll}c_{1}(E^{*}_{2}(\mathfrak{b}f))=&-c_{1}(E_{2})+2\mathfrak{b}f=-C_{0}+(3\mathfrak{b}-\mathfrak{d})f\thinspace\thinspace\text{and}\\

by Riemann-Roch Theorem we have

3.4

Moreover, by (
19

, we have

\operatorname{h}^{0}E_{3}\geq\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f)=\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{C}(\mathfrak{b})=b+1-g=\frac{1}{5}(c_{2}-2e-3+d)+1-g.

∎

.

5.
Higher rank prioritary bundles

The goal of this section is to prove the existence of simple prioritary vector bundles of arbitrary high rank with at least certain number of sections. In general it is a difficult problem to construct indecomposable rank
r
vector bundles on a smooth projective variety
X
when
r
is big compared with
\dim X
. We will achive our goal using a generalization of Cayley-Bacharach property and in addition we will see that the bundles that we construct are prioritary with a certain number of sections. To this end, we start with a technical Lemma that we will use later on.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
X
be a ruled surface over a non singular curve
C
of genus
g\geq 0.

Let
E
be a rank
r
vector bundle on
X
given by a nontrivial extension of type

(29)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f)\rightarrow E\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r-1}I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i})\rightarrow 0

where for every
i
,

Z_{i}
is a 0-dimensional subscheme.
Let us assume that:

i) for every
i\neq j
,
D_{i}-D_{j}
is non effective,

ii) for every i,
\mathfrak{b}f-D_{i}
is non effective,

iii) for every
i\neq j
,
D_{i}-D_{j}+K_{X}+f
is non effective,

iv) for every
i
,
\mathfrak{b}f-D_{i}+K_{X}+f
is non effective,

v) for every
i
,
\operatorname{h}^{0}I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i}-\mathfrak{b}f)=0
,

vi) for every
i
,
\operatorname{h}^{0}I_{Z_{i}}(K_{X}+f+D_{i}-\mathfrak{b}f)=0
.

Then,
E
is a simple prioritary vector bundle.

Proof.

First of all we will see that
E
is simple.
Applying the functor
\mathrm{Hom}(-,E)
to the exact sequence (
29
), we get

0\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r-1}I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i}),E)\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(E,E)\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f),E)\rightarrow\cdots

Claim 1:
\mathrm{Hom}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r-1}I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i}),E)=0

Proof of Claim 1:

Since
\mathrm{Hom}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r-1}I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i}),E)\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r-1}\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i}),E)
, it is enough to prove that, for any
i
,
\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i}),E)=0
.

Let us fix
i_{0}\in I:=\{1,\dots,r-1\}
. Applying the functor
\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z_{i_{0}}}(D_{i_{0}}),-)
to (
29
) we get the long exact sequence

(30)

0\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z_{i_{0}}}(D_{i_{0}}),\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f))\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z_{i_{0}}}(D_{i_{0}}),E)\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z_{i_{0}}}(D_{i_{0}}),\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r-1}I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i}))

\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(I_{Z_{i_{0}}}(D_{i_{0}}),\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f))\rightarrow\cdots

On one hand, by ii),

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z_{i_{0}}}(D_{i_{0}}),\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f))&\cong\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f),I_{Z_{i_{0}}}(D_{i_{0}}+K_{X}))\\
&\cong\operatorname{H}^{2}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i_{0}}-\mathfrak{b}f+K_{X})\\
&\cong\operatorname{H}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f-D_{i_{0}})=0.\end{array}

On the other hand,

\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z_{i_{0}}}(D_{i_{0}}),\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r-1}I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i}))\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r-1}\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z_{i_{0}}}(D_{i_{0}}),I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i})).

If
i=i_{0}
, by Lemma
2.5
, we get

\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z_{i_{0}}}(D_{i_{0}}),I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i}))\cong\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z_{i_{0}}},I_{Z_{i_{0}}})\cong K.

Assume
i\neq i_{0}
. Applying the functor
\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z_{i_{0}}},-)
to the exact sequence

0\rightarrow I_{Z_{i}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Z_{i}}\rightarrow 0

twisted by
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i}-D_{i_{0}})
, we get

\dim\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z_{i_{0}}},I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i}-D_{i_{0}}))\leq\dim\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z_{i_{0}}},\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i}-D_{i_{0}})).

On the other hand,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\hom(I_{Z_{i_{0}}},\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i}-D_{i_{0}}))&=\mathrm{ext}^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i}-D_{i_{0}}),I_{Z_{i_{0}}}(K_{X}))$$$$\\
&=\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i}-D_{i_{0}})=0,\end{array}

where the last equality follows from assumption i).
Putting altogether,

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r-1}\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z_{i_{0}}}(D_{i_{0}}),I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i}))\cong K.

In addition,

\hom(I_{Z_{i_{0}}}(D_{i_{0}}),\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f))=\operatorname{h}^{2}(I_{Z_{i_{0}}}(D_{i_{0}}+K_{X}-\mathfrak{b}f))=\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f-D_{i_{0}})=0,

where the last inequality follows from ii).
Hence, the exact sequence (
30
) turns to be

0\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z_{i_{0}}},E)\rightarrow K\overset{\gamma_{i_{0}}}{\rightarrow}\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(I_{Z_{i_{0}}}(D_{i_{0}}),\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f))\rightarrow\cdots,

which means that

\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z_{i_{0}}},E)\cong\ker(\gamma_{i_{0}}).

By construction, the extension
e=(e_{1},\dots,e_{r-1})\in\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r-1}I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i}),\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f))
is defined by nontrivial extensions
e_{i}\in\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i}),\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f))
and
\gamma_{i_{0}}
is the map that sends
1\in K
to the nontrivial extension
e_{i_{0}}\in\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(I_{Z_{i_{0}}},\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f))
. Thus,
\gamma_{i_{0}}
is injective and hence

\mathrm{Hom}(I_{Z_{i_{0}}},E)\cong\ker(\gamma_{i_{0}})=0.

Consider the exact sequence (
29
) twisted by
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-\mathfrak{b}f)
and taking cohomology we get

0\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{0}E(-\mathfrak{b}f)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r-1}\operatorname{H}^{0}I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i}-\mathfrak{b}f)\rightarrow\cdots

Thus, by iv),

\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f),E)\cong\operatorname{H}^{0}E(-\mathfrak{b}f)\cong\operatorname{H}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}\cong K.

Therefore,
1\leq\dim\mathrm{Hom}(E,E)\leq 1
and hence
E
is simple.

Let us now see that
E
is prioritary.
Applying the functor
\mathrm{Hom}(E,-)
to the exact sequence (
29
) twisted by
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-f)
, we obtain

(31)

\cdots\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(E,\mathcal{O}_{X}((\mathfrak{b}-1)f))\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(E,E(-f))\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(E,\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r-1}I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i}-f))\rightarrow 0.

Claim 2:
\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(E,\mathcal{O}_{X}((\mathfrak{b}-1)f))=0
.

Proof of Claim 2:

By duality,
\mathrm{ext}^{2}(E,\mathcal{O}_{X}((\mathfrak{b}-1)f))=\operatorname{h}^{0}E(K_{X}-(\mathfrak{b}-1)f).

Twisting (
29
) by
\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}-(\mathfrak{b}-1)f)
and taking cohomology we get

0\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+f)\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{0}E(K_{X}-(\mathfrak{b}-1)f)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r-1}\operatorname{H}^{0}I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i}+K_{X}-(\mathfrak{b}-1)f)\rightarrow\cdots

By v),
\operatorname{H}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+f)=\operatorname{H}^{0}I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i}+K_{X}-(\mathfrak{b}-1)f)=0,
which implies

\operatorname{H}^{0}E(K_{X}-(\mathfrak{b}-1)f)=0.

Claim 3:
\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(E,\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r-1}I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i}-f))=0.

Proof of Claim 3:

Notice that

\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(E,\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r-1}I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i}-f))\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r-1}\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(E,I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i}-f)).

Then, it is sufficient to prove that, for a fixed
i_{0}\in I:=\{1,\dots,r-1\}
, we have that
\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(E,I_{Z_{i_{0}}}(D_{i_{0}}-f))=0
. Applying the functor
\mathrm{Hom}(-,I_{Z_{i_{0}}}(D_{i_{0}}-f))
to (
29
), we get

(32)

\cdots\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r-1}I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i}),I_{Z_{i_{0}}}(D_{i_{0}}-f))\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(E,I_{Z_{i_{0}}}(D_{i_{0}}-f))\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f),I_{Z_{i_{0}}}(D_{i_{0}}-f))\rightarrow 0

Let us first check that

\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r-1}I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i}),I_{Z_{i_{0}}}(D_{i_{0}}-f))=0.

Since for any
i_{0}
, we have the exact sequence

\cdots\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i}),I_{Z_{i_{0}}}(D_{i_{0}}-f))\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i}),I_{Z_{i_{0}}}(D_{i_{0}}-f))\rightarrow 0

and by iii)

\mathrm{ext}^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i}),I_{Z_{i_{0}}}(D_{i_{0}}-f))=\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i}-D_{i_{0}}+K_{X}+f)=0,

\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i}),I_{Z_{i_{0}}}(D_{i_{0}}-f))=0,

which implies that

\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r-1}I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i}),I_{Z_{i_{0}}}(D_{i_{0}}-f))=0.

Finally, by iv),

Putting altogether, by (

Theorem 5.2
.

Let

and

c_{1}(E)=c_{1}
and
c_{2}(E)=c_{2}
such that
\operatorname{h}^{0}E\geq k:=r-1+t-g

\mathfrak{b}\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)
be a divisor of degree

and consider

divisors

such that:

\begin{array}[]{ll}D_{1}&\equiv-(\sum_{j=2}^{r-1}j)C_{0}+(r-1+t)f,\\
D_{i}&\equiv iC_{0}+(r-i)f,\thinspace\thinspace\text{for}\thinspace 2\leq i\leq r-2\thinspace\thinspace\text{and}\\
D_{r-1}&\equiv(r-1+s)C_{0}-(b+\sum_{j=2}^{r-1}j)f.\end{array}

Let us define

\begin{array}[]{ll}f(b):=&r-2+\sum_{i=2}^{r-2}\max\{0,\chi\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f-D_{i}),(i+1)(r-i-b+1)\}+\sum_{i<j}D_{i}\cdot D_{j}\\
&+(\mathfrak{b}f)(\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}D_{i})+\max\{0,\chi\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f-D_{r-1})\}\end{array}

and notice that, since
c_{2}\gg 0
and
f(b)
is independent of
c_{2}
, we can assume
c_{2}>f(b)
. Let
Z_{i}
be a generic 0-dimensional subschemes of length

\begin{array}[]{ll}|Z_{i}|&=\max\{0,\chi\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f-D_{i}),(i+1)(r-i-b+1)\}+1,\thinspace\thinspace\text{for}\thinspace 2\leq i\leq r-2,\\
|Z_{r-1}|&=c_{2}-1-\sum_{i=2}^{r-2}|Z_{i}|-\sum_{i<j}D_{i}\cdot D_{j}-(\mathfrak{b}f)(\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}D_{i}),\end{array}

and satisfying
Z_{i}\cap Z_{j}=\emptyset
for
i\neq j
.

Notice that, since
c_{2}>f(b)
, we get by definition that

(33)

|Z_{r-1}|=c_{2}-1-\sum_{i=2}^{r-2}|Z_{i}|-\sum_{i<j}D_{i}\cdot D_{j}-(\mathfrak{b}f)(\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}D_{i})>

f(b)-1-\sum_{i=2}^{r-2}|Z_{i}|-\sum_{i<j}D_{i}\cdot D_{j}-(\mathfrak{b}f)(\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}D_{i})=\max\{0,\chi\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f-D_{r-1})\}.

Keeping the above notation, let us consider the family
\mathcal{F}
of rank
r
vector bundles
E
on
X
with
c_{1}(E)=c_{1}
and
c_{2}(E)=c_{2}
given by a nontrivial extension of type

(34)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f)\rightarrow E\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r-1}I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i})\rightarrow 0

where
Z_{1}\in X
is a point such that
Z_{1}\not\in Z_{i}
for any
2\leq i\leq r-1
.

Claim :
\mathcal{F}\neq\emptyset
.

Proof of the Claim :

First of all let us see that the dimension of the space of extensions of type (
34
) is positive. Notice that, for any
i
, we have that

\mathrm{ext}^{1}(I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i}),\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f))\geq-\chi I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i}+K_{X}-\mathfrak{b}f)=-\chi I_{Z_{i}}(\mathfrak{b}f-D_{i}).

Notice that, since
b<r-1+t
,
\mathfrak{b}f-D_{1}
and
D_{1}-\mathfrak{b}f+K_{X}
are non effective divisors. Therefore,

-\chi I_{Z_{1}}(\mathfrak{b}f-D_{1})=1-\chi\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f-D_{1})>0.

For
2\leq i\leq r-2,
by construction

-\chi I_{Z_{i}}(\mathfrak{b}f-D_{i})=|Z_{i}|-\chi\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f-D_{i})>0.

Finally, for
i=r-1
,

-\chi I_{Z_{r-1}}(\mathfrak{b}f-D_{i})=|Z_{r-1}|-\chi\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f-D_{i})>0,

where the last inequality follows from (
33
).

Therefore
\mathrm{ext}^{1}(I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i}),\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f))>0
for every
i
and hence the dimension of the space of extensions of type (
34
) is positive.

Let us now see that the couple
(\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i}-\mathfrak{b}f+K_{X}),Z_{i})
satisfies the Cayley-Bacharach property for every
i
.
First of all, since the divisor
D_{1}-\mathfrak{b}f+K_{X}
is non effective,

\operatorname{h}^{0}I_{Z_{1}}(D_{1}-\mathfrak{b}f+K_{X})=0.

For
2\leq i\leq r-2
, since
Z_{i}
is generic and

\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i}-\mathfrak{b}f+K_{X})<i(r-i-b+1)<|Z_{i}|,

we have
\operatorname{h}^{0}I_{Z_{i}}(D_{i}-\mathfrak{b}f+K_{X})=0
. Finally, since the divisor
D_{r-1}-\mathfrak{b}f+K_{X}
is non effective, we also have
\operatorname{h}^{0}I_{Z_{r-1}}(D_{r-1}-\mathfrak{b}f+K_{X})=0
. Therefore,
(\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i}-\mathfrak{b}f+K_{X}),Z_{i})
satisfies the Cayley-Bacharach property for every
i
and, by
[
10
, Proposition 2.2]
, this guaranties that
E
is a rank
r
vector bundle with
c_{1}(E)=sC_{0}+\mathfrak{t}f
and

c_{2}(E)=1+\sum_{i=2}^{r-1}|Z_{i}|+\sum_{i<j}D_{i}\cdot D_{j}+(\mathfrak{b}f)(\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}D_{i})=c_{2}.

Hence
\mathcal{F}
is non empty.

Notice that the divisors
D_{i}
verify all the assumptions of Lemma
5.1
. In fact, i)-iv) are straigthfoward and also v) and vi) for the cases
i=1
and
i=r-1
. For
2\leq i\leq r-2
, to see v) and vi) we have two possibilities. If
D_{i}-\mathfrak{b}f
and
K_{X}+f+D_{i}-\mathfrak{b}f
are non effective divisors, we directly get
\operatorname{h}^{0}I_{Z}(D_{i}-\mathfrak{b}f)=0
and
\operatorname{h}^{0}I_{Z}(K_{X}+f+D_{i}-\mathfrak{b}f)=0
. Otherwise we use the fact that
Z_{i}
is generic of length
|Z_{i}|>\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i}-\mathfrak{b}f+K_{X}+f)
and
|Z_{i}|>\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i}-\mathfrak{b}f)
since

\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i}-\mathfrak{b}f)\leq(i+1)(r-i-b+1)

and

\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i}-\mathfrak{b}f+K_{X}+f)\leq(i-1)(r-i-b-\overline{e}+2).

Hence,
E
is a simple prioritary vector bundle.

Moreover, since
b=r-2+t\geq\max\{2(g-1),0\}
, by (
34
) we get

\operatorname{h}^{0}E\geq\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f)=b+1-g=r-1+t-g.

∎

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
X
be a ruled surface over a non singular curve
C
of genus
g\geq 0
such that
\overline{e}:=e-2(g-1)<-1
. Fix
r\geq 4
and
c_{2}\gg 0
integers and
c_{1}=sC_{0}+\mathfrak{t}f\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)
with
0\leq s\leq r-1
and

t:=\deg(\mathfrak{t})>\max\{0,2g-2\}+\frac{[(r-1)(r-2)+2]\overline{e}}{2}.

Then there exists a rank
r
simple prioritary vector bundle
E
with
c_{1}(E)=c_{1}
and
c_{2}(E)=c_{2}
, such that
\operatorname{h}^{0}E\geq t-g-[\frac{(r-1)(r-2)+2}{2}]\overline{e}
.

Proof.

Since the proof is similar to the one of Theorem
5.2
, we highlight the main differences and left the details to the reader.
First of all let us consider
\mathfrak{b}\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)
of degree

b=t-1-[\frac{(r-1)(r-2)+2}{2}]\overline{e}

and define the following
r-1
divisors
D_{i}\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)

\begin{array}[]{ll}D_{i}&\equiv i(C_{0}+\overline{e})f,\thinspace\text{for}\thinspace\thinspace 1\leq i\leq r-2\\
D_{r-1}&=c_{1}-\mathfrak{b}f-\sum_{i=1}^{r-2}D_{i}\end{array}

and

f(b):=r-2+\sum_{i<j}D_{i}\cdot D_{j}+(\mathfrak{b}f)(\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}D_{i})+\max\{0,\chi\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f-D_{r-1}),0\}.

Notice that, since
c_{2}\gg 0
,

c_{2}>f(b)
. Let us consider
Z_{r-1}
a 0-dimensional subscheme of length

\begin{array}[]{ll}|Z_{r-1}|&=c_{2}-(r-2)-\sum_{i<j}D_{i}\cdot D_{j}-(\mathfrak{b}f)(\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}D_{i}).\end{array}

Notice that, since
c_{2}>f(b)
,

|Z_{r-1}|>\max\{0,\chi\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f-D_{r-1})\}.

Keeping the above notation, consider the family
\mathcal{F}
of rank
r
vector bundles
E
on
X
given by a nontrivial extension of type

(35)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f)\rightarrow E\rightarrow[\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r-2}I_{P_{i}}(D_{i})]\oplus I_{Z_{r-1}}(D_{r-1})\rightarrow 0,

\operatorname{h}^{0}E\geq\operatorname{h}^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathfrak{b}f)=b+1-g=t-g-[\frac{(r-1)(r-2)+2}{2}].

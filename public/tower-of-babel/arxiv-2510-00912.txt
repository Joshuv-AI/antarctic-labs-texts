Notions of Cauchy completeness for normed categories
Dirk Hofmann, Walter Tholen

Abstract
As already mentioned by Lawvere in his 1973 paper, the characterisation of Cauchy completeness of metric spaces in terms of representability of adjoint distributors amounts to the idempotent-split property of an ordinary category when the governing symmetric monoidal-closed category is changed from the extended real half-line to the category of sets. In this paper, for any commutative quantale 𝒱 \mathcal{V} , we extend these two characterisations of Lawvere-style completeness to 𝒱 \mathcal{V} -normed categories, thus replacing [ 0 , ∞ ] [0,\infty] and 𝖲𝖾𝗍 \mathsf{Set} more generally by the category 𝖲𝖾𝗍 / / 𝒱 \mathsf{Set}{/\!\!/}\mathcal{V} of 𝒱 \mathcal{V} -normed sets. We also establish improvements of recent results regarding the normed convergence of Cauchy sequences in two important 𝒱 \mathcal{V} -normed categories.

Introduction

The primary motivation for this paper stems from the following two achievements of Lawvere’s famous article
[
Lawvere, 1973
]
, which is widely known for the interpretation of (generalized) metric spaces as small categories enriched in the real half-line
[0,\infty]
:

•

The characterisation of Cauchy completeness of metric spaces as an instance of a categorical notion of completeness (in terms of the representabilty of adjoint distributors) for categories enriched in a symmetric monoidal-closed category
\mathcal{W}
which, for
\mathcal{W}=\mathsf{Set}
, amounts to the idempotent-split property of ordinary categories;

•

The suggestion of a notion of
[0,\infty]
-normed category as a category enriched in the symmetric monoidal-closed category
\mathcal{W}=\mathsf{Set}{/\!\!/}[0,\infty]
of
[0,\infty]
-normed sets, and the close interaction of such normed categories with metric spaces.

The juxtaposition of these two achievements naturally leads to the question of
how to characterize
[0,\infty]
-normed categories that are complete in Lawvere’s sense
? The principal result of this paper (Theorem
2.25
) anwers this question, even in the more general setting employed in the recent article
[
Clementino et al., 2025a
]
where
[0,\infty]
has been replaced by a commutative
quantale

\mathcal{V}
,
i.e.
, by any small and “thin” symmetric monoidal-closed category.

In order to characterise
Lawvere completeness
for a
\mathcal{V}
-normed category
\mathbb{A}
(that is: for a
\mathsf{Set}{/\!\!/}\mathcal{V}
-enriched category
\mathbb{A}
), one needs to carefully analyse the notion of adjoint
\mathsf{Set}{/\!\!/}\mathcal{V}
-distributor (or, in Lawvere’s terminology, of adjoint
\mathsf{Set}{/\!\!/}\mathcal{V}
-bimodule). Since a
\mathcal{V}
-normed category
\mathbb{A}
may be seen as an ordinary category carrying additional structure, it seemed natural to us trying to “lift” the proof of the characterisation of adjoint distributors as given by Borceux in
[
Borceux, 1994a
]
from the
\mathsf{Set}
- to the
\mathsf{Set}{/\!\!/}\mathcal{V}
-level. But when encountering some intricacies in the process we found it necessary to clarify, and be very precise about, various elements of enriched category theory, some of which don’t seem to be easily available in sufficient detail in the literature, in particular those concerning enriched distributors. We therefore decided to summarise these elements comprehensively in the introductory Section
1
and to illustrate them in the three cases of interest for this paper, namely when the enriching category
\mathcal{W}
is the category
\mathsf{Set}
, a quantale
\mathcal{V}
, or the category
\mathsf{Set}{/\!\!/}\mathcal{V}
of
\mathcal{V}
-normed sets.

With all needed elements of enriched category readily available, in the principal Section
2
of the paper we are then prepared to analyse Lawvere’s completeness notion for
\mathcal{W}
-categories in the three cases of interest. In presenting the largely known characterisations in the cases
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{W}=\mathsf{Set}
, following
[
Avery and Leinster, 2021
]
we found that the use of the
Isbell conjugation
helps shortening the proofs, and this applies also to the case of principal interest,
\mathcal{W}=\mathsf{Set}{/\!\!/}\mathcal{V}
. It may not be surprising that, for a
\mathcal{V}
-normed category
\mathbb{A}
to be Lawvere complete, it is necessary that the ordinary category
\mathbb{A}_{\circ}
of
\mathbb{A}
-morphism morphisms with norm at least
\mathrm{k}
(the tensor neutral element in
\mathcal{V}
) be so. Since a left adjoint distributor
\mathsf{E}{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}\mathbb{A}_{\circ}
(with
\mathsf{E}
the one-morphism category) must therefore be a retract of a representable functor (seen as a distributor), it seems natural to expect that the needed additional ingredient for Lawvere completeness of
\mathbb{A}
as a
\mathcal{V}
-normed category is the condition that the natural transformations involved in the presentation as a retract should comply in some way with the norm of
\mathbb{A}
. The specification of this compliance is described in Definition
2.18
and then leads to Theorem
2.25
which clarifies our initial vague expectation.

Unlike the self-dual notion of Lawvere completeness (see Remark
2.4
), the notion of
normed colimit
of a sequence in a
\mathcal{V}
-normed category as introduced in
[
Clementino et al., 2025a
]
(in generalization of the notion of
forward limit
of a generalized metric space as in
[
Bonsangue et al., 1998
]
) is clearly not invariant under dualization. In Section
3
we briefly revisit the resulting notion of
Cauchy cocompleteness
of a
\mathcal{V}
-normed category in order to improve upon on a couple of results of
[
Clementino et al., 2025a
]
. Expanding further on the note
[
Clementino et al., 2025b
]
, but without imposing any additional condition on
\mathcal{V}
, we show that the
\mathcal{V}
-normed category
\mathsf{Set}{|\!|}\mathcal{V}
of
\mathcal{V}
-normed sets and arbitrary maps as morphisms is Cauchy cocomplete (Theorem
3.4
). And under a modest restriction on the quantale that already appeared in
[
Clementino et al., 2025a
]
we provide an appropriate
\mathcal{V}
-generalization of the fact that the normed category of generalized metric spaces with arbitrary maps is Cauchy cocomplete as well (Theorem
3.7
).

Finally we note that the notion of Lawvere completeness is intrinsically double-categorical as it relates adjoint “loose” arrows (distributors) and “strict” arrows (functors). A natural extension of the notion of Lawvere completeness to double categories is introduced in
[
Paré, 2021
]
and further studied in
[
Niefield, 2025
]
.

Example 1.2
.

For
\mathcal{W}=\mathsf{Set}
, we have

Example 1.3
.

We consider now the case that
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{V}
is a quantale. Since
\mathcal{V}
is a thin category, all coherence conditions are automatically satisfied, moreover, all structure arrows become properties. Therefore a
\mathcal{V}
-category

consists of a set

and a function

\mathcal{V}
-functor

. Finally, for
\mathcal{V}

-natural transformation

For

[

]
).

Proposition 1.5
.

For each quantale
\mathcal{V}
, the forgetful functor

\mO\colon\mathsf{Set}{/\!\!/}\mathcal{V}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Set}

is symmetric strict monoidal and topological.

For the notion of topological functor we refer to
[
Hofmann et al., 2014
]
, for instance. We recall from
[
Clementino et al., 2025a
]
that, for a family
(f_{i}\colon A\to B_{i})_{i\in I}
of maps where each

is normed, the
initial structure
on

is given by

|a|=\bigwedge_{i\in I}|f_{i}(a)|;

likewise, for a family
(g_{i}\colon A_{i}\to B)_{i\in I}
where each
A_{i}
is normed, the
final structure
on

is given by

|b|=\bigvee\{|a|\mid a\in A_{i},\,g_{i}(a)=b,i\in I\}.

The strict monoidal functor

induces the 2-functors

which forget the norm on each hom-set. Moreover, both functors are strict monoidal: for normed categories
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
,

{\mO}^{\mathrm{fun}}(\mathbb{E})=\mathsf{E}\quad\text{and}\quad{\mO}^{\mathrm{fun}}(\mathbb{A}\otimes\mathbb{B})={\mO}^{\mathrm{fun}}(\mathbb{A})\times{\mO}^{\mathrm{fun}}(\mathbb{B}).

Following

, we also consider the representable 2-functors

(-)_{\circ}:=(\mathsf{Cat}{/\!\!/}\mathcal{V})(\mathbb{E},-)\colon\mathsf{Cat}{/\!\!/}\mathcal{V}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Cat}\quad\text{and}\quad(-)_{\circ}:=(\mathsf{CAT}{/\!\!/}\mathcal{V})(\mathbb{E},-)\colon\mathsf{CAT}{/\!\!/}\mathcal{V}\longrightarrow\mathsf{CAT}

which send a
\mathcal{V}
-normed category
\mathbb{A}
to the category
\mathbb{A}_{\circ}
with the same objects as
\mathbb{A}
, but with only those morphisms

in
\mathbb{A}
with
\mathrm{k}\leq|f|
. We note that

is induced by the symmetric lax monoidal functor

\mathsf{Set}{/\!\!/}\mathcal{V}(E,-)\colon\mathsf{Set}{/\!\!/}\mathcal{V}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Set}

which sends the
\mathcal{V}
-normed set

to the set

\{a\in A\mid\mathrm{k}\leq|a|\}

and
\mathcal{V}
-normed maps to the corresponding restrictions.

\mathcal{W}
-functor

. A family
(\gamma_{A}\colon T(A,A)\to K)_{A}
, with

-natural

2
Completeness
à la
Lawvere

Throughout this section
\mathcal{W}
denotes a closed symmetric monoidal complete and cocomplete category.

2.1
Lawvere completeness for enriched categories

We start by recalling the well-known key property of the
\mathcal{W}
-distributors induced by a
\mathcal{W}
-functor.

Proposition 2.1
.

For every
\mathcal{W}
-functor
F\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
between small
\mathcal{W}
-categories,
F_{*}\dashv F^{*}
in
\mathcal{W}\text{-}\mathsf{Dist}
.

The unit and the counit of
F_{*}\dashv F^{*}
have components

\mathcal{A}(A,A^{\prime})\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}(FA,FA^{\prime})\cong F^{*}\cdot F_{*}(A,A^{\prime})

and

\displaystyle F_{*}\cdot F^{*}(B,B^{\prime})

\displaystyle\cong\int^{A\in\ob\mathcal{A}}F_{*}(A,B^{\prime})\otimes F^{*}(B,A)

\displaystyle\cong\int^{A\in\ob\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{B}(FA,B^{\prime})\otimes\mathcal{B}(B,FA)\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}(B,B^{\prime}),

respectively.

Definition 2.2
.

A small
\mathcal{W}
-category
\mathcal{A}
is called
Lawvere complete
whenever every left adjoint
\mathcal{W}
-distributor
\Phi\colon\mathcal{X}{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}\mathcal{A}
in
\mathcal{W}\text{-}\mathsf{Dist}
is representable, that is, whenever there exists a
\mathcal{W}
-functor
F\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{A}
so that
\Phi\cong F_{*}
.

We note that, if
\Phi\cong F_{*}
and
\Phi\dashv\Psi
in
\mathcal{W}\text{-}\mathsf{Dist}
, then also
\Psi\cong F^{*}
by Proposition
2.1
.

Remark 2.3
.

It suffices to consider left adjoints
\Phi\colon\mathcal{E}{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}\mathcal{A}
in the definition above. Also recall that an adjunction
\Phi\dashv\Psi
in
\mathcal{W}\text{-}\mathsf{Dist}
with
\Psi\colon\mathcal{A}{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}\mathcal{E}
is given by units
\eta
and
\varepsilon

\displaystyle E=\mathcal{E}(\star,\star)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447pt\eta=\eta_{\star}\hskip 8.19447pt}\int^{A\in\ob\mathcal{A}}\Psi(A)\otimes\Phi(A)=\Psi\cdot\Phi(\star,\star),

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(A,B)

\displaystyle\xleftarrow{\hskip 8.19447pt\varepsilon_{A,B}\hskip 8.19447pt}\Phi(B)\otimes\Psi(A)=\Phi\cdot\Psi(A,B),

subject to the usual equations. In particular, for
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{V}
a quantale and
(\varphi\colon E{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}A)\dashv(\psi\colon A{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}E)
in
\mathcal{V}\text{-}\mathsf{Dist}
, the unit and the counit are given by the inequalities

\mathrm{k}\leq\bigvee\{\psi(c)\otimes\varphi(c)\mid c\in A\}\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi(b)\otimes\psi(a)\leq X(a,b)\qquad(a,b\in A),

(2.i)

respectively. Also note that we may identify any
\mathcal{W}
-functor
\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{A}
with the image
A
of the unique object of
\mathcal{E}
, and we write
A_{*}\colon\mathcal{E}{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}\mathcal{A}
and
A^{*}\colon\mathcal{A}{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}\mathcal{E}
for the induced
\mathcal{W}
-distributors. The left adjoint
\Phi\colon\mathcal{E}{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}\mathcal{A}
(and the adjunction
\Phi\dashv\Psi
) is representable if and only if there exists an object
A
in
\mathcal{A}
with
A_{*}\cong\Phi
(and necessarily
A^{*}\cong\Psi
).

Remark 2.4
.

The notion of Lawvere completeness is self dual in the sense that
\mathcal{A}
is Lawvere complete if and only if
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}
is Lawvere complete. One way to see this is to observe that, by Remark
1.11
, the passage
\mathcal{A}\mapsto\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}
extends to a pseudofunctor
(-)^{\mathrm{op}}\colon\mathcal{W}\text{-}\mathsf{Dist}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{W}\text{-}\mathsf{Dist}
which sends
\Phi\colon\mathcal{A}{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}\mathcal{B}
to
\Phi^{\mathrm{op}}\colon\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{op}}{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}
defined as the composite

(\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{op}})^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes\mathcal{}A^{\mathrm{op}}=\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes\mathcal{B}\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447pt\Phi\hskip 8.19447pt}\mathcal{W},

and that
(\Phi^{\mathrm{op}})^{\mathrm{op}}=\Phi
. The pseudofunctor
(-)^{\mathrm{op}}
sends the adjunction
(\Phi\colon\mathcal{E}{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}\mathcal{A})\dashv(\Psi\colon\mathcal{A}{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}\mathcal{E})
to the adjunction
(\Psi^{\mathrm{op}}\colon\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{op}}{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}})\dashv(\Phi^{\mathrm{op}}\colon\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}\mathcal{E})
, hence left adjoint distributors
\mathcal{E}{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}\mathcal{A}
are in bijection with left adjoint distributors
\mathcal{E}{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}
. Furthermore, for every
\mathcal{W}
-functor
F\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
,
(F_{*})^{\mathrm{op}}=(F^{\mathrm{op}})^{*}
and
(F^{*})^{\mathrm{op}}=(F^{\mathrm{op}})_{*}
. Therefore every left adjoint distributor
\mathcal{E}{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}\mathcal{A}
is representable if and only if every left adjoint distributor
\mathcal{E}{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}
is representable.

Remark 2.5
.

Alternatively, Lawvere completeness of a small
\mathcal{W}
-category
\mathcal{A}
may be equivalently described as cocompleteness with respect to the class of all right adjoint weights
\Psi\colon\mathcal{A}{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}\mathcal{E}
(see
[
Street, 1983
;
Borceux and Dejean, 1986
]
). With this description as the definition of Lawvere completeness for a general (not necessarily small)
\mathcal{W}
-category
\mathcal{X}
, we conclude that every cocomplete
\mathcal{W}
-category
\mathcal{X}
is Lawvere complete; hence, in particular, the
\mathcal{W}
-category
\mathcal{W}
is Lawvere complete.

2.2
Quantale-enriched categories

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a quantale. A left adjoint
\mathcal{V}
-distributor
\varphi\colon E{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}A
with right adjoint
\psi\colon A{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}E
is representable if and only if there exists an element
a\in A
with
\mathrm{k}\leq\varphi(a)
and
\mathrm{k}\leq\psi(a)
.

Proof.

A representing element
a\in A
for
\varphi\dashv\psi
must clearly satisfy
\mathrm{k}\leq\varphi(a)
and
\mathrm{k}\leq\psi(a)
. Conversely, by the Yoneda lemma, the conditions
\mathrm{k}\leq\varphi(a)
and
\mathrm{k}\leq\psi(a)
imply
a_{*}\leq\varphi
and
a^{*}\leq\psi
. With the Isbell adjunction one obtains
a_{*}={a^{*}}^{\vee}\geq\psi^{\vee}=\varphi
, hence
\varphi=a_{*}
.
∎

Example 2.7
.

Consider the quantale
\mathcal{V}=\textit{2}
and an adjunction
(\varphi\colon E{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}A)\dashv(\psi\colon A{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}E)
. The first inequality of (
2.i
) tells us that there exists
c\in A
so that
1=\varphi(c)
and
1=\psi(c)
, that is, that
\varphi\colon E{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}A
is representable. Since
\mathcal{V}\text{-}\mathsf{Cat}=\mathsf{Ord}
, it follows that every ordered set is Lawvere complete. More generally, if the unit
\mathrm{k}
is
totally compact
in any quantale
\mathcal{V}
(meaning that
\mathrm{k}\leq\bigvee S
implies
\mathrm{k}\leq s
for some
s\in S
, for all
S\subseteq\mathcal{V}
) and satisfies
k\leq u\otimes v
only if
\mathrm{k}\leq u
and
\mathrm{k}\leq v
, for all
u,v\in\mathcal{V}
, then every
\mathcal{V}
-category is Lawvere complete.

Example 2.8
.

A set, viewed as a
1
-category, is Lawvere complete if and only if it is non-empty.

Example 2.9
.

We consider now the quantale
\mathcal{V}=[0,\infty]
with addition and unit element
0
, ordered by
\geq
, so that
\mathcal{V}\text{-}\mathsf{Cat}=\mathsf{Met}
(see Example
1.3
). As observed in
[
Lawvere, 1973
]
, a metric space is Lawvere complete if and only if it is Cauchy complete in the classic sense that every Cauchy sequence converges.

As usual, for a (generalised) metric space
X
we consider the induced (symmetric) topology where, for
x\in X
and
A\subseteq X
,

x\in\overline{A}\iff 0=\inf\{X(x,a)+X(a,x)\mid a\in A\}.

Note that
X
and
X^{\mathrm{op}}
induce the same topology on the set
X
, and the topology induced by
[0,\infty]
is the usual Euclidean topology: for
u\in[0,\infty]
and
S\subseteq[0,\infty]
,

u\in\overline{S}\iff 0=\inf\{|v-u|\mid v\in S\}.

For the proof, assume first that
X
is Cauchy complete. Let
(\varphi\colon E{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}X)\dashv(\psi\colon X{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}E)
be an adjunction in
[0,\infty]\text{-}\mathsf{Dist}
. We note that both maps
\varphi\colon X\to[0,\infty]
and
\psi\colon X\to[0,\infty]
are continuous, therefore also the map

\sigma\colon X\longrightarrow[0,\infty],\quad x\longmapsto\varphi(x)+\psi(x)

is continuous. By (
2.i
), for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we can choose some
x_{n}\in X
so that
\sigma(x_{n})=\varphi(x_{n})+\psi(x_{n})\leq\frac{1}{n}
, and the sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is Cauchy since

X(x_{n},x_{m})\leq\varphi(x_{m})+\psi(x_{n})\leq\sigma(x_{m})+\sigma(x_{n})\leq\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{n},

for all
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
X
is Cauchy complete,
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to some
x\in X
, therefore
(\sigma(x_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
\sigma(x)
, hence
0=\sigma(x)=\varphi(x)+\psi(x)
. Consequently,
\varphi(x)=0
and
\psi(x)=0
, by Proposition
2.6
, the adjunction
\varphi\dashv\psi
is representable.

Conversely, assume now that
X
is Lawvere complete. We recall from
[
Hofmann and Tholen, 2010
]
that, for the Yoneda embedding
y_{X}\colon X\to[X^{\mathrm{op}},[0,\infty]]
,

\overline{y_{X}(X)}=\{\psi\in[X^{\mathrm{op}},[0,\infty]]\mid\psi\colon X{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}E\text{ is right adjoint}\}.

Since
X
is Lawvere complete,
y_{X}(X)=\overline{y_{X}(X)}
is a closed subset of the Cauchy complete space
[X^{\mathrm{op}},[0,\infty]]
, therefore
y_{X}(X)
is Cauchy complete and, since
X\cong y_{X}(X)
, also
X
is Cauchy complete.

b\in\ob\mathbb{B}
.

For a
\mathcal{V}
-category

, consider the
\mathcal{V}
-normed category

and an adjunction

; therefore, by Lemma

x
in
\mathbb{X}
and split epimorphic natural transformations

Lemma 2.15
.

Let

\mathcal{V}
-category and consider the

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
\Phi\colon\mathbb{E}{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}\mathbb{A}
in
\mathsf{Dist}{/\!\!/}\mathcal{V}
. Consider an object
a
of
\mathbb{A}
and (not necessarily
\mathcal{V}
-normed) natural transformations

(2.iii)

here
\alpha
corresponds to an element
u\in\Phi(a)
and
\beta^{\vee}\colon\mathsf{A}(-,a)\to\Phi^{\vee}
to
v=\beta\in\Phi^{\vee}(a)
. Then the following assertions are equivalent,

(i)

\Phi\dashv\Phi^{\vee}
in
\mathsf{Dist}{/\!\!/}\mathcal{V}
with unit
\eta=[(v,u)]
where
\mathrm{k}\leq|u|
and
\mathrm{k}\leq|v|
,

(ii)

the natural transformations
\alpha
and
\beta
are
\mathcal{V}
-normed and
\alpha\beta=1_{\Phi}
in
\mathsf{Dist}{/\!\!/}\mathcal{V}

Proof.

Assume first
(i)
. Then
\mathrm{k}\leq|u|=|\alpha|
and
\mathrm{k}\leq|\beta|
, that is,
\alpha
and
\beta
are
\mathcal{V}
-normed natural transformations. Therefore,
(ii)
follows with Lemma

. Assume now

\eta
is a
\mathcal{V}

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{b,c}\colon\Phi(c)\otimes\Phi^{\vee}(b)

\displaystyle(w_{1},w_{2})

Definition 2.18
.

We say that
\Phi\colon\mathbb{E}{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}\mathbb{A}

\mathcal{V}
-normed retract of a representable distributor

a
of
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathcal{V}
-normed natural transformations

We say that a left adjoint
\mathcal{V}
-normed distributor

in
\mathbb{A}
and elements

and

of the adjunction

and
\mathrm{k}\leq|v|

By the uniqueness of adjoints in bicategories, given adjunctions
(\Phi\colon\mathbb{E}{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}\mathbb{A})\dashv(\Psi\colon\mathbb{A}{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\;\;}_{\;\;}}\mathbb{E})

3
Completeness
à la
Cauchy

3.1
Normed convergence

In the previous section we have seen how the classic metric notion of “Cauchy completeness” relates in a metric space to the categorical question of representability of distributors. We stress that this notion is intrinsically symmetric: a sequence
s
in a metric space
X
is Cauchy if and only if it is Cauchy in
X
if and only if it is Cauchy in the symmetrisation of
X
, and similarly,
s
has a limit in
X
if and only if it has a limit in
X^{\mathrm{op}}
if and only if it has a limit in the symmetrisation of
X
. A non-symmetric notion of “Cauchy completeness” was introduced by
Bonsangue et al., 1998
: a sequence
s=(x_{n})
in a metric space
X
is
(forward) Cauchy
whenever

\inf_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{n\geq m\geq N}X(x_{m},x_{n})=0,

and an element
x\in X
is a
(forward) limit
of
s
whenever

X(x,y)=\inf_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{n\geq N}X(x_{n},y),

for all
y\in X
. Then a metric space
X
is
complete
if every Cauchy sequence in
X
has a limit.

For normed categories, (limits of) Cauchy sequences of arrows were considered in
[
Kubiś, 2017
;
Neeman, 2020
]
. A revised notion and theory of normed convergence in the context of
\mathcal{V}
-normed categories was developed in
[
Clementino et al., 2025a
]
, which we recall first. We consider here the ordered set
\mathbb{N}
as a category, discretely
\mathcal{V}
-normed with constant value
\bot
for all non-identical arrows. This way a
\mathcal{V}
-normed functor
\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{X}
can be written as a sequence
s=(x_{m}\xrightarrow{s_{m,n}}x_{n})_{m\leq n\in\mathbb{N}}
of arrows in
\mathbb{X}
where, for all
m\leq n\leq l
in
\mathbb{N}
,
s_{m,m}=1_{x_{m}}
and
s_{m,l}=s_{n,l}\cdot s_{m,n}
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
s=(x_{m}\xrightarrow{s_{m,n}}x_{n})_{m\leq n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in a
\mathcal{V}
-normed category
\mathbb{X}
. An object
x
together with a cocone
\gamma=(x_{n}\xrightarrow{\gamma_{n}}x)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a
normed colimit
of
s
in
\mathbb{X}
if

(C1)

\gamma\colon s\to\Delta x
is a colimit cocone in the ordinary category
\mathbb{X}
, and

(C2)

for all objects
y
in
\mathbb{X}
, the canonical
\mathsf{Set}
-bijections

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Nat}(s_{|N},\Delta y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{X}(x,y),}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(f\cdot\gamma_{n})_{n\geq N}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(N\in\mathbb{N})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\kappa_{N}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\kappa_{N}^{-1}}

form a colimit cocone in
\mathsf{Set}{/\!\!/}\mathcal{V}
, where
s_{|N}
is the restriction of
s
to
\uparrow\!N=\{N,N+1,\dots\}
and
\mathrm{Nat}(s_{|N},\Delta y)=[\uparrow\!N,\mathbb{X}](s_{|N},\Delta y)\;(N\in\mathbb{N})
is considered as a sequence in
\mathsf{Set}{/\!\!/}\mathcal{V}
, with all connecting maps given by restriction. Also note that a colimit
x
of
s
in the ordinary category
\mathbb{X}
is also a colimit of every restricted sequence
s_{|N}
.

We also recall from
[
Clementino et al., 2025a
]
the following characterisation of normed colimits.

Corollary 3.2
.

An object
x
with a cocone
\gamma\colon s\to\Delta x
is a normed colimit of a sequence
s
in a
\mathcal{V}
-normed category
\mathbb{X}
if and only if
\gamma
is a colimit cocone in the ordinary category
\mathbb{X}
such that

(C2a)

\mathrm{k}\leq\bigvee_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\bigwedge_{n\geq N}|\gamma_{n}|
,

(C2b)

|f|\geq\bigvee_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\bigwedge_{n\geq N}|f\cdot\gamma_{n}|
, for every morphism
f\colon x\to y
in
\mathbb{X}
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a quantale and
\mathbb{X}
be a
\mathcal{V}
-normed category. A sequence
s=(x_{m}\xrightarrow{s_{m,n}}x_{n})_{m\leq n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
(forward) Cauchy
whenever
\mathrm{k}\leq\bigvee_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\bigwedge_{N\leq m\leq n}|s_{m,n}|
. The
\mathcal{V}
-normed category
\mathbb{X}
is
Cauchy cocomplete
whenever every Cauchy sequence has a normed colimit in
\mathbb{X}
.

3.2
Examples of Cauchy cocomplete normed categories

Several
\mathcal{V}
-normed categories were shown to be Cauchy cocomplete in
[
Clementino et al., 2025a
]
, in particular, the presheaf categories
[\mathbb{X},\mathsf{Set}{|\!|}\mathcal{V}]
are Cauchy cocomplete
under certain conditions on
\mathcal{V}
. Our first result shows that, at least for
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{E}
, this result holds for all quantales
\mathcal{V}
.

Theorem 3.4
.

For every quantale
\mathcal{V}
, the
\mathcal{V}
-normed category
\mathsf{Set}{|\!|}\mathcal{V}
is Cauchy cocomplete.

Proof.

The proof proceeds as in the second part of the proof of
[
Clementino et al., 2025a
, Theorem 7.1]
. Consider a Cauchy sequence
s=(A_{m}\xrightarrow{s_{m,n}}A_{n})_{m\leq n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathsf{Set}{|\!|}\mathcal{V}
, hence,
\mathrm{k}\leq\bigvee_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\bigwedge_{n\geq m\geq N}|s_{m,n}|
. Take the colimit
(\gamma_{n}\colon A_{n}\to A)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathsf{Set}
and structure
A
by

|a|=\bigwedge_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\Phi_{a}(N),\qquad\text{where }\Phi_{a}(N)=\bigvee_{n\geq N}\bigvee_{a^{\prime}\in\gamma_{n}^{-1}(a)}|a^{\prime}|,

for all
a\in A
. Note that, since
\Phi_{a}
is decreasing in
N
, one has
a=\bigwedge_{N\geq K}\Phi_{a}(N)
for every
K\in\mathbb{N}
. Also note that, for every
n\geq K
in
\mathbb{N}
and every
a\in A_{K}
, one has
|s_{K,n}|\otimes|a|\leq|s_{K,n}(a)|
. To see
(C2a)
, we show first that, for all
K\in\mathbb{N}
,

\bigwedge_{n\geq K}|s_{K,n}|\leq|\gamma_{K}|.

Take
a\in A_{K}
, then

\displaystyle|a|\otimes\bigwedge_{n\geq K}|s_{K,n}|

\displaystyle\leq|a|\otimes\bigwedge_{m\geq K}\bigvee_{n\geq m}|s_{K,n}|

\displaystyle\leq\bigwedge_{m\geq K}\bigvee_{n\geq m}\underbrace{|s_{K,n}|\otimes|a|}_{\leq|s_{K,n}(a)|}

\displaystyle\leq\bigwedge_{m\geq K}\Phi_{\gamma_{K}(a)}(m)=|\gamma_{K}(a)|.

Therefore

\bigwedge_{n\geq K}|s_{K,n}|\leq\bigwedge_{a\in A_{K}}[|a|,|\gamma_{K}(a)|]=|\gamma_{K}|.

Finally,

\mathrm{k}\leq\bigvee_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\bigwedge_{K\geq N}\bigwedge_{n\geq K}|s_{K,n}|\leq\bigvee_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\bigwedge_{K\geq N}|\gamma_{K}|,

which proves
(C2a)
. Regarding
(C2b)
, let
\varphi\colon A\to B
in
\mathsf{Set}{|\!|}\mathcal{V}
,
a\in A
and
K\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\displaystyle\Big(\bigwedge_{n\geq K}|\varphi\cdot\gamma_{n}|\Big)\otimes\Big(\bigwedge_{N\geq K}\Phi_{a}(N)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\bigwedge_{N\geq K}\Big(\Big(\bigwedge_{n\geq K}|\varphi\cdot\gamma_{n}|\Big)\otimes\Big(\bigvee_{m\geq N}\bigvee_{a^{\prime}\in\gamma_{m}^{-1}(a)}|a^{\prime}|\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\bigwedge_{N\geq K}\bigvee_{m\geq N}\bigvee_{a^{\prime}\in\gamma_{m}^{-1}(a)}\Big(\Big(\bigwedge_{n\geq K}|\varphi\cdot\gamma_{n}|\Big)\otimes|a^{\prime}|\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\bigwedge_{N\geq K}\bigvee_{m\geq N}\bigvee_{a^{\prime}\in\gamma_{m}^{-1}(a)}\bigwedge_{n\geq K}(|\varphi\cdot\gamma_{n}|\otimes|a^{\prime}|)

\displaystyle\leq\bigwedge_{N\geq K}\bigvee_{m\geq N}\bigvee_{a^{\prime}\in\gamma_{m}^{-1}(a)}(|\varphi\cdot\gamma_{m}|\otimes|a^{\prime}|)

\displaystyle\leq\bigwedge_{N\geq K}\bigvee_{m\geq N}\bigvee_{a^{\prime}\in\gamma_{m}^{-1}(a)}|\varphi\cdot\gamma_{m}(a^{\prime})|\leq|\varphi(a)|.

Therefore

\bigwedge_{n\geq K}|\varphi\cdot\gamma_{n}|\leq\bigwedge_{a\in A}[|a|,\varphi(a)|]=|\varphi|,

which proves
(C2b)
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

We note that
\mathsf{Set}{|\!|}\mathcal{V}
is also Lawvere complete, by Remark
2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{V}\text{-}\mathsf{DSet}
be the normed category of “
\mathcal{V}
-distance sets”:
\mathcal{V}\text{-}\mathsf{DSet}
has as objects sets
X
equipped with an arbitrary (distance) function
X(-,-)\colon X\times X\to\mathcal{V}
, and as morphisms maps
\varphi\colon X\to Y
with norm

|\varphi|=\bigwedge_{x,x^{\prime}\in X}[X(x,x^{\prime}),Y(\varphi x,\varphi x)].

We note that there is a canonical norm-preserving functor

D\colon\mathcal{V}\text{-}\mathsf{DSet}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Set}{|\!|}\mathcal{V}

sending
X
to
X\times X
.

Proposition 3.6
.

For every quantale
\mathcal{V}
, the normed category
\mathcal{V}\text{-}\mathsf{DSet}
is Cauchy cocomplete.

Proof.

Let
s=(X_{m}\xrightarrow{s_{m,n}}X_{n})_{m\leq n}
be a Cauchy sequence in
\mathcal{V}\text{-}\mathsf{Cat}
. Then
Ds=(X_{m}\times X_{m}\xrightarrow{s_{m,n}\times s_{m,n}}X_{n}\times X_{n})_{m\leq n}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathsf{Set}{|\!|}\mathcal{V}
and, by Theorem
3.4
, has a
\mathcal{V}
-normed colimit in
\mathsf{Set}{|\!|}\mathcal{V}
. Since directed colimits commute with finite products in
\mathsf{Set}
, this colimit must have the form

(X_{n}\times X_{n}\xrightarrow{\;\gamma_{n}\times\gamma_{n}\;}X\times X)_{n\in\mathbb{N}},

where
(\gamma_{n}\colon X_{n}\to X)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a colimit in
\mathsf{Set}
. Then Conditions
(C2a)
and
(C2b)
hold for
\gamma
since they hold for
D\gamma
.
∎

\mathcal{V}_{\otimes}\text{-}\mathsf{Lip}_{\odot}

. We simply write
\mathcal{V}\text{-}\mathsf{Lip}
if both quantale structures coincide. We recall that, for
u,v\in\mathcal{V}
,
u
is
totally below

v
, in symbols:
u\lll v
, whenever, for all
S\subseteq\mathcal{V}
,
v\leq S
implies
u\leq s
for some
s\in S
. Moreover,
v
is
approximated from totally below
whenever
v=\bigvee\{u\in\mathcal{V}\mid u\lll v\}
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Assume that the unit element
e
of
\mathcal{V}_{\odot}
is approximated from totally below. Then
\mathcal{V}_{\otimes}\text{-}\mathsf{Lip}_{\odot}
is Cauchy cocomplete.

Proof.

We show that
\mathcal{V}_{\otimes}\text{-}\mathsf{Lip}_{\odot}
, seen as a full subcategory of
\mathcal{V}_{\odot}\text{-}\mathsf{DSet}
, is closed under the formation of normed colimits of Cauchy sequences in
\mathcal{V}_{\otimes}\text{-}\mathsf{Lip}_{\odot}
. To this end, let
s=(X_{m}\xrightarrow{s_{m,n}}X_{n})
be a Cauchy sequence in
\mathcal{V}_{\otimes}\text{-}\mathsf{Lip}_{\odot}
, hence
e\leq\bigvee_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\bigwedge_{n\geq m\in\mathbb{N}}|s_{m,n}|
. Let
(\gamma_{n}\colon X_{n}\to X)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a normed colimit of
s
in
\mathcal{V}_{\odot}\text{-}\mathsf{DSet}
, in particular, for all
x,y\in X
and any
K\in\mathbb{N}
,

X(x,y)=\bigwedge_{N\geq K}\Phi_{x,y}(N),\quad\Phi_{x,y}(N)=\bigvee_{n\geq N}\bigvee_{x^{\prime}\in\gamma_{n}^{-1}(x)}\bigvee_{y^{\prime}\in\gamma_{n}^{-1}(y)}X_{n}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}).

Clearly,
e\leq X(x,x)
for all
x\in X
. Let now
x,y,z\in X
and let
\varepsilon,\eta\lll e
. Since
s
is Cauchy, we can find
K\in\mathbb{N}
so that, for all
n\geq m\geq K
,
\max\{\varepsilon,\eta\}\leq|s_{m,n}|
. Firstly,

\displaystyle(\varepsilon\odot X(x,y))\otimes(\eta\odot X(y,z))

\displaystyle=\Big(\varepsilon\odot\bigwedge_{N\geq K}\Phi_{x,y}(N)\Big)\otimes\Big(\eta\odot\bigwedge_{M\geq K}\Phi_{y,z}(M)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\bigwedge_{N\geq K}\big(\varepsilon\odot\Phi_{x,y}(N)\big)\otimes\big(\eta\odot\Phi_{y,z}(N)\big)

\displaystyle=\bigwedge_{N\geq K}\bigvee_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq N,\\
m\geq N\end{subarray}}\bigvee_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma_{n}(x^{\prime})=x,\\
\gamma_{n}(y^{\prime})=y\end{subarray}}\bigvee_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma_{m}(y^{\prime\prime})=y,\\
\gamma_{m}(z^{\prime})=z\end{subarray}}\big(\varepsilon\odot X(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\big)\otimes\big(\eta\odot X(y^{\prime\prime},z^{\prime})\big).

Let now
N\geq K
,
m,n\geq N
and
x^{\prime},y^{\prime},y^{\prime\prime},z^{\prime}\in X
with
\gamma_{n}(x^{\prime})=x
,
\gamma_{n}(y^{\prime})=y
,
\gamma_{m}(y^{\prime})=y
and
\gamma_{m}(z^{\prime})=z
. Since
\gamma_{n}(y^{\prime})=\gamma_{m}(y^{\prime\prime})
, there is some
l\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\leq l
,
m\leq l
and
s_{n,l}(y^{\prime})=s_{m,l}(y^{\prime\prime})
. Then we get

\displaystyle\big(\varepsilon\odot X(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\big)\otimes\big(\eta\odot X(y^{\prime\prime},z^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle\leq\big(|s_{n,l}|\odot X(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\big)\otimes\big(|s_{m,l}|\odot X(y^{\prime\prime},z^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle\leq X_{l}(s_{n,l}(x^{\prime}),s_{n,l}(y^{\prime}))\otimes X_{l}(s_{m,l}(y^{\prime\prime}),s_{m,l}(z^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\leq X_{l}(s_{n,l}(x^{\prime}),s_{m,l}(z^{\prime}))\leq\Phi_{x,z}(N).

We conclude that

X(x,y)\otimes X(y,z)=\bigvee_{\varepsilon\lll e}(\varepsilon\odot X(x,y))\otimes\bigvee_{\eta\lll e}(\eta\odot X(y,z))\\
\leq X(x,z).\qed

Remark 3.8
.

A normed colimit in
\mathcal{V}_{\otimes}\text{-}\mathsf{Lip}_{\odot}
of a Cauchy sequence of symmetric
\mathcal{V}_{\otimes}
-categories is symmetric.

As a corollary, choosing
\odot=\otimes
and
e=\mathrm{k}
, we obtain
[
Clementino et al., 2025a
, Theorem 7.1]
.

Corollary 3.9
.

Assume that the unit element
\mathrm{k}
of
\mathcal{V}
is approximated from totally below. Then
\mathcal{V}\text{-}\mathsf{Lip}
is Cauchy cocomplete.

One obtains another interesting instance of Theorem
3.7
by considering the lattice
[0,\infty]
with
\otimes=+
and neutral element
\mathrm{k}=0
, and
\odot=\cdot
multiplication with
e=1
. Here
\alpha\cdot\infty=\infty
for every
\alpha\in[0,\infty]
since the tensor preserves the bottom element. The internal hom is given by
[\alpha,\beta]=\frac{\beta}{\alpha}
for all
\alpha,\beta\in[0,\infty]
; here

\frac{0}{0}=0,\quad\frac{\alpha}{0}=\infty\;(\alpha>0),\quad\frac{\alpha}{\infty}=\frac{\infty}{\infty}=0.

Corollary 3.10
.

The category of Lawvere metric spaces and arbitrary maps
\varphi\colon X\to Y
between them, normed by

|\varphi|=\sup_{x,x^{\prime}\in X}\frac{Y(\varphi x,\varphi x^{\prime})}{X(x,x^{\prime})},

is Cauchy cocomplete.

Corollary 3.11
.

The normed category
\mathsf{Met}_{\infty}
of Lawvere metric spaces and arbitrary maps
\varphi\colon X\to Y
between them, normed by

|\varphi|=\max\{0,\log\Big(\sup_{x,x^{\prime}\in X}\frac{Y(\varphi x,\varphi x^{\prime})}{X(x,x^{\prime})}\Big)\},

is Cauchy cocomplete.

Uniqueness of Kähler Ricci shrinkers on toric orbifolds
Yu Li, and, Wenjia Zhang

Abstract
In this paper, we prove the uniqueness of Kähler Ricci shrinkers on toric orbifolds, extending the corresponding results previously established for toric manifolds.



1
Introduction

A Ricci shrinker
(M,g,f)
on a manifold or orbifold
M
is a complete Riemannian manifold or orbifold coupled with a smooth function
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Rc}+\nabla^{2}f=\frac{1}{2}g.

Ricci shrinkers on manifolds have been a central topic of study in the theory of Ricci flow. They are significant for two main reasons. First, they can be regarded as canonical metrics on manifolds, serving as natural generalizations of Einstein metrics. While Einstein metrics are static solutions to the Ricci flow, Ricci shrinkers represent self-similar solutions. Second, as critical metrics of Perelman’s celebrated
\mu
-functionals, Ricci shrinkers play a crucial role in the singularity analysis of Ricci flow. They provide models for understanding the formation of singularities, allowing for the classification and resolution of those singular regions.

In dimensions 2 and 3, all Ricci shrinkers on manifolds have been fully classified; see
[
28
]

[
41
]

[
39
]

[
40
]

[
14
]
, among others. The complete list consists of
\mathbb{R}^{2},S^{2},\mathbb{R}^{3},S^{3},S^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
and their quotients. A Kähler Ricci shrinker
(M,g,J,f)
is a Ricci shrinker on a complex manifold
(M,J)
so that
g
is a Kähler metric. The classification of Kähler Ricci shrinkers is crucial for understanding singularity formation along the Kähler Ricci flow. Recently, the classification of all Kähler Ricci shrinkers on complex surfaces has been fully completed (cf.
[
21
]

[
20
]

[
9
]
and
[
35
]
). However, in higher dimensions, the classification of Ricci shrinkers remains an open and challenging problem that seems beyond current reach.

The uniqueness of Ricci shrinkers within certain classes is one of the most important questions in the study of Ricci shrinkers. On compact complex manifolds, Tian and Zhu
[
49
]

[
50
]
have established a general uniqueness theorem for Kähler Ricci shrinkers in the anti-canonical class. However, for noncompact cases, the situation is far more complicated and remains unresolved in general. In the specific case of complex toric manifolds, the uniqueness of Kähler Ricci shrinkers was proven by Cifarelli
[
19
]
, offering progress in this particular setting.

Orbifolds are natural generalizations of manifolds that allow for singularities and are locally modeled on quotients of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by a finite group action. They arise naturally in various areas, such as differential geometry and algebraic geometry. Ricci flows and Ricci shrinkers on orbifolds have been studied by many people. For instance, it was shown in
[
51
]
that there exists a unique Ricci shrinker on any
2
-dimensional bad orbifold without boundary. Another notable result is that the application of Ricci flow on orbifolds provides a new proof of the geometrization of
3
-dimensional orbifolds
[
32
]
. Even in the context of Ricci flow on manifolds, Ricci shrinkers on orbifolds emerge naturally as models for singularities (cf.
[
18
]

[
8
]
).

In this paper, we extend the aforementioned uniqueness result to toric orbifolds. Our main theorem is:

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
(M^{2n},J,\mathbb{T}^{n})
is a complex orbifold with an effective and holomorphic
\mathbb{T}^{n}
-action. Then, up to equivariant biholomorphism, there is at most one
\mathbb{T}^{n}
-invariant Kähler Ricci shrinker
(M,g,J,f)
with bounded scalar curvature.

In the setting of Theorem
1.1
, if a Kähler Ricci shrinker
(M,g,J,f)
exists, an important observation is that
(M^{2n},\omega,\mathbb{T}^{n})
, where
\omega
is the corresponding Kähler form, is a symplectic toric orbifold with a Hamiltonian
\mathbb{T}^{n}
-action (cf. Proposition
4.2
(ii)). Furthermore, the holomorphic Killing field
J\nabla f
belongs to
\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}
, the Lie algebra of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
(cf. Proposition
4.2
(iii)). To ensure this, it is necessary to assume that the scalar curvature of
g
is globally bounded below, a condition that automatically holds in the manifold case.

Symplectic toric orbifolds, like symplectic toric manifolds, have been well understood for compact cases. Lerman and Tolman
[
33
]
demonstrated that a compact symplectic toric orbifold corresponds one-to-one with a labeled, rational, simple polytope (cf. Definition
2.24
), which is the image of the moment map. The local structure groups of the orbifold can be determined from the integers attached to the facets of the polytope. However, the noncompact case is far more complicated. Without additional conditions, the image of the moment map may not even be convex (cf.
[
43
]
). In our case, we prove that the image of the moment map of
(M^{2n},\omega,\mathbb{T}^{n})
is also a proper, rational, simple polyhedron
P
in
\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
, with an integer attached to each facet. A key point is that the potential function
f
, which is a Hamiltonian function of
J\nabla f
, is proper and bounded below. Additionally, the boundedness of the scalar curvature ensures that the set of critical points of
f
must be compact, implying that there are only finitely many fixed points of the
\mathbb{T}^{n}
-action. In fact, we establish a one-to-one correspondence between all such toric symplectic orbifolds and their associated labeled polyhedra; see Theorem
2.30
and Theorem
2.31
.

In particular, there exists a standard symplectic toric orbifold
(M_{P},\omega_{P},J_{P})
from Delzant’s construction, which is also a toric variety (see Definition
2.32
). On the one hand, there exists a
\mathbb{T}^{n}
-invariant symplectomorphism between
(M,\omega)
and
(M_{P},\omega_{P})
. On the other hand, under our assumptions, the
\mathbb{T}^{n}
-action extends uniquely to a
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
-action. By using the holomorphic slice theorem (cf. Theorem
2.23
), one can demonstrate that
(M,J,(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n})
is also a toric variety, biholomorphic to
(M_{P},J_{P})
(cf. Theorem
2.34
). Moreover, the labeled polyhedron
P
depends only on the class of
[\omega]
in the orbifold de Rham cohomology
H^{2}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)
; see Corollary
3.4
.

In our case, the labeled polyhedron
P
is determined by the anti-canonical class. Using the renowned Abreu-Guillemin theory, the Ricci shrinker equation on the principal orbit of
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
can be transformed into a Monge-Ampère-type equation on the interior of
P
, regarding the corresponding symplectic potential (cf. Proposition
4.6
), with appropriate boundary conditions (cf. Theorem
3.5
). The equation of the symplectic potential is determined, as the holomorphic Killing field
J\nabla f
is independent of the metric
g
. This fact is well-known in the compact case, where it is established using the weighted Futaki invariant (cf.
[
46
, Theorem 1.4]
). Following the methods in
[
21
]
and
[
19
]
, we show that
J\nabla f
is the unique minimum of the weighted volume functional; see Definition
4.7
.

Since the metric
g
is assumed to be complete, the uniqueness of the Kähler Ricci shrinker in our case reduces to proving the uniqueness of the solution to the equation regarding the symplectic potential. This follows from the convexity and rigidity of the Ding functional along geodesics in the space of symplectic potentials
[
10
]

[
19
]
. The convexity ensures that the critical point, which corresponds to a solution, must be unique if it exists. Moreover, the rigidity implies that any two such solutions can differ only by the addition of a linear function. These facts together confirm the uniqueness of the Kähler Ricci shrinker, thereby completing the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

If we do not assume that the Kähler Ricci shrinker is
\mathbb{T}^{n}
-invariant, but instead impose the stronger assumption of bounded Ricci curvature, we have the following result, which is also known in the manifold case
[
19
, Theorem B]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
(M^{2n},J,\mathbb{T}^{n})
is a complex orbifold with an effective and holomorphic
\mathbb{T}^{n}
-action. Then, up to biholomorphism, there is at most one Kähler Ricci shrinker
(M,g,J,f)
with
J\nabla f\in\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}
and bounded Ricci curvature.

The key element in the proof of Theorem
1.2
is a Matsushima-type theorem, originally proved for Kähler Ricci shrinkers on manifolds
[
21
, Theorem 5.1]
. This theorem is the primary reason why the stronger assumption of bounded Ricci curvature is required. This result can be directly extended to the orbifold setting with nearly the same proof. With this in place, any Kähler Ricci shrinker in the context of Theorem
1.2
can be made
\mathbb{T}^{n}
-invariant after applying a biholomorphism of
M
. Therefore, the uniqueness of the shrinker follows from Theorem
1.1
.

The organization of this paper is as follows. In Section 2, we review fundamental concepts and properties of orbifolds. Additionally, we establish a one-to-one correspondence between a class of symplectic toric orbifolds and labeled polyhedra. We also revisit Delzant’s construction and demonstrate that symplectic toric orbifolds in our case are toric varieties. In Section 3, we review the Abreu-Guillemin theory for Kähler metrics on toric orbifolds and present some key estimates for Ricci shrinkers on orbifolds. Finally, in Section 4, we prove our main theorems using the results and concepts developed in the preceding sections.

Definition 2.1

(Vector orbispace)
.

A vector orbispace is a pair
(V,\Gamma)
, where
V
is a vector space and
\Gamma
is a finite group that acts linearly and effectively on
V
. A
linear map
between vector orbispaces
(V,\Gamma)
and
(V^{\prime},\Gamma^{\prime})
consists of a linear map
T:V\to V^{\prime}
and a homomorphism
h:\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\prime}
such that for any
g\in\Gamma
and
v\in V
, the relation
h(g)(Tv)=T(gv)
holds.

Definition 2.2

(Local chart)
.

An
n
-dimensional local chart
(U_{p},\tilde{U}_{p},\Gamma_{p},\pi_{p})
around a point
p
in a topological space
X
consists of:

1.

An open neighborhood
U_{p}
of
p
in
X
;

2.

An open connected subset
\tilde{U}_{p}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
\Gamma_{p}
is a finite group acting smoothly and effectively on
\tilde{U}_{p}
;

3.

A
\Gamma_{p}
-equivariant projection
\pi_{p}:\tilde{U}_{p}\to U_{p}
, where the action is trivial on
U_{p}
, inducing a homeomorphism
\tilde{U}_{p}/\Gamma_{p}\to U_{p}
.

By slight abuse of notation, a local chart
(U,\tilde{U},\Gamma,\pi)
is often denoted simply by
(\tilde{U},\Gamma)
.

Definition 2.3

(Smooth orbifolds)
.

An
n
-dimensional smooth orbifold
M
consists of a connected, Hausdorff, paracompact topological space
|M|
, known as the underlying space, along with a maximal collection of
n
-dimensional local charts
(\tilde{U}_{i},\Gamma_{i})
such that:

1.

The collection
{U_{i}}
forms an open cover of
|M|
;

2.

For any
p\in U_{1}\cap U_{2}
, there exists a local chart
U_{3}
with
p\in U_{3}\subset U_{1}\cap U_{2}
and embeddings
(\tilde{U}_{3},\Gamma_{3})\to(\tilde{U}_{1},\Gamma_{1})
and
(\tilde{U}_{3},\Gamma_{3})\to(\tilde{U}_{2},\Gamma_{2})
.

An embedding
(\tilde{U},\Gamma)\to(\tilde{U}^{\prime},\Gamma^{\prime})
between two local charts is defined as a smooth embedding
\tilde{\phi}:\tilde{U}\to\tilde{U}^{\prime}
, together with a group homomorphism
\bar{\phi}:\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\prime}
such that
\tilde{\phi}
is
\bar{\phi}
-equivariant.

Given a smooth orbifold
M
and any

, a local chart
(U_{p},\tilde{U}_{p},\Gamma_{p},\pi_{p})
around
p
is called

\pi_{p}^{-1}(p)
consists of a single point
\tilde{p}
. In this case,

is referred to as the

at

can always be found, and the structure group at

is independent of the choice of local chart. Without loss of generality, we will consider only good local charts. The tangent space

is called the

and is denoted by
\tilde{T}_{p}M
. The
tangent space
at
p
, denoted by
T_{p}M
, is defined to be the vector orbispace
(\tilde{T}_{p}M,\Gamma_{p})
, where the group action of
\Gamma_{p}
on
\tilde{T}_{p}M
is induced by the differential.

The
regular part

consists of points with a trivial structure group. It is clear that

forms a smooth manifold and constitutes an open, dense set of
|M|
. The
singular part
of

is defined as the set
|M|_{\mathrm{sing}}=|M|\backslash M_{\mathrm{reg}}
. We denote by
\Sigma_{\Gamma}
the subset of

consisting of points with the structure group
\Gamma
. This leads to the canonical stratification

|M|=\coprod_{\alpha}\Sigma_{\alpha},

(2.1)

Definition 2.5

(Suborbifold)

An orbifold

(Orbifold fundamental group)

Let

Definition 2.10

(Riemannian orbifold)
.

A Riemannian orbifold
(M,g)
is a smooth orbifold
M
with a smooth metric

, a symmetric
(0,2)
-tensor which is positive everywhere.

By using the partition of unity (cf.
[

(M,g)
defines a metric structure described as follows.

We say a curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to|M|
is

if

can be decomposed into a countable number of subintervals

so that
\gamma_{(t_{i},t_{i+1})}

(cf.
[

is defined as

d(x,y)=\inf\{L(\gamma)\mid\gamma\text{ is an admissible curve joining $x$ to $y$}\}.

Then

becomes a length space. Furthermore, we say that the Riemannian orbifold
(M,g)
is

if the metric space
(|M|,d)
is complete. In this case, any two points in

can be joined by a minimizing geodesic that realizes the distance

. Moreover, it follows from

that if

. In particular, this implies that

is

.

Definition 2.11

(Complex orbifolds)
.

A

(M,J)
of complex dimension

Definition 2.12

(Symplectic/Kähler orbifold)
.

A

(M,\omega)
with a closed non-degenerate

\omega
. A

Now, we recall the definition of the group action on orbifolds.

Definition 2.13

(Group action)
.

Let
G
be a connected compact Lie group, and let
M
be an orbifold. A smooth map
\mu:G\times M\rightarrow M
is called a smooth action of

|\mu|:G\times|M|\rightarrow|M|

For a group
G

,
|\mu|(g,x)

(g,x)\in G\times|M|
. Moreover, for a fixed

\{g\in G\mid gx=x,\,\forall x\in|M|\}=\{1\}

Each orbit

Proposition 2.14

Definition 2.20

(Symplectic toric orbifold)

2.2
Symplectic toric orbifolds and labeled polyhedra

In this section, we consider a symplectic toric orbifold
(M^{2n},\omega,\mathbb{T}^{n},\Phi)
(cf. Definition (
2.20
)). In the case of manifolds, Delzant
[
23
]
proved that compact toric manifolds correspond one-to-one with smooth and simple polytopes through the moment map (cf. Definition
2.24
). Later, this result was generalized by Lerman and Tolman
[
33
]
to compact toric orbifolds, establishing a connection with rational and simple polytopes. We will prove a similar correspondence for a class of symplectic toric orbifolds.
First, we recall the following definitions. Throughout, we assume
\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}
to be the Lie algebra of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
with lattice
\mathfrak{l}
,
\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
to be its dual, and
\mathfrak{l}^{*}
the dual lattice.

Definition 2.24
.

Let
\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}
be the Lie algebra of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
with lattice
\mathfrak{l}
, and
\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
be its dual and

1.

(Polyhedron)
A
polyhedron
is a finite intersection of affine half spaces
H^{+}_{n_{i},a_{i}}:=\{x\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}\;|\;\langle n_{i},x\rangle+a_{i}\geq 0\}
with
n_{i}\in\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}},a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
. A
face
of a polyhedron

is a polyhedron
P\cap H_{n,a}
, where
H_{n,a}:=\{x\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}\;|\;\langle n,x\rangle+a=0\}
is an affine hyperplane such that
P\subset H^{+}_{n,a}
. Faces of dimension
n-1
,
1
and
0
are called
facet
,
edge
and

, respectively.

2.

(Polytope)
A
polytope
is a bounded polyhedron. Equivalently, a polytope is the convex hull of finite vertices.

3.

(Polyhedral cone)
A
polyhedral cone
is a finite intersection of
H^{+}_{n_{i},0}
. Equivalently, a polyhedral cone is the convex hull of finite rays from
0
. A polyhedral cone is called
rational
if each edge is generated by an element in
\mathfrak{l}
. A polyhedral cone is called
proper
if it does not contain a line.

4.

(Asymptotic cone)
For a polyhedron
P=\cap H^{+}_{n_{i},a_{i}}
, its asymptotic cone, denoted by
C(P)
, is a polyhedral cone
C(P):=\cap H^{+}_{n_{i},0}
.

5.

(Proper/Rational/Simple)
A polyhedron
P
is
proper
if its asymptotic cone
C(P)
is proper.
P
is
rational
if, for each vertex
x\in P
, the polyhedral cone
C_{v}:=\mathrm{Cone}(\mathbb{R}_{+}(P-v))
is rational.
P
is called

if there are exactly
n

\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
over
\mathbb{R}
.

For a polyhedron, we have the following structure result (cf.
[
54

Lemma 2.25

Any polyhedron
P
can be decomposed as

\displaystyle P=\mathrm{Conv}(\{v_{i}\})+C(P),

where

is the set of vertices of

For a proper, rational, simple polyhedron

so that each

Note that

is closed and convex, which would contradict the assumption in Definition

Therefore, to prove (i), we only need to show that

with an inner product that identifies
\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}
with
\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
. Suppose, to the contrary, that there exists

\displaystyle\omega=2\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\phi\quad\text{and}\quad g=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial y_{i}\partial y_{j}}dy_{i}\otimes dy_{j}+\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial y_{i}\partial y_{j}}\theta_{i}\otimes\theta_{j}\right).

Proposition 3.3

For the Kähler toric orbifold

(ii)

Proof.

in

3.2
Geometry of Ricci shrinker orbifolds

In this section, we consider the Riemannian Ricci shrinker orbifolds and prove some fundamental estimates. Most estimates and proofs are adapted from those for Ricci shrinkers on manifolds.

Definition 3.6

(Ricci shrinker orbifold)
.

A Ricci shrinker orbifold
(M^{n},g,f)
is a complete Riemannian orbifold (cf. Definition
2.10
) coupled with a smooth function
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Rc}+\nabla^{2}f=\frac{1}{2}g.

(3.9)

As in the manifold case, the function
R+|\nabla f|^{2}-f
is always a constant on a Ricci shrinker orbifold. Therefore, we normalize
f
by adding a constant so that

\displaystyle R+|\nabla f|^{2}=f.

(3.10)

It is well-known that any Ricci shrinker on a manifold has nonnegative scalar curvature (cf.
[
17
]
and
[
53
]
). We demonstrate that a Ricci shrinker orbifold also has nonnegative scalar curvature if it is bounded below. Furthermore, the scalar curvature must be positive unless the orbifold is flat.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a Ricci shrinker orbifold such that
\inf_{|M|}R>-\infty
. Then
R>0
everywhere unless
(M,g)
is isometric to the flat cone
\mathbb{R}^{n}/\Gamma
, where
\Gamma\leq\mathrm{O}(n)
is a finite subgroup.

Proof.

We first prove
R\geq 0
on
M
. By our assumption, we assume
\inf_{|M|}R>-B
for a positive constant
B
. We set
\bar{f}=f+B
, then by (
3.9
) and (
3.10
),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Rc}+\nabla^{2}\bar{f}=\frac{1}{2}g\quad\text{and}\quad|\nabla\bar{f}|^{2}\leq\bar{f}.

(3.11)

In particular, it implies that
\bar{f}\geq 0
and
\sqrt{\bar{f}}
is
1/2
-Lipschitz on
|M|
. We fix a point
p\in M_{\mathrm{reg}}
. Then, it follows from the geodesically convexity of
M_{\mathrm{reg}}
and the same argument as
[
15
, Proposition 2.1]
(see also
[
29
, Lemma 2.1]
and
[
34
, Lemma 1]
) that for any
x\in M_{\mathrm{reg}}
,

\displaystyle\frac{d(p,x)}{2}+\frac{1}{3}-2n\leq\sqrt{\bar{f}(p)}+\sqrt{\bar{f}(x)}.

(3.12)

Since
\bar{f}
is smooth and
M_{\mathrm{reg}}
is dense, it is clear that (
3.12
) holds for any
x\in|M|
. In particular,
\bar{f}
is proper and bounded below.

Next, we recall the definition of weighted Laplacian
\Delta_{f}:=\Delta-\langle\nabla f,\cdot\rangle
. The following identities then hold:

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}\bar{f}=

\displaystyle\frac{n}{2}-\bar{f},

(3.13)

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}R=

\displaystyle R-2|\mathrm{Rc}|^{2}.

(3.14)

Note that both identities are well-known for Ricci shrinkers on manifolds (see, for instance,
[
42
]
), and hence, they hold on Ricci shrinker orbifolds if one uses the local charts. We take a smooth decreasing cutoff function
\eta(t)
on
\mathbb{R}
such that
\eta=1
on
(-\infty,1]
and
\eta=0
on
[2,\infty)
. Additionally, we require that

\displaystyle\frac{(\eta^{\prime})^{2}}{\eta}+|\eta^{\prime\prime}|\leq C

(3.15)

for a universal constant
C
.

For any constant
A>1
, we set
\varphi=\eta(\bar{f}/A)
and
u=\varphi R
, which are smooth functions on
M
. From direct computation by using (
3.11
), (
3.13
) and (
3.14
), we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}u=

\displaystyle\varphi\Delta_{f}R+R\Delta_{f}\phi+2\langle\nabla R,\nabla\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi(R-2|\mathrm{Rc}|^{2})+R\left(\eta^{\prime}\frac{n/2-\bar{f}}{A}+\eta^{\prime\prime}\frac{|\nabla\bar{f}|^{2}}{A^{2}}\right)+2\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{A}\langle\nabla R,\nabla f\rangle.

(3.16)

Suppose that
u
takes its minimum value at
q\in|M|
. By the maximum principle, considering the local chart around
q
if necessary, we have at
q
:

\displaystyle\Delta_{f}u\geq 0\quad\text{and}\quad\nabla u=\varphi\nabla R+\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{A}R\nabla\bar{f}=0.

Combining with (
3.15
) and (
3.16
), we obtain at
q
:

\displaystyle 0\leq\varphi(R-2R^{2}/n)+R\left(\eta^{\prime}\frac{n/2-\bar{f}}{A}+\eta^{\prime\prime}\frac{|\nabla\bar{f}|^{2}}{A^{2}}-2\frac{(\eta^{\prime})^{2}}{\varphi}\frac{|\nabla\bar{f}|^{2}}{A^{2}}\right).

(3.17)

Notice that
|\nabla\bar{f}|^{2}\leq\bar{f}\leq 2A
on the support of
u
, and
\eta^{\prime}\leq 0
. If
u(q)\leq 0
, it follows from (
3.15
) and (
3.17
) that

\displaystyle u(q)\geq-\frac{C}{A},

for a constant
C
depending on
n
. By letting
A\to+\infty
, we conclude that
R\geq 0
everywhere.

If there exists
x_{0}\in|M|
such that
R(x_{0})=0
, it follows from (
3.14
) and the strong maximum principle that
R\equiv 0
on
M
. By (
3.14
) again, we conclude that
\mathrm{Rc}\equiv 0
and hence

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}f=\frac{g}{2}

(3.18)

on
M
. As discussed above,
f
is proper and bounded below, so there exists a minimum point
p
, which may be a singular point. Let
r:=d(p,\cdot)
, and we claim

\displaystyle f=\frac{r^{2}}{4}+f(p).

(3.19)

Indeed, for any
x\in M_{\mathrm{reg}}
, there exists a unit minimizing geodesic
\gamma(t):t\in[0,r(x)]
starting from
p
to
x
such that
\gamma((0,r(x)])\subset M_{\mathrm{reg}}
. Since
p
is a minimum point of
f
, it follows immediately from (
3.18
) that (
3.19
) holds at
x
. Since
M_{\mathrm{reg}}
is dense, (
3.19
) holds everywhere.

Given that
|\nabla f|^{2}=f
, it follows from (
3.18
) and (
3.19
) that, away from
p
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\nabla r}g=\frac{2}{r}(g-dr^{2}),

which implies that for any vector field
U,V
such that
[U,\nabla r]=[V,\nabla r]=0
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{r}(g(U,V))=(\mathcal{L}_{\nabla r}g)(U,V)=\frac{2}{r}g(U,V).

Therefore, if we denote the level set
f=1/4
by
(\Sigma,g_{\Sigma})
, which is a Riemannian orbifold, then

\displaystyle g=dr^{2}+r^{2}g_{\Sigma},

which means
(M,g)
is a cone orbifold. Since the tangent cone at
p
is a flat cone
\mathbb{R}^{n}/\Gamma
, where
\Gamma
is the structure group at
p
, we conclude immediately that
(M,g)
is isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}/\Gamma
.

In sum, the proof is complete.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

For a Ricci shrinker orbifold with a compact singular set
|M|_{\mathrm{sing}}
, it is also true that
R\geq 0
. Indeed, this fact can be proved by the same argument as in
[
53
, Theorem 1.3]
, by applying the maximum principle for
\phi R
, where
\phi
is a cutoff function constructed by using the distance function. Note that one can assume that the singular set is contained in
\{\phi=1\}
. We conjecture that Proposition
3.7
still holds without the assumption
\inf_{|M|}R>-\infty
.

For a Ricci shrinker orbifold, since its regular part is geodesically convex, the following result can be proved as in
[
15
, Proposition 2.1]
or
[
29
, Lemma 2.1]
, by using Proposition
3.7
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a Ricci shrinker orbifold such that
\inf_{|M|}R>-\infty
. Then there exists a point
p\in|M|
where
f
attains its miminum and
f
satisfies the quadratic growth estimate

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left(d(p,x)-5n\right)_{+}^{2}\leq f(x)\leq\frac{1}{4}\left(d(p,x)+\sqrt{2n}\right)^{2},

for all
x\in|M|
, where
a_{+}:=\max\{a,0\}
.

By using Proposition
3.9
, we can follow the same proof as in
[
15
, Theorem 1.2]
or
[
29
, Lemma 2.2]
verbatim to show the following volume estimate.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a Ricci shrinker orbifold such that
\inf_{|M|}R>-\infty
. Then, there exists a constant
C=C(n)>0
such that

\displaystyle|B(p,r)|\leq Cr^{n},

where
p
is a minimum point of
f
.

Next, we prove the following result, which is also well-known for Ricci shrinkers on manifolds (cf.
[
36
]

[
52
]
).

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a Ricci shrinker orbifold such that
\inf_{|M|}R>-\infty
. Then, the orbifold fundamental group
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(M)
is finite.

Proof.

Let
\rho:(\tilde{M},\tilde{g},\tilde{f})\to(M,g,f)
be the universal cover, which is also a Ricci shrinker orbifold by using the pullback metric
\tilde{g}=\rho^{*}g
and potential function
\tilde{f}=\rho^{*}f
. Let
p
be a minimum point of
f
and fix
\tilde{p}\in\rho^{-1}(p)
. Then,
\tilde{p}
is also a minimum point of
\tilde{f}
, so the estimates in Proposition
3.9
also hold for
\tilde{f}
, that is,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left(d(\tilde{p},x)-5n\right)_{+}^{2}\leq\tilde{f}(x)\leq\frac{1}{4}\left(d(\tilde{p},x)+\sqrt{2n}\right)^{2},

for any
x\in|\tilde{M}|
. Therefore, it implies that the map
\rho
is proper. Since
\rho
is a covering map, the preimage of any regular point in
|M|
has a finite number of points. Consequently,
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(M)
is finite.
∎

As a corollary, we prove

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a Ricci shrinker orbifold such that

. Then,

\displaystyle H^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)=0.

Since there exists a natural epimorphism
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(M)\to\pi_{1}(|M|)
(cf.
[

), we conclude by Proposition

.

Proof of the main theorems

4.1
Kähler Ricci shrinkers on toric orbifolds

In this section, we consider Ricci shrinker orbifolds with extra structures and symmetries. First, we have the following definition.

Definition 4.1

(Toric Kähler Ricci shrinker orbifold)
.

A
toric Kähler Ricci shrinker orbifold

(M^{2n},g,J,f,\mathbb{T}^{n})
is a Ricci shrinker orbifold
(M,g,f)
with a compatible complex structure
J
so that
\omega:=g(J\cdot,\cdot)
is closed, together with an effective, holomorphic and isometric action of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.

In particular, it follows from the equation (
3.9
) that
\nabla f
is a real holomorphic vector field and
J\nabla f
is a Killing field. For a toric Kähler Ricci shrinker orbifold with bounded scalar curvature, we prove

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(M^{2n},g,J,f,\mathbb{T}^{n})
be a toric Kähler Ricci shrinker orbifold with bounded scalar curvature. Then the following assertions hold:

(i)

The
\mathbb{T}^{n}
-action preserves the potential function
f
.

(ii)

The
\mathbb{T}^{n}
-action on the symplectic form
\omega:=g(J\cdot,\cdot)
is Hamiltonian.

(iii)

J\nabla f\in\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}
, the Lie algebra of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.

(iv)

The action of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
extends to a holomorphic
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
-action.

Proof.

(i): We claim that for any
Y\in\mathfrak{l}
, the lattice of
\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}
, we have
Yf\equiv 0
. Indeed, we denote the family of isometries
e^{2\pi tY}
on
M
by
\phi_{t}
, where
t\in[0,1]
. Since
\phi_{t}
is isometric, it follows from (
3.9
) that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Rc}+\nabla^{2}(\phi_{t}^{*}f)=\frac{1}{2}g.

Taking the derivative with respect to
t
at
t=0
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}(Yf)=0.

(4.1)

If
(M,g)
does not split off a line, it follows from (
4.1
) that
Yf
must be a constant. Since
f
has a minimum point by Proposition
3.9
, it is immediate that
Yf\equiv 0
. On the other hand, if
(M,g,f)
indeed splits off a line, we assume that
(M,g,f)
is isometric to
(N\times\mathbb{R}^{k},g_{N}\times g_{E},f_{N}+|x|^{2}/4)
, where
(N,g_{N},f_{N})
is a Ricci shrinker orbifold that does not split off a line, and
x
is the coordinate of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Since
Y
belongs to the lattice, we conclude that
Y
is tangent to
N
, and hence
Yf=Yf_{N}
. Therefore, the same argument yields
Yf\equiv 0
. Since
\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}
is generated by
\mathfrak{l}
, we conclude that
Yf\equiv 0
for any
Y\in\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}
.

(ii): Since the
\mathbb{T}^{n}
-action is holomorphic and isometric, it is also symplectic with respect to
\omega
. Let
\{Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{n}\}
be a basis of
\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}
. Since

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{Y_{i}}\omega=d(\iota_{Y_{i}}\omega)=0,

it follows from Corollary
3.12
that there exists a smooth function
h_{i}
such that

\displaystyle-\iota_{Y_{i}}\omega=dh_{i}.

For a general
Y=\sum c_{i}Y_{i}\in\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}
, we define the map
\Phi:M\to\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
by

\displaystyle\langle\Phi,Y\rangle=\sum c_{i}h_{i}.

To show that the
\mathbb{T}^{n}
-action is Hamiltonian, we only need to prove that
\Phi
is invariant under
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\omega(Y_{i},Y_{j})\equiv 0.

(4.2)

By direct computation, we have

\displaystyle d(\omega(Y_{i},Y_{j}))=d\iota_{Y_{j}}\iota_{Y_{i}}\omega=\mathcal{L}_{Y_{j}}\iota_{Y_{i}}\omega-\iota_{Y_{i}}\mathcal{L}_{Y_{j}}\omega=\iota_{[Y_{j},Y_{i}]}\omega=0.

Consequently, it implies that
\omega(Y_{i},Y_{j})\equiv c
for a constant
c
. By Proposition
3.9
, the level set
\{f=L\}
is compact and nonempty for all large constant
L
. We assume
h_{j}
takes its minimum on
\{f=L\}
at
q
. By (i),
\mathbb{T}^{n}
preserves
\{f=L\}
, so
\omega(Y_{i},Y_{j})=Y_{i}h_{j}=0
at
q
. Therefore, (
4.2
) holds.

(iii): As before, we set
\{Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{n}\}
to be a basis of
\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}
. Since the
\mathbb{T}^{n}
-action is effective, there exists an open dense set
U\subset M_{\mathrm{reg}}
on which
\mathbb{T}^{n}
has no fixed point. It is clear from (ii) that
T_{x}M=\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}\oplus J\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}
orthogonally at each point
x\in U
. Moreover, since each
Y_{i}
preserves
f
, we can set

\displaystyle J\nabla f=\sum_{i}b_{i}Y_{i},

on
U
, where
b_{i}
is a smooth function. By direct computation, for any
k
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i}Y_{k}(b_{i})Y_{i}=[Y_{k},\sum_{i}b_{i}Y_{i}]=[Y_{k},J\nabla f]=J[Y_{k},\nabla f]=0,

where we have used the fact that
Y_{k}
is a holomorphic Killing field which preserves
f
. Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i}JY_{k}(b_{i})Y_{i}=[JY_{k},\sum_{i}b_{i}Y_{i}]=[JY_{k},J\nabla f]=-[Y_{k},\nabla f]=0.

Therefore, we conclude that
b_{i}
is a constant on
U
. By continuity, it is immediate that
J\nabla f\in\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}
.

(iv): We only need to prove that
JY
is complete for any
Y\in\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}
. By our assumption that the scalar curvature
R
is bounded, it follows from (
3.10
) and Proposition
3.9
that the set of critical points of
f
is compact. Since
[JY,\nabla f]=0
, it follows from
[
21
, Lemma 2.34]
that
JY
is complete.
∎

As a corollary, we have

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
(M^{2n},g,J,f,\mathbb{T}^{n})
be a toric Kähler Ricci shrinker orbifold with bounded scalar curvature. Then, it is a Kähler toric orbifold with
(\dagger)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.2
(ii)(iv), we have shown that
(M,\omega,\mathbb{T}^{n},\Phi)
has a Hamiltonian
\mathbb{T}^{n}
-action which extends to a holomorphic
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
-action, where
\omega=g(J\cdot,\cdot)
, and
\Phi
is a moment map.

To show
(\dagger)
, we first note that the set of critical points of
f
is compact since
R
is bounded. Then, it follows from Proposition
4.2
(iii) that there are only finitely many fixed points of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, since all fixed points are isolated. In addition, it is clear that

\displaystyle d\langle\Phi,J\nabla f\rangle=-\iota_{J\nabla f}\omega=g(\cdot,\nabla f)=df,

and hence by Proposition
3.9
,
\langle\Phi,J\nabla f\rangle
is proper and bounded below.
∎

As a Kähler toric orbifold, the labeled polyhedron is determined for a toric Kähler Ricci shrinker orbifold with bounded scalar curvature.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(M^{2n},g,J,f,\mathbb{T}^{n})
be a toric Kähler Ricci shrinker orbifold with bounded scalar curvature. Then, the associated labeled polyhedron
P
is independent of the metric
g
.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.4
, we only need to prove that the class
[\omega]
is determined. This is true since by the Ricci shrinker equation (
3.9
)

\displaystyle\frac{[\omega]}{4\pi}=-\mathcal{K},

in
H^{2}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)
, where
\mathcal{K}
is the canonical class.
∎

For a toric Kähler Ricci shrinker orbifold
(M^{2n},g,J,f,\mathbb{T}^{n})
with bounded scalar curvature, it follows from
[
22
, Theorem 8.2.3]
that

\displaystyle-\mathcal{K}=\sum_{i}[D_{i}].

Therefore, by the proof of Corollary
3.4
, we conclude that the image of a moment map can be expressed as

\displaystyle P=\bigcap_{i=1}^{N}\{x\in\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}\;|\langle x,m_{i}n_{i}\rangle+2\geq 0\}.

In general, for any positive integer
m
, we consider the rescaled metric
g^{\prime}=\frac{m}{2}g
and
\omega^{\prime}=g^{\prime}(J\cdot,\cdot)
. It is clear that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Rc}(g^{\prime})+\nabla_{g^{\prime}}^{2}f=\frac{1}{m}g^{\prime}.

Therefore, the image of the moment map with respect to
\omega^{\prime}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle P^{\prime}=\frac{2}{m}P=\bigcap_{i=1}^{N}\{x\in\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}\;|\langle x,m_{i}n_{i}\rangle+m\geq 0\}.

(4.3)

Since
P^{\prime}
is a rational, simple polyhedron, we conclude from (
4.3
) that all vertices belong to
\mathfrak{l}^{*}
, if
m
is appropriately chosen. In other words,
P^{\prime}
is a lattice polyhedron. Consequently, the following result is immediate from Theorem
2.33
and
[
22
, Proposition 7.2.9]
.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
(M^{2n},g,J,f,\mathbb{T}^{n})
be a toric Kähler Ricci shrinker orbifold with bounded scalar curvature. Then,
(M,J)
is a quasiprojective toric variety.

4.2
Uniqueness of solutions

Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
. Let
(M^{2n},g,J,f,\mathbb{T}^{n})
be a toric Kähler Ricci shrinker orbifold with bounded scalar curvature. Then, it follows from Corollary
4.3
that
(M,\omega,J,(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n},\Phi)
is a Kähler toric orbifold with
(\dagger)
, where
\omega=g(J\cdot,\cdot)
, and
\Phi
is a moment map. Moreover, we set
P=\Phi(M)
to be the labeled polyhedron, which is defined independently of
g
by Lemma
4.4
and can be normalized and represented as

\displaystyle P=\bigcap_{i=1}^{N}\{x\in\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}\;|\langle x,m_{i}n_{i}\rangle+2\geq 0\}.

(4.4)

For the standard Kähler toric orbifold
(M_{P},\omega_{P},J_{P},(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n},\Phi_{P})
, we fix a momentum-angle coordinates
\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n};t_{1},\cdots,t_{n}\}
on
\mathring{P}\times\mathbb{T}^{n}\cong\mathring{M_{P}}
. It follows from Theorem
2.30
that there exists a
\mathbb{T}^{n}
-invariant symplectomorphism
\varphi:M_{P}\to M
. We set
J_{1}:=\varphi^{*}J
,
f_{1}:=\varphi^{*}f
and
g_{1}:=\omega_{P}(\cdot,J_{1}\cdot)
. Moreover, we denote
J_{1}\nabla^{g_{1}}f_{1}
by
X
. Note that
X
is a real holomorphic vector field with
X\in\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}
by Proposition
4.2
(iii).

Then, it is clear that
(M_{P},g_{1},J_{1},f_{1},\mathbb{T}^{n})
is a toric Kähler Ricci shrinker orbifold with bounded scalar curvature. Let
u
be the symplectic potential on
P
, with respect to
g_{1}
, together with its Legendre transform by
\phi
, i.e.,
\phi(y)+u(x)=\langle y,x\rangle
, where
y_{i}=\partial u/\partial x_{i}
. Notice that since the
\mathbb{T}^{n}
-action also extends to
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
-action on
M_{P}
, with respect to
J_{1}
, we have
\nabla u:P\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is surjective, that is,
y=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})
are coordinates of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Now, one can rewrite the Ricci shrinker equation (
3.9
) in terms of the symplectic potential.

Proposition 4.6
.

With the above assumptions, the symplectic potential
u
, after adding a constant if necessary, satisfies:

\displaystyle u_{i}x_{i}-u(x)-\log\det(u_{ij})=\langle b_{X},x\rangle,

(4.5)

on
\mathring{P}
, where
b_{X}\in\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}
is a constant vector.

Proof.

From Lemma
3.2
, it follows that

\displaystyle\omega_{P}=2\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\phi,

(4.6)

where the underlying complex structure is
J_{1}
. Thus, the Ricci form of
g_{1}
, denoted by
\mathrm{Rc}_{1}
, can be expressed as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Rc}_{1}=-\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\log\det(\phi_{ij}).

(4.7)

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle-\mathcal{L}_{J_{1}X}\omega_{P}=-d\iota_{J_{1}X}\omega_{P}=-d\left(g_{1}(X,\cdot)\right)=dd^{c}f_{1},

(4.8)

where
d^{c}=J_{1}d
. The Ricci shrinker equation (
3.9
) can be written as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Rc}_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{J_{1}X}\omega_{P}-\frac{1}{2}\omega_{P}=0.

Therefore, from (
4.6
), (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), we have

\displaystyle\partial\bar{\partial}\left(-\log\det(\phi_{ij})+f_{1}-\phi\right)\equiv 0,

and thus

\displaystyle-\log\det(\phi_{ij})+f_{1}-\phi=\langle a,y\rangle+c,

(4.9)

for a constant vector
a=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})
and a constant
c
. Applying the Legendre transformation to (
4.9
), we obtain

\displaystyle u_{i}x_{i}-u(x)+a_{i}u_{i}-\log\det(u_{ij})+c=f_{1}.

(4.10)

Since
f_{1}
is a Hamiltonian function of
X
, it follows from Proposition
4.2
(iii) that there exists a vector
b_{X}\in\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}
and constant
c^{\prime}
such that
f_{1}=\langle b_{X},x\rangle+c^{\prime}
. Therefore, after absorbing all constants into
u
, we obtain from (
4.10
)

\displaystyle u_{i}x_{i}-u(x)+a_{i}u_{i}-\log\det(u_{ij})=\langle b_{X},x\rangle.

(4.11)

Now, we claim that the vector
a\equiv 0
. Indeed, we set
L_{k}(x)=\langle x,m_{k}n_{k}\rangle+2
. Then, we can rewrite (
4.11
) as

\displaystyle(u_{P})_{i}x_{i}-u_{P}(x)+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\log L_{k}+a_{i}u_{i}=\log\left(\det(u_{ij})\prod_{k=1}^{N}L_{k}\right)+(u_{P}-u)_{i}x_{i}+u(x)-u_{P}(x)+\langle b_{X},x\rangle.

(4.12)

It follows from Theorem
3.5
that the right-hand side of (
4.12
) is a smooth function on
P
. On the other hand, the term
(u_{P})_{i}x_{i}-u_{P}(x)
, which is the Legendre transformation of
u_{P}
, satisfies by Proposition
3.3
(i)

\displaystyle(u_{P})_{i}x_{i}-u_{P}(x)=-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\log L_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\langle x,m_{k}n_{k}\rangle.

(4.13)

Combining (
4.12
) with (
4.13
), we conclude that the term
a_{i}u_{i}
, and hence
a_{i}(u_{P})_{i}
are smooth on
P
. However, we know that

\displaystyle a_{i}(u_{P})_{i}=\frac{1}{2}a_{i}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(L_{k})_{i}\left(\log L_{k}+1\right).

(4.14)

Note that
(L_{k})_{i}
is constant. Therefore, (
4.14
) forces
L_{k}(a)=2
for any
k
and hence

\displaystyle\langle n_{k},a\rangle=0.

Since
P
is rational and simple, it implies that
a\equiv 0
. In sum, the proof is complete.
∎

Next, we show that the vector
b_{X}\in\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}
in the equation (
4.5
) is independent of the choice of the Ricci shrinker metric
g_{1}
. To achieve this, we consider the following weighted volume functional as in
[
21
]
and
[
19
]
.

Definition 4.7

(Weighted volume functional)
.

Given a polyhedron as in (
4.4
), the weighted volume functional is defined for
b\in\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}
by

\displaystyle F(b)=\int_{P}e^{-\langle b,x\rangle}\,dx.

It follows from the representation (
4.4
) that
P
contains
0
as an interior point. Now, we have

Lemma 4.8
.

F(b)
is finite if and only if
b\in\mathrm{int}(C^{\prime}(P))
, the interior of the dual of the asymptotic cone of
P
. In addition,
F(b)
is convex and proper on
\mathrm{int}(C^{\prime}(P))
.

Proof.

The proof follows verbatim from
[
19
, Proposition 3.1]
.
∎

By taking the differential, the unique critical point
b
of
F(b)
, whose existence is guaranteed by Lemma
4.8
, is characterized by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial b^{j}}F=-\int_{P}x_{j}e^{-\langle b,x\rangle}dx=0.

For later applications, we then prove

Lemma 4.9
.

For any integer
m\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{P}|x^{m}|e^{-\langle b_{X},x\rangle}dx<\infty.

Proof.

Since
b_{X}\in\mathrm{int}(C^{\prime}(P))
, there exists a constant
\epsilon>0
such that
\langle b_{X},w\rangle\geq\epsilon|w|
for any
w\in C(P)
. By Lemma
2.25
, we can decompose
P=V+C(P)
, where
V
is the polytope that is the convex hull of all vertices of
P
. Therefore, we conclude

\displaystyle\langle b_{X},x\rangle\geq\epsilon|x|-C

for any
x\in P
. From this, the conclusion is immediate.
∎

Now, one can prove

Lemma 4.10
.

The constant vector
b_{X}
in (
4.5
) is the critical point of
F
, which is determined independently of
g_{1}
.

Proof.

We first show that
F(b_{X})
is finite. Under the momentum-angle coordinates, we have

\displaystyle\int_{P}e^{-\langle b_{X},x\rangle}dx=(2\pi)^{-n}\int_{\mathring{P}\times\mathbb{T}^{n}}e^{-\langle b_{X},x\rangle}dxdt=(2\pi)^{-n}\int_{(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}}e^{-f_{1}+c}\omega_{P}^{n}=C\int_{M_{P}}e^{-f_{1}}\,dV_{g_{1}},

where we have used the fact that
\omega=\sum_{i}dx_{i}\wedge dt_{i}
from (
3.3
) and
f_{1}=\langle b_{X},x\rangle+c
as shown before. Therefore, it follows from Proposition
3.9
and Proposition
3.10
that
F(b_{X})
is finite.

Furthermore, since
\phi
is the Legendre transformation of
u
, it follows from (
4.5
) that

\displaystyle\int_{P}x_{j}e^{-\langle b_{X},x\rangle}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y_{j}}e^{-\phi}dy=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\partial e^{-\phi}}{\partial y_{j}}dy.

(4.15)

Notice that by Lemma
4.9
, the first integral above is well-defined. Since
P
contains
0
, it follows from
[
10
, Lemma 2.6]
that
\phi
satisfies

\displaystyle\phi(y)\geq C^{-1}|y|-C

(4.16)

for a constant
C>1
. Applying the integration by parts, (
4.15
) yields that for any
1\leq j\leq n
,

\displaystyle\int_{P}x_{j}e^{-\langle b_{X},x\rangle}dx=0,

which finishes the proof.
∎

Suppose now
(M,g^{\prime},J,f^{\prime},\mathbb{T}^{n})
is another toric Kähler Ricci shrinker orbifold with bounded scalar curvature. As before, we choose a
\mathbb{T}^{n}
-invariant symplectomorphism
\psi:M_{P}\to M
and set
J_{2}:=\psi^{*}J
,
f_{2}:=\psi^{*}f
and
g_{2}:=\omega_{P}(\cdot,J_{2}\cdot)
. Moreover, we denote the symplectic potential of
g_{2}
by
u^{\prime}
. Clearly,
u^{\prime}
satisfies as (
4.5
)

\displaystyle u^{\prime}_{i}x_{i}-u^{\prime}(x)-\log\det(u^{\prime}_{ij})=\langle b_{X},x\rangle.

(4.17)

To prove the uniqueness, we first set up the moduli space of symplectic potentials with respect to (
4.17
).

Definition 4.11

(Space
\mathcal{E}
)
.

The moduli space
\mathcal{E}
consists of all smooth function
v
satisfying

(i)

v
is a smooth and strictly convex function on
\mathring{P}
;

(ii)

v-u_{P}
is a smooth function on
P
;

(iii)

\nabla v:P\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is surjective;

(iv)

\displaystyle\int_{P}|v|e^{-\langle b_{X},x\rangle}\,dx<\infty
.

We first show

Lemma 4.12
.

The symplectic potentials
u
and
u^{\prime}
belong to
\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

(i) (ii) and (ii) in the Definition
4.11
are clear, so we only need to prove (iv) for
u
. First, we notice that since
\nabla u:P\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is surjective,
u
has a minimum point in
\mathring{P}
. Since
\int_{P}e^{-\langle b_{X},x\rangle}\,dx<\infty
, we only need to prove

\displaystyle\int_{P}ue^{-\langle b_{X},x\rangle}dx<\infty.

(4.18)

Recall that
\phi(y)
is the Legendre transformation of
u
so that
\phi(y)
grows at least linearly in
|y|
by (
4.16
). We compute

\displaystyle\int_{P}ue^{-\langle b_{X},x\rangle}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\langle\nabla\phi,y\rangle-\phi)e^{-\phi}\,dy\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle\nabla\phi,y\rangle e^{-\phi}\,dy+C.

On the other hand, by using the polar coordinates and applying the integration by parts, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle\nabla\phi,y\rangle e^{-\phi}dy=\int_{S^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}e^{-\phi}drd\Theta=\frac{n}{2}\int_{S^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n-1}e^{-\phi}drd\Theta=n\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\phi}dy.

Therefore, (
4.18
) holds.
∎

Now, we can state the uniqueness result in the space
\mathcal{E}
.

Proposition 4.13
.

Up to an addition of a linear function, there is at most one solution
v\in\mathcal{E}
on
\mathring{P}
of

\displaystyle v_{i}x_{i}-v(x)-\log\det(v_{ij})=\langle b_{X},x\rangle.

(4.19)

Proof.

This proof follows from
[
19
, Theorem 3.11]
verbatim, and we sketch it for readers’ convenience.

Suppose
v_{0},v_{1}\in\mathcal{E}
are two solutions to
(
4.19
)
. We consider the geodesic of the symplectic potentials
v_{t}=(1-t)v_{0}+tv_{1}
for
t\in[0,1]
. It is straightforward that
v_{t}
satisfies Definition
4.11
(i) (ii) and (iv). Moreover, (iii) also holds, as shown in
[
19
, Lemma 3.6]
. Therefore,
v_{t}\in\mathcal{E}
for any
t\in[0,1]
.

Denoting the Legendre transformation of
v_{t}
by
\phi_{t}
, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{1}(v_{t})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\phi_{t}(y)}\,dy

and the Ding functional:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(v_{t})=\frac{1}{\mathcal{D}_{1}(v_{t})}\int_{P}v_{t}e^{-\langle b_{X},x\rangle}\,dx-\log\mathcal{D}_{1}(v_{t}).

We first assume
w:=v_{1}-v_{0}
is compactly supported. By the general formula
\dot{v_{t}}(x)+\dot{\phi_{t}}(\nabla v_{t}(x))=0
, a direct computation shows that

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\mathcal{D}_{1}(v_{t})=\int_{P}we^{-\langle b_{X},x\rangle}\,dx

and hence

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\mathcal{D}(v_{t})=0.

(4.20)

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=1}\mathcal{D}(v_{t})=0.

(4.21)

By the convexity and rigidity of the Ding functional (cf.
[
10
, Proposition 2.14]
and
[
19
, Proposition 3.9]
), it follows from (
4.20
) and (
4.21
) that
v_{1}=v_{0}+L
, where
L
is a linear function on
P
.

In the general case when
w
is not compactly supported, one can use a sequence of compactly supported functions to approximate
w
and the value of
\mathcal{D}(v_{t})
; see
[
19
, Lemma 3.10]
for details.

In sum, the proof is complete.
∎

If we apply Proposition
4.13
to
u
and
u^{\prime}
, we conclude that
u=u^{\prime}+L
for a linear function
L
on
P
. Therefore, under the momentum-angle coordinates
\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n};t_{1},\cdots,t_{n}\}
, it follows from (
3.8
) that
g_{1}=g_{2}
on
\mathring{M_{P}}
. Since both
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
are complete, it implies that
g_{1}=g_{2}
on
M_{P}
. Now, we define
\Psi:=\psi\circ\varphi^{-1}:M\to M
. Then, it is clear that
\Psi^{*}g^{\prime}=g
. Moreover, since
\Psi^{*}\omega^{\prime}=\omega
, it follows that
\Psi
is a
\mathbb{T}^{n}
-invariant self-biholomorphism of
(M,J)
. In sum, we have completed the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2

Suppose
(M^{2n},J,\mathbb{T}^{n})
is a complex orbifold with an effective and holomorphic
\mathbb{T}^{n}
-action. Moreover, we assume
(M,g,J,f)
is a Kähler Ricci shrinker orbifold with
X=J\nabla f\in\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}
and bounded Ricci curvature.

Let
\mathfrak{aut}^{X}(M)
be the Lie algebra of real holomorphic vector fields on
M
that commute with
JX
and hence
X
, and let
\mathfrak{g}^{X}
be the Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{aut}^{X}(M)
which is also Killing. The following Matsushima-type theorem is proved in
[
21
, Theorem 5.1]
for the manifold case and can be generalized to the orbifold case by the same proof. Note that the only difference in the proof is the choice of the cutoff function, which, in the orbifold case, can be chosen as in the proof of Proposition
3.7
as
\eta(f/A)
.

Theorem 4.14

(Matsushima theorem)
.

With the above assumptions, one has the decomposition:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{aut}^{X}(M)=\mathfrak{g}^{X}\oplus J\mathfrak{g}^{X}.

Furthermore, we can show as
[
21
, Proposition 5.6, Proposition 5.9]
that
\mathfrak{aut}^{X}(M)
is the Lie algebra of the finitely-dimensional Lie group
\mathrm{Aut}_{0}^{X}(M)
, which is the identity component of the group consists of all holomorphic automorphisms of
M
that commute with the flow of
JX
. In addition,
\mathfrak{g}^{X}
is the Lie algebra of a connected Lie group
G_{0}^{X}
, which is a maximal compact Lie subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}_{0}^{X}(M)
.
Using the Matsushima theorem, we have the following Lemma similar to
[
19
, Lemma 4.1]
.

Lemma 4.15
.

There exists a biholomorphism
\varphi:(M,J)\to(M,J)
such that
\varphi^{*}g
is
\mathbb{T}^{n}
-invariant.

Proof.

By our assumption,
[Y,X]=[Y,JX]=0
for any
Y\in\mathbf{\mathfrak{t}}
. Then, the holomorphic action of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
gives
\mathbb{T}^{n}\subset\mathrm{Aut}_{0}^{X}(M)
. Then,
\mathbb{T}^{n}
is contained in a maximal compact subgroup
G
of
\mathrm{Aut}_{0}^{X}(M)
. By the Iwasawa’s theorem
[
31
]
, all maximal compact subgroups are conjugate, and hence there exists
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}_{0}^{X}(M)
so that
\varphi\circ\mathbb{T}^{n}\circ\varphi^{-1}\leq G_{0}^{X}
. Therefore,
\varphi^{*}g
is
\mathbb{T}^{n}
-invariant.
∎

With Lemma
4.15
and Theorem
1.1
, the proof of Theorem
1.2
is straightforward.

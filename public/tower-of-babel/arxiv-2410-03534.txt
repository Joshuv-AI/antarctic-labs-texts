Characterizations, Dynamical Systems and Gradient Methods for Strongly Quasiconvex Functions
Felipe Lara, Raúl T. Marcavillaca, Phan T. Vuong

Abstract
We study differentiable strongly quasiconvex functions for providing new properties for algorithmic and monotonicity purposes. Furthemore, we provide insights into the decreasing behaviour of strongly quasiconvex functions, applying this for establishing exponential convergence for first- and second-order gradient systems without relying on the usual Lipschitz continuity assumption on the gradient of the function. The explicit discretization of the first-order dynamical system leads to the gradient descent method while discretization of the second-order dynamical system with viscous damping recovers the heavy ball method. We establish the linear convergence of both methods under suitable conditions on the parameters as well as comparisons with other classes of nonconvex functions used in the gradient descent literature. Keywords : Nonconvex optimization; Quasiconvex function; Dynamical systems; Gradient descent; Heavy ball method; Linear convergence.



1
Introduction

Given a differentiable function
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
we are interested in solving the following minimization problem:

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h(x),

numerically via gradient methods, who simplest version takes the form:
Take
x^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and compute

x^{k+1}=\,x^{k}-\beta_{k}\nabla h(x^{k}),~\forall~k\geq 0,

(1)

where
\{\beta_{k}\}_{k}
is a sequence of positive stepsizes to be
chosen.

The gradient method and its accelerated versions are fundamental and outstanding methods for finding local minimum points of differentiable functions. These methods are particularly valuable for classes of functions where every local minimum is also a global minimum, such as in (strongly) convex functions. The convergence properties of gradient-type methods for solving (strongly) convex functions are well understood
[
26
,
28
]
(see also
[
1
,
2
,
8
]
among others).

In order to extend the good convergence rate properties of the gradient-type
methods for classes of nonconvex functions, a natural step is to consider
classes of generalized convex functions as, for instance, classes of
quasiconvex functions having the same useful property that every local
minimum is global minimum (e.g., pseudoconvexity, strong quasiconvexity,
etc). A crucial step in achieving this is to establish a decreasing property based on the gradient of these generalized convex functions, similar to the case of strongly convex functions.

In the case of differentiable quasiconvex functions, the cornerstone result
is due to Arrow and Enthoven in
[
6
]
(see also
[
7
, Theorem
3.11]
), in which they characterized differentiable quasiconvex
functions via a decreasing property for all points belonging to a specific
sublevel set of the function. As a consequence of this result, a differentiable
function is quasiconvex (resp. pseudoconvex) if and only if its gradient is
quasimonotone (resp. pseudomonotone), see
[
11
,
14
]
for
instance. However, these decreasing properties alone are insufficient to guarantee exponential convergence to a local solution in continuous gradient descent, or a linear convergence rate in gradient-type methods. This limitation is highlighted in results like
[
9
, Theorem 4.1]
,
[
12
, Theorems 1 and 2]
, and
[
31
, Theorem 3.3]
, where convergence to critical points was shown under a quasiconvexity assumption. Thus, a stronger form of quasiconvexity must be assumed.

A promising and natural extension of strongly convex functions that could ensure good convergence rates for gradient methods is the class of strongly quasiconvex functions, introduced by Polyak in
[
29
]
. Members of this class possess the valuable property that every local minimum is also a global minimum. It is worth noting that every strongly convex function is strongly quasiconvex, although the reverse is not true in general. Despite this, no gradient methods had been developed for this class of nonconvex functions for a long time, probably because the existence of solutions to the problem of minimizing strongly quasiconvex functions was uncertain until the recent contribution
[
21
]
, which provided a fresh perspective on this class of functions.

Therefore, a natural subsequent step is to determine whether gradient-type methods can ensure convergence to the unique solution of the minimization problem for a differentiable strongly quasiconvex function. If so, the next objective is to establish the convergence rate. This precisely defines the motivation for our research.

As a first contribution, we study
differentiable quasiconvex and strongly quasiconvex functions via the
behaviour of their gradients. Furthermore, these results
provide us a decreasing property which is almost as good as the one for strongly convex functions and allow us to provide relationships between
differentiable strongly quasiconvex functions with other classes of nonconvex
functions used in gradient type methods such as quasi-strongly convex
[
25
]

and functions for which the Polyak-Łojasiewicz property holds
(see
[
23
,
27
]
).

As a second contribution, we leverage the decreasing property to establish exponential convergence for both continuous first- and second- order dynamical systems, without requiring Lipschitz continuity of the gradient. Finally, by considering the explicit discretization of these dynamical systems, we obtain the gradient descent method and the Polyak’s heavy-ball method
[
28
]
, respectively for minimizing strongly quasiconvex functions. We establish the linear convergence
rate for both methods under suitable conditions on the parameters.

The structure of the paper is as follows: In Section
2
, we present
preliminaries and basic definitions regarding generalized convexity and nonsmooth analysis. In Section
3
, we study differentiable strongly quasiconvex functions via
the behaviour of their gradients and, as a consequence, a new generalized
monotonicity notion is proposed. In Section
4
, we provide exponential
convergence for the first order continuous steepest descent dynamical system
and linear convergence rate for the gradient method under a locally Lipschitz
assumption on the gradient of the function. Finally, in Section
5
, we
establish exponential convergence for the second order continuous dynamical
system by assuming a weaker assumption than Lipschitz continuity on the
gradient of the function and, furthermore, we show the linear convergence
rate for the corresponding Heavy ball method.

Preliminaries

The inner product in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the Euclidean norm are
denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and
\lVert\cdot\rVert
,
respectively. The set
]0,+\infty[
is denoted by
\mathbb{R}_{++}
.
Given any
x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
the following relations hold:

\displaystyle\lVert\beta x+(1-\beta)y\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\beta\lVert x\rVert^{2}+(1-\beta)\lVert y\rVert^{2}-\beta(1-\beta)\lVert x-y\rVert^{2},

(2)

\displaystyle\langle x-z,y-x\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lVert z-y\rVert^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\lVert x-z\rVert^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\lVert y-x\rVert^{2}.

(3)

Given any extended-valued function
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, the effective domain of
h
is defined by

{\rm dom}\,h:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:h(x)<+\infty\}
. It is said
that
h
is proper if
{\rm dom}\,h
is nonempty and
h(x)>-\infty
for
all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The notion of properness is important when
dealing with minimization problems.

It is indicated by
{\rm epi}\,h:=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}:h(x)\leq t\}
the epigraph of
h
, by
S_{\lambda}(h):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:h(x)\leq\lambda\}
the sublevel set of
h
at
the height
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and by
{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h
the set of all minimal points of
h
. A function

h
is lower semicontinuous at
\overline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

if for any sequence
\{x_{k}\}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
x_{k}\rightarrow\overline{x}
,
h(\overline{x})\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}h(x_{k})
. Furthermore, the current convention
\sup_{\emptyset}h:=-\infty
and
\inf_{\emptyset}h:=+\infty
is adopted.

A function
h
with convex domain is said to be

(a)

convex if, given any
x,y\in\mathrm{dom}\,h
, then

h(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)\leq\lambda h(x)+(1-\lambda)h(y),~\forall~\lambda\in[0,1],

(4)

(b)

strongly convex on
{\rm dom}\,h
with modulus
\gamma\in\,]0,+\infty[
if for all
x,y\in\mathrm{dom}\,h
and all

\lambda\in[0,1]
, we have

h(\lambda y+(1-\lambda)x)\leq\lambda h(y)+(1-\lambda)h(x)-\lambda(1-\lambda)\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert x-y\rVert^{2},

(5)

(c)

quasiconvex if, given any
x,y\in\mathrm{dom}\,h
, then

h(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)\leq\max\{h(x),h(y)\},~\forall~\lambda\in[0,1],

(6)

(d)

strongly quasiconvex on
{\rm dom}\,h
with modulus

\gamma\in\,]0,+\infty[
if for all
x,y\in\mathrm{dom}\,h

and all
\lambda\in[0,1]
, we have

h(\lambda y+(1-\lambda)x)\leq\max\{h(y),h(x)\}-\lambda(1-\lambda)\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert x-y\rVert^{2}.

(7)

It is said that
h
is strictly convex (resp. strictly
quasiconvex) if the inequality in (
4
) (resp.
(
6
)) is strict whenever
x\neq y
.

The relationship between all these notions is summarized below (we
denote quasiconvex by qcx):

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}{\rm strongly~convex}&\Longrightarrow&{\rm strictly~convex}&\Longrightarrow&{\rm convex}\\
\Downarrow&&\Downarrow&&\Downarrow\\
{\rm strongly~qcx}&\Longrightarrow&{\rm strictly~qcx}&\Longrightarrow&{\rm qcx}\end{array}

All the reverse statements do not hold in general (see

[
7
,
11
,
14
,
21
]
).

Before continuing, let us show some examples of strongly quasiconvex
functions which are not convex.

Remark 1
.

(i)

Let
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be given
by
h(x)=\sqrt{\lVert x\rVert}
. Clearly,
h
is nonconvex, but it is
strongly quasiconvex on any
\mathbb{B}(0,r)
,
r>0
, with modulus

\gamma=\frac{1}{5^{\frac{1}{4}}2^{\frac{5}{4}}r^{\frac{1}{2}}}

by
[
21
, Theorem 17]
.

(ii)

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be two symmetric
matrices,
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, and

h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the function given by:

h(x)=\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}:=\frac{\frac{1}{2}\langle Ax,x\rangle+\langle a,x\rangle+\alpha}{\frac{1}{2}\langle Bx,x\rangle+\langle b,x\rangle+\beta}.

(9)

Take
0<m<M
and define:

K:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:~m\leq g(x)\leq M\}.

If
A
is a positive definite matrix and at least one of the following
conditions holds:

(a)

B=0
(the null matrix),

(b)

f
is nonnegative on
K
and
B
is negative semidefinite,

(c)

f
is nonpositive on
K
and
B
is positive semidefinite,

then
h
is strongly quasiconvex on
K
with modulus
\gamma=\frac{\lambda_{\min}(A)}{M}
by
[
15
, Corollary 4.1]
, where

\lambda_{\min}(A)
is the minimum eigenvalue of
A
.

(iii)

Let
h_{1},h_{2}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

be two strongly quasiconvex functions with modulus
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}>0
, respectively. Then
h:=\max\{h_{1},h_{2}\}
is
strongly quasiconvex with modulus
\gamma:=\min\{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\}>0
(straightforward).

(iv)

Let
\alpha>0
and
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

be a strongly quasiconvex functions with modulus
\gamma>0
. Then
\alpha h
is
strongly quasiconvex with modulus
\gamma\alpha>0
(straightforward).

Recently, it was proved in
[
21
]
that any lsc strongly
quasiconvex function has an unique minimizer as we recall next:

Lemma 2
.

(
[
21
, Corollary 3]
)

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed and convex set and
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
a proper, lsc,
and strongly quasiconvex function on
K\subseteq{\rm dom}\,h

with modulus
\gamma>0
. Then,
{\rm argmin}_{K}h
is a singleton.

The literature on strongly quasiconvex functions has been increasing very fast in the last years and several kind of algorithms have been developed for them (see
[
13
,
15
,
17
,
21
,
22
]
).

A function
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
its said to be

L
-smooth on
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
L>0
, if it is differentiable on

K
and

\lVert\nabla h(x)-\nabla h(y)\rVert\leq L\lVert x-y\rVert,~\forall~x,y\in K.

(10)

For
L
-smooth functions, a fundamental result is the descent lemma

[
26
, Lemma 1.2.3]
, that is, if
h
is an
L
-smooth
function on a convex set
K
with value
L>0
, then for every

x,y\in K
, we have

\lvert h(y)-h(x)-\langle\nabla h(x),y-x\rangle\rvert\leq\frac{L}{2}\lVert x-y\rVert^{2}.

(11)

Another relevant result is the sufficient decrease lemma, which is given
as follows: Suppose that
h\in C_{L}^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(continuously
differentiable function with
L
-Lipschitz gradient,
L>0
). Then for any

x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\beta>0
, holds

\displaystyle h(x)-h(x-\beta\nabla\,h(x))\geq\beta\left(1-\frac{\beta L}{2}\right)\|\nabla\,h(x)\|^{2}.

(12)

Given a nonempty set
C
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a single-valued
operator
T:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
T
is said
to be:

(a)

monotone on
C
, if for all
x,y\in C
, we have

\langle T(y)-T(x),y-x\rangle\geq 0.

(13)

(b)

strongly monotone on
C
with modulus
\gamma>0
,
if for all
x,y\in C
, we have

\langle T(y)-T(x),y-x\rangle\geq\gamma\lVert y-x\rVert^{2}.

(14)

(c)

pseudomonotone on
C
, if for all
x,y\in C
, we have

\langle T(y),x-y\rangle\geq 0~\Longrightarrow~\langle T(x),y-x\rangle\leq 0.

(15)

(d)

strongly pseudomonotone on
C
with modulus

\gamma>0
, if for all
x,y\in C
, we have

\langle T(y),x-y\rangle\geq 0~\Longrightarrow~\langle T(x),y-x\rangle\leq-\gamma\lVert y-x\rVert^{2}.

(16)

(e)

quasimonotone on
C
, if for all
x,y\in C
, we have

\langle T(y),x-y\rangle>0~\Longrightarrow~\langle T(x),y-x\rangle\leq 0.

(17)

(f)

strongly quasimonotone on
C
with modulus
\gamma>0
, if for all
x,y\in C
, we have

\langle T(y),x-y\rangle>0~\Longrightarrow~\langle T(x),y-x\rangle\leq-\gamma\lVert y-x\rVert^{2}.

(18)

The relationship between all these notions is summarized below (we
denote monotone by mon):

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}{\rm strongly~monotone}&\Longrightarrow&{\rm strongly~pseudomonotone}&\Longrightarrow&{\rm strongly~quasimonotone}\\
\Downarrow&&\Downarrow&&\Downarrow&&\\
{\rm monotone}&\Longrightarrow&{\rm pseudomonotone}&\Longrightarrow&{\rm quasimonotone}&&\end{array}

while the reverse statements do not hold in general (see

[
11
,
14
]
).

3
Differentiable Strongly Quasiconvex Functions

We begin this section with an important result, which characterizes
differentiable strongly quasiconvex functions. This result is mentioned in
[
16
, Theorem 1]
as a consequence of more general results from
[
33
]
.

Theorem 3
.

(
[
33
, Theorems 2 and 6]
)

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex set and
h:K\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
a differentiable function. Then
h
is strongly
quasiconvex with modulus
\gamma\geq 0
if and only if for every

x,y\in K
, we have

h(x)\leq h(y)~\Longrightarrow~\langle\nabla h(y),x-y\rangle\leq-\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert y-x\rVert^{2}.

(19)

From Theorem
3
we recover the characterization given by Arrow and Enthoven in
[
6
]
(
\gamma=0
).

Corollary 4
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex set and
h:K\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
a differentiable function. Then
h
is
quasiconvex if and only if for every
x,y\in K
, we have

h(x)\leq h(y)~\Longrightarrow~\langle\nabla h(y),x-y\rangle\leq 0.

(20)

A straightforward sufficient condition for a differentiable strongly
quasiconvex function to be strongly convex is given below.

Corollary 5
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex set and
h:K\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
a differentiable and strongly quasiconvex function with
modulus
\gamma\geq 0
. If for every
x,y\in K
, we have

\langle\nabla h(x),x-y\rangle\geq 0,~\forall~x\in S_{h(y)}(h),

then
h
is strongly convex with modulus
\gamma\geq 0
.

Theorem
3
provided a better understanding of the
class of strongly quasiconvex functions since we can connect this class
with other well-known classes of nonconvex functions which are extremely
useful for algorithmic purposes.

A differentiable function
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is
said to be
\mu
-quasi-strongly convex,
\mu>0
, if (see
[
25
]
)

\langle\nabla h(x),x-x^{*}\rangle\geq h(x)-h(x^{*})+\frac{\mu}{2}\|x-x^{*}\|^{2},~\forall~x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(21)

where
x^{*}
denotes the projection of
x
onto
{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
. It is worth to mention that
\mu
-quasi-strongly
convex functions are not necessarily convex.

A sufficient condition for
\mu
-quasi-strongly convex functions for being
strongly quasiconvex is given below.

Proposition 6
.

Let
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a continuous
differentiable function. Assuming that
h
is
\mu
-quasi-strongly convex
function (
\mu>0
) and admits a unique minimizer, then
h
is strongly
quasiconvex with modulus
\mu>0
.

Proof.

Since
h
has a unique minimizer, say
\overline{x}
, it follows from
relation (
21
) that

\displaystyle\langle\nabla h(x),x-\overline{x}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq h(x)-h(\overline{x})+\frac{\mu}{2}\|x-\overline{x}\|^{2},

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\mu}{2}\|x-\overline{x}\|^{2},~\forall~x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Therefore,
h
is strongly quasiconvex with modulus
\mu>0
by Theorem

3
.
∎

Following
[
23
,
27
]
, we also recall that a differentiable function
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfies the
Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PL henceforth) property if there exists
\mu>0
such
that

\lVert\nabla h(x)\rVert^{2}\geq\mu(h(x)-h(\overline{x})),~\forall~x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(22)

where
\overline{x}\in{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
. PL property is
implied by strong convexity, but it allows for multiple minima and does
not require any convexity assumption (see
[
18
]
for instance).

Another useful consequence of Theorem
3
is given below (see also
[
20
, Theorem 2]
).

Proposition 7
.

Let
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a strongly quasiconvex function with modulus

\gamma>0
and differentiable with
L
-Lipschitz continuous gradient
(
L>0
). Then the PL property holds with modulus
\mu:=\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2L}>0
, that is,

\lVert\nabla h(x)\rVert^{2}\geq\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2L}(h(x)-h(\overline{x})),~\forall~x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(23)

where
\overline{x}={\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
.

Proof.

Using Theorem
3

and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert x-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}\leq\langle\nabla h(x),x-\overline{x}\rangle\leq\lVert\nabla h(x)\rVert\lVert x-\overline{x}\rVert

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\Longrightarrow\,\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert x-\overline{x}\rVert\leq\lVert\nabla h(x)\rVert,~\forall~x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Therefore, by using relation (
11
) and that

\nabla h(\overline{x})=0
, we have

\lVert\nabla h(x)\rVert^{2}\geq\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert x-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}\geq\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2L}(h(x)-h(\overline{x})),~\forall~x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 8
.

(i)

The converse statement in Proposition
7
does not
hold in general. Indeed, let us consider a system of nonlinear equations
as in
[
26
, Example 4.1.3]
:

\displaystyle g(x)=0,

(24)

with
g=(g_{1},\dots,g_{m}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
being a
differentiable vector function. Assuming that
m\leq n
and (
24
)
admits a solution, say
x^{*}
, we consider the function

h(x)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}g_{i}^{2}(x).

Let us assume in addition that
\mu:=\inf_{x\in K}\lambda_{\text{min}}J(x)J^{T}(x)>0
, where
K
is some convex set containing
x^{*}
and

J^{T}(x)=(\nabla g_{1}(x),\dots,\nabla g_{m}(x)).

Note that

\|\nabla h(x)\|^{2}=\langle J(x)J^{T}(x)g(x),g(x)\rangle\geq\mu(g(x))^{2}=\mu(h(x)-h(x^{*})).

Thus,
h
satisfies the PL property, however, if
m<n
, then the set of
solutions is not necessarily a singleton, i.e.,
h
may not be strongly
quasiconvex by Lemma
2
.

(ii)

The function
h:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

given by
h(x)=x^{2}+3\sin^{2}x
is an example of a strongly
quasiconvex function satisfying the PL property and without being convex.

In the following statement, we characterize differentiable strongly
quasiconvex functions via a generalized monotonicity notion for their
gradient. We emphasize that the generalized monotonicity property in
relation (
25
) (see below) is new.

Proposition 9
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex set and
h:K\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
a differentiable function. If
h
is strongly quasiconvex
with modulus
\gamma\geq 0
, then for every
x,y\in K
, we have

\langle\nabla h(x),y-x\rangle>-\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert y-x\rVert^{2}~\Longrightarrow~\langle\nabla h(y),x-y\rangle\leq-\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert y-x\rVert^{2}.

(25)

Proof.

Since
h
is strongly quasiconvex with modulus

\gamma\geq 0
, by Theorem
3
we have for every

x,y\in K
that

h(x)\leq h(y)~\Longrightarrow~\langle\nabla h(y),x-y\rangle\leq-\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert y-x\rVert^{2}.

By interchanging
x
and
y
, we have

h(y)\leq h(x)~\Longrightarrow~\langle\nabla h(x),y-x\rangle\leq-\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert y-x\rVert^{2}.

(26)

Thus, if
\langle\nabla h(x),y-x\rangle>-\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert y-x\rVert^{2}
with
x\neq y
, then
h(y)>h(x)
by (
26
).
Using (
19
) we obtain
\langle\nabla h(y),x-y\rangle\leq-\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert y-x\rVert^{2}
, so (
25
) holds.
∎

Remark 10
.

The reverse statement in Proposition
9
remains open, and we do not yet have a counter-example. Furthermore, if
\gamma=0
, then Proposition
9
reduces to the following characterization for differentiable quasiconvex functions:
h
is quasiconvex if and only if
\nabla h
is quasimonotone (see, for instance,
[
11
, Theorem 5.5.2
(b)
]
).

If
K
is open and
\gamma>0
, then we can say something more.

Proposition 11
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open convex and
h:K\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

a differentiable function. Consider the following statements:

(a)

h
is strongly quasiconvex with modulus
\gamma>0
.

(b)

For each
x,y\in K
, the following implication holds:

\langle\nabla h(x),y-x\rangle\geq-\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert y-x\rVert^{2}~\Longrightarrow~\langle\nabla h(y),x-y\rangle\leq-\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert y-x\rVert^{2}.

(27)

(c)

For each
x,y\in K
, the following implication holds:

\langle\nabla h(x),y-x\rangle>-\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert y-x\rVert^{2}~\Longrightarrow~\langle\nabla h(y),x-y\rangle\leq-\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert y-x\rVert^{2}.

(28)

Then,

(a)~\Longrightarrow~(b)~\Longrightarrow~(c).

Proof.

(a)\Rightarrow(c)
is Proposition
9
while

(b)\Rightarrow(c)
is straightforward.

(a)\Rightarrow(b)
: Since
(a)\Rightarrow(c)
, we only need to analyze the case when
\langle\nabla h(x),y-x\rangle=-\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert y-x\rVert^{2}
. Since
K
is open, take

t>0
small enough such that
y_{t}:=y+t(y-x)\in K
. Then,

\displaystyle\langle\nabla h(x),y_{t}-x\rangle

\displaystyle=(1+t)\langle\nabla h(x),y-x\rangle=-(1+t)\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert y-x\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\gamma}{2(1+t)}\lVert y_{t}-x\rVert^{2}>-\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert y_{t}-x\rVert^{2}.

(29)

Since
(a)
holds, from Theorem
3
we have
\langle\nabla h(x),y_{t}-x\rangle>-\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert y_{t}-x\rVert^{2}

. Since

is continuous,

, and by using Theorem

(

).

Remark 12
.

The reverse statement in Proposition

Before continuing, we recall the following definition.

Definition 13
.

(
[

Let

Remark 14
.

(i)

Note that every strongly convex function is strongly
quasiconvex, and every strongly quasiconvex function is sharply
quasiconvex.

(ii)

If
\nabla h
is strongly monotone with modulus
\gamma\geq 0
, then satisfies relation (

). Indeed, let
x,y\in K

such that
\langle\nabla h(y)-\nabla h(x),y-x\rangle\geq\gamma\lVert y-x\rVert^{2}

\langle\nabla h(y),y-x\rangle-\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert y-x\rVert^{2}\geq\langle\nabla h(x),y-x\rangle+\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert y-x\rVert^{2}.

Hence, if

, then
\langle\nabla h(y),y-x\rangle>\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert y-x\rVert^{2}

satisfies
(

).

Furthermore, clearly, if
\nabla h
satisfies (

), then

\nabla h
is strongly pseudomonotone with modulus

.

In order to close the relationships between all these notions, we resume
the results below.

Proposition 15
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex set,

be differentiable function and
\gamma\geq 0
. Then

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}h\,{\rm is}\,\gamma-{\rm strongly\,convex}&\Longrightarrow&h\,{\rm is}\,\gamma-{\rm strongly\,qcx}&\Longrightarrow&h\,{\rm is}\,\gamma-{\rm sharply}.\\
\Updownarrow&&\Downarrow&&\Updownarrow&\\
\nabla h\,{\rm is}\,\gamma-{\rm strongly\,mon}&\Longrightarrow&\nabla h\,{\rm satisfies}\,\eqref{new:mon}&\Longrightarrow&\nabla h\,{\rm is}\,\frac{\gamma}{2}-{\rm strongly\,pseudo}&\end{array}

Proof.

We simple note that

is strongly pseudomonotone
with modulus

by

while the case when

4
First-Order Gradient Dynamics

In this section, we study the minimization problem

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(x).

(31)

where
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuously differentiable
function. Our goal is the study of problem (
31
) via a first order
dynamical system and its corresponding explicit discretization and, in virtue
of Proposition
7
, without assuming a Lipschitz continuity property
on the gradient of the function.

4.1
Continuous Dynamics

We consider the following dynamical system:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dot{x}(t)+\nabla h(x(t)))=0,\,t>0,\\[5.69054pt]
x(t_{0})=x_{0}.\end{array}\right.

In the following theorem, we provide exponential convergence of the
trajectories generated by (
4.1
) to the unique solution of
problem (
31
).

Theorem 16
.

Let the function
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be
continuously differentiable and strongly quasiconvex with modulus

\gamma>0
and
\overline{x}={\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
.
Then the following assertions hold:

(a)

t\mapsto h(x(t))
is nonincreasing.

(b)

Any trajectory
x(t)
to the dynamical system
(
4.1
) satisfy that

\lVert x(t)-\overline{x}\rVert\leq\lVert x_{0}-\overline{x}\rVert e^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}t},

(34)

i.e.,
x(t)
converges exponentially to the unique solution of
(
31
);

(c)

For any trajectory
x(t)
to the dynamical system (
4.1
)
there exists
T>0
and
L>0
such that

h(x(t))-h(\overline{x})\leq\min\left\{\frac{L}{2}\lVert x_{0}-\overline{x}\rVert e^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}t},\left(h(x_{0})-h(\overline{x})\right)e^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2L}t}\right\},~\forall~t\geq T,

(35)

as a consequence,
h(x(t))
converges exponentially to
h^{*}=\min_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h
.

Proof.

(a)
: Note that for each
t>t_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}h(x(t))=\langle\nabla h(x(t)),\dot{x}(t)\rangle=-\|\nabla h(x(t))\|^{2}\leq 0.

(36)

Hence,
h
decreases along the solutions and does so strictly unless
it hits a critical point.

(b)
: Let
\overline{x}={\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h
. Then
for every
t\in[0,+\infty[
, we define the auxiliar function
E(t):=\frac{1}{2}\|x(t)-\overline{x}\|^{2}
. Then

\displaystyle\dot{E}(t)=\langle x(t)-\overline{x},\dot{x}(t)\rangle=-\langle x(t)-\overline{x},\nabla h(x(t))\rangle.

Since
\overline{x}={\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h
and
h
is
strongly quasiconvex with modulus
\gamma\geq 0
, it follows from
Theorem
3
that

\displaystyle\langle\nabla h(x(t)),x(t)-\overline{x}\rangle\geq\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert x(t)-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}.

(37)

Hence

\dot{E}(t)\leq-\gamma E(t).

By integrating, we obtain

E(t)\leq E(t_{0})e^{-\gamma t}~\Longleftrightarrow~\lVert x(t)-\overline{x}\rVert\leq\lVert x_{0}-\overline{x}\rVert e^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}t}.

(38)

(c)
: We know that any continuously differentiable function is locally
Lipschitz continuous. Hence, since
x(t)
converges exponentially to the
unique solution
\overline{x}(t)
, for some
T
sufficiently large, the
function
h
is Lipschitz continuous around
\overline{x}(t)
. Therefore,
there exists
L>0
such that

h(x(t))-h(\overline{x})\leq\frac{L}{2}\lVert x(t)-\overline{x}\rVert\leq\frac{L}{2}\lVert x_{0}-\overline{x}\rVert e^{-\gamma t},

(39)

for all
t\geq T
.

Moreover, using (
36
) and (
22
), we have

\frac{d}{dt}\left(h(x(t))-h(\overline{x})\right)=\langle\nabla h(x(t)),\dot{x}(t)\rangle=-\lVert\nabla\,h(x(t))\rVert^{2}\leq-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2L}\left(h(x(t))-h(\overline{x})\right).

Integrating, we have

h(x(t))-h(\overline{x})\leq\left(h(x_{0})-h(\overline{x})\right)e^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2L}t}.

(40)

Finally, relation (
35
) follows from (
39
) and
(
40
).
∎

4.2
Steepest Descent Method

As a discretization version of (
4.1
), let us consider the classical
gradient descent method (see, for instance,
[
26
, page 68]
).

Algorithm 1

The Gradient Method

Step 0.

Take
x^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
k=0
and a
sequence
\{\beta_{k}\}_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{++}
.

Step 1.

Compute
\nabla h(x^{k})
and

\displaystyle x^{k+1}=x^{k}-\beta_{k}\nabla h(x^{k}).

(41)

Step 2.

If
x^{k+1}=x^{k}
, then STOP,
x^{k}\in{\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
. Otherwise, take
k=k+1
and go to
Step 1.

Before continuing with the convergence analysis, we note the following.

Remark 17
.

Let
h
be a differentiable strongly quasiconvex function with modulus

\gamma>0
. If
\overline{x}={\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
,
then it follows from Theorem
3
that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\langle\nabla h(x^{k}),\overline{x}-x^{k}\rangle\leq-\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert x^{k}-\overline{x}\rVert^{2},

(42)

where
\{x^{k}\}_{k}
is the sequence generate by Algorithm
1
.

In order to study the convergence of Algorithm
1
under a locally
Lipschitz continuity property for the gradient of
h
, we note also the
following.

Remark 18
.

(i)

Assuming that
h
has a Lipschitz continuous gradient with
modulus
L>0
and
0<\beta_{k}\leq\frac{2}{L}
, it follows from (
12
) and (
41
) that

\displaystyle h(x^{k})-h(x^{k+1})\geq\beta_{k}\left(1-\frac{\beta_{k}L}{2}\right)\|\nabla\,h(x^{k})\|^{2}\geq 0,~\forall~k\in\mathbb{N}.

(43)

(ii)

Since
h
is strongly quasiconvex, its level set are compact
by
[
21
, Theorem 1]
, thus given any starting point
x^{0}\in K
,
the set
S_{h(x^{0})}(h)
is compact. Furthermore, if
\nabla h
is
assumed to be locally Lipschitz continuous, it is Lipschitz continuous on
bounded sets, in particular, there exists
L_{0}>0
such that
\nabla h

is Lipschitz continuous on
S_{h(x^{0})}(h)
. Hence, if
\beta_{k}\leq\frac{2}{L_{0}}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the sequence
\{x^{k}\}_{k}\subseteq S_{h(x^{0})}(h)
by part
(i)
of this Remark, thus

\{x^{k}\}_{k}
is bounded too, and following
[
24
]
, we
know that
\nabla h
is Lipschitz continuous on
\mathcal{C}=\overline{{\rm{conv}}}\{\overline{x},x^{0},x^{1},\ldots\}
. Hence,

\|\nabla h(x^{k+1})-\nabla h(x^{k})\|\leq L_{0}\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|,~\forall~k\in\mathbb{N}.

Therefore, for Algorithm
1
, we may assume that
h
has a locally
Lipschitz continuous gradient.

By using the previous remark, we obtain the following.

Proposition 19
.

Let
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a strongly
quasiconvex function with modulus
\gamma>0
and differentiable
with locally Lipschitz continuous gradient,
\{\beta_{k}\}_{k}
be a
positive sequence such that
0<\beta_{k}\leq\min\left\{\frac{\gamma}{L_{0}^{2}},\frac{2}{L_{0}}\right\}
, then for every

k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\|x^{k+1}-\overline{x}\|^{2}\leq\left(1-\beta_{k}(\gamma-\beta_{k}L_{0}^{2})\right)\|x^{k}-\overline{x}\|^{2},

(44)

where
\{x^{k}\}_{k}
is the sequence generate by Algorithm
1
.

Proof.

From (
41
), (
19
) and the fact that
\nabla h(\overline{x})=0
, we have

\displaystyle\|x^{k+1}-\overline{x}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|x^{k}-\beta_{k}\nabla h(x^{k})-\overline{x}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|x^{k}-\overline{x}\|^{2}-2\beta_{k}\left<\nabla h(x^{k}),x^{k}-\overline{x}\right>+\beta_{k}^{2}\|\nabla h(x^{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|x^{k}-\overline{x}\|^{2}-\gamma\beta_{k}\|x^{k}-\overline{x}\|^{2}+\beta_{k}^{2}\|\nabla h(x^{k})-\nabla h(\overline{x})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|x^{k}-\overline{x}\|^{2}-\gamma\beta_{k}\|x^{k}-\overline{x}\|^{2}+\beta_{k}^{2}L_{0}^{2}\|x^{k}-\overline{x}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-\beta_{k}(\gamma-\beta_{k}L_{0}^{2})\right)\|x^{k}-\overline{x}\|^{2},

and the result follows.
∎

As a consequence, we obtain the convergence result.

Theorem 20
.

Let
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a strongly
quasiconvex function with modulus
\gamma>0
and differentiable
with locally Lipschitz continuous gradient. Let
\overline{x}={\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
and
\{\beta_{k}\}_{k}
be a
positive sequence satisfying

0<\underline{\beta}\leq\beta_{k}\leq\overline{\beta}<\min\left\{\frac{\gamma}{L_{0}^{2}},\frac{2}{L_{0}}\right\}.

(45)

Then the sequence
\{x^{k}\}_{k}
, generated by Algorithm
1
, converges linearly to the unique solution
\overline{x}
.

Proof.

From (
44
) and (
45
) we have

\displaystyle\|x^{k+1}-\overline{x}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1-\beta_{k}(\gamma-\beta_{k}L_{0}^{2})\right)\|x^{k}-\overline{x}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1-\underline{\beta}(\gamma-\overline{\beta}L_{0}^{2})\right)\|x^{k}-\overline{x}\|^{2},

which implies that
\{x^{k}\}_{k}
converges linearly to the unique
solution
\overline{x}
with a linear rate of at least
q=\sqrt{1-\underline{\beta}(\gamma-\overline{\beta}L_{0}^{2})}\in\,]0,1[
.
∎

.

Note that if the function
h

\gamma>0

Assume that hypotheses of Theorem fulfilled of Theorem

holds,
\gamma<2L_{0}
and

\displaystyle h(x^{k})-h(\overline{x})\leq\left(1-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4L_{0}^{2}}\right)^{k-1}\|x^{0}-\overline{x}\|^{2},

\displaystyle h(x^{k})-h(\overline{x})\leq\left(1-\frac{\gamma^{3}}{4L_{0}^{3}}\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{4L_{0}}\right)\right)^{k-1}(h(x^{0})-h(\overline{x}))

5
Second-Order Gradient Dynamic and Discretization

As in the previous section, we will study problem (
31
) via a second
order dynamical system without assuming a Lipschitz continuity property
on the gradient of the function.

5.1
Continuous dynamical system

We consider the following gradient dynamical system:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\ddot{x}(t)+\alpha\dot{x}(t)+\nabla h(x(t))=0,\,t>0,\\[5.69054pt]
x(0)=x_{0},\quad\dot{x}(0)=v_{0}.\end{array}\right.

For the second-order continuous gradient dynamic (
5.1
), we
provide exponential convergence to a solution of problem (
31
) when
h
is strongly quasiconvex.
We emphasize that in contrast to requiring Lipschitz continuity assumptions
on the gradient of
h
, we use a weaker hypothesis: there exists

\kappa\in\,]0,+\infty[
such that for every trajectory
x(t)
of the
dynamical system (
5.1
) we have

\langle\nabla h(x(t)),x(t)-\overline{x}\rangle\geq\kappa(h(x(t))-h(\overline{x})),

(50)

where
\overline{x}={\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h
. This assumption
holds trivially with
\kappa=1
for convex and strongly convex functions,
hence can be considered as a generalized convexity assumption. Assumption
(
50
) was used in
[
10
]
, and later in

[
3
,
5
]
with the stronger requirement that
\kappa\geq 1
.

Note that if the function
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is
differentiable with
L
-Lipschitz gradient and strongly quasiconvex with
modulus
\gamma>0
, then it follows from (
19
),
(
11
) and
\nabla h(\overline{x})=0
that

\langle\nabla h(x),x-\overline{x}\rangle\geq\frac{\gamma}{2}\lVert x-\overline{x}\rVert^{2}\geq\frac{\gamma}{L}(h(x)-h(\overline{x})),

which implies (
50
) with
\kappa=\frac{\gamma}{L}
.

Theorem 23
.

Let
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a differentiable and
strongly quasiconvex function with modulus
\gamma>0
and
\overline{x}={\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h
. Suppose that assumption
(
50
) holds. Then any trajectory
x(t)
generated by
(
5.1
) converges exponentially to the unique solution
\overline{x}

of problem (
31
), and the function values
h(x(t))
converge exponentially to the optimal value
h(\overline{x})
.

Proof.

Let
h^{*}=\min_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,h
, we consider the following
energy (Lyapunov) function

\Sigma(t):=h(x(t))-h^{*}+\frac{1}{2}\|\lambda(x(t)-\overline{x})+\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{\xi}{2}\|x(t)-\overline{x}\|^{2},

where
\lambda
and
\xi
are some positive parameters to be determined
later. Differentiating the energy function
\Sigma
we have

\dot{\Sigma}(t)=\langle\nabla h(x(t)),\dot{x}(t)\rangle+\langle\lambda(x(t)-\overline{x})+\dot{x}(t),\lambda\dot{x}(t)+\ddot{x}(t)\rangle+\xi\langle x(t)-\overline{x},\dot{x}(t)\rangle.

Combining with (
5.1
) we deduce

\dot{\Sigma}(t)=-\lambda\langle\nabla h(x(t)),x(t)-\overline{x}\rangle+(\lambda-\alpha)\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+(\xi+\lambda(\lambda-\alpha))\langle x(t)-\overline{x},\dot{x}(t)\rangle.

On the other hand, from (
19
) (see Theorem

3
) and (
50
) we can have

-\lambda\langle\nabla h(x(t)),x(t)-\overline{x}\rangle\leq-\lambda\left(\frac{\gamma}{4}\|x(t)-\overline{x}\|^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{2}(h(x(t))-h^{*})\right).

Hence, by combining the last two inequalities, we have

\displaystyle\dot{\Sigma}(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\lambda\left(\frac{\gamma}{4}\|x(t)-\overline{x}\|^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{2}(h(x(t))-h^{*})\right)

\displaystyle\quad+(\lambda-\alpha)\|\dot{x}(t)\|^{2}+(\xi+\lambda(\lambda-\alpha))\langle x(t)-\overline{x},\dot{x}(t)\rangle,

which implies

\displaystyle\dot{\Sigma}(t)+\frac{\lambda\kappa}{2}\Sigma(t)\leq\frac{\lambda}{4}\left(\kappa\lambda^{2}+\kappa\xi-\gamma\right)\|x(t)-\overline{x}\|^{2}+\left(\lambda+\frac{\lambda\kappa}{4}-\alpha\right)\|\dot{x}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(\xi+\lambda(\lambda-\alpha)+\frac{\lambda^{2}\kappa}{2}\right)\langle x(t)-\overline{x},\dot{x}\rangle.

(51)

To establish the exponential convergence of
\Sigma(t)
to
0
,
it remains to choose the positive parameters
\lambda
and
\xi
such
that the right-hand side of (
51
) is non-positive.

For simplicity, let us choose
\xi=\lambda^{2}
then we would require

\kappa\lambda^{2}+\kappa\xi-\gamma=2\kappa\lambda^{2}-\gamma\leq 0,

\lambda+\frac{\lambda\kappa}{4}-\alpha\leq 0,

and

\xi+\lambda(\lambda-\alpha)+\frac{\lambda^{2}\kappa}{2}=\left(2+\frac{\kappa}{2}\right)\lambda^{2}-\alpha\lambda\leq 0.

The above inequalities hold whenever
\lambda\leq\min\{\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{2\kappa}},\frac{2\alpha}{\kappa+4}\}
.

With this choice of parameters, we can deduce from (
51
) that

\Sigma(t)
converges exponentially to
0
. Indeed, from (
51
)
we have

\displaystyle\dot{\Sigma}(t)+\frac{\lambda\kappa}{2}\Sigma(t)\leq 0.

Then applying Gronwall’s inequality, we obtain

\Sigma(t)\leq\Sigma(0)\,e^{-\frac{\lambda\kappa}{2}t}.

Since

h(x(t))-h(\overline{x})+\frac{\xi}{2}\|x(t)-\overline{x}\|^{2}\leq\Sigma(t),

it is clear that
x(t)
converges exponentially to the unique solution

\overline{x}
and and the function values
h(x(t))
converges
exponentially to the optimal value
h(\overline{x})
.
∎

Remark 24
.

As a consequence of Theorem
23
, the following asymptotic
exponential convergence rate hold

\displaystyle h(x(t))-h^{*}=\mathcal{O}\left(e^{-\frac{\lambda\kappa}{2}t}\right),

(52)

\displaystyle\|x(t)-\overline{x}\|=\mathcal{O}\left(e^{-\frac{\lambda\kappa}{4}t}\right),

(53)

with
\lambda=\min\{\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{2\kappa}},\frac{2\alpha}{\kappa+4}\}
.
Note that we can always choose
\alpha>0
such that

\lambda=\min\left\{\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{2\kappa}},\frac{2\alpha}{\kappa+4}\right\}=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{2\kappa}}

and the convergence rate will be
\mathcal{O}\left(e^{-\sqrt{\gamma\kappa}t}\right)
for both objective values and trajectories. If

\kappa=1
(e.g. strongly convex functions), then this rate

\mathcal{O}\left(e^{-\sqrt{\gamma}t}\right)
is faster than the
one in the first-order dynamical system (
4.1
) (which is

\mathcal{O}\left(e^{-\gamma t}\right)
), whenever
\gamma\leq 1
.

5.2
Discrete system: gradient descent with momentum

Following
[
4
,
34
]
let us consider an explicit discretization of
(
5.1
). We take a time step
\eta>0
, and set

t_{k}:=k\eta
, and
x_{k}:=x(t_{k})
. By a finite-difference scheme
in (
5.1
) with centered second-order variation, one obtains

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\frac{x_{k+1}-2x_{k}+x_{k-1}}{\eta^{2}}+\frac{\alpha}{\eta}(x_{k}-x_{k-1})+\nabla h(x_{k})=0.

Rearranging the above relation, we get

x_{k+1}-x_{k}-(1-\alpha\eta)(x_{k}-x_{k-1})+\eta^{2}\nabla\,h(x^{k}).

Setting
\theta:=1-\alpha\eta
and
\beta:=\eta^{2}
, we have the
following well known Heavy-ball algorithmic scheme:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}x_{k+1}=x_{k}+\theta(x_{k}-x_{k-1})-\beta\nabla h(x_{k}).\end{array}

The linear convergence of the Heavy-ball algorithm is presented in
the following statement.

Theorem 25
.

Let
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a differentiable with

L
-Lipschitz gradient and strongly quasiconvex function with modulus

\gamma>0
,
\overline{x}={\rm argmin}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h
,
\theta\in\,]0,1[
and
\beta\in\,]0,\frac{1-\theta^{2}}{L}]
. Then the
sequence
\{x_{k}\}_{k}
, generated by the heavy ball method
(
5.2
), converges linearly to
\overline{x}
and the sequence

\{h(x_{k})\}_{k}
converges linearly to the optimal value
h^{*}=h(\overline{x})
.

Proof.

Since
\nabla h
is Lipschitz continuous, we have

\displaystyle h(x_{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h(x_{k})+\langle\nabla h(x_{k}),x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}.

(55)

From (
5.2
), we can substitute
x_{k+1}-x_{k}
by

\theta(x_{k}-x_{k-1})-\beta\nabla h(x_{k})
into
(
55
) to deduce

\displaystyle h(x_{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h(x_{k})+\langle\nabla h(x_{k}),\theta(x_{k}-x_{k-1})-\beta\nabla h(x_{k})\rangle

(56)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{L}{2}\|\theta(x_{k}-x_{k-1})-\beta\nabla h(x_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h(x_{k})-\beta\left(1-\frac{\beta L}{2}\right)\|\nabla h(x_{k})\|^{2}+\frac{L\theta^{2}}{2}\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\theta(1-\beta L)\langle\nabla h(x_{k}),x_{k}-x_{k-1}\rangle.

We also have

\displaystyle\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\theta(x_{k}-x_{k-1})-\beta\nabla h(x_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta^{2}\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}+\beta^{2}\|\nabla h(x_{k})\|^{2}-2\theta\beta\langle\nabla h(x_{k}),x_{k}-x_{k-1}\rangle

Multiplying both sides of the above equality with
\frac{1-\beta L}{2\beta}

and adding the results to (
56
), we obtain

h(x_{k+1})+\frac{1-\beta L}{2\beta}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}\leq h(x_{k})-\frac{\beta}{2}\|\nabla h(x_{k})\|^{2}+\frac{\theta^{2}}{2\beta}\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}.

Defining the energy function

E_{k}:=h(x_{k})-h^{*}+\frac{\theta^{2}}{2\beta}\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}

we can write the last inequality as

\displaystyle E_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle E_{k}-\frac{\beta}{2}\|\nabla h(x_{k})\|^{2}-\left(\frac{1-\beta L-\theta^{2}}{2\beta}\right)\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}

(57)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle E_{k}-\rho(\|\nabla h(x_{k})\|^{2}+\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}),

where
\rho=\min\{\frac{\beta}{2},\frac{1-\beta L-\theta^{2}}{2\beta}\}>0
.

It remains to bound
E_{k}
from
\|\nabla h(x_{k})\|^{2}+\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}
. It follows from Proposition
7
that

h(x_{k})-h^{*}\leq\frac{2L}{\gamma^{2}}\|\nabla h(x_{k})\|^{2}.

(58)

On the other hand, using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality again, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\beta}\theta^{2}\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\beta}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}+\beta\nabla h(x_{k})\|^{2}

(59)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}+\beta\|\nabla h(x_{k})\|^{2}.

Adding (
58
) and (
59
) we deduce

\displaystyle E_{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2L}{\gamma^{2}}+\beta\right)\|\nabla h(x_{k})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\beta}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sigma\left(\|\nabla h(x_{k})\|^{2}+\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}\right),

where
\sigma=\max\{\frac{2L}{\gamma^{2}}+\beta,\frac{1}{\beta}\}>0
.

Finally, combining the last inequality with (
57
)
we deduce

E_{k+1}\leq\left(1-\frac{\rho}{\sigma}\right)E_{k}\leq\left(1-\frac{\rho}{\sigma}\right)^{k}E_{1},

which means that the sequence
\{E_{k}\}_{k}
converges linearly to
0
. As a consequence, the sequence

\{h(x_{k})\}_{k}
converges linearly to the optimal value

h^{*}
and the sequence
\{\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|\}_{k}
converges linearly to
0
.
To deduce the linear convergence of
\{x_{k}\}_{k}
to
\overline{x}
, we note from (
5.2
) that

\beta\|\nabla h(x_{k})\|\leq\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|+\theta\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|,

which implies also that
\{\|\nabla h(x_{k})\|\}_{k}
converges linearly to
0
. Hence, it follows from (
44
)
that
\{x_{k}\}_{k}
converges linearly to
\overline{x}
.
∎

Corollary 26

(Convergence rate)
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
25
, we have
the following convergence rates

\displaystyle h(x_{k+1})-h^{*}\leq\left(1-\frac{\rho}{\sigma}\right)^{k}E_{1},

\displaystyle\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}\leq\frac{2\beta}{\theta^{2}}\left(1-\frac{\rho}{\sigma}\right)^{k}E_{1}.

Moreover, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla h(x_{k})\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(1+\theta)}{\theta}\left(1-\frac{\rho}{\sigma}\right)^{\frac{k-1}{2}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\beta}}\sqrt{E_{1}},

\displaystyle\|x_{k}-\overline{x}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2(1+\theta)}{\gamma\,\theta}\left(1-\frac{\rho}{\sigma}\right)^{\frac{k-1}{2}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\beta}}\sqrt{E_{1}},

where
\theta\in\,]0,1[
,
\beta\in\,]0,\frac{1-\theta^{2}}{L}]
,

\rho=\min\{\frac{\beta}{2},\frac{1-\beta L-\theta^{2}}{2\beta}\}
,

\sigma=\max\{\frac{2L}{\gamma^{2}}+\beta,\frac{1}{\beta}\}
and

E_{1}:=h(x_{0})-h^{*}+\frac{\theta^{2}}{2\beta}\|x_{1}-x_{0}\|^{2}
.

Remark 27
.

A second-order differential equation without viscous damping (i.e.
\alpha=0
) and gradient ascent (i.e.
\ddot{x}(t)=\nabla h(x(t))
) was recently
considered in
[
31
]
for (strongly) quasiconvex functions. The convergence
results for continuous and discrete time provided there were obtained under stronger
conditions.

6
Conclusions

We contributed to the discussion on strongly quasiconvex functions by studying the differentiable case. In particular, we developed new properties for differentiable strongly quasiconvex functions that were used for studying gradient dynamics in the first and second order as well as their discretizations for the gradient method and the heavy ball acceleration.

We hope that our contribution could provide new lights for further developments on accelerated versions of first order algorithms for generalized convex problems and, in particular, for strongly quasiconvex functions.

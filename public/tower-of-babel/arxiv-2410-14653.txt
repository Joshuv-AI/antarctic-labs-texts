Toric varieties modulo reflections
Colin Crowley, Tao Gong, and Connor Simpson

Abstract
. Let W W be a finite group generated by reflections of a lattice M M . If an M M -rational polytope P ⊂ M ⊗ ℤ ℝ P\subset M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R} is preserved by W W , then we show that the quotient of the projective toric variety X P X_{P} by W W is isomorphic to the toric variety X P ∩ D X_{P\cap D} , where D D is a fundamental domain for the action of W W . This answers a question of Horiguchi-Masuda-Shareshian-Song, and recovers results of Blume, of the second author, and of Gui-Hu-Liu. We also study quotients of real toric varieties, proving that X P ℝ / W X_{P}^{\mathbb{R}}/W is contractible when P P is a permutohedron.

Nonetheless, Lorenz

Let

A

generated by reflections.

[

The semigroup

Saturated affine semigroup algebras are precisely the coordinate rings of normal affine toric varieties

1.1

If

2.
Finite reflection groups

We review the theory of finite reflection groups, following
[
Hum90
]
.

2.1.

Let
V
be a real vector space equipped with a positive definite symmetric bilinear form
\left\langle,\right\rangle
.
For
R=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{R}
, we write
R\left\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\right\rangle
for the
R
-span of vectors
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}
(the use of angle brackets for both span and inner product should cause no confusion).
The
reflection
by a nonzero
\alpha\in V
is the linear transformation given by

s_{\alpha}(v):=v-2\frac{\left\langle v,\alpha\right\rangle}{\left\langle\alpha,\alpha\right\rangle}\alpha.

A
reflection group
in
V
is a subgroup of
\GL(V)
generated by reflections.
All reflection groups appearing in this paper are assumed finite.

2.2.

A
root system
is a finite set
\Phi\subset V
such that for all
\alpha\in\Phi
,

(i)

\phi\cap\mathbb{R}\left\langle\alpha\right\rangle=\{\alpha,-\alpha\}
, and

(ii)

s_{\alpha}(\Phi)=\Phi
.

Elements of
\Phi
are
roots
.
If
\Phi
is a root system, then the subgroup
W
of
\GL(V)
generated by
\{s_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\Phi\}
is a finite reflection group, and all such groups arise in this manner.
Any root system contains a
simple system
, that is, is a linearly independent subset
\Delta\subset\Phi
such that

(i)

\Delta
and
\Phi
span the same subspace of
V
, and

(ii)

every element of
\Phi
is real linear combination of elements of
\Delta
with coefficients all of the same sign.

Elements of
\Delta
are
simple roots
. Properties of
\Delta
include:

(i)

W
is generated by
\{s_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\Delta\}

[
Hum90
, Theorem 1.5]
.

(ii)

D=\{v\in V:\left\langle v,\alpha\right\rangle\geq 0,\;\alpha\in\Delta\}
is a fundamental domain for the action of
W
on
V
(i.e. each
W
-orbit intersects
D
in exactly one point)
[
Hum90
, Theorem 1.12]
.

Remark 2.1
.

The definition of “reflection” used here differs from the one used in
[
Lor05
]
, but the two definitions coincide under the hypotheses of
Theorem

1.1
.
Lorenz states
Theorem

1.1
for a subgroup
W
of
\GL(M)
generated by transformations
s
such that the fixed space of
s
on
\mathbb{Q}\otimes M
has codimension 1.
Since
s
lies in
\GL(M)
, it has determinant
\pm 1
; therefore,
s
is conjugate over
\mathbb{Q}
to a diagonal matrix with entries
1,1,1,\ldots,1,-1
.
Replacing
\left\langle,\right\rangle
with
\sum_{(w_{1},w_{2})\in W\times W}\left\langle w_{1}-,w_{2}-\right\rangle
, we may assume that the inner product on
V=\mathbb{R}\otimes M
is
W
-invariant.
If
\alpha
is a
-1
-eigenvector of
s
and
v
is fixed by
s
, then

2\left\langle\alpha,v\right\rangle=\left\langle\alpha,v\right\rangle+\left\langle s\alpha,sv\right\rangle=\left\langle\alpha,v\right\rangle+\left\langle-\alpha,v\right\rangle=0,

so
s=s_{\alpha}
, which is a reflection as defined here.

Remark 2.2
.

The definition of “root system” used here is that of
[
Hum90
]
, but differs slightly from the definition used in some other sources.
To assure the reader that this discrepancy causes no problem, we will provide proofs for results from references other than
[
Hum90
]
.

2.3.

Fix a simple system
\Delta
for
W
, with corresponding fundamental domain
D
.
Define a partial order on
V
by
u\geq v
if and only if
u-v
is a non-negative real linear combination of simple roots.

Proposition 2.3
.

[
Hum90
, Lemma 1.12]

If
v\in D
, then
v-wv\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\left\langle\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}\right\rangle
for all
w\in W
. In particular,
v\geq wv
.

Let
\conv(A)
denote the convex hull of a subset
A\subset V
.

Proposition 2.4
.

[
BD03
, Proposition VI.2.4(ii)]

If
u,v\in D
, then
u\geq v
if and only if
\conv(Wu)
contains
v
.

Proof.

If
v\in\conv(Wu)
, then there are non-negative real numbers
c_{w}
,
w\in W
, such that
\sum_{w}c_{w}=1
and
v=\sum_{w}c_{w}wu
.
By
Proposition

2.3
,
wu\leq u
, so

v=\sum_{w}c_{w}wu\leq\sum_{w}c_{w}u=u.

Conversely, suppose
v\not\in\conv(Wu)
. Then there is
l\in V
such that for all
w\in W
,
\left\langle v,l\right\rangle>\left\langle wu,l\right\rangle=\left\langle u,w^{-1}l\right\rangle
.
In particular, if
l^{\prime}=w^{-1}l\in D
, then by
Proposition

2.3
,

\left\langle v,l^{\prime}\right\rangle\geq\left\langle w^{-1}v,l^{\prime}\right\rangle=\left\langle v,wl^{\prime}\right\rangle>\left\langle u,l^{\prime}\right\rangle,

hence
\left\langle u-v,l^{\prime}\right\rangle<0
.
This proves that
u\not\geq v
because an element of
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\left\langle\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}\right\rangle
has non-negative inner product with all elements of
D
.
∎

2.4.

Call a finite reflection group
W

crystallographic
if it preserves a lattice in
V
(i.e. the
\mathbb{Z}
-span of a basis of
V
).
Given a simple system
\Delta=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}\}
for
W
, the associated
weight group
is

\Lambda=\{v\in V:2\frac{\left\langle v,\alpha_{i}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i}\right\rangle}\in\mathbb{Z},\,1\leq i\leq r\}.

The weight group decomposes as

\Lambda=Z\oplus\mathbb{Z}\left\langle\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}\right\rangle,

where
Z=\cap_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}^{\perp}
and the vectors
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}\in Z^{\perp}
are the
fundamental weights
, determined by

2\frac{\left\langle\lambda_{i},\alpha_{j}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\alpha_{j},\alpha_{j}\right\rangle}=\begin{cases}1,&i=j\\
0,&\text{else.}\end{cases}

The fundamental weights are linearly independent.
Likewise, the fundamental domain decomposes as

D=Z\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\left\langle\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}\right\rangle.

Proposition 2.5
.

[
Spe
, §15]

Let
W
be a finite reflection group that preserves a lattice
M\subset V
.
There is a simple system
\Delta
for
W
such that

\Delta\subset M\subset\Lambda.

Moreover, the action of
W
preserves
\Lambda
.

Proof.

Let
\Delta_{0}
be a simple system for
W
.
If
\alpha\in\Delta_{0}
and
m\in M\setminus\alpha^{\perp}
, then

M\ni m-s_{\alpha}(m)=2\frac{\left\langle m,\alpha\right\rangle}{\left\langle\alpha,\alpha\right\rangle}\alpha,

so
M\cap\mathbb{R}\left\langle\alpha\right\rangle\neq\{0\}
. Consequently,
M\cap\mathbb{R}\left\langle\alpha\right\rangle
is a rank 1 lattice with a unique generator
\alpha^{\prime}
satisfying
\left\langle\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\right\rangle>0
.
The set
\Delta=\{\alpha^{\prime}:\alpha\in\Delta_{0}\}
is a simple system for
W
that is contained in
M
.

The weight lattice
\Lambda
associated to
\Delta
contains
M
because for any
\alpha\in\Delta
and
m\in M
,

\mathbb{Z}\left\langle\alpha\right\rangle=\mathbb{R}\left\langle\alpha\right\rangle\cap M\ni m-s_{\alpha}(m)=2\frac{\left\langle m,\alpha\right\rangle}{\left\langle\alpha,\alpha\right\rangle}\alpha.

The action of
W
preserves
\Lambda
because for each
\alpha\in\Delta
and
\lambda\in\Lambda
,

s_{\alpha}(\lambda)=\lambda-2\frac{\left\langle\lambda,\alpha\right\rangle}{\left\langle\alpha,\alpha\right\rangle}\alpha\in\Lambda+\mathbb{Z}\left\langle\alpha\right\rangle=\Lambda,

and the reflections associated to simple roots generate
W
.
∎

3.1.

[

3.2.

The elements

Lemma 3.2

Let

(i)

(ii)

Write

We may expand

a_{v}=\#\left\{(v_{ij})\in\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1,\ldots,r\\
j=1,\ldots,b_{i}\end{subarray}}W\lambda_{i}:\sum_{ij}v_{ij}=v\right\}.

If

j=1,\ldots,b_{i}\end{subarray}}v_{ij}

By

This inequality is an equality if and only if

Finally,

Lemma 3.3

If

\supp\Psi(\chi^{u})\subset S

Proof.

Let

5.2.

We first describe the face structure of
P\cap D
, following
[
Gon24
, §3]
(see also
[
BGH24
]
).
The polytope
P\cap D
is combinatorially equivalent to a cube
[0,1]^{r}\subset\mathbb{R}^{r}
.
For each pair of disjoint subsets
I,J\subset[r]:=\{1,\ldots,r\}
, there is a face
H_{I}F_{J}
of dimension
r-\#I-\#J
with inner normal cone generated by

\{\alpha_{i}:i\in I\}\cup\{-\lambda_{j}^{\vee}:j\in J\}.

Two faces
H_{I}F_{J}
and
H_{I^{\prime}}F_{J^{\prime}}
are disjoint if and only if
(I\cap J^{\prime})\cup(J\cap I^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
. Otherwise, their intersection is
H_{I}F_{J}\cap H_{I^{\prime}}F_{J^{\prime}}=H_{I\cup I^{\prime}}F_{J\cup J^{\prime}}
. We will often write
H_{I}
and
F_{J}
for the faces
H_{\emptyset}F_{J}
and
H_{I}F_{\emptyset}
, respectively.

If
U\subset P\cap D
is a union of faces, then there is a unique decomposition

U=H_{I_{1}}F_{J_{1}}\cup H_{I_{2}}F_{J_{2}}\cup\cdots\cup H_{I_{k}}F_{J_{k}},

such that no component
H_{I_{j}}F_{J_{j}}
in the union is contained in any other.
The components of this union are the
irreducible components
of
U
.
To establish
Lemma

5.1
, we will employ the following criterion for contractibility.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
U\subset P\cap D
be a union of faces.
If there is an irreducible component of
U
that intersects all other irreducible components of
U
, then
U
is contractible.

Proof.

Identify
P\cap D\cong[0,1]^{r}
so that each face
H_{I}F_{J}
is identified with
\{0\}^{I}\times\{1\}^{J}\times[0,1]^{[r]\setminus(I\cup J)}
.
The map

[0,1]\times\mathbb{R}^{I}\times\mathbb{R}^{J}\times\mathbb{R}^{[r]\setminus(I\cup J)}\to\mathbb{R}^{I}\times\mathbb{R}^{J}\times\mathbb{R}^{[r]\setminus(I\cup J)},\quad(t,x,y,z)\mapsto(tx,(1-t)(1,1,\ldots,1)+ty,z)

gives a deformation retraction of
[0,1]^{r}
onto
H_{I}F_{J}
, and on each face
H_{I^{\prime}}F_{J^{\prime}}
that intersects
H_{I}F_{J}
, it restricts to a retraction of
H_{I^{\prime}}F_{J^{\prime}}
onto
H_{I^{\prime}}F_{J^{\prime}}\cap H_{I}F_{J}
. Consequently, if
H_{I}F_{J}
is an irreducible component of
U
that intersects all others, then
U
is homotopy equivalent to the contractible space
H_{I}F_{J}
.
∎

5.3.

We develop some combinatorial notions that will facilitate use of
Proposition

5.2
.
A

of length
\ell

\mathbf{w}\in[r]^{\ell}
.
Let
\mathbf{w}+\mathbf{w}^{\prime}
denote the concatenation of two words
\mathbf{w}
and
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}
, and define the

of
\mathbf{w}
, denoted

, to be the subset of

that appears in
\mathbf{w}
.
A

of
\mathbf{w}
is any word formed by deleting some entries of
\mathbf{w}
, and a

of
\mathbf{w}
is a subword of the form
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}=(w_{k},w_{k+1},\ldots,w_{k^{\prime}-1},w_{k^{\prime}})
for some

Each word

has an associated group element

, where

is the simple reflection corresponding to

.

Definition 5.3
.

A word
\mathbf{w}
is

on an element

if there is no subword
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}
of
\mathbf{w}
such that
s_{\mathbf{w}}(u)=s_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}(u)

Write

for the set of words efficient on

, and let

We will use the following fact freely and without mention.

Proposition 5.4
.

If
\mathbf{w}\in\operatorname{Eff}
, then all segments of
\mathbf{w}
are in
\operatorname{Eff}
.

Let
\Gamma
be the directed graph with vertex set
[r]
, and an edge
i\to j
whenever the Cartan integer
\left\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\alpha_{j}\right\rangle
is odd. This graph is essentially the Dynkin diagram associated to
W
, and we will always label its vertices as in
[
Bou02
, PLATES I-IX]
.

Proposition 5.5
.

If
u=\sum_{j=1}^{r}u_{j}\lambda_{j}^{\vee}\in\mathcal{S}
,
\mathbf{w}\in\operatorname{Eff}(u)
, and
i\in[r]
, then

s_{\mathbf{w}}(u)_{i}=u_{i}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\to i\\
\text{is an edge of $\Gamma$}\end{subarray}}\#\{k\in[\ell]:w_{k}=j\}\lambda_{j}^{\vee}\in\Lambda^{\vee}/2\Lambda^{\vee}.

Proof.

We have

s_{i}(\lambda_{i}^{\vee})=\lambda_{i}^{\vee}-\left\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\lambda_{i}^{\vee}\right\rangle\alpha_{i}=\lambda_{i}^{\vee}-\left\langle\alpha_{i},\lambda_{i}^{\vee}\right\rangle\alpha_{i}^{\vee}=\lambda_{i}^{\vee}-\sum_{j=1}^{r}\left\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\alpha_{j}\right\rangle\lambda_{j}^{\vee}.

Consequently, for
u=\sum_{j=1}^{r}u_{j}\lambda_{j}^{\vee}\in\mathcal{S}=\Lambda^{\vee}/2\Lambda^{\vee}
with
u_{i}=1
,

s_{i}(u)_{j}=\begin{cases}u_{j},&\left\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\alpha_{j}\right\rangle\equiv 0\mod 2\\
u_{j}+1,&\left\langle\alpha_{i}^{\vee},\alpha_{j}\right\rangle\equiv 0\mod 2.\end{cases}

If
\mathbf{w}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{\ell})\in\operatorname{Eff}(u)
, then for each

,
s_{w_{i}}\in\operatorname{Eff}(s_{w_{i+1}}s_{w_{i+2}}\cdots s_{w_{\ell}}(u))
, so we must have

(otherwise, we could delete

to make a shorter word

with

Consequently, we may sum the contributions of the individual simple reflection to obtain the desired formula.
∎

We induct on the length of

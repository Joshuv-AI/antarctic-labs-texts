Linear Programming Bounds on k k -Uniform States
Yu Ning, Fei Shi, Tao Luo, Xiande Zhang

Abstract
The existence of k k -uniform states has been a widely studied problem due to their applications in several quantum information tasks and their close relation to combinatorial objects like Latin squares and orthogonal arrays. With the machinery of quantum enumerators and linear programming, we establish several improved non-existence results and bounds on k k -uniform states. • First, for any fixed l ≥ 1 l\geq 1 and q ≥ 2 q\geq 2 , we show that there exists a constant c c such that ( ⌊ n / 2 ⌋ − l ) (\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor-l) -uniform states in ( ℂ q ) ⊗ n (\mathbb{C}^{q})^{\otimes n} do not exist when n ≥ c ​ q 2 + o ⁡ ( q 2 ) n\geq cq^{2}+o(q^{2}) . The constant c c equals 4 4 when l = 1 l=1 and 6 6 when l = 2 l=2 , which generalizes Scott’s bound (2004) for l = 0 l=0 . • Second, when n n is sufficiently large, we show that there exists a constant θ < 1 / 2 \theta<1/2 for each q ≤ 9 q\leq 9 , such that k k -uniform states in ( ℂ q ) ⊗ n (\mathbb{C}^{q})^{\otimes n} exist only when k ≤ θ ​ n k\leq\theta n . In particular, this provides the first bound (to the best of our knowledge) of k k for 4 ≤ q ≤ 9 4\leq q\leq 9 and confirms a conjecture posed by Shi et al . (2023) when q = 5 q=5 in a stronger form. • Finally, we improve the shadow bounds given by Shi et al . (2023) by a constant for q = 3 , 4 , 5 q=3,4,5 and small n n . When q = 4 q=4 , our results can update some bounds listed in the code tables maintained by Grassl (2007–2024).

II-B

Quantum Enumerators

We follow the notations in

[
12
,
22
,
16
]
, and
focus on the enumerators associated with a pure state. Let
\left\{e_{j}\right\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{q^{2}}}
, with
e_{0}=I
, be an orthogonal list of error basis of linear
operators acting on
\mathbb{C}^{q}
, with respect to the trace inner product, i.e.,

\tr(e_i^\dagger e_j)=\delta_{i,j}q
. For the qubit system, the Pauli
operators serve as such a basis. For
q\geq 3
, the existence of such an error
basis is assured by the Weyl operators
[
24
]
.
Let
\mathcal{E}
be the set of
n
-fold tensor products of the error basis, i.e.,
an element
E\in\mathcal{E}
is of the form

E=e_{j_{1}}\otimes e_{j_{2}}\otimes\dots\otimes e_{j_{n}},

where
j_{1},\dots,j_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{q^{2}}
. Naturally,
\mathcal{E}
serves as an orthogonal
list of error basis for the space

(\mathbb{C}^{q})^{\otimes n}
. For
E\in\mathcal{E}
,
let
\wt(E)
denote the weight of
E
, that is
the number of tensor factors in
E
not equal to the identity.

Let
\ket{\psi}\in(\mathbb{C}^{q})^{\otimes n}
be a pure state, and

\rho=\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}
be its density matrix. The
quantum weight
distribution

associated with
\ket{\psi}
is defined as

A_{i}\triangleq\sum_{\wt(E)=i}\tr(E\rho)\tr(E^\dagger\rho),\quad B_{i}\triangleq\sum_{\wt(E)=i}\tr(E\rho E^\dagger\rho),\quad i\in[0,n],

((1))

where the index of summand ranges over all elements in
\mathcal{E}
of
weight
i
.
The associated
quantum weight enumerators
are defined as

A(x,y)\triangleq\sum_{i=0}^{n}A_{i}x^{n-i}y^{i},\quad B(x,y)\triangleq\sum_{i=0}^{n}B_{i}x^{n-i}y^{i}.

((2))

Then
A(x,y)
and
B(x,y)
satisfy the quantum MacWilliams transformation

[
21
,
12
]

B(x,y)=A\quantity(\frac{x+(q^2-1)y}{q},\frac{x-y}{q}).

((3))

Also, as
\ket{\psi}
is a pure state, we have that
A_{0}=1
and
A_{i}=B_{i}\geq 0
for all
i\in[0,n]
, and thus
A(x,y)=B(x,y)

[
19
]
.
Moreover, if
\ket{\psi}
is
k
-uniform, we have that

A_{1}=A_{2}=\dots=A_{k}=0

[
19
]
. If
\ket{\psi}
is a
stabilizer state,
A(x,y)

has the combinatorial interpretation as the weight enumerator of the
corresponding stabilizer group regarded as an additive code

[
16
]
.

Next, we recall the unitary enumerator associated with
\ket{\psi}

[
22
,
12
]
.
For
T\subset[n]
, we write
\rho_{T}=\tr_{T^{\complement}}(\rho)
, where
T^{\complement}
is the complement of

T
in
[n]
, and define

A_{T}^{\prime}=\tr(\rho_T^2)\text{ and }A_{i}^{\prime}=\sum_{\left|{T}\right|=i}A_{T}^{\prime},\quad i\in[0,n],

((4))

where the index of summand ranges over all subsets of
[n]
of size
i
.
The
unitary enumerator
associated with
\ket{\psi}
is defined as

A^{\prime}(x,y)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}A_{i}^{\prime}x^{n-i}y^{i}.

((5))

The unitary enumerator and the quantum weight enumerator are related as

[
12
]

A(x,y)=A^{\prime}(x-y,qy),\quad A^{\prime}(x,y)=A\quantity(x+\frac{y}{q},\frac{y}{q}).

((6))

As
\ket{\psi}
is a pure state, by Schmidt decomposition, we have that

[
25
]

A_{T}^{\prime}=A_{T^{\complement}}^{\prime},\quad A_{i}^{\prime}=A_{n-i}^{\prime},\quad A^{\prime}(x,y)=A^{\prime}(y,x),

((7))

for arbitrary
T\subset[n]
and
i\in[0,n]
.
Also by Schmidt decomposition, we have that
[
25
]

A_{T}^{\prime}\geq\frac{1}{q^{\min(\left|{T}\right|,n-\left|{T}\right|)}},\quad A_{i}^{\prime}\geq\frac{1}{q^{\min(i,n-i)}}{n\choose i},

((8))

where
T\subset[n]
and
i\in[0,n]
are arbitrary.
Moreover, if
\ket{\psi}
is
k
-uniform, we have that
[
25
]

A_{T}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{q^{\left|{T}\right|}},\quad A_{i}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{q^{i}}{n\choose i},

((9))

where
T\subset[n]
is of size at most
k\leq\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor
and
i\in[0,k]
.

Finally, we recall the shadow enumerator
[
16
,
12
]
.
Let

S_{i}=\sum_{\left|{R}\right|=i}\sum_{T\subset[n]}(-1)^{\left|{T\cap R^{\complement}}\right|}A_{T}^{\prime},

((10))

where the summand
R
ranges over all subsets of
[n]
of size
i
.
The
shadow enumerator
associated with
\ket{\psi}
is defined as

S(x,y)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}S_{i}x^{n-i}y^{i}.

((11))

The shadow enumerator is related to the quantum weight enumerator and the unitary enumerator as in
[
12
]

S(x,y)=A\quantity(\frac{(q-1)x+(q+1)y}{q},\frac{y-x}{q}),\quad S(x,y)=A^{\prime}(x+y,y-x).

((12))

As
\ket{\psi}
is a pure state,
S_{j}\geq 0
for all
j\in[0,n]

and
S_{n-j}=0
for odd
j\in[0,n]

[
12
]
.
Also, if
\ket{\psi}
is a stabilizer state,
S(x,y)
has the
combinatorial interpretation as the weight enumerator of the
shadow code of the stabilizer group regarded as an additive code

[
16
]
.

II-C

A Linear Program

The transformations among the enumerators of
\ket{\psi}
and
the
k
-uniform condition will impose linear constraints on the
coefficients of enumerators of
\ket{\psi}
.
The linear program concerning the quantum weight enumerator and the shadow
enumerator has been considered by many authors

[
23
,
22
,
16
,
13
,
19
,
12
]
.
In this paper, we will focus on the linear program related to the
quantum weight enumerator and the unitary enumerator, which we formulate
precisely in the following.

Let
\ket{\psi}\in(\mathbb{C}^{q})^{\otimes n}
be a
k
-uniform state,
and
A(x,y)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}A_{i}x^{n-i}y^{i}
,
A^{\prime}(x,y)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}A_{i}^{\prime}x^{n-i}y^{i}
be the
weight enumerator and the unitary enumerator of
\ket{\psi}
, respectively. Since

\sum_{i=0}^{n}A_{i}^{\prime}x^{n-i}y^{i}=A^{\prime}(x,y)=A\quantity(x+\frac{y}{q},\frac{y}{q})=\sum_{j=0}^{n}A_{j}\quantity(x+\frac{y}{q})^{n-j}\quantity(\frac{y}{q})^{j},

((13))

by comparing the coefficients on both sides, we have

q^{i}A_{i}^{\prime}=\sum_{j=0}^{i}A_{j}\binom{n-j}{i-j},\qquad i\in[0,n].

((14))

Then, we have the following linear constraints on enumerators for a
k
-uniform
state
[
12
]
:

\displaystyle A_{0}=1;\quad A_{1}=A_{2}=\dots=A_{k}=0;\quad A_{i}\geq 0,\,\forall i\in[k+1,n];

((15))

\displaystyle A_{i}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{q^{i}}\binom{n}{i},\,\forall i\in[0,k];\quad A_{i}^{\prime}\geq\frac{1}{q^{i}}\binom{n}{i},\,\forall i\in[k+1,\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor];\quad A_{i}^{\prime}=A_{n-i}^{\prime},\,\forall i\in[0,n];

((16))

\displaystyle q^{i}A_{i}^{\prime}=\sum_{j=0}^{i}A_{j}\binom{n-j}{i-j},\,\forall i\in[0,n].

((17))

If we can show that the constraints of
Eqs.
(15)
–
(17)

do not hold, then
k
-uniform states in
(\mathbb{C}^{q})^{\otimes n}
do
not exist.

III-A

A Proof of
Theorem III.1
(1)

Let
k=\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor-1
, and assume that
\ket{\psi}
is
k
-uniform.
Plugging the values of
Eq. (15)
and

Eq. (16)
into
Eq. (17)

for
i=k+2
and
k+3
, we have

\displaystyle q^{k+2}A_{k+2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\binom{n}{k+2}+A_{k+1}(n-(k+1))+A_{k+2},

((18))

\displaystyle q^{k+3}A_{k+3}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\binom{n}{k+3}+A_{k+1}\binom{n-(k+1)}{2}+A_{k+2}(n-(k+2))+A_{k+3}

\displaystyle=q^{2(k+3)-n}\binom{n}{k+3}.

In the following, we will split into two cases where
n
is even or odd.

When
n
is even, that is,
n=2k+2
, we have
A_{k+2}^{\prime}=A_{k}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{q^{k}}\binom{n}{k+2}
by
Eq. (16)
.
Solving
A_{k+2}
and
A_{k+3}
in

Eq. (18)
,
we have that

\displaystyle A_{k+2}

\displaystyle=\quantity(q^{2}-1)\binom{2k+2}{k+2}-A_{k+1}(k+1),

((19))

\displaystyle A_{k+3}

\displaystyle=(q^{4}-1)\binom{2k+2}{k+3}-\binom{k+1}{2}A_{k+1}-kA_{k+2}

\displaystyle=\quantity(q^{4}-1)\binom{2k+2}{k+3}+A_{k+1}\binom{k+1}{2}-k\quantity(q^{2}-1)\binom{2k+2}{k+2}.

With the constraints that
A_{k+2}\geq 0
and
A_{k+3}\geq 0
, we have that

\frac{q^{2}-1}{k+1}\binom{2k+2}{k+2}\geq A_{k+1}\geq\frac{1}{\binom{k+1}{2}}k(q^{2}-1)\quantity(k(q^2-1)\binom{2k+2}{k+2}-(q^4-1)\binom{2k+2}{k+3})-(q^{4}-1)\binom{2k+2}{k+3}.

((20))

So if
Eq. (20)
is violated, i.e.,

\frac{q^{2}-1}{k+1}\binom{k+1}{2}\binom{2k+2}{k+2}<k(q^{2}-1)\binom{2k+2}{k+2}-(q^{4}-1)\binom{2k+2}{k+3},

((21))

then AME states of defect
1
in
(\mathbb{C}^{q})^{\otimes n}
do not exist.
Since
Eq. (21)
holds when
k>2q^{2}-1
, that is

n=2k+2>4q^{2}
,
Theorem III.1
(1)
follows for even
n
.

When
n
is odd, that is,
n=2k+3
, we still have that

A_{k+3}^{\prime}=A_{k}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{q^{k}}\binom{n}{k}
.
But
A_{k+2}^{\prime}=A_{k+1}^{\prime}
is unknown to us, which makes the
analysis a little bit more complicated.
We need one more equation for
i=k+1
in
Eq. (17)
,
i.e.,

q^{k+1}A_{k+1}^{\prime}=\binom{n}{k+1}+A_{k+1}.

((22))

Combining
Eq. (22)
and
Eq. (18)
, we have that

\displaystyle q^{k+1}A_{k+2}^{\prime}=q^{k+1}A_{k+1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\binom{2k+3}{k+1}+A_{k+1},

((23))

\displaystyle q^{k+2}A_{k+2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\binom{2k+3}{k+2}+A_{k+1}(k+2)+A_{k+2},

\displaystyle q^{3}\binom{2k+3}{k+3}=q^{k+3}A_{k+3}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\binom{2k+3}{k+3}+A_{k+1}\binom{k+2}{2}+A_{k+2}(k+1)+A_{k+3}.

Solving
A_{k+2},A_{k+3}
out, we have that

\displaystyle A_{k+2}

\displaystyle=q^{k+2}A_{k+2}^{\prime}-\binom{2k+3}{k+1}-A_{k+1}(k+2)

((24))

\displaystyle=(q-1)\binom{2k+3}{k+1}-(k+2-q)A_{k+1},

\displaystyle A_{k+3}

\displaystyle=(q^{3}-1)\binom{2k+3}{k+3}-A_{k+1}\binom{k+2}{2}-A_{k+2}(k+1)

\displaystyle=(q^{3}-1)\binom{2k+3}{k+3}-(k+1)(q-1)\binom{2k+3}{k+1}+(k+1)\quantity(\frac{k+2}{2}-q)A_{k+1}.

As
A_{k+1},A_{k+2},A_{k+3}
are all non-negative, we have that

\displaystyle(q-1)\binom{2k+3}{k+1}

\displaystyle\geq(k+2-q)A_{k+1}\geq(k+2-2q)A_{k+1}

((25))

\displaystyle\geq 2(q-1)\binom{2k+3}{k+1}-\frac{2(q^{3}-1)}{k+1}\binom{2k+3}{k+3}.

However, when
k>2q^{2}+2q-1
,

(q-1)\binom{2k+3}{k+1}-\frac{2(q^{3}-1)}{k+1}\binom{2k+3}{k+3}>0,

((26))

that is,
Eq. (25)
does not hold. So when
n=2k+3>4q^{2}+4q+1
, AME states of defect
1
in

(\mathbb{C}^{q})^{\otimes n}

do not exist, which proves
Theorem III.1
(1) for odd
n
.

IV-A

The Dual Linear Program

First, we rewrite the linear program of
Eqs.
(15)
–
(17)

more explicitly as follows.
Let

M=\left(\hskip-5.0pt\,\begin{array}[]{ccccc}{n-k-1\choose 0}\\
{n-k-1\choose 1}&{n-k-2\choose 0}\\
{n-k-1\choose 2}&{n-k-2\choose 1}&{n-k-3\choose 0}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\\
{n-k-1\choose n-k-1}&{n-k-2\choose n-k-2}&{n-k-3\choose n-k-3}&\dots&{0\choose 0}\end{array}\hskip-5.0pt\,\right).

((47))

Then all equations in
Eq. (17)
can be written as

M\left(\hskip-5.0pt\,\begin{array}[]{c}A_{k+1}\\
A_{k+2}\\
\vdots\\
A_{n}\end{array}\hskip-5.0pt\,\right)=\left(\hskip-5.0pt\,\begin{array}[]{c}q^{k+1}A_{k+1}^{\prime}\\
q^{k+2}A_{k+2}^{\prime}\\
\vdots\\
q^{n}A_{n}^{\prime}\end{array}\hskip-5.0pt\,\right)-\left(\hskip-5.0pt\,\begin{array}[]{c}\binom{n}{k+1}\\
\binom{n}{k+2}\\
\vdots\\
\binom{n}{n}\end{array}\hskip-5.0pt\,\right).

((48))

Let

\mathbf{m}^{\intercal}_{k+1},\mathbf{m}^{\intercal}_{k+2},\dots,\mathbf{m}^{\intercal}_{n}
be the rows of

M
, and let

\mathbf{c}^{\intercal}=(0,\dots,0)_{n-k}
be the zero vector of length
n-k
.
Let
\mathbf{x}=(x_{k+1},x_{k+2},\dots,x_{n})^{\intercal}
be a list of variables
of
length
n-k
,
and think of
x_{i}
as
A_{i}
for
i\in[k+1,n]
.
The linear program of
Eqs.
(15)
–
(17)

can be written as

minimize

\displaystyle\mathbf{c}^{\intercal}\mathbf{x}

((49))

subject to

\displaystyle\mathbf{m}_{i}^{\intercal}\mathbf{x}\geq 0\triangleq b_{i},\quad i\in[k+1,\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor],

((50))

\displaystyle\mathbf{m}_{i}^{\intercal}\mathbf{x}\geq\quantity(q^{2i-n}-1)\binom{n}{i}\triangleq b_{i},\quad i\in[\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor+1,n-k-1],

((51))

\displaystyle\mathbf{m}_{i}^{\intercal}\mathbf{x}=\quantity(q^{2i-n}-1)\binom{n}{i}\triangleq b_{i},\quad i\in[n-k,n],

((52))

\displaystyle x_{i}\geq 0,\quad i\in[k+1,n].

((53))

Denote this linear program by
U(n,k,q)
.
Note that there are no objective functions contained in
Eqs.
(15)
–
(17)
,
but here in
U(n,k,q)
, we artificially add an objective function
(
Eq. (49)
), which is
0
as long as

U(n,k,q)
has a feasible solution. The non-feasibility of

U(n,k,q)
will imply the non-existence of
k
-uniform states in

(\mathbb{C}^{q})^{\otimes n}
as before.

In the following, we present the dual linear program of
U(n,k,q)
, denoted by

V(n,k,q)
, as in
[
26
, Chapter 4]
. Let
\mathbf{M}_{k+1},\mathbf{M}_{k+2},\dots,\mathbf{M}_{n}
be the columns of

M
, and
let
\mathbf{b}=(b_{k+1},b_{k+2},\dots,b_{n})^{\intercal}
be defined in Eqs.

(50)
–
(52)
.
Setting
\mathbf{p}=(p_{k+1},\dots,p_{n})^{\intercal}
to be the variables of
the dual
program, we can write
V(n,k,q)
as

maximize

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}^{\intercal}\mathbf{b},

((54))

subject to

\displaystyle p_{i}\geq 0,\quad i\in[k+1,n-(k+1)],

((55))

\displaystyle p_{i}\text{ free},\quad i\in[n-k,n],

((56))

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}^{\intercal}\mathbf{M}_{i}\leq 0,\quad i\in[k+1,n].

((57))

By
[
26
, Theorem 4.3]
, if
\mathbf{x}
is a feasible
solution to

U(n,k,q)
, and
\mathbf{p}
is a feasible solution to
V(n,k,q)
, then

\mathbf{p}^{\intercal}\mathbf{b}\leq\mathbf{c}^{\intercal}\mathbf{x}=0
.
Equivalently, if we can find a solution
\mathbf{p}
to
V(n,k,q)
such that

\mathbf{p}^{\intercal}\mathbf{b}>0
, then
U(n,k,q)
is not feasible, and thus

k
-uniform states in
(\mathbb{C}^{q})^{\otimes n}
do not exist.
We summarize this observation in the following lemma.

Lemma IV.1
.

Let
q\geq 2
,
k\leq\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor-1
, and

p_{k+1},p_{k+2},\dots,p_{n}
be real numbers such that

p_{i}\geq 0
for
i\in[k+1,n-(k+1)]
. If

\sum_{i=j}^{n}p_{i}\binom{n-j}{i-j}\leq 0,\quad\forall j\in[k+1,n],

((58))

and

\sum_{i=\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor+1}^{n}p_{i}\quantity(q^{2i-n}-1)\binom{n}{i}>0,

((59))

then
k
-uniform states in
(\mathbb{C}^{q})^{\otimes n}
do not exist.

In the following, we will focus on constructing a solution

\mathbf{p}=(p_{k+1},\dots,p_{n})

satisfying the conditions in
IV.1
.

IV-B

Bounds from Dual Solutions

Let
u=\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor+1
and
v=n-k
. Assume that
u<v
. For simplicity, we
set
\mathbf{p}=(p_{k+1},\dots,p_{n})^{\intercal}
with
p_{u}>0
,
p_{v}=-1

and all other entries zero. This setting satisfy
the easy constraint of
p
in
IV.1
. We next deduce the
sufficient conditions on the existence of
p_{u}
so that
p

satisfies all requirements in
IV.1
.
The constraints in
Eq. (58)
requires that

\binom{n-k-1}{n-u}p_{u}\leq\binom{n-k-1}{n-v},\binom{n-k-2}{n-u}p_{u}\leq\binom{n-k-2}{n-v},\dots,\binom{n-u}{n-u}p_{u}\leq\binom{n-u}{n-v}.

((60))

As
\frac{\binom{x}{n-v}}{\binom{x}{n-u}}
is decreasing with respect to
x
,
it suffices that

p_{u}\leq\frac{\binom{n-k-1}{n-v}}{\binom{n-k-1}{n-u}}.

((61))

On the other hand,
Eq. (59)
is equivalent to

p_{u}>\frac{(q^{2v-n}-1)\binom{n}{v}}{(q^{2u-n}-1)\binom{n}{u}}.

((62))

So if the inequalities in
Eq. (61)
and
Eq. (62)
have a common solution
p_{u}
,
then there exists a solution
p
to
V(n,k,q)

satisfying the conditions in
IV.1
.
We conclude in the following theorem.

Theorem IV.2
.

Let
k\leq\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor-1
,
u=\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor+1
and
v=n-k
.
If
u<v
and

\frac{\binom{n-k-1}{n-v}}{\binom{n-k-1}{n-u}}>\frac{(q^{2v-n}-1)\binom{n}{v}}{(q^{2u-n}-1)\binom{n}{u}},

((63))

then
k
-uniform states in

(\mathbb{C}^{q})^{\otimes n}
do not exist.

n
and odd
n
in
Theorem IV.2

when

V-A

Review of the Approach to the Shadow Bounds

We review the proof of the shadow bounds, by setting up
some notations and introducing some necessary results.
For more details, we refer to
[
13
,
19
]
and
the references therein.

Let
\ket{\psi}
be a pure state in
(\mathbb{C}^{q})^{\otimes n}
. Let
A(x,y)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}A_{j}x^{n-j}y^{j}
be the weight enumerator associated with

\ket{\psi}
.
By a Gleason-type theorem
[
27
]
,

A(x,y)
is a polynomial in
x+(q-1)y
and
y(x-y)
,
so we may write (see
[
19
, Eq. (16)]
)

A(x,y)=\sum_{i=0}^{\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor}c_{i}(x+(q-1)y)^{n-2i}(y(x-y))^{i},

((66))

where
c_{i}\in\mathbb{R},i\in[0,\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor]
.
Relating
Eq. (66)
with the weight enumerator, we can write

c_{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{i}\alpha_{i,j}A_{j},\quad 0\leq i\leq\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor,

((67))

where
\alpha_{i,j}
are some real numbers (see
[
19
, Eq. (20)]
).
We know that

\alpha_{i,i}=1,\quad i\in[0,\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor].

((68))

For
i\in[1,\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor]
,
\alpha_{i,0}
can be expressed as
(see
[
19
, Appendix B and Lemma 5]
)

\displaystyle\alpha_{i,0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{n(q-1)}{i}\quantity[\text{coeff. of } y^{i-1} \text{ in }
(1+(q-1)y)^{-(n+1-2i)}(1-y)^{-i}],

((69))

\displaystyle=-\frac{n(q-1)}{i}\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}(1-q)^{j}{n-2i+j\choose n-2i}{2i-2-j\choose i-1}.

Let
S(x,y)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}S_{j}x^{n-j}y^{j}
be the shadow enumerator associated with
\ket{\psi}
. As
S(x,y)=A\quantity(\frac{(q-1)x+(q+1)y}{q},\frac{y-x}{q})
, we have that

S(x,y)=\sum_{i=0}^{\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor}(-1)^{i}c_{i}2^{n-2i}q^{-i}y^{n-2i}(x^{2}-y^{2})^{i}.

((70))

By comparing coefficients between
Eq. (70)
and
the shadow enumerator,

[
19
, Lemma 6]
shows that

(-1)^{i}c_{i}\geq 0\text{ for all }0\leq i\leq\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor.

((71))

The shadow bounds in
Theorem II.3

can be derived by determining the smallest
i\leq\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor
such that
(-1)^{i}c_{i}<0
.
In fact, if
\ket{\psi}
is a
k
-uniform state for
k\geq i
, then

A_{0}=1
and
A_{1}=A_{2}=\dots=A_{i}=0
. Thus, by
Eq. (67)
,
c_{i}=\alpha_{i,0}
.
If
(-1)^{i}\alpha_{i,0}<0
, we have a
contradiction to
Eq. (71)
, which implies the non-existence of
k
-uniform states
(see
[
19
, Lemma 7]
), and thus
k\leq i-1
. The difficulty
lies in determining the sign of
\alpha_{i,0}
for
i\in[1,\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor]
in

Eq. (69)
. The shadow bounds in
II.1
were
verified for small
n
by checking the sign of
\alpha_{i,0}
numerically in

[
19
]
.

V-B

Refinement of the Shadow Bounds

The non-existence of
k
-uniform states in
Theorem II.3

are derived by inspecting the sign of
c_{k}
as discussed before.
In this subsection, we further inspect
c_{k+1}
and
c_{k+2}
to
strengthen the shadow bounds.

Suppose that
k\leq\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor-2
is odd and
\ket{\psi}
is a
k
-uniform
state in
(\mathbb{C}^{q})^{\otimes n}
. Then
A_{0}=1
and

A_{1}=A_{2}=\dots=A_{k}=0
.
By
Eq. (67)
and
Eq. (68)
, we have

\displaystyle c_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{k+1,0}+A_{k+1},

((72))

\displaystyle c_{k+2}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{k+2,0}+\alpha_{k+2,k+1}A_{k+1}+A_{k+2}.

((73))

Since
k
is odd, by
Eq. (71)
,
c_{k+1}\geq 0
and
c_{k+2}\leq 0
.
Combining
Eq. (72)
and

Eq. (73)
, we have that

\displaystyle A_{k+2}

\displaystyle\leq-\alpha_{k+2,0}-\alpha_{k+2,k+1}A_{k+1}

((74))

\displaystyle\leq-\alpha_{k+2,0}+\alpha_{k+2,k+1}\alpha_{k+1,0},

((75))

where the inequality in
Eq. (75)
is based on the assumption that

\alpha_{k+2,k+1}\geq 0
. As
A_{k+2}\geq 0
, if furthermore

-\alpha_{k+2,0}+\alpha_{k+2,k+1}\alpha_{k+1,0}<0
, we will have a
contradiction, implying the non-existence of the pure
k
-uniform state

\ket{\psi}
in
(\mathbb{C}^{q})^{\otimes n}
. The value of
\alpha_{k+2,k+1}
can be computed in the following lemma, whose proof can be
found
in Appendix
C
.

Lemma V.1
.

\alpha_{k+2,k+1}=(k+1)(2q-1)-(q-1)n
.

We summarize the above argument in the following proposition.

Proposition V.2
.

Let
k
be odd and
k\leq\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor-2
. Let
\alpha_{i,0}
be defined in

Eq. (69)
for
i\in[1,\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor]
. If
(k+1)(2q-1)-(q-1)n\geq 0
and

((k+1)(2q-1)-(q-1)n)\alpha_{k+1,0}<\alpha_{k+2,0},

((76))

then
k
-uniform states in
(\mathbb{C}^{q})^{\otimes n}
do not exist.

A Proof of
III.2

The proof is again by contradiction and we assume that there is a choice of

t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
such that each entry of
\mathbf{y}
in
Eq. (46)

is non-negative. Focusing on the first
4
entries of
\mathbf{y}
, we have that

\displaystyle t_{1}

\displaystyle\geq 0,

((77))

\displaystyle t_{2}q

\displaystyle\geq t_{1}(n-k-1),

((78))

\displaystyle t_{1}\binom{n-k-1}{2}+\frac{a_{k+3}}{q^{k+1}}

\displaystyle\geq t_{2}q(n-k-2-q),

((79))

\displaystyle t_{2}q\pqty{\binom{n-k-2}{2}-q(n-k-3)}-q(n-k-3)+\frac{a_{k+4}}{q^{k+1}}

\displaystyle\geq t_{1}\pqty{\binom{n-k-1}{3}-q^3}-q^{3}.

((80))

Combining
Eq. (78)
and
Eq. (79)
, we have
that

\displaystyle t_{2}q\frac{n-k-2}{2}\geq t_{1}{n-k-1\choose 2}\geq t_{2}q(n-k-2-q)-\frac{a_{k+3}}{q^{k+1}},

which is simplified as

\frac{a_{k+3}}{q^{k+1}}\geq t_{2}q\quantity(\frac{n-k-2}{2} - q).

((81))

Multiplying
\frac{3}{n-k-3}
on both sides of
Eq. (80)
,
we have that

3t_{2}q\quantity(\frac{n-k-2}{2}-q)+\frac{3a_{k+4}}{(n-k-3)q^{k+1}}\geq t_{1}\quantity(\binomial{n-k-1}{2}-\frac{3q^3}{n-k-3}).

((82))

By
Eq. (78)
, we have that

-t_{1}\frac{3q^{3}}{n-k-3}\geq-\frac{3t_{2}q^{4}}{(n-k-1)(n-k-3)}.

((83))

Substituting
Eq. (83)
and
Eq. (79)
into

Eq. (82)
, we have that

3t_{2}q\quantity(\frac{n-k-2}{2} -q)+\frac{3a_{k+4}}{(n-k-3)q^{k+1}}\geq t_{2}q(n-k-2-q)-\frac{a_{k+3}}{q^{k+1}}-\frac{3t_{2}q^{4}}{(n-k-1)(n-k-3)},

which is further simplified as

t_{2}q\quantity(\frac{n-k-2}{2} - 2q + \frac{3q^3}{(n-k-1)(n-k-3)})\geq-\frac{a_{k+3}}{q^{k+1}}-\frac{3a_{k+4}}{(n-k-3)q^{k+1}}.

((84))

Note that
t_{2}\geq 0
by
Eq. (77)
and

Eq. (78)
.
By the assumption that
k>3q^{2}+3q-1
in
III.2
, we have that

\frac{3q^{3}}{(n-k-1)(n-k-3)}\leq q.

((85))

Then, combining
Eq. (81)
and
Eq. (84)
,
we
have that

\frac{a_{k+3}}{q^{k+1}}\geq-\frac{a_{k+3}}{q^{k+1}}-\frac{3a_{k+4}}{(n-k-3)q^{k+1}}.

((86))

Substituing the values of
n=2k+5
and
a_{k+3},a_{k+4}
into

.

Appendix B
A Proof of
Theorem IV.5

First, we recall Stirling’s approximation
[
28
]
:

\frac{1}{12n+1}<\log(\factorial{n})-\quantity[
\qty(n+\frac{1}{2})\log n - n + \frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi)
]<\frac{1}{12n},\quad n=1,2,3,\dots

((87))

where
\log
denotes the natural logarithm.

Now, we follow the notations of

Theorem IV.2
and show
Theorem IV.5
.
Simplifying the condition in
Eq. (63)
, we have that

\frac{v!(u-k-1)!}{u!(n-2k-1)!}=\frac{{n-k-1\choose n-v}{n\choose u}}{{n-k-1\choose n-u}{n\choose v}}>\frac{q^{2v-n}-1}{q^{2u-n}-1}.

((88))

Note that
2u-n
equals
1
or
2
, depending on the parity of
n
.
Taking the natural logarithm, for
Eq. (88)
to hold, it
suffices that

\log(\factorial{v})+\log(\factorial{(u-k-1)})-\log(\factorial{u})-\log(\factorial{(n-2k-1)})\geq(2v-n)\log q.

((89))

By Stirling’s approximation,
Eq. (89)
holds if

(v+1/2)\log(v)+(u-k-1/2)\log(u-k-1)-\\
(u+1/2)\log(u)-(n-2k-1/2)\log(n-2k-1)\geq(2v-n)\log q.

((90))

Let
\theta=\frac{k}{n}
.
Eq. (90)
holds
asymptotically, as long as
the following equation holds asymptotically:

(1-\theta)\log((1-\theta)n)+(1/2-\theta)\log((1/2-\theta)n)\\
-\frac{1}{2}\log(n/2)-(1-2\theta)\log((1-2\theta)n)-\order{\frac{\log n}{n}}>(1-2\theta)\log q.

((91))

The left-hand side of
Eq. (91)
simplifies as

\frac{1}{2}\log\quantity(
\frac{(1/2-\theta)n}{n/2}
)+(1-\theta)\log\quantity(
\frac{(1-\theta)n}{(1-2\theta)n}
)+\theta\log\quantity(
\frac{(1-2\theta)n}{(1/2-\theta)n}
)-\order{\frac{\log n}{n}},

((92))

which converges to

\frac{1}{2}\log(1-2\theta)+(1-\theta)\log\quantity(\frac{1-\theta}{1-2\theta})+\theta\log(2)

((93))

as
n
goes to infinity.
In summary, for
Theorem IV.2
to hold asymptotically, it
suffices that

\frac{1}{2}\log(1-2\theta)+(1-\theta)\log\quantity(\frac{1-\theta}{1-2\theta})+\theta\log(2)-(1-2\theta)\log q>0.

((94))

The proof is completed as desired.

Appendix C
A Proof of
V.1

Let
f(y)
be a formal power series, and we use the notation
f[i]
to denote
the coefficient of
y^{i}
in
f(y)
for integers
i\geq 0
. The proof of

V.1
is a routine application of the
Lagrange-Bürmann theorem.

Lemma C.1

(Lagrange-Bürmann

[
19
,
13
,
29
]
)
.

Let
f(y)
and
g(y)
be formal power series. Assume that
g(0)=0
and

g^{\prime}(0)\neq 0
.
Then
f(y)
can be expanded as a series in
g(y)
as

f(y)=\sum_{i\geq 0}k_{i}g(y)^{i},

where

k_{i}=\frac{1}{i}\quantity[f^\prime(y)\qty(\frac{y}{g(y)})^i][i-1],\quad i\geq 1.

Now, we proceed with the proof of
V.1
, and we follow the notations set up in

Section V
. By
Eq. (67)
, if we set

A_{k+1}=1,\mbox{\quad and\quad}A_{i}=0,i\in[0,\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor],i\neq k+1,

we have that

c_{k+2}=\alpha_{k+2,k+1}.

It suffices to compute
c_{k+2}
in the following.
Substituting this assigment of
A_{i},0\leq i\leq\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor
into

Eq. (66)
and setting
x=1
, we have that

y^{k+1}+\sum_{j=\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor+1}^{n}A_{j}y^{j}=\sum_{i=0}^{\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor}c_{i}(1+(q-1)y)^{n-2i}(y(1-y))^{i}.

Dividing by
(1+(q-1)y)^{n}
, we have that

\frac{y^{k+1}}{(1+(q-1)y)^{n}}=\sum_{i=0}^{\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor}c_{i}\quantity(\frac{y(1-y)}{(1+(q-1)y)^2})^{i}-\frac{1}{(1+(q-1)y)^{n}}\sum_{j=\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor+1}^{n}A_{j}y^{j}.

Let
g(y)=\frac{y(1-y)}{(1+(q-1)y)^{2}}
. It is easy to check that
g(0)=0

and
g^{\prime}(0)\neq 0
. Now we may apply
C.1
.
Let
L(y)=\frac{1}{(1+(q-1)y)^{n}}\sum_{j=\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor+1}^{n}A_{j}y^{j}
.
Note that

L(y)=\sum_{j\geq\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor+1}u_{j}y^{j},

where
u_{j},j\geq\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor+1
are some coefficients whose value we do not
care.
Thus, the expansion of
L(y)
in terms of
g(y)
must be of the form

L(y)=\sum_{j\geq\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor+1}v_{j}g(y)^{j},

where
v_{j},j\geq\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor+1
again are some coefficients whose value we do
not care.
In conclusion, the expansion of
\frac{y^{k+1}}{(1+(q-1)y)^{n}}
in terms of
g(y)

is of the form

\frac{y^{k+1}}{(1+(q-1)y)^{n}}=\sum_{i=0}^{\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor}c_{i}g(y)^{i}-\sum_{j\geq\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor+1}v_{j}g(y)^{j}.

By
C.1
,

\displaystyle c_{k+2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k+2}\quantity[\qty(\frac{y^{k+1}}{(1+(q-1)y)^n})^\prime\qty(\frac{(1+(q-1)y)^2}{1-y})^{k+2}][k+1]

\displaystyle=\frac{k+1}{k+2}\quantity[\frac{y^{k}}{(1+(q-1)y)^{n-2k-4}(1-y)^{k+2}}][k+1]

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{n(q-1)}{k+2}\quantity[
\frac{y^{k+1}}{(1+(q-1)y)^{n-2k-3}(1-y)^{k+2}}
][k+1]

\displaystyle=\frac{k+1}{k+2}\quantity(\frac{1}{(1+(q-1)y)^{n-2k-4}(1-y)^{k+2}})^{\prime}(0)-\frac{n(q-1)}{k+2}

\displaystyle=\frac{k+1}{k+2}((n-2k-4)(1-q)+k+2)+\frac{n(1-q)}{k+2}

\displaystyle=(2q-1)(k+1)+(1-q)n.

The proof is completed as desired.

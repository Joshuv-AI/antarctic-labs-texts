Probing the electromagnetic dipole moment of the τ \tau lepton in the e + ​ e − → γ ∗ / Z → τ + ​ τ − e^{+}e^{-}\to\gamma^{*}/Z\to\tau^{+}\tau^{-} reaction
Peng-Cheng Lu, Zong-Guo Si, Han Zhang

Abstract
High-precision measurements of the lepton’s electromagnetic dipole moments provide a powerful probe for testing the Standard Model. A non-zero value of the τ \tau lepton’s electric(weak) dipole moment ( d τ γ d_{\tau}^{\gamma} , d τ Z d_{\tau}^{Z} ) would serve as a smoking gun of the new physics. On the one hand, current and future high energy colliders offer an ideal environment for such measurements. On the other hand, it is essential to investigate the optimal measurement method for extracting not only d τ γ d_{\tau}^{\gamma} and d τ Z d_{\tau}^{Z} , but also the anomalous magnetic dipole moment ( a τ a_{\tau} ). In this work, we analyze the precision of observables, particularly optimal observables, for determining these physics quantities through the process e + ​ e − → γ ∗ / Z → τ + ​ τ − e^{+}e^{-}\to\gamma^{*}/Z\to\tau^{+}\tau^{-} with τ \tau leptons undergoing (semi-)leptonic and hadronic decays at the centre-of-mass energies at the Z pole mass and significantly below m Z m_{Z} . By considering the full kinematic information of the decay products, we find that the sensitivities to Im d τ d_{\tau} and Re d τ d_{\tau} can reach 10 − 21 10^{-21} e ​ c ​ m ecm at CEPC s = m Z \sqrt{s}=m_{Z} , compared to 10 − 20 10^{-20} e ​ c ​ m ecm at Belle-II Υ ⁡ ( 4 ​ S ) \Upsilon(4S) resonance and 10 − 17 10^{-17} e ​ c ​ m ecm at BEPCII ψ ⁡ ( 2 ​ S ) \psi(2S) resonance. For a τ a_{\tau} , we find that Im a τ a_{\tau} and Re a τ a_{\tau} with a precision of 10 − 5 10^{-5} at Belle-II s = 10.58 \sqrt{s}=10.58 GeV can be attained.

\displaystyle e^{-}(p_{1})+e^{+}(p_{2})\to\tau^{-}(k_{1},\alpha)+\tau^{+}(k_{2},\beta)\to A+\bar{B}.

(4)

In this process, the general form factors for

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\mu}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=-ieQ_{\tau}(\gamma^{\mu}+\frac{\sigma_{\mu\nu}q^{\nu}}{2m}[iF_{1}(q^{2})+F_{2}(q^{2})\gamma_{5}])

(5)

\displaystyle+\frac{g_{Z}}{2}(\gamma^{\mu}(V_{\tau}-\gamma_{5}A_{\tau})+\frac{\sigma_{\mu\nu}q^{\nu}}{2m}[iG_{1}(q^{2})+G_{2}(q^{2})\gamma_{5}]),

\tau^{\pm}

\displaystyle\frac{eQ_{\tau}}{2m}F_{2}(0)=d^{\gamma}_{\tau}.

(7)

\displaystyle s^{\mu}_{1}

\displaystyle=(\frac{\mathbb{k_{1}}\cdot\hat{s}_{1}}{m},\hat{s}_{1}+\frac{\mathbb{k_{1}}(\mathbb{k_{1}}\cdot\hat{s}_{1})}{m(m+k_{0})}),

\displaystyle=(-\frac{\mathbb{k_{1}}\cdot\hat{s}_{2}}{m},\hat{s}_{2}+\frac{\mathbb{k_{1}}(\mathbb{k_{1}}\cdot\hat{s}_{2})}{m(m+k_{0})}),

III
Observables

In this section, we discuss the simple observables and the so-called optimal observables, along with their sensitivities for determining the values of
d_{\tau}
and
a_{\tau}^{NP}
. We decompose the differential cross section of the reaction(
4
) according to
g_{j}
, neglecting terms quadratic in
g_{j}
:

\displaystyle d\sigma_{a\bar{b}}=(S_{0}+S_{1,Red_{\tau}}\text{Re}[d_{\tau}]+S_{1,Imd_{\tau}}\text{Im}[d_{\tau}]+S_{1,Rea_{\tau}}\text{Re}[a_{\tau}]+S_{1,Ima_{\tau}}\text{Im}[a_{\tau}])d\phi,

(13)

here
g_{j}
(
j=1,2,3,4
) represents the quantities
\text{Re}[d_{\tau}],\text{Im}[d_{\tau}],\text{Re}[a^{NP}_{\tau}],\text{and}~\text{Im}[a^{NP}_{\tau}]
to be determined.
To determine these quantities, one needs to choose four observables
\mathcal{O}_{i}
and measure their expectation values in the same experimental run:

\displaystyle E[\mathcal{O}_{i}]=E_{0}[\mathcal{O}_{i}]+\sum_{j}c_{ij}g_{j},

(14)

E_{0}[\mathcal{O}_{i}]
denotes the expectation value without contributions from
g_{j}
and
c_{ij}
is the coefficient matrix that specifies the relationship between
E[\mathcal{O}_{i}]
and the
g_{j}
.
The goal is to measure
g_{j}
as accurately as possible. Consider a set of unbiased estimators, denoted as
\gamma_{i}
, defined as

\gamma_{i}=\sum_{j}c^{-1}_{ij}(E[\mathcal{O}_{i}]-E_{0}[\mathcal{O}_{i}])
. The expectation values for these estimators are
E[\gamma_{i}]=g_{i}
. Expanding in
g_{j}
gives

\displaystyle E[\mathcal{O}_{i}]=\frac{\int d\phi S_{0}\mathcal{O}_{i}}{\int d\phi S_{0}}+\sum_{j}\left(\frac{\int d\phi S_{1,j}\mathcal{O}_{i}}{\int d\phi S_{0}}-\frac{\int d\phi S_{0}\mathcal{O}_{i}\int d\phi S_{1,j}}{(\int d\phi S_{0})^{2}}\right)g_{j}.

(15)

To further assess the sensitivity of observables to those small couplings, one must compare with the errors in the measurement of expectation of observables. If
N
events are recorded, these error will be given by
\sqrt{V(\mathcal{O})_{ij}/N}
, with

\displaystyle V(\mathcal{O})_{ij}=\frac{d\phi S_{0}\mathcal{O}_{i}\mathcal{O}_{j}}{\int d\phi S_{0}}-\frac{\int d\phi S_{0}\mathcal{O}_{i}\int d\phi S_{0}\mathcal{O}_{j}}{(\int d\phi S_{0})^{2}}

(16)

being the covariance matrix of the quantities
\mathcal{O}
for
g_{j}=0
. This matrix is symmetric and positive semi-definite and will be a diagonal matrix in the case of all
\mathcal{O}
being un-correlated observables. Thus, the ‘one-sigma’ standard deviation or the so-called background-to-signal ratio
\delta g_{i}
, with which the presence of non-zero
g_{i}
can be ascertained reads

\displaystyle\delta g_{i}=\frac{1}{|c_{ij}|}\sqrt{\frac{V(\mathcal{O})_{ij}}{N}}.

(17)

We now discuss the four optimal observables which reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{i}=\frac{S_{1,i}}{S_{0}},(i=Red_{\tau},Imd_{\tau},Rea_{\tau},Ima_{\tau})

(18)

that can optimize the signal-to-background ratio in the measurements
Atwood:1991ka
;
Diehl:1993br
.

S_{1,i}
and
S_{0}
are the differential cross section defined by eq.(
13
). Clearly, optimal observables inherit whatever discrete asymmetries
S_{1,i}
has with respect to
S_{0}
. In this work, it is suitable to define observables especially CP odd observables in terms of process (
4
) and its charge conjugated decays. The expectation value of the observables of interest are defined as the following average

\displaystyle E^{ab}[\mathcal{O}]=\frac{1}{2}(\frac{\int d\sigma_{a\bar{b}}\mathcal{O}}{\int d\sigma_{a\bar{b}}}+\frac{\int d\sigma_{b\bar{a}}\mathcal{O}}{\int d\sigma_{b\bar{a}}}),

(19)

where
d\sigma_{b\bar{a}}
is the differential cross section for the charge-conjugate process of reaction(
4
).
These optimal observables share the same transformation properties under CP and T as the couplings. Specifically, observables sensitive to the
\gamma\tau\tau
couplings will also be sensitive to the corresponding
Z\tau\tau
couplings.
For example, the expectation value of CP odd observables can only project CP odd term of the differential cross section in eq.(
13
). Consequently, for CP even SM contribution, any non-zero expectation value for a CP odd observable serves as a clear signal of CP violation in the reaction. The reverse is also true: CP even term of differential cross section do not contribute to the expectation value of CP odd observable. The discussion here assumes the phase space cuts in experimental measurements are CP blind.

In addition to optimal observables, we also study simple observables constructed solely from the momenta of the decay products of
\tau^{+}\tau^{-}
. For the case of the imaginary and real E(W)DM couplings, we choose simple observables of the form

\displaystyle T^{ij}=(\mathbb{q}_{+}-\mathbb{q}_{-})^{i}(\mathbb{q}_{+}\times\mathbb{q}_{-})^{j}+(i\leftrightarrow j),~~\text{odd under CP; odd under T},

(20)

\displaystyle\hat{O}^{ij}=(\hat{\mathbb{q}}_{+}+\hat{\mathbb{q}}_{-})^{i}(\hat{\mathbb{q}}_{+}-\hat{\mathbb{q}}_{-})^{j}+(i\leftrightarrow j),~~\text{odd under CP; even under T},

which have the same symmetry as
S_{1,Red_{\tau}}
and
S_{1,Imd_{\tau}}
. Here,
\mathbb{q}_{\pm}
are the three-momenta of the final states defined in the
e^{+}e^{-}
frame,
\hat{\mathbb{q}}_{\pm}=\mathbb{q}_{\pm}/|\mathbb{q}_{\pm}|
and
i,j
are the Cartesian vector indices.
For the case of real and imaginary AMDM couplings, we consider simple observables of the form

\displaystyle T_{a}^{ij}=(\mathbb{q}_{+}+\mathbb{q}_{-})^{i}(\mathbb{q}_{+}\times\mathbb{q}_{-})^{j}+(i\leftrightarrow j),~~\text{even under CP; odd under T},

(21)

and

\displaystyle\hat{O}^{ij}_{a}=\hat{\mathbb{q}}^{i}_{+}\cdot\hat{\mathbb{q}}^{j}_{-}+(i\leftrightarrow j),~~\text{even under CP; even under T},

(22)

which have the same symmetry as
S_{1,Ima_{\tau}}
and
S_{1,Rea_{\tau}}
.

In the case
a=b
, as discussed above, for the CP odd observables, only the term corresponding to the CP odd differential cross section contributes to their expectation value. This is a direct consequence of the CP symmetry properties, which imply

\displaystyle E_{0}^{a\bar{a}}[\mathcal{O}_{Red_{\tau}}]=0,~~E_{0}^{a\bar{a}}[\mathcal{O}_{Imd_{\tau}}]=0,

(23)

\displaystyle E_{0}^{a\bar{a}}[T^{ij}]=0,~~E_{0}^{a\bar{a}}[\hat{O}^{ij}]=0.

(24)

The cross-term drops out by further applying T symmetry properties, which imply

\displaystyle E_{0}^{a\bar{a}}[\mathcal{O}_{Red_{\tau}}\mathcal{O}_{Imd_{\tau}}]=0,

(25)

\displaystyle E_{0}^{a\bar{a}}[T^{ij}\hat{O}^{ij}]=0.

Thus, the covariance matrix and coefficient matrix are both diagonal. For cases where
a\neq b
, applying the T transformation results in the following SM expectation values:

\displaystyle E_{0}^{a\bar{b}}[\mathcal{O}_{Red_{\tau}}]=0,~~~~E_{0}^{a\bar{b}}[T^{ij}]=0,

(26)

\displaystyle E_{0}^{a\bar{b}}[\mathcal{O}_{Red_{\tau}}\mathcal{O}_{Imd_{\tau}}]=0,~~E_{0}^{a\bar{b}}[T^{ij}\hat{O}^{ij}]=0,

while
E_{0}^{a\bar{b}}[\hat{O}^{ij}]\neq 0
,
E_{0}^{a\bar{b}}[\mathcal{O}_{Imd_{\tau}}]\neq 0
. This behavior arises because the final states are no longer CP symmetric in such cases. Consequently, the average observables defined in eq.(
19
) are used. With the CP property of
\hat{O}^{ij}
and
\mathcal{O}_{Imd_{\tau}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle E_{0}^{ab}[\hat{O}^{ij}]=0,~~E_{0}^{ab}[\mathcal{O}_{Imd_{\tau}}]=0.

(27)

Any non-zero value of
E^{ab}[\mathcal{O}]
may be regarded as a potential signal of the NP.

For the process where
\tau
decays into three
\pi
, the
E_{0}^{ab}[\mathcal{O}_{Red_{\tau}}\mathcal{O}_{Imd_{\tau}}]\neq 0
is induced by absorptive parts originating from the finite widths of the intermediate resonances. However, this term is significantly smaller compared to our numerical uncertainties and can thus be safely neglected. Again
E_{0}^{ab}[\mathcal{O}_{Red_{\tau}}]=0
and
E_{0}^{ab}[\mathcal{O}_{Imd_{\tau}}]=0
hold in this 3
\pi
process.

Now, we consider the expectation values of CP even observables.
Since the SM expectation value of
\mathcal{O}_{Ima_{\tau}}
and
\mathcal{O}_{Rea_{\tau}}
are not equal to zero, we utilize the general relationship between expectation values of observables and the values of
a_{\tau}^{NP}
as defined in eq.(
15
).

Based on the above discussion, the expectation values of the simple observables eq.(
20
) for E(W)DM and eq.(
21
), eq.(
22
) for AMDM are of the form

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}E^{ab}[T^{ij}]\\
E^{ab}[\hat{O}^{ij}]\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}c_{ab}&0\\
0&\bar{c}_{ab}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\text{Re}[\hat{d}^{V}_{\tau}]~s^{ij}\\
\text{ Im}[\hat{d}^{V}_{\tau}]~s^{ij}\end{array}\right),

with dimensionless E(W)DM form factors defined by

\displaystyle\text{Re}[\hat{d}^{V}_{\tau}]=\frac{\sqrt{s}}{e}\text{Re}[d^{V}_{\tau}],~~\text{ Im}[\hat{d}^{V}_{\tau}]=\frac{\sqrt{s}}{e}\text{Im}[d_{\tau}],

(34)

and

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}E^{ab}[T^{ij}_{a}]\\
E^{ab}[\hat{O}^{ij}_{a}]\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}k^{11}_{a\bar{b}}&k^{12}_{a\bar{b}}\\
\bar{k}^{21}_{a\bar{b}}&\bar{k}^{22}_{a\bar{b}}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\text{Im}[a^{NP}_{\tau}]~s^{ij}\\
\text{Re}[a^{NP}_{\tau}]~s^{ij}\end{array}\right)+\left(\begin{array}[]{c}E_{0}^{ab}[T_{a}^{ij}]\\
E_{0}^{ab}[\hat{O}_{a}^{ij}]\\
\end{array}\right)

where
s_{ij}
is the tensor polarization of the intermediate
\gamma^{*}/Z
state, which can be written as
\text{diag}(-\frac{1}{6},-\frac{1}{6},\frac{1}{3})
if the beam direction is identified with the z-axis.
When the coefficient matrix is not diagonal in eq.(
IV
), using the ‘whitening transformation’ to de-correlate the observables, one can find a linearly-approximated optimal basis out of the original chosen ones.
The resulting ideal one standard deviation (1 s.d.) sensitivity of the E(W)DM couplings are given by

\displaystyle\delta\rm Red_{\tau}=\frac{e}{\sqrt{s}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{ab}}}\frac{3[E^{ab}_{0}[T^{2}_{33}]]^{1/2}}{|c_{ab}|},~~~\delta\rm Imd_{\tau}=\frac{e}{\sqrt{s}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{ab}}}\frac{3[E^{ab}_{0}[\hat{O}^{2}_{33}]]^{1/2}}{|\bar{c}_{ab}|},

(43)

where
N_{ab}=2N_{\tau\tau}Br(\tau\to a)Br(\tau\to b)
with
N_{\tau\tau}
being the number of
\tau
pair events. After the diagonalization operation of the covariance matrix, the 1 s.d. sensitivity of the AMDM couplings can be written as

\displaystyle\delta\rm Rea_{\tau}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{ab}}}\frac{3[V^{{}^{\prime}}(\hat{O}_{a,33}^{ab})]^{1/2}}{|\kappa_{ab}|},~~~\delta\rm Ima_{\tau}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{ab}}}\frac{3[V^{{}^{\prime}}(T_{a,33}^{ab})]^{1/2}}{|\bar{\kappa}_{ab}|},

(44)

where
\kappa_{ab}
,
\bar{\kappa}_{ab}
are components of the new diagonal coefficient matrix and
V^{{}^{\prime}}(\hat{O}_{a,33}^{ab})
,
V^{{}^{\prime}}(T_{a,33}^{ab})
are components of the new diagonal covariance matrix.

The expectation values of the optimal observables defined in eq.(
18
) are of the form

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}E^{ab}[\mathcal{O}_{Red_{\tau}}]\\
E^{ab}[\mathcal{O}_{Imd_{\tau}}]\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\omega_{ab}&0\\
0&\bar{\omega}_{ab}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\text{Re}[\hat{d}^{V}_{\tau}]\\
\text{ Im}[\hat{d}^{V}_{\tau}]\end{array}\right),

and

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}E^{ab}[\mathcal{O}_{Rea_{\tau}}]\\
E^{ab}[\mathcal{O}_{Ima_{\tau}}]\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\xi^{11}_{a\bar{b}}&\xi^{12}_{a\bar{b}}\\
\bar{\xi}^{21}_{a\bar{b}}&\bar{\xi}^{22}_{a\bar{b}}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\text{Im}[a^{NP}_{\tau}]\\
\text{Re}[a^{NP}_{\tau}]\end{array}\right)+\left(\begin{array}[]{c}E_{0}^{ab}[\mathcal{O}_{Rea_{\tau}}]\\
E_{0}^{ab}[\mathcal{O}_{Ima_{\tau}}]\\
\end{array}\right).

The definition of the variables in the above expressions are similar to simple observables.

V
Summary

The precise measurement of the
\tau
lepton’s electric(weak) dipole moment and anomalous magnetic dipole moment provide a powerful probe for testing the SM and uncovering NP. Electron-positron colliders offer an ideal place for these studies. With ongoing advancements in both the collider energy and luminosity,
e^{+}e^{-}
facilities will produce large datasets of
\tau^{+}\tau^{-}
, hence the search for
\tau
E(W)DM and AMDM will enter a precision era.

In this work, we study
\tau
pair production in
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions with subsequent decays of
\tau^{\pm}
to (semi)leptonic or hadronic channels in various scenarios. For all one-prong and multi-prongs decay channels considered, we take the full kinematic information of the final-states system into account by incorporating the respective differential
\tau
lepton decay density matrix into its production density matrix. The one standard deviation sensitivity to the real and imaginary components of E(W)DM and AMDM are estimated through both simple and optimal observables, with comprehensive analysis of their expectation values and covariance.

At BEPC II, optimal observables yield an order of magnitude improvement in precision over simple observables for measurements of real and imaginary components of the
\tau
EDM at center-of-mass energies of 3.686 GeV and 5.6 GeV. Specifically, with
3.5\times 10^{6}

\tau
pair production at
\sqrt{s}=3.686
GeV, the sensitivities derived from optimal observables read
\delta\rm Red_{\tau}=4.87\times 10^{-17}

ecm
and
\delta\rm Imd_{\tau}=2.40\times 10^{-17}

ecm
, with further enhancement at higher energies.
For Belle-II, our results project the precisions of
9.42\times 10^{-20}

ecm
and
2.43\times 10^{-20}

ecm
for
\rm Red_{\tau}
and
\rm Imd_{\tau}
respectively, while for AMDM
\delta\rm Rea_{\tau}=1.88\times 10^{-5}
and
\delta\rm Ima_{\tau}=1.76\times 10^{-5}
. CEPC has the capability to measure
\tau
lepton WDM with precisions reach the level of
10^{-21}

ecm
, with specific values of
\delta\rm Red_{\tau}=1.12\times 10^{-21}and~\delta\rm Imd_{\tau}=1.78\times 10^{-21}
at the Z mass energy, which is sufficient to be sensitive to certain BSM models. Precision measurement of the
\tau
lepton’s electromagnetic moments using optimal observables at current and future electron-positron colliders will provide significant information about physics beyond the standard model, offering high sensitivity to leptonic CP violation.

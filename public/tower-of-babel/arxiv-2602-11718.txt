Introduction

3.

3.1.
Excess Bundle for Lagrangian Intersections

Lemma 3.1
.

If
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are two smooth Lagrangians of
X
with a clean intersection
B
, then the excess bundle is isomorphic to the cotangent bundle of
B
:

E\cong\Omega_{B}.

Proof.

It depends on the following local calculations.
Let
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
be two linear Lagrangian subspaces of a symplectic vector space
(V,\omega)
. Let
E
be the excess space of
L_{1}\cap L_{2}
,
i.e.

E\cong V/(L_{1}+L_{2})
.
Define a linear map

\varphi:V\longrightarrow(L_{1}\cap L_{2})^{\vee}

by
\varphi(v)=\omega(v,-)\in(L_{1}\cap L_{2})^{\vee}
, which is clearly surjective. Then the linear functional
\omega(v,-)
vanishes for every
w\in L_{1}\cap L_{2}
as long as
v\in L_{1}+L_{2}
by the Lagrangian conditions, which means
\operatorname{Ker}(\varphi)\cong L_{1}+L_{2}
, and hence
E\cong(L_{1}+L_{2})^{\vee}
.
∎

The following is observed by Mladenov in
[
18
]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
be two smooth Lagrangians of
X
with a clean intersection
B
. Then

\displaystyle(K_{C_{1}}^{\vee}\otimes K_{C_{2}})|_{B}\otimes\det(N_{B/C_{2}})^{\otimes 2}\cong\mathcal{O}_{B}.

where
K_{C_{i}}
is the canonical bundle on
C_{i}
(
i=1,2
).

Proof.

By Lemma
2.3
and
3.1
, there is short exact sequence:

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow N_{B/C_{2}}\longrightarrow N_{C_{1}/X}|_{B}\longrightarrow\Omega_{B}\longrightarrow 0.

Note that
N_{C_{1}/X}\cong\Omega_{C_{1}}
, and then

(3.1)

\displaystyle K_{C_{1}}|_{B}\cong K_{B}\otimes\det(N_{B/C_{2}}).

The determinant of the conormal sequence of
B\subset C_{2}
gives

(3.2)

\displaystyle K_{C_{2}}\cong K_{B}\otimes\det(N_{B/C_{2}}^{\vee}).

Thus
(K_{C_{1}}^{\vee}\otimes K_{C_{2}})|_{B}\otimes\det(N_{B/C_{2}})^{\otimes 2}\cong\mathcal{O}_{B}
holds by (
3.1
) and (
3.2
).
∎

3.2.
Lagrangian Intersections and Ext Groups

The excess sequence (
2.12
) becomes

0\longrightarrow TC_{1}|_{B}+TC_{2}|_{B}\longrightarrow TX|_{B}\longrightarrow\Omega_{B}\longrightarrow 0,

which is also equivalent to the conormal sequence of
B\subset X
:

(3.3)

0\longrightarrow N_{B/X}^{\vee}\longrightarrow\Omega_{X}|_{B}\longrightarrow\Omega_{B}\longrightarrow 0.

Based on Theorem
2.7
and Corollary
2.8
, we have:

Proposition 3.3
.

If
(C_{1},C_{2})
is a pair of smooth Lagrangians in
X
with a clean intersection
B
, and the conormal sequence (
3.3
) of
B\subset X
splits, then

(3.4)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ext}^{\bullet}(j_{1*}\mathcal{O}_{C_{1}},j_{2*}\mathcal{O}_{C_{2}})\cong\bigoplus_{p+q+m=\bullet}\operatorname{H}^{q}(B,\Omega_{B}^{p-m}\otimes det(N_{B/C_{2}})),

(3.5)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ext}_{X}^{\bullet}(j_{1*}F_{1},j_{2*}F_{2})\cong\bigoplus_{p+q+m=\bullet}\operatorname{H}^{q}(B,\Omega_{B}^{p-m}\otimes(F_{1}^{\vee}\otimes F_{2})|_{B}\otimes det(N_{B/C_{2}}))

where
m=\operatorname{codim}(B,C_{2})
, and
F_{1}
(resp.
F_{2}
) is a perfect complex on
C_{1}
(resp.
C_{2}
).

As mentioned in the introduction, there exists cases that
\det(N_{B/C_{2}})
or
(F_{1}^{\vee}\otimes F_{2})|_{B}\otimes\det(N_{B/C_{2}})
is torsion so that we can deduce that RHS of (
3.4
) or (
3.5
) calculates Hodge cohomology of
B
in local coefficients.

3.2.1.
2-torsion local system from spin Lagrangian intersections

Recall that a Kähler manifold is spin if and only if its canonical bundle admits square roots. Now suppose that both the Lagrangians
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
of
X
are spin, and the square roots of the canonical bundles
K_{C_{1}}
of
C_{1}
and
K_{C_{2}}
of
C_{2}
are denoted as
K_{C_{1}}^{1/2}
and
K_{C_{2}}^{1/2}
.
Then Lemma
3.2
gives
(K_{C_{1}}^{\vee}\otimes K_{C_{2}})|_{B}\otimes\det(N_{B/C_{2}})^{\otimes 2}\cong\mathcal{O}_{B}
, which implies that
(K_{C_{1}}^{-1/2}\otimes K_{C_{2}}^{1/2})|_{B}\otimes\det(N_{B/C_{2}})
determines a 2-torsion
\mathbb{C}
-local system
\mathfrak{E}
on
B
.

3.2.2.
Covering trick

If we assume that
\det(N_{B/C_{2}})^{\otimes n}\cong\mathcal{O}_{B}
, and thus
\det(N_{B/C_{2}})
determines a n-torsion rank one
\mathbb{C}
-local system
\mathfrak{L}:=\det(N_{B/C_{2}})_{\delta}
on
B
, then RHS of (
3.4
) calculates the
\mathfrak{L}
-twisted Hodge cohomology of
B
.
By the covering trick in Chapter 1 of
[
5
]
, there is a
n
-cyclic covering

c:\widetilde{B}\longrightarrow B
such that

c_{*}\mathbb{C}\cong\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathfrak{L}^{\otimes k}.

Then by adjointness of
c_{*}
and
c^{-1}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(\widetilde{B},\mathbb{C})\cong\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(B,c_{*}\mathbb{C})=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n-1}\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(B,\mathfrak{L}^{\otimes k}).

Similarly, we have
c_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{B}}\cong\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathfrak{L}^{\otimes k}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{B}
, and
c^{*}\Omega_{B}\cong\Omega_{\widetilde{B}}
. Then

(3.7)

\displaystyle\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(\widetilde{B},\Omega^{i}_{\widetilde{B}})\cong\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(\widetilde{B},c^{*}{\Omega_{B}})=\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(B,c_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{B}}\otimes\Omega^{i}_{B})\cong\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n-1}\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(B,\Omega^{i}\otimes\mathfrak{L}^{\otimes k}).

There is a twisted Hodge-de Rham spectral sequence

(3.8)

\displaystyle E_{2}^{p,q}=\operatorname{H}^{q}(B,\Omega^{p}\otimes\mathfrak{L}^{\otimes k})\Longrightarrow\operatorname{H}^{p+q}(B,\mathfrak{L}^{\otimes k})\quad\text{for each }k,

which implies an inequality

(3.9)

\displaystyle\dim\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(B,\mathfrak{L}^{\otimes k})\leq\sum_{p+q=\bullet}\dim\operatorname{H}^{q}(B,\Omega^{p}\otimes\mathfrak{L}^{\otimes k}).

If
B
is proper and thus
\tilde{B}
is also proper, then Hodge theory on
\widetilde{B}
implies that

(3.10)

\displaystyle\dim\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(\widetilde{B},\mathbb{C})=\sum_{p+q=\bullet}\dim\operatorname{H}^{q}(\widetilde{B},\Omega^{p}_{\widetilde{B}}).

Now the combination of the equalities (
3.6
), (
3.7
) and (
3.10
) forces (
3.9
) to be an equality for each
k
, and hence the spectral sequence (
3.8
) degenerates at
E_{2}
page, which implies that

\bigoplus_{p+q=\bullet}\operatorname{H}^{q}(B,\Omega_{B}^{p}\otimes\mathfrak{L})\cong\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(B,\mathfrak{L}).

So we have:

Proposition 3.4
.

(1)

Let
(C_{1},C_{2})
be a pair of Lagrangians of
X
as in Proposition
3.3
. If
B
is proper and
\det(N_{B/C_{2}})
is torsion, then

\operatorname{Ext}_{X}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{O}_{C_{1}},\mathcal{O}_{C_{2}})\cong\operatorname{H}^{\bullet-m}(B,\det(N_{B/C_{2}})_{\delta}),

where
m=\operatorname{codim}(B,C_{2})
.

(2)

As a special case, if
C\subset X
is a proper Lagrangian such that the conormal sequence of
C\subset X
splits, then
B=C_{1}=C_{2}=C
,
\det(N_{B/C_{2}})_{\delta})\cong\mathbb{C}
, and

\operatorname{Ext}^{\bullet}_{X}(\mathcal{O}_{C},\mathcal{O}_{C})\cong\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(C,\mathbb{C}).

The following is a weaker version of Theorem 0.1.9 in
[
18
]
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
(C_{1},C_{2})
be a pair of Lagrangians of
X
as in Proposition
3.3
. If both
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are spin and
B
is proper, then

\operatorname{Ext}^{\bullet}_{X}(K_{C_{1}}^{1/2},K_{2}^{1/2})\cong\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(B,(K_{C_{1}}^{-1/2}|_{B}\otimes K_{C_{2}}^{1/2}|_{B}\otimes\det(N_{B/C_{2}}))_{\delta}).

3.3.
Cotangent Bundle

Let
X=T^{\vee}M:=\operatorname{Tot}_{M}(\Omega_{M})
be the total space of the cotangent bundle of a smooth quasi-projective variety
M
, which admits a canonical holomorphic symplectic 2-form
\omega_{can}
. Denote
\pi:X\longrightarrow M
the bundle morphism. There is a canonical 1-form
\theta_{can}
on
X
, which can be described explicitly. For
(p,q)\in T^{\vee}M
, or
p\in M
and
q\in T^{\vee}_{p}M
,

\theta_{can}(p,q)=\pi^{*}q,\,\,\text{and }\omega_{can}=d\theta_{can}.

3.3.1.
Zero section Lagrangian

The zero section
j:M\hookrightarrow T^{\vee}M
is always a Lagrangian of
T^{\vee}M=X
with split normal sequence. Thus,

Corollary 3.6
.

The self Ext group of the zero section is the Hodge cohomology:

(3.11)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ext}_{T^{\vee}M}^{\bullet}(j_{*}\mathcal{O}_{M},j_{*}\mathcal{O}_{M})\cong\bigoplus_{p+q=\bullet}\operatorname{H}^{q}(M,\Omega_{M}^{p}).

If
M
is proper, then by Hodge theory,

(3.12)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ext}_{T^{\vee}M}^{\bullet}(j_{*}\mathcal{O}_{M},j_{*}\mathcal{O}_{M})\cong\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(M,\mathbb{C}).

3.3.2.
Graph of closed 1-forms

The graph
\Gamma_{\eta}
of a holomorphic closed 1-form
\eta\in\operatorname{H}^{0}(M,\Omega)
is a Lagrangian of
X=T^{\vee}M
.

Lemma 3.7
.

If the zero locus of
\eta
,
Z(\eta)
is a smooth variety, then
\det(N_{Z(\eta)/M})^{\otimes 2}\cong\mathcal{O}_{Z(\eta)}
.

Proof.

We need to show that
\det(N_{Z(\eta)/M})^{\otimes 2}\cong\mathcal{O}_{Z(\eta)}
.
Let
F
be a vector bundle on
M
, and
s\in\operatorname{H}^{0}(M,F)
be a section. Assume that the zero locus
Z(s)\subset M
is a smooth variety. Then the differential of the section
Ds|_{Z}
gives a morphism
TM|_{Z(s)}\longrightarrow F|_{Z(s)}
, which descends to an injective morphism

N_{Z(s)/M}\longrightarrow F|_{Z(s)}.

Now
F=\Omega_{M}
and
s=\eta
. Then there is an embedding.

N_{Z(\eta)/M}\hookrightarrow\Omega_{M}|_{Z(\eta)}.

If we compose the above embedding by the natural map
\Omega_{M}|_{Z(\eta)}\longrightarrow\Omega_{Z(\eta)}
, it becomes a zero map. Hence, there is an embedding
N_{Z(\eta)/M}\hookrightarrow N_{Z(\eta)/M}^{\vee}
which is forced to be an isomorphism by equal ranks of both sides. Therefore, the statement concludes by taking the determinant of them.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
\eta
be a holomorphic closed 1-form on
M
with smooth zero locus
Z(\eta)
, then

\operatorname{Ext}_{T^{\vee}M}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma(\eta)},\mathcal{O}_{M})\cong\bigoplus_{p+q=\bullet}\operatorname{H}^{q-n}(Z(\eta),\Omega_{Z(\eta)}^{p}(\mathfrak{L}))),

with
n=\operatorname{codim}(Z(\eta),M)
and if
Z(\eta)
is proper, then

\operatorname{Ext}_{T^{\vee}M}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma(\eta)},\mathcal{O}_{M})\cong\operatorname{H}^{\bullet-n}(Z(\eta),\det(N_{Z(\eta)/M})_{\delta}).

3.3.3.
Conormal varieties

The conormal bundle
N_{Z/M}^{\vee}
of a smooth variety
Z\subset M
is a Lagrangian of
X=T^{\vee}M
. Let
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
be two smooth subvarieties of
M
with a transverse intersection
Z=Z_{1}\cap Z_{2}
. Then
C_{1}\cap C_{2}=Z
and the conormal sequence of
Z\subset X=T^{\vee}M
splits.

Corollary 3.9
.

For conormal varieties
C_{1}=N_{Z_{1}/M}^{\vee},C_{2}=N_{Z_{2}/M}^{\vee}\subset T^{\vee}M
as above,

\operatorname{Ext}^{\bullet}_{T^{\vee}M}(\mathcal{O}_{C_{1}},\mathcal{O}_{C_{2}})\cong\bigoplus_{p+q+n=\bullet}\operatorname{H}^{q}(Z,\Omega_{Z}^{p-n}\otimes\det(N_{Z_{1}/M})\otimes\det(N_{Z_{2}/M}^{\vee})),

where
n=\operatorname{codim}(Z,M)
. In particular, if furthermore,
\det(N_{Z_{1}/M})=\det(N_{Z_{2}/M})
and
Z
is proper, then

\operatorname{Ext}^{\bullet}_{T^{\vee}M}(\mathcal{O}_{C_{1}},\mathcal{O}_{C_{2}})\cong\operatorname{H}^{\bullet-\operatorname{codim}(Z,M)}(Z,\mathbb{C}).

Remark 3.10
.

In general, the derived fiber product of two conormal varieties is quasi-isomorphic to the
(-1)
-shifted cotangent space of the derived fiber product
Z_{1}\times_{M}Z_{2}
by
[
7
]
,

C_{1}\times_{X}C_{2}\cong\operatorname{Tot}_{Z_{1}\times_{M}Z_{2}}(\mathbb{L}_{Z_{1}\times_{M}Z_{2}}[-1]),

which is quasi-isomorphic to
\operatorname{Tot}_{Z_{1}\cap Z_{2}}(\Omega_{Z_{1}\cap Z_{2}}[-1])
if
Z_{1}\cap Z_{2}
transversely.

4.
Equivariant Lagrangian Intersection and Symplectic reduction

We expect that Proposition
3.3
and
3.4
hold for symplectic reduction of
X
by Hamiltonian
G
-action with
G
a reductive group.

4.1.
G-invariant Lagrangians

Suppose that there is a Hamiltonian
G
-action on
(X,\omega)
with a holomorphic moment map

\mu:\,X\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\vee},
and let
C\hookrightarrow X
be a
G
-invariant connected Lagrangian,
i.e.
,
C
is a connected Lagrangian, and
g\cdot C\subset C
for every
g\in G
.
We first recall the following fact.

Lemma 4.1
.

The moment image of
C
consists of a single central element
c\in\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha(t)\subset C
be any curve and
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
. Denote
\xi^{\#}
be the generating vector field on
X
. By definition of moment map,
d\mu_{\xi}(\alpha^{\prime}(t))=\omega(\alpha^{\prime}(t),\xi^{\#})=0
since
\alpha^{\prime}(t)
is an isotropic vector. Hence, the tangent map
d\mu|_{C}=0
, which implies that the image of
M
consists of a single point
c
. Note that
\mu
is
G
-equivariant, and
C
is G-invariant,
c
must be a central element.
∎

4.2.
Symplectic Reductions and Shifted Symplectic Structures

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu}

We write the derived stack

as
X\sslash G
, called the
symplectic quotient stack
of
X
by
G
. See
[
20
]
,
[

]
and

]
for the background of shifted symplectic structures, reductions and shifted Lagrangians.

Proposition 4.2

(

and
[

The derived quotient stack

is a

induces a (0-shifted) Lagrangian morphism
\alpha:C/G\longrightarrow X\sslash G

\mathbb{T}_{C/G}\cong\mathbb{L}_{\alpha}[-1]
, where
\mathbb{L}_{\alpha}
is the relative cotangent complex and
\mathbb{T}_{C/G}
is the tangent complex of

Proof.

Note that
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}/G\cong T^{\vee}[1]BG:=\operatorname{Tot}_{BG}(\mathbb{T}_{BG}[1])
is a 1-shifted symplectic stack. The shifted symplectic structure comes from the canonical symplectic structure of the 1-shifted cotangent stack, which is an analogue of the canonical symplectic structure of the cotangent bundle of a smooth variety.

Both
\mu:X/G\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}/G
and
c:BG\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}/G
are Lagrangian morphisms. The first one can be seen by calculating the relative cotangent complex by the distinguished triangle:

\mu^{*}\mathbb{L}_{\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}/G}\cong[\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g^{\vee}}]\longrightarrow\mathbb{L}_{X/G}\cong[\Omega_{X}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}]\longrightarrow\mathbb{L}_{\mu}\xrightarrow{[1]},

which implies that
\mathbb{L}_{\mu}\cong[\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\Omega_{X}\cong T_{X}]\cong\mathbb{T}_{X/G}.
Similarly for the latter one. Then the derived fiber product
X\sslash G
is a Lagrangian intersection for the 1-shifted symplectic stack
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}/G
, which admits a 0-shifted symplectic structure by
[
20
]
.

The
G
-invariant Lagrangian
C\subset X
induces a morphism

. Proposition
4.2
implies that
\mu\circ j
factors through the right vertical morphism

fitting into the commutative diagram.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C/G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\sslash G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces BG=\{*\}/G}

Appendix A
Twisted Kirwan Surjectivity

A.1.
Twisted Kirwan map

Let
G
be a linear reductive algebraic group, acting on a smooth projective over affine variety
M
with a
G
-equivariant ample line bundle
L
. Suppose that there is a
G
-equivariant
\mathbb{C}
-local system
\mathfrak{L}
on
M
. Then the natural embedding
M^{ss}/G\hookrightarrow M/G
induces a map
Kir
on the twisted cohomology below, called
twisted Kirwan map
:

Kir_{\mathfrak{L}}:\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}_{G}(M,\mathfrak{L})\longrightarrow\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}_{G}(M^{ss},\mathfrak{L}).

When
\mathfrak{L}
is trivial, it is the usual Kirwan map
[
14
]
if
G
acts on
M^{ss}
freely so that the quotient stack
M^{ss}/G
is homeomorphic to the symplectic quotient of
M
by the compact form
G_{c}
of
G
. The aim of this appendix is to give a proof of the following twisted Kirwan surjection. Let
M_{G}=M\times_{BG}EG
be the homotopy quotient of
M
by
G
. Then it is known that
\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}_{G}(M,\mathfrak{L})\cong\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(M_{G},\mathfrak{L})
.

Theorem A.1

(twisted Kirwan surjection)
.

Let
(M,G,L)
be above, then the twisted Kirwan map

Kir:\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}_{G}(M,\mathfrak{L})\longrightarrow\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}_{G}(M^{ss},\mathfrak{L})

is surjective, where
\mathfrak{L}
is any
G
-invariant
\mathbb{C}
-local system on
M
.

We follow the main arguments in
[
14
]
.

A.2.
HKKN stratification of unstable locus

The first ingredient is the HKKN stratification of the unstable locus
M^{us}:=M-M^{ss}
, which will be reviewed briefly. The reader can refer to
[
14
]
for details.
Let
\lambda:\mathbb{G}_{m}\longrightarrow G
be a 1-parameter subgroup (1-PS), and denote
Z\subset M^{\lambda}
a connected component of the fixed locus
M^{\lambda}
. Then the
blade
of
Z
is defined to be the following subvariety of
M
:

Y_{Z,\lambda}=\{x\in M|\lim_{t\to 0}\lambda(t).x\in Z\}.

Then define
S_{\lambda,Z}:=G\cdot Y_{\lambda,Z}
.

Let
L_{\lambda}
be the centralizer of
\lambda
in
G
, and define
U_{\lambda}=\{g\in G|\lim_{t\to 0}\lambda(t)g\lambda(t)^{-1}=1_{G}\}
and
P_{\lambda}=\{g\in G|\lim_{t\to 0}\lambda(t)g\lambda(t)^{-1}\text{ exists}\}
. It is shown in
[
19
]
that
P_{\lambda}
is parabolic,
U_{\lambda}
(resp.
L_{\lambda}
) is the unipotent radical (resp. Levi component) of
P_{\lambda}
. It is not hard to see that
Z_{\lambda}
is
L_{\lambda}
-invariant, and
Y_{\lambda}
is
P_{\lambda}
-invariant.

Theorem A.2

(HKKN stratification
[
14
]
,
[
11
]
)
.

Let
(M,G,L)
be above. There is a finite stratification

M^{us}=\bigsqcup_{\alpha}S_{\alpha},

indexed by 1-PS’s
\lambda_{\alpha}
and connected components of their fixed locus
M^{\lambda_{\alpha}}
, satisfying the following

(1)

The canonical projection
\pi_{\alpha}:Y_{\alpha}\longrightarrow Z_{\alpha}
given by
x\mapsto\lim_{t\to 0}\lambda(t).x
is an affine space bundle;

(2)

The canonical map
G\times_{P_{\alpha}}Y_{\alpha}\longrightarrow G\cdot Y_{\alpha}:=S_{\alpha}
is an isomorphism.

A.3.
Long exact sequences of equivariant cohomology in local coefficient

The second ingredient is the long exact sequences of equivariant cohomology in local coefficients for the HKKN stratification. The reader can refer to
Chapter VI of
[
25
]
for details on cohomology in local coefficients. Let
U_{\beta}=M-\bigsqcup_{\alpha\leq\beta}S_{\alpha}
and
U_{\beta}^{\prime}=M-\bigsqcup_{\alpha<\beta}S_{\alpha}
. Then for the pair
(U_{\beta},U^{\prime}_{\beta})
, there is a long exact sequence:

\cdots\longrightarrow\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}_{G}(U_{\beta},U_{\beta}^{\prime};\mathfrak{L})\xrightarrow{f_{\beta}}\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}_{G}(U_{\beta},\mathfrak{L})\xrightarrow{g_{\beta}}\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}_{G}(U_{\beta}^{\prime},\mathfrak{L})\longrightarrow\operatorname{H}^{\bullet+1}_{G}(U_{\beta},U_{\beta}^{\prime};\mathfrak{L})\longrightarrow\cdots.

Note that

\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}_{G}(U_{\beta},U_{\beta}^{\prime};\mathfrak{L})\cong\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}_{G}(N_{\beta},N_{\beta}^{0};\mathfrak{L})\cong\operatorname{H}^{\bullet-2r_{\beta}}_{G}(S_{\beta},\mathfrak{L}),

where
N_{\beta}
is the normal bundle of
S_{\beta}\subset M
, and
r_{\beta}=rank(N_{\beta})
. The first
\cong
comes from the twisted excision in
[
25
]
. The second is the twisted Thom isomorphism, which holds by a Mayer-Vietoris argument as in the usual untwisted Thom isomorphism.

It is noted that the twisted Kirwan map
Kir_{\mathfrak{L}}
is the composition
\prod_{\beta}g_{\beta}
. In order to show
Kir
is surjective, it is sufficient to show that each
g_{\beta}
is surjective. By means of each long exact sequence, it is equivalent to show that
f_{\beta}
is injective, or equivalently, the following

\operatorname{H}^{\bullet-2r_{\beta}}_{G}(S_{\beta},\mathfrak{L})\longrightarrow\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}_{G}(U_{\beta},\mathfrak{L})

is injective.

We consider the following composition

\operatorname{H}^{\bullet-2r_{\beta}}_{G}(S_{\beta},\mathfrak{L})\longrightarrow\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}_{G}(U_{\beta},\mathfrak{L})\longrightarrow\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}_{G}(S_{\beta},\mathfrak{L}),

where the second map is induced by the inclusion
S_{\beta}\hookrightarrow U_{\beta}
. It is known that the composition is just cupping product with the equivariant Euler class of
N_{\beta}
. Now it is suffices to show that

\operatorname{H}^{\bullet-2r_{\beta}}_{G}(S_{\beta},\mathfrak{L})\xrightarrow{\cup e_{G}(N_{\beta})}\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}_{G}(S_{\beta},\mathfrak{L})

is injective.

A.4.
Atiyah-Bott criterion

The last ingredient lies in the injectivity criterion in Section 13 of
[
4
]
.
Let
Y
be a connected Kähler manifold with a compact Lie group
K
action,
N
be a
G
-equivariant complex vector bundle on it, and
\mathfrak{L}
be a
G
-invariant
\mathbb{C}
-local system on
Y
. The following is the criterion on injectivity of the cup product with
e_{K}(N)
due to Atiyah-Bott
[
4
]
:

Proposition A.3
.

Suppose that there is a subtorus
T_{0}\leq K
which acts trivially on
Y
and that the representation of
T_{0}
on the fibre of
N
at any point of
Y
has no nonzero fixed vectors. Then

\operatorname{H}^{\bullet-2\,rank(N)}_{K}(Y,\mathfrak{L})\xrightarrow{\cup e_{K}(N)}\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}_{K}(Y,\mathfrak{L})

is injective.

Proof.

Let
T_{0}\subset T\subset K
be the maximal torus containing
T_{0}
. Then there is a fibration

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K/T}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y_{T}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y_{K}}

and the associated Leray spectral sequence degenerates at the
E_{2}
page since
K/T
has no odd cohomology:

\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(Y_{T},\mathfrak{L})\cong\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(Y_{K},\mathfrak{L})\otimes\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(K/T),

which implies that

\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(Y_{K},\mathfrak{L})\subset\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(Y_{T},\mathfrak{L})

is a direct summand. Therefore, we may assume that
K=T=T_{0}\times T_{1}
, where
T_{0}
acts on
Y
trivially. Then

\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(Y_{T},\mathfrak{L})\cong\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(BT_{0})\otimes\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(Y_{T_{1}},\mathfrak{L}),

\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(Y_{T},\mathbb{C})\cong\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(BT_{0})\otimes\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(Y_{T_{1}},\mathbb{C}).

Since
\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(BT_{0})
is a polynomial ring, and any
0\neq\alpha_{0}\otimes 1\in\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(BT_{0})\otimes\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(Y_{T_{1}},\mathbb{C})
defines an injective map on
\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(BT_{0})\otimes\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(Y_{T_{1}},\mathfrak{L})
.
Now observe that
\alpha_{0}\otimes 1+\text{higher degree terms}
w.r.t.
Y_{T_{1}}
in
\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(BT_{0})\otimes\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(Y_{T_{1}},\mathbb{C})
also defines an injective map. Since
T_{0}
acts on Y trivially, and the representation of
N
is non-trivial,
e_{K}(N)\in\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(BT_{0})\otimes\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}(Y_{T_{1}},\mathbb{C})
is such a form, and the criterion concludes.

A.5.
Proof of twisted Kirwan surjection

By the argument in

, we reduce to show that

\operatorname{H}^{\bullet-2r_{\beta}}_{G}(S_{\beta},\mathfrak{L})\xrightarrow{\cup e_{G}(N_{\beta})}\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}_{G}(S_{\beta},\mathfrak{L})

is injective. Note that

\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}_{G}(S_{\beta},\mathfrak{L})\cong\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}_{P_{\alpha}}(Y_{\beta},\mathfrak{L})\cong\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}_{L_{\beta}}(Y_{\beta},\mathfrak{L})\cong\operatorname{H}^{\bullet}_{L_{\beta}}(Z_{\beta},\mathfrak{L}).

The first
\cong

A.2
(2), and the second one comes from the

and the last one comes from Theorem

(1). Note also that

is reductive, and contains the 1-PS

trivially. By Hilbert-Mumford’s criterion, the

has to be negative. Now to conclude our statement, it is sufficient to apply Proposition
A.3
then.

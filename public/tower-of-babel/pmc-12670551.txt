Outcomes of immune checkpoint inhibitor rechallenge in advanced urothelial carcinoma: results from a global real-world evidence study☆
Authors: B.A. Maiorano, A. Cigliola, V. Tateo, C. Mercinelli, G.L. Pastorino, N. Dizman, H. Ebrahimi, S.K. Pal, S. Gupta, P. Grivas, A.M. Kamat, P.E. Spiess, N. Agarwal, A. Necchi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSeveral immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) are approved for urothelial carcinoma (UC), but limited information is currently available regarding the efficacy of rechallenging ICIs in patients with previous ICI exposure.Patients and methodsA retrospective study was carried out using the TriNetX® database for a large-scale search of patients with advanced UC (aUC) who had received two separate courses of ICIs (either alone or in combinations). Kaplan–Meier analysis was used to estimate progression-free and overall survival (PFS, OS) of ICI rechallenge. Propensity score matching analysis was run to balance and compare groups based on the duration of ICI rechallenge and stage at diagnosis.ResultsOut of 35 789 patients, a cohort of 292 (0.81%) treated with ≥2 ICI-including lines between 2014 and 2024 was identified. There were 86% bladder versus 14% upper tract UC. At initial diagnosis, 49% had stage IV, whereas 51% had stage I-III disease. The median time on prior ICI treatment was 19.5 months; the median time from the end of ICI to the start of ICI rechallenge was 9.4 months. After a median follow-up of 21.7 months, the median OS of ICI rechallenge was 20.5 months, the median PFS was 10.3 months. Previous use of an ICI in a nonmetastatic setting was associated with longer median OS than both lines given in aUC (P < 0.001). Among those who received a previous ICI in a metastatic setting, a cut-off of 12 months for the ICI rechallenge was associated with a longer median OS (P = 0.027). The longest duration of rechallenge treatment was achieved with anti-programmed cell death protein 1 given after anti-programmed death-ligand 1 therapy (18.2 months, P < 0.001).ConclusionsAlthough it is an uncommon and not standard strategy, previous ICI in nonmetastatic UC and a period of ≥12 months after previous ICI in the metastatic setting were associated with better outcomes after ICI rechallenge in patients with aUC.

Introduction

During the last decade, several anti-programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) and anti-programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) drugs have been approved by regulatory agencies for urothelial carcinoma (UC), initially in advanced UC (aUC).1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 Subsequently, ICIs have been investigated in earlier UC stages.10,11 We are seeing patients progressing to aUC who were already treated with an ICI in the perioperative stages more commonly than in the past years.12, 13, 14

Limited information is available regarding the efficacy of rechallenge with ICI following prior ICI-based therapy.15, 16, 17, 18 The ‘ICI rechallenge’ strategy—defined as the re-administration of an ICI after a previous line of ICI as monotherapy or in combination therapies—has been explored in many tumor types, including UC. However, most available data are represented by very limited retrospective cohorts.19, 20, 21

An increasing number of patients may likely experience aUC progressing after previous ICI treatment received in earlier/localized stages, in which most UC diagnoses occur. Therefore, we explored the characteristics of the patients for whom ICI rechallenge was used and the factors associated with clinical outcomes to evaluate possible patients who may benefit from this treatment strategy for designing future trials.

Patients and methods

Patient selection and data collection

A global population-based retrospective study was carried out using the TriNetX research database for a large-scale search of patients with aUC who underwent two lines of ICIs in their therapeutic course at health care organizations (HCOs) collaborating through a TriNetX-mediated network. TriNetX is a global federated health research platform that provides access to anonymized electronic medical records, including diagnoses, procedures, medications, laboratory, and genomic information from >135 million patients affected by different diseases, including cancer, internationally (United States, Europe, Asia-Pacific, Latin America regions).22

Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines were followed.23

Patients included in the studies were selected from the TriNetX platform, and 143 global HCOs participated. Included patients were affected by UC of the bladder or renal pelvis, ureters, urethra, and treated with at least two separate courses of ICIs, including avelumab, pembrolizumab, nivolumab, durvalumab, and atezolizumab (Supplementary Table S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.105862).

Statistical analyses

Descriptive statistics were used to summarize clinical and demographic characteristics of patients at baseline, using frequencies and proportions for categorical variables, as well as medians, means, ranges, and standard deviations for continuous variables. Fischer’s and t-tests were used to compare categorical and continuous variables. Kaplan–Meier analysis was used to estimate progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) of ICI rechallenge measured from the second ICI start. A propensity-score estimation and matching (PSM) 1 : 1 for age, race, disease site (bladder versus upper tract tumors), and type of administered ICI, was used, aiming to adjust and compare groups of patients based on the timing of ICI rechallenge (with cut-offs of 6 and 12 months from the end of the first ICI to the rechallenge start) and stage at first ICI administration (metastatic versus nonmetastatic).

Treatment durations were compared among the three aggregated groups: anti-PD-L1 post-anti-PD1, anti-PD1 post-anti-PD1, and anti-PD1 post-anti-PD-L1, using the Kruskal–Wallis test. Post hoc pairwise comparisons were conducted using the Mann–Whitney U test with Bonferroni correction to control for multiple comparisons. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05. All analyses were generated and carried out with the TriNetX platform (TriNetX, Cambridge, MA) and R (version 2023.03.0+386) software on 5 January 2025.

The median time on prior ICI was 19.5 months (range 16-27.5 months), considering the overall populations of patients treated with the first ICI both in the nonmetastatic and metastatic stages. The median time from the end of ICI to the start of ICI rechallenge was 9.4 months (range 5.6-30 months). The most common ICI sequence was nivolumab followed by pembrolizumab (28.5%), then avelumab followed by pembrolizumab (22.4%), atezolizumab followed by pembrolizumab (20.8%), pembrolizumab followed by nivolumab (14.9%), pembrolizumab followed by avelumab (13.4%). Pembrolizumab was used as a single agent (50%) or combined with enfortumab vedotin (40%) or cisplatin/carboplatin (10%) (Figure 1B,C).

After a median follow-up of 21.7 months, the median PFS of ICI rechallenge was 10.3 months (range 5.3-13.6 months), and the median OS of ICI rechallenge was 20.5 months (range 12.6-26.4 months). There were three cases of third-line ICI (avelumab, pembrolizumab after nivolumab, or atezolizumab), with an OS of 1.1 months for avelumab and 5.8 and 2.2 months for pembrolizumab after nivolumab and atezolizumab, respectively. As for progression risk, before PSM adjustment, the efficacy of ICI rechallenge was independent of primary tumor site (bladder versus upper tract, P = 0.06), type of previous ICI (P = 0.48), time from previous ICI cut-off (6 months, P = 0.19; 12 months, P = 1.00).

The rechallenging strategies were then grouped by ICI mechanisms of action. The groups had statistically significant differences in the median duration of treatment (mDoT) of the second ICI (H = 35.58, P < 0.001). Discussion

In this real-world data study conducted on a large sample size, we assessed the outcomes of 292 patients with UC who received a rechallenge with ICI-based treatments. Compared with the entire cohort of patients with UC treated with ICIs in the database, we found that this strategy is uncommon (<1% of patients received a second ICI, and almost none a subsequent second rechallenge). Despite lacking prospective data and not being standard, it could represent a potential therapeutic option for a few selected patients. In a scenario with limited treatment options for sequencing strategies, ICI-based therapy can induce response and survival for patients diagnosed with UC in both the earlier to the more advanced stages. However, there is limited evidence of efficacy from ICI rechallenge in patients progressing after previous ICI treatment.

Our data represent the largest series of patients treated with a second ICI after disease progression following a previous ICI. These provide insights into potential therapeutic outcomes when such a treatment approach is adopted. The high number of included patients strengthens the clinical relevance of the data, and a worldwide cohort of patients with aUC who were treated across 143 HCOs is represented. Such patients have been classically excluded from randomized controlled trials evaluating ICI, but considering that ICI-based combinations are increasingly being approved and used across different disease therapy settings of UC, they may progressively become more common in the very near future.

We found that rechallenging an ICI in the subsequent lines of therapy, after progression to a previous ICI, might have efficacy, especially for patients with aUC achieving durable disease control with the first ICI-based therapy, such as those rechallenging after >12 months following the last ICI dose (HR 0.46, P = 0.027). The results could potentially impact recommendations regarding ICI use, based on the paradigm platinum-based chemotherapy rechallenge in patients with UC (e.g., EAU guidelines24). Of course, the results could be partly explained by a more favorable underlying biology and indolent clinical course of the tumor, with major selection bias. Similarly, ICI rechallenge in the metastatic setting after the first ICI was administered in the localized setting was associated with longer median OS (HR 0.35, P < 0.0001). No other factors, including the ICI agent used and shorter cut-off time points for the rechallenge (6 months), gave a positive interaction for survival (all P values > 0.05). This is in line with data from trials testing ICIs, which may often have a more relevant impact on OS than on PFS. The long duration of ICI treatments across the different lines could be partially explained by the use of effective combinations, such as pembrolizumab plus enfortumab vedotin or pembrolizumab plus carboplatin/cisplatin-based chemotherapy in the first course. Finally, there was an interesting trend for a high efficacy of ICI rechallenging in bladder compared with upper tract UC which, even not reaching statistical significance, could be hypothesis generating for future studies.

Using a different ICI in the rechallenge strategy could achieve greater efficacy than maintaining the same ICI type. Initial studies pointed to a putative distinct effect of ICI linked to a particular tumor biology and host immune system.25,26 For example, when used preoperatively in patients with muscle-invasive tumors, atezolizumab and pembrolizumab revealed different activity across the molecular subtypes.27 In our analysis, the longest mDoT was achieved by patients treated with anti-PD1 after anti-PD-L1. This is in contrast with a smaller retrospective cohort of 25 patients treated with ICI rechallenge by Makrakis et al., achieving better responses when changing ICI class between anti-PD1 and anti-PD-L1 than retaining the same class (disease control rate 75% versus 20%).17 ICIs may differ in their exact mechanisms of action. Anti-PD-L1 is more specific for PD-L1 and does not target PD-L2, which is bound by anti-PD1. Therefore, this possible mechanism of resistance to anti-PD-L1 may be targeted by anti-PD1.28,29 This is only a hypothesis that may be further evaluated via direct comparisons.

Rechallenging ICI in patients who have previously experienced an immune-related adverse event (irAE) could raise concerns about the risk of further irAEs. Previous reports in the same population may support potential ICI rechallenge in well-selected patients developing irAEs, which may grant disease control.30 Unfortunately, toxicity data were unavailable because the platform did not allow individual data analyses, so we could not test this hypothesis in our population. However, such toxicity information (e.g. type, severity, management, and outcome of prior irAEs), pre-existing autoimmune conditions, and availability of alternative treatment options, among other factors, may also impact ICI rechallenge informed and shared decision-making.31

Our study has several inherent limitations. First, its retrospective design lacks randomization, potentially leading to selection and confounding biases. We tried to adjust the analyses using PSM between cohorts derived from the same real-world platform. Moreover, surveillance protocols and clinical practice could vary across the included institutions, and diagnosis was solely based on the International Classification of Disease-10 codes classification. Also, a centralized revision of pathologic or radiologic diagnoses and therapy responses was not possible due to the nature of the real-world data platform. Clinical, laboratory, and radiological data may be missing, which limits the conclusions. For example, we could not draw conclusions about the depth of the response and clinical (symptomatic) benefit. Finally, no data regarding the inclusions in clinical trials is available. Notwithstanding, the high number of analyzed patients and the hypothesis-generating results support the design of future trials (e.g. the ECOG ACRIN 8231 phase III trial).

Conclusions

Although it is an uncommon and not standard strategy, ICI rechallenges after prior ICI-based therapy may benefit a subset of patients with aUC, with outcomes varying based on the specific ICI used. The results of our study could generate hypotheses for future trials and be useful for clinicians facing limited options for patients with UC previously treated with ICIs and other therapies. Further studies and prospective data are warranted for optimal patient selection and tailoring treatments.
